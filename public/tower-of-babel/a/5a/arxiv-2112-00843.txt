Ramified descent and transcendental Brauer–Manin obstruction
Julian Lawrence Demeio

Abstract
We investigate the following problem. Given a smooth geometrically connected variety X X defined over a number field K K , and an étale torsor V → U V\rightarrow U over a Zariski-open U U of X X , which adelic points of X X can be approximated by adelic points that lift to a (twist of a) V V ? The question has long been investigated in the literature when U = X U=X , but less so in the general case. We introduce a Brauer–Manin obstruction to the problem, and provide an example where this obstruction is non-trivial and purely transcendental. This answers in the negative a question posed by Harari at a 2019 workshop. Our example is also an explicit example of a non-trivial transcendental Brauer–Manin obstruction on a smooth compactification of a quotient S ​ L n / G SL_{n}/G , with G G constant metabelian.



1
Introduction

Descent theory has long been used to understand how rational points
X(K)
of a smooth complete variety
X
defined over a number field
K
are distributed in the adelic points
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})
. It was first developed for proper varieties by Colliot-Thélène and Sansuc
[
5
]

[
27
]
, and it was later extended to open varieties by Harari and Skorobogatov
[
16
, Chapter 6]
. We investigate the matter of “ramified descent”, i.e. the behaviour of open descent theory under compactification, and answer a question of Harari on this topic.

For a torsor
\lambda:V\rightarrow U
under a group of multiplicative type
M/K
, the
descent set
is defined to be the set of those adelic points of
U
that lift to adelic points of a
K
-twist of
V
:

U(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\lambda}=\bigcup_{\sigma\in H^{1}(K,M)}\lambda_{\sigma}(V_{\sigma}(\mathbb{A}_{K})).

It follows from open descent theory
[
16
, Proposition 3.1]
that this set may be described in terms of a(n algebraic) Brauer–Manin obstruction: i.e. there exists a subgroup
\Br_{\lambda}U\subseteq\Br_{1}U
such that

U(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\lambda}=U(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\Br_{\lambda}U}.

(1.1)

Let now
X
be a smooth compactification of
U
, and
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\lambda}
be the adelic closure of
U(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\lambda}
in
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})
. The first result of this paper is that
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\lambda}
provides an obstruction to the Hasse Principle and weak approximation for
X
:

Theorem 1.1
.

The inclusion
\overline{X(K)}\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\lambda}
holds.

The proof we present, quite compact, is due to Olivier Wittenberg, whom the author thanks profoundly. (The original proof that the author had in mind was much more involved.)

We are mainly interested in this paper in the case where
M
is finite, in which case we call
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\lambda}
the “ramified descent set” (the adjective “ramified” indicates that the relative normalization of
U
in
V
is allowed to be ramified). (See also Section
3
for an alternative definition of the ramified descent set.)

In a 2019 workshop, Harari formulated a question to investigate how (
1.1
) behaves under “compactification”. We present his question in Section
4
(Question
4.1
), but a special, yet exemplative, case may be reformulated as follows (see Proposition
4.2
):

Question 1.2

(Harari)
.

Assume that
\Br X/\Br_{0}X
and
M
are finite. Does the identity
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\lambda}=X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\Br_{\lambda}U\cap\Br X}
hold?

Note that the inclusion
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\lambda}\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\Br_{\lambda}U\cap\Br X}
follows from (
1.1
).
Harari’s question was motivated by the fact that the analog of Question
1.2
has a positive answer when
M
is a torus (see
[
3
, Proposition 3.1]
). Moreover, when
M=\mu_{n}
is cyclic, and some mild ramification assumptions are satisfied, then a positive answer follows from a result of Colliot-Thélène and Skorobogatov
[
7
, Theorem 14.2.25]
(see Appendix
B
for more details).

We answer Question
1.2
negatively. In order to so, we introduce in Section
5
a new Brauer subgroup
\Br_{\lambda}^{ram}X\subseteq\Br X
, defined as the intersection
\Br_{\lambda}^{ram}U\cap\Br X
, where
\Br_{\lambda}^{ram}U
is the image of the composition

H^{2}(\Gamma_{M},\overline{K}^{*})\rightarrow H^{2}(\Gamma_{M},\overline{K}[V]^{*})\xrightarrow{\check{C}_{\mathfrak{l}}}H^{2}(U,\mathbb{G}_{m})=\Br U,

where
\check{C}_{\mathfrak{l}}
is the Čech-to-étale map associated to the
V_{\overline{K}}\rightarrow U
, which is a profinite torsor under the constant profinite group
\Gamma_{M}\coloneqq M(\overline{K})\rtimes\Gamma_{K}
(see Section
5
for more details). In the special case where both
\Pic V
and
\overline{K}[V]^{*}/\overline{K}^{*}
vanish, one has
\Br_{\lambda}^{ram}X=\Ker(\Br X\rightarrow\Br V_{\overline{K}})
, see Remark
5.1.4
. (In Example
5.1.5
we provide some examples where this vanishing happens.)
One verifies that:

Proposition 1.3
.

The inclusion
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\lambda}\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\Br_{\lambda}^{ram}X}
holds.

The group
\Br_{\lambda}^{ram}X
contains
\Br_{\lambda}U\cap\Br X
, see Proposition
5.1.6
. However, the former may in general be a bigger group and provide a bigger Brauer–Manin obstruction. We provide some explicit families where this is indeed the case in Section
6.1
, where we prove:

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
K
be a number field and
e
be a natural number such that
\mu_{e}\subset K^{*}
. If
H
is a constant metabelian finite subgroup of
SL_{n}
of exponent
e
,
H^{ab}
is its abelianization, and
\lambda
is the
H^{ab}
-torsor
SL_{n}/[H,H]\rightarrow SL_{n}/H
, then
\Br_{\lambda}^{ram}X=\Br X
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For every number field
K
and every prime
p\geq 5
such that
\mu_{p}\subset K^{*}
, there exists a constant nilpotent metabelian finite group
H
of exponent
p
such that, for any embedding
H\hookrightarrow SL_{n,K}
, letting
X
be a smooth compactification of
SL_{n,K}/H
, we have
\Br_{a}X=0
and

X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\Br X}\neq X(\mathbb{A}_{K}).

Here
\Br_{a}X
denotes the algebraic Brauer group of
X
modulo constants.
Combining Proposition
1.4
, Theorem
1.5
, and Proposition
1.3
we obtain the sought negative answer to Harari’s question: indeed for
X
as in the theorem, we have that
\Br_{\lambda}U\cap\Br X\subseteq\Br_{1}X
is constant as
\Br_{a}X=0
, while
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\lambda}\subseteq X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\Br_{\lambda}^{ram}X}=X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\Br X}\neq X(\mathbb{A}_{K})
.

Incidentally, Theorem
1.5
appears to be only the second known example of transcendental obstruction to weak approximation for quotients
SL_{n}/H
, or, in other words (see
[
13
, Sec. 1.2]
) to the Grunwald Problem for a finite group
H
. The first such example was obtained by Demarche, Lucchini and Neftin in
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
. However, unlike in
loc.cit.
, where the existence of a transcendental obstruction is proven non-constructively, we show the non-triviality of the transcendental Brauer–Manin pairing by computing it explicitly.

Structure of the paper

In Section
2
we settle our notation. In Section
3
we prove some basic facts about the “descent set”

for a finite

. Since it requires no further effort, we replace

here with a general finite group scheme

(not necessarily commutative). In the same section, we prove that

).
In Section

, we formulate Harari’s question precisely and show its relation with Question

.
In Section

, we introduce the Brauer subgroup

, prove that it contains

and that it obstructs

.
In Section
6
, we prove Proposition
1.4
and Theorem
1.5
. We do so by explicitly computing the unramified Brauer–Manin pairing on

for
H
nilpotent metabelian of odd prime exponent. This explicit computation extends earlier work of Bogomolov
[

, Sec. 5]
.
Appendix

contains some elementary lemmas that are used in Section

. Appendix

4
Harari’s question

Setup

Recall that
p:U\rightarrow\Spec K
is a geometrically integral smooth variety over a number field
K
,
X
is a smooth compactification of
U
, and
\lambda:V\rightarrow U
is a torsor under a finite group scheme
G/K
. We assume here that
G=A
is commutative, and let

be its Cartier dual. For every

(this is not a subgroup in general). Let

be a finite set of places of

, let
\left(P_{v}\right)_{v\in S}\in\prod_{v\in S}U\left(K_{v}\right)
, and

.

Let
\Br_{\lambda}U<\operatorname{Br}_{1}(U)
be the subgroup generated by the cup-products

, as

varies in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(K,A^{\prime})
, and let

.

Question 4.1

(Harari)
.

Assume that there is no Brauer–Manin obstruction for
(P_{v})_{v\in S}
with respect to
B
. Does there exist then an

such that
a_{v}=f_{v}
for all
v\in S
and
a_{v}\in E_{v}
for

?

Using Poitou-Tate duality, one may obtain a positive answer to Question
4.1
by replacing

with the subgroup
\left\langle E_{v}\right\rangle
of

generated by it (we leave this as an exercise to the interested reader, or see
[
10
]
). However, as originally remarked by Harari, there is a big difference between

and

in general!

The following proposition relates Question
4.1
with Question
1.2
:

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that
\Br X/\Br_{0}X
is finite and
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})\neq\emptyset
. Then the identity

holds if and only if there exists a finite

such that, for all

, Question
4.1
has a positive answer.

Proof.

The assumption implies that
B
is finite.

We prove the forward implication first. Let
S_{0}
be a set of places such that the Brauer–Manin pairing associated to
B
is trivial outside
S_{0}
.
Let
S\subseteq M_{K}
be a finite set containing
S_{0}
, and
\left(P_{v}\right)_{v\in S}\in\left(\prod_{v\in S}U\left(K_{v}\right)\right)^{B}
. We wish to find an
a\in H^{1}(K,A)
such that
a_{v}=f_{v}
for all
v\in S
and
a_{v}\in E_{v}
for all
v\notin S
. Let
P_{v},v\notin S
be any point of
U(K_{v})
. Note that
(P_{v})_{M_{K}}\in X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{B}.
Since
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\lambda}=X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{B},
we may approximate arbitrarily well
(P_{v})_{M_{K}}
with an adelic point
(Q_{v})_{v\in M_{K}}
such that there exists
a\in H^{1}(K,A)
for which
(Q_{v})_{v\in M_{K}}\in\lambda_{a}(V_{a}(\mathbb{A}_{K}))
. In particular,

for each
v
, or in other words the torsor
[V_{a}|_{Q_{v}}]
over
K_{v}
contains a
K_{v}
-point and is thus trivial. It follows that
0=[V_{a}|_{Q_{v}}]=[V|_{Q_{v}}]-a_{v}\in H^{1}(K_{v},A)
for all
v
, and hence
a_{v}\in E_{v}
for all
v
. Moreover, the map
[V|_{-}]:U(K_{v})\rightarrow H^{1}(K_{v},A)
is locally constant, and thus
[V|_{Q_{v}}]=[V|_{P_{v}}]=f_{v}
for

. The class

is now the sought class.

For the other direction, assume there is an
S_{0}
as in the statement. We need to show that
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\lambda}=X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{B}.
We may assume after enlarging
S_{0}
that its Brauer–Manin pairing on

is trivial for all places
v\notin S_{0}
.

Let
(P_{v})_{M_{K}}\in X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{B}
. Since
B
is finite,

is open. In particular, after an arbitrarily small approximation, we may assume that

. Then, for all

, our assumption that the answer to Question

is “yes” shows that there exists an

such that

for

and

for some

for

. Thus the

-torsor

specializes to the trivial torsor over

and over

, and hence there exists a point

whose image is

for

and

for

. In particular,

and therefore

, and we may thus modify

for

5
A Brauer–Manin obstruction to ramified descent

We recall that
\lambda:V\rightarrow U
is a torsor under a finite group scheme
G/K
, that
U
is smooth and geometrically integral over
K
, that
X
is a compactification of
U
, and that
Y\rightarrow X
is the relative normalization of

in

.
We defined:

X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\lambda}=\overline{{\bigcup_{\xi\in H^{1}(K,G)}\lambda_{\xi}(V_{\xi}(\mathbb{A}_{K}))}}

We define in this section a subgroup
\Br_{\lambda}^{ram}X\subseteq\Br X
such that

. The group

.

5.1
Definition of
\Br_{\lambda}^{ram}X

Let
\mathfrak{l}
be the composition
V_{\overline{K}}\rightarrow V\xrightarrow{\lambda}U
. There are natural

- and
\Gamma_{K}
-actions on

, the first induced by the

-action on

Lemma 5.1.1
.

The

- and the
\Gamma_{K}
- actions generate a
(G(\overline{K})\rtimes\Gamma_{K})
-action on
V_{\overline{K}}
. The profinite étale cover
\mathfrak{l}:V_{\overline{K}}\rightarrow U
is a profinite torsor under this
(G(\overline{K})\rtimes\Gamma_{K})
-action.

Proof.

Let
L/K
be a field extension. The points of
V_{L}
with values in a
K
-algebra
R
are described by

V_{L}(R)=\bigsqcup_{\iota:L\hookrightarrow R}V(R),

(5.1)

where
\iota
ranges among all
K
-embeddings of
L
in
R
. Consider the natural actions:

(i)

the right action of
G(L)
on
V_{L}
defined by letting
G(L)
act via the map
G(L)\rightarrow G(R)
on each disjoint set appearing in (
5.1
);

(ii)

when
L/K
is Galois, the right
\Gal(L/K)
-action defined by letting
\gamma\in\Gal(L/K)
act via
\iota\mapsto\iota\circ\gamma^{-1}
.

For
g\in G(L)
and
\gamma\in\Gal(L/K)
, we have
{\vphantom{\gamma}}{}^{\gamma}g\cdot\gamma\cdot x=\gamma\cdot g\cdot x
, where
x
is an

-point of
V_{L}
. By this relation, the two actions above generate a
(G(L)\rtimes\Gal(L/K))
-action on
V_{L}
. This action is fixed-point-free and it commutes with the projection
V_{L}\rightarrow U
. In addition, when

is finite and splits

(i.e. when

), letting

, we have

. Thus the morphism

, where

denotes the

-action on

, is an injective morphism of finite étale covers of

of the same degree, and hencean isomorphism. In other words,

is a torsor under

.

When

, the two actions (i) and (ii) are the ones described before the statement of the lemma, and taking an inverse limit over all finite Galois subextensions

that split

(these form a cofinal subset) finishes the proof.
∎

Let
\Gamma_{G}\coloneqq G(\overline{K})\rtimes\Gamma_{K}

under

For Section

Let

Theorem 6.1
.

If
\Sha^{1}_{\omega}(K,B^{\prime})=0
, then
\Br X=\Br_{\lambda}^{ram}X
.

(Recall that

denotes a smooth compactification of

.)

Proof.

It suffices to prove that
\Br_{ur}U\subseteq\im H^{2}(\Gamma_{A},\overline{K}^{*})
.
Consider the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence of
\overline{V}\xrightarrow{\Gamma_{G}}U
, keeping in mind that
\overline{K}[V]^{*}=\overline{K}^{*}
and

, we get the following exact sequence:

(6.1)

Since

, we have
\Br_{ur}V=\Br K
by
[
13
, Prop. 4]
. Thus any element of
\Br_{ur}U
lies in the kernel of
\Br U\rightarrow\Br\overline{V}
. In addition, we know that after base-changing to
\overline{K}

\Br_{ur}U

(see
[
1
, Lemma 5.1]
). In particular,
\Br_{ur}U
maps to
\Ker(H^{1}(\Gamma_{A},B^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\res}H^{1}(A,B^{\prime}))
in the sequence above. By the inflation-restriction five-term sequence of

, we have that
\Ker(H^{1}(\Gamma_{A},B^{\prime})\rightarrow H^{1}(A,B^{\prime}))=H^{1}(\Gamma_{K},B^{\prime})
. For
\beta\in\Br_{ur}U
, we denote by
\delta(\beta)
its image in

.

Finally, by functoriality of the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence, we get the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{2}(\Gamma_{A},\overline{K}^{*})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Ker(\Br U\rightarrow\Br\overline{V})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{1}(\Gamma_{A},B^{\prime})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{2}(\Gamma_{K},\overline{K}^{*})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Ker(\Br V\rightarrow\Br\overline{V})}

6.1

{H^{1}(\Gamma_{K},B^{\prime})}

In particular, we shall always tacitly identify

Our aim in this subsection is to explicitly describe

We may naturally associate to the central extension

[-,-]:A\times A\rightarrow B

of them in

Let

be the natural homomorphism
a_{1}\wedge a_{2}\mapsto[a_{1},a_{2}]

Recall that
A^{D}=\Hom(A,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})

\Lambda^{2}(A^{D})=(\Lambda^{2}A)^{D},\alpha_{1}\wedge\alpha_{2}\mapsto\left(a_{1}\wedge a_{2}\mapsto\alpha_{1}(a_{1})\alpha_{2}(a_{2})-\alpha_{1}(a_{2})\alpha_{2}(a_{1})\right).

We denote

\Lambda^{2}A^{D}
.
Let

\beta(a_{1}\wedge a_{2})=0

.

Let
\xi_{U}:\Lambda^{2}A^{D}=\Lambda^{2}H^{1}(A,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow\Br U
be the composition of the three maps:

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}H^{1}(A,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\cup}H^{2}(A,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})

(cup-product)

(6.2)

\displaystyle H^{2}(A,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})=H^{2}(A,\mu_{p})\xrightarrow{\check{C}_{\lambda}}H^{2}(U,\mu_{p})

(Čech-to-étale map)

(6.3)

\displaystyle H^{2}(U,\mu_{p})\rightarrow H^{2}(U,\mathbb{G}_{m})=\Br U

(changing coefficients)

(6.4)

We also define analogously a map
\xi_{\overline{U}}:\Lambda^{2}A^{D}\rightarrow\Br\overline{U}.

The following is a reformulation of a result of Bogomolov (see Lemma 5.1 of
[

]

Theorem 6.1.1

(Bogomolov, reformulated)
.

Assume that

is algebraically closed. The image of
(\Lambda^{2}A^{D})_{bic}
under
\xi_{U}
is unramified, and the following sequence is exact:

B^{D}\xrightarrow{\mathfrak{c}^{D}}(\Lambda^{2}A^{D})_{bic}\xrightarrow{\xi_{U}}\Br_{ur}U\rightarrow 1.

(6.5)

We introduce the following notation used in the proof. Let
\gamma\in\Lambda^{2}A^{D}=\linebreak\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda^{2}A,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
be a cocycle. Define
H_{\gamma}
as the central extension of
A
by
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
, characterized by the following property: for any
a_{1},a_{2}\in A
the commutator of their corresponding lifts
\bar{a}_{1},\bar{a}_{2}
in
H_{\gamma}
is

\bar{a}_{1}\bar{a}_{2}\bar{a}_{1}^{-1}\bar{a}_{2}^{-1}=\gamma(a_{1}\wedge a_{2})\in\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}.

Proof of Theorem

.

By Lemma 5.1 of

]
,

is isomorphic to

). It only remains to show that the induced homomorphism

One may read from pp. 462 and 469 in

\Lambda^{2}A^{D}\cong H^{2}(A,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})=H^{2}(A,\mu_{\infty})\rightarrow H^{2}(A,k^{*})\xrightarrow{\check{C}_{\lambda}}\Br U,

(6.6)

where the last map is defined via the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence, and the first isomorphism is defined by sending an element
\gamma\in\Lambda^{2}A^{D}=\Hom(\Lambda^{2}A,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})

of the central extension

.

.

Lemma 6.1.2
.

The isomorphism
\Lambda^{2}A^{D}\cong H^{2}(A,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}),\gamma\mapsto[H_{\gamma}]
coincides with the composition
\Lambda^{2}A^{D}\xrightarrow{\cup}H^{2}(A,\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{2}(A,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})

Proof.

For a monomial
\gamma=\gamma_{1}\wedge\gamma_{2}\in\Lambda^{2}A^{D}
, the extension

may be realized as the set
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}\times A
with multiplication defined by
(b_{1},a_{1})\cdot(b_{2},a_{2})=(b_{1}+b_{2}+\gamma_{1}(a_{1})\gamma_{2}(a_{2}),a_{1}+a_{2})

[H_{\gamma}]\in H^{2}(A,\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z})
is then represented by the

a_{1},a_{2}\mapsto\gamma_{1}(a_{1})\gamma_{2}(a_{2})

[

[

. Thus the two maps coincide on monomials

. Since these span
\Lambda^{2}A^{D}
, the two maps coincide, as wished.

6.2
A decomposition of
H^{2}(A,B)

For general abstract commutative groups
A
and
B
, we have a split exact sequence
[
2
, V.6.5]
:

0\rightarrow\operatorname{Ext}(A,B)\rightarrow H^{2}(A,B)\xrightarrow{\omega_{B}}\Hom(\Lambda^{2}A,B)\rightarrow 0.

(6.8)

The splitting is non-canonical in general, but it is canonical when
\#B
is odd: in this case a section
s_{B}
of
\omega_{B}
is given by
s_{B}:\gamma\mapsto\left(a_{1},a_{2}\mapsto\frac{1}{2}\gamma(a_{1}\wedge a_{2})\right)
. If
B=\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
, then
\Hom(\Lambda^{2}A,\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})=\Lambda^{2}\Hom(A,\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
and
2\cdot s_{B}
is the cup-product:

2\cdot s_{B}=\cup:\Lambda^{2}\Hom(A,\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})\rightarrow H^{2}(A,\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}).

(6.9)

The splitting
s_{B}
of (
6.8
) induces a canonical decomposition
H^{2}(A,B)=\operatorname{Ext}(A,B)\oplus\Hom(\Lambda^{2}A,B)
.

Lemma 6.2.1
.

Let
p
be an odd prime number, and assume that
A
and
B
have exponent
p
. Let
1\rightarrow B\rightarrow H\xrightarrow{\pi}A\rightarrow 1
be a nilpotent central extension of exponent
p
. Then the class
[H]\in H^{2}(A,B)
lies in the image of
s_{B}
.

Proof.

Equivalently, we have to prove that
[H]
maps to
0
under the projection
H^{2}(A,B)\rightarrow{\operatorname{Ext}(A,B)}
. This projection is natural in
A
(and
B
as well, but we do not need this).
Therefore, since the functor
{\operatorname{Ext}(A,B)}
is additive in
A
, and every abelian group decomposes into a direct sum of cyclic groups, it suffices to prove the result for a cyclic
A
. If
A
is cyclic and generated by
a\in A
, every
h\in\pi^{-1}(a)
must be of order
p
because
H
has exponent
p
. The element
h
thus provides a section
a\mapsto h
of
\pi
, and
H=B\oplus A
. Hence
[H]=0\in H^{2}(A,B)
, and in particular
H
maps to
0
in
{\operatorname{Ext}(A,B)}
as wished.
∎

6.3
Formula for the Brauer–Manin pairing

We work in the setting of
6.1
, but we also assume that
k=K
is a number field. All cohomology and homomorphism groups appearing in this subsection have a natural
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space structure, so whenever we take tensor products, alternating products, etc, we mean that these operations are performed as
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector spaces.

Let
v
be a finite place of
K
,
\Gamma_{v}=\Gal(\overline{K}_{v}/K_{v})
, and
G_{v}=\Gamma_{v}^{ab}/p\Gamma_{v}^{ab}
. Remembering that we fixed an isomorphism
\mu_{p}\cong\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
, and using the identifications
H^{1}(\Gamma_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})=\Hom(\Gamma_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})=\Hom(G_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})
, we may identify the perfect local duality pairing:

\cup:H^{1}(\Gamma_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow H^{2}(\Gamma_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})\cong\mathbb{F}_{p},

with a pairing:

H_{v}:\Lambda^{2}\Hom(G_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}.

(Note that this is alternating because
p
is odd.)

We choose a basis
g_{1},\ldots,g_{r}
of
G_{v}
, and a basis
t_{1},\ldots,t_{a}
of
A
.
These induce an identification:

\displaystyle\Hom(G_{v},A)

\displaystyle=Mat_{a\times r}(\mathbb{F}_{p})

(6.10)

For a finite-dimensional vector space
V/\mathbb{F}_{p}
, a basis
v_{1},\ldots,v_{l}
of
V
induces an identification:

\Lambda^{2}(V^{D})\cong Mat_{ant,l\times l}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),\ \ \ v_{i}^{D}\wedge v_{j}^{D}\mapsto e_{i}e_{j}^{T}-e_{j}e_{i}^{T},

where
e_{1},\ldots,e_{l}
denotes the standard basis of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{l}
, and
Mat_{ant}
denotes antisymmetric matrices. Specializing to
V=A
and
V=G_{v}
, we get identifications

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}A^{D}

\displaystyle=Mat_{a\times a,ant}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),

(6.11)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{2}G_{v}^{D}

\displaystyle=Mat_{r\times r,ant}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),

(6.12)

Let
\varphi\in\Hom(G_{v},A),\beta\in\Lambda^{2}A^{D},\gamma\in\Lambda^{2}G_{v}^{D}
, and let
M_{\varphi}\in Mat_{a\times r}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),M_{\beta}\in Mat_{a\times a,ant}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),M_{\gamma}\in Mat_{r\times r,ant}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
be their corresponding matrices under the identifications above. Let
\tilde{H}_{v}\in Mat_{r\times r,ant}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
be the matrix defined by
(\tilde{H}_{v})_{i,j}=\frac{1}{2}H_{v}(g_{j}\wedge g_{i})
.
One easily verifies that:

\begin{matrix}&\varphi^{*}\beta\in\Lambda^{2}G_{v}^{D}&\text{ corresponds to}&{M_{\varphi}}^{T}M_{\beta}{M_{\varphi}}&\in Mat_{r\times r,ant}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),\\
\text{ and}&H_{v}(\gamma)&=&\tr(\tilde{H_{v}}M_{\gamma})&\in\mathbb{F}_{p}.\end{matrix}

(6.13)

Recall that we have a homomorphism

\xi_{U}:\Lambda^{2}\Hom(A,\mathbb{F}_{p})\xrightarrow{\cup}H^{2}(A,\mathbb{F}_{p})\rightarrow\Br U.

We may compute the local Brauer pairing between
\im\xi_{U}
and
U(K_{v})
as follows:

Lemma 6.3.1
.

Let
\beta\in\Lambda^{2}\Hom(A,\mathbb{F}_{p})
,
b\coloneqq\xi_{U}(\beta)\in\Br U
,
P\in U(K_{v})
, and

be induced by the torsor type
[\lambda|_{P}]\in H^{1}(\Gamma_{v},A)=\Hom(\Gamma_{v},A)\cong\Hom(G_{v},A)
. Then:

(b,P)_{v}=\tr(\tilde{H_{v}}\tilde{M_{\varphi}}^{T}M_{\beta}M_{\varphi}).

Proof.

This follows by just unravelling the notation. Namely, we have that
(b,P)_{v}=\inv_{v}(P^{*}b)
, and the latter is equal to
H_{v}(\varphi^{*}\beta)=\inv_{v}(P^{*}b)
by the commutativity of the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\xi_{U}:}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda^{2}A^{D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{2}(A,\mathbb{F}_{p})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{2}(U,\mathbb{F}_{p})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\Br U)[p]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H_{v}:}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda^{2}G_{v}^{D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{2}(G_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{2}(\Gamma_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})=H^{2}(K_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\Br K_{v})[p]\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}}.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cup}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\check{C}_{\lambda}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cup}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\inf}

The formulas (

) now give the statement.
∎

Proof of Theorem

be the set of torsor types to which

.

\Hom(\Gamma_{v},A)\rightarrow H^{2}(\Gamma_{v},B),\ \ \xi\mapsto\xi^{*}([H]),

(6.14)

where

is the class representing

.

(Note that this inverse image is not a subspace in general!)

The commutative diagram with exact first row:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces SL_{n}(K_{v})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(SL_{n}/H)(K_{v})=U(K_{v})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{1}(K_{v},H)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{1}(K_{v},SL_{n})=0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{H^{1}(K_{v},A)=\Hom(\Gamma_{v},A)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces[\lambda_{-}]}

shows that

is the image of

.
Equivalently,
\Theta_{v}
consists of those homomorphisms that lift from

to the central extension

. The statement now follows from the theory of central extensions
[

, Section IV.3]
.
∎

Proposition 6.4.2
.

For every
p\geq 5
, number field
K
with
\mu_{p}\subseteq K^{*}
, and place
v
of
K
dividing
p
, there exists a constant
c(v)\geq 0
such that for any

, there exists a metabelian nilpotent
H
of exponent
p
with

of rank

such that, letting

:

(i)

(\Lambda^{2}A^{D})_{bic}=\Lambda^{2}A^{D}
;

(ii)

there exists
\alpha\in\Lambda^{2}A^{D}
such that the local pairing
(-,\xi_{U}(\alpha)):U(K_{v})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
attains at least two values.

Proof.

Let
a
be a natural number and
A\coloneqq(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{a}
. Lemma
A.2
of the appendix guarantees the existence of a metabelian nilpotent
H
of exponent

with

such that

has rank

and
(\Lambda^{2}A^{D})_{bic}=\Lambda^{2}A^{D}
.

Recall that
\Theta_{v}
is the image of
[\lambda|_{-}]:U(K_{v})\rightarrow\Hom(\Gamma_{v},A)
. Let
\Xi_{v}\subseteq\Theta_{v}
be the image under
[\lambda|_{-}]
of the left kernel of
U(K_{v})\times\Br_{e,ur}U\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},(P,b)\mapsto\inv_{v}(P^{*}b)
. Since
e\in\Xi_{v}
,
\Xi_{v}
is non-empty and to prove point
(ii)
it suffices to prove that
\Xi_{v}\neq\Theta_{v}
. We do so by proving that the cardinality of these two sets have different
p
-adic valuation for
a\geq c(v)
. We start by computing
v_{p}(\#\Theta_{v}).

Recall the identifications

\operatorname{Hom}\left(G_{v},A\right)=\operatorname{Mat}_{a\times r}\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right),\Lambda^{2}A^{D}=Mat_{a\times a,ant}\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right),\Lambda^{2}G_{v}^{D}=Mat_{r\times r,ant}\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right).

Identifying

with
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{b}
we get identifications

and
H^{2}(\Gamma_{v},B)=H^{2}(\Gamma_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})^{b}\xrightarrow[\sim]{\inv_{v}}\mathbb{F}_{p}^{b}
. By Lemma
6.2.1
and (

), there exist

such that
[H]=(\mathfrak{h}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{h}_{b})\in H^{2}(A,\mathbb{F}_{p})^{b},\ \mathfrak{h}_{i}\coloneqq\cup(h_{i})
, where
\cup
indicates the cup-product
\Lambda^{2}\Hom(A,\mathbb{F}_{p})=\Lambda^{2}H^{1}(A,\mathbb{F}_{p})\rightarrow H^{2}(A,\mathbb{F}_{p})
. Then by (
6.14
):

\Theta_{v}=\{M\in\Hom(\Gamma_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})\mid\inv_{v}(M^{*}\mathfrak{h}_{i})=0,\ i=1,\ldots,b\}.

(6.15)

By Lemma
6.3.1
, under the identification
\Hom(\Gamma_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})=\Hom(G_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})=Mat_{a\times r}\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)
,
\Theta_{v}
corresponds to:

{\Theta}_{v}=\{M\in Mat_{a\times r}\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)\mid\tr(\tilde{H_{v}}\tilde{M}^{T}\tilde{h_{i}}\tilde{M})=0,\ i=1,\ldots,b\}.

(6.16)

A special case of the Ax-Katz theorem
[
20
]
states that the
p
-adic valuation of the number of solutions of a system of
m
polynomial equations of degree
\leq d
in
n
(affine) variables in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is at least

.
Hence, since (
6.16
) describes
\Theta_{v}
as the solution set of

v_{p}(\#\Theta_{v})\geq\left\lceil\frac{ra-2b}{2}\right\rceil=\left\lceil\frac{(r-4)a+6}{2}\right\rceil\geq\left\lceil\frac{2a+6}{2}\right\rceil

as

by
[

, Thm. 7.5.11]
.

We now compute
v_{p}(\#\Xi_{v})
, using Lemma
A.3
from the appendix. Recall that
(\Lambda^{2}A^{D})_{bic}=\Lambda^{2}A^{D}
by our choice of

, and thus
\xi_{U}(\Lambda^{2}A^{D})=\Br_{ur}U
by Theorem

. Hence Lemma

implies that

lies in the left kernel of the local Brauer pairing
U(K_{v})\times\Br_{ur}U\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
if and only if
H_{v}(M_{P}^{*}\alpha)=(P,\xi_{U}(\alpha))_{v}=0
for all
\alpha\in\Lambda^{2}A^{D}
.
Therefore, (

) implies that the image of
\Xi_{v}
under the identification
\Hom(\Gamma_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})=\Hom(G_{v},\mathbb{F}_{p})=Mat_{a\times r}\left(\mathbb{F}_{p}\right)
is:

\displaystyle\Xi_{v}

\displaystyle=\{M\in\Theta_{v}\subseteq Mat_{a\times r}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\mid\tr(\tilde{H}_{v}M^{T}NM)=0,\text{ for all }N\in Mat_{a\times a,ant}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\}

\displaystyle=\{M\in Mat_{a\times r}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\mid\tr(\tilde{H}_{v}M^{T}NM)=0,\text{ for all }N\in Mat_{a\times a,ant}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\},

\displaystyle=\{M\in Mat_{a\times r}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\mid\tr(M\tilde{H}_{v}M^{T}N)=0,\text{ for all }N\in Mat_{a\times a,ant}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\},

where the second identity holds because the

quadratic equations describing
\Theta_{v}
are redundant in the description of
\Xi_{v}
(see (

)).
Since
\tr(X^{T}Y)
induces a perfect pairing on
Mat_{a\times a,ant}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
and

lies in

, we obtain from the above:

\Xi_{v}^{T}=\{M\in Mat_{r\times a}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\mid M\tilde{H}_{v}^{T}M^{T}=0\}.

The matrix

is invertible by local duality, and so Lemma

shows that there exists a function

(depending only on

) such that

if

. Recall that

, and let

If

, then

by the ultrametric triangular inequality.
Now

∎

.

:

Appendix A

Lemma A.1
.

Let

be positive integers and

a subset of cardinality

. There exists then an

such that

.

Proof.

Let

be the smallest integer such that

-dimensional subspace in

. If

, there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, let

be a

-dimensional subspace such that
L\cap X=\emptyset
. Let
\pi_{L}:\mathbb{P}^{N}\setminus l\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{N-k}
be a projection outside of
L
. We know by assumption that
\pi_{L}(X(\mathbb{F}_{p}))=\mathbb{P}^{N-k}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
, hence
\#X(\mathbb{F}_{p})\geq\#\mathbb{P}^{N-k}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\Rightarrow N-k<n
, i.e.
k\geq N-n+1
. Hence the dimension of
L
is
\geq N-n
and it is the sought subspace.
∎

The following lemma is inspired by
[
1
, Sec. 5]
, see also
[
6
, p. 37]
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
p\neq 2
be a prime. For every
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space
A
of dimension
4\leq a<\infty
, there exists an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space
B
, of dimension
b=2a-3
, and a (surjective) morphism

\mathfrak{c}:\Lambda^{2}A\rightarrow B,

(A.1)

such that, if
1\rightarrow B\rightarrow H\xrightarrow{\pi}A\rightarrow 1
is the extension whose commutator map is
\mathfrak{c}
, we have that there are no pure wedges
0\neq a_{1}\wedge a_{2}\in\Lambda^{2}A
lying in the kernel of
\mathfrak{c}
.

Proof.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(\Lambda^{2}A)
be the image of the “alternating Segre morphism”

-\wedge-:\mathbb{P}(A)\times\mathbb{P}(A)\setminus\Delta\rightarrow\mathbb{P}(\Lambda^{2}A),

which is isomorphic to the Grassmanian variety
\Gr_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(2,A)
. Since
X(\mathbb{F}_{p})
parametrizes two-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-subspaces of
A
:

\#X(\mathbb{F}_{p})=\frac{(p^{a}-1)(p^{a-1}-1)}{(p^{2}-1)(p-1)}.

(A.2)

It suffices to show that there exists a
(2a-3)
-codimensional subspace
L
in
\mathbb{P}(\Lambda^{2}A)
such that
L\cap X(\mathbb{F}_{p})=\emptyset
, and choose
\mathfrak{c}
such that
\Lambda^{2}A\supseteq\mathbb{F}_{p}\cdot L(\mathbb{F}_{p})=\Ker\mathfrak{c}
.
Noting that:

\frac{(p^{a}-1)(p^{a-1}-1)}{(p^{2}-1)(p-1)}<\frac{(p^{a}-1)(p^{a-1}-1)}{(p+1)(p-1)}\leq\frac{(p^{2a-2}-1)(p+1)}{(p+1)(p-1)}=\#\mathbb{P}^{2a-3}(\mathbb{F}_{p}),

such a subspace always exists by Lemma
A.1
.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Let
A,V
be
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector spaces with
p\neq 2
, and let
a:=\dim A,r:=\dim V
. Assume that
a\geq 2r
, and that
V
is endowed with an alternating non-degenerate bilinear form
b:V\times V\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{p}
. We then have that:

\Xi(A,V)\coloneqq\#\{\xi\in\Hom(A,V)\ \mid\ \xi^{*}b=0\}\equiv C(r)\mod p^{a},

where
C(r)
is a non-zero integer depending only on
r
.

Proof.

Let

M_{d}\coloneqq\#\{\text{isotropic $d$-dimensional subspaces in $V$}\},

I_{d}\coloneqq\#\{\text{surjective homomorphisms from $A$ to a $d$-dimensional $\mathbb{F}_{p}$-vector space}\}.

We then have that:

\Xi(A,V)=\sum_{d=0}^{\min(a,\frac{r}{2})}I_{d}M_{d}

(note that the fact that
V
is endowed with a non-degenerate alternating linear form and
p\neq 2
implies that
r
is even).
One can easily see that:

I_{d}=(p^{a}-1)\cdot(p^{a}-p)\cdots(p^{a}-p^{d-1}),\ \text{for every $d\leq a$},

M_{d}=\frac{(p^{r}-1)\cdot(p^{r-1}-p)\cdots(p^{r-d+1}-p^{d-1})}{(p^{d}-1)\cdot(p^{d}-p)\cdots(p^{d}-p^{d-1})},\ \text{for every $d\leq r/2$}.

In particular,
\Xi(A,V)=\Xi^{\prime}(a,r)
depends only on
a
and
r
. Note that, for a fixed
r
,
\Xi^{\prime}(a,r)
converges
p
-adically, as
a\rightarrow\infty
, to the following sum (which happens to be an integer number):

\displaystyle C(r)\coloneqq\Xi^{\prime}(\infty,r)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{d=0}^{r/2}(-1)^{d}\frac{(p^{r}-1)\cdot(p^{r-1}-p)\cdots(p^{r-d+1}-p^{d-1})}{(p-1)\cdot(p^{2}-1)\cdots(p^{d}-1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{d=0}^{r/2}(-1)^{d}\binom{r/2}{d}_{p^{2}}(p+1)\cdots(p^{d}+1),

where the subscript in the binomial denotes a Gaussian binomial coefficient (an integer number).
Moreover,
\Xi^{\prime}(a,r)\equiv\Xi^{\prime}(\infty,r)\mod p^{a}
if
a\geq 2r
.
Denoting by
a(d)
the term multiplying the
(-1)^{d}
appearing above, we notice that the sequence
a(0),\ldots,a(r/2)
is strictly increasing, as follows by induction from the fact that
\frac{p^{r-d+1}-p^{d-1}}{p^{d}-1}>1
for all
d\in\{0,\ldots,r/2\}
. In particular, a standard elementary calculus argument (à la Leibniz’ rule) shows that
\Xi^{\prime}(\infty,r)\neq 0
.
∎

Appendix B
Other works where ramified descent appears

Let me mention other works where the idea of “ramified descent” has already appeared. One is
[
17
]
by Harpaz and Skorobogatov (successor to Skorobogatov and Swinnerton-Dyer’s work
[
28
]

[
31
]
), where the authors use the cyclic ramified covers of some specific Kummer surfaces to prove that, under certain technical assumptions, these satisfy the Hasse principle.

Another work is Corvin and Schlank’s paper
[
8
]
, where the authors build upon Poonen’s example
[
24
]
to show (employing one specific ramified
S_{4}
-cover) that the following obstruction is
stronger
than étale-Brauer-Manin obstruction:

X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\Br,ram,sol}=\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi:Y\rightarrow X\\
G-\text{cover}\\
G\text{ solvable}\end{subarray}}\overline{\bigcup_{\xi\in H^{1}(K,G)}\psi^{\prime}_{\xi}(Y_{\xi}^{sm}(\mathbb{A}_{K}))^{\Br Y_{\xi}^{sm}}},

where the
\psi^{\prime}_{\xi}
is the composition
Y_{\xi}^{sm}\rightarrow Y_{\xi}\xrightarrow{\psi_{\xi}}X
.

Lastly, we mention Sections 11.5 and 14.2.5 of Colliot-Thélène and Skorobogatov’s book
[
7
]
, where ramified descent is investigated for
\mu_{n}
-covers. In particular, in Theorem 14.2.25 of
loc.cit.
, the authors prove a result which translates in our language to saying that, if
\lambda:V\rightarrow U
is a
\mu_{n}
-torsor such that there is a divisor on
X
over which the “compactification”
\psi:Y\rightarrow X
of
\lambda
(notation as in Section
3
) is totally ramified, then
X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\lambda}=X(\mathbb{A}_{K})^{\Br_{\lambda}^{ram}X}
. Their result and our Proposition
1.3
naturally lead us to the question:

Question B.1
.

Let
\lambda:V\rightarrow U
and
\psi:Y\rightarrow X
be as in Section
3
. Assume that the cover
Y\rightarrow X
is totally ramified, i.e.
Y
is geometrically integral and
Y\rightarrow X
does not have any unramified subcovers. Does one then have that
X\left(\mathbb{A}_{K}\right)^{\lambda}=X\left(\mathbb{A}_{K}\right)^{\mathrm{Br}_{\lambda}^{ram}X}
?

Note that a positive answer to the question above would guarantee that, for instance, if
Y
is a variety all of whose
G
-twists satisfy the Hasse principle, then
X
satisfies the Hasse principle up to Brauer-Manin obstruction.

Let us mention that, when
G
is supersolvable and
Y
is rationally connected,
Harpaz and Wittenberg prove that
[
19
, Theorem 1.4]
:

X\left(\mathbb{A}_{K}\right)^{\mathrm{Br}X}=\overline{\bigcup_{\xi\in H^{1}(K,G)}\psi^{\text{sm}}_{\xi}\left(Y_{\xi}\left(\mathbb{A}_{K}\right)^{\mathrm{Br}Y^{\text{sm}}_{\xi}}\right)}

(using our notation). It follows that
X\left(\mathbb{A}_{K}\right)^{\lambda}\supset X\left(\mathbb{A}_{K}\right)^{\mathrm{Br}X}
, i.e. Brauer–Manin obstruction is finer than ramified descent obstruction, but it also seems likely that their methods could be in fact used to give a positive answer to Question
B.1
in this case. For instance, when, in addition to the conditions above,
\overline{K}[V]^{*}/\overline{K}^{*}=0
and
\Pic\overline{V}=0
(e.g. if
V=SL_{n}
), then
\mathrm{Br}_{\lambda}^{ram}X=\Br X
by Remark
5.1.4
, and a positive answer to the question follows.

The Number of Solutions to the Trinomial Thue Equation
Greg Knapp

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the number of integer pair solutions to the equation | F ⁡ ( x , y ) | = 1 |F(x,y)|=1 where F ⁡ ( x , y ) ∈ ℤ ⁡ [ x , y ] F(x,y)\in\mathbb{Z}[x,y] is an irreducible (over ℤ \mathbb{Z} ) binary form with degree n ⩾ 3 n\geqslant 3 and exactly three nonzero summands. In particular, we improve Thomas’ explicit upper bounds on the number of solutions to this equation (see [ 13 ] ). For instance, when n ⩾ 219 n\geqslant 219 , we show that there are no more than 32 integer pair solutions to this equation when n n is odd and no more than 40 integer pair solutions to this equation when n n is even, an improvement on Thomas’ work in [ 13 ] , where he shows that there are no more than 38 such solutions when n n is odd and no more than 48 such solutions when n n is even.

2.
Counting With Gaps

The main technical accomplishment of this paper is the following version of a counting technique often used in conjunction with the “gap principle.”

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
L,M,T,p,y_{0},\dots,y_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
satisfy the following conditions:

(1)

L\leqslant y_{0}\leqslant\dots\leqslant y_{\ell}\leqslant M

(2)

p>2

(3)

L^{p-2}>T

(4)

y_{i+1}\geqslant{T}^{-1}y_{i}^{p-1}
for each
0\leqslant i<\ell

Then

\ell\leqslant\frac{\log\left[\frac{\log(MT^{-1/(p-2)})}{\log\left(LT^{-1/(p-2)}\right)}\right]}{\log(p-1)}.

This lemma is comparable to Lemma 1 in
[
11
]
. However, by fixing any
L>0
,
p>2
,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and
0<T<L^{p-2}
, then setting
y_{0}=L
,
y_{i}=T^{-1}y_{i-1}^{p-1}
, and
M=y_{\ell}
, one can see that this upper bound is sharp where the upper bound in Lemma 1 of
[
11
]
is not.

Proof.

By induction, we have

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\geqslant y_{\ell}\geqslant\frac{y_{\ell-1}^{p-1}}{T}\geqslant\frac{\left(\frac{y_{\ell-2}^{p-1}}{T}\right)^{p-1}}{T}=\frac{y_{\ell-2}^{(p-1)^{2}}}{T\cdot T^{p-1}}\geqslant\cdots

\displaystyle\cdots\geqslant\frac{y_{0}^{(p-1)^{\ell}}}{T^{\sum_{j=0}^{\ell-1}(p-1)^{j}}}=\frac{y_{0}^{(p-1)^{\ell}}}{T^{\frac{(p-1)^{\ell}-1}{p-2}}}\geqslant\frac{L^{(p-1)^{\ell}}}{T^{\frac{(p-1)^{\ell}-1}{p-2}}}

and we multiply both sides of

M\geqslant\frac{L^{(p-1)^{\ell}}}{T^{\frac{(p-1)^{\ell}-1}{p-2}}}

by
T^{-1/(p-2)}
to get

MT^{-1/(p-2)}\geqslant\left(LT^{-1/(p-2)}\right)^{(p-1)^{\ell}}.

Taking a log on both sides (and using the fact that
LT^{-1/(p-2)}>1
) yields

\frac{\log\left(MT^{-1/(p-2)}\right)}{\log\left(LT^{-1/(p-2)}\right)}\geqslant(p-1)^{\ell}

and taking logs again and using the fact that
p>2
yields the desired inequality.
∎

Given an algebraic
\beta
, Lemma 2 of
[
4
]
only counts the number of rational numbers which are nearest to
\beta
out of all of the conjugates of
\beta
and which form good approximations of
\beta
. If there were a real root
\alpha>1
of
f(x)
and a large special solution
(p,q)
of (
3
) for which
p/q
belonged to
\alpha
yet there was a root
\beta
of
f(x)
with
\beta
closer to
p/q
than
\alpha
, Lemma 2 of
[
4
]
would not count
p/q
. However, Lemma
3.4
confirms that this is not the case.

We first prove these two lemmas:

Proof of Lemma
3.3
.

Y_{0}
depends on the Mahler measure
M(F)
rather than the height
H(F)
. These are related (for trinomials
F(x,y)
) by
M(F)\leqslant 3^{1/2}H(F)
, which follows from the fact that
M(F)\leqslant\ell_{2}(F)
(see Lemma 1.6.7 in
[
3
]
). Now, using Thomas’ notation, we have that

Y_{0}:=\left(2C\right)^{\frac{1}{n-\lambda}}\left(4e^{A_{1}}\right)^{\frac{\lambda}{n-\lambda}}

where

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=(2n^{1/2}M(F))^{n}

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{n+a^{2}}}

\displaystyle A_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}}{2-nt^{2}}\left(\log M(F)+\frac{n}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{t(1-b)}.

Some of our other constants regularly appear in the estimation which shows
Y_{0}<Y_{F}
and we list them here for simplicity:

\displaystyle A=\frac{1}{a^{2}}=\frac{2}{2(n+a^{2})-2n}=\frac{2}{n+a^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{2-\frac{2n}{n+a^{2}}}=\frac{t^{2}}{2-nt^{2}}

\displaystyle L=\frac{\sqrt{2(n+a^{2})}}{1-b}=\frac{2}{1-b}\sqrt{\frac{n+a^{2}}{2}}=\frac{2}{t(1-b)}=\lambda

\displaystyle D=\frac{L}{n-L}=\frac{\lambda}{n-\lambda}

Note also that this implies that
D+1=\frac{n}{n-\lambda}
.

Before making the final estimate, we take a moment to observe that

\left(\sqrt{3}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-\lambda}+AD}<2^{\frac{n-1}{n-\lambda}}.

This estimate is tedious, but not difficult. One can show that

\left(\sqrt{3}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-\lambda}+AD}<2^{\frac{n-1}{n-\lambda}}

occurs if and only if

2<\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^{n+A\lambda}.

Estimating
A
from below by

A>\frac{1}{(1-\sqrt{2(n+1)/n^{2}})^{2}}

and estimating
\lambda
from below by
\lambda>\sqrt{2n}
gives that

2<\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^{n+A\lambda}

is implied by

2<\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^{n+\frac{n^{2}\sqrt{2n}}{n^{2}-2n\sqrt{2n+2}+2n+2}}.

Upon observing that

n+\frac{n^{2}\sqrt{2n}}{n^{2}-2n\sqrt{2n+2}+2n+2}\geqslant 20

when
n\geqslant 6
for instance, one can now see that

2<\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\right)^{n+\frac{n^{2}\sqrt{2n}}{n^{2}-2n\sqrt{2n+2}+2n+2}}

and as a result, we must have

\left(\sqrt{3}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-\lambda}+AD}<2^{\frac{n-1}{n-\lambda}}.

We can now conclude

\displaystyle Y_{0}

\displaystyle=(2C)^{\frac{1}{n-\lambda}}(4e^{A_{1}})^{\frac{\lambda}{n-\lambda}}

\displaystyle=(2(2n^{1/2}M(F))^{n})^{\frac{1}{n-\lambda}}(4e^{A(\log M(F)+\frac{n}{2})})^{\frac{\lambda}{n-\lambda}}

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{1+n+2\lambda}{n-\lambda}}\cdot n^{\frac{n}{2(n-\lambda)}}\cdot M(F)^{\frac{n}{n-\lambda}}e^{AD(\log M(F)+\frac{n}{2})}

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{1+n+2\lambda}{n-\lambda}}\cdot n^{\frac{n}{2(n-\lambda)}}\cdot M(F)^{\frac{n}{n-\lambda}+AD}\cdot e^{\frac{ADn}{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2^{\frac{1+n+2\lambda}{n-\lambda}}\cdot n^{\frac{n}{2(n-\lambda)}}\cdot(\sqrt{3}H(F))^{\frac{n}{n-\lambda}+AD}\cdot e^{\frac{ADn}{2}}.

Next we use the fact that
\left(\sqrt{3}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-\lambda}+AD}<2^{\frac{n-1}{n-\lambda}}
to find that

\displaystyle Y_{0}

\displaystyle<2^{\frac{1+n+2\lambda}{n-\lambda}+\frac{n-1}{n-\lambda}+AD}\cdot n^{\frac{n}{2(n-\lambda)}}\cdot H(F)^{\frac{n}{n-\lambda}+AD}\cdot e^{\frac{ADn}{2}}

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{2n+2\lambda}{n-\lambda}+AD}\cdot n^{\frac{D+1}{2}}\cdot H(F)^{1+D+AD}\cdot e^{\frac{ADn}{2}}

\displaystyle=H(F)^{\chi_{n}}\cdot\exp\left(\left(\frac{2n+2\lambda}{n-\lambda}+AD\right)\log(2)+\frac{D+1}{2}\log n+\frac{ADn}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=H(F)^{\chi_{n}}\cdot\exp\left(\left(\frac{4\lambda+2(n-\lambda)}{n-\lambda}+AD\right)\log(2)+\frac{D+1}{2}\log n+\frac{ADn}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=H(F)^{\chi_{n}}\cdot\exp\left(\left(4D+2+AD\right)\log(2)+\frac{D+1}{2}\log n+\frac{ADn}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=H(F)^{\chi_{n}}\cdot e^{\pi_{n}}

\displaystyle=Y_{F}.

∎

Proof of Lemma
3.4
.

Since
\frac{p}{q}
is a large special solution, we have

\displaystyle p>q\geqslant Y_{F}

\displaystyle=H^{\chi_{n}}e^{\pi_{n}}.

Since
\frac{p}{q}
belongs to
\alpha
, Thomas’ Corollary 3.1 in
[
13
]
indicates that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{p}{q}-\alpha\right|

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{p^{n^{*}}q}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{Y_{F}^{n/2}}.

Suppose, by contradiction, that there exists
\beta\in\mathbb{C}
with
f(\beta)=0
and
\left|\frac{p}{q}-\beta\right|<\left|\frac{p}{q}-\alpha\right|
. Then by the triangle equality, we find that

\displaystyle\left|\alpha-\beta\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|\frac{p}{q}-\beta\right|+\left|\frac{p}{q}-\alpha\right|

(12)

\displaystyle<\frac{2}{Y_{F}^{n/2}}

Since
\alpha
and
\beta
are distinct roots of
f
, Theorem 4 in
[
10
]
indicates that

(13)

\displaystyle|\alpha-\beta|>\frac{1}{2n^{n/2+2}(4H)^{n}}=\frac{1}{2^{2n+1}n^{n/2+2}H^{n}}.

Combining (
12
) and (
13
), we find that

\frac{1}{2^{2n+1}n^{n/2+2}H^{n}}<\frac{2}{Y_{F}^{n/2}}

and rearranging yields

\frac{Y_{F}^{n/2}}{2^{2n+2}n^{n/2+2}H^{n}}<1.

From here, we can use the fact that
Y_{F}=H^{\chi_{n}}e^{\pi_{n}}
to find

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle>\frac{H^{n(\chi_{n}/2-1)}e^{n\pi_{n}/2}}{2^{2n+2}n^{n/2+2}}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{e^{n\pi_{n}/2}}{2^{2n+2}n^{n/2+2}}

where the last inequality follows because
\chi_{n}\geqslant 2
. After rearranging, this implies that

\displaystyle\pi_{n}

\displaystyle<\frac{(4n+4)\log(2)+(n+4)\log(n)}{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant 5\log(2)+2\log(n)

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
n\geqslant 6
. However, the last inequality contradicts our hypothesis that
\pi_{n}\geqslant 5\log(2)+2\log(n)
, so no such
\beta
can exist and the closest root of
f(x)
to
p/q
is
\alpha
.
∎

Finally, we prove proposition
3.2
.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Let
\alpha>1
be a real root of
f(x)
. By lemma
3.3
, every large special solution
(p,q)
so that
p/q
belongs to
\alpha
is large in the sense of
[
4
]
. Moreover, by lemma
3.4
, any large special solution
(p,q)
so that
p/q
belongs to
\alpha
has

\left|\frac{p}{q}-\alpha\right|=\min_{f(\beta)=0}\left|\frac{p}{q}-\beta\right|.

Hence, every large special solution
(p,q)
so that
p/q
belongs to
\alpha
is large (in the sense of
[
4
]
) and is nearest to
\alpha
among all the roots of
f(X)
. Lemma 2 of
[
4
]
indicates that there are no more than

Z=\left\lfloor\frac{\log E+2\log(n)-\log(L-2)}{\log(n-1)}\right\rfloor+2

large solutions
(p,q)
so that
p/q
is nearest to
\alpha
among all the roots of
f(X)
and so we conclude that there are no more than
Z
large special solutions
(p,q)
with
p/q
belonging to
\alpha
.
∎

, solves the Thue equation

, then stores the trinomials and their solution lists in a .csv file called

import itertools
import csv

ef TrinomialThueWriter(degree,height):
filename = ”thue_equation_solution_data/degree__height__thue_equations.csv”.format(degree,height)
columnHeads = [”Number of Solutions to —F(x,y)— = 1”, ”Leading Coefficient”, ”Middle Coefficient”, ”Constant Coefficient”, ”Middle Degree”, ”List of Solutions to —F(x,y)— = 1”]
rows = []
# Note that we only need to check positive leading coefficients since F(x,y) will have the same solutions as -F(x,y).
# Note also that if the leading coefficient is larger than the absolute value of the constant coefficient, then we will have already computed the reciprocal polynomial F(y,x). Hence, we can skip polynomials where the constant coefficient has absolute value less than the leading coefficient.
for leadCoeff in range(1,height + 1):
for midCoeff in itertools.chain(range(-height,0),range(1,height+1)):
for constantCoeff in itertools.chain(range(-height,-leadCoeff+1), range(leadCoeff,height+1)):
if (abs(leadCoeff) == height or abs(midCoeff)== height or abs(constantCoeff)== height) and (gcd(gcd(leadCoeff,midCoeff),constantCoeff)==1):
for midDegree in range(1,degree):
P = leadCoeff * x^degree + midCoeff * x^midDegree + constantCoeff
if P.is_irreducible():
thueInfo = gp.thueinit(P)
negSolns = gp.thue(thueInfo,-1)
posSolns = gp.thue(thueInfo,1)
totalSolns = len(negSolns)+len(posSolns)
rows.append([totalSolns, leadCoeff, midCoeff, constantCoeff, midDegree, gp.concat(posSolns,negSolns)])
with open(filename,’w’) as csvfile:
csvwriter = csv.writer(csvfile)
csvwriter.writerow(columnHeads)
csvwriter.writerows(rows)

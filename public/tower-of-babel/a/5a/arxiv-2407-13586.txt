Abstract
. Let R \mathrm{R} be a real closed field, S ⊂ R n S\subset\mathrm{R}^{n} a closed and bounded semi-algebraic set and 𝐟 = ( f 1 , … , f p ) : S → R p \mathbf{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{p}):S\rightarrow\mathrm{R}^{p} a continuous semi-algebraic map. We study the poset module structure in homology induced by the simultaneous filtrations of S S by the sub-level sets of the functions f i f_{i} from an algorithmic and quantitative point of view. For fixed dimensional homology we prove a singly exponential upper bound on the complexity of these modules which are encoded as certain semi-algebraically constructible functions on R p × R p \mathrm{R}^{p}\times\mathrm{R}^{p} . We also deduce for semi-algebraic filtrations of bounded complexity, upper bounds on the number of equivalence classes of finite poset modules that such a filtration induces – establishing a tight analogy with a well-known graph theoretical result on the “speed” of algebraically defined graphs.

More generally, persistent homology theory over arbitrary posets have been developed

Study of persistent homology restricted to tame spaces – such

Let

We study in this paper semi-algebraic multi-persistence modules induced by filtrations of the sublevels sets of continuous semi-algebraic maps

We address the following problems.

(A)

(B)

We also consider the problem of designing an efficient algorithm for computing the constructible function mentioned above.

(C)

Finally, we consider the problem of understanding the whole class of finite poset modules obtainable from semi-algebraic multi-filtrations of bounded complexity.

1.2.
Complexity of semi-algebraic multi-persistence modules

In order to state our results we need to recall some basic definitions.
Let
\mathbf{k}
be a field fixed for the rest of the paper.

We denote by
\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbf{k}}
the category of
\mathbf{k}
-vector spaces and linear maps.

Definition 1.1

(Poset modules)
.

Given a partially ordered set (poset)
(P,\preceq)
, a

\mathbf{P}:(P,\preceq)\rightarrow\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbf{k}},

where we consider
(P,\preceq)
as a poset category – namely, the category whose objects are elements
p\in P
, and the set of morphisms
\mathrm{Hom}_{(P,\preceq)}(p,p^{\prime})
is empty if
p\not\preceq p^{\prime}
and a singleton set,
which will denote by “
p\preceq p^{\prime}
”, otherwise.

Definition 1.2

(Isomorphism of poset modules)
.

We say that two poset modules
\mathbf{P},\mathbf{P^{\prime}}:P\rightarrow\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbf{k}}
are isomomorphic if there exists a natural transformations
F,G
as depicted below which are inverses of each other:

\textstyle{P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathbf{P}}
\scriptstyle{\mathbf{P}^{\prime}}
\textstyle{F\Uparrow}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbf{k}},}
\textstyle{P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathbf{P}}
\scriptstyle{\mathbf{P}^{\prime}}
\textstyle{\Downarrow G}
\textstyle{\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbf{k}}.}

We will deal mostly with the poset
(\mathrm{R}^{p},\preceq)
,
where the partial order
\preceq
is defined coordinate-wise i.e.

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{p})=\mathbf{a}\preceq\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{p})

if and only if
a_{i}\leq b_{i},1\leq i\leq p
.

For any poset
(P,\preceq)
, we will denote by
\mathrm{pairs}(P)=\{(p,p^{\prime})\;\mid\;p\preceq p^{\prime}\}
.

In the case
P=\mathrm{R}^{p}
, we will identify

\mathrm{pairs}(\mathrm{R}^{p})
with the semi-algebraic subset of
\mathrm{R}^{p}\times\mathrm{R}^{p}=\mathrm{R}^{2p}
, defined by the inequalities
Y_{i}-Y_{i}^{\prime}\leq 0,1\leq i\leq p
, where
(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{p},Y_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,Y_{p}^{\prime})
are the coordinates
with respect to the standard basis of
\mathrm{R}^{2p}
.

A key role in what follows will be played by semi-algebraically constructible functions whose definition we recall below.

Definition 1.3
.

A
semi-algebraically constructible function

F:S\rightarrow\mathbf{k},

where
S
is a semi-algebraic set, is
a
\mathbf{k}
-linear combination of the characteristic functions of some finite number of semi-algebraic subsets of
S
.
More generally, we say that a function
F=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{M}):S\rightarrow\mathbf{k}^{M}
is semi-algebraically constructible, if
each
f_{i}
is semi-algebraically constructible.

Clearly, to each semi-algebraically constructible function
F:S\rightarrow\mathbf{k}^{M}
, there exists a finite partition of
S
into semi-algebraic subsets on which
F
is constant.
We will say that such a partition is
subordinate to
F
. Clearly,
if a partition is subordinate to
F
, and a finer partition will also
be subordinate to
F
.

We will make use of
semi-algebraic partitions of a special kind which we introduce below.

Notation 1.1

(Sign conditions, realization,
\mathrm{Cc}(\cdot)
)
.

For a finite subset
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathrm{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
we will call any element
of
\{0,1,-1\}^{\mathcal{P}}
a
sign condition on
\mathcal{P}
. We will denote by

{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma)=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathrm{R}^{n}\;|\;\mbox{\bf sign}(P(\mathbf{x}))=\sigma(P),P\in\mathcal{P}\}.

We denote by

\mbox{\rm Sign}(\mathcal{P})=\{\sigma\in\{0,1,-1\}^{\mathcal{P}}\mid{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma)\neq\emptyset\},

and call
\mbox{\rm Sign}(\mathcal{P})
the set of realizable sign conditions of

\mathcal{P}
.

We will denote
\mathrm{Cc}(\sigma)
the set of semi-algebraically connected components of
{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma)
, and denote

\mathrm{Cc}(\mathcal{P})=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\mbox{\rm Sign}(\mathcal{P})}\mathrm{Cc}(\sigma).

(Note that
\left(C\right)_{C\in\mathrm{Cc}(\mathcal{P})}
is a finite partition of
\mathrm{R}^{n}
into non-empty locally closed semi-algebraic sets.)

Notation 1.2
.

For a finite subset
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathrm{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
,
we will denote
\deg(\mathcal{P})=\max_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\deg(P)
.

We now define a notion of complexity of a semi-algebraically constructible function in terms of the “complexity” of partitions
subordinate to it.

Definition 1.4

(Complexity of semi-algebraically constructible functions)
.

We will say that
a semi-algebraically constructible function
F:S\rightarrow\mathbf{k}^{M},S\subset\mathrm{R}^{n}
has complexity
\leq D
, if there exists

\mathcal{P}\subset\mathrm{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
, such that
the partition

\left(C\right)_{C\in\mathrm{Cc}(\mathcal{P}),C\subset S}
is subordinate to

F
,
and

\mathrm{card}(\mathcal{P})\cdot\deg(\mathcal{P})\leq D
.

Remark 1

(Alternative definitions of complexity)
.

An alternative definition of the complexity of
F
would be
to say that complexity of a semi-algebraically constructible function
F
is
\leq D
if there exists
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathrm{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]

such that the partition

\left({\mathcal{R}}(\sigma)\right)_{\sigma\in\mbox{\rm Sign}(\mathcal{P}),{\mathcal{R}}(\sigma)\subset S}

is subordinate to

F
,
and

\mathrm{card}(\mathcal{P})\cdot\deg(\mathcal{P})\leq D.

However, the semi-algebraically connected components of
a
\mathcal{P}
-semi-algebraic set, where
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathrm{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]_{\leq d},\mathrm{card}(\mathcal{P})\leq s
, are all

\mathcal{Q}
-semi-algebraic sets, for some
\mathcal{Q}\subset\mathrm{R}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]
, with
\mathrm{card}(\mathcal{Q})\leq s^{n}d^{O(n^{4})}
and
\deg(\mathcal{Q})\leq d^{O(n^{3})}

[
8
, Theorem 16.11]
. So if the complexity of
F
is bounded by
D
according to Definition
1.4
, then it is bounded by
D^{n^{O(1)}}
by this alternative definition – and so the asymptotic bounds in this paper, such as in Theorem
1
,
is unchanged if we use this alternative definition.

Semi-algebraically constructible functions have been studied from the point of view computational complexity theory earlier in
[
11
]
. In that paper, the complexity (in terms of formula size) of a semi-algebraically constructible function is also defined in terms of subordinate partitions
[
11

. However, the definition in
[
11
]
is a bit more nuanced than the definitions given above, since it is aimed at developing a broader theory of complexity classes of constructible functions (and sheaves) which is not our aim here. So we do not repeat it here but mention that the upper bound in Theorem
1
would apply even with the definition given in
[
11
]
.

We now arrive at one of the central objects of interest in this paper.

Definition 1.5

(Semi-algebraically constructible poset modules and their complexity)
.

We say that a poset module

\mathbf{P}:(\mathrm{R}^{p},\preceq)\rightarrow\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbf{k}},

is semi-algebraically constructible, if there exists
K\geq 0
, and a semi-algebraically constructible function

F:\mathrm{pairs}(\mathrm{R}^{p})\rightarrow\mathbf{k}^{K\times K},

such that
\mathbf{P}
is isomorphic (see Definition
1.2
) to the poset module

\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}:(\mathrm{R}^{p},\preceq)\rightarrow\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbf{k}},

defined by:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{k}^{\dim\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{y})},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbf{P}}(\mathbf{y}\preceq\mathbf{y}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle L_{\mathrm{Trunc}_{\dim\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{y}^{\prime}),\dim\mathbf{P}(\mathbf{y})}^{K}(F(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}^{\prime}))},

where for
0\leq p,q\leq K
,

\mathrm{Trunc}_{p,q}^{K}:\mathbf{k}^{K\times K}\rightarrow\mathbf{k}^{p\times q},

is the map that extracts the
p\times q
matrix out of an
K\times K
matrix by extracting the first
p
rows and the first
q
columns, and for
M\in\mathbf{k}^{p\times q}
,
L_{M}:\mathbf{k}^{q}\rightarrow\mathbf{k}^{p}
denotes the homomorphism

\mathbf{x}\mapsto M\cdot\mathbf{x}
.

We will say that
F
is associated to
\mathbf{P}
,
and that
\mathbf{P}

has complexity
\leq D
, if
the semi-algebraically constructible function
F
has complexity
\leq D
(see Definition
1.4
).

We finally arrive at the notion of poset modules induced by semi-algebraic multi-filtrations.

Definition 1.6

(Poset modules induced by a semi-algebraic multi-filtration)
.

Let
S\subset\mathrm{R}^{n}
be a closed and bounded semi-algebraic set and

\mathbf{f}:S\rightarrow\mathrm{R}^{p}
be a continuous semi-algebraic map.

For
\mathbf{y}\in\mathrm{R}^{p}
, we denote by
S_{\mathbf{f}\preceq\mathbf{y}}=\{x\in S\mid\mathbf{f}(x)\preceq\mathbf{y}\}
.

For
\ell\geq 0
, we denote by

\mathbf{P}_{S,\mathbf{f},\ell}:(\mathrm{R}^{p},\preceq)\rightarrow\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbf{k}}

the poset module (which we call the
\ell
-th multi-persistence module of the filtration of
S
by
\mathbf{f}
) defined by

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{S,\mathbf{f},\ell}(\mathbf{y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm H}_{i}(S_{\mathbf{f}\preceq\mathbf{y}}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{S,\mathbf{f},\ell}(\mathbf{y}\preceq\mathbf{y}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\iota_{\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}^{\prime},\ell},

where

\mbox{\rm H}_{\ell}(\cdot)
denotes the
\ell
-th homology group with coefficients in
\mathbf{k}
, and

\iota_{\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}^{\prime},\ell}:\mbox{\rm H}_{i}(S_{\mathbf{f}\preceq\mathbf{y}})\rightarrow\mbox{\rm H}_{i}(S_{\mathbf{f}\preceq\mathbf{y}^{\prime}})

is the homomorphism induced by the inclusion

S_{\mathbf{f}\preceq\mathbf{y}}\hookrightarrow S_{\mathbf{f}\preceq\mathbf{y}^{\prime}}
.

1.3.
Speed of semi-algebraic multi-persistence modules

Our next result involves the notion of “speed” of semi-algebraic
multi-persistence modules that we introduce in this paper.
In order to motivate it we recall an important notion from graph theory
that plays a guiding role.

1.3.1.
Speed of semi-algebraic graphs

Suppose
F\subset\mathrm{R}^{p}\times\mathrm{R}^{p}
be a
fixed

semi-algebraic relation (i.e. a semi-algebraic subset of
\mathrm{R}^{p}\times\mathrm{R}^{p}
). A labelled (directed) graph
(V,E\subset V\times V)
, with
V=\{1,\ldots,N\}
is called an
F
-graph if there exists

(\mathbf{y}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{y}_{N})\in(\mathrm{R}^{p})^{N}
, such that

(i,j)\in E\Leftrightarrow(\mathbf{y}_{i},\mathbf{y}_{j})\in F.

Families of
F
-graphs, where
F
is a semi-algebraic relation, have been studied in graph theory from several perspectives such as Ramsey theory, Zarankiewicz problem etc.
(see for example,
[
2
,
33
]
). We will be concerned
with another very well-studied property, called “speed”, of such families.

The function
G_{F}:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
, defined by

G_{F}(N)=\mbox{ number of $F$-graphs with vertex set $[1,N]$}

is called the
speed of the family of graphs defined by
F
.

The following theorem (slightly simplified) is a key result in the
theory of semi-algebraic graphs.

Theorem
.

[
45
, Theorem 1.2]

For every semi-algebraic relation
F\subset\mathrm{R}^{p}\times\mathrm{R}^{p}
,

G_{F}(N)\leq N^{(1+o_{F}(N))pN}

(where the
o_{F}(N)
term depends on
F
).

1
1

1

Theorem 1.2 in
[
45
]
is slightly more general in that it gives a bound on the number of edge-labellings taking values in some fixed finite set
\Lambda
. The Theorem stated here is the special case when
\Lambda=\{0,1\}
.

There is obviously a compelling analogy between semi-algebraic families of graphs semi-algebraically defined families of multi-persistence modules –
which leads naturally to the question whether there is an analog of
the above theorem for semi-algebraically defined families of multi-persistence modules
as well. We answer this question positively in this paper.

First, we need to specify when to call two poset modules equivalent.

Definition 1.7

(Strong and weak equivalence of poset modules)
.

We will say that two poset modules,

\mathbf{P}^{\prime}:(P^{\prime},\preceq^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{P}^{\prime\prime}:(P^{\prime\prime},\preceq^{\prime\prime})\rightarrow\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbf{k}},

are
strongly equivalent
if

(P^{\prime},\preceq^{\prime})=(P^{\prime\prime},\preceq^{\prime\prime})

and the two functors

\mathbf{P}^{\prime},\mathbf{P}^{\prime\prime}
are isomorphic
(see Definition
1.2
).

We will say that two poset modules,

\mathbf{P}^{\prime}:(P^{\prime},\preceq^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbf{k}},\mathbf{P}^{\prime\prime}:(P^{\prime\prime},\preceq^{\prime\prime})\rightarrow\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbf{k}},

are
weakly equivalent
if there is a poset isomorphism

\phi:(P^{\prime},\preceq^{\prime\prime})\rightarrow(P^{\prime\prime},\preceq^{\prime\prime}),

such that two poset modules

\mathbf{P}^{\prime},\mathbf{P}^{\prime\prime}\circ\phi:P^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbf{k}}

are strongly equivalent.

Clearly, strong equivalence implies weak equivalence but not conversely.

Definition 1.8
.

Suppose
S
is a closed and bounded semi-algebraic set and

\mathbf{f}:S\rightarrow\mathrm{R}^{p}
a continuous semi-algebraic map.

For
\ell\geq 0
, and any finite tuple

T=(\mathbf{t}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{t}_{N})\in(\mathrm{R}^{p})^{N},

we denote by

\preceq_{T}
the partial order on
\{1,\ldots,N\}
defined by

j\preceq_{T}j^{\prime}\Leftrightarrow\mathbf{t}_{j}\preceq\mathbf{t}_{j^{\prime}},

and we denote

\mathbf{P}_{S,\mathbf{f},T,\ell}:(\{1,\ldots,N\},\preceq_{T})\rightarrow\mathrm{Vect}_{\mathbf{k}}

the poset module defined by

\mathbf{P}_{S,\mathbf{f},T,\ell}(j)=\mbox{\rm H}_{\ell}(S_{\mathbf{f}\preceq\mathbf{t}_{j}}).

Theorem 2

(Speed of semi-algebraic multi-persistence)
.

Let
S\subset\mathrm{R}^{n}
be a
\mathcal{P}
-closed bounded semi-algebraic set and

\mathbf{f}:S\rightarrow\mathrm{R}^{p}
a
\mathcal{Q}
-closed continuous semi-algebraic map.
Then for every
\ell\geq 0
, and

2.
Background, significance and prior work

2.1.
Algorithmic semi-algebraic geometry

The algorithmic problem of computing topological invariants of a given semi-algebraic set described by a quantifier-free formula, whose atoms are of the form,
P>0,P\in\mbox{\rm D}[X_{1},\ldots,X_{k}]
is very well studied. These include the problems of deciding emptiness,
computing the number of semi-algebraically connected components as as well as the higher Betti numbers, the Euler-Poincaré characteristic etc.
[
46
,
17
,
4
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
.
More recently, in
[
13
]
, the authors give algorithms for computing a newer topological invariant – namely, the
persistent homology barcodes
of a filtration of a semi-algebraic set, by the sub-level sets of a given continuous semi-algebraic function.
It is generalization of this latter result and some important quantitative consequences that is the main topic of the current paper.

2.2.
Complexity – singly vs doubly exponential

Complexity of algorithms for computing topological invariants will play a key role in our results. We briefly survey the
state-or-the art in the area of algorithmic complexity of computing topological invariants of semi-algebraic sets. These algorithms fall into classes.

2.2.1.
Doubly exponential

First recall that closed and bounded semi-algebraic subsets
S\subset\mathrm{R}^{n}
are semi-algebraically triangulable – and moreover given a description of the semi-algebraic set by a quantifier-free formula, such a triangulation can be effectively computed with complexity
(measured in terms of the number
s
of polynomials appearing in the description and their maximum degree
d
) which is
doubly exponential
in
n
– more precisely
(sd)^{2^{O(n)}}
.
Together with standard algorithms of linear algebra, this gives an algorithm for computing all the Betti numbers

4
4

4

Here and everywhere else in the paper all homology groups considered are with coefficients in a fixed field
\mathbf{k}
.

of a given semi-algebraic set with doubly exponential complexity (we define “complexity of algorithms” more precisely later in Definition
1.9
).
Computing Betti numbers of semi-algebraic sets using the above method is not sensitive to the dimension of the homology – the doubly exponential complexity persists even if we are interested in computing the small Betti numbers only, even for example, the zero-th Betti number which is just the number of semi-algebraically connected components.

Singly exponential

Classical bounds coming from Morse theory
[

Additionally, it is known that the problem of computing the zero-th Betti number of

More recently algorithms with singly exponential complexity has been obtained for several other problems in semi-algebraic geometry:

Remark 7

Algorithms for computing the Betti numbers of semi-algebraic sets have also been

However, in these results the complexity depends on the condition number of the input, and the algorithm will fail for ill-conditioned input when the condition number becomes

So these approaches are not comparable.
There has not been any attempt to extend the
numerical algorithms mentioned above for computing Betti numbers to computing
persistent homology of semi-algebraic filtrations. Such an extension would necessarily involve defining a
good notion of a “condition number of a semi-algebraic filtration” which has not been attempted to the best of our knowledge.
Moreover, note that numerical algorithms typically do not produce quantitative
mathematical results – for example, Theorems

and

in the current paper, as well as many other such results (see Remark

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{a,b}(y\rightarrow v)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{a,b}(z\rightarrow v)

\displaystyle[a,b]^{t}

Claim 2.1
.

If
[a:b]\neq[c:d]
as points of
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbf{k}}

the poset modules

Proof.

Let

Persistent homology is a central object in the emerging field of topological data analysis

Notation 3.5

(Diagram of various unions of a finite number of subspaces)
.

Let
J
be a finite set,
A
a closed and bounded semi-algebraic set,
and
\mathcal{A}=(A_{j})_{j\in J}
a tuple of closed and bounded semi-algebraic subsets of
A
indexed by
J
.

For any subset

J^{\prime}\subset J
,
we denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{J^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcup_{j^{\prime}\in J^{\prime}}A_{j^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{J^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigcap_{j^{\prime}\in J^{\prime}}A_{j^{\prime}},

We consider
2^{J}
as a category whose objects are elements of
2^{J}
, and whose only morphisms
are given by:

\displaystyle 2^{J}(J^{\prime},J^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\emptyset\mbox{ if }J^{\prime}\not\subset J^{\prime\prime},

\displaystyle 2^{J}(J^{\prime},J^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\iota_{J^{\prime},J^{\prime\prime}}\}\mbox{ if }J^{\prime}\subset J^{\prime\prime}.

We denote by
\mathbf{Simp}^{J}(\mathcal{A}):2^{J}\rightarrow\mathbf{SA}_{\mathrm{R}}
the functor (or the diagram) defined by

\mathbf{Simp}^{J}(\mathcal{A})(J^{\prime})=\mathcal{A}^{J^{\prime}},J^{\prime}\in 2^{J},

and

\mathbf{Simp}^{J}(\mathcal{A})(\iota_{J^{\prime},J^{\prime\prime}})
is the inclusion map
\mathcal{A}^{J^{\prime}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}^{J^{\prime\prime}}
.

3.3.3.
Parametrized algorithm for simplicial replacement

The original (i.e. unparametrized) algorithm takes as input a tuple
of closed formulas,
\Phi=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{M})
, such that the
realizations,
{\mathcal{R}}(\phi_{j})\subset\mathrm{R}^{n},j\in J=[0,M]
are all semi-algebraically contractible, and
m\geq 0
. It produces as output
a simplicial complex
\Delta=\Delta^{J}
, having a subcomplex
\Delta^{J^{\prime}}
for each
J^{\prime}\subset J
, with
\Delta^{J^{\prime}}\subset\Delta^{J^{\prime\prime}}
whenever

J^{\prime}\subset J^{\prime\prime}\subset J
, and such that the diagram of inclusions

(|\Delta^{J^{\prime}}|\hookrightarrow|\Delta^{J^{\prime\prime}}|)_{J^{\prime}\subset J^{\prime\prime}\subset J}

is homologically
m
-equivalent to the diagram of inclusions

({\mathcal{R}}(\Phi^{J^{\prime}})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{R}}(\Phi^{J^{\prime\prime}}))_{J^{\prime}\subset J^{\prime\prime}\subset J}

where for
J^{\prime}\subset J
,
\Phi^{J^{\prime}}=\bigvee_{j\in J^{\prime}}\phi_{j}
.

We will need a

Spontaneous Emission in the presence of Quantum Mirrors
Kanu Sinha, Jennifer Parra-Contreras, Annyun Das, Pablo Solano

Abstract
Arrays of atoms coupled to waveguides can behave as mirrors. We consider an array of Λ \Lambda -type three-level atoms wherein preparing the atoms in one ground state or another leads to reflection or transmission of the guided electromagnetic field; a superposition of the two ground states thus corresponds to a coherent superposition of mirror-like and transparent boundary conditions. We analyze the spontaneous emission of an excited two-level atom in the presence of such a quantum mirror , and inside a cavity formed by quantum mirrors, demonstrating that the resulting dynamics of the excited atom can exhibit exotic features, e.g., a superposition of Rabi cycle and exponential decay. Our results pave the way for exploring quantum electrodynamics (QED) phenomena in a paradigm wherein boundary conditions can exhibit quantum superpositions and correlations.



Introduction
.— The modification of the quantized electromagnetic (EM) field in the presence of boundaries is a ubiquitous aspect of Quantum Electrodynamics (QED), and integral to a wide range of phenomena from cavity QED
Walther et al. 2006
;
Mabuchi and Doherty 2002
, to nanophotonics
Novotny and Hecht 2012
;
D’Amico et al. 2019
, and Casimir physics
Milonni 1994
. While boundary conditions are typically described in terms of the macroscopic polarization induced in a medium in the presence of an applied EM field
Jackson 1999
, their emergence can be more rigorously established from a microscopic treatment of media as a continuous distribution of atomic scatterers
Cook and Milonni 1987
;
Fearn et al. 1996
;
Feng and Ujihara 1990
.

The remarkable progress in experimental control of atomic systems has enabled creation of materials at an atom-by-atom scale
Chang et al. 2018
. For example, arrays of atoms arranged in a Bragg configuration can exhibit strong reflection on an incident EM field. Such atomic Bragg mirrors have been explored theoretically and experimentally with atoms trapped in free-space optical lattices
Deutsch et al. 1995
;
Weidemüller et al. 1995
;
Birkl et al. 1995
;
Schilke et al. 2011
;
Slama et al. 2005
, as well as near waveguides, enabling enhanced light-matter interactions, and thereby a strong and coherent reflection of light
Chang et al. 2011
;
Le Kien and Rauschenbeutel 2014
. Such systems are relevant to implementations of various quantum information processing protocols
Chang et al. 2012
;
González-Tudela et al. 2015
;
Paulisch et al. 2016
;
Asenjo-Garcia et al. 2017
;
González-Tudela et al. 2017
;
Shah et al. 2024
.

As a striking phenomenon, recent experiments with subwavelength Rydberg atom arrays and superconducting circuits have demonstrated coherent switching of the optical response of boundary conditions realized by atomic, or atom-like, mirrors
Srakaew et al. 2023
;
Mirhosseini et al. 2019
. These experiments bring forth the novel prospect of realizing coherent quantum superpositions of two disparate boundary conditions on the EM field. The emergent boundary conditions in the presence of such atomic arrays must account for the fact that the macroscopic polarization induced in the medium can exist in a quantum superposition. The consequent QED phenomena in the presence of such “quantum boundary conditions” are, as yet, largely unexplored.

In this letter we propose and analyze a system with
quantum mirrors
, wherein the emergent optical properties of an atomic array depend on the specific quantum states of its constituent atoms. Preparing the array of atoms in a macroscopic quantum superposition of the two states that correspond to distinct boundary conditions on the EM field, we analyze the spontaneous emission of a two-level atom near such a quantum mirror (QM), and inside a cavity formed by two QMs.

Discussion.
—
We have demonstrated that an ordered array of waveguide-coupled
\Lambda
-type three-level atoms, prepared in a superposition of two ground states, can engender a superposition of two distinct boundary conditions, e.g., mirror-like and transparent, for the guided EM field modes. We examine the effect of such a quantum superposition of boundary conditions on the spontaneous emission of a two-level atom near such a
quantum mirror
or QM, demonstrating that the corresponding atomic decay dynamics is entangled with the state of the QM. We show that making a measurement on the state of the atoms composing the QM can erase the ‘which-boundary-condition’ information, resulting in an interference between the system dynamics from the distinct boundary conditions. We further analyze the spontaneous emission dynamics of a two-level atom inside a cavity formed by two QMs. Preparing the QMs in a superposition of the two ground states, the atomic dynamics occurs in a superposition of having an open waveguide and the atom decaying inside a cavity. For specific arrangements the atoms constituting the QMs can be resonantly excited, thereby precluding an effective static boundary condition description of the system.

Our results are pertinent to ongoing state-of-the-art experiments in waveguide QED
Sheremet et al. 2023
, as well as those with free-space atomic arrays
Srakaew et al. 2023
. As was recently demonstrated in the experiment by Srakaew
et al
, the optical response of a subwavelength 2D array of Rydberg atoms can be switched from mirror-like to transparent via an ancilla atom. Preparing such a system in a superposition can potentially result in a quantum mirror-like boundary condition, which can be tested by measuring the system in a basis that erases the ‘which-state’ information on the ancilla, thereby leading to an interference between the two boundary conditions.

From the perspective of applications in quantum information processing, similar subwavelength 2D arrays of atoms have also been proposed for generation of photonic Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger (GHZ) states
Bekenstein et al. 2020
. Here we start with a GHZ state of atomic arrays coupled to a waveguide and obtain the subsequent dynamics of the system. Future works will explore time-reversed renditions of the present dynamics wherein one can potentially herald an entangled state by starting with an initially unentangled state and making appropriate measurements on the system.

Finally, quantization of EM field in the presence of boundary conditions that can be in a quantum superposition or quantum correlated states, is as yet an open problem. Such a quantization scheme can be studied as an extension of the previous works on microscopic models of boundary conditions
Cook and Milonni 1987
, assuming the induced polarization in a medium to be in a macroscopic quantum superposition. This will open a new direction of exploring QED phenomena in the presence of ‘quantum boundary conditions’.

\displaystyle-i\sum_{k}g_{k}c_{k}(t)e^{ikx_{n}}e^{-i\left(\omega-\omega_{0}\right)t}

(S4)

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{\mathrm{A}}}{{d}t}=

\displaystyle-i\sum_{k}g_{k}c_{k}(t)e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{0})t};\quad\frac{{d}c^{\prime}_{\mathrm{A}}}{{d}t}=-i\sum_{k}g_{k}c^{\prime}_{k}(t)e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{0})t}

(S5)

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{k}}{{d}t}=

\displaystyle-ig_{k}\left[c_{\mathrm{A}}(t)e^{i(\omega-\omega_{0})t}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{\mathrm{QM},n}(t)e^{-ikx_{n}}e^{i(\omega-\omega_{0})t}\right];\quad\frac{{d}c^{\prime}_{k}}{{d}t}=-ig_{k}c^{\prime}_{\mathrm{A}}(t)e^{i(\omega-\omega_{0})t}

(S6)

Substituting the field excitation amplitudes in the atomic equations of motion, yields:

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{\mathrm{QM},n}}{{d}t}\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{2}\left[c_{\mathrm{A}}\left(t\right)e^{i\omega_{0}x_{n}/v}+\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}c_{\mathrm{QM},\nu}\left(t\right)e^{i\omega_{0}\left|x_{n}-x_{\nu}\right|}\right]

(S7)

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{\mathrm{A}}}{{d}t}\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{2}\left[c_{\mathrm{A}}\left(t\right)+\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{\mathrm{QM},n}\left(t\right)e^{i\omega_{0}x_{n}/v}\right]

(S8)

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c^{\prime}_{\mathrm{A}}}{{d}t}\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{2}c_{G^{\prime}}^{e,0}(t),

(S9)

where we have assumed that
g_{k}\approx g_{0}
for the field modes around the atomic resonance, and the spontaneous emission rate of an atom into a waveguide is defined as
\gamma\approx 2\pi\left|g_{0}\right|^{2}
.

Taking Laplace transform of the above equations of motion as
\tilde{c}_{j}(s)\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}tc_{j}(t)e^{-st}
, we get:

\displaystyle\left(s+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)c_{\mathrm{QM},n}(s)+\frac{\gamma}{2}\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A}}\left(s\right)e^{i\omega_{0}x_{n}/v}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{\nu\neq n}\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM},\nu}\left(s\right)e^{i\omega_{0}\left|x_{n}-x_{\nu}\right|/v}=

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{QM},n}(0)

(S10)

\displaystyle\left(s+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A}}(s)+\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM},n}\left(s\right)e^{i\omega_{0}x_{n}/v}=

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{A}}(0)

(S11)

\displaystyle\left(s+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\tilde{c}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{A}}(s)=

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{A}(0).

(S12)

One can define the collective excitation amplitude for the mirror atoms as:

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{QM}}(t)\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{\mathrm{QM},n}(t)(-1)^{n-1},

(S13)

where we have assumed that

\displaystyle x_{n}=x_{1}+(n-1)\lambda_{0}/2.

(S14)

The Laplace equations of motion thus simplify to:

\displaystyle\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM}}(s)+\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A}}\left(s\right)e^{ik_{0}x_{1}}=

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{QM}}(0)

(S15)

\displaystyle\left(s+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A}}(s)+\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM}}\left(s\right)e^{ik_{0}x_{1}}=

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{A}}(0)

(S16)

\displaystyle\left(s+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\tilde{c}^{\prime}_{A}(s)=

\displaystyle c^{\prime}_{A}(0).

(S17)

The equations of motion Eq.(3–4) in the main text correspond to the inverse Laplace transform of the above.

Assuming that the atoms in the QM are initially unexcited (
c_{\mathrm{QM}}(0)=0
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A}}(s)=

\displaystyle\frac{s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}}{\left(s+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)-\frac{N\gamma^{2}}{4}e^{2ik_{0}x_{1}}}c_{\mathrm{A}}(0)

(S18)

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM}}(s)=

\displaystyle-\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}e^{ik_{0}x_{1}}\frac{1}{\left(s+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)-\frac{N\gamma^{2}}{4}e^{2ik_{0}x_{1}}}c_{\mathrm{A}}(0)

(S19)

The poles of the above equation are given by
\gamma_{\pm}\equiv-\frac{\gamma}{2}\left[\frac{N+1}{2}\pm\sqrt{\frac{(N+1)^{2}}{4}+Ne^{2ik_{0}x_{1}}}\right]
. This yields the time dynamics of the two-level atom as:

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{A}}(t)=\frac{\gamma_{+}+\frac{N\gamma}{2}}{\gamma_{+}-\gamma_{-}}e^{\gamma_{+}t}-\frac{\gamma_{-}+\frac{N\gamma}{2}}{\gamma_{+}-\gamma_{-}}e^{\gamma_{-}t}

(S20)

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{QM}}(t)=-\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2\left(\gamma_{+}-\gamma_{-}\right)}e^{ik_{0}x_{1}}\left(e^{\gamma_{+}t}-e^{\gamma_{-}t}\right).

(S21)

I.1
Large
N
limit

For
N\gg 1
, one obtains from Eq. (
S18
):

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A}}(s)\approx\frac{c_{\mathrm{A}}(0)}{s+\frac{\gamma}{2}\left(1-e^{2ik_{0}x_{1}}\right)}\implies c_{\mathrm{A}}(t)\approx e^{-\frac{\gamma t}{2}\left(1+e^{2ik_{0}x_{1}}\right)},

(S22)

which corresponds to the decay of an excited atom in front of a mirror
Cook and Milonni 1987
. From Eq. (
S19
) it can be seen that the collective excitation amplitude for the QM atoms scales as
c_{\mathrm{QM}}\sim 1/\sqrt{N}
, which vanishes in the large
N
limit.

I.2
Field dynamics

The intensity of the field emitted by the atoms as a function of position and time can be evaluated as
I\left(x,t\right)=\frac{\epsilon_{0}c}{2}\left\langle\Psi\left(t\right)|\hat{E}^{\dagger}\left(x,t\right)\hat{E}\left(x,t\right)|\Psi\left(t\right)\right\rangle
, where
\hat{E}\left(x,t\right)=\sum_{k}\,\mathcal{E}_{k}\hat{a}_{k}e^{i(kx-\omega t)}
is the electric field operator at position
x
and time
t
. More explicitly, we obtain

\displaystyle I(x,t)/I_{0}=

\displaystyle\left\langle\Psi(t)\right|\left[\sum_{k_{1}}\hat{a}_{k_{1}}^{\dagger}e^{-i(k_{1}x-\omega_{1}t)}\right]\left[\sum_{k_{2}}\hat{a}_{k_{2}}e^{i(k_{2}x-\omega_{2}t)}\right]\left|\Psi(t)\right\rangle

(S23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}}\sum_{k_{2}}\left[\left\langle G\right|\left\langle g\right|c_{k_{1}}^{\ast}e^{-ik_{1}x}+\left\langle G^{\prime}\right|\left\langle g\right|(c^{\prime}_{k_{1}})^{\ast}e^{-ik_{1}x}\right]\left[c_{k_{2}}e^{ik_{2}x}\left|G\right\rangle\left|g\right\rangle+c^{\prime}_{k_{2}}e^{ik_{2}x}\left|G^{\prime}\right\rangle\left|g\right\rangle\right]e^{i(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})t}

(S24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{k}c_{k}(t)e^{i(kx-\omega t)}\right|^{2}+\left|\sum_{k}c^{\prime}_{k}(t)e^{i(kx-\omega t)}\right|^{2}

(S25)

We substitute the field excitation amplitudes in terms of the atomic excitation amplitudes using Eq.(
S6
) and separating the positive and negative wavevectors
k
explicitly.:

\displaystyle I/I_{0}=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau e^{-i\omega_{0}\tau}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\omega e^{-i\omega(t-\tau)}\left[\left\{c_{\mathrm{A}}\left(\tau\right)+\sum_{n}c_{\mathrm{QM},n}\left(\tau\right)e^{-ikx_{n}}\right\}e^{ikx}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\left\{c_{\mathrm{A}}\left(\tau\right)+\sum_{n}c_{\mathrm{QM},n}\left(\tau\right)e^{ikx_{n}}\right\}e^{-ikx}\right]\right|^{2}+\left|\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{d}\tau e^{-i\omega_{0}\tau}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}\omega e^{-i\omega(t-\tau)}\left[c^{\prime}_{\mathrm{A}}(\tau)e^{ikx}+c^{\prime}_{\mathrm{A}}(\tau)e^{-ikx}\right]\right|^{2}

(S26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\left[e^{i\omega_{0}x/v}c_{\mathrm{A}}\left(t-x/v\right)\left[\Theta\left(t-x/v\right)-\Theta\left(-x/v\right)\right]+e^{-i\omega_{0}x/v}c_{\mathrm{A}}\left(t+x/v\right)\left[\Theta\left(t+x/v\right)-\Theta\left(x/v\right)\right]\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\sum_{n}e^{i\omega_{0}(x-x_{n})/v}c_{\mathrm{QM},n}\left(t-(x-x_{n})/v\right)\left[\Theta\left(t-(x-x_{n})/v\right)-\Theta\left(-(x-x_{n})/v\right)\right]\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\sum_{n}e^{-i\omega_{0}(x-x_{n})/v)}c_{\mathrm{QM},n}\left(t+(x-x_{n})/v\right)\left[\Theta\left(t+(x-x_{n})/v\right)-\Theta\left((x-x_{n})/v\right)\right]\right]\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left|e^{-i\omega_{0}x/v}c^{\prime}_{\mathrm{A}}(t+x/v)\left[\Theta\left(t+x/v\right)-\Theta\left(x/v\right)\right]+e^{i\omega_{0}x/v}c^{\prime}_{A}(t-x/v)\left[\Theta\left(t-x/v\right)-\Theta\left(-x/v\right)\right]\right|^{2}.

(S27)

We note that the total intensity emitted by the atoms is an incoherent sum of that emitted from the QM atoms in
\left|G\right\rangle
and
\left|G^{\prime}\right\rangle
. In the limit where the QM atoms have a separation that is small comparable to the coherence length of the emitted photon
\gamma\left|x_{1}-x_{N}\right|/c\ll 1
, the time delay effects between the different
c_{\mathrm{QM},n}
coefficients can be ignored such that
c_{\mathrm{QM,n}}(t\pm(x-x_{n})/v)\approx c_{\mathrm{QM,n}}(t\pm(x-x_{1})/v)

Sinha et al. 2020a
;
Sinha et al. 2020b
. Thus in the absence of time-delay effects the above expression for the intensity can be simplified to:

\displaystyle I/I_{0}\approx

\displaystyle\left|\left[e^{i\omega_{0}x/v}c_{\mathrm{A}}\left(t-x/v\right)\left[\Theta\left(t-x/v\right)-\Theta\left(-x/v\right)\right]+e^{-i\omega_{0}x/v}c_{\mathrm{A}}\left(t+x/v\right)\left[\Theta\left(t+x/v\right)-\Theta\left(x/v\right)\right]\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+e^{i\omega_{0}(x-x_{1})/v}c_{\mathrm{QM}}\left(t-(x-x_{1})/v\right)\left[\Theta\left(t-(x-x_{1})/v\right)-\Theta\left(-(x-x_{1})/v\right)\right]\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+e^{-i\omega_{0}(x-x_{1})/v}c_{\mathrm{QM}}\left(t+(x-x_{1})/v\right)\left[\Theta\left(t+(x-x_{1})/v\right)-\Theta\left((x-x_{1})/v\right)\right]\right]\right|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left|e^{-i\omega_{0}x/v}c^{\prime}_{\mathrm{A}}(t+x/v)\left[\Theta\left(t+x/v\right)-\Theta\left(x/v\right)\right]+e^{i\omega_{0}x/v}c^{\prime}_{A}(t-x/v)\left[\Theta\left(t-x/v\right)-\Theta\left(-x/v\right)\right]\right|^{2}.

(S28)

II
Quantum Cavity

In this section we consider an atomic array as a mirror. For the sake of simplicity the initial state of the total system is assumed to be:

\displaystyle\left|\Psi(0)\right\rangle=\left|e\right\rangle\otimes\left({c}_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)\left|G_{1}\right\rangle\left|G_{2}\right\rangle+{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG^{\prime}}(0)\left|G_{1}\right\rangle\left|G^{\prime}_{2}\right\rangle+{c}_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G}(0)\left|G^{\prime}_{1}\right\rangle\left|G_{2}\right\rangle+{c}_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G^{\prime}}(0)\left|G^{\prime}_{1}\right\rangle\left|G^{\prime}_{2}\right\rangle\right)\left|\left\{0\right\}\right\rangle

(S29)

where we consider all of the atoms in QM1 and QM2 to be initially in states
\left|G_{1,2}\right\rangle=\left|gg...g\right\rangle_{1,2}
or
\left|G^{\prime}_{1,2}\right\rangle=\left|g^{\prime}g^{\prime}...g^{\prime}\right\rangle_{1,2}
.

The total Hamiltonian for this system is:

\displaystyle H=H_{A}+H_{F}+H_{\mathrm{int}}

(S30)

where

\displaystyle H_{A}=\hbar\omega_{0}\left|e\right\rangle\left\langle e\right|+\sum_{m=-1}^{-N}\hbar\omega_{0}\left|e_{m}\right\rangle\left\langle e_{m}\right|+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\hbar\omega_{0}\left|e_{n}\right\rangle\left\langle e_{n}\right|.

(S31)

Here
m
and
n
are the index for the mirror atoms in QM1 on the left, and QM2 on the right.

The interaction Hamiltonian in the interaction picture becomes

\displaystyle\tilde{H}_{\mathrm{int}}=\sum_{k}\hbar g_{k}\hat{a}_{k}\left(\sum_{m=-1}^{-N}\hat{\sigma}_{m}^{+}e^{ikx_{m}}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{+}e^{ikx_{n}}+\sum_{k}\hat{\sigma}^{+}e^{ikx_{A}}\right)e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{0})t}+\mathrm{H.c.}

(S32)

With the above interaction, the atomic state at any time is given by:

\displaystyle\left|\Psi(t)\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\left[\left\{c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(t)\hat{\sigma}^{+}+\sum_{m=-1}^{-N}c_{e_{m}G}(t)\hat{\sigma}_{m}^{+}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{Ge_{n}}(t)\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{+}+\sum_{k}c_{k,GG}(t)\hat{a}_{k}^{\dagger}\right\}\left|G_{1}\right\rangle\left|G_{2}\right\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left\{c_{\mathrm{A},GG^{\prime}}(t)\hat{\sigma}^{+}+\sum_{m=-1}^{-N}c_{e_{m}G^{\prime}}(t)\hat{\sigma}_{m}^{+}+\sum_{k}c_{k,GG^{\prime}}(t)\hat{a}_{k}^{\dagger}\right\}\left|G_{1}\right\rangle\left|G^{\prime}_{2}\right\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left\{c_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G}(t)\hat{\sigma}^{+}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{G^{\prime}e_{n}}(t)\hat{\sigma}_{n}^{+}+\sum_{k}c_{k,G^{\prime}G}(t)\hat{a}_{k}^{\dagger}\right\}\left|G^{\prime}_{1}\right\rangle\left|G_{2}\right\rangle\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left\{c_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G^{\prime}}(t)\hat{\sigma}^{+}+\sum_{k}c_{k,G^{\prime}G^{\prime}}(t)\hat{a}_{k}^{\dagger}\right\}\left|G^{\prime}_{1}\right\rangle\left|G^{\prime}_{2}\right\rangle\right]\left|g\right\rangle\left|\left\{0\right\}\right\rangle.

(S33)

The equations of motion for the atomic excitation amplitudes are:

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(t)}{{d}t}=

\displaystyle-i\sum_{k}g_{k}c_{k,GG}(t)e^{ikx_{A}}e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{0})t};\quad\frac{{d}c_{\mathrm{A},GG^{\prime}}(t)}{{d}t}=-i\sum_{k}g_{k}c_{k,GG^{\prime}}(t)e^{ikx_{A}}e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{0})t}

(S34)

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G}(t)}{{d}t}=

\displaystyle-i\sum_{k}g_{k}c_{k,G^{\prime}G}(t)e^{ikx_{A}}e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{0})t};\quad\frac{{d}c_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G^{\prime}}(t)}{{d}t}=-i\sum_{k}g_{k}c_{k,G^{\prime}G^{\prime}}(t)e^{ikx_{A}}e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{0})t}

(S35)

Similarly, we have the equations of motion for the excitation amplitudes of the atoms composing the quantum mirrors:

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{e_{m}G}(t)}{{d}t}=

\displaystyle-i\sum_{k}g_{k}c_{k,GG}(t)e^{ikx_{m}}e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{0})t};\quad\frac{{d}c_{e_{m}G^{\prime}}(t)}{{d}t}=-i\sum_{k}g_{k}c_{k,GG^{\prime}}(t)e^{ikx_{m}}e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{0})t}

(S36)

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{Ge_{n}}(t)}{{d}t}=

\displaystyle-i\sum_{k}g_{k}c_{k,GG}(t)e^{ikx_{n}}e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{0})t};\quad\frac{{d}c_{G^{\prime}e_{n}}(t)}{{d}t}=-i\sum_{k}g_{k}c_{k,G^{\prime}G}(t)e^{ikx_{n}}e^{-i(\omega-\omega_{0})t}

(S37)

For the field excitation amplitudes:

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{k,GG}(t)}{{d}t}=

\displaystyle-i\left[g_{k}c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(t)e^{-ikx_{A}}+\sum_{m=-1}^{-N}g_{k}c_{e_{m}G}(t)e^{-ikx_{m}}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}g_{k}c_{Ge_{n}}(t)e^{-ikx_{n}}\right]e^{i(\omega-\omega_{0})t}

(S38)

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{k,GG^{\prime}}(t)}{{d}t}=

\displaystyle-i\left[g_{k}c_{\mathrm{A},GG^{\prime}}(t)e^{-ikx_{A}}+\sum_{m=-1}^{-N}g_{k}c_{e_{m}G^{\prime}}(t)e^{-ikx_{m}}\right]e^{i(\omega-\omega_{0})t}

(S39)

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{k,G^{\prime}G}(t)}{{d}t}=

\displaystyle-i\left[g_{k}c_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G}(t)e^{-ikx_{A}}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}g_{k}c_{G^{\prime}e_{n}}(t)e^{-ikx_{m}}\right]e^{i(\omega-\omega_{0})t}

(S40)

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{k,G^{\prime}G^{\prime}}(t)}{{d}t}=

\displaystyle-ig_{k}c_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G^{\prime}}(t)e^{-ikx_{A}}e^{i(\omega-\omega_{0})t}

(S41)

Substituting the field amplitudes in the atomic equations of motion, and making the Born-Markov approximation one obtains:

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(t)}{{d}t}\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{2}\left[c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(t)+\sum_{m=-1}^{-N}c_{e_{m}G}(t)e^{-i\omega_{1}(x_{m}-x_{A})/v}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{Ge_{n}}(t)e^{i\omega_{2}(x_{n}-x_{A})/v}\right]

(S42)

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{\mathrm{A},GG^{\prime}}(t)}{{d}t}\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{2}\left[c_{\mathrm{A},GG^{\prime}}(t)+\sum_{m=-1}^{-N}c_{e_{m}G^{\prime}}(t)e^{-i\omega_{1}(x_{m}-x_{A})/v}\right]

(S43)

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G}(t)}{{d}t}\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{2}\left[c_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G}(t)+\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{G^{\prime}e_{n}}(t)e^{i\omega_{2}(x_{n}-x_{A})/v}\right]

(S44)

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G^{\prime}}(t)}{{d}t}\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{2}c_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G^{\prime}}(t)

(S45)

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{e_{m}G}(t)}{{d}t}\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{2}\left[\sum_{\mu=-1}^{-N}c_{e_{\mu}G}(t)e^{i\omega_{1}\left|x_{m}-x_{\mu}\right|/v}+c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(t)e^{-i\omega_{A}(x_{m}-x_{A})/v}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{Ge_{n}}(t)e^{i\omega_{2}(x_{n}-x_{m})/v}\right]

(S46)

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{e_{m}G^{\prime}}(t)}{{d}t}\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{2}\left[\sum_{\mu=-1}^{-N}c_{e_{\mu}G^{\prime}}(t)e^{i\omega_{1}\left|x_{m}-x_{\mu}\right|/v}+c_{\mathrm{A},GG^{\prime}}(t)e^{-i\omega_{A}(x_{m}-x_{A})/v}\right]

(S47)

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{Ge_{n}}(t)}{{d}t}\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{2}\left[c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(t)e^{i\omega_{A}(x_{n}-x_{A})/v}+\sum_{m=-1}^{-N}c_{e_{m}G}(t)e^{i\omega_{1}(x_{n}-x_{m})/v}+\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}c_{Ge_{\nu}}(t)e^{i\omega_{2}\left|x_{n}-x_{\nu}\right|/v}\right]

(S48)

\displaystyle\frac{{d}c_{G^{\prime}e_{n}}(t)}{{d}t}\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{2}\left[c_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G}(t)e^{i\omega_{A}(x_{n}-x_{A})/v}+\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}c_{G^{\prime}e_{\nu}}(t)e^{i\omega_{2}\left|x_{n}-x_{\nu}\right|/v}\right].

(S49)

We have assumed the spontaneous emission rate to be
\gamma\approx 2\pi|g\left(\omega_{0}\right)|^{2}
for a sufficiently flat spectral density of the field. We now define the collective excitation amplitudes for the QMs:

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{QM1}}(t)\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{m=1}^{N}c_{e_{m}G}(t)(-1)^{m-1};\quad c^{\prime}_{\mathrm{QM1}}(t)\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{m=1}^{N}c_{e_{m}G^{\prime}}(t)(-1)^{m-1}

(S50)

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{QM2}}(t)\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{Ge_{n}}(t)(-1)^{n-1};\quad c^{\prime}_{\mathrm{QM2}}(t)\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}c_{G^{\prime}e_{n}}(t)(-1)^{n-1}

(S51)

Note that the position of the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
atom in mirror 2 is

\displaystyle x_{n}=

\displaystyle x_{1}+(n-1)\lambda_{0}/2

(S52)

\displaystyle\implies e^{ik_{0}x_{n}}=

\displaystyle e^{ik_{0}x_{1}}e^{i\pi(n-1)}=e^{ik_{0}x_{1}}(-1)^{(n-1)}

(S53)

Similarly, the position of the
m^{\mathrm{th}}
atom in mirror 1 is:

\displaystyle x_{m}=

\displaystyle x_{-1}-(m-1)\lambda_{0}/2

(S54)

\displaystyle\implies e^{-ik_{0}x_{m}}=

\displaystyle e^{-ik_{0}x_{-1}}e^{i\pi(m-1)}=e^{ik_{0}x_{1}}(-1)^{(m-1)},

(S55)

where we have assumed, without loss of generality, that
x_{-1}=-x_{1}
.

Taking the Laplace transform of the atomic equations of motion (Eq.(
S42
)–(
S49
)) in terms of the collective mirror excitation amplitudes, we obtain:

\displaystyle\left(s+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG}(s)+\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}e^{ik_{0}(x_{1}+x_{A})}\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM1}}(s)+\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}e^{ik_{0}(x_{1}-x_{A})}\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM2}}(s)=c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)

(S56)

\displaystyle\left(s+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG^{\prime}}(s)+\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}e^{ik_{0}(x_{1}+x_{A})}\tilde{c}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{QM1}}(s)=c_{\mathrm{A},GG^{\prime}}(0)

(S57)

\displaystyle\left(s+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G}(s)+\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}e^{ik_{0}(x_{1}-x_{A})}\tilde{c}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{QM2}}(s)=c_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G}(0)

(S58)

\displaystyle\left(s+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G^{\prime}}(s)=c_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G^{\prime}}(0)

(S59)

\displaystyle\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM1}}(s)+\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}e^{ik_{0}(x_{1}+x_{A})}\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG}(s)+\frac{N\gamma}{2}e^{2ik_{0}x_{1}}\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM2}}(s)=c_{\mathrm{QM1}}(0)

(S60)

\displaystyle\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)\tilde{c}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{QM1}}(s)+\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}e^{ik_{0}(x_{1}+x_{A})}\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG^{\prime}}(s)=c^{\prime}_{\mathrm{QM1}}(0)

(S61)

\displaystyle\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM2}}(s)+\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}e^{ik_{0}(x_{1}-x_{A})}\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG}(s)+\frac{N\gamma}{2}e^{2ik_{0}x_{1}}\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM1}}(s)=c_{\mathrm{QM2}}(0)

(S62)

\displaystyle\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)\tilde{c}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{QM2}}(s)+\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}e^{ik_{0}(x_{1}-x_{A})}\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G}(s)=c^{\prime}_{\mathrm{QM2}}(0),

(S63)

Each QM acts as an effective collective spin with a cooperative coupling of
\sqrt{N}\gamma
to the waveguide. Eq. (
S59
) can be immediately solved to obtain an exponential decay for
c_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G^{\prime}}(t)=c_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G^{\prime}}(0)e^{-\gamma t/2}
.

The remaining equations can be expressed in matrix notation as:

\displaystyle\bar{\bar{M}}(\tilde{s})\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG}(\tilde{s})\\
\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG^{\prime}}(\tilde{s})\\
\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G}(\tilde{s})\\
\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM1}}(\tilde{s})\\
\tilde{c}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{QM1}}(\tilde{s})\\
\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM2}}(\tilde{s})\\
\tilde{c}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{QM2}}(\tilde{s})\end{array}\right)}_{\equiv\tilde{{\mathbf{C}}}(\tilde{s})}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\underbrace{\left(\begin{array}[]{c}c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)\\
c_{\mathrm{A},GG^{\prime}}(0)\\
c_{\mathrm{A},G^{\prime}G}(0)\\
c_{\mathrm{QM1}}(0)\\
c^{\prime}_{\mathrm{QM1}}(0)\\
c_{\mathrm{QM2}}(0)\\
c^{\prime}_{\mathrm{QM2}}(0)\end{array}\right)}_{\equiv{\mathbf{C}}(0)},

where
\tilde{s}\equiv s/\gamma
and the matrix
\bar{\bar{M}}(\tilde{s})
is defined as:

\displaystyle\bar{\bar{M}}(\tilde{s})=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}\tilde{s}+\frac{1}{2}&0&0&\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2}e^{ik_{0}(x_{1}+x_{A})}&0&\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2}e^{ik_{0}(x_{1}-x_{A})}&0\\
0&\tilde{s}+\frac{1}{2}&0&0&\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}e^{ik_{0}(x_{1}+x_{A})}&0&0\\
0&0&\tilde{s}+\frac{1}{2}&0&0&0&\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2}e^{ik_{0}(x_{1}-x_{A})}\\
\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2}e^{ik_{A}(x_{1}+x_{A})}&0&0&\tilde{s}+\frac{N}{2}&0&\frac{N}{2}e^{2ik_{0}x_{1}}&0\\
0&\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2}e^{ik_{A}(x_{1}+x_{A})}&0&0&\tilde{s}+\frac{N}{2}&0\\
\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2}e^{ik_{A}(x_{1}-x_{A})}&0&0&\frac{N}{2}e^{2ik_{0}x_{1}}&0&\tilde{s}+\frac{N}{2}&0\\
0&0&\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2}e^{ik_{A}(x_{1}-x_{A})}&0&0&0&\tilde{s}+\frac{N}{2}\end{array}\right)

The solution to the atomic dynamics can be thus numerically obtained in terms of the inverse Laplace
{\mathbf{C}}(t)=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{\gamma}\bar{\bar{M}}^{-1}(\tilde{s}){\mathbf{C}}(0)\right]
.

II.1
Large
N
limit

Let us consider the dynamics in the subspace where both QMs are in the ground state
\left|G\right\rangle
(Eqs.(
S56
), (
S60
), (
S62
)). Using the initial conditions
c_{\mathrm{QM1}}(0)=c_{\mathrm{QM2}}(0)=0
in Eq.(
S60
) and (
S62
), yields:

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM1}}(s)=-\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}e^{ik_{0}x_{1}}\frac{\left[e^{ik_{0}x_{A}}\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)-e^{-ik_{0}(x_{A}-2x_{1})}\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right]}{\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{N\gamma}{2}e^{2ik_{0}x_{1}}\right)^{2}}\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG}(s)

(S85)

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM2}}(s)=-\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}e^{ik_{0}x_{1}}\frac{\left[e^{-ik_{0}x_{A}}\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)-e^{ik_{0}(x_{A}+2x_{1})}\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right]}{\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{N\gamma}{2}e^{2ik_{0}x_{1}}\right)^{2}}\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG}(s)

(S86)

Substituting the above in Eq.(
S56
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG}(s)=c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)\left[\frac{\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{N\gamma}{2}e^{2ik_{0}x_{1}}\right)^{2}}{\left(s+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\left\{\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)^{2}-\left(\frac{N\gamma}{2}e^{2ik_{0}x_{1}}\right)^{2}\right\}-\frac{N\gamma^{2}}{2}e^{2ik_{0}x_{1}}\left\{\cos\left(2k_{0}x_{A}\right)\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)-\frac{N\gamma}{2}e^{2ik_{0}x_{1}}\right\}}\right].

(S87)

We now consider the large
N
limit for the QMs for following cases as discussed in the main text:

1.

Atom at cavity node
(x_{A}=0,x_{1}=p\lambda_{0}/2,p\in\mathbb{N})
: One can simplify Eq.(
S87
) in this case as follows

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG}(s)=c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)\left[\frac{{s+N\gamma}}{\left(s+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\left(s+N\gamma\right)-\frac{N\gamma^{2}}{2}}\right],

(S88)

such that in the large
N
limit this can be approximated as:

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG}(s)\approx\frac{1}{s}c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)\implies c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(t)=c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0).

(S89)

Substituting Eq.(
S88
) in Eq.(
S85
) and (
S86
), we get
\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM1}}(s)=\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM2}}(s)=-\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}\frac{1}{s+N\gamma}\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG}(s)
, such that in the large
N
limit both
c_{\mathrm{QM1}}(t),c_{\mathrm{QM2}}(t)\propto 1/\sqrt{N}\rightarrow 0
.

2.

Atom at cavity antinode
(x_{A}=0,x_{1}=p\lambda_{0}/4,p\in\mathbb{N})
: In this case Eq.(
S87
) becomes:

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG}(s)=c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)\frac{s}{s^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}s+\frac{N\gamma^{2}}{2}},

(S90)

which can be approximated in the large
N
limit as:

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG}(s)\approx\frac{c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)}{2}\left[\frac{1}{s+\frac{\gamma}{4}+i\sqrt{\frac{N}{2}}\gamma}+\frac{1}{s+\frac{\gamma}{4}-i\sqrt{\frac{N}{2}}\gamma}\right]

(S91)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(t)\approx c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)e^{-\gamma t/4}\cos\left(\sqrt{\frac{N}{2}}\gamma t\right).

(S92)

Substituting Eq.(
S90
) in Eq.(
S85
) and (
S86
) yields:

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM1}}(s)=\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM2}}(s)=-\frac{i\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2\left(s^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}s+\frac{N\gamma^{2}}{2}\right)}c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)

(S93)

Taking the large
N
limit,

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM1}}(s)=\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM2}}(s)\approx\frac{c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)}{\sqrt{2}}\left[\frac{1}{s+\frac{\gamma}{4}+i\sqrt{\frac{N}{2}}\gamma}-\frac{1}{s+\frac{\gamma}{4}-i\sqrt{\frac{N}{2}}\gamma}\right]

(S94)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{QM1}}(t)=c_{\mathrm{QM2}}(t)\approx\sqrt{2}ic_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)e^{-\gamma t/4}\sin\left(\sqrt{\frac{N}{2}}\gamma t\right)

(S95)

3.

Atom near cavity node
(0<k_{0}x_{A}\ll 1,x_{1}=p\lambda_{0}/2,p\in\mathbb{N})
: We define
\phi_{A}\equiv k_{0}x_{A}
, such that
\cos\left(2k_{0}x_{A}\right)\approx 1-2\phi_{A}^{2}
, thus Eq.(
S87
) becomes:

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG}(s)\approx

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)\left[\frac{s\left(s+N\gamma\right)}{s\left(s+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\left(s+N\gamma\right)-\frac{N\gamma^{2}}{2}\left\{s-2\phi_{A}^{2}\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)\right\}}\right]

(S96)

Taking the large
N
limit:

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{A},GG}(s)\approx

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)\left[\frac{s}{s^{2}+\phi_{A}^{2}\frac{N\gamma^{2}}{2}}\right]\implies c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(t)\approx c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)\cos\left(\phi_{A}\sqrt{\frac{N}{2}}\gamma t\right)

(S97)

To obtain the dynamics of the atoms constituting the QMs, we substitute Eq.(
S96
) in Eqs.(
S85
) and (
S86
):

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM1}}(s)\approx-\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}\frac{e^{ik_{0}x_{A}}\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)-e^{-ik_{0}x_{A}}\frac{N\gamma}{2}}{s\left(s+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\left(s+N\gamma\right)-\frac{N\gamma^{2}}{2}\left\{s-2\phi_{A}^{2}\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)\right\}}c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)

(S98)

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM2}}(s)\approx-\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}\frac{e^{-ik_{0}x_{A}}\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)-e^{ik_{0}x_{A}}\frac{N\gamma}{2}}{s\left(s+\frac{\gamma}{2}\right)\left(s+N\gamma\right)-\frac{N\gamma^{2}}{2}\left\{s-2\phi_{A}^{2}\left(s+\frac{N\gamma}{2}\right)\right\}}c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)

(S99)

Taking the large
N
limit of the above, we get:

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM1}}(s)\approx-\tilde{c}_{\mathrm{QM2}}(s)\approx-\frac{\sqrt{N}\gamma}{2}\frac{i\phi_{A}}{s^{2}+\frac{N\gamma^{2}}{2}\phi_{A}^{2}}c_{\mathrm{A},GG}(0)

(S100)

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{QM1}}(t)\approx-c_{\mathrm{QM2}}(t)\approx-\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\sin\left(\phi_{A}\sqrt{\frac{N}{2}}\gamma t\right).

(S101)

The geodesic cover problem for butterfly networks
Paul Manuel, Sandi Klavžar, R. Prabha, Andrew Arokiaraj

Abstract
A geodesic cover, also known as an isometric path cover, of a graph is a set of geodesics which cover the vertex set of the graph. An edge geodesic cover of a graph is a set of geodesics which cover the edge set of the graph. The geodesic (edge) cover number of a graph is the cardinality of a minimum (edge) geodesic cover. The (edge) geodesic cover problem of a graph is to find the (edge) geodesic cover number of the graph. Surprisingly, only partial solutions for these problems are available for most situations. In this paper we demonstrate that the geodesic cover number of the r r -dimensional butterfly is ⌈ ( 2 / 3 ) ​ 2 r ⌉ \lceil(2/3)2^{r}\rceil and that its edge geodesic cover number is 2 r 2^{r} .

Introduction

Let

A

of a graph

[
4
,
5
,
8
,
9
,
12
]

[
8
]
.
Throughout this paper,

denotes the set of all geodesics of

denotes the set of all maximal (with respect to inclusion) geodesics of

.

Given

and

of the triple

Given

and
S\subseteq V(G)

When

and

,

{\rm gcover}(Y,G)
.

When

and

{\rm gcover}(S,G)

When

and

.

Given

and

, the
geodesic cover problem
of

{\rm gcover}(Y,S,G)
of

. The
geodesic cover problem
of

of
G

An

of a graph is a set of geodesics which cover the edge set of the graph.
The

The
edge geodesic cover problem
of a graph

The geodesic cover problem is known to be NP-complete

14

. Apollonio et al.

The geodesic cover number of the

[

]
. On the other hand, the complete solution of the geodesic cover problem for the two-dimensional grid is still unknown, cf.
[
9
]
. There is no literature for the geodesic cover problem on multi-dimensional grids.

The geodesic cover problems for cylinder and

-dimensional grids are discussed in

]
. In particular, the isometric path cover number of the

-dimensional torus is

[

Preliminaries

The results discussed in this section will be used as tools to prove the key results of this paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
G
is a connected graph, then the following hold.

(i)

If
S^{\prime}\subseteq S^{\prime\prime}\subseteq V(G)
and
Y\subseteq Z(G)
, then
{\rm gcover}(Y,S^{\prime\prime},G)

\geq

{\rm gcover}(Y,S^{\prime},G)
.

(ii)

If
Y^{\prime}\subseteq Y^{\prime\prime}\subseteq Z(G)
and
S\subseteq V(G)
, then
{\rm gcover}(Y^{\prime\prime},S,G)\leq{\rm gcover}(Y^{\prime},S,G)
.

(iii)

{\rm gcover}(G)

=

{\rm gcover}(M(G),G)
.

Proof.

Assertions (i) and (ii) are straightforward, hence we consider only (iii). Since
M(G)\subseteq Z(G)
, (ii) implies
{\rm gcover}(G)={\rm gcover}(Z(G),V,G)\leq{\rm gcover}(M(G),V,G)={\rm gcover}(M(G),G)
. Since each geodesic is a subpath of some maximal geodesic, for each geodesic cover
S
of
Z(G)
, there exists a geodesic cover
S^{\prime}
of
M(G)
such that
|S|=|S^{\prime}|
. Therefore,
{\rm gcover}(Z(G),V,G)\geq{\rm gcover}(M(G),V,G)
and consecutively
{\rm gcover}(G)={\rm gcover}(Z(G),V,G)\geq{\rm gcover}(M(G),V,G)={\rm gcover}(M(G),G)
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

If
K_{r,r}
,
r\geq 2
, is a complete bipartite graph, then
{\rm gcover}(K_{r,r})=\lceil(2/3)r\rceil
.

Proof.

Clearly, each maximal geodesic of
K_{r,r}
is a (diametral) path of length
2
. Therefore,
{\rm gcover}(K_{r,r})\geq\lceil(2/3)r\rceil
. On the other hand, it is a simple exercise to construct a geodesic cover of cardinality
\lceil(2/3)r\rceil
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

A geodesic of
{\rm BF}(r)
contains at most two vertices of level
0
and at most two vertices of level
r
. Moreover, if a geodesic contains two vertices of level
0
, then they are the ends of the geodesic (and similarly for level r).

Proof.

Let us assume that there exists a geodesic
P
which contains more than two vertices of level
0
, say
v_{i},v_{j}
, and
v_{k}
. See Fig.
2
(b). Then one of these three vertices must be an internal vertex of
P
, say
v_{j}
. The deletion of the vertices at level

from
{\rm BF}(r)
disconnects
{\rm BF}(r)
into two vertex disjoint components

and
G_{2}
, where both

and

are isomorphic to
{\rm BF}(r-1)
, cf.
[
7
,
13
,
16
]
. Since

is an internal vertex of
P
and of degree
2
, its neighbors
v_{j-1}
and
v_{j+1}
also lie in

. Moreover, one of the adjacent vertices
v_{j-1}
,
v_{j+1}
lie in

and the other lie in
G_{2}
. Also,

has two adjacent vertices, say

and

. Since

and

are isomorphic, the

-geodesic and the

-geodesic are isomorphic, which in turn implies that

∎

As the butterfly network is symmetrical with respect to level

, cf.
[

,
13
,
16
]
. Using the logic of the proof of Lemma

Corollary 2.4
.

If both end vertices of a geodesic

are either at level

, then

is maximal.

Lemma 2.5
.

A geodesic of
{\rm BF}(r)
covers at most three vertices of degree

.

Proof.

Suppose a geodesic

are at level

or at level

, three of these vertices can not be at the same level. Assume without loss of generality that

and

are at level

and

and

are at level

∎

Corollary 2.6

If a geodesic

of
{\rm BF}(r)
covers three vertices of degree

5
Conclusion

The geodesic cover problem is one of the fundamental problems in graph theory, but only partial solutions are available for most situations. The geodesic cover number in both vertex and edge version was unknown for butterfly networks, in this paper we provide a complete solutions for both versions.

Even though the geodesic cover and geodesic partition are frequently used in fixed interconnection networks, the exact values of geodesic cover number and geodesic partition
number are unknown for popular architectures such as shuffle-exchange, de Bruijn, Kautz, star, pancake, circulant, wrapped butterfly, CCC networks. The geodesic cover problem and the geodesic partition problem (their edge versions) are wide open for researcher.

Chiral Decoupling from Irrelevant Deformations Preprint: TIFR/TH/20-2
Subhroneel Chakrabarti, and Madhusudhan Raman

Abstract
We show that at the level of the equations of motion (i.e. classically), in the limit in which the T ​ T ¯ \text{T}\overline{\text{T}} deformation parameter is sent to infinity, the left- and right-chiral sectors of T ​ T ¯ \text{T}\overline{\text{T}} -deformed free theories decouple.



The last few years have witnessed a surge of interest in a class of soluble deformations of two-dimensional quantum field theories, among them the so-called
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
deformation
Zamolodchikov:2004ce
. This irrelevant deformation is triggered by the composite operator

\mathrm{T}\overline{\mathrm{T}}=-\operatorname{det}T_{\mu\nu}\ .

(1)

It is a curious feature of this deformation that a number of physically interesting quantities such as the deformed classical Lagrangian
Cavaglia:2016oda
;
Bonelli:2018kik
;
Conti:2018jho
, the finite volume spectrum
Smirnov:2016lqw
, the S-matrix
Dubovsky:2012wk
;
Dubovsky:2013ira
;
Dubovsky:2017cnj
;
Rosenhaus:2019utc
, and the torus partition function
Cardy:2018sdv
;
Datta:2018thy
;
Aharony:2018bad
are determined in terms of the data of the undeformed theory.
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
deformations have also been studied from the point of view of holography
McGough:2016lol
;
Kraus:2018xrn
;
Taylor:2018xcy
;
Hartman:2018tkw
;
Caputa:2019pam
, little string theories
Giveon:2017nie
;
Giveon:2017myj
;
Asrat:2017tzd
;
Giribet:2017imm
;
Chakraborty:2018kpr
;
Chakraborty:2018aji
, and in the context of supersymmetric quantum field theories
Baggio:2018rpv
;
Chang:2018dge
;
Jiang:2019hux
;
Chang:2019kiu
;
Ferko:2019oyv
;
Coleman:2019dvf
. For a pedagogical introduction to
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
deformations, we invite the reader to consult
Jiang:2019hxb
and references therein.

In this short note, we focus on
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
deformations of free quantum field theories.

Bosons

We start with a free boson in two dimensional Euclidean space.
1
1

1

We adopt the conventions of
DiFrancesco:1997nk
.
It is described by the Lagrangian

L_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\partial\phi\bar{\partial}\phi\ .

(2)

Consider
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
deformations of this Lagrangian, first discussed in
Cavaglia:2016oda
. A well-known result in the literature shows that it is possible to resum these deformations to all orders in the
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
coupling constant
\lambda
to get the non-local Lagrangian

L_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(-1+\sqrt{1+\lambda\,\partial\phi\bar{\partial}\phi}\right)\ .

(3)

The above Lagrangian can be interpreted as describing the dynamics of a Nambu-Goto string in a three-dimensional target space

\displaystyle L_{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\lambda}\sqrt{\operatorname{det}\left(\sum_{\mu=1}^{3}\partial_{\alpha}X^{\mu}\partial_{\beta}X^{\mu}\right)}\ ,

(4)

in the so-called static gauge, i.e. when one makes the identifications

X^{1}\rightarrow x^{0}\ ,\quad X^{2}\rightarrow x^{1}\ ,\quad X^{3}\rightarrow\sqrt{\lambda}\,\phi\ .

(5)

Here,
x^{0}
and
x^{1}
are Euclidean worldsheet coordinates, while the boson
\phi
makes up the (single) transverse oscillator.

Given the tantalising square-root form of the above Lagrangian, it is natural to wonder if these deformations are in any way related to the physics of D-branes
Chang:2018dge
;
Brennan:2019azg
. Indeed, the Lagrangian (
3
), after an analytic continuation to Minkowski spacetime, can be written in a more suggestive form as

L_{\mathrm{D}}=-\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(-1+\sqrt{-\operatorname{det}\left(\eta_{\mu\nu}+\lambda\,\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi\right)}\right)\ .

(6)

As is well-known, a
p
-brane embedded in
(D+1)
-dimensional Minkowski space will break translation invariance along directions orthogonal to it. The dynamics of Nambu-Goldstone modes corresponding to the spontaneously broken translation invariance are governed by the Dirac Lagrangian. For
p=1
, i.e. for a string, the Lagrangian (
6
) is the Dirac Lagrangian, with the tension
T
of the string is related to
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
coupling
\lambda
as

T\longleftrightarrow\frac{1}{\lambda}\ .

(7)

To summarise, the
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
-deformed free boson Lagrangian, upon analytic continuation, is just the Dirac Lagrangian for a string with tension inversely proportional to the
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
coupling
\lambda
.
2
2

2

The relationship between Nambu-Goto strings and
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
deformations has also been studied in
Beratto:2019bap
.

In this article we call to attention a curious feature of the above deformation in the infinite coupling limit (i.e.
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
) of a
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
deformation. In particular, we consider the
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
limit
after
the
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
-deformed free boson Lagrangian (
3
) has been analytically continued to Minkowski spacetime. To the best of our knowledge, such a limit has remained unexplored in the literature. The tensionless limit of the Dirac Lagrangian, however, has been recently studied by
Townsend:2019koy
, and we draw on some of the results of these investigations, bringing them to bear upon the study of
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
deformations via the aforementioned correspondence.

The Hamiltonian corresponding to the Dirac Lagrangian is

H_{\text{D}}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(-1+\sqrt{1+\lambda\left[\pi^{2}+\left(\phi^{\prime}\right)^{2}\right]+\lambda^{2}\left[\phi^{\prime}\pi\right]^{2}}\right)\ ,

(8)

where
\pi
is the canonical momentum conjugate to
\phi
.
3
3

3

While this note was being completed, we came across
Jorjadze:2020ili
, where
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
deformations are also considered within the Hamiltonian framework.
In the above equations, the dots are time derivatives and the primes are spatial derivatives, as usual. While the zero-coupling limit of (
8
) returns the Hamiltonian for a free boson, the infinite-coupling limit yields

\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}H_{\text{D}}=\left|\phi^{\prime}\pi\right|\ ,

(9)

and so the equations of motion in the infinite-coupling limit depend on the sign of
\pi\phi^{\prime}
. We argue that both choices of sign correspond to co-existent sectors of the theory. The equations of motion for each of these sector can be written down provided we impose the constraint
\pi=\pm\phi^{\prime}
, only to be imposed at the level of equations of motion. It is easy, then, to verify using the equations of motion that this choice of sign
\pi=\pm\phi^{\prime}
implies in turn a choice of chirality.

We conclude from this that the classical Hamiltonian breaks up into two sectors describing left-chiral and right-chiral bosons. In other words, the infinite-coupling limit effects a decoupling of the chiral halves of the boson. It is impossible to simultaneously write down the equations of motion for both sectors; nontheless, the two sectors are straightforwardly related by a parity transformation.

Instead of describing the chiral bosons using their equations of motions, we may wish to make the chirality of a specific sector manifest at the level of the Hamiltonian. For example, we find that with (say) the choice
\pi=+\phi^{\prime}
, we have

H_{\text{D}}=\left(\phi^{\prime}\right)^{2}\ ,

(10)

and the resulting Lagrangian in configuration space is

L_{\text{FJ}}=\phi^{\prime}\left(\dot{\phi}-\phi^{\prime}\right)\ .

(11)

The Lagrangian (
11
) is the well-known Floreanini-Jackiw Lagrangian for a chiral boson
Floreanini:1987as
. Therefore, we conclude that at the level of classical Lagrangians, the infinite coupling limit of a
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
-deformed free boson effects a decoupling of the left-chiral and right-chiral halves of the bosons.

At this stage, it may be tempting to conclude that the infinite coupling limit halves the number of degrees of freedom and leaves us with a chiral boson. If this were true, only one of the two possible signs of
\pi\phi^{\prime}
is physical, not both. This conclusion is incorrect. The infinite coupling limit effectively maps the original (free) theory to a theory in which both chiralities which are decoupled. There is no reason to privilege one choice of the sign over the other. Additionally, there is nothing to suggest that on taking the infinite coupling limit, a parity-invariant theory should go into a parity-violating theory. Finally, this conclusion is consistent with expectations from considerations pertaining to the gravitational anomaly. The theory (
3
), when coupled to a metric, is anomaly free. If and only if the infinite coupling limit returns both left- and right-chiral sectors will the final theory, on coupling to a metric, be free of gravitational anomalies.
4
4

4

We couple to the metric only
after
performing
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
deformations and taking the infinite coupling limit.
Finally, since the infinite-coupling limit does not seem to affect any considerations pertaining to gravitational anomaly, it is natural to expect that both the initial and the final theories, on coupling to a metric, yield a theory devoid of gravitational anomalies.

It is well known that equal numbers of left- and right-chiral bosons cannot always be thought of as describing ordinary bosons. The observation central to this note provides a novel (but not unique) example contrary to this common expectation. This is guaranteed because the
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
deformation is an invertible deformation. In principle, given a pair of left- and right-chiral bosons, we can always reverse the steps taken in this note, i.e. restore the coupling and run the
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
deformation machinery in reverse to obtain a theory of ordinary free bosons. Other instances in which a scalar is known to undergo chiral decoupling are when the scalar is either compact or twisted. It seems that the chiral decoupling presented in this article is unrelated to either of those known instances of chiral decoupling.

Often in the literature the
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
deformation is thought of as a “reversal of the renormalization group flow.” This is true in the sense that the theory (
3
), when thought of as an UV theory, under the renormalization group flow ends up in an IR fixed point given by the free boson (
2
). If one thinks of the infinite-coupling limit as a part of the flow, albeit towards the UV starting from (
6
), then one observes the following curious feature: a local theory of chirally decoupled bosons first flows down to a
non-local
interacting theory of a regular boson, and then flows down to a local theory of regular boson interacting via derivative interactions, to eventually a local theory of free bosons in the IR.
5
5

5

We acknowledge helpful correspondence with Shouvik Datta regarding this point.

Fermions

Analogously, one may study the
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
-deformed free fermion in the limit of infinite coupling. We start out in Euclidean space, as before. The
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
-deformed Lagrangian for a free fermion is
6
6

6

This Lagrangian has been shown in
Cribiori:2019xzp
to be equivalent to the
N=(2,2)
Volkov-Akulov Lagrangian.

L_{\lambda}=\psi_{-}^{\dagger}\partial\psi_{-}+\psi_{+}^{\dagger}\bar{\partial}\psi_{+}-\lambda\left[\left(\psi_{-}^{\dagger}\partial\psi_{-}\right)\left(\psi_{+}^{\dagger}\bar{\partial}\psi_{+}\right)-\left(\psi_{-}^{\dagger}\bar{\partial}\psi_{-}\right)\left(\psi_{+}^{\dagger}\partial\psi_{+}\right)\right]\ .

(12)

After analytic continuation, we compute the Hamiltonian in the usual way, via a Legendre transform. The result is

H_{\lambda}=-\frac{1}{2\lambda}\left(-1+\sqrt{1+4\lambda\left[\Pi_{-}\psi_{-}^{\prime}-\Pi_{+}\psi_{+}^{\prime}\right]+4\lambda^{2}\left[\Pi_{-}\psi_{-}^{\prime}+\Pi_{+}\psi_{+}^{\prime}\right]^{2}}\right)\ ,

(13)

where primes denote spatial derivatives, and
\Pi_{\pm}
are the canonical momenta conjugate to
\psi_{\pm}
. In the limit of vanishing
\lambda
, this returns

H_{0}=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}H_{\lambda}=-\Pi_{-}\psi_{-}^{\prime}+\Pi_{+}\psi_{+}^{\prime}\ ,

(14)

as one would expect. At large
\lambda
, however, one gets

H_{\infty}=\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow\infty}H_{\lambda}=-\left|\Pi_{-}\psi_{-}^{\prime}+\Pi_{+}\psi_{+}^{\prime}\right|\ ,

(15)

and the corresponding first-order action is

S=\int\text{d}^{2}\sigma\left[\Pi_{+}\dot{\psi}_{+}+\Pi_{-}\dot{\psi}_{-}-H_{\infty}\right]\ .

(16)

At this stage itself, we can conclude that the infinite-coupling limit yields decoupled chiral fermions. Let us be a little more explicit, for clarity.

In a form structurally reminiscent of the bosons, the equations of motion in the infinite-coupling limit depend on a sign, in this case of
\left(\Pi_{-}\psi_{-}^{\prime}+\Pi_{+}\psi_{+}^{\prime}\right)
. There are, once again, two sectors corresponding to two possible choices of sign, and they are described by the following actions: for the
(+)
sign, we have

\displaystyle H_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\left(\Pi_{-}\psi_{-}^{\prime}+\Pi_{+}\psi_{+}^{\prime}\right)\ ,

\displaystyle S_{\text{R}}

\displaystyle=\int\text{d}^{2}\sigma\left[\Pi_{+}\partial_{-}\psi_{+}+\Pi_{-}\partial_{-}\psi_{-}\right]\ ,

(17)

and for the
(-)
sign, we have

\displaystyle H_{\infty}

\displaystyle=-\left(\Pi_{-}\psi_{-}^{\prime}+\Pi_{+}\psi_{+}^{\prime}\right)\,,

\displaystyle S_{\text{L}}

\displaystyle=\int\text{d}^{2}\sigma\left[\Pi_{+}\partial_{+}\psi_{+}+\Pi_{-}\partial_{+}\psi_{-}\right]\,.

(18)

From the actions (
17
) and (
18
) we conclude that in either sector, the two chiralities are decoupled.

In close analogy with the bosons, we may choose to impose constraints on the equations of motions that resolve the sign ambiguity introduced by the absolute value. A suitable pair of constraints is

\displaystyle\textsc{Right Sector:}\quad\Pi_{-}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{and}\quad\Pi_{+}\psi_{+}^{{}^{\prime}}>0\,\,,

(19)

\displaystyle\textsc{Left Sector:}\quad\Pi_{+}

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{and}\quad\Pi_{-}\psi_{-}^{{}^{\prime}}>0\,\,.

(20)

The choice of setting the non-zero term in
H_{\infty}
to be
less
than zero forces us to conclude that
\psi_{-}
propagates in the
(+)
direction, and likewise for
\psi_{+}
and the
(-)
direction. We reject these branches on the basis of a definite convention: the chiral fermion
\psi_{+}
(respectively,
\psi_{-}
) propagates along the
(+)
(respectively,
(-)
) direction.

It is once again straightforward to see that the constraints (
19
) and (
20
) imposed on the equations of motion obtained from the actions (
17
) and (
18
) respectively are nothing but chirality constraints, describing the classical dynamics of chiral fermions. Once again the two sectors are simply related to each other by a parity transformation.

Further Comments

It is interesting to compare and contrast our findings with what is already known about the
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
deformation. One of the beautiful aspects of these deformations is the ability to determine the exact finite-volume spectrum of the deformed theory. Consider a quantum field theory wrapped around a cylinder of circumference
R
, whose undeformed energies and quantised momenta are
E_{n}
and
P_{n}
respectively. The energy spectrum is deformed by the
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
deformation, while the momenta remain unchanged. The deformed spectrum
E^{\lambda}_{n}
is

E^{\lambda}_{n}=\frac{R}{\lambda}\left(-1+\sqrt{1+\frac{\lambda\,E_{n}}{R}+\frac{\lambda^{2}\,P_{n}^{2}}{R^{2}}}\right)\ .

(21)

On considering the above result in the limit
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
, we find

E^{\infty}_{n}=\left|P_{n}\right|\ ,

(22)

which is the dispersion relation of a classical
chiral
field. This moves us to conclude that the chiral decoupling we have discussed in this paper may be generic—in this regard, free field theories are only the simplest and most transparent examples of this decoupling. Further, since the deformed spectrum is quantum mechanically exact, it is likely that chiral decoupling at infinite
\lambda
is a generic feature of
\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}
-deformed quantum field theories.

Another attractive feature of these irrelevant deformations are that the exact S-matrix of the deformed theory is arrived at by multiplying the S-matrix of the undeformed theory by a phase. For the scattering of massless particles
(i,j)\rightarrow(k,l)
with relative rapidity
\theta=\theta_{i}-\theta_{j}
, the S-matrix changes as

S_{ij}^{kl}(\theta)\overset{\text{T}\overline{\text{T}}}{\longrightarrow}S_{ij}^{kl}(\theta)\,e^{i\delta_{ij}^{(\lambda)}(\theta)}\ ,

(23)

where the phase is referred to as the CDD factor, and takes the form

\delta_{ij}^{(\lambda)}\left(\theta\right)=-2\lambda\,p_{i}^{(+)}p_{j}^{(-)}\ ,

(24)

where
p_{i}^{(+)}
and
p_{j}^{(-)}
are the momenta of the left- and right-moving particles. Implicit in the derivation of the CDD factor is the assumption that
\lambda
is finite. Since the coupling
\lambda
only appears in the phase, rigorously taking the limit
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
(with other parameters held fixed) of the CDD factor is at best ambiguous. One possible resolution to this ambiguity would be to start with the classical theory at infinite coupling, following the analysis presented in this paper, and then compute the S-matrix. It would be interesting to better understand how the infinite coupling limit should be interpreted in the context of scattering.

Finally, irrespective of the imposition of the constraints, the chiral decoupling of fermions in the infinite-coupling limit represents a curious development. The original action (
12
) appears to be an interacting theory with coupling constant
\lambda
. In the limit where the coupling constant goes to infinity — when the interaction term is dominant — we find that the theory breaks up into two sectors, each describing a chiral fermion.

Indeed, the same is true of the bosonic case as well: the theory is interacting at finite, non-zero values of coupling and when the coupling constant goes to infinity, the theory breaks up into two sectors, each describing a chiral boson.

How might one explain this development? Since our analyses in this note are purely based on equations of motion, it would be interesting to study the corresponding quantum field theories, perhaps along the lines of
Rosenhaus:2019utc
;
Callebaut:2019omt
. We hope to return to these questions in the near future.

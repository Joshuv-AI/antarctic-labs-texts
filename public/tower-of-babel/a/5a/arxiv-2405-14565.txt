A scalar conservation law is a quasilinear partial differential equation of the form

It is well known that, in general, global (in time) differentiable solutions for the scalar conservation law do not exist. Consequently, the concept of weak solutions (or integral solutions) is introduced. Weak solutions are obtained by multiplying the scalar conservation law by a test function and formally

Remarks about the assumptions on the flux

In this section we give some remarks about the mentioned above Assumption
1

Remark 2.1
.

(Uniform differentiability almost everywhere)

Assume
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d},f=f(x,k),
is a measurable function. Let us distinguish between the following two assertions:

1.

For every
k\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists
\Theta_{k}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\mathcal{L}^{d}(\Theta_{k})=0
, and for every
k\in\mathbb{R}
, the function
x\mapsto f(x,k)
is differentiable at every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Theta_{k}
;

2.

Uniform differentiability almost everywhere
: there exists
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\mathcal{L}^{d}(\Theta)=0
, and for every
k\in\mathbb{R}
, the function
x\mapsto f(x,k)
is differentiable at every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Theta
.

The second assertion implies the first assertion, but not the opposite. In case
f
is a Lipschitz function, Rademacher’s Theorem (see Theorem
3.1
) tells us that for every
k\in\mathbb{R}
, the function
f(\cdot,k):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is differentiable almost everywhere, and we get the first assertion. However, it does not imply uniform differentiability almost everywhere. Notice that item 2a in Assumption
1
requires that the flux
f
has the property of uniform differentiability almost everywhere.

Remark 2.2
.

(Continuous differentiability of the flux
f
implies property (
1.1
))

In this remark, we want to show that if the flux

as in Assumption
1
is continuously differentiable, then the property (
1.1
) holds.

It is enough to prove it for scalar functions
f
. Let

be a continuously differentiable function. Then, for

and a compact set

,

is uniformly continuous on
\overline{B}_{1}(x)\times K
, so for arbitrary
\xi\in(0,\infty)
there exists

such that, for every

|D_{x}f(x_{1},k_{1})-D_{x}f(x_{2},k_{2})|\leq\xi

\lim_{y\to x}\left(\sup_{k\in K}\frac{|f(y,k)-f(x,k)-D_{x}f(x,k)(y-x)|}{|y-x|}\right)=0.

Remark 2.3

(Comparison with an alternative setting of conditions)

In

(Differentiation under the sign of the integral for Lipschitz functions)

4
Entropy solutions for scalar conservation laws with Lipschitz continuous flux

In this section, we introduce the main concepts of this work: scalar conservation laws and the notion of entropy solutions for such laws.

For every
k\in\mathbb{R}
, we assume the existence of a function
f(\cdot,k):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
f=f(x,k)
. Let
u_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function. Denote
I:=(0,\infty)
.
Consider the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u\left(x,t\right)+\diver f\left(x,u\left(x,t\right)\right)=0&,(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I\\
u\left(x,0\right)=u_{0}\left(x\right)&,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{cases}.

(4.1)

Here,
u:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I\to\mathbb{R}
is the unknown function. The function
u_{0}
represents the initial data. We refer to the equation
\partial_{t}u(x,t)+\text{div}\,f(x,u(x,t))=0
as a
scalar conservation law
, and we call the function
f
the
flux
of the law. The divergence operator
\diver
is taken with respect to the spatial variable
x
.

By Proposition
3.4
we can define a well-defined notion of an "entropy flux" as follows:

Definition 4.1
.

(Entropy pair)

Let
f
be a flux as in Assumption
1
. For
C^{2}
convex function
\eta:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

and
k_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
let us define a
function

q\left(x,k\right):=\intop_{k_{0}}^{k}\eta^{\prime}\left(\omega\right)\partial_{\omega}f\left(x,\omega\right)d\omega,\quad q:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(4.2)

We refer to the function
\eta
as the
entropy
and the function
q
as the
entropy flux
. The combination
\left(\eta,q\right)
is termed an
entropy pair
.

Remark 4.2
.

(Uniqueness of derivatives of the entropy flux)
For a given
\eta
as in Definition
4.1
, an entropy flux
q
for which
(\eta,q)
is an entropy pair is not unique, in general. Different choices of the number
k_{0}
result in different entropy fluxes. More precisely, for
k_{0},k_{1}
, we have

\intop_{k_{0}}^{k}\eta^{\prime}\left(\omega\right)\partial_{\omega}f\left(x,\omega\right)d\omega=\intop_{k_{0}}^{k_{1}}\eta^{\prime}\left(\omega\right)\partial_{\omega}f\left(x,\omega\right)d\omega+\intop_{k_{1}}^{k}\eta^{\prime}\left(\omega\right)\partial_{\omega}f\left(x,\omega\right)d\omega.

(4.3)

However, by item 1 of Proposition
3.4
and Rademacher’s Theorem we get for almost every

(x,k)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}

\partial_{k}\intop_{k_{0}}^{k}\eta^{\prime}\left(\omega\right)\partial_{\omega}f\left(x,\omega\right)d\omega=\partial_{k}\intop_{k_{1}}^{k}\eta^{\prime}\left(\omega\right)\partial_{\omega}f\left(x,\omega\right)d\omega.

(4.4)

Which means that two different entropy fluxes for a given entropy
\eta
have the same derivative with respect to the variable
k
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
.

We now provide the definition of entropy solutions. Some notation involved in this definition will be explained in Remark
4.4
.

Definition 4.3
.

(Entropy solution)
For
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
we say that

, where
I=(0,\infty)
is the time interval,

for
(
4.1
)

if and only if the following two conditions hold:

1.

For every entropy pair
\left(\eta,q\right)
and test function

0\leq\varphi\in\operatorname{Lip}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I\right)
3
3

3

The space

with compact

it follows that

\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg[\left(\partial_{t}\varphi\left(x,t\right)\right)\eta\left(u\left(x,t\right)\right)+\varphi\left(x,t\right)\bigg(\diver_{x}q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right)\right)-\eta^{\prime}\left(u\left(x,t\right)\right)\diver_{x}f\left(x,u\left(x,t\right)\right)\bigg)\\
+\left(\nabla_{x}\varphi\left(x,t\right)\right)\cdot q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right)\right)\bigg]dxdt\geq 0.

\partial_{t}u=-\diver f(x,u)=-\diver_{x}f\left(x,u\right)-\partial_{k}f(x,k)|_{k=u(x,t)}\cdot\nabla_{x}u,

\partial_{k}q(x,k)|_{k=u(x,t)}=\eta^{\prime}\left(u\right)\partial_{k}f(x,k)|_{k=u(x,t)}.

Therefore, by (

\partial_{t}\eta\left(u\right)+\diver q\left(x,u\right)=\eta^{\prime}\left(u\right)\partial_{t}u+\diver_{x}q\left(x,u\right)+\partial_{k}q(x,k)|_{k=u(x,t)}\cdot\nabla_{x}u\\

4.24
), (
4.25
) and Dominated Convergence Theorem, we obtain (
4.21
).

We now prove that:

\lim_{n\to\infty}\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\,\diver_{x}q_{n}(x,u)dxdt=\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\,\diver_{x}q(x,u)dxdt.

(4.26)

First we prove that for almost every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
we get

\lim_{n\to\infty}\diver_{x}q_{n}(x,u(x,t))=\diver_{x}q(x,u(x,t)).

(4.27)

For this purpose, let us prove the existence of a set
\Theta\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathcal{L}^{d}(\Theta)=0
, such that for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Theta
and
k\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\diver q_{n}(x,k)=\diver q(x,k).

(4.28)

From (
4.28
), we deduce (
4.27
)
because for every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
such that
(x,t)
lies in the domain of
u
and
(x,t)\notin\Theta\times I
, we obtain (
4.27
) for
x
and
k=u(x,t)
.
Let
\Theta
be as in item 2a of Assumption
1
. For all

k\in\mathbb{R}

and for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Theta

we obtain

\diver q_{n}(x,k)=\diver\left[\intop_{k_{0}}^{k}\eta^{\prime}_{n}(w)\partial_{w}f(x,w)dw\right]\\
=\diver\left[-\intop_{k_{0}}^{k}\eta^{\prime\prime}_{n}(w)f(x,w)dw+\eta_{n}^{\prime}(k)f(x,k)-\eta_{n}^{\prime}(k_{0})f(x,k_{0})\right]\\
=-\intop_{k_{0}}^{k}\eta^{\prime\prime}_{n}(w)\diver f(x,w)dw+\eta_{n}^{\prime}(k)\diver f(x,k).

(4.29)

In the last equality of (
4.29
) we use
\eta_{n}^{\prime}(k_{0})=0
. The integration by parts and the differentiation under the sign of the integral follows from Lemmas
3.2
,
3.3
. Let us analyse the integral on the right hand side of (
4.29
). More precisely, we show that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\intop_{k_{0}}^{k}\eta^{\prime\prime}_{n}(w)\diver f(x,w)dw=\sign(k-k_{0})\diver f(x,k_{0}).

(4.30)

Let
k\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
be such that
w\longmapsto\diver f(x,w)
is continuous (almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
has this property according to item 2a of Assumption
1
). So it is continuous at
k_{0}
. Let
\epsilon\in(0,\infty).
There exists
\delta\in(0,\infty)
such that
|\diver f(x,w)-\diver f(x,k_{0})|<\epsilon
whenever
|w-k_{0}|<\delta
. Assume that
k>k_{0}
. It follows that

\intop_{k_{0}}^{k}\eta^{\prime\prime}_{n}(w)\diver f(x,w)dw=\intop_{k_{0}}^{k}\eta^{\prime\prime}_{n}(w)\diver f(x,k_{0})dw-\intop_{k_{0}}^{k}\eta^{\prime\prime}_{n}(w)\left(\diver f(x,k_{0})-\diver f(x,w)\right)dw\\
=\diver f(x,k_{0})\left(\eta^{\prime}_{n}(k)-\eta^{\prime}_{n}(k_{0})\right)-\intop_{k_{0}+\delta}^{k}\eta^{\prime\prime}_{n}(w)\left(\diver f(x,k_{0})-\diver f(x,w)\right)dw\\
-\intop_{k_{0}}^{k_{0}+\delta}\eta^{\prime\prime}_{n}(w)\left(\diver f(x,k_{0})-\diver f(x,w)\right)dw.

(4.31)

Observe that since

\eta_{n}

is convex we know that

\eta_{n}^{\prime\prime}

is a nonnegative function. By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus we get

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\intop_{k_{0}+\delta}^{k}\eta^{\prime\prime}_{n}(w)|\diver f(x,k_{0})-\diver f(x,w)|dw\\
\leq\sup_{w\in[k_{0},k]}|\diver f(x,k_{0})-\diver f(x,w)|\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\eta^{\prime}_{n}(k)-\eta^{\prime}_{n}(k_{0}+\delta)\right)=0.

(4.32)

In addition,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\intop_{k_{0}}^{k_{0}+\delta}\eta^{\prime\prime}_{n}(w)|\diver f(x,k_{0})-\diver f(x,w)|dw\leq\epsilon\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\eta^{\prime}_{n}(k_{0}+\delta)-\eta^{\prime}_{n}(k_{0})\right)=\epsilon.

(4.33)

Therefore, we get (
4.30
) from (
4.31
), (
4.32
) and (
4.33
).

Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
in
(
4.29
)
, and taking into account
(
4.30
)
, we obtain (
4.28
).
The case
k<k_{0}
is similar. It completes the proof of (
4.27
).

Next, according to

(
4.29
)
,
we get for almost every

(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I

\displaystyle\diver_{x}q_{n}(x,u(x,t))=-\intop_{k_{0}}^{u(x,t)}\eta^{\prime\prime}_{n}(w)\diver f(x,w)dw+\eta_{n}^{\prime}(u(x,t))\diver_{x}f(x,u(x,t)).

(4.34)

Notice that, since
u
is bounded and
f
is locally Lipschitz, the sequence of functions
(x,t)\longmapsto\diver_{x}q_{n}(x,u(x,t))
is uniformly bounded on compact sets in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
, which allows us to use the Dominated Convergence Theorem to get (
4.26
).

Taking the limit as
n\to\infty
in

(
4.17
)
and taking into account
(
4.18
),
(
4.20
),
(
4.21
)
and (
4.26
), we see that the pair
(\eta,q)
, as defined in (
4.13
),(
4.14
), satisfies the entropy inequality
\eqref{eq:entropy inequality}.

5
L^{1}
-contraction property

In this section, we prove several contraction properties.

Recall the Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem
[
16
]
:

Theorem 5.1
.

(Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem)

Let
h\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, almost every point in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a Lebesgue point of
h
, i.e. a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

such that

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\fintop_{B_{\epsilon}\left(x\right)}\left|h\left(x\right)-h\left(y\right)\right|dy:=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{d}(B_{\epsilon}\left(x\right))}\intop_{B_{\epsilon}\left(x\right)}\left|h\left(x\right)-h\left(y\right)\right|dy=0.

(5.1)

Here
B_{\epsilon}(x)

is a closed ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
centred at
x
with radius
\epsilon
.

Recall that the
d-
dimensional standard
mollifier kernel
\rho_{\epsilon}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is given by

\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x\right):=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{d}}\rho\left(\frac{x}{\epsilon}\right),\quad\rho\left(x\right)=\begin{cases}C\,\exp\left(\frac{1}{\left|x\right|^{2}-1}\right)&\left|x\right|<1\\
0&\left|x\right|\geq 1\end{cases},

(5.2)

where
C>0
is a constant such that
\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho\left(x\right)dx=1
.

The following lemma is the main result of this paper, in which we establish a localized contraction property, sometimes referred to as Kato’s inequality.

Lemma 5.2

(Localized contraction property for entropy solutions)
.

Let
u,\tilde{u}
be entropy solutions as in definition
4.3
. Let
(\eta,q)
be the entropy pair defined by

\eta=\eta\left(k_{1},k_{2}\right)=\left|k_{1}-k_{2}\right|,\quad q=q\left(x,k_{1},k_{2}\right)=\sign\left(k_{1}-k_{2}\right)\left(f\left(x,k_{1}\right)-f\left(x,k_{2}\right)\right),\quad k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{R},

(5.3)

where
f
is a flux as in Assumption
1
. Then, for every
0\leq\psi\in\operatorname{Lip}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I\right)

\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg[\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)\eta\left(u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)+\nabla_{x}\psi(x,t)\cdot q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\bigg]dxdt\geq 0.

(5.4)

Proof.

The proof is divided into
2
steps.

Step 1

Let us fix
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
k_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
s\in I
. We choose a pair
of functions
\left(\eta,q\right)
defined by

\eta=\eta\left(k_{1},k_{2}\right)=\left|k_{1}-k_{2}\right|,\quad q=q\left(x,k_{1},k_{2}\right)=\sign\left(k_{1}-k_{2}\right)\left(f\left(x,k_{1}\right)-f\left(x,k_{2}\right)\right).

(5.5)

By Lemma
4.5
we know that this pair
satisfies the entropy inequality (
4.5
). Let us
choose a test function of the form

\varphi=\varphi\left(x,t,y,s\right):=\psi(x,t)\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right).

(5.6)

Here
\omega_{\epsilon}
stands for the
1
-dimensional standard mollifier kernel and
\rho_{\epsilon}
stands for the
d
-dimensional standard mollifier
kernel as in (
5.2
),
0\leq\psi\in\operatorname{Lip}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I\right)
. Since
u

is an entropy solution, we get by Definition
4.3

\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg[\partial_{t}\varphi\,\eta\left(u,k_{2}\right)+\varphi\,\bigg(\diver_{x}q\left(x,u,k_{2}\right)-\partial_{1}\eta\left(u,k_{2}\right)\diver_{x}f\left(x,u\right)\bigg)+\nabla_{x}\varphi\cdot q\left(x,u,k_{2}\right)\bigg]dxdt\geq 0.

(5.7)

Here
u=u\left(x,t\right)
, and
\partial_{1}\eta
stands for the
partial derivative of
\eta
with respect to the first variable. We choose

k_{2}=\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)
. By integrating the last inequality
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
with respect to
dyds
and using Fubini’s Theorem we get

\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Bigg\{\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg[\partial_{t}\varphi\,\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)+\varphi\bigg(\diver_{x}q\left(x,u,\tilde{u}\right)-\partial_{1}\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)\diver_{x}f\left(x,u\right)\bigg)+\nabla_{x}\varphi\cdot q\left(x,u,\tilde{u}\right)\bigg]dyds\Bigg\}dxdt\geq 0.

(5.8)

In a similar manner, for fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
k_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
, and

t\in I
, by the assumption that
\tilde{u}
is an entropy solution,
we have

\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg[\partial_{s}\varphi\,\eta\left(k_{1},\tilde{u}\right)+\varphi\,\bigg(\diver_{y}q\left(y,k_{1},\tilde{u}\right)-\partial_{2}\eta\left(k_{1},\tilde{u}\right)\diver_{y}f\left(y,\tilde{u}\right)\bigg)+\nabla_{y}\varphi\cdot q\left(y,k_{1},\tilde{u}\right)\bigg]dyds\geq 0.

(5.9)

Here
\tilde{u}=\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)
and
\partial_{2}\eta

is the derivative of
\eta
with respect to the second variable. We choose

k_{1}=u\left(x,t\right)
. By integrating the last inequality on

\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
with respect to
dxdt
we get

\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Bigg\{\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg[\partial_{s}\varphi\,\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)+\varphi\,\bigg(\diver_{y}q\left(y,u,\tilde{u}\right)-\partial_{2}\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)\diver_{y}f\left(y,\tilde{u}\right)\bigg)+\nabla_{y}\varphi\cdot q\left(y,u,\tilde{u}\right)\bigg]dyds\Bigg\}dxdt\geq 0.

(5.10)

We add up (
5.8
) and (
5.10
), and
we get

\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Bigg\{\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg[\partial_{t}\varphi\,\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)+\varphi\,\bigg(\diver_{x}q\left(x,u,\tilde{u}\right)-\partial_{1}\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)\diver_{x}f\left(x,u\right)\bigg)+\nabla_{x}\varphi\cdot q\left(x,u,\tilde{u}\right)\bigg]dyds\\
+\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg[\partial_{s}\varphi\,\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)+\varphi\,\bigg(\diver_{y}q\left(y,u,\tilde{u}\right)-\partial_{2}\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)\diver_{y}f\left(y,\tilde{u}\right)\bigg)+\nabla_{y}\varphi\cdot q\left(y,u,\tilde{u}\right)\bigg]dyds\Bigg\}dxdt\geq 0.

(5.11)

In (
5.11
) we have
\varphi=\varphi(x,t,y,s)
. Note that we have in (
5.11
) an integral of the form
\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{\cdot\right\}\,dxdt
, and within
\left\{\cdot\right\}
, we observe analogous terms characterized by the interchange of roles between
x,t
and
y,s
.
For instance, the term
\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{t}\varphi\,\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)\,dyds
is analogous to the term
\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{s}\varphi\,\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)\,dyds
.

We sum up analogous terms from
\left\{\cdot\right\}
in the inequality (
5.11
).

By the definition of
\varphi
, we get

\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{t}\varphi\,\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)dyds+\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\partial_{s}\varphi\,\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)dyds\\
=\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\biggl(\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)+\psi(x,t)\partial_{t}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)+\psi(x,t)\partial_{s}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\biggr)\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)dyds\\
=\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)dyds,

(5.12)

where in the second equality of (
5.12
) we have
\psi(x,t)\partial_{t}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)+\psi(x,t)\partial_{s}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)=0
.

Next, we sum up analogous terms that involve
\varphi
(without derivative). Before doing that, note that

\diver_{x}q\left(x,u,\tilde{u}\right)-\partial_{1}\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)\diver_{x}f\left(x,u\right)\\
=\sign\left(u-\tilde{u}\right)\bigg(\diver_{x}f\left(x,u\right)-\diver_{x}f\left(x,\tilde{u}\right)\bigg)-\sign\left(u-\tilde{u}\right)\diver_{x}f\left(x,u\right)\\
=-\sign\left(u-\tilde{u}\right)\diver_{x}f\left(x,\tilde{u}\right);

(5.13)

\diver_{y}q\left(y,u,\tilde{u}\right)-\partial_{2}\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)\diver_{y}f\left(y,\tilde{u}\right)\\
=\sign\left(u-\tilde{u}\right)\bigg(\diver_{y}f\left(y,u\right)-\diver_{y}f\left(y,\tilde{u}\right)\bigg)+\sign\left(u-\tilde{u}\right)\diver_{y}f\left(y,\tilde{u}\right)\\
=\sign\left(u-\tilde{u}\right)\diver_{y}f\left(y,u\right).

(5.14)

Therefore, from (
5.13
) and (
5.14
), we get

\bigg[\diver_{x}q\left(x,u,\tilde{u}\right)-\partial_{1}\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)\diver_{x}f\left(x,u\right)\bigg]+\bigg[\diver_{y}q\left(y,u,\tilde{u}\right)-\partial_{2}\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)\diver_{y}f\left(y,\tilde{u}\right)\bigg]\\
=\sign\left(u-\tilde{u}\right)\big(\diver_{y}f\left(y,u\right)-\diver_{x}f\left(x,\tilde{u}\right)\big).

(5.15)

Therefore,

\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\,\bigg(\diver_{x}q\left(x,u,\tilde{u}\right)-\partial_{1}\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)\diver_{x}f\left(x,u\right)\bigg)dyds\\
+\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi\,\bigg(\diver_{y}q\left(y,u,\tilde{u}\right)-\partial_{2}\eta\left(u,\tilde{u}\right)\diver_{y}f\left(y,\tilde{u}\right)\bigg)dyds\\
=\psi(x,t)\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\sign\left(u-\tilde{u}\right)\bigg(\diver_{y}f\left(y,u\right)-\diver_{x}f\left(x,\tilde{u}\right)\bigg)dyds.

(5.16)

Next, we add up analogous terms which contain
\nabla_{x}\varphi,\nabla_{y}\varphi
.
We get

\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\varphi\cdot q\left(x,u,\tilde{u}\right)dyds+\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\nabla_{y}\varphi\cdot q\left(y,u,\tilde{u}\right)dyds\\
=\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg(\nabla_{x}\psi(x,t)\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)+\psi(x,t)\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\nabla_{x}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\bigg)\cdot q\left(x,u,\tilde{u}\right)dyds\\
+\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x,t)\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\nabla_{y}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\cdot q\left(y,u,\tilde{u}\right)dyds\\
=\psi(x,t)\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\bigg(\nabla_{x}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\cdot q\left(x,u,\tilde{u}\right)+\nabla_{y}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\cdot q\left(y,u,\tilde{u}\right)\bigg)dyds\\
+\nabla_{x}\psi(x,t)\cdot\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)q\left(x,u,\tilde{u}\right)dyds\\
=\psi(x,t)\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\nabla_{y}\rho_{\epsilon}(x-y)\cdot\bigg(q(y,u,\tilde{u})-q(x,u,\tilde{u})\bigg)dyds\\
+\nabla_{x}\psi(x,t)\cdot\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)q\left(x,u,\tilde{u}\right)dyds,

(5.17)

where in the last equation of (
5.17
) we use
\nabla_{x}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)=-\nabla_{y}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)
.

We substitute (
5.12
), (
5.16
), and (
5.17
) into (
5.11
) to obtain for
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)

\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bigg\{\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)I_{1}^{\epsilon}(x,t)+\nabla_{x}\psi(x,t)\cdot I_{2}^{\epsilon}(x,t)+\psi(x,t)\bigg(I_{3}^{\epsilon}(x,t)+I_{4}^{\epsilon}(x,t)\bigg)\bigg\}dxdt\geq 0.

(5.18)

Here

I_{1}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right):=\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}(x-y)\eta\left(u(x,t),\tilde{u}(y,s)\right)dyds,

(5.19)

I_{2}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right):=\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)q\left(x,u(x,t),\tilde{u}(y,s)\right)dyds,

(5.20)

I_{3}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right):=\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\sign\left(u(x,t)-\tilde{u}(y,s)\right)\bigg(\diver_{y}f\left(y,u(x,t)\right)-\diver_{x}f\left(x,\tilde{u}(y,s)\right)\bigg)dyds,

(5.21)

I_{4}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right):=\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\nabla_{y}\rho_{\epsilon}(x-y)\cdot\biggl(q(y,u(x,t),\tilde{u}(y,s))-q(x,u(x,t),\tilde{u}(y,s))\biggr)dyds.

(5.22)

Step 2

We intend to take the limit as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
in (
5.18
) using the Dominated Convergence Theorem to interchange the limit and the integral. To apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem, we need to prove the following four assertions, which tell us that the families of functions
I^{\epsilon}_{j}
,
j\in\{1,2,3,4\}
, indexed by
\epsilon
with variable
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
, converge almost everywhere and are bounded by locally integrable functions:

1.

For almost every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
we get

\begin{cases}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{1}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right)=\eta(u(x,t),\tilde{u}(x,t))\\
\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,\infty)}|I_{1}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right)|\leq\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)}+|u(x,t)|\end{cases},

(5.23)

and the function on the right hand side of the inequality in (
5.23
) lies in the space
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)
.

2.

For almost every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
we get

\begin{cases}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{2}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right)=q(x,u(x,t),\tilde{u}(x,t))\\
\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,\infty)}|I_{2}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right)|\leq\|f(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\image\tilde{u})}+|f(x,u(x,t))|\end{cases},

(5.24)

and the function on the right hand side of the inequality in (
5.24
) lies in the space
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)
.

3.

Let
P_{x}(\Supp(\psi))
be the projection on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the compact support of
\psi
, and let
U_{0}
be any open and bounded set which contains it. Let us denote

. For almost every
(x,t)\in P_{x}(\Supp(\psi))\times I
we have

\begin{cases}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{3}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right)=\diver_{x}q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\\
\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,D)}|I_{3}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right)|\leq\|\diver_{y}f(\cdot,u(x,t))\|_{L^{\infty}(U_{0})}+\|\diver_{x}f(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\image\tilde{u})}\end{cases},

(5.25)

and the function on the right hand side of the inequality in (

) lies in the space
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)
.

4.

Moreover, let
U_{0}
and
D
as above, and let
A\subset\mathbb{R}
be any bounded set such that
\mathcal{L}^{1}\big(A\Delta\left[\image u\cup\image\tilde{u}\right]\big)=0
. Let us denote by
L
the Lipschitz constant of
f
on the bounded set
U_{0}\times A
. Then, for almost every
(x,t)\in P_{x}(\Supp(\psi))\times I
we get

\begin{cases}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}I_{4}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right)=-\diver_{x}q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\\
\sup_{\epsilon\in(0,D)}|I_{4}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right)|\leq 2L\mathcal{L}^{d}(B_{1}(0))\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sqrt{d}\end{cases}.

(5.26)

From (
5.18
), (
5.23
), (
5.24
), (
5.25
), and (
5.26
), we obtain by the Dominated Convergence Theorem the localized contraction property (
5.4
).

The proofs of (
5.23
), (
5.24
) and (
5.25
) rely mainly upon Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem (Theorem
5.1
). The proof of (
5.26
) is a bit more complicated than the others, and it is established using Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem along with an approximating argument.

We prove (
5.23
). Note that for
t\in(0,\infty)
, we get for every sufficiently small
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
that
\intop_{I}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)ds=1
. Hence, by the definition of
\eta
and the triangle inequality we get

\Bigl|I_{1}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right)-\eta\left(u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\Bigr|\\
=\left|\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}(x-y)\eta\left(u(x,t),\tilde{u}(y,s)\right)dyds-\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}(x-y)\eta\left(u(x,t),\tilde{u}(x,t)\right)dyds\right|\\
\leq\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\big|\eta\left(u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)-\eta\left(u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\big|dyds\\
\leq\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\intop_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}\frac{1}{\epsilon^{d}}\intop_{B_{\epsilon}\left(x\right)}\left|\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)-\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right|dyds.

(5.27)

Since
\tilde{u}\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I\right)
, then
\tilde{u}\in L_{\text{loc}}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I\right)
and, according to Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem,
we get for almost every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
the limit in (
5.23
). The inequality in (
5.23
) follows from the definition of
I^{\epsilon}_{1}
and properties of
\omega_{\epsilon},\rho_{\epsilon}
: for every
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
and for almost every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
we have

|I_{1}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right)|\leq\ess-sup\limits_{(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I}\eta(u(x,t),\tilde{u}(y,s))\leq\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)}+|u(x,t)|.

(5.28)

Since
u,\tilde{u}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)
, the function
(x,t)\mapsto\|\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)}+|u(x,t)|
lies in the space
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)
.
It proves (
5.23
).

We prove (
5.24
). We get
for almost every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
and every small enough
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)

\big|I_{2}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right)-q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\big|\\
=\left|\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)q\left(x,u(x,t),\tilde{u}(y,s)\right)dyds-\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)q\left(x,u(x,t),\tilde{u}(x,t)\right)dyds\right|\\
\leq\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}(x-y)\big|q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)-q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\big|dyds\\
\leq\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\intop_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}\frac{1}{\epsilon^{d}}\intop_{B_{\epsilon}\left(x\right)}\left|q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)-q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\right|dyds.

(5.29)

Since
\tilde{u}\in L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I\right)

and
f(x,\cdot)\in L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we know that the function

\left(y,s\right)\longmapsto q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)=\sign\left(u\left(x,t\right)-\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)\big(f\left(x,u\left(x,t\right)\right)-f\left(x,\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)\big)

(5.30)

lies in
L_{\text{loc}}^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I\right)
. Therefore, by Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem, almost every

\left(x,t\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
is a Lebesgue point of this
function, so we get the limit in (
5.24
). The inequality in (
5.24
) follows from the definition of
I^{\epsilon}_{2}
: for every
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
and almost every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
we get

|I_{2}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right)|\leq\ess-sup_{(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I}|q(x,u(x,t),\tilde{u}(y,s))|\leq\ess-sup_{(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I}|f(x,u(x,t))-f(x,\tilde{u}(y,s))|\\
\leq|f(x,u(x,t))|+\|f(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\image\tilde{u})}.

(5.31)

Since
u,\tilde{u}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)
and
f\in L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{d})
(because
f
is continuous), the function
(x,t)\mapsto|f(x,u(x,t))|+\|f(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\image\tilde{u})}
lies in the space
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)
.
It proves (
5.24
).

We prove now (
5.25
). For almost every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
and sufficiently small
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
we have

\left|I_{3}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right)-\diver_{x}q(x,u(x,t),\tilde{u}(x,t))\right|\\
=\Bigg|\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\sign\left(u(x,t)-\tilde{u}(y,s)\right)\bigg(\diver_{y}f\left(y,u(x,t)\right)-\diver_{x}f\left(x,\tilde{u}(y,s)\right)\bigg)dyds\\
-\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\diver_{x}q(x,u(x,t),\tilde{u}(x,t))dyds\Bigg|\\
\leq\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\bigg|\sign\left(u(x,t)-\tilde{u}(y,s)\right)\bigg(\diver_{y}f\left(y,u(x,t)\right)-\diver_{x}f\left(x,\tilde{u}(y,s)\right)\bigg)\\
-\diver_{x}q(x,u(x,t),\tilde{u}(x,t))\bigg|dyds\\
\leq\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\intop_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}\frac{1}{\epsilon^{d}}\intop_{B_{\epsilon}\left(x\right)}\bigg|\sign\left(u(x,t)-\tilde{u}(y,s)\right)\bigg(\diver_{y}f\left(y,u(x,t)\right)-\diver_{x}f\left(x,\tilde{u}(y,s)\right)\bigg)\\
-\diver_{x}q(x,u(x,t),\tilde{u}(x,t))\bigg|dyds.

(5.32)

Since
\tilde{u}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)
and
f
is locally Lipschitz, we get for almost every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
that the function

(y,s)\longmapsto\sign(u(x,t)-\tilde{u}(y,s))\bigg(\diver_{y}f(y,u(x,t))-\diver_{x}f(x,\tilde{u}(y,s))\bigg)

(5.33)

lies in the space
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)
. Indeed, note that for
k:=u(x,t)
, the function
y\mapsto f(y,k)
is locally Lipschitz in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Therefore, by Rademacher’s Theorem, we get that

\diver_{y}f(\cdot,k)\in L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, so the function
(y,s)\mapsto\diver_{y}f(y,u(x,t))
lies in
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)
. By item 2a in Assumption
1
, for almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we get that the function
k\mapsto\diver_{x}f(x,k)
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}
, so it is locally bounded on
\mathbb{R}
. Since
\tilde{u}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)
, we have that the function
(y,s)\mapsto\diver_{x}f(x,\tilde{u}(y,s))
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
. Therefore, we get that the function in (
5.33
) lies in
L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)
. Thus, almost every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
is a Lebesgue point of this function according to Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem. Therefore, for almost every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
, we obtain by (
5.32
), the limit in (
5.25
). The inequality in (
5.25
) follows from the definition of
I_{3}^{\epsilon}
:
for almost every
(x,t)\in P_{x}(\Supp(\psi))\times I
and every
\epsilon\in(0,D)
we have
B_{\epsilon}(x)\subset U_{0}
and

|I_{3}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right)|\leq\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\bigg|\diver_{y}f\left(y,u(x,t)\right)-\diver_{x}f\left(x,\tilde{u}(y,s)\right)\bigg|dyds\\
\leq\ess-sup_{(y,s)\in U_{0}\times I}\big|\diver_{y}f\left(y,u(x,t)\right)-\diver_{x}f\left(x,\tilde{u}(y,s)\right)\big|\\
\leq\|\diver_{y}f(\cdot,u(x,t))\|_{L^{\infty}(U_{0})}+\|\diver_{x}f(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\image\tilde{u})}.

(5.34)

The function on the right hand side of (
5.34
) is locally bounded in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
since
u,\tilde{u}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)
and
\diver_{x}f\in L^{\infty}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R})
.
It completes the proof of (
5.25
).

Now we prove (
5.26
). First note that by the definition of the inner product we get

I_{4}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right)=-\sum_{i=1}^{d}\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\partial_{y_{i}}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\,\bigg(q_{i}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)-q_{i}\left(y,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)\bigg)dyds,

(5.35)

where we denote
q=(q_{1},...,q_{d})
. Therefore, for proving the limit in (
5.26
), according to (
5.35
), it is enough to show that for every natural number
1\leq i\leq d
and almost every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\partial_{y_{i}}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\,\bigg(q_{i}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)-q_{i}\left(y,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)\bigg)dyds\\
=(q_{i})_{x_{i}}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)=e_{i}\cdot\nabla_{x}q_{i}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right),

(5.36)

where
e_{i}
is the standard unit vector with
1
in the
i
-th coordinate and zero in the other coordinates. Here
(q_{i})_{x_{i}}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)=\partial_{x_{i}}q_{i}\left(x,k_{1},k_{2}\right)|_{k_{1}=u(x,t),k_{2}=\tilde{u}(x,t)}
.

Let us fix
1\leq i\leq d,i\in\mathbb{N}
. We now prove that, for almost every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
the following formula holds:

q_{i}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)-q_{i}\left(y,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)=\nabla_{x}q_{i}(x,u(x,t),\tilde{u}(y,s))\cdot\left(x-y\right)+\Theta(x,t,y,s)

(5.37)

for almost every
(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
. Here
\Theta(x,t,y,s)
is a function with the following property: for every
\xi\in(0,\infty)
there exists
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
such that for almost every
(y,s)\in B_{\epsilon}(x)\times I
we have
|\Theta(x,t,y,s)|\leq\xi|x-y|
.

Let us denote
f=(f_{1},...,f_{d})
. Let
(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
be any point in the domain of
u
such that the derivative of the
i
-th function coordinate of the flux,
f_{i}
, at the point
x_{0}
,
\nabla_{x}f_{i}(x_{0},k)
, is a continuous function in
k\in\mathbb{R}
; by item 2a of Assumption
1
almost every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
has this property. Let
(y_{0},s_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
be a point in the domain of
\tilde{u}
. Let us denote
k_{1}:=u\left(x_{0},t_{0}\right)
and
k_{2}:=\tilde{u}\left(y_{0},s_{0}\right)
. Let us define

g(y):=q_{i}\left(y,k_{1},k_{2}\right)=\sign\left(k_{1}-k_{2}\right)\left(f_{i}\left(y,k_{1}\right)-f_{i}\left(y,k_{2}\right)\right).

(5.38)

By the choice of
x_{0}
, the function
g
is differentiable at
x_{0}
and

g(x_{0})-g(y)=\nabla g(x_{0})\cdot(x_{0}-y)+o(x_{0}-y),\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(5.39)

Therefore, we get (
5.39
) for
y=y_{0}
. It proves (
5.37
). Note that

\frac{|\Theta(x,t,y,s)|}{|x-y|}=\frac{\left|q_{i}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)-q_{i}\left(y,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)-\nabla_{x}q_{i}(x,u(x,t),\tilde{u}(y,s))\cdot\left(x-y\right)\right|}{|x-y|}\\
\leq\frac{\left|f_{i}(x,u(x,t))-f_{i}(y,u(x,t))-\nabla_{x}f_{i}(x,u(x,t))\cdot(x-y)\right|}{|x-y|}\\
+\frac{\left|f_{i}(x,\tilde{u}(y,s))-f_{i}(y,\tilde{u}(y,s))-\nabla_{x}f_{i}(x,\tilde{u}(y,s))\cdot(x-y)\right|}{|x-y|}\\
\leq 2\sup_{k\in K}\frac{\left|f_{i}(x,k)-f_{i}(y,k)-\nabla_{x}f_{i}(x,k)\cdot(x-y)\right|}{|x-y|},

(5.40)

where
K\subset\mathbb{R}
is any compact set for which
\mathcal{L}^{1}\left(K\Delta\left[\image u\cup\image\tilde{u}\right]\right)=0
. By item 2b of Assumption
1
we have for arbitrary positive number
\xi
a number
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
such that
|\Theta(x,t,y,s)|\leq\xi|x-y|
for almost every
(y,s)\in B_{\epsilon}(x)\times I
.

We get by (
5.37
)

\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\partial_{y_{i}}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\,\bigg(q_{i}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)-q_{i}\left(y,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)\bigg)dyds\\
=\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\partial_{y_{i}}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\left(x-y\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}q_{i}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)dyds+o_{\epsilon}\left(1\right),

(5.41)

where

o_{\epsilon}\left(1\right):=\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\partial_{y_{i}}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\Theta(x,t,y,s)dyds\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}o_{\epsilon}\left(1\right)=0.

(5.42)

In order to prove the limit in (
5.42
), note that for
y\in B_{\epsilon}\left(x\right)
, we get

\left|\partial_{y_{i}}\rho_{\epsilon}(x-y)\right||x-y|=\left|\frac{1}{\epsilon^{d}}\nabla\rho\left(\frac{x-y}{\epsilon}\right)\cdot\left(-\frac{1}{\epsilon}e_{i}\right)\right||x-y|\leq\left|\frac{1}{\epsilon^{d}}\nabla\rho\left(\frac{x-y}{\epsilon}\right)\right|\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{d}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(5.43)

and for arbitrarily small
\xi\in(0,\infty)
, there exists
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
such that for almost every
(y,s)\in B_{\epsilon}(x)\times I

|\Theta(x,t,y,s)|\leq\xi|x-y|

|o_{\epsilon}\left(1\right)|\leq\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathcal{L}^{d}(B_{1}(0))\xi

In addition, note that by Fubini’s Theorem and integration by parts we have for every sufficiently small
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)

\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\partial_{y_{i}}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\left(x-y\right)dyds=e_{i}.

(5.44)

More precisely, note that on the left-hand side of (
5.44
), we have a vector due to the expression
x-y
. Examine the
j
-th coordinate of this vector, meaning that
\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\partial_{y_{i}}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\left(x_{j}-y_{j}\right)dyds
. Utilize Fubini’s Theorem and integration by parts to transfer the derivative
\partial_{y_{i}}
to
x_{j}-y_{j}
. In the case where

, we obtain zero; in the case where

, we obtain

.

Hence, using (
5.43
) and (
5.44

\bigg|\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\partial_{y_{i}}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\left(x-y\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}q_{i}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)dyds-e_{i}\cdot\nabla_{x}q_{i}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\bigg|\\
=\bigg|\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\partial_{y_{i}}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\left(x-y\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}q_{i}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)dyds\\
-\left(\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\partial_{y_{i}}\rho_{\epsilon}\left(x-y\right)\left(x-y\right)dyds\right)\cdot\nabla_{x}q_{i}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\bigg|\\
\leq\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)\left|\partial_{y_{i}}\rho_{\epsilon}(x-y)\right||x-y|\bigg|\nabla_{x}q_{i}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)-\nabla_{x}q_{i}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\bigg|dyds\\
\leq\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\intop_{t-\epsilon}^{t+\epsilon}\frac{1}{\epsilon^{d}}\intop_{B_{\epsilon}\left(x\right)}\bigg|\nabla_{x}q_{i}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)-\nabla_{x}q_{i}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\bigg|dyds.

(5.45)

By item 2a of Assumption
1
on the flux
f
and
\tilde{u}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)
, we get that

(y,s)\longmapsto\nabla_{x}q_{i}\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)\right)

(5.46)

lies in the space
L_{\text{loc}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)
. Therefore, by Lebesgue Differentiation Theorem, (
5.41
) and (
5.45
), we obtain (
5.36
), from which we get the limit in
(
5.26
)
. For the inequality in
(
5.26
)
, note that, for
k_{1}:=u(x,t)
and

k_{2}:=\tilde{u}\left(y,s\right)
we get

q(y,k_{1},k_{2})-q(x,k_{1},k_{2})=\sign(k_{1}-k_{2})(f(y,k_{1})-f(y,k_{2}))-\sign(k_{1}-k_{2})(f(x,k_{1})-f(x,k_{2}))\\
=\sign(k_{1}-k_{2})\bigg(f(y,k_{1})-f(x,k_{1})+f(x,k_{2})-f(y,k_{2})\bigg).

(5.47)

Let
U_{0},A,D
and
L
as in the formulation above (
5.26
). Notice that for almost every
(x,t)\in P_{x}(\Supp(\psi))\times I
and for every
\epsilon\in(0,D)
, we get by (
5.47
)

|I_{4}^{\epsilon}\left(x,t\right)|\leq\intop_{I}\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)|\nabla_{y}\rho_{\epsilon}(x-y)|\Bigl|q(y,u(x,t),\tilde{u}(y,s))-q(x,u(x,t),\tilde{u}(y,s))\Bigr|dyds\\
\leq\intop_{I}\intop_{B_{\epsilon}(x)}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)|\nabla_{y}\rho_{\epsilon}(x-y)|\Bigl|f(x,u(x,t))-f(y,u(x,t))\Bigr|dyds\\
+\intop_{I}\intop_{B_{\epsilon}(x)}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)|\nabla_{y}\rho_{\epsilon}(x-y)|\Bigl|f(x,\tilde{u}(y,s))-f(y,\tilde{u}(y,s))\Bigr|dyds\\
\leq 2L\intop_{I}\intop_{B_{\epsilon}(x)}\omega_{\epsilon}(t-s)|\nabla_{y}\rho_{\epsilon}(x-y)||x-y|dyds\leq 2L\mathcal{L}^{d}(B_{1}(0))\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sqrt{d},

(5.48)

where in the last inequality of (
5.48
) we use: for every
y\in B_{\epsilon}(x)

\left|\nabla_{y}\rho_{\epsilon}(x-y)\right||x-y|=\left|\frac{1}{\epsilon^{d}}\nabla\rho\left(\frac{x-y}{\epsilon}\right)\cdot\left(-\frac{1}{\epsilon}I_{d\times d}\right)\right||x-y|\\
\leq\left|\frac{1}{\epsilon^{d}}\nabla\rho\left(\frac{x-y}{\epsilon}\right)\right|\sqrt{d}\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{d}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sqrt{d},

(5.49)

where
I_{d\times d}
is the identity matrix of size
d\times d
.
It completes the proof of (
5.26
).
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

(Local
L^{1}
-contraction property)
Let
u
,
\tilde{u}
be two entropy solutions of
(
4.1
)
, and
f
is a flux as in Assumption
1
. Let us define

M:=\max\left\{\left|\left|{u}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)},\left|\left|{\tilde{u}}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)}\right\}<\infty,\quad I=(0,\infty),

(5.50)

and for each
R\in(0,\infty)
we define

N:=N_{M}(R):=\sup\left\{{\frac{|f(x,k)-f(x,k^{\prime})|}{|k-k^{\prime}|}}\,\middle|\,{x\in B_{R}(0),\,{k,k^{\prime}\in[-M,M]},\,k\neq k^{\prime}}\right\}<\infty.

(5.51)

The finiteness in (
5.51
) is due to the assumption that the flux
f=f(x,k)
is locally Lipschitz in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Then, there exists a set
\mathcal{N}\subset(0,N^{-1}R)
such that
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{N})=0
and

\intop_{B_{R-\tau N}(0)}\left|u(x,\tau)-\tilde{u}(x,\tau)\right|dx\leq\intop_{B_{R-\rho N}(0)}\left|u(x,\rho)-\tilde{u}(x,\rho)\right|dx,

(5.52)

for every
\rho,\tau\in(0,N^{-1}R)\setminus\mathcal{N}
with
\rho\leq\tau
. In other words, the function
t\mapsto\left\|u\left(\cdot,t\right)-\tilde{u}\left(\cdot,t\right)\right\|_{L^{1}(B_{R-tN}(0))}
is non-increasing on
(0,N^{-1}R)
outside a set of measure zero.

The proof we give here for Theorem
5.3
was originally given by Kruzkov (see
[
24
]
). However, we provide the proof here for the sake of completeness and with additional details.

Proof.

We divide the proof into three parts. In the first part, we introduce the functions
\alpha_{h}
and
\chi_{\epsilon}
and establish their properties. In the second part, we construct a test function
\psi
for the localized inequality (
5.4
) using the functions
\alpha_{h}
and
\chi_{\epsilon}
, and derive estimates for
\partial_{t}\psi
and
\nabla_{x}\psi
. In the third part, we use the localized inequality and the choice of
\psi
to establish the local
L^{1}
-contraction property.

Part 1

For the numbers
N,R
, we define a set

\mathcal{K}:=\left\{{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I}\,\middle|\,{t\in{(0,N^{-1}R)},\,\,x\in B_{R-tN}(0)}\right\}.

(5.53)

The set
\mathcal{K}
is an open cone with base in
B_{R}(0)\times\{0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
and vertex at the point
(0,N^{-1}R)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Let us define for every
h\in(0,\infty)

\alpha_{h}(\sigma):=\intop_{-\infty}^{\sigma}\omega_{h}(s)ds,\quad\sigma\in\mathbb{R},

(5.54)

where
\omega_{h}
is the one-dimensional mollifier kernel as defined in (
5.2
). Recall the three properties of
\omega_{h}
:
\omega_{h}\geq 0
,
\intop_{-\infty}^{\infty}\omega_{h}(s)ds=1
, and
\Supp(\omega_{h})=[-h,h]
.
Note that the function
\alpha_{h}
has the properties:
0\leq\alpha_{h}\leq 1
, and it is monotonically non-decreasing on
\mathbb{R}
. For every
\epsilon\in(0,\infty)
, let us define the function

\chi_{\epsilon}(x,t):=1-\alpha_{\epsilon}\left(|x|-[R-tN]+\epsilon\right),\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I.

(5.55)

Note that

\forall\epsilon\in(0,\infty),\\
\forall(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I\quad\Longrightarrow\quad 0\leq\chi_{\epsilon}(x,t)\leq 1.

(5.56)

In addition, we have

\forall\epsilon\in(0,\infty),\forall(x,t)\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I\right)\setminus\mathcal{K}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\chi_{\epsilon}(x,t)=0.

(5.57)

Indeed, if
(x,t)\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I\right)\setminus\mathcal{K}
, then
t\geq N^{-1}R
or
|x|\geq R-tN
. If
t\geq N^{-1}R
, then
|x|-[R-tN]+\epsilon\geq|x|+\epsilon
; if
|x|\geq R-tN
, then
|x|-[R-tN]+\epsilon\geq\epsilon
. Hence, in both cases
1\geq\alpha_{\epsilon}\left(|x|-[R-tN]+\epsilon\right)\geq\alpha_{\epsilon}(\epsilon)=1
. Therefore,
\chi_{\epsilon}(x,t)=1-\alpha_{\epsilon}\left(|x|-[R-tN]+\epsilon\right)=0
. This proves (
5.57
).

The next property of
\chi_{\epsilon}
we need is the following: for every
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\chi_{\epsilon}(x,t)=\chi_{\mathcal{K}}(x,t),

(5.58)

where
\chi_{\mathcal{K}}
is the characteristic function of
\mathcal{K}
. In case
(x,t)\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I\right)\setminus\mathcal{K}
, we get the limit in (
5.58
) from (
5.57
). For
(x,t)\in\mathcal{K}
we denote
\xi(x,t):=|x|-[R-tN]<0
. For every
\epsilon
such that
\xi(x,t)+\epsilon<-\epsilon
we get

0\leq\alpha_{\epsilon}\left(|x|-[R-tN]+\epsilon\right)=\alpha_{\epsilon}\left(\xi(x,t)+\epsilon\right)\leq\alpha_{\epsilon}(-\epsilon)=\intop_{-\infty}^{-\epsilon}\omega_{\epsilon}(s)ds=0.

(5.59)

Hence, for every
(x,t)\in\mathcal{K}
, we have the limit

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\chi_{\epsilon}(x,t)=1-\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\alpha_{\epsilon}\left(|x|-[R-tN]+\epsilon\right)=1.

(5.60)

Therefore, we get (
5.58
).

Part 2

Notice that since
u,\tilde{u}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)
, the function

t\longmapsto\int_{B_{R-tN}(0)}|u(x,t)-\tilde{u}(x,t)|dx

(5.61)

is integrable on
(0,N^{-1}R)
. Indeed, we have the estimate:

\int_{(0,N^{-1}R)}\left[\int_{B_{R-tN}(0)}|u(x,t)-\tilde{u}(x,t)|dx\right]dt\leq\int_{(0,N^{-1}R)}\left[\int_{B_{R}(0)}|u(x,t)-\tilde{u}(x,t)|dx\right]dt\\
\leq\left(\left|\left|{u}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)}+\left|\left|{\tilde{u}}\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I)}\right)N^{-1}R\,\mathcal{L}^{d}(B_{R}(0))<\infty.

(5.62)

Hence almost every point is a Lebesgue point of this function. Let
0<\rho<\tau<N^{-1}R
be Lebesgue points of this function. Using (
5.54
) and (
5.55
), we define

\psi(x,t):=\left(\alpha_{h}(t-\rho)-\alpha_{h}(t-\tau)\right)\chi_{\epsilon}(x,t).

(5.63)

We will use the function
\psi
as a test function in the localized inequality (
5.4
). Let us prove that
\psi\geq 0
and
\psi\in\operatorname{Lip}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I\right)
. Since
\alpha_{h}
is monotonically non-decreasing, and (
5.56
), we get that
\psi
is a product of non-negative functions and hence non-negative.
By (
5.57
), we have
\psi(x,t)=0
whenever
|x|>R
or
t>N^{-1}R
because
\chi_{\epsilon}(x,t)=0
. Note that if
0<h<\rho
and
t<\rho-h
, then

0\leq\alpha_{h}(t-\tau)\leq\alpha_{h}(t-\rho)=\int_{-\infty}^{t-\rho}\omega_{h}(s)ds=0.

(5.64)

Therefore,
\psi(x,t)=0
for
0<t<\rho-h
. Hence,
\Supp(\psi)\subset\overline{B}_{R}(0)\times[\rho-h,N^{-1}R]
, so it is a compact set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
provided
0<h<\rho
.

Note that
\psi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I\to[0,\infty)
is continuous function. Let us compute the partial derivatives of
\psi
. By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus we have

\partial_{t}\left(\alpha_{h}(t-\rho)-\alpha_{h}(t-\tau)\right)=\omega_{h}(t-\rho)-\omega_{h}(t-\tau)

(5.65)

and

\partial_{t}\chi_{\epsilon}(x,t)=-\omega_{\epsilon}\left(|x|-[R-tN]+\epsilon\right)N.

(5.66)

Therefore, by (
5.65
) and (
5.66
), we obtain

\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)=\partial_{t}\left(\alpha_{h}(t-\rho)-\alpha_{h}(t-\tau)\right)\chi_{\epsilon}(x,t)+\left(\alpha_{h}(t-\rho)-\alpha_{h}(t-\tau)\right)\partial_{t}\chi_{\epsilon}(x,t)\\
=\left(\omega_{h}(t-\rho)-\omega_{h}(t-\tau)\right)\chi_{\epsilon}(x,t)+\left(\alpha_{h}(t-\rho)-\alpha_{h}(t-\tau)\right)\left(-\omega_{\epsilon}\left(|x|-[R-tN]+\epsilon\right)N\right).

(5.67)

From (
5.67
), we get the estimate

|\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)|\leq 2\|\omega_{h}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+2N\|\omega_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\forall(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I.

(5.68)

For every
x\neq 0
, we get by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

\nabla_{x}\chi_{\epsilon}(x,t)=-\omega_{\epsilon}\left(|x|-[R-tN]+\epsilon\right)\frac{x}{|x|}.

(5.69)

Therefore, using (
5.69
), we get

\nabla_{x}\psi(x,t)=\left(\alpha_{h}(t-\rho)-\alpha_{h}(t-\tau)\right)\nabla_{x}\chi_{\epsilon}(x,t)\\
=\left(\alpha_{h}(t-\rho)-\alpha_{h}(t-\tau)\right)\left(-\omega_{\epsilon}\left(|x|-[R-tN]+\epsilon\right)\frac{x}{|x|}\right).

(5.70)

Therefore, by (
5.70
), we have the estimate

|\nabla_{x}\psi(x,t)|\leq 2\|\omega_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\forall(x,t)\in\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\right)\times I.

(5.71)

Since
\psi
is continuous in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
, and we have the bounds (
5.68
), (
5.71
), we conclude that
\psi
is Lipschitz in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
. Therefore,
\psi
is a legal test function for the localized inequality (
5.4
).

Part 3

Let
(\eta,q)
be as in (
5.3
). By Lemma
5.2
, we get

\iint_{\mathcal{K}}\bigg[\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)\eta\left(u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)+\nabla_{x}\psi(x,t)\cdot q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\bigg]dxdt\geq 0.

(5.72)

From (
5.67
) and (
5.70
) we have

\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)\eta\left(u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)+\nabla_{x}\psi(x,t)\cdot q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\\
=\Big[\left(\omega_{h}(t-\rho)-\omega_{h}(t-\tau)\right)\chi_{\epsilon}(x,t)+\left(\alpha_{h}(t-\rho)-\alpha_{h}(t-\tau)\right)\left(-\omega_{\epsilon}\left(|x|-[R-tN]+\epsilon\right)N\right)\Big]\eta\left(u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\\
+\Big[\left(\alpha_{h}(t-\rho)-\alpha_{h}(t-\tau)\right)\left(-\omega_{\epsilon}\left(|x|-[R-tN]+\epsilon\right)\frac{x}{|x|}\right)\Big]\cdot q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\\
=\left(\omega_{h}(t-\rho)-\omega_{h}(t-\tau)\right)\chi_{\epsilon}(x,t)\eta\left(u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\\
-\omega_{\epsilon}\left(|x|-[R-tN]+\epsilon\right)\left(\alpha_{h}(t-\rho)-\alpha_{h}(t-\tau)\right)\Bigg[\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)+N\eta\left(u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\Bigg].

(5.73)

Recall that

q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right):=\sign\left(u(x,t)-\tilde{u}(x,t)\right)\Big(f(x,u(x,t))-f(x,\tilde{u}(x,t))\Big).

(5.74)

For almost every
(x,t)\in\mathcal{K}
, we get by the definition of the number
N
(see (
5.51
))

\left|\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\right|\leq\left|f(x,u(x,t))-f(x,\tilde{u}(x,t))\right|\leq N|u(x,t)-\tilde{u}(x,t)|=N\eta\left(u(x,t),\tilde{u}(x,t)\right).

(5.75)

Let us denote the term in the last line of (
5.73
) by
H(x,t)
, meaning that

H(x,t):=\omega_{\epsilon}\left(|x|-[R-tN]+\epsilon\right)\left(\alpha_{h}(t-\rho)-\alpha_{h}(t-\tau)\right)\Bigg[\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot q\left(x,u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)+N\eta\left(u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)\Bigg].

(5.76)

Note that
H
is a non-negative function as a product of non-negative functions: the mollifier kernel
\omega_{\epsilon}
is non-negative by its definition; the function
\alpha_{h}
is monotonically non-decreasing and we assume that
\rho<\tau
; and the last function,
|x|^{-1}x\cdot q(x,u,\tilde{u})+N\eta(u,\tilde{u})
, is non-negative by (
5.75
).

Now, substituting (
5.73
) into (
5.72
), and using the non-negativity of
H
, we obtain:

\intop_{0}^{N^{-1}R}\intop_{B_{R-tN}(0)}\left(\omega_{h}(t-\rho)-\omega_{h}(t-\tau)\right)\chi_{\epsilon}(x,t)\eta\left(u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)dxdt\geq\intop_{0}^{N^{-1}R}\intop_{B_{R-tN}(0)}H(x,t)dxdt\geq 0.

(5.77)

Taking the limit as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}
in (
5.77
) and taking into account (
5.58
) and (
5.56
), we get by Dominated Convergence Theorem

\intop_{0}^{N^{-1}R}\intop_{B_{R-tN}(0)}\left(\omega_{h}(t-\rho)-\omega_{h}(t-\tau)\right)\eta\left(u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)dxdt\geq 0.

(5.78)

From (
5.78
) and linearity of integral, we get

\intop_{0}^{N^{-1}R}\omega_{h}(t-\rho)\left[\intop_{B_{R-tN}(0)}\eta\left(u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)dx\right]dt\geq\intop_{0}^{N^{-1}R}\omega_{h}(t-\tau)\left[\intop_{B_{R-tN}(0)}\eta\left(u\left(x,t\right),\tilde{u}\left(x,t\right)\right)dx\right]dt.

(5.79)

Recall the choice of
\tau,\rho
as Lebesgue points (see (
5.61
) and below it). Note also that for every
h\in(0,\infty)
with
h<\min\{\rho,N^{-1}R-\tau\}
, the intervals
(\rho-h,\rho+h)
and
(\tau-h,\tau+h)
are subsets of
(0,N^{-1}R)
. Taking the limit in (
5.79
) as
h\to 0^{+}
, we get

\intop_{B_{R-\rho N}(0)}\eta\left(u(x,\rho),\tilde{u}(x,\rho)\right)dx\geq\intop_{B_{R-\tau N}(0)}\eta\left(u(x,\tau),\tilde{u}(x,\tau)\right)dx.

(5.80)

This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

(Global
L^{1}
-contraction property)
Let
u
,
\tilde{u}
be two entropy solutions of
(
4.1
)
, and
f
is a flux as in Assumption
1
. Let
M
be as in (
5.50
); for
R\in(0,\infty)
we define
N:=N_{M}(R)
as in (
5.51
). Assume that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{N}{R}=0.

(5.81)

Then, there exists a set
\mathcal{N}\subset I=(0,\infty)
such that
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{N})=0
and

\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|u(x,\tau)-\tilde{u}(x,\tau)\right|dx\leq\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|u(x,\rho)-\tilde{u}(x,\rho)\right|dx,

(5.82)

for every
\rho,\tau\in I\setminus\mathcal{N}
with
\rho\leq\tau
.

Proof.

Let
\{R_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of positive numbers which converges to infinity as
j\to\infty
. From Theorem
5.3
, we have for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
a set
\mathcal{N}_{j}\subset(0,N_{j}^{-1}R_{j})
,
N_{j}:=N_{M}(R_{j})
, such that
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{N}_{j})=0
and

\intop_{B_{R_{j}-\tau N_{j}}(0)}\left|u(x,\tau)-\tilde{u}(x,\tau)\right|dx\leq\intop_{B_{R_{j}-\rho N_{j}}(0)}\left|u(x,\rho)-\tilde{u}(x,\rho)\right|dx\leq\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|u(x,\rho)-\tilde{u}(x,\rho)\right|dx,

(5.83)

for every
\rho,\tau\in(0,N_{j}^{-1}R_{j})\setminus\mathcal{N}_{j}
with
\rho\leq\tau
. The right-hand side of (
5.83
) can be equal to
\infty
. We get from the assumption (
5.81
)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\left(R_{j}-\tau N_{j}\right)=\lim_{j\to\infty}R_{j}\left(1-\tau\frac{N_{j}}{R_{j}}\right)=\infty.

(5.84)

Let us define
\mathcal{N}:=\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{N}_{j}
. Note that
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{N})=0
. Let
\rho,\tau\in I\setminus\mathcal{N}
be such that
\rho\leq\tau
. From (
5.81
), we get for every big enough
j
that
0<\rho\leq\tau<N_{j}^{-1}R_{j}
. Therefore, we get from (
5.83
)

\intop_{B_{R_{j}-\tau N_{j}}(0)}\left|u(x,\tau)-\tilde{u}(x,\tau)\right|dx\leq\intop_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|u(x,\rho)-\tilde{u}(x,\rho)\right|dx.

(5.85)

Taking the limit as
j\to\infty
in (
5.85
) and taking into account (
5.84
), we obtain (
5.82
). Note that we do not assume global integrability of
u,\tilde{u}
.
∎

Corollary 5.5

(Uniqueness of entropy solutions)
.

Let
u
,
\tilde{u}
be two entropy solutions of
(
4.1
)

with the same initial data
u_{0}
. Let
M
be as in (
5.50
); for
R\in(0,\infty)
we define
N:=N_{M}(R)
as in (
5.51
). Assume that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{N}{R}=0.

(5.86)

Then,
u=\tilde{u}
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
.

Proof.

By the contraction property, Theorem
5.3
, there exists a set
\mathcal{N}\subset(0,N^{-1}R)
such that
\mathcal{L}^{1}(\mathcal{N})=0
and for
\rho,\tau\in(0,N^{-1}R)\setminus\mathcal{N}
,
\rho\leq\tau
, we have

\intop_{B_{R}(0)}|u(x,\rho)-u_{0}(x)|dx+\intop_{B_{R}(0)}|\tilde{u}(x,\rho)-u_{0}(x)|dx\\
\geq\intop_{B_{R}(0)}|u(x,\rho)-\tilde{u}(x,\rho)|dx\geq\intop_{B_{R-\rho N}(0)}|u(x,\rho)-\tilde{u}(x,\rho)|dx\geq\intop_{B_{R-\tau N}(0)}|u(x,\tau)-\tilde{u}(x,\tau)|dx.

(5.87)

Therefore, for every
\tau\in(0,N^{-1}R)\setminus\mathcal{N}
, by property (
4.7
) (the assumption about the initial data
u_{0}
in Definition
4.3
), we get from (
5.87
)

\intop_{B_{R-\tau N}(0)}|u(x,\tau)-\tilde{u}(x,\tau)|dx=0.

(5.88)

Hence

\intop_{0}^{N^{-1}R}\intop_{B_{R-\tau N}(0)}|u(x,\tau)-\tilde{u}(x,\tau)|dxd\tau=0.

(5.89)

Therefore, from (
5.89
) we conclude that
u=\tilde{u}
almost everywhere in

\mathcal{K}_{R}:=\left\{{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I}\,\middle|\,{t\in{(0,N^{-1}R)},\,\,x\in B_{R-tN}(0)}\right\}.

(5.90)

Let us prove that

\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I=\bigcup_{R\in(0,\infty)}\mathcal{K}_{R}.

(5.91)

Let
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
. By the assumption that
\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{N}{R}=0
, we get that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{R}{N}=\infty,\quad\lim_{R\to\infty}(R-tN)=\lim_{R\to\infty}R\left(1-t\frac{N}{R}\right)=\infty.

(5.92)

Therefore, there exists sufficiently large
R
such that
t\in(0,N^{-1}R)
and
|x|<R-tN
. Hence
(x,t)\in\mathcal{K}_{R}
. This proves (
5.91
). From (
5.92
) we get the existence of an increasing to
\infty
sequence
\{R_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\infty)
such that the two sequences
\left\{N_{j}^{-1}R_{j}\right\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\{R_{j}-tN_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
are monotonically increasing to
\infty
as
j\to\infty
. Hence, for every
j<i
we have
\mathcal{K}_{R_{j}}\subset\mathcal{K}_{R_{i}}
and from (
5.91
), we obtain

\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{K}_{R_{j}}.

(5.93)

Since
u=\tilde{u}
almost everywhere in each
\mathcal{K}_{R_{j}}
, we get that
u=\tilde{u}
almost everywhere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times I
.
∎

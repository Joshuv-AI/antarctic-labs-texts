A note on L. Zhou’s result on Finsler surfaces with K = 0 K=0 and J = 0 J=0
S. G. Elgendi, Nabil L. Youssef

Abstract
In this note, we show that the examples of non Berwaldian Landsberg surfaces with vanishing flag curvature, obtained in [ 5 ] , are in fact Berwaldian. Consequently, Bryant’s claim is still unverified.

Introduction

In Finsler geometry, the question of existence of a regular Landsberg Finsler metric which is non-Berwladian is still an open problem. In the two-dimensional case, it seems that the problem is more complicated. Although some singular Landsberg Finsler metrics which are non-Berwladian are obtained in higher dimensions (cf.
[
1
,
3
,
4
]
), to the best of our knowledge, nothing is found in dimension two except the examples obtained by L. Zhou
[
5
]
. The main purpose of
[
5
]
was to verify R. Bryant’s claim
[
2
]
: there exist singular Landsberg Finsler surfaces with vanishing flag curvature which are non-Berwaldian. Unfortunately, the examples obtained in
[
5
]
, are in fact Berwaldian as will be shown below.

2
The note

In what follow, we use the notations and terminology of
[
5
]
. Let
(\Omega,F)
be a spherically symmetric Finsler surface in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, where
\Omega
is a domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
F
is defined on
\Omega
by the formula:

F=u\exp\left(\int_{0}^{s}\frac{(c+1)s^{2}-\left(2r^{2}c_{0}-1\right)s\sqrt{r^{2}-s^{2}}-2r^{2}c}{\left(r^{2}-s^{2}\right)\left(\left(2c_{0}r^{2}-1\right)\sqrt{r^{2}-s^{2}}-(c+1)s\right)}ds\right)a(r),

(2.1)

where
c
is a constant,
c_{0}
is a smooth function of
r
and

a(r)=\exp\left(\int-\frac{2c_{0}r^{2}-1+2c^{2}-2c}{r(2c_{0}r^{2}-1)}dr\right),

r=\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}},\quad u=\sqrt{y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}},\quad s=\frac{x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2}}{u},

(x_{1},x_{2})\in\Omega,\quad(x_{1},x_{2};y_{1},y_{2})\in T\Omega\backslash\{0\}.

Moreover, the coefficients
G^{i},i=1,2,
of the geodesic spray of
F
considered in
[
5
]
are

G^{i}=uPy^{i}+u^{2}Qx^{i},

(2.2)

P=f_{1}(r)s+f_{2}(r)\sqrt{r^{2}-s^{2}},\quad Q=c_{0}(r)+c_{2}(r)s^{2}+c_{1}(r)s\sqrt{r^{2}-s^{2}},

(2.3)

where
f_{1},f_{2},c_{1},c_{2}
are smooth functions of
r
.

It has been proven in
[
5
, Theorem 2.7]
that the above surface
(\Omega,F)
is Landsbergian and whenever
f_{2}(r)\neq-\frac{r^{2}c_{1}(r)}{3}
, it is not Berwaldain.

In the following we prove that the coefficients
G^{i}
given by (
2.2
) are quadratic for any choice of the functions
f_{1},\,f_{2},\,c_{0},\,c_{1},\,c_{2}
and, consequently,
(\Omega,F)
is Berwaldian.
To show that
G^{i}
are quadratic, it suffices to show that the function
uP
is of first order in
y
and the function
u^{2}Q
is quadratic in
y
, where
P
and
Q
are given by (
2.3
).

Now, one can see that

us=u\frac{x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2}}{u}=x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2},

(2.4)

r^{2}-s^{2}=x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}-\frac{(x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2})^{2}}{u^{2}}=\frac{(x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1})^{2}}{u^{2}}.

From which we have

u\sqrt{r^{2}-s^{2}}=x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1}.

(2.5)

Moreover, we can show that

\displaystyle u^{2}s\sqrt{r^{2}-s^{2}}=

\displaystyle us(x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2})(x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{1}^{2}y_{1}y_{2}-x_{1}x_{2}y_{1}^{2}+x_{1}x_{2}y_{2}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}y_{1}y_{2}.

(2.6)

Therefore, using (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we get

\displaystyle uP

\displaystyle=f_{1}(r)us+f_{2}(r)u\sqrt{r^{2}-s^{2}}

\displaystyle=f_{1}(r)(x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2})+f_{2}(r)(x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1}).

That is,
uP
is of first order in
y
.

On the other hand, using (
2.4
) and (
2
), we have

\displaystyle u^{2}Q

\displaystyle=c_{0}(r)u^{2}+c_{2}(r)s^{2}u^{2}+c_{1}(r)u^{2}s\sqrt{r^{2}-s^{2}}

\displaystyle=c_{0}(r)(y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2})+c_{2}(r)(x_{1}y_{1}+x_{2}y_{2})^{2}+c_{1}(r)(x_{1}^{2}y_{1}y_{2}-x_{1}x_{2}y_{1}^{2}+x_{1}x_{2}y_{2}^{2}-x_{2}^{2}y_{1}y_{2}).

That is,
u^{2}Q
is quadratic in
y
.

Consequently, the spray coefficients given by (
2.2
) are quadratic and hence
(\Omega,F)
is Berwaldian,
contrary to Theorem 2.7 of
[
5
]
.

Now, the question is how to interpret these conflicting results? In the following, we will try to provide an answer. The proof of
[
5
, Theorem 2.7]
is based mainly on
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
and the problem resides in fact in this proposition. In
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
, it has been shown that (in any dimension) the condition

(n+1)(P-sP_{s})+(r^{2}-s^{2})(Q_{s}-sQ_{ss})=0,

(2.7)

where
P
and
Q
are given in
[
5
]
and
r=|x|,u=|y|,s={\langle x,y\rangle}/|y|
,
is a necessary and sufficient condition for the vanishing of the mean Berwald curvature
E_{ij}
. However, we claim that,
in dimension two
, condition (
2.7
) is only sufficient but not necessary, and this is the essence of the resulting contradiction. Indeed, the mean Berwald curvature
E_{ij}
has the expression
[
5
]
:

\displaystyle E_{ij}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{ij}}{u}((n+1)(P-sP_{s})+(r^{2}-s^{2})(Q_{s}-sQ_{ss}))

\displaystyle+\frac{y_{i}y_{j}}{u^{3}}((n+1)(s^{2}P_{ss}+sP_{s}-P)+r^{2}(s^{2}Q_{sss}+sQ_{ss}-Q_{s})+3s^{2}Q_{s}-3s^{3}Q_{ss}-s^{4}Q_{sss})

\displaystyle+\frac{x_{i}x_{j}}{u}((n+1)P_{ss}+2(Q_{s}-sQ_{ss})+(r^{2}-s^{2})Q_{sss})

\displaystyle-\frac{s(x_{i}y_{j}+x_{j}y_{i})}{u^{2}}((n+1)P_{ss}+2(Q_{s}-sQ_{ss})+(r^{2}-s^{2})Q_{sss}),

where, for simplicity, we denote the position arguments by
x_{i}
and the direction arguments by
y_{i}
.
We rewrite
E_{ij}
as follows:

E_{ij}=\frac{\delta_{ij}}{u}H-\frac{y_{i}y_{j}}{u^{3}}(sH_{s}+H)+\frac{s(x_{i}y_{j}+x_{j}y_{i})-ux_{i}x_{j}}{su^{2}}H_{s},

(2.8)

where
H
denotes the LHS of (
2.7
):

H:=(n+1)(P-sP_{s})+(r^{2}-s^{2})(Q_{s}-sQ_{ss}).

Now,
in dimension two
, for the following choice of
P
and
Q
, cf. Equation (
2.3
),

P=f_{1}(r)s+f_{2}(r)\sqrt{r^{2}-s^{2}},\quad Q=c_{0}(r)+c_{2}(r)s^{2}+c_{1}(r)s\sqrt{r^{2}-s^{2}},

we obtain

H=\frac{(3f_{2}(r)+c_{1}(r))r^{2}}{\sqrt{r^{2}-s^{2}}},\quad H_{s}=\frac{(3f_{2}(r)+c_{1}(r))sr^{2}}{(r^{2}-s^{2})^{3/2}},

sH_{s}+H=\frac{(3f_{2}(r)+c_{1}(r))r^{4}}{(r^{2}-s^{2})^{3/2}}.

Substituting the above equations into (
2.8
), we get

E_{ij}=\frac{(3f_{2}(r)+c_{1}(r))r^{2}}{u^{3}({r^{2}-s^{2}})^{3/2}}\left(\delta_{ij}u^{2}(r^{2}-s^{2})-r^{2}y_{i}y_{j}+su(x_{i}y_{j}+x_{j}y_{i})-u^{2}x_{i}x_{j}\right).

(2.9)

Using the formula (
2.9
), the components
E_{ij},\,i,j\in\{1,2\}
, are calculated as follows:

\displaystyle E_{11}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(3f_{2}(r)+c_{1}(r))r^{2}}{u^{3}({r^{2}-s^{2}})^{3/2}}\left(r^{2}u^{2}-s^{2}u^{2}-r^{2}y_{1}^{2}+2sux_{1}y_{1}-u^{2}x_{1}^{2}\right)=0,

\displaystyle E_{22}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(3f_{2}(r)+c_{1}(r))r^{2}}{u^{3}({r^{2}-s^{2}})^{3/2}}\left(r^{2}u^{2}-s^{2}u^{2}-r^{2}y_{2}^{2}+2sux_{2}y_{2}-u^{2}x_{2}^{2}\right)=0,

\displaystyle E_{12}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(3f_{2}(r)+c_{1}(r))r^{2}}{u^{3}({r^{2}-s^{2}})^{3/2}}\left(-r^{2}y_{1}y_{2}+su(x_{1}y_{2}+x_{2}y_{1})-u^{2}x_{1}x_{2}\right)=0.

Consequently, the mean Berwald curvature
E_{ij}
vanishes for all
i,j\in\{1,2\}
, even though the function
H
does not vanish. In conclusion,
condition (
2.7
) is sufficient but not necessary in dimension two.

3
Concluding remarks

We conclude the present note by the following comments and remarks:

•

L. Zhou in
[
5
]
gave an example of a non-Bewaldian Landsbergian surface
[
5
, Theorem 2.7]
, where he assumed that the geodesic spray coefficients
G^{i}
are given by (
2.2
) and (
2.3
). In this note we have proved that Zhou’s example is in fact Berwaldian, contrary to
[
5
, Theorem 2.7]
. This has been achieved by showing that the coefficients
G^{i}
are quadratic for any choice of the functions
f_{1},f_{2},c_{0},c_{1},c_{2}
.

•

Decoding the puzzle: The proof of
[
5
, Theorem 2.7]
, in which Zhou’s example is constructed, depends upon the necessity of

[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
. The later asserts that condition (
2.7
) is a necessary and sufficient condition for the vanishing of the mean Berwald curvature. Nevertheless, we have proved that, in dimension 2, this condition is not necessary. Hence,
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
is not valid in dimension 2 and consequently
[
5
, Theorem 2.7]
is not true.

•

As a consequence of the preceding point,
[
5
, Theorem 3.3]
is also not true because it is based on the non-true
[
5
, Theorem 2.7]
. Note that the restriction in Theorem 3.3 on the constant
c
(
c\neq 1/3
) has been imposed for one to be able to apply Theorem 2.7
(
f_{2}(r)\neq-r^{2}c_{1}(r)/3
).

•

Bryant’s claim
[
2
]
(there does exist singular Landsberg Finsler surface with a vanishing flag curvature which is not Berwaldian) remains unverified.

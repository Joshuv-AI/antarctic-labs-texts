Integral means of solutions of the one-dimensional Poisson equation with Robin boundary conditions
Christos Papadimitriou

Abstract
. Consider the one-dimentional Poisson equation − u ′′ = f -u^{\prime\prime}=f on the interval [ − π , π ] [-\pi,\pi] , where f f is an non-negative integrable function, with Robin boundary conditions − u ′ ​ ( − π ) + α ​ u ​ ( − π ) = u ′ ​ ( π ) + α ​ u ​ ( π ) = 0 -u^{\prime}(-\pi)+\alpha u(-\pi)=u^{\prime}(\pi)+\alpha u(\pi)=0 , where α > 0 \alpha>0 is a constant. In this paper we prove inequalities for the convex integral means of solutions of this problem, using the polarization of functions and its properties. We find solutions with maximal convex integral means and prove their uniqueness.

1.
Introduction

Imagine a metal rod or a wire of length
2\pi
that is heated through by an external heat source with distribution
f\in L^{1}[-\pi,\pi]
. We allow the rod to interact with its environment by submerging its ends in liquid or gas with temperature zero and letting heat to be dissipated via Newton’s law of cooling, i.e. the heat flux at the end-points is proportional to their temperature. The mathematical formulation of this law is achieved by introducing Robin boundary conditions to our setting. Therefore, we are interested in the temperature function
u_{f}
of the steady-state of the rod, meaning that the temperature of the rod is independent of the time variable.

Putting all this into equations, we have that
u_{f}
has to satisfy the Poisson equation

(1.1)

-u^{\prime\prime}=f\text{ on }[-\pi,\pi]

with Robin boundary conditions

(1.2)

-u^{\prime}(-\pi)+\alpha u(-\pi)=u^{\prime}(\pi)+\alpha u(\pi)=0,

for some constant
\alpha>0
(which might depend on the material of the rod and other parameters).

Recently, J. J. Langford and P. McDonald
[
7
]
, studying this problem, provided an explicit formula for the temperature
u_{f}
of the solution (see Proposition
2.2
). They were also interested in the convex integral means and the
L^{p}
-norms of
u_{f}
and how these quantities compare with the corresponding ones of
u_{f^{\#}}
, where
f^{\#}
is the symmetric decreasing rearrangement of
f
(see Definition
2.4
). To this end, they proved a comparison principle (
[
7
, Theorem 1.3]
) considering
u_{f}
and
u_{f^{\#}}
.
Related research has been contacted considering other extremal problems in our setting. In
[
2
]
, for example, D. Betsakos and A. Solynin studied the temperature gap or oscillation

\operatorname{osc}(u_{f})=\max_{[-\pi,\pi]}u_{f}(x)-\min_{[-\pi,\pi]}u_{f}(x)

of
u_{f}
. Already in
[
7
]
, it was observed that among all rearrangements of the heat source
f
(see Subsection 2.2 for more on rearrangements), this quantity is not maximized by
u_{f^{\#}}
. D. Betsakos and A. Solynin
[
2
]
determined the exact rearrangement of
f
that yields the maximal temperature gap for a special class of heat sources.
In the present paper, we investigate how the convex integral means and the
L^{p}
-norms of
u_{f}
are affected under rearrangements of the heat source
f
such as the s.d.r
f^{\#}
but also its polarizations
f_{H}
(see Definition
3.1
). Let us now present the main results of this paper.

Theorem 1.1

(Polarization and convex integral means)
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
0\leq f\in L^{1}[-\pi,\pi]
and
u_{f}
be the solution to the Robin problem
(\ref{Poisson})
,
(\ref{Robin})
. Let
b\in(-\pi,0)\cup(0,\pi)
. Let
f_{H}
be the polarization of
f
w.r.t.
b
, and let
u_{f_{H}}
be the solution to the Robin problem with heat source
f_{H}
. Then

(1.3)

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\phi\bigl(u_{f}(x)\bigr)dx\leq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\phi\bigl(u_{f_{H}}(x)\bigr)dx

for any convex and increasing function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}.

Furthermore, if
\phi
is strictly increasing, then
(\ref{ineq polar})
holds as an equality if and only if
f=f_{H}
a.e. on
[-\pi,\pi]
.

In particular, for
\phi(\cdot)=|\cdot|^{p},
where
p\geq 1
, we get inequalities for the
L^{p}
-norms of
u_{f}
and
u_{f_{H}}
. Letting
p\to+\infty
we get the inequality for their
L^{\infty}
-norms, i.e.

(1.4)

||u_{f}||_{L^{p}}\leq||u_{f_{H}}||_{L^{p}}\text{, for }1\leq p\leq+\infty.

Theorem 1.2

(S.d.r. and convex integral means)
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
0\leq f\in L^{1}[-\pi,\pi]
and
u_{f}
be the solution to the Robin problem
(\ref{Poisson})
,
(\ref{Robin})
. Let
f^{\#}
be the symmetric decreasing rearrangement of
f
and
u_{f^{\#}}
the solution to the Robin problem with heat source the function
f^{\#}
, then

(1.5)

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\phi\bigl(u_{f}(x)\bigr)dx\leq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\phi\bigl(u_{f^{\#}}(x)\bigr)dx

for any convex and increasing function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}.

If
\phi
is strictly increasing, then
(\ref{ineq sdr})
holds as an equality if and only if
f=f^{\#}
a.e. on
[-\pi,\pi]
.

Again,
(\ref{ineq sdr})
implies that

(1.6)

||u_{f}||_{L^{p}}\leq||u_{f^{\#}}||_{L^{p}}\text{, for }1\leq p\leq+\infty.

Note that the inequality
(\ref{ineq sdr})
is the previously mentioned comparison principle of J. J. Langford and P. McDonald. They achieved this result using Baernstein’s
\star
-function. They did not investigate the existence of conditions for when the equality is attained.

Using our method, we get the precise condition on when
(\ref{ineq sdr})
is attained as an equality and we gain the intermediate Theorem
1.1
, and, therefore, we can interpolate the inequality
(\ref{ineq sdr})
as so

\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\phi\bigl(u_{f}(x)\bigr)dx\leq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\phi\bigl(u_{f_{H}}(x)\bigr)dx\leq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\phi\bigl(u_{f^{\#}}(x)\bigr)dx.

Additionally, we obtain the inequality

||u_{f}||_{L^{\infty}}\leq||u_{f^{\#}}||_{L^{\infty}},

not as just a consequence of Theorem
1.2
, but also in a more straightforward way (see Theorem
4.3
), and we provide a condition on when it holds as an equality (something that cannot be deduced by the Theorem
1.2
itself).

The paper is structured as follows: In Section 2, we present essential properties of
u_{f}
, we provide the precise definition of s.d.r., some properties of s.d.r and rearrangements in general. Section 3 is dedicated to the polarization. In Section 4, we have two Hardy-Littlewood inequalities and the previously mentioned Theorem
4.3
about the
L^{\infty}
-norms. Finally, the proofs of our main theorems are given in Section 5.

Definition 2.4

(Decreasing and Symmetric Decreasing Rearrangements in one dimension)
.

Let
X
be a Lebesgue measurable subset of the real line and
f\in L^{1}(X)
such that the Lebesgue measure of the set
\{x\in X:f(x)>t\}
is finite for all
\displaystyle t>\essinf_{X}f
. For
\lambda(A)
be the Lebesgue measure of a set
A
. We define
f^{\ast}:[0,\lambda(X)]\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
as

f^{\ast}(t)=\begin{cases}\esssup_{X}f,&t=0,\\
\inf\bigl\{s\in\mathbb{R}:\lambda\bigl(\{x\in X:f(x)>s\}\bigr)\leq t\bigr\},&t\in(0,\lambda(X)),\\
\essinf_{X}f,&t=\lambda(X).\end{cases}

The function
f^{\ast}
is called the decreasing rearrangement of
f
.

We also define
f^{\#}:[-\frac{1}{2}\lambda(X),\frac{1}{2}\lambda(X)]\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
the symmetric decreasing rearrangement of
f
, given by
f^{\#}(t)=f^{\ast}(2|t|).

Without getting into much detail, we will call two integrable functions
f:X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and
g:Y\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
such that the sets
\{x\in X:f(x)>t\}
and
\{y\in Y:g(y)>s\}
have finite Lebesgue measure for all
\displaystyle t>\essinf_{X}f
and for all
\displaystyle s>\essinf_{Y}g
respectively,
equidistributed (with respect to the Lebesgue measure)
or
rearrangements of each other
if
\lambda(X)=\lambda(Y)
and
f^{\ast}=g^{\ast}
on
[0,\lambda(X)]
(and
f^{\#}=g^{\#}
on
[-\frac{1}{2}\lambda(X),\frac{1}{2}\lambda(X)]
).

Let us present some of the properties of the decreasing and the symmetric decreasing rearrangements. The following proposition combines results found throughout the first chapter of

Definition 3.1
.

Let
b\in(-\pi,0)\cup(0,\pi)
and
H
be the open half-line of

.
Let

•

If

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

. Then

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(y)g(y)dy\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f_{H}(y)g_{H}(y)dy.

Equality holds if and only if the set

A_{H}=B_{H}\cup C_{H}\text{ has zero Lebesgue measure,}

B_{H}=\{y\in H:f(y)<f(\overline{y})\text{ and }g(y)>g(\overline{y})\}

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(y)g(y)dy\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f^{\#}(y)g^{\#}(y)dy.

Equality holds if and only if the set

A=\bigl\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:f(x)<f(y)\text{ and }g(x)>g(y)\bigr\}

has zero two-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

\displaystyle||u_{f}||_{\infty}

\displaystyle=u_{f}(x_{0})=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}G(x_{0},y)f(y)dy=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}G_{x_{0}}(y)f(y)dy

\displaystyle\overset{(1)}{\leq}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(G_{x_{0}})_{H}(y)f_{H}(y)dy\overset{(2)}{\leq}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bigl((G_{x_{0}})_{H}\bigr)^{\#}(y)(f_{H})^{\#}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(G_{x_{0}})^{\#}(y)f^{\#}(y)dy=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(G_{x_{0}})^{\#}(y)f^{\#}(y)dy

where

is any open half-line in

,

and
2.6

Now, quite trivially, if

Conversely, assume that

If

2.6

H

is not a.e. equal to
f
. Let us compute the set

mentioned

. Since the function
G(0,y)=-\dfrac{1}{2}|y|+\dfrac{1}{2c_{\alpha}}
is its own symmetric decreasing rearrangement, it is easy to see that
G(0,y)>G(0,\overline{y})
for all
y\in H
. Thus

\displaystyle B_{H}

\displaystyle=\{y\in H:f(y)<f(\overline{y})\text{ and }G(0,y)>G(0,\overline{y})\}

\displaystyle=\{y\in H:f(y)<f(\overline{y})\}

and

C_{H}=\{y\in H:f(y)>f(\overline{y})\text{ and }G(0,y)<G(0,\overline{y})\}=\varnothing,

so
A_{H}=\{y\in H:f(y)<f(\overline{y})\}
.
So, the condition that
\lambda(A_{H})=0
turns out to be equivalent to
f(y)\geq f(\overline{y})
almost everywhere on
H
which, in turn, is equivalent to
f
being equal to
f_{H}
almost everywhere on
H.
But, as mentioned above,
f
is not a.e. equal to
f_{H}
, thus
\lambda(A_{H})>0
and, by Proposition
4.1
, (1), and subsequently
(\ref{ineq max})
, holds as a strict inequality.
∎

Lemma 5.1

(Karamata’s inequality)
.

Let
\phi:(a,b)\to\mathbb{R}
be a convex and increasing function. Let
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
and
\{y_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be two n-tuples of real numbers contained in the open interval
(a,b)
such that

(a)

x_{1}\geq x_{2}\geq\dots\geq x_{n}
and
y_{1}\geq y_{2}\geq\dots\geq y_{n}
,

(b)

x_{1}\leq y_{1}
,

(c)

x_{1}+x_{2}+\dots+x_{k}\leq y_{1}+y_{2}+\dots+y_{k}
for all
k\in\{2,\dots,n\}
.

Under these assumptions, the following inequality holds

(5.1)

\phi(x_{1})+\phi(x_{2})+\dots+\phi(x_{n})\leq\phi(y_{1})+\phi(y_{2})+\dots+\phi(y_{n}).

If the function
\phi
is strictly convex, then
(\ref{karamata ineq})
holds as an equality if and only if
x_{i}=y_{i}
for all
i=1,2,\dots,n.

Now, we shall present some more properties of Green’s functions, which, in turn, will imply some useful inequalities involving the solutions of Robin’s problems.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\alpha>0
, let
G
be the Green’s function for the problem
(\ref{Poisson})
,
(\ref{Robin})
, let
b\in(-\pi,0)\cup(0,\pi)
. If
b>0
(respectively
b<0
), let
I
be the interval
[b,\pi]
(respectively
[-\pi,b]
). For all
x,y\in I
, the following two inequalities hold

(5.2)

G(x,y)+G(x^{\prime},y)\leq G(x,y^{\prime})+G(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})

and

(5.3)

G(x,y)+G(x,y^{\prime})\leq G(x^{\prime},y)+G(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}),

where
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
are the reflections of
x
and
y
with respect to
b
, i.e.
x^{\prime}=2b-x
and
y^{\prime}=2b-y
.

Proof.

Due to symmetry, we can consider only the case that
b\in(-\pi,0)
. Therefore, we have
I=[-\pi,b]
. Let
x,y\in I
and write down the formulas for
G(x,y)
,
G(x^{\prime},y)
,
G(x,y^{\prime})
and
G(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
. Then, it is obvious that
(\ref{5.2})
is equivalent to

(5.4)

c_{\alpha}xy+|x-y|+c_{\alpha}x^{\prime}y+|x^{\prime}-y|\geq c_{\alpha}xy^{\prime}+|x-y^{\prime}|+c_{\alpha}x^{\prime}y^{\prime}+|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|.

Note that
|x-y|=|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|
and
|x^{\prime}-y|=|x-y^{\prime}|
. Also, the constant
c_{a}
is positive, which ultimately means that the inequality we have to prove is equivalent to

(5.5)

xy+x^{\prime}y\geq xy^{\prime}+x^{\prime}y^{\prime}.

Substituting
x^{\prime}=2b-x
and
y^{\prime}=2b-y
we see that
(\ref{5.5})
is equivalent to
y\leq b
, which is true because
y\in I
.
In the exact same manner, one can prove
(\ref{5.3})
.
∎

This lemma has immediate consequences on the solution of a Robin problem with given heat source
f
and the solution of the Robin problem with heat source any polarization

of

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
0\leq f\in L^{1}[-\pi,\pi]
and
u_{f}
be the solution to the corresponding Robin problem, let
b\in(-\pi,0)\cup(0,\pi)

f_{H}
be the polarization of

b
, and let

be the solution to the Robin problem with heat source
f_{H}

I
be as defined in Lemma

. The following hold:

(a)

If
x\in I
, let
x^{\prime}=2b-x
. Then

(5.6)

u_{f}(x)+u_{f}(x^{\prime})\leq u_{f_{H}}(x)+u_{f_{H}}(x^{\prime}).

(b)

If
x\in I
, let
x^{\prime}=2b-x
. Then

(5.7)

u_{f}(x)\leq u_{f_{H}}(x^{\prime}).

(c)

If
b>0
(respectively
b<0
), let
x\in[-\pi,b]
(respectively
x\in[b,\pi]
). Then

(5.8)

u_{f}(x)\leq u_{f_{H}}(x).

For
b>0
(resp.
b<0
),
(\ref{uf uf polwsh})
holds as an equality for some
x\in[-\pi,b]
(resp.
x\in[b,\pi]
) if and only if it holds as an equality for all
x\in[-\pi,b]
(resp.
x\in[b,\pi]
) if and only if
f=f_{H}
almost everywhere on
[-\pi,\pi]
if and only if
u_{f}\equiv u_{f_{H}}
on
[-\pi,\pi]
.

Proof.

Due to symmetry, we can consider only the case that
b\in(-\pi,0)
.

(a) Let
x\in[-\pi,b]
and
x^{\prime}=2b-x\in[b,2b+\pi].
From
(\ref{5.2})
we have that
G(x,\cdot)+G(x^{\prime},\cdot)
coincides with its polarization with respect to
b
. Thus, by Proposition
4.1
,

\displaystyle u_{f}(x)+u_{f}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\bigl(G(x,y)+G(x^{\prime},y)\bigr)f(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\bigl(G(x,y)+G(x^{\prime},y)\bigr)_{H}f_{H}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\bigl(G(x,y)+G(x^{\prime},y)\bigr)f_{H}(y)dy=u_{f_{H}}(x)+u_{f_{H}}(x^{\prime}).

(b) Let
x\in[-\pi,b]
and
x^{\prime}=2b-x\in[b,2b+\pi].
By the definition of polarization,
f\equiv f_{H}
on
(2b+\pi,\pi]
.
It is quite elementary to show that
G(x,y)\leq G(x^{\prime},y)
for all
y\in[2b+\pi,\pi]
, and thus

\int_{2b+\pi}^{\pi}G(x,y)f(y)dy\leq\int_{2b+\pi}^{\pi}G(x^{\prime},y)f(y)dy=\int_{2b+\pi}^{\pi}G(x^{\prime},y)f_{H}(y)dy.

Directly by the definition of polarization,
f_{H}(y)\leq f_{H}(y^{\prime})
for all
y\in[-\pi,b],

y^{\prime}=2b-y
and either

f_{H}(y)=f(y)\text{ and }f_{H}(y^{\prime})=f(y^{\prime})

or

f_{H}(y)=f(y^{\prime})\text{ and }f_{H}(y^{\prime})=f(y).

Combining these facts with Lemma
5.2
and integrating over
[-\pi,b]
with respect to
y
yields

\int_{-\pi}^{b}\bigl(G(x,y)f(y)+G(x,y^{\prime})f(y^{\prime})\bigr)dy\leq\!\!\int_{-\pi}^{b}\bigl(G(x^{\prime},y)f_{H}(y)+G(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})f_{H}(y^{\prime})\bigr)dy,

which in turn, after a change of variables, gives

\int_{-\pi}^{2b+\pi}G(x,y)f(y)dy\leq\int_{-\pi}^{2b+\pi}G(x^{\prime},y)f_{H}(y)dy.

We have already shown that

\int_{2b+\pi}^{\pi}G(x,y)f(y)dy\leq\int_{2b+\pi}^{\pi}G(x^{\prime},y)f_{H}(y)dy,

and, by adding the two inequalities together, we get that
u_{f}(x)\leq u_{f_{H}}(x^{\prime}).

(c) Let
x\in[b,\pi]
. By Example
3.3
,
G(x,\cdot)\equiv\bigl(G(x,\cdot)\bigr)_{H}
. Thus, by Proposition
4.1
,

(5.9)

\displaystyle u_{f}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}G(x,y)f(y)dy\leq\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\bigl(G(x,y)\bigr)_{H}f_{H}(y)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}G(x,y)f_{H}(y)dy=u_{f_{H}}(x).

We examine the case of
(\ref{apodeiksh karamata uf})
holding as an equality in accordance with the condition for equality on Proposition
4.1
, namely we compute the corresponding set
A_{H}
in our context and determine when its Lebesgue measure is zero. We do not have to show all the equivalences on Lemma
5.3
.(c) since most of them have already been established to be true. It suffices to show that if
(\ref{apodeiksh karamata uf})
holds as an equality for one

, then
f=f_{H}
a.e. on
[-\pi,\pi]
. After some simplifications, namely that
f
and
G(x,\cdot)
are identically zero outside of
[-\pi,\pi]
and that
G(x,\cdot)
is its own polarization with respect to

A_{H}=\{y\in(b,2b+\pi]:f(y)<f(y^{\prime})\}

almost everywhere on

and

coincide almost everywhere on
[-\pi,\pi]
.
∎

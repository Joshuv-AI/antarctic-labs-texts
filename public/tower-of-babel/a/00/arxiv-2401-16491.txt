Higher order Tsirelson Spaces and their modified versions are isomorphic Thanks: The work was supported in part by the grant DMS-2054443 from the National Science Foundation.
Hùng Việt Chu, Thomas Schlumprecht

Abstract
. We prove that for every countable ordinal ξ \xi , the Tsirelson’s space T ξ T_{\xi} of order ξ \xi , is naturally, i.e., via the identity, 3 3 -isomorphc to its modified version. For the first step, we prove that the Schreier family 𝒮 ξ \mathcal{S}_{\xi} is the same as its modified version 𝒮 ξ M \mathcal{S}^{M}_{\xi} , thus answering a question by Argyros and Tolias. As an application, we show that the algebra of linear bounded operators on T ξ T_{\xi} has 2 𝔠 2^{\mathfrak{c}} closed ideals.

1.
Introduction

In 1974, Tsirelson
[
17
]
constructed the first known space that does not contain any isomorphic copies of
\ell_{p}
,
1\leqslant p<\infty
, or
c_{0}
. Soon later,
Figiel and Johnson
[
8
]
introduced the dual of Tsirelson’s original space, and showed that the
p
-convexification of it is a uniformly convex Banach space, not containing any
\ell_{q}
,

1<q<\infty
. Nowadays the space defined by Figiel and Johnson is called
Tsirelson space
, and denoted by
T
, while
Tsirelson’s original space is often denoted by
T^{*}
.

In 1976, Johnson
[
11
]
defined a modified version
T^{M}
of
T
, nowadays called the
modified Tsirelson space
, which also does not contain
\ell_{p}
,
1\leqslant p<\infty
, or
c_{0}
, but has the additional property that for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there is a co-finite dimensional subspace
Y
in which every
m
-dimensional subspace is 2-isomorphic to a subspace of a finite-dimensional subspace

F
of
Y
which is
2
-isomorphic to
\ell_{1}^{\mbox{dim}(F)}
.
Later, Casazza and Odell
[
7
]
showed that the norms on
T^{M}
and
T
are actually equivalent.

The space
T
is a reflexive space with a
1
-unconditional basis
(e_{i})
, which has the property that the averages of any normalized block basis are not converging in norm to zero. It is therefore a reflexive example of a space in which a weakly null sequence may not have Cesáro summing subsequences. In
[
1
]
, Alspach and Argyros introduced
iterated averages
of order
\xi
,
\xi
being a countable ordinal, and constructed reflexive spaces
T_{\xi}
for which
the iterated averages of order
\xi
do not converge to
0
. We refer to these spaces as
higher order Tsirelson spaces
.
As in the base case, we can also define their modified version. The main goal of this paper is to show that
Tsirelson spaces of any order
\xi<\omega_{1}
are isomorphic (via the identity) to their modified version. This extends
a result by Manoussakis
[
14
]
who proved this result for finite ordinals
\xi
.

Our paper is organized as follows: in Section 2, we recall Schreier families
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
of order
\xi<\omega_{1}
, as well as their modified version
\mathcal{S}^{M}_{\xi}
,
\omega_{1}
denoting the first uncountable ordinal. The main goal of Section 3, is to show the equality of

\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
and
\mathcal{S}^{M}_{\xi}
.
In Section 4, we recall the definition of Tsirelson spaces
T_{\xi}
of order
\xi<\omega_{1}
, and their modified versions
T^{M}_{\xi}
, and it is shown that their norms are equivalent.
Section 5 is concerned with the cardinality of the closed ideals of the algebra of bounded
linear operators on
T_{\xi}
.

2.
Schreier families of higher order

In this section, we recall the definition of
Schreier families
of order
\xi
, where
\xi
is a countable ordinal, and their modified version.
We introduce some notation:
[\mathbb{N}]
,
[\mathbb{N}]^{<\omega}
, and
[\mathbb{N}]^{\omega}
denote the subsets, the finite subsets, and the infinite subsets of the natural numbers
\mathbb{N}
.
For
A\in[\mathbb{N}]^{<\omega}
and
B\in[\mathbb{N}]
, we write
A<B
, if
a<b
for all
a\in A
and
b\in B
.
For
A\in[\mathbb{N}]
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we let

A_{>k}=A\cap\{k+1,k+2,\ldots\}
,
A_{\geqslant k}=A\cap\{k,k+1,k+2,\ldots\}
,
A_{<k}=A\cap\{1,2,\ldots,k-1\}
, and

A_{\leqslant k}=A\cap\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
.
As a matter of convention, we put
\max\emptyset=0
and
\min\emptyset=\infty
,
and thus,
A<\emptyset
and
A>\emptyset
for all
A\in[\mathbb{N}]^{<\omega}
.
For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we write
m\leqslant A
(or
m<A
, respectively), if

m\leqslant\min A
(or
m<\min A
, respectively).

Identifying elements of
[\mathbb{N}]
in the canonical way with
\{0,1\}
-valued functions on
\mathbb{N}
and thus elements of
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{N}}
, we equip
[\mathbb{N}]
with the product
topology of the discrete topology on
\{0,1\}
.
We say that
\mathcal{F}\subset[\mathbb{N}]^{<\omega}
is
compact
if it is compact in that product
topology.
We say that
\mathcal{F}\subset[\mathbb{N}]^{<\omega}
is
hereditary
if
A\in\mathcal{F}
and
B\subset A
imply that
B\in\mathcal{F}
.
Given
a_{1}<\cdots<a_{n},b_{1}<\cdots<b_{n}
in
\mathbb{N}
, we say that
\{b_{1},\dots,b_{n}\}

is
a spread
of
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
if
a_{i}\leqslant b_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. A family
\mathcal{F}\subset[\mathbb{N}]^{<\omega}
is called

spreading
if every spread of every element of
\mathcal{F}
is also in

\mathcal{F}
. A family that is compact, hereditary, and spreading is said to be
regular
.

We now define the Schreier family
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}\subset[\mathbb{N}]^{<\omega}
for each
\xi<\omega_{1}
by transfinite induction. Particularly,

\mathcal{S}_{0}\ :=\ \{\{n\}\,:\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}\cup\{\emptyset\}.

Assuming that
\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}
has been defined for all
\gamma<\xi
. If
\xi=\gamma+1
, we let

\mathcal{S}_{\xi}\ =\ \left\{\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}\,:\,n\leqslant E_{1}<E_{2}<\cdots<E_{n},E_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma},i=1,2,\ldots,n\right\}.

(2.1)

If
\xi
is a limit ordinal, we choose a fixed sequence of successor ordinals
(\alpha(\xi,n))_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset[1,\xi)
, which increases to
\xi
and put

\mathcal{S}_{\xi}\ =\ \{E:\exists n\ \leqslant\ E\mbox{ with }E\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,n)}\}.

(2.2)

The sequence
(\alpha(\xi,n))_{n=1}^{\infty}
is called a
\xi
-approximating sequence. As needed later in Section
5
,
it follows by transfinite induction that
\mathcal{S}_{1}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
for all
\xi<\omega_{1}
. Furthermore, we can and will later put additional conditions on
(\alpha(\xi,n))_{n=1}^{\infty}
.

The standard transfinite induction gives that the families
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
, for
\xi<\omega_{1}
, are regular, and they are almost increasing in the following sense: for all ordinals
\gamma<\xi
,

\mbox{ there is an }m\in\mathbb{N}\mbox{ so that if }A\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}\mbox{ and }\min A\geqslant m,\mbox{ then }A\in\mathcal{S}_{\xi}.

(2.3)

In the case of a limit ordinal
\xi
, Property (
2.3
) enables us to require the following additional property on a
\xi
-approximating sequence
(\alpha(\xi,n))_{n=1}^{\infty}
:

\displaystyle\alpha(\xi,1)=1,

\displaystyle\text{and for all }n\in\mathbb{N},\text{ if }A,B\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,n)}\mbox{ with }1<A<B,\mbox{ then }A\cup B\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,n+1)}.

(2.4)

Indeed, suppose that we have a limit ordinal
\xi
and have chosen approximating sequences
(\alpha(\gamma,n))_{n=1}^{\infty}
for all limit ordinals
\gamma<\xi
. We first choose an arbitrary
\xi
-approximating sequence
(\alpha^{\prime}(\xi,n))_{n=1}^{\infty}
. We shall build a new
\xi
-approximating sequence
(\alpha(\xi,n))_{n=1}^{\infty}
from
(\alpha^{\prime}(\xi,n))_{n=1}^{\infty}

be choosing
\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}<\ell_{3}<\cdots
recursively so that

\{A\cup B:A,B\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}(\xi,n)+\ell_{n}},1<A<B\}\ \subset\ \mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}(\xi,n+1)+\ell_{n+1}},

and then putting
\alpha(\xi,n)=\alpha^{\prime}(\xi,n)+\ell_{1}
.

Set
\ell_{1}=0
. Assume that
\ell_{n}
has been defined. Consider
\alpha^{\prime}(\xi,n+1)+\ell_{n}>\alpha^{\prime}(\xi,n)+\ell_{n}=\alpha(\xi,n)
. By (
2.3
), there exists
k_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
A_{\geqslant k_{n}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}(\xi,n+1)+\ell_{n}}
, for any
A\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,n)}
.
Thus for any two sets
1<A<B
in
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,n)}=\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}(\xi,n)+\ell_{n}}

it follows that
U:=A_{\geqslant k_{n}}\cup B_{\geqslant k_{n}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}(\xi,n+1)+\ell_{n}+1}
. By (
2.1
),
\{2,3,\ldots,k_{n}-1\}\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}(\xi,n+1)+k_{n}-3}
; hence,

V:=A_{<k_{n}}\cup B_{<k_{n}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}(\xi,n+1)+k_{n}-3}
. Since
V<U
are in
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}(\xi,n+1)+\max\{k_{n}-3,\ell_{n}+1\}}
, we know that
U\cup V\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha^{\prime}(\xi,n+1)+\max\{k_{n}-2,\ell_{n}+2\}}
. Therefore, if we set
\ell_{n+1}=\max\{k_{n}-2,\ell_{n}+1\}+1
and
\alpha(\xi,n+1)=\alpha^{\prime}(\xi,n+1)+\ell_{n+1}
. The new
\xi
-approximating sequence
(\alpha(\xi,n))_{n=1}^{\infty}
increases to
\xi
and satisfies
(
2
)
.

As a consequence of (
2
), for a
\xi
-approximating sequence
(\alpha(\xi,n))_{n=1}^{\infty}
, we have

{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha(\xi,m)}\subset{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha(\xi,n)}
whenever
m\leqslant n
, which, in combination with (
2.2
), gives a neat way to write
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
as

\mathcal{S}_{\xi}\ =\ \{E\,:\,\exists n\leqslant\min E\mbox{ with }E\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,n)}\}\ =\ \{E\,:\,E\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min E)}\}.

(2.5)

For
\xi<\omega_{1}
, we denote by MAX
(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
the maximal elements of
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
. Since
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
is compact, every element of
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
is a subset of some maximal element of
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
(see
[
5
, Section 2]
for details).

Proposition 2.1
.

If
0<\xi=\gamma+1<\omega_{1}
and
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
, then there are unique elements
A_{i}\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}),i=1,2,\ldots,\min A
, so that

A_{1}\ <\ A_{2}\ <\ \cdots\ <\ A_{\min A}\mbox{ and }A\ =\ \cup_{i=1}^{\min A}A_{i}.

Proof.

Pick
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
. By definition, we can write
A=\cup_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}
, for
n\leqslant A_{1}<\cdots<A_{n}
, and
A_{i}
are nonempty sets in
\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}
. We shall show that
n=\min A
, and each
A_{i}
is in
\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\gamma})
.

Observe that
\min A_{1}=\min A
, so
n\leqslant\min A
. If
n<\min A
, then find some
m>A_{n}
to have

n+1\ \leqslant\ A_{1}<\cdots<A_{n}<A_{n+1}\ :=\ \{m\}

By definition, the set
A^{\prime}=\cup_{i=1}^{n+1}A_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
and
A\subsetneq A^{\prime}
, which contradicts the maximality of
A
. Hence,
n=\min A
.

We claim that every
A_{i}
is in
\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\gamma})
. Otherwise, let
j
be the smallest such that
A_{j}\notin\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\gamma})
. If
j=\min A
, then form
A^{\prime}_{\min A}:=A_{\min A}\cup\{m\}\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}
, for some
m>A
. Then the set
\cup_{i=1}^{j-1}A_{i}\cup A^{\prime}_{\min A}
is in
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
, again contradicting the maximality of
A
. If
j<\min A
, we form

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{j}\ :=\ A_{j}\cup\{\min A_{j+1}\}

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{i}\ :=\ (A_{i}\backslash\{\min A_{i}\})\cup\{\min A_{i+1}\}\mbox{ for }j\ <\ i\ \leqslant\ \min A-1,\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{\min A}\ :=\ (A_{\min A}\backslash\{\min A_{\min A}\})\cup\{m\},\mbox{ for some }m>A.

Clearly,
A^{\prime}_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}
for
i=j,\ldots,\min A
. Hence,

\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{j-1}A_{i}\right)\cup\left(\bigcup_{i=j}^{\min A}A^{\prime}_{i}\right)\ \in\ \mathcal{S}_{\xi},

contradicting the maximality of
A
.

Finally, the uniqueness of the decomposition is obvious. Indeed, suppose that
A=\cup_{i=1}^{\min A}A_{i}=\cup_{i=1}^{\min A}A^{\prime}_{i}
, where
A_{1}<\cdots<A_{\min A}\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\gamma})
and
A^{\prime}_{1}<\cdots<A^{\prime}_{\min A}\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\gamma})
. Let
j
be the smallest (if exists) such that
A_{j}\neq A^{\prime}_{j}
. Then either
A_{j}\subsetneq A^{\prime}_{j}
or
A^{\prime}_{j}\subsetneq A_{j}
. Either way violates that both
A_{j}
and
A_{j}^{\prime}
are in MAX
(\mathcal{S}_{\gamma})
. Therefore,
A_{j}=A_{j}^{\prime}
for
j=1,\ldots,\min A
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

If
\xi<\omega_{1}
is a limit ordinal, then
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
if and only if
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min A)})
.

Proof.

Let
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
. By (
2.5
),
A\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min A)}
. If
A\notin\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min A)})
, then form
A^{\prime}:=A\cup\{\max A+1\}
, which must still be in
\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min A)}=\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min A^{\prime})}
. Hence,
A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
, contradicting the maximality of
A
.

Conversely, let
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min A)})
. Then
A\in\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
. If
A\notin\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
, then
A^{\prime}:=A\cup\{\max A+1\}\in\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
. By the definition of
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
,
A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min A^{\prime})}=\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min A)}
, thus contradicting the maximality of
A
.
∎

It is convenient to define a
\xi
-approximating sequence
(\alpha(\xi,n))_{n=1}^{\infty}
also for a successor ordinal
\xi<\omega_{1}
by simply letting
\alpha(\xi,n)=\xi
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, it follows that
A\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min A)}
for
A\in\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
(whether
\xi
is a limit ordinal or not), and we have the following unified recursive definition of
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
:

\mathcal{S}_{\xi}\ =\ \left\{\bigcup_{i=1}^{\min A_{1}}A_{i}\,:\,A_{1}<\cdots<A_{\min A_{1}}\mbox{ and }A_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min A_{1})-1},i=1,\ldots,\min A_{1}\right\},

where
\gamma-1
is the predecessor for a sucessor ordinal
\gamma
. Moreover, if
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
, then this decomposition into increasing sets
A_{i}\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min A_{1})-1})
is unique.

We conclude this section by introducing the
modified Schreier families
, which are defined using transfinite induction for all
\xi<\omega_{1}
, as follows

\mathcal{S}_{0}^{M}\ =\ \mathcal{S}_{0}\ =\ \{\{n\}\,:\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}\cup\{\emptyset\}.

Assuming that
\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}^{M}
has been defined for all
\gamma<\xi
. If
\xi=\gamma+1
, we let

\mathcal{S}_{\xi}^{M}=\left\{\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}\,:\,\mbox{sets }E_{i}\mbox{'s are pairwise disjoint, }E_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}^{M},\mbox{ and }E_{i}\geqslant n,i=1,\ldots,n\right\}.

If
\xi
is a limit ordinal, we choose a fixed sequence
(\alpha(\xi,n):n\in\mathbb{N})\subset[1,\xi)
which increases to
\varepsilon
and put

\mathcal{S}^{M}_{\xi}=\{E\,:\,\exists n\ \leqslant\ \min E\mbox{ with }E\in\mathcal{S}^{M}_{\alpha(\xi,n)}\}.

3.
Equality of the families
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
and
\mathcal{S}^{M}_{\xi}
,
\xi<\omega_{1}

In this section, we answer a question stated in
[
3
, pp. 16]
by showing that for any
\xi<\omega_{1}
, the Schreier family
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
of order
\xi<\omega_{1}
coincides with its modified version
\mathcal{S}^{M}_{\xi}
. This result was independently obtained in
[
6
]
.
We would like to give here a different, self-contained proof and lay the foundation for the next section, where we prove that the spaces
T_{\xi}
and
T^{M}_{\xi}
are naturally (via the formal identity) isomorphic. More precisely, we will prove Theorem
3.4
, from which, it will easily follow that
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}=\mathcal{S}_{\xi}^{M}
, for
\xi<\omega_{1}
. Theorem
3.4
will also be used in Section
4
.

Definition 3.1

(The
\xi
-analysis of maximal
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
sets)
.

Given
\xi<\omega_{1}
, we define recursively the
\xi
-analysis
T(\xi,A)
of a maximal
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
set
A
or
the
\xi
-analysis tree of
A
, for short. We also define for any
D\in T(\xi,A)
, the
\xi
-order of
D
inside
A
, denoted by
\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(D)
, which is an ordinal not larger than
\xi
.

If
\xi=0
and
A=\{a\}\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{0})
, put
T(0,A)=\{A\}
and order
{}_{\xi,A}(A)=0
.
Assume that
T(\gamma,A)
has been defined for all
\gamma<\xi
, and
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\gamma})
, and for all
D\in T(\gamma,A)
, we have defined the ordinal
\mbox{order}_{\gamma,A}(D)\leqslant\gamma
. We define
T(\xi,A)
for
A\in\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
.

Case 1:

\xi
is a successor ordinal. Write
\xi=\gamma+1
. For
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
, write
A=\cup_{i=1}^{\min A}A_{i}
, where
A_{1}<\cdots<A_{\min A}
and
A_{i}\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\gamma})
. Then

T(\xi,A)\ =\ \{A\}\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{\min A}T(\gamma,A_{i}),\mbox{ and }

\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(A)\ =\ \xi,\mbox{ and }\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(D)\ =\ \mbox{order}_{\gamma,A_{i}}(D),

for
D\in T(\gamma,A_{i})
and
i=1,\ldots,\min A
.

Case 2:

\xi
is a limit ordinal. For
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
, let
T(\xi,A)=T(\alpha(\xi,\min A),A)
, and
\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(D)=\mbox{order}_{\alpha(\xi,\min A),A}(D)
for all
D\in T(\xi,A)
.

Remark 3.2
.

For
\xi<\omega_{1}
and
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
, we make the following observations:

(1)

If
\xi
is a successor ordinal or
0
, we have
\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(A)=\xi
; if
\xi
is a limit ordinal, we have
\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(A)=\alpha(\xi,\min A)
, and
T(\xi,A)=T(\alpha(\xi,\min A),A)
.

(2)

For every
D\in T(\xi,A)
,
\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(D)
is a successor ordinal, and furthermore,
D\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(D)})
.

(3)

If
D,E\in T(\xi,A)
and
D\subsetneq E
, then
\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(D)<\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(E)
.

(4)

T(\xi,A)
is a tree with respect to inclusion, i.e.,
D
is a successor of
E
if
D\subsetneq E
. In this tree,
A
is the root, while the leaves (or terminal nodes) consist of all the singletons
\{a\}
, for
a\in A
.

(5)

Let
D\in T(\xi,A)
be not a terminal node. Denote the set of immediate successors of
D
in
T(\xi,A)
by
\mbox{succ}_{\xi}(D)
, i.e.,

\mbox{succ}_{\xi}(D)=

\{E\in T(\xi,A)\,:\,E\subsetneq D\mbox{ and }\not\exists E^{\prime}\in T(\xi,A)\mbox{ s.t. }E\subsetneq E^{\prime}\subsetneq D\}.

Then
D=\cup_{E\in\mbox{succ}(D)}E
. If we write
\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(D)=\gamma+1
, then
\mbox{succ}(D)
consists of
\min D
elements
D_{1}<D_{2}<\cdots<D_{\min D}
, where
D_{i}\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\gamma})
. Then there are two cases:

•

either
\gamma
is a successor ordinal, in which case
\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(D_{i})=\gamma
.

•

or
\gamma
is a limit ordinal, in which case, we have
D_{i}\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\gamma,\min D_{i})})
and
\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(D_{i})\ =\ \alpha(\gamma,\min D_{i})
for
i=1,2,\ldots,\min D
.

(6)

For
D\in T(\xi,A)
,
T(\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(D),D)
is a subtree of
A
, and

T(\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(D),D)\ =\ \{E\in T(\xi,A)\,:\,E\subset D\}.

(7)

Let
D,E\in T(\xi,A)
,
D\neq E
, then there are only four possibilities:

•

D\subsetneq E
, and order
{}_{\xi,A}(D)<\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(E)
,

•

E\subsetneq D
, and order
{}_{\xi,A}(E)<\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(D)
,

•

E<D
,

•

D<E
.

We define for
\xi<\omega_{1}
,
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
, and
a\in A
,
the
\xi
-order of
a
inside
A
:

\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(a)\ =\ \max\{\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(D)\,:\,D\in T(\xi,A)\mbox{ and }a\ =\ \min D\}.

Roughly speaking, to find
\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(a)
, we look for the set
D
closest to the root and whose minimum is
a
, then set
\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(a)=\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(D)
. Note that for every
a\in A
, we have
\{a\}\in T(\xi,A)
, so
\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(a)
is well-defined, and
\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(a)=0
if and only if
a
is not the minimum of any other node of
T(\xi,A)
. For
a\in A
, let
D_{\xi,A}(a)
be the unique set
D
closest to the root (possibly the root itself) and whose minimum is
a
. If
E\in T(\xi,A)
and
a\in E
with
a>\min E
, then
D_{\xi,A}(a)=D_{\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(E),E}(a)
and
\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(a)=\mbox{order}_{\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(E),E}(a)
.

Lemma 3.3

(The replacement lemma)
.

Let
\xi<\omega_{1}
,
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
, and
a\in A
with
a>\min A
. Put

\displaystyle m_{1}

\displaystyle\ =\ \max\{\max D\,:\,D\in T(\xi,A),D<D_{\xi,A}(a)\},\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle m_{2}

\displaystyle\ =\ \min\{\min D\,:\,D\in T(\xi,A),D>D_{\xi,A}(a)\},

where
\min\emptyset=\infty
in the case there is no node after
D_{\xi,A}(a)
. Let
E
be the immediate predecessor of
D_{\xi,A}(a)
in
T(\xi,A)
. Write
\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(E)=\gamma+1
. Then for every
\widetilde{D}\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\gamma})
with
\widetilde{D}\subset(m_{1},m_{2})
, we have

\widetilde{A}\ :=\ (A\backslash D_{\xi,A}(a))\cup\widetilde{D}\ \in\ \text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi}).

Proof.

We prove the lemma by induction on
n
, the number of predecessors of
D_{\xi,A}(a)
.

Base case:
n=1
. Let
\xi<\omega_{1}
,
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
, and
a\in A
with
a>\min A
.
Since
n=1
,
A
is the immediate predecessor of
D_{\xi,A}(a)
in
T(\xi,A)
. Write
A=\cup_{i=1}^{\min A}A_{i}
, where
\min A\leqslant A_{1}<A_{2}<\cdots<A_{\min A}
and
A_{i}\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\gamma})
. For some
i_{0}=2,3,\ldots,\min A
, we have
A_{i_{0}}=D_{\xi,A}(a)
. Note that
i_{0}\neq 1
because
a>\min A
. We have

A_{1}\ <\ A_{2}\ <\ \cdots\ <\ A_{i_{0}-1}\ <\ \widetilde{D}\ <\ A_{i_{0}+1}\ <\ \cdots\ <\ A_{\min A}.

It may be possible that in the above chain,
\widetilde{D}
is the last set, which happens exactly when
i_{0}=\min A
. By definition,

\widetilde{A}\ =\ (A\backslash D_{\xi,A}(a))\cup\widetilde{D}\ =\ \bigcup_{i=1,i\neq i_{0}}^{\min A}A_{i}\cup\widetilde{D}\ \in\ \text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi}).

Inductive hypothesis: assume that the lemma is true for
n
predecessors and all
\xi<\omega_{1}
.

Let us fix
\xi<\omega_{1}
,
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
, and
a\in A
with
a>\min A
. Suppose that
D_{\xi,A}(a)
has
n+1
predecessors
(E_{i})_{i=1}^{n+1}
in
T(\xi,A)
with

D_{\xi,A}(a)\ =\ E_{0}\ \subsetneq\ E_{1}\ \subsetneq\ E_{2}\ \subsetneq\ \cdots\ \subsetneq\ E_{n}\ \subsetneq\ E_{n+1}\ =\ A.

Put

\displaystyle m_{1}

\displaystyle\ =\ \max\{\max D\,:\,D\in T(\xi,A),D<D_{\xi,A}(a)\},\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle m_{2}

\displaystyle\ =\ \min\{\min D\,:\,D\in T(\xi,A),D>D_{\xi,A}(a)\}.

Choose
\widetilde{D}\subset(m_{1},m_{2})
such that
\widetilde{D}\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(a)})
.

Let
\xi=\gamma+1
and consider the tree
T(\gamma,E_{n})
. Put

\displaystyle m^{\prime}_{1}

\displaystyle\ =\ \max\{\max D\,:\,D\in T(\gamma,E_{n}),D<D_{\gamma,E_{n}}(a)\},\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle m^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle\ =\ \min\{\min D\,:\,D\in T(\gamma,E_{n}),D>D_{\gamma,E_{n}}(a)\}.

Since
D_{\gamma,E_{n}}(a)=D_{\xi,A}(a)
and
T(\gamma,E_{n})
is a subtree of
T(\xi,A)
, we know that

m^{\prime}_{1}\ \leqslant\ m_{1}<m_{2}\ \leqslant\ m_{2}^{\prime}.

Furthermore,
a>\min E_{n}
because
D_{\xi,A}(a)
has at least two predecessors. Applying the inductive hypothesis to the tree
T(\gamma,E_{n})
and
a\in E_{n}
, we have

(E_{n}\backslash D_{\xi,A}(a))\cup\widetilde{D}\ =\ (E_{n}\backslash D_{\gamma,E_{n}}(a))\cup\widetilde{D}\ \in\ \text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}).

It is worth noting that due to how
m_{1}^{\prime}
and
\widetilde{D}
are chosen,
(E_{n}\backslash D_{\xi,A}(a))\cup\widetilde{D}
and
E_{n}
have the same minimum. Write
A=\cup_{i=1}^{\min A}A_{i}
, for
\min A\leqslant A_{1}<\cdots<A_{\min A}
and
A_{i}\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\gamma})
. Let
A_{i_{0}}=E_{n}
. We have

A_{1}\ <\ \cdots\ <\ A_{i_{0}-1}\ <\ (E_{n}\backslash D_{\xi,A}(a))\cup\widetilde{D}\ <\ A_{i_{0}+1}\ <\ \cdots<A_{\min A}.

These sets are in
\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\gamma})
, and the transformation does not change the minimum of
A_{i_{0}}
. Therefore,

(A\backslash D_{\xi,A}(a))\cup\widetilde{D}\ =\ \left(\cup_{i=1,i\neq i_{0}}^{\min A}A_{i}\right)\cup((E_{n}\backslash D_{\xi,A}(a))\cup\widetilde{D})\ \in\ \text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi}).

This completes our proof.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\xi<\omega_{1}
. If
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, are pairwise disjoint, nonempty sets in
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
with

\min A_{1}<\cdots<\min A_{n}
, then there exist nonempty sets
A_{1}^{\prime}<\cdots<A_{n}^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
such that

\cup_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}=\cup_{i=1}^{n}A_{i}^{\prime}
and
\min A_{i}\leqslant\min A_{i}^{\prime}
, for
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Remark 3.5
.

Let us first note that if
\xi<\omega_{1}
and
A\in\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
with
1\in A
, then
A=\{1\}
. Therefore, we can assume in the statement of Theorem
3.4
that
1\notin A_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

We will prove the theorem by transfinite induction for all
\xi<\omega_{1}
. For the base case
\xi=0
, the theorem is trivially true.

Inductive hypothesis: suppose that the theorem is true for all
\eta<\xi
, for some
\xi\geqslant 1
. We shall show that the theorem is true for the ordinal
\xi
. We do so in three steps:

•

Step 1: we prove the theorem for a successor ordinal
\xi
and
n=2
.

•

Step 2: we prove the theorem for a limit ordinal
\xi
and
n=2
.

•

Step 3: we show that the theorem holds for an arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Step 1:
Write
\xi=\eta+1
. Let
A,B\in\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
be nonempty. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
2\leqslant\min A<\min B<\max A
. By enlarging
A
, if necessary, we also assume that
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
.

We decompose
A
as
A=\cup_{i=1}^{\min A}A_{i}
with
A_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{\eta}\backslash\{\emptyset\}
and
\min A\leqslant A_{1}<\cdots<A_{\min A}
. Also decompose
B=\cup_{i=1}^{\ell}B_{i}
with
B_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{\eta}\backslash\{\emptyset\}
and
\ell\leqslant B_{1}<\cdots<B_{\ell}
. Then we order the sets
\{A_{i}:i\leqslant\min A\}\cup\{B_{i}:i\leqslant\ell\}
into sets
(D_{i})_{i=1}^{\min A+\ell}
and apply the inductive hypothesis to obtain nonempty sets
(\widetilde{D}_{i})_{i=1}^{\min A+\ell}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\eta}
such that

\widetilde{D}_{1}\ <\ \cdots\ <\ \widetilde{D}_{\min A+\ell}\mbox{ and }\bigcup_{i=1}^{\min A+\ell}D_{i}\ =\ \bigcup_{i=1}^{\min A+\ell}\widetilde{D}_{i}.

We form
D^{\prime}_{1}
by unioning
\widetilde{D}_{1}
with the smallest elements of
\cup_{i=2}^{\min A+\ell}\widetilde{D}_{i}
, if necessary, until we obtain a maximal
\mathcal{S}_{\eta}
set. Then form
D^{\prime}_{2}
from the smallest elements in
(\widetilde{D}_{i})_{i=2}^{\min A+\ell}\backslash D^{\prime}_{1}
so that
D^{\prime}_{2}
is a maximal
\mathcal{S}_{\eta}
set. In general, we form
D^{\prime}_{j}
, for
j=2,\ldots,\min A
, from the smallest elements in
(\widetilde{D}_{i})_{i=j}^{\min A+\ell}\backslash\left(\cup_{i=1}^{j-1}D^{\prime}_{i}\right)
. Then the sets
(D^{\prime}_{i})_{i=1}^{\min A}
are maximal
\mathcal{S}_{\eta}
. Let
\widetilde{A}=\cup_{i=1}^{\min A}D^{\prime}_{i}
.

After the above process, some of the sets
(\widetilde{D}_{i}\backslash\widetilde{A})_{i=\min A+1}^{\min A+\ell}
are possibly empty. Discard the empty sets and rename the nonempty ones as
(D^{\prime}_{i})_{i=\min A+1}^{\min A+\ell^{\prime}}
. Here
\ell^{\prime}\leqslant\ell
, and there must exist some nonempty sets; otherwise, we have a contradiction with the maximality of
A
. Let
\widetilde{B}=\cup_{i=\min A+1}^{\min A+\ell^{\prime}}D^{\prime}_{i}
.

Since all the sets
(B_{i})_{i=1}^{\ell}
have
\min B_{i}\geqslant\min B_{1}
, there are at least
\ell
sets
\widetilde{D}_{i}
’s whose minimum is at least
\min B_{1}
. Hence,
we have

\displaystyle\min\widetilde{A}\ =\ \min D^{\prime}_{1}\ =\ \min\widetilde{D}_{1}\ =\ \min A,

\displaystyle\min\widetilde{B}\ =\ \min D^{\prime}_{\min A+1}\ \geqslant\ \min\widetilde{D}_{\min A+1}\ \geqslant\ \min B_{1}\ \geqslant\ \ell\ \geqslant\ \ell^{\prime}.

Hence, both
\widetilde{A}
and
\widetilde{B}
are in
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
,
\widetilde{A}<\widetilde{B}
, and
\min A=\min\widetilde{A}
and
\min B\leqslant\min\widetilde{B}
, as desired. Moreover, if
A
is
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
maximal, then
\widetilde{A}
is
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
maximal as well.

Step 2:
Let
\xi
be a limit ordinal and
n=2
. Let
A,B\in\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
be nonempty. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\min A<\min B<\max A
and
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
. Let
a=\min\{x\in A:x>\min B\}
and let
\gamma+1=\mbox{order}_{\xi,A}(E)
, where
E
is the immediate predecessor of
D_{\xi,A}(a)
in the tree
T(\xi,A)
.

Put
F=\{x\in A\cup B:x\geqslant\min B\}
and write
F=\{f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{\ell}\}
, with
\min B=f_{1}<f_{2}<\cdots<f_{\ell}
. Then
D_{\xi,A}(a)\cup B\subset F
. Let
m<\ell
be such that
\widetilde{D}=\{f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{m}\}\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\gamma})
. Such an
m
must exist because
F
contains a maximal
\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}
set, namely
D_{\xi,A}(a)
. Both
\widetilde{D}
and
D_{\xi,A}(a)=\{f_{1}^{\prime},f_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,f_{k}^{\prime}\}
are subsets of
F
, and because
\widetilde{D}
consists of the smallest elements of
F
, we know that
f_{i}<f_{i}^{\prime}
for
i=1,\ldots,\min\{m,k\}
. It must be that
k\geqslant m
; otherwise, the spreading property and
\widetilde{D}\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}
would imply that
D_{\xi,A}(a)\cup\{a^{\prime}\}\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}
, for every
a^{\prime}>\max A\cup B
, is in
\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\gamma})
. This contradicts the maximality of
D_{\xi,A}(a)
. Therefore,
\max\widetilde{D}=f_{m}<f^{\prime}_{k}=\max D_{\xi,A}(a)
. We obtain

\max\widetilde{D}\ <\ \max D_{\xi,A}(a)\ <\ \min\{\min D\,:\,D\in T(\xi,A)\mbox{ and }D>D_{\xi,A}(a)\}\ =\ m_{2}.

Furthermore,

\min\widetilde{D}\ =\ \min B\ >\ \max\{\max D\,:\,D\in T(\xi,A)\mbox{ and }D<D_{\xi,A}(a)\}\ =\ m_{1}.

It follows from Lemma
3.3
that
\widetilde{A}=(A\backslash D_{\xi,A}(a))\cup\widetilde{D}\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
, and
\min A=\min\widetilde{A}
. Define
\widetilde{B}=(B\backslash\widetilde{D})\cup(D_{\xi,A}(a)\backslash\widetilde{D})
. It is easy to verify that

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}\cup\widetilde{B}\ =\ A\cup B\mbox{ and }\min B\ <\ \min\widetilde{B},

\displaystyle B\backslash\widetilde{D}\ \in\ \mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min B)},\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle D_{\xi,A}(a)\backslash\widetilde{D}\ \in\ \mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min A)}\ \subset\ \mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min B)}.

Applying the inductive hypothesis to
\widetilde{B}
, we can find two sets
\widetilde{B}_{1},\widetilde{B}_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min B)}
with
\widetilde{B}_{1}<\widetilde{B}_{2}
. By (
2
), we know that
\widetilde{B}=\widetilde{B}_{1}\cup\widetilde{B}_{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min B+1)}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\alpha(\xi,\min\widetilde{B})}
. Hence,
\widetilde{B}\in\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
.

Either we are now in the situation that
\widetilde{A}<\widetilde{B}
in which case, we are done, or
\max\widetilde{A}>\min\widetilde{B}
, and we would repeat the above process. However, we can only iterate the process finitely many times because, after each iteration, we increase the minimum of a subset of
A\cup B
by
1
. This completes our Step 2. As in Step 1, our new set
\widetilde{A}
is can be chosen to be in
\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
if
A\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
.

Step 3:
We show by induction on
n
that if
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{n}
are disjoint, nonempty sets in
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
, then there are nonempty sets
\widetilde{A}_{1},\widetilde{A}_{2},\ldots,\widetilde{A}_{n}
in
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
so that
\widetilde{A}_{1}<\widetilde{A}_{2}<\cdots<\widetilde{A}_{n}
and
\cup_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}=\cup_{j=1}^{n}\widetilde{A}_{j}
. Assume that
\min A_{1}<\min A_{2}<\cdots<\min A_{n+1}
. By possibly enlarging the sets, we can also assume that
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots,A_{n+1}\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
.

We can recursively find
n
pairs of nonempty sets
(\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(j)},A_{j}^{\prime})\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})\times\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
, where
j=2,\ldots,n+1
, so that

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(j)}\ <\ A_{i}^{\prime},\quad i=2,\ldots,j,\mbox{ and}

(3.1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(j)}\cup\bigcup_{i=2}^{j}A_{i}^{\prime}\ =\ \bigcup_{i=1}^{j}A_{i}.

(3.2)

Indeed, the case
j=2
follows from Step 1 and Step 2. Suppose we have chosen
\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(j)}
and
A^{\prime}_{i}
for
j\leqslant n
and
i=2,\ldots,j
. From
\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(j)}
and
A_{j+1}
, we obtain
\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(j+1)}\in\text{\rm MAX}(\mathcal{S}_{\xi})
and
A^{\prime}_{j+1}\in\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
so that
\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(j+1)}<A^{\prime}_{j+1}
and
\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(j+1)}\cup A^{\prime}_{j+1}=\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(j)}\cup A_{j+1}
. Note that
\max\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(j+1)}\leqslant\max\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(j)}
because otherwise,
\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(j)}\subsetneq\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(j+1)}\in\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
, which contradicts the
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
-maximality of
\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(j)}
.
This together with the supposition that (
3.1
) holds for
j
guarantees that (
3.1
) holds for
j+1
. Using
\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(j+1)}\cup A^{\prime}_{j+1}=\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(j)}\cup A_{j+1}
and the supposition that (
3.2
) holds for
j
, we can easily verify that (
3.2
) holds for
j+1
. This shows the existence of
\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(j)}
and
A_{j}^{\prime}
,
j=2,3,\ldots,n+1
.

Put
\widetilde{A}_{1}=\widetilde{A}_{1}^{(n+1)}
. Then
\widetilde{A}_{1}<A_{i}^{\prime}
, for
i=1,2,\ldots,n+1
. Apply the inductive hypothesis to the
n
sets
(A_{i}^{\prime})_{i=2}^{n+1}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
to have
(\widetilde{A}_{i})_{i=2}^{n+1}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
satisfying

\widetilde{A}_{2}\ <\ \cdots\ <\ \widetilde{A}_{n+1}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\bigcup_{i=2}^{n+1}\widetilde{A}_{i}\ =\ \bigcup_{i=2}^{n+1}A^{\prime}_{i}.

It follows from (

) that

\widetilde{A}_{1}\ <\ \widetilde{A}_{2}\ <\ \cdots\ <\ \widetilde{A}_{n+1}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\bigcup_{i=1}^{n+1}\widetilde{A}_{i}\ =\ \widetilde{A}_{1}^{(n+1)}\cup\bigcup_{i=2}^{n+1}A^{\prime}_{i}\ =\ \bigcup_{i=1}^{n+1}A_{i}.

This completes our proof.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

For all countable ordinal
\xi\in[1,\omega_{1})
, we have
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}=\mathcal{S}^{M}_{\xi}
.

Proof.

We proceed by transfinite induction.
The case
\xi=1
is trivial. Assume that the corollary holds for all
\gamma<\xi
for some
\xi\geqslant 2
.

Case 1:
if
\xi
is a successor ordinal, then write
\xi=\gamma+1
. Let
F\in\mathcal{S}^{M}_{\xi}
. By definition,
F
can be written as
\cup_{i=1}^{k}F_{i}
, for
(F_{i})_{i=1}^{k}\subset\mathcal{S}^{M}_{\gamma}
, being pairwise disjoint, and
(\min F_{i})_{i=1}^{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
. By the inductive hypothesis,
\mathcal{S}^{M}_{\gamma}=\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}
; hence,
(F_{i})_{i=1}^{k}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}
.
By Theorem
3.4
, we can find
(F_{i}^{\prime})_{i=1}^{k}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}
such that

F^{\prime}_{1}\ <\ F^{\prime}_{2}\ <\ \cdots\ <\ F^{\prime}_{k},\{\min F^{\prime}_{i}\,:\,1\leqslant i\leqslant k\}\in\mathcal{S}_{1},\mbox{ and }\cup_{i=1}^{k}F^{\prime}_{i}\ =\ \cup_{i=1}^{k}F_{i}.

Therefore,
F
is in
\mathcal{S}_{\xi}
.

Case 2:

\xi
is a limit ordinal. Pick
F\in\mathcal{S}^{M}_{\xi}
, then
F\in\mathcal{S}^{M}_{\alpha(\xi,k)}
for some
k\leqslant\min F
. By the inductive hypothesis,

, which implies that

.
∎

4.
Modified Tsirelson spaces of higher order

Manoussakis
[
14
]
proved that the Tsirelson space
T[\mathcal{S}_{\xi},\theta]
is naturally isomorphic to its modified version
T_{M}[\mathcal{S}_{\xi},\theta]
for all finite ordinals
\xi
. To do so, he compared
T[\mathcal{S}_{\xi},\theta]
to an auxiliary space
Y
then compared
T_{M}[\mathcal{S}_{\xi},\theta]
to
Y
, where the latter step used a technique from Bellenot
[
4
]
. In this section, we extended Manoussakis’ result by proving the isomorphism between
T[\mathcal{S}_{\xi},\theta]
and
T_{M}[\mathcal{S}_{\xi},\theta]
for all ordinals
\xi<\omega_{1}
. To compare
T[\mathcal{S}_{\xi},\theta]
to
Y
, we borrow Manoussakis’ argument and adapt it to handle limit ordinals (see Lemma
4.3
.) To compare
T_{M}[\mathcal{S}_{\xi},\theta]
to
Y
, we present a different proof than Manoussakis’ and Bellenot’s.

Let
c_{00}
be the vector space of all sequences
x=(x_{n})
in
\mathbb{R}
for which
the support,
\text{supp }x=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:x_{n}\not=0\}
, is finite. We denote by
(e_{n})
the unit vector basis of
c_{00}
and by
(e^{*}_{n})
its coordinate functionals.
All our Banach spaces will be completions of
c_{00}
under some norm, for which
(e_{n})
is a normalized and unconditional basis.

Let
\mathcal{F}
denote a regular family of finite subsets of
\mathbb{N}
. A sequence of finite sets
(E_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
of positive integers is said to be
\mathcal{F}
-
admissible
if

E_{1}\ <\ E_{2}\ <\ \cdots\ <\ E_{k}\mbox{ and }\{\min E_{i}\,:\,1\leqslant i\leqslant k\}\ \in\ \mathcal{F}.

A sequence of finite sets
(E_{i})_{i=1}^{k}
of positive integers is said to be
\mathcal{F}
-
allowable
if

(E_{i})_{i=1}^{k}\mbox{ are pairwise disjoint and }\{\min E_{i}\,:\,1\leqslant i\leqslant k\}\ \in\ \mathcal{F}.

Let
\theta
be a number with
0<\theta<1
.
The Tsirelson space
T[\mathcal{F},\theta]
is the completion of
c_{00}
under the implicit norm

\|x\|_{\mathcal{F},\theta}\ =\ \max\left\{\|x\|_{\infty},\theta\sup\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|E_{i}x\|\,:\,(E_{i})_{i=1}^{k}\mbox{ is }\mathcal{F}\mbox{-admissible}\right\}\right\}.

The modified Tsirelson space
T_{M}[\mathcal{F},\theta]
is the completion of
c_{00}
under the implicit norm

\|x\|_{\mathcal{F},\theta,M}\ =\ \max\left\{\|x\|_{\infty},\theta\sup\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|E_{i}x\|\,:\,(E_{i})_{i=1}^{k}\mbox{ is }\mathcal{F}\mbox{-allowable}\right\}\right\}.

It was shown in
[
1
]
, where the space
T[\mathcal{S}_{\xi},\theta]
was introduced, that

T[\mathcal{S}_{\xi},\theta]
is reflexive, and, thus, in particular, the unit basis
cannot have a block basis equivalent to the unit vector basis
of
\ell_{1}
. It follows from
[
2
, Theorem 1.27]
that
T_{M}[\mathcal{S}_{\xi},\theta]
is also reflexive.

Our goal in this section is to show that
T[\mathcal{S}_{\xi},\theta]
is
3
-isomorphic to
T_{M}[\mathcal{S}_{\xi},\theta]
for every countable ordinal
\xi
and for every
0<\theta<1
. To do so, we shall use the auxiliary space
T[\mathcal{S}_{\xi}[\mathcal{A}_{2}],\theta]
, where
\mathcal{A}_{n}=\{E\subset\mathbb{N}:|E|\leqslant n\}
. It suffices to prove the following two theorems.

Theorem 4.1
.

For every
x\in c_{00}
, we have

\mathcal{J}^{\mathcal{T}}=\overline{\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}A_{j}\circ T_{j}\circ B_{j}:n\in\mathbb{N},\,T_{j}\in\mathcal{T},\,A_{j},B_{j}\in\mathcal{L}(X),j=1,2,\ldots,n\right\}}.

For

\mathcal{J}^{T}=\mathcal{J}^{\{T\}}

a
singly generated closed ideal

.

Using an argument from

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
(T_{N}:N\in[\mathbb{N}]^{\omega})

which satisfies the following tree condition for some

:

T_{M}\ \in\ \mathcal{J}^{T_{N}}\text{ if $M\subset N$, and }\mbox{dist}(T_{M},\mathcal{J}^{T_{N}})\ \geqslant\ c\text{ if }M\setminus N\in[\mathbb{N}]^{\omega}.

(T)

Let
\mathcal{C}\subset[\mathbb{N}]^{\omega}

\mathfrak{c}
, consisting of pairwise almost disjoint sets
(meaning that

,

(\mathcal{J}^{\{T_{N}:N\in\mathcal{A}\}}:\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{C})

Theorem 5.4
.

[
12
]

Assume that
X
is a Banach space with a
1
-unconditional basis
(e_{j})

which is not equivalent to the unit vector basis of
\ell_{1}
and satisfies the following condition for some
c>0
and some sequence
(L(m))_{m=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{N}
:

For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and any
m
-dimensional subspace
E
of
X_{L(m)}=\overline{\text{span}(e_{j}:j\geqslant L(m))}
, there exists a finite-dimensional subspace
F
of
X_{L(m)}

generated by a basis
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{L(m)}
, which is
c
-equivalent to the

\ell_{1}^{L(m)}
-unit basis, and there exists an automorphism
S:X_{L(m)}\to X_{L(m)}
, with

\|S^{-1}\|\cdot\|S\|\leqslant 2
and
S(E)\subset F
.

Then there exists a family
(T_{N}:N\in[\mathbb{N}]^{\omega})

of spatial projections (
T_{N}
is a projection on some subspace of
X
generated by a subsequence of
(e_{j})
), which satisfies
(T).

Proof.

Using on the one hand that
(e_{j})
is not equivalent to the unit vector basis of
\ell_{1}
,
on the other hand the sequence
(L(m))
, we
choose recursively natural numbers
m_{1}<n_{1}<m_{2}<n_{2}<\cdots
as follows:

We let
m_{1}=1
, and assuming that
m_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
has been chosen, we choose
n_{k}>m_{k}
so that
there exist numbers
(p_{j}^{(k)})_{j=m_{k}}^{n_{k}}\subset[0,1]
so that

\sum_{j=m_{k}}^{n_{k}}p_{j}^{(k)}=1
and

\left\|\sum_{j=m_{k}}^{n_{k}}p^{(k)}_{j}e_{j}\right\|\ <\ \frac{1}{km_{k}},

(5.1)

and assuming
n_{k}\in\mathbb{N}
has been chosen, we choose
m_{k+1}=L(n_{k})
.

For
N\in[\mathbb{N}]^{\omega}
, we define
T_{N}
to be the projection onto the subspace of
X
generated by
(e_{i}:i\in\bigcup_{j\in N}[m_{j},n_{j}])

and claim that the so defined family
(T_{N}:N\in[\mathbb{N}]^{\omega})
satisfies (T).
The first condition in (T) is trivially satisfied. To verify the second condition, we define for
k\in\mathbb{N}
the following functional
\Psi_{k}\in\mathcal{L}^{*}(X)
:

\Psi_{k}(S)\ =\ \sum_{j=m_{k}}^{n_{k}}p^{(k)}_{j}e^{*}_{j}(S(e_{j})),\text{ for }S\in\mathcal{L}(X).

Clearly,
\|\Psi_{k}\|_{\mathcal{L}^{*}(X)}=1
, and if
N\in[\mathbb{N}]^{\omega}
and
k\in N
, then
\Psi_{k}(T_{N})=1
.
It is therefore enough to show that for
A,B\in\mathcal{L}(X)
,
\|A\|,\|B\|\leqslant 1
, and
M,N\in[\mathbb{N}]^{\omega}
, with

M\setminus N\in[\mathbb{N}]^{\omega}
, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty,k\in M\setminus N}\Psi_{k}(A\circ T_{N}\circ B)\ =\ 0

(5.2)

We compute for
k\not\in N
that

\displaystyle\big|\Psi_{k}(A\circ T_{N}\circ B)\big|

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \sum_{j=m_{k}}^{n_{k}}p^{(k)}_{j}|e^{*}_{j}(AT_{N}B)(e_{j})|

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \sum_{j=m_{k}}^{n_{k}}p^{(k)}_{j}\|T_{N}B(e_{j})\|

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ \underbrace{\sum_{s=1}^{n_{k-1}}\sum_{j=m_{k}}^{n_{k}}p^{(k)}_{j}|B_{s}(e_{j})|}_{=\ \Sigma_{1}}+\underbrace{\sum_{j=m_{k}}^{n_{k}}p^{(k)}_{j}\|P\circ B(e_{j})\|}_{=\ \Sigma_{2}},

where
B_{s}=e^{*}_{s}\circ B\in X^{*}
, with
\|B_{s}\|_{X^{*}}\leqslant 1
, for
s\in\mathbb{N}
, and

P:X\to X_{m_{k+1}}
is the basis projection onto
X_{m_{k+1}}=\overline{\mbox{span}(e_{j}:j\geqslant m_{k+1})}
.

To estimate
\Sigma_{1}
, we observe that

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}\ =\ \sum_{s=1}^{n_{k-1}}B_{s}\left(\sum_{j=m_{k}}^{n_{k}}p^{(k)}_{j}\mbox{sign}(B_{s}(e_{i}))e_{j}\right)\ \leqslant\ \sum_{s=1}^{n_{k-1}}\left\|\sum_{j=m_{k}}^{n_{k}}p^{(k)}_{j}\mbox{sign}(B_{s}(e_{i}))e_{j}\right\|\ \leqslant\ \frac{1}{k}.

(5.3)

To estimate
\Sigma_{2}
, we consider the space
E=P\circ B(\mbox{span}(e_{j}:m_{k}\leqslant j\leqslant n_{k}))

which is a subspace of
X_{m_{k+1}}=X_{L(n_{k})}
and has dimension not larger than
n_{k}
.
Therefore, we can find a finite-dimensional subspace
F
of
X_{L(n_{k})}
, with a basis
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\mbox{dim}F}
which is
c
-equivalent to the
\ell_{1}^{\mbox{dim}F}
-unit basis, and an automorphism
S
on
X_{m_{k+1}}
,
for which
S(E)\subset F
, and
\|S\|\cdot\|S^{-1}\|\leqslant 2
. We have

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ 2\sum_{j=m_{k}}^{n_{k}}p^{(k)}_{j}\|SPB(e_{j})\|

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ 2\sum_{j=m_{k}}^{n_{k}}p^{(k)}_{j}\sum_{s=1}^{\dim(F)}|\widetilde{B}_{s}(e_{j})|

where
\widetilde{B}_{s}(x)=f^{*}_{s}(SPB(x))
, for
s=1,2,\ldots,\mbox{dim}F
,
x\in X
,
(f^{*}_{j})_{j=1}^{\mbox{dim}F}
being the
coordinate functionals in
F
,

\displaystyle\ =\ 2\sum_{s=1}^{\dim(F)}\sum_{j=m_{k}}^{n_{k}}p^{(k)}_{j}|\widetilde{B}_{s}(e_{j})|

\displaystyle\ =\ 2\sum_{s=1}^{\dim(F)}\widetilde{B}_{s}\left(\sum_{j=m_{k}}^{n_{k}}p^{(k)}_{j}\mbox{sign}(\widetilde{B}_{s}(e_{j}))e_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ 2c\left\|\sum_{s=1}^{\dim(F)}\widetilde{B}_{s}\left(\sum_{j=m_{k}}^{n_{k}}p^{(k)}_{j}\mbox{sign}(\widetilde{B}_{s}(e_{j}))e_{j}\right)f_{s}\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\ =\ 2c\left\|SPB\left(\sum_{j=m_{k}}^{n_{k}}p^{(k)}_{j}\mbox{sign}(\widetilde{B}_{s}(e_{j}))e_{j}\right)\right\|_{F}

\displaystyle\ \leqslant\ 4c\left\|\sum_{j=m_{k}}^{n_{k}}p^{(k)}_{j}e_{j}\right\|\ \leqslant\ \frac{4c}{k}.

This, together with (
5.3
), implies our claim (
5.2
).
∎

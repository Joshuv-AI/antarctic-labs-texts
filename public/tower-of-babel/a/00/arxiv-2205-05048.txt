Faint light of old neutron stars and detectability at the James Webb Space Telescope
Shiuli Chatterjee, Raghuveer Garani, Rajeev Kumar Jain, Brijesh Kanodia, M. S. N. Kumar, Sudhir K. Vempati

Abstract
Isolated ideal neutron stars (NS) of age > 10 9 >10^{9} yrs exhaust thermal and rotational energies and cool down to temperatures below 𝒪 ⁡ ( 100 ) \mathcal{O}(100) K. Accretion of particle dark matter (DM) by such NS can heat them up through kinetic and annihilation processes. This increases the NS surface temperature to a maximum of ∼ 2550 \sim 2550 K in the best case scenario. The maximum accretion rate depends on the DM ambient density and velocity dispersion, and on the NS equation of state and their velocity distributions. Upon scanning over these variables, we find that the effective surface temperature varies at most by ∼ 40 % \sim 40\% . Black body spectrum of such warm NS peak at near infrared wavelengths with magnitudes in the range potentially detectable by the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST). Using the JWST exposure time calculator, we demonstrate that NS with surface temperatures ≳ 2400 \gtrsim 2400 K, located at a distance of 10 pc can be detected through the F150W2 (F322W2) filters of the NIRCAM instrument at SNR ≳ 10 \gtrsim 10 (5) within 24 hours of exposure time. Independently of DM, an observation of NS with surface temperatures ≳ 2500 \gtrsim 2500 K will be a formative step towards testing the minimal cooling paradigm during late evolutionary stages.

5. Conclusion.—

DM from the halo can accrete on to old NS leading to anomalous heating of these objects during late evolutionary stages
Kouvaris 2008
. Leveraging this argument, it was pointed out in ref.
Baryakhtar et al. 2017
that NS heated by DM could be observable by a state-of-the-art facility such as the JWST, and that the discovery of sufficiently cold and old NS in the local bubble would constrain the interactions of particle DM with the Standard Model in a model independent way.

In this
letter
, we have
quantified
the observational prospects for maximal (kinetic and annihilation) DM heating scenario of NS, corresponding to geometric values of scattering cross section
\sim 10^{-45}
cm
2
, and for the minimal cooling scenario representative of NS of age
\sim 15
Myrs. The effective surface temperature of NS due to DM heating depends on the NS mass and radius through the EoS, the NS velocity in the local bubble, DM velocity and number density. The corresponding variations of the NS effective surface temperature have been assessed together with its impact on the observability of such NS using the NIRCAM instrument on the JWST. Our study implies that NS warmer than
\gtrsim
2600 K, in the local bubble, are observable with strategies requiring shorter exposure times than discussed here. Such scenarios can be realized in a particle physics model dependent way through ‘Auger effect’, if DM is charged under baryon number
McKeen et al. 2021a
;
McKeen et al. 2021b
;
Goldman et al. 2022
, or through DM capture from a clumpy halo with large boost factors
Bramante et al. 2021
, or due to other internal heating mechanisms independent of DM
Gonzalez and Reisenegger 2010
;
Hamaguchi et al. 2019
. Observation of such NS would not only shed light on late evolutionary stages, but also on their equation of state.

Multi-solitons for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with repulsive Dirac delta potential
Stephen Gustafson, Takahisa Inui, Ikkei Shimizu

Abstract
. We prove the existence of multi-soliton solutions for the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with repulsive Dirac delta potential and L 2 L^{2} -supercritical focusing nonlinear term. Our main contribution is to treat the unmoving part of the multi-solitons, which is the ground state of the equation. The linearized operator around it has two unstable eigenvalues. This is the main difference from NLS without potential, whose existence of multi-solitons is investigated by Côte, Martel, and Merle (2011).

1.
Introduction

We consider the following nonlinear Schrödinger eqaution with repulsive Dirac delta potential in one dimension:

(
\delta
NLS)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u+\gamma\delta u+|u|^{p-1}u=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},

where
\gamma<0
,
\delta
denotes the Dirac delta at the origin, and
p>1
. The condition
\gamma<0
means that the potential is repulsive. From the view point of the scaling, we say that the equation is
L^{2}
-subcritical when
1<p<5
,
L^{2}
-critical when
p=5
, and
L^{2}
-supercritical when
p>5
. We mainly consider the
L^{2}
-supercritical case in the present paper.
Here, the Schrödinger operator
-\Delta_{\gamma}:=-\partial_{x}^{2}-\gamma\delta
is defined by

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\gamma}f

\displaystyle:=-\partial_{x}^{2}f\text{ for }f\in\mathcal{D}(-\Delta_{\gamma}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(-\Delta_{\gamma})

\displaystyle:=\{f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}):f^{\prime}(0+)-f^{\prime}(0-)=-\gamma f(0)\}.

Since
\gamma<0
,
-\Delta_{\gamma}
is a nonnegative self-adjoint operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
(see
[
1
]
for more details), which implies that the linear Schrödinger propagator
e^{it\Delta_{\gamma}}
is well-defined on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
by Stone’s theorem. The operator
-\Delta_{\gamma}
may also be defined by its quadratic form:

\displaystyle q(f,g):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{\prime}(x)\overline{g^{\prime}(x)}dx-\gamma f(0)\overline{g(0)}

for
f,g\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. See e.g.
[
21
,
27
]
.

It is known that the equation (

δ

NLS
) is locally well-posed in the energy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover, the energy
E_{\gamma}
and mass
M
, which are defined by

\displaystyle E_{\gamma}(f)

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{x}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}|f(0)|^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\|f\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1},

\displaystyle M(f)

\displaystyle:=\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

are conserved (see
[
12
,
10
]
).

It is also known that there exists a ground state standing wave solution
e^{i\omega t}Q_{\omega,\gamma}
, where
Q_{\omega,\gamma}
is the positive function defined by

\displaystyle Q_{\omega,\gamma}(x):=\left[\frac{(p+1)\omega}{2}\sech^{2}\left\{\frac{(p-1)\sqrt{\omega}}{2}|x|+\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2\sqrt{\omega}}\right)\right\}\right]^{\frac{1}{p-1}}

for
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
. See e.g.
[
9
]
.
This function
Q_{\omega,\gamma}
is a unique positive solution of

\displaystyle-\partial_{x}^{2}Q_{\omega,\gamma}-\gamma\delta Q_{\omega,\gamma}+\omega Q_{\omega,\gamma}=Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p}.

We are interested in the existence of multi-soliton solutions for (

δ

NLS
). First, we recall results for NLS without potential.
We consider the following nonlinear Schrödinger equation:

(1.1)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u+|u|^{p-1}u=0,\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},

where
p>5
, which is (

δ

NLS
) with
\gamma=0
.
The function
e^{i\omega t}Q_{\omega,0}
for
\omega>0
is a ground state standing wave solution of the equation (
1.1
). The equation (
1.1
) is gauge invariant, translation invariant, and Galilean invariant. That is, if
u
is a solution, then

\displaystyle u_{1}(t,x):=u(t,x-v_{1}t-x_{1})e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{1}x-\frac{1}{4}v_{1}^{2}t+\theta_{1}\right)}

is also a solution for
v_{1},x_{1},\theta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore, for given
v_{1},x_{1},\theta_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\omega_{1}>0
, the function

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{1}(t,x):=Q_{\omega_{1},0}(x-v_{1}t-x_{1})e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{1}x-\frac{1}{4}v_{1}^{2}t+\omega_{1}t+\theta_{1}\right)}

is a one-soliton solution of the equation (
1.1
). The following theorem showed existence of a multi-soliton solution.

Theorem

(
[
6
]
)
.

Assume
p>5
. Let
K\geq 2
,
v_{1},...,v_{K},x_{1},...,x_{K},\theta_{1},...,\theta_{K}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\omega_{1},...,\omega_{K}>0
. We assume that
v_{1}<v_{2}<...<v_{K}
. Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{R}_{k}(t,x),

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=Q_{\omega_{k},0}(x-v_{k}t-x_{k})e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{k}x-\frac{1}{4}v_{k}^{2}t+\omega_{k}t+\theta_{k}\right)}.

Then there exists
T_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
C,c_{0}>0
, and a solution
u\in C([T_{0},\infty);H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
to (
1.1
) such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)-\mathcal{R}(t)\|_{H^{1}}\leq Ce^{-c_{0}t}

for
t\in[T_{0},\infty)
.

Remark 1.1
.

In fact, for NLS with
H^{1}
-subcritical nonlinearity in general dimensions, the existence of the multi-solitons is known. See
[
24
,
23
,
6
]
.

We turn now to equation (

δ

NLS
). We will construct multi-soliton solutions for (

δ

NLS
). If
v\neq 0
, then the interaction between the Dirac delta potential at the origin and the moving one-soliton

\displaystyle Q_{\omega_{1},0}(x-v_{1}t-x_{1})e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{1}x-\frac{1}{4}v_{1}^{2}t+\omega_{1}t+\theta_{1}\right)}

are exponentially decaying in time. Thus we expect that we can construct multi-soliton for (

δ

NLS
) if their solitons have non-zero velocity. Moreover, if one of them has zero velocity, then we expect that the multi-soliton with
Q_{\omega,\gamma}
replacing
Q_{\omega,0}
can be constructed.

1.1.
Main results

Before giving our results, we introduce some notations.
Let
K
be a positive integer.
We set
\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket:=\{1,2,...,K\}
. For a given
K
, we let

\displaystyle v_{1},....,v_{K}\in\mathbb{R},\quad\omega_{1},,...,\omega_{K}\in(0,\infty),\quad x_{1},,...,x_{K}\in\mathbb{R},\quad\theta_{1},...,\theta_{K}\in\mathbb{R}.

We assume that
v_{k}\neq v_{j}
for any distinct
j,k\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
. Therefore, in what follows, we assume that

\displaystyle v_{1}<v_{2}<....<v_{K}

by renumbering.
We additionally assume that
\omega_{k_{0}}>\gamma^{2}/4
and
x_{k_{0}}=0
if
k_{0}\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
satisfies
v_{k_{0}}=0
.
For simplicity of the notation, we set

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}:=\begin{cases}\gamma&\text{ for }k\text{ such that }v_{k}=0,\\
0&\text{ for }k\text{ such that }v_{k}\neq 0.\end{cases}

We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\mathcal{R}_{k}(t,x),

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k}(t,x)

\displaystyle:=Q_{\omega_{k},\gamma_{k}}(x-v_{k}t-x_{k})e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{k}x-\frac{1}{4}v_{k}^{2}t+\omega_{k}t+\theta_{k}\right)}.

We note that if
k_{0}\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
satisfies
v_{k_{0}}=0
then
\mathcal{R}_{k_{0}}(t,x)=Q_{\omega_{k_{0}},\gamma}(x)e^{i(\omega_{k_{0}}t+\theta_{k_{0}})}
.

The following is our main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p>5
and
K\geq 2
. We assume that there exists
k_{0}\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
such that
v_{k_{0}}=0
(and additionally that
\omega_{k_{0}}>\gamma^{2}/4
and
x_{k_{0}}=0
).
Then, there exist
T_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
C,c_{0}>0
and a solution
u\in C([T_{0},\infty):H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
to (

δ

NLS
) such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)-\mathcal{R}(t)\|_{H^{1}}\leq Ce^{-c_{0}t}

for all
t\in[T_{0},\infty)
.

Remark 1.2
.

We do not need to consider the case
K=1
here since the unmoving soliton
\mathcal{R}_{1}
is a ground state standing wave solution to (

δ

NLS
).

The following theorem is also obtained by a similar argument.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p>5
and
K\geq 1
. We assume that
v_{k}\neq 0
for any
k\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
.
Then, there exist
T_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
C,c_{0}>0
and a solution
u\in C([T_{0},\infty):H^{1}(\mathbb{R}))
to (

δ

NLS
) such that

\displaystyle\|u(t)-\mathcal{R}(t)\|_{H^{1}}\leq Ce^{-c_{0}t}

for all
t\in[T_{0},\infty)
.

Remark 1.3
.

Unlike Theorem
1.1
, the case
K=1
with non-zero velocity is of interest. Indeed, since the equation (

δ

NLS
) is not Galilean invariant, the moving soliton
\mathcal{R}_{1}
is not a solution. Thus, the existence of moving one-soliton is non-trivial.

Remark 1.4
.

Theorem
1.2
in the case of
K=1
shows that the previous global dynamics results in
[
19
,
2
,
20
]
is optimal, where it was proved that solutions to (

δ

NLS
) with
E_{\gamma}(u_{0})M(u_{0})^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}\leq E_{0}(Q_{1,0})M(Q_{1,0})^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}
either scatter or blow up (possibly grow up) in both time directions, where
s_{c}:=1/2-2/(p-1)
. Theorem
1.2
means that there exists non-scattering, global (forward-in-time), and bounded solution whenever
E_{\gamma}(u_{0})M(u_{0})^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}>E_{0}(Q_{1,0})M(Q_{1,0})^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}
.
Moreover, in a similar way, Theorem
1.2
with
K=2
also shows that the scattering and blow-up dichotomy result for even solutions obtained recently in
[
15
]
is optimal.

Remark 1.5
.

In the subcritical case
1<p<5
, we expect that Theorem
1.2
can be shown in a similar way to
[
23
]
. However, for multi-solitons with one zero velocity as in Theorem
1.1
, we need to pay more attention. This is because the ground state standing wave solution
e^{i\omega t}Q_{\omega,\gamma}
is unstable for any
1<p<5
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
(see
[
22
]
). Thus we need to control the unstable direction even in the subcritical case unlike the usual NLS.
However, we expect that Theorem
1.1
can be shown by the similar method in the subcritical case except for the degenerate case
3<p<5
and
\omega=\omega_{*}(p)\in(\gamma^{2}/4,\infty)
satisfying
\partial_{\omega}\|Q_{\omega,\gamma}\|_{L^{2}}=0
(see
[
25
, Section 8.2]
for the instability result).

Remark 1.6
.

Combet
[
5
]
showed the existence of a family of multi-soliton solutions for the usual NLS with
L^{2}
-supercritical nonlinearity in one dimension. We can expect that a similar result holds for our equation but we do not develop this in the paper.

1.2.
Idea of proofs

We use the method developed by Côte, Martel, and Merle
[
6
]
.

Our main interest is the case that one soliton has zero velocity. In this case, we should consider
Q_{\omega,\gamma}(x)e^{i\omega t+i\theta}
instead of
Q_{\omega,0}(x-x_{1})e^{i\omega_{1}t+i\theta_{1}}
.
To construct the multi-soliton for NLS without potential (
1.1
), the linearized operator around
Q_{\omega,0}
plays an important role. In the
L^{2}
-supercritical case, the linearized operator has one unstable eigenfunction.
On the other hand, for (

δ

NLS
), the linearized operator around
Q_{\omega,\gamma}
has two unstable eigenfunctions, one of which is even and the other is odd. The other solitons
\mathcal{R}_{k}
move away from the origin and thus their interaction with the delta at the origin exponentially decays and we can ignore it. The method of
[
6
]
to control the unstable direction is still applicable, taking into account the two unstable directions for the linearized operator around
Q_{\omega,\gamma}
. According to the method, local well-posedness in
H^{s}
for some
s<1
is also important for the compactness argument. We are not aware of a proof that the equation (

δ

NLS
) is locally well-posed in
H^{s}
for some
s<1
, so we provide one by showing the equivalence of the Sobolev type norm related to
(-\Delta_{\gamma}+1)^{s}
and the usual fractional Sobolev
H^{s}
for
s\in(0,3/2)
.

1.3.
Construction of the paper

Section
2
is devoted to properties of the linearized operator.
In Section
2.1
, we investigate the spectrum of the linearized operator and show that it has two unstable directions when
\gamma<0
. In Section
2.2
, we show coercivity results. Section
3
is devoted to the main result, Theorem
1.1
. In Section
3.1
, we give the statement of a uniform backward estimate and show the main theorem by assuming the uniform backward estimate. We give modulation arguments in Section
3.2
. In Section
3.3
, we give a proof of the uniform backward estimate. In Appendix
A
, we give a proof of the local well-posedness of (
1.1
) in
H^{s}
for
s\in(1/2,1)
, which is used in the proof of the main result by a compactness argument. To show it, we give the equivalency of the Sobolev norm related to
(-\Delta_{\gamma}+1)^{s}
and the usual fractional Sobolev norm with order
s
for
s\in(0,3/2)
. In Appendix
B
, we give the modifications required for the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Linearized operator

2.1.
Spectrum of the linearized operator

Throughout this section, let
\gamma\leq 0
and
\omega>\gamma^{2}/4
.
We also use the notation

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle f,g\rangle:=\Re\int f\overline{g}dx.

We consider the operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}f:=-iL^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}f_{1}+L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma}f_{2},\qquad f=f_{1}+if_{2}\in\mathcal{D}(-{\Delta}_{\gamma}),

where

\displaystyle L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}:=-{\Delta}_{\gamma}+\omega-pQ_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1},

\displaystyle L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma}:=-{\Delta}_{\gamma}+\omega-Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}.

\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}
corresponds to the linear part of the equation (

δ

NLS
) for perturbations around
Q_{\omega,\gamma}
. Indeed,
if we write
u(t,x)=e^{i\omega t}\left(Q_{\omega,\gamma}+v(t,x)\right)
, then (

δ

NLS
) is equivalent to

(2.1)

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}v=\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}v+O(v^{2}).

Note that
\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}
is just
\mathbb{R}
-linear, not
\mathbb{C}
-linear.
For this reason, we sometimes write
\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}
in the matrix form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}=\begin{pmatrix}0&L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma}\\
-L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma})=\mathcal{D}(-{\Delta}_{\gamma})^{2}

by the identification
\mathbb{C}\sim\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

The first part of the main claim in this section is the spectral properties of
\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma}
as follows. The proof is given in Section
2.1.2
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The following are true.

(1)

If
\gamma=0
, then

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{\omega,0})\setminus i\mathbb{R}=\{\pm\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,0}\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,0}>0.

(2)

If
\gamma<0
, then

\displaystyle\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{\omega,\gamma})\setminus i\mathbb{R}=\{\pm\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma},\pm\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}\},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma},\ \mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}>0.

(3)

For
\gamma\leq 0
, the eigenspace associated with
\pm\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}
is one-dimensional, spanned by an even function
Y^{\pm}_{\omega,\gamma}\in\mathcal{D}(-{\Delta}_{\gamma})
with

\langle{Y^{\pm}_{\omega,\gamma}},{Q_{\omega,\gamma}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0
.

(4)

For
\gamma<0
, the eigenspace associated with
\pm\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}
is one-dimensional, spanned by an odd function
Z^{\pm}_{\omega,\gamma}\in\mathcal{D}(-{\Delta}_{\gamma})
.

The new assertion of this paper is the case
\gamma<0
, while
the case
\gamma=0
has been already proven by
[
13
, Corollary 3.1]
(see also
[
26
]
).
In this case, the new unstable mode emerges due to the eigenvalues branching from the kernel of
\mathcal{L}_{\omega,0}
.

2.1.1.
Lemmas

We only consider
\gamma<0
.
We first recall the spectral properties of
L_{\omega,\gamma}^{\pm}
obtained in
[
22
]
.

Lemma 2.2

(The spectrum of
L^{\pm}_{\omega,\gamma}
)
.

The following properties hold:

(1)

We have

\displaystyle\sigma(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})=\{0\}\cup\Sigma^{-}\cup[\omega,\infty),\qquad\Sigma^{-}\subset[\rho^{-},\omega)\quad\text{for some }\rho^{-}>0

where
\Sigma^{-}
consists of point spectrum. Moreover,

\displaystyle\ker(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})=\Span\{Q_{\omega,\gamma}\}.

(2)

We have

\displaystyle\sigma(L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma})=\{\lambda_{0}(\gamma),\lambda_{1}(\gamma)\}\cup\Sigma^{+}(\gamma)\cup[\omega,\infty),\quad\lambda_{0}(\gamma)<\lambda_{1}(\gamma)<0,

\displaystyle\Sigma^{+}(\gamma)\subset[\rho^{+},\omega)\quad\text{for some }\rho^{+}>0,

where
\Sigma^{+}
consists of discrete points. Moreover,

\displaystyle\dim\ker(L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}-\lambda_{0}(\gamma))=\dim\ker(L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}-\lambda_{1}(\gamma))=1,

and the eigenfunction associated with
\lambda_{0}(\gamma)
(resp.
\lambda_{1}(\gamma)
) is even (resp. odd).

We also recall the properties of
Q_{\omega,\gamma}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

The following are true.

(1)

L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}Q_{\omega,\gamma}=-(p-1)Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p}
.

(2)

{\partial}_{\omega}Q_{\omega,\gamma}\in\mathcal{D}(-{\Delta}_{\gamma})_{\mathord{\mathrm{even}}}
, and
L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}{\partial}_{\omega}Q_{\omega,\gamma}=-Q_{\omega,\gamma}
.

(3)

(Vakitov–Kolokolov condition)

\displaystyle{\partial}_{\omega}\left\|{Q_{\omega,\gamma}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\frac{1}{2\omega}\left(\frac{1}{p-1}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\left\|{Q_{\omega,\gamma}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}-\frac{1}{p-1}\frac{{\partial}_{\omega}\alpha_{\omega}}{\sqrt{\omega}}Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{2}(0),

where

\alpha_{\omega}:=\tanh^{-1}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2\sqrt{\omega}}\right)
.
In particular, if
p>5
and
\gamma\leq 0
, then
{\partial}_{\omega}\left\|{Q_{\omega,\gamma}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}<0
.

(4)

If
p>5
and
\gamma\leq 0
, then

\displaystyle\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\mathcal{D}(-{\Delta}_{\gamma})\\
f\perp Q_{\omega,\gamma},\ \left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{2}}=1\end{subarray}}\langle{L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}<0.

Proof.

See
[
9
]
for (1) and (3). (2) follows from direct calculations. Substituting for
f
the normalization of
\phi-\frac{\langle Q_{\omega,\gamma},\phi\rangle_{L^{2}}}{\langle Q_{\omega,\gamma},\partial_{\omega}Q_{\omega,\gamma}\rangle_{L^{2}}}\partial_{\omega}Q_{\omega,\gamma}
, where
\phi
is an even eigenfunction of
L_{\omega,\gamma}^{+}
or substituting a normalized odd eigenfunction
\psi
of
L_{\omega,\gamma}^{+}
, we get (4).
∎

2.1.2.
Proof of Proposition
2.1

We now prove Proposition
2.1
.
We apply the argument of
[
4
]
under even/odd restrictions. Note that the even case is already investigated in
[
16
]
.
For the reader’s convenience, however, we give a proof including both cases.
For a subspace
X\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we define

X_{\perp}:=\{f\in X\ |\ \langle{f},{Q_{\omega,\gamma}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0\}.

We divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1.
Consider

(2.2)

\mu_{1}:=\inf_{(H^{1}_{\mathord{\mathrm{even}}})_{\perp}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\langle{L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}}{\langle{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}}.

This is well-defined since Lemma
2.2
implies that
L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma}
is bijective mapping from
\mathcal{D}(-{\Delta}_{\gamma})_{\perp}\to L^{2}_{\perp}
.
We claim that
-\infty<\mu_{1}<0
. First note that for
f\in(H^{1}_{\mathord{\mathrm{even}}})_{\perp}
, we have

\langle{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle{g},{L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma}g}\rangle_{L^{2}}\geq\rho^{-}\left\|{g}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\qquad g:=(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f.

Hence, by Lemma
2.3
(4), we have
\mu_{1}<0
.
We next show
\mu_{1}>-\infty
. For this,
let
f\in H^{1}_{\perp}
, and write
g=(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f
.
First note that
f\in\mathcal{D}((L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{1/2})
, since
Friedrichs’ second representation theorem implies

\mathcal{D}((L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{1/2})=H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Then for
\varepsilon_{1}>0
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle{f},{L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma}g}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{1/2}f},{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{1/2}g}\rangle\leq\left\|{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{1/2}f}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{1/2}g}\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{1}\left\|{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{1/2}f}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon_{1}}\left\|{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{1/2}g}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{1}\langle{L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}+C_{\varepsilon_{1}}\langle{L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma}g},{g}\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{1}\langle{L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}+(p-1)\varepsilon_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}|f|^{2}dx+C_{\varepsilon_{1}}\langle{f},{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f}\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{1}\langle{L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}+C_{0}(p-1)\varepsilon_{1}\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon_{1}}\langle{f},{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f}\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Now we take
\varepsilon_{1}
such that
C_{0}(p-1)\varepsilon_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
. Then we obtain

(2.3)

0\leq\frac{1}{C}\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\langle{L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}+C\langle{f},{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f}\rangle_{L^{2}},

which yields

-C\leq\frac{\langle{L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}}{\langle{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}}.

Hence the claim follows.

Step 2.
We next claim that the infimum in (
2.2
) is attained. Let
\{f_{n}\}
be a sequence with

\langle{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f_{n}},{f_{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=1,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\langle{L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}f_{n}},{f_{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}}\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}\mu_{1}.

Then by (
2.3
),
\{f_{n}\}
is bounded in
L^{2}
. Since
\mu_{1}<0
, we may assume that
\langle{L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}f_{n}},{f_{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}}<0
for all
n
, which yields

\left\|{{\partial}_{x}f_{n}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\gamma|f_{n}(0)|^{2}+\omega\left\|{f_{n}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}<p\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}|f_{n}|^{2}dx\leq C\left\|{f_{n}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Thus
\{f_{n}\}
is, in fact, bounded in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore,
there exists
f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that
f_{n}\rightharpoonup f
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
by taking subsequence if necessary.
Clearly
f\in(H^{1}_{\mathord{\mathrm{even}}})_{\perp}
. Also, we have

f_{n}(0)\to f(0)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}|f_{n}|^{2}dx\to\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}|f|^{2}dx,

where the latter follows from the Rellich–Kondrachov theorem, after the restriction to a uniformly bounded domain in
n
. Therefore, by lower semi-continuity of norms, we have

\langle{L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\langle{L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}f_{n}},{f_{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\mu_{1}.

This especially implies
f\neq 0
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\leq\langle{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}(f-f_{n})},{(f-f_{n})}\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\langle{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}-2\langle{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f},{f_{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f_{n}},{f_{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}},

and taking
\liminf_{n\to\infty}
gives

\displaystyle 0\leq-\langle{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}+\liminf_{n\to\infty}\langle{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f_{n}},{f_{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\langle{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\langle{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f_{n}},{f_{n}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=1.

Therefore, since
\mu_{1}<0
, we get

\langle{L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}\leq\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{1}\langle{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}},

and thus
f
attains the minimum.

Step 3.
By the Lagrange multiplier theorem, the minimum
\xi_{1}\in(H_{\mathord{\mathrm{even}}}^{1})_{\perp}\setminus\{0\}
should satisfy

L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}\xi_{1}=\alpha(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}\xi_{1}+\beta Q_{\omega,\gamma}

for some
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then, taking inner product with
\xi_{1}
implies
\alpha=\mu_{1}
.
Letting
\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}:=\sqrt{-\mu_{1}}>0
, we have

\begin{pmatrix}0&L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma}\\
-L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\xi_{1}\\
\pm\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}\xi_{1}\mp\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\beta Q_{\omega,\gamma}\end{bmatrix}=\pm\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}\begin{bmatrix}\xi_{1}\\
\pm\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}\xi_{1}\mp\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\beta Q_{\omega,\gamma}\end{bmatrix}.

Thus
\pm\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}
are eigenvalues, with even eigenfunctions

Y^{\pm}_{\omega,\gamma}:=\xi_{1}\pm i(\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}\xi_{1}-\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}^{-1}\beta Q_{\omega,\gamma})
.

Step 4.
We next consider

\mu_{2}:=\inf_{u\in H^{1}_{\mathord{\mathrm{odd}}}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\langle{L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}}{\langle{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}f},{f}\rangle_{L^{2}}}.

Since
H^{1}_{\mathord{\mathrm{odd}}}\subset H^{1}_{\perp}
,
(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}
is well-defined on
H^{1}_{\mathord{\mathrm{odd}}}
.
Moreover, we have
\mu_{2}<0
by Lemma
2.2
(2).
Hence, by the same argument
as above, we have
-\infty<\mu_{2}<0
, and also the infimum is attained by some
\xi_{2}\in H^{1}_{\mathord{\mathrm{odd}}}\setminus\{0\}
. By variational argument, we have

L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}\xi_{2}=\alpha(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}\xi_{2}.

Taking inner product with
\xi_{2}
, we have
\alpha=\mu_{2}<0
.
Define
\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}=\sqrt{-\mu_{2}}
. Then

\begin{pmatrix}0&L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma}\\
-L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\xi_{2}\\
\pm\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}\xi_{2}\end{bmatrix}=\pm\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}\begin{bmatrix}\xi_{2}\\
\pm\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}\xi_{2}\end{bmatrix}.

Thus
\pm\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}
is an eigenvalue, with odd eigenfunctions
Z^{\pm}_{\omega,\gamma}:=\xi_{2}\pm i\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}\xi_{2}
.

Step 5.
Finally, we claim that no other spectrum exist in
\mathbb{C}\setminus i\mathbb{R}
. Since
Y^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}\in H^{1}_{\mathord{\mathrm{even}}}
and
Z^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}\in H^{1}_{\mathord{\mathrm{odd}}}
, we have

\langle{Y^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}},{Z^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}}\rangle=0
,
and thus
Y^{+}_{\omega,\gamma},Z^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}
are linearly independent.
On the other hand, by Lemma
2.2
and the Grillakis–Shatah–Strauss theorem (see
[
14
, Theorem 5.8]
), the dimension of eigenspace for eigenvalues with positive real part should be no more than
2
. Thus,
\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma},\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}
are the all such eigenvalues.
The negative side follows by symmetry. Hence the proof is complete.

2.2.
Coercivity

Next we consider the quadratic form

B_{\omega,\gamma}(u,w):=B^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}(\Re u,\Re w)+B^{-}_{\omega,\gamma}(\Im u,\Im w),\quad u,w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}),

where for
f,g\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we define

\displaystyle B^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}(f,g):=

\displaystyle\langle{L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}f},{g}\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\partial}_{x}f{\partial}_{x}gdx+\omega\int_{\mathbb{R}}fgdx-\gamma f(0)g(0)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}pQ_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}fgdx,

\displaystyle B^{-}_{\omega,\gamma}(f,g):=

\displaystyle\langle{L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma}f},{g}\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\partial}_{x}f{\partial}_{x}gdx+\omega\int_{\mathbb{R}}fgdx-\gamma f(0)g(0)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}fgdx.

B_{\omega,\gamma}
corresponds to the Hessian of the action functional around
Q_{\omega,\gamma}
; namely,

E_{\gamma}(u)+\frac{\omega}{2}M(u)=E_{\gamma}(Q_{\omega,\gamma})+\frac{\omega}{2}M(Q_{\omega,\gamma})+\frac{1}{2}B_{\omega,\gamma}(u,u)+o(|u|^{2}).

By this,
since
E_{\gamma}
and
M
are conserved quantities for (

δ

NLS
),
the coercive subspace of
B_{\omega,\gamma}
can be controlled by conserved quantities.
In this section, we investigate the coercivity of
B_{\omega,\gamma}
when
\gamma<0
,
in the same spirit as in
[
8
,
6
]
where the case
\gamma=0
is considered.

The main claim of this section is the following:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\gamma<0
. Set

G:=\Span\{iQ_{\omega,\gamma},\ i\Re Y^{+}_{\omega,\gamma},\Im Y^{+}_{\omega,\gamma},i\Re Z_{\omega,\gamma}^{+},\Im Z_{\omega,\gamma}^{+}\}.

Then there exists
c>0
such that

(2.4)

B_{\omega,\gamma}(f,f)\geq c\left\|{f}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2},\qquad\forall f\in G^{\perp}.

2.2.1.
Bound of unstable dimension

We first prepare an auxiliary lemma which gives a bound of the unstable dimension for
B_{\omega,\gamma}
. To begin with, we write
the eigenfunctions of
L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}
associated with
\lambda_{0}(\gamma)
,
\lambda_{1}(\gamma)
as
g_{0}
,
g_{1}
, respectively.
Then we claim the following.

Lemma 2.5
.

Set

G_{1}:=\Span\{iQ_{\omega,\gamma},\ g_{0},\ g_{1}\}.

Then there exists
c>0
such that

(2.5)

B_{\omega,\gamma}(f,f)\geq c\left\|{f}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2},\qquad\forall f\in G_{1}^{\perp}.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, there exists
c>0
such that

(2.6)

B_{\omega,\gamma}(f,f)\geq c\left\|{f}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\qquad\forall f\in G_{1}^{\perp}.

To upgrade it into
H^{1}
-coercivity,
we argue by contradiction. Suppose (
2.5
) is not ture, then
there exists
\{f_{n}\}\subset G_{1}^{\perp}
such that

\left\|{f_{n}}\right\|_{H^{1}}=1\qquad\text{and}\qquad B_{\omega,\gamma}(f_{n},f_{n})\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.

By compactness, taking subsequence if necessary, we have
f_{n}\rightharpoonup f_{*}
for some
f_{*}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. By (
2.6
), we have
f_{n}\to 0
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and thus
f_{*}=0
.
Hence

\displaystyle\left\|{{\partial}_{x}f_{n}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=

\displaystyle B_{\omega,\gamma}(f_{n},f_{n})+\gamma|f_{n}(0)|^{2}-\omega\left\|{f_{n}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle+p\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}|f_{1n}|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}|f_{2n}|^{2}dx\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0,

which implies
f_{n}\to 0
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. This contradicts
\left\|{f_{n}}\right\|_{H^{1}}=1
.
∎

2.2.2.
Proof of Proposition
2.4

We divide the proof into several steps.

Step 1.
Let us denote
B[f]:=B_{\omega,\gamma}(f,f)
, and
Y_{\omega,\gamma}^{\pm}=Y_{1}\pm iY_{2}
,
Z_{\omega,\gamma}^{\pm}=Z_{1}\pm iZ_{2}
. We omit subscripts
\omega,\gamma
and just write
Y^{\pm},Z^{\pm}
. We first show

(2.7)

B[f]>0,\qquad\forall f\in G^{\perp}\setminus\{0\}.

Suppose the contrary. Then there exists
h\in G^{\perp}\setminus\{0\}
such that
B[h]\leq 0
.
Then
h
satisfies

(2.8)

\langle{Q_{\omega,\gamma}},{h_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle{Y_{1}},{h_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle{Y_{2}},{h_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle{Z_{1}},{h_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle{Z_{2}},{h_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0,\qquad B[h]\leq 0.

Now we claim

(2.9)

\forall f\in E:=\Span\{iQ_{\omega,\gamma},\ Y^{+},\ Z^{+},\ h\},\qquad B[f]\leq 0.

First note that

B_{\omega,\gamma}(iQ_{\omega,\gamma},f)=0,\qquad\forall f\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}).

Moreover, for
f=f_{1}+if_{2}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle B_{\omega,\gamma}(Y^{+},f)=\langle{L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}Y_{1}},{f_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle{L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma}Y_{2}},{f_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=-\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Y_{2}},{f_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}+\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Y_{1}},{f_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle B_{\omega,\gamma}(Z^{+},f)=\langle{L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}Z_{1}},{f_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}+\langle{L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma}Z_{2}},{f_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=-\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Z_{2}},{f_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}+\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Z_{1}},{f_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}.

Therefore

\left\{\begin{aligned} &B_{\omega,\gamma}(Y^{+},h)=-\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Y_{2}},{h_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}+\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Y_{1}},{h_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0,\\
&B_{\omega,\gamma}(Z^{+},h)=-\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Z_{2}},{h_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}+\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Z_{1}},{h_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0,\\
&B_{\omega,\gamma}(Y^{+},Z^{+})=-\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Y_{2}},{Z_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}+\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Y_{1}},{Z_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

where first two follow from (
2.8
), and the last one follows from

\langle{Y_{j}},{Z_{k}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0\qquad({}^{\forall}j,k=1,2)

by
H^{1}_{\mathord{\mathrm{even}}}\perp H^{1}_{\mathord{\mathrm{odd}}}

. Also,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &B[Y^{+}]=-\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Y_{2}},{Y_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}+\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Y_{1}},{Y_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0,\\
&B[Z^{+}]=-\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Z_{2}},{Z_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}+\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Z_{1}},{Z_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Hence, combining these with the assumption
B[h]\leq 0
, we have (
2.9
).

Step 2.
Next we claim
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}E=4
,
which leads to contradiction to Lemma
2.5
, since
E\cap G_{1}^{\perp}

cannot be empty by
\dim_{\mathbb{R}}G_{1}\leq 3
.
It suffices to show that
iQ_{\omega,\gamma},Y^{+},Z^{+},h
are linearly independent.
Suppose that for some real numbers
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\alpha_{1}iQ_{\omega,\gamma}+\alpha_{2}Y^{+}+\alpha_{3}Z^{+}+\alpha_{4}h=0.

First we take the
B_{\omega,\gamma}
-coupling with
Y^{-}
. Then

\displaystyle B_{\omega,\gamma}(Y^{+},Y^{-})

\displaystyle=-\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Y_{2}},{Y_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}-\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Y_{1}},{Y_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=-2\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Y_{1}},{Y_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-2\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}^{2}\langle{Y_{1}},{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}Y_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}\neq 0

since
Y_{1}\in H^{1}_{\mathord{\mathrm{even}}}\setminus 0
with
Y_{1}\perp Q_{\omega,\gamma}
.
Moreover,

B_{\omega,\gamma}(Z^{+},Y^{-})=-\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Z_{2}},{Y_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}-\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Z_{1}},{Y_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0,

\displaystyle B_{\omega,\gamma}(h,Y^{-})

\displaystyle=\langle{L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}Y_{1}},{h_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}-\langle{L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma}Y_{2}},{h_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Y_{2}},{h_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}-\mathfrak{y}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Y_{1}},{h_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0.

Hence we have
\alpha_{2}=0
. Next we consider the coupling with
Z^{-}
. Then

\displaystyle B_{\omega,\gamma}(Z^{+},Z^{-})

\displaystyle=-\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Z_{2}},{Z_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}-\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Z_{1}},{Z_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=-2\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Z_{1}},{Z_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-2\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}^{2}\langle{Z_{1}},{(L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma})^{-1}Z_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}\neq 0,

\displaystyle B_{\omega,\gamma}(h,Z^{-})

\displaystyle=\langle{L^{+}_{\omega,\gamma}Z_{1}},{h_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}-\langle{L^{-}_{\omega,\gamma}Z_{2}},{h_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Z_{2}},{h_{1}}\rangle_{L^{2}}-\mathfrak{z}_{\omega,\gamma}\langle{Z_{1}},{h_{2}}\rangle_{L^{2}}=0.

Thus we have
\alpha_{3}=0
. Inner product with
iQ_{\omega,\gamma}
in
L^{2}
yields

\alpha_{1}=0
. Since
h\neq 0
, we have
\alpha_{4}=0
, which concludes the claim.

Step 3.
We next show the coercivity (
2.4
).
Suppose the contrary. Since
B[f]>0
in
G^{\perp}

\inf_{f\in G^{\perp}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{B_{\omega,\gamma}(f,f)}{\left\|{f}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}}=0.

Thus there exists
\{f_{n}\}\subset G^{\perp}
s.t.

\left\|{f_{n}}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=1\qquad\text{and}\qquad B_{\omega,\gamma}(f_{n},f_{n})\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0.

By compactness, taking subsequence if necessary, we have
f_{n}\rightharpoonup f_{*}
for some
f_{*}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.
Then the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
2.5

On the other hand, since

is weakly closed, we have
f_{*}\in G^{\perp}
.
Hence (
2.7
) implies
B_{\omega,\gamma}(f_{*},f_{*})=0
, and thus

. However,

\displaystyle\left\|{{\partial}_{x}f_{n}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\omega\left\|{f_{n}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=B_{\omega,\gamma}(f_{n},f_{n})+\gamma|f_{n}(0)|^{2}+p\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}|f_{1n}|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{\omega,\gamma}^{p-1}|f_{2n}|^{2}dx\xrightarrow{n\to\infty}0,

.

Proposition 2.6

(Coercivity for

)

\displaystyle\left\|{{\partial}_{x}f_{n}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\omega\left\|{f_{n}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

which contradicts that
\left\|{f_{n}}\right\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=1
for all
n
. Hence the proof is complete.
∎

3.2.
Modulation

3.2.1.
Time independent modulation

Given
y_{1}^{0},...,y_{k_{0}-1}^{0},y_{k_{0}+1}^{0},...,y_{K}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mu_{1}^{0},...,\mu_{K}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
let

\mathcal{R}^{0}(x):=\sum_{k=1}^{K}Q_{k}(x-y_{k}^{0})e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{k}x+\mu_{k}^{0}\right)}

with
y_{k_{0}}^{0}=0
.
For
\varepsilon>0
, we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}:=\{f\in H^{1}:\|f-\mathcal{R}^{0}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\varepsilon\}.

Lemma 3.2

(Modulation for function independent of time)
.

There exist
\varepsilon_{0},L_{1},C_{1}>0
such that the following holds; if
\min\{|y_{k}^{0}-y_{j}^{0}|:k\neq j\}\geq L_{1}
,
u\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}
,
then there exists a unique
C^{1}
function
(y_{1}(u),...,y_{k_{0}-1}(u),y_{k_{0}+1}(u),...,y_{K}(u);\mu_{1}(u),...,\mu_{K}(u)):\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}\to\mathbb{R}^{K-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{K}
such that

\displaystyle\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}Q_{k})(x-y_{k}^{0}-y_{k})e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{k}x+\mu_{k}^{0}+\mu_{k}\right)}\overline{w(x)}dx=0\qquad(k\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket\setminus\{k_{0}\}),

\displaystyle\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{k}(x-y_{k}^{0}-y_{k})e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{k}x+\mu_{k}^{0}+\mu_{k}\right)}\overline{w(x)}dx=0\qquad(k\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket\setminus\{k_{0}\}),

\displaystyle\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{k_{0}}(x)e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{k_{0}}x+\mu_{k_{0}}^{0}+\mu_{k_{0}}\right)}\overline{w(x)}dx=0,

where

\displaystyle w(x):=u(x)-\sum_{k\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket\setminus\{k_{0}\}}Q_{k}(x-y_{k}^{0}-y_{k})e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{k}x+\mu_{k}^{0}+\mu_{k}\right)}-Q_{k_{0}}(x)e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{k_{0}}x+\mu_{k_{0}}^{0}+\mu_{k_{0}}\right)},

with the property

\displaystyle\|w\|_{H^{1}}+\sum_{k\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket\setminus\{k_{0}\}}|y_{k}|+\sum_{k\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket}|\mu_{k}|\leq C_{1}\left\|{u-\mathcal{R}^{0}}\right\|_{H^{1}}.

Proof.

For simplicity of notation, we suppose
k_{0}=K
. For
{\bm{y}}=(y_{k})_{k=1}^{K-1}
and
{\bm{\mu}}=(\mu_{k})_{k=1}^{K}
, let

w(u,{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}):=u-\sum_{k=1}^{K}Q_{k}(\cdot-y_{k}^{0}-y_{k})e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{k}x+\mu_{k}^{0}+\mu_{k}\right)}

with identification
y_{K}=0
. Obviously, we have

w(\mathcal{R}^{0},\bm{0},\bm{0})=0.

Define
\Phi:B_{\varepsilon_{0}}\times\mathbb{R}^{K-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{K}\to\mathbb{R}^{K-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{K}
by

\Phi(u,{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})=\Phi_{{\bm{y}}^{0},{\bm{\mu}}^{0}}(u,{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}):=\left((\rho_{k}^{1}(u,{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})_{k=1}^{K-1},(\rho_{k}^{2}(u,{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})_{k=1}^{K})\right)

with
{\bm{y}}^{0}:=({\bm{y}}^{0}_{k})_{k=1}^{K-1}
,
{\bm{\mu}}^{0}:=(\mu_{k}^{0})_{k=1}^{K}
and

\rho_{k}^{1}(u,{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}):=\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}Q_{k})(x-y_{k}^{0}-y_{k})e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{k}x+\mu_{k}^{0}+\mu_{k}\right)}\overline{w(u,{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})}dx,\qquad k\in\llbracket 1,K-1\rrbracket,

\rho_{j}^{2}(u,{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}):=\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{j}(x-y_{j}^{0}-y_{j})e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}v_{j}x+\mu_{j}^{0}+\mu_{j}\right)}\overline{w(u,{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})}dx,\qquad j\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket.

First we note
\Phi(\mathcal{R}^{0},\bm{0},\bm{0})=\bm{0}
. By calculation, for
k,k^{\prime}\in\llbracket 1,K-1\rrbracket
and
j,j^{\prime}\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}\rho^{1}_{k}}{{\partial}y_{k^{\prime}}}(\mathcal{R}^{0},\bm{0},\bm{0})

\displaystyle=\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\partial_{x}Q_{k})(x-y_{k}^{0})(\partial_{x}Q_{k^{\prime}})(x-y_{k^{\prime}}^{0})e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}(v_{k}-v_{k^{\prime}})x+(\mu_{k}^{0}-\mu_{k^{\prime}}^{0})\right)}dx

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left\|{{\partial}_{x}Q_{k}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&\text{if }k=k^{\prime},\\
&O\left(e^{-\min\{\sqrt{\omega_{k}},\sqrt{\omega_{k^{\prime}}}\}|y_{k}^{0}-y_{k^{\prime}}^{0}|}\right)&\text{if }k\neq k^{\prime};\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}\rho^{1}_{k}}{{\partial}\mu_{j^{\prime}}}(\mathcal{R}^{0},\bm{0},\bm{0})

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0&\text{if }k=j^{\prime}\\
&-\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\partial}_{x}Q_{k}(x-y_{k}^{0})Q_{j^{\prime}}(x-y_{j^{\prime}}^{0})e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}(v_{k}-v_{j^{\prime}})x+(\mu_{k}^{0}-\mu_{j^{\prime}}^{0})\right)}dx&\text{if }j\neq k^{\prime}\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle=O\left(e^{-\min\{\sqrt{\omega_{k}},\sqrt{\omega_{j^{\prime}}}\}|y_{k}^{0}-y_{j^{\prime}}^{0}|}\right);

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}\rho^{2}_{j}}{{\partial}y_{k^{\prime}}}(\mathcal{R}^{0},\bm{0},\bm{0})

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &0&\text{if }j=k^{\prime}\\
&\Im\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{j}(x-y_{j}^{0})\partial_{x}Q_{k^{\prime}}(x-y_{k^{\prime}}^{0})e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}(v_{j}-v_{k^{\prime}})x+(\mu_{j}^{0}-\mu_{k^{\prime}}^{0})\right)}dx&\text{if }j\neq k^{\prime}\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle=O\left(e^{-\min\{\sqrt{\omega_{j}},\sqrt{\omega_{k^{\prime}}}\}|y_{j}^{0}-y_{k^{\prime}}^{0}|}\right);

\displaystyle\frac{{\partial}\rho^{2}_{j}}{{\partial}\mu_{j^{\prime}}}(\mathcal{R}^{0},\bm{0},\bm{0})

\displaystyle=\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q_{j}(x-y_{j}^{0})Q_{j^{\prime}}(x-y_{j^{\prime}}^{0})e^{i\left(\frac{1}{2}(v_{j}-v_{j^{\prime}})x+(\mu_{j}^{0}-\mu_{j^{\prime}}^{0})\right)}dx

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\left\|{Q_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&\text{if }j=j^{\prime},\\
&O\left(e^{-\min\{\sqrt{\omega_{j}},\sqrt{\omega_{j^{\prime}}}\}|y_{j}^{0}-y_{j^{\prime}}^{0}|}\right)&\text{if }j\neq j^{\prime}.\end{aligned}\right.

Hence, if
\min\{|y_{k}^{0}-y_{j}^{0}|:k\neq j\}\geq L_{1}
, then

{\nabla}_{{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}}\Phi(\mathcal{R}^{0},\bm{0},\bm{0})=D+O(e^{-CL_{1}})

for some
C>0
, where

D=\begin{pmatrix}\diag\left(\left\|{{\partial}_{x}Q_{k}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)_{k\in\llbracket 1,K-1\rrbracket}&0\\
0&\diag\left(\left\|{Q_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)_{j\in\llbracket 1,K\rrbracket}\end{pmatrix}.

In particular,
{\nabla}_{{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}}\Phi(\mathcal{R}^{0},\bm{0},\bm{0})
is invertible if
L_{1}
is sufficiently large, and

(3.3)

\left\|{({\nabla}_{{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}}}\Phi(\mathcal{R}^{0},\bm{0},\bm{0}))^{-1}}\right\|\leq C\quad\text{with }C\text{ independent of }{\bm{y}}^{0},{\bm{\mu}}^{0}.

Thus, by the implicit function theorem, there exist
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and a unique
C^{1}
-class function
({\bm{y}}(u),{\bm{\mu}}(u)):\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}\to\mathbb{R}^{K-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{K}
such that

({\bm{y}}(\mathcal{R}^{0});{\bm{\mu}}(\mathcal{R}^{0}))=(\bm{0},\bm{0})

and
\Phi(u,{\bm{y}}(u),{\bm{\mu}}(u))=\bm{0}

for all
u\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}
.
Then
\frac{d{\bm{y}}}{du}
,
\frac{d{\bm{\mu}}}{du}
is bounded in
\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon_{0}}
, with uniform bound in
{\bm{y}}^{0},{\bm{\mu}}^{0}
, since we have (
3.3
) and since

\left\|{\frac{{\partial}\Phi}{{\partial}u}(u,{\bm{y}},{\bm{\mu}})}\right\|_{H^{-1}}

is uniformly bounded in
H^{1}\times\mathbb{R}^{K-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{K}
.
Hence, for
\varepsilon>0
we have

|({\bm{y}}(u),{\bm{\mu}}(u))-({\bm{y}}(\mathcal{R}^{0}),{\bm{\mu}}(\mathcal{R}^{0}))|\leq C\varepsilon

for
u\in\mathcal{B}_{\varepsilon}
. The rest of the proof is straightforward.
∎

Elementary lemmas

We have

\displaystyle\widehat{G_{i\sqrt{\lambda+t}}}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda+t)},

(

1

T

For

,

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{\gamma}-k^{2})^{-1}=(-\Delta_{0}-k^{2})^{-1}+2\gamma k(-i\gamma+2k)^{-1}\langle\cdot,\overline{G_{k}}\rangle G_{k},

A.1.3.
Proof of Theorem
A.1

To show Theorem
A.1
, we prove some lemmas.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
s\in(0,2)
.
A function
g\in\mathcal{D}((-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2})
is written by

\displaystyle g=\mathcal{A}(g)+\mathcal{B}(g),

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(g)

\displaystyle:=(-\Delta_{0}+\lambda)^{-s/2}(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2}g,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(g)

\displaystyle:=\frac{2\gamma\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}s\right)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{s}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda+t}c_{g}^{\gamma}(t)}{-\gamma+2\sqrt{\lambda+t}}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\lambda+t}|x|}}{2\sqrt{\lambda+t}}dt,

\displaystyle c_{g}^{\gamma}(t)

\displaystyle:=\langle(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2}g,\overline{G_{i\sqrt{\lambda+t}}}\rangle.

Moreover,
\mathcal{A}(g)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
,
\mathcal{B}(g)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{D}((-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2})\to H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
is surjective.

Proof.

By fractional calculus and Lemma
A.2
, we have

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{-s/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}s\right)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{s}{2}-1}(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{-1}\{t+(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{-1}\}^{-1}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}s\right)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{s}{2}-2}\{-\Delta_{\gamma}+(\lambda+t^{-1})\}^{-1}dt.

Applying Lemma
A.4
as
k=i\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}
, we get

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{-s/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}s\right)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{s}{2}-2}\{-\Delta_{\gamma}+(\lambda+t^{-1})\}^{-1}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}s\right)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{s}{2}-2}\{-\Delta_{0}+(\lambda+t^{-1})\}^{-1}dt

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\gamma\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}s\right)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{s}{2}-2}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}\langle\cdot,\overline{G_{i\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}}}\rangle}{-\gamma+2\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}}G_{i\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}}dt

\displaystyle=(-\Delta_{0}+\lambda)^{-s/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\gamma\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}s\right)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{s}{2}-2}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}\langle\cdot,\overline{G_{i\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}}}\rangle}{-\gamma+2\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}}G_{i\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}}dt.

Applying this to
(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2}g
for
g\in\mathcal{D}((-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2})
, we get

\displaystyle g=\mathcal{A}(g)+\mathcal{B}(g),

where it holds that

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(g)=\frac{2\gamma\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}s\right)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{s}{2}-2}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}\langle(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2}g,\overline{G_{i\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}}}\rangle}{-\gamma+2\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}}G_{i\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}}dt

by
G_{i\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}}(x)=\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}|x|}}{2\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}}
, the definition of
c_{g}^{\gamma}
, and change of variables. Therefore, the first statement is shown.
The statement
\mathcal{A}(g)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
is obvious and

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(g)=g-\mathcal{A}(g)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Take
\widetilde{f}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
. Then

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}:=(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{-s/2}(-\Delta_{0}+\lambda)^{s/2}\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{D}((-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2})

. This shows that
\mathcal{A}:\mathcal{D}((-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2})\to H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
is surjective.
∎

Lemma A.6
.

Let
s\in(0,2)
.
For
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2}\varphi=(-\Delta_{0}+\lambda)^{s/2}\varphi+\frac{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}s\right)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{-\gamma t^{\frac{s}{2}}\kappa_{\varphi}(t)}{-\gamma+2\sqrt{t+\lambda}}e^{-\sqrt{t+\lambda}|x|}dt

in the distributional sense, where

\displaystyle\kappa_{\varphi}(t):=\langle\varphi,\overline{G_{i\sqrt{t+\lambda}}}\rangle.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.2
and fractional calculus, we have

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2}\varphi

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}s\right)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{s}{2}-1}(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)\{t+(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)\}^{-1}\varphi dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}s\right)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{s}{2}-1}\{(-\Delta_{\gamma}+t+\lambda)-t\}(-\Delta_{\gamma}+t+\lambda)^{-1}\varphi dt

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}s\right)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{s}{2}-1}\{1-t(-\Delta_{\gamma}+t+\lambda)^{-1}\}\varphi dt.

We also have the similar equation for
(-\Delta_{0}+\lambda)^{s/2}\varphi
. Thus we get

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2}\varphi-(-\Delta_{0}+\lambda)^{s/2}\varphi

\displaystyle=\frac{-\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}s\right)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\frac{s}{2}}\{(-\Delta_{\gamma}+t+\lambda)^{-1}-(-\Delta_{0}+t+\lambda)^{-1}\}\varphi dt.

By using Lemma
A.4
as

\displaystyle(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2}\varphi-(-\Delta_{0}+\lambda)^{s/2}\varphi

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}s\right)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{-\gamma t^{\frac{s}{2}}}{-\gamma+2\sqrt{t+\lambda}}\langle\varphi,\overline{G_{i\sqrt{t+\lambda}}}\rangle e^{-\sqrt{t+\lambda}|x|}dt.

This completes the proof.

We set

\displaystyle c_{f}^{0}(t):=\langle\overline{G_{i\sqrt{\lambda+t}}},(-\Delta_{0}+\lambda)^{s/2}f\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{e^{-\sqrt{\lambda+t}|y|}}{2\sqrt{\lambda+t}}(-\Delta_{0}+\lambda)^{s/2}f(y)dy,

Lemma A.7
.

s>0

For

Lemma A.8
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\|I_{F}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\widehat{I_{F}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\frac{\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}s\right)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{-\gamma t^{\frac{s}{2}}}{-\gamma+2\sqrt{t+\lambda}}\kappa_{F}(t)\frac{2\sqrt{\lambda+t}}{\sqrt{2\pi}(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda+t)}dt\right|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\frac{s}{2}}}{-\gamma+2\sqrt{t+\lambda}}|\kappa_{F}(t)|\frac{\sqrt{\lambda+t}}{|\xi|^{2}+\lambda+t}dt\right)^{2}d\xi

Here, for

\displaystyle|\kappa_{F}(t)|

\displaystyle=|\langle F,\overline{G_{i\sqrt{t+\lambda}}}\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\frac{s}{2}}}{-\gamma+2\sqrt{t+\lambda}}\frac{\|F\|_{H^{s}}}{(\lambda+t)^{1-a}}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda+t}}{(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda)^{b}(\lambda+t)^{1-b}}dt\right)^{2}d\xi

since

.

Next we show
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\supset\mathcal{D}((-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2})
. Let
g\in\mathcal{D}((-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2})
. Then, by Lemma
A.5
, we have
g=\mathcal{A}(g)+\mathcal{B}(g)
. Now, letting

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(f):=\frac{2\gamma\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}s\right)}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{s}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda+t}}{-\gamma+2\sqrt{\lambda+t}}c_{f}^{0}(t)G_{i\sqrt{\lambda+t}}dt

for
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, we have
\mathcal{B}(g)=\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}(g))
. Indeed, this follows from

\displaystyle c_{g}^{\gamma}(t)=\langle(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2}g,\overline{G_{i\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}}}\rangle=\langle(-\Delta_{0}+\lambda)^{s/2}\mathcal{A}(g),\overline{G_{i\sqrt{\lambda+t^{-1}}}}\rangle=c_{\mathcal{A}(g)}^{0}(t).

Thus we have
g=\mathcal{A}(g)+\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}(g))
, where we note that
\mathcal{A}(g)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
. For
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, we have
\mathcal{C}(f)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed, by Lemma
A.7
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{C}(f)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda)^{s/2}\widehat{\mathcal{C}(f)}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda)^{s}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{s}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda+t}}{-\gamma+2\sqrt{\lambda+t}}c_{f}^{0}(t)\widehat{G_{i\sqrt{\lambda+t}}}(\xi)dt\right|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda)^{s}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{s}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda+t}}{-\gamma+2\sqrt{\lambda+t}}c_{f}^{0}(t)\frac{1}{(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda+t)}dt\right|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda)^{s}\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{s}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda+t}}{-\gamma+2\sqrt{\lambda+t}}c_{f}^{0}(t)\frac{1}{(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda)^{a}(\lambda+t)^{1-a}}dt\right|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda)^{s-2a}d\xi\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{s}{2}}\frac{\sqrt{\lambda+t}}{-\gamma+2\sqrt{\lambda+t}}c_{f}^{0}(t)(\lambda+t)^{-1+a}dt\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda)^{s-2a}d\xi\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{s}{2}}c_{f}^{0}(t)(\lambda+t)^{-1+a}dt\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|\xi|^{2}+\lambda)^{s-2a}d\xi\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{-\frac{s}{2}}(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{4}-1+a}dt\right|^{2}\|f\|_{H^{s}}^{2},

where
0\leq a\leq 1
.
Take
a=\frac{2s+9}{12}
, which is in the interval
(3/4,1)
for
s\in(0,3/2)
. Then we have

\displaystyle 2s-4a=\frac{4}{3}s-3<-1,\quad-\frac{s}{2}-\frac{3}{4}-1+a=-\frac{1}{3}s-1<-1,\text{ and }-\frac{s}{2}>-1

for any
s\in(0,3/2)
and thus the integrals for
\xi
and
t
are finite. This implies

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{C}(f)\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Therefore, we get
g=\mathcal{A}(g)+\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}(g))\in H^{s}
.
This means
\mathcal{D}((-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2})\subset H^{s}
.

At last, we show the norm equivalency.
Let
g\in\mathcal{D}((-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2})=H^{s}
.
Since we have
g=\mathcal{A}(g)+\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}(g))
and
\|\mathcal{C}(f)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|g\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|\mathcal{A}(g)\|_{H^{s}}+\|\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{A}(g))\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|\mathcal{A}(g)\|_{H^{s}}.

We have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{A}(g)\|_{H^{s}}=\|(-\Delta_{0}+\lambda)^{s/2}\mathcal{A}(g)\|_{L^{2}}=\|(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2}g\|_{L^{2}}=\|g\|_{H_{\gamma}^{s}}

and thus we get
\|g\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|g\|_{H_{\gamma}^{s}}
.
Moreover, by Lemma
A.8
, we also have

\displaystyle\|g\|_{H_{\gamma}^{s}}=\|(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2}g\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|(-\Delta_{0}+\lambda)^{s/2}g\|_{L^{2}}+\|I_{g}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|g\|_{H^{s}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

A.2.
Sketch of the proof of local well-posedness

We give a sketch proof of the local well-posedness of (

δ

NLS
) in
H^{s}
for
s\in(1/2,1)
.

By Duhamel’s formula, we have

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|e^{it\Delta_{\gamma}}u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta_{\gamma}}(|u|^{p-1}u)(\tau)d\tau\|_{H^{s}}

By the equivalency, i.e., Theorem
A.1
, we get

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|e^{it\Delta_{\gamma}}u_{0}\|_{H_{\gamma}^{s}}+\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta_{\gamma}}(|u|^{p-1}u)(\tau)d\tau\|_{H_{\gamma}^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|e^{it\Delta_{\gamma}}(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{i(t-\tau)\Delta_{\gamma}}(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2}(|u|^{p-1}u)(\tau)d\tau\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\|(-\Delta_{\gamma}+\lambda)^{s/2}(|u|^{p-1}u)\|_{L_{t}^{1}L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\||u|^{p-1}u\|_{L_{t}^{1}H^{s}}.

By the fractional chain rule (see e.g.
[
28
, Lemma A.9]
and
[
17
, Lemma 2.3 (2.15)]
) and the Sobolev embedding

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+T\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L^{\infty}}^{p-1}\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+T\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H^{s}}^{p}.

This gives the local well-posedness for
H^{s}
, where
1/2<s<1
.

Quantitative Amenability for Actions of Finitely Generated Groups
Zihan Xia

Abstract
We generalize the notion of isoperimetric profiles of finitely generated groups to their actions by measuring the boundary of finite subgraphings of the orbit graphing. We prove that like the classical isoperimetric profiles for groups, decay of the isoperimetric profile for an essentially-free action is equivalent to amenability of the action in the sense of Zimmer. For measure-preserving actions, we relate the isoperimetric profiles of the actions and the group.

Let

\mathcal{I}_{G}(n)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\subset G\\

where

[
5
]
. Such isoperimetric profiles for groups have two properties:

1.

The asymptotics of the isoperimetric profile for a finitely generated group are independent of the chosen generators.

2.

the isoperimetric profile converges to zero if and only if

is amenable.

These isoperimetric profiles and Følner functions have been studied by Vershik
[
14
]
, Pittet
[

]
, Stankov
[

]
, Cavaleri
[

]
, Erschler
[

and many others. In this paper we construct isoperimetric profiles for group actions, and we will prove that they satisfy analogue of the above-mentioned properties (1) and (2).

Now assume that
(X,\mu)
is a standard probability measure space and
(G,S)
acting on it. The notion of isoperimetric profile for groups inspires us to construct the similar notion of isoperimetric profile for such action
(G,S)\curvearrowright(X,\mu)

and computing the boundary ratio, we partitioned each orbit into components of finite points. Since every group action induces an orbit measurable equivalence relation

, we choose the measurable subequivalence relation of

to be such partition. In this way, the isoperimetric profile for the actions is computed by the average of the boundary ratio of the orbit subequivalence classes, and then take infimum. In the case of measure-preserving action, such formula can be written as the following definition, and we decide to use this formula for the nonmeasure-preserving actions.

Definition 1.1
.

The isoperimetric profile

of an action
(G,S)\curvearrowright(X,\mu)
is defined and as follows:

\mathcal{I}_{(G,S)\curvearrowright X}(n)\vcentcolon=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta_{n}\subset\mathcal{R}\\
|\theta_{n}|\leq n\end{subarray}}\mu(\partial\theta_{n})=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta_{n}\subset\mathcal{R}\\
|\theta_{n}|\leq n\end{subarray}}\mu\{x\in X|sx\notin[x]_{\theta_{n}}\text{ for some }s\in S\}

We will omit
S
and write

if the generating set is fixed and this does not cause confusion.

In general, we can define the similar isoperimetric profiles for graphings, but currently we don’t know if the relationship between the decay of

and amenability(Theorem
1.2
) still holds in this case.

We will prove that if the Radon-Nikodym derivatives of an action are in

F_{n}=[0,n]\times[0,n]\times[0,n^{2}]\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}
. Translating this cuboid
F_{n}
by
(k(n+1),0,0)
or
(0,0,k(n+1)),k\in\mathbb{Z}
will still result in the same shapes, just like in
(\mathbb{Z}^{3},+)
. If we translate by
(0,k(n+1),0)
, then consider
F_{n}=\sqcup_{i=0}^{n}{F_{in}}
, where
F_{in}=\{(a,b,c)\in F_{n}\mid a=i\}
, and we have

(0,k(n+1),0)F_{in}=\{(a,b+k(n+1),c+k(n+1)i)\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\mid(a,b,c)\in F_{in}\}

(5.7)

So clearly, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
F_{n}
is a tile with the center set

C_{n}={a_{n}\cdot b_{n}\cdot c_{n}:a_{n}\in A_{n},b_{n}\in B_{n},c_{n}\in C_{n}},

(5.8)

where

\displaystyle A_{n}=\{(0,k_{1}(n+1),0)k_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}\}

\displaystyle B_{n}=\{(k_{2}(n+1),0,0)k_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}\}

\displaystyle A_{n}=\{0,0,(k_{1}(n+1))k_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}\}

By computation, it is easy to find that
\frac{|\partial F_{n}|}{|F_{n}|}=\frac{4n^{2}+2n+5}{(n+1)(n^{2}+1)}\sim\frac{4}{n}
. According to the Theorem
1.2
from Stankov’s paper
[
13
]
, which gives a lower bound of the
\mathcal{I}_{G}(n)
, this convergence rate will be exactly the same as
\mathcal{I}(n)
up to a multiplicative constant.

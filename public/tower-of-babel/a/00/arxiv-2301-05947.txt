1.
Introduction

A well known application of Wold decomposition
[
40
]
is Beurling’s theorem
[
4
]
which is a characterization of the shift invariant subspaces of the Hardy space
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}).

Considering doubly commuting isometries, Słociński
in
[
35
]
gave a Wold-type decomposition. In
[
28
]
, Rudin explained that the Beurling theorem fails in general in the multivariable case, that is, for the shift invariant subspaces of the Hardy space on the polydisc. However, assuming
(M_{z_{1}},M_{z_{2}})
is doubly commuting, Mandrekar
[
18
]
gave a Beurling’s type theorem for the Hardy space over the bidisc
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{2})
with help of the Słociński’s decomposition. The case of polydisc
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}),
was discussed by Sarkar, Sasane and Wick
[
31
]
. One of the important problem in multivariable operator theory is to find concrete structure of quotient modules (backward shift invariant subspaces) of function spaces, see for example
[
7
,
9
,
13
,
15
,
38
,
39
]
. A definite application of these multivariable Beurling type theorems in the doubly commuting setting is considered recently by Bhattacharjee, Das, Debnath and Sarkar in
[
6
]
. Indeed, in
[
6
]
, an application to the model space theory in terms of regular dilation is discussed based on a characterization of a Beurling quotient module of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}^{n}).

Isometric covariant representations of
C^{*}
-correspondences play an important role in the theory of Cuntz-Pimsner algebras, see
[
24
]
.
In
[
20
]
, Muhly and Solel proved Wold decomposition in the case of the isometric covariant representations which is based on Popescu’s Wold decomposition
[
26
]
in the setting of a row
isometry. Tensor product system (see
[
2
,
14
]
) is utilized by Solel in
[
34
]
where the notion of doubly commuting covariant representations of the product systems of
C^{*}
-correspondences is considered to study regular dilations.
Skalski and Zacharias proved Wold-type decomposition for the doubly commuting isometric covariant representations. In this direction, Trivedi and Veerabathiran
[
36
]
provided a Beurling–Lax-type theorem for the pure doubly commuting isometric covariant representations of product systems of
C^{*}
-correspondences which is a generalization of Beurling–Lax-type theorem due to Popescu
[
26
]
, Mandrekar
[
18
]
, and Sarkar, Sasane and Wick
[
31
]
. Along this direction and on the theme of
[
6
]
, in this article, we characterize doubly commuting Beurling quotient subspaces and study its application to Sz.Nagy-Foias model space theory in this setting based on regular dilation due to Solel
[
34
]
and Skalski
[
32
]
.

1.1.
Preliminaries and basic results

Assume

\mathbb{E}
to be a family of
C^{*}
-correspondences
\{E_{1},\ldots,E_{k}\},
over a
C^{*}
-algebra

With the help of these unitary isomorphisms, we may identify

for all

Define maps

In this case, we say that

is a

[

A

[

]
) of

and

V^{(i)}(a\xi_{i}b)=\sigma(a)V^{(i)}(\xi_{i})\sigma(b),\;\;\;a,b\in\mathcal{B},\xi_{i}\in E_{i},

and satisfying the commutation relation

(1.1)

\widetilde{V}^{(i)}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(j)})=\widetilde{V}^{(j)}(I_{E_{j}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(i)})(t_{i,j}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}_{V}})

We shall use notations
\widetilde{V}^{(i)}_{l}:E_{i}^{\otimes l}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{V}\to\mathcal{H}_{V}

\widetilde{V}^{(i)}_{l}(\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{l}\otimes h):=V^{(i)}(\xi_{1})\cdots V^{(i)}(\xi_{l})h

\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{l}\in E_{i},h\in\mathcal{H}_{V}

We say that two such a completely bounded covariant representations

and also for each

\xi\in E_{i}

The concept of doubly commuting isometric representation that follows is a generalization of both non-commuting and doubly

Definition 1.1
.

A CB-representation
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\ldots,

2.
Beurling quotient subspace for the covariant representations

In this section, we prove a necessary and sufficient condition regarding when a QS becomes BQS for DCI-representations of a product system. For this purpose, we need some fundamental lemmas regarding BQS and cross-commutators. First, we recall a Beurling-type characterization for the doubly commuting invariant subspaces for DCI- representations.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
be a covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{V}
and
\mathcal{K}
be a
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
-invariant subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{V}.
The natural restriction of
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
to
\mathcal{K}
provides a new representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{K}
and it will be denoted by
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})|_{\mathcal{K}}.
This means that, for
i\in I_{k}
define
V|_{\mathcal{K}}^{(i)}:E_{i}\rightarrow B(\mathcal{K})
by
V|_{\mathcal{K}}^{(i)}(\xi_{i})h=V^{(i)}(\xi_{i})h,\xi_{i}\in E_{i},h\in\mathcal{K},
and the restriction map
{\widetilde{V}|_{\mathcal{K}}^{(i)}}:E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{K}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}
by
{\widetilde{V}|_{\mathcal{K}}^{(i)}}(\xi_{i}\otimes h)=V^{(i)}(\xi_{i}\otimes h).
That is,
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})|_{\mathcal{K}}=(\sigma|_{\mathcal{K}},V|_{\mathcal{K}}^{(1)},\dots,V|_{\mathcal{K}}^{(k)}).

Theorem 2.1
.

[
36

for

and

We are looking for a covariant representation
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)},\dots,V^{(k)})

(\rho,S^{(1)},\dots,S^{(k)})
to QS of

Since

(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)},\dots,V^{(k)})
of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}_{V}

Therefore, consider a completely contractive covariant representation
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)},\dots,V^{(k)})

\mathbb{E}

We need to prove that there exists a isometry
\Pi_{V}:\mathcal{H}_{V}\to\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{H}

(3.2)

\displaystyle\Pi_{V}\sigma(a)=\rho(a)\Pi_{V}\>\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>\>(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\Pi_{V})\widetilde{V}^{(i)*}=\widetilde{S}^{(i)*}\Pi_{V},

Suppose such an isometry

Now using Equation(
3.2
), we obtain

(3.3)

\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}=(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{n}})\widetilde{V}^{(i)*}

for every
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}
and

Indeed

\displaystyle(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{n}})\widetilde{V}^{(i)*}

\displaystyle=(I_{E_{i}}\otimes P_{{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{n})}\otimes\mathcal{H}})(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\Pi_{V})\widetilde{V}^{(i)*}=(I_{E_{i}}\otimes P_{{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{n})}\otimes\mathcal{H}})\widetilde{S}^{(i)*}\Pi_{V}

\displaystyle=(I_{E_{i}}\otimes P_{{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{n})}\otimes\mathcal{H}})\Pi_{V}=P_{{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i})}\otimes\mathcal{H}}\Pi_{V}=\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}},

where
\mathbf{e}_{i}:=(0,0,\dots,1,0,\dots 0),
i.e.,
1
at
i^{th}
place and
0
otherwise. More generally, for
{m_{i}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{n}+{m_{i}}\mathbf{e}_{i}}=(I_{E_{i}^{\otimes m_{i}}}\otimes\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{n}})\widetilde{V}^{(i)*}_{m_{i}},

and hence

\displaystyle\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{n}}=(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{n})}\otimes\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{0}})\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}^{*}.

Indeed, for
\mathbf{n}=({n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{k}})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}

\displaystyle\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{n}}

\displaystyle=(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes{{n_{1}}}}}\otimes\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{n}-{n_{1}}\mathbf{e}_{1}})\widetilde{V}^{(1)*}_{{n_{1}}}

\displaystyle=(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes{{n_{1}}}}}\otimes(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes{{n_{2}}}}}\otimes\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{n}-({n_{1}}\mathbf{e}_{1}+{n_{2}}\mathbf{e}_{2})})\widetilde{V}^{(2)*}_{{n_{2}}})\widetilde{V}^{(1)*}_{{n_{1}}}

\displaystyle=(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes{{n_{1}}}}}\otimes(I_{E_{2}^{\otimes{{n_{2}}}}}\otimes(I_{E_{3}^{\otimes{{n_{3}}}}}\otimes\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{n}-({n_{1}}\mathbf{e}_{1}+{n_{2}}\mathbf{e}_{2}+{n_{3}}\mathbf{e}_{3})})\widetilde{V}^{(3)*}_{{n_{3}}})\widetilde{V}^{(2)*}_{{n_{2}}})\widetilde{V}^{(1)*}_{{n_{1}}}=\cdots

\displaystyle=(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{n})}\otimes\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{0}})\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}^{*}.

Thus we get
\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{n}}=(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{n})}\otimes\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{0}})\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}^{*}.
Therefore, it is sufficient to find
\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{0}}
only, since the remaining
\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{n}}
will be determined by
(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{n})}\otimes\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{0}})\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}^{*}.
Since
\Pi_{V}
is an isometry, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|h\|^{2}=\|\Pi_{V}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}\|\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{n}}h\|^{2}=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}\|(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{n})}\otimes\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{0}})\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}^{*}h\|^{2},\>\>\>\>\>h\in\mathcal{H}_{V}.

Suppose
u=\{u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{r}\}\subseteq I_{k}
such that
u_{1}<u_{2}<\dots<u_{r}.
Define
\mathbf{e}(u):=\mathbf{e}_{u_{1}}+\mathbf{e}_{u_{2}}+\dots+\mathbf{e}_{u_{r}}.

Note that

\displaystyle(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{e}(u))}\otimes\Pi_{V})\widetilde{V}^{*}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}=(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{e}(u))}\otimes\bigoplus_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{n})}\otimes\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{0}})\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}^{*})\widetilde{V}^{*}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}.

Since
(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{e}(u))}\otimes\Pi_{V})

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}^{*}h\|^{2}=\|(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{e}(u))}\otimes\Pi_{V})\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}^{*}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}\|(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{e}(u))}\otimes(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{n})}\otimes\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{0}})\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}^{*})\widetilde{V}^{*}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}h\|^{2},\>\>\>h\in\mathcal{H}_{V}.

Denote by
|u|
the cardinality of
u.
It follows from Equation (
3

\displaystyle\sum_{u\subseteq I_{k}}(-1)^{|u|}\|\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}^{*}h\|^{2}=\sum_{u\subseteq I_{k}}(-1)^{|u|}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}\|(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{e}(u))}\otimes(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{n})}\otimes\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{0}})\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}^{*})\widetilde{V}^{*}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}h\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{k}_{0}}\left({\sum_{{\mathbf{n}\leq\mathbf{m}},{\max\{m_{j}-n_{j}\}\leq 1}}}(-1)^{|\mathbf{m}|-|\mathbf{n}|}\|(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{m})}\otimes\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{0}})\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{m}}^{*}h\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\|\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{0}}h\|^{2}.

Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{u\subseteq I_{k}}(-1)^{|u|}\|\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}^{*}h\|^{2}\geq 0,\>\>\>\>h\in\mathcal{H}_{V},

equivalently

\displaystyle\sum_{u\subseteq I_{k}}(-1)^{|u|}\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}^{*}\geq 0.

Now, define
\Delta_{*}(V)=(\sum_{u\subseteq I_{k}}(-1)^{|u|}\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}^{*})^{\frac{1}{2}}
and
\mathcal{D}_{*,V}=\overline{Im\Delta_{*}(V)}.
Therefore we can choose for
\mathcal{H}
is
\mathcal{D}_{*,V}
and
\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{0}}=\Delta_{*}(V),
thus
\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{n}}=(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{n})}\otimes\Delta_{*}(V))\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}^{*}.
Then for each

we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{V}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}}\|\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{n}}h\|^{2}=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}},{\max n_{j}\leq p-1}}\|(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{n})}\otimes\Pi_{V}^{\mathbf{0}})\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}^{*}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}},{\max n_{j}\leq p-1}}\sum_{{u}\subseteq{I_{k}}}(-1)^{|u|}\|(I_{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{n})}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{*}_{\mathbf{e}(u)})\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}^{*}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{{\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}},{\max m_{j}\leq p}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{n}\leq\mathbf{m},\max\{{m_{j}-n_{j}}\}\leq 1,{\max n_{j}\leq p-1}}(-1)^{|\mathbf{m}|-|\mathbf{n}|}\|\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{m}}^{*}h\|^{2}\right).

Observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{n}\leq\mathbf{m},\max\{{m_{j}-n_{j}}\}\leq 1,{\max n_{j}\leq p-1}}(-1)^{|\mathbf{m}|-|\mathbf{n}|}=0,

whenever
0<m_{i}<p,
for all
i\in I_{k}.
If
m_{i}=0
or
m_{i}=p,
then

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{n}\leq\mathbf{m},\max\{{m_{j}-n_{j}}\}\leq 1,{\max n_{j}\leq p-1}}(-1)^{|\mathbf{m}|-|\mathbf{n}|}=(-1)^{|{\supp\mathbf{m}}|}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{V}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{u\subseteq I_{k}}(-1)^{|u|}\|\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}^{*p}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|h\|^{2}+\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\emptyset\neq{u\subseteq I_{k}}}(-1)^{|u|}\|\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}^{*p}h\|^{2},\>\>\>\>h\in\mathcal{H}_{V}.

Hence
\Pi_{V}
will be an isometry if
SOT-\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\widetilde{V}^{({j})*}_{p}=0
for
j\in I_{k}.

Now we define the following Brehmer-Solel condition.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)},\dots,V^{(k)})
be a pure, completely contractive representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}_{V}.
Then we say it is satisfy

(cf.
[

\displaystyle\sum_{u\subseteq I_{k}}(-1)^{|u|}\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}^{*}\geq 0.

The above observations yield the following theorem: a generalization of
[
1

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)},\dots,V^{(k)})
be a completely contractive covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}_{V},
satisfying Brehmer-Solel condition. Then there exists
\Pi_{V}:\mathcal{H}_{V}\to\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{D}_{*,V},
satisfy

\displaystyle\Pi_{V}\sigma(a)=\rho(a)\Pi_{V}\>\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>\>(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\Pi_{V})\widetilde{V}^{(i)*}=\widetilde{S}^{(i)*}\Pi_{V},

where

and

such that

\displaystyle\|\Pi_{V}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{u\subseteq I_{k}}(-1)^{|u|}\|\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}^{*p}h\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|h\|^{2}+\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{\emptyset\neq{u\subseteq I_{k}}}(-1)^{|u|}\|\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}^{*p}h\|^{2},\>\>\>\>h\in\mathcal{H}_{V}.

In particular, if we take a pure, completely contractive covariant representation, we obtain the following corollary, a generalization of
[
1

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)},\dots,V^{(k)})
be a pure, completely contractive covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{V},
satisfying Brehmer-Solel condition. Then it is unitarily equivalent to the restriction of the induced representation
(\rho,S^{(1)},\dots,S^{(k)})
to QS

Furthermore it dilates the induced representation
(\rho,S^{(1)},S^{(2)},\dots,S^{(k)}),
therefore there exists an isometry
\Pi_{V}:\mathcal{H}_{V}\to\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{H}

3.2
).
Then
\mathcal{K}=\Pi_{V}\mathcal{H}_{V}

\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})\otimes\mathcal{H},
therefore
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)},\dots,V^{(k)})
is isomorphic to
(P_{\mathcal{K}}\rho,P_{\mathcal{K}}S^{(1)},P_{\mathcal{K}}S^{(2)},\dots,P_{\mathcal{K}}S^{(k)})|_{\mathcal{K}}.

Note that if
k=1,
then
\mathcal{K}
will be a BQS under
(\rho,S)
by Theorem
1.5
, i.e. there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{T},
a pure isometric representation
(\mu,T)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}_{T}
and an isometric multi-analytic operator

\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}=(M_{\Theta}(\mathcal{H}_{T}))^{\perp}

The space
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}
is said to be

corresponding to
(\sigma,V).
Therefore, each pure, completely contractive covariant representation is unitarily equivalent to compression of the induced representation to the model space.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
be a completely contractive covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}_{V}.

Now in the
k>1
case, consider
M_{\Theta}:\mathcal{H}_{V}\to\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})\otimes\mathcal{H}
be an isometric multi-analytic operator. Let
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}=(M_{\Theta}(\mathcal{H}_{V}))^{\perp}
and
\mathcal{S}_{\Theta}=M_{\Theta}(\mathcal{H}_{V})
which are BQS and BS of
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})\otimes\mathcal{H}
, respectively. For each

, define

for

and

on

Indeed,

\displaystyle(\phi^{(i)}(a)\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})\otimes\mathcal{H}})\widetilde{W}_{\Theta}^{(i)*}

\displaystyle=(\phi^{(i)}(a)\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})\otimes\mathcal{H}})(I_{E_{i}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}})\widetilde{S}^{(i)*}P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}}

\displaystyle\sum_{{u}\subseteq{I_{k}}}(-1)^{|u|}\widetilde{W}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}\widetilde{W}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}^{*}=\sum_{{u}\subseteq{I_{k}}}(-1)^{|u|}P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}}\widetilde{S}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}\widetilde{S}_{\mathbf{e}(u)}^{*}P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}},

satisfies the Brehmer-Solel condition whenever they are equivalent. One can now ask which type of covariant representation are unitarily equivalent to

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
be a completely contractive covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{V}.
Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

for some BQS
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}\subseteq\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})\otimes\mathcal{H}

Proof.

: Since

which gives the unitary equivalence of representations
\sigma
and

and also for each

P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}}\widetilde{S}^{(i)}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}})=\widetilde{W}_{\Theta}^{(i)}=U\widetilde{V}^{(i)}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes U^{*}),

and Equation (

4.
Factorizations and invariant subspaces

This section establishes a relationship between invariant subspaces of compression of a pure isometric covariant representation with factorization of the corresponding inner function.

Let
(\sigma,V)
and
(\mu,T)
be pure isometric representations of
E
on Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{V}
and
\mathcal{H}_{T},
respectively. Let
{M}_{\Theta}:\mathcal{H}_{V}\to\mathcal{H}_{T}
be an isometric multi-analytic operator.
Let us denote
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}=\mathcal{H}_{T}\ominus M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V},
and
M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}
be BQS and Beurling subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{T}
corresponding to
\Theta,
respectively.
Define

\pi_{\Theta}^{\prime}(a):=P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}}\mu(a)|_{{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}}}\>\mbox{and}\>\>W^{\prime}_{\Theta}(\xi)=P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}}T(\xi)|_{{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}}},

for
\xi\in E
and
a\in\mathcal{B}.
Therefore
(\pi^{\prime}_{\Theta},W^{\prime}_{\Theta})
is a completely contractive covariant representation of
E
on
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{T}.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(\sigma,V)
and
(\mu,T)
be pure isometric representations of
E
on Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{V}
and
\mathcal{H}_{T},
respectively. Suppose
{\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}_{V}}}
and
{\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}_{T}}}
are the generating wandering subspace for
(\sigma,V)
and
(\mu,T),
respectively. Let
{M}_{\Theta}:\mathcal{H}_{V}\to\mathcal{H}_{T}
be an isometric multi-analytic operator. Then the covariant representation
(\pi^{\prime}_{\Theta},W^{\prime}_{\Theta})
has an IS if and only if there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{R},
a pure isometric representation
(\nu,R)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}_{R}
, isometric multi-analytic operators
{M}_{\Phi}:{\mathcal{H}_{R}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{T}}
,
{M}_{\Psi}:{\mathcal{H}_{V}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{R}}
such that

{\Theta}={\Phi}{\Psi}.

Proof.

(
\implies
)
Let
\mathcal{S}
be a nontrivial
(\pi^{\prime}_{\Theta},W^{\prime}_{\Theta})
-IS of
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{T},
then
\mathcal{S}\oplus M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}
is a

-IS of
\mathcal{H}_{T}.
Since
(\mu,T)
is pure, using Theorem
1.5
, there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{R}
, a pure isometric representation
(\nu,R)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}_{R}
and an isometric multi-analytic operator

\mathcal{S}\oplus M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}=M_{\Phi}{\mathcal{H}_{R}}.

Note that
M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}\subseteq M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R}
and
\mathcal{S}=M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R}\ominus M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}.
Then by Douglas’s range inclusion theorem
[
12
]
, there exists a contraction

such that

Using multi-analytic property of
M_{\Phi}
and
M_{\Theta}
, we obtain

Thus
\widetilde{R}(I_{E}\otimes Z)=Z\widetilde{V}
and similarly
\nu(a)Z=Z\sigma(a),
for
a\in\mathcal{B},
i.e.,
Z
is multi-analytic, therefore

, for some inner operator
\Psi:\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}_{V}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{R}}.
Indeed,
{\Theta}={\Phi}{\Psi}
and
M_{\Phi}
and
M_{\Theta}
are isometries, we deduce

\|h\|=\|M_{\Theta}h\|=\|M_{\Phi}M_{\Psi}h\|=\|M_{\Psi}h\|,\>\>\>h\in\mathcal{H}_{V}.

(

) On the other hand, suppose there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{R},
a pure isometric representation
(\nu,R)
of
E
on
\mathcal{H}_{R}
and isometric multi-analytic operators
M_{\Psi}:{\mathcal{H}_{V}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{R}}
and
M_{\Phi}:{\mathcal{H}_{R}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}_{T}}
such that

{\Theta}={\Phi}{\Psi}.

Since
{\Theta}={\Phi}{\Psi},
we have
M_{\Theta}(\mathcal{H}_{V})\subseteq M_{\Phi}(\mathcal{H}_{R}).
Define

Clearly
\mathcal{S}

Also note that

Since

and

, we have
\mathcal{K}_{\Phi}

.. Indeed let

\displaystyle\widetilde{W^{\prime*}_{\Theta}}h=(I_{E}\otimes P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}})\widetilde{T}^{*}P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}}h=\widetilde{T}^{*}P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}}h=\widetilde{T}^{*}h=\widetilde{T}^{*}P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Phi}}h=\widetilde{W^{\prime*}_{\Phi}}h\in\mathcal{K}_{\Phi},

where

Also in similar way
\mathcal{S}^{\perp}

That is

But this result is not true in general for multi-variable case (see

classify factorizations of inner functions in terms of invariant subspaces of tuples of module operators

and

be BQS and Beurling subspace of

corresponding to
\Theta,

For each

and hence

\mathbb{E}
on

We are now ready for the multi-variable analog of Theorem
4.1
. The following theorem is a generalization of
[
6
, Theorem 4.4]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
and
(\mu,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k)})
be pure DCI-representations of
\mathbb{E}
on Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{V}
and
\mathcal{H}_{T},
respectively. Let
{M}_{\Theta}:\mathcal{H}_{V}\to\mathcal{H}_{T}
be an isometric multi-analytic operator. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

There exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{R},
a pure DCI-representation
(\nu,R^{(1)},\dots,R^{(k)})
of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}_{R}
and isometric multi-analytic operators
{M}_{\Phi}:{\mathcal{H}_{R}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{T}}
,
{M}_{\Psi}:{\mathcal{H}_{V}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{R}}
such that

{\Theta}={\Phi}{\Psi}.

(2)

There exists
(\pi^{\prime}_{\Theta},W^{{}^{\prime}(1)}_{\Theta},\dots,W^{{}^{\prime}(k)}_{\Theta})
-IS
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}
such that
\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathcal{S}_{\Theta}
is a Beurling subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{T}.

(3)

There exists
(\pi^{\prime}_{\Theta},W^{{}^{\prime}(1)}_{\Theta},\dots,W^{{}^{\prime}(k)}_{\Theta})
-IS
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}
of
\mathcal{H}_{T}
such that

\displaystyle(I_{E_{j}}\otimes((I_{E_{i}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}\ominus\mathcal{S}})-\widetilde{U}^{(i)*}\widetilde{U}^{(i)}))(t_{i,j}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}_{T}})

\displaystyle\>\>\>\>\>(I_{E_{i}}\otimes((I_{E_{j}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}\ominus\mathcal{S}})-\widetilde{U}^{(j)*}\widetilde{U}^{(j)}))=0,

where
\widetilde{U}^{(i)}=P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}\ominus\mathcal{S}}\widetilde{W^{\prime}_{\Theta}}^{(i)}{{}_{|_{{E_{i}}\otimes{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}\ominus\mathcal{S}}}}},
for each
i\in I_{k}.

Proof.

(1)\implies(2)
: Suppose there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{R},
a pure DCI-representation
(\nu,R^{(1)},\dots,R^{(k)})
of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}_{R}
and isometric multi-analytic operators
{M}_{\Phi}:{\mathcal{H}_{R}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{T}}
,
{M}_{\Psi}:{\mathcal{H}_{V}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{R}}
such that
{\Theta}={\Phi}{\Psi}.
Therefore
M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}\subseteq M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R},
we can define
\mathcal{S}:=M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R}\ominus M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}.
Clearly
\mathcal{S}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}.
Also note that
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}\ominus\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{K}_{\Phi}.
Since
\widetilde{W^{\prime}_{\Theta}}^{(i)*}=(I_{E_{i}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}})\widetilde{T}^{(i)*}P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}},
and
\mathcal{K}_{\Phi}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}
, we have
\mathcal{K}_{\Phi}
is invariant under
\pi^{\prime}_{\Theta}
and
\widetilde{W^{\prime}_{\Theta}}^{(i)*}
for each
i\in I_{k}.
Indeed, let
h\in\mathcal{K}_{\Phi}

\displaystyle\widetilde{W^{\prime}_{\Theta}}^{(i)*}h

\displaystyle=(I_{E}\otimes P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}})\widetilde{T}^{(i)*}P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}}h=\widetilde{T}^{(i)*}P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}}h=\widetilde{T}^{(i)*}h=\widetilde{T}^{(i)*}P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Phi}}h

\displaystyle=\widetilde{W^{\prime}_{\Phi}}^{(i)*}h\in\mathcal{K}_{\Phi}.

Also in similar way
\mathcal{S}^{\perp}
is invariant under
\widetilde{W^{\prime}_{\Theta}}^{(i)}
. That is
\mathcal{S}
is an
(\pi_{\Theta},W^{{}^{\prime}(1)}_{\Theta},\dots,W^{{}^{\prime}(k)}_{\Theta})
-IS.

(2)\implies(1)
: Let
\mathcal{S}
be a
(\pi^{\prime}_{\Theta},W^{{}^{\prime}(1)}_{\Theta},\dots,W^{{}^{\prime}(k)}_{\Theta})
-IS of
\mathcal{H}_{T}
such that
\mathcal{S}\oplus M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}
is a DCS of
\mathcal{H}_{T}
. Therefore by using Theorem
2.1
, there exist a pure DCI-representation
(\nu,R^{(1)},\dots,R^{(k)})
of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}_{R}
and an isometric multi-analytic operator
M_{\Phi}:{\mathcal{H}_{R}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{T}}
such that

(4.1)

\mathcal{S}\oplus M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}=M_{\Phi}{\mathcal{H}_{R}}.

Thus
M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}\subseteq M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R},
and hence by Douglas’s range inclusion theorem, there exists a contraction
Z:\mathcal{H}_{V}\to\mathcal{H}_{R}
such that

M_{\Theta}=M_{\Phi}Z.

Using multi-analytic property of
M_{\Phi}
and
M_{\Theta}
, we get

M_{\Phi}\widetilde{R}^{(i)}(I_{E}\otimes Z)=\widetilde{T}^{(i)}(I_{E}\otimes M_{\Phi}Z)=\widetilde{T}^{(i)}(I_{E}\otimes M_{\Theta})=M_{\Theta}\widetilde{V}^{(i)}=M_{\Phi}Z\widetilde{V}^{(i)},

for each
i\in I_{k}.
Thus
\widetilde{R}^{(i)}(I_{E}\otimes Z)=Z\widetilde{V}^{(i)}
, similarly
\nu(a)Z=Z\sigma(a),
for
a\in\mathcal{B}.
Then there exists an inner operator
\Psi:\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}_{V}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{R}}
such that
Z={M}_{\Psi}.
Indeed,
{\Theta}={\Phi}{\Psi}
and using
M_{\Phi}
and
M_{\Theta}
are isometries, we deduce

\|h\|=\|M_{\Theta}h\|=\|M_{\Phi}M_{\Psi}h\|=\|M_{\Psi}h\|,\>\>\>h\in\mathcal{H}_{V}.

(1)\implies(3)
: Suppose there exist a pure DCI-representation

(\nu,R^{(1)},\dots,R^{(k)})
of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}_{R}
and isometric multi-analytic operators
M_{\Phi}:{\mathcal{H}_{R}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{T}}
,
M_{\Psi}:{\mathcal{H}_{V}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{R}}
such that
{\Theta}={\Phi}{\Psi}.
Define
\mathcal{S}=M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R}\ominus M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}
and then
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}\ominus\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{K}_{\Phi}.
Therefore
\mathcal{K}_{\Phi}
is a BQS of
\mathcal{H}_{T}
using Theorem
1.7
, we get

(I_{E_{j}}\otimes((I_{E_{i}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Phi}})-\widetilde{U}^{(i)*}\widetilde{U}^{(i)}))(t_{i,j}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}_{T}})(I_{E_{i}}\otimes((I_{E_{j}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Phi}})-\widetilde{U}^{(j)*}\widetilde{U}^{(j)}))=0,

where
\widetilde{U}^{(i)}=P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Phi}}\widetilde{W^{\prime}_{\Theta}}^{(i)}{{}_{|_{{E_{i}}\otimes{\mathcal{K}_{\Phi}}}}},
for each
i\in I_{k}.

(3)\implies(2)
: Since
\widetilde{W^{\prime}_{\Theta}}^{(i)}=(I_{E}\otimes P_{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}})\widetilde{T}^{(i)*}|_{{\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}}},
it follows that
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}\ominus\mathcal{S}
is a QS of
\mathcal{H}_{T}
. Therefore by hypothesis and using Theorem
1.7
,
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}\ominus\mathcal{S}
is a BQS. Now observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}\ominus\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{H}_{T}\cap(M_{\Theta}(\mathcal{H}_{V})\cup\mathcal{S})^{\perp}=\mathcal{H}_{T}\ominus(M_{\Theta}(\mathcal{H}_{V})\oplus\mathcal{S}),

which implies that
\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathcal{S}_{\Theta}
is a Beurling submodule of
\mathcal{H}_{T}.

∎

In the above theorem we discussed only non-trivial
(\pi^{\prime}_{\Theta},W^{{}^{\prime}(1)}_{\Theta},\dots,W^{{}^{\prime}(k)}_{\Theta})
-IS
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}
of
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}
. Let us discuss a trivial case:

(1)

First take
\mathcal{S}=\{0\}
. Recall that
\mathcal{S}=M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R}\ominus M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}
therefore
M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R}\ominus M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}=\{0\}
, it follows that
M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R}\subseteq M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}.
Since
{\Theta}={\Phi}{\Psi}
,
M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}\subseteq M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R}.
Hence

\displaystyle M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R}=M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}=M_{\Phi}M_{\Psi}\mathcal{H}_{V},

we obtain
M_{\Psi}\mathcal{H}_{V}=\mathcal{H}_{R}.
Thus
{\Psi}
is a unitary.

(2)

Now take
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}
. Therefore

\displaystyle M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R}\ominus M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}=\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}=\mathcal{H}_{T}\ominus M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V},

it implies that we obtain
M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R}=\mathcal{H}_{T}.
Thus
{\Phi}
is a unitary.

Therefore from the above discussion, if
\mathcal{S}
is a non-trivial
(\pi^{\prime}_{\Theta},W^{{}^{\prime}(1)}_{\Theta},\dots,W^{{}^{\prime}(k)}_{\Theta})
-IS of
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}
if and only if isometric multi-analytic operators
{M}_{\Phi}:{\mathcal{H}_{R}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{T}}
,
{M}_{\Psi}:{\mathcal{H}_{V}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{R}}
are not unitary.
The following corollary is a generalization of
[
6
, Corollary 4.5]
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},\dots,V^{(k)})
and
(\mu,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k)})
be pure DCI-representations of
\mathbb{E}
on Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{V}
and
\mathcal{H}_{T},
respectively. Let
{M}_{\Theta}:{\mathcal{H}_{V}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{T}}
be an isometric multi-analytic operator. Then isometric multi-analytic operators
{M}_{\Phi}:{\mathcal{H}_{R}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{T}}
,
{M}_{\Psi}:{\mathcal{H}_{V}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{R}},
coming from Theorem (
4.2
), are non-unitary if and only if the following holds:

(1)

\mathcal{S}
is a non-trivial
(\pi^{\prime}_{\Theta},W^{{}^{\prime}(1)}_{\Theta},\dots,W^{{}^{\prime}(k)}_{\Theta})
-IS of
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}.

(2)

\mathcal{S}
is not a Beurling subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{T}.

(3)

\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}\ominus\mathcal{S}
does not reduce
(\mu,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k)})
.

Proof.

First assume isometric multi-analytic operators
{M}_{\Phi}:{\mathcal{H}_{R}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{T}}
,
{M}_{\Psi}:{\mathcal{H}_{V}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{R}}
are non-unitary. As by above discussion, if
\mathcal{S}
is a non-trivial
(\pi^{\prime}_{\Theta},W^{{}^{\prime}(1)}_{\Theta},\dots,W^{{}^{\prime}(k)}_{\Theta})
-IS of
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}
if and only if isometric multi-analytic operators
{M}_{\Phi}
,
{M}_{\Psi}
are not unitary. It proves (1).

Let, if possible,
\mathcal{S}
be a Beurling subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{T}
. Therefore by using Theorem
2.1
, there exist a pure DCI-representation
(\mu_{1},R_{1}^{(1)},\dots,R_{1}^{(k)})
of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}_{R_{1}}
and an isometric multi-analytic operator
M_{\Phi_{1}}:{\mathcal{H}_{R_{1}}}\to{\mathcal{H}_{T}}
such that
\mathcal{S}=M_{\Phi_{1}}{\mathcal{H}_{R_{1}}}.
But

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R}\ominus M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}=M_{\Phi_{1}}{\mathcal{H}_{R_{1}}},

we have
M_{\Phi_{1}}{\mathcal{H}_{R_{1}}}\subseteq M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R},
and hence by Douglas’s range inclusion theorem, there exists a contraction
Z:\mathcal{H}_{R_{1}}\to\mathcal{H}_{R}
such that

M_{\Phi_{1}}=M_{\Phi}Z.

Using multi-analytic property of
M_{\Phi}
and
M_{\Phi_{1}}
, we get

M_{\Phi}\widetilde{R}^{(i)}(I_{E}\otimes Z)=\widetilde{T}^{(i)}(I_{E}\otimes M_{\Phi}Z)=\widetilde{T}^{(i)}(I_{E}\otimes M_{\Phi_{1}})=M_{\Phi_{1}}\widetilde{R}_{1}^{(i)}=M_{\Phi}Z\widetilde{R}_{1}^{(i)},

for each
i\in I_{k}.
Thus
\widetilde{R}^{(i)}(I_{E}\otimes Z)=Z\widetilde{R}_{1}^{(i)}
, similarly
\nu(a)Z=Z\mu_{1}(a),
for
a\in\mathcal{B}.
Then there exists inner operator
\Phi_{2}:\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}_{R_{1}}}\to\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{H}_{R}}
such that
Z={M}_{\Phi_{2}}.
Indeed,
\Phi_{1}=\Phi\Phi_{2}
and
M_{\Phi}
and
M_{\Phi_{1}}
are isometries, we deduce

\|h\|=\|M_{\Phi_{1}}h\|=\|M_{\Phi}M_{\Phi_{2}}h\|=\|M_{\Phi_{2}}h\|,\>\>\>h\in\mathcal{H}_{R_{1}}.

By using Equation (
4.2
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=M_{\Phi_{1}}{\mathcal{H}_{R_{1}}}M_{\Phi}(M_{\Phi_{2}}{\mathcal{H}_{R_{1}}})=M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R}\ominus M_{\Theta}\mathcal{H}_{V}

\displaystyle=M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R}\ominus M_{\Phi}M_{\Psi}\mathcal{H}_{V}

\displaystyle=M_{\Phi}(\mathcal{H}_{R}\ominus M_{\Psi}\mathcal{H}_{V}),

we obtain
M_{\Phi_{2}}{\mathcal{H}_{R_{1}}}=\mathcal{H}_{R}\ominus M_{\Psi}\mathcal{H}_{V}=\mathcal{K}_{\Psi}.
It implies that
\mathcal{K}_{\Psi}
is

(\nu,R^{(1)},\dots,R^{(k)})
-reducing subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{R}
It implies that
\mathcal{K}_{\Psi}
is trivial subspace which means that
\Psi
is an unitary. But
\Psi
is not a unitary operator; thus, it is a contradiction. Hence
\mathcal{S}
is not a Beurling subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{T}
. It proves (2).

Suppose
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}\ominus\mathcal{S}
reduces
(\mu,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k)}).
Then
(\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}\ominus\mathcal{S})^{\perp}=\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathcal{S}_{\Theta}
is also reduces
(\mu,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k)}).
On the other hand, By using Equation (
4.1
)
\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathcal{S}_{\Theta}=M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R},
it follows that
\Phi
is an unitary, which is contradiction as
\Phi
is not unitary inner operator.

For the converse part, suppose
\mathcal{S}
is a non-trivial
(\pi_{\Theta},W^{(1)}_{\Theta},\dots,W^{(k)}_{\Theta})
-IS of
\mathcal{K}_{\Theta}.
Since
\Theta=\Phi\Psi
and
\Theta
is non constant, both
\Phi
and
\Psi
can not be unitaries. It remains to show that
\Phi
and
\Psi
can not be isometry operators. Let, if possible,
\Phi\equiv X_{1}
for some non-unitary isometry
X_{1}
and
\Psi
is not unitary. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathcal{S}_{\Theta}=M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R}=X_{1}\mathcal{H}_{R}=\mathcal{H}_{X_{1}R}

and hence
\mathcal{S}\oplus\mathcal{S}_{\Theta}
reduces
(\mu,T^{(1)},\dots,T^{(k)}),
which contradicts to (3). On the other hand, if
\Psi\equiv X_{2}
for some non-unitary isometry
X_{2}
and
\Phi
is not unitary. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=M_{\Phi}(\mathcal{H}_{R}\ominus M_{\Psi}\mathcal{H}_{V})=M_{\Phi}(\mathcal{H}_{R}\ominus(X_{2}\mathcal{H}_{V}))=M_{\Phi}\mathcal{H}_{R-X_{2}V}

is a Beurling subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{T},
which contradicts to (2). It completes the proof.
∎

1.
Introduction

The paper deals with the following boundary value problem for elliptic equations

\begin{cases}\mathrm{div}\,(\mathcal{A}(x,Du))=b(x)\qquad\qquad\text{in}\,\,\Omega\cr\cr u=u_{0}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded open set and the operator
\mathcal{A}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Carathédory map satisfying the following set of assumptions for a couple of exponents
p,q
such that
1<p<q
and
1<p\leq 2

\langle\mathcal{A}(x,\xi)-\mathcal{A}(x,\eta),\xi-\eta\rangle\geq\nu|\xi-\eta|^{2}(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}

(1.2)

|\mathcal{A}(x,\xi)-\mathcal{A}(x,\eta)|\leq L|\xi-\eta|\left[(1+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{q-2}{2}}+{(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}}\right]

(1.3)

|\mathcal{A}(x,\xi)-\mathcal{A}(y,\xi)|\leq|x-y|(k(x)+k(y))(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{q-1}{2}}

(1.4)

where
L,\nu
are fixed constants,
\mu\in[0,1]
is a parameter and
k\in L^{r}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
with
r>n
is a non negative function.

Let us observe that if we assume that

\mathcal{A}(x,0)=0,

assumption (
1.3
) implies

|\mathcal{A}(x,\xi)|\leq L(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{q-1}{2}}

(1.5)

Then, setting
f(x)=\mathcal{A}(x,0)\in L^{\infty}
, we have

|\mathcal{A}(x,\xi)|\leq L(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{q-1}{2}}+f(x)

To simplify the presentation, from now on, we shall assume that (

Assumption (

), by virtue of the characterization of the Sobolev function due to Hajlasz,
[

belongs to the Sobolev space

such that

|D_{x}\mathcal{A}(x,\xi)|\leq\tilde{k}(x)(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{q-1}{2}}.

(1.6)

We prove the following existence and higher differentiability result.

Theorem 1.1

Assume that

satisfy (

or if

\frac{q}{p}<1+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}\qquad\qquad\qquad

(1.8)

The following lemma has important applications in the so called

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
h:[r,R_{0}]\to\mathbb{R}
be a nonnegative bounded function and
0<\vartheta<1
,

A,B\geq 0
and
\beta>0
. Assume that

h(s)\leq\vartheta h(t)+\frac{A}{(t-s)^{\beta}}+B,

h(r)\leq\frac{cA}{(R_{0}-r)^{\beta}}+cB,

where
c=c(\vartheta,\beta)>0
.

We introduce the usual notation

\displaystyle 2^{*}=\begin{cases}\frac{2n}{n-2}\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{if}\,\,n>2\\
\text{any finite exponent}\,\,t>2\qquad\,\,\,\text{if}\,\,n=2\end{cases}

For
p>1
and
\mu\in[0,1]
, let us define

V_{p}(\xi)=(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\xi,\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(2.1)

We shall use the following estimates, whose proof can be found in
[
1
]

[
34

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
1<p<2
. There exists a constant
c=c(n,p)>0

,
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

c^{-1}\Bigl(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\leq\frac{|V_{p}(\xi)-V_{p}(\eta)|^{2}}{|\xi-\eta|^{2}}\leq c\Bigl(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}\Bigr)^{\frac{p-2}{2}}

(2.2)

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
1<p<2
and
u\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
. Then

V_{p}(Du)\in L^{2^{*}}_{\textnormal{loc}}(\Omega)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Du\in L^{\frac{2^{*}p}{2}}_{\textnormal{loc}}(\Omega).

Proof.

Note that the thesis is obvious if
\mu=0
.
In case
\mu>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|Du|^{\frac{2^{*}p}{2}}\,dx=\int_{B_{R}}\left||Du|^{\frac{p}{2}-1}Du\right|^{2^{*}}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{x\in B_{R}:\,|Du|\leq\mu\}}\left||Du|^{\frac{p}{2}-1}Du\right|^{2^{*}}\,dx+\int_{\{x\in B_{R}:\,|Du|>\mu\}}\left||Du|^{\frac{p}{2}-1}Du\right|^{2^{*}}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\{x\in B_{R}:\,|Du|\leq\mu\}}\left||Du|^{\frac{p}{2}-1}Du\right|^{2^{*}}\,dx+\int_{\{x\in B_{R}:\,|Du|>\mu\}}\left|\left(\frac{|Du|^{2}}{2}+\frac{|Du|^{2}}{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}|Du|\right|^{2^{*}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu^{\frac{2^{*}p}{2}}|B_{R}|+\int_{\{x\in B_{R}:\,|Du|>\mu\}}\left|\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{2}+\frac{|Du|^{2}}{2}\right)^{\frac{p-2}{4}}|Du|\right|^{2^{*}}\,dx,

since
p-2<0
. Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|Du|^{\frac{2^{*}p}{2}}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\leq\mu^{\frac{2^{*}p}{2}}|B_{R}|+\int_{\{x\in B_{R}:\,|Du|>\mu\}}\left|V_{p}(Du)\right|^{2^{*}}\,dx

(2.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\left(1+\left|V_{p}(Du)\right|^{2^{*}}\right)\,dx<+\infty,

(2.5)

where we also used that
\mu\leq 1
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
1<p<2
and
u\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
. Then

V_{p}(Du)\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u\in W^{2,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

Proof.

By the use of Hölder’s inequality we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|D^{2}u|^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|D^{2}u|^{p}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p(p-2)}{4}}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p(2-p)}{4}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B_{R}}|D^{2}u|^{2}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{R}}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\left(\int_{B_{R}}|D(V_{p}(Du))|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{R}}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{\frac{2-p}{2}}

∎

Difference quotient

We recall some properties of the finite difference

We start with the description of some elementary properties that can be found, for example, in

]

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
f
and
g
be two functions such that

f,g\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, with
p\geq 1
, and let us consider the set

\Omega_{|h|}:=\left\{x\in\Omega:dist(x,\partial\Omega)>|h|\right\}.

Then

(d1)

\tau_{h}f\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and

D_{i}(\tau_{h}f)=\tau_{h}(D_{i}f).

(d2)

If at least one of the functions
F
or
G
has support contained
in
\Omega_{|h|},
then

\int_{\Omega}f\,\tau_{h}g\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}g\,\tau_{-h}f\,dx.

(d3)

We have

\tau_{h}(fg)(x)=f(x+h)\tau_{h}g(x)+g(x)\tau_{h}f(x).

The next result about finite difference operator is a kind of integral version

Lemma 2.6

,

3.
Proof of the a-priori estimate

This section is devoted to the proof of an a-priori estimate, which is at the same time a first regularity result, for local solution to the following equation

\mathrm{div}(\mathcal{A}(x,Du))=b(x)\qquad\qquad\text{in}\,\,\,\Omega

(3.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded open set and the operator
\mathcal{A}:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N\times n}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Carathéodory map satisfying the following set of assumptions for a couple of exponents
1<p<q
with
1<p\leq 2

\langle\mathcal{A}(x,\xi)-\mathcal{A}(x,\eta),\xi-\eta\rangle\geq\nu|\xi-\eta|^{2}(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}

(3.2)

|\mathcal{A}(x,\xi)-\mathcal{A}(x,\eta)|\leq L|\xi-\eta|\left[(1+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{p+q-4}{4}}+(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\right]

(3.3)

|\mathcal{A}(x,\xi)-\mathcal{A}(y,\xi)|\leq|x-y|(k(x)+k(y))(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p+q-2}{4}}

(3.4)

where
c_{1},c_{2},L,\nu>0
are fixed constants,
\mu\in[0,1]
is a parameter,
k\in L^{r}
with
r>n
is a non negative function.

We define a local solution to (
3.1
) as a function
u\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
that satisfies the following integral identity

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathcal{A}(x,Du),D\varphi\rangle\,dx=\int_{\Omega}b(x)\varphi(x)\,dx\qquad\text{for all}\,\,\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega).

(3.5)

Our next aim is to prove the following

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
u\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
be a local solution to (
3.1
) under the assumptions (
3.2
)–(
3.4
) such that

Du\in L^{\frac{2^{*}p}{2}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega).

(3.6)

Assume moreover that

\frac{q}{p}<1+\min\left\{2\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{r}\right),\,\frac{4(p-1)}{p(n-2)}\right\}\qquad\text{if}\,\,n>2

(3.7)

or

\frac{q}{p}<1+2\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{r}\right),\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\text{if}\,\,n=2.

(3.8)

Then

u\in W^{2,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega),\qquad V_{p}(Du)\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)

and the following estimates

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}|D(V_{p}(Du))|^{2}\,dx\leq c(1+||k||_{L^{r}(B_{R})}+||b||_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B_{R})})^{\gamma}\left(1+\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\gamma},

(3.9)

and

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\frac{R}{2}}}|D^{2}u|^{p}\,dx\leq c(1+||k||_{L^{r}(B_{R})}+||b||_{L^{p^{\prime}}(B_{R})})^{\gamma}\left(1+\int_{B_{R}}(1+|Du|)^{p}\,dx\right)^{\gamma}

(3.10)

hold for every ball
B_{R}\Subset\Omega
, for and exponent
\gamma=\gamma(p,q,n,r)
, and
c=c(\nu,L,n,p,q,r,R)
.

Proof.

Let
B_{R}\Subset\Omega
be a ball, fix radii
0<\frac{R}{2}<s<t<R
and a cut off function
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{t})
such that
\eta\equiv 1
on
B_{s}
and
|D\eta|\leq\frac{C}{t-s}
.
We choose
\varphi=\tau_{-h}(\eta^{2}\tau_{h}u)\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)
as test function in
(
3.5
)
to deduce that

\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathcal{A}(x,Du),\tau_{-h}(D(\eta^{2}\tau_{h}u))\rangle\,dx=\int_{\Omega}b\tau_{-h}(\eta^{2}\tau_{h}u)\,dx

i.e.

\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathcal{A}(x,Du),\tau_{-h}(\eta^{2}\tau_{h}Du)+2\tau_{-h}(\eta D\eta\tau_{h}u)\rangle\,dx=\int_{\Omega}b\tau_{-h}(\eta^{2}\tau_{h}u)\,dx.

From previous equality, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathcal{A}(x,Du),\tau_{-h}(\eta^{2}\tau_{h}Du)\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\int_{\Omega}\langle\mathcal{A}(x,Du),\tau_{-h}(\eta D\eta\tau_{h}u)\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega}b\tau_{-h}(\eta^{2}\tau_{h}u)\,dx.

which, by virtue of (d2) in Proposition
2.5
, implies

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\eta^{2}\langle\tau_{h}(\mathcal{A}(x,Du)),\tau_{h}Du\rangle\,dx

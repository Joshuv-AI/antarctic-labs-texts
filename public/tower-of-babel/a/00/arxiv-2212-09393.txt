Low-dimensional metal-organic magnets as a route towards the S = 2 S=2 Haldane phase
Jem Pitcairn, Andrea Iliceto, Laura Cañadillas-Delgado, Oscar Fabelo, Cheng Liu, Christian Balz, Andreas Weilhard, Stephen P. Argent, Andrew J. Morris, Matthew J. Cliffe

Abstract
Metal-organic magnets (MOMs), modular magnetic materials where metal atoms are connected by organic linkers, are promising candidates for next-generation quantum technologies. MOMs readily form low-dimensional structures, and so are ideal systems to realise physical examples of key quantum models, including the Haldane phase, where a topological excitation gap occurs in integer-spin antiferromagnetic (AFM) chains. Thus far the Haldane phase has only been identified for S = 1 S=1 , with S ≥ 2 S\geq 2 still unrealised because the larger spin imposes more stringent requirements on the magnetic interactions. Here, we report the structure and magnetic properties of CrCl 2 \text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} ( pym ) (pym=pyrimidine), a new quasi-1D S = 2 S=2 AFM MOM. We show, using X-ray and neutron diffraction, bulk property measurements, density-functional theory calculations and inelastic neutron spectroscopy (INS) that CrCl 2 \text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} ( pym ) consists of AFM CrCl 2 \text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} spin chains ( J 1 = − 1.13 ​ ( 4 ) J_{1}=-1.13(4)\; meV) which are weakly ferromagnetically coupled through bridging pym ( J 2 = 0.10 ​ ( 2 ) J_{2}=0.10(2)\; meV), with easy-axis anisotropy ( D = − 0.15 ​ ( 3 ) D=-0.15(3)\; meV). We find that although small compared to J 1 J_{1} , these additional interactions are sufficient to prevent observation of the Haldane phase in this material. Nevertheless, the proximity to the Haldane phase together with the modularity of MOMs suggests that layered Cr(II) MOMs are a promising family to search for the elusive S = 2 S=2 Haldane phase.

Metal-organic magnets (MOM) are assembled from metal nodes bridged by
organic molecular linkers into extended
networks.
(
Thorarinsdottir and Harris 2020
)
This gives
them a number of advantages over conventional inorganic magnets: there
is a much wider diversity of organic than atomic
ligands;
(
Zhao et al. 2011
)
the modularity of
their construction allows for tuning of interactions while retaining the
topology
(
Cortijo et al. 2013
)
and their longer
lengths facilitate magnetic
low-dimensionality,
(
Canepa et al. 2013
;
Harcombe et al. 2016
)

and thus enhanced quantum
fluctuations.
(
Vasiliev et al. 2018
)
Perhaps most
excitingly, it has recently been demonstrated that redox-active radical
ligands can introduce into MOFs both high electronic conductivity (0.45
Scm
-1
)
(
Ziebel et al. 2018
)
and strong
magnetic
interactions,
(
Pedersen et al. 2018
;
Perlepe et al. 2020
)

despite the long-distances between metal centres. This suggests that
MOMs could form the basis for practical new quantum
technology.
(
Murphy et al. 2021
;
Park et al. 2021
;
Yamada et al. 2017
;
Huang et al. 2018
;
Coronado 2020
)

MOM spin chains are now well established as host materials for
distinctively quantum behaviour, from spin fractionalisation in

\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{5}}}\text{COO}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\,{\cdot}\,\text{3}\,\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
(
Dender et al. 1997
)
to
the quantum sine-Gordon physics of
Cu(pym)(NO
3
)(H
2
O)
2
(
Zvyagin et al. 2004
)

and

\text{[}\text{Cu}\text{(}\text{pym}\text{)}\text{(}\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{]}\text{SiF}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{6}}}\,{\cdot}\,\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{O}
.
(
Liu et al. 2019
)

One of the most striking quantum discoveries in MOMs was the measurement
of the topological Haldane gap in the antiferromagnetic
S=1
spin
chain MOM
\text{Ni}\text{(}\text{C}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{8}}}\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{)}\,{\cdot}\,\text{2}\,\text{NO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{ClO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{4}}}\text{)}

(
Renard et al. 1987
;
Haldane 1983
;
Haldane 1983
)

and subsequent efforts have uncovered a number of other high-quality
model
systems.
(
Mutka et al. 1989
;
Takeuchi et al. 1992
;
Landee et al. 1997
;
Williams et al. 2020
)

The Haldane phase is yet to be experimentally realised for spins

S>1
.

The difficulty of reaching the Haldane phase for
S\geq 2
is largely
because the size of the Haldane gap relative to the intrachain exchange,

\Delta/J_{1}
, decreases significantly from
\Delta/J_{1}=0.41
for

S=1
to
\Delta/J_{1}=0.087
for
S=2
, making the gap both more
sensitive to the presence of single-ion anisotropy and non-Heisenberg
exchange interactions, and harder to detect when
present.
(
Schollwöck and Jolicoeur 1995
)
These challenges have
meant that although AFM
S=2
spin chains which could be candidates to
host the Haldane phase have been identified, the
S=2
gap has not yet
been
observed.
(
Stone et al. 2013
;
Granroth et al. 2002
;
Léone et al. 2004
;
Stock et al. 2009
;
Birk et al. 2011
)

The combination of modularity and low-dimensionality of MOMs means they
are an ideal platform to search for the
S=2
Haldane phases. However,
the most synthetically accessible
S=2
transition metal ion is

\text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2+}}
, which typically possesses large single ion anisotropy due
to its partially quenched
{}^{5}T_{2\mathrm{g}}
ground state, and other

S=2
ions,
\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
and
\text{Cr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2+}}
, are usually sensitive to
reduction or oxidation in ambient conditions. As a result, the chemistry
of MOMs which could host
S=2
Haldane phases is comparatively
underexplored, and their quantum states thus unrealised.

Here we report
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
, a new 2D layered magnetic coordination
polymer consisting of
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
chains bridged by pym ligands with a
structure analogous to that of the other transition metal monopyrimidine
chlorides (
\text{MCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
, M = Mn, Fe, Co, Ni,
Cu),
(
Ferraro et al. 1969
)
the Mn, Co and Cu analogues of
which are reported to possess antiferromagnetic coupling without order
down to
1.8\;
K.
(
Zusai et al. 2000
)
We
first describe its synthesis and structural characterisation using X-ray
diffraction, where the presence of a pronounced Jahn-Teller (JT)
distortion confirms the presence of
\text{Cr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2+}}
. We then go on to show
using comprehensive magnetic characterisation, including bulk
magnetisation, heat capacity measurements, powder neutron diffraction
(PND) and powder inelastic neutron scattering (INS) measurements of
fully protonated samples, that
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
orders into a Néel ground
state at
T_{\mathrm{N}}=20.0(3)
K, with AFM ordering along the

\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
chain, FM coupling of the chains through pym and interlayer
FM correlations. Through detailed analysis of the neutron scattering
data, in combination with density-functional theory (DFT) calculations,
we quantitatively determine the size of the key magnetic interactions,
which suggest that
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
is a well separated
S=2
AFM with
near isotropic single ion properties. We therefore suggest that through
careful ligand choice this family of MOMs offers a potential route to
realise the Haldane phase for
S=2
.

the Fermi energy and is shown by the dashed line. The projected density
of states has been decomposed by
element.

To understand the origin of the observed low-dimensional interactions we
carried out collinear spin-polarized plane-wave density-functional
theory (DFT) calculations, by exploring the electronic structure of the
DFT ground-state spin configuration and calculating the exchange
energies using the broken symmetry
approach.
(
Ciofini and Daul 2003
)
We first optimised
the geometry of the experimental structure using the PBE functional
along with a many-body semi-empirical dispersion correction
(MBD*)
(
Tkatchenko et al. 2012
)
to describe the weak
van der Waals forces between the
layers.
(
Formalik et al. 2018
)
We found that this
structure was both too dense, with a unit-cell volume of 297.68
Å
3
, 4.8% smaller than experimental value of 312.75 Å
3
,
and lacked the JT distortion characteristic of Cr(II). We therefore
included an effective Coulomb on-site energy,
U_{\mathrm{eff}}=U-J

where
U
is the on-site repulsion and
J
the exchange energy, to
account for the overly delocalised Cr d-states. A range of values for

U_{\mathrm{eff}}
have been previously explored for Cr, from

U_{\mathrm{eff}}=2.1\;
eV to

U_{\mathrm{eff}}=3.5\;
eV.
(
Rhee and Singh 2010
;
Wang et al. 2006
)

We found that
U_{\mathrm{eff}}=3\;
eV was able to accurately capture
the physics of this system, and produced a structure with both a JT
distortion and, as a bonus, a volume within +0.2% of experiment.

Exchange interactions were calculated using a
2\times 2\times 1

supercell of the optimised structure (
i.e.
containing eight
distinct Cr atoms) decorated with eight distinct magnetic orderings.
Single point energy calculations were then carried out on each
configurations, and these DFT+
U
total energies were then fitted to
the Hamiltonian described in Eqn.
1
with
D=0
,

i.e.
the Heisenberg limit. We carried out these calculations
using a series of values of
U_{\mathrm{eff}}
to ensure consistency
of behaviour (Fig. S10). For our optimised value of

U_{\mathrm{eff}}=3\;
eV, we obtained a self-consistent set of
superexchange interactions of
J_{1}=-2.53(5)\;
meV,

J_{2}=0.30(5)\;
meV,
J_{3}=-0.09(5)\;
meV. To test the robustness of
our DFT+U calculations, we performed hybrid calculations using fraction
of Fock exchange as implemented in the HSE
functional
(
Heyd et al. 2003
;
Janesko et al. 2009
;
Zhu et al. 2020
)

while maintaining a
U_{\mathrm{eff}}=3\;
eV. HSE calculations are
computationally expensive due to the calculation of Fock exchange and
require the use of norm-conserving pseudopotentials within CASTEP, which
limited the sampling of the Brillouin zone and our ability to explore
geometry optimisations. Nevertheless, we found that using the HSE
functional comparable exchange interactions
J_{1}=-2.39(1)\;
meV,

J_{2}=0.46(1)\;
meV,
J_{3}=-0.15(1)\;
meV. These energies are
comparable in magnitude to those found experimentally for

\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
, but are notably larger, likely due to the unphysically
large degree of delocalisation.

Our calculations not only allow us to predict the interaction energies,
but also to explore the electronic structure of this material (Fig.

6
). The predicted thermal band gap is
approximately
1.2
eV, and the projection of the DOS onto local
orbitals shows that the top of the valence band is broadly Cr and Cl
based, whilst the organic linker pym states are the bottom of the
conduction band. This can also be observed in the frontier orbitals,
where the HOMO resembles the Cr
d_{z^{2}}
orbital antibonding with Cl

p
orbitals and the LUMO is an antibonding
\pi
molecular orbital
with a single additional node, suggesting that the lowest lying
excitations will be of MLCT character. The spin density is predominantly
around the Cr, however, there is significant density on both Cl and pym
ligands (Fig.
7
). Notably, the spin density on pym
appears to be primarily of
\pi
character, and alternates in sign
round the ring (Fig.
7
b).

-3
) highlighting

U_{\mathrm{eff}}=3\;
eV) and c2x
calculations.

Rutter 2018
)

3
Discussion

Metal N-heterocycle dihalides are a diverse family of MOMs and our study
of
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
provides one of the most in-depth investigations of
the magnetic properties of these materials. There are two common
compositions:
\text{MX}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{L}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{MX}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{L}
. The monoligand analogues usually
contain linear
\text{MX}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
chains, and therefore tend to show primarily 1D
magnetic behaviour,
e.g.

\text{NiCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{pyrazine}\text{)}
consists of
ferromagnetic
\text{NiCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
chains antiferromagnetically coupled with

T_{\mathrm{N}}=10.2\;
K,
(
Cortijo et al. 2013
)

\text{CuCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{pyrazine}\text{)}
is also a very good example of a 1D magnet with no
order reported down to
1.8\;
K, but the strongest interaction in fact
occurs through Cu—pyrazine—Cu bridges (
J=-28\;
K), due to the JT
distortion suppressing exchange in the
\text{CuCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

chain.
(
Butcher et al. 2008
)

Preliminary studies of the magnetism of pyrimidine analogues,

\text{MCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
M = Mn, Co, Cu, also detect no magnetic order down to 1.8
K although there are weak AFM interactions
present.
(
Zusai et al. 2000
)
The strong
interactions, particularly occurring through the
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
chain, and
magnetic order found in
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
are therefore in striking
contrast. Additionally, the ferromagnetic exchange we observe occuring
through the pym ligand is relatively uncommon for molecular ligands, for
example, antiferromagnetic interactions are the norm for
pyrazine-bridged
MOMs.
(
Manson et al. 2011
;
Dos Santos et al. 2016
;
Kubus et al. 2018
;
Perlepe et al. 2018
;
Näther et al. 2013
;
Wriedt et al. 2009
;
Näther and Greve 2003
)

This ferromagnetic exchange has been previously observed in pym-bridged
MOMs,
e.g.

\text{M}\text{(}\text{NCS}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{pym}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
M=
Ni and
Co),
(
Lloret et al. 1999
;
Lloret et al. 1998
;
Wriedt et al. 2009
)

and has been rationalised by a three-atom
\pi
-pathway. Our DFT
calculations give further credence to the importance of this pathway.

The bispyrimidine metal chlorides,
\text{MCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{pym}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
M = Fe, Co, Ni and

\text{MBr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{pym}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
M = Co, unlike most materials in this family adopt 3D
chiral diamondoid
structures.
(
Feyerherm et al. 2004
;
Hashizume et al. 1999
;
Kreitlow et al. 2005
)

\text{MCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
all magnetically order with canted AFM structures,

T_{\mathrm{N}}
= 7.4 K, 4.7 K and 16.3 K for M = Fe, Co and Ni
respectively, likely arising from the interplay between the
superexchange interactions and the significant single-ion anisotropy,
the principal axes of which are
non-collinear.
(
Feyerherm et al. 2004
)
Bulk
susceptibility studies have shown enhancement of
T_{\mathrm{N}}
at
moderate pressure (
\Delta T_{\mathrm{N}}/T_{\mathrm{N}}=15
% at 0.7
GPa),
(
Kreitlow et al. 2005
)
which suggests
that high pressure investigations of
Cr
-based MOMs may also uncover
pressure-switchable magnetic
functionality.
(
Wehinger et al. 2018
)

The presence of a JT distortion is strong evidence of
\text{Cr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2+}}
,
which stands in contrast to the related
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{pyrazine}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, in which

\text{Cr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2+}}
spontaneously reduces the ligated pyrazine to a radical
anion, and thereby dramatically enhancing its conductivity and magnetic
superexchange.
(
Pedersen et al. 2018
)
The
sensitivity of this metal-ligand redox to the coordination sphere is
shown by
\text{Cr}\text{(}\text{OSO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{CH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{pyrazine}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, in which Cr remains as

\text{Cr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2+}}
with a JT distortion.
(
Perlepe et al. 2018
)

Studies of molecular complexes have shown this non-innocent behaviour is
favoured by a strong ligand-field environment and a low energy ligand
LUMO,
(
Scarborough et al. 2012
)
and is consistent with
the observed innocence of
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
, which has both weaker ligand
field than
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{(}\text{pyz}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and a higher energy ligand LUMO (pyrazine,

E_{\mathrm{red}}=+1.10\;
V and pym
E_{\mathrm{red}}=+0.84\;
V
vs.
\text{Li}\text{/}\text{Li}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
).
(
Assary et al. 2014
)

Our data clearly show that
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
has a conventional Néel AFM
ground state,
T_{\mathrm{N}}=20.0(3)
K, but also that there is
significant magnetic low-dimensionality above
T_{\mathrm{N}}
.

The frustration parameter,

f=\frac{|\theta_{\mathrm{CW}}|}{T_{\mathrm{N}}}=2.7
, derived from
bulk property measurements hints at suppression of magnetic order. As
the magnetic lattice does not show an obvious mechanism for geometric
frustration, this is likely due to a combination of single-ion
anisotropy and low-dimensionality arising from the large differences in
strength of superexchange in different crystallographic directions.

Additionally, the presence of magnetic diffuse scattering at 30 K not
present at 1.5 K, indicates the presence of short-range magnetic
correlations retained above
T_{\mathrm{N}}
. Finally, our analysis of
the INS spectra show that the AFM superexchange through the Cr–Cl–Cr
bridge is an order of magnitude larger than all other superexchange
interactions,
|\frac{J_{1}}{J_{2}}|=11(2)
.

The importance of low-dimensionality can also been seen in the reduction
in the apparent size of the
\text{Cr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2+}}
ordered moment determined via
neutron diffraction. The low-dimensionality of the structure can reduce
the refined moment through disorder, both static short-chain defects and
stacking
faults
(
Winkelmann et al. 1995
;
Hirakawa et al. 1982
)

and dynamic zero-point
fluctuations
(
Cliffe et al. 2018
)
.
Additionally, as is common in many metal organic
magnets,
(
Kmety et al. 2000
)
there is
appreciable delocalisation of the spin-density onto the ligands, which
Mulliken analysis of the DFT-derived electron density suggests is
approximately 10%. These factors in combination explain the substantial
reduction in the ordered moment (approximately one-third) from that
expected moment size, though it is challenging to evaluate their
relative contributions.

Despite this low-dimensionality, our data indicate that like other

S=2
candidate AFM chains,
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
does not show clear
Haldane physics. The presence of long range order at

T_{\mathrm{N}}/J_{1}=1.5
hinders observations at low temperatures and
the non-negligible single ion anisotropy (
D=-0.15(3)
meV,

D/J_{1}=0.13(2)
) is sufficient to suppress the Haldane phase, for
which the critical value is predicted to be

D/J_{1}=0.04
.
(
Schollwöck and Jolicoeur 1995
)

\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
is
therefore comparable to the other identified candidate
S=2
spin
chains in both of these
parameters,
(
Léone et al. 2004
;
Stock et al. 2009
;
Birk et al. 2011
)

including
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
,
(
Stone et al. 2013
)

\text{MnCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{(}\text{bipy}\text{)}

(
Granroth et al. 1996
;
Granroth et al. 2002
)

and
\text{CsCrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
,
(
Itoh et al. 2002
)
but none have
shown clear evidence of a gapped inelastic neutron spectrum in the
disordered phase.

The compound
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
is most similar to, both structurally and
magnetically, is

\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
,
(
Stone et al. 2013
)
which also has
quasi-1D antiferromagnetic
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
chains formed from edge-sharing
octahedra (
J_{1}=-1.13(13)
meV,
D^{\prime}=-0.11(2)
meV). However, closer
examination reveals significant structural differences that make these
magnetic similarities quite surprising. In
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
the JT
distortion means every superexchange pathway within the
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
spin
chain passes through a significantly lengthened bond, whereas in

\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
the equivalent JT distortion lies out of the spin-chain plane
and so all
Cr
–
Cl
bonds in the chain are short. Superexchange
through a JT-lengthened pathway is ordinarily weak, as is indeed found
for the direction perpendicular to the
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
spin chain in
inorganic
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
, with an order of magntiude weaker exchange

J_{2}=-0.12(7)
meV.

A second distinction between these two compounds is the potential for
tuning the interactions through substitution of the ligands. Replacing
pyrimidine by a larger bridging ligand may reduce inter-chain exchange,
suppressing long-range order and allowing access to the paramagnetic

S=2
quasi-1D AFM at lower temperatures. For example, in
\text{NiCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{L}

substituting pyrazine for 1,2-bis(4-pyridyl)ethane reduces

T_{\mathrm{N}}
from 10.2 K to 5.6
K.
(
Cortijo et al. 2013
)
Equally, optimisation
of the octahedral coordination environment can minimise
D
, for
example in a family of closely related
\text{Ni}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2+}}
compounds, matching
of the ligand field strengths reduces the size of the easy-plane
anisotropy by a factor of
4.
(
Manson et al. 2020
)
Our measurements of the
INS data already suggest that the interlayer interactions are not
significant, but delamination of these van der Waals sheets, as
demonstrated for other magnetic metal-organic
nanosheets,
(
López-Cabrelles et al. 2018
)

may provide an alternative route to better magnetic isolation. These
results suggest therefore that bridging
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
spin chains with
organic ligands may provide promising future candidates for
S=2

Haldane chains.

4
Conclusion

We have reported the crystal structure, bulk magnetic properties,
magnetic ground state and magnetic excitations of a new coordination
polymer,
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
. We have shown that the oxidation state of
chromium in this compound is
\text{Cr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{2+}}
, remaining
S=2
, unlike
related
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
derived MOMs which undergo redox to form triplet

\text{Cr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{3+}}
-radical ligand pairs.

(
Pedersen et al. 2018
;
Scarborough et al. 2012
)

\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
is found to be a
S=2
quasi-one-dimensional
antiferromagnet, with an order of magnitude separation in energy scales
of superexchange,
|\frac{J_{1}}{J_{2}}|=11(2)
. However we did not find
clear evidence of the Haldane gap in the disordered phase, suggesting
the small
J_{2}
and
D
are sufficient in this compound to either
suppress the
S=2
Haldane phase or mask it through the stabilisation
of long range order. The proximity of
\text{CrCl}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(
pym
)
to the Haldane
region of the phase diagram, and the modularity inherent to MOMs suggest
that optimising the magnetic properties of these systems, including both
superexchange
(
Cortijo et al. 2013
)
and
single-ion-anisotropy,
(
Manson et al. 2020
)
is
a new and promising route to the
S=2
Haldane phase.

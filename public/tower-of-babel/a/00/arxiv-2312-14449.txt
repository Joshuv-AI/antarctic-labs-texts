On the Borel summability of formal solutions of certain higher-order linear ordinary differential equations
Gergő Nemes

Abstract
. We consider a class of n th n^{\text{th}} -order linear ordinary differential equations with a large parameter u u . Analytic solutions of these equations can be described by (divergent) formal series in descending powers of u u . We demonstrate that, given mild conditions on the potential functions of the equation, the formal solutions are Borel summable with respect to the parameter u u in large, unbounded domains of the independent variable. We establish that the formal series expansions serve as asymptotic expansions, uniform with respect to the independent variable, for the Borel re-summed exact solutions. Additionally, we show that the exact solutions can be expressed using factorial series in the parameter, and these expansions converge in half-planes, uniformly with respect to the independent variable. To illustrate our theory, we apply it to an n th n^{\text{th}} -order Airy-type equation.

Introduction and main results

In this paper, we study
n^{\text{th}}
-order (
n\geq 2
) linear ordinary differential equations of the form

(1.1)

\bigg(-\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}z^{n}}+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-2}u^{n-k}f_{k}(u,z)\frac{\mathrm{d}^{k}}{\mathrm{d}z^{k}}\bigg)w(u,z)=0,

where
u
is a real or complex parameter,
z
lies in some domain
\mathbf{D}
, bounded or otherwise, of a Riemann surface, and the potential functions
f_{k}(u,z)
are analytic functions of
z
possessing asymptotic expansions of the form

(1.2)

f_{0}(u,z)\sim\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{f_{0,m}(z)}{u^{m}},\quad f_{k}(u,z)\sim\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{f_{k,m}(z)}{u^{m}}\qquad(1\leq k\leq n-2)

as
u\to\infty
uniformly with respect to
z\in\mathbf{D}
. It is assumed that the coefficient functions
f_{k,m}(z)
are analytic functions of
z
in the domain
\mathbf{D}
and are independent of
u
. Furthermore, we suppose that
f_{0,0}(z)
does not vanish in
\mathbf{D}
. Note that the term in the
(n-1)^{\text{th}}
derivative is not present in the equation (
1.1
). This term can always be removed by an appropriate change of dependent variable.

We are interested in asymptotic expansions of analytic solutions of (
1.1
) when the parameter
u
becomes large. Equation (
1.1
) belongs to a commonly studied class of linear ordinary differential equations with a large parameter (see, e.g.,
[
19
, Ch. 5, Sec. 1.4]
). In general, not all of the terms corresponding to
m=0
in the expansions of the functions
f_{k}(u,z)
(for
k\neq 0
) need to be zero. However, in those instances, the roots of the characteristic equation
\xi^{n}=f_{n-2,0}(z)\xi^{n-2}+\ldots+f_{0,0}(z)
associated with (
1.1
) may not possess a closed form, thus introducing difficulties in the analysis. In our case, the characteristic equation takes the simple form

(1.3)

\xi^{n}=f_{0,0}(z).

After choosing a specific branch of
f_{0,0}^{1/n}(z)
, we obtain
n
unique solutions
\xi_{j}=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j/n}f_{0,0}^{1/n}(z)
, where
0\leq j\leq n-1
. This suggests the introduction of the transformed variables
\xi
and
W(u,\xi)
given by

(1.4)

\xi=\int_{z_{0}}^{z}f_{0,0}^{1/n}(t)\mathrm{d}t,\quad W(u,\xi)=f_{0,0}^{(1-1/n)/2}(z)w(u,z).

The integration path, with the potential exception of its endpoint
z_{0}
, must lie entirely within
\mathbf{D}
. If
z_{0}
happens to be a boundary point of
\mathbf{D}
, it is assumed that the integral converges as
t
approaches
z_{0}
along the integration contour. The inclusion of the factor
f_{0,0}^{(1/n-1)/2}(z)
in the definition of
W(u,\xi)
results in the
(n-1)^{\text{th}}
derivative remaining absent in the transformed equation, thereby providing a simple asymptotic ansatz for solutions. The transformation (
1.4
) maps
\mathbf{D}
on a domain
\mathbf{G}
, say. In terms of the new variables, equation
(
1.1
) becomes

(1.5)

\bigg(-\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}\xi^{n}}+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-2}u^{n-k}\psi_{k}(u,\xi)\frac{\mathrm{d}^{k}}{\mathrm{d}\xi^{k}}\bigg)W(u,\xi)=0,

where the functions
\psi_{k}(u,\xi)
are analytic with respect to
\xi
in
\mathbf{G}
, and admit asymptotic expansions of the form

(1.6)

\psi_{k}(u,\xi)\sim\delta_{0,k}+\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}{\frac{{\psi_{k,m}(\xi)}}{{u^{m}}}}\qquad(0\leq k\leq n-2),

as
u\to\infty
uniformly with respect to
\xi\in\mathbf{G}
. Here
\delta_{0,k}
represents the Kronecker delta. The functions
\psi_{k}(u,\xi)
and
\psi_{k,m}(\xi)
may be expressed in terms of the functions
f_{k}(u,z)
and
f_{k,m}(z)
, respectively (see Appendix
A
).

It can be verified through direct substitution that equation (
1.5
) has, for each
0\leq j\leq n-1
, formal solutions of the form

(1.7)

\widehat{W}_{j}(u,\xi)=\exp\Big(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j/n}u\xi+X_{j}(\xi)\Big)\bigg(1+\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathsf{A}_{j,m}(\xi)}{u^{m}}\bigg),

with

(1.8)

X_{j}(\xi)=\frac{1}{n}\int^{\xi}\bigg(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-2}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j(k+1)/n}\psi_{k,1}(t)\bigg)\mathrm{d}t.

The coefficients
\mathsf{A}_{j,m}(\xi)
, which are analytic functions of
\xi
in
\mathbf{G}
, can be determined
recursively via

(1.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathsf{A}_{j,m}(\xi)=-\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{p=2}^{\min(n,m+1)}{\binom{n}{p}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j(n-p+1)/n}\sum\limits_{r=0}^{p}{\binom{p}{r}\int^{\xi}{\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p-r}\frac{{\mathrm{d}^{r}\mathsf{A}_{j,m-p+1}(t)}}{{\mathrm{d}t^{r}}}\mathrm{d}t}}}\\
&+\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-2}{\sum\limits_{p=1}^{\min(m,k)}{\binom{k}{p}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j(k-p+1)/n}\sum\limits_{r=0}^{p}{\binom{p}{r}\int^{\xi}{\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p-r}\psi_{k,1}(t)\frac{{\mathrm{d}^{r}\mathsf{A}_{j,m-p}(t)}}{{\mathrm{d}t^{r}}}\mathrm{d}t}}}}\\
&+\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-2}{\sum\limits_{q=0}^{m-1}{\sum\limits_{p=0}^{\min(k,q)}{\binom{k}{p}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j(k-p+1)/n}\sum\limits_{r=0}^{p}{\binom{p}{r}\int^{\xi}{\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p-r}\psi_{k,m-q+1}(t)\frac{{\mathrm{d}^{r}\mathsf{A}_{j,q-p}(t)}}{{\mathrm{d}t^{r}}}\mathrm{d}t}}}}},\end{split}

with the convention
\mathsf{A}_{j,0}(\xi)=1
. The constants of integration in (
1.8
) and (
1.9
) are arbitrary. In (
1.9
),
\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p}=\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p}(X^{\prime}_{j}(\xi),X^{\prime\prime}_{j}(\xi),\ldots,X_{j}^{(p)}(\xi))=\exp(-X_{j}(\xi))\partial_{\xi}^{p}\exp(X_{j}(\xi))
is a
p^{\text{th}}
complete exponential Bell polynomial (refer to Appendix
C
). For a detailed derivation, see Appendix
B
.

When
n=2
, equation (
1.1
) simplifies to the Schrödinger-type equation

(1.10)

\bigg(-\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}z^{2}}+u^{2}f_{0}(u,z)\bigg)w(u,z)=0.

In this particular case, the formal solutions (
1.7
) are commonly known as WKB solutions, named after the physicists Wentzel
[
42
]
, Kramers
[
26
]
, and Brillouin
[
8
]
, who independently discovered them within the context of quantum mechanics in 1926. However, for complete accuracy, it should be noted that the WKB solutions were discussed earlier by Carlini
[
9
]
in 1817, by Liouville
[
27
]
and Green
[
22
]
in 1837, by Strutt (Lord Rayleigh)
[
37
]
in 1912, and once again by Jeffreys
[
24
]
in 1924. For detailed historical discussion and critical overview, the interested reader is directed to the books by Dingle
[
15
, Ch. XIII]
and Wasow
[
41
, Ch. I]
.

Typically, the series in (
1.7
) do not converge, and the most that can be established is that in certain subregions of
\mathbf{G}
they provide uniform asymptotic expansions of solutions of the differential equation (
1.5
). For the Schrödinger-type equation (
1.10
), Thorne
[
39
]
and Olver
[
33
, Ch. 10, §9]
demonstrated the asymptotic nature of the expansions (
1.7
) by constructing explicit error bounds. Fedoryuk
[
19
, Ch. 5]
employed a similar technique to analyse the higher-order case.

In this paper we take a different approach and study the formal solutions
\widehat{W}_{j}(u,\xi)
from the perspective of Borel summability. Our primary objective is to demonstrate that the expansions (
1.7
) are Borel summable within specific subdomains
\Gamma_{j}
of
\mathbf{G}
, under the condition that the functions
\psi_{k}(u,\xi)
meet certain moderate criteria. That is, we shall construct exact, analytic solutions
W_{j}(u,\xi)
of the differential equation (
1.5
) in terms of Laplace transforms (with respect to the parameter
u
) of certain associated functions, known as the Borel transforms of the formal solutions. The formal expansions presented in (
1.7
) will then arise as asymptotic series of these exact solutions. Furthermore, it will be demonstrated that the exact solutions
W_{j}(u,\xi)
can be represented through factorial series in the parameter
u
, and that these expansions exhibit convergence in half-planes with respect to
u
, uniformly in the independent variable
\xi
.

In recent decades, there has been a growing interest in the study of Borel summability of asymptotic expansions, and this field is closely connected to exponential asymptotics (see, for instance,
[
32
]
). Indeed, extensive progress has been made in the summability of formal WKB solutions of the second-order equation (
1.10
), with comparatively fewer general results available in the case of higher-order equations. The crucial relationship between Borel summability and the analysis of WKB solutions was initially recognised by Bender and Wu
[
4
]
. Dunster et al.
[
16
]
studied differential equations of the type (
1.10
) in the case that
f_{0}(u,z)=f_{0,0}(z)+f_{0,2}(z)u^{-2}
. They established the Borel summability of WKB solutions away from Stokes curves under appropriate conditions on
f_{0}(u,z)
, and furthermore, they derived convergent factorial series expansions for these solutions. Further contributions in this direction can be found in the works of Giller and Milczarski
[
21
]
as well as Bodine and Schäfke
[
7
]
. Borel summability results for the WKB solutions of equations of the type (
1.10
), featuring more general potentials
f_{0}(u,z)
, have been demonstrated in their most recent works by Nikolaev
[
30
]
and the author of the present work
[
29
]
.

Simultaneously, efforts were made to develop connection formulae for WKB solutions across Stokes curves. In the seminal work by Voros
[
40
]
, he showed how to analyse Borel re-summed WKB solutions and demonstrated the relationship between the singular points of the Borel transforms and the global connection formulae. Aoki et al.
[
1
]
proposed an approach to perform this singularity analysis by transforming the original equation into a canonical form near the transition point, employing specific transformation series, and investigating the properties of the resulting canonical equation, which are better understood. The idea of a transformation series, in fact, has much earlier roots and can be traced back to a paper by Cherry
[
11
]
. A rigorous treatment of these transformation series for specific types of transition points has been provided in the works of Kamimoto and Koike
[
25
]
, as well as by Sasaki
[
35
]
. An alternative method for obtaining connection formulae, using convergent factorial series, was proposed by Dunster
[
17
,
18
]
. Further developments, drawing upon Écalle’s theory of resurgent functions, were undertaken by Delabaere et al.
[
13
,
14
]
.

As mentioned above, the study of Borel summability of formal solutions of higher-order equations has remained largely unexplored. Aoki et al.
[
3
]
introduced the exact steepest descent method to analyse the Borel summability of formal solutions to equation (
1.1
) in the particular case where the potential functions
f_{k}(u,z)
are polynomials in
z
of degree at most two and independent of
u
. Possible extension to higher-order polynomial potentials relies on the Borel summability of formal solutions to certain higher-order equations.

In 1982, Berk et al.
[
6
]
studied the connection formulae across Stokes curves for formal solutions of a third-order equation given by

\bigg(-\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}z^{3}}-u^{2}3\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}+u^{3}\mathrm{i}z\bigg)w(u,z)=0.

(Note that this equation is not within the scope of the class studied in this paper since the potential
f_{1}(u,z)=3
does not exhibit an asymptotic expansion of the form specified in (
1.2
).) They discovered that the Stokes curves intersected each other at non-critical points, requiring the introduction of new Stokes curves in the monodromy of solutions. These additional Stokes curves originated from what appeared to be regular points rather than transition points, later referred to as virtual turning points by Aoki et al.
[
2
]
. Notably, it was observed that the newly introduced Stokes curves only became “active” once they passed through the crossing points of the ordinary Stokes curves. No Stokes phenomenon occurred along the segments connecting the virtual turning point and the crossing points. The explanation of this remarkable phenomenon, now known as the higher-order Stokes phenomenon, was subsequently provided by Howls et al.
[
23
,
10
]
, employing hyperasymptotic techniques. In a higher-order Stokes phenomenon, a Stokes multiplier itself can change its value, explaining the apparent sudden birth of new Stokes curves at regular points (virtual turning points). This phenomenon was found to be present also in the case of integrals with saddles, inhomogeneous equations and partial differential equations. Nevertheless, these new Stokes curves are absent in our analysis and hence do not affect the results presented in this paper. This absence could likely be attributed to either the simplicity of the characteristic equation (
1.3
) associated with (
1.1
) or the restrictions on the domains
\Gamma_{j}
where Borel summability is established.

Before presenting our results, we will introduce the necessary assumptions, notation, and definitions. For any
n\geq 2
, we will denote by
\mathfrak{a}_{n}
the unique positive real solution of the equation

(1+\mathfrak{a}_{n})^{n}=1+2n\mathfrak{a}_{n}.

The sequence
\mathfrak{a}_{n}
will naturally arise when describing the region where the Borel transforms are analytic, particularly when estimating the coefficients
\mathsf{A}_{j,m}(\xi)
. For basic properties of this sequence, the reader is referred to Appendix
D
. We define, for any
r>0
,

B(r)=\left\{t:\left|t\right|<r\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad U(r)=\left\{t:\operatorname{Re}(t)>0,\,|\operatorname{Im}(t)|<r\right\}\cup B(r).

Thus,
U(r)
comprises all points whose distance from the positive real axis is strictly less than
r
. Subsequently, we will assume that the asymptotic expansions (
1.6
) of the functions
\psi_{k}(u,\xi)
are Borel summable, and that their respective Borel transforms satisfy certain growth conditions. The precise conditions are as follows.

Condition 1.1
.

There is a non-empty domain
\Delta\subseteq\mathbf{G}
and a set of functions
\Psi_{k}(t,\xi)
, where
0\leq k\leq n-2
, that satisfy the following three conditions.

(i)

The functions
\Psi_{k}(t,\xi)
are analytic on
U(\mathfrak{a}_{n}d^{\prime})\times\Delta
, where
d^{\prime}
is a positive constant independent of
u
,
\xi
and
k
.

(ii)

There exist constants
c>0
,
\rho>0
, and
\sigma^{\prime}\geq 0
, which are independent of
u
,
\xi
and
k
, such that for all
(t,\xi)\in U(\mathfrak{a}_{n}d^{\prime})\times\Delta
, the following inequalities hold:

|\Psi_{k}(t,\xi)|\leq c\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\sigma^{\prime}\operatorname{Re}(t)}}{1+\left|\xi\right|^{1+\rho}}\qquad(0\leq k\leq n-2).

(iii)

It holds that

(1.11)

\psi_{k}(u,\xi)=\delta_{0,k}+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-ut}\Psi_{k}(t,\xi)\mathrm{d}t\qquad(0\leq k\leq n-2),

when
\operatorname{Re}(u)>\sigma^{\prime}
and
\xi\in\Delta
.

We shall introduce some additional definitions as follows. Write

\theta_{j,\ell}=-\arg(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\ell/n}-\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j/n}),\qquad 0\leq\ell\leq n-1,\quad\ell\neq j.

Although these angles are initially defined modulo
2\pi
, we can always choose them in such a way that the following condition holds for each
0\leq j\leq n-1
:

(1.12)

\max_{\ell\neq j}\theta_{j,\ell}-\min_{\ell\neq j}\theta_{j,\ell}=\frac{n-2}{n}\pi.

Henceforth, we will assume that this condition is met. It is worth noting that

\theta_{j,\ell}=\frac{{(2-\operatorname{sgn}(j-\ell))n-2(j+\ell)}}{{2n}}\pi\bmod 2\pi.

Let
d>0
. We denote, for each
0\leq j\leq n-1
, by
\Gamma_{j}(d)
any (non-empty) subdomain of
\Delta
that satisfies the following
two requirements:

(i)

The distance between each point of
\Gamma_{j}(d)
and each boundary point of
\Delta
has lower bound
d
(which is to be chosen independently of
u
).

(ii)

If
\xi\in\Gamma_{j}(d)
, then for each
0\leq\ell\leq n-1
,
\ell\neq j
, the infinite path
\mathscr{P}_{j,\ell}(\xi)
parametrised by

[0,+\infty)\ni t\mapsto\xi+t\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j,\ell}}

lies wholly within
\Gamma_{j}(d)
.

Note that condition (ii) is equivalent to the requirement that if
\xi\in\Gamma_{j}(d)
, then
\Gamma_{j}(d)
contains the closed, infinite sector with vertex
\xi
, opening
\frac{n-2}{n}\pi
, and radii parametrised by

[0,+\infty)\ni t\mapsto\xi+t\exp\Big(\mathrm{i}\min_{\ell\neq j}\theta_{j,\ell}\Big)\quad\text{and}\quad[0,+\infty)\ni t\mapsto\xi+t\exp\Big(\mathrm{i}\max_{\ell\neq j}\theta_{j,\ell}\Big)

(if
n=2
, the sector degenerates into a half line); cf. Figure
1(a)
. Furthermore, condition (ii) implies that
\Delta
(and hence

) must be unbounded.

(1.13)

\theta_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\min_{\ell\neq j}\theta_{j,\ell}+\max_{\ell\neq j}\theta_{j,\ell}\Big).

X_{j}(\xi)=-\frac{1}{n}\int_{\mathscr{P}_{j}(\xi)}\bigg(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-2}\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j(k+1)/n}\psi_{k,1}(t)\bigg)\mathrm{d}t,

, and the functions

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\left[\frac{{\mathrm{d}^{s}\eta_{j}(u,\zeta)}}{{\mathrm{d}\zeta^{s}}}\right]_{\zeta=\xi+t\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}}}=0\qquad(0\leq s\leq n-2).

\eta_{j}(u,\xi)\sim\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathsf{A}_{j,m}(\xi)}{u^{m}},

We are now in a position to formulate our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<d<d^{\prime}
. If Condition
1.1
holds then the differential equation (
1.5
) admits unique solutions
W_{j}(u,\xi)
,
0\leq j\leq n-1
, of the form (
1.14
) with the following properties. The functions
\eta_{j}(u,\xi)
are analytic in
\left\{u:\operatorname{Re}(u)>\sigma^{\prime}\right\}\times\Gamma_{j}(d)
, meeting the limit conditions (
1.16
), and can be represented in the form

\eta_{j}(u,\xi)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-ut}F_{j}(t,\xi)\mathrm{d}t.

The Borel transforms
F_{j}(t,\xi)
are analytic functions in
U(\mathfrak{a}_{n}d)\times\Gamma_{j}(d)
, with convergent power series expansions

(1.20)

F_{j}(t,\xi)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathsf{A}_{j,m+1}(\xi)}{m!}t^{m},

valid for
(t,\xi)\in B(\mathfrak{a}_{n}d)\times\Gamma_{j}(d)
. Moreover, for each
\sigma>\sigma^{\prime}
and
0<r<d
, there exists a positive constant
K=K(\sigma,r)
, independent of
u
,
t
,
\xi
, and
j
, such that

(1.21)

\left|F_{j}(t,\xi)\right|\leq K\mathrm{e}^{\sigma\operatorname{Re}(t)}\sum\limits_{\ell\neq j}\frac{1}{{(\max(1,\operatorname{Re}(\xi\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{j,\ell}})))^{\rho}}},

for
(t,\xi)\in U(\mathfrak{a}_{n}r)\times\Gamma_{j}(d)
. Finally, for each
\sigma>\sigma^{\prime}
,

(1.22)

\eta_{j}(u,\xi)\sim\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\mathsf{A}_{j,m}(\xi)}{u^{m}}

as

.

Remarks.

(i)

To gain a better understanding of the main result, consider the following particular case. Suppose that the functions
\psi_{k}(u,\xi)
are (potentially multivalued) analytic functions in the
\xi
-plane, except for a finite set of singularities (which could involve branch points) situated at
\xi=\xi_{m}
, where
m=1,2,\ldots
. For simplicity, we focus on the Borel summability of the formal solution
\widehat{W}_{0}(u,\xi)
. We introduce branch cuts extending from each singularity
\xi_{m}
towards

\xi_{m}-\infty\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta_{0}}.

Subsequently, we remove each infinite (closed) sector that has its vertex at
\xi_{m}
, an opening angle of
\frac{n-2}{n}\pi
, and the branch cut serving as the axis of symmetry. The pre-image in the
z
-plane of each of the radii of these sectors is a Stokes curve. These curves emanate either from a zero of
f_{0,0}(z)
or from a singularity of one of the functions
f_{k}(u,z)
. Suppose that the functions
\psi_{k}(u,\xi)
satisfy Condition
1.1
within this resulting
\xi
-domain, which we denote by
\Delta_{0}
. Then we can set

\Gamma_{0}(d)=\Big\{\xi:\xi\in\Delta_{0},\,\inf_{\zeta\in\Delta_{0}}|\xi-\zeta|\geq d\Big\};

for example, see Figure
1(b)
. Consequently, under these circumstances, Theorem
1.1
ensures the Borel summability of the formal solution
\widehat{W}_{0}(u,\xi)
within a Stokes region, away from Stokes curves. A similar assertion applies to the remaining
n-1
formal solutions.

(ii)

Drawing from the theory of second-order equations
[
20
,
21
]
, we anticipate that, in quite general scenarios, the singularities of the Borel transform
F_{j}(t,\xi)
within the
t
-plane will be situated at

t=-(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\ell/n}-\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j/n})(\xi-\xi_{m}),\quad\ell\neq j.

In line with the preceding remark,
\xi_{m}
denotes a singularity of any of the functions
\psi_{k}(u,\xi)
. Consequently, the power series (
1.20
) should converge within the disc

\left|t\right|<\min_{\ell\neq j}\big|\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\ell/n}-\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j/n}\big|\inf_{\xi\in\Gamma_{j}(d)}\left|\xi-\xi_{m}\right|=2\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right)d.

For
n=2
, this aligns with the disc
B(\mathfrak{a}_{2}d)=B(2d)
, and for large values of
n
,

2\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{n}\right)\sim\frac{2\pi}{n}\sim(5.0008191965\ldots)\times\mathfrak{a}_{n}

via Proposition
D.1
. Consequently, the domains
B(\mathfrak{a}_{n}d)
and
U(\mathfrak{a}_{n}d)
that our analysis suggests do not seem unrealistic.

(iii)

Using Theorem
1.1
, we can establish the Borel summability of formal solutions whose
m^{\text{th}}
asymptotic expansion coefficients evaluate to some prescribed complex values
\alpha_{j,m}
at certain fixed
\beta_{j}\in\Gamma_{j}(d)
. Assuming the formal series

\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha_{j,m}}{u^{m}}

are Borel summable in
U(\mathfrak{a}_{n}d)
with some
0<d<d^{\prime}
, provided
\operatorname{Re}(u)>\sigma^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
, let
G_{j}(t)
denote their respective Borel transforms. If

represent the exact solutions given by (

1.1
, then

W_{j}(u,\xi,\beta_{j})=W_{j}(u,\xi)\left(1+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-ut}F_{j}(t,\beta_{j})\mathrm{d}t\right)^{-1}\left(1+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-ut}G_{j}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)

also stand as solutions of the differential equation (
1.5
) for sufficiently large

. The right-hand side can be re-expressed as

\exp\Big(\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathrm{i}j/n}u\xi+X_{j}(\xi)\Big)\left(1+\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-ut}F_{j}(t,\xi,\beta_{j})\mathrm{d}t\right),

where, for each
0<r<d

are analytic in
(t,\xi)\in U(\mathfrak{a}_{n}r)\times\Gamma_{j}(d)

(cf.

[

, the Laplace transforms yield asymptotic expansions in inverse powers of the large parameter

(iv)

In the case of second-order equations (
n=2
) with a potential function of the form
f_{0}(u,z)=f_{0,0}(z)+f_{0,1}(z)u^{-1}+f_{0,2}(z)u^{-2}
, Theorem
1.1
is equivalent to Theorem 1.1 of the recent paper by the present author
[
29
]
. In
[

]
, the Borel transforms

, where

introduces complexities that complicate such an analysis, leading to the breakdown of the argument used in
[

]
. In the present work, addressing this difficulty, we adopted an alternative proof strategy relying on ordinary differential equation techniques instead of partial differential equation methods.

In the following theorem, we present convergent alternatives to the asymptotic expansions (
1.22
). The coefficients in these expansions depend on an additional (positive) parameter
\omega
and can be computed via the formula

\mathsf{B}_{j,m}(\omega,\xi)=\sum\limits_{r=0}^{m}\omega^{m-r}|s(m,r)|\mathsf{A}_{j,r+1}(\xi),

where

, and

denote the Stirling numbers of the first kind. Note that the
\mathsf{B}_{j,m}(\omega,\xi)
are polynomials in
\omega
of degree

and are analytic functions of
\xi\in\Gamma_{j}(d)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0<d<d^{\prime}
and
\omega>\frac{\pi}{2\mathfrak{a}_{n}d}
. If Condition
1.1
holds, then the
functions
\eta_{j}(u,\xi)
given in Theorem

possess factorial expansions of the form

\eta_{j}(u,\xi)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathsf{B}_{j,m}(\omega,\xi)}{u(u+\omega)\cdots(u+m\omega)},

which are absolutely convergent for

, uniformly for
\xi\in\Gamma_{j}(d)
.

The deduction of Theorem
1.2
from Theorem
1.1
is completely analogous to that of the corresponding result in the specific case of
n=2

[
29
, Theorem 1.3]
. Consequently, the proof is omitted.

The remaining part of the paper is organised as follows. The proof of Theorem
1.1
consists of three steps. The first step is presented in Section
2
, where we establish that the coefficients
\mathsf{A}_{j,m}(\xi)
and their derivatives satisfy Gevrey-type estimates. Moving on to the second step, in Section
3
, we construct the functions
\eta_{j}(u,\xi)
specified in Theorem
1.1
in the form of truncated asymptotic expansions, each accompanied by its respective remainder term, possessing a Gevrey-type bound. The proof relies on solving specific integral equations, building upon the results established in Section
2
. The third and final step, detailed in Section

. This process relies on inverse Laplace transforming the functions
\eta_{j}(u,\xi)
constructed in Section
3
. We illustrate our theory in Section

by applying it to an

2.
Gevrey-type estimates for the coefficients

The primary objective of this section is to establish Gevrey-type estimates for the coefficients
\mathsf{A}_{j,m}(\xi)
and their derivatives. Our findings can be summarised as follows.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
0<d<d^{\prime}
. If Condition
1.1
is satisfied, then the following inequalities hold for each
0\leq j\leq n-1
, and any positive integers
m
and
s
:

\displaystyle\left|\mathsf{A}_{j,m}(\xi)\right|\leq\frac{1}{\mathfrak{a}_{n}^{m-1}}\frac{1}{d^{m}}\frac{(m+L+1)!}{(\max(1,\operatorname{Re}(\xi\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{j}})))^{\rho}},

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}^{s}\mathsf{A}_{j,m}(\xi)}{\mathrm{d}\xi^{s}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\mathfrak{a}_{n}^{m-1}}\frac{1}{d^{m+s-1}}\frac{(m+L+s)!}{1+\left|\xi\right|^{1+\rho}}

provided
\xi\in\Gamma_{j}(d)
. Here,
L
is a suitable positive integer that is independent of
u
,
\xi
,
j
,
m
and
s
.

Equation (
1.5
) can be transformed into a first-order system using standard methods. Sibuya
[
36
]
demonstrated that the coefficients of a formal solution to such a system satisfy Gevrey-type estimates. However, in our specific scenario, we require more explicit information regarding the domain of validity and the specific constants featured in these estimates.

In order to prove Proposition
2.1
, we shall establish a series of lemmas.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\gamma
be non-negative integers with
\beta\geq\gamma
. Then, we have

\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{q}(\gamma+q)!(\alpha+\beta-\gamma-q)!=\frac{{(\alpha+\beta+1)!}}{{(\beta+1)\binom{\beta}{\gamma}}}.

Proof.

We express the given sum in terms of Pochhammer symbols and the Gauss hypergeometric function
{}_{2}F_{1}
evaluated at unity, and then employ the Chu–Vandermonde identity
[
34
,
Eq. 15.4.24
]
:

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\alpha}{\binom{\alpha}{q}(\gamma+q)!(\alpha+\beta-\gamma-q)!}=\frac{{(\alpha+\beta)!}}{{\binom{-\alpha-\beta+\gamma+1}{\gamma}}}\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\alpha}{(-1)^{\gamma}\frac{{(-\alpha)_{q}(\gamma+1)_{q}}}{{(-\alpha-\beta+\gamma)_{q}}}\frac{1}{{q!}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{{(\alpha+\beta)!}}{{\binom{\alpha+\beta}{\gamma}}}\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\alpha}{\frac{{(-\alpha)_{q}(\gamma+1)_{q}}}{{(-\alpha-\beta+\gamma)_{q}}}\frac{1}{{q!}}}=\gamma!(\alpha+\beta-\gamma)!{}_{2}F_{1}(-\alpha,\gamma+1,-\alpha-\beta+\gamma,1)

\displaystyle=\gamma!(\alpha+\beta-\gamma)!\frac{{(-\alpha-\beta-1)_{\alpha}}}{{(-\alpha-\beta+\gamma)_{\alpha}}}=\gamma!(\alpha+\beta-\gamma)!\frac{{(\alpha+\beta+1)!(\beta-\gamma)!}}{{(\beta+1)!(\alpha+\beta-\gamma)!}}=\frac{{(\alpha+\beta+1)!}}{{(\beta+1)\binom{\beta}{\gamma}}}.

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
\alpha
and
\beta
are non-negative integers. Then, the following inequalities hold:

(2.1)

\alpha!\beta!\leq(\alpha+\beta-1)!

provided that
\alpha,\beta\geq 1
,

(2.2)

\alpha!\beta!\leq(\alpha+\beta)!

provided that
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
. Furthermore, if
\alpha\geq 1
, we have

(2.3)

\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\alpha-1}{(\alpha-q)!(q+1)!}\leq 4\cdot\alpha!

and

(2.4)

\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\alpha}{(\alpha-q)!q!}\leq 2\cdot\alpha!.

Finally, if
\alpha\geq 1
and
\beta\geq 2
, then

(2.5)

\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\alpha-1}{\frac{{(\alpha-q)!(q+\beta)!}}{{q+1}}}\leq 2\frac{{(\alpha+\beta)!}}{{\alpha+1}}

and

(2.6)

\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\alpha}{\frac{{(\alpha-q)!(q+\beta)!}}{{q+1}}}\leq 3\frac{{(\alpha+\beta)!}}{{\alpha+1}}.

Proof.

Inequality (
2.1
) is clearly satisfied when
\alpha\geq 1
and
\beta=1
. If
\alpha\geq 1
and
\beta\geq 2
, then

(\alpha+\beta-1)!=\alpha!(\alpha-1+2)\cdots(\alpha-1+\beta)\geq\alpha!\beta!.

If either
\alpha
or
\beta
(or both) is equal to
0
, then inequality (
2.2
) certainly holds. Conversely, when both
\alpha
and
\beta
are greater than
0
, it is evident that inequality (
2.2
) is less restrictive compared to (
2.1
).

The validity of inequality (
2.3
) can be directly verified for
\alpha=1
and
2
, and for
\alpha\geq 3
:

\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\alpha-1}{(\alpha-q)!(q+1)!}=2\cdot\alpha!+(\alpha+1)!\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\alpha-2}\frac{1}{\binom{\alpha+1}{q+1}}\\
\leq 2\cdot\alpha!+(\alpha+1)!\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\alpha-2}{\frac{2}{{(\alpha+1)\alpha}}}\leq 2\cdot\alpha!+2\cdot\alpha!=4\cdot\alpha!.

The validity of inequality (
2.4
) is apparent when
\alpha=1
. For
\alpha\geq 2
, we can make use of inequality (
2.1
) as follows:

\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\alpha}{(\alpha-q)!q!}=\alpha!+\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\alpha-1}{(\alpha-q)!q!}\leq\alpha!+\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\alpha-1}{(\alpha-1)!}\leq 2\cdot\alpha!.

The validity of inequality (
2.5
) is readily apparent for
\alpha=1
, and for
\alpha\geq 2
, we have

\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\alpha-1}{\frac{{(\alpha-q)!(q+\beta)!}}{{q+1}}}=\frac{{(\alpha+\beta)!}}{{\alpha(\alpha+\beta)}}+(\alpha+\beta)!\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\alpha-2}{\frac{1}{{(q+1)\binom{\alpha+\beta}{q+\beta}}}}\\
\leq\frac{{(\alpha+\beta)!}}{{\alpha(\alpha+\beta)}}+(\alpha+\beta-2)!\sum\limits_{q=0}^{\alpha-2}{\frac{2}{{q+1}}}\leq\frac{{(\alpha+\beta)!}}{{\alpha(\alpha+\beta)}}+(\alpha+\beta-1)!\leq 2\frac{{(\alpha+\beta)!}}{{\alpha+1}}.

Inequality (
2.6
) is clearly satisfied when
\alpha=1
and
2
. If
\alpha\geq 3
, then

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\alpha}{\frac{{(\alpha-q)!(q+\beta)!}}{{q+1}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{{(\alpha+\beta)!}}{{\alpha+1}}+\frac{{(\alpha+\beta-1)!}}{\alpha}+(\alpha+\beta)!\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\alpha-2}{\frac{1}{{(q+1)\binom{\alpha+\beta}{q+\beta}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{(\alpha+\beta)!}}{{\alpha+1}}+\frac{{(\alpha+\beta)!}}{{\alpha(\alpha+\beta)}}+(\alpha+\beta-2)!\sum\limits_{q=1}^{\alpha-2}{\frac{2}{{q+1}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{{(\alpha+\beta)!}}{{\alpha+1}}+\frac{{(\alpha+\beta)!}}{{\alpha(\alpha+\beta)}}+(\alpha+\beta-1)!\leq 3\frac{{(\alpha+\beta)!}}{{\alpha+1}}.

∎

In this section, we construct the Borel transforms
F_{j}(t,\xi)
of the formal solutions (
1.7
) and demonstrate their properties as outlined in Theorem
1.1
. We start with the simple observation that if the Borel transforms
F_{j}(t,\xi)
of the formal solutions (
1.7
) exist, they must be the inverse Laplace transforms of the functions
\eta_{j}(u,\xi)
, given in Proposition
3.1
, with respect to
u
. Hence, for
t\geq 0
and
\xi\in\Gamma_{j}(d)
, we define the functions
F_{j}(t,\xi)
as

F_{j}(t,\xi)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\lim_{U\to+\infty}\int_{\sigma-\mathrm{i}U}^{\sigma+\mathrm{i}U}\mathrm{e}^{ut}\eta_{j}(u,\xi)\mathrm{d}u,

where
\sigma
is any real number greater than
\sigma^{\prime}
. Our aim is to show that these functions extend analytically to an appropriate neighbourhood of the positive real axis with respect to
t
, exhibiting at most exponential growth. Upon substituting the truncated expansions (
3.1
) of the functions
\eta_{j}(u,\xi)
with
N
being replaced by
N+2
and integrating term-by-term, we obtain

(4.1)

F_{j}(t,\xi)=\sum_{m=0}^{N}\frac{\mathsf{A}_{j,m+1}(\xi)}{m!}t^{m}+\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\sigma-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{\sigma+\mathrm{i}\infty}\mathrm{e}^{ut}R_{j,N+2}(u,\xi)\mathrm{d}u.

Note that, due to the bounds (
3.2
) for
R_{j,N+2}(u,\xi)
, the contour integrals converge absolutely, and the convergence is uniform with respect to
\xi\in\Gamma_{j}(d)
for any
N\geq 0
. Consequently, the
F_{j}(t,\xi)
are analytic functions of
\xi
in
\Gamma_{j}(d)
. Differentiating both sides of (
4.1
)
N
times with respect to
t
yields

(4.2)

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{N}F_{j}(t,\xi)}{\mathrm{d}t^{N}}=\mathsf{A}_{j,N+1}(\xi)+\frac{1}{2\pi\mathrm{i}}\int_{\sigma-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{\sigma+\mathrm{i}\infty}\mathrm{e}^{ut}u^{N}R_{j,N+2}(u,\xi)\mathrm{d}u.

From Proposition
3.1
, we infer

\left|\mathsf{A}_{j,N+1}(\xi)\right|=\mathop{\lim}\limits_{u\to+\infty}u^{N+1}\left|{R_{j,N+1}(u,\xi)}\right|\leq c_{5}\frac{1}{\mathfrak{a}_{n}^{N+1}}\frac{{(N+n+L+1)!}}{{d^{N+1}}}\sum\limits_{\ell\neq j}{\frac{1}{(\max(1,\operatorname{Re}(\xi\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{j,\ell}})))^{\rho}}}.

By combining these estimates with (
3.2
), we obtain the following bounds:

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}^{N}F_{j}(t,\xi)}{\mathrm{d}t^{N}}\right|\leq|\mathsf{A}_{j,N+1}(\xi)|+\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\sigma-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{\sigma+\mathrm{i}\infty}\left|\mathrm{e}^{ut}u^{N}R_{j,N+2}(u,\xi)\right||\mathrm{d}u|

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\frac{1}{{N+n+L+2}}+\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{a}_{n}d}}\mathrm{e}^{\sigma t}\frac{1}{{2\pi}}\int_{\sigma-\mathrm{i}\infty}^{\sigma+\mathrm{i}\infty}{\frac{{\left|{\mathrm{d}u}\right|}}{{\left|u\right|^{2}}}}\bigg)c_{5}\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{a}_{n}^{N+1}}}\frac{{(N+n+L+2)!}}{{d^{N+1}}}\sum\limits_{\ell\neq j}{\frac{1}{{(\max(1,\operatorname{Re}(\xi\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{j,\ell}})))^{\rho}}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left({1+\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{a}_{n}d}}\frac{1}{{2\sigma}}}\right)c_{5}\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{a}_{n}^{N+1}}}\frac{{(N+n+L+2)!}}{{d^{N+1}}}\mathrm{e}^{\sigma t}\sum\limits_{\ell\neq j}{\frac{1}{{(\max(1,\operatorname{Re}(\xi\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{j,\ell}})))^{\rho}}}}

for any
t\geq 0
,
\xi\in\Gamma_{j}(d)
, and
N\geq 0
. Accordingly,

\limsup_{N\to+\infty}\left|\frac{1}{N!}\frac{\mathrm{d}^{N}F_{j}(t,\xi)}{\mathrm{d}t^{N}}\right|^{1/N}\leq\frac{1}{\mathfrak{a}_{n}d}.

Therefore, according to the Cauchy–Hadamard theorem, for any
t_{0}\geq 0
, the Taylor series

(4.3)

\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{m!}\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m}F_{j}(t,\xi)}{\mathrm{d}t^{m}}\right]_{t=t_{0}}(t-t_{0})^{m}

converge absolutely within the open disc
|t-t_{0}|<\mathfrak{a}_{n}d
. As a result, the power series (
4.3
) provide the analytic continuation of the
F_{j}(t,\xi)
into the domain
U(\mathfrak{a}_{n}d)
. Moreover, based on the established bounds for the
N
th derivatives, we deduce the uniform convergence of series (
4.3
) on
\Gamma_{j}(d)
. Consequently, the functions
F_{j}(t,\xi)
are analytic with respect to
\xi
for every fixed
t\in U(\mathfrak{a}_{n}d)
. Given their continuity in
(t,\xi)
and analyticity in each respective variable, these functions are analytic functions within
U(\mathfrak{a}_{n}d)\times\Gamma_{j}(d)
.

By taking
t=0
and letting
\sigma\to+\infty
in (
4.2
), it follows that

\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{N}F_{j}(t,\xi)}{\mathrm{d}t^{N}}\right]_{t=0}=\mathsf{A}_{j,N+1}(\xi),

thus confirming the validity of the power series expansions in (
1.20
).

To establish the exponential-type bounds (
1.21
) on
|F_{j}(t,\xi)|
, we can proceed as follows. Assume
0<r<d
and
t\in U(\mathfrak{a}_{n}r)
. When
\operatorname{Re}(t)\geq 0
, the following inequalities hold:

\displaystyle\left|F_{j}(t,\xi)\right|\leq\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{{m!}}\left|\left[\frac{\mathrm{d}^{m}F_{j}(t,\xi)}{\mathrm{d}t^{m}}\right]_{t=\operatorname{Re}(t)}\right|\left|{t-\operatorname{Re}(t)}\right|^{m}

\displaystyle\leq\left({1+\frac{1}{\mathfrak{a}_{n}d}\frac{1}{{2\sigma}}}\right)\frac{{c_{5}}}{{\mathfrak{a}_{n}d}}\mathrm{e}^{\sigma\operatorname{Re}(t)}\sum\limits_{\ell\neq j}{\frac{1}{{(\max(1,\operatorname{Re}(\xi\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{j,\ell}})))^{\rho}}}}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}{\frac{{(m+n+L+2)!}}{{m!}}\left({\frac{{\left|{t-\operatorname{Re}(t)}\right|}}{{\mathfrak{a}_{n}d}}}\right)^{m}}

\displaystyle\leq\left({1+\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{a}_{n}d}}\frac{1}{{2\sigma}}}\right)\frac{{c_{5}}}{{\mathfrak{a}_{n}d}}\mathrm{e}^{\sigma\operatorname{Re}(t)}\sum\limits_{\ell\neq j}{\frac{1}{{(\max(1,\operatorname{Re}(\xi\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{j,\ell}})))^{\rho}}}}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}{\frac{{(m+n+L+2)!}}{{m!}}\left({\frac{r}{d}}\right)^{m}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\left({1+\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{a}_{n}d}}\frac{1}{{2\sigma}}}\right)\frac{c_{5}}{\mathfrak{a}_{n}d}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{{(m+n+L+2)!}}{{m!}}\left({\frac{r}{d}}\right)^{m}\bigg)\mathrm{e}^{\sigma\operatorname{Re}(t)}\sum\limits_{\ell\neq j}\frac{1}{{(\max(1,\operatorname{Re}(\xi\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\theta_{j,\ell}})))^{\rho}}},

where the infinite series is convergent due to the constraint
0<r<d
. For
\operatorname{Re}(t)<0
, a similar series of estimates can be repeated, replacing
\operatorname{Re}(t)
with
0
. Overall, it is found that with the choice

K(\sigma,r)=\bigg(\left({1+\frac{1}{{\mathfrak{a}_{n}d}}\frac{1}{{2\sigma}}}\right)\frac{{c_{5}}}{{\mathfrak{a}_{n}d}}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{{(m+n+L+2)!}}{{m!}}\left({\frac{r}{d}}\right)^{m}\bigg)\mathrm{e}^{\sigma\mathfrak{a}_{n}r},

the estimates (
1.21
) hold for
(t,\xi)\in U(\mathfrak{a}_{n}r)\times\Gamma_{j}(d)
.

The asymptotic expansions in (
1.22
) follow directly from Proposition
3.1
. This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

An application

n^{\text{th}}
-order (
n\geq 2
) Airy-type equation with a large parameter
u

(5.1)

\bigg(-\frac{\mathrm{d}^{n}}{\mathrm{d}z^{n}}+u^{n}z\bigg)w(u,z)=0.

When
n=2

[
38

\mathbf{D}

n^{\text{th}}

1.5

(5.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\psi_{k}(u,\xi)&=\delta_{0,k}-\frac{1}{{u^{n-k}}}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}}{{z^{(n-k)(1+1/n)}}}\sum\limits_{p=k}^{n}{\binom{n}{p}\left({\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{1}{{2n}}}\right)_{n-p}\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p,k}\left({1,-\tfrac{1}{n},\ldots,\left({-\tfrac{1}{n}}\right)_{p-k}}\right)}\\

6.
Discussion

We studied the Borel summability of formal solutions of certain
n^{\text{th}}
-order linear ordinary differential equations with a large parameter. We demonstrated that, given mild conditions on the potential functions of the equation, the formal solutions are Borel summable with respect to the large parameter in vast, unbounded domains of the independent variable. We established that the formal series expansions serve as asymptotic expansions, uniform with respect to the independent variable, for the Borel re-summed exact solutions. Additionally, we showed that the exact solutions can be expressed using factorial series in the parameter, and these expansions converge in half-planes, uniformly in the independent variable.

The theory presented here marks an initial step toward a global analysis of formal solutions of the differential equation (
1.1
). The primary result of this paper demonstrates the Borel summability of formal solutions within a Stokes region, provided that the independent variable remains bounded away from Stokes curves emerging either from a zero of
f_{0,0}(z)
or from a singularity of one of the functions
f_{k}(u,z)
. Importantly, it does not provide any information regarding connection formulae joining formal solutions across the Stokes curves.

In the following discussion, we briefly explore a potential extension of the theory to turning point problems. Turning points represent the simplest form of transition points. For illustration, consider the following third-order differential equation:

(6.1)

\bigg(-\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}z^{3}}+u^{3}f(z)\bigg)w(u,z)=0.

Suppose that the potential function
f(z)
is analytic in a domain
\mathbf{D}
and
f(z)
vanishes at exactly one point
z_{0}
, say, of
\mathbf{D}
. For the sake of simplicity, we assume that
z_{0}
is a simple zero of
f(z)
. It is convenient to work in terms of the transformed variables
\zeta
and
\mathcal{W}(u,\zeta)
given by

(6.2)

\frac{3}{4}\zeta^{4/3}=\int_{z_{0}}^{z}f^{1/3}(t)\mathrm{d}t,\quad\mathcal{W}(u,\zeta)=\zeta^{-1/3}f^{1/3}(z)w(u,z).

Subsequently, equation (
6.1
) transforms into

(6.3)

\bigg(-\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}\zeta^{3}}+\phi(\zeta)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\zeta}+u^{3}\zeta+\psi(\zeta)\bigg)\mathcal{W}(u,\zeta)=0,

with

\phi(\zeta)=\bigg(\frac{\mathrm{d}\widehat{f}^{-1/3}(z)}{{\mathrm{d}z}}\bigg)^{2}-2\widehat{f}^{-1/3}(z)\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}\widehat{f}^{-1/3}(z)}{\mathrm{d}z^{2}},\quad\psi(\zeta)=-\widehat{f}^{-2/3}(z)\frac{{\mathrm{d}^{3}\widehat{f}^{-1/3}(z)}}{{\mathrm{d}z^{3}}}

and
\widehat{f}(z)=\zeta^{-1}f(z)
. The functions
\phi(\zeta)
and
\psi(\zeta)
are analytic in the corresponding
\zeta
domain
\mathbf{H}
, say. The transformation (
6.2
) maps the simple turning point
z_{0}
into the origin in
\mathbf{H}
, and the four Stokes curves issuing from
z_{0}
are mapped into the rays
\arg\zeta=0,\frac{\pi}{2}\mathrm{i},\pi\mathrm{i},\frac{3\pi}{2}\mathrm{i}
. Let
Y(x)
be any solution of the third-order Airy-type equation

(6.4)

\bigg(-\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}}{\mathrm{d}x^{3}}+x\bigg)Y(x)=0.

It can be verified by direct substitution that equation (
6.3
) has a formal solution of the form

(6.5)

\mathcal{W}(u,\zeta)=Y(u^{3/4}\zeta)\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{A_{m}(\zeta)}{u^{3m}}+u^{-9/4}Y^{\prime}(u^{3/4}\zeta)\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{B_{m}(\zeta)}{u^{3m}}+u^{-3/2}Y^{\prime\prime}(u^{3/4}\zeta)\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{C_{m}(\zeta)}{u^{3m}}.

The coefficients
A_{m}(\zeta)
,
B_{m}(\zeta)
and
C_{m}(\zeta)
, which are analytic functions of
\zeta
in
\mathbf{H}
, can be determined recursively via

\displaystyle A_{0}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=1,\quad C_{0}(\zeta)=\frac{1}{3}\zeta^{-2/3}\int_{0}^{\zeta}t^{-1/3}\phi(t)\mathrm{d}t,

\displaystyle B_{0}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3}\zeta^{-1/3}\int_{0}^{\zeta}t^{-2/3}\left(3(tC^{\prime}_{0}(t))^{\prime}-\phi(t)tC_{0}(t)-\psi(t)\right)\mathrm{d}t,

and

\displaystyle A_{m}(\zeta)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{3}\int^{\zeta}\left(3B^{\prime\prime}_{m-1}(t)-\phi(t)B_{m-1}(t)+C^{\prime\prime\prime}_{m-1}(t)-\phi(t)C^{\prime}_{m-1}(t)-\psi(t)C_{m-1}(t)\right)\mathrm{d}t,

Appendix C
Bell polynomials

Due to the frequent occurrences of the Bell polynomials in this paper, we have compiled in this appendix their properties that are used throughout the paper. The partial exponential Bell polynomials
\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p,r}
are a triangular array of polynomials defined by the generating function

\frac{1}{r!}\bigg(\sum\limits_{p=1}^{\infty}x_{p}\frac{z^{p}}{p!}\bigg)^{r}=\sum\limits_{p=r}^{\infty}\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p,r}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{p-r+1})\frac{z^{p}}{p!},\quad r\geq 0.

They can be computed efficiently by a recurrence relation:

(C.1)

\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p+1,r+1}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{p-r+1})=\sum\limits_{q=0}^{p-r}\binom{p}{q}x_{q+1}\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p-q,r}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{p-r-q+1}),

where
\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{0,0}=1
,
\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p,0}=0
for
p\geq 1
, and
\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{0,r}=0
for
r\geq 1
. The partial exponential Bell polynomials satisfy the following homogeneity relation:

(C.2)

\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p,r}(\alpha\beta x_{1},\alpha\beta^{2}x_{2},\ldots,\alpha\beta^{p-r+1}x_{p-r+1})=\alpha^{r}\beta^{p}\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p,r}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{p-r+1}).

By applying the chain rule, one can derive the following identity:

(C.3)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p,r}(x_{1}(z),x_{2}(z),\ldots,x_{p-r+1}(z))=\sum_{q=1}^{p-r+1}\binom{p}{q}x_{q}^{\prime}(z)\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p-q,r-1}(x_{1}(z),x_{2}(z),\ldots,x_{p-q-r+2}(z)).

The sum

\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{p})=\sum_{r=0}^{p}\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p,r}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{p-r+1})

is called the
p^{\text{th}}
complete exponential Bell polynomial. These polynomials can be computed recursively via

(C.4)

\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p+1}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{p+1})=\sum\limits_{q=0}^{p}\binom{p}{q}x_{q+1}\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p-q}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{p-q}),

with the initial value
\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{0}=1
. The exponential Bell polynomials play a role in the celebrated Faà di Bruno formula, which generalises the chain rule to higher derivatives. In particular,

(C.5)

\frac{\mathrm{d}^{p}\exp(x(z))}{\mathrm{d}z^{p}}=\exp(x(z))\text{\large$\mathtt{B}$}_{p}(x^{\prime}(z),x^{\prime\prime}(z),\ldots,x^{(p)}(z)).

For further properties of the Bell polynomials, we refer to
[
12
, Ch. III]
.

Appendix D
The sequence
\mathfrak{a}_{n}

In this appendix, we establish certain properties of the sequence
\mathfrak{a}_{n}
with
n\geq 2
, where the
n^{\text{th}}
term is defined as the unique positive solution of the equation

(1+\mathfrak{a}_{n})^{n}=1+2n\mathfrak{a}_{n}.

The verification that this equation possesses a distinct positive solution for each
n\geq 2
is left to the reader.

Proposition D.1
.

Let
a=1.2564312086\ldots
be the unique positive solution of the equation
\mathrm{e}^{a}=1+2a
. Then for any integer
n\geq 2
, the double inequality

(D.1)

\frac{a}{n}\leq\mathfrak{a}_{n}\leq\frac{2}{n-1}

holds. Furthermore, the sequence
\mathfrak{a}_{n}
satisfies the asymptotic limit

(D.2)

\mathfrak{a}_{n}\sim\frac{a}{n},

as
n\to+\infty
.

Here, we remark that the constant
a
can be represented using particular branches of the Lambert
W
-function (see, for instance,
[
34
,
§4.13
]
):

a=-\tfrac{1}{2}-W_{\pm 1}\big(-\tfrac{1}{{2\sqrt{\mathrm{e}}}}\mp 0\mathrm{i}\big).

Proof.

First, we note that
1+\mathfrak{a}_{n}\leq\mathrm{e}^{\mathfrak{a}_{n}}
. Consequently,

1+2n\mathfrak{a}_{n}\leq\mathrm{e}^{n\mathfrak{a}_{n}},

meaning

2\leq\frac{{\mathrm{e}^{n\mathfrak{a}_{n}}-1}}{{n\mathfrak{a}_{n}}}.

Since the function
x\mapsto(\mathrm{e}^{x}-1)/x
is monotonically increasing for
x>0
, the above inequality implies
a\leq n\mathfrak{a}_{n}
. On the other hand, employing the binomial theorem yields

(1+\mathfrak{a}_{n})^{n}\geq 1+n\mathfrak{a}_{n}+\frac{{n(n-1)}}{2}\mathfrak{a}_{n}^{2}

for any
n\geq 2
. Consequently,

1+n\mathfrak{a}_{n}+\frac{{n(n-1)}}{2}\mathfrak{a}_{n}^{2}\leq 1+2n\mathfrak{a}_{n}

which can be simplified to

\mathfrak{a}_{n}\leq\frac{2}{{n-1}}.

To establish the asymptotic equality (
D.2
), we make note of the fact that
(1+x)\mathrm{e}^{-x}=1+\mathcal{O}(x^{2})
as
x\to 0
. Combining this with the double inequality (
D.1
), we deduce that

\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}((1+\mathfrak{a}_{n})\mathrm{e}^{-\mathfrak{a}_{n}})^{n}=1.

As the limit is positive and the exponential function exhibits monotonic growth, we ascertain that

\limsup\limits_{n\to+\infty}(1+\mathfrak{a}_{n})^{n}=\limsup\limits_{n\to+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{n\mathfrak{a}_{n}}\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}((1+\mathfrak{a}_{n})\mathrm{e}^{-\mathfrak{a}_{n}})^{n}=\exp\Big(\limsup\limits_{n\to+\infty}(n\mathfrak{a}_{n})\Big).

Therefore,

\exp\Big(\limsup\limits_{n\to+\infty}(n\mathfrak{a}_{n})\Big)\leq 1+2\limsup\limits_{n\to+\infty}(n\mathfrak{a}_{n}),

which, given that
a\leq\limsup_{n\to+\infty}(n\mathfrak{a}_{n})\leq 2
as inferred from (
D.1
), implies

\frac{\exp\Big(\limsup\limits_{n\to+\infty}(n\mathfrak{a}_{n})\Big)-1}{\limsup\limits_{n\to+\infty}(n\mathfrak{a}_{n})}\leq 2.

Using once more the monotonous nature of the function
x\mapsto(\mathrm{e}^{x}-1)/x
, we deduce

\limsup\limits_{n\to+\infty}(n\mathfrak{a}_{n})\leq a,

and with reference to (
D.1
), this results in the desired limit

\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}(n\mathfrak{a}_{n})=a.

∎

Hausdorff moment problem for combinatorial numbers of Brown and Tutte: exact solution
K. A. Penson, K. Górska, A. Horzela, G. H. E. Duchamp

Abstract
We investigate the combinatorial sequences A ⁡ ( M , n ) A(M,n) introduced by W. G. Brown (1964) and W. T. Tutte (1980) appearing in enumeration of convex polyhedra. Their formula is A ⁡ ( M , n ) = 2 ​ ( 2 ​ M + 3 ) ! ( M + 2 ) ! ​ M ! ​ ( 4 ​ n + 2 ​ M + 1 ) ! n ! ​ ( 3 ​ n + 2 ​ M + 3 ) ! A(M,n)=\frac{2(2M+3)!}{(M+2)!M!}\,\frac{(4n+2M+1)!}{n!(3n+2M+3)!} with n , M = 0 , 1 , 2 , … n,M=0,1,2,\ldots , and we conceive it as Hausdorff moments, where M M is a parameter and n n enumerates the moments. We solve exactly the corresponding Hausdorff moment problem: A ⁡ ( M , n ) = ∫ 0 R x n ​ W M ​ ( x ) ​ d ⁡ x A(M,n)=\int_{0}^{R}x^{n}W_{M}(x)\D x on the natural support ( 0 , R ) (0,R) , R = 4 4 / 3 3 R=4^{4}/3^{3} , using the method of inverse Mellin transform. We provide explicitly the weight functions W M ​ ( x ) W_{M}(x) in terms of the Meijer G-functions G 4 , 4 4 , 0 G_{4,4}^{4,0} , or equivalently, the generalized hypergeometric functions F 2 3 {{}_{3}F_{2}} (for M = 0 , 1 M=0,1 ) and F 3 4 {{}_{4}F_{3}} (for M ≥ 2 M\geq 2 ). For M = 0 , 1 M=0,1 , we prove that W M ​ ( x ) W_{M}(x) are non-negative and normalizable, thus they are probability distributions. For M ≥ 2 M\geq 2 , W M ​ ( x ) W_{M}(x) are signed functions vanishing on the extremities of the support. By rephrasing this problem entirely in terms of Meijer G representations we reveal an integral relation which directly furnishes W M ​ ( x ) W_{M}(x) based on ordinary generating function of A ⁡ ( M , n ) A(M,n) as an input. All the results are studied analytically as well as graphically.

II
Definitions and preliminaries

The main tool that we employ in the treatment of various moment problems is the Mellin transform
\mathcal{M}
and it inverse
\mathcal{M}^{-1}
. In the following we group several definitions and informations about the Mellin transform of a function
f(x)

INSneddon72

\mathcal{M}[f(x);s]=f^{\star}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{s-1}f(x)dx,

(10)

along with its inverse

\mathcal{M}^{-1}[f^{\star}(s);x]=f(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}x^{-s}f^{\star}(s)ds.

(11)

For the role of constant
c
consult
INSneddon72
.

If
\mathcal{M}[f(x);s]=f^{\star}(s)
and
\mathcal{M}[g(x);s]=g^{\star}(s)
then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{-1}[f^{\star}(s)g^{\star}(s);x]&=\int_{0}^{\infty}f\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)g(t)\frac{1}{t}\D t\\
&=\int_{0}^{\infty}g\left(\frac{x}{t}\right)f(t)\frac{1}{t}\D t.\end{split}

(12)

The last two integrals are called Mellin (i.e. multiplicative) convolutions of
f(x)
with
g(x)
. For fixed
a>0
,
h\neq 0
, the Mellin transform satisfies the following scaling property:

\mathcal{M}[x^{b}f(ax^{h});s]=\frac{1}{|h|}a^{-\frac{s+b}{h}}f^{\star}(\mbox{$\frac{s+b}{h}$}).

(13)

Among known Mellin transforms a very special role is played by those entirely expressible through products and ratios of Euler’s gamma functions. The Meijer G-function is defined as an inverse Mellin transform

\displaystyle G^{m,n}_{p,q}\left(x\Big|{\alpha_{1}\ldots\alpha_{p}\atop\beta_{1}\ldots\beta_{q}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}^{-1}\left[\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma(\beta_{j}+s)\,\prod_{j=1}^{n}\Gamma(1-\alpha_{j}-s)}{\prod_{j=m+1}^{q}\Gamma(1-\beta_{j}-s)\,\prod_{j=n+1}^{p}\Gamma(\alpha_{j}+s)};x\right]

(14)

\displaystyle\qquad={\rm MeijerG}([[\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}],[\alpha_{n+1},\ldots,\alpha_{p}]],

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad[[\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}],[\beta_{m+1},\ldots,\beta_{q}]],x).

(15)

The notation for

\displaystyle\begin{split}&z\neq 0,\quad 0\leq m\leq q,\quad 0\leq n\leq p,\\
&\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{C},\quad j=1,\ldots,p;\quad\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{C},\quad j=1,\ldots,q.\end{split}

(16)

See Refs.
APPrudnikov-v3

KGorska13

\times G^{1,p}_{p,q+1}\left(-z\Big|{1-a_{1},\ldots,1-a_{p}\atop 0,1-b_{1},\ldots,1-b_{q}}\right)\!,

IV
Linking generating and weight functions

We start with a general Hausdorff moment problem in the form of Eq. (
8
). Solving Eq. (
8
) means to obtain
W(x)
given the set
\rho(n)
,
n=0,1,\ldots
. We define the ordinary generating function (ogf) of moments
\rho(n)
as

G(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\rho(n)z^{n},

(42)

with the radius of convergence equal to
1/R
, i.e.
z<1/R
. We classically observe that

\displaystyle\begin{split}G(z)&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}\left[\int_{0}^{R}x^{n}W(x)\D x\right]\\
&=\int_{0}^{R}W(x)\left[\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(xz)^{n}\right]\D x\\
&=\int_{0}^{R}\frac{W(x)}{1-zx}\D x,\end{split}

(43)

with
zx<1
. Since in Eq. (
43
)
0\leq x\leq R
, it implies
z<1/R
. From Eq. (
43
) it follows that

\frac{1}{z}G\left(\frac{1}{z}\right)=\int_{0}^{R}\frac{W(x)}{z-x}\D x,\qquad\text{with}\quad z>R.

(44)

The above equations constitute the seed of the inversion procedure by Stieltjes to solve Eq. (
44
) using the complex analysis. For singularity analysis of
G(z)
in complex plane see
JGLiu16
. For a recent detailed application of this method, along with the exhaustive reference list, see
ABostan20
. A very complete exposition of the Stieltjes method can be found in
AHora07
.

The above transformations are fairly standard, however in view of results of Sec.
III
, a certain pattern does appear that permits one to deduce
W(x)
directly from
G(z)
, via Eq. (
44
). In order to make explicit this pattern several manipulations with
G(z)\equiv G(M,z)
are needed.

We use the definition of Pochhammer symbols to write down the ogf
G(M,z)
of the moments
A(M,n)
, and it reads

\displaystyle G(M,z)=\frac{2(2M+1)!}{(M+2)!M!}\,{{}_{4}F_{3}}\left({\Delta(4,2M+2)\atop\Delta(3,2M+4)};Rz\right)

(45)

\displaystyle=\frac{2(2M+1)!}{(M+2)!M!}\,{{}_{4}F_{3}}\big(\big[1+\mbox{$\frac{M}{2}$},\mbox{$\frac{3}{4}$}+\mbox{$\frac{M}{2}$},\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}+\mbox{$\frac{M}{2}$},\mbox{$\frac{5}{4}$}+\mbox{$\frac{M}{2}$}\big],

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\big[2+\mbox{$\frac{2M}{3}$},\mbox{$\frac{5}{3}$}+\mbox{$\frac{2M}{3}$},\mbox{$\frac{4}{3}$}+\mbox{$\frac{2M}{3}$}\big];Rz\big),\quad z<R.

(46)

We come back to Eq. (
18
) in order to frame Eq. (
45
) in the Meijer G-notation. Carrying out the products of gamma functions in Eq. (
18
) this furnishes:

\displaystyle G(M,z)=r_{G}(M)G^{1,4}_{4,4}\left(-Rz\Big|{\Delta(4,-2M-1)\atop 0,\Delta(3,-2M-3)}\right)

(47)

\displaystyle=r_{G}(M){\rm MeijerG}\big(\big[\big[-\mbox{$\frac{M}{2}$},\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}-\mbox{$\frac{M}{2}$},\mbox{$\frac{1}{4}$}-\mbox{$\frac{M}{2}$},-\mbox{$\frac{1}{4}$}-\mbox{$\frac{M}{2}$}\big],\big[\,\,\,\big]\big],

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\big[\big[0\big],\big[-\mbox{$\frac{2}{3}$}-\mbox{$\frac{2M}{3}$},-\mbox{$\frac{1}{3}$}-\mbox{$\frac{2M}{3}$},-1-\mbox{$\frac{2M}{3}$}\big]\big],-Rz\big)

(48)

with

r_{G}(M)=\frac{4}{81\sqrt{\pi}}3^{\frac{1}{2}-2M}2^{4M+\frac{1}{2}}P(M),

(49)

where in obtaining Eq. (
49
) the use of Eq. (
21
) was again made.

Further transformations of Eq. (
47
) are necessary in order to take the full advantage of Eq. (
44
). For that purpose we apply the Eq. (
19
) to Eq. (
47
):

G^{1,4}_{4,4}\left(\frac{1}{z}\Big|{a_{1},\ldots,a_{4}\atop b_{1},\ldots,b_{4}}\right)=G^{4,1}_{4,4}\left(z\Big|{1-b_{1},\ldots,1-b_{4}\atop 1-a_{1},\ldots,1-a_{4}}\right),

(50)

where
(a_{p})
,
p=4
, and
(b_{q})
,
q=4
can be read off Eq. (
47
). Then
G(M,z)
becomes

\displaystyle\begin{split}G(M,z)&=r_{G}(M)\\
&\times G^{4,1}_{4,4}\left(-\frac{1}{Rz}\Big|{1,\frac{4}{3}+\frac{2M}{3},\frac{5}{3}+\frac{2M}{3},2+\frac{2M}{3}\atop\frac{3}{4}+\frac{M}{2},1+\frac{M}{2},\frac{5}{4}+\frac{M}{2},\frac{3}{2}+\frac{M}{2}}\right),\end{split}

(51)

which permits to evaluate

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{z}G&\left(M,\frac{1}{z}\right)=r_{G}(M)\,\frac{1}{z}\\
&\times G^{4,1}_{4,4}\left(-\frac{z}{R}\Big|{1,\frac{4}{3}+\frac{2M}{3},\frac{5}{3}+\frac{2M}{3},2+\frac{2M}{3}\atop\frac{3}{4}+\frac{M}{2},1+\frac{M}{2},\frac{5}{4}+\frac{M}{2},\frac{3}{2}+\frac{M}{2}}\right).\end{split}

(52)

Eq. (

\frac{1}{z}G\left(M,\frac{1}{z}\right)=r_{G}(M)\,G^{4,1}_{4,4}\left(-\frac{z}{R}\Big|{a^{\prime}_{1}-1,\ldots,a^{\prime}_{4}-1\atop b^{\prime}_{1}-1,\ldots,b^{\prime}_{4}-1}\right),

(53)

where now
(a^{\prime}_{4})
and
(b^{\prime}_{4})
are read off the lists in Eq. (

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{z}G&\left(M,\frac{1}{z}\right)=-\frac{r_{G}(M)}{R}\\

V
Discussion and Conclusions

We have exactly solved the moment problem of Eq. (
8
) following two different, and seemingly unrelated paths. The first method used was the inverse Mellin transform which resulted in exact and explicit expression for the weight functions
W_{M}(x)
formulated in the language of Meijer G-functions. The second, less orthodox approach, consists in ”upgrading” the notation for the ogf of moments
G(M,z)
, which initially was a generalized hypergeometric function
{{}_{4}F_{3}}
, to express it in terms of a Meijer G-function. This procedure has revealed a hitherto hidden relation between the parameter lists of
G(M,z)
and the parameter lists of solutions
W_{M}(x)
. That observation is quite fertile, as it allows one, almost automatically, to obtain explicit forms of
W_{M}(x)
, without having recourse to any further manipulations. We believe that the sequence
A(M,n)
of Eq. (
1
) belongs to a larger family of moment sequences for which analogous relations of type Eqs. (
59
) and (
60
) hold. This feature is under active consideration.

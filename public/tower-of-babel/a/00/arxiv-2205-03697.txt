Abstract
Recently, Andrews and Merca have given a new combinatorial interpretation of the total number of even parts in all partitions of n n into distinct parts. We generalise this result and consider many more variations of their work. We also highlight some connections with the work of Fu and Tang.



Extensions and variations of Andrews-Merca identities

Key words
: partition; bijection; generating function.

MSC 2010
: 05A15, 05A17, 05A19, 05A30, 11P81.

1
Introduction

A partition of a positive integer
n
is a representation
\lambda=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\ldots+\lambda_{r}
where
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{r}\geq 1
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}\lambda_{i}=n
. A more compact notation for
\lambda
is
(\mu_{1}^{m_{1}},\mu_{2}^{m_{2}},\ldots)
where
\mu_{1}>\mu_{2}>\cdots
and
m_{i}
is the multiplicity of the summand
\mu_{i}
. For instance,
14+14+10+10+7+7+7+1+1+1+1
can be written as
(14^{2},10^{2},7^{3},1^{4})
. The summands in a partition are called
parts
.The union of two partitions
\lambda
and
\beta
is simply the multiset union
\lambda\cup\beta
where
\lambda
and
\beta
are treated as multisets.
Partition identities involving various classes of partitions have been studied. We recall a popular one: For a positive integer
t
, the number of partitions of
n
into parts appearing at most
t-1
times is equal to the number of partitions of
n
into parts not divisible by
t
. This identity is due to J. W. L. Glaisher
[
6
]
. Glaisher’s bijection established a one-to-one correspondence between the aforesaid classes of partitions. We shall let
\phi_{t}
denote Glaisher’s map, mapping the set of partitions into parts appearing at most
t-1
times to partitions into parts not divisible by
t
. For more on this map and partition identities, we refer the reader to
[
3
]
. The case of
t=2
has posed interesting questions and various studies conducted in many directions. Recently, Andrews and Merca
[
2
]
considered the partition functions:

a(n)
: the total number of even parts in all partitions of
n
into distinct parts;

c_{e}(n)
(resp.
c_{o}(n)
): the number of partitions in which exactly one part is repeated and this part is even (resp. odd);

b_{o}(n)
: the number of partitions of
n
into an odd number of parts in which the set of even parts has only one element;

b_{e}(n)
: the number of partitions of
n
into an even number of parts in which the set of even parts has only one element;

b(n)=b_{o}(n)-b_{e}(n)
and
c(n)=c_{o}(n)-c_{e}(n)
.

They obtained the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

For all
n\geq 1
,

a(n)=c(n)=(-1)^{n}b(n).

Note that, if
b^{\prime}(n)
denotes the number of partitions of
n
in which the set of even parts is singleton, then
b^{\prime}(n)=b_{o}(n)+b_{e}(n)
. Hence, one of the implications of the theorem is that

2\mid a(n)-b^{\prime}(n).

(1.1)

In this paper, we generalize the identitity
a(n)=c(n)
of Theorem
1.1
and also generalize (
1.1
). We then consider some variations. As a consequence, some connections with the work of Fu and Tang in
[
5
]
are highlighted and bijective proofs are provided in such cases.

We recall the
q
-series notation:

For
a,q\in\mathbb{C}
, we have
(a;q)_{n}=\prod\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{i})
for
n\geq 1
and
(a;q)_{0}=1
, and also for
|q|<1
,

(a;q)_{\infty}=\prod\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}(1-aq^{i}).

Below are some useful
q
-identities:

(q;q)_{\infty}=\sum_{j=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{\frac{j(3j-1)}{2}}=1+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}q^{\frac{j(3j\pm 1)}{2}},

(1.2)

(q;q)_{\infty}^{3}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{j}(2j+1)q^{\frac{j(j+1)}{2}}.

(1.3)

2
Generalizing the identity
a(n)=c(n)
of Theorem
1.1

We first observe the following.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
p
and
r
are non-negative integers such that
p\geq r+2
. Denote by
a_{r}(n,p)
the total number of parts congruent to
-r\pmod{p}
in partitions of
n
into distinct parts. Let
g_{r}(n,p)
denote the number of partitions in which exactly one part is repeated and the multiplicity of this repeated part is at least
p-r
and congruent to
-r,-r+1\pmod{p}
. Let
g_{r,o}(n,p)
(resp.
g_{r,e}(n,p)
) denote the number of
g_{r}(n,p)
-partitions in which the repeated part is odd (resp. even) and let
g_{r}(n,p)=g_{r,o}(n,p)-g_{r,e}(n,p)
. Then

a_{r}(n,p)=g_{r}(n,p).

It is important to realize that setting
p=2
and
r=0
yields the first equality in Andrews and Merca’s theorem, Theorem
1.1
.

Proof.

Let
F(z,q)
be the multivariable generating function for
g_{r}(n,p)
in which
z
tracks the repeated part. Then

\displaystyle F(z,q)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{n}(q^{(p-r)n}+q^{(p-r+p)n}+q^{(p-r+2p)n}+\cdots+q^{(p-r+1)n}+q^{(p-r+1+p)n}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+q^{(p-r+1+2p)n}+\cdots)\prod\limits_{j\neq n,j=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{j})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{n}(q^{(p-r)n}(1+q^{pn}+q^{2pn}+q^{3pn}+\cdots)+q^{(p-r+1)}(1+q^{pn}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+q^{2pn}+q^{3pn}+\cdots))\prod\limits_{j\neq n,j=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{j})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{n}q^{(p-r)n}(1+q^{n})(1+q^{pn}+q^{2pn}+q^{3pn}+\cdots)\prod\limits_{j\neq n,j=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{j})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}q^{(p-r)n}(1+q^{n})}{1-q^{pn}}\prod\limits_{j\neq n,j=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{j})

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}q^{(p-r)n}}{1-q^{pn}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{j})

\displaystyle=(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}q^{(p-r)n}}{1-q^{pn}}.

Using the fact that
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(g_{r,o}(n,p)-g_{r,e}(n,p))q^{n}=-F(-1,q),
we have

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{r}(n,p)q^{n}

\displaystyle=-F(-1,q)

\displaystyle=-(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{(p-r)n}}{1-q^{pn}}

\displaystyle=-(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{-rn}\frac{q^{pn}}{1-q^{pn}}

\displaystyle=-(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{-rn}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}q^{pnm}

\displaystyle=-(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{-rn}q^{pnm}

\displaystyle=-(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(-q^{-r+pm})^{n}

\displaystyle=-(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{-q^{-r+pm}}{1+q^{pm-r}}

\displaystyle=(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{-r+pm}}{1+q^{pm-r}}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{r}(n,p)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\Big|_{z=1}\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+zq^{pn-r})\prod\limits_{i=1,i\neq r}^{p}\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{pn-i})

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{i=1,i\neq r}^{p}\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{pn-i})\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+zq^{pn-r})\Big|_{z=1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\Big|_{z=1}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\log(1+zq^{pn-r})

\displaystyle=\prod\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{n})\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{pn-r}}{1+q^{pn-r}}

\displaystyle=(-q;q)_{\infty}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{-r+pn}}{1+q^{pn-r}}.

Thus

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{r}(n,p)q^{n}=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{r}(n,p)q^{n}

and the theorem follows.
∎

The above theorem provides an a new combinatorial interpretation of the total number of parts congruent to
-r
modulo
p
in partitions of
n
into distinct parts.

Recall the function
O_{p,j}(n)
in
[
5
]
. For
j=1
, we shall denote this function by
o_{p}(n)
which counts partitions of
n
in which the set of parts congruent to 0 modulo
p
is singleton. Define
a(n,p)
to be the total number of parts congruent to 0 modulo
p
in all partitions of
n
into parts appearing at most
p-1
times. Clearly
a(n,2)=a(n)=a_{0}(n,2)
and
o_{2}(n)=b^{\prime}(n)
. We have the following property which extends (
1.1
).

Theorem 2.2
.

For all
n\geq 0
,

p\mid a(n,p)-o_{p}(n).

Proof.

The generating function for
o_{p}(n)
is given by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}o_{p}(n)q^{n}=\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{pn}}{1-q^{pn}}

and the generating function for
a(n,p)
is given by Herden, Sepanski, et al.
[
4
]

\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{pn}+2q^{2pn}+3q^{3pn}+\ldots(p-1)q^{(p-1)pn}}{1+q^{pn}+q^{2pn}+\ldots+q^{(p-1)pn}}.

Recall the identity:

\sum_{k=1}^{n}kx^{k}=\frac{x(1-x^{n+1})-(n+1)(1-x)x^{n+1}}{(1-x)^{2}}.

(2.1)

Manipulating the generating function, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n,p)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{p-1}jq^{pnj}}{\frac{1-q^{p^{2}n}}{1-q^{pn}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\frac{q^{pn}(1-q^{p^{2}n})-p(1-q^{pn})q^{p^{2}n}}{(1-q^{pn})^{2}}}{\frac{1-q^{p^{2}n}}{1-q^{pn}}}\,\,\,\,(\text{by \eqref{form} with}\,\,\,n=p-1,x=q^{pn})

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-q^{pn}}{1-q^{p^{2}n}}\,\,\frac{q^{pn}(1-q^{p^{2}n})-p(1-q^{pn})q^{p^{2}n}}{(1-q^{pn})^{2}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-q^{pn}}{1-q^{p^{2}n}}\,\,\frac{q^{pn}(1-q^{p^{2}n})}{(1-q^{pn})^{2}}\pmod{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{pn}}{1-q^{pn}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}o_{p}(n)q^{n}.

∎

Denoting by
s(n)
the number of (unrestricted)partitions of
n
and letting
f(q)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}-p\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{pn}}{1-q^{pn}}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n,p)q^{n}

\displaystyle=(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}\left(\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{pn}}{1-q^{pn}}-p\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{p^{2}n}}{1-q^{p^{2}n}}\right)\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}

\displaystyle=(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}f(q^{p})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}s(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}q^{j}(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}f(q^{p})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}s(pn+j)q^{pn}

so that

\sum\limits_{j=0}^{p-1}q^{j}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(pn+j,p)q^{pn}=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}q^{j}(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}f(q^{p})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}s(pn+j)q^{pn}

which implies that, for a fixed
0\leq j\leq p-1
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(pn+j,p)q^{pn+j}=(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}f(q^{p})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}s(pn+j)q^{pn+j}

so that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(pn+j,p)q^{pn}=(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}f(q^{p})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}s(pn+j)q^{pn}.

Replacing
q
with
q^{1/p}
yields

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(pn+j,p)q^{n}=(q;q)_{\infty}f(q)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}s(pn+j)q^{n}.

Thus, for
n\geq 0
,

s(pn+j)\equiv 0\pmod{p}\,\,\Rightarrow\,\,a(pn+j,p)\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

In particular, applying the Ramanujan’s partition congruences:
s(5n+4)\equiv 0\pmod{5}
,
s(7n+5)\equiv 0\pmod{7}
and
s(11n+6)\equiv 0\pmod{11}
, we have the following result.

Theorem 2.3
.

For all
n\geq 0
,

a(5n+4,5)\equiv 0\pmod{5},

a(7n+5,7)\equiv 0\pmod{7}

and

a(11n+6,11)\equiv 0\pmod{11}.

Let
o_{p,e}(n)
(resp.
o_{p,o}(n)
) be the number of
o_{p}(n)
-partitions in which the number of parts congruent to 0 modulo
p
is even (resp. odd) and

H(z,q)=\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{zq^{pn}}{1-zq^{pn}}.

Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}o_{p,o}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(H(1,q)-H(-1,q)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{2(q;q)_{\infty}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{pn}}{1-q^{pn}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{pn}}{1+q^{pn}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{pn}}{1-q^{2pn}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q^{pn}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}q^{2pnm}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}q^{(p+2pm)n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{p+2pm}}{1-q^{2pm+p}}

(2.2)

and similarly,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}o_{p,e}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(H(1,q)+H(-1,q)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2pn}}{1-q^{2pn}}.

(2.3)

Thus, if for
i=0,p
,
h_{i}(n,p)
is the number of partitions in which parts are congruent to
1,2,3,\ldots,p-1
modulo
p
or congruent to
i
modulo
2p
and the set of parts
\equiv i\pmod{2p}
is singleton, then

o_{p,o}(n)=h_{p}(n,p)\,\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,o_{p,e}(n)=h_{0}(n,p).

3
Further variations

Consider the following partition functions:

d_{e}(n)
: the number of partitions of
n
in which exactly one even part is repeated and odd parts are unrestricted.

d_{o}(n)
: the number of partitions of
n
in which exactly one odd part is repeated and even parts are unrestricted.

For
i=0,2
,
f_{i}(n)
: the number of partitions of
n
in which the set of parts congruent to
i\pmod{4}
is singleton.
Then we have the following theorem:

Theorem 3.1
.

For
n\geq 1
,

d_{e}(n)=f_{0}(n)\,\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\,\,d_{o}(n)=f_{2}(n).

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{e}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2n+2n}}{1-q^{2n}}\prod\limits_{j\neq n,j=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{2j})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{4n}(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(1-q^{2n})(1+q^{2n})}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{4n}}{1-q^{4n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q;q^{2})_{\infty}(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{4n}}{1-q^{4n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{4n}}{1-q^{4n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q;q^{4})_{\infty}(q^{2};q^{4})_{\infty}(q^{3};q^{4})_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(q^{4n}+q^{4n+4n}+q^{4n+4n+4n}+\ldots)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{0}(n)q^{n}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{o}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2n-1+2n-1}}{1-q^{2n-1}}\prod\limits_{j\neq n,j=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{2j-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{4n-2}(-q;q^{2})_{\infty}}{(1-q^{2n-1})(1+q^{2n-1})}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-q;q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{4n-2}}{1-q^{4n-2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-q;q^{2})_{\infty}(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}{(q^{2};q^{2})_{\infty}(q;q^{2})_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{4n-2}}{1-q^{4n-2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{2};q^{4})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{4n-2}}{1-q^{4n-2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(q;q^{4})_{\infty}(q^{3};q^{4})_{\infty}(q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(q^{4n-2}+q^{2(4n-2)}+q^{3(4n-2)}+\ldots)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{2}(n)q^{n}.

∎

We also note the following bijective proof which is uniform for both identities in the theorem.

The bijection

Let
\lambda=\left(\lambda_{1}^{m_{1}},\lambda_{2}^{m_{2}},\ldots,\lambda_{l}^{m_{l}}\right)
be a partition enumerated by
f_{2}(n)/f_{0}(n)
. Then

\lambda_{i}^{m_{i}}\mapsto\begin{cases}\left(\dfrac{\lambda_{i}}{2}\right)^{2m_{i}},\quad\lambda_{i}\equiv 0,2\pmod{4};\\
\\
\phi_{2}^{-1}(\lambda_{i}^{m_{i}}),\quad\lambda_{i}\equiv 1,3\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

To invert the mapping, let
\mu=\left(\mu_{1}^{s_{1}},\mu_{2}^{s_{2}},\ldots,\mu_{l}^{s_{l}}\right)
be a partition enumerated by
d_{o}(n)/d_{e}(n)
. Then

\mu_{i}^{s_{i}}\mapsto(2\mu_{i})^{\lfloor\frac{s_{i}}{2}\rfloor}\cup\phi_{2}(\mu_{i}^{s_{i}-2\lfloor\frac{s_{i}}{2}\rfloor}).

Recall that

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{e}(n)q^{n}=\frac{(q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{4n}}{1-q^{4n}}

so that

(q;q)_{\infty}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{e}(n)q^{n}=(q^{4};q^{4})_{\infty}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{4n}}{1-q^{4n}}.

(3.1)

Using (
1.2
), we have

\displaystyle(q;q)_{\infty}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{e}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{\frac{n(3n\pm 1)}{2}}\right)\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{e}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}c_{k}d_{e}(n-k)q^{n}

(3.2)

where

c_{k}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{j},&\text{ if $k=\frac{j(3j\pm 1)}{2},j\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}$};\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Note that the right-hand side of (
3
) is equal to

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[d_{e}(n)+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{1+\sqrt{24n+1}}{6}\rfloor}(-1)^{j}\left(d_{e}\left(n-\frac{j(3j+1)}{2}\right)+d_{e}\left(n-\frac{j(3j-1)}{2}\right)\right)\right]q^{n}.

Invoking (
1.2
) and the identity

\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n}}{1-q^{n}}=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{0}(n)q^{n}

where
\sigma_{0}(n)
is the number of positive divisors of
n
, (
3.1
) becomes

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{n(3n+1)/2}\cdot\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{e}(n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{2n(3n+1)}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma_{0}(n)q^{4n}.

Hence, we have the following.

Corollary 3.1
.

For all integers
r\geq 0
, let

A(r)=\{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}:2j(3j+1)\equiv r\pmod{4}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,j\leq\left\lfloor\frac{\sqrt{6r+1}-1}{6}\right\rfloor\}

and

B(r)=\{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}:2j(3j-1)\equiv r\pmod{4}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,j\leq\left\lfloor\frac{1+\sqrt{6r+1}}{6}\right\rfloor\}.

Then for a positive integer
n
,

d_{e}(n)=\begin{cases}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{1+\sqrt{24n+1}}{6}\rfloor}(-1)^{j+1}\left(d_{e}\left(n-\frac{j(3j+1)}{2}\right)+d_{e}\left(n-\frac{j(3j-1)}{2}\right)\right)&\text{if $n\not\equiv 0\pmod{4}$}\\
\\
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{1+\sqrt{24n+1}}{6}\rfloor}(-1)^{j+1}\left(d_{e}\left(n-\frac{j(3j+1)}{2}\right)+d_{e}\left(n-\frac{j(3j-1)}{2}\right)\right)+\gamma(n)&\text{if $n\equiv 0\pmod{4}$}\end{cases}

where

\gamma(n)=\sigma_{0}(n/4)+\sum_{j\in A(n)}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{0}\left(\frac{n-2j(3j+1)}{4}\right)+\sum_{j\in B(n)}(-1)^{j}\sigma_{0}\left(\frac{n-2j(3j-1)}{4}\right).

In this case, we have a relationship between
d_{e}(n)
and
\sigma_{0}(n)
.

Example 3.1
.

Consider
n=8
.

Since
8\equiv 0\pmod{4}
, we have

\displaystyle d_{e}(8)

\displaystyle=d_{e}(8-2)+d_{e}(8-1)-d_{e}(8-7)-d_{e}(8-5)+\gamma(8)

\displaystyle=d_{e}(6)+d_{e}(7)-d_{e}(1)-d_{e}(3)+\gamma(8)

4
Generalizations

The partition function
d_{o}(n)
can be generalized as follows:
Let
k,p\geq 2
and
0\leq r<p
be integers and define
d_{p}(n,k,r)
as the number of partitions of
n
in which exactly one part
\equiv r\pmod{p}
appears at least
k
times. In this definition, parts not congruent to
r\pmod{p}
appear with unrestricted multiplicity. Similary, let
f_{p}(n,k,r)
denote the number of partitions of
n
in which parts
\equiv kr\pmod{pk}
form a singleton set.

If
r\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{p}(n,k,r)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{r};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{k(pn+r)}}{1-q^{pn+r}}\prod\limits_{j\neq n,j=0}^{\infty}(1+q^{pj+r}+q^{2(pj+r)}+\cdots+q^{(k-1)(pj+r)})

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{r};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{k(pn+r)}}{(1-q^{pn+r})\sum\limits_{i=0}^{k-1}q^{(pn+r)i}}\prod\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1-q^{k(pj+r)}}{1-q^{pj+r}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{r};q^{p})_{\infty}(q^{kr};q^{kp})_{\infty}}{(q^{r};q^{p})_{\infty}(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{pkn+kr}}{1-q^{pkn+kr}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{kr};q^{kp})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{pkn+kr}}{1-q^{pkn+kr}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{p}(n,k,r)q^{n}.

If
r=0
, then

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{p}(n,k,r)q^{n}=\frac{(q^{r};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{k(pn+r)}}{1-q^{pn+r}}\prod\limits_{j\neq n,j=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{pj+r}+q^{2(pj+r)}+\cdots+q^{(k-1)(pj+r)})

which simplifies to

\frac{(q^{kr};q^{kp})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{pkn+kr}}{1-q^{pkn+kr}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}f_{p}(n,k,r)q^{n}.

Thus, we have:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
p\geq 2
. For an integer
n\geq 1
,

f_{p}(n,k,r)=d_{p}(n,k,r).

A bijection (which extends the one for
d_{e}(n)/f_{0}(n)
and
d_{o}(n)/f_{2}(n)
), is given as follows:

Let
\lambda=\left(\lambda_{1}^{m_{1}},\lambda_{2}^{m_{2}},\ldots,\lambda_{l}^{m_{l}}\right)
be a partition enumerated by
f_{p}(n,k,r)
. Then

\lambda_{i}^{m_{i}}\mapsto\begin{cases}\left(\dfrac{\lambda_{i}}{k}\right)^{km_{i}},&\text{if $\lambda_{i}\equiv 0,k,2k,\ldots,(p-1)k\pmod{pk}$};\\
\\
\phi_{k}^{-1}(\lambda_{i}^{m_{i}}),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

From
\lambda
, note that there is only one part congruent to
kr\pmod{pk}
(with unrestricted multiplicity). Under the bijection, this part is mapped to the exactly one part which is congruent to
r\pmod{p}
repeated at least
k
times. For other parts of
\lambda
that are not congruent to
kr\pmod{kp}
, there are two cases.

Case 1: If the part is divisible by
k
, it can be shown that such a part must be congruent to
jk\pmod{pk}
where
j\in\{0,1,2,\ldots,p-1\}\setminus\{r\}
. This part is mapped to a part not congruent to
r\pmod{p}
with multiplicity at least
k
.

Case 2: If that part of
\lambda
is not divisible by
k
, we apply
\phi_{k}^{-1}
.

Conversely, let
\mu=\left(\mu_{1}^{s_{1}},\mu_{2}^{s_{2}},\ldots,\mu_{l}^{s_{l}}\right)
be a partition enumerated by
d_{p}(n,k,r)
. Then

\mu_{i}^{s_{i}}\mapsto(k\mu_{i})^{\lfloor\frac{s_{i}}{k}\rfloor}\cup\phi_{k}(\mu_{i}^{s_{i}-k\lfloor\frac{s_{i}}{k}\rfloor})

(4.1)

represents the inverse mapping.

4.2
A slightly different partition function

For
\alpha\geq k
, let
g(n,\alpha,k,p)
denote the number of partitions of
n
in which only one part appears at least
\alpha
times and its multiplicity is congruent to
\alpha+j
modulo
p
where
0\leq j\leq k-1
and all other parts appear at most
k-1
times. Denote by
g_{o}(n,\alpha,k,p)
(resp.
g_{e}(n,\alpha,k,p)
the number of
g(n,\alpha,k,p)
-partitions in which the part repeated at least
\alpha
times is odd (resp. even). Then we have:

Theorem 4.3
.

\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{o}(n,\alpha,k,p)-g_{e}(n,\alpha,k,p)q^{n}=\frac{(q^{k};q^{k})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{pn+\alpha}}{1+q^{pn+\alpha}}.

(4.4)

Proof.

If
z
tracks the part repeated at least
\alpha
times, observe that

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g(n,\alpha,k,p)q^{n}z^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(z^{n}q^{\alpha n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+2p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+3p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+4p)n}+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\cdots+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+1+p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+1+2p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+1+3p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+1+4p)n}+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\cdots+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+2+p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+2+2p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+2+3p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+2+4p)n}+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\cdots+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+3+p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+3+2p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+3+3p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+3+4p)n}+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\vdots\right.

\displaystyle\left.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\cdots+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+k-2+p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+k-2+2p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+k-2+3p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+k-2+4p)n}+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\cdots+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+k-1+p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+k-1+2p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+k-1+3p)n}+z^{n}q^{(\alpha+k-1+4p)n}+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\cdots\right)\prod_{j=1,j\neq n}^{\infty}(1+q^{j}+q^{2j}+q^{3j}+\cdots+q^{(k-1)j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{n}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}q^{(\alpha+i+mp)n}\prod_{j=1,j\neq n}^{\infty}(1+q^{j}+q^{2j}+q^{3j}+\cdots+q^{(k-1)j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}z^{n}q^{\alpha n}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}q^{in}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}q^{mpn}\prod_{j=1,j\neq n}^{\infty}(1+q^{j}+q^{2j}+q^{3j}+\cdots+q^{(k-1)j})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}q^{\alpha n}}{1-q^{pn}}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}q^{in}\prod_{j=1,j\neq n}^{\infty}(1+q^{j}+q^{2j}+q^{3j}+\cdots+q^{(k-1)j}).

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}q^{\alpha n}}{1-q^{pn}}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1+q^{j}+q^{2j}+q^{3j}+\cdots+q^{(k-1)j}).

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}q^{\alpha n}}{1-q^{pn}}\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1-q^{kj}}{1-q^{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{k};q^{k})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}q^{\alpha n}}{1-q^{pn}}

so that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}(g_{o}(n,\alpha,k,p)-g_{e}(n,\alpha,k,p))q^{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(q^{k};q^{k})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}q^{\alpha n}}{1-q^{pn}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(q^{k};q^{k})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{\alpha n}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}q^{pnm}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(q^{k};q^{k})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-q^{\alpha+pm})^{n}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(q^{k};q^{k})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{-q^{\alpha+pm}}{1+q^{\alpha+pm}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{k};q^{k})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{\alpha+pm}}{1+q^{\alpha+pm}}.

∎

We have the following relationship between
h(n,p)
and
g(n,p,p,p)
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For all
n\geq 0
,

g_{o}(n,p,p,p)=h_{0}(n,p)\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,g_{e}(n,p,p,p)=h_{p}(n,p).

Proof.

From the fact that

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g(n,p,p,p)q^{n}z^{m}=\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}q^{pn}}{1-q^{pn}},

recall that
z
tracks the part which is repeated at least
p
times. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{o}(n,p,p,p)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=1,n\equiv 1\pmod{2}}^{\infty}\frac{q^{pn}}{1-q^{pn}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2pn-p}}{1-q^{2pn-p}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_{0}(n,p)q^{n}

and on the same note,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{e}(n,p,p,p)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=1,n\equiv 0\pmod{2}}^{\infty}\frac{q^{pn}}{1-q^{pn}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q^{p};q^{p})_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{q^{2pn}}{1-q^{2pn}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}h_{p}(n,p)q^{n}.

∎

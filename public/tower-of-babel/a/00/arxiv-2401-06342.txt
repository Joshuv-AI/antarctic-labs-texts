Dualizing complexes on the moduli of parabolic bundles
Linus Hamann, Naoki Imai

Abstract
For a non-archimedean local field F F and a connected reductive group G G over F F equipped with a parabolic subgroup P P , we show that the dualizing complex on Bun P \Bun_{P} , the moduli stack of P P -bundles on the Fargues–Fontaine curve, can be described explicitly in terms of the modulus character of P P . As applications, we identify various characters appearing in the theory of local and global Shimura varieties, show the Harris–Viehmann conjecture in the Hodge–Newton reducible case, and carry out some computations of the geometric Eisenstein functors for general parabolics.

In other words, the dualizing complex is the geometrization of the modulus character in the representation theory of

Using Proposition

, as in Lemma
4.25
, we consider
A\in D(L_{\theta}(F),\Lambda)
and write
w_{\theta*}(A)\in D(w_{\theta}(L)_{w_{\theta}(\theta)}(F),\Lambda)

for the image under the equivalence of categories induced by the isomorphism
L_{\theta}\cong w_{\theta}(L)_{w(\theta)}
. We have an isomorphism

\mathrm{nEis}_{P}^{G,\theta}(i^{\theta}_{!}(A))|_{\Bun_{G}^{b}}\cong i_{Q_{b,w_{\theta}}(F)}^{G_{b}(F)}(w_{\theta*}(A))\otimes\delta_{b}^{-1/2}[-\langle 2\rho_{G},\nu_{b}\rangle]

in
D(\Bun_{G}^{b})\cong D(G_{b}(F),\Lambda)
, where
Q_{b,w_{\theta}}
is as in Lemma
4.26
.
Moreover, if
\theta
is HN-dominant with respect to the Borel for which
P
is standard then this defines an isomorphism

\mathrm{nEis}_{P}^{G,\theta}(i^{\theta}_{!}(A))\cong i^{b}_{!}(i_{Q_{b,w_{\theta}}(F)}^{G_{b}(F)}(w_{\theta*}(A))\otimes\delta_{b}^{-1/2})[-\langle 2\rho_{G},\nu_{b}\rangle]

of objects in
D(\Bun_{G})
.

Proof.

The second claim follows immediately from the first by noting that, in the case that
\theta
is HN-dominant with respect to the Borel
B
for which
P
is standard then the space
\Bun_{P}^{\theta}
is a classifying stack given by the point defined by the split reduction quotiented out by the torsor. In particular, its topological image under
\mathfrak{p}_{P}
is just
b
.

For the first claim, we set
w:=w_{\theta}
. By applying Lemma
4.20
, we reduce to computing

\mathrm{nEis}_{w(P)}^{G,w(\theta)}(i^{w(\theta)}_{!}(w_{*}(A)).

We let
P^{b}
be the standard Levi subgroup with Levi factor
L^{b}
given by the centralizer of the slope homomorphism of
b
. We let
b_{L^{b}}\in B(L^{b})_{\mathrm{basic}}
be the canonical dominant reduction.

By definition of
w_{\theta}=w
, we have that
w(L)\subset L^{b}
. We let
P^{b,w}
be the unique parabolic subgroup whose Levi factor is equal to
L^{b}
and contains
w(P)
. In other words, the standard parabolic with Levi factor
L^{b}
and defined with respect to the Borel
w(B)
. The element
w(\theta)

maps under the natural composition
B(w(L))_{\mathrm{basic}}\cong\pi_{1}(w(L))_{\Gamma_{F}}\rightarrow\pi_{1}(L^{b})_{\Gamma_{F}}\cong B(L^{b})_{\mathrm{basic}}
to the dominant reduction
b_{L^{b}}
of
b\in B(G)_{L}
, using the second part of Lemma
4.25
.
Therefore it follows, by applying Lemma
4.19
, that we can rewrite this as

\mathrm{nEis}_{P^{b,w}}^{G,b_{L^{b}}}\circ\mathrm{nEis}_{w(P)\cap L^{b}}^{L^{b},w(\theta)}(i^{w(\theta)}_{!}(w_{*}(A))).

By Lemma
4.27
, this is isomorphic to

\mathrm{nEis}_{P^{b,w}}^{G,b_{L^{b}}}(i^{b_{L^{b}}}_{!}(i_{Q_{b,w}(F)}^{G_{b}(F)}(w_{*}(A))).

Therefore, we have reduced to showing the following.

Lemma 4.29
.

For all
A\in D(G_{b}(F),\Lambda)\cong D(\Bun_{L^{b}}^{b_{L^{b}}})
, we have an isomorphism

\mathrm{nEis}_{P^{b,w}}^{G,b_{L^{b}}}(i^{b_{L^{b}}}_{!}(A))|_{\Bun_{G}^{b}}\cong A\otimes\delta_{b}^{-1/2}[-\langle 2\rho_{G},\nu_{b}\rangle]

of objects in
D(G_{b}(F),\Lambda)\cong D(\Bun_{G}^{b})
.

Proof.

Let
U^{b}
,
U^{b,-}
,
U^{b,w}
denote the unipotent radical of
P^{b}
,
P^{b,-}
,
P^{b,w}
, respectively.

We write
\Bun_{P^{b,w}}^{\theta,b}
for the preimage of
\Bun_{G}^{b}\cong[\ast/\widetilde{G}_{b}]
along the map
\Bun_{P^{b,w}}^{\theta}\rightarrow\Bun_{G}
induced by
\mathfrak{p}_{P^{b,w}}
. Two applications of proper base change tell us that we are tasked with computing

\mathfrak{p}_{!}\mathfrak{q}^{*}(A\otimes\delta_{P^{b,w},b_{L^{b}}}^{1/2})[\langle\xi_{P^{b,w}},\overline{b}_{L^{b}}\rangle],

with maps as in the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Bun_{P^{b,w}}^{b_{L^{b}},b}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left[\ast/\widetilde{G}_{b}\right]\cong\Bun_{G}^{b}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Bun_{L^{b}}^{b_{L^{b}}}\cong\left[\ast/\underline{G_{b}(F)}\right].}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{p}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{q}}

We look at
\Bun_{P^{b,w},\ast}^{b_{L^{b}},b}
, the fiber of
\mathfrak{q}
over
\ast\rightarrow[\ast/\underline{G_{b}(F)}]
. This defines a closed subspace of
\Bun_{P^{b,w},\ast}^{b_{L^{b}}}
, where, by Lemma
3.7
, the latter is an iterated fibration of the Picard
v
-groupoids
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{L}\mathrm{ie}(U^{b,w})_{\alpha,b_{L^{b}}}[1])\rightarrow\ast
for all roots
\alpha
occurring in
\Lie(U^{b,w})
. In particular,

\Bun_{P^{b,w},*}^{b_{L^{b}}}\cong X_{n},

where
X_{n}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow X_{0}=\ast
is a sequence of fibrations
f_{i}\colon X_{i}\rightarrow X_{i-1}
in the spaces
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{L}\mathrm{ie}(U^{b,w})_{\alpha_{i-1},b_{L^{b}}}[1])
, where
\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{n-1}
are the roots occurring in
\Lie(U^{b,w})
.
Moreover, there is a filtration

U^{b,w}=U_{0}\supset U_{1}\supset\cdots\supset U_{n}=\{1\}

of the unipotent radical of
P^{b,w}

stable under the conjugation action of
P^{b,w}

such that
X_{i}(S)
parametrizes the torsors under
\mathcal{E}_{b_{L^{b}},S}^{\mathrm{alg}}\times^{L^{b}}(U_{0}/U_{i})
for
S\in\AffPerf_{k}
by the proof of Lemma
3.7
.
Let
Y_{i}
be the closed substack of
X_{i}
corresponding to the trivial torsor.
Then we redefine
Y_{i}=f_{i}^{-1}(Y_{i-1})
if
\langle\alpha_{i},\overline{b}_{L^{b}}\rangle\leq 0
and
Y_{i}\cong Y_{i-1}
if
\langle\alpha_{i},\overline{b}_{L^{b}}\rangle>0
. The closed substack
Y_{n}\hookrightarrow X_{n}\cong\Bun_{P^{b,w},*}^{b_{L^{b}}}
then identifies with
\Bun_{P^{b,w},\ast}^{b_{L^{b}},b}
.

It follows that
\Bun_{P^{b,w}}^{b_{L^{b}},b}\cong[\ast/\widetilde{G}_{w}]
is a classifying stack attached to a group diamond
\widetilde{G}_{w}
. The group diamond
\widetilde{G}_{w}
is an iterated fibration of
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{L}\mathrm{ie}(U^{b,w})_{\alpha,b_{L^{b}}})
over
\underline{G_{b}(F)}
, for
\alpha
a root occurring in
U^{b,w}
such that
\langle\alpha,\overline{b}_{L^{b}}\rangle\leq 0
.
We get an induced map of classifying stacks
[\ast/\widetilde{G}_{w}]\rightarrow[\ast/\widetilde{G}_{b}]
that will be induced by a natural inclusion of group diamonds
\widetilde{G}_{w}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{G}_{b}
. We write
\widetilde{G}^{w}
for the quotient of
\widetilde{G}_{b}
by
\widetilde{G}_{w}
.
In light of the isomorphism
\Bun_{P^{b,-}}^{b_{L^{b}}}\cong[\ast/\widetilde{G}_{b}]
, as in the proof of Proposition
3.15
, we can write
\widetilde{G}_{b}
as an iterated fibration of
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{L}\mathrm{ie}(U^{b,-})_{\alpha,b_{L^{b}}})
for all roots
\alpha
occurring in
\Lie(U^{b,-})
over
\underline{G_{b}(F)}
.
It follows that
\widetilde{G}^{w}
is an iterated fibration of
\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{L}\mathrm{ie}(U^{b,-})_{\alpha,b_{L^{b}}})
for all negative roots
\alpha<0
such that
w^{-1}(\alpha)<0
.
This sits in a Cartesian diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{G}^{w}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ast}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left[\ast/\widetilde{G}_{w}\right]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\left[\ast/\widetilde{G}_{b}\right].}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widetilde{\mathfrak{p}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{p}}

We claim we can now reduce to the following.

Lemma 4.30
.

The compactly supported cohomology of
\widetilde{G}^{w}
is isomorphic to
\delta_{P^{b,w},b_{L^{b}}}^{-1/2}\otimes\delta_{b}^{-1/2}[-2(\langle\xi_{P^{b}},\nu_{b}\rangle-\langle\xi_{P^{b}}^{w},\nu_{b}\rangle)]

as a
G_{b}(F)
-representation,
where
\xi_{P^{b}}^{w}
is the sum of positive roots
\alpha>0
occurring in
\Lie(U_{b})
such that
w^{-1}(\alpha)<0
.

We note that we have a chain of equalities:
d_{P^{b,w},b_{L^{b}}}=\langle\xi_{P^{b,w}},\overline{b}_{L^{b}}\rangle=\langle\xi_{P^{b,w}},\nu_{b_{L^{b}}}\rangle=\langle\xi_{P^{b,w}},\nu_{b}\rangle
,
by definition of
b_{L^{b}}
. Since
\nu_{b}
is dominant and
P^{b}
is a standard parabolic, we see that this is equal to

\langle\xi_{P^{b}},\nu_{b}\rangle-2\langle\xi_{P^{b}}^{w},\nu_{b}\rangle.

We deduce the following chain of isomorphisms:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{!}\mathfrak{q}^{*}(A\otimes\delta_{P^{b,w},b_{L^{b}}}^{1/2})[d_{P^{b,w},b_{L^{b}}}]

\displaystyle\cong\mathfrak{p}_{!}\mathfrak{q}^{*}(A\otimes\delta_{P^{b,w},b_{L^{b}}}^{1/2})[\langle\xi_{P^{b}},\nu_{b}\rangle-2\langle\xi_{P^{b}}^{w},\nu_{b}\rangle]

\displaystyle\cong\mathfrak{p}_{!}\mathfrak{p}^{*}(A\otimes\delta_{P^{b,w},b_{L^{b}}}^{1/2})[\langle\xi_{P^{b}},\nu_{b}\rangle-2\langle\xi_{P^{b}}^{w},\nu_{b}\rangle]

\displaystyle\cong A\otimes\delta_{P^{b,w},b_{L^{b}}}^{1/2}\otimes\mathfrak{p}_{!}(\Lambda)[\langle\xi_{P^{b}},\nu_{b}\rangle-2\langle\xi_{P^{b}}^{w},\nu_{b}\rangle]

\displaystyle\cong A\otimes\delta_{P^{b,w},b_{L^{b}}}^{1/2}\otimes\delta_{b}^{-1/2}\otimes\delta_{P^{b,w},b_{L^{b}}}^{-1/2}[-\langle\xi_{P^{b}},\nu_{b}\rangle]

\displaystyle\cong A\otimes\delta_{b}^{-1/2}[-\langle\xi_{P^{b}},\nu_{b}\rangle].

For the second isomorphism, we have used that
\mathfrak{q}
can be identified with the composition
p_{b}\circ\mathfrak{p}
, where
p_{b}\colon[\ast/\widetilde{G}_{b}]\rightarrow[\ast/\underline{G_{b}(F)}]

is the natural map. For the third isomorphism, we have used a projection formula. We have used Lemma
4.30
in the fourth isomorphism. We now note that
-\langle\xi_{P^{b}},\nu_{b}\rangle=-\langle 2\rho_{G},\nu_{b}\rangle
, by virtue of the fact that
P^{b}
is a standard parabolic and that
\nu_{b}
pairs trivially with all roots of
G
lying in its centralizer
L^{b}
. This shows the desired claim.
∎

This completes the proof of Theorem
4.28
.

Proof of Lemma
4.30
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathrm{Lie}(G)
. Note that
\delta_{b}
identifies with the modulus character of
P^{b}
transferred to
(L^{b})_{b_{L^{b}}}
along the inner twisting,
since the group is quasi-split. Therefore, we have that
\delta_{P^{b,w},b_{L^{b}}}^{-1/2}\otimes\delta_{b}^{-1/2}
is the unique rational cocharacter of
G_{b}(F)

such that, for
t\in A_{G_{b}}(F)
,
we obtain that

\delta_{P^{b,w},b_{L^{b}}}^{-1/2}\otimes\delta_{b}^{-1/2}(t)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha>0\\
w^{-1}(\alpha)>0\end{subarray}}|\mathrm{det}(t|\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha})|^{-1},

as in the equality
\xi_{P^{b}}+\xi_{P^{b,w}}=2(\xi_{P}-\xi_{P_{b}}^{w})

or alternatively
\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha<0\\

We also note that

The claim now follows from the previous description of

\mathcal{H}^{0}(\mathcal{L}\mathrm{ie}(U^{b,-})_{\alpha,b_{L^{b}}})
for negative roots

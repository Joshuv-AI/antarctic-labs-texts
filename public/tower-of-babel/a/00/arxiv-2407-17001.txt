1.
Introduction

The path homology of digraphs, developed by Grigory’an-Lin-Muranov-Yau, and their coauthors in a series of papers
[
2
,
3
,
9
,
7
,
4
,
8
,
6
]
, has many parallels with the homology theory of spaces.
These include an analogue of the Künneth formula for the box product of digraphs and join of digraphs, a homotopy category of digraphs, and the path homology being a homotopy invariant.
Moreover, there is a notion of a fundamental group, whose abelianization coincides with the first path homology group over integers. Attempts have been made to build a consistent theory of homotopy groups
[
14
]
and to generalize this theory to various other structures, such as quivers
[
8
,
12
]
. This entire research direction is now known as GLMY theory. As Asao shows in
[
1
]
, the path homology is closely related to the magnitude homology. Based on this connection, Hepworth and Roff developed the bigraded path homology of digraphs
[
10
]
, which was also generalised to arbitrary quasimetric spaces in
[
13
]
.

As with spaces, for a digraph
G
and a field
\mathbb{F}
, one can construct a chain complex
\Omega_{\bullet}(G;\mathbb{F})
and a cochain complex
\Omega^{\bullet}(G;\mathbb{F})
, whose homology and cohomology are called path homology
\mathrm{PH}_{*}(G;\mathbb{F})
and path cohomology
\mathrm{PH}^{*}(G;\mathbb{F})
, respectively.
Furthermore,
\Omega^{\bullet}(G;\mathbb{F})
is a differential graded algebra (dg-algebra), and this structure turns the path cohomology into a graded algebra. However, unlike the case of spaces, the definitions of the complexes
\Omega_{\bullet}(G;\mathbb{F})
and
\Omega^{\bullet}(G;\mathbb{F})
are given in such a way that there is no explicit description of the basis of its components.

It is known that the dimension of
\Omega_{0}(G;\mathbb{F})
equals the number of vertices, the dimension of
\Omega_{1}(G;\mathbb{F})
equals the number of arrows, and the dimension of
\Omega_{2}(G;\mathbb{F})
equals the number of triangles plus the number of squares. A digraph
G
is said to have no multisquares if, for any pair of vertices
x,y
with directed distance two, there are at most two shortest paths from
x
to
y.
Grigory’an showed
[
5
, Theorem 2.10]
that if a digraph
G
does not have multisquares, a basis of
\Omega_{3}(G;\mathbb{F})
consists of special digraphs called trapezohedrons and their mapping images.
However, for general cases, the dimensions of
\Omega_{n}(G;\mathbb{F})
remain unclear.

In this paper, we describe a basis of
\Omega_{n}(G;\mathbb{F})
for
G
containing no multisquares and
n\geq 0
, which generalises the basis of
\Omega_{3}(G;\mathbb{F})
by Grigory’an. We start by analysing the identification of the dg-algebra
\Omega^{\bullet}(G;\mathbb{F})
as a quotient of the path algebra of
G
, as described in
[
11
, Theorem 5.4]
,

\Omega^{\bullet}(G;\mathbb{F})\cong\mathbb{F}G/T,

where
T
is the ideal defined by certain relations
t_{x,y}
; see also Theorem
2.2
.
When
G
does not have multisquares, the relations defining this quotient have a simpler form. This allows us to describe a basis for
\Omega^{n}(G;\mathbb{F})
, yielding the dual basis for
\Omega_{n}(G;\mathbb{F})
(see Corollary
4.4
and Theorem
4.7
).

To proceed, we construct an undirected graph
\mathcal{S}_{n}(G)
, whose vertices correspond to the
n
-paths in
G
(see Definition
3.1
).
The dimension of
\Omega_{n}(G;\mathbb{F})

can be calculated by counting the number of certain connected components of
\mathcal{S}_{n}(G)
. Notably, it depends on the characteristic of
\mathbb{F}
. For fields with characteristic two, the dimension of
\Omega_{n}(G;\mathbb{F})
may be greater than for fields of other characteristics.
When the characteristic is not two, an additional bipartite property is required for the components of
\mathcal{S}_{n}(G)
corresponding to the elements of the basis, unlike the case when the characteristic is two.
Basis elements of
\Omega_{n}(G;\mathbb{F})
are expressed as a certain linear sum of vertices from each of these components of
\mathcal{S}_{n}(G)
. Refer to Section
4
for a detailed description of the basis.

If the number of nontrivial path homology groups
\mathrm{PH}_{n}(G;\mathbb{F})
of a digraph
G
is finite, we define the
\mathbb{F}
-path Euler characteristic of
G
as the alternating sum

\chi^{\mathbb{F}}(G)=\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathrm{PH}_{n}(G;\mathbb{F})).

When the complex
\Omega_{\bullet}(G;\mathbb{F})
is bounded, this is equal to the alternating sum of the dimensions of
\Omega_{n}(G;\mathbb{F})
.
Therefore, our description of bases for
\Omega_{n}(G;\mathbb{F})
can be used to compute
\chi^{\mathbb{F}}(G)
of a digraph
G
that does not contain multisquares and whose associated chain complex
\Omega_{\bullet}(G;\mathbb{F})
is bounded.

Unlike spaces, the
\mathbb{F}
-path Euler characteristics can vary depending on the field
\mathbb{F}
. This demonstrates the differences between GLMY theory and the homology theory of spaces. To illustrate this, we provide an example of a digraph
\mathcal{G}
in Subsection
5.4
. This example highlights the fact that for certain digraphs, it is not possible to construct a space with homology isomorphic to the path homology for all coefficients.
As a side note, we discover that over integers, the associated path cochain complex
\Omega^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G};\mathbb{Z})
contains torsion elements.

To summarise, we prove the following theorem (see Theorem
5.4
and Proposition
5.3
).

Open questions.
In conclusion of the introduction, we present a list of open questions that continue this line of research.

•

For a digraph
G
and a field
\mathbb{F},
is the path cohomology algebra
\mathrm{PH}^{*}(G;\mathbb{F})
graded commutative?

•

Let
G
be a digraph and
p
be an odd prime such that
\Omega_{\bullet}(G;\mathbb{Q})
and
\Omega_{\bullet}(G;\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
are bounded chain complexes. Our results imply that, if
G
has no multisquares, we have

\chi^{\mathbb{Q}}(G)=\chi^{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}(G).

Does this property also hold for digraphs with multisquares?

•

For a digraph
G
without multisquares, are all nontrivial entries of the matrix representing the boundary map

2.
Path chains and cochains

Throughout the article, all digraphs are finite,
\mathbb{K}
denotes a commutative ring, and
\mathbb{F}
denotes a filed. Our terminology differs from that used in
[
2
]
and other works of Grigor’yan-Lin-Muranov-Yau, but our notations are the same. For example, by a
path
in
G
, we mean a sequence of vertices
p=(p_{0},\dots,p_{n})
such that
(p_{i},p_{i+1})
is an arrow for
0\leq i\leq n-1
. If we need to specify the length
n,
we call it an
n
-path, or a path of length
n.
Elements of
\Omega_{\bullet}(G)
are called path chains, and elements of
\Omega^{\bullet}(G)
are called path cochains.
We also use asterisks
H_{*}
,
H^{*}
for graded objects (without a differential) and bullet points
C_{\bullet}
,
C^{\bullet}
for (co)chain complexes and dg-algebras.

2.1.
Definition

We start with the definitions of
\Omega_{\bullet}(G)
and
\Omega^{\bullet}(G)
following Grigor’yan-Lin-Muranov-Yau
[
2
]
; see also
[
11
, §5]
. We will work over arbitrary commutative ring
\mathbb{K}
rather than a field. Later, when discussing duality between
\Omega_{\bullet}(G)
and
\Omega^{\bullet}(G),
we will assume that
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{F}
is a field.

Assume that
\mathbb{K}
is a commutative ring, and by a “module” we mean a
\mathbb{K}
-module. For a finite set
X
, denote by
\Lambda_{\bullet}(X)
the chain complex of modules, where the
n
-th component
\Lambda_{n}(X)
is a free module generated by formal elements
e_{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}}
indexed by tuples
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})\in X^{n+1}
. In other words, we have

(2.1)

e_{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}}\in\Lambda_{n}(X).

The differential on
\Lambda_{\bullet}(X)
is defined by the alternating sum

(2.2)

\partial(e_{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{n}e_{x_{0},\dots,\hat{x}_{i},\dots x_{n}}.

Denote by
\mathcal{R}_{\bullet}(X)
the quotient complex of
\Lambda_{\bullet}(X)
by the subcomplex whose components are spanned by tuples
e_{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}}
with consecutive repetitions, i.e.,
x_{i}=x_{i+1}
for some
0\leq i\leq n-1.

A reader familiar with simplicial methods may notice that
\Lambda_{\bullet}(X)
has an obvious structure of a simplicial module, and
\mathcal{R}_{\bullet}(X)
is the associated normalised complex.

For a digraph
G
, we set
\mathcal{R}_{\bullet}(G)=\mathcal{R}_{\bullet}(V(G)),
where
V(G)
is the set of vertices. We denote by
\mathcal{A}_{n}(G)\subseteq\mathcal{R}_{n}(G)
the submodule generated by all paths of length
n.
So
\mathcal{A}_{n}(G)
consists of elements that can be written as linear combinations

(2.3)

\sum_{p}\alpha_{p}\cdot e_{p}\in\mathcal{A}_{n}(G),

where
p
runs over all
n
-paths and
\alpha_{p}\in\mathbb{K}.

Then
\Omega_{\bullet}(G)
is defined as the largest subcomplex of
\mathcal{R}_{\bullet}(G)
that is contained in
\mathcal{A}_{*}(G).

The
n
-th component
\Omega_{n}(G)
can be also defined by the following formula

(2.4)

\Omega_{n}(G)=\mathcal{A}_{n}(G)\cap\partial^{-1}(\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(G)).

We call
\Omega_{\bullet}(G)
the path chain complex and call an element in
\Omega_{\bullet}(G)
a path chain. If we need to specify the ring
\mathbb{K}
, we write
\Omega_{\bullet}(G;\mathbb{K}).

Dually, the cochain complex
\Lambda^{\bullet}(X)
is given by

\Lambda^{\bullet}(X)=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{K}}(\Lambda_{\bullet}(X),\mathbb{K}).

The elements
e^{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}}\in\Lambda^{n}(X)
denote the dual basis of
e_{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}}
in
\Lambda_{n}(X)
indexed by tuples
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
. Then the differential on
\Lambda^{\bullet}(X)
follows by the formula

(2.5)

\partial(e^{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}})=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\sum_{v\in X}(-1)^{i}e^{x_{0},\dots,x_{i-1},v,x_{i},\dots,x_{n}}.

We turn
\Lambda^{\bullet}(X)
into a dg-algebra by letting

(2.6)

e^{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}}\cdot e^{y_{0},\dots,y_{m}}=\begin{cases}e^{x_{0},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}},&y_{0}=x_{n}\\
0,&\text{else}.\end{cases}

Consider the dg-subalgebra
\mathcal{R}^{\bullet}(X)\subseteq\Lambda^{\bullet}(X),
where
\mathcal{R}^{n}(X)
is spanned by the elements
e^{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}}
such that
x_{i}\neq x_{i+1}
for all
0\leq i\leq n-1
.
A reader familiar with simplicial methods may note that
\Lambda^{\bullet}(X)
has an obvious structure of a cosimplicial algebra, where the codegeneracy map
\sigma^{i}:\Lambda^{n}(X)\to\Lambda^{n-1}(X)
is trivial on
e^{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}}
if
x_{i}\neq x_{i+1}
and
\sigma^{i}(e^{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}})=e^{x_{0},\dots,\hat{x}_{i},\dots,x_{n}}
if
x_{i}=x_{i+1}.

Then
\mathcal{R}^{\bullet}(X)
is the associated normalised dg-algebra.

For a digraph
G
, let us define the dg-algebra
\Omega^{\bullet}(G)
of path cochains.
First we set
\mathcal{R}^{\bullet}(G)=\mathcal{R}^{\bullet}(V(G)).

Denote by
\mathcal{N}^{n}(G)\subseteq\mathcal{R}^{n}(G)
the subspace spanned by elements
e^{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}},
where
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
is not a path of
G.
Then
\mathcal{N}^{*}(G)
is a graded ideal of
\mathcal{R}^{\bullet}(G)
but not necessarily a dg-ideal. Let
\mathcal{J}^{\bullet}(G)\triangleleft\mathcal{R}^{\bullet}(G)
be the smallest dg-ideal containing
\mathcal{N}^{*}(G)
.
Then the
n
-th component
\mathcal{J}^{n}(G)
can be expressed as

(2.7)

\mathcal{J}^{n}(G)=\mathcal{N}^{n}(G)+\partial(\mathcal{N}^{n-1}(G)).

The dg-algebra of path cochains is defined as the quotient algebra

(2.8)

\Omega^{\bullet}(G)=\mathcal{R}^{\bullet}(G)/\mathcal{J}^{\bullet}(G).

2.2.
Path cochain algebra as a quadratic algebra

Denote by
\mathbb{K}G
the path algebra of
G.
As a module, it is spanned by paths
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
for
n\geq 0,
including paths of length zero.
The product of two paths is defined by

(2.9)

(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})(y_{0},\dots,y_{m})=\begin{cases}(x_{0},\dots,x_{n},y_{1},\dots,y_{m}),&\text{ if }x_{n}=y_{0};\\
0,&\text{ else.}\end{cases}

The unit
1\in\mathbb{K}G
is the sum of paths of length zero

1\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{v\in V(G)}(v).

Note that
\mathbb{K}G
is a graded algebra, where the grading of a path
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
equals its length, written as

|(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})|=n.

Let us write
(\mathbb{K}G)^{n}
as the
n
-th component of
\mathbb{K}G
.

For any vertices
x,y\in V(G)
such that
d(x,y)=2,
consider the element
t_{x,y}
defined as the linear sum of all
2
-paths
(x,v,y)
from
x
to
y
:

(2.10)

t_{x,y}=\sum_{v\in V(G)}(x,v,y)\in(\mathbb{K}G)^{2}.

We denote by
T\triangleleft\mathbb{K}G
the ideal generated by all such elements
t_{x,y}.

Lemma 2.1
.

(1)

There is an isomorphism of graded algebras

(2.11)

\varphi:\mathcal{R}^{\bullet}(G)/\mathcal{N}^{\bullet}(G)\xrightarrow{\cong}\mathbb{K}G

sending
e^{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}}+\mathcal{N}^{\bullet}(G)
to
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
, for any path
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
in
G
.

(2)

The image of
\mathcal{J}^{\bullet}(G)/\mathcal{N}^{\bullet}(G)
under
\varphi
is the ideal
T
.

Proof.

See
[
11
, Lemmas 5.2 & 5.3]
.
∎

Theorem 2.2

(
[
11
, Th. 5.4]
)
.

The isomorphism (
2.11
) induces an isomorphism of dg-algebras

\Omega^{\bullet}(G)\cong\mathbb{K}G/T,

where the differential on
\mathbb{K}G/T
is defined by the formula

\partial(p+T)=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\ \sum_{v}(-1)^{i}(p_{0},\dots,p_{i-1},v,p_{i},\dots,p_{n})+T,

for any path
p=(p_{0},p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
in

,
and

.

2.3.
Duality between path chains and cochains

In this subsection, we assume that
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{F}
is a field. The isomorphism

\mathcal{R}^{\bullet}(G)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{R}_{\bullet}(G),\mathbb{F}),\quad e^{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}}\mapsto e_{x_{0},\dots,x_{n}}

induces an isomorphism by restricting to
\mathcal{A}_{\bullet}(G)
:

(2.12)

\mathcal{R}^{\bullet}(G)/\mathcal{N}^{\bullet}(G)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{A}_{\bullet}(G),\mathbb{F}).

Over
\mathbb{F}
, the above isomorphism (
2.12
) induces an isomorphism

(2.13)

\Omega^{\bullet}(G)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega_{\bullet}(G),\mathbb{F}).

Hence
\Omega^{\bullet}(G)
is dual to
\Omega_{\bullet}(G)
.
See
[
2
, Lemma 3.19]
or
[
12
, Prop. 3.16]
for more details.

Given any
n
-paths
p
and
q
in
G
, the Kronecker delta
\delta_{p,q}

determines a non-degenerate bilinear form

(2.14)

\langle-,-\rangle:\mathcal{A}_{n}(G)\times(\mathbb{F}G)^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}\quad\text{by}\quad\langle e_{p},q\rangle=\delta_{p,q}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
T^{n}=T\cap(\mathbb{F}G)^{n}
be the
n
-th component of
T
. Then
\Omega_{n}(G)
is the orthogonal complement of
T^{n}
with respect to the bilinear form (
2.14
).

Proof.

First, we consider the following bilinear form

(2.15)

\mathcal{R}_{n}(G)\times\mathcal{R}^{n}(G)\to\mathbb{F},\quad B(e_{p},e^{q})=\delta_{p,q},

where
\delta_{p,q}
is the Kroneck delta.
With respect to (
2.15
), one can check that
\mathcal{N}^{n}(G)
and
\partial(\mathcal{N}^{n-1}(G))
are the orthogonal complements of
\mathcal{A}_{n}(G)
and
\partial^{-1}(\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(G))
, respectively.
Then it follows from (
2.4
) that the orthogonal complement of
\Omega_{n}(G)
with respect to the bilinear form (
2.15
) is

\mathcal{J}^{n}(G)=\mathcal{N}^{n}(G)+\partial(\mathcal{N}^{n-1}(G)).

Second, the bilinear form (
2.14
) is induced from (
2.15
) by restricting
\mathcal{R}_{n}(G)
to
\mathcal{A}_{n}(G)
and the identifications

\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathcal{A}_{n},\mathbb{F})\cong\mathcal{R}^{n}(G)/\mathcal{N}^{n}(G)\xrightarrow{\varphi}(\mathbb{F}G)^{n},

where
\varphi
is an isomorphism given in (
2.11
). Hence the orthogonal complement of
\Omega_{n}(G)
with respect to (
2.14
) is the image of
\mathcal{J}^{n}(G)/\mathcal{N}^{n}(G)
under
\varphi
, which is
T^{n}
by Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

There is a non-degenerate bilinear form

\langle-,-\rangle:\Omega_{n}(G)\times(\mathbb{F}G/T)^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}

defined by

\langle\sum_{p}\alpha_{p}\cdot e_{p},q+T^{n}\rangle=\sum_{p}\alpha_{p}\cdot\delta_{p,q},
where
\delta_{p,q}
is the Kronecker delta.

3.2.
Thin, thick and bipartite

-classes

We say that
G
is a digraph without multisquares if for any two vertices
x,y
such that
d(x,y)=2
, there are at most two distinct
2
-paths from
x
to
y
.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
G
be a digraph without multisquares.

(1)

A pair
(x,y)
with
d(x,y)=2
is called
thin
if there is only one
2
-path from
x
to
y
, and
thick
if there are two different
2
-paths from
x
to
y
.

(2)

An
n
-path
(p_{0},p_{1},\ldots,p_{n})
is called
thin
if there exists
0<i<n
such that the pair
(p_{i-1},p_{i+1})
is thin. An
\mathcal{S}_{n}
-class is called
thin
if it contains at least one thin path. An
\mathcal{S}_{n}
-class is
thick
if it is not thin.

In other words, an
\mathcal{S}_{n}
-class is thick if all its elements are not thin paths.
In particular, every
\mathcal{S}_{n}
-class is thick if
n=0
or
1
.
In Examples
3.3
and
3.4
, the
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-class is thin for the left-hand digraph and thick for the right-hand digraph.
In Examples
3.5
and
3.6
the
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-classes are thick.

Definition 3.8
.

An
\mathcal{S}_{n}
-class is called
bipartite
if it is bipartite as a graph.

In Examples
3.3
,
3.4
,
3.5
and
3.6
, the
\mathcal{S}_{3}
-classes are bipartite. In Subsection
5.4
, we will give an example that has a non-bipartite
\mathcal{S}_{4}
-class
due to the existence of a 9-cycle in this
\mathcal{S}_{4}
-class.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
G
be a digraph without multisquares. Then any
\mathcal{S}_{n}
-class is bipartite for
n\leq 3
. In other words, the graph
\mathcal{S}_{n}
is bipartite for
n\leq 3
.

Proof.

For
n\leq 2
the statement is obvious. Let us prove it for
n=3.

The graph
\mathcal{S}_{3}
has edge labelled by two colors
1
and
2.

Since
G
has no multisquares, any vertex
p
of
\mathcal{S}_{3}
is adjacent to at most one edge of color
1
and at most one edge of color
2.
Therefore any vertex of
\mathcal{S}_{3}
has degree at most
2.
Hence
\mathcal{S}_{3}
is a union of cycles and line graphs. Moreover, the two edges have different colors for any vertex of
\mathcal{S}_{3}
with degree two. It follows that all the cycles are even. Therefore
\mathcal{S}_{3}
is bipartite.
∎

4.
The basis of the path (co)chain complex

In this section, a digraph
G
is assumed to be without multisquares unless stated otherwise.
We continue to let
\mathbb{K}
be a commutative ring and
\mathbb{F}
be a field.

4.1.
The basis of
\Omega^{n}

The following lemma is used to describe the basis of
\Omega^{n}(G)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be an undirected simple graph,
\mathcal{V}=\mathbb{K}\cdot V(\Gamma)
be the free module generated by the set of vertices, and
\mathcal{E}\leq\mathcal{V}
be its submodule generated by elements of the form
v+v^{\prime},
where
(v,v^{\prime})\in E(\Gamma).
Assume that
B\subseteq V(\Gamma)
is a complete set of representatives of bipartite connected components of
\Gamma,
and
N\subseteq V(\Gamma)
is a complete set of representatives of non-bipartite connected components of
\Gamma.

Then the obvious maps
B,N\to\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{E}
induce an isomorphism

(4.1)

\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{E}\cong(\mathbb{K}\cdot B)\oplus((\mathbb{K}/2\mathbb{K})\cdot N).

Proof.

We present
\Gamma=\coprod_{i}\Gamma_{i}
as a disjoint union of its connected components. For each
\Gamma_{i}
we obtain a free module
\mathcal{V}_{i}
generated by
V(\Gamma_{i})
and a submodule
\mathcal{E}_{i}\subseteq\mathcal{V}_{i}
generated by the sums
v+v^{\prime}
where
(v,v^{\prime})\in E(\Gamma_{i}).
It is easy to see that

\mathcal{V}=\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{V}_{i},\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{E}=\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{E}_{i},\hskip 14.22636pt\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{E}\cong\bigoplus_{i}\mathcal{V}_{i}/\mathcal{E}_{i}.

Therefore, it suffices to prove the statement for a connected graph
\Gamma
.

Assume that
\Gamma
is a connected bipartite graph. We present the set of its vertices as the disjoint union
V(\Gamma)=U\sqcup W
such that there are no edges between vertices of
U,
and there are no edges between vertices of
W.
It is easy to see that in this case for any the module
\mathcal{E}
is generated by elements of the form
u-u^{\prime},w-w^{\prime},u+w
where
u,u^{\prime}\in U,w,w^{\prime}\in W.
Then we can consider a homomorphism
f:\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{K}
sending all elements of
U
to
1,
all elements of
W
to
-1
and prove that
\mathcal{E}=\mathrm{Ker}(f).
Therefore
\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{E}\cong\mathbb{K}.

Assume that
\Gamma
is connected non-bipartite graph. Then there is an odd cycle: a sequence of vertices
v_{0},\dots,v_{2k+1}
such that
(v_{i},v_{i+1})\in E(\Gamma)
and
v_{2k+1}=v_{0}.
Summing up the elements
(-1)^{i}(v_{i}+v_{i+1})\in\mathcal{E},
we obtain
2v_{0}\in\mathcal{E}.
Since
\Gamma
is connected, this implies that
2v\in\mathcal{E}
for any vertex
v.
Therefore
\mathcal{E}
is generated by all elements of the form
2v
and
v-v^{\prime},
where
(v,v^{\prime})\in E(\Gamma).
Consider a homomorphism
f:\mathcal{V}\to\mathbb{K}/2\mathbb{K}
sending
v
to
1\in\mathbb{K}/2\mathbb{K}.
It is easy to see that
\mathrm{Ker}(f)=\mathcal{E}.
Therefore
\mathcal{V}/\mathcal{E}\cong\mathbb{K}/2\mathbb{K}.

∎

(1)

The assertions follow immediately from the definitions of thin and thick pairs.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a commutative ring, and

be a digraph without multisquares.
Let

be a complete set of representatives of thick bipartite

-classes, and

be a complete set of representatives of thick non-bipartite

-classes. Then the obvious maps

induce an isomorphism of

\Omega^{n}(G;\mathbb{K})\cong(\mathbb{K}\cdot B)\oplus((\mathbb{K}/2\mathbb{K})\cdot N).

Moreover, thin

Proof.

By Lemmas

and

is a complete set of representatives of non-bipartite

The dual basis of
\Omega_{n}

Now we work over a field

.

The following statements hold:

(1)

If

then the elements of the form
s^{\prime}_{c}
form a basis of
\Omega_{n}(G),
where

runs over all thick
\mathcal{S}_{n}
-classes.

(2)

If
{\sf char}(\mathbb{F})\neq 2,
then the elements of the form

form a basis of
\Omega_{n}(G),
where
c
runs over all thick bipartite
\mathcal{S}_{n}
-classes.

Moreover, these bases are dual to bases described in Corollary
4.4
with respect to the non-degenerate bilinear form described in Corollary

.

Proof.

Let us prove part (2); the proof of part (1) is similar. First of all, we need to check that
s_{c}\in\Omega_{n}(G)
for any thick bipartite
\mathcal{S}_{n}
-class

2.3
and
4.2

s_{c}
is orthogonal to
p+q
for

and orthogonal to any thin
n
-path
p.
If
p
is a thin
n

p\notin c,
so
s_{c}
is obviously orthogonal to

If
(p,q)\in E(\mathcal{S}_{n}),
then they are in the same
\mathcal{S}_{n}

If

again, it is obvious that

is orthogonal to

Assume that

Since

we obtain either

and

or
p\in c^{-}
and
q\in c^{+}.
Without lost of generality we assume that
p\in c^{+}
and
q\in c^{-}.
Then we have

\begin{split}\left\langle\sum_{r\in c^{+}}e_{r},p\right\rangle=1,\quad&\left\langle\sum_{r\in c^{-}}e_{r},p\right\rangle=0;\\
\left\langle\sum_{r\in c^{-}}e_{r},q\right\rangle=1,\quad&\left\langle\sum_{r\in c^{+}}e_{r},q\right\rangle=0.\end{split}

Therefore
\langle s_{c},p+q\rangle=0.

Consider a complete set
B\subseteq\Omega^{n}(G)
of representatives of thick bipartite
\mathcal{S}_{n}
-classes, which is a basis of
\Omega^{n}(G)
by Corollary
4.4
. For any
b\in B
we have

\langle s_{c},b\rangle=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }b\in c;\\
0&\text{ if }b\notin c.\end{cases}

Therefore the elements
s_{c}
form the dual basis of
B
in
\Omega_{n}(G).

∎

Remark 4.8
.

For
n=3
our basis of
\Omega_{3}(G;\mathbb{F})
coincides with the basis of trapezohedral paths and their merging images constructed by Grigor’yan in
[
5
, Theorem 2.10]
. This follows from the proof of
[
5
, Theorem 2.10]
, in which the graph
P
considered coincides with the connected component of
\mathcal{S}_{3}(G).

5.
Nonexistence of spaces

In this section we introduce the notion of the path Euler characteristic of a digraph and demonstrate its use in proving the nonexistence of spaces whose homology coincides with the path homology of certain digraphs with coefficients in different commutative rings simultaneously.

5.1.
Euler characteristic

We say that a space
X
has homology of finite type if the integral homology group
H_{n}(X;\mathbb{Z})
is finitely generated for any
n.
Similarly, a chain complex
C_{\bullet}
of free abelian groups has homology of finite type if
H_{n}(C_{\bullet})
is finitely generated for any
n.

Given a chain complex
C_{\bullet}
of free abelian groups and a field
\mathbb{F},
we say that the Euler characteristic
\chi^{\mathbb{F}}(C_{\bullet})
of
C_{\bullet}
over
\mathbb{F}
is defined, if
H_{n}(C_{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F})
is finitely dimensional for all
n,
and equals to zero for all but a finite number of
n
’s. In this case, we set

(5.1)

\chi^{\mathbb{F}}(C_{\bullet})=\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(H_{n}(C_{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F})).

The following lemma seems to be known but we add it here for completeness.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
C_{\bullet}
be a chain complex of free abelian groups with homology of finite type and
p
be a prime.
Suppose
\chi^{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\bullet})
and
\chi^{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}(C_{\bullet})
are defined. Then

\chi^{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\bullet})=\chi^{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}(C_{\bullet}).

Proof.

Since
C_{\bullet}
has homology of finite type, we obtain

H_{n}(C_{\bullet})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{a_{n}}\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{b_{n}}\oplus A_{n},

where
A_{n}
is a finite abelian group that does not have
p
-torsion. By the universal coefficient theorem for chain complexes
[
15
, Th.3.6.2]
, we have

H_{n}(C_{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Q}^{a_{n}}\text{ and }H_{n}(C_{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{a_{n}+b_{n}+b_{n-1}}.

The hypothesis that
\chi^{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\bullet})
and
\chi^{\mathbb{Z}/p}(C_{\bullet})
are defined implies that
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
are zero for all but a finite number of
n
’s. Therefore, we have

\chi^{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\bullet})=\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}a_{n}=\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}(a_{n}+b_{n}+b_{n-1})=\chi^{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}(C_{\bullet}).\qed

In the next example, we see that the condition of having homology of finite type is sufficient for the statement of Lemma
5.1
.

Example 5.2
.

Let
C_{\bullet}
be a free resolution of the abelian group
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
(a chain complex of free abelian groups such that
C_{n}=0
for
n<0
, whose homology is concentrated in degree
0,
and isomorphic to
\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z}
).
Then
\chi^{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\bullet})
and
\chi^{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}(C_{\bullet})
are defined but

(5.2)

0=\chi^{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\bullet})\neq\chi^{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}(C_{\bullet})=-1.

This is because the homology of
C_{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
is trivial, and the homology of
C_{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
is concentrated in degree one:
H_{1}(C_{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathrm{Tor}(\mathbb{Q}/\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}.

5.2.
Path Euler characteristic

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field. The path Euler characteristic
\chi^{\mathbb{F}}(G)
of a finite digraph
G
(may contain multisquares) is defined, if the path homology groups
\mathrm{PH}_{n}(G;\mathbb{F})
are trivial for all but a finite number of
n
’s.
In this case, we set

(5.3)

\chi^{\mathbb{F}}(G)=\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathrm{PH}_{n}(G;\mathbb{F})).

If there exists
m
such that
\Omega_{m}(G;\mathbb{F})=0,
then
\Omega_{n}(G;\mathbb{F})=0
for
n\geq m.
In this case,

(5.4)

\chi^{\mathbb{F}}(G)=\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{n}\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega_{n}(G;\mathbb{F})).

In the case that
G
has no multisquares, we apply Corollary
4.4
to compute the path Euler characteristics. Denote by
N_{n}
the number of thick bipartite
\mathcal{S}_{n}
-classes and by
N^{\prime}_{n}
the number of thick
\mathcal{S}_{n}
-classes. Then we have

(5.5)

\chi^{\mathbb{F}}(G)=\begin{cases}\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}N_{n},&\text{ if }{\sf char}(\mathbb{F})\neq 2\ \text{ and }\ N_{m}=0\text{ for some }m;\\
\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}N^{\prime}_{n},&\text{ if }{\sf char}(\mathbb{F})=2\ \text{ and }\ N^{\prime}_{m}=0\text{ for some }m.\end{cases}

5.3.
Path homology and nonexistence of spaces

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
G
be a finite digraph and
p
be a prime such that the path Euler characteristics
\chi^{\mathbb{Q}}(G)
and
\chi^{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}(G)
are defined. Suppose
\chi^{\mathbb{Q}}(G)\neq\chi^{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}(G)
.
Then the following statements hold:

(1)

There is no space
X
such that there are isomorphisms of graded abelian groups

\mathrm{PH}_{*}(G;\mathbb{K})\cong H_{*}(X;\mathbb{K})
for
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

There is no space
X
with homology of finite type such that there are isomorphisms of vector spaces

\mathrm{PH}_{*}(G;\mathbb{F})\cong H_{*}(X;\mathbb{F})
for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
.

(3)

There is no chain complex
C_{\bullet}
of free abelian groups with homology of finite type such that

\mathrm{PH}_{*}(G;\mathbb{F})\cong H_{*}(C_{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F})
for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}
.

(4)

There exists
n
such that there is no “universal coefficient” short exact sequence

0\to\mathrm{PH}_{n}(G;\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}\to\mathrm{PH}_{n}(G;\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\to\mathrm{Tor}(\mathrm{PH}_{n-1}(G;\mathbb{Z}),\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\to 0.

Proof.

We first show part (3), while part (2) follows from (3). Assume the contrary, that
C_{\bullet}
is a chain complex of free abelian groups of finite homology type such that

H_{n}(C_{\bullet}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{F})\cong\mathrm{PH}_{\bullet}(G;\mathbb{F})
for
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}.

Then
\chi^{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\bullet})
and
\chi^{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}(C_{\bullet})
are defined. Lemma
5.1
implies
\chi^{\mathbb{Q}}(C_{\bullet})=\chi^{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}(C_{\bullet})
,
so
\chi^{\mathbb{Q}}(G)=\chi^{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}(G)
by assumption.
This makes a contradiction.

Next we prove part (1). Suppose there is a space
X
such that

\mathrm{PH}_{*}(G;\mathbb{Z})\cong H_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z}).

As
G
is finite, the space
X
has finite homology type. By Proposition
2.5
, we have

\mathrm{PH}_{*}(G;\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathrm{PH}_{*}(G;\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong H_{*}(X;\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathbb{Q}\cong H_{*}(X;\mathbb{Q}).

This contradicts to part (2).

Finally, we prove part (4).
We follow an argument similar to the proof of Lemma
5.1
.
Assume the contrary.
Since
G
is finite, we have

\mathrm{PH}_{n}(G;\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{a_{n}}\oplus(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{b_{n}}\oplus A_{n},

where
A_{n}
is a finite abelian group without
p
-torsion. By Proposition
2.5
, we have
\mathrm{PH}_{n}(G;\mathbb{Q})\cong\mathbb{Q}^{a_{n}}.

By assumption, we have

\mathrm{PH}_{n}(G;\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})\cong(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{a_{n}+b_{n}+b_{n-1}}.

The fact that
\chi^{\mathbb{Q}}(G)
and
\chi^{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}(G)
are defined implies that
a_{n}
and
b_{n}
are zero for all but a finite number of
n
’s. Therefore

\chi^{\mathbb{Q}}(G)=\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}a_{n}=\sum_{n}(-1)^{n}(a_{n}+b_{n}+b_{n-1})=\chi^{\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z}}(G),

which makes a contradiction.
∎

Next we will give an explicit example that satisfies the assumption of Proposition
5.3
.

Theorem 5.4
.

For the digraph
\mathcal{G}
defined in (
5.9
), the following statements hold:

(1)

The rational and mod-
2
path Euler characteristics of
\mathcal{G}
are not equal

\chi^{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{G})\neq\chi^{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}(\mathcal{G}).

(2)

\Omega^{4}(\mathcal{G};\mathbb{Z})
has
2
-torsion.

Proof.

We first check that
\mathcal{G}
has no multisquares and has no thin pairs.
It is easy to check that the pairs
(x,y)
such that
d(x,y)=2
are the pairs in the form

(5.10)

(x_{0},x_{2}^{i}),

(x_{0},x_{3}^{i}),

(x_{1}^{i},x_{3}^{i+1}),

(x_{1}^{i},x_{4}),

(x_{2}^{i},x_{4}).

For pairs
(x,y)
in (
5.10
), there are exactly two vertices
v_{1},v_{2}
such that
(x,v_{i},y)
is a path:

\displaystyle(x_{0},x_{2}^{i}):

\displaystyle\quad x_{1}^{i},~x_{1}^{i-1};

\displaystyle(x_{0},x_{3}^{i}):

\displaystyle\quad x_{1}^{i},~x_{1}^{i+1};

\displaystyle(x_{1}^{i},x_{3}^{i+1}):

\displaystyle\quad x_{2}^{i},~x_{2}^{i+1};

\displaystyle(x_{1}^{i},x_{4}):

\displaystyle\quad x_{3}^{i},~x_{3}^{i+2};

\displaystyle(x_{2}^{i},x_{4}):

\displaystyle\quad x_{3}^{i},~x_{3}^{i+1}.

Therefore
\mathcal{G}
has no multisquares, and has no thin paths.

The graph
\mathcal{S}_{4}(\mathcal{G})
coincides with the graph
\mathcal{S}_{4}(G)
drawn in (
5.7
). It follows that all
4
-paths of
\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{S}_{4}
-class. By Theorems
4.7
and
2.2

\Omega_{4}(\mathcal{G};\mathbb{Q})=0,\hskip 14.22636pt\Omega_{4}(\mathcal{G};\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z},\hskip 14.22636pt\Omega^{4}(\mathcal{G};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}.

\geq 5,

\Omega_{n}(G;\mathbb{F})=0

\mathbb{F}.
On the other hand by Corollary

\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}(\Omega_{n}(\mathcal{G};\mathbb{Q}))=\dim_{\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}}(\Omega_{n}(\mathcal{G};\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}))

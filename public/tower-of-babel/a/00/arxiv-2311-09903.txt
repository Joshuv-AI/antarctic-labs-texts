Abstract
. The exact value of the separating Noether number of some finite abelian groups is determined, including the direct sums of cyclic groups of the same order.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 13A50; Secondary 11B75, 20D60

1.
Introduction

A long-standing open question in arithmetic combinatorics is whether the
Davenport constant
(i.e. the maximal length of an irreducible zero-sum sequence) over the direct sum
C_{n}^{r}
of
r
copies of the cyclic group of order
n
equals
1+(n-1)r
(see
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
15
]
). Equivalently, the question is whether
the
Noether number
(i.e. the general upper bound for the degrees of the generators of the algebras of polynomial invariants) of
C_{n}^{r}
equals
1+(n-1)r
.

In recent years the study of separating systems of polynomial invariants
(as a relaxation of the concept of generating systems) received much attention. In particular, in analogy with the Noether number, the notion of the
separating Noether number
of a finite group was introduced in
[
14
]
. In the present paper we shall determine the exact value of the separating Noether number of
C_{n}^{r}
, by
proving certain results on zero-sum sequences over finite
abelian groups.

Given a finite dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space
V
endowed with an action of a finite group
G
via linear transformations, we shall write

\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}
for the algebra of polynomial functions
V\to\mathbb{C}
that are constant on
G
-orbits. This is a graded subalgebra of the
algebra
\mathbb{C}[V]
of polynomial functions on
V

(which can be identified with the
n
variable polynomial algebra over
\mathbb{C}
where
n=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(V)
, equipped with the standard grading).
It is known that if
v,w\in V
have different
G
-orbits, then there exists a homogeneous polynomial invariant
f\in\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}
of degree at most
|G|
with
f(v)\neq f(w)
, see
[
2
, Theorem 3.12.1]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Write
\beta_{sep}(G,V)
for the minimal positive integer
d
such that for any
v,w\in V
with different
G
-orbits,
there exists a homogeneous
f\in\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}
of degree at most
d
with
f(v)\neq f(w)
. The separating Noether number
\beta_{sep}(G)
of a finite group
G
is defined by

\beta_{sep}(G):=\underset{V}{\sup}\{\beta_{sep}(G,V):V
is a finite dimensional representation of
G\}

The above definition (which appeared first in
[
14
]
) is motivated by the Noether number
\beta(G)
, introduced in
[
20
]

as the maximal degree in a minimal homogeneous generating system of the algebra
\mathbb{C}[V]^{G}
, as
V
ranges over all possible finite dimensional
G
-modules. By the famous theorem of Noether, we have

\beta(G)\leq|G|
(cf.
[
16
]
). Note also the obvious inequality

\beta_{sep}(G)\leq\beta(G).

In this paper the focus will be on determining the separating Noether numbers of some infinite families of abelian groups.
Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.2
.

For positive integers
n\geq 2
and
r
denote by
C_{n}^{r}
the direct sum
C_{n}\oplus\cdots\oplus C_{n}
of
r
copies of the cyclic group
C_{n}
of order
n
, and let
p
be the minimal prime divisor of
n
.
Then we have

\beta_{sep}(C_{n}^{r})=\begin{cases}ns,&\mbox{ if }r=2s-1\mbox{ is odd}\\
ns+\frac{n}{p},&\mbox{ if }r=2s\mbox{ is even}.\\
\end{cases}

In fact Theorem
1.2
is a special case of our
Theorem
6.1
and Theorem
6.2
. Note that in sharp contrast with Theorem
1.2
, the exact values of the Noether numbers (equivalently Davenport constants) of the groups
C_{n}^{r}
are not known, despite much interest in determining them (
[
7
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
15
]
).

Recall that the
Davenport constant

\mathsf{D}(G)
of a finite abelian group
G
is the maximal length of an atom (i.e. irreducible element) in the monoid of zero-sum sequences over
G
.
The most common examples of infinite families of abelian groups for which the exact values of the Davenport constants are known are the abelian groups of rank two and abelian
p
-groups (for such groups
G
we have
\mathsf{D}(G)=\mathsf{d}^{*}(G)+1
)
(see (
3
) for the definition of
\mathsf{d}^{*}(G)
). The determination of the separating Noether number of
these groups seems to be more laborious or difficult than the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
In a parallel paper
[
19
]
we are discussing the exact value of the separating Noether number of abelian groups of rank two and three.

It has long been known that for an abelian group, the values of the Noether number and the Davenport constant coincide:

(1)

\beta(G)=\mathsf{D}(G)

(see
[
20
]
; see also
[
1
]
for further discussion of this connection). The separating Noether number of a finite abelian group
G
also has an interpretation in terms of zero-sum sequences over
G
that we shall explain in Section
2
. In particular, in this part we define the concept of
group atom
(see Definition
2.3
), and recall its role in the study of the separating Noether number. In Section
3
we give a general upper bound for the separating Noether number of an abelian group. In Section
4
we prove some lemmas that will help us in detecting group atoms. By using these lemmas we construct certain group atoms in Section
5
.
In Section
6
we
deduce Theorem
6.1
and
Theorem
6.2
, which give the separating
Noether number for a class of finite abelian groups, including the groups
C_{n}^{r}
. Theorem
1.2
follows therefore as a special case.

2.
The separating Noether number of an abelian group

Let
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}
be distinct elements of the (additively written) finite abelian group
G
. Then

\mathcal{G}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k}):=\{[m_{1},\dots,m_{k}]\in\mathbb{Z}^{k}:\sum m_{i}g_{i}=0\in G\}

is a subgroup of the additive group of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
. Its submonoid

\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k}):=\mathbb{N}^{k}\cap\mathcal{G}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})

is a finitely generated Krull monoid, playing an essential role in the
multiplicative ideal theory of Krull domains, see
[
9
, Chapter 2]
). These monoids are also called
block monoids
, see for example
[
9
, Definition 2.5.5]
.
It is not hard to see that
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
generates
\mathcal{G}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
as a group.

An element of the monoid
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
is an
atom
, if it can not be written as the sum of two non-zero elements of the monoid. The
length
of an element
\mathsf{m}=[m_{1},\dots,m_{k}]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
is
|\mathsf{m}|=\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}
.
Denote by
\mathsf{D}(\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k}))
the maximal length of an atom in the monoid
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
. The support of an element is defined as:

\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mathsf{m})=\{i\in\{1,\dots,k\}:m_{i}\neq 0\}

The size of the support is
|\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mathsf{m})|
. Let
\mathsf{e_{i}}
be the
i^{th}
standard basis vector of
\mathbb{Z}^{k}
, and
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})
the order of
g_{i}
in
G
. Of course,
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})\mathsf{e_{i}}\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
, so
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})\mathsf{e_{i}}\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
. If
0\leq m_{i}\leq\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})
for any
i
, then we define the
complementer
of
\mathsf{m}
:

(2)

\mathsf{m^{*}}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})\mathsf{e_{i}}-\mathsf{m}\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})

In the special case when
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}
is an enumeration of all the elements of
G
, then by slight abuse of notation we write
\mathcal{B}(G):=\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
and

\mathsf{D}(G):=\mathsf{D}(\mathcal{B}(G))
;
clearly, the isomorphism type of the monoid endowed with the length function does not depend on the ordering of the elements of
G
.

Remark 2.1
.

The monoid
\mathcal{B}(G)
can be interpreted as the monoid of zero-sum sequences over
G
.
Namely, associate with
\mathsf{m}\in\mathcal{B}(G)=\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
the sequence over
G
containing
g_{i}\in G
with multiplicity
m_{i}
.
For a survey on zero-sum sequences over abelian groups see
[
8
]
,
[
9
, Chapter 5]
,
[
12
, Chapter 10]
.

Convention 2.2
.

Throughout this article the following convention will be used:
G
will stand for the abelian group
G=C_{n_{1}}\oplus C_{n_{2}}\oplus\dots\oplus C_{n_{r}}
where
2\leq n_{r}\mid\dots\mid n_{2}\mid n_{1}
, and
r=2s
or
2s-1
, depending on its parity,
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}
are fixed distinct elements of
G
, and
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
is the corresponding block monoid. Note that here
r
is the
rank
(i.e. the minimal number of generators), and
n_{1}=\exp(G)
is the
exponent
(i.e. the least common multiple of the orders of the elements) of the group
G
.

Setting

(3)

\mathsf{d}^{*}(G):=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(n_{i}-1),

we have the inequality

(4)

\mathsf{d}^{*}(G)+1\leq\mathsf{D}(G)

(see for example Section 3 in
[
8
]
).
Results from arithmetic combinatorics show that for p-groups (see
[
17
]
) or for groups of rank two (see
[
18
]
) equality holds in (
4
). There are some infinite families of abelian groups for which the inequality is known to be strict
(see for example
[
11
]
). In general it is not known when equality holds.

In order to state the analogues of (
1
) for the separating
Noether number we need
the following definition:

Definition 2.3
.

An element
\mathsf{m}\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
is called a
group atom in

\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})

if
\mathsf{m}
can not be written as an
integral
linear combination of elements
of
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
that have length strictly smaller than
|\mathsf{m}|
.

Remark 2.4
.

If
\mathsf{m}
is a group atom in the monoid
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
, then it is also an atom in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
, hence in particular we have the inequalities
m_{i}\leq\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})
for all
i=1,\dots,k
. So its complementer,
\mathsf{m}^{*}\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
is defined as in (
2
).

The relation between the separating Noether number of an abelian group and the theory of zero-sum sequences
is established in
[
4
]
(building on
[
3
]
and
[
6
]
) by the following statement:

Lemma 2.5
.

(Corollary 2.6. in
[
4
]
)

The number
\beta_{sep}(G)
is the maximal length of a group atom in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
, where
\{g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\}
ranges over all subsets of size
k\leq\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(G)+1
of the abelian group
G
.

For related results on not necessarily finite abelian groups see
[
5
]
.

3.
A general upper bound

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathsf{m}
be a group atom in the monoid
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
such that
|\mathsf{m}|>\max\{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})\mbox{ for }i=1,\dots,k\}
. Then we have the following inequalities:

(i)

|\mathsf{m}|\leq|\mathsf{m}^{*}|
.

(ii)

2|\mathsf{m}|\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})
.

Proof.

Both (i) and (ii) follow from the equality

\mathsf{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})\mathsf{e_{i}}-\mathsf{m}^{*}
.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

For any finite abelian group
G
we have the inequality

\beta_{sep}(G)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{\exp(G)\cdot(\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(G)+1)}{2}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{m}
be a group atom in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
. If
|\mathsf{m}|\leq\max\{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})\mbox{ for }i=1,\dots,k\}
, then
|\mathsf{m}|\leq\frac{\exp(G)\cdot(\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(G)+1)}{2}
, since
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})\leq\exp(G)
, and
1\leq\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(G)
.

If
|\mathsf{m}|>\max\{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})\mbox{ for }i=1,\dots,k\}
, then
|\mathsf{m}|\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})}{2}
by Lemma
3.1
(ii). Lemma
2.5
implies that we need to consider only the case
k\leq\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(G)+1
, while
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})\leq\exp(G)
by definition. So the maximal length of the group atoms in the monoids given by Lemma
2.5
is bounded from above by
\frac{\exp(G)\cdot(\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(G)+1)}{2}
in any case. So
\beta_{sep}(G)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{\exp(G)\cdot(\mathop{\rm rank}\nolimits(G)+1)}{2}\right\rfloor
, since
\beta_{sep}(G)
must be an integer.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

The bound for
\beta_{sep}(G)
in Corollary
3.2
is useful when the size of more than half of the cyclic direct summands of
G
equals the exponent of
G
. Moreover, the inequality in Corollary
3.2
is sharp for the groups
C_{n}^{2s-1}
for all
n
and for the groups
C_{n}^{2s}
with even
n
by
Theorem
1.2
.

4.
Detecting group atoms

Next we give a sufficient condition for an element in a block monoid to be a group atom.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathsf{m}=[m_{1},\dots,m_{k}]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
be an element with
|\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mathsf{m})|=k
, such that
for a fixed
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
and an integer
d
, we have that

(i)

d

divides the
i^{th}
entry of each atom in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
whose support is of size strictly smaller than
k
;

(ii)

d
does not divide
m_{i}
;

(iii)

|\mathsf{m}|
is minimal among the lengths of the atoms in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
with support of size
k
.

Then
\mathsf{m}
is a group atom in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{k})
.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction, that
\mathsf{m}
can be written as a linear combination of elements of lower length. By the assumptions, the size of their support is strictly smaller than
k
, so their
i^{th}
entries are divisible by
d
. Of course each linear combination of them does so. This is a contradiction, since
d
does not divide
m_{i}
. So
\mathsf{m}
is indeed a group atom.
∎

We continue with an elementary number theoretic lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
be positive integers with
\gcd(\alpha,\beta)=1
. Then there exists an
\ell\in\{1,2,\dots,\alpha\gamma-1\}
, for which
\gcd(\ell,\alpha\gamma)=1
and
\ell\beta\equiv 1

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

\alpha
hold.

Proof.

Here
\gcd(\alpha,\beta)=1
implies that there exists
\hat{\beta}\in\{1,2,\dots,\alpha-1\}
, such that
\hat{\beta}\beta\equiv 1

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

\alpha
, (of course,
\gcd(\alpha,\hat{\beta})=1
). Let
p_{1},\dots,p_{t}
be the distinct prime divisors of
\gamma
that do not divide
\alpha
.
By the Chinese Remainder Theorem, the system
x\equiv\hat{\beta}

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

\alpha
,
x\equiv 1

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

p_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,t
has a unique solution
\ell
in
\{1,\dots,\alpha p_{1}\dots p_{t}-1\}
contained in
\{1,2,\dots,\alpha\gamma-1\}
. Clearly,
\ell
is coprime to
\alpha\gamma
as well.
∎

Using Lemma
4.2
we can prove the following result:

Lemma 4.3
.

Consider the case when
r=2s
and
n_{s+1}=\dots=n_{1}
. Let
\mathsf{m}=[m_{1},\dots,m_{2s+1}]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{2s+1})
, for elements
g_{1},\dots,g_{2s+1}\in G
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})=n_{1}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,2s\}

(ii)

|\mathsf{m}|>sn_{1}+\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})}{p}
, where
p
is the minimal prime divisor of
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})
.

Then
\mathsf{m}
is not a group atom in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{2s+1})
.

Proof.

Suppose for contradiction that
\mathsf{m}
is a group atom in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{2s+1})
.
Set

d:=\gcd(|\mathsf{m}|,\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1}))=\gcd(|\mathsf{m}|-sn_{1},\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})).

So
|\mathsf{m}|=sn_{1}+bd
for some positive integer
b
with
\gcd(b,\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})}{d})=1
. We use Lemma
4.2
with

\alpha=\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})}{d},\quad\beta=b,\quad\gamma=\frac{dn_{1}}{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})}.

This way we get an
\ell\in\{1,2,\dots,n_{1}-1\}
, for which
\gcd(\ell,n_{1})=1
and
\ell b\equiv 1

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})}{d}
hold, implying in particular that

(5)

\ell bd\equiv d\ \mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1}).

Denote by
m_{\ell,i}
the unique element in

\{0,1,\dots,\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})-1\}
such that
m_{\ell,i}\equiv\ell m_{i}

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})
. Observe that
\mathsf{m}_{\ell}:=[m_{\ell,1},\dots m_{\ell,2s+1}]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{2s+1})
. Then

|\mathsf{m}_{\ell}|=\sum_{i=1}^{2s+1}m_{\ell,i}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{2s+1}\ell m_{i}=\ell|\mathsf{m}|=\ell(sn_{1}+bd)\equiv\ell bd\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})

implying by (
5
) that

|\mathsf{m}_{\ell}|\equiv d\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1}).

Taking into account

|\mathsf{m}_{\ell}|<\sum_{i=1}^{2s+1}\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})=2sn_{1}+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})

we get that

(6)

|\mathsf{m}_{\ell}|\in\left\{d+j\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})\mid j=0,1,\dots,\frac{2sn_{1}}{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})}\right\}.

Since
\gcd(\ell,n_{1})=1
we have that there exists an
\hat{\ell}\in\{1,2,\dots,n_{1}-1\}
, such that
\ell\hat{\ell}\equiv 1

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

n_{1}
(hence
\ell\hat{\ell}\equiv 1

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})
also holds).
Moreover,

\hat{\ell}m_{\ell,i}\equiv\hat{\ell}\ell m_{i}\equiv m_{i}\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i}),

so for each
i\in\{1,\dots,2s+1\}
there exists an integer
t_{i}
such that

\hat{\ell}m_{\ell,i}-t_{i}\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})=m_{i}.

This shows that
\mathsf{m}
can be expressed by using
\mathsf{m}_{\ell}
and
\mathsf{e_{i}}
as follows:

\mathsf{m}=\hat{\ell}\mathsf{m}_{\ell}-\sum_{i=1}^{2s+1}t_{i}(\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})\mathsf{e_{i}})
.

Since
|\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})\mathsf{e_{i}}|=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})<sn_{1}+\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})}{p}<|\mathsf{m}|
, the assumption that
\mathsf{m}
is a group atom in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{2s+1})
implies that

(7)

|\mathsf{m}_{\ell}|\geq|\mathsf{m}|>sn_{1}+\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})}{p}.

Of course,
\mathsf{m}
can be expressed by using
\mathsf{m}_{\ell}^{*}
and
\mathsf{e_{i}}
too:

\mathsf{m}=\hat{\ell}(\sum_{i=1}^{2s+1}\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})\mathsf{e_{i}}-\mathsf{m}_{\ell}^{*})-\sum_{i=1}^{2s+1}t_{i}(\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})\mathsf{e_{i}})
.

Again, by the fact that
\mathsf{m}
is a group atom in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{2s+1})
with
|\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})\mathsf{e_{i}}|<|\mathsf{m}|
, we get that
sn_{1}+\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})}{p}<|\mathsf{m}|\leq|\mathsf{m}_{\ell}^{*}|
, hence

(8)

|\mathsf{m}_{\ell}|=2sn_{1}+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})-|\mathsf{m}_{\ell}^{*}|<sn_{1}+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})-\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})}{p}.

Combining (
7
) and (
8
) we get

(9)

sn_{1}+\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})}{p}<|\mathsf{m}_{\ell}|<sn_{1}+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})-\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})}{p}.

Now (
6
) and (
9
) imply that

|\mathsf{m}_{\ell}|=d+sn_{1}
,
showing in turn by (
9
) that

\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})}{p}<d<\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})(1-\frac{1}{p}).

However, this is a contradiction, since
d<\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})(1-\frac{1}{p})
implies that
d
is a proper divisor of
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})
, so
\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})}{p}<d
can not hold (recall that
p
is the minimal prime divisor of
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})
).
This shows that
|\mathsf{m}|
is not a group atom.
∎

Example 4.4
.

We mention that an element
\mathsf{m}
satisfying the conditions of Lemma
4.3
may well be an atom in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{2s+1})
. Indeed, consider for example the group
C_{12}\oplus C_{4}
, and the element
[11,1,3]\in\mathcal{B}((1,0),(1,1),(0,1))
. In this case
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{1})=\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2})=12
, while
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{3})=4
, and so
\frac{\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{3})}{p}=2
. Here
15=11+3+1>1\cdot 12+2=14
, so all the conditions of Lemma
4.3
are satisfied, hence it is not a group atom. Indeed, we can express
[11,1,3]
as an integral linear combination of such elements of
\mathcal{B}((1,0),(1,1),(0,1))
that have length strictly smaller than
15
:
[11,1,3]=7[5,7,1]-2[12,0,0]-4[0,12,0]-[0,0,4]
. On the other hand,
[11,1,3]
is easily seen to be an atom in the monoid
\mathcal{B}((1,0),(1,1),(0,1))
.

5.
A general lower bound

Developing further an idea from the proof of
Proposition 3.8 of
[
4
]
, we shall give a lower bound for the separating Noether number of an abelian group of odd rank
r=2s-1
.
The following notation is used in Proposition
5.1
and Lemma
5.2
.
Denote by
e_{1},\dots,e_{s},f_{1},\dots,f_{s-1}
the generators of the direct factors of
G
, (so the order of
e_{i}
is
n_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,s
and the order of
f_{j}
is
n_{j+s}
for
j=1,\dots,s-1
). Set
g_{1}=e_{1}
,
g_{2i}=e_{i}+f_{i}
and
g_{2i+1}=f_{i}+e_{i+1}
for
i=1,\dots,s-1
, and
g_{r+1}=e_{s}
. The elements
g_{1},\dots,g_{r+1}

are listed in the following table:

(10)

\begin{matrix}&e_{1}&e_{2}&\dots&e_{s-1}&e_{s}&f_{1}&f_{2}&\dots&f_{s-2}&f_{s-1}&\\
g_{1}=&(1&0&\dots&0&0&0&0&\dots&0&0)\\
g_{2}=&(1&0&\dots&0&0&1&0&\dots&0&0)\\
g_{3}=&(0&1&\dots&0&0&1&0&\dots&0&0)\\
g_{4}=&(0&1&\dots&0&0&0&1&\dots&0&0)\\
&&&&&&\dots\\
g_{r-2}=&(0&0&\dots&1&0&0&0&\dots&1&0)\\
g_{r-1}=&(0&0&\dots&1&0&0&0&\dots&0&1)\\
g_{r}=&(0&0&\dots&0&1&0&0&\dots&0&1)\\
g_{r+1}=&(0&0&\dots&0&1&0&0&\dots&0&0)\\
\end{matrix}

Proposition 5.1
.

Suppose that
r=2s-1
is odd. Then each coordinate of an atom
\mathsf{m}\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{r+1})
(where
g_{1},\dots,g_{r+1}
are listed in (
10
)) with
|\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mathsf{m})|<r+1
is divisible by
n_{r}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{m}=[m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{r+1}]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{r+1})
be an atom. We have the congruences

(11)

\displaystyle m_{2i-1}+m_{2i}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ \mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits\ n_{i}\quad(i=1,\dots,s),

\displaystyle m_{2i}+m_{2i+1}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ \mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits\ n_{s+i},\quad(i=1,\dots,s-1)

Since
n_{r}
divides
n_{i}
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
, all the congruences can be seen modulo
n_{r}
, which gives the following chain of congruences:

(12)

m_{1}\equiv-m_{2}\equiv\dots\equiv m_{r}\equiv-m_{r+1}\hbox{ }\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits\hbox{ }n_{r}

If
|\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mathsf{m})|<r+1
, then we have an
i_{0}\in\{1,\dots,r+1\}
, for which
m_{i_{0}}=0
. Putting this in equation (
12
) gives that
m_{i}\equiv 0

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

n_{r}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,r+1\}
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
G=C_{n_{1}}\oplus C_{n_{2}}\oplus\dots\oplus C_{n_{r}}
, where
2\leq n_{r}\mid n_{r-1}\mid\dots\mid n_{1}
. Then for
r=2s-1
,
\beta_{sep}(G)\geq n_{1}+\dots+n_{s}
.

Proof.

We shall apply Lemma
4.1
with

k=r+1
,
i=r+1
,
d=n_{r}
, and

\mathsf{m}=[n_{1}-1,1,n_{2}-1,1,\dots,n_{s}-1,1]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{r+1})
.

Condition (ii) of Lemma
4.1
obviously holds for
\mathsf{m}
, and
by Proposition
5.1
, condition (i) of Lemma
4.1
holds as well.
Next let
\mathsf{z}=[z_{1},\dots,z_{r+1}]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{r+1})
be an element with
|\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mathsf{z})|=r+1
. So
z_{i}>0
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,r+1\}
. Moreover,
\mathsf{z}
fulfills the conditions given in congruences (
11
). So
z_{2i-1}+z_{2i}\equiv 0

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

n_{i}
with
z_{2i-1}>0
and
z_{2i}>0
, which yields that
z_{2i-1}+z_{2i}\geq n_{i}
. So for any element
\mathsf{z}\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{r+1})
with
|\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mathsf{z})|=r+1
we have:

|\mathsf{z}|=(z_{1}+z_{2})+\dots+(z_{r}+z_{r+1})\geq n_{1}+\dots+n_{s}
.

This shows that assumption (iii) of Lemma
4.1
is also satisfied by
\mathsf{m}
. Consequently,
\mathsf{m}
is a group atom in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{r+1})
of length
n_{1}+\dots+n_{s}
by Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

In the special case, when the size of more
than half of the cyclic direct summands of
G
equals
\exp(G)
, the inequality in Lemma
5.2
turns out to be sharp, see Theorem
6.1
.

By slight modification of the construction of Lemma
5.2
, we are able to give similar results in the case when
r=2s
. In this case let

p
be a prime divisor of
n_{r}
, and let
g_{1}=e_{1}-\frac{n_{s+1}}{p}f_{1}
,
g_{2i}=e_{i}+f_{i+1}
and
g_{2i+1}=f_{i+1}+e_{i+1}
for
i=1,\dots,s-1
, and
g_{r}=e_{s}
,
g_{r+1}=f_{1}
. The elements
g_{1},\dots,g_{r+1}
are listed in the table:

(13)

\begin{matrix}&e_{1}&e_{2}&\dots&e_{s-1}&e_{s}&f_{1}&f_{2}&\dots&f_{s-1}&f_{s}&\\
g_{1}=&(1&0&\dots&0&0&-\frac{n_{s+1}}{p}&0&\dots&0&0)\\
g_{2}=&(1&0&\dots&0&0&0&1&\dots&0&0)\\
g_{3}=&(0&1&\dots&0&0&0&1&\dots&0&0)\\
g_{4}=&(0&1&\dots&0&0&0&0&\dots&0&0)\\
&&&&&&\dots\\
g_{r-2}=&(0&0&\dots&1&0&0&0&\dots&0&1)\\
g_{r-1}=&(0&0&\dots&0&1&0&0&\dots&0&1)\\

g_{r+1}=&(0&0&\dots&0&0&1&0&\dots&0&0)\\
\end{matrix}

Proposition 5.4
.

Suppose that
r=2s
is even. Then each coordinate of an atom
\mathsf{m}\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{r+1})
(where
g_{1},\dots,g_{r+1}
are listed in (
13
)) with
|\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mathsf{m})|<r+1
is divisible by each prime divisor
p
of
n_{r}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathsf{m}=[m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{r+1}]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{r+1})
be an atom, and let
p
be a prime divisor of
n_{r}
. Then we have the congruences

\displaystyle m_{2i-1}+m_{2i}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ \mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits\ n_{i}\quad(i=1,\dots,s),

\displaystyle m_{2i-2}+m_{2i-1}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ \mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits\ n_{s+i}\quad(i=2,\dots,s),

(14)

\displaystyle m_{r+1}-m_{1}\frac{n_{s+1}}{p}

\displaystyle\equiv 0\hbox{ }\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits\hbox{ }n_{s+1}.

Since
p
divides
n_{i}
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
, the congruences can be seen modulo
p
:

(15)

m_{1}\equiv-m_{2}\equiv\dots\equiv m_{r-1}\equiv-m_{r}\hbox{ }\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits\hbox{ }p

If
|\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mathsf{m})|<r+1
, then there exists an
i_{0}\in\{1,\dots,r\}
for which
m_{i_{0}}=0
. If
i_{0}\neq r+1
, then by (
15
) we get that
m_{i}\equiv 0\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

p
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,r\}
. Moreover, writing
m_{1}\equiv 0

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

p
in congruence (
14
) implies that
m_{r+1}\equiv 0

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

n_{s+1}
, so
m_{r+1}\equiv 0

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

p
is also true.

If
i_{0}=r+1
, then
m_{r+1}=0
, so by congruence (
14
) we get that
m_{1}\frac{n_{s+1}}{p}\equiv 0

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

n_{s+1}
. From here,
m_{1}\equiv 0

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

p
, which by chain of congruences in (
15
) implies that
m_{1}\equiv m_{2}\equiv\dots\equiv m_{r}\equiv 0

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

p
. Since
m_{r+1}=0
, we are done.
∎

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
G=C_{n_{1}}\oplus C_{n_{2}}\oplus\dots\oplus C_{n_{r}}
, where
2\leq n_{r}\mid n_{r-1}\mid\dots\mid n_{1}
and
r=2s
. Suppose that
p
is a prime divisor of
n_{r}
. Then
\beta_{sep}(G)\geq n_{1}+\dots+n_{s}+\frac{n_{s+1}}{p}
.

Proof.

We shall apply Lemma
4.1
again, now with

k=r+1
,
i=2
,
d=p
, and

\mathsf{m}=[n_{1}-1,1,n_{2}-1,1,\dots,n_{s}-1,1,\frac{n_{s+1}}{p}]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{r+1})
.

Condition (ii) of Lemma
4.1
obviously holds for
\mathsf{m}
, and by Proposition
5.4
, condition (i) of Lemma
4.1
holds as well. Next let
\mathsf{z}=[z_{1},\dots,z_{r+1}]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{r+1})
be an element with
|\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mathsf{z})|=r+1
. Then
z_{2i-1}+z_{2i}\geq n_{i}
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, moreover,
z_{r+1}-z_{1}\frac{n_{s+1}}{p}\equiv 0

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

n_{s+1}
, which gives that
z_{r+1}\equiv 0

\mathop{\rm mod}\nolimits

\frac{n_{s+1}}{p}
, so
z_{r+1}\geq\frac{n_{s+1}}{p}
. So for any element
\mathsf{z}\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{r+1})
with
|\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(\mathsf{z})|=r+1
we have:

|\mathsf{z}|=(z_{1}+z_{2})+\dots+(z_{r}+z_{r+1})\geq n_{1}+\dots+n_{s}+\frac{n_{s+1}}{p}

Hence the assumption (iii) of Lemma
4.1
is also satisfied by
\mathsf{m}
. Consequently,
\mathsf{m}
is a group atom in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{r+1})
of length
n_{1}+\dots+n_{s}+\frac{n_{s+1}}{p}
by Lemma
4.1
.
∎

6.
Main results and their proofs

Theorem 6.1
.

Assume that
2\leq n_{2s-1}\mid n_{2s-2}\mid\dots\mid n_{s+1}\mid n_{s}=n_{s-1}=\dots=n_{1}
.
Then we have

\beta_{sep}(C_{n_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus C_{n_{2s-1}})=sn_{1}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.2
we get that
\beta_{sep}(C_{n_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus~C_{n_{2s-1}})\geq n_{1}+n_{2}+\dots+n_{s}=sn_{1}
, while
\beta_{sep}(C_{n_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus C_{n_{2s-1}})\leq\lfloor\frac{2sn_{1}}{2}\rfloor=sn_{1}
by Corollary
3.2
. So
\beta_{sep}(C_{n_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus C_{n_{2s-1}})=sn_{1}
.
∎

Theorem 6.2
.

Assume that
2\leq n_{2s}\mid n_{2s-1}\mid\dots\mid n_{s+2}\mid n_{s+1}=\dots=n_{1}
and that the minimal prime divisor
p
of
n_{1}
divides
n_{2s}
. Then
\beta_{sep}(C_{n_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus C_{n_{2s}})=sn_{1}+\frac{n_{1}}{p}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.5
we have that
\beta_{sep}(C_{n_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus C_{n_{2s}})\geq n_{1}s+\frac{n_{1}}{p}
. Suppose that
\beta_{sep}(C_{n_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus C_{n_{2s}})>n_{1}s+\frac{n_{1}}{p}
. This implies that there exist elements
g_{1},\dots,g_{2s+1}\in G
for which there exists a group atom
\mathsf{m}=[m_{1},\dots,m_{2s+1}]\in\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{2s+1})
with
|\mathsf{m}|>n_{1}s+\frac{n_{1}}{p}
. By Lemma
3.1
(ii), the inequality
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{1})+\dots+\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{2s+1})\geq 2|\mathsf{m}|>2(n_{1}s+\frac{n_{1}}{p})
also holds. This can only happen, if
\mathop{\rm ord}\nolimits(g_{i})=n_{1}
for each
i\in\{1,\dots,2s+1\}
. So the first condition of Lemma
4.3
is satisfied.

Since
p
is the minimal divisor of both of
n_{2s}
and
n_{1}
, the condition (ii) of Lemma
4.3
is also satisfied. So
|\mathsf{m}|
can not be a group atom in
\mathcal{B}(g_{1},\dots,g_{2s+1})
. This contradiction implies that
\beta_{sep}(C_{n_{1}}\oplus\dots\oplus C_{n_{2s}})=n_{1}s+\frac{n_{1}}{p}
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem
1.2
.

Statement (i) is the special case
n_{1}=\dots=n_{2s-1}=n
of
Theorem
6.1
, whereas (ii) is the special case
n_{1}=\dots=n_{2s}=n

of Theorem
6.2
.

\square

Hybrid Statistics of a Random Model of Zeta over Intervals of Varying Length
Christine Chang

Abstract
Arguin, Dubach & Hartung recently conjectured that an intermediate regime exists between IID and log-correlated statistics for extreme values of a random model of the Riemann zeta function. For the same model, we prove a matching upper and lower tail for the distribution of its maximum. This tail interpolates between that of the two aforementioned regimes. We apply the result to yield a new sharp estimate on moments over short intervals, generalizing a result by Harper. In particular, we observe a hybrid regime for moments with a distinctive transition to the IID regime for intervals of length larger than exp ⁡ ( log ⁡ log ⁡ T ) \exp(\sqrt{\log\log T}) .



1
Introduction

The Riemann zeta function is defined for
\Re(s)>1
where
s\in{\mathbb{C}}
as

(1)

\zeta(s)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n^{s}}=\prod_{p\geq 1}\frac{1}{1-p^{-s}},

and by analytic continuation otherwise.
Fyodorov, Hiary & Keating
[
FHK12
]
and Fyodorov & Hiary
[
FK14
]
were the first to study the local maximum of zeta over smaller, randomly chosen intervals. Their conjecture gives an upper bound on the distribution of the local maximum of zeta over an interval of order one.
Let
\tau
be uniformly distributed on
[T,2T]
,
y>0
, and
y=O\left(\frac{\log\log T}{\log\log\log T}\right)
. Arguin, Bourgade & Radziwill (
[
ABR20
]
,
[
ABR23
]
) prove the upper and lower bound of the conjecture and show that

(2)

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{|h|\leq 1}|\zeta(1/2+i(\tau+h))|>\frac{\log T}{(\log\log T)^{3/4}}e^{y}\right)\asymp ye^{-2y}e^{-y^{2}/\log\log T}.

There is however a discrepancy in the subleading order exponent and right tail when considering the maximum over intervals of size
(\log T)^{\theta}
, where
\theta>0
is fixed. Let
y=o\left(\frac{\log\log T}{\log\log\log T}\right)
. Then for
0\leq\theta<3
, Arguin & Bailey
[
AB23
]
prove that

(3)

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}|\zeta(1/2+i(\tau+h))|>\frac{(\log T)^{\sqrt{1+\theta}}}{(\log\log T)^{\frac{1}{4\sqrt{1+\theta}}}}e^{y}\right)\ll e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/\log\log T}.

The notation
\ll
denotes big O notation, further explained in Section
1.2
.
Notice that as
\theta\to 0
, there is a jump discontinuity between (
2
) and (
3
) (i.e., when
\theta=0
and
\theta>0
, respectively). The interval of length
(\log T)^{\theta}
approaches an interval of length one, but the subleading order exponent jumps from
\frac{1}{4\sqrt{1+\theta}}
to
3/4
; the right tail gains a factor of
y
. The discrepancy between the subleading order terms was addressed recently for a random model of zeta – Arguin, Dubach & Hartung
[
ADH24
]
introduced the expression
\frac{1+2\alpha}{4\sqrt{1+\theta}}
where
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
\theta=t^{-\alpha}
. This term interpolates between
\frac{1}{4\sqrt{1+\theta}}
and
3/4
. The same subleading order prefactor,
1+2\alpha
, was given in the random energy model setting
[
Kis15
,
SK15
]
.

The main result of this paper (Theorem
1
) addresses the right tail discontinuity. We continue the work of
[
ADH24
]
on a random model of
|\zeta|
, defined as

(4)

R_{T}(h)=\exp\left({\sum_{p\leq T}}\Re\dfrac{(G_{p}p^{-ih})}{p^{1/2}}\right),

where the
G_{p}
are IID complex standard normal random variables, indexed by prime numbers
p
(cf. Section 1.1). We give a matching upper and lower bound for the distribution of its maximum over intervals of varying length. Observe that the prefactor of the tail in Theorem
1
interpolates between
1
and
y
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
T
be large, let
y>0
and
y=O\left(\frac{\log\log T}{\log\log\log T}\right)
. Let
\theta=(\log\log T)^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}R_{T}(h)>\frac{(\log T)^{\sqrt{1+\theta}}}{(\log\log T)^{\frac{1+2\alpha}{4\sqrt{1+\theta}}}}e^{y}\right)\asymp\left(1+\frac{y}{(\log\log T)^{1-\alpha}}\right)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{\log\log T}}.

Theorem
1
proves the general case for when
y
grows slowly with
T
. Observe that for
y
at most
(\log\log T)^{1-\alpha}
, the tail is
\ll e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/\log\log T}
. On the other hand, for
y
in the large deviation regime, i.e. when
y
is larger than
(\log\log T)^{1-\alpha}
, the tail is
\ll\left(1+\frac{y}{(\log\log T)^{1-\alpha}}\right)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{\log\log T}}
. A nontrivial consequence is a new normalization for the moments of zeta over short intervals. By Theorem
1
, we prove the following corollary.

Corollary 1
.

For
\beta_{c}=2\sqrt{1+\theta}
and
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
, we have for
A>0
,

(5)

\frac{1}{(\log T)^{\theta}}\int_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}(R_{T}(h))^{\beta_{c}}\;dh\asymp_{A}\frac{(\log T)^{\frac{\beta_{c}^{2}}{4}}}{(\log\log T)^{\alpha-1/2}},

for all
t\in[T,2T]
except possibly on a subset of Lebesgue measure
\ll 1/A
.

In relation to other work, for
\theta=0
, a consequence of Theorem 1.9 from
[
SW20
]
shows that

\dfrac{\sqrt{\log\log T}}{\log T}\int_{|h|\leq 1}|R_{T}(h)|^{2}\;dh

converges in distribution as
T\to\infty
. Now, we note that Corollary
1
also holds for
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
, though the result is not sharp. Arguin & Bailey
[
AB23
]
prove an upper bound for all
\theta>0
for the Riemann zeta function:

(6)

\frac{1}{(\log T)^{\theta}}\int_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}|\zeta(1/2+it+ih)|^{\beta_{c}}\;dh\ll A(\log T)^{\frac{\beta_{c}^{2}}{4}}.

This holds for all
t\in[T,2T]
except possibly on a subset of Lebesgue measure
\ll 1/A
. Hence, for
\alpha\in(0,1/2]
, we expect no correction for the random model.

Now, equation (
6
) corresponds to what is known for the IID regime where
\theta>0
and no correction is expected
[
BKL02
]
. But for the log-correlated regime (
\theta=0
), Harper gives the correction
1/\sqrt{\log\log T}
:

Theorem 2

(Theorem 1 and Corollary 1 in
[
Har19
]
)
.

Uniformly for all large
T
, we have

\int_{|h|\leq 1}|\zeta(1/2+it+ih)|^{2}dh\leq A\frac{\log T}{\sqrt{\log\log T}}

for all
t\in[T,2T]
except possibly on a subset of Lebesgue measure
\ll\frac{(\log A)\wedge\sqrt{\log\log T}}{A}.

Corollary
1
yields the normalization
1/(\log\log T)^{\alpha-1/2}
for
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
and generalizes Harper’s result in the setting of the random model (
4
) . This correction interpolates between
1/\sqrt{\log\log T}
and
1
, i.e. between the correction for the log-correlated regime and that of the IID regime. In contrast, for
\alpha\leq 1/2
, i.e. for intervals that have length greater than
\exp(\sqrt{\log\log T})
, we observe a transition into the IID regime, as shown in Arguin & Bailey’s result (
6
). A similar threshold at
\alpha=1/2
was noted in the realm of random multiplicative functions
[
Xu23
]
.

In addition to proving a bound for the right tail of the maximum, we prove a bound for its left tail:

Theorem 3
.

Let
T
be large, let
y<0
and
y=O\left(\frac{\log\log T}{\log\log\log T}\right)
. Let
\theta=(\log\log T)^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}R_{T}(h)>\frac{(\log T)^{\sqrt{1+\theta}}}{(\log\log T)^{\frac{1+2\alpha}{4\sqrt{1+\theta}}}}e^{y}\right)=1-O\left(\frac{e^{2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}}{1-y}\right),

as
t\to\infty
.

Theorem
1
and Theorem
3
imply tightness of the sequence

\max_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}\log(R_{T}(h))-\left(\sqrt{1+\theta}\log\log T-\frac{1+2\alpha}{4\sqrt{1+\theta}}\log\log\log T\right),

for large enough
T
. The order of magnitude of the maximum was used to conjecture the size of pseudomoments
[
Ger20
]
.

Finally, Theorem
1
and Corollary
1
motivate the following conjectures for the Riemann zeta function, respectively. The first part of Conjecture
1
extends the conjecture stated on Pg. 2 of
[
ADH24
]
.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
\theta=(\log\log T)^{-\alpha}
. Then

\max_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}|\zeta(1/2+i\tau+ih)|=\frac{(\log T)^{\sqrt{1+\theta}}}{(\log\log T)^{\frac{1+2\alpha}{4\sqrt{1+\theta}}}}e^{\mathcal{M}_{T}},

where
\tau
is uniformly distributed on
[T,2T]
, and (
\mathcal{M}_{T},T>1)
is a tight sequence of random variables converging as
T\to\infty
to a random variable
\mathcal{M}
with right tail

{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{M}>y)\sim\left(1+\frac{y}{(\log\log T)^{1-\alpha}}\right)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{\log\log T}}.

Moreover, for
\beta_{c}=2\sqrt{1+\theta}
and
\alpha\in(1/2,1)
, we have for
A>0
,

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\frac{1}{(\log T)^{\theta}}\int_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}|\zeta(1/2+it)|^{\beta_{c}}dt>A\frac{(\log T)^{\frac{\beta_{c}^{2}}{4}}}{(\log\log T)^{\alpha-1/2}}\right)\ll\frac{1}{A}.

1.1
The Random Model of
\log|\zeta|

To study
\log|\zeta|
on a short interval
I
on the critical axis, we consider its random model:

(7)

\log(R_{T}(h))={\sum_{p\leq T}}\Re\dfrac{(G_{p}p^{-ih})}{p^{1/2}},\;\;\;h\in I.

To obtain (
7
), we first take the logarithm of the Euler Product (
1
) at
s=1/2+i(\tau+h)
, where
\tau
is chosen uniformly from
[T,2T]
and
h\in I
. Next, we perform a Taylor expansion and obtain

(8)

\log\zeta(1/2+i(\tau+h))\approx\log\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{p^{1/2+i(\tau+h)}}\right)\approx\sum_{p\leq T}\dfrac{p^{-i\tau}p^{-ih}}{p^{1/2}}.

We then identify
p^{-i\tau}
with
G_{p}
, as defined in (
4
). After taking the real part of the expression in (
8
), we obtain
\log(R_{T}(h))
, the random model of
\log|\zeta|
. The process
\log(R_{T}(h))
is Gaussian with mean 0 and variance that is approximately
(\log\log T)/2
.

To evoke the branching structure of the process, we rewrite
\log(R_{T}(h))
as the sum of IID increments by using a multiscale decomposition. To that end, write
J=e^{e^{j}}
,
K=e^{e^{k}}
and
T=e^{e^{t}}
. For any
h\in I
, consider the partial sum

(9)

S_{j}(h)=\sum_{m=1}^{j}Y_{m}(h),\;\;\;\text{for all}\;\;j\leq t\;\;\;\text{where}\;\;\;\;Y_{m}(h)=\sum_{e^{m-1}<\log p\leq e^{m}}\Re\frac{(G_{p}p^{-ih})}{p^{1/2}}.

The increments
Y_{m}
are Gaussian and have an approximate variance of
1/2
by Merten’s estimate, which states that for any
x\geq 1
and some constant
C>0
, we have

\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{1}{p}=\log\log x+C+O\left(\frac{1}{\log x}\right).

Since the increments are not perfectly IID, the random model
S_{j}(h)
is an approximate random walk for every
h
. Throughout the proofs, we primarily use the partial sum notation. Observe that
S_{j}(h)=\log(R_{J}(h))
.

We will often consider the process
S_{j}(h)
and its covariance at varying subintervals of time as done in
[
ADH24
]
. Define the process
S_{k,l}(h)
where
1\leq k<l\leq t
:

(10)

S_{k,l}(h)=\sum_{m=k}^{l}Y_{m}(h),\;\;\;|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}.

Let
h,h^{{}^{\prime}}\in[-(\log T)^{\theta},(\log T)^{\theta}]
. To find the covariance
{\mathbb{E}}[S_{k,l}(h),S_{k,l}(h^{{}^{\prime}})]
, we perform straightforward computations and use the laws of
G_{p}
to obtain

(11)

{\mathbb{E}}[S_{k,l}(h)\cdot S_{k,l}(h^{{}^{\prime}})]=\sum_{k\leq m<l}\;\sum_{e^{m-1}<\log p\leq e^{m}}\frac{\cos(h-h^{{}^{\prime}})\log p}{2p}.

The following corollary shows that the covariance (
11
) depends on the distance between the two points
h,h^{{}^{\prime}}
:

Corollary 2
.

Define
S_{k,l}(h)
as in (
10
). For
h,h^{{}^{\prime}}\in[-(\log T)^{\theta},(\log T)^{\theta}]
, we have

(12)

{\mathbb{E}}[S_{k,l}(h),S_{k,l}(h^{{}^{\prime}})]=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}(l-k)+O(e^{2l}|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|^{2})&\text{if }|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|<e^{-l}\\
O(e^{-k}|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|^{-1})&\text{if }|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|>e^{-k}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Use (
11
) and apply Lemma 2.2 of
[
ADH24
]
.
∎

Notice that when
|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|<e^{-l}
, the covariance is nearly equal to the variance; hence, two random variables
S_{k,l}(h)
and
S_{k,l}(h^{{}^{\prime}})
are roughly the same. But when
|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|>e^{-k}
, the two random variables decorrelate as
h
and
h^{{}^{\prime}}
grow further apart.

1.2
Proof Outline

To prove the upper bound of the right tail (Theorem
1
), we adopt the method from Theorem 1.3 in
[
ADH24
]
, but perform a union bound over
\ll e^{k+t\theta}
discrete points since
S_{k}(h)\approx S_{k}(h^{\prime})
whenever
|h-h^{\prime}|<e^{-k}
. We use a barrier that is similar in spirit to the one in
[
ADH24
]
, but include an extra term
(1-k/t)t^{1-\alpha}
due to the larger interval size. This causes the barrier to be high and ineffective in constraining process values for
k\in[0,t-t^{\alpha})
. We thus omit the barrier for this range. The main contribution comes from the latter range of
k
where applying the ballot theorem contributes significantly to the new tail bound. Details are provided in Section 2.

The proof for the lower bound of the right tail (Theorem
1
) and for the left tail bound (Theorem
3
) uses a Paley-Zygmund inequality argument. Both proofs are similar to the argument in Section 4 of
[
ADH24
]
, but employ a different barrier and slope. We consider negative values of
y
for Theorem
3
.

For Corollary
1
, a heuristic for the proof is provided on pg. 26 in
[
AB23
]
, but we give a rigorous argument in the spirit of Corollary 1.4 and use a
good
event that includes the barrier used in Theorem
1
(cf. equation (
74
)). This modification induces an application of the tail bound from Theorem
1
, which yields the new correction term.

For general literature on extreme values of the Riemann zeta function, we refer the reader to sources such as
[
Arg17
]
,
[
ABH17
]
and
[
BK22
]
.

Notation.
We state the notation commonly used throughout the paper. We write

or

C\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}

|f(x)|\leq Cg(x)
for all
x\geq x_{0}
. The expression
f(x)\asymp g(x)
means that both
f(x)\ll g(x)
and
f(x)\gg g(x)

T

\log\log

2
Upper Bound of the Right Tail

The upper bound of the right tail in Theorem
1
is proved in Proposition
2.1
. The left bound of the right tail is proved in the next section.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
T
be large, let
y>0
and
y=O\left(\frac{\log\log T}{\log\log\log T}\right)
. Let
\theta=(\log\log T)^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}R_{T}(h)>\frac{(\log T)^{\sqrt{1+\theta}}}{(\log\log T)^{\frac{1+2\alpha}{4\sqrt{1+\theta}}}}e^{y}\right)\ll\left(1+\frac{y}{(\log\log T)^{1-\alpha}}\right)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{\log\log T}}.

Proof.

Let
\mu
denote the slope

(13)

\mu=\sqrt{1+\theta}-\dfrac{1+2\alpha}{4\sqrt{1+\theta}}\frac{\log t}{t}.

Define

(14)

b(k)=(1-k/t)t^{1-\alpha}+\frac{11}{4\alpha}\psi(k)+1,\;\;\text{for any }\;0\leq k\leq t,

where the logarithmic ”bump function”
\psi(k)
is

(15)

\psi(k)=\begin{cases}\log(\min(k,t-k)),&\text{if }1\leq k\leq t-1\\
0&\text{if }k\in\{0,t\}.\end{cases}

We define the barrier
M(k)
to be

(16)

M(k)=\mu k+y+b(k),\;\;\text{for any }\;0\leq k\leq t.

In the definition of
b(k)
, we multiply
\psi(k)
by
\frac{11}{4\alpha}
to ensure convergence of sums (cf. equations (
24
), (
36
)).

The key idea of the proof is to consider the
first hitting time
of the process. We decompose the main event over when the maximum of the process first crosses the barrier, say at
k+1
:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}S_{t}(h)>M(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}{\mathbb{P}}(\max_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}S_{t}(h)>M(t),\;\;\max_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}S_{j}(h)<M(j);\forall j\leq k,\;

(17)

\displaystyle\hskip 227.62204pt\max_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}S_{k+1}(h)>M(k+1)).

We drop the first event in (
17
) and estimate the following sum over two ranges of
k
:

(18)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{t-1}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}S_{j}(h)<M(j),\forall j\leq k,\;\max_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}S_{k+1}(h)>M(k+1)\right).

We base the two cases on when the barrier
M(k)
does not contribute to sharp estimates. Indeed, observe that at
k=0
, the barrier
M(k)
is high at
t^{1-\alpha}+y
and continues to far exceed the process values for
k\leq t-t^{\alpha}
. Hence, the barrier
M(j)
for all
j\leq k
is included only for
k>t-t^{\alpha}
. The dominant contribution comes from the latter range of
k
.

\bullet\textbf{ Case 1}:0<k\leq t-t^{\alpha}

First, we omit the barrier for all
j\leq k
from the event in (
18
). Next, we divide the interval
[-(\log T)^{\theta},(\log T)^{\theta}]
into subintervals of length
e^{-(k+1)}
and perform a union bound:

(19)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{t-t^{\alpha}}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}S_{k+1}(h)>M(k+1)\right)\ll\sum_{k=0}^{t-t^{\alpha}}e^{k+1+t\theta}\;{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{|h|\leq e^{-(k+1)}}S_{k+1}(h)>M(k+1)\right).

To handle the probability on the right hand side of (
19
), we use the following lemma:

Lemma 2.1

(Lemma 3.6 of
[
ADH24
]
)
.

Let
C>0
and
1\leq j\leq t
. For any
1<y\leq C_{j}
, we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{|h|\leq e^{-j}}S_{j}(h)>y\right)\ll_{C}\frac{e^{-y^{2}/j}}{j^{1/2}},

where
\ll_{C}
means that the implicit constant depends on
C
.

The essential observation of Lemma
2.1
is that the maximum of
S_{k+1}
over a subinterval of size
e^{-(k+1)}
behaves like a single Gaussian random variable with mean 0 and variance
k+1
. Hence, by Lemma
2.1
, the inequality (
19
) is bounded by

(20)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{k=0}^{t-t^{\alpha}}e^{k+1+t\theta}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{k+1}}\exp\left(-\dfrac{(M(k+1))^{2}}{k+1}\right).

The inequality (
20
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{k=0}^{t-t^{\alpha}}\dfrac{\exp\left(k+t\theta-\mu^{2}(k+1)-2\mu b(k+1)-2\mu y-y^{2}/(k+1)\right)}{\sqrt{k+1}}

(21)

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{k=0}^{t-t^{\alpha}}\dfrac{\exp((k-t)\theta+\dfrac{1+2\alpha}{2\sqrt{1+\theta}}\log t\cdot\dfrac{k+1}{t}-\frac{11}{2\alpha}\psi(k+1)-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y-y^{2}/t)}{\sqrt{k+1}}.

We have the estimate

(22)

\exp(-2\mu y)\ll e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta y}},

since
y=O(\frac{t}{\log t}).
Also, since
(\log x)/x
is a decreasing function for
x>e
, we deduce that

(23)

\dfrac{1+2\alpha}{2\sqrt{1+\theta}}\log t\cdot\dfrac{k+1}{t}\leq\dfrac{1+2\alpha}{2\sqrt{1+\theta}}\log(k+1).

Then by (
23
), and since
e^{(k-t)\theta}\leq e^{-c}
when
k<t-t^{\alpha}
for some constant
c
, equation (
21
) is

(24)

\displaystyle\ll e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t}\;\sum_{k=0}^{t-t^{\alpha}}\frac{\exp\left(\dfrac{1+2\alpha}{2\sqrt{1+\theta}}\log(k+1)-\frac{11}{2\alpha}\psi(k+1)\right)}{\sqrt{k+1}}.

Finally, since the log bump function
\psi(k)
is symmetric around
t/2
, we have:

(25)

\log(\min(k,t-k))=\begin{cases}\log k&\mbox{ if $0<k<t/2$}\\
\log(t-k)&\mbox{ if $t/2<k\leq t-t^{\alpha}$ }.\end{cases}

After considering the two ranges in (
25
), the sum (
24
) simplifies to
\ll e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t}
. This concludes the upper bound estimate for
0\leq k\leq t-t^{\alpha}
.

\bullet\textbf{ Case 2: }t-t^{\alpha}<k\leq t

We consider the sum (
18
) for
t-t^{\alpha}<k\leq t
. By a union bound and translation invariance of the distribution, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=t-t^{\alpha}}^{t}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}S_{j}(h)<M(j);\forall j\leq k,\;\max_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}S_{k+1}(h)>M(k+1)\right)

(26)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=t-t^{\alpha}}^{t}e^{k+1+t\theta}\;{\mathbb{P}}\left(S_{j}(0)<M(j);\forall j\leq k,\;\;\max_{|h|\leq e^{-{k}}}S_{k+1}(h)>M(k+1)\right).

To find the probability in (
26
), we first decompose the event over all possible values
u
of the process at time
k
. For ease of notation, we define

(27)

\mathcal{B}_{k+1}(u)=\{S_{j}(0)<M(j);\forall j\leq k,\;S_{k+1}(0)\in[u,u+1]\}.

Using (
27
), the probability in (
26
) is

(28)

\sum_{u\leq M(k)}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\mathcal{B}_{k+1}(u),\;\;\max_{|h|\leq e^{-{k}}}S_{k+1}(h)>M(k+1)\right).

We will need two estimates for (
28
) since there are two cases for how the maximum of
S_{k+1}(h)
over
|h|\leq e^{-k}
can exceed
M(k+1)
: either the difference
S_{k+1}(h)-S_{k+1}(0)
is large for some
|h|\leq e^{-k}
or the increment
S_{k+1}(0)-S_{k}(0)
is large. Both cases yield the same estimate. We mainly focus on proving the second case.

For the first case, assume that the difference
S_{k+1}(h)-S_{k+1}(0)
is large for some
|h|\leq e^{-k}
. Then, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{k=t-t^{\alpha}}^{t}e^{k+1+t\theta}\;\sum_{u\leq M(k)}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\mathcal{B}_{k+1}(u),\;\;\max_{|h|\leq e^{-{k}}}S_{k+1}(h)-S_{k+1}(0)>M(k+1)-u\right)

(29)

\displaystyle\ll\left(1+\frac{y}{t^{1-\alpha}}\right)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t}.

For the proof of the first case, we defer to Lemma 3.2 of
[
ADH24
]
where the same argument is used, but with modifications to the barrier and interval size. We use the barrier
M(k)
as defined in (
16
), which changes the estimate in equation 3.34 of
[
ADH24
]
.

For the second case, we assume that the increment
S_{k+1}(0)-S_{k}(0)
is large. Then (
28
) is

(30)

\displaystyle\sum_{u\leq M(k)}{\mathbb{P}}\left(\mathcal{B}_{k+1}(u),\;S_{k+1}(0)-S_{k}(0)>M(k+1)-u-1\right).

By independence of Gaussian increments, the sum (
30
) is

(31)

\displaystyle\sum_{u\leq M(k)}{\mathbb{P}}(\mathcal{B}_{k+1}(u))\cdot{\mathbb{P}}\left(S_{k+1}(0)-S_{k}(0)>M(k+1)-u-1\right).

It remains to find the two probabilities in (
31
). For the first probability in (
31
), an application of the Ballot Theorem
A.2
gives

(32)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(S_{j}(h)<M(j);\forall j\leq k,\;S_{k}\in[u,u+1]\right)\ll\dfrac{e^{-u^{2}/k}}{k^{3/2}}(t^{1-\alpha}+y)(M(k)-u-1).

For the second probability in (
31
), a Gaussian estimate yields

(33)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}\left(S_{k+1}(0)-S_{k}(0)>M(k+1)-u-1\right)\ll e^{-(M(k+1)-u-1)^{2}}.

After substituting the two probability bounds (
32
) and (
33
) into the sum (
31
), we have

\displaystyle\ll\sum_{u\leq M(k)}(M(k)-u-1)\cdot(t^{1-\alpha}+y)k^{-3/2}e^{-(M(k+1)-u-1)^{2}-u^{2}/k}

(34)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{v\geq 0}(t^{1-\alpha}+y)(v-1)\cdot k^{-3/2}e^{-\frac{(M(k)-v)^{2}}{k}-(v+O(1))^{2}}.

A change of variable is performed in line (
34
) with
v=M(k)-u
denoting the distance from the process
S_{k}(h)
to the barrier
M(k)
. (Recall that
u
represents all possible values of
S_{k}(h)
.) This concludes the estimate for the probability in (
26
).

All that remains is to estimate the sum (
26
). Substituting the probability bound (
34
) into (
26
), we obtain

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=t-t^{\alpha}}^{t}e^{k+1+t\theta}\left(\sum_{v\geq 0}\dfrac{e^{-\mu^{2}k-2b(k)-2\mu y-\frac{y^{2}}{k}+2\mu v}}{k^{3/2}}(v-1)(t^{1-\alpha}+y)e^{-(v+O(1))^{2}}\right)

(35)

\displaystyle\ll(t^{1-\alpha}+y)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t}\sum_{k=t-t^{\alpha}}^{t}\frac{e^{k+1+t\theta}e^{-\mu^{2}k-2b(k)}}{k^{3/2}}\sum_{v\geq 0}(v-1)e^{2v-(v+O(1))^{2}}.

Observe that the sum over
v
is finite. After simplifying (
35
), we obtain

\displaystyle\ll(t^{1-\alpha}+y)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t}\sum_{k=t-t^{\alpha}}^{t}\frac{e^{-(t-k)\theta+\frac{1+2\alpha}{2}\log k-\frac{11}{2\alpha}\psi(k)}}{k^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\ll t^{1-\alpha}\left(1+\frac{y}{t^{1-\alpha}}\right)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t}\sum_{k=t-t^{\alpha}}^{t}k^{\alpha-1}e^{-(t-k)\theta-\frac{11}{2\alpha}\log(t-k)}

(36)

\displaystyle\ll\left(1+\frac{y}{t^{1-\alpha}}\right)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t}\sum_{k=t-t^{\alpha}}^{t}\left(t/k\right)^{1-\alpha}(t-k)^{-\frac{11}{2\alpha}}

(37)

\displaystyle\ll\left(1+\frac{y}{t^{1-\alpha}}\right)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t}.

The estimates for the first (
29
) and second case (
37
) are the same. This concludes the upper bound estimate for
t-t^{\alpha}<k\leq t
.
∎

3
Lower Bound of the Right Tail

In this section, we prove a lower bound to Theorem
1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
T
be large, let
y>0
and
y=O\left(\frac{\log\log T}{\log\log\log T}\right)
. Let
\theta=(\log\log T)^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\max_{|h|\leq(\log T)^{\theta}}e^{X_{T}(h)}>\frac{(\log T)^{\sqrt{1+\theta}}}{(\log\log T)^{\frac{1+2\alpha}{4\sqrt{1+\theta}}}}e^{y}\right)\gg\left(1+\frac{y}{(\log\log T)^{1-\alpha}}\right)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-\frac{y^{2}}{\log\log T}}.

Before we prove Proposition
3.1
, we introduce a few definitions. Consider the following set of discrete points

(38)

\mathcal{H}=[-e^{t\theta},e^{t\theta}]\cap e^{-t}\mathbb{Z},

separated by a distance of
e^{-t}
.
To prove Proposition
3.1
, we use the Paley-Zygmund inequality to compare the first and second moment of the
number of exceedances

(39)

Z_{\delta}=\sum_{h\in\mathcal{H}}1_{J(h)}

of the event

\displaystyle J(h)=\{S_{t}(h)\in[\mu t+y,\mu t+y+\delta],\;S_{k}(h)<M(k);\forall\;k\in[1,t]\cap\mathbb{Z}\}

for some fixed
\delta>0
. Recall that the slope
\mu=\sqrt{1+\theta}-\frac{1+2\alpha}{4\sqrt{1+\theta}}\frac{\log t}{t}
. Define the barrier
M(k)
to be

(40)

\displaystyle M(k)=\mu k+\left(1-\frac{k}{t}\right)t^{1-\alpha}+y+1,\;\;\text{for any}\;\;0\leq k\leq t.

We establish bounds for the first and second moment of
Z_{\delta}
in the following two lemmas:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
Z_{\delta}
be as defined in
(\ref{exceedances})
with
\delta>0
. Then we have

{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}]\gg\left(1+\frac{y}{t^{1-\alpha}}\right)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t},

where
y(t)\to\infty.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
Z_{\delta}
be as defined in
(
39
)
with
\delta>0
. Then we have

{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}^{2}]\ll\left(1+\frac{y}{t^{1-\alpha}}\right)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t},

where
y(t)\to\infty
.

The proof of Lemma
3.1
is a straightforward application of the Ballot Theorem
A.1
. We mainly focus on the proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

By Lemma
3.1
, Lemma
3.2
and the Paley-Zygmund inequality, we have

{\mathbb{P}}(Z_{\delta}\geq 1)\geq\dfrac{({\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}])^{2}}{{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}^{2}]}\gg\left(1+\frac{y}{t^{1-\alpha}}\right)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t}.

∎

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

By linearity of expectation, Fubini’s Theorem, and translation invariance of the distribution, we have

{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}]=2e^{t+t\theta}\;{\mathbb{P}}(J(0)).

By the Ballot Theorem
A.1
, we obtain

(41)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(J(0))\gg\frac{t^{1-\alpha}+y}{t}\left(\frac{e^{\frac{-(\mu t+y)^{2}}{t}}}{\sqrt{t}}\right)=\left(1+\frac{y}{t^{1-\alpha}}\right)t^{-1/2-\alpha}e^{-\frac{(\mu t+y)^{2}}{t}}.

The exponential in (
41
) simplifies to

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{(\mu t+y)^{2}}{t}}=e^{-(1+\theta)t}t^{1/2+\alpha}e^{-O((\log^{2}t)/t)}e^{-2\mu y-y^{2}/t}.

It follows that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(J(0))\gg\left(1+\frac{y}{t^{1-\alpha}}\right)e^{-(1+\theta)t}e^{-2\mu y-y^{2}/t}.

Recall that
\exp(-2\mu y)\ll e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta y}}
since
y=O(\frac{t}{\log t}).
It follows that

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}]=2e^{t+t\theta}\;{\mathbb{P}}(J(0))\gg\left(1+\frac{y}{t^{1-\alpha}}\right)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{\frac{-y^{2}}{t}}.

∎

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

We first express the second moment as a sum over all possible pairs
(h,h^{{}^{\prime}})
in
I=[-e^{t\theta},e^{t\theta}]
:

(42)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}[(Z_{\delta})^{2}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(h,h^{{}^{\prime}})\in I\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{P}}(J(h)\cap J(h^{{}^{\prime}}))\leq(\#{\mathcal{H}})\sum_{|h|\leq e^{t\theta}}{\mathbb{P}}(J(h)\cap J(0)).

By translation invariance of the distribution, we let
h^{{}^{\prime}}=0
. For precise estimates, we divide
|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|<e^{t\theta}
into three cases to evoke the covariance structure of the processes. The three cases are
1<|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|\leq e^{t\theta/2}
,
|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|>e^{t\theta/2},
and
|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|\leq 1
. The main contribution comes from
|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|\leq 1
. In particular, there is a coupling or decoupling of walks in each case for which we use the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3

(Lemma 7 of
[
ABR23
]
)
.

Let
|\rho|<\mathfrak{s}^{2}
. Consider the following Gaussian vectors and their covariance matrices:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{1}^{{}^{\prime}}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathfrak{s}^{2}&\rho\\
\rho&\mathfrak{s}^{2}\\
\end{array}}\right),

\displaystyle(\mathcal{N}_{2},\mathcal{N}_{2}^{{}^{\prime}}),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{2}=\left({\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathfrak{s}^{2}+|\rho|&0\\
0&\mathfrak{s}^{2}+|\rho|\\
\end{array}}\right).

Then for any measurable set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have

{\mathbb{P}}((\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{1}^{{}^{\prime}})\in A)\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mathfrak{s}^{2}+|\rho|}{\mathfrak{s}^{2}-|\rho|}}\cdot{\mathbb{P}}((\mathcal{N}_{2},\mathcal{N}_{2}^{{}^{\prime}})\in A).

The proof of Lemma
3.3
is straighforward and entails expanding the covariance matrix. See Lemma 7 of
[
ABR23
]
for more details.

\bullet\textbf{ Case 1: }1<|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|\leq e^{t\theta/2}

Observe that there are
\ll e^{t\theta/2+t}
number of
h^{\prime}
s of length
e^{-t}
in an interval of length
2(e^{t\theta/2}-1).
Hence,

(43)

\displaystyle(\#\mathcal{H})\sum_{1<|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|\leq e^{t\theta/2}}{\mathbb{P}}(J(h)\cap J(h^{{}^{\prime}}))\ll e^{t(1+\theta)}e^{t\theta/2+t}\;{\mathbb{P}}(J(h)\cap J(0)).

We use translation invariance of the distribution in (
43
). To find
{\mathbb{P}}(J(h)\cap J(0))
, we use Lemma
3.3
and the Ballot Theorem
A.2
:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(J(h)\cap J(0))

\displaystyle\ll{\mathbb{P}}(J(0))^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\left(\frac{(t^{1-\alpha}+y+1)}{t}\frac{\exp(-(\mu t+y)^{2}/t)}{t^{1/2}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\ll\left(\frac{(t^{1-\alpha}+y+1)}{t^{3/2}}\exp(-\mu^{2}t+\frac{1+2\alpha}{2}\log t-2\mu y-y^{2}/t)\right)^{2}

(44)

\displaystyle\ll\left(\left(1+\frac{y}{t^{1-\alpha}}\right)\exp(-t(1+\theta)-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y-y^{2}/t)\right)^{2}.

After applying the probability estimate (
44
) to the inequality (
43
), we obtain

(45)

\displaystyle(\#\mathcal{H})\sum_{1<|h|\leq e^{t\theta/2}}{\mathbb{P}}(J(h)\cap J(0))\ll\left(\left(1+\frac{y}{t^{1-\alpha}}\right)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t}\right)^{2}e^{-t\theta/2}.

Recall that
{\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}]\gg\left(1+\frac{y}{t^{1-\alpha}}\right)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t}
. Hence, the estimate (
45
) is
o({\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}]^{2}).

\bullet\textbf{ Case 2: }|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|>e^{t\theta/2}

We proceed as in the previous argument. Since there are
2e^{t+t\theta}(1-o(1))\ll e^{t+t\theta}
number of
h^{\prime}
s of length
e^{-t}
in an interval of length
2(e^{t\theta}-e^{t\theta/2})
, we obtain

(46)

\displaystyle(\#\mathcal{H})\sum_{|h|>e^{t\theta/2}}{\mathbb{P}}(J(h)\cap J(0))\ll\left(\left(1+\frac{y}{t^{1-\alpha}}\right)e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t}\right)^{2}.

This is
O({\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}]^{2}).

\bullet\textbf{ Case 3: }|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|\leq 1

We handle case 3 differently by studying subintervals that depend on the branching time
k_{b}=\log|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|^{-1}
. More precisely, we consider subintervals where
e^{-k_{b}-1}<|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|\leq e^{-k_{b}}
for
0<k_{b}\leq t
. Define
({\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}^{2}])_{k_{b}}
to be the contribution from such intervals:

(47)

\displaystyle({\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}^{2}])_{k_{b}}=(\#\mathcal{H})\sum_{e^{-k_{b}-1}<|h|\leq e^{-k_{b}}}{\mathbb{P}}(J(0)\cap J(h))

so that

(\#\mathcal{H})\sum_{|h|\leq 1}{\mathbb{P}}(J(0)\cap J(h))=\sum_{k_{b}}({\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}^{2}])_{k_{b}}.

(By translation invariance of the distribution, we let
h^{{}^{\prime}}=0
.) Observe that the number of
h
’s such that
|h|\approx e^{-k_{b}}
in a discretized interval of order one is
e^{t}\cdot e^{-k_{b}}
. Continuing from (
47
) we have

(48)

\displaystyle\ll e^{t+t\theta}e^{t-k_{b}}\;\;{\mathbb{P}}(J(h)\cap J(0)).

We will estimate
{\mathbb{P}}(J(h)\cap J(0))
in two cases, which cover two ranges of
k_{b}
. The first case covers the range
1\leq k_{b}<t-t^{\alpha+\epsilon}
for some fixed
\epsilon
with
0<\epsilon<1-\alpha
. The second case covers remaining
k
. We omit the barrier in the first case for the same reason given in Section
2
. The dominant contribution comes from the latter range of
k
.

We will now prove the two cases.

\bullet\;\;\text{The contribution from}\;\;|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|\leq 1\;\;\text{for}\;\;1\leq k_{b}<t-t^{\alpha+\epsilon}.

To find the probability in (
48
) for
1\leq k_{b}<t-t^{\alpha+\epsilon}
, we drop the barrier from
J(h)
and
J(0)
:

(49)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(J(h)\cap J(0))

\displaystyle\ll{\mathbb{P}}(S_{t}(h)>\mu t+y,S_{t}(0)>\mu t+y).

It is convenient to rewrite the two processes
S_{t}(h)
and
S_{t}(0)
in terms of their center of mass
\overline{S}_{k}=\frac{S_{k}(h)+S_{k}(0)}{2}
and difference
S^{\perp}_{k}=\frac{S_{k}(h)-S_{k}(0)}{2}
. This is because
S_{k}(0)\approx S_{k}(h)
for
k\leq k_{b}
.
Let
S_{k_{b}}(h)=q_{1}
and
S_{k_{b}}(0)=q_{2}
so that the average
v=\frac{q_{1}+q_{2}}{2}
and difference
d=\frac{q_{1}-q_{2}}{2}
. We rewrite
S_{t}(h)
and
S_{t}(0)
:

(50)

\displaystyle S_{t}(h)=q_{1}+S_{k_{b},t}(h)=v+d+S_{k_{b},t}(h)

and

(51)

\displaystyle S_{t}(0)=q_{2}+S_{k_{b},t}(0)=v-d+S_{k_{b},t}(0).

Using (
50
) and (
51
), the probability in (
49
)

(52)

\displaystyle={\mathbb{P}}(v+d+S_{k_{b},t}(h)\in(\mu t+y,\mu t+y+\delta],v-d+S_{k_{b},t}(0)\in(\mu t+y,\mu t+y+\delta]).\hfill

Next, we decompose the probability (
52
) over all possible averages
v
and differences
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
at the branching time
k_{b}
:

(53)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v<M(k_{b})}\sum_{d}{\mathbb{P}}(\overline{S}_{k_{b}}\in(v,v+1],S_{k_{b}}^{\perp}\in(d,d+1])\cdot

\displaystyle\phantom{{}=1}{\mathbb{P}}(S_{k_{b},t}(h)+v+d\in(\mu t+y,\mu t+y+\delta],S_{k_{b},t}(0)+v-d\in(\mu t+y,\mu t+y+\delta]).

The two probabilities in (
53
) are by the independence of random walk increments. To find the first probability in (
53
), we use Lemma
3.3
to obtain

(54)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(\overline{S}_{k_{b}}\in(v,v+1],S_{k_{b}}^{\perp}\in(d,d+1])\ll\frac{e^{-v^{2}/k_{b}}}{\sqrt{\pi k_{b}}}e^{-cd^{2}},

where
c<0
. Similarly, for the second probability in (
53
), we have

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(S_{k_{b},t}(h)+v+d\in(\mu t+y,\mu t+y+\delta],S_{k_{b},t}(0)+v-d\in(\mu t+y,\mu t+y+\delta])

(55)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{\exp{-\frac{(-v-d+\mu t+y)^{2}}{t-k_{b}}}\exp{-\frac{(-v+d+\mu t+y)^{2}}{t-k_{b}}}}{\pi(t-k_{b})}.

We are now in a position to find the contribution to

({\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}^{2}])_{k_{b}}
for
1\leq k_{b}<t-t^{\alpha+\epsilon}
. By the probability estimates in (
54
) and (
55
), we have

\displaystyle({\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}^{2}])_{k_{b}}

\displaystyle\ll e^{t+t\theta}e^{t-k_{b}}\;\;{\mathbb{P}}(J(h)\cap J(0))

\displaystyle\ll e^{t+t\theta}e^{t-k_{b}}\;\;\sum_{v<M(k_{b})}\;\sum_{d\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\frac{e^{-v^{2}/k_{b}}}{\sqrt{k_{b}}}e^{-cd^{2}}\frac{e^{-\frac{(-v-d+\mu t+y)^{2}}{t-k_{b}}}e^{-\frac{(-v+d+\mu t+y)^{2}}{t-k_{b}}}}{(t-k_{b})}.

By a change of variable with
\overline{v}=v-\mu(k_{b})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\ll e^{t+t\theta}e^{t-k_{b}}\sum_{\overline{v}<(t-k_{b})\theta+y+1}\sum_{d}\frac{e^{-\frac{(\overline{v}+\mu k)^{2}}{k_{b}}}}{\sqrt{k_{b}}}e^{-cd^{2}}\frac{e^{-\frac{(\mu(t-k_{b})+y-\overline{v}+d)^{2}}{t-k_{b}}}e^{-\frac{(\mu(t-k_{b})+y-\overline{v}-d)^{2}}{t-k_{b}}}}{t-k_{b}}

(56)

\displaystyle\ll\frac{e^{t+t\theta}e^{t-k_{b}}e^{-2\mu^{2}(t-k_{b})-\mu^{2}(k_{b})-4\mu y}}{\sqrt{k_{b}}(t-k_{b})}\sum_{\overline{v}<(t-k_{b})\theta+y+1}e^{2\mu\overline{v}-\frac{2(y-\overline{v})^{2}}{t-k_{b}}-\frac{\overline{v}^{2}}{k_{b}}}\sum_{d}e^{-cd^{2}-\frac{2d^{2}}{t-k_{b}}}.

The sum over
d
is finite; to handle the sum over
\overline{v}
, we use Laplace’s method. Let
f(\overline{v})=2\mu\overline{v}-\frac{2(y-\overline{v})^{2}}{t-k_{b}}-\frac{\overline{v}^{2}}{k_{b}}
. Now the maximum of
f(\overline{v})
is where
k_{b}
is nearly close to
t
. Subbing in
t-1
for
k_{b}
, we find that
f(\overline{v})
has a stationary point at
\overline{v_{0}}=\frac{\mu+2y}{2+\frac{1}{t-1}}.

Hence, by Laplace’s method, we obtain

(57)

\displaystyle\sum_{\overline{v}<(t-k_{b})\theta+y+1}e^{f(\overline{v})}\leq\int_{-\infty}^{(t-k_{b})\theta+y+1}e^{f(\overline{v})}\;d\overline{v}\ll e^{f(\overline{v_{0}})}\ll e^{2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t}.

After applying the estimate (
57
) to (
56
), the contribution is now

\displaystyle({\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}^{2}])_{k_{b}}

\displaystyle\ll\frac{e^{t+t\theta}e^{t-k_{b}}e^{-2\mu^{2}(t-k_{b})-\mu^{2}(k_{b})-4\mu y}}{\sqrt{k_{b}}(t-k_{b})}e^{2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t}.

This simplifies to

\displaystyle\ll\frac{e^{-\theta(t-k_{b})+(1+2\alpha)\log(t-k_{b})+\frac{1+2\alpha}{2}\log k_{b}}e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t}}{\sqrt{k_{b}}(t-k_{b})}

(58)

\displaystyle\ll e^{-\theta(t-k_{b})+2\alpha\log(t-k_{b})+\alpha\log k_{b}}e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t}.

In line (
58
), we use the fact that
(\log x)/x
is a decreasing function for
x>e
.

It remains to sum over
k_{b}
up to
t-t^{\alpha+\epsilon}
for a fixed
\epsilon
with
0<\epsilon<1-\alpha
. (We choose
t-t^{\alpha+\epsilon}
to ensure sum convergence.) After performing an index change by letting
\hat{k}=t-k_{b}
, we obtain

(59)

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{b}=1}^{t-t^{\alpha+\epsilon}}({\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}^{2}])_{k_{b}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\hat{k}=t^{\alpha+\epsilon}}^{t-1}e^{-\theta(\hat{k})+2\alpha\log\hat{k}+\alpha\log(t-\hat{k})}e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y-y^{2}/t}\ll e^{-2\sqrt{1+\theta}y}e^{-y^{2}/t}.

This proves the case for
1\leq k_{b}<t-t^{\alpha+\epsilon}
. The estimate (
59
) is
o({\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}]^{2})
.

\bullet\;\;\text{The contribution from}\;\;|h-h^{{}^{\prime}}|\leq 1\;\;\text{for}\;\;t-t^{\alpha+\epsilon}\leq k_{b}\leq t
.

We proceed similarly to the case for
1\leq k_{b}<t-t^{\alpha+\epsilon}
. We continue from (
53
), but preserve the barrier constraint in the events
J(h)
and
J(0)
:

\displaystyle(\#\mathcal{H})\sum_{e^{-k_{b}-1}<|h|<e^{-k_{b}}}{\mathbb{P}}(J(h)\cap J(0))

\displaystyle=e^{t+t\theta}e^{t-k_{b}}\;\;\sum_{v<M(k_{b})}\sum_{d}{\mathbb{P}}(\overline{S}_{k_{b}}\in(v,v+1],S_{k_{b}}^{\perp}\in(d,d+1],S_{k_{b}}\leq M(k_{b}))\cdot

Observe that for the first probability in (

It remains to find the second probability in (

\displaystyle{\mathbb{P}}(S_{k_{b},\;t}(h)+v+d\in(m_{y}(t),m_{y}(t)+\delta],S_{k_{b},\;t}(0)+v-d\in(m_{y}(t),m_{y}(t)+\delta],

\displaystyle({\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}^{2}])_{k_{b}}

When

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{(t-k_{b})\theta+y}e^{f(\overline{v})}\;d\overline{v}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{2\pi}{|f^{{}^{\prime\prime}}(\overline{v_{0}})|}}e^{f(\overline{v_{0}})}\ll\sqrt{(t-k_{b})}((t-k_{b})\theta+1)^{3}e^{-y^{2}/t+2\mu y}.

By the estimate in
(\ref{eqn: a})
, the contribution
(\ref{eqn: b})

\displaystyle({\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}^{2}])_{k_{b}}

\displaystyle\ll\left(1+\frac{y}{t^{1-\alpha}}\right)\sqrt{(t-k_{b})}((t-k_{b})\theta+1)^{3}

(68)

\displaystyle\phantom{{}=1}\frac{e^{t+t\theta}e^{t-k_{b}}e^{\log t\theta}e^{-2\mu^{2}(t-k_{b})-\mu^{2}(k_{b})-2\mu y-y^{2}/t}}{k_{b}^{3/2}(t-k_{b})^{3}}.

.

Finally, we sum over

\displaystyle\sum_{k=t-t^{\alpha+\epsilon}}^{t}({\mathbb{E}}[Z_{\delta}^{2}])_{k_{b}}

Let
0<\delta\leq 1
. Define the intervals
I_{x}=(x,x+\delta]
for

,
I_{x}=(x-\delta,x]
if
x<0
, and

if
x=0
. The propositions are from the appendix in
[
ADH24
]
. We omit the proofs.

Proposition A.1

(Ballot theorem with linear barrier: Lower bound)
.

Let
(S_{j},j\geq 1)
be a random walk with Gaussian increments of mean
0
and variance
\sigma_{j}^{2}
with
c^{-1}<\sigma_{j}<c
for some fixed
c\geq 1
. Define the linear barrier

b(j)=aj+b(0).

Then, for any
j\geq 1
,
(b(j)-x)\cdot b(0)\leq j
, we have

(83)

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\forall 1\leq\ell\leq j,\ S_{\ell}\leq b(\ell),\ S_{j}\in I_{x}\right)\gg\frac{b(0)(b(j)-x)}{j^{3/2}}\delta e^{-\frac{cx^{2}}{j}}.

Proposition A.2

(Ballot theorem with linear barrier: Upper bound)
.

Let
(S_{j},j\geq 1)
be a random walk with Gaussian increments of mean
0
and variance
1/2
. Define the linear barrier

b(j)=aj+b(0).

j\geq 1

b(0)>0
and
x\leq b(j)

(84)

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\forall 1\leq\ell\leq j,\ S_{\ell}\leq b(\ell),\ S_{j}\in I_{x}\right)\ll\frac{(b(0)+1)(b(j)-x+1)}{j^{3/2}}.

Proposition A.3

Scattering of spinning compact objects from a worldline EFT Preprint: UUITP-31/23
Maor Ben-Shahar

Abstract
We study the EFT of a spinning compact object and show that with appropriate gauge fixing, computations become amenable to worldline quantum field theory techniques. We use the resulting action to compute Compton and one-loop scattering amplitudes at fourth order in spin. By matching these amplitdes to solutions of the Teukolsky equations, we fix the values of Wilson coefficients appearing in the EFT such that it reproduces Kerr black hole scattering. We keep track of the spin supplementary condition throughout our computations and discuss alternative ways to ensure its preservation.

We begin by recalling the action for the relativistic spinning particle

;
Porto:2005ac
;

Our strategy is to apply the machinery of the WQFT

\displaystyle Z=\int\mathcal{D}[h]\mathcal{D}[W]e^{iS+iS_{EH}+S_{gf}}\ ,

(1)

where

represents the full set of worldline fluctuations

introduced in section

\displaystyle S_{EH}=-\frac{2}{\kappa^{2}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{g}R\ ,\hskip 56.9055ptS_{gf}=\int d^{4}x\Big(\partial_{\nu}h^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}h\Big)^{2}\ ,

2.1
The worldline EFT

Rotational degrees of freedom can be added to a worldline particle by introducing tetrad fields
e_{\mu}^{I}
with Greek indices for the general spacetime manifold and capital Latin indices for the corotating, body-fixed frame.
We lower and raise indices with the spacetime metric
g_{\mu\nu}
and the flat metric
\eta_{IJ}
for the corotating frame, and use mostly-minus signature throughout. The tetrad
e_{\mu}^{I}
can be decomposed further by writing
e^{\mu}_{I}=e^{\mu}_{a}\Lambda^{a}_{I}
, where
e^{a}_{\mu}=e^{a}_{\mu}(x)
now transforms from the spacetime manifold to a flat local frame, with metric
\eta_{ab}
, and
\Lambda_{I}^{a}=\Lambda_{I}^{a}(\tau)
are Lorentz matrices defined on the worldline.
We treat the fields
S_{ab}
and
\Lambda_{I}^{a}
as the fundamental spin degrees of freedom, but it is sometimes convenient to write them with curved indices using the frame fields
e_{\mu}^{a}
and
e_{a}^{\mu}
.
It will turn out later that in the scattering description the flat frame will be co-rotating with the particle in the asymptotic past, so the Lorentz matrices
\Lambda^{a}_{I}
parametrize deviations of the particle’s body-fixed frame at time
\tau
from the body-fixed frame of the asymptotic particle.

The Lorentz matrices carry six degrees of freedom, therefore in the Hamiltonian formalism they will be accompanied by six conjugate degrees of freedom, appearing through the antisymmetric spin tensor
S_{ab}
(see Appendix
B
for an alternative construction). The spin tensor couples to the angular velocity

\displaystyle\Omega^{ab}=\Lambda^{aI}\frac{D\Lambda^{b}_{I}}{d\tau}\ ,

(4)

via the kinetic term,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{kin}=\frac{1}{2}S_{ab}\Omega^{ab}\ .

(5)

As it stands, the spin sector contains six degrees of freedom in each of
S
and
\Lambda
. Therefore one usually considers imposing the spin-supplementary condition
Hanson:1974qy

S_{ab}p^{b}=0
, as well as the conjugate constraint
\Lambda_{a0}=\hat{p}_{a}
, where we use the normalized momentum
\hat{p}^{\mu}\equiv p^{\mu}/\sqrt{p^{2}}
.
This particular choice of constraints is called the covariant SSC.
Such constraints are motivated by examining the particle in its rest frame, where it is expected that a rigid body should have just three degrees of freedom in the spin sector, namely the boost components
\Lambda^{a}_{0}
should not be dynamical
Steinhoff:2015ksa
, see Fig.
1
.

\tau

The body-fixed frame tracks the orientation of a point (in red) on the rigid body as it rotates relative to the flat frame (in black). Only the spatial

Since it is challenging to apply perturbation theory to an action with Lagrange multipliers, it would be beneficial to find another formulation of the action such that it propagates the same degrees of freedom but is free from Lagrange multipliers.
The first step is to resolve the pair of second-class constraints
S_{ab}p^{b}=0
and
\Lambda_{0a}=\hat{p}_{a}
into a single constraint, at the cost of introducing a gauge symmetry.
The introduction of the gauge symmetry was achieved in
Levi:2015msa
by “unfixing” the gauge, such that the full minimally coupled worldline action is

\displaystyle S=-\int d\tau\Big(p_{\mu}\dot{x}^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}S_{ab}\Omega^{ab}+\frac{1}{p^{2}}\frac{Dp_{\mu}}{d\tau}S^{\mu\nu}p_{\nu}-\frac{\ell}{2}(p^{2}-m^{2})-\ell_{a}S^{ab}\big(\hat{p}_{b}+\Lambda_{0b}\big)\Big),

(6)

where
\ell
and
\ell^{\mu}
are Lagrange multipliers, and recall
\hat{p}^{\mu}
is unit-normalized,
\hat{p}^{\mu}=p^{\mu}/\sqrt{p^{2}}
.
This action is not exactly in Hamiltonian form due to the acceleration term containing a derivative acting on the momentum. Several approaches have been implemented that do not have this acceleration term, for example by finite shifts of the worldline position
Vines:2016unv
or by solving the constraints for
p

Porto:2005ac
.
But we will leave the acceleration term alone for the most part, as in the end it just contributes one extra term to the worldline propagators.
Since the action (
6
) was obtained by unfixing the covariant gauge, one can expect that the
\Lambda_{0}^{a}
fields and the Lagrange multiplier
\ell^{\mu}
will no longer be determined by the equations of motion. Therefore there should be some gauge symmetry in the action. The gauge symmetries can be guessed based on their form in flat space
Steinhoff:2015ksa
, and indeed the following transformations leave the action invariant,

\displaystyle\delta S_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle 2\hat{p}_{[\mu}S_{\nu]\alpha}\epsilon^{\alpha}\ ,

\displaystyle\delta\Lambda_{I}{}^{\mu}=

\displaystyle 2\epsilon^{[\mu}\hat{p}^{\nu]}\Lambda_{I\nu}+2\epsilon^{[\mu}\Lambda_{0}^{\nu]}\Lambda_{I\nu}\ ,

(7)

\displaystyle\delta\ell^{\mu}=

\displaystyle-\frac{D{\epsilon}^{\mu}}{d\tau}+\ldots\ .

The ellipses in the last gauge transformation include all additional terms needed to compensate for the variation of the action and constraint itself, we explain this below.
When Lagrange multipliers are undetermined, any variation of the action which results in something proportional to their associated constraint, in this case
S{\cdot}(\hat{p}+\Lambda_{0})
, can be absorbed into a compensating transformation for the Lagrange multiplier itself. Therefore when checking gauge invariance one need only work on the constraint surface. Note that here the variation of the constraint itself also vanishes on the constraint surface, such that again it can be absorbed into the variation of the Lagrange multiplier again.
For the gauge transformations (
2.1
) to be of physical significance the constraints in (
6
) must be first class (see chapter three of ref.
Henneaux:1992ig
). We included a check of spin gauge independence of the amplitudes in Appendix
C
, and in appendix
D
we show that the constraint
S{\cdot}(\hat{p}+\Lambda_{0})
is preserved in time, implying it is indeed first class.

Conservation of spin magnitude:

Before moving on to construct non-minimal couplings, it is worthwhile seeing the preservation of the spin magnitude from the gauge-invariant action.
We have an invariant spin tensor in the spatial part of the body-fixed frame,
S_{ij}=S_{ab}\Lambda^{a}_{i}\Lambda^{b}_{j}
, where
i,j
run over
1,2,3
. This conserved charge comes from the global
SO(3)
little group symmetry of the body-fixed frame (full
SO(1,3)
is explicitly broken by the appearance of
\Lambda_{0}^{a}
in the action). Since this charge is conserved, its square is also conserved,

\displaystyle S_{ij}S^{ij}=

\displaystyle\textrm{tr}\Big((\eta-\Lambda_{0}\Lambda_{0})S(\eta-\Lambda_{0}\Lambda_{0})S\Big)\ ,

(8)

and we can now recognize that in the covariant SSC (
\Lambda_{0}^{\mu}=p^{\mu}
and
S^{\mu\nu}p_{\nu}=0
) the right-hand side is proportional to the magnitude of the spin vector
S^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2m}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}p_{\nu}S_{\nu\rho}
.

Non-minimal couplings:

Our next task is to add to (
6
) additional spin and curvature dependent operators, which are necessary to model generic compact objects.
Kerr black holes require some of these operators too, meaning they are not correctly modeled by the minimally coupled action. In section
4
we will fix the new non-minimal couplings through fourth order in spin to match Kerr scattering.

It is clear that non-minimal couplings will be invariant under the gauge transformations (
2.1
) if they are solely functions of the spin-vector,
S^{\mu}=\frac{1}{2m}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}p_{\nu}S_{\nu\rho}
, or the spin tensor with transverse projectors
\tilde{S}_{\alpha\beta}=S_{\mu\nu}\mathcal{P}^{\mu}_{\alpha}\mathcal{P}^{\nu}_{\beta}
, where
\mathcal{P}^{\mu}_{\alpha}=\delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}-\hat{p}^{\mu}\hat{p}_{\alpha}

Steinhoff:2015ksa
.
Since we will work in four dimensions, we will exclusively use the spin vector.
We first introduce the electric (parity even) and magnetic (parity odd) components of the Riemann tensor,

\displaystyle E_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle R_{\mu\rho\nu\sigma}p^{\rho}p^{\sigma}\ ,

(9)

\displaystyle B_{\mu\nu}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}R_{\alpha\beta\rho\mu}\epsilon^{\alpha\beta\gamma}{}_{\nu}p^{\rho}p_{\gamma}\ .

(10)

With these, all couplings linear in Riemann which respect the spin gauge symmetry are
Levi:2015msa
,

\mathcal{O}(RS^{2n})\sim D_{\mu_{2n}}\ldots D_{\mu_{3}}E_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}S^{\mu_{1}}\ldots S^{\mu_{2n}}\ ,

(11)

for even powers in spin and

\mathcal{O}(RS^{2n+1})\sim D_{\mu_{2n+1}}\ldots D_{\mu_{3}}B_{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}}S^{\mu_{1}}\ldots S^{\mu_{2n+1}}\ ,

(12)

for odd powers in spin.
These operators should be added inside the mass-shell constraint to maintain reparametrization invariance, so we add to our action

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{R}=\frac{\ell c_{ES^{2}}}{2m^{2}}E_{\mu\nu}S^{\mu}S^{\nu}-\frac{\ell c_{BS^{3}}}{6m^{3}}D_{\mu}B_{\nu\rho}S^{\mu}S^{\nu}S^{\rho}-\frac{\ell c_{ES^{4}}}{24m^{4}}D_{\mu}D_{\nu}E_{\rho\sigma}S^{\mu}S^{\nu}S^{\rho}S^{\sigma},

(13)

which are all the couplings up to
S^{4}
that are linear in Riemann. One should interpret
D_{\mu}D_{\nu}E_{\rho\sigma}=(D_{\mu}D_{\nu}R_{\rho\alpha\sigma\beta})p^{\alpha}p^{\beta}
and similarly for derivatives of
B_{\mu\nu}
.
Aside from these, we will also add a single SSC-breaking operator,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{K}=-\frac{\ell c_{RSK}}{2m^{2}}R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}S^{\mu\nu}K^{\rho}p^{\sigma}\ ,

(14)

where
K^{\mu}=S^{\mu\nu}p_{\nu}
.
This operator would appear naturally in the action if we eliminate the acceleration term using the Mathisson–Papapetrou–Dixon equation,

\displaystyle\frac{Dp_{\mu}}{d\tau}=-\frac{1}{2}R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\dot{x}^{\nu}S^{\rho\sigma}\ +\ldots,

(15)

in which case we would find (
14
) with
c_{RSK}\to 1
, plus corrections at higher order in spin. In other words, if we worked without the SSC and the acceleration terms we would be able to obtain SSC-respecting results by fixing Wilson coefficients to definite values, see for example
Bern:2023ity
for similar observations. We will use this as a consistency check of our calculations, and we will not add other SSC breaking operators even though others could be written down.
At quadratic order in curvature and restricting to operators respecting the SSC we have (see e.g. refs.
Levi:2022rrq
;
Siemonsen:2019dsu
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{R^{2}}=\frac{\ell c_{E^{2}S^{4}}}{m^{6}}(E_{\mu\nu}S^{\mu}S^{\nu})^{2}+\frac{\ell c^{\prime}_{E^{2}S^{4}}}{m^{6}}S^{2}S^{\mu}E_{\mu\nu}E^{\nu}{}_{\rho}S^{\rho}+\frac{\ell c^{\prime\prime}_{E^{2}S^{4}}}{m^{6}}S^{4}E^{2}+(E\leftrightarrow B).

(16)

In writing these operators we restricted to dimensionless Wilson coefficients which are independent of Newton’s constant
G
, with the assumption that our compact object’s characteristic size is not much greater than the Schwarzchild radius
2Gm
, and thus can not be used to soak up length scales without introducing factors of
G
in the action. Note that for physical black holes, spin scales as
Gm^{2}
so operators such as
S^{4}R^{2}/m^{4}
contribute at the same order as
G^{2}S^{2}R^{2}
.
But we will make comparisons to solutions of the Teukolsky equations at order
G^{2}

;
Bautista:2022wjf
, so we can stick to operators with no factors of
G
in the action.
This concludes the construction of the action that we will use up to
S^{4}
, all terms are collected together in Appendix
A
for convenience.

3
Gauge fixing and Feynman rules

In order to apply the WQFT techniques to the action in the previous section we need to study the flat-space equations of motion, and expand around their solutions.
We gauge fix the action by setting (see appendix
C
for a more general gauge fixing),

\displaystyle\ell_{\mu}=\frac{1}{p}\frac{Dp_{\mu}}{d\tau}\ ,

(17)

which fixes our gauge to be the covariant SSC plus corrections at higher order in spin. We additionally fix reparametrization invariance by setting
\ell=\frac{1}{m}
.
The minimally coupled action is then,

\displaystyle S=

\displaystyle-\int d\tau\Big(p_{\mu}\dot{x}^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}S_{ab}\Omega^{ab}-\frac{1}{2m}(p^{2}-m^{2})-\frac{1}{p}\frac{Dp_{\mu}}{d\tau}S^{\mu\nu}\Lambda_{\nu 0}\Big)\ .

(18)

The flat space solutions have constant spin variables,
\dot{S}_{\mu\nu}=0
and
\dot{\Lambda}^{\mu}_{I}=0
, and we have

\displaystyle p_{\mu}=mv_{\mu}\ ,\hskip 56.9055ptx^{\mu}=b^{\mu}+mv^{\mu}\tau\ ,

(19)

together with
v^{2}=1
. In addition, in the scattering scenario we will take our asymptotic states to obey the covariant SSC, meaning
\Lambda^{\mu}_{0}=v^{\mu}
and
S_{\mu\nu}v^{\nu}=0
.
We now use these solutions to the equations of motion as background fields, and add worldline fluctuations on top.
This is achieved by the substitution,

\displaystyle p_{\mu}

\displaystyle\to mv_{\mu}+\pi_{\mu}\ ,

(20)

\displaystyle x^{\mu}

\displaystyle\to b^{\mu}+v^{\mu}\tau+z^{\mu}\ ,

(21)

\displaystyle S_{ab}

\displaystyle\to S_{ab}+s_{ab}\ ,

(22)

\displaystyle\Lambda^{a}_{I}

\displaystyle\to\Lambda_{I}^{a}+\lambda^{ab}\Lambda_{Ib}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda^{ab}\lambda_{bc}\Lambda^{c}_{I}+\ldots\ .

(23)

From now on we interpret
{S}_{ab}
and
\Lambda_{I}^{a}
as the constant solutions to the equations of motion, and our dynamical fields are
\pi_{\mu}
,
z^{\mu}
,
s_{ab}
and
\lambda_{ab}
. The
\lambda_{ab}
matrices are antisymmetric, and are introduced through the exponential map of an infinitesimal rotation acting on the
\Lambda
fields.
In principle we could have just written
\Lambda\to\Lambda+\delta
but then
\delta
must be constrained since
\Lambda+\delta
must still be a Lorentz matrix. The antisymmetric infinitesimal rotation matrices trivialize these constraints and make it manifest that the
\Lambda
sector contains six degrees of freedom. The downside of using these matrices is that we have an infinite set of vertices involving the
\lambda_{ab}
fields. In practice only a small number of them will play a part in our classical perturbative computations.

3.1
Propagators

We can now expand the action up to quadratic order in the worldline perturbations and zeroth order in the gravitons in order to compute the propagators. The quadratic part of the action is,

\displaystyle S_{kin}=

\displaystyle-\int d\tau\Big(\dot{z}^{\mu}\pi_{\mu}-\frac{1}{2m}\pi^{2}+\frac{1}{2}S^{\mu\nu}\lambda_{\mu\rho}\dot{\lambda}^{\nu\rho}-\frac{1}{2}\dot{\lambda}^{\mu\nu}s_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\hskip 170.71652pt-\frac{1}{m}S^{\mu\nu}\dot{\pi}_{\mu}\lambda_{\nu\rho}v^{\rho}-\frac{1}{m}\dot{\pi}^{\mu}s_{\mu\nu}v^{\nu}\Big)\ ,

(24)

and we work in momentum/energy space so we need to insert the Fourier transforms,

\displaystyle z^{\mu}(\tau)=\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}e^{i\omega t}z^{\mu}(\omega)\ ,

(25)

with similar expressions for the other worldline perturbations (here we work with incoming momenta and energies).
We are considering the propagation of one real particle, so when solving the free theory we pull out a factor of
\frac{1}{2}
from the action and write the free partition function as
\textrm{exp}(\frac{i}{2}J{\cdot}\Delta{\cdot}J)
. Then the nontrivial two-point functions are,

\displaystyle\langle z^{\mu}(-\omega)z^{\nu}(\omega)\rangle=

\displaystyle-i\frac{1}{m\omega^{2}}\eta^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{m^{2}\omega}{S}^{\mu\nu}\ ,

\displaystyle\langle p^{\mu}(-\omega)z^{\nu}(\omega)\rangle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\omega}\eta^{\mu\nu}\ ,

\displaystyle\langle s_{\mu\nu}(-\omega)s_{\rho\sigma}(\omega)\rangle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{\omega}(\eta_{\nu[\sigma}{S}_{\rho]\mu}-\eta_{\mu[\sigma}{S}_{\rho]\nu})\ ,

(26)

\displaystyle\langle s_{\mu\nu}(-\omega)\lambda_{\rho\sigma}(\omega)\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\omega}\eta_{\mu[\rho}\eta_{\sigma]\nu}\ ,

\displaystyle\langle\lambda^{\mu\nu}(-\omega)z_{\rho}(\omega)\rangle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{m\omega}v^{[\mu}\delta^{\nu]}_{\rho}\ .

The retarded and advanced propagators are obtained by replacing
\frac{1}{\omega}\to\frac{1}{\omega\pm i0}
. Here we use the average of the two, meaning our background fields are the average of the far past/future background fields, see ref.
Mogull:2020sak
for more detail.
Note that only the last propagator introduces a mixing between the spin and position sectors, and it is the direct result of the acceleration and gauge-fixing terms.
The propagator used for the gravitons is obtained by substituting
g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}+\kappa h_{\mu\nu}
in equation (
2
),

\displaystyle\langle h_{\mu\nu}h_{\rho\sigma}\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{i}{2(k^{2}+i0)}(\eta_{\mu\rho}\eta_{\sigma\nu}+\eta_{\mu\sigma}\eta_{\rho\nu}-\eta_{\mu\nu}\eta_{\rho\sigma})\ .

(27)

We use the Feynman propagator which is suitable for the amplitude computations we do here.

3.2
Vertices

Simplified graviton self-interactions can be found in ref.
Kalin:2020mvi
for example, so we turn our attention to constructing vertives involving the worldline fields and gravitons.
We work in momentum space, so in the worldline action we substitute the Fourier transform

\displaystyle h_{\mu\nu}(x)=\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{ik{\cdot}(b+v\tau+z)}h_{\mu\nu}(k)\ ,

(28)

which when expanded in powers of
z

Mogull:2020sak
. The extra
e^{ik{\cdot}b}
factor will be dropped from the Feynman rules.
We will also ignore the momentum integrals that come from the worldline action, and restore them at the end of the computation. If in the one-loop amplitude below we restore all momentum integrals that appear in the action we will be computing the Eikonal phase
Mogull:2020sak

The
\tau

m
worldline perturbations and
n

\displaystyle\delta(\omega_{1}+\ldots+\omega_{m}+(k_{1}+\ldots+k_{n}){\cdot}v)\ ,

The

5
Conclusion

We have computed scattering amplitudes from a worldline EFT, employing the WQFT techniques. We were able to extend current computational tools up to fourth order in spin, and fix Wilson coefficients in the action by matching the scattering amplitudes to solutions of the Teukolsky equations, see table
1
for those results. The six constraints we found on the Wilson coefficients are compatible with those identified previously in
Siemonsen:2019dsu
for certain linear combinations of our coefficients. We additionally assembled the one-loop amplitude for the scattering of two spinning compact objects through
S^{4}
, and found our result is in agreement with the literature.
These computations relied on a new gauge-fixing condition for the point-particle EFT which allowed us to easily construct propagators and thus apply the formalism of the WQFT. We collected our amplitudes in an ancillary file, keeping all free parameters that appear in the action.

For the gauge fixing to be valid one needs to know that the transformations in (
2.1
) are not trivial, meaning that they are not proportional to the equations of motion. This requires checking that the constraints in the action are first class, which we show for the gauge-invariant action with generic non-minimal couplings in appendix
D
. The gauge fixing was also tested by keeping a free scalar parameter in front of the gauge-fixing condition and checking that the final results were independent of this parameter, these checks are explained in appendix
C
.
The invariance of the computations under the choice of this one scalar parameter in the action essentially amounts to checking a weakened Ward identity. We expect that since our gauge freedom here eliminates three degrees of freedom from the action we should be able to obtain three independent scalar Ward identities. Further investigation would be interesting and also useful because the Ward identities could help identify amplitudes that preserve the SSC even if they were guessed by a bootstrap procedure.

In this paper we worked up to quartic order in spin, but in principle there is no barrier to extend computations to higher orders. It would therefore be interesting to extend our Feynman rules and use them to study higher-spin effects in radiation
DeAngelis:2023lvf
;
brandhuber2023resummed
;
aoude2023leadingorder
, as well as higher loop corrections to scattering amplitudes and potentials. Since computations involving gravity Feynman rules can become quite cumbersome, it would be useful to develop a double-copy prescription
Shi:2021qsb
for the classical spinning EFT.
The techniques developed here could also be extended to include non-conservative effects. These can be added in the worldline equations of motion for example
Saketh:2022xjb
, and such equations of motion could still in principle be solved perturbatively using Feynman rules similar to the ones we developed here.
Inclusion of non-conservative effects would allow for a full matching to Kerr scattering results beyond fourth order in spin
Bautista:2021wfy
;
Bautista:2022wjf
, but even without them it could be possible to identify worldline actions that match conservative sectors of Kerr Compton amplitudes beyond
S^{4}
. Such actions could shed light on the guiding principles for constraining effective actions for Kerr black holes at higher orders in curvature. We leave these topics for future research.

Appendix A
The worldline action

The full worldline action up to
S^{4}
and before gauge fixing is,

\displaystyle S=

\displaystyle-\int d\tau\Big(p_{\mu}\dot{x}^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}S_{ab}\Omega^{ab}+\frac{1}{p^{2}}\frac{Dp_{\mu}}{d\tau}S^{\mu\nu}p_{\nu}-\frac{\ell}{2}(p^{2}-m^{2})-\ell_{a}S^{ab}(\hat{p}_{b}+\Lambda_{b0})

\displaystyle\hskip 45.52458pt+\frac{\ell c_{ES^{2}}}{2m^{2}}E_{\mu\nu}S^{\mu}S^{\nu}-\frac{\ell c_{RSK}}{2m^{2}}R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}S^{\mu\nu}K^{\rho}p^{\sigma}-\frac{\ell c_{BS^{3}}}{6m^{3}}D_{\mu}B_{\nu\rho}S^{\mu}S^{\nu}S^{\rho}

\displaystyle\hskip 45.52458pt-\frac{\ell c_{ES^{4}}}{24m^{4}}D_{\mu}D_{\nu}E_{\rho\sigma}S^{\mu}S^{\nu}S^{\rho}S^{\sigma}

(57)

\displaystyle\hskip 45.52458pt+\frac{\ell c_{E^{2}S^{4}}}{m^{6}}(E_{\mu\nu}S^{\mu}S^{\nu})^{2}+\frac{\ell c_{B^{2}S^{4}}}{m^{6}}(B_{\mu\nu}S^{\mu}S^{\nu})^{2}+\frac{\ell c^{\prime}_{E^{2}S^{4}}}{m^{6}}S^{2}S^{\mu}E_{\mu\nu}E^{\nu}{}_{\rho}S^{\rho}

\displaystyle\hskip 45.52458pt+\frac{\ell c^{\prime}_{B^{2}S^{4}}}{m^{6}}S^{2}S^{\mu}B_{\mu\nu}B^{\nu}{}_{\rho}S^{\rho}+\frac{\ell c^{\prime\prime}_{E^{2}S^{4}}}{m^{6}}S^{4}E^{2}+\frac{\ell c^{\prime\prime}_{B^{2}S^{4}}}{m^{6}}S^{4}B^{2}\Big)

where
D/d\tau
is the covariant derivative along the worldline, e.g.
Dp_{\mu}/d\tau=\dot{p}_{\mu}-\Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\nu}p_{\rho}\dot{x}^{\nu}
. For completeness, the angular velocity is defined by,

\displaystyle\Omega^{ab}=

\displaystyle\Lambda_{I}^{a}\frac{D}{d\tau}\Lambda^{bI}=\Lambda_{I}^{a}\dot{\Lambda}^{bI}-\omega_{\mu}{}^{ab}\ ,

(58)

where
\omega_{\mu}^{ab}=e^{a}_{\nu}\partial_{\mu}e^{\nu b}+\Gamma_{\mu\nu}^{\rho}e^{a}_{\mu}e^{b\nu}
is the spin connection for the flat corotating frame of the initial state.

Appendix B
Composite spin tensor

Our starting point was a worldline with six degrees of freedom contained in the
\Lambda_{I}^{a}
, and six conjugate degrees of freedom contained in
S_{ab}
. It is natural to ask if instead of
S_{ab}
we could have introduced a matrix
\chi_{a}^{I}
conjugate to the
\Lambda_{I}^{a}
. It turns out that we can via the action,

\displaystyle S_{\textrm{comp. spin}}=-\int d\tau\Big(\chi_{a}^{I}\dot{\Lambda}^{a}_{I}-\ell_{IJ}(\Lambda_{a}^{I}\Lambda^{aJ}-\eta^{IJ})\Big)\ .

(59)

The constraint
(\Lambda_{a}^{I}\Lambda^{aJ}-\eta^{IJ})
generates a gauge symmetry,

\displaystyle\delta_{\epsilon}\chi_{a}^{I}=-2\epsilon_{IJ}\Lambda_{a}^{J}\ ,

(60)

which reduces the number of physical degrees of freedom in
\chi
to six.
It is straightforward to see that the composite spin tensor defined by

\displaystyle S_{ab}=\Lambda_{a}^{I}\chi_{bI}-\lambda_{b}^{I}\chi_{aI}\ ,

(61)

is gauge invariant under the gauge transformations (
60
), and that the kinetic terms with
S_{ab}
or with
\chi_{a}^{I}
are equal

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}S_{ab}\Omega^{ab}=\chi_{a}^{I}\frac{D\Lambda^{a}_{I}}{d\tau}\ .

(62)

Appendix C
R_{\xi}
gauge fixing

The gauge fixing condition was relaxed to,

\displaystyle\ell_{\mu}=\frac{\xi}{p}\frac{Dp_{\mu}}{d\tau}\ ,

(63)

where
\xi
was left as a free parameter throughout the computations. With this choice of gauge fixing the two point functions become,

\displaystyle\langle z^{\mu}(-\omega)z^{\nu}(\omega)\rangle=

\displaystyle-i\frac{1}{m\omega^{2}}\eta^{\mu\nu}-\alpha\frac{1}{m^{2}\omega}{S}^{\mu\nu}\ ,

(64)

\displaystyle\langle p^{\mu}(-\omega)z^{\nu}(\omega)\rangle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\omega}\eta^{\mu\nu}\ ,

(65)

\displaystyle\langle s_{\mu\nu}(-\omega)s_{\rho\sigma}(\omega)\rangle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{\omega}\eta_{\nu][\sigma}{S}_{\rho][\mu}\ ,

(66)

\displaystyle\langle s_{\mu\nu}(-\omega)\lambda_{\rho\sigma}(\omega)\rangle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\omega}\eta_{\mu[\rho}\eta_{\sigma]\nu}\ ,

(67)

\displaystyle\langle\lambda^{\mu\nu}(-\omega)z_{\rho}(\omega)\rangle=

\displaystyle-\alpha(2\xi-1)\frac{2}{m\omega}v^{[\mu}\delta^{\nu]}_{\rho}\ ,

(68)

\displaystyle\langle z^{\mu}(-\omega)s^{\nu\rho}(\omega)\rangle=

\displaystyle\alpha(1-\xi)\frac{2}{\omega}S^{\mu[\nu}v^{\rho]}\ .

(69)

In addition to the
\xi
parameter we also introduced
\alpha
, which multiplies everything to do with gauge fixing and acceleration terms. The SSC is preserved by setting
\alpha\to 1
, and
\xi
can be arbitrary. We also checked that the SSC is preserved when setting
\alpha\to 0
and
c_{RSK}\to 1
. If one instead sends
\alpha\to 0
and keeps
c_{RSK}
free the SSC will be broken. In the case when the SSC is kept, we have verified that the two ways of computing the Compton amplitude give identical results, and also that the
\alpha\to 1
method is independent of
\xi
. These checks were done through fourth order in spin.

Appendix D
First-class constraints

Here we show that the constraint
S{\cdot}(\hat{p}+\Lambda_{0})
is first class, whenever the non-minimal interactions preserve the gauge symmetry. This is equivalent to checking if the constraints are preserved in time on the constraint surface.
We rewrite the action as

\displaystyle S=

\displaystyle-\int d\tau\Big(p_{\mu}\dot{x}^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}S_{ab}\Omega^{ab}+\frac{D\hat{p}_{\mu}}{d\tau}S^{\mu\nu}\hat{p}_{\nu}-\frac{\ell}{2}\big(p^{2}-\mathcal{N}\big)-\ell_{a}S^{ab}(\hat{p}_{b}+\Lambda_{b0})\Big)\ ,

(70)

where the dynamical mass squarred
\mathcal{N}=m^{2}+\ldots
was introduced and includes all of the non-minimal interactions.
We need only use the two equations of motion,

\displaystyle\Omega^{\mu\nu}+2\frac{D\hat{p}^{[\mu}}{d\tau}\hat{p}^{\nu]}-2\ell^{[\mu}\big(\hat{p}^{\nu]}+\Lambda_{0}^{\nu]}\big)+\ell\mathcal{N}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=0\ ,

(71)

\displaystyle\frac{D}{d\tau}S_{\mu\nu}-2S_{[\mu|\rho}\Omega^{\rho}{}_{\nu]}-2\ell_{\rho}S^{\rho}{}_{[\mu}\Lambda_{\nu]0}

\displaystyle=0\ ,

(72)

where we introduced
\mathcal{N}^{\mu\nu}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{N}}{\partial S_{\mu\nu}}
. Gauge invariance required us to write
\mathcal{N}
only in terms of the spin vector, or spin tensor contracted with projectors transverse to
p^{\mu}
, therefore we have
\mathcal{N}_{\mu\nu}p^{\nu}=0
.
Using the equation of motion for
S_{\mu\nu}
we find,

\displaystyle\frac{D}{d\tau}S^{\mu\nu}(\hat{p}_{\nu}+\Lambda_{\nu 0})

\displaystyle=\big(2S_{[\mu|\rho}\Omega^{\rho}{}_{\nu}+2\ell_{\rho}S^{\rho}{}_{[\mu}\Lambda_{\nu]0}\big)\big(\hat{p}^{\nu}+\Lambda^{\nu}_{0}\big)+S^{\mu\nu}\Big(\frac{D\hat{p}_{\nu}}{d\tau}-\Omega_{\nu}{}^{\rho}\Lambda_{\rho 0}\Big)

(73)

\displaystyle\approx\big(S_{\mu\rho}\Omega^{\rho}{}_{\nu}+\ell_{\rho}S^{\rho}{}_{\mu}\Lambda_{\nu 0}\big)\big(\hat{p}^{\nu}+\Lambda^{\nu}_{0}\big)+S^{\mu\nu}\Big(\frac{D\hat{p}_{\nu}}{d\tau}-\Omega_{\nu}{}^{\rho}\Lambda_{\rho 0}\Big)

(74)

\displaystyle=S^{\mu\nu}\frac{D\hat{p}_{\nu}}{d\tau}+S_{\mu\rho}\Omega^{\rho}{}_{\nu}\hat{p}^{\nu}+\ell_{\rho}S^{\rho\mu}(\Lambda_{0}{\cdot}\hat{p}+1)\ ,

(75)

where
\approx
means equality on the constraint surface. Next we substitute
\Omega_{\mu\nu}
, and working on the constraint surface as above, we find

\displaystyle\frac{D}{d\tau}S^{\mu\nu}(\hat{p}_{\nu}+\Lambda_{\nu 0})

\displaystyle\approx S_{\mu\rho}\hat{p}^{\rho}\frac{D\hat{p}^{\nu}}{d\tau}\hat{p}_{\nu}\ ,

(76)

which is zero due to the fact that
\hat{p}
is unit normalized.

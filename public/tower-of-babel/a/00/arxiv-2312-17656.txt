Canonical mirror models for maximal orthogonal Grassmannians
Peter Spacek and Charles Wang

Abstract
In this report we use the methods of Spacek_Wang_Exceptional_family and the upcoming generalization Spacek_Wang_Canonical_models to complete the construction of canonical mirror models for all cominuscule homogeneous spaces, by considering the maximal orthogonal Grassmannians OG ⁡ ( n + 1 , 2 ​ n + 2 ) \mathrm{OG}(n+1,2n+2) .

2
The canonical mirror models

The superpotential is written as
\mathcal{W}_{\text{can}}=\sum_{i=0}^{n+1}\mathcal{W}_{i}
, where each
\mathcal{W}_{i}
is the quotient of homogeneous polynomials in Plücker coordinates of degree equal to the coefficient of the
i
th simple root in the highest root, except
\mathcal{W}_{0}=\frac{p_{\hskip 0.0pt{\vbox{\vbox{\hrule height=0.3pt\hbox{\vrule height=1.92pt,width=0.3pt,depth=0.47998pt\hbox to2.4pt{\hfil}\vrule height=1.92pt,width=0.3pt,depth=0.47998pt}\hrule height=0.3pt}\vskip-0.3pt}}\hskip 0.0pt}}{p_{\!\varnothing}}
. For
i\in\{1,n,n+1\}
, the coefficient of the
i
th simple root in the highest root is equal to
1
, and the corresponding terms in our superpotential are:

\mathcal{W}_{1}=\frac{p_{\lambda_{1}^{+}}}{p_{\lambda_{1}}},\quad\mathcal{W}_{n}=\frac{p_{\mu_{n-1}^{+}}}{p_{\mu_{n-1}}},\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{W}_{n+1}=q\frac{p_{\mu_{n-2}}}{p_{\mu_{n}}}.

For
i\in[2,n-1]
, the coefficient of the
i
th simple root in the highest root is equal to
2
, and we will give an recursive definition for
\mathcal{W}_{i}
for
2\leq i\leq n-1
.

Beginning with
M_{i,0}=\{(\mu_{i-1},\lambda_{i})\}
, we recursively define
M_{i,j}
as the set of all pairs of Young diagrams that can be obtained from a pair
(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\in M_{i,j-1}
by moving a single box from
\tau
to
\tau^{\prime}
. Note that
\mu_{i-1}
has
\ell_{i-1}=\binom{i}{2}
boxes, so this process terminates.

Next, define
N_{i,j}
to be those pairs of Young diagrams that are obtained from pairs
(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\in M_{i,j}
by adding a box labeled
n+1-i
to either
\tau
or
\tau^{\prime}
, if possible. It will follow from the type-independent results to be presented in
Spacek_Wang_Canonical_models
that a box labeled
n+1-i
can only be added to at most one of
\tau
and
\tau^{\prime}
. For
(\mu_{i-1},\lambda_{i})\in M_{i,0}
this can be seen directly: a box labeled
n+1-i
can be added at the bottom of the first row of
\mu_{i-1}
, but
\lambda_{i}
only accepts a box labeled
n
or
n+1
(depending on
i
odd or even); hence, we always have
N_{i,0}=\{(\mu_{i-1}^{+},\lambda_{i})\}
. The term
\mathcal{W}_{i}
is then given by

\mathcal{W}_{i}=\frac{\sum_{j}\sum_{(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\in N_{i,j}}(-1)^{j}p_{\tau}p_{\tau^{\prime}}}{\sum_{j}\sum_{(\tau,\tau^{\prime})\in M_{i,j}}(-1)^{j}p_{\tau}p_{\tau^{\prime}}}.

Finally, the canonical mirror variety is the complement
X^{\vee}_{\text{can}}\subset\mathbb{X}^{\vee}
of the singular locus of
\mathcal{W}_{\text{can}}
. This is the origin of the term
canonical
mirror model: the degrees of the denominators of the terms of
\mathcal{W}_{\text{can}}
add up to the index of
\mathbb{X}^{\vee}
so they form an anti-canonical divisor and
\mathcal{W}_{\text{can}}
is regular on the complement of an anti-canonical divisor.

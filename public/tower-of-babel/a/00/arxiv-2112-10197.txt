Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to introduce and to investigate the basic properties of q q -convex, q q -affine and q q -concave sequences and to establish their surprising connection to Chebyshev polynomials of the first and of the second kind. One of the main results shows that q q -concave sequences are the pointwise minima of q q -affine sequences. As an application, we consider a nonlinear selfmap of the n n -dimensional space and prove that it has a unique fixed point. For the proof of this result, we introduce a new norm on the space in terms of a q q -concave sequence and show that the nonlinear operator becomes a contraction with respect to this norm, and hence, the Banach Fixed Point theorem can be applied.

Let
\mathbb{R}
,
\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\mathbb{Z}
and
\mathbb{N}
denote the sets of real, positive real, integer and positive integer numbers in this paper. Given
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
with
2\leq m-n
, let
\mathscr{S}(n|m)
denote the linear space
\mathbb{R}^{\{n,\dots,m\}}
of all real sequences, i.e., the collection of all functions
p:\{n,\dots,m\}\to\mathbb{R}
. It is natural to define the notions of concavity, convexity and affinity for the elements of
\mathscr{S}(n|m)
. A sequence
p=(p_{n},\dots,p_{m})\in\mathscr{S}(n|m)
is called
convex
if, for all
i\in\{n+1,\dots,m-1\}
,

(1)

\displaystyle 2p_{i}\leq p_{i-1}+p_{i+1}.

If, for all
i\in\{n+1,\dots,m-1\}
, the reversed inequality holds in (
1
), then the sequence is termed
concave
. Finally, if a sequence is simultaneously convex and concave, then it is said to be
affine
. If the inequality (
1
) holds with strict inequality sign, then we speak about strict convexity and concavity, respectively.

In what follows, we extend the above definitions and introduce the notions of
q
-convex,
q
-concave, and
q
-affine sequences with respect to a positive number
q
. A sequence
p=(p_{n},\dots,p_{m})\in\mathscr{S}(n,m)
is called
q
-convex
if, for all
i\in\{n+1,\dots,m-1\}
,

(2)

\displaystyle 2qp_{i}\leq p_{i-1}+p_{i+1}.

If, for all
i\in\{n+1,\dots,m-1\}
, the reversed inequality holds in (
2
), then the sequence is termed
q
-concave
. If a sequence is simultaneously
q
-convex and
q
-concave, then it is said to be
q
-affine
.

We can easily see that the strict convexity of a positive (or negative) sequence implies its
q
-convexity for some
q
. Indeed, if
p\in\mathscr{S}(nm)
is a positive strictly convex sequence then, for all
i\in\{n+1,\dots,m-1\}
,

\displaystyle 1<\frac{p_{i-1}+p_{i+1}}{2p_{i}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle 1<q:=\min_{i\in\{n+1,\dots,m-1\}}\frac{p_{i-1}+p_{i+1}}{2p_{i}},

which implies that
p
is
q
-convex with a number
q>1
.
Analogously,
p\in\mathscr{S}(nm)
is a negative strictly convex sequence, then it is
q
-convex with a number
0<q<1
.

The subclasses of
q
-convex and
q
-concave sequences in
\mathscr{S}(n|m)
will be denoted
\mathscr{C}^{\cup}_{q}(n|m)
and
\mathscr{C}^{\cap}_{q}(n|m)
, respectively. Finally,
\mathscr{A}_{q}(n|m)
will stand for the subclass of
q
-affine sequences, that is,

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{q}(n|m):=\mathscr{C}^{\cup}_{q}(n|m)\cap\mathscr{C}^{\cap}_{q}(n|m).

It is easy to see that
\mathscr{A}_{q}(n|m)
is a linear subspace of
\mathscr{S}(n|m)
and
\mathscr{C}^{\cup}_{q}(n|m)
and
\mathscr{C}^{\cap}_{q}(n|m)
are convex cones in
\mathscr{S}(n|m)
, i.e., they are closed with respect linear combinations with nonnegative coefficients.

The aim of this paper is to investigate the basic properties of these classes of sequences and to show their surprising connection to Chebyshev polynomials of the first and of the second kind. Therefore, in the next section, we recall the notions of Chebyshev polynomials and establish the basic relationships among them.

In Section 3, we describe all
q
-affine sequences in terms of Chebyshev polynomials and show that
\mathscr{A}_{q}(n|m)
is a two-dimensional linear subspace of
\mathscr{S}(n|m)
. In another result of this section, we deduce inequalities that are consequences of the
q
-convexity/concavity and we also establish an analogue of the so called support theorem and thus we obtain that
q
-concave sequences are the pointwise minima of
q
-affine sequences.

In Section 4, we consider minimum problems for positive sequences in terms of a (power) mean
M
. In the cases when
M
is either the arithmetic, or the geometric, or the maximum mean we obtain the precise solution of this minimum problem. For a general power mean with a positive parameter, we only obtain lower bounds. The case when
M=\max
is strongly connected to the results obtained for
q
-concave sequences.

In the last section, we consider a nonlinear selfmap of the
n
-dimensional space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and prove that it has a unique fixed point. For the proof of this result, we introduce a new norm in terms of
q
-concave sequences and show that the nonlinear operator becomes a contraction with respect to this norm, and hence, by the Banach Fixed Point theorem, it has a unique fixed point.

2.
Auxiliary results for Chebyshev polynomials

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, let
T_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
U_{k}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
denote the Chebyshev polynomials of the first and of the second kind of order
k
, which are defined by the system of equations

(3)

\displaystyle T_{0}(x)

\displaystyle:=1,

\displaystyle\qquad T_{1}(x)

\displaystyle:=x,

\displaystyle\qquad T_{k-1}(x)+T_{k+1}(x)

\displaystyle=2xT_{k}(x)

\displaystyle\qquad(k\in\mathbb{Z}),

\displaystyle U_{0}(x)

\displaystyle:=1,

\displaystyle\qquad U_{1}(x)

\displaystyle:=2x,

\displaystyle\qquad U_{k-1}(x)+U_{k+1}(x)

\displaystyle=2xU_{k}(x)

\displaystyle\qquad(k\in\mathbb{Z}),

respectively. The last equalities in (
3
) rewritten as

\displaystyle T_{k+1}(x)=2xT_{k}(x)-T_{k-1}(x),\qquad U_{k+1}(x)=2xU_{k}(x)-U_{k-1}(x),

can be used to compute
T_{k}
and
U_{k}
for
k\geq 2
recursively. If we rewrite them as

\displaystyle T_{k-1}(x)=2xT_{k}(x)-T_{k+1}(x),\qquad U_{k-1}(x)=2xU_{k}(x)-U_{k+1}(x),

then
T_{k}
and
U_{k}
can be determined for
k\leq-1
.
One can easily prove that, for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

\displaystyle T_{-k}=T_{k}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad U_{-k}=-U_{k-2}.

In particular,
U_{-1}=0
. It is clear that, for
k\geq 0
, the degree of
T_{k}
and
U_{k}
equals
k
.
It is well-known that these polynomials satisfy the equalities

(4)

\displaystyle T_{k}(\cos(u))=\cos(ku)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad T_{k}(\cosh(u))=\cosh(ku)\qquad(u\in\mathbb{R},\,k\in\mathbb{Z})

and

(5)

\displaystyle U_{k}(\cos(u))=\frac{\sin((k+1)u)}{\sin(u)}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad U_{k}(\cosh(u))=\frac{\sinh((k+1)u)}{\sinh(u)}\qquad(u\in\mathbb{R},\,k\in\mathbb{Z}).

From these representations it easily follows that the roots of
T_{k}
(for
k\neq 0
) and
U_{k-1}
(for
k\not\in\{-1,0,1\}
) are given by

\displaystyle\bigg\{\cos\bigg(\frac{2i-1}{2k}\pi\bigg)\mid i\in\{1,\ldots,|k|\}\bigg\}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\bigg\{\cos\bigg(\frac{i}{k}\pi\bigg)\mid i\in\{1,\ldots,|k|-1\}\bigg\},

respectively. Therefore, the largest root of
T_{k}
(for
k\neq 0
) and
U_{k-1}
(for
k\not\in\{-1,0,1\}
) are given by

\displaystyle\cos\bigg(\frac{\pi}{2k}\bigg)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\cos\bigg(\frac{\pi}{k}\bigg),

respectively.

Lemma 2.1
.

For
0\leq x<1
, the sequence
(T_{k}(x))_{k=1}^{\tau(x)}
is strictly decreasing, where
\tau(x):=\big\lfloor\frac{\pi}{\arccos(x)}\big\rfloor
. For
x>1
, the sequence
(T_{k}(x))_{k=0}^{\infty}
is strictly increasing.

Proof.

If
x>1
, then there exists
u>0
such that
x=\cosh(u)
. Thus, in view of the second formula in (
4
), we have

\displaystyle T_{k}(x)=T_{k}(\cosh(u))=\cosh(ku)\qquad(k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}),

which by the strict monotonicity of the
\cosh
function implies that the right hand side is a strictly increasing function of
k
.

If
0\leq x<1
, then there exists
u\in\,]0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
such that
x=\cos(u)
. In view of the first formula in (
4
), we have

\displaystyle T_{k}(x)=T_{k}(\cos(u))=\cos(ku)\qquad(k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}),

which, using that
\cos
is strictly decreasing on
[0,\pi]
, implies that
T_{k}(x)
is strictly decreasing for
k\in\{0,\dots,\big\lfloor\frac{\pi}{u}\big\rfloor\}
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be an odd number. Then, for all
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
with the notation
x_{i+n}:=x_{i}
(
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
), we have

(6)

\displaystyle\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sin\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}x_{i+j}\bigg)\sin(x_{i})=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sin\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}x_{i+j}\bigg)\cos(x_{i})=0.

Proof.

For
n=1
, the statement is trivial, thus we may assume that
n\geq 3
. Let
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
and denote

\displaystyle y_{i}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}x_{i+j}\qquad(i\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}).

Then, by the well-known product-to-sum identities

\displaystyle 2\sin\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}x_{i+j}\bigg)\sin(x_{i})

\displaystyle=2\sin(x_{i})\sin(y_{i})=\cos(x_{i}-y_{i})-\cos(x_{i}+y_{i}),

\displaystyle 2\sin\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}x_{i+j}\bigg)\cos(x_{i})

\displaystyle=2\cos(x_{i})\sin(y_{i})=\sin(x_{i}+y_{i})-\sin(x_{i}-y_{i}).

Observe that, by the equality
x_{i}=x_{i+n}
and by the oddness of
n
, we have

\displaystyle x_{i}-y_{i}

\displaystyle=x_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}x_{i+j}=x_{i}+x_{i+1}+\sum_{j=2}^{n-1}(-1)^{j-1}x_{i+j}

\displaystyle=x_{i+1}+(-1)^{n-1}x_{i+n}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-2}(-1)^{j}x_{i+1+j}=x_{i+1}+y_{i+1}.

Therefore

\displaystyle 2\sin\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}x_{i+j}\bigg)\sin(x_{i})

\displaystyle=\cos(x_{i+1}+y_{i+1})-\cos(x_{i}+y_{i}),

\displaystyle 2\sin\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{j}x_{i+j}\bigg)\cos(x_{i})

\displaystyle=\sin(x_{i}+y_{i})-\sin(x_{i+1}+y_{i+1}).

Summing up these equalities side by side for
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, respectively, we can see that the right hand sides are telescopic sums which are equal to zero, hence both equalities in (
6
) hold true.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For all
i,j,k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

(7)

\displaystyle U_{k-j-1}U_{i}+U_{j-i-1}U_{k}=U_{k-i-1}U_{j}\qquad\text{and}\qquad U_{k-j-1}T_{i}+U_{j-i-1}T_{k}=U_{k-i-1}T_{j}.

Furthermore, for
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we also have

(8)

\displaystyle U_{i-j}+U_{i+j}=2T_{j}U_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad T_{i-j}+T_{i+j}=2T_{j}T_{i}.

Proof.

In the particular case
n=3
, with
x_{1}:=x
,
x_{2}:=y
and
x_{3}:=z
, the identities in (
6
) yield

(9)

\displaystyle\sin(z-y)\sin(x)+\sin(y-x)\sin(z)

\displaystyle=\sin(z-x)\sin(y),

\displaystyle\sin(z-y)\cos(x)+\sin(y-x)\cos(z)

\displaystyle=\sin(z-x)\cos(y).

Let
q\in\,]-1,1[\,
be arbitrary, let
u:=\arccos(q)
and let
i,j,k\in\mathbb{Z}
. With the substitutions
(x,y,z):=((i+1)u,(j+1)u,(k+1)u)
and
(x,y,z):=(iu,ju,ku)
, the first and second identities in (
9
) imply

\displaystyle\frac{\sin((k-j)u)}{\sin(u)}\cdot\frac{\sin((i+1)u)}{\sin(u)}+\frac{\sin((j-i)u)}{\sin(u)}\cdot\frac{\sin((k+1)u)}{\sin(u)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin((k-i)u)}{\sin(u)}\cdot\frac{\sin((j+1)u)}{\sin(u)},

\displaystyle\frac{\sin((k-j)u)}{\sin(u)}\cdot\cos(iu)+\frac{\sin((j-i)u)}{\sin(u)}\cdot\cos(ku)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin((k-i)u)}{\sin(u)}\cdot\cos(ju).

In view of (
4
), from these equalities we can easily obtain that

\displaystyle U_{k-j-1}(q)U_{i}(q)+U_{j-i-1}(q)U_{k}(q)

\displaystyle=U_{k-i-1}(q)U_{j}(q),

\displaystyle U_{k-j-1}(q)T_{i}(q)+U_{j-i-1}(q)T_{k}(q)

\displaystyle=U_{k-i-1}(q)T_{j}(q)

hold for all
q\in\,]-1,1[\,
and hence for all
q\in\mathbb{R}
. This completes the proof of the equalities in (
7
).

To prove (
8
), let
q\in\,]-1,1[\,
be arbitrary, let
u:=\arccos(q)
and
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Using (
4
) and the addition formula for the sine and cosine functions, we obtain

\displaystyle U_{i-j}(q)+U_{i+j}(q)

\displaystyle=U_{i-j}(\cos(u))+U_{i+j}(\cos(u))=\frac{\sin((i-j+1)u)}{\sin(u)}+\frac{\sin((i+j+1)u)}{\sin(u)}

\displaystyle=2\frac{\sin((i+1)u)}{\sin(u)}\cos(ju)=2U_{i}(\cos(u))T_{j}(\cos(u))=2U_{i}(q)T_{j}(q)

and

\displaystyle T_{i-j}(q)+T_{i+j}(q)

\displaystyle=T_{i-j}(\cos(u))+T_{i+j}(\cos(u))=\cos((i-j)u)+\cos((i+j)u)

\displaystyle=2\cos(iu)\cos(ju)=2T_{i}(\cos(u))T_{j}(\cos(u))=2T_{i}(q)T_{j}(q).

This completes the proof of (
8
).
∎

j=1

Remark 2.4
.

For the difference of two Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind, using the equality
-U_{k}=U_{-k-2}
, we can deduce the following identity:

(10)

\displaystyle U_{i+j}-U_{i-j}=U_{i+j}+U_{-i+j-2}=U_{(j-1)+(i+1)}+U_{(j-1)-(i+1)}=2T_{i+1}U_{j-1}.

On the other hand, to compute the difference of two Chebyshev polynomials of the first kind, the following equality can be established:

(11)

\displaystyle T_{j-i}(q)-T_{j+i}(q)=2(1-q^{2})U_{j-1}(q)U_{i-1}(q).

To prove this, let
q\in\,]-1,1[\,
be arbitrary, let
u:=\arccos(q)
and
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
. Using (
4
) and the addition formula for the cosine function, we get

\displaystyle 2U_{j-1}(q)U_{i-1}(q)

\displaystyle=2U_{j-1}(\cos(u))U_{i-1}(\cos(u))=2\frac{\sin(ju)}{\sin(u)}\frac{\sin(iu)}{\sin(u)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\cos((j-i)u)-\cos((j+i)u)}{\sin^{2}(u)}=\frac{T_{j-i}(q)-T_{j+i}(q)}{1-\cos^{2}(u)}=\frac{T_{j-i}(q)-T_{j+i}(q)}{1-q^{2}}.

From here, (

3.
q
-concave, convex and affine sequences

The next proposition shows that
\mathscr{A}_{q}(n|m)
is a two dimensional subspace of
\mathscr{S}(n|m)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

A sequence
p\in\mathscr{S}(n|m)
is
q
-affine if and only if there exist
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(12)

\displaystyle p_{i}:=aU_{i-n}(q)+bT_{i-n}(q)\qquad(i\in\{n,\dots,m\}).

In addition, if
p\in\mathscr{A}_{q}(n|m)
, then, for all
i,j,k\in\{n,\dots,m\}
,

(13)

\displaystyle U_{k-j-1}(q)p_{i}+U_{j-i-1}(q)p_{k}=U_{k-i-1}(q)p_{j}.

In particular, for
i\in\{n,\dots,m\}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,\min(i-n,m-i)\}
,

(14)

\displaystyle p_{i-j}+p_{i+j}=2T_{j}(q)p_{i}.

Proof.

First assume that
p=(p_{n},\dots,p_{m})
is
q
-affine. Define

\displaystyle a:=\frac{p_{n+1}}{q}-p_{n},\qquad b:=2p_{n}-\frac{p_{n+1}}{q}.

We prove the equality (
12
) by induction with respect to
i
. Observe that
p_{n}=a+b=aU_{0}(q)+bT_{0}(q)
and
p_{n+1}=a(2q)+bq=aU_{1}(q)+bT_{1}(q)
, which show that (
12
) holds for
i=n
and
i=n+1
. Assume that we have proved (
12
) for

i\leq\ell
, where
n+1\leq\ell\leq m-1
. Then, applying the
q
-affinity of the sequence, the inductive hypothesis and finally the recursive property of Chebyshev polynomials, we obtain

\displaystyle p_{\ell+1}=2qp_{\ell}-p_{\ell-1}

\displaystyle=2q(aU_{\ell-n}(q)+bT_{\ell-n}(q))-(aU_{\ell-1-n}(q)+bT_{\ell-1-n}(q))

\displaystyle=a(2qU_{\ell-n}(q)-U_{\ell-1-n}(q))+b(2qT_{\ell-n}(q)-T_{\ell-1-n}(q))

\displaystyle=aU_{\ell+1-n}(q)+bT_{\ell+1-n}(q).

This shows the validity of (
12
) for
i=\ell+1
.

For the sufficiency part of our assertion, assume that (
12
) holds for some
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, by the recursive property of Chebyshev polynomials, for
i\in\{n+1,\dots,m-1\}
, we have that

\displaystyle p_{i+1}=aU_{i+1-n}(q)+bT_{i+1-n}(q)

\displaystyle=a(2qU_{i-n}(q)-U_{i-1-n}(q))+b(2qT_{i-n}(q)-T_{i-1-n}(q))

\displaystyle=2q(aU_{i-n}(q)+bT_{i-n}(q))-(aU_{i-1-n}(q)+bT_{i-1-n}(q))

\displaystyle=2qp_{i}-p_{i-1},

which proves that
p
is a
q
-affine sequence.

To verify the last two assertions let
p\in\mathscr{A}_{q}(n|m)
. Then, as we have seen it, (
12
) holds for some
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let first
i,j,k\in\{n,\dots,m\}
be arbitrary. Then, applying Lemma
2.3
, we get

\displaystyle U_{k-j-1}(q)U_{i-n}(q)+U_{j-i-1}(q)U_{k-n}(q)

\displaystyle=U_{k-i-1}(q)U_{j-n}(q)\qquad\text{and}

\displaystyle U_{k-j-1}(q)T_{i-n}(q)+U_{j-i-1}(q)T_{k-n}(q)

\displaystyle=U_{k-i-1}T_{j-n}(q).

Multiplying the first and second equalities by
a
and
b
, respectively, and then adding them up side by side, we obtain

\displaystyle U_{k-j-1}(q)(aU_{i-n}(q)+bT_{i-n}(q))+U_{j-i-1}(q)

\displaystyle(aU_{k-n}(q)+bT_{k-n}(q))

\displaystyle=U_{k-i-1}(q)(aU_{j-n}(q)+bT_{j-n}(q)),

which, in view of (
12
), shows that (
13
) holds.

Finally, let
i\in\{n,\dots,m\}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,\min(i-n,m-i)\}
. In view of (
8
), we have that

\displaystyle U_{i-j-n}(q)+U_{i+j-n}(q)=2T_{j}(q)U_{i-n}(q),\qquad T_{i-j-n}(q)+T_{i+j-n}(q)=2T_{j}(q)T_{i-n}(q).

Multiplying the first and second equalities by
a
and
b
, respectively, and then adding them up side by side, we obtain

\displaystyle p_{i-j}+p_{i+j}

\displaystyle=(aU_{i-j-n}(q)+bT_{i-j-n}(q))+(aU_{i+j-n}(q)+bT_{i+j-n}(q))

\displaystyle=2T_{j}(q)(aU_{i-n}(q)+bT_{i-n}(q))=2T_{j}(q)p_{i}.

This completes the proof of (
14
).
∎

In the following statement, we establish some properties of the class of
q
-concave (and hence of
q
-convex) sequences.

Proposition 3.2
.

The cone
\mathscr{C}_{q}^{\cap}(n|m)
is closed with respect to the pointwise minimum and the cone
\mathscr{C}_{q}^{\cup}(n|m)
is closed with respect to the pointwise maximum.

Proof.

To prove the statement for
\mathscr{C}_{q}^{\cap}(n|m)
, let
p,r\in\mathscr{C}_{q}^{\cap}(n|m)
be arbitrary and denote
s:=\min(p,r)
(i.e.,
s_{i}:=\min(p_{i},r_{i})
for all
i\in\{n,\dots,m\}
). Let
i\in\{n+1,\dots,m-1\}
. Then, by the
q
-concavity of
p
and
r
, we have

\displaystyle s_{i-1}+s_{i+1}\leq p_{i-1}+p_{i+1}\leq qp_{i}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad s_{i-1}+s_{i+1}\leq r_{i-1}+r_{i+1}\leq qr_{i}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle s_{i-1}+s_{i+1}\leq\min(qp_{i},qr_{i})=q\min(p_{i},r_{i})=qs_{i},

which shows that
s
is also
q
-concave. The proof of the statement for
\mathscr{C}_{q}^{\cup}(n|m)
is analogous.
∎

As
q
-affine sequences are
q
-concave and also
q
-convex, we obtain that the pointwise minimum and maximum of a finite family of

-affine sequences are
q
-concave and also
q
-convex, respectively.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
i,j,k\in\{n,\dots,m\}
with
i<j<k
. Assume that

(15)

\displaystyle q\geq\cos\bigg(\frac{\pi}{\max(j-i,k-j)}\bigg).

Then, for all

,

(16)

\displaystyle U_{k-j-1}(q)p_{i}+U_{j-i-1}(q)p_{k}\leq U_{k-i-1}(q)p_{j}.

In particular, if
i\in\{n+1,\dots,m-1\}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,\min(i-n,m-i)\}
and

(17)

\displaystyle q>\cos\bigg(\frac{\pi}{j}\bigg),

then

(18)

\displaystyle p_{i-j}+p_{i+j}\leq 2T_{j}(q)p_{i}.

Proof.

We shall verify (
16
) by induction
on
\ell:=k-i
. If
\ell=2
, that is,

, then (

) is equivalent to the

-concavity of

, because

and

.

Assume that we have verified (
16
) for all

with

, where

. Suppose that

and (

\max(j-i,k-j)\geq 2
. We now distinguish two cases.

The first the case is when

. Then
k-(i+1)=\ell
and

\displaystyle q\geq\cos\bigg(\frac{\pi}{\max(j-(i+1),k-j)}\bigg)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad q\geq\cos\bigg(\frac{\pi}{\max((i+1)-i,j-(i+1))}\bigg).

Thus, applying the inductive hypotheses for the triplets

, we obtain

\displaystyle U_{k-j-1}(q)p_{i+1}+U_{j-i-2}(q)p_{k}

\displaystyle\leq U_{k-i-2}(q)p_{j},

\displaystyle U_{j-i-2}(q)p_{i}+U_{0}(q)p_{j}

\displaystyle\leq U_{j-i-1}(q)p_{i+1}.

The inequality (

is nonsmaller than the largest roots of

and
U_{k-j-1}

. Multiplying the first inequality by
U_{j-i-1}(q)

U_{k-j-1}(q)
, and adding up the inequalities so obtained side by side, we get

\displaystyle U_{k-j-1}(q)U_{j-i-2}(q)p_{i}+U_{j-i-1}(q)U_{j-i-2}(q)p_{k}\leq\big(U_{j-i-1}(q)U_{k-i-2}(q)-U_{k-j-1}(q)U_{0}(q)\big)p_{j}.

On the other hand, applying Lemma

\displaystyle U_{j-i-1}(q)U_{k-i-2}(q)=U_{0}(q)U_{k-j-1}(q)+U_{j-i-2}(q)U_{k-i-1}(q).

Therefore, the above inequality can be rewritten as

\displaystyle U_{k-j-1}(q)U_{j-i-2}(q)p_{i}+U_{j-i-1}(q)U_{j-i-2}(q)p_{k}\leq U_{j-i-2}(q)U_{k-i-1}(q)p_{j}.

By (

),

is strictly bigger than

, which is the largest root of

. If

. Now dividing the last inequality by this positive value side by side, we arrive at the desired inequality (

The proof in the second case when

Finally, let
i\in\{n+1,\dots,m-1\}
and

(19)

\displaystyle U_{j-1}(q)p_{i-j}+U_{j-1}(q)p_{i+j}\leq U_{2j-1}(q)p_{i}=2U_{j-1}(q)T_{j}(q)p_{i}.

In view of (

∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
j,k\in\{n,\dots,m\}
with
j<k
. In addition, assume that

(20)

\displaystyle q>\cos\Big(\frac{\pi}{k-j}\Big).

Let

and define

\displaystyle r_{i}:=p_{k}\frac{U_{i-j-1}(q)}{U_{k-j-1}(q)}+p_{j}\frac{U_{k-i-1}(q)}{U_{k-j-1}(q)}\qquad(i\in\{n,\dots,m\}).

Then,
r=(r_{n},\dots,r_{m})
is a
q
-affine sequence and, for

,

\displaystyle r_{i}\begin{cases}\geq p_{i}&\text{ if }\ i<j\text{ or }k<i.\\
=p_{i}&\text{ if }\ i\in\{j,k\}.\\
\leq p_{i}&\text{ if }\ j<i<k.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
k-j=1
, then
U_{k-j-1}(q)=U_{0}(q)=1>0
. If
k-j\geq 2
, then
q
is bigger than the largest root of
U_{k-j-1}
. Therefore
U_{k-j-1}(q)>0
and hence the sequence
(r_{i})
is well-defined. From the recursive formula (
3
) of Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind, for

, it follows that

\displaystyle U_{(i-1)-j-1}(q)+U_{(i+1)-j-1}(q)=2qU_{i-j-1}(q),\qquad U_{k-(i-1)-1}(q)+U_{k-(i+1)-1}(q)=2qU_{k-i-1}(q).

Multiplying theses equalities by
\frac{p_{k}}{U_{k-j-1}(q)}
and by

\frac{p_{j}}{U_{k-j-1}(q)}
, respectively, and then adding them up side by side, we obtain that

, which shows that
(r_{i})
is a

-affine sequence.

If
i=j
, or
i=k
, then, by
U_{-1}=0
, we can see that

and

. Suppose first that

. From the equality (

) of the second assertion of Proposition

-affine sequence
(r_{i})

\displaystyle U_{k-j-1}(q)r_{i}=U_{k-i-1}(q)r_{j}+U_{i-j-1}(q)r_{k}.

On the other hand, applying inequality (

3.3

(p_{i})

\displaystyle U_{k-i-1}(q)p_{j}+U_{i-j-1}(q)p_{k}\leq U_{k-j-1}(q)p_{i}

Using that

and

\displaystyle U_{k-j-1}(q)r_{i}=U_{k-i-1}(q)r_{j}+U_{i-j-1}(q)r_{k}=U_{k-i-1}(q)p_{j}+U_{i-j-1}(q)p_{k}\leq U_{k-j-1}(q)p_{i},

which, by

.

For the remaining inequalities, suppose first that

. By the

, the second assertion of Proposition

implies

\displaystyle U_{k-i-1}(q)r_{j}=U_{k-j-1}(q)r_{i}+U_{j-i-1}(q)r_{k}

and hence

\displaystyle U_{k-j-1}(q)r_{i}=U_{k-i-1}(q)r_{j}-U_{j-i-1}(q)r_{k}.

On the other hand, applying inequality (

) of Proposition

for the

\displaystyle U_{k-j-1}(q)p_{i}+U_{j-i-1}(q)p_{k}\leq U_{k-i-1}(q)p_{j}

\displaystyle U_{k-i-1}(q)p_{j}-U_{j-i-1}(q)p_{k}\geq U_{k-j-1}(q)p_{i}.

Combining these inequalities and using

\displaystyle U_{k-j-1}(q)r_{i}=U_{k-i-1}(q)r_{j}-U_{j-i-1}(q)r_{k}=U_{k-i-1}(q)p_{j}-U_{j-i-1}(q)p_{k}\geq U_{k-j-1}(q)p_{i}.

The proof of

In the following proposition, we establish a characterization of

-concave sequences.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let

. Then

is

-concave if and only if, for all

, there exists

such that

(21)

\displaystyle p_{j}=r_{j},\qquad p_{j+1}=r_{j+1},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad p_{i}\leq r_{i}\quad\mbox{for}\quad i\in\{n,\dots,m\}.

Proof.

Assume first that

is
q
-concave and let

. Then, with

, we can see that (

) holds, therefore applying Proposition

, the sequence

defined by

\displaystyle r_{i}:=p_{j+1}U_{i-j-1}(q)+p_{j}U_{j-i}(q)

is

-affine and satisfies all te conditions in (

).

To prove the sufficiency part of the assertion, assume that

, there exists

-affine sequence

such that

\displaystyle p_{j}=r_{j}^{j},\qquad p_{j+1}=r_{j+1}^{j},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad p_{i}\leq r_{i}^{j}\quad\mbox{for}\quad i\in\{n,\dots,m\}.

which shows that

∎

4.
A minimax-type problem

Throughout this section,
n,m
are integers with
2\leq m-n
and we consider the following minimum problem: Let
M:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{m-n-1}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be an
(m-n-1)
-variable mean. Our aim is to find the largest nonnegative constant
C_{M}
such that, for all
p\in\mathscr{S}(n|m)
with
p_{n},p_{m}\geq 0
and
p_{n+1},\dots,p_{m-1}>0
,

\displaystyle C_{M}\leq M\bigg(\frac{p_{n}+p_{n+2}}{2p_{n+1}},\dots,\frac{p_{i-1}+p_{i+1}}{2p_{i}},\dots,\frac{p_{m-2}+p_{m}}{2p_{m-1}}\bigg).

By taking
p
as a constant sequence, one can see that the right hand side of this inequality then equals
1
, hence it follows that
C_{M}\leq 1
. As we shall see below, this estimate can be essentially improved for several concrete means.

In the case when
M
is the
(m-n-1)
-variable arithmetic mean
A_{m-n-1}
, we can obtain the following result.

Proposition 4.1
.

C_{A}=\frac{m-n-2}{m-n-1}
, that is, for all
p\in\mathscr{S}(n|m)
with
p_{n},p_{m}\geq 0
and
p_{n+1},\dots,p_{m-1}>0
,

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{m-n-2}{m-n-1}\leq\frac{1}{m-n-1}\sum_{i=n+1}^{m-1}\frac{p_{i-1}+p_{i+1}}{2p_{i}}

and the constant on the left hand side is the best possible.

Proof.

If
m-n=2
, that is,
m=n+2
, then the left hand side of (
22
) equals zero, thus, the inequality is trivial. On the other hand, for
(p_{n},p_{n+1},p_{n+2})=(0,1,0)
equality holds in (
22
).
Thus, in the rest of the proof, we may assume that
m-n>2
.

To prove (
22
), let
p\in\mathscr{S}(n|m)
with
p_{n},p_{m}\geq 0
and
p_{n+1},\dots,p_{m-1}>0
. Then (using the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality in the last step), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=n+1}^{m-1}\frac{p_{i-1}+p_{i+1}}{2p_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{n}+p_{n+2}}{2p_{n+1}}+\sum_{i=n+2}^{m-2}\frac{p_{i-1}+p_{i+1}}{2p_{i}}+\frac{p_{m-2}+p_{m}}{2p_{m-1}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{p_{n+2}}{2p_{n+1}}+\sum_{i=n+2}^{m-2}\bigg(\frac{p_{i-1}}{2p_{i}}+\frac{p_{i+1}}{2p_{i}}\bigg)+\frac{p_{m-2}}{2p_{m-1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=n+1}^{m-2}\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{p_{i+1}}{p_{i}}+\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i+1}}\bigg)\geq\sum_{i=n+1}^{m-2}\sqrt{\frac{p_{i+1}}{p_{i}}\cdot\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i+1}}}=m-n-2.

Dividing the above obtained inequality by
m-n-1
side by side, we can see that (
22
) holds. On the other hand, for
(p_{n},p_{n+1},\dots,p_{m-1},p_{m})=(0,1,\dots,1,0)
equality holds in (
22
), therefore, the left hand side of (
22
) is the largest possible, indeed.
∎

In order to reach a higher level of generality, for
r\in[-\infty,\infty]
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
k
-variable
r
th power mean (or Hölder mean)
of the variables
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
by

\displaystyle H_{r,k}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k}):=\begin{cases}\min(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})&\mbox{if }r=-\infty,\\[5.69054pt]
\bigg(\dfrac{u_{1}^{r}+\dots+u_{k}^{r}}{k}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}&\mbox{if }r\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\},\\[8.53581pt]
\sqrt[k]{u_{1}\cdots u_{k}}&\mbox{if }r=0,\\[5.69054pt]
\max(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})&\mbox{if }r=\infty.\end{cases}

Obviously, the mean
H_{1,k}
equals the
k
-variable arithmetic mean
A_{k}
and
H_{0,k}
equals the
k
-variable geometric mean
G_{k}
. It is well known that, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
-\infty\leq r\leq s\leq\infty
, the comparison inequality
H_{r,k}\leq H_{s,k}
holds. In particular,
G_{k}\leq A_{k}
, which is the celebrated inequality between the geometric and arithmetic means.

For the investigation of the more general problem in terms of power means, for
r\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we introduce the function
F_{r,k}:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
by

\displaystyle F_{r,k}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k}):=u_{1}^{r}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\Big(\frac{1}{u_{i}}+u_{i+1}\Big)^{r}+\frac{1}{u_{k}^{r}}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
r>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

(23)

\displaystyle F_{r,k}\geq\begin{cases}2&\mbox{if }k=1,\\
2^{\frac{r+1}{2}}+(k-2)2^{r}+2^{\frac{r+1}{2}}&\mbox{if }k\geq 2,\,r\leq 1,\\
2^{r}k^{1-r}\big(2^{\frac{1-r}{2r}}+(k-2)+2^{\frac{1-r}{2r}}\big)^{r}&\mbox{if }k\geq 2,\,r\geq 1,\\
\end{cases}

and the estimates are sharp if
k\in\{1,2\}
or
r=1
. Furthermore, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}

(24)

\displaystyle F_{r,k}\geq k2^{r+\frac{1-r}{k}},

which is also sharp if if
k\in\{1,2\}
or
r=1
.
In the particular case when
k
is odd, we also have that

(25)

\displaystyle F_{r,k}\geq k+1,

which is sharp if
k=1
and which is sharper than (
23
) and (
24
) if
r
is a sufficiently small positive number.

Proof.

If
k=1
then, by the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, for all
u_{1}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we easily get

\displaystyle F_{r,1}(u_{1})=u_{1}^{r}+\frac{1}{u_{1}^{r}}=2A_{2}\Big(u_{1}^{r},\frac{1}{u_{1}^{r}}\Big)\geq 2G_{2}\Big(u_{1}^{r},\frac{1}{u_{1}^{r}}\Big)=2\sqrt{u_{1}^{r}\cdot\frac{1}{u_{1}^{r}}}=2.

Observe that
F_{r,1}(1)=2
, hence the lower estimate
2
is best possible in this case.

Now assume that
r\leq 1
and
k\geq 2
, and let
u_{1},\dots,u_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
be arbitrary. Then, by the comparison inequality
H_{r,2}\leq H_{1,2}=A_{2}
, for all
i\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{u_{i}}+u_{i+1}\Big)=A_{2}\Big(\frac{1}{u_{i}},u_{i+1}\Big)\geq H_{r,2}\Big(\frac{1}{u_{i}},u_{i+1}\Big)=\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{u_{i}^{r}}+(u_{i+1})^{r}\Big)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

Using this inequality and arithmetic-geometric mean inequality at the end, we obtain

\displaystyle F_{r,k}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})

\displaystyle:=u_{1}^{r}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}2^{r}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{u_{i}}+u_{i+1}\Big)\Big)^{r}+\frac{1}{u_{k}^{r}}

\displaystyle\geq u_{1}^{r}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}2^{r}\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{u_{i}^{r}}+(u_{i+1})^{r}\Big)+\frac{1}{u_{k}^{r}}

\displaystyle=u_{1}^{r}+2^{r-1}\frac{1}{u_{1}^{r}}+\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}2^{r-1}\Big(\frac{1}{u_{i}^{r}}+u_{i}^{r}\Big)+2^{r-1}u_{k}+\frac{1}{u_{k}}

\displaystyle=2A_{2}\Big(u_{1}^{r},2^{r-1}\frac{1}{u_{1}^{r}}\Big)+\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}2^{r}A_{2}\Big(\frac{1}{u_{i}^{r}},u_{i}^{r}\Big)+2A_{2}\big(2^{r-1}u_{k},\frac{1}{u_{k}}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq 2G_{2}\Big(u_{1}^{r},2^{r-1}\frac{1}{u_{1}^{r}}\Big)+\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}2^{r}G_{2}\Big(\frac{1}{u_{i}^{r}},u_{i}^{r}\Big)+2G_{2}\big(2^{r-1}u_{k},\frac{1}{u_{k}}\Big)

\displaystyle=2^{\frac{r+1}{2}}+(k-2)2^{r}+2^{\frac{r+1}{2}}.

This proves the assertion when
r\leq 1
and
k\geq 2
.
Finally, by arithmetic-geometric mean inequality again, we get

\displaystyle F_{r,k}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})

\displaystyle\geq 2^{\frac{r+1}{2}}+(k-2)2^{r}+2^{\frac{r+1}{2}}=kA_{k}\big(2^{\frac{r+1}{2}},2^{r},\dots,2^{r},2^{\frac{r+1}{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\geq kG_{k}\big(2^{\frac{r+1}{2}},2^{r},\dots,2^{r},2^{\frac{r+1}{2}}\big)=k\big(2^{(k-2)r+r+1}\big)^{\frac{1}{k}}=k2^{r+\frac{1-r}{k}},

which shows that (
24
) is also valid.

In the case
r\geq 1
and
k\geq 2
, using the comparison inequality
A_{2k}=H_{1,2k}\geq H_{\frac{1}{r},2k}
and the
2
-variable arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle F_{r,k}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})

\displaystyle=u_{1}^{r}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}2\cdot\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{u_{i}}+u_{i+1}\Big)^{r}+\frac{1}{u_{k}^{r}}

\displaystyle=2kA_{2k}\bigg(u_{1}^{r},\dots,\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{u_{i}}+u_{i+1}\Big)^{r},\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{u_{i}}+u_{i+1}\Big)^{r},\dots\frac{1}{u_{k}^{r}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq 2kH_{\frac{1}{r},2k}\bigg(u_{1}^{r},\dots,\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{u_{i}}+u_{i+1}\Big)^{r},\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{u_{i}}+u_{i+1}\Big)^{r},\dots\frac{1}{u_{k}^{r}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2k\bigg(\frac{1}{2k}\bigg(u_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}2\cdot 2^{-\frac{1}{r}}\Big(\frac{1}{u_{i}}+u_{i+1}\Big)+\frac{1}{u_{k}}\bigg)\bigg)^{r}

\displaystyle=(2k)^{1-r}\bigg(u_{1}+2^{1-\frac{1}{r}}\frac{1}{u_{1}}+\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}2^{1-\frac{1}{r}}\Big(\frac{1}{u_{i}}+u_{i}\Big)+2^{1-\frac{1}{r}}u_{k}+\frac{1}{u_{k}}\bigg)^{r}

\displaystyle\geq(2k)^{1-r}\big(2\cdot 2^{\frac{r-1}{2r}}+2(k-2)2^{1-\frac{1}{r}}+2\cdot 2^{\frac{r-1}{2r}}\big)^{r}

\displaystyle=2^{r}k^{1-r}\big(2^{\frac{1-r}{2r}}+(k-2)+2^{\frac{1-r}{2r}}\big)^{r}.

This proves the assertion when
r\geq 1
and
k\geq 2
.
Finally, by arithmetic-geometric mean inequality again, we get

\displaystyle F_{r,k}(u_{1},\dots,u_{k})

\displaystyle\geq 2^{r}k^{1-r}\big(2^{\frac{1-r}{2r}}+(k-2)+2^{\frac{1-r}{2r}}\big)^{r}=2^{r}kA_{k}\big(2^{\frac{1-r}{2r}},1,\dots,1,2^{\frac{1-r}{2r}}\big)^{r}

\displaystyle\geq 2^{r}kG_{k}\big(2^{\frac{1-r}{2r}},1,\dots,1,2^{\frac{1-r}{2r}}\big)^{r}=2^{r}k\sqrt[k]{2^{\frac{1-r}{r}}}=k2^{r+\frac{1-r}{k}},

which shows that (
24
) is also valid.

If
k=2
, then the lower estimates (
23
) and (
24
) simplify to the inequality

\displaystyle F_{r,2}\geq 2^{\frac{3+r}{2}}.

On the other hand, with
u_{1}:=2^{\frac{r-1}{2r}}
and

5.
An application of
q
-concave sequences

In this section, we consider a selfmap of the space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which originates from the investigation of approximately convex real functions. Our main aim here is to prove that it has a unique fixed point.

In what follows, we will adopt the following convention: For an arbitrary sequence
a\in\mathscr{S}(1|n)
, let
a
be extended to be in
\mathscr{S}(0|n+1)
by setting
a_{0}:=0
and
a_{n+1}:=0

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and for a vector
\gamma=\big(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\rfloor}\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\rfloor}

\mathscr{T}_{\gamma}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

\displaystyle\big(\mathscr{T}_{\gamma}(a)\big)_{i}:=\min_{1\leq j\leq\min(i,n+1-i)}\Big(\frac{a_{i-j}+a_{i+j}}{2}+\gamma_{j}\Big)\qquad(a\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,i\in\{1,\dots,n\}).

In order to make the map
\mathscr{T}_{\gamma}
a contraction with respect to a suitable norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we construct new norms in terms of positive sequences. Let
|\cdot|_{\infty}
denote the maximum norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is defined as
|a|_{\infty}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|a_{i}|
. If
p\in\mathscr{S}(1|n)
is a sequence with positive members, then we define
\|\cdot\|_{p}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\|a\|_{p}:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}p_{i}^{-1}|a_{i}|=|p^{-1}a|_{\infty}\qquad(a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}).

It is easy to check that
\|\cdot\|_{p}
is a norm, and hence
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a Banach space with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{p}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let

be a sequence with positive members and define

\displaystyle q:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\frac{p_{i-1}+p_{i+1}}{2p_{i}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad q^{*}:=\begin{cases}q&\mbox{if }q\leq 1,\\

\mathscr{T}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\min(x)=\min_{1\leq i\leq k}x_{i}

\displaystyle\leq\min_{1\leq i\leq k}y_{i}+|x-y|_{\infty}=\min(y)+|x-y|_{\infty}.

Interchanging the roles of

\displaystyle-\big(\mathscr{T}_{\gamma}(b)\big)_{i}\big|

\displaystyle=p_{i}^{-1}\bigg|\min_{1\leq j\leq\min(i,n+1-i)}\Big(\frac{a_{i-j}+a_{i+j}}{2}+\gamma_{j}\Big)-\min_{1\leq j\leq\min(i,n+1-i)}\Big(\frac{b_{i-j}+b_{i+j}}{2}+\gamma_{j}\Big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq p_{i}^{-1}\max_{1\leq j\leq\min(i,n+1-i)}\bigg|\Big(\frac{a_{i-j}+a_{i+j}}{2}+\gamma_{j}\Big)-\Big(\frac{b_{i-j}+b_{i+j}}{2}+\gamma_{j}\Big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq\min(i,n+1-i)}\frac{|a_{i-j}-b_{i-j}|+|a_{i+j}-b_{i+j}|}{2p_{i}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq\min(i,n+1-i)}\frac{p_{i-j}+p_{i+j}}{2p_{i}}\|a-b\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{1\leq j\leq\min(i,n+1-i)}T_{j}(q)\|a-b\|_{p}\leq q^{*}\|a-b\|_{p}.

Now, upon taking the maximum with respect to
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\big\|\mathscr{T}_{\gamma}(a)-\mathscr{T}_{\gamma}(b)\big\|_{p}\leq q^{*}\|a-b\|_{p},

which completes the proof of the
q^{*}
-Lipschitz property of
\mathscr{T}_{\gamma}
on
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{p})
.

If if the sequence
p
is strictly concave, then it is
q
-concave with some
q<1
. Therefore, the
q
-Lipschitz property of
\mathscr{T}_{\gamma}
shows that
\mathscr{T}_{\gamma}
is a
q
-contraction on
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{p})
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

For all
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{\lfloor\frac{n+1}{2}\rfloor}
, the mapping
\mathscr{T}_{\gamma}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
has a unique fixed point in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
p_{i}:=i(n+1-i)
for
i\in\{0,\dots,n+1\}
. Then, by the geometric mean-arithmetic mean inequality, we have that
p_{i}\leq\big(\frac{n+1}{2}\big)^{2}
. Thus, for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
j\in\{1,\dots,\min(i,n+1-i)\}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{p_{i-j}+p_{i+j}}{2p_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(i-j)(n+1-i+j)+(i+j)(n+1-i-j)}{2i(n+1-i)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2i(n+1)-2i^{2}-2j^{2}}{2i(n+1-i)}=\frac{i(n+1)-i^{2}-j^{2}}{i(n+1-i)}\leq\frac{i(n+1-i)-1}{i(n+1-i)}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{i}-1}{p_{i}}\leq\frac{\big(\frac{n+1}{2}\big)^{2}-1}{\big(\frac{n+1}{2}\big)^{2}}=\frac{n^{2}+2n-3}{n^{2}+2n+1}=\frac{(n-1)(n+3)}{(n+1)^{2}}.

Therefore, the sequence
p\in\mathscr{S}(0,n+1)
is
q
-concave with
q=\frac{(n-1)(n+3)}{(n+1)^{2}}<1
. According to the Theorem
5.1
, the mapping
\mathscr{T}_{\gamma}
is a
q
-contraction on
(\mathbb{R}^{n},\|\cdot\|_{p})
. Therefore, by the Banach Fixed Point theorem, it possesses a unique fixed point.
∎

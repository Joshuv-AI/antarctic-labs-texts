New characterizations of the Whitney spheres and the contact Whitney spheres Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. Primary 53C24; Secondary 53C25, 53D12 Thanks: This project was supported by NSF of China, Grant Number 11771404.
Zejun Hu and Cheng Xing

Abstract
. In this paper, based on the classical K. Yano’s formula, we first establish an optimal integral inequality for compact Lagrangian submanifolds in the complex space forms, which involves the Ricci curvature in the direction J ​ H → J\vec{H} and the norm of the covariant differentiation of the second fundamental form h h , where J J is the almost complex structure and H → \vec{H} is the mean curvature vector field. Second and analogously, for compact Legendrian submanifolds in the Sasakian space forms with Sasakian structure ( φ , ξ , η , g ) (\varphi,\xi,\eta,g) , we also establish an optimal integral inequality involving the Ricci curvature in the direction φ ​ H → \varphi\vec{H} and the norm of the modified covariant differentiation of the second fundamental form. The integral inequality is optimal in the sense that all submanifolds attaining the equality are completely classified. As direct consequences, we obtain new and global characterizations for the Whitney spheres in complex space forms as well as the contact Whitney spheres in Sasakian space forms. Finally, we show that, just as the Whitney spheres in complex space forms, the contact Whitney spheres in Sasakian space forms are locally conformally flat manifolds with sectional curvatures non-constant.

As the first main result of this paper, we have obtained an optimal integral inequality

Let

n

\int_{M^{n}}{\rm Ric}\,(J\vec{H},J\vec{H})~dV_{M^{n}}\leq\tfrac{(n-1)(n+2)}{3n^{2}}\int_{M^{n}}\|\bar{\nabla}h\|^{2}~dV_{M^{n}},

in
N^{n}(4c)

2.1.

\displaystyle g(Y,Z)X-g(X,Z)Y

\displaystyle+g(JY,Z)JX-g(JX,Z)JY-2g(JX,Y)JZ\big].

Let
M^{n}\hookrightarrow N^{n}(4c)
be a Lagrangian immersion. Then, we have (cf. e.g.
[
15
]
)

(2.4)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}^{\perp}JY=J\nabla^{\perp}_{X}Y,\quad A_{JX}Y=-Jh(X,Y)=A_{JY}X,

and thus
g(h(X,Y),JZ)
is totally symmetric in
X
,
Y
and
Z
:

(2.5)

g(h(X,Y),JZ)=g(h(Y,Z),JX)=g(h(Z,X),JY).

We choose a local adapted Lagrangian frame field

\{e_{1},...,e_{n},e_{1^{*}},\ldots,e_{n^{*}}\}
such that
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
are
orthonormal tangent vector fields, and
e_{1^{*}}=Je_{1},\ldots,e_{n^{*}}=Je_{n}

are orthonormal normal vector fields of
M^{n}\hookrightarrow N^{n}(4c)
,
respectively. In follows we shall make use of the indices convention:

i^{*}=n+i,\ \ 1\leq i,j,k,\ldots\leq n
.

Denote by
\{\omega^{1},\ldots,\omega^{n}\}
the dual frame of
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
.
Let
\omega_{i}^{j}
and
\omega_{i^{*}}^{j^{*}}
denote the connection

1
-forms of
TM^{n}
and
T^{\perp}M^{n}
, respectively:

\nabla e_{i}=\sum^{n}_{j=1}\omega_{i}^{j}e_{j},\ \ \nabla^{\perp}e_{i^{*}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\omega_{i^{*}}^{j^{*}}e_{j^{*}},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n,

where
\omega_{i}^{j}+\omega^{i}_{j}=0
and by (
2.4
) it holds that

\omega_{i}^{j}=\omega_{i^{*}}^{j^{*}}
.
Put
h^{k^{*}}_{ij}=g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),Je_{k})
. From (
2.5
), we see that

(2.6)

h^{k^{*}}_{ij}=h^{j^{*}}_{ik}=h^{i^{*}}_{jk},\ \ 1\leq i,j,k\leq n.

Let
R_{ijkl}:=g\big(R(e_{i},e_{j})e_{l},e_{k}\big)
and

R_{ijk^{*}l^{*}}:=g\big(R^{\perp}(e_{i},e_{j})e_{l^{*}},e_{k^{*}}\big)
be the components of
the curvature tensors of
\nabla
and
\nabla^{\bot}
, respectively. Then the equations
of Gauss, Ricci and Codazzi of
M^{n}\hookrightarrow N^{n}(4c)
are given by

(2.7)

R_{ijkl}=c(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{il}\delta_{jk})+\sum_{m}(h^{m^{*}}_{ik}h^{m^{*}}_{jl}-h^{m^{*}}_{il}h^{m^{*}}_{jk}),

(2.8)

R_{ijk^{*}l^{*}}=c(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{il}\delta_{jk})+\sum_{m=1}^{n}(h^{m^{*}}_{ik}h^{m^{*}}_{jl}-h^{m^{*}}_{il}h^{m^{*}}_{jk})=R_{ijkl},

(2.9)

\displaystyle h^{l^{*}}_{ij,k}=h^{l^{*}}_{ik,j},

where
h^{l^{*}}_{ij,k}
denotes the components of the covariant differentiation
of
h
, namely
\bar{\nabla}h
, defined by

(2.10)

\sum_{l=1}^{n}h^{l^{*}}_{ij,k}e_{l^{*}}:=\nabla^{\perp}_{e_{k}}\big(h(e_{i},e_{j})\big)-h(\nabla_{e_{k}}e_{i},e_{j})-h(e_{i},\nabla_{e_{k}}e_{j}).

The mean curvature vector field
\vec{H}
of
M^{n}\hookrightarrow N^{n}(4c)

is defined by

(2.11)

\vec{H}:=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(e_{i},e_{i})=:\sum_{j=1}^{n}H^{j^{*}}e_{j^{*}},\ \ H^{j^{*}}=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}h^{j^{*}}_{ii},\ \ 1\leq j\leq n.

Put
\nabla^{\perp}_{e_{i}}\vec{H}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}H^{j^{*}}_{,i}e_{j^{*}}
,
1\leq i\leq n
.
From (
2.6
) and (
2.9
), we obtain

(2.12)

H^{j^{*}}_{,i}=H^{i^{*}}_{,j},\ \ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

2.2.
Legendrian submanifolds of the Sasakian space form
\tilde{N}^{2n+1}(\tilde{c})

The following facts of this subsection are referred to e.g.
[
14
]
. The curvature
tensor of the Sasakian space form
\tilde{N}^{2n+1}(\tilde{c})
is given by

(2.13)

\begin{split}\bar{R}(X,Y)Z=&\tfrac{\tilde{c}+3}{4}[g(Y,Z)X-g(X,Z)Y]+\tfrac{\tilde{c}-1}{4}\big[\eta(X)\eta(Z)Y\\
&-\eta(Y)\eta(Z)X+g(X,Z)\eta(Y)\xi-g(Y,Z)\eta(X)\xi\\
&+g(\varphi Y,Z)\varphi X-g(\varphi X,Z)\varphi Y+2g(X,\varphi Y)\varphi Z\big].\end{split}

Moreover, for tangent vector fields
X,Y
of
\tilde{N}^{2n+1}(\tilde{c})
, the Sasakian structure

(\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
of
\tilde{N}^{2n+1}(\tilde{c})
satisfy:

(2.14)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\eta(X)=g(X,\xi),\ \ \varphi\xi=0,\ \ \eta(\varphi X)=0,\\
&\varphi^{2}X=-X+\eta(X)\xi,\ \ d\eta(X,Y)=g(X,\varphi Y),\\
&g(\varphi X,\varphi Y)=g(X,Y)-\eta(X)\eta(Y),\ \ {\rm rank}\,(\varphi)=2n,\\
&\bar{\nabla}_{X}\xi=-\varphi X,\ \ (\bar{\nabla}_{X}\varphi)Y=g(X,Y)\xi-\eta(Y)X.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
M^{n}\hookrightarrow\tilde{N}^{2n+1}(\tilde{c})
be a Legendrian immersion.
Then, we have

(2.15)

A_{\varphi Y}X=-\varphi h(X,Y),\quad\nabla^{\bot}_{X}\varphi Y=\varphi\nabla_{X}Y+g(X,Y)\xi.

In follows we shall make the following convention on range of indices:

\alpha^{*}=\alpha+n;\ \ 1\leq i,j,k,l,m\leq n;\ \ 1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq n+1.

We choose a local
Legendre frame field

\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n},e_{1^{*}},\ldots,e_{n^{*}},e_{2n+1}=\xi\}
along
M^{n}\hookrightarrow\tilde{N}^{2n+1}(\tilde{c})

such that
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
is an orthonormal frame field of
M^{n}
, and
\{e_{1^{*}}=\varphi e_{1},\ldots,e_{n^{*}}=\varphi e_{n},e_{2n+1}=\xi\}
are the orthonormal normal vector fields
of
M^{n}\hookrightarrow\tilde{N}^{2n+1}(\tilde{c})
. Let
\omega_{i}^{j}
and
\omega_{\alpha^{*}}^{\beta^{*}}

denote the connection
1
-forms of
TM^{n}
and
T^{\perp}M^{n}
, respectively:

\nabla e_{i}=\sum^{n}_{j=1}\omega_{i}^{j}e_{j},\ \ \nabla^{\perp}e_{\alpha^{*}}=\sum_{\beta=1}^{n+1}\omega_{\alpha^{*}}^{\beta^{*}}e_{\beta^{*}},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n,\ \ 1\leq\alpha\leq n+1,

where
\omega_{i}^{j}+\omega_{j}^{i}=0
and
\omega_{\alpha^{*}}^{\beta^{*}}+\omega_{\beta^{*}}^{\alpha^{*}}=0
. Moreover, by (
2.15
), we have

\omega_{i}^{j}=\omega_{i^{*}}^{j^{*}}
and
\omega_{i^{*}}^{2n+1}=\omega^{i}
.
Put
h^{k^{*}}_{ij}=g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),\varphi e_{k})
and
h^{2n+1}_{ij}=g(h(e_{i},e_{j}),e_{2n+1})
.
From (
2.14
) and (
2.15
), we have

(2.16)

h^{k^{*}}_{ij}=h^{j^{*}}_{ik}=h^{i^{*}}_{jk},\quad h^{2n+1}_{ij}=0,\ \ 1\leq i,j,k\leq n.

Now, with the same notations as in the preceding subsection, the equations of Gauss, Ricci
and Codazzi of
M^{n}\hookrightarrow\tilde{N}^{2n+1}(\tilde{c})
are as follows:

(2.17)

R_{ijkl}=\tfrac{\tilde{c}+3}{4}(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{il}\delta_{jk})+\sum_{m=1}^{n}(h_{ik}^{m^{*}}h_{jl}^{m^{*}}-h_{il}^{m^{*}}h_{jk}^{m^{*}}),

(2.18)

R_{ijk^{*}l^{*}}=\tfrac{\tilde{c}-1}{4}(\delta_{ik}\delta_{jl}-\delta_{il}\delta_{jk})+\sum_{m=1}^{n}(h^{m^{*}}_{ik}h^{m^{*}}_{jl}-h^{m^{*}}_{il}h^{m^{*}}_{jk}),\ \ R_{ijk^{*}(2n+1)}=0,

(2.19)

h^{\alpha^{*}}_{ij,k}=h^{\alpha^{*}}_{ik,j},

where as usual
h^{\alpha^{*}}_{ij,k}
is defined by

(2.20)

\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n+1}h^{\alpha^{*}}_{ij,k}e_{\alpha^{*}}:=\nabla_{e_{k}}^{\perp}\big(h(e_{i},e_{j})\big)-h(\nabla_{e_{k}}e_{i},e_{j})-h(e_{i},\nabla_{e_{k}}e_{j}),\ \ 1\leq i,j,k\leq n.

Moreover, associated to
\nabla,\,\nabla^{\perp}
and
\bar{\nabla}
, we can naturally define
a modified covariant differentiation
\bar{\nabla}^{\xi}h
of the second fundamental form
by

(2.21)

(\bar{\nabla}^{\xi}_{X}h)(Y,Z):=\nabla_{X}^{\perp}(h(Y,Z))-h(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)-h(Y,\nabla_{X}Z)-g(h(Y,Z),\varphi X)\xi.

Recall that the second fundamental form
h
of
M^{n}\hookrightarrow\tilde{N}^{2n+1}(\tilde{c})

is said to be
C
-parallel if and only if
\bar{\nabla}^{\xi}h=0
(cf.
[
14
]
). Actually, we have

g((\bar{\nabla}^{\xi}_{X}h)(Y,Z),\xi)=0
for any
X,Y,Z\in TM^{n}
. Thus, we
can denote

(2.22)

(\bar{\nabla}^{\xi}_{e_{k}}h)(e_{i},e_{j}):=\sum_{l=1}^{n}\bar{h}^{l^{*}}_{ij,k}e_{l^{*}},\ \ 1\leq i,j,k\leq n.

Then, by (
2.20
), (
2.21
) and the above discussions, we have

(2.23)

h_{ij,k}^{(n+1)^{*}}=h_{ij}^{k^{*}},\ \ h^{l^{*}}_{ij,k}=\bar{h}^{l^{*}}_{ij,k},\ \ \forall\,i,j,k,l.

From (
2.16
), the mean curvature vector
\vec{H}
of

M^{n}\hookrightarrow\tilde{N}^{2n+1}(\tilde{c})
becomes:

(2.24)

\vec{H}=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}h(e_{i},e_{i})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}H^{k^{*}}e_{k^{*}},\ \ H^{k^{*}}:=\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}h^{k^{*}}_{ii},\ \ 1\leq k\leq n.

Put

\nabla^{\perp}_{e_{i}}\vec{H}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n+1}H^{\alpha^{*}}_{,i}e_{\alpha^{*}},\ \ \bar{\nabla}^{\xi}_{e_{i}}\vec{H}:=\nabla^{\perp}_{e_{i}}\vec{H}-g(\vec{H},e_{i^{*}})\xi=:\sum_{k=1}^{n}\bar{H}^{k^{*}}_{,i}e_{k^{*}},\ \ 1\leq i\leq n.

From (
2.16
), (
2.19
) and (
2.23
), we get

(2.25)

H^{j^{*}}_{,i}=H^{i^{*}}_{,j},\quad\bar{H}^{j^{*}}_{,i}=H^{j^{*}}_{,i},\ \ 1\leq i,j\leq n.

Yano’s formula

In order to prove Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
, we still need
the following useful formula due to K. Yano
[
19
]
. A simply proof is referred
also to
[
13
]
.

Lemma 2.1

(cf. Lemma 5.1 of
[
13
]
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold with Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
. Then,
for any tangent vector field
X
on
M
, it holds that

(2.26)

\displaystyle{\rm div}(\nabla_{X}X-({\rm div}X)X)={\rm Ric}\,(X,X)+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\mathcal{L}_{X}g\|^{2}-\|\nabla X\|^{2}-({\rm div}X)^{2},

where
\mathcal{L}_{X}g
is the Lie derivative of
g
with respect to

and

.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

First of all, we state the following simple fact without proof.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
x:M^{n}\rightarrow N^{n}(4c)
be an
n
-dimensional Lagrangian submanifold
with mean curvature vector field
\vec{H}
. Then, it holds that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|\nabla J\vec{H}\|^{2}\geq\tfrac{1}{n}({\rm div}J\vec{H})^{2}.

Moreover, the equality in (
3.1
) holds if and only if

\nabla J\vec{H}=\frac{1}{n}({\rm div}\,J\vec{H})\,{\rm id}
, i.e.,

J\vec{H}
is a conformal vector field on
M^{n}
, or equivalently,

M^{n}
is a Lagrangian submanifold with conformal Maslov form.

We also need the following result due to Li-Vrancken
[
15
]
:

Lemma 3.2

(cf. Lemma 3.2 in
[
15
]
)
.

Let
x:M^{n}\rightarrow N^{n}(4c)
be an
n
-dimensional Lagrangian submanifold
with mean curvature tensor
\vec{H}
. Then, it holds that

(3.2)

\|\bar{\nabla}h\|^{2}\geq\tfrac{3n^{2}}{n+2}\|\nabla^{\perp}\vec{H}\|^{2}.

Moreover, the equality in (
3.2
) holds if and only if

(3.3)

(\bar{\nabla}_{Z}h)(X,Y)=\tfrac{n}{n+2}\big[g(Y,Z)\nabla_{X}^{\perp}\vec{H}+g(X,Z)\nabla_{Y}^{\perp}\vec{H}+g(X,Y)\nabla_{Z}^{\perp}\vec{H}\big].

Now, we are ready to complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
M^{n}\hookrightarrow N^{n}(4c)
be a compact Lagrangian submanifold
and
\{e_{1},...,e_{n},e_{1^{*}},\ldots,e_{n^{*}}\}
be a local adapted Lagrangian
frame field along
M^{n}
. From (
2.4
) and that

\nabla^{\perp}_{e_{i}}\vec{H}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}H^{j^{*}}_{,i}e_{j^{*}}
, we have

(3.4)

\displaystyle\|\nabla J\vec{H}\|^{2}=\|\nabla^{\perp}\vec{H}\|^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(H^{j^{*}}_{,i})^{2}.

Then, by (
2.12
), calculating the squared length of the Lie derivative

\mathcal{L}_{J\vec{H}}g
of
g
with respect to
J\vec{H}
, we obtain

(3.5)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{J\vec{H}}g\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\big[(\mathcal{L}_{J\vec{H}}g)(e_{i},e_{j})\big]^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\big(H^{j^{*}}_{,i}+H^{i^{*}}_{,j}\big)^{2}=4\|\nabla^{\perp}\vec{H}\|^{2}.

Thus, we can apply Lemma
2.1
and (
3.1
) to obtain that

(3.6)

\displaystyle{\rm div}(\nabla_{J\vec{H}}J\vec{H}-({\rm div}J\vec{H})J\vec{H})

\displaystyle={\rm Ric}\,(J\vec{H},J\vec{H})+\|\nabla J\vec{H}\|^{2}-({\rm div}J\vec{H})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq{\rm Ric}\,(J\vec{H},J\vec{H})-(n-1)\|\nabla^{\perp}\vec{H}\|^{2},

where the equality in (
3.6
) holds if and only if

\nabla J\vec{H}=\frac{1}{n}({\rm div}\,J\vec{H})\,{\rm id}
,
or equivalently,
M^{n}
is a Lagrangian submanifold with conformal Maslov form.

From (
3.6
), by further applying Lemma
3.2
, we get

(3.7)

{\rm div}(\nabla_{J\vec{H}}J\vec{H}-({\rm div}J\vec{H})J\vec{H})\geq{\rm Ric}\,(J\vec{H},J\vec{H})-\tfrac{(n-1)(n+2)}{3n^{2}}\|\bar{\nabla}h\|^{2},

where the equality holds if and only if both
\nabla J\vec{H}=\frac{1}{n}({\rm div}\,J\vec{H})\,{\rm id}

and (
3.3
) hold.

By the compactness of
M^{n}
, we can integrate the inequality (
3.7
) over
M^{n}
.
Then, applying for the divergence theorem, we obtain the integral inequality (
1.4
).

It is easily seen that the equality holds in (
1.4
) if and only if
the equality in (
3.2
) holds identically. Thus, according to Main Theorem in
[
15
]
,
equality in (
1.4
) holds if and only if either
x(M^{n})

is of parallel second fundamental form, or
x(M^{n})
is one of the Whitney spheres
in
N^{n}(4c)
.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

Let
x:M^{n}\rightarrow\tilde{N}^{2n+1}(\tilde{c})
be an
n
-dimensional
Legendrian submanifold in the Sasakian space form
\tilde{N}^{2n+1}(\tilde{c})

with Sasakian structure
(\varphi,\xi,\eta,g)
. First of all, similar to
Lemma
3.1
, we have the following simple result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
x:M^{n}\rightarrow\tilde{N}^{2n+1}(\tilde{c})
be an
n
-dimensional
Legendian submanifold with mean curvature vector field
\vec{H}
. Then,
it holds that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\varphi\vec{H})\|^{2}\geq\tfrac{1}{n}({\rm div}\,\varphi\vec{H})^{2}.

Moreover, the equality in (
4.1
) holds if and only if

\nabla(\varphi\vec{H})=\frac{1}{n}({\rm div}\,\varphi\vec{H})\,{\rm id}
, i.e.,

\varphi\vec{H}
is a conformal vector field on
M^{n}
.

We also need the following result:

Lemma 4.2

(cf. Lemma 3.3 in
[
14
]
)
.

Let
x:M^{n}\rightarrow\tilde{N}^{2n+1}(\tilde{c})
be an
n
-dimensional Legendrian
submanifold with second fundamental form
h
and mean curvature
vector field
\vec{H}
. Then, it holds that

(4.2)

\|\bar{\nabla}^{\xi}h\|^{2}\geq\tfrac{3n^{2}}{n+2}\|\bar{\nabla}^{\xi}\vec{H}\|^{2},

where, with respect to a local Legendre frame field
\{e_{A}\}_{A=1}^{2n+1}
,

\|\bar{\nabla}^{\xi}h\|^{2}=\sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{n}(h^{l^{*}}_{ij,k})^{2},\ \ \|\bar{\nabla}^{\xi}\vec{H}\|^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(H^{j^{*}}_{,i})^{2}.

Moreover, the equality in (
4.2
) holds if and only if

(4.3)

h^{l^{*}}_{ij,k}=\tfrac{n}{n+2}\big(H^{l^{*}}_{,i}\delta_{jk}+H^{l^{*}}_{,j}\delta_{ik}+H^{l^{*}}_{,k}\delta_{ij}\big),\ \ 1\leq i,j,k,l\leq n.

Now, we are ready to complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
x:M^{n}\rightarrow\tilde{N}^{2n+1}(\tilde{c})
be a compact
n
-dimensional
Legendrian submanifold and
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n},e_{1^{*}},\ldots,e_{n^{*}},e_{2n+1}=\xi\}

be a local adapted Legendre frame field along
M^{n}
. By definition, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\varphi\vec{H})\|^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(g(\nabla_{e_{i}}(\varphi\vec{H}),e_{j}))^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}(\bar{H}^{j^{*}}_{,i})^{2}=\|\bar{\nabla}^{\xi}\vec{H}\|^{2}.

Then, by (
2.25
), calculating the squared length of the Lie derivative

\mathcal{L}_{\varphi\vec{H}}g
of
g
with respect to
\varphi\vec{H}
, we obtain

(4.5)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{L}_{\varphi\vec{H}}g\|^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\big[(\mathcal{L}_{\varphi\vec{H}}g)(e_{i},e_{j})\big]^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\big(H^{j^{*}}_{,i}+H^{i^{*}}_{,j}\big)^{2}=4\|\nabla(\varphi\vec{H})\|^{2}.

Thus, we can apply Lemma
2.1
and (
4.1
) to obtain that

(4.6)

\displaystyle{\rm div}(\nabla_{\varphi\vec{H}}(\varphi\vec{H})-({\rm div}\,\varphi\vec{H})\varphi\vec{H})

\displaystyle={\rm Ric}\,(\varphi\vec{H},\varphi\vec{H})+\|\nabla(\varphi\vec{H})\|^{2}-({\rm div}\varphi\vec{H})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq{\rm Ric}\,(\varphi\vec{H},\varphi\vec{H})-(n-1)\|\nabla(\varphi\vec{H})\|^{2},

where the equality in (
4.6
) holds if and only if

\nabla(\varphi\vec{H})=\frac{1}{n}({\rm div}\,\varphi\vec{H})\,{\rm id}
.

From (
4.6
) and that
\|\nabla(\varphi\vec{H})\|^{2}=\|\bar{\nabla}^{\xi}\vec{H}\|^{2}
,
by further applying Lemma
4.2
, we get

(4.7)

{\rm div}(\nabla_{\varphi\vec{H}}(\varphi\vec{H})-({\rm div}\,\varphi\vec{H})\varphi\vec{H})\geq{\rm Ric}\,(\varphi\vec{H},\varphi\vec{H})-\tfrac{(n-1)(n+2)}{3n^{2}}\|\bar{\nabla}^{\xi}h\|^{2},

where the equality holds if and only if both
\nabla(\varphi\vec{H})=\frac{1}{n}({\rm div}\,\varphi\vec{H})\,{\rm id}

and (
4.3
) hold.

By the compactness of
M^{n}
, we can integrate the inequality (
4.7
)
over
M^{n}
. Then, applying for the divergence theorem, we obtain the integral
inequality (
1.10
).

It is easily seen from the above arguments that the equality in (
1.10
) holds
if and only if the equality in (
4.2
) holds identically. Thus, according to
Theorem 1.1 in
[
14
]
, equality in (
1.10
) holds if and only if either

x(M^{n})
is of
C
-parallel second fundamental form, or
x(M^{n})
is one of the contact
Whitney spheres in
\tilde{N}^{2n+1}(\tilde{c})
.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

As final remarks, we would mention that all the Whitney spheres in the complex space
forms are conformally equivalent to the round sphere (cf.
[
17
]
and
[
6
]
).
Now, for any one of the contact Whitney spheres,
x:\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow\tilde{N}^{2n+1}(\tilde{c})
,
its second fundamental form
h
has the expression (
1.9
). Thus, by using the
Gauss equation and direct calculations, we can immediately obtain the following

Theorem 4.1
.

The sectional curvatures of the contact Whitney spheres are not constant. Nevertheless, all
the contact Whitney spheres in each of the Sasakian space forms are conformally equivalent
to the round sphere.

First Derivative of Automorphic Function of Triangle Group
Md. Shafiul Alam, Bijan Krishna Saha, Chinmayee Podder

Abstract
. For a triangle group G G , the G G -automorphic function is the inverse of Schwarz triangle function. In this paper, we compute the first derivative of the G G -automorphic function for the triangle group G G in terms of the Gaussian hypergeometric function.

11F03; 33C05.

1.
Introduction

If
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathbb{C}
for
\gamma\neq 0,\,-1,\,-2,\,\dots
, and
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, then the function
{}_{2}F_{1}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;\xi)
defined by

{}_{2}F_{1}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;\xi)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha,j)(\beta,j)}{(\gamma,j)j!}\xi^{j},\quad\lvert\xi\rvert<1,

where

(\alpha,j)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }j=0\text{ and }\alpha\neq 0\\
\alpha(\alpha+1)\cdots(\alpha+j-1)&\text{if }j\geq 1\end{cases},

is known as the Gaussian hypergeometric function.
By the Euler’s integral representation formula, one can extend
{}_{2}F_{1}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;\xi)
analytically to
\mathbb{C}\setminus[1,+\infty)
(cf.
[
3
, Chapter II]
,
[
15
, Chapter XIV]
).

For the group
\operatorname{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
given by

\operatorname{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{R})=\Bigg\{\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}:a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{R},\penalty\ \ ad-bc=1\Bigg\},

the group
\operatorname{PSL_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
is defined as
\operatorname{PSL_{2}}(\mathbb{R})=\operatorname{SL_{2}}(\mathbb{R})/\{I_{2},-I_{2}\}
, where
I_{2}
is the
2\times 2
identity matrix (see
[
14
, Chapter VII]
). Let
\mathcal{H}
denote the complex upper half-plane
\{w\in\mathbb{C}:{\operatorname{Im}\,}w>0\}
. The action of
\operatorname{PSL_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
on
\mathcal{H}
is given by

w\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}w=\frac{aw+b}{cw+d},\,\text{for}\,\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{PSL_{2}}(\mathbb{R}),\,w\in\mathcal{H}.

Let
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{PSL_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
. The element
A
is called parabolic if
a+d=\pm 2
, elliptic if
(a+d)\in\mathbb{R}
and
|a+d|<2
, hyperbolic if
(a+d)\in\mathbb{R}
and
|a+d|>2
, loxodromic if
(a+d)\in\mathbb{C}
(see Theorem 15, Section 10 of
[
10
]
). Let
G\subset\operatorname{PSL_{2}}(\mathbb{R})
be a triangle group with signature
(0;n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})
acting on
\mathcal{H}
. Consider a parabolic element
A_{1}\in G
and an elliptic element
A_{2}\in G
. If
A_{1}(x)=x
, where
x\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
, then the point
x
is called a cusp of the group
G
. If
A_{2}(w)=w
, where
w\in\mathcal{H}
, then we call the point
w
an elliptic point of
G
. Since the action of
G
on
\mathcal{H}
is properly discontinuous, we obtain the quotient Riemann surface
G\backslash\mathcal{H}
(see
[
5
]
and
[
11
]
). In
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
, a special type of triangle group known as the Hecke group is investigated to study modular equations.

For a hyperbolic triangle
[w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}]
with angles
\frac{\pi}{n_{1}},\,\frac{\pi}{n_{2}},\,\frac{\pi}{n_{3}}
, let
f
denote the Schwarz triangle function. The
G
-automorphic function
\xi:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow{G\backslash\mathcal{H}^{*}}
, where
\mathcal{H}^{*}
is the union of
\mathcal{H}
and the set of cusps of
G
, is the inverse of
f
and

\xi\Big(\displaystyle\frac{aw+b}{cw+d}\Big)=\xi(w)

for
w\in\mathcal{H}
and
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in G
. The function
\xi(w)
can be normalized in such a way that the orders of the ramifications are
n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}
over
0,\,1,\,\infty
, respectively (see
[
8
]
). Suppose that
n_{1},\,n_{2}
and
n_{3}
are the orders of the elliptic points
w_{1},\,w_{2}
and
w_{3}
, respectively, such that

\displaystyle\xi(w_{1})=0,\quad\xi(w_{2})=1,\quad\xi(w_{3})=\infty.

Then,
\xi(w)
satisfies the following hypergeometric differential equation

(1.1)

\xi(1-\xi)\frac{d^{2}g}{d\xi^{2}}+\{\gamma-(\alpha+\beta+1)\xi\}\frac{dg}{d\xi}-\alpha\beta g=0.

Let
g_{1}(\xi)
and
g_{2}(\xi)
be linearly independent solutions of (
1.1
), then
f(\xi)=\displaystyle\frac{g_{2}(\xi)}{g_{1}(\xi)}
is the Schwarz triangle function. The Schwarzian derivative, denoted by
\{f,\xi\}
, of
f(\xi)
is given by

\{f,\xi\}=\displaystyle\Big(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(\xi)}{f^{\prime}(\xi)}\Big)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(\xi)}{f^{\prime}(\xi)}\Big)^{2}.

The function
f(z)
satisfies the Schwarzian equation given by

(1.2)

\{f,\xi\}=\frac{1-p_{1}^{2}}{2\xi^{2}}+\frac{1-p_{2}^{2}}{2(1-\xi)^{2}}+\frac{1-p_{1}^{2}-p_{2}^{2}+p_{3}^{2}}{2\xi(1-\xi)},

where
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
are known as accessory parameters and
p_{1}=\frac{1}{n_{1}},\,p_{2}=\frac{1}{n_{2}},\,p_{3}=\frac{1}{n_{3}}
(cf. Chap. V, Sect. 7 of
[
12
]
). These facts are presented in the following theorem.

Theorem A

(
[
4
, Theorem 6.2]
)
.

For
\gamma\neq 1
, let

\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha-\gamma+1,\penalty\ \ \beta^{\prime}=\beta-\gamma+1,\penalty\ \ \gamma^{\prime}=2-\gamma,

then the functions

g_{1}(\xi)={{}_{2}F_{1}}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;\xi)\quad\text{and}\quad g_{2}(\xi)=\xi^{1-\gamma}{{}_{2}F_{1}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime};\gamma^{\prime};\xi)}

are linearly independent solutions of (
1.1
). Let
w=f(\xi)=\displaystyle\frac{g_{2}(\xi)}{g_{1}(\xi)}
, then
\mathcal{H}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{C}:{\operatorname{Im}\,}\xi>0\}
is mapped by
f(\xi)
conformally to the hyperbolic (non-Euclidean) triangle
[w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}]
in the
w
-plane, where

\displaystyle w_{1}=f(0)=0,

\displaystyle w_{2}=f(1)=\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(2-\gamma)\Gamma(\gamma-\alpha)\Gamma(\gamma-\beta)}{\Gamma(\gamma)\Gamma(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\beta)},

\displaystyle w_{3}=f(\infty)=\displaystyle e^{\pi i(1-\gamma)}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\gamma-\beta)\Gamma(2-\beta)}{\Gamma(\gamma)\Gamma(\alpha-\gamma+1)\Gamma(1-\beta)}.

The interior angles at the vertices
w_{1}
,
w_{2}
and
w_{3}
are, respectively,
(1-\gamma)\pi,\,(\gamma-\alpha-\beta)\pi
and
(\beta-\alpha)\pi
.

For
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
, let us denote the hyperbolic (non-Euclidean) distance between
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
by
d_{h}(w_{1},w_{2})
. Since at the vertices
w_{1},w_{2}
and
w_{3}
of the hyperbolic triangle
[w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}]
, the angles are
\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{n_{1}}
,
\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{n_{2}}
and
\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{n_{3}}
, respectively, we have

(1.3)

{}\tanh^{2}{\frac{d_{h}(w_{1},w_{2})}{2}}=\displaystyle\frac{\cos{\big\{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}}+\frac{1}{n_{3}}\big)\pi\big\}\cos{\big\{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}}-\frac{1}{n_{3}}\big)\pi\big\}}{\cos{\big\{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\frac{1}{n_{2}}+\frac{1}{n_{3}}-\frac{1}{n_{1}}\big)\pi\big\}\cos{\big\{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\frac{1}{n_{3}}+\frac{1}{n_{1}}-\frac{1}{n_{2}}\big)\pi\big\}}

(see Equation (75.1) of
[
7
]
, where
a_{i}=d_{h}(w_{1},w_{2})
,
\alpha_{i}=\frac{\pi}{n_{3}}
,
\alpha_{j}=\frac{\pi}{n_{1}}
,
\alpha_{k}=\frac{\pi}{n_{2}}
). Also, by Equation (85.3) of
[
7
]
, we have

(1.4)

\tanh^{2}{\frac{d_{h}(w_{1},w_{2})}{2}}=\displaystyle\Big|\frac{w_{2}-w_{1}}{w_{2}-\overline{w}_{1}}\Big|.

In the following section, we present the main results and their proofs.

2.
Main Results and Proofs

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
G
is a triangle group with signature
(0;n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})
. Let

\xi:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow{G\backslash\mathcal{H}^{*}}

be the
G
-automorphic function and let
w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}\in\mathcal{H}
be the elliptic points of orders
n_{1},\,n_{2}
and
n_{3}
, respectively, such that

\displaystyle\xi(w_{1})=0,\quad\xi(w_{2})=1,\quad\xi(w_{3})=\infty.

Then,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{d\xi}{dw}=C\xi^{(1-\frac{1}{n_{1}})}(1-\xi)^{(1-\frac{1}{n_{2}})}\Big({{}_{2}F_{1}}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;\xi)-K\xi^{\frac{1}{n_{1}}}\ _{2}F_{1}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime};\gamma^{\prime};\xi)\Big)^{2},

where
C\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
,
\alpha,\beta\notin\mathbb{Z}
,
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha-\gamma+1,\beta^{\prime}=\beta-\gamma+1,\gamma^{\prime}=2-\gamma
and

\displaystyle K=\bigg(\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\beta)\Gamma(\alpha^{\prime})\Gamma(\beta^{\prime})}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma(1-\alpha^{\prime})\Gamma(1-\beta^{\prime})}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\gamma)}{\Gamma(\gamma^{\prime})}.

We need the following lemma in the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
w_{1},w_{2}\in\mathcal{H}
be the elliptic points of orders
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
, respectively. Then the hyperbolic (non-Euclidean) distance,
d_{h}(w_{1},w_{2})
, between
w_{1}
and
w_{2}
is given by

\tanh^{2}{\frac{d_{h}(w_{1},w_{2})}{2}}=\displaystyle\frac{\sin{\pi\alpha}\sin{\pi\beta}}{\sin{\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\sin{\pi\beta^{\prime}}}.

Proof.

We have

(2.2)

\frac{1}{n_{1}}=1-\gamma,\quad\frac{1}{n_{2}}=\gamma-\alpha-\beta,\quad\frac{1}{n_{3}}=\beta-\alpha

and

(2.3)

\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha-\gamma+1,\quad\beta^{\prime}=\beta-\gamma+1,\quad\gamma^{\prime}=2-\gamma.

From (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), it follows that

(2.4)

\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-\frac{1}{n_{1}}-\frac{1}{n_{2}}-\frac{1}{n_{3}}\Big),\quad\beta=\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-\frac{1}{n_{1}}-\frac{1}{n_{2}}+\frac{1}{n_{3}}\Big),\quad\gamma=1-\frac{1}{n_{1}}

and

(2.5)

\alpha^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(1+\frac{1}{n_{1}}-\frac{1}{n_{2}}-\frac{1}{n_{3}}\Big),\quad\beta^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}\Big(1+\frac{1}{n_{1}}-\frac{1}{n_{2}}+\frac{1}{n_{3}}\Big),\quad\gamma^{\prime}=1+\frac{1}{n_{1}}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}}+\frac{1}{n_{3}}=1-2\alpha,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}}-\frac{1}{n_{3}}=1-2\beta,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}}+\frac{1}{n_{3}}=1-2\alpha^{\prime},

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}}-\frac{1}{n_{3}}=1-2\beta^{\prime}.

From (
1.3
), we have

\displaystyle\tanh^{2}{\frac{d_{h}(w_{1},w_{2})}{2}}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{\cos{\big\{\frac{1}{2}}\big(1-2\alpha\big)\pi\big\}\cos{\big\{\frac{1}{2}}\big(1-2\beta\big)\pi\big\}}{\cos{\big\{\frac{1}{2}}\big(1-2\alpha^{\prime}\big)\pi\big\}\cos{\big\{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(1-2\beta^{\prime}\big)\pi\big\}}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\frac{\sin{\pi\alpha}\sin{\pi\beta}}{\sin{\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\sin{\pi\beta^{\prime}}}.

∎

Recall that the linearly independent solutions of (
1.1
) are
g_{1}(\xi)={{}_{2}F_{1}}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;\xi)
and
g_{2}(\xi)=\xi^{1-\gamma}{{}_{2}F_{1}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime};\gamma^{\prime};\xi)}
for
0<\gamma<1
. The quotient
w=f(\xi)=\displaystyle\frac{g_{2}(\xi)}{g_{1}(\xi)}
maps conformally
\mathcal{H}
to the interior of
[w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}]
. Let
\partial[w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}]
and
\partial\mathcal{H}
denote the boundary of the triangle
[w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}]
and the boundary of
\mathcal{H}
, respectively. Then
f(\xi)
forms a homeomorphism between
\partial\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
and
\partial[w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}]
. The orders of
w_{1},\,w_{2}
and
w_{3}
are
n_{1},\,n_{2}
and
n_{3}
, respectively, where
n_{1},\,n_{2},\,n_{3}
are integers greater than
2
or infinity. Since

\frac{1}{n_{1}}+\frac{1}{n_{2}}+\frac{1}{n_{3}}<1,

the inverse
\xi(w)
of the Schwarz triangle function
f(\xi)
is single-valued (see p. 416 in
[
13
]
). Also,
\xi(w_{1})=0,\,\xi(w_{2})=1
and
\xi(w_{3})=\infty
.

Consider the elliptic point
w_{1}\in\mathcal{H}
of order
n_{1}
and let
A\in G
be the generator of the stabilizer subgroup for
w_{1}
, then

(2.6)

\frac{Aw-w_{1}}{Aw-\overline{w}_{1}}=e^{\frac{2\pi i}{n_{1}}}\frac{w-w_{1}}{w-\overline{w}_{1}}

(see (9) of Section 48 in
[
10
]
). Also, for
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in G
and
w=\frac{g_{2}}{g_{1}}
, we have

(2.7)

w\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}w=\frac{aw+b}{cw+d}=\frac{ag_{2}+bg_{1}}{cg_{2}+dg_{1}}.

The two sides (hyperbolic lines) of
[w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}]
through
w_{1}
are mapped to straight lines through the origin by the Möbius transformation

w\rightarrow\displaystyle\frac{w-w_{1}}{w-\overline{w}_{1}}.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

The points on
G\backslash\mathcal{H}
are the
G
-orbits of
w
near the elliptic point
w_{1}
such that

(2.8)

\frac{w-w_{1}}{w-\overline{w}_{1}}=K\frac{w_{2}}{w_{1}}=K\frac{\xi^{1-\gamma}{{}_{2}F_{1}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime};\gamma^{\prime};\xi)}}{{{}_{2}F_{1}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;\xi)}}

for a nonzero constant
K
(see (48) of
[
9
]
).
By assumptions,
\xi(w_{2})=1
and

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}-\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\gamma-\alpha-\beta,

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=1-\beta,

\displaystyle\gamma^{\prime}-\alpha^{\prime}

\displaystyle=1-\alpha.

Thus,

\displaystyle{{}_{2}F_{1}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;\xi(w_{2}))}=\frac{\Gamma(\gamma)\Gamma(\gamma-\alpha-\beta)}{\Gamma(\gamma-\alpha)\Gamma(\gamma-\beta)}

(see Section 14.11 of
[
3
]
) and

\displaystyle{{}_{2}F_{1}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime};\gamma^{\prime};\xi(w_{2}))}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\gamma^{\prime})\Gamma(\gamma^{\prime}-\alpha^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime})}{\Gamma(\gamma^{\prime}-\alpha^{\prime})\Gamma(\gamma^{\prime}-\beta^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\gamma^{\prime})\Gamma(\gamma-\alpha-\beta)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\beta)}.

Therefore, when
w\rightarrow w_{2}
, from (
2.8
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{w_{2}-w_{1}}{w_{2}-\overline{w}_{1}}=K\frac{\Gamma(\gamma^{\prime})\Gamma(\gamma-\alpha)\Gamma(\gamma-\beta)}{\Gamma(\gamma)\Gamma(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\beta)}

or,

(2.9)

{}K=\displaystyle\frac{w_{2}-w_{1}}{w_{2}-\overline{w}_{1}}\frac{\Gamma(\gamma)\Gamma(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\beta)}{\Gamma(\gamma^{\prime})\Gamma(1-\alpha^{\prime})\Gamma(1-\beta^{\prime})}.

Since
\sin{\pi\alpha=\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)}}
for
\alpha\notin\mathbb{Z}
, by Lemma
2.2
we have

(2.10)

{}\tanh^{2}{\frac{d_{h}(w_{1},w_{2})}{2}}=\displaystyle\frac{\sin{\pi\alpha}\sin{\pi\beta}}{\sin{\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\sin{\pi\beta^{\prime}}}=\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\alpha^{\prime})\Gamma(1-\alpha^{\prime})\Gamma(\beta^{\prime})\Gamma(1-\beta^{\prime})}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(1-\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma(1-\beta)}.

From (
1.4
), it follows that

(2.11)

{}\tanh^{2}{\frac{d_{h}(w_{1},w_{2})}{2}}=\Big(\displaystyle\frac{w_{2}-w_{1}}{w_{2}-\overline{w}_{1}}\Big)^{2}.

Thus, from (
2.9
), (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), we have

\displaystyle K=\bigg(\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\beta)\Gamma(\alpha^{\prime})\Gamma(\beta^{\prime})}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)\Gamma(1-\alpha^{\prime})\Gamma(1-\beta^{\prime})}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\gamma)}{\Gamma(\gamma^{\prime})}.

Now, (
2.8
) implies

(2.12)

w=\frac{w_{1}g_{1}-K\overline{w}_{1}g_{2}}{g_{1}-Kg_{2}}.

Therefore,

(2.13)

\frac{dw}{d\xi}=K(w_{1}-\overline{w}_{1})\frac{g_{1}\displaystyle\frac{dg_{2}}{d\xi}-\displaystyle\frac{dg_{1}}{d\xi}g_{2}}{(g_{1}-Kg_{2})^{2}}.

Let
W
denote the Wronskian of
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
given by

W=g_{1}\displaystyle\frac{dg_{2}}{d\xi}-\displaystyle\frac{dg_{1}}{d\xi}g_{2}.

Then,

(2.14)

\frac{dw}{d\xi}=K(w_{1}-\overline{w}_{1})\frac{W}{(g_{1}-Kg_{2})^{2}}.

From (
1.1
), we have

(2.15)

\frac{d^{2}g}{d\xi^{2}}+P(\xi)\frac{dg}{d\xi}+Q(\xi)g=0,

where

P(\xi)=\displaystyle\frac{\gamma-(\alpha+\beta+1)\xi}{\xi(1-\xi)}

and

Q(\xi)=\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\beta}{\xi(\xi-1)}.

Since the linearly independent solutions of (
2.15
) are
g_{1}(\xi)
and
g_{2}(\xi)
, we have by Abel’s theorem (see Theorem 3.3.2 of
[
6
]
)

(2.16)

W(\xi)=W(\xi_{0})\exp{\Big(-\int\limits_{\xi_{0}}^{\xi}}P(\xi)d\xi\Big),

where
W(\xi_{0})\neq 0
. For some constant
C_{0}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, it follows from (
2.16
) that

(2.17)

W(\xi)=C_{0}\xi^{-\gamma}(1-\xi)^{\gamma-\alpha-\beta-1}.

Therefore, from (
2.14
) and (
2.17
) we have

(2.18)

\frac{dw}{d\xi}=C_{0}K(w_{1}-\overline{w}_{1})\frac{\xi^{-\gamma}(1-\xi)^{\gamma-\alpha-\beta-1}}{(g_{1}-Kg_{2})^{2}},

which implies

\displaystyle\frac{d\xi(w)}{dw}=C\xi^{(1-\frac{1}{n_{1}})}(1-\xi)^{(1-\frac{1}{n_{2}})}\Big({{}_{2}F_{1}}(\alpha,\beta;\gamma;\xi)-K\xi^{\frac{1}{n_{1}}}\ _{2}F_{1}(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime};\gamma^{\prime};\xi)\Big)^{2},

where

(2.19)

C=\displaystyle\frac{1}{C_{0}K(w_{1}-\overline{w}_{1})}.

\square

Corollary 2.2.1
.

The constants
C
and
K
in Theorem

Proof.

From (
2.19
), it follows that
C\propto\frac{1}{K}
.
∎

Explicit corrector in homogenization of monotone operators and its application to nonlinear dielectric elastomer composites
Thuyen Dang, Yuliya Gorb, Silvia Jiménez Bolaños

Abstract
This paper concerns the rigorous periodic homogenization for a weakly coupled electroelastic system of a nonlinear electrostatic equation with an elastic equation enriched with electrostriction. Such coupling is employed to describe dielectric elastomers or deformable (elastic) dielectrics. It is shown that the effective response of the system consists of a homogeneous dielectric elastomer described by a nonlinear weakly coupled system of PDEs whose coefficients depend on the coefficients of the original heterogeneous material, the geometry of the composite, and the periodicity of the original microstructure. The approach developed here for this nonlinear problem allows us to obtain an explicit corrector result for the homogenization of monotone operators with minimal regularity assumptions. Two L p − L^{p}- gradient estimates for elastic systems with discontinuous coefficients are also obtained.



1
Introduction

In recent years there has been a growing interest towards a class of materials known as dielectric elastomers that can exhibit coupled electrical and mechanical behavior, see cf.
[
14
]
.
A unique property possessed by such materials, known as
electrostriction
, which means they can respond to an external electric field by changing their size and shape, makes them appealing for various practical applications, e.g.
soft robotics, artificial muscles, active camouflage, haptic devices,
energy harvesting, and others, see e.g.
[
14
]
and
[
29
]
.
Homogenization theory can be used to guide the design of dielectric elastomers with enhanced electromechanical couplings, e.g.
[
35
]
, and this paper is devoted to the rigorous periodic homogenization of such a coupling.

The governing equations describing the system under consideration consist of a nonlinear (scalar) electrostatic equation in the presence of a bounded free body charge weakly coupled with an elastic (vectorial) equation that involves an
electrostriction
term. Here,
weakly
coupling means that the elastic displacement does not enter the electrostatic equation. The PDE system is posed on a heterogeneous bounded domain with
periodic microstructure
of size
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. For simplicity, we focus on Dirichlet boundary conditions only.
The goals of this paper are twofold. First, it aims at determining the
macroscopic
or
effective
behavior of the considered periodic composite under the assumption of scale separation. This amounts to developing an asymptotic analysis of the limiting response of the given PDE system as
\varepsilon
, the size of the microstructure, goes to zero. Second, since many necessary facts (e.g. the regularity of the solution to the original fine-scale problem) are not readily available, this paper presents a number of stand-alone results that could be utilized in future contributions to the topic of periodic homogenization for nonlinear electrostatic and/or elastic composite materials under minimal regularity assumptions of the original system.

The rigorous justification of a model for dielectric elastomers and the
derivation of its effective system, using the mathematical theory of
homogenization, were carried out in

[
51
,
32
]

and references therein. To carry out the homogenization of their system, the
authors of
[
51
]
made a
strict integrability assumption, requiring that the solution of the
electrostatic equation belongs to a class of least
W^{1,4}
-functions. The results in

[
32
]
showed
that if the coefficients of the electrostatic equation are piecewise
Hölder continuous, then indeed its solution belongs to
W^{1,p}
,
for any
p\in[1,\infty)
. Later, in

[
25
]
, this result was extended to
the case
p=\infty
. A similar model for the case of magnetic
suspensions was investigated in

[
24
,
25
]
.

In the contributions cited above

[
51
,
32
]
, the materials studied were
linear
, i.e., the constitutive
relationship between the electric field
\mathbf{E}
and the electric
displacement
\mathbf{D}
was assumed linear. However, when this
relationship is
nonlinear
, e.g., as in the case of
ferroelectric materials
, a new model, as well as a new approach,
are required to obtain the corresponding homogenized response. In
this paper, we consider
\mathbf{E}
and
\mathbf{D}
satisfying a nonlinear
constitutive relation that yields a
nonlinear divergence
equation
. Therefore, the improved gradient estimates, obtained by
the compactness method used in

[
32
,
25
]
,
are no longer available. Instead, in this paper, we derive a new
approach that does not require such estimates or the renormalization
framework of

[
33
,
44
]
,
which are the typical techniques to deal with problems of low
regularity source terms. To gain additional regularity of the
solution, we apply regularity theory to not only the fine-scale or the
effective systems but also to the two-scale homogenized one. Two-scale
convergence acts as an “intermediate” topology between weak and
strong convergence that allows canceling the dependence on the size of
microstructure, thus providing a system with rather nice
coefficients, besides the effective one. This idea enables us to relax
several regularity assumptions needed before in

[
32
,
51
,
25
]
,
and also to extend the cited works to
nonlinear cases.

To implement this idea for the current problem, several ingredients
are needed: a result from the nonlinear Caldéron-Zygmund theory

[
46
]
(see also

[
39
,
43
]
),
an estimate for elliptic systems

[
40
]
, the existence of the
(generalized) Green’s function
[
22
]
,
the SOLA technique (existence of the solution by limit of
approximations
[
8
]
), results
from the theory of two-scale convergence (for
L^{p}
and
\mathrm{BV}

functions)

[
1
,
45
,
2
,
31
]
,
an interpolation theorem
[
34
]
, a
corrector result in homogenization of monotone operators
[
23
]
, and a duality argument.

Along with the sought-after homogenized response, in this paper we
obtain three stand-alone results, namely: (i)
Theorem

1
,
which provides an explicit first-order corrector for the nonlinear electrostatic problem
with minimal regularity assumptions, while (ii)
Proposition

A.3
and (iii)

Proposition

A.4
provide
L^{p}-
gradient estimates for the elasticity
system via an interpolation argument.
Besides the fact that our approach is built for the
nonlinear
problem with
minimal regularity assumptions
, combined with ideas from

[
17
,
27
,
7
]
it could also be extended to the
high-contrast
case, for which the minimal and maximal values of coefficients of the underlying PDEs are vastly different. This case will be reported in future publications by the authors elsewhere.

, the conclusions

We claim that

Indeed, for
t>0
, let

and define

\displaystyle\mu^{\varepsilon}(x)\coloneqq\nabla\left(\varphi^{0}(x)+\varepsilon\eta^{1}\left(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)+t\eta\left(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right),

(4.5)

then

\displaystyle\mu^{\varepsilon}\xrightharpoonup[\hphantom{~2~}]{2}\mu^{0}\coloneqq\nabla\varphi^{0}(x)+\nabla_{y}\eta^{1}(x,y)+t\eta(x,y).

(4.6)

By monotonicity
(A4)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(F^{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{a}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\mu^{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\cdot\left(\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon}-\mu^{\varepsilon}\right)\di x\geq 0,

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(-\varphi^{\varepsilon}\Div F^{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{a}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\mu^{\varepsilon}\right)\cdot\nabla\varphi^{\varepsilon}-F^{\varepsilon}\cdot\mu^{\varepsilon}+\mathbf{a}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\mu^{\varepsilon}\right)\cdot\mu^{\varepsilon}\right)\di x\geq 0.

We have
-\Div F^{\varepsilon}=f
by (
2.7a
) and, by
(A3)

and (

), we have
\mathbf{a}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\mu^{\varepsilon}\right)
and
\mu^{\varepsilon}
are admissible in the sense of

Definition

, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\int_{\Omega\times Y}\left(f\varphi^{0}-\mathbf{a}(y,\mu^{0})\cdot\left(\nabla\varphi^{0}+\nabla_{y}\varphi^{1}\right)-F^{0}\cdot\mu^{0}+\mathbf{a}(y,\mu^{0})\cdot\mu^{0}\right)\di x\di y\geq 0,

or equivalently,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\int_{\Omega\times Y}\left(f\varphi^{0}-F^{0}\cdot\mu^{0}+\mathbf{a}(y,\mu^{0})\cdot\left(\nabla_{y}(\eta^{1}-\varphi^{1})+t\eta\right)\right)\di x\di y\geq 0,

(4.7)

where
\mu^{0}
is given by (
4.6
). In (
4.7
), choose a sequence
\eta^{1}
that strongly
converges to
\varphi^{1}
in

, we conclude that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\int_{\Omega\times Y}f\varphi^{0}-F^{0}\cdot\left(\nabla\varphi^{0}+\nabla_{y}\varphi^{1}+t\eta\right)\di x\di y\\
&\quad+\frac{1}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\int_{\Omega\times Y}\mathbf{a}(y,\nabla\varphi^{0}+\nabla_{y}\varphi^{1}+t\eta)\cdot t\eta\di x\di y\geq 0.\end{split}

(4.8)

By (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), (
4.8
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\int_{\Omega\times Y}-F^{0}\cdot t\eta+\mathbf{a}(y,\nabla\varphi^{0}+\nabla_{y}\varphi^{1}+t\eta)\cdot t\eta\di x\di y\geq 0.

Dividing both sides by
t>0
, then letting
t\to 0
and using

(A3)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\int_{\Omega\times Y}-F^{0}\cdot\eta+\mathbf{a}(y,\nabla\varphi^{0}+\nabla_{y}\varphi^{1})\cdot\eta\di x\di y\geq 0,

(4.9)

for all
\eta\in\mathcal{D}\left(\Omega,C_{\mathrm{per}}^{\infty}(Y)\right).

Therefore,
F^{0}=\mathbf{a}\left(y,\nabla\varphi^{0}+\nabla_{y}\varphi^{1}\right)
and the two-scale limits
\varphi^{0}

and
\varphi^{1}
satisfy the
following system

\displaystyle-\Div\left[\frac{1}{\left\lvert Y\right\rvert}\int_{Y}\mathbf{a}\left(y,\nabla\varphi^{0}+\nabla_{y}\varphi^{1}\right)\di y\right]

\displaystyle=f,\text{ in }\Omega,

\displaystyle-\Div_{y}\mathbf{a}\left(y,\nabla\varphi^{0}+\nabla_{y}\varphi^{1}\right)

\displaystyle=0,\text{ in }\Omega\times Y.

Since the system above has a unique solution by monotonicity

(A4)
, the entire sequence

\varphi^{\varepsilon}
is convergent.

Proposition 5.6
.

We have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},M^{\varepsilon}(\nabla\varphi^{0})(x)\right)\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{p}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\left(y,M^{\varepsilon}(\nabla\varphi^{0})(x)\right)\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times Y,{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{p}\right)=0.

(5.21)

Proof.

For
\varepsilon>0
, let
y=\frac{x}{\varepsilon}-i
and

Z_{i}=\varepsilon^{-1}(Z_{i}^{\varepsilon}-i).
Note that
\mathbf{p}
is
periodic with respect to the
y
variable and
Z_{i}^{\varepsilon}=Y_{i}^{\varepsilon}
,
Z_{i}=Y
whenever
i\in I^{\varepsilon}
. On
Y_{i}^{\varepsilon}\times Y
, note that

M^{\varepsilon}(\nabla\varphi^{0})(x)
is independent of

x\in Y_{i}^{\varepsilon}
and
y\in Y
by definition. Thus, on
Y_{i}^{\varepsilon}
, we let

\xi^{i}\coloneqq M^{\varepsilon}(\nabla\varphi^{0}(\cdot))\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
We have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},M^{\varepsilon}\left(\nabla\varphi^{0}\right)(x)\right)\right\rvert^{p}\di x

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I^{\varepsilon}\cup J^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Z_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},M^{\varepsilon}\left(\nabla\varphi^{0}\right)(x)\right)\right\rvert^{p}\di x

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Z_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\xi^{i}\right)\right\rvert^{p}\di x+\sum_{i\in J^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Z_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},0\right)\right\rvert^{p}\di x

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Y_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\xi^{i}\right)\right\rvert^{p}\di x+\sum_{i\in J^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Z_{i}^{\varepsilon}}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},0\right)\right\rvert^{p}\di x

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Y}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}(y,\xi^{i})\right\rvert^{p}\di y~\varepsilon^{d}+\sum_{i\in J^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Z_{i}}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}\left(y,0\right)\right\rvert^{p}\di y~\varepsilon^{d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Y_{i}^{\varepsilon}\times Y}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}\left(y,\xi^{i}\right)\right\rvert^{p}\di x\di y+\sum_{i\in J^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Z_{i}}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}\left(y,0\right)\right\rvert^{p}\di y~\varepsilon^{d}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Y_{i}^{\varepsilon}\times Y}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}(y,M^{\varepsilon}(\nabla\varphi^{0})(x))\right\rvert^{p}\di x\di y+\sum_{i\in J^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Z_{i}^{\varepsilon}\times Y}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}(y,0)\right\rvert^{p}\di x\di y

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i\in J^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Z_{i}}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}\left(y,0\right)\right\rvert^{p}\di y~\varepsilon^{d}-\sum_{i\in J^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Z_{i}^{\varepsilon}\times Y}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}(y,0)\right\rvert^{p}\di x\di y

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\times Y}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}(y,M^{\varepsilon}(\nabla\varphi^{0})(x))\right\rvert^{p}\di x\di y

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{i\in J^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Z_{i}}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}\left(y,0\right)\right\rvert^{p}\di y~\varepsilon^{d}-\sum_{i\in J^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Z_{i}^{\varepsilon}\times Y}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}(y,0)\right\rvert^{p}\di x\di y.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\lvert\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},M^{\varepsilon}(\nabla\varphi^{0})(x)\right)\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{p}-\left\lVert\mathbf{p}\left(y,M^{\varepsilon}(\nabla\varphi^{0})(x)\right)\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times Y,{\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{p}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lvert\sum_{i\in J^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Z_{i}}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}\left(y,0\right)\right\rvert^{p}\di y~\varepsilon^{d}-\sum_{i\in J^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Z_{i}^{\varepsilon}\times Y}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}(y,0)\right\rvert^{p}\di x\di y\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in J^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Y}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}\left(y,0\right)\right\rvert^{p}\di y~\varepsilon^{d}+\sum_{i\in J^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Y}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}(y,0)\right\rvert^{p}\di y~\varepsilon^{d}\left\lvert Z_{i}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{i\in J^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Y}\left\lvert\mathbf{p}\left(y,0\right)\right\rvert^{p}\di y~\varepsilon^{d}

\displaystyle\leq 2\varepsilon^{d}\sum_{i\in J^{\varepsilon}}C\qquad\text{(by
\eqref{eq:122})}

\displaystyle\leq 2C\varepsilon^{d}\cdot\frac{\left\lvert\partial\Omega\right\rvert}{\varepsilon^{d-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon\quad\xrightarrow[\hphantom{~2~}]{}0\text{ as }\varepsilon\to 0.

Proposition 5.7

(Characterization of strong two-scale convergence)
.

Let

}L^{p}(\Omega\times Y).

(5.22)

Proof.

The sufficient condition

Conclusions

This paper is devoted to the periodic homogenization of nonlinear electric elastomers. More specifically, the nonlinear system (
2.7
)-(
2.9
) of an electrostatic equation coupled with an elasticity equation with periodic, highly oscillatory coefficients is considered, where
\varepsilon\ll 1
is the size of microstructure.
It is shown that the effective response of this system, given by (
3.13
) with (
3.12
), consists of the homogeneous dielectric elastomer described by a nonlinear weakly coupled system of PDEs, whose coefficients depend on the coefficients of the original heterogeneous material and the geometry of the composite and the periodicity of the original microstructure. In particular, the effective coefficients
(
3.12
) are written in terms of solutions to the cell problems (
3.8
), (
3.9
), (
3.10
). The main homogenization result is given in
Theorem

2
, and the explicit corrector for the solution to the electrostatic problem is presented in
Theorem

1
. It is worth noticing that in most of the existing literature on the topic of homogenization of monotone operators
[
23
,
36
,
12
]
, the corrector results were not explicit. However, unlike the ones that did obtain the explicit corrector, e.g.
[
1
]
, our results were obtained under minimal regularity assumptions.
The

when

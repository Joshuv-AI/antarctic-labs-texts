Abstract
. We show that the first eigenfunction of the fractional Laplacian ( − Δ ) s {\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s} , s ∈ ( 1 / 2 , 1 ) s\in(1/2,1) , is superharmonic in the unitary ball up to dimension 11 11 . To this aim, we also rely on a computer-assisted step to estimate a rather complicated constant depending on the dimension and the power s s .



1.
Introduction

The fractional Laplace operator is an integro-differential nonlocal operator of non-integer order.
It is defined as

\displaystyle{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}u(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{4^{s}\Gamma(n/2+s)}{\pi^{n/2}\big|\Gamma(-s)\big|}\;\mathrm{p.v.}\!\!\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{{|x-y|}^{n+2s}}\;dy

\displaystyle s\in(0,1),\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where “
\mathrm{p.v.}\!\!
” means that the integral is taken in the principal value sense.
We refer to
[
10
,
7
,
1
,
12
]
for all its basic features.

Here, we recall that it is naturally related to the fractional Sobolev space

\displaystyle H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\left\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):[u]^{2}_{s}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{{|u(x)-u(y)|}^{2}}{{|x-y|}^{n+2s}}\;dy\;dx<\infty\right\}

and, when the attention is restricted to a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, to the space

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)=\big\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\big\},

which is encoding a natural notion of homogeneous boundary conditions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
:
for this reason it is sometimes also known as the
restricted
fractional Laplacian.
From a functional analytic perspective,
{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}
is a positive self-adjoint operator on
\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):u=0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
with compact inverse.
It has therefore a discrete spectrum and the eigenvalues have finite multiplicity. In particular, the first eigenvalue, which we denote by
\lambda=\lambda(\Omega)
, is simple. It is known that the first eigenfunction
\phi\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
is smooth inside
\Omega
and that it can be chosen to be strictly positive.

In this paper we partially answer a conjecture raised by Bañuelos, Kulczycki, and Méndez-Hernández
[
5
]
*Conjecture 1.1:

(1.1)

If
n=1
and
\Omega=(-1,1)
, then
\phi
is concave in its support.

This has been previously established by Bañuelos and Kulczycki
[
6
]
*Theorem 4.7, for
s=\frac{1}{2}
, and
by Kaßmann and Silvestre
[
13
]
and Bañuelos and DeBlassie
[
4
]
*Theorem 1.1, whenever
s^{-1}\in\mathbb{N}
;
moreover, in general dimension and for a general bounded Lipschitz domain,
[
4
]
*Theorem 1.1 also shows that
\phi
is superharmonic, again under the assumption
s^{-1}\in\mathbb{N}
.
Another related result is contained in
[
5
]
*Theorem 1.1, which states that
\phi
is
mid-concave
(see
[
5
]
*Definition 1.1) on rectangles
\Omega=(-a_{1},a_{1})\times\cdots\times(-a_{n},a_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Here, we give a computer-aided proof of (
1.1
) for any
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and
n=1
. Let
\phi\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{0}\big((-1,1)\big)
denote the first eigenfunction of
{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}
on the interval
(-1,1)
. Then

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime\prime}<0\qquad\text{in }(-1,1).

More generally, our approach is able to reach the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
,
2\leq n\leq 11
, and
B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the unitary ball. Let
\phi\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{0}(B_{1})
denote the first eigenfunction of
{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}
on
B_{1}
. Then

(1.2)

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi>0\qquad\text{in }B_{1}.

We believe the threshold
n\leq 11
to be merely technical and due to a few sub-optimal estimates involved in our analysis.

Our strategy begins with a purely analytic approach to reduce
\Delta\phi
in an integral form.
At the core of this strategy we exploit the semigroup property of
{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}
in that we split

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi

\displaystyle={(-\Delta)}^{1-s}{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\phi={(-\Delta)}^{1-s}\big(\lambda\phi+\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\phi\big)

(1.3)

\displaystyle=\lambda{(-\Delta)}^{1-2s}\big(\lambda\phi+\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\phi\big)+{(-\Delta)}^{1-s}\big(\mathbf{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\phi\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\text{in }B_{1}.

Next, we write suitable integral representations for the different terms that appear: these use in a crucial way our standing assumption
s>\frac{1}{2}
, so that
(-\Delta)^{1-2s}
stands for the convolution with the fundamental solution in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A central role in these formulas is played by the nonlocal Poisson kernel of
B_{1}

(1.4)

\displaystyle P_{s}(x,y):=\frac{\gamma(n,s)}{{|x-y|}^{n}}\frac{\big(1-|x|^{2}\big)^{s}}{\big(|y|^{2}-1\big)^{s}},\quad\gamma(n,s):=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\pi^{n/2}\Gamma(s)\,\Gamma(1-s)},\qquad x\in B_{1},\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}.

The splitting of
-\Delta\phi
is performed in Section
2
.
A refinement of (
1.3
) leads to write (
1.2
) as an integral inequality not involving directly
\phi
or
\lambda
(see (
2.8
) below).
At this point we split our analysis in three different cases:

•

For
n=1
and
s=\frac{3}{4}
the argument can be concluded by hand, without too much of a hustle: this is done in Section
3
.

•

For
n=1
and
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)\setminus\{\frac{3}{4}\}
the integral quantities can be simplified a lot via estimates from below; still, the resulting inequality contains a quite complicated expression in
s
and we therefore plot and verify it using a computer: this is done in Section
4
;

•

For
n\geq 2
a similar approach to the previous point is taken, with the important difference that in this case hypergeometric functions make their appearance in our study: these make the analysis even more complicated and we consequently need to rely even more on numerical evaluations: this is done in Section
5
.

Many details will be deferred to Appendices, in the attempt of not breaking the flow of the exposition with technicalities. Nevertheless, we would like to mention that in Appendix
B
we derive an upper bound for the first eigenvalue
\lambda
on
B_{1}
for general dimension
n
and power
s
, while in Appendix
C
we derive from a representation formula for certain
s
-harmonic functions some symmetry and monotonicity properties which are useful in our analysis and might be of independent interest.

1.1.
Notations

We denote by
B_{r}
the
n
-dimensional ball of radius
r>0
centered at
0
. We set

(1.5)

\displaystyle F_{\tau}(z)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\kappa(n,\tau)|z|^{2\tau-n},\quad\kappa(n,\tau):=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}-\tau)}{4^{\tau}\pi^{n/2}\big|\Gamma(\tau)\big|},&&\tau\in\mathbb{R},\ \tau-\frac{n}{2}\not\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\\

\displaystyle z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

Note that

is the fundamental solution of

in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if

. For a measurable set

2.1.
Representation of the Laplacian of the eigenfunction

In this paragraph we perform the splitting of
-\Delta\phi
announced in (
1.3
).

Lemma 2.1
.

For
s\in(0,1)
it holds
{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\phi=\lambda\phi-\mathbf{1}_{B_{1}^{c}}(F_{-s}\ast\phi)\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\partial B_{1}.

Proof.

Consider
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi\,{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}\phi\,{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\mathbb{G}_{s}[{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\psi]=\lambda\int_{B_{1}}\phi\,\mathbb{G}_{s}[{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\psi]

Above, we have denoted by
\mathbb{G}_{s}:L^{2}(B_{1})\to{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{0}(B_{1})
the solution map to the Dirichlet problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} {\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}u&=f&&\text{in }B_{1},\ f\in L^{2}(B_{1}),\\
u&\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{0}(B_{1}),\end{aligned}\right.

which admits a representation in terms of the Green function

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{s}[f](x)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}G_{s}(x,y)\,f(y)\;dy

\displaystyle\text{for }x\in B_{1},

\displaystyle G_{s}(x,y)

\displaystyle=F_{s}(x-y)-\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(x,z)\,F_{s}(z-y)\;dz

\displaystyle\text{for }x,y\in B_{1},

(2.2)

\displaystyle P_{s}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}F_{-s}(z-y)\,G_{s}(x,z)\;dz

\displaystyle\text{for }x\in B_{1},\ y\in B_{1}^{c},

see
[
8
]
*Definition 1.9 and
[
2
]
*equation (25) and Theorem 1.2. We have then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi\,{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}\phi(x)\int_{B_{1}}F_{s}(x-y)\,{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\psi(y)\;dy\;dx

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{B_{1}}\phi(x)\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(x,z)\int_{B_{1}}F_{s}(z-y)\,{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\psi(y)\;dy\;dz\;dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}\phi\psi-\int_{B_{1}}\phi(x)\int_{B_{1}^{c}}F_{s}(x-y)\,{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\psi(y)\;dy\;dx

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{B_{1}}\phi(x)\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(x,z)\,\psi(z)\;dz\;dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{1}}\phi(x)\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(x,z)\int_{B_{1}^{c}}F_{s}(z-y)\,{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\psi(y)\;dy\;dz\;dx

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}\phi\psi-\int_{B_{1}}\phi(x)\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(x,z)\,\psi(z)\;dz\;dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{1}}\phi(x)\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\bigg(\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(x,z)\,F_{s}(z-y)\;dz-F_{s}(x-y)\bigg){\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\psi(y)\;dy\;dx.

As it holds (see
[
14
]
*equation (1.6.12’))

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(x,z)F_{s}(z-y)\;dz=F_{s}(x-y)\qquad\text{for }x\in B_{1},\ y\in B_{1}^{c},

then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\phi\,{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\psi

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{B_{1}}\phi\,\psi-\lambda\int_{B_{1}}\phi(x)\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(x,z)\,\psi(z)\;dz\;dx

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{B_{1}}\phi\,\psi-\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\psi(z)\int_{B_{1}}P_{s}(x,z)\,\lambda\phi(x)\;dx\;dz

(2.3)

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{B_{1}}\phi\,\psi-\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\psi\,(F_{-s}\ast\mathbb{G}_{s}[\lambda\phi])=\lambda\int_{B_{1}}\phi\,\psi-\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\psi\,(F_{-s}\ast\phi)

where we have used (
2.2
).
The stated equality (
2.1
) holds also pointwisely in view of
[
17
]
*Proposition 2.4.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

For
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
it holds

(2.4)

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}F_{2s-1}\ast\phi+\big(F_{s-1}-\lambda F_{2s-1}\big)\ast\big[\mathbf{1}_{B_{1}^{c}}(F_{-s}\ast\phi)\big]

(2.5)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}F_{2s-1}\ast\phi+\lambda\big(F_{s-1}-\lambda F_{2s-1}\big)\ast\mathbf{P}_{s}[\phi]

\displaystyle\text{in }B_{1},

where

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{s}[\phi](y)=\mathbf{1}_{B_{1}^{c}}(y)\int_{B_{1}}P_{s}(x,y)\,\phi(x)\;dx\qquad\text{for }y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Proof.

Starting from the last lemma, we compute (all equalities hold only in
B_{1}
)

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi={(-\Delta)}^{1-s}{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\phi={(-\Delta)}^{1-s}\big[\lambda\phi-\mathbf{1}_{B_{1}^{c}}(F_{-s}\ast\phi)\big]=\lambda{(-\Delta)}^{1-s}\phi+F_{s-1}\ast[\mathbf{1}_{B_{1}^{c}}(F_{-s}\ast\phi)].

Note that

\displaystyle{(-\Delta)}^{1-s}\phi=F_{2s-1}\ast{\left(-\Delta\right)}^{s}\phi=F_{2s-1}\ast\big[\lambda\phi-\mathbf{1}_{B_{1}^{c}}(F_{-s}\ast\phi)\big]

thus (
2.4
) follows.
In expanded form, (
2.4
) reads, for
x\in B_{1}
,

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\int_{B_{1}}F_{2s-1}(x-y)\,\phi(y)\;dy

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\big(F_{s-1}(x-z)-\lambda F_{2s-1}(x-z)\big)\int_{B_{1}}F_{-s}(z-y)\,\phi(y)\;dy\;dz

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}\phi(y)\bigg[\lambda^{2}F_{2s-1}(x-y)+\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\big(F_{s-1}(x-z)-\lambda F_{2s-1}(x-z)\big)F_{-s}(z-y)\;dz\bigg]\;dy.

Also, identity

\displaystyle(F_{-s}\ast\phi)(z)=\lambda\int_{B_{1}}P_{s}(y,z)\,\phi(y)\;dy\qquad\text{for }z\in B_{1}^{c},

holds (we have already used this one in (
2.3
) exploiting (
2.2
)).
In expanded form, (
2.5
) reads

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{2}\int_{B_{1}}F_{2s-1}(x-y)\,\phi(y)\;dy

\displaystyle\qquad+\lambda\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\big(F_{s-1}(x-z)-\lambda F_{2s-1}(x-z)\big)\int_{B_{1}}P_{s}(y,z)\,\phi(y)\;dy\;dz

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{B_{1}}\phi(y)\bigg[\lambda F_{2s-1}(x-y)+\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(y,z)\big(F_{s-1}(x-z)-\lambda F_{2s-1}(x-z)\big)\;dz\bigg]\;dy.

∎

We know that, by uniqueness,
\phi
and
-\Delta\phi
are radial, so that for any
x\in B_{1}
fixed

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi(x)=-\frac{1}{\big|\partial B_{|x|}\big|}\int_{\partial B_{|x|}}\Delta\phi(\theta)\;d\theta.

Keeping this in mind, we define

(2.6)

\displaystyle J_{\tau}(x;y):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{\big|\partial B_{|x|}\big|}\int_{\partial B_{|x|}}F_{\tau}(\theta-y)\;d\theta&&\text{for }n\geq 2,\,x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,x\neq y\\
&\frac{F_{\tau}(x-y)+F_{\tau}(x+y)}{2}&&\text{for }n=1,\,x,y\in\mathbb{R},\,x\neq y.\end{aligned}\right.

Using (
2.5
), we then write

\displaystyle-\Delta\phi(x)

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{B_{1}}\phi(y)\bigg[\lambda J_{2s-1}(x;y)+\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(y,z)\big(J_{s-1}(x;z)-\lambda J_{2s-1}(x;z)\big)\;dz\bigg]\;dy,\quad x\in B_{1}.

Since

\lambda J_{2s-1}(x;y)+\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(y,z)\big(J_{s-1}(x;z)-\lambda J_{2s-1}(x;z)\big)\;dz=\\
=\lambda\bigg(J_{2s-1}(x;y)-\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(y,z)\,J_{2s-1}(x;z)\;dz\bigg)+\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(y,z)\,J_{s-1}(x;z)\;dz,

the positivity of
-\Delta\phi
follows once we show

\displaystyle\lambda\bigg(J_{2s-1}(x;y)-\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(y,z)\,J_{2s-1}(x;z)\;dz\bigg)+\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(y,z)\,J_{s-1}(x;z)\;dz

\displaystyle\geq 0,\qquad x,y\in B_{1}.

As the second addend is clearly positive in the above inequality, we may replace
\lambda
with a larger constant (see Appendix
B
, equation (
B.1
))

(2.7)

\displaystyle\lambda\leq\Lambda(n,s):=\frac{4^{s}\Gamma(1+s)^{2}\,\Gamma(1+2s+\frac{n}{2})}{(s+\frac{n}{2})\,\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\,\Gamma(1+2s)}

and it is then enough to show

(2.8)

s\in\Big(\frac{1}{2},1\Big),\ x,y\in B_{1}.

Note that

for all

if and only if

and

. Hence, this case differs strongly from the other cases as an effect of definitions (

).

Lemma 2.3
.

For any

,

\displaystyle=J_{\tau}(|x|e_{1},|y|e_{1})

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(y,z)J_{\tau}(x;z)\ dz

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(|y|e_{1},z)\,J_{\tau}(|x|e_{1};z)\;dz

\displaystyle x,y\in B_{1},\,x\neq y.

Proof.

First note that
F_{\tau}(x)=F_{\tau}(|x|e_{1})
for any

by definition (
1.5
). To see the statement for

, note that this is obviously true for

) and for

\displaystyle J_{\tau}(x;y)=\frac{1}{|\partial B_{|x|}|}\int_{\partial B_{|x|}}F_{\tau}\big(|\theta-y|e_{1}\big)\;d\theta=\frac{1}{|\partial B_{|x|}|}\int_{\partial B_{|x|}}F_{\tau}\Big(\sqrt{1-2\theta\cdot y+|y|^{2}}\,e_{1}\Big)\;d\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|\partial B_{|x|}|}\int_{\partial B_{|x|}}F_{\tau}\Big(\sqrt{1-2|y|\nu_{1}+|y|^{2}}e_{1})\;d\nu=\frac{1}{|\partial B_{|x|}|}\int_{\partial B_{|x|}}F_{\tau}\big(\nu-|y|e_{1}\big)\;d\nu=J_{\tau}(|x|e_{1};|y|e_{1}).

Similarly, we have with a rotation

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(y,z)\,J_{\tau}(x;z)\;dz

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\frac{(1-|y|^{2})^{s}}{(|z|^{2}-1)^{s}|y-z|^{n}}\,J_{\tau}(|x|e_{1};|z|e_{1})\;dz

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\frac{(1-|y|^{2})^{s}}{(|z|^{2}-1)^{s}\big(|y|^{2}-2y\cdot z+|z|^{2}\big)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\,J_{\tau}(|x|e_{1};|z|e_{1})\;dz

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\frac{(1-|y|^{2})^{s}}{(|v|^{2}-1)^{s}\big(|y|^{2}-2|y|v_{1}+|v|^{2}\big)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\,J_{\tau}(|x|e_{1};|v|e_{1})\;dv

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma(s)\Gamma(1-s)}\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\frac{(1-|y|^{2})^{s}}{(|v|^{2}-1)^{s}\big||y|e_{1}-v\big|^{n}}\,J_{\tau}(|x|e_{1};|v|e_{1})\;dv

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(|y|e_{1},v)\,J_{\tau}(|x|e_{1};v)\;dv.

∎

In view of the last lemma (

s\in\Big(\frac{1}{2},1\Big),\ x,y\in B_{1}.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
n\geq 2
. For
\varepsilon,r>0
and

it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{r}}\frac{dx}{\big|x-\varepsilon e_{1}\big|^{2\alpha}}=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}\,r^{n-1}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}}{\big(r^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}-2\varepsilon rt\big)^{\alpha}}\;dt

In case
r=\varepsilon
, we additionally require
2\alpha<n-1
. In particular, we have for
\tau-\frac{n}{2}\notin\mathbb{N}_{0}

where we require additionally

if

Proof.

Via an explicit calculation

\int_{\partial B_{r}}\frac{dx}{\big|x-\varepsilon e_{1}\big|^{2\alpha}}=r^{n-1}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{dy}{\big|ry-\varepsilon e_{1}\big|^{2\alpha}}=r^{n-1}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{dy}{\big(r^{2}-2r\varepsilon y_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}\big)^{\alpha}}=\\
=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}\,r^{n-1}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}}{\big(r^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}-2\varepsilon rt\big)^{\alpha}}\;dt.

The last part follows by setting

,
\varepsilon=|y|
, and
\alpha=\frac{n}{2}-\tau
.
∎

Lemma 2.5

Lemma 2.9
.

For

and

it holds

\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{(z-x)^{2s-3}+(z+x)^{2s-3}}{z\,\big(z^{2}-1\big)^{s}}\;dz\geq\frac{\Gamma(1-s)\sqrt{\pi}}{\Gamma(\frac{3}{2}-s)}\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{s-2}\big(2x-1\big).

Proof.

With the change of variables
z=\frac{1}{t}
, we write

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{(z-x)^{2s-3}+(z+x)^{2s-3}}{z\,\big(z^{2}-1\big)^{s}}\;dz

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-xt)^{2s-3}+(1+xt)^{2s-3}}{\big(1-t^{2}\big)^{s}}\,t^{2}\;dt=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-xt)^{2s-3}}{\big(1-t^{2}\big)^{s}}\,t^{2}\;dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-xt)^{2s-3}}{\big(1-t^{2}\big)^{s}}\;dt-\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-xt)^{2s-3}}{\big(1-t^{2}\big)^{s-1}}\;dt.

We integrate the second integral in the above expression by parts, obtaining

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(1-xt)^{2s-3}\big(1-t^{2}\big)^{1-s}\;dt=\frac{1}{x}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-xt)^{2s-2}\big(1-t^{2}\big)^{-s}t\;dt.

Via another change of variable, namely
t=2\tau-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-xt)^{2s-3}}{\big(1-t^{2}\big)^{s}}\;dt

\displaystyle=2^{1-2s}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1+x-2x\tau)^{2s-3}}{\tau^{s}\,(1-\tau)^{s}}\;d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{1-2s}\Gamma(1-s)^{2}}{\Gamma(2-2s)}(1+x)^{2s-3}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\Big(3-2s,1-s;2-2s\Big|\frac{2x}{1+x}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{1-2s}\Gamma(1-s)^{2}}{\Gamma(2-2s)}(1+x)^{2s-3}\Big(1-\frac{2x}{1+x}\Big)^{s-2}\Big(1-\frac{x}{1+x}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{1-2s}\Gamma(1-s)^{2}}{\Gamma(2-2s)}\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{s-2}

(where we have used (
A.3
) and (
A.9
)) and, similarly,

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-xt)^{2s-2}}{\big(1-t^{2}\big)^{s}}\,t\;dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-xt)^{2s-2}}{\big(1-t^{2}\big)^{s}}\;dt-\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-xt)^{2s-2}}{(1+t)^{s}}\,(1-t)^{1-s}\;dt

\displaystyle=2^{1-2s}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1+x-2x\tau)^{2s-2}}{\tau^{s}\,(1-\tau)^{s}}\;d\tau-2^{2-2s}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1+x-2x\tau)^{2s-2}}{\tau^{s}}\,(1-\tau)^{1-s}\;d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{1-2s}\Gamma(1-s)^{2}}{\Gamma(2-2s)}(1+x)^{2s-2}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\Big(2-2s,1-s;2-2s\Big|\frac{2x}{1+x}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad-2^{2-2s}(1+x)^{2s-2}\Big(1-\frac{2x}{1+x}\Big)\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(1-\frac{2x}{1+x}\tau)^{2s-3}}{\tau^{s}}\,(1-\tau)^{1-s}\;d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{1-2s}\Gamma(1-s)^{2}}{\Gamma(2-2s)}(1+x)^{2s-2}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\Big(2-2s,1-s;2-2s\Big|\frac{2x}{1+x}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{2^{2-2s}\Gamma(1-s)\Gamma(2-s)}{\Gamma(3-2s)}(1+x)^{2s-3}(1-x)\,{}_{2}F_{1}\Big(3-2s,1-s;3-2s\Big|\frac{2x}{1+x}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{1-2s}\Gamma(1-s)^{2}}{\Gamma(2-2s)}(1+x)^{2s-2}\Big(1-\frac{2x}{1+x}\Big)^{s-1}

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{2^{1-2s}\Gamma(1-s)^{2}}{\Gamma(2-2s)}(1+x)^{2s-3}(1-x)\Big(1-\frac{2x}{1+x}\Big)^{s-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{1-2s}\Gamma(1-s)^{2}}{\Gamma(2-2s)}\bigg[\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{s-1}-(1+x)^{s-2}(1-x)^{s}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{1-2s}\Gamma(1-s)^{2}}{\Gamma(2-2s)}\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{s-2}(1-x)\cdot 2x.

We then deduce

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{(z-x)^{2s-3}+(z+x)^{2s-3}}{z\,\big(z^{2}-1\big)^{s}}\;dz\geq\frac{2^{1-2s}\Gamma(1-s)^{2}}{\Gamma(2-2s)}\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{s-2}\big[1-2(1-x)\big].

The constant in front the above expression can be transformed using the identities on the Gamma function, namely the Legendre duplication formula

concluding the proof.

\displaystyle\frac{2x^{4s-3}-(1-x)^{4s-3}-(1+x)^{4s-3}}{3-4s}+\mu(1-x^{2})^{s-2}(2x-1)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq\frac{2b^{4s-3}-(1+b)^{4s-3}}{3-4s}-\frac{(1-x)^{4s-3}}{3-4s}+(1+b)^{s-2}(2a-1)\mu(1-x)^{s-2}=:q_{a,b}(s,x).

is controlled from below in

Keeping this in mind, we split

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{3}{5},1\Big)=\big(a_{1},b_{1}\big]\cup\big(a_{2},b_{2}\big]\cup\big(a_{3},b_{3}\big]\cup\big(a_{4},b_{4}\big)

\displaystyle a_{1}=\frac{3}{5},\ b_{1}=a_{2}=\frac{7}{10},\ b_{2}=a_{3}=\frac{4}{5},\ b_{3}=a_{4}=\frac{9}{10},\ b_{4}=1.

In each of these subintervals it holds that

q_{a_{i},b_{i}}(s,x)\geq q_{a_{i},b_{i}}\big(s,x_{a_{i},b_{i}}(s)\big)>0\qquad\text{for }x\in(a_{i},b_{i}),\ s\in\Big(\frac{1}{2},1\Big)\setminus\Big\{\frac{3}{4}\Big\},\ i\in\{1,2,3,4\},

see Figure

. From this it follows that (

) holds for

Appendix A
Special functions

For the reader’s convenience we list here the definitions and some properties about the special functions that we use.

A.1.
The Gamma function

As usual, the Gamma function is defined by

\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{z-1}e^{-x}\;dx,\qquad\text{for }z>0.

As it satisfies the recursive formula

\displaystyle\Gamma(z+1)=z\,\Gamma(z)

its definition can be extended using this formula to
z\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0,-1,-2,\ldots\}
.
The Gamma function satisfies in particular the duplication formula (see,
e.g.
,
[
3
]
*equation 6.1.18)

(A.1)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(2z)=2^{2z-1}\Gamma(z)\,\Gamma\Big(z+\frac{1}{2}\Big)\qquad\text{for }z>0.

Moreover, it holds (
e.g.
,
[
3
]
*equation 6.1.17)

\displaystyle\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-z)=\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi z)}\qquad\text{for }z\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Z}.

Furthermore (
e.g.
,
[
3
]
*equation 6.2.1),

(A.2)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}t^{z-1}(1-t)^{w-1}\;dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{z-1}}{(1+t)^{z+w}}\;dt=\frac{\Gamma(z)\,\Gamma(w)}{\Gamma(z+w)},\qquad z,w>0.

A.2.
The hypergeometric function

We collect here some facts about the hypergeometric function
\,{}_{2}F_{1}
. We suppose in all the following that
a,b,c,z\in\mathbb{R}
with
c>b>0
and
z\in[0,1)
, although some formulas might hold in broader generality (we refer to
[
3
]
*Chapter 15).

Recall first the integral representation

(A.3)

\displaystyle\,{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c|z)=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(b)\,\Gamma(c-b)}\int_{0}^{1}t^{b-1}(1-t)^{c-b-1}(1-zt)^{-a}\;dt,

see
[
3
]
*equation 15.3.1, and the series expansion

(A.4)

\displaystyle\,{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c|z)=\frac{\Gamma(c)}{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\Gamma(a+k)\Gamma(b+k)}{\Gamma(c+k)}\frac{z^{k}}{k!},

see
[
3
]
*equation 15.1.1.

In particular one can consider
-a\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, in which case one has that
\,{}_{2}F_{1}
reduces to a polynomial of degree
-a
. For example:

(A.5)

\displaystyle\,{}_{2}F_{1}(0,b;c|z)

\displaystyle=1,

(A.6)

\displaystyle\,{}_{2}F_{1}(-1,b;c|z)

\displaystyle=1-\frac{b}{c}\,z,

see
[
3
]
*equation 15.4.1.

Among the many possible transformations, the following one is important to our purposes:

(A.7)

\displaystyle\,{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;c|z)=(1-z)^{c-a-b}\,{}_{2}F_{1}(c-a,c-b;c|z).

Indeed, (
A.7
) alongside (
A.5
) and (
A.6
) respectively, bears the following identities (corresponding to the particular cases
c=a
and
c=a-1
respectively):

(A.8)

\displaystyle\,{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;a|z)

\displaystyle=(1-z)^{-b}

\displaystyle\text{if }a>b>0,

(A.9)

\displaystyle\,{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;a-1|z)

\displaystyle=(1-z)^{-b-1}\Big(1-\frac{a-b-1}{a-1}\,z\Big)

\displaystyle\text{if }a-1>b>0.

Finally, according to
[
3
]
*formula 15.3.17,

(A.10)

\displaystyle\,{}_{2}F_{1}(a,b;2b|z)=2^{2a}\big(1+\sqrt{1-z}\big)^{-2a}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\bigg(a,a-b+\frac{1}{2};b+\frac{1}{2}\Big|\Big(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-z}}{1+\sqrt{1+z}}\Big)^{2}\bigg).

Appendix B
A bound on the first eigenvalue

Let
\lambda
be the first eigenvalue of
(-\Delta)^{s}
in
B_{1}
. A direct bound in terms the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
of the classical Dirichlet Laplacian
-\Delta
on the same ball is given by

\lambda\leq\big(\lambda_{1}\big)^{s},

see
[
16
]
*Theorem 1.1 or, also,
[
9
,
15
]
.
To have a more explicit estimate—which turns out to be a better one for
s
away from
1
and
n=1
—recall that the function
u_{1}\in C^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
u_{1}(x)=\kappa_{n,s}(1-|x|^{2})^{s}_{+}
, where

\kappa_{n,s}=\frac{\Gamma(n/2)4^{-s}}{\Gamma(1+s)\Gamma(s+\frac{n}{2})},

satisfies
(-\Delta)^{s}u_{1}=1
in
B_{1}
. In particular, we have

\lambda=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in{\mathcal{H}}^{s}_{0}(B_{1})\\
u\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{[u]^{2}_{s}}{\|u\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}}\leq\frac{[u_{1}]^{2}_{s}}{\|u_{1}\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}}.

Here,

\displaystyle[u_{1}]^{2}_{s}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}}u_{1}(x)\;dx=\kappa_{n,s}\frac{2\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r^{2})^{s}\,r^{n-1}\;dr

\displaystyle=\kappa_{n,s}\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{s}\,t^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\;dt=\kappa_{n,s}\pi^{n/2}\frac{\Gamma(1+s)}{\Gamma(1+s+\frac{n}{2})},

\displaystyle\|\tau\|_{L^{2}(B_{1})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{n,s}^{2}\frac{2\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r^{2})^{2s}\,r^{n-1}\;dr=\kappa_{n,s}^{2}\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{2s}\,t^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\ dt

\displaystyle=\kappa_{n,s}^{2}\pi^{n/2}\frac{\Gamma(1+2s)}{\Gamma(1+2s+\frac{n}{2})},

where we used (
A.2
) twice.
Thus,

(B.1)

\lambda\leq\frac{1}{\kappa_{n,s}}\frac{\Gamma(1+s)\Gamma(1+2s+\frac{n}{2})}{\Gamma(1+2s)\Gamma(1+s+\frac{n}{2})}=\frac{4^{s}\Gamma(1+s)^{2}\,\Gamma(1+2s+\frac{n}{2})}{(s+\frac{n}{2})\,\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\,\Gamma(1+2s)}=\Lambda(n,s).

In the particular case
n=1
, we have with the properties of the Gamma function (see Appendix
A
, in particular (
A.1
))

(B.2)

\displaystyle\lambda\leq\frac{4^{s}\Gamma(1+s)^{2}\,\Gamma(\frac{3}{2}+2s)}{(\frac{1}{2}+s)\,\Gamma(\frac{1}{2})\,\Gamma(1+2s)}=\frac{s4^{s}\Gamma(s)^{2}\,\Gamma(\frac{3}{2}+2s)}{\sqrt{\pi}(1+2s)\,\Gamma(2s)}=\frac{2s\Gamma(s)\,\Gamma(\frac{3}{2}+2s)}{(1+2s)\,\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+s)}=\frac{\Gamma(1+s)\,\Gamma(\frac{3}{2}+2s)}{\Gamma(\frac{3}{2}+s)}.

Related results in this direction are contained in Dyda, Kuznetsov, and Kwaśnicki
[
11
]
.

Appendix C
On the shape of some
s
-harmonic functions

We discuss here some features of
s
-harmonic functions in
B_{1}
associated with particular data in
B_{1}^{c}
.
Specifically, we assume

(C.1)

\displaystyle g:(1,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}\quad\text{is a non-increasing function,}

(C.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{|g(y)|}{1+{|y|}^{n+2s}}\;dy<\infty.

We denote by

(C.3)

\displaystyle b_{t}=\sup\{y\in(1,\infty):g(y)>t\}\quad\text{for }t\in(-\infty,\overline{g}),\qquad\overline{g}=\lim_{y\downarrow 1}g(y).

Let
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be the
s
-harmonic extension of
y\to g(|y|)
in
B_{1}
, namely

(C.4)

\displaystyle h(x)=\int_{B_{1}^{c}}P_{s}(x,y)\,g(|y|)\;dy=\gamma_{n,s}\int_{B_{1}^{c}}\bigg(\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{|y|^{2}-1}\bigg)^{s}\frac{g(|y|)}{{|y-x|}^{n}}\;dy,\qquad\text{for }x\in B_{1}.

Proposition C.1
.

Assume (
C.1
), (
C.2
), and that
g
is non-negative.
The function
h
defined as in (
C.4
) is
radial, radially increasing, and subharmonic in
B_{1}
.

Proof.

Starting from the representation formula (
C.4
), we write

\displaystyle h(x)=\gamma_{n,s}\big(1-|x|^{2}\big)^{s}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{n-1}\,g(\rho)}{\big(\rho^{2}-1\big)^{s}}\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{d\theta}{\big|\rho\theta-x\big|^{n}}\;d\rho

and, in view of Lemma
2.4
, it holds for
x\in B_{1}
and
\rho>1

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{1}}\frac{d\theta}{\big|\rho\theta-x\big|^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}}{(\rho^{2}+|x|^{2}-2\rho|x|t)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\;dt

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n-1}\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\tau^{\frac{n-3}{2}}{(1-\tau)}^{\frac{n-3}{2}}}{\big((\rho+|x|)^{2}-4\rho|x|\tau\big)^{\frac{n}{2}}}\;d\tau

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n-1}\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}{\Gamma(n-1)}\big(\rho+|x|\big)^{-n}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\bigg(\frac{n}{2},\frac{n-1}{2};n-1\Big|\frac{4\rho|x|}{(\rho+|x|)^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n-1}\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}{\Gamma(n-1)}\rho^{-n}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\bigg(\frac{n}{2},1;\frac{n}{2}\Big|\frac{|x|^{2}}{\rho^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=2^{n-1}\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}{\Gamma(n-1)}\frac{\rho^{2-n}}{\rho^{2}-|x|^{2}}

where we have used (
A.3
), (
A.10
), and (
A.8
) in this order.
Using the
layer-cake representation
for
g

\displaystyle g(\rho)=\int_{0}^{\overline{g}}\mathbf{1}_{(1,b_{t})}(\rho)\;dt,\qquad\rho>1,

with
b_{t}
and
\overline{g}
defined as in (
C.3
), we write for
x\in B_{1}

\displaystyle h(x)

\displaystyle=\gamma_{1,s}\big(1-|x|^{2}\big)^{s}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{2\rho\,g(\rho)}{\big(\rho^{2}-1\big)^{s}(\rho^{2}-|x|^{2})}\;d\rho

\displaystyle=\gamma_{1,s}\big(1-|x|^{2}\big)^{s}\int_{0}^{\overline{g}}\int_{1}^{b_{t}}\frac{2\rho}{\big(\rho^{2}-1\big)^{s}(\rho^{2}-|x|^{2})}\;dy\;dt

where, for any
b\geq 1
,

\int_{1}^{b}\frac{2\rho}{\big(\rho^{2}-1\big)^{s}(\rho^{2}-|x|^{2})}\;d\rho=\int_{1}^{b^{2}}\frac{dz}{{(z-1)}^{s}(z-|x|^{2})}=\int_{0}^{b^{2}-1}\frac{dz}{z^{s}(z+1-|x|^{2})}=\\
=\big(1-|x|^{2}\big)^{-s}\int_{0}^{\frac{b^{2}-1}{1-|x|^{2}}}\frac{dw}{w^{s}(w+1)}=\big(1-|x|^{2}\big)^{-s}\int_{\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{b^{2}-1}}^{\infty}\frac{v^{s-1}}{v+1}\;dv.

Therefore

\displaystyle h(x)=\gamma_{1,s}\int_{0}^{\overline{g}}\int_{\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{b_{t}^{2}-1}}^{\infty}\frac{v^{s-1}}{v+1}\;dv\;dt\qquad\text{for }x\in B_{1}.

As a consequence, for any
x\in B_{1}
,

\displaystyle\nabla h(x)

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{1,s}\int_{0}^{\overline{g}}\bigg(\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{b_{t}^{2}-1}\bigg)^{s-1}\frac{1}{\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{b_{t}^{2}-1}+1}\,\frac{-2x}{b_{t}^{2}-1}\;dt=2\gamma_{1,s}\,x\big(1-|x|^{2}\big)^{s-1}\int_{0}^{\overline{g}}\frac{\big(b_{t}^{2}-1\big)^{1-s}}{b_{t}^{2}-|x|^{2}}\;dt.

This proves the radial monotonicity. Moreover, for any
x\in B_{1}
,

\displaystyle-\Delta h(x)

\displaystyle=-2\gamma_{1,s}\;\mathrm{div}\bigg(x\big(1-|x|^{2}\big)^{s-1}\int_{0}^{\overline{g}}\frac{\big(b_{t}^{2}-1\big)^{1-s}}{b_{t}^{2}-|x|^{2}}\;dt\bigg)

\displaystyle=-2n\gamma_{1,s}\,\big(1-|x|^{2}\big)^{s-1}\int_{0}^{\overline{g}}\frac{\big(b_{t}^{2}-1\big)^{1-s}}{b_{t}^{2}-|x|^{2}}\;dt-4(1-s)\gamma_{1,s}\,|x|^{2}\big(1-|x|^{2}\big)^{s-2}\int_{0}^{\overline{g}}\frac{\big(b_{t}^{2}-1\big)^{1-s}}{b_{t}^{2}-|x|^{2}}\;dt

\displaystyle\qquad-4\gamma_{1,s}\,|x|^{2}\big(1-|x|^{2}\big)^{s-1}\int_{0}^{\overline{g}}\frac{\big(b_{t}^{2}-1\big)^{1-s}}{\big(b_{t}^{2}-|x|^{2}\big)^{2}}\;dt

is (strictly) negative for
x\in B_{1}
.
∎

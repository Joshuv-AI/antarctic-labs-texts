Ext-distinction for p p -adic symmetric spaces
Chang Yang

Abstract
. Let G / H G/H be a p p -adic symmetric space. We compute explicitly the higher relative extension groups for all discrete series representations of G G in two examples: the symplectic case and the linear case. The results have immediate applications to the computation of the Euler-Poincaré pairing, the alternating sum of the dimensions of the Ext-groups. In the linear case we confirm a conjecture of Wan which asserts the equality of the Euler-Poincaré pairing with the geometric multiplicity for any irreducible representation of G G . In the symplectic case we reduce the verification of Wan’s conjecture to the case of discrete series representations. We also determine all relatively supercuspidal representations in both cases.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Main results

Throughout this work, let
F
be a nonarchimedean local field of characteristic
0
. Let
G={\mathbb{G}}(F)
be be the group of
F
-points of a connected reductive group
{\mathbb{G}}
over
F
and
H=G^{\theta}
be the subgroup of fixed points of
\theta
of
G
, where
\theta
is an involution on
{\mathbb{G}}
defined over
F
. Let
A_{G,H}
be the maximal split torus of
Z_{G}\cap H
with
Z_{G}
the center of
G
. For an irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
, the multiplicity
m(\pi)
of
\pi
is defined by

\displaystyle m(\pi)=\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{H}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}}).

Harmonic analysis on the symmetric space
G/H
requires a good understanding of
m(\pi)
(c.f.
[
SV
]
). In
[
Wan
]
, build upon the works where a relative trace formula is exploited to study the multiplicities, Wan defines a geometric multiplicity
m_{geom}(\vartheta)
for any quasi-character
\vartheta
on
G
as an integral formula involving
\vartheta
( the definition in loc. cit. is for general spherical varieties, and we refer to
[
Wan
, Definition 6.1]
for a precise form of the definition.) The conjectured multiplicity formula in
[
Wan
, Conjecture 6.4]
is that

(1.1)

\displaystyle m(\pi)=m_{geom}(\theta_{\pi}),

where
\theta_{\pi}
is the Harish-Chandra character of
\pi
, for any supercuspidal representation
\pi
of
G
. Moreover, when
G/H
is tempered (resp. strongly tempered), that is, all the matrix coefficients of discrete series representations (resp. tempered representations) of
G
are integrable over
H/A_{G,H}
, the multiplicity formula (
1.1
) holds for all discrete series representations (resp. tempered representations). We refer the reader to
[
Wan
]
and references therein for the examples where the multiplicity formula has been proven.

In
[
Pra1
]
, Prasad proposes a homological algebra approach to the study of
m(\pi)
in the context of branching laws for representations of a group to its subgroups. In our context, according to
[
Pra1
]
, it is natural to consider the higher extension groups
\textup{Ext}^{n}(\pi|_{H},{\mathbb{C}})
in the category of smooth representations of
H/A_{G,H}
. As observed by Prasad, in order for (
1.1
) to hold for all irreducible representations, the multiplicity
m(\pi)
should be replaced by
\textup{EP}(\pi)
, the Euler-Poincaré paring of
\pi
, the alternating sum of the dimensions of the
Ext
-groups. The conjectured
EP
formula in
[
Wan
, Conjecture 6.5]
is then

(1.2)

\displaystyle\textup{EP}(\pi)=m_{geom}(\theta_{\pi}).

for any finite length representation
\pi
of
G
. In view of the multiplicity formula (
1.1
), it is natural to expect the vanishing of higher extension groups:

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{n}(\pi|_{H},{\mathbb{C}})=0,\quad n>0

for every discrete series representation (resp. tempered representations)
\pi
when
G/H
is tempered (resp. strongly tempered). These expectations reflect a deep relationship between harmonic analysis on
G/H
and homological algebra of
G/H
.

The main purpose of this paper is to verify the
EP
formula (
1.2
) in two specific examples, the symplectic case and the linear case. Since both sides of (
1.2
) can be viewed as a function on the Grothendieck group of the category of smooth representations of finite length of
G
, it suffices to verify (
1.2
) for standard modules of
G
. By inductive arguments for
m_{geom}(\theta_{\pi})
and
\textup{EP}_{H}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})
respectively in Section
5.4
and Section
6.4
, we reduce the verification to the case of discrete series.

1.1.1.
The linear case

Let
G={\mathrm{GL}}_{2n}(F)
,
Z
be the center of
G
and
H={\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)\times{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F)
. The multiplicity formula for discrete series has been established by Beuzart-Plessis and Wan in
[
BPW2
]
, combining with the relationship with Shalika model. Our main result in this case is the following vanishing result, from which (
1.2
) follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\pi
be a discrete series representation of
G
with trivial central character. Then

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{n}_{H/Z}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=0,\quad n>0.

Remark 1.2
.

In the Jacquet-Rallis case
(G,H)=({\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(E),{\mathrm{GL}}_{n}(F))
where
E/F
is a quadratic extension, we have a similar vanishing result. The proof is almost identical to that in the linear case. So in the Jacquet-Rallis case, the
EP
formula (
1.2
) also holds.

As
G/H
is a tempered variety, our result meets the general expectation mentioned above. In other contexts, there are some recent progresses on the
Ext
-vanishing results, see
[
CS
,
Cha
]
by Chan for the Rankin-Selberg case,
[
CF2
]
by Cai and Fan for the triple product case, and
[
Che
]
by Chen for the Gan-Gross-Prasad case.

1.1.2.
The symplectic case

Let
G={\mathrm{GL}}_{2n}(F)
and
H={\mathrm{Sp}}_{2n}(F)
. The multiplicity formula for cuspidal representations has been proven by the recent work of Beuzart-Plessis and Wan
[
BPW1
]
. Our main result in this case is the following computations.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\rho
be a cuspidal representation of
{\mathrm{GL}}_{d}(F)
and
\pi=\textup{St}_{k}(\rho)
be the generalized Steinberg representation of
G
(
2n=kd
). Then

•

If
k\neq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{i}_{H}(\textup{St}_{k}(\rho),{\mathbb{C}})=0\ \textup{ for all }i\geqslant 0.

•

If
k=2
, we have

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{1}_{H}(\textup{St}_{2}(\rho),{\mathbb{C}})={\mathbb{C}},\quad\textup{Ext}^{i}_{H}(\textup{St}_{2}(\rho),{\mathbb{C}})=0,\ i\neq 1.

Due to the lack of precise information of characters of discrete series representations, we are unable to very (
1.2
) except for the
n=1
case, which is a direct consequence of Kazhdan orthogonality (see
[
Pra2
, Section 6]
). Note that in this case
G/H
is not tempered, so our computations are in alignment with the general expectations mentioned above.

In this paper we also discuss another notion that is important for the harmonic analysis on
p
-adic symmetric spaces,
the relatively supercuspidal representations
. By the subrepresentation theorem of Kato-Takano
[
KT
]
, these representations are the building blocks of irreducible distinguished representations (i.e.,

), the same role as supercuspidal representations to irreducible representations. In
[

]
, Cai and Fan introduced a new idea to determine relatively supercuspidals by computing the highest extension groups via the Schneider-Stuhler duaility. Following their idea, we determine all relatively supercuspidals in both cases in Proposition
5.12
and Proposition

. We refer the reader to Section
4
for unexplained notation below.

Theorem 1.4
.

(1)
In the symplectic case, an irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
is relatively supercuspidal if and only if
\pi=Z(\nu^{-1/2}\rho,\nu^{1/2}\rho)
is the unique subrepresentation of
\nu^{-1/2}\rho\times\nu^{1/2}\rho
for some cuspidal
\rho
.

(2)
In the linear case, an irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
is relatively supercuspidal if and only if
\pi=I_{P}^{G}(\sigma)
where
P=MU
is the standard parabolic subgroup of type
(2n_{1},2n_{2},\cdots,2n_{k})
and
\sigma
is a regular
M\cap H
-distinguished cuspidal representation of

.

In the symplectic case, the representations in Theorem
1.4
have been shown to be relatively supercuspidal by Kato-Takano in
[
KT
, Section 8.3]
by the Jacquet module methods.

Preliminaries

In this section we prepare some general facts that are useful for our computations later. Let
G
be an
\ell
-group (c.f.
[
BZ
, Section 1]
). Let
Z
be a closed subgroup contained in the center
Z(G)
of

and
\chi
be a character of
Z
. We denote by
{\mathcal{M}}^{\,\chi}(G;Z)
the category of smooth representations of

on which

acts through the character

. We have a natural equivalence of categories between

denotes the trivial representation of

Lemma 2.1
.

The category
{\mathcal{M}}^{\,\chi}(G,Z)
is abelian and has enough projective objects and injective objects.

Proof.

The category
{\mathcal{M}}(G)
has enough projective objects and injective objects (see
[
Pra1
, Section 2]
). Taking
(Z,\chi)
-coinvariants in projective objects and
(Z,\chi)
-invariants in injective objects in
{\mathcal{M}}(G)
will give rise to projective and injective objects in
{\mathcal{M}}^{\,\chi}(G,Z)
.
∎

By the lemma above, we can then define the extension group
\textup{Ext}^{q}_{{\mathcal{M}}^{\,\chi}(G,Z)}(V,W)
for any two smooth representations of
G
on which

acts via

. When

, we write simple
\textup{Ext}^{q}_{G}(\cdot,\cdot)
for
\textup{Ext}^{q}_{{\mathcal{M}}(G)}(\cdot,\cdot)
.

[

, Section 1.8]
, let

and

,

The Frobenius reciprocity takes the following form.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{q}_{{\mathcal{M}}^{\,\chi}(G,Z)}(V_{1},I_{U,\psi}(W))

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{q}_{{\mathcal{M}}^{\,\chi}(G,Z)}(i_{U,\psi}(W),V_{2}^{\vee})

If

Lemma 2.3
.

Let

. Let

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{q}_{G/Z}(V_{1}\otimes V_{2},V^{\vee})\cong\textup{Ext}^{q}_{{\mathcal{M}}^{\,\chi}(G,Z)}(V_{1}\otimes V,V_{2}^{\vee})\cong\textup{Ext}^{q}_{{\mathcal{M}}^{\,\chi^{-1}}(G,Z)}(V\otimes V_{2},V_{1}^{\vee}).

By

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{q}_{G/Z}(V_{1}\otimes V_{2},V^{\vee})\cong\textup{Ext}^{q}_{G/Z}(V,(V_{1}\otimes V_{2})^{\vee}).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let

\displaystyle{\mathcal{M}}(G)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\textup{Vec}

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle\mapsto V_{G}.

It is easily seen that

(2.2)

\displaystyle H_{i}(G,V)^{\ast}\cong\textup{Ext}^{i}_{G}(V,{\mathbb{C}}),

for all smooth

[

]

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

be a closed normal subgroup of

. If
V
is a projective

is a projective

\displaystyle E_{2}^{p,q}=\textup{Ext}^{q}_{G/N}(H_{p}(N,V),W)\Rightarrow\textup{Ext}^{p+q}_{G}(V,W).

Note that the homology group

, a priori a vector space over

, can be viewed canonically as a smooth

-module. In fact, take a projective resolution

of

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

and

be smooth modules of

and

respectively. Then we have a canonical isomorphism of smooth

\displaystyle H_{p}(G_{1},V_{1}\boxtimes V_{2})\cong H_{p}(G_{1},V_{1})\otimes V_{2},

where

acts trivially on

Proof.

Let

be a projective resolution of

in

. We may assume that

acts trivially on

so that we write

. Note that

is projective in

since it is a direct sum of

. Thus

is a projective resolution of

For applications of the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence in the sequel, the closed normal subgroup

is a representation of

\displaystyle H_{p}((F^{\times})^{n},V)\cong H_{p}((F^{\times})^{n},{\mathbb{C}}))\otimes V.

Lemma 2.7

We have

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{p}_{(F^{\times})^{n}}({\mathbb{C}},{\mathbb{C}})=\begin{cases}{\mathbb{C}}^{n\choose p},&p=0,1,\cdots,n,\\

For

, we would rather like to provide the following simple computation for the first extension groups.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
V
and
W
be smooth
(F^{\times})^{n}
-modules on which
(F^{\times})^{n}
acts trivially. Then we have a canonical isomorphism of vector spaces

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{1}_{(F^{\times})^{n}}(V,W)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}(V,W).

Proof.

We use the interpretation of
\textup{Ext}^{1}_{(F^{\times})^{n}}(V,W)
in terms of exact sequences. There is a canonical isomorphism between the abelian group
\textup{Ext}^{1}_{(F^{\times})^{n}}(V,W)
and the abelian group of equivalent classes of short exact sequences

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow W\rightarrow E\rightarrow V\rightarrow 0,

where the addition on the latter group is given by Baer sum (see
[
Mac
, Chapter III]
for more details). Note that every extension
E
of
V
by
W
is isomorphic to
W\oplus V
as a vector space over
{\mathbb{C}}
, so we need only to specify the
(F^{\times})^{n}
action on
W\oplus V
. Given
L=(\ell_{1},\cdots,\ell_{n})\in\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}(V,W)
, we may construct an extension
(W\oplus V,\mu_{L})
of
V
by
W
as follows:

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow W\rightarrow W\oplus V\rightarrow V\rightarrow 0,

where

\displaystyle\mu_{L}((z_{1},\cdots,z_{n}))((w,v))=(w+\sum\nu_{F}(z_{i})\ell_{i}(v),v).

It is easy to check that the map
L\mapsto(W\oplus V,\mu_{L})
is an injective homomorphism of abelian groups. Now let
(W\oplus V,\mu)
be any extension of
V
by
W
, we show that
\mu=\mu_{L}
for some
L\in\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}^{n}(V,W)
. Since the action of
(F^{\times})^{n}
on
V
is trivial, to

we can associate a map
\mu^{\prime}
defined by

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}:(F^{\times})^{n}\times V

\displaystyle\rightarrow W

\displaystyle\ (\underline{z},v)

\displaystyle\mapsto\mu(\underline{z})v-v.

Note that for any

, we have
\mu^{\prime}(\underline{z}_{1}\underline{z}_{2},v)=\mu^{\prime}(\underline{z}_{1},v)+\mu^{\prime}(\underline{z}_{2},v)
as
(F^{\times})^{n}
acts trivially on

. By the smoothness of

, the image
\mu^{\prime}((\mathfrak{o}_{F}^{\times})^{n},v)
in

is a abelian subgroup of

with finitely many values, hence must be

. Thus we can define
\ell_{i}\in\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(V,W)
by

\displaystyle\ell_{i}(v)=\mu^{\prime}((1,\cdots,1,\varpi_{F},1,\cdots,1),v)

for

, where

is at the

. It is easy to verify that

.
∎

For two smooth representations

and

G
on which

\displaystyle\textup{EP}_{{\mathcal{M}}^{\,\chi}(G,Z)}(V,W)=\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\textup{Ext}^{i}_{{\mathcal{M}}^{\,\chi}(G,Z)}(V,W)),

once it is well-defined, that is,
\textup{Ext}^{i}_{{\mathcal{M}}^{\,\chi}(G,Z)}(V,W)
is finite dimensional for all

. As before, we write simply
\textup{EP}_{G}
for
\textup{EP}_{{\mathcal{M}}(G)}
. We have the following vanishing result for

.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
H\subset G
be a closed subgroup of
G
and
\chi:H\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
be a character of
H
. Let
V
be a finite length smooth representation of
G
. If there is a subgroup
Z\subset Z(G)\cap H
such that
Z\cong F^{\times}
, then

\displaystyle\textup{EP}_{H}(V,\chi)=0,

whenever it is well-defined.

Proof.

By the additivity of
EP
, we may assume that
\pi
is an irreducible representation of
G
so that it has a central character
\chi_{\pi}
. By Lemma
2.4
, if
\chi_{\pi}\neq\chi
on
Z
, then
\textup{EP}_{H}(\pi,\chi)=0
. If
\chi_{\pi}=\chi
on
Z
, then we may assume that
V
is a smooth representation of
H
on which
Z
acts trivially and
\chi=\mathbf{1}
is the trivial character. By the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence in Lemma
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle E_{2}^{p,q}=\textup{Ext}^{q}_{H/Z}(H_{p}(Z,V),{\mathbb{C}})\Longrightarrow\textup{Ext}^{p+q}_{H}(V,{\mathbb{C}}).

By Lemma
2.7
,
H_{0}(Z,V)=H_{1}(Z,V)=V
and
H_{i}(Z,V)=0
for

. Thus the spectral sequence collapse at the second page and

is finite dimensional for all

. The proposition then follows from the Euler-Poincaré principle, which asserts that

\textup{EP}_{H}(V,{\mathbb{C}})=\sum_{p,q}(-1)^{p+q}\dim_{{\mathbb{C}}}(E_{2}^{p,q}).

∎

Remark 2.10
.

This proposition is a generalization of
[

, which, by Lemma

5.5.
Relatively supercuspidal representations

We begin with some general notation. For a connected reductive group
{\mathbb{G}}
defined over
F
and
G={\mathbb{G}}(F)
. Let
{\mathbb{H}}\subset{\mathbb{G}}
be a closed subgroup and
H={\mathbb{H}}(F)
. Let
A_{G,H}
be the maximal split torus of
Z(G)\cap H
with
Z(G)
the center of
G
. Let
\pi
be an irreducible representation of
G
with central character
\omega_{\pi}
. We say that
\pi
is
H
-distinguished if
\mathrm{Hom}_{H}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})\neq 0
. In particular, the central character
\omega_{\pi}
of
\pi
is trivial on
A_{G,H}
. We say that
\pi
is
H
-relatively supercuspidal if
\pi
is
H
-distinguished and for every nonzero
\ell\in\mathrm{Hom}_{H}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})
, the relative matrix coefficient
\ell(\pi(\cdot)v)
for every
v
in the space of
\pi
is compactly supported modulo
Z_{G}H
. Assume that the central character
\omega_{\pi}
is unitary. We say that
\pi
is a
H
-relatively discreter series representation if
\pi
is
H
-distinguished and for all nonzero
\ell\in\mathrm{Hom}_{H}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})
and
v
in the space of
\pi
, the relative matrix coefficient
\ell(\pi(\cdot)v)
lies in
L^{2}(Z_{G}H\backslash G)
. It is clear that an
H
-relatively supercuspidal representation is an
H
-relatively discrete series representation.

Now we focus on our symplectic case. It is shown in
[
KT
, Proposition 8.3.4]
by the Jacquet module method that
Z([\nu^{-1/2}\rho,\nu^{1/2}\rho])
is
H
-relatively supercuspidal for any irreducible cuspidal representation
\rho
of
G_{d}
. Following the idea in
[
CF1
]
we reprove this result and prove also that they actually exhaust all
H
-relative supercuspidal representations.

Proposition 5.12
.

An irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
is
H
-relatively supercuspidal if and only if
\pi=Z([\nu^{-1/2}\rho,\nu^{1/2}\rho])
for some irreducible cuspidal
\rho
.

Proof.

By
[
LO
, Corollary, Section 1.2]
, an irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
is an
H
-relatively discrete series if and only if
\pi=\textup{L}(\nu^{-1/2}\Delta,\nu^{1/2}\Delta)
for some segment
\Delta
. So we need to show that such a representation
\pi
is
H
-relatively supercuspidal if and only if
l(\Delta)=1
. Now let
\pi=\textup{L}(\nu^{-1/2}\Delta,\nu^{1/2}\Delta)
. It is well known that the Aubert-Zelevinsky involution
D(\pi)
of
\pi
is
Z(\nu^{-1/2}\Delta,\nu^{1/2}\Delta)
(see
[
LM
, Appendix A.5]
). Since we have multiplicity one in this case, by
[
CF1
, Proposition 4.2]
,
\pi
is
H
-relatively supercuspidal if and only if

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{2l-1}_{H}(D(\pi)^{\vee},{\mathbb{C}})=\textup{Ext}^{2l-1}_{H}(Z(\nu^{-1/2}\Delta^{\vee},\nu^{1/2}\Delta^{\vee}),{\mathbb{C}})\neq 0,

where
l=l(\Delta)
and
\Delta^{\vee}
is the dual segment of
\Delta
. When
l=1
so that
\Delta=\{\rho\}
,
D(\pi)
is nothing but the generalized Steinberg representation
\textup{St}_{2}(\rho)
. The if part of the proposition then follows from Theorem
5.9
. For the only if part, by Proposition
4.1
, we first have an exact sequence

(5.13)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow Z(\nu^{-1/2}\Delta^{\vee},\nu^{1/2}\Delta^{\vee})\rightarrow Z(\nu^{1/2}\Delta^{\vee})\times Z(\nu^{-1/2}\Delta^{\vee})\rightarrow Z(\Delta_{1})\times Z(\Delta_{2})\rightarrow 0,

where
\Delta_{1}
resp.
\Delta_{2}
is the union resp. intersection of the segments
\nu^{-1/2}\Delta^{\vee}
and
\nu^{1/2}\Delta^{\vee}
. By the long exact sequence associated to (
5.13
), it suffices for us to show that, for
l>1
,

(5.14)

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{i}_{H}(Z(\nu^{1/2}\Delta^{\vee})\times Z(\nu^{-1/2}\Delta^{\vee}),{\mathbb{C}})=0\text{ for all }i\geqslant 2l-1

and

(5.15)

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{i}_{H}(Z(\Delta_{1})\times Z(\Delta_{2}),{\mathbb{C}})=0\text{ for all }i\geqslant 2l.

The proofs of (
5.14
) and (
5.15
) are similar, so we prove only the former one. Let
P=MU
be the standard parabolic subgroup of type
(ld,ld)
. For any
P
-orbit
{\mathcal{O}}
, let
L,x
and
\delta_{x}
be associated to
{\mathcal{O}}
as in Section
3
. Note that
L_{x}
is a direct product of symplectic groups and general linear groups. By Proposition
3.3
, the Kunneth theorem
2.11
and the description of
L_{x},\delta_{x}
in Lemma
5.2
, we see that

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{2l-1}_{H}((Z(\nu^{1/2}\Delta^{\vee})\times Z(\nu^{-1/2}\Delta^{\vee}))_{{\mathcal{O}}},{\mathbb{C}})

is a direct sum of tensor products of extension groups of the form

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{i}_{H_{2n}}(Z(\Delta_{2}),{\mathbb{C}})\text{ and }\textup{Ext}^{j}_{G_{m}}(Z(\Delta_{3}),Z(\Delta_{3})).

By
[
Pra1
, Proposition 2.9]
, we have

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{j}_{G_{m}}(Z(\Delta_{3}),Z(\Delta_{3}))=0\text{ for all }j>l(\Delta_{3}).

Thus, combined with Proposition
5.5
, the largest number
d
such that

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{d}_{H}((Z(\nu^{1/2}\Delta^{\vee})\times Z(\nu^{-1/2}\Delta^{\vee}))_{{\mathcal{O}}},{\mathbb{C}})

is nonzero is not greater than
l
, which is strictly less than
2l-1
as
l>1
. This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

6.3.
Vanishing of higher extension groups

Let
\rho
be an irreducible cuspidal representation of
G_{d}
with trivial central character. Let

\displaystyle\Delta=\{\nu^{-(k-1)/2}\rho,\nu^{-(k-3)/2}\rho,\cdots,\nu^{(k-1)/2}\rho\}

be a segment and
\pi=\textup{L}(\Delta)
be the essentially square-integrable representations associated to
\Delta
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\rho,d
be as above. Let
r,s
be two nonnegative integers such that
d=r+s
. We have

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{1}_{G_{r}\times G_{s}}(\rho,{\mathbb{C}})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{r}\times G_{s}}(\rho,{\mathbb{C}})

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{i}_{G_{r}\times G_{s}}(\rho,{\mathbb{C}})=0,\ \text{ for all }i\geqslant 2.

Proof.

Let
Z
be the center of
G_{d}
. By Lemma
2.5
, we have a spectral sequence

\displaystyle E^{p,q}_{2}=\textup{Ext}^{q}_{G_{r}\times G_{s}/Z}(H_{p}(Z,\rho),{\mathbb{C}})\Longrightarrow\textup{Ext}^{p+q}_{G_{r}\times G_{s}}(\rho,{\mathbb{C}})

By Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
2.7
, we have

\displaystyle H_{0}(Z,\rho)=H_{1}(Z,\rho)=\rho,\quad H_{i}(Z,\rho)=0,\ i\geqslant 2.

Thus the spectral sequence collapse at page
E_{2}
. Since
\rho
is projective in
{\mathcal{M}}(G_{d}/Z)
, its restriction to
G_{r}\times G_{s}
is also projective in
{\mathcal{M}}(G_{r}\times G_{s}/Z)
. The lemma then follows easily from the spectral sequence above.
∎

Theorem 6.5
.

We have
\textup{Ext}^{i}_{H/Z}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=0
for
i\geqslant 1
.

Proof.

We are going to prove this by induction on
k
. The case

is obvious since the restriction of
\rho
to
H
is projective in
{\mathcal{M}}(H/Z)
. Assume that
k\geqslant 2
. Let
\Delta_{1}=\Delta\backslash\{\nu^{-(k-1)/2}\rho\}
. Then by Proposition
4.1
we have two short exact sequences

(6.1)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\pi\rightarrow\textup{L}(\Delta_{1})\times\nu^{-(k-1)/2}\rho\rightarrow L(\nu^{-(k-1)/2}\rho,\Delta_{1})\rightarrow 0

and

(6.2)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow L(\nu^{-(k-1)/2}\rho,\Delta_{1})\rightarrow\nu^{-(k-1)/2}\rho\times\textup{L}(\Delta_{1})\rightarrow\pi\rightarrow 0

By the long exact sequences associated to (
6.1
) and (
6.2
), the theorem will follow from the fact that

(6.3)

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{i}_{H/Z}(\textup{L}(\Delta_{1})\times\nu^{-(k-1)/2}\rho,{\mathbb{C}})=0,\ \text{ for all }i\geqslant 1

and that

(6.4)

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{i}_{H/Z}(\nu^{-(k-1)/2}\rho\times\textup{L}(\Delta_{1}),{\mathbb{C}})=0,\ \text{ for all }i\geqslant 2.

For (
6.3
), when
k=2
, the only possible

-orbit
{\mathcal{O}}
that is relevant to

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{i}_{H/Z}(\nu^{1/2}\rho\times\nu^{-1/2}\rho,{\mathbb{C}})

. By Proposition
3.3
and Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{i}_{H/Z}(\nu^{1/2}\rho\times\nu^{-1/2}\rho,{\mathbb{C}})\cong\textup{Ext}^{i}_{G_{d}/Z_{d}}(\rho\otimes\rho,{\mathbb{C}})\cong\textup{Ext}^{i}_{G_{d}/Z_{d}}(\rho,\rho^{\vee}).

Thus the formula (
6.3
) holds in this case since
\rho
is projective in
{\mathcal{M}}(G_{d}/Z_{d})
. When

, the formula (
6.3
) follows easily from the central character considerations in part (1) of Lemma
6.3
.

Now we prove (
6.4
). It is easy to verify a special case where

and

. That is, we have

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{i}_{G_{1}\times G_{1}/Z_{2}}(\nu^{-1/2}\times\nu^{1/2},{\mathbb{C}})=0,\ \text{ for all }i\geqslant 2.

In general, let

be the standard parabolic subgroup of

of type

, let

and

be the data associated to

. By the cuspidality of

such that

or

. We split the discussion into two cases.

Case (I). Let
r+s=d
and
{\mathcal{O}}={\mathcal{O}}_{(r,s)}
. If
-(k-1)/2\neq-(r-s)^{2}/2
, then
{\mathcal{O}}
is not relevant by the central character considerations in part (1) of Lemma
6.3
. Assume on the contrary that

. We may further assume that

. Then by Proposition
3.3
we have

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{i}_{H/Z}((\nu^{-(k-1)/2}\rho\times\textup{L}(\Delta_{1}))_{{\mathcal{O}}},{\mathbb{C}})=\textup{Ext}^{i}_{L_{x}/Z}(\rho\otimes\textup{L}(\Delta_{1})^{\prime},{\mathbb{C}}),

where

is a twist of

with a trivial central character. Note that we may view

as a normal subgroup of

with quotient isomorphic to

and Lemma

, we have a spectral sequence

(6.5)

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{q}_{G_{n-r}\times G_{n-s}/Z_{2n-r-s}}(H_{p}(G_{r}\times G_{s},\rho)\otimes\textup{L}(\Delta_{1})^{\prime},{\mathbb{C}})\Longrightarrow\textup{Ext}^{p+q}_{L_{x}/Z}(\rho\otimes\textup{L}(\Delta_{1})^{\prime},{\mathbb{C}}).

Case (II). Let

. By Proposition

, we have

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{i}_{H/Z}((\nu^{-(k-1)/2}\rho\times\textup{L}(\Delta_{1}))_{{\mathcal{O}}},{\mathbb{C}})=\textup{Ext}^{i}_{L_{x}/Z}(\nu^{-(k-1)/2}\rho\otimes\nu^{(k-1)/2}\rho\otimes\textup{L}(\Delta_{2}),{\mathbb{C}}),

(6.6)

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{q}_{G_{n-d}\times G_{n-d}/Z_{2n-2d}}(H_{p}(G_{d},

\displaystyle\nu^{-(k-1)/2}\rho\otimes\nu^{(k-1)/2}\rho)\otimes\textup{L}(\Delta_{2}),{\mathbb{C}})

\displaystyle\textup{Ext}^{p}_{G_{d}}(\nu^{-(k-1)/2}\rho\otimes\nu^{(k-1)/2}\rho,{\mathbb{C}})\cong\textup{Ext}^{p}_{G_{d}}(\rho,\rho^{\vee}),\text{ for all }p\geqslant 0.

Hence the spectral sequence (

n=n_{1}+n_{2}
, such that they are
G_{n_{1}}\times G_{n_{1}}
resp.
G_{n_{2}}\times G_{n_{2}}
-distingusihed. Then one may see easily that
\textup{Ext}^{1}_{H/Z}(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2},{\mathbb{C}})\cong{\mathbb{C}}
.

6.4.
The multiplicity formula

Let
H_{0}=\{\textup{diag}(a,a)|\;a\in G_{n}\}
. In this case the geometric multiplicity is defined to be

m_{geom}(\theta)=\int_{\Gamma_{ell}(H_{0}/Z_{G})}D^{H}(t)c_{\theta}(t)dt

where
\theta
is a quasi-character on
G/Z_{G}
. Since for discrete series the multiplicity is equal to the multiplicity of the Shalika model, combining with the multiplicity formula of the Shalika model proved in
[
BPW2
]
, we know that the multiplicity formula

m(\pi)=m_{geom}(\theta_{\pi})

holds for all discrete series of
G/Z_{G}
.

Let
P=MN
be a parabolic subgroup of
G
and assume that
\theta
is the parabolic induction of a quasi-chracter
\theta_{M}
of
M
(in the sense of Section 4.7 of
[
BP
]
). The next proposition is a direct consequence of Proposition 4.7.1 of
[
BP
]
.

Proposition 6.7
.

If
M\neq G_{n}\times G_{n}
, then
m_{geom}(\theta)=0
. If
M=G_{n}\times G_{n}
, we may just take
M
to be
H
. Then

m_{geom}(\theta)=\int_{\Gamma_{ell}(H_{0}/Z_{G})}D^{H_{0}}(t)\theta_{M}(t)dt.

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
2n=n_{1}+n_{2}
. Let
\pi_{1}
resp.
\pi_{2}
be a finite length smooth representation of
G_{n_{1}}
resp.
G_{n_{2}}
with central characters
\chi
resp.
\chi^{-1}
so that
\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}
has trivial central character. If
n_{1}\neq n_{2}
, then we have

\displaystyle\textup{EP}_{H/Z}(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2},{\mathbb{C}})=0.

If
n_{1}=n_{2}=n
, then

\displaystyle\textup{EP}_{H/Z}(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2},{\mathbb{C}})=\textup{EP}_{G_{n}/Z_{n}}(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2},{\mathbb{C}}).

Proof.

This follows immediately from part (2) of Lemma
6.3
and Proposition
3.3
.
∎

Corollary 6.9
.

We have
\textup{EP}_{H/Z}(\pi,{\mathbb{C}})=m_{geom}(\pi)
for any finite length representation
\pi
of
G
.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.7
and Proposition
6.8
it suffices to show that, when
M=H
and
\pi=\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\textup{EP}_{G_{n}/Z_{n}}(\pi_{1}\otimes\pi_{2},{\mathbb{C}})=\int_{\Gamma_{ell}(H_{0}/Z_{G})}D^{H_{0}}(t)\theta_{M}(t)dt.

This follows immediately from the Kazhdan orthogonality proved by Schneider and Stuhler and by Bezrukavnikov (see
[
Pra2
, Theorem 6.1]
).
∎

6.5.
H
-relatively supercuspidal representations

For our last result it will be convenient to take
\theta=\text{Ad}(\varepsilon)
and
H=G^{\theta}
, where
\varepsilon=\textup{diag}(1,-1,1,\cdots,1,-1)
. Let
M
be a standard Levi subgroup of
G
of type
(n_{1},\cdots,n_{k})
. Recall that an irreducible representation
\sigma_{1}\boxtimes\sigma_{2}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes\sigma_{k}
of
M
is said to be regular if
\sigma_{i}\ncong\sigma_{j}
for
i\neq j
.

Proposition 6.10
.

An irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
is
H
-relatively supercuspidal if and only if
\pi=I_{P}^{G}(\sigma)
where
P=MU
is the standard parabolic subgroup of type
(2n_{1},2n_{2},\cdots,2n_{k})
and
\sigma
is a regular
M\cap H
-distinguished supercuspidal representation of
M
.

Proof.

The line of arguments is very similar to that in the Jacquet-Rallis case in
[
CF1
]
, so we omit the details. We point out only that the Plancherel decomposition for
G/H
has been established in the work of Duhamel
[
Duh
]
. It follows that
\pi
is an
H
-relatively discrete series representation if and only if
\pi
lies in the image of discrete series of
{\mathrm{SO}}_{2n+1}(F)
under the endoscopy transfer. By
[
Art
]
, this is equivalent to say that
\pi=I_{P}^{G}(\sigma)
where
\sigma
is a regular
M\cap H
-distinguished discrete series of
M
.
∎

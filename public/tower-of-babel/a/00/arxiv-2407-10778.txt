The moduli space of twisted Laplacians and random matrix theory Thanks: Research supported by EPSRC grant EP/W007010/1. Data supporting this study are included within the article. MSC (2020): 81Q50 (Primary); 11F72, 30F60, 58J50 (Secondary)
Jens Marklof, Laura Monk

Abstract
. Rudnick recently proved that the spectral number variance for the Laplacian of a large compact hyperbolic surface converges, in a certain scaling limit and when averaged with respect to the Weil-Petersson measure on moduli space, to the number variance of the Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble of random matrix theory. In this article we extend Rudnick’s approach to show convergence to the Gaussian Unitary Ensemble for twisted Laplacians which break time-reversal symmetry, and to the Gaussian Symplectic Ensemble for Dirac operators. This addresses a question of Naud, who obtained analogous results for twisted Laplacians on high degree random covers of a fixed compact surface.



1.
Introduction

One of the long-standing conjectures in quantum chaos is that the spectral statistics of quantum
systems with chaotic classical limits are governed by random matrix theory
[
4
]
. Despite
convincing heuristics
[
10
,
20
]
, there is currently not a single example where this
phenomenon can be established rigorously. What is more, counter examples are known in arithmetic
settings
[
5
,
6
,
11
]
, so the conjecture can only be assumed to hold
generically. Rudnick recently pointed out that random matrix statistics emerge in a certain scaling
regime for the spectrum of the Laplacian on hyperbolic surfaces of large genus
[
17
]
, if
one averages over the moduli space of such surfaces with respect to the Weil-Petersson measure. (See
also
[
2
]
for an early numerical study of spectral statistics on average over moduli
space.) These statistics are given by the Gaussian Orthogonal Ensemble (GOE), which is consistent
with Bohigas, Giannoni and Schmit’s conjecture
[
4
]
, since the underlying classical dynamics
(the geodesic flow on the hyperbolic surfaces) is time-reversal invariant. Twisted Laplacians, which
model the Aharonov-Bohm effect on hyperbolic surfaces, break time-reversal symmetry, and their
statistics are expected to follow the Gaussian Unitary Ensemble (GUE)
[
4
]
. The purpose of
this paper is to construct the moduli space of such twisted Laplacians, which allows us to extend
Rudnick’s result to these operators, and prove compatibility with GUE statistics in the same scaling
regime for twisted Laplacians on large genus hyperbolic surfaces with random flux. Analogous results,
both for GUE and for GOE statistics, were established by Naud
[
15
]
in the case of random
covers of a fixed compact hyperbolic surface with and without Aharonov-Bohm flux. The mechanism that
produces the difference between GUE and GOE through the application of semiclassical trace formulas
was first pointed out by Berry
[
3
]
in his diagonal approximation argument. The idea of
averaging over Aharonov-Bohm flux lines to obtain GUE statistics goes back to a paper of Fishman and
Keating
[
8
]
, which investigates the case of planar quantum billiards.

The framework developed in this paper also produces analogous results for Dirac operators, where we
establish convergence to the Gaussian Symplectic Ensemble (GSE) in the presence of time-reversal
symmetry, and otherwise to GUE.

Rudnick and Wigman have refined the results of
[
17
]
by proving a central limit theorem for
the smoothed spectral counting function
[
18
]
and asymptotic almost sure
convergence of the spectral variance to GOE
[
19
]
, in the same scaling limit used
in
[
17
]
and the present paper. We will leave these and other possible extensions to the
GUE and GSE setting to future study.

The set-up for twisted Laplacians is as follows; cf.
[
16
]
for more details and
background. We represent a compact oriented hyperbolic surface of genus
g
as

X=\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}
, where
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
is a co-compact Fuchsian group acting by
Möbius transformations on the complex upper half plane
{\mathbb{H}}={\mathbb{R}}+\mathrm{i}{\mathbb{R}}_{>0}
. The Laplacian

\Delta=y^{2}(\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2})
acts on twice differentiable functions
\varphi:{\mathbb{H}}\to{\mathbb{C}}

parametrised by
z=x+\mathrm{i}y
. Given a unitary character
\chi:\Gamma\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
(with
{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}

the multiplicative group of complex numbers), we restrict to the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}_{\chi}
of
functions satisfying the relation

(1.1)

\varphi(\eta\cdot z)=\chi(\eta)\,\varphi(z)\quad\text{for all $\eta\in\Gamma$,}

and with inner product

(1.2)

\langle\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\rangle=\int_{\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}}\overline{\varphi_{1}(z)}\;\varphi_{2}(z)\;\frac{dx\,dy}{y^{2}}.

The restriction of
\Delta
to
{\mathcal{H}}_{\chi}
is essentially self-adjoint; we denote its self-adjoint extension by
\Delta_{\chi}
. The operator
-\Delta_{\chi}
has non-negative discrete spectrum

(1.3)

0\leq\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\ldots\to\infty

with only accumulation point at
\infty
. If
\chi
is not a real-valued function, we will refer
to
\Delta_{\chi}
as a twisted Laplacian. This operator models the Aharonov-Bohm effect of a free
quantum particle on the surface subject to
2g
magnetic flux lines of strength

\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{2g}
. Explicitly,

\chi(\eta)=\chi_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}(\eta)=\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}(\theta_{1}[\eta]_{1}+\ldots+\theta_{2g}[\eta]_{2g}))

with
[\eta]_{j}
the topological winding number around the
j
th flux line of the closed geodesic
corresponding to
\eta
. Provided that
\chi\neq\overline{\chi}
, the twisted Laplacian
violates time-reversal symmetry, and one expects the spectral statistics to be described by the
unitary (GUE) rather than the orthogonal (GOE) random matrix ensemble.

It is convenient to define
r_{j}=\sqrt{\lambda_{j}-1/4}
with the branch of the square root
chosen so that
\arg r_{j}\in[0,\pi/2]
. That is,
r_{j}\in{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
for
\lambda_{j}\geq 1/4
and
r_{j}\in\mathrm{i}(0,1/2]
for
\lambda_{j}<1/4
.
The main tool to analyse the distribution of
r_{j}
is the Selberg trace formula, which relates spectral to geometric data. For any even test function

(1.4)

h(r)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{h}(u)\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}ur}du,

with
\hat{h}\in\operatorname{C{}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
compactly supported, we have

(1.5)

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}h(r_{j})=(g-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(r)r\tanh(\pi r)\,dr+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\{\eta\}}\chi(\eta)^{n}\,\frac{\ell_{\eta}\hat{h}(n\ell_{\eta})}{2\sinh(n\ell_{\eta}/2)}.

Here the second sum is over all conjugacy classes
\{\eta\}=\{\delta\eta\delta^{-1}:\delta\in\Gamma\}
of primitive elements
\eta\in\Gamma
,
\eta\neq 1
(
\eta
is called primitive in
\Gamma
, if
\eta=\delta^{n}
for some
\delta\in\Gamma
implies
n=\pm 1
).
The set of primitive conjugacy classes
\{\eta\}
is in one-to-one correspondence with primitive closed oriented geodesics on
X
, and their lengths are denoted by
\ell_{\eta}
.

Following Rudnick
[
17
]
, we consider the distribution of the smoothed counting function of the
r_{j}
,

(1.6)

N_{f,L,\tau}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}h_{L,\tau}(r_{j}),\qquad h_{L,\tau}(r)=f(L(r-\tau))+f(L(r+\tau)),

with
L,\tau>0
and assuming
f
is even with a smooth and compactly supported Fourier transform

(1.7)

\hat{f}(u)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(r)\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}ur}dr.

This ensures our assumptions on
h=h_{L,\tau}
are satisfied.
More precisely, we are interested in the statistical distribution of
N_{f,L,\tau}
for random choices of
(X,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})
.

The main new construction presented in this paper is the moduli space
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
of the
pair
(X,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})
for a given genus
g
, together with a family of natural probability measures

\mu_{g,q}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. These measures may be viewed as an extension of the classical
Weil-Petersson probability measure
\mu_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}
on the moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
of hyperbolic
surfaces
X
of genus
g
considered in
[
17
]
. The index

q\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
indicates whether the measure on
\theta
is Lebesgue
(
q=\infty
) or discrete, supported on rationals with denominator
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
. The cases
q=1
and

q=2
correspond to the trivial resp. general real characters
\chi(\eta)\in\{\pm 1\}
(where we
expect GOE), and
3\leq q\leq\infty
to complex characters (where we expect GUE).

We will now state our principal result and postpone the precise definition of the extended moduli
space
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
and measures
\mu_{g,q}
to the remainder of the paper. We denote by

{\mathbb{E}}_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}
and
{\mathbb{E}}_{g,q}
the expected values defined by

{\mathbb{E}}_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}[\ldots]=\int_{{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}[\ldots]\,d\mu_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}},\qquad{\mathbb{E}}_{g,q}[\ldots]=\int_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}[\ldots]\,d\mu_{g,q}.

Let
\Sigma_{g,q}^{2}(L,\tau;f)
denote the variance of the counting function
N_{f,L,\tau}
, defined as

(1.8)

\Sigma_{g,q}^{2}(L,\tau;f)={\mathbb{E}}_{g,q}\bigg[\big|N_{f,L,\tau}-{\mathbb{E}}_{g,q}[N_{f,L,\tau}]\big|^{2}\bigg].

Theorem 1
.

Fix
\tau>0
. For
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
even with smooth, compactly supported Fourier transform, we have, for
L\geq 2
,

(1.9)

\lim_{g\to\infty}\Sigma_{g,q}^{2}(L,\tau;f)=\begin{cases}\Sigma_{\mathrm{GOE}}^{2}(f)+O(L^{-2}\log L)&(q=1,2)\\
\Sigma_{\mathrm{GUE}}^{2}(f)+O(L^{-2}\log L)&(3\leq q\leq\infty),\end{cases}

with the random matrix densities

(1.10)

\Sigma_{\mathrm{GOE}}^{2}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}2|x|\,|\hat{f}(x)|^{2}dx,\qquad\Sigma_{\mathrm{GUE}}^{2}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|\,|\hat{f}(x)|^{2}dx.

The analogous result for Dirac operators is given in Section
8
.

The plan for this paper is as follows. In Section
2
we define a fiber bundle over the Teichmüller space of hyperbolic surfaces
X
of genus
g
, and a natural action of the mapping class group on this extended Teichmüller space. The fiber at each point
X
is a torus which parametrises the twist parameter of the Laplacian. By quotienting the extended Teichmüller space by the mapping class group, we obtain a natural moduli space of twisted Laplacians. We then define invariant probability measures on this space in Section
3
, which model continuous and discrete random twists. The projection of these measures to the base moduli space is the classical Weil-Petersson measure. Although not essential for subsequent discussion, we also introduce the Fourier transform on the fiber, and describe the action of the mapping class group on Fourier space. In Section
4
we focus on a particular class of functions on the extended moduli space. These are length-holonomy functions which generalise Mirzakhani’s geometric functions and appear on the geometric side of the Selberg trace formula for twisted Laplacians. The precise parametrisation of twisted Laplacians in terms of the extended Teichmüller space is explained in Section
5
. Section
6
states and proves the main lemmas that are required for the proof of Theorem
1
in Section
7
. We extend our approach to Dirac operators with and without time-reversal symmetry in Section
8
, and prove convergence to GSE resp. GUE statistics.

2.
The affine mapping class group

Let
S_{g}
be a given compact, connected, oriented, smooth surface of genus
g\geq 2
, which we shall refer to as the base surface. The Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{g}
is the set of equivalence classes of marked hyperbolic surfaces
(X,\phi)
, where (i)
X
is a hyperbolic surface, (ii)
\phi
is a diffeomorphism
S_{g}\rightarrow X
(the marking), and (iii)
(X_{1},\phi_{1})
,
(X_{2},\phi_{2})
are called
equivalent
if there exists an isometry
m:X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}
such that
m\circ\phi_{1}
is isotopic to
\phi_{2}
.

The mapping class group
\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
of the base surface
S_{g}
is defined as the group of positive (i.e., orientation-preserving) diffeomorphisms
\psi:S_{g}\rightarrow S_{g}
modulo isotopies.
The mapping class group acts naturally on the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{g}
by

(2.1)

\psi\cdot(X,\phi)=(X,\phi\circ\psi^{-1}).

This action allows us to “forget” the marking, and so the quotient
\mathcal{M}_{g}:=\mathrm{MCG_{g}}\backslash\mathcal{T}_{g}
yields the moduli space of hyperbolic surfaces
X
of genus
g
modulo isometries.

The first homology
H_{1}(S_{g};{\mathbb{Z}})
of the base surface
S_{g}
is the
abelianisation of its fundamental group
\pi_{1}(S_{g})
. Let us fix a basis for
H_{1}(S_{g};{\mathbb{Z}})

by choosing the family
a_{1},\ldots,a_{g}
,
b_{1},\ldots,b_{g}
of fundamental oriented loops, so that

(2.2)

\pi_{1}(S_{g})=\langle a_{i},b_{i}\,|\,[a_{1},b_{1}]\cdots[a_{g},b_{g}]\rangle,

where

[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}
is the commutator.
H_{1}(S_{g};{\mathbb{Z}})
can be
equipped with a symplectic form
\hat{\iota}
for which

a_{1},\ldots,a_{g},b_{1},\ldots,b_{g}
form the standard symplectic basis, so that
\hat{\iota}
is represented by the matrix

(2.3)

J_{g}=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{g}\\
-I_{g}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Any positive diffeomorphism
\psi:S_{g}\rightarrow S_{g}
induces an automorphism

\psi_{\ast}:H_{1}(S_{g};{\mathbb{Z}})\rightarrow H_{1}(S_{g};{\mathbb{Z}})
, which preserves
\hat{\iota}
,
and this automorphism does not depend on the isotopy class of
\psi
. This allows us to define the
symplectic representation of the mapping class group,

(2.4)

\mathrm{MCG_{g}}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sp}(2g,{\mathbb{Z}}),\qquad\psi\mapsto M_{\psi},

which associates with each
\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
a symplectic matrix
M_{\psi}
representing
\psi_{\ast}
in the basis

(a_{i},b_{i})_{1\leq i\leq g}
. Here
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,{\mathbb{Z}})
denotes the set of
2g\times 2g
matrices
M

with integer coefficients satisfying
\,{}^{\mathrm{t}}\!MJ_{g}M=J_{g}
.

In order to define the affine mapping class group
\Lambda_{g}({\mathbb{R}})
, we need to attach additional data to the surface
X
, in the form of a row vector
{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2g}
representing the Aharonov-Bohm flux.
We denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{g}={\mathcal{T}}_{g}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2g}
the extended Teichmüller space.

A natural action of
\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
on the extended Teichmüller space
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
is given by

(2.5)

\psi\cdot(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})=(X,\phi\circ\psi^{-1},{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}M_{\psi}^{-1}).

We furthermore define the
{\mathbb{R}}^{2g}
action on
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
by translation of the vector
component by

(2.6)

{\text{\boldmath$\eta$}}\cdot(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})=(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}+{\text{\boldmath$\eta$}}).

This suggests the definition of the semidirect product, the
affine mapping class group

(2.7)

\Lambda_{g}({\mathbb{R}})=\mathrm{MCG_{g}}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}^{2g}

with multiplication law

(2.8)

(\psi,{\text{\boldmath$\eta$}})(\psi^{\prime},{\text{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{\prime}):=(\psi\circ\psi^{\prime},{\text{\boldmath$\eta$}}+{\text{\boldmath$\eta$}}^{\prime}M_{\psi}^{-1}).

Note that
(\psi,{\text{\boldmath$\eta$}})=(\operatorname{id},{\text{\boldmath$\eta$}})(\psi,{\text{\boldmath$0$}})
and that
A_{g}({\mathbb{R}})=\{\operatorname{id}\}\ltimes{\mathbb{R}}^{2g}
is a normal subgroup of
\Lambda_{g}({\mathbb{R}})
. The action of
\Lambda_{g}({\mathbb{R}})
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
is thus

(2.9)

(\psi,{\text{\boldmath$\eta$}})\cdot(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}):=(X,\phi\circ\psi^{-1},{\text{\boldmath$\eta$}}+{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}M_{\psi}^{-1}).

Define the subgroup
\Lambda_{g}({\mathbb{Z}})=\mathrm{MCG_{g}}\ltimes{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}
and its normal subgroup
A_{g}({\mathbb{Z}})=\{\operatorname{id}\}\ltimes{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}
. Furthermore, set
{\mathbb{T}}^{2g}={\mathbb{R}}^{2g}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}
. We then have the following.

Proposition 2
.

The extended moduli space
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g}:=\Lambda_{g}({\mathbb{Z}})\backslash\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
is a non-trivial fiber bundle over
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
with fiber
{\mathbb{T}}^{2g}
.

Proof.

We have that
A_{g}({\mathbb{Z}})\backslash\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{g}={\mathcal{T}}_{g}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2g}
. That is, we can view
A_{g}({\mathbb{Z}})\backslash\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
as a trivial fiber bundle over
{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
with fiber
{\mathbb{T}}^{2g}
. This proves that
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
is a torus bundle over
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. By the normality of
A_{g}({\mathbb{Z}})
, the action of the mapping class group defined in (
2.5
) induces an action on
{\mathcal{T}}_{g}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2g}
given by

(2.10)

\psi\cdot(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}+{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g})=(X,\phi\circ\psi^{-1},{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}M_{\psi}^{-1}+{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}).

Note that
\psi\mapsto M_{\psi}\in\operatorname{Sp}(2g,{\mathbb{Z}})
acts non-trivially on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2g}
, hence the bundle is non-trivial.
∎

Formula (
2.9
) shows that if
{\mathcal{F}}_{g}
is a fundamental domain for the action of
\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
, then
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}={\mathcal{F}}_{g}\times(-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}]^{2g}
is a fundamental domain for the action of
\Lambda_{g}({\mathbb{Z}})
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
.

We furthermore note that
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
can be represented as
\Lambda_{g}({\mathbb{R}})\backslash\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
, and the base projection
\Pi:\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
is then given by
\Lambda_{g}({\mathbb{Z}})\cdot(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})\mapsto\Lambda_{g}({\mathbb{R}})\cdot(X,\phi,{\mathbb{R}}^{2g})
.

3.
Invariant measures and Fourier transforms

We denote by
\operatorname{Vol}_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}
the Weil-Petersson measure on
{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
. Since it is
\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
-invariant, it descends to a measure
\mu_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}
on
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. This measure is finite and we normalise it to be a probability measure. That is, for any Borel set
{\mathcal{B}}\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
,

(3.1)

\mu_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}({\mathcal{B}})=\frac{\operatorname{Vol}_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}({\mathcal{F}}_{g}\cap\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{B}})}{\operatorname{Vol}_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}({\mathcal{F}}_{g})},

where
\pi
is the canonical projection
{\mathcal{T}}_{g}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
and
{\mathcal{F}}_{g}
is a measurable fundamental domain of
\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
.

Let us now lift this measure to natural measures on the extended Teichmüller and moduli spaces. For
q\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
, we define the measure
\operatorname{Vol}_{g,q}
on
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
by

(3.2)

\operatorname{Vol}_{g,q}=\operatorname{Vol}_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}\times\nu_{q}^{2g}

where
\nu_{\infty}^{k}=\operatorname{Vol}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{k}}
is the standard Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{k}
and otherwise

(3.3)

\nu_{q}^{k}=\frac{1}{q^{k}}\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$m$}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{k}}\delta_{q^{-1}{\text{\boldmath$m$}}}.

Here
\delta_{\text{\boldmath$\eta$}}
denotes the Dirac mass at
\eta
. The point of these definitions is that
each
\nu_{q}^{2g}
is invariant under the action of
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,{\mathbb{Z}})
(by linear transformations) and translation invariant by a subgroup containing
{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}
. Therefore they descend to natural
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,{\mathbb{Z}})
-invariant probability measures on
{\mathbb{T}}^{2g}
, which for simplicity we will also denote by
\nu_{q}^{2g}
. Explicitly,
\nu_{\infty}^{k}=\operatorname{Vol}_{{\mathbb{T}}^{k}}
the Haar-Lebesgue probability measure and, for
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(3.4)

\nu_{q}^{k}=\frac{1}{q^{k}}\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$m$}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{k}}\delta_{q^{-1}{\text{\boldmath$m$}}}

with the shorthand
{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}={\mathbb{Z}}/q{\mathbb{Z}}
.

With these assumptions, the measure
\operatorname{Vol}_{g,q}
is invariant under the extended mapping class group
\Lambda_{g}({\mathbb{Z}})
, hence descends, after the appropriate normalisation, to a probability measure
\mu_{g,q}
defined on Borel sets
{\mathcal{B}}\subset\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
by

(3.5)

\mu_{g,q}({\mathcal{B}})=\frac{\operatorname{Vol}_{g,q}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g}\cap\pi^{-1}{\mathcal{B}})}{\operatorname{Vol}_{g,q}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g})}

where
\pi
now denotes the canonical projection

\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{g}\rightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. Note that

\operatorname{Vol}_{g,q}(\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}_{g})=\operatorname{Vol}_{g}({\mathcal{F}}_{g})
, since
\nu_{q}^{2g}({\mathbb{T}}^{2g})=1
.

In the following we denote by
\,{}^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{2g}
the set of integer column vectors
\,{}^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}}

with
2g
coefficients in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}={\mathbb{Z}}/q{\mathbb{Z}}
. For functions
F:{\mathcal{T}}_{g}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2g}\to{\mathbb{C}}
so that

F(X,\phi,\,\cdot\,)\in\operatorname{L{}}^{1}({\mathbb{T}}^{2g},\nu_{q}^{2g})
, we define the
fiber Fourier transform

\widehat{F}_{q}:{\mathcal{T}}_{g}\times\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{2g}\to{\mathbb{C}}
by

(3.6)

\widehat{F}_{q}(X,\phi,\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2g}}F(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})\,e(-{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})\,d\nu_{q}^{2g}({\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}),

with
e(x)=\mathrm{e}^{2i\pi x}
.
This is the standard Fourier transform for
q=\infty
(put
{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}={\mathbb{Z}}
), and the discrete Fourier transform if
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Of particular interest is the zeroth Fourier coefficient, which we denote by

(3.7)

\breve{F}_{q}(X,\phi)=\widehat{F}_{q}(X,\phi,\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$0$}}).

We define the dual action of
\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
on
{\mathcal{T}}_{g}\times\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}
by

(3.8)

\psi\star(X,\phi,\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})=(X,\phi\circ\psi^{-1},M_{\psi}\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}}).

Then

(3.9)

\widehat{F}_{q}(\psi\star(X,\phi,\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}}))=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2g}}F(\psi\cdot(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}))\,e(-{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})\,d\nu_{q}^{2g}({\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}).

Hence, if
F
is invariant under
\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
, and thus can be viewed as a function on
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
, then

(3.10)

\widehat{F}_{q}(\psi\star(X,\phi,\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}}))=\widehat{F}_{q}(X,\phi,\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})

and

(3.11)

\breve{F}_{q}(\psi\cdot(X,\phi))=\breve{F}_{q}(X,\phi)

for all
\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
.

In the following, we identify
\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
-invariant functions of
{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
with functions of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
, and
\Lambda_{g}({\mathbb{Z}})
-invariant functions of
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
with functions of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
, using the same notation.

With this convention, we can define the fiber Fourier transform for
F\in\operatorname{L{}}^{1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g},\mu_{g,q})
by

(3.12)

\widehat{F}_{q}(X,\phi,\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})=\int_{\Pi^{-1}(X,\phi)}F(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})\,e(-{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})\,d\nu_{q}^{2g}({\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})

where
\widehat{F}_{q}
is now viewed as a function of the quotient
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g,q}^{\star}:=\mathrm{MCG_{g}}\backslash({\mathcal{T}}_{g}\times\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{2g})
with respect to the
\star
-action. The zeroth Fourier coefficient
\breve{F}_{q}
is identified with a function of
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. The above discussion in particular implies the following.

Proposition 3
.

If
F\in\operatorname{L{}}^{1}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g},\mu_{g,q})
then
\breve{F}_{q}\in\operatorname{L{}}^{1}({\mathcal{M}}_{g},\mu_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}})
with

(3.13)

\int_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}F\,d\mu_{g,q}=\int_{{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}\breve{F}_{g}\,d\mu_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}.

We will now apply the above formula to a class of functions that arise in the geometric analysis of twisted Laplacians.
The reduction to a standard integral over moduli space will then allow us to apply Mirzakhani’s integration formulas.

4.
Length-holonomy functions

Given a closed non-contractible oriented loop
\gamma
on
S_{g}
, let
\ell_{\gamma}(X,\phi)
denote the length of the oriented closed geodesic
\phi(\gamma)
on
X
, and
\operatorname{Stab}_{\gamma}
the stabiliser of
\gamma
in
\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
. We denote by
[\gamma]\in H_{1}(S_{g};\mathbb{Z})
the holonomy of
\gamma
represented as a column vector written in the basis
(a_{i},b_{i})_{1\leq i\leq g}
, as defined above.

Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{T}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a smooth compactly supported function. We naturally
associate to
f
and
\gamma
a function
f_{\gamma}
defined on
{\mathcal{T}}_{g}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{2g}
by

(4.1)

f_{\gamma}(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})=f(\ell_{\gamma}(X,\phi),{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\,[\gamma]).

For
\psi\in\operatorname{Stab}_{\gamma}
, we have
\ell_{\gamma}(X,\phi\circ\psi^{-1})=\ell_{\gamma}(X,\phi)
and
{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}M_{\psi}^{-1}\,[\gamma]={\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\,[\psi^{-1}(\gamma)]={\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\,[\gamma]
, and therefore

(4.2)

f_{\gamma}(\psi\cdot(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}))=f_{\gamma}(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}).

We now define the function
F_{\gamma}
by

(4.3)

F_{\gamma}(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})=\sum_{\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}/\operatorname{Stab}_{\gamma}}f_{\gamma}(\psi\cdot(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})).

This function is, by construction,
\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
-invariant, i.e.,
F_{\gamma}(\psi\cdot(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}))=F_{\gamma}(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})
for all
\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
, and may therefore be viewed as a function on
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. Its fiber Fourier transform is as follows.

Proposition 4
.

For
\gamma
as above,
q\leq\infty
,
{\text{\boldmath$m$}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{2g}
and
f
smooth with compact support, we
have

(4.4)

\widehat{F}_{\gamma,q}(X,\phi,\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})=\sum_{\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}/\operatorname{Stab}_{\gamma}}\widehat{f}_{\gamma,q}(\psi\star(X,\phi,\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})),

where the fiber Fourier transform
\hat{f}_{\gamma,q}
of
f_{\gamma}
is equal to

(4.5)

\widehat{f}_{\gamma,q}(X,\phi,\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}\widehat{f}_{q}(\ell_{\gamma}(X,\phi),n)\;{\mathbbm{1}}(\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}}=n[\gamma]\bmod q\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g})

with, for
(x,n)\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}
,

(4.6)

\widehat{f}_{q}(x,n)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x,y)\,e(-ny)\,d\nu_{q}^{1}(y).

Proof.

In view of (
3.6
), we have

(4.7)

\widehat{F}_{q}(X,\phi,\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})=\sum_{\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}/\operatorname{Stab}_{\gamma}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2g}}f_{\gamma}(\psi\cdot(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}))\,e(-{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})\,d\nu_{q}^{2g}({\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}).

Now (
3.9
) yields

(4.8)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2g}}f_{\gamma}(\psi\cdot(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}))\,e(-{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})\,d\nu_{q}^{2g}({\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})=\widehat{f}_{\gamma,q}(\psi\star(X,\phi,\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})),

where

(4.9)

\widehat{f}_{\gamma,q}(X,\phi,\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2g}}f_{\gamma}(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})\,e(-{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})\,d\nu_{q}^{2g}({\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})

is the fibre Fourier transform of
f_{\gamma}
. This establishes (
4.4
).

Next, we expand the length-holonomy function in terms of the Fourier series of the test function
f
,

(4.10)

f_{\gamma}(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})=f(\ell_{\gamma}(X,\phi),{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\,[\gamma])=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}}\widehat{f}_{q}(\ell_{\gamma}(X,\phi),n)\;e(n{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\,[\gamma]).

Formula (
4.5
) then follows by inserting this expression in (
4.9
)
and using the identity

(4.11)

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2g}}e({\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$s$}})\,d\nu_{q}^{2g}({\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})={\mathbbm{1}}({\text{\boldmath$s$}}={\text{\boldmath$0$}}\bmod q{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g})

with
\,{}^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$s$}}=n[\gamma]-\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}}
.
∎

In the special case of the zeroth Fourier coefficient, the formula reads

(4.12)

\breve{F}_{\gamma,q}(X,\phi)=\sum_{\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}/\operatorname{Stab}_{\gamma}}\breve{f}_{\gamma,q}(\ell_{\gamma}(\psi\cdot(X,\phi))

where

(4.13)

\breve{f}_{\gamma,q}(x)=\sum_{n\in\ker_{q}[\gamma]}\widehat{f}_{q}(x,n),\qquad\ker_{q}[\gamma]=\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}:n[\gamma]=\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$0$}}\bmod q\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}\}.

In particular, for
q=\infty
, we have

\ker_{\infty}[\gamma]=\begin{cases}\{0\}&([\gamma]\neq\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$0$}})\\
{\mathbb{Z}}&([\gamma]=\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$0$}})\end{cases}

and

\breve{f}_{\gamma,\infty}(x)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x,y)\,d\nu_{\infty}^{1}(y)&([\gamma]\neq\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$0$}})\\[10.0pt]
f(x,0)&([\gamma]=\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$0$}}).\end{cases}

Note that although the holonomy
[\psi(\gamma)]
depends on the choice of
\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
, the
condition
n[\psi(\gamma)]=\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$0$}}\bmod q\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}
is independent of that choice, since
the integer lattice
q\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!\mathbb{Z}^{2g}
is stabilised by
\operatorname{Sp}(2g,{\mathbb{Z}})
. Hence

\ker_{q}[\gamma]=\ker_{q}[\psi(\gamma)]
for
\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
. We conclude that

\breve{F}_{\gamma,q}(X,\phi)
is
\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
-invariant and therefore a well-defined function on the
moduli space
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. In fact,
\breve{F}_{\gamma,q}(X,\phi)
belongs to the family of “geometric
functions” studied by Mirzakhani when
\gamma
is simple
[
12
,
13
]
, and by
Anantharaman and the second author for general loops
[
1
]
.

We extend the above discussion to multi-loops
{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k})
, with each

\gamma_{j}
a closed non-contractible oriented loop on the base surface
S_{g}
. We set

{\text{\boldmath$\ell$}}_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}=(\ell_{\gamma_{1}},\ldots,\ell_{\gamma_{k}})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{k}
and

(4.14)

\operatorname{Stab}_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}=\{\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}:\psi(\gamma_{j})=\gamma_{j}\;\forall j=1,\ldots,k\}.

In the following,
[{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}]=([\gamma_{1}],\ldots,[\gamma_{k}])\in H_{1}(S_{g};\mathbb{Z})^{k}
is
represented as a
(2g\times k)
matrix. For a compactly supported smooth function

f:{\mathbb{R}}^{k}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{k}\to{\mathbb{C}}
and
(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})\in{\mathcal{T}}_{g}\times\mathbb{T}^{2g}
, define

(4.15)

f_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})=f({\text{\boldmath$\ell$}}_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(X,\phi),{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\,[{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}])

and

(4.16)

F_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})=\sum_{\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}/\operatorname{Stab}_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}f_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\psi\cdot(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})).

We have the same
\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
-invariance as above, i.e.,

F_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(\psi\cdot(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}))=F_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})
for all
\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
,
and can therefore view
F_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}
as a function on
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. Its fiber Fourier
transform is given in the following proposition.

Proposition 5
.

For
\gamma
a multi-loop as above,
q\leq\infty
,
{\text{\boldmath$m$}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{2g}
and
f
smooth with compact support, we have

(4.17)

\widehat{F}_{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}},q}(X,\phi,\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})=\sum_{\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}/\operatorname{Stab}_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}\widehat{f}_{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}},q}(\psi\star(X,\phi,\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})),

where the fiber Fourier transform
\widehat{f}_{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}},q}
of
f_{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}
is equal to

(4.18)

\widehat{f}_{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}},q}(X,\phi,\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}})=\sum_{{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{q}^{k}}\widehat{f}_{q}({\text{\boldmath$\ell$}}_{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}(X,\phi),{\text{\boldmath$n$}})\;{\mathbbm{1}}(\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$m$}}=[{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}]\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\bmod q\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g})

with, for
({\text{\boldmath$x$}},{\text{\boldmath$n$}})\in\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{k}
,

(4.19)

\widehat{f}_{q}({\text{\boldmath$x$}},{\text{\boldmath$n$}})=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{k}}f({\text{\boldmath$x$}},{\text{\boldmath$y$}})\,e(-{\text{\boldmath$n$}}\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$y$}})\,d\nu_{q}^{k}({\text{\boldmath$y$}}).

Proof.

This is analogous to the proof of Proposition
4
.
∎

The zeroth Fourier coefficient

\breve{F}_{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}},q}(X,\phi)=\widehat{F}_{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}},q}(X,\phi,\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$0$}})
is again
\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
-invariant
and thus well-defined on
{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. Specifying
{\text{\boldmath$m$}}={\text{\boldmath$0$}}
in (
4.17
), (
4.18

(
4.12
) and (
4.13
) in the case of multi-loops.

5.
Twisted Laplacians

We will now explain how the data
(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})
is related to the Fuchsian group
\Gamma
and its character
\chi_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}
used in the introduction to define the twisted Laplacian.

Given
{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2g}
, we define the unitary character

\chi_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}:\pi_{1}(S_{g})\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
by

(5.1)

\chi_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}([\gamma])=e({\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\,[\gamma]),

where
[\gamma]\in H_{1}(S_{g};\mathbb{Z})
is represented as a column vector written in the basis

a_{1},\ldots,a_{g}
,
b_{1},\ldots,b_{g}
of
H_{1}(S_{g};\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
X=\Gamma(X)\backslash{\mathbb{H}}
be a compact hyperbolic surface and
\phi:S_{g}\rightarrow X
a
marking. Let us pick a (fixed) basepoint
z_{0}
on our base surface
S_{g}
. We use
\phi(z_{0})
as a
basepoint to define the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(X)
, and choose a lift
\tilde{z}
of
\phi(z_{0})

in
{\mathbb{H}}
. For any
\eta\in\Gamma(X)
, the geodesic arc from
\tilde{z}
to
\eta(\tilde{z})
on

{\mathbb{H}}
projects onto a geodesic loop on
X
, which we denote as
\eta_{z_{0}}\in\pi_{1}(X)
. Then, taking
the preimage by the marking naturally induces a group isomorphism

(5.2)

\begin{split}\iota_{(X,\phi)}:\Gamma(X)&\rightarrow\pi_{1}(S_{g})\\
\eta&\mapsto\phi^{-1}(\eta_{z_{0}}).\end{split}

We can now define the unitary character
\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}:\Gamma(X)\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
by

(5.3)

\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}(\eta)=\chi_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}([\iota_{(X,\phi)}(\eta)])=e({\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}[\phi^{-1}(\eta_{z_{0}})]).

Note that, because the homology class is invariant by change of basepoint and homotopy,

\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}
is a well-defined function of

(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})\in\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
, and independent from our choice of basepoint

z_{0}
. Furthermore, for
\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
, we have

(5.4)

\chi_{\psi\cdot(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}(\eta)=e({\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}M_{\psi}^{-1}\,[\psi\circ\phi^{-1}(\eta_{z_{0}})])=\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}(\eta)

and, for
{\text{\boldmath$m$}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}
,

(5.5)

\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}+{\text{\boldmath$m$}})}(\eta)=\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}(\eta).

Therefore the character family
\chi=\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}
is invariant under
\Lambda_{g}({\mathbb{Z}})
and hence parametrised by the extended moduli space
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. This in turn yields a
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
-family of twisted Laplacians
\Delta_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}=\Delta_{\chi}
.

We will consider
\Delta_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}
as random operators by drawing
(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})
with respect to the probability measures
\mu_{g,q}
defined in (
3.5
). For
q=1
, the character is trivial, and for
q=2
real-valued. The following proposition states that for
q\geq 3
the characters are real-valued with vanishing probability in the limit of large genus.

Proposition 6
.

For every
(X,\phi)\in{\mathcal{T}}_{g}
, we have

(5.6)

\nu_{q}^{2g}\big\{{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2g}:\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}^{2}=\operatorname{id}\big\}=\begin{cases}0&(q=\infty)\\
1/q^{2g}&(q\in 2{\mathbb{N}}+1)\\
(2/q)^{2g}&(q\in 2{\mathbb{N}}).\end{cases}

Proof.

This follows straightforwardly from the observation that
\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}^{2}=\operatorname{id}
if and only if
{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\in\{0,1/2\}^{2g}\bmod{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}
.
∎

This observation is consistent with Theorem
1
which shows that, although the random ensemble with respect to the measure
\mu_{g,q}
(
q\geq 3
) includes twisted Laplacians with GOE statistics, these do not spoil the GUE statistics of the full ensemble.

We now state the main lemmas required for the proof of Theorem

∎

Proposition 10
.

Under the assumptions for Theorem
1
, we have

(7.5)

\lim_{g\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{g,q}\Bigg[\bigg|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\\
\mathrm{prim.}\end{subarray}}\frac{\ell_{\gamma}\,\hat{h}_{L,\tau}(n\ell_{\gamma})\,e({\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}[\gamma]n)}{2\sinh(n\ell_{\gamma}/2)}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]\\
=\begin{cases}\Sigma_{\mathrm{GOE}}^{2}(f)+|I_{f,q}(L,\tau)|^{2}+O(L^{-2}\log L)&(q=1,2)\\
\Sigma_{\mathrm{GUE}}^{2}(f)+|I_{f,q}(L,\tau)|^{2}+O(L^{-2}\log L)&(3\leq q\leq\infty).\end{cases}

Proof.

Let us first consider
q<\infty
. By Lemma
8
, we need to find the large
g
asymptotics of

(7.6)

{\mathbb{E}}_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}\Bigg[\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\;\mathrm{prim.}\\
n_{1}[\gamma_{1}]=n_{2}[\gamma_{2}]\\
\bmod q\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}\end{subarray}}\frac{\ell_{\gamma_{1}}\hat{h}_{L,\tau}(n_{1}\ell_{\gamma_{1}})}{2\sinh(n_{1}\ell_{\gamma_{1}}/2)}\;\frac{\ell_{\gamma_{2}}\overline{\hat{h}}_{L,\tau}(n_{2}\ell_{\gamma_{2}})}{2\sinh(n_{2}\ell_{\gamma_{2}}/2)}\Bigg].

As above, the lower order terms for
q=1
dominate those for
q\geq 2
, and hence we can refer to
the estimates of
[
17
, §5.2, §6]
.

The only leading order terms come from pairs
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
of simple non-separating oriented
loops in one of the following three constellations: (i)
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}
, (ii)

\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}^{-1}
, or (iii)
\gamma_{1}\cap\gamma_{2}=\emptyset
.

In case (i), the loops are represented as a single orbit under
\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
of a simple non-separating
oriented loop
\gamma
with
[\gamma]=\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!(1,0,\ldots,0)
. The contribution of these terms to (

) is thus

(7.7)

{\mathbb{E}}_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}\Bigg[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}=1\\
n_{1}=n_{2}\bmod q\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\sum_{\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}/\operatorname{Stab}_{\gamma}}\frac{\ell_{\psi(\gamma)}^{2}\hat{h}_{L,\tau}(n_{1}\ell_{\psi(\gamma)})\overline{\hat{h}}_{L,\tau}(n_{2}\ell_{\psi(\gamma)})}{4\sinh(n_{1}\ell_{\psi(\gamma)}/2)\sinh(n_{2}\ell_{\psi(\gamma)}/2)}\Bigg].

Rudnick shows in
[
17
, Lemma 5.2]
that for

contribute at
most
O(L^{-2}\log L)
, and hence the same is true for the contribution of those terms to
(
7.7
). The remaining terms are

, and is worked out in
[
17
, Lemma 5.2]
,

(7.8)

{\mathbb{E}}_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}\Bigg[\sum_{\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}/\operatorname{Stab}_{\gamma}}\frac{\ell_{\psi(\gamma)}^{2}|\hat{h}_{L,\tau}(\ell_{\psi(\gamma)})|^{2}}{4\sinh^{2}(\ell_{\psi(\gamma)}/2)}\Bigg]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x|\,|\hat{f}(x)|^{2}dx+O(L^{-2}).

We here recognize the GUE variance

\Sigma_{\mathrm{GUE}}^{2}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|x||\hat{f}(x)|^{2}\,dx
. [Note here that our
summation is over oriented rather than the non-oriented loops in
[
17
]

differences in the way factors of

obviously yielding the same final results because there is a
2

Case (ii) is analogous, again represented as a single orbit under
\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
of a simple non-separating
oriented loop
\gamma
with
[\gamma]=\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!(1,0,\ldots,0)
. Since

\ell_{\psi(\gamma)}=\ell_{\psi(\gamma^{-1})}
, the contribution of these terms is now

(7.9)

{\mathbb{E}}_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}\Bigg[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},n_{2}=1\\
n_{1}=-n_{2}\bmod q\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\sum_{\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}/\operatorname{Stab}_{\gamma}}\frac{\ell_{\psi(\gamma)}^{2}\hat{h}_{L,\tau}(n_{1}\ell_{\psi(\gamma)})\overline{\hat{h}}_{L,\tau}(n_{2}\ell_{\psi(\gamma)})}{4\sinh(n_{1}\ell_{\psi(\gamma)}/2)\sinh(n_{2}\ell_{\psi(\gamma)}/2)}\Bigg].

As before, the

case bounds above terms with

by

. This leaves
the case

, which is compatible with the condition

or

. In the latter case (
7.9
) becomes (
7.8
), so that the sum of the two

. We have
thus explained the GOE resp. GUE term in (
7.5
).

In the remaining case (iii), the loops are generated by the
\mathrm{MCG_{g}}
-orbit of one

{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
with representatives
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
which we can
pick so that
[\gamma_{1}]=\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!(1,0,\ldots,0)
and
[\gamma_{2}]=\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!(0,1,0,\ldots,0)

means that the condition
n_{1}[\gamma_{1}]=n_{2}[\gamma_{2}]\bmod q\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\mathbb{Z}}^{2g}
simplifies to

. The contribution of these terms is therefore

(7.10)

{\mathbb{E}}_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}\Bigg[\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\psi\in\mathrm{MCG_{g}}/\operatorname{Stab}_{{\text{\boldmath$\gamma$}}}}\frac{\ell_{\psi(\gamma_{1})}\ell_{\psi(\gamma_{2})}\hat{h}_{L,\tau}(n_{1}q\ell_{\psi(\gamma_{1})})\overline{\hat{h}}_{L,\tau}(n_{2}q\ell_{\psi(\gamma_{2})})}{4\sinh(n_{1}q\ell_{\psi(\gamma_{1})}/2)\sinh(n_{2}q\ell_{\psi(\gamma_{2})}/2)}\Bigg].

The same argument as in
[

can be used to show that the terms for

.
[Recall once more that our summation is over oriented loops, which explains an extra factor of

.]

The above proof remains the same for

∎

Proof of Theorem

.

We expand (

) as

\Sigma_{g,q}^{2}(L,\tau;f)={\mathbb{E}}_{g,q}\bigg[\big|N_{f,L,\tau}^{\mathrm{osc}}\big|^{2}\bigg]-\big|{\mathbb{E}}_{g,q}\big[N_{f,L,\tau}^{\mathrm{osc}}\big]\big|^{2},

Here the second equality is Selberg’s trace formula (

8.
Dirac operators and GSE statistics

The objective of this final section is to explain how the above construction can be extended to study random Dirac
operators, and in particular to prove GSE-statistics in the case of spin-systems with time-reversal
invariance. We refer the reader to
[
7
,
14
]
for a more detailed introduction to Dirac operators on hyperbolic surfaces.

The Dirac operator on the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}
is given as the matrix-valued
operator

(8.1)

\mathrm{D}=i\begin{pmatrix}0&iy\partial_{x}+y\partial_{y}-\frac{1}{2}\\
-iy\partial_{x}+y\partial_{y}-\frac{1}{2}&0\end{pmatrix}

acting on differentiable functions
{\mathbb{H}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
. We now consider a discrete co-compact subgroup
\overline{\Gamma}
of
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
, which is assumed to contain
-1
. It is the double cover of
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})=\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})/\{\pm 1\}
used in the setting of Laplacians, with the covering map given by
\eta\mapsto\pm\eta
. Since
\Gamma
is the fundamental group of a smooth hyperbolic surface, we have that
\overline{\Gamma}
is strictly hyperbolic, i.e., the only elements with
|\operatorname{tr}\eta|\leq 2
are
\eta=\pm 1
. The action of
\eta\in\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
on
{\mathbb{H}}\times\operatorname{S{}}^{1}
(with
\operatorname{S{}}^{1}
the unit circle in the complex plane) is defined as

(8.2)

(z,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})\mapsto\left(\frac{az+b}{cz+d},\frac{cz+d}{|cz+d|}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t}\right),\qquad\eta=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}}).

In particular,
-1
acts by
(z,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})\mapsto(z,-\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})
.
For
\eta\in\overline{\Gamma}
as above, set

(8.3)

j_{\eta}(z)=\frac{cz+d}{|cz+d|},\qquad J_{\eta}(z)=\begin{pmatrix}j_{\eta}(z)&0\\
0&j_{\eta}(\bar{z})\end{pmatrix}.

We fix a unitary character

\chi:\overline{\Gamma}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
such that
\chi(-1)=-1
. We consider the action of

\mathrm{D}
on the space of differentiable functions
\varphi:{\mathbb{H}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
satisfying

(8.4)

\varphi(\eta\cdot z)=\chi(\eta)\,J_{\eta}(z)\,\varphi(z)\qquad\text{for all }\eta\in\overline{\Gamma},

and define the Dirac operator
\mathrm{D}_{\chi}
for
X=\Gamma\backslash{\mathbb{H}}
to be the self-adjoint extension of the restriction of
\mathrm{D}
to this space of automorphic forms.

If
\varphi=\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{1}\\
\varphi_{2}\end{pmatrix}
is an eigenform of
\mathrm{D}_{\chi}
with eigenvalue
r
, then
\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{1}\\
-\varphi_{2}\end{pmatrix}
is an eigenform with eigenvalue
-r

[
7
, Lemma 1]
.
This so-called
chiral symmetry
implies therefore that the spectrum of the operator
\mathrm{D}_{\chi}
is symmetric
around
0
. We denote by
0\leq r_{0}\leq r_{1}\leq\ldots\to\infty
its non-negative eigenvalues with multiplicity, including half of the eigenvalues that are equal to
0
.

As in the case of the twisted Laplacian, this counting function can be studied by means of the trace formula
[
7
, Theorem 1]

(8.5)

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}h(r_{j})=(g-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(r)\,r\coth(\pi r)\,dr+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{\eta\}\\
\operatorname{tr}\eta>2\end{subarray}}\chi(\eta)^{n}\,\frac{\ell_{\eta}\hat{h}(n\ell_{\eta})}{2\sinh(n\ell_{\eta}/2)}

where the sum is, as in the classical Selberg trace formula, over
\overline{\Gamma}
-conjugacy classes of
primitive hyperbolic elements, but now with
\overline{\Gamma}
considered a subgroup of
\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
,
and the summation restricted to the elements
\eta
with
\operatorname{tr}\eta>2
.

As noted in
[
7
, Proposition 1]
, the sequence of
\lambda_{j}=r_{j}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}

coincides with the eigenvalues of the Maaß-Laplacian of weight
1
, hence the above trace
formula is in fact the same as the classical Selberg trace formula in that setting
[
9
, Theorem
4.11]
.

Let us now construct random ensembles of Dirac operators

\mathrm{D}_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}=\mathrm{D}_{\chi}
with
(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})
sampled from

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
with respect to the probability measure
\mu_{g,q}
defined in Section

3
.

Let

X=\Gamma(X)\backslash{\mathbb{H}}
be a compact hyperbolic surface and
\phi:S_{g}\rightarrow X
a
marking. We define, as set out above, the group
\overline{\Gamma}(X)
as the double
cover of
\Gamma(X)\subset\operatorname{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
,

\overline{\Gamma}(X)=\{\eta\in\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}}),\pm\eta\in\Gamma(X)\}.

We recall that the marking induces a group isomorphism

\iota_{(X,\phi)}:\Gamma(X)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(S_{g})
, defined in (
5.2
), and that we
have fixed a family of fundamental loops
a_{1},\ldots,a_{g},b_{1},\ldots,b_{g}
on
S_{g}
. For
every
i\in\{1,\ldots,g\}
, the group elements
\iota_{(X,\phi)}^{-1}(a_{i})
and

\iota_{(X,\phi)}^{-1}(b_{i})\in\Gamma(X)
each have two representatives in

\overline{\Gamma}(X)
; we pick
\tilde{a}_{i}
and
\tilde{b}_{i}\in\overline{\Gamma}(X)
to be
their respective representatives with positive trace. Then, the elements
-1
,

\tilde{a}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{a}_{g}
,
\tilde{b}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{b}_{g}
generates the group

\overline{\Gamma}(X)
. For
{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2g}
, we define the unitary character

\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}:\overline{\Gamma}(X)\to{\mathbb{C}}^{\times}
by

(8.6)

\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}(\tilde{a}_{j})=\chi_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}(a_{j})=e(\theta_{j}),\qquad\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}(\tilde{b}_{j})=\chi_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}(b_{j})=e(\theta_{j+g}),\qquad\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}(-1)=-1,

where
\chi_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}
is the character of
\pi_{1}(S_{g})
defined in (
5.1
).

For a general
\eta\in\overline{\Gamma}
and its associated loop
\gamma\in\pi_{1}(S_{g})
, we have

\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}(\eta)=\pm\chi_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}(\gamma)
.
We capture the
sign in this relation by defining
\sigma_{\gamma}\in\{\pm 1\}
as

(8.7)

\sigma_{\gamma}=\operatorname{sign}(\operatorname{tr}\eta)\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}(\eta)\chi_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}(\gamma)^{-1}.

We observe that positive trace is not preserved under multiplication, and
\sigma_{\gamma}
is
therefore not multiplicative in
\gamma
. We have, however, the following.

Lemma 11
.

\sigma_{\gamma^{-1}}=\sigma_{\gamma}
and more generally
\sigma_{\gamma^{n}}=\sigma_{\gamma}^{n}
for

n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

To see this, note that if
\operatorname{tr}\eta>0
then we have
\operatorname{tr}\eta^{n}>0
(this is trivial for
\eta=1
,
and a fact for all
\eta\in\operatorname{SL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
with
\operatorname{tr}\eta>2
). Hence, by the multiplicativity of
characters, we have

\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}(\eta)^{n}=\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}(\eta^{n})=\sigma_{\gamma^{n}}\chi_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}(\gamma^{n})=\sigma_{\gamma^{n}}\chi_{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}(\gamma)^{n}
, which yields

\sigma_{\gamma^{n}}=\sigma_{\gamma}^{n}
.
∎

This lemma allows us to write the right hand side of the trace formula (
8.5
) in terms of geometric (in place of algebraic) data. We have

(8.8)

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}h(r_{j})=(g-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}}h(r)\,r\coth(\pi r)\,dr+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\\
\mathrm{prim.}\end{subarray}}\frac{\sigma_{\gamma}^{n}\ell_{\gamma}\,\hat{h}(n\ell_{\gamma})\,e({\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}[\gamma]n)}{2\sinh(n\ell_{\gamma}/2)}.

We now sample
{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}\in{\mathbb{T}}^{2g}
with respect to the probability measure
\nu_{q}^{2g}
as defined in Section
3
. Since the range of
\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}
contains
-1
, it is natural to consider only even values of
q
as well as
q=\infty
.

The case
q=2
corresponds to real characters
\overline{\chi}=\chi
, which means
\mathrm{D}_{\chi}

is symmetric under time-reversal and we have Kramer’s degeneracy
[
7
, Lemma 2]
; that is, every eigenvalue

r_{j}
has even multiplicity. This motivates the definition of the
following reduced counting function taking into account the systematic degeneracy,

(8.9)

N_{f,L,\tau}^{q}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}h_{L,\tau}(r_{j})&(q=2)\\[5.0pt]
\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}h_{L,\tau}(r_{j})&(q\geq 4).\end{cases}

As in the case of the Laplacian, we are interested in the large
g
asymptotics of the spectral variance of the Dirac operator
D_{\chi}
on
X
with
\chi=\chi_{(X,\phi,{\text{\boldmath$\theta$}})}
,

(8.10)

\widetilde{\Sigma}_{g,q}^{2}(L,\tau;f)={\mathbb{E}}_{g,q}\bigg[\big|N_{f,L,\tau}^{q}-{\mathbb{E}}_{g,q}[N_{f,L,\tau}^{q}]\big|^{2}\bigg].

The following theorem shows that the spectral statistics of a Dirac operator are also consistent with the expected random matrix distributions, i.e., GSE statistics in the presence of time-reversal symmetry, and GUE otherwise.

Theorem 12
.

Fix
\tau>0
. For
f:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
even with smooth, compactly supported Fourier transform, we have, for
L\geq 2
,

(8.11)

\lim_{g\to\infty}\widetilde{\Sigma}_{g,q}^{2}(L,\tau;f)=\begin{cases}\Sigma_{\mathrm{GSE}}^{2}(f)+O(L^{-2}\log L)&(q=2)\\
\Sigma_{\mathrm{GUE}}^{2}(f)+O(L^{-2}\log L)&(q\in 2{\mathbb{N}}+2,\;q=\infty),\end{cases}

with the random matrix density
\Sigma_{\mathrm{GUE}}^{2}(f)
defined in Theorem
1
and

(8.12)

\Sigma_{\mathrm{GSE}}^{2}(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|x|}{2}\,|\hat{f}(x)|^{2}dx.

The proof of Theorem
12
mirrors that of Theorem
1
, and the key steps are the following
analogues of Propositions
9
and
10
.

Proposition 13
.

Under the assumptions for Theorem
12
, we have

(8.13)

\lim_{g\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{g,q}\Bigg[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\\
\mathrm{prim.}\end{subarray}}\frac{\sigma_{\gamma}^{n}\ell_{\gamma}\,\hat{h}_{L,\tau}(n\ell_{\gamma})\,e({\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}[\gamma]n)}{2\sinh(n\ell_{\gamma}/2)}\Bigg]=I_{f,q}(L,\tau),

with
I_{f,q}(L,\tau)
as in (
7.1
).

Proof.

The same argument as in proof of Proposition
9
tells us that only primitive loops
with
\gcd[\gamma]=1
contribute to the leading order term. The analogous calculation as in Lemma

7
shows that, for the restriction of the sum over primitive geodesics
\gamma
with

\gcd[\gamma]=1
, we have

(8.14)

{\mathbb{E}}_{g,q}\Bigg[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\\
\mathrm{prim.}\\
\gcd[\gamma]=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\sigma_{\gamma}^{n}\,\ell_{\gamma}\,\hat{h}_{L,\tau}(n\ell_{\gamma})\,e({\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}[\gamma]n)}{2\sinh(n\ell_{\gamma}/2)}\Bigg]=\begin{cases}0&(q=\infty)\\
\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}_{g}^{\mathrm{WP}}\bigg[\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\\
\mathrm{prim.}\\
\gcd[\gamma]=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\ell_{\gamma}\hat{h}_{L,\tau}(nq\ell_{\gamma})}{2\sinh(nq\ell_{\gamma}/2)}\bigg]&(q\in 2{\mathbb{N}}).\end{cases}

The key point here is that, for
q<\infty
, the condition
\gcd[\gamma]=1
restricts the sum over
n
to multiples
of
q
(as in Lemma
7
). These are even (since
q
is), and hence

\sigma_{\gamma}^{n}=1
for such
n
. For
q=\infty
, the average restricts the sum to
[\gamma]=\,^{\mathrm{t}}\!{\text{\boldmath$0$}}
(cf. Lemma
7
), for which
\gcd[\gamma]=0
and hence these terms do not contribute. We can now proceed as in the proof of
Proposition
9
.
∎

Proposition 14
.

Under the assumptions for Theorem
12
, we have

(8.15)

\lim_{g\to\infty}{\mathbb{E}}_{g,q}\Bigg[\bigg|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma\\
\mathrm{prim.}\end{subarray}}\frac{\sigma_{\gamma}^{n}\ell_{\gamma}\,\hat{h}_{L,\tau}(n\ell_{\gamma})\,e({\text{\boldmath$\theta$}}[\gamma]n)}{2\sinh(n\ell_{\gamma}/2)}\bigg|^{2}\Bigg]\\
=\begin{cases}\Sigma_{\mathrm{GOE}}^{2}(f)+|I_{f,q}(L,\tau)|^{2}+O(L^{-2}\log L)&(q=2)\\
\Sigma_{\mathrm{GUE}}^{2}(f)+|I_{f,q}(L,\tau)|^{2}+O(L^{-2}\log L)&(q\in 2{\mathbb{N}}+2,\;q=\infty).\end{cases}

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
10
, the only leading order terms in the high genus limit come again from pairs
(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
of simple non-separating oriented loops, such that one of the following holds:
(i)
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}
,
(ii)
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{2}^{-1}
, or
(iii)
\gamma_{1}\cap\gamma_{2}=\emptyset
.
To evaluate these terms, we follow the argument used to prove Proposition
10
, where we showed that in case (i) we have
n_{1}=n_{2}\bmod q
, in case (ii)
n_{1}=-n_{2}\bmod q
, and in case (iii)
n_{1}=n_{2}=0\bmod q
. Thus, if
q\in 2{\mathbb{N}}
, we have for (i) and (ii) that

\sigma_{\gamma_{1}}^{n_{1}}\sigma_{\gamma_{2}}^{n_{2}}=\sigma_{\gamma_{1}}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}=\sigma_{\gamma_{1}}^{n_{1}-n_{2}}=\sigma_{\gamma_{1}}^{q}=1
, and for (iii)

\sigma_{\gamma_{1}}^{n_{1}}\sigma_{\gamma_{2}}^{n_{2}}=\sigma_{\gamma_{1}}^{q}\sigma_{\gamma_{2}}^{q}=1
. The case
q=\infty
is analogous (drop the mod
q
).
This proves that the leading order terms in the high genus limit are exactly the same as in Proposition
10
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
12
.

We apply the Selberg trace formula (
8.8
) for the Dirac operator with test function
h=h_{L,\tau}
in order to convert the spectral variance to the geometric sums of Propositions
13
and
14
. Hence the claim is proved when
q=4,6,\ldots,\infty
. For the remaining case
q=2
, we observe that due to time-reversal symmetry (
\chi
is real-valued) the counting function (
8.9
) is reduced by a factor of 2 to account for Kramer’s degeneracy. Hence we should only take
\frac{1}{2}
of (
8.13
), and
\frac{1}{4}
of (
8.15
). This indeed yields the formula for the GSE density, since
\Sigma_{\mathrm{GSE}}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\Sigma_{\mathrm{GOE}}^{2}
.
∎

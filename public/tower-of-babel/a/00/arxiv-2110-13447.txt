Extremal Sidon Sets are Fourier Uniform, with Applications to Partition Regularity
Miquel Ortega, Sean Prendiville

Abstract
. Generalising results of Erdős-Freud and Lindström, we prove that the largest Sidon subset of a bounded interval of integers is equidistributed in Bohr neighbourhoods. We establish this by showing that extremal Sidon sets are Fourier-pseudorandom, in that they have no large non-trivial Fourier coefficients. As a further application we deduce that, for any partition regular equation in five or more variables, every finite colouring of an extremal Sidon set has a monochromatic solution.

1.1.
Partition regularity over extremal Sidon sets

We offer a further application of Theorem
1.2
in proving a colouring analogue of a recent result of Conlon, Fox, Sudakov and Zhao
[
3
]
, this being the original motivation for our paper.

Informally, call a Sidon subset of
[N]

dense
if its cardinality is a
positive proportion of
N^{1/2}
, say
\frac{1}{100}N^{1/2}
. The authors of

[
3
]
show that any dense Sidon subset of
[N]
contains a
non-trivial
6
6

6

For instance, with all variables distinct.
solution to the equation
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}=4x_{5}
. The essential features of this equation are that its coefficients sum to zero and that it has at least five variables. The five variable condition cannot be relaxed: every Sidon set lacks non-trivial solutions to the four-variable equation
x_{1}-x_{2}-x_{3}+x_{4}=0
. The assumption that the coefficients sum to zero is also necessary, as we now show.

Proposition 1.13
.

There exists a Sidon subset of
[N]
with at least

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}N^{1/2}(1+o(1))
elements, and which has no solutions to the equation
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}=x_{5}

Proof.

Take an extremal Sidon subset
S_{0}
of
[\frac{N-1}{2}]
and set
S:=2\cdot S_{0}+1
.
∎

Remark 1.14
.

The above construction can be adapted to show that for any homogeneous linear equation whose coefficients do not sum to zero there exists a dense Sidon sets lacking solutions to the equation.

To accommodate equations whose coefficients do not sum to zero, one may speculate on whether a colouring analogue of the results of Conlon, Fox, Sudakov and Zhao
[
3
]
should hold; cf. Rado’s criterion for partition regularity
[
14
, §3.2]
versus Roth’s criterion for density regularity
[
20
]
. Proposition
1.13
indicates that one cannot hope to always find monochromatic solutions to
x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}=x_{5}
in colourings of
dense
Sidon sets. In the following theorem we show that such a result does hold for colourings of
extremal
Sidon sets.

Theorem 1.15

(Partition regularity over extremal Sidon sets)
.

Let
c_{1},\dots,

c_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathopen{}\left\{0\mathclose{}\right\}
with
s\geqslant 5
and suppose that there exists a non-empty index set
I\subset[s]
satisfying
\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}=0
. Let

be a positive integer and
S\subset[N]
a Sidon set. Then at least one of the following holds:

•

N
is small, in that
N\ll_{c_{1},\dots,c_{s},r}1
.

•

S
is not extremal, in that
\mathopen{}\left||S|-N^{1/2}\mathclose{}\right|\gg_{c_{1},\dots,c_{s},r}N^{1/2}
.

•

Partition regularity: For any
r
-colouring
S=C_{1}\cup\dots\cup C_{r}
, there exists a colour class
C_{j}
such that

\sum_{c_{1}x_{1}+\dots+c_{s}x_{s}=0}1_{C_{j}}(x_{1})\dotsm 1_{C_{j}}(x_{s})\gg_{c_{1},\dots,c_{s},r}|S|^{s}N^{-1}

Remark 1.16
.

If
\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}\neq 0
for all
\emptyset\neq I\subset[s]
, then there exists a finite colouring of
\mathbb{N}
with no monochromatic solutions to the equation

(see Rado’s criterion for partition regularity

14

Conlon, Fox, Sudakov and Zhao
[
3
]
derive their results on dense Sidon sets via a removal lemma for

-free graphs. Since “extremal”

-free graphs are pseudorandom, see

[

Notation

Standard conventions

We use

to denote the set of integers
\{1,2,\dots,N\}
. We use

, so that for
f,g:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}

\mathopen{}\left\|f\mathclose{}\right\|_{p}:=\biggl(\sum_{x}|f(x)|^{p}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\ \text{and}\ (f*g)(x):=\sum_{y}f(y)g(x-y).

Any sum of the form

. The

\mathrm{supp}(f):=\mathopen{}\left\{x\in\mathbb{Z}:f(x)\neq 0\mathclose{}\right\}
.
For a finite set

, denote the average of

\mathbb{E}_{s\in S}f(s):=\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{s\in S}f(s).

We use Haar probability measure on

\mathopen{}\left\|F\mathclose{}\right\|_{p}:=\biggl(\int_{\mathbb{T}}|F(\alpha)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\alpha\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}=\biggl(\int_{0}^{1}|F(\alpha)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\alpha\biggr)^{\frac{1}{p}}

Write

Asymptotic notation

For a complex-valued function

We write

Local conventions

Up to normalisation, all of the above are widely used in the literature. Next, we list notation specific to our paper. We have tried to minimise this in order to aid the casual reader.

2.
Fourier uniformity

Given an extremal Sidon set
S\subset[N]
, our main goal in this section is to
show that
S
is a Fourier uniform subset of
[N]
, by proving Theorem
1.2
. Recalling our notation (
1.8
) for the Fejér kernel, we begin with the following version of van der Corput’s inequality.

Lemma 2.1

(Van der Corput differencing)
.

Suppose that
1\leqslant H\leqslant N
,
f\colon\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{C}
and
\mathrm{supp}(f)\subset[N]
. Then

(2.1)

\biggl|\sum_{x}f(x)\biggr|^{2}\leqslant(N+H)\sum_{h}\mu_{H}(h)\sum_{x}f(x)\overline{f(x+h)}.

Proof.

We have

\biggl|\sum_{x}f(x)\biggr|^{2}=\biggl|\mathbb{E}_{[H]}\sum_{x}f(x+h)\biggr|^{2}=\biggl|\sum_{x}\mathbb{E}_{[H]}f(x+h)\biggr|^{2}.

By Cauchy-Schwarz, the latter quantity is bounded by

(N+H)\sum_{x}\biggl|\mathbb{E}_{[H]}f(x+h)\biggr|^{2}=(N+H)\sum_{x}\frac{1}{\lfloor H\rfloor^{2}}\sum_{h_{1},h_{2}\in[H]}f(x+h_{1})\overline{f(x+h_{2})}.

We obtain the desired
inequality on changing variables in
x
and using (
1.8
).
∎

The next lemma tells us that, on taking an
appropriately sized
H
and supposing that
S
is extremal Sidon, the sum of
\mu_{H}(h)

over
S-S
is nearly
1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
S\subset[N]
be a Sidon set. Then

\sum_{h\in(S-S)\setminus\mathopen{}\left\{0\mathclose{}\right\}}\mu_{H}(h)\geqslant\frac{|S|^{2}}{N+H}-\frac{|S|}{\mathopen{}\left\lfloor H\right\rfloor}.

Proof.

Since
S
is Sidon
1_{S}*1_{-S}(x)=1_{S-S}
if
x\neq 0
. In addition
1_{S}*1_{-S}(0)=|S|
, but this does not require
S
to be Sidon. Hence

(2.2)

\sum_{h\in(S-S)\setminus\mathopen{}\left\{0\mathclose{}\right\}}\mu_{H}(h)=\sum_{h}1_{S}*1_{-S}(h)\mu_{H}(h)-\frac{|S|}{\mathopen{}\left\lfloor H\right\rfloor}.

Using (
1.8
) and expanding convolutions we have

\sum_{h}1_{S}*1_{-S}(h)\mu_{H}(h)=\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\left\lfloor H\right\rfloor^{2}}\sum_{h}1_{S}*1_{-S}(h)1_{[H]}*1_{-[H]}(h)\\
=\frac{1}{\mathopen{}\left\lfloor H\right\rfloor^{2}}\sum_{h}1_{S}*1_{[H]}(h)^{2}.

We may now apply Cauchy-Schwarz to this last sum, giving us

\sum_{h}1_{S}*1_{-S}(h)\mu_{H}(h)\geqslant\frac{1}{(N+H)\mathopen{}\left\lfloor H\right\rfloor^{2}}\left(\sum_{h}1_{S}*1_{[H]}(h)\right)^{2}=\frac{|S|^{2}}{(N+H)},

which gives the claimed inequality.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Theorem
1.2
.
Our use of van der Corput’s inequality is reminiscent of
the moving averages argument used by Erdős and Turán
[
10
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let
f_{1}:=1_{S}
,
f_{2}:=\frac{|S|}{N}1_{[N]}
and
f:=f_{1}-f_{2}
. Applying van der Corput’s inequality
(
2.1
) to
\hat{f}
, with
1\leqslant H\leqslant N
to be determined, we obtain

|\hat{f}(\alpha)|^{2}\leqslant(N+H)\sum_{h}\mu_{H}(h)\\
\times\left|\sum_{x}\bigl[f_{1}(x)f_{1}(x+h)-f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x+h)-f_{2}(x)f_{1}(x+h)+f_{2}(x)f_{2}(x+h)\bigr]\right|.

We claim that
\sum_{h}\mu_{H}(h)||S|^{2}N^{-1}-\sum_{x}f_{i}(x)f_{j}(x+h)|
is
small for all choices of
i,j
, so that main terms cancel and we are left only with error
terms.

Since
f_{2}(x)f_{2}(x+h)=|S|^{2}N^{-2}1_{[N]\cap([N]-h)}(x)
we have

\sum_{h}\mu_{H}(h)\left||S|^{2}N^{-1}-\sum_{x}f_{2}(x)f_{2}(x+h)\right|=\frac{|S|^{2}}{N^{2}}\sum_{h}\mu_{H}(h)|h|\leqslant\frac{H|S|^{2}}{N^{2}}.

We have the identity
f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x+h)=|S|N^{-1}1_{S\cap([N]-h)}(x)=|S|N^{-1}[1_{S}(x)-1_{S\cap I_{h}}(x)]
, where
I_{h}\subset[N]
is an interval of
|h|
integers. Since
S
is a Sidon set, the bound (
1.1
) gives that

(2.3)

|S\cap I_{h}|\ll\sqrt{|h|}.

Hence

\sum_{h}\mu_{H}(h)\left||S|^{2}N^{-1}-\sum_{x}f_{1}(x)f_{2}(x+h)\right|\ll\sum_{h}\mu_{H}(h)|S|N^{-1}|h|^{1/2}\\
\ll\frac{|S|H^{1/2}}{N}.

By symmetry, the same bound applies to the term involving
f_{2}(x)f_{1}(x+h)
.

Note that

f_{1}(x)f_{1}(x+h)=1_{S\cap(S-h)}(x)
. On account of
S

being Sidon, for
h\neq 0
we have

\begin{split}\mathopen{}\left||S|^{2}N^{-1}-\sum_{x}f_{1}(x)f_{1}(x+h)\mathclose{}\right|&\leqslant 1-1_{S-S}(h)+\mathopen{}\left||S|^{2}-N\mathclose{}\right|N^{-1}\\
&=1-1_{S-S}(h)+O\mathopen{}\left(\mathopen{}\left||S|-N^{1/2}\mathclose{}\right|N^{-1/2}\mathclose{}\right).\end{split}

Thus by Lemma
2.2
we obtain

\sum_{h}\mu_{H}(h)\left||S|^{2}N^{-1}-\sum_{x}f_{1}(x)f_{1}(x+h)\right|\leqslant 1-\frac{|S|^{2}}{N+H}\\
+O\mathopen{}\left(\frac{|S|}{\mathopen{}\left\lfloor H\right\rfloor}+\frac{\mathopen{}\left||S|-N^{1/2}\mathclose{}\right|}{N^{1/2}}\mathclose{}\right).

Putting everything together, we deduce that

|\hat{f}(\alpha)|^{2}\ll N^{1/2}\mathopen{}\left||S|-N^{1/2}\mathclose{}\right|+\frac{N^{3/2}}{\mathopen{}\left\lfloor H\right\rfloor}+N^{1/2}H^{1/2}.

Balancing error terms, we set
H:=N^{2/3}
which gives

|\hat{f}(\alpha)|^{2}\ll N^{1/2}\mathopen{}\left||S|-N^{1/2}\mathclose{}\right|+N^{5/6}.\qed

Equidistribution in progressions

In this section we derive Corollaries

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
S\subset[N]
be a Sidon set and
P\subset\mathbb{Z}

a finite arithmetic progression. Then

|S\cap P|=\frac{|[N]\cap P||S|}{N}+O_{\varepsilon}\mathopen{}\left(N^{1/2+\varepsilon}\mathopen{}\left(\mathopen{}\left|\tfrac{|S|}{N^{1/2}}-1\mathclose{}\right|+N^{-1/6}\mathclose{}\right)^{1/2}\mathclose{}\right).

Theorem
3.1
follows from Theorem
1.2
by the Erdős-Turán inequality (see, for example,
[
18
, Corollary 1.1]
). We prove a cheap version of this inequality that suffices for our purposes. We begin with a standard estimate for the
L^{1}
norm of the Fourier transform of a progression.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
P\subset\mathbb{Z}
be an arithmetic progression. Then

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|\hat{1}_{P}(\alpha)\right|\mathrm{d}\alpha\ll\log(|P|).

Proof.

With a suitable change of variables, we may assume that
P=\{1,\dots,|P|\}
. Let us first prove a pointwise bound. Summing the
geometric series gives

\left|\hat{1}_{P}(\alpha)\right|\ll\frac{1}{|1-e(\alpha)|}=\frac{1}{|e(-\alpha/2)-e(\alpha/2)|}=\frac{1}{|2\sin(\pi\alpha)|}\ll\frac{1}{\|\alpha\|},

where we have used that
2|\alpha|\leqslant|\sin(\pi\alpha)|
for
\alpha\in[-1/2,1/2]
. Thus,

\left|\hat{1}_{P}(\alpha)\right|\ll\min\left(|P|,\frac{1}{\|\alpha\|}\right).

We now use dyadic decomposition to complete the proof. Let

A_{k}=\left\{\alpha\in\mathbb{T}\colon\frac{2^{k-1}}{|P|}\leqslant\|\alpha\|\leqslant\frac{2^{k}}{|P|}\right\}

for
k=\left\{1,\dots,\lceil\log_{2}(|P|)\rceil\right\}
and
A_{0}=\left\{\alpha\in\mathbb{T}\colon\|\alpha\|\leqslant 1/|P|\right\}
. For
k\geqslant 1
we have

\int_{A_{k}}\left|\hat{1}_{P}(\alpha)\right|\mathrm{d}\alpha\leqslant\int_{A_{k}}\frac{1}{\|\alpha\|}\mathrm{d}\alpha\leqslant\frac{|P|\mathrm{meas}(A_{k})}{2^{k-1}}\ll 1.

On the other hand,

\int_{A_{0}}\left|\hat{1}_{P}(\alpha)\right|\mathrm{d}\alpha\ll|A_{0}||P|\ll 1.

Since
\mathbb{T}\subset\bigcup_{k}A_{k}
, the claimed bound follows.
∎

We are now able to deduce Theorem
3.1
from Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Using orthogonality we have

\displaystyle|S\cap P|-\tfrac{|[N]\cap P||S|}{N}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}1_{P\cap[N]}(x)\mathopen{}\left(1_{S}(x)-\tfrac{|S|}{N}1_{[N]}(x)\mathclose{}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hat{1}_{P\cap[N]}(\alpha)\mathopen{}\left(\hat{1}_{S}-\tfrac{|S|}{N}\hat{1}_{[N]}\mathclose{}\right)(-\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha.

The result follows from Lemma
3.2
and
the Fourier uniformity obtained in Theorem
1.2
.
∎

4.
Equidistribution in Bohr neighbourhoods

One can prove Corollaries
1.7
and
1.11
using Erdős–Turán type arguments, see for instance
[
18
, Chapter 1]
. We opt for the following cruder trigonometric approximation, a proof of which can be found in Appendix
B
.

Lemma 4.1

(Trigonometric approximation)
.

Let
F:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to[0,1]
have Lipschitz constant
K\geqslant 1
with respect to the metric

(4.1)

\max_{j}\mathopen{}\left\|\alpha_{j}-\beta_{j}\mathclose{}\right\|_{\mathbb{T}}.

For any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a trigonometric polynomial
F_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to[0,1]
with
\mathopen{}\left\|F-F_{\varepsilon}\mathclose{}\right\|_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon
and such that

F_{\varepsilon}(\alpha)=\sum_{|m_{i}|\leqslant M}\hat{F_{\varepsilon}}(m)e(m\cdot\alpha)

with
M\ll K^{2}\varepsilon^{-3}
.

Deduction of Corollary
1.7
.

Let
F:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to[0,1]
be a 1-bounded function with Lipschitz constant
K\geqslant 1
with respect to the metric (
4.1
).
We apply Lemma
4.1
to obtain a trigonometric approximation
F_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to[0,1]
, with
\varepsilon>0
to be determined.

Expanding
F_{\varepsilon}
in terms of its Fourier coefficients gives

\sum_{x\in S}F_{\varepsilon}(\alpha x)=\sum_{|m_{i}|\leqslant M}\hat{F_{\varepsilon}}(m)\sum_{x\in S}e(m\cdot\alpha x).

Approximating
\sum_{x\in S}e(m\cdot\alpha x)
with
\sum_{x\in[N]}e(m\cdot\alpha x)
, we deduce that there exists an absolute constant
C
such that

\biggl|\sum_{x\in S}F_{\varepsilon}(\alpha x)-\tfrac{|S|}{N}\sum_{x\in[N]}F_{\varepsilon}(\alpha x)\biggr|\leqslant(CK^{2}/\varepsilon^{-3})^{d}\big\|\hat{1}_{S}-\tfrac{|S|}{N}\hat{1}_{N}\big\|_{\infty}.

Balancing the above error term with
\varepsilon|S|
, we take

\varepsilon:=\mathopen{}\left(|S|^{-1}\mathopen{}\left(CK^{2}\mathclose{}\right)^{d}\big\|\hat{1}_{S}-\tfrac{|S|}{N}\hat{1}_{N}\big\|_{\infty}\mathclose{}\right)^{\frac{1}{3d+1}}

to yield the asymptotic

\mathopen{}\left|\mathbb{E}_{x\in S}F(\alpha x)-\mathbb{E}_{x\in[N]}F(\alpha x)\mathclose{}\right|\\
\ll\mathopen{}\left(K^{2d}\mathopen{}\left(\mathopen{}\left|\tfrac{|S|}{N^{1/2}}-1\mathclose{}\right|+N^{-1/6}\mathclose{}\right)^{1/2}\mathclose{}\right)^{\frac{1}{3d+1}},

on employing Theorem
1.2
.
∎

Deduction of Corollary
1.11
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be a small quantity to be determined. Let
F_{1}:\mathbb{T}\to[0,1]
be a piecewise linear function with
F_{1}(\alpha)=1
on
[-\rho,\rho]
,
F_{1}(\alpha)=0
on
\mathbb{T}\setminus[-\rho-\varepsilon,\rho+\varepsilon]
and with Lipschitz constant at most
\varepsilon^{-1}
. By a telescoping identity, the function
F(\alpha):=F_{1}(\alpha_{1})\dotsm F_{1}(\alpha_{d})
has Lipschitz constant at most
d\varepsilon^{-1}
. Hence by Corollary
1.7
we have

\mathbb{E}_{x\in S}1_{B}(x)\leqslant\mathbb{E}_{x\in S}F\mathopen{}\left(\alpha x\mathclose{}\right)\\
=\mathbb{E}_{x\in[N]}F\mathopen{}\left(\alpha x\mathclose{}\right)+O\mathopen{}\left((d/\varepsilon)^{\frac{2}{3}}\mathopen{}\left(\mathopen{}\left|\tfrac{|S|}{N^{1/2}}-1\mathclose{}\right|+N^{-1/6}\mathclose{}\right)^{\frac{1}{8d}}\mathclose{}\right).

Since
F
is supported on
B(\alpha,\rho+\varepsilon)
, regularity (Definition
1.9
) ensures that

\sum_{x\in[N]}F\mathopen{}\left(\alpha x\mathclose{}\right)\leqslant|B|+O\mathopen{}\left(d\rho^{-1}\varepsilon N\mathclose{}\right).

Therefore

\mathbb{E}_{x\in S}1_{B}(x)\leqslant\tfrac{|B|}{N}\\
+O\mathopen{}\left(d\rho^{-1}\varepsilon+(d/\varepsilon)^{\frac{2}{3}}\mathopen{}\left(\mathopen{}\left|\tfrac{|S|}{N^{1/2}}-1\mathclose{}\right|+N^{-1/6}\mathclose{}\right)^{\frac{1}{8d}}\mathclose{}\right).

Choosing
\varepsilon
to balance error terms, then bounding exponents somewhat crudely, we obtain

\mathbb{E}_{x\in S}1_{B}(x)\leqslant\tfrac{|B|}{N}\\
+O\mathopen{}\left(d\rho^{-1}\mathopen{}\left(\mathopen{}\left|\tfrac{|S|}{N^{1/2}}-1\mathclose{}\right|+N^{-1/6}\mathclose{}\right)^{\frac{1}{14d}}\mathclose{}\right).

The corresponding lower bound is proved analogously.
∎

5.
Partition regularity

Our deduction of Theorem
1.15
from Theorem
1.2
requires two additional results, the first being the following transference principle for colourings, a proof of which can be found in Appendix
C
.

Lemma 5.1

(Dense model lemma)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\leqslant 1
and
\nu:[N]\to[0,\infty)
be such that there exist functions
f_{i}:[N]\to[0,\infty)
with
f_{1}+\dots+f_{r}\leqslant\nu
and such that for any
g_{i}:[N]\to[0,\infty)
satisfying
g_{1}+\dots+g_{r}\leqslant 1_{[N]}
there exists
i
with

\big\|\hat{f}_{i}-\hat{g}_{i}\big\|_{\infty}>\varepsilon N.

Then

(5.1)

\big\|\hat{\nu}-\hat{1}_{[N]}\big\|_{\infty}\gg_{\varepsilon,r}N.

The second additional result underlying Theorem
1.15
is a lower bound on the number of monochromatic solutions to a partition regular equation in an interval.

Lemma 5.2

(Counting monochromatic solutions in an interval of integers)
.

Let
c_{1}
, …,
c_{s}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\mathopen{}\left\{0\mathclose{}\right\}
and suppose that there is a non-empty index set
I\subset[s]
satisfying
\sum_{i\in I}c_{i}=0
. Then for any functions
g_{1},\dots,g_{r}:[N]\to[0,\infty)
with
1_{[N]}=g_{1}+\dots+g_{r}
, either
N\ll_{c_{1},\dots,c_{s},r}1
or there exists
g_{j}
satisfying

(5.2)

\sum_{c_{1}x_{1}+\dots+c_{s}x_{s}=0}g_{j}(x_{1})\dotsm g_{j}(x_{s})\gg_{c_{1},\dots,c_{s},r}N^{s-1}.

Proof.

When each
g_{i}
is a characteristic function of a set
C_{i}\subset[N]
, the result is a special case of Frankl, Graham and Rödl
[
11
, Theorem 1]
. In general, for each
x\in[N]
fix an index
i=i(x)
such that
g_{i}(x)\geqslant 1/r
. At least one such index exists by the pigeon-hole principle. On setting
C_{i}=\mathopen{}\left\{x\in[N]:i(x)=i\mathclose{}\right\}
we obtain a colouring, and the lower bound (
5.2
) follows on employing
[
11
, Theorem 1]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.15
.

Let
S\subset[N]
be a Sidon set and
S=C_{1}\cup\dots\cup C_{r}
. Writing
\delta=\delta_{c_{1},\dots,c_{s},r}>0
for the implicit constant in (
5.2
), suppose that

(5.3)

\sum_{c_{1}x_{1}+\dots+c_{s}x_{s}=0}1_{C_{j}}(x_{1})\dotsm 1_{C_{j}}(x_{s})\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}\delta|S|^{s}N^{-1}\qquad(1\leqslant j\leqslant r).

Define
f_{i}:=N|S|^{-1}1_{C_{i}}
, so that
f_{1}+\dots+f_{r}=N|S|^{-1}1_{S}
. Then, by Lemma
5.2
, we either have
N\ll_{c_{1},\dots,c_{s},r}1
, or for any functions
g_{j}\geqslant 0
with
g_{1}+\dots+g_{r}=1_{[N]}
there exists
g_{j}
such that

(5.4)

\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{c_{1}x_{1}+\dots+c_{s}x_{s}=0}\mathopen{}\left[g_{j}(x_{1})\dotsm g_{j}(x_{s})-f_{j}(x_{1})\dotsm f_{j}(x_{s})\mathclose{}\right]\mathclose{}\right|\gg_{c_{1},\dots,c_{s},r}N^{s-1}.

Given (
5.4
), by a telescoping identity, there exists
h_{1},\dots,h_{s}\in\mathopen{}\left\{g_{j},f_{j},f_{j}-g_{j}\mathclose{}\right\}
, exactly one of which is equal to
f_{j}-g_{j}
, and such that

(5.5)

\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{c_{1}x_{1}+\dots+c_{s}x_{s}=0}\mathopen{}\left[g_{j}(x_{1})\dotsm g_{j}(x_{s})-f_{j}(x_{1})\dotsm f_{j}(x_{s})\mathclose{}\right]\mathclose{}\right|\\
\ll_{s}\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{c_{1}x_{1}+\dots+c_{s}x_{s}=0}h_{1}(x_{1})\dotsm h_{s}(x_{s})\mathclose{}\right|.

By orthogonality and Hölder’s inequality

(5.6)

\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{c_{1}x_{1}+\dots+c_{s}x_{s}=0}h_{1}(x_{1})\dotsm h_{s}(x_{s})\mathclose{}\right|=\mathopen{}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}}\hat{h}_{1}(c_{1}\alpha)\dotsm\hat{h}_{s}(c_{s}\alpha)\mathrm{d}\alpha\mathclose{}\right|\\
\leqslant\big\|\hat{f}_{j}-\hat{g}_{j}\big\|_{\infty}\max\mathopen{}\left\{\big\|\hat{f}_{j}\big\|_{s-1},\big\|\hat{g}_{j}\big\|_{s-1}\mathclose{}\right\}^{s-1}.

Since
s-1\geqslant 2
and
0\leqslant g_{j}\leqslant 1_{[N]}
, Parseval’s identity gives that

\big\|\hat{g}_{j}\big\|_{s-1}^{s-1}\leqslant N^{s-2}.

Since
s-1\geqslant 4
and
0\leqslant f_{j}\leqslant N|S|^{-1}1_{S}
, orthogonality and the Sidon property give that

\big\|\hat{f}_{j}\big\|_{s-1}^{s-1}\leqslant N^{s-1}|S|^{-4}\sum_{x-x^{\prime}=y-y^{\prime}}1_{S}(x)1_{S}(x^{\prime})1_{S}(y)1_{S}(y^{\prime})\leqslant 2N^{s-1}|S|^{-2}.

Supposing that
|S|\geqslant\frac{1}{100}N^{1/2}
, the latter quantity is
O(N^{s-2})
. We may assume that
|S|\geqslant\frac{1}{100}N^{1/2}
for otherwise
||S|-N^{1/2}|\gg N^{1/2}
.

From the above deliberations, we conclude that if (
5.3
) holds, then either
N\ll_{c_{1},\dots,c_{s},r}1
, or
||S|-N^{1/2}|\gg N^{1/2}
, or for any
g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\geqslant 0
with
g_{1}+\dots+g_{r}=1_{[N]}
there exists
g_{j}
such that

(5.7)

\big\|\hat{f}_{j}-\hat{g}_{j}\big\|_{\infty}\gg_{c_{1},\dots,c_{s},r}N.

Henceforth we assume that we are not in the situation that
N\ll_{c_{1},\dots,c_{s},r}1
or
||S|-N^{1/2}|\gg N^{1/2}
. Let
\eta=\eta(c_{1},\dots,c_{r},r)>0
denote the implicit constant in (
5.7
). If it is the case that there exists
g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\geqslant 0
with
g_{1}+\dots+g_{r}=1_{[N]}
such that for all
1\leqslant j\leqslant r-1
we have

\big\|\hat{f}_{j}-\hat{g}_{j}\big\|_{\infty}\leqslant\tfrac{\eta}{2r}N,

then (
5.7
) holds with

\big\|N|S|^{-1}\hat{1}_{S}-\hat{1}_{[N]}\big\|_{\infty}\geqslant\tfrac{1}{2}\eta N\gg_{c_{1},\dots,c_{s},r}N.

Let us show that this conclusion also holds when for any
g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\geqslant 0
with
g_{1}+\dots+g_{r}=1_{[N]}
there exists
1\leqslant j\leqslant r-1
such that

\big\|\hat{f}_{j}-\hat{g}_{j}\big\|_{\infty}>\tfrac{\eta}{2r}N.

Since

\mathopen{}\left\{(g_{1},\dots,g_{r}):g_{1}+\dots+g_{r}=1_{[N]}\text{ and }g_{i}\geqslant 0\text{ for all }i\mathclose{}\right\}\\
=\mathopen{}\left\{(g_{1},\dots,g_{r-1},1_{[N]}-g_{r}):g_{1}+\dots+g_{r-1}\leqslant 1_{[N]}\text{ and }g_{i}\geqslant 0\text{ for all }i\mathclose{}\right\},

we may apply the dense model lemma (Lemma
5.1

\big\|N|S|^{-1}\hat{1}_{S}-\hat{1}_{[N]}\big\|_{\infty}\gg_{c_{1},\dots,c_{s},r}N.

Hence by Theorem
1.2
we have

(5.8)

N^{1/4}\mathopen{}\left||S|-N^{1/2}\mathclose{}\right|^{1/2}+N^{5/12}\gg_{c_{1},\dots,c_{s},r}|S|.

Again assuming
|S|\geqslant\tfrac{1}{100}N^{1/2}
(as we may), (
5.8
) implies that either
N\ll_{c_{1},\dots,c_{s},r}1
or
\mathopen{}\left||S|-N^{1/2}\mathclose{}\right|\gg_{c_{1},\dots,c_{s},r}N^{1/2}

6.
Improving Fourier uniformity

In previous sections we have seen that the quality of Fourier uniformity dictates the level of equidistribution of an extremal Sidon set. In this section we give a modified proof of Theorem
1.2
with an increased power saving in the quality of Fourier uniformity. This is accomplished by incorporating an estimate of Cilleruelo
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
on the level of equidistribution in short intervals.

Theorem 6.1

(Cilleruelo)
.

Let
S\subset[N]
be a Sidon set and

I\subset[N]
an interval. Then

\mathopen{}\left||S\cap I|-\frac{|I||S|}{N}\mathclose{}\right|\ll\mathopen{}\left(N^{1/4}+|I|^{1/2}N^{-1/8}\mathclose{}\right)\mathopen{}\left(1+\mathopen{}\left(1-\tfrac{|S|}{N^{1/2}}\mathclose{}\right)_{+}^{1/2}N^{1/8}\mathclose{}\right),

where
x_{+}=\max(0,x)
.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
S\subset[N]
be a Sidon set and

I\subset[N]
an interval with
|I|\leqslant N^{3/4}
. Then

|S\cap I|\ll N^{1/4}+\mathopen{}\left|1-\tfrac{|S|}{N^{1/2}}\mathclose{}\right|^{1/2}N^{3/8}.

Using this result we can refine the bounds obtained in
Theorem
1.2
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
S\subset[N]
be a Sidon set. Then

(6.1)

\left\|\hat{1}_{S}-\tfrac{|S|}{N}\hat{1}_{[N]}\right\|_{\infty}\ll N^{1/2}\mathopen{}\left(\mathopen{}\left|1-\tfrac{|S|}{N^{1/2}}\mathclose{}\right|+N^{-1/4}\mathclose{}\right)^{1/2}.

Proof.

The proof is identical to that given for Theorem

1.2
, albeit taking
H:=N^{3/4}
and replacing our use of (
1.1
) in (
2.3
) with Corollary
6.2
. This gives

|S\cap I_{h}|\ll N^{1/4}+\mathopen{}\left|1-\tfrac{|S|}{N^{1/2}}\mathclose{}\right|^{1/2}N^{3/8}.

Hence when
i\neq j
we have

\sum_{h}\mu_{H}(h)\left||S|^{2}N^{-1}-\sum_{x}f_{i}(x)f_{j}(x+h)\right|\\
\ll|S|\mathopen{}\left(N^{-3/4}+\mathopen{}\left|1-\tfrac{|S|}{N^{1/2}}\mathclose{}\right|^{1/2}N^{-5/8}\mathclose{}\right).

Putting everything together, as in the proof of Theorem
1.2
, then gives the desired bound.
∎

Remark 6.4
.

This second version of our Fourier uniformity bound
solves a quirk of the previous proof, where we took
H=N^{2/3}
,
whereas in many results on extremal Sidon sets taking
H=N^{3/4}
appears naturally.

Of course, this new version cannot be used to improve the bounds on uniform
distribution in intervals, since it would give a circular argument. However, it
may be applied to improve our bounds on distribution in residue classes.

Appendix A
The size of a Sidon set

In this appendix we establish the well-known bound (
1.1
), again
using van der Corput’s variant of the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality.
Let
S\subset(n,n+N]
be a Sidon set and
H
be a positive integer (to be determined). Applying
(
2.1
) to
\sum_{x}1_{S}(x)
we obtain

|S|^{2}\leqslant\mathopen{}\left(N+\mathopen{}\left\lfloor H\right\rfloor\mathclose{}\right)\sum_{h}\mu_{H}(h)\sum_{x}1_{S}(x)1_{S}(x+h).

Using the defining property of Sidon sets, and the fact that
\mu_{H}
is a probability measure, we deduce that

|S|^{2}\leqslant\mathopen{}\left(N+H\mathclose{}\right)\mathopen{}\left(\frac{|S|}{H}+1\mathclose{}\right).

By the quadratic formula
x^{2}\leqslant bx+c
only if
x\leqslant(b+\sqrt{b^{2}+4c})/2
, which in turn implies that
x\leqslant b+\sqrt{c}
.
Hence

|S|\leqslant\sqrt{N+H}+(N+H)H^{-1}\leqslant N^{1/2}+HN^{-1/2}+NH^{-1}+1.

The bound (
1.1
) follows on taking, say,
H=\mathopen{}\left\lceil N^{3/4}\mathclose{}\right\rceil
.

Appendix B
Trigonometric approximation

Definition B.1
.

Given an integrable function
F\colon\mathbb{T}^{d}\to[0,1]
, we define its Fourier
transform to be the function
\hat{F}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
given by

\hat{F}(m)=\int_{\alpha\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}F(\alpha)e(-m\cdot\alpha),

where
m\cdot\alpha=m_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+m_{d}\alpha_{d}
.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Let
\lambda_{M}(\alpha)=\lambda_{M}(\alpha_{1})\dotsm\lambda_{M}(\alpha_{d})
denote the following renormalised Fourier transform of the Féjer kernel:

\sum_{m}\mathopen{}\left(1-\frac{|m_{1}|}{M}\mathclose{}\right)_{+}\dotsm\mathopen{}\left(1-\frac{|m_{d}|}{M}\mathclose{}\right)_{+}e(m\cdot\alpha)\\
=M^{-d}\mathopen{}\left(1_{[M]^{d}}*1_{-[M]^{d}}\mathclose{}\right)\hat{\ }(\alpha)=M^{-d}\mathopen{}\left|\hat{1}_{[M]^{d}}(\alpha)\mathclose{}\right|^{2}.

Set

F_{M}(\alpha):=F*\lambda_{M}(\alpha)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}F(\alpha-\beta)\lambda_{M}(\beta)\mathrm{d}\beta.

One can check that

F*\lambda_{M}(\alpha)=\sum_{m}\mathopen{}\left(1-\frac{|m_{1}|}{M}\mathclose{}\right)_{+}\dotsm\mathopen{}\left(1-\frac{|m_{d}|}{M}\mathclose{}\right)_{+}\hat{F}(m)e(m\cdot\alpha).

We utilise the following three properties of the Féjer kernel.

(a)

(Non-negativity)
\lambda_{M}\geqslant 0
.

(b)

(Mass one)
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\lambda_{M}=1
.

(c)

(Quantitative decay)
\lambda_{M}(\alpha)\leqslant M^{-1}\mathopen{}\left\|\alpha_{j}\mathclose{}\right\|^{-2}\prod_{i\neq j}\lambda_{M}(\alpha_{i})
.

The first two facts ensure that
0\leqslant F_{M}\leqslant 1
, since
0\leqslant F\leqslant 1
. Let us estimate the error
\mathopen{}\left\|F-F*\lambda_{M}\mathclose{}\right\|_{\infty}
. By definition of convolution

\begin{split}F(\alpha)-F*\lambda_{M}(\alpha)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathopen{}\left(F(\alpha)-F(\alpha-\beta)\mathclose{}\right)\lambda_{M}(\beta)\mathrm{d}\beta.\end{split}

Since the Féjer kernel is non-negative and has integral 1, the Lipschitz continuity of
F
gives that

\mathopen{}\left|\int_{\max_{i}|\beta_{i}|\leqslant\eta}\bigl(F(\alpha)-F(\alpha-\beta)\bigr)\lambda_{M}(\beta)\mathrm{d}\beta\mathclose{}\right|\leqslant K\eta.

By the quantitative decay estimate

\mathopen{}\left|\int_{\max_{i}|\beta_{i}|>\eta}\bigl(F(\alpha)-F(\alpha-\beta)\bigr)\lambda_{M}(\beta)\mathrm{d}\beta\mathclose{}\right|\\
\leqslant\frac{2}{M\eta^{2}}.

Taking
\eta^{3}=1/(KM)
then gives

\mathopen{}\left\|F-F*\lambda_{M}\mathclose{}\right\|_{\infty}\leqslant 3K^{2/3}M^{-1/3}.

Setting
M=\mathopen{}\left\lceil 27K^{2}\varepsilon^{-3}\mathclose{}\right\rceil
we have

\mathopen{}\left\|F-F*\lambda_{M}\mathclose{}\right\|_{\infty}\leqslant\varepsilon.

We note that we may assume that
\varepsilon\leqslant 1
, so that
M\ll K^{2}\varepsilon^{-3}
, for otherwise the result is immediate on taking
F_{\varepsilon}=0
and
M=0
.
∎

Appendix C
A dense model lemma

Lemma C.1

(Separating hyperplane theorem)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be closed and convex and
v\notin K
. Then there exists
\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that for all
u\in K
we have
v\cdot\phi>u\cdot\phi
.

Proof.

See

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperplane_separation_theorem
.∎

Lemma C.2
.

For
f,\phi:[N]\to\mathbb{R}
write

\mathopen{}\left\|f\mathclose{}\right\|:=\big\|\hat{f}\big\|_{\infty}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathopen{}\left\|\phi\mathclose{}\right\|^{*}:=\sup_{\mathopen{}\left\|f\mathclose{}\right\|\leqslant 1}\biggl|\sum_{x}f(x)\phi(x)\biggr|.

Then for any
f,\phi,\psi:[N]\to\mathbb{R}
we have

•

\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{x}f(x)\phi(x)\mathclose{}\right|\leqslant\mathopen{}\left\|f\mathclose{}\right\|\mathopen{}\left\|\phi\mathclose{}\right\|^{*}
;

•

\mathopen{}\left\|\phi\psi\mathclose{}\right\|^{*}\leqslant\mathopen{}\left\|\phi\mathclose{}\right\|^{*}\mathopen{}\left\|\psi\mathclose{}\right\|^{*}
;

•

\mathopen{}\left\|\phi\mathclose{}\right\|_{\infty}\leqslant\mathopen{}\left\|\phi\mathclose{}\right\|^{*}
.

Proof.

The first inequality follows from the definition of
\mathopen{}\left\|\cdot\mathclose{}\right\|^{*}
.

Let
e_{\alpha}
denote the map
x\mapsto e(\alpha x)
. Then
\mathopen{}\left\|\cdot\mathclose{}\right\|
is invariant under multiplication by
e_{\alpha}
, so for any
f,\phi:[N]\to\mathbb{R}
the first inequality gives that

\mathopen{}\left|\widehat{f\phi}(\alpha)\mathclose{}\right|=\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{x}f(x)e_{\alpha}(x)\phi(x)\mathclose{}\right|\leqslant\mathopen{}\left\|fe_{\alpha}\mathclose{}\right\|\mathopen{}\left\|\phi\mathclose{}\right\|^{*}=\mathopen{}\left\|f\mathclose{}\right\|\mathopen{}\left\|\phi\mathclose{}\right\|^{*}.

Hence

\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{x}f(x)\phi(x)\psi(x)\mathclose{}\right|\leqslant\mathopen{}\left\|f\phi\mathclose{}\right\|\mathopen{}\left\|\psi\mathclose{}\right\|^{*}\leqslant\mathopen{}\left\|f\mathclose{}\right\|\mathopen{}\left\|\phi\mathclose{}\right\|^{*}\mathopen{}\left\|\psi\mathclose{}\right\|^{*}.

The second inequality follows.

Suppose that
\mathopen{}\left\|\phi\mathclose{}\right\|_{\infty}=1
, so that
|\phi(x)|=1
for some
x\in[N]
. Notice that the function
f:=1_{\mathopen{}\left\{x\mathclose{}\right\}}
has Fourier transform bounded in magnitude by
1
. Therefore

\mathopen{}\left\|\phi\mathclose{}\right\|^{*}\geqslant\mathopen{}\left|\sum_{y}f(y)\phi(y)\mathclose{}\right|=1=\mathopen{}\left\|\phi\mathclose{}\right\|_{\infty}.

The third inequality then follows on renormalising.
∎

Proof of Lemma
5.1
.

We closely follow Conlon and Gowers
[
5

. Notice that

(1+\tfrac{\varepsilon}{2})^{-1}(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})

is not a member of the closed convex set

\mathopen{}\left\{(g_{1}+h_{1},\dots,g_{r}+h_{r}):g_{i}\geqslant 0,\ g_{1}+\dots+g_{r}\leqslant 1_{[N]},\ \big\|\hat{h}_{i}\big\|_{\infty}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{4}\varepsilon N\mathclose{}\right\}.

Hence by the separating hyperplane theorem, there exists
(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{r})
such that for any
g_{i}\geqslant 0
with
g_{1}+\dots+g_{r}\leqslant 1_{[N]}
and
\big\|\hat{h}_{i}\big\|_{\infty}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{4}\varepsilon N
we have

(C.1)

\mathopen{}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon\mathclose{}\right)^{-1}\sum_{i}\sum_{x}f_{i}(x)\phi_{i}(x)>\sum_{i}\sum_{x}g_{i}(x)\phi_{i}(x)+\sum_{i}\sum_{x}h_{i}(x)\phi_{i}(x).

Taking all

and
h_{i}
zero shows that the left-hand side of (

(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{r})
to give

(C.2)

\sum_{i}\sum_{x}f_{i}(x)\phi_{i}(x)=(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon)N

and for all
g_{i}\geqslant 0
with
g_{1}+\dots+g_{r}\leqslant 1_{[N]}
and
\big\|\hat{h}_{i}\big\|_{\infty}\leqslant\tfrac{1}{4}\varepsilon N
we have

(C.3)

\sum_{i}\sum_{x}g_{i}(x)\phi_{i}(x)+\sum_{i}\sum_{x}h_{i}(x)\phi_{i}(x)<N.

Notice that

(C.4)

\sum_{i}\sum_{x}f_{i}(x)\phi_{i}(x)\leqslant\sum_{i}\sum_{x}f_{i}(x)\max\mathopen{}\left\{\phi_{1}(x),\dots,\phi_{r}(x),0\mathclose{}\right\}\\
\leqslant\sum_{x}\nu(x)\max\mathopen{}\left\{\phi_{1}(x),\dots,\phi_{r}(x),0\mathclose{}\right\}.

For each
x\in[N]
fix
i(x)\in[r]
such that

\max\mathopen{}\left\{\phi_{1}(x),\dots,\phi_{r}(x)\mathclose{}\right\}=\phi_{i(x)}(x).

Define

g_{i}(x):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }i=i(x)\text{ and }\phi_{i}(x)\geqslant 0,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

By substituting this function into (
C.3
) with
h_{i}=0
, and using both (
C.2
) and (
C.4
), we deduce that

\sum_{x}\mathopen{}\left[\nu(x)-1_{[N]}(x)\mathclose{}\right]\max\mathopen{}\left\{\phi_{1}(x),\dots,\phi_{r}(x),0\mathclose{}\right\}>\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon N.

Using the notation and content of Lemma
C.2
, the inequality gives that

(C.5)

\mathopen{}\left\|\phi_{i}\mathclose{}\right\|_{\infty}\leqslant\mathopen{}\left\|\phi_{i}\mathclose{}\right\|^{*}\leqslant 4/\varepsilon.

By the Stone--Weierstrass theorem

\mathopen{}\left|\max\mathopen{}\left\{x_{1},\dots,x_{r},0\mathclose{}\right\}-P_{\varepsilon}(x_{1},\dots,x_{s})\mathclose{}\right|\leqslant\varepsilon/100.

Notice that we may assume that

, otherwise we are done. Hence

\sum_{x}\mathopen{}\left[\nu(x)-1_{[N]}(x)\mathclose{}\right]P_{\varepsilon}\mathopen{}\left(\phi_{1}(x),\dots,\phi_{r}(x)\mathclose{}\right)>\tfrac{1}{4}\varepsilon N.

Expanding the polynomial, and applying the pigeon-hole principle, there exist

Recalling (

\mathopen{}\left\|\psi_{1}\dotsm\psi_{R}\mathclose{}\right\|^{*}\ll_{\varepsilon,r}1.

Hence, again applying the first inequality in Lemma

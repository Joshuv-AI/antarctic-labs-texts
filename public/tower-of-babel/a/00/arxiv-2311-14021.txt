1.
The greedy algorithm

Let
h
be a positive integer.
A finite or infinite set
A
of nonnegative integers is a
B_{h}
-set if no integer has two different
representations as sums of
h
elements of
A
.
Equivalently, the set
A
is a
B_{h}
-set if the equation

a_{i_{1}}+\cdots+a_{i_{h}}=a_{j_{1}}+\cdots+a_{j_{h}}

with
a_{i_{1}},\ldots,a_{i_{h}},a_{j_{1}},\ldots,a_{j_{h}}\in A

a_{i_{1}}\leq\cdots\leq a_{i_{h}}

and

a_{j_{1}}\leq\cdots\leq a_{j_{h}}

implies
a_{i_{r}}=a_{j_{r}}
for all
r=1,\ldots,h
.
A
B_{2}
-set is also called a Sidon set.

For every positive integer
h
, we use a greedy algorithm to construct a
B_{h}
-set

\{a_{k}(h):k=0,1,2,\ldots\}
as follows:
a_{0}(h)=0
and,
if
\{a_{0}(h),a_{1}(h),\ldots,a_{k}(h)\}
is a
B_{h}
-set, then
a_{k+1}(h)
is
the least positive integer such that
\{a_{0}(h),a_{1}(h),\ldots,a_{k}(h),a_{k+1}(h)\}

is a
B_{h}
-set.
The greedy
B_{1}
-set is simply the set
\mathbf{N}_{0}
of nonnegative integers, that is,
a_{k}(1)=k
for all

k\in\mathbf{N}_{0}
. The greedy
B_{2}
-set is the (shifted) Mian-Chowla sequence
(Mian-Chowla
[
2
]
, Guy
[
1
, Section E28]
).

For all
h\geq 1
we have

a_{0}(h)=0,\quad a_{1}(h)=1,\qand a_{2}(h)=h+1.

From computer calculations of initial segments of
B_{h}
-sets for small

, O’Bryant
[
4
]

conjectured that, for all positive integers

a_{3}(h)=h^{2}+h+1.

This formula for
a_{3}(h)
is proved in
[
3
]
.
In this paper we obtain an explicit quasi-polynomial for

Theorem 1

The fourth positive element in the greedy

-set is

\displaystyle a_{4}(h)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\left(h^{3}+3h^{2}+3h+1\right)&\text{if $h$ is odd}\\
\frac{1}{2}\left(h^{3}+2h^{2}+3h+2\right)&\text{if $h$ is even.}\end{cases}

Using the floor function, we may also write

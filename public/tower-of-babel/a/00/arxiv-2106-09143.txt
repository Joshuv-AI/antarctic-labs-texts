Staircase symmetries in Hirzebruch surfaces Thanks: NSF Graduate Research Grant DGE-1650441
Nicki Magill, Dusa McDuff

Abstract
. This paper continues the investigation of staircases in the family of Hirzebruch surfaces formed by blowing up the projective plane with weight b b , that was started in Bertozzi, Holm et al. in arXiv:2010.08567. We explain the symmetries underlying the structure of the set of b b that admit staircases, and show how the properties of these symmetries arise from a governing Diophantine equation. We also greatly simplify the techniques needed to show that a family of steps does form a staircase by using arithmetic properties of the accumulation function. There should be analogous results about both staircases and mutations for the other rational toric domains considered, for example, by Cristofaro-Gardiner et al. in arXiv:2004.07829 and by Casals–Vianna in arXiv:2004.13232.

The work presented here leads to many interesting questions. Here are some of them.

\bullet
The picture developed here seems to make up the first level of an iterative “fractal” kind of structure
for the Hirzebruch surfaces
H_{b}
.
One might consider the family of blocking classes
{\bf{B}}^{U}_{n},n\geq 0
, extended by two seeds as in Proposition
1.2.2
, to be the backbone of the first level of this structure. This level also includes the associated staircase classes. We prove in Proposition
2.2.9
that all the staircase classes are also center-blocking classes. Further, numerical evidence suggests that there are staircases whose steps have centers with
4
-periodic continued fractions, indeed it seems with any even period.
What seems to be the case is that each pair of adjacent ascending/descending staircases at level one shares a first step, and that this first step is a blocking class with associated
4
-periodic staircases. Thus the backbone of the second level should consist of these shared steps, with associated
4
-periodic staircases generated by appropriate seeds at level one. For more details, see
[
MMW
]
.

\bullet
It also would be very interesting to analyse Usher’s results using the current framework, to see if there are analogs of blocking classes, seeds and staircase families.
One might be able to build a bridge between the two cases by thinking of a polydisc as a degenerate two-point blowup of
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
, and then looking at
the ellipsoidal capacity function for the family of two-fold blowups of
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
that join the two cases. This will also be the subject of future work.

\bullet

The recursive patterns behind the staircases for rational target manifolds
X

are related to almost toric structures and the transformations called mutations that appear for example in
[
CG-HMP
]

and Casals–Vianna
[
CV
]
. It would be very interesting to know how the symmetries discussed here appear in those contexts.

Main results

. Suppose that

{\bf{E}}_{ov}=(d_{ov},m_{ov},{\bf{m}})
is an overshadowing class,
and denote by

\mu_{{\bf{E}}_{ov},b}(z)=\frac{A+Cz}{d_{ov}-m_{ov}b}.

the corresponding obstruction.
Assume the slope
C/(d_{ov}-m_{ov}b_{\infty})
of
\mu_{{\bf{E}}_{ov},b_{\infty}}(z)
is
>1/(3-b_{\infty})
.
Then there is a positive integer
k
such that

(i)
If
b_{\infty}>1/3
and
m_{{\bf{B}}}/d_{{\bf{B}}}>1/3
, then
m_{ov}/d_{ov}<1/3
and
d_{ov}-3m_{ov}=kt_{{\bf{B}}}

(ii)
If
b_{\infty}<1/3
and
m_{{\bf{B}}}/d_{{\bf{B}}}<1/3
,
m_{ov}/d_{ov}>1/3
and
3m_{ov}-d_{ov}=kt_{{\bf{B}}}
.

Proof.

Note first that the function
\mu_{{\bf{E}}_{ov},b_{\infty}}
must obstruct an interval
(z_{\infty},z_{\infty}+\varepsilon)
to the right of the limit point and hence have break point
a_{ov}>z_{\infty}
. Hence (
4.3.1
) implies that
A\geq 0
and
C\geq q_{ov}
.
Further, the condition on the slope implies that
C>A
.

Next note that

\frac{1+z_{\infty}}{3-b_{\infty}}=\frac{p_{{\bf{B}}}}{d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty}}=\frac{A+Cz_{\infty}}{d_{ov}-m_{ov}b_{\infty}}.

This implies

\displaystyle(1+z_{\infty})(d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty})=p_{{\bf{B}}}(3-b_{\infty}),

\displaystyle\qquad\Longrightarrow z_{\infty}=\frac{p_{{\bf{B}}}(3-b_{\infty})-(d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty})}{d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty}}=\frac{b_{\infty}(m_{{\bf{B}}}-p_{{\bf{B}}})+3p_{{\bf{B}}}-d_{{\bf{B}}}}{d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty}}

and

\displaystyle(A+Cz_{\infty})(d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty})=p_{{\bf{B}}}(d_{ov}-m_{ov}b_{\infty})

\displaystyle\qquad\Longrightarrow z_{\infty}=\frac{p_{{\bf{B}}}(d_{ov}-b_{\infty}m_{ov})-A(d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty})}{C(d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty})}=\frac{b_{\infty}(Am_{{\bf{B}}}-pm_{ov})+p_{{\bf{B}}}d_{ov}-Ad_{{\bf{B}}}}{C(d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty})}

Therefore

b_{\infty}(Am_{{\bf{B}}}-p_{{\bf{B}}}m_{ov})+p_{{\bf{B}}}d_{ov}-Ad_{{\bf{B}}}=C\bigl(b_{\infty}(m_{{\bf{B}}}-p_{{\bf{B}}})+3p_{{\bf{B}}}-d_{{\bf{B}}}\bigr).

All quantities here are integers except for
b_{\infty}
which is irrational because
z_{\infty}
is. Therefore the coefficients of
b_{\infty}
must be equal. Thus we have

Am_{{\bf{B}}}-p_{{\bf{B}}}m_{ov}=C(m_{{\bf{B}}}-p_{{\bf{B}}}),\qquad p_{{\bf{B}}}d_{ov}-Ad_{{\bf{B}}}=C(3p_{{\bf{B}}}-d_{{\bf{B}}}).

We can solve these equations for
m_{ov},d_{ov}
to get

(4.3.2)

\displaystyle m_{ov}=\frac{(A-C)m_{{\bf{B}}}+Cp_{{\bf{B}}}}{p_{{\bf{B}}}}=C+(A-C)m_{{\bf{B}}}/p_{{\bf{B}}},

\displaystyle d_{ov}=\frac{3p_{{\bf{B}}}C+(A-C)d_{{\bf{B}}}}{p_{{\bf{B}}}}=3C+(A-C)d_{{\bf{B}}}/p_{{\bf{B}}}.

As
m_{ov},d_{ov}
are integers,
(A-C)m_{{\bf{B}}}/p_{{\bf{B}}}
and
(A-C)d_{{\bf{B}}}/p_{{\bf{B}}}
are both integers.
Then, by Lemma
4.3.2
,
p_{{\bf{B}}}|(A-C)
. This proves
k=(C-A)/p_{{\bf{B}}}
is an integer.

To see
k>0
, we have an equation of the form
(A+Cz)/{\lambda}=(1+z)/{\lambda}^{\prime}
and assume that
C/{\lambda}>1/{\lambda}^{\prime}
, i.e.
C{\lambda}^{\prime}>{\lambda}
.
Then

{\lambda}+{\lambda}z=A{\lambda}^{\prime}+Cz{\lambda}^{\prime}>A{\lambda}^{\prime}+{\lambda}z,

so that
A{\lambda}^{\prime}<{\lambda}<C{\lambda}^{\prime}
. In other words,
A<C
.

The formulas in (
4.3.2
) then imply that if
b_{\infty}>1/3
, so that we also have
m_{{\bf{B}}}/d_{{\bf{B}}}>1/3
by Corollary
4.2.6
, then
3m_{ov}<d_{ov}
.
We can compute

d_{ov}-3m_{ov}=3C-kd_{{\bf{B}}}-3C+3km_{{\bf{B}}}=k(3m_{{\bf{B}}}-d_{{\bf{B}}})=kt_{{\bf{B}}}.

This proves (i).
The proof for (ii) follows similarly.
∎

We prove a similar result for ascending staircases.

Lemma 4.3.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be an ascending pre-staircase with irrational accumularion point
z_{\infty}
that is associated to
a perfect blocking class

{\bf{B}}=(d_{{\bf{B}}},m_{{\bf{B}}},p_{{\bf{B}}},q_{{\bf{B}}},t_{{\bf{B}}})
. Suppose that

{\bf{E}}_{ov}=(d_{ov},m_{ov},{\bf{m}})
is an overshadowing class,
and denote by

\mu_{{\bf{E}}_{ov},b}(z)=\frac{A+Cz}{d_{ov}-m_{ov}b}.

the corresponding obstruction. Then there is a positive integer
k
such that

(i)
If
b_{\infty}>1/3
and
m_{{\bf{B}}}/d_{{\bf{B}}}>1/3
, then
m_{ov}/d_{ov}<1/3
and
d_{ov}-3m_{ov}=kt_{{\bf{B}}}

(ii)
If
b_{\infty}<1/3
and
m_{{\bf{B}}}/d_{{\bf{B}}}>1/3
, then
m_{ov}/d_{ov}>1/3
and
3m_{ov}-d_{ov}=kt_{{\bf{B}}}
.

Proof.

Notice first that we must have
A-C>0
since if
A\leq C
the slope of the obstruction
\mu_{E_{ov},b_{\infty}}
is at most that of the line
z\mapsto\frac{1+z}{3-b_{\infty}}
which is
<V_{b_{\infty}}(z)
for
z<z_{\infty}
, and so is not obstructive for
z<z_{\infty}
, while the overshadowing class is obstructive.

Next, as
in Lemma
4.3.3
we consider the equalities

\frac{1+z_{\infty}}{3-b_{\infty}}=\frac{q_{{\bf{B}}}z_{\infty}}{d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty}}=\frac{A+Cz_{\infty}}{d_{ov}-m_{ov}b_{\infty}}.

This implies

\displaystyle(1+z_{\infty})(d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty})=q_{{\bf{B}}}z_{\infty}(3-b_{\infty}),

\displaystyle\qquad\Longrightarrow{z_{\infty}}=\frac{d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty}}{q_{{\bf{B}}}(3-b_{\infty})-(d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty})}

and

\displaystyle(A+Cz_{\infty})(d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty})=q_{{\bf{B}}}z_{\infty}(d_{ov}-m_{ov}b_{\infty})

\displaystyle\qquad\Longrightarrow{z_{\infty}}=\frac{A(d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty})}{q_{{\bf{B}}}(d_{ov}-m_{ov}b_{\infty})-C(d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty})}

Hence we must have

A(q_{{\bf{B}}}(3-b_{\infty})-(d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty}))=q_{{\bf{B}}}(d_{ov}-m_{ov}b_{\infty})-C(d_{{\bf{B}}}-m_{{\bf{B}}}b_{\infty}).

As before, the coefficients of
b_{\infty}
on both sides must agree, which gives

(4.3.3)

\displaystyle d_{ov}=3A-(A-C)d_{{\bf{B}}}/q_{{\bf{B}}},\qquad m_{ov}=A-(A-C)m_{{\bf{B}}}/q_{{\bf{B}}}.

Since
A-C>0
and
d_{ov},m_{ov}
are integers, Lemma
4.3.2
imply
k=(A-C)/q_{{\bf{B}}}
is a positive integer.

Furthermore, these formulas prove that if
b_{\infty}>1/3
and hence

m_{{\bf{B}}}/d_{{\bf{B}}}>1/3
then
d_{ov}>3m_{ov}
. We can again compute

d_{ov}-3m_{ov}=k\,t_{{\bf{B}}}.

This proves (i). The proof for (ii) follows similarly.
∎

We use the results of Lemma
4.3.3
and Lemma
4.3.4
to rule out overshadowing classes for a large set of pre-staircases, which implies that none of the pre-staircases considered in this paper have overshadowing classes.

Lemma 4.3.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a pre-staircase with irrational accumulation point
z_{\infty}
that is associated to a perfect blocking class
{\bf{B}}=(d_{{\bf{B}}},m_{{\bf{B}}},p_{{\bf{B}}},q_{{\bf{B}}},t_{{\bf{B}}})
such that
t_{{\bf{B}}}\geq 3
. Suppose that either
b_{\infty}>1/3
and the
m_{\kappa}/d_{\kappa}
strictly decrease or
b_{\infty}<1/3
and the
m_{{\kappa}}/d_{{\kappa}}
strictly increase. Assume further that if
{\mathcal{S}}
is descending any overshadowing class must have slope
>1/(3-b_{\infty})
.
Then, the pre-staircase
{\mathcal{S}}
has no overshadowing classes at all.

Proof.

These conditions and Lemma
4.3.1
ensure that the conditions in either Lemma
4.3.3
or Lemma
4.3.4
hold for
{\mathcal{S}}
. For a class
{\bf{E}}_{ov}=(d_{ov},m_{ov},{\bf{m}}_{ov})
to be overshadowing, it must be obstructive for
b=b_{\infty}
at its break point
a_{ov}
.
By
[
BHM
, Lem.15]

this is possible only if
|d_{ov}b_{\infty}-m_{ov}|<1
.
This estimate and the results in Lemma
4.3.3
and Lemma
4.3.4
imply that for each staircase there is a positive integer
k
such that

3>3|b_{\infty}d_{ov}-m_{ov}|>|d_{ov}-3m_{ov}|=t_{{\bf{B}}}k\geq 3k.

As
k
must be positive, no such
d_{ov}
and
m_{ov}
can exist.
∎

Corollary 4.3.6
.

For each
T\in\mathcal{G}
, the pre-staircase family
T^{\sharp}({\mathcal{S}}^{U})
has no overshadowing classes.

Proof.

Because
t_{{\bf{B}}}=2n+3
in all cases, the results of Corollary
4.2.6
and Lemma
4.3.1
show that all the staircases in
T^{\sharp}({\mathcal{S}}^{U})
except for
{\mathcal{S}}^{U}_{u,0}
satisfy the conditions in Lemma
4.3.5
and hence have no overshadowing classes. The proof for
{\mathcal{S}}^{U}_{u,0}
is given in
[
BHM
, Ex.70]
.
∎

Now, we establish a straightforward way to check if a perfect pre-staircase is live based on the above overshadowing arguments.

Proposition 4.3.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a perfect pre-staircase with irrational accumulation point
z_{\infty}
associated to a blocking class
{\bf{B}}
with recursion parameter
t_{\bf{B}}\geq 3
. Suppose that either
b_{\infty}>1/3
and the
m_{\kappa}/d_{\kappa}
strictly decrease or
b_{\infty}<1/3
and the
m_{{\kappa}}/d_{{\kappa}}
strictly increase. Assume further that if
{\mathcal{S}}
is descending the slope of any overshadowing class must be
>1/(3-b_{\infty})
. Then,
{\mathcal{S}}
is a staircase, namely it is perfect and live.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.2.3
and Lemma
4.3.5
, the perfect pre-staircase
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
4.2.1
implying that
{\mathcal{S}}
is live.
∎

A consequence of this proposition is Theorem
1.2.6
.

Corollary 4.3.8
.

For each
T\in\mathcal{G}
,
T^{\sharp}({\mathcal{S}}^{U})
is live.

Proof.

For all staircases except
{\mathcal{S}}^{U}_{u,0}
(which was shown to be live in
[
BHM
]
), the
conditions of Proposition
4.3.7
are satisfied since
t_{{\bf{B}}}=2n+3
, and Corollary
4.2.6
and Lemma
4.3.1
hold.
∎

Introduction

The non-negative polynomials

\mathrm{Pos}(K):=\{f\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\,|\,f\geq 0\ \text{on}\ K\}

on closed
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
,
n\in\mathds{N}
, are extensively studied in the literature.
Various types of Positivstellensätze of real algebraic geometry provide powerful descriptions of non-negative or positive polynomials on semi-algebraic sets
K

[
12
]
.

In contrast, linear maps

T:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}],

(1)

which satisfy

T\mathrm{Pos}(K)\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(K)

(2)

are studied much less and mainly for
K=\mathds{R}^{n}
, see e.g.
[
9
,
14
,
1
]
.
A linear map
T
satisfying (
2
) is called a
K
-positivity preserver
.

The linear operators
T
can be nicely described as differential operators of infinite order, i.e., for each
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
there exists a unique polynomial
q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
such that

T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}q_{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}.

(3)

This result is folklore.
A proof can e.g. be found in
[
14
, Lemma 2.3]
.
The representation (
3
) is called the
canonical representation
of
T
.

The main objects of this paper are
K
-positivity preservers with closed
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
and generators of semi-groups of
K
-positivity preservers.
That is, we study the following problems:

I)

When is an operator
T
of the form (
3
) a
K
-positivity preserver?

II)

For which linear map
A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is
e^{tA}
a
K
-positivity preserver for all
t\geq 0
?

For differential operators
T
with
K=\mathds{R}^{n}
problem (I) has been solved in
[
1
]
and with constant coefficients
q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}
problem (II) has been solved in
[
4
]
.
The present paper treats operators
T
with polynomial coefficients
q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and general closed
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
.
This is technically much more involved.

The importance of the second problem is motivated by the following.
For any
f_{0}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
we have that
e^{tA}f_{0}
is the unique solution of

\partial_{t}f=Af\quad\text{with}\quad f(\,\cdot\,,0)=f_{0}.

(4)

If
f_{0}\in\mathrm{Pos}(K)
for some closed subset
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
, it is natural to ask whether the solution
f
of (
4
) is also in
\mathrm{Pos}(K)
for all
t\geq 0
.
If we require this for arbitrary
f_{0}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
then it is equivalent to
e^{tA}\mathrm{Pos}(K)\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(K)
for all
t\geq 0
.
This is one reason for the interest in operators
A
which satisfy
e^{tA}\mathrm{Pos}(K)\subseteq\mathrm{Pos}(K)
for all
t\geq 0
.

One easily sees that when optimizing a linear functional
L:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}
over some set
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
we can apply
e^{tA}
and
e^{-tA^{*}}
to get

L(p)=L(e^{-tA}e^{tA}p)=(e^{-tA^{*}}L)(e^{tA}p).

Hence, if
A
and
t\geq 0
are chosen such that
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}:=e^{tA}\mathcal{C}
consists only of sums of squares we have to optimize
\tilde{L}:=e^{-tA^{*}}L
over the subset
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
of sums of squares:

\min_{p\in\mathcal{C}}L(p)=\min_{p\in\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}\tilde{L}(p).

Optimizing over (a subset of) sums of squares is computationally much more efficient.
In
[
2
]
we observed that under the heat equation several non-negative polynomials which are not sums of squares, e.g. the Motzkin, the Robinson, and the Choi–Lam polynomial, become a sum of squares and in
[
2
, Thm. 3.20]
we found that every non-negative polynomial
p\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}]_{\leq 4}
becomes a sum of squares after a time
\tau>0
(independent on
p
).

For the study of the generator problem we shall define and use a non-commutative regular Fréchet Lie group.

Definition 1.1
.

We define

\mathfrak{D}:=\left\{T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}q_{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}\;\middle|\;q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq|\alpha|},\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n},\ \text{and}\ \ker T=\{0\}\right\}

\mathfrak{d}:=\left\{\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}q_{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}\;\middle|\;q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq|\alpha|},\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}\right\}.

We define
\mathds{1}
by

and
q_{\alpha}=0
otherwise.

Clearly, the linear mappings

leave the vector spaces

invariant for all

.
This property is necessary such that

is well-defined as an operator
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

2.2
Fréchet Spaces,
\mathds{C}[[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]]
, and
\mathds{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{\sharp}[[\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n}]]

A Fréchet space is a metrizable, complete, and locally convex topological vector space
\mathcal{V}
.
The vector space
\mathds{C}[[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]]
of formal power series is a Fréchet space with the family
\{p_{d}\}_{d\in\mathds{N}_{0}}
of semi-norms
p_{s}(f)=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}:|\alpha|\leq d}|c_{\alpha}|
of
f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}c_{\alpha}\cdot x^{\alpha}
, see e.g.
[
20
, pp. 91–92, Ex. III]
.
The convergence for this Fréchet space is the coordinate-wise convergence.
Similarly, the space of all sequences indexed by a countable set is a Fréchet space.

Definition 2.8
.

We define

\mathds{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{\sharp}[[\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n}]]:=\left\{\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}p_{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}\,\middle|\,p_{\alpha}\in\mathds{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq|\alpha|},\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}\right\}.

Lemma 2.9
.

The vector space
\mathds{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{\sharp}[[\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n}]]
is a Fréchet space with the coordinate-wise convergence.

Proof.

We have

\mathds{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{\sharp}[[\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n}]]=\\
\left\{\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\sum_{\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}:|\beta|\leq|\alpha|}c_{\alpha,\beta}\cdot x^{\beta}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}\,\middle|\,c_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathds{C}\ \text{for all}\ \alpha,\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}\ \text{with}\ |\beta|\leq|\alpha|\right\},

i.e.,
\mathds{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{\sharp}[[\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n}]]
is as a vector space isomorphic to the vector space of sequences

\mathcal{S}:=\big\{(c_{\alpha,\beta})_{\alpha,\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}:|\beta|\leq|\alpha|}\,\big|\,c_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathds{C}\ \text{for all}\ \alpha,\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}\ \text{with}\ |\beta|\leq|\alpha|\big\}.

But
\mathcal{S}
as the vector space of sequences with the coordinate-wise convergence is a Fréchet space and hence so is
\mathds{C}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]^{\sharp}[[\partial_{1},\dots,\partial_{n}]]
.
∎

Lemma 3.16
.

(\mathfrak{d},\,\cdot\,,+)
is an algebra.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that

Let

Lemma 3.17
.

The exponential map

\exp:\mathfrak{d}\to\mathfrak{D},\quad A\mapsto\exp A:=\sum_{k\in\mathds{N}_{0}}\frac{A^{k}}{k!}

Proof.

Let

For

∎

Corollary 3.19
.

Let

Then we have

\exp A=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\mathds{1}+\frac{A}{k}\right)^{k}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\mathds{1}-\frac{A}{k}\right)^{-k}.

Proof.

Let

From the previous example we see that the degree of

Proposition 4.22

Let

p_{2i}(x)=c_{i}+x^{2k_{i}r_{i}}\quad\text{and}\quad p_{2i+1}(x)=0

for all
i\in\mathds{N}_{0}
.

Proof.

By
Proposition

2.3
it is sufficient to show that

is a moment sequence for all

Since we are in the one-dimensional case this is equivalent to

for all Hankel matrices

,

, see e.g.

But for this it is sufficient to ensure that

(8)

for all
i\in\mathds{N}_{0}
and
y\in\mathds{R}
.
We construct the sequences
(c_{i})_{i\in\mathds{N}_{0}}
and
(k_{i})_{i\in\mathds{N}_{0}}
from (

i=0
:
We have

and

i\to i+1

Assume we have

Since

The following is a necessary condition that a linear map

K
-positivity preserver.

Lemma 4.23
.

Let
q_{\alpha}\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
for
\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
.
Suppose that

T=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}q_{\alpha}\cdot\partial^{\alpha}

is a
K
-positivity preserver,

closed.
Then

(\alpha!\cdot q_{\alpha}(y))_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}

is a
(K-y)
-moment sequence for all
y\in K
.

Proof.

Since
T
is a
K
-positivity preserver we have that

L_{y}:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R},\quad f\mapsto L_{y}(f):=(T_{y}f)(y)

is a
K
-moment sequence, i.e., there exists a Radon measure
\mu_{y}
with
\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_{y}\subseteq K

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}q_{\alpha}(y)\cdot f^{(\alpha)}(y)=(T_{y}f)(y)=L_{y}(f)=\int_{K}f(x)\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{y}(x)

holds for all

.
Using the Taylor formula

f(x)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{f^{(\alpha)}(y)}{\alpha!}\cdot(x-y)^{\alpha}

we obtain

\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}q_{\alpha}(y)\cdot f^{(\alpha)}(y)=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}\frac{f^{(\alpha)}(x)}{\alpha!}\cdot\int(x-y)^{\alpha}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{y}(x)

for all
f\in\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
For
\beta\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}
set
f_{\beta}(x):=(x-y)^{\beta}
.
Then
f^{(\alpha)}_{\beta}(y)=\beta!\cdot\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
and hence

\beta!\cdot q_{\beta}(y)=\int_{K}(x-y)^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{y}(x)=\int_{K-y}z^{\beta}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\mu_{y}(z+y),

i.e.,
(\alpha!\cdot q_{\alpha}(y))_{\alpha\in\mathds{N}_{0}^{n}}
is a
(K-y)
-moment sequence with representing measure
\nu_{y}(\,\cdot\,):=\mu_{y}(\,\cdot\,+y)

Lemma 4.24
.

Let

In the previous section we have characterized all

We want to deal now with

We start with some definitions.

Definition 5.38
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a convex subset of
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
which is closed in the LF-topology of
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
We denote by

\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{C}}:=\left\{T\in\mathfrak{D}\,\middle|\,T\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{C}\right\}

the set of all
\mathcal{C}
-preserves
and by

\mathfrak{d}_{\mathcal{C}}:=\left\{A\in\mathfrak{d}\,\middle|\,e^{tA}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\mathcal{C}}\ \text{for all}\ t\geq 0\right\}

the set of all
generators of
\mathcal{C}
-preserving semi-groups
.
If
\mathcal{C}=\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})
we abbreviate

\mathfrak{D}_{+}:=\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{d}_{+}:=\mathfrak{d}_{\mathrm{Pos}(\mathds{R}^{n})}.

Positive semi-groups on Banach lattices can be characterized in terms of their resolvents.
It seems that the case of the LF-space
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
is rarely considered in the literature;
[
4
]
might be the first work on this topic.
Following ideas from the proof of
[
16
, Prop. 2.3]
we obtain the following description of
\mathfrak{d}_{\mathcal{C}}
via the resolvent.
Note that we only assume
\mathcal{C}
to be convex and closed; it does
not
need to be a cone.

Proposition 5.39
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be a linear subspace of
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and let
\mathcal{C}
be a non-empty convex subset of
\mathcal{V}
which is closed in the LF-topology.
Let
A\in\mathfrak{d}
be such that
A\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
.
For
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
we set

A_{d}:=A|_{\mathcal{V}\cap\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{C}_{d}:=\mathcal{C}\cap\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}.

Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

A\in\mathfrak{d}_{\mathcal{C}}
.

(ii)

For every
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
there exists an
\varepsilon_{d}>0
such that

(\mathds{1}-\lambda A_{d})^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{d}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{d}

holds for all
\lambda\in[0,\varepsilon_{d})
.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii):
Let
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
and
p\in\mathcal{C}_{d}
.
Let
\|\cdot\|_{d}
be a norm on
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}
.
We denote by
\|\cdot\|_{d}
the corresponding operator norm for operators
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]_{\leq d}
.
For
\gamma>\|A_{d}\|_{d}
and all
t\geq 0
we have

\left\|e^{-\gamma t}\cdot e^{tA_{d}}p\right\|_{d}\leq\|p\|_{d}\cdot e^{(\|A_{d}\|_{d}-\gamma)\cdot t}

and hence

\gamma(\gamma\mathds{1}-A_{d})^{-1}\,p=\gamma\cdot\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma t}\cdot e^{tA_{d}}p\penalty\ \mathrm{d}t.

Therefore, since
\gamma\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\gamma t}\penalty\ \mathrm{d}t=1
we conclude that
\lambda\cdot(\lambda\mathds{1}-A_{d})^{-1}p
is a convex linear combination of elements
e^{tA_{d}}p
in the closed convex set
\mathcal{C}_{d}
, i.e.,

\gamma(\gamma\mathds{1}-A_{d})^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{d}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{d}

for all
\gamma>\|A_{d}\|_{d}
.
With
\varepsilon_{d}:=\|A_{d}\|_{d}^{-1}
and hence
\lambda:=\gamma^{-1}\in[0,\varepsilon_{d})
we get the assertion (ii) for every
d\in\mathds{N}_{0}
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i): Let
t\geq 0
and
p\in\mathcal{C}
.
Set
d:=\deg p
.
Then we have that

e^{tA}p\quad\overset{\text{Cor.\ \ref{cor:Ad}}}{=}\quad e^{tA_{d}}p\quad\overset{\text{Cor.\ \ref{cor:expLimAd}}}{=}\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\left(\mathds{1}-\frac{tA_{d}}{k}\right)^{-k}p\quad\in\mathcal{C}_{d}

holds since

\left(\mathds{1}-\frac{tA_{d}}{k}\right)^{-1}\mathcal{C}_{d}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{d}

holds for all
k>\varepsilon_{d}^{-1}
.
The limit
k\to\infty
of

\left(\mathds{1}-\frac{tA_{d}}{k}\right)^{-k}p

is in
\mathcal{C}_{d}
because
\mathcal{C}_{d}
is closed.
Since
t\geq 0
and
p\in\mathcal{C}
were arbitrary we have that
e^{tA}\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
holds for all
t\geq 0
, i.e.,
A\in\mathfrak{d}_{\mathcal{C}}
.
∎

The linear subspace
\mathcal{V}
allows one to treat the sparse case when not all monomials of

are in
\mathcal{V}

But in this general case the description (ii) via the resolvent might be hard to check even in the easiest cases.

Summary

In
Section

3
we showed that
(\mathfrak{D},\,\cdot\,)
is a regular Fréchet Lie group with Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{d},\,\cdot\,,+)
.
This gave us a sufficiently analytic background to investigate
K
-positivity preservers and their generators for general closed subsets
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
.

In
Section

4
we investigated
K
-positivity preservers.
At first we characterized in
Theorem

4.25
all
K
-positivity preservers for general closed subsets
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
.
We then treated
K
-positivity preservers with constant coefficients more deeply.
While a
K^{\sharp}
-moment sequence always gives a
K
-positivity preserver (
Lemma

4.30
) we have that the reverse only holds for determinate sequences (
Theorem

4.32
) so far.
It is open if this also holds for the non-determinate case.

Open Problem 7.56
.

In
Lemma

4.30
we showed that

(i)

s
is a
K^{\sharp}
-moment sequence

implies that

(ii)

D(s)
is a
K
-positivity preserver.

The reverse holds for
K
being a cone (
Proposition

4.36
) and compact
K
(
Corollary

4.33
).
In general it is open whether “(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)” holds.
Is there a counter example where it does not hold or a proof that is always holds?

In
Corollary

4.33
we showed that every
K
-positivity preserver with constant coefficients for compact
K
is only the scaling
Tf=cf
for some
c\geq 0
.

In
Section

5
we characterized the generators
A
of
K
-positivity preservers, i.e., all maps
A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
such that
e^{tA}
is a
K
-positivity preserver for all
t\geq 0
.
For that we need the regular Fréchet Lie group properties established in
Section

3
.

At first we show in
Proposition

5.42
that the generators can always be fully characterized via the resolvent.
This characterization is well-known in the semi-group theory on Banach lattices.
However, the characterization through the resolvent gives no specific information for our case of the LF-space
\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and hence we use other techniques to gain more information about the generators.

In
Theorem

5.49
we show that
A
is a generator of a
\mathds{R}^{n}
-positivity preserving semi-group if and only if
A_{y}
is a generator of a
\mathds{R}^{n}
-positivity preserving semi-group with constant coefficients for all
y\in\mathds{R}^{n}
.
The constant coefficient case was fully solved in
[
4
]
and hence we have explicit expressions how the coefficients of
A
resp.
A_{y}
look like.

Since for cones
K\subseteq\mathds{R}^{n}
and the stripes
K=C\times[0,\infty)
and
C\times\mathds{R}^{n}
with
C\subseteq\mathds{R}^{m}
compact we have that
s
being a
K^{\sharp}
-moment sequence is necessary and sufficient we use the same technique via infinitely divisible representing measures from
[
4
]
here again for the constant coefficient case.
We get a complete description in
Theorem

5.45
.

For the non-constant coefficient case we have
Theorem

5.48
, i.e., if
A_{y}
is a generator of a
K
-positivity preserving semi-group for all
y\in K
then
e^{tA}
is a
K
-positivity preserver for all
t\geq 0
.
We give examples that the reverse of this statement does not hold in general.
Hence, it is still open how the generator of a
K
-positivity preserving semi-group look like in the non-constant coefficient case.

Open Problem 7.57
.

How does

A:\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\to\mathds{R}[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]

look like if
e^{tA}
is a
K
-positivity preserver for all
t\geq 0
with
K\subsetneq\mathds{R}^{n}
closed?

In
Proposition

6.52
we showed that while
A=(x\partial_{x})^{3}
is not a generator of a positivity preserver we have that
(e^{tA})_{t\geq 0}
is an eventually positive semi-group, i.e.,
e^{tA}
is a positivity preserver for all
t\geq\tau

\tau>0
.
In
Proposition

6.54
we showed the same for
A=(x^{2}-1)\cdot\partial_{x}^{2}+a\partial_{x}
on
\mathds{R}[x]_{\leq 2}
.
Moments add therefore an additional tool to investigate eventually positive semi-groups
[

,
5
,

,

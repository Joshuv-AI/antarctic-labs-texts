Navier–Stokes equations, the algebraic aspect
Zharinov V.V

Abstract
Analysis of the Navier-Stokes equations in the frames of the algebraic approach to systems of partial differential equations (formal theory of differential equations) is presented.



Keywords:
Navier–Stokes equations, integrability conditions, evolution,
constraints, differential algebra, symmetries, cohomologies.

1
Preliminaries.

1.1
Navier-Stokes equations

The Navier-Stokes equations in physical notation are
(see
[
1
]
–
[
6
]
, for example)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}_{t}+(\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=\nu\Delta\mathbf{u}-\nabla p,

(1)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=0,

(2)

where
\mathbf{u}=(u^{1},u^{2},u^{3})
is the velocity field,
t
is the time variable, the dot
”
\cdot
” denotes the scalar product,
\nabla=(\nabla_{1},\nabla_{2},\nabla_{3})
is the gradient
with respect to the spacial variables
\mathbf{x}=(x^{1},x^{2},x^{3})
, the parameter
\nu>0

is the viscosity of the flow (do not confuse it with the index
\nu
),

\Delta=\nabla^{2}_{1}+\nabla^{2}_{2}+\nabla^{2}_{3}
is the Laplacian,
p
is the pressure.

Here we study the Navier-Stokes equations from the algebra-geometrical point of view.
The system (
1
)-(
2
) is not formally integrable, as we need, to get the equivalent formally integrable system we add (see
[
7
]
, for example)
the trivial differential prolongations

\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}_{t}=0,\quad\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u})=0,

(3)

and the non-trivial differential prolongation (
hidden integrability condition
)

\Delta p+\nabla((\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathbf{u})=\Delta p+\nabla\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}=0

(4)

(we have used the equation (
2
)).

Remark 1
.

The equation (
4
) is a Poisson equation for the pressure
p
with the density

\rho=-\nabla\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}
, it can be considered as the

inner constraint
for the Navier-Stokes equations.

1.3
The base components

The base space for the Navier–Stokes equations in the algebraic approach
is the infinite dimensional space
\mathbf{B}=\mathrm{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{M}}_{\mathbb{I}}\times\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{I}}
,
where
\mathrm{X}
is the set of independent variables (space coordinates),

\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{M}}_{\mathbb{I}}
is the set of differential variables (velocity coordinates
and their partial derivatives),
\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{I}}
is the set of differential variables
(pressure and its partial derivatives).

The base algebra in the algebraic approach is the unital commutative associative algebra

{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})={\cal C}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\mathbf{B})
of all smooth real functions
on the base space
\mathbf{B}

of a finite order
, i.e., depending
on a finite number of the variables
x^{\mu},u^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}},p_{\mathrm{i}}
, where

\mu\in\mathrm{M}
,
\mathrm{i}\in\mathbb{I}
.
In more detail, the integer
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
is called the
\mathbf{u}
-order
of a function

f(x,\mathbf{u},\mathbf{p})\in{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})
, we write
\ord_{\mathbf{u}}f=r
, if the partial derivative

\partial_{u^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}}f\neq 0
for some variable
u^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}
,
|\mathrm{i}|=r
, while partial derivatives

\partial_{u^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}}f=0
for all
|\mathrm{i}|>r
.
In the same way, the
\mathbf{p}
-order is defined.

The base algebra of derivations of the algebra
{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})
is the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{B})=\mathfrak{D}({\cal A}(\mathbf{B}))=\big\{\zeta=\zeta^{\mu}\partial_{x^{\mu}}+\zeta^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}\partial_{u^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}}+\zeta_{\mathrm{i}}\partial_{p_{\mathrm{i}}}\ \big|\ \zeta^{\mu},\zeta^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}},\zeta_{\mathrm{i}}\in{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})\big\}.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{B})
splits into the
vertical
and
horizontal
subalgebras,

\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{B})=\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(\mathbf{B})\oplus_{{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})}\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B}),

where

•

\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(\mathbf{B})=\{\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{B})\mid\zeta|_{{\cal C}^{\infty}(\mathrm{X})}=0\}

(note,
{\cal C}^{\infty}(\mathrm{X})\subset{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})
)

\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(\mathbf{B})

=\{\zeta=\zeta^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}\partial_{u^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}}+\zeta_{\mathrm{i}}\partial_{p_{\mathrm{i}}}\mid\zeta^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}},\zeta_{\mathrm{i}}\in{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})\}
;

•

\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B})=\{\zeta=\zeta^{\mu}D_{\mu}\mid\zeta^{\mu}\in{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})\}
,
(
D_{\mu}|_{{\cal C}^{\infty}(\mathrm{X})}=\partial_{x^{\mu}}
)

\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B})\!=\!\{\zeta=\zeta^{\mu}

D_{\mu}=\partial_{x^{\mu}}+u^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{i}+(\mu)}\partial_{u^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{i}}}+p_{\mathrm{i}+(\mu)}\partial_{p_{\mathrm{i}}}
,

\lambda,\mu\in\mathrm{M}
;

•

[D_{\mu},D_{\nu}]=0
,
[D_{\mu},\zeta]=(D_{\mu}\zeta^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{i}}-\zeta^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{i}+(\mu)})\partial_{u^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{i}}}+(D_{\mu}\zeta_{\mathrm{i}}-\zeta_{\mathrm{i}+(\mu)})\partial_{p_{\mathrm{i}}}
,

for all
\lambda,\mu,\nu\in\mathrm{M}
,

\zeta=\zeta^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{i}}\partial_{u^{\lambda}_{\mathrm{i}}}+\zeta_{\mathrm{i}}\partial_{p_{\mathrm{i}}}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(\mathbf{B})
.

We denote

•

D_{\mathrm{i}}=(D_{1})^{i^{1}}\com\dots\com(D_{m})^{i^{m}}
for all
\mathrm{i}=(i^{1},\dots,i^{m})\in\mathbb{I}
.

The pair
({\cal A}(\mathbf{B}),\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B}))
is called the

differential algebra
associated with the base space
\mathbf{B}
.

The Lie algebra

\Sym({\cal A}(\mathbf{B}),\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B}))=\big\{\zeta=\ev_{\mathrm{f}}\in\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{V}}(\mathbf{B})\ \big|\ [D_{\mu},\ev_{\mathrm{f}}]=0,\ \mu\in\mathrm{M}\big\}

is the
Lie algebra of symmetries
of the differential algebra

({\cal A}(\mathbf{B}),\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B}))
,
where

•

\mathrm{f}=(f^{\mu},f)\in{\cal A}^{\mathrm{M}}(\mathbf{B})\times{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})
,
f^{\mu}=\zeta^{\mu}_{0}
,
f=\zeta_{0}
,

•

\ev_{\mathrm{f}}=D_{\mathrm{i}}f^{\mu}\cdot\partial_{u^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}}+D_{\mathrm{i}}f\cdot\partial_{p_{\mathrm{i}}}
,
D_{\mathrm{i}}f^{\mu}=\zeta^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}
,
D_{\mathrm{i}}f=\zeta_{\mathrm{i}}
.

The
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})
-module
\Omega_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B})=\oplus_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}\Omega^{q}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B})

of
horizontal differential forms
is defined as follows,

\Omega^{q}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B})=\begin{cases}0,&q<0,q>m;\\
{\cal A}(\mathbf{B}),&q=0;\\
\Hom_{{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})}(\wedge^{q}\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B});{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})),&1\leq q\leq m.\end{cases}

Here,

\displaystyle\Hom_{{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})}

\displaystyle(\wedge^{q}\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B});{\cal A}(\mathbf{B}))

\displaystyle=\big\{\omega^{q}=\omega_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{q}}\cdot dx^{\mu_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx^{\mu_{q}}\ \big|\ \omega_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{q}}\in{\cal A}(\mathbf{B}),\text{+ s-s}\big\},

where the abbreviation ”+s-s” states that components
\omega_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{q}}

are skew-symmetric in indices
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{q}\in\mathrm{M}
.

The
horizontal differential

d_{\mathrm{H}}\in\End_{\mathbb{R}}(\Omega_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B}))
,
d_{\mathrm{H}}\com d_{\mathrm{H}}=0
,
is defined by the rule,

\displaystyle d^{q}_{\mathrm{H}}=d_{\mathrm{H}}\big|_{\Omega^{q}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B})}:\Omega^{q}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B})

\displaystyle\to\Omega^{q+1}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B}),

\displaystyle\omega_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{q}}\cdot dx^{\mu_{1}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx^{\mu_{q}}

\displaystyle\mapsto D_{[\mu_{0}}\omega_{\mu_{1}\dots\mu_{q}]}\cdot dx^{\mu_{0}}\wedge\dots\wedge dx^{\mu_{q}},

the brackets
[\dots]
denote the skew-symmetrization in indices

\mu_{0},\dots,\mu_{q}\in\mathrm{M}
, so
d^{q+1}_{\mathrm{H}}\com d^{q}_{\mathrm{H}}=0
,
q\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The
\mathbb{Z}
-graded linear space

H(\Omega_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B});d_{\mathrm{H}})=\oplus_{q\in\mathbb{Z}}H^{q}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B})
of cohomologies
of the differential algebra
({\cal A}(\mathbf{B});\mathfrak{D}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B}))
is defined in the usual way,

H(\Omega_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B});d_{\mathrm{H}})=\ke d_{\mathrm{H}}\big/\im d_{\mathrm{H}}
,
H^{q}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B})=\ke d^{q}_{\mathrm{H}}\big/\im d^{q-1}_{\mathrm{H}}
,

q\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Theorem 1

(
The main theorem of the formal calculus of variations
,
see, for example,
[
8
]
, and the references therein)
.

The linear spaces

H^{q}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B})=\begin{cases}0,&q<0,q>m;\\
\mathbb{R},&q=0;\\
0,&1\leq q\leq m-1;\\
{\cal H}(\mathbf{B}),&q=m;\end{cases}

where (in our setting) the Helmholtz linear space

{\cal H}(\mathbf{B})=\big\{\chi=(\chi_{\mu},\chi)\in{\cal A}_{\mathrm{M}}(\mathbf{B})\times{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})\ \big|\ \chi_{*}=\chi^{*}\big\},

the linear mappings

\chi_{*},\chi^{*}:{\cal A}^{\mathrm{M}}(\mathbf{B})\times{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})\to{\cal A}_{\mathrm{M}}(\mathbf{B})\times{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})

act by the rules:

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{\mathrm{M}}(\mathbf{B})\times{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})\ni\mathrm{f}=(f^{\mu},f)\mapsto\chi_{*}\mathrm{f}=\mathrm{g}=(g_{\mu},g)\in{\cal A}_{\mathrm{M}}(\mathbf{B})\times{\cal A}(\mathbf{B}),

\displaystyle g_{\mu}=\partial_{u^{\nu}_{\mathrm{i}}}\chi_{\mu}\cdot D_{\mathrm{i}}f^{\nu}+\partial_{p_{\mathrm{i}}}\chi_{\mu}\cdot D_{\mathrm{i}}f,\quad g=\partial_{u^{\nu}_{\mathrm{i}}}\chi\cdot D_{\mathrm{i}}f^{\nu}+\partial_{p_{\mathrm{i}}}\chi\cdot D_{\mathrm{i}}f;

\displaystyle{\cal A}^{\mathrm{M}}(\mathbf{B})\times{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})\ni\mathrm{f}=(f^{\mu},f)\mapsto\chi^{*}\mathrm{f}=\mathrm{g}=(g_{\mu},g)\in{\cal A}_{\mathrm{M}}(\mathbf{B})\times{\cal A}(\mathbf{B}),

\displaystyle g_{\mu}=(-D)_{\mathrm{i}}\big(f^{\nu}\!\cdot\partial_{u^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}}\chi_{\nu}+f\!\cdot\partial_{u^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}}\chi\big),\quad g=(-D)_{\mathrm{i}}\big(f^{\nu}\!\cdot\partial_{p_{\mathrm{i}}}\chi_{\nu}+f\!\cdot\partial_{p_{\mathrm{i}}}\chi\big).

The isomorphism
\delta=(\delta_{u^{\mu}},\delta_{p}):H^{m}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B})\simeq{\cal H}(\mathbf{B})
of linear spaces
is defined as the variational derivatives:

\displaystyle\Omega^{m}_{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbf{B})\ni\omega=L\cdot d^{m}x\mapsto\chi=\delta L=(\delta_{u^{\mu}}L,\delta_{p}L),

\displaystyle\delta_{u^{\mu}}L=(-D)_{\mathrm{i}}\partial_{u^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}}L,\quad\delta_{p}L=(-D)_{\mathrm{i}}\partial_{p_{\mathrm{i}}}L.

.

Note, cohomologies are defined up to isomorphisms.

2.1
The divergence-free space

The continuity equation
\mathrm{CE}=\{\partial_{x^{\mu}}u^{\mu}=0\}
(see (
2
))
has the algebraic counterpart

\mathbf{CE}=\big\{(x,\mathbf{u},\mathbf{p})\in\mathbf{B}\ \big|\ \mathrm{CE}_{\mathrm{i}}=u^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}+(\mu)}=0,\ \mathrm{i}\in\mathbb{I}\big\}.

The subset
\mathbf{CE}\subset\mathbf{B}
is called the
solution manifold
of the equation

\mathrm{CE}
, because a smooth function
\phi(\mathrm{x})=(\phi^{\mu}(\mathrm{x}))\in{\cal C}^{\infty}(\mathrm{X};\mathbb{R}^{\mathrm{M}})

is a solution of the equation
\mathrm{CE}
iff the graph

\graph\mathbf{j}\phi(\mathrm{x})=\big\{(\mathrm{x},\mathbf{u},\mathbf{p})\ \big|\ u^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}=\partial_{x^{\mathrm{i}}}\phi^{\mu}(\mathrm{x}),\ \mu\in\mathrm{M},\ \mathrm{i}\in\mathbb{I}\big\}\subset\mathbf{CE},

where

\mathbf{j}\phi(\mathrm{x})=\{\partial_{x^{\mathrm{i}}}\phi^{\mu}(\mathrm{x})\mid\mathrm{i}\in\mathbb{I},\ \mu\in\mathrm{M}\}

is the
jet
of the function
\phi(\mathrm{x})
, the variables
\mathbf{p}=(p_{\mathrm{i}})\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{I}}
are parameters,

\partial_{x^{\mathrm{i}}}=(\partial_{x^{1}})^{i^{1}}\com\dots\com(\partial_{x^{m}})^{i^{m}}
,
\mathrm{i}\in\mathbb{I}
.

With the solution manifold
\mathbf{CE}
there is associated the ideal

\displaystyle{\cal I}=\big\{f\in{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})\ \big|\ f\big|_{\mathbf{CE}}=0\big\}

\displaystyle=\big\{f=P(D)u^{\mu}_{(\mu)}\ \big|\ P(D)\in{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})[D]\big\}

\displaystyle=\big\{f=\phi^{\mathrm{i}}\cdot u^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}+(\mu)}\ \big|\ \phi^{\mathrm{i}}\in{\cal A}(\mathbf{B}),\ \mathrm{i}\in\mathbb{I},+\mathrm{fin}\big\}

of the algebra
{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})
, where
{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})[D]
is the unital associative algebra
of all polynomials in the indeterminates
D_{1},\dots,D_{m}
with coefficients in
{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})
,
i.e.,

{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})[D]=\big\{P(D)=\phi^{\mathrm{i}}\cdot D_{\mathrm{i}}\ \big|\ \phi^{\mathrm{i}}\in{\cal A}(\mathbf{B}),\ \mathrm{i}\in\mathbb{I},+\mathrm{fin}\big\},

where the abbreviation ”+fin” states that only a finite number of the coefficients

\phi^{\mathrm{i}}\neq 0
. The ideal
{\cal I}
is
differential
, i.e.,

D_{\mu}\big|_{{\cal I}}:{\cal I}\to{\cal I}
,
\mu\in\mathrm{M}
.

By definition, we assume
{\cal A}(\mathbf{CE})={\cal A}(\mathbf{B})\big/{\cal I}
, i.e., we define the algebra

{\cal A}(\mathbf{CE})
of
smooth functions on the space
\mathbf{CE}
as the factor-algebra.
We denote

\bar{f}=f|_{\mathbf{CE}}=f+{\cal I}
the restriction of the function
f\in{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})

to the solution manifold
\mathbf{CE}
and its equivalence class in
{\cal A}(\mathbf{CE})
.

We shall need:

•

the subalgebra of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{B})

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{\cal I}(\mathbf{B})

\displaystyle=\big\{\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{B})\ \big|\ \zeta|_{{\cal I}}:{\cal I}\to{\cal I}\big\}=\big\{\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{B})\ \big|\ \zeta|_{{\cal I}}\in\mathfrak{D}({\cal I})\big\}

\displaystyle=\big\{\zeta=\zeta^{\mu}\partial_{x^{\mu}}+\zeta^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}\partial_{u^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}}+\zeta_{\mathrm{i}}\partial_{p_{\mathrm{i}}}\ \big|\ \zeta^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}+(\mu)}\in{\cal I},\ \mathrm{i}\in\mathbb{I}\big\}

of all derivations of the algebra
{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})
leaving the ideal
{\cal I}
invariant;

•

the subalgebra of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{B})
and the ideal
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{D}_{\cal I}(\mathbf{B})\subset\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{B})

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{B};{\cal I})

\displaystyle=\big\{\zeta\in\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{B})\ \big|\ \zeta:{\cal A}(\mathbf{B})\to{\cal I}\big\}

\displaystyle=\big\{\zeta=\zeta^{\mu}\partial_{x^{\mu}}+\zeta^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}\partial_{u^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}}}+\zeta_{\mathrm{i}}\partial_{p_{\mathrm{i}}}\ \big|\ \zeta^{\mu},\zeta^{\mu}_{\mathrm{i}},\zeta_{\mathrm{i}}\in{\cal I}\big\}

of all derivations with the image in the ideal
{\cal I}
,

•

the Lie factor-algebra
\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{CE})=\mathfrak{D}_{\cal I}(\mathbf{B})\big/\mathfrak{D}(\mathbf{B};{\cal I})

of derivations of the factor-algebra
{\cal A}(\mathbf{CE})
.

Here, the mapping

3.3
Conclusion.

It can be seen from the above constructions that the Navier-Stokes equations
are subject to meaningful analysis within the framework of the algebraic approach
to differential equations. The resulting equations for finding algebraic characteristics
of Navier-Stokes equations, such as symmetries and cohomologies, are essentially complicated.
One may hope to find their partial solutions at least, especially using analytical
computational packets (Mathematica, for example).

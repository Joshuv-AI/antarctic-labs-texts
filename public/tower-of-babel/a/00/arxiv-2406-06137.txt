Matrix norm shrinkage estimators and priors
Xiao Li 1 , Takeru Matsuda 1,2 , Fumiyasu Komaki 1,2 1 Graduate School of Information Science and Technology, The University of Tokyo, 7-3-1 Hongo, Bunkyo-ku, Tokyo 113-8656, Japan 2 RIKEN Center for Brain Science, 2-1 Hirosawa, Wako, Saitama 351-0198, Japan

Abstract
We develop a class of minimax estimators for a normal mean matrix under the Frobenius loss, which generalizes the James–Stein and Efron–Morris estimators. It shrinks the Schatten norm towards zero and works well for low-rank matrices. We also propose a class of superharmonic priors based on the Schatten norm, which generalizes Stein’s prior and the singular value shrinkage prior. The generalized Bayes estimators and Bayesian predictive densities with respect to these priors are minimax. We examine the performance of the proposed estimators and priors in simulation.

Introduction

Suppose that we have a matrix observation
X\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
whose entries are independent normal random variables
X_{ij}\sim{\rm N}(M_{ij},1)
, where
n\geq m
and
M\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
is an unknown mean matrix. In matrix normal notation, it is expressed as
X\sim{\rm N}_{n,m}(M,I_{n},I_{m})
.
In this setting, we consider estimation of
M
under the Frobenius loss

l(M,\hat{M})=\|\hat{M}-M\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\hat{M}_{ij}-M_{ij})^{2}.

Although the maximum likelihood estimator
\hat{M}=X
is minimax, it is inadmissible when
nm\geq 3
from Stein’s paradox
(
14
)
.

There are two important estimators that dominate the maximum likelihood estimator, and are thus minimax.
One is the James–Stein (JS) estimator
(
9
)
given by

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{\mathrm{JS}}=\Big(1-\frac{nm-2}{\|X\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}}\Big)X

(1)

when
nm\geq 3
.
This estimator shrinks towards the zero matrix.
The other is the Efron–Morris (EM) estimator
(
4
)
given by

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{\mathrm{EM}}=X\left(I_{m}-(n-m-1)(X^{\top}X)^{-1}\right)

(2)

when
n\geq m+2
.
This estimator shrinks towards the space of low-rank matrices.
Namely, let
X=USV^{\top}
,
U\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
,
V\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times m}
,
S={\rm diag}(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})
be the singular value decomposition of
X
, where
U^{\top}U=V^{\top}V=I_{m}
and

s_{1}\geq\cdots\geq s_{m}
are the singular values of
X
.
Then,
\hat{M}_{{\rm EM}}=U\hat{\Sigma}V^{\top}
with
\hat{\Sigma}={\rm diag}(\hat{\sigma}_{1},\dots,\hat{\sigma}_{m})
, where

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{i}=\left(1-\frac{n-m-1}{s_{i}^{2}}\right)s_{i},\quad i=1,\ldots,m.

Whereas the James–Stein estimator shrinks towards the zero matrix as seen from

\hat{M}_{\mathrm{JS}}=\frac{nm-2}{\|X\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}}O+\left(1-\frac{nm-2}{\|X\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}}\right)X,

the Efron–Morris estimator can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\hat{M}_{{\rm EM}}=\frac{n-m-1}{s_{1}^{2}}X_{(0)}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\left(\frac{n-m-1}{s_{k+1}^{2}}-\frac{n-m-1}{s_{k}^{2}}\right)X_{(k)}+\left(1-\frac{n-m-1}{s_{m}^{2}}\right)X_{(m)},

where

\displaystyle X_{(k)}=U\mathrm{diag}(s_{1},\dots,s_{k},0,\dots,0)V^{\top}

is the rank-
k
truncation of
X
.
Note that
X_{(0)}=O
and
X_{(m)}=X
.
In this sense, the Efron–Morris estimator shrinks
X
towards the low rank matrices
X_{(k)}
with strength determined by its singular values.
Thus, the Efron–Morris estimator shrinks the singular values towards zero and works well when
M
is close to low-rank (see
13
for details).
Note that the number of nonzero singular values of a matrix is equal to its rank.
Thus, the James–Stein and Efron–Morris estimators can be viewed as scalar and matricial shrinkage estimators, respectively.
Recently,
17
proposed a minimax estimator that combines these two estimators by using a weight determined by minimizing an unbiased estimate of risk.

Correspondingly, there are two important superharmonic priors of
M
, where a prior
\pi(M)
is said to be superharmonic if

\Delta\pi(M)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\partial^{2}\pi}{\partial M_{ij}^{2}}(M)\leq 0

for every
M
.
One is Stein’s prior
(
14
)
given by

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathrm{S}}(M)=\|M\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2-nm},

(3)

for
nm\geq 3
.
The generalized Bayes estimator with respect to Stein’s prior shrinks towards the origin like the James–Stein estimator (
1
).
The other is the singular value shrinkage (SVS) prior
(
11
)
given by

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathrm{SVS}}(M)=\det(M^{\top}M)^{-(n-m-1)/2},

(4)

for
n-m\geq 2
.
The generalized Bayes estimator with respect to the singular value shrinkage prior shrinks the singular values towards zero like the Efron–Morris estimator (
2
).
Since a generalized Bayes estimator with respect to a superharmonic prior is minimax
(
14
)
, the generalized Bayes estimators with respect to these priors are minimax under the Frobenius loss.
See
16
;
15
for other types of minimax (generalized) Bayes estimators.

Superharmonic priors work well for Bayesian prediction as well
(
10
;
6
;
11
)
.
Specifically, suppose that we observe
X\sim{\rm N}_{n,m}(M,I_{n},I_{m})
and predict
{Y}\sim{\rm N}_{n,m}(M,I_{n},I_{m})
by a predictive density
\hat{p}({Y}\mid X)
.
We evaluate predictive densities by the Kullback–Leibler loss:

\displaystyle D({p}(\cdot\mid M),\hat{p}(\cdot\mid X))=\int{p}({Y}\mid M)\log\frac{{p}({Y}\mid M)}{\hat{p}({Y}\mid X)}{\rm d}{Y}.

The Bayesian predictive density based on a prior
\pi(M)
is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{\pi}({Y}\mid X)

\displaystyle=\int{p}({Y}\mid M)\pi(M\mid X){\rm d}M,

where
\pi(M\mid X)
is the posterior distribution of
M
given
X
, and it minimizes the Bayes risk
(
1
)
.
Although the Bayesian predictive density with respect to the uniform prior is minimax, it is inadmissible and dominated by Bayesian predictive densities based on superharmonic priors
(
10
;
6
)
.

In this study, we propose a broad class of shrinkage estimators and priors by generalizing the above ones.
It is based on the (quasi-)norm of a matrix
A
defined by

\displaystyle\|A\|_{p}=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}^{p}\Big)^{1/p},

(5)

for
p>0
, where
\sigma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{m}
are the singular values of
A
.
For
p\geq 1
, it is called the Schatten norm.
In particular, the Schatten norm with
p=2
coincides with the Frobenius norm and the Schatten norm with
p=1
is called the nuclear norm, which is often used for low-rank regularization.
Note that
\|\cdot\|_{p}
with
p\in(0,1)
is not a norm but a quasinorm.
We derive sufficient conditions for the proposed estimators to be minimax and for the proposed priors to be superharmonic.
Numerical results show their advantages compared to existing ones.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2
, we introduce a class of matrix norm shrinkage estimators.
In Section
3
, we propose a class of matrix norm shrinkage priors associated with the estimators in Section
2
.
Numerical experiments are presented in Section
4
.
Proofs of the theorems are given in the Appendix.

2
Matrix norm shrinkage estimator

Let
X=USV^{\top}
be a singular value decomposition of
X
, where
{S}=\mathrm{diag}(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})
with
s_{1}\geq\dots\geq s_{m}
. When
M
is the zero matrix, the probability density function (PDF) of
S
with respect to
{\rm d}{S}
is proportional to

\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}^{2}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}^{n-m}\prod_{i<j}(s_{i}^{2}-s_{j}^{2}),

thus
P(s_{1}>s_{2}>\dots>s_{m}>0)=1
holds
(
8
)
. Using the boundedness of the ratio between the PDF of
X
that follows
X_{ij}\sim{\rm N}(M_{ij},1)
and the PDF of
X
that follows
X_{ij}\sim{\rm N}(0,1)
within
\{X\mid\|X\|_{\mathrm{F}}\leq R\}
for any given
R
, we know that
P(s_{1}>s_{2}>\dots>s_{m}>0)=1
for the general case. Therefore, we assume
s_{1}>s_{2}>\dots>s_{m}>0
in this paper.
We consider an equivariant estimator

\displaystyle\hat{M}=U\hat{\Sigma}V^{\top},

(6)

where
\hat{\Sigma}=\mathrm{diag}(\hat{\sigma}_{1}(s),\dots,\hat{\sigma}_{m}(s))
and
s=(s_{1},\dots,s_{m})
.
For convenience, we specify such an equivariant estimator by using
\hat{\sigma}_{1},\dots,\hat{\sigma}_{m}
in the following.
An unbiased estimate of its Frobenius risk has been derived as follows, which also appeared in
16
;
3
.

Proposition 1
.

(
14
;
12
)

For an equivariant estimator
\hat{M}
in (
6
) with
\hat{\sigma}_{i}=(1-\phi_{i}(s))s_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,m
, its Frobenius risk is given by

{\rm E}_{M}\left[\|\hat{M}-M\|_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}\right]={\rm E}_{M}\left[nm+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big\{s_{i}^{2}\phi_{i}^{2}-2(n-m+1)\phi_{i}-2s_{i}\frac{\partial\phi_{i}}{\partial s_{i}}\Big\}-4\sum_{i<j}\frac{s_{i}^{2}\phi_{i}-s_{j}^{2}\phi_{j}}{s_{i}^{2}-s_{j}^{2}}\right].

By extending the James–Stein and Efron–Morris estimators, we introduce the equivariant estimator
\hat{M}_{p,\alpha}
defined by

\hat{\sigma}_{i}=\left(1-\alpha\frac{s_{i}^{p-2}}{\sum_{j=1}^{m}s_{j}^{p}}\right)s_{i},\quad i=1,\dots,m.

Note that the James–Stein estimator (
1
) corresponds to
p=2
and
\alpha=nm-2
, whereas the Efron–Morris estimator (
2
) corresponds to
p=0
and
\alpha=m(n-m-1)
.
This estimator attains minimaxity for an appropriate choice of
p
and
\alpha
as follows.

Theorem 1
.

For
p\in[1,2]
,
\hat{M}_{p,\alpha}
is minimax if
\alpha\in[0,2nm-(2-p)m(m+1)-2p].

For
p\in(0,1)
,
\hat{M}_{p,\alpha}
is minimax if
\alpha\in[0,2nm-2m(m+1)+2(m-1)p].

We refer to
\hat{M}_{p,\alpha}
with
p=1
and
\alpha=nm-m(m+1)/2-1
as the nuclear norm shrinkage estimator (NNSE) and denote it by
\hat{M}_{\mathrm{NNS}}
.
This estimator shrinks the singular values by a constant value that depends on the nuclear norm
\|X\|_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}s_{j}
of
X
:

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{i}=s_{i}-\frac{nm-m(m+1)/2-1}{\sum_{j=1}^{m}s_{j}},\quad i=1,\dots,m.

(7)

From Corollary 3.1 of
16
,
\hat{M}_{\mathrm{NNS}}
is dominated by its positive-part, which we call
\hat{M}_{\mathrm{NNS+}}
.
Namely,
\hat{M}_{\mathrm{NNS+}}
is defined by

\hat{\sigma}_{i}=\max\left(s_{i}-\frac{nm-m(m+1)/2-1}{\sum_{j=1}^{m}s_{j}},0\right),\quad i=1,\dots,m.

Remark 1
.

Note that
\hat{M}_{\mathrm{NNS+}}
has a similar form to the Singular Value Thresholding (SVT) estimator by
2
:

\hat{\sigma}_{i}=\max(s_{i}-\lambda,0)
, which is the solution of the nuclear norm regularized optimization problem:

\min_{M}\{-\log p(X\mid M)+\lambda\|M\|_{1}\}.

This estimator is widely used in the estimation of low-rank matrices.
In practice, the regularization parameter
\lambda
is usually determined by minimizing Stein’s unbiased risk estimate
(
3
)
.
The estimator
\hat{M}_{\mathrm{NNS+}}
can be viewed as determining
\lambda
based on the nuclear norm of
X
:
\lambda=(nm-m(m+1)/2-1)/\|X\|_{1}
.
While
\hat{M}_{\mathrm{NNS+}}
may not work better than SVT in estimation of low-rank matrices,
\hat{M}_{\mathrm{NNS+}}
attains minimaxity.

We also develop another class of minimax estimators.
Let
\hat{M}^{*}_{p,\alpha}
be an equivariant estimator defined by

\hat{\sigma}_{i}=s_{i}-\alpha s_{i}^{p-1},\quad i=1,\dots,m.

When
n-m+p-1>0
, the unbiased estimate of risk in Proposition
1
for
\hat{M}^{*}_{p,\alpha}
is

nm+\alpha\left\{\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}^{2p-2}-2(n-m+p-1)\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}^{p-2}-4\sum_{i<j}\frac{s_{i}^{p}-s_{j}^{p}}{s_{i}^{2}-s_{j}^{2}}\right\},

which is minimized with respect to
\alpha
at

\alpha(s,p)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}^{2p-2}\right)^{-1}\left((n-m+p-1)\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}^{p-2}+2\sum_{i<j}\frac{s_{i}^{p}-s_{j}^{p}}{s_{i}^{2}-s_{j}^{2}}\right).

(8)

Thus, we consider the estimator
\hat{M}^{*}_{p}
defined by

\hat{\sigma}_{i}=s_{i}-\alpha(s,p)s_{i}^{p-1},\quad i=1,\dots,m.

For
p=2
, it is approximately equal to the James–Stein estimator from
\alpha(s,2)=nm/(\sum_{i=1}^{m}s_{i}^{2})
.
For
p=0
, it coincides with the Efron–Morris estimator from
\alpha(s,0)=n-m-1
.
This estimator attains minimaxity under certain conditions as follows.

Theorem 2
.

For
p\in(0,1]
,
\hat{M}^{*}_{p}
is minimax if
n-m\geq 5-3p
. For
p\in(1,2]
,
\hat{M}^{*}_{p}
is minimax if
n-m\geq 3-p+(4p-4)/m
.

Let
\hat{M}^{*,+}_{p}
be the positive-part estimator derived from
\hat{M}^{*}_{p}
given by

\hat{\sigma}_{i}=\max\left(s_{i}-\alpha(s,p)s_{i}^{p-1},0\right),\quad i=1,\dots,m.

From
16
,
\hat{M}^{*,+}_{p}
dominates
\hat{M}^{*}_{p}
.
Thus, from Theorem
2
,
\hat{M}^{*,+}_{p}
is also minimax for
p\in(0,1]
and
n-m\geq 5-3p.

3
Matrix norm shrinkage prior

Let
\sigma_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\sigma_{m}\geq 0
be the singular values of
M
.
We consider a class of priors that shrink the matrix norm (
5
) of
M
towards zero:

\pi_{p,\alpha}(M)=\|M\|_{p}^{-\alpha}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}^{p}\right)^{-\alpha/p}.

For
p=2
and
\alpha=nm-2
, it coincides with Stein’s prior (
3
).
Also, from

\lim_{p\to 0}\|M\|_{p}m^{-1/p}=\lim_{p\to 0}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(1+p\log\sigma_{i}+o(p))\Big)^{1/p}m^{-1/p}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}^{1/m}=\det(M^{\top}M)^{1/(2m)},

the singular value shrinkage prior (
4
) can be viewed as
\pi_{p,\alpha}
for
p=0
and
\alpha=m(n-m-1)
.

We derive a sufficient condition for
\pi_{p,\alpha}
to be superharmonic.
Since
\pi_{p,\alpha}(M)
only depends on the singular values of
M
, we use the following Laplacian formula in the singular value coordinate system.

Proposition 2
.

(
14
;
12
)

If a twice weakly differentiable matrix function
f(M)
only depends on the singular values of
M
, then its Laplacian is given by

\Delta f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial M_{ij}^{2}}=2\sum_{i<j}\frac{\sigma_{i}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\sigma_{i}}-\sigma_{j}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\sigma_{j}}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2}}+(n-m)\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\sigma_{i}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial\sigma_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\sigma_{i}^{2}}.

Theorem 3
.

For
p\in[1,2]
,
\pi_{p,\alpha}(M)
is superharmonic if
\alpha\in[0,nm-(1-p/2)m(m+1)-p]
.
For
p\in(0,1),

\pi_{p,\alpha}(M)
is superharmonic if
\alpha\in[0,nm-m(m+1)+(m-1)p]
.

Corollary 1
.

For
p\in[1,2]
, the (generalized) Bayes estimator with respect to
\pi_{p,\alpha}(M)
is minimax if
\alpha\in[0,nm-(1-p/2)m(m+1)-p]
.
For
p\in(0,1),
the (generalized) Bayes estimator with respect to
\pi_{p,\alpha}(M)
is minimax if
\alpha\in[0,nm-m(m+1)+(m-1)p]
.

Using Theorem
3
, the prior
\pi_{p,\alpha}
with
p=1
and
\alpha=nm-m(m+1)/2-1
is superharmonic.
We call it a nuclear norm shrinkage (NNS) prior:

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathrm{NNS}}(M)=\|M\|_{1}^{m(m+1)/2+1-nm}.

(9)

The proposed prior works well in Bayesian prediction as well.
Suppose that we observe
X\sim{\rm N}(M,I_{n},I_{m})
and predict
{Y}\sim{\rm N}(M,I_{n},I_{m})
by a predictive density
\hat{p}({Y}\mid X)
.
We evaluate predictive densities by the Kullback–Leibler loss:

\displaystyle D({p}(\cdot\mid M),\hat{p}(\cdot\mid X))=\int{p}({Y}\mid M)\log\frac{{p}({Y}\mid M)}{\hat{p}({Y}\mid X)}{\rm d}{Y}.

The Bayesian predictive density based on a prior
\pi(M)
is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{\pi}({Y}\mid X)

\displaystyle=\int{p}({Y}\mid M)\pi(M\mid X){\rm d}M,

where
\pi(M\mid X)
is the posterior distribution of
M
given
X
, and it minimizes the Bayes risk
(
1
)
.
The Bayesian predictive density with respect to the uniform prior is minimax.
However, it is inadmissible and dominated by Bayesian predictive densities based on superharmonic priors
(
10
;
6
)
.
Thus, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 2
.

For
p\in[1,2]
, the Bayesian predictive density with respect to
\pi_{p,\alpha}(M)
is minimax if
\alpha\in[0,nm-(1-p/2)m(m+1)-p]
.
For
p\in(0,1),
the Bayesian predictive density with respect to
\pi_{p,\alpha}(M)
is minimax if
\alpha\in[0,nm-m(m+1)+(m-1)p]
.

Discussion

In this paper, we proposed a class of minimax estimators for a normal mean matrix and a class of matrix norm shrinkage priors. We confirmed the effectiveness of these estimators and priors by numerical results.

In the numerical experiment presented in Figure
1
, the JS estimator and the generalized Bayes estimator based on Stein’s prior exhibit similar performance, as do the EM estimator and SVS prior. However, the NNSE estimator and NNS prior display certain differences. The differences in Frobenius risk among these estimators require future study. Note that we only proved the minimaxity of the proposed estimator

X

Appendix B
Technical Lemmas

Lemma 1
.

When
p\in(0,2]
,

(x^{p}-1)/(x^{2}-1)
is a decreasing function of
x>1
and a decreasing function of
x\in(0,1)
. Moreover,
(x^{p}-1)/(x^{2}-1)<p/2
when
x>1
and
(x^{p}-1)/(x^{2}-1)>p/2
when
x\in(0,1).

Proof.

The differential function is

\frac{(x^{p}-1)^{\prime}(x^{2}-1)-(x^{p}-1)(x^{2}-1)^{\prime}}{(x^{2}-1)^{2}}=\frac{(p-2)x^{p+1}-px^{p-1}+2x}{(x^{2}-1)^{2}}.

We define
g(x)=(p-2)x^{p}-px^{p-2}+2
. Then the differential function is
xg(x)/(x^{2}-1)^{2}
. Note that
g(1)=0
and

g^{\prime}(x)=(p-2)px^{p-1}-p(p-2)x^{p-3}=(2-p)px^{p-3}(1-x^{2}).

Thus,
g(x)\leq 0
.

Moreover,
\lim_{x\to 1}(x^{p}-1)/(x^{2}-1)=p/2
. Therefore,
(x^{p}-1)/(x^{2}-1)<p/2
when
x>1
and
(x^{p}-1)/(x^{2}-1)>p/2
when
x\in(0,1).

∎

Lemma 2
.

When
p\in[1,2]
and
\sigma_{i},\ \sigma_{j}
are different nonnegative numbers,

\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2p}-\sigma_{j}^{2p}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2}}\geq\frac{p}{2}(\sigma_{i}^{2p-2}+\sigma_{j}^{2p-2}).

(10)

Proof.

When
\sigma_{i}>\sigma_{j}
, we set
x=\sigma_{i}/\sigma_{j}
; otherwise, we set
x=\sigma_{j}/\sigma_{i}
. Dividing both sides of (
10
) by

\min(\sigma_{i},\sigma_{j})^{2p-2}
, we need only show that

\frac{x^{2p}-1}{x^{2}-1}\geq\frac{p}{2}(x^{2p-2}+1)

for any
x>1
. Define

g(x)=x^{2p}-1-p/2(x^{2p-2}+1)(x^{2}-1),

we need only show that
g(x)\geq 0
for any
x>1
. Note

g^{\prime}(x)=(2-p)px^{2p-1}-px+p(p-1)x^{2p-3}=:pxh(x),

where
h(x)=(2-p)x^{2p-2}-1+(p-1)x^{2p-4}
. Note that

h^{\prime}(x)=(2-p)(2p-2)x^{2p-3}-(p-1)(4-2p)x^{2p-5}=(2-p)(2p-2)(x^{2p-3}-x^{2p-5})\geq 0.

Thus,
h(x)\geq h(1)=0
. Thus,
g^{\prime}(x)=pxh(x)\geq 0
. Hence
g(x)\geq g(1)=0.

∎

Lemma 3
.

(Chebyshev’s sum inequality)
If
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq\cdots\geq a_{m}
and
b_{1}\leq b_{2}\leq\cdots\leq b_{m}
, then

\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}\Big)\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\Big)\geq m\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}b_{i}.

Lemma 4
.

When
p\in(0,2]
and
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m}
are different nonnegative numbers,

\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}^{p}\Big)\sum_{i<j}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{p}-\sigma_{j}^{p}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2}}\geq\frac{m}{2}\sum_{i<j}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2p}-\sigma_{j}^{2p}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2}}.

(11)

Proof.

Since swapping the values of
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m}
arbitrarily does not affect the value on either side of (
11
), we can assume without loss of generality that
\sigma_{1}>\cdots>\sigma_{m}
.
For given
i
, using Lemma
1
,

\frac{\sigma_{i}^{p}-\sigma_{j}^{p}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2}}=\sigma_{i}^{p-2}\frac{(\sigma_{j}/\sigma_{i})^{p}-1}{(\sigma_{j}/\sigma_{i})^{2}-1}

is a decreasing function of
\sigma_{j}
. Therefore, using
\sigma_{1}>\cdots>\sigma_{m}
,
(\sigma_{i}^{p}-\sigma_{j}^{p})/(\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2})
is an increasing function of
j
. For given
i
,
\sigma_{i}^{p}+\sigma_{j}^{p}
is a decreasing function of
j
. Therefore, for given
i
, using Chebyshev’s sum inequality,

\Big(\sum_{j\neq i}(\sigma_{i}^{p}+\sigma_{j}^{p})\Big)\Big(\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{p}-\sigma_{j}^{p}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2}}\Big)\geq(m-1)\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2p}-\sigma_{j}^{2p}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2}}.

(12)

For given
j
, using Lemma
1
,
(\sigma_{i}^{p}-\sigma_{j}^{p})/(\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2})
is a decreasing function of
\sigma_{i}
. Therefore, if
\sigma_{i_{1}}\geq\sigma_{i_{2}}
,

\sum_{j\neq i_{1}}(\sigma_{i_{1}}^{p}-\sigma_{j}^{p})/(\sigma_{i_{1}}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2})-\sum_{j\neq i_{2}}(\sigma_{i_{2}}^{p}-\sigma_{j}^{p})/(\sigma_{i_{2}}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2})

is nonpositive. Therefore, using
\sigma_{1}>\cdots>\sigma_{m}
,
\sum_{j\neq i}(\sigma_{i}^{p}-\sigma_{j}^{p})/(\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2})
is an increasing function of
i
. In addition,
\sum_{j\neq i}(\sigma_{i}^{p}+\sigma_{j}^{p})=\sum_{j}\sigma_{j}^{p}+(m-2)\sigma_{i}^{p}
is a decreasing function of
i
. Therefore, using Chebyshev’s sum inequality,

\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j\neq i}(\sigma_{i}^{p}+\sigma_{j}^{p})\Big)\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{p}-\sigma_{j}^{p}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2}}\Big)\geq m\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big(\sum_{j\neq i}(\sigma_{i}^{p}+\sigma_{j}^{p})\Big)\Big(\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{p}-\sigma_{j}^{p}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2}}\Big).

(13)

Combining (
12
) and (
13
), we have

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}^{p}\Big)\sum_{i<j}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{p}-\sigma_{j}^{p}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4(m-1)}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j\neq i}(\sigma_{i}^{p}+\sigma_{j}^{p})\Big)\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{p}-\sigma_{j}^{p}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{m}{4(m-1)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Big(\sum_{j\neq i}(\sigma_{i}^{p}+\sigma_{j}^{p})\Big)\Big(\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{p}-\sigma_{j}^{p}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{m}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2p}-\sigma_{j}^{2p}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2}}=\frac{m}{2}\sum_{i<j}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2p}-\sigma_{j}^{2p}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2}}.

∎

Lemma 5
.

When
p\in[1,2]
and
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m}
are different nonnegative numbers,

\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}^{p}\Big)\sum_{i<j}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{p}-\sigma_{j}^{p}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2}}\geq\frac{pm(m-1)}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}^{2p-2}.

Proof.

Combining Lemmas
2
and
4
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}^{p}\Big)\sum_{i<j}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{p}-\sigma_{j}^{p}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{m}{2}\sum_{i<j}\frac{\sigma_{i}^{2p}-\sigma_{j}^{2p}}{\sigma_{i}^{2}-\sigma_{j}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{pm}{4}\sum_{i<j}(\sigma_{i}^{2p-2}+\sigma_{j}^{2p-2})=\frac{pm(m-1)}{4}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sigma_{i}^{2p-2}.

∎

Flexible String Model of Unsaturated Lipid Bilayer
Boris Kheyfets, Sergei Mukhin

Abstract
An analytically solvable model of unsaturated lipid bilayer is derived by introducing finite bending angle of the unsaturated bond relative to straight part of the lipid chain considered previously in our model of semi-flexible strings. It is found that lateral pressure profile of unsaturated lipids has distinct maximum in the unsaturated bond region due to enhanced excluded volume effect caused by the bent bond, leading to an increase of entropic repulsion between the lipid chains. Simultaneously, just away from the unsaturated bond some parts of the neighbouring lipid chains have less probability to collide by geometrical reasons, causing depletion of entropic repulsion relative to saturated lipid chains case and resulting in the local minima of the lateral pressure profile surrounding the maximum at the bent unsaturated bond. Lipid chain order parameter, lateral pressure profile, and area per lipid are computed for POPC and compared with those for DPPC lipid bilayer.

I
Introduction

Consider lipid bilayers that differ only by lipid chain unsaturation: one bilayer consists of saturated lipids, and the other one of unsaturated lipids. Compared to saturated lipid bilayer, unsaturated one would have larger area per lipid (at the same temperature)
[
1
,
2
]
, lower gel phase transition temperature
[
1
]
, a drop in the chains order parameter
[
3
,
4
]
, and a different lateral pressure profile
[
3
]
. In this paper we reproduce all these characteristic features by making minimal, but pivotal changes to our previously developed analytically solvable flexible string model
[
5
]
of saturated lipid bilayer. The changes are two. The principal one is made in the self-consistent calculation procedure: lipid unsaturation is introduced by allowing only bent chain conformations at the double bond position, while the others are filtered out from the summation over lipid chain conformations in the partition function of the lipid bilayer. The second change lies in a slight (
\leq 10\%
) tuning of the flexible-string model parameter for incompressible chain’s cross-section area of unsaturated lipid relative to saturated one. The latter was done to match equilibrium area per lipid for the unsaturated bilayer found in
[
1
,
2
]
.

For reference we used DPPC (16:0) lipids for saturated bilayer, and POPC (18:1/16:0) lipids for unsaturated bilayer. These two cases were chosen as ones of the most well-characterized lipids. POPC is monounsaturated lipid; poly-unsaturated lipids can also be modelled within our model approach, however here we have concentrated solely on DPPC-POPC bilayer differences.

We start with a short overview of the flexible string model for saturated lipid bilayer, including liquid-gel phase transition visible in a free energy plot. These calculations mostly reproduce our previous work
[
6
]
with minor simplifications. We then introduce lipid chains unsaturation via specially developed conformational filtering procedure in partition function of the lipids system, which is followed by derivations of analytical expressions for the order parameter and lateral pressure profile. Finally, we apply these approaches to DPPC and POPC models and discuss the results.

For a concrete approach we use a model of a single lipid in the mean-field potential that arises from entropic repulsion between fluctuating neighbouring lipid chains due to excluded volume effect
[
5
]
. The minimal model consists of a flexible string in a parabolic mean-field potential, see Fig.
2
. The energy functional of the string consists of kinetic energy, bending energy, and energy arising from the confining mean-field potential:

E_{t}=\int\limits_{0}^{L}\left[\frac{\rho\dot{\mathbf{R}}^{2}}{2}+\frac{K_{f}(\mathbf{R}^{\prime\prime})^{2}}{2}+\frac{B\mathbf{R}^{2}}{2}\right]{\,\mathrm{d}z}\;,

(1)

here
{\bf{R}}(z)=\{R_{x}(z),R_{y}(z)\}
is a vector in the plane of the membrane giving deviation of a string from the
z
axis (see Fig.
2
),
\rho
is a string linear density,
K_{f}
is a string bending rigidity, and
B
is self-consistently derived parameter of entropic repulsion strength, that characterises the parabolic mean-field potential as function of a deviation of the string centres from
z
-axis,
|\mathbf{R}(z)|
,
L
is a hydrophobic thickness of the monolayer.

Let’s find expression for the free energy with the energy functional
E_{t}
, Eq.
1
. For this, we impose boundary conditions on the
\mathbf{R}
, this in turn allows us to represent
E_{t}
in the operator form, and finally express free energy
F_{t}
in terms of eigenvalues of that operator.

Boundary conditions for a model flexible string take into account the following physical assumptions (same is assumed also for component
R_{y}(z)
):

\displaystyle R_{x}^{\prime}(0)=0-\text{chain director is parallel to}

the monolayer normal at membrane surface;

\displaystyle R_{x}^{\prime\prime\prime}(0)=0-\text{net force at chain's head is zero;}

(2)

\displaystyle R_{x}^{\prime\prime}(L)=0-\text{net torque at chain free end is zero;}

\displaystyle R_{x}^{\prime\prime\prime}(L)=0-\text{net force at chain free end is zero.}

The first boundary condition reflects the orientational asymmetry of the monolayer due to the water-lipid interface which is clearly seen from data on the molecules orientational order parameter
[
7
,
8
]
: lipid tails are more ordered in the vicinity of head-groups constrained by the hydrophobic tension. Yet, the tilt transition when director forms finite angle with the monolayer normal is not considered here
[
kheyfets_tilt_2019
]
. The other boundary conditions reflect the freely moving lipid head-group and hydrocarbon tail end: zero force acting on the head-group and zero torque and force acting on the lipid tail end.

Under the boundary conditions Eq. (
2
) potential energy part of the functional Eq. (
1
) can be equivalently rewritten in terms of linear Hermitian operator
\hat{H}=B+K_{f}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{4}}
in the form:

\displaystyle E_{t(\text{pot})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=x,y}E_{\alpha}

(3)

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}\int\limits^{L}_{0}\left[R_{\alpha}(z)\hat{H}R_{\alpha}(z)\right]{\,\mathrm{d}z}

Then, an arbitrary conformation of the chain can be expressed as the deviation of the centers of the string,
R_{x,y}(z)
, from the straight vertical line (see Fig.
2
), and is parameterized by a set of coefficients
C_{n}
of the linear decomposition of the function
R_{x,y}(z)
over the eigenfunctions
R_{n}(z)
of the operator
\hat{H}
:

\displaystyle R_{\alpha=x,y}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}C_{n,\alpha}R_{n}(z)

(4)

\displaystyle\hat{H}R_{n}(z)

\displaystyle=E_{n}R_{n}(z)

Number of
C_{n}
coefficients is related to the number of
CH_{2}
groups
N_{\mathrm{max}}
in the single chain of the lipid.

Substituting Eq. (
4
) into Eq. (
1
) and using the standard orthogonality property of the eigenfunctions of operator
\hat{H}
, enables a simple decomposition of the energy functional into the series:

E_{t}=\sum_{n}\frac{1}{2}\left\{\rho\dot{C}_{n}^{2}+E_{n}C_{n}^{2}\right\}

(5)

Hence, Eq. (
5
) reduces the problem of calculating the free energy of the spring to a sum of free energies of non-interecting 1-dimensional harmonic oscillators with ”spring rigidities”
E_{n}
and ”coordinates”
C_{n}
. The corresponding eigenvalues
E_{n}
and orthonormalized eigenfunctions
R_{n}(z)
of the operator
\hat{H}
are
[
5
]
:

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} E_{0}&=B\\
R_{0}(z)&=\sqrt{\frac{1}{L}}\end{aligned}\right.

(6)

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\in[1..N_{\mathrm{max}}]

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &E_{n}=B+c_{n}^{4}\frac{K_{f}}{L^{4}};\;c_{n}=\pi n-\pi/4;\\
&R_{n}(z)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\left[\cos\left(c_{n}\frac{z}{L}\right)+\frac{\cos(c_{n})}{\cosh(c_{n})}\cosh\left(c_{n}\frac{z}{L}\right)\right]\end{aligned}\right.

Arbitrary chain conformation is thus :

R_{x}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\mathrm{max}}}R_{n}(z)C_{n}
. Due to normalization,
R_{n}(z)
dimensionality is
\sim L^{-1/2}
, and hence dimensionality of
C_{n}\sim L^{3/2}
.
Assuming membrane to be locally isotropic in the lateral plane, we split partition function into a product of two equal components,
Z=Z_{x}Z_{y}=Z_{x}^{2}
, and thus free energy of the lateral oscillations of the chain equals to
[
9
]
:

F_{t}=-2k_{B}T\log Z_{x};\;Z_{x}=\prod_{n}^{N_{\mathrm{max}}}\frac{k_{B}T}{\hbar\omega_{n}};\;\omega_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{E_{n}}{\rho}}\;.

(7)

In order to see how
F_{t}
depends on the area per lipid,
A
(see Fig.
2
), we note that expression
\partial F_{t}/\partial B
can be computed in two ways - directly via Eqs.(
6
,
7
):

\frac{\partial F_{t}}{\partial B}=k_{B}T\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\mathrm{max}}}\frac{1}{B+c_{n}^{4}\frac{K_{f}}{L^{4}}}\;,

(8)

and also by representing partition function,
Z_{x}
as path integral over all chain conformations,
Z_{x}=\int\exp(-E_{tx}(R_{x})/k_{B}T)DR_{x}
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{t}}{\partial B}=-2k_{B}T\frac{\partial Z_{x}}{\partial B}{Z_{x}}^{-1}=

\displaystyle=\frac{\int\left[\int\limits_{0}^{L}R_{x}^{2}(z)\;\mathrm{d}z\right]\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-E_{tx}\{R_{x}(z)\}}{k_{B}T}}{\,\mathrm{D}R_{x}}}{\int\mathrm{e}^{\frac{-E_{tx}\{R_{x}(z)\}}{k_{B}T}}{\,\mathrm{D}R_{x}}}=

\displaystyle=\left\langle\int\limits_{0}^{L}R_{x}^{2}(z)\;\mathrm{d}z\right\rangle=L\langle R^{2}\rangle\;,

(9)

here
\mathrm{D}
denotes path integration. Hence, Eq.(
9
) shows that
\partial F_{t}/\partial B
is proportional to the mean area swept by the centers of the chain. The latter area is also related to the mean area swept by the chain,
A
, and incompressible area of the chain,
A_{n}
, as:
(\sqrt{A/\pi}-\sqrt{A_{n}/\pi})^{2}
. Hence we conclude that:

\frac{\partial F_{t}}{\partial B}=L\frac{\left(\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{A_{n}}\right)^{2}}{\pi}

(10)

Comparing Eq.(
8
) with Eq.(
10
) one finds:

k_{B}T\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\mathrm{max}}}\frac{1}{B+c_{n}^{4}\frac{K_{f}}{L^{4}}}=L\frac{\left(\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{A_{n}}\right)^{2}}{\pi}

(11)

We conclude that
B
is a monotonically decreasing function of
A
, and hence
F_{t}
is also monotonically decreasing function of
A
(see Eqs. (
6
,
7
)). Eq.(
11
) constitutes self-consistency equation as it relates mean-field parameter,
B
, with the area per lipid,
A
.

Another contribution to the string’s free energy is surface energy,
F_{s}=\gamma A
, where
\gamma
is a hydrophobic tension of the lipid membrane, and
A
is mean area swept by the string. Thus,
F_{s}
is monotonically increasing function of mean area swept by the chain.
F_{t}+F_{s}
has a single minimum as a function of
A
, while two minima are needed in order to reproduce liquid to gel phase transition. Hence, in order to get liquid-gel first-order phase transition we need to add one more contribution to the free energy. That contribution is van der Waals interaction between the neighbouring lipids.

Analytical expression for attractive van der Waals interaction between two hydrocarbon chains has been calculated in
[
10
]
on the basis of quantum mechanics:

F_{VdW}=-U\frac{3\pi}{8L}\frac{N_{CH_{2}}^{2}}{D^{5}}

(12)

here
U
is an interaction constant, and
D
is a lipid’s cross-section diameter.

In the equilibritum free energy of the chain,
F_{T}=F_{t}+F_{s}+F_{VdW}
, is minimal, hence we require that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial F_{T}}{\partial A}=0

(13)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad k_{B}T\frac{\partial B}{\partial A}\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\mathrm{max}}}\frac{1}{E_{n}}+\gamma+\frac{5}{2}\frac{{\tilde{U}}N_{CH_{2}}^{2}}{LA^{7/2}}=0

Here we used renormalized van der Waals coefficient
\tilde{U}=9\pi^{7/2}U/2^{8}
which arises from using area per lipid instead of lipid diameter, and additional factor of 3 accounts for the fact that every lipid is surrounded by other lipids, assuming a tightly packed lattice, which has a coordination number 6.

Solving minimum energy condition, Eq.(
13
), together with self-consistency equation, Eq.(
11
), for
A
and
B
allows to find equilibrium area per lipid at the free energy minima, see Figs.
3
and
4
. We used the following input parameters: incompressible area
A_{n}=20
Å
2
,
L=15
Å,
\gamma=19.37
erg/cm
2
, string bending rigidity
K_{f}=k_{B}TL/2
,
U=761
kcal Å
6
/mol,
N_{CH_{2}}=16
number of
CH_{2}
groups per chain,
N_{\mathrm{max}}=10
as a maximal number of oscillation modes of the string.

Calculation

II.1
Flexible String Model of Unsaturated Lipid

Let us introduce unsaturated bond in the lipid chain by allowing only bent chain conformations in the partition function of the string. Dropping conformation independent kinetic energy contributions, one finds:

\displaystyle Z_{x}\equiv Z^{1}_{x}+Z^{2}_{x}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\prod_{n}^{N_{\mathrm{max}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{C_{n}^{2}E_{n}}{2k_{B}T}}\cdot\delta\left(R_{x}^{\prime}(z_{0})-C\right){\mathrm{d}C_{n}}+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\prod_{n}^{N_{\mathrm{max}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{C_{n}^{2}E_{n}}{2k_{B}T}}\cdot\delta\left(R_{x}^{\prime}(z_{0})+C\right){\mathrm{d}C_{n}}

(14)

Here Dirac delta function
\delta\left(R_{x}^{\prime}(z_{0})\pm C\right)
filters out string conformations that do not have a particular bending angle at the depth coordinate
z_{0}
inside the bilayer , i.e. at the position of the double bond.
R_{x}^{\prime}(z_{0})=\tan(\alpha[z_{0}])=\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\mathrm{max}}}C_{n}R_{n}^{\prime}(z_{0})=\pm C
, and
\alpha[z_{0}]
is tangent angle of the chain at the double bond,
1/2
takes into account that the chain is bent in either of two sides, i.e.
\tan(\alpha[z_{0}])=\pm C
correspondently, with the same probability.

For self-consistency equation, Eq.(
10

that include

\displaystyle Z^{1}_{x}=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\mathrm{max}}}\frac{C_{n}^{2}E_{n}}{2k_{B}T}}\cdot\delta\left(\sum_{n=0}^{N_{\mathrm{max}}}C_{n}R_{n}^{\prime}(z_{0})-C\right)\times

\displaystyle\prod_{n=0}^{N_{\mathrm{max}}}\frac{\mathrm{d}{C}_{n}}{L^{3/2}}

(15)

where

is introduced to keep the corresponding

part dimensionless. One has to use the following trick to calculate the above path-integral. Namely, the Dirac delta function is expressed using the well known identity:

\delta(x)=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{ixk}\frac{dk}{2\pi}

(16)

Hence, substituting the Dirac delta function in Eq.(
15
) by identity from Eq.(
16
) and integrating first over all the
\prod_{n=0}^{N_{\mathrm{max}}}{\mathrm{d}{C}_{n}}
and then over
dk
, one finds:

\displaystyle Z^{1}_{x}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\prod_{n}\frac{1}{\sqrt{E_{n}L^{3}}}\right]\cdot\exp\left\{-\frac{C^{2}}{2k_{B}T\sum\limits_{n}\frac{\left[R_{n}^{\prime}(z_{0})\right]^{2}}{E_{n}}}\right\}\times

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{\sum\limits_{n}\frac{\left[R_{n}^{\prime}(z_{0})\right]^{2}}{E_{n}}}}=Z^{2}_{x};\;Z_{x}=2Z^{1}_{x}

(17)

where the last equality in Eq. (
17

Z_{x}^{1}
depends only on
C^{2}
, and hence
Z^{1}_{x}=Z^{2}_{x}=1/2Z_{x}
.
Substituting this into Eq.(
7
) one finds:

\displaystyle F_{t}=k_{B}T\ln\left(\left[\prod\limits_{n}E_{n}L^{3}\right]\sum\limits_{n}\frac{\left[R_{n}^{\prime}(z_{0})\right]^{2}}{E_{n}}\right)+

\displaystyle+\frac{C^{2}}{\sum\limits_{n}\frac{\left[R_{n}^{\prime}(z_{0})\right]^{2}}{E_{n}}}

(18)

Self-consistency equation for unsaturated lipids then reads:

\displaystyle k_{B}T\left(\sum\limits_{n}\frac{1}{B+c_{n}^{4}\frac{K_{f}}{L^{4}}}\right)=L\frac{(\sqrt{A}-\sqrt{A_{n}})^{2}}{\pi}+

\displaystyle+\frac{\sum\limits_{n}\frac{[R_{n}^{\prime}(z_{0})]^{2}}{E_{n}^{2}}}{\sum\limits_{n}\frac{[R_{n}^{\prime}(z_{0})]^{2}}{E_{n}}}\left[k_{B}T-\frac{C^{2}}{\sum\limits_{n}\frac{[R_{n}^{\prime}(z_{0})]^{2}}{E_{n}}}\right]

(compare with Eq.(
11

\frac{\partial F_{T}}{\partial A}=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad k_{B}T\frac{\partial B}{\partial A}\left\{\left(\sum\limits_{n}\frac{1}{E_{n}}\right)-\frac{\sum\limits_{n}\frac{[R_{n}^{\prime}(z_{0})]^{2}}{E_{n}^{2}}}{\sum\limits_{n}\frac{[R_{n}^{\prime}(z_{0})]^{2}}{E_{n}}}\left[1-\frac{C^{2}K_{f}}{k_{B}TL\sum\limits_{n}\frac{[R_{n}^{\prime}(z_{0})]^{2}}{E_{n}}}\right]\right\}+\gamma+\frac{5}{2}\frac{{\tilde{U}}N^{2}}{LA^{7/2}}=0

(compare with Eq.(

II.2
Lateral Pressure Profile

Lateral pressure profile is a function
\Pi(z)
which is equal to hydrophobic tension,
\gamma
, when integrated over the thickness of the monolayer:

\int_{0}^{L}\Pi(z){\,\mathrm{d}z}=\gamma

(21)

Comparing this with equilibrium condition, Eq.(
13
) or Eq.(
20
), we see that it has two components – one is due to entropic repulsion,
F_{t}
, and the other is due to van der Waals attraction,
F_{VdW}
:

\int_{0}^{L}\Pi(z){\,\mathrm{d}z}={-\frac{\partial F_{t}}{\partial A}}\quad{-\frac{5}{2}\frac{{\tilde{U}}N_{CH_{2}}^{2}}{LA^{7/2}}}

(22)

For saturated lipids, lateral pressure profile due to entropic repulsion is

\int_{0}^{L}\Pi_{t}(z){\,\mathrm{d}z}=-\frac{\partial F_{t}}{\partial A}=-k_{B}T\sum_{n=0}\frac{1}{E_{n}}\frac{\partial E_{n}}{\partial A}

(23)

By definition,
E_{n}
and
R_{n}(z)
are eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of operator
\hat{H}
in Eq.
3
:
\hat{H}R_{n}(z)=E_{n}R_{n}(z)
. Hence we write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}R_{n}{\hat{H}}R_{n}{\,\mathrm{d}z}=\int_{0}^{L}R_{n}E_{n}R_{n}{\,\mathrm{d}z}\equiv E_{n}\int_{0}^{L}R_{n}^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}z}

(24)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad\frac{\partial E_{n}}{\partial A}=\frac{\partial E_{n}}{\partial A}\int_{0}^{L}R_{n}^{2}{\,\mathrm{d}z}

Comparing this with Eq.(
23
) we conclude that

\displaystyle\Pi_{t}(z)

\displaystyle=-k_{B}T\sum_{n=0}\frac{1}{E_{n}}{\frac{\partial E_{n}}{\partial A}}R_{n}^{2}(z)

(25)

\displaystyle\equiv-k_{B}T\frac{\partial B}{\partial A}\sum_{n=0}\frac{R_{n}^{2}(z)}{E_{n}}{\frac{\partial E_{n}}{\partial B}}

\displaystyle=-k_{B}T\frac{\partial B}{\partial A}\sum_{n=0}\frac{R_{n}^{2}(z)}{E_{n}}

Similarly, for unsaturated lipids, one arrives at the following expression for lateral pressure profile:

\Pi_{t}(z)=-k_{B}T\frac{\partial B}{\partial A}\sum_{n=0}R_{n}^{2}(z)\left[\frac{1}{E_{n}}-\frac{\frac{[R_{n}^{\prime}(z_{0})]^{2}}{E_{n}^{2}}}{\sum\limits_{m}\frac{[R_{m}^{\prime}(z_{0})]^{2}}{E_{m}}}\left\{1-\frac{C^{2}K_{f}}{k_{B}TL\sum\limits_{m}\frac{[R_{m}^{\prime}(z_{0})]^{2}}{E_{m}}}\right\}\right]

(26)

Lateral pressure profile plots for saturated and unsaturated lipids are plotted in Fig.
6
. We see that lateral pressure peak at the midplane area (
z/L=1
) becomes smaller with unsaturation. Lateral pressure profile was studied in
[
3
]
by means of molecular dynamics: in Fig. 7 of their paper
[
3
]
the authors plot lateral pressure profile of lipids with increasing unsaturation and also find that the peak at the midplane area diminishes with unsaturation.

II.3
Order Parameter

By definition, molecular order parameter is

S_{\mathrm{mol}}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\left(3\langle\cos^{2}\theta(z)\rangle-1\right)

(27)

In flexible string model we have an approximation for tangent:

\tan\theta(z)\approx R_{x}^{\prime}=\sum C_{n}R_{n}^{\prime}(z)

(28)

hence we express
\cos\theta
via
\tan\theta
as:

\cos^{2}\theta=\frac{1}{1+\tan^{2}{\theta}}

(29)

Also note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+a(z)^{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-x}\left[\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}a(z)x}+\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}a(z)x}\right]{\,\mathrm{d}x}

(30)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\left\langle\frac{1}{1+a(z)^{2}}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-x}\left[\langle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}a(z)x}\rangle+\langle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}a(z)x}\rangle\right]\mathrm{d}x

Thus, intermediate expression for the order parameter is

S_{\mathrm{mol}}(z)=\frac{1}{2}\left[3\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-x}\left[\left\langle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}x\sum C_{n}R_{n}^{\prime}(z)}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}x\sum C_{n}R_{n}^{\prime}(z)}\right\rangle\right]{\,\mathrm{d}x}-1\right]

(31)

Starting with saturated lipids, we write:

\left\langle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\cdot\sum C_{n}R_{n}^{\prime}(z)\cdot x}\right\rangle=\frac{\int\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\cdot\sum C_{n}R_{n}^{\prime}(z)\cdot x}\cdot\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\sum C_{n}^{2}E_{n}}{2k_{B}T}}\prod{\,\mathrm{d}C_{n}}}{\int\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\sum C_{n}^{2}E_{n}}{2k_{B}T}}\prod{\,\mathrm{d}C_{n}}}

(32)

The numerator integral here is:

\int\mathrm{e}^{-\sum\limits_{n}\frac{E_{n}}{2k_{B}T}\left[C_{n}^{2}-2C_{n}\mathrm{i}xR_{n}^{\prime}\frac{k_{B}T}{E_{n}}+\left(\mathrm{i}xR_{n}^{\prime}\frac{k_{B}T}{E_{n}}\right)^{2}-\left(\mathrm{i}xR_{n}^{\prime}\frac{k_{B}T}{E_{n}}\right)^{2}\right]}\prod{\,\mathrm{d}C_{n}}=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{x^{2}k_{B}T}{2}\sum\limits_{n}\frac{(R_{n}^{\prime})^{2}}{E_{n}}}\prod\sqrt{\frac{\pi\cdot 2k_{B}T}{E_{n}}}

(33)

The denominator integral is the same, but with
x=0
. Hence, one obtains:

\left\langle\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\cdot\sum C_{n}R_{n}^{\prime}(z)\cdot x}\right\rangle=\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{x^{2}k_{B}T}{2}\sum\limits_{n}\frac{(R_{n}^{\prime})^{2}}{E_{n}}}

(34)

III
Discussion

To summarise, we have modelled unsaturated POPC lipid in our flexible strings model by allowing only strings conformations that possess fixed nonzero tangent angle of the double bond with respect to preceding it saturated bonds at some definite position
z_{0}
inside the bilayer, e.g.
\tan[\alpha(z_{0}=0.5L)]=0.4,0.6,...
etc. This results in larger area per lipid compared to saturated lipids, as e.g. in DPPC lipid, and produces lateral pressure profiles and order parameter distributions that match qualitatively the ones reported in
[
3
]
. The most important physics conclusion that follows from our results is obvious from Figs.
6
,
10
. Namely, the lateral pressure profiles of unsaturated lipids near the unsaturated bonds go beyond saturated lipids profile, as would be expected from considerations of excluded volume effect on the entropic repulsion between the lipid chains. The unsaturated bond has finite bending angle with respect to straight line of the lipid chain and, therefore, increases locally the lipid occupied area, thus causing stronger entropic repulsion between the lipid chains. Simultaneously, away from the unsaturated (bent) bond the neighbouring lipid chains have less probability to collide just by geometrical reasons, and therefore, the entropic repulsion between them is depleted relative to saturated lipid chains case, thus causing relative decrease of the lateral pressure profile with respect to saturated lipids case in these same regions away from the bent unsaturated bond, as is seen in Figs.
6
,
10
.

In support of the above reasoning we also have checked predictions of our model for the cases of unsaturated double bond with different values of the bending angle and with different positions of the unsaturated bond along the lipid chains.

Let’s start with different values of the double bond angle. Presented here Fig.
8
shows lateral pressure profiles for various values of the fixed double bond tangent angle calculated in our model, see Eq. (
26

25

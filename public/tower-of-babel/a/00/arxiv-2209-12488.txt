On the mean curvature type flow for convex capillary hypersurfaces in the ball
Yingxiang Hu, Yong Wei, Bo Yang, Tailong Zhou

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the mean curvature type flow for hypersurfaces in the unit Euclidean ball with capillary boundary, which was introduced by Wang-Xia [ Wang-Xia2019 ] and Wang-Weng [ WW2020 ] . We show that if the initial hypersurface is strictly convex, then the solution of this flow is strictly convex for t > 0 t>0 , exists for all positive time and converges smoothly to a spherical cap. As an application, we prove a family of new Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities for convex hypersurfaces in the unit Euclidean ball with capillary boundary.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in the mean curvature type flow for hypersurfaces in the unit Euclidean ball with capillary boundary, which was introduced recently by Wang-Xia
[
Wang-Xia2019
]
and Wang-Weng
[
WW2020
]
. We first describe some definitions and notations for such hypersurfaces. The readers are referred to
[
Weng-Xia2022
]
for more details. Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
be a properly embedded smooth hypersurface in the unit Euclidean ball
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
, given by an embedding
x:\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
satisfying

\displaystyle\Sigma=x(\mathbb{B}^{n})\subset\mathbb{B}^{n+1},\quad\partial\Sigma=x(\partial\mathbb{B}^{n})\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Let
\bar{N}
be the unit outward normal of
\mathbb{S}^{n}=\partial\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
, and
\nu
be a smooth choice of the unit normal of
\Sigma
. For
\theta\in(0,\pi)
, we call that
\Sigma
has
\theta
-capillary boundary
\partial\Sigma
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
if
\Sigma
intersects
\mathbb{S}^{n}
at the constant contact angle
\theta
, that is,

\langle\bar{N}\circ x,\nu\rangle=-\cos\theta,\quad\text{along $\partial\mathbb{B}^{n}$}.

In particular, if
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
, i.e.,
\Sigma
intersects
\mathbb{S}^{n}
orthogonally, we call that
\Sigma
has free boundary. Two model examples are the spherical cap of radius
r
around a constant unit vector
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\theta
-capillary boundary, given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle C_{\theta,r}(e):=\left\{x\in\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}:\left|x-\sqrt{r^{2}+2r\cos\theta+1}e\right|=r\right\},

and the flat ball around
e
with
\theta
-capillary boundary, given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle C_{\theta,\infty}(e):=\{x\in\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}:\langle x,e\rangle=\cos\theta\}.

Denote the principal curvatures of
\Sigma
by
\kappa=(\kappa_{1},\cdots,\kappa_{n})
. For
k=1,\cdots,n
, we denote by
H_{k}
the normalized
k
th mean curvature of
\Sigma
, which is defined as the normalized
k
th elementary symmetric polynomial of
\kappa
:

H_{k}=\binom{n}{k}^{-1}\sigma_{k}(\kappa),\quad k=1,\cdots,n.

We also denote the mean curvature as
H=nH_{1}
. We say that
\Sigma
is convex (resp. strictly convex) if its principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}\geq 0
(resp.
\kappa_{i}>0
). In this paper, we always assume that
\Sigma
is convex. Denote by
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
the convex body in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
enclosed by
\partial\Sigma
, and
\widehat{\Sigma}
the convex domain in
\overline{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
enclosed by
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}
and
\Sigma
. See Figure
1
. We choose the unit normal
\nu
of
\Sigma
as the one pointing outward of
\widehat{\Sigma}
. For each
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, the smooth vector field
X_{e}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
defined by

(1.3)

X_{e}=\langle x,e\rangle x-\frac{1}{2}(|x|^{2}+1)e

is a conformal Killing vector field in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and its restriction on
\partial\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
is a tangential vector field on
\partial\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
(see
[
Wang-Xia2019-2
, Prop. 3.1]
). Using
X_{e}
, the following Minkowski type formula (see
[
Weng-Xia2022
, Prop. 2.8]
) holds:

(1.4)

\int_{\Sigma}{H_{k-1}\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle}\,dA=\int_{\Sigma}{H_{k}\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle}\,dA,\quad k=1,\cdots,n.

Based on the formula (
1.4
), the following mean curvature type flow with
\theta
-capillary boundary was introduced by Wang and Xia
[
Wang-Xia2019
]
for the case
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
, and later by Wang and Weng
[
WW2020
]
for general case
\theta\in(0,\pi)
:

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(\partial_{t}x\right)^{\bot}&=\Big(n\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle-H\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle\Big)\nu&\text{in}\quad\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,T),\\
\langle\bar{N}\circ x,\nu\rangle&=-\cos\theta&\text{on}\quad\partial\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,T),\\
x(\cdot,0)&=x_{0}(\cdot)&\text{on}\quad\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n},\end{aligned}\right.

where
(\cdot)^{\bot}
denotes the projection to the normal bundle of
\Sigma
. Denote
\Sigma_{t}=x(\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n},t)
as the solution of the flow (
1.5
). The flow (
1.5
) has a property that the volume
|\widehat{\Sigma_{t}}|
is preserved which follows from (
1.4
) for
k=1
. The convergence results
[
Wang-Xia2019
,
WW2020
]
of the flow (
1.5
) for star-shaped initial hypersurface
\Sigma
, i.e.,
\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle>0
holds everywhere on
\Sigma
, can be stated as follows.

Theorem A
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
be a properly embedded smooth hypersurface with
\theta
-capillary boundary, given by an embedding:
x:\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
. Assume that
\Sigma
is star-shaped with respect to
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, i.e.,
\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle>0
holds everywhere in
\Sigma
.

(i)

[
Wang-Xia2019
]
If
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
, then the solution
\Sigma_{t}
of the flow (
1.5
) exists for all positive time and converges smoothly to a spherical cap with free boundary as
t\rightarrow\infty
, whose enclosed domain has the same volume as
|\widehat{\Sigma}|
.

(ii)

[
WW2020
]
If
\theta
satisfies
|\cos\theta|<\frac{3n+1}{5n-1}
, then the solution
\Sigma_{t}
of the flow (
1.5
) exists for all positive time and subsequently converges smoothly to a spherical cap with
\theta
-capillary boundary as
t\rightarrow\infty
, whose enclosed domain has the same volume as
|\widehat{\Sigma}|
.

The first aim of this paper is to prove the preservation of the strict convexity along the flow (
1.5
). The main tool is the tensor maximum principle in Theorem
4.1
, which was developed by the authors in
[
HWYZ2022
]
. With help of the convexity preserving, we establish the convergence result of the flow (
1.5
) for convex hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary in the unit Euclidean ball.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
be a properly embedded, strictly convex smooth hypersurface with
\theta
-capillary boundary (
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
), given by an embedding:
x:\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
. Then there exists a constant unit vector
e\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
such that
\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle>0
holds everywhere on
\Sigma
, and the solution
\Sigma_{t}
of the flow (
1.5
) starting from
\Sigma
is strictly convex and exists for all time
t>0
. Moreover,
\Sigma_{t}
converges smoothly to a spherical cap
C_{\theta,r_{\infty}}(e)
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, where
r_{\infty}
is determined by
|\widehat{C_{\theta,r_{\infty}}}(e)|=|\widehat{\Sigma}|
.

The mean curvature type flow (
1.5
) is a locally constrained curvature flow, which was previously considered by Guan and Li
[
GL-2015
]
for closed hypersurfaces in space forms. Other kinds of locally constrained curvature flows have been investigated by many authors, see
[
BGL
,
GLW-2019
,
Lambert-Scheuer2021
,
Sch21
,
SX-2019
,
HLW2020
,
GL-2021
,
Wei-X2022a
,
Wei-X2022b
]
, etc. A main motivation of studying these locally constrained curvature flows is their powerful applications in proving geometric inequalities including the Alexandrov-Fenchel type inequalities for quermassintegrals.

The quermassintegrals for convex hypersurfaces with
\theta
-capillary boundary in
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
were first introduced by Scheuer, Wang and Xia
[
Scheuer-Wang-Xia2018
]
for
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
, and later by Weng and Xia
[
Weng-Xia2022
]
for
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
be a properly embedded, convex smooth hypersurface with
\theta
-capillary boundary
\partial\Sigma\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
, where
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. Then the quermassintegrals
W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
for
\widehat{\Sigma}
are defined by (
2.2
)–(
2.4
). In particular,

\displaystyle W_{0,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=

\displaystyle|\widehat{\Sigma}|,\qquad W_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=\frac{1}{n+1}\left(|\Sigma|-\cos\theta|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|\right).

For strictly convex hypersurfaces in
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
with
\theta
-capillary boundary, the following flow

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(\partial_{t}x\right)^{\bot}&=\left(\frac{\langle x,e\rangle+\cos\theta\langle\nu,e\rangle}{H_{n}/H_{n-1}}-\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle\right)\nu&\text{in}\quad\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,T),\\
\langle\bar{N}\circ x,\nu\rangle&=-\cos\theta&\text{on}\quad\partial\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,T),\\
x(\cdot,0)&=x_{0}(\cdot)&\text{on}\quad\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n},\end{aligned}\right.

was studied in
[
Scheuer-Wang-Xia2018
,
Weng-Xia2022
]
, and as an application they proved that for any
\ell=0,1,\cdots,n-1
, there holds

(1.7)

W_{n,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})\geq(f_{n}\circ f_{\ell}^{-1})(W_{\ell,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})),

where
f_{\ell}(r)
is the strictly increasing function given by
f_{\ell}(r)=W_{\ell,\theta}(\widehat{C_{\theta,r}})
. Equality holds in (
1.7
) if and only if
\Sigma
is a spherical cap or a flat ball with
\theta
-capillary boundary.

As an application of our Theorem
1.1
, we prove new sharp Alexandrov-Fenchel type inequalities relating
W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
and
W_{0,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
for
k\geq 1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
be a properly embedded, convex smooth hypersurface in the unit ball with
\theta
-capillary boundary, where
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. Then for any
k=1,\cdots,n-1
, there holds

(1.8)

W_{k,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})\geq(f_{k}\circ f_{0}^{-1})(W_{0,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})).

Equality holds in (
1.8
) if and only if
\Sigma
is a spherical cap or a flat ball with
\theta
-capillary boundary.

.

The inequality (
1.8
) for

is the inequality (
1.7

[

2.2.
Quermassintegrals

The quermassintegrals for
\theta
-capillary hypersurfaces in the unit ball were first introduced for
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
by Scheuer, Wang and Xia
[
Scheuer-Wang-Xia2018
]
, and later generalized by Weng and Xia
[
Weng-Xia2022
]
to
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be a smooth convex, embedded hypersurface with
\theta
-capillary boundary
\partial\Sigma\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
. The quermassintegrals for
\widehat{\Sigma}
are defined by

(2.2)

\displaystyle W_{0,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=

\displaystyle|\widehat{\Sigma}|,

(2.3)

\displaystyle W_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n+1}\left(|\Sigma|-\cos\theta W_{0}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})\right),

and for
1\leq k\leq n-1
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &W_{k+1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=\frac{1}{n+1}\left\{\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}dA-\cos\theta\sin^{k}\theta W_{k}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})\right.\\
&\quad\quad\quad\left.-\cos^{k-1}\theta\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}\frac{(-1)^{k+\ell}}{n-\ell}\binom{k}{\ell}\left[(n-k)\cos^{2}\theta+k-\ell\right]\tan^{\ell}\theta W_{\ell}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})\right\}.\end{aligned}

For the free boundary case
\theta=\pi/2
, the definition (
2.4
) simplifies as

(2.5)

\displaystyle W_{k+1,\frac{\pi}{2}}(\widehat{\Sigma})=\frac{1}{n+1}\left\{\int_{\Sigma}H_{k}dA-\frac{k}{n-k+1}W_{k-1}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})\right).

Here

is the area element and
H_{k}
is the
k
th normalized mean curvature of the hypersurface
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
respectively, and for a
k

(with or without boundary),
|M|
denotes the
k
-dimensional Hausdorff measure of

. The quermassintegrals

in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
are defined by (see
[

ChenGLS22
]

\displaystyle W_{0}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})=

\displaystyle|\widehat{\partial\Sigma}|,

\displaystyle W_{1}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}|\partial\Sigma|,

\displaystyle W_{k+1}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\int_{\partial\Sigma}H_{k}^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}ds+\frac{k}{n-k+1}W^{\mathbb{S}^{n}}_{k-1}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma}),\quad 1\leq k\leq n-1,

5.
Proofs of Theorem

and Theorem

In this section, we prove the long time existence and smooth convergence of the flow (

5.1.
Long-time existence

Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
be a smooth properly embedded, strictly convex hypersurface with
\theta
-capillary boundary (
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
), given by an embedding:
x:\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
. By Theorem
4.3
, the solution
\Sigma_{t}
of the flow (
3.4
) starting from
\Sigma
remains to be strictly convex for
t>0
. Proposition
2.5
implies that
\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle\geq\delta_{2}>0
and hence
\Sigma_{t}
is star-shaped for
t\geq 0
. Then we can reduce the flow (
3.4
) to a scalar parabolic equation with oblique boundary condition as in
[
WW2020
,
Wang-Xia2019
]

We briefly review the transformation of the flow (
1.5
) to a scalar parabolic equation on the hemisphere
\bar{\mathbb{S}}_{+}^{n}
, and refer the readers to
[
WW2020
,
Wang-Xia2019
]
for more details. Assume that
e
is the
(n+1)
-th coordinate vector
e_{n+1}
. Consider the following Möbius transformation:

\displaystyle\varphi:~\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}\quad

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\quad\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1},

(5.1)

\displaystyle(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\frac{2(x^{\prime},0)+(1-|x^{\prime}|^{2}-x_{n+1}^{2})e}{|x^{\prime}|^{2}+(x_{n+1}-1)^{2}}:=(y^{\prime},y_{n+1})=\tilde{y},

where
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n+1})
with
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
x_{n+1}=\langle x,e\rangle
. Then
\varphi
maps
\mathbb{S}^{n}=\partial\mathbb{B}^{n+1}
to
\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}:=\{(y^{\prime},y_{n+1})\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:y_{n+1}=0\}
and

\displaystyle\varphi^{\ast}(\delta_{\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}})=\frac{4}{(|x^{\prime}|^{2}+(x_{n+1}-1)^{2})^{2}}\delta_{\mathbb{B}^{n+1}}:=e^{-2w}\delta_{\mathbb{B}^{n+1}},

which implies that
\varphi
is a conformal transformation from
(\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1},\delta_{\mathbb{B}^{n+1}})
to
(\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1},\delta_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}})
. Then a properly embedded hypersurface
\Sigma_{t}=x(\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n},t)

can be identified with
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{t}:=\tilde{y}(\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n},t)

\tilde{y}=\varphi\circ x
.

Note that

is a conformal vector field such that
\varphi_{\ast}(X_{e})=-\tilde{y}
. For a hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
with capillary boundary

, one has

\displaystyle e^{-2w}\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle=\langle\varphi_{\ast}(X_{e}),\varphi_{\ast}(\nu)\rangle=|\varphi_{\ast}(\nu)|\langle\tilde{y},\tilde{\nu}\rangle,

where
|\varphi_{\ast}(\nu)|=\frac{1}{2}(|y^{\prime}|^{2}+(y_{n+1}+1)^{2})
and
\tilde{\nu}:=-\frac{\varphi_{\ast}(\nu)}{|\varphi_{\ast}(\nu)|}
. Hence, the hypersurface
\varphi(\Sigma)
is star-shaped in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
with respect to the origin, i.e.,
\langle\tilde{y},\tilde{\nu}\rangle>0
on
\varphi(\Sigma)
, if and only if
\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle>0
on
\Sigma
. In particular, since
\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle>0
on
\Sigma_{t}
by Proposition
2.5
, the hypersurface
\widetilde{\Sigma}_{t}
in
(\bar{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{n+1},(\varphi^{-1})^{\ast}\delta_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}})
can be written as a radial graph over
\bar{\mathbb{S}}^{n}_{+}
.

In
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n+1}
, we use the polar coordinate
\tilde{y}=(\rho,z)\in[0,\infty)\times\bar{\mathbb{S}}_{+}^{n}
, where
\rho
is the distance from
\tilde{y}
to the origin and we write
z=(\beta,\xi)\in[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
for the spherical polar coordinate of

. Then

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \rho^{2}=&|y^{\prime}|^{2}+y_{n+1}^{2},\\
y_{n+1}=&\rho\cos\beta,\quad|y^{\prime}|=\rho\sin\beta.\end{aligned}\right.

Let

and

, where

is the Levi-Civita connection on
\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}
with respect to the round metric

. We have

(5.3)

\displaystyle\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle=e^{w}\frac{\rho}{v}=\frac{2\rho}{\sqrt{1+|\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}u|^{2}}(\rho^{2}+2\rho\cos\beta+1)}.

Furthermore, up to a time-dependent tangential diffeomorphism, one can rewrite the flow (
1.5
) equivalently as the following scalar parabolic equation on
\bar{\mathbb{S}}_{+}^{n}
:

(5.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial t}u&=F(\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{S}}u,\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}u,\rho,\beta)\qquad&\text{in}\quad\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}\times[0,T),\\
\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}_{\partial_{\beta}}u&=-\cos\theta\sqrt{1+|\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}u|^{2}}\qquad&\text{on}\quad\partial\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}\times[0,T),\\

where

,
\rho_{0}
is radial function of the initial hypersurface
\varphi(\Sigma_{0})
,
\partial_{\beta}
is the unit outward normal of

and

\displaystyle F(\nabla^{2}_{\mathbb{S}}u,\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}u,\rho,\beta):=

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}_{\mathbb{S}^{n}_{+}}\left(\frac{\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}u}{\rho e^{w}v}\right)-\frac{n+1}{v}\sigma\left(\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}u,\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}(\frac{1}{\rho e^{w}})\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{n\cos\theta}{2}\cdot\frac{\rho^{2}-1}{\rho}\sin\beta\sigma(\nabla^{\mathbb{S}}u,\partial_{\beta})

\displaystyle+\frac{n\cos\theta}{2}\cdot\frac{\rho^{2}\cos\beta+2\rho+\cos\beta}{\rho}.

As

, we have

The barrier estimate in Lemma

and the convexity of

imply the uniform

estimate of
\Sigma_{t}
. In fact, under the transformation (
5.1
), using (

\displaystyle\rho^{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{|x^{\prime}|^{4}+(1-x_{n+1}^{2})^{2}+2(1+x_{n+1}^{2})|x^{\prime}|^{2}}{|x^{\prime}|^{4}+(1-x_{n+1})^{4}+2(1-x_{n+1})^{2}|x^{\prime}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{4x_{n+1}}{|x^{\prime}|^{2}+(1-x_{n+1})^{2}}.

By Proposition
2.5
, we have
x_{n+1}=\langle x,e\rangle\geq\cos\theta+\delta_{0}
and

\displaystyle\rho^{2}\geq

\displaystyle 1+2(\cos\theta+\delta_{0})\geq 1+2\delta_{0},

\displaystyle\rho^{2}\leq

\displaystyle 1+\frac{4}{|x-e|^{2}}\leq 1+\frac{4}{|\langle x-e,\nu\rangle|^{2}}\leq 1+\frac{4}{\delta_{1}^{2}}.

Since

, we obtain the uniform

estimate of

\displaystyle\frac{2\rho}{\delta_{2}\sqrt{\rho^{2}+2\rho\cos\beta+1}}

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\delta_{2}}\sqrt{1+\frac{4}{\delta_{1}^{2}}}=:c_{3},

where

is a positive constant depending on

(see also
[

of (

. Equivalently, we have

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}(n\geq 2)
be a properly embedded, strictly convex hypersurface with
\theta
-capillary boundary
\partial\Sigma\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
(
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
), given by an embedding:
x:\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
. Then the solution
\Sigma_{t}
of the flow (
1.5
) starting from

has uniform

and exists for all time

.

5.2.
Convergence to a spherical cap

To prove the smooth convergence to the spherical cap, we explore the monotonicity of the flow (
3.4
). By the case
k=1
of (
2.2
), we have

(5.5)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}{W_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma_{t}})}=\frac{n^{2}}{n+1}\int_{\Sigma_{t}}(H_{2}-H_{1}^{2})\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle dA_{t}\leq 0.

The
C^{0}
estimate of
\Sigma_{t}
implies that
{W_{1,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma_{t}})}
is uniformly bounded from below. Then the long-time existence and uniform
C^{\infty}
-estimates imply that

\int_{\Sigma_{t}}{(H_{2}-H_{1}^{2})\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle dA_{t}}\to 0,\,\quad\text{as $t\to\infty$}.

Since
\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle\geq\delta_{2}>0
along the flow, any limit of
\Sigma_{t}
is totally umbilical and hence must be a spherical cap.

The limit spherical cap is uniquely determined and is independent of the subsequence of times. In fact, for any sequence of times
t_{j}\to\infty
such that
\Sigma_{t_{j}}
converges smoothly to a
\theta
-capillary spherical cap
C_{\theta,r_{\infty}}(e_{\infty})
, the radius
r_{\infty}
is uniquely determined by the fact
W_{0,\theta}(\widehat{C_{\theta,r_{\infty}}}(e_{\infty}))=W_{0,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})
. Furthermore, we must have
e_{\infty}=e
, by using a similar argument as in
[
Scheuer-Wang-Xia2018
,
Weng-Xia2022
]
for the locally constrained inverse curvature flow (
1.6
). The idea is simply that any spherical cap
C_{\theta,r_{\infty}}(e_{\infty})
with the center
e_{\infty}\neq e
is not stationary and must evolve towards
e
along the flow (
3.4
). We refer the readers to
[
Scheuer-Wang-Xia2018
,
Weng-Xia2022
]
for the similar justification of this fact along the flow (
1.6
).

Therefore, we conclude that along the flow (
3.4
), the solution
\Sigma_{t}
converges smoothly to the unique spherical cap
C_{\theta,r_{\infty}}(e)
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

5.3.

1.2

Firstly, we assume that
\Sigma
is strictly convex, then by Proposition

, there exists a constant vector

\displaystyle W_{0,\theta}(\widehat{\Sigma})=

Since

and in particular the initial hypersurface

5.4.
Further question

To establish the Alexandrov-Fenchel type inequalities comparing quermassintegrals
W_{k,\theta}
and
W_{\ell,\theta}
for
k>\ell
, it’s also natural to study the following fully nonlinear locally constrained curvature flow

(5.7)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(\partial_{t}x\right)^{\bot}&=\Big(\langle x+\cos\theta\nu,e\rangle-\frac{H_{k}}{H_{k-1}}\langle X_{e},\nu\rangle\Big)\nu&\text{in}\quad\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,T),\\

. Along the flow (

),

is preserved while

can be proved. Furthermore, we can prove that the strict convexity is preserved along the flow (

Appendix A
Approximation result

In this appendix, we prove that a convex hypersurface
\Sigma
with
\theta
-capillary boundary in the unit ball can be approximated by a sequence of strictly convex hypersurfaces with

-capillary boundary in the unit ball in the

sense. The free boundary case (i.e.
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
) has been treated by Lambert and Scheuer in
[

]
. We adapt their idea and include a proof for the capillary case.

Firstly, we show the following lemma which could be considered as a natural generalization of
[
Lambert-Scheuer2017
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be a convex hypersurface with
\theta
-capillary boundary for

, such that
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}\subset\widehat{\partial C_{\theta,\infty}}(e)
. Moreover, either
\partial\Sigma=\partial C_{\theta,\infty}(e)
, or
\partial\Sigma\subset\operatorname{int}(\widehat{\partial C_{\theta,\infty}}(e))
and in this case there exists an interior point in
\Sigma
such that
h_{ij}>0
at this point.

Proof.

The case
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
is treated in
[
Lambert-Scheuer2017
, Lemma 3.1]
. In the following, we assume
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
.

Step 1.
We first show that there exists
e\in\operatorname{int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
such that
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}\subset\widehat{\partial C_{\theta,\infty}}(e)
.

By Proposition
2.1
and
\Sigma
is convex, one has
\widehat{h}_{\alpha\beta}\geq\cot\theta\delta_{\alpha\beta}>0
on
\partial\Sigma
. By
[
NAndo
, Theorem 1.2]
, we know that
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}\subset\bigcap_{q\in\partial\Sigma}\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{q,\theta}}
, where
\mathcal{S}_{q,\theta}
is the circumscribed sphere of
\partial\Sigma
in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
with radius
\theta
such that it touches
\partial\Sigma
tangentially at
q
. On the other hand, by the convexity of
\partial\Sigma
, we have
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}=\bigcap_{q\in\partial\Sigma}\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{q,\frac{\pi}{2}}}
which contains
\bigcap_{q\in\partial\Sigma}\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{q,\theta}}
. Therefore, we have
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}=\bigcap_{q\in\partial\Sigma}\widehat{\mathcal{S}_{q,\theta}}
.

Let
\bar{B}_{r}(e)\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
be the closed geodesic ball with the smallest radius

and center

such that
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}\subset\bar{B}_{r}(e)

. We show that

must support

: for each hyperplane of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
which passes through

,

Let

We prove the following approximation result:

Theorem A.3
.

Let
\Sigma\subset\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n+1}
be a convex hypersurface with capillary boundary supported on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
at a contact angle
\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
. Suppose that
\Sigma_{t}
,

is a solution to the flow (
A.1
) starting from
\Sigma
. Then either
\Sigma
is a flat ball
C_{\theta,\infty}
, or
\Sigma_{t}
is strictly convex for all time
t>0
as long as the flow exists.

Proof.

By Lemma
A.1
, there exists a point
e\in\operatorname{int}(\widehat{\partial\Sigma})
such that
\widehat{\partial\Sigma}\subset\widehat{\partial C_{\theta,\infty}}(e)
, and either
\partial\Sigma=\partial C_{\theta,\infty}(e)
or
\partial\Sigma\subset\operatorname{int}(\widehat{\partial C_{\theta,\infty}}(e))
.

For the first case
\partial\Sigma=\partial C_{\theta,\infty}(e)
, by item (2) of Proposition
2.1
, we have
h_{\alpha\beta}=0
for
\alpha,\beta=1,\cdots,n-1
on the boundary
\partial\Sigma
. If
h_{\mu\mu}>0
on
\partial\Sigma
, then item (4) of Proposition
2.1
implies that
\nabla_{\mu}h_{\alpha\beta}>0
on
\partial\Sigma
and then
h_{\alpha\beta}<0
at some interior points sufficiently close to the boundary
\partial\Sigma
. So we must have
h_{\mu\mu}=0
on
\partial\Sigma
. This implies that if
\Sigma
is not
C_{\theta,\infty}(e)
, then there exists an interior point where the second fundamental form is positive definite. For the second case
\partial\Sigma\subset\operatorname{int}(\widehat{\partial C_{\theta,\infty}}(e))
, by Lemma
A.1
, there is also a strictly convex point in the interior of
\Sigma
.

In the following, we assume that
\Sigma
is not the flat ball
C_{\theta,\infty}(e)
. Let

\chi(x,t)=\min_{|\xi|=1}{h_{ij}{\xi}^{i}{\xi}^{j}},\quad x\in\Sigma_{t}

be the smallest principal curvature at the point
x\in\Sigma_{t}
. Since
h_{ij}
is smooth, the function
\chi(x,t)
is Lipschitz continuous in space and by a cut-off function argument, we find a smooth function
\phi_{0}:\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
0\leq\phi_{0}\leq\chi(x,0)
and there exists an interior point
y
such that
\phi_{0}(y)>0
. We extend the function
\phi_{0}
to
\phi:\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,\delta^{\prime})\to\mathbb{R}
by solving a linear parabolic PDE:

(A.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi&=\Delta\phi+\nabla_{V}{\phi},\qquad\text{in}\quad\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,\delta^{\prime}),\\
\nabla_{\mu}\phi&=0\qquad\text{on}\quad\partial\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\times[0,\delta^{\prime}),\\
\phi(\cdot,0)&={\phi}_{0}(\cdot)\qquad\text{on}\quad\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Delta
and
\nabla
are Laplacian operator and Levi-Civita connection with respect to the induced metric on
\Sigma_{t}=x(\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n},t)
of the flow (
A.1
). The solution
\phi
of (
A.2
) exists at least for a short time interval
[0,\delta^{\prime})
. By the strong maximum principle for scalar functions (see
[
Stahl1996-2
, Corollary 3.2]
), we have
\phi(\cdot,t)>0
in
\bar{\mathbb{B}}^{n}
and
t\in(0,\delta^{\prime})
.

We take
\tau=\frac{1}{2}\min\{\delta,\delta^{\prime}\}
and consider the tensor:

(A.3)

M_{ij}=h_{ij}-\phi g_{ij}

for time

. By the construction of
\phi_{0}
, we see that
M_{ij}\geq 0
at time

. We now apply the tensor maximum principle (i.e. Theorem
4.1
) to deduce that
M_{ij}\geq 0
is preserved along the flow (
A.1
).

By a direct computation using Proposition
3.2
for
f=-H
, we have:

(A.4)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}M_{ij}=\Delta M_{ij}+\nabla_{V}M_{ij}+N_{ij},

where

(A.5)

N_{ij}=|A|^{2}M_{ij}+|A|^{2}\phi g_{ij}-2HM_{i}^{\ell}M_{j\ell}-2H\phi M_{ij}+M_{j}^{k}\nabla_{i}{V_{k}}+M_{i}^{k}\nabla_{j}{V_{k}}.

We easily see that whenever
M_{ij}\geq 0
and
M_{ij}\xi^{i}=0
at a point, we have

(A.6)

N_{ij}\xi^{i}\xi^{j}=|A|^{2}\phi\geq 0

and thus the condition (
4.1
) is satisfied. The boundary condition (
4.2

. Hence Theorem
4.1
implies that

A.1
) for time

, and it follows that

is strictly convex for time interval

.

To show the strict convexity for the whole time interval

holds everywhere on

) for all time

as long as the flow (

) exists, which finishes the proof.

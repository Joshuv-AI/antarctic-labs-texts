Abstract
We deal with non-hydrostatic mesoscale atmospheric modeling using the fully implicit space-time discontinuous Galerkin method in combination with the anisotropic h ​ p hp -mesh adaptation technique. The time discontinuous approximation allows the treatment of different meshes at different time levels in a natural way which can significantly reduce the number of degrees of freedom. The presented approach generates a sequence of triangular meshes consisting of possible anisotropic elements and varying polynomial approximation degrees such that the interpolation error is below the given tolerance and the number of degrees of freedom at each time step is minimal. We describe the discretization of the problem together with several implementation issues related to the treatment of boundary conditions, algebraic solver and adaptive choice of the size of the time steps. The computational performance of the proposed method is demonstrated on several benchmark problems.

Introduction

The numerical solution of the compressible Euler and/or Navier-Stokes
equations has become the standard tool for the simulation of the atmosphere
in the last decades, see e.g. the reviews in
SteppelrALL_MAP03
or

GiraldoRostelli_JCP08
.
Due to the large scale simulation, it is advantageous to use higher order discretization
schemes since they significantly reduce the number of degrees of freedom (
\mathrm{DoF}
)
needed to achieve the required accuracy. It is possible to use
the spectral element (SE) method
Giraldo_JCP05
,
Giraldo_MWR04

or the finite volume (FV) approach with higher-order reconstruction schemes
as ENO, WENO or TENO,
cf.
NavasMontilla_JCP23
and the references cited therein.
In the recent years, large attention has been devoted to the discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods
which employ the advantageous properties of SE and FV methods,
cf.
RestelliGiraldo_SISC09
,
YelashALL_JCP14
,
BustoALL_CF20
,
WaruszewskiALL_JCP22
,
JiangALL_JCP22

for example.
DG methods employ piecewise polynomial but discontinuous approximation, they
provide high accuracy and flexibility,
local conservation, stability, and parallel efficiency.
However, the disadvantage is a large number of
\mathrm{DoF}
in comparison to FV and SE methods.

Due to the high-order spatial approximation, the use of an explicit time discretization
is very inefficient, since the size of the time steps is strongly restricted by the stability
condition, cf.
Giraldo-book
.
Therefore, several variants of semi-implicit time discretization techniques have been
developed, e.g.,
the implicit-explicit (IMEX) formulation in
RestelliGiraldo_SISC09
,
GiraldoALL_SISC10
,
its recent improvement in
ReddyALL_JCP23
,
the IMEX evolution Galerkin method in
LukacovaALL_SISC04
,
YelashALL_JCP14
,
the positivity-preserving well-balanced central discontinuous Galerkin method in

JiangALL_JCP22
or the techniques based on the Eulerian-Lagrangian approach
in
BustoALL_CF20
.

In the last two decades, we have developed, analyzed and tested the discontinuous Galerkin method
for solving the compressible Navier-Stokes equations with applications
in aerodynamics, including supersonic, transonic, subsonic, and low-Mach number flows,
cf.
DGM-book
.
The prominent scheme seems to us to be the fully implicit space-time discontinuous Galerkin (STDG)
method, which uses a discontinuous piecewise polynomial approximation with
respect to both spatial and temporal coordinates.
In our experience, the time discontinuous Galerkin method is more stable than IMEX techniques
and
it can naturally handle different meshes at different time levels. However,
these advantages are
compensated by a higher number of
\mathrm{DoF}
compared to multi-step or
diagonally implicit Runge-Kutta methods.

A fully implicit time discretization leads to the need
to solve a nonlinear algebraic system at each time step. We employ an iterative Newton-like method whose first step
corresponds to our earlier semi-implicit scheme from
CiCP
.
Therefore, the additional iterative steps improve the
accuracy (and stability) of the computation. In practice, only a few iterative steps
are necessary to reduce the algebraic errors.

In the last decade, we have dealt with the
anisotropic
hp
-mesh adaptation
(
hp
-AMA) method
which constructs meshes consisting of triangles of different size, shape and orientation
together with varying polynomial approximation degrees for each mesh element.
The idea is to minimize the number of
\mathrm{DoF}
such that
an estimate of the interpolation error is below the given tolerance.
This anisotropic mesh adaptation
technique is more general than the approach from
WaruszewskiALL_JCP22
which allows
the movement of grid nodes but not local refinement/coarsening.
On the other hand, the adaptive technique from
OrlandoALL_JCAM23
offers
the local mesh refinement/de-refinement but the initial mesh structure is fixed and then
the capturing of phenomena that are not parallel to the boundary domain is not optimal.
The theoretical as well as practical
issues of the
hp
-AMA method are summarized in
AMA-book
.

The STDG discretization can naturally handle grids generated by
the
hp
-AMA method since it is
a one-step method and the approximate solutions at different time levels
are weakly connected together
by a penalty term.
In
AMAtdp
, we developed the
hp
-AMA approach for solving time-dependent
problems and demonstrated its performance by solving the Burgers equation,
the isentropic vortex propagation, the Kelvin-Helmholtz instability and
single-ring infiltration in porous media flow.
The computational benefit of anisotropic meshes
for the numerical solution of unsteady flow problems
has been demonstrated in many works, e.g.,

BelmeDervieuxAlauzet_JCP12
,
AlauzetLoseille_JCP18
,
FreyAlauzet
,
HabashiAll
and
the references cited therein.

The goal of this paper is to apply the STDG and
hp
-AMA methods to
the numerical simulation of non-hydrostatic mesoscale atmospheric problems.
The presented algorithm controls the errors arising from an inexact solution of nonlinear
algebraic systems and chooses adaptively the size of the time steps whereas
the polynomial approximation degree with respect to time is kept fixed.
Hence, we can speak of
hp\tau
-adaptation.
Moreover, at each time step, the algorithm evaluates an estimate of the interpolation
error. If the error estimate exceeds the prescribed tolerance,
a completely new (possibly anisotropic)
mesh with varying polynomial approximation degrees with respect to the space is generated.
Attention is paid to the realization
of the boundary conditions and the choice of the physical quantity to be controlled.
The particular aspects of the presented numerical scheme are justified by numerical experiments.

The outline of the rest of the paper is as follows.
In Section
2
, we present the governing
equations with the constitutive relations and the initial/boundary conditions.
In Section
3
, we introduce the discretization of the problem including
the realization of the boundary conditions. Furthermore, Section
4
presents
the solution strategy of the arising algebraic system with an adaptive stopping
criterion and the adaptive choice of the time step.
Section
5
contains a description of the
hp
-AMA technique and
its application to atmospheric modeling.
The performance of the proposed method is demonstrated in Section
6
by
numerical experiments for several benchmark examples from
GiraldoRostelli_JCP08
.
Finally, we give several concluding remarks in Section
7
.

2
Problem formulation

Let
{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the computational domain,
T>0
the physical time to be reached,
and
Q_{T}:={\Omega}\times(0,T)
. We use the following notation:
\rho
is the density,

\mathrm{p}
is the pressure,
\bm{v}=(v_{1},v_{2})^{\mathrm{T}}
is the velocity vector,

\theta
is the absolute temperature and

e=\rho c_{\mathrm{v}}\theta+\rho|\bm{v}|^{2}/2
is the energy per unit volume
including the interior and kinetic energies (and excluding the gravitational energy).
Moreover,

c_{\mathrm{v}}>0
and

c_{\mathrm{p}}>0
are the specific heat capacities at constant volume and pressure, respectively,

\kappa=c_{\mathrm{p}}/c_{\mathrm{v}}>1
is the Poisson adiabatic constant,

R=c_{\mathrm{p}}-c_{\mathrm{v}}
is the gas constant,

\mu
is the dynamic viscosity,
g=9.81\,\mathrm{m}\cdot\mathrm{s}^{-2}
is the gravity constant,

{\bm{k}}=(k_{1},k_{2})^{\mathrm{T}}
is the upward pointing unit vector,
\Pr
is the Prandtl number,
and
\tau_{ij},\ i,j=1,2
denote the components of the viscous part of the stress tensor.
The symbols
\partial_{t}{}
and
\partial_{x_{i}}{}
denote the partial derivatives with respect to

t
and
x_{i},\ i=1,2
, respectively.

We consider the system of the compressible Navier-Stokes equations with the
gravity forces written in the conservative form
GiraldoRostelli_JCP08
,
OrlandoALL_JCAM23

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{{\bm{w}}}+\nabla\cdot\vec{{\bm{f}}}({\bm{w}})-\nabla\cdot\vec{\bm{R}}({\bm{w}},\nabla{\bm{w}})={\bm{g}}\quad\mbox{in}\ Q_{T},

(1)

where
\nabla
and
\nabla\cdot
denote the gradient and divergence operators, respectively.
The unknown state vector
{\bm{w}}
, the convective flux
\vec{{\bm{f}}}=(\bm{f}_{1},\bm{f}_{2})
,
the diffusive one
\vec{\bm{R}}=(\bm{R}_{1},\bm{R}_{2})

and the exterior forces
{\bm{g}}
are given by

\displaystyle{\bm{w}}

\displaystyle=(\rho,\,\rho v_{1},\rho v_{2},\,e)^{\mathrm{T}},\qquad\bm{f}_{i}=(\rho v_{i},\,\rho v_{i}v_{1}+\delta_{i1}\mathrm{p},\,\rho v_{i}v_{2}+\delta_{i2}\mathrm{p},\,(e+\mathrm{p})\,v_{i})^{\mathrm{T}},\qquad i=1,2,

(2)

\displaystyle\bm{R}_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(0,\,\tau_{i1},\,\tau_{i2},\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{2}\tau_{ik}v_{i}+\frac{\mu\,c_{\mathrm{p}}}{\Pr}\partial_{x_{i}}{\theta}\right)^{\mathrm{T}},\ i=1,2,\qquad{\bm{g}}=\left(0,\,-\rho gk_{1},\,-\rho gk_{2},\,-\rho g{\bm{k}}\cdot\bm{v}\right)^{\mathrm{T}},

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta and the symbol
\cdot
denotes the scalar product.
The relations (
1
) – (
2
) are accompanied by the
constitutive relations of a perfect gas and the viscous part of the stress tensor as

\displaystyle\mathrm{p}=R\rho\theta\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\tau_{ij}=\mu\left(\partial_{x_{j}}{v_{i}}+\partial_{x_{i}}{v_{j}}\right)-\tfrac{2}{3}\delta_{ij}\nabla\cdot\bm{v},\quad i,j=1,2,

(3)

respectively.
In atmospheric applications, the potential temperature
\Theta
is the preferred physical
quantity given as

\displaystyle\Theta={\theta}/{\mathrm{P}},\qquad\mbox{where}\quad\mathrm{P}=\left({\mathrm{p}}/{\mathrm{p}_{0}}\right)^{(\kappa-1)/\kappa}

(4)

is the Exner pressure and
\mathrm{p}_{0}=10^{5}\,\,\mathrm{Pa}
is the reference pressure.

The viscous fluxes can be written in the form

\displaystyle\vec{\bm{R}}({\bm{w}},\nabla{\bm{w}})=\widehat{\bm{K}}({\bm{w}})\nabla{\bm{w}}\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\bm{R}_{i}({\bm{w}},\nabla{\bm{w}})=\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{2}\bm{K}_{i,j}({\bm{w}})\partial_{x_{j}}{{\bm{w}}},\quad i=1,2

(5)

where
\widehat{\bm{K}}
is the “matrix of matrices” (fourth-order tensor) whose entries are

4\times 4
matrices
\bm{K}_{i,j}(\cdot)
dependent on
{\bm{w}}
(and independent of
\nabla{\bm{w}}
).
Their explicit form of
\bm{K}_{i,j}(\cdot)
,
i,j=1,2
can be found
e.g., in
CiCP
or
[
DGM-book
, Section 9.1]
.

The term
{\bm{g}}
representing the gravity forces can be expressed as

\displaystyle{\bm{g}}=\bm{B}{\bm{w}},\qquad\bm{B}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0\\
0&0&0&0\\
-g&0&0&0\\
0&0&-g&0\\
\end{pmatrix},

(6)

where
g
is the gravity constant (note that
{\bm{k}}=(k_{1},k_{2})^{\mathrm{T}}=(0,1)^{\mathrm{T}}
).

The above equations are accompanied by the initial and boundary conditions.
Particularly, we set
{\bm{w}}(x,0)={\bm{w}}_{0}(x)
in
{\Omega}
,
where
{\bm{w}}_{0}
is the given function, typically as a perturbation in terms of the potential
temperature of a steady-state flow where the mean values
\bar{\Theta}
and
\bar{\mathrm{P}}

of the Exner pressure and the potential temperature, respectively,
are in a hydrostatic balance, cf.
GiraldoRostelli_JCP08
, i.e.,

\displaystyle c_{\mathrm{p}}\bar{\Theta}\frac{\mathrm{d}\bar{\mathrm{P}}}{\mathrm{d}x_{2}}=-g.

(7)

Moreover, we consider several types of boundary conditions on the boundary
{\Gamma}:=\partial{\Omega}
:

•

the
no-flux
or
reflecting boundary condition

\bm{v}\cdot\vec{n}=0
,
where
\vec{n}
is the outer normal to the boundary,

•

the
nonreflecting boundary conditions
representing an
artificial boundary of an infinite domain,

•

the
periodic boundary conditions
,

•

the
symmetric boundary conditions
.

Their practical realization in the context of the discontinuous Galerkin method
is described in Section
3.2
.

3
Discontinuous Galerkin discretization

We solve numerically the system of equations (
1
) – (
3
) by the

space-time discontinuous Galerkin method
.
We refer to
[
DGM-book
, Chapters 8–9]

for the detailed discretization of the Navier-Stokes equations
without the external gravity forces.
Here, only the final necessary formulas are presented.

3.1
Functional spaces

Let
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{r}=T
be a partition of
(0,T)
. We set

I_{m}=(t_{m-1},t_{m})
and
\tau_{m}=t_{m}-t_{m-1}
for
m=1,\ldots,r
.
For each
t_{m},\ m=0,\ldots,r
, we consider a
space partition
{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}
consisting of a finite number
of closed triangles
K
with mutually disjoint interiors and covering
\overline{{\Omega}}
,
the meshes
{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}
can vary for
m=0,\dots,r
, in general.
By
{\vec{n}_{\!\!\;\scriptscriptstyle K}}
we denote the unit outer normal to the element boundary
\partial K
,
K\in{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}
.

For each
K\in{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}
,
m=0,\dots,r
, we assign a positive integer number
p_{K}

denoting the polynomial approximation degree on
K
. Then we define the spaces of
discontinuous piecewise-polynomial functions by

\displaystyle S_{m,h,{\bm{p}}}=\{\varphi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R};\ \varphi(x)|_{K}\in P_{p_{K}}(K)\ \forall\,K\in{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}\},\quad\bm{S}_{m,h,{\bm{p}}}=[S_{m,h,{\bm{p}}}]^{4},\qquad m=0,\dots,r,

(8)

where
P_{p_{K}}(K)
denotes the space of all polynomials on
K
of degree
\leq p_{K}
.

The set of all edges of elements of mesh
{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}
is denoted by
{{\mathcal{F}}_{h,m}}
,
the symbols
{{\mathcal{F}}_{h,m}^{I}}
and
{{\mathcal{F}}_{h,m}^{B}}
denote its subsets consisting
of all interior and boundary edges of
{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}
, respectively.
Moreover,
\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{\partial K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(+)}
and
\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{\partial K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-)}
are
the interior and exterior traces of
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in\bm{S}_{m,h,{\bm{p}}}
on
\partial K,\ K\in{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}
, respectively.
The symbols
\left\langle{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right\rangle
and
[\![{\boldsymbol{\psi}}]\!]
denote
the mean value and the jump of functions
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in\bm{S}_{m,h,{\bm{p}}}
on edges

\gamma\subset\partial K,\ K\in{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}
given by

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{llll}&\left\langle{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right\rangle_{\gamma}=\tfrac{1}{2}\left(\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{\partial K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(+)}+\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{\partial K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-)}\right),&[\![{\boldsymbol{\psi}}]\!]_{\gamma}=\left(\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{\partial K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(+)}-\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{\partial K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-)}\right){\vec{n}_{\!\!\;\scriptscriptstyle K}}&\mbox{for }\gamma\in{{\mathcal{F}}_{h,m}^{I}},\\[5.69054pt]

\end{array}

Furthermore, let
q\geq 0
be the polynomial approximation degree with respect to time.
We define the space of discontinuous piecewise polynomial
functions on the space-time domain
Q_{T}
by

\displaystyle\bm{S}_{h,{\bm{p}}}^{\tau,q}=

\displaystyle\left\{\boldsymbol{\psi}:{\Omega}\times(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{4};\ \boldsymbol{\psi}(x,t)|_{{\Omega}\times I_{m}}=\sum\nolimits_{l=0}^{q}t^{l}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}(x),\ \ \boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\in\bm{S}_{m,h,{\bm{p}}},\ l=0,\dots,q,\ m=0,\dots,r\right\}.

(11)

Since
\boldsymbol{\psi}\in\bm{S}_{h,{\bm{p}}}^{\tau,q}
is discontinuous with respect to the time variable at

t_{m},\ m=1,\dots,r-1
, we define the jump with respect to time
on the time level
t_{m},\ m=0,\dots,r-1
by

\displaystyle\left\{{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right\}_{m}:=\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{m}^{+}-\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{m}^{-},\qquad\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{m}^{\pm}:=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0\pm}\boldsymbol{\psi}(t_{m}+\delta),

(12)

where
\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{0}^{-}
is typically taken from the initial condition.

3.2
Space semi-discretization

The space semi-discretization of the system (
1
) employs the following
properties and relations:

•

the convective fluxes fulfill
\bm{f}_{i}({\bm{w}})=\bm{A}_{i}({\bm{w}}){\bm{w}}
, where
\bm{A}_{i}
are the
Jacobian matrices of
\bm{f}_{i}({\bm{w}})
,
i=1,2
,

•

the physical inviscid flux through element boundary
\partial K
,
K\in{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}

having the normal vector
{\vec{n}_{\!\!\;\scriptscriptstyle K}}=(n_{1},n_{2})

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{2}\bm{f}_{i}({\bm{w}})n_{i}|_{\partial K}\approx\bm{P}^{\scriptscriptstyle+}({\bm{w}},{\vec{n}_{\!\!\;\scriptscriptstyle K}}){\bm{w}}|_{\gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle(+)}+\bm{P}^{\scriptscriptstyle-}({\bm{w}},{\vec{n}_{\!\!\;\scriptscriptstyle K}}){\bm{w}}|_{\gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-)},

(13)

where

\bm{P}^{\scriptscriptstyle\pm}
are matrices from a suitable
decomposition of the matrix
\sum_{i=1}^{2}\bm{A}_{i}({\bm{w}})n_{i}
, e.g.,
Lax-Friedrichs or Vijayasundaram numerical fluxes can be written in this form
FEI2
,

•

the diffusive terms in (
1
) are discretized by the symmetric interior penalty
variant of DG method.

By symbol
\left({\bm{u}},{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{M}
we denote the

-scalar product of vector-valued functions

\bm{u},\boldsymbol{\psi}:Q_{T}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}

.
In order to simplify the notation, we introduce

Then

\displaystyle\left({\vec{\bm{A}}({\bm{w}}){\bm{w}}},{\nabla\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{M}=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{2}\left({\bm{A}_{i}({\bm{w}}){\bm{w}}},{\partial_{x_{i}}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}}\right)_{M}.

(14)

Similarly, in virtue of (
5

), we use the notation

\displaystyle\left({\widehat{\bm{K}}({\bm{w}})\nabla{\bm{w}}},{\nabla\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{M}=\sum\nolimits_{i,j=1}^{2}\left({\bm{K}_{ij}({\bm{w}})\partial_{x_{j}}{{\bm{w}}}},{\partial_{x_{i}}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}}\right)_{M}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \left({\left\langle{\widehat{\bm{K}}(\bar{{{\bm{w}}}})\nabla{\bm{w}}}\right\rangle},{[\![{\boldsymbol{\psi}}]\!]}\right)_{\gamma}=\sum\nolimits_{i,j=1}^{2}\left({\bm{K}_{ij}(\bar{{{\bm{w}}}})\partial_{x_{j}}{{\bm{w}}}},{(\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{\gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle(+)}-\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{\gamma}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-)})}\right)_{\gamma}n_{i},

where

We define the forms representing the space semi-discretization of the convective,
diffusive and gravity terms in (

\displaystyle{a_{h,m}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}(\bar{{{\bm{w}}}},{\bm{w}},\boldsymbol{\psi}):=-{\sum_{K\in{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}}\left({\vec{\bm{A}}(\bar{{{\bm{w}}}}){\bm{w}}},{\nabla\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{K}}+{\sum_{K\in{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}}}\left(\left({\bm{P}^{\scriptscriptstyle+}(\bar{{{\bm{w}}}},{\vec{n}_{\!\!\;\scriptscriptstyle K}}){\bm{w}}|_{\partial K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(+)}},{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{\partial K}+\left({\bm{P}^{\scriptscriptstyle-}(\bar{{{\bm{w}}}},{\vec{n}_{\!\!\;\scriptscriptstyle K}}){\bm{w}}|_{\partial K}^{\scriptscriptstyle(-)}},{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{\partial K\setminus{\Gamma}}\right)-{\sum_{K\in{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}}\left({\bm{B}{\bm{w}}},{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{K}}

\displaystyle\qquad+{\sum_{K\in{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}}\left({\widehat{\bm{K}}(\bar{{{\bm{w}}}})\nabla{\bm{w}}},{\nabla\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{K}}-{\sum_{\gamma\in{{\mathcal{F}}_{h,m}}}\left({\left\langle{\widehat{\bm{K}}(\bar{{{\bm{w}}}})\nabla{\bm{w}}}\right\rangle},{[\![{\boldsymbol{\psi}}]\!]}\right)_{\gamma}}-{\sum_{\gamma\in{{\mathcal{F}}_{h,m}}}\left({\left\langle{\widehat{\bm{K}}(\bar{{{\bm{w}}}})\nabla\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right\rangle},{[\![{{\bm{w}}}]\!]}\right)_{\gamma}}+{\sum_{\gamma\in{{\mathcal{F}}_{h,m}}}\left({\sigma[\![{{\bm{w}}}]\!]},{[\![{\boldsymbol{\psi}}]\!]}\right)_{\gamma}},

where

is the penalty parameter
and
{\bm{w}}_{D}
is the state vector following from the boundary conditions. We note that the form

The boundary state vector
{\bm{w}}_{D}
is set in the following way.

•

No-flux boundary
conditions: the vector
{\bm{w}}_{D}

has the same density, energy and the tangential component of the velocity

{\bm{w}}_{\partial K}
(= the trace of

)
but the opposite normal component of the velocity, i.e., we set

\displaystyle{\bm{w}}_{D}|_{\partial K}={\mathscr{M}}({\bm{w}}|_{\partial K},{\vec{n}_{\!\!\;\scriptscriptstyle K}}),\quad\mbox{where}\quad{\mathscr{M}}({\bm{w}},\vec{n})=\left(\rho,\rho(\bm{v}-2(\bm{v}\cdot\vec{n})\vec{n}),e\right)^{\mathrm{T}}\quad\mbox{for}\quad{\bm{w}}=(\rho,\rho\bm{v},e)^{\mathrm{T}}.

(16)

•

Periodic boundary conditions
:
we set
{\bm{w}}_{D}|_{\gamma^{+}}={\bm{w}}|_{\gamma^{-}}
and
{\bm{w}}_{D}|_{\gamma^{-}}={\bm{w}}|_{\gamma^{+}}
,
where
\gamma^{+}
and
\gamma^{-}
denote the
pair of the corresponding periodic edges.

•

Symmetric boundary conditions
:
{\bm{w}}_{D}
is set similarly as for the
periodic boundary conditions, depending on the line of symmetry. E.g., if the symmetry
is prescribed along the axes
x_{1}=0
, the vector
{\bm{w}}_{D}

has the same density, energy and the vertical component of the velocity
v_{2}

but the opposite horizontal component of the velocity
v_{1}
.

•

Non-reflecting boundary conditions
: we employ the approach
from
[
DGM-book
, Section 8.3.2]
, where
{\bm{w}}_{D}
is the solution of the
nonlinear Riemann problem
{\mathscr{B}}^{\rm RP}({\bm{w}}_{\Gamma},{\bm{w}}_{\mathrm{init}})

defined for the Euler equations (
\vec{\bm{R}}=0
in (
1
)) without
the source (gravity) terms. Here,
{\bm{w}}_{\partial K}
is the trace of
{\bm{w}}

on
\partial K\subset{\Gamma}
and
{\bm{w}}_{\mathrm{init}}
is typically given by
the initial (unperturbed) flow field.

We are aware that the aforementioned realization of the
non-reflecting boundary conditions is rather heuristic since the
treated Riemann problem does not involve the gravity term.
However, this approach works very well provided that
high polynomial approximation degrees are employed, which is documented by
the numerical experiments in Section
3.3
.

3.3
Performance of the non-reflecting boundary conditions

We consider
the steady-state solution of hydrostatic flow in
the computational
domain
{\Omega}=(-25\,600,25\,600)\times(0,6\,400)\,\mathrm{m}
with the constant potential temperature

\bar{\Theta}=300\,\mathrm{K}
and the velocity

3.4
Full space-time discretization

To define the full space-time DG discretization of (
1
), we introduce the
forms

\displaystyle{A_{h,m}}({\bm{w}},\boldsymbol{\psi})

\displaystyle:=\int_{I_{m}}\Bigl(\left({\partial_{t}{{\bm{w}}}},{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{\Omega}+{a_{h,m}}({\bm{w}},\psi)\Bigr){\,\mathrm{d}t}+\left({\left\{{{\bm{w}}}\right\}_{m-1}},{\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{m-1}^{+}}\right)_{{\Omega}},

(18)

\displaystyle{A_{h,m}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}(\bar{{{\bm{w}}}},{\bm{w}},\boldsymbol{\psi})

\displaystyle:=\int_{I_{m}}\left(\left({\partial_{t}{{\bm{w}}}},{\boldsymbol{\psi}}\right)_{\Omega}+{a_{h,m}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}(\bar{{{\bm{w}}}},{\bm{w}},\psi)\right){\,\mathrm{d}t}+\left({\left\{{{\bm{w}}}\right\}_{m-1}},{\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{m-1}^{+}}\right)_{{\Omega}},\quad\bar{{{\bm{w}}}},{\bm{w}},\boldsymbol{\psi}\in\bm{S}_{h,{\bm{p}}}^{\tau,q},\quad m=1,\dots,r.

We note that form
{A_{h,m}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}

is linear with respect to its second and third arguments.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}\in\bm{S}_{h,{\bm{p}}}^{\tau,q}
is the
space-time discontinuous Galerkin
solution
of (
1
) if

\displaystyle{A_{h,m}}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h})=0\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\in\bm{S}_{h,{\bm{p}}}^{\tau,q},\quad m=1,\dots,r,

(19)

where we set
{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}|_{0}^{-}:={\bm{w}}_{0}
(= the initial condition).

The term
\left({\left\{{{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}}\right\}_{m-1}},{\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{m-1}^{+}}\right)_{{\Omega}}
in (
18
)
represents the time penalty which
joins together the approximate solution on two subsequent time layers
I_{m-1}
and
I_{m}

in a weak sense.
This term is applicable also for varying spaces
\bm{S}_{m-1,h,{\bm{p}}}
and
\bm{S}_{m,h,{\bm{p}}}
.
Indeed, due to (
12
), we have

\displaystyle\left({\left\{{{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}}\right\}_{m-1}},{\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{m-1}^{+}}\right)_{{\Omega}}=\left({{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}|_{m-1}^{+}},{\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{m-1}^{+}}\right)_{{\Omega}}-\left({{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}|_{m-1}^{-}},{\boldsymbol{\psi}|_{m-1}^{+}}\right)_{{\Omega}}.

(20)

If
\bm{S}_{m-1,h,{\bm{p}}}\not=\bm{S}_{m,h,{\bm{p}}}
then the last term in (
20
) exhibits the
integration of the product of two functions which are piecewise polynomial on different meshes

{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m-1}}
and
{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}
.
In Section
5
, we introduce the
hp
-AMA algorithm which generates completely
different meshes for two sequential time steps, see
Figure
1
for an illustration.
In this case, the evaluation of the last integral in (
20
) is a little delicate
since we integrate
{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}|_{m-1}^{-}
over
K\in{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}
but
{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}|_{m-1}^{-}
is discontinuous on
K
.
In
AMAtdp
, we presented a strategy, which employs a non-adaptive
composite quadrature rule. The evaluation of this integral exhibits another source
of computational errors. However, the numerical experiments in
Section
5.1
show that the inaccuracy of the mass and energy conservation
is on the same level as the computation on a fixed mesh. On the other hand, if

{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m-1}}={{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}
, the numerical integration
of the terms in (
20
) can be carried out exactly (up to rounding errors)
since the integrated functions are polynomial.

{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m-1}}
(red) and
{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}
(blue),
total view (left) and a detail (right).

The STDG discretization (
19
) represents the system of
{N_{h,m}}

algebraic equations for each
m=1,\dots,r
, where

\displaystyle{N_{h,m}}=(q+1){M_{h,m}}\quad\mbox{with}\quad{M_{h,m}}=\sum_{K\in{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}}\nolimits(p_{K}+1)(p_{K}+2)/2.

(21)

On the contrary,
the diagonally implicit Runge-Kutta methods give
q
systems of size
{M_{h,m}}

and multi-step methods give only one system of size
{M_{h,m}}
, hence their solution is cheaper.
This is the main disadvantage of the time discontinuous Galerkin discretization.
We note that if
q=0
(piecewise constant in time) then the time DG scheme is identical
to the backward (implicit) Euler method.

On the other hand, the STDG method has the super-convergence property in the time
nodes
t_{m},\ m=1,\dots,r
, namely
O(\tau^{2q+1})
. Therefore, the use
of piecewise linear approximation (
q=1
)
seems to be sufficient for the majority of numerical experiments since
it provides a third-order method at time nodes. Moreover, whereas multi-step of Runge-Kutta methods give the approximate solution only at
time nodes
t_{m},\ m=1,\dots,r
, the STDG discretization
provides an approximate solution on the whole interval
I_{m}
,
m=1,\dots,r
directly.
It is advantageous in the definition of the space and time discretization errors,
cf. Section
4.2
.

4
Solution strategy

In this section, we describe the strategy for solving the discrete problem (
19
).
We omit the details and we refer to
stdgm_est
,
[
DGM-book
, Chapters 8-9]
for
a complete description.

4.1
Solution of nonlinear algebraic systems

Let
m=1,\dots,r
be an arbitrary but fixed index of the time level.
The nonlinear algebraic system (
19
) is solved iteratively by a
Newton-like method where we do not differentiate the form
{A_{h,m}}
but employ
its linearization
{A_{h,m}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}
, cf. (
18
). Particularly, let
{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m}:={{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}|_{{\Omega}\times I_{m}}

denote the restriction of
{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}
on the space-time layer
{\Omega}\times I_{m}
.
Then we define the sequence
{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m,k}
,
k=0,1,\dots,
approximating
{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m}
by

\displaystyle{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m,0}

\displaystyle={\mathscr{E}}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m-1}),

(22a)

\displaystyle{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m,k}

\displaystyle={{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m,k-1}+\lambda_{k}{\bm{u}}_{h\tau}^{k},\qquad k=1,2,\dots

(22b)

\displaystyle\quad\mbox{where }{\bm{u}}_{h\tau}^{k}\in\bm{S}_{h,{\bm{p}}}^{\tau,q}:\ \ {A_{h,m}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m,k-1},{\bm{u}}_{h\tau}^{k},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h})=-{A_{h,m}}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m,k-1},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h})\quad\forall\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\in\bm{S}_{h,{\bm{p}}}^{\tau,q}.

(22c)

The symbol
{\mathscr{E}}
in formula (
22a
) means that
the initial guess
{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m,0}
is extrapolated either
from the previous time layer or from the initial condition.
If
\bm{S}_{m-1,h,{\bm{p}}}\not=\bm{S}_{m,h,{\bm{p}}}
, we have to project
{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m-1}\in\bm{S}_{m-1,h,{\bm{p}}}

to the new space
\bm{S}_{m,h,{\bm{p}}}
but we do not emphasize it explicitly.
Moreover, the function
{\bm{u}}_{h\tau}^{k}
in (
22b
) is the update in the
k
-th iteration
and it is obtained from the linear algebraic system (
22c
).
The convergence is improved by the
suitable choice of the damping factor
\lambda_{k}\in(0,1]
. Its choice is
controlled by the monitoring factor
newton

\displaystyle\zeta_{k}=\frac{\left\|\left\{{A_{h,m}}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m,k},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{i})\right\}_{i=1}^{{N_{h,m}}}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}}{\left\|\left\{{A_{h,m}}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m,k-1},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{i})\right\}_{i=1}^{{N_{h,m}}}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}},

(23)

where
\boldsymbol{\psi}_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,{N_{h,m}}
are the basis functions on
{\Omega}\times I_{m}
such that

\left\|\boldsymbol{\psi}_{i}\right\|_{L^{2}({\Omega}\times I_{m})}=1
and
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{\ell^{2}}
is the Euclidean
discrete norm. Hence, the damping factor
\lambda_{k}
has to be chosen such that

\zeta_{k}<1
for each iterates
k=1,2,\dots
.

It is advantageous to solve the linear algebraic system (
22c
) iteratively
since
{\bm{u}}_{h\tau}^{k}=0
is a suitable initial guess and only a few iterative steps are necessary.
We employ the GMRES method with block ILU(0) preconditioner but other solvers are possible.
The stopping criterion for the iterative process
(
22
) is presented in Section
4.2
.
We note that it is not necessary to update the matrix on the left-hand side of
(
22c
) for each
k
. It means that in the first argument of
{A_{h,m}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}
, we can employ
a former approximation
{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m,k-s}
for some
s>1
. Particularly, if the monitoring
function
\zeta_{k}
is sufficiently smaller than 1, we do not update the matrix on the
left-hand side of (
22c
).

The presented approach can be treated also in the context of the IMEX formulation.
Let
{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}
be the approximate solution given by (
19
) and
\lambda\in(0,1]
then
we have the identity

\displaystyle{A_{h,m}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau},{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h})={A_{h,m}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau},{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h})-\lambda{A_{h,m}}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h})\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\in\bm{S}_{h,{\bm{p}}}^{\tau,q},\quad m=1,\dots,r.

(24)

Treating
{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}
in the second argument of
{A_{h,m}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}
on the left-hand side of (
24
) implicitly
and in the other arguments explicitly by extrapolating
from the previous time layer (cf. (
22a
)),
we obtain the linear systems

\displaystyle{A_{h,m}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}({\mathscr{E}}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m-1}),{{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h})={A_{h,m}^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{L}}}({\mathscr{E}}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m-1}),{\mathscr{E}}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m-1}),\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h})-\lambda{A_{h,m}}({\mathscr{E}}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}^{m-1}),\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h})\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\in\bm{S}_{h,{\bm{p}}}^{\tau,q},\quad m=1,\dots,r.

(25)

The formulation (
25
) is equivalent to the first iterative step of method (
22
) for

k=1
.
So the additional iterates in (
22
) for
k\geq 2
exhibits an improvement of the accuracy
which can be controlled by the stopping criterion presented in Section
4.2
.
In our experience, the fully-implicit technique (
22
) is more stable than the
semi-implicit one (
25
), particularly it is possible to use larger time steps.
We demonstrate this effect by a numerical example in Section
4.3
.

4.2
Choice of the time step and iterative stopping criterion

The accuracy and efficiency of the whole computational process strongly depend on
the choice of the time step and the stopping criterion for the iterative solver
(
22
). A too large time step and/or a too weak stopping criterion cause a loss of accuracy.
On the other hand, a too small time step and/or a too strong stopping criterion lead to an
increase in the computational time without a significant impact on the accuracy.
In order to balance these two aspects, we employ residual
based estimators indicating the errors arising from the spatial and temporal discretizations
and the inaccuracy of iterative algebraic solvers.

For each time interval
I_{m},\ m=1,\dots,r
, we define the estimators

\displaystyle\eta^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{A}}_{m}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau})=\sup_{0\not=\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\in\bm{S}_{h,{\bm{p}}}^{\tau,q}}\frac{{A_{h,m}}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h})}{\left\|\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\right\|_{X}},\ \ \eta^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{S}}_{m}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau})=\sup_{0\not=\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\in\bm{S}_{h,{\bm{p}}+1}^{\tau,{q}}}\frac{{A_{h,m}}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h})}{\left\|\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\right\|_{X}},\ \ \eta^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{T}}_{m}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau})=\sup_{0\not=\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\in\bm{S}_{h,{\bm{p}}}^{\tau,{q}+1}}\frac{{A_{h,m}}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau},\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h})}{\left\|\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\right\|_{X}},

(26)

where the space of functions
\bm{S}_{h,{\bm{p}}}^{\tau,q}
is given by (
11
) and
the spaces
\bm{S}_{h,{\bm{p}}+1}^{\tau,{q}}
and
\bm{S}_{h,{\bm{p}}}^{\tau,{q}+1}
are given analogously by an increase of the polynomial
approximation degree by one with respect to space and time, respectively. Particularly, we have

\displaystyle\bm{S}_{h,{\bm{p}}+1}^{\tau,{q}}=

\displaystyle\left\{\boldsymbol{\psi}:{\Omega}\times(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{4};\ \boldsymbol{\psi}(x,t)|_{{\Omega}\times I_{m}}=\sum\nolimits_{l=0}^{q}t^{l}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}(x),\ \ \boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\in\bm{S}_{m,h,{\bm{p}}+1},\ l=0,\dots,q,\ m=0,\dots,r\right\},

(27)

\displaystyle\bm{S}_{h,{\bm{p}}}^{\tau,{q}+1}=

\displaystyle\left\{\boldsymbol{\psi}:{\Omega}\times(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{4};\ \boldsymbol{\psi}(x,t)|_{{\Omega}\times I_{m}}=\sum\nolimits_{l=0}^{q+1}t^{l}\,\boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}(x),\ \ \boldsymbol{\varphi}_{k}\in\bm{S}_{m,h,{\bm{p}}},\ l=0,\dots,q+1,\ m=0,\dots,r\right\},

where (cf. (
8
))

\displaystyle\bm{S}_{m,h,{\bm{p}}+1}=[S_{m,h,{\bm{p}}+1}]^{4}\quad\mbox{with}\quad S_{m,h,{\bm{p}}+1}=\{\varphi:\Omega\to\mathbb{R};\ \varphi(x)|_{K}\in P_{p_{K}+1}(K)\ \forall\,K\in{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}\},\qquad m=0,\dots,r.

(28)

Moreover, the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X}
is defined by

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\right\|_{X}=\left(\left\|\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}({\Omega}\times I_{m})}^{2}+\left\|\nabla\boldsymbol{\psi}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}({\Omega}\times I_{m})}^{2}+\left\|\partial_{t}{\boldsymbol{\psi}}_{h}\right\|_{L^{2}({\Omega}\times I_{m})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

(29)

The quantity
\eta^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{A}}_{m}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau})
represents the
inaccuracy of the solution of the algebraic system (
19
) since it vanishes
if (
19
) is solved exactly. In the same spirit,
\eta^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{S}}_{m}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau})
and
\eta^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{T}}_{m}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau})

represent the inaccuracy of the spatial and temporal discretization, respectively.
Particularly, they vanish if the approximate solution is equal to the exact one.
The numerical study of these error estimators was presented in
stdgm_est
.
We note that due to the choice of the norm (
29
), the estimators in (
26
)
can be evaluated as the sum of local estimators computed for each space-time
element
K\times I_{m}
,
K\in{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}
separately.
These local estimators are computable by standard tools of the
constrained minimization since the corresponding systems of equations are small and
thus the evaluation of (
26
) is fast.

Employing estimators (
26
), we define the stopping criterion of the iterative method
(
22
) by

\displaystyle\eta^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{A}}_{m}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau})\leq c_{A}\min\left(\eta^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{S}}_{m}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}),\eta^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{T}}_{m}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau})\right),\quad m=1,\dots,r,

(30)

where
c_{A}\in(0,1)
.
It means that the algebraic error estimator has to be bounded with respect
to the spatial and temporal ones.
Similarly, we choose adaptively the time step
\tau_{m}
,
m=1,\dots,r
such that
spatial and temporal error estimators are balanced, particularly, we set the time step
such that

\displaystyle\eta^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{T}}_{m}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau})\approx c_{T}\eta^{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{S}}_{m}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}),\quad m=1,\dots,r,

(31)

for some
c_{T}\in(0,1)
. In practice, we employ the values
c_{A}=0.01
and
c_{T}=0.2
.

5
Mesh adaptation

Mesh adaptive methods try to reduce the number of
degrees of freedom (
\mathrm{DoF}
) while the accuracy is preserved.
This is beneficial namely for the simulation of evolution problems with traveling phenomena
having sharp interfaces. The idea of the presented
adaptation procedure is to generate the sequence
of the spaces
S_{m,h,{\bm{p}}}
,
m=0,\dots,r
(i.e., the meshes
{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}
and the corresponding
polynomial degrees
p_{K}
,
K\in{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}
) such that

(i)

the interpolation error of a selected scalar quantity
is under the given tolerance
\omega>0
,

(ii)

the number of degrees of freedom (
={N_{h,m}}
, cf. (
21
)) is minimal for each
m=0,\dots,r
.

More precisely, let
{q}_{h\tau}={q}_{h\tau}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}):{\Omega}\times(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}
denote a scalar
solution-dependent quantity, e.g., the density and the Mach number are commonly
used for aerodynamic problems,
cf.
BelmeDervieuxAlauzet_JCP12
,
AlauzetLoseille_JCP18
,
FreyAlauzet
,
HabashiAll
.
Then condition
(i)
means

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{q}_{h\tau}(t)-\Pi\widehat{q}_{h\tau}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}({\Omega})}\leq\omega\quad\forall t\in I_{m},\ m=1,\dots,r,

(33)

where
\widehat{q}_{h\tau}(t)\in S_{m,h,{\bm{p}}+1}
, cf. (
28
)
is the higher-order reconstruction of the scalar piecewise polynomial function

{q}_{h\tau}(t)={q}_{h\tau}({{\bm{w}}}_{h\tau}(\cdot,t))
for
t\in I_{m}
and
\Pi
is the
L^{2}({\Omega})
-projection to
S_{m,h,{\bm{p}}}
.
In practice, we relax condition (
33
) and require

Algorithm 1

Space-time anisotropic
hp
-mesh adaptive algorithm.

The
hp
-AMA method sets an optimal size of mesh elements by the equidistribution
of an interpolation error estimate, i.e., the aim is to fulfill the condition

\displaystyle\max_{t\in\mathcal{J}_{m}}\left\|\widehat{q}_{h\tau}(t)-\Pi\widehat{q}_{h\tau}(t)\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}\approx\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{\#{\mathscr{T}}_{h}}}\quad\forall K\in{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}},\ m=1,\dots,r,

(35)

where
\#{\mathscr{T}}_{h}
is the number of elements of
{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}
.
Moreover, the shape and orientation of mesh elements are optimized locally in such a way that
for each
K\in{{\mathscr{T}}_{h,m}}
, we fix its size and seek a new shape and orientation by minimizing
the interpolation error estimate. Similarly, we can modify the polynomial approximation
degree such that we fix the density of the number of degrees of freedom and
minimize the interpolation error estimate. This estimate depends on the
(
p_{K}+1
)-directional derivatives of the reconstructed solution
\widehat{q}_{h\tau}
.

There is a natural question of which quantity
{q}_{h\tau}
should be used for the higher-order
reconstruction.
Solving non-hydrostatic mesoscale atmospheric problems, the very frequent quantity of
interest is the perturbation of the potential temperature
\Theta
, cf. (
4
)
with respect to the initial state. Hence, it seems to be favorable to employ
\Theta

in the
hp
-AMA algorithm. Nevertheless, numerical experiments in
Section
5.1
demonstrate that the simple choice
{q}_{h\tau}
as the density
is also possible.

Remark 5.1
.

We are aware that the computational time necessary for the solution of algebraic systems
(the most time-consuming part of the whole computational process) does not
depend only on the size of these systems. It is influenced also by the
shape of elements, mesh grading, polynomial approximation degrees and the size of time steps.
Nevertheless, the reduction of the number of
\mathrm{DoF}
typically accelerates numerical simulations.

7
Conclusion

We presented the
hp\tau
-adaptive space-time discontinuous Galerkin method for the
numerical solution of non-hydrostatic mesoscale atmospheric problems.
The time discontinuous discretization with a weak coupling by the penalty
admits using different
meshes at different time levels in a natural way. We demonstrated by numerical experiments
that the complete re-meshing during the computational process does not significantly affect
the accuracy of the approximation. Moreover, measuring the interpolation error
in terms of the density is sufficient for the anisotropic mesh adaptation algorithm
to generate appropriately adapted meshes, namely to capture physically relevant quantity as
the perturbation of the potential temperature. The robustness of the adaptive algorithm
is supported by numerical simulation of several benchmarks.

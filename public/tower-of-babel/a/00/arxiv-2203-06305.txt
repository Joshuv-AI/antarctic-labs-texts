Min-max free boundary minimal surface of genus g ≥ 1 g\geq 1
Yuchin Sun

Abstract
. In this paper, we build up a min-max theory for minimal surfaces using sweepouts of surfaces of genus g ≥ 1 g\geq 1 and m ≥ 1 m\geq 1 ideal boundary components. We show that the width for the area functional can be achieved by a bubble tree limit consisting of branched genus g g free boundary minimal surfaces with nodes, and possibly finitely many branched minimal spheres and free boundary minimal disks. Our result extends the min-max theory by [ 4 ] [ 10 ] [ 9 ] to all genera.



1.
Introduction

Existence theory of minimal surfaces is a fundamental object in Riemannian geometry. There are lots of interesting results concerning general existence theory using conformal harmonic parametrization. Sacks-Uhlenbeck developed a general existence theory for minimal surfaces in compact manifold using Morse theory for perturbed energy functional
[
15
]
. Micallef-Moore used Sacks-Uhlenbeck’s theory to prove the topological sphere theorem
[
12
]
. Chen-Tian gave a general existence theorem for minimal surfaces of arbitrary genus by extending
[
15
]
to stratified Riemann surfaces. For free boundary minimal surfaces, Fraser
[
5
]
developed a partial Morse theory for finding minimal disk with free boundary in any co-dimensions using the perturbed energy functional approach by Sacks-Uhlenbeck, and proved the existence of solutions with bounded Morse index when the relative homotopy group is nontrivial.

Colding and Minicozzi used min-max theory to construct min-max minimal spheres and proved the finite time extinction for three-dimensional Ricci flow under certain topological conditions
[
4
]
. Inspired by the work of Douglas and Radó they replaced the area by the Dirichlet energy functionaly, and inspired by the Birkhoff’s so-called curve-shortening procedure, gave a hramonic replacement procedure. Generalizations for tori and higher genus surfaces was performed by Zhou in
[
16
]
[
17
]
. Following the work by Colding and Minicozzi, the free boundary minimal disk was proved in
[
10
]
by Zhou, Lin and Ao, and in
[
9
]
by Laurain and Petrides. In the free boundary case, in order to perform the harmonic replacement procedure, one needs to get the uniqueness of free boundary harmonic map with small energy with respect to the partial Dirichlet boundary data. It’s much harder in the general case for free boundary harmonic map and was solved by Zhou, Lin and Ao in
[
10
]
, and by Laurain and Petrides in
[
9
]
. In this paper, we extend the results of
[

]

[

1.1.
Main Result

Given a closed Riemannian manifold
M
and an embedded submanifold
N
of co-dimension at least one. Let
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
be a bordered Riemann surface of genus
g\geq 1
and with
m\geq 1
ideal boundary components, and none of the ideal boundary component is a puncture. We consider the following variational space:

\mathbin{\Omega}=\left\{\mathbin{\gamma}:\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\times[0,1]\to M,\begin{aligned} &\mathbin{\gamma}(\cdot,t):[0,1]\to C^{0}(\bar{\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{0}},M)\cap W^{1,2}(\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{0},M)\text{ is continuous, }\\
&\text{and }\mathbin{\gamma}(\cdot,t)(\partial\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{0})\subset N,\>\forall t\in[0,1],\\
&\text{and }\mathbin{\gamma}(\cdot,0),\mathbin{\gamma}(\cdot,1)\text{ are constant maps.}\end{aligned}\right\},

and we call
\mathbin{\gamma}\in\mathbin{\Omega}
a
sweepout
. Denote by
\mathbin{\Omega}_{\mathbin{\beta}}
the homotopy class of
\mathbin{\beta}
in
\mathbin{\Omega}
. The width corresponding to a given homotopy class
\mathbin{\Omega}_{\mathbin{\beta}}
is defined to be

\mathbin{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbin{\Omega}_{\mathbin{\beta}}):=\inf_{\mathbin{\gamma}\in\mathbin{\Omega}_{\mathbin{\beta}}}\max_{t\in[0,1]}\text{Area}(\mathbin{\gamma}(t)).

A smooth bordered Riemann
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
is said to be of type
(g,m)
if
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
is topologically a sphere attached with
g
handles and
m
disks removed. It is topologically equivalent to a compact surface of genus
g
with
m
punctures. A nodal bordered Riemann surface is of type
(g,m)
if it is a degeneration of a smooth bordered Riemann surface of the same type.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any homotopically nontrivial sweepout
\mathbin{\gamma}\in\mathbin{\Omega}
. If
\mathbin{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbin{\Omega}_{\mathbin{\gamma}})>0
, then there exist a bordered Riemann surface of same type
(g,m)
(possibly a nodal bordered Riemann surface), conformal harmonic map
u_{0}:\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}\to M
with free boundary
u_{0}|_{\mathbin{\partial}\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}}\subset N
, finitely many harmonic spheres
v_{i}:S^{2}\to M
, finitely many free boundary minimal disk
\mathbin{\omega}_{j}:D\to N
with
\mathbin{\omega}_{j}|_{\mathbin{\partial}D}\subset N
, and a minimizing sequence
\mathbin{\gamma}_{n}
in
\mathbin{\Omega}_{\mathbin{\gamma}}
,
\{t_{n}\}\subset[0,1]
, such that
\mathbin{\gamma}_{n}(t_{n})
converges to
\{u_{0},v_{i},\mathbin{\omega}_{j}\}
in the bubble tree sense, and

(1)

\text{Area}(u_{0})+\sum_{i,j}\text{Area}(v_{i})+Area(\mathbin{\omega}_{j})=\mathbin{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbin{\Omega}_{\mathbin{\gamma}}).

The novelty of Theorem
1.1
lies on the area identity. Since the width w.r.t. energy functional equals to the width w.r.t. the area functional (see Theorem
4.1
), and the
u_{0}
in Theorem
1.1
is a conformal free boundary harmonic map, the area identity in (
1
) implies the energy identity. The possible loss of energy during the bubble tree convergence has attracted a lot of interests. The energy identity, equivalent to no loss of energy, has played an important role in the study of geometric analysis
[
13
]
[
3
]
[
8
]
[
14
]
. In Theorem
1.1
, the minimizing sequence
\mathbin{\gamma}_{n}(t_{n})
is actually defined on domain with varying conformal structure. The energy identity of such sequence doesn’t hold trure for general case, see
[
13
]
and
[
11
]
.

The organization of the paper is the following: in section 2 we review various definitions and properties of the Teichmüller space of bordered Riemann Surfaces, in section 3 we define what’s the homotopy equivalence for sweepout defined on different domain, in section 4 we prove that the width w.r.t. area functional is equivalent to the width w.r.t. energy functional, and we can perturb the minimizing sequence so that the energy and area of it is sufficiently close, in section 5 we build a systematic replacement procedure for free boundary harmonic replacement, in section 6 we state the convergence result, discuss the case where the domain surface degenerates and prove Theorem
1.1
.

2.1.
Teichmüler space of closed Riemann surfaces

Marked Surface and Fuchsian Group:

Given a fixed closed Riemann surface
\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0}
of genus
g\geq 2
, consider all the surfaces
(\mathbin{\Sigma},f)
, where.
f:\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0}\to\mathbin{\Sigma}
is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism. We say that
(\mathbin{\Sigma},f)
and
(\mathbin{\Sigma}^{\prime},g)
are equivalent if
g\circ f^{-1}:\mathbin{\Sigma}\to\mathbin{\Sigma}^{\prime}
is homotopic to a conformal diffeomorphism from
\mathbin{\Sigma}
to
\mathbin{\Sigma}^{\prime}
. We call
(\mathbin{\Sigma},f)
a
marked surface
. The set of all equivalent classes of marked surfaces
\{[(\mathbin{\Sigma},f)]\}
is the Teichmüller spaces
\mathbin{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0})
of
\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0}
. By the Uniformization Theorem, all the closed surfaces
\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0}
with genus
g\geq 2
have their universal covering space the upper half plane
\mathbin{\mathbb{H}}
. The covering transformatin group of
\pi:\mathbin{\mathbb{H}}\to\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0}
is called
Fuchsian group
, which will be denoted by
\mathbin{\Gamma}
and
(\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0},\mathbin{\Gamma})
is called
Fuchsian model
. The holomorphic diffeomorphism group of
\mathbin{\mathbb{H}}
is PSL
(2,\mathbin{\mathbb{R}})
, so
\mathbin{\Gamma}\subset\text{PSL}(2,\mathbin{\mathbb{R}})
. Given a Fuchsian model
(\mathbin{\Sigma},\mathbin{\Gamma})
, there’s a set of normalized generators
\{\mathbin{\alpha}_{i},\mathbin{\beta}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{g}
for
\mathbin{\Gamma}
, where
\mathbin{\alpha}_{g}
has attractive fixed point at
1
and
\mathbin{\beta}_{g}
has repelling and attractive fixed point at
0
and
\infty
respectively. Moreover, the set of normalized generators is uniquely determined by the equivalent class in
\mathbin{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0})
.
\{\mathbin{\alpha}_{i}\}
,
\{\mathbin{\beta}_{i}\}
can be uniquely written as
\mathbin{\alpha}_{i}=\frac{a_{i}z+b_{i}}{c_{i}z+d_{i}}\in\text{PSL}(2,\mathbin{\mathbb{R}})
and
\mathbin{\beta}_{i}=\frac{a_{i}^{\prime}z+b_{i}^{\prime}}{c_{i}^{\prime}z+d_{i}^{\prime}}\in\text{PSL}(2,\mathbin{\mathbb{R}})
. Hence we can define the
Fricke coordinates
:

\mathbin{\mathcal{F}}_{g}:\mathbin{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0})\to\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}^{6g-6}
as the following

\mathbin{\mathcal{F}}_{g}([(\mathbin{\Sigma},f)])=(a_{i},c_{i},d_{i},a_{i}^{\prime},c_{i}^{\prime},d_{i}^{\prime})_{i=1}^{g-1}.

\mathbin{\mathcal{F}}_{g}
is injective by
[
7
, Theorem 2.25]
. Hence we have an induced topology on
\mathbin{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbin{\Sigma})
by the Fricke coordinates.

Quasi-conformal Map:

We introduce quasi-conformal maps and combine the marked surface model with the quasi-conformal maps. Let
f:\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0}\to\mathbin{\Sigma}
be an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism between two Riemann surfaces. Given local complex coordinates
(z,\bar{z})
,
(w,\bar{w})
on
\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0}
and
\mathbin{\Sigma}
. Denote
f(z)=w\circ f\circ z
. The Beltrami coefficient
\mu_{f}
is defined by
\frac{f_{\bar{z}}}{f_{z}}
. If
|\mu_{f}|\leq k<1
, then we call such
f
a quasi-conformal map. Given a marked surface
(\mathbin{\Sigma},f)
,
f:\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0}\to\mathbin{\Sigma}
. By
[
7
, Theorem 5.8]
, there exists a unique holomorphic quadratic differential
\phi
on
\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0}
with
L^{1}
-norm
\|\phi\|
less than
1
, and a unique quasi-conformal map
f_{\phi}:\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0}\to\mathbin{\Sigma}
homotopic to
f
, such that
\mu_{f_{\phi}}=\|\phi\|\frac{\bar{\phi}}{|\phi|}
. Denote the set of all holomorphic quadratic differentials on
\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0}
with
L^{1}
-norm less than one by
\mathbin{\mathcal{Q}}^{1}_{\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0}}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
7
, Theorem 5.15]

\mathcal{F}:\mathbin{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0}}^{1}\to\mathbin{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0})
defined by
\mathcal{F}(\phi)=[(\mathbin{\Sigma},f_{\phi})]
is a homeomorphism.

Remark 2.2
.

Since the map
\mathbin{\mathcal{F}}
is a homeomorphism and the equivalent class of each marked surface uniquely determines a set of normalized generators, then the map defined by

\Phi:\mathbin{\mathcal{Q}}^{1}_{\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0}}\to\mathbin{\mathcal{F}}_{g}(\mathbin{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0})),\quad\Phi(\phi)=\mathbin{\mathcal{F}}_{g}([(\mathbin{\Sigma},f_{\phi})])

is also a homeomorphism.

So we know that for a given
\phi\in\mathbin{\mathcal{Q}}^{1}_{\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0}}
, there exists the corresponding equivalent class of a marked surface
\mathcal{F}(\phi)=[(\mathbin{\Sigma},f_{\phi})]
and the corresponding normalized Fuchsian model
(\mathbin{\Sigma}_{0},\mathbin{\Gamma}).

2.2.
Teichmüler space of bordered Riemann surfaces

Now we consider Riemann surface with boundary. Let
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}

\mathbin{\zeta}

\mathbin{\zeta}

\mathbin{\beta}

\mathbin{\Gamma}

\mathbin{\beta}

\mathbin{\beta}

joining two fixed points of
\mathbin{\beta}

\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}

is separating
\mathbin{\mathbb{H}}

projects to a neighborhood of

\partial A

\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}

We define the double

\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}^{0}\supset N\xrightarrow{z_{\mathbin{\alpha}}}\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}

of
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}
. Then
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}^{d}=\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}\cup_{\sigma}\bar{\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}}
.

Now we focus on the case

and
m\geq 1
so that the double
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}^{d}
carries a hyperbolic structure and

Claim 2.3
.

Each border component
\partial\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}
of
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}
is the geodesic in its free homotopy class on
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}^{d}
.

Definition 2.4
.

We call
\mathbin{\omega}
an admissible quadratic differential on
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}
if
\mathbin{\omega}
is the restriction to
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}
of a holomorphic quadratic differential
\mathbin{\omega}^{d}
on the doubled surface
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}^{d}
such that
\int_{\partial\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}}\sqrt{\mathbin{\omega}}\in\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}

.

Let
\mathbin{\omega}
be an admissible quadratic differential on
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}
. Away from zeros of
\mathbin{\omega}
we can define a local coordinate by

\xi+i\eta=\int\sqrt{w}.

If we let
\xi^{\prime}+i\eta^{\prime}=K\xi+i\eta

\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}
away from zeros. Such conformal structure extends to the zeros so we have a new conformal structure

. We call this new conformal structure

of
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}
given by
\mathbin{\omega}
and

. As above,
\mathbin{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}}
denote the space of admissible quadratic differentials on
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}

\mathbin{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}}^{1}

\mathbin{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}}
.

Definition 2.5
.

The Teichmüller space
\mathbin{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbin{\mathcal{S}})
on a bordered Riemann surface

is defined as the set of all the Teichmüller deformation

\mathbin{\omega}\in\mathbin{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}}^{1}
with the topology induced by that on
\mathbin{\mathcal{Q}}^{1}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}}
.

2.5
. Given
\mathbin{\omega}\in\mathbin{\mathcal{Q}}_{\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}}^{1}

2.4

w

\mathbin{\omega}^{d}

3.
Homotopy equivalence of the variational space

Given a closed Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, isometrically embedded in
\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, and an embedded submanifold

\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
be a fixed Riemann surface of genus
g\geq 1
with

\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{0}^{d}

\mathbin{\varsigma}(t)\in\mathbin{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{0})

Definition 3.1
.

The variational spaces are defined as

\mathbin{\Omega}=\left\{\mathbin{\gamma}:\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{0}\times[0,1]\to M,\begin{aligned} &\mathbin{\gamma}(\cdot,t):[0,1]\to C^{0}(\bar{\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{0}},M)\cap W^{1,2}(\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{0},M)\text{ is continuous, }\\

&\text{and }\mathbin{\gamma}(\cdot,0),\mathbin{\gamma}(\cdot,1)\text{ are constant maps.}\end{aligned}\right\},

\tilde{\mathbin{\Omega}}=\left\{(\mathbin{\gamma}(t),\mathbin{\varsigma}(t)),\>\begin{aligned} &\mathbin{\gamma}(\cdot,t):[0,1]\to C^{0}(\bar{\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}}_{\mathbin{\varsigma}(t)}(t),M)\cap W^{1,2}(\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbin{\varsigma}(t)},M)\text{ is continuous, }\\
&\text{and }\mathbin{\varsigma}\in C^{0}([0,1],\mathbin{\mathcal{T}}(\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{0})),\\
&\text{and }\mathbin{\gamma}(\cdot,t)(\partial\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbin{\varsigma}(t)})\subset N,\>\forall t\in[0,1],\\

\mathbin{\gamma}

For a given

Definition 3.2

(Width)
.

We denote

and
\text{Area}(\cdot)
as the Dirichlet energy and area functional. Associated to each homotopy class
\mathbin{\Omega}_{\mathbin{\gamma}}

\mathbin{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbin{\Omega}_{\mathbin{\gamma}}):=\inf_{\mathbin{\rho}\in\mathbin{\Omega}_{\mathbin{\gamma}}}\max_{t\in[0,1]}\text{Area}(\mathbin{\rho}(t)).

Similarly, for a homotopy class

\mathbin{\mathcal{W}}_{E}(\tilde{\mathbin{\Omega}}_{(\mathbin{\gamma},\mathbin{\varsigma})}):=\inf_{(\mathbin{\rho},\mathbin{\varsigma})\in{\tilde{\mathbin{\Omega}}_{(\mathbin{\gamma},\mathbin{\varsigma})}}}\max_{t\in[0,1]}E(\mathbin{\rho}(t),\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbin{\varsigma}(t)}).

Definition 3.3
.

Given a sweepout
\tilde{\mathbin{\gamma}}\in\mathbin{\Omega}
, it has the corresponding homotopy class
\mathbin{\Omega}_{\tilde{r}}
. We called a sequence of sweepouts
\{\mathbin{\gamma}_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbin{\mathbb{N}}}\subset\mathbin{\Omega}_{\tilde{\mathbin{\gamma}}}
a minimizing sequence if

\lim_{j\to\infty}\max_{t\in[0,1]}\text{Area}(\mathbin{\gamma}_{j}(t))=\mathbin{\mathcal{W}}(\mathbin{\Omega}_{\tilde{\mathbin{\gamma}}}).

6.
Convergence results

6.1.
Degeneration of the conformal structure

We’ve defined bordered Riemann surface back in section
2.2
, and mentioned that the fixed bordered Riemann surface
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}_{0}
that we use as domain for the sweepout is a bordered Riemann surface with genus
g\geq 1
and
m\geq 1
ideal boundary components, and none of the ideal boundary component is a puncture. Moreover, the double
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}^{d}_{0}
of such bordered Riemann surface carries a hyperbolic structure. For any bordered Riemann surface
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}
, there exists an anti-holomorphic involution map
\sigma:\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}^{d}\to\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}^{d}
such that
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}=\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}^{d}/\sigma
and
\mathbin{\partial}\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}
is just the fixed point set of
\sigma
. Now we introduce
nodal bordered Riemann surface
: a smooth bordered Riemann
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}
is said to be of type
(g,m)
if
\mathbin{\mathcal{S}}
is topologically a sphere attached with
g
handles and
m
disks removed. It is topologically equivalent to a compact surface of genus
g
with
m
punctures. A nodal bordered Riemann surface is of type
(g,m)
if it is a degeneration of a smooth bordered Riemann surface of the same type.

There are three types of nodes for a nodal bordered Riemann surface. Let
(z,w)
be the coordinate on
\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
and
\sigma(z,w)=(\bar{z},\bar{w})
be the complex conjugation. A node on a bordered Riemann surface is a singularity which is locally isomorphic to one of the following:

(1)

Interior node:
(0,0)\in\{zw=0\}

(2)

Type I boundary node:
(0,0)\in\{z^{2}-w^{2}=0\}/\sigma

(3)

Type II boundary node:
(0,0)\in\{z^{2}+w^{2}=0\}/\sigma

1:

Interior node

Let
U
be a neighborhood of
(0,0)\in\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
and
\mathbin{\Sigma}=\{(z,w)\in U|zw=0\}
. Then
\mathbin{\Sigma}
has two components
\mathbin{\Sigma}_{1}=\{(z,w)\in U|z=w\}
and
\mathbin{\Sigma}_{2}=\{(z,w)\in U|z=-w\}
, which are attached at the nodal point
\mathbin{\Sigma}_{1}\cap\mathbin{\Sigma}_{2}=\{(0,0)\}
. Note that the metric near the node on each component is flat.

2:

Type I boundary node

Let
\mathbin{\Sigma}=\{(z,w)\in U|z^{2}-w^{2}=0\}
. Then has two components

\mathbin{\Sigma}_{1}=\{(z,w)\in U|z=w\}\text{ and }\mathbin{\Sigma}_{2}=\{(z,w)\in U|z=-w\},

which are attached at the point
\mathbin{\Sigma}_{1}\cap\mathbin{\Sigma}_{2}=\{(0,0)\}
. The map
\sigma
gives an involution on each component
\mathbin{\Sigma}_{i}
. The fixed point set of the involution is
F=\{(z,w)\in\mathbin{\mathbb{C}}^{2}|Im(z)=Im(w)=0\}
. The boundary of
\mathbin{\Sigma}_{1}/\sigma
is just the fixed point set of the involution in
\mathbin{\Sigma}_{1}
, i.e.,
c_{1}=F\cap\mathbin{\Sigma}_{1}=\{z=w\in\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}^{1}\}
, which is a 1-dimensional curve. Similarly, the boundary of
\mathbin{\Sigma}_{2}/\sigma
is the curve
c_{2}=F\cap\mathbin{\Sigma}_{2}=\{z=-w\in\mathbin{\mathbb{R}}^{1}\}
. Thus the boundary curves
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
intersects at the node
(0,0)
.

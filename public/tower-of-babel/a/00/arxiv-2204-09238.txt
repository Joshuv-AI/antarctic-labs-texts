Genera and crossing numbers of 2 2 -bridge knots Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification . Primary 57K31, Secondary 57K32.
Masaaki Suzuki, Anh T. Tran

Abstract
. In this paper, we determine the average genus of all the 2 2 -bridge knots with a given crossing number. As a consequence, we obtain the oblique asymptote of this value as the crossing number grows.



1.
Introduction

Dunfield provided experimental data in
[
6
]

which suggests that the genus of any knot grows linearly with respect to its crossing number.
It is known that the family of
2
-bridge knots is a fundamental class of knots.
Many knot properties are first studied for
2
-bridge knots before for the general case.

Let
\overline{g}_{c}
be the average genus of all the
2
-bridge knots with
c
crossings.
Motivated by Dunfield’s result, Cohen in
[
2
]

[
3
]
gave lower and upper bounds on
\overline{g}_{c}
in terms of
c
.
In this paper, we will determine the explicit value of
\overline{g}_{c}
.
As a consequence, we can obtain the oblique asymptote of
\overline{g}_{c}
.
That is to say, the following is the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem 1
.

For
c\geq 3
, we have

\overline{g}_{c}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\frac{1}{4}c+\frac{1}{12}+\frac{c-5}{2^{c}-4}}&c\equiv 0\pmod{2}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{4}c+\frac{1}{12}+\frac{1}{3\cdot 2^{(c-3)/2}}}&c\equiv 1\pmod{4}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{4}c+\frac{1}{12}+\frac{2^{(c+1)/2}-3c+11}{12\left(2^{c-3}+2^{(c-3)/2}+1\right)}}&c\equiv 3\pmod{4}\end{array}\right..

In particular,
\overline{g}_{c}\sim\displaystyle{\frac{1}{4}c+\frac{1}{12}}
as
c\to\infty
.

This result supports Dunfield’s observation affirmatively.
Cohen and Lowrance
[
4
]
obtained the same asymptote independently.

2.
Preliminary

In this section, we review some known facts about
2
-bridge knots from
[
8
]
,
[
5
]
,
and show some fundamental formulas about binomial coefficients.

It is known that a
2
-bridge knot corresponds to a rational number.
We denote by
K([a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}])
the
2
-bridge knot determined by a continued fraction

[a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n}]=\frac{1}{a_{1}+\frac{1}{a_{2}+\frac{1}{\ddots\frac{1}{a_{n}}}}}.

The genus of the
2
-bridge knot corresponding to a rational number is a half of the length of the even continued fraction.
Namely, if we take the even continued fraction
[2a_{1},2a_{2},\ldots,2a_{2m}]
of a rational number where
a_{i}\neq 0
,
then the genus of
K=K([2a_{1},2a_{2},\ldots,2a_{2m}])
is
m
.
Remark that the lengths of the even continued fractions for
2
-bridge knots are always even.
Moreover, the crossing number
c
of
K
is given by

c=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2m}2|a_{i}|\right)-\ell

where
\ell
is the number of sign changes in the sequence
(2a_{1},2a_{2},\ldots,2a_{2m})
(See
[
9
]
,
[
11
]
, and
[
10
]
).
Since
|a_{i}|\geq 1
and
0\leq\ell\leq 2m-1
,
we have
4m\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2m}2|a_{i}|=c+\ell\leq c+(2m-1)
.
This implies that
2m\leq c-1
and then
0\leq\ell\leq 2m-1\leq c-2
.
Since
\sum_{i=1}^{2m}2|a_{i}|
is even,
\ell
and
c
have the same parity.
For each
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{2m})
where
b_{i}\geq 1
,
let
S_{\mathbf{b},\ell}=S_{(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{2m}),\ell}
be the set of all the
2
-bridge knots
K([2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m}])

such that
a_{i}=\pm b_{i}
and that the number of sign changes in the sequence
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{2m})
is exactly
\ell
.
For example, we have

S_{(1,1),0}=\{K([2,2]\},\quad S_{(1,1),1}=\{K([2,-2],K([-2,2])\}.

Note that
K([2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m}])
and
K([-2a_{2m},-2a_{2m-1},\dots,-2a_{1}])
represent the same
2
-bridge knot.
Moreover,
K([-2a_{1},-2a_{2},\dots,-2a_{2m}])=K([2a_{2m},2a_{2m-1},\dots,2a_{1}])

is the mirror image of
K([2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m}])=K([-2a_{2m},-2a_{2m-1},\dots,-2a_{1}])
.
These two knots are distinct if and only if
(2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m})
is not symmetric.

In
[
7
]
, Ernst and Sumners determined the number of
2
-bridge knots with respect to crossing number.

Theorem 2.1

(Ernst-Sumners
[
7
]
)
.

For
c\geq 3
, the number of all the
2
-bridge knots with
c
crossings is given by

TK(c)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\frac{1}{3}(2^{c-2}-1)}&c\equiv 0\pmod{2}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{3}(2^{c-2}+2^{(c-1)/2})}&c\equiv 1\pmod{4}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{3}(2^{c-2}+2^{(c-1)/2}+2)}&c\equiv 3\pmod{4}\end{array}\right..

In this paper, we make use of several identities on binomial coefficients in order to prove Theorem
1
.
In addition to well-known formulas:

\binom{a}{b}\binom{b}{c}=\binom{a}{c}\binom{a-c}{b-c},\quad\sum_{q=0}^{n}\binom{n}{q}=2^{n},\quad\sum_{q=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\binom{n}{2q}=2^{n-1},\quad\sum_{q=0}^{n}q\binom{n}{q}=n2^{n-1},

we prepare the following.

Lemma 2.2
.

We have

\sum_{q=0}^{n-1}x^{q}\binom{2n-1-q}{q}=\frac{\left(\frac{2x+1+\sqrt{4x+1}}{2}\right)^{n}-\left(\frac{2x+1-\sqrt{4x+1}}{2}\right)^{n}}{\sqrt{4x+1}},

and

\sum_{q=0}^{n}x^{q}\binom{2n-q}{q}=\frac{\frac{\sqrt{4x+1}+1}{2}\left(\frac{2x+1+\sqrt{4x+1}}{2}\right)^{n}+\frac{\sqrt{4x+1}-1}{2}\left(\frac{2x+1-\sqrt{4x+1}}{2}\right)^{n}}{\sqrt{4x+1}}.

In particular, it follows that

\sum_{q=0}^{n-1}2^{q}\binom{2n-1-q}{q}=\frac{4^{n}-1}{3}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{q=0}^{n}2^{q}\binom{2n-q}{q}=\frac{2\cdot 4^{n}+1}{3}.

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{n}=\sum_{q=0}^{n-1}x^{q}\binom{2n-1-q}{q}
and
\beta_{n}=\sum_{q=0}^{n}x^{q}\binom{2n-q}{q}
. Then

\displaystyle\alpha_{n+1}-\beta_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{n}x^{q}\left[\binom{2n+1-q}{q}-\binom{2n-q}{q}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{q=1}^{n}x^{q}\binom{2n-q}{q-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\sum_{q=1}^{n}x^{q-1}\binom{2n-1-(q-1)}{q-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\sum_{q^{\prime}=0}^{n-1}x^{q^{\prime}}\binom{2n-1-q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\alpha_{n}.

This implies that
\beta_{n}=\alpha_{n+1}-x\alpha_{n}
.

Similarly

\displaystyle\beta_{n}-\alpha_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{n}x^{q}\left[\binom{2n-q}{q}-\binom{2n-1-q}{q}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{q=1}^{n}x^{q}\binom{2n-1-q}{q-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\sum_{q=1}^{n}x^{q-1}\binom{2n-2-(q-1)}{q-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\sum_{q^{\prime}=0}^{n-1}x^{q^{\prime}}\binom{2n-2-q^{\prime}}{q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\beta_{n-1}.

This implies that

\alpha_{n}=\beta_{n}-x\beta_{n-1}=(\alpha_{n+1}-x\alpha_{n})-x(\alpha_{n}-x\alpha_{n-1})=\alpha_{n+1}-2x\alpha_{n}+x^{2}\alpha_{n-1}.

Hence
\alpha_{n+1}-(2x+1)\alpha_{n}+x^{2}\alpha_{n-1}=0
. Since
\alpha_{0}=0
and
\alpha_{1}=1
, we obtain

\alpha_{n}=\frac{\left(\frac{2x+1+\sqrt{4x+1}}{2}\right)^{n}-\left(\frac{2x+1-\sqrt{4x+1}}{2}\right)^{n}}{\sqrt{4x+1}}.

Then

\displaystyle\beta_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{n+1}-x\alpha_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\frac{2x+1+\sqrt{4x+1}}{2}\right)^{n+1}-\left(\frac{2x+1-\sqrt{4x+1}}{2}\right)^{n+1}}{\sqrt{4x+1}}-x\frac{\left(\frac{2x+1+\sqrt{4x+1}}{2}\right)^{n}-\left(\frac{2x+1-\sqrt{4x+1}}{2}\right)^{n}}{\sqrt{4x+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{\sqrt{4x+1}+1}{2}\left(\frac{2x+1+\sqrt{4x+1}}{2}\right)^{n}+\frac{\sqrt{4x+1}-1}{2}\left(\frac{2x+1-\sqrt{4x+1}}{2}\right)^{n}}{\sqrt{4x+1}}.

∎

By using the above lemma, we obtain the following identities, which play an important role in the proof of Theorem
1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{n-1}q\,2^{q}\binom{2n-1-q}{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{27}\left((4^{n}-1)(3n-2)-3n\right),

\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{n}q\,2^{q}\binom{2n-q}{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{27}\left((4^{n}-1)(6n-1)+12n\right).

Proof.

Let
a=\frac{2x+1+\sqrt{4x+1}}{2}
and
b=\frac{2x+1-\sqrt{4x+1}}{2}
.
Then their derivatives with respect to
x
are
a^{\prime}=1+\frac{1}{\sqrt{4x+1}}
and
b^{\prime}=1-\frac{1}{\sqrt{4x+1}}
.
Since
\alpha_{n}=\frac{a^{n}-b^{n}}{a-b}
, the derivative of
\alpha_{n}
with respect to
x
is given by

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n(a^{n-1}a^{\prime}-b^{n-1}b^{\prime})(a-b)-(a^{n}-b^{n})(a^{\prime}-b^{\prime})}{(a-b)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n(a^{n-1}-b^{n-1}+(a^{n-1}+b^{n-1})\frac{1}{\sqrt{4x+1}})\sqrt{4x+1}-(a^{n}-b^{n})\frac{2}{\sqrt{4x+1}}}{4x+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n((a^{n-1}-b^{n-1})\sqrt{4x+1}+a^{n-1}+b^{n-1})-(a^{n}-b^{n})\frac{2}{\sqrt{4x+1}}}{4x+1}.

Hence

\displaystyle\alpha^{\prime}_{n}|_{x=2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n[3(4^{n-1}-1)+4^{n-1}+1]-(4^{n}-1)\frac{2}{3}}{9}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{27}((4^{n}-1)(3n-2)-3n).

By
\sum_{q=0}^{n-1}q\,2^{q}\binom{2n-1-q}{q}=2\alpha^{\prime}_{n}|_{x=2}
, we obtain the first desired equality.

Since
\beta_{n}=\alpha_{n+1}-x\alpha_{n}
, we have

\beta^{\prime}_{n}=\alpha^{\prime}_{n+1}-x\alpha^{\prime}_{n}-\alpha_{n}.

Hence

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}_{n}|_{x=2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{9}\left((4^{n+1}-1)(n+\frac{1}{3})-n-1\right)-\frac{2}{9}\left((4^{n}-1)(n-\frac{2}{3})-n\right)-\frac{4^{n}-1}{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{27}((4^{n}-1)(6n-1)+12).

By
\sum_{q=0}^{n}q\,2^{q}\binom{2n-q}{q}=2\beta^{\prime}_{n}|_{x=2}
, we also obtain the second desired equality.
∎

3.
Total genus

In this section, we compute the total genus of
2
-bridge knots with a given crossing number, which is denoted by
TG(c)
for a given crossing number
c
. By definition, we have

TG(c)=\sum_{g}g\cdot\sharp\{K\mbox{: $2$-bridge knot}\,|\,c(K)=c,g(K)=g\},

where
c(K)
and
g(K)
are the crossing number and the genus of a knot
K
respectively.

Proposition 3.1
.

For
c\geq 3
, we have

TG(c)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\frac{1}{36}\left((3c+1)\cdot 2^{c-2}-16\right)}&c\equiv 0\pmod{2}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{36}\left((3c+1)\cdot 2^{c-2}+(3c+5)\cdot 2^{(c-1)/2}+8\right)}&c\equiv 1\pmod{4}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{36}\left((3c+1)\cdot 2^{c-2}+(3c+5)\cdot 2^{(c-1)/2}+24\right)}&c\equiv 3\pmod{4}\end{array}\right..

Combining Theorem
2.1
and Proposition
3.1
, we obtain Theorem
1
.
We will divide the proof of Proposition
3.1
into the cases of even crossing number and odd crossing number.

3.1.
Even crossing number

We first consider the case
c=2k
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Since
c
and
\ell
have the same parity, we may write
\ell=2l
, where
l\in\mathbb{Z}
and
0\leq l\leq k-1
.
For every
0\leq l\leq k-1
, the equation

\sum_{i=1}^{2m}b_{i}=k+l,

where
b_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, has exactly
\binom{k+l-1}{2m-1}
solutions
(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{2m})
.
The number of ways of choosing
a_{i}=\pm b_{i}
such that the number of sign changes among
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{2m})
is exactly
\ell=2l

is equal to twice the number of solutions
(d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{\ell+1})\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1})^{\ell+1}
of the equation

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell+1}d_{i}=2m,

and so it is equal to
2\binom{2m-1}{\ell}=2\binom{2m-1}{2l}
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{2m})
is not symmetric, namely
(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{2m})\neq(b_{2m},\dots,b_{2},b_{1})
.
If
K([2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m}])
belongs to
S_{\mathbf{b},\ell}
,
then

K([2a_{2m},2a_{2m-1},\dots,2a_{1}])
does not belong to
S_{\mathbf{b},\ell}
.
Since the two knots
K([2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m}])
and
K([-2a_{1},-2a_{2},\dots,-2a_{2m}])
in
S_{\mathbf{b},\ell}
are distinct knots.
Hence
S_{\mathbf{b},\ell}
has cardinality
2\binom{2m-1}{\ell}=2\binom{2m-1}{2l}
.
Remark that if
(b_{2m},b_{2m-1},\dots,b_{1})=(b^{\prime}_{1},b^{\prime}_{2},\dots,b^{\prime}_{2m})
, then
S_{(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{2m}),\ell}=S_{(b^{\prime}_{1},b^{\prime}_{2},\dots,b^{\prime}_{2m}),\ell}
.

Suppose that
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{2m})
is symmetric
(this only occurs when
k+l
is even and the number of symmetric cases is equal to
\binom{(k+l)/2-1}{m-1}
).
If
K([2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m}])
belongs to
S_{\mathbf{b},\ell}
,
then both
K([2a_{2m},2a_{2m-1},\dots,2a_{1}])
and
K([-2a_{2m},-2a_{2m-1},\dots,-2a_{1}])
also belong to
S_{\mathbf{b},\ell}
.
We need to consider the two cases where
(2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m})
is symmetric and not.
If
(2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m})
is not symmetric,
then
K([2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m}])=K([-2a_{2m},-2a_{2m-1},\dots,-2a_{1}])
and
K([-2a_{1},-2a_{2},\dots,-2a_{2m}])=K([2a_{2m},2a_{2m-1},\dots,2a_{1}])
are distinct knots.
However,
if
(2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m})
is symmetric, namely
(2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m})=(2a_{2m},2a_{2m-1},\dots,2a_{1})

(the number of symmetric cases is equal to
2\binom{m-1}{l}
),
then
K([2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m}])=K([-2a_{2m},-2a_{2m-1},\dots,-2a_{1}])
and
K([-2a_{1},-2a_{2},\dots,-2a_{2m}])=K([2a_{2m},2a_{2m-1},\dots,2a_{1}])
are the same knot.
Note that
(2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m})\not=(-2a_{2m},-2a_{2m-1},\dots,-2a_{1})
.
(Otherwise, we have
(2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m})=(2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{m},-2a_{m},\dots,-2a_{2},-2a_{1})
which implies that the number of sign changes is an odd number.)
Therefore if
\mathbf{b}
is symmetric,
S_{\mathbf{b},\ell}
has cardinality
\binom{2m-1}{2l}
.
Remark that in this case,
S_{\mathbf{b},\ell}=S_{\mathbf{b}^{\prime},\ell}
if and only if
\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{b}^{\prime}
.

If
k+l
is odd, then
(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{2m})
is not symmetric.
Therefore the number of
2
-bridge knots with crossing number
c=2k
and number of sign changes
\ell=2l
is equal to

A_{l}=\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l)/2}\binom{k+l-1}{2m-1}\cdot 2\binom{2m-1}{2l}\cdot\frac{1}{2}=\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l)/2}\binom{k+l-1}{2l}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-1}

and the total genus, namely, the total number of the genera of these
2
-bridge knots is

B_{l}=\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l)/2}m\binom{k+l-1}{2l}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-1}.

If
k+l
is even, then there are both cases where
(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{2m})
is symmetric and not symmetric.
Therefore the number of
2
-bridge knots with crossing number
c=2k
and number of sign changes
\ell=2l
is equal to

\displaystyle A_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l)/2}\left[\binom{k+l-1}{2m-1}-\binom{(k+l)/2-1}{m-1}\right]\binom{2m-1}{2l}+\binom{(k+l)/2-1}{m-1}\binom{2m-1}{2l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l)/2}\binom{k+l-1}{2m-1}\binom{2m-1}{2l}=\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l)/2}\binom{k+l-1}{2l}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-1}

and the total genus is

B_{l}=\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l)/2}m\binom{k+l-1}{2l}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-1}.

As a consequence,
A_{l}
and
B_{l}
are the same regardless of the parity of
k+l
.

In order to simplify
B_{l}
, we compute
2B_{l}-(2l+1)A_{l}
and
A_{l}
as follows:

\displaystyle 2B_{l}-(2l+1)A_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l)/2}(2m-2l-1)\binom{k+l-1}{2l}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{k+l-1}{2l}\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l)/2}(2m-2l-1)\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}\sum_{m^{\prime}=0}^{(k-l)/2-1}(2m^{\prime}+1)\binom{k-l-1}{2m^{\prime}+1}.

If
0\leq l\leq k-2
, then

\displaystyle A_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{k+l-1}{2l}\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l)/2}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}\sum_{m^{\prime}=0}^{(k-l)/2-1}\binom{k-l-1}{2m^{\prime}+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}\sum_{m^{\prime\prime}=0}^{k-l-1}\binom{k-l-1}{m^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{k-l-2}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}.

If
l=k-1
, then
A_{l}=0=2^{k-l-2}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}2^{k-l-2}-\frac{1}{2}
.
When we take the sum of
B_{l}
with respect to
l
,

B_{l}
depends on the three cases,
0\leq l\leq k-3
,
l=k-2
, and
l=k-1
.
If
0\leq l\leq k-3
, then

\displaystyle 2B_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2B_{l}-(2l+1)A_{l}+(2l+1)A_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}\sum_{m^{\prime\prime}=0}^{k-l-1}m^{\prime\prime}\binom{k-l-1}{m^{\prime\prime}}+(2l+1)2^{k-l-2}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k-l-1}{2}2^{k-l-2}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}+(2l+1)2^{k-l-2}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+3l+1)2^{k-l-3}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}.

If
l=k-2
, then

\displaystyle 2B_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2B_{l}-(2l+1)A_{l}+(2l+1)A_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}+(2l+1)2^{k-l-2}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k-l-1)2^{k-l-3}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}+\frac{2k-3}{2}+(2l+1)2^{k-l-2}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+3l+1)2^{k-l-3}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}+\frac{2k-3}{2}.

If
l=k-1
, then

\displaystyle 2B_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2B_{l}-(2l+1)A_{l}+(2l+1)A_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0+(2l+1)\left[2^{k-l-2}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}-\frac{1}{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k-l-1)2^{k-l-3}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}+(2l+1)2^{k-l-2}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}-\frac{2k-1}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+3l+1)2^{k-l-3}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}-\frac{2k-1}{2}.

By Lemma
2.2
, Lemma
2.3
and
c=2k
, we conclude

\displaystyle TG(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}B_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}(k+3l+1)2^{k-l-3}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2k-3}{2}-\frac{2k-1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}(k+3l+1)2^{k-l-1}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2k-1}{4}\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}2^{k-l-1}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}-\frac{3}{8}\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}(k-l-1)2^{k-l-1}\binom{k+l-1}{k-l-1}-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2k-1}{4}\sum_{q=0}^{k-1}2^{q}\binom{2k-2-q}{q}-\frac{3}{8}\sum_{q=0}^{k-1}q\,2^{q}\binom{2k-2-q}{q}-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2k-1}{4}\,\frac{2(4^{k-1}-1)+1}{3}-\frac{3}{8}\,\frac{2}{27}\left((4^{k-1}-1)(6k-7)+12k-12\right)-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c-1}{4}\,\frac{2(2^{c-2}-1)+1}{3}-\frac{1}{36}\left((2^{c-2}-1)(3c-7)+6c-12\right)-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{36}\left((3c+1)\cdot 2^{c-2}-16\right).

Therefore Proposition
3.1
for the case of even crossing number is proved.

3.2.
Odd crossing number

We now consider the case
c=2k+1
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Similarly, we may write
\ell=2l+1
, where
l\in\mathbb{Z}
and
0\leq l\leq k-1
. For every
0\leq l\leq k-1
, the equation

\sum_{i=1}^{2m}b_{i}=k+l+1,

where
b_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, has exactly
\binom{k+l}{2m-1}
solutions
(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{2m})
.
The number of ways of choosing
a_{i}=\pm b_{i}
such that the number of sign changes among
a_{i}
’s is exactly
\ell=2l+1
is equal to twice the number of solutions
(d_{1},d_{2},\dots,d_{\ell+1})\in(\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1})^{\ell+1}
of the equation

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell+1}d_{i}=2m,

and so it is equal to
2\binom{2m-1}{\ell}=2\binom{2m-1}{2l+1}
.

Since the number of sign changes is odd,
(2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m})
is not symmetric.
Then
K([2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m}])=K([-2a_{2m},-2a_{2m-1},\dots,-2a_{1}])

and
K([2a_{2m},2a_{2m-1},\dots,2a_{1}])=K([-2a_{1},-2a_{2},\dots,-2a_{2m}])
are always distinct knots.

Suppose that
(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{2m})
is not symmetric, then

S_{(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{2m}),\ell}
has cardinality
2\binom{2m-1}{\ell}=2\binom{2m-1}{2l+1}
.
Remark that if
(b_{2m},b_{2m-1},\dots,b_{1})=(b^{\prime}_{1},b^{\prime}_{2},\dots,b^{\prime}_{2m})
, then
S_{(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{2m}),\ell}=S_{(b^{\prime}_{1},b^{\prime}_{2},\dots,b^{\prime}_{2m}),\ell}
.

Suppose that
(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{2m})
is symmetric (this only occurs when
k+l+1
is even and the number of symmetric cases is equal to
\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{m-1}
).
Note that
(-2a_{2m},-2a_{2m-1},\dots,-2a_{1})=(2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m})
if and only if
(2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{2m})=(2a_{1},2a_{2},\dots,2a_{m},-2a_{m},\dots,-2a_{2},-2a_{1})
.
Therefore
S_{(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{2m}),\ell}
has cardinality

\frac{1}{2}(2\binom{2m-1}{\ell}-2\binom{m-1}{l})+2\binom{m-1}{l}=\binom{2m-1}{\ell}+\binom{m-1}{l}=\binom{2m-1}{2l+1}+\binom{m-1}{l}
.

If
k+l
is even, then
(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{2m})
is not symmetric.
Therefore the number of
2
-bridge knots with crossing number
c=2k+1
and number of sign changes
\ell=2l+1
is equal to

A_{l}=\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l)/2}\binom{k+l}{2m-1}\cdot 2\binom{2m-1}{2l+1}\cdot\frac{1}{2}=\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l)/2}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}

and the total genus is

B_{l}=\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l)/2}m\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}.

If
k+l
is odd, then there are both cases where
(b_{1},b_{2},\dots,b_{2m})
is symmetric and not symmetric.
Therefore the number of
2
-bridge knots with crossing number
c=2k+1
and the number of sign changes
\ell=2l+1
is equal to

\displaystyle A_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}\left[\left[\binom{k+l}{2m-1}-\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{m-1}\right]\binom{2m-1}{2l+1}\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\left.\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{m-1}\left[\binom{2m-1}{2l+1}+\binom{m-1}{l}\right]\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}\left[\binom{k+l}{2m-1}\binom{2m-1}{2l+1}+\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{m-1}\binom{m-1}{l}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}\left[\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}+\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{l}\binom{(k-l-1)/2}{m-l-1}\right]

and the total genus is

B_{l}=\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}m\left[\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}+\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{l}\binom{(k-l-1)/2}{m-l-1}\right].

Hence we obtain the total genus

\displaystyle TG(2k+1)=\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}B_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}m\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}

\displaystyle+\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l+k+1\equiv 0\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{k-1}\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}m\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{l}\binom{(k-l-1)/2}{m-l-1}.

Moreover, we simplify this expression by using binomial coefficient formulas.
When we take the sum of
B_{l}
with respect to
l
of the first term,

B_{l}
depends on the three cases,
0\leq l\leq k-3
,
l=k-2
, and
l=k-1
.
If
0\leq l\leq k-3
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}m\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}(m-l-1)\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}+(l+1)\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}(m-l-1)\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}+(l+1)\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\sum_{m^{\prime}=0}^{(k-l-1)/2}m^{\prime}\binom{k-l-1}{2m^{\prime}}+(l+1)\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\sum_{m^{\prime}=0}^{(k-l-1)/2}\binom{k-l-1}{2m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\sum_{m^{\prime\prime}=0}^{k-l-1}m^{\prime\prime}\binom{k-l-1}{m^{\prime\prime}}+\frac{l+1}{2}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\sum_{m^{\prime\prime}=0}^{k-l-1}\binom{k-l-1}{m^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k-l-1}{4}2^{k-l-2}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}+\frac{l+1}{2}2^{k-l-1}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+3l+3)2^{k-l-4}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}.

If
l=k-2
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}m\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}(m-l-1)\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}+(l+1)\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}(m-l-1)\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}+(l+1)\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0+\frac{l+1}{2}2^{k-l-1}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k-l-1}{4}2^{k-l-2}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}-\frac{k-1}{2}+\frac{l+1}{2}2^{k-l-1}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+3l+3)2^{k-l-4}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}-\frac{k-1}{2}.

If
l=k-1
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}m\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(m-l-1)\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}+(l+1)\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}(m-l-1)\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}+(l+1)\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0+(l+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+3l+3)2^{k-l-4}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}+\frac{k}{2}.

Since
-\frac{k-1}{2}+\frac{k}{2}=\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}m\binom{k+l}{2l+1}\binom{k-l-1}{2m-2l-2}=\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}(k+3l+3)2^{k-l-4}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}+\frac{1}{2}.

Similarly, if
l+k+1\equiv 0\pmod{2}
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}m\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{l}\binom{(k-l-1)/2}{m-l-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}(m-l-1)\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{l}\binom{(k-l-1)/2}{m-l-1}

\displaystyle+(l+1)\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{l}\binom{(k-l-1)/2}{m-l-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{l}\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}(m-l-1)\binom{(k-l-1)/2}{m-l-1}

\displaystyle+(l+1)\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{l}\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l+1)/2}\binom{(k-l-1)/2}{m-l-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{l}\sum_{m^{\prime}=0}^{(k-l-1)/2}m^{\prime}\binom{(k-l-1)/2}{m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+(l+1)\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{l}\sum_{m^{\prime}=0}^{(k-l-1)/2}\binom{(k-l-1)/2}{m^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k-l-1}{2}2^{(k-l-3)/2}\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{l}+(l+1)2^{(k-l-1)/2}\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+3l+3)2^{(k-l-5)/2}\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{l}.

Then we obtain

\displaystyle TG(2k+1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}(k+3l+3)2^{k-l-4}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l+k+1\equiv 0\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{k-1}(k+3l+3)2^{(k-l-5)/2}\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}(k+3l+3)2^{k-l-1}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}+\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l+k+1\equiv 0\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{k-1}\frac{k+3l+3}{4}2^{(k-l-1)/2}\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{l}.

Furthermore, the first term of the above expression can be simplified by using the following:

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}(k+3l+3)2^{k-l-1}\binom{k+l}{2l+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}(k+3l+3)2^{k-l-1}\binom{k+l}{k-l-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4k\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}2^{k-l-1}\binom{k+l}{k-l-1}-3\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}(k-l-1)2^{k-l-1}\binom{k+l}{k-l-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4k\sum_{q=0}^{k-1}2^{q}\binom{2k-1-q}{q}-3\sum_{q=0}^{k-1}q\,2^{q}\binom{2k-1-q}{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4k(4^{k}-1)}{3}-\frac{2}{3}\left((4^{k}-1)(k-\frac{2}{3})-k\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{9}\left((3c+1)2^{c-1}-4\right),\qquad c=2k+1.

The third term depends on the parity of
k
.
In both cases, Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
2.3
play an important role to simplify the expression.
If
k=2n
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l+k+1\equiv 0\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{k-1}\frac{k+3l+3}{4}\,2^{(k-l-1)/2}\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}\frac{n+3p+3}{2}\,2^{n-p-1}\binom{n+p}{2p+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2n\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}2^{n-p-1}\binom{n+p}{n-p-1}-\frac{3}{2}\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}(n-p-1)\,2^{n-p-1}\binom{n+p}{n-p-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2n\sum_{q=0}^{n-1}2^{q}\binom{2n-1-q}{q}-\frac{3}{2}\sum_{q=0}^{n-1}q\,2^{q}\binom{2n-1-q}{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2n(4^{n}-1)}{3}-3\cdot\frac{n(4^{n}-2)-(4^{n}-1)\frac{2}{3}}{9}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2n(4^{n}-1)}{3}-\frac{n(4^{n}-2)-(4^{n}-1)\frac{2}{3}}{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4^{n}-1}{3}\left(n+\frac{2}{3}\right)+\frac{n}{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{36}\left((3c+5)\cdot 2^{(c-1)/2}-8\right),\qquad c=4n+1.

Therefore if
c\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, we conclude

\displaystyle TG(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\cdot\frac{1}{9}\left((3c+1)2^{c-1}-4\right)+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{36}\left((3c+5)\cdot 2^{(c-1)/2}-8\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{36}\left((3c+1)\cdot 2^{c-2}+(3c+5)\cdot 2^{(c-1)/2}+8\right).

If
k=2n+1
, then

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l+k+1\equiv 0\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{k-1}\frac{k+3l+3}{4}\,2^{(k-l-1)/2}\binom{(k+l-1)/2}{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{n}\frac{n+3p+2}{2}\,2^{n-p}\binom{n+p}{2p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2n+1)\sum_{p=0}^{n}2^{n-p}\binom{n+p}{n-p}-\frac{3}{2}\sum_{p=0}^{n}(n-p)\,2^{n-p}\binom{n+p}{n-p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2n+1)\sum_{q=0}^{n}2^{q}\binom{2n-q}{q}-\frac{3}{2}\sum_{q=0}^{n}q\,2^{q}\binom{2n-q}{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(2n+1)\,\frac{2\times 4^{n}+1}{3}-\frac{3}{2}\,2\,\left[\frac{4^{n}-1}{3}(\frac{2n}{3}-\frac{1}{9})+\frac{4n}{9}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4^{n}-1}{3}\left(2n+\frac{7}{3}\right)+\frac{2n}{3}+1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{36}\left((3c+5)\cdot 2^{(c-1)/2}+8\right),\qquad c=4n+3.

Therefore if
c\equiv 3\pmod{4}
, we conclude

\displaystyle TG(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\cdot\frac{1}{9}\left((3c+1)2^{c-1}-4\right)+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{36}\left((3c+5)\cdot 2^{(c-1)/2}+8\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{36}\left((3c+1)\cdot 2^{c-2}+(3c+5)\cdot 2^{(c-1)/2}+24\right).

Hence we obtain Proposition
3.1
for odd crossing number and
this completes the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

4.
Average genus

In this section, we show Theorem
1
by using Theorem
2.1
and Proposition
3.1
.

Proof for Theorem
1
.

The average genus
\overline{g}_{c}
is given by
TG(c)/TK(c)
.
Both
TG(c)
and
TK(c)
are obtained by Theorem
2.1
and Proposition
3.1
.

Case 1: for
c\equiv 0\pmod{2}
,

\displaystyle\overline{g}_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{36}\left((3c+1)2^{c-2}-16\right)}{\frac{1}{3}\left(2^{c-2}-1\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left((3c+1)2^{c-2}-(3c+1)-16+(3c+1)\right)}{12\left(2^{c-2}-1\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3c+1}{12}+\frac{3c-15}{12\left(2^{c-2}-1\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{4}+\frac{1}{12}+\frac{c-5}{2^{c}-4}.

Case 2: for
c\equiv 1\pmod{4}
,

\displaystyle\overline{g}_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{36}\left((3c+1)\cdot 2^{c-2}+(3c+5)\cdot 2^{(c-1)/2}+8\right)}{\frac{1}{3}\left(2^{c-2}+2^{(c-1)/2}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(3c+1)\cdot 2^{c-2}+(3c+1)\cdot 2^{(c-1)/2}+4\cdot 2^{(c-1)/2}+8}{12\left(2^{c-2}+2^{(c-1)/2}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3c+1}{12}+\frac{2^{(c-1)/2}+2}{3\left(2^{c-2}+2^{(c-1)/2}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{4}+\frac{1}{12}+\frac{2^{(c-1)/2}+2}{3\cdot 2^{(c-3)/2}\left(2^{(c-1)/2}+2\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{4}+\frac{1}{12}+\frac{1}{3\cdot 2^{(c-3)/2}}.

Case 3: for
c\equiv 3\pmod{4}
,

\displaystyle\overline{g}_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\frac{1}{36}\left((3c+1)\cdot 2^{c-2}+(3c+5)\cdot 2^{(c-1)/2}+24\right)}{\frac{1}{3}\left(2^{c-2}+2^{(c-1)/2+2}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left((3c+1)\cdot 2^{c-2}+(3c+1)\cdot 2^{(c-1)/2}+(3c+1)\cdot 2+4\cdot 2^{(c-1)/2}-6c+22\right)}{12\left(2^{c-2}+2^{(c-1)/2}+2\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{3c+1}{12}+\frac{2\cdot 2^{(c-1)/2}-3c+11}{6\left(2^{c-2}+2^{(c-1)/2}+2\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{c}{4}+\frac{1}{12}+\frac{2^{(c+1)/2}-3c+11}{12\left(2^{c-3}+2^{(c-3)/2}+1\right)}.

∎

5.
Appendix

In this section, a knot is regarded as the same as its mirror image.
This implies that the following four
2
-bridge knots are considered as the same knot:

\displaystyle K([2a_{1},2a_{2},\ldots,2a_{2m}]),K([-2a_{1},-2a_{2},\ldots,-2a_{2m}]),

\displaystyle K([2a_{2m},\ldots,2a_{2},2a_{1}]),K([-2a_{2m},\ldots,-a_{2},-2a_{1}]).

If we do not distinguish a knot with its mirror image,
then the number of
2
-bridge knots and the total genus, and the average genus are different from the above results.
We determine the average genus of
2
-bridge knots with respect to crossing number, up to mirror image.

Ernst and Sumners in
[
7
]
also gave the number of
2
-bridge knots with respect to crossing number, up to mirror image.

Theorem 5.1

(Ernst-Sumners
[
7
]
)
.

For
c\geq 3
, the number of
2
-bridge knots with
c
crossings up to mirror image is given by

TK^{*}(c)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\frac{1}{3}(2^{c-3}+2^{(c-4)/2})}&c\equiv 0\pmod{4}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{3}(2^{c-3}+2^{(c-3)/2})}&c\equiv 1\pmod{4}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{3}(2^{c-3}+2^{(c-4)/2}-1)}&c\equiv 2\pmod{4}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{3}(2^{c-3}+2^{(c-3)/2}+1)}&c\equiv 3\pmod{4}\end{array}\right..

We get the total genus of
2
-bridge knots with respect to crossing number up to mirror image, which is denoted by
TG^{*}(c)

in a similar way.

Proposition 5.2
.

For
c\geq 3
, we have

TG^{*}(c)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle{\frac{1}{72}\left((3c+1)\cdot 2^{c-2}+(3c+2)\cdot 2^{(c-2)/2}-8\right)}&c\equiv 0\pmod{4}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{72}\left((3c+1)\cdot 2^{c-2}+(3c+5)\cdot 2^{(c-1)/2}+8\right)}&c\equiv 1\pmod{4}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{72}\left((3c+1)\cdot 2^{c-2}+(3c+2)\cdot 2^{(c-2)/2}-24\right)}&c\equiv 2\pmod{4}\\
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{72}\left((3c+1)\cdot 2^{c-2}+(3c+5)\cdot 2^{(c-1)/2}+24\right)}&c\equiv 3\pmod{4}\end{array}\right..

Sketch of Proof.

First, we consider the case that the crossing number is even.

is given by

\displaystyle TG^{*}(c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{k-1}\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l)/2}\frac{m}{2}\binom{k+l-1}{2m-1}\binom{2m-1}{2l}

\displaystyle+\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l+k\equiv 0\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{k-1}\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l)/2}\frac{m}{2}\binom{(k+l-2)/2}{m-1}\binom{m-1}{l}

\displaystyle+\,\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=0\\
l+k\equiv 0\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}^{k-1}\sum_{m=l+1}^{(k+l)/2}\frac{m}{2}\binom{(k+l-2)/2}{l}\binom{(k-l-2)/2}{m-l-1}.

Pogorelov type C 2 C^{2} estimates for Sum Hessian equations and a rigidity theorem Thanks: Research of the last author is supported by an NSFC Grant No. 11871243
Yue Liu, Changyu Ren

Abstract
. We mainly study Pogorelov type C 2 C^{2} estimates for solutions to the Dirichlet problem of Sum Hessian equations. We establish respectively Pogorelov type C 2 C^{2} estimates for k k -convex solutions and admissible solutions under some conditions. Furthermore, we apply such estimates to obtain a rigidity theorem for k k -convex solutions of Sum Hessian equations in Euclidean space.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following Dirichlet problem of Sum
Hessian equations,

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}{{\sigma}_{k}}(D^{2}u)+\alpha{{\sigma}_{k-1}}(D^{2}u)=f(x,u,Du),&in&\Omega,\\
u=0,&on&\partial{\Omega}.\end{array}\right.

Here,
u
is an unknown function defined on
\Omega
,
\alpha
is a
positive constant. Denote by
Du
and
D^{2}u
the gradient and the
Hessian of
u
. We also require
f>0
and smooth enough with respect
to every variables. Let
\sigma_{k}(D^{2}u)=\sigma_{k}(\lambda(D^{2}u))

denotes the
k
-th elementary symmetric function of the eigenvalues
of the Hessian matrix
D^{2}u
. Namely, for

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\sigma_{k}(\lambda)=\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{k}}.

As we all know, the following classic
k
-Hessian equation

(1.2)

\sigma_{k}(D^{2}u)=f(x,u,Du),~~~~~~~~x\in\Omega

is an important research content in the field of fully nonlinear
partial differential equations and geometric analysis, which is
closely related to many important geometric problems, such as

[
1
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
17
,
18
,
26
,
28
,
33
]
. The equation (
1.1
) is
a natural extension of (
1.2
), which is a Hessian type equation
formed by linear combination of the
k
Hessian operators. These
Hessian type equations have been studied and applied in geometry.
Harvey and Lawson
[
21
]
considered the special Lagrangian
equations which is one of this category. Krylov
[
23
]
and Dong

[
14
]
also considered equations close to this type and
obtained the curvature estimates using the concavity of the
operators. In
[
20
]
, Guan and Zhang studied the curvature
estimates for equations of such type with the right hand side not
depending on gradient term but with coefficients depending on the
position of the hypersurfaces. The geometrical problems in the
hyperbolic space also reduce to equations of this type
[
15
]
.

An important problem in the study of Hessian equations (
1.2
)
is how to obtain
C^{2}
estimates. For the relative work, we refer
the readers to
[
7
,
8
,
13
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
. Especially when
the right hand side function depends on the gradient, how to obtain

C^{2}
estimates for equations (
1.2
) is a longstanding problem

[
17
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in Pogorelov type
C^{2}
estimates
for the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
). Pogorelov type
C^{2}

estimates is a type of interior
C^{2}
estimates with boundary
information. This type of
C^{2}
estimates were established at first
by Pogorelov
[
28
]
for Monge-Ampère equations, which play an
important role in studying the regularity for fully nonlinear
equations. For example, Pogorelov type
C^{2}
estimates were
established in order to study the regularity for degenerate
Monge-Ampère equations
[
4
,
31
]
. For
k
-Hessian
equations (
1.2
), if the right hand side function is

f=f(x,u)
, Chou and Wang
[
13
]
established Pogorelov
C^{2}

estimates of
k
convex solutions:

(-u)^{1+\delta}\Delta u\leq C.

Sheng-Ubas-Wang
[
32
]
established Pogorelov type curvature
estimates for the curvature equations of the graphic hypersurface
including the Hessian equations. If the right hand side function
depends on the gradient, Li-Wang and the last author
[
25
]

established Pogorelov type
C^{2}
estimates for
k+1
convex
solutions for equation (
1.2
):

(-u)\Delta u\leq C.

In particular, for
2
convex solutions of the case
k=2
, the
following Pogorelov type
C^{2}
estimates hold:

(-u)^{\beta}\Delta u\leq C.

Here, positive constants
\beta
and
C
depend on the domain

\Omega
,
f
,
\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega}|u|
and

\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega}|Du|
.

A natural question is whether Pogorelov type
C^{2}
estimates are
still valid for the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
)?

First, we give the definition of
k
convex solution
[
8
]
:

Definition 1.1
.

For a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, a function
v\in C^{2}(\Omega)
is called
k
convex if the eigenvalues
\lambda(x)=(\lambda_{1}(x),\cdots,\lambda_{n}(x))
of the Hessian
\nabla^{2}v(x)
is in
\Gamma_{k}
for all
x\in\Omega
, where
\Gamma_{k}
is
the Garding’s cone

\Gamma_{k}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\ |\quad\sigma_{m}(\lambda)>0,\quad m=1,\cdots,k\}.

The main results are as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
u\in C^{4}(\Omega)\cap C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})
be a
k-1
convex
solution for the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
), where
f(x,u,p)\in C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a
positive function. Then we have

(a) For
k=2,k=3
, there are some positive constants
\beta
and

C
, such that

(1.3)

(-u)^{\beta}\Delta u\leq C.

(b) For
1<k\leq n
, if
f^{\frac{1}{k}}(x,u,p)
is a convex
function with respect to
p
, there are some positive constants

\delta
and
C
, such that

(1.4)

(-u)^{1+\delta}\Delta u\leq C.

Here,
\delta>0
can be arbitrarily small, the positive constants

\beta
and
C
depend on the domain
\Omega
,
\alpha
,
f
,

\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega}|u|
and
\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega}|Du|
.

Remark 1.3
.

In
[
24
]
, Li-Wang and the last author proved that Sum Hessian
operator
\sigma_{k}+\alpha\sigma_{k-1}
is elliptic operator in the set

\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}=\Gamma_{k-1}\cap\{\lambda|\sigma_{k}(\lambda)+\alpha\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda)>0\},

i.e.,
\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}
is the admissible set of the equations

Remark 1.4
.

Similar to the result in
[
24
]
, because

depends on the
gradient, the constant
\beta
is large in theorem

, and
we cannot improve
\beta
to 1 or

.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
u\in C^{4}(\Omega)\cap C^{0,1}(\overline{\Omega})
be a
k
convex solution
for the Dirichlet problem (
1.1
), where
f(x,u,p)\in C^{1,1}(\overline{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a
positive function. Then we have

(-u)\Delta u\leq C.

Here, positive constant
C
depends on the domain
\Omega
,

\alpha
,
f
,
\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega}|u|
and

\displaystyle\sup_{\Omega}|Du|
.

An application of the interior
C^{2}
estimates is to prove rigidity
theorems for equations. For the following
k
-Hessian equations in

n
-dimensional Euclidean spaces,

(1.5)

\sigma_{k}(D^{2}u)=1,

Chang and Yuan
[
11
]
proposed a problem that: Are the entire
solutions of (
1.5
) with lower bound only quadratic
polynomials?

For
k=1
, equation (
1.5
) is a linear equation. It is a
obvious result coming from the Liouville property of the harmonic
functions. For
k=n
, equation (
1.5
) is Monge-Ampère
equation, which is a well known theorem. The work of
Jörgens
[
22
]
, Calabi
[
10
]
, Pogorelov
[
27
,
28
]

proved that every entire strictly convex solution is a quadratic
polynomial. Then, Cheng and Yau
[
12
]
gave another more
geometry proof.
In 2003, Caffarelli and Li

[
6
]
extended the theorem of Jörgens, Calabi and
Pogorelov.

For general
1<k<n
, there are few related results. For
k=2
, Chang
and Yuan
[
11
]
have proved that, if

D^{2}u\geq\delta-\sqrt{\dfrac{2n}{n-1}},

for any
\delta>0
, then the entire solution of the equation
(
1.5
) only has quadratic polynomials. For general
k
,
Bao-Chen-Guan-Ji
[
3
]
proved that strictly entire convex
solutions of equation (
1.5
) satisfying a quadratic growth are
quadratic polynomials. Here, the quadratic growth means that: there
are some positive constants
c,b
and sufficiently large
R
, such
that

(1.6)

u(x)\geq c|x|^{2}-b,|x|\geq R.

[
25
]
relaxed the condition of strictly convex solutions in

[
3
]
to
k+1
convex solutions, and proved that the entire

k+1
convex solutions of the equations (
1.5
) with quadratic
growth are quadratic polynomials. In 2016, Warren
[
34
]

proved that equation (
1.5
) has non-polynomial entire
k

convex solutions for
n\geq 2k-1
by constructing examples.

In this paper, we apply Pogorelov type
C^{2}
estimates to prove the
rigidity theorem of the following Sum Hessian equations in Euclidean
space,

(1.7)

\sigma_{k}(D^{2}u)+\alpha\sigma_{k-1}(D^{2}u)=1.

Theorem 1.6
.

The entire
k
convex solutions of the equations (
1.7
) defined
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with quadratic growth (
1.6
) are quadratic
polynomials.

At last, using the idea of Warren
[
34
]
, we can give an
example of non-polynomial entire
k-1
convex solution.

Example 1.7
.

When
n=3
, the

1
-convex function

u(x,y,t)=\dfrac{e^{4t}-1}{4}(x^{2}+y^{2})+\dfrac{1}{16}(\dfrac{7e^{-4t}}{4}-\dfrac{e^{4t}}{4}-4t^{2})

solves

\sigma_{2}(D^{2}u)+\sigma_{1}(D^{2}u)=1.

2.
Preliminary

In this section, we give the preliminary knowledge related to our
theorems and their proofs. To make it convenient, we denote

S_{k}(\lambda):=\sigma_{k}(\lambda)+\alpha\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda).

For the related notation of
\sigma_{k}
, we use the denotation in

[
24
,
29
]
. Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

(i)
S_{k}^{pp}(\lambda):=\dfrac{\partial S_{k}(\lambda)}{\partial\lambda_{p}}=\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|p)+\alpha\sigma_{k-2}(\lambda|p)=S_{k-1}(\lambda|p)
,

p=1,2,\cdots,n
;

(ii)

S_{k}^{pp,qq}(\lambda):=\dfrac{\partial^{2}S_{k}(\lambda)}{\partial\lambda_{p}\partial\lambda_{q}}=S_{k-2}(\lambda|pq)
,

p,q=1,2,\cdots,n
,  and
S_{k}^{pp,pp}(\lambda)=0
;

(iii)
S_{k}(\lambda)=\lambda_{i}S_{k-1}(\lambda|i)+S_{k}(\lambda|i),

i=1,\cdots,n
;

(iv)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}S_{k}(\lambda|i)=(n-k)S_{k}(\lambda)+\alpha\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda);

(v)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}S_{k-1}(\lambda|i)=kS_{k}(\lambda)-\alpha\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda).

In the joint work with Li, Wang and the last author
[
24
]

proved that the admissible solution set of Sum Hessian operator

S_{k}(\lambda)
is

\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}=\Gamma_{k-1}\cap\{\lambda|S_{k}>0\},

and
S_{k}^{\frac{1}{k}}(\lambda)
and

(\dfrac{S_{k}}{S_{l}})^{\frac{1}{k-l}}
are concave functions on

\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}
. Using the method in
[
19
]
, we can get the
following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
k>l
, for
\vartheta=\dfrac{1}{k-l}
, then for

\lambda\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}
, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{S_{k}^{pp,qq}}{S_{k}}u_{pph}u_{qqh}+\dfrac{S_{l}^{pp,qq}}{S_{l}}u_{pph}u_{qqh}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\dfrac{(S_{k})_{h}}{S_{k}}-\dfrac{(S_{l})_{h}}{S_{l}}\right)\left((\vartheta-1)\dfrac{(S_{k})_{h}}{S_{k}}-(\vartheta+1)\dfrac{(S_{l})_{h}}{S_{l}}\right).

Furthermore, for sufficiently small
\delta>0
, we have

(2.2)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle S_{k}^{pp,qq}u_{pph}u_{qqh}+(1-\vartheta+\dfrac{\vartheta}{\delta})\dfrac{(S_{k})_{h}^{2}}{S_{k}}

\displaystyle\geq S_{k}(\vartheta+1-\delta\vartheta)\left[\dfrac{(S_{l})_{h}}{S_{l}}\right]^{2}-\dfrac{S_{k}}{S_{l}}S_{l}^{pp,qq}u_{pph}u_{qqh}.

The following Lemma comes from
[
2
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Denote by
Sym(n)
the set of all
n\times n
symmetric matrices.
Let
F
be a
C^{2}
symmetric function defined in some open subset

\Psi\subset Sym(n)
. At any diagonal matrix
A\in\Psi

with distinct eigenvalues, let
\ddot{F}(B,B)
be the second derivative of
C^{2}
symmetric function
F
in direction
B\in Sym(n)
, then

\displaystyle\ddot{F}(B,B)=\sum_{j,k=1}^{n}{\ddot{f}}^{jk}B_{jj}B_{kk}+2\sum_{j<k}\frac{\dot{f}^{j}-\dot{f}^{k}}{{\kappa}_{j}-{\kappa}_{k}}B_{jk}^{2}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}
. If

S_{k+1}(\lambda)>0
, then
\lambda\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{k+1}
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\tilde{\Gamma}_{k+1}
, we only need to prove

\sigma_{k}(\lambda)>0
. If
\sigma_{k}(\lambda)\leq 0
, since
S_{k}(\lambda)>0,S_{k+1}(\lambda)>0
, then

\alpha\sigma_{k-1}>|\sigma_{k}|,\quad\sigma_{k+1}>|\alpha\sigma_{k}|,

namely,
\alpha\sigma_{k-1}\sigma_{k+1}>\alpha\sigma_{k}^{2}
, this
contradicts Newton’s inequality.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Using Lemma
2.3
, we can define
\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}
as

\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|S_{m}(\lambda)>0,\quad m=1,2,\cdots,k\}.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}
,

1\leq k\leq n
,
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
, then

(a) For any
1\leq s<k
, we have

(2.3)

S_{s}(\lambda)>\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{s}+\alpha\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{s-1};

(b) For any
1\leq j\leq k-1
, there exists a positive constant

\theta
depending on
n,k
, such that

(2.4)

S_{k}^{jj}(\lambda)\geq\dfrac{\theta S_{k}(\lambda)}{\lambda_{j}}.

Proof.

(a) Using
\lambda\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}\subset\Gamma_{s}
, we have

\lambda_{1}>0,~\lambda_{2}>0,\cdots,~\lambda_{s}>0,

and

S_{s}(\lambda|1)>0,~S_{s-1}(\lambda|12)>0,\cdots,~S_{1}(\lambda|12\cdots s)>0.

Using the above inequalities, we get

\displaystyle S_{s}(\lambda)=

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}S_{s-1}(\lambda|1)+S_{s}(\lambda|1)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}S_{s-1}(\lambda|1)=\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}S_{s-2}(\lambda|12)+\lambda_{1}S_{s-1}(\lambda|12)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}S_{s-2}(\lambda|12)=\cdots

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\cdots\lambda_{s-1}(\lambda_{s}+\alpha).

(b) If
S_{k}(\lambda|j)\leq 0
, obviously we have

S_{k}^{jj}(\lambda)=\dfrac{S_{k}(\lambda)-S_{k}(\lambda|j)}{\lambda_{j}}\geq\dfrac{S_{k}(\lambda)}{\lambda_{j}}.

If
S_{k}(\lambda|j)>0
, then
(\lambda|j)\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}
by Lemma

2.3
. Using (
2.3
), we obtain

S_{k}^{jj}(\lambda)>\dfrac{\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k}+\alpha\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k-1}}{\lambda_{j}}.

On the other hand,
(\lambda|j)\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}
implies
\lambda_{k}>0
and

\lambda_{k}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}+\alpha>0.

So there exists some constant
\theta
depending on
n,k
, such that

\theta S_{k}(\lambda)\leq\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k}+\alpha\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k-1}.

Then we obtain (
2.4
).
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

For
\lambda\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}
, we have

S_{k}^{2}(\lambda)-S_{k-1}(\lambda)S_{k+1}(\lambda)\geq 0.

Proof.

Since
\lambda\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{k}
,
S_{k}(\lambda)>0,S_{k-1}(\lambda)>0
. If

S_{k+1}(\lambda)\leq 0
, the Lemma obviously holds.

If
S_{k+1}(\lambda)>0
, Lemma
2.3
implies

\lambda\in\tilde{\Gamma}_{k+1}\subset\Gamma_{k}
. Thus

\displaystyle S_{k}^{2}-S_{k-1}S_{k+1}=

\displaystyle(\sigma_{k}+\alpha\sigma_{k-1})^{2}-(\sigma_{k-1}+\alpha\sigma_{k-2})(\sigma_{k+1}+\alpha\sigma_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}^{2}+\alpha^{2}\sigma_{k-1}^{2}+\alpha\sigma_{k}\sigma_{k-1}-(\sigma_{k-1}\sigma_{k+1}+\alpha^{2}\sigma_{k-2}\sigma_{k}+\alpha\sigma_{k-2}\sigma_{k+1})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 0.

In the above inequality, we used Newton’s inequality and the
following inequality which obtained from Newton’s inequality

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}\sigma_{k-1}\geq

\displaystyle\sigma_{k-2}\sigma_{k+1}.

∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Assume that
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})\in\Gamma_{k}
,

1\leq k\leq n
,
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
, and
S_{k}\leq N_{0}
for some positive constant
N_{0}>0
. Then

(a) There exists some positive constant
K_{0}
, such that

(2.5)

\lambda_{k-1}\leq(\dfrac{N_{0}}{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{k-1}},~~~~~~\lambda_{n}>-K_{0};

(b) Denote
\kappa_{i}=\lambda_{i}+K_{0}
. For any
1\leq i\leq n
, we
have

(2.6)

2\kappa_{1}^{k+2}S_{k}^{11}(\lambda)\geq\kappa_{i}^{k+2}S_{k}^{ii}(\lambda),

if
\lambda_{1}
is sufficiently large;

(c) For any
\varepsilon_{0}>0
, we have

(2.7)

S_{k}(\lambda)>(1-\varepsilon_{0})\lambda_{1}S_{k}^{11}(\lambda),

if
\lambda_{1}
is sufficiently large;

(d) For any
1\leq i\leq n
, there exists some positive constant

C_{0}
, such that

(2.8)

C_{0}S_{k}(\lambda)\geq\lambda_{i}S_{k}^{ii}(\lambda).

Proof.

(a) Since
\lambda\in\Gamma_{k}
, by Lemma 11 in
[
30
]
, we have

\displaystyle N_{0}\geq S_{k}=\sigma_{k}+\alpha\sigma_{k-1}>

\displaystyle\alpha\sigma_{k-1}\geq\alpha\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k-1}>\alpha\lambda_{k-1}^{k-1}.

Thus,
\lambda_{k-1}\leq(\dfrac{N_{0}}{\alpha})^{\frac{1}{k-1}}
. Using

\lambda_{k}\geq 0
, we have

\lambda_{n}>-(n-k)\lambda_{k}\geq-(n-k)\lambda_{k-1}>-K_{0}.

(b) From (
2.5
), we get
\kappa_{i}>0
. We divide into two cases
to prove (
2.6
).

Case 1:

\lambda_{i}\geq\lambda_{1}^{1/(k+2)}
. Since

(2.9)

\displaystyle 2\kappa_{1}^{2}S_{k}^{11}-\kappa_{i}^{2}S_{k}^{ii}=

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}^{2}S_{k}^{11}+\kappa_{1}^{2}[\lambda_{i}S_{k}^{11,ii}+S_{k-1}(\lambda|1i)]

\displaystyle-\kappa_{i}^{2}[\lambda_{1}S_{k}^{11,ii}+S_{k-1}(\lambda|1i)]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}^{2}S_{k}^{11}+(\kappa_{1}^{2}-\kappa_{i}^{2})S_{k-1}(\lambda|1i)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(\kappa_{1}^{2}-\kappa_{i}^{2})[S_{k}^{11}+S_{k-1}(\lambda|1i)].

Using
\lambda\in\Gamma_{k}
, we have

S_{k-1}(\lambda|1i)=\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|1i)+\alpha\sigma_{k-2}(\lambda|1i)\geq\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|1i)>C_{n-2}^{k-1}\dfrac{\lambda_{2}\cdots\lambda_{k}\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{i}},

and

(2.10)

S_{k}^{11}=\sigma_{k-1}(\lambda|1)+\alpha\sigma_{k-2}(\lambda|1)\geq\alpha\lambda_{2}\cdots\lambda_{k-1}.

In view of (
2.5
), we have
|\lambda_{k}\lambda_{n}|<K_{0}^{2}
. Using
the above two formulas, we have

(2.11)

S_{k}^{11}+S_{k-1}(\lambda|1i)>\lambda_{2}\cdots\lambda_{k-1}(\alpha+C_{n-2}^{k-1}\dfrac{\lambda_{k}\lambda_{n}}{\lambda_{i}})>0,

if
\lambda_{1}
is sufficiently large. Combining (
2.11
) with
(
2.9
), we can get (
2.6
).

Case 2:

\lambda_{i}<\lambda_{1}^{1/(k+2)}
. In this case,

2\kappa_{1}>\kappa_{i}^{k+2}
, using (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}^{k+1}S_{k}^{11}>\kappa_{1}^{k}\lambda_{1}S_{k}^{11}\geq\kappa_{1}^{k}c_{0}S_{k}\geq\kappa_{1}c_{0}S_{k}\lambda_{1}^{k-1}\geq S_{k}^{ii},

when
\lambda_{1}
is sufficiently large. Then we get

2\kappa_{1}^{k+2}S_{k}^{11}\geq\kappa_{i}^{k+2}S_{k}^{ii}.

(c) Using (
2.10
) and

\sigma_{k}(\lambda|1)>C_{n-1}^{k}\lambda_{2}\cdots\lambda_{k}\lambda_{n},

we get

\displaystyle S_{k}-(1-\varepsilon_{0})\lambda_{1}S_{k}^{11}=

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}\lambda_{1}S_{k}^{11}+S_{k}(\lambda|1)

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}\lambda_{1}S_{k}^{11}+\sigma_{k}(\lambda|1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}\cdots\lambda_{k-1}(\varepsilon_{0}\lambda_{1}\alpha+C_{n-1}^{k}\lambda_{k}\lambda_{n}).

By (
2.5
), we know
|\lambda_{k}\lambda_{n}|<K_{0}^{2}
. Thus when

\lambda_{1}
is sufficiently large, the above formula is
non-negative.

(d) Since
\lambda\in\Gamma_{k}
, we have

S_{k}=\sigma_{k}+\alpha\sigma_{k-1}\geq\alpha\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k-1},

and

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{P_{m}}\Big[\displaystyle\sum_{j}\kappa_{j}^{m-1}u_{jjii}+(m-1)\displaystyle\sum_{j}\kappa_{j}^{m-2}u_{jji}^{2}+\displaystyle\sum_{p\neq q}\dfrac{\kappa_{p}^{m-1}-\kappa_{q}^{m-1}}{\kappa_{p}-\kappa_{q}}u_{pqi}^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle-\dfrac{m}{P_{m}^{2}}\Big(\displaystyle\sum_{j}\kappa_{j}^{m-1}u_{jji}\Big)^{2}+\dfrac{\beta u_{ii}}{u}-\dfrac{\beta u_{i}^{2}}{u^{2}}+1.

At

, differentiating the equation (

) twice, we have

Using (

Contacting
S_{k}^{ii}
in both sides of (

), using

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle+S_{k}^{ii}\displaystyle\sum_{p\neq q}\dfrac{\kappa_{p}^{m-1}-\kappa_{q}^{m-1}}{\kappa_{p}-\kappa_{q}}u_{pqi}^{2}\Big]-\dfrac{(m+\frac{2}{\beta})S_{k}^{ii}}{P_{m}^{2}}\Big(\displaystyle\sum_{j}\kappa_{j}^{m-1}u_{jji}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle+\dfrac{\beta S_{k}^{ii}u_{ii}}{u}-\dfrac{2S_{k}^{ii}x_{i}^{2}}{\beta}+\displaystyle\sum_{i}S_{k}^{ii}.

Using the definitions of

and (

), we

(5.9)

\displaystyle 0\geq

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[A_{i}+B_{i}+C_{i}+D_{i}-(1+\dfrac{2}{m\beta})E_{i}\Big]+\dfrac{\beta S_{k}^{ii}u_{ii}}{u}

\displaystyle-\dfrac{2S_{k}^{ii}x_{i}^{2}}{\beta}+\displaystyle\sum_{i}S_{k}^{ii}-\dfrac{C}{\kappa_{1}}.

Proof of Theorem 1.3:
The proof is classical as in

[
33
,
25
]
. For the convenience of the readers, the complete
proof is given here. Suppose
u
is an entire solution of the
equation (
1.7
). For arbitrary positive constant
R>1
, we
consider the set

\Omega_{R}=\{y\in R^{n};u(Ry)\leq R^{2}\}.

Let

v(y)=\dfrac{u(Ry)-R^{2}}{R^{2}}.

We consider the following Dirichlet problem

(5.11)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}{{\sigma}_{k}}(D^{2}v)+\alpha{{\sigma}_{k-1}}(D^{2}v)=1,&in&\Omega_{R},\\
v=0,&on&\partial{\Omega_{R}}.\end{array}\right.

By Lemma
5.1
, we have the following estimates

(5.12)

(-v)^{\beta}\Delta v\leq C.

Here the constants
C
and
\beta
depend on
k
and the diameter of
the domain
\Omega_{R}
. Now using the quadratic growth condition
appears in Theorem
1.6
, we have

c|Ry|^{2}-b\leq u(Ry)\leq R^{2}.

Namely

|y|^{2}\leq\dfrac{1+b}{c}.

Hence,
\Omega_{R}
is bounded. Thus, the constant
C
and
\beta

become two absolutely constants. Consider the domain

\Omega_{R}^{\prime}=\{y;u(Ry)\leq\dfrac{R^{2}}{2}\}\subset\Omega_{R}.

In
\Omega_{R}^{\prime}
, we have

v(y)\leq-\dfrac{1}{2}.

Hence, (
5.12
) implies that in
\Omega_{R}^{\prime}
, we have

\Delta v\leq 2^{\beta}C.

Note that

\nabla_{y}^{2}v=\nabla_{x}^{2}u.

Thus, using the above two formulas, in

\Omega_{R}^{\prime}=\{x;u(x)\leq\dfrac{R^{2}}{2}\}
, we have

\Delta u\leq C,

where
C
is an absolutely constant. Since
R
is arbitrary, we have
the above inequality in whole
R^{n}
. Using Evan-Krylov
theory
[
10
]
, we have

|D^{2}u|_{C^{\alpha}(B_{R})}\leq C\dfrac{|D^{2}u|_{C^{0}(B_{R})}}{R^{\alpha}}\leq\dfrac{C}{R^{\alpha}}.

Hence, when
R\rightarrow+\infty
, we obtain Theorem
1.3
.

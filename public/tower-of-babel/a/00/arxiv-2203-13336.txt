Long time well-posedness of Whitham-Boussinesq systems
Martin Oen Paulsen

Abstract
. Consideration is given to three different full dispersion Boussinesq systems arising as asymptotic models in the bi-directional propagation of weakly nonlinear surface waves in shallow water. We prove that, under a non-cavitation condition on the initial data, these three systems are well-posed on a time scale of order 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ε ) \mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}) , where ε {\varepsilon} is a small parameter measuring the weak non-linearity of the waves. This result seems new for one of these systems, even for short time. The two other systems involve surface tension, and for one of them, the non-cavitation condition has to be sharpened when the surface tension is small. The proof relies on suitable symmetrizers and the classical theory of hyperbolic systems. However, we have to track the small parameters carefully in the commutator estimates to get the long time well-posedness. Finally, combining our results with the recent ones of Emerald provide a full justification of these systems as water wave models in a larger range of regimes than the classical ( a , b , c , d ) (a,b,c,d) -Boussinesq systems.

1.1.
Full dispersion models

The Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation is an asymptotic model for the unidirectional propagation of small
amplitude, long waves on the surface of an ideal fluid of constant depth. It was introduced in
[
8
,
32

to model the propagation of solitary waves in shallow water with a wide range of applications both mathematically and physically. However, its dispersion is too strong in high frequencies when compared to the full water wave system. In particular, the KdV equation does not feature wave breaking or peaking waves. To overcome these shortcomings, Whitham introduced in
[
52

1.2.
Full justification

A fundamental question in the derivation of an asymptotic model is whether its solution converges to the solution of the original physical system. In particular, we say that an asymptotic model is a valid approximation of the Euler equations with a free surface if we can answer the following points in the affirmative
[
33
]
:

(1)

The solutions of the water wave equations exist on the relevant scale
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}})
.

(2)

The solutions of the asymptotic model exist (at least) on the scale
\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}})
.

(3)

Lastly, we must establish the
consistency
between the asymptotic model and the water wave equations, and then show that the error is of order
\mathcal{O}(\mu{\varepsilon}t)
when comparing the two solutions.

The first point was proved by Alvarez-Samaniego and Lannes
[
2
]
for surface gravity waves and Ming, Zhang and Zhang
[
37
]
for gravity-capillary waves in the weakly transverse regime, while points
2.
and
3.
are specific to the asymptotic model under consideration. For instance, in the case of the Whitham equation, Klein
et al.

[
31
]
compared its solution rigorously with those of the KdV equation. In particular, they proved that the difference of two solutions evolving from the same initial datum is bounded by
\mathcal{O}({\varepsilon}^{2}t)
for all
0\leq t\lesssim{\varepsilon}^{-1}
with
{\varepsilon},\mu
in the KdV-regime:

\mathcal{R}_{KdV}=\{({\varepsilon},\mu)\>:\>0\leq\mu\leq 1,\quad\mu={\varepsilon}\},

which justified the Whitham equation as a water wave model in this regime by relying on the justification of the KdV equation
[
9
,
33
]
.

On the other hand, due to the improved dispersion relation of (
1.1
), Emerald
[
22
]
was able to decouple the parameters
({\varepsilon},\mu)
and prove an error estimate between the Whitham equation and the water wave system with a precision
\mathcal{O}(\mu{\varepsilon}t)
for
0\leq t\lesssim{\varepsilon}^{-1}
in the shallow water regime:

(1.8)

\mathcal{R}_{SW}=\{({\varepsilon},\mu)\>:\>0\leq\mu\leq 1,\quad 0\leq{\varepsilon}\leq 1\}.

Consequently, the Witham equation is valid for a larger set of small parameters when compared to the KdV equation.

In the case of the Boussinesq systems (
1.3

Lemma 2.7
.

Let

,

,

(2.18)

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+(c_{\beta}^{2}\|g\|_{H^{s}}+\sqrt{\beta}\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}g\|_{H^{s}})\|\partial_{x}f\|_{H^{t_{0}}}.

In the high regularity setting, the proof will follow the same lines as in
[
51
]

\mu

Proof.

First, write the commutator as a bilinear form:

\displaystyle\big\|[\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}}(D)J^{s},f]\partial_{x}g\big\|_{L^{2}}=\bigg\|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\Big(\sqrt{K_{\mu}(\xi)}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}-\sqrt{K_{\mu}(\rho)}\langle\rho\rangle^{s}\Big)\hat{f}(\xi-\rho)\widehat{\partial_{x}g}(\rho)\>d\rho\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}.

Then if
a=\min\{|\xi-\rho|,|\rho|\}
and
b=\max\{|\xi-\rho|,|\rho|\}
, we can use the mean value theorem, leaving us to estimate the following terms

\displaystyle\Big|\sqrt{K_{\mu}(\xi)}\langle\xi\rangle^{s}-\sqrt{K_{\mu}(\rho)}\langle\rho\rangle^{s}\Big|\lesssim\sup\limits_{\omega\in(a,b)}|m(\omega)|\>|\xi-\rho|,

where

m(\omega)=m_{1}(\omega)+m_{2}(\omega)=\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\frac{d}{d\xi}\sqrt{K_{\mu}(\xi)}+\langle\xi\rangle^{s-1}\sqrt{K_{\mu}(\xi)}.

But using (
2.5
) to estimate

and (
2.4
) to treat

, we deduce

(2.19)

m(\omega)\lesssim c_{\beta}^{2}\langle\omega\rangle^{s-1}+\sqrt{\beta}\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\langle\omega\rangle^{s-1}|\omega|^{\frac{1}{2}},

where the upper bound is increasing for
s\geq 1
. Therefore the supremum is attained at

or
|\rho|
by definition of

. In particular, if
b=|\xi-\rho|
then we may conclude by Minkowski integral inequality, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (

) that

\displaystyle\big\|[\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}}(D)J^{s},f]\partial_{x}g\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim c_{\beta}^{2}\bigg\|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\langle\xi-\rho\rangle^{s-1}|\xi-\rho|\>|\hat{f}(\xi-\rho)|\>|\widehat{\partial_{x}g}(\rho)|\>d\rho\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\sqrt{\beta}\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\bigg\|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\langle\xi-\rho\rangle^{s-1}|\xi-\rho|^{\frac{1}{2}}|\xi-\rho|\>|\hat{f}(\xi-\rho)|\>|\widehat{\partial_{x}g}(\rho)|\>d\rho\bigg\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(c_{\beta}^{2}\|f\|_{H^{s}}+\sqrt{\beta}\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{H^{s}})\|\partial_{x}g\|_{H^{t_{0}}},

\mathcal{F}(\chi^{(i)}(D)f)(\xi)=\chi^{(i)}(|\xi|)\hat{f}(\xi),

for any

with the following properties:

and

Moreover, we denote the scaled version in

We have the results:

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
s>\frac{3}{2}
,
\mu\in(0,1)
and

.

•

Let

(2.24)

and

(2.25)

•

We define the symbol

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}}(D)

\displaystyle:=\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}|D|}+\beta\sqrt{\mu}|D|\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.27)

(2.28)

•

Lastly, we define the symbol

Then

(2.30)

\|(\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}\sigma_{\mu,0})(D)f\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{\beta}\|f\|_{L^{2}}

and

(2.31)

\|[(\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}\sigma_{\mu,0})(D),f]\partial_{x}g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{\beta}\|f\|_{H^{s}}\|g\|_{L^{2}}.

The proof is postponed to Appendix

, where we also will prove the following commutator estimates at the

level:

Lemma 2.12
.

Let

,

•

For the composition of

While for the usual Bessel potential there holds,

\|[\sqrt{T_{\mu}}(D)J^{s},f]\partial_{x}g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{s}}\|J^{s}g\|_{L^{2}}.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
s>2
,
{\varepsilon},\mu\in(0,1)
and
(\eta,u)={\varepsilon}(\zeta,v)\in C([0,T_{0}];V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}))

. Moreover, assume there exist

when

, and that

(3.5)

-\frac{\beta}{2}\leq\eta(x,t),\quad\forall(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,T_{0}]\quad\text{and}\quad\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\|(\eta,u)\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}\leq h_{1},

Then, for the energy given in Definition

and

(3.6)

, and

(3.7)

Remark 3.2
.

Note that we aim to prove (

on the right-hand side. This result will prove essential in getting the time of existence

)
-

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

We first prove estimate (
3.7
) in the case
\beta\geq 1/3
. By definition, we have that

\displaystyle E_{s}(\mathbb{U})=\|J^{s}\eta\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big(J^{s}u,(\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)+\eta)J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}.

Thus, as a result of the non-cavitation condition (
3.4
) and the estimate (
2.7
), there holds

\displaystyle\big(J^{s}u,(\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)+\eta)J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{h_{0}}{2}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+c\sqrt{\mu}\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

The reverse inequality holds for any
\beta>0
and is a consequence of (
2.7
), Hölder’s inequality, the Sobolev embedding with
s>\frac{3}{2}
, and conditions (
3.4
)
-
(
3.5
). Indeed, we observe that

E_{s}(\mathbb{U})\leq\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}}(D)u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\leq c_{\beta}\|(\eta,u)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{2}.

In the case
0<\beta<\frac{1}{3}
, we impose the
\beta-
dependent surface condition (
3.5
), leaving less to be absorbed for the coercivity and in conjunction with (
2.8
). This implies

\displaystyle\big(J^{s}u,(\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)+\eta)J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}\geq\frac{\beta}{2}\|u\|^{2}_{H^{s}}+c\sqrt{\mu}\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

As a consequence, we have that (
3.7
) is established for all
\beta>0
.

Next, we prove (
3.6
). By using (
3.1
) and the fact that
Q(\mathbb{U},D)
is self-adjoint, we compute

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}E_{s}(\mathbb{U})

\displaystyle=\big(J^{s}\partial_{t}\mathbb{U},Q(\mathbb{U},D)J^{s}\mathbb{U}\big)_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\big(J^{s}\mathbb{U},(\partial_{t}Q(\mathbb{U},D))J^{s}\mathbb{U}\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\big(J^{s}M(\mathbb{U},D)\mathbb{U},Q(\mathbb{U},D)J^{s}\mathbb{U}\big)_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\big(J^{s}\mathbb{U},(\partial_{t}Q(\mathbb{U},D))J^{s}\mathbb{U}\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:-I+II.

Control of
I
. We may write

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\big([J^{s},M(\mathbb{U},D)]\mathbb{U},Q^{(1)}(\mathbb{U},D)J^{s}\mathbb{U}\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(Q^{(1)}(\mathbb{U},D)M(\mathbb{U},D)J^{s}\mathbb{U},J^{s}\mathbb{U}\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt+\big(J^{s}M(\mathbb{U},D)\mathbb{U},Q^{(2)}(\mathbb{U},D)J^{s}\mathbb{U}\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

Control of
I_{1}
.
It follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality that

|I_{1}|\leq\|[J^{s},M(\mathbb{U},D)]\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{2}}\|Q^{(1)}(\mathbb{U},D)J^{s}\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{2}}.

The second term is easily treated,

\displaystyle\|Q^{(1)}(\mathbb{U},D)J^{s}\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|J^{s}\eta\|_{L^{2}}+\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|J^{s}u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|(\eta,u)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}},

by Hölder’s inequality, the Sobolev embedding with
s>\frac{1}{2}
, and assumption (
3.4
). Furthermore, using the Kato-Ponce commutator estimate (
2.23
) yields

\displaystyle\|[J^{s},M(\mathbb{U},D)]\mathbb{U}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|[J^{s},u]\partial_{x}\eta\|_{L^{2}}+\|[J^{s},\eta]\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|[J^{s},u]\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}+\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\eta,u)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{2}.

The desired bound on
I_{1}
follows:

|I_{1}|\lesssim\|(\eta,u)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{3}.

Control of
I_{2}+I_{3}
.
First note that
(a_{ij})=Q^{(1)}(\mathbb{U},D)M(\mathbb{U},D)
is given by,

\displaystyle(a_{ij})=\begin{pmatrix}u\partial_{x}&(\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)+\eta)\partial_{x}\\
\eta\partial_{x}&\eta u\partial_{x}\end{pmatrix}.

We must estimate each piece below,

\displaystyle\big(Q^{(1)}(\mathbb{U},D)M(\mathbb{U},D)J^{s}\mathbb{U},J^{s}\mathbb{U}\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\big(a_{11}J^{s}\eta,J^{s}\eta\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(a_{12}J^{s}u,J^{s}\eta\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(a_{21}J^{s}\eta,J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(a_{22}J^{s}u,J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:A_{11}+A_{12}+A_{21}+A_{22}.

As we will shortly see,
A_{12}+A_{21}
needs to be compensated by
B_{21}
, that is defined by the remaining part:

\displaystyle\big(J^{s}M(\mathbb{U},D)\mathbb{U},Q^{(2)}(\mathbb{U},D)J^{s}\mathbb{U}\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\big(\partial_{x}J^{s}\eta,\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(J^{s}(u\partial_{x}u),\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:B_{21}+B_{22},

while
B_{22}
is the price we pay for symmetry.

Control of
A_{11}
.
Integration by part and the Sobolev embedding yields

\displaystyle|A_{11}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\big|\big(\partial_{x}uJ^{s}\eta,J^{s}\eta\big)_{L^{2}}\big|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\|(\eta,u)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{3}.

Control of
A_{12}+A_{21}+B_{21}
.
By definition, consideration is given to the expression

\displaystyle A_{12}+A_{21}+B_{21}

\displaystyle=\big((\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)+\eta)\partial_{x}J^{s}u,J^{s}\eta\big)_{L^{2}}+\big((\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)+\eta)\partial_{x}J^{s}\eta,J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}.

Observe, after integration by parts that

\displaystyle A_{12}

\displaystyle=-\big(J^{s}u,(\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)+\eta)\partial_{x}J^{s}\eta\big)_{L^{2}}-\big(J^{s}u,\partial_{x}\eta J^{s}\eta\big)_{L^{2}}.

The first term cancels with
(A_{21}+B_{21})
, while the Sobolev embedding easily controls the remaining part,

|\big(J^{s}u,\partial_{x}\eta J^{s}\eta\big)_{L^{2}}|\leq\|\partial_{x}\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|(\eta,u)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{3}.

Control of
A_{22}
.
We simply use integration by parts as above together with (
3.4
)
-
(
3.5
) to deduce

|A_{22}|\leq|\big(\eta u\partial_{x}J^{s}u,J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}|\leq c_{\beta}^{2}\|(\eta,u)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{3}.

Control of
B_{22}
.
We observe, after integrating by parts that

\displaystyle B_{22}

\displaystyle=\big(J^{s}(u\partial_{x}u),\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\big([\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}}(D)J^{s},u]\partial_{x}u,\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}}(D)J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\big((\partial_{x}u)\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}}(D)J^{s}u,\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}}(D)J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}.

Thus, we deduce by using Hölder’s inequality, estimates (
2.7
) and (
2.7
) that

\displaystyle|B_{22}|\leq c_{\beta}^{2}\|(\eta,u)\|_{V_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{s}}^{3}.

Control of
II
.
First we claim that
\|\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim_{\beta}\|(\eta,u)\|_{V_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{s}}
for

. Indeed, it follows from (

) and the Sobolev embedding

that

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{x}u\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}}}+\beta\sqrt{\mu}\|D^{1}\partial_{x}u\|_{H^{s-\frac{3}{2}}}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq c_{\beta}^{2}(\|u\|_{H^{s}}+\sqrt{\mu}\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{H^{s}}).

Then we observe by using equation (
3.1
) yields,

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle=\big(J^{s}u,(\partial_{t}\eta)J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}=-\big(J^{s}u,(\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)\partial_{x}u)J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}-\big(J^{s}u,(\partial_{x}(\eta u))J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}.

Consequently, the desired estimate follows from Hölder’s inequality, the Sobolev embedding, and the above claim that,

\displaystyle\ II\lesssim\|\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|\partial_{x}(\eta u)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\lesssim_{\beta}\|(\eta,u)\|_{V_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{s}}^{3}.

Adding together all the estimates, combined with (

and completes the proof of Proposition

Next, we prove (

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{1}{2}\big(J^{s}J_{\mu}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{U},(\partial_{t}\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{U},D))J^{s}J_{\mu}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{U}\big)_{L^{2}}

Control of

.
By definition of (
3.13
) we decompose
\mathcal{I}
in four pieces,

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle=\big(J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}(u\partial_{x}\eta),J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)\eta\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\big((1+\eta)\partial_{x}u\big),J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)\eta\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt+\big(J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)\partial_{x}\eta,(1+\eta)J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}u\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}u\partial_{x}u,(1+\eta)J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}u\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{A}_{11}+\mathcal{A}_{12}+\mathcal{A}_{21}+\mathcal{A}_{22}.

Control of
\mathcal{A}_{11}
.
We aim to exploit symmetries, and we first write
\mathcal{A}_{11}

as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{11}

\displaystyle=\big([J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),u]\partial_{x}\eta,J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\eta\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\big(uJ^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\partial_{x}\eta,J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\eta\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:\mathcal{A}_{11}^{1}+\mathcal{A}_{11}^{2}.

The first term is treated by the commutator estimate (
2.21
) with
s>\frac{3}{2}
, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
2.16
). Thus, there
holds

|\mathcal{A}_{11}^{1}|\leq\|[J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),u]\partial_{x}\eta\|_{L^{2}}\|J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\eta\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Similar to previous estimates, we use integration by parts and exploit the symmetries of
\mathcal{A}_{11}^{2}
, then conclude by (
2.16
), and the Sobolev embedding
H^{\frac{1}{2}^{+}}({\mathbb{R}})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
that

|\mathcal{A}_{11}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\big|\big((\partial_{x}u)J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\eta,J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\eta\big)_{L^{2}}\big|\lesssim\|(\eta,u)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{3}.

Control of
\mathcal{A}_{12}+\mathcal{A}_{21}
.
We first decompose
\mathcal{A}_{12}
in two parts

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{12}

\displaystyle=\big([J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),\eta]\partial_{x}u,J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\eta\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\big((1+\eta)J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\partial_{x}u,J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\eta\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{12}^{1}+\mathcal{A}_{12}^{2}.

We estimate
\mathcal{A}_{12}^{1}
the same way we did for
\mathcal{A}_{11}^{1}
and obtain

|\mathcal{A}_{12}^{1}|\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

For the second term, after integration by parts, we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{12}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\big((\partial_{x}\eta)J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)u,J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\eta\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt-\big((1+\eta)J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)u,J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\partial_{x}\eta\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{12}^{2,1}+\mathcal{A}_{12}^{2,2}.

By using the Sobolev embedding and (
2.16
), we find that

|\mathcal{A}_{12}^{2,1}|\leq\|\partial_{x}\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}u\|_{H^{s}}\|J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}\eta\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

On the other hand, we cannot estimate
\mathcal{A}_{12}^{2,2}
on its own. We must therefore cancel it with
\mathcal{A}_{21}
. Observe

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{21}

\displaystyle=\big(J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\partial_{x}\eta,[\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),\eta]J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}u\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\big(J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\partial_{x}\eta,(1+\eta)J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)u\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{21}^{1}+\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2}.

First, by using integration by parts, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
2.16
), (
2.35
) and (
2.17
) we find that

(3.17)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}_{21}^{1}|=\|J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\eta\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{x}[\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),\eta]J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|(\eta,u)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{3}.

On the other hand, we observe that
\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2}=-\mathcal{A}_{21}^{2,2}
. We may therefore conclude that the sum satisfies:

|\mathcal{A}_{12}+\mathcal{A}_{21}|\lesssim\|(\eta,u)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{3}.

Control of
\mathcal{A}_{22}
.
Similar to
\mathcal{A}_{11}
we write the expression with the good commutator:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{22}

\displaystyle=\big([J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu},u]\partial_{x}u,(1+\eta)J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}u\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(uJ^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}\partial_{x}u,(1+\eta)J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}u\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{A}_{22}^{1}+\mathcal{A}_{22}^{2}.

Then use the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
3.14
), (
2.20
) with
s>\frac{3}{2}
, and the Sobolev embedding to get

|\mathcal{A}_{22}^{1}|\lesssim\|[J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu},u]\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}}(1+\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}})\|J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|(\eta,u)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{3}.

While for
\mathcal{A}_{22}^{2}
we integrate by parts, apply the Sobolev embedding, and again bound each term by the
V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}-
norm of
(\eta,u)
to obtain that

|\mathcal{A}_{22}^{2}|\lesssim(\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}\eta\|_{L^{\infty}})\|J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\|(\eta,u)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{3}.

Gathering all these estimates, we conclude that

(3.18)

|\mathcal{I}|\lesssim\|(\eta,u)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{3}.

Control of
\mathcal{II}
.
By defintion of (
3.12
) and (
3.10
) we get that,

\displaystyle\mathcal{II}

\displaystyle=\big(J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}u,(\partial_{t}\eta)J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}u\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\big(J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}u,(\partial_{x}u)J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}u\big)_{L^{2}}-\big(J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}u,(\partial_{x}(\eta u))J^{s}J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}u\big)_{L^{2}}

Then, by using Hölder’s inequality, the Sobolev embedding, (
3.14
) and (
2.17
), we deduce that

(3.19)

|\mathcal{II}|\lesssim(\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\partial_{x}(\eta u)\|_{L^{\infty}})\|J^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\|(\eta,u)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{3}.

Consequently, we may add (
3.18
) and (
3.19
), then apply (
3.16
) to conclude the proof of estimate (
3.15
).

∎

3.3.
Estimates for system (
1.7
)

As in the former subsections we let
\mathbb{U}=(\eta,u)^{T}={\varepsilon}(\zeta,v)^{T}
and write the system on the form

(3.20)

\partial_{t}\mathbb{U}+\mathscr{M}(\mathbb{U},D)\mathbb{U}=\mathbb{0},

with

(3.21)

\mathscr{M}(\mathbb{U},D)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)(u\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\cdot})&\partial_{x}+\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)(\eta\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\cdot})\\
\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)\partial_{x}&\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)(u\partial_{x}\boldsymbol{\cdot})\end{pmatrix}.

The symmetrizer is defined by

(3.22)

\mathscr{Q}(\mathbb{U},D)=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-1}(D)\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)&0\\
0&\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-1}(D)+\eta\end{pmatrix}.

Then the energy given in Definition
1.19
can be written as

(3.23)

\mathscr{E}_{s}(\mathbb{U})=\big(J^{s}\mathbb{U},\mathscr{Q}(\mathbb{U},D)J^{s}\mathbb{U}\big)_{L^{2}},

and the
a priori estimate
for (
1.7
) is stated in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
s>\frac{3}{2}
,
{\varepsilon},\mu\in(0,1)
,
\beta>0
, and let
(\eta,u)={\varepsilon}(\zeta,v)\in C([0,T_{0}];X^{s}_{\beta,\mu}(\mathbb{R}))
be a solution to (
1.7
) on a time interval
[0,T_{0}]
for some
T_{0}>0
. Moreover, assume that there exist
h_{0}\in(0,1)
and
h_{1}>0
such that

(3.24)

h_{0}-1\leq\eta(x,t),\quad\forall(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,T_{0}]\quad\text{and}\quad\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\|(\eta,u)\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}\leq h_{1}.

Then, for the energy given in Definition
1.19
, there holds,

(3.25)

\frac{d}{dt}\mathscr{E}_{s}(\mathbb{U})\lesssim c_{\beta}^{2}\big(\mathscr{E}_{s}(\mathbb{U})\big)^{\frac{3}{2}},

and the energy is coercive:

(3.26)

\|(\eta,u)\|^{2}_{X^{s}_{\beta,\mu}}\lesssim\mathscr{E}_{s}(\mathbb{U})\lesssim\|(\eta,u)\|^{2}_{X^{s}_{\beta,\mu}}.

Proof of Proposition
3.4
.

We will first provide the coercivity estimate (
3.26
). By Definition
1.19
for the energy, the non-cavitation condition (
3.24
) and (
2.15
) we obtain the lower bound

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{s}(\mathbb{U})

\displaystyle=\|\langle\sqrt{\beta\mu}D\rangle\eta\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\big(J^{s}u,(\mathcal{T}^{-1}_{\mu}(D)+\eta)J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\beta\mu\|D^{1}\eta\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\frac{h_{0}}{2}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+c\sqrt{\mu}\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}u\|_{H^{s}}^{2},

for some
c>0
. The reverse inequality follows by the upper bound in (
2.15
), the Sobolev embedding and (
3.24
):

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}_{s}(\mathbb{U})\leq\|\langle\sqrt{\beta\mu}D\rangle\eta\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|\mathcal{T}^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\mu}(D)J^{s}u\|^{2}+\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|J^{s}u\|^{2}\lesssim\|(\eta,u)\|_{X^{s}_{\beta,\mu}}^{2}.

We may now prove (
3.25
). To do so, we use (
3.20
) and the self-adjointness of
\mathscr{Q}(\mathbb{U},D)
to write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\mathscr{E}_{s}(\mathbb{U})

\displaystyle=-\big(J^{s}\mathscr{M}(\mathbb{U},D)\mathbb{U},\mathscr{Q}(\mathbb{U},D)J^{s}\mathbb{U}\big)_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\big(J^{s}\mathbb{U},(\partial_{t}\mathscr{Q}(\mathbb{U},D))J^{s}\mathbb{U}\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:\mathscr{I}+\mathscr{II}.

Control of
\mathscr{I}
.
By definition of (
3.23
) we must estimate the following terms:

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}

\displaystyle=\big(J^{s}(u\partial_{x}\eta),\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)J^{s}\eta\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(J^{s}\partial_{x}u+J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)(\eta\partial_{x}u),\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-1}(D)\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)J^{s}\eta\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt+\big(J^{s}\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)\partial_{x}\eta,(\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-1}(D)+\eta)J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)(u\partial_{x}u),(\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-1}(D)+\eta)J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:\mathscr{A}_{11}+\mathscr{A}_{12}+\mathscr{A}_{21}+\mathscr{A}_{22}.

Control of
\mathscr{A}_{11}
.
We rewrite
\mathscr{A}_{11}
as

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{11}

\displaystyle=\big([J^{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}}(D),u]\partial_{x}\eta,J^{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}}(D)\eta\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(uJ^{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}}(D)\partial_{x}\eta,J^{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}}(D)\eta\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:\mathscr{A}_{11}^{1}+\mathscr{A}_{11}^{2}.

For the first term, we first note by interpolation and Young’s inequality that

(3.27)

\sqrt{\beta}\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(\beta\sqrt{\mu}\|\eta\|_{H^{s+1}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim_{\beta}\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}+\beta\sqrt{\mu}\|D^{1}\eta\|_{H^{s}},

and thus
\mathscr{A}_{11}^{1}
is treated by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, the commutator estimate (
2.7
) with
s>\frac{3}{2}
, (
2.7
), and (
3.27
):

|\mathscr{A}_{11}^{1}|\lesssim c_{\beta}^{2}(\|u\|_{H^{s}}+\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}})(\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}+\sqrt{\beta}\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}})^{2}\lesssim c_{\beta}^{2}\|(\eta,u)\|_{X^{s}_{\beta,\mu}}^{3}.

On the other hand, for
\mathscr{A}_{11}^{2}
we conclude by integration by parts, (
2.7
), (
3.27
), and the Sobolev embedding with
s>\frac{3}{2}
that

|\mathscr{A}_{11}|\lesssim|\mathscr{A}_{11}^{1}|+\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}}(D)\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}}(D)\eta\|_{H^{s}}\lesssim c_{\beta}^{2}\|(\eta,u)\|_{X^{s}_{\beta,\mu}}^{3}.

Control of
\mathscr{A}_{12}+\mathscr{A}_{21}
.
By using integration by parts we write,

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{12}

\displaystyle=\big(J^{s}(\eta\partial_{x}u),\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)J^{s}\eta\big)_{L^{2}}-\big(J^{s}u,\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-1}(D)\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)J^{s}\partial_{x}\eta\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}_{12}^{1}+\mathscr{A}_{12}^{2}.

For
\mathscr{A}_{12}^{1}
, observe

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{12}^{1}

\displaystyle=\big([J^{s},\eta]\partial_{x}u,\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)J^{s}\eta\big)_{L^{2}}-\big((\partial_{x}\eta)J^{s}u,\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)J^{s}\eta\big)_{L^{2}}-\big(\eta J^{s}u,\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)J^{s}\partial_{x}\eta\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}_{12}^{1,1}+\mathscr{A}_{12}^{1,2}+\mathscr{A}_{12}^{1,3}.

Then the Kato-Ponce commutator estimate (
2.9
), the Sobolev embedding, and the pointwise estimate (
2.1
) combined with Plancherel imply that

\displaystyle|\mathscr{A}_{12}^{1,1}+\mathscr{A}_{12}^{1,2}|\lesssim c_{\beta}^{2}\|u\|_{H^{s}}\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}(\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}+\beta\sqrt{\mu}\|D^{1}\eta\|_{H^{s}})\lesssim c_{\beta}^{2}\|(\eta,u)\|_{X^{s}_{\beta,\mu}}^{3}.

While the contribution of remaining terms,
\mathscr{A}_{12}^{1,3}+\mathscr{A}_{12}^{2}
, will be canceled by
\mathscr{A}_{21}
. Indeed, we observe that

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{21}=\big(J^{s}\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)\partial_{x}\eta,\eta J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(J^{s}\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)\partial_{x}\eta,\mathcal{T}^{-1}_{\mu}(D)J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}=-\mathscr{A}_{12}^{1,3}-\mathscr{A}_{12}^{2}.

Hence, combining these identities and estimates gives the bound

|\mathscr{A}_{12}+\mathscr{A}_{21}|\lesssim c_{\beta}^{2}\|(\eta,u)\|_{X^{s}_{\beta,\mu}}^{3}.

Control of
\mathscr{A}_{22}
.
Consider the two terms:

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{22}=\big(J^{s}(u\partial_{x}u),J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(J^{s}\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)(u\partial_{x}u),\eta J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}=\mathscr{A}_{22}^{1}+\mathscr{A}_{22}^{2}.

The control of
\mathscr{A}_{22}^{1}
is a direct consequence of the Kato-Ponce commutator estimate (
2.9
) and integration by parts. Since
s>\frac{3}{2}
, we have that

|\mathscr{A}_{22}^{1}|\leq\big|\big([J^{s},u]\partial_{x}u,J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}\big|+\frac{1}{2}\big|\big((\partial_{x}u)J^{s}u,J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}\big|\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{3}.

To deal with
\mathscr{A}_{22}^{2}
, we make the decomposition

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{22}^{2}

\displaystyle=\big([J^{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),u]\partial_{x}u,\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\eta J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(uJ^{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\partial_{x}u,[\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),\eta]J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\big(uJ^{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\partial_{x}u,\eta\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{A}_{22}^{2,1}+\mathscr{A}_{22}^{2,2}+\mathscr{A}_{22}^{2,3}.

Then for
\mathscr{A}_{22}^{2,1}
we employ the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
2.33
), (
3.24
), (
2.14
), and the Sobolev embedding to deduce

\displaystyle|\mathscr{A}_{22}^{2,1}|\leq\|[J^{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),u]\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{2}}\|\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)(\eta J^{s}u)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|(\eta,u)\|_{X^{s}_{\beta,\mu}}^{3}.

Before we treat
\mathscr{A}_{22}^{2,2,1}
, we note that
\|[\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),\eta]J^{s}u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}
. Indeed, using (
2.14
) and the Sobolev embedding we find that

(3.28)

\displaystyle\|[\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),\eta]J^{s}u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|J^{s}u\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}.

Consequently, using integration by parts, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
3.28
) and (
2.35
) we get

\displaystyle|\mathscr{A}_{22}^{2,2}|

\displaystyle=\big|\big((\partial_{x}u)J^{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)u,[\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),\eta]J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}\big|

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\big|\big(uJ^{s}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)u,\partial_{x}[\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),\eta]J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\eta\|_{H^{s}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{3},

then use (
3.24
) on one term. Similarly, for
\mathscr{A}_{22}^{2,1}
we use integration by parts, the Sobolev embedding, and (
2.14
) to get the bound

|\mathscr{A}_{22}^{2,3}|\lesssim\|\partial_{x}(\eta u)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}.

Therefore, we conclude by (
3.24
) and gathering all these estimates that

|\mathscr{A}_{22}|\lesssim\|(\eta,u)\|_{X^{s}_{\beta,\mu}}^{3},

and by extension, we have the bound

|\mathscr{I}|\lesssim_{\beta}\|(\eta,u)\|_{X^{s}_{\beta,\mu}}^{3}.

Control of
\mathscr{II}
.
By defintion of (
3.22
) and (
3.20
) we get that,

\displaystyle\mathscr{II}

\displaystyle=\big(J^{s}u,(\partial_{t}\eta)J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\big(J^{s}u,(\partial_{x}u)J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}}-\big(J^{s}u,(\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)\partial_{x}(\eta u))J^{s}u\big)_{L^{2}},

Then the final estimate follows by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
3.24
) and the fact that
\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)
is bounded on
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
, then apply Hölder’s inequality, and the Sobolev embedding to deduce

|\mathscr{II}|\leq\|\partial_{x}u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}+\|\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)\partial_{x}(\eta u)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{H^{s}}^{2}\lesssim\|(\eta,u)\|_{X^{s}_{\beta,\mu}}^{3}.

∎

4.
Estimates for the difference of two solutions

4.1.
Estimates for system (
1.5
)

We will now estimate the difference between two solutions of (
1.5
) given by
\mathbb{U}_{1}=(\eta_{1},u_{1})^{T}={\varepsilon}(\zeta_{1},v_{1})^{T}
and
\mathbb{U}_{2}=(\eta_{2},u_{2})^{T}={\varepsilon}(\zeta_{2},v_{2})^{T}
. For convenience, we define
(\psi,w)=(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2},u_{1}-u_{2})
. Then
\mathbb{W}=(\psi,w)^{T}
solves

(4.1)

\partial_{t}\mathbb{W}+M(\mathbb{U}_{1},D)\mathbb{W}=\mathbb{F},

with
M
defined as in (
3.2
) and
\mathbb{F}=-\big(M(\mathbb{U}_{1},D)-M(\mathbb{U}_{2},D)\big)\mathbb{U}_{2}
. Specifically, the source term is given by

(4.2)

\mathbb{F}=-\begin{pmatrix}w\partial_{x}\eta_{2}+\psi\partial_{x}u_{2}\\
w\partial_{x}u_{2}\end{pmatrix}.

The energy associated to (
4.1
) is given in terms of the symmetrizer
Q(\mathbb{U}_{1},D)
defined in (
3.3
) and reads

(4.3)

\tilde{E}_{s}(\mathbb{W}):=\big(J^{s}\mathbb{W},Q(\mathbb{U}_{1},D)J^{s}\mathbb{W}\big)_{L^{2}}.

The main result of this section reads:

Proposition 4.1
.

Take
s>2
and
{\varepsilon},\mu\in(0,1)
. Let
(\eta_{1},u_{1}),(\eta_{2},u_{2})\in C([0,T_{0}]:V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}))
be two solutions of (
1.5
) on a time interval
[0,T_{0}]
for some
T_{0}>0
. Moreover, assume there exist
h_{0}\in(0,1)
and
h_{1}>0
such that

(4.4)

h_{0}-1\leq\eta_{1}(x,t),\quad\forall(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,T_{0}]\quad\text{and}\quad\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\|(\eta_{1},u_{1})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}\leq h_{1},

when
\beta\geq\frac{1}{3}
, and that

(4.5)

-\frac{\beta}{2}\leq\eta_{1}(x,t),\quad\forall(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times[0,T_{0}]\quad\text{and}\quad\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\|(\eta_{1},u_{1})\|_{H^{s}\times H^{s}}\leq h_{1},

when
0<\beta<\frac{1}{3}
.

Define the difference to be
\mathbb{W}=(\psi,w)=(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2},u_{1}-u_{2})
. Then, for the energy defined by (
4.3
), there holds

(4.6)

\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{E}_{0}(\mathbb{W})\lesssim_{\beta}\max\limits_{i=1,2}\|(\eta_{i},u_{i})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}\|(\psi,w)\|_{V^{0}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{2},

and

(4.7)

\|(\psi,w)\|^{2}_{V^{0}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}\lesssim_{\beta}\tilde{E}_{0}(\mathbb{W})\lesssim_{\beta}\|(\psi,w)\|^{2}_{V^{0}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}.

Furthermore, we have the following estimate at the

Proposition 4.4

Take

,
{\varepsilon},\mu\in(0,1)

Furthermore, we have the following estimate at the

\frac{d}{dt}\mathscr{\tilde{E}}_{s}(\mathbb{W})\lesssim_{\beta}|\big(J^{s}\mathbb{F},\mathscr{Q}(\mathbb{U}_{1},D)J^{s}\mathbb{W}\big)_{L^{2}}|+\max\limits_{i=1,2}\|(\eta_{i},u_{i})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}\|(\psi,w)\|_{X^{s}_{\beta,\mu}}^{2},

Proof.

By previous arguments, we note that the proofs of (
4.25
) and (
4.27
) are similar to the proof of (
3.26
).

Moreover, we will only prove (
4.24
) since the control of (
4.26

3.4
.

We will now prove (
4.24
). Then we first use (
4.20
) and the self-adjointness of
\mathscr{Q}(\mathbb{U}_{1},D)
to write

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\tilde{\mathscr{E}}_{0}(\mathbb{W})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\big(\mathbb{W},(\partial_{t}\mathscr{Q}(\mathbb{U}_{1},D))\mathbb{W}\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(\mathbb{F},\mathscr{Q}(\mathbb{U}_{1},D)\mathbb{W}\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt-\big(\mathscr{M}(\mathbb{U}_{1},D)\mathbb{W},\mathscr{Q}(\mathbb{U}_{1},D)\mathbb{W}\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=:\mathscr{I}-\mathscr{II}-\mathscr{III}.

Control of
\mathscr{I}
.
By (
4.20
), Hölder’s inequality, the Sobolev embedding and (

\displaystyle|\mathscr{I}|=\frac{1}{2}|\big(w,(\partial_{t}\eta_{1})w\big)_{L^{2}}|\lesssim\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s}}(1+\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{s}})\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\max\limits_{i=1,2}\|(\eta_{i},u_{i})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}\|(\psi,w)\|_{X^{0}_{\beta,\mu}}^{2}.

Control of
\mathscr{II}

The contribution from the source term is given by,

\displaystyle\mathscr{II}

\displaystyle=\big(w\partial_{x}\eta_{2},\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)\psi\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(\psi\partial_{x}\eta_{2},\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)\psi\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt+\big(w\partial_{x}u_{2},w\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)(w\partial_{x}u_{2}),\eta_{1}w\big)_{L^{2}}

Control of
\mathscr{II}_{1}+\mathscr{II}_{2}
.

2.1

s>\frac{3}{2}

\displaystyle|\mathscr{II}_{1}|+|\mathscr{II}_{2}|

\displaystyle\lesssim(\|w\|_{L^{2}}+\|\psi\|_{L^{2}})\|\eta_{2}\|_{H^{s}}\|\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)\psi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\max\limits_{i=1,2}\|(\eta_{i},u_{i})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}\|(\psi,w)\|_{X^{0}_{\beta,\mu}}^{2}.

Control of
\mathscr{II}_{3}+\mathscr{II}_{4}
.
Both terms are treated with the Cauchy-Schwatz inequality, (
2.14
) and the Sobolev embedding. Consequently, for
s>\frac{3}{2}
there holds

\displaystyle|\mathscr{II}_{3}|+|\mathscr{II}_{4}|\lesssim(1+\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{s}})\|u_{2}\|_{H^{s}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\max\limits_{i=1,2}\|(\eta_{i},u_{i})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}\|(\psi,w)\|_{X^{0}_{\beta,\mu}}^{2}.

Gathering all these estimates yields

Control of
\mathscr{III}
.
The symmetrized term
\mathscr{III}
reads:

\displaystyle\mathscr{III}=

\displaystyle\big(u_{1}\partial_{x}\psi,\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)\psi\big)_{L^{2}}+\big((1+\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)\eta_{1})\partial_{x}w,\mathcal{T}^{-1}_{\mu}(D)\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)\psi\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\hskip 0.0pt+\big(\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)\partial_{x}\psi,(\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-1}(D)+\eta_{1})w\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)(u_{1}\partial_{x}w),(\mathcal{T}_{\mu}^{-1}(D)+\eta_{1})w\big)_{L^{2}}

Control of
\mathscr{A}_{11}
.
We decompose
\mathscr{A}_{11}
as

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{11}

\displaystyle=\big(u_{1}\partial_{x}\psi,((\chi^{(1)}_{\mu})^{2}\mathcal{K}_{\mu})(D)\psi\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(u_{1}\partial_{x}\psi,((\chi_{\mu}^{(2)})^{2}(\sigma_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}})^{2})(D)\psi\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle|\mathscr{A}_{11}|=\big(u_{1}\partial_{x}\psi,\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)\psi\big)_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{\beta}\|u_{1}\|_{H^{s}}(\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\sqrt{\beta}\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\psi\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}).

Then use inequality (

) to conclude that

|\mathscr{A}_{11}|\lesssim\max\limits_{i=1,2}\|(\eta_{i},u_{i})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}\|(\psi,w)\|_{X^{0}_{\beta,\mu}}^{2}.

Control of
\mathscr{A}_{12}+\mathscr{A}_{21}
.
Treating the off-diagonal terms we first observe by integrating by parts that

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{12}

\displaystyle=-\big((\partial_{x}\eta_{1})w,\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)\psi\big)_{L^{2}}-\mathscr{A}_{21}.

Therefore, we may apply Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding to deduce

|\mathscr{A}_{12}+\mathscr{A}_{21}|\lesssim\|\eta_{1}\|_{H^{s}}\|(\psi,w)\|_{X^{0}_{\beta,\mu}}.

Control of
\mathscr{A}_{22}

We decompose
\mathscr{A}_{22}

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{22}

\displaystyle=\big(u_{1}\partial_{x}w,w\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(\mathcal{T}_{\mu}(D)(u_{1}\partial_{x}w),\eta_{1}w\big)_{L^{2}}

Next, we decompose

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{22}^{2}

\displaystyle=\big([\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),u_{1}]\partial_{x}w,\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)(\eta_{1}w)\big)_{L^{2}}+\big(u_{1}\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)\partial_{x}w,[\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),\eta_{1}]w\big)_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle|\mathscr{A}_{22}^{2,1}|\leq\|[\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),u_{1}]\partial_{x}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)(\eta_{1}w)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(4.28)

\|[\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),\eta_{1}]w\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|\eta_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|_{L^{2}},

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{x}u_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D)w\|_{L^{2}}\|[\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),\eta_{1}]w\|_{L^{2}}

and as a result the proof of Proposition

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.6

Proof.

The proof is divided into eigth steps, utilizing the results above.

Step
1
:

Existence of solutions for a regularized system.
Let
s>\frac{1}{2}
,
\delta>0
, and
\varphi_{\delta}(D)
be as given in Definition
2.15
. Then, for initial data on the form
\varphi_{\delta}(D)\mathbb{U}_{0}:=(\varphi_{\delta}(D)\eta_{0},\varphi_{\delta}(D)u_{0})
, we claim that there exist

, and a time

(5.1)

0<T_{\delta}:=T_{\delta}\big(\|(\eta_{0},u_{0})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}\big)=\frac{c_{\beta}\delta}{1+\|(\eta_{0},u_{0})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}}

such that

(5.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\eta^{\delta}+u^{\delta}\partial_{x}\varphi_{\delta}(D)\eta^{\delta}+(\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)+\eta^{\delta})\partial_{x}\varphi_{\delta}(D)u^{\delta}=0\\
\partial_{t}u^{\delta}+\partial_{x}\varphi_{\delta}(D)\eta^{\delta}+u^{\delta}\partial_{x}\varphi_{\delta}(D)u^{\delta}=0\end{cases}

The proof of the existence of a unique solution is a consequence of the contraction mapping principle. First, define the notation
\varphi_{\delta}(D)\mathbb{U}^{\delta}(t):=(\varphi_{\delta}(D)\eta^{\delta},\varphi_{\delta}(D)u^{\delta})^{T}(t)
. Then we shall show for
M
defined by (
3.2
) that

(5.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}^{\delta}(t)=\varphi_{\delta}(D)\mathbb{U}_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}M(\mathbb{U}^{\delta}(s),D)\cdot\varphi_{\delta}(D)\mathbb{U}^{\delta}(s)\>ds=:\Phi_{\mathbb{U}_{0}}(\mathbb{U}^{\delta})(t)

defines a contraction on the compete metric space

\displaystyle Y^{s}(T,a):=\big\{(f^{1},f^{2})\in C([0,T];V_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{s}(\mathbb{R}))\>:\>\|(f^{1},f^{2})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}V_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{s}}\leq a\big\}.

Step
1.1
:

\Phi
is well-defined on
Y^{s}(T,a)
.
Indeed, let
\mathbb{U}^{\delta}=(\eta^{\delta},u^{\delta})\in Y^{s}(T,a)

2.1
), (

), the Sobolev embedding and (

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{1}(\mathbb{U}^{\delta})\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\eta_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+T\big(\delta^{-1}\|u^{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}}\|\eta^{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}}

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\delta^{-\frac{3}{2}}\|\mathcal{K}_{\mu}(D)u^{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s-\frac{1}{2}}}+\delta^{-1}\|\eta^{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}}\|u^{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{s}}\big)

Similarly, for the second coordinate there holds

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{2}(\mathbb{U}^{\delta})\|_{H^{s}}+\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}\Phi^{2}(\mathbb{U}^{\delta})\|_{H^{s}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|D^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{0}\|_{H^{s}}+Tc_{\beta}(a+a^{2})(\delta^{-1}+\delta^{-\frac{3}{2}}).

Gathering these estimates, with
\|(\eta_{0},u_{0})||_{V_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{s}}\leq\frac{a}{2}
and

implies

\displaystyle\|(\Phi^{1}(\mathbb{U}^{\delta}),\Phi^{2}(\mathbb{U}^{\delta}))\|_{V_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{s}}\leq a.

Step
1.2
:

\Phi
defines a contraction map on
Y^{s}(T,a)

Let
\mathbb{U}^{\delta}_{1}=(\eta_{1}^{\delta},u_{1}^{\delta})^{T},\mathbb{U}^{\delta}_{2}=(\eta_{2}^{\delta},u_{2}^{\delta})^{T}\in Y^{s}(T,a)
. Then if we define

\displaystyle\|(\Phi^{1}(\mathbb{U}_{1}^{\delta})-\Phi^{1}(\mathbb{U}_{2}^{\delta}),\Phi^{2}(\mathbb{U}_{1}^{\delta})-\Phi^{2}(\mathbb{U}_{2}^{\delta}))\|_{V_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{s}}<\|(\psi^{\delta},w^{\delta})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}.

by choosing

We conclude by the Fixed Point Theorem that there exist a unique solution of (

) in

.

Remark 5.1

A consequence of Step

Step 2:

The blow-up alternative.
We define the maximal time of existence to be

. As a result, we conclude that (

Step

The proof relies on a bootstrap argument similar to the proof of Lemma

in

. In fact, the long time existence is a direct consequence of the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
s>2
and
{\varepsilon}
be as in (
1.11
). Let
(\eta^{\delta},u^{\delta})={\varepsilon}(\zeta^{\delta},v^{\delta})\in C([0,T_{\delta}^{\star});V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}))
be a solution of (
5.2
) with initial data
{\varepsilon}(\zeta_{0},v_{0})=(\eta_{0},u_{0})\in V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}(\mathbb{R})
, defined on its maximal time of existence and satisfying the blow-up alternative (
5.4
). Moreover, let
\eta_{0}={\varepsilon}\zeta_{0}
satisfy either the non-cavitation condition (
1.9
) or the
\beta-
dependent surface condition (
1.10
), depending on whether
\beta\geq\frac{1}{3}
or
0<\beta<\frac{1}{3}
, respectivly. Then there exists a time

(5.9)

T_{0}=\frac{1}{k_{\beta}^{1}\|(\eta_{0},u_{0})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}},

such that
T^{\star}_{\delta}>T_{0}
and

(5.10)

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{0}]}\|(\eta^{\delta},u^{\delta})(t)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}\leq 4k^{2}_{\beta}\|(\eta_{0}^{\delta},u_{0}^{\delta})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}.

The constants are on the form

k^{2}_{\beta}=\frac{c_{\beta}^{2}}{c_{\beta}^{1}}\quad\text{and}\quad k_{\beta}^{1}=\begin{cases}\frac{C_{1}}{\beta}\>\>\quad\quad\text{for}\>\>0<\beta<\frac{1}{3}\\
C_{2}\beta^{2}\>\>\>\quad\text{for}\>\>\beta\geq\frac{1}{3}\end{cases}

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
are two positive constants to be fixed in the proof.

Proof.

We define the set

(5.11)

\tilde{T}_{\delta}=\sup\limits\Big\{T_{\delta}\in(0,T_{\delta}^{\star})\>:\>\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T_{\delta}]}\|(\eta^{\delta},u^{\delta})(t)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}\leq 4k^{2}_{\beta}\|(\eta_{0}^{\delta},u^{\delta}_{0})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}\Big\}.

Then we first note that
\tilde{T}_{\delta}<T^{\star}_{\delta}
, or else it would contradict the blow-up alternative (
5.4
). For the proof we argue by contradiction that
\tilde{T}_{\delta}\leq T_{0}
.

The main idea is to improve the estimate given in (
5.11
). First, we verify that the solution
(\eta^{\delta},u^{\delta})
satisfy (
3.4
). Indeed, recalling assumption (
1.11
):

0<{\varepsilon}\leq\frac{1}{k^{2}_{\beta}\|(\zeta_{0},v_{0})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}},

implies

(5.12)

\displaystyle\|(\eta^{\delta},u^{\delta})\|_{H^{s}}\leq k^{2}_{\beta}\|(\eta_{0}^{\delta},u_{0}^{\delta})\|_{V_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{s}}=4{\varepsilon}k^{2}_{\beta}\|(\zeta_{0},v_{0})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}\leq 4,

for all
t\in[0,\tilde{T}_{\delta}]
. Next, the solution
(\eta^{\delta},u^{\delta})
satisfy the non-cavitation condition. We will prove this as a consequence of two claims.

Claim 1:
If
\eta_{0}
satisfies the non-cavitation condition, then the same is true for
\eta^{\delta}_{0}:=\varphi_{\delta}(D)\eta_{0}
. Indeed, by the Sobolev embedding
H^{\frac{1}{2}^{+}}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
and (
2.41
) to deduce that

\|\eta_{0}^{\delta}-\eta_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|\varphi_{\delta}(D)\eta_{0}-\eta_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}^{+}}}\underset{\delta\rightarrow 0}{=}o(\delta),

since
s-1>\frac{1}{2}
.

Claim 2:
We have the following bound on
\partial_{t}\eta^{\delta}
:

\sup\limits_{\tau\in[0,\tilde{T}_{\delta}]}|\partial_{t}\eta^{\delta}(\tau)|\leq k_{\beta}^{2}\beta^{2}.

Indeed, by similar argument as for (
3.8
), we use (
5.2
), (
5.12
), and
H^{\frac{1}{2}^{+}}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
to find

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\eta^{\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c_{\beta}^{2}\|(\eta^{\delta},u^{\delta})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}+\|\eta^{\delta}\|_{H^{s}}\|u^{\delta}\|_{H^{s}}\leq 4k^{2}_{\beta}c_{\beta}^{2}\|(\eta_{0},u_{0})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}.

Now, let
\delta
small such that
0<o(\delta)<\frac{h_{0}}{4}
. Then, by Claim
1
and Claim
2
, we use the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus to obtain

(5.13)

\displaystyle 1+\eta^{\delta}(x,t)=1+\eta_{0}^{\delta}+\int^{t}_{0}\partial_{t}\eta^{\delta}(x,s)\>ds\geq\frac{3}{4}h_{0}-k_{\beta}^{2}\beta^{2}\tilde{T}_{\delta},

for all
t\in[0,\tilde{T}_{\delta}]
. On the one hand, if
\beta\geq\frac{1}{3}
, then

k^{2}_{\beta}=\frac{c^{2}_{\beta}}{c^{1}_{\beta}}=c\beta.

Thus, for
C_{2}>0
large enough, we get that
k^{1}_{\beta}\geq C_{2}\beta^{2}\geq\frac{c\beta^{2}}{h_{0}}
. Moreover, by the assumption
\tilde{T}_{\delta}\leq T_{0}
, we conclude from (
5.13
) that

1+\eta^{\delta}(x,t)\geq\frac{3}{4}h_{0}-k_{\beta}^{2}\beta^{2}T_{0}\geq\frac{h_{0}}{2},

for all
t\in[0,\tilde{T}_{\delta}]
. On the other hand, in the case when
\beta\in(0,\frac{1}{3})
we need to verify (
3.5
). But this can be done the same way by choosing
k_{\beta}^{1}\geq\frac{C_{1}}{\beta}\geq\frac{c}{\beta h_{\beta}}
for
C_{1}>0
large enough.

The hypotheses of Proposition
3.1
are now verified, leaving us (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) at our disposal. With this at hand, we observe that

E_{s}(\mathbb{U}^{\delta})(t)\leq E_{s}(\mathbb{U}^{\delta})(0)+c^{2}_{\beta}\int_{0}^{t}\big(E_{s}(\mathbb{U}^{\delta})(s^{\prime})\big)^{\frac{3}{2}}\>ds^{\prime}:=\psi(t).

By the above inequality, we then have
\psi^{\prime}(t)\leq c^{2}_{\beta}\>\big(E_{s}(\mathbb{U}^{\delta})(t)\big)^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq c^{2}_{\beta}\>(\psi(t))^{\frac{3}{2}}
. We solve the differential inequality and use (
3.7
) to relate the energy with the
V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}-
norm of the solution and deduce that

(5.14)

\displaystyle c_{\beta}^{1}\|(\eta^{\delta},u^{\delta})(t)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}\leq\frac{c_{\beta}^{2}\|(\eta_{0}^{\delta},u_{0}^{\delta})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}}{1-\frac{(c_{\beta}^{2})^{2}}{2}t\|(\eta_{0}^{\delta},u_{0}^{\delta})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}},

for all
t\in[0,\tilde{T}_{\delta}]
. Finally, if
C_{1},C_{2}>0
is large enough then since
\tilde{T}_{\delta}\leq T_{0}
we have that

\displaystyle\|(\eta^{\delta},u^{\delta})(t)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}

\displaystyle\leq 2k^{2}_{\beta}\|(\eta_{0}^{\delta},u_{0}^{\delta})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}.

Though, by continuity of the solution in time
t\in[0,T_{\delta}^{\star})
, there exists
\tau>0
such that
\|(\eta^{\delta},u^{\delta})(\tau)\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}\leq 3k^{2}_{\beta}\|(\eta_{0},u_{0})\|_{V^{s}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}
for
\tilde{T}_{\delta}<\tau<T_{\delta}^{\star}
. This contradicts the definition of
\tilde{T}_{\delta}
. Thus, we may conclude
T_{0}<\tilde{T}_{\delta}
for all
\delta>0
and that
T_{0}
is independent from
\delta
by its definition in (
5.9
).

A.1.
Pointwise estimates for

Before turning to the proof of the pointwise estimates in Lemma

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\mu>0
and take any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

•

Then
T_{\mu}(\xi)
satisfies

(A.1)

\Big|\frac{d^{n}}{d\xi^{n}}\sqrt{T_{\mu}(\xi)}\Big|\lesssim\mu^{\frac{n}{2}}\langle\sqrt{\mu}\xi\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}-n}.

•

Similarly,
K_{\mu}(\xi)
satisfies

(A.2)

\Big|\frac{d^{n}}{d\xi^{n}}\sqrt{K_{\mu}(\xi)}\Big|\lesssim_{\beta}\mu^{\frac{n}{2}}\langle\sqrt{\mu}\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}-n}.

Proof.

The proof is a generalization of Lemma
8
in
[
16
]
. Following their arguments, we observe that since
T_{1}(\sqrt{\mu}r)=T_{\mu}(r)
for
r>0
, it is sufficient to show that

\Big|\frac{d^{n}}{dr^{n}}\sqrt{T_{1}(r)}\Big|\lesssim\langle r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}-n}.

We divide the proof into two steps.

First, let
0<r<\frac{1}{2}
and prove that any derivative of
\sqrt{T_{1}(r)}
is bounded. We have that
\sqrt{T_{1}(r)}
is bounded in this region. By direct computation, we observe

(A.3)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dr}\sqrt{T_{1}(r)}=\frac{sech^{2}(r)}{(2r)^{2}\sqrt{T_{1}(r)}}\bigg(2r+\frac{e^{-2r}-e^{2r}}{2}\bigg)=:\frac{sech^{2}(r)}{\sqrt{T_{1}(r)}}G(r),

where
G(r)
can be written as a series by expanding the exponentials. Indeed, we have that

\displaystyle G(r)=\frac{1}{(2r)^{2}}\bigg(2r+\frac{e^{-2r}-e^{2r}}{2}\bigg)=-\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2r)^{2k+1}}{(2k+3)!}.

The series is uniformly convergent for
r\geq 0
. Moreover,
G(r)
and its derivatives are bounded for
0<r<\frac{1}{2}
. By extension, since for all
n\geq 0
there holds
\frac{d^{n}}{dr^{n}}sech^{2}(r)\lesssim e^{-2r}
we have that

\Big|\frac{d^{n}}{dr^{n}}\sqrt{T_{1}(r)}\Big|\lesssim 1.

Now, we let
r\geq\frac{1}{2}
and prove the necessary decay estimate. We use the identity

(A.4)

tanh(r)=1-\frac{2}{e^{2r}+1},

and deduce by the chain rule that

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{d^{n}}{dr^{n}}\sqrt{T_{1}(r)}\Big|\lesssim\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big(\frac{1}{r}\big(1-\frac{2}{e^{2r}+1}\big)\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}-k}r^{-k-n}\lesssim\langle r\rangle^{-\frac{1}{2}-n}.

Lastly, we have that (
A.2
) follows by the Leibniz rule. Indeed, we observe

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{d^{n}}{dr^{n}}\sqrt{K_{1}(r)}\Big|

\displaystyle=\Big|\frac{d^{n}}{dr^{n}}\sqrt{T_{1}(r)(1+\beta r^{2})}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}\Big|\frac{d^{n-k}}{dr^{n-k}}\sqrt{T_{1}(r)}\Big|\Big|\frac{d^{k}}{dr^{k}}\sqrt{1+\beta r^{2}}\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\langle r\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}-n},

which concludes the proof of Lemma
A.1
.
∎

\displaystyle\langle\xi\rangle^{n}\Big|\frac{d^{n}}{d\xi^{n}}\big(\chi^{(1)}_{\mu}(\xi)\sqrt{K_{\mu}(\xi)}\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big|\frac{d^{n-k}}{d\xi^{n-k}}\big(\chi^{(1)}(\sqrt{\mu}\xi)\big)\>\frac{d^{k}}{d\xi^{k}}\big(\sqrt{K_{\mu}(\xi)}\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mu^{\frac{n-k}{2}}\big|(\frac{d^{n-k}}{d\xi^{n-k}}\chi^{(1)})(\sqrt{\mu}\xi)\big|\langle\xi\rangle^{-k}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mu^{\frac{n-k}{2}}\langle\xi\rangle^{n-k}\big|(\frac{d^{n-k}}{d\xi^{n-k}}\chi^{(1)})(\sqrt{\mu}\xi)\big|\lesssim_{\beta}1.

Hence,
(\chi^{(1)}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}})\in\mathcal{S}^{0}
and
\mathcal{N}^{0}(\chi^{(1)}_{\mu}\sqrt{\mathcal{K}_{\mu}})\lesssim_{\beta}1
independently from
\mu
, proves (
A.10
).

Next, we consider estimates (
2.27
) and (
2.28
). Recalling (
2.26
) we define

(A.12)

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)=\mu^{-\frac{1}{4}}\sigma_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}}\>\frac{1}{\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\>(1+\mu\beta\xi^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then, it suffices to prove that

(A.13)

\sup\limits_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}}\>\langle\xi\rangle^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\Big|\frac{d^{n}}{d\xi^{n}}(\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}\tilde{\sigma}_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}})(\xi)\Big|\lesssim_{\beta}1,

for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and any
\mu\in(0,1)
. Indeed, if we assume (
A.13
) and take
n=0
we deduce from Plancherel’s identity that

\|(\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}\sigma_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}})(D)f\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{\beta}\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim_{\beta}\|f\|_{L^{2}}+\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|D^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|_{L^{2}}.

which proves (
2.27
). Moreover, (
A.13
) also implies that
(\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}\tilde{\sigma}_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}})\in\mathcal{S}^{\frac{1}{2}}
with
\mathcal{N}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}\tilde{\sigma}_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}})\lesssim_{\beta}1
so that

\|[(\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}{\sigma}_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}})(D),f]\partial_{x}g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim_{\beta}\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|f\|_{H^{s}}\|g\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}},

by Lemma
A.3
. Now we prove (
A.13
). First, we consider the functions,

\displaystyle a_{\mu}(\xi)=\frac{1}{\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\quad\text{and}\quad b_{\mu}(\xi)=(1+\mu\beta\xi^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then, since
|\xi|>\sqrt{\mu}|\xi|>1
on the support of
\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}
, we observe that

(A.14)

\displaystyle\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}(\xi)\Big|\frac{d^{k}}{d\xi^{k}}a_{\mu}(\xi)\Big|\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}(\xi)\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}|\xi|}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{k-\frac{1}{2}}}\lesssim\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}-k}\langle\sqrt{\mu}\xi\rangle^{-1}.

While
b_{\mu}(\xi)\lesssim_{\beta}\langle\sqrt{\mu}\xi\rangle
and its deriatives satisfy the bound,

(A.15)

\displaystyle\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}(\xi)\Big|\frac{d^{k}}{d\xi^{k}}b_{\mu}(\xi)\Big|\lesssim_{\beta}\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}(\xi)\mu^{\frac{k}{2}}\langle\sqrt{\mu}\xi\rangle^{1-k}\lesssim_{\beta}\langle\sqrt{\mu}\xi\rangle\langle\xi\rangle^{-k}.

Thus, if all derivatives falls on
\tilde{\sigma}_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}}
, the Leibniz rule, (
A.14
) and (
A.15
) imply

\displaystyle\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}(\xi)\Big|\frac{d^{k}}{d\xi^{k}}\tilde{\sigma}_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)\Big|\lesssim\mu^{-\frac{1}{4}}\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}(\xi)\sum_{j=0}^{k}\Big|\frac{d^{k-j}}{d\xi^{k-j}}a_{\mu}(\xi)\Big|\>\Big|\frac{d^{j}}{d\xi^{j}}b_{\mu}(\xi)\Big|\lesssim_{\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}-k}.

On the other hand, when derivatives fall the cut-off function, we observe

(A.16)

\mu^{\frac{k}{2}}\langle\xi\rangle^{k}\Big|(\frac{d^{k}}{d\xi^{k}}\chi^{(2)})(\sqrt{\mu}\xi)\Big|\lesssim 1,\quad k\geq 1,

since the support of
\frac{d^{k}}{d\xi^{k}}\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}
is contained in the support of
\chi^{(1)}_{\mu}
. As a result, there holds

\displaystyle\langle\xi\rangle^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\Big|\frac{d^{n}}{d\xi^{n}}\big(\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}(\xi)\tilde{\sigma}_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big|\frac{d^{n-k}}{d\xi^{n-k}}\big(\chi^{(2)}(\sqrt{\mu}\xi)\big)\Big|\>\Big|\frac{d^{k}}{d\xi^{k}}\big(\tilde{\sigma}_{\mu,\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{n-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mu^{\frac{n-k}{2}}\big|(\frac{d^{n-k}}{d\xi^{n-k}}\chi^{(2)})(\sqrt{\mu}\xi)\big|\>\langle\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}-k}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mu^{\frac{n-k}{2}}\langle\xi\rangle^{n-k}\big|(\frac{d^{n-k}}{d\xi^{n-k}}\chi^{(2)})(\sqrt{\mu}\xi)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}1.

The estimate is uniform in
\mu\in(0,1)
, and (
A.13
) is proved, which provides the desired result.

Lastly, we prove (
2.30
) and (
2.31
) arguing in the same vein. First, we write:

\displaystyle\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}(\xi)\sigma_{\mu,0}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}(\xi)\cdot\>\frac{1}{\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\>\cdot\Big(1+\mu\beta\xi^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\cdot\Big(\frac{2}{e^{2\sqrt{\mu}|\xi|}+1}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=:\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}(\xi)\>a_{\mu}(\xi)\>b_{\mu}(\xi)\>c_{\mu}(\xi),

making use of the identity (
A.4
). Then we observe for all

that

(A.17)

\displaystyle\langle\xi\rangle^{k}\langle\sqrt{\mu}\xi\rangle^{N}\Big|\frac{d^{k}}{d\xi^{k}}c_{\mu}(\xi)\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mu^{\frac{k}{2}}\langle\xi\rangle^{k}\langle\sqrt{\mu}\xi\rangle^{N}e^{-\sqrt{\mu}|\xi|}\lesssim 1.

As a result, we deduce by (
A.15
) and (
A.17
) with
N=1
that

\displaystyle\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}(\xi)\Big|\frac{d^{k}}{d\xi^{k}}(b_{\mu}(\xi)\>c_{\mu}(\xi))\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}(\xi)\sum_{j=0}^{k}\big|\frac{d^{k-j}}{d\xi^{k-j}}\big(b_{\mu}(\xi)\big)\big|\>\big|\frac{d^{j}}{d\xi^{j}}\big(c_{\mu}(\xi)\big)\big|

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\langle\xi\rangle^{-(k-j)}\langle\sqrt{\mu}\xi\rangle\>\langle\xi\rangle^{-j}\langle\sqrt{\mu}\xi\rangle^{-1}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{-k}.

Moreover, we use (
A.14
) to deduce

\displaystyle\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}(\xi)\Big|\frac{d^{k}}{d\xi^{k}}\sigma_{\mu,0}(\xi)\Big|\lesssim\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}(\xi)\sum_{j=0}^{k}\Big|\frac{d^{k-j}}{d\xi^{k-j}}\big(a_{\mu}(\xi)\big)\Big|\>\Big|\frac{d^{j}}{d\xi^{j}}\big(b_{\mu,\beta}(\xi)\>c_{\mu}(\xi)\big)\Big|\lesssim_{\beta}\langle\xi\rangle^{-k},

from which we find

\displaystyle\langle\xi\rangle^{n}\Big|\frac{d^{n}}{d\xi^{n}}\big(\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}(\xi)\sigma_{\mu,0}(\xi)\big)\Big|\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Big|\frac{d^{n-k}}{d\xi^{n-k}}\big(\chi^{(2)}_{\mu}(\xi)\big)\Big|\>\Big|\frac{d^{k}}{d\xi^{k}}\big(\sigma_{\mu,0}(\xi)\big)\Big|\lesssim_{\beta}1,

by (
A.16
). Arguing as above, we may conclude that the estimates (
2.30
) and (
2.31

Proof of Lemma

Next, we consider (

\displaystyle|\hat{a}_{1}(\xi)(f,g)|

\displaystyle\|\partial_{x}[\sqrt{\mathcal{T}_{\mu}}(D),f]g\|_{L^{2}}=\|\hat{a}_{1}(\xi)(f,g)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(\|f\|_{H^{t_{0}}}+\|\partial_{x}f\|_{H^{t_{0}}})\|g\|_{L^{2}}.

|\rho|>1

Finally, we estimate (

(A.19)

\frac{\xi^{2}-\rho^{2}}{|\xi-\rho|\>|\xi|}\leq\frac{|\xi|+|\rho|}{|\xi|}\lesssim 1.

As a consequence, recalling
\mu\in(0,1)

\displaystyle b_{2}(\xi,\rho)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(1-\frac{1+\mu\rho^{2}}{1+\mu\xi^{2}}\Big)\frac{\langle\sqrt{\mu}\xi\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\langle\sqrt{\mu}\rho\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}|\rho|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu(\xi^{2}-\rho^{2})}{\mu^{\frac{1}{4}}(1+\mu\xi^{2})^{\frac{3}{4}+\frac{1}{4}}}\frac{(1+\mu\xi^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}}}{\langle\rho\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}|\rho|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\xi^{2}-\rho^{2}}{|\xi|}\frac{|\rho|}{|\xi|^{\frac{1}{2}}\langle\rho\rangle^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle b_{2}(\xi,\rho)

We may therefore conclude that (

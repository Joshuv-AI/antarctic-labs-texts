Hölder and Harnack estimates for integro-differential operators with kernels of measure
Jingya Chen

Abstract
We establish Hölder and Harnack estimates for weak solutions of a class of elliptic nonlocal equations that are modeled on integro-differential operators with kernels of measure. The approach is of De Giorgi-type, as developed by DiBenedetto, Gianazza and Vespri in a local setting. Our results generalize the work by Dyda and Kassmann (2020).



1
Introduction

The aim of this work is to prove regularity estimates for local weak solutions of elliptic equations of type

\mathscr{L}u\equiv\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\big(u(x)-u(y)\big)\mu(x,dy)=0\quad\text{in}~E.

(1.1)

Here,
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}~(N\geqslant 2)
is a bounded domain, the symbol
p.v.
means “in the principal value sense”, whereas
(\mu(x,\cdot))_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
is a family of measures on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
whose properties will be specified next.

Given a general family of measures that satisfy basic structural conditions such as symmetry, a Poincaré-type inequality and a proper decay estimate, locally bounded, local weak solutions of (
1.1
) are locally Hölder continuous. Under the same assumptions on the measures, we obtain a weak Harnack inequality for local weak super-solutions of (
1.1
) as well. If
\mu
is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure and the density function satisfies a certain “uniform jump and symmetry” condition, then we conclude a boundedness estimate for local weak solutions, that will lead to a full Harnack inequality.

Our concept of solutions involves quadratic forms related to
\mu(x,dy)
. The following two subsections are devoted to the set-up, assumptions and the statements of our main results.

1.1
Definitions and Notation

Throughout this note, we will use
B_{r}(x_{0})
to denote the ball of radius
r
and center
x_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. When
x_{0}
is the origin, we will omit the center
x_{0}
from the symbol for simplicity.

Let
\mu=(\mu(x,\cdot))_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
be a family of Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. We say
\Omega
is
\mu
-measurable if it is
\mu(x,dy)
-measurable for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. They are symmetric in the following sense.

Assumption 1.1
.

For any
\mu
-measurable sets
\Omega_{1}
and
\Omega_{2}
with
\Omega_{1}\cap\Omega_{2}=\varnothing
, we have that
x\mapsto\int_{\Omega_{i}}\mu(x,dy),~i=1,2
are Lebesgue measurable and

\int_{\Omega_{1}}\int_{\Omega_{2}}\mu(x,dy)dx=\int_{\Omega_{2}}\int_{\Omega_{1}}\mu(x,dy)dx.

(S)

For a given family
\mu
we consider the following quadratic forms on
L^{2}(D)\times L^{2}(D)
, where
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is some open set:

\mathcal{E}_{D}^{\mu}(u,\varphi)=\int_{D}\int_{D}(u(y)-u(x))(\varphi(y)-\varphi(x))\,\mu(x,dy)dx.

Definition 1.1

(function spaces)
.

We say
u\in H_{\text{loc}}^{\mu}(E)
if
\mathcal{E}_{D}^{\mu}(u,u)
is finite for all
\bar{D}\subseteq E
.
Let
H^{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be the vector space of functions
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\mathcal{E}^{\mu}(u,u)=\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{\mu}(u,u)
is finite.

Definition 1.2

(solution concept)
.

A Lebesgue measurable function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a local weak sub(super)-solution of (
1.1
), if it satisfies
u\in H_{\text{loc}}^{\mu}(E)
,

\sup_{x\in B_{\frac{7}{8}R}(x_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}(x_{0})}|u(y)|\,\mu(x,dy)<\infty,\quad\text{for any}~B_{R}(x_{0})\subset E

and

\mathcal{E}^{\mu}(u,\varphi)\leqslant(\geqslant)0

(1.2)

for every non-negative
\varphi\in H^{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with supp
u\subseteq E
.
A function
u
that is both a local weak sub-solution and a local weak super-solution of (
1.1
) is a local weak solution.

Note that
\mathcal{E}^{\mu}(u,\varphi)
is finite as can be seen from the decomposition

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\mu}(u,\varphi)=

\displaystyle\int_{D}\int_{D}(u(y)-u(x))(\varphi(y)-\varphi(x))\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle+2\int_{D}\int_{D^{c}}(u(y)-u(x))(\varphi(y)-\varphi(x))\,\mu(x,dy)dx,

where we used (S), and from the function spaces
u
and
\varphi
belong to.

A central object of our study is the
nonlocal tail
of a sub(super)-solution
u
given by

{\rm Tail}(u;x_{0},r_{1},r_{2}):=r_{2}^{\alpha}\underset{x\in B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}{\text{sup}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r_{2}}(x_{0})}u(y)\,\mu(x,dy),

where
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})\subset B_{r_{2}}(x_{0})\subset E
. When
x_{0}=0
, we omit it from the notation. According to the notion of solutions, the tail is finite if
r_{1}\leqslant\frac{7}{8}r_{2}
.

1.2
Assumptions and Main results

In this subsection, we list the other assumptions on
\mu
. Throughout this paper, we take
\alpha\in(0,2)
. Firstly, a Poincaré-type inequality holds true.

Assumption 1.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}_{1}\geqslant 1
. For every ball
B_{\rho}(x_{0})\subset E
and every
v\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\mu}(E)
,

\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}(v(x)-[v]_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})})^{2}dx\leqslant\mathfrak{C}_{1}\rho^{\alpha}\mathcal{E}_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}^{\mu}(v,v),

(H
1
)

where

[v]_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}=\fint_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}v(x)dx.

We also need an additional assumption on the decay of the measures
\mu
considered.

Assumption 1.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{C}_{2}\geqslant 1
. For every
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
r>0
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r}(x_{0})}\mu(x_{0},dy)\leqslant\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{2}}{r^{\alpha}}.

(H
2
)

Remark 1.1
.

An important example satisfying the above conditions (
S
), (
H1
) and (
H2
) is given by

\mu_{\alpha}(x,dy)=(2-\alpha)\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+\alpha}}\,dy.

We denote by
H^{\alpha/2}(E)
the usual Sobolev space of fractional order
\alpha/2\in(0,1)
with the norm

\|u\|_{H^{\alpha/2}(E)}=(\|u\|_{L^{2}(E)}^{2}+\mathcal{E}_{E}^{\mu_{\alpha}}(u,u))^{\frac{1}{2}}.

In particular, Assumption
1.2
plays the role of an ellipticity condition. It naturally holds if for every ball
B_{\rho}(x_{0})\subset E
and every
v\in H^{\alpha/2}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))\cap H_{\text{loc}}^{\mu}(E)
,

\mathcal{E}_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}^{\mu_{\alpha}}(v,v)\leqslant C\mathcal{E}_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}^{\mu}(v,v)

for some
C>0
. This is an assumption used in
[
14
]
. It is also assumed that the reverse estimate holds in
[
14
]
. We find, however, this is redundant.

Now, let us formulate some of our main results.

Theorem 1.1

(Hölder regularity)
.

Let
\mu
satisfy (
S
), (
H1
) and (
H2
). Let
u
be a locally bounded, local weak solution of (
1.1
) in
E
. Then,
u
is locally Hölder continuous in
E
. More precisely, there exist constants
\gamma>1
and
\alpha_{*}\in(0,1)
that can be determined a priori only in terms of the data
\{\mathfrak{C}_{1},\mathfrak{C}_{2},N,\alpha\}
, such that for any
0<\rho<R,
there holds

\underset{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}{\rm{osc}}~u\leqslant\gamma\omega\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha_{*}},

provided the ball
B_{R}(x_{0})
is included in
E
, where

\omega=2\underset{B_{R}(x_{0})}{\rm{sup}}~|u|+{\rm Tail}(|u|;x_{0},\tfrac{7}{8}R,R).

Theorem 1.2

(weak Harnack estimate)
.

Let
\mu
satisfy (
S
), (
H1
) and (
H2
). Let
u
be a local weak super-solution of (
1.1
) in
E
, such that
u\geqslant 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
. Then, for any
0<r<R/32
, there holds

\inf_{B_{2r}(x_{0})}u\geqslant\eta\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u^{\varepsilon}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\varepsilon}}-\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},\tfrac{7}{8}R,R),

provided the ball
B_{R}(x_{0})
is included in
E
, where the constants
\varepsilon,\eta\in(0,1)
depend only on
\mathfrak{C}_{1},\mathfrak{C}_{2},N
and
\alpha
.

Under the mere conditions (
S
), (
H1
) and (
H2
), a full Harnack estimate generally cannot be reached; see
[
2
,
21
]
for counterexamples. In what follows, we adopt a standard assumption, called (
UJS
), in order to establish a full Harnack estimate.

We assume that
\mu
can be written as

\mu(x,dy)=K(x,y)\,dy

(1.3)

where
K:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is a measurable function that is symmetric in the sense that
K(x,y)=K(y,x)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
K(x,y)
satisfies (
UJS
) as follows.

Assumption 1.4
.

There exists
\hat{c}>0
such that for every
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and every
r\leqslant\frac{|x-y|}{2}
we have

K(x,y)\leqslant\hat{c}\fint_{B_{r}(x)}K(z,y)\,dz.

(UJS)

Apparently
\mu_{\alpha}
satisfies (
UJS
) condition. We can find some more examples that satisfy (
UJS
) in
[
21
, Chapter 12]
. Here, we point out that the (
UJS
) assumption together with (
H2
) implies a pointwise upper bound of
K
; see Lemma
2.5
.

Now we are ready to formulate the other two main results in this paper.

Theorem 1.3

(Harnack estimate)
.

Let
\mu
be represented by (
1.3
) and satisfy (
S
), (
H1
), (
H2
) and (
UJS
). Let
u
be a local weak solution of (
1.1
) in
E
, such that
u\geqslant 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
. Then, for any
0<r<R/32
, there holds

\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u\leqslant\gamma\inf_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u+\gamma\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},\tfrac{7}{8}R,R),

provided the ball
B_{R}(x_{0})
is included in
E
, where the constant
\gamma>1
depends only on
\mathfrak{C}_{1},\mathfrak{C}_{2},N,\alpha
and
\hat{c}
.

In pursuit of the Harnack estimate, we also establish a boundedness estimate for sub-solutions.

Theorem 1.4

(Local boundedness estimate)
.

Let
\mu
be represented by (
1.3
) and satisfy (
S
), (
H1
), (
H2
) and (
UJS
). Let
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset E
. Let
u
be a local weak sub-solution of (
1.1
) in
E
, such that
u\geqslant 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
. Then, for any
0<r<R
,

\sup_{B_{\frac{r}{2}}(x_{0})}u\leqslant\delta r^{\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\frac{r}{2}}(x_{0})}\frac{u_{+}(x)}{|x-x_{0}|^{N+\alpha}}\,dx+\gamma\frac{1}{\delta^{\frac{N}{2\alpha}}}\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u_{+}^{2}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},

for any
\delta\in(0,1]
, where the constant
\gamma
depends only on
\mathfrak{C}_{1},\mathfrak{C}_{2},N,\alpha
and
\hat{c}
.

Throughout this article, we refer to
\{\mathfrak{C}_{1},\mathfrak{C}_{2},N,\alpha\}
as
data
. In various estimates, we will use
\gamma
as a generic constant.

1.3
Novelty and significance

The local regularity theory for elliptic equations driven by non-local operators has become a highly active field of research in recent years. In the article
[
14
]
, the authors considered a kind of general integro-differential operators with kernels of measure which are allowed to be singular. They established regularity results including a weak Harnack inequality for super-solutions and Hölder regularity for globally bounded weak solutions of (
1.1
). On general metric measure spaces with the volume doubling property, Harnack inequalities and Hölder estimates of elliptic and parabolic equations were established in
[
6
,
7
,
15
]
in connection with Dirichlet forms.

Our study is motivated by
[
14
]
and clarifies some assumptions on the measures
(\mu(x,\cdot))_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}
that lead to regularity estimates. In particular, we find that a Poincaré-type inequality and a decay estimate of the measures are sufficient for Hölder regularity and weak Harnack estimates to hold. In addition, if we assume a so-called (
UJS
) condition, then Harnack estimates follow. This kind of simplification is probably known to experts. However, we do not find it in print.

Different from the Moser-type approach adopted in
[
14
]
, the conclusion in this paper mainly follows from DeGiorgi’s approach, developed by DiBenedetto, Gianazza and Vespri in local settings. We also follow some ideas by Liao in
[
19
]
to complete our proofs. This approach differs from the Moser iteration and circumvents a large amount of algebraic inequalities. In fact, we find that the regularity properties are encoded in the energy estimates in Proposition
3.1
and logarithmic estimate in Proposition
3.2
.

In regularity theory one of the essential tools is the so-called
expansion of positivity
, which translates measure theoretical estimates of positivity into a pointwise one. Inspired by
[
12
]
, a logarithmic estimate plays an important role in the derivation of the expansion of positivity, which is the key step for obtaining Hölder estimate and weak Harnack estimate. The Hölder regularity (Theorem
1.1
) provides an extension of the analogous result in
[
14
]
to the general measure framework as it weakens the conditions on the measure
\mu
and manages the tail.

Theorem
1.2
concerns a weak Harnack inequality. Its proof hinges on an improved expansion of positivity, avoiding crossover estimates based on BMO estimates of
\log\,u
. A classic method to prove the improved expansion of positivity lemma is to apply Krylov-Safanov covering arguments, see for instance
[
8
,
11
]
. This dates back to the pioneering work of DiBenedetto and Trudinger
[
9
]
. In our work, we provide an alternative approach via the clustering technique of DiBenedetto, Gianazza and Vespri stated in Lemma
2.6
. This more geometric approach has the potential to be generalized to study different nonlinear, nonlocal operators, also parabolic ones, See in this connection
[
3
]
.

To establish a full Harnack inequality, we additionally assume that
\mu
can be represented as in (
1.3
) and satisfies (
UJS
). A key step towards the Harnack inequality is a boundedness estimate stated in Theorem
1.4
. Joining the boundedness estimate and the weak Harnack inequality, we conclude a full Harnack inequality via an interpolation argument. To the best of the author’s knowledge, the (
UJS
) assumption was first introduced by Barlow, Bass and Kumagai in
[
1
]
, where it appeared in the setting of discrete graphs. See
[
5
]
for a general setting of metric measure spaces. Our (
UJS
) assumption is a localized version, which is also used by Schulze in
[
21
]
.

1.4
Structure of the paper

This article is structured as follows. In Section 2, we provide some preliminary results. In Section 3 we deduce energy estimates for local weak sub(super)-solutions and an expansion of positivity lemma for local weak super-solutions. Section 4 provides the proof of Hölder regularity, whereas the weak Harnack inequality is proved in Section 5. In Section 6, we prove local boundedness estimates for weak solutions or sub-solutions. Finally, in Section 7 we develop a full Harnack inequality.

2
Auxiliary results

The purpose of this section is to provide several implicant of (
S
), (
H1
) and (
H2
). In addition, we establish a clustering lemma. The fact that the Poincaré inequality implies the Faber-Krahn inequality is well-known in local settings; see for example
[
20
]
. However, we do not find a detailed proof for our purpose in print. For the convenience of readers, we state and prove the relative lemma applicable to this paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

If (
H1
) holds,
v\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\mu}(B_{\rho})
and
\mathrm{supp}\,v\subset\Omega_{0}\subset B_{\rho}
, then there exists a constant
c_{0}
only depending on
\mathfrak{C}_{1},N
and
\alpha
, such that

c_{0}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{\mu}(v,v)\geqslant|\Omega_{0}|^{-\frac{\alpha}{N}}\int_{\Omega_{0}}|v|^{2}\,dx.

(FK)

Proof.

Let
f\in H_{\text{loc}}^{\mu}(B_{\rho})
satisfy
\text{supp}\,f\subset B_{\rho}
and set
f_{r}(x):=\fint_{B_{r}(x)}f(y)dy
for any
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. It follows from triangle’s inequality that

\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}\leqslant 2\|f-f_{r}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}+2\|f_{r}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}.

(2.1)

Fix
r>0
and let
\{x_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a countable set in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
satisfying

\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{r}(x_{n})=\mathbb{R}^{N}\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}{{\mathchoice{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\displaystyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\textstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptstyle\chi$}}{\raisebox{0.0pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle\chi$}}}}_{B_{2r}(x_{n})}(y)\leqslant\theta_{N},~\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

where
\theta_{N}
depends only on
N
. Besicovitch’s covering theorem ensures that there exists such a countable set
\{x_{n}\}
.
Then, by (
H1
) we have

\displaystyle\|f-f_{r}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}=

\displaystyle~|B_{r}|^{-2}\int_{B_{\rho}}\Big(\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f(x)-f(y)|\,dy\Big)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~|B_{r}|^{-2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{r}(x_{n})}\Big(\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f(x)-f(y)|\,dy\Big)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~|B_{r}|^{-2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{r}(x_{n})}\Big(\int_{B_{2r}(x_{n})}|f(x)-f(y)|\,dy\Big)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\mathfrak{C}_{1}|B_{r}|^{-2}|B_{2r}|^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(2r)^{\alpha}\mathcal{E}_{B_{2r}(x_{n})}^{\mu}(f,f)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{1},N,\alpha)r^{\alpha}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{\mu}(f,f).

(2.2)

For the second term on the right-hand side of (
2.1
), it is easy to verify that

\|f_{r}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}\leqslant\|f_{r}\|_{L^{1}(B_{\rho})}\|f_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}.

Let us estimate the right-hand side separately. First, since
\text{supp}\,f\subset B_{\rho}
, we have

\|f_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}=\sup_{x\in B_{\rho}}\bigg|\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}(x)}f(y)\,dy\bigg|\leqslant\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{\rho}}|f(y)|\,dy.

Next, using a similar covering argument as (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}\bigg|\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}(x)}f(y)\,dy\bigg|dx\,\leqslant

\displaystyle~\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{r}(x_{n})}\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|f(y)|\,dydx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{B_{r}(x_{n})}\frac{1}{|B_{r}|}\int_{B_{2r}(x_{n})}|f(y)|\,dydx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma(N)\int_{B_{\rho}}|f(y)|\,dy.

Therefore, we acquire

\|f_{r}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}\leqslant\gamma(N)|B_{r}|^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{1}(B_{\rho})}^{2}.

(2.3)

Inserting (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) into (
2.1
), we get

\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}\leqslant\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{1},N,\alpha)\left(r^{\alpha}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{\mu}(f,f)+|B_{r}|^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{1}(B_{\rho})}^{2}\right)

for every
r>0
. Next, choose
r
such that

r^{\alpha}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{\mu}(f,f)=|B_{r}|^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{1}(B_{\rho})}^{2}

i.e.

r^{N+\alpha}=\frac{\|f\|_{L^{1}(B_{\rho})}^{2}}{|B_{1}|\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{\mu}(f,f)}.

As a result, we obtain that

\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2}\leqslant\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{1},N,\alpha)\|f\|_{L^{1}(B_{\rho})}^{\frac{2\alpha}{N+\alpha}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{\mu}(f,f)^{\frac{N}{N+\alpha}},

which we can also write as

\|f\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2(1+\frac{\alpha}{N})}\leqslant\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{1},N,\alpha)\|f\|_{L^{1}(B_{\rho})}^{\frac{2\alpha}{N}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{\mu}(f,f).

(2.4)

Now, let
v\in H_{\text{loc}}^{\mu}(B_{\rho})
satisfy
\text{supp}\,v\subset\Omega_{0}\subset B_{\rho}
. Using Hölder’s inequality
\|v\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{0})}\leqslant|\Omega_{0}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}
, we apply (
2.4
) to
v
and obtain that

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}^{2(1+\frac{\alpha}{N})}=\|v\|_{L^{2}(B_{\rho})}^{2(1+\frac{\alpha}{N})}\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma\|v\|_{L^{1}(B_{\rho})}^{\frac{2\alpha}{N}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{\mu}(v,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\gamma\|v\|_{L^{1}(\Omega_{0})}^{\frac{2\alpha}{N}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{\mu}(v,v)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma|\Omega_{0}|^{\frac{\alpha}{N}}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}^{\frac{2\alpha}{N}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{\mu}(v,v)

for some
\gamma=\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{1},N,\alpha)
. After simplification, we get

\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{0})}^{2}\leqslant\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{1},N,\alpha)|\Omega_{0}|^{\frac{\alpha}{N}}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{\mu}(v,v)

which is (
FK
) for
c_{0}=\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{1},N,\alpha)
. Since
v
was arbitrary, the desired result follows.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

It is worthy to mention that a generalized Faber-Krahn inequality, which was introduced in
[
6
,
7
,
17
]
, can play an equally effective role as (
FK
) in the regularity analysis of integro-differential equations. Particularly, it can be stated as follows:

There exist
c_{0},s>0
, such that for all
\Omega_{0}\subset B_{\rho}(x_{0})
and every
v\in H_{\text{loc}}^{\mu}(B_{\rho}(x_{0}))
with
\text{supp}\,v\subset\Omega_{0}
,

c_{0}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{\mu}(v,v)\geqslant\rho^{-\alpha}\bigg(\frac{|B_{\rho}(x_{0})|}{|\Omega_{0}|}\bigg)^{s}\int_{\Omega_{0}}|v|^{2}\,dx.

In this paper, we often do not directly use (
H2
) when we estimate measures, instead we use the following corollary.

Lemma 2.2
.

If (
H2
) holds, then for every
0<r_{1}<r_{2}
,

\sup_{x\in B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r_{2}}(x_{0})}\mu(x,dy)\leqslant\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{2}}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{\alpha}}.

Proof.

Pick
x\in B_{r_{1}}(x_{0})
.
Since
r_{1}<r_{2}
, there has

\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r_{2}}(x_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r_{2}-r_{1}}(x).

Therefore, by using (
H2
), we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r_{2}}(x_{0})}\mu(x,dy)\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r_{2}-r_{1}}(x)}\mu(x,dy)\leqslant\frac{\mathfrak{C}_{2}}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{\alpha}}.

The proof is completed by the arbitrariness of
x
.
∎

The following lemma indicates that the assumption (
H2
) implies the local behavior of the measure
\mu
under a weight of quadratic functions. The proof is inspired by
[
4
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

If (
H2
) holds, then for every
r>0
, there holds

\frac{1}{r^{2}}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|x-y|^{2}\mu(x,dy)\leqslant\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{2},\alpha)\frac{1}{r^{\alpha}}.

Proof.

By using (
H2
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x)}|x-y|^{2}\mu(x,dy)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\int_{B_{2^{-n}r}(x)\backslash B_{2^{-n-1}r}(x)}|x-y|^{2}\mu(x,dy)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(2^{-n}r)^{2}\int_{B_{2^{-n}r}(x)\backslash B_{2^{-n-1}r}(x)}\mu(x,dy)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathfrak{C}_{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(2^{-n}r)^{2}(2^{-n-1}r)^{-\alpha}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{C}_{2}r^{2-\alpha}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}2^{-2n+\alpha n+\alpha}

\displaystyle=\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{2},\alpha)r^{2-\alpha}.

Since
x
is arbitrary, the desired result follows.
∎

Now we verify two properties of
\mu
which satisfies (
1.3
) and (
UJS
). The first property is stated as follows.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mu
be represented by (
1.3
) and satisfy (
UJS
). Then, for any non-negative function
v:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and any
r>0,\rho\in(0,r/2)
, there holds

\rho^{N}\sup_{x\in B_{r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r+\rho}}v(y)\,\mu(x,dy)\leqslant\gamma\int_{B_{r+\frac{\rho}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r+\rho}}v(y)\,\mu(x,dy)dx,

where the constant
\gamma
depends only on
N
and
\hat{c}
.

Proof.

Due to (
1.3
) and (
UJS
), by Fubini’s theorem we have

\displaystyle\rho^{N}\sup_{x\in B_{r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r+\rho}}v(y)\,\mu(x,dy)

\displaystyle~\qquad=\rho^{N}\sup_{x\in B_{r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r+\rho}}v(y)K(x,y)\,dy

\displaystyle~\qquad\leqslant\gamma(N)2^{N}\hat{c}\sup_{x\in B_{r}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r+\rho}}v(y)\bigg(\int_{B_{\rho/2}(x)}K(z,y)\,dz\bigg)dy

\displaystyle~\qquad\leqslant\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r+\rho}}v(y)\bigg(\int_{B_{r+\rho/2}}K(x,y)\,dx\bigg)dy

\displaystyle~\qquad=\gamma\int_{B_{r+\rho/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r+\rho}}v(y)K(x,y)\,dydx

\displaystyle~\qquad=\gamma\int_{B_{r+\rho/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r+\rho}}v(y)\,\mu(x,dy)dx

for some
\gamma=\gamma(N,\hat{c})
.
∎

The second property is that a point-wise upper bound condition follows from (
H2
) and (
UJS
).

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mu
be represented by (
1.3
) and satisfy (
H2
), (
UJS
). Then, there exists a constant
\gamma\geqslant 1
depending only on
\mathfrak{C}_{2},N,\alpha
and
\hat{c}
, such that

K(x,y)\leqslant\gamma\frac{1}{|x-y|^{N+\alpha}}\quad\text{for almost all}~~x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

Proof.

For any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, set
r=\frac{|x-y|}{2}
. Obviously, it holds
B_{r}(x)\subset B_{3r}(y)
and
r\leqslant|y-z|\leqslant 3r
for every
z\in B_{r}(x)
. Therefore, by (
H2
), (
UJS
) and the symmetry of
K
, we have

\displaystyle K(x,y)\leqslant

\displaystyle~\hat{c}\fint_{B_{r}(x)}K(z,y)dz

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma r^{-N-2}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|y-z|^{2}K(z,y)dz

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma r^{-N-2}\int_{B_{3r}(y)}|y-z|^{2}\mu(y,dz)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma|x-y|^{-N-\alpha},

where
\gamma
depends only on
\mathfrak{C}_{2},N,\alpha
and
\hat{c}
.
∎

As mentioned in the introduction, we provide an approach to the improved expansion of positivity lemma without applying the Krylov-Safanov covering argument. Instead, we pursue the approach of DiBenedetto, Gianazza and Vespri via a clustering lemma.

In order to state our result, for all
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and all
\rho>0
let

Q_{\rho}(x_{0})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\Big(x_{0}^{i}-\frac{\rho}{2},x_{0}^{i}+\frac{\rho}{2}\Big)

be the
N
-dimensional open cube centered at
x_{0}
with side length
\rho
.

Lemma 2.6

(Clustering lemma)
.

Let
\mu
satisfy (
H1
) and
M>0
. Assume
u\in H_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\mu}(Q_{\rho}(x_{0}))
satisfies

(a)

|\{u\geqslant M\}\cap Q_{\rho}(x_{0})|>\beta|Q_{\rho}(x_{0})|
for some
\beta\in(0,1)
;

(b)

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{\rho}(x_{0})}\int_{Q_{\rho}(x_{0})}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx\leqslant\Lambda M^{2}\rho^{N-\alpha}
for some
\Lambda>0
.

Then, for all
\delta,\lambda\in(0,1)
there exist
x_{1}\in Q_{\rho}(x_{0}),\eta\in(0,1)
, such that

|\{u>(1-\lambda)M\}\cap Q_{\eta\rho}(x_{1})|>(1-\delta)|Q_{\eta\rho}(x_{1})|,

where the constant
\eta
depends only on
\mathfrak{C}_{1},\alpha,\beta,\Lambda,\delta
and
\lambda
.

Proof.

Fix any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we partition
Q_{\rho}(x_{0})
into a family
\mathcal{F}_{k}
of cubes with side
\rho/k
, so that for all
Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}
we have

|Q|=\left(\frac{\rho}{k}\right)^{N}.

(2.5)

We use
\#\mathcal{F}
to represent the number of cubes in a family
\mathcal{F}
. Clearly,
\#\mathcal{F}_{k}=k^{N}
. Let
\beta\in(0,1)
be as in (a) and divide all
Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}
into two classes according to

\begin{cases}Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{+}\quad\text{if}\quad|Q\cap\{u>M\}|\geqslant\frac{\beta}{2}|Q|,\\
Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{-}\quad\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

By hypothesis (a), for all
Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{+}
we have

\displaystyle\beta k^{N}|Q|=\beta|Q_{\rho}(x_{0})|<

\displaystyle~|\{u\geqslant M\}\cap Q_{\rho}(x_{0})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}}|\{u\geqslant M\}\cap Q|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{+}}|\{u\geqslant M\}\cap Q|+\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{-}}|\{u\geqslant M\}\cap Q|.

Dividing both sides by
|Q|
, by the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{k}^{\pm}
, we estimate

\displaystyle\beta k^{N}<

\displaystyle\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{+}}\frac{|\{u\geqslant M\}\cap Q|}{|Q|}+\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{-}}\frac{|\{u\geqslant M\}\cap Q|}{|Q|}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\#\mathcal{F}_{k}^{+}+\frac{\beta}{2}\#\mathcal{F}_{k}^{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\frac{\beta}{2}k^{N}+\Big(1-\frac{\beta}{2}\Big)\#\mathcal{F}_{k}^{+},

which can be rephrased as

\#\mathcal{F}_{k}^{+}>\frac{\beta}{2-\beta}k^{N}.

(2.6)

Now fix
\delta,\lambda\in(0,1)
.
We claim that when
k
is large enough, there must exist
Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{+}
, such that

|\{u>(1-\lambda)M\}\cap Q|>(1-\delta)|Q|.

(2.7)

Otherwise, for all
Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{+}
we have

|\{u\leqslant(1-\lambda)M\}\cap Q|\geqslant\delta|Q|.

(2.8)

We will reach a contradiction. First, since
\lambda\in(0,1)
and
Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{+}
, we have

\Big|\Big\{u>\frac{2-\lambda}{2}M\Big\}\cap Q\Big|\geqslant|\{u>M\}\cap Q|\geqslant\frac{\beta}{2}|Q|.

(2.9)

Next fix
x,y\in Q
, such that

u(x)\leqslant(1-\lambda)M,\quad u(y)>\frac{2-\lambda}{2}M,

which yields

u(y)-u(x)>\frac{\lambda}{2}M.

(2.10)

Let
B
be the ball circumscribing
Q
such that
Q\subset B
. Applying (
H1
) and Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{Q}(u(x)-[u]_{Q})^{2}\,dx\leqslant

\displaystyle~2\int_{Q}(u(x)-[u]_{B})^{2}dx+2\int_{Q}([u]_{B}-[u]_{Q})^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~4\int_{B}(u(x)-[u]_{B})^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~4\mathfrak{C}_{1}\left(\frac{\rho}{k}\right)^{\alpha}\int_{B}\int_{B}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

By using (
2.8
), (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{Q}(u(x)-[u]_{Q})^{2}\,dx=

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\bigg(\fint_{Q}(u(y)-u(x))\,dy\bigg)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\int_{\{u\leqslant(1-\lambda)M\}\cap Q}\bigg(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{\{u>\frac{2-\lambda}{2}M\}\cap Q}(u(y)-u(x))\,dy\bigg)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle~\frac{\beta^{2}\lambda^{2}M^{2}}{16}|\{u\leqslant(1-\lambda)M\}\cap Q|

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle~\frac{\beta^{2}\lambda^{2}M^{2}\delta}{16}|Q|.

Therefore, under our current assumptions, the inequality

\frac{\beta^{2}\lambda^{2}M^{2}\delta}{16}|Q|\leqslant 4\mathfrak{C}_{1}\left(\frac{\rho}{k}\right)^{\alpha}\int_{B}\int_{B}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

holds for all
Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{+}
. Summing over
Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{+}
and using (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) we get

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{3}\lambda^{2}M^{2}\delta}{16(2-\beta)}\rho^{N}\leqslant

\displaystyle~\frac{\beta}{2-\beta}k^{N}\frac{\beta^{2}\lambda^{2}M^{2}\delta}{16}|Q|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~4\mathfrak{C}_{1}\left(\frac{\rho}{k}\right)^{\alpha}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{+}}\int_{B}\int_{B}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma(N,\mathfrak{C}_{1})\left(\frac{\rho}{k}\right)^{\alpha}\int_{Q_{\rho}(x_{0})}\int_{Q_{\rho}(x_{0})}|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\frac{\Lambda\gamma(N,\mathfrak{C}_{1})M^{2}\rho^{N}}{k^{\alpha}},

where we used hypothesis (b) in the last line. Rewriting the above inequality we get

k\leqslant\left(\frac{16(2-\beta)\Lambda\gamma(N,\mathfrak{C}_{1})}{\beta^{3}\lambda^{2}\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

(2.11)

That means if all
Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{+}
violate (
2.7
), then
k
must have an upper bound by (
2.11
), which is a contradiction. Therefore, if we take

k=\left(\frac{32(2-\beta)\Lambda\gamma(N,\mathfrak{C}_{1})}{\beta^{3}\lambda^{2}\delta}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},

(2.12)

there must exist
Q\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{+}
, such that (
2.7
) holds. Then, let
x_{1}\in Q_{\rho}(x_{0})
be the center of
Q,\eta=\frac{1}{k}\in(0,1),
and the conclusion follows.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

The idea behind the above clustering lemma mainly comes from
[
10
, Chapter 2, Lemma 3.1]
. An adaptation to a fractional setting appears in
[
13
]
. However, the result of
[
13
]
does not apply to our general setting.

Remark 2.3
.

This clustering lemma is stated on cubes, for balls, it is essentially equivalent. We only need to replace the segmentation argument in the above proof with a covering argument to see that.

3
Fundamental estimates

This section contains several important modules for the proof of regularity estimates. Indeed, we deduce the energy estimates and the expansion of positivity lemma for the solutions of (
1.1
). In what follows, when we state “
u
is a sub(super)-solution…” and use “
\pm
” or “
\mp
”, we mean the sub-solution corresponds to the upper sign and the super-solution corresponds to the lower sign in the statement.

3.1
Energy estimates

This subsection is devoted to energy estimates satisfied by local weak sub(super)-solutions of (
1.1
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mu
satisfy (
S
). Let
u
be a local weak sub(super)-solution of (
1.1
) in
E
. Then, for every non-negative piecewise smooth cutoff function
\zeta(\cdot)
compactly supported in
B_{R}\equiv B_{R}(x_{0})\subset E
, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}(\zeta(y)w_{\pm}(y)-\zeta(x)w_{\pm}(x))^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{B_{R}}w_{\pm}(x)\zeta^{2}(x)\,dx\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w_{\mp}(y)\,\mu(x,dy)\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad\leqslant 2\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}\max\{w_{\pm}^{2}(x),w_{\pm}^{2}(y)\}(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y))^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle\qquad+2\int_{B_{R}}w_{\pm}(x)\zeta^{2}(x)\,dx\bigg(\underset{x\in\mathrm{supp}\,\zeta(\cdot)}{\mathrm{sup}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}}w_{\pm}(y)\,\mu(x,dy)\bigg).

Here, we have denoted
w=u-k
.

Proof.

We will only deal with the case of sub-solution as the other case is similar. We use
\varphi=w_{+}\zeta^{2}
as a testing function in the weak form (
1.2
) of (
1.1
). Due to the support of
\zeta
and (
S
),
\mathcal{E}^{\mu}(u,\varphi)
can be split into two parts, that is

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\mu}(u,\varphi)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u(y)-u(x))[(u(y)-k)_{+}\zeta^{2}(y)-(u(x)-k)_{+}\zeta^{2}(x)]\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(u(y)-u(x))[(u(y)-k)_{+}\zeta^{2}(y)-(u(x)-k)_{+}\zeta^{2}(x)]\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle+2\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}}(u(x)-u(y))(u(x)-k)_{+}\zeta^{2}(x)\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle~I_{1}+2I_{2}.

In the first term, due to
w_{-}(y)w_{+}(y)=w_{-}(x)w_{+}(x)=0
, we have

\displaystyle(u(y)-u(x))[(u(y)-k)_{+}\zeta^{2}(y)-(u(x)-k)_{+}\zeta^{2}(x)]

\displaystyle\quad=[\zeta(y)w_{+}(y)-\zeta(x)w_{+}(x)]^{2}+w_{-}(y)w_{+}(x)\zeta^{2}(x)+w_{-}(x)w_{+}(y)\zeta^{2}(y)

\displaystyle~\qquad-w_{+}(y)w_{+}(x)(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y))^{2}.

Due to the support of
\zeta
and symmetry of
\mu
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}[\zeta(y)w_{+}(y)-\zeta(x)w_{+}(x)]^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle~+2\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w_{-}(y)w_{+}(x)\zeta^{2}(x)\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle~-\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}w_{+}(y)w_{+}(x)(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y))^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}[\zeta(y)w_{+}(y)-\zeta(x)w_{+}(x)]^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle~+2\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w_{-}(y)w_{+}(x)\zeta^{2}(x)\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle~-\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}\max\{w_{+}^{2}(x),w_{+}^{2}(y)\}(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y))^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx.

To treat the second term, we first estimate

\displaystyle-(u(x)-u(y))(u(x)-k)_{+}\zeta^{2}(x)=

\displaystyle~(u(y)-u(x))(u(x)-k)_{+}\zeta^{2}(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~(u(y)-k)_{+}(u(x)-k)_{+}\zeta^{2}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~w_{+}(y)w_{+}(x)\zeta^{2}(x).

Therefore,

\displaystyle-I_{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}}w_{+}(y)w_{+}(x)\zeta^{2}(x)\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}w_{+}(x)\zeta^{2}(x)\,dx\bigg(\underset{x\in\text{supp}\,\zeta}{\text{sup}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}}w_{+}(y)\,\mu(x,dy)\bigg).

Finally, we put all these estimates together and use the symmetry of
\mu
to conclude.
∎

3.2
Expansion of Positivity

In regularity theory for elliptic and parabolic integro-differential equations, one of the essential tools is the expansion of positivity lemma. Before stating it, let us introduce two preliminary lemmas, i.e. a critical mass lemma and a measure shrinking lemma.

Lemma 3.1

(critical mass)
.

Let
\mu
satisfy (
S
), (
H1
) and (
H2
). Let
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset E
,
0<\rho<R
and
M>0
. Let
u
be a local weak super-solution of (
1.1
) in
E
, such that
u\geqslant 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
. There exists a constant
\nu\in(0,1)
depending only on the data such that, if

|\{u<M\}\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})|\leqslant\nu|B_{\rho}(x_{0})|,

then either

\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)>M

or

u\geqslant\frac{M}{2}\quad\text{a.e. in}\quad B_{\rho/2}(x_{0}).

Proof.

Having fixed the pair of balls
B_{\rho/2}(x_{0})\subset B_{\rho}(x_{0}),
assume
x_{0}=0
and introduce for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

\displaystyle k_{n}=\frac{M}{2}+\frac{M}{2^{n+1}},\quad\rho_{n}=\frac{\rho}{2}+\frac{\rho}{2^{n+1}},\quad\widetilde{\rho}_{n}=\frac{\rho_{n}+\rho_{n+1}}{2},

\displaystyle B_{n}=B_{\rho_{n}},\qquad\widetilde{B}_{n}=B_{\widetilde{\rho}_{n}}.

Observe that

B_{n+1}\subset\widetilde{B}_{n}\subset B_{n}.

Take
\zeta_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to be a function such that
\text{supp}\,\zeta_{n}(\cdot)\subset\widetilde{B}_{n},~\|\zeta_{n}\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1,~\zeta_{n}(x)=1
in
B_{n+1}
and
|\nabla\zeta|\leqslant\gamma(N)2^{n+3}\rho^{-1}
. According to assumption (
H2
) and Lemma
2.3
, we have

\sup_{x\in B_{n}}\int_{B_{n}}(\zeta_{n}(y)-\zeta_{n}(x))^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)\leqslant\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{2},N,\alpha)\left(\frac{\rho}{2^{n+3}}\right)^{-\alpha}.

By Proposition
3.1
and (
S
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{n}}\int_{B_{n}}|\zeta_{n}(y)(u(y)-k_{n})_{-}-\zeta_{n}(x)(u(x)-k_{n})_{-}|^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{2},N,\alpha)\left(\frac{\rho}{2^{n+3}}\right)^{-\alpha}\int_{B_{n}}(u(x)-k_{n})_{-}^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle~+2\int_{B_{n}}(u(x)-k_{n})_{-}\,dx\bigg(\underset{x\in\text{supp}\,\zeta_{n}}{\text{sup}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{n}}(u(y)-k_{n})_{-}\,\mu(x,dy)\bigg).

(3.1)

It is standard to obtain

\int_{B_{n}}(u(x)-k_{n})_{-}^{2}dx\leqslant M^{2}|\{u<k_{n}\}\cap B_{n}|.

(3.2)

For the second term, recalling
u\geqslant 0
in
B_{R}
by assumption and
k_{n}\leqslant M
, we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{B_{n}}(u(x)-k_{n})_{-}\,dx\bigg(\underset{x\in\text{supp}\,\zeta_{n}}{\text{sup}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{n}}(u(y)-k_{n})_{-}\,\mu(x,dy)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~M|\{u<k_{n}\}\cap B_{n}|\bigg(\underset{x\in\widetilde{B}_{n}}{\text{sup}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{n}}k_{n}\,\mu(x,dy)+\underset{x\in\widetilde{B}_{n}}{\text{sup}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{n}}u_{-}(y)\,\mu(x,dy)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~M|\{u<k_{n}\}\cap B_{n}|\bigg(M\mathfrak{C}_{2}2^{(n+3)\alpha}\rho^{-\alpha}+\underset{x\in\widetilde{B}_{0}}{\text{sup}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{n}}u_{-}(y)\,\mu(x,dy)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~M|\{u<k_{n}\}\cap B_{n}|\Big(M\mathfrak{C}_{2}2^{(n+3)\alpha}\rho^{-\alpha}+\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\Big),

where we have used the assumption (
H2
) of
\mu
and Lemma
2.2
. If we impose

\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\leqslant M,

then the above estimate becomes

\displaystyle\int_{B_{n}}(u(x)-k_{n})_{-}\,dx\bigg(\underset{x\in\text{supp}\,\zeta_{n}}{\text{sup}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{n}}(u(y)-k_{n})_{-}\,\mu(x,dy)\bigg)\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{2})2^{(n+3)\alpha}M^{2}\rho^{-\alpha}|\{u<k_{n}\}\cap B_{n}|.

(3.3)

Combining (
3.1
), (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{n}}\int_{B_{n}}|\zeta_{n}(y)(u(y)-k_{n})_{-}-\zeta_{n}(x)(u(x)-k_{n})_{-}|^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle\quad\qquad\leqslant\gamma 2^{(n+3)\alpha}M^{2}\rho^{-\alpha}|\{u<k_{n}\}\cap B_{n}|,

for some
\gamma=\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{2},N,\alpha)
.
Furthermore, since

\displaystyle\int_{B_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{n}}|\zeta_{n}(x)(u(x)-k_{n})_{-}|^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\leqslant\underset{x\in\widetilde{B}_{n}}{\text{ sup}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{n}}\mu(x,dy)\int_{B_{n}}(u(x)-k_{n})_{-}^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\leqslant\mathfrak{C}_{2}2^{(n+3)\alpha}\rho^{-\alpha}M^{2}|\{u<k_{n}\}\cap B_{n}|,

we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{\mu}(\zeta_{n}(u-k_{n})_{-},\zeta_{n}(u-k_{n})_{-})

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\int_{B_{n}}\int_{B_{n}}|\zeta_{n}(y)(u(y)-k_{n})_{-}-\zeta_{n}(x)(u(x)-k_{n})_{-}|^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+2\int_{B_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{n}}|\zeta_{n}(x)(u(x)-k_{n})_{-}|^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant~\gamma 2^{(n+3)\alpha}M^{2}\rho^{-\alpha}|\{u<k_{n}\}\cap B_{n}|,

for some
\gamma=\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{2},N,\alpha)
.

An application of Lemma
2.1
with
v=\zeta_{n}(u-k_{n})_{-},\Omega_{0}=\widetilde{B}_{n}\cap\{u<k_{n}\}
gives that

c_{0}\mathcal{E}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}^{\mu}(\zeta_{n}(u-k_{n})_{-},\zeta_{n}(u-k_{n})_{-})\geqslant|\widetilde{B}_{n}\cap\{u<k_{n}\}|^{-\frac{\alpha}{N}}\int_{\widetilde{B}_{n}\cap\{u<k_{n}\}}(u-k_{n})_{-}^{2}\,dx.

By the definitions of
k_{n},B_{n},\widetilde{B}_{n}
, obviously we have

B_{n+1}\cap\{u<k_{n+1}\}\subset\widetilde{B}_{n}\cap\{u<k_{n}\}\subset B_{n}\cap\{u<k_{n}\},

hence we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{B}_{n}\cap\{u<k_{n}\}}(u-k_{n})_{-}^{2}\,dx\geqslant

\displaystyle\,\int_{B_{n+1}\cap\{u<k_{n+1}\}}(u-k_{n})_{-}^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\,\int_{B_{n+1}\cap\{u<k_{n+1}\}}(k_{n}-k_{n+1})^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\Big(\frac{M}{2^{n+2}}\Big)^{2}|B_{n+1}\cap\{u<k_{n+1}\}|,

and

|\widetilde{B}_{n}\cap\{u<k_{n}\}|^{-\frac{\alpha}{N}}\geqslant|B_{n}\cap\{u<k_{n}\}|^{-\frac{\alpha}{N}}.

Combing the above inequalities, we get

|B_{n}\cap\{u<k_{n}\}|^{-\frac{\alpha}{N}}|B_{n+1}\cap\{u<k_{n+1}\}|\leqslant\gamma 2^{(2+\alpha)n}\rho^{-\alpha}|B_{n}\cap\{u<k_{n}\}|.

In terms of
Y_{n}:={|\{u<k_{n}\}\cap B_{n}|}/{|B_{n}|},
this estimate leads to

Y_{n+1}\leqslant\gamma 2^{(2+\alpha)n}Y_{n}^{1+\frac{\alpha}{N}}

for some
\gamma=\gamma({\it data})
.
By the fast geometric convergence, cf.
[
10
, Chapter 2 Lemma 5.1]
, if we require that

Y_{0}\leqslant\gamma^{-\frac{N}{\alpha}}2^{-\frac{N(2+\alpha)}{\alpha^{2}}}=:\nu\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{|\{u<M\}\cap B_{\rho}|}{|B_{\rho}|}\leqslant\nu,

then
Y_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, i.e.
|\{u<\frac{M}{2}\}\cap B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}|=0.

∎

To continue, we introduce the following
logarithmic estimate
, which plays the key role in the proof of the measure shrinking lemma. The idea of proof comes from
[
16
]
. This is a simplified version of
[
14
, Lemma 4.4]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mu
satisfy (
S
) and (
H2
). Let
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset E
. Let
u
be a local weak super-solution of (
1.1
) in
E
, such that
u\geqslant 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
. There exists
\gamma>1
depending only on the data
\{\mathfrak{C}_{2},N,\alpha\}
, such that for any
0<\rho<R/2
and any
h>0
, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\left|\log\left(\frac{u(x)+h}{u(y)+h}\right)\right|^{2}\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\gamma\rho^{N-\alpha}\bigg[h^{-1}\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)+1\bigg].

Proof.

For simplicity, we assume
x_{0}=0
. Let
h>0
be a parameter and
take
\zeta:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to be a function, such that
\text{supp}\,\zeta(\cdot)\subset B_{\frac{7}{4}\rho},~\|\zeta\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1,~\zeta(x)=1
in
B_{\rho}
and
|\nabla\zeta|\leqslant\gamma(N)\rho^{-1}
. According to assumption (
H2
) and Lemma
2.3
, we have

\sup_{x\in B_{2\rho}}\int_{B_{2\rho}}(\zeta(y)-\zeta(x))^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)\leqslant\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{2},N,\alpha)\rho^{-\alpha}.

(3.4)

We use in the weak formulation (
1.2
) of (
1.1
), the testing function
\varphi
defined by
\varphi=\frac{\zeta^{2}}{u+h}.

Note that since
u\geqslant 0
in the support of
\zeta
, the test function is well-defined. Consequently, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{B_{2\rho}}(u(x)-u(y))\bigg(\frac{\zeta^{2}(y)}{u(y)+h}-\frac{\zeta^{2}(x)}{u(x)+h}\bigg)\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\leqslant 2\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{2\rho}}(u(x)-u(y))\frac{\zeta^{2}(x)}{u(x)+h}\,\mu(x,dy)dx.

(3.5)

For convenience, we denote

\displaystyle I_{1}=\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{B_{2\rho}}(u(x)-u(y))\bigg(\frac{\zeta^{2}(y)}{u(y)+h}-\frac{\zeta^{2}(x)}{u(x)+h}\bigg)\,\mu(x,dy)dx,

\displaystyle I_{2}=\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{2\rho}}(u(x)-u(y))\frac{\zeta^{2}(x)}{u(x)+h}\,\mu(x,dy)dx.

Setting

E:=E(x,y)=\frac{u(x)+h}{u(y)+h}\qquad\text{and}\qquad F:=F(x,y)=\frac{\zeta(x)}{\zeta(y)},

we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{B_{2\rho}}\zeta(x)\zeta(y)\bigg(\frac{E}{F}+\frac{F}{E}-F-\frac{1}{F}\bigg)\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{B_{2\rho}}\zeta(x)\zeta(y)\Bigg[\bigg(\frac{E}{F}+\frac{F}{E}-2\bigg)-\bigg(\sqrt{F}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{F}}\bigg)^{2}\Bigg]\,\mu(x,dy)dx.

By Taylor’s theorem, it holds that

e^{a}+e^{-a}-2=2\sum_{1}^{\infty}\frac{a^{2k}}{(2k)!}\geqslant a^{2},\quad\forall\,a\in\mathbb{R}.

Let
b=e^{a}
, then

b+\frac{1}{b}-2\geqslant(\log b)^{2}\quad\forall\,b>0.

Therefore, setting
b=\frac{E}{F}
, we have

\frac{E}{F}+\frac{F}{E}-2\geqslant\left(\log E-\log F\right)^{2}.

Furthermore, it is easy to verify

\zeta(x)\zeta(y)\bigg(\sqrt{F}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{F}}\bigg)^{2}=(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y))^{2}.

Thus, noticing
\zeta=1
in
B_{\rho}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{1}\geqslant

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{B_{2\rho}}\zeta(x)\zeta(y)\left(\log E-\log F\right)^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{B_{2\rho}}(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y))^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{B_{2\rho}}\zeta(x)\zeta(y)\left(\log\frac{u(x)+h}{\zeta(x)}-\log\frac{u(y)+h}{\zeta(y)}\right)^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{B_{2\rho}}(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y))^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}\int_{B_{\rho}}\left|\log\left(\frac{u(x)+h}{u(y)+h}\right)\right|^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx-\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{B_{2\rho}}(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y))^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx.

(3.6)

For the right-hand side of (
3.5
), namely
I_{2}
, we can proceed as follows. First of all, notice that
u(y)\geqslant 0
when
y\in B_{R},
and hence

\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{u(x)+h}\leqslant 1\qquad\forall~x\in B_{2\rho},~y\in B_{R}.

Moreover, when
y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}
,

u(x)-u(y)\leqslant[u(x)+u_{-}(y)]\qquad\forall~x\in B_{2\rho}.

Therefore, by splitting the integral region and using the fact that
\frac{u(x)}{u(x)+h}<1
, we have

\displaystyle I_{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{B_{R}\backslash B_{2\rho}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{u(x)+h}\zeta^{2}(x)\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}}\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{u(x)+h}\zeta^{2}(x)\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{B_{R}\backslash B_{2\rho}}\zeta^{2}(x)\mu(x,dy)dx+\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}}\frac{u(x)\zeta^{2}(x)}{u(x)+h}\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}}\frac{u_{-}(y)\zeta^{2}(x)}{u(x)+h}\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{2\rho}}\zeta^{2}(x)\,\mu(x,dy)dx+h^{-1}\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}}u_{-}(y)\zeta^{2}(x)\,\mu(x,dy)dx.

(3.7)

By (
H2
), Lemma
2.2
and the fact that the support of
\zeta
is included in
B_{\frac{7}{4}\rho}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{2\rho}}\zeta^{2}(x)\,\mu(x,dy)dx\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma(N)\rho^{N}\underset{x\in B_{\frac{7}{4}\rho}}{\text{sup}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{2\rho}}\mu(x,dy)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{2},N)\rho^{N-\alpha}

(3.8)

and

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}}u_{-}(y)\zeta^{2}(x)\,\mu(x,dy)dx\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma(N)|B_{2\rho}|R^{-\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{4}\rho,R)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma(N)\rho^{N-\alpha}\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R).

(3.9)

By combining (
3.7
) with (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we obtain

I_{2}\leqslant\gamma\rho^{N-\alpha}\left[1+h^{-1}\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\right],

which together with (
3.6
) in (
3.5
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}\int_{B_{\rho}}\left|\log\left(\frac{u(x)+h}{u(y)+h}\right)\right|^{2}\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle~\quad\leqslant\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{B_{2\rho}}|\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)|^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx+\gamma\rho^{N-\alpha}\left[1+h^{-1}\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\right].

Finally, by (
3.4
), we get the following estimate

\int_{B_{2\rho}}\int_{B_{2\rho}}|\zeta(x)-\zeta(y)|^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx\leqslant\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{2},N,\alpha)\rho^{-\alpha}|B_{2\rho}|=\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{2},N,\alpha)\rho^{N-\alpha}

to conclude the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to deduce the measure shrinking lemma.

Lemma 3.2

(measure shrinking)
.

Let
\mu
satisfy (
S
), (
H1
) and (
H2
). Let
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset E,~\beta\in(0,1]
and
M>0
. Let
u
be a local weak super-solution of (
1.1
) in
E
, such that
u\geqslant 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
. There exists
\gamma>1
depending only on the data, such that for any
0<\rho<R
, if

|\{u\geqslant M\}\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})|\geqslant\beta|B_{\rho}(x_{0})|,

then for any
\sigma\in(0,1),
either

\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)>\sigma M

or

|\{u\leqslant\sigma M\}\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})|\leqslant\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\bigg[\log\bigg(\frac{1+\sigma}{2\sigma}\bigg)\bigg]^{-1}|B_{\rho}(x_{0})|.

Proof.

For simplicity, we assume
x_{0}=0.
As a restatement, we will show that there exists
\gamma
, such that if

\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\leqslant\sigma M

(3.10)

and if

|\{u\geqslant M\}\cap B_{\rho}|\geqslant\beta|B_{\rho}|,

(3.11)

then

|\{u\leqslant\sigma M\}\cap B_{\rho}|\leqslant\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\bigg[\log\bigg(\frac{1+\sigma}{2\sigma}\bigg)\bigg]^{-1}|B_{\rho}|.

(3.12)

Consider the function
v
defined as follows

v:=\log_{+}\left(\frac{M+h}{u+h}\right),

(3.13)

where
h>0
is to be chosen.
By the assumption (
H1
) of
\mu
we get

\int_{B_{\rho}}(v-[v]_{B_{\rho}})^{2}\,dx\leqslant\mathfrak{C}_{1}\rho^{\alpha}\int_{B_{\rho}}\int_{B_{\rho}}|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx.

By the definition of
v
, it follows that

\int_{B_{\rho}}\int_{B_{\rho}}|v(x)-v(y)|^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx\leqslant\int_{B_{\rho}}\int_{B_{\rho}}\left|\log\left(\frac{u(x)+h}{u(y)+h}\right)\right|^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx.

Combining the previous two estimates and applying Proposition
3.2
, we get

\fint_{B_{\rho}}|v-[v]_{B_{\rho}}|^{2}\,dx\leqslant\gamma\left(h^{-1}\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)+1\right)

for some
\gamma=\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{1},\mathfrak{C}_{2},N)
.
Therefore, choosing
h=\sigma M
and using (
3.10
), we get from the last line that

\fint_{B_{\rho}}|v-[v]_{B_{\rho}}|^{2}\,dx\leqslant\gamma,

(3.14)

where the constant
\gamma
depends only on
\mathfrak{C}_{1},\mathfrak{C}_{2}
and
N
.

Using the definition of
v
given in (
3.13
), we obtain

|\{v=0\}\cap B_{\rho}|\cdot[v]_{B_{\rho}}=\int_{\{v=0\}\cap B_{\rho}}|v-[v]_{B_{\rho}}|\,dx\leqslant\int_{B_{\rho}}|v-[v]_{B_{\rho}}|\,dx.

(3.15)

By (
3.11
), (
3.15
), Hölder’s inequality and (
3.14
) we have

\beta\fint_{B_{\rho}}v\,dx\leqslant\frac{|\{v=0\}\cap B_{\rho}|}{|B_{\rho}|}\fint_{B\rho}v\,dx\leqslant\fint_{B_{\rho}}|v-[v]_{B_{\rho}}|\,dx\leqslant\gamma\bigg(\fint_{B_{\rho}}|v-[v]_{B_{\rho}}|^{2}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leqslant\gamma.

The left-hand side of the last inequality has the lower bound as follows,

\beta\fint_{B_{\rho}}v\,dx\geqslant\frac{\beta}{|B_{\rho}|}\int_{\{u\leqslant\sigma M\}\cap B_{\rho}}\log_{+}\left(\frac{M+h}{u+h}\right)\,dx\geqslant\beta\frac{|\{u\leqslant\sigma M\}\cap B_{\rho}|}{|B_{\rho}|}\log\left(\frac{1+\sigma}{2\sigma}\right).

Combining the last two inequalities yields (
3.12
). We have completed the proof.
∎

Combining the critical mass lemma and the measure shrinking lemma yields the expansion of positivity lemma as follows.

Proposition 3.3

(expansion of positivity)
.

Let
\mu
satisfy (
S
), (
H1
) and (
H2
). Let
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset E
and
0<\rho<R/4
. Let
u
be a local weak super-solution of (
1.1
), such that
u\geqslant 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
. Suppose for some constants
\beta\in(0,1]
and
M>0,
there holds

|\{u\geqslant M\}\cap B_{\rho}(x_{0})|\geqslant\beta|B_{\rho}(x_{0})|.

Then there exists a constant
\sigma\in(0,1)
depending only on the data and
\beta
, such that either

\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)>\sigma M

or

u\geqslant\sigma M\quad\text{a.e. in}\quad B_{2\rho}(x_{0}).

Proof.

For simplicity, we assume
x_{0}=0.
Since

|\{u\geqslant M\}\cap B_{4\rho}|\geqslant|\{u\geqslant M\}\cap B_{\rho}|\geqslant\beta|B_{\rho}|=4^{-N}\beta|B_{4\rho}|,

using Lemma
3.2
, if

4^{\alpha}\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\leqslant\sigma M,

(3.16)

we have

|\{u\leqslant\sigma M\}\cap B_{4\rho}|\leqslant 4^{N}\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\bigg[\log\bigg(\frac{1+\sigma}{2\sigma}\bigg)\bigg]^{-1}|B_{4\rho}|,

where
\gamma=\gamma({\it data})>1
is the constant from Lemma
3.2
. Take
\sigma
small enough to satisfy

4^{N}\frac{\gamma}{\beta}\left[\log\left(\frac{1+\sigma}{2\sigma}\right)\right]^{-1}\leqslant\nu,

where
\nu=\nu({\it data})\in(0,1)
is the constant from Lemma
3.1
. Using Lemma
3.1
, we have if (
3.16
) holds, then

u\geqslant\frac{\sigma M}{2}\quad\text{a.e. in}~B_{2\rho}.

Redefining
\min\{\frac{\sigma}{4^{\alpha}},\frac{\sigma}{2}\}
as
\sigma
finishes the proof.
∎

4
Local Hölder continuity

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Without loss of generality, we take
x_{0}=0
and set

\mu^{+}=\underset{B_{R}}{\text{sup}}~u,\quad\mu^{-}=\underset{B_{R}}{\text{inf}}~u.

Obviously,
\underset{B_{R}}{\text{osc}}~u=\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}\leqslant\omega
, where

\omega=2~\underset{B_{R}}{\rm{sup}}~|u|+{\rm Tail}(|u|;\tfrac{7}{8}R,R).

From now on, let
\sigma=\sigma({\it data})\in(0,1)
be determined in Proposition
3.3
with
\beta=\frac{1}{2}.
For some
c\in(0,\frac{1}{4})
to be chosen, consider two alternatives

\begin{cases}|\{u(\cdot)-\mu^{-}\geqslant\frac{1}{4}\omega\}\cap B_{cR}|\geqslant\frac{1}{2}|B_{cR}|,\\
|\{\mu^{+}-u(\cdot)\geqslant\frac{1}{4}\omega\}\cap B_{cR}|\geqslant\frac{1}{2}|B_{cR}|.\end{cases}

Assuming
\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\omega,
one of the two alternatives must hold. Let us suppose the first alternative holds for instance.
Proposition
3.3
is applied to
u-\mu^{-}
instead of
u
with
\beta=\frac{1}{2},~M=\frac{1}{4}\omega,~\rho=cR
. Then we obtain that either

c^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}((u-\mu^{-})_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)>\frac{1}{4}\sigma\omega

(4.1)

or

u-\mu^{-}\geqslant\frac{\sigma}{4}\omega\quad\text{a.e. in}~B_{cR},

which gives the reduction of oscillation

\underset{B_{cR}}{\text{osc}}~u\leqslant\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}-\frac{1}{4}\sigma\omega\leqslant\Big(1-\frac{1}{4}\sigma\Big)\omega=:\omega_{1}.

(4.2)

The number
c
is chosen to ensure that (
4.1
) does not happen. Indeed, we may first estimate by using (
H2
), Lemma
2.2
and the definition of
\omega
,

{\rm Tail}((u-\mu^{-})_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\leqslant\mathfrak{C}_{2}|\mu^{-}|R^{\alpha}(R/8)^{-\alpha}+{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\leqslant\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{2})\omega.

Then, we choose

c^{\alpha}\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{2})\omega\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\sigma\omega\quad\text{i.e.}\quad c\leqslant\Big(\frac{\sigma}{4\gamma}\Big)^{1/\alpha},

such that (
4.1
) does not occur. If
\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}<\omega/2,
there holds

\underset{B_{cR}}{\text{osc}}~u<\underset{B_{R}}{\text{osc}}~u<\frac{\omega}{2}<\Big(1-\frac{1}{4}\sigma\Big)\omega

because of
c\in(0,1/4)
and
\sigma\in(0,1).
That means (
4.2
) still holds when
\mu^{+}-\mu^{-}<\frac{1}{2}\omega.

Now, we may proceed by induction. Suppose up to
i=1,\dots,j
, we have built

\begin{cases}r_{0}=R,~~~r_{i}=cr_{i-1},~~~B_{i}=B_{r_{i}},\\
\omega_{0}=\omega,~~\omega_{i}=(1-\frac{\sigma}{8})~\omega_{i-1},\\
\mu_{i}^{+}=\underset{B_{i}}{\text{sup}}~u,~\mu_{i}^{-}=\underset{B_{i}}{\text{inf}}~u,~\underset{B_{i}}{\text{osc}}~u\leqslant\omega_{i}.\end{cases}

The induction argument will show that the above oscillation estimate continues to hold for the
(j+1)
-th step. Let
\sigma
be fixed as before, whereas
c\in(0,1/4)
is subject to a further choice. We consider two alternatives

\begin{cases}|\{u(\cdot)-\mu_{j}^{-}\geqslant\frac{1}{4}\omega_{j}\}\cap B_{cr_{j}}|\geqslant\frac{1}{2}|B_{cr_{j}}|,\\
|\{\mu_{j}^{+}-u(\cdot)\geqslant\frac{1}{4}\omega_{j}\}\cap B_{cr_{j}}|\geqslant\frac{1}{2}|B_{cr_{j}}|.\end{cases}

Like in the first step, we may assume
\mu_{j}^{+}-\mu_{j}^{-}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\omega_{j},
so that one of the two alternatives must hold. Otherwise, the case
\mu_{j}^{+}-\mu_{j}^{-}<\frac{1}{2}\omega_{j}
can be trivially incorporated into the forthcoming oscillation estimate (
4.4
).

Let us suppose the first case holds for instance. Proposition
3.3
is applied in
B_{j}
to
u-\mu_{j}^{-}
intead of
u
with
\beta=\frac{1}{2},~M=\frac{1}{4}\omega_{j},~\rho=cr_{j}
. Then we obtain that either

c^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}((u-\mu_{j}^{-})_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}r_{j},r_{j})>\frac{1}{4}\sigma\omega_{j}

(4.3)

or

u-\mu_{j}^{-}\geqslant\frac{1}{4}\sigma\omega_{j}\quad\text{a.e. in}~B_{cr_{j}},

which, thanks to the
j
-th induction assumption, gives the reduction of oscillation

\underset{B_{cr_{j}}}{\text{osc}}u\leqslant\mu_{j}^{+}-\mu_{j}^{-}-\frac{1}{4}\sigma\omega_{j}\leqslant\Big(1-\frac{1}{4}\sigma\Big)\omega_{j}=:\omega_{j+1}.

(4.4)

The final choice of
c
is made to ensure that (
4.3
) does not happen, independent of
j
. In order for that, we first rewrite the tail as follows:

\displaystyle{\rm Tail}((u-\mu_{j}^{-})_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}r_{j},r_{j})

\displaystyle~\quad=r_{j}^{\alpha}\underset{x\in B_{\frac{7}{8}r_{j}}}{\text{sup}}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}}(u(y)-\mu_{j}^{-})_{-}\,\mu(x,dy)+\sum_{i=1}^{j}\int_{B_{i-1}\backslash B_{i}}(u(y)-\mu_{j}^{-})_{-}\,\mu(x,dy)\bigg].

The first integral is estimated by using (
H2
), Lemma
2.2
and the definition of
\omega
. Indeed, noticing that
r_{j}\leqslant R
, we have

\displaystyle\underset{x\in B_{\frac{7}{8}r_{j}}}{\text{sup}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}}(u(y)-\mu_{j}^{-})_{-}\,\mu(x,dy)\leqslant\frac{8^{\alpha}\mathfrak{C}_{2}}{R^{\alpha}}|\mu_{j}^{-}|+\frac{1}{R^{\alpha}}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\leqslant\gamma\frac{\omega}{R^{\alpha}},

where
\gamma
depends only on
\mathfrak{C}_{2}
.
Whereas the second integral is estimated by using the simple fact that, for
i=1,\dots,j,

(u-\mu_{j}^{-})_{-}\leqslant\mu_{j}^{-}-\mu_{i-1}^{-}\leqslant\mu_{j}^{+}-\mu_{i-1}^{-}\leqslant\mu_{i-1}^{+}-\mu_{i-1}^{-}\leqslant\omega_{i-1}~\text{a.e. in}~B_{i-1}.

Using also the assumption (
H2
) of
\mu
and Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\underset{x\in B_{\frac{7}{8}r_{j}}}{\text{sup}}\int_{B_{i-1}\backslash B_{i}}(u(y)-\mu_{j}^{-})_{-}\,\mu(x,dy)\leqslant

\displaystyle~\underset{x\in B_{\frac{7}{8}r_{i}}}{\text{sup}}\int_{B_{i-1}\backslash B_{i}}\omega_{i-1}\,\mu(x,dy)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\mathfrak{C}_{2}\omega_{i-1}\left(\frac{r_{i}}{8}\right)^{-\alpha}\leqslant\gamma\frac{\omega_{i-1}}{r_{i}^{\alpha}},

where
\gamma
depends only on
\mathfrak{C}_{2}
. Combine them and further estimate the tail by

\displaystyle{\rm Tail}((u-\mu_{j}^{-})_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}r_{j},r_{j})\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma\bigg(r_{j}^{\alpha}\frac{\omega}{R^{\alpha}}+r_{j}^{\alpha}\sum_{i=1}^{j}\frac{\omega_{i-1}}{r_{i}^{\alpha}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma\omega_{j}\bigg[\Big(1-\frac{1}{4}\sigma\Big)^{-j}c^{j\alpha}+\sum_{i=1}^{j}\Big(1-\frac{1}{4}\sigma\Big)^{i-j-1}c^{(j-i)\alpha}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma\omega_{j}\bigg[\sum_{i=0}^{j}\Big(1-\frac{1}{4}\sigma\Big)^{i-j-1}c^{(j-i)\alpha}\bigg].

The summation in the last display is bounded by
2(1-\frac{1}{4}\sigma)^{-1}
if we restrict the choice of
c
by

c^{\alpha}\Big(1-\frac{1}{4}\sigma\Big)^{-1}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{i.e.}\quad c\leqslant 2^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{4}\sigma\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}.

Consequently, if we choose
c
to verify

c^{\alpha}\gamma\Big(1-\frac{1}{4}\sigma\Big)^{-1}\leqslant\frac{\sigma}{4}\quad\text{i.e.}\quad c\leqslant\Big[\frac{\sigma}{4\gamma}\Big(1-\frac{1}{4}\sigma\Big)\Big]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},

where
\gamma
is from the last tail estimate, then (
4.3
) does not happen. The final choice of
c
is as follows

c:=\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{8},\Big[\frac{\sigma}{4\gamma}\Big(1-\frac{1}{4}\sigma\Big)\Big]^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},2^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{4}\sigma\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\bigg\}<\frac{1}{4}.

Letting
r_{j+1}=cr_{j}
, we obtain from (
4.4
) that

\underset{B_{j+1}}{\text{osc}}~u\leqslant\omega_{j+1},

which completes the induction argument. Now, we have

\underset{B_{c^{i}R}}{\text{osc}}~u\leqslant\Big(1-\frac{1}{4}\sigma\Big)^{i}\omega,~~i=0,1,2,\dots

Let
\rho\in(0,R)
. There must be some
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

c^{i+1}R\leqslant\rho<c^{i}R.

As a result, we have

\underset{B_{\rho}}{\text{osc}}~u\leqslant\underset{B_{c^{i}R}}{\text{osc}}~u\leqslant\Big(1-\frac{1}{4}\sigma\Big)^{i}\omega

and

i+1\leqslant\frac{\ln(\rho/R)}{\ln c},

which means

\underset{B_{\rho}}{\text{osc}}~u\leqslant\frac{4}{4-\sigma}\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\frac{\ln(1-\sigma/4)}{\ln c}}\omega.

Setting
\alpha_{*}=\frac{\ln(1-\sigma/4)}{\ln c}
, we complete the proof.

).

Consequently, we deduce

\sup_{B_{\frac{r}{2}}}u\leqslant k\leqslant\delta\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r/2}}\frac{u_{+}(y)}{|y|^{N+\alpha}}\,dy+\delta^{-\frac{N}{2\alpha}}H\left(\fint_{B_{r}}u_{+}^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

6.2
Local boundedness for local weak solutions

In this subsection, we aim to introduce another version of the locally bounded theorem for local weak solutions, see Theorem
6.1
, which is an important prior estimate for the establishment of Harnack inequality. To prove that, we first introduce a useful lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\mu
satisfy (
H2
). Let
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset E
. Let
u
be a local weak super-solution of (
1.1
) in
E
, such that
u\geqslant 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
. Then, for
0<\rho<\frac{7}{8}R,~\ell\in(\frac{1}{4},1)
, we have

\int_{B_{l\rho}(x_{0})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u_{+}(y)\,\mu(x,dy)dx\leqslant\gamma\rho^{N-\alpha}\bigg(\sup_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u+\bigg(\frac{\rho}{R}\bigg)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\bigg),

where the constant
\gamma
depends only on
\mathfrak{C}_{2},\alpha
and
N
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we assume
x_{0}=0.
Set
k=\underset{B_{\rho}}{\sup}~u
and
w=u-2k
. Take
\zeta:\mathbb{R}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
to be a function such that
\text{supp}\,\zeta(\cdot)\subset B_{\frac{1+\ell}{2}\rho},~\|\zeta\|_{\infty}\leqslant 1,~\zeta(x)=1~\text{in}~B_{\ell\rho}
and
|\nabla\zeta|\leqslant\frac{\gamma(N)}{(1-\ell)\rho}
. According to assumption (
H2
) and Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}(\zeta(y)-\zeta(x))^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)\leqslant 2^{\alpha}\mathfrak{C}_{2}\left[(1-\ell)\rho\right]^{-\alpha}.

(6.9)

By Proposition
3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}w_{-}(x)\zeta^{2}(x)\,dx\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w_{+}(y)\,\mu(x,dy)\bigg)

\displaystyle~\qquad\leqslant\gamma\int_{B_{R}}\int_{B_{R}}\max\{w_{-}^{2}(x),w_{-}^{2}(y)\}(\zeta(x)-\zeta(y))^{2}\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle~\quad\qquad+\gamma\int_{B_{R}}w_{-}(x)\zeta^{2}(x)\,dx\bigg(\underset{x\in\text{supp}\,\zeta}{\text{sup}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{R}}u_{-}(y)\,\mu(x,dy)\bigg)

\displaystyle~\qquad=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

(6.10)

Noticing
w_{-}\leqslant 2k
, together with (
6.9
), we can deduce

I_{1}\leqslant 2^{\alpha+2}\gamma\mathfrak{C}_{2}k^{2}\rho^{N}[(1-\ell)\rho]^{-\alpha},

(6.11)

and

I_{2}\leqslant 2\gamma k\rho^{N}R^{-\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R).

(6.12)

Next, we treat the left-hand side of (
6.10
). We know that
w_{+}=0
in
B_{\rho}
from the definition of
k
, thus,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w_{+}(y)\,\mu(x,dy)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\rho}}w_{+}(y)\,\mu(x,dy).

Recalling the definition of
w
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}w_{-}(x)\zeta^{2}(x)\,dx\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w_{+}(y)\,\mu(x,dy)\bigg)\geqslant

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\ell\rho}}w_{-}(x)\zeta^{2}(x)\,dx\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\rho}}w_{+}(y)\,\mu(x,dy)\bigg)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle~k\int_{B_{\ell\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\rho}}(u_{+}(y)-2k)\,\mu(x,dy)dx.

(6.13)

Combining (
6.10
), (
6.11
), (
6.12
) and (
6.13
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\ell\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\rho}}u_{+}(y)\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle~\quad\leqslant 2k\int_{B_{\ell\rho}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{\rho}}\mu(x,dy)dx+\gamma\Big[k\rho^{N}[(1-\ell)\rho]^{-\alpha}+\rho^{N}R^{-\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};7R/8,R)\Big]

\displaystyle~\quad\leqslant\gamma k[(1-\ell)\rho]^{-\alpha}|B_{\ell\rho}|+\gamma\Big[k\rho^{N}[(1-\ell)\rho]^{-\alpha}+\rho^{N}R^{-\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\Big]

\displaystyle~\quad\leqslant\gamma\rho^{N-\alpha}\left[k+\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\right]

for some
\gamma=\gamma(\mathfrak{C}_{2},N,\alpha)
, where we used the assumption (
H2
), Lemma
2.2
and
\ell\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. This is the desired result, recalling the definition of
k
.
∎

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\mu
be represented by (
1.3
) and satisfy (
S
), (
H1
), (
H2
) and (
UJS
). Let
u
be a local weak solution of (
1.1
) in
E
, such that
u\geqslant 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
. Then, for any
0<r<R
, there holds

\sup_{B_{r/2}(x_{0})}u\leqslant\delta\bigg[\sup_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u+\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\bigg]+\gamma\delta^{-\frac{N}{2\alpha}}\bigg(\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}u_{+}^{2}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},

for any
\delta\in(0,1]
, provided the ball
B_{R}(x_{0})
is included in
E
, where the constant
\gamma>1
depends only on data and
\hat{c}
.

Proof.

We closely follow the arguments in the proof of Theorem
1.4
. By using Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
6.1
, we revise the estimation (
6.4
) as follows

\displaystyle\int_{B_{n}}\widetilde{w}_{n}(x)\zeta_{n}^{2}(x)\,dx\bigg(\underset{x\in\text{supp}\,\zeta_{n}}{\text{sup}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{n}}\widetilde{w}_{n}(y)\,\mu(x,dy)\bigg)

\displaystyle~\qquad\leqslant\int_{B_{n}}\widetilde{w}_{n}(x)\zeta_{n}^{2}(x)\,dx\bigg(\underset{x\in\widetilde{B}_{n}}{\text{sup}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{n}}w_{0}(y)\,\mu(x,dy)\bigg)

\displaystyle~\qquad\leqslant\gamma\int_{B_{n}}\widetilde{w}_{n}(x)\zeta_{n}^{2}(x)\,dx\left(\frac{r}{2^{n+3}}\right)^{-N}\int_{\widehat{B}_{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{n}}w_{0}(y)\,\mu(x,dy)dx

\displaystyle~\qquad\leqslant\gamma r_{n}^{N-\alpha}\int_{B_{n}}\widetilde{w}_{n}(x)\zeta_{n}^{2}(x)\,dx\left(\frac{r}{2^{n+3}}\right)^{-N}\bigg[\sup_{B_{n}}u+\left(\frac{r_{n}}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\bigg]

\displaystyle~\qquad\leqslant\gamma r_{n}^{2N-\alpha}\left(\frac{r}{2^{n+3}}\right)^{-N}\fint_{B_{n}}\frac{w_{n}^{2}(x)}{\widetilde{k}_{n}-k_{n}}\,dx\bigg[\sup_{B_{r}}u+\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\bigg]

\displaystyle~\qquad\leqslant\gamma 2^{(n+2)N}r_{n}^{N-\alpha}k^{-1}\fint_{B_{n}}w_{n}^{2}(x)\,dx\bigg[\sup_{B_{r}}u+\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\bigg],

where
\gamma
depend on
\mathfrak{C}_{2},N,\alpha
and
\hat{c}
. The difference from (
6.4
) is that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B_{r/2}}\frac{u_{+}(y)}{|y|^{N+\alpha}}\,dy

is replaced by

\sup_{B_{r}}u+\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R).

The rest of the proof is the same as the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
∎

7
Full Harnack inequality

Based on the locally boundedness estimates in Section 6 and the weak Harnack inequality from Section
5
, a full Harnack inequality can be established through a standard iterative process.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The estimate in Theorem
6.1
yields

\sup_{B_{\frac{\rho}{2}}(x_{0})}u\leqslant\gamma\frac{1}{\delta^{\frac{N}{2\alpha}}}\bigg(\fint_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u_{+}^{2}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\delta\sup_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u+\delta 2^{-\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};x_{0},\tfrac{7}{4}\rho,2\rho),

(7.1)

for any
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset E
and
0<\rho<\frac{1}{2}R
. To proceed, we first need to perform a covering argument. Let
\frac{1}{2}\leqslant\sigma^{\prime}<\sigma\leqslant 1
. Note that

B_{\sigma^{\prime}r}\subseteq\underset{z\in B_{\sigma^{\prime}r}}{\bigcup}B_{\frac{\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}}{2}r}(z)~~\text{and}~~B_{(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})r}(z)\subset B_{\sigma r}~~\text{for any}~~z\in B_{\sigma^{\prime}r}.

Otherwise, since
r<\frac{R}{32}
, we have

B_{\frac{7}{4}r(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})}(z)\subset B_{\frac{7}{8}R}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{2r(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})}(z)\subset B_{R},~~\text{for any}~~z\in B_{\sigma^{\prime}r}.

Due to
u\geqslant 0
in
B_{R}(x_{0})
, we also have

2^{-\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};z,\tfrac{7}{4}(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})r,2(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})r)\leqslant\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R).

Therefore, by using (
7.1
) with
\rho=(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})r
and
x_{0}=z
, we get

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{\sigma^{\prime}r}}u\leqslant

\displaystyle\sup_{z\in B_{\sigma^{\prime}r}}\Bigg(\sup_{B_{\frac{\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}}{2}r}(z)}u\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma\delta^{-\frac{N}{2\alpha}}\bigg(\fint_{B_{(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})r}(z)}u_{+}^{2}\,dx\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\delta\sup_{B_{(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})r}(z)}u+\delta 2^{-\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};z,\tfrac{7}{4}(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})r,2(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})r)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma\delta^{-\frac{N}{2\alpha}}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma-\sigma^{\prime}}\Big)^{\frac{N}{2}}\left(\fint_{B_{\sigma r}}u_{+}^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\delta\sup_{B_{\sigma r}}u+\delta 2^{-\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};z,\tfrac{7}{4}(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})r,2(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})r)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle~\gamma\frac{\delta^{-\frac{N}{2\alpha}}}{(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})^{\frac{N}{2}}}(\sup_{B_{\sigma r}}u)^{\frac{2-\varepsilon}{2}}\left(\fint_{B_{\sigma r}}u_{+}^{\varepsilon}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\delta\sup_{B_{\sigma r}}u+\delta\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R),

where
\varepsilon=\varepsilon({\it data})\in(0,1)
is the constant from Theorem
1.2
.
By choosing
\delta=\frac{1}{4}
, a standard application of Young’s inequality yields

\sup_{B_{\sigma^{\prime}r}}u\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\sup_{B_{\sigma r}}u+\frac{\gamma}{(\sigma-\sigma^{\prime})^{\frac{N}{\varepsilon}}}\left(\fint_{B_{r}}u_{+}^{\varepsilon}\,dx\right)^{\varepsilon}+\gamma\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R).

(7.2)

Next, consider the sequence
\{t_{i}\}
defined by

t_{0}=\frac{1}{2},\quad t_{i+1}-t_{i}=\frac{1}{2}(1-\tau)\tau^{i}

with
0<\tau<1
to be fixed. By (
7.2
) we get

\sup_{B_{t_{i}r}}u\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\sup_{B_{t_{i+1}r}}u+\frac{\gamma}{[(1-\tau)\tau^{i}]^{\frac{N}{\varepsilon}}}\left(\fint_{B_{r}}u_{+}^{\varepsilon}\,dx\right)^{\varepsilon}+\gamma\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R),

i=0,1,\cdots
By iteration, we obtain

\sup_{B_{t_{0}r}}u\leqslant\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{k}\sup_{B_{t_{k}r}}u+\bigg[\frac{2^{\frac{N}{\varepsilon}}\gamma}{(1-\tau)^{\frac{N}{\varepsilon}}}\left(\fint_{B_{r}}u_{+}^{\varepsilon}\,dx\right)^{\varepsilon}+\gamma\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\bigg]\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}2^{-i}\tau^{-\frac{N}{\varepsilon}i}.

We choose now
\tau
such that
\frac{1}{2}\tau^{-\frac{N}{\varepsilon}}<1
and let
k\to\infty
, to get

\sup_{B_{r/2}}u\leqslant\gamma\left(\fint_{B_{r}}u_{+}^{\varepsilon}\,dx\right)^{\varepsilon}+\gamma\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R),

where the constant
\gamma
depends also on
\varepsilon
. Combining the above estimate with the weak Harnack inequality established in Theorem
1.2
, we get

\sup_{B_{r/2}}u\leqslant\gamma\inf_{B_{2r}}u+\gamma\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R)\leqslant\gamma\inf_{B_{r/2}}u+\gamma\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{\alpha}{\rm Tail}(u_{-};\tfrac{7}{8}R,R).

Redefine
r/2
as
r
to conclude.
∎

Topological magnon-polaron transport in a bilayer van der Waals magnet
Zhi-Xing Lin, Shu Zhang

Abstract
The stacking of intrinsically magnetic van der Waals materials provides a fertile platform to explore tunable transport effects of magnons, presenting significant prospects for spintronic applications. The possibility of having topologically nontrivial magnons in these systems can further expand the scope of exploration. In this work, we consider a bilayer system with intralayer ferromagnetic exchange and a weak interlayer antiferromagnetic exchange, and study the topological magnon-polaron excitations induced by magnetoelastic couplings. Under an applied magnetic field, the system features a metamagnetic transition, where the magnetic ground state changes from antiparallel layers to parallel. We show that the metamagnetic transition is accompanied by a transition of the topological structure of the magnon polarons, which results in discernible changes in the topology induced transport effects. The magnetic-field dependence of the thermal Hall conductivity and spin Nernst coefficient is analyzed with linear response theories.

To reveal the topological behavior of magnon polarons and the associated transport properties of such a system, we theoretically study a bilayer model, where two ferromagnetic triangular-lattice layers are coupled with a weak antiferromagnetic interaction [Fig.

We find that the metamagnetic phase transition triggers a topological transition of the magnon-polaron bands. This leads to a sign flip in thermal Hall conductance, but not in the spin Nernst coefficient, which instead exhibits a smooth sign change in the antiferromagnetic phase. Our discussions can be generally applicable in vdW magnets with similar magnetic structures and magnon-phonon couplings.

In the rest of this work, we focus on the consequent topological transport of heat and spin by performing linear-response calculations in the BdG form.
The nontrivial topology of the hybridized magnon-phonon excitations gives rise to thermal Hall and spin Nernst effects, which refer to a transverse heat and spin current, respectively, driven by a longitudinal temperature gradient.
The thermal Hall conductance is given by

This is not surprising given the transition is first-order. There is a sudden shift of the energies and thus thermal occupation of the magnon-polaron bands, in addition to the abrupt change of the topological structure.

In the antiparallel phase, as the energy of the spin-up band is lowered by the increasing field, it contributes more to the thermally driven transport properties dictated by the bosonic occupation. As the field increases above the critical field of the metamagnetic transition, only spin-down magnons are present, which split due to the the interlayer interaction instead. Across the transition, the dominating topological feature in thermal effects therefore switches from the

Such effects can thus be significant in systems with strong anisotropic spin interactions. In our case, the magnetoelastic coupling

Our work motivates further study of magnon-polaron transport in vdW magnets.

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbf{k}}

writes,

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbf{k}}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{H}_{\text{m}}&\mathcal{H_{\text{me}}}\\
\mathcal{H_{\text{me}}^{\dagger}}&\mathcal{H}_{\text{e}}\end{pmatrix}.

(S1)

To derive the magnon Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{m}
, we begin with the magnetic sector
H_{\text{m}}
in the real space (Eq.
1
),

H_{\text{m}}\!=\!-J\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\langle\text{intra}\;ij\rangle}\!\!\!\!\!\mathbf{S}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{j}+J^{\prime}\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\langle\text{inter}\;ij\rangle}\!\!\!\!\!\mathbf{S}_{i}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{j}-\frac{K}{2}\!\sum_{i}\!\left(S_{i}^{z}\right)^{2}-h\!\sum_{i}\!S_{i}^{z}.

(S2)

where
h\!=\!\mu_{B}g_{\parallel}B
denotes the Zeeman energy with the Bohr magneton
\mu_{B}
and the Landé g-factor
g_{\parallel}
along the easy axis. By taking Holstein-Primakoff, we can obtain the magnon Hamiltonian in the momentum space. For the antiparallel bilayer, we have,

\mathcal{H}_{\text{m}}^{\text{AP}}=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{+}^{m}&0&0&SJ^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}\\
0&\epsilon_{-}^{m}&SJ^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}^{\ast}&0\\
0&SJ^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}&\epsilon_{+}^{m}&0\\
SJ^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}^{\ast}&0&0&\epsilon_{-}^{m}\end{pmatrix},

(S3)

where
\epsilon_{\pm}^{m}=S(JC_{\mathbf{k}}+3J^{\prime}+K)\pm h,C_{\mathbf{k}}=6-2\cos k_{x}a-4\cos(k_{x}a/2)\cos(\sqrt{3}k_{y}a/2),\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}=\exp(-i\sqrt{3}k_{y}a/3)+2\cos(k_{x}a/2)\exp(i\sqrt{3}k_{y}a/6)
, and the bosonic operator
a_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}

(b_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger})
creates a magnon excitation carrying spin down (up)
s^{z}=-1(+1)
in layer A (B). And for the parallel bilayer, the magnon Hamiltonian takes the form of,

\mathcal{H}_{\text{m}}^{\text{P}}=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{+}^{m}&SJ^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}&0&0\\
SJ^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}^{\ast}&\epsilon_{+}^{m}&0&0\\
0&0&\epsilon_{+}^{m}&SJ^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}\\
0&0&SJ^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}^{\ast}&\epsilon_{+}^{m}\end{pmatrix}.

(S4)

where both
a_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}
and
b_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}
create a spin-down magnon with
s^{z}=-1
.

In the real space, the elastic part is written as,

H_{\text{e}}\!=\!\frac{1}{2M}\!\sum_{i}\left(p^{z}_{i}\right)^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\!\!\!\!\sum_{\;\langle\text{intra}\;ij\rangle}\!\!\!\!\!\left(u^{z}_{i}-u^{z}_{j}\right)^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{2}\!\!\!\!\sum_{\,\langle\text{inter}\;ij\rangle}\!\!\!\!\!\left(u^{z}_{i}-u^{z}_{j}\right)^{2},

(S5)

where
u_{i}^{z}
denotes the out-of-plane displacement on site
i
. Fourier transform yields

H_{\text{e}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\mathbf{k}}(u_{A,\mathbf{k}},u_{B,\mathbf{k}},p_{A,\mathbf{k}},p_{B,\mathbf{k}})\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{e}}(u_{A,\mathbf{k}},u_{B,\mathbf{k}},p_{A,\mathbf{k}},p_{B,\mathbf{k}})^{T},

(S6)

with

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{e}}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda C_{\mathbf{k}}+3\lambda^{\prime}&-\lambda^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}&0&0\\
-\lambda^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}^{\ast}&\lambda C_{\mathbf{k}}+3\lambda^{\prime}&0&0\\
0&0&1/M&0\\
0&0&0&1/M\end{pmatrix}.

(S7)

To obtain the BdG Hamiltonian with bosonic commutation relation, we
introduce the following transform,

u_{i,\mathbf{k}}=\sqrt{\hbar/2}(\lambda M)^{-1/4}(c_{i,\mathbf{k}}+c_{i,-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger})\quad p_{i,\mathbf{k}}=-i\sqrt{\hbar/2}(\lambda M)^{1/4}(c_{i,-\mathbf{k}}-c_{i,\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger})\quad(i=A,B).

(S8)

Expressing the elastic sector in the bosonic basis yields,

\mathcal{H}_{\text{e}}=\frac{\hbar}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\lambda}{M}}\begin{pmatrix}1+C_{\mathbf{k}}+3\lambda^{\prime}/\lambda&-\lambda^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}/\lambda&-1+C_{\mathbf{k}}+3\lambda^{\prime}/\lambda&-\lambda^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}/\lambda\\
-\lambda^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}^{\ast}/\lambda&1+C_{\mathbf{k}}+3\lambda^{\prime}/\lambda&-\lambda^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}^{\ast}/\lambda&-1+C_{\mathbf{k}}+3\lambda^{\prime}/\lambda\\
-1+C_{\mathbf{k}}+3\lambda^{\prime}/\lambda&-\lambda^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}/\lambda&1+C_{\mathbf{k}}+3\lambda^{\prime}/\lambda&-\lambda^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}/\lambda\\
-\lambda^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}^{\ast}/\lambda&-1+C_{\mathbf{k}}+3\lambda^{\prime}/\lambda&-\lambda^{\prime}\gamma_{\mathbf{k}}^{\ast}/\lambda&1+C_{\mathbf{k}}+3\lambda^{\prime}/\lambda\end{pmatrix}.

(S9)

Then we consider the symmetry-allowed magnetoelastic couplings
Kittel 1958
;
Thingstad et al. 2019
;
Go et al. 2019
;
Zhang et al. 2020
on the triangular lattice,

H_{\text{me}}=\kappa\sum_{i}\sum_{\delta}S^{z}_{i}(\mathbf{S}_{i}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{\delta})(u_{i}^{z}-u_{i+\delta}^{z}),

(S10)

where
\kappa
is the magnetoelastic coupling constant,
\delta
iterates over the intralayer nearest neighbors of site
i
, and
\hat{\mathbf{e}}_{\delta}
is the unit vector pointing along the bond. This term originates from spin-orbit coupling and breaks the conservation of
s^{z}
.

This magnetoelastic energy can also be expressed in
\tilde{\phi}_{\mathbf{k}}
basis,

\mathcal{H}_{\text{me}}^{\text{AP}}=\kappa\sqrt{S^{3}\hbar}(\lambda M)^{-1/4}\begin{pmatrix}-\nu_{1\mathbf{k}}+\nu_{2\mathbf{k}}&0&-\nu_{1\mathbf{k}}+\nu_{2\mathbf{k}}&0\\
0&\nu_{1\mathbf{k}}+\nu_{2\mathbf{k}}&0&\nu_{1\mathbf{k}}+\nu_{2\mathbf{k}}\\
-\nu_{1\mathbf{k}}-\nu_{2\mathbf{k}}&0&-\nu_{1\mathbf{k}}-\nu_{2\mathbf{k}}&0\\
0&\nu_{1\mathbf{k}}-\nu_{2\mathbf{k}}&0&\nu_{1\mathbf{k}}-\nu_{2\mathbf{k}}\end{pmatrix},

(S11)

and,

\mathcal{H}_{\text{me}}^{\text{P}}=\kappa\sqrt{S^{3}\hbar}(\lambda M)^{-1/4}\begin{pmatrix}-\nu_{1\mathbf{k}}+\nu_{2\mathbf{k}}&0&-\nu_{1\mathbf{k}}+\nu_{2\mathbf{k}}&0\\
0&-\nu_{1\mathbf{k}}+\nu_{2\mathbf{k}}&0&-\nu_{1\mathbf{k}}+\nu_{2\mathbf{k}}\\
-\nu_{1\mathbf{k}}-\nu_{2\mathbf{k}}&0&-\nu_{1\mathbf{k}}-\nu_{2\mathbf{k}}&0\\
0&-\nu_{1\mathbf{k}}-\nu_{2\mathbf{k}}&0&-\nu_{1\mathbf{k}}-\nu_{2\mathbf{k}}\end{pmatrix}.

(S12)

where
\nu_{1\mathbf{k}}=i\sin k_{x}a+i\sin(k_{x}a/2)\cos(\sqrt{3}k_{y}a/2),\nu_{2\mathbf{k}}=\sqrt{3}\cos(k_{x}a/2)\sin(\sqrt{3}k_{y}a/2)
.

At the final step, we rearrange our original basis
\tilde{\phi}_{\mathbf{k}}

into the canonical BdG basis
\phi_{\mathbf{k}}=(a_{\mathbf{k}},b_{\mathbf{k}},c_{A,\mathbf{k}},c_{B,\mathbf{k}},a_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger},b_{-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger},c_{A,-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger},c_{B,-\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger})^{T}
.
To this end, we perform a global basis transformation,

\mathcal{H}_{\mathbf{k}}=P\tilde{\mathcal{H}}_{\mathbf{k}}P^{\dagger},

(S13)

with

P=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{1}_{2\times 2}&\mathbf{0}_{2\times 2}&\mathbf{0}_{2\times 2}&\mathbf{0}_{2\times 2}\\
\mathbf{0}_{2\times 2}&\mathbf{0}_{2\times 2}&\mathbf{1}_{2\times 2}&\mathbf{0}_{2\times 2}\\
\mathbf{0}_{2\times 2}&\mathbf{1}_{2\times 2}&\mathbf{0}_{2\times 2}&\mathbf{0}_{2\times 2}\\
\mathbf{0}_{2\times 2}&\mathbf{0}_{2\times 2}&\mathbf{0}_{2\times 2}&\mathbf{1}_{2\times 2}\end{pmatrix}.

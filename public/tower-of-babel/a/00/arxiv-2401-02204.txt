The line bundles on the moduli stack of principal bundles on families of curves
Roberto Fringuelli, Filippo Viviani

Abstract
. Given a connected reductive algebraic group G G over an algebraically closed field, we investigate the Picard group of the moduli stack of principal G G -bundles over an arbitrary family of smooth curves.



1.
Introduction

The aim of this paper, which is a sequel of the papers
[
FV22
]
and
[
FV23
]
, is to study
the Picard group of the
moduli stack of (principal)
G
-bundles
\Bun_{G}(C/S)
, which parametrizes
G
-bundles, where
G
is a connected and smooth linear algebraic group over a field
k=\overline{k}
, over
an arbitrary family

\pi:C\to S
of (connected, smooth and projective)
k
-curves of genus
g=g(C/S)\geq 0
.

The moduli stack
\Bun_{G}(C)
of (principal)
G
-bundles, where
G
is a complex reductive group and
C
is a complex curve, has been deeply studied because of its relation to the Wess-Zumino-Witten (=WZW) model associated to
G
, which form a special class of rational conformal field theories, see
[
Bea94
]
,
[
Sor96
]
and
[
Bea96
]
for nice surveys. In the WZW-model associated to a simply connected group
G
, the spaces of conformal blocks can be interpreted as spaces of generalized theta functions, that is spaces of global sections of suitable line bundles (e.g. powers of determinant line bundles) on
\Bun_{G}(C)
, see
[
BL94
,
KNR94
,
Fal94
,
Pau96
,
LS97
]
.
The above application to conformal field theory leads naturally to the study of the Picard group of
\Bun_{G}(C)
(for
G
reductive over
k=\overline{k}
), which was computed in increasing degree of generality thanks to the effort of many mathematicians: the case of
\SL_{r}
dates back to pioneering work of Drezet-Narasimhan in the late eighties
[
DN89
]
; the case of a simply connected, almost-simple
G
is dealt with in
[
KN97
,
LS97
,
Sor99
,
Fal03
,
BK05
]
; the case of a semisimple almost-simple
G
is dealt with in
[
Las97
,
BLS98
,
Tel98
]
; the case of an arbitrary reductive group was finally established by Biswas-Hoffmann
[
BH10
]
.

Motived by the long term project of generalizing the study of the vector bundle of conformal blocks (also known as Verlinde bundle) behind the simply connected case, the authors in
[
FV22
]
and
[
FV23
]
have computed the Picard group of
\Bun_{G}({\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}/{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})
, for the universal family
{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
over the moduli stack of
n
-pointed curves of genus
g
, as well as the restriction homomorphism to the Picard group of the fibers of
\Bun_{G}({\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}/{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
, generalizing the previous works of Melo-Viviani
[
MV14
]
for
G=\Gm
and Fringuelli
[
Fri18
]
for
G=\GL_{r}
.

In this follow up paper, we study the Picard group of
\Bun_{G}(C/S)
for an arbitrary family
C/S
of curves over an algebraic stack
S
(which, for tecnichal reasons, it is often assumed to be an integral and regular quotient stack), in which case very little was known before. A remarkable exception is the work of Faltings
[
Fal03
]
, which provides a functorial identification
\Pic\Bun_{G}(C/S)\cong{\mathbb{Z}}
for a simply connected and almost simple group
G
and an arbitrary family
C/S
admitting a section.
Our Theorem
B
can be seen as a generalization of Faltings’ result (see Corollary
C
and the discussion following it).

We can now explain our main results focusing, in this introduction, on families
C/S
of positive genus
g(C/S)>0
, and referring to the body of the paper for the sligthly different, and easier, case
g(C/S)=0
.

Recall that the stack
\Bun_{G}(C/S)
is an algebraic stack, locally of finite type and smooth over
S
and its relative connected components are in functorial bijection with the fundamental group
\pi_{1}(G)
(see Fact
2.6
). We will denote the connected components and the restriction of the forgetful morphism by

\Phi_{G}^{\delta}:\Bun_{G}^{\delta}(C/S)\to S\quad\text{ for any }\delta\in\pi_{1}(G).

The pull-back morphism at the level of the Picard groups is injective, so will often focus onto the relative Picard group

\RPic\Bun_{G}^{\delta}(C/S):=\frac{\Pic\Bun_{G}^{\delta}(C/S)}{(\Phi^{\delta})^{*}(\Pic S)}.

We proved in
[
FV22
, Thm. A]
that if
\red:G\twoheadrightarrow G^{\red}
is the reductive quotient of
G
, i.e. the quotient of
G
by its unipotent radical, then for any
\delta\in\pi_{1}(G)\xrightarrow[\cong]{\pi_{1}(\red)}\pi_{1}(G^{\red})
the pull-back homomorphism

\red_{\#}^{*}:\Pic(\Bun_{G^{\red}(C/S)}^{\delta})\xrightarrow{\cong}\Pic(\Bun_{G}^{\delta}(C/S))

is an isomorphism. Hence, throughout this paper, we will restrict to the case of a
reductive group

G
.

A first source of line bundles on
\Bun_{G}^{\delta}(C/S)
comes from the determinant of cohomology
d_{\widehat{\pi}}(-)
and the Deligne pairing
\langle-,-\rangle_{\widehat{\pi}}
of line bundles on the universal curve
\widehat{\pi}:C\times_{S}\Bun_{G}^{\delta}(C/S)\to\Bun_{G}^{\delta}(C/S)
.
To be more precise, any character
\chi\in\Lambda^{*}(G):=\Hom(G,\Gm)
gives rise to a morphism of
S
-stacks

\chi_{\#}:\Bun_{G}^{\delta}(C/S)\to\Bun_{\Gm}^{\chi(d)}(C/S)

and, by pulling back via
\chi_{\#}
the universal
\Gm
-bundle (i.e. line bundle) on the universal curve
C\times_{S}\Bun_{\Gm}^{\chi(\delta)}(C/S)\to\Bun_{\Gm}^{\chi(\delta)}(C/S)
, we get a line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{\chi}
on
C\times_{S}\Bun_{G}^{\delta}(C/S)
. Then, using these line bundles
\mathcal{L}_{\chi}
and the line bundles
\{p_{1}^{*}(M)\>:\>M\in\Pic(C)\}
pull-backed from the family
C/S
, we define the following line bundles, that we call
tautological line bundles
, on
\Bun_{G}^{\delta}(C/S)

\displaystyle\left\{d_{\widehat{\pi}}({\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}(M))\>:\chi\in\Lambda^{*}(G),M\in\Pic(C)\right\},

\displaystyle\left\{\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}(M),{\mathcal{L}}_{\mu}(N)\rangle_{\widehat{\pi}}:\chi,\mu\in\Lambda^{*}(G),M,N\in\Pic(C)\right\},

where we set
{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}(M):={\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}(p_{1}^{*}(M))
for simplicity.

Note that since any character of
G
factors through the abelianization of
\mathrm{ab}:G\to G^{\mathrm{ab}}
of
G
,
then the tautological line bundles lie in the subgroup

\Pic\Bun_{G^{\mathrm{ab}}}^{\delta^{\mathrm{ab}}}(C/S)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{ab}_{\sharp}^{*}}}{{\hookrightarrow}}\Pic\Bun_{G}^{\delta}(C/S),

where
\delta^{\mathrm{ab}}:=\pi_{1}(\mathrm{ab})(\delta)
.

The next Theorem describes, for an arbitrary torus
T
and an arbitrary
d\in\pi_{1}(T)
(which includes the case
T=G^{\mathrm{ab}}
and
\delta^{\mathrm{ab}}=d
), the structure of the

tautological subgroup

\Pic^{\taut}\Bun_{T}^{d}(C/S)\subseteq\Pic\Bun_{T}^{d}(C/S)

generated by the tautological line bundles (or, in other words, it encodes all the relations among tautological line bundles), and it describes when it coincides with the full Picard group.

Theorem A
.

(see Theorems
3.8
,
3.9
)
Let
C\to S
be a family of curves of genus
g>0
on a regular and integral quotient stack. Let
T
be a torus and fix
d\in\Lambda(T)=\pi_{1}(T)
.

(1)

The relative tautological Picard group of
\Bun_{T}^{d}(C/S)
sits in the following exact sequence, functorial in
S
and in
T
:

0\to\Lambda^{*}(T)\otimes\RPic(C/S)\xrightarrow{i_{T}^{d}}\RPic^{\taut}\Bun_{T}^{d}(C/S)\xrightarrow{\gamma_{T}^{d}}\Bil^{s}(\Lambda(T))\to 0

where
\RPic(C/S)
is the relative Picard group of
C/S
, and the homomorphisms
i_{T}^{d}
and
\gamma_{T}^{d}
are defined by

i_{T}^{d}(\chi\otimes M)=\langle{\mathcal{L}}_{\chi},M\rangle_{\widehat{\pi}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\gamma_{T}^{d}(d_{\widehat{\pi}}({\mathcal{L}}_{\chi}(M))=\chi\otimes\chi.

(2)

If
\End(J_{C_{\overline{\eta}}})={\mathbb{Z}}
for a geometric generic point
\overline{\eta}\to S
and the natural map
\Pic(C)\rightarrow\Pic_{C/S}(S)
is surjective, then

\Pic\Bun_{T}^{d}(C/S)=\Pic^{\taut}\Bun_{T}^{d}(C/S).

Moreover, in Theorem
3.8
, we give two alternative presentations of the relative tautological Picard group of
\Bun_{T}^{d}(C/S)
.

The case of families of genus zero curves is easier and it is dealt with in Theorem
3.6
. In particular, we will show that the Picard group of
\Bun_{T}^{d}(C/S)
is always generated by tautological line bundles if
g(C/S)=0
.

Theorem B
.

(see Theorems

Let

be a family of curves of genus

(b)

\textstyle{\Sym^{2}\Lambda^{*}(G^{\mathrm{ab}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some definitions and preliminaries results that we will need throughout the paper.

2.1.
Family of curves

A
curve
is a connected, smooth and projective scheme of dimension one over an algebraically closed field. The genus of a curve
C
is
g(C):=\dim H^{0}(C,\omega_{C})
.

A
family of curves

\pi:C\to S
is a proper, flat and representable morphism of algebraic stacks whose geometric fibers are curves. If all the geometric fibers of
\pi
have the same genus
g
, then we say that
\pi:C\to S
is a family of curves of genus
g
(or a family of curves with relative genus
g
) and we set
g(C/S):=g
. Note that any family of curves
\pi:C\to S
with
S
connected is a family of genus
g
curves for some
g\geq 0
.
For technical reasons, we will often restrict to families of curves
\pi:C\to S
over a regular and integral quotient stack
S
over
k
.

Given
g,n\geq 0
, we denote by
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
the algebraic stack parametrizing families of curves of genus
g
with
n
ordered and pairwise disjoint sections, and by
{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
the
universal family of
n
-marked curves of genus
g
. We omit the
n
from the notation when it is zero. Note that
{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
is a DM (=Deligne-Mumford) stack if and only if
2g-2+n>0
.

A family of curves
\pi:C\to S
is
locally projective
if, Zariski-locally on the base, it is a closed subscheme of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{S}
for some
n\geq 1
. Equivalently,
\pi:C\to S
is locally projective if there exists
a
\pi
-relatively ample line bundle on
C
, or equivalently a line bundle on
C
having
\pi
-relative positive degree.
Note that a family of curves
\pi:C\to S
is locally projective if either
g(C/S)\neq 1
(because the relative dualizing line bundle
\omega_{\pi}
has relative degree
2g(C/S)-2
) or
\pi
has a section
\sigma:S\to C
.
However, there are examples of families of genus one curves without sections that are not locally projective, e.g. the universal family
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
of curves of genus one (see Remark
2.2
(
2
), and also
[
Ray70
, XIII, 3.2]
and
[
Zom
]
for other examples).

In the results of this paper, an important role is played by the following invariant of the family
C/S
:

Definition 2.1
.

For a family of curves
C/S
, we define

\delta(C/S):=\begin{cases}\min\{\deg_{\pi}(M)>0\>:M\in\Pic(C)\},&\text{ if }\pi\text{ is locally projective;}\\
\ 0,&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Equivalently, if
\pi
is locally projective, then
\delta(C/S)
is the non-negative generator of the non-zero image of the relative degree map

(2.1)

\displaystyle\deg_{\pi}:\Pic(C)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow{\mathbb{Z}},

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle\mapsto\deg_{\pi}(M).

Remark 2.2
.

(1)

Since the relative dualizing line bundle
\omega_{\pi}
of
\pi:C\to S
has relative degree equal to
2g(C/S)-2
, then

\delta(C/S)\>\text{ divides }\>2g(C/S)-2.

(2)

It follows from the weak Franchetta conjecture (see
[
AC87
]
,
[
Sch03
]
,
[
FVb
]
) that, for the universal family
{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
of
n
-marked curves of genus
g
, we have that

\delta({\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}/{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n})=\begin{cases}|2g-2|&\text{ if }n=0,\\
1&\text{ if }n>0.\end{cases}

Notice that
\delta({\mathcal{C}}_{1}/{\mathcal{M}}_{1})=0
, which implies that
{\mathcal{C}}_{1}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
is not locally projective.

(3)

The invariant
\delta(C/S)
has been computed for many interesting families of curves:

•

If
C/S
is the universal family of (smooth) complete intersections curves in
{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
of type
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r-1})
, then
\delta(C/S)=d_{1}\ldots d_{r-1}
(see
[
Cil86
, Thm. 4.4]
).
In particular, if
C/S
is the universal family of (smooth) plane curves of degree
d
, then
\delta(C/S)=d
(see
[
Cil86
, Thm. 4.2]
).

•

If
C/S
is the universal family of hyperplanes sections of the universal family of polarized genus
g\geq 3
K3 surfaces, then
\delta(C/S)=2g-2
(see
[
Cil86
, Prop. 4.5]
).

•

If
C/S
is the universal family of one of the following

–

the unique component with general moduli of the Hilbert scheme of curves in
{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
with
r\geq 3
of genus
g
and degree
d
;

–

the Severi variety of curves of genus
g
together with a birational embedding as a nodal plane curve of degree
d
;

–

the Hurwtiz scheme of degree
d
covers of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
of genus
g
;

and we assume that
\rho:=g-(r+1)(g+r-d)\geq 2
, then
\delta(C/S)=\gcd\{2g-2,d\}
(see
[
Cil87
, Thm. 1.1]
and
[
Cil86
, Thm. 5.3]
).

•

If
C/S
is the universal family of hyperelliptic genus
g
curves then (see
[
MR85
, Thm. 3.5]
)

\delta(C/S)=\begin{cases}4&\text{ if }g\>\text{ is odd, }\\
2&\>\text{ if }g\>\text{ is even.}\end{cases}

(4)

If
C/S
is a family of curves of genus
g=0
, then

\delta(C/S)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }C/S\text{ is Zariski-locally trivial, }\\
2&\text{ if }C/S\text{ is not Zariski-locally trivial.}\\
\end{cases}

Note also that
C/S
is Zariski locally trivial if and only if
C/S
admits a section.

We will denote by
\Pic_{C/S}
the
relative Picard functor
of the family
C\to S
, which is an algebraic stack representable and locally of finite presentation over
S

(by Artin’s representability theorem, see e.g.
[
BLR90
, §8.3]
). We have an exact sequence (see
[
BLR90
, §8.1, Prop. 4]
)

0\to\Pic(S)\xrightarrow{\pi^{*}}\Pic(C)\to\Pic_{C/S}(S)\to\Br(S)\xrightarrow{\pi^{*}}\Br(C),

where
\Br(-)=H^{2}(-,\Gm)
denote the cohomological Brauer group. In particular, we get an injective homomorphism

(2.2)

\RPic(C/S):=\frac{\Pic(C)}{\Pic(S)}\hookrightarrow\Pic_{C/S}(S).

Several of our results are restricted to families such that the inclusion (
2.2
) is an equality, or equivalently such that the natural map
\Pic(C)\to\Pic_{C/S}(S)
is surjective.
In the next Remark, we describe several families where this assumption holds true.

Remark 2.3
.

The inclusion (
2.2
) is an equality in the following cases:

(1)

if
\pi:C\to S
admits a section (because
\pi^{*}:\Br(S)\to\Br(C)
is injective); in particular, if
C/S={\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}/{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
with
n>0
. Therefore, equality in (
2.2
) can always be achieved after an étale cover of the base
S
.

(2)

if
\Br(S)=0
, e.g. if
S
is the spectrum of a field
k=\overline{k}
, or the spectrum of strictly henselian valuation ring, or a smooth curve over
k=\overline{k}
.

(3)

if
C/S={\mathcal{C}}_{g}/{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
and
g\geq 2
by the strong Franchetta’s conjecture (see
[
Mes87
]
).

(4)

if the relative Jacobian
J^{d}_{C/S}:=\Pic^{d}_{C/S}\to S
in degree
d
admits a Poincaré line bundle for every
d\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(5)

if
\delta(C/S)=1
(see
[
MR85
, Lemma 2.2]
).

Several of our results are restricted to families of curves
\pi:C\to S
over an integral base
S
, that satisfy the following condition

(2.3)

\End(J_{C_{\overline{\eta}}})={\mathbb{Z}},

where
\overline{\eta}
is a geometric generic point of
S
.

In the next result, we collect some sufficient and necessary conditions for (
2.3
) to hold.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\pi:C\to S
a family of curves over an integral base
S
(locally of finite type over
k=\overline{k}
) and let
\overline{\eta}\to S
be a geometric generic point.

(1)

If
\End(J_{C_{s}})={\mathbb{Z}}
for some
s\in S(k)
then
\End(J_{C_{\overline{\eta}}})={\mathbb{Z}}
.

(2)

If
k
is uncountable and
\End(J_{C_{\overline{\eta}}})={\mathbb{Z}}
, then
\End(J_{C_{s}})={\mathbb{Z}}
for
s
very general in
S(k)
.

(3)

If
k\not\subseteq\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
then
\End(J_{C_{\overline{\eta}}})={\mathbb{Z}}
if and only if there exists
s\in S(\overline{k})
such that
\End(J_{C_{s}})={\mathbb{Z}}
.

Note that part (
3
) is false if
k=\overline{k}=\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, see
[
MP12
, Rmk. 1.12]
.

Proof.

Parts (
1
) and (
2
) follow from the well-know fact (see the proof of
[
MP12
, Prop. 1.13]
) that the jumping locus

S(k)_{\mathrm{jump}}=\{s\in S(k)\>:\>\End(J_{C_{\overline{\eta}}})\hookrightarrow\End(J_{C_{s}})\text{ is an isomorphism}\}

is the set of
k
-points of a union of countably many proper substacks of
S
.

Part (
3
) follows from the non-trivial result (proved in
[
MP12
]
,
[
Chr
]
,
[
Amb23
]
) that
S(k)_{\mathrm{jump}}\neq S(k)
if
k\neq\overline{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

(1)

The condition
\End(J_{C_{\overline{\eta}}})={\mathbb{Z}}
is known to hold for several general families of curves, e.g.:

•

the universal family

over an arbitrary field

by
[

]
;

•

.

(2)

is equal to

) holds true for any family

of genus

2.2.
Reductive groups

A
reductive group
(over
k=\overline{k}
) is a smooth and connected linear algebraic group over
k
(i.e. a closed algebraic subgroup of
(\GL_{n})_{k}
, or equivalently an affine group scheme of finite type over
k
), which does not contain non-trivial connected normal unipotent algebraic subgroups. To any reductive group
G
, we can associate a cross-like diagram of reductive groups

(2.5)

\textstyle{\mathscr{D}(G)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathscr{D}}
\textstyle{\mathscr{R}(G)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathscr{R}}
\textstyle{G\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{ab}}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{ss}}
\textstyle{G^{\mathrm{ss}}}
\textstyle{G^{\mathrm{ab}}}

where

•

\mathscr{D}(G):=[G,G]
is the derived subgroup of
G
;

•

G^{\mathrm{ab}}:=G/\mathscr{D}(G)
is called the abelianization of
G
;

•

\mathscr{R}(G)
is the radical subgroup of
G
, which is equal (since
G
is reductive) to the connected component
\mathscr{Z}(G)^{o}
of the center
\mathscr{Z}(G)
;

•

G^{\mathrm{ss}}:=G/\mathscr{R}(G)
is called the semisimplification of
G
.

In the above diagram, the horizontal and vertical lines are short exact sequences of reductive groups, the morphisms
\mathscr{D}(G)\twoheadrightarrow G^{\mathrm{ss}}
and
\mathscr{R}(G)\twoheadrightarrow G^{\mathrm{ab}}
are central isogenies of, respectively, semisimple groups and tori with the same kernel.
Since the two semisimple groups
\mathscr{D}(G)
and
G^{\mathrm{ss}}
are isogenous, they share the same simply connected cover, that we will denote by
G^{\mathrm{sc}}
, and the same adjoint quotient, that we will denote by
G^{\mathrm{ad}}
.

Given a reductive group
G
over
k
, we will denote by
T_{G}
a maximal torus of
G
and by
B_{G}
the positive Borel subgroup of
G
containing
T_{G}
.
As usual, we denote by
\mathscr{W}_{G}:=N(T_{G})/T_{G}
its Weyl group.

Given a torus
T
, we will denote by
\Lambda(T):=\Hom(\Gm,T)
its cocharacter lattice and by
\Lambda^{*}(T):=\Hom(T,\Gm)
its character lattice. Note that these lattices are dual to each other via the bilinear map

\displaystyle\Lambda^{*}(T)\times\Lambda(T)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Hom(\Gm,\Gm)={\mathbb{Z}},

\displaystyle(\chi,d)

\displaystyle\mapsto\chi\circ d:=\chi(d).

The
fundamental group
of
G
is
\displaystyle\pi_{1}(G):=\frac{\Lambda(T_{G})}{\Lambda_{\mathrm{coroots}}},

where
\Lambda_{\mathrm{coroots}}
is the sublattice of
\Lambda(T_{G})
which is generated by integral linear combinations of coroots. The vertical exact sequence in (
2.5
) induces
an exact sequence of finitely generated abelian groups

(2.6)

0\to\pi_{1}(\mathscr{D}(G))\to\pi_{1}(G)\xrightarrow{\pi_{1}(\mathrm{ab})}\pi_{1}(G^{\mathrm{ab}})\to 0,

which identifies
\pi_{1}(\mathscr{D}(G))
with the torsion subgroup of
\pi_{1}(G)
and
\pi_{1}(G^{\mathrm{ab}})
with the torsion-free quotient of
\pi_{1}(G)
.

2.3.
The stack of
G
-bundles on a family of curves

In this subsection,
G
will be a connected and smooth linear algebraic group over
k=\overline{k}
.
Further restrictions on
G
, like reductiveness, will be specified when needed.

For any family of curves
\pi:C\to S
, we denote by
\Bun_{G}(C/S)
the
moduli stack of
G
-bundles on
C\to S
, i.e. the algebraic stack over
S
whose fiber over
V\to S
is the groupoid of
G
-bundles
E\to C_{V}:=C\times_{S}V
. By definition, we have a forgetful surjective morphism

(2.7)

\Phi_{G}=\Phi_{G}(C/S):\Bun_{G}(C/S)\longrightarrow S.

The stack
\Bun_{G}(C/S)
comes equipped with a universal
G
-torsor
{\mathcal{P}}
on the universal family
\widehat{\pi}:\Bun_{G}(C/S)\times_{S}C\to\Bun_{G}(C/S)
.

The stack
\Bun_{G}(C/S)
is functorial in
S
, i.e. for any
V\to S
we have that

\Bun_{G}(C_{V}/V)=\Bun_{G}(C/S)\times_{S}V.

In particular, the fiber of
\Phi_{G}(C/S):\Phi_{G}(C/S)\to S
over a
k
-point
s\in S
is the moduli stack
\Bun_{G}(C_{s})
of
G
-bundles on the curve
C_{s}
.

In the special case where the family of curves is the universal family
{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
over the moduli stack of
n
-pointed curves of genus
g
, we set

\Bun_{G,g,n}:=\Bun_{G}({\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}/{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}).

In particular, if the family
C\to S
has constant relative genus
g=g(C/S)
then we have that

\Bun_{G}(C/S)=S\times_{{\mathcal{M}}_{g}}\Bun_{G,g},

with respect to the modular morphism
S\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
associated to the family
C\to S
.

Any morphism of connected and smooth linear algebraic groups
\phi:G\to H
determines a morphism of stacks over
S

(2.8)

\begin{array}[]{lccc}\phi_{\sharp}=\phi_{\sharp}(C/S):&\Bun_{G}(C/S)&\longrightarrow&\Bun_{H}(C/S)\\
&\Big(E\to C_{V}\Big)&\longmapsto&\Big(\phi_{\sharp}(E):=E\times^{\phi,G}H=(E\times H)/G\to C_{V}\Big)\end{array}

where the (right) action of
G
on
E\times H
is
(p,h).g:=(p.g,\phi(g)^{-1}h)
.

We collect in the following Fact the geometric properties of
\Bun_{G}(C/S)
and of the forgetful morphism
\Phi_{G}(C/S)
.

Fact 2.6
.

Let
G
be a connected and smooth linear algebraic group and let
\pi:C\to S
be a family of curves.

(1)

The morphism
\Phi_{G}(C/S)
is locally of finite presentation, smooth, with affine and finitely presented relative diagonal.

(2)

There is a functorial decomposition into a disjoint union

(2.9)

\Phi_{G}(C/S):\coprod_{\delta\in\pi_{1}(G)}\Bun_{G}^{\delta}(C/S)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Phi_{G}^{\delta}(C/S)}}{{\longrightarrow}}S,

such that the morphism
\Phi_{G}^{\delta}(C/S):\Bun_{G}^{\delta}(C/S)\to S
has geometric integral fibers for every
\delta\in\pi_{1}(G)
.

(3)

If

is reductive, then for any
\delta\in\pi_{1}(G)
the morphism
\Phi_{G}^{\delta}(C/S):\Bun_{G}^{\delta}(C/S)\to S
is Stein, i.e. it is fpqc and cohomologically flat in degree zero (which means that the natural morphism
(\Phi_{G}^{\delta}(C/S)^{\sharp}:{\mathcal{O}}_{S}\to\Phi_{G}^{\delta}(C/S)_{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{\Bun_{G}^{\delta}(C/S)})

(g-1)\dim G
.

Proof.

Part (
1
): see
[
FV22
, Thm. 3.1]
and the references therein. Part (
2
): see
[
FV22
, Thm. 3.1.1, Cor. 3.1.2]
and the references therein.
Part (
3
): see
[
FV22
, Thm. 3.1.3]
and the proof of
[
FV22
, Prop. 3.3.2]
.
∎

Since the center
\mathscr{Z}(G)
of a reductive group

acts functorially on any
G
-bundle, we have that
\mathscr{Z}(G)
sits functorially inside the automorphism group of any
T
-point

of

for any

which turns out to be a
\mathscr{Z}(G)
-gerbe, i.e. a gerbe banded by

The tautological Picard group

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Hom\left(\Lambda(G^{\mathrm{ab}}),\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\delta(C/S){\mathbb{Z}}}\right)

\displaystyle[b]

\displaystyle\mapsto\left\{x\mapsto\left[b(\delta\otimes\widetilde{x})+(1-g)b(\widetilde{x}\otimes\widetilde{x})\right]\right\},

where
b\in\NS(\mathfrak{Bun}_{G,g,n}^{\delta})\subset\Bil^{s,\mathscr{D}-\ev}(\Lambda(T_{G}))^{\mathscr{W}_{G}}
, and
\widetilde{x}\in\Lambda(T_{G})

x\in\Lambda(G^{\mathrm{ab}})
.

This Theorem was proved for the universal family
{\mathcal{C}}_{g,n}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
for

in
[
FV23
, Thm. 5.7]

and for a curve
C
over
k=\overline{k}
of genus
g(C)\geq 3
in
[
BH12
, Thm. 6.8]
(and in both results the general case of an arbitrary reductive group is also treated).

Proof.

This is deduced from Theorem
3.18
, arguing as in the proofs of

, Thm. 5.7]
.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Same assumptions as in Theorem
4.4
.

(1)

If
\delta(C/S)=1
then there is an isomorphism

\widetilde{\Lambda_{\mathscr{D}}^{*}}:\coker(\w_{G}^{\delta})\xrightarrow{\cong}\coker(\ev_{\mathscr{D}(G)}^{\delta}).

(2)

If
G=T
is a torus and
d\in\Lambda(T)
, then there is an exact sequence

(4.10)

0\to\coker(\overline{\gamma_{T}^{d}})\xrightarrow{\partial_{T}^{d}}\Hom\left(\Lambda(T),\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\delta(C/S){\mathbb{Z}}}\right)\xrightarrow{}\coker(\w_{T}^{d})\to 0,

Moreover, we have that

\displaystyle\coker(\overline{\gamma_{T}^{d}})\cong\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\frac{\delta(C/S)}{\gcd(\delta(C/S),\mathrm{div}(d)+1-g)}{\mathbb{Z}}}\oplus\left[\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\frac{\delta(C/S)}{\gcd(\delta(C/S),g-1,\mathrm{div}(d))}{\mathbb{Z}}}\right]^{\oplus(\dim(T)-1)}

\displaystyle\coker(\omega_{T}^{d})\cong\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\gcd(\delta(C/S),\mathrm{div}(d)+1-g){\mathbb{Z}}}\oplus\left[\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\gcd(\delta(C/S),g-1,\mathrm{div}(d)){\mathbb{Z}}}\right]^{\oplus(\dim(T)-1)},

where
\mathrm{div}(d)
is the divisibility of
d
in the lattice
\Lambda(T)
, with the convention that
\coker(\overline{\gamma_{T}^{d}})=\{0\}
if
\delta(C/S)=0
(when the above expression for
\coker(\overline{\gamma_{T}^{d}})
is not well-defined).

The above Corollary for
G=\Gm
recovers the main result of
[
MR85
]
: a Poincaré line bundle exists on
\Bunr_{\Gm}^{d}(C/S)\times_{S}C
(which is equivalent to the triviality of the
\Gm
-gerbe
\nu_{\Gm}^{d}
) if and only if
\gcd(\delta(C/S),d+1-g)=1
where
d\in\Lambda(\Gm)={\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

Part (
1
) and the first part of (
2
) follow from the above Theorem
4.3
.

The second part of (
2
) follows by choosing a basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{r}\}
of
\Lambda(T)
(with
r=\dim T
) such that
d=\mathrm{div}(d)e_{1}
and computing the composition

\overline{\partial_{T}^{d}}:\Bil^{s}(\Lambda(T))\twoheadrightarrow\coker(\overline{\gamma_{T}^{d}})\xrightarrow{\partial_{T}^{d}}\Hom\left(\Lambda(T),\frac{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\delta(C/S){\mathbb{Z}}}\right)

on the basis
\left\{\{e_{i}^{*}\otimes e_{i}^{*}\}_{i},\{e_{i}^{*}\otimes e_{j}^{*}+e_{j}^{*}\otimes e_{i}^{*}\}_{i>j}\right\}
of
\Bil^{s}(\Lambda(T))
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \overline{\partial_{T}^{d}}(e_{1}^{*}\otimes e_{1}^{*})=[\mathrm{div}(d)+1-g]e_{1}^{*},\\
\overline{\partial_{T}^{d}}(e_{i}^{*}\otimes e_{i}^{*})=[1-g]e_{i}^{*}\>\text{ for any }i\geq 2,\\
\overline{\partial_{T}^{d}}(e_{1}^{*}\otimes e_{i}^{*}+e_{i}^{*}\otimes e_{1}^{*})=\mathrm{div}(d)e_{i}^{*}\>\text{ for any }i\geq 2,\\
\overline{\partial_{T}^{d}}(e_{i}^{*}\otimes e_{j}^{*}+e_{j}^{*}\otimes e_{i}^{*})=0\>\text{ for any }2\leq i<j.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\pi:C\to S
is a family of smooth curves of genus
g(C/S)=0
over an integral and regular quotient stack
S
. Assume that at least one of the following two assumptions is satisfied:

(a)

the family
C/S
is Zariski locally trivial;

(b)

the derived subgroup
\mathscr{D}(G)
is simply connected.

For a given
\delta\in\pi_{1}(G)
, consider the homomorphism

(4.11)

\displaystyle\widehat{\ev}_{\mathscr{D}(G)}^{\delta}:\left\{\begin{aligned} b\in\left(\Sym^{2}\Lambda^{*}(T_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}})\right)^{\mathscr{W}_{G}}:\\
b^{{\mathbb{Q}}}(d^{\mathrm{ss}},-)_{|\Lambda(T_{\mathscr{D}(G)})}\text{ is integral }\end{aligned}\right\}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\frac{\Lambda^{*}(T_{\mathscr{D}(G)})}{\Lambda^{*}(T_{G^{\mathrm{ad}})}}

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle\mapsto b(\delta^{\mathrm{ss}}\otimes-):=[b^{{\mathbb{Q}}}(d^{\mathrm{ss}}\otimes-)],

where
d^{\mathrm{ss}}\in\Lambda(T_{G^{\mathrm{ss}}})
is any lift of
\delta^{\mathrm{ss}}:=\pi_{1}(\mathrm{ss})(\delta)\in\pi_{1}(G^{\mathrm{ss}})=\frac{\Lambda(T_{G^{\mathrm{ss}}})}{\Lambda(T_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}})}
and
d^{{\mathbb{Q}}}
is the rational extension of
b
to
\Lambda(T_{G^{\mathrm{ss}}})
.

(1)

We have an isomorphism

\RPic(\Bunr_{G}^{\delta}(C/S))\cong\ker(\widehat{\ev}_{\mathscr{D}(G)}^{\delta}),

which is functorial in
S
and in
G
.

(2)

The homomorphism
\overline{\Lambda_{\mathscr{D}}^{*}}
of (
4.2
) induces a surjection

(4.12)

\widetilde{\Lambda_{\mathscr{D}}^{*}}:\coker(\w_{G}^{\delta})\twoheadrightarrow\coker(\widehat{\ev}_{\mathscr{D}(G)}^{\delta}),

whose kernel is equal to

(4.13)

\ker(\widetilde{\Lambda_{\mathscr{D}}^{*}})=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if either }\delta(C/S)=1\>\text{ or }\delta^{\mathrm{ab}}\>\text{ is $2$-divisible in }\Lambda^{*}(G^{\mathrm{ab}}),\\
{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}&\text{ if }\delta(C/S)=2\>\text{ and }\delta^{\mathrm{ab}}\>\text{ is not $2$-divisible in }\Lambda^{*}(G^{\mathrm{ab}}).\\
\end{cases}

This Theorem can be deduced for the universal family
{\mathcal{C}}_{0,n}\to{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
with
n\geq 1
(which is Zariski locally trivial) from
[
FV23
, Rmk. 5.10]
.
Note that if
\mathscr{D}(G)
is simply connected, then the homomorphism
\widehat{\ev}_{\mathscr{D}(G)}^{\delta}
coincides with the homomorphism
\ev_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}}^{\delta}
by Lemma
3.13
(
3
).

Proof.

Set

Q:=\left\{\begin{aligned} b\in\left(\Sym^{2}\Lambda^{*}(T_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}})\right)^{\mathscr{W}_{G}}:\\
b^{{\mathbb{Q}}}(d^{\mathrm{ss}},-)_{|\Lambda(T_{\mathscr{D}(G)})}\text{ is integral }\end{aligned}\right\}.

Consider the following diagram

(4.14)

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\RPic\left(\Bun_{G_{\mathrm{ab}}}^{\delta^{\mathrm{ab}}}(C/S)\right)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{ab}_{\sharp}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\w_{G^{\mathrm{ab}}}^{\delta^{\mathrm{ab}}}}
\textstyle{\RPic\left(\Bun_{G}^{\delta}(C/S)\right)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\w_{G}^{\delta}}
\textstyle{Q\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\widehat{\ev}_{\mathscr{D}(G)}^{\delta}}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Lambda^{*}(G^{\mathrm{ab}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\Lambda_{\mathrm{ab}}^{*}}}
\textstyle{\frac{\Lambda^{*}(T_{G})}{\Lambda^{*}(T_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}})}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\overline{\Lambda_{\mathscr{D}}^{*}}}
\textstyle{\frac{\Lambda^{*}(T_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}})}{\Lambda^{*}(T_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}})}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0.}

where the first row is exact by Corollary
3.21
, the second row is exact by (
4.2
) and the diagram is commutative by the same proof of
[
FV23
, Prop. 5.2, Prop. 5.4]
.

By applying the snake lemma and using that
\w_{G^{\mathrm{ab}}}^{\delta^{\mathrm{ab}}}
is injective by Theorem
3.6
(
2
), we get an exact sequence

0\to\RPic(\Bunr_{G}^{\delta}(C/S))\to\ker(\widehat{\ev}_{\mathscr{D}(G)}^{\delta}\xrightarrow{\partial}\coker(\w_{G^{\mathrm{ab}}}^{\delta^{\mathrm{ab}}})\to\coker(\w_{G}^{\delta})\xrightarrow{\widetilde{\Lambda_{\mathscr{D}}^{*}}}\coker(\widehat{\ev}_{\mathscr{D}(G)}^{\delta})\to 0.

Observe now that Theorem
3.6
(
3
) implies that
\coker(\w_{G^{\mathrm{ab}}}^{\delta^{\mathrm{ab}}})
is equal to the group in the right hand side of (
4.13
). Hence, we conclude by the previous exact sequence and the following

Claim:
the coboundary map
\partial
is zero.

Indeed, assume that
\coker(\w_{G^{\mathrm{ab}}}^{\delta^{\mathrm{ab}}})\cong{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
, for otherwise the statement is trivially true. In particular,
C/S
is not Zariski locally trivial and hence
\mathscr{D}(G)=G^{\mathrm{sc}}
due to our assumptions. This implies that
\widehat{\ev}_{\mathscr{D}(G)}^{\delta}=\ev_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}}^{\delta}
as observed above.
Let
b\in\ker(\ev_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}}^{\delta})\subset\left(\Sym^{2}\Lambda^{*}(T_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}})\right)^{\mathscr{W}_{G}}
. By Lemma
3.13
(
1
), we can choose
\widetilde{b}\in\left(\Sym^{2}\Lambda^{*}(T_{G})\right)^{\mathscr{W}_{G}}
such that
\widetilde{b}_{|\Lambda(T_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}})\times\Lambda(T_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}})}=b
, which implies that the class of
\tau_{G}^{\delta}(\widetilde{b})\in\RPic\left(\Bun_{G}^{\delta}(C/S)\right)
is a lift of
b
. By the same argument of
[
FV23
, Def./Lemma 3.7, Prop. 5.2]
, we have that

\w_{G}^{\delta}(\tau_{G}^{\delta}(\widetilde{b}))=\widetilde{b}(\delta\otimes-):=[\widetilde{b}(d\otimes-)]\in\frac{\Lambda^{*}(T_{G})}{\Lambda^{*}(T_{G^{\mathrm{ad}}})},

where
d\in\Lambda(T_{G})
is any lift of
\delta
. Since
b
is in the kernel of
\ev_{G^{\mathrm{sc}}}^{\delta}
, the element
\w_{G}^{\delta}(\tau_{G}^{\delta}(\widetilde{b}))
lies in the image of
\overline{\Lambda_{\mathrm{ab}}^{*}}
, i.e. there exists
\chi\in\Lambda^{*}(G^{\mathrm{ab}})
such that

(4.15)

\widetilde{b}(\delta\otimes-)=\chi(\Lambda_{\mathrm{ab}}(-)).

By the definition of the boundary homomorphism, we have that

\partial(b)=[\chi(\delta^{\mathrm{ab}})]\in{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}\cong\coker(\w_{G^{\mathrm{ab}}}^{\delta^{\mathrm{ab}}}).

Since
\delta^{\mathrm{ab}}=\Lambda_{\mathrm{ab}}(d)
for any lift
d\in\Lambda(T_{G})
of
\delta
, (
4.15
) implies that

\chi(\delta^{\mathrm{ab}})=\widetilde{b}(\delta\otimes b)=\widetilde{b}(d\otimes d).

But
\widetilde{b}(d\otimes d)
is even since
\widetilde{b}\in\left(\Sym^{2}\Lambda^{*}(T_{G})\right)^{\mathscr{W}_{G}}\cong\Bil^{s,\ev}(\Lambda(T_{G}))^{\mathscr{W}_{G}}
, which forces
\partial(b)
to be zero, q.e.d.
∎

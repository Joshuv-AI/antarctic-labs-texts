Characterization of multiframelet set on local fields of positive characteristic
Debasis Haldar

Abstract
. This paper presents a discussion on multiframelet set, multiwavelet set and set correspond to super wavelet on local fields of positive characteristic. We characterize Parseval multiframelet set and give equivalent conditions multiwavelet set holds. Furthurmore, we characterize MRA multiwavelet set with the help of dimension function.

2.1.
Local fields

Throughout this article,
\mathfrak{L}
denotes the set
\{1,\ldots,L\}
,
\mathds{1}_{S}
denotes characteristic function of the set
S
,
K
denotes a local field and a topological space. By a locally compact field or, in short, a local field we mean a locally compact, complete, non-discrete and totally disconnected field.
K^{*}
and
K^{+}
denote as the multiplicative and additive groups of
K
respectively.

Readers are referred to
[
7
,
8
]
for proofs of all the results stated in this section. The valuation
|x|
of
x\in K
satisfies the following properties

(i)

|x|\geq 0
and
|x|=0
if and only if
x=0
;

(ii)

|xy|=|x||y|
;

(iii)

|x+y|\leq
max
\{|x|,|y|\}
.
|x+y|=\{|x|,|y|\}
if
|x|\neq|y|
.

Property (iii) is called the ultrametric inequality and hence
K
is an non-Archimedean field. Define
\mathfrak{B}=\{x\in K:|x|<1\}
and
\mathfrak{D}=\{x\in K:|x|\leq 1\}
. Then they both are compact and open.
\mathfrak{B}
is the prime ideal in
K
and the ring of integers
\mathfrak{D}
is the unique maximal compact subring of
K
.

The set of valuation of the elements of
K
is a discrete set of the form
\{v^{s}:s\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cup\{0\}
for some positive
v
as
K
is totally disconnected. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be an element of maximum valuation in
\mathfrak{B}
. Then
\mathfrak{B}=\langle\mathfrak{p}\rangle=\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{D}
. It is not difficult to see that
\mathfrak{D}
is compact and open, hence
\mathfrak{B}
is so. Therefore
\mathfrak{D}/\mathfrak{B}\cong GF(q)
, where
q=p^{c}
for some prime
p
and
c\in\mathbb{N}
as
GF(q)
is a vector space of
c
-dimension over
GF(p)
. Let
\mathfrak{B}^{s}=\mathfrak{p}^{s}\mathfrak{D}=\{x\in K:|x|\leq q^{-s}\}
for
s\in\mathbb{Z}
. These
\mathfrak{B}^{s}
are said to be fractional ideals which are also compact open subgroup of
K^{+}
.

K^{+}
is being a LCAG, one may choose a Haar measure
dx
for
K^{+}
.
d(\alpha x)
is also a Haar measure and
d(\alpha x)=|\alpha|dx
for
\alpha\in K\setminus\{0\}
. This Haar measure is normalized by
\int\limits_{K}\mathds{1}_{\mathfrak{D}}(x)dx=1
. If
dx
is a Haar measure on
K^{+}
, then Haar measure on
K^{*}
is given by
\frac{dx}{|x|}
. It is easy to see the following

•

|\mathfrak{p}|=q^{-1}=\mu(\mathfrak{B})
.

•

valuation set of
K
is
\{q^{s}:s\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cup\{0\}
.

The Hilbert space of all complex-valued functions on
K
, square integrable with respect to the
measure
dx
, is denoted by
L^{2}(K)
. The inner product in this space is given by

\langle f,g\rangle=\int\limits_{K}f(x)\overline{g(x)}dx~~~~~\text{ for }f,g\in L^{2}(K).

Any
x\in\mathfrak{B}^{s}
can be uniquely expressed as

x=\sum\limits_{n=s}^{\infty}a_{n}\mathfrak{p}^{n},~~~~~~~~~a_{n}\in\mathfrak{U},~a_{s}\neq 0

where
\mathfrak{U}=\{c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{q-1}\}
is a fixed full set of coset representatives of
\mathfrak{B}
in
\mathfrak{D}
. In this case,
|x|=q^{-s}
. As
K
is a local field, consider
\chi
be a fixed non-trivial, unitary, continuous character on
K^{+}
which is trivial on
\mathfrak{D}
but non-trivial on
\mathfrak{B}^{-1}
. Fix
y\in K
, we define
\chi_{y}(x)=\chi(yx)
for
x\in K
. This character satisfy following important result given by Taibleson
[
8
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
\{u(n)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is complete list of distinct coset representatives of
\mathfrak{D}
in
K^{+}
, then
\{\chi_{u(n)}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a complete list of distinct characters on
\mathfrak{D}
. Further, it is a complete orthonormal system on
\mathfrak{D}
.

The Fourier series of
f\in L^{1}(\mathfrak{D})
is defined as
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(u(n))\chi_{u(n)}
where Fourier coefficients are given by
\hat{f}(u(n)):=\int\limits_{\mathfrak{D}}f(x)\overline{\chi_{u(n)}(x)}dx
.

Definition 2.2
.

(Fourier transform).
For
f\in L^{1}(K)\cap L^{2}(K)
, the Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}
is defined by

\mathcal{F}[f](\xi):=\hat{f}(\xi)=\int\limits_{K}f(x)\overline{\chi_{\xi}(x)}dx=\int\limits_{K}f(x)\chi(-\xi x)dx.

This Fourier transform satisfy following

•

\mathcal{F}:L^{1}(K)\rightarrow L^{\infty}(K)
is a bounded linear transformation satisfying
\|\hat{f}\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{1}
.

•

If
f\in L^{1}(K)
then
\hat{f}
is uniformly continuous on
K
.

•

Plancheral Theorem :

\|\hat{f}\|_{2}=\|f\|_{2}
, for
f\in L^{2}(K)
.

•

Parseval Theorem :

\langle f,g\rangle=\langle\hat{f},\hat{g}\rangle
, for all
f,g\in L^{2}(K)
.

•

Riemann-Lebesgue Theorem :
If
f\in L^{1}(K)
, then
\hat{f}(\xi)\rightarrow 0
as
|\xi|\rightarrow\infty
.

In order to impose ‘natural’ order on
\{u(n)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
used to develop the Fourier series theory on
L^{2}(\mathfrak{D})
, we use a set
\{1=\epsilon_{0},\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{c-1}\}\subset\mathfrak{D}^{*}=\mathfrak{D}/\mathfrak{B}
such that span
\{\epsilon_{0},\ldots,\epsilon_{c-1}\}\cong GF(q)
. Let
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. We now give an example of a character
\chi
on
K
that is trivial on
\mathfrak{D}
but non-trivial on
\mathfrak{B}^{-1}
as follows
[
10
]

\chi(\epsilon_{k}\mathfrak{p}^{-j})=\begin{cases}e^{2\pi\frac{i}{p}}&\mbox{if $k=0,~j=1$};\\
1&\mbox{if $k=1,\ldots,c-1$ or $j\neq 1$.}\end{cases}

Definition 2.3
.

Any
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cap[0,q)
can be written as
n=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{c-1}a_{k}p^{k},~~~~~~~\text{where }0\leq a_{k}<p.

Define
u(n)=(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{c-1}a_{k}\epsilon_{k})\mathfrak{p}^{-1}
.
Note that
\{u(n)\}_{n=0}^{q-1}
is a complete set of coset representatives of
\mathfrak{D}
in
\mathfrak{B}^{-1}
. Now for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cap[q,\infty)
, we have,
n=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}b_{k}q^{k},~~~~~~~0\leq b_{k}<q.

Define
u(n)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{s}\mathfrak{p}^{-k}u(b_{k})
.

This defines
u(n)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. In general that
u
is not linear but satisfies

u(rq^{k}+s)=\mathfrak{p}^{-k}u(r)+u(s)~~\text{where }r,k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},0\leq s<q^{k}.

In the following proposition, Behera and Jahan
[
5
]
proved important properties of aforementioned
u(n)
which has repeated application rest of the paper.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
u(n)
be defined as above for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

(a)

u(n)=0
if and only if
n=0
. If
k\geq 1
, then we have
|u(n)|=q^{k}
if and only if
q^{k-1}\leq n<q^{k}
.

(b)

\{u(n)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}=\{-u(n)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.

(c)

For a fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have
\{u(m)+u(n)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}=\{u(n)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.

It clearly follows from Proposition
2.4
that
\{u(n)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a subgroup of
K^{+}
. Since
\bigcup\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathfrak{p}^{-j}\mathfrak{D}=K
, we shall use
\mathfrak{p}^{-1}
as the dilation and since
\{u(n)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a complete list of distinct coset representatives of
\mathfrak{D}
in
K
, we shall use
\{u(n)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
as the translation set in rest of this article.

A function
f\in L^{2}(K)
is said to be integral periodic if

f(\cdot+u(n))=f(\cdot),\hskip 14.22636pt\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

2.2.
Multiframelets

Definition 2.5
.

(Multiwavelet).
A set of functions
\Psi=\{\psi^{1},\ldots,\psi^{L}\}\subset L^{2}(K)
is said to be a multiwavelet of order
L
if affine system

\mathcal{W}(\Psi):=\{\psi^{l}_{j,t}:=q^{\frac{j}{2}}\psi^{l}(\mathfrak{p}^{-j}\cdot-u(t)):j\in\mathbb{Z},~t\in\mathbb{N}_{0},~l\in\mathfrak{L}\}

is an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}(K)
.

Note that for
f\in L^{2}(K)
and
\xi\in K
,

\widehat{f_{j,t}}{(\xi)}=q^{-\frac{j}{2}}\chi_{u(t)}(-\mathfrak{p}^{j}\xi)\hat{f}(\mathfrak{p}^{j}\xi).

Definition 2.6
.

(Multiwavelet Set).
A measurable set
W\subset K
is said to be a multiwavelet set of order
L
if
W=\bigcup\limits_{l=1}^{L}W_{l}
for measurable sets
W_{l}
and
\hat{\psi}^{l}=\mathds{1}_{W_{l}}
where
\{\psi^{1},\ldots,\psi^{L}\}
is a multiwavelet for
L^{2}(K)
.

When
L=1
,
W
is simply said to be a wavelet set.

Definition 2.7
.

(Multiframelet).
For a set of basic functions
\mathcal{F}=\{f^{(1)},\ldots,f^{(L)}\}\subset L^{2}(K)
, affine system
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{F})
is said to be a multiframelet of order
L
if there exist finite
A,B>0
so that for all
g\in L^{2}(K)
we have,

(1)

A\left\|g\right\|^{2}\leq\sum\limits_{l\in\mathfrak{L}}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|\langle g,f^{(l)}_{j,t}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\left\|g\right\|^{2}.

When
L=1
, it is simply said to be a framelet.
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{F})
is said to be a besselet with bound
B
if it satisfy later inequality of equation (
1
).
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{F})
is called a tight multiframelet if
A=B
and Parseval multiframelet if
A=1=B
in equation (
1
). It is well-known that
\mathcal{W}(\mathcal{F})
is a Parseval multiframelet for
L^{2}(K)
if and only if
g=\sum\limits_{l\in\mathfrak{L}}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\langle g,f^{(l)}_{j,t}\rangle f^{(l)}_{j,t}
for all
g\in L^{2}(K)
. Therefore, Parseval multiframelet is a generalization of the concept of orthonormal basis to a system having no minimality condition. Similar to multiwavelet set, we define multiframelet set.

We now give three equivalent conditions for Parseval multiframelet set.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Psi=\{\psi^{(1)},\ldots\psi^{(L)}\}\subset L^{2}(K)
with

.

(2)

\{\mathfrak{p}^{j}W_{m}:j\in\mathbb{Z},~m=1,\ldots,L\}
is a measurable partition of
K
a.e.

(3)

\int\limits_{\bigcup\limits_{m=1}^{L}W_{m}}\frac{1}{|\xi|}d\xi=\frac{q-1}{q}

Using (1) of Theorem
2.10
and applying Parseval theorem, we obtain, for all
f\in L^{2}(K)

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=1}^{L}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|\langle f,\psi^{(m)}_{j,t}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=1}^{L}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|\langle\hat{f},\widehat{\psi^{(m)}_{j,t}}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=1}^{L}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\left\lvert~q^{-\frac{j}{2}}\int\limits_{K}\hat{f}(\xi)\overline{\chi_{u(t)}(-\mathfrak{p}^{j}\xi)\mathds{1}_{W_{m}}(\mathfrak{p}^{j}\xi)}d\xi\right\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=1}^{L}\sum\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}q^{-j}\left\lvert~\int\limits_{\mathfrak{p}^{-j}W_{m}}\hat{f}(\xi)\chi_{u(t)}(\mathfrak{p}^{j}\xi)d\xi\right\rvert^{2}.

4.
Multiresolution Analysis

Wavelet on local field can be constructed from a multiresolution analysis (MRA) similar to
\mathbb{R}
. Jiang et al.
[
9
]
define an MRA and construct orthogonal wavelet on local field of positive characteristic. We now discuss MRA and its properties.

Definition 4.1
.

(Multiresolution analysis).
Let
K
be a local field of positive characteristic. A multiresolution analysis of
L^{2}(K)
is an sequence
\{V_{j}:j\in\mathbb{Z}\}
of closed subspaces of
L^{2}(K)
satisfying following conditions

(i)

V_{j}\subset V_{j+1}
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(ii)

\overline{\bigcup\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{j}}=L^{2}(K)
,

(iii)

\bigcap\limits_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{j}=\{0\}
,

(iv)

f\in V_{j}
if and only if
f(\mathfrak{p}^{-1}\cdot)\in V_{j+1}
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(v)

there exist a function
\phi\in V_{0}
, namely the scaling function, such that
\{\phi(\cdot-u(t)):t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
is an orthonormal basis for
V_{0}
.

If we replace (v) by (vi) as follows, then the sequence
\{V_{j}:j\in\mathbb{Z}\}
is said to be a Parseval multiresolution analysis (PMRA).

(vi)

there is a function
\phi\in V_{0}
, namely the Parseval scaling function, such that
\{\phi(\cdot-u(t)):t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
is an Parseval frame for
V_{0}
.

A measurable set
S\subset K
is a scaling set/Parseval scaling set if
\hat{\phi}=\mathds{1}_{S}
for some scaling function/Parseval scaling function
\phi
.

Theorem 4.2
.

[
5
]

A function
\phi\in L^{2}(K)
is a scaling function for a multiresolution analysis of
L^{2}(K)
if and only if

(1)

\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\lvert\hat{\phi}(\xi+u(t))\rvert^{2}=1
for
\xi\in\mathfrak{D}
a.e.

(2)

\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\lvert\hat{\phi}(\mathfrak{p}^{j}\xi)\rvert=1
for
\xi\in K
a.e.

(3)

there exist an integral periodic function
m_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{D})
such that

\hat{\phi}(\xi)=m_{0}(\mathfrak{p}\xi)\hat{\phi}(\mathfrak{p}\xi)\hskip 14.22636pt\text{for }\xi\in K\text{ a.e.}

We deduce the following result with the help of Theorem
4.2
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
S
be the scaling set correspond to scaling function
\phi
of an MRA in
L^{2}(K)
. Then
\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\mathfrak{p}^{-j}S=K
.

Proof.

From (2) of Theorem
4.2
, we have, for
\xi\in K
a.e.

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\lvert\hat{\phi}(\mathfrak{p}^{j}\xi)\rvert=1

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\lvert\mathds{1}_{S}(\mathfrak{p}^{j}\xi)\rvert=1

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\mathds{1}_{\mathfrak{p}^{-j}S}(\xi)=1

\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\xi\in\mathfrak{p}^{-j}S\text{ as }j\to\infty

So
K=\mathfrak{p}^{-j}S
a.e. when
j
is large enough.

It is also to be noted from (1) of Theorem
4.2
that
\{S+u(t):t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
is a partition of
\mathfrak{D}
.
∎

Shukla et al.
[

]
characterize of Parseval scaling function in term of Parseval scaling set in following theorem.

Theorem 4.4
.

A function
\phi
such that
\hat{\phi}=\mathds{1}_{S}
, for some measurable
S\subset K
, is a Parseval scaling function of a PMRA if and only if

(a)

\{S+u(t):t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
is a measurable partition of a subset of

,

(b)

(c)

S\subset\mathfrak{p}^{-j}S
.

Definition 4.5
.

(Dimension function).
Suppose
\Psi=\{\psi^{m}:m=1,2,\ldots,L\}\subset L^{2}(K)
is a multiwavelet for
L^{2}(K)
. The dimension function of
\Psi
is defined as

D_{\Psi}(\xi)=\sum\limits_{m=1}^{L}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|\widehat{\psi^{m}}(\mathfrak{p}^{-j}(\xi+u(t)))|^{2},~~~\text{for }\xi\in K\text{ a.e.}

It is to be observe that if
\psi^{1},\ldots,\psi^{L}\in L^{2}(K)
, then

\int\limits_{\mathfrak{D}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|\widehat{\psi^{m}}(\mathfrak{p}^{-j}(\xi+u(t)))|^{2}d\xi=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}q^{-j}\int\limits_{K}|\widehat{\psi^{m}}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi.

Gripenberg
[
14
]
and Wang
[
15
]
independently proved that a wavelet is an MRA wavelet if and only if its dimension function is 1 a.e. in real setting.

Theorem 4.6
.

A multiwavelet set
W=W_{1}\cup\ldots\cup W_{q-1}\subset K
induces an MRA if and only if
\bigcup\limits_{m=1}^{q-1}\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigcup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\{\mathfrak{p}^{j}W_{m}+u(t)\}=K
a.e.

Proof.

As
W
is a multiwavelet set, there exists a multiwavelet
\Psi=\{\psi^{m}:m=1,\ldots,q-1\}
such that
\widehat{\psi^{m}}=\mathds{1}_{W_{m}}
.

We know from
[
3
]
that a multiwavelet is an MRA multiwavelet if and only if
D_{\Psi}\equiv 1
on
K
a.e. Therefore for
\xi\in K
a.e

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=1}^{q-1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mathds{1}_{W_{m}}(\mathfrak{p}^{-j}(\xi+u(t)))=1

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=1}^{q-1}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mathds{1}_{\mathfrak{p}^{j}W_{m}+u(t)}(\xi)=1

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\bigcup\limits_{m=1}^{q-1}\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}\bigcup\limits_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\{\mathfrak{p}^{j}W_{m}+u(t)\}=K.

This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Super-wavelet

In this section we discuss different properties of super-wavelet and associated set.

Definition 5.1
.

(Super-wavelet).
Let
\Theta=\{\eta^{(1)},\ldots,\eta^{(m)}\}\subset L^{2}(K)
such that
\mathcal{W}(\Theta)
is a Parseval multiframelet for
L^{2}(K)
. We call
\Theta
a super-wavelet of length
m
if

\mathcal{B}(\Theta):=\{\bigoplus\limits_{i=1}^{m}q^{\frac{j}{2}}\eta^{(i)}(\mathfrak{p}^{-j}\cdot-u(t)):j\in\mathbb{Z},t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}

is an orthonormal basis for
\bigoplus\limits_{m}L^{2}(K)
. Each
\eta^{(i)}
is called a component of the super-wavelet. Also,
\Theta
is called a Parseval frame super-wavelet if
\mathcal{B}(\Theta)
is a Parseval frame for
\bigoplus\limits_{m}L^{2}(K)
.

Definition 5.2
.

Two Parseval frame super-wavelets
(\eta^{1},\ldots,\eta^{m})
and
(\zeta^{1},\ldots,\zeta^{n})
are said to be equivalent if there exists a unitary operator

U:\bigoplus\limits_{j=1}^{m}L^{2}(K)\to\bigoplus\limits_{j=1}^{n}L^{2}(K)

such that for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

U(\eta_{k,l}^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\eta_{k,l}^{m})=\zeta_{k,l}^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\zeta_{k,l}^{n}.

Definition 5.3
.

A Parseval framelet
\Upsilon
is said to be a
n
-decomposable, where
n>1
, if
\Upsilon
is equivalent to a Parseval frame super-wavelet of length
n
.

.

[

]

Let
(\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{m})
and

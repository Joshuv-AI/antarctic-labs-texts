Elements of random analysis about the gamma generalized hyperbolic distribution Levy stochastic process
Nafy Ngom, Aladji Babacar Niang, Soumaila Dembelé, Gane Samb Lo

Abstract
. In this paper, we study some aspects on random analysis on the Lévy stochastic processes with margins following generalized hyperbolic distributions generated by gamma laws. In particular we study the boundedness of its total variations and the quadratic variations. Next we give an empirical construction that enables the graphical representation of the paths of such stochastic processes. Comparisons with the Brownian motions are considered. 1.
Introduction

In the recent years, the generalized hyperbolic distribution (
GHD
) is attracting much attention in various branches of Statistics and Probability theory, especially in statistical estimation and in finance modeling (see
McNeil et al. 2019
). This class of laws is seen as a credible alternative to the Gaussian assumption of data both in skewness and in shape. Also, as infinitely divisible laws, the Lévy processes they generate began to be studied (see
Applebaum 2004
).

To be able to expose the aim of this paper and to describe its motivation, we need to develop a little the three aspects aforementioned in the next line.

1.1.
Infinitely divisibility laws or random variables

The probability law of a real-valued random variable
X
associated to the characteristic function
\psi_{X}
is infinitely divisible, denoted as [
idecomp
], if and only if it satisfies one of the criteria below.

(Criterion 1) A random variable
X
is infinitely divisible or infinitely decomposable (
idecomp
) if and only if for any
n\geq 1
, it can be equal in law to a sum of
n
independent and identically distributed random variables.

(Criterion 2) A random variable
X
of characteristic function
\psi_{X}
(or of third moment generating function
\phi_{X}
if
X\geq 0
) is
idecomp
if and only if: for any
n\geq 1
, there exists a characteristic function
\psi
(or a third moment generating function
\phi
of a non-negative random variable) such that

\psi_{X}=\psi^{n}\ \ (or\ \ \phi_{X}=\phi^{n}).

(Criterion 3) A random variable
X
of characteristic function
\psi_{X}
(or of third moment generating function
\phi_{X}
if
X\geq 0
) is
idecomp
if and only if: for any
n\geq 1
,
\psi_{X}^{1/n}
is a characteristic function (or
\phi_{X}^{1/n}
is a third moment generating function).

Examples
. Let us consider three classical
idecomp
probability laws: normal law
U_{1}=\mathcal{N}(m,\sigma^{2})
, translation of a Poisson law
\mathcal{P}(\lambda)
by
a\geq 0
:
U_{2}=a+\mathcal{P}(\lambda)=:\mathcal{P}(a,\lambda)
, and translation of a gamma law
\gamma(a,\beta)
by
a\geq 0
:
U_{3}=a+\gamma(a,\beta)=:\gamma(a,a,\beta)
with characteristic functions, for
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\displaystyle\psi_{U_{1}}(t)=\exp(imt-\sigma^{2}t^{2}/2),\ \ \ \psi_{U_{2}}(t)=\exp\left(iat+\lambda(e^{it}-1)\right)

\displaystyle\psi_{U_{3}}(t)=e^{iat}\left(1-it/\beta\right)^{-a}.

By taking the powers at
1/n
,
n\geq 1
, we get for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\psi_{U_{1}}(t)^{1/n}=\exp\left(i\{m/n\}t-(\sigma/\sqrt{n})^{2}t^{2}/2\right),\ \ \psi_{U_{2}}(t)^{1/n}=\exp\left(i\{a/n\}t+\{\lambda/n\}(e^{it}-1)\right),

\ \ \ \ \ \psi_{U_{3}}(t)^{1/n}=e^{i\{a/n\}t}\left(1-it/\beta\right)^{\{-a/n\}}.

By identifying the characteristic functions, we can see that the
\psi_{U_{i}}(t)^{1/n}
,
1\leq i\leq 3
, are
cha.f
’s of random variables of law
\mathcal{N}(m/n,\sigma^{2}/n)
,
\mathcal{P}(a/n,\lambda/n)
and
\gamma(a/n,a/n,\beta)
respectively. So, these probability laws are
idecomp
.

Full exposure of
idecomp
laws are treated in
Loève 1997
,
Feller 1968b
,
Zolotarev 1986
, etc., and the readers are directed to them for getting deeper properties.

1.2.
Generalized Hyperbolic Distributions (
GHD
)

A real valued-random variable
X
is said to follow a normal variance mixture (
NVM
) law if it is of the form

(1.1)

X=\mu+\sigma Z\sqrt{W},

where
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
\sigma>0
,
Z\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)

Z
independent of
W

W
is some positive random variable.

As in
McNeil et al. 2019
, we consider a few number of distributions for
W
:

a) The gamma distribution
\gamma(a,\beta)
of parameters
a>0
and
\beta>0
of probability density function (
pdf
)

f_{1}\left(x\right)=\frac{\beta^{a}}{\Gamma\left(a\right)}x^{a-1}e^{-\beta x},\ x>0;

b) Inverse gamma distribution
Ig(a,\beta)
of
a>0
and
\beta>0
of
pdf

f_{2}\left(x\right)=\frac{\beta^{a}}{\Gamma\left(a\right)}x^{-(a+1)}e^{-\beta/x},\ \ x>0;

c) The generalized inverse gamma
\textit{GIG}(a,b,c)
distribution of parameters
a\geq 0
,
b\geq 0
,
c\geq 0
of
pdf

f_{3}(x)=\frac{1}{C\left(a,b,c\right)}x^{a-1}e^{-\left(bx+c/x\right)/2},\ x>0,

where

C\left(a,b,c\right)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}x^{a-1}e^{-\left(\left(bx+c/x\right)/2\right)}dx.

Some authors use the Bessel functions
K_{a}
to express the constant of the distribution, and we have

\frac{1}{C\left(a,b,c\right)}=\frac{b^{-a}\left(bc\right)^{\frac{a}{2}}}{2K_{a}\left(\left(bc\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)},

with

K_{a}\left(\left(bc\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)=\frac{c^{-a}\left(bc\right)^{\frac{a}{b}}}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}x^{a-1}e^{-\left(\left(bx+c/x\right)/2\right)}dx.

The parameters
a
,
b
and
c
satisfy the conditions

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0:b>0\ \ and\ c>0,

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 0:b>0\ \ and\ c\geq 0,

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle 0:b\geq 0\ and\ c>0.

We study the cases (

\gamma\left(a,b/2\right)
of

f_{4}\left(x\right)=\frac{(b/2)^{a}}{\Gamma\left(a\right)}x^{a-1}e^{-bx/2},\ \ x\geq 0

and the second to the following inverse gamma

distribution of

f_{2,1}\left(x\right)=\frac{1}{D\left(a,c\right)}x^{-\left(a+1\right)}e^{-c/2x},\ \ x>0,

with
a>0
,
c>0

D\left(a,c\right)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}x^{-\left(a+1\right)}e^{-c/2x}\ dx.

We also have

D\left(a,c\right)=\left(\frac{c}{2}\right)^{-a}\Gamma^{-}\left(a\right),\ \ \Gamma^{-}\left(a\right)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}x^{-\left(a+1\right)}e^{-c/2x}\ dx,\ \ a>0,c>0.

For any random variable

The

The distributions of

1.3.
Lévy processes

By definition, a Lévy process
\biggr(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P},(K_{t})_{(t\geq 0)}\biggr)
is defined as a stochastic process with marginal laws
\{K(t),\ t\geq 0\}
such that:

(ML)
\forall t\geq 0
,
L(t)\sim K(t)
, with
L(0)=0

a.s
;

(II)
\forall k\geq 0
,
\forall\ 0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\cdots<t_{k}
, the
r.v
’s
L(t_{j})-L(t_{j-1})
are independent;

(IS)
\forall 0\leq s<t
,
L(t)-L(s)\sim K(t-s)
;

(CS) The process
\{L(t),\ t\geq 0\}
is stochastically continuous at zero.

NB.
Each marginal law
K(t)
is an infinitely decomposable (
idecomp
) law.

Examples
. Here are three examples. (1) The Brownian process
(B_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
K(t)=\mathcal{N}(0,t)
,
t\geq 0
; (2) The simple Poisson process
(N_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with
K(t)=\mathcal{P}(t)
,
t\geq 0
; (3) The
\gamma(a,b)
-process
(G_{a,b,t})_{t\geq 0}
with
K(t)=\gamma(at,b)
.

For Lévy processes, we mainly refer to
Applebaum 2004
.

2

Antoine-Marie Bogso, Olivier Menoukeu Pamen

Abstract
. We prove path-by-path uniqueness of solution to hyperbolic stochastic partial differential equations when the drift coefficient is the difference of two componentwise monotone Borel measurable functions of spatial linear growth. The Yamada-Watanabe principle for SDE driven by Brownian sheet then allows to derive strong uniqueness for such equation and thus extending the results in [Bogso, Dieye and Menoukeu Pamen, Elect. J. Probab., 27:1-26, 2022] and [Nualart and Tindel, Potential Anal., 7(3):661–680, 1997]. Assuming that the drift is globally bounded, we show that the unique strong solution is Malliavin differentiable. The case of spatial linear growth drift coefficient is also studied.

1.
Introduction

The existence, uniqueness and Malliavin differentiability of strong solutions of SDEs on the plane with smooth coefficients have been obtained in several settings of varying generality. However there are not many results when the coefficient of the such equation are singular. The purpose of the present paper is two-fold: first we obtain the existence and uniqueness of strong solution of the following integral form equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle X_{s,t}=\xi+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}b(s_{1},t_{1},X_{s_{1},t_{1}})\mathrm{d}s_{1}\mathrm{d}t_{1}+W_{s,t},\text{ for }(s,t)\in\Gamma,

when the drift
b
is the difference of two componentwise monotone functions and of spatial linear growth. We address this problem by using the Yamada-Watanabe argument for SDEs driven by Brownian sheet derived in
[
34
]
(see also
[
44
]
,
[
42
, Remark 2]
), that is, we combine weak existence and pathwise uniqueness to obtain the existence of a unique strong solution. More particularly, we replace the pathwise uniqueness by a stronger notion of uniqueness, namely, the path-by-path uniqueness introduced in
[
12
]
(see also
[
17
]
) in the ecase of SDEs driven by one-parameter Brownian motion. This notion was introduced in
[
4
]
for the two parameter process, as follows:

Definition 1.1
.

We say that the path-by-path uniqueness of solutions to (
1.1
) holds when there exists a full
\mathbb{P}
-measure set
\Omega_{0}\subset\Omega
such that for all
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
the following statement is true: there exists at most one function
y\in\mathcal{V}
which satisfies

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{0}^{T}|b(s,t,y_{s,t})|\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t<\infty,\text{ }\partial y=x,\text{ for some }x\in\partial\mathcal{V}\text{ and }T>0

and

(1.2)

\displaystyle y_{s,t}=x+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}b(s_{1},t_{1},y_{s_{1},t_{1}})\mathrm{d}s_{1}\mathrm{d}t_{1}+W_{s,t}(\omega),\text{ }\forall\,(s,t)\in[0,T]^{2}.

The study of path-by-path uniqueness is motivated by the problem of regularisation by noise of random ordinary (or partial) differential equations (ODEs or PDEs). In the case of of SDEs driven by Brownian motion, path-by-path uniqueness of equation (
1.1
) was proved in
[
12
]
assuming that the drift is bounded and measurable, and the diffusion is constant. This result was extended to the non-constant diffusion in
[
13
]
using rough path analysis. There has now been several generalisation of this result. The authors in
[
3
]
proved a Sobolev regularity of solutions to the linear stochastic transport and continuity equations with drift in critical
L^{p}
spaces. Such a result does not hold for the corresponding deterministic equations. In
[
6
]
, the authors analysed the regularisation by noise for a non-Lipschitz stochastic heat equation and proved path-by-path uniqueness for any initial condition in a certain class of a set of probability one. In
[
2
]
, the path-by-path uniqueness for transport equations driven by
the fractional Brownian motion of Hurst index
H<1/2
with bounded and integrable vector-fields
is investigated. In
[
8
;
18
]
the authors solved the regularisation by noise problem from the point of view of additive perturbations. In particular, the work
[
8
]
considered generic perturbations without any specific probabilistic setting whereas authors in
[
1
]
construct a new Gaussian noise of fractional nature and proved that it has a strong regularising effect on a large class of ODEs. More recently, the regularisation by noise problem for ODEs with vector fields given by Schwartz distributions was studied in
[
20
]
. It was also proved that if one perturbs such an equation by adding an infinitely regularising path, then it has a unique solution. Let us also mention the recent work
[
24
]
in which the authors looked at multidimensional SDEs with distributional drift driven by symmetric
\alpha
-stable Lévy processes for
\alpha\in(1,2]
. In all of the above mentioned works, the driving noise considered are one parameter processes.

Our method to prove path-by-path uniqueness follows as in
[
4
]
. We show the path-by-path uniqueness on
\Gamma_{0}=[0,1]^{2}
. More precisely, we consider the integral equation

(1.3)

\displaystyle X_{s,t}=\xi+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}b(s_{1},t_{1},X_{s_{1},t_{1}})\mathrm{d}s_{1}\mathrm{d}t_{1}+W_{s,t}\text{ for }(s,t)\in\Gamma_{0},

where the drift is of spatial linear growth. It is shown in
[
4
, Section 1]
(see also
[
12
, Section 1]
) that path-by-path uniqueness of solutions to (
1.3
) holds if and only if, with probability one, there is no nontrivial solution
u\in\mathcal{V}^{1}_{0}
of

(1.4)

\displaystyle u(s,t)=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}\{b(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}}+u(s_{1},t_{1}))-b(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\mathrm{d}t_{1},\text{ for }(s,t)\in\Gamma_{0}.

This is the statement of Theorem
2.10
which is extended to unbounded monotone drifts in Theorem
2.9
.
The proof of Theorem
2.10
relies on some estimates for an averaging operator along the sheet (see Lemma
2.3
). This result plays a key role in the proof of a Gronwall type lemma (see Lemma
2.14
) which enables us to prove path-by-path uniqueness of solutions to (
1.3
). The latter combined with the weak existence yield the existence of a unique strong solution. Note that when the drift coefficient is , similar result can be found in
[
4
;
33
]
.

Secondly, in this paper, we prove Malliavin smoothness of the unique solution to the SDE (
1.3
). When the coefficient are smooth, the authors in
[
31
;
32
]
showed existence, uniqueness, Malliavin differentiability and smoothness of density of solutions to SDEs on the plane. Here, assuming that the drift is the difference of two componentwise nondecreasing functions, we show that the solution is Malliavin differentiable. In the one parameter case, the Malliavin differentiablity of solutions to SDEs with bounded and measeurable coefficients was studied in
[
28
]
under an additional commutativity assumption. The later assumption was removed in
[
27
]
. It is worth mentioning that in the above work, the Malliavin smoothness of the unique solutions to the SDEs with rough coefficients and driven by Brownian motion is obtained as a byproduct of the method used to study existence and uniqueness. This technique was introduced in
[
36
]
and has now been extensively utilised; see for example the work
[
19
]
for the case of singular SDEs driven by Lévy noise,
[
26
]
for the case of random coefficients and
[
2
;
1
]
for the case of singular SDEs driven by fractional noise. In order to prove the Malliavin differentiability of the solution to the SDE (
1.3
), we take advantage of Gaussian white noise theory and a local time-space integration formula provided in
[
5
, Proposition 3.1]
to show that the sequence of approximating sequence of solution converges strongly in

to the solution of the SDE (compare with
[
27

) and we use a compactness criteria given in
[

to conclude.

Equation (
1.1
) can also be written in a differential form as the following hyperbolic stochastic partial differential equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\partial^{2}X_{s,t}}{\partial s\partial t}=b(s,t,X_{s,t})+\dot{W},&(s,t)\in\Gamma,\\
&\\
\partial X=\xi,\end{array}\right.

where
\partial X
is the restriction of
X
to the boundary
\partial\Gamma=\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\{0\}
of
\Gamma:=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}
,
b:\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is Borel measurable,
\dot{W}=(\dot{W}^{(1)},\ldots,\dot{W}^{(d)})
is a
d
-dimensional white noise on
\Gamma
given on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and
(s,t)\longmapsto\xi_{s,t}(\omega)
is continuous on
\partial\Gamma
for all
\omega\in\Omega
. Recall that by a
d
-dimensional white noise on
\Gamma
we mean a mean-zero Gaussian process
\dot{W}=(\dot{W}^{(1)},\ldots,\dot{W}^{(d)})
indexed by the Borel field
\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)
on
\Gamma
with covariance functions

\mathbb{E}\Big[\dot{W}^{(i)}(A)\dot{W}^{(j)}(B)\Big]=\delta_{i,j}|A\cap B|,\quad\forall\,A,B\in\mathcal{B}(\Gamma)

where
|\cdot|
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\Gamma
and
\delta_{i,j}=1
if
i=j
and
\delta_{i,j}=0
otherwise. The process
W=\left(W_{s,t}:=\dot{W}([0,s]\times[0,t]),(s,t)\in\Gamma\right)
is mean-zero Gaussian process with covariance functions

\mathbb{E}\Big[W^{(i)}_{s,t}W^{(j)}_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}\Big]=\delta_{i,j}(s\wedge s^{\prime})(t\wedge t^{\prime}),\quad\forall\,(s,t),(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\Gamma.

By the Kolmogorov continuity theorem, there exists a continuous version of
W
, still denoted by
W
, which is a
d
-dimensional Brownian sheet. We consider a
nondecreasing and right-continuous family
\mathbb{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{s,t})
of sub-
\sigma
-algebras of
\mathcal{F}
each of which contains all negligible sets in
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
such that
W
and
\xi
are
\mathbb{F}
-adapted, that is
W_{s,t}
(respectively,
\xi_{s,t}
) is
\mathcal{F}_{s,t}
-measurable for every
(s,t)\in\Gamma
(respectively,
(s,t)\in\partial\Gamma
).
We refer the reader to Khoshnevisan
[
23
]
for a complete
analysis on multi-parameter processes and their applications.

Equation (
1
) is a particular case of the quasilinear stochastic hyperbolic differential equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{\partial^{2}X_{s,t}}{\partial s\partial t}=b(s,t,X_{s,t})+a(s,t,X_{s,t})\dot{W},&\quad(s,t)\in\Gamma\\
&\\
\partial X=\xi,&\end{array}\right.

where
a:\,\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a Borel measurable matrix function. A formal
\frac{\pi}{4}
rotation transforms (
1
) into the following nonlinear stochastic wave equation

(1.11)

\displaystyle\dfrac{\partial^{2}Y_{\rho,\theta}}{\partial\rho^{2}}-\dfrac{\partial^{2}Y_{\rho,\theta}}{\partial\theta^{2}}=\tilde{b}(\rho,\theta,Y_{\rho,\theta})+\tilde{a}(\rho,\theta,Y_{\rho,\theta})\dot{\tilde{W}},\quad(\rho,\theta)\in\tilde{\Gamma},

with the Goursat-Darboux type boundary condition
\partial Y=\tilde{\xi}
,
where
\tilde{\Gamma}=\{(\rho,\theta):\,\theta\geq 0\text{ and }|\rho|\leq\theta\}
,
\dot{\tilde{W}}
is a
d
-dimensional white noise on
\tilde{\Gamma}
,
\tilde{b}(\rho,\theta,y)=b(\frac{\theta+\rho}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{\theta-\rho}{\sqrt{2}},y)
(the same applies to
\tilde{a}
),
Y_{\rho,\theta}=X_{\frac{\theta+\rho}{\sqrt{2}},\frac{\theta-\rho}{\sqrt{2}}}
,
\tilde{\xi}_{\theta,\theta}=\xi_{\sqrt{2}\theta,0}
and
\tilde{\xi}_{-\theta,\theta}=\xi_{0,\sqrt{2}\theta}
. The
\frac{\pi}{4}
rotation has been used by Carmona and Nualart
[
7
]
(see also
[
16
, Section 0]
and
[
38
, Section 1]
) to prove existence and uniqueness of solution to (
1.11
) under a different boundary condition when
\tilde{a}
and
\tilde{b}
are time-homogeneous.

(
1
) can also be seen as a noisy analog of the so-called Darboux problem given by

(1.12)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}y}{\partial s\partial t}=b\Big(s,t,y,\frac{\partial y}{\partial s},\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}\Big)\quad\text{for }(s,t)\in[0,T]\times[0,T],

with the initial conditions

(1.13)

\displaystyle y(0,t)=\sigma(t)\,\text{ on }[0,T]\,\text{ and }\,y(s,0)=\tau(s)\,\text{ on }[0,T],

where
\sigma
and
\tau
are absolutely continuous on
[0,T]
.

Using Caratheodory’s theory
of differential equations, Deimling
[
14
]
proved an existence theorem for the system (
1.12
)-(
1.13
)
when

b
is Borel measurable in the first two variables and bounded and continuous in the last three variables. Hence the results obtained here can also be seen as a generalisation to the stochastic setting of the above mentioned one.

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows: In Section
2
, we provide a path-by-path uniqueness result for (
1.3
) when the drift

is of linear growth. In Section
3
, we study the Malliavin differentiability of the strong solution to (
1.3
). We show that this solution is Malliavin differentiable for uniformly bounded drifts and when the drift

is of linear growth, we obtain Malliavin differentiability of the solution only for sufficiently small time parameters.

2.
Existence and uniqueness results

In this section, we show that the SDE (
1.3
) has a unique strong solution. Our approach is based on the Yamada-Watanabe principle introduced in
[
34
]
. As pointed out earlier, instead of showing the weak existence and pathwise uniqueness, we show weak existence and path by path uniqueness (which implies pathwise uniqueness as shown in
[
8
]
). The following preliminary results that have been obtained by applying a local time-space integration formula for Brownian sheets (see
[
5
]
for more information) are needed to show path-by-path uniqueness.

\displaystyle=

We first recall some basic facts on Malliavin calculus for Wiener functionals on the plane which can be found in

[

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be the canonical space associated to the
d

\Omega

\omega:\,\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
which vanish on the axes,
\mathbb{P}

H=\Big\{\omega\in\Omega:\,\text{there exists }\dot{\omega}\in L^{2}(\Gamma,\mathbb{R}^{d})\text{ such that }\omega(s,t)=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}\dot{\omega}(s_{1},t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1},\text{ for any }(s,t)\in\Gamma\Big\}.

\langle\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\rangle_{H}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}\int_{\Gamma}\dot{\omega}_{1}^{(i)}(s_{1},t_{1})\dot{\omega}_{2}^{(i)}(s_{1},t_{1})\mathrm{d}s_{1}\mathrm{d}t_{1},

Every smooth functional

DF(h)=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}^{(j)}}(\omega(s_{1},t_{1}),\ldots,\omega(s_{n},t_{n}))h^{(j)}(s_{i},t_{i})=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d}\int_{\Gamma}\xi_{j}(q,r)\dot{h}^{(j)}(q,r)\mathrm{d}q\mathrm{d}r,

In order to apply the above result, we consider the fractional Sobolev space:

\mathbb{G}^{2}_{\beta}(U;\mathbb{R}):=\Big\{g\in L^{2}(U,\mathbb{R}):\,\int_{U}\int_{U}\frac{|g(u)-g(u^{\prime})|^{2}}{|u-u^{\prime}|^{p+2\beta}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}<\infty\Big\},

. Suppose

(3.1)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|X^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty\,\text{ and }\,\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|D_{\cdot,\cdot}X^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times[0,1]^{2},\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}<\infty

as well as

(3.2)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\|D_{s,t}X^{(n)}-D_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}X^{(n)}\|^{2}\right]}{(|s-s^{\prime}|+|t-t^{\prime}|)^{2+2\beta}}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}<\infty

for some
\beta\in(0,1)
. Then
(X^{(n)},n\in\mathbb{N})
is relatively compact in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

The proof is inspired from
[
19
, Section 5]
. We consider the symmetric form
\mathcal{L}
on
L^{2}((0,1)^{2},\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f,g)=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}f(s,t)\cdot g(s,t)\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{(f(s,t)-f(s^{\prime},t^{\prime}))\cdot(g(s,t)-g(s^{\prime},t^{\prime}))}{(|s-s^{\prime}|+|t-t^{\prime}|)^{2+2\beta}}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

for functions
f
,
g
in the dense domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})\subset L^{2}((0,1)^{2},\mathbb{R}^{d})
and a fixed
\beta\in(0,1)
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})=\Big\{g:\,\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}((0,1)^{2},\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{|g(s,t)-g(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})|^{2}}{(|s-s^{\prime}|+|t-t^{\prime}|)^{2+2\beta}}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}<\infty\Big\}.

Then
\mathcal{L}
is a positive symmetric closed form and, by Kato’s first representation theorem (see e.g.
[
21
]
), one can find a positive self-adjoint operator
T_{\mathcal{L}}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f,g)=\langle f,T_{\mathcal{L}}g\rangle_{L^{2}((0,1)^{2},\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for all
g\in\mathcal{D}(T_{\mathcal{L}})
and
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})
. Further, one may observe that the form
\mathcal{L}
is bounded from below by a positive number. Indeed,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(g,g)\geq\|g\|_{L^{2}((0,1)^{2},\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for all
g\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})
. Hence, we have that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{D}(T^{1/2}_{\mathcal{L}})
.

Now, define the operator
A
as
A=T^{1/2}_{\mathcal{L}}
. It follows from Lemma 1 in
[
25
, Section 1]
(see also
[
19
, Lemma 9]
) and Lemma
3.2
applied to
p=2
,
U=(0,1)^{2}
that
A
has a discrete spectrum and a compact inverse
A^{-1}
. Then, using (
3.1
)-(
3.3
), the operator
A
and the sequence
(X^{(n)},n\in\mathbb{N})
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
.
∎

3.2.
Malliavin differentiability for bounded drifts

In this subsection, we assume in addition the drift
b
is bounded. The main result of this subsection which significantly generalised those in
[
32
]
when the diffusion is constant is the following

Theorem 3.4
.

The strong solution
\{X^{\xi}_{s,t},\,s,t\in[0,1]\}
of the SDE (
1.3
) is Malliavin differentiable.

The proof of this theorem is done is two steps:

Step 1:
We use standard approximation procedure to approximate the drift coefficient
b=\hat{b}-\check{b}
by a sequence of functions

b_{n}:=\hat{b}_{n}-\check{b}_{n},n\geq 1

such that
\hat{b}_{n}=(\hat{b}_{1,n},\ldots,\hat{b}_{d,n})
,
\check{b}_{n}=(\check{b}_{1,n},\ldots,\check{b}_{d,n})
,
(\hat{b}_{j,n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(\check{b}_{j,n})_{n\geq 1}
are smooth and componentwise non-decreasing functions with
\sup_{n}\|\hat{b}_{j,n}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\hat{b}_{j}\|_{\infty}<\infty
and
\sup_{n}\|\check{b}_{j,n}\|_{\infty}\leq\|\check{b}_{j}\|_{\infty}<\infty
. In addition,
(\hat{b}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
(respctively,
(\check{b}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
) converges to
\hat{b}
(respctively,
\check{b}
) in
(s,t,x)\in[0,1]^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mathrm{d}s\times\mathrm{d}t\times\mathrm{d}x
-a.e. We know that for such smooth drift coefficients, the corresponding SDEs have a unique strong solution denoted by
X^{\xi,n}
. We then show that for each
s,t\in[0,1]
, the sequence
(X^{\xi,n}_{s,t})_{n\geq 1}
is relatively compact in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Step 2:
We show that the the sequence of solution
(X^{\xi,n})_{n\geq 1}
converges strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

From
Step 1
and
Step 2
, the result will follow by application of a compactness criteria
[
29
, Lemma 1.2.3]
.

The next result corresponds to a
L^{2}(\Omega)
compactness criteria. It is analogous to the result derived in
[
27
]
for the case of Brownian motion.

Theorem 3.5
.

For every
(s,t)\in\Gamma_{0}
, the sequence
(X^{\xi,n}_{s,t})_{n\geq 1}
, is relatively compact in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

The proof of the above theorem uses Corollary
3.3
, which in our case is reduced to proving:

Lemma 3.6
.

There exists
C_{1}>0
such that for every
(s,t)\in\Gamma_{0}
, the sequence
(X^{\xi,n}_{s,t})_{n\geq 1}
satisfies

(3.4)

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 1}\|X^{\xi,n}_{s,t}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{P};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leq C_{1}

and

(3.5)

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 1}\,\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq r\leq s\\
0\leq u\leq t\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\left[\|D_{r,u}X^{\xi,n}_{s,t}\|^{2}\right]\leq C_{1}.

Moreover for all
0\leq r^{\prime},r\leq s,\,\,0\leq u^{\prime},u\leq t
,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|D_{r,u}X^{\xi,n}_{s,t}-D_{r^{\prime},u^{\prime}}X^{\xi,n}_{s,t}\|^{2}\right]\leq C_{1}(|r-r^{\prime}|+|u-u^{\prime}|),

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the max norm.

Remark 3.7
.

If (
3.6
) is satisfied, then for any
\beta\in(0,1/2)
, (
3.2
) holds. Indeed, since, for any
s
,
s^{\prime}
,
t
,
t^{\prime}
, one has

\displaystyle|s-s^{\prime}|^{1/2}|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/2}\leq\frac{1}{2}(|s-s^{\prime}|+|t-t^{\prime}|),

then for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\|D_{s,t}X^{(n)}-D_{s^{\prime},t^{\prime}}X^{(n)}\|^{2}\right]}{(|s-s^{\prime}|+|t-t^{\prime}|)^{2+2\beta}}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}{(|s-s^{\prime}|+|t-t^{\prime}|)^{1+2\beta}}\leq\dfrac{C_{1}}{2}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}}{|s-s^{\prime}|^{1/2+\beta}}\Big)\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}}{|t-t^{\prime}|^{1/2+\beta}}\Big)<\infty.

To prove the result above, we need some preliminary estimates.

Lemma 3.8
.

There exists a non-decreasing function
\widetilde{C}_{1}:\,\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that for any
0<r<s\leq 1
, any
0<u<t\leq 1
, any
k\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and any
i,j\in\{1,\cdots,d\}
,

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{k}{\delta(r,s)\delta(u,t)}\int_{r}^{s}\int_{u}^{t}\partial_{i}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\Big)\Big]\leq\widetilde{C}_{1}(k,\|\hat{b}_{j,n}\|_{\infty})

and

(3.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{k}{\delta(r,s)\delta(u,t)}\int_{r}^{s}\int_{u}^{t}\partial_{i}\check{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\Big)\Big]\leq\widetilde{C}_{1}(k,\|\check{b}_{j,n}\|_{\infty}),

where
\delta(r,s)=\sqrt{s-r}
and
\delta(u,t)=\sqrt{t-u}
.

Proof.

We only prove (
3.7
) and the proof of (
3.8
) follows anagously. Using integration with respect to local time formula (see e.g.
[
5
, Corollary 2.3]
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{s}\int_{u}^{t}\partial_{i}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}

(3.9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{r}^{s}\int_{u}^{t}b_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\frac{\mathrm{d}_{t_{1}}W^{(i)}_{s_{1},t_{1}}}{s_{1}}\mathrm{d}s_{1}+\int_{r}^{s}\int_{1-t}^{1-u}b_{j,n}(s_{1},1-t_{1},W_{s_{1},1-t_{1}})\frac{\mathrm{d}_{t_{1}}B^{(i)}_{s_{1},t_{1}}}{s_{1}}\mathrm{d}s_{1}

\displaystyle-\int_{r}^{s}\int_{1-t}^{1-u}b_{j,n}(s_{1},1-t_{1},W_{s_{1},1-t_{1}})\frac{W^{(i)}_{s_{1},1-t_{1}}}{s_{1}(1-t_{1})}\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1},

where
(B^{(i)}_{s,t},(s,t)\in[0,1]^{2})
is the Brownian sheet given by the following representation provided by Dalang and Walsh
[
11
]

\displaystyle W^{(i)}_{s,1-t}=W^{(i)}_{s,1}+B^{(i)}_{s,t}-\int_{0}^{t}\frac{W^{(i)}_{s,1-u}}{1-u}\mathrm{d}u.

Since the function
x\longmapsto e^{3x}
is convex,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{k}{\delta(r,s)\delta(u,t)}\Big|\int_{r}^{s}\int_{u}^{t}\partial_{i}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\Big|\Big)\Big]

(3.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{3k}{\delta(r,s)\delta(u,t)}\Big|\int_{r}^{s}\int_{u}^{t}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\frac{\mathrm{d}_{t_{1}}W^{(i)}_{s_{1},t_{1}}}{s_{1}}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\Big|\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{3k}{\delta(r,s)\delta(u,t)}\Big|\int_{r}^{s}\int_{1-t}^{1-u}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},1-t_{1}})\frac{\mathrm{d}_{t_{1}}B^{(i)}_{s_{1},t_{1}}}{s_{1}}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\Big|\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{3k}{\delta(r,s)\delta(u,t)}\Big|\int_{r}^{s}\int_{1-t}^{1-u}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},1-t_{1}})\frac{W^{(i)}_{s_{1},1-t_{1}}}{s_{1}(1-t_{1})}\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\Big|\Big)\Big]\Big\}=\frac{1}{3}(I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}).

By Jensen inequality

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{3k}{\delta(r,s)\delta(u,t)}\Big|\int_{r}^{s}\int_{u}^{t}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\frac{\mathrm{d}_{t_{1}}W^{(i)}_{s_{1},t_{1}}}{s_{1}}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\Big|\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{s}\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{6k(\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{r})}{\delta(r,s)\delta(u,t)}\Big|\int_{u}^{t}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\frac{\mathrm{d}_{t_{1}}W^{(i)}_{s_{1},t_{1}}}{\sqrt{s_{1}}}\Big|\Big)\Big]\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{1}}{2\sqrt{s_{1}}(\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{r})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{s}\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{6k}{\delta(u,t)}\Big|\int_{u}^{t}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\frac{\mathrm{d}_{t_{1}}W^{(i)}_{s_{1},t_{1}}}{\sqrt{s_{1}}}\Big|\Big)\Big]\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{1}}{2\sqrt{s_{1}}(\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{r})}.

Since for every
s_{1}\in[r,s]
,

\Big(Y_{s_{1},t_{1}}:=\int_{u}^{t_{1}}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{2},W_{s_{1},t_{2}})\frac{\mathrm{d}_{t_{2}}W^{(i)}_{s_{1},t_{2}}}{\sqrt{s_{1}}},u\leq t_{1}\leq t\Big)

is a square integrable martingale, it follows from the Barlow-Yor inequality that there exists a positive constant
c_{1}
such that for any positive integer
m
and any
s_{1}\in[r,s]
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|Y_{s_{1},t}|^{m}]\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup\limits_{u\leq t_{1}\leq t}|Y_{s_{1},t_{1}}|^{m}\Big]\leq c_{1}^{m}\sqrt{m}^{\,m}\mathbb{E}[\langle Y_{s_{1},\cdot}\rangle^{m/2}_{t}]=c_{1}^{m}\sqrt{m}^{\,m}\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{u}^{t}\hat{b}^{2}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\Big)^{m/2}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{1}^{m}\sqrt{m}^{\,m}\delta(u,t)^{m}\|\hat{b}_{j,n}\|^{m}_{\infty}.

Hence, using the following exponential expansion formula,

\displaystyle\exp\Big(\frac{6k}{\delta(u,t)}|Y_{s_{1},t}|\Big)=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{6^{m}k^{m}|Y_{s_{1},t}|^{m}}{\delta(u,t)^{m}m!},

we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{s}\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{6k}{\delta(u,t)}\Big|\int_{u}^{t}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\frac{\mathrm{d}_{t_{1}}W_{s_{1},t_{1}}}{\sqrt{s_{1}}}\Big|\Big)\Big]\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{1}}{2\sqrt{s_{1}}(\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{r})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{s}\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{6k}{\delta(u,t)}|Y_{s_{1},t}|\Big)\Big]\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{1}}{2\sqrt{s_{1}}(\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{r})}=\int_{r}^{s}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{6^{m}k^{m}\mathbb{E}[|Y_{s_{1},t}|^{m}]}{\delta(u,t)^{m}m!}\,\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{1}}{2\sqrt{s_{1}}(\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{r})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{6^{m}k^{m}c_{1}^{m}\sqrt{m}^{\,m}\|\hat{b}_{j,n}\|^{m}_{\infty}}{m!}:=\widetilde{C}_{1,1}(k,\|\hat{b}_{j,n}\|_{\infty}),

which is finite by ratio test. Similarly, we also have

\displaystyle I_{2}\leq\widetilde{C}_{1,1}(k,\|\hat{b}_{j,n}\|_{\infty}).

To estimate
I_{3}
we apply one more Jensen inequality and obtain

\displaystyle I_{3}=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{3k}{\delta(r,s)\delta(u,t)}\Big|\int_{r}^{s}\int_{1-t}^{1-u}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},1-t_{1}})\frac{W^{(i)}_{s_{1},1-t_{1}}}{s_{1}(1-t_{1})}\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\Big|\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{s}\int_{1-t}^{1-u}\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{12k(\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{r})(\sqrt{1-u}-\sqrt{1-t})}{\delta(r,s)\delta(u,t)}|\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},1-t_{1}})|\Big|\frac{W^{(i)}_{s_{1},1-t_{1}}}{\sqrt{s_{1}}\sqrt{1-t_{1}}}\Big|\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\frac{\mathrm{d}t_{1}}{2\sqrt{1-t_{1}}(\sqrt{1-u}-\sqrt{1-t})}\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{1}}{2\sqrt{s_{1}}(\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{r})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{r}^{s}\int_{1-t}^{1-u}\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(12k\|\hat{b}_{j,n}\|_{\infty}\Big|\frac{W^{(i)}_{s_{1},1-t_{1}}}{\sqrt{s_{1}}\sqrt{1-t_{1}}}\Big|\Big)\Big]\frac{\mathrm{d}t_{1}}{2\sqrt{1-t_{1}}(\sqrt{1-u}-\sqrt{1-t})}\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{1}}{2\sqrt{s_{1}}(\sqrt{s}-\sqrt{r})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\exp\Big(72k^{2}\|\hat{b}_{j,n}\|^{2}_{\infty}\Big):=\widetilde{C}_{1,2}(k,\|\hat{b}_{j,n}\|_{\infty}),

since
\Big|\frac{W^{(i)}_{s_{1},1-t_{1}}}{\sqrt{s_{1}}\sqrt{1-t_{1}}}\Big|
is a reduced Gaussian random variable.

The proof is completed by choosing
\widetilde{C}_{1}=2\widetilde{C}_{1,1}+\widetilde{C}_{1,2}
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.6
.

Without loss of generality, we suppose
\xi=0
. Further we set
X^{n}_{s,t}:=X^{0,n}_{s,t}
. We start with the proof of (
3.5
). Suppose for every
j\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
,
\hat{b}_{j,n}
(respectively,
\check{b}_{j,n}
) is componentwise nondecreasing and continuously differentiable with bounded derivatives.
Then for any
(r,u)\in[0,1]^{2}
with
(0,0)\prec(r,u)\prec(s,t)
,

(3.11)

\displaystyle D_{r,u}X^{n}_{s,t}=I_{d}+\int_{r}^{s}\int_{u}^{t}\nabla b_{n}(s_{1},t_{1},X^{n}_{s_{1},t_{1}})\,D_{r,u}X^{n}_{s_{1},t_{1}}\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1},

where
I_{d}
is the identity matrix,
\nabla b_{n}=(\partial_{i}b_{j,n})_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
,
\partial_{i}b_{j,n}
being the partial derivative of
b_{j}(s,t,\cdot)
with respect to
x_{i}
,
and
D_{r,u}X_{s,t}^{n}=(D_{r,u}^{i}X_{s,t}^{n,j})_{1\leq i,j\leq d}
. Since
\partial_{i}\hat{b}_{j,n}
and
\partial_{i}\check{b}_{j,n}
are
nonnegative
, we have

\displaystyle\|D_{r,u}X^{n}_{s,t}\|\leq 1+\int_{r}^{s}\int_{u}^{t}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\Big\{\partial_{i}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},X^{n}_{s_{1},t_{1}})+\partial_{i}\check{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},X^{n}_{s_{1},t_{1}})\Big\}\|D_{r,u}X^{n}_{s_{1},t_{1}}\|\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}.

Therefore (see e.g.
[
37
, Lemma 5.1.1]
),

(3.12)

\displaystyle\|D_{r,u}X^{n}_{s,t}\|\leq

\displaystyle\exp\Big(\int_{r}^{s}\int_{u}^{t}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\Big\{\partial_{i}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},X^{n}_{s_{1},t_{1}})+\partial_{i}\check{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},X^{n}_{s_{1},t_{1}})\Big\}\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\Big).

Squaring both sides of the inequality, taking the expectation and using the Girsanov theorem (see e.g.
[
10
, Theorem 3.5]
and
[
30
, Proposition 1.6]
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|D_{r,u}X^{n}_{s,t}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(2\int_{r}^{s}\int_{u}^{t}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\Big\{\partial_{i}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},X^{n}_{s_{1},t_{1}})+\partial_{i}\check{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},X^{n}_{s_{1},t_{1}})\Big\}\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathcal{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}b_{n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\cdot\mathrm{d}W_{s_{1},t_{1}}\Big)\exp\Big(2\int_{r}^{s}\int_{u}^{t}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\Big\{\partial_{i}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})+\partial_{i}\check{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\Big\}\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\Big)\Big]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\exp\Big(4\int_{r}^{s}\int_{u}^{t}\sum\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\Big\{\partial_{i}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},X^{n}_{s_{1},t_{1}})+\partial_{i}\check{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},X^{n}_{s_{1},t_{1}})\Big\}\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\Big)\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{0}\mathbb{E}\Big[\prod\limits_{i,j=1}^{d}\exp\Big(4\int_{r}^{s}\int_{u}^{t}\partial_{i}\hat{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\Big)\exp\Big(4\int_{r}^{s}\int_{u}^{t}\partial_{i}\check{b}_{j,n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\Big)\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}},

where

(3.13)

\displaystyle C_{0}:=\sup\limits_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\Big[\mathcal{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}b_{n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\cdot\mathrm{d}W_{s_{1},t_{1}}\Big)^{2}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}

is finite (see Lemma
.14
and Remark
.15
in the Appendix), with

\mathcal{E}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}b_{n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\cdot\mathrm{d}W_{s_{1},t_{1}}\Big)=\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}b_{n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})\cdot\mathrm{d}W_{s_{1},t_{1}}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}|b_{n}(s_{1},t_{1},W_{s_{1},t_{1}})|^{2}\cdot\mathrm{d}s_{1}\mathrm{d}t_{1}\Big).

Hence, by Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|D_{r,u}X^{n}_{s,t}\|^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq

3.3.
Malliavin regularity under linear growth condition

As in the previous section, we approximate the drift coefficient
b=\hat{b}-\check{b}
by a sequence of functions

b_{n}:=\hat{b}_{n}-\check{b}_{n},n\geq 1
,
where
\hat{b}_{n}=(\hat{b}_{1,n},\ldots,\hat{b}_{d,n})
,
\check{b}_{n}=(\check{b}_{1,n},\ldots,\check{b}_{d,n})
,
(\hat{b}_{j,n})_{n\geq 1}
and
(\check{b}_{j,n})_{n\geq 1}
are smooth, componentwise non-decreasing and bounded functions satisfying:

\bullet

There exists
\tilde{M}>0
such that
\|\hat{b}_{j,n}(s,t,x)\|\leq\tilde{M}(1+|x|)
and
\|\hat{b}_{j,n}(s,t,x)\|\leq\tilde{M}(1+|x|)
for all
n\geq 1
and
(s,t,x)\in\Gamma_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\bullet

(\hat{b}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
(respctively,
(\check{b}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
) converges to
\hat{b}
(respctively,
\check{b}
) in
(s,t,x)\in\Gamma_{0}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mathrm{d}s\times\mathrm{d}t\times\mathrm{d}x
-a.e.

One verifies that for such smooth drift coefficients, the corresponding SDEs have a unique strong solution denoted by
X^{\xi,n}
. We show that for

small enough, the sequence

3.8
and
3.6
and are found in Appendix.

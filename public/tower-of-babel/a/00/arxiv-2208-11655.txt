LINKS OF SINGULARITIES OF INNER NON-DEGENERATE MIXED FUNCTIONS
Raimundo N. Araújo dos Santos Benjamin Bode, Eder L. Sanchez Quiceno

Abstract
We introduce the notion of a (strongly) inner non-degenerate mixed function f : ℂ 2 → ℂ f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C} . We show that inner non-degenerate mixed polynomials have weakly isolated singularities and strongly inner non-degenerate mixed polynomials have isolated singularities. Furthermore, under one additional assumption, which we call “niceness”, the links of these singularities can be completely characterized in terms of the Newton boundary of f f . In particular, adding terms above the Newton boundary does not affect the topology of the link. This allows us to define an infinite family of real algebraic links in the 3-sphere. Keywords: real algebraic links, mixed singularities, semiholomorphic function, isolated singularity, Newton non-degenerate, P-fibered braid, Newton boundary. Mathematics Subject Classification: Primary 32S55, 57K10; Secondary 14M25, 14P05, 14P15, 14P25, 32S05.



1  INTRODUCTION

It is a key objective in singularity theory to obtain information about geometric, topological or analytical properties of a (algebraic) variety in a neighbourhood of a singular point by studying the defining set of (polynomial) equations. A classical example of this occurs in the study of complex plane curves, the zeros of a complex polynomial
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
, where we can obtain the Puiseux expansion of each branch via the Newton polygon of
f.
See for instance
[
10
]
. The Puiseux pairs then allow a complete characterization of algebraic curves and their singularities from a topological viewpoint (cf. for example
[
11
]
). Analogous results does not hold in general over fields that are not algebraically closed, such as the real numbers, since there might not even exists a Puiseux expansion for every branch of the curve. In this paper we show that under some assumptions we can nonetheless completely describe the topology of a real algebraic variety near a singularity by studying the boundary of an appropriately defined Newton polygon.

We consider polynomial maps
f:\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
and their singularities, i.e., points where their Jacobian matrix does not have full rank. We say that the origin
O
is a
weakly isolated singularity
if it is a singularity, i.e.
O\in\Sigma_{f}\cap V_{f},
and there is an open neighbourhood
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{4}
of the origin such that
U\cap V_{f}\cap\Sigma_{f}=\{O\}
, where
V_{f}:=\{f=0\}
is the variety defined by
f
and
\Sigma_{f}:=\{x\in U:\text{rank J}f(x)\leq 1\}
is the set of critical points (or, the singular locus) of
f.

We say that
O
is an
isolated singularity
if it is a singularity and there is a neighbourhood
U
of the origin such that
U\cap\Sigma_{f}=\{O\}.

Naturally, holomorphic polynomials functions form a special case of this family of real polynomial mappings and indeed this real setting has several similarities with the complex case. Intersecting
V_{f}
with a 3-sphere of a small enough radius centered at the singular point, it results in a smooth link whose isotopy type is independent of the chosen radius. In contrast to the local topological classification of the singularities of complex plane curves, the links that can arise as the links of isolated singularities of real polynomial maps, called
real algebraic links
, are not classified yet.

Milnor proved in
[
18
]
that the set of real algebraic links is a subset of the set of fibered links and it was conjectured by Benedetti-Shiota in
[
2
]
that these two sets are identical. In general, it is well-known that every link arises as the link of a weakly isolated singularities as proved by Akbulut and King in
[
1
]
. However, besides those links that come from holomorphic polynomials from
\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
the current literature only offers relatively few examples of fibered links that are known to admit realizations as real algebraic links
[
3
,
5
,
6
,
17
,
23
,
24
,
25
]
. As a consequence of our main results we can add an infinite family to this list of examples.

Following Oka
[
21
]
we write real polynomials
f:\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
as
mixed functions
, that is, as polynomials
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
in complex variables
u
and
v
and their complex conjugates
\overline{u}
and
\overline{v}
. Mixed functions are a natural generalization of holomorphic functions as will be shown in the Section
2
. Working with mixed functions allows us to find analogues of tools that have proven useful in the complex setting. In
[
21
]
Oka also defines the
Newton polygon

\Gamma_{+}(f)
for any such mixed polynomial function, which in the case that it does not depend on the variables
\overline{u}
and
\overline{v}
naturally reduces to the usual definition of the Newton polygon of a complex plane curve. Using this definition he finds conditions on the boundary of the Newton polygon, called
(strong) non-degeneracy
and
convenience
that, if satisfied simultaneously, guarantee that the mixed function has a weakly isolated singularity (or, an isolated singularity) at the origin.

Similarly, in the case of complex plane curves Mondal’s and Wall’s conditions of being partially non-degenerate
[
19
]
or inner non-degenerate
[
28
]
(which in this case are equivalent) imply an isolated singularity
[
27
]
. Although these definitions have been generalized to fields with non-zero characteristic
[
9
]
, the concepts and results in general do not translate to fields that are not algebraically closed.

Motivated by the holomorphic setting we introduce in this paper what it means for a mixed polynomial to be (strongly) inner non-degenerate. For holomorphic functions our definition of being inner non-degenerate matches with those of
[
19
]
and
[
28
]
. The set of (strongly) inner non-degenerate mixed polynomials contains all convenient (strongly) non-degenerate polynomials and we show that inner non-degenerate mixed functions have weakly isolated singularities and strongly inner non-degenerate mixed functions have isolated singularities, even if they are not necessarily convenient. Our main result is that under our new inner non-degeneracy condition and some small additional assumption (“being
nice
”) the links of the resulting singularities are completely determined by the terms on the boundary of the Newton polygon, as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a mixed polynomial with a nice, inner non-degenerate boundary. Then the link of the singularity is ambient isotopic to
L([L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{N-1}],L_{N})
.

Here
N
is the number of compact 1-faces of the Newton boundary. Throughout the paper we assume that
N\geq 1
, which implies that all mixed polynomials in this paper satisfy
f(O)=0
. Every
L_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,N
, is a link (i.e., a finite disjoint union of smoothly embedded circles) that corresponds to the zeros of an appropriate function
g_{i}
associated with the
i
th 1-face, where
L_{1}
is a link in
\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}
,
L_{i}
with
i\in\{2,3,\ldots,N-1\}
is a link in
(\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\})\times S^{1}
and
L_{N}
is a link in
S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
. The link
L([L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{N-1}],L_{N})
can then be explicitly constructed (see Definitions
5.1
and
5.2
, Figure
4
) as a link in
S^{3}
and contains the
L_{i}
s (interpreted as links in
S^{3}
by appropriately embedding
\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}
and
S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
into
S^{3}
) as sublinks. The construction in Definitions
5.1
and
5.2
is formulated in terms of parametrisations of the
L_{i}
s, but the isotopy class of
L([L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{N-1}],L_{N})
only depends on the isotopy classes of the
L_{i}
s in
\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}
,
(\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\})\times S^{1}
and
S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
, respectively.

It follows from the classification of holomorphic functions
\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
with an isolated singularity (see,
[
16
,
26
]
) and by Fukui and Yoshinaga in
[
13
]
that the links of the singularities of non-degenerate, holomorphic polynomials from
\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
with the same Newton principal part
f_{\Gamma}
, consisting of all the terms on the Newton boundary
\Gamma
, are ambient isotopic. Since the isotopy class of
L([L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{N-1}],L_{N})
only depends on the Newton principal part of a mixed polynomial, our result can be seen as a generalization of the result in the holomorphic case.

A special family of mixed polynomials is the set of
semiholomorphic
polynomials, which by definition do not depend on the variable
\overline{u}
and are therefore holomorphic in the variable
u
. This makes the family of semiholomorphic polynomials an interesting intermediate between the general mixed setting and the holomorphic setting. They admit various connections to the study of complex polynomials and (from a topological perspective) braided vanishing sets. We show for example that the links
L_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,N
, in Theorem
1.1
can be understood as closed braids in
\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}
if the Newton principal part of
f
is semiholomorphic. We write
B_{i}
for the corresponding braid in
\mathbb{C}\times[0,2\pi]
. Furthermore, the holomorphicity in
u
makes it significantly easier to find critical points and in turn prove that a singularity is isolated. In fact, several constructions of real polynomials with isolated singularities have taken advantage of these desirable properties, as one can see for instance in
[
3
,
25
]
.

Taking advantage of that, we prove that for a mixed polynomial
f
whose Newton principal part
f_{\Gamma}
is semiholomorphic the conditions from Theorem
1.1
can be further weakened. More precisely, we prove:

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be an inner non-degenerate mixed polynomial, whose Newton principal part
f_{\Gamma}
is semiholomorphic. Then
f
has a weakly isolated singularity at the origin and the link
L_{f}=L_{f_{\Gamma}}
is isotopic to the closure of the braid
B(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
if
f
is
u
-convenient. If
f
is not
u
-convenient, then the link of the singularity is
B^{o}(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
.

The property of
u
-convenience is defined in Section
2
. The explicit constructions of the braids
B(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
and the links
B^{o}(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
are given in Definition
4.1
and Figure
3
. As mentioned above the braids
B_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,N
, are the links
L_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,N
, from Theorem
1.1
interpreted as closed braids. Thus,
B_{1}
is a braid in
\mathbb{C}\times[0,2\pi]
and
B_{i}
with
i>1
is a braid in
(\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\})\times[0,2\pi]
, defined as the zeros of an appropriate function
g_{i}
associated with the
i
th 1-face. Again, the construction of the braids
B(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
and the links
B^{o}(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
is given in terms of parametrizations of curves. However the isotopy class of the constructed link only depends on the braid isotopy classes of the
B_{i}
s in
\mathbb{C}\times[0,2\pi]
and
(\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\})\times[0,2\pi]
, respectively. Note that in Proposition
1.2
the assumption that
f
is nice is no longer needed.

Combining our results on strong inner non-degeneracy with the theory of
P
-fibered braids
[
5
]
, we obtain a
new family of real algebraic links
as the below result shows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
be a sequence of braids such that for all
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
the braid
B_{i}
consists of
s_{i}
strands and is P-fibered with
O
-multiplicity
\sum_{j<i}s_{j}
. Let
r_{N}:=1
.

Then for every sequence of positive integers
(r_{j},r_{j+1},\ldots,r_{N})
there is a positive integer
N_{j-1}
such that the closure of
B(B_{1}^{2r_{1}},B_{2}^{2r_{2}},\ldots,B_{N-1}^{2r_{N-1}},B_{N}^{2r_{N}})
is real algebraic if
r_{j}>N_{j}
for all
j=1,2,\ldots,N-1
.

Here
B_{i}^{2r_{i}}
refers to
2r_{i}
th power of
B_{i}
in the braid group.

This paper focuses on classical links, that is, embeddings of 1-dimensional closed manifolds in 3-dimensional spaces, which means that we only consider mixed polynomials
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
. However, we expect that our definitions and most of our results stated in the paper have analogues in the more general setting of mixed polynomials
f:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
.

The remainder of this paper is structured as follows.
In Section
2
we recall the concepts of mixed polynomials and mixed singularities as well as the construction of the Newton polygon and different notions of non-degeneracy. We also review definitions and properties of braids and their relation to polynomials and singularities. In Section
3
we introduce our notion of being inner non-degenerate, investigate its relation to other notions of non-degeneracy and show that it implies the existence of a weakly isolated singularity at the origin. The braids
(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
and
B^{o}(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
are described in Section
4
, where we also prove Proposition
1.2
. In Section
5
, we define the links
L([L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{N-1}],L_{N})
that appear as the links of singularities of mixed polynomials with a nice, inner non-degenerate boundary. We also prove the main result Theorem
1.1
. In Section
6
we study the isolatedness of singularities of mixed polynomials with regards to our definition of being strongly inner non-degenerate. In particular, we show that strongly inner non-degenerate polynomials have isolated singularities and show that convenient strongly non-degenerate polynomials are strongly inner non-degenerate. We use this property to prove Theorem
1.3
in Section
7
.

2  PRELIMINARIES

2.1  Mixed polynomials and Newton boundaries

In this section we review the essential tools concerning mixed functions and their Newton boundaries. Most of this background material can be found in more detail in
[
21
]
.

We consider the germ of a mixed polynomial
f:(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
,

f(z,\bar{z})=\sum_{\nu,\mu}c_{\nu,\mu}z^{\nu}\bar{z}^{\mu},

where
z=(u,v)
,
\bar{z}=(\bar{u},\bar{v})
,
z^{\nu}={u}^{\nu_{1}}v^{\nu_{2}}
for
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
(respectively
\bar{z}^{\mu}=\bar{u}^{\mu_{1}}{\bar{v}}^{\mu_{2}}
for
\mu=(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})
). Most readers might be more accustomed to the notations
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})
and
\bar{z}=(\bar{z}_{1},\bar{z}_{2},\ldots,\bar{z}_{n})
. However, in this case where
n=2
we prefer to work with complex variables
u
and
v
in order to be consistent with the notation in
[
3
]
.

We will slightly abuse notation and use the same notation for the germ of a function or a set and its representative. Furthermore, the origins in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
and in
\mathbb{C}
will both be denoted by 0 if there is no risk of confusion. Occasionally, we will also write
O
for the origin in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

By definition, the zero set
V_{f}:=f^{-1}(0)
is called
the mixed hypersurface
. It follows that
0\in V_{f}
, where
0
is the origin of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. For a fixed variable
x\in\{u,v,\bar{u},\bar{v}\}
we say that
f
is a
x
-semiholomorphic polynomial
if
f
does not depend on the variable
\bar{x}
, where naturally
\overline{(\bar{u})}=u
and
\overline{(\bar{v})}=v
. With this definition we have that
f
is holomorphic if and only if
f
is both
u
-semiholomorphic and
v
-semiholomorphic. Analogously,
f
is anti-holomorphic if and only if
f
is both
\bar{u}
-semiholomorphic and
\bar{v}
-semiholomorphic.

Following the convention in
[
8
]
we often refer to a
u
-semiholomorphic polynomial simply as semiholomorphic. We also denote the partial derivative of
f
with respect to the variable
u
by
f_{u}
. Analogously, we write
f_{\bar{u}}
,
f_{v}
and
f_{\bar{v}}
for the partial derivatives with respect to the variables
\bar{u},v
and
\bar{v}
, respectively.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a mixed polynomial
f:(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
, the singular set of
f
, denoted by
\Sigma_{f},
is defined as the solution of

\Sigma_{f}:=\begin{cases}&s_{1,f}:=f_{u}\bar{f}_{\bar{v}}-\bar{f}_{\bar{u}}f_{v}=0,\\
&s_{2,f}:=|f_{u}|^{2}-|f_{\bar{u}}|^{2}=0\\
&s_{3,f}:=|f_{v}|^{2}-|f_{\bar{v}}|^{2}=0\end{cases}

(1)

considered as a germ of a set at the origin.

Definition
2.1
is equivalent to the Oka’s definition in
[
21
, Proposition 8]
and Rudolph’s definition in
[
25
, Machinery 4.1]
. If there is a neighbourhood
U
of the origin
0\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
with
U\cap\Sigma_{f}=\{0\}
, we say that the origin is an isolated mixed singularity of
f
. If there is a neighbourhood
U
of the origin
0
with
U\cap\Sigma_{f}\cap V_{f}=\{0\}
, we say that the origin is a weakly isolated mixed singularity of
f
.

Note that if the origin is a (weakly) isolated mixed singularity of the germ
f:(\mathbb{C}^{2},0)\to(\mathbb{C},0)
, it is also a (weakly) isolated singularity of the respective real mapping germ
f:(\mathbb{R}^{4},0)\to(\mathbb{R}^{2},0)
, as defined in the introduction.

The support of
f
is defined as
supp(f):=\{\nu+\mu:\ c_{\nu,\mu}\neq 0\}\subset\mathbb{N}^{2}
. Given
\nu+\mu\in supp(f)
we denote by
M_{\nu,\mu}:=M_{\nu,\mu}(z,\bar{z})=c_{\nu,\mu}z^{\nu}\bar{z}^{\mu}
the mixed monomial of the corresponding degrees in
z
and
\bar{z}
. Let
P=(p_{1},p_{2})\in(\mathbb{N}^{+})^{2}
be a non-zero vector, that we call a
weight vector
. The
radial degree
of the monomial
M_{\nu,\mu}
with respect to weight vector
P
is defined by
rdeg_{P}(M_{\nu,\mu}):=\sum^{2}_{j=1}p_{j}(\nu_{j}+\mu_{j}).

We call
f(z,\bar{z})
a
radially weighted homogeneous polynomial of radial type

(P;d_{r}),
if

d_{r}=rdeg_{P}(M_{\nu,\mu})
for every
\nu+\mu\in supp(f).

The
Newton polygon

\Gamma_{+}(f)
of a mixed polynomial
f
is the convex hull of

\bigcup_{\nu+\mu\in supp(f)}(\nu+\mu)+(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{2}.

See Figure
1
for an illustration of an example.
The
Newton boundary

\Gamma(f)
of a Newton polygon is defined to be the set of lattice points
w\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
that lie on some compact face of
\Gamma_{+}(f).
These faces are either 0-dimensional (“vertices”) or 1-dimensional (“edges”). The
Newton principal part

f_{\Gamma}
of
f
is defined by

f_{\Gamma}(z,\bar{z})=\sum_{\nu+\mu\in\Gamma(f)}c_{\nu,\mu}z^{\nu}\bar{z}^{\mu}.

(2)

When
f\equiv f_{\Gamma}
, we say that
f
is a
boundary polynomial
.

By definition, we say that
f(z,\bar{z})
is
u-
convenient
if
\Gamma(f)
intersects the horizontal axis of
(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{2}.
Likewise, we say that
f(z,\bar{z})
is
v
-convenient if
\Gamma(f)
intersects the vertical axis of
(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{2}.
A mixed function is called
convenient
if it is both
u
-convenient and
v
-convenient.

For each compact face
\Delta
(0- or 1-dimensional) we define the
face function associated to the face
\Delta

f_{\Delta}(z,\bar{z})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{\nu+\mu\in\Delta}c_{\nu,\mu}z^{\nu}\bar{z}^{\mu}.

For a given compact 1-dimensional face, or 1-face for short,
\Delta
of
\Gamma_{+}(f)
there exists a unique weight vector
P=(p_{1},p_{2})
orthogonal to
\Delta
with
\gcd(p_{1},p_{2})=1,

such that the linear function
\ell_{P}
on
\Gamma(f)
defined by
\ell_{P}(w):=p_{1}w_{1}+p_{2}w_{2},
for

takes its minimal value on
\Delta
. In this case we write
\Delta:=\Delta(P,f)
or,
\Delta(P)
for short, and denote the minimal value by

. We then also refer to
f_{\Delta}
as the
face function associated to the weight
P
and denote it by
f_{P}
. Note that the face function
f_{\Delta(P)}(z,\bar{z})
is radially weighted homogeneous of type
(P,d_{r}),
with
d_{r}=d(P;f)
.

Remark 2.2
.

More generally, for any non-zero weight vector
Q=(q_{1},q_{2})\in(\mathbb{N}^{+})^{2}
one may consider the linear function
\ell_{Q}
on a given Newton boundary
\Gamma(f)
via
\ell_{Q}(x)=q_{1}a+q_{2}b,
for
x=(a,b)\in\Gamma(f).
Now, for a given mixed polynomial
f
with Newton boundary
\Gamma(f)
let
d(Q;f)
be the minimal value that
\ell_{Q}
takes on
\Gamma(f)
. The
Q-relative face function associated to the Newton boundary
\Gamma(f)
is defined and denoted by

f_{Q}(z,\bar{z}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu+\mu\in\Gamma(f),\\
\ell_{Q}(\nu+\mu)=d(Q;f)\end{subarray}}c_{\nu,\mu}z^{\nu}\bar{z}^{\mu}.

If
Q
is the weight vector associated to some 1-face
\Delta(Q)
, then we have
f_{Q}=f_{\Delta(Q)}
.

We say that a lattice point
w\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
lies
above the Newton boundary
of
f
if
\ell_{Q}(w)>d(Q;f)
for all positive weight vectors
Q
.

On the set of non-zero weight vectors we consider the following total order: for
P=(p_{1},p_{2})\in(\mathbb{N}^{+})^{2}
and
Q=(q_{1},q_{2})\in(\mathbb{N}^{+})^{2}

\gcd(p_{1},p_{2})=\gcd(q_{1},q_{2})=1,
we write

if and only if
\tfrac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}>\tfrac{q_{1}}{q_{2}}
.

On the Newton boundary
\Gamma(f)
consider the sequence of positive weight vectors
\mathcal{P=}\{P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{N}\}
associated to the

\Gamma_{+}(f),
indexed according to the order above so that

In this subsection we recall the fundamentals on braids. A standard reference is

Two geometric braids

In other words, the two sets of parametric curves are related by a smooth isotopy that maintains the geometric braid property throughout the deformation without changing the endpoints. Braid isotopies define an equivalence relation on the set of geometric braids for a fixed choice of
{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{s}}

However, in this article some constructions depend on the actual geometry of the parametric curves as opposed to the topological properties of its equivalence class. In order to emphasize this, we refer to an isotopy class as a braid and call a representative of that isotopy class a geometric braid.

A geometric braid is called

(see for example
[

29

a geometric braid in

(\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\})\times[0,2\pi]

Identifying the
t=0

\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}

With the term

we usually refer to the link in
\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}
and we denote it by
B
, the same as the (geometric) braid in
\mathbb{C}\times[0,2\pi]

\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}
such that the projection map
\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}\to S^{1}

and

\mathbb{C}\times[0,2\pi]
where the chosen complex numbers

Since we are specifically interested in links in the 3-sphere, we use the standard untwisted embedding of the solid torus in

as the neighbourhood of an unknot to obtain a link in

(8)

The curve

In this article we employ the connection between braids and complex polynomials, or more precisely, between braids and loops in certain spaces of polynomials.

For a given geometric braid

Note that for every value of

By definition we have that

Given a loop

(1)

3  INNER NON-DEGENERATE MIXED POLYNOMIALS

In this section we introduce the notion of an inner non-degenerate mixed function. We study its relation to Oka’s non-degeneracy and show that inner non-degenerate mixed polynomials have a weakly isolated singularity.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a mixed polynomial with Newton polygon
\Gamma_{+}(f)
, whose boundary has at least one compact 1-face. A 0-dimensional face of the boundary of
\Gamma_{+}(f)
, that bounds a compact 1-face, is called an
extreme vertex
if it is the boundary of a unique 1-face. Otherwise, we call it a
non-extreme vertex
. Let
\{P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{N}\}
be the sequence of weight vectors corresponding to the compact 1-faces of
\Gamma_{+}(f)
, ordered as in the previous section. With these notions we consider the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
f
has an inner Newton non-degenerate boundary if both of the following conditions hold:

(i)

the face functions
f_{P_{1}}
and
f_{P_{N}}
have no critical points in
V_{f_{P_{1}}}\cap(\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{v=0\})
and
V_{f_{P_{N}}}\cap(\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{u=0\})
, respectively.

(ii)

on each 1-face and non-extreme vertex
\Delta
, the face function
f_{\Delta}
has no critical points in
V_{f_{\Delta}}\cap(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
.

As in the other notions of non-degeneracy, we sometimes refer to

itself as being inner non-degenerate if it has an inner Newton non-degenerate boundary.

Remark 3.2
.

The following remarks follow directly from this definition:

For the ordered sequence
\{P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{N}\}
of weight vectors corresponding to the compact 1-faces of
\Gamma_{+}(f)
and
k_{i}=\tfrac{p_{i,1}}{p_{i,2}}
i=1,N, as in the expression (
5
), consider any positive weight vector

with

has no critical point in
V_{f_{P}}\cap(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
.

The Condition (ii) is the Newton non-degeneracy condition as in
[

If
f
is holomorphic, Definition
3.1
is equivalent to the definition of weakly inner non-degenerate polynomials
[
27
]
, which is equivalent to partial non-degeneracy in
[

]
and
[

for germ of functions

[

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

be a Newton non-degenerate, convenient mixed polynomial. Then

is inner non-degenerate.

Proof.

Suppose that
f
is inner degenerate, that is, not inner non-degenerate. Since
f
is Newton non-degenerate, Condition (ii) in Definition
3.1
is satisfied and hence Condition (i) in Definition
3.1
is not satisfied. Therefore, there exists a critical point of

in

or a critical point of

in

Let
(u_{*},v_{*})\in V_{f_{P_{1}}}\cap\mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\{v=0\}
be a critical point of

. Since

is non-degenerate,

has no critical point in
V_{f_{P_{1}}}\cap\mathbb{C}^{*2}
and hence

. Since

is convenient, its Newton boundary intersects the vertical axis and we call the corresponding vertex
\Delta
. Since

, we write

in a neighbourhood of

as

for some

. Note that
f_{\Delta}(u,v)=f_{P_{1}}(0,v)
for all

.

With

we have that
f_{\Delta}(u,v_{*})=f_{P_{1}}(0,v_{*})=0
implies that
\Phi(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{*}})=0
. We calculate the real Jacobian matrix of

\displaystyle Df_{\Delta}(u,v_{*})

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial\text{Re}(f_{\Delta})}{\partial\text{Re}(u)}(u,v_{*})&\frac{\partial\text{Re}(f_{\Delta})}{\partial\text{Im}(u)}(u,v_{*})&\frac{\partial\text{Re}(f_{\Delta})}{\partial r}(u,v_{*})&\frac{\partial\text{Re}(f_{\Delta})}{\partial t}(u,v_{*})\\
\frac{\partial\text{Im}(f_{\Delta})}{\partial\text{Re}(u)}(u,v_{*})&\frac{\partial\text{Im}(f_{\Delta})}{\partial\text{Im}(u)}(u,v_{*})&\frac{\partial\text{Im}(f_{\Delta})}{\partial r}(u,v_{*})&\frac{\partial\text{Im}(f_{\Delta})}{\partial t}(u,v_{*})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&kr_{*}^{k-1}\text{Re}(\Phi)(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{*}})&r_{*}^{k}\frac{\partial\text{Re}(\Phi)}{\partial t}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{*}})\\
0&0&kr_{*}^{k-1}\text{Im}(\Phi)(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{*}})&r_{*}^{k}\frac{\partial\text{Im}(\Phi)}{\partial t}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{*}})\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&r_{*}^{k}\frac{\partial\text{Re}(\Phi)}{\partial t}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{*}})\\
0&0&0&r_{*}^{k}\frac{\partial\text{Im}(\Phi)}{\partial t}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{*}})\end{pmatrix},

(13)

which follows from the fact that

4  SEMIHOLOMORPHIC PRINCIPAL PARTS

In this section we are going to study the topology of inner non-degenerate mixed polynomials whose principal part is semiholomorphic. In this case, we can explicitly describe the link of the singularity via braids that correspond to the zeros of the individual face functions.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
be a sequence of geometric braids on
S_{i}
strands, where
B_{i}
is parametrized by

\bigcup_{j=1}^{S_{i}}\bigcup_{t\in[0,2\pi]}\{(u_{i,j}(t),t)\}\subset\mathbb{C}\times[0,2\pi],

(30)

u_{i,j}(t)\neq 0
for all
j
,
t
and
i\neq 1
, i.e.,
B_{i}
with
i>1
is affine. We define the braid
B(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
to be the braid given by

\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}\bigcup_{j=1}^{S_{i}}\bigcup_{t\in[0,2\pi]}\{(\varepsilon^{k_{i}}u_{i,j}(t),t)\}\subset\mathbb{C}\times[0,2\pi],

(31)

for some sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
and a sequence of strictly decreasing positive real numbers
k_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,N
.

We denote by
B^{o}(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
the link given by the union of the closed braid
B(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
and its braid axis

\bigcup_{\varphi\in[0,2\pi]}\{(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi},\pi)\}\subset\mathbb{C}\times[0,2\pi].

(32)

Note that the braid type of
B(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
does not depend on
\varepsilon
as long as it is chosen sufficiently small and does not depend on the chosen sequence

as long as it is strictly decreasing and positive. See Figure (
3
) for an illustration of an example with

.

Figure 3

This construction clearly depends on the explicit parametrization of the geometric braids

\bigcup_{j=1}^{M^{\prime}}\bigcup_{\tau\in[0,2\pi]}\left\{\left(\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^{2}|v_{j}(\tau)|^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{j}(\tau)},\varepsilon v_{j}(\tau)\right)\right\}

(52)

for some small
\varepsilon>0
, respectively.

Then we write
L(K_{1},K_{2})
for the link in
S^{3}
given by
\widetilde{K_{1}}\cup\widetilde{K_{2}}
.

Figure 4
:
In the top row: links
L_{1}
in
\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}
,
L_{2}
in
(\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\})\times S^{1}
, and
L_{3}
in the complementary torus

. In the bottom row: the links
[L_{1},L_{2}]
and

.

Figure
4
displays the constructions defined in Definition
5.1
and Definition
5.2
. While they are given in terms of parametrizations of the links
L_{i}
, the link type of
L([L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{N-1}],L_{N})
only depends on the isotopy class of
L_{1}
in
\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}
, the isotopy classes of the
L_{i}
s with

in
(\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\})\times S^{1}
and the isotopy class of

in

.

The relation between the links in solid tori and links in
S^{3}
can be understood as follows. The 3-sphere can be decomposed into two solid tori, which are glued together on their boundary. The cores of these solid tori are the unknots
\alpha:=\{(0,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})\}_{t\in[0,2\pi]}
and
\beta:=\{(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi},0)\}_{\varphi\in[0,2\pi]}
, respectively. Definition
5.2
thus interprets the solid torus
\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}
containing
K_{1}
as the solid torus in
S^{3}
with core
\alpha
. Likewise the solid torus
S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
containing
K_{2}
is identified with the complementary solid torus, whose core is
\beta
. Figure
4
shows the corresponding links projected into
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, so that the solid torus
\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}
is identified with a tubular neighbourhood of the unit circle in the
xy-
plane. Its complementary solid torus
S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
in
S^{3}
is then obviously its complementary solid torus in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
including the point at infinity. The image of
\beta
under the stereographic projection map is the
z
-axis.

There are very close relations between Definitions
5.1
and
5.2
and Definition
4.1
. If
K_{2}
does not intersect the core of its solid torus, i.e.,
v_{j}(\tau)\neq 0
for all
\tau\in[0,2\pi]
and all
j=1,2,\ldots,M^{\prime}
, then
K_{2}
can also be interpreted as a link in
\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}
via

\bigcup_{j=1}^{M^{\prime}}\bigcup_{\tau\in[0,2\pi]}\{(u_{j}(\tau),\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{j}(\tau)})\},

(53)

where
|u_{j}(\tau)|=\sqrt{1-\varepsilon|v_{j}(\tau)|^{2}}
,
\arg(u_{j}(\tau))=\varphi_{j}(\tau)
and
t_{j}(\tau)=\arg(v_{j}(\tau))
. In this case
L(K_{1},K_{2})
is equal to
[K_{1},K_{2}]
interpreted as a link in
\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}\subset S^{3}
.

In particular, if
K_{2}
does not intersect the core of its solid torus
S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
and forms a closed geometric braid
B_{N}
in
\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}
, then
L([B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N-1}],B_{N})=B(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N-1},B_{N})=[B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N-1},B_{N}]
for any sequence of closed geometric braids
B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N-1}
.

If
K_{2}
contains a component that is equal to the core
(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi},0)
and the remaining components of
K_{2}
(interpreted as a link in
\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}
) form a closed geometric braid

, we have
L([B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N-1}],K_{2})=B^{o}(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N-1},B_{N})
.

Definition
5.1
and Definition
5.2
allow us to construct links from a sequence of links in a way that generalizes the nested behaviour of the braids in Definition
4.1
. However, they allow us to deal with links in solid tori that are not closed braids and with links
K_{2}\subset S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
that intersect the core of the solid torus.

As stated in Theorem
1.1
the links of the singularities of mixed polynomials with a certain specified behavior on its Newton boundary take the form
L([L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{N-1}],L_{N})
. Note that we could define an analogue of the construction in Definition
5.1
for links in the solid torus
S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
, which produces for any sequence of links
L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{N}
in
S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
a link
[L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{N}]
in
S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
. Then the link
L([L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{N-1}],L_{N})
could be equivalently represented by
L([L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{k}],[L_{N},L_{N-1},\ldots,L_{k+1}])
for any
k=2,3,\ldots,N-1
. Note the order in the second bracket is reversed. This is because Definition
5.1
arranges the links
L_{i}
such that the
u
-coordinates of
L_{i}
are strictly smaller than those of
L_{j}
for all
j>i
and a smaller
u
-coordinate corresponds to a larger
v
-coordinate in
S^{3}
.

In order to prove an analogue of Proposition
1.2
for mixed polynomials we need an additional assumption.

Definition 5.3
.

We say that a mixed polynomial
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
is
nice
if for every non-extreme vertex
\Delta
of
f
,
V_{f_{\Delta}}\cap(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}=\emptyset
.

Note that
f
in Proposition
4.5
is nice, since
f
is nice in a semiholomorphic vertex if and only if
f
is Newton non-degenerate for this vertex (cf. Lemma
4.3
).

Let
\Delta
be a non-extreme vertex and consider the weight vector
P_{i}
such that
\Delta=\Delta(P_{i-1})\cap\Delta(P_{i})
. By definition of
m_{i}
the polynomials
g_{i}
and
f_{\Delta}=R^{m_{i}}\Phi(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi},\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})
are both divisible by
R^{m_{i}}
. We may thus consider the function
\tfrac{g_{i}}{R^{m_{i}}}
as a function of
R
,
\varphi
and
t
. Again, we may vary
\varphi
from 0 to
2\pi
even when
R=0
.

Note that
\tfrac{g_{i}}{R^{m_{i}}}(0,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi},\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})=\Phi(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi},\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})
. The condition of being nice guarantees that
\Phi
never vanishes and thus we have for all values of
t
and
\varphi
that
\tfrac{g_{i}}{R^{m_{i}}}(0,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi},\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})\neq 0
. Therefore the vanishing set of
g_{i}
splits into two sets of connected component: The set
u=0
with multiplicity
m_{i}
and another set of components
L_{i}\subset\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}
disjoint from
\{(0,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})\}_{t\in[0,2\pi]}
.

As we have seen in the previous section this condition becomes simpler if
g_{i}
is semiholomorphic. It means that
\tfrac{g_{i}}{u^{m_{i}}}(0,0,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})\neq 0
. (Note that in this case
g_{i}
is divisible by
u^{m_{i}}
.) Furthermore, the degree of
g_{i}
with respect to
u
does not depend on
t
, so that the roots of
g_{i}(\cdot,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})
trace out a braid as
t
varies from 0 to
2\pi
.

Define
L_{1}
as the link in
\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}
formed by the zeros of the function
g_{1}
associated to
f_{P_{1}},
as in Eq. (
34
) and
L_{N}
be the link in
S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}
formed by the zeros of the function
\widehat{g}_{N}:S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by

f_{P_{N}}(u,\bar{u},v,\bar{v})=f_{P_{N}}(R\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi},R\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\varphi},v,\bar{v})=R^{\hat{s}_{i}/k_{N}+m_{N}}\widehat{g}_{N}(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi},v,\bar{v}),

(54)

where
\hat{s}_{i}
is the degree of
f_{P_{N}}
with respect to
r=|v|=|\bar{v}|
.

Define
L_{i},\ i=2,3,\ldots,N-1,
as the link formed by the zeros of the function
g_{i}
with non-zero
u
-coordinate as above. The fact that every
L_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,N
is a link rather than a set of curves with (self)intersections follows from the same line of reasoning as the proof of Lemma
4.4
. If there were some critical point of
g_{i}
on
g_{i}^{-1}(0)
, then there would exist a corresponding line of critical points of
f_{P_{i}}
in
V_{f_{P_{i}}}\cap(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
contradicting the assumption that
f
is inner non-degenerate.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a mixed polynomial with a nice inner non-degenerate boundary. Then the link of the singularity
L_{f}
contains the link
L([L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{N-1}],L_{N})
as a sublink.

Proof.

Again the proof follows the ideas from Proposition
4.5
. We consider
f_{i}:\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\times[0,2\pi]
as in Eq. (
33
) and
again we have
f_{i}(u,\bar{u},0,t)=g_{i}(u,t)
and the non-degeneracy of
f_{P_{i}}
implies that we can evoke the implicit function theorem. However, since
g_{i}
is not necessarily holomorphic with respect to
u
anymore, this requires a bit of additional explanation. We know that the real Jacobian
Df_{P_{i}}
of
f_{P_{i}}
has full rank at any zero of
f_{P_{i}}
in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
. We can choose local coordinates
(x,y,\tau,r)
such that
\partial_{\tau}
is the direction along the zeros
L_{i}
of
g_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,N-1
. For
i=N
the local coordinates may take the form
(\tau,R,x,y)
. The following arguments apply to
i=N
when the roles of
r
and
R
are exchanged. Since
f_{P_{i}}
is radially weighted homogeneous and
g_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,N-1
, does not depend on
r
, i.e.,
f_{P_{i}}
depends on
r
only via scaling, the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial\text{Re}(g_{i})}{\partial x}&\frac{\partial\text{Re}(g_{i})}{\partial y}\\
\frac{\partial\text{Im}(g_{i})}{\partial x}&\frac{\partial\text{Im}(g_{i})}{\partial y}\end{pmatrix}

(55)

has full rank.

Hence, by the implicit function theorem, as long as
r
is chosen small enough, the zeros of
g_{i}
are parametrized by
r
and
\tau
, i.e.
(x_{i,\ell}(r,\tau),y_{i,\ell}(r,\tau))
, where
\ell=1,2,\ldots,M_{i}
, now enumerates the connected components of the zeros of
g_{i}
. In particular, varying
r
yields an explicit isotopy in
\mathbb{C}\times S^{1}
between
L_{i}
, the zeros of
g_{i}
, and
L_{i}^{\prime}(r)
, the zeros of
f_{i}
close to
L_{i}
at a fixed value of
r
, with
L_{i}^{\prime}(0)=L_{i}
. We can rewrite the parametrization of the links in terms of our usual coordinates and obtain
(u_{i,\ell}(r,\tau),r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{i,\ell}(r,\tau)})
.

Again we obtain the cones of the
L_{i}
s as zeros of
f
, where the scaling of the
u
-coordinate (the “sharpness” of the cone) for each
i
depends on the weight vector
P_{i}
via
k_{i}=\tfrac{p_{i,1}}{p_{i,2}}
. To be precise,

f(r^{k_{i}}u_{i,\ell}(r,\tau),r^{k_{i}}\overline{u_{i,\ell}(r,\tau)},r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t_{i,\ell}(r,\tau)},r\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t_{i,\ell}(r,\tau)})=0

(56)

for all
i=1,2,\ldots,N-1
,
\ell=1,2,\ldots,M_{i}
,
\tau\in S^{1}
and small enough
r\geq 0
. Likewise, for
i=N
we obtain

f(R\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi_{j}(R,\tau)},R\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\varphi_{j}(R,\tau)},R^{1/k_{N}}v_{j}(R,\tau),R^{1/k_{N}}\overline{v_{j}(R,\tau)})=0,

(57)

for all small enough
R\geq 0
, with
j=1,2,\ldots,M^{\prime}
going over the
M^{\prime}
components of
L_{N}
.

Therefore
L([L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{N-1}],L_{N})
is a sublink of the link of the singularity of
f
.
∎

Note that in contrast to Proposition
1.2
we do not need to consider two different cases. The construction in Definition
5.2
was devised in such a way that if
f
is not
u
-convenient,
L_{N}
has a component with
v=0
, but it does not affect the construction. It is simply the analogue of
B_{1}
in Proposition
1.2
having a component with
u=0
. We would like to emphasize again that
g_{i}(u,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})
or
\widehat{g_{N}}
could potentially be non-vanishing for all
u\in\mathbb{C}
,
t\in[0,2\pi]
, in which case
L_{i}
is the empty link.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
and
L_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,N
, be as in Lemma
5.4
. Then the link of the singularity is equal to
L([L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{N-1}],L_{N})
.

Proof.

Take
\rho>0
sufficiently small and note that the set
V_{f}\cap D_{\rho}^{4}\cap(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
is a semi-algebraic set. Therefore any of its connected components is a semi-algebraic set. We can thus apply the curve selection lemma to each of its connected components.

Suppose now that the link of the singularity has a component
K
besides
L([L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{N-1}],L_{N})
and different from the core
\{(0,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})\}_{t\in[0,2\pi]}
and the core
\{(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi},0)\}_{\varphi\in[0,2\pi]}
. Then by the curve selection lemma there is a real-analytic path
\gamma:[0,\varepsilon)\to(\mathbb{C^{*}})^{2}
with
\gamma(0)=O
and
\gamma(\chi)
in the connected component of
V_{f}\cap D_{\rho}^{4}\cap(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
corresponding to
K
for all
\chi\in(0,\varepsilon)
. We write
u_{*}(\chi)
and
v_{*}(\chi)
for the
u
- and
v
-coordinate of this path. It follows that
u_{*}(\chi)
and
v_{*}(\chi)
are analytic functions of
\chi
.

Since the curve lies in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}\cup\{O\}
, neither of the two power series vanishes completely. Let
k
be the quotient of the orders of these power series, i.e., if
u_{*}(\chi)=a_{j}\chi^{j}+h.o.t.
and
v_{*}(\chi)=b_{j^{\prime}}\chi^{j^{\prime}}+h.o.t.
, then
k=j/j^{\prime}
.
Note that this implies that

\lim_{\chi\to 0}u_{*}(\chi)/|v_{*}(\chi)|^{k}=\tfrac{a_{j}}{|b_{j\textquoteright}|^{k}}

is a finite, well-defined value.

If
k=k_{i}
for some 1-face
P_{i}
of the Newton boundary, then
\left(\tfrac{u_{*}(\chi)}{|v_{*}(\chi)|^{k_{i}}},v_{*}(\chi)\right)
is a root of
f_{i}
for every
\chi\in[0,\varepsilon)
. In particular,
\lim_{\chi\to 0}|u_{*}(\chi)/|v_{*}(\chi)|^{k_{i}}=\tfrac{a_{j}}{|b_{j\textquoteright}|^{k_{i}}}
is a root of
g_{i}
with non-zero
u
-coordinate. Therefore, it lies on
L_{i}
and the path
(u_{*}(\chi),v_{*}(\chi))
lies on the component of
V_{f}\cap D_{\rho}^{4}\cap(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
corresponding to
L_{i}
, which is a contradiction.

Therefore,
k\neq k_{i}
for any 1-face
P_{i}
of the Newton boundary of
f
. In this case, it corresponds to the weight vector of some vertex
\Delta
of the Newton boundary, say with coordinates
(m,n)
. Assume that
k>k_{N}
. Then we may define
f_{(m,n)}
via

f(u,\bar{u},r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t},r\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t})=r^{km+n}f_{(m,n)}\left(\frac{u}{r^{k}},\frac{\bar{u}}{r^{k}},r,t\right).

(58)

Note that we have (like for the
f_{i}
s defined for 1-faces)
r^{km+n}f_{(m,n)}\left(\frac{u}{r^{k}},\frac{\bar{u}}{r^{k}},0,t\right)=f_{\Delta}(u,\bar{u},v,\bar{v})
.

The curve
\left(\tfrac{u_{*}(\chi)}{|v_{*}(\chi)|^{k}},\tfrac{\overline{u_{*}(\chi)}}{|v_{*}(\chi)|^{k}},|v_{*}(\chi)|,\arg(v_{*}(\chi))\right)
is in
V_{f_{(m,n)}}
for all sufficiently small
\chi>0
. It follows that the limit value

\lim_{\chi\to 0}u_{*}(\chi)|/|v_{*}(\chi)|^{k}=\tfrac{a_{j}}{|b_{j\textquoteright}|^{k}}

is the non-zero
u
-coordinate of a root of
f_{(m,n)}(u,\bar{u},0,t)
. Since
f_{\Delta}(u,\bar{u},v,\bar{v})=r^{km+n}f_{(m,n)}\left(\frac{u}{r^{k}},\frac{\bar{u}}{r^{k}},0,t\right)
, this implies that there exists a corresponding root of
f_{\Delta}
in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
, which contradicts the assumption that
f
has a nice Newton boundary.

If
k<k_{N}
, the vertex
\Delta
is the extreme vertex on
\Delta(P_{N})
and we define
f_{(m,n)}
accordingly:

f(R\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\varphi},R\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\varphi},v,\bar{v})=R^{n/k+m}f_{(m,n)}\left(R,\varphi,\frac{v}{R^{1/k}},\frac{\bar{v}}{R^{1/k}}\right).

(59)

Analogously to the previous case, the limit value

\lim_{\chi\to 0}v_{*}(\chi)|/|u_{*}(\chi)|^{1/k}=\tfrac{b_{j^{\prime}}}{|a_{j}|^{1/k}}

would constitute a root of
f_{(m,n)}(0,\varphi,v,\bar{v})
, leading to a root of
f_{\Delta}
in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
and thereby contradicting the niceness assumption. Therefore
V_{f}\cap D_{\rho}^{4}\cap(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
has no connected components besides the different
L_{i}
.

Suppose there is another component of
V_{f}\cap D_{\rho}^{4}\backslash\{O\}
that is contained in
\{(u,0):u\in\mathbb{C}\}
, i.e.,
v=0
. Then since all points of
V_{f}\cap D_{\rho}^{4}\backslash\{O\}
are regular, this extra component is a 2-dimensional manifold. In other words, for
v=0
the roots of
f(u,\bar{u},0,0)
are a 2-dimensional subset of
\mathbb{C}
. But that means that
f(u,\bar{u},0,0)=0
for all
u\in\mathbb{C}
. Thus
f
is not
u
-convenient and
\{(u,0):u\in\mathbb{C}\}
is a part of
L_{N}
.

Similarly, if there is another component with
u=0
, then
f
is not
v
-convenient and the corresponding component is a component of
L_{1}
. Therefore, the link of the singularity is
L([L_{1},L_{2},\ldots,L_{N-1}],L_{N})
.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a mixed polynomial with a nice inner non-degenerate boundary. Then the number of components of the link of its weakly isolated singularity is equal to the sum of the number of components of
L_{i}
.
In particular, if a knot
K
is the link of a weakly isolated singularity of a true mixed polynomial
f
with a nice Newton inner non-degenerate boundary, then the Newton boundary of
f
has exactly one compact 1-face. Therefore, there is a radially weighted homogeneous polynomial with a weakly isolated singularity and
K
as the link of that singularity.

Compare this with Theorem 43 of Oka
[
21
]
, which calculates the number of link components as the sum of the number of components of
L_{i}
(summed over
i
) under the assumption that all vertices of
\Gamma(f)
are simple.

Another application of our theorems is analogous to a result by Eyral and Oka
[
12
]
for the class of non-convenient strongly non-degenerate mixed polynomial with isolated singularity.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
f
be a non-convenient mixed polynomial, whose boundary is nice and Newton inner non-degenerate, and let
M_{1}(v,\bar{v})
and
M_{2}(u,\bar{u})
be mixed polynomials satisfying that the smallest exponents of the variable
|v|
in
M_{1}
and of the variable
|u|
in
M_{2}
are greater than
d(P_{1};f)/p_{1,2}
and
d(P_{N};f)/p_{N,1}
, respectively. Then
f+M_{1}+M_{2}
is a convenient weak realization of
L_{f}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
f
is not
v
-convenient. Then
L_{0}:=S^{3}_{\rho}\cap\{u=0\}
is a link component of
L_{1}\subset L_{f}
. Take
F=f+M_{1}
, where
M_{1}
is a sum of monomials which satisfy that the exponent in variable
|v|
is greater than
d(P_{1};f)/p_{1,2}
. Then
\mathcal{P}(F)=\{P_{0}\}\cup\mathcal{P}(f)
for some weight vector
P_{0}
with
k_{0}>k_{i}
for all
i=1,2,\dots,N
. Note that there exists a
d>0
and functions
\phi_{1}
,
\phi_{2}
satisfying

F_{P_{0}}(u,\bar{u},re^{it},re^{-it})=r^{d}(\phi_{1}(e^{it},e^{-it})u+\phi_{2}(e^{it},e^{-it})\bar{u}+r^{-d}(M_{1})_{P_{0}}(re^{it},re^{it})),

where
\lim_{r\to 0}r^{-d}(M_{1})_{P_{0}}(re^{it},re^{it}))=0
. By Condition (i) of Definition
3.1
we have that
(u=0,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})
is a regular point of
\phi_{1}(e^{it},e^{-it})u+\phi_{2}(e^{it},e^{-it})\bar{u}
. Thus we can apply the implicit function theorem and the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
to show that the link associated to
F_{P_{0}}
is isotopic to
L_{0}
. In fact, this isotopy does not leave a tubular neighbourhood of
L_{0}
. The other components of
L_{f+M_{1}+M_{2}}
are isotopic to those of
L_{f}
, since for every
P_{i}
,
i=1,2,\ldots,N
, the polynomial
M_{1}
lies above
\Delta(P_{i},f)
and hence adding
M_{1}
does not result in a change of the topology of
L_{i}
nor of the way that it is linked with the other components. Following the same procedure if
f
is not
u
-convenient we obtain that
f(u,\bar{u},v,\bar{v})+M_{1}(v,\bar{v})+M_{2}(u,\bar{u})
is a convenient mixed polynomial and weak realization of
L_{f}
.
∎

6  STRONGLY INNER NON-DEGENERATE MIXED POLYNOMIALS

Mixed polynomials with isolated singularities are of particular interest, since they are much rarer than weakly isolated singularities and harder to construct. In this section we show that under a strong Newton inner non-degeneracy condition of the boundary we get an isolated singularity at the origin.

Definition 6.1
.

We say that
f
has a strongly Newton inner non-degenerate boundary if both of the following conditions hold:

i.

the face functions
f_{P_{1}}
and
f_{P_{N}}
have no critical points in
\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{v=0\}
and
\mathbb{C}^{2}\setminus\{u=0\}
, respectively.

ii.

on each 1-face and non-extreme vertex
\Delta
, the face function
f_{\Delta}
has no critical points in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
.

Following the same idea of the proof of Proposition
3.6
we get.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a mixed polynomial with a strongly Newton inner non-degenerate boundary. Then
f
has an isolated singularity at the origin.

Proof.

The proof is almost identical to that of Proposition
3.6
. Assuming that the singularity is not isolated results via the curve selection lemma in a real-analytic curve
z(\tau)
of critical points starting at the origin. Evaluating the defining equations
s_{1,f}
,
s_{2,f}
and
s_{3,f}
of
\Sigma_{f}
on this curve yields a system of equations as in Eq. (
22
)-(
24
) in terms of power series of
\tau
. Comparing lowest order terms yields a solution to the equations that define the strong inner non-degeneracy of
f_{P}
, where
P=(p_{1},p_{2})
is the weight vector defined from the lowest order terms of
z(\tau)
as in Proposition
3.6
. Again there are the same cases to consider as in the proof of Proposition
3.6
:
k_{1}\geq\tfrac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}\geq k_{N}
,
\tfrac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}<k_{N}
,
\tfrac{p_{1}}{p_{2}}>k_{1}
, and the vanishing of one of the complex coordinates of
z(\tau)
. In all of these cases, the arguments from Proposition
3.6
remain unchanged, but since
z(\tau)
is no longer assumed to be in
V_{f}
, it proves by contradiction that the origin is an isolated singularity.
∎

As in the case of inner non-degeneracy our notion of strong inner non-degeneracy generalizes Oka’s convenient strongly non-degenerate functions.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
f:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
be a convenient mixed function that is strongly non-degenerate in Oka’s sense. Then
f
has a strongly Newton inner non-degenerate boundary.

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
3.3
we only have to show that a critical point of
f_{\Delta}
in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
for
\Delta
an extreme vertex of
f
leads to a contradiction with Condition (i) in the definition of strong inner non-degeneracy. Assume that
\Delta
is the extreme vertex lying on
\Delta(P_{1})
. Since
f
is convenient,
f_{\Delta}
takes the form
f_{\Delta}(u,\bar{u},v,\bar{v})=A(v,\bar{v})
for some function
A:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
. Thus
s_{1,f_{\Delta}}=s_{2,f_{\Delta}}=0
and a critical point of
f_{\Delta}
in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
corresponds to a non-zero root of
s_{3,f_{\Delta}}=|A_{v}(v,\bar{v})|^{2}-|A_{\bar{v}}(v,\bar{v})|^{2}
.

Condition (i) in Definition
6.1
is equivalent to the non-existence of solutions
\{(0,v):v\neq 0\}
to the first three equations in Eq. (
27
). In particular at
u=0
, the third equation is
s_{3,f_{P_{1}}}|_{u=0}=s_{3,f_{\Delta}}
. Hence if
f
is strongly non-degenerate, there are no non-zero roots of
s_{3,f_{\Delta}}
and thus Condition (i) is satisfied for
f_{P_{1}}
. The arguments for
f_{P_{N}}
are analogous.
∎

7  REAL ALGEBRAIC LINKS

We prove that for certain braids of the form
B(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
we can construct semiholomorphic polynomials that are strongly inner non-degenerate with the closure of
B(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
as the link of a singularity. By Proposition
6.2
the singularity at the origin is not only weakly isolated, but isolated and hence the closure of
B(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
is real algebraic.

In order to characterize the sequences of braids
(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
for which this procedure works, we need to generalize the concept of P-fiberedness from
[
5
]
. Recall that a braid is P-fibered if the corresponding loop in the space of polynomials
g(\cdot,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})
, with
g^{-1}(0)=B
, defines a fibration via
\arg(g):(\mathbb{C}\times S^{1})\backslash B\to\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
B
be a geometric braid on
s
strands, parametrized as in Eq. (
7
), but with the property that
u_{j}(t)\neq 0
for all
t\in[0,2\pi]
. That is, none of the strands intersects the line
\{0\}\times[0,2\pi]\subset\mathbb{C}\times[0,2\pi]
. Let
g(\cdot,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})
be the corresponding loop of polynomials given by
g(u,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})=\prod_{j=1}^{s}(u-u_{j}(t))
. Then we say that
B
is
P-fibered with
O
-multiplicity
m
if
\arg(u^{m}g):(\mathbb{C}\times S^{1})\backslash(B\cup(\{0\}\times S^{1}))\to S^{1}
is a fibration map, i.e., if it has no critical points.

P-fibered braids of
O
-multiplicity 0 are P-fibered braids. If
B
is a P-fibered braid of
O
-multiplicity 1, then
B\cup(\{0\}\times[0,2\pi])
is a P-fibered braid. If the
O
-multiplicity
m
is greater than 1, the closure of
B\cup(\{0\}\times[0,2\pi])
is not necessarily a fibered link, since in this case
\arg(u^{m}g)
does not take the required form on a tubular neighbourhood of
\{0\}\times S^{1}
.

Note that, just like for P-fibered braids, a geometric braid
B
is P-fibered with
O
-multiplicity
m
, if and only if
B^{n}
is P-fibered with
O
-multiplicity
m
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}
.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
(B_{1},B_{2},\ldots,B_{N})
be a sequence of geometric braids such that
B_{i}
consists of
s_{i}
strands and is P-fibered with
O
-multiplicity
m_{i}:=\sum_{j<i}s_{j}
for all
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
. Let
r_{N}:=1
.

Then for every sequence of positive integers
(r_{j},r_{j+1},\ldots,r_{N})
there is a positive integer
N_{j-1}
such that the closure of
B(B_{1}^{2r_{1}},B_{2}^{2r_{2}},\ldots,B_{N-1}^{2r_{N-1}},B_{N}^{2r_{N}})
is real algebraic if
r_{j}>N_{j}
for all
j=1,2,\ldots,N-1
.

Proof.

Let
g_{i}(\cdot,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})
denote the loop in the space of monic polynomials corresponding to the geometric braid
B_{i}
and let
\tilde{g}_{i}(u,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t}):=u^{m_{i}}g_{i}(u,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})
. Then the zeros of
\tilde{g}_{N}(u,\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}t})
form the union of the geometric braid
B_{N}^{2}
and the 0-strand
\{0\}\times[0,2\pi]
. As in
[
3
]
we define the
u
-convenient, semiholomorphic, radially weighted homogeneous polynomial

f_{N}(u,v,\bar{v})=f_{N}(u,r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t},r\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t}):=r^{k_{N}s_{N}}\tilde{g}_{N}\left(\frac{u}{r^{k_{N}}},\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}t}\right)

(60)

with
k_{N}
some sufficiently large even integer. Recall that
f_{N}
has no critical points in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
if
\arg(\tilde{g}_{N})
is a fibration map.
Since
B_{N}^{2}
is P-fibered with
O
-multiplicity
m_{N}
, we have a fibration map
\arg(\tilde{g}_{N})
, which implies the non-existence of critical points of
f_{N}
in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
. Let
a_{N}(r,t)
denote the coefficient of
u^{m_{N}}
in
f_{N}
. Since the strands of
B_{N}^{2}
do not intersect the 0-strand,
a_{N}(r,t)
is never zero if
r>0
. We define

f_{N-1}(u,v,\bar{v})=f_{N-1}(u,r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t},r\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t}):=a_{N}(r,t)r^{k_{N-1}s_{N-1}}\tilde{g}_{N-1}\left(\frac{u}{r^{k_{N-1}}},\mathrm{e}^{2r_{N-1}\mathrm{i}t}\right),

(61)

where
k_{N-1}
is some large even integer, greater than
k_{N}
, and for now
r_{N-1}
is an arbitrary integer. Note that the geometric braid formed by the zeros of
\tilde{g}_{N-1}(u,\mathrm{e}^{2r_{N-1}}\mathrm{i}t)
is the union of
B_{N-1}^{2r_{N-1}}
and the 0-strand. We want to show that if
r_{N-1}
is sufficiently large, then
f_{N-1}
has no critical points in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
. Since
f_{N-1}
is radially weighted homogeneous and semiholomorphic, this is true if
\arg(a_{N}(1,t)\tilde{g}_{N-1}(u,\mathrm{e}^{2r_{N-1}\mathrm{i}t}))
is a fibration map.

Let
v_{N-1,j}(t)
,
j=1,2,\ldots,s_{N-1}-m_{N-1}-1
, denote the non-zero critical values of
\tilde{g}_{N-1}(\cdot,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})
. Since
B_{N-1}
is P-fibered with
O
-multiplicity
m_{N-1}
, we have a fibration map
\arg(\tilde{g}_{N-1})
, which is equivalent to
\tfrac{\partial\arg(v_{N-1,j})}{\partial t}(t)\neq 0
for all
j=1,2,\ldots,s_{N-1}-m_{N-1}-1
and all
t\in[0,2\pi]
. Then the non-zero critical values of
a_{N}(1,t)\tilde{g}_{N-1}(u,\mathrm{e}^{2r_{N-1}\mathrm{i}t})
are
a_{N}(1,t)v_{N-1,j}(2r_{N-1}t)
and hence

\frac{\partial\arg(a_{N}(1,t)v_{N-1,j}(2r_{N-1}t))}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial\arg(a_{N}(1,t))}{\partial t}+2r_{N-1}\frac{\partial\arg(v_{N-1,j})}{\partial t},

(62)

which is non-vanishing as long as
r_{N-1}
is chosen sufficiently large. Therefore,
\arg(a_{N}(1,t)\tilde{g}_{N-1}(u,\mathrm{e}^{2r_{N-1}\mathrm{i}t}))
is a fibration map and
f_{N-1}
has no critical points in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
if
r_{N-1}
is large. Note that since we introduced the factor
a_{N}
in Eq. (
61
), the coefficient of
u^{s_{N-1}+m_{N-1}}
(the highest order term) in
f_{N-1}
is the same as the coefficient of
u^{m_{N}}=u^{s_{N-1}+m_{N-1}}
(the lowest order term) in
f_{N}
.

We proceed like this inductively, defining
a_{j}(r,t)
to be the coefficient of
u^{m_{j}}
in
f_{j}
and

f_{j-1}(u,v,\bar{v})=f_{j-1}(u,r\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t},r\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t}):=a_{j}(r,t)r^{k_{j-1}s_{j-1}}\tilde{g}_{j-1}\left(\frac{u}{r^{k_{j-1}}},\mathrm{e}^{2r_{j-1}\mathrm{i}t}\right),

(63)

where
k_{j-1}
is chosen greater than
k_{j}
. As above, we can guarantee that
f_{j-1}
has no critical points in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
by choosing
r_{j-1}
sufficiently large. Note however that
a_{j}(r,t)
depends on
r_{j}
and
k_{j}
and the range of values for
r_{j-1}
that is sufficient depends on
a_{j}(r,t)
. Likewise, the range of values of
k_{j}
that is sufficient depends on the choice of
r_{j}
. Therefore, we cannot know how to chose
r_{j-1}
before we have chosen
r_{j}
and then
k_{j}
.

There is one case for which the proof that
f_{1}
is strongly non-degenerate differs slightly from the above. Namely, if
s_{1}=1
, then there are no critical values of
\tilde{g}_{1}(\cdot,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t})
for any
t\in[0,2\pi]
. In other words,
\tfrac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial u}
never vanishes, so that
f_{1}
is strongly non-degenerate. Note that in this case there is no restriction on our choice of
r_{1}\neq 0
.

Since the lowest order term of
f_{j}
(with respect to
u
) is equal to the highest order term of
f_{j-1}
, we can combine the different
f_{j}
s to one semiholomorphic boundary polynomial
f
with face functions
f_{P_{i}}=f_{i}
, where
P_{i}=(k_{i},1)
.

We have already shown that the face functions
f_{j}
associated with 1-faces are strongly non-degenerate. Since
f
is semiholomorphic, all non-extreme vertices are associated with strongly non-degenerate face functions as well. Therefore
f
satisfies Condition (ii) in Definition
6.1
.

Thus in order to prove that
f
is strongly inner non-degenerate we only need to check that
f_{P_{1}}=f_{1}
has no critical points in
\mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\{(u,0):u\in\mathbb{C}\}
and
f_{P_{N}}=f_{N}
has no critical points in
\mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\{(0,v):v\in\mathbb{C}\}
. Since we already know that
f_{1}
and
f_{N}
have no critical points in
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
we only need to check points in
\{(0,v):v\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\}
and
\{(u,0):u\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\}
, respectively.

By construction the only term of
f_{N}
that does not depend on
v
or
\bar{v}
is
u^{\sum_{i=1}^{N}s_{i}}
, so that
(f_{N})_{u}(u,0,0)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}s_{i}\right)u^{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}s_{i}\right)-1}
, whose only root is
u=0
. Hence
f_{N}
satisfies Condition (i) in Definition
6.1
.

Since by assumption
B_{1}
has
O
-multiplicity
m_{1}=0
, we have that
f_{1}
is
v
-convenient.

Recall that, since
f_{1}
is radially weighted homogeneous, semiholomorphic and
v
-convenient, its critical points in
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
correspond to critical points of
\arg(a_{2}(1,t)\tilde{g}_{1}(u,\mathrm{e}^{2r_{1}\mathrm{i}t}))
. However,
r_{1}
is chosen such that
\arg(a_{2}(1,t)\tilde{g}_{1}(u,\mathrm{e}^{2r_{1}\mathrm{i}t}))
is a fibration, so
f_{1}
satisfies Condition (i) in Definition
6.1
and
f
is strongly inner non-degenerate.

It follows from Proposition
6.2
that
f
has an isolated singularity. Since strong inner non-degeneracy implies inner non-degeneracy, we know from Proposition
1.2
that the link of its singularity is given by the closure of the braid
B(B_{1}^{2r_{1}},B_{2}^{2r_{2}},\ldots,B_{N}^{2r_{N}})
.
∎

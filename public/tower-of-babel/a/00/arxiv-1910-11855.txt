A Weyl Law for the p p -Laplacian
Liam Mazurowski

Abstract
. We show that a Weyl law holds for the variational spectrum of the p p -Laplacian. More precisely, let ( λ i ) i = 1 ∞ (\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty} be the variational spectrum of Δ p \Delta_{p} on a closed Riemannian manifold ( X , g ) (X,g) and let N ⁡ ( λ ) = # ⁡ { i : λ i < λ } N(\lambda)=\#\{i:\,\lambda_{i}<\lambda\} be the associated counting function. Then we have a Weyl law N ⁡ ( λ ) ∼ c ​ vol ⁡ ( X ) ​ λ n / p . N(\lambda)\sim c\operatorname{vol}(X)\lambda^{n/p}. This confirms a conjecture of Friedlander. The proof is based on ideas of Gromov [ 5 ] and Liokumovich, Marques, Neves [ 7 ] .



1.
Introduction

The classical Weyl law states that the Dirichlet eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
of
\Delta
on a domain
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
grow according to the asymptotics

\#\{i:\,\lambda_{i}<\lambda\}\sim c\operatorname{vol}(U)\lambda^{n/2}

where
c
is a universal constant that depends only on
n
.
Here the notation
f(\lambda)\sim g(\lambda)
means that
f(\lambda)/g(\lambda)\to 1
as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Briefly, the idea of the proof is to use the variational characterization

(1)

\lambda_{i}=\inf_{i\text{-planes }P\subset W^{1,2}_{0}(U)}\bigg(\sup_{u\in P\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\int_{U}|\nabla u|^{2}}{\int_{U}|u|^{2}}\bigg)

to relate the eigenvalues of
\Delta
on
U
with the eigenvalues of
\Delta
on a union of cubes that closely approximates
U
. The eigenvalues of
\Delta
on a cube can be computed explicitly and the formula then follows. The Weyl law also holds for the spectrum of
\Delta
on a closed Riemannian manifold. This can be proved by studying the asymptotics of the heat kernel.

In
[
1
]
, the authors used variational methods to produce a sequence of eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
for the
p
-Laplacian
\Delta_{p}
. These eigenvalues are given by a min-max formula roughly similar to (
1
).
In
[
3
]
, Friedlander studied the asymptotic growth of these eigenvalues of
\Delta_{p}
and proved growth bounds of the form

C_{1}\operatorname{vol}(U)\lambda^{n/p}\leq\#\{i:\,\lambda_{i}<\lambda\}\leq C_{2}\operatorname{vol}(U)\lambda^{n/p}

for some constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
that depend only on
n
and
p
.
Moreover, Friedlander conjectured that a Weyl law should hold in this setting.
In this paper, we prove the following theorem which confirms Friedlander’s conjecture.

Theorem A
.

Let
1<p<\infty
. Let
(X^{n},g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold and let
(\lambda_{i})
be the variational spectrum of
\Delta_{p}
on
X
. Then

\#\{i:\,\lambda_{i}<\lambda\}\sim c\operatorname{vol}(X)\lambda^{n/p}

where
c
is a universal constant that depends only on
n
and
p
.

The proof is based on a general framework for studying Weyl laws proposed by Gromov in
[
5
]
. We also use ideas from the proof of the Weyl law for the volume spectrum due to Liokumovich, Marques, and Neves
[
7
]
. It is worth noting that the proof seems to be new even in the case
p=2
in the sense that it avoids the use of the heat kernel.

2.
Preliminaries: The Variational Spectrum of
\Delta_{p}

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary. If
u
is a smooth function on
U
then the
p
-Laplacian of
u
is defined by

\Delta_{p}u:=\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u).

The Dirichlet eigenvalue problem for
\Delta_{p}
asks for a function
u
satisfying

(2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u,&\text{in }U\\
u=0,&\text{on }\partial U.\end{cases}

A function
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(U)
is called a weak solution of (
2
) provided

\int_{U}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi=\lambda\int_{U}|u|^{p-2}u\phi

for every test function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
. If
u
is a non-trivial weak solution of (
2
) then
\lambda
is called a Dirichlet eigenvalue for
\Delta_{p}
on
U
and
u
is called an eigenfunction with eigenvalue
\lambda
.

It is possible to use variational methods to produce a sequence of eigenvalues for
\Delta_{p}
on
U
. This was done in
[
1
]
. There is also a detailed treatment in the book
[
8
]
. For clarity we outline the argument from
[
8
]
below.
Let
\mathcal{M}^{0}=\mathcal{M}^{0}(U)
be the set

\mathcal{M}^{0}:=\left\{u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(U):\,\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(U)}=1\right\}

equipped with the topology it inherits as a subspace of
W^{1,p}_{0}
with the norm topology.
This is a
C^{1}
Banach manifold (see
[
8
]
). There is an energy functional
E:\mathcal{M}^{0}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

E(u):=\frac{\int_{U}|\nabla u|^{p}}{\int_{U}|u|^{p}}

and the eigenfunctions of (
2
) are precisely the critical points of
E
on
\mathcal{M}^{0}
. Moreover,
E
satisfies the Palais-Smale compactness condition (
[
8
]
Lemma 4.5).

Critical points of
E
can be produced using a min-max argument with the cohomological index. The following discussion of the cohomological index is based on Chapter 2 of
[
8
]
. Note that there is a natural
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-action on
\mathcal{M}^{0}
and that
E
respects this action, i.e.,
E(u)=E(-u)
.

Definition 2.1
.

A subset
A\subset\mathcal{M}^{0}
is symmetric provided that
u\in A
if and only if
-u\in A
for every
u\in\mathcal{M}^{0}
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-space is a Hausdorff, paracompact topological space equipped with a free
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
action.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
A
and
B
be
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-spaces. A map
f:A\to B
is called odd provided

f(-a)=-f(a)\text{ for all }a\in A.

A symmetric set
A\subset\mathcal{M}^{0}
is paracompact since
\mathcal{M}^{0}
is a metric space and every metric space is paracompact. Moreover,
A
comes equipped with a free
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-action
u\mapsto-u
.
Thus
A
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-space. Now assume that
A
is any
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-space and let
\overline{A}
be the quotient
A/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Then

\pi:A\to\overline{A}

is a principal
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-bundle over
\overline{A}
and so there is a classifying map

f\colon\overline{A}\to{\mathbb{R}\text{P}}^{\infty}.

Let
H^{*}_{AS}
denote Alexander-Spanier cohomology.
The cohomology ring
H_{AS}^{*}({\mathbb{R}\text{P}}^{\infty};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[\sigma]
where
\sigma
is the non-zero element in
H_{AS}^{1}({\mathbb{R}\text{P}}^{\infty};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
and the classifying map induces a map in cohomology

f^{*}:H_{AS}^{*}({\mathbb{R}\text{P}}^{\infty};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\to H_{AS}^{*}(\overline{A};\mathbb{Z}_{2}).

The following definition is originally due to Fadell and Rabinowitz
[
2
]
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
A
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-space and let
f
be a classifying map for the bundle
\pi\colon A\to\overline{A}
. Then the cohomological index of
A
is

\operatorname{ind}_{AS}(A)=\sup\{k\geq 1:\,f^{*}(\sigma^{k-1})\neq 0\text{ in }H_{AS}^{*}(\overline{A};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\}.

By convention
\operatorname{ind}_{AS}(\emptyset)=0
.

The properties of the cohomological index will be discussed further in the next section of the paper.

It remains to perform the min-max argument. For complete details see Chapter 4 of
[
8
]
. Let
\mathcal{F}^{0}=\mathcal{F}^{0}(U)
be the collection of all symmetric subsets of
\mathcal{M}^{0}
. Define the classes

\mathcal{F}_{k}^{0}=\mathcal{F}_{k}^{0}(U)=\{A\in\mathcal{F}^{0}:\,\operatorname{ind}_{AS}(A)\geq k\}

and then consider the min-max values

\lambda_{k}=\lambda_{k}(U)=\inf_{A\in\mathcal{F}_{k}^{0}}\sup_{u\in A}E(u).

By
[
8
]
Theorem 4.6, the numbers
\lambda_{k}
are Dirichlet eigenvalues of
\Delta_{p}
on
U
and
\lambda_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. Moreover, if we define the counting function

N_{U}^{0}(\lambda)=\#\{j:\,\lambda_{j}<\lambda\}

then
N_{U}^{0}
satisfies
N_{U}^{0}(\lambda)=\operatorname{ind}_{AS}(E^{-1}[0,\lambda))
. The sequence
(\lambda_{k})
is called the variational spectrum of
\Delta_{p}
on
U
.
It doesn’t seem to be known (see
[
8
]
page 71) whether the variational spectrum contains every eigenvalue of
\Delta_{p}
.

Remark
.

Let
A
be a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-space with classifying map
f
. Let
H^{*}
denote singular cohomology and let
\sigma
be the non-zero element in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}\text{P}}^{\infty};\mathbb{Z}_{2})
. Define

\operatorname{ind}(A)=\sup\{k\geq 1:\,f^{*}(\sigma^{k-1})\neq 0\text{ in }H^{*}(\overline{A};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\}.

If
A
is locally contractible then the Alexander-Spanier cohomology of
A
is isomorphic to the singular cohomology of
A
(see
[
9
]
) and thus
\operatorname{ind}_{AS}(A)=\operatorname{ind}(A)
. Suppose now that
A=E^{-1}[0,\lambda)\subset\mathcal{M}^{0}
. Note that
\mathcal{M}^{0}
is locally contractible since it is a Banach manifold. Thus
A
is also locally contractible since it is an open subset of a locally contractible space. It follows that
\operatorname{ind}_{AS}(A)=\operatorname{ind}(A)
. In particular, the counting function satisfies
N^{0}_{U}(\lambda)=\operatorname{ind}(E^{-1}[0,\lambda))
.

3.
Preliminaries: The Neumann Problem

It will be useful to simultaneously investigate the corresponding Neumann eigenvalue problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{p}u=\lambda|u|^{p-2}u,&\text{in }U\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=0,&\text{on }\partial U.\end{cases}

To this end, define the set

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}(U):=\left\{u\in W^{1,p}(U):\,\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(U)}=1\right\}.

Again there is an energy
E:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

E(u)=\frac{\int_{U}|\nabla u|^{p}}{\int_{U}|u|^{p}}.

Define the counting function

N_{U}(\lambda)=\operatorname{ind}(E^{-1}[0,\lambda)).

Then
N_{U}^{0}(\lambda)\leq N_{U}(\lambda)
for every
\lambda
.
We will show that the Neumann counting function also satisfies a Weyl law
N_{U}(\lambda)\sim c\operatorname{vol}(U)\lambda^{n/p}
.

Remark
.

Throughout the paper objects associated with the Dirichlet problem will be decorated with a superscript zero while the corresponding objects in the Neumann problem will appear without decoration. Thus
N^{0}_{U}
denotes the counting function for the Dirichlet problem while
N_{U}
denotes the counting function for the Neumann problem, and so on. This is consistent with the notation in
[
5
]
.

4.
Preliminaries: Properties of the Cohomological Index

This section collects a few properties of the cohomological index that will be needed later.
Proofs of the following properties can be found in
[
2
]
or
[
8
]
. Since the proofs are relatively short, we reproduce them below for the convenience of the reader.

Proposition 4.1
.

The index
\operatorname{ind}
satisfies the following properties.

(i)

If
A
,
B
are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-spaces and there is an odd continuous map
f:A\to B
then
\operatorname{ind}(A)\leq\operatorname{ind}(B)
.

(ii)

If
X
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-space and
A,B\subset X
are open, paracompact symmetric subsets with
X=A\cup B
then
\operatorname{ind}(X)\leq\operatorname{ind}(A)+\operatorname{ind}(B)
.

Proof.

(i) Let
\overline{f}:\overline{A}\to\overline{B}
be the induced map and let
g:\overline{B}\to{\mathbb{R}\text{P}}^{\infty}
be a classifying map. Then
g\overline{f}:\overline{A}\to{\mathbb{R}\text{P}}^{\infty}
serves as a classifying map for
\overline{A}
. Notice that there are maps

H^{*}({\mathbb{R}\text{P}}^{\infty};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\overset{g^{*}}{\longrightarrow}H^{*}(\overline{B};\mathbb{Z}_{2})\overset{\overline{f}^{*}}{\longrightarrow}H^{*}(\overline{A};\mathbb{Z}_{2})

and thus
\operatorname{ind}(A)\leq\operatorname{ind}(B)
.

(ii) Let
f:\overline{X}\to{\mathbb{R}\text{P}}^{\infty}
be a classifying map and let

i_{A}:\overline{A}\to\overline{X},\quad i_{B}:\overline{B}\to\overline{X}

be the inclusions. Then
f\iota_{A}
is a classifying map for
\overline{A}
and
f\iota_{B}
is a classifying map for
\overline{B}
. Without loss, we may assume that
\operatorname{ind}(A)=i
and
\operatorname{ind}(B)=j
are finite. Put
\theta=f^{*}\sigma
. Then
\iota_{A}^{*}\theta^{i}=0
and
\iota_{B}^{*}\theta^{j}=0
. There are exact sequences

\displaystyle H^{i}(\overline{X},\overline{A})\to H^{i}(\overline{X})\to H^{i}(\overline{A}),

\displaystyle H^{j}(\overline{X},\overline{B})\to H^{j}(\overline{X})\to H^{i}(\overline{B}),

and hence there are classes
\theta_{A}\in H^{i}(\overline{X};\overline{A})
and
\theta_{B}\in H^{j}(\overline{X};\overline{B})
that map to
\theta
.

Since
A
,
B
are open in
X
there is a relative cup product

\smile:H^{*}(\overline{X},\overline{A})\times H^{*}(\overline{X},\overline{B})\to H^{*}(\overline{X},\overline{A}\cup\overline{B}).

Moreover, since
\overline{X}=\overline{A}\cup\overline{B}
, it follows that
\theta_{A}\smile\theta_{B}=0
in
H^{*}(\overline{X},\overline{A}\cup\overline{B})
. By naturality,
\theta^{i+j}
is the image of
\theta_{A}\smile\theta_{B}
under the map

H^{*}(\overline{X},\overline{A}\cup\overline{B})\to H^{*}(\overline{X}).

Therefore
\theta^{i+j}=0
and the result follows.
∎

One further property will be required. Assume that
A
and
B
are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-spaces. By definition, their join is the quotient

A*B:=(A\times B\times[0,1])/\sim

where
(a_{1},b,0)\sim(a_{2},b,0)
and
(a,b_{1},1)\sim(a,b_{2},1)
. This is also
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-space in a natural way.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
A
and
B
be
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-spaces. Then

\operatorname{ind}(A)+\operatorname{ind}(B)\leq\operatorname{ind}(A*B).

Remark
.

The special case of this proposition where
B=S^{0}
is proven in
[
2
]
. Presumably the general case is also known, but we could not find a reference in the literature and hence we provide a proof below.

Our proof of Proposition
4.2
is based on a join operation in homology constructed in
[
4
]
.
We summarize the construction below.
Let
X
and
Y
be topological spaces.
If
\Delta^{m}
and
\Delta^{n}
are the standard simplices, then there is a natural identification
\Delta^{m}*\Delta^{n}\cong\Delta^{m+n+1}
. Thus given singular simplices
\alpha:\Delta^{m}\to X
and
\beta:\Delta^{n}\to Y
one can form a new singular simplex

\alpha*\beta:\Delta^{m+n+1}\to X*Y.

In the following all groups have
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-coefficients, even where this is not explicitly indicated in the notation.
Extending linearly, there is a map on chains

*:C_{m}(X)\otimes C_{n}(Y)\to C_{m+n+1}(X*Y)

and since

\partial(\alpha*\beta)=(\partial\alpha)*\beta+\alpha*(\partial\beta),

this descends to a map in homology

*:H_{m}(X)\otimes H_{n}(Y)\to H_{m+n+1}(X*Y).

Using this, it is possible to construct an equivariant join operation on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-spaces.

Suppose now that
X
and
Y
are
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-spaces and recall that
\overline{X}=X/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
\overline{Y}=Y/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
Let
g
be the antipodal map on some
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-space
Z
. Then a chain
c
in
Z
is called
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-equivariant provided
g_{\#}c=c
. Following
[
4
]
, let
C_{*}(Z)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
denote the set of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-equivariant chains in
Z
. There is a natural identification

C_{*}(\overline{Z})\leftrightarrow C_{*}(Z)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}

given by sending
\alpha:\Delta^{m}\to\overline{Z}
to the sum of its two lifts to
Z
. Since the join operation respects equivariance, there is an induced operation

\displaystyle*:C_{m}(X)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\otimes C_{n}(Y)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}\to C_{m+n+1}(X*Y)^{\mathbb{Z}_{2}}.

Using the above equivalence, this gives an operation

\star:C_{m}(\overline{X})\otimes C_{n}(\overline{Y})\to C_{m+n+1}(\overline{X*Y})

and again this descends to give an operation in homology

\star:H_{m}(\overline{X})\otimes H_{n}(\overline{Y})\to H_{m+n+1}(\overline{X*Y}).

It is possible to compute this map in the case where
X=S^{\infty}
and
Y=S^{\infty}
.

Take
X=S^{\infty}
and
Y=S^{\infty}
. Note that
\overline{X}
and
\overline{Y}
are both homeomorphic to
{\mathbb{R}\text{P}}^{\infty}
and hence
H_{*}(\overline{X})\cong H_{*}(\overline{Y})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}[x]
in the sense of additive groups. Now think of
S^{\infty}
as the set of points
(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{\infty}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}x_{i}^{2}=1

and all but finitely many
x_{i}
are equal to 0. There is a homeomorphism
j:X*Y\to S^{\infty}
given by

j((x_{i}),(y_{i}),t)=(x_{1}\sqrt{t},y_{1}\sqrt{1-t},x_{2}\sqrt{t},y_{2}\sqrt{1-t},\ldots).

This gives an identification of
X*Y
with
S^{\infty}
and hence
H_{*}(\overline{X*Y})
is also isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}[x]
.

Fix a pair of non-negative integers
m
and
n
. Let
\mathcal{X}
be the set of points
(x_{i})
in
X
such that
x_{i}=0
for all
i>m+1
, and let
\mathcal{Y}
be the set of points
(y_{i})
in
Y
such that
y_{i}=0
for all
i>n+1
. Let

\overline{\mathcal{X}}\subset\overline{X},\quad\overline{\mathcal{Y}}\subset\overline{Y}

be the quotients. Then
[\overline{\mathcal{X}}]
is the non-trivial element of
H_{m}(\overline{X})
and
[\overline{\mathcal{Y}}]
is the non-trivial element of
H_{n}(\overline{Y})
. Let
\mathcal{Z}
be the set of points
(z_{i})
in
S^{\infty}
such that
z_{2i-1}=0
for all
i>m+1
and
z_{2j}=0
for all
j>n+1
.
Then from the definition of the operation
\star
, it follows that

j_{*}([\overline{\mathcal{X}}]\star[\overline{\mathcal{Y}}])=[\overline{\mathcal{Z}}].

Since
j_{*}
is an isomorphism, this means that
[\overline{\mathcal{X}}]\star[\overline{\mathcal{Y}}]
is the non-trivial class in
H_{m+n+1}(\overline{X*Y})
.

Proof.

(Proposition
4.2
)
Let
\overline{A}=A/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and
\overline{B}=B/\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and let

f:\overline{A}\to{\mathbb{R}\text{P}}^{\infty},\quad g:\overline{B}\to{\mathbb{R}\text{P}}^{\infty}

be classifying maps for the bundles
A\to\overline{A}
,
B\to\overline{B}
. Recall that there is a homeomorphism
j:S^{\infty}*S^{\infty}\to S^{\infty}
.
Since
f,g
are classifying maps, we get the following commutative diagrams.
A
\overline{A}
S^{\infty}
{\mathbb{R}\text{P}}^{\infty}
\widetilde{f}
f
B
\overline{B}
S^{\infty}
{\mathbb{R}\text{P}}^{\infty}
\widetilde{g}
g

Thus it is possible to define a map
\widetilde{h}=\widetilde{f}*\widetilde{g}:A*B\to S^{\infty}*S^{\infty}
.
Moreover
\widetilde{h}
is odd and so it induces a map

h:\overline{A*B}\to\overline{S^{\infty}*S^{\infty}}\cong{\mathbb{R}\text{P}}^{\infty}.

This is a classifying map for the bundle
A*B\to\overline{A*B}
.

Since
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is a field, the universal coefficient theorem implies that

H^{i}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong\operatorname{Hom}(H_{i}(X;\mathbb{Z}_{2}),\mathbb{Z}_{2}).

Now assume
m+1\leq\operatorname{ind}(A)
and
n+1\leq\operatorname{ind}(B)
. Then
f^{*}(\sigma^{m})
and
g^{*}(\sigma^{n})
are non-zero and hence there exist classes

\alpha\in H_{m}(\overline{A};\mathbb{Z}_{2}),\quad\beta\in H_{n}(\overline{B};\mathbb{Z}_{2})

such that
f_{*}\alpha
and

are non-zero. Define
\gamma=\alpha\star\beta\in H_{m+n+1}(\overline{A*B})
. By naturality of the above construction, it follows that

But we know the class on the right hand side of the above equation is non-zero and therefore

is non-zero. The result follows.
∎

5.
The Dirichlet Domain Monotonicity Inequality

Assume that
V,W
are disjoint open sets with
V,W\subseteq U
. Recall that when
p=2
the Dirichlet eigenvalues of the Laplacian satisfy a domain monotonicity inequality of the form

N^{0}_{U}(\lambda)\geq N^{0}_{V}(\lambda)+N^{0}_{W}(\lambda).

We want to show that a similar inequality holds for arbitrary
p
. The arguments in this section closely follow Gromov in
[
5
]
. The first step is to prove an inequality relating the energy of functions
v
on
V
and
w
on
W
with the energy of
v+w
on
U
.

Lemma 5.1

(See
[
5
]
Lemma 3.2.A)
.

Assume
V,W
are disjoint open sets with
V,W\subset U
. Let
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(V)
and
w\in W^{1,p}_{0}(W)
not both zero and define
u:=v+w\in W^{1,p}_{0}(U)
. Then
E(u)\leq\max\{E(v),E(w)\}
.

Proof.

This is obvious if either
v\equiv 0
or
w\equiv 0
, so we may assume that
v
and
w
are not identically 0. Without loss of generality, we may also assume that
E(w)\leq E(v)
. Define the numbers

\displaystyle a:=\int_{V}|\nabla v|^{p},\quad b:=\int_{W}|\nabla w|^{p},\quad c:=\int_{V}|v|^{p},\quad d:=\int_{W}|w|^{p}.

Elementary manipulations show that

\frac{a+b}{c+d}\leq\frac{a}{c}\iff\frac{b}{d}\leq\frac{a}{c},

and the inequality on the right holds since
E(w)\leq E(v)
. It follows that

E(u)=\frac{a+b}{c+d}\leq\frac{a}{c}=E(v).

and the lemma is proven.
∎

The next step is to prove a monotonicity inequality.

Proposition 5.2

(See
[
5
]
3.2.A
1
)
.

Assume
V,W
are disjoint open sets with
V,W\subset U
. Then we have
N_{U}^{0}(\lambda)\geq N^{0}_{V}(\lambda)+N^{0}_{W}(\lambda)
.

Proof.

Define the sets

\displaystyle A:=\{v\in\mathcal{M}^{0}(V):\,E(v)<\lambda\},

\displaystyle B:=\{w\in\mathcal{M}^{0}(W):\,E(w)<\lambda\},

\displaystyle C:=\{u\in\mathcal{M}^{0}(U):\,E(u)<\lambda\}.

Then
N^{0}_{V}(\lambda)=\operatorname{ind}(A)
,
N^{0}_{W}(\lambda)=\operatorname{ind}(B)
,
N^{0}_{U}(\lambda)=\operatorname{ind}(C)
and hence we must verify that

\operatorname{ind}(A)+\operatorname{ind}(B)\leq\operatorname{ind}(C).

Consider the join
A*B
. The map
A*B\to\mathcal{M}^{0}(U)
given by

(v,w,t)\mapsto tv+(1-t)w

is a homeomorphism onto its image. Hence
A*B
can be viewed as a subset of
\mathcal{M}^{0}(U)
.
Lemma
5.1
shows that actually
A*B\subset C
and thus

\operatorname{ind}(A*B)\leq\operatorname{ind}(C)

by Proposition
4.1
(i).
But Proposition
4.2
says

\operatorname{ind}(A)+\operatorname{ind}(B)\leq\operatorname{ind}(A*B).

Combining these inequalities gives
\operatorname{ind}(A)+\operatorname{ind}(B)\leq\operatorname{ind}(C)
, as needed.
∎

It is possible to be slightly more general. Given
U
and a positive real number
a
, let
aU
be the set
\{ax:\,x\in U\}
. Then the scaling properties of the energy lead to a relationship between
N^{0}_{U}(\lambda)
and
N^{0}_{aU}(\lambda)
. This is the content of the following proposition.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
a
be a positive real number. Then
N^{0}_{aU}(\lambda)=N^{0}_{U}(a^{p}\lambda)
.

Proof.

Define a map
g:W^{1,p}_{0}(U)\setminus\{0\}\to W^{1,p}_{0}(aU)\setminus\{0\}
by

(gu)(x)=u(x/a).

This map induces a homeomorphism

g:\mathcal{M}^{0}(U)\cong\mathcal{M}^{0}(aU).

Thus
\operatorname{ind}(A)=\operatorname{ind}(g(A))
for every symmetric
A\subset\mathcal{M}^{0}(U)
. Moreover, a straightforward calculation shows that

E(gu)=a^{-p}E(u)

for every
u\in\mathcal{M}^{0}(U)
. The result follows.
∎

Now suppose that
U,U_{1},\ldots,U_{m}
are open sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and that
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
are positive real numbers. Following Gromov
[
5
]
, we will write

\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}U_{i}\prec U

if there exist elements
b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that the translates
a_{i}U_{i}+b_{i}
are all disjoint and contained in
U
. Using Proposition
5.2
and Proposition
5.3
and induction shows that

(3)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}U_{i}\prec U\implies N^{0}_{U}(\lambda)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}N_{U_{i}}^{0}(a_{i}^{p}\lambda).

We will refer to this as the Dirichlet domain monotonicity inequality.

6.
The Weyl Law for Dirichlet eigenvalues

In this section we prove the Dirichlet Weyl law for domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The first step is to prove the Weyl law for a cube. The argument is essentially the same as the proof of Lemma 3.3 in
[
7
]
. Also see the Trivial Lemma in Section 3.4 of
[
5
]
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
C
be the unit cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and define
f(\lambda)=\lambda^{-n/p}N^{0}_{C}(\lambda)
. Then
f
tends to a limit as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Proof.

Choose sequences
(\lambda_{j})
,
(\mu_{k})
so that

\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}f(\lambda)=\lim_{j\to\infty}f(\lambda_{j}),\quad\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}f(\lambda)=\lim_{k\to\infty}f(\mu_{k}).

Now fix some
j
and consider
k
large. Let
M_{k}
be the largest integer such that it is possible to pack
M_{k}
disjoint open cubes of volume
(\lambda_{j}/\mu_{k})^{n/p}
into
C
. Then

\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{k}}({\lambda_{j}}/{\mu_{k}})^{1/p}C\prec C

and it follows by the domain monotonicity inequality (
3
) that

N_{C}^{0}(\lambda)\geq M_{k}N_{C}^{0}(\lambda_{j}\lambda/\mu_{k}).

Choose
\lambda=\mu_{k}
and multiply both sides by
\mu_{k}^{-n/p}
to get

\mu_{k}^{-n/p}N_{C}^{0}(\mu_{k})\geq M_{k}\mu_{k}^{-n/p}N_{C}^{0}(\lambda_{j}).

Now let
k\to\infty
and use the fact that
M_{k}\mu_{k}^{-n/p}\to\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}
to get

\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}f(\lambda)\geq\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}N_{C}^{0}(\lambda_{j}).

Finally let
j\to\infty
to get

\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}f(\lambda)\geq\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}f(\lambda).

The result follows.
∎

Define
c^{0}=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}f(\lambda)
.
As mentioned in Section 1, estimates of Friedlander
[
3
]
imply that

C_{1}\lambda^{n/p}\leq N_{C}^{0}(\lambda)\leq C_{2}\lambda^{n/p},\quad\text{as }\lambda\to\infty.

It follows at once that
0<c^{0}<\infty
. Thus the Weyl law holds on the unit cube, i.e., we have
N_{C}^{0}(\lambda)\sim c^{0}\lambda^{n/p}
.

It is also possible to give a self-contained proof that
0<c^{0}<\infty
which does not rely on the estimates of Friedlander. The next proposition shows that
c^{0}>0
. We will give the proof that
c^{0}<\infty
in a later section.

Proposition 6.2
.

Put
c^{0}=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-n/p}N^{0}_{C}(\lambda)
. Then
c^{0}>0
.

Proof.

As above let
f(\lambda)=\lambda^{-n/p}N^{0}_{C}(\lambda)
. Fix some
\lambda>0
and some positive integer
k
. It is possible to divide unit cube into
k^{n}
disjoint open cubes of volume
k^{-n}
. Hence

\sum_{\ell=1}^{k^{n}}k^{-1}C\prec C

and so the domain monotonicity inequality implies

N^{0}_{C}(\lambda)\geq k^{n}N^{0}_{C}(k^{-p}\lambda).

Multiplying by
\lambda^{-n/p}
yields

f(\lambda)=\lambda^{-n/p}N^{0}_{C}(\lambda)\geq(k^{-p}\lambda)^{-n/p}N^{0}_{C}(k^{-p}\lambda)=f(k^{-p}\lambda).

Now there exists some
\lambda_{1}
such that
N^{0}_{C}(\lambda)\geq 1
for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{1}
. Hence
f
has a positive minimum
C_{1}
on the interval
[\lambda_{1},2^{p}\lambda_{1}]
. Since

(k+1)^{p}\leq(2k)^{p},\quad\text{for all }k\geq 1

it follows that

[\lambda_{1},\infty)=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}[k^{p}\lambda_{1},k^{p}2^{p}\lambda_{1}].

Therefore, given an arbitrary
\lambda\geq\lambda_{1}
, there exists some positive integer
k
such that
k^{-p}\lambda\in[\lambda_{1},2^{p}\lambda_{1}]
. Using the above inequality, it follows that

f(\lambda)\geq f(k^{-p}\lambda)\geq C_{1}.

This proves that
c^{0}\geq C_{1}>0
, as needed.
∎

Finally we derive the Weyl law for a general domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary. Then
N_{U}^{0}(\lambda)\sim c^{0}\operatorname{vol}(U)\lambda^{n/p}
as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
U
has unit volume. Define the numbers

\underline{\beta}(U)=\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-n/p}N_{U}^{0}(\lambda),\quad\overline{\beta}(U)=\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-n/p}N_{U}^{0}(\lambda).

Let
\varepsilon>0
and choose numbers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}C\prec U

and
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{n}\geq 1-\varepsilon
. Applying the domain monotonicity inequality gives

N_{U}^{0}(\lambda)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}N_{C}^{0}(a_{i}^{p}\lambda).

But
N_{C}^{0}(a_{i}^{p}\lambda)\sim c^{0}a_{i}^{n}\lambda^{n/p}
as
\lambda\to\infty
since the Weyl law holds for cubes. Hence multiplying both sides of the above inequality by
\lambda^{-n/p}
and letting
\lambda\to\infty
it follows that

\underline{\beta}(U)\geq c^{0}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{n}\right)\geq c(1-\varepsilon).

Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary this implies that
\underline{\beta}(U)\geq c^{0}
.

It remains to show that
\overline{\beta}(U)\leq c^{0}
. To this end, fix
a>0
so that some translate of
aU
is contained in
C
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and choose numbers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
so that

aU+\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}C\prec C

and
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{n}\geq 1-a^{n}-\varepsilon
. Then

N_{C}^{0}(\lambda)\geq N_{U}^{0}(a^{p}\lambda)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}N_{C}^{0}(a_{i}^{p}\lambda).

Pick a sequence
\lambda_{j}\to\infty
so that

a^{-n}\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}N_{U}^{0}(a^{p}\lambda_{j})\to\overline{\beta}(U).

Taking
\lambda=\lambda_{j}
in the above equation and multiplying both sides by
a^{-n}\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}
gives

a^{-n}\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}N_{C}^{0}(\lambda_{j})\geq a^{-n}\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}N_{U}^{0}(a^{p}\lambda_{j})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}a^{-n}\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}N_{C}^{0}(a_{i}^{p}\lambda_{j}).

Letting
j\to\infty
now yields

\displaystyle a^{-n}c^{0}

\displaystyle\geq\overline{\beta}(U)+a^{-n}\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{n}c^{0}

\displaystyle\geq\overline{\beta}(U)+a^{-n}(1-a^{n}-\varepsilon)c^{0}.

Therefore

\overline{\beta}(U)\leq(1+a^{-n}\varepsilon)c^{0}

and hence
\overline{\beta}(U)\leq c^{0}
since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary. This proves that
\overline{\beta}(U)=\underline{\beta}(U)=c^{0}
and so the Weyl law
N_{U}^{0}(\lambda)\sim c^{0}\lambda^{n/p}
holds for
U
.
∎

7.
The Neumann Monotonicity Inequality

Assume that
V,W
are open subsets of
U
with
\overline{U}=\overline{V}\cup\overline{W}
and that
\overline{V}\cap\overline{W}
has measure 0. Recall that when
p=2
, the Neumann eigenvalues of the Laplacian satisfy a domain monotonicity inequality of the form

N_{U}(\lambda)\leq N_{V}(\lambda)+N_{W}(\lambda).

We want to show that a similar inequality holds for arbitrary
p
. Again the first step is to prove an energy inequality.

Lemma 7.1

(See
[
5
]
Lemma 3.2.A)
.

Assume
V,W
are open subsets of
U
with
\overline{U}=\overline{V}\cup\overline{W}
and that
\overline{V}\cap\overline{W}
has measure 0. Let
u\in W^{1,p}(U)
and then define
v=u|_{V}\in W^{1,p}(V)
and
w=u|_{W}\in W^{1,p}(W)
so that
u=v+w
. Assume neither
v
nor
w
is identically zero. Then
E(u)\geq\min\{E(v),E(w)\}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
E(v)\leq E(w)
. Define the numbers

a:=\int_{V}|\nabla v|^{p},\quad b:=\int_{W}|\nabla w|^{p},\quad c:=\int_{V}|v|^{p},\quad d:=\int_{W}|w|^{p}.

Elementary manipulations show that

\frac{a+b}{c+d}\geq\frac{a}{c}\iff\frac{a}{c}\leq\frac{b}{d}

and the inequality on the right holds since
E(w)\geq E(v)
.
It follows that

E(u)=\frac{a+b}{c+d}\geq\frac{a}{c}=E(v),

as needed.
∎

The next step is a monotonicity inequality.

Proposition 7.2

(See
[
5
]
3.2.A
2
)
.

Assume
V,W
are open subsets of
U
with
\overline{U}=\overline{V}\cup\overline{W}
and that
\overline{V}\cap\overline{W}
has measure 0. Then
N_{U}(\lambda)\leq N_{V}(\lambda)+N_{W}(\lambda)
.

Proof.

Define the sets

\displaystyle A:=\{v\in\mathcal{M}(V):\,E(v)<\lambda\},

\displaystyle B:=\{w\in\mathcal{M}(W):\,E(w)<\lambda\},

\displaystyle C:=\{u\in\mathcal{M}(U):\,E(u)<\lambda\}.

Then
N_{U}(\lambda)=\operatorname{ind}(C)
and
N_{V}(\lambda)+N_{W}(\lambda)=\operatorname{ind}(A)+\operatorname{ind}(B)
. Hence it is enough to show that

\operatorname{ind}(C)\leq\operatorname{ind}(A)+\operatorname{ind}(B).

To see this, define the sets

\displaystyle C_{A}:=\{u\in C:\,u|_{V}\not\equiv 0\text{ and }E(u|_{V})<\lambda\},

\displaystyle C_{B}:=\{u\in C:\,u|_{W}\not\equiv 0\text{ and }E(u|_{W})<\lambda\}.

Lemma
7.1
shows that
C\subset C_{A}\cup C_{B}
. Moreover, the sets
C_{A}
and
C_{B}
are open in
C
and hence
\operatorname{ind}(C)\leq\operatorname{ind}(C_{A})+\operatorname{ind}(C_{B})
by Proposition
4.1
(ii). But there is an odd continuous map

\displaystyle C_{A}\to A,\quad u\mapsto\frac{u|_{V}}{\|u|_{V}\|}

and therefore
\operatorname{ind}(C_{A})\leq\operatorname{ind}(A)
by Proposition
4.1
(i). The same argument shows that
\operatorname{ind}(C_{B})\leq\operatorname{ind}(B)
and thus
\operatorname{ind}(C)\leq\operatorname{ind}(A)+\operatorname{ind}(B)
. The result follows.
∎

As in the Dirichlet case, this can be combined with the scaling properties of the energy to give a more general monotonicity inequality. Suppose
U,U_{1},\ldots,U_{n}
are open in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
are positive real numbers. Then we will write

\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}U_{i}\approx U

if there exist elements
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\overline{U}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{m}(\overline{a_{i}U+b_{i}})

and each intersection
(\overline{a_{i}U_{i}+b_{i}})\cap(\overline{a_{j}U_{j}+b_{j}})
has measure zero. Using Proposition
4
, the scaling properties of the energy, and induction shows that

(4)

\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}U_{i}\approx U\implies N_{U}(\lambda)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}N_{U_{i}}(a_{i}^{p}\lambda).

We will refer to this as the Neumann domain monotonicity inequality.

8.
The Weyl Law for Neumann Eigenvalues

In this section we prove the Neumann Weyl law for domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The first step is to prove the Weyl law on a cube.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
C
be the unit cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and define
g(\lambda)=\lambda^{-n/p}N_{C}(\lambda)
. Then
g
tends to a limit as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Proof.

Choose sequences
(\lambda_{j})
,
(\mu_{k})
so that

\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}g(\lambda)=\lim_{j\to\infty}g(\lambda_{j}),\quad\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}g(\lambda)=\lim_{k\to\infty}g(\mu_{k}).

Now fix some
k
and consider
j
large. Let
\varepsilon_{j}\geq 0
be the smallest number such that
(\mu_{k}/\lambda_{j}+\varepsilon_{j})^{1/p}
is the reciprocal of an integer. Then cubes of volume
(\mu_{k}/\lambda_{j}+\varepsilon_{j})^{n/p}
partition the cube
C
. Let
M_{j}
be the number of cubes in such a partition.

We now estimate
\varepsilon_{j}
and
M_{j}
. Define
t_{j}:=\mu_{k}/\lambda_{j}
and then let
\ell_{j}
be the largest integer such that

\ell_{j}\leq t_{j}^{-1/p}.

Then
\varepsilon_{j}=\ell_{j}^{-p}-t_{j}\geq 0
. Moreover
t_{j}^{-1/p}-1\leq\ell_{j}\leq t_{j}^{-1/p}
and thus

\varepsilon_{j}\lambda_{j}\leq\left((t_{j}^{-1/p}-1)^{-p}-t_{j}\right)\frac{\mu_{k}}{t_{j}}\to 0,\quad\text{as }j\to\infty.

Also notice that
M_{j}=\ell_{j}^{n}
and therefore

(t_{j}^{-1/p}-1)^{n}\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{t_{j}}\right)^{-n/p}\leq M_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}\leq\mu_{k}^{-n/p}

so that
M_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}\to\mu_{k}^{-n/p}
as
j\to\infty
.

Given these estimates, the result can be obtained as follows. Observe that

\sum_{\ell=1}^{M_{j}}(\mu_{k}/\lambda_{j}+\varepsilon_{j})^{1/p}C\approx C

and hence the domain monotonicity inequality gives

N_{C}(\lambda)\leq M_{j}N_{C}(\mu_{k}\lambda/\lambda_{j}+\varepsilon_{j}\lambda).

Choosing
\lambda=\lambda_{j}
and multiplying both sides by
\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}
yields

\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}N_{C}(\lambda_{j})\leq M_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}N_{C}(\mu_{k}+\varepsilon_{j}\lambda_{j}).

Letting
j\to\infty
and using the fact that
M_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}\to\mu_{k}^{-n/p}
and
\varepsilon_{j}\lambda_{j}\to 0
this gives

\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}g(\lambda)\leq\mu_{k}^{-n/p}N_{C}(\mu_{k}).

Finally let
k\to\infty
to get

\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}g(\lambda)\leq\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}g(\lambda),

as needed.
∎

Define
c=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}g(\lambda)
. It is obvious that
c^{0}\leq c
and hence
c>0
. The next proposition shows that
c<\infty
. It will be shown in a later section that actually
c^{0}=c
.

Proposition 8.2
.

Set
c=\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-n/p}N_{C}(\lambda)
. Then
c<\infty
.

Proof.

As above, let
g(\lambda)=\lambda^{-n/p}N_{C}(\lambda)
. Fix some
\lambda>0
and some positive integer
k
. The unit cube can be partitioned into
k^{n}
cubes of volume
k^{-n}
. Hence

\sum_{\ell=1}^{k^{n}}k^{-1}C\approx C

and so the domain monotonicity inequality implies

N_{C}(\lambda)\leq k^{n}N_{C}(k^{-p}\lambda).

Multiplying by
\lambda^{-n/p}
gives

g(\lambda)=\lambda^{-n/p}N_{C}(\lambda)\leq(k^{-p}\lambda)^{-n/p}N_{C}(k^{-p}\lambda)=g(k^{-p}\lambda).

Now
g
has some finite maximum
C_{2}
on the interval
[1,2^{p}]
. Since

(k+1)^{p}\leq(2k)^{p},\quad\text{for all }k\geq 1

it follows that

[1,\infty)=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}[k^{p},k^{p}2^{p}].

Therefore, given an arbitrary
\lambda\geq 1
, there exists some positive integer
k
such that
k^{-p}\lambda\in[1,2^{p}]
. Using the above inequality, it follows that

g(\lambda)\leq g(k^{-p}\lambda)\leq C_{2}.

This proves that
c\leq C_{2}<\infty
, as needed.
∎

The proof of the following proposition is somewhat technical so we delay it until Section
11
.

Proposition 8.3
.

Let
W\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set with Lipschitz boundary. Then

N_{W}(\lambda)\leq C_{2}\operatorname{vol}(W)\lambda^{n/p},

as
\lambda\to\infty
. Here
C_{2}
is a constant that depends on
n,

p
, and the Lipschitz constant of
W
.

We can now prove the Neumann Weyl law for a general domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 8.4
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open bounded set with Lipschitz boundary. Then we have
N_{V}(\lambda)\sim c\operatorname{vol}(V)\lambda^{n/p}
as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
U
has unit volume. Define the numbers

\underline{\gamma}(U)=\lim\inf_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-n/p}N_{U}(\lambda),\quad\overline{\gamma}(U)=\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-n/p}N_{U}(\lambda).

Given
\varepsilon>0
, there exist numbers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
and a set
V
with Lipschitz boundary such that
\operatorname{vol}(V)\leq\varepsilon
and

V+\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}C\approx U.

Moreover, it is possible to choose
V
so that
\operatorname{lip}(V)\leq K
for some constant
K
that depends only on
\operatorname{lip}(U)
and the dimension
n
. In particular, this means that
N_{V}(\lambda)
satisfies a growth bound

N_{V}(\lambda)\leq C_{2}\operatorname{vol}(V)\lambda^{n/p}\leq C_{2}\varepsilon\lambda^{n/p}

as
\lambda\to\infty
where the constant
C_{2}
is independent of
\varepsilon
.

Applying the domain monotonicity inequality shows that

N_{U}(\lambda)\leq N_{V}(\lambda)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}N_{C}(a_{i}^{p}\lambda).

But
N_{C}(a_{i}^{p}\lambda)\sim ca_{i}^{n}\lambda^{n/p}
as
\lambda\to\infty
by the Weyl law for cubes. Hence multiplying both sides of the above inequality by
\lambda^{-n/p}
and letting
\lambda\to\infty
it follows that

\overline{\gamma}(U)\leq c\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{n}\right)+C_{2}\varepsilon=c(1-\varepsilon)+C_{2}\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary, this implies
\overline{\gamma}(U)\leq c
.

It remains to show that
\underline{\gamma}(U)\geq c
. To this end, fix
a>0
so that some translate of
aU
is contained in
C
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and choose numbers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}
and a set
V
with Lipschitz boundary such that
\operatorname{vol}(V)\leq\varepsilon
and

C\approx V+aU+\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}C.

It is possible to pick
V
so that
\operatorname{lip}(V)\leq K
where
K
is some constant depending only on
\operatorname{lip}(U)
and
n
.
Notice then that
\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{n}=1-a^{n}-\operatorname{vol}(V)
.
The domain monotonicity inequality gives

N_{C}(\lambda)\leq N_{V}(\lambda)+N_{U}(a^{p}\lambda)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}N_{C}(a_{i}^{p}\lambda).

Pick a sequence
\lambda_{j}\to\infty
so that

a^{-n}\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}N_{U}(a^{p}\lambda_{j})\to\underline{\gamma}(U).

Taking
\lambda=\lambda_{j}
in the above equation and multiplying both sides by
a^{-n}\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}
yields

a^{-n}\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}N_{C}(\lambda_{j})\leq a^{-n}\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}\left(N_{V}(\lambda_{j})+N_{U}(a^{p}\lambda_{j})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}N_{C}(a_{i}^{p}\lambda_{j})\right)

Letting
j\to\infty
and using the fact that
\lambda_{j}^{-n/p}N_{V}(\lambda_{j})\leq C_{2}\operatorname{vol}(V)\leq C_{2}\varepsilon
for all large
j
, it follows that that

a^{-n}c\leq a^{-n}C_{2}\varepsilon+\underline{\gamma}(U)+a^{-n}\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{n}c.

Thus

\displaystyle\underline{\gamma}(U)

\displaystyle\geq a^{-n}\left(c-\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}^{n}c-C_{2}\varepsilon\right)

\displaystyle=a^{-n}c-a^{-n}(1-a^{n}-\operatorname{vol}(V))c-a^{n}C_{2}\varepsilon\geq c-a^{n}C_{2}\varepsilon.\phantom{\bigg(}

But
\varepsilon
is arbitrary and
C_{2}
is independent of
\varepsilon
and hence
\underline{\gamma}(U)\geq c
. This proves that
\overline{\gamma}(U)=\underline{\gamma}(U)=c
and so the Weyl law
N_{U}(\lambda)\sim c\lambda^{n/p}
holds for
U
.
∎

9.
Equality of Dirichlet and Neumann Constants

In this section we prove that the constant
c^{0}
in the Dirichlet Weyl law is equal to the constant
c
in the Neumann Weyl law.
The following lemma of Gromov (
[
5
]
Lemma 3.2.E
1
) is the key ingredient in the proof.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary. Let
\varepsilon>0
and define the set

U_{\varepsilon}:=\{x\in U:\,0<\text{dist}(x,\partial U)<\varepsilon\}.

Let
\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime}>0
and set

\lambda=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime}}{\lambda^{\prime}+\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\varepsilon^{-1}}.

Then there is an inequality

N_{U}(\lambda^{p})\leq N^{0}_{U}((\lambda^{\prime})^{p})+N_{U_{\varepsilon}}((\lambda^{\prime\prime})^{p}).

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon
,
\lambda
,
\lambda^{\prime}
,
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
be as in the statement of the lemma. Define the sets

\displaystyle A:=\{v\in\mathcal{M}^{0}(U):\,E(v)<(\lambda^{\prime})^{p}\},

\displaystyle B:=\{w\in\mathcal{M}(U_{\varepsilon}):\,E(w)<(\lambda^{\prime\prime})^{p}\},

\displaystyle C:=\{u\in\mathcal{M}(U):\,E(u)<\lambda^{p}\}.

Define
\varphi:U\to\mathbb{R}
by

\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}\varepsilon^{-1}\text{dist}(x,\partial U),&\text{if }x\in U_{\varepsilon}\\
1,&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

and then set

C_{A}:=\{u\in C:\,E(\varphi u)<(\lambda^{\prime})^{p}\}.

Also define

C_{B}:=\{u\in C:\,u|_{U_{\varepsilon}}\not\equiv 0\text{ and }E(u|_{U_{\varepsilon}})<(\lambda^{\prime\prime})^{p}\}.

We claim that
C\subset C_{A}\cup C_{B}
. Given this the result follows. Indeed
C_{A}
and
C_{B}
are open in
C
and so Proposition
4.1
(ii) gives

\operatorname{ind}(C)\leq\operatorname{ind}(C_{A})+\operatorname{ind}(C_{B}).

Moreover, there are odd continuous maps

\displaystyle C_{A}\to A,\quad u\mapsto\frac{\varphi u}{\|\varphi u\|}

\displaystyle C_{B}\to B,\quad u\mapsto\frac{u|_{U_{\varepsilon}}}{\|u|_{U_{\varepsilon}}\|}.

Hence Proposition
4.1
(i) gives

\operatorname{ind}(C_{A})\leq\operatorname{ind}(A),\quad\operatorname{ind}(C_{B})\leq\operatorname{ind}(B)

and it follows that
\operatorname{ind}(C)\leq\operatorname{ind}(A)+\operatorname{ind}(B)
, as needed.

It remains to show the claim. So suppose that
u\in C
. If
u|_{U_{\varepsilon}}=0
, then
\varphi u=u
and so
E(\varphi u)=E(u)<\lambda^{p}<(\lambda^{\prime})^{p}
. Thus
u\in C_{A}
. Hence we may assume that
u|_{U_{\varepsilon}}\neq 0
. Suppose for contradiction that
u\notin C_{A}\cup C_{B}
. Then

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\varphi u)\|_{L^{p}(U)}\geq\lambda^{\prime}\|\varphi u\|_{L^{p}(U)},

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(U)}\geq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(U_{\varepsilon})}\geq\lambda^{\prime\prime}\|u\|_{L^{p}(U_{\varepsilon})}.

Moreover, there are inequalities

\displaystyle\|\nabla(\varphi u)\|_{L^{p}(U)}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(U)}+\varepsilon^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{p}(U_{\varepsilon})},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{p}(U)}\leq\|\varphi u\|_{L^{p}(U)}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(U_{\varepsilon})}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(U)}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{\prime\prime}\|u\|_{L^{p}(U_{\varepsilon})}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\prime\prime}\left(\|u\|_{L^{p}(U_{\varepsilon})}+\|\varphi u\|_{L^{p}(U)}\right)-\lambda^{\prime\prime}\|\varphi u\|_{L^{p}(U)}\phantom{\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{\prime\prime}\|u\|_{L^{p}(U)}-\frac{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}{\lambda^{\prime}}\|\nabla(\varphi u)\|_{L^{p}(U)}

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{\prime\prime}\|u\|_{L^{p}(U)}-\frac{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}{\lambda^{\prime}}\left(\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(U)}+\varepsilon^{-1}\|u\|_{L^{p}(U_{\varepsilon})}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda^{\prime\prime}\|u\|_{L^{p}(U)}-\left(\frac{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}{\lambda^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon\lambda^{\prime}}\right)\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(U)}.

Rearranging and using the definition of
\lambda
gives

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(U)}\geq\lambda\|u\|_{L^{p}(U)}.

This is a contradiction, and the claim follows.
∎

Theorem 9.2
.

Let
c^{0}
be the constant appearing in the Dirichlet Weyl law and let
c
be the constant appearing the Neumann Weyl law. Then
c^{0}=c
.

Proof.

Let
B
be the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Fix some small
\eta>0
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and for each
\lambda>0
put

\lambda^{\prime}=(1+\eta)\lambda,\quad\lambda^{\prime\prime}=\frac{\lambda+\varepsilon^{-1}}{\eta}

so that

\lambda=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime}}{\lambda^{\prime}+\lambda^{\prime\prime}+\varepsilon^{-1}}.

Notice that for
\lambda
large enough there is an inequality

\lambda^{\prime\prime}\leq{2\lambda}/{\eta}.

Using this inequality in conjunction with Lemma
9.1
shows that

\displaystyle\lambda^{-n}N_{B}(\lambda^{p})

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-n}N^{0}_{B}((1+\eta)^{p}\lambda^{p})+\lambda^{-n}N_{B_{\varepsilon}}(2^{p}\lambda^{p}/\eta^{p})

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-n}N^{0}_{B}((1+\eta)^{p}\lambda^{p})+C_{2}\operatorname{vol}(B_{\varepsilon})(2/\eta)^{n}

for all large
\lambda
. Letting
\lambda\to\infty
, this implies that

c\leq c^{0}(1+\eta)^{n}+C_{2}\operatorname{vol}(B_{\varepsilon})(2/\eta)^{n}.

Taking
\varepsilon\to 0
this gives
c\leq c^{0}(1+\eta)^{n}
, and then letting
\eta\to 0
yields

c\leq c^{0}.

The opposite inequality
c^{0}\leq c
is clear, and the result follows.
∎

10.
The Weyl Law on Closed Manifolds

Let
(X^{n},g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold. One defines the
p
-Laplacian
\Delta_{p}u=\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
and the space

\mathcal{M}(X)=\left\{u\in W^{1,p}(X):\,\|u\|_{W^{1,p}}=1\right\}.

The variational spectrum of
\Delta_{p}
on
X
and the counting function
N_{X}(\lambda)
are then defined as before via a min-max procedure involving the cohomological index. The goal of this section is to show that a Weyl law
N_{X}(\lambda)\sim c\operatorname{vol}(X)\lambda^{n/p}
holds on
X
, thus proving Theorem A.

As a first step consider some
U\subset X
with Lipschitz boundary. Suppose
U
is small enough that we can find a chart
\varphi:V\to U
where the metric
g=(g_{ij})
on
U
satisfies

(1-\varepsilon)^{2}(\delta_{ij})\leq(g_{ij})\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{2}(\delta_{ij}).

We will call such a set
\varepsilon
-admissible.
If
U
is
\varepsilon
-admissible then energy function on
U
is given by

E_{U}(u)=\frac{\int_{V}\left|g^{ij}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}\right|^{p/2}\sqrt{g}\,dx}{\int_{V}|u|^{p}\sqrt{g}\,dx}

and hence there are inequalities

K_{1}(\varepsilon)E_{V}(u)\leq E_{U}(u)\leq K_{2}(\varepsilon)E_{V}(u)

where

K_{1}(\varepsilon),K_{2}(\varepsilon)\to 1,\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

It follows that there are comparisons

N^{0}_{V}(K_{2}(\varepsilon)^{-1}\lambda)\leq N^{0}_{U}(\lambda)\leq N_{U}(\lambda)\leq N_{V}(K_{1}(\varepsilon)^{-1}\lambda)

for every
\lambda>0
. There is also a volume comparison

(1-\varepsilon)^{n}\operatorname{vol}(V)\leq\operatorname{vol}(U)\leq(1+\varepsilon)^{n}\operatorname{vol}(V).

It is now possible to prove a Weyl law for
\Delta_{p}
on
X
.

Theorem A
.

Let
(X^{n},g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold. Then there is a Weyl law
N_{X}(\lambda)\sim c\operatorname{vol}(X)\lambda^{n/p}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we may assume that
\operatorname{vol}(X)=1
. Define the quantities

\underline{\gamma}(X)=\liminf_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-n/p}N_{X}(\lambda),\quad\overline{\gamma}(X)=\limsup_{\lambda\to\infty}\lambda^{-n/p}N_{X}(\lambda).

Pick some
\varepsilon>0
. Choose disjoint
\varepsilon
-admissible open sets
U_{1},\ldots,U_{m}
in
M
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\operatorname{vol}(U_{i})\geq 1-\varepsilon.

For each
i
, let
\varphi_{i}:V_{i}\to U_{i}
be a chart where the metric is almost Euclidean.
Arguing as in Section
5
there is an inequality

N_{M}(\lambda)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}N_{U_{i}}^{0}(\lambda)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}N^{0}_{V_{i}}(K_{2}(\varepsilon)^{-1}\lambda).

Multiplying by
\lambda^{-n/p}
and then letting
\lambda\to\infty
and using the Weyl law for domains in Euclidean space, this implies

\underline{\gamma}(M)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}c\operatorname{vol}(V_{i})K_{2}(\varepsilon)^{-n/p}\geq c(1+\varepsilon)^{-n}(1-\varepsilon)K_{2}(\varepsilon)^{-n/p}.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
it follows that
\underline{\gamma}(M)\geq c
.

It remains to show that
\overline{\gamma}(M)\leq c
. To this end, let
\varepsilon>0
and then choose
\varepsilon
-admissible sets
U_{1},\ldots,U_{m}
so that

M=\overline{U}_{1}\cup\ldots\cup\overline{U}_{m},

and each intersection
\overline{U}_{i}\cap\overline{U}_{j}
has measure 0. Such sets can be constructed using the argument in the proof of 4.2 in
[
7
]
. For each
i
, let
\varphi_{i}:V_{i}\to U_{i}
be a chart where the metric is almost Euclidean. Arguing as in Section
7
there is an inequality

N_{M}(\lambda)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}N_{U_{i}}(\lambda)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}N_{V_{i}}(K_{1}(\varepsilon)^{-1}\lambda).

Multiplying by
\lambda^{-n/p}
and letting
\lambda\to\infty
and using the Weyl law for domains in Euclidean space, this implies

\displaystyle\overline{\gamma}(M)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}c\operatorname{vol}(V_{i})K_{1}(\varepsilon)^{-n/p}\leq c(1-\varepsilon)^{-n}K_{1}(\varepsilon)^{-n/p}.

Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
it follows that
\overline{\gamma}(M)\leq c
. This proves the result.
∎

11.
Growth Bound for the Neumann Counting Function

The goal of this final section is to prove the growth bound in Proposition
8.3
. The argument uses Sobolev extension operators.

Definition 11.1
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set. Then
U
is
L
-Lipschitz provided there is a covering of
\partial U
by balls
(B_{i})
with the following property: for each
i
there is an
L
-Lipschitz function

f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}

such that, up to translation and rotation,
U\cap B_{i}
coincides with the set

\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\,x_{n}<f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})\}\cap B_{i}.

Jones introduced the following more general class of domains in
[
6
]
.

Definition 11.2
.

Let
U
be an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and let
\varepsilon,\delta>0
. Then
U
is called an
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-domain provided for every
x,y\in U
with
|x-y|<\delta
there is a rectifiable arc
\gamma
joining
x
to
y
with

(i)

\text{Length}(\gamma)\leq\dfrac{|x-y|}{\varepsilon}
,

(ii)

\operatorname{dist}(z,\partial U)\geq\dfrac{\varepsilon|x-z||y-z|}{|x-y|}
for all
z\in\gamma
.

Every
L
-Lipschitz domain
U
is an
(\varepsilon,\delta)
domain for some choice of
\varepsilon
and
\delta
. Moreover,
\varepsilon
can be taken to depend only on
L
and
n
.
The following result is due to Jones
[
6
]
.

Theorem 11.3
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-domain. Then there exists a continuous linear map

\mathcal{E}:W^{1,p}(U)\to W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

with the property that
(\mathcal{E}u)|_{U}=u
. Moreover, the norm of
\mathcal{E}
depends only on
n
,
p
,
\varepsilon
, and
\delta
.

It is now possible to prove the growth bound.

Proof.

(Proposition
8.3
)
Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set and assume that
U
is
L
-Lipschitz. We need to check that

N_{U}(\lambda)\leq C\operatorname{vol}(U)\lambda^{n/p},\quad\text{as }\lambda\to\infty

for some constant
C
that depends only on
n
,
p
, and
L
. Notice that if
U
is
L
-Lipschitz then so is the scaled copy
aU
for any
a>0
. Since the above inequality is scale invariant, it suffices to show that

N_{aU}(\lambda)\leq C\operatorname{vol}(aU)\lambda^{n/p},\quad\text{as }\lambda\to\infty

for some
a>0
.

Since
U
is
L
-Lipschitz, it is an
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-domain for some choice of
\varepsilon
and
\delta
. Hence for
a
large enough,
aU
will be an
(\varepsilon,1)
-domain. Choose an open set
V
containing the closure of
U
with
\operatorname{vol}(V)\leq 2\operatorname{vol}(U)
. Then it is still true that
\operatorname{vol}(aV)\leq 2\operatorname{vol}(aU)
. Moreover, for
a
large enough
aV
will contain the 1-neighborhood of
aU
.
For notational convenience put

\widetilde{U}=aU\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{V}=aV.

By Theorem
11.3
there is an extension operator

\mathcal{E}:W^{1,p}(\widetilde{U})\to W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Since
\delta=1
and
\varepsilon
depends only on
L
and
n
, it follows that the norm of
\mathcal{E}
depends only on
n
,
p
, and
L
.

Let
\zeta
be a cutoff function with
\zeta\equiv 1
on
\widetilde{U}
and
\zeta\equiv 0
outside of
\widetilde{V}
. It is possible to choose
\zeta
so that
|\nabla\zeta|\leq 2
everywhere. Define an odd continuous mapping

\displaystyle G:W^{1,p}(\widetilde{U})\to W^{1,p}_{0}(\widetilde{V}),

\displaystyle u\mapsto\zeta\cdot\mathcal{E}u.

There is an estimate

\displaystyle E(Gu)=\frac{\int_{\widetilde{V}}|\nabla(\zeta\cdot\mathcal{E}u)|^{p}}{\int_{\widetilde{V}}|\zeta\cdot\mathcal{E}u|^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\frac{\int_{\widetilde{V}}|\nabla(\mathcal{E}u)|^{p}|\zeta|^{p}+|\mathcal{E}u|^{p}|\nabla\zeta|^{p}}{\int_{\widetilde{U}}|u|^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{C}\left(\frac{\int_{\widetilde{V}}|\nabla(\mathcal{E}u)|^{p}+|\mathcal{E}u|^{p}}{\int_{\widetilde{U}}|u|^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{C}\left(\frac{\int_{\widetilde{U}}|\nabla u|^{p}+|u|^{p}}{\int_{\widetilde{U}}|u|^{p}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq{C}(E(u)+1).\phantom{\bigg(}

Now let
A=\{u\in W^{1,p}(\widetilde{U}):\,E(u)<\lambda\}
. Then the above estimate implies that

E(Gu)\leq{C}(\lambda+1)

for every
u\in A
. By Proposition
4.1
(i) this implies

N_{\widetilde{U}}(\lambda)\leq N^{0}_{\widetilde{V}}\left({C}(\lambda+1)\right)\leq C\operatorname{vol}(\widetilde{V})(\lambda+1)^{n/p}.

Therefore

N_{\widetilde{U}}(\lambda)\leq C\operatorname{vol}(\widetilde{U})\lambda^{n/p}

for all sufficiently large
\lambda
, as needed.
∎

1.
Introduction and main results

Orthogonal polynomials play an important role in mathematics and applied sciences.
The theory of orthogonal polynomials and their main properties
is
well documented
[
Fr71
,
Sze75
,
Ne79
,
VA10
,
Ch11
]
. In this
paper we define for every positive normalized arithmetic function
h(n)
an orthogonal polynomial sequence with positive definite
moment functionals. We propose certain properties on
h(n)
which imply Turán inequalities.
We show that
h(n)=h_{s}(n):=n^{s}
for
s\in[0,1]
provides complete results.

In 1948,
in a seminal work, Szegő
[
Sze48
]
called attention to a discovery
by Turán
[
Tu50
]
.
Studying the zeros of Legendre polynomials
P_{n}(x)
of degree
n
, Turán
proved and utilized a new type of inequalities for orthogonal polynomials:

T_{n}(x):=P_{n}(x)^{2}-P_{n-1}(x)\,P_{n+1}(x),\qquad x\in[-1,1].

Szegő presented several proofs and showed that Turán inequalities are also satisfied
by other orthogonal polynomials, including ultraspherical, associated Laguerre,
and Hermite polynomials.
Proofs include transcendental functions and Jensen polynomials.

This initiated research in several
directions
[
KS60
]
, including higher Turán inequalities
capturing the conjectured hyperbolic property of Jensen polynomials
associated with the Riemann zeta
function
[
Cs14
,
CJW19
,
GORZ19
]
. We also refer to the open session report
by van Assche
[
VA10
]
,
where Nevai emphasized also the importance of
studying Turán inequalities. The way we normalize the polynomials is essential for the Turán
inequality to hold, we refer to the recent results of Szwarc
[
Szw98
,
Szw21
]
. In this paper we
follow a uniform approach.

We define a sequence of polynomials
\{P_{n}^{g,h}(x)\}_{n}

associated with
positive valued normalized arithmetic functions
g
and
h
. Let
P_{0}^{g,h}(x):=1
and

P_{n}^{g,h}(x):=\frac{x}{h(n)}\sum_{k=1}^{n}g(k)\,P_{n-k}^{g,h}(x).

The Nekrasov–Okounkov polynomials
[
NO06
]
are provided for

g(n)=\sigma(n):=\sum_{d\mid n}d
and
h(n)=\mathop{\rm id}(n)=n
.
Much simpler, but still interesting is the case
g(n)={n}
, which involves associated
Laguerre polynomials
L_{n}^{(1)}(x)
and Chebyshev polynomials

of the second kind
[
Ch11
]
.
We state our first result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
g=\mathop{\rm id}
and
h

be arbitrary positive real valued normalized arithmetic functions.
The sequence
\{q_{n}^{h}(x)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
defined by

q_{n}^{h}(x):=\frac{\prod_{k=1}^{n+1}h(k)}{x}\,P_{n+1}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)

is an orthogonal polynomial sequence (OPS) with a positive definite moment functional
\Lambda
.

In Section

we recall basic properties for the OPS and prove Theorem
1.1
.
This result leads to the following application.

Corollary 1.2
.

The polynomials
P_{n}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)
have simple, real, and non-positive

Moreover, the
zeros of
\frac{1}{x}\,P_{n}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)

are interlacing.

This follows from (
[
Ch11
]
, Theorem 5.2 and Theorem 5.3). Note that the coefficients of

Suppose

is log-concave and satisfies some further properties, then

P_{n}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)
satisfy Turán inequalities.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

be a real-valued normalized arithmetic function with positive values, extended by

Suppose that

2.
OPS and proof of Theorem
1.1

We first recall basic definitions and results related to Favard’s theorem.
Then we provide a three
term
recurrence relation for
\left\{P_{n}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}\left(x\right)\right\}
and finally prove Theorem
1.1
.

2.1.
Orthogonal polynomial sequences and Favard’s theorem

Every linear functional
\Lambda
on the vector space of polynomials
\mathbb{C}[x]
is given and
uniquely determined by the complex values
\Lambda(x^{n})=\mu_{n}
on the standard basis.
The sequence
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is
called moment sequence and
\Lambda
moment functional.
We closely follow Chihara (
[
Ch11
]
, Sections 1.2, 1.3, and 1.4).

Definition
.

A sequence
\{P_{n}(x)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is called orthogonal polynomial sequence (OPS) with respect to
a moment functional
\Lambda
if for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
:

a)

P_{n}(x)
is a polynomial of degree
n
,

b)

\Lambda\left(P_{m}(x)\,P_{n}(x)\right)=k_{n}\,\delta_{m,n},\quad k_{n}\neq 0.

If this is the case we say,
\{P_{n}(x)\}
is an OPS for
\Lambda
.

Let
\left\{\mu_{n}\right\}
be a moment
sequence
and let
\Delta_{n}
be the determinant of the Hankel matrix

(\mu_{i+j})_{i,j=0}^{n}
. Let
\Lambda
be a moment functional with moment sequence
\left\{\mu_{n}\right\}
.
Then we have (
[
Ch11
]
, Theorem 3.1) for moment functionals
\Lambda
:

\text{OPS for }\Lambda\text{ exists }\Longrightarrow\Delta_{n}\neq 0,\qquad n\geq 0.

Let
E\subset(-\infty,\infty)
. Let
P(E)
be the set of all polynomials with real coefficients,
restricted to
E
non-negative and not the zero polynomial.
A moment functional is said to be positive definite on
E

if
\Lambda
restricted to
P(E)
is non-negative. The set
E
is called a supporting set for
\Lambda
.
If
E=(-\infty,\infty)
we say
\Lambda
is positive definite,
\Lambda>0
.

Let
\Lambda
be positive definite, then one can conclude that the functional moments are real and that
a corresponding OPS of real polynomials exists (
[
Ch11
]
, Theorem 3.3). With a little bit more effort one
obtains the important characterization of positive definite moment functionals (
[
Ch11
]
, Theorem 3.4):

\Lambda>0\Leftrightarrow\mu_{n}\in\mathbb{R}\text{ and }\Delta_{n}>0,\quad n\geq 0.

All
\Lambda
with
\Delta_{n}\neq 0
,
n\geq 0
, are
called quasi-definite. Now we are able to
state an important result relating OPS to three
term recurrence relations.

Theorem 2.1

(Favard’s theorem)
.

Let
\{c_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
\left\{\lambda_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

be arbitrary sequences of complex numbers.
Let
\{P_{n}(x)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be defined by the
recurrence relation

(2.1)

P_{n}(x)=(x-c_{n})\,P_{n-1}(x)-\lambda_{n}\,P_{n-2}(x),\quad n\geq 1,

with initial values
P_{-1}(x):=0
and
P_{0}(x):=1
.
Then there exists a unique moment functional
\Lambda
, such that
\Lambda(1)=\lambda_{1}
and
such that for all
non-negative integers
m,n
with
m\neq n
, we have
\Lambda(P_{m}(x)\,P_{n}(x))=0
. Further,

•

\Lambda
is quasi-definite if and only if

\lambda_{n}\neq 0
for all
n
,

•

\Lambda
is positive definite if and only if

c_{n}
is real and
\lambda_{n}>0
for all
n
.

Remarks
.

1)

Theorem
2.1
was first announced in 1935 by Favard, and independently
discovered by Shohat and Natanson at the same time. It is also contained implicitly in some work
by Stieltjes (
[
Ch11
]
, Chapter I, Section 4).

2)

Let
\Lambda
be quasi-definite. Then there exists
c_{n}
and
\lambda_{n}\neq 0
, such that
the monic OPS of
\Lambda
satisfies (
2.1
). If
\Lambda
is positive definite, then
c_{n}
is real and

\lambda_{n}>0
(converse of Favard’s theorem).

3)

An OPS defined by (
2.1
) is independent from
\lambda_{1}
. Usually one puts
\lambda_{1}=\Lambda(1)=\mu_{1}
.

2.1.1.
Normalization

We have the following very useful property.
Suppose
\{p_{n}(x)\}_{n}
satisfies the three
term relation (
2.1
). Let
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
with
a\neq 0
.
Then
\{\tilde{p}_{n}(x)\}_{n}
, which is defined
as
\tilde{p}_{n}(x):=a^{-n}\,p_{n}(ax+b)
, satisfies (
2.1
) with

\tilde{p}_{n}(x)=\left(x-\frac{c_{n}-b}{a}\right)\,\tilde{p}_{n-1}(x)-\frac{\lambda_{n}}{a^{2}}\tilde{p}_{n-2}(x).

Moreover, let
\{p_{n}(x)\}_{n}
be an OPS with moments
\{\mu_{n}\}_{n}
. Then
\tilde{p}_{n}(x)
is an OPS with
moments

\tilde{\mu}_{n}=a^{-n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\,(-b)^{n-k}\,\mu_{k}.

Polynomials satisfying (
2.1
) are monic. Suppose
\{\hat{p}_{n}(x)\}
is an OPS with positive definite moment functional

\Lambda
satisfying (
2.1
). Then
\Lambda(\hat{p}_{n}(x)\,\hat{p}_{m}(x))=k_{n}\,\delta_{n,m}
,
k_{n}\neq 0
.
To obtain
k_{n}=1
one can drop the monicity property and study a modified three
term recurrence relation.

Let
\{a_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be a sequence of positive numbers and
\{b_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
a sequence of real numbers:

x\,p_{n}(x)=a_{n+1}\,p_{n+1}(x)+b_{n}\,p_{n}(x)+a_{n}\,p_{n-1}(x),\quad n\geq 0,

with initial values
p_{-1}(x)=0
and
p_{0}(x)=1
. Let
\Lambda(p_{n}(x)p_{m}(x))=\delta_{i,j}
.
Then
\hat{p}_{n}(x)=(a_{1}\,a_{2}\,\cdots a_{n})\,p_{n}(x)
, satisfying (
2.1
).
This implies

\hat{p}_{n+1}(x)=(x-b_{n})\hat{p}_{n}(x)-a_{n}^{2}\,\hat{p}_{n-1}(x),\quad n\geq 0,

with
\hat{p}_{-1}(x)=p_{-1}(x)=0
and
\hat{p}_{0}(x)=p_{0}(x)=1
. Here

c_{n}=b_{n-1}
and
\lambda_{n}=a_{n-1}^{2}
.

Applications

Let
\{U_{n}(x)\}_{n}
be the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind. The first polynomials
are given by:
U_{0}(x)=1,U_{1}(x)=2x,U_{2}(x)=4x^{2}-1,U_{3}(x)=8x^{3}-4x
. Then
\hat{U}_{n}(x)=2^{-n}U_{n}(x)
and

\hat{U}_{n}(x)=x\hat{U}_{n-1}(x)-\frac{1}{4}\hat{U}_{n-2}(x).

The Chebyshev polynomials
U_{n}(x)
of the second kind satisfy:

U_{n}(x)=2xU_{n-1}(x)-U_{n-2}\left(x\right),\quad n\geq 1

with initial values
U_{-1}(x)=0
and
U_{0}(x)=1
. We have
c_{n}=0
and
\lambda_{n}=1
.
This implies that
\{U_{n}(x)\}
is an OPS for a positive definite functional
\Lambda
.
Let
p_{n}(x):=a^{-n}U_{n}(ax+b)
for
a\neq 0
. Then

p_{n}(x)=\left(x-\frac{-b}{a}\right)\,p_{n-1}(x)-\frac{1}{a^{2}}p_{n-2}(x),\quad n\geq 1,

with initial values
p_{-1}(x)=0
and
p_{0}(x)=1
. Thus,
\{p_{n}(x)\}
is also OPS for a positive definite
\Lambda
.
Let
a=1/2
and
b=1
. Then we have
\tilde{c}_{n}=-2
and
\tilde{\lambda}_{n}=4
for this OPS.
Let
\alpha>-1
. Then the
\alpha
-associated Laguerre polynomial
L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)
is defined
by

(2.2)

L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n+\alpha}{n-k}\frac{(-x)^{k}}{k!}.

The polynomials have the leading coefficient
k_{n}=(-1)^{n}/n!
. It is known that

nL_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)=(2n+\alpha-1-x)\,L_{n-1}^{(\alpha)}(x)-(n+\alpha-1)\,L_{n-2}^{(\alpha)}(x).

Therefore, associated Laguerre polynomials are orthogonal polynomials.
For the corresponding monic polynomials
\hat{L}_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x):=\frac{1}{k_{n}}\,L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)
we obtain

\hat{L}_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)=(x-2n-\alpha+1)\,\hat{L}_{n-1}^{(\alpha)}(x)-(n-1)(n+\alpha-1)\,\hat{L}_{n-2}^{(\alpha)}(x).

Let
\alpha=1
. Then we obtain

\hat{L}_{n}^{(1)}(x)=(x-2n)\,\hat{L}_{n-1}^{(1)}(x)-n\,(n-1)\,\hat{L}_{n-2}^{(1)}(x),

where
c_{n}=2n
and
\lambda_{n}=(n-1)n
. Moreover let
p_{n}(x):=(-1)^{n}\,\hat{L}_{n}^{(1)}(-x)
.
Then we obtain:

p_{n}(x)=\left(x+2n\right)\,p_{n-1}(x)-(n-1)\,n\,p_{n-2}(x),\quad n\geq 1,

Let us extend (
2.2
) to
\alpha=-1
. Then
\{L_{n}^{\left(-1\right)}(x)\}
is not an OPS.
This is still interesting, since
P_{n}^{\mathop{\rm id},\mathop{\rm id}}(x)=L_{n}^{\left(-1\right)}(x)
. This leads almost
to an OPS, since
L_{n}^{(-1)}(x)=\frac{x}{n}L_{n-1}^{(1)}(-x)
.

2.2.
Three term
recurrence relation for
\{P_{n}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)\}_{n}

Let
\{P_{n}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)\}_{n}
be given. Let
g(n)=\mathop{\rm id}(n)=n

Then we

.

Let
n\geq 1
. Let

be a non-vanishing function on
\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
h(0)=0

with initial values
q_{-1}^{h}\left(x\right)=0
and

q_{0}^{h}\left(x\right)=1
.

Proof.

With
\alpha_{2}=1
,

\alpha_{1}=-2
, and
\alpha_{0}=1

(2.3)

\alpha_{2}g\left(n+1\right)+\alpha_{1}g\left(n\right)+\alpha_{0}g\left(n-1\right)=0

, see (
[
HNT20
]

With

\alpha_{1}\,g\left(1\right)+\alpha_{2}g\left(2\right)=-2+4=2
.
From (
[
HNT20
]

we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{2}\left(\alpha_{m}\frac{h\left(n-1+m\right)}{h\left(n+1\right)}-\frac{x}{h\left(n+1\right)}\sum_{k=1}^{2-m}\alpha_{m+k}g\left(k\right)\right)P_{n-1+m}^{g,h}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{h\left(n-1\right)}{h\left(n+1\right)}P_{n-1}^{g,h}\left(x\right)-\left(\frac{2h\left(n\right)+x}{h\left(n+1\right)}\right)P_{n}^{g,h}\left(x\right)+P_{n+1}^{g,h}\left(x\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0

for

with the initial values

and

.
Note that the right hand side is

as in (

).
Therefore, we obtain

q_{n}^{h}\left(x\right)=\left(x+2h\left(n\right)\right)q_{n-1}^{h}\left(x\right)-h\left(n-1\right)h\left(n\right)q_{n-2}^{h}\left(x\right)

for
n\geq 2
. For

we obtain from the initial value

that

q_{1}^{h}\left(x\right)=x+2
.
∎

We can also give
direct proof of (

) illustrating
the
general idea
of the proof of Theorem 2.1 of

\displaystyle h\left(n+1\right)P_{n+1}^{g,h}\left(x\right)

Proof of Theorem
1.3

Let
h
be a real-valued arithmetic function, positive and normalized.
Then the polynomials
\{P_{n}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)\}_{n}
satisfy

the three term recurrence relation

(3.1)

h(n+1)\,P_{n+1}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)=(2\,h(n)+x)\,P_{n}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)-h(n-1)\,P_{n-1}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x),

with the initial values
P_{1}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)=x
,

P_{2}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}\left(x\right)=\left(x+2\right)x/h\left(2\right)

and the extension

.
Note that for

, (

) is equivalent to

\frac{P_{n+1}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)}{P_{n}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)}=\frac{x+2\,h(n)}{h(n+1)}-\,\frac{h\left(n-1\right)}{h(n+1)}\,\frac{P_{n-1}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)}{P_{n}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)}.

Remarks
.

Let
P_{n}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)=0
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
, then
T^{h}_{n}(x)\geq 0
. Numerical investigations suggest that

T_{n}^{h}(x)\geq 0
for all

. In this paper we focus on

.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
x=0
then (
1.2
)
holds
true. Now let
x>0
.
The Turán inequality
T_{n}^{h}(x)\geq 0
holds
true if and only if

\left(\frac{P_{n}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)}{P_{n-1}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)}\right)^{2}-\frac{x+2\,h(n)}{h(n+1)}\,\frac{P_{n}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)}{P_{n-1}^{\mathop{\rm id},h}(x)}+\frac{h(n-1)}{h(n+1)}\,\geq\,0.

We study the roots

Inequalities among two rowed immanants of the q q -Laplacian of Trees and Odd height peaks in generalized Dyck paths
Arbind Kumar Lal, Mukesh Kumar Nagar, Sivaramakrishnan Sivasubramanian

Abstract
Let T T be a tree on n n vertices and let ℒ q T \mathcal{L}_{q}^{T} be the q q -analogue of its Laplacian. For a partition λ ⊢ n \lambda\vdash n , let the normalized immanant of ℒ q T \mathcal{L}_{q}^{T} indexed by λ \lambda be denoted as imm ¯ λ ​ ( ℒ q T ) \overline{\mathrm{imm}}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{L}_{q}^{T}) . A string of inequalities among imm ¯ λ ​ ( ℒ q T ) \overline{\mathrm{imm}}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{L}_{q}^{T}) is known when λ \lambda varies over hook partitions of n n as the size of the first part of λ \lambda decreases. In this work, we show a similar sequence of inequalities when λ \lambda varies over two row partitions of n n as the size of the first part of λ \lambda decreases. Our main lemma is an identity involving binomial coefficients and irreducible character values of 𝔖 n \mathfrak{S}_{n} indexed by two row partitions. Our proof can be interpreted using the combinatorics of Riordan paths and our main lemma admits a nice probabilisitic interpretation involving peaks at odd heights in generalized Dyck paths or equivalently involving special descents in Standard Young Tableaux with two rows. As a corollary, we also get inequalities between imm ¯ λ 1 ​ ( ℒ q T 1 ) \overline{\mathrm{imm}}_{\lambda_{1}}(\mathcal{L}_{q}^{T_{1}}) and imm ¯ λ 2 ​ ( ℒ q T 2 ) \overline{\mathrm{imm}}_{\lambda_{2}}(\mathcal{L}_{q}^{T_{2}}) when T 1 T_{1} and T 2 T_{2} are comparable trees in the 𝖦𝖳𝖲 n \mathsf{GTS}_{n} poset and when λ 1 \lambda_{1} and λ 2 \lambda_{2} are both two rowed partitions of n n , with λ 1 \lambda_{1} having a larger first part than λ 2 \lambda_{2} .

Lemma 4

For positive integers

\alpha_{n,k,i}=\alpha_{n-1,k,i}+\alpha_{n-1,k-1,i}

Proof:

By definition, we have

\displaystyle 2^{i}\alpha_{n,k,i}=\sum_{j=0}^{i}\mathrm{TwoRow\_\chi}_{n,k}(j)\binom{i}{j}

By the Murnaghan-Nakayama lemma (see Sagan’s book

\mathrm{TwoRow\_\chi}_{n,k}(j)=\mathrm{TwoRow\_\chi}_{n-1,k}(j)+\mathrm{TwoRow\_\chi}_{n-1,k-1}(j)

\displaystyle\alpha_{n,k,i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{i}}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\mathrm{TwoRow\_\chi}_{n,k}(j)\binom{i}{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{i}}\sum_{j=0}^{i}\Big[\mathrm{TwoRow\_\chi}_{n-1,k}(j)+\mathrm{TwoRow\_\chi}_{n-1,k-1}(j)\Big]\binom{i}{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha_{n-1,k,i}+\alpha_{n-1,k-1,i}.

The proof is complete.

3.

\alpha_{2\ell,k,\ell}=\mathrm{last}_{\ell,k}=p_{\ell,k}-p_{\ell,k-1}
is a positive integer.

Proof:

1.

2.

By the definition of

, we have

\displaystyle p_{\ell,k}

\displaystyle=\mbox{ Coeff. of }x^{k}\mbox{ in }(1+x+x^{2})^{\ell}

\displaystyle=\mbox{ Coeff. of }x^{k}\mbox{ in }(1+x+x^{2})^{\ell-1}(1+x+x^{2})=p_{\ell-1,k}+p_{\ell-1,k-1}+p_{\ell-1,k-2}.

3.

We induct on

is a positive

\displaystyle=\mathrm{last}_{\ell-1,k}+\mathrm{last}_{\ell-1,k-1}+\mathrm{last}_{\ell-1,k-2}

\displaystyle=p_{\ell-1,k}-p_{\ell-1,k-1}+p_{\ell-1,k-1}-p_{\ell-1,k-2}+p_{\ell-1,k-2}-p_{\ell-1,k-3}

Lemma 14

Fix positive integers
\ell,k
with
1\leq k\leq\ell-1
and
with
\ell\geq 3
. Then, we have

\dfrac{\mathrm{last}_{\ell,k+1}}{{{2\ell}\choose{k+1}}-{{2\ell}\choose{k}}}\leq\dfrac{\mathrm{last}_{\ell,k}}{{{2\ell}\choose{k}}-{{2\ell}\choose{k-1}}}.

(12)

Proof:

Note that
{{2\ell}\choose{k}}
equals the coefficient of

x^{k}
in the expansion of

(1+x)^{2\ell}=(1+x+x^{2}+x)^{\ell}=\sum_{r=0}^{\ell}{\ell\choose r}x^{r}(1+x+x^{2})^{\ell-r}.

Thus,
{{2\ell}\choose{k}}=\sum_{r=0}^{\ell}{\ell\choose r}p_{\ell-r,k-r},

and hence

\displaystyle{{2\ell}\choose{k+1}}-{{2\ell}\choose{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{\ell}{\ell\choose r}p_{\ell-r,k+1-r}-\sum_{r=0}^{\ell}{\ell\choose r}p_{\ell-r,k-r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=0}^{\ell}{\ell\choose r}\mathrm{last}_{\ell-r,k+1-r}=\mathrm{last}_{\ell,k+1}+\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}{\ell\choose r}\mathrm{last}_{\ell-r,k+1-r}.

Hence,

\dfrac{{{2\ell}\choose{k+1}}-{{2\ell}\choose{k}}}{\mathrm{last}_{\ell,k+1}}=1+\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}{\ell\choose r}\dfrac{\mathrm{last}_{\ell-r,k+1-r}}{\mathrm{last}_{\ell,k+1}}.

(13)

Similarly,

\dfrac{{{2\ell}\choose{k}}-{{2\ell}\choose{k-1}}}{\mathrm{last}_{\ell,k}}=1+\sum_{r=1}^{\ell}{\ell\choose r}\dfrac{\mathrm{last}_{\ell-r,k-r}}{\mathrm{last}_{\ell,k}}.

(14)

Thus, to obtain our required result, we need to show that

\dfrac{\mathrm{last}_{\ell-r,k+1-r}}{\mathrm{last}_{\ell,k+1}}\geq\dfrac{\mathrm{last}_{\ell-r,k-r}}{\mathrm{last}_{\ell,k}}\Leftrightarrow\dfrac{\mathrm{last}_{\ell,k+1}}{\mathrm{last}_{\ell,k}}\leq\dfrac{\mathrm{last}_{\ell-r,k+1-r}}{\mathrm{last}_{\ell-r,k-r}}.

4.3
Successive differences

We combine the two definitions of the polynomials

In the following lemma, we give a similar
successive difference

interpretation for other
\alpha_{n,k,i}
’s.

Lemma 19

With the notation above, when
\ell\geq 2
and
1\leq i\leq\ell
,
we have the following.

1.

p_{2\ell+s,i,k}=p_{2\ell+s-2,i-1,k}+p_{2\ell+s-2,i-1,k-1}+p_{2\ell+s-2,i-1,k-2}.

2.

\alpha_{2\ell+s,k,i}=p_{2\ell+s,i,k}-p_{2l+s,i,k-1}.

Proof:

1.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle p_{2\ell+s,i,k}

\displaystyle=\mbox{ Coeff. of }x^{k}\mbox{ in }(1+x)^{2\ell-2i+s}(1+x+x^{2})^{i}

\displaystyle=\mbox{ Coeff. of }x^{k}\mbox{ in }(1+x)^{2(\ell-i+1)+s-2}(1+x+x^{2})^{i-1}(1+x+x^{2})

\displaystyle=\mbox{ Coeff. of }x^{k}\mbox{ in }p_{2\ell+s-2,i-1}(x)(1+x+x^{2})

\displaystyle=p_{2\ell+s-2,i-1,k}+p_{2\ell+s-2,i-1,k-1}+p_{2\ell+s-2,i-1,k-2}.

2.

To prove the second part, we use induction on

n=2\ell+s
as done in the proof of Lemma
9
. It
is easy to verify the statement for small values of
\ell,k
and
i
.
Assume that the lemma is true for all
values less than
n=2\ell+s
. We use the
Murnaghan Nakayama lemma to prove the lemma when
n=2\ell+s
. From the definition of

\alpha_{n,k,i}
we have

\displaystyle 2^{i}\alpha_{2\ell+s,k,i}=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{i}\mathrm{TwoRow\_\chi}_{2\ell+s,k}(j)\binom{i}{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\>2\left[\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\mathrm{TwoRow\_\chi}_{2\ell+s-2,k}(j)\binom{i-1}{j}+\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\mathrm{TwoRow\_\chi}_{2\ell+s-2,k-1}(j)\binom{i-1}{j}\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\sum_{j=0}^{i-1}\chi_{2\ell+s-2,k-2}(j)\binom{i-1}{j}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\>2\left[2^{i-1}\alpha_{2\ell+s-2,k,i-1}+2^{i-1}\alpha_{2\ell+s-2,k-1,i-1}+2^{i-1}\alpha_{2\ell+s-2,k-2,i-1}\right]

By induction, we get

\displaystyle\alpha_{2\ell+s,k,i}=

\displaystyle\>\alpha_{2\ell+s-2,k,i-1}+\alpha_{2\ell+s-2,k-1,i-1}+\alpha_{2\ell+s-2,k-2,i-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\>p_{2\ell+s-2,i-1,k}-p_{2\ell+s-2,i-1,k-1}+p_{2\ell+s-2,i-1,k-1}-p_{2\ell+s-2,i-1,k-2}

\displaystyle\>+p_{2\ell+s-2,i-1,k-2}-p_{2\ell+s-2,i-1,k-3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\>p_{2\ell+s,i,k}-p_{2\ell+s,i,k-1}.

where the last equality follows from first part. The proof is complete.

5

In this section, we give a path based interpretation and recast

16
in a probabilistic setting. Recall
from Remark
6

\alpha_{n,k,0}=\mathrm{TwoRow\_\chi}_{n,k}(\mathsf{id})
, where
\mathrm{TwoRow\_\chi}_{n,k}(\mathsf{id})

\mathfrak{S}_{n}

Consider non negative lattice paths on the plane from
(0,0)
to

(n,n-2k)
, consisting of
n-k
Up steps which go from
(x,y)
to
(x+1,y+1)

denoted U and
k
Down steps which go from
(x,y)
to
(x+1,y-1)
,
denoted D, that stay on or above the
x
-axis. By definition, all
paths we consider stay on or to the right of the
y
-axis and a
lattice path is termed
non negative
if it stays on or above
the
x
-axis.
For
k\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor
, let
\mathrm{NLP}(n,n-2k)
be the set of
such non negative lattice paths from

T\in\mathsf{SYT}(n-k,k)
define position
i
to
be a peak
if
i
appears in the first row and
i+1
appears in the second
row. This is precisely saying that
i\in\mathrm{DES}(T)
where

\mathrm{DES}(T)
is the descent set of
T
, which is a well studied
statistic (see the book by Stanley

15

). For a descent
i
, to get the height of its
peak under this mapping, consider
T_{|i}
, the restriction of
T

to the entries
\{1,2,\ldots,i\}
. Note that
T_{|i}
is also
an SYT. Let
T_{|i}=a_{i},b_{i}
where
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
are the number of
elements in the first and second row of
T_{|i}
respectively.
Define
\mathrm{RowDiff}(T_{|i})=a_{i}-b_{i}
to be the difference between the number of elements in the
first row and the number of elements in the second row of
T_{|i}
.
Define an descent
i\in T
to have even (or odd) height
if
\mathrm{RowDiff}(T_{|i})
is even (or odd respectively).
With these definitions, recalling the set
D_{\ell-i}
, we
can give the SYT version of Lemma
31
.

Lemma 32

Fix a positive integer
n
and let
i\leq\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor
. Then, the
probability that an SYT of shape
n-k,k
has all its descents
with odd height in
D_{\lfloor n/2\rfloor-i}
decreases as the number
k

increases (and hence the shape of
T
changes).

Riesz projection and essential S S -spectrum in quaternionic setting
Hatem Baloudi, Sayda Belgacem, Aref Jeribi

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to the investigation of the Weyl and the essential S − S- spectra of a bounded right quaternionic linear operator in a right quaternionic Hilbert space. Using the quaternionic Riesz projection, the S − S- eigenvalue of finite type is both introduced and studied. In particular, we have shown that the Weyl and the essential S − S- spectra do not contain eigenvalues of finite type. We have also described the boundary of the Weyl S − S- spectrum and the particular case of the spectral theorem of the essential S − S- spectrum.

The obscurity that seemed to surround the precise definition of the quaternionic spectrum of a linear operator was deemed a stumbling block. However, in 2006, F. Colombo and I. Motivated by the new concept of

[

, if

is a separable quaternionic Hilbert space, then
\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
(the set of all right bounded operators) is a quaternionic two-sided

In order to explain the objective of this work, we start by recalling a few results concerning the discrete spectrum and the Riesz projection in the complex setting. Let
T
be a linear operator acting on a complex Banach space

\displaystyle P_{\sigma}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{C_{\sigma}}(z-T)^{-1}dz

where

\sigma(T)\backslash\sigma
. The discrete spectrum of

, is the set of isolated point

of

such that the corresponding Riesz projection

are finite dimensional, see
[
21
,
28

. Note that in general we have
\sigma_{e}(T)\subset\sigma(T)\backslash\sigma_{d}(T)

denotes the set of essential spectrum of

[

,

]
for more properties of

. Note that, if
A
is a self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space, then
\sigma_{e}(T)=\sigma(T)\backslash\sigma_{d}(T)
. In particular, the essential spectrum is empty if and only if

. We point out that this point, namely the absence of the essential spectrum, has been studied in many works, e.g.,
[

27

.

In the quaternionic setting, if
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})

is not an isolated part of

\displaystyle\Omega:=\sigma_{S}(T)/\sim

where

the set of representative, then if

is an isolated part of

The first aim of this work is to study the isolated part of the
S-
spectrum of a bounded right quaternionic operators and its relation with the essential
S
-spectrum. To begin with, we consider the Riesz projector associated with a given quaternionic operator

2.1.
Quaternions

Let
\mathbb{H}
be the Hamiltonian skew field of quaternions. This class of numbers can be written as:

\displaystyle q=q_{0}+q_{1}i+q_{2}j+q_{3}k,

where
q_{l}\in\mathbb{R}
for
l=0,1,2,3
and
i\ ,j\ ,k
are the three quaternionic imaginary units satisfying

\displaystyle i^{2}=j^{2}=k^{2}=ijk=-1.

The real and the imaginary part of
q
is defined as
{\rm Re}(q)=q_{0}
and
{\rm Im}(q)=q_{1}i+q_{2}j+q_{3}k
, respectively. Then, the conjugate and the usual norm of the quaternion q are given respectively by

\displaystyle\overline{q}=q_{0}-q_{1}i-q_{2}j-q_{3}k\mbox{ and }|q|=\sqrt{q\overline{q}}.

The set of all imaginary unit quaternions in
\mathbb{H}
is denoted by
\mathbb{S}
and defined as

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}=\Big\{q_{1}i+q_{2}j+q_{3}k:\ q_{1},\ q_{2},\ q_{3}\in\mathbb{R},q_{1}^{2}+q_{2}^{2}+q_{3}^{2}=1\Big\}.

The name imaginary unit is due the fact that, for any
I\in\mathbb{S}
, we have

\displaystyle I^{2}=-\overline{I}I=-|I|^{2}=-1.

For every
q\in\mathbb{H}\backslash\mathbb{R}
, we associate the unique element

\displaystyle I_{q}:=\frac{{\rm Im}(q)}{|{\rm Im}(q)|}\in\mathbb{S}

such that

\displaystyle q={\rm Re}(q)+I_{q}|{\rm Im}(q)|.

This implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}=\bigcup_{I\in\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{C}_{I}.

where

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}_{I}:=\mathbb{R}+I\mathbb{R}.

We can associate to
q\in\mathbb{H}
the
2-
dimensional sphere

\displaystyle[q]:=\{{\rm Re}(q)+I|{\rm Im}(q)|:\ I\in\mathbb{S}\}.

This sphere has center at the real point
{\rm Re}(q)
and radius
|{\rm Im}(q)|
.

2.2.
Right quaternionic Hilbert space and operator

In this subsection, we recall the concept of right quaternionic Hilbert space and right linear operator (see,
[
1
,
12
,
19
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

[
1
]

Let
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
be a right vector space. The map

\displaystyle\langle.,.\rangle:V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}\times V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{H}

is called an inner product if it satisfies the following properties:

(i)

\langle f,gq+h\rangle=\langle f,g\rangle q+\langle f,h\rangle
, for all
f,g,h\in V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
and
q\in\mathbb{H}
.

(ii)

\langle f,g\rangle=\overline{\langle g,f\rangle}
, for all
f,g\in V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
.

(iii)
If
f\in V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
, then
\langle f,f\rangle\geq 0
and
f=0
if
\langle f,f\rangle=0
.

The pair
(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R},\langle.,.\rangle)
is called a right quaternionic pre-Hilbert space. Moreover,
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
is said to be right quaternionic Hilbert space, if

\displaystyle\|f\|=\sqrt{\langle f,f\rangle}

defines a norm for which
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
is complete.

In the sequel, we assume that
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
is complete and separable.
We now recall the concept of Hilbert basis in the quaternionic case. First, we review the following proposition, the proof of which is similar to its complex version, see
[
19
,
33
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
be a right quaternionic Hilbert space and let
\mathcal{F}=\{f_{k}:\ k\in\mathbb{N}\}
be an orthonormal subset of
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
. The following properties are equivalent:

(1)

For every
f,g\in V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
, the series
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\langle f,f_{k}\rangle\langle f_{k},g\rangle
converges absolutely and

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\langle f,f_{k}\rangle\langle f_{k},g\rangle.

(2)

For every
f\in V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|\langle f_{k},f\rangle|^{2}

(3)

\mathcal{F}^{\bot}:=\Big\{f\in V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}:\ \langle f,g\rangle=0\mbox{ for all }g\in\mathcal{F}\Big\}=\{0\}
.

(4)

\langle\mathcal{F}\rangle:=\Big\{\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{m}f_{l}q_{l}:\ f_{l}\in\mathcal{F},\ q_{l}\in\mathbb{H},\ m\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}
is dense in
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an orthonormal subset of
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
.
\mathcal{F}
is said to be Hilbert basis of
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
if
\mathcal{F}
verifies one of the equivalent conditions of Proposition
2.2
.

The proof of the following proposition is the same as its complex version, see
[
19
,
33
]
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
be a right quaternionic Hilbert space. Then,

(1)

V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
admits a Hilbert basis.

(2)

Two Hilbert basis of
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
have the same cardinality.

(3)

If
\mathcal{F}
is a Hilbert basis of
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
, then every
f\in V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
can be uniquely decomposed as follows:

\displaystyle f=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}f_{k}\langle f_{k},f\rangle

where the series
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}f_{k}\langle f_{k},f\rangle
converges absolutely in
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
.

The quaternionic multiplication is not commutative. Afterwards, we recall that if
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
is a right separable quaternionic Hilbert space, we can define the left scalar multiplication on
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
using an arbitrary Hilbert basis on
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
. We refer to
[
19
]
for an explanation of this construction. Let
\mathcal{F}=\Big\{f_{k}:\ k\in\mathbb{N}\Big\}
be a Hilbert basis of
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
. The left scalar multiplication on
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
induced by
\mathcal{F}
is defined as the map

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}\times V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}\longrightarrow V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}

\displaystyle\ (q,f)\longmapsto\ qf=\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}f_{k}q\langle f_{k},f\rangle.

The properties of the left scalar multiplication are described in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.5
.

[
19
, Proposition 3.1]
Let
f,g\in V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
and
p,q\in\mathbb{H}
, then

(1)

q(f+g)=qf+qg
and
q(fp)=(qf)p
.

(2)

\|qf\|=|q|\|f\|
.

(3)

q(pf)=(qp)f
.

(4)

\langle\overline{q}f,g\rangle=\langle f,qg\rangle
.

(5)

rf=fr
, for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
.

(6)

qf_{k}=f_{k}q
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

It is easy to see that
(p+q)f=pf+qf
, for all
p,q\in\mathbb{H}
and
f\in V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
. In the sequel, we consider
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
as a right quaternionic Hilbert space equipped with the left scalar multiplication.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
be a right quaternionic Hilbert space. A mapping
T:\mathcal{D}(T)\subset V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}\longrightarrow V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
, where
\mathcal{D}(T)
denote the domain of
T
, is called
quaternionic right linear
if

\displaystyle T(f+gq)=T(f)+T(g)q,\mbox{ for all }f,\ g\in\mathcal{D}(T)\mbox{ and }q\in\mathbb{H}.

The operator
T
is called closed, if the graph
\mathcal{G}(T):=\{(f,Tf):\ f\in\mathcal{D}(T)\}
is a closed right linear subspace of
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}\times V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
.

We call an quaternionic right operator
T
bounded if

\displaystyle\|T\|:=\sup\Big\{\|Tf\|:\ f\in V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R},\ \|f\|=1\Big\}<+\infty.

The set of all bounded right operators on
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
is denoted by
\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
and the identity operator on
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
will be denoted by
\mathbb{I}_{V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}}
. Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
, we denote the null space of
T
by
N(T)
and its range space by
R(T)
. A closed subspace
M
of
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
is said to be
T-
invariant subspace if
T(M)\subset M
. Note that the function
f\longmapsto Tf-fq
is not right linear, we refer to
[
12
,
13
]
for this point of view. The fundamental suggestion of
[
12
]
is to define the spectrum using the Cauchy kernel series. We recall these concepts from the book
[
13
]
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
T:\mathcal{D}(T)\subset V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}\longrightarrow V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
be a right linear operator. We define the operator
Q_{q}(T):\mathcal{D}(T^{2})\longrightarrow V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
by

\displaystyle Q_{q}(T):=T^{2}-2{\rm Re}(q)T+|q|^{2}\mathbb{I}_{V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}}.

1)
The
S-
resolvent
set of
T
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\rho_{S}(T):=\Big\{q\in\mathbb{H}:\ N(Q_{q}(T))=\{0\},\overline{R(T)}=\mathbb{H}\mbox{ and }Q_{q}(T)^{-1}\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})\Big\}.

2)
The
S-
spectrum
of
T
is defined as:

\displaystyle\sigma_{S}(T)=\mathbb{H}\backslash\rho_{S}(T).

3)
The
point
S-
spectrum
of
T
is given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{pS}(T):=\Big\{q\in\mathbb{H}:\ N(Q_{q}(T))\neq\{0\}\Big\}.

For
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
, the
S-
spectrum
\sigma_{S}(T)
is a non-empty compact set, see
[
13
]
. We recall that if
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
and
q\in\sigma_{S}(T)
, then all the elements of the sphere
[q]
belong to
\sigma_{S}(T)
, see
[
3
, Theorem 7.2.8]
.

Let
v\in V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}\backslash\{0\}
, then
v
is a right eigenvalue of
T
if
T(v)
is a right quaternionic multiple of
v
. That, is

\displaystyle T(v)=vq

where
q\in\mathbb{H}
, known as the right eigenvalue. The set of right eigenvalues coincides with the point
S-
spectrum, see
[
19
, Proposition 4.5]
.

Now, we recall the definition of essential
S-
spectrum, we refer to
[
29
,
30
]
for more details. Let
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
be a separable right quaternionic Hilbert space equipped with a left scalar multiplication. Using

\displaystyle\pi:\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}):=\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})/\mathcal{K}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})

\displaystyle\ \ \quad\ \quad T\longmapsto\ \ [T]=T+\mathcal{K}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}).

Note that

\displaystyle\|[T]\|=\inf_{K\in\mathcal{K}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})}\|A+K\|.

.

[

2.3.
The quaternionic functional calculus

The quaternionic functional calculus is defined on the class of slice regular function
f:U\longrightarrow\mathbb{H}
for some set
U\subset\mathbb{H}
. We recall this concept and refer to
[
9
,
11
,
13
]
and the references therein on the matter.

Definition 2.9
.

A set
U\subset\mathbb{H}
is called

(i)
axially symmetric if
[x]\subset U
for any
x\in U
and

(ii)
a slice domain if
U
is open,
U\cap\mathbb{R}\neq\emptyset
and
U\cap\mathbb{C}_{I}
is a domain in
\mathbb{C}_{I}
, for any
I\in\mathbb{S}
.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{H}
be an open set.
A real differentiable function
f:U\longrightarrow\mathbb{H}
is said to be left
s-
regular
(
resp. right
s-
regular
)
if for every
I\in\mathbb{S}
, the function
f
satisfy

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}[\frac{\partial}{\partial x}f(x+Iy)+I\frac{\partial}{\partial y}f(x+Iy)]=0\ \Big(resp.\frac{1}{2}[\frac{\partial}{\partial x}f(x+Iy)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\frac{\partial}{\partial y}f(x+Iy)I]=0\Big).

We denote the class of left
s-
regular
(
resp. right
s-
regular
)
by
\mathcal{R}^{L}(U)

(
resp.
\mathcal{R}^{R}(U))
. We recall that
\mathcal{R}^{L}(U)
is a right
\mathbb{H}-
module and
\mathcal{R}^{R}(U)
is a left
\mathbb{H}-
module. Let
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
be a separable right quaternionic Hilbert space equipped with a Hilbert basic
\mathcal{N}
and with a left scalar multiplication. We recall that
\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
is a two-sided ideal quaternionic Banach algebra with respect to the left multiplication given by

\displaystyle(q.T)f=\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\mathcal{N}}gq\langle g,Tf\rangle\mbox{ and }(Tq)f=\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\mathcal{N}}T(g)q\langle g,f\rangle.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
and

. The

S-
resolvent operator
is given by

\displaystyle S_{L}^{-1}(q,T):=-Q_{q}(T)^{-1}(T-\overline{q}\mathbb{I}_{V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}}),

and the

S-
resolvent operator
is defined by

\displaystyle S_{R}^{-1}(q,T):=-(T-\overline{q}\mathbb{I}_{V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}})Q_{q}(T)^{-1}.

Definition 2.12
.

[
4

Let

and let

be an axially symmetric

. We say that

is a
T-
admissible open set.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
,
W\subset\mathbb{H}
be open set. A function

)

.

We denote by

(
resp.
\mathcal{R}^{R}_{\sigma_{S}(T)})
the set of locally left
(
resp. right
)
regular functions on
\sigma_{S}(T)
. Now, we recall the two versions of the quaternionic functional calculus.

Definition 2.14
.

[

Let

and

be a
T-
admissible domain. Then,

(1)

f(T)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{I})}S_{L}^{-1}(q,T)dq_{I}f(q)\ \forall f\in\mathcal{R}^{L}_{\sigma_{S}(T)}

(2)

f(T)=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\partial(U\cap\mathbb{C}_{I})}f(q)dq_{I}S_{R}^{-1}(q,T)\ \forall f\in\mathcal{R}^{R}_{\sigma_{S}(T)}

where

The two integrals that appear in Eqs

and
(\ref{e:5})
are independent of the choice of imaginary unit

\displaystyle\sigma_{S}(T)=\sigma_{pS}(T)=\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}(0,1),

where
\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}(0,1)
is the closed quaternionic unit ball. By Theorem
3.10
, none of these

eigenvalues are of finite type.

We recall:

Lemma 3.13
.

[

Let
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
be a quaternionic right vector space and
E
be a right linear independent subspace of
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
. Then, there exists a right basis
B
of
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
such that

. In particular, every quaternionic right vector space has a right basis.

Proposition 3.14
.

[
24
, Proposition 4.44]

Let
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
be a quaternionic Hilbert space and
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
. If
q_{1},....,q_{n}\in\mathbb{H}
are right eigenvalues of

such that
[q_{i}]\neq[q_{j}]
,
\forall 1\leq i<j\leq n\ (n\geq 2)
, and

, then
x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}
are right-linearly independent in

.

We have the following lemma:

Lemma 3.15
.

Assume that
\dim(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})<\infty
and let

. Then,
\#E_{T}<\infty
. In this case, set

\displaystyle E_{T}=\Big\{q_{1},q_{2},...,q_{n}\Big\},

then

is a direct sum of
T-
invariant right subspaces
V_{1,\mathbb{H}}^{R},\ V_{2,\mathbb{H}}^{R},\ ....,\ V_{n,\mathbb{H}}^{R}
. Moreover, if
T_{i}:=T|_{V_{i,\mathbb{H}}^{R}}:\ V_{i,\mathbb{H}}^{R}\longrightarrow V_{i,\mathbb{H}}^{R}
, for
i=1,...,n
, then

\displaystyle\sigma_{S}(T_{i})=\Big\{hq_{i}h^{-1}:\ h\in\mathbb{H}^{*}\Big\}.

Proof.

Combine Lemma
3.13
, Proposition
3.14
and Theorem
3.10
∎

Let
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
be a quaternionic right Hilbert space,
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
and
q
be a right eigenvalue of
T
of finite type. In this way, we see that

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}=R(P_{[q]})\oplus R(P_{\sigma_{S}(T)\backslash[q]}).

Definition 3.16
.

The algebraic multiplicity of the right eigenvalue
q
is, by definition, the dimension of the space
R(P_{[q]})
.

In the next, we write:

\displaystyle m_{T}(q):=\dim R(P_{[q]}).

We refer to
[
21
,
25
]
for the definition in complex setting. Now, inspired by the description of the Riesz projection in the complex setting, see
[
21
]
, we give a quaternionic version of a result describing the finite part of right eigenvalues of finite type.

Theorem 3.17
.

Let
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
be a quaternionic Hilbert space,
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
and
\sigma

be an axially symmetric isolated part of
\sigma_{S}(T)
. Set
P_{\sigma}
the Riesz projection correspond to
\sigma
and take

\displaystyle E_{T}^{\sigma}=\sigma/\cong

where
q\cong p
if and only if
p\in[q]
. Then,
\dim R(P_{\sigma})<\infty
if and only if
\#E_{T}^{\sigma}<\infty
and
q
is a right eigenvalue of finite type for all
q\in E_{T}^{\sigma}
. Besides, if so, then

\displaystyle\dim R(P_{\sigma})=\displaystyle\sum_{q\in E_{T}^{\sigma}}\dim R(P_{[q]}).

Proof.

If
\dim R(P_{\sigma})<\infty
, then
P_{\sigma}T
is a finite rank operator. In this way, we see that

\displaystyle\#E_{T|_{R(P_{\sigma})}}^{\sigma}=n<\infty.

By Lemma
3.15
,

\displaystyle R(P_{\sigma})=V_{1,\mathbb{H}}^{R}\oplus V_{2,\mathbb{H}}^{R}\oplus...\oplus V_{n,\mathbb{H}}^{R}

where
V_{j,\mathbb{H}}^{R}
is
T-
invariant with the properties

\displaystyle\sigma_{S}(T|_{V_{j,\mathbb{H}}^{R}})=[q_{j}],\ j=1,...,n.

Let
i\in\{1,...,n\}
. Since
P_{\sigma}
is a projection, then

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}=N(P_{\sigma})\oplus R(P_{\sigma}).

In this fashion, we have

\displaystyle V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}=V_{i,\mathbb{H}}^{R}\oplus W_{i,\mathbb{H}}^{R}

where

\displaystyle W_{i,\mathbb{H}}^{R}=V_{1,\mathbb{H}}^{R}\oplus...\oplus V_{i-1,\mathbb{H}}^{R}\oplus V_{i+1,\mathbb{H}}^{R}\oplus...\oplus V_{n,\mathbb{H}}^{R}\oplus N(P_{\sigma}).

As consequence,
V_{i,\mathbb{H}}^{R}
and
W_{i,\mathbb{H}}^{R}
are
T-
invariant and
\sigma_{S}(T|_{V_{i,\mathbb{H}}^{R}})=[q_{i}]
. So,
q_{i}
is a right eigenvalue of finite type.

Conversely, set
E_{T}^{\sigma}=\{q_{1},\ q_{2}...,q_{n}\}
, where
q_{i}
is a right eigenvalue of
T
of finite type for all
i\in\{1,2,...,n\}
.
Applying
[
12
, Theorem 5.6]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{I}_{R(P_{\sigma})}=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}P_{[q_{i}]}|_{R(P_{\sigma})}.

Since
P_{[q_{i}]}P_{[q_{j}]}=0
for all
i\neq j
, then

\displaystyle R(P_{\sigma})=R(P_{[q_{1}]})\oplus R(P_{[q_{2}]})\oplus...\oplus R(P_{[q_{n}]}).

This implies that

\displaystyle\dim(R(P_{\sigma}))=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dim(R(P_{[q_{i}]})).

In particular, we have
P_{\sigma}
which is a finite rank operator.∎

Remark 3.18
.

In the complex spectral theory, much attention has ben paid to eigenvalue of finite type, see
[
10
,
21
,
25
,
26
,
28
]
. It is useful for the study of the essential spectrum of certain operators-matrices. We refer to
[
10
]
for this point on the two-groupe transport operators. More precisely, let
V_{\mathbb{C}}
be a complex Banach space and let
T
be a closed operator in
V_{\mathbb{C}}
. The Browder resolvent set of
T
is given by

\displaystyle\rho_{B}(T):=\rho(T)\cup\sigma_{d}(T),

where we use the notation
\rho(.)
for the resolvent set of
T
and
\sigma_{d}(.)
the set of eigenvalues of finite type of
T
. In fact, the usual resolvent

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}(A):=(A-\lambda)^{-1}

can be extended to
\rho_{B}(T)
, e.g.
[

]
. Motivated by this,
[

]
gives a version of the Frobenius-Schur factorization using the Browder resolvent. This makes it possible to study the essential spectrum of serval types operators-matrices. In this paper, we have described the discrete

4.
Some results on the Weyl
S
-spectrum

In this section, we develop a deeper understanding of the concept of the Weyl
S
-spectrum of the bounded right linear operator. More precisely, we describe the boundary of the
S
-spectrum. Likewise, we deal with the particular case of the spectral theorem. To begin with, we recall:

Definition 4.1
.

[
30
]
Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
. The Weyl
S-
spectrum is the set

\displaystyle\sigma_{W}^{S}(T)=\displaystyle\bigcap_{K\in\mathcal{K}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})}\sigma_{S}(T+K).

The study of the essential and the Weyl
S-
spectra are established using the Fredholm theory, see
[
29
,
30
]
. We refer to
[
7
]
for the investigation of the Fredholm and Weyl elements with respect to a quaternionic Banach algebra homomorphism.

Definition 4.2
.

A Fredholm operator is an operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
such that
N(T)
and
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}/R(T)
are finite dimensional. We will denote by
\Phi(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
the set of all Fredholm operators.

From
[
29
,
30
]
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})=\Phi_{l}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})\cap\Phi_{r}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{l}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})=\Big\{T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}):\mbox{ R(T) is closed and }\dim(N(T))<\infty\Big\}

and

\displaystyle\Phi_{r}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})=\Big\{T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}):\mbox{ R(T) is closed and }\dim(N(T^{{\dagger}}))<\infty\Big\}.

Let
T\in\Phi_{l}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})\cup\Phi_{r}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
. Then, the index of
T
is given by

\displaystyle i(T):=\dim N(T)-\dim(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}/R(T)).

Theorem 4.3
.

[
29
,
30
]

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
. Then,

\displaystyle\sigma_{e}^{S}(T)=\mathbb{H}\backslash\Phi_{T}\mbox{ and }\sigma_{W}^{S}(T)=\mathbb{H}\backslash W_{T}

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{T}:=\Big\{q\in\mathbb{H}:\ Q_{q}(T)\in\Phi(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})\Big\}

and

\displaystyle W_{T}:=\Big\{q\in\mathbb{H}:\ Q_{q}(T)\in\Phi(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})\mbox{ and }i(Q_{q}(T))=0\Big\}.

Remark 4.4
.

Let
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
be a quaternionic space and
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
.

(1)

Note that, in general, we have

\displaystyle\sigma_{e}^{S}(T)\subset\sigma_{W}^{S}(T)=\sigma_{1,W}^{S}(T)\cup\sigma_{2,W}^{S}(T)\subset\sigma_{S}(T)\backslash\sigma_{d}(T).

where

\displaystyle\sigma_{1,W}^{S}(T):=\mathbb{H}\backslash\Big\{q\in\mathbb{H}:\ Q_{q}(T)\in\Phi_{l}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})\mbox{ and }i(Q_{q}(T))\leq 0\Big\}

and

\displaystyle\sigma_{2,W}^{S}(T):=\mathbb{H}\backslash\Big\{q\in\mathbb{H}:\ Q_{q}(T)\in\Phi_{r}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})\mbox{ and }i(Q_{q}(T))\geq 0\Big\}.

In particular,
\sigma_{W}^{S}(T)
does not contain eigenvalues of finite type.

(2)

In
[
7
]
, one proves that
q\longmapsto i(T)
is constant on any component of
\Phi_{T}
. In this way, we see that if
\Phi_{T}
is connected, then

\displaystyle\sigma_{e}^{S}(T)=\sigma_{W}^{S}(T).

The first result in this section is the next theorem.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
. Then,

\displaystyle\partial\sigma_{W}^{S}(T)\subset\sigma_{1,W}^{S}(T).

In particular, if
\Phi_{T}
is connected, then

\displaystyle\partial\sigma_{e}^{S}(T)=\partial\sigma_{W}^{S}(T)\subset\Big\{q\in\mathbb{H}:\ Q_{q}(T)\not\in\Phi_{l}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})\Big\}.

To prove Theorem
4.5
, we first study the concept of the minimum modulus. Let
V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}
be a separable right Hilbert space and
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
. The minimum modulus of
T
is given by

\displaystyle\mu(T):=\displaystyle\inf_{\|x\|=1}\|Tx\|.

To begin with, we give the following lemma.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
T
and
S
be two bounded right linear operators on a right quaternionic Hilbert space. Then,

(1)

If
\|T-S\|<\mu(T)
, then
\mu(S)>0
and
\overline{R(S)}
is not a proper subset of
\overline{R(T)}
.

(2)

If
\|T-S\|<\frac{\mu(T)}{2}
, then
\overline{R(S)}
is not a proper subset of
\overline{R(T)}
and
\overline{R(T)}

is not a proper subset of
\overline{R(S)}
.

Proof.

The proof is the same as for the complex Banach space, see Lemma 2.3 and lemma 2.4 in
[
20
]
for a complex proof.∎

For
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
,
q\in\mathbb{H}
and
\varepsilon>0
we set:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(T,q,\varepsilon):=\Big\{q^{\prime}\in\mathbb{H}:\ 2|{\rm Re}(q)-{\rm Re}(q^{\prime})|\|T\|+||q^{\prime}|^{2}-|q|^{2}|<\varepsilon.\Big\}

It is clear that
\mathcal{O}(T,q,\varepsilon)
is an open set in
\mathbb{H}
.

Corollary 4.7
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
and
q_{0}\in\rho_{S}(T)
. Then,
q\in\rho_{S}(T)
for each
q\in\mathcal{O}(T,q_{0},\mu(Q_{q_{0}}(T)))
.

Proof.

Let
q\in\mathcal{O}(T,q_{0},\mu(Q_{q_{0}}(T)))
. Then,

\displaystyle\|Q_{q}(T)-Q_{q_{0}}(T)\|

\displaystyle=\|2({\rm Re}(q_{0})-{\rm Re}(q))T+|q|^{2}-|q_{0}|^{2}\|

\displaystyle\leq 2|({\rm Re}(q_{0})-{\rm Re}(q)|\|T\|+||q|^{2}-|q_{0}|^{2}|

\displaystyle<\mu(Q_{q_{0}}(T)).

We can apply Lemma
4.6
to conclude that

\displaystyle\mu(Q_{q}(T))>0\mbox{ and }\overline{R(Q_{q}(T))}=\overline{R(Q_{q_{0}}(T))}=V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}.

By
[
29
, Proposition 3.5]
,
R(Q_{q}(T))
is closed. Hence,
q\in\rho_{S}(T)
.∎

We recall:

Lemma 4.8
.

[
3
, Lemma 7.3.9]
Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
q,s\in\mathbb{H}
. Set

\displaystyle P_{2n}(q)=q^{2n}-2{\rm Re}(s^{n})q^{n}+|s^{n}|^{2}.

Then,

\displaystyle P_{2n}(q)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}_{2n-2}(q)(q^{2}-2{\rm Re}(s)q+|s|^{2})

\displaystyle=(q^{2}-2{\rm Re}(s)q+|s|^{2})\mathcal{Q}_{2n-2}(q),

where
\mathcal{Q}_{2n-2}(q)
is a polynomial of degree
2n-2
in
q
.

Proof of Theorem
4.5

Set:

\displaystyle f(T):=\displaystyle\sup_{K\in\mathcal{K}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})}\mu(T+K).

Similar proof in the complex case, we have
f(T)>0
if and only if

\displaystyle T\in\Phi_{l}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})\mbox{ and }\dim N(T)\leq\dim(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}/R(T)).

Now,
Since
\sigma_{e}^{S}(T)
is not empty (e.g.,
[
29
, Proposition 7.14]
) and
\sigma_{e}^{S}(T)\subset\sigma_{W}^{S}(T)
, then
\partial\sigma_{W}^{S}(T)
is not empty. Let us then take an element
p
in
\partial\sigma_{W}^{S}(T)
. Assume that
p\not\in\sigma_{1,w}^{S}(T)
. Then,

\displaystyle f(Q_{p}(T))>0.

So, there exists
K_{0}\in\mathcal{K}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
such that

\displaystyle\mu(Q_{p}(T)+K_{0})>0.

Since
\mathcal{O}(T,p,\frac{\mu(Q_{p}(T)+K_{0})}{2})
is an open neighborhood of
p
and

\displaystyle p\in\overline{\Big\{q\in\mathbb{H}:\ Q_{q}(T)\in\Phi(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})\mbox{ and }i(Q_{q}(T))=0\Big\}},

then there exists
p_{0}\in\mathcal{O}(T,p,\frac{\mu(Q_{p}(T)+K_{0})}{2})
such that

\displaystyle p_{0}\in W_{T}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\|Q_{p_{0}}(T)+K_{0}-Q_{p}(T)-K_{0}\|

\displaystyle\leq||p_{0}|^{2}-|p|^{2}|+2|Re(p)-Re(p_{0})|\|T\|

\displaystyle<\displaystyle\frac{\mu(Q_{p}(T)+K_{0})}{2}.

Applying Lemma
4.6
, we obtain

\displaystyle R(Q_{p}(T)+K_{0})=V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}.

Indeed, since
\mu(Q_{p_{0}}+K_{0})>0
and
p_{0}\in W_{T}
, then

\displaystyle\dim(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}/R(Q_{p_{0}}(T)+K_{0}))=0.

In this way, we see that

\displaystyle Q_{p}(T)+K_{0}\in\Phi(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})\mbox{ and }i(Q_{p}(T)+K_{0})=0.

This implies that,
p\not\in\sigma_{W}^{S}(T)
.

The rest of the proof follows immediately from
[
7
, Theorem 5.13]
.∎

We will now deal with the particular spectral theorem for the essential S-spectra.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
. Then,

\displaystyle\sigma_{e}^{S}(T^{n})=\Big\{q^{n}\in\mathbb{H}:\ q\in\sigma_{e}^{S}(T)\Big\}=(\sigma_{e}^{S}(T))^{n}.

Proof.

According to
[
3
, Lemma 3.10]
and the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 7.3.11]
we have

\displaystyle T^{2n}-2{\rm Re}(q)T^{n}+|q|^{2}\mathbb{I}_{V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}}=\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}(T^{2}-2Re(q_{j})T+|q_{j}|^{2}\mathbb{I}_{V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}}),

where
q_{j},j=0,...,n-1
are the solutions of
p^{n}=q
in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}_{I_{q}}
. Let
q\in\sigma_{e}^{S}(T^{n})
. Then,
Q_{q}(T^{n})\not\in\Phi(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
.
We can apply
[
29
, Theorem 6.13]
, we infer that there exists
i\in\{0,1,...,n-1\}
such that

\displaystyle Q_{q_{i}}(T)\not\in\Phi(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}).

Therefore,
q_{i}\in\sigma_{e}^{S}(T)
. In this way, we see that
q=q_{i}^{n}\in(\sigma_{e}^{S}(T))^{n}
. To prove the inverse inclusion, we consider
p=q^{n}
, where
q\in\sigma_{e}^{S}(T)
.
By Lemma
4.8
and
[
3
, Theorem 7.3.7]
, we get

\displaystyle T^{2n}-2{\rm Re}(q^{n})T^{n}+|q^{n}|^{2}\mathbb{I}_{V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{Q}_{2n-2}(T)(T^{2}-2{\rm Re}(q)T+|q|^{2}\mathbb{I}_{V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}})

\displaystyle=(T^{2}-2{\rm Re}(q)T+|q|^{2}\mathbb{I}_{V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}})\mathcal{Q}_{2n-2}(T).

Since
Q_{q}(T)\not\in\Phi(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R})
, we can apply
[
29
, Corollary 6.14]
, we deduce that

\displaystyle T^{2n}-2{\rm Re}(q^{n})T^{n}+|q^{n}|^{2}\mathbb{I}_{V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}}\not\in\Phi(V_{\mathbb{H}}^{R}).

So,
p\in\sigma_{e}^{S}(T^{n})
. ∎

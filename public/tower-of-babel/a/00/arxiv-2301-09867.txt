Restricted optimal pebbling is NP-hard
László F. Papp

Abstract
Consider a distribution of pebbles on a graph. A pebbling move removes two pebbles from a vertex and place one at an adjacent vertex. A vertex is reachable under a pebble distribution if it has a pebble after the application of a sequence of pebbling moves. A pebble distribution is solvable if each vertex is reachable under it. The size of a pebble distribution is the total number of pebbles. The optimal pebbling number π ∗ ​ ( G ) \pi^{*}(G) is the size of the smallest solvable distribution. A t t -restricted pebble distribution places at most t t pebbles at each vertex. The t t -restricted optimal pebbling number π t ∗ ​ ( G ) \pi_{t}^{*}(G) is the size of the smallest solvable t t -restricted pebble distribution. We show that deciding whether π 2 ∗ ​ ( G ) ≤ k \pi^{*}_{2}(G)\leq k is NP-complete. We prove that π t ∗ ​ ( G ) = π ∗ ​ ( G ) \pi_{t}^{*}(G)=\pi^{*}(G) if δ ⁡ ( G ) ≥ 2 ​ | V ⁡ ( G ) | 3 − 1 \delta(G)\geq\frac{2|V(G)|}{3}-1 and we show infinitely many graphs which satisfies δ ⁡ ( H ) ≈ 1 2 ​ | V ⁡ ( H ) | \delta(H)\approx\frac{1}{2}|V(H)| but π t ∗ ​ ( H ) ≠ π ∗ ​ ( H ) \pi_{t}^{*}(H)\neq\pi^{*}(H) , where δ \delta denotes the minimum degree.

Introduction

Graph pebbling is a game on graphs initiated by a method of Saks and Lagarias to answer a number-theoretic question of Erdős and Lemke, which was successfully carried out by Chung in 1989
[
4
]
.
Each graph in this paper is simple.
We denote the vertex set and the edge set of graph
G
by
V(G)
and
E(G)
, respectively.
A
pebble distribution

D
on graph
G
is a function mapping the vertex set to non-negative integers. We can imagine that each vertex
v
has
D(v)
pebbles. A
pebbling move
removes two pebbles from a vertex
v
(having at least two pebbles) and places one on an adjacent vertex
u
. We denote this move by
(v\rightarrow u)
.

If
\sigma
is a sequence of pebbling moves, then let
D\sigma
be the distribution which we obtain after the application of
\sigma
to distribtuion
D
.
A sequence of pebbling moves is
executable
under
D
if for any
\tau
prefix of
\sigma

D\tau
is a pebble distribution. In other words,
\sigma
never removes two pebbles from a vertex which does not have at least two pebbles. A vertex
v
is
k
-reachable
under a distribution

if there is an executable sequence of pebbling moves
\sigma
such that
D\sigma(v)\geq k
. When
k=1
we write reachable instead of
1
-reachable.
We say that a distribution
D
is
solvable
if each vertex of the graph is reachable under
D
.

Let
|D|
denotes the total number of pebbles placed on the graph by
D
, so
|D|=\sum_{v\in V(G)}D(v)
. We call this quantity as the
size of

D
.
A pebble distribution
D
on a graph
G
is called
optimal
if it is solvable and
its size is the smallest possible. This size is called the
optimal pebbling number
and denoted by
\pi^{*}(G)
.

The optimal pebbling number of several graph families are known. For example exact values were given for paths and cycles
[
6
]

[
13
]
, ladders
[
1
]
, caterpillars
[
7
]
,
m
-ary trees
[
8
]
and some staircase graphs
[
9
]
.
The values for graphs with diameter smaller than four are also characterized by some easily checkable domination conditions
[
12
]
. However, determining the optimal pebbling number for a given graph is NP-hard
[
10
]
.

In
[
2
]
, Chelalli
et al.
introduced a new version of pebbling. In this setting, a pebble distribution is
t
-restricted
if no vertex has more than
t
pebbles.
The
t
-restricted
optimal pebbling number, denoted by
\pi^{*}_{t}
, is the size of the solvable
t
-restricted distribution containing the least number of pebbles. It is easy to see that
\pi_{2}^{*}(G)\geq\pi_{t}^{*}(G)\geq\pi_{t+1}^{*}(G)\geq\pi^{*}(G)
.

A set
S
is a dominating set of graph
G
if
S\subseteq V(G)
and each vertex of
G
is contained in
S
or adjacent to an element of
S
. The domination number
\gamma(G)
of graph
G
is the size of the smallest domination set of
G
. A function
f:V(G)\rightarrow\{0,1,2\}
is called as a Roman domination function if each
v\in V(G)
for which
f(v)=0
there is a vertex
u
which is adjacent to
v
and

. The weight of a Roman domination function is
\sum_{v\in V(G)}f(v)
. The Roman domination number
\gamma_{R}(G)
is the minimum weight of a Roman domination function.

Chelalli
et al.
proved several bounds on these domination parameters by using the
2
-restricted optimal pebbling number. They showed that
\pi_{2}^{*}(G)\leq\gamma_{R}(G)

[
2
]
. These domination parameters are well studied and they are in the limelight. Computing the Roman domination number is a hard task. More specifically, the decision version of the Roman domination number is NP-complete
[
5
]

Recently Shiue
et al.
in
[
3
]
defined a new version of pebbling called
(d,t)
-pebbling. In this new model a pebble distribution

is called
(d,t)
-solvable if each vertex

can have

pebbles after a sequence of pebbling moves which only remove pebbles from vertices whose distance from

(d,t)
-pebbling number

of

is the minimum size of a
(d,t)
-solvable distribution of

. Shiue showed that
\gamma_{R}(G)=\pi^{*}_{(1,1)}(G)

[
14
]
, so the Roman domination number is a special case of this generalised pebbling parameter. In a
(1,1)
-solvable distribution no vertex has more than
2
pebbles, because
2
pebbles at a vertex are enough to reach the adjacent vertices and we cannot move a pebble further in this setting. So to sum up,
\pi^{*}(G)\leq\pi^{*}_{2}(G)\leq\pi^{*}_{(1,1)}=\gamma_{R}(G)
.

Therefore the
t
-restricted optimal pebbling number is trapped between two graph parameters whose calculation is an NP-hard task. Somebody may ask that what is the computational complexity of the
2
-restricted optimal pebbling number?

In Section
2
we show that deciding whether the

-restricted optimal pebbling number is at most

is NP-complete for any

. Note that the

denotes the lexicographic graph product. We use this result to give a reduction from optimal pebbling to

The authors of

Lemma 2.1

Let

be a connected graph whose order is at least
2
.
G
has an optimal distribution

such that for every vertex

which is not 2-reachable under

we have that

Proof:

If a vertex is not 2-reachable but it has some pebbles, then it contains exactly one pebble.
Let

be an optimal distribution on graph

and let

be the set of vertices which are not 2-reachable under

but each of them has a pebble.

Consider a vertex

. Since the graph is connected it has a neighbor

. This vertex can receive a pebble without the usage of the pebble placed at
v
.
If we remove the pebble placed at
v
and place it at

, then under the obtained distribution

is 2-reachable so

is reachable. The relocated pebble could not be moved under
D
so if a vertex was
k
-reachable under
D
, then it is also
k
-reachable under the obtained distribution
D^{\prime}
. Therefore
D^{\prime}
is optimal and
S(D^{\prime})\subseteq S(D)
. Since
v
is not in
S(D^{\prime})
we have that
|S(D^{\prime})|<|S(D)|
. If we repeat this procedure, then we end up with a distribution

such that

.

\Box

G\cdot H
denotes the lexicographic product of graphs

and

V(G\cdot H)=V(G)\times V(H)
and
(g_{1},h_{1})
and
(g_{2},h_{2})

\{g_{1},g_{2}\}\in E(G)

.

Let

We say that

collapses

Theorem 2.3

If

is a connected n-vertex graph,

\pi^{*}(G)=\pi^{*}\left(G\cdot K_{m}\right)=\pi^{*}_{t}\left(G\cdot K_{m}\right).

Proof:

Let

be an optimal pebble distribution of

which satisfies that if a vertex has one pebble then it is 2-reachable under

. Lemma

guarantees that such a pebble distribution exists.

Since
|D|\leq\left\lceil\frac{2n}{3}\right\rceil

.

Denote the vertices of

with

Q\left((g,i)\right)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\ i=0\ \text{and}\ D(g)\ \text{is odd}\\
2&\text{if}\ 1\leq i\leq\frac{D(g)}{2}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

For each

. Therefore

.
Now we show that

is a solvable distribution on

. To do this we show that any vertex

is reachable under

.

If

When

We construct

.
The lengths of

and

will be the same. If

, then let

\tau_{i}=\begin{cases}((g,k)\rightarrow(g^{\prime},0))\ \text{if}\ k\leq\frac{D(g)}{2}\\

Finally, if the last step of

.

Lemma 3.1

Let
G
be a graph. If
D
is a pebble distribution on
G
such that
D(v)=3
for a particular vertex

and

for any other vertex

is not optimal.

Proof:

To reach a vertex the third pebble of

is not needed. Any nonempty sequence of pebbling moves, which can be applied to

, removes two pebbles from
v
in the first step.
Then at each step only one vertex has more than one pebble and every other vertex has at most as many pebbles as initially. When the third pebble of
v
is used each vertex has at most as many pebbles as it has under
D
. Therefore the subsequence whose first move removes the third pebble of
v
and contains all the later moves is executable under
D
and reaches the same vertex as the original sequence of pebbling moves. But it removes only two pebbles from
v
, so one useless pebble remains there.

\Box

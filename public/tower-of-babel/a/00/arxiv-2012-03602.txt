Partial derivatives in the nonsmooth setting
Nicola Gigli, Chiara Rigoni

Abstract
We study partial derivatives on the product of two metric measure structures, in particular in connection with calculus via modules as proposed by the first named author in [ 12 ] . Our main results are: i) The extension to this non-smooth framework of Schwarz’s theorem about symmetry of mixed second derivatives ii) A quite complete set of results relating the property f ∈ W 2 , 2 ​ ( 𝖷 × 𝖸 ) f\in W^{2,2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}) on one side with that of f ⁡ ( ⋅ , y ) ∈ W 2 , 2 ​ ( 𝖷 ) f(\cdot,y)\in W^{2,2}({\sf X}) and f ⁡ ( x , ⋅ ) ∈ W 2 , 2 ​ ( 𝖸 ) f(x,\cdot)\in W^{2,2}({\sf Y}) for a.e. y , x y,x respectively on the other. Here 𝖷 , 𝖸 {\sf X},{\sf Y} are 𝖱𝖢𝖣 {\sf RCD} spaces so that second order Sobolev spaces are well defined. These results are in turn based upon the study of Sobolev regularity, and of the underlying notion of differential, for a map with values in a Hilbert module: we mainly apply this notion to the map x ↦ d 𝗒 ​ f ​ ( x , ⋅ ) x\mapsto{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f(x,\cdot) in order to build, under the appropriate regularity requirements, its differential d 𝗑 ​ d 𝗒 ​ f {\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f .



1
Introduction

Given two smooth manifolds
M,N
and points
p\in M
,
q\in N
, it is a classical fact in differential geometry that

T_{(p,q)}(M\times N)\cong T_{p}M\times T_{q}N

(1.0.1)

where the isomorphism is given by
({\mathrm{d}}\pi_{N},{\mathrm{d}}\pi_{M})
, with
\pi_{N},\pi_{M}
being the projection onto the respective coordinates and
{\mathrm{d}}\pi_{N},{\mathrm{d}}\pi_{M}
denote their differentials. This being valid for any
(p,q)\in M\times N
we can regard any vector field
v
on
M\times N
via its components, i.e. we can write
v=(v_{x},v_{y})
where
v_{x}
is a vector field on
M
parametrized by
q\in N
(or, better, a section of the pullback of the tangent bundle of
M
via
\pi_{M}
) and symmetrically
v_{y}
is a vector field on
N
parametrized by
p\in M
.

In the nonsmooth setting the analogue of (
1.0.1
) has been investigated in our earlier work
[
16
]
. Here the concept of tangent space is interpreted in terms of the language of normed modules introduced in
[
12
]
and is tightly linked to Sobolev calculus. In this sense it should not surprise that the validity of (the analogue of) (
1.0.1
) on metric measure spaces is related to the validity of appropriate tensorization properties of Sobolev spaces (this latter topic has been investigated only relatively recently: the first studies we are aware of have been conducted in
[
4
]
, see also
[
6
]
and
[
13
]
for more recent contributions). Without entering into details here, let us just say that these tensorization properties of Sobolev spaces we are alluding to are always satisfied if the spaces
{\sf X},{\sf Y}
are
{\sf RCD}
spaces (see Proposition
4.1
) and that in this case the appropriate analogue of (
1.0.1
) holds (see Theorem
2.8
). This has been established in our earlier work
[
16
]
.

In the present manuscript we push the investigation further, in particular in connection with higher order differentiations (in
[
16
]
only first order derivatives have been considered). Our main results are:

i)

A generalization to this setting of the classical theorem by Schwarz about symmetry of second order derivatives: we shall see in Theorem
3.15
that under fairly general assumptions the identity

{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f={\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f

(1.0.2)

holds even in this framework

ii)

A thorough study of the relation between second order regularity in the product of two
{\sf RCD}
spaces and second order regularity in the factors which-among other things-shows that the expected formula

{\rm Hess}(f)=\Bigg(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(f)&{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f\\
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f&{\rm Hess}_{\sf y}(f)\end{array}\Bigg)

(1.0.3)

holds true (see Theorems
4.7
,
4.12
and Propositions
4.8
,
4.13
). The discussion here is complicated by the fact that on
{\sf RCD}
spaces there is no single ‘second order Sobolev space’ as it is not clear whether the
`H
’ version (obtained by completion of the space of ‘smooth’ functions) coincides with the
`W
’ one (obtained via integration by parts). Along similar lines we also investigate differentiability properties of vector fields in relation to that of their components (see Theorems
4.15
,
4.18
and Proposition
4.19
).

A crucial aspect of our analysis, and perhaps the most important advance to the theory among those given in the current manuscript, is in the possibility of speaking of Sobolev regularity for functions with values in a (Hilbert) module, and in the related concept of differential. Notice indeed that in order to just state the identities (
1.0.2
) and (
1.0.3
), it is crucial to know what
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f
is and given that for a function
f
in two variables the object
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f
can be interpreted as the map sending
x
to
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f(x,\cdot)\in L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf Y})
, it is imperative to be able to differentiate this sort of maps. This analysis is carried out in Section
3.2
.

Notation and preliminary results

For us a metric measure space
({\sf X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
will always be a complete and separable metric space
({\sf X},\mathsf{d})
equipped with a reference non-negative (and non-zero) Borel measure
\mathfrak{m}
which is finite on bounded sets.

2.1
Sobolev spaces for locally integrable objects

In this section we briefly recall some basic definitions of Sobolev-related objects, with particular focus on quantities which are only integrable on bounded sets. For the definition and properties of Sobolev functions and minimal weak upper gradients we refer to
[
7
]
(see also
[
20
]
and the more recent
[
3
,
4
]
whose presentation we are going to follow) while for what concerns
L^{\infty}
and
L^{0}
normed modules we refer to
[
12
,
16
]
.

Given a metric measure space
({\sf X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
, by
L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
we mean the space of (equivalence classes w.r.t.
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. equality of) Borel functions
f:{\sf X}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{B}f\in L^{2}({\sf X})
for every bounded Borel set
B\subset{\sf X}
. A sequence
(f_{n})\subset L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
converges to
f
in
L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
provided
{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{B}f_{n}\to{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{B}f
in
L^{2}({\sf X})
for every bounded Borel set
B\subset{\sf X}
.

A test plan
\pi
is a Borel probability measure on
C([0,1],{\sf X})
such that

\begin{split}({\rm{e}}_{t})_{*}{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}&\leq C\mathfrak{m},\qquad\forall t\in[0,1],\\
\iint_{0}^{1}|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|\,{\mathrm{d}}t\,{\mathrm{d}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\pi$}}(\gamma)&<\infty,\end{split}

for some
C>0
.

The Sobolev class
{S}^{2}({\sf X})
(resp.
{S}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
) is the space of all Borel functions
f:{\sf X}\to\mathbb{R}
for which there exists
G\in\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{m})
(resp.
G\in\mathit{L}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}(\mathfrak{m})
) non-negative, called weak upper gradient, such that for every test plan
\bm{\pi}
it holds

\int|f(\gamma_{1})-f(\gamma_{0})|\,{\mathrm{d}}\pi(\gamma)\leq\iint_{0}^{1}G(\gamma_{t})|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|\,{\mathrm{d}}t\,{\mathrm{d}}\bm{\pi}(\gamma).

It can be proved that
f\in{S}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
and
G
is a weak upper gradient if and only if for every test plan
\pi
we have that for
\pi
-a.e.
\gamma
the map
t\mapsto f(\gamma_{t})
is in
W^{1,1}(0,1)
and

\Big|\frac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}f(\gamma_{t})\Big|\leq G(\gamma_{t})|\dot{\gamma}_{t}|\qquad a.e.\ t\in[0,1].

In particular, this characterization implies the existence of a minimal weak upper gradient in the
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. sense: we call it
minimal weak upper gradient
and denote it by
|\mathrm{D}f|
.

The Sobolev space
\mathit{W}^{1,2}({\sf X})
(resp.
\mathit{W}^{1,2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
) is defined as
\mathit{L}^{2}\cap{S}^{2}({\sf X})
(resp.
\mathit{L}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}\cap{S}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
). It can be proved that
f\in\mathit{W}^{1,2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
if and only if
\eta f\in\mathit{W}^{1,2}({\sf X})
for every
\eta
Lipschitz with bounded support. We recall that
\mathit{W}^{1,2}({\sf X})
is a Banach space when endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathit{W}^{1,2}({\sf X})}^{2}:=\|f\|_{\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{m})}^{2}+\||\mathrm{D}f|\|_{\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{m})}^{2}.

We say (
[
11
]
) that
({\sf X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is
infinitesimally Hilbertian
provided
\mathit{W}^{1,2}({\sf X})
is a Hilbert space.

It is useful to recall that minimal weak upper gradients have the following important locality property:

|\mathrm{D}f|=|\mathrm{D}g|\qquad\mathfrak{m}-a.e.\text{ on }\{f=g\}\qquad\qquad\forall f,g\in{S}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}).

From the notion of minimal weak upper gradient it is possible to extract the one of differential via the following result:

Theorem/Definition 2.1
.

There exists a unique couple
(\mathit{L}^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X}),\mathrm{d})
, where
\mathit{L}^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})
is a
\mathit{L}^{0}({\sf X})
-normed module and
\mathrm{d}:{S}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})\to\mathit{L}^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})
is a linear map, such that

i)

|\mathrm{d}f|=|\mathrm{D}f|

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. for every
f\in{S}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
,

ii)

\mathit{L}^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})
is generated by
\{\mathrm{d}f:f\in{S}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})\}
, i.e.
\mathit{L}^{0}
-linear combinations of objects of the form
\mathrm{d}f
are dense in
\mathit{L}^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})
.

Uniqueness is intended up to unique isomorphism, i.e. if
(\mathscr{M},\mathrm{d}^{\prime})
is another such couple, then there is a unique isomorphism
\Phi\colon\mathit{L}^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})\to\mathscr{M}
such that
\Phi(\mathrm{d}f)=\mathrm{d}^{\prime}f
for every
f\in{S}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
.

The space of vector fields
\mathit{L}^{0}(T{\sf X})
is defined as the dual of the
\mathit{L}^{0}
-normed module
\mathit{L}^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})
. It can be equivalently characterized as the
\mathit{L}^{0}
-completion of the dual
\mathit{L}^{2}(T{\sf X})
of the
\mathit{L}^{2}
-normed module
\mathit{L}^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X})
(see
[
12
]
,
[
8
]
).
\mathit{L}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}(T{\sf X})\subset\mathit{L}^{0}(T{\sf X})
is the space of
X
’s such that
|X|\in\mathit{L}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}(\mathfrak{m})
.

We say that
X\in\mathit{L}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}(T{\sf X})
has
divergence
in
\mathit{L}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}
, and write
X\in\mathrm{D}({\rm{div}}_{\mathsf{loc}})
, if there exists
h\in\mathit{L}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
such that

\displaystyle\int fh\ \mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}=-\int\mathrm{d}f(X)\ \mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m},\quad\text{for every}\,\,\,f\in\mathit{W}^{1,2}({\sf X})\,\,\,\text{with bounded support}.

In this case we call
h
(which is unique by the density of
\mathit{W}^{1,2}({\sf X})
in
\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{m})
) the divergence of
X
, and denote it by
{\rm div}(X)
.

Let us now assume that
({\sf X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is infinitesimally Hilbertian, so that the pointwise norms in
\mathit{L}^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})
and in
\mathit{L}^{0}(T{\sf X})
induce pointwise scalar products. In this case the modules
\mathit{L}^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})
and
\mathit{L}^{0}(T{\sf X})
are canonically isomorphic via the Riesz (musical) isomorphism

\flat\colon\mathit{L}^{0}(\mathit{T}{\sf X})\rightarrow\mathit{L}^{0}(\mathit{T}^{\ast}{\sf X})\qquad\ \text{and}\qquad\ \sharp\colon\mathit{L}^{0}(\mathit{T}^{\ast}{\sf X})\rightarrow\mathit{L}^{0}(\mathit{T}{\sf X})

defined by

X^{\flat}(Y):=\left<X,Y\right>\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left<\omega^{\sharp},X\right>:=\omega(X)

for every
X,Y\in\mathit{L}^{0}(T{\sf X})
and
\omega\in\mathit{L}^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})
. The
gradient
of a function
f\in\mathit{W}^{1,2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
is defined as
\nabla f:=(\mathrm{d}f)^{\sharp}\in\mathit{L}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}(T{\sf X})
.

We say that
f\in\mathit{W}^{1,2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
has
Laplacian
in
\mathit{L}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}(\mathfrak{m})
, namely
f\in\mathrm{D}(\Delta_{\mathsf{loc}})
, if there exists
h\in\mathit{L}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}(\mathfrak{m})
such that it holds

\displaystyle\int gh\,\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m}=-\int\left<\nabla f,\nabla g\right>\mathrm{d}\mathfrak{m},\quad\text{for every}\,\,\,g\in\mathit{W}^{1,2}({\sf X})\,\,\,\text{with bounded support}

(this is the same as requiring that
\nabla f\in\mathrm{D}({{\rm{div}}}_{\mathsf{loc}})
with
{{\rm{div}}}(\nabla f)=h
). In this case we call
h
the Laplacian of
f
, and we denote it by
\Delta f
. If
f,h\in L^{2}({\sf X})
we shall write
f\in D(\Delta)
instead of
f\in D(\Delta_{\mathsf{loc}})
, and in this case the Laplacian is equivalently defined as infinitesimal generator of the Dirichlet form

\mathsf{E}(f):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dfrac{1}{2}\displaystyle{\int|{\mathrm{d}}f|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}}&\qquad\text{ if }f\in\mathit{W}^{1,2}({\sf X}),\\
+\infty&\qquad\text{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

(2.1.1)

From the properties of the minimal upper gradient we deduce that
\mathsf{E}
is convex, lower semicontinuous and with dense domain, namely
\{f:\mathsf{E}(f)<\infty\}
is dense in
L^{2}(\mathfrak{m})
. Hence, the classical theory of gradient flows of convex functions on Hilbert spaces ensures existence and uniqueness of a 1-parameter semigroup
({{\sf h}}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
of continuous operators from
L^{2}(\mathfrak{m})
to itself such that for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{m})
the curve
t\mapsto{{\sf h}}_{t}(f)\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{m})
is continuous on
[0,\infty)
, absolutely continuous on
(0,\infty)
and satisfies

\dfrac{{\mathrm{d}}}{{\mathrm{d}}t}{{\sf h}}_{t}(f)=\Delta f,\qquad\text{for a.e. }t>0,

where it is part of the statement the fact that
{{\sf h}}_{t}(f)\in\mathrm{D}(\Delta)
for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{m})
and
t>0
(see
[
2
]
, and the references therein). We remark that in the case in which
({\sf X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
is infinitesimally Hilbertian, the Laplacian and the operators
{{\sf h}}_{t}
are linear.

2.2
Calculus tools on product spaces

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X})
and
({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be two metric measure spaces. The product space
{\sf X}\times{\sf Y}
will be always implicitly endowed with the product measure and the distance

({\sf d}_{\sf X}\otimes{\sf d}_{\sf Y})^{2}\big((x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})\big):={\sf d}_{\sf X}^{2}(x_{1},x_{2})+{\sf d}_{\sf Y}^{2}(y_{1},y_{2}).

In the following we will denote by
\pi_{\sf X}\colon{\sf X}\times{\sf Y}\to{\sf X}
, the canonical projection on the first coordinate. Observe that this is a map of local bounded deformation, meaning that for every bounded set
B\subset{\sf X}\times{\sf Y}
there is a constant
C(B)>0
such that
\pi_{\sf X}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{B}$}
is
C(B)
-Lipschitz and
(\pi_{\sf X})_{*}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{B}$})\leq C(B)\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
: this in particular allows to construct a pullback module over
{\sf X}\times{\sf Y}
starting from a
L^{0}({\sf X})
-module
\mathscr{M}
over
{\sf X}
(see
[
16
, Section 3.1]
for the definition of such pullback). Given the particular structure of the projection map, a very explicit construction can be given to such pullback, as we briefly discuss now.

Let us consider a
L^{0}({\sf X})
-module
\mathscr{M}
over
{\sf X}
. We have on one side the pullback
([\pi_{\sf X}^{\ast}]\mathscr{M},[\pi_{\sf X}^{\ast}])
of
\mathscr{M}
through
\pi_{\sf X}
(see
[
16
, Theorem/Definition 3.2]
and notice that in particular
[\pi_{\sf X}^{\ast}]\mathscr{M}
is a
L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
-module) and on the other the module
L^{0}({\sf Y},\mathscr{M})
that we are going to define now.
L^{0}({\sf Y},\mathscr{M})
is the space of all the equivalence classes up to
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e. equality of strongly measurable (i.e., Borel and essentially separably valued) functions from
{\sf Y}
to
\mathscr{M}
. This space canonically carries the structure of a
L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
-module. Indeed:

-

the multiplication of an element of
L^{0}({\sf Y},\mathscr{M})
by a function
f\in L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
is defined as the map
{\sf Y}\ni y\mapsto f(\cdot,y)v(\cdot,y)\in\mathscr{M}
. Recalling that
L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})\sim L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}({\sf X}))
and approximating
f\in L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
with functions with finite range as maps from
{\sf Y}
to
L^{0}({\sf X})
, it is not hard to see that
y\mapsto f(\cdot,y)v(\cdot,y)
has essentially separable range, provided that
y\mapsto v(\cdot,y)
does;

-

the pointwise norm of
v\in L^{0}({\sf Y},\mathscr{M})
is obtained by composing the map
y\mapsto v(\cdot,y)\in\mathscr{M}
with the pointwise norm on
\mathscr{M}
. Again the isomorphism
L^{0}({\sf Y},L^{0}({\sf X}))\sim L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
ensures that the so-defined map takes values in
L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
.

It is then clear that this pointwise norm induces a complete distance on
L^{0}({\sf Y},\mathscr{M})
via the formula

{\sf d}_{L^{0}}(v,w):=\int 1\wedge|v-w|\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{n},

where
\mathfrak{n}\in\mathscr{P}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
has the same negligible sets of
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\times\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
(the topology induced by
{\sf d}_{L^{0}}
is independent on the choice of the particular
\mathfrak{n}
) and thus that, as claimed,
L^{0}({\sf Y},\mathscr{M})
is a
L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
-module.

By construction,
L^{0}({\sf Y},\mathscr{M})
is generated by constant maps and for any
v\in\mathscr{M}
the map
\hat{v}\in L^{0}({\sf Y},\mathscr{M})
constantly equal to
v
satisfies
|\hat{v}|=|v|\circ\pi_{\sf X}
. In other words, by the characterization of pullback of modules, the module
L^{0}({\sf Y},\mathscr{M})
and the map
v\mapsto\hat{v}
can be identified with
([\pi_{\sf X}^{\ast}]\mathscr{M},[\pi_{\sf X}^{\ast}])
, meaning that there is a unique isomorphism
\Phi:L^{0}({\sf Y},\mathscr{M})\to[\pi_{\sf X}^{\ast}]\mathscr{M}
such that
\Phi(\hat{v})=[\pi_{\sf X}^{\ast}]v
for any
v\in\mathscr{M}
.

In what will come next, we shall often implicitly use this identification in the case in which
\mathscr{M}=L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})
, namely
L^{0}({\sf Y},L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X}))\sim[\pi_{\sf X}^{*}]L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})
.

The fact that
\pi_{\sf X}
is of local bounded deformation ensures that it can be used to pullback 1-forms (see
[
12
]
and
[
16
, Section 3.1.2]
): for any
f\in{S}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}_{1})
we have that

f\circ\pi_{\sf X}\in{S}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})\,\,\,\text{with}\,\,\,\lvert{\mathrm{d}}(f\circ\pi_{\sf X})\rvert=\lvert{\mathrm{d}}f\rvert\circ\pi_{\sf X},\quad\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}\text{-a.e.},

(2.2.1)

and from this fact it follows that there exists a unique linear and continuous map
\pi_{\sf X}^{\ast}\colon L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{\ast}{\sf X})\to L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{\ast}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
with the property that

\begin{split}\pi_{\sf X}^{\ast}({\mathrm{d}}f)&={\mathrm{d}}(f\circ\pi_{\sf X}),\,\,\qquad\forall f\in{S}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}),\\
\pi_{\sf X}^{\ast}(g\omega)&=g\circ\pi_{\sf X}\,\pi_{\sf X}^{\ast}\omega,\,\,\quad\forall g\in L^{0}({\sf X}),\omega\in L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{\ast}{\sf X}),\\
\lvert\pi_{\sf X}^{\ast}\omega\rvert&=\lvert\omega\rvert\circ\pi_{\sf X},\,\,\qquad\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}\text{-a.e.},\,\forall\omega\in L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{\ast}{\sf X}).\end{split}

All these constructions can be repeated with the roles of
{\sf X}
and
{\sf Y}
inverted.

By the universal property of the pullback of modules it is easy to see that the map
\pi_{\sf X}^{*}
just described splits through the pullback map from
L^{0}(T{\sf X})
to
L^{0}({\sf Y},L^{0}(T{\sf X}))
and a module morphism
\Phi_{\sf X}:L^{0}({\sf Y},L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X}))\to L^{0}(T^{*}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
. More precisely we have the following proposition (see
[
16
, Proposition 3.7]
for the proof):

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X})
and
({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be two metric measure spaces. There exists a unique
L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
-linear and continuous map
\Phi_{\sf x}
from
L^{0}({\sf Y},L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
to
L^{0}(T^{*}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
such that

\Phi_{\sf x}(\widehat{{\mathrm{d}}g})={\mathrm{d}}(g\circ\pi_{\sf X})\qquad\forall g\in{S}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}),

where
\widehat{{\mathrm{d}}g}:{\sf Y}\to L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})
is the function identically equal to
{\mathrm{d}}g
. Such map preserves the pointwise norm.

Similarly, there is a unique
L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
-linear and continuous map
\Phi_{\sf y}:L^{0}({\sf X},L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf Y}))\to L^{0}(T^{*}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
such that

\Phi_{\sf y}(\widehat{{\mathrm{d}}h})={\mathrm{d}}(h\circ\pi_{\sf Y})\qquad\forall h\in{S}^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf Y}),

where
\widehat{{\mathrm{d}}h}:{\sf X}\to L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf Y})
is the function identically equal to
{\mathrm{d}}h
, and such map preserves the pointwise norm.

A way to think at the above is the following. Say that we have two smooth manifolds
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
and a map assigning to every
x_{2}\in M_{2}
a 1-form
\omega(x_{2})
on
M_{1}
. Then we might think at such map as the 1-form
\Omega
on
M_{1}\times M_{2}
which at the point
(x_{1},x_{2})
has value
(\omega(x_{2})(x_{1}),0)
. Here a way of thinking at
(\omega(x_{2})(x_{1}),0)
as element of the cotangent space at
(x_{1},x_{2})
is the following: say that
\omega(x_{2})(x_{1})={\mathrm{d}}_{x_{1}}f
for some smooth function
f\colon M_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
(
f

3.1
Partial first order derivatives for functions in two variables

Aim of this section is to review, in preparation of the subsequent results, the properties of those functions of two variables which are differentiable with respect one of them. We start with the following:

Definition 3.1

(The space
L^{0}({\sf Y},S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
and the operator
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}
)
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}),({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be two metric measure spaces. Then
L^{0}({\sf Y},S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))\subset L^{0}({\sf Y},L^{0}({\sf X}))\sim L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
is the space of functions
f\in L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
such that
f(\cdot,y)\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
, and the map
{\sf Y}\ni y\mapsto{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f\in L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})
belongs to
L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
.

We then define
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}:L^{0}({\sf Y},S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))\to L^{0}({\sf Y},L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
by
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f(y):={\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f(\cdot,y)
for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
.

We shall also denote by
L^{2}({\sf Y},S^{2}({\sf X}))\subset L^{0}({\sf Y},S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
the space of functions
f
such that
\int|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})<\infty
.

The definition above guarantees that
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f
is a well defined element of
L^{0}({\sf Y},L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
(i.e. that it is Borel and essentially separably valued).

It is worth noticing that if the space
W^{1,2}({\sf X})
is separable (as it is the case if it is Hilbert - recall
[
1
, Proposition 7.6]
), then elements of
L^{0}({\sf Y},S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
can be easily singled out:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}),({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be two metric measure spaces with
W^{1,2}({\sf X})
separable and
f\in L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
.

Then
f\in L^{0}({\sf Y},S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
if and only if for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
we have
f(\cdot,y)\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
.

Proof.
We start with the ‘only if’ and to this aim define
\eta^{m}:=1\wedge(m-{\sf d}_{\sf X}(x,\bar{x}))^{+}
where
\bar{x}\in{\sf X}
is a fixed point. Then it follows from our assumption that for every
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
we have that for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
the function
x\mapsto\eta^{m}(x)f^{n}(x,y)
, where
f^{n}:=n\wedge f\vee(-n)
, belongs to
W^{1,2}({\sf X})
. Fix
y\in{\sf Y}
for which this holds and let first
m\to\infty
and then
n\to\infty
to deduce that
x\mapsto f(x,y)
belongs to
S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
, giving the claim.

For the ‘if’ we notice that by the very definition of
L^{0}({\sf Y},S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
and up to replace
f
by
\eta^{m}f^{n}
with
\eta^{m},f^{n}
as above and letting first
m\to\infty
and then
n\to\infty
, it is sufficient to deal with the case
f\in L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
with
f(\cdot,y)\in W^{1,2}({\sf X})
for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
.

In this case to conclude it is sufficient to prove that
f\in L^{0}({\sf Y},W^{1,2}({\sf X}))
that is to say, as
W^{1,2}({\sf X})
is separable, that
y\mapsto f(\cdot,y)\in W^{1,2}({\sf X})
is (the equivalence class up to
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e. equality of) a Borel map. Since
y\mapsto f(\cdot,y)\in L^{2}({\sf X})
is Borel by (
2.2.1
) and Assumption
2.5
, to conclude it is sufficient to show that the Borel structure on
W^{1,2}({\sf X})
induced by the
W^{1,2}
-distance coincides with that induced by the
L^{2}
-distance. Since
\|f\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|f\|_{W^{1,2}}
it is clear that
L^{2}
-open sets are also
W^{1,2}
-open, whence the same is true for Borel ones. For the opposite inclusion we notice that since the
W^{1,2}
-norm is
L^{2}
-lower semicontinuous, we have that a
W^{1,2}
-closed ball is a
L^{2}
-closed set, thus a
W^{1,2}
-open ball is the union of a countable number of
L^{2}
-closed sets, hence
L^{2}
-Borel. The conclusion follows.

\square

Remark 3.3

(Partial differentiation as example of
D
-structure)
.

In
[
17
]
the authors present an axiomatic approach to the theory of Sobolev spaces over abstract metric measure spaces, introducing the notion of
D
-structure. Given a metric measure space
({\sf X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
and an exponent
p\in(1,\infty)
, a
D
-structure on
({\sf X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
is any map
D
associating to any function
u\in L^{p}_{\mathsf{loc}}(\mathfrak{m})
a family of non-negative Borel functions
D[u]
, called pseudo-gradients, such that a proper list of axioms is fulfilled (see
[
17
, Section 1.2]
). Intuitively, these pseudo-gradients provide a control from above on the variation of
u
, in a suitable sense.

This allows to define the space
W^{p}({\sf X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m},D)
as the set of all functions in
L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
admitting a pseudo-gradient in
L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
. Moreover, using standard techniques of functional analysis, to any Sobolev function
u\in W^{p}({\sf X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m},D)
it can be associated a uniquely determined minimal object
\underline{D}u\in D[u]\cap L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
, called minimal pseudo-gradient of
u
.

In
[
15
]
, the authors prove that, under suitable locality assumptions, these
D
-structures give rise to a first-oder differential structure, namely to a natural notion of cotangent module
L^{p}(T^{\ast}{\sf X};D)
, whose properties are analogous to the ones of the cotangent module
L^{2}(T^{\ast}{\sf X})
: the main result of such paper shows the existence of an abstract differential
{\mathrm{d}}\colon W^{1,p}({\sf X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m},D)\to L^{p}(T^{\ast}{\sf X};D)
, which is a linear operator such that for any
u\in W^{1,p}({\sf X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m},D)
the pointwise norm
\lvert{\mathrm{d}}u\rvert\in L^{p}(\mathfrak{m})
of
{\mathrm{d}}u
coincides with
\underline{D}u
in the
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. sense.

We point out that an example of
D
-structure satisfying all the locality conditions as investigated in
[
15
]
is the one that assigns to any
u\in L^{2}({\sf Y},W^{1,2}({\sf X}))\subset L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
the set
D[u]:=\{G\in L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}):G\geq|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}u|\ \mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\times\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}-a.e.\}
.

\blacksquare

The operator
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}
defined on
L^{0}({\sf Y},S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
trivially inherits all the calculus rules of
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}:S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})\to L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})
:

Proposition 3.4

(Calculus rules)
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}),({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be two metric measure spaces. Then the following hold:

i)

Closure
. Let
(f_{n})\subset L^{2}({\sf Y};S^{2}({\sf X}))
be
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e. converging to
f_{\infty}
. Assume also that for some open sets
\Omega^{k}\subset{\sf X}
(resp. Borel subsets
B^{k}\subset{\sf Y}
) with
\cup_{k}\Omega^{k}={\sf X}
(resp.
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}({\sf Y}\setminus\cup_{k}B^{k})=0
) and
\omega\in L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
we have that
({\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{\Omega^{k}}{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{B^{k}}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{n})
converges to
{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{\Omega^{k}}{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{B^{k}}\omega
in the weak topology of the Banach space
L^{2}({\sf Y},L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
(notice that this holds in particular if
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{n}\rightharpoonup\omega
in
L^{2}({\sf Y},L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
).

Then
f_{\infty}\in L^{2}({\sf Y};S^{2}({\sf X}))
and
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{\infty}=\omega
.

The same conclusion holds if
(f_{n})\subset L^{2}({\sf Y};W^{1,2}({\sf X}))
converges to
f_{\infty}
in the weak topology of
L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
.

ii)

Locality
. For any
f,g\in L^{0}({\sf Y},S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
we have

{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f={\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\qquad\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}-a.e.\ on\ \{f=g\}.

(3.1.1)

iii)

Chain rule
. Let
f\in L^{0}({\sf Y},S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
and
N\subset\mathbb{R}
Borel and
\mathcal{L}^{1}
-negligible. Then
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f=0

\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e. on
f^{-1}(N)
. Moreover, for
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
Lipschitz we have
\varphi\circ f\in L^{0}({\sf Y},S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
and

{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(\varphi\circ f)=\varphi^{\prime}\circ f{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,

(notice that by what just claimed, the actual definition of
\varphi^{\prime}\circ f
on the set
f^{-1}(\{\text{non differentiability points of }\varphi\})
is irrelevant because on such set
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f
is zero).

iv)

Leibniz rule
. Let
f,g\in L^{0}({\sf Y},S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))\cap L^{\infty}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
. Then
fg\in L^{0}({\sf Y},S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
as well with

{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(fg)=f{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g+g{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f.

If
g
only depends on the
y
variable, then the above holds without assuming that
f\in L^{\infty}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
.

Proof.
Locality, Chain rule and Leibniz rule follow directly from the definition of
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}
and the analogous properties of functions depending solely on the
x
variable. For the closure we notice that first using Mazur’s lemma, then passing to subsequences and finally with a diagonal argument we can assume that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have
{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{\Omega^{k}}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{n}(y)\to{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{\Omega^{k}}\omega(y)
in
L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X})
for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
. Thus the claim follows from the closure of the differential operator on functions depending solely on the
x
variable and on the fact that the result does not depend on the subsequence chosen. The second claim about the closure follows along similar lines by using Mazur’s lemma to reduce to strong convergence in
L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
and then passing to a subsequence to achieve
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e. convergence.

\square

The definitions given allow to reformulate Theorem
2.8
in the following way:

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X})
and
({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be two metric measure spaces satisfying Assumption
2.5
and let
f\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
.

Then
y\mapsto f(\cdot,y)
is in
L^{0}({\sf Y},S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
and, symmetrically,
x\mapsto f(x,\cdot)
is in
L^{0}({\sf X},S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf Y}))
, Moreover we have

{\mathrm{d}}f=\Phi_{\sf x}({\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f)+\Phi_{\sf y}({\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f).

(3.1.2)

Proof.

This is simply a restatement of Theorem
2.8
.

\square

In what follows we shall adopt the more compact (and more similar to the one adopted in the smooth setting and in (
2.2.3
)) notation

{\mathrm{d}}f=({\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f)

(3.1.3)

in place of (
3.1.2
): this should hopefully clarify that we are dealing with partial derivatives and that
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f
are the two components of
{\mathrm{d}}f
. Recalling the definition of
\Phi_{\sf x}
, this means that for
f\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
we have

{\mathrm{d}}(f\circ\pi_{\sf X})=({\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,0).

Similarly, if the spaces under consideration are also infinitesimally Hilbertian (as it often will be the case) then we shall adopt a similar notation for the gradients to the one in (
2.2.4
), so that
\nabla f=(\nabla_{\sf x}f,\nabla_{\sf y}f)
and, for
f\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
,
\nabla(f\circ\pi_{\sf X})=(\nabla_{\sf x}f,0)
.

In this direction it will be useful to notice that what just said and Proposition
2.11
allow to write

{\rm div}(h\nabla(g\circ\pi_{\sf X}))={\rm div}_{\sf x}(h\nabla_{\sf x}g)\qquad\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}-a.e.

(3.1.4)

for any
h\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{\sf bs}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
and
g\in D(\Delta_{\sf X})
.

We now show that under appropriate integrability assumptions for both the function and the differential, belonging to
L^{0}({\sf Y};S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
can be checked via integration by parts. Notice that this requires also that Assumption
2.5
holds and that
{\sf X}
is infinitesimally Hilbertian (this latter point can be slightly weakened, but we won’t push in this direction).

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}),({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be two metric measure spaces satisfying Assumption
2.5
and with
{\sf X}
being infinitesimally Hilbertian. Also, let
f\in L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
. Then the following are equivalent:

i)

f\in L^{2}({\sf Y},W^{1,2}({\sf X}))
,

ii)

There is
A\in L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
with
|A|\in L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
such that

-\int f{\rm div}(h\nabla(g\circ\pi_{\sf X}))\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})=\int h\left<A,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})

(3.1.5)

holds for any
h\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{\sf bs}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
and
g\in D(\Delta_{\sf X})
.

Moreover, if this is the case the choice
A={\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f
is the only one for which
(ii)
holds.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
From (
3.1.4
) and the definition of
{\rm div}_{\sf x}
we obtain

\begin{split}-\int f{\rm div}(h\nabla(g\circ\pi_{\sf X}))\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})&=-\int\Big(\int f(\cdot,y){\rm div}_{\sf x}(h(\cdot,y)\nabla_{\sf x}g)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\Big){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y)\\
&=\int\Big(\int h\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\Big){\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y},\end{split}

which proves
(ii)
with
A:={\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
(A^{n}_{i})
be a Borel partition of
{\sf Y}
made of at most countable sets, with
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A^{n}_{i})\in(0,\infty)
,
{\rm diam}(A^{n}_{i})\leq\frac{1}{n}
and so that
(A^{n+1}_{i})
is a refinement of
(A^{n}_{i})
. Put
f^{n}_{i}:=\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A^{n}_{i})^{-1}\int_{A^{n}_{i}}f(\cdot,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y)\in L^{2}({\sf X})
and
f^{n}(x,y):=\sum_{i}{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{A^{n}_{i}}(y)f^{n}_{i}(x)\in L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
. It is clear that
f^{n}\to f
in
L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
and thus the lower semicontinuity of the Cheeger-Dirichlet energy
{\sf E}_{\sf X}
on
{\sf X}
easily gives

\int{\sf E}_{\sf X}(f(\cdot,y))\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y)\leq\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\int{\sf E}_{\sf X}(f^{n}(\cdot,y))\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y).

(3.1.6)

For every
n,i
let
(h^{n}_{i,m})\subset\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{\sf bs}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
be a sequence of functions 1-Lipschitz in the
x
variable, with values in
[0,1]
and such that
(h^{n}_{i,m}),(|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}h^{n}_{i,m}|)
converge
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e. to
{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{{\sf X}\times A^{n}_{i}}
and
0
, respectively, as
m\to\infty
. Also, for
t>0
let us put
g^{n}_{i,t}:={{\sf h}}_{{\sf X},t}(f^{n}_{i})\in D(\Delta_{\sf X})
. Then an application of the dominate convergence theorem shows that for every
n,i,t
we have

\begin{split}-\int f{\rm div}_{\sf x}(h^{n}_{i,m}\nabla_{\sf x}(g^{n}_{i,t}))\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})&\quad\to\quad-\int_{{\sf X}\times A^{n}_{i}}f\Delta_{\sf X}g^{n}_{i,t}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\\
\int h^{n}_{i,m}\left<A,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g^{n}_{i,t}\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})&\quad\to\quad\int\left<A,{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{{\sf X}\times A^{n}_{i}}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g^{n}_{i}\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\end{split}

as
m\to\infty
, so that taking into account our assumption and the identity (
3.1.4
) we obtain

-\int_{{\sf X}\times A^{n}_{i}}f\Delta_{\sf X}g^{n}_{i,t}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})=\int\left<A,{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{{\sf X}\times A^{n}_{i}}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g^{n}_{i}\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).

(3.1.7)

Now observe that from the closure of the differential it is easy to justify the following computation:

\begin{split}-\int_{{\sf X}\times A^{n}_{i}}f\Delta_{\sf X}g^{n}_{i,t}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})&=-\int_{A^{n}_{i}}\int_{\sf X}f(\cdot,y)\Delta_{{\sf X}}{{\sf h}}_{{\sf X},t}(f^{n}_{i})\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y)\\
&=-\int_{A^{n}_{i}}\int_{\sf X}{{\sf h}}_{{\sf X},t/2}(f(\cdot,y))\Delta_{{\sf X}}{{\sf h}}_{{\sf X},t/2}(f^{n}_{i})\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y)\\
&=\int_{\sf X}\int_{A^{n}_{i}}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}{{\sf h}}_{{\sf X},t/2}(f(\cdot,y)),{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}{{\sf h}}_{{\sf X},t/2}(f^{n}_{i})\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\\
&=\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A^{n}_{i})\int_{\sf X}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}{{\sf h}}_{{\sf X},t/2}(f^{n}_{i})|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\\
&=2\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A^{n}_{i}){\sf E}_{\sf X}\big({{\sf h}}_{{\sf X},t/2}(f^{n}_{i})\big).\end{split}

On the other hand by Young’s inequality and the fact that
{\sf E}_{\sf X}
is decreasing along the heat flow we have

\begin{split}\int\left<A,{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{{\sf X}\times A^{n}_{i}}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g^{n}_{i}\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\sf X}\times A^{n}_{i}}|A|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})+\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A^{n}_{i}){\sf E}_{\sf X}(g^{n}_{i})\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\sf X}\times A^{n}_{i}}|A|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})+\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A^{n}_{i}){\sf E}_{\sf X}\big({{\sf h}}_{{\sf X},t/2}(f^{n}_{i})\big).\end{split}

Coupling these last two (in)equalities with (
3.1.7
) we deduce that

\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A^{n}_{i}){\sf E}_{\sf X}(f^{n}_{i})=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A^{n}_{i}){\sf E}_{\sf X}\big({{\sf h}}_{{\sf X},t/2}(f^{n}_{i})\big)\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\sf X}\times A^{n}_{i}}|A|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).

Summing over
i
and recalling (
3.1.6
) we conclude that

\int_{\sf Y}{\sf E}_{\sf X}(f(\cdot,y))\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y)\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\sf X}\times{\sf Y}}|A|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}),

showing that
f\in L^{2}({\sf Y},W^{1,2}({\sf X}))
(recall Proposition
3.2
), as desired.

To conclude that
A={\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f
is the only choice for which (
3.1.5
) holds, it is enough to show that the vector space generated by elements of the form
h{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g
with
h,g
as in the statement is dense in
L^{2}({\sf Y};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
. To see this, notice that arguing as we just did the closure of such space contains all the elements of the form
{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{B\times C}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g
for
B,C
Borel subsets of
{\sf X},{\sf Y}
respectively and
g\in W^{1,2}({\sf X})
. Then the conclusion follows from the fact that
L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X})
is generated by differentials of
W^{1,2}
-functions and the fact that piecewise constant maps from
{\sf Y}
to
L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X})
are dense in
L^{2}({\sf Y};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
.

\square

3.2
Sobolev maps with values in a Hilbert module

In this section we study the differential of a function on
{\sf X}
with values in a Hilbert module
\mathscr{H}
over
{\sf Y}
. The prototype case we want to cover is that of
\mathscr{H}:=L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf Y})
as we aim to give a meaning, in the non-smooth context, to the Sobolev regularity of
x\mapsto{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f
and to its differential
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f
.

For the purpose of the present discussion, a Hilbert module on
{\sf Y}
is a
L^{2}
-normed
L^{\infty}
-module on
{\sf Y}
. Given such a module
\mathscr{H}
and its dimensional decomposition
(E_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}}
(see
[
12
]
), a local Hilbert base
(e_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a collection of elements of the
L^{0}
-completion of
\mathscr{H}
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have
\left<e_{i},e_{j}\right>=\delta_{ij}
on
E_{n}
for every
i,j<n
.

Definition 3.7

(The space
S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
)
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}),({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be two metric measure spaces, with
{\sf X}
infinitesimally Hilbertian. Moreover, let
\mathscr{H}
be a separable Hilbert module over
{\sf Y}
, and
(e_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be a local Hilbert base of
\mathscr{H}
with
|e_{i}|\in L^{\infty}({\sf Y})
for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Then the space
S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})\subset L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})
is the space of functions
f
such that:

i)

the map
y\mapsto\left<f(\cdot),e_{i}\right>(y)
is in
L^{0}({\sf Y};S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
, for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
,

ii)

the function
|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f|\colon{\sf X}\times{\sf Y}\to[0,\infty]
defined by

|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f|^{2}:=\sum_{i}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(\Phi_{i}\circ f)|^{2}

(3.2.1)

belongs to
L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
(i.e. is such that
|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f|(\cdot,y)\in L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
), where
\Phi_{i}:\mathscr{H}\to L^{0}({\sf Y})
is given by
\Phi_{i}(v):=\left<v,e_{i}\right>
.

By
W^{1,2}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})\subset S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
we denote the space of functions
f\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
with
|f|,|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f|\in L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
.

Remark 3.8
.

Picking
\mathscr{H}:=L^{2}({\sf Y})
and comparing the above with Definition
3.1
we see that
S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf Y}))=L^{0}({\sf Y},S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
.

Then, taking into account that the infinitesimal Hilbertianity of
{\sf X}
yields the separability of
W^{1,2}({\sf X})
and Proposition
3.2
it is clear that
W^{1,2}({\sf X};L^{2}({\sf Y}))\sim L^{2}({\sf Y};W^{1,2}({\sf X}))
as both spaces are made of those functions
f\in L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
such that
f(\cdot,y)\in W^{1,2}({\sf X})
for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y
and so that
|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f|\in L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
.

\blacksquare

Remark 3.9
.

A priori, Definition
3.7
depends on the particular fixed local Hilbert base
(e_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
but we shall see in Proposition
3.10
below that this is not the case. Such independence is also based on the assumption that
{\sf X}
is infinitesimally Hilbertian, which is needed in order to ensure that
\lvert{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f\rvert
is well defined (i.e. that it does not depend on the particular base chosen). In fact, the proof of Proposition
3.10
below is based on the fact that we can make use of the parallelogram identity in the chain of equalities (
3.2.2
), and this is actually guaranteed by the fact that
{\sf X}
is infinitesimally Hilbertian.

\blacksquare

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}),({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be two metric measure spaces, with
{\sf X}
infinitesimally Hilbertian and let
\mathscr{H}
be a separable Hilbert module over
{\sf Y}
.

Then the space
S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
does not depend on the particular local base
(e_{i})
of
\mathscr{H}
chosen and for
f\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
the function
|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f|
defined by (
3.2.1
) also does not depend on the base.

Moreover the following holds:

i)

Locality.
For any
f\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
we have

|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f|=0\qquad\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}-a.e.\ on\ \{f=0\}.

ii)

Subadditivity.
For any
f,g\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
we have

|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(\alpha f+\beta g)|\leq|\alpha||{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f|+|\beta||{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g|\qquad\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}-a.e..

iii)

Lower semicontinuity.
Let
(f_{n})\subset\mathit{W}^{1,2}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})
be weakly
L^{2}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})
-converging to
f\in L^{2}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})
and such that
|\mathrm{D}_{\sf x}f_{n}|\rightharpoonup G
in the weak topology of
L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
for some
G
. Then
f\in\mathit{W}^{1,2}({{\sf X};\mathscr{H}})
and
|\mathrm{D}_{\sf x}f|\leq G

\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e..

Proof.
Let
\{e_{i}\}
be a local Hilbert base of
\mathscr{H}
such that
f\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
in the sense of the Definition
3.7
above, and let
\{\tilde{e}_{j}\}
be another local Hilbert base of
\mathscr{H}
. Put
f_{n}:=\sum_{i\leq n}e_{i}\Phi^{i}\circ f
so that
f_{n}\to f

\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
-a.e. as
n\to\infty
and, as direct consequence of the definitions,
f_{n}\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
.

We have
\langle f_{n},\tilde{e}_{j}\rangle=\sum_{i\leq n}\langle\tilde{e}_{j},e_{i}\rangle\Phi^{i}\circ f_{n}
, which is trivially an element in
L^{0}({\sf Y};S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
(notice that
\Phi^{i}\circ f_{n}\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
by assumption and
\langle\tilde{e}_{j},e_{i}\rangle\in L^{0}({\sf Y})
, hence both functions can be seen as elements of
L^{0}({\sf Y},S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
and then apply the Leibniz rule in Proposition
3.4
), and

\begin{split}\sum_{j}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\langle f_{n},\tilde{e}_{j}\rangle|^{2}&=\sum_{j}\sum_{i,k\leq n}\langle\tilde{e}_{j},e_{i}\rangle\langle\tilde{e}_{j},e_{k}\rangle\,\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(\Phi^{i}\circ f_{n}),{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(\Phi^{k}\circ f_{n})\right>\\
&=\sum_{i,k\leq n}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(\Phi^{i}\circ f_{n}),{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(\Phi^{k}\circ f_{n})\right>\underbrace{\sum_{j}\langle\tilde{e}_{j},e_{i}\rangle\langle\tilde{e}_{j},e_{k}\rangle}_{=\left<e_{i},e_{k}\right>=\delta_{ik}}=\sum_{i\leq n}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(\Phi^{i}\circ f_{n})|^{2},\end{split}

(3.2.2)

where the order of summation can be swapped because
i,k
run over a finite set. In particular we have

\sum_{j}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\langle f_{n},\tilde{e}_{j}\rangle|^{2}\leq\sum_{i}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(\Phi^{i}\circ f)|^{2}\quad\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}-a.e.\ \qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.2.3)

It follows that for every bounded Borel subset
B\subset{\sf X}
we have that for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
the sequence of functions
({\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{B}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\langle f_{n},\tilde{e}_{j}\rangle|(\cdot,y))
is bounded in
L^{2}({\sf X})
. Also, for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
the functions
(\langle f_{n}(\cdot),\tilde{e}_{j}\rangle(y))
converge to
\langle f(\cdot),\tilde{e}_{j}\rangle(y)

\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
-a.e., hence what just said and the lower semicontinuity of minimal weak upper gradients gives that
\langle f(\cdot),\tilde{e}_{j}\rangle(y)\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
with
|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\langle f,\tilde{e}_{j}\rangle|(\cdot,y)\lower 3.0pt\hbox{$|_{B}$}\leq G_{j}(\cdot,y)

\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
-a.e., where
G_{j}
is any weak
L^{2}
-limit of some subsequence of
({\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{B}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\langle f_{n},\tilde{e}_{j}\rangle|(\cdot,y))
. Then from Lemma
3.11
below, (
3.2.3
) and the arbitrariness of
B
we deduce that

\sum_{j}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\langle f,\tilde{e}_{j}\rangle|^{2}\leq\sum_{i}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(\Phi^{i}\circ f)|^{2}\quad\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}-a.e..

Swapping the roles of the bases
(e_{i})
and
(\tilde{e}_{j})
we conclude.

Finally, properties
(i),(ii),(iii)
are direct consequences of the definitions and the analogous properties for
L^{2}({\sf Y})
-valued functions (for
(iii)
we also use Lemma
3.11
below).

\square

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d},\mathfrak{m})
be a metric measure space and for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
let
(g^{i}_{n})\subset L^{2}({\sf X})
be a sequence of non-negative functions such that
\sqrt{\sum_{i}|g^{i}_{n}|^{2}}\rightharpoonup G
in
L^{2}({\sf X})
for some
G\geq 0
as
n\to\infty
. Also, let
g^{i}\in L^{2}({\sf X})
be non-negative and such that
g^{i}\leq G^{i}

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. for any weak
L^{2}
-limit
G^{i}
of
(g^{i}_{n})
. Then

\sqrt{\sum_{n}|g^{i}|^{2}}\leq G\qquad\mathfrak{m}-a.e..

Proof.
With a diagonalization argument, up to pass to a subsequence we can assume that
g^{i}_{n}\rightharpoonup G^{i}
for some
(G^{i})\subset L^{2}({\sf X})
. Now let
A\subset\ell_{2}
be countable and dense in the set of non-negative sequences of
\ell_{2}
-norm
\leq 1
and notice that

\sqrt{\sum_{i}|h_{i}|^{2}}=\sup_{(a_{i})\in A}\sum_{i}a_{i}h_{i}\quad\text{for any sequence of non-negative numbers $h_{i}$}.

(3.2.4)

For any
(a_{i})\in A
,
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. we have

\begin{split}\sum_{i}a_{i}g^{i}\leq\sum_{i}a_{i}G^{i}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}G^{i}=\lim_{N\to\infty}\lw_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}g^{i}_{n}\leq\lw_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i}a_{i}g^{i}_{n}.\end{split}

Now observe that since for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have that
\mathfrak{m}
-a.e. it holds
\sum_{i}a_{i}g^{i}_{n}\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i}|g^{i}_{n}|^{2}}
, the same relation is in place for the respective weak
L^{2}
-limits, thus from the above we get
\sum_{i}a_{i}g^{i}\leq G

\mathfrak{m}
-a.e.. Then the arbitrariness of
(a_{i})\in A
and (
3.2.4
) give the conclusion.

\square

To the Sobolev space
S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
we can canonically associate a differentiation operator:

Theorem 3.12

(Module-valued partial derivatives)
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}),({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be two metric measure spaces, with
{\sf X}
infinitesimally Hilbertian and let
\mathscr{H}
be a separable Hilbert module over
{\sf Y}
.

Then there exists a unique couple
(L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X};\mathscr{H}),{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x})
where
L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X};\mathscr{H})
is a
L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
-normed module,
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}:S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})\to L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X};\mathscr{H}^{0})
is linear and satisfies

i)

for any
f\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
the pointwise norm of
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f
coincides with
|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f|

\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.,

ii)

L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
-linear combinations of elements of the form
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f
for
f\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
are dense in
L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X};\mathscr{H})
.

Uniqueness here is intended up to unique isomorphism, i.e. if
(\tilde{\mathscr{M}},\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x})
is another such couple, then there is a unique isomorphism
\Phi:L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X};\mathscr{H})\to\tilde{\mathscr{M}}
such that
\Phi\circ{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}=\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}
.

An explicit example of couple as above is given by the module

L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X};\mathscr{H}):=L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X}))\otimes L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})

(3.2.5)

and the operator

S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})\ni f\quad\mapsto\quad{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f:=\sum_{i}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(\Phi_{i}\circ f)\otimes\hat{e}_{i},

(3.2.6)

where
(e_{i})\subset\mathscr{H}
is a local Hilbert base of
\mathscr{H}
,
\hat{e}_{i}\in L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})
is the function constantly equal to
e_{i}
and
\Phi_{i}:\mathscr{H}\to L^{0}({\sf Y})
is given by
\Phi_{i}(\cdot):=\left<\cdot,e_{i}\right>
, for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.
Uniqueness is a simple consequence of the definitions, see e.g. the arguments in
[
16
, Theorem/Definition 3.2]
and notice that they can be easily adapted - we omit the details. Existence also follows by mimicking the construction in
[
12
]
,
[
16
]
or, alternatively, by the explicit construction we provide below.

To check that the couple made by the module
L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X}))\otimes L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}^{0})
and the operator
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}
as defined by (
3.2.6
) satisfies the requirements, we must first check that
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}
is well defined, i.e. that the series in (
3.2.6
) converges in
L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X}))\otimes L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}^{0})
. To see this, notice that the addends are pointwise orthogonal and that
\{\hat{e}_{i}=0\}\subset\{\Phi^{i}\circ f=0\}
, thus for any
N,M\in\mathbb{N}
,
N<M
we have

\Big|\sum_{i=N}^{M}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(\Phi_{i}\circ f)\otimes\hat{e}_{i}\Big|^{2}=\sum_{i=N}^{M}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(\Phi_{i}\circ f)|^{2},

(3.2.7)

and since for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
and bounded Borel set
B\subset{\sf X}
the series
\sum_{i}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(\Phi_{i}\circ f)|^{2}(\cdot,y){\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{B}
is convergent in
L^{2}({\sf X})
, this is sufficient to conclude that the series in (
3.2.6
) converges in
L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X};L^{0}({\sf Y}))\otimes L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}^{0})
.

Then the fact that
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}
is linear is obvious from the definition, while the fact that
(i)
holds follows from (
3.2.7
) above.

To check
(ii)
, notice that for any
f\in L^{0}({\sf Y};S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the function
F:=fe_{i}\colon{\sf X}\to\mathscr{H}
, defined by
F(x)=f(x,\cdot)e_{i}
for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
-a.e.
x\in{\sf X}
, satisfies
\Phi^{j}\circ F=0
for
j\neq i
and
\Phi^{i}\circ F=f{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{{\sf X}\times\{e_{i}\neq 0\}}
. In particular, directly from Definition
3.1
we have that
\Phi^{i}\circ F\in L^{0}({\sf Y};S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
with
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(\Phi^{i}\circ F)={\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{{\sf X}\times\{e_{i}\neq 0\}}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f
. It is then clear, by the very Definition
3.7
, that
F\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
with

{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}F=({\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{{\sf X}\times\{e_{i}\neq 0\}}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f)\otimes\hat{e}_{i}={\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f\otimes({\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{{\sf X}\times\{e_{i}\neq 0\}}\hat{e}_{i})={\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f\otimes\hat{e}_{i}.

The conclusion follows from the fact that differentials of functions in
L^{0}({\sf Y};S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
generate
L^{0}({\sf Y};T^{*}{\sf X}))
(trivial consequence of the fact that differentials of functions on
S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
generate
L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})
) and the family
(\hat{e}_{i})
generates
L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})
.

\square

The explicit representation as in (
3.2.6
) allows some manipulation of the object
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f
. For instance, if
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
is another Hilbert module over
{\sf Y}
and
T\colon L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})\to L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}^{\prime})
is a module morphism, then
T
induces a map, still denoted by
T
(with a slight abuse of notation, as perhaps
{\rm Id}\otimes T
would be the canonical choice), from
L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X};L^{0}({\sf Y}))\otimes L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})
to
L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X};L^{0}({\sf Y}))\otimes L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}^{\prime})
by acting on the ‘second factors’. More precisely, the map

L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X};L^{0}({\sf Y}))\otimes L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})\ni\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\otimes v_{i}\quad\mapsto\quad\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\otimes(Tv_{i})\in L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X};L^{0}({\sf Y}))\otimes L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}^{\prime})

which is trivially well defined by the
L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
-linearity of
T
, can be shown (see below) to be continuous, and thus can be uniquely extended to a continuous map from
L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X};L^{0}({\sf Y}))\otimes L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})
to
L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X};L^{0}({\sf Y}))\otimes L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}^{\prime})
. To check the continuity one possibility is to notice that we can always rewrite a finite sum such as
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\otimes v_{i}
so that the
\omega_{i}
’s are pointwise orthogonal and once this is done we have

\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\otimes(Tv_{i})\Big|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\omega_{i}|^{2}|Tv_{i}|^{2}\leq|T|^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\omega_{i}|^{2}|v_{i}|^{2}=|T|^{2}\,\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{i}\otimes v_{i}\Big|^{2}.

Thus for
T
as above it makes sense to speak about
T({\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f)
for
f\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
.

We shall mostly apply this construction in two cases: either when - as in formula (
3.2.9
) -
T:\mathscr{H}\to\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
is a module morphism which induces by post-composition a module morphism from
L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})
to
L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}^{\prime})
, or when - as in formula (
3.2.8
) - a given element
w\in L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})
is considered and
T:L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})\to L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
is given by
T(v):=\left<v,w\right>
.

We now collect some properties of the newly defined differentiation operator:

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}),({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be two metric measure spaces, with
{\sf X}
infinitesimally Hilbertian and let
\mathscr{H}
be a separable Hilbert module over
{\sf Y}
. Then:

i)

Closure.
Let
(f_{n})\subset S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}}))
be
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
-a.e. converging to some
f\in L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})
. Assume also that for some open sets
\Omega^{k}\subset{\sf X}
(resp. Borel subsets
B^{k}\subset{\sf Y}
) with
\cup_{k}\Omega^{k}={\sf X}
(resp.
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}({\sf Y}\setminus\cup_{k}B^{k})=0
) and
\omega\in L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X};\mathscr{H})
we have that
({\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{\Omega^{k}}{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{B^{k}}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{n})
converges to
{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{\Omega^{k}}{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{B^{k}}\omega
in the weak topology of
L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X};\mathscr{H})
(this happens or instance if
({\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{n}\rightharpoonup\omega)
in
L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X};\mathscr{H})
).

Then
f\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}}))
with
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f=\omega
.

ii)

Locality.
For any
f,g\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
we have

{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f={\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\qquad\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}-a.e.\ on\ \{f=g\}.

iii)

Leibniz rule.
For any
f,g\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
with
|f|,|g|\in L^{\infty}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
we have
\left<f,g\right>\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};L^{0}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf Y}))
with

{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\left<f,g\right>=\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,g\right>+\left<f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\right>.

(3.2.8)

iv)

Chain rule.
Let
\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
be another Hilbert module over
{\sf Y}
,
T:\mathscr{H}\to\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
a module morphism with
|\mathit{T}|\in L^{\infty}({\sf Y})
and
f\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
. Then
T\circ f\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{loc}})
and

{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(T\circ f)=T({\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f).

(3.2.9)

Proof.
The closure property
(i)
is a direct consequence of the the very definition (
3.2.6
) and of the closure of the differential of functions in
L^{0}({\sf Y};S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}))
established in point
(i)
of Proposition
3.4
. Similarly, point
(ii)
is a direct consequence of the analogous property (
3.1.1
) and Definition (
3.2.6
).

For what concerns
(iii)
, let
(e_{i})
be a local Hilbert base of
\mathscr{H}
, write
f_{i}:=\Phi^{i}\circ f
,
g_{i}:=\Phi^{i}\circ g
for brevity and notice that
\left<f,g\right>=\sum_{i}f_{i}g_{i}
with the series converging
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.. Then observe that from the Leibniz rule proved in point
(iv)
of Proposition
3.4
we have that

{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}g_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}g_{i}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{i},

thus by the closure of the differential the conclusion will follow if we prove that for any
\Omega\subset{\sf X}
open and bounded there is a sequence
k\mapsto B^{k}\subset{\sf Y}
of Borel sets with
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}({\sf Y}\setminus\cup_{k}B^{k})=0
such that
{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{\Omega}{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{B^{k}}(\sum_{i=1}^{N}g_{i}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{i}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{i})\to{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{\Omega}{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{B^{k}}(\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,g\right>+\left<f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\right>)
in
L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X};L^{2}({\sf Y}))
as
N\to\infty
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We pick
B^{k}:=\{y\in{\sf Y}\ :\ \int_{\Omega}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f|^{2}(\cdot,y)+|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g|^{2}(\cdot,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}<k\}\cap B_{k}(\bar{y})
for some fixed
\bar{y}\in{\sf Y}
, and notice that the assumption
f,g\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}}))
gives
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}({\sf Y}\setminus\cup_{k}B^{k})=0
. Then we observe that

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega\times B^{k}}\Big|\sum_{i=N}^{M}g_{i}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{i}\Big|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{X}\times\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})&\leq\int_{\Omega\times B^{k}}{\sum_{i=N}^{M}|g_{i}|^{2}}{\sum_{i=N}^{M}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{i}|^{2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{X}\times\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\\
&\leq\||g|\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times B^{k})}\int_{\Omega\times B^{k}}{\sum_{i\geq N}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{i}|^{2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{X}\times\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\end{split}

and that by construction it holds

\int_{\Omega\times B^{k}}{\sum_{i\geq 0}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{i}|^{2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{X}\times\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})=\int_{\Omega\times B^{k}}{\sum_{i\geq 0}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{i}|^{2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{X}\times\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\leq k\,\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}(\Omega)\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(B_{k}(\bar{y}))<\infty.

These show that
{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{\Omega}{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{B^{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}g_{i}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{i}\to{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{\Omega}{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{B^{k}}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}g_{i}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{i}
in
L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X};L^{2}({\sf Y}))
as
N\to\infty
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, so that the conclusion follows noticing that
\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,g\right>=\sum_{i}g_{i}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{i}
(from
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f=\sum_{i}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{i}\otimes\hat{e}_{i}
and
g=\sum_{i}g_{i}\hat{e}_{i}
) and arguing similarly for the other addend.

We pass to
(iv)
and start claiming that for
f\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};L^{0}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf Y}))
and
v\in\mathscr{H}
with
|v|\in L^{\infty}({\sf Y})
, the function
{\sf X}\ni x\mapsto f(x,\cdot)v\in\mathscr{H}
belongs to
S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}}))
with
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(fv)={\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f\otimes v
. To see this, notice that we can write
v=|v|e_{1}
, where
e_{1}\in\mathscr{H}
is the first element of some local Hilbert base, use the fact that
f|v|\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};L^{0}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf Y}))
with
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(f|v|)=|v|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f
(point
(iii)
of Proposition
3.4
) and conclude recalling the very definitions of
S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}_{\mathsf{loc}})
and of
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}
given in (
3.2.6
).

Now denote by
(e_{i}),(e^{\prime}_{j})
local Hilbert basis of
\mathscr{H},\mathscr{H}^{\prime}
respectively and put for brevity
f_{i}:=\Phi^{i}\circ f\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};L^{0}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf Y}))
and
a_{ij}:=\langle Te_{i},e^{\prime}_{j}\rangle\in L^{\infty}({\sf Y})
. Then what we just proved ensures that for any
i,j
we have
f_{i}a_{ij}e^{\prime}_{j}\in S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{loc}})
with
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(f_{i}a_{i,j}e^{\prime}_{j})={\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{i}\otimes(a_{ij}e^{\prime}_{j})
. Adding up in
j
and using the closure of the differential proved in point
(i)
above (notice that
\sum_{j}|a_{ij}|^{2}=|e_{i}|^{2}\in L^{\infty}({\sf Y})
) we deduce that
T(f_{i}e_{i})=f_{i}T(e_{i})=\sum_{j}f_{i}a_{ij}e^{\prime}_{j}
belongs to
S^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X};\mathscr{H}^{\prime}_{\mathsf{loc}})
with

{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\big(T(f_{i}e_{i})\big)=\sum_{j}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{i}\otimes(a_{ij}e^{\prime}_{j})={\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{i}\otimes\Big(\sum_{j}a_{ij}e^{\prime}_{j}\Big)={\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{i}\otimes T(e_{i})=T\big({\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f_{i}\otimes e_{i}\big),

where the last identity comes from the very definition of the action of
T
on
L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X};\mathscr{H})=L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X};L^{0}({\sf Y}))\otimes L^{0}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})
. The conclusion (
3.2.9
) now follows adding up in
i
and using again the closure of the differential.

\square

We conclude the section with a statement similar to Proposition
3.6
which allows to check whether
f
belongs to
W^{1,2}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})
via integration by parts:

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}),({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be two metric measure spaces satisfying Assumption
2.5
with
{\sf X}
being infinitesimally Hilbertian,
\mathscr{H}
an Hilbert module over
{\sf Y}
and
f\in L^{2}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})
. Then the following are equivalent:

i)

f\in W^{1,2}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})
,

ii)

There is
A\in L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X};\mathscr{H})
with
|A|_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}\in L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
and a generating set
D\subset\mathscr{H}
made of bounded elements such that

-\int\left<f,v\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla(g\circ\pi_{\sf X}))\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})=\int h\left<A,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\otimes v\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})

(3.2.10)

holds for any
v\in D
,
h\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{\sf bs}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
and
g\in D(\Delta_{\sf X})
.

Moreover, if this is the case the choice
A={\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f
is the only one for which
(ii)
holds and (
3.2.10
) holds for every bounded element
v\in\mathscr{H}
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
Let
v\in\mathscr{H}
be with
|v|\in L^{\infty}
and
T:\mathscr{H}\to L^{0}({\sf Y})
be given by
T(\cdot):=\left<\cdot,v\right>
. Then point
(iv)
in Proposition
3.13
gives that
\left<f,v\right>\in W^{1,2}({\sf X};L^{2}({\sf Y}))
with
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}(\left<f,v\right>)=\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,v\right>\in L^{2}({\sf Y};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
. Thus we can apply (
3.1.5
) of Proposition
3.6
to
\left<f,v\right>
with
g,h
as in the statement to get

\begin{split}-\int\left<f,v\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla(g\circ\pi_{\sf X}))\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})&=\int h\langle\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,v\right>,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\\
&=\int h\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\otimes v\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}),\end{split}

which is the claim with
A={\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
Applying Proposition
3.6
to
\left<f,v\right>
for
v\in D
we obtain that
\left<f,v\right>\in W^{1,2}({\sf X};L^{2}({\sf Y}))
with
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\left<f,v\right>=\left<A,v\right>
(this expression meaning that
\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\left<f,v\right>,\omega\right>=\left<A,\omega\otimes v\right>
for any
\omega\in L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
). By the linearity of the differential and taking also into account the second claim in the Leibniz rule in point
(iv)
of Proposition
3.4
we deduce that for
v_{i}\in D
and
a_{i}\in L^{\infty}({\sf Y})
we have
\left<f,\sum_{i}a_{i}v_{i}\right>\in W^{1,2}({\sf X};L^{2}({\sf Y}))
with

{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\big\langle f,\big(\sum_{i}a_{i}v_{i}\big)\big\rangle=\sum_{i}a_{i}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\langle f,v_{i}\rangle=\sum_{i}a_{i}\left<A,v_{i}\right>=\langle A,\sum_{i}a_{i}v_{i}\rangle,

where the sums here are finite.
From the fact that
D
generates
\mathscr{H}
it is easy to deduce that for any
h\in\mathscr{H}
with
|h|\in L^{\infty}({\sf Y})
there exists a sequence of
L^{\infty}
-linear combinations of elements of
D
whose pointwise norm is uniformly bounded and
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e. converging to
h
. Hence from what we proved above and the closure of the differential we conclude that for any
h\in\mathscr{H}
with
|h|\in L^{\infty}
we have

\left<f,h\right>\in W^{1,2}({\sf X};L^{2}({\sf Y}))\quad\text{ with }\quad{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\left<f,h\right>=\langle A,h\rangle.

(3.2.11)

In particular this holds for
h=e_{i}
with
(e_{i})
a local Hilbert base of
\mathscr{H}
. Hence from the very definition of
W^{1,2}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})
, the identity
\sum_{i}|\left<A,e_{i}\right>|^{2}=|A|_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}^{2}
and the assumption
|A|_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}\in L^{2}({\sf X},\times{\sf Y})
we conclude that
f\in W^{1,2}({\sf X};\mathscr{H})
.

To obtain that
A={\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f
notice that from (
3.2.6
) we have
\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,e_{i}\right>={\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\left<f,e_{i}\right>
and recall (
3.2.11
) to deduce that
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\left<f,h_{i}\right>=\left<A,e_{i}\right>
for any
i
. The claim follows.

\square

3.3
Schwarz’s theorem on symmetry of mixed second derivatives

Let
\mathscr{H}^{1},\mathscr{H}^{2}
be two Hilbert modules over the same space (in our case that would be
{\sf X}\times{\sf Y}
). Then there is a natural
transposition
map
A\mapsto A^{\rm tr}
from
\mathscr{H}^{1}\otimes\mathscr{H}^{2}
to
\mathscr{H}^{2}\otimes\mathscr{H}^{1}
, defined as the only
L^{\infty}
-linear and continuous extension of the map sending
v_{1}\otimes v_{2}
to
v_{2}\otimes v_{1}
for any
v_{i}\in\mathscr{H}^{i}
,
i=1,2
. It is trivial to check that the transposition is well defined by this as well as the fact that transposing twice returns the identity.

Now consider infinitesimally Hilbertian spaces
{\sf X},{\sf Y}
and a function
f\in L^{2}({\sf Y};W^{1,2}({\sf X}))
. Then in principle we have
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f\in L^{2}({\sf Y};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
. Now assume that in fact we have more regularity and that in fact it holds
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f\in W^{1,2}({\sf Y};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
. Then recalling (
3.2.5
) we see that the differential
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f
is in

L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf Y};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X}))\subset L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf Y};L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X}))\cong L^{0}({\sf X};L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf Y}))\otimes L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})).

(3.3.1)

In these circumstances and assuming also that
f\in L^{2}({\sf X};W^{1,2}({\sf Y}))
(see Example
3.16
below), it is natural to wonder if we also have
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f\in W^{1,2}({\sf X};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf Y}))
and whether mixed derivatives commute. Inspecting the spaces where
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f
and
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f
belong, we see that the correct way to phrase this question is to ask whether

{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f=({\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f)^{\rm tr}.

(3.3.2)

holds, where here the transposition is acting on
L^{0}({\sf X};L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf Y}))\otimes L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
(recall (
3.3.1
)).

In the following theorem we prove that this is actually the case under natural assumptions. Before stating it let us remark that for
{\sf X},{\sf Y}
infinitesimally Hilbertian we have

W^{1,2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})=W^{1,2}({\sf X};L^{2}({\sf Y}))\cap W^{1,2}({\sf Y};L^{2}({\sf X})),

as can be seen from Proposition
3.5
together with the equivalence
W^{1,2}({\sf X};L^{2}({\sf Y}))\sim L^{2}({\sf Y};W^{1,2}({\sf X}))
pointed out in Remark
3.8
. In particular, for
f\in W^{1,2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
both
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f
and
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f
are defined and these objects belong to
L^{2}({\sf Y};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
and
L^{2}({\sf X};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf Y}))
respectively.

In the course of the forthcoming proof and also in subsequent part of the paper, given
f_{1}\in L^{0}({\sf X})
and
f_{2}\in L^{0}({\sf Y})
we shall denote by
f_{1}\otimes f_{2}\in L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
the function given by

f_{1}\otimes f_{2}(x,y):=f_{1}(x)f_{2}(y).

We then have:

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}),({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be two infinitesimally Hilbertian metric measure spaces satisfying Assumption
2.5
. Let
f\in W^{1,2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
and assume that
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f\in W^{1,2}({\sf Y};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
.

Then
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f\in W^{1,2}({\sf X};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf Y}))
as well and (
3.3.2
) holds.

Proof.

Let
g_{1}\in D(\Delta_{\sf x})\cap\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits({\sf X})
(resp.
g_{2}\in D(\Delta_{\sf y})\cap\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits({\sf Y})
) and
h=h_{1}\otimes h_{2}\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{\sf bs}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
for
h_{1}\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{\sf bs}({\sf X})
and
h_{2}\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{\sf bs}({\sf Y})
. Then from Proposition
3.14
applied with the roles of
{\sf X},{\sf Y}
swapped and
\mathscr{H}:=L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X})
together with the assumption
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f\in W^{1,2}({\sf Y};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
we conclude that

-\int\langle{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\rangle\,{\rm div}(h\nabla(g_{2}\circ\pi_{\sf Y}))\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})=\int h\langle{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\otimes{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).

(3.3.3)

Recalling (
3.1.4
) we get

{\rm div}(h\nabla(g_{2}\circ\pi_{\sf Y}))={\rm div}_{\sf y}(h\nabla_{\sf y}g_{2})=h\Delta_{\sf y}g_{2}+\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}h,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\right>=h\Delta_{\sf y}g_{2}+h_{1}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}h_{2},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\right>,

where in the last step we used the identity
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}h=h_{1}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}h_{2}
which follows directly from Definition
3.1
. It is clear that the rightmost side in the above belongs to
L^{2}({\sf Y};W^{1,2}({\sf X}))
with differential
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}
given by
h_{2}\Delta_{\sf y}g_{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}h_{1}+\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}h_{2},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\right>{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}h_{1}
, thus we have

\begin{split}-\int\langle{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\rangle&\,{\rm div}(h\nabla(g_{2}\circ\pi_{\sf Y}))\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\\
&=-\int\int\langle{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\rangle\,\big(h\Delta_{\sf y}g_{2}+h_{1}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}h_{2},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\right>\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}\\
&=\int\int f\big(h\Delta_{\sf x}g_{1}\Delta_{\sf y}g_{2}+h_{1}\Delta_{\sf x}g_{1}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}h_{2},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\right>\\
&\qquad\qquad+h_{2}\Delta_{\sf y}g_{2}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}h_{1}\right>+\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}h_{2},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\right>\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}h_{1}\right>\big)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}\\
&=\cdots\text{(by the symmetry of the last expression)}\\
&=-\int\langle{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\rangle\,{\rm div}(h\nabla(g_{1}\circ\pi_{\sf X}))\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).\end{split}

Hence from (
3.3.3
) we get

-\int\langle{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\rangle\,{\rm div}(h\nabla(g_{1}\circ\pi_{\sf X}))\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})=\int h\langle{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\otimes{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})

and the claim follows by the arbitrariness of
g_{1},g_{2},h
, by applying Proposition
3.14
again (with
D:=\{\nabla_{\sf y}g_{2}:g_{2}\in D(\Delta_{\sf y})\cap\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits({\sf Y})\}
).

\square

Example 3.16
.

Let
{\sf X}={\sf Y}=[0,1]
with the Euclidean distance and measure and consider the function
f:={\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{[0,1]\times[0,1/2]}\in L^{2}([0,1]\times[0,1])
. It trivially belongs to
L^{2}({\sf Y};W^{1,2}({\sf X}))
with
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f=0
, thus evidently
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f\in W^{1,2}({\sf Y};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
with
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f=0
. Yet, obviously
f\notin L^{2}({\sf X};W^{1,2}({\sf Y}))
, showing that the assumption
f\in L^{2}({\sf X};W^{1,2}({\sf Y}))
is necessary in Theorem
3.15
.

\blacksquare

4
Second order partial derivatives on the product space

4.1
Basic material

4.1.1
Reminders about calculus on
{\sf RCD}
spaces

Here we briefly recall those notions about calculus on
{\sf RCD}
spaces that will be used in the rest of the manuscript. These will be used throughout the text without further notice.

From now on we fix a metric measure space
({\sf X},\mathsf{d},\mathfrak{m})
, satisfying the
{\sf RCD}(K,\infty)
condition for some
K\in\mathbb{R}
, meaning that it is an infinitesimally Hilbertian metric measure space satisfying the
{\sf CD}(K,\infty)
condition of Lott-Villani
[
18
]
and Sturm
[
21
]
. This in particular means that the energy functional introduced in (
2.1.1
) is a quadratic form: we call
heat flow

({{\sf h}}_{t})
its gradient flow in
\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{m})
, which is linear and self-adjoint. It is useful to recall the following standard a priori estimates:

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}({{\sf h}}_{t}f)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4t}\|f\|^{2}_{\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{m})},

(4.1.1)

\displaystyle\|\Delta{{\sf h}}_{t}f\|^{2}_{\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{m})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2t^{2}}\|f\|^{2}_{\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{m})},

(4.1.2)

valid for every
t>0
and every
f\in\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{m})
. Moreover, it holds

\|{{\sf h}}_{t}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{m})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathfrak{m})},\qquad\forall t\geq 0,\,\forall f\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{m}).

(4.1.3)

Finally, a crucial property of the heat flow which is strongly related to the lower curvature bound is the
Bakry-Émery contraction estimate
(see
[
14
]
and
[
4
]
):

\lvert{\mathrm{d}}{{\sf h}}_{t}f\rvert^{2}\leq e^{-2Kt}{{\sf h}}_{t}(\lvert{\mathrm{d}}f\rvert^{2}),\qquad\mathfrak{m}\text{-a.e.},\quad\forall t\geq 0,\quad\forall f\in\mathit{W}^{1,2}({\sf X})

(4.1.4)

from which it follows the
L^{\infty}-\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits
regularization estimate

\||{\mathrm{d}}{{\sf h}}_{t}f|\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2I_{2K}(t)}}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}},\qquad\forall f\in L^{2}(\mathfrak{m}),\ t>0,

(4.1.5)

where
I_{K}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}e^{Ks}\,{\mathrm{d}}s
.

Then the space of
test functions

{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
on
{\sf X}
is defined as

{\rm TestF}({\sf X}):=\Big\{f\in L^{\infty}\cap W^{1,2}({\sf X})\cap D(\Delta)\ :\ |{\mathrm{d}}f|\in L^{\infty}({\sf X}),\ \Delta f\in W^{1,2}({\sf X})\Big\}.

It turns out that
{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
is an algebra dense in
W^{1,2}({\sf X})
(in particular gradients of test functions generate the whole
L^{2}(T{\sf X})
) and that
|{\mathrm{d}}f|^{2}\in W^{1,2}({\sf X})
for any
f\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
(see
[
19
]
). Another direct consequence of the Ricci curvature lower bound is the following regularity result, which allows to pass from a Sobolev information to a metric one (see
[
4
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
):

\text{any}\,f\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})\,\text{ has a Lipschitz representative }\,\bar{f}\colon{\sf X}\to\mathbb{R}\,\text{ with }\,\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(\bar{f})\leq\|\lvert\nabla f\rvert\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})}.

(4.1.6)

Moreover, it is also useful to know that

f\in L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}(\mathfrak{m})\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad{{\sf h}}_{t}f\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}),\ \forall t>0,

(4.1.7)

which in particular guarantees the density of
{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
in
W^{1,2}({\sf X})
, and that

\forall B\subset{\sf X}\text{ bounded there is $f\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})$ with bounded support identically 1 on $B$.}

(4.1.8)

The space
W^{2,2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
is the space of
f\in W^{1,2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
for which there exists
A\in L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}((T^{*})^{\otimes 2}{\sf X})
such that

\begin{split}2\int&hA(\nabla g,\nabla\tilde{g})\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\\
&=-\int\left<\nabla f,\nabla g\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla\tilde{g})+\left<\nabla f,\nabla\tilde{g}\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla g)+h\left<\nabla f,\nabla(\left<\nabla g,\nabla\tilde{g}\right>)\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}\end{split}

(4.1.9)

for every
g,\tilde{g},h\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
with bounded support. In this case we call
A
the Hessian of
f
and denote it by
{\rm Hess}f
. If
f\in\mathit{W}^{1,2}({\sf X})
and
{\rm Hess}f\in L^{2}((T^{*})^{\otimes 2}{\sf X})
we say that
f\in W^{2,2}({\sf X})
(it is not hard to check that this definition coincides with the one proposed in
[
12
]
). The space
W^{2,2}({\sf X})
is a separable Hilbert space when endowed the norm

\|f\|_{W^{2,2}({\sf X})}^{2}:=\|f\|_{L^{2}({\sf X})}^{2}+\||\mathrm{D}f|\|_{L^{2}({\sf X})}^{2}+\||{\rm Hess}f|_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}\|_{L^{2}({\sf X})}^{2}.

We have
D(\Delta)\subset W^{2,2}({\sf X})
with

\int|{\rm Hess}(f)|_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\leq\int|\Delta f|^{2}-K|{\mathrm{d}}f|^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\qquad\forall f\in D(\Delta).

(4.1.10)

The space
H^{2,2}({\sf X})
is the
W^{2,2}({\sf X})
-closure of
D(\Delta)
(or, equivalently, of
{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
) and, similarly,
H^{2,2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
as the
W^{2,2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
-closure of
{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
, i.e.:
f\in H^{2,2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})\subset W^{2,2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X})
provided there exists a sequence
(f_{n})\subset{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
such that
f_{n},{\mathrm{d}}f_{n},{\rm Hess}{f_{n}}
converge to
f,{\mathrm{d}}f,{\rm Hess}f
in
L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}({\sf X}),L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}(T^{*}{\sf X}),L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}((T^{*})^{\otimes 2}{\sf X})
respectively.

We shall also often use the identity

2{\rm Hess}f(\nabla g,\nabla h)={\mathrm{d}}\left<\left<{\mathrm{d}}f,{\mathrm{d}}g\right>,{\mathrm{d}}h\right>+{\mathrm{d}}\left<\left<{\mathrm{d}}f,{\mathrm{d}}h\right>,{\mathrm{d}}g\right>-{\mathrm{d}}\left<{\mathrm{d}}f,\left<{\mathrm{d}}g,{\mathrm{d}}h\right>\right>

valid for any
f,g,h\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
.

The space of Sobolev vector fields
W^{1,2}_{C,\mathsf{loc}}(T{\sf X})
is defined as the space of
v\in L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}(T{\sf X})
for which there is
T\in L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}(T^{\otimes 2}{\sf X})
such that

\int hT:(\nabla g\otimes\nabla\tilde{g})\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}=\int-\left<v,\nabla\tilde{g}\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla g)+h{\rm Hess}{\tilde{g}}(X,\nabla g)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}

for every
h,g,\tilde{g}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
with bounded support. In this case we call
T
the
covariant derivative
of
v
and denote it by
\nabla_{C}v
. If
|v|,|\nabla_{C}v|_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}\in L^{2}({\sf X})
we shall say that
v\in W^{1,2}_{C}(T{\sf X})
; this definition of
W^{1,2}_{C}(T{\sf X})
coincides with the one given in
[
12
]
. Vector fields of the form
g\nabla f
for
f,g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
are in
W^{1,2}_{C}(T{\sf X})
: we denote by
{\rm{Test}}V({\sf X})
the linear span of such vector fields, and by
H^{1,2}_{C}(T{\sf X})
its
W^{1,2}_{C}
-closure . The space
H^{1,2}_{C,\mathsf{loc}}(T{\sf X})
is then equivalently defined either as the subspace of
L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}(T{\sf X})
made of vectors
v
of such that
fv\in H^{1,2}_{C}(T{\sf X})
for every
f\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
with bounded support or as the
W^{1,2}_{C,\mathsf{loc}}
-closure of
H^{1,2}_{C}(T{\sf X})
, i.e. as the space of vector fields
v\in W^{1,2}_{C,\mathsf{loc}}(T{\sf X})
such that there is
(v_{n})\subset H^{1,2}_{C}(T{\sf X})
such that
v_{n}\to v
and
\nabla_{C}v_{n}\to\nabla_{C}v
in
L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}(T{\sf X})
and
L^{2}_{\mathsf{loc}}(T^{\otimes 2}{\sf X})
as
n\to\infty
.

A useful result we are going to use later on is the following (we refer to
[
12

4.1.3
Setting up the problem

Let us fix two
{\sf RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces
{\sf X},{\sf Y}
. Much like in the previous chapter we shall put subscripts
{\sf x},{\sf y}
to differentiation operators to specify that they act on the
x,y
variable respectively. Thus, for instance, with some abuse of notation we shall denote by
{\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(f)
both the Hessian of a function
f\in W^{2,2}({\sf X})
and the map
y\mapsto{\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(f(\cdot,y))
, if
f
is defined on
{\sf X}\times{\sf Y}
and is such that
f(\cdot,y)\in W^{2,2}({\sf X})
for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
. It may also occur that for
f\in W^{2,2}({\sf X})
we write
{\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(f)
to denote the constant map
y\mapsto{\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(f)\in L^{2}((T^{*})^{\otimes 2}{\sf X})
: this choice will alleviate the notational burden, and it will be clear from the context when we will be doing so.

With this said, in Section
4.2
we prove that the Hessian of a function
f
on
{\sf X}\times{\sf Y}
admits a decomposition of the form

{\rm Hess}(f)=\Bigg(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(f)&{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f\\
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f&{\rm Hess}_{\sf y}(f)\end{array}\Bigg)

meaning that the right hand side is a well defined tensor in
L^{2}
if and only if so it is the left hand side, and similarly in Section
4.3
we prove that the Covariant Derivative of a vector field
v=(v_{{\sf x}},v_{{\sf y}})
in the tangent module over
{\sf X}\times{\sf Y}
admits a decomposition of the form

\nabla_{C}v=\Bigg(\begin{array}[]{cc}\nabla_{C,{\sf x}}v_{{\sf x}}&\quad{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}v_{{\sf y}}\\
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}v_{\sf x}&\quad\nabla_{C,{\sf y}}v_{{\sf y}}\end{array}\Bigg).

To make this rigorous we start from Theorem
2.8
and notice that
\Phi_{\sf x}\colon L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{*}{\sf X}))\to L^{0}(T^{*}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
induces a map, denoted by
\Phi_{\sf x}^{\otimes 2}
, from
L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{*}{\sf X}))\otimes L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{*}{\sf X}))
to
L^{0}(T^{*}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))\otimes L^{0}(T^{*}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))=L^{0}((T^{*})^{\otimes 2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
by acting component-wise. More precisely, the
L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
-linear extension of the map

\omega_{1}\otimes\omega_{2}\quad\mapsto\quad\Phi_{\sf x}(\omega_{1})\otimes\Phi_{\sf x}(\omega_{2})\qquad\forall\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\in L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{*}{\sf X}))

(which is easily seen to be well defined) can be extended by continuity to a norm-preserving map. This is a consequence of the fact that
\Phi_{\sf x}
itself is norm-preserving (see Proposition
2.2
), so that we have

\begin{split}\Big|\sum_{i}\Phi_{\sf x}(\omega_{i,1})\otimes\Phi_{\sf x}(\omega_{i,2})\Big|^{2}&=\sum_{i,j}\left<\Phi_{\sf x}(\omega_{i,1}),\Phi_{\sf x}(\omega_{j,1})\right>\left<\Phi_{\sf x}(\omega_{i,2}),\Phi_{\sf x}(\omega_{j,2})\right>\\
&=\sum_{i,j}\left<\omega_{i,1},\omega_{j,1}\right>\left<\omega_{i,2},\omega_{j,2}\right>=\Big|\sum_{i}\omega_{i,1}\otimes\omega_{i,2}\Big|^{2}\end{split}

for any finite choice of
\omega_{i,1},\omega_{i,2}\in L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{*}{\sf X}))
.

In a similar way we can define the maps

\begin{split}\Phi_{\sf y}^{\otimes 2}&:L^{0}({\sf X};L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{*}{\sf Y}))\otimes L^{0}({\sf X};L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{*}{\sf Y}))\quad\to\quad L^{0}((T^{*})^{\otimes 2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))\\
\Phi_{\sf x}\otimes\Phi_{\sf y}&:L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{*}{\sf X}))\otimes L^{0}({\sf X};L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{*}{\sf Y}))\quad\to\quad L^{0}((T^{*})^{\otimes 2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))\\
\Phi_{\sf y}\otimes\Phi_{\sf x}&:L^{0}({\sf X};L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{*}{\sf Y}))\otimes L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{*}{\sf X}))\quad\to\quad L^{0}((T^{*})^{\otimes 2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))\end{split}

and prove that they are
L^{0}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
-linear and norm preserving.

Then, from the fact that
\Phi_{\sf x}\oplus\Phi_{\sf y}
is an isomorphism of modules, we deduce that

(\Phi_{\sf x}\oplus\Phi_{\sf y})^{\otimes 2}=(\Phi_{\sf x}^{\otimes 2})\oplus(\Phi_{\sf x}\otimes\Phi_{\sf y})\oplus(\Phi_{\sf y}\otimes\Phi_{\sf x})\oplus(\Phi^{\otimes 2}_{\sf y})

is also an isomorphism of modules, in the sense made precise by the following statement:

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
{\sf X},{\sf Y}
be
{\sf RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces. Then, with the notation introduced above, the following holds.

For any
A\in L^{0}((T^{*})^{\otimes 2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
there are unique
A_{{\sf x}{\sf x}},A_{{\sf x}{\sf y}},A_{{\sf y}{\sf x}},A_{{\sf y}{\sf y}}\in L^{0}((T^{*})^{\otimes 2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
in the images of
\Phi_{\sf x}^{\otimes 2},\Phi_{\sf x}\otimes\Phi_{\sf y},\Phi_{\sf y}\otimes\Phi_{\sf x},\Phi^{\otimes 2}_{\sf y}
, respectively, such that

A=A_{{\sf x}{\sf x}}+A_{{\sf x}{\sf y}}+A_{{\sf y}{\sf x}}+A_{{\sf y}{\sf y}}.

(4.1.13)

Moreover, we have
\left<A_{ij},A_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right>=0
whenever
(i,j)\neq(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
and thus

|A|^{2}=|A_{{\sf x}{\sf x}}|^{2}+|A_{{\sf x}{\sf y}}|^{2}+|A_{{\sf y}{\sf x}}|^{2}+|A_{{\sf y}{\sf y}}|^{2}\qquad\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}\text{-a.e.}.

(4.1.14)

Proof.
We start proving that the images of
\Phi_{\sf x}^{\otimes 2}
and
\Phi_{\sf x}\otimes\Phi_{\sf y}
are pointwise orthogonal. Since we know that these maps are continuous, it is sufficient to check that
\left<\Phi_{\sf x}^{\otimes 2}(V),\Phi_{\sf x}\otimes\Phi_{\sf y}(W)\right>=0
for arbitrary
V,W
in dense subsets of the respective source spaces. We thus pick
V=\sum_{i}v_{1,i}\otimes v_{2,i}
and
W=\sum_{j}v_{3,j}\otimes w_{j}
with
v_{1,i},v_{2,i},v_{3,j}\in L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{*}{\sf X}))
and
w_{j}\in L^{0}({\sf X};L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{*}{\sf Y}))
. Then

\begin{split}\left<\Phi_{\sf x}^{\otimes 2}(V),\Phi_{\sf x}\otimes\Phi_{\sf y}(W)\right>&=\sum_{i,j}\left<\Phi_{\sf x}(v_{1,i}){\otimes}\Phi_{\sf x}(v_{2,i}),\Phi_{\sf x}(v_{3,j})\otimes\Phi_{\sf y}(w_{j})\right>\\
&=\sum_{i,j}\left<\Phi_{\sf x}(v_{1,i}),\Phi_{\sf x}(v_{3,j})\right>\underbrace{\left<\Phi_{\sf x}(v_{2,i}),\Phi_{\sf y}(w_{j})\right>}_{\equiv 0\atop\text{ by \eqref{eq:orto}}}=0\end{split}

as desired. Similarly for other couple of images. This is enough to prove that whenever (
4.1.13
) holds, the identity (
4.1.14
) holds as well and in turn this proves uniqueness of the
A_{ij}
’s as in the statement.

Thus it remains to prove the existence of
A_{11},A_{12},A_{21}
, and
A_{22}
. Since (
4.1.14
) shows that the maps
A\mapsto A_{ij}
,
i,j\in\{{\sf x},{\sf y}\}
, are continuous, we can validate such existence just for
A
’s running in a dense subset of
L^{0}((T^{*})^{\otimes 2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
. We thus pick
A
of the form
A=\sum_{i}\omega_{i}\otimes\omega_{i}^{\prime}
for
\omega_{i},\omega_{i}^{\prime}\in L^{0}(T^{*}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
, and recall that, from the surjectivity part of the statement of Theorem
2.8
, for any
i
we have
\omega_{i}=\Phi_{\sf x}(\omega_{i,{\sf x}})+\Phi_{\sf y}(\omega_{i,{\sf y}})
, and similarly
\omega_{i}^{\prime}=\Phi_{\sf x}(\omega^{\prime}_{i,{\sf x}})+\Phi_{\sf y}(\omega^{\prime}_{i,{\sf y}})
, for appropriate
\omega_{i,{\sf x}},\omega^{\prime}_{i,{\sf x}}\in L^{0}({\sf Y};L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{*}{\sf X}))
and
\omega_{i,{\sf y}},\omega^{\prime}_{i,{\sf y}}\in L^{0}({\sf X};L^{0}(\mathit{T}^{*}{\sf Y}))
.

It is then clear from the definitions that with the choices

\begin{array}[]{rll}&A_{{\sf x}{\sf x}}:=\Phi_{\sf x}^{\otimes 2}\Big(\sum_{i}\omega_{i,{\sf x}}\otimes\omega^{\prime}_{i,{\sf x}}\Big),&A_{{\sf x}{\sf y}}:=\Phi_{\sf x}\otimes\Phi_{\sf y}\Big(\sum_{i}\omega_{i,{\sf x}}\otimes\omega^{\prime}_{i,{\sf y}}\Big),\\
&A_{{\sf y}{\sf x}}:=\Phi_{\sf y}\otimes\Phi_{\sf x}\Big(\sum_{i}\omega_{i,{\sf y}}\otimes\omega^{\prime}_{i,{\sf x}}\Big),&A_{{\sf y}{\sf y}}:=\Phi_{\sf y}^{\otimes 2}\Big(\sum_{i}\omega_{i,{\sf y}}\otimes\omega^{\prime}_{i,{\sf y}}\Big),\\
\end{array}

the equality (
4.1.13
) holds, thus giving the conclusion.

\square

4.2
Hessian on the product space

4.2.1
From regularity on the factors to regularity on the product

In this section we prove that if a function of two variables is such that the functions obtaining by freezing one variable are in
W^{2,2}
and with appropriate integrability assumptions, then the function itself belongs to
W^{2,2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
, see Theorem
4.7
for the precise statement (and Proposition
4.8
for a suboptimal version about the space
H^{2,2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
).

It will be useful to consider the following algebra of functions:

Definition 4.4

(The space
{\rm{Test}}_{\times}\mathrm{F}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
)
.

Let
{\sf X},{\sf Y}
be two
{\sf RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces.

Then
{\rm{Test}}_{\times}\mathrm{F}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
is the space of finite linear combinations of functions of the form
g_{1}\otimes g_{2}
, with
g_{1}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
and
g_{2}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf Y})
.

From the tensorization of the Cheeger energy in Definition
2.3
(which holds in this setting because of Proposition
4.1
), and the tensorization of the Laplacian given in Corollary
2.13
, it is easy to see that
{\rm{Test}}_{\times}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})\subset{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
. This newly defined space of functions will be useful in proving the main result of this section, Theorem
4.7
, because, as we will see in Proposition
4.6
, to check whether a function is in
W^{2,2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
it is sufficient to verify the defining property of the Hessian only for functions in the smaller space
{\rm{Test}}_{\times}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
, where computations are easier. In turn, this latter fact will be a direct consequence of the following density result:

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
{\sf X},{\sf Y}
be two
{\sf RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces and
g\in L^{2}\cap L^{\infty}({{\sf X}\times{\sf Y}})
(this in particular holds if
g\in{\rm TestF}(X\times{\sf Y})
). Then we can find functions
g_{n,t}\in{\rm{Test}}_{\times}\mathrm{F}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
, parametrized by
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t>0
, such that for every
t>0
it holds:

i)

\sup_{n}\|g_{n,t}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
,

ii)

\sup_{n}\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits(g_{n,t})<\infty
,

iii)

(g_{n,t}),(\Delta g_{n,t})
converge to
{{\sf h}}_{t}g,\Delta{{\sf h}}_{t}g
respectively in
L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
as
n\to\infty
,

iv)

(\nabla g_{n,t}),(\nabla(|\nabla g_{n,t}|^{2}))
converge to
\nabla{{\sf h}}_{t}g,\nabla(|\nabla{{\sf h}}_{t}g|^{2})
respectively in
L^{2}(T({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.
It is easy to see that we can find a sequence of equibounded functions
(g_{n})

L^{2}
-converging to
g
of the form
g_{n}=\sum_{i}^{N_{n}}\alpha_{i}{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{A_{n,i}\times B_{n,i}}
, where the sets
A_{i}\subset{\sf X}
and
B_{i}\subset{\sf Y}
are Borel and bounded and
\alpha_{i}\in\real
. We then put
g_{n,t}:={{\sf h}}_{t}(g_{n})
and notice that Corollary
4.2
and (
4.1.7
) ensures that
g_{n,t}\in{\rm{Test}}_{\times}\mathrm{F}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
t>0
.

Then property
(i)
follows from (
4.1.3
) and
(ii)
from (
4.1.5
), (
4.1.6
) and
(i)
. The first convergence in
(iii)
follows from the continuity of the heat flow as a map from
L^{2}
into itself and the second by also taking into account the a priori estimate (
4.1.2
). The fact that
\nabla g_{n,t}\to\nabla{{\sf h}}_{t}g
in
L^{2}(T({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
follows from (
4.1.1
), which ensures that
{{\sf h}}_{t}:L^{2}\to W^{1,2}
is a continuous operator.

It remains to prove
L^{2}
-convergence of
(\nabla(|\nabla g_{n,t}|^{2}))
to
\nabla(|\nabla{{\sf h}}_{t}g|^{2})
. To see this, start observing that
\nabla(|\nabla f|^{2})=2{\rm Hess}(f)(\nabla f)
for any
f\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
, and notice that what already proved together with (
4.1.10
) show that
{\rm Hess}(g_{n,t})\to{\rm Hess}({{\sf h}}_{t}g)
in
L^{2}((T^{*})^{\otimes 2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
as
n\to\infty
for every
t>0
.

Now put
g_{t}:={{\sf h}}_{t}g
and observe that

\begin{split}&\|{\rm Hess}(g_{n,t})(\nabla g_{n,t})-{\rm Hess}(g_{t})(\nabla g_{t})\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\|{\rm Hess}(g_{n,t}-g_{t})(\nabla g_{n,t}-\nabla g_{t})\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\rm Hess}(g_{t})(\nabla g_{n,t}-\nabla g_{t})\|_{L^{2}}+\|{\rm Hess}(g_{n,t}-g_{t})(\nabla g_{t})\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

As
n\to\infty
we see from
(ii)
and the
L^{2}
-convergence of Hessians that the first and third addends in the right hand side go to 0. For the second we use the
L^{2}
-convergence of gradients and the fact that they are equibounded to conclude with the dominated convergence theorem.

\square

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
{\sf X},{\sf Y}
be two
{\sf RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces,

and

Proof.

The ‘only if’ follows from the definitions of

and Hessian and the inclusion

. For the ‘if’ we start observing that from the symmetry in

of the definition of Hessian (
4.1.9
) and the fact that

4.2.1
) holds for

g,h
and apply Lemma
4.5
to find corresponding functions

as in the statement. Hence we know that (
4.2.1
) holds with
g_{n,t},h_{n,t}
in place of

respectively and letting

in such identity and using the conclusions of Lemma

) holds also with

\square

and thus

\begin{split}\left<{\mathrm{d}}f,{\mathrm{d}}g\right>\,{\rm div}(h\nabla g)=&\underbrace{|g_{2}|^{2}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\right>{\rm div}_{\sf x}(h\nabla_{\sf x}g_{1})}_{A}+\underbrace{g_{1}g_{2}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\right>{\rm div}_{\sf y}(h\nabla_{\sf y}g_{2})}_{B}\\
&+\underbrace{g_{1}g_{2}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\right>{\rm div}_{\sf x}(h\nabla_{\sf x}g_{1})}_{C}+\underbrace{|g_{1}|^{2}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\right>{\rm div}_{\sf y}(h\nabla_{\sf y}g_{2})}_{D}.\end{split}

(4.2.5)

Now notice that
|{\mathrm{d}}g|^{2}=|g_{2}|^{2}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}|^{2}+|g_{1}|^{2}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}|^{2}
, therefore

{\mathrm{d}}\frac{|{\mathrm{d}}g|^{2}}{2}=\big(|g_{2}|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\frac{|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}|^{2}}{2}+g_{1}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}|^{2},g_{2}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}|^{2}+|g_{1}|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}\frac{|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}|^{2}}{2}\big)

and

\begin{split}h\left<{\mathrm{d}}f,{\mathrm{d}}\frac{|{\mathrm{d}}g|^{2}}{2}\right>=&\underbrace{h|g_{2}|^{2}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\frac{|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}|^{2}}{2}\right>}_{A^{\prime}}+\underbrace{hg_{1}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}|^{2}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\right>}_{B^{\prime}}\\
&+\underbrace{hg_{2}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}|^{2}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\right>}_{C^{\prime}}+\underbrace{h|g_{1}|^{2}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}\frac{|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}|^{2}}{2}\right>}_{D^{\prime}}.\end{split}

(4.2.6)

Now observe that, from assumption
(i)
and the integrability properties of
g,h
, it follows that

\begin{split}-\iint A+A^{\prime}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}&=\int|g_{2}|^{2}\int h{\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(f)(\nabla_{\sf x}g_{1},\nabla_{\sf x}g_{1})\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}\\
&=\int h{\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(f)(\nabla_{\sf x}g,\nabla_{\sf x}g)\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).\end{split}

Similarly from assumption
(ii)
we obtain

-\iint D+D^{\prime}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}=\int h{\rm Hess}_{\sf y}(f)(\nabla_{\sf y}g,\nabla_{\sf y}g)\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).

Also, assumption
(iii)
and the chain rule in point
(iv)
of Proposition
3.13
with
{\sf X},{\sf Y}
swapped,
\mathscr{H}=L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})
and

ensure that
\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\right>\in W^{1,2}({\sf Y};L^{2}({\sf X}))
with

{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\right>=\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\right>.

(4.2.7)

In particular, for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
-a.e.
x\in{\sf X}
the function
g_{2}(\cdot)\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\right>(x,\cdot)
is in
W^{1,2}({\sf Y})
and the following integration by parts is justified:

\begin{split}-\iint B\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}&=\int g_{1}\int h\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}(g_{2}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\right>),{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\\
&=\iint hg_{1}|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}|^{2}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\right>+hg_{1}g_{2}\underbrace{\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\right>,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\right>}_{=\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\otimes{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\right>\text{ by }\eqref{eq:dxdy2}}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).\end{split}

Hence we have

-\iint B+B^{\prime}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}=\int h\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g\otimes{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})

and analogously
-\iint C+C^{\prime}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}=\int h\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g\otimes{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).

Collecting what proved so far and recalling (
4.2.5
) and (
4.2.6
) we obtain (
4.2.4
) and the conclusion.

\square

4.2.2
From regularity on the product to regularity on the factors

In this section we investigate the validity of the converse implication w.r.t. the one proved before, namely we study what the assumption
f\in W^{2,2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}),H^{2,2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
implies in terms of regularity of
f(\cdot,y),f(x,\cdot)
.

We start with the following result, which is similar in spirit to Propositions
3.6
,
3.14
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X})
and
({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be
{\sf RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces and
f\in W^{1,2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
. Then the following are equivalent:

i)

for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
we have
f(\cdot,y)\in W^{2,2}({\sf X})
with
\iint|{\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(f)|^{2}_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}<\infty
,

ii)

there is
A\in L^{2}({\sf Y};L^{2}((T^{*})^{\otimes 2}{\sf X}))
such that the identity

\begin{split}2\int hA(\nabla_{\sf x}g,\nabla_{\sf x}g^{\prime})\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}&\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})=\int-\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla(g^{\prime}\circ\pi_{\sf X}))\\
\qquad\qquad&-\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g^{\prime}\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla(g\circ\pi_{\sf X}))-h\langle{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g^{\prime}\right>\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\end{split}

holds for every
g,g^{\prime}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
and
h\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{\sf bs}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
.

Moreover, if this holds then the choice
A={\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(f)
is the only one for which
(ii)
holds.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
The fact that
(ii)
holds with the choice
A:={\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(f)
is a direct consequence of the definitions of
W^{2,2}({\sf X})
and of the Hessian, of the identity (
3.1.4
) and of the fact that for
h\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{\sf bs}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
we have that
h(\cdot,y)\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{\sf bs}({\sf X})
for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
. The fact that
{\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(f)
is the only choice is a consequence of the density of the linear span of elements of the form
h{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\otimes{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g^{\prime}
in
L^{2}({\sf Y};L^{2}((T^{*})^{\otimes 2}{\sf X}))
for
g,g^{\prime},h
as in the statement, which in turn is a trivial consequence of the fact that differentials of test functions generate the cotangent module.

(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
Recall from
[
12
, Theorem 3.3.2]
that the functional
{\sf E}_{2,{\sf x}}\colon W^{1,2}({\sf X})\to[0,\infty]
defined by
{\sf E}_{2,{\sf x}}(f):=\frac{1}{2}\int|{\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(f)|_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
if
f\in W^{2,2}({\sf X})
and
{\sf E}_{2,{\sf x}}(f):=+\infty
otherwise, is convex, lower semicontinuous and satisfies

\begin{split}2{\sf E}_{2,{\sf x}}(f)=&\sup\bigg(\sum_{j}\int-\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{j}\right>{\rm div}_{\sf x}(h_{j}h^{\prime}_{j}\nabla_{\sf x}g_{i}^{\prime})-\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{j}^{\prime}\right>{\rm div}_{\sf x}(h_{j}h_{j}^{\prime}\nabla_{\sf x}g_{j})\\
&\qquad\qquad-h_{j}h_{j}^{\prime}\big\langle{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\langle{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{j},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g^{\prime}_{j}\rangle\big\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\bigg)-\bigg\|\sum_{j}(h_{j}\nabla_{\sf x}g_{j})\otimes(h^{\prime}_{j}\nabla g_{j}^{\prime})\bigg\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\sf X})},\end{split}

(4.2.8)

where the sup is taken among all finite collections of functions
g_{j},g^{\prime}_{j},h_{j},h^{\prime}_{j}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
.

Now, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
(A^{n}_{i})
be a Borel partition of
{\sf Y}
made of at most countable sets, with
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A^{n}_{i})\in(0,\infty)
,
{\rm diam}(A^{n}_{i})\leq\frac{1}{n}
and so that
(A^{n+1}_{i})
is a refinement of
(A^{n}_{i})
. Put
f^{n}_{i}:=\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A^{n}_{i})^{-1}\int_{A^{n}_{i}}f(\cdot,y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y)\in L^{2}({\sf X})
and
f^{n}(x,y):=\sum_{i}{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{A^{n}_{i}}(y)f^{n}_{i}(x)\in L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
. We have already noticed in the proof of Proposition
3.6
that
f^{n}\to f
in
L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
and the proof of the same proposition shows that
\int{\sf E}_{\sf x}(f^{n}(\cdot,y))\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y)\to\int{\sf E}_{\sf x}(f(\cdot,y))\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y)
. Hence taking into account that
L^{2}({\sf Y};W^{1,2}({\sf X}))
is a Hilbert space (because
W^{1,2}({\sf X})
is so) we just proved that
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{2}({\sf Y};W^{1,2}({\sf X}))
and thus up to pass to a non-relabeled subsequence we have that
f^{n}(\cdot,y)\to f(\cdot,y)
in
W^{1,2}({\sf X})
for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
. Hence the
W^{1,2}({\sf X})
-lower semicontinuity of
{\sf E}_{2,{\sf x}}
and Fatou’s lemma give that

\int{\sf E}_{2,{\sf x}}(f(\cdot,y))\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y)\leq\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\int{\sf E}_{2,{\sf x}}(f^{n}(\cdot,y))\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y).

(4.2.9)

Now fix
n,i
and pick a finite family of functions
g_{j},g^{\prime}_{j},h_{j},h^{\prime}_{j}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
in the identity (
4.2.8
) written for the function
f^{n}_{i}
in place of
f
. Then for every
j
and
t>0
define
h_{j,t}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
as

h_{j,t}:={{\sf h}}_{t}\big({\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{{\sf X}\times A^{n}_{i}}(h_{j}h^{\prime}_{j})\circ\pi_{\sf X}\big)\,\eta\circ\pi_{\sf Y}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:tenscal2}}}{{=}}{{\sf h}}^{\sf X}_{t}(h_{j}h^{\prime}_{j})\otimes\big({{\sf h}}_{t}^{\sf Y}({\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{A^{n}_{i}})\,\eta\big),

where
\eta\in{\rm TestF}({\sf Y})
has bounded support and is identically 1 on
A^{n}_{i}
. Then from the weak maximum principle (
4.1.3
) and the Bakry-Émery gradient estimates (
4.1.4
) it easily follows that
(|h_{j,t}-h_{j}|),(|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}h_{j,t}-{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}h_{j}|)
are uniformly bounded and converge to 0 in
L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
as
t\downarrow 0
. Hence the dominate convergence theorem, the identity (
3.1.4
) and the closure of the differential give that

\begin{split}\int-\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{j}\right>{\rm div}(h_{j,t}\nabla(g^{\prime}_{j}\circ\pi_{\sf X}))-\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{j}^{\prime}\right>{\rm div}(h_{j,t}&\nabla(g_{j}\circ\pi_{\sf X}))\\
-h_{j,t}\langle{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,&{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{j},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{j}^{\prime}\right>\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\\
\to\quad\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A^{n}_{i})\int-\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f^{n}_{i},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{j}\right>{\rm div}_{\sf x}(h_{j}h^{\prime}_{j}\nabla_{\sf x}g_{i}^{\prime})-&\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f^{n}_{i},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{j}^{\prime}\right>{\rm div}_{\sf x}(h_{j}h_{j}^{\prime}\nabla_{\sf x}g_{j})\\
-h_{j}h_{j}^{\prime}\langle{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f^{n}_{i}&,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{j},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g^{\prime}_{j}\right>\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\end{split}

(4.2.10)

as
t\downarrow 0
for any
j
.
Similarly

\int\langle A,h_{j,t}\nabla_{\sf x}g_{j}\otimes\nabla_{\sf x}g^{\prime}\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\quad\to\quad\int_{{\sf X}\times A^{n}_{i}}\langle A,(h_{j}\nabla_{\sf x}g_{j})\otimes(h_{j}^{\prime}\nabla_{\sf x}g_{j}^{\prime})\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})

(4.2.11)

Now observe that from assumption
(ii)
we have that

\begin{split}\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\sum_{j}\int-\langle{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f&,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{j}\rangle{\rm div}(h_{j,t}\nabla(g^{\prime}_{j}\circ\pi_{\sf X}))-\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{j}^{\prime}\right>{\rm div}(h_{j,t}\nabla(g_{j}\circ\pi_{\sf X}))\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-h_{j,t}\langle{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{j},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{j}^{\prime}\right>\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\\
&=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\sum_{j}2\int\langle A,h_{j,t}\nabla_{\sf x}g_{j}\otimes\nabla_{\sf x}g^{\prime}\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\\
\text{by \eqref{eq:ahj}}\qquad&=2\int\langle{\raise 1.29167pt\hbox{$\chi$}}_{{\sf X}\times A^{n}_{i}}A,\sum_{j}(h_{j}\nabla_{\sf x}g_{j})\otimes(h_{j}^{\prime}\nabla_{\sf x}g_{j})^{\prime}\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\\
&\leq\int_{{\sf X}\times A^{n}_{i}}|A|_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})+\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A^{n}_{i})\Big\|\sum_{j}(h_{j}\nabla_{\sf x}g_{j})\otimes(h^{\prime}_{j}\nabla g_{j}^{\prime})\Big\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\sf X})},\end{split}

having used Young’s inequality in the last step. This inequality, the arbitrariness of
g_{j},g_{j}^{\prime},h_{j},h_{j}^{\prime}
, (
4.2.10
) and (
4.2.8
) give that

\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A^{n}_{i}){\sf E}_{2,{\sf x}}(f^{n}_{i})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{{\sf X}\times A^{n}_{i}}|A|_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).

Adding up over
i
, then letting

keeping in mind (

) we deduce that

\int{\sf E}_{2,{\sf x}}(f(\cdot,y))\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{{\sf X}\times{\sf Y}}|A|_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}),

which is
(i)
.

\square

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X})
and
({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be
{\sf RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces and
f\in W^{2,2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
. Then the identity

\begin{split}2\int&h{\rm Hess}(f)(\nabla g_{1},\nabla g_{2})\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\\
&=-\int\left<{\mathrm{d}}f,{\mathrm{d}}g_{1}\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla g_{2})+\left<{\mathrm{d}}f,{\mathrm{d}}g_{2}\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla g_{1})-h\left<{\mathrm{d}}f,{\mathrm{d}}\left<{\mathrm{d}}g_{1},{\mathrm{d}}g_{2}\right>\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\end{split}

(4.2.12)

holds for any
h\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{\sf bs}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in\{g\circ\pi_{\sf X}:g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})\}\cup\{g\circ\pi_{\sf Y}:g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf Y})\}
.

Proof.
Suppose at first that
h\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
has bounded support and let
\eta\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
be with bounded support and such that
\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(h)\subset\{\eta=1\}
(see (
4.1.8
)). Then for

we have that
\eta g_{1},\eta g_{2}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
and the validity of (

) follows from the very definition of Hessian and the locality property of the differential, which ensures that
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e. on
\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(h)
it holds
{\mathrm{d}}(\eta g_{i})={\mathrm{d}}g_{i}
,

, and

Now let

,
\eta
as before and
(h_{n})\subset{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
a sequence

-converging to

\eta h_{n}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
for every

and

in

. From the fact that (

) holds with

and the fact that these functions have uniformly bounded support it is now easy to pass to the limit in

and get the claim.

\square

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X})
and
({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be
{\sf RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces,
g:{\sf X}\times{\sf Y}\to\mathbb{R}
of the form

for some
\tilde{g}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
. Then for every

with
\tilde{h}\in W^{1,2}({\sf Y})
we have

{\rm Hess}(g)(\nabla h)=0\qquad\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}-a.e..

(4.2.13)

Similarly with the roles of
{\sf X}
and
{\sf Y}
swapped.

Proof.

Recalling that gradients of test functions on
{\sf Y}
generate
L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf Y})
, we can reduce to prove (
4.2.13
) for
h
as in the statement with
\tilde{h}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf Y})
. Then taking into account Theorem

we see that to conclude it is sufficient to show that

\begin{split}{\rm Hess}(g)(\nabla h,\nabla f)&=0,\quad\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}-a.e.,\quad\forall f=\tilde{f}\circ\pi_{\sf X}\quad\text{with }\tilde{f}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}),\\
{\rm Hess}(g)(\nabla h,\nabla f)&=0,\quad\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}-a.e.,\quad\forall f=\tilde{f}\circ\pi_{\sf Y}\quad\text{with }\tilde{f}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf Y}).\end{split}

(4.2.14)

We start with the first of the above and notice that

\begin{split}2{\rm Hess}(g)(\nabla h,\nabla f)&=\langle{\mathrm{d}}\underbrace{\left<{\mathrm{d}}g,{\mathrm{d}}h\right>}_{\equiv 0\atop\text{ by \eqref{eq:orto}}},{\mathrm{d}}f\rangle+\left<{\mathrm{d}}\left<{\mathrm{d}}g,{\mathrm{d}}f\right>,{\mathrm{d}}h\right>-\langle{\mathrm{d}}g,{\mathrm{d}}\underbrace{\left<{\mathrm{d}}h,{\mathrm{d}}f\right>}_{\equiv 0\atop\text{ by \eqref{eq:orto}}}\rangle,\end{split}

while for the middle term we notice that by Proposition
2.2
(in particular by polarization from the fact that
\Phi_{\sf x}
preserves the pointwise norm) we have
\left<{\mathrm{d}}g,{\mathrm{d}}f\right>=\langle{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\tilde{g},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\tilde{f}\rangle\circ\pi_{\sf X}
, so that this function depends only on the
x
variable and the scalar product of its differential with that of
h
is 0, again by (
2.2.5
).

Similarly, for the second in (
4.2.14
) we have

\begin{split}2{\rm Hess}(g)(\nabla h,\nabla f)&=\langle{\mathrm{d}}\underbrace{\left<{\mathrm{d}}g,{\mathrm{d}}h\right>}_{\equiv 0\atop\text{ by \eqref{eq:orto}}},{\mathrm{d}}\tilde{f}\rangle+\langle{\mathrm{d}}\underbrace{\langle{\mathrm{d}}g,{\mathrm{d}}f\rangle}_{\equiv 0\atop\text{ by \eqref{eq:orto}}},{\mathrm{d}}h\rangle-\langle{\mathrm{d}}g,{\mathrm{d}}{\langle{\mathrm{d}}h,{\mathrm{d}}f\rangle}\rangle,\end{split}

and in the last addend arguing as above we see that the function
{\langle{\mathrm{d}}h,{\mathrm{d}}f\rangle}
depends only on
y
, while
g
depends only on
x
, so that (
2.2.5
) ensures that it is identically 0, as claimed.

\square

We now come to the main result of this section:

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X})
and
({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be
{\sf RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces and
f\in W^{2,2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
. Then

i)

for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
we have
f(\cdot,y)\in W^{2,2}({\sf X})
with
\iint|{\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(f)|^{2}_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}<\infty
,

ii)

for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
-a.e.
x\in{\sf X}
we have
f(x,\cdot)\in W^{2,2}({\sf Y})
with
\iint|{\rm Hess}_{\sf y}(f)|^{2}_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}<\infty
,

iii)

we have
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}f\in W^{1,2}({\sf X};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf Y}))
and
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f\in W^{1,2}({\sf Y};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
.

Also, the identities (
4.2.2
) and (
4.2.3
) hold.

Proof.
Point
(i)
follows by a direct application of Lemma
4.10
above with
g_{1}=g_{2}\in\{g\circ\pi_{\sf X}:g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})\}
in conjunction with Proposition
4.9
. Similarly for
(ii)
.

To get
(iii)
we start claiming that for
g_{1},g_{2}\in\{g\circ\pi_{\sf X}:g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})\}\cup\{g\circ\pi_{\sf Y}:g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf Y})\}
and
h\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
with bounded support it holds

\begin{split}&2\int h{\rm Hess}(g_{1})(\nabla f,\nabla g_{2})\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\\
&=\int-\left<{\mathrm{d}}g_{1},{\mathrm{d}}f\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla g_{2})+h\left<{\mathrm{d}}\left<{\mathrm{d}}g_{1},{\mathrm{d}}g_{2}\right>,{\mathrm{d}}f\right>+\left<{\mathrm{d}}f,{\mathrm{d}}g_{2}\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla g_{1})\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).\end{split}

Indeed, this identity holds for
f\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
by the very definition of Hessian and two integration by parts. Then the claim follows from the continuity of both sides of the identity in
f
w.r.t. the
W^{1,2}
-topology. Adding up this identity with (
4.2.12
) we obtain

\int h{\rm Hess}(f)(\nabla g_{1},\nabla g_{2})+h{\rm Hess}(g_{1})(\nabla f,\nabla g_{2})\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})=-\int\left<{\mathrm{d}}f,{\mathrm{d}}g_{1}\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla g_{2})\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).

Here we pick
g_{1}=g\circ\pi_{\sf X}
and
g_{2}=\tilde{g}\circ\pi_{\sf Y}
with
g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
and
\tilde{g}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf Y})
respectively and take into account Lemma
4.11
to deduce that

\begin{split}\int h{\rm Hess}(f)(\nabla(g\circ\pi_{\sf X}),\nabla(\tilde{g}\circ\pi_{\sf Y}))\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})&=-\int\left<{\mathrm{d}}f,{\mathrm{d}}(g\circ\pi_{\sf X})\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla(\tilde{g}\circ\pi_{\sf Y}))\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\\
\end{split}

Hence recalling Proposition
2.2
(in particular the fact that
\Phi_{\sf x}
preserves the pointwise norm) and denoting by
A
the restriction of the Hessian of
f
to
\Phi_{\sf x}(L^{2}({\sf Y};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X})))\otimes\Phi_{\sf y}(L^{2}({\sf X};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf Y})))\subset L^{2}((T^{*})^{\otimes 2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
we obtain

\int h\left<A,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\otimes{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}\tilde{g}\right>=-\int\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla(\tilde{g}\circ\pi_{\sf Y}))\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).

(4.2.15)

We have established (
4.2.15
) for
\tilde{g}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf Y})
and
h\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
with bounded support, but a simple approximation argument based on the heat flow and a multiplication with a test cut-off function with bounded support shows that it actually holds for
h\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{\sf bs}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
and
\tilde{g}\in D(\Delta_{\sf y})
.

Since
\{{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g:g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})\}
generates
L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X})
, we are now in position to apply Proposition
3.14
with
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f
in place of
f
to conclude that
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f\in W^{1,2}({\sf Y};L^{2}(T^{*}{\sf X}))
, as desired (with
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f=A
). The argument for
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f
is analogous.

The fact that the identities (
4.2.2
) and (
4.2.3
) hold is now a direct consequence of the proof (alternatively, now that we know
(i),(ii),(iii)
, of Proposition
4.7
).

\square

If the function
f
in the previous statement is assumed to be in
H^{2,2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
, also the fibers can be proved to be in the respective
H^{2,2}
spaces:

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X})
and
({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be
{\sf RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces and
f\in H^{2,2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
.

Then in addition to the conclusions of Theorem
4.12
above we have:

i’)

for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
we have
f(\cdot,y)\in H^{2,2}({\sf X})
,

ii’)

for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
-a.e.
x\in{\sf X}
we have
f(x,\cdot)\in H^{2,2}({\sf Y})
.

Proof.

Let
(f_{n})\subset{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
be
W^{2,2}
-converging to
f
and notice that with a diagonalization argument based on the fact that
{{\sf h}}_{t}f_{n}\to f_{n}
in
W^{2,2}
as

(recall (
4.1.10
)), we see that
h_{t_{n}}f_{n}\to f
in
W^{2,2}
for some

.

The conclusion then follows from the following two simple facts (and their analogue with inverted variables):

for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
-a.e.
x\in{\sf X}
we have
{{\sf h}}_{t_{n}}f_{n}(x,\cdot)\in{\rm TestF}({\sf Y})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}

and

for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
-a.e.
x\in{\sf X}
we have
{{\sf h}}_{t_{n_{k}}}f_{n_{k}}(x,\cdot)\to f(x,\cdot)
in
W^{2,2}({\sf Y})
for some

.

The first follows from (
4.1.11
) and (
4.1.7
), while for the second we notice that the identities (
2.2.2
) and (
4.1.14
), (
4.2.2
) (which is valid thanks to Theorem
4.12
) imply that

|{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g(x,\cdot)|(y)\leq|{\mathrm{d}}g|(x,y)\qquad\text{ and }\qquad|{\rm Hess}_{\sf y}g(x,\cdot)|_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}(y)\leq|{\rm Hess}g|_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}(x,y)

\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
(x,y)\in{\sf X}\times{\sf Y}
. Then we apply these to
g:=f-f_{n}
and use the assumption

in

\square

4.3
Covariant derivative on the product space

In this part of the paper we investigate the relation between Sobolev regularity of vector fields on base spaces and in the product.

4.3.1
From regularity on the factors to regularity on the product

The following is analogue of Proposition
4.6
:

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
{\sf X},{\sf Y}
be two
{\sf RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces,
v\in L^{0}(T({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
and
T\in L^{2}(T^{\otimes 2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
. Then
v\in W^{1,2}_{C}(T({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
with
\nabla v=T
if and only if it holds

-\int\langle v,\nabla\tilde{g}\rangle{\rm div}(h\nabla g)+h{\rm Hess}(\tilde{g})(v,\nabla g)\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\,=\,\int h\langle T,\nabla g\otimes\nabla\tilde{g}\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).

(4.3.1)

for every
g,\tilde{g},h\in{\rm{Test}}_{\times}\mathrm{F}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
.

Proof.

The ‘only if’ part follows directly from the definitions of
W^{1,2}_{C}
and Covariant Derivative, and the inclusion
{\rm{Test}}_{\times}\mathrm{F}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})\subset{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
. As for the ‘if’ part, fix
g,\tilde{g},h\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
, and apply Lemma
4.5
to find corresponding functions
g_{n,t},\tilde{g}_{n,t},h_{n,t}
as in the statement. Therefore, (
4.3.1
) holds with
g_{n,t},\tilde{g}_{n,t},h_{n,t}
in place of
g,\tilde{g},h
, respectively. Now, letting
n\to\infty
in such identity, and using the conclusions of Lemma
4.5
, we obtain that (
4.2.1
) holds also with
{{\sf h}}_{t}g,{{\sf h}}_{t}\tilde{g}_{n},{{\sf h}}_{t}h
in place of
g,\tilde{g},h
, respectively. In order to conclude, we pass to the limit as
t\downarrow 0
in the resulting identity and we get that (
4.3.1
) actually holds for the
g,\tilde{g},h\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
initially chosen, as desired.

\square

We then the following result, analogue of Theorem
4.7
.

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
{\sf X},{\sf Y}
be two
{\sf RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces and let
v\in L^{0}(T({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
,
v=(v_{\sf x},v_{\sf y})
, be such that:

i)

for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
-a.e.
x\in{\sf X}
we have that
v_{\sf y}(x,\cdot)\in W^{1,2}_{C}(T{\sf Y})
with
\iint|\nabla_{C,{\sf y}}(v_{\sf y})|^{2}_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}<\infty
,

ii)

for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
we have that
v_{\sf x}(\cdot,y)\in W^{1,2}_{C}(T{\sf X})
with
\iint|\nabla_{C,{\sf x}}(v_{\sf x})|^{2}_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}<\infty
,

iii)

the map
{\sf Y}\ni y\mapsto v_{\sf x}(\cdot,y)
belongs to
W^{1,2}({\sf Y};L^{2}(T{\sf X}))
, and the map
{\sf X}\ni x\mapsto v_{\sf y}(x,\cdot)
belongs to
W^{1,2}({\sf X};L^{2}(T{\sf Y}))
.

Then
v\in W^{1,2}_{C}(T({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
with

\nabla_{C}v=\Phi^{\otimes 2}_{\sf x}(\nabla_{C,{\sf x}}v_{\sf x})+\Phi^{\otimes 2}_{\sf y}(\nabla_{C,{\sf y}}v_{\sf y})+\Phi_{\sf x}\otimes\Phi_{\sf y}({\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}v_{\sf y})+\Phi_{\sf y}\otimes\Phi_{\sf x}({\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}v_{\sf x})

(4.3.2)

i.e. for every
w,z\in L^{0}(T({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
, writing
w=(w_{\sf x},w_{\sf y})
and
z=(z_{\sf x},z_{\sf y})
(recall (
3.1.3
)) we have

\nabla_{C}v:(w\otimes z)=\langle\nabla_{C,{\sf x}}v_{\sf x},w_{\sf x}\otimes z_{\sf x}\rangle+\langle\nabla_{C,{\sf y}}v_{\sf y},w_{\sf y}\otimes z_{\sf y}\rangle+\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}v_{\sf y},w_{\sf x}\otimes z_{\sf y}\right>+\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}v_{\sf x},w_{\sf y}\otimes z_{\sf x}\right>.

(4.3.3)

Proof.

First of all, we observe that the assumptions guarantee that the right hand side of (
4.3.2
) defines a tensor in
L^{2}(T^{\otimes 2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
. Hence, keeping in mind the very definition of
W^{1,2}_{C}(T({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
and of the Covariant Derivative, Proposition
4.14
above and the equivalent version (
4.3.3
) of (
4.3.2
), in order to conclude we have to prove that for
g_{1},\tilde{g}_{1},h_{1}
(resp.
g_{2},\tilde{g}_{2},h_{2}
) test functions on
{\sf X}
(resp.
{\sf Y}
), once we set
g:=g_{1}\otimes g_{2}
,
\tilde{g}:=\tilde{g}_{1}\otimes\tilde{g}_{2}
, and
h:=h_{1}\otimes h_{2}
, we have

\begin{split}-\int\langle v,\nabla\tilde{g}\rangle{\rm div}(h\nabla g)+h{\rm Hess}(\tilde{g})(v,\nabla g)\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}&\times\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\\
=\int h\Big(\langle\nabla_{C,{\sf x}}v_{\sf x},\nabla_{\sf x}g\otimes\nabla_{\sf x}\tilde{g}\rangle+&\langle\nabla_{C,{\sf y}}v_{\sf y},\nabla_{\sf y}g\otimes\nabla_{\sf y}\tilde{g}\rangle\\
+\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}v_{\sf x},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g\otimes{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\tilde{g}\right>&+\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}v_{\sf y},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g\otimes{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\tilde{g}\right>\Big)\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).\end{split}

(4.3.4)

From the fact that
\nabla\tilde{g}=(\tilde{g}_{2}\nabla_{\sf x}\tilde{g}_{1},\tilde{g}_{1}\nabla_{\sf y}\tilde{g}_{2})
, we have

\left<v,\nabla\tilde{g}\right>=\tilde{g}_{2}\left<v_{\sf x},\nabla_{\sf x}\tilde{g}_{1}\right>+\tilde{g}_{1}\left<v_{\sf y},\nabla_{\sf y}\tilde{g}_{2}\right>.

Moreover, from Proposition
2.11
we have that for any
h\in{\rm{Test}}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
it holds

{\rm div}(h\nabla g)={\rm div}_{\sf x}(h\nabla_{\sf x}g)+{\rm div}_{\sf y}(h\nabla_{\sf y}g)=g_{2}{\rm div}_{\sf x}(h\nabla_{\sf x}g_{1})+g_{1}{\rm div}_{\sf y}(h\nabla_{\sf y}g_{2}),

and thus

\begin{split}\left<v,\nabla\tilde{g}\right>\,{\rm div}(h\nabla g)=&\underbrace{g_{2}\tilde{g}_{2}\left<v_{\sf x},\nabla_{\sf x}\tilde{g}_{1}\right>{\rm div}_{\sf x}(h\nabla_{\sf x}g_{1})}_{A}+\underbrace{g_{1}\tilde{g}_{2}\left<v_{\sf x},\nabla_{\sf x}\tilde{g}_{1}\right>{\rm div}_{\sf y}(h\nabla_{\sf y}g_{2})}_{B}\\
&+\underbrace{\tilde{g}_{1}g_{2}\left<v_{\sf y},\nabla_{\sf y}\tilde{g}_{2}\right>{\rm div}_{\sf x}(h\nabla_{\sf x}g_{1})}_{C}+\underbrace{g_{1}\tilde{g}_{1}\left<v_{\sf y},\nabla_{\sf y}\tilde{g}_{2}\right>{\rm div}_{\sf y}(h\nabla_{\sf y}g_{2})}_{D}.\end{split}

(4.3.5)

At this point, a direct application of Theorem
4.12
ensures that

\begin{split}h{\rm Hess}(\tilde{g})(v,\nabla g)=&\underbrace{hg_{2}\tilde{g}_{2}{\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(\tilde{g}_{1})(v_{\sf x},\nabla_{\sf x}g_{1})}_{A^{\prime}}+\underbrace{hg_{1}\left<v_{\sf x}\otimes\nabla_{\sf y}g_{2},\nabla_{\sf x}\tilde{g}_{1}\otimes\nabla_{\sf y}\tilde{g}_{2}\right>}_{B^{\prime}}\\
&+\underbrace{hg_{2}\left<v_{\sf y}\otimes\nabla_{\sf x}g_{1},\nabla_{\sf y}\tilde{g}_{2}\otimes\nabla_{\sf x}\tilde{g}_{1}\right>}_{C^{\prime}}+\underbrace{hg_{1}\tilde{g}_{1}{\rm Hess}_{\sf y}(\tilde{g}_{2})(v_{\sf y},\nabla_{\sf y}g_{2})}_{D^{\prime}}.\end{split}

(4.3.6)

Now, observe that from assumption
(i)
, and the integrability properties of
g,\tilde{g},h
it follows that

\begin{split}-\int\int A+A^{\prime}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf x}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf y}&=\int g_{2}\tilde{g}_{2}\int h\nabla_{C,{\sf x}}v_{\sf x}:(\nabla_{\sf x}g_{1}\otimes\nabla_{\sf x}\tilde{g}_{1})\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf x}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf y}\\
&=\int h\nabla_{C,{\sf x}}v_{\sf x}:(\nabla_{\sf x}g\otimes\nabla_{\sf x}\tilde{g})\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf x}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf y}),\end{split}

and, similarly, from assumption
(ii)
we obtain

-\int\int D+D^{\prime}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf x}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf y}=\int h\nabla_{C,{\sf y}}v_{\sf y}:(\nabla_{\sf y}g\otimes\nabla_{\sf y}\tilde{g})\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf x}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf y}).

Moreover, assumption
(iii)
and the chain rule in point
(iv)
of Proposition
3.13
with
{\sf X},{\sf Y}
swapped,
\mathscr{H}=L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})
and
T\colon L^{0}(T^{*}{\sf X})\to L^{0}({\sf X})
given by
T(\cdot)=\left<\cdot,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\right>
ensure that
\left<v_{\sf x},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\right>\in W^{1,2}({\sf Y};L^{2}({\sf X}))
with

{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}\left<v_{\sf x},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\right>=\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}v_{\sf x},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\right>.

(4.3.7)

In particular, for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
-a.e.
x\in{\sf X}
the function
g_{2}(\cdot)\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}f,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g_{1}\right>(x,\cdot)
is in
W^{1,2}({\sf Y})
and the following integration by parts is justified:

\begin{split}-\iint B\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}&=\int g_{1}\int h\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}(\tilde{g}_{2}\left<v_{\sf x},\nabla_{\sf x}\tilde{g}_{1}\right>),{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\\
&=\iint hg_{1}\underbrace{\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}\tilde{g}_{2},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\right>\left<v_{\sf x},\nabla_{\sf x}\tilde{g}_{1}\right>}_{=\left<v_{\sf x}\otimes\nabla_{\sf y}\tilde{g}_{2},\nabla_{\sf x}\tilde{g}_{1}\otimes\nabla_{\sf y}g_{2}\right>}+hg_{1}\tilde{g}_{2}\underbrace{\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}\left<v_{\sf x},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\tilde{g}_{1}\right>,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\right>}_{=\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}v_{\sf x},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g_{2}\otimes{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\tilde{g}_{1}\right>\text{ by }\eqref{eq:dxdyCOV}}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).\end{split}

Therefore we get

-\iint B+B^{\prime}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}=\int h\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}v_{\sf x},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}g\otimes{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}\tilde{g}\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}),

and similarly
-\iint C+C^{\prime}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}=\int h\left<{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}v_{\sf y},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\otimes{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}\tilde{g}\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
. Summing up (
4.3.5
) and (
4.3.6
), and keeping in mind all these identities, we obtain (
4.3.4
) and the conclusion.

\square

4.3.2
From regularity on the product to regularity on the factors

The following statement is the analogue of Proposition
4.9
.

Proposition 4.16
.

Let
({\sf X},{\sf d}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X})
and
({\sf Y},{\sf d}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be
{\sf RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces, and let
v\in\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathit{T}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
,
v=(v_{\sf x},v_{\sf y})
.
Then the following are equivalent:

i)

for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
, we have
v_{\sf x}(\cdot,y)\in\mathit{W}^{1,2}_{C}(\mathit{T}{\sf X})
with
\iint\lvert\nabla_{C,{\sf X}}(v_{\sf x})\rvert^{2}_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}<\infty
,

ii)

there exists
T\in\mathit{L}^{2}({\sf Y};\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathit{T}^{\otimes 2}{\sf X}))
such that the identity

\begin{split}\int hT:(\nabla g\otimes\nabla\tilde{g})\,&{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\\
&=\int-\left<v_{\sf x},\nabla_{x}\tilde{g}\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla(g\circ\pi_{\sf X}))-h{\rm Hess}(\tilde{g})(v_{\sf x},g)\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\end{split}

holds for every
g,\tilde{g}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
and
h\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{\sf bs}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
.

Moreover, in this case the choice
T=\nabla_{C,{\sf X}}(v_{\sf x})
is the only one for which the identity in
ii)
holds.

Proof.

i)\Rightarrow ii)
Similarly to the proof of Proposition
4.9
, the validity of the identity in
ii)
with the only choice
T=\nabla_{\sf x}v
is guaranteed by the definition of the Covariant Derivative and the space
\mathit{W}^{1,2}_{C}(\mathit{T}{\sf X})
, the identity (
3.1.4
) and the fact that
h(\cdot,y)\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{\sf bs}({\sf X})
for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
.

ii)\Rightarrow i)
We start recalling that the connection energy functional
{\sf E}_{C,{\sf x}}\colon\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathit{T}{\sf X})\to[0,\infty]
defined by
{\sf E}_{C,{\sf x}}(v):=\frac{1}{2}\int|\nabla v|_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
if
v\in W^{1,2}_{C}(\mathit{T}{\sf X})
and
{\sf E}_{C,{\sf x}}(v):=+\infty
otherwise, is convex, lower semicontinuous and satisfies

\begin{split}{\sf E}_{C,{\sf x}}(v)=\sup\bigg\{\sum_{j}&\int-\left<v,z_{j}\right>{\rm div}_{\sf x}(w_{j})-\nabla z_{j}:(w_{j}\otimes v)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}-\frac{1}{2}\bigg\|\sum_{j}w_{j}\otimes z_{j}\bigg\|^{2}_{\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathit{T}^{\otimes 2}{\sf X})}\bigg\},\end{split}

(4.3.8)

where the sup is taken among all finite collections of vector fields
w_{j},z_{j}\in{\rm{Test}}\mathit{V}({\sf X})
, and over all finite collections of functions
g_{j,\ell},h_{j,\ell}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
such that
z_{\ell}=\sum_{j}h_{j,\ell}\nabla g_{j,\ell}
(
[
12
, Theorem 3.4.2]
).

Hence, arguing similarly as in the proof of Proposition
4.9
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
we denote by
(A_{i}^{n})
a Borel partition of
{\sf Y}
made of at most countable sets such that
0<\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A_{i}^{n})<\infty
,
\text{diam}(A_{i}^{n})\leq 1/n
and with the property that
(A^{n+1}_{i})
is a refinement of
(A^{n}_{i})
. Then we define
v^{n}_{i}:=\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A_{i}^{n})^{-1}\int_{A_{i}^{n}}v_{\sf x}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y)\in L^{2}(T{\sf X})
, and
v^{n}:=\sum_{i}\chi_{A_{i}^{n}}(y)\Phi_{\sf x}(v_{i}^{n})\in L^{2}(T({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
. It is clear that
(v^{n})
converges to
(v_{\sf x},0)
in
L^{2}(\mathit{T}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
as
n\to\infty
, thus from the lower semicontinuity of
{\sf E}_{C,{\sf x}}
on
{\sf X}
we easily get

\int{\sf E}_{C,{\sf x}}(v_{\sf x})\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y)\leq\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\int{\sf E}_{C,{\sf x}}(v^{n}_{\sf x})\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y).

(4.3.9)

At this point, we fix
n,i\in\mathbb{N}
and pick a finite family of vector fields
w_{j},z_{j}=\sum_{\ell}h_{j,\ell}\nabla g_{j,\ell}\in{\rm{Test}}V({\sf X})
in the identity (
4.3.8
) written for
v_{i}^{n}
in place of
v
. Thus, for every fixed
j,\ell
and
t>0
, we define
h_{j,\ell,t}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
by posing

h_{j,\ell,t}:={{\sf h}}_{t}\big(\chi_{{\sf X}\times A_{i}^{n}}h_{j,\ell}\circ\pi_{\sf X}\big)\eta\circ\pi_{\sf Y}\overset{\eqref{eq:tenscal2}}{=}{{\sf h}}_{t}^{\sf X}(h_{j,\ell})\otimes\big({{\sf h}}_{t}^{\sf Y}(\chi_{A_{i}^{n}})\eta\big),

for a function
\eta\in{\rm TestF}({\sf Y})
with bounded support and identically equal to
1
on
A_{i}^{n}
. Thus
(\lvert h_{j,\ell,t}-h_{j,\ell}\rvert),(\lvert{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}h_{j,\ell,t}-{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}h_{j,\ell}\rvert)
are uniformly bounded and converge to
0
in
L^{2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
as
t\downarrow 0
. Therefore, the dominate convergence theorem, the identities (
3.1.4
) and (
4.2.13
), and the closure of the differential give that, as
t\downarrow 0
,

\begin{split}\int-&\left<v_{\sf x},\nabla_{\sf x}g_{j,\ell}\right>{\rm div}(h_{j,\ell,t}w_{j})-h_{j,\ell,t}{\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(g_{j,\ell})(w_{j},v_{\sf x})\circ\pi_{\sf X}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\\
&{\to}\quad\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A_{i}^{n})\int-\left<v_{\sf x},\nabla_{\sf x}g_{j,\ell}\right>{\rm div}_{\sf x}(h_{j,\ell}w_{j})-h_{j,\ell}{\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(g_{j,\ell})(w_{j},v_{\sf x})\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\end{split}

(4.3.10)

for every fixed
j,\ell
. In a similar way we get that

\int\left<T,h_{j,\ell,t}\nabla_{\sf x}g_{j,\ell}\otimes w_{j}\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\,\overset{t\downarrow 0}{\longrightarrow}\,\int_{{\sf X}\times A_{i}^{n}}\left<T,h_{j,\ell}\nabla_{\sf x}g_{j,\ell}\otimes w_{j}\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).

(4.3.11)

Moreover, directly from
ii)
, we have that

\begin{split}\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\sum_{j,\ell}\int-&\left<v_{\sf x},\nabla_{\sf x}g_{j,\ell}\right>{\rm div}(h_{j,\ell,t}w_{j})-h_{j,\ell,t}{\rm Hess}_{\sf x}(g_{j,\ell})(w_{j},v_{\sf x})\circ\pi_{\sf X}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\\
&\quad=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\int\langle T,w_{j}\otimes\sum_{j,\ell}h_{j,\ell,t}\nabla_{\sf x}g_{j,\ell}\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:Thj}}{=}\int\langle\chi_{{\sf X}\times A_{i}^{n}}T,\sum_{j}w_{j}\otimes z_{j}\rangle\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})\\
&\quad\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{{\sf X}\times A_{i}^{n}}\lvert T\rvert_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})+\tfrac{1}{2}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A_{i}^{n})\Big\|\sum_{j}w_{j}\otimes z_{j}\Big\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\sf X})}\end{split}

where in the last step follows from Young’s inequality. This inequality, the fact that the choice of
w_{j}
and
z_{j}
is arbitrary, (
4.3.10
) and (
4.3.8
) provide

\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(A_{i}^{n}){\sf E}_{C,{\sf x}}(v_{i}^{n})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{{\sf X}\times A_{i}^{n}}\lvert T\rvert^{2}_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).

Finally, adding up over
i
and letting
n\to\infty
, thanks to (
4.3.9
), we get

\int{\sf E}_{C,{\sf x}}(v_{\sf x})\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}(y)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{{\sf X}\times{\sf Y}}\lvert T\rvert_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}^{2}\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}),

which is
i)
.

\square

The following is a natural variant of Lemma
4.10
:

Lemma 4.17
.

Let
({\sf X},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X})
and
({\sf Y},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be
{\sf RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces, and
v\in W_{C}^{1,2}(\mathit{T}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
. Then the identity

\int h\nabla_{C}v:(\nabla g_{1}\otimes\nabla g_{2})\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})=-\int\left<v,\nabla g_{1}\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla g_{2})-h{\rm Hess}(g_{2})(v,\nabla g_{1})\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})

(4.3.12)

holds for any
h\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{\sf bs}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
and
g_{1},g_{2}\in\{g\circ\pi_{\sf X}:g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})\}\cup\{g\circ\pi_{\sf Y}:g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf Y})\}
.

Proof.

First of all, let us consider the case in which
h\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
has bounded support, and let
\eta\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
be with bounded support and such that
\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(h)\subset\{\eta=1\}
, whose existence is guaranteed by (
4.1.8
). Then for
g_{1},g_{2}\in\{g\circ\pi_{\sf X}:g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})\}\cup\{g\circ\pi_{\sf Y}:g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf Y})\}
, we have that
\eta g_{1},\eta g_{2}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
, and the validity of (
4.3.12
) follows from the definition of Covariant Derivative and the locality property of the differential and the Hessian, which ensures that
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\times\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e. on
\mathop{\rm supp}\nolimits(h)
it holds
\nabla(\eta g_{i})=\nabla g_{i}
,
i=1,2
and
{\rm Hess}(\eta g_{2})={\rm Hess}(g_{2})
.

At this point, in order to get the conclusion, we take
h\in\mathop{\rm Lip}\nolimits_{\sf bs}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
,
\eta
as before and
(h_{n})\subset{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
a sequence
W^{1,2}
-converging to
h
. Then
\eta h_{n}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\eta h_{n}\to\eta h=h
in
W^{1,2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
. Since (
4.3.12
) holds with
\eta h_{n}
and the functions have uniformly bounded support, we can pas to the limit in
n
, and get the claim.

\square

We are now ready to prove the main result of the section:

Theorem 4.18
.

Let
({\sf X},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X})
and
({\sf Y},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be
{\sf RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces, and
v=(v_{\sf x},v_{\sf y})\in W_{C}^{1,2}(\mathit{T}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
. Then

i)

for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
it holds
v_{\sf x}(\cdot,y)\in W^{1,2}_{C}(\mathit{T}{\sf X})
with
\iint\lvert\nabla_{C,{\sf x}}(v_{\sf x})\rvert^{2}_{{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}<\infty
,

ii)

for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
-a.e.
x\in{\sf X}
it holds
v_{\sf y}(x,\cdot)\in W^{1,2}_{C}(\mathit{T}{\sf Y})
with
\iint\lvert\nabla_{C,{\sf y}}(v_{\sf y})\rvert^{2}_{{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}<\infty
,

iii)

we have
y\mapsto v_{\sf x}(\cdot,y)\in W^{1,2}({\sf Y};\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathit{T}{\sf X}))
and
x\mapsto v_{\sf y}(x,\cdot)\in W^{1,2}({\sf X};\mathit{L}^{2}(\mathit{T}{\sf Y}))
.

In particular, the identities (
4.3.2
) and (
4.3.3
) hold.

Proof.

Point
i)
is a direct consequence of Lemma
4.17
with
g_{1},g_{2}\in\{g\circ\pi_{\sf X}:g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})\}
together with Proposition
4.16
. In the same way we get also
ii)
.

In order to prove
iii)
, we start observing that directly from (
4.3.12
) it holds

\int h\nabla_{C}v:(\nabla g_{1}\otimes\nabla g_{2})+h{\rm Hess}(g_{2})(v,g_{1})\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})=-\int\left<v,\nabla g_{1}\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla g_{2})\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})

for
g_{1},g_{2}\in\{g\circ\pi_{\sf X}:g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})\}\cup\{g\circ\pi_{\sf Y}:g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf Y})\}
, and
h\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
with bounded support. Thus, if we take
g_{1}=g\circ\pi_{\sf X}
and
g_{2}=\tilde{g}\circ\pi_{\sf Y}
, with
g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})
and
\tilde{g}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf Y})
respectively, and we take into account Lemma
4.11
, we get

\int h\nabla_{C}v:(\nabla(g\circ\pi_{\sf X})\otimes\nabla(\tilde{g}\circ\pi_{\sf Y}))\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})=-\int\left<v,\nabla(g\circ\pi_{\sf X})\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla(\tilde{g}\circ\pi_{\sf Y}))\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).

Recalling Proposition
2.2
and denoting by
T
the restriction of the Covariant Derivative of
v
to
\Phi_{\sf x}(L^{2}({\sf Y};L^{2}(\mathit{T}{\sf X})))\otimes\Phi_{\sf y}(L^{2}({\sf X};L^{2}(\mathit{T}{\sf Y})))\subset L^{2}(T^{\otimes 2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
, we obtain

\int h\left<T,{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\otimes{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf y}\tilde{g}\right>\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})=-\int\left<v_{\sf x},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g\right>{\rm div}(h\nabla(\tilde{g}\circ\pi_{\sf Y}))\,{\mathrm{d}}(\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}\otimes\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}).

(4.3.13)

By now we have proven the validity of (
4.3.13
) for
\tilde{g}\in{\rm TestF}({\sf Y})
and

with bounded support: a standard approximation argument involving the heat flow and the multiplication with a test cut-off function with bounded support allows to conclude that actually this equality holds for

and
\tilde{g}\in D(\Delta_{\sf y})
.

Since
\{{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}g:g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X})\}
generates

, we can then apply Proposition
3.14
to conclude that

with

. An analogous argument applies to the case
{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf x}v_{\sf y}
.

At this point we have that
i),ii),iii)
in Proposition
4.15
hold true, and this in particular means that the identities (
4.3.2
) and (
4.3.3
) are valid.

\square

We conclude this section providing an analogous of Proposition
4.13
: we prove that if the vector field
v=(v_{\sf x},v_{\sf y})
in the previous statement is assumed to be in
H_{C}^{1,2}(\mathit{T}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
, then also the components are in the respective
H_{C}^{1,2}
spaces.

Proposition 4.19
.

Let
({\sf X},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf X},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X})
and
({\sf Y},{\mathrm{d}}_{\sf Y},\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y})
be two
{\sf RCD}(K,\infty)
spaces, and
v=(v_{\sf x},v_{\sf y})\in H_{C}^{1,2}(\mathit{T}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
. Then in addition to the conclusions of Theorem
4.18
above it holds:

i’)

v_{\sf x}(\cdot,y)\in H^{1,2}_{C}(\mathit{T}{\sf X})
for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
,

ii’)

v_{\sf y}(x,\cdot)\in H^{1,2}_{C}(\mathit{T}{\sf Y})
for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
-a.e.
x\in{\sf X}
.

Proof.

We start claiming that for
f,g\in{\rm TestF}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
if we consider functions
\{f_{n,t}\},\{g_{n,t}\}\subset{\rm{Test}}_{\times}\mathrm{F}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
parametrized by
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t>0
as given by Lemma
4.5
we have that

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n,t}\nabla g_{n,t}=f\nabla g\qquad\textrm{ in }W^{1,2}_{C}(T({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})).

(4.3.14)

Indeed we have

\begin{split}\|f_{n,t}\nabla g_{n,t}-f\nabla g\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|f_{n,t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla(g_{n,t}-g)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\lvert\nabla g\rvert\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f_{n,t}-f\|_{L^{2}}\end{split}

and
\sup_{n,t}\|f_{n,t}\|_{L^{\infty}}<\infty
by
(i)
of Lemma
4.5
,
\lim_{t}\lim_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f_{n,t}-f\|_{L^{2}}=0
by
(iii)
of Lemma
4.5
and
\lim_{t}\lim_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla(g_{n,t}-g)\|_{L^{2}}=0
by
(iv)
of Lemma
4.5
and the continuity in
W^{1,2}
of the heat flow. This proves that the limit in (
4.3.14
) holds in
L^{2}(T({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
. To obtain that it holds in
W^{1,2}_{C}(T({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
, taking into account the identity
\nabla(f\nabla g)=\nabla f\otimes\nabla g+f{\rm Hess}g^{\sharp}
, we see that it remains to prove that

\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\lim_{n\to\infty}\Big\|\Big(\nabla f_{n,t}\otimes\nabla g_{n,t}+f_{n,t}(\mathrm{Hess}(g_{n,t}))^{\sharp}\Big)-\Big(\nabla f\otimes\nabla g+f(\mathrm{Hess}(g))^{\sharp}\Big)\Big\|_{L^{2}(T^{\otimes 2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))}=0,

(4.3.15)

The convergence of
\nabla f_{n,t}\otimes\nabla g_{n,t}
to
\nabla f\otimes\nabla g
in
L^{2}(T^{\otimes 2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
is ensured by
iv)
in Lemma
4.5
, (
4.1.4
), and a diagonalization argument. For the other term we notice that (
4.1.10
) together with
iii)
and
iv)
in Lemma
4.5
gives that
{\rm Hess}g_{n,t}\to{\rm Hess}{{\sf h}}_{t}g
in
L^{2}((T^{*})^{\otimes 2}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
as
n\to\infty
for any
t>0
. In particular, for every
t>0
the sequence
|{\rm Hess}g_{n,t}-{\rm Hess}{{\sf h}}_{t}g|_{\lower 0.90417pt\hbox{\scriptsize{\sf HS}}}
is dominated in
L^{2}
and this fact together with
L^{2}
-convergence of
(f_{n,t})
to
{{\sf h}}_{t}f
, the uniform
L^{\infty}
-bound on these functions and an application of the dominated convergence theorem gives

\|(f_{n,t}-{{\sf h}}_{t}f)(\mathrm{Hess}(g_{n,t})-\mathrm{Hess}({{\sf h}}_{t}g))\|_{L^{2}}\to 0

as
n\to\infty
for every
t>0
. It is then clear that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|f_{n,t}\mathrm{Hess}(g_{n,t})-{{\sf h}}_{t}f\mathrm{Hess}({{\sf h}}_{t}g)\|_{L^{2}}=0.

A similar line of thought gives
\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\|{{\sf h}}_{t}f\mathrm{Hess}({{\sf h}}_{t}g)-f\mathrm{Hess}(g)\|_{L^{2}}=0
hence (
4.3.15
), and thus (
4.3.14
), follows.

From this claim and the definition of
H^{1,2}_{C}(T({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
it follows that for
v\in H^{1,2}_{C}(T({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
there is a sequence
(v_{n})
converging to
v
in
W^{1,2}_{C}(T({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
so that each
v_{n}
is of the form
\sum f_{i}\nabla g_{i}
for
f_{i},g_{i}\in{\rm{Test}}_{\times}\mathrm{F}({\sf X}\times{\sf Y})
. Up to pass to a subsequence and using the bounds
|v|\geq|v_{\sf x}|
and
|\nabla_{C}v|\geq|\nabla_{C,{\sf x}}f_{\sf x}|
(for the latter recall (
4.3.2
)) we can assume that for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
we have
v_{n,{\sf x}}(y)\to v_{\sf x}(y)
in
W^{1,2}({\sf X})
as
n\to\infty
(recall that the component
v_{\sf x}
of a vector field
v\in L^{2}(T({\sf X}\times{\sf Y}))
belongs to
L^{2}({\sf Y};L^{2}(T{\sf X}))
) and similarly that
v_{n,{\sf y}}(x)\to v_{\sf y}(x)
in
W^{1,2}({\sf Y})
for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
-a.e.
x\in{\sf X}
.

The conclusion then follows from the observation that vectors
v_{n}
as those considered are such that
v_{n,{\sf x}}(y)\in{\rm{Test}}\mathit{V}({\sf X})
for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Y}
-a.e.
y\in{\sf Y}
and, similarly,
v_{n,{\sf y}}(x)\in{\rm{Test}}\mathit{V}({\sf Y})
for
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf X}
-a.e.
x\in{\sf X}
.

\square

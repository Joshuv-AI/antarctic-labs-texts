Recovering Initial States in Semilinear Parabolic Problems from Time-Averages
Lina Sophie Schmitz, Christoph Walker

Abstract
. Well-posedness of certain semilinear parabolic problems with nonlocal initial conditions is shown in time-weighted spaces. The result is applied to recover the initial states in semilinear parabolic problems with nonlinearities of superlinear behavior near zero from small time-averages over arbitrary time periods.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35K58,35Q99

1.
Introduction

The problem of recovering the unknown initial state
u(0,x)
in linear uniformly parabolic diffusion equations

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\mathrm{div}\big(d(x)\nabla u\big)+c(x)u

\displaystyle=f(t,x)\,,\qquad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\Omega\,,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=0\,,\qquad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\partial\Omega\,,

(1.1b)

from time-averages

a\,u(T,x)+\int_{0}^{T}b(t)\,u(t,x)\,\mathrm{d}t=M(x)\,,\quad x\in\Omega\,,

(1.1c)

is addressed in
[
9
]
in the Hilbert space setting of
L_{2}(\Omega)
. Here,
T\in(0,\infty)
is a fixed given time. Paraphrasing the main result of
[
9
, Theorem 1]
it is shown therein that this problem admits a unique weak solution
u
for each given
M\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap\mathring{H}^{1}(\Omega)
provided that
a\geq 0
,
b\geq 0
with
\mathrm{essinf}_{t\in[0,t_{0}]}b(t)>0
for some
t_{0}\in(0,T)
, and that
f
is a (suitable)
L_{2}
-function.
Well-posedness in an
L_{p}
-setting of a linear fractional diffusion equation with Riemann–Liouville derivative including a time-average condition like (
1.1c
) is established in
[
14
]
.

We shall consider herein strong solutions to general abstract semilinear problems with nonlinear functions
f=f(u,t)
not restricting to a Hilbert space framework. More precisely, we focus our attention on semilinear parabolic problems

u^{\prime}=Au+f(u,t)\,,\quad t\in(0,T]\,,\qquad a\,u(T)+\int_{0}^{T}b(t)\,u(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=M\,,

(1.2)

involving a generator
A
of an analytic semigroup on a Banach space
E_{0}
, a nonlinear function
f=f(u,t)
, and a nontrivial weight function
b
. Roughly speaking, we shall show that for Lipschitz continuous functions
f
(depending locally or nonlocally on
u
) with superlinear behavior near zero one can recover the (unique) initial state from small values of time-averages over arbitrary time periods. That is, problem (
1.2
) is well-posed for small values of
M
. The smallness condition is due to the fact that
T>0
is
a priori
fixed and one thus seeks for global solutions to a nonlinear problem. Since
b=0
yields an ill-posed final value problem (in the sense that solutions exist only for particular smooth data), we restrict to the case
b\not=0
.

A conceptually different situation than (
1.2
) occurs if initial and final values are related

u^{\prime}=Au+f(u,t)\,,\quad t\in(0,T]\,,\qquad u(0)-bu(T)=M\,,

(1.3)

or if the initial value is related to nonlocal time-averages

u^{\prime}=Au+f(u,t)\,,\quad t\in(0,T]\,,\qquad u(0)+\int_{0}^{T}b(t)\,u(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=M\,.

(1.4)

Results regarding solvability, uniqueness, and regularity of solutions to the corresponding linear cases of (
1.3
)-(
1.4
) with
f
independent of
u
are derived in Hilbert space settings in
[
5
,
6
,
7
]

and for nonlinear problems in
[
17
]
(see also the references therein). In
[
5
,
6
,
8
]
also the connection to stochastic differential equations is exploited. Moreover, for recent results regarding final value problems for semilinear (and linear) equations with fractional diffusion and Riemann–Liouville derivative or conformable time derivative we refer e.g. to
[
15
,
4
,
16
,
18
]
and the references therein.

Despite the conceptual differences, we shall treat problems (
1.2
), (
1.3
), and (
1.4
) simultaneously; that is, as special cases of the following semilinear problem with nonlocal initial condition

u^{\prime}=Au+f(u,t)\,,\quad t\in(0,T]\,,\qquad u(0)=\Sigma_{T}(u)\,,

(1.5)

where
\Sigma_{T}(u)
is a (nonlocal) function of
u
adapted to the specific problem. Under suitable conditions on the nonlinearities
f
and
\Sigma_{T}
we shall prove that (
1.5
) is globally well-posed and implies the well-posedness of each of the problems (
1.2
), (
1.3
), and (
1.4
).
As we shall later see, in the context of problem (
1.2
) the initial condition
\Sigma_{T}(u)
belongs merely to the ambient space
E_{0}
and a corresponding solution can thus only be continuous at
t=0
in the norm of
E_{0}
. In order to put only mild conditions on the nonlinearity
f
when investigating problem (
1.5
) with
\Sigma_{T}(u)\in E_{0}
, this necessitates the use of a time-weighted space as a phase space for solutions. Such spaces have been used in various research papers in different contexts, e.g. see
[
13
,
12
,
20
]
and the references therein.

2.
Main Results

Notations and Assumptions

Throughout this paper we assume that
E_{0}
and
E_{1}
are Banach spaces such that
E_{1}
is dense and continuously embedded in
E_{0}
. Moreover, we fix

A\in\mathcal{H}(E_{1},E_{0})\,,

(2.1)

that is,
A
is the generator of an analytic semigroup
(e^{tA})_{t\geq 0}
on
E_{0}
with domain
E_{1}
.

Given
\theta\in(0,1)
, let
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta}
be an arbitrary admissible interpolation functor of
exponent
\theta
(see e.g.
[
3
, I.Section 2.11]
) and set
E_{\theta}:=(E_{0},E_{1})_{\theta}
for the corresponding interpolation space with norm
\|\cdot\|_{\theta}
.

Given
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
we denote by
C_{\mu}((0,T],E_{\theta})
the Banach space of all functions

u\in C((0,T],E_{\theta})
such that

t^{\mu}u(t)\rightarrow 0
in
E_{\theta}
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
, equipped with the norm

u\mapsto\|u\|_{C_{\mu}((0,T],E_{\theta})}:=\sup\left\{t^{\mu}\,\|u(t)\|_{\theta}\,:\,t\in(0,T]\right\}\,.

In the following, we assume that there are

\gamma\in[0,1]\,,\qquad\theta\in(0,1]\,,\qquad(\gamma,\theta)\not=(0,1)\,,\qquad\nu\in[0,1)\,,\qquad\ell>0\,,

(2.2a)

for which

f:C_{\theta}\big((0,T],E_{\theta}\big)\to C_{\nu}\big((0,T],E_{\gamma}\big)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Sigma_{T}:C_{\theta}\big((0,T],E_{\theta}\big)\to E_{0}

are Lipschitz mappings satisfying

\|f(v)-f(w)\|_{C_{\nu}((0,T],E_{\gamma})}\leq c_{T}\,L^{\ell}\,\|v-w\|_{C_{\theta}((0,T],E_{\theta})}\,,\qquad f(0)=0\,,

(2.2b)

and

\|\Sigma_{T}(v)-\Sigma_{T}(w)\|_{0}\leq c_{T}\,L^{\ell}\,\|v-w\|_{C_{\theta}((0,T],E_{\theta})}

(2.3)

for
L\in(0,1)
and
v,w\in C_{\theta}((0,T],E_{\theta})
with

\|v\|_{C_{\theta}((0,T],E_{\theta})}+\|w\|_{C_{\theta}((0,T],E_{\theta})}\leq L\,,\qquad\sup_{t\in(0,T]}\big(\|v(t)\|_{0}+\|w(t)\|_{0}\big)\leq L\,,

and where
c_{T}>0
is a suitable constant.
We use the notation

f(u,t):=f(u)(t)\,,\qquad t\in(0,T]\,,\quad u\in C_{\theta}\big((0,T],E_{\theta}\big)\,.

Moreover, we write
\mathcal{L}(E,F)
for the space of bounded linear operators between two Banach spaces
E
and
F
, while
\mathcal{L}is(E,F)
stands for the subspace of isomorphisms. By
\mathcal{K}(E)
we denote the subspace of
\mathcal{L}(E):=\mathcal{L}(E,E)
consisting of compact operators.

Under these assumptions and notations we can state the main results regarding the general problem (
1.5
) as well as its specifications
(
1.2
)-(
1.4
)
.

Main Result for the General Problem (
1.5
)

Regarding problem (
1.5
) with an a priori given final time
T>0
we prove the following well-posedness result for small values of
\Sigma_{T}(0)
:

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
T>0
. Assume (
2.1
), (
2.2
), and (
2.3
). Then there is
m(T)>0
such that (
1.5
) has a unique global mild solution

u\in C\big([0,T],E_{0}\big)\cap C_{\theta}\big((0,T],E_{\theta}\big)

provided that
\|\Sigma_{T}(0)\|_{0}\leq m(T)
. Moreover, if
\gamma>0
, then

u\in C^{1}\big((0,T],E_{0}\big)\cap C\big((0,T],E_{1}\big)

is a strong solution to (
1.5
).

If
T>0
is chosen small, one can relax assumption (
2.2b
) in the sense that the condition
f(0)=0
is not required. We state this explicitly:

Remark 2.2
.

If
f(0)\not=0
there are
T_{0}>0
and
m(T_{0})>0
such that for every
T\in(0,T_{0})
with
\|\Sigma_{T}(0)\|_{0}\leq m(T_{0})
there is a unique mild solution

u\in C\big([0,T],E_{0}\big)\cap C_{\theta}\big((0,T],E_{\theta}\big)

to (
1.5
) that is a strong solution if additionally
\gamma>0
. The proof is along the lines of the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

It is worth emphasizing that assumption (
2.2
) on the nonlinearity
f=f(u)
includes the case of a nonlocal dependence on
u
with respect to time (see Section
7
for such examples). Nevertheless, the result also includes the particular case of local time dependence that we state explicitly for completeness. To this end, assume that there are

\ell>0\,,\qquad 0<\theta<\frac{1}{\ell+1}\,,

(2.4a)

such that
f:E_{\theta}\to E_{0}

satisfies

\|f(v)-f(w)\|_{0}\leq c\left(\|v\|_{\theta}^{\ell}+\|w\|_{\theta}^{\ell}\right)\|v-w\|_{\theta}\,,\qquad v,w\in E_{\theta}\,,\qquad f(0)=0\,,

(2.4b)

for some constant
c>0
.
With the understanding
f(u,t)=f(u(t))
we then have for this local case:

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
T>0
. Assume (
2.1
), (
2.3
), and (
2.4
). Then there is
m(T)>0
such that (
1.5
) has a unique global strong solution

u\in C^{1}\big((0,T],E_{0}\big)\cap C\big((0,T],E_{1}\big)\cap C_{\theta}\big((0,T],E_{\theta}\big)\cap C\big([0,T],E_{0}\big)

provided that
\|\Sigma_{T}(0)\|_{0}\leq m(T)
.

Note that growth conditions like (
2.4b
) are common in the context of time-weighted spaces
[
13
,
12
,
20
]
. Also note that we obtain in Corollary
2.3
strong solutions even though
f
maps only into
E_{0}
.

Applications to Initial State Recovering Problems

In order to apply Theorem
2.1
to
problem (
1.2
), we formulate the latter in the form of (
1.5
). To this end, suppose that
u
is a mild solution to (
1.2
) for a given
M\in E_{1}
; that is,

u(t)=e^{tA}u(0)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}f(u,s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\qquad t\in[0,T]\,.

(2.5)

Plugging this into the nonlocal condition

au(T)+\int_{0}^{T}b(t)\,u(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=M

yields

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=a\,e^{TA}u(0)+a\int_{0}^{T}e^{(T-s)A}f(u,s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{T}b(t)e^{tA}u(0)\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{0}^{T}b(t)\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}f(u,s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

Hence, introducing the notion

\Phi_{T}:=a\,e^{TA}+\int_{0}^{T}b(t)\,e^{tA}\,\mathrm{d}t\in\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{1})

(2.6)

and

\Psi_{T}g:=a\int_{0}^{T}e^{(T-s)A}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{T}b(t)\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

(2.7)

we (formally) obtain that
u(0)
is given by

u(0)=\Phi_{T}^{-1}\big(M-\Psi_{T}(f(u))\big)\,.

(2.8)

That is,
u
is a mild solution to (
1.2
) if and only if
u
is a mild solution to (
1.5
) with

\Sigma_{T}(u)=\Phi_{T}^{-1}\big(M-\Psi_{T}(f(u))\big)\,.

(2.9)

We will investigate properties of the operators
\Phi_{T}
and
\Psi_{T}
later on. In fact, in order to guarantee the invertibility of
\Phi_{T}
under an assumption that is easy to check in applications (see Proposition
4.1
and (
2.11
) below)
we assume that

\text{$E_{1}$ is compactly embedded into $E_{0}$}\,.

(2.10)

Then we can prove the following well-posedness result for problem (
1.2
):

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
T>0
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, and
b\in C^{1}([0,T],\mathbb{R})
with
b(0)\not=0
be given. Suppose (
2.1
), (
2.2
) with
\gamma\in(0,1]
, and suppose (
2.10
). Moreover, assume that the kernel of

\Phi_{T}\in\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{1})
defined in (
2.6
)
is trivial, that is,

\mathrm{ker}(\Phi_{T})=\{0\}\,.

(2.11)

Then, there is
m(T)>0
such that, for each
M\in E_{1}
with
\|M\|_{1}\leq m(T)
, problem (
1.2
) has a unique strong solution

u\in C^{1}\big((0,T],E_{0}\big)\cap C\big((0,T],E_{1}\big)\cap C\big([0,T],E_{0}\big)\cap C_{\theta}\big((0,T],E_{\theta}\big)\,.

Before continuing let us remark the following about the linear case when
f
is independent of
u
:

Remarks 2.5
.

(a)
In the linear case that
f
is independent of
u
, there is no smallness condition required for
M\in E_{1}
in order to obtain the existence of a unique solution to problem (
1.2
) since (
2.8
) determines the initial state
u(0)
uniquely for each
M\in E_{1}
. We therefore provide an alternative proof of
[
9
, Theorem 1]
(in fact, we prove a more general result). Also note that parabolic regularity enforces
M\in E_{1}
since
\Phi_{T}
maps into
E_{1}
(see Proposition
4.1
).

(b)
In
[
9
]
, also conditions of the form

au(T)+b\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}u(t)\,\mathrm{d}t=M

with
0<\varepsilon<T
are considered, for which a similar result as in Theorem
2.4
can be obtained along the same lines.

Let us also comment on the premises of Theorem
2.4
.

Remark 2.6
.

The condition
b(0)\not=0
is also imposed in
[
9
]
for the corresponding linear problem (see Condition 1 therein) and guarantees that certain information on the initial state is contained in the weighted time-average. Together with condition (
2.11
) it implies that
\Phi_{T}\in\mathcal{L}is(E_{0},E_{1})
, see Proposition
4.1
. If
A
represents a symmetric, elliptic differential operator, condition (
2.11
) is not difficult to verify, see Corollary
6.3
and Corollary
6.4
in Section
6
.

In the particular case that
a=0
and
b=const\not=0
, condition (
2.11
) is implied by

\sigma(A)\cap\frac{2\pi i}{T}\mathbb{Z}=\emptyset

owing to the fact that

A\Phi_{T}z=b\int_{0}^{T}Ae^{tA}z\,\mathrm{d}t=b\big(e^{TA}-1\big)z\,,\quad z\in E_{0}\,,

and
\sigma\big(e^{TA}\big)\setminus\{0\}=e^{T\sigma(A)}
by the spectral mapping theorem
[
10
, V.Corollary 2.10]
.

As pointed out in the introduction, our result also applies to problem (
1.3
) and problem (
1.4
). The statements are similar, we thus combine them in one theorem:

Theorem 2.7
.

Assume (
2.1
) and (
2.10
).
Let
T>0
and let
f
satisfy (
2.2
).

(i)

For problem (
1.3
) assume
b\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
and the spectral condition

1\notin b\,e^{T\sigma(A)}\,.

(2.12)

(ii)

For problem (
1.4
) assume
b\in C([0,T],\mathbb{R})
with
b\not=0
and the spectral condition

-1\notin\sigma_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{T}b(t)\,e^{tA}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)\,.

(2.13)

Then, there is
m(T)>0
such that, for each
M\in E_{0}
with
\|M\|_{0}\leq m(T)
, problem (
1.3
) respectively problem (
1.4
) has a unique mild solution

u\in C\big([0,T],E_{0}\big)\cap C_{\theta}\big((0,T],E_{\theta}\big)\,.

In addition, if
\gamma>0
or if
f
satisfies (
2.4
), then

u\in C^{1}\big((0,T],E_{0}\big)\cap C\big((0,T],E_{1}\big)

is a strong solution.

Remark 2.8
.

The spectral condition (
2.13
) is satisfied whenever the spectral radius of the operator
\int_{0}^{T}b(t)e^{tA}\,\mathrm{d}t\in\mathcal{K}(E_{0})

is strictly less than 1. For instance, this is the case if
T>0
is small enough. For concrete examples not assuming
T>0
to be small see Corollary
6.3
and Corollary
6.4
in Section
6
.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section
3
we prove Theorem
2.1
by a fixed point argument and thus establish the well-posedness of the general problem (
1.5
). Subsequently, in Section
4
we demonstrate how to apply these findings to problem (
1.2
) by deriving the invertibility of the operator
\Phi_{T}
defined in (
2.6
). This yields then Theorem
2.4
. In Section
5
we show how to deduce Theorem
2.7
by applying Theorem
2.1
to problems (
1.3
) and (
1.4
). We then consider more concrete situations. We provide in Section
6
examples for operators
A
satisfying the spectral conditions imposed in the previous theorems. In particular, examples include symmetric uniformly elliptic differential operators of second or fourth order, see Corollary
6.3
respectively Corollary
6.4
. In Section
7
we give examples for nonlinearities
f
that obey assumption (
2.2
). Finally, in Section
8
we consider the semilinear version of problem (
1.1
) and illustrate how to apply our result in such a setting.

3.
Proof of Theorem
2.1
and Corollary
2.3

We focus on the general problem (
1.5
); that is, on

u^{\prime}=Au+f(u,t)\,,\quad t\in(0,T]\,,\qquad u(0)=\Sigma_{T}(u)\,.

First, we prove Theorem
2.1
and then derive Corollary
2.3
as a consequence.

Proof of Theorem
2.1

In order to prove Theorem
2.1
, we assume (
2.1
), (
2.2
), and (
2.3
).
We introduce the complete metric space

\mathcal{V}_{L}:=\big\{v\in C_{\theta}\big((0,T],E_{\theta}\big)\cap C\big([0,T],E_{0}\big)\,;\,\|v\|_{C_{\theta}((0,T],E_{\theta})}+\|v\|_{C([0,T],E_{0})}\leq L\big\}

with
L\in(0,1)
to be determined later and aim at deriving a fixed point of the mapping
\Lambda
defined by

\Lambda(u)(t):=e^{tA}\Sigma_{T}(u)+\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)A}f(u,s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,,\qquad t\in[0,T]\,,\quad u\in\mathcal{V}_{L}\,.

Such a fixed point corresponds to a (mild) solution to (
1.5
). The existence of a fixed point will be guaranteed by Banach’s fixed point theorem once we have established that
\Lambda:\mathcal{V}_{L}\to\mathcal{V}_{L}
is a contraction (for
L
chosen suitably).

We first recall from
[
3
, II. Lemma 5.1.3]
the estimates

\|e^{tA}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\vartheta})}+t^{\alpha-\beta_{0}}\,\|e^{tA}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\beta},E_{\alpha})}\leq\omega(T)\,,\qquad 0\leq t\leq T\,,

(3.1)

for
\vartheta\in[0,1]
and
0\leq\beta_{0}\leq\beta\leq\alpha\leq 1

with
\beta_{0}<\beta
if
0<\beta<\alpha<1
, where
\omega(T)>1
depends also on these parameters. We put
\gamma_{0}:=0
if
\gamma=0
and otherwise chose
\gamma_{0}\in\big(0,\min\{\gamma,\theta\}\big)
arbitrarily.

Let
u\in\mathcal{V}_{L}
and
0<t\leq T
. Since (
2.2b
) and (
2.3
) imply

\|f(u,t)\|_{\gamma}\leq c_{T}\,L^{\ell+1}\,t^{-\nu}

(3.2)

and

\|\Sigma_{T}(u)\|_{0}\leq c_{T}\,L^{\ell+1}+\|\Sigma_{T}(0)\|_{0}\,,

(3.3)

respectively, we readily obtain from
\Sigma_{T}(u)\in E_{0}
that
\Lambda(u)\in C\big([0,T],E_{0}\big)
. Moreover, using (
3.1
),
\nu<1
, and denoting by
c_{\gamma}
the norm of the embedding
E_{\gamma}\hookrightarrow E_{0}
we deduce

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(u)(t)\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{tA}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}\,\|\Sigma_{T}(u)\|_{0}+c_{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{(t-s)A}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0})}\,\|f(u,s)\|_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\omega(T)\,\big\{c_{T}\,L^{\ell+1}+\|\Sigma_{T}(0)\|_{0}\big\}+c_{\gamma}\,\omega(T)c_{T}\,L^{\ell+1}\,T^{1-\nu}\,.

(3.4)

Since, due to (
3.1
),

\displaystyle t^{\theta}\|e^{tA}\Sigma_{T}(u)\|_{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq t^{\theta}\|e^{tA}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\theta})}\,\|\Sigma_{T}(u)-u_{0}\|_{0}+t^{\theta}\|e^{tA}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\theta})}\,\|u_{0}\|_{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq\omega(T)\big(\|\Sigma_{T}(u)-u_{0}\|_{0}+t^{\theta}\|u_{0}\|_{\theta}\big)

for
t\in(0,T]
and each
u_{0}\in E_{\theta}
, we may use the density of
E_{\theta}
in
E_{0}
and
\theta\in(0,1]
to derive that

\displaystyle\big[t\mapsto e^{tA}\Sigma_{T}(u)\big]\in C_{\theta}\big((0,T],E_{\theta}\big)

(3.5)

with

\|e^{\cdot A}\Sigma_{T}(u)\|_{C_{\theta}((0,T],E_{\theta})}\leq\omega(T)\,\|\Sigma_{T}(u)\|_{0}\leq\omega(T)\big\{c_{T}\,L^{\ell+1}+\,\|\Sigma_{T}(0)\|_{0}\big\}\,,

where we used (
3.3
) for the second inequality.
Recall that
\theta<1+\gamma_{0}
and
\nu<1
in view of (
2.2a
). Hence, using (
3.1
) and (
3.2
), we estimate

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(u)(t)-e^{tA}\Sigma_{T}(u)\|_{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{(t-s)A}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\gamma},E_{\theta})}\,\|f(u,s)\|_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\omega(T)\,c_{T}\,L^{\ell+1}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\gamma_{0}-\theta}\,s^{-\nu}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\omega(T)\,c_{T}\,L^{\ell+1}\,t^{1+\gamma_{0}-\theta-\nu}\,\mathsf{B}(1+\gamma_{0}-\theta,1-\nu)

with
\mathsf{B}
denoting the Beta function. Consequently, since
1+\gamma_{0}-\nu>0
and using (
3.5
), we derive that
\Lambda(u)\in C_{\theta}\big((0,T],E_{\theta}\big)
with

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(u)\|_{C_{\theta}((0,T],E_{\theta})}

\displaystyle\leq\omega(T)\big\{c_{T}\,L^{\ell+1}+\,\|\Sigma_{T}(0)\|_{0}\big\}

\displaystyle\quad+\omega(T)c_{T}\,L^{\ell+1}\,T^{1+\gamma_{0}-\nu}\,\mathsf{B}(1+\gamma_{0}-\theta,1-\nu)\,.

(3.6)

Likewise, for

,

, we obtain from (

) and (

)

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(u)(t)-\Lambda(v)(t)\|_{\theta}

\displaystyle\leq\|e^{tA}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{0},E_{\theta})}\,\|\Sigma_{T}(u)-\Sigma_{T}(v)\|_{0}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{(t-s)A}\|_{\mathcal{L}(E_{\gamma},E_{\theta})}\,\|f(u,s)-f(v,s)\|_{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\omega(T)t^{-\theta}\,c_{T}\,L^{\ell}\,\|u-v\|_{C_{\theta}((0,T],E_{\theta})}

\displaystyle\quad+\omega(T)\,c_{T}\,L^{\ell}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\gamma_{0}-\theta}\,s^{-\nu}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\|u-v\|_{C_{\theta}((0,T],E_{\theta})}

and therefore

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(u)

\displaystyle-\Lambda(v)\|_{C_{\theta}((0,T],E_{\theta})}

\displaystyle\leq\omega(T)\,c_{T}\,L^{\ell}\,\big[1+T^{1+\gamma_{0}-\nu}\,\mathsf{B}(1+\gamma_{0}-\theta,1-\nu)\big]\,\|u-v\|_{C_{\theta}((0,T],E_{\theta})}\,.

(3.7)

A similar but simpler estimate based on (

) and (

\displaystyle\|\Lambda(u)-\Lambda(v)\|_{C([0,T],E_{0})}\leq\omega(T)\,c_{T}\,L^{\ell}\,\Big[1+\frac{c_{\gamma}\,T^{1-\nu}}{1-\nu}\Big]\,\|u-v\|_{C_{\theta}((0,T],E_{\theta})}\,.

Since

, we deduce from (

) that there is

such that

is a contraction whenever

. Hence, Banach’s fixed point theorem ensures the existence of a unique

with

. Clearly,

is a mild solution to (
1.5
).

Finally, we show that

is a strong solution if

. For each

, the function

with

. Since

,

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose (

Fix

[

is an isomorphism on

\Phi_{T}=(A-\mu)^{-1}(A-\mu)\Phi_{T}\in\mathcal{L}is(E_{0},E_{1})

∎

For

we note:

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume (
2.1
) and let

,

\displaystyle\|\Psi_{T}g\|_{1}

Proof of Theorem

The Hilbert Space Case

In the Hilbert space case one may use Fourier series so that the spectral conditions take a particular form as has been observed in

Let

\begin{split}&\text{$A\leq\alpha<\infty$ be a closed, densely defined, self-adjoint operator}\\
&\text{on the Hilbert space $\big(E_{0},(\cdot|\cdot)\big)$ with compact resolvent},\end{split}

and similarly for
\Phi_{T}^{0}
from (

from which we readily derive the kernel and spectral conditions:

Assume (

Elliptic Differential Operators

To consider more specifically elliptic differential operators, let

be a bounded smooth domain with outer unit normal

8.

)

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open and bounded (sufficiently smooth) domain. We consider the semilinear version of problem (

)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\mathrm{div}\big(d(x)\nabla u\big)+c(x)u

\displaystyle=g(t,x,u)\,,\qquad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\Omega\,,

(8.1a)

\displaystyle(1-\delta)u+\delta\partial_{\nu}u

\displaystyle=0\,,\qquad(t,x)\in(0,T]\times\partial\Omega\,,

\displaystyle a\,u(T)+\int_{0}^{T}b(t)\,u(t)\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=M\,,

(8.1c)

\begin{split}&d\in C^{1}\big(\bar{\Omega},\mathbb{R}_{sym}^{n\times n}\big)\ \text{ with }\ d(x)\zeta\cdot\zeta\geq\underline{d}\,|\zeta|^{2}\,,\quad(\zeta,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\bar{\Omega}\,,\\
&c\in C(\bar{\Omega},\mathbb{R}^{+})\ \text{ with \ $\min_{\bar{\Omega}}c>0$ if $\delta=1$}\,,\\

\displaystyle g\in C^{2}\big([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}\big)\,,\qquad g(t,x,0)=0\,,\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\,,

\displaystyle|g(t,x,v)-g(t,x,w)|\leq c\,\big(|v|^{\ell}+|w|^{\ell}\big)\,|v-w|\,,\quad\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\,,\quad v,w\in\mathbb{R}\,.

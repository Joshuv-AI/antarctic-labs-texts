R R -matrix for the XX spin chain at the special imaginary value of staggered magnetic field
P. N. Bibikov

Abstract
It is pointed, that the 16 × 16 16\times 16 Hamiltonian density matrix, corresponding to XX spin chain in a staggered magnetic field, satisfies the Reshetikhin condition as well, as the two higher ones. All of them are necessary for the existence of the corresponding R R -matrix. At the special imaginary value of the staggered field, the R R -matrix is obtained explicitly. It is shown that the corresponding Hamiltonian has purely imaginary spectrum.



The XX model, suggested in
[
1
]
, and related to the Hamiltonian

\hat{H}=\sum_{n}H_{n,n+1},

(1)

where

H_{n,n+1}=\frac{1}{2}\Big({\bf S}^{+}_{n}{\bf S}^{-}_{n+1}+{\bf S}^{-}_{n}{\bf S}^{+}_{n+1}\Big),

(2)

is now one of the most studied integrable spin chains (see
[
2
]
and references therein). Here
{\bf S}^{j}_{n}
(
j=+,-,z
) are the spin-1/2 operators
associated with the
n
-th site of the chain.

The integrability of (
1
), (
2
) has been first demonstrated within the Jordan-Wigner transformation to the free-fermion model and then
confirmed by various alternative approaches (see
[
2
]
and references therein). Namely, the Hamiltonian density
4\times 4
matrix

H=\frac{1}{2}\Big({\bf S}^{+}\otimes{\bf S}^{-}+{\bf S}^{-}\otimes{\bf S}^{+}\Big),

(3)

related to (
2
), may be represented in the form

H=\frac{1}{2}\frac{dR(\lambda)}{d\lambda}\Big|_{\lambda=0},

(4)

where the
R
-matrix

R(\lambda)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\cos{\lambda}&0&0&0\\
0&1&\sin{\lambda}&0\\
0&\sin{\lambda}&1&0\\
0&0&0&\cos{\lambda}\end{array}\right),

(5)

satisfies the Yang-Baxter equation (in the Braid group form)
[
4
]

R_{12}(\lambda-\mu)R_{23}(\lambda)R_{12}(\mu)=R_{23}(\mu)R_{12}(\lambda)R_{23}(\lambda-\mu),

(6)

(as usual,
R_{12}(\lambda)\equiv R(\lambda)\otimes I_{2}
and
R_{23}(\lambda)\equiv I_{2}\otimes R(\lambda)
) and the initial condition

R(0)=I_{4}.

(7)

Here by
I_{M}
we denote the
M\times M
identity matrix.

Within the slightly modified version of the
[
1
]
approach, it has been proved
[
3
]
, that the model (
1
), (
2
) remains integrable even under addition of a staggered longitudinal magnetic field, when

H_{n,n+1}(h_{\rm st})=\frac{1}{2}\Big[{\bf S}^{+}_{n}{\bf S}^{-}_{n+1}+{\bf S}^{-}_{n}{\bf S}^{+}_{n+1}+h_{\rm st}\Big((-1)^{n}{\bf S}^{z}_{n}+(-1)^{n+1}{\bf S}^{z}_{n+1}\Big)\Big],

(8)

instead of (
2
). Namely, with the use of the Fermi operators representation

{\bf S}^{+}_{n}=\prod_{m<n}\sigma_{m}a_{n},\qquad{\bf S}^{-}_{n}=\prod_{m<n}\sigma_{m}a_{n}^{\dagger},\qquad{\bf S}^{z}_{n}=\frac{1}{2}[a_{n},a_{n}^{\dagger}]=\frac{1}{2}-a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n},

(9)

where
\sigma_{n}\equiv 1-2a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}
and
\{a_{m},a_{n}\}=0
, and
\{a_{m},a_{n}^{\dagger}\}=\delta_{mn}
, the Hamiltonian (
1
), (
2
)
takes the well tractable bilinear form

\hat{H}(h_{\rm st})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n}\Big[a_{n+1}^{\dagger}a_{n}+a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n+1}-h_{\rm st}\Big((-1)^{n}a_{n}^{\dagger}a_{n}+(-1)^{n+1}a_{n+1}^{\dagger}a_{n+1}\Big)\Big],

(10)

which, under the substitutions
a_{2n}\equiv b_{n}
, and
a_{2n+1}\equiv\tilde{b}_{n}
, turns into

\hat{H}(h_{\rm st})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n}\Big(\tilde{b}_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n}+b_{n}^{\dagger}\tilde{b}_{n}+b_{n+1}^{\dagger}\tilde{b}_{n}+\tilde{b}_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n+1}+h_{\rm st}(2\tilde{b}_{n}^{\dagger}\tilde{b}_{n}-b_{n}^{\dagger}b_{n}-b_{n+1}^{\dagger}b_{n+1})\Big).

(11)

With the use of the Fourier representations

b_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}dk{\rm e}^{-ikn}c_{1}(k),\qquad\tilde{b}_{n}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}dk{\rm e}^{-ikn}c_{2}(k),

(12)

where

\{c_{j}^{\dagger}(k),c_{l}(p)\}=\delta_{jl}\delta(k-p),\qquad\{c_{j}(k),c_{l}(p)\}=0,

(13)

(
10
) is reduced to the form

\hat{H}(h_{\rm st})=\int_{0}^{2\pi}dk\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}c_{1}^{\dagger}(k)&c_{2}^{\dagger}(k)\end{array}\right)M(k,h_{\rm st})\left(\begin{array}[]{c}c_{1}(k)\\
c_{2}(k)\end{array}\right),

(14)

where

M(k,h_{\rm st})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-2h_{\rm st}&1+{\rm e}^{ik}\\
1+{\rm e}^{-ik}&2h_{\rm st}\end{array}\right).

(15)

As it has been shown in
[
3
]
, for real
h_{\rm st}
the Hamiltonian (
14
) may be diagonalized by unitary transformation, so that the model (
1
), (
2
) is integrable.

It is desirable to confirm this result by presentation of the corresponding
R
-matrix.
Since (
8
) is invariant only under even translations (
H_{n,n+1}=H_{n+2,n+3}\neq H_{n+1,n+2}
), the approach based on a
4\times 4
matrix needs a modification.
Namely, one should pass from (
3
) to the
16\times 16
matrix, corresponding to the representation

\hat{H}(h_{\rm st})=\sum_{n}{\cal H}_{2n,2n+3}(h_{\rm st}),

(16)

more general than (
1
). Here

{\cal H}_{2n,2n+3}(h_{\rm st})=\frac{1}{2}\Big(H_{2n,2n+1}(h_{\rm st})+H_{2n+2,2n+3}(h_{\rm st})\Big)+H_{2n+1,2n+2}(h_{\rm st}),

(17)

and the corresponding
16\times 16
Hamiltonian density matrix is

{\cal H}(h_{\rm st})=\frac{1}{2}\Big(H^{(+)}(h_{\rm st})\otimes I_{4}+I_{4}\otimes H^{(+)}(h_{\rm st})\Big)+I_{2}\otimes H^{(-)}(h_{\rm st})\otimes I_{2},

(18)

where

H^{(\pm)}(h_{\rm st})=\frac{1}{2}\Big[{\bf S}^{+}\otimes{\bf S}^{-}+{\bf S}^{-}\otimes{\bf S}^{+}\pm h_{\rm st}\Big({\bf S}^{z}\otimes I_{2}-I_{2}\otimes{\bf S}^{z}\Big)\Big].

(19)

The direct calculation shows, that the matrix (
18
) satisfies the Reshetikhin condition
[
4
]

[{\cal H}(h_{\rm st})\otimes I_{4}+I_{4}\otimes{\cal H}(h_{\rm st}),[{\cal H}(h_{\rm st})\otimes I_{4},I_{4}\otimes{\cal H}(h_{\rm st})]]=I_{4}\otimes{\cal K}-{\cal K}\otimes I_{4},

(20)

(where the
16\times 16
matrix
{\cal K}
may be readily calculated).

According to (
20
), the sum

\hat{Q}=\sum_{n}[{\cal H}_{2n-2,2n+1}(h_{\rm st}),{\cal H}_{2n,2n+3}(h_{\rm st})],

(21)

is the first integral of motion

[\hat{H},\hat{Q}]=0.

(22)

In fact,
\hat{Q}
does not depend on
h_{\rm st}
and may be reduced to the form

\hat{Q}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n}[H_{n-1,n},H_{n,n+1}].

(23)

Really, following (
17
),

[{\cal H}_{2n-2,2n+1}(h_{\rm st}),{\cal H}_{2n,2n+3}(h_{\rm st})]=\frac{1}{2}\Big([H_{2n-1,2n}^{(-)},H^{(+)}_{2n,2n+1}]+[H^{(+)}_{2n,2n+1},H^{(-)}_{2n+1,2n+2}]\Big).

(24)

At the same time,

\displaystyle[H^{(+)}_{2n,2n+1},H^{(-)}_{2n+1,2n+2}]=[H_{2n,2n+1},H_{2n+1,2n+2}]+h_{\rm st}[{\bf S}^{z}_{2n+1},H_{2n,2n+1}-H_{2n+1,2n+2}],

\displaystyle[H_{2n-1,2n}^{(-)},H_{2n,2n+1}^{(+)}]=[H_{2n-1,2n},H_{2n,2n+1}]+h_{\rm st}[{\bf S}^{z}_{2n},H_{2n,2n+1}-H_{2n-1,2n}].

(25)

Since
[{\bf S}^{z}_{n}+{\bf S}^{z}_{n+1},H_{n,n+1}]=0
, the substitution of (
25
) into (
24
) gives

\displaystyle[{\cal H}_{2n-2,2n+1}(h_{\rm st}),{\cal H}_{2n,2n+3}(h_{\rm st})]=\frac{1}{2}\Big([H_{2n-1,2n},H_{2n,2n+1}]+[H_{2n,2n+1},H_{2n+1,2n+2}]\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{h_{\rm st}}{4}\Big([{\bf S}^{z}_{2n-1}-{\bf S}^{z}_{2n},H_{2n-1,2n}]-[{\bf S}^{z}_{2n+1}-{\bf S}^{z}_{2n+2},H_{2n+1,2n+2}]\Big).

(26)

Under the substitution of (
26
) into (
21
), the terms proportional to
h_{\rm st}
are canceled, and one gets (
23
).

It may be readily proved by direct calculations, that additionally to (
20
) the Hamiltonian density matrix satisfies two higher order integrability conditions, presented in
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
.
Both of them also are necessary (but not sufficient) for representation of
{\cal H}(h_{\rm st})
in the form (
4
), but with the
16\times 16

R
-matrix
{\cal R}(\lambda,h_{\rm st})
, which
satisfies as the Yang-Baxter equation (
6
), as the initial condition (
7
) (with
I_{4}
replaced by
I_{16}
).

As it has been suggested in
[
6
]
, and shown in
[
7

]

R

16

(27)

and for both of them

{\cal R}_{ji}={\cal R}_{ij},\qquad{\cal R}_{17-i,17-j}=\bar{\cal R}_{ij},

\displaystyle{\cal R}_{16,16}={\cal R}_{11},\quad{\cal R}_{15,15}={\cal R}_{99},\quad{\cal R}_{14,14}={\cal R}_{55},

\displaystyle{\cal R}_{12,12}={\cal R}_{33},\quad{\cal R}_{10,10}={\cal R}_{77},\quad{\cal R}_{8,8}={\cal R}_{22},

\displaystyle{\cal R}_{14,15}={\cal R}_{59},\quad{\cal R}_{10,11}={\cal R}_{7,11},\quad{\cal R}_{10,13}={\cal R}_{7,13},

Let us study the case
h_{\rm st}=i
in more details. Introducing the operators

\displaystyle c_{+}(k)=\frac{c_{1}(k)+\gamma c_{2}(k)}{\sqrt{1+|\gamma|^{2}}},\qquad c_{-}(k)=\frac{\bar{\gamma}c_{1}(k)+c_{2}(k)}{\sqrt{1+|\gamma|^{2}}},\qquad\gamma\equiv\frac{1+{\rm e}^{ik}}{2i(1-\sin{k/2})},

(36)

and using the identities

|\gamma|^{2}=\frac{\cos^{2}{k/2}}{(1-\sin{k/2})^{2}},\qquad 1+|\gamma|^{2}=\frac{2}{1-\sin{k/2}},\qquad 1-|\gamma|^{2}=-\frac{2\sin{k/2}}{1-\sin{k/2}},

(37)

one readily gets from (
13
) and (
14
)

\{c_{\pm}^{\dagger}(k),c_{\pm}(p)\}=\delta(k-p),\qquad\{c_{+}^{\dagger}(k),c_{-}(p)\}=i\frac{1+{\rm e}^{-ik}}{2}\delta(k-p),

(38)

and

\displaystyle\hat{H}(i)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{dk}{\sin{k/2}}\Big[i(c_{+}^{\dagger}(k)c_{+}(k)-c_{-}^{\dagger}(k)c_{-}(k))

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Big((1+{\rm e}^{ik})c_{+}^{\dagger}(k)c_{-}(k)+(1+{\rm e}^{-ik})c_{-}^{\dagger}(k)c_{+}(k)\Big)\Big].

(39)

According to (
38
) and (
39
),

[\hat{H}(i),c^{\dagger}_{\pm}(k)]=\pm i\sin{\frac{k}{2}}c^{\dagger}_{\pm}(k),

(40)

and

\displaystyle[\hat{H}(i),c_{+}(k)]=\frac{1}{\sin{k/2}}\Big[-i\Big(1+\cos^{2}{\frac{k}{2}}\Big)c_{+}(k)+(1+{\rm e}^{ik})c_{-}(k)\Big],

\displaystyle[\hat{H}(i),c_{-}(k)]=\frac{1}{\sin{k/2}}\Big[(1+{\rm e}^{-ik})c_{+}(k)+i\Big(1+\cos^{2}{\frac{k}{2}}\Big)c_{-}(k)\Big].

(41)

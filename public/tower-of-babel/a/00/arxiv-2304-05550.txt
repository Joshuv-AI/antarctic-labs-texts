Sign-changing solution for an overdetermined elliptic problem on unbounded domain Thanks: Research was supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grants No. 12371110 and No. 12301133).
Guowei Dai, Yong Zhang

Abstract
We prove the existence of two smooth families of unbounded domains in ℝ N + 1 \mathbb{R}^{N+1} with N ≥ 1 N\geq 1 such that − Δ ​ u = λ ​ u ​ in ​ Ω , u = 0 , ∂ ν u = const ​ on ​ ∂ Ω -\Delta u=\lambda u\,\,\text{in}\,\,\Omega,\,\,u=0,\,\,\partial_{\nu}u=\text{const}\,\,\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega admits a sign-changing solution. The domains bifurcate from the straight cylinder B 1 × ℝ B_{1}\times\mathbb{R} , where B 1 B_{1} is the unit ball in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} . These results can be regarded as counterexamples to the Berenstein conjecture on unbounded domain. Unlike most previous papers in this direction, a very delicate issue here is that there may be two-dimensional kernel space at some bifurcation point. Thus a Crandall-Rabinowitz type bifurcation theorem from high-dimensional kernel space is also established to achieve the goal.

Berenstein conjecture.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded
C^{2,\alpha}
domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
. If there exists a nontrivial
solution

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta u+\lambda u=0&\text{in}\,\,\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}u=\text{const}&\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

then
\Omega
is a ball, where
\nu

For

3

proved that the existence of infinitely many eigenvalues for (

being a disk, which was extended
to the Poincare disk with the hyperbolic metric by Berenstein and Yang

In
[
19

, Liu proved that the Berenstein
conjecture holds if and only if the second order interior normal derivative of the corresponding
Dirichlet eigenfunction

is constant on the boundary of
\Omega
.

The other long standing open problem considered in
[
4
,
5
]
, which is very similar to (
1.1

Schiffer Conjecture.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded regular domain. Assume
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

is a solution to the problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta u+\lambda u=0&\text{in}\,\,\Omega,\\
u=1&\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}u=0&\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

Then
\Omega
is a ball and
u
is radially symmetric.

The Schiffer conjecture is closely connected with Pompeiu problem
[
22
,
23
]
.
Let
\Omega
be a nonempty bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with
N\geq 2
and let
\mathcal{M}
denote the set of rigid
motions of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
onto itself. The domain
\Omega
is said to have the Pompeiu property if and only if
f\equiv 0

\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the only continuous function satisfying

\int_{\sigma(\Omega)}f(x)\,\text{d}x=0

for every
\sigma\in\mathcal{M}
. The Pompeiu problem originated from harmonic analysis, which consists in characterizing the class of domains in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the Pompeiu property. It has been proved
[
38

that the problem (
1.2

\lambda>0
if and only if the smooth
\Omega
with
\partial\Omega
connected fails to possess the Pompeiu property. The Schiffer conjecture has also been included in Yau’s famous list of problems
[
39

. So far, there are few results about this conjecture. In
[
4
,
5
]
, Berenstein and Yang found that the existence of infinitely many eigenvalues to (

must be a round ball. Recently, Fall, Minlend and Weth
[

constructed a nontrivial family of compact subdomains of the flat cylinder
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
such that (
1.2

On the other hand, if we only take the positive solutions of (
1.1
) into consideration, which is strongly related with Berestycki-Caffarelli-Nirenberg (BCN) conjecture
[
6

. It is well known that many positive answers to the BCN conjecture have been given in bounded domain by using the so-called moving plane method. Assume that
\Omega

or
\mathbb{R}^{3}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta u+\lambda u=0&\text{in}\,\,\Omega,\\
u>0&\text{in}\,\,\Omega,\\
u=0&\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega,\\
\partial_{\nu}u=\text{const}&\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

That is to say if (

must be half-space, which can be seen as a confirm answer to BCN or Berenstein conjecture on unbounded domain. The first counterexample to BCN conjecture on unbounded domain was constructed by Sicbaldi

via showing that the cylinder

with
N\geq 2
can be perturbed to an unbounded domain whose boundary is a periodic
hypersurface of revolution with respect to the

-axis and such that problem (

) has a bounded solution, where

is the unit ball of

centered on the origin.
Subsequently, Schlenk and Sicbaldi
[

further proved that the above conclusion is valid for

and these new extremal domains belong to a smooth bifurcation family of domains. Nontrivial domain emanating from the half-space has been obtained in
[

]
in

, and bifurcating from the complement of a ball has been given in
[

These results on positive solutions have enriched counterexamples to the BCN conjecture on unbounded domain. The natural question is whether there is a nontrivial unbounded region on which (
1.1
) has nonsymmetric sign-changing solution, which means that we can construct a counterexample to the Berenstein conjecture on an unbounded region. The main contribution of this paper is to give a confirm answer to the question as follows.

Theorem 1.1.

Let

\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}_{\text{even},0}\left(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\right)

be the space of even
2\pi
-periodic
\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}
functions of mean zero.
For each

there exists a positive number
T_{*}
with

T_{*}\in\left(\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda_{2}}},\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}}\right),

where
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are the first and second eigenvalues of the zero-Dirichlet Laplacian on the unit ball, and a smooth map

(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\rightarrow\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}_{\text{even},0}\left(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\right)\times\mathbb{R}:s\mapsto\left(w_{s},T_{s}\right)

with
w_{0}=0
,
T_{0}=T_{*}
and such that for each
s\in(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
, problem (
1.1
) has a sign-changing
T_{s}
-periodic solution

u_{s}\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}\left(\Omega_{s}\right)
on the modified cylinder

\Omega_{s}=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}:|x|<1+s\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{T_{s}}t\right)+sw_{s}\left(\frac{2\pi}{T_{s}}t\right)\right\}.

Moreover, there exists a unique
T_{s}
-periodic function
r\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}\left(\mathbb{R}\right)
such that

r(t):\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\left(0,1+s\cos\left(\frac{2\pi}{T_{s}}t\right)+sw_{s}\left(\frac{2\pi}{T_{s}}t\right)\right)

and
u_{s}(r(t),t)=0
.

From Theorem 1.1, we see that the obtained solution is sign-changing and the bifurcation point
T_{*}
is bounded from the above. In addition, we also find another bifurcation point
T^{*}
which may be corresponding to high-dimensional kernel space.

Theorem 1.2.

Under the assumptions of Theorem 1.1, there exist a positive number
T^{*}
with

T^{*}>\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}}

and a smooth map

Remark 1.3.

For
N=1
, in Section 5, we will find that
T_{*}=4/3
and
T^{*}=4\sqrt{5}/5
by an exact computation, which implies that
\beta=1
and
\gamma=0
in this case. However, for
N\geq 2
,
T^{*}
may be the integer multiple of
T_{*}
, which gives that
\beta
,
\gamma
are nonzero constants with
\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}=1
. That is to say, the corresponding kernel space is exactly two-dimensional,
which leads to that the classical Crandall-Rabinowitz bifurcation theorem cannot be used directly. Therefore, we also establish a new Crandall-Rabinowitz type bifurcation theorem with high-dimensional kernel space in Section 4.

As shown in Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2, the main goal of the present paper
is to construct a nontrivial unbounded domain such that problem (
1.1
) admits a sign-changing solution. We choose the eigenfunction corresponding to the second eigenvalue of zero-Dirichlet Laplacian on the unit ball as the trivial sign-changing solution. In the spirit of local bifurcation, we successfully achieve this aim. As far as we know, this is a new way to construct the sign-changing solution to the overdetermined problems. This method may be applied in other settings, such as other operators, domains in Riemannian Manifolds. However, there may be some new difficulties that need to be overcome with more effort.
Here the Bessel functions play a crucial role. For other operators or domains in Riemannian Manifolds, Bessel functions may not be used directly.
Perhaps studying other hypergeometric functions (such as the Legendre functions) can work for investigating domains in Riemannian Manifolds.
By establishing the relationship between eigenvalues and the results of this paper, the expected properties of other operators may be derived. These questions are very interesting to be considered in future.

We also like to mention the recent work

2
Preliminaries

The main strategy of this paper is to apply Crandall-Rabinowitz bifurcation theorem.
The first step is to transform the problem (
1.1
) into an abstract operator equation.
To achieve this goal, we show two elementary results on zero-Dirichlet Laplacian eigenvalue problems on the unit ball and the cylinder in the following.

2.1
Some results on eigenvalue problems

We first consider the following eigenvalue problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta u+\lambda u=0&\text{in}\,\,B_{1},\\
u=0&\text{on}\,\,\partial B_{1}.\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

It is well known (see
[
9
,
17
]
or

[
37
, p. 269]
) that problem (
2.1
) possesses a sequence eigenvalues
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\cdots<\lambda_{k}\nearrow+\infty
for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Let
\overline{\phi}_{k}
be the corresponding radial eigenfunction
to
\lambda_{k}
with
\int_{B_{1}}\overline{\phi}_{k}^{2}\,\text{d}x=1/\left(2\pi\right)
and
\overline{\phi}_{k}(0)>0
.
From now on, we use
C
or
C_{k}
to denote positive constant whose exact value may change from line to line.

In particular, when
N=1
, we have that

\lambda_{k}=\frac{(2k-1)^{2}\pi^{2}}{4}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\overline{\phi}_{k}(r)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\cos\left(\frac{(2k-1)\pi}{2}r\right).

While, for
N=3
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1.

When
N=3
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
, one has that

\lambda_{k}=k^{2}\pi^{2}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,C_{k}\overline{\phi}_{k}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\sin(k\pi r)}{r}&\text{if}\,\,r>0,\\
k\pi&\text{if}\,\,r=0,\end{array}\right.

where
C_{k}
is chosen such that
\int_{B_{1}}\overline{\phi}_{k}^{2}\,\text{d}x=1/\left(2\pi\right)
.

Proof.
Let

f(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}r)}{r}&\text{if}\,\,r>0,\\
\sqrt{\lambda}&\text{if}\,\,r=0.\end{array}\right.

Then, for
r>0
, we have that

f^{\prime}(r)=\sqrt{\lambda}\frac{\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}r)}{r}-\frac{\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}r)}{r^{2}}

and

f^{\prime\prime}(r)=\lambda\frac{-\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}r)}{r}-2\sqrt{\lambda}\frac{\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}r)}{r^{2}}+2\frac{\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}r)}{r^{3}}.

Hence we have that

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{2}{r}f^{\prime}(r)+\lambda f(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\frac{-\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}r)}{r}-2\sqrt{\lambda}\frac{\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}r)}{r^{2}}+2\frac{\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}r)}{r^{3}}

\displaystyle+2\sqrt{\lambda}\frac{\cos(\sqrt{\lambda}r)}{r^{2}}-2\frac{\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}r)}{r^{3}}+\lambda\frac{\sin(\sqrt{\lambda}r)}{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Further, we can verify that
f^{\prime}(0)=0
.
Combining this with the boundary condition, we obtain that

\lambda_{k}=k^{2}\pi^{2}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,C_{k}\overline{\phi}_{k}(r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\sin(k\pi r)}{r}&\text{if}\,\,r>0,\\
k\pi&\text{if}\,\,r=0.\end{array}\right.

for
r\in[0,1]
.∎

It is well known that, for the one-dimensional case, the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions can be calculated by explicit expressions, which indicates that the Weyl’s asymptotic formula holds
[
7
]
.
The conclusion of Lemma 2.1 shows that, for the three-dimensional case, the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions can still be calculated by explicit expressions, which plays an important role in our subsequent argument. This result also indicates that the equality holds in the Weyl’s asymptotic formula (in fact, the radial situation can be understood as one-dimensional problem) to
three-dimensional radial eigenvalue problem.

Note that
\left(\lambda_{k},{\phi}_{k}(x,t)\right)
with
{\phi}_{k}(x,t)=\overline{\phi}_{k}(x)
is also a solution pair of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{\mathring{g}}{\phi}+\lambda{\phi}=0&\text{in}\,\,C_{1}^{T},\\
{\phi}=0&\text{on}\,\,\partial C_{1}^{T},\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

where
\mathring{g}
is the Euclidean metric and

C_{1}^{T}=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z}:|x|<1\right\}.

That is to say, the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of (
2.1
) are also the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of (
2.2
).
Clearly, we see that

\int_{C_{1}^{2\pi}}{\phi}_{k}^{2}\,\text{d}\text{vol}_{\mathring{g}}=1.

Since
\phi_{k}
does not depend on
t
and is radial, we will denote
\phi_{k}(x,t)
by
\phi_{k}(r)
with
r=|x|
.

2.2
Rephrasing the problem

We now transform the aim problem into some abstract operator equation.
For each
v\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}_{\text{even},0}\left(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}\right)
with
v>-1
, define

C_{1+v}^{T}=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z}:|x|<1+v\left(\frac{2\pi t}{T}\right)\right\}

for all
T>0
.
We consider the following eigenvalue problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{\mathring{g}}{\phi}+\lambda{\phi}=0&\text{in}\,\,C_{1+v}^{T},\\
{\phi}=0&\text{on}\,\,\partial C_{1+v}^{T}.\end{array}\right.

(2.3)

It follows from
[
2
, Theorem 1.13]
that the problem (
2.3
) possesses a sequence eigenvalues

0<\lambda_{1,v}<\lambda_{2,v}\leq\cdots,\lambda_{k,v}\nearrow+\infty.

Let
{\phi}_{2,v}
be an eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda_{2,v}
such that

\int_{C_{1+v}^{2\pi}}{\phi}_{2,v}^{2}\left(x,\frac{T}{2\pi}t\right)\,\text{dvol}_{\mathring{g}}=1,

where

\partial C_{1+v}^{T}=\left\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z}:|x|=1+v\left(\frac{2\pi t}{T}\right)\right\}.

Applying
[
15
, Theorem 11.4]
, we see that
\phi_{2,v}\in\mathcal{C}^{2,\alpha}\left(\overline{C_{1+v}^{T}}\right)
.
Clearly,
{\phi}_{2,v}
and
\lambda_{2,v}
depend smoothly on
v
(the smoothness can also be obtained by the Implicit Function Theorem as that of
[
29
, Proposition 4.1]
), and
{\phi}_{2,0}=\pm{\phi}_{2}
,
\lambda_{2,0}=\lambda_{2}
.
Without loss of generality, we assume
{\phi}_{2,0}={\phi}_{2}
.
For any fixed
t
, we have that
{\phi}_{2,v}
is radially symmetric with respect to the first variable.
Hence, for any fixed
t
,
{\phi}_{2,v}(r,t)
has a unique simple zero in
(0,1+v(t))
which is denoted by
r_{1}(t)
.
The Implicit Function Theorem implies that
r_{1}(t)
is
C^{2,\alpha}
in local. By the arbitrariness of

,
{\phi}_{2,v}(x,t)
is positive
in
C_{1+v}^{T,+}=\left\{(x,t)\in C_{1+v}^{T}:|x|<r_{1}(t)\right\}
and is negative in
C_{1+v}^{T,-}=\left\{(x,t)\in C_{1+v}^{T}:|x|>r_{1}(t)\right\}
.
That is to say
r_{1}(t)
is the unique zero line of
{\phi}_{2,v}(r,t)
.

Define the operator

\mathcal{N}(v,T)=\mathring{g}\left(\nabla{\phi}_{v},\vartheta\right)\Big|_{\partial C_{1+v}^{T}}-\frac{1}{\text{Vol}_{\mathring{g}}\left(\partial C_{1+v}^{T}\right)}\int_{\partial C_{1+v}^{T}}\mathring{g}\left(\nabla{\phi}_{v},\vartheta\right)\,\text{dvol}_{\mathring{g}},

where
\vartheta
denotes the unit normal vector field to
\partial C_{1+v}^{T}
.
Note that
\mathcal{N}
depends only on the variable

. Thus, we can define

F(v,T)(t)=\mathcal{N}(v,T)\left(\frac{T}{2\pi}t\right).

Since
\partial_{r}{\phi}_{2}(1)
is a constant, it follows that
F(0,T)=0
for any

.
Therefore, finding nontrivial domains emanating from
B_{1}\times\mathbb{R}
such that the problem (
1.1
) has a sign-changing solution
is equivalent to study the nontrivial solutions of

.
In the spirit of Crandall-Rabinowitz local bifurcation theorem, it’s key to find the degenerate point of linearization operator of

and verify the transversality condition.
To study the linearization operator of
F
with respect to
v
at point
(0,T)
,
we first consider the following equation

\left(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{N-1}{r}\partial_{r}+\lambda_{2}\right)c-\left(\frac{2k\pi}{T}\right)^{2}c=0

(2.4)

with
c(1)=-\partial_{r}\phi_{2}(1)
and
c^{\prime}(0)=0
.

Proposition 2.1.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

•

problem (
2.4
) has a unique solution
c_{k}
if
T\neq 2k\pi/\sqrt{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}
,

•

there is no solution to problem (
2.4
) when
T=2k\pi/\sqrt{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}
.

Moreover,
c_{k}^{\prime}
is analytic if
T\neq 2k\pi/\sqrt{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}
.

Proof.
In fact, for
T=2k\pi/\sqrt{\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1}}
, we have that

\left(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{N-1}{r}\partial_{r}+\lambda_{1}\right)c=0.

Set
\vartheta(r):=c(r)+\partial_{r}\phi_{2}(1)
. Then we see that

\left(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{N-1}{r}\partial_{r}+\lambda_{1}\right)\vartheta=\lambda_{1}\phi_{2}^{\prime}(1)

(2.5)

with
\vartheta^{\prime}(0)=0
and
\vartheta(1)=0
.
Note that
\phi_{1}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(r^{N-1}\phi_{1}^{\prime}\right)^{\prime}+\lambda_{1}r^{N-1}\phi_{1}=0&\text{in}\,\,(0,1),\\
\phi_{1}^{\prime}(0)=\phi_{1}(1)=0.\end{array}\right.

Multiplying the equation by
\phi^{\prime}_{2}(1)
and integrating, we have that

\lambda_{1}\phi_{2}^{\prime}(1)\int_{0}^{1}r^{N-1}\phi_{1}\,dr=-\phi_{2}^{\prime}(1)\phi_{1}^{\prime}(1)>0.

It follows that
\lambda_{1}\phi_{2}^{\prime}(1)
is not orthogonal to

Some conclusions on Bessel functions

For

, the Bessel function of the first kind is defined by

J_{\tau}(s)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{m}\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)^{2m+\tau}}{m!\Gamma(\tau+m+1)},

which is the solution of the differential equation

s^{2}f^{\prime\prime}(s)+sf^{\prime}(s)+\left(s^{2}-\tau^{2}\right)f(s)=0.

If
\tau
is an integer, then
J_{-\tau}(s)=(-1)^{\tau}J_{\tau}(s)
.
For any

and

we have the following relations

J_{\tau-1}(s)-J_{\tau+1}(s)=2J_{\tau}^{\prime}(s),

(2.9)

sJ_{\tau}^{\prime}(s)+\tau J_{\tau}(s)=sJ_{\tau-1}(s)

(2.10)

and

sJ_{\tau}^{\prime}(s)-\tau J_{\tau}(s)=-sJ_{\tau+1}(s).

(2.11)

When
\tau
is fixed and
s\rightarrow 0
, it has that

J_{\tau}(s)\thicksim\frac{\left(\frac{s}{2}\right)^{\tau}}{\Gamma(\tau+1)}

(2.12)

for
\tau\neq-1,-2,-3,\ldots
. In addition, one has that
J_{0}(0)=1
and
J_{\tau}(0)=0
for all
\tau>0
.

It is well known that the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the Dirichlet Laplacian on the unit ball have the following relations

\lambda_{k}=j_{\nu,k}^{2}\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,|x|^{\nu}\overline{\phi}_{k}=C_{k}J_{\nu}\left(j_{\nu,k}|x|\right),

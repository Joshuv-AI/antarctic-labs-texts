Simplicial intersection homology revisited.
David Chataur ID ​, Martintxo Saralegi-Aranguren ID ​, Daniel Tanré ID ​

Abstract
. Intersection homology is defined for simplicial, singular and PL chains and it is well known that the three versions are isomorphic for a full filtered simplicial complex. In the literature, the isomorphism, between the singular and the simplicial situations of intersection homology, uses the PL case as an intermediate. Here we show directly that the canonical map between the simplicial and the singular intersection chains complexes is a quasi-isomorphism. This is similar to the classical proof for simplicial complexes, with an argument based on the concept of residual complex and not on skeletons. This parallel between simplicial and singular approaches is also extended to the intersection blown-up cohomology that we introduced in a previous work. In the case of an orientable pseudomanifold, this cohomology owns a Poincaré isomorphism with the intersection homology, for any coefficient ring, thanks to a cap product with a fundamental class. So, the blown-up intersection cohomology of a pseudomanifold can be computed from a triangulation. Finally, we introduce a blown-up intersection cohomology for PL spaces and prove that it is isomorphic to the singular one.

In this work, the simplicial complexes are not supposed to be oriented, but we use oriented simplices.
An

of a simplicial complex

of its vertices, where two orderings are equivalent if they differ by an even permutation.
The

As a simplex is determined by its vertices we denote it by

So, two writings corresponding to an even permutation of the vertices are identified.
For an odd permutation,

Let

A simplex

-

(8)

\|\sigma\|_{S}=\dim(\sigma\cap S)\leq\dim\sigma-{\rm codim\,}_{v}S+\overline{p}(S).

This condition is always satisfied when the stratum

is regular. Thus, the

\overline{p}
-allowability condition
is equivalent to the inequality,

(9)

\|\sigma\|_{S}=\dim(\sigma\cap S)\leq\dim\sigma-2-D\overline{p}(S),

for each
singular
stratum
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{K}
.
Notice that
\sigma\cap S
is a union of open faces of
\sigma
, so the dimension of
\sigma\cap S
makes sense.
A chain
c\in{C}_{{}_{*}}{(K)}
is a
\overline{p}
-
allowable chain
if any simplex with a non-zero coefficient in
c
is

\overline{p}
-allowable. It is a
\overline{p}
-
intersection chain
if
c
and
\partial c
are
\overline{p}
-allowable chains.
The complex of
\overline{p}
-intersection chains is denoted
{C}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left(K\right)}
.
The associated homology is the
simplicial
\overline{p}
-intersection homology

{H}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left(K\right)}
, or simply
the
simplicial intersection homology
if there is no ambiguity.

If
K_{n-1}=\emptyset
, then
{C}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left(K\right)}
is the usual simplicial chain complex
{C}_{{}_{*}}{(K)}
.

If
K
is full, for each
\ell\in I_{\sigma}
the number
\|\sigma\|_{S_{\ell}}

is equal to
\|\sigma\|_{\ell}=\dim(\sigma_{0}*\dots*\sigma_{\ell})
and the allowability condition (
8
) becomes,
for each
\ell\in I_{\sigma}
(i.e.,
\sigma_{\ell}\neq\emptyset
),

(10)

\|\sigma\|_{\ell}\leq\dim\sigma-(n-\ell)+\overline{p}(S_{\ell}).

There is an important difference between the complex of simplicial chains and that of
\overline{p}
-intersection chains.
The first one is a free module over the family of simplices.
This is not the case in the second context since there are
\overline{p}
-allowable simplices which are not

\overline{p}
-intersection chains.

2.1.
Relative intersection homology

Let
(K,\overline{p})
be a perverse filtered simplicial complex
and
L\subset K
be a simplicial subcomplex, endowed with the induced filtration and perversity.
The allowability condition of a simplex
\sigma\in L
can be understood in
L
itself or in
K
.
Both points of view are equivalent. Let us see that.
The simplex
\sigma
is
\overline{p}
-allowable in
K
if, and only if,

\|\sigma\|_{\ell}\leq\dim\sigma-(n-\ell)+\overline{p}(S_{\ell}),

for each
\ell\in I^{K}_{\sigma}
.
The simplex
\sigma
is
\overline{p}
-allowable in
L
if, and only if,

\|\sigma\|_{\ell}\leq\dim\sigma-(n-\ell)+\overline{p}(T_{\ell}),

for each
\ell\in I^{L}_{\sigma}
.
These conditions are equivalent since the two decompositions of
\sigma
are the same,
I^{K}_{\sigma}=I^{L}_{\sigma}
,
and
\overline{p}(S_{\ell})=\overline{p}(T_{\ell})
. The natural inclusion
L\hookrightarrow K
gives the following exact sequence

(11)

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{C}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left(L\right)}}
\textstyle{{C}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left(K\right)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\displaystyle\frac{{C}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left(K\right)}}{{C}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left(L\right)}}={C}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left(K,L\right)}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0,}

defining the
relative simplicial intersection complex

{C}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left(K,L\right)}
.
Its homology is the
relative simplicial intersection homology
denoted

{H}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left(K,L\right)}
.

2.2.
Example

The determination of the relative complex
{C}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left(K^{(\ell)},K^{(\ell-1)}\right)}
does not go through the family of
\ell
-simplices of
K
as in the classical case.
Let us see an example.

As simplicial complex
K
, we consider the suspension of a triangle
L
.
We denote
\beta_{1}
,
\beta_{2}
the apexes of the suspension.
The 2-skeleton of
K
is
K
and its 1-skeleton is the union of the edges.
This simplicial complex
K
is endowed with the filtration

\emptyset=K_{-1}\subset\{\beta_{0},\beta_{1}\}=K_{0}=K_{1}\subset K_{2}=K.

A straightforward calculation gives, for the
\overline{0}
-perversity,
{H}^{{}^{\overline{0}}}_{{}_{j}}{\left(K^{(2)},K^{(1)}\right)}=0
, for
j\neq 2
and

{H}^{{}^{\overline{0}}}_{{}_{2}}{\left(K^{(2)},K^{(1)}\right)}={\sf R}\oplus{\sf R}.

On the other hand, we have
\bigoplus_{\sigma\in K,\dim\sigma=2}{H}^{{}^{\overline{0}}}_{{}_{2}}{\left(\sigma,\partial\sigma\right)}=0
.
Therefore the formula (
1
) cannot be true.

For having a decomposition of the relative intersection homology, we must replace the skeleton by the residual complex.
Let us notice that
{\mathcal{L}}(K)=L
, which is also the link of
\beta_{0},\beta_{1}
. Here, we do have the
following decomposition, for any
j
,

{H}^{{}^{\overline{0}}}_{{}_{j}}{\left(K,{\mathcal{L}}(K)\right)}={H}^{{}^{\overline{0}}}_{{}_{j}}{\left(K,L\right)}=\bigoplus_{i=0,1}{H}^{{}^{\overline{0}}}_{{}_{j}}{\left(\beta_{i}*L,L\right)}=\bigoplus_{i=0,1}{H}^{{}^{\overline{0}}}_{{}_{j}}{\left(\beta_{i}*L,{\mathcal{L}}(\beta_{i}*L)\right)}.

This decomposition exists in the general case of a full simplicial complex, as we prove in Proposition
2.3
.
The proof proceeds by induction on the complexity.
Firstly, we need the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(K,\overline{p})
be a perverse full filtered simplicial complex.
Consider a face
\alpha
of a clot
\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}(K)
and a simplex
\varepsilon
of the link
{L_{\beta}}
.
If the stratum

containing
\beta
is singular, we have the following equivalence:

(12)

\ \alpha\ast\varepsilon\hbox{ is a $\overline{p}$-allowable simplex }\Longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dim\varepsilon\geq D\overline{p}(Q)+1\hbox{ and}\\
\varepsilon\hbox{ is a $\overline{p}$-allowable simplex. }\end{array}\right.

Proof.

We write
q=\dim_{v}Q
.
We have

\displaystyle I_{\alpha\ast\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{\ell\in\{0,\ldots,n\}\mid(\alpha\ast\varepsilon)\cap\left(|(\alpha*L_{\beta})_{\ell}|\backslash|(\alpha*L_{\beta})_{\ell-1}|\right)\neq\emptyset\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{q\}\cup\{\ell\in\{a+1,\ldots,n\}\mid\varepsilon\cap\left(|(L_{\beta})_{\ell}|\backslash|(L_{\beta})_{\ell-1}|\right)\neq\emptyset\}=\{q\}\cup I_{\varepsilon}.

So,

\displaystyle\alpha\ast\varepsilon\hbox{ is a $\overline{p}$-allowable simplex}\Longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\|\alpha\ast\varepsilon\|_{q}\leq\dim(\alpha\ast\varepsilon)-(n-q)+\overline{p}(Q)\hbox{ and}\\
\|\alpha\ast\varepsilon\|_{\ell}\leq\dim(\alpha\ast\varepsilon)-(n-\ell)+\overline{p}(S_{\ell}),\ \forall\ell\in I_{\varepsilon}\backslash\{n\},\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dim\alpha\leq\dim\alpha+\dim\varepsilon+1-(n-q)+\overline{p}(Q)\hbox{ and}\\
\dim\alpha+\|\varepsilon\|_{\ell}+1\leq\dim\alpha+1+\dim\varepsilon-(n-\ell)+\overline{p}(S_{\ell}),\ \forall\ell\in I_{\varepsilon}\backslash\{n\},\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}0\leq\dim\varepsilon+1-(n-q)+\overline{p}(Q)\hbox{ and}\\
\|\varepsilon\|_{\ell}\leq\dim\varepsilon-(n-\ell)+\overline{p}(S_{\ell}),\ \forall\ell\in I_{\varepsilon}\backslash\{n\},\end{array}\right.\Longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\dim\varepsilon\geq D\overline{p}(Q)+1\hbox{ and}\\
\varepsilon\hbox{ is a $\overline{p}$-allowable simplex. }\end{array}\right.

∎

In the case of a face
\alpha
of the clot
\beta
, we have

(16)

\alpha\hbox{ is a $\overline{p}$-allowable simplex}\Longleftrightarrow\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}Q\text{ is singular and }D\overline{p}(Q)+2\leq 0,\text{ or }\\
Q\text{ is regular.}\end{array}\right.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(K,\overline{p})
be a perverse full filtered simplicial complex. Then, we have

{C}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left(K\right)}={C}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left({\mathcal{L}}(K)\right)}+\bigoplus_{\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}(K)}{C}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left(\beta\ast L_{\beta}\right)}.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the inclusion
\subseteq
.
From (
5
), we know that any chain
c\in{C}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left(K\right)}
can be written

c=f+\sum_{\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}(K)}c_{\beta}

with
f\in{C}_{{}_{*}}{\left({\mathcal{L}}(K)\right)}
and
c_{\beta}\in{C}_{{}_{*}}{\left(\beta*L_{\beta}\right)}
.
As (see (
6
))
{\mathcal{L}}(K)\cap\bigcup_{\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}(K)}\beta*L_{\beta}=\bigcup_{\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}(K)}\partial\beta*L_{\beta}
,
we obtain a unique writing of
c
as

c=f+\sum_{\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}(K)}c_{\beta}=f+\sum_{\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}(K)}n_{\beta}\ \beta*e_{\beta}+m_{\beta}\ \beta,

with

n_{\beta},m_{\beta}\in\sf R
and
e_{\beta}\in L_{\beta}
.
Since the elements of this decomposition are independent, the
\overline{p}
-allowability of

gives the
\overline{p}
-allowability
of the chains
f
,
c_{\beta}
,
e_{\beta}
(see Lemma
2.1
).
Let
Q_{\beta}
be the stratum containing
\beta
. From Lemma
2.1
and (
16
), we
also deduce

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\dim e_{\beta}\geq D\overline{p}(Q_{\beta})+1&\text{if }n_{\beta}\neq 0,\text{ and}\\[5.69046pt]
D\overline{p}(Q_{\beta})+2\leq 0&\text{if }m_{\beta}\neq 0\text{ and }Q_{\beta}\text{ singular}.\end{array}\right.

The boundary
\partial c
of

is given by

(17)

\partial c=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}(K)}n_{\beta}\ \partial\beta*e_{\beta}+\sum_{\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}(K)}(-1)^{\dim\beta+1}n_{\beta}\ \beta*\partial e_{\beta}+\sum_{\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}(K)}m_{\beta}\ \partial\beta+\partial f.

(In the case

\partial\beta*e_{\beta}
means

, and similarly if
\dim e_{\beta}=0
.)
Following a new time Lemma
2.1
and (

), we get that each element of

) is a
\overline{p}
-allowable chain. Therefore, the chain

\sum_{\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}(K)}(-1)^{\dim\beta+1}n_{\beta}\ \beta*\partial e_{\beta}+\sum_{\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}(K)}m_{\beta}\ \partial\beta+\partial f

is
\overline{p}
-allowable and so is every element of this sum.
In short, we get

f\in{C}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left({\mathcal{L}}(K)\right)}

and

c_{\beta}\in{C}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left(\beta\ast L_{\beta}\right)}
,
for each

, which gives the claim.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
(K,\overline{p})
be a perverse full filtered simplicial complex. The inclusion maps induce an isomorphism of chain complexes,

(18)

\bigoplus_{\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}(K)}{C}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left(\beta*L_{\beta},\partial\beta*L_{\beta}\right)}\xrightarrow{\cong}{C}^{{}^{\overline{p}}}_{{}_{*}}{\left(K,{\mathcal{L}}(K)\right)}.

Proof.

Notice that, for each clot
\beta\in{\mathcal{B}}(K)
, we have

\partial\beta*L_{\beta}={\mathcal{L}}(\beta*L_{\beta})\subset{\mathcal{L}}(K)
.
(The case
\dim\beta=0
corresponds to

L_{\beta}={\mathcal{L}}(\beta*L_{\beta})\subset{\mathcal{L}}(K)
.)
Thus the inclusion maps are well defined.
The result comes from Lemma
2.2
and (
6
).
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Let us consider a filtered simplicial complex
K
with
\dim K=b<\infty
and the trivial filtration

.
So, we have

3.
Singular intersection homology

The singular version of the intersection homology goes back to King

We focus here on the singular intersection homology of the realization of a filtered simplicial complex

As in the simplicial case of Section

(cf. Proposition

Definition 3.1
.

A
filtered space
is a Hausdorff topological space
X
endowed with a filtration by closed subspaces,

X=X_{n}\supset X_{n-1}\supset\dots\supset X_{0}\supset X_{-1}=\emptyset.

The integer
n
is the dimension of
X
. The
i
-strata of
X
are the non-empty connected components of
X_{i}\backslash X_{i-1}
.
The open strata are called
regular
,
the other ones being called
singular.
The set of singular strata of
X
is denoted
{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
,
or
{\mathcal{S}}
if there is no ambiguity.
The
dimension
of a stratum
S\subset X_{i}\backslash X_{i-1}
is
\dim_{v}S=i
.
Its
codimension
is
{\rm codim\,}_{v}S=n-i
.

Given a
n
-dimensional filtered simplicial complex
K
, the associated filtration

|K|=|K_{n}|\supset|K_{n-1}|\supset|K_{n-2}|\supset\ldots\supset|K_{0}|\supset K_{-1}=\emptyset,

defines a
n
-dimensional filtered space.
By definition, there is a canonical bijection
{\mathcal{S}}_{|K|}\cong{\mathcal{S}}_{K}
.

3.1.
Induced filtered spaces

Let
X=X_{n}\supset X_{n-1}\supset X_{n-2}\supset\ldots\supset X_{0}\supset X_{-1}=\emptyset
be a filtered space. Given a subset
Y\subset X
,
the induced filtration
Y_{i}=Y\cap X_{i}
defines a filtered space structure on
Y
.
Since
Y_{i}\subset X_{i}
for any
i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
, then for any stratum
T\in{\mathcal{S}}_{Y}
there exists a unique stratum
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
with
T\subset S
.
We say that
S
is the
source
of
T
. Notice that
\dim_{v}T=\dim_{v}S
.

3.2.
Perversities

A
perversity
on a filtered space

is a map

\overline{p}\colon{\mathcal{S}}_{X}\to\overline{\mathbb{Z}}
taking the value 0 on the regular strata.
The couple
(X,\overline{p})
is a
perverse filtered space
.
Given a subset
Y\subset X
and a perversity
\overline{p}
on
X
, we also denote
\overline{p}
the perversity defined on the induced filtered space
Y
by

\overline{p}(T)=\overline{p}(S),

where
T\in{\mathcal{S}}_{Y}
and
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
is the source of
T
.

3.3.
Perverse degree

For each stratum
S\in{\mathcal{S}}_{X}
, the
perverse degree of a singular simplex
\sigma\colon\Delta\to X
along
S
is

\|\sigma\|_{S}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}-\infty&\text{if }S\cap\sigma(\Delta)=\emptyset,\\
\dim\sigma^{-1}(S)&\text{if not.}\end{array}\right.

By definition, the
dimension
of a non-empty subset
A\subset\Delta
is the smallest
s\in\mathbb{N}

for which the subset
A
is included in the
s
-skeleton of
\Delta
.

3.4.

We have all the ingredients needed for the definition of the intersection homology of a filtered space.

Definition 3.2

Let

The result is no longer true if we remove the hypothesis “full,”

Consider a perverse full filtered simplicial complex

With the “well ordering theorem” applied inductively to

Let

If

So, the chain complex of oriented simplices

Associated to such simplicial simplex, we have the singular simplex

.

The next statement is the existence of an isomorphism between the singular and the simplicial intersection homologies, for a full complex.

Theorem 4.2

5.
Blown-up intersection cohomologies

Intersection homology and cohomology do not verify the Poincaré duality for any ring of coefficient (see
[
12
,
10
]
).
The blown-up intersection cohomology has been introduced in order to recover this property through a cap product with a fundamental class (see
[
5
]
).
In this section, we extend the paradigm (simplicial, singular) to the blown-up intersection cohomology.
After their definitions, we show in Propositions (
5.10
), (
5.11
) and (
5.12
) that the process used for intersection homology is still valid here.
Finally, we prove the existence of an isomorphism between the simplicial and singular blown-up intersection cohomologies in Theorem
5.13
, for a full filtered simplicial complex.

Definition 5.1
.

Let

be a filtered space of dimension

A

of

\Delta

\mathrm{Sing}^{{\mathcal{F}}}X

The subfamily of regular simplices is denoted
\mathrm{Sing}^{{\mathcal{F}}}_{+}X

Definition 5.2

A

\Delta

The

Without loss of the generality, it suffices

Let us begin with basic recalls on PL spaces.

Definition 6.1
.

A

A

.

(b)

If

.

.

Let

and

be two PL spaces.

\psi\colon X\to Y
, such that for any

and any

, there is a subdivision

of

.

.
We can suppose that the orientations of
\sigma
and
\sigma\{\ell\}
are compatible.
Each simplex of the subdivision is obtained by adding new vertices to a subset of
{\mathrm{Vert}\,}(K)
.
These new vertices belongs to a stratum and we can write
\Delta_{\sigma\{\ell\}}

as a join product

\Delta_{\sigma\{\ell\}}=\Delta_{\sigma\{\ell\},0}\ast\dots\ast\Delta_{\sigma\{\ell\},n}
.

\bullet

Construction of
j_{K^{\prime}K}
.

For any
\varepsilon
as in Definition
5.3

and any
\ell\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, we set

j\left({\boldsymbol{1}}_{(\Delta_{\sigma\{\ell\}},\varepsilon)}\right)={\boldsymbol{1}}_{(\Delta_{\sigma},\varepsilon)}.

We get a cochain map
j\colon{{\widetilde{N}}}^{{}^{*}}{(K^{\prime})}\to{{\widetilde{N}}}^{{}^{*}}{(K)}
. Concerning the perverse degrees, let us notice that

\dim(\Delta_{\sigma\{\ell\}}\cap S)\leq\dim(\Delta_{\sigma}\cap S)
.
Thus, we have
\|{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(\Delta_{\sigma},\varepsilon)}\|\leq\|{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(\Delta_{\sigma\{\ell\}},\varepsilon)}\|

for any
\ell\in\{1,\dots,k\}
.
Defined on a basis, the association
j
extends linearly in a map

j_{K^{\prime}K}\colon{{\widetilde{N}}}^{{}^{*}}_{{}_{\overline{p}}}{\left(K^{\prime}\right)}\to{{\widetilde{N}}}^{{}^{*}}_{{}_{\overline{p}}}{\left(K\right)}.

On each factor of the join product, this map is the transposed map of the subdivision map.
From
[
9
, Lemmas 3.3.1, 3.3.15]
, it follows that
j_{K^{\prime}K}
is a chain map.

\bullet

Construction of
\varphi_{KK^{\prime}}
.

We use a simplicial map,

\nu\colon K^{\prime}\to K
,
built by Goresky and MacPherson in
[
16
, Appendix]
.
Let us recall their construction, using
[
9
, Subsection 3.3.4]
, assuming that
K
is full
and that the set
{\mathrm{Vert}\,}(K)
is well ordered.
The map
\nu
is defined from a map
\overline{\nu}\colon{\mathrm{Vert}\,}(K^{\prime})\to{\mathrm{Vert}\,}(K)
between the set of vertices of
K
and
K^{\prime}
,
that is extended linearly on simplices.

If the vertex
v^{\prime}\in K^{\prime}
is already in
K
, we set
\overline{\nu}(v^{\prime})=v^{\prime}
.
Otherwise,
v^{\prime}
is in the interior of a simplex
\sigma
of
K
.
Denote
S
the stratum of
|K|
containing the interior of
\sigma
.
One knows (see
[
16
]
or
[
9
, Lemma 3.3.25]
) that the interior of a simplex
is contained in the stratum
S
if, and only if, all the vertices of
\sigma
are contained in the closure
\overline{S}

and at least one vertex of
\sigma
is in
S
.
We define
\overline{\nu}(v^{\prime})
as the vertex of
\sigma
in
S
that is greatest in the selected order.
As the vertices
v^{\prime}
and
\overline{\nu}(v^{\prime})
are in the same stratum (see
[
9
, Proof of Lemma 3.3.21]
), the map
\overline{\nu}
is compatible with the strata decomposition.
Also, from the same proof, it is explicit that
only one

of the simplices
\Delta_{\sigma\{\ell\}}
of the subdivision has an image by
\nu
which is
of the same dimension than
\Delta_{\sigma}
. Let us denote it
\Delta_{\sigma\{\nu\}}
and set

\varphi_{KK^{\prime}}\left({\boldsymbol{1}}_{(\Delta_{\sigma},\varepsilon)}\right)={\boldsymbol{1}}_{(\Delta_{\sigma\{\nu\}},\varepsilon)}.

As
\nu\colon K^{\prime}\to K
is a simplicial map, compatible with the strata, we have

\|{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(\Delta_{\sigma},\varepsilon)}\|=\|{\boldsymbol{1}}_{(\Delta_{\sigma\{\nu\}},\varepsilon)}\|
and a map

\varphi_{KK^{\prime}}\colon{{\widetilde{N}}}^{{}^{*}}_{{}_{\overline{p}}}{\left(K\right)}\to{{\widetilde{N}}}^{{}^{*}}_{{}_{\overline{p}}}{\left(K^{\prime}\right)}.

As the association
\overline{\nu}
gives a chain map (see
[
16
, Appendix]
or
[
9
, Lemma 3.3.21]
),
by duality,
\varphi_{KK^{\prime}}
is a chain map.
The equality
j_{K^{\prime}K}\circ\varphi_{KK^{\prime}}={\mathrm{id}}
follows directly from the definitions of the two maps.
∎

6.2.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{T}})

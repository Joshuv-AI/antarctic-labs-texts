Shortcuts to Squeezed Thermal States
Léonce Dupays, Aurélia Chenu

Abstract
Squeezed state in harmonic systems can be generated through a variety of techniques, including varying the oscillator frequency or using nonlinear two-photon Raman interaction. We focus on these two techniques to drive an initial thermal state into a final squeezed thermal state with controlled squeezing parameters—amplitude and phase—in arbitrary time. The protocols are designed through reverse engineering for both unitary and open dynamics. Control of the dissipation is achieved using stochastic processes, readily implementable via, e.g., continuous quantum measurements. Importantly, this allows controlling the state entropy and can be used for fast thermalization. The developed protocols are thus suited to generate squeezed thermal states at controlled temperature in arbitrary time.



keywords
squeezed thermal state, shortcut to adiabaticity, control of open quantum systems, generalized harmonic oscillator, generalized Gibbs state

Squeezed thermal states

We consider the thermal state
\sigma_{0}=e^{-\beta_{0}H_{0}}/\mathrm{Tr}(e^{-\beta_{0}H_{0}})

H_{0}=\hbar\omega_{0}(a^{\dagger}_{0}a_{0}+1/2)

(1)

\varepsilon_{t}=\hbar\omega_{t}\beta_{t}
and its initial value
\varepsilon_{0}=\hbar\omega_{0}\beta_{0}

The squeezing operator

(2)

and phase
\phi_{t}
, which defines the correlations between position and momentum. The annihilation operator
a_{0}
is defined from

a_{t}=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega_{t}}{2\hbar}}\hat{x}+i\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\hbar m\omega_{t}}}\hat{p}.

(3)

The static properties of state (
1
) have been thoroughly studied and described in e.g.
[

]
. We focus on its dynamics to design control protocols generating a squeezed thermal state at arbitrary final temperature
\beta_{f}^{-1}
and target parameters

at the end of the control protocol,
t_{f}
.

1

\displaystyle H_{\textsc{gho}}=\hbar\omega_{t}(A_{t}^{\dagger}A_{t}+1/2).

(6)

Under unitary evolution, the state dynamics

The explicit form of this Hamiltonian is obtained from the

—see App.
A

\displaystyle H_{\textsc{cd}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\frac{\dot{\phi}_{t}}{2}(A_{t}^{\dagger}A_{t}+\frac{1}{2}-(a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{0}+\frac{1}{2}))

(7)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{\dot{r}_{t}}{2}(a_{0}^{2}e^{-i\phi_{t}}{-}a^{\dagger 2}_{0}e^{i\phi_{t}}).

Case
\phi_{t}=0

Squeezing with no final correlation between

and

such that, for a proper choice of

Case

The generalized HO

R(\theta)S_{r,\phi}R^{\dagger}(\theta)=S_{r,\phi-\theta}

.

\frac{d\tilde{\sigma}_{t}}{dt}=\frac{1}{i\hbar}[i\hbar\frac{\dot{r}_{t}}{2}(a_{0}^{2}{-}a^{\dagger 2}_{0}),\tilde{\sigma}_{t}].

(9)

This unitary evolution is dictated by the two-photon Raman Hamiltonian. We further detail how this allows implementing (

3
Fast Squeezing and Thermalization through trap and dephasing control

On one hand, it is quite established that squeezing can be achieved with a change of the trap frequency, as proposed in trapped ions already decades ago
[
59
,
60
,
61
]
and demonstrated experimentally
[
62
]
.
On the other hand, creating a thermal state from the thermal state of a different system requires
rearrangement of the initial distribution of eigenstates so as to match the Gibbs distribution of the final system. STA protocols generate, in a finite time, the adiabatic evolution of a reference Hamiltonian
[
46
]
, thus preserving the initial eigenvalue distribution. Although these two results are well established, the connection between squeezing and STA on a HO seems to not always be made. We explicit it here and then consider dissipative dynamics to extend the technique to generate squeezed thermal state at arbitrary temperature. To do so, this Section focuses on generating squeezed thermal state with no correlation between
\hat{x}
and
\hat{p}
, i.e.
\phi=0
.

3.1
Squeezing through trap control

We first consider unitary, isentropic dynamics, i.e.
\varepsilon_{t}=\varepsilon_{0}
constant. In the case
\phi_{t}=0
, the squeezed thermal state directly maps to the instantaneous thermal state of a HO with time-dependent frequency
\omega_{t}
,

\sigma_{t}(\phi=0)=S_{r,0}\sigma_{0}S_{r,0}^{\dagger}=\frac{e^{-\beta_{t}H_{t}}}{{\rm Tr}\left(e^{-\beta_{t}H_{t}}\right)}.

(10)

Indeed, for
\phi_{t}=0
, the
A_{t}
operator (
5
) becomes
a_{t}=S_{r,0}\,a_{0}\,S_{r,0}^{\dagger}=a_{0}\cosh r_{t}+a^{\dagger}_{0}\sinh r_{t}
. It evolves as
\dot{a}_{t}=\dot{r}_{t}a^{\dagger}_{t}
which maps, for
r_{t}=\ln\sqrt{\omega_{t}/\omega_{0}}
, to the annihilation operator factorizing the time-dependent HO,
H_{\textsc{gho}}^{(0)}=H_{t}=\hbar\omega_{t}(a^{\dagger}_{t}a_{t}+\frac{1}{2})

Note that the operation
S_{r,0}
is also known as a dilatation

63
]

S_{r,0}f(\hat{x})S_{r,0}^{\dagger}=f(\hat{x}/w)

S_{r,0}f(\hat{p})S_{r,0}^{\dagger}=f(w\hat{p})
, respectively.
The time-dependent HO itself is thus equivalently a squeezed or dilated HO

H_{t}=\frac{\omega_{t}}{\omega_{0}}S_{r_{t},0}H_{0}S_{r_{t},0}^{\dagger}=T_{w}\frac{H_{0}}{w^{2}}T_{w}^{\dagger}.

(11)

The dynamics directly follows from (
8
) as
\dot{\sigma}_{t}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[H_{t}+H_{\textsc{cd}}^{(0)},\sigma_{t}]
.

For the purpose of experimental implementation, let us consider the state
\varrho_{t}=U_{\Omega_{0}}\sigma_{t}U_{\Omega_{0}}^{\dagger}
in a frame rotated by the unitary
U_{\Omega_{0}}=\exp\big({i\frac{\Omega_{0}m}{2\hbar}\hat{x}^{2}}\big)=\exp\big(i\frac{\Omega_{0}}{4\omega_{t}}\big(a_{t}+a^{\dagger}_{t}\big)^{2}\big)
, where
\Omega_{0}
so far is an arbitrary, time-dependent frequency. The evolution of this density matrix
\dot{\varrho}_{t}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[H_{c},\varrho_{t}]
is governed
by the control Hamiltonian
H_{c}=U_{\Omega_{0}}(H_{t}+H_{\textsc{cd}}^{(0)})U_{\Omega_{0}}^{\dagger}+i\hbar\dot{U}_{\Omega_{0}}U_{\Omega_{0}}^{\dagger}
. Using the fact that
U_{\Omega_{0}}a_{t}U_{\Omega_{0}}^{\dagger}=a_{t}-i\frac{\Omega_{0}}{2\omega_{t}}(a_{t}+a_{t}^{\dagger})
, this Hamiltonian reads
3
3

3

We used
a_{t}^{2}-a^{\dagger 2}_{t}=(\cosh(r_{t})a_{0}+\sinh(r_{t})a^{\dagger}_{0})^{2}-(\cosh(r_{t})a^{\dagger}_{0}+\sinh(r_{t})a_{0})^{2}=a_{0}^{2}-a^{\dagger 2}_{0}
.

\displaystyle H_{c}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hbar\omega_{t}(a_{t}^{\dagger}a_{t}+\frac{1}{2})

(12)

\displaystyle+\frac{\hbar}{4\omega_{t}}\left(\Omega_{0}^{2}+\Omega_{0}\frac{\dot{\omega}_{t}}{\omega_{t}}-\dot{\Omega}_{0}\right)(a_{t}+a_{t}^{\dagger})^{2}

\displaystyle+i\hbar\left(\frac{\Omega_{0}}{2}+\frac{\dot{\omega}_{t}}{4\omega_{t}}\right)(a_{t}^{2}-a_{t}^{\dagger 2}).

Taking
\Omega_{0}=-\dot{\omega}_{t}/(2\omega_{t})=-\dot{r}_{t}=\dot{w}/w
removes the correlations in position and momentum. Under this condition, the control Hamiltonian
H_{c}=\frac{\hat{p}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}_{c}\hat{x}^{2}
is a HO with time-dependent control frequency (
13
)

\displaystyle\omega_{c}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega^{2}_{t}-\Omega^{2}_{0}-\dot{\Omega}_{0}

(13)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{t}^{2}-\frac{3}{4}\frac{\dot{\omega}_{t}^{2}}{\omega_{t}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\ddot{\omega}_{t}}{\omega_{t}}\right).

So for a given reference trajectory with frequency

,

is the frequency to be implemented to do the shortcut. This control frequency can be recovered inserting

in the Ermakov equation
[

]

\ddot{w}+\omega_{\rm eff}^{2}w=\omega_{0}^{2}/w^{3}
, and corresponds to known results from local counterdiabatic driving

The state implemented in the lab evolves as

\varrho_{t}=U_{\Omega_{0}}S_{r,0}\sigma_{0}S_{r,0}^{\dagger}U^{\dagger}_{\Omega_{0}}

3.2
Extending the range of accessible squeezed states with a control dissipator

The thermal state (
10
) is diagonal in the instantaneous Fock state basis and reads
\sigma_{t}=\sum_{n}p_{n,t}|n_{t}\rangle\langle n_{t}|
, with
p_{n,t}=e^{-\varepsilon_{t}n}(1-e^{-\varepsilon_{t}})
.
Its von Neumann entropy
-\mathrm{Tr}(\sigma_{t}\ln\sigma_{t})=\varepsilon_{t}/(e^{\varepsilon_{t}}-1)-\ln(1-e^{-\varepsilon_{t}})
is conserved during any unitary evolution.
The product
\beta\omega
, that characterizes the eigenvalue distribution of the thermal state, is constant under unitary evolution. We refer to ‘cooling’ for processes decreasing the von Neumann entropy
[
66
]
, which contrast with phase-space preserving processes—with constant entropy. To extend the range of accessible states, we allow for changes in temperature and entropy during the dynamics, taking
\varepsilon_{t}
time dependent.

Figure 1:
Map of accessible variances:
The bold lines correspond to constant relative variances, with values
10\log_{10}(\frac{\Delta x_{f}}{\Delta x_{0}})
given in dB.
The black point represents the identity process. The light contour lines correspond to isentropic processes (constant
\beta\omega
). Unitary dynamics are along these isentropic lines and restrict the target state to
\beta_{f}\omega_{f}=\beta_{0}\omega_{0}
, thus also restricting the accessible variances for given initial conditions. By contrast, open dynamics and engineered dissipation extend the variances at reach to the full map. Blue background correspond to cooling processes, orange is for heating.

Another motivation to design open protocols for squeezing is to enhance the variance. Indeed, the variance in position of a squeezed thermal state depends on both the average phonon number
\bar{n}=\frac{1}{e^{\varepsilon_{t}}-1}
and the squeezing parameter
r_{t}

[
42
]
. Specifically, using the operator
\hat{x}=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar}{m\omega_{0}}}(a_{0}+a^{\dagger}_{0})
on the state
\sigma_{t}(\phi=0)=(1+\bar{n})^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\bar{n}}{\bar{n}+1}\right)^{n}|n_{t}\rangle\langle n_{t}|
has a variance in position

\Delta x_{t}=\langle\hat{x}^{2}\rangle-\langle\hat{x}\rangle^{2}=\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega_{0}}(2\bar{n}+1)e^{-2r_{t}}.

(14)

Using the link to the time-dependent harmonic oscillator through
r_{t}=\ln(\sqrt{\omega_{t}/\omega_{0}})
yields
\Delta x_{t}=\frac{1}{2k^{2}_{t}\tanh(\varepsilon_{t}/2)}
with
k_{t}=\sqrt{\frac{m\omega_{t}}{\hbar}}
—see App.
B
for details. So open protocols allow controlling the variance with two parameters,
(\omega_{t},\varepsilon_{t})
. Figure
1
shows the variance ratio between final and initial states as function of the inverse temperature and trap frequency relative changes. While the variances at reach with unitary protocols are limited to those on isentropic lines, the map is extended to arbitrary values (including beyond the 3dB limit) thanks to changes in entropy.

Let us now detail the open control protocols. When entropy is allowed to change—
\varepsilon_{t}
is time-dependent—direct derivation of the state matrix
\sigma_{t}=\frac{1}{Z_{t}}e^{-\varepsilon_{t}(a^{\dagger}_{t}a_{t}+\frac{1}{2})}
yields

\dot{\sigma}_{t}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[H_{t}+H^{(0)}_{\textsc{cd}},\sigma_{t}]-\dot{\varepsilon}_{t}\sigma_{t}\left(a_{t}^{\dagger}a_{t}+\frac{1}{1-e^{\varepsilon_{t}}}\right),

(15)

with
H^{(0)}_{\textsc{cd}}
given in Eq. (
8
).
The system is initialized in the thermal state (
10
) and evolves according to (
15
). As in the unitary case, for the sake of experimental implementation, we consider the state matrix
\varrho_{t}=U_{\Omega}\sigma_{t}U_{\Omega}^{\dagger}
rotated by the unitary
U_{\Omega}=\exp\big({i\frac{\Omega_{t}}{4\omega_{t}}\big(a_{t}+a_{t}^{\dagger}\big)^{2}}\big)
, with
H_{\varrho}\equiv U_{\Omega}(H_{t}+H_{\textsc{cd}}^{(0)})U_{\Omega}^{\dagger}+i\hbar\dot{U}_{\Omega}U_{\Omega}^{\dagger}

\dot{\varrho}_{t}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[H_{\varrho},\varrho_{t}]+\mathcal{D}_{\textsc{cd}}(\varrho_{t}),

where all terms accounted for population changes are in the counter-diabiatic dissipator,
\mathcal{D}_{\textsc{cd}}(\varrho_{t})=\sum_{n}\dot{p}_{n,t}U_{\Omega}|n_{t}\rangle\langle n_{t}|U_{\Omega}^{\dagger}

Alternatively, part of the counter-diabatic Hamiltonian can be written as a ‘control’ harmonic oscillator,
H_{c}
, with a ‘control’ frequency chosen of the form of the closed results (
13
), i.e.
\omega_{c}^{2}\equiv\omega_{t}^{2}-\Omega_{t}^{2}-\dot{\Omega}_{t}

\displaystyle H_{\varrho}=

\displaystyle H_{c}+\frac{\hbar}{4\omega_{t}}\left(2\Omega_{t}^{2}+\Omega_{t}\frac{\dot{\omega}_{t}}{\omega_{t}}\right)(a_{t}+a_{t}^{\dagger})^{2}

\displaystyle+i\hbar\left(\frac{\Omega_{t}}{2}+\frac{\dot{\omega}_{t}}{4\omega_{t}}\right)(a_{t}^{2}-a_{t}^{\dagger 2})

(17)

with
H_{c}=\hat{p}^{2}/(2m)+\frac{1}{2}m\omega_{c}^{2}\hat{x}^{2}=\hbar\omega_{t}(a_{t}^{\dagger}a_{t}+\frac{1}{2})+\frac{\hbar}{4\omega_{t}}\omega_{c}^{2}(a_{t}+a^{\dagger}_{t})^{2}.

Then, the frequency
\Omega_{t}\equiv\Omega_{0}+\Omega_{1}
is taken to be composed of
\Omega_{0}=-\dot{\omega}_{t}/(2\omega_{t})
—to cancel the term in

that accounts for changes due to the open dynamics.
The master equation (

\dot{\varrho}_{t}=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[H_{c},\varrho_{t}]+\mathcal{D}_{c}(\varrho_{t}).

(18)

The ‘control’ dissipator can be written in a compact form by defining the annihilation operator

b_{t}\equiv U_{\Omega}a_{t}U_{\Omega}^{\dagger}=a_{t}-i\frac{\Omega_{t}}{2\omega_{t}}(a_{t}+a_{t}^{\dagger}),

which gives (see App.
C

\mathcal{D}_{c}(\varrho_{t}){=}\frac{\Omega_{1}}{2}\big[b_{t}^{2}{-}b_{t}^{\dagger 2},\varrho_{t}\big]{-}\dot{\varepsilon}_{t}\varrho_{t}\Big(b^{\dagger}_{t}b_{t}{+}\frac{1}{1{-}e^{\varepsilon_{t}}}\Big).

(19)

Note that
a_{t}+a^{\dagger}_{t}=b_{t}+b_{t}^{\dagger}
, so the position operator

is equivalently written in one basis or the other.

a_{t}\sigma_{t}=e^{-\varepsilon_{t}(a_{t}a^{\dagger}_{t}+1/2)}a_{t}Z_{t}^{-1}=\sigma_{t}a_{t}e^{-\varepsilon_{t}}

translate to

and
b_{t}^{\dagger}\varrho_{t}=\varrho_{t}b_{t}^{\dagger}e^{\varepsilon_{t}}
. By setting

\Omega_{1}\equiv\frac{\dot{\varepsilon}_{t}}{(1-e^{\varepsilon_{t}})(1+e^{-\varepsilon_{t}})}=-\frac{\dot{\varepsilon}_{t}}{2\sinh(\varepsilon_{t})},

(20)

the dissipator (
19

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{c}(\varrho_{t})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Gamma_{t}[(b_{t}+b^{\dagger}_{t}),[(b_{t}+b^{\dagger}_{t}),\varrho_{t}]]

(21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\gamma_{t}[\hat{x},[\hat{x},\varrho_{t}]],

where
\Gamma_{t}\equiv\frac{\dot{\varepsilon}_{t}}{2(1-e^{\varepsilon_{t}})(1-e^{-\varepsilon_{t}})}=\frac{\hbar}{2m\omega_{t}}\gamma_{t}
.
The control dissipator thus becomes the well-known form of localization in the position eigenbasis, often referred to as Joos-Zeh term
[
68
,
69
]
, and is easily implementable in current experimental platforms.
In turn, the modulation of
\gamma_{t}
can be engineered, e.g., by post-selection measurement of the position or via stochastic parametric driving, as proposed in
[
51
]
and also used below in Section
4.3
.

The designed master equation of interest thus reads

\frac{d\varrho_{t}}{dt}=\frac{1}{i\hbar}\left[\frac{\hat{p}^{2}}{2m}+\frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}_{c}\hat{x}^{2},\varrho_{t}\right]-\gamma_{t}[\hat{x},[\hat{x},\varrho_{t}]].

(22)

with the control parameters

\displaystyle\omega^{2}_{c}

\displaystyle=\omega^{2}_{t}-(\Omega_{0}+\Omega_{1})^{2}-\dot{\Omega}_{0}-\dot{\Omega}_{1},

(23a)

\displaystyle\gamma_{t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{m\omega_{t}}{\hbar}\frac{\dot{\varepsilon}_{t}}{4\sinh^{2}(\varepsilon_{t}/2)}.

(23b)

It allows generating the target state
\varrho_{f}=\sigma_{f}
at final time—since then
\dot{\omega}_{f}=\dot{\varepsilon}_{f}=0
.
Implementation easily follows from knowledge of the control parameters (
23
): given the boundary conditions
\beta_{0},\beta_{f}
and
\omega_{0},\omega_{f}
, one can fixe the time evolution as e.g. a fifth-order polynomial Ansatz,
p(\tau)=10\tau^{3}-15\tau^{4}+6\tau^{5}
, on
\beta_{t}
and
\omega_{t}
. Specifically
\omega_{t}=\omega_{0}+(\omega_{f}-\omega_{0})p(t/t_{f})
and
\beta_{t}=\beta_{0}+(\beta_{f}-\beta_{0})p(t/t_{f})
.
With this choice, we illustrate the dynamics in Figure

\omega_{f}=3\omega_{0}
), the Wigner representation of the final state evidences a thermal state squeezed in position.

4
Squeezing and Thermalization with two-photon Raman interaction

A squeezing protocol alternative to controlling the trap frequency is based on two-photon Raman interaction, that was successfully used to squeeze the ground vibrational of trapped ions
[
70
,
71
,
72
]
. We detail below the experimental setup used for implementation of equation (
9
), and extend the known technique to allow for (i) squeezing in arbitrary time thanks to reverse engineering, and (ii) at arbitrary temperature with engineered dephasing. We notably explain how the modulation of the lasers amplitudes allow to modify the variance (
14
) in arbitrary time.

4.1
Experimental setup

Consider a trapped ion
interacting with two mono-chromatic laser beams—see Fig.
3
.
In the experimental situation of interest, the electronic structure of the ion is reduced to a two-level system described by the atomic Hamiltonian
H_{a}=\frac{\hbar\omega}{2}\sigma_{z}
, with
\sigma_{z}=|e\rangle\langle e|-|g\rangle\langle g|
.
The motion of the trapped atom can be considered harmonic in all three dimensions, as obtained either from a classical or quantum-mechanical treatment
[
73
,
74
]
, and thus described by
H_{m}=\hbar\omega_{0}(a^{\dagger}a+1/2)
. With suitable electromagnetic fields, the electronic levels can be coupled to each other and to the vibrational motional degrees. Each of the electromagnetic field is treated as a classical plane wave of the form, in the direction
{\bf x}
of interest,
{\bf E}_{l}(\hat{x},t)\cdot{\bf x}=A_{l}(t)(e^{i(k_{l}\hat{x}-\omega_{l}t-\Phi_{l})}+{\rm c.c.})/2
with time-dependent amplitude
A_{l}(t)
, wave vector
{\bf k}_{l}=k_{l}{\bf x}
, and detuning
\delta_{l}
from the atomic transition,
\omega_{l}-\delta_{l}=\omega
. The interaction Hamiltonian resulting from the applied two laser fields can be described as
[
74
]

H_{\rm int}(t)={\sum_{l=\{1,2\}}}\frac{\hbar}{2}\Omega_{l}\sigma_{x}\left(e^{i(k_{l}\hat{x}-\omega_{l}t-\Phi_{l})}{+}{\rm h.c.}\right),

(24)

with
\sigma_{x}=|g\rangle\langle e|+|e\rangle\langle g|
.
The Rabi frequency describing dipole coupling to a single charge
q
is given by
\hbar\Omega_{l}/2=q\langle g|\hat{x}|e\rangle A_{l}(t)
.

We aim at preparing a squeezed thermal state on the vibrational levels of the system with total Hamiltonian

H_{\rm tot}(t)=H_{\rm a}+H_{\rm m}+H_{\rm int}(t),

(25)

starting from an initial vibrational state that is thermal.

4.2
Closed dynamics

We first consider the unitary dynamics and denote
|\psi_{t}\rangle
the solution of the Schrödinger equation. It is useful to change the energy scale
[
75
]
and look at the evolution of
|\Psi_{t}\rangle=U_{\rm r,t}|\psi_{t}\rangle
rotated by a unitary transformation
U_{{\rm r},t}\equiv e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}H_{\rm r}t}
. The rescaling Hamiltonian
H_{\rm r}=H_{\rm a}+H_{\rm m}+\frac{\hbar\Delta}{2}\sigma_{z}
effectively shifts the electronic energy of the gap
\hbar\omega
into an energy defined by the average laser detuning,
\hbar\Delta=\hbar(\delta_{1}+\delta_{2})/2
, and yields to an interaction picture. The rotated state evolves as

i\hbar|\dot{\Psi}_{t}\rangle=H|\Psi_{t}\rangle
, with
H\equiv U_{\rm r,t}H_{\rm tot}U_{\rm r,t}^{\dagger}+i\hbar\dot{U}_{\rm r,t}U_{\rm r,t}^{\dagger}
explicitly reading

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\hbar\Delta}{2}\sigma_{z}+U_{\rm r,t}H_{\rm int}(t)U_{\rm r,t}^{\dagger}

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{-}\frac{\hbar\Delta}{2}\sigma_{z}+\sum_{l=1,2}\frac{\hbar}{2}\Omega_{l}

\displaystyle\>\times\left(e^{i(\omega_{l}{-}\omega-\Delta)t}e^{i\Phi_{l}}e^{-i\eta_{l}(a_{t}+a_{t}^{\dagger})}|g\rangle\langle e|+{\rm h.c.}\right).

The position being quantized, we used
\hat{x}=x_{0}(a+a^{\dagger})
to write
e^{ik_{l}\hat{x}}=e^{i\eta_{l}(a+a^{\dagger})}
in the expression of the electromagnetic field
[
72
]
. The interaction picture leads to using the time-dependent operators
a_{t}\equiv U_{{\rm r},t}aU_{{\rm r},t}=ae^{-i\omega_{0}t}
and
\hat{x}_{t}\equiv U_{{\rm r},t}\hat{x}U_{{\rm r},t}=x_{0}(a_{t}+a^{\dagger}_{t})
. The Lamb-Dicke parameter
\eta_{l}=k_{l}x_{0}
is defined from the extension of the ground-state wave function of the reference oscillator,
x_{0}=\sqrt{\hbar/(2m\omega_{0})}
.
The evolving wave function is a superposition of the electronic ground and excited states dressed with the vibrational levels
|n\rangle
, and we look for a solution in the form

|\Psi_{t}\rangle=\sum_{n}\big(g_{n}(t)|g,n\rangle+e_{n}(t)|e,n\rangle\big)
. The electronic and vibrational degrees of freedom can be decoupled through an adiabatic elimination
[
75
]
, which assumes constant excited-state population. We follow
[
56
,
76
]
and set
\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}=2\omega_{0}
. Keeping only the resonant, second blue sideband, which effectively is a vibrational form of the RWA, and neglecting the Lamb term shifting, the evolution of the atom state density is dictated by
the effective squeezing Hamiltonian as computed in App.
E

H_{\rm eff}(t)=\hbar(\eta_{2}{-}\eta_{1})^{2}\frac{\Omega_{1}\Omega_{2}}{4\Delta}\big(e^{i(\Phi_{1}{-}\Phi_{2})}a^{2}{+}{\rm h.c}\big)|g\rangle\langle g|.

(27)

One can rewrite the evolution of
|\Psi_{t}\rangle
in the Liouville-von Neumann form, so that
\rho_{t}=|\Psi_{t}\rangle\langle\Psi_{t}|
evolves as

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{t}}{dt}

\displaystyle=-i[\alpha_{t}a^{2}+\alpha^{*}_{t}a^{\dagger 2},\rho_{t}],

(28)

with

\alpha_{t}=(\eta_{2}-\eta_{1})^{2}\frac{\Omega_{1}(t)\Omega_{2}(t)}{4\Delta}e^{i\Phi_{t}}

(29)

and
\Phi_{t}=(\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2})
. Choosing the dephasing between the lasers to be
\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2}=\frac{\pi}{2}
recovers the 2-photon Raman Hamiltonian.

r_{t}

, Eq. (

).
Increasing the squeezing parameter linearly in time (
\dot{r}_{t}
constant, see inset) yields to a variance that decreases exponentially in time. STA techniques, through reverse-engineering of the dynamics, allow to reach a target squeezing amplitude in a controlled time. For example, taking
r_{\tau}=r_{0}+(r_{f}-r_{0})(10\tau^{3}-15\tau^{4}+6\tau^{5})
with
\tau=t/t_{f}
leads to a desired squeezing in arbitrary time (
r_{f}=4
and
t_{f}=1
here).

Importantly, this recovers the dynamics of the squeezed state given by (
9
) in the rotated frame, thus generating the general squeezing Hamiltonian (
7
) in the original frame. In this setup, the squeezing parameter is directly related to the experimental parameters as

\frac{\dot{r}_{t}}{2}=|\alpha_{t}|.

(30)

This simple relation is experimentally very relevant. It implies that using a constant amplitude for the lasers leads to a squeezing parameter linear in time. So to achieve a fixed squeezing parameter, one must wait a given time. By contrast, the same squeezing parameter can be achieved in an arbitrary time through reverse engineering. For example, consider a fifth-order polynomial interpolating between the initial and final squeezing parameter,
r_{t}=r_{0}+(r_{f}-r_{0})p(t/t_{f})
. Its derivative gives, through Eq. (
30
), the laser amplitudes needed to reach the target squeezing in arbitrary time. This is further illustrated in Fig.
4.2
, that shows the benefit of reserve engineering the laser amplitudes over using a constant amplitude in order to reach a target squeezing parameter in a desired time. Furthermore, as mentioned in Sec.
2
, the time of the process determines the phase. It is then possible to choose the time of the process accordingly to the desired target phase,
t_{f}=-\frac{\phi_{f}}{2\omega_{0}}
.
We now extend the dynamics to open processes and solve the state evolution. In addition to provide control over the temperature, the derived solution allows generating an arbitrary squeezing phase.

4.3
Open dynamics: Engineering the master equation in a stochastically-shaken trapped ion

In order to generate a controlled dissipation and extend the map of final states at reach, we process as in the HO, and use stochastic processes. Specifically, we add to the total Hamiltonian a stochastic component, and consider

h_{\rm st}=H_{\rm tot}(t)+\hbar\sqrt{2\gamma_{t}}\xi_{t}\hat{x}\otimes|g\rangle\langle g|.

(31)

This system is now characterized by the Wiener process
W_{t}=W_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\xi_{t^{\prime}}dt^{\prime}
defined in terms of the normally distributed random variable
\xi_{t}
taken as white noise—with zero mean and vanishing correlation,
\langle\xi_{t}\xi_{t^{\prime}}\rangle=\delta(t-t^{\prime})
.
The stochastic term allows to create the control dissipator and has the advantage of being readily implementable via continuous quantum measurement or in a stochastically-shaken trap
[
77
]
. Note that the stochastic term in (
31
) is taken as acting only on the electronic ground state.
While this yields a rigorous analytical derivation, it can be experimentally challenging, so an alternative scheme is presented in App.
G
that, instead of shaking the trap, relies on two additional laser beams, one having a stochastic amplitude.

Let
|\psi_{t}\rangle
denote the solution of the Schrödinger equation,
|\psi_{t+dt}\rangle=e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}h_{\rm st}dt}|\psi_{t}\rangle
.
Following the unitary results, we look at the evolution of the state vector
|\Psi_{t}\rangle=U_{\rm r,t}|\psi_{t}\rangle
. The influence of the additional stochastic term is detailed in App.
F
.
The density matrix of interest is the ensemble one, obtained from averaging over the realizations of the noise and denoted
\rho_{t}=\langle|\Psi_{t}\rangle\langle\Psi_{t}|\rangle
.
We find its evolution dictated by the master equation

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{t}}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{\hbar}[H_{\rm eff}(t),\rho_{t}]-\gamma_{t}[\hat{x}_{t},[\hat{x}_{t},\rho_{t}]]

(32)

\displaystyle=-i[\alpha_{t}a^{2}+\alpha_{t}^{*}a^{\dagger 2},\rho_{t}]+2\kappa_{t}\left(\mathcal{D}(a)+\mathcal{D}(a^{\dagger})\right),

the second line following from the RWA. It corresponds to a master equation of Lindblad form, where
the dissipators, defined from
\mathcal{D}(a)=a\rho_{t}a^{\dagger}-\frac{1}{2}\{a^{\dagger}a,\rho_{t}\}
, are modulated with an amplitude
\kappa_{t}=\gamma_{t}x_{0}^{2}
. The parameter
\alpha_{t}
is the same as in the unitary case—Eq. (
29
). We solve the dynamics and show how this setup can be used to generate a target squeezed thermal state.

4.4
Solving the dynamics

The system is initialized in a state with density matrix
|g\rangle\langle g|\otimes\sigma_{0}
, where
\sigma_{t}\equiv e^{-\beta_{t}H_{m}}/Z_{t}
denotes the thermal state on the vibrational manifold at initial inverse temperature
\beta_{0}
.
We next solve the dynamics to find the dynamical control parameters
\{\alpha_{t},\kappa_{t}\}
for which the squeezed thermal state

\displaystyle\rho_{t}

\displaystyle=|g\rangle\langle g|\otimes S_{r,\phi}\sigma_{0}S^{\dagger}_{r,\phi}

\displaystyle=|g\rangle\langle g|\otimes\frac{1}{Z_{t}}e^{\lambda_{t}\left(S_{r,\phi}a^{\dagger}aS^{\dagger}_{r,\phi}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}

(33)

is solution of the dynamics (
32
). The time-dependent parameter
\lambda_{t}\equiv-\beta_{t}\hbar\omega_{0}
allows varying the temperature. It is useful to work with the factorized form, that we derive in normal ordering following McCoy
[
78
]
as (see App.
D
)

\displaystyle e^{\lambda S_{r,\phi}a^{\dagger}aS^{\dagger}_{r,\phi}}

\displaystyle=e^{\lambda(\cosh(2r)a^{\dagger}a{+}\cosh r\sinh r(e^{i\phi}a^{\dagger 2}{+}e^{{-}i\phi}a^{2}){+}\sinh^{2}r\mathbbm{1})}

\displaystyle=K_{t}e^{J^{*}_{t}a^{\dagger 2}}e^{-B_{t}a^{\dagger}a}e^{J_{t}a^{2}},

(34)

where the parameters are defined as
J_{t}=j(r_{t},\lambda_{t})e^{i\phi_{t}}
with the real functions
j(r,\lambda)\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\sinh(2r)(e^{2\lambda}-1)}{2(\cosh^{2}r-\sinh^{2}re^{2\lambda})}\right)
,
B_{t}\equiv-\ln\left(\left|1+\frac{(e^{\lambda_{t}}-1)(\cosh^{2}(r_{t})+\sinh^{2}(r_{t})e^{\lambda_{t}})}{\cosh^{2}(r_{t})-\sinh^{2}(r_{t})e^{2\lambda_{t}}}\right|\right)
, and the normalizing constant
K_{t}
—given explicitly in Eq. (
S24
).
The master equation (
32
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{t}}{dt}\rho_{t}^{-1}

\displaystyle=-i\alpha_{t}^{*}a^{\dagger 2}-i\alpha_{t}a^{2}-2\kappa_{t}-2\kappa_{t}a^{\dagger}a

\displaystyle+\rho_{t}\left(i\alpha_{t}^{*}a^{\dagger 2}+i\alpha_{t}a^{2}-2\kappa_{t}a^{\dagger}a\right)\rho_{t}^{-1}

\displaystyle+2\kappa_{t}a\rho_{t}a^{\dagger}\rho_{t}^{-1}+2\kappa_{t}a^{\dagger}\rho_{t}a\rho_{t}^{-1}.

(35)

Using the adjoint representation, detailed in App.
F
, yields to the simple system linking the vector of the control parameters
v_{c}=(\kappa\quad\alpha_{R}\quad\alpha_{I})^{T}
to the vector of the squeezing parameters
v_{sq}=(\dot{J}_{R}\quad\dot{J}_{I}\quad\dot{B})^{T}
with
T
the transposition. Specifically, we obtain

\displaystyle v_{c}=M_{t}^{-1}v_{sq}

(36)

with the transfer matrix

\displaystyle M_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}4(e^{-B}-1)J_{R}&-8J_{I}J_{R}&4(J^{2}_{R}-J^{2}_{I})+(e^{-2B}-1)\\
4(e^{-B}-1)J_{I}&4(J^{2}_{R}-J^{2}_{I})+(1-e^{-2B})&8J_{R}J_{I}\\
-4\left(\cosh B-1+2e^{B}(J^{2}_{R}+J^{2}_{I})\right)&8J_{I}&-8J_{R}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(37)

This is the main result of this section. Eqs. (
36
-
37
) give the control parameters
\alpha_{t}=|\alpha_{t}|e^{i\Phi_{t}}=\alpha_{R}+i\alpha_{I}
and
\kappa_{t}=\gamma_{t}x_{0}^{2}
to engineer the squeezed state characterized by

J_{t}=J_{R}+iJ_{I}
and
B_{t}
at the desired temperature through
\lambda_{t}\equiv-\beta_{t}\hbar\omega_{0}
.

We show numerical applications of the controlled parameters to be implemented to drive an initial (possibly squeezed) thermal state characterized by
\{r_{0},\phi_{0},\lambda_{0}\}
into a target final state
\{r_{f},\phi_{f},\lambda_{f}\}
. The state parameters
J_{R},J_{I}
and
B_{t}
are assumed to follow a smooth evolution taken as a fifth-order polynomial, with additional boundary conditions taken as null first and second derivatives at initial and final times.
The relative detuning between the lasers is fixed to

5
Conclusion

Starting from the general evolution for a squeezed thermal, we first clarified how squeezing without phase control can be achieved in arbitrary time by modulating the trap-frequency of a harmonic oscillator and as such, relates to known STA techniques. In turn, control of the phase can be implemented with a two-photon Raman Hamiltonian. Importantly, the two approaches presented here include dissipative dynamics in order to control the state entropy, that is engineered using stochastic fields.
We provided a detailed analysis in a trapped-ion setup, giving the control laser amplitude, relative phase, and dephasing strength suited to generate a target squeezed state in arbitrary time.
The general formalism could also capture, e.g., photonic thermal states squeezed by parametric downconversion in a lossy cavity
[
84
]
and is thus adaptable to other experimental platforms.
Among possible applications, the generated squeezed states can be used for trapped-ion transport
[
85
]
, which is relevant to quantum computing architectures.

Appendix A
Finding an expression for
S_{r,\phi}\dot{S}^{\dagger}_{r,\phi}

To find an explicit expression of
S_{r,\phi}\dot{S}^{\dagger}_{r,\phi}
for any squeezing angle
\phi_{t}
, it is useful to define the operator
\tilde{a}_{t}=e^{-i\frac{\phi_{t}}{2}}a_{0}
. It fulfills the bosonic commutator relation
[\tilde{a}_{t},\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{t}]
, and gives
S_{r,\phi}=e^{\frac{r_{t}}{2}(\tilde{a}_{t}^{2}-\tilde{a}_{t}^{\dagger 2})}
. We can expand the exponential in Taylor series and look for
S_{r,\phi}\dot{S}^{\dagger}_{r,\phi}
in terms of the
k
th derivatives of
\tilde{a}_{t}^{2}-\tilde{a}_{t}^{\dagger 2}
. From the time derivative
\dot{\tilde{a}}_{t}=-i\frac{\dot{\phi}_{t}}{2}\tilde{a}_{t}
, it follows that

\frac{d(\tilde{a}_{t}^{2}-\tilde{a}_{t}^{\dagger 2})}{dt}=-i\dot{\phi}_{t}(\tilde{a}_{t}^{2}+\tilde{a}_{t}^{\dagger 2})
. We use the fact that
\tilde{a}_{t}^{\dagger}\tilde{a}_{t}+\tilde{a}_{t}\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{t}=-\frac{1}{4}[\tilde{a}_{t}^{2}+\tilde{a}_{t}^{\dagger 2},\tilde{a}_{t}^{2}-\tilde{a}_{t}^{\dagger 2}]
and
\tilde{a}_{t}^{2}+\tilde{a}_{t}^{\dagger 2}=\frac{1}{2}[\tilde{a}_{t}^{2}-\tilde{a}_{t}^{\dagger 2},\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{t}\tilde{a}_{t}]
to obtain the expression for
k=1
in the form of commutators, explicitly,

\frac{d(\tilde{a}_{t}^{2}-\tilde{a}_{t}^{\dagger 2})}{dt}=i\frac{\dot{\phi}_{t}}{2}[\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{t}\tilde{a}_{t},\tilde{a}_{t}^{2}-\tilde{a}_{t}^{\dagger 2}].

(S1)

This form allows to generalize the results and obtain

\frac{d(\tilde{a}_{t}^{2}-\tilde{a}_{t}^{\dagger 2})^{k}}{dt}=i\left[\frac{\dot{\phi}_{t}}{2}\tilde{a}^{\dagger}_{t}\tilde{a}_{t},(\tilde{a}_{t}^{2}-\tilde{a}_{t}^{\dagger 2})^{k}\right].

(S2)

Eventually, we get

\displaystyle S_{r,\phi}\dot{S}^{\dagger}_{r,\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{r,\phi}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{r_{t}}{2}\right)^{k}\frac{d}{dt}(\tilde{a}_{t}^{2}-\tilde{a}_{t}^{\dagger 2})^{k}

(S3)

\displaystyle=

Appendix D
Factorization of the squeezed thermal state

It is useful to write the squeezed thermal state in a product form in order to solve its dynamics. We show how to obtain

e^{\lambda S_{r,\phi}a^{\dagger}aS_{r,\phi}^{\dagger}}=e^{\lambda A^{\dagger}A}=Ke^{\frac{X^{*}}{2}a{{}^{\dagger 2}}}e^{Ya^{\dagger}a}e^{\frac{X}{2}a^{2}}.

(S11)

The full analytical demonstration we propose here is alternative to the one provided in
[
86
]
.

The factorized form of a function can be obtained following the use of differential equations, as first proposed by McCoy
[
78
]
.
For any function
g(a^{\dagger},a)
of the non-commuting operators
[a,a^{\dagger}]=c
, the partial derivative can be defined as
[

]

c\frac{\partial g}{\partial a^{\dagger}}=[a,g],\quad{\rm and}\quad c\frac{\partial g}{\partial a}=-[a^{\dagger},g].

(S12)

Note that
c
is a constant that will be taken equal to unity at the end, but is useful in the general derivation for the purpose of normalization.
The expression of the function

in normal ordering (annihilation operators

to the right, creation
a^{\dagger}
to the left) is obtained by integration of a system of partial derivatives. We consider the particular function
\rho=e^{\lambda A^{\dagger}A}=\sum_{n}\frac{\lambda^{n}}{n!}(A^{\dagger}A)^{n}

(S13)

To obtain differential equations, we start from the obvious observation that
(A^{\dagger}A)^{n}A^{\dagger}=A^{\dagger}(AA^{\dagger})^{n}
, which gives

e^{\lambda A^{\dagger}A}A^{\dagger}=A^{\dagger}e^{\lambda AA^{\dagger}}=e^{c\lambda}A^{\dagger}e^{\lambda A^{\dagger}A}.

(S14)

Using the relations between the ‘

’ and ‘

5
) this readily gives

f_{+}^{*}[\rho,a^{\dagger}]+f_{-}^{*}[\rho,a]=(f_{+}^{*}a^{\dagger}+f_{-}^{*}a)(e^{\lambda}-1)\rho.

(S15)

With the future integration in mind, we write the
a\rho
term on the r.h.s as
\rho a+\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial a^{\dagger}}

and obtain the first differential equation

cf_{+}^{*}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial a}-cf_{-}^{*}e^{c\lambda}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial a^{\dagger}}=f_{-}^{*}(e^{c\lambda}-1)\rho a+f_{+}^{*}(e^{c\lambda}-1)a^{\dagger}\rho.

(S16)

A similar equation can be obtained starting from the observation that

[A,\rho]=(e^{c\lambda}-1)\rho A
and yields to the differential equation

-e^{c\lambda}f_{-}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial a}+f_{+}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial a^{\dagger}}=(e^{c\lambda}-1)f_{+}\rho a+f_{-}(e^{c\lambda}-1)a^{\dagger}\rho

where again, we have chosen to have terms on the r.h.s in the ordering

and
a^{\dagger}\rho

So we now have the system of differential equation

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial a}=X\rho a+(e^{Y}-1)a^{\dagger}\rho\\
\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial a^{\dagger}}=(e^{Y}-1)\rho a+X^{*}a^{\dagger}\rho\\
\end{cases}

(S18)

with the constants

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\frac{f_{-}^{*}f_{+}(e^{2c\lambda}-1)}{|f_{+}|^{2}-|f_{-}|^{2}e^{2c\lambda}}

(S19)

\displaystyle e^{Y}-1

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{c}\frac{(e^{c\lambda}-1)(|f_{+}|^{2}+|f_{-}|^{2}e^{c\lambda})}{|f_{+}|^{2}-|f_{-}|^{2}e^{2c\lambda}}.\equiv y

Appendix E

We are interested in the evolution of the state
|\Psi_{t}\rangle
characterized by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\hbar\Delta}{2}\sigma_{z}+U_{\rm r,t}H_{\rm int}(t)U_{\rm r,t}^{\dagger}.

Let us first give the explicit form of the interaction Hamiltonian in the rotated frame.
The atomic part evolves as

\displaystyle U_{\rm r,t}H_{\rm int}(t)U_{\rm r,t}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{2}\sum_{l=1,2}\Omega_{l}(t)\left(e^{\frac{i}{2}(\omega+\Delta)\sigma_{z}}\sigma_{x}e^{-\frac{i}{2}(\omega+\Delta)\sigma_{z}}\right)\left(e^{i\omega_{0}ta^{\dagger}a}e^{i\eta_{l}(a^{\dagger}+a)}e^{-i\omega_{0}ta^{\dagger}a}e^{-i(\Phi_{l}+\omega_{l}t)}+{\rm h.c.}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{2}\sum_{l=1,2}\Omega_{l}(t)\left(e^{i(\omega_{l}-\omega-\Delta)t}e^{i\Phi_{l}}e^{-i\eta_{l}(a^{\dagger}e^{i\omega_{0}t}+ae^{-i\omega_{0}t})}|g\rangle\langle e|+{\rm h.c.}\right).

(S27)

\displaystyle\dot{e}_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=i\frac{\Delta}{2}e_{n}(t)-i\sum_{l=1,2}\frac{\Omega_{l}}{2}e^{i(\omega+\Delta-\omega_{l})t}e^{-i\Phi_{l}}\sum_{n^{\prime}}\langle n|e^{i\eta_{l}(a^{\dagger}_{t}+a_{t})}|n^{\prime}\rangle g_{n^{\prime}}(t),

(S28a)

\displaystyle\dot{g}_{n}(t)

\displaystyle={-}i\frac{\Delta}{2}g_{n}(t){-}i{\sum_{n^{\prime},l=1,2}}\frac{\Omega_{l}}{2}e^{i(\omega_{l}-\omega-\Delta)t}e^{i\Phi_{l}}\langle n|e^{-i\eta_{l}(a^{\dagger}_{t}+a_{t})}|n^{\prime}\rangle e_{n^{\prime}}(t).

(S28b)

For large detuning,
|\Delta|\gg|\Omega_{l}|,\omega_{0}
, a state initially in the electronic ground state mainly remains in this electronic level. The small population of the electronic excited state can be eliminated abiabatically.

. The evolution follows as

\displaystyle\begin{split}i\hbar\frac{d|\Psi_{t}\rangle}{dt}=&i\hbar\sum_{n}\dot{g}_{n}(t)|g,n\rangle\\

\displaystyle H_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\hbar}{2}\left(\Delta+\frac{\Omega^{2}_{1}+\Omega^{2}_{2}}{\Delta}\right)|g\rangle\langle g|+\frac{\hbar}{4}(\eta_{2}-\eta_{1})^{2}\frac{\Omega_{1}\Omega_{2}}{\Delta}\left(e^{i(\Phi_{1}-\Phi_{2})}a^{2}+{\rm h.c.}\right)|g\rangle\langle g|.

(S31)

27

Appendix G
Two-photon Raman interaction and stochastically driven Jaynes-Cummings Hamiltonian

We now consider to generate the dissipator through a stochastic laser field rather than through shaking the trap. This leads to a Jaynes-Cummings (JC) Hamiltonian
[
70
,
71
]
in its stochastic form
[
89
,
90
]
. Note that the effect of dissipation in the JC model has been considered
[
91
,
92
,
67
]
, mainly focusing on the influence over the populations.

The set-up is similar to the one presented in Sec.
4.1
, but with
two additional beams used to engineer the dissipator (see Fig.
G
for an illustration).
The interaction Hamiltonian resulting from the applied laser fields now reads
[
74
]

H_{\rm int}(t)={\sum_{l=\{0,\dots,3\}}}\frac{\hbar}{2}\Omega_{l}\sigma_{x}\left(e^{i(k_{l}\hat{x}-\omega_{l}t-\Phi_{l})}+{\rm h.c.}\right),

(S41)

where the Rabi frequency
\Omega_{0}
will be taken as stochastic
\Omega_{0}^{\rm st}
.
We aim at preparing a squeezed thermal state on the vibrational levels of the system,
H_{m}=\hbar\nu(a^{\dagger}a+1/2)
, with total Hamiltonian

h_{\rm tot}(t)=H_{\rm a}+H_{\rm m}+H_{\rm int}(t).

(S42)

starting from an initial vibrational state that is thermal. As discussed above, this will be done by reverse engineering of the master equation to allow both squeezing and thermalization.
We proceed as before and look at the evolution of the rotated vector
|\Psi_{t}\rangle\equiv U_{\rm r,t}|\Psi_{t}\rangle
. The unitary
U_{\rm r,t}\equiv e^{\frac{i}{\hbar}H_{r}t}
is defined from the rotation Hamiltonian
H_{\rm r}=H_{\rm a}+H_{\rm m}+\frac{\hbar\tilde{\Delta}}{2}\sigma_{z}
, the average detuning now being
\tilde{\Delta}=\sum_{l=\{0,\dots,3\}}\delta_{l}/4
.
The rotated state evolves as
|\dot{\Psi}_{t+dt}\rangle=e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}H_{\rm tot}dt}|\Psi_{t}\rangle
with

H_{\rm tot}\equiv U_{\rm r,t}h_{\rm tot}U_{\rm r,t}^{\dagger}+i\hbar\dot{U}_{\rm r,t}U_{\rm r,t}^{\dagger}=-\frac{\hbar\tilde{\Delta}}{2}\sigma_{z}+U_{\rm r,t}H_{\rm int}(t)U_{\rm r,t}^{\dagger}
. The interaction Hamiltonian in the rotated frame, after the RWA, reads

\displaystyle U_{\rm r,t}H_{\rm int}(t)U_{\rm r,t}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{2}\sum_{l}\Omega_{l}(t)\left(e^{\frac{i}{2}(\omega+\tilde{\Delta})\sigma_{z}}\sigma_{x}e^{-\frac{i}{2}(\omega+\tilde{\Delta})\sigma_{z}}\right)\left(e^{i\omega_{0}ta^{\dagger}a}e^{i\eta_{l}(a^{\dagger}+a)}e^{-i\omega_{0}ta^{\dagger}a}e^{-i(\Phi_{l}+\omega_{l}t)}+{\rm h.c.}\right)

(S43)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{2}\sum_{l}\Omega_{l}(t)\left(\hat{h}_{l}|g\rangle\langle e|+{\rm h.c.}\right),

where we have defined
\hat{h}_{l}\equiv e^{-i(\omega-\tilde{\Delta}-\omega_{l})t}e^{i\Phi_{l}}e^{-i\eta_{l}(a^{\dagger}_{t}+a_{t})}
.

\captionof

figure

: 2-photon Raman interaction is generated by the (blue) laser pair with

The open dynamics is generated using a white noise on top of the ‘0’ laser’s amplitude, namely taking
\Omega^{\rm st}_{0}\rightarrow\sqrt{\Omega_{0}}\xi_{t}
.
It is then convenient to split the total Hamiltonian into its deterministic and stochastic contributions
H_{\rm tot}=H_{\rm det}+\xi_{t}H_{0}
, defined as

\displaystyle H_{\rm det}

\displaystyle=-\hbar\frac{\tilde{\Delta}}{2}\sigma_{z}+\frac{\hbar}{2}\sum_{l=\{1,2,3\}}\Omega_{l}(\hat{h}_{l}|g\rangle\langle e|+\hat{h}_{l}^{\dagger}|e\rangle\langle g|)

(S44a)

\displaystyle\xi_{t}H_{0}

\displaystyle=\xi_{t}\frac{\hbar}{2}\sqrt{\Omega_{0}}(\hat{h}_{0}|g\rangle\langle e|+\hat{h}_{0}^{\dagger}|e\rangle\langle g|).

(S44b)

We look for a solution of the wave function as

|\Psi_{t}\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(e_{n}(t)|e,n\rangle+g_{n}(t)|g,n\rangle\right).

The evolution of this state over a small increment of time
dt
reads

. This yields the coefficients evolving as

\displaystyle\dot{e}_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=i\frac{\tilde{\Delta}}{2}e_{n}(t)-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{n^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{l\neq 0}\Omega_{l}\langle n|\hat{h}_{l}^{\dagger}|n^{\prime}\rangle+\sqrt{\Omega_{0}}\xi_{t}\langle n|\hat{h}_{0}^{\dagger}|n^{\prime}\rangle\right)g_{n^{\prime}}(t)-\frac{1}{8}\Omega_{0}\langle n|\hat{h}_{0}^{\dagger}\hat{h}_{0}|n^{\prime}\rangle e_{n^{\prime}}(t)

(S45a)

\displaystyle\dot{g}_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=-i\frac{\tilde{\Delta}}{2}g_{n}(t)-\frac{i}{2}\sum_{n^{\prime}}\left(\sum_{l\neq 0}\Omega_{l}\langle n|\hat{h}_{l}^{\dagger}|n^{\prime}\rangle+\sqrt{\Omega_{0}}\xi_{t}\langle n|\hat{h}_{0}^{\dagger}|n^{\prime}\rangle\right)e_{n^{\prime}}(t)-\frac{1}{8}\Omega_{0}\langle n|\hat{h}_{0}^{\dagger}\hat{h}_{0}|n^{\prime}\rangle g_{n^{\prime}}(t)

(S45b)

For large detuning,
|\tilde{\Delta}|\gg|\Omega_{l}|,\nu
, a state initially in the electronic ground state mainly remains in this electronic level.
The small population of the electronic excited state can be eliminated abiabatically.
We thus set
\dot{e}_{n}(t)=0

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{d|\Psi_{t}\rangle}{dt}

(S46)

We then split the term
e^{-i(\eta_{l}-\eta_{l^{\prime}})(a_{t}^{\dagger}+a_{t})}

Choosing
\omega_{2}-\omega_{3}=2\nu

1
Introduction

Let
d,r\in\mathbb{N}
and let
c
be a Clifford multiplication over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
in
\mathbb{C}^{r}
, and consider the associated Dirac operator
c\nabla
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle c\nabla f=\sum_{j=1}^{d}c\left({\rm d}x^{j}\right)\partial_{x^{j}}f,\ f\in C^{1}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}\right).

(1)

Let
H
be a separable complex Hilbert space. Denote
H^{r}:=\mathbb{C}^{r}\otimes H
. We consider
c\nabla
as a self-adjoint linear operator in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
with domain
\mathrm{Dom}\left(c\nabla\right):=W^{1,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
, the
L^{2}
-Sobolev space of order
1
with values in
H^{r}
.

Now let
A=\left(A\left(x\right)\right)_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a family of self-adjoint operators in
H^{r}
. Then we associate to
A
the fibre wise multiplication operator
A\left(X\right)
in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
, given by

\displaystyle\left(A\left(X\right)f\right)\left(x\right):=

\displaystyle A\left(x\right)f\left(x\right),\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(2)

The Callias operator
D
is then

\displaystyle D:=ic\nabla+A\left(X\right).

(3)

The goal of this paper is to give conditions on the operator family
A
, such that the resolvent powers of
D^{\ast}D
and
DD^{\ast}
are trace comparable, i.e. such that for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\left(D^{\ast}D+1\right)^{-N}-\left(DD^{\ast}+1\right)^{-N}\in S^{1}\left(L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)\right),

(4)

where
S^{p}\left(Y\right)
denotes the
p
-th Schatten-von Neumann operators on a Hilbert space
Y
.

Before we proceed with a proper introduction of the involved operators, let us briefly contextualize why one might be interested in property (
4
).

The name ”Callias operator” reflects the contribution of C. Callias in
[
5
]
to the Fredholm index problem of the operator
D
for a family of matrix potentials
A
, which arises from Yang-Mills theory. A similar index problem in the special case of dimension
d=1
had been considered in the seminal series of articles by M. Atiyah, V. Patodi, and I. Singer in
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
, and especially
[
4
]
, dealing with the index problem on manifolds with boundary. In their setup, the family
A
is given by first order differential operators on a compact manifold.

A. Pushnitski showed in
[
10
]
that the Callias operator
D
in one dimension
d=1
for a family
A\left(x\right)=A_{-}+B\left(x\right)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
, where

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|B^{\prime}\left(x\right)\right\|_{S^{1}\left(H\right)}{\rm d}x<\infty,

(5)

satisfies condition (
4
) for
N=1
, and one obtains the trace formula

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R},H\right)}\left(\left(D^{\ast}D+z\right)^{-1}-\left(DD^{\ast}+z\right)^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2z}\mathrm{tr}_{H}\left(g_{z}\left(A_{+}\right)-g_{z}\left(A_{-}\right)\right),\ z\geq 1,

\displaystyle g_{z}\left(\lambda\right)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\lambda}{\left(\lambda^{2}+z\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}},

(6)

where the limits
A_{\pm}=\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}A\left(x\right)
exist in an appropriate sense. Furthermore it was shown that one can calculate the Fredholm index of
D
in terms of the spectral shift function of the operators
A_{+}
and
A_{-}
.

The results by Pushnitski were shown under less restrictive conditions on the family
\left(A\left(x\right)\right)_{x\in\mathbb{R}}
by F. Gesztesy et al. in
[
7
]
. The authors were especially able to weaken the required condition (
5
) to

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\|B^{\prime}\left(x\right)\left(A_{-}^{2}+1\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{S^{1}\left(H\right)}{\rm d}x<\infty,

(7)

which is the first instance where the family of operators
A\left(x\right)
are generated by possibly unbounded, non-commutative and non-discrete perturbations.

More recently, A. Carey et al. in
[
6
]
showed property (
4
) holds for an appropriate
N\in\mathbb{N}
in the one-dimensional case
d=1
, if the family
A
is itself given by perturbations of a Dirac operator by a matrix potential, and if one imposes weaker trace-class requirements on
A
than (
7
), dependent on a parameter (on which the choices for
N
also depend).

The goal of this paper is to present a collection of conditions on the family
A
for general
d\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
A
can be obtained by unbounded, non-commutative and non-discrete perturbations of a general self-adjoint model operator
A_{0}
, such that the associated Callias operator
D
satisfies property (
4
) for a suitable
N\in\mathbb{N}
. The necessary requirements on the trace-class properties of
A
will be weaker for larger
N
. To determine the trace and index formula for
D
in this instance will however be a task for future work.

Let us present the main result of this paper and review the conditions on the operator family
A
.

We start with reasonably generic assumptions, which allow us to differentiate the operator family
A
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. These conditions are chosen such that one obtains favourable domain properties of
D
, and they do not pertain to the trace-class properties directly.

Let
A_{0}
be a self-adjoint operator in
H^{r}
and let
A\left(x\right)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, be symmetric operators, with the dense set

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{rg}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{\left[-n,n\right]}\left(A_{0}\right)\right)\subseteq H^{r},

(8)

contained in all domains
\mathrm{Dom}\left(A\left(x\right)\right)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For compatibility with Clifford multiplication we require that
c\left({\rm d}x^{j}\right)\left(\mathcal{D}\right)\subseteq\mathrm{Dom}\left(A_{0}\right)
,
j\in\left\{1,\ldots,d\right\}
. We assume that for all
\phi\in\mathcal{D}
and
\psi\in H^{r}
the function

\displaystyle x\mapsto\langle A\left(x\right)\phi,\psi\rangle_{H^{r}},

(9)

is Lebesgue measurable on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see Hypothesis
2.1
). For a yet to choose
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we then require that
x\mapsto A\left(x\right)\phi
is
2N-1
-times weakly differentiable for
\phi\in\mathcal{D}
, with derivatives in
L^{2}_{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
, which we abbreviate with
A\in W^{2N-1,2,End}_{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\left(\mathcal{D},H^{r}\right)\right)
(see Definition
2.2
). We impose Kato-Rellich type bounds on the derivatives of
A
to ensure that
\left(D^{\ast}D\right)^{N}
and
\left(DD^{\ast}\right)^{N}
are self-adjoint operators on the domain
W^{2N,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)\cap L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{Dom}\left(A_{0}^{2N}\right)\right)
(see Proposition
3.7
).

Let us now discuss the trace-class conditions on
A
, which are the essential assumptions.

Denote
n:=\max\left(\lfloor\frac{d}{2}-2\rfloor+1,0\right)\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
. If
A\left(x\right)
commutes with
c\left({\rm d}x^{j}\right)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
j\in\left\{1,\ldots,d\right\}
, then let
N\in\mathbb{N}
with
N>\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{n}{2}+\frac{d}{4}
, otherwise let
N>\max\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}+\frac{n}{2}+\frac{d}{4},\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{n}{2}+\frac{d}{4}+\frac{1}{2}\right)
.

Denote

\displaystyle c\nabla^{End}A

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}c\left({\rm d}x^{j}\right)\partial_{x^{j}}^{End}A,

\displaystyle\nabla^{End}Ac

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{x^{j}}^{End}Ac\left({\rm d}x^{j}\right),

(10)

where
\left(\partial_{x^{j}}^{End}A\right)f=\partial_{x^{j}}\left(Af\right)-A\partial_{x^{j}}f
are appropriately defined partial derivatives of operators (see Definition
2.2
). Then we assume

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|\partial^{\gamma,End}\left(c\nabla^{End}A\langle A_{0}\rangle^{-\alpha}\right)\left(x\right)\right\|_{S^{1}\left(H^{r}\right)}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle<\infty,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|\partial^{\gamma,End}\left(\nabla^{End}Ac\langle A_{0}\rangle^{-\alpha}\right)\left(x\right)\right\|_{S^{1}\left(H^{r}\right)}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle<\infty,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|\partial^{\gamma,End}\left(\left[c\left({\rm d}x^{j}\right),A\right]\langle A_{0}\rangle^{-\beta}\right)\left(x\right)\right\|_{S^{1}\left(H^{r}\right)}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle<\infty,\ j\in\left\{1,\ldots,d\right\},

(11)

for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
with
\left|\gamma\right|\leq n
.

We should stress that
n=0
for dimensions
d\leq 3
, which contains the situation discussed in
[
10
]
,
[
7
]
, and
[
6
]
. We note especially that the first line of (
1
) is analogous to the condition in the one-dimensional case for example like (
7
) in
[
7
]
. In case
d=r=1
the second and third line of (
1
) is trivially satisfied since (scalar)
c
commutes with
A
automatically. In dimensions
d\geq 4
we see that
N>1
becomes necessary. In any case, we should note the basic feature of all the conditions in (
1
): One can increase
\alpha
and
\beta
to decrease the restriction on the family
A
, in exchange for a possibly larger
N
, which weakens property (
12
) of the Callias operator
D
. This is for example useful if
A
is a family of (pseudo-)differential operators (cf. Chapter 4 in
[
11
]
). We will illustrate this last point in Example
4.6
for the concrete case of a Dirac operator perturbed by a potential.

All presented conditions on
A
together (see Hypothesis
4.5
) imply the desired property

\displaystyle\left(D^{\ast}D+1\right)^{-N}-\left(DD^{\ast}+1\right)^{-N}\in S^{1}\left(L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)\right),

(12)

for the Callias operator
D
, which is the principal result of this paper (see Theorem
4.7
).

2
Notation and Basic Definitions

We fix
i
as the imaginary unit in
\mathbb{C}
. Let
H
be a separable complex Hilbert space and denote with
H^{r}:=H\otimes\mathbb{C}^{r}
for
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
be the space of
p
-Lebesgue-integrable elements on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
W^{k,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
the
L^{2}
-Sobolev space of order
k
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
denote with
L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
the
H^{r}
-valued (Bochner-Lebesgue-)
L^{p}
-elements over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.
f\in L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
if for all
\phi\in H^{r}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni x

\displaystyle\mapsto\langle f\left(x\right),\phi\rangle_{H^{r}}\ \text{is Lebesgue measurable},

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)}^{p}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|f\left(x\right)\right\|_{H^{r}}^{p}{\rm d}x<\infty,\ p<\infty,

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{L^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)}

\displaystyle:=\esssup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|f\left(x\right)\right\|_{H^{r}}<\infty,\ p=\infty.

(13)

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the (isometric) Fourier transform

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r},{\rm d}x\right)

\displaystyle\rightarrow L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r},\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{d}}{\rm d}\xi\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left(f\right)\left(\xi\right)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\langle x,\xi\rangle_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}f\left(x\right){\rm d}x,

(14)

obtained via continuous extension and Hilbert space tensor product from the classical Fourier transform on Schwartz functions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Usually we write
\widehat{f}:=\mathcal{F}\left(f\right)
. Note that as in the scalar case the Fourier transform satisfies by complex interpolation for
f\in L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
,
1\leq p\leq 2
, and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
,

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{f}\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)}.

(15)

We also use the abbreviations
\langle z\rangle:=\left(1+\left|z\right|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
,
\mathbbm{1}_{M}\left(z\right):=\begin{cases}1,&z\in M,\\
0,&z\notin M,\end{cases}

for
M\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, and
z\in\mathbb{C}
, and
\mathcal{B}\left(V\right)
for the Borel
\sigma
-algebra of a topological space
V
.

Denote with
W^{k,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
the
H^{r}
-valued
L^{2}
-Sobolev space of order
k\geq 0
over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.
f\in W^{k,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
if
f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
, and

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni\xi\mapsto\langle\xi\rangle^{k}\widehat{f}\left(\xi\right)\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right).

(16)

We abbreviate partial derivatives by

\displaystyle\left(\partial^{\gamma}f\right)\left(x\right)=\frac{\partial^{\left|\gamma\right|}}{\partial_{x^{1}}^{\gamma_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot\partial_{x^{d}}^{\gamma_{d}}}f\left(x^{1},\ldots,x^{d}\right),\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ f\in C^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right),\ \gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{d},

(17)

and denote the extension of
\partial^{\gamma}
to the Sobolev space
W^{\left|\gamma\right|,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
with the same letter. We note that for
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle f\in W^{k,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)\Leftrightarrow\partial^{\gamma}f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right),\ \left|\gamma\right|\leq k.

(18)

Let
c
be a Clifford multiplication over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of rank
r\in\mathbb{N}
, and consider the associated Dirac operator
c\nabla
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e. there are Clifford matrices
\left(c\left({\rm d}x^{j}\right)\right)_{j=1}^{d}\in\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}
, such that

\displaystyle c\left({\rm d}x^{k}\right)c\left({\rm d}x^{l}\right)+c\left({\rm d}x^{l}\right)c\left({\rm d}x^{k}\right)=-2\delta_{kl}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{C}^{r\times r}},\ k,l\in\left\{1,\ldots,d\right\}.

(19)

Then
c\nabla
is the operator in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
given by

\displaystyle c\nabla f

\displaystyle:=\sum_{j=1}^{d}c\left({\rm d}x^{j}\right)\partial_{x^{j}}f,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dom}\left(c\nabla\right)

\displaystyle:=W^{1,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right).

(20)

It is well-known that
c\nabla
with the above domain is self-adjoint, and that
\mathrm{Dom}\left(\langle c\nabla\rangle^{k}\right)=W^{k,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
as Banach spaces. The square of the Dirac operator is the Laplacian
\Delta=\left(c\nabla\right)^{2}
, with

\displaystyle\Delta f

\displaystyle=-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{j}}^{2}f,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dom}\left(\Delta\right)

\displaystyle=W^{2,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right).

(21)

Note that
\Delta+1=\langle c\nabla\rangle^{2}
.

The Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}
diagonalizes
\Delta
, i.e. for
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a Borel function, and if
f\left(\left|X\right|^{2}\right)
denotes the multiplication operator

\displaystyle\left(f\left(\left|X\right|^{2}\right)g\right)\left(\xi\right)

\displaystyle:=f\left(\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)g\left(\xi\right),\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dom}\left(f\left(\left|X\right|^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle:=\left\{g\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r},\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{d}}{\rm d}\xi\right)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left.\left|\xi\mapsto f\left(\left|\xi\right|^{2}\right)g\left(\xi\right)\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r},\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{d}}{\rm d}\xi\right)\right.\right\},

(22)

we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}:\mathrm{Dom}\left(f\left(\Delta\right)\right)\rightarrow\mathrm{Dom}\left(f\left(\left|X\right|^{2}\right)\right)

(23)

is an isometry with respect to the graph norms and

\displaystyle f\left(\Delta\right)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}f\left(\left|X\right|^{2}\right)\mathcal{F}.

(24)

Throughout this paper we assume that
A_{0}
is a given self-adjoint operator in
H^{r}
with domain
\mathrm{Dom}\left(A_{0}\right)
, which we may consider as a Banach space if equipped with the graph norm of
A_{0}
. We introduce the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}:=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathrm{rg}\left(\mathbbm{1}_{\left[-n,n\right]}\left(A_{0}\right)\right)\subseteq H^{r},

(25)

which is dense in
H^{r}
, a simple consequence of self-adjoint functional calculus.

In
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
we may define the constant multiplication operator
\widehat{A_{0}}
by

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{A_{0}}f\right)\left(x\right)

\displaystyle:=A_{0}f\left(x\right),\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dom}\left(\widehat{A_{0}}\right)

\displaystyle:=L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{Dom}\left(A_{0}\right)\right).

(26)

We introduce the constant coefficient Callias operator
D_{0}
in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{C}^{r}\right)
, given by

\displaystyle D_{0}

\displaystyle:=ic\nabla+\widehat{A_{0}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dom}\left(D_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=W^{1,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)\cap\mathrm{Dom}\left(\widehat{A_{0}}\right).

(27)

It has been shown in (Lemma 4.2,
[
7
]
) that
D_{0}
is closed and its graph-norm coincides with the sum of the norms of
W^{1,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
and
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{Dom}\left(A_{0}\right)\right)
. Furthermore
D_{0}
is normal with

\displaystyle D_{0}^{\ast}

\displaystyle=-ic\nabla+\widehat{A_{0}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dom}\left(D_{0}^{\ast}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Dom}\left(D_{0}\right).

(28)

The automatically non-negative, self-adjoint operator
H_{0}:=D_{0}^{\ast}D_{0}
satisfies
H_{0}=\Delta+\widehat{A_{0}}^{2}
via the commuting functional calculi of
c\nabla
and
\widehat{A_{0}}
.

Let us now introduce the first basic conditions on the operator family
A
.

Hypothesis 2.1
.

Assume
A=\left(A\left(x\right)\right)_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is a family of symmetric operators in
H^{r}
, with
\mathrm{Dom}\left(A\left(x\right)\right)\supseteq\mathcal{D}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Furthermore we suppose that for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni x\mapsto\langle A\left(x\right)\phi,\psi\rangle_{H^{r}}

(29)

is (Lebesgue-)measurable.

Under these conditions on
A
we may define the multiplications operator
A\left(X\right)
in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
by

\displaystyle\left(A\left(X\right)f\right)\left(x\right)

\displaystyle:=A\left(x\right)f\left(x\right),\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dom}\left(A\left(X\right)\right)

\displaystyle:=\left\{f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)\left|x\mapsto A\left(x\right)f\left(x\right)\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)\right.\right\}.

(30)

Note that so far we have not enough assumptions on the family
A
to claim that
A\left(X\right)
is densely defined.

However we may associate the Callias operator
D
to the operator family
A
via

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=ic\nabla+A\left(X\right),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dom}\left(D\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Dom}\left(c\nabla\right)\cap\mathrm{Dom}\left(A\left(X\right)\right).

(31)

Again, we can not claim that
D
is densely defined. This will be our first goal in the sequel to find additional conditions on the family
A
to conclude that
D
is a closed operator with domain
\mathrm{Dom}\left(D\right)=\mathrm{Dom}\left(D_{0}\right)
.

Before we proceed with the first results, we also need to introduce a notion of differentiability for the family
A
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\left(Y,\left\|\cdot\right\|_{Y}\right)
be a Banach space, and assume that
\mathcal{E}\subseteq Y
is dense. Let
\left(B\left(x\right)\right)_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a measurable family of operators in
Y
, with domains
\mathrm{Dom}\left(B\left(x\right)\right)\supseteq\mathcal{E}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
If for
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
there exists a measurable family of densely defined operators
C_{\gamma}\left(x\right)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with
\mathrm{Dom}\left(C_{\gamma}\left(x\right)\right)\supseteq\mathcal{E}
, such that for all
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\otimes\mathcal{E}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}C_{\gamma}\left(x\right)f\left(x\right){\rm d}x=\left(-1\right)^{\left|\beta\right|}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}B\left(x\right)\partial^{\beta}f\left(x\right){\rm d}x,

(32)

where the integral converges in
Y
as Bochner integrals, we say
\left(B\left(x\right)\right)_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}
is
\gamma
-times weakly differentiable on
\mathcal{E}
, and we set

\displaystyle\left(\partial^{\gamma,End}B\right)\left(x\right)\psi:=C_{\gamma}\left(x\right)\psi,\ \psi\in\mathcal{E}.

(33)

If additionally for all
\left|\gamma\right|\leq n\in\mathbb{N}
,
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
compact, and
\psi\in\mathcal{E}
,

\displaystyle\int_{K}\left\|\left(\partial^{\gamma,End}B\right)\left(x\right)\psi\right\|^{2}_{Y}{\rm d}x<\infty,

(34)

we write
B\in W^{n,2,End}_{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\left(\mathcal{E},Y\right)\right)
.

In our setup the spaces are
Y=H^{r}
and
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{D}
.

3
Domain Properties of
A\left(X\right)
and
D

In this section we will establish conditions on the family
A
which ensure that the Callias operator
D
is closed, the operators
D^{\ast}D
and
DD^{\ast}
are self-adjoint, and their powers have the same domain as the powers of
H_{0}=D_{0}^{\ast}D_{0}
.

We begin by by establishing a class of dense sets in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
, which will be cores for some operators in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
we will discuss.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
s\geq 0
. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{rg}\left(\left.\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{s}\right|_{C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\otimes\mathcal{D}}\right)

(35)

is dense in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
, such that for all
g\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}
,

\displaystyle\langle\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{s}\left(g\otimes\psi\right),f\rangle_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)}=0.

(36)

If
\widehat{g}
denotes the Fourier transform of
g
and
\widehat{f}\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
the Fourier transform of
f
, by Plancherel’s theorem we find that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{g}\left(\xi\right)\langle\left(1+\left|\xi\right|^{2}+A_{0}^{2}\right)^{s}\psi,\widehat{f}\left(\xi\right)\rangle_{H^{r}}{\rm d}\xi=0.

(37)

Because
\left\{\widehat{g}\left|g\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\right.\right\}
is dense in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
, we conclude that for all
\psi\in\mathcal{D}
exists a nullset
N_{\psi}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, such that for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash N_{\psi}

\displaystyle\langle\left(1+\left|\xi\right|^{2}+A_{0}^{2}\right)^{s}\psi,\widehat{f}\left(\xi\right)\rangle_{H^{r}}=0.

(38)

H^{r}
is separable and
\mathcal{D}
a dense subspace. Therefore there exists an at most countable subset
\left\{\psi_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
, which is dense in
H^{r}
.
On the other hand for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have
\phi_{n}:=\left(1+\left|\xi\right|^{2}+A_{0}^{2}\right)^{-s}\psi_{n}\in\mathcal{D}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Also
N=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}N_{\phi_{n}}
is still a nullset in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash N
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{n},\widehat{f}\left(\xi\right)\rangle_{H^{r}}=\langle\left(1+\left|\xi\right|^{2}+A_{0}^{2}\right)^{s}\phi_{n},\widehat{f}\left(\xi\right)\rangle_{H^{r}}=0.

(39)

But
\left\{\psi_{n}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is dense in
H^{r}
, so
\widehat{f}\left(\xi\right)=0
for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Thus
f=0
as an element of
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
. Consequently
\mathrm{rg}\left(\left.\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{s}\right|_{C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\otimes\mathcal{D}}\right)
is dense in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
.
∎

Let us show that the operator families
B
we want to consider give rise to densely defined multiplication operators
B\left(X\right)
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

\displaystyle B\in L^{2,End}_{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\left(\mathcal{D},H^{r}\right)\right):=W^{0,2,End}_{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\left(\mathcal{D},H^{r}\right)\right).

(40)

Then
C_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\otimes\mathcal{D}\in\mathrm{Dom}\left(B\left(X\right)\right)
. Especially
B\left(X\right)
is densely defined.

Proof.

Let
g\in C^{\infty}_{c}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
and
\psi\in\mathcal{D}
. We need to show that
x\mapsto g\left(x\right)B\left(x\right)\psi\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
, the statement of the Lemma then follows by linearity of
B\left(X\right)
. To this end let
\supp g\subseteq K
, where
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is compact. Then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|g\left(x\right)\right|^{2}\left\|B\left(x\right)\psi\right\|_{H^{r}}^{2}{\rm d}x\leq\sup_{x\in K}\left|g\left(x\right)\right|^{2}\cdot\int_{K}\left\|B\left(x\right)\psi\right\|_{H^{r}}^{2}{\rm d}x<\infty.

(41)

∎

The next Lemma enables us to decide if a family of bounded operators
B
gives rise to a bounded multiplication operator
B\left(X\right)
between
L^{q}
- and
L^{2}
-spaces.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
B\in L^{2,End}_{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\left(\mathcal{D},H^{r}\right)\right)
. Assume for
p\geq 2
,

\displaystyle x\mapsto\left\|B\left(x\right)\right\|_{B\left(H^{r}\right)}\in L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right),

(42)

then for
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2}
,

\displaystyle L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathrm{Dom}\left(B\left(X\right)\right),

\displaystyle\left\|B\left(X\right)\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)\to L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|x\mapsto\left\|B\left(x\right)\right\|_{B\left(H^{r}\right)}\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}.

(43)

Proof.

The statement follows immediately by Hölder’s inequality.
∎

Since we want to use Kato-Rellich’s theorem for perturbations of the operator
H_{0}^{\frac{N}{2}}
, we will need to know if a multiplication operator
B\left(X\right)
is relatively bounded with bound strictly less than
1
. The next Lemma establishes such bounds via Sobolev inequalities.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
B\in L^{2,End}_{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\left(\mathcal{D},H^{r}\right)\right)
, and
u\geq 0
. Assume there exists
t\in\left[0,u\right]
and
p\in\left[2,+\infty\right]
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|x\mapsto\left\|B\left(x\right)\langle A_{0}\rangle^{-2t}\right\|_{B\left(H^{r}\right)}\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}<\infty,\ \text{for }u-t>\frac{d}{2p},

(44)

or

\displaystyle\left\|x\mapsto\left\|B\left(x\right)\left(A_{0}^{2}+z\right)^{-t}\right\|_{B\left(H^{r}\right)}\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}=o\left(1\right),\ z\to+\infty,

\displaystyle\text{for }\left(u=t,p=2\right)\vee\left(d\geq 3,\frac{d}{p}\in\mathbb{N},u-t=\frac{d}{2p}\right),

(45)

then

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dom}\left(H_{0}^{u}\right)

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathrm{Dom}\left(B\left(X\right)\right),

\displaystyle\left\|B\left(X\right)\left(H_{0}+z\right)^{-u}\right\|_{B\left(L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle=o\left(1\right),\ z\to+\infty.

(46)

Proof.

Let
z\geq 1
. Then for
p\in\left(2,+\infty\right]
,
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{2}
,

\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
, and
f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
,

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\Delta+z\right)^{-s}f\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)}\leq\left\|\left(\left|X\right|^{2}+z\right)^{-s}\widehat{f}\right\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\left|\xi\right|^{2}+z\right)^{-sp}{\rm d}\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(2\pi\right)^{-pd}z^{\frac{d}{2p}-s}\left\|\xi\mapsto\langle\xi\rangle^{-2s}\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)}.

(47)

Consequently for
s>\frac{d}{2p}
there is a constant
C<\infty
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\Delta+z\right)^{-s}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)\to L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)}\leq Cz^{\frac{d}{2t}-s}=o\left(1\right),\ z\to+\infty.

(48)

For
p=2
we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\Delta+z\right)^{-s}\right\|_{B\left(L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\left|X\right|^{2}+z\right)^{-s}\right\|_{B\left(L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r},\frac{1}{\left(2\pi\right)^{d}}d\xi\right)\right)}=\esssup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\left|\xi\right|^{2}+z\right)^{-s}

\displaystyle=z^{-s}=\begin{cases}o\left(1\right),\ z\to+\infty,&s>0,\\
1,&s=0.\end{cases}

(49)

If
d\geq 3
and
2s\geq\lceil\frac{d}{p}\rceil
, then the Sobolev inequality (Theorem 4.12 Part III,
[
1
]
) yields a constant
C<\infty
, such that for
z\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\Delta+z\right)^{-s}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)\to L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)}\leq C.

(50)

This result is only interesting for
\frac{d}{p}\in\mathbb{N}
, otherwise it is superseded by previous estimates.

We also note that by the commuting functional calculi of
\Delta
and
\widehat{A_{0}}
, that for
s,t\geq 0
,
s+t=u
,

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\Delta+z\right)^{s}\left(\widehat{A_{0}}^{2}+z\right)^{t}\left(H_{0}+z\right)^{-u}\right\|_{B\left(L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)\right)}\leq 1,\ z\geq 1,

(51)

Consequently we obtain for
s,t\geq 0
,
s+t=u
, by Lemma
3.3
and the assumptions in this Lemma,

\displaystyle\left\|B\left(X\right)\left(H_{0}+z\right)^{-u}\right\|_{B\left(L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|B\left(X\right)\left(\widehat{A_{0}}^{2}+z\right)^{-t}\right\|_{L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)\to L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)}\left\|\left(\Delta+z\right)^{-s}\right\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)\to L^{q}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|x\mapsto\left\|B\left(x\right)\left(A_{0}^{2}+z\right)^{-t}\right\|_{B\left(H^{r}\right)}\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}

\displaystyle\cdot\begin{cases}o\left(1\right),&s>\frac{d}{2p},\\
C,&\left(s=0,p=2\right)\vee\left(d\geq 3,\frac{d}{p}\in\mathbb{N},s=\frac{d}{2p}\right),\end{cases}\ z\to+\infty,

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle o\left(1\right),\ z\to+\infty.

(52)

∎

The next ingredient to discuss perturbations of
H_{0}
-powers is a Leibniz rule for multiplication operators
B\left(X\right)
in the next statement. The proof is standard, we however give it for self sufficiency in this operator-valued setup.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
B\in W^{N,2,End}_{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\left(\mathcal{D},H^{r}\right)\right)
. for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
. If
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\otimes\mathcal{D}
, then
B\left(X\right)f\in\mathrm{Dom}\left(\partial^{\gamma}\right)=W^{\left|\gamma\right|,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
for
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,
\left|\gamma\right|\leq N
, and

\displaystyle\partial^{\gamma}B\left(X\right)f=\sum_{\delta\leq\gamma}\binom{\gamma}{\delta}\left(\partial^{\delta,End}B\right)\left(X\right)\partial^{\gamma-\delta}f.

(53)

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
N
. The statements of the Lemma hold for
\left|\gamma\right|=0
, by Lemma
3.2
. Let us assume the statements are true for
n\leq N
. Let
\left|\widehat{\gamma}\right|=N+1
. Then there is
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, and
j\in\left\{1,\ldots,d\right\}
, such that
\widehat{\gamma}=\gamma+\delta_{j}
. Since by assumption
B\left(X\right)f\in W^{N,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
, it suffices to check that
\partial^{\gamma}B\left(X\right)f\in W^{1,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
to conclude
B\left(X\right)f\in W^{N+1,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
. Also by assumption we know that

\displaystyle\partial^{\gamma}B\left(X\right)f=\sum_{\delta\leq\gamma}\binom{\gamma}{\delta}\left(\partial^{\delta,End}B\right)\left(X\right)\partial^{\gamma-\delta}f.

(54)

Since
\left(\partial^{\delta,End}B\right)\left(X\right)\partial^{\gamma-\delta}f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
by Lemma
3.2
, we may consider
\left(\partial^{\delta,End}B\right)\left(X\right)\partial^{\gamma-\delta}f
as a
H^{r}
-valued distribution. As such, for any
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)
,

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{x_{j}}\left(\partial^{\delta,End}B\right)\left(X\right)\partial^{\gamma-\delta}f\right)\left[\phi\right]=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\partial^{\delta,End}B\right)\left(x\right)\left(\partial^{\gamma-\delta}f\right)\left(x\right)\left(\partial_{x_{i}}\phi\right)\left(x\right){\rm d}x

(55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left(\partial^{\delta,End}B\right)\left(X\right)\partial^{\gamma-\delta+\delta_{j}}f\right)\left[\phi\right]-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\partial^{\delta,End}B\right)\left(x\right)\left(\partial_{x_{i}}\left(\left(\partial^{\gamma-\delta}f\right)\phi\right)\right)\left(x\right){\rm d}x

(56)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left(\partial^{\delta,End}B\right)\left(X\right)\partial^{\gamma-\delta+\delta_{j}}f\right)\left[\phi\right]+\left(\left(\partial^{\delta+\delta_{j},End}B\right)\left(X\right)\partial^{\gamma-\delta}f\right)\left[\phi\right].

(57)

The above steps are justified by Lemma
3.2
and the fact that for any
\eta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
the derivative
\partial^{\eta}f\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\otimes\mathcal{D}
, and
\partial^{\delta,End}B,\ \partial^{\delta+\delta_{j},End}B\in L^{2,End}_{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\left(\mathcal{D},H^{r}\right)\right)
. This argument also yields

\displaystyle\left(\partial^{\delta,End}B\right)\left(X\right)\partial^{\gamma-\delta+\delta_{j}}+\left(\partial^{\delta+\delta_{j},End}B\right)\left(X\right)\partial^{\gamma-\delta}f\in L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right),

(58)

which implies by (
55
) that

\displaystyle\left(\partial^{\delta,End}B\right)\left(X\right)\partial^{\gamma-\delta}f\in W^{1,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right),

(59)

and thus by (
54
),
\partial^{\gamma}B\left(X\right)f\in W^{1,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
. Finally we combine (
54
) and (
55
) to conclude

\displaystyle\partial^{\widehat{\gamma}}B\left(X\right)f

\displaystyle=\sum_{\delta\leq\gamma}\binom{\gamma}{\delta}\left(\left(\partial^{\delta+\delta_{j},End}B\right)\left(X\right)\partial^{\gamma-\delta}f+\left(\partial^{\delta,End}B\right)\left(X\right)\partial^{\gamma-\delta+\delta_{j}}f\right)

(60)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\leq\widehat{\gamma}}\binom{\widehat{\gamma}}{\delta}\left(\partial^{\delta,End}B\right)\left(X\right)\partial^{\widehat{\gamma}-\delta}f.

(61)

∎

The next Lemma is closely related with the Leibniz rule in Lemma
3.5
, however its usefulness will become only apparent in the next section, when we discuss trace-class properties of a certain class of operators in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
. Its rough content is that differentiability of an operator family
B
enables us to extract smoothing operators in
L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)
from the left of the multiplication operator
B\left(X\right)
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
B\in W^{2l+k,2,End}_{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\left(\mathcal{D},H^{r}\right)\right)
for
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
k\in\left\{0,1\right\}
. Then there are constant matrices

\displaystyle C^{k,l}_{\gamma,\delta}\in\left\{\lambda_{0}1_{H^{r}}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_{j}c\left({\rm d}x^{j}\right),\ \lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}+i\mathbb{Z},\ j\in\left\{0,\ldots,d\right\}\right\},\ \gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{N}^{d},

(62)

such that for
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\otimes\mathcal{D}
,

\displaystyle B\left(X\right)f=\left(ic\nabla+1\right)^{-k}\left(\Delta+1\right)^{-l}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma,\delta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}}{{\left|\gamma+\delta\right|\leq 2l+k}}}C_{\gamma,\delta}^{k,l}\left(\partial^{\gamma,End}B\right)\left(X\right)\partial^{\delta}f,

(63)

Proof.

The conditions on
B
imply by Lemma
3.5
that for any
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,
\left|\gamma\right|\leq 2l+k
,

\displaystyle B\left(X\right)f\in\mathrm{Dom}\left(\partial^{\gamma}\right).

(64)

On the other hand we may expand
\left(c\nabla+i\right)^{k}\left(\Delta+1\right)^{l}
on
C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\otimes\mathcal{D}
to find matrices

\displaystyle c^{k,l}_{\gamma,\delta}\in\left\{\lambda_{0}1_{H^{r}}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_{j}c\left({\rm d}x^{j}\right),\ \lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}+i\mathbb{Z},\ j\in\left\{0,\ldots,d\right\}\right\},

(65)

such that

\displaystyle\left(c\nabla+i\right)^{k}\left(\Delta+1\right)^{l}g=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}}{{\left|\alpha\right|\leq 2l+k}}}c_{\alpha}^{k,l}\partial^{\alpha}g,\ g\in C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\otimes\mathcal{D}.

(66)

Thus
B\left(X\right)f\in\mathrm{Dom}\left(\left(c\nabla+i\right)^{k}\left(\Delta+1\right)^{l}\right)
, and by Lemma
3.5
,

\displaystyle\left(\left(c\nabla+i\right)^{k}\left(\Delta+1\right)^{l}\right)B\left(X\right)f=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{d}}}{{\left|\gamma\right|\leq 2l+k}}}c_{\alpha}^{k,l}\sum_{\delta\leq\gamma}\binom{\gamma}{\delta}\left(\partial^{\delta,End}B\right)\left(X\right)\partial^{\gamma-\delta}f

(67)

By applying
\left(c\nabla+i\right)^{-k}\left(\Delta+1\right)^{-l}
to the left, and using that the resolvents of
c\nabla
and
\Delta
commute, we obtain the claimed statement with
C^{k,l}_{\gamma,\delta}=\binom{\gamma+\delta}{\gamma}c_{\gamma}^{k,l}
.
∎

We arrive at the fundamental result of this section which establishes conditions on the operator family
A
such that the Callias operator
D
is closed and
\mathrm{Dom}\left(\left(D^{\ast}D\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}\right)=\mathrm{Dom}\left(\left(DD^{\ast}\right)^{\frac{N}{2}}\right)=\mathrm{Dom}\left(H_{0}^{\frac{N}{2}}\right)
, for given
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
N\geq 1
, and
A\in W^{N-1,2,End}_{loc}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\left(\mathcal{D},H^{r}\right)\right)
. Assume for
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,
1\leq\left|\gamma\right|\leq N-1
, there exist
t\in\left[0,\frac{\left|\gamma\right|}{2}\right]
and
p\in\left[2,+\infty\right]
such that

\displaystyle\left\|x\mapsto\left\|\left(\partial^{\gamma,End}A\right)\left(x\right)\langle A_{0}\rangle^{-2t}\right\|_{B\left(H^{r}\right)}\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}<\infty,\ \text{for }\frac{\left|\gamma\right|}{2}-t>\frac{d}{2p},

(68)

or

\displaystyle\left\|x\mapsto\left\|\left(\partial^{\gamma,End}A\right)\left(x\right)\left(A_{0}^{2}+z\right)^{-t}\right\|_{B\left(H^{r}\right)}\right\|_{L^{p}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)}=o\left(1\right),\ z\to+\infty,

\displaystyle\text{for }\left(\frac{\left|\gamma\right|}{2}=t,p=2\right)\vee\left(d\geq 3,\frac{d}{p}\in\mathbb{N},\frac{\left|\gamma\right|}{2}-t=\frac{d}{2p}\right),

(69)

As well as

\displaystyle\esssup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\|A_{0}^{n}\left(A\left(x\right)-A_{0}\right)\left(A_{0}^{2}+z\right)^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}\right\|_{B\left(H^{r}\right)}=o\left(1\right),\ z\to+\infty,\ 0\leq n\leq N-1.

(70)

Denote for
j\in\left\{0,1\right\}
the operators
D_{j}=\left(-1\right)^{j}ic\nabla+A\left(X\right)
, and
D_{j,0}=\left(-1\right)^{j}ic\nabla+\widehat{A_{0}}
. Then for any
\eta\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{N}
, the operator
\prod_{k=1}^{N}D_{\eta_{k}}
is closed with

\displaystyle\mathrm{Dom}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{N}D_{\eta_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Dom}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{N}D_{\eta_{k},0}\right)=\mathrm{Dom}\left(H_{0}^{\frac{N}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=W^{N,2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},H^{r}\right)\cap L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathrm{Dom}\left(A_{0}^{N}\right)\right).

(71)

Additionally
\prod_{k=1}^{N}D_{\eta_{k}}
is self-adjoint if it is symmetric.

Proof.

We first note that by Lemma
3.4
we have
\mathrm{Dom}\left(\left(\partial^{\gamma,End}A\right)\left(X\right)\right)\supseteq\mathrm{Dom}\left(H_{0}^{\frac{\left|\gamma\right|+1}{2}}\right)
for
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,
1\leq\left|\gamma\right|\leq N-1
, and

For
0\leq k\leq N-1
and
\phi\in\mathrm{rg}\left(\left.\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{N}\right|_{C_{c}^{\infty}\left(\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)\otimes\mathcal{D}}\right)
one obtains

\displaystyle\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{-k-1}\left[D,D^{\ast}\right]\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{-N+k}\phi=2i\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{-k-1}\left[c\nabla,A\left(X\right)\right]\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{-N+k}\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{-k-1}\left[c\left({\rm d}x^{j}\right),A\right]\left(X\right)\partial_{x^{j}}\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{-N+k}\phi

\displaystyle+2i\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{-k-1}c\left({\rm d}x^{j}\right)\left(\partial^{End}_{x^{j}}A\right)\left(X\right)\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{-N+k}\phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2i\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{-k-1}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left[c\left({\rm d}x^{j}\right),A\right]\left(X\right)\partial_{x^{j}}\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{-N+k}\phi

\displaystyle+2i\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{-k-1}\left(c\nabla^{End}A\right)\left(X\right)\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{-N+k}\phi,

(138)

where we use that the conditions posed on
A
imply that
\mathrm{Dom}\left(A\left(x\right)\right)=\mathrm{Dom}\left(A_{0}\right)
, for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
(see Remark
3.8
), and that
c\left({\rm d}x^{j}\right)\left(\mathcal{D}\right)\subseteq\mathrm{Dom}\left(A_{0}\right)
,
j\in\left\{1,\ldots,d\right\}
. Since
\partial_{x^{j}}\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
is bounded for
j\in\left\{1,\ldots,d\right\}
, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{-k-1}\left[c\left({\rm d}x^{j}\right),A\right]\left(X\right)\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{-N+k+\frac{1}{2}},\ j\in\left\{1,\ldots,d\right\},

\displaystyle\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{-k-1}\left(c\nabla^{End}A\right)\left(X\right)\left(H_{0}+1\right)^{-N+k},

(139)

admit trace-class extensions for
0\leq k\leq N-1
, which is a direct consequence of Corollary
4.4
.
∎

4.7
should provide a large enough store of operator families
A
such that one may investigate trace formulas and spectral shift functions of the pair
\left(D^{\ast}D,DD^{\ast}\right)
, which might lead to new results for the index of the Callias operator
D
in this abstract setup.

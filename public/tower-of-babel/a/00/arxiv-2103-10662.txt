Ermakov-Pinney equation for time-varying mass systems
Bijan Bagchi, Achal Vinod

Abstract
We extend Fring-Tenney approach of constructing invariants of constant mass time-dependent system to the case of a time-dependent mass particle. From a coupled set of equations described in terms of guiding parameter functions, we track down a modified Ermakov-Pinney equation involving a time-dependent mass function. As a concrete example we focus on an exponential choice of the mass function.

2
Constraining equations for time-dependent coefficients

Let us begin with the time-dependent non-relativistic Schrödinger equation which in one-dimension reads

i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi(x,t)=\left[-\frac{1}{2m}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}+V(x,t)\right]\psi(x,t)

(2.1)

where
m=m(t)
. We work with natural units
\hbar=c=1
.

For a general quantum mechanical Hamiltonian
\hat{H}
, the time evolution operator
U(t,t_{0})
obeys
[
24
]

i\dot{U}(t,t_{0})=\hat{H}U(t,t_{0}),\quad U(t_{0},t_{0})=I,\quad t>t_{0}

(2.2)

If the Hamiltonian does not depend on time then the solution of the above equation is simply

U(t,t_{0})=e^{-i\hat{H}(t-t_{0})}

(2.3)

where Hamiltonian is considered to be Hermitian.

Against the background of the evolution equation obeying
(2.2)
, we note that the time-dependent invariant operator
\hat{I}(t)
satisfies

\frac{d\hat{I}}{dt}=\dot{\hat{I}}(t)+\frac{1}{i}[\hat{I},H]=0

(2.4)

where
H
is time-dependent
\hat{H}=\hat{H}(x,t)
. As a concrete model of our analysis we consider the following representative Hamiltonian

\hat{H}(x,t)=\frac{p^{2}}{2m(t)}+V(x,t)+xE(t)

(2.5)

where the mass and the potential have been taken to be time-dependent. The latter is assumed to be factorizable
2
2

2

For a wider class of time-dependent potentials that are factorizable and also exactly solvable see
[
25
,
26
]
in that
V(x,t)=\kappa(t)V(x)
,
where
\kappa
is a function of
t
. Additionally, a Stark like term is included in
(2.5)
which points to the presence of an electric field
E(t)
. The angular frequency
\omega
is taken to be constant.

The choice of the representation for the invariant is flexible. In the following we adopt the form

\hat{I}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\alpha(t)p^{2}+\gamma(t)x+\delta(t)\{x,p\}+\varepsilon(t)x^{2}\right]

(2.6)

where
\{x,p\}=xp+px
and the coefficient functions
\alpha,\gamma,\delta
and
\varepsilon
are time-dependent functions. Of course, these are distinct from
m(t)
or
\kappa(t)
and have to be solved for through the consistency equations.

We now address the simplest class of time-dependent Hamiltonian as induced by a varying mass harmonic oscillator potential. In such a case
\kappa
corresponds to
m(t)
while
V(x)=\frac{1}{2}\omega^{2}x^{2}
. Substituting in
(2.4)
we obtain through the use of
(2.5)
the commutator

[\hat{I},\hat{H}]=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\alpha m(-\hbar^{2}\omega^{2}-2i\hbar\omega^{2}xp)+\alpha E(-2i\hbar)p+\frac{\gamma}{2m}(2i\hbar)p+\frac{\delta}{2m}(4i\hbar)p^{2}\\
+\delta m(-2i\hbar)\omega^{2}x^{2}+\delta E(-2i\hbar)x+\frac{\varepsilon}{2m}(4i\hbar xp+2\hbar^{2})\bigg)

(2.7)

When substituted in
(2.4)
the following set of conditions is easily obtained

\displaystyle\dot{\alpha}=-\frac{2\delta}{m}

(2.8)

\displaystyle\dot{\delta}=\alpha m\omega^{2}-\frac{\varepsilon}{m}

(2.9)

\displaystyle\dot{\varepsilon}=2\delta m\omega^{2}

(2.10)

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}=2\delta E

(2.11)

We also have an additional relation

\displaystyle\gamma=2m\alpha E

(2.12)

along with a closed form for
E(t)
given by

E(t)=\frac{1}{m}e^{\int Q(t)dt},\quad Q(t)=\frac{3\delta}{\alpha m}

(2.13)

We should mention that in the constant mass case, the set of equations
(2.8)-(2.12)
reduce to those of the corresponding ones in
[

3
Ermakov-Pinney equation and its modification

We aim at writing down a modified EP equation in the presence of
m(t)
. Redefining
\alpha=\sigma^{2}
the remaining parameters read

\displaystyle\delta=-m\dot{\sigma}\sigma

(3.1)

\displaystyle\gamma=2\sigma^{2}mE(t)

(3.2)

\displaystyle\varepsilon=m^{2}\dot{\sigma}^{2}+m^{2}\sigma\ddot{\sigma}+m\dot{m}\dot{\sigma}\sigma+m^{2}\sigma^{2}\omega^{2}

(3.3)

Introducing
\tau\equiv\tau(\sigma)
as an arbitrary function of
\sigma
which is defined through

\displaystyle m^{2}\sigma\ddot{\sigma}+m\dot{m}\dot{\sigma}\sigma+m^{2}\sigma^{2}\omega^{2}=\frac{\tau m^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}

(3.4)

it is clear that
(3.4)
stands as the signature of a dissipative EP equation for the time-varying mass case

\ddot{\sigma}+\omega^{2}\sigma+\frac{\dot{m}}{m}\dot{\sigma}=\frac{\tau}{\sigma^{3}}

(3.5)

We immediately see that when
m
is stationary and
\tau
is a constant equal to
\lambda^{2}
the standard equation
(1.1)
is recovered.

(3.3)
now reads

\varepsilon=m^{2}\dot{\sigma}^{2}+\frac{\tau m^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}

(3.6)

Clearly, no unique set of solutions of the parameter functions exists satisfying
\sigma
-dependent equations
(3.1)
,
(3.2)
and
(3.6)
, for a given functional choice of the mass function
m(t)
and the electric field
E(t)
.

Identifying the EP equation in the extended form
(3.5)
constitutes the central result of this note. Although not exactly coincident with
(3.5)
, a dissipative
EP
equation was also derived by Fring and Tenney
[
12
]
but in a different context

\ddot{\chi}+f^{2}\chi-\frac{\dot{f}}{f}\dot{\chi}=\frac{f^{2}}{\chi^{3}},\quad\chi=\chi(t)

(3.7)

where
f
is a time-dependent parametrizing function.
f
seems to play the analog of the mass function but notice that while the coefficient of
\sigma
in
(3.5)
is a constant, the same in the corresponding
(3.7)
is a function of time.

Our next endeavour would be to analyse
(3.6)
by taking its time-derivative and comparing with
(2.10)
. Towards this end we obtain the pair of equations

\displaystyle\dot{\varepsilon}=2m\dot{m}\dot{\sigma}^{2}+2\dot{\sigma}\ddot{\sigma}m^{2}+\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}(2m\dot{m}\tau+m^{2}\tau^{\prime}\dot{\sigma})-\frac{2m^{2}\tau\dot{\sigma}}{\sigma^{3}}

(3.8)

\displaystyle\dot{\varepsilon}=-2m^{2}\omega^{2}\sigma\dot{\sigma}

(3.9)

where
(3.1)
is used. Equating them we straightforwardly obtain a differential equation for
\tau

\tau^{\prime}+2\frac{\dot{m}}{m\dot{\sigma}}\tau=0

(3.10)

where the prime denotes a derivative with respect to
\sigma
.

4
Solving the modified
EP
equation

Since
\tau=\tau(\sigma)
let us assume a monomial form for it

\tau=\tau_{0}\sigma^{4}

(4.1)

where
\tau_{0}
is a constant assumed to be
>0
. The EP equation, in principle, admits of complex solutions given by the general form

\sigma(t)=\xi(t)e^{i\eta(t)},\quad\xi,\eta\in\Re

(4.2)

where
\xi
and
\eta
are linked by a set of coupled equations

\displaystyle\ddot{\xi}+\frac{\dot{m}}{m}\dot{\xi}+(\Omega^{2}-\dot{\eta}^{2})=0

(4.3)

\displaystyle 2\dot{\xi}\dot{\eta}+\xi\left(\ddot{\eta}+\frac{\dot{m}}{m}\dot{\eta}\right)=0

(4.4)

where
\Omega^{2}=(\omega^{2}-\tau_{0})
. These are very complicated equations to tackle. So we concentrate on real solutions only and in this regard consider a mass function
m(t)
of the exponential type

m(t)=m_{0}e^{-qt},\quad q>0

(4.5)

where its damping character is in keeping with [21, 22] but with respect to the t-variable. For
\sigma
we find the solution

\sigma(t)=e^{qt/2}

(4.6)

subject to the the following constraint on
\tau_{0}

\tau_{0}=\omega^{2}-\frac{q^{2}}{4}

(4.7)

\tau
is insensitive to the sign of
q
. For completeness we note that
\delta
turns out to be a constant while the coefficient function
\gamma
and the electric field
E
show a decaying behaviour
\backsim e^{-qt/2}
.

\omega

\tau_{0}=0.01

m

5
Summary

In this article we considered the possibility of introducing a time-dependent mass while constructing an invariant for a time-dependent quantum Hamiltonian. Assuming a time-dependent harmonic oscillator with a Stark-like Hamiltonian we were able to derive the invariant conditions in their entirety. We then obtained the time-dependent mass version of the
EP
equation that included an additional term as compared to the constant-mass case. Because of its strongly nonlinear character we inquired into simple parameter variations to assess the mass function. For the specific case of a damping mass with a certain input value of
\tau_{0}
, its graphical behavior was plotted over a wide range of
t
.

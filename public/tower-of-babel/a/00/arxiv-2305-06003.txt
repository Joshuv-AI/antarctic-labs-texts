On Riccati contraction in time-varying linear-quadratic control
Jintao Sun, Michael Cantoni

Abstract
Contraction properties of the Riccati operator are studied within the context of non-stationary linear-quadratic optimal control. A lifting approach is used to obtain a bound on the rate of strict contraction, with respect to the Riemannian metric, across a sufficient number of iterations. This number of iterations is related to an assumed uniform controllability and observability property of the dynamics and stage-cost in the original formulation of the problem.



I
Introduction

Consider the following infinite-horizon linear-quadratic (LQ) optimal control problem:

\displaystyle\min_{u,x}

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}x_{k}^{\prime}Q_{k}x_{k}+u_{k}^{\prime}R_{k}u_{k}

(1a)

subject to
x_{0}=\xi
and

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=A_{k}x_{k}+B_{k}u_{k},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(1b)

where
Q_{k}
is positive semi-definite,
R_{k}
is positive definite,
A_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
,
B_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
, and the initial state
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given. The task is to determine the cost minimizing input
u=(u_{0},u_{1},\ldots)
and corresponding state sequence
x=(x_{0},x_{1},\dots)
over the infinite horizon.

Under assumptions of uniform stabilizability and uniform detectability, it is well-known (e.g., see
[
1
,
2
]
) that the optimal policy for (
1
) is given by the stabilizing linear time-varying state-feedback controller

\displaystyle u_{k}=-(R_{k}+B_{k}^{\prime}P_{k+1}B_{k})^{-1}B_{k}^{\prime}P_{k+1}A_{k}x_{k},~k\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(2)

where
P_{k}
is the unique positive semi-definite solution of

\displaystyle P_{k}=\mathcal{R}_{k}(P_{k+1})

(3)

and the Riccati operator is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k}(P):=Q_{k}+A_{k}^{\prime}\left(P-PB_{k}(R_{k}\!+\!B_{k}^{\prime}PB_{k})^{-1}B_{k}^{\prime}P\right)A_{k}.

(4)

For the infinite-horizon problem, the recursion (
3
) does not have a boundary condition. Its unique symmetric positive semi-definite solution is stabilizing and attractive for all symmetric positive semi-definite solutions of (
3
) over a finite horizon with suitable boundary conditions
[
3
]
.

The cost associated with the optimal policy (
2
) is given by
\xi^{\prime}P_{0}\xi

[
1
]
.
By the principle of optimality
[
2
]
, the least infinite-horizon cost is achieved by the receding finite-horizon control scheme given by the feedback policy

\displaystyle u_{k}=u_{k}^{*}(x_{k})

for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
where

\displaystyle(u_{k}^{*}(x_{k}),\dots,u_{k+T-1}^{*}(x_{k}))

\displaystyle\!=\argmin_{u}\!\!\sum_{l=k}^{k+T-1}\!\!\!x_{l}^{\prime}Q_{l}x_{l}+u_{l}^{\prime}R_{l}u_{l}+x_{k+T}^{\prime}P_{k+T}x_{k+T}

(5)

with

x_{l+1}=A_{l}x_{l}+B_{l}u_{l}
for
l\in[k:k+T-1]
. However, without exact knowledge of the problem data
(A_{k},B_{k},Q_{k},R_{k})
beyond the prediction horizon, one can only approximate the optimal cost-to-go
x_{k+T}^{\prime}P_{k+T}x_{k+T}
with an alternative terminal penalty.

In the stationary setting, where
A_{k}=A
,
B_{k}=B
,
Q_{k}=Q
,
R_{k}=R
, and
\mathcal{R}_{k}=\mathcal{R}
in all
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,
the optimal cost-to-go matrix
P_{k+T}
for (
5
) is the constant stabilizing solution to the corresponding algebraic Riccati equation
P=\mathcal{R}_{k}(P)=\mathcal{R}(P)
. When it is approximated by any constant positive semi-definite
\tilde{P}
such that
\mathcal{R}(\tilde{P})\preceq\tilde{P}
, the resulting control policy remains stabilizing
[
4
]
.
Riccati contraction based analysis of receding horizon schemes appears in
[
5
,
6
]
.
In
[
6
]
, it is shown that the closed-loop performance degradation is bounded in terms of the induced
2
-norm of the approximation error
P-\tilde{P}
.

In the stationary non-linear continuous-time setting of
[
7
]
, it is established that when the approximate terminal penalty is set to zero, there exists a finite prediction horizon that guarantees stability. A similar result appears in
[
8
]
, where a performance bound is also quantified for the zero terminal penalty approximation in a stationary non-linear discrete-time setting. Related time-varying results appear in
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
. In
[
9
]
, the performance degradation is analyzed for receding-horizon approximations with a constraint that corresponds to infinite terminal penalty.
In
[
10
]
, it is shown that for linear dynamics, a sufficiently long prediction horizon, and a time-invariant terminal penalty, the dynamic regret decays exponentially with respect to the horizon.

Subsequent consideration of the contraction properties of (
4
) is motivated by the possibility of using the result of iterating (
3
) over a finite horizon for a suitable boundary condition, to approximate
P_{k+T}
in (
5
). Riccati contraction informed design of horizon length and terminal penalty, given uncertain problem data, is the topic of ongoing investigation. Initial results are reported in
[
11
]
. Here, the main contribution relates to characterizing a bound on the strict contraction rate of a sufficient number of iterations of the Riccati recursion (
3
), building upon a foundation result from
[
12
]
. The sufficient number of iterations is related to an assumed uniform controllability and observability property of the time-varying dynamics and stage costs. The development involves a lifted reformulation of the problem (
1
), in which the system model evolves by this fixed number of steps per stage. The fixed number of steps and the corresponding bound on the strict contraction rate are given explicitly in terms of the original problem data.

The paper is organized as follows.
Contraction properties of the Riccati operator with respect to the Riemannian metric are presented in Section
II
. The lifting approach for characterizing the strict contraction rate is developed in Section
III
. A numerical example is presented in Section
IV

Lemma 1
.

The operator
\mathcal{R}_{k}
in (
4

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{k}(P)=(E_{k}P+F_{k})(G_{k}P+H_{k})^{-1},

(6)

\displaystyle E_{k}

\displaystyle=A_{k}^{\prime}+Q_{k}A_{k}^{-1}B_{k}R_{k}^{-1}B_{k}^{\prime},

(7a)

\displaystyle F_{k}

\displaystyle=Q_{k}A_{k}^{-1},

(7b)

\displaystyle G_{k}

\displaystyle=A_{k}^{-1}B_{k}R_{k}^{-1}B_{k}^{\prime},

(7c)

\displaystyle H_{k}

\displaystyle=A_{k}^{-1}.

(7d)

Definition 1
.

The Riemannian distance between
U,V\in\mathbb{S}^{n}_{++}
is given by

\displaystyle\delta(U,V)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log^{2}\lambda_{i}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

where

are the eigenvalues of

Note,

]
.
The following result is taken from
[

Proposition 1
.

Consider the operator
\mathcal{R}_{k}
in (
6
).
If the corresponding matrices in (
7
) are such that
E_{k}
is non-singular and
F_{k}E_{k}^{\prime},E_{k}^{\prime}G_{k}\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{n}
, then for any
X,Y\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\delta(\mathcal{R}_{k}(X),\mathcal{R}_{k}(Y))\leq\delta(X,Y).

Further, if

F_{k}E_{k}^{\prime},E_{k}^{\prime}G_{k}\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{n}
, then for any
X,Y\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\delta(\mathcal{R}_{k}(X),\mathcal{R}_{k}(Y))\leq\rho_{k}\cdot\delta(X,Y)

(8)

with

\rho_{k}=\zeta_{k}/(\zeta_{k}+\epsilon_{k})<1
,
where

\displaystyle\zeta_{k}=\|(F_{k}E_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}\|_{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad\epsilon_{k}=\lambda_{\min}((E_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}G_{k}^{\prime}).

(9)

Under Assumption
1
, since
R_{k}+B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}Q_{k}A_{k}^{-1}B_{k}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}_{++}
, application of the Woodbury matrix identity yields

\displaystyle E_{k}^{-1}

\displaystyle=(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}\!-(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}Q_{k}A_{k}^{-1}B_{k}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\times(R_{k}+B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}Q_{k}A_{k}^{-1}B_{k})^{-1}B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}.

That is,
E_{k}
is non-singular. On the other hand,

\displaystyle F_{k}E_{k}^{\prime}=Q_{k}+Q_{k}A_{k}^{-1}B_{k}R_{k}^{-1}B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}Q_{k}

(10)

and

\displaystyle E_{k}^{\prime}G_{k}=B_{k}(R_{k}^{-1}+R_{k}^{-1}B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}Q_{k}A_{k}^{-1}B_{k}R_{k}^{-1})B_{k}^{\prime}

(11)

are positive semi-definite but not necessarily positive definite. So in view of Proposition
1
and Lemma
1
, the operator
\mathcal{R}_{k}
in (
4
) is a contraction, but not necessarily a strict contraction. A sufficient condition for strict contraction follows.

Proposition 2
.

Consider
\mathcal{R}_{k}
in (
4
).
If
Q_{k}\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{n}
, and
B_{k}
has full row rank, then for any
X,Y\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{n}
, (
8
) holds with
{\rho_{k}=\zeta_{k}/(\zeta_{k}+\epsilon_{k})}<1
, where

\displaystyle\zeta_{k}\!

\displaystyle=\!\|(Q_{k}+Q_{k}A_{k}^{-1}B_{k}R_{k}^{-1}B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}Q_{k})^{-1}\|_{2},

(12a)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{k}\!

\displaystyle=\!\lambda_{\min}(A_{k}^{-1}B_{k}(R_{k}\!+\!B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})\!^{-1}Q_{k}A_{k}^{-1}B_{k})\!^{-1}\!B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})\!^{-1}).

(12b)

Proof.

From (
10
) and (
11
), if
Q_{k}
is positive definite and
B_{k}
has full row rank, then
F_{k}E_{k}^{\prime},E_{k}^{\prime}G_{k}\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{n}
, and the strict contraction properties follow from Proposition
1
. Consider
E_{k},F_{k},G_{k}
in (
7
). Then, (

) leads to (

). In particular, by application of the Woodbury matrix identity,

\displaystyle(E_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}G_{k}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\!(A_{k}+B_{k}R_{k}^{-1}B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}Q_{k})^{-1}B_{k}R_{k}^{-1}B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\!(I_{n}\!+\!A_{k}^{-1}B_{k}R_{k}^{-1}B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}Q_{k})^{-1}\!A_{k}^{-1}B_{k}R_{k}^{-1}B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\!(I_{n}\!-\!A_{k}^{-1}B_{k}(R_{k}\!+\!B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})\!^{-1}\!Q_{k}A_{k}^{-1}B_{k})^{-1}\!B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})\!^{-1}Q_{k})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times A_{k}^{-1}B_{k}R_{k}^{-1}B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\!A_{k}^{-1}B_{k}\Big(I_{n}\!-\!(R_{k}\!+\!B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}Q_{k}A_{k}^{-1}B_{k})^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}Q_{k}A_{k}^{-1}B_{k}\Big)R_{k}^{-1}B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\!A_{k}^{-1}\!B_{k}(R_{k}\!+\!B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1}Q_{k}A_{k}^{-1}B_{k})^{-1}\!R_{k}R_{k}^{-1}B_{k}^{\prime}(A_{k}^{\prime})^{-1},

which with (

∎

A numerical example is presented to illustrate the strict contraction properties of the Riccati operator. Consider the following instance of the time-varying LQ control problem (

V
Conclusion

Our attention is focused on the non-stationary Riccati operator associated with the time-varying LQ control problem. The lifting approach presented in this paper provides a procedure to measure the strict contraction rate of the non-stationary Riccati operator. Further extensions to the results in this paper may be possible by replacing the controllability and observability assumptions with weaker assumptions such as stabilizability and detectability. Future work is focused on the impact of error in the cost-to-go approximations on the performance of the receding horizon scheme.

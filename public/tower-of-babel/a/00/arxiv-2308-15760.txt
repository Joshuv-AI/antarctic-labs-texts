M. H. Li


Abstract




Variational Analysis of Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz Property, Exponent and Modulus

Abstract
: The Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KŁ) property, exponent and modulus have played a very important role in the study of global convergence and rate of convergence for optimal algorithms. In this paper, at a stationary point of a locally lower semicontinuous function, we obtain complete characterizations of the KŁ property and the KŁ modulus via the outer limiting subdifferential of an auxilliary function and a newly-introduced subderivative function respectively. In particular, for a class of prox-regular, twice epi-differentiable and subdifferentially continuous functions, we show that the KŁ property and the KŁ modulus can be described by its Moreau envelopes and a quadratic growth condition. We apply the obtained results to establish the KŁ property with exponent
\frac{1}{2}
and to provide calculation of the modulus for a smooth function, the pointwise maximum of finitely many smooth functions and regularized functions respectively. These functions often appear in the modelling of structured optimization problems.

Keywords
: Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz property, outer limiting subdifferential, subderivative, Moreau envelope, prox-regular and twice epi-differentiable function.

49J53, 90C30

Introduction

In connection with many important notions in variational analysis and optimization such as stability, error bounds and the strong metric subregularity of subgradient mappings, the property of quadratic growth has been studied intensively, see
[
1
,
5
,
17
,
19
,
22
,
43
]
and references therein. Recently this property has been used in
[
7
,
18
,
24
]
for establishing the linear convergence of optimization algorithms.
For an extended-real-valued function
f
and a point
\bar{x}
where 0 is a subgradient, it is well-known
[
43
, Theorem 13.24]
that the quadratic growth condition holds at
\bar{x}
if and only if
d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)(w)>0
for all
w\not=0
, where
d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)
is the second subderivative of
f
at
\bar{x}
for 0 and can be regarded as a construction on primal space.

For an extended-real-valued function
f
which is prox-regular, twice epi-differentiable and subdifferentially continuous at
\bar{x}
for 0, it has recently been shown in
[
14
, Theorem 3.7]
, among other things, that the quadratic growth condition holds at
\bar{x}
if and only if
D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)
is positive definite in the sense that
\langle v,w\rangle>0
for all
v\in D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)(w)
with
w\not=0
. Here
D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)
is the subgradient graphical derivative of
f
at
\bar{x}
for 0, which is defined as the graphical derivative acting on the subgradient mapping
\partial f
and can be regarded as a construction on dual space. This result is a generalization of the corresponding one in
[
1
, Corollay 3.7]
presented for a convex function.

The class of prox-regular functions covers all lower semicontinuous, proper, convex functions, lower-
\mathcal{C}^{2}
functions, strongly amenable functions, and fully amenable functions, hence a large core of functions of interest in variational analysis and optimization, see
[
40
]
and
[
43
, Chapter 13]
. The twice epi-differentiability of a function was first introduced in
[
41
]
, and then justified in
[
42
]
for fully
amenable functions and in
[
15
,
25
]
for some composite
functions. In a recent paper
[
36
]
, the twice epi-differentiablity of composite functions was justified under parabolic regularity
[
35
]
, and some precise formulas for the second subderivatives under the metric subregularity constraint qualification were provided.
The subdifferential continuity
[
43
, Definition 13.28]
of a function is often assumed for technical reasons, which guarantees that nearness of
(x,v)
to
(\bar{x},0)
in the graph of
\partial f
automatically entails nearness of
f(x)
to
f(\bar{x})
.

Besides the important quadratic growth property, the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (for short, KŁ) property is another very important one studied extensively in variational analysis and optimization.
For an extended-real-valued function
f
and a point
\bar{x}
where
f
is finite and locally lower semicontinuous, the KŁ property of
f
at
\bar{x}
amounts to the existence of some
\epsilon>0
and
\nu\in(0,+\infty]
such that the KŁ inequality

\psi^{\prime}(f(x)-f(\bar{x}))\,d(0,\partial f(x))\geq 1

holds for all
x
with
\|x-\bar{x}\|\leq\epsilon
and
f(\bar{x})<f(x)<f(\bar{x})+\nu
, where
d(0,\partial f(x))
stands for the distance of 0 from the limiting subdifferential
\partial f(x)
of
f
at
x
, and
\psi:[0,\nu)\to\mathbbm{R}_{+}
, served as a desingularizing function, is assumed to be continuous, concave and of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
on
(0,\nu)
with
\psi(0)=0
and
\psi^{\prime}>0
over
(0,\nu)
. The term “KŁ property” was first coined in Bolte et al.
[
10
]
. The original and pioneering work of Łojasiewicz
[
33
]
and Kurdyka
[
23
]
on differentiable functions laid the foundation of the KŁ property, which was later extended to nonsmooth functions by Bolte et al.
[
8
,
9
]
. Very recently, by employing nonsmooth desingularizing functions, a generalized version of the concave KŁ property, together with its exact modulus, was introduced and studied in
[
44
]
.

In this paper, we intend to study the KŁ property associated with the widely used desingularizing function
\psi(s):=\frac{1}{\mu}s^{1-\theta}
for some exponent
\theta\in[0,1)
and modulus
\mu>0
, in which case the corresponding KŁ inequality can be written as

d(0,\partial f(x))\geq\frac{\mu}{1-\theta}(f(x)-f(\bar{x}))^{\theta}.

(1)

The KŁ property with its associated exponent (in particular the case of
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
) and the modulus plays a key role in estimating local convergence rates of many first-order algorithms, see
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
27
,
28
]
and references therein. To further elaborate the role of the KŁ property in the convergence analysis, the following questions may arise for the quadruple
(f,\bar{x},\theta,\mu)
:

(Q1)

How to verify the KŁ property for
f
at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\theta
?

(Q2)

Is it possible to argue the
sharpness
of
\theta
in the sense that
f
satisfies the KŁ property at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\theta
but not
with any exponent smaller than
\theta
?

(Q3)

What is the threshold for the modulus, i.e., the supremum of all the possible modulus
\mu>0
, along with some
\epsilon>0
and
\nu\in(0,+\infty]
, such that (
1
) holds? For the sake of simplicity, we denote such a threshold by KŁ
(f,\bar{x},\theta)
.

(Q4)

Beyond the class of
\mathcal{C}^{2}
functions as justified in
[
3
,
49
]
, is there any larger class of functions such that once
f
falls into it with the quadratic growth condition being fulfilled at
\bar{x}
, the KŁ property of
f
then holds automatically at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
?

Part of the answers to the above questions can be found in the literature.

For some answers to
(Q1)
, we refer the reader to
[
3
,
11
,
28
,
29
,
32
,
45
,
48
,
49
]
and references therein. In most of these references, the KŁ exponent
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
was verified for a convex function or a nonconvex function with a structure. By developing several important calculus rules for the KŁ exponent,
Li and Pong
[
28
]
recently estimated the KŁ exponents for functions appearing in structured optimization problems. This line of research was further explored by Yu et al.
[
48
]
to show that the KŁ exponent can be preserved under the inf-projection operation, which is a significant generalization of the operation of taking the minimum of finitely many functions.
However, for the purpose of verifying the KŁ property for general nonconvex functions
f
having no particular structure, there was no condition or framework proposed by virtue of the theory of generalized differentiation, such as the second subderivative
d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)
and the subgradient graphical derivative
D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)
just reviewed for the quadratic growth condition.

As for
(Q2)
, to our best of knowledge, there was no such a result in the literature discussing sharpness of the KŁ exponent.

Part of the answers to
(Q3)
in the case of
\theta=0
can be found in
[
12
,
13
,
20
,
30
,
31
]
in terms of the outer limiting subdifferential
\partial^{>}f(\bar{x})
of
f
at
\bar{x}
, which says that KŁ
(f,\bar{x},0)
is bounded from above by the distance of 0 from a lower estimate of
\partial^{>}f(\bar{x})
. As a subset of the limiting subdifferential
\partial f(\bar{x})
of
f
at
\bar{x}
, the outer limiting subdifferential
\partial^{>}f(\bar{x})
consists of limiting subgradients of
f
at
\bar{x}
that are generated from subgradients of
f
at nearby points
x
of
\bar{x}
with
f(x)
close enough to
f(\bar{x})
from above, see Definition
2.1
below. The notion of outer limiting subdifferentials was first introduced by Ioffe and Outrata in
[
26
]
to formulate a sufficient condition for error bounds. It was recently shown in
[
34
, Theorem 4.1]
that the level-set mapping
S:\alpha\mapsto\{x\mid f(x)\leq\alpha\}
has the Lipschitz-like property relative to
\{\alpha\mid\alpha\geq f(\bar{x})\}
at
f(\bar{x})
for
\bar{x}
if and only if 0 does not belong to the outer limiting subdifferential
\partial^{>}f(\bar{x})
. This demonstrates the ability of
\partial^{>}f(\bar{x})
to characterize stability properties.

Our main contributions and the organization of the paper are as follows.

In Section
2
, we first recall some necessary notation and mathematical preliminaries, and then for an extended-real-valued function
f
and a point
\bar{x}
where
f
is finite and locally lower semicontinuous and by utilizing the outer limiting subdifferential
\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})
with
g(x):=(\max\{f(x)-f(\bar{x}),0\})^{1-\theta}
and
\theta\in[0,1)
, we give answers to
(Q1)-(Q3)
in an abstract level as follows:

(A1)

f
satisfies the KŁ property at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\theta
if and only if
0\not\in\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})
.

(A2)

If
0\not\in\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})
with
\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})\not=\emptyset
, then
\theta
is a
sharp
KŁ exponent for
f
at
\bar{x}
.

(A3)

The KŁ modulus of
f
at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\theta
coincides with the distance of 0 from
\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})
, i.e., KŁ
(f,\bar{x},\theta)=d(0,\partial^{>}g(\bar{x}))
.

See Theorem
2.1
and its proof below for more details. The equivalent description of the KŁ property in
(A1)-(A3)
can be regarded as a limit form of the KŁ property, and hence a framework for the study of the KŁ property.

In Section
3
, for a given extended-real-valued function
f
and a point
\bar{x}
where
f
is finite and locally lower semicontinuous, we first show in Lemma
3.1
that, the outer limiting subdifferential
\partial^{>}f(\bar{x})
of
f
at
\bar{x}
includes as a subset the outer limiting subdifferential
\partial^{>}[df(\bar{x})](0)
of the subderivative
df(\bar{x})
at 0. This inclusion can be used to recover the lower estimates of
\partial^{>}f(\bar{x})
presented in
[
12
,
13
,
20
]
for functions with a particular structure. With the help of Lemma
3.1
, we further show in Theorem
3.1
that, the KŁ property of
f
at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\theta\in[0,1)
can be inherited by its
\frac{1}{1-\theta}
-th subderivative
h
(defined by (
12
)) at
0
with the same exponent
\theta
. This is done by showing that the union set (
14
) is a subset of
\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})
. Moreover, in Theorem
3.1
, we also show that KŁ
(f,\bar{x},\theta)
is bounded above by KŁ
((1-\theta)h,0,\theta)
, and that if there is
w
such that
0<h(w)<+\infty
, then
\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})\not=\emptyset
, as required in
(A2)
, can be verified. Note that the
\frac{1}{1-\theta}
-th subderivative
h
reduces to the (first) subderivative
df(\bar{x})
when
\theta=0
and to the second subderivative
d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)
when
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
.

In Section
4
, we focus on case of
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
. If
f
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for 0 and
D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)
is nonsingular in the sense that
[D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)]^{-1}(0)=\{0\}
, we show in Theorem
4.1
that
f
satisfies the KŁ property at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\frac{1}{2}
, which is
sharp
if there is some
w
such that
0<d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)(w)<+\infty
. This is done by verifying
0\not\in\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})
with
g(x):=\sqrt{\max\{f(x)-f(\bar{x}),0\}}
and then relying on
(A1)
and
(A2)
. In the circumstance that
f
is not only prox-regular but also twice epi-differentiable and subdifferentially continuous at
\bar{x}
for 0, we show in Theorem
4.1

(a)
that the union set (
14
) with
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
turns out to be equal to
\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})
and that the modulus KŁ
(f,\bar{x},\frac{1}{2})
coincides with its second subderivative counterpart KŁ
(\frac{1}{2}d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0),0,\frac{1}{2})
. Similar results for the Moreau envelope functions
e_{\lambda}f
for all
\lambda>0
sufficiently small are presented in Theorem
4.1

(b)
and some connections between the KŁ moduli of

and
e_{\lambda}f
are established in Theorem
4.1

(c)
. As a direct consequence of Theorem
4.1
, we provide in Corollary
4.1
an answer to
(Q4)
as follows:

(A4)

Whenever
f
falls into the class of prox-regular, twice epi-differentiable and subdifferentially continuous functions,
the quadratic growth condition at
\bar{x}
guarantees the KŁ property of
f
at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
.

In Section
5
, we apply Theorems
2.1
and
4.1
to establish the KŁ property with exponent
\frac{1}{2}
and to provide calculation of KŁ
(f,\bar{x},\frac{1}{2})
for functions often appearing in optimization problems: smooth functions, the pointwise max of finitely many smooth functions,
and the
\ell_{p}
(
0<p\leq 1
) regularized functions.

2
Notation and Mathematical Preliminaries

Throughout the paper we use the standard notation of variational analysis; see the seminal book
[
43
]
by Rockafellar and Wets.
The Euclidean norm of a vector
x
is denoted by
||x||
, and the inner product of vectors
x
and
y
is denoted by
\langle x,y\rangle
.
Let
{\mathbb{B}}
denote the closed unit Euclidean ball and let
{\mathbb{S}}
denote the unit sphere. We denote by
{\mathbb{B}}_{\delta}(x)
the closed ball centered at
x
with radius
\delta>0
.

Let
A\subset\mathbbm{R}^{n}
be a nonempty set. We say that
A
is locally closed at a point
x\in A
if
A\cap U
is closed for some closed neighborhood
U
of
x
. We denote the interior, the relative interior and the closure of
A
respectively by
\inte A
,
\ri A
and
\cl A
.
The distance from
x
to
A
is defined by

d(x,A):=\inf_{y\in A}||y-x||.

The indicator function
\delta_{A}
of
A
is defined by

\delta_{A}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{if}\;x\in A,\\
+\infty&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Let
x\in A
. The notions of tangent cone and various normal cones are as follows.

(a)

The tangent cone to
A
at
x
is denoted by
T_{A}(x)
, i.e.
w\in T_{A}(x)
if there exist sequences
t_{k}\downarrow 0

and
w_{k}\rightarrow w
with
x+t_{k}w_{k}\in A
for all
k
.

(b)

The regular normal cone,
\widehat{N}_{A}(x)
, to
A
at
x
is the polar cone of
T_{A}(x)
. That is,
v\in\widehat{N}_{A}(x)
if and only if
\langle v,w\rangle\leq 0
for all
w\in T_{A}(x)
.

(c)

A vector
v\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
belongs to the limiting normal cone
N_{A}(x)
to
A
at
x
,
if there exist sequences
x_{k}\to x
and
v_{k}\to v
with
x_{k}\in A
and
v_{k}\in\widehat{N}_{A}(x_{k})
for all
k
.

Let
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbbm{R}}:=\mathbbm{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
be an extended real-valued function. We denote the epigraph and the domain of
f
by

\epi f:=\{(x,\alpha)\mid f(x)\leq\alpha\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\dom f:=\{x\mid f(x)<+\infty\},

respectively. We call
f
a proper function if
f(x)<+\infty
for at least one
x\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
, and
f(x)>-\infty
for all
x\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
.
f
is said to be lower semicontinuous (for short, lsc) if
\epi f
is closed.

Let
\bar{x}
be a point with
f(\bar{x})
finite.
f
is said to be locally lsc at
\bar{x}
, if there is an
\epsilon>0
such that
all sets of the form
\{x\mid\|x-\bar{x}\|\leq\epsilon,\,f(x)\leq\alpha\}
with
\alpha\leq f(\bar{x})+\epsilon
are closed, see
[
43
, Definition 1.33]
. It is well-known that
f
is locally lsc at
\bar{x}
if and only if
\epi f
is locally closed at
\left(\bar{x},f(\bar{x})\right)
.

We say that
f
satisfies the quadratic growth condition at
\bar{x}
, if there is some
\kappa>0
such that the inequality
f(x)\geq f(\bar{x})+\frac{\kappa}{2}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}
holds for all
x
close enough to
\bar{x}
.

Definition 2.1
.

The notions of various subgradients are defined as follows.

(a)

A vector
v\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
is called a proximal subgradient of
f
at
\bar{x}
, if there exist
\rho>0
and
\delta>0
such that

f(x)\geq f(\bar{x})+\langle v,x-\bar{x}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}\rho\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2}
for all
x\in{\mathbb{B}}_{\delta}(\bar{x})
.

(b)

A vector
v\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
is a regular subgradient of
f
at
\bar{x}
, written
v\in\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x})
, if

f(x)\geq f(\bar{x})+\langle v,x-\bar{x}\rangle+o(||x-\bar{x}||)
. Equivalently,
v\in\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x})
if and only if

\liminf_{x\to\bar{x},\,x\not=\bar{x}}\frac{f(x)-f(\bar{x})-\langle v,x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\geq 0.

(c)

A vector
v\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
is a limiting subgradient of
f
at
\bar{x}
, written
v\in\partial f(\bar{x})
, if there exist sequences
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
and
v_{k}\to v
with
f(x_{k})\to f(\bar{x})
and
v_{k}\in\widehat{\partial}f(x_{k})
.

(d)

A vector
v\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
is an outer limiting subgradient of
f
at
\bar{x}
, written
v\in\partial^{>}f(\bar{x})
, if there exist sequences
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
and
v_{k}\to v
with
f(x_{k})\to f(\bar{x})
,
f(x_{k})>f(\bar{x})
and
v_{k}\in\partial f(x_{k})
.

The outer limiting subdifferential set
\partial^{>}f(\bar{x})
of
f
at
\bar{x}
was first introduced in
[
26
]
, see also
[
21
]
.
A closely related notion, called the right-sided subdifferential, was given in
[
37
]
and defined by using a weak inequality
f(x_{k})\geq f(\bar{x})
instead of the strict inequality
f(x_{k})>f(\bar{x})
used here. See
[
38
, Definition 1.100 and Theorem 1.101]
for more details on the right-sided subdifferential and its applications.

For a set-valued mapping
S:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbbm{R}^{m}
, we denote by

\gph S:=\{(x,u)\mid u\in S(x)\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\dom S:=\{x\mid S(x)\not=\emptyset\}

the graph and the domain of
S
, respectively.
The inverse mapping
S^{-1}:\mathbbm{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbbm{R}^{n}
is defined by setting
S^{-1}(u):=\{x\mid u\in S(x)\}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Consider a set-valued mapping
S:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbbm{R}^{m}
and a point
(\bar{x},\bar{u})\in\gph S
. The graphical derivative of
S
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
is
the mapping
DS(\bar{x}\mid\bar{u}):\mathbbm{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbbm{R}^{m}
defined by

z\in DS(\bar{x}\mid\bar{u})(w)\Longleftrightarrow(w,z)\in T_{\gph S}(\bar{x},\bar{u}).

The notation
DS(\bar{x}\mid\bar{u})
is simplified to
DS(\bar{x})
when
S
is single-valued
at
\bar{x}
, i.e.,
S(\bar{x})=\bar{u}
.

Following
[
16
, section 3H]
, we say that
S
is metrically subregular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}\in S(\bar{x})
with modulus
\kappa>0
if
there exists a neighborhood
U
of
\bar{x}
such that
d(x,S^{-1}(\bar{u}))\leq\kappa d(\bar{u},S(x))
for all
x\in U
. The infimum of all
such
\kappa
is the modulus of metrically subregularity, denoted by
{\rm subreg}\,S(\bar{x}\mid\bar{u})
. If in addition
\bar{x}
is an isolated point of
S^{-1}(\bar{u})
, we say that
S
is strongly metrically subregular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
. It is known from
[
16
, Theorem 4E.1]
that
S
is strongly metrically subregular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{u}
if and only if
DS(\bar{x}\mid\bar{u})^{-1}(0)=\{0\}
. Moreover, in the latter case, its modulus of (strong) metric subregularity is computed by

{\rm subreg}\,S(\bar{x}\mid\bar{u})=1/\inf\{\|v\|\mid v\in DS(\bar{x}\mid\bar{u})(w),\,w\in{\mathbb{S}}\}.

(2)

As
DS(\bar{x}\mid\bar{u})^{-1}(0)=\{0\}
means the only way to get
0\in DS(\bar{x}\mid\bar{u})(w)
is to take
w=0
, we say that the mapping
DS(\bar{x}\mid\bar{u})
is nonsingular when
DS(\bar{x}\mid\bar{u})^{-1}(0)=\{0\}
. Following
[
14
]
, we say that the mapping
DS(\bar{x}\mid\bar{u})
is positive definite if
\langle v,w\rangle>0
for all
v\in DS(\bar{x}\mid\bar{u})(w)
with
w\not=0
.

Definition 2.3

(first and second subderivatives)
.

Consider a function
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
, a point
\bar{x}\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
with
f(\bar{x})
finite and some
v\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
.

(a)

The (first) subderivative of
f
at
\bar{x}
is defined by

df(\bar{x})(w):=\liminf_{t\downarrow 0,w^{\prime}\to w}\frac{f(\bar{x}+tw^{\prime})-f(\bar{x})}{t}.

(b)

The second subderivative of
f
at
\bar{x}
for
v
is defined by

d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid v)(w):=\liminf_{t\downarrow 0,w^{\prime}\to w}\frac{f(\bar{x}+tw^{\prime})-f(\bar{x})-t\langle v,w^{\prime}\rangle}{\frac{1}{2}t^{2}}.

(c)

f
is said to be twice epi-differentiable at
\bar{x}
for
v
, if for every
w\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
and choice of
t^{\nu}\downarrow 0
there exists some
w^{\nu}\to w
such that

d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid v)(w)=\lim_{\nu\to\infty}\frac{f(\bar{x}+t^{\nu}w^{\nu})-f(\bar{x})-t^{\nu}\langle v,w^{\nu}\rangle}{\frac{1}{2}(t^{\nu})^{2}}.

Following
[
3
,
8
,
28
]
, we introduce the notion of the KŁ property associated with the widely used desingularizing function
\psi(s):=\frac{1}{\mu}s^{1-\theta}
for some exponent
\theta\in[0,1)
and modulus
\mu>0
. For some later developments, we also define the threshold for the modulus and the sharpness of the exponent.

Definition 2.4
.

Consider a function
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
and a point
\bar{x}
where
f(\bar{x})
is finite and locally lsc.

(i)

We say that
f
satisfies the KŁ property at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\theta\in[0,1)
and modulus
\mu>0
, if there exist some
\epsilon>0
and
\nu\in(0,+\infty]
so that

d(0,\partial f(x))\geq\frac{\mu}{1-\theta}\left(\max\{f(x)-f(\bar{x}),0\}\right)^{\theta}

(3)

whenever
\|x-\bar{x}\|\leq\epsilon
and
f(x)<f(\bar{x})+\nu
.

(ii)

Let
\theta\in[0,1)
. We define by KŁ
(f,\bar{x},\theta)
the supremum of all possible
\mu>0
, associated with some
\epsilon>0
and
\nu\in(0,+\infty]
, such that (
3
) holds for all
x\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
with
\|x-\bar{x}\|\leq\epsilon
and
f(x)<f(\bar{x})+\nu
, where the convention
\sup\emptyset:=0
is used.
We call KŁ
(f,\bar{x},\theta)
the KŁ modulus of
f
at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\theta
. Alternatively, we have

\mbox{K{\L}}(f,\bar{x},\theta):=\liminf_{x\to_{f}\bar{x},\,f(x)>f(\bar{x})}\frac{(1-\theta)d(0,\partial f(x))}{(f(x)-f(\bar{x}))^{\theta}},

(4)

where the lower limit is set to be
+\infty
if there is no
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
with
f(x_{k})\to f(\bar{x})+
.

(iii)

If
f
satisfies the KŁ property at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\theta\in(0,1)

but not with any exponent
\theta^{\prime}\in[0,\theta)
, we say that
\theta
is a
sharp

KŁ exponent of
f
at
\bar{x}
. By convention we say that 0 is a
sharp
KŁ
exponent of
f
at
\bar{x}
, if
f
satisfies the KŁ property at
\bar{x}
with exponent 0 and
\partial^{>}f(\bar{x})\not=\emptyset
.

In terms of outer limiting subgradients, the limit form of the KŁ property and its characteristics on exponents and moduli can be given as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

Consider a function
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
and a point
\bar{x}\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
where
f
is finite and locally lsc. Let
0\leq\theta<1
and let
g(x):=(\max\{f(x)-f(\bar{x}),0\})^{1-\theta}
. Then we have

\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})=\limsup_{x\to_{f}\;\bar{x},f(x)>f(\bar{x})}\frac{(1-\theta)\partial f(x)}{(f(x)-f(\bar{x}))^{\theta}},

(5)

and the following hold:

(a)

f
satisfies the KŁ property at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\theta
if and only if

0\not\in\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})
.

(b)

If
\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})\not=\emptyset
, then

0\in\partial^{>}\tilde{g}(\bar{x})
holds for all
\tilde{g}(x):=(\max\{f(x)-f(\bar{x}),0\})^{1-\tilde{\theta}}
with
\tilde{\theta}<\theta
.

(c)

f
satisfies the KŁ property at
\bar{x}
with a
sharp
exponent
\theta
if

0\not\in\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})
with
\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})\not=\emptyset
.

(d)

KŁ
(f,\bar{x},\theta)=d\left(0,\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})\right)
.

Proof.

As
f
is assumed to be lsc, we get from
[
46
, Proposition 2.1]
that for all
x\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
with
f(x)>f(\bar{x})
,

\partial g(x)=\frac{(1-\theta)\partial f(x)}{(f(x)-f(\bar{x}))^{\theta}}
, by virtue of which, the formula (
5
) follows immediately from the definition of the outer limiting subdifferential set.
By (
4
) and (
5
), we get the equality in
(d)
, from which the equivalence in
(a)
follows in a straightforward way.
In view of the definition of sharpness of the KŁ exponent, we get
(c)
from
(a)
and
(b)
immediately.
It remains to show
(b)
. Let
v\in\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})
and let
\tilde{\theta}<\theta
. By definition there exist
x^{k}\to_{f}\bar{x}
with
f(x^{k})>f(\bar{x})
and
v_{k}\in\partial f(x^{k})

such that
\frac{(1-\theta)v_{k}}{(f(x^{k})-f(\bar{x}))^{\theta}}\to v
implies

\frac{(1-\theta)v_{k}}{(f(x^{k})-f(\bar{x}))^{\tilde{\theta}}}=\frac{(1-\theta)v_{k}}{(f(x^{k})-f(\bar{x}))^{\theta}}(f(x^{k})-f(\bar{x}))^{\theta-\tilde{\theta}}\to 0,

which implies by definition and
[

, Proposition 2.1]
that
0\in\partial^{>}\tilde{g}(\bar{x})
. This completes the proof. ∎

3
Preservation of the KŁ property in passing to the subderivative

In this section, we are given some
\theta\in[0,1)
, a function
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
and a point
\bar{x}
where
f
is finite and locally lsc.
We will show in Theorem

that the KŁ property of

at
\bar{x}
with exponent

-th subderivative (cf. (

that, the outer limiting subdifferential of

at
\bar{x}
includes as a subset the outer limiting subdifferential of its (first) subderivative

at 0, which is a result of independent interest.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
is finite and locally lsc at
\bar{x}
. In terms of
h:=df(\bar{x})
and
W:=\{w\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}\mid 0<h(w)<+\infty\}
, the following hold:

(a)

For each
w\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
and
v\in\widehat{\partial}h(w)
, there exist
t_{l}\to 0+
,
w_{l}\to w
and
v_{l}\to v
such that
v_{l}\in\widehat{\partial}f(\bar{x}+t_{l}w_{l})
for all
l
and

h(w)=\lim_{l\to\infty}\frac{f(\bar{x}+t_{l}w_{l})-f(\bar{x})}{t_{l}}
.

(b)

If
h
is proper (i.e., h(0)=0), then for any
\bar{w}\in W
,

\partial h(\bar{w})\subset\partial^{>}h(0)\subset\partial^{>}f(\bar{x}),

(6)

where
\partial^{>}h(0)=\displaystyle\cl\bigcup_{w\in W}\partial h(w)
is nonempty if and only if

.

(c)

If

is improper (i.e.,

), the results in
(b)
hold with
\partial^{>}h_{+}(0)
in place of
\partial^{>}h(0)
.

Proof.

We first show
(a)
. Let
w\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
and let

be given arbitrarily. By the definition of subderivative, there exist some
\bar{t}_{k}\to 0+
and
\bar{w}_{k}\to w
such that

h(w)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{f(\bar{x}+\bar{t}_{k}\bar{w}_{k})-f(\bar{x})}{\bar{t}_{k}}.

(7)

Let
\alpha_{l}\to 0+
as
l\to+\infty
. For each
k
and
l
, we consider the minimization of

F_{k,l}(x):=f(x)-f(\bar{x})-\langle v,x-\bar{x}\rangle+\frac{1}{\alpha_{l}\bar{t}_{k}}||x-(\bar{x}+\bar{t}_{k}\bar{w}_{k})||^{2}

over the compact set
X:=\bar{x}+\bar{t}_{k}\bar{w}_{k}+\bar{t}_{k}\alpha_{l}{\mathbb{B}}
. Since
F_{k,l}
is locally lsc at
\bar{x}
due to
f
being assumed to be locally lsc at
\bar{x}
, the optimal solution set
\argmin_{x\in X}F_{k,l}(x)
is nonempty. For each
k
and
l
, let
u_{k,l}\in{\mathbb{B}}
be such that
\bar{x}+\bar{t}_{k}\bar{w}_{k}+\bar{t}_{k}\alpha_{l}u_{k,l}\in\argmin_{x\in X}F_{k,l}(x)
. Then we have

F_{k,l}(\bar{x}+\bar{t}_{k}\bar{w}_{k}+\bar{t}_{k}\alpha_{l}u_{k,l})\leq F_{k,l}(\bar{x}+\bar{t}_{k}\bar{w}_{k})
, or explicitly

\frac{f(\bar{x}+\bar{t}_{k}\bar{w}_{k}+\bar{t}_{k}\alpha_{l}u_{k,l})-f(\bar{x})}{\bar{t}_{k}}\leq\frac{f(\bar{x}+\bar{t}_{k}\bar{w}_{k})-f(\bar{x})}{\bar{t}_{k}}+\langle v,\alpha_{l}u_{k,l}\rangle-\alpha_{l}||u_{k,l}||^{2}\quad\forall k,l.

(8)

Since
u_{k,l}\in{\mathbb{B}}
for all
k
and
l
, by taking a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that for each
l
, there is some
u_{l}
such that
u_{k,l}\to u_{l}\in{\mathbb{B}}
as
k\to+\infty
. Then by letting
k\to\infty
, it follows from (
7
) and (
8
) that

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}\frac{f(\bar{x}+\bar{t}_{k}\bar{w}_{k}+\bar{t}_{k}\alpha_{l}u_{k,l})-f(\bar{x})}{\bar{t}_{k}}\leq h(w)+\langle v,\alpha_{l}u_{l}\rangle-\alpha_{l}||u_{l}||^{2}
for all

. By the definition of subderivative, we further have

h(w+\alpha_{l}u_{l})\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\frac{f(\bar{x}+\bar{t}_{k}\bar{w}_{k}+\bar{t}_{k}\alpha_{l}u_{k,l})-f(\bar{x})}{\bar{t}_{k}}
for all
l
.
Therefore, we get

h(w+\alpha_{l}u_{l})\leq h(w)+\langle v,\alpha_{l}u_{l}\rangle-\alpha_{l}||u_{l}||^{2}
for all
l
, or alternatively that,

\frac{h(w+\alpha_{l}u_{l})-h(w)}{\alpha_{l}}\leq\langle v,u_{l}\rangle-||u_{l}||^{2}\quad\forall l.

(9)

Since

for all

as

. By letting

, we get from (

As

, we have

and hence

\langle v,u^{*}\rangle\leq\langle v,u^{*}\rangle-||u^{*}||^{2}
.
This yields

.

For each
l
, define

as a positive integer such that

and

as

. Then we have

as

. Due to

and

as

as
l\to\infty
. By taking a subsequence if necessary, we assume that

for all

. For each

, according to the previous argument,
\bar{x}+\bar{t}_{k(l)}\bar{w}_{k(l)}+\bar{t}_{k(l)}\alpha_{l}u_{k(l),l}

for all

, we get from the optimality condition that

for all

.

, let

,

as

, and moreover

for all

. Similarly as the derivation of (

), we get from the inequality

\frac{f(\bar{x}+t_{l}w_{l})-f(\bar{x})}{t_{l}}\leq\frac{f(\bar{x}+\bar{t}_{k(l)}\bar{w}_{k(l)})-f(\bar{x})}{\bar{t}_{k(l)}}+\langle v,\alpha_{l}u_{k(l),l}\rangle-\alpha_{l}||u_{k(l),l}||^{2}\quad\forall l.

In view of (

Remark 3.1
.

The idea of using the auxiliary function
F_{k,l}(x)
in our proof for perturbation analysis is borrowed from
[

, Lemma 1]
. It is worth noting that the inclusions in (

[

, that is,

\partial h(\bar{w})\subset\partial h(0)\subset\partial f(\bar{x})

from above in the definition of the outer limiting subdifferential.

The following technical result extends the one presented in
[

, Lemma 3.2]
for a proper and positively homogeneous function of degree 2 to a positively homogeneous function of any degree
\gamma>0
, not necessarily being proper. Instead of using proximal subgradients for perturbation analysis as in the proof of
[
14
, Lemma 3.2]
, we utilize regular subgradients in our proof.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
h:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
is positively homogeneous of degree
\gamma>0
in the sense that
h(\lambda w)=\lambda^{\gamma}h(w)
for all
\lambda>0
and
w\in\dom h
. Then for any
z\in\partial h(w)
with
w\in\dom h
, we have
\langle z,w\rangle=\gamma h(w)
.

Proof.

Let
z\in\partial h(w)
with
w\in\dom h
. Then we have
h(w)>-\infty
, for otherwise
\partial h(w)
is undefined.
If
w=0
, then by the positive homogeneity of degree
\gamma
, we have
h(w)=0
and in this case
\langle z,w\rangle=\gamma h(w)
holds trivially.
Now assume that
w\not=0
. By definition, there exist some
w_{k}\to w
and
z_{k}\to z
with
h(w_{k})\to h(w)
and
z_{k}\in\widehat{\partial}h(w_{k})
for all
k
. By taking a subsequence if necessary, we assume that
w_{k}\not=0
for all
k
. By the definition of regular subgradients, we have

\liminf_{u\to w_{k},\;u\not=w_{k}}\frac{h(u)-h(w_{k})-\langle z_{k},u-w_{k}\rangle}{\|u-w_{k}\|}\geq 0\quad\forall k.

(11)

By letting
u=\lambda w_{k}
with
\lambda\to 1+
in (
11
), we get from the positive homogeneity of
h
of degree
\gamma
that

\gamma h(w_{k})-\langle z_{k},w_{k}\rangle\geq 0
for all
k
.
Similarly, by letting
u=\lambda w_{k}
with
\lambda\to 1-
in (
11
), we get

-\gamma h(w_{k})+\langle z_{k},w_{k}\rangle\geq 0
for all

.
Therefore, we have

\langle z_{k},w_{k}\rangle=\gamma h(w_{k})
for all

, which implies
\langle z,w\rangle=\gamma h(w)
by letting

on both sides. This completes the proof. ∎

By a direct application of Lemma
3.1
and the limit form of the KŁ property refined in Theorem

, our main results in this section are summarized in the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\rightarrow\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
is finite and locally lsc at
\bar{x}
. Let
0\leq\theta<1
and

by

(12)

and let

W:=\{w\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}\mid 0<h(w)<+\infty\}.

(13)

The following hold:

(a)

The function
h
is lsc and positively homogeneous of degree
\frac{1}{1-\theta}
with

h(w)=\langle v,w\rangle
for all
v\in\partial[(1-\theta)h](w)
. Moreover, we have either
h(0)=0
or
h(0)=-\infty
, and
h
is proper if and only if

.

(b)

The outer limiting subdifferential set
\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})
has

\displaystyle\bigcup_{w\in W}\frac{(1-\theta)\partial[(1-\theta)h](w)}{[(1-\theta)h(w)]^{\theta}}

(14)

. Moreover, we have

\partial^{>}[dg(\bar{x})](0)=\cl\displaystyle\bigcup_{w\in W}\frac{(1-\theta)\partial[(1-\theta)h](w)}{[(1-\theta)h(w)]^{\theta}}
.

(c)

If
W\not=\emptyset
, then
f
cannot satisfy the KŁ property at
\bar{x}
with any exponent
\theta^{\prime}\in[0,\theta)
.

(d)

Let
\mu>0
. If
f
satisfies the KŁ property at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\theta
and modulus
\mu
, then

d\left(0,\partial[(1-\theta)h](w)\right)\geq\frac{\mu}{1-\theta}[(1-\theta)h(w)]^{\theta}\quad\forall w\in W.

(15)

(e)

If
h
is proper, we have KŁ
(f,\bar{x},\theta)\leq\mbox{K{\L}}((1-\theta)h,0,\theta)
. Otherwise, KŁ
(f,\bar{x},\theta)\leq\mbox{K{\L}}((1-\theta)h_{+},0,\theta)
.

Proof.

For all
\tau>0
, define the
\frac{1}{1-\theta}
-th-order difference quotients
\Delta_{\tau}f(\bar{x}):\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
by

\Delta_{\tau}f(\bar{x})(w):=\frac{f(\bar{x}+\tau w)-f(\bar{x})}{(1-\theta)\tau^{\frac{1}{1-\theta}}}
.
Then the function
h
is given by the lower epi-limit of the family of functions
\Delta_{\tau}f(\bar{x})
as
\tau\to 0+
, and thus lsc (cf.
[
43
, Proposition 7.4 (a)]
). The positive homogeneity of degree
\frac{1}{1-\theta}
arises from the form of the
\frac{1}{1-\theta}
-th-order difference quotients in having

\Delta_{\tau}f(\bar{x})(\lambda w)=\lambda^{\frac{1}{1-\theta}}\Delta_{\tau\lambda}f(\bar{x})(w)
for
\lambda>0
. By the lower semicontinuity and positive homogeneity of degree
\frac{1}{1-\theta}
of
h
, one can easily verify that either
h(0)=0
or
h(0)=-\infty
, and that
h
is proper if and only if
h(0)=0
. Moreover, by Lemma
3.2
, we get
h(w)=\langle v,w\rangle
for all
w\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
and
v\in\partial[(1-\theta)h](w)
, implying that the subset defined by (
14
) does not contain 0. Clearly,
g
is by definition locally lsc at
\bar{x}
with
g(\bar{x})=0
. Moreover, we have by definition

dg(\bar{x})=[(1-\theta)h]_{+}^{1-\theta}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\partial[dg(\bar{x})](w)=\frac{(1-\theta)\partial[(1-\theta)h](w)}{[(1-\theta)h(w)]^{\theta}}\quad\forall w\in W.

(16)

Therefore, we have
dg(\bar{x})(0)=0
, and
0<dg(\bar{x})(w)<+\infty
if and only if
w\in W
. Then by applying Lemma
3.1

(b)
to
g
, we get all the results in
(a)
and
(b)
.

If
W\not=\emptyset
, we have
\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})\not=\emptyset
. It then follows from Theorem
2.1

(a)
and
(b)
that
f
cannot satisfy the KŁ property at
\bar{x}
with any exponent
\theta^{\prime}\in[0,\theta)
. This verifies
(c)
. According to
(b)
and (
16
), we have
\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})\supset\partial^{>}[dg(\bar{x})](0)=\partial^{>}[(1-\theta)h]_{+}^{1-\theta}(0)
, which implies by Theorem
2.1

(d)
that KŁ
(f,\bar{x},\theta)\leq
KŁ
((1-\theta)h_{+},0,\theta)
. That is, if
f
satisfies the KŁ property at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\theta
and modulus
\mu
, then there exist some
\epsilon>0
and
\nu\in(0,+\infty)
so that

d(0,\partial[(1-\theta)h]_{+}(w))\geq\frac{\mu}{1-\theta}((1-\theta)h(w))_{+}^{\theta}

whenever
\|w\|\leq\epsilon
and
h(w)<\nu
. This latter condition can be refined as (
15
) by virtue of the lower semicontinuity and the positive homogeneity of degree
\frac{1}{1-\theta}
of
h
. If
h
is proper, we have
h(0)=0
and by definition KŁ
((1-\theta)h_{+},0,\theta)=
KŁ
((1-\theta)h,0,\theta)
. This completes the proof. ∎

Remark 3.2
.

We refer the reader to
[
30
, Example 2.1]
for situations where the subset (
14
) of
\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})
is not closed in the case of
\theta=0
. The lower estimates given in
[
20
, Theorem 1]
for the outer limiting subdifferential of a pointwise min of finitely many functions having sublinear Hadamard directional derivatives, and in
[
20
, Corollary 3]
and
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
for the outer subdifferential of the pointwise max of finitely many smooth functions, can be easily recovered from (
14
) with
\theta=0
.

According Theorem
3.1

(c)
, once the KŁ property of
f
at
\bar{x}
with some exponent
\theta\in[0,1)
is verified, it suffices to show
W\not=\emptyset
in order to justify the sharpness of
\theta
.

Note that the function
h
given by (
12
) reduces to the (first) subderivative
df(\bar{x})
of
f
at
\bar{x}
in the case of
\theta=0
, and to the second subderivative
d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)
of
f
at
\bar{x}
for 0 in the case of
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
. Therefore, the function
h
given by (
12
) in the general case can be regarded as the
\frac{1}{1-\theta}
-th subderivative of
f
at
\bar{x}
.
This subderivative function, as demonstrated by Theorem
3.1

(d)
and
(e)
, can preserve the KŁ property from that of
f
with the same exponent, but not conversely. Below is a nontrivial example.

For an illustration of Theorem
3.1
with
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
, we consider a function
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
and a point
\bar{x}
around which
f
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
with
\nabla f(\bar{x})=0
.
In this case,
d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)=\langle\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w,w\rangle
is a proper quadratic function with
\partial[\frac{1}{2}d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)](w)=\{\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w\}
.
By the spectral decomposition theorem and (
4
), we get

\mbox{K}{\L}(\frac{1}{2}d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0),0,\frac{1}{2})=\min\left\{\frac{\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w\|}{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}\langle w,\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w\rangle}}\mid\langle w,\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w\rangle>0\right\}=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{\min}}{2}}>0

where
\lambda_{\min}
is the smallest positive eigenvalue of
\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})
if
\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})
is not negative-semidefinite, and otherwise it is
set to be
+\infty
.

However, the inequality KŁ
(f,\bar{x},\frac{1}{2})\leq
KŁ
(\frac{1}{2}d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0),0,\frac{1}{2})
in Theorem
3.1

(e)
may hold as a strict one, as can be seen from the instance that

f_{0}(x):=-(x_{1}+x_{2}-6)^{2}-16\sqrt{|x_{1}|}-16\sqrt{|x_{2}|}

with
\bar{x}:=(1,1)^{T}
. Clearly,
f_{0}
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
around
\bar{x}
with
\nabla f_{0}(\bar{x})=0
and a singular Hessian matrix

\nabla^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x})=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}2&-2\\
-2&2\end{array}\right]
having two eigenvalues:
4
and 0.
By the third-order Taylor expansion, we have

f_{0}(\bar{x}+\tau w)-f_{0}(\bar{x})=(w_{1}-w_{2})^{2}\tau^{2}+\tau^{3}(-w_{1}^{3}-w_{2}^{3})+o(\tau^{3})
for all
w\in\mathbbm{R}^{2}
. Then the third subderivative
h
defined by (
12
) with
\theta=\frac{2}{3}
can be calculated to be

h(w)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-3(w_{1}^{3}+w_{2}^{3})&\mbox{if}\;w_{1}=w_{2},\\
+\infty&\mbox{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

and accordingly

defined by (
13
) is exactly the nonempty set
\{t(-1,-1)^{T}\mid t>0\}
. By Theorem
3.1

(c)
,
f_{0}
cannot satisfy the KŁ property at
\bar{x}
with any exponent
\theta^{\prime}\in[0,\frac{2}{3})
. Therefore,
we have

\mbox{K}{\L}(f_{0},\bar{x},\frac{1}{2})=0\,<\,\mbox{K}{\L}(\frac{1}{2}d^{2}f_{0}(\bar{x}\mid 0),0,\frac{1}{2})=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{\min}}{2}}=\sqrt{2}.

This unsatisfactory situation can be avoided if the nonsingularity of
\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})
is presented,
as can be seen from Proposition
5.1
below, where we show by assuming the nonsingularity of
\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})
that,
the inequality
\mbox{K}{\L}(f,\bar{x},\frac{1}{2})\leq\mbox{K}{\L}(\frac{1}{2}d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0),0,\frac{1}{2})
holds as an equality and the subset (
14
) is closed and coincides with the outer limiting subdifferential set
\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})
with
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
.
These results are obtained as a direct consequence of some more general results presented for prox-regular and twice epi-differentiable functions in the next section.

4
The KŁ property for a prox-regular and twice epi-differentiable function and its Moreau envelopes

In this section, we study the KŁ property with exponent
\frac{1}{2}
for a prox-regular and twice epi-differentiable function by virtue of its Moreau envelopes.

To begin with, we make it clear that the function
g
defined previously for general
\theta\in[0,1)
is now specified with
\theta=\frac{1}{2}
in this section:

g(x):=\sqrt{\max\{f(x)-f(\bar{x}),0\}},

(17)

and recall the notions of prox-regularity and subdifferential continuity as follows.

Definition 4.1
.

[
43
, Definition 13.27]
A function
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}
if
f
is finite and locally lsc at
\bar{x}
with
\bar{v}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
, and there exist
\epsilon>0
and
\rho\geq 0
such that

f(x^{\prime})\geq f(x)+\langle v,x^{\prime}-x\rangle-\frac{\rho}{2}\|x^{\prime}-x\|^{2}\quad\forall x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{B}}_{\epsilon}(\bar{x})

when
v\in\partial f(x)
,
\|v-\bar{v}\|<\epsilon
,
\|x-\bar{x}\|<\epsilon
and
f(x)<f(\bar{x})+\epsilon
. When this holds for all
\bar{v}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
,
f
is said to be prox-regular at
\bar{x}
.

Definition 4.2
.

[
43
, Definition 13.28]

A function
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
is called subdifferentially continuous at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{v}
if
\bar{v}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
and, whenever
(x^{\nu},v^{\nu})\to(\bar{x},\bar{v})
with
v^{\nu}\in\partial f(x^{\nu})
, one has
f(x^{\nu})\to f(\bar{x})
. If this holds for all
\bar{v}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
,
f
is said to be subdifferentially continuous at
\bar{x}
.

As pointed out in the Introduction, the class of prox-regular functions covers a large core of functions of interest in variational analysis and optimization
[
40
,
43
]
, and the subdifferential continuity of a function is often assumed for technical reasons.

For a function
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
that is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
0
, we will show that, every outer
limiting subgradient
u\in\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})
corresponds to a nonnegative scalar
\tau
and a vector
w\in{\mathbb{S}}
such that

\sqrt{2\tau}u\in D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)(w),

(18)

where
\tau
,
w
, and
\sqrt{2\tau}u
can be generated from nearby points
(x,v)
of
(\bar{x},0)
in
\gph\partial f
with
f(x)
close enough to
f(\bar{x})
from above.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
0
. Let
g
be given by (
17
). For every
u\in\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})
, there exist some
\tau\geq 0
,
w\in{\mathbb{S}}
,
x^{k}\to_{f}\bar{x}
with
f(x^{k})>f(\bar{x})
and
v^{k}\in\partial f(x^{k})
such that

\frac{f(x^{k})-f(\bar{x})}{\frac{1}{2}\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|^{2}}\to\tau,\quad\quad\frac{x^{k}-\bar{x}}{\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|}\to w\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{v^{k}}{\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|}\to\sqrt{2\tau}u\in D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)(w).

Proof.

Let
u\in\partial^{>}g(\bar{x})
. By definition there are
x^{k}\to_{f}\bar{x}
with
f(x^{k})>f(\bar{x})
and
v^{k}\in\partial f(x^{k})
such that

v^{k}/\sqrt{4(f(x^{k})-f(\bar{x}))}=\frac{v^{k}}{\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|}/\sqrt{2\frac{f(x^{k})-f(\bar{x})}{\frac{1}{2}\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|^{2}}}\to u.

(19)

As
f(x^{k})\to f(\bar{x})
, we have
v^{k}\to 0
. In view of the prox-regularity of
f
at
\bar{x}
for
0
, we can find some
\rho\geq 0
such that
f(\bar{x})\geq f(x^{k})+\langle v^{k},\bar{x}-x^{k}\rangle-\frac{\rho}{2}\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|^{2}
for all
k
large enough, implying that

\frac{\|v^{k}\|}{\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|}+\frac{\rho}{2}\geq\langle\frac{v^{k}}{\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|},\frac{x^{k}-\bar{x}}{\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|}\rangle+\frac{\rho}{2}\geq\frac{f(x^{k})-f(\bar{x})}{\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|^{2}}>0\quad\forall k.

(20)

Combing (
19
) and (
20
), we have
\frac{\|v^{k}\|}{\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|}/\sqrt{\frac{4\|v^{k}\|}{\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|}+2\rho}\leq\|u\|+1
for all
k
sufficiently large,
which implies the boundedness of the sequence
\{\frac{v^{k}}{\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|}\}
and hence that of the sequence
\{\frac{f(x^{k})-f(\bar{x})}{\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|^{2}}\}
. By taking some subsequences if necessary, we assume that there exist some
w\in{\mathbb{S}}
,
v^{*}\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
and
\tau\geq 0
such that
\frac{x^{k}-\bar{x}}{\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|}\to w
,
\frac{v^{k}}{\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|}\to v^{*}
and
\frac{f(x^{k})-f(\bar{x})}{\frac{1}{2}\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|^{2}}\to\tau
.
As
v^{k}\in\partial f(x^{k})
for all
k
with
x^{k}\equiv\bar{x}+\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|\,\frac{x^{k}-\bar{x}}{\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|}
and
v^{k}\equiv 0+\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|\,\frac{v^{k}}{\|x^{k}-\bar{x}\|}
, we have by definition
v^{*}\in D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)(w)
. It then follows from (
19
)
that
v^{*}=\sqrt{2\tau}u
. This completes the proof. ∎

Whenever
f
is not only prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous, but also twice epi-differentiable (cf. Definition
2.3
) at
\bar{x}
for
0
, we summarize in Lemma
4.2
below a few nice properties on
f
and the Moreau envelopes
e_{\lambda}f
and
e_{\lambda}[\frac{1}{2}d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)]
, most of which can be found in
[
40
]
and
[
43
, Chapter 13]
.

For a proper, lsc function
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
and parameter value
\lambda>0
, the Moreau envelope function
e_{\lambda}f
and proximal mapping
P_{\lambda}f
are defined by

e_{\lambda}f(x):=\inf_{w}\{f(w)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|w-x\|^{2}\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad P_{\lambda}f(x):=\arg\min_{w}\{f(w)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|w-x\|^{2}\}.

Such a function is prox-bounded if there exists
\lambda>0
such that
e_{\lambda}f(x)>-\infty
for some
x\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
. In particular, if
f
is bounded from below, then
f
is prox-bounded.

Lemma 4.2
.

Assume that
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
is prox-regular, subdifferentially continuous and twice epi-differentiable at
\bar{x}
for
0
. The following properties hold:

(a)

The function
d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)
is proper, lsc, prox-bounded and positively homogeneous of degree 2. Moreover, it is prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous everywhere with

\partial[\frac{1}{2}d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)]=D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)

(21)

and

d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)(w)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\langle v,w\rangle&\forall v\in D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)(w),\\
+\infty&\forall w\not\in\dom D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0).\end{array}\right.

(22)

(b)

For all
\lambda>0
sufficiently small there is a neighborhood of
\bar{x}
on which
P_{\lambda}f
is single-valued and Lipschitz continuous with
P_{\lambda}f(\bar{x})=\bar{x}
and
P_{\lambda}f=(I+\lambda\partial f)^{-1}
, and
e_{\lambda}f
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1+}
with
\nabla e_{\lambda}f(\bar{x})=0
and

\nabla e_{\lambda}f=\lambda^{-1}[I-P_{\lambda}f]=(\lambda I+(\partial f)^{-1})^{-1}.

Moreover, for all
\lambda>0
sufficiently small, the Moreau envelope function
e_{\lambda}f
is prox-regular, subdifferentially continuous, twice epi-differentiable (and twice semidifferentiable, cf.
[
43
, Definition 13.6]
) at
\bar{x}
and the Moreau envelope function
e_{\lambda}[\frac{1}{2}d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)]
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{1+}
and positively homogeneous of degree 2, with

e_{\lambda}[\frac{1}{2}d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)]=d^{2}[\frac{1}{2}e_{\lambda}f(\bar{x})],

(23)

\nabla e_{\lambda}[\frac{1}{2}d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)]=D(\nabla e_{\lambda}f)(\bar{x})=\left[\,\lambda I+[D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)]^{-1}\,\right]^{-1},

(24)

and

e_{\lambda}(d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0))(w)=\langle\nabla e_{\lambda}(\frac{1}{2}d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0))(w),w\rangle\quad\forall w\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}.

(25)

Proof.

By
[
43
, Proposition 13.5]
,
d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)
is lsc and positively homogeneous of degree 2. In view of the fact that 0 is a proximal subgradient of
f
at
\bar{x}
, we get from
[
35
, Theorem 4.1]
that
d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)
is proper and prox-bounded. In view of the fact that
f
is assumed to be prox-regular, subdifferentially continuous and twice epi-differentiable at
\bar{x}
for
0
, we get the equality (
21
) directly from
[
43
, Theorem 13.40]
. The subdifferential continuity of
d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)
and the equality (
22
) follow directly from Lemma
3.2
and the equality (
21
). The prox-regularity of
d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)
follows from
[
43
, Proposition 13.49]
. That is, all the results in

(a)
follow. Observing that all the properties involved depend on the nature of
f
in some neighborhood of
\bar{x}
, there’s no harm, therefore, by adding to
f
the indicator of some neighborhood of
\bar{x}
so as to make
f
being bounded from below and hence prox-bounded. Thus, without assuming explicitly that
f

is prox-bounded, we can get all the results in
(b)
from
[
43
, Proposition 13.37 and Exercise 13.45]
in a straightforward way. This completes the proof. ∎

For a vector
w\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
and a function
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
which is prox-regular, subdifferentially continuous and twice epi-differentiable at some
\bar{x}
for
0
, the equality

d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)(w)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{f(\bar{x}+t_{k}w_{k})-f(\bar{x})}{\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}}

(26)

is not always guaranteed for any sequences
t_{k}\to 0+
and
w_{k}\to w
(cf.
[
43
, Example 13.10]
), unless there is a convergent sequence
v_{k}
such that
t_{k}v_{k}\in\partial f(\bar{x}+t_{k}w_{k})
for all
k
sufficiently large, as can be seen from below.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
is prox-regular, subdifferentially continuous and twice epi-differentiable at
\bar{x}
for
0
.
For any sequences
t_{k}\to 0+
and
w_{k}\to w
, (
26
) holds if there is a convergent sequence
v_{k}
such that
t_{k}v_{k}\in\partial f(\bar{x}+t_{k}w_{k})
for all
k
sufficiently large.

Proof.

Let
t_{k}\to 0+
and
w_{k}\to w
. Assume that there are some
v\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
and

v_{k}\to v
with
t_{k}v_{k}\in\partial f(\bar{x}+t_{k}w_{k})
for all
k
sufficiently large. Let
\lambda>0
be sufficiently small such that all the properties
in Lemma
4.2

(b)
hold, and let
y_{k}:=\bar{x}+t_{k}w_{k}+\lambda t_{k}v_{k}
. Then we have
\bar{x}+t_{k}w_{k}\in(I+\lambda\partial f)^{-1}(y_{k})
for all
k
sufficiently large.
By Lemma
4.2

(b)
, we have
\bar{x}+t_{k}w_{k}=P_{\lambda}f(y_{k})
and
e_{\lambda}f(y_{k})=f(\bar{x}+t_{k}w_{k})+\frac{\lambda}{2}t_{k}^{2}\|v_{k}\|^{2}
for all
k
sufficiently large.
Therefore, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{f(\bar{x}+t_{k}w_{k})-f(\bar{x})}{\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}}&=\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{e_{\lambda}f(y_{k})-e_{\lambda}f(\bar{x})-\frac{\lambda}{2}t_{k}^{2}\|v_{k}\|^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}}\\
&=d^{2}e_{\lambda}f(\bar{x})(w+\lambda v)-\lambda\|v\|^{2}\\
&=e_{\lambda}(d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0))(w+\lambda v)-\lambda\|v\|^{2},\end{array}

(27)

where the second equality follows from the twice semidifferentiability of
e_{\lambda}f
(cf. Lemma
4.2

(b)
), and the third one follows from (
23
).
As (
27
) holds for all
\lambda>0
sufficiently small, we further have

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{f(\bar{x}+t_{k}w_{k})-f(\bar{x})}{\frac{1}{2}t_{k}^{2}}=\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0+}\left(e_{\lambda}(d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0))(w+\lambda v)-\lambda\|v\|^{2}\right)=d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)(w),

where the second equality follows from
[
43
, Theorem 1.25]
. This completes the proof. ∎

With Lemmas
4.1
-
4.3
at hand, we now present our main results.
The idea in our argument is as follows: by assuming the prox-regularity of
f
at
\bar{x}
for 0 and the nonsingularity of
D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)
, we observe that
u
in (
18
) cannot be 0, implying by Theorem
2.1
that
f
satisfies the KŁ property at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\frac{1}{2}
; and by further assuming that
f
is in addition twice epi-differentiable and subdifferentially continuous at
\bar{x}
for
0

The following corollary relates the nonsigularity of

with the positive definiteness of

or in other words the quadratic growth condition at
\bar{x}
.

Corollary 4.1
.

Assume that
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbbm{R}}
is prox-regular, subdifferentially continuous and twice epi-differentiable at
\bar{x}
for
0
. Consider the following conditions:

(i)

f
satisfies the quadratic growth condition at
\bar{x}
.

(ii)

The inequality

holds for all

.

(iii)

C:=\{w\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}\mid d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)(w)\leq 1\}
is bounded.

(iv)

D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)
is positive definite.

(v)

[D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)]^{-1}(0)=\{0\}
, i.e.,
D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)
is nonsingular.

{\bf(i)}\Longleftrightarrow{\bf(ii)}\Longleftrightarrow{\bf(iii)}\Longleftrightarrow{\bf(iv)}\Longrightarrow{\bf(v)}
and all the conditions are equivalent in the case of
f
being convex.
If one of conditions
(i)-(iv)
is satisfied, then
f
satisfies the KŁ property at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\frac{1}{2}
, which is
sharp
if
\dom[D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)]\not=\{0\}
or in other words
C\not=\{0\}
.

Proof.

The equivalence of
(i)
and
(ii)
can be found in
[
43
, Theorem 13.24]
. The equivalence of
(ii)
and
(iii)
follows readily from the lsc and the positive homogeneity of
d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)
.
The equivalence of
(i)
and
(iv)
can be found in
[
14

. The implication from
(iv)
to
(v)
is straightforward.
To show that all the conditions are equivalent by assuming that
f
is convex, it suffices to show
{\bf(v)}\Longrightarrow{\bf(ii)}
. Suppose by contradiction that
(ii)
is not satisfied, i.e., there is some

. As

43

.
Let
w\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
be any vector such that

w^{T}\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w>0
and let

v:=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\frac{\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w}{\sqrt{w^{T}\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w}}
.
In terms of
y:=U^{T}w
, we have
w^{T}\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w>0
if and only if
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}y_{j}^{2}>0
, and moreover

\|v\|^{2}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}^{2}y_{i}^{2}}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}y_{j}^{2}}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{i}y_{i}^{2}}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}y_{j}^{2}}\lambda_{i}
.
As
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\lambda_{i}y_{i}^{2}}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}y_{j}^{2}}=1
, we have
\|v\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{\min}
and the equality holds whenever
y_{i}=0
for all
i
with
\lambda_{i}\not=\lambda_{\min}
. This verifies the equality
\mbox{K}{\L}\left(f,\bar{x},\frac{1}{2}\right)=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda_{\min}}{2}}
when (
41
) and Theorem
2.1
are taken into account. The proof is completed. ∎

5.2
The pointwise max of finitely many smooth functions

In this subsection, we consider a function
f:=\max\{f_{1},\cdots,f_{m}\}
for functions
f_{i}
of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
on
\mathbbm{R}^{n}
and a point
\bar{x}\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
with
0\in\partial f(\bar{x})
. According to
[
43
, Example 10.24 and Theorems 13.16]
,
f
is fully amenable everywhere and thus prox-regular, twice epi-differentiable, and subdifferentially continuous at
\bar{x}
for 0 (hence Theorem
4.1
is applicable) with

d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)(w)=\max_{\lambda\in\Lambda(\bar{x})}\{\sum_{i\in I(\bar{x})}\lambda_{i}\langle\nabla^{2}f_{i}(\bar{x})w,\;w\rangle\}+\delta_{\mathcal{V}(\bar{x})}(w),

(42)

where

I(\bar{x}):=\{i\mid f_{i}(\bar{x})=f(\bar{x})\}
,
\mathcal{V}(\bar{x}):=\{w\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}\mid\langle\nabla f_{i}(\bar{x}),\;w\rangle\leq 0\;\forall i\in I(\bar{x})\}
and

\Lambda(\bar{x}):=\{\lambda\in\mathbbm{R}^{m}\mid\lambda_{i}\geq 0\;\forall i\in I(\bar{x}),\;\sum_{i\in I(\bar{x})}\lambda_{i}=1,\sum_{i\in I(\bar{x})}\lambda_{i}\nabla f_{i}(\bar{x})=0\}.

Moreover, it has been shown in
[
39
, Theorem 2]
that

D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)(w)=\{\sum_{i\in I(\bar{x})}\lambda_{i}\nabla^{2}f_{i}(\bar{x})w\mid\lambda\in\Lambda(\bar{x},w)\}+N_{\mathcal{V}(\bar{x})}(w),

where
\Lambda(\bar{x},w)
is the optimal solution set of the linear programming problem

\max_{\lambda}\quad\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I(\bar{x})}\lambda_{i}\langle\nabla^{2}f_{i}(\bar{x})w,\;w\rangle\quad\mbox{subject to}\quad\lambda\in\Lambda(\bar{x}),

(43)

whose dual can be formulated as

\min_{(\kappa,z)}\quad\kappa\quad\mbox{subject to}\quad\kappa-\langle\nabla f_{i}(\bar{x}),z\rangle-\langle\nabla^{2}f_{i}(\bar{x})w,w\rangle\geq 0\quad\forall i\in I(\bar{x}).

(44)

Then it follows from the complementary slackness conditions and characterization of optimality for linear programming problems
[
6
, Theorem 2.7.3 and its Corollary 3]
that, for any feasible
\lambda
to (
43
) and any feasible
(\kappa,z)
to (
44
),
\lambda
and
(\kappa,z)
are, respectively, optimal to (
43
) and (
44
) if and only if
\lambda_{i}(\kappa-\langle\nabla f_{i}(\bar{x}),z\rangle-\langle\nabla^{2}f_{i}(\bar{x})w,w\rangle)=0
for all
i\in I(\bar{x})
.
Therefore, for any
(w,v)\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}\times\mathbbm{R}^{n}
, we have
v\in D(\partial f)(\bar{x}\mid 0)(w)
if and only if there is some
(\kappa,\lambda,\beta,z)\in\mathbbm{R}\times\mathbbm{R}^{m}\times\mathbbm{R}^{m}\times\mathbbm{R}^{n}
such that

with

\sum_{i\in I(\bar{x})}\lambda_{i}=1,\quad\sum_{i\in I(\bar{x})}\lambda_{i}\nabla f_{i}(\bar{x})=0,

(45)

\left.\begin{array}[]{r}\beta_{i}\geq 0,\;\langle\nabla f_{i}(\bar{x}),w\rangle\leq 0,\;\beta_{i}\,\langle\nabla f_{i}(\bar{x}),w\rangle=0\\
\lambda_{i}\geq 0,\;\kappa-\langle\nabla f_{i}(\bar{x}),z\rangle-\langle\nabla^{2}f_{i}(\bar{x})w,w\rangle\geq 0\\
\lambda_{i}\,[\kappa-\langle\nabla f_{i}(\bar{x}),z\rangle-\langle\nabla^{2}f_{i}(\bar{x})w,w\rangle\,]=0\end{array}\right\}\quad\forall i\in I(\bar{x}).

(46)

Moreover, for any
(\kappa,\lambda,\beta,w,z)\in\mathbbm{R}\times\mathbbm{R}^{m}\times\mathbbm{R}^{m}\times\mathbbm{R}^{n}\times\mathbbm{R}^{n}
satisfying (
45
) and (
46
), we get
d^{2}f(\bar{x}\mid 0)(w)=\kappa
. In view of these formulas and results just recalled, we present the following proposition by applying Theorem
4.1
to
f
in a straightforward way.

Proposition 5.2
.

Consider a function
f:=\max\{f_{1},\cdots,f_{m}\}

of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
on
\mathbbm{R}^{n}

(47)

(c)

The optimal value of the following smooth optimization problem is positive:

subject to

-

) with

.

5.3
The
\ell_{1}
regularized functions

In this subsection, we study the KŁ property of the
\ell_{1}
regularized function

F(x):=f(x)+\mu\|x\|_{1},

(48)

where
f:\mathbbm{R}^{n}\to\mathbbm{R}
is assumed to be of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
and
\mu>0
. Note that
F
in the form of (
48
) includes the
\ell_{1-2}
minimization function studied in
[
47
]
as a special case. Now consider some
\bar{x}\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
with
0\in\partial F(\bar{x})
. By
[
43
, Theorem 10.24
(g)
]
,
F
is a fully amenable function, and thus prox-regular, twice epi-differentiable, and subdifferentially continuous at
\bar{x}
for 0
[
43
, Theorem 13.14 and Proposition 13.32]
. To apply Theorem
4.1
to
F
, we recall from the literature some formulas and results relating to
D(\partial F)(\bar{x}\mid 0)
and
d^{2}F(\bar{x}\mid 0)
.
By some calculus rules of subgradients, we deduce from
0\in\partial F(\bar{x})
that each
j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
falls into one of the following distinct index sets:

\begin{array}[]{ll}I:=\{i\mid\bar{x}_{i}=0,\;-\mu<(\nabla f(\bar{x}))_{i}<\mu\},&\hskip-8.5359pt\;\;\;K^{+}:=\{i\mid\bar{x}_{i}=0,\;(\nabla f(\bar{x}))_{i}=\mu\},\\
J:=\{i\mid\bar{x}_{i}\not=0,\;(\nabla f(\bar{x}))_{i}+\mu\sign(\bar{x}_{i})=0\},&K^{-}:=\{i\mid\bar{x}_{i}=0,\;(\nabla f(\bar{x}))_{i}=-\mu\},\end{array}

(49)

and that
K^{+}\cup K^{-}=\emptyset
if in particular
0\in\ri\partial F(\bar{x})
. According to
[
7
, Proposition 3.1 and Theorem 3.1]
, we have
v\in D(\partial F)(\bar{x}\mid 0)(w)
if and only if

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcrl}w_{j}=0&\forall j\in I,&v_{j}-(\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w)_{j}=0&\forall j\in J\\
w_{j}\leq 0,&v_{j}-(\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w)_{j}\geq 0,&w_{j}(v_{j}-(\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w)_{j})=0&\forall j\in K^{+}\\
w_{j}\geq 0,&v_{j}-(\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w)_{j}\leq 0,&w_{j}(v_{j}-(\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w)_{j})=0&\forall j\in K^{-}\end{array}\right\}.

(50)

By virtue of (
22
), we get
d^{2}F(\bar{x}\mid 0)(w)=\langle\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w,w\rangle+\delta_{\mathcal{V}(\bar{x})}(w)
for all
w\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}
, where

\mathcal{V}(\bar{x}):=\{w\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}\mid w_{j}=0\;\forall j\in I,\;w_{j}\leq 0\;\forall j\in K^{+},\;w_{j}\geq 0\;\forall j\in K^{-}\}.

(51)

In view of the results just recalled, we present the following proposition by applying Theorem
4.1
to
F
in a straightforward way, of which the proof is omitted.

Proposition 5.3
.

Consider the
\ell_{1}
-regularized function
F
given by (
48
) and some
\bar{x}
with
0\in\partial F(\bar{x})
. Let
I,J,K^{+},K^{-}
be given by (
49
) and
\mathcal{V}(\bar{x})
be given by (
51
).
Assume that
D(\partial F)(\bar{x}\mid 0)^{-1}(0)=\{0\}
or equivalently that

\left.\begin{array}[]{rcrl}w_{j}=0&\forall j\in I,&(\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w)_{j}=0&\forall j\in J\\
w_{j}\leq 0,&(\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w)_{j}\leq 0,&w_{j}(\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w)_{j}=0&\forall j\in K^{+}\\
w_{j}\geq 0,&(\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w)_{j}\geq 0,&w_{j}(\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w)_{j}=0&\forall j\in K^{-}\\
\end{array}\right\}\Longrightarrow w=0.

(52)

Then
F
satisfies the KŁ property at
\bar{x}
with exponent
\frac{1}{2}
, which is
sharp
if there is some
w\in\mathcal{V}(\bar{x})
with
\langle\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})w,w\rangle>0
.
Moreover,
\mbox{K{\L}}(F,\bar{x},\frac{1}{2})
is equal to the square root of the optimal value of the smooth optimization problem

\min_{(w,v)}\quad\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\|v\|^{2}}{\langle v,w\rangle}\quad\mbox{subject to}\quad(\ref{chigua}),\quad\|w\|^{2}=1,\quad\langle v,w\rangle>0.

(53)

In particular when
0\in\ri\partial F(\bar{x})
, we have
K^{+}\cup K^{-}=\emptyset
. In this case, condition (
52
) reduces to the nonsingularity of the submatrix
[\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})_{i,j}]_{i,j\in J}
, and to solve (
53
) is to find the smallest positive eigenvalue of
[\nabla^{2}f(\bar{x})_{i,j}]_{i,j\in J}
.

5.4
The
\ell_{p}
regularized least square functions

In this subsection, we study the KŁ property of the
\ell_{p}
(
0<p<1
) regularized least square function

F(x):=\|Ax-b\|_{2}^{2}+\mu\|x\|_{p}^{p}\quad\quad\forall x\in\mathbbm{R}^{n},

(54)

where
A\in\mathbbm{R}^{m\times n}
,
b\in\mathbbm{R}^{m}
,
\mu>0
and
\|x\|_{p}^{p}:=\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{p}
. To avoid the technical verification of the prox-regularity and the twice epi-differentiability of
F
, we will not apply Theorem
4.1
in a direct way. Instead, we will apply Theorem
2.1
and Proposition
5.1
by converting the study of the KŁ property of

F
to that of a restricted smooth function. To begin with, we get the formula for
\partial F(x)
by using the calculus rules of subgradients
[
43
, Exercise 8.8 and Proposition 10.5]
as follows:

\partial F(x)\hskip-1.42271pt=\hskip-1.42271pt\left\{2A^{T}(Ax-b)+\mu pv\hskip-1.42271pt\mid\hskip-1.42271ptv_{i}={\rm sign}(x_{i})|x_{i}|^{p-1}\forall i\in\supp(x),v_{i}\in\mathbbm{R}\;\forall i\not\in\supp(x)\right\}

(55)

by virtue of which, and due to
|t|^{p-1}\to+\infty
as
t\to 0
with
t\not=0
, we further have the equality

\limsup_{y\to x,\;y_{i_{0}}\not=0}\partial F(y)=\emptyset\quad\forall i_{0}\not\in\supp(x).

(56)

Here
\supp(x):=\{i\mid x_{i}\not=0\}
denotes the support index set of
x
.
For each nonempty
I\subset\{1,\cdots,n\}
, let
A_{I}:=[A_{i}]_{i\in I}
with
A_{i}
being the
i
-th column of
A
and define
F_{I}:\mathbbm{R}^{|I|}\to\mathbbm{R}
by

F_{I}(y):=\|A_{I}y-b\|^{2}+\mu\|y\|_{p}^{p}.

(57)

Then for each
\bar{x}\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
, we have in terms of
I:=\supp(\bar{x})
,
\bar{x}_{I}:=(\bar{x}_{i})_{i\in I}
,
v_{I}:=(v_{i})_{i\in I}
,
G(x):=(\max\{F(x)-F(\bar{x}),0\})^{1-\theta}
, and
G_{I}(y)=(\max\{F_{I}(y)-F_{I}(\bar{x}_{I}),0\})^{1-\theta}
,

\partial^{>}G(\bar{x})=\{v\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}\mid v_{I}\in\partial^{>}G_{I}(\bar{x}_{I})\}\quad\forall\theta\in[0,1),

(58)

which follows readily from the definition of outer limiting subdifferential sets by taking (
5
), (
55
) and (
56
) into account.
Moreover, we have
\partial^{>}F(0)=\emptyset
due to (
56
). Therefore,
F
satisfies the KŁ property at
\bar{x}=0
with exponent
0
, which is not
sharp
.
With the help of Theorem
2.1
and the identity (
58
), the study of the KŁ property of

F
at some
\bar{x}\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
can be converted to that of
F_{I}
(with
I=\supp(\bar{x})
) at
\bar{x}_{I}
,
around which
F_{I}
is of class
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
. We demonstrate this idea in the following proposition.

Proposition 5.4
.

Consider the
\ell_{p}
regularized least square function

given by (
54

\bar{x}\in\mathbbm{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}

(b)

(c)

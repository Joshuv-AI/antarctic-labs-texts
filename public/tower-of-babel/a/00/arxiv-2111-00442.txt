Ehsan Shahoseini, Ali Rajaei ∗, Abbas Maarefparvar 1

Abstract
. For L / K L/K a finite Galois extension of number fields, the relative Pólya group Po ⁡ ( L / K ) \Po(L/K) coincides with the group of strongly ambiguous ideal classes in L / K L/K . In this paper, using a well known exact sequence related to Po ⁡ ( L / K ) \Po(L/K) , in the works of Brumer-Rosen and Zantema, we find short proofs for some classical results in the literatur. Then we define the “Ostrowski quotient” Ost ⁡ ( L / K ) \Ost(L/K) as the cokernel of the capitulation map into Po ⁡ ( L / K ) \Po(L/K) , and generalize some known results for Po ⁡ ( L / ℚ ) \Po(L/\mathbb{Q}) to Ost ⁡ ( L / K ) \Ost(L/K) .

Notations.
The following notations will be used throughout this article:

For a number field
K
, the notations
I(K)
,
P(K)
,
\Cl(K)
,
\mathcal{O}_{K}
,
h_{K}
,
U_{K}
,
H(K)
, and
\Gamma(K)
denote the group of fractional ideals, group of principal fractional ideals, ideal class group, ring of integers, class number, group of units, Hilbert class field, and genus field of
K
, respectively.

For a finite extension
L/K
of number fields,

\mathcal{N}_{L/K}:\Cl(L)\rightarrow\Cl(K)
denotes the induced homomorphism by the ideal norm homomorphism
N_{L/K}:I(L)\rightarrow I(K)
; and
{\epsilon}_{L/K}:\Cl(K)\rightarrow\Cl(L)
denotes the transfer of ideal classes induced by the homomorphism
j_{L/K}:\mathfrak{a}\in I(K)\mapsto\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{O}_{L}\in I(L)
.
Whenever
L/K
is Galois, for a prime ideal
\mathfrak{P}
of
K
denote by
e_{\mathfrak{P}(L/K)}
and
f_{\mathfrak{P}(L/K)}
the ramification index and residue class degree of
\mathfrak{P}
in
L/K
, respectively.

1.
Introduction

Let
L
be an algebraic number field with ring of integers
\mathcal{O}_{L}
. For every prime number
p
and every integer
f\geq 1
, the
Ostrowski ideal

\Pi_{p^{f}}(L)
of
L
is defined as follows
[
18
]
:

(1.1)

\Pi_{p^{f}}(L):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{m}\in Max(\mathcal{O}_{L})\\
\mathcal{N}_{L/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{m})=p^{f}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{m},

where by the convention, if
L
has no ideal with norm
p^{f}
, then
\Pi_{p^{f}}(L)=\mathcal{O}_{L}
.
Following Zantema
[
25
]
,
L
is called a
Pólya field
if all the Ostrowski ideals
\Pi_{p^{f}}(L)
of
L
for arbitrary prime powers
p^{f}
are principal. As an “obstruction measure” for
L
to be a Pólya field, the notion of
Pólya-Ostrowski group
or
Pólya group
was introduced in
[
3
]
:

Definition 1.1
.

The Pólya group of a number field
L
is the subgroup
\Po(L)
of
the class group
\Cl(L)
generated by the classes of the Ostrowski ideals
\Pi_{q}(L)
.

Hence
L
is Pólya iff its Pólya group is trivial. Obviously every number field with class number one is a Pólya field, but not conversely, see e.g. Proposition
1.2
below.

Now let
L
be a Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
with Galois group
G
. In this case, Pólyaness of
L
is equivalent to principality of
\prod_{i=1}^{g}\mathfrak{P}_{i}
, where
\mathfrak{P}_{1},\mathfrak{P}_{2},\dots,\mathfrak{P}_{g}
are all distinct prime ideals of
L
above a
ramified prime

p
, see
[
25
, Section 1]
. On the other hand, one can show that the
Ostrowski ideals

\Pi_{q}(L)
freely generate the ambiguous ideals
I(L)^{G}
, see
[
2
, Section 2]
. Thus
\Po(L)
is the subgroup of
\Cl(L)
generated by the classes of ambiguous ideals. As the first result in this subject, Pólya groups of quadratic fields have been described by Hilbert
[
7
]
:

Proposition 1.2
.

[
7
, Theorem 105-106]

Let
L
be a quadratic field and denote the number of ramified primes in
L/\mathbb{Q}
by
r_{L}
. Then:

\#Po(L)=\begin{cases}2^{r_{L}-2}\,&:\,\text{$L$ is real with no unit of negative norm}\\
2^{r_{L}-1}\,&:\,\text{Otherwise}\end{cases}

The above theorem of Hilbert has been generalized by Zantema:

Proposition 1.3
.

[
25
, Section 3, page 9]

Let
L/\mathbb{Q}
be a Galois extension with Galois group
G
. Denote the ramification index of a prime
p
in
L
by
e_{p}
.Then the following sequence is exact:

(1.2)

\{0\}\longrightarrow H^{1}(G,U_{L})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\text{p prime}}\mathbb{Z}/e_{p}\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\Po(L)\longrightarrow\{0\}.

Remark 1.4
.

In
[
16
]
we called (
1.2
) “Zantema’s exact sequence”, however a version of this sequence can be seen in
[
2
]
and therefore we call a generalization of this exact sequence the “Brumer-Rosen-Zantema” exact sequence or shortly “BRZ”, see Theorem
2.2
below.

2.
Relative Pólya group

Recently, the notion of Pólya group has been generalized to
Relative Pólya group
(independently in
[
4
,
14
]
) in the following sense:

Definition 2.1
.

Let
L/K
be a finite extension of number fields. The
relative Pólya group
of
L
over
K
, is the subgroup of
\Cl(L)
generated by the classes of the
relative Ostrowski ideals
:

\displaystyle\Pi_{\mathfrak{P}^{f}}(L/K):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{M}\in Max(\mathcal{O}_{L})\\
N_{L/K}(\mathfrak{M})=\mathfrak{P}^{f}\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{M},

where
\mathfrak{P}
is a prime ideal of
K
,
f
is a positive integer and by the convention, if
L
has no ideal with relative norm
\mathfrak{P}^{f}
(over
K
), then
\Pi_{\mathfrak{P}^{f}}(L/K)=\mathcal{O}_{L}
.
We denote the relative Pólya group of
L
over
K
by
\Po(L/K)
. In particular,
\Po(L/\mathbb{Q})=\Po(L)
and
\Po(L/L)=\Cl(L)
.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
4
,
16
]

Let
L/K
be a finite Galois extension of number fields with Galois group
G
. Then the following sequence is exact:

(BRZ)

\hskip 18.49988pt\{0\}\rightarrow\Ker({\epsilon}_{L/K})\rightarrow H^{1}(G,U_{L})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{P}}\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{e_{\mathfrak{P}(L/K)}\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\frac{\Po(L/K)}{{\epsilon}_{L/K}(\Cl(K))}\rightarrow\{0\}.

We summarize some interesting consequences of Theorem
2.2
(for more results see
[
4
,
16
]
):

Corollary 2.3
.

For
L/K
a finite Galois extension of number fields with Galois group
G
, the following assertions hold:

(i)

[
4
, Proposition 4.4]

\#\Po(L/K)=\frac{h_{K}.\prod_{\mathfrak{P}}e_{\mathfrak{P}(L/K)}}{\#H^{1}(G,U_{L})}
.

(ii)

[
16
, Remark 2.3]

If
\gcd(h_{L},[L:K])=1
, then
\Po(L/K)={\epsilon}_{L/K}(\Cl(K))
.

(iii)

[
16
, Corollary 2.4]

If
gcd(h_{K},[L:K])=1
then
\Cl(K)\hookrightarrow\Po(L/K)
. In particular,
h_{K}\mid h_{L}
. Moreover, the following sequence is exact:

\{0\}\rightarrow H^{1}(G,U_{L})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{P}|\disc(L/K)}\mathbb{Z}/e_{\mathfrak{P}}\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\Po(L/K)/\Cl(K)\rightarrow\{0\}.

(iv)

[
16
, Corollary 2.9]

If every ideal class of
K
extended to
L
is principal and all finite places of
K
are unramified in
L
, then
\Cl(K)\simeq H^{1}(G,U_{L})
and
\Po(L/K)=\{0\}
. In particular,
\Po(H(K)/K)
is trivial, for the Hilbert class field
H(K)
of
K
.

Remark 2.4
.

Indeed classically,
\#Po(L/K)
can be obtained also from Brumer-Rosen results in
[
2
, Lemma 2.1 and Proposition 2.2]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

[
16
, Lemma 2.10]

Let
F\subseteq K\subseteq L
be a tower of finite extensions of number fields.

(i)

If
K/F
and
L/F
are Galois extensions, then
\epsilon_{L/K}(\Po(K/F))\subseteq\Po(L/F)
.

(ii)

If
L/K
is a Galois extension, then
\Po(L/F)\subseteq\Po(L/K)
.

Remark 2.6
.

Note that if either
K/F
or
L/F
is not Galois, then the containment in the part
(i)
in Lemma
2.5
might not hold. For instance, consider the pure cubic field
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{19})
. One can show that the Galois closure
L
of
K
is a Pólya field while
\Po(K)=\Cl(K)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}
, see
[
15
, Example 2.9]
. On the other hand, since
gcd(h_{K},[L:K])=1
,
\mathcal{N}_{L/K}\,\circ\,{\epsilon}_{L/K}:\bar{\mathfrak{a}}\in\Cl(K)\mapsto\bar{\mathfrak{a}}^{[L:K]}\in\Cl(K)
is injective, hence so is
{\epsilon}_{L/K}
. Therefore
{\epsilon}_{L/K}(\Po(K))=\Po(K)=\Cl(K)\not\subseteq\Po(L)
.

2.1.
Some Applications

In this section, using the exact sequence (
BRZ
) we find easier and shorter proofs for some results in the literature.
We begin with the following result of Tannaka:

Proposition 2.7
.

[
22
, Theorem 8]

For a number field
K
, one has

(2.1)

H^{1}(Gal(H(K)/K),U_{H(K)})\simeq Gal(H(K)/K),

where
H(K)
denotes the Hilbert class field of
K
.

Proof.

By the principal ideal theorem,
\Ker(\epsilon_{H(K)/K})=\Cl(K)
. Since
H(K)/K
is an unramified extension,
using the isomorphism induced by Artin reciprocity,
and the exact sequence (
BRZ
), we find

Gal(H(K)/K)\simeq\Cl(K)=\Ker(\epsilon_{H(K)/K})\simeq H^{1}(Gal(H(K)/K),U_{H(K)}).

∎

Remark 2.8
.

Isomorphism (
2.1
) also has been obtained independently by Khare and Prasad, see
[
10
,
\lx@sectionsign
4, Corollary 3]
. They mention that this isomorphism is equivalent to the principal ideal theorem, see
[
10
,
\lx@sectionsign
4, Remark 3]
.

A similar statement holds for
any
finite extension of the Hilbert class field, due to Iwasawa:

Proposition 2.9
.

[
8
]

For
L/K
a finite Galois extension of number fields which is unramified at all finite places, if
H(K)\subseteq L
then

H^{1}(Gal(L/K),U_{L})\simeq Cl(K).

Proof.

Since
\epsilon_{L/K}=\epsilon_{L/H(K)}\,\circ\,\epsilon_{H(K)/K}
, by the principal ideal theorem we have
\Ker(\epsilon_{L/K})=\Cl(K)
. The exact sequence (
BRZ
) yields the claim.
∎

In a more general case (without considering the Hilbert class field), Iwasawa
[
8
]
and Khare-Prasad
[
10
,
\lx@sectionsign
4, Proposition 2]
independently proved that:

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
L/K
be a finite Galois extension of number fields that is unramified at all finite places. Then
\Ker(\epsilon_{L/K})\simeq H^{1}(Gal(L/K),U_{L})
.

Proof.

Immediately follows from the exact sequence (
BRZ
).
∎

Remark 2.11
.

Note that in our proofs, we have assumed a weaker assumption, i.e.
finite places
are unramified
versus
all places
.
For instance, for
L=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-1},\sqrt{3})
and
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{3})
, ramification happens only for infinite places of
K
in
L
; whereas by the exact sequence (
BRZ
)
H^{1}(Gal(L/K),U_{L})
is trivial, since
L/K
is unramified in all finite places (Note that
h_{K}=1
).

As another application of the exact sequence (
BRZ
) we find the following results on the “kernel” and “cokernel” of the capitulation map in cyclic unramified extensions:

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
L/K
be a finite cyclic extension of number fields with Galois group
G
.

(i)

If
L
is unramified at all finite and infinite places of
K
, then

(2.2)

\#\Ker(\epsilon_{L/K})=\left(U_{K}:Norm_{L/K}(U_{L})\right).[L:K].

(ii)

If
L
is unramified at all finite places of
K
, then

(2.3)

H^{2}(G,U_{L})\hookrightarrow\Coker(\epsilon_{L/K}).

Proof.

(i)

Since
L/K
is unramified, by the exact sequence (
BRZ
) we have

(2.4)

\#{H}^{1}(G,U_{L})=\#Ker(\epsilon_{L/K}).

On the one hand, since
L/K
is cyclic, we can use the
Herbrand quotient
:

(2.5)

\displaystyle Q(G,U_{L})=\frac{\#\hat{H}^{0}(G,U_{L})}{\#{H}^{1}(G,U_{L})},

where

(2.6)

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{0}(G,U_{L})

\displaystyle=\frac{U_{L}^{G}}{Norm_{L/K}(U_{L})}=\frac{U_{K}}{Norm_{L/K}(U_{L})}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle Q(G,U_{L})=\frac{2^{s}}{[L:K]},

where
s
is the number of infinite places of
K
ramified in
L

[
12
, Chapter IX]
. By the assumption
L/K
is unramified which implies that
s=0
i.e.

(2.7)

Q(G,U_{L})=\frac{1}{[L:K]}.

Using relations (
2.4
)–(
2.7
) we obtain the desired equality.

(ii)
Consider the following exact sequence

\{0\}\longrightarrow P(L)\longrightarrow I(L)\longrightarrow\Cl(L)\longrightarrow\{0\}.

Taking Cohomology, we get the sequence

\{0\}\longrightarrow P(L)^{G}\longrightarrow I(L)^{G}\longrightarrow\left(\Cl(L)\right)^{G}\longrightarrow H^{1}(G,P(L))\longrightarrow\{0\}

is exact, since
H^{1}(G,I(L))=\{0\}
, see e.g.
[
11
, proof of Theorem 1]
or
[
24
, Lemma 2.1]
.
Since
L/K
is Galois,
\Po(L/K)=\frac{I(L)^{G}}{P(L)^{G}}
, see
[
16
,
\lx@sectionsign
2]
. Hence the last exact sequence can be rewritten as

\{0\}\longrightarrow\Po(L/K)\longrightarrow\left(\Cl(L)\right)^{G}\longrightarrow H^{1}(G,P(L))\longrightarrow\{0\},

which implies that

(2.8)

\frac{\left(\Cl(L)\right)^{G}}{\Po(L/K)}\simeq H^{1}(G,P(L)).

On the other hand, Kisilevsky
[
11
, Lemma 1]
proved that

(2.9)

H^{1}(G,P(L))\simeq H^{2}(G,U_{L}).

Therefore

(2.10)

\frac{\left(\Cl(L)\right)^{G}}{\Po(L/K)}\simeq H^{2}(G,U_{L}).

Since all finite places of
K
are unramified in
L
, the exact sequence (
BRZ
) yields

(2.11)

\Po(L/K)=\epsilon_{L/K}\left(\Cl(K)\right).

Using equations (
2.10
) and (
2.11
) we have

(2.12)

H^{2}(G,U_{L})\simeq\frac{\left(\Cl(L)\right)^{G}}{\epsilon_{L/K}\left(\Cl(K)\right)}

which is a subgroup of
\Coker(\epsilon_{L/K})
.
∎

Remark 2.13
.

Part
(i)
of Theorem
2.12
can be thought of as a
quantitative version
of “Hilbert’s Theorem 94”
[
7
]
.

3.
Ostrowski quotient

By exact sequence (
1.2
), there exists a surjective map from
\bigoplus_{p}\dfrac{\mathbb{Z}}{e_{p(L/\mathbb{Q})}\mathbb{Z}}
onto
\Po(L)
. Whereas switching to the relative case, namely
\Po(L/K)
for
L/K
a finite Galois extension of number fields, this statement
does not
hold, in general. There are finite Galois extensions
L/K
such that the only map from
\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}}\dfrac{\mathbb{Z}}{e_{\mathfrak{p}(L/K)}\mathbb{Z}}
to
\Po(L/K)
is the zero map while
\Po(L/K)
is non trivial:

Example 3.1
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
and
L=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^{n}})
where
n\geq 1
and
p
is a regular prime, i.e.
p\nmid h_{K}
, and
\zeta_{p}
(resp.
\zeta_{p^{n}}
) denote the
p
-th (resp.
p^{n}
-th) primitive root of unity. By a result of Iwasawa
[
9
]
, since
h_{K}
is not divisible by
p
, so is not
h_{L}
. In particular
p\nmid\#\Po(L/K)
. The only prime of
K
that ramifies in
L
is
\mathfrak{p}=<1-\zeta_{p}>
whose ramification index is

[L:K]=p^{n-1}
, i.e.
\mathfrak{p}
is totally ramified. Therefore the only map from
\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{p}}\dfrac{\mathbb{Z}}{e_{\mathfrak{p}(L/K)}\mathbb{Z}}
to
\Po(L/K)
is the zero map. Masley and Montgomery
[
17
]
proved that
h_{K}\neq 1
for
p>19
. Since
gcd(h_{K},[L:K])=1
, one has
\Cl(K)\hookrightarrow\Po(L/K)
, see
[
16
, Corollary 2.4]
.
Hence for
p>19
,
\Po(L/K)
is nontrivial.
Note that

\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}_{L}=\left(1-\zeta_{p}\right)\mathcal{O}_{L}=\left<1-\zeta_{p^{n}}\right>^{p^{n-1}}

is a principal ideal of
L
, hence is trivial in
\Po(L/K)
.

The above example shows that the controllability of the Pólya group by ramification, cannot transfer to the relative Pólya group. This motivates us to modify the notion of relative Pólya group to arrive at a notion directly governed by ramification.

Definition 3.2
.

[
20
]

For a finite extension
L/K
of number fields, the
Ostrowski quotient

\Ost(L/K)
is defined as

(3.1)

\Ost(L/K):=\dfrac{\Po(L/K)}{\Po(L/K)\cap\epsilon_{L/K}(Cl(K))}.

In particular,
\Ost(L/\mathbb{Q})=\Po(L/\mathbb{Q})=\Po(L)
and
\Ost(L/L)=\{0\}
. The extension
L/K
is called “
Ostrowski
” (or
L
is called
K
-Ostrowski) if
\Ost(L/K)
is trivial.

Remark 3.3
.

If
L/K
is a Galois extension with Galois group
G
, then
\epsilon_{L/K}(\Cl(K))\subseteq\Po(L/K)

[
16
,
\lx@sectionsign
2]
, and the exact sequence (
BRZ
) can be rewritten as follows

(3.2)

\textstyle{\{0\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Ker({\epsilon}_{L/K})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\theta_{L/K}}
\textstyle{H^{1}(G,U_{L})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{P}}\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{e_{\mathfrak{P}(L/K)}\mathbb{Z}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Ost(L/K)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\{0\}.}

Hence in this case we have

(3.3)

\#\Ost(L/K)=\frac{\#\Ker(\epsilon_{L/K}).\prod_{\mathfrak{P}|disc(L/K)}e_{\mathfrak{P}(L/K)}}{\#H^{1}(G,U_{L})},

which relates the Ostrowski quotient of
L/K
to the “
capitulation kernel
”.

Using Corollary
2.3
, we immediately find:

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
L/K
be a finite Galois extension of number fields with Galois group
G
.

(i)

If
gcd(h_{K},[L:K])=1
, then the following sequence is exact:

\{0\}\rightarrow H^{1}(G,U_{L})\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{P}}\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{e_{\mathfrak{P}(L/K)}\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\Ost(L/K)\rightarrow\{0\}.

(ii)

If either
gcd(h_{L},[L:K])=1
or all finite places of
K
are unramified in
L
, then
L/K
is Ostrowski. In particular, the extensions
H(K)/K
and
\Gamma(K)/K
are Ostrowski, where
H(K)
and
\Gamma(K)
denote the Hilbert class field and genus field of
K
, respectively.

For “abelian” number fields, Zantema proved:

Proposition 3.5
.

[
25
, Proposition 2.5]

If
K/\mathbb{Q}
is an abelian extension ramifying at only one prime, then
K
is a Pólya field.

Using some notions in class field theory for finite abelian extensions, namely the “conductor” and the “ray class field”
[
12
, Chapter X,
\lx@sectionsign
3]
, we can find an analogous statement to Proposition
3.5
for the Ostrowski quotient:

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
K/F
be a finite abelian extension of number fields such that only one prime of
F
is ramified in
K
. Let
L
be the ray class field of
F
for the modulus
\mathfrak{c}(K/F)
, where
\mathfrak{c}(K/F)
denotes the conductor of
K
over
F
. If
L/F
is Ostrowski, then so is

K/F
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be the only prime of
F
ramified in
K
and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}_{K}

\displaystyle=(\mathfrak{P}_{1}\mathfrak{P}_{2}\dots\mathfrak{P}_{g})^{e_{\mathfrak{p}(K/F)}}=\left(\Pi_{\mathfrak{p}^{f_{K}}}(K/F)\right)^{e_{\mathfrak{p}(K/F)}},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}_{L}

\displaystyle=(\mathfrak{C}_{1}\mathfrak{C}_{2}\dots\mathfrak{C}_{t})^{e_{\mathfrak{p}(L/F)}}=\left(\Pi_{\mathfrak{p}^{f_{L}}}(L/F)\right)^{e_{\mathfrak{p}(L/F)}}.

Since
L/F
is Ostrowski,
\Pi_{\mathfrak{p}^{f_{L}}}(L/F)\in\epsilon_{L/F}(\Cl(F))
. Equivalently
\Pi_{\mathfrak{p}^{f_{L}}}(L/F)=\epsilon_{L/F}([\mathfrak{a}])
for some
[\mathfrak{a}]\in\Cl(F)
.
Hence

(3.5)

\mathcal{N}_{L/K}\left(\Pi_{\mathfrak{p}^{f_{L}}}(L/F)\right)=\mathcal{N}_{L/K}\left(\epsilon_{L/F}([\mathfrak{a}])\right)=\mathcal{N}_{L/K}\left(\epsilon_{L/K}(\epsilon_{K/F}([\mathfrak{a}]))\right)=\left(\epsilon_{K/F}([\mathfrak{a}])\right)^{[L:K]}.

On the other hand,

(3.6)

\mathcal{N}_{L/K}\left(\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}_{L}\right)=\left(\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}_{K}\right)^{[L:K]}=\left(\Pi_{\mathfrak{p}^{f_{K}}}(K/F)\right)^{[L:K].e_{\mathfrak{p}(K/F)}}.

By relations (
3
), (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) we get

(3.7)

\Pi_{\mathfrak{p}^{f_{K}}}(K/F)=\left(\epsilon_{K/F}([\mathfrak{a}])\right)^{\frac{e_{\mathfrak{p}(L/F)}}{e_{\mathfrak{p}(K/F)}}}\in\epsilon_{K/F}(\Cl(F)).

Finally, since
K/F
is a Galois extension, for each prime
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}\neq\mathfrak{p}
of
F
we have
\Pi_{{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}^{f_{K}}}(K/F)\in\epsilon_{K/F}(\Cl(F))
, see part
(i)
of Lemma
4.2
below. Therefore
\Ost(K/F)=\{0\}
as claimed.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

For
F=\mathbb{Q}
, triviality of
\Ost(L/F)
is a classical result for
cyclotomic
fields
[
25
, Proposition 2.6]
.

Leriche proved that the Hilbert class field
H(K)
of
K
is a Pólya field
[
13
, Corollary 3.2]
, which recently has been generalized to the triviality of
\Po(H(K)/K)
, see
[
16
, Corollary 2.9]
.
Using the same method as in
[
16
,
\lx@sectionsign
2]
we have:

Theorem 3.8
.

For a finite extension
L/K
of number fields, the extension
H(L)/K
is Ostrowski.

Proof.

Using the principal ideal theorem, we have

\epsilon_{H(L)/K}(\Cl(K))=\epsilon_{H(L)/L}\left(\epsilon_{L/K}(\Cl(K))\right)\subseteq\epsilon_{H(L)/L}\left(\Cl(L)\right)=\{0\}.

Therefore

\Ost(H(L)/K)=\Po(H(L)/K)\subseteq\Po(H(L)/L)=\{0\}.

Indeed, the first equality follows from Definition
3.2
, the middle containment follows from part
(ii)
of Lemma
2.5
and the last equality follows from part
(iii)
of Corollary
2.3
.

∎

Remark 3.9
.

There is another proof for Theorem
3.8
: One can relativize Leriche’s method in
[
13
, proof of Proposition 3.1]
to show that all
relative Ostrowski ideals

\Pi_{\mathfrak{p}^{f}}(H(L)/K)
belong to
\epsilon_{H(L)/K}\left(\Cl(K)\right)
. Further, in the case that
L/K
is a Galois extension, using equality (
3.3
) one can see that
the result of Brumer-Rosen in
[
2
, Proposition 2.4]
coincides with Theorem
3.8
.

Definition 3.10
.

[
13
, Definition 2.3]

An extension
L/K
is said to be a
Pólya extension
if
\epsilon_{L/K}(\Po(K))=\{0\}
.

Remark 3.11
.

There is another notion of Pólya extension due to Spickermann
[
21
]
: a finite Galois extension
L/K
of number fields with Galois group
G
is said to be a
Pólya extension
, in the sense of Spickermann, if
I(L)^{G}\subseteq I(K).P(L)
. Indeed, this definition is equivalent to
\Po(L/K)=\epsilon_{L/K}(\Cl(K))
, see
[
4
, page 11]
. In other words, for Galois extensions the notion of Pólya extension in Spickermann’s sense coincides with
\Ost(L/K)=\{0\}
.

Convention.
Throughout this article, by a
Pólya extension
we mean the same notion as in Definition
3.10
, and not in the sense of Spickermann.

The following relation between Pólya fields and Pólya extensions has been found by Leriche:

Proposition 3.12
.

[
13
, Proposition 3.4]

Let
L/K
be a Pólya extension of Galois number fields. If all finite places are unramified in the estension
L/K
, then
L
is a Pólya field.

We aim to generalize the above result of Leriche. First we relativize the notion of Pólya extension:

Definition 3.13
.

For
F\subseteq K\subseteq L
a tower of finite extensions of number fields, the extension
L/K
is called an
F
-relative Pólya extension
whenever
\epsilon_{L/K}(\Po(K/F))=\{0\}
. Note that
\mathbb{Q}
-relative Pólya extension is the same notion as in Definition
3.10

.

Let

If both

Corollary 3.15
.

With the same notations and assumptions in Theorem

, the following assertions hold:

(i)

If all finite places of
K
are unramified in

\Ost(L/F)=\psi(\Ost(K/F))

(ii)

If
L/K
is an

\{0\}\rightarrow\Ost(K/F)\rightarrow\Coker(\gamma)\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\mathfrak{P}}\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{e_{\mathfrak{P}(L/K)}\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\Ost(L/F)\rightarrow\{0\}.

Remark 3.16
.

Combining the first two parts or using part

of Corollary
3.15
, we find a generalization of Leriche’s result in Proposition
3.12
.

We recall that for a finite extension
K/F
of number fields, the
relative genus field
of
K
over
F
, denoted by
\Gamma(K/F)
, is the maximal abelian unramified extension of
K
of the form
KF_{*}
for some abelian extension
F_{*}/F

[
6
]
. By the
genus field
of
K
, denoted by
\Gamma(K)
, we mean the absolute genus field of
K
(i.e.
F=\mathbb{Q}
). As an application of Proposition
3.12
, Leriche proved that:

.

[
13
, Proposition 3.4, Theorem 3.8]

If
K
is an abelian number field then the genus field
\Gamma(K)
of

is Pólya.

Unlike the Hilbert class field, the relative Pólya group of the genus field, even for abelian number fields, is not necessarily trivial. For instance, one can show that for

, we have
\Po(\Gamma(K)/K)=\Cl(K)\simeq\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}

, Example 2.14]
. Whereas using the following principal ideal theorem of Terada

]
, one can generalize Proposition

for Ostrowski quotients in “cyclic extensions”:

Proposition 3.18
.

[

]

Let
K/F
be a finite cyclic extension of number fields. Then every ambiguous ideal class in

.

.

For
K/F
a finite cyclic extension of number fields, the extension

is Ostrowski.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.18
,
\epsilon_{\Gamma(K/F)/K}(\Po(K/F))=\{0\}
, i.e.

.
∎

4.
Relativization of the of pre-Pólya group

As stated before, for a Galois number field
L
all the Ostrowski ideals above unramified primes in
L
are automatically principal, whereas in the non-Galois case this is not true, in general. Based on this, Zantema introduced the notion of the
pre-Pólya field
and gave an interesting group theoretical description of the pre-Pólya condition for number fields
[
25
,
\lx@sectionsign
6]
. Recently, the pre-Pólya condition has been generalized to the notion of the
pre-Pólya group

\Po(-)_{nr}
by Chabert and Halberstadt
[
5
]
.
Relativizing these notions, one can define:

Definition 4.1
.

The
pre-Ostrowski quotient

\Ost(L/K)_{nr}
for a finite extension
L/K
of number fields, is the subgroup of
\Ost(L/K)
defined as follows:

(4.1)

\Ost(L/K)_{nr}=\frac{Po(L/K)_{nr}}{Po(L/K)_{nr}\cap\epsilon_{L/K}(Cl(K))},

where

(4.2)

Po(L/K)_{nr}=\left<\left[\Pi_{\mathfrak{P}^{f}}(L/K)\right]\,:\,\mathfrak{P}\,\text{is a prime in}\,K\,\text{unramified in}\,L\,,\,f\in\mathbb{N}\right>.

In particular
\Ost(L/\mathbb{Q})_{nr}
coincides with the “
pre-Pólya group
”
Po(L)_{nr}
defined in
[
5
,
\lx@sectionsign 1
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
L/K
a finite extension of number fields,

(i)

\Ost(L/K)_{nr}
is trivial if and only if
Po(L/K)_{nr}\subseteq\epsilon_{L/K}(\Cl(K))
. In particular if
L/K
is a Galois extension, then
\Ost(L/K)_{nr}=\{0\}
.

(ii)

if
\epsilon_{L/K}(\Cl(K))=\{0\}
, then
\Ost(L/K)_{nr}=Po(L/K)_{nr}
;

(iii)

if
L/K
is Galois and all finite places of
K
are unramified in
L
, then

\Ost(L/K)_{nr}
is trivial and

\Po(L/K)_{nr}=\Po(L/K)=\epsilon_{L/K}(\Cl(K)).

Proof.

(i)
. The first assertion is obvious by Definition
4.1
. If
L/K
is Galois, then for a prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
K
unramified in
L
, all primes of
L
above
\mathfrak{p}
have the same residue class degree, say
f_{\mathfrak{p}}
, which implies that

\Pi_{\mathfrak{p}^{f_{\mathfrak{p}}}}(L/K)=\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}_{L}\in\epsilon_{L/K}(\Cl(K)).

(Note that for the special case
K=\mathbb{Q}
every Galois number field
L
is a “pre-Pólya” field, see
[
25
,
\lx@sectionsign
6]
).

(ii)
Immediately follows from Definition
4.1
.

(iii)
Since
L/K
is Galois and unramified, exact sequence (
3.2
) yields
\Ost(L/K)
is trivial or equivalently

\Po(L/K)=\epsilon_{L/K}(\Cl(K)).

The equality
\Po(L/K)_{nr}=\Po(L/K)
holds since
L/K
is unramified.
∎

Example 4.3
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-4027})
and
\alpha
be a root of
f(x)=x^{3}+10x+1
. One can show that
L=K(\alpha)

is a cyclic unramified extension of
K
, see
[
15
, Example 2.14]
. By Lemma
4.2
,
Ost(L/K)_{nr}
is trivial and

\Po(L/K)_{nr}=\Po(L/K)=\epsilon_{L/K}(\Cl(K)).

Indeed, since
L/K
is unramified, by exact sequence (
3.2
) we find

\#\Ker(\epsilon_{L/K})=\#H^{1}(Gal(L/K),U_{L})=\frac{\#\hat{H}^{0}(Gal(L/K),U_{L})}{Q(Gal(L/K),U_{L})},

where
Q(Gal(L/K),U_{L})
denotes the the Herbrand quotient of
U_{L}
(as
Gal(L/K)
-module). One can easily show that

Q(Gal(L/K),U_{L})=1/3,\quad\#\hat{H}^{0}(Gal(L/K),U_{L})=1,

since
U_{K}=\{\pm 1\}
and
N_{L/K}(-1)=-1
(for more details see proof of Theorem
2.12
). Therefore the capitulation kernel of
L/K
has order three. On the other hand, since
h_{K}=9
, we have

\#\Po(L/K)_{nr}=\#\Po(L/K)=\#\epsilon_{L/K}(\Cl(K))=3.

Hence
\Po(L/K)
is a nontrivial proper subgroup of
\Cl(L)
, since
h_{L}=108
.

Theorem 4.4
.

For a finite Galois extension
L/K
of number fields, we have

\Ost(L/K)\subseteq\frac{\Po(L/K)}{\Po(L/K)_{nr}}.

Proof.

Since
L/K
is Galois, the containments

(4.3)

Po(L/K)_{nr}\subseteq\epsilon_{L/K}(\Cl(K))\subseteq\Po(L/K)

prove the assertions.
∎

Abstract
. The first named author introduced the notion of upper stability for metric spaces in [ 9 ] as a relaxation of stability. The motivation was a search for a new invariant to distinguish the class of reflexive Banach spaces from stable metric spaces in the coarse and uniform category. In this paper we show that property Q Q does in fact imply upper stability. We also provide a direct proof of the fact that reflexive spaces are upper stable by relating the latter notion to the asymptotic structure of Banach spaces.

1.
Introduction

In
[
18
]
, Kalton proved two landmark results about the
coarse and uniform geometry of infinite-dimensional Banach spaces. The
first result was a nonlinear embedding result that states that every
stable metric space admits a coarse and uniform embedding into a
reflexive Banach space.

While uniform embeddings have long been considered in nonlinear Banach
space geometry (see
[
10
]
for an extensive
account), the notion of coarse embedding was introduced more recently
by Gromov (under a different terminology) in
[
15
]
as a
notion of metric faithfulness that turned out to be of paramount
importance in geometric group theory and noncommutative geometry. We
refer to the monograph
[
27
]
for a further discussion of
coarse embeddings. Kalton’s embedding result was quantitatively
refined in
[
7
]
, where it was observed that the
embeddings possess even better metric faithfulness properties: they
can be arbitrarily close to isometric embeddings.

Krivine and Maurey introduced the notion of stability for separable
Banach spaces
[
22
]
in order to extend a result of
Aldous
[
1
]
by showing that every stable Banach space
admits an isomorphic copy of
\ell_{p}
for some

p\in[1,\infty)
. Garling
[
14
]
then studied this
isometric Banach space notion in the more general context of arbitrary
metric spaces.

Definition
.

A metric space
(M,d_{M})
is
stable
if for any pair
\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}

of free ultrafilters on
\mathbb{N}
and for any two bounded sequences

(x_{n}),(y_{n})
in
M
we have

\lim_{m,\mathcal{U}}\lim_{n,\mathcal{V}}d_{M}(x_{m},y_{n})=\lim_{n,\mathcal{V}}\lim_{m,\mathcal{U}}d_{M}(x_{m},y_{n})\ .

A close connection between stability and reflexivity in the category
of metrizable topological spaces and continuous maps was known
since the original work of Krivine and Maurey, and the new insight of
Kalton was to establish another connection in the category of metric
spaces and Lipschitz maps. In the latter connection, certain spaces of
Lipschitz functions play a decisive role. Both connections rely on an
important factorization result for weakly compact operators via
reflexive spaces
[
12
]
. Kalton raised a few
natural problems.

Problem A

(
[
18
]
*Problem 6.1)
.

Does every (separable) reflexive Banach space coarsely (or
uniformly) embed into a stable metric space? Does every reflexive
Banach space admit an embedding, which is simultaneously coarse and
uniform, into a stable metric space?

Kalton explicitly suspected that Problem
A
might
have a negative solution, but that Problem
B
below
could have a positive solution.

Problem B

(
[
18
]
*Problem 6.2)
.

If
X
is a separable reflexive Banach space, does
B_{X}
embed
uniformly into a stable metric space?

We will now mainly focus on the coarse case of
Problem
A
(as the discussion of the uniform case
is similar). Recall that a metric space
(M,d_{M})

coarsely
embeds
into a metric space
(N,d_{N})
if there exist a map

f\colon M\to N
and non-decreasing functions

\omega,\rho\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
with

\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho(t)=\infty
such that for all
x,y\in M
we have

(1)

\rho\big(d_{M}(x,y)\big)\leqslant d_{N}\big(f(x),f(y)\big)\leqslant\omega\big(d_{M}(x,y)\big)\ .

A time-tested method to provide a negative answer to
Problem
A
is to find a coarse invariant,
i.e.,
a
property that is preserved under coarse embeddings, that is possessed
by every stable metric space but fails for at least one separable
reflexive Banach space. In
[
18
]
, Kalton introduced several
variants of a concentration property for Lipschitz maps defined on
certain infinite graphs, namely the
interlacing graphs
. Given
an infinite subset
L
of
\mathbb{N}
and an integer
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we consider
the set
{[L]}^{k}
of all subsets of
L
of size
k
as the
vertex set of a graph equipped with the interlacing adjacency
relation, where two vertices
\accentset{\rule{6.5556pt}{0.6pt}}{m}=\{m_{1},\dots,m_{k}\}
and

\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n}=\{n_{1},\dots,n_{k}\}
in
{[L]}^{k}
with
m_{1}<\dots<m_{k}
and

n_{1}<\dots<n_{k}
are adjacent if and only if they
interlace
,
i.e.,

\accentset{\rule{6.5556pt}{0.6pt}}{m}\neq\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n}
and either

m_{1}\leqslant n_{1}\leqslant m_{2}\leqslant n_{2}\leqslant\dots\leqslant m_{k}\leqslant n_{k}
or

n_{1}\leqslant m_{1}\leqslant n_{2}\leqslant m_{2}\leqslant\dots\leqslant n_{k}\leqslant m_{k}
. The graph
metric induced by the interlacing adjacency relation will be denoted
by
d_{I}
.

Definition
.

A metric space
(M,d_{M})
is said to have Kalton’s
property
Q

if there is a constant
C\geqslant 0
such that for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
for all bounded functions
f\colon({[\mathbb{N}]}^{k},d_{I})\to(M,d_{M})
,
there exists
L\in{[\mathbb{N}]}^{\omega}
such that

\sup_{\accentset{\rule{6.5556pt}{0.6pt}}{m},\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n}\in{[L]}^{k}}d_{M}\big(f(\accentset{\rule{6.5556pt}{0.6pt}}{m}),f(\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n})\big)\leqslant C\lip(f)\ .

The infimum of all constants
C
satisfying the above will be
denoted
Q_{M}
and called the
property
Q
constant of
M
.

Here
{[\mathbb{N}]}^{\omega}
denotes the set of all infinite subsets of
\mathbb{N}

and
\lip(f)
is the Lipschitz constant of
f
. Kalton’s original
definition of property
Q
for Banach spaces was given in terms of a
modulus
[
18
]
*pages 403–404, but eventually took the
form above in
[
21
]
*page 1057 (see
also
[
20
]
*page 1283).

The study of concentration inequalities for Lipschitz maps defined on
infinite graphs with countable degree, which began with property
Q

in
[
18
]
for interlacing graphs and continued shortly
thereafter in
[
17
]
for Hamming graphs, was a genuine
breakthrough towards unlocking many mysteries regarding the nonlinear
geometry of Banach spaces in terms of their infinite-dimensional
and asymptotic properties
(see
[
3
]
[
19
]
[
2
]
[
24
]
[
6
]
[
26
]
[
11
]
[
23
]
[
4
]
[
5
]
[
8
]
[
13
]
).

Kalton observed
[
18
]
*Proposition 3.1 that every stable
metric space has property
Q
, and that if a Banach space
X
admits a
coarse embedding into a metric space with property
Q
, then
X
has
property
Q
. The interlacing graph metric can be seen as being
induced by the summing basis of
\mathrm{c}_{0}
, and thus every Banach space
containing an isomorphic copy of
\mathrm{c}_{0}
contains bi-Lipschitz copies of
the sequence
\big({[\mathbb{N}]}^{k},d_{I}\big)_{k=1}^{\infty}
of
interlacing graphs with uniformly bounded distortion. It follows that

\mathrm{c}_{0}
does not embed coarsely into any metric space with
property
Q
. The second remarkable result of Kalton
from
[
18
]
is that reflexive Banach spaces also have
property
Q
. An important consequence is that
\mathrm{c}_{0}
does not coarsely
embed into any reflexive Banach space, thereby solving a longstanding
open question in the coarse geometry of Banach spaces. Kalton also
showed that property
Q
is a uniform as well as coarse invariant for
Banach spaces: if a Banach space
X
, or its closed unit ball
B_{X}
,
admits a uniform embedding into a metric space with property
Q
, then

X
has property
Q
. It follows that
\mathrm{c}_{0}
, or indeed the closed unit
ball of
\mathrm{c}_{0}
, does not uniformly embed into any reflexive Banach space,
solving yet another old open problem in the nonlinear theory of Banach
spaces.

The fact that reflexive spaces have property
Q
rules out
property
Q
as a potential coarse invariant to resolve negatively
Problem
A
. In an attempt to attack Problem

A
, the first author introduced
in
[
9
]
another invariant called upper stability. This
bi-Lipschitz invariant is a natural relaxation of the notion of
stability in light of a characterization of stability due to
Raynaud
[
28
]
(see Theorem
1

below). Upper stability was shown to imply property
Q
and to be
preserved under coarse embeddings (for Banach space domains and metric
space targets akin to property
Q
).

Definition
.

Let
(M,d_{M})
be a metric space and
C
be a non-negative real
number. We say that
M
is
C
-upper stable
if for all
integers
1\leqslant l<k
, bounded functions

f\colon{[\mathbb{N}]}^{l}\to M
and
g\colon{[\mathbb{N}]}^{k-l}\to M
,
infinite sets
L\subset\mathbb{N}
and permutations
\pi\in S_{k}
preserving
the order on
\{1,\dots,l\}
and on
\{l+1,\dots,k\}
,

\inf_{\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n}\in{[L]}^{k}}d_{M}\big(f(n_{1},\dots,n_{l}),g(n_{l+1},\dots,n_{k})\big)\leqslant\\
C\cdot\sup_{\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n}\in{[L]}^{k}}d_{M}\big(f(n_{\pi(1)},\dots,n_{\pi(l)}),g(n_{\pi(l+1)},\dots,n_{\pi(k)})\big)\ .

We say that
M
is
upper stable
if it is
C
-upper stable
for some
C
.

There are good reasons to think that an approach to solving
Problem
A
via upper stability would work. Indeed,
if
X
is a Banach space containing no copies of
\ell_{p}
for

1\leqslant p<\infty
, then
X
cannot isomorphically embed into a stable
Banach space because of the result of Krivine and Maurey mentioned
above. If in addition
X
is separable, then
X
is not even
bi-Lipschitzly embeddable into a reflexive stable Banach space due to
a result of Heinrich and
Mankiewicz
[
16
]
*Theorem 3.5. Thus, any such space
X

(Tsirelson’s space, Schlumprecht’s space, Gowers-Maurey space,
etc.
)
could be a candidate for a reflexive space failing upper stability. In
this paper, we dash this hope in two different ways, and at the same
time, we further our understanding of the connection between
stability, reflexivity and property
Q
.

In Section
3
, we
prove that every reflexive Banach space is upper stable. Since
upper stability implies property
Q
(see
[
9
]
), we thus
recover the fact, originally proved by Kalton in
[
18
]
,
that reflexive spaces have property
Q
. Kalton’s original proof
relies on some clever “calculus” for certain limiting operators in
reflexive spaces. In order to prove the a priori stronger property of
upper stability, we take a more conceptual approach as we relate
upper stability with the asymptotic structure of Banach spaces in the
sense of Maurey, Milman and Tomczak-Jaegermann
[
25
]
.

First, however, we prove in
Section
2
the more general
result that property
Q
and upper stability are equivalent notions
(up to the value of the constants involved) in the category of metric
spaces.

2.
Upper stability and property
Q

We begin by stating the following characterization of stability, which
combines work of Krivine and Maurey
[
22
]
and
Raynaud
[
28
]
(see also
[
9
]
).

Theorem 1
.

The following are equivalent for a metric space
(M,d_{M})
.

(i)

M
is stable.

(ii)

For all
1\leqslant l<k
, bounded functions
f\colon{[\mathbb{N}]}^{l}\to M

and
g\colon{[\mathbb{N}]}^{k-l}\to M
, free ultrafilters

\mathcal{U}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{U}_{k}
and permutations
\pi\in S_{k}
preserving the
order on
\{1,\dots,l\}
and on
\{l+1,\dots,k\}
,

\lim_{n_{1},\mathcal{U}_{1}}\dots\lim_{n_{k},\mathcal{U}_{k}}d_{M}\big(f(n_{1},\dots,n_{l}),g(n_{l+1},\dots,n_{k})\big)=\\
\lim_{n_{\pi^{-1}(1)},\mathcal{U}_{\pi^{-1}(1)}}\dots\lim_{n_{\pi^{-1}(k)},\mathcal{U}_{\pi^{-1}(k)}}d_{M}\big(f(n_{1},\dots,n_{l}),g(n_{l+1},\dots,n_{k})\big)\ .

(iii)

For all
1\leqslant l<k
, bounded functions
f\colon{[\mathbb{N}]}^{l}\to M

and
g\colon{[\mathbb{N}]}^{k-l}\to M
, free ultrafilters
\mathcal{U}
and
permutations
\pi\in S_{k}
preserving the order on
\{1,\dots,l\}

and on
\{l+1,\dots,k\}
,

\lim_{n_{1},\mathcal{U}}\dots\lim_{n_{k},\mathcal{U}}d_{M}\big(f(n_{1},\dots,n_{l}),g(n_{l+1},\dots,n_{k})\big)=\\
\lim_{n_{1},\mathcal{U}}\dots\lim_{n_{k},\mathcal{U}}d_{M}\big(f(n_{\pi(1)},\dots,n_{\pi(l)}),g(n_{\pi(l+1)},\dots,n_{\pi(k)})\big)\ .

(iv)

For all
1\leqslant l<k
, bounded functions
f\colon{[\mathbb{N}]}^{l}\to M

and
g\colon{[\mathbb{N}]}^{k-l}\to M
, infinite sets
L\subset\mathbb{N}

and permutations
\pi\in S_{k}
preserving the order on

\{1,\dots,l\}
and on
\{l+1,\dots,k\}
,

\inf_{\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n}\in{[L]}^{k}}d_{M}\big(f(n_{1},\dots,n_{l}),g(n_{l+1},\dots,n_{k})\big)\leqslant\\
\sup_{\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n}\in{[L]}^{k}}d_{M}\big(f(n_{\pi(1)},\dots,n_{\pi(l)}),g(n_{\pi(l+1)},\dots,n_{\pi(k)})\big)\ .

In light of the above characterization, the definition of
upper stability given in the Introduction is a very natural relaxation
of stability. There is a certain asymmetry in part (iv) above, and
hence in the definition of upper stability, in the sense that the
permutation is applied only to the right-hand side of the defining
inequality. We will now introduce an a priori stronger version of
upper stability by “symmetrizing” this inequality. We will also
introduce a different formulation in terms of cuts instead of
permutations.

Fix
k,l,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
k=l+m
. By a
cut of
\{1,\dots,k\}
of
size
l

we mean simply a subset
P
of
\{1,\dots,k\}
of size

l
. If
P=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{l}\}
with
p_{1}<\dots<p_{l}
, then for

n_{1}<\dots<n_{k}
in
\mathbb{N}
, we write
(n_{i}:\,i\in P)
for

(n_{p_{1}},\dots,n_{p_{l}})
. The proof of Raynaud’s characterization of
stability shows the following.

Proposition 2
.

For a metric space
(M,d_{M})
and a constant
C\geqslant 1
, the following
are equivalent.

(i)

For all
k,l,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
k=l+m
, free ultrafilters
\mathcal{U}
,
bounded functions
f\colon{[\mathbb{N}]}^{l}\to M
and

g\colon{[\mathbb{N}]}^{m}\to M
and cuts
P,Q
of
\{1,\dots,k\}
of
size
l
, we have

\lim_{n_{1},\mathcal{U}}\dots\lim_{n_{k},\mathcal{U}}d_{M}\big(f(n_{i}:\,i\in P),g(n_{i}:\,i\in P^{c})\big)\leqslant\\
C\cdot\lim_{n_{1},\mathcal{U}}\dots\lim_{n_{k},\mathcal{U}}d_{M}\big(f(n_{i}:\,i\in Q),g(n_{i}:\,i\in Q^{c})\big)\ .

(ii)

For all
k,l,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
k=l+m
, bounded functions

f\colon{[\mathbb{N}]}^{l}\to M
and
g\colon{[\mathbb{N}]}^{m}\to M
,
infinite sets
L\subset\mathbb{N}
and cuts
P,Q
of
\{1,\dots,k\}
of
size
l
, we have

\inf_{\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n}\in{[L]}^{k}}d_{M}\big(f(n_{i}:\,i\in P),g(n_{i}:\,i\in P^{c})\big)\leqslant\\
C\cdot\sup_{\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n}\in{[L]}^{k}}d_{M}\big(f(n_{i}:\,i\in Q),g(n_{i}:\,i\in Q^{c})\big)\ .

Definition
.

We say a metric space
(M,d_{M})
is
C
-cut stable
if it
satisfies one of the two equivalent conditions in
Proposition
2
. We say
M
is

cut stable
if it is
C
-cut stable for some
C\geqslant 1
.

Remarks
.

We recover the notion of upper stability by taking
P
to be the
trivial cut
\{1,2,\dots,l\}
above. We will later see that every
upper stable metric space is cut stable, although this is not
entirely trivial. It is clear that upper stability is a Lipschitz
invariant, and hence in particular an isomorphic invariant for
Banach spaces. In contrast, stability is an isometric invariant. It
is easy to find equivalent norms on the stable Banach space

\ell_{1}
, for instance, which is not stable.

As mentioned in the Introduction, Kalton’s original definition of
property
Q
was given in terms of a modulus, which we now recall.

Definition

(Kalton
[
18
]
)
.

Let
(M,d_{M})
be a metric space. Given
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta\geqslant 0
,
we say that
M
has the
Q(\varepsilon,\delta)
-property
if for all

k\in\mathbb{N}
and for all functions

f\colon\big({[\mathbb{N}]}^{k},d_{I}\big)\to M
with

\lip(f)\leqslant\delta
, there exists
L\in{[\mathbb{N}]}^{\omega}
such that

d_{M}(f(\accentset{\rule{6.5556pt}{0.6pt}}{m}),f(\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n}))<\varepsilon\qquad\text{for all }\accentset{\rule{6.5556pt}{0.6pt}}{m}<\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n}\text{ in
}{[L]}^{k}\ .

For
\varepsilon>0
we define

\Delta_{M}(\varepsilon)=\sup\{\delta\geqslant 0:\,M\text{ has the
$Q(\varepsilon,\delta)$-property}\}\ ,

and set
q_{M}
to be the supremum of all constants
c\geqslant 0
for
which
\Delta_{M}(\varepsilon)\geqslant c\varepsilon
for all
\varepsilon>0
.

Remark
.

It is immediate from above that
M
has property
Q
if and only if

q_{M}>0
in which case
q_{M}^{-1}\leqslant Q_{M}\leqslant 2q_{M}^{-1}
. Kalton proved
in
[
18
]
that for a stable metric space
M
,
q_{M}\geqslant 1
,
and for a reflexive Banach space
X
,
q_{X}\geqslant 1/2
(and

Q_{X}\leqslant 2
). Note that for any infinite-dimensional Banach space

X
,
q_{X}\leqslant 1
. Indeed, for any
\delta>1
there exists

f\colon\mathbb{N}\to X
with
1<\lVert f(m)-f(n)\rVert<\delta
for all
m<n
,
and thus
X
does not have the
Q(1,\delta)
-property.

Remark
.

Let
(e_{i})
and
(s_{i})
denote the unit vector basis and,
respectively, the summing basis of
\mathrm{c}_{0}
. Thus,

s_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
define

f\colon({[\mathbb{N}]}^{k},d_{I})\to\mathrm{c}_{0}
by

f(\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}s_{n_{i}}
. Note that

\lVert f(\accentset{\rule{6.5556pt}{0.6pt}}{m})-f(\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n})\rVert=d_{I}(\accentset{\rule{6.5556pt}{0.6pt}}{m},\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n})=1
for adjacent vertices, and
hence
\lip(f)=1
. Moreover,
\lVert f(\accentset{\rule{6.5556pt}{0.6pt}}{m})-f(\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n})\rVert=k
for all

\accentset{\rule{6.5556pt}{0.6pt}}{m}<\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n}
in
{[\mathbb{N}]}^{k}
. Thus
\mathrm{c}_{0}
has the worst possible
behaviour for property
Q
. Indeed, this behaviour of the summing
basis motivated the proof by Kalton that
\mathrm{c}_{0}
does not uniformly or
coarsely embed into a reflexive space. It was also the motivation
behind the interlacing graph. Indeed,

\lVert f(\accentset{\rule{6.5556pt}{0.6pt}}{m})-f(\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n})\rVert\leqslant d_{I}(\accentset{\rule{6.5556pt}{0.6pt}}{m},\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n})\leqslant 2\lVert f(\accentset{\rule{6.5556pt}{0.6pt}}{m})-f(\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n})\rVert\qquad\text{for all }\accentset{\rule{6.5556pt}{0.6pt}}{m},\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n}\in{[\mathbb{N}]}^{k}

and for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Remark
.

If follows easily by scaling that for a Banach space
X
, the
function
\Delta_{X}
is linear, and thus
\Delta_{X}(\varepsilon)=q_{X}\varepsilon

for all
\varepsilon>0
. Another observation of Kalton seems slightly less
obvious, so we provide a proof next.

Proposition 3
.

For a Banach space
X
,
\Delta_{X}(\varepsilon)=\Delta_{B_{X}}(\varepsilon)
for
all
0<\varepsilon<1
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\Delta_{X}(\varepsilon)\leqslant\Delta_{B_{X}}(\varepsilon)
for any

\varepsilon>0
. For the converse, we will first show that
\Delta_{B_{X}}

is “linear” on
(0,1)
. Fix
b
with
0<b<1
. Given

0<\varepsilon_{1}<\varepsilon_{2}\leqslant b\ ,

write
\varepsilon_{2}=a\varepsilon_{1}
for suitable
a>1
. We show that

\Delta_{B_{X}}(\varepsilon_{2})=a\Delta_{B_{X}}(\varepsilon_{1})
. This will show
linearity on
(0,b]
, and hence on
(0,1)
. To show that

\Delta_{B_{X}}(\varepsilon_{2})=a\Delta_{B_{X}}(\varepsilon_{1})
, we may clearly
assume that
a\leqslant b^{-1}
as the general case will then follow by
repeated applications of the special case. The inequality

\Delta_{B_{X}}(\varepsilon_{2})\geqslant a\Delta_{B_{X}}(\varepsilon_{1})
follows by simple
scaling. Conversely, assume that
B_{X}
has the

Q(\varepsilon_{2},\delta)
-property. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and

f\colon\big({[\mathbb{N}]}^{k},d_{I}\big)\to B_{X}
be a function with

\lip(f)\leqslant\delta/a
. Then in particular,

\lip(f)\leqslant\delta
, and hence there exists
L\in{[\mathbb{N}]}^{\omega}

such that
\lVert f(\accentset{\rule{6.5556pt}{0.6pt}}{m})-f(\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n})\rVert<\varepsilon_{2}
for all
\accentset{\rule{6.5556pt}{0.6pt}}{m}<\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n}

in
{[L]}^{k}
. Enumerate
L
as
l_{1}<l_{2}<\dots
and set

\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{l}=\{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}\}
. Define

g\colon\big({[\mathbb{N}]}^{k},d_{I}\big)\to X
by

\displaystyle=\lim_{n_{1},\mathcal{U}_{1}}\dots\lim_{n_{k},\mathcal{U}_{k}}z^{*}(n_{1},\dots,n_{j})\Big(x+\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}t(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\lim_{n_{1},\mathcal{U}_{1}}\dots\lim_{n_{k},\mathcal{U}_{k}}\Big\lVert x+\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}t(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})\Big\rVert

\displaystyle=N_{t}^{\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}}\Big(x\oplus\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}e_{i}\Big)\ .

∎

Remark
.

Lemma
10
shows that
N_{t}^{\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}}
is in fact a
norm on
X\oplus\mathbb{R}^{k}
extending the norm of
X
, and the unit
vector basis of
\mathbb{R}^{k}
is a normalized, monotone basis with respect
to

. By an earlier remark, the restriction of

to
\mathbb{R}^{k}

asymptotic

. It is not very hard to show that if

\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}

Our next result approximates bounded functions, like those appearing
in Raynaud’s characterization of stability, by weakly null trees.

Lemma 11
.

Assume
X
is reflexive. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{U}_{k})

be a sequence of free ultrafilters and
f\colon{[\mathbb{N}]}^{k}\to X

a bounded function. Then there exist a normalized,
\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}
-weakly null
rooted tree
t
in
X
and non-negative scalars
a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{k}

such that

\lim_{\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n},\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}}\Big\lVert f(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})-\textstyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}t(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})\Big\rVert=0\ .

Proof.

Define for each
1\leqslant j\leqslant k
the function

\displaystyle\partial^{j}f\colon{[\mathbb{N}]}^{k-j}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\partial^{j}f(n_{1},\dots,n_{k-j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\wlim_{n_{k-j+1},\mathcal{U}_{k-j+1}}\dots\wlim_{n_{k},\mathcal{U}_{k}}f(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})\ .

We will identify the function
\partial^{k}f
on

{[\mathbb{N}]}^{0}=\{\emptyset\}
with its image in
X
. Note that for
each
\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n}\in{[\mathbb{N}]}^{k}
we have

(3)

f(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})=\partial^{k}f+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\big(\partial^{k-i}f(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})-\partial^{k-i+1}f(n_{1},\dots,n_{i-1})\big)\ .

Define an unrooted tree
a
in
\mathbb{R}
of height
k
by setting

a(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})=\big\lVert\partial^{k-i}f(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})-\partial^{k-i+1}f(n_{1},\dots,n_{i-1})\big\rVert

for
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
and
n_{1}<\dots<n_{i}
in
\mathbb{N}
. We next define

t(\emptyset)\in S_{X}
and non-negative scalars
a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{k}

by

\displaystyle\partial^{k}f

\displaystyle=a_{0}t(\emptyset)

\displaystyle a_{i}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n_{1},\mathcal{U}_{1}}\dots\lim_{n_{i},\mathcal{U}_{i}}a(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})\qquad\text{for }1\leqslant i\leqslant k\ .

We now define our rooted tree
t
in
S_{X}
as follows. We have
already defined
t(\emptyset)
. Fix
1\leqslant i\leqslant k
. If
a_{i}=0
,
then for any
n_{1}<\dots<n_{i-1}
in
\mathbb{N}
, we choose

\big(t(n_{1},\dots,n_{i-1},n)\big)_{n=n_{i-1}+1}^{\infty}
to be an
arbitrary normalized weakly null sequence. If
a_{i}\neq 0
, then for

n_{1}<\dots<n_{i}
we set

t(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})=\begin{cases}\frac{\partial^{k-i}f(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})-\partial^{k-i+1}f(n_{1},\dots,n_{i-1})}{a(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})}&\text{if }a(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})\neq 0\ ,\\
\text{an arbitrary norm-$1$ vector}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

If
a_{i}\neq 0
, then
\forall\,_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}n_{1}\dots\forall\,_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}n_{i}

a(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})>a_{i}/2
, from which it follows that
t
is

\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}
-weakly null. Now observe that for
n_{1}<\dots<n_{k}
we have

f(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})-\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}t(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})\\
=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Big[\big(\partial^{k-i}f(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})-\partial^{k-i+1}f(n_{1},\dots,n_{i-1})\big)-a(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})t(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})\Big]\\
+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\big(a(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})-a_{i}\big)t(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})\ ,

where we used (
3
). It follows immediately that

\lim_{\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n},\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}}\Big\lVert f(n_{1},\dots,n_{k})-\textstyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}t(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})\Big\rVert=0\ ,

as required.
∎

An important tool in showing that reflexive spaces are cut stable will

Definition
.

Let
k,l,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
k=l+m
. Let
s
and

be trees in
X

of height

and

, respectively. For a cut

of

in

defined by

(s\text{\textcircled{z}}_{P}t)(n_{1},\dots,n_{j})=\begin{cases}s(n_{i}:\,i\in P,\ i\leqslant j)&\text{if }j\in P\ ,\\
t(n_{i}:\,i\in P^{c},\ i\leqslant j)&\text{if }j\in P^{c}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\text{for }1\leqslant i\leqslant l\ ,

\displaystyle(s\text{\textcircled{z}}_{P}t)(n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{q_{j}})

Given a sequence
\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{U}_{k})

\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}_{P}=(\mathcal{U}_{p_{1}},\dots,\mathcal{U}_{p_{l}})

\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}_{P^{c}}=(\mathcal{U}_{q_{1}},\dots,\mathcal{U}_{q_{m}})
. Note that if

\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}_{P}

\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}_{P^{c}}

Although the standard basis of

is not in general unconditional

Proposition 12
.

Assume

is reflexive. Let
k,l,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
k=l+m
and
P
be a
cut of
\{1,\dots,k\}
of size
l
. Let
\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{U}_{k})

be a sequence of free ultrafilters. Let
s
and
t
be normalized
trees in
X
of height
l
and
m
, respectively, such that

s
is
\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}_{P}
-weakly null and
t
is
\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}_{P^{c}}
-weakly

(4)

N^{\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{s\text{\textcircled{z}}_{P}t}\Big(\textstyle\sum_{i\in P}a_{i}e_{i}\Big)\leqslant N^{\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{s\text{\textcircled{z}}_{P}t}\Big(\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}e_{i}\Big)

and

(5)

N^{\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{s\text{\textcircled{z}}_{P}t}\Big(\textstyle\sum_{i\in P^{c}}a_{i}e_{i}\Big)\leqslant N^{\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{s\text{\textcircled{z}}_{P}t}\Big(\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}e_{i}\Big)

(a_{i})_{i=1}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
.

Proof.

Write
P
as
P=\{p_{1},\dots,p_{l}\}
with
p_{1}<\dots<p_{l}
and
P^{c}
as

P^{c}=\{q_{1},\dots,q_{m}\}
with
q_{1}<\dots<q_{m}
. Put
u=s\text{\textcircled{z}}_{P}t
. We
claim that

(6)

N^{\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{u}\Big(\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{p_{i}}e_{p_{i}}+\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}a_{q_{j}}e_{q_{j}}\Big)\leqslant N^{\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{u}\Big(\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}e_{i}\Big)\ .

for all
(a_{i})_{i=1}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
. Using the proof of this claim, we
can repeatedly eliminate coefficients to
deduce (
4
). The proof of (
5
) is
similar.

To see the claim, fix
\varepsilon>0
,
n_{1}<\dots<n_{q_{m}-1}
in
\mathbb{N}
and

L\in\mathcal{U}_{q_{m}}
such that

0\in\overline{\{u(n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{q_{m}}):\,n_{q_{m}}\in L\}}^{w}

and for all
n_{q_{m}}\in L
and for all
(a_{i})_{i=1}^{k}\in[-1,1]^{k}
,
we have

\bigg\lvert N^{\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{u}\Big(\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}e_{i}\Big)-\\
\lim_{n_{q_{m}+1},\mathcal{U}_{q_{m}+1}}\dots\lim_{n_{k},\mathcal{U}_{k}}\Big\lVert\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}u(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})\Big\rVert\bigg\rvert<\varepsilon\ .

Note that
\forall\,_{\mathcal{U}_{1}}n_{1}\dots\forall\,_{\mathcal{U}_{q_{m}-1}}n_{q_{m}-1}

exists. By Mazur’s theorem there is a finite set

\sum_{n\in F}\tau_{n}u(n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{q_{m}-1},n)

Since

\max F<n_{q_{m}+1}<\dots<n_{k}
such

\bigg\lvert N^{\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{u}\Big(\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}e_{i}\Big)-\\

for all

and for all

and observe that

\displaystyle N^{\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{u}\Big(\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{p_{i}}e_{p_{i}}

\displaystyle+\textstyle\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}a_{q_{j}}e_{q_{j}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leqslant N^{\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{u}\Big(\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}e_{i}\Big)+5\varepsilon\ .

Here it is crucial that for
i>q_{m}
,

does not

.

We are now ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 13
.

Every reflexive Banach space is
5
-cut stable, and thus, in
particular, upper stable.

Proof.

Fix
k,l,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
k=l+m
, a sequence

\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}=(\mathcal{U}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{U}_{k})
of free ultrafilters and cuts
P,Q
of

\{1,\dots,k\}
each of size
l
. Let
X
be an
infinite-dimensional, reflexive Banach space and

f\colon{[\mathbb{N}]}^{l}\to X
and
g\colon{[\mathbb{N}]}^{m}\to X
be
bounded functions. We will show that

(7)

\lim_{\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n},\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}}\lVert f(n_{i}:\,i\in P)+g(n_{i}:\,i\in P^{c})\rVert\\
\leqslant 5\lim_{\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n},\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}}\lVert f(n_{i}:\,i\in Q)+g(n_{i}:\,i\in Q^{c})\rVert

in the special case when the ultrafilters
\mathcal{U}_{i}
are all the
same. This will then complete the proof.

Write
P=\{p_{1}\,\dots,p_{l}\}
with
p_{1}<\dots<p_{l}
and

P^{c}=\{q_{1},\dots,q_{m}\}
with
q_{1}<\dots<q_{m}
. By
Lemma
11
we may assume that there exist
normalized, rooted trees
s
and
t
in
X
of height
l
and
m
,
respectively, such that
s
is
\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}_{P}
-weakly null,
t
is

\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}_{P^{c}}
-weakly null, and there exist non-negative scalars

a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{l}
and
b_{0},b_{1},\dots,b_{m}
such that

f(n_{1},\dots,n_{l})=a_{0}s(\emptyset)+\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{i}s(n_{1},\dots,n_{i})\qquad\text{for }n_{1}<\dots<n_{l}

and

g(n_{1},\dots,n_{m})=b_{0}t(\emptyset)+\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}t(n_{1},\dots,n_{j})\qquad\text{for }n_{1}<\dots<n_{m}\ .

u=s\text{\textcircled{z}}_{P}t

c_{p_{i}}=a_{i}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant l
and

for

N^{\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}}_{u}\Big(\textstyle\sum_{i\in P}c_{i}e_{i}\Big)=N^{\accentset{\rule{4.91673pt}{0.6pt}}{\mathcal{U}}_{P}}_{s}\Big(\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{i}e_{i}\Big)\ .

Now observe that for
\accentset{\rule{3.27773pt}{0.6pt}}{n}\in{[\mathbb{N}]}^{k}

(8)

Hence (

), (

)
and (

as appropriate.
∎

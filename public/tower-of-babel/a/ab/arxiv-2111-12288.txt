Stable determination of an elastic medium scatterer by a single far-field measurement and beyond
Zhengjian Bai, Huaian Diao, Hongyu Liu, Qingle Meng

Abstract
. We are concerned with the time-harmonic elastic scattering due to an inhomogeneous elastic material inclusion located inside a uniformly homogeneous isotropic medium. We establish a sharp stability estimate of logarithmic type in determining the support of the elastic scatterer, independent of its material content, by a single far-field measurement when the support is a convex polyhedral domain in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , n = 2 , 3 n=2,3 . Our argument in establishing the stability result is localized around a corner of the medium scatterer. This enables us to further establish a byproduct result by proving that if a generic medium scatterer, not necessary to be a polyhedral shape, possesses a corner, then there exists a positive lower bound of the scattered far-field patterns. The latter result indicates that if an elastic material object possesses a corner on its support, then it scatters every incident wave stably and invisibility phenomenon does not occur. Keywords: Inverse shape problem, elastic scattering, stability, single measurement, corner scattering, invisibility 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35Q60, 78A46, 35P25



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Mathematical setup

We are mainly concerned with the time-harmonic elastic wave scattering due to the impingement of an incident field on an inhomogeneous isotropic medium scatterer as well as the associated inverse problem of determining the scatterer from the corresponding far-field measurement. We first introduce the mathematical formulation of our study.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a connected complement
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\overline{\Omega}
,
n=2,3
. In the physical setup,
\Omega
is the support of an inhomogeneous elastic scatterer embedded in a uniformly homogeneous background space. The elastic medium parameters are characterised by
\rho
and
\lambda,\mu
, which are respectively referred to as the density and the bulk moduli. It is assumed that
\lambda
and
\mu
are real constants satisfying the strong convexity conditions:

\mu>0\quad\mbox{ and }\quad n\,\lambda+2\mu>0.

It is also assumed that
\rho\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
{\Omega=\mathrm{supp}(1-\rho)}
. That is, by normalisation, we assume that the density of the homogeneous background space is
1
, whereas the scatterer is characterised by the inhomogeneous density
\rho
in
\Omega
.

To introduce the Lamé system that describes the elastic scattering, we let
\omega\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
signify the angular frequency of the time-harmonic elastic wave. Denote

\kappa_{\mathrm{s}}=\omega\sqrt{1/\mu}\mbox{ and }\kappa_{\mathrm{p}}=\omega\sqrt{1/(\lambda+2\mu)}

by the shear and compressional wave numbers, respectively. Let
\mathbf{u}^{i}
denote the time-harmonic plane incident wave of the following form:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}^{i}=\alpha_{1}\,\mathbf{d}\,e^{\imath\kappa_{\mathrm{p}}\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{d}}+\alpha_{2}\,\mathbf{d}^{\perp}e^{\imath\kappa_{\mathrm{s}}\mathbf{x}\cdot\mathbf{d}},\quad\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{C},\quad|\alpha_{1}|+|\alpha_{2}|\neq 0,

where
\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is called the incident direction, and
\mathbf{d}^{\perp}
is a unit vector orthogonal to
\mathbf{d}
. Due to the interaction between the incident elastic wave
\mathbf{u}^{i}
and the elastic scatter
\Omega
, the scattered elastic wave
\mathbf{u}^{s}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
is generated. This induces the total elastic wave
\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, which is the superposition of
\mathbf{u}^{i}
and
\mathbf{u}^{s}
, namely
\mathbf{u}:=\mathbf{u}^{i}+\mathbf{u}^{s}
, and satisfies the Navier equation

(1.2)

\Delta^{*}\mathbf{u}+\rho\,\omega^{2}\mathbf{u}={\bf 0},\quad\Delta^{*}\mathbf{u}:=\mu\Delta\mathbf{u}+(\lambda+\mu)\nabla(\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}),\quad\mbox{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\overline{\Omega}.

Clearly,
\mathbf{u}^{i}
is an entire solution to (
1.2
) with
\rho=1
. By the Helmholtz decomposition, any solution
\mathbf{u}\in H_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{n}
to (
1.2
) can be decomposed as follows:

(1.3)

\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}+\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{s}},

where
\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}
and
\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{s}}
satisfy the equations:

(1.4)

\begin{cases}(\Delta+\kappa^{2}_{\mathrm{p}})\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}={\bf 0},&\quad\nabla\times\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}=\bf 0,\\
(\Delta+\kappa^{2}_{\mathrm{s}})\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{s}}={\bf 0},&\quad\nabla\,\cdot\,\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{s}}=0.\end{cases}

The scattered wave
\mathbf{u}^{s}
satisfies the Kurpradze radiation condition

(1.5)

\begin{cases}\lim_{|\mathbf{x}|\to\infty}|\mathbf{x}|^{(n-1)/2}(\frac{\partial{\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}^{s}}}{\partial{|\mathbf{x}|}}-\imath\kappa_{p}\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}^{s})=\bf 0,\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\\
\lim_{|\mathbf{x}|\to\infty}|\mathbf{x}|^{(n-1)/2}(\frac{\partial{\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{s}}^{s}}}{\partial{|\mathbf{x}|}}-\imath\kappa_{s}\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{s}}^{s})=\bf 0,\end{cases}

and admits the following asymptotic expansion (cf.
[
31
]
):

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}^{s}(\mathbf{x})=\dfrac{\exp(\imath\kappa_{\mathrm{p}}|\mathbf{x}|)}{|\mathbf{x}|^{(n-1)/2}}\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{p}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})+\dfrac{\exp(\imath\kappa_{\mathrm{s}}|\mathbf{x}|)}{|\mathbf{x}|^{(n-1)/2}}\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{s}}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})+\mathcal{O}(|\mathbf{x}|^{-\frac{n+1}{2}})\quad\mbox{as}\quad|\mathbf{x}|\to\infty,

which holds uniformly in all directions
\hat{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, where
\imath:=\sqrt{-1}
is the imaginary unit. The vector fields
\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{p}}
and
\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{s}}
are referred to as the longitudinal and transversal far-field patterns, respectively, which respectively characterize the asymptotic behaviors of the normal part
\mathbf{u}^{s}_{\mathrm{p}}
and of the tangential part
\mathbf{u}^{s}_{\mathrm{s}}
of
\mathbf{u}^{s}
. Let
\mathbf{U}=(\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{p}},\,\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{s}})
denote the far-field pattern of
\mathbf{u}^{s}
, which is an element of the liner space
L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})^{n}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})^{n}
equipped with the following norm

\|(\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n})}=\|\mathbf{w}_{1}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})^{n}}+\|\mathbf{w}_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})^{n}},

where
(\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{w}_{2})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})^{n}\times L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})^{n}
.

The inverse problem of our study can be introduced as determining the support of the inhomogeneous scattering object, namely
\Omega
, independent of its material content
(\lambda,\mu,\rho)
, by knowledge of the far-field pattern
\mathbf{U}(\hat{\mathbf{x}})
. By introducing an operator
\mathscr{F}
which is defined by the forward scattering system (
1.1
)–(
1.6
) and sends the scatterer
(\Omega;\lambda,\mu,\rho)
to the corresponding far-field pattern associated with an incident field
\mathbf{u}^{i}
, the inverse problem can be abstractly given as:

(1.7)

\mathscr{F}(\Omega)=\mathbf{U}(\hat{\mathbf{x}};\mathbf{u}^{i}),\quad\hat{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}.

It is emphasized that in our study of (
1.7
), the material content of
\Omega
, namely
(\lambda,\mu,\rho)
, is not required to be known in advance though it belongs to a certain general a-priori class as shall be introduced in what follows. Moreover, we shall consider the case that only a fixed
\mathbf{u}^{i}
is used, namely
\mathbf{u}^{i}
is given in (
1.1
) with fixed
\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\mathbf{d}
and
\mathbf{d}^{\perp}
. In such a case,
\mathbf{U}(\hat{\mathbf{x}};\mathbf{u}^{i})
is said to be a single far-field measurement. The inverse scattering problem with a single far-field measurement constitutes a longstanding problem in the literature. Finally, we remark that by direction verifications, the inverse problem (
1.7
) is nonlinear and it is formally-determined with a single far-field measurement.

The second focus of our study is under what conditions,
\mathbf{U}\equiv\mathbf{0}
. This is another aspect of the inverse scattering problem (
1.7
) and in such a case, the underlying object
(\Omega;\lambda,\mu,\rho)
is invisible with respect to the far-field measurement.

1.2.
Statement of the main stability result and its implication to invisibility

First, we introduce the a-priori class of elastic scatterers for our study.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with a connected complement
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\overline{\Omega}
,
n=2,3
. In addition,
\Omega=\mathrm{supp}(1-\rho)\subset\overline{B_{R}}
, where
B_{R}
signifies a central ball of radius
R
.
We say
(\Omega;\rho)\in\mathcal{K}
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(a)

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a convex polygon and the opening angle at each vertex of
\Omega
is in
(2\alpha_{m},2\alpha_{M})
,
\alpha_{m}>0
and
\alpha_{M}<\pi/2
;

(b)

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a convex polyhedron;

(c)

The distances of any vertex of
\Omega
to its non-adjacent edges are at least
l_{0}
,
0<l_{0}\leq 1
;

(d)

\rho(\mathbf{x})
is a uniformly
\theta
-Hölder continuous function in
\overline{\Omega}
,
0<\theta\leq 1
. In addition,
|\rho(\mathbf{x}_{0})-1|\geq\epsilon_{0}>0
at any vertex
\mathbf{x}_{0}
of
\Omega
.

Definition 1.2
.

We say
\rho
is called an admissible density function if for a time-harmonic plane incident wave
\mathbf{u}^{i}
of the form (
1.1
), the forward scattering system (
1.1
)–(
1.5
) admits a unique solution
\mathbf{u}\in H_{loc}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{n}
such that the scattered wave
\mathbf{u}^{s}=\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{i}
is the radiating solution and
\|\mathbf{u}^{s}\|_{H^{2}(B_{2R})^{n}}\leq\mathcal{N}
, where
\mathcal{N}
is an a-priori positive constant and
\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x})\neq 0
in
S_{\Omega}\cup B_{R}\backslash\overline{\Omega}
, where
S_{\Omega}
denotes the set of vertices of
\Omega
.

In what follows,
(\Omega,\rho)
is said to be an admissible polyhedral scatterer if it fulfils the conditions in Definitions
1.1
and
1.2
. The parameters
\{R,\mathcal{N},l_{0},\epsilon_{0},\alpha_{m},\alpha_{M}\}
in the above admissibility definitions are referred to as the a-priori parameters. It is remarked that the admissibility requirements in Definitions
1.1
and
1.2
will be needed in our stability study and can be fulfilled in certain general scenarios, which shall become clearer in our subsequent discussion.

Next, we present the logarithmic stability estimate for the inverse problem (
1.7
) on the shape determination of the elastic medium scatterer by a single far-field pattern.
Henceforth, we define the Hausdorff distance of two medium scatterers
(\Omega;\rho)
and
(\Omega^{\prime};\rho^{\prime})
as follows,

(1.8)

d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})=\max\left\{\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega}\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},\,\Omega^{\prime}),\,\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\Omega^{\prime}}\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},\,\Omega)\right\}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\Omega;\rho)
and
(\Omega^{\prime};\rho^{\prime})
be two admissible polyhedral scatterers. Let
\mathbf{u}^{i}
be a common time-harmonic plane incident wave of the form (
1.1
). Assume that
\mathbf{U}
and
\mathbf{U}^{\prime}
are the far-field patterns of the scattered waves
\mathbf{u}^{s}
and
\mathbf{u}^{\prime s}
by the medium scatterers
(\Omega;\rho)
and
(\Omega^{\prime};\rho^{\prime})
, respectively. For sufficiently small
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, if

\|\mathbf{U}-\mathbf{U}^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n})}\leq\varepsilon,

then

d_{\mathcal{H}}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})\leq C(\ln\ln(\mathcal{N}/\varepsilon))^{-\gamma},

where
\mathcal{N}
is given in Definition
1.2
, and
C
and
\gamma
are positive constants, depending only on the a-priori parameters involved in Definitions
1.1
and
1.2
as well as the Lamé constants
\lambda,\mu
.

The argument in proving Theorem
1.1
is localized around a corner of the underlying polyhedral scatterer. As an interesting byproduct, we can establish another stability result where the medium scatterer is not necessarily polyhedral as long as it possesses a corner. The full technical details of the result will be given in Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
, and we only provide a rough summary in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Consider the scattering problem (
1.1
)–(
1.5
) associated with a general medium scatterer
(\Omega,\rho)
, which is not necessarily polyhedral. Suppose that
\partial\Omega
possesses a corner as described in Theorem
5.2
. Then it holds that

(1.9)

\|\mathbf{U}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n})}\geq C,

where
C
is a positive constant depending on a certain set of a-priori parameters.

Theorem
1.2
indicates that for a general medium scatterer, if it possesses a corner, then it generically scatters every incident field stably, i.e. invisibility phenomenon cannot occur.

2.
Propagation of smallness from far-field to boundary

The main goal of this section is to show how the smallness from the far-field pattern propagates to the boundary of the elastic medium scatterer, which is a key ingredient in the stability proof of Theorem
1.1
. Our argument follows the general strategy developed in
[
5
]
for the acoustic scattering. Throughout the rest of the paper, we let
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}
, respectively, denote the total wave fields corresponding to the scatterers
(\Omega;\rho)
and
(\Omega^{\prime};\rho^{\prime})
in Theorem
1.1
.

2.1.
Stability Estimates: from Far-field to Near-field

In this subsection, our aim is to estimate the difference of
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{u}^{\prime}
in
B_{2R}\backslash B_{R}
close to the convex hull of
\Omega
and
\Omega^{\prime}
.
To begin with, we need to generalise Theorem 4.1 in
[
33
]
and Proposition 5.2 in
[
5
]
to the elastic inhomogeneous medium scatterer case.

Lemma 2.1
.

Fix
t\in(1,2]
and
\mathcal{T}>0
. Let
\mathbf{u}\in H^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{n}
be a solution to the Navier equation (
1.2
) in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\overline{B_{R}}
with
\rho=1
, which satisfies the Kupradze radiation condition. Let
\mathbf{U}
be the far field pattern of
\mathbf{u}
with the norm
\varepsilon=\|\mathbf{U}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n})}
. Assume that
\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R}\backslash B_{R})^{n}}\leq\mathcal{T},\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s}
, where
\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}
and
\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{s}}
are the longitudinal and the transversal parts of
\mathbf{u}
, respectively. Then there exists a constant
C=C(\kappa_{\mathrm{p}},\kappa_{\mathrm{s}},\mathcal{T},n,R,t)>0
such that the following stability estimate holds for
\varepsilon\leq\frac{\mathcal{T}}{C}
:

(2.1)

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2tR}\backslash B_{tR})^{n}}\leq Ce^{-\hat{c}\sqrt{\ln\frac{\mathcal{T}}{\varepsilon}}},

where
\kappa=\min\{\kappa_{\mathrm{p}},\kappa_{\mathrm{s}}\}
and
\hat{c}\leq\ln t\sqrt{\frac{e\kappa R}{2}}
.

Proof.

Let
u^{j}
and
u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}
denote the
j
-th components of
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}
, respectively, where

\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s}
. From (
1.3
) and (
1.4
), we obtain that
u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}
satisfies the Helmoltz equation

(\Delta+\kappa^{2}_{\mathrm{\alpha}})u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}=0,\quad j=1,2,\ldots,n.

Then by
[
5
, Proposition 5.2]
, we know that if
\varepsilon\leq\frac{\mathcal{T}}{C_{\mathrm{\alpha}}}
, then

\|u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2tR}\backslash B_{tR})}\leq C_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\mathcal{T}e^{-\hat{c}\sqrt{\ln\frac{\mathcal{T}}{\varepsilon}}},

where
C_{\mathrm{\alpha}}=C_{\mathrm{\alpha}}(\kappa_{\mathrm{\alpha}},n,R,t)>0
,
\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s}
,
\hat{c}\leq\ln t\sqrt{\frac{e\kappa R}{2}}
and
\kappa=\min\{\kappa_{\mathrm{p}},\kappa_{\mathrm{s}}\}
.

When
\varepsilon\leq\frac{\mathcal{T}}{\sqrt{2n\,(C^{2}_{\mathrm{p}}+C^{2}_{\mathrm{s}})}}
, we can derive

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2tR}\backslash B_{tR})^{n}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|u^{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{2tR}\backslash B_{tR})}}\leq\sqrt{\sum_{\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{2tR}\backslash B_{tR})}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{2n\,(C^{2}_{\mathrm{p}}+C^{2}_{\mathrm{s}})}\,e^{-\hat{c}\sqrt{\ln\frac{\mathcal{T}}{\varepsilon}}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

With the help of elliptic interior regularity, we can derive the following stability estimate of the near-fields in
B_{2R}\backslash\overline{B_{R}}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Fix
t\in(1,2]
and
\mathcal{T}>0
. Assume that
\mathbf{u}\in H^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{n}
satisfies the Navier equation
(\Delta^{*}+\omega^{2})\mathbf{u}=\bf 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\overline{B_{R}}
and the Kupradze radiation condition. Let
\mathbf{U}
be the far field pattern of
\mathbf{u}
with the norm
\varepsilon=\|\mathbf{U}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{C}^{n})}
. Assume that
\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R}\backslash B_{R})^{n}}\leq\mathcal{T},\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s}
, where
\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}
and
\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{s}}
are the longitudinal and the transversal parts of
\mathbf{u}
, respectively. Let
K\subset B_{2R}\backslash\overline{B_{tR}}
be a domain. Then there exists a positive constant
C=C(\kappa_{\mathrm{p}},\kappa_{\mathrm{s}},n,R,t)
such that the following stability estimate holds for
\varepsilon\leq\frac{\mathcal{T}}{C}
:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{r}(K)^{n}}\leq C\mathcal{T}e^{-\hat{c}\sqrt{\ln\frac{\mathcal{T}}{\varepsilon}}},

where
r\in\mathbb{N}
is a smoothness index,
\kappa=\min\{\kappa_{\mathrm{p}},\kappa_{\mathrm{s}}\}
and
\hat{c}\leq\ln t\sqrt{\frac{e\kappa R}{2}}
.

Proof.

Note that
\mathbf{u}
is the superposition of
\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}
and
\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{s}}
,
which are vector-valued weak solutions to the Helmholtz equation with wave numbers
\kappa_{\mathrm{p}}
and
\kappa_{\mathrm{s}}
, respectively.
We use
u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}
to denote the
j
-th component of
\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}
, where
\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s}
.
Given two domains
\tilde{K}
and
K^{\prime}
with
\tilde{K}\subset K^{\prime}\subset B_{2R}\backslash\overline{B_{tR}}
and
\mathrm{dist}(\partial\tilde{K},\partial K^{\prime})>0
. For any
s\in\mathbb{R}
and the smooth cutoff function
\tilde{\phi}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(K)
with
\tilde{\phi}\equiv 1
in
\tilde{K}
,
it is not difficult to prove the two subsequent properties:

\|u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{H^{s+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|(1+\kappa^{2}_{\mathrm{\alpha}})u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}-(\Delta+\kappa^{2}_{\mathrm{\alpha}})u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

and

\|(\Delta+\kappa^{2}_{\mathrm{\alpha}})(\tilde{\phi}\,u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}})\|_{H^{s+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|2\,\nabla\tilde{\phi}\cdot\nabla u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}+u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\Delta\phi\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{H^{s+1}(\Omega)}.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|^{2}_{H^{s+2}(\tilde{K})^{n}}

\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}+\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{s}}\|^{2}_{H^{s+2}(\tilde{K})^{n}}\leq\sum_{\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s}}\sum^{n}_{j=1}\|u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|^{2}_{H^{s+2}(\tilde{K})}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s}}\sum^{n}_{j=1}\|\tilde{\phi}\,u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|^{2}_{H^{s+2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\sum_{\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s}}\sum^{n}_{j=1}C^{2}_{\kappa_{\mathrm{\alpha}},\tilde{\phi}}\,\|u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|^{2}_{H^{s}(K^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\kappa_{\mathrm{p}},\kappa_{\mathrm{s}},n,\tilde{\phi}}^{2}\,\|\mathbf{u}\|^{2}_{H^{s+1}(K^{\prime})^{n}},

which implies that

(2.3)

\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{s+2}(\tilde{K})^{n}}\leq C_{\kappa_{\mathrm{p}},\kappa_{\mathrm{p}},n,\tilde{\phi}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{s+1}(K^{\prime})^{n}}.

By fixing
r\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a subdomain sequence
\{K_{j},\phi_{j}\}^{r}_{j=0}
such that

\begin{cases}&K_{j}\subset K_{j-1},\,\,\mathrm{dist}(\partial K_{j},\partial K_{j-1})>0,\\
&\phi_{j}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(K_{j-1}),\quad\phi_{j}\equiv 1\quad\mathrm{in}\,\,K_{j},\\
&K_{0}=B_{2R}\backslash\overline{B_{tR}},\quad\,K_{r}=K.\end{cases}

By using (
2.3
) and Lemma
2.1
repeatedly, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{r}(K)^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\kappa_{\mathrm{p}},\kappa_{\mathrm{s}},\phi_{r}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{H^{r-1}(K)^{n}}\leq\cdots\leq C_{\kappa_{\mathrm{p}},\kappa_{\mathrm{s}},\phi_{r},\ldots,\phi_{1}}\|\mathbf{u}\|_{L^{2}(K)^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\kappa_{\mathrm{p}},\kappa_{\mathrm{s}},\phi_{r},\ldots,\phi_{1}}\mathcal{T}e^{-\hat{c}\sqrt{\ln(\mathcal{T}/\varepsilon)}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.2

[

]

There exist positive constants
\hat{R}
,
C
and

\kappa
only. Let

\frac{c\log\frac{r_{3}}{s}}{\log\frac{r_{3}}{r_{1}}}\leq\beta\leq 1-\frac{c\log\frac{s}{r_{1}}}{\log\frac{r_{3}}{r_{1}}}.

From now on, we fix

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded connected domain and
\Gamma\subset V
be a rectifiable curve with endpoints
\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
. Fix positive constants
\mathcal{T}
and
\hat{R}
such that

and
\mathcal{T}\geq 1
. Let
B(\Gamma,4r)=\bigcup_{\mathbf{y}\in\Gamma}B_{4r}(\mathbf{y})\subset V
. Moreover, we assume that
\mathbf{u}\in L^{\infty}(V)^{n}
satisfies

\begin{cases}&(\Delta^{*}+\omega^{2})\mathbf{u}={\bf 0},\quad\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{\infty}(V)^{n}}\leq\mathcal{T},\,\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s},\\
&\max\big\{\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(\mathbf{x}))^{n}},\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{s}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(\mathbf{x}))^{n}}\big\}\leq 1,\end{cases}

where
\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}
and
\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{s}}
represent the longitudinal and the transversal parts of
\mathbf{u}
, respectively. Then the following result holds

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))^{n}}\leq C\mathcal{T}\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|^{\beta^{\frac{d_{\Gamma}}{r}+1}}_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(\mathbf{y}))^{n}},\quad\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s},

where
d_{\Gamma}
is the distance measured along
\Gamma
.

Proof.

Let
N=\lceil d_{\Gamma}/r\rceil
. There exists a sequence of balls, each of radius
r
and centred respectively at
\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2},\cdots,\mathbf{x}_{N+1}=\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
, such that
\mathbf{x}_{k}\in\Gamma
and
|\mathbf{x}_{k+1}-\mathbf{x}_{k}|\leq d_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{x}_{k+1},\mathbf{x}_{k})\leq r
, where
d_{\Gamma}(\mathbf{x}_{k+1},\mathbf{x}_{k})
signifies the length along the curve
\Gamma
between the points
\mathbf{x}_{k}
and
\mathbf{x}_{k+1}
. Clearly,
B_{r}(\mathbf{x}_{k})\subset B_{2r}(\mathbf{x}_{k-1})
. Let

denote the

\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}
,
\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{n}_{j=1}\|u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))}\leq\sum^{n}_{j=1}\Big\{C_{1}\mathcal{T}^{1-\beta}\|u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(\mathbf{x}_{N}))}\Big\}

\displaystyle\leq\cdots

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{n}_{j=1}\Big\{C_{N}\mathcal{T}^{(1-\beta)(1+\beta+\cdots+\beta^{N-1})}\|u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|^{\beta^{N}}_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(\mathbf{x}))}\Big\}.

Note that
1+\beta+\cdots+\beta^{N}\leq 1/(1-\beta)
and
N\leq d_{\Gamma}/r+1
, hence we have the following claim

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))^{n}}\leq C\mathcal{T}\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|^{\beta^{N}}_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(\mathbf{x}))^{n}}\leq C\mathcal{T}\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|^{\beta^{\frac{d_{\gamma}}{r}+1}}_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(\mathbf{x}))^{n}},\quad\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s}.

The proof is complete.
∎

We now give the stability estimations of
\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}
and
\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{s}}
near the scatterer
(\Omega;\rho)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Given positive constants
\mathcal{T},\hat{R}
and
\theta,\beta\in(0,1)
such that
\mathcal{T}\geq 1
.
Let
Q\subset B_{R}
be a convex polytope. Let
\mathbf{u}\in H^{2}_{loc}(B_{2R})^{n}
satisfy the Navier equation
\Delta^{*}\mathbf{u}+\omega^{2}\mathbf{u}=\bf 0
in
B_{2R}\backslash\overline{Q}
. Moreover, we assume that

\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{C^{\theta}\big(\overline{B_{3/2R}}\big)^{n}}\leq\mathcal{T},\quad\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{7R/4}\backslash B_{5R/4})^{n}}\leq\delta<1,\,\,\alpha=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s},

where
\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}
and
\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{s}}
are the longitudinal and the transversal parts of
\mathbf{u}
, respectively. Let

(2.4)

\delta<\Big[\exp\exp\Big\{\frac{4\,r\ln|\ln\beta|}{\min\{\hat{R},R/2\}}\Big\}\Big]^{-1},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{\prime})^{n}}\leq M(\ln|\ln\delta|)^{-\theta}\mathcal{T},\quad\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s}.

Here
Q^{\prime}=\big\{\mathbf{x}\in B_{3/2R}\,\big|\,\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial Q)\leq 4\,r\big\}
and
M=M(\omega,R,\theta,\beta)>1
.

Proof.

Let
Q_{1}=\big\{\mathbf{x}\in B_{2R}\,\big|\,\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},\partial Q)<4\,r\big\}
. It is easy to reduce that
4\,r<\hat{R}
and
2\,r<\frac{R}{4}
from the upper bound on
\delta
and the expression of
r
. For any point
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in B_{2R}\backslash\overline{Q_{1}}
, there always exists a ray from
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
into
B_{2R}\backslash B_{5/4R}
.
We consider a special segment of the ray with endpoints
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{y}

\mathbf{y}\in B_{7/4R}\backslash B_{5/4R}
and
\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{y},\partial B_{5/4R})=r
. One can see that
\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(\mathbf{y}))^{n}}\leq\delta,\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s}
. The length of that segment is smaller than
\frac{5R}{2}+r
. By Lemma
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))^{n}}\leq C\mathcal{T}\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|^{\beta^{\frac{5R}{2\,r}+2}}_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(\mathbf{y}))^{n}}\leq C\mathcal{T}\delta^{\beta^{\frac{5R}{2r}+2}},\quad\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s}.

Then

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2R}\backslash Q_{1})^{n}}\leq C^{\prime}\mathcal{T}\delta^{\beta^{\frac{5R}{2r}+2}},\quad\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s}.

For any point
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in Q_{1}
, there must be
\mathbf{y}\in\partial Q
such that
|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}-\mathbf{y}|\leq 4\,r
. By the convexity of
Q
, there exists
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash Q
such that
\mathrm{dist}(\mathbf{x},Q)=|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|=4\,r
. The upper bound of

implies

, and thus

|\mathbf{x}|\leq|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}|+|\mathbf{y}|\leq 4\,r+R\leq\frac{3R}{2}.

Moreover,
|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}-\mathbf{x}|\leq|\mathbf{x}^{\prime}-\mathbf{y}|+|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}|\leq 8\,r
.
From the
\theta

\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}
and proposition 5.7 in
[

]
, we can obtain that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{n}_{j=1}\|u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\leq\sum^{n}_{j=1}\Big(\|u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{\mathbf{C}^{\theta}(B_{3/2R})}|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}|^{\theta}+\|u^{j}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})}\Big)

(2.6)

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{n}_{j=1}C\mathcal{T}\Big((8\,r)^{\theta}+\delta^{\beta^{\frac{5R}{2\,r}+2}}\Big)\leq\widetilde{C}\mathcal{T}\Big((8\,r)^{\theta}+\delta^{\beta^{\frac{5R}{2\,r}+2}}\Big),

where
\widetilde{C}
is a positive constant depending on

and

Combining (
2.4

(2.7)

\displaystyle r^{\theta}=\frac{(9R|\ln\beta|)^{\theta}}{((4-4\theta)\ln|\ln\delta|)^{\theta}},\quad\quad\frac{5R}{2\,r}=\frac{10(1-\theta)\ln|\ln\delta|}{9|\ln\beta|},

and

\displaystyle\delta^{\beta^{\frac{5R}{2\,r}+2}}

\displaystyle=e^{-|\ln\delta|\beta^{\frac{5R}{2\,r}+2}}=e^{-\beta^{2}|\ln\delta|^{\frac{10\theta-1}{9}}}\leq e^{-\beta^{2}|\ln\delta|^{\theta}}

(2.8)

\displaystyle\leq\beta^{-2}(|\ln\delta|)^{-\theta}\,\leq\,\beta^{-2}(\ln|\ln\delta|)^{-\theta}.

Therefore, it yields that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{\prime})^{n}}\leq\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q_{1})^{n}}\leq M(\ln|\ln\delta|)^{-\theta}\mathcal{T},\quad\mathrm{\alpha}=\mathrm{p},\mathrm{s},

where

The proof is complete.

\displaystyle\sup_{\partial Q}\big(|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{u}^{\prime}|+|\nabla\mathbf{u}-\nabla\mathbf{u}^{\prime}|\big)\leq C\left(\ln\ln(\mathcal{N}/\varepsilon)\right)^{-1/2},

and

Proof.

Denote

\displaystyle\|\nabla\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)^{n}}\leq\|\mathbf{v}\|_{H^{2}(\Omega)^{n}}\leq C_{1}\mathcal{N}e^{-c_{0}\sqrt{\ln\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\varepsilon_{0}}}}\leq C_{1}\mathcal{N}e^{-c_{0}\sqrt{\ln\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\varepsilon}}},

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{C^{1,\frac{1}{2}}(\overline{B_{3/2R}})^{n}}\leq C_{\mathrm{\alpha}}(1+\|\rho\|_{C^{\frac{1}{2}}}(B_{3/2R})^{n})\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{\alpha}}\|_{H^{2}(B_{2R})^{n}}\leq C_{\mathrm{\alpha}}(1+\mathcal{M})(\mathcal{P}+\mathcal{N}),

and

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q^{\prime})^{n}}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{C_{\mathrm{p}},C_{\mathrm{s}}\}(1+\mathcal{M})(2\mathcal{P}+\mathcal{N})\big(\ln\ln(\mathcal{N}/\varepsilon)^{\frac{1}{4}}\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

(2.10)

(III)

Case 3: If

Similar to the above two cases, let us consider the following two situations. If
\Re\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\in\Pi
(including
\Re\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\mathrel{/\mkern-5.0mu/}\Pi

\mathbf{p}^{\perp}\in\Pi

fulfilling the following inequality as

\Re\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\cdot\mathbf{p}^{\perp}\geq 0.

By the same deduction for Case 1, we can also obtain the inequality (
(I)

The proof is complete.

\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{s}}\|_{H^{2}(B_{2R})^{n}}+\|\mathbf{u}_{\mathrm{p}}\|_{H^{2}(B_{2R})^{n}}+\|\mathbf{u}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{s}}\|_{H^{2}(B_{2R})^{n}}+\|\mathbf{u}^{\prime}_{\mathrm{p}}\|_{H^{2}(B_{2R})^{n}}.

Finally, it is easy to see that

(3.29)

C\big|(\rho(\mathbf{x}_{0})-1)\int_{\mathcal{A}}e^{\xi\cdot(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{0})}\eta\cdot\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x}_{0})\mathrm{dx}\big|\leq R_{1}+R_{2}+R_{3}+I_{1}+I_{2},

where

. Substituting (
3.23a
) to (
3.23d
), (
3.27
) and (
3
) into (

), we prove (

).
∎

Theorem 5.1

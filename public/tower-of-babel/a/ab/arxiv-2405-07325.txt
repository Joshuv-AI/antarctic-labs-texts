On the distance problem over finite p-adic rings
Thang Pham, Boqing Xue

Abstract
In this paper, we study the distance problem in the setting of finite p-adic rings. In odd dimensions, our results are essentially sharp. In even dimensions, we clarify the conjecture and provide examples to support it. Surprisingly, compared to the finite field case, in this setting, we are able to provide a large family of sets such that the distance conjecture holds. By developing new restriction type estimates associated to circles and orbits, with a group theoretic argument, we will prove the 4 / 3 4/3 -parallel result in the two dimensions. This answers a question raised by Alex Iosevich. In a more general scenario, the existence/distribution of geometric/graph configurations will be also considered in this paper. The main results present improvements and extensions of the recent work due to Ben Lichtin (2019, 2023). In comparison with Lichtin’s method, our approach is much simpler and flexible, which is also one of the novelties in this paper.



1
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime,
r
be a positive integer, and
\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z}
be a finite
p
-adic ring. For a postive integer
n
, the
distance function
between two points
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
and
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})
in
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
,
denoted by
||\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}||:=F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
, is given by

F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=(x_{1}-y_{1})^{2}+\cdots+(x_{n}-y_{n})^{2}\quad(\textsf{mod }p^{r}).

Given subsets
E_{1},E_{2}\subset(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
, the distance set determined by points in
E_{1}\times E_{2}
is denoted by
\Delta_{n,r}(E_{1},E_{2})
, i.e.

\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Delta_{n,r}(E_{1},E_{2})=\{F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}):\,\mathbf{x}\in E_{1},\,\mathbf{y}\in E_{2}\}.

For simplicity, we write
\Delta_{n,r}(E)=\Delta_{n,r}(E,E)
. We also denote the density by
\delta_{E_{1},E_{2}}:=\frac{\sqrt{|E_{1}||E_{2}|}}{p^{rn}}
and
\delta_{E}:=\frac{|E|}{p^{rn}}
.

In the setting of finite
p
-adic rings, the Erdős–Falconer distance problem is stated as follows.

Question 1.1
.

What is the smallest density threshold
\delta\in(0,1)
independent of
r
such that
|\Delta_{n,r}(E)|\gg p^{r}
whenever
E
is a subset of
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
with
\delta_{E}\geq\delta
?

This problem was initially studied
with the distance function
in the case
r=1
, i.e. over finite fields, due to Iosevich and Rudnev
[
7
]
. More precisely, they proved that if
|E|\geq Cp^{\frac{n+1}{2}}
for some sufficient large constant
C
, then the distance set
\Delta_{n,1}(E)
covers the whole field. Hart, Iosevich, Koh, and Rudnev
[
4
]
indicated that the exponent
\frac{n+1}{2}
is sharp in odd dimensions. In even dimensions, it is conjectured that the right exponent should be
n/2
. In two dimensions, Chapman, Erdogan, Hart, Iosevich, and Koh
[
2
]
proved the exponent
4/3
by using an extension theorem associated to circles in the plane. This result was recently improved to
5/4
by Murphy, Petridis, the first listed author, Rudnev, and Stevens in
[
21
]
by using algebraic methods and results from incidence geometry.

When
r>1
, by extending the techniques from finite fields, Covert, Iosevich and Pakianathan
[
1
]
proved that
U_{r}\subset\Delta_{n,r}(E)
whenever
\delta_{E}\gg r(r+1)p^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}
, where
U_{r}:=(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{\ast}
is the set of units. This result is only non-trivial when
r
is bounded, and does not offer a uniform density independent of
r
, to Question
1.1
. By using a different and sophisticated approach, namely, a combination of
p
-adic analysis and estimates for a class of exponential sums mod
p
, Lichtin
[
16
]
proved that
U_{r}\subset\Delta_{n,r}(E)
if
\delta_{E}\gg p^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}
. As mentioned in his paper, the main advantage of his approach is that the argument detects nontrivial cancellations within certain exponential sums mod
p^{r}
which were not used in the work of Covert et al.
[
1
]
.

In this paper, the first purpose is to study the distance problem in a general setting when the distance function is replaced by
a more general polynomial
. In the case of the usual distance function, one of the novelties of this paper is to present a simpler and flexible approach than that of Lichtin. Our results are essentially sharp in odd dimensions. The second purpose of this paper is to clarify the conjecture in even dimensions and provide examples to support it. In two dimensions, by developing new restriction type estimates associated to circles and orbits, with a group theoretic argument, we will prove the
4/3
-parallel result. Surprisingly, in comparison with the finite field case, in this setting, we are able to provide a large family of sets such that the distance conjecture holds. The third purpose is to study the existence/distribution of geometric/graph configurations. To be precise, we are interested in the following configuration-type questions.

Question 1.2
.

Let
H
be a given graph,
E
be any subset of
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
and
j
be any element of
U_{r}
. What is the smallest density threshold
\delta\in(0,1)
independent of
r
such that
E
contains a copy of
H
at distance
j
whenever
\delta_{E}\gg\delta
?

Question 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a given geometric configuration and
E
be any set in
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
. What is the smallest density threshold
\delta\in(0,1)
not depending on
r
such that
E
contains a copy of
\mathcal{C}
whenever
\delta_{E}\gg\delta
?

This paper addresses the graphs of being cycles, chains, and trees, and a geometric configuration of rectangles. In contrast to initial methods/results over finite fields, the main challenge one has to deal with in this setting is to find an effective approach to obtain a uniform density which does not depend on
r
. Lichtin’s method in
[
16
]
is based on a combination of
p
-adic analysis and estimates for a class of exponential sums mod
p
. To find cancellations in exponential sums, he investigated paths of points (descending family of neighbourhoods), and his arguments are heavily relied on computing Hessian matrix of all levels. The approach we introduce in this paper is simpler, which involves polynomial congruences and Fourier analysis in finite rings. Our arguments only rely on the
first order Jacobian matrix
, and avoid the
second order Hessian matrix
.

1.1
Results on the generalized distance sets

For a polynomial
F(\mathbf{x})\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{x}]
in
n
variables, we denote
F_{i}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}(\mathbf{x})
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, and denote
(\nabla F)(\mathbf{x})=(F_{1}(\mathbf{x}),F_{2}(\mathbf{x}),\ldots,F_{n}(\mathbf{x}))
. Our first result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
F(\mathbf{x})
be a given polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{x}]
in
n\geq 2
variables. Suppose that the following conditions hold for some positive constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
with
c_{1}<1
, for some prime
p
, and for some
j\in\mathbb{Z}
with
(j,p)=1
:

(i)
(\nabla F)(\mathbf{x})\not\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p)
when
F(\mathbf{x})\equiv j\,(\textsf{mod }p)
;

(ii)
\big|\#\{\mathbf{x}\,(\textsf{mod }p):\,F(\mathbf{x})\equiv j\,(\textsf{mod }p)\}-p^{n-1}\big|\leq c_{1}p^{n-1}
;

(iii) When
\mathbf{m}\not\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p)
,

\Big|\sum\limits_{\mathbf{x}\,(\textsf{mod }p)\atop F(\mathbf{x})\equiv j\,(\textsf{mod }p)}\,\,e_{p}\left(-\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{x}\right)\Big|\leq c_{2}p^{\frac{n-1}{2}};

(iv) When
\mathbf{m}\not\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p)
,

\displaystyle\#\big\{\mathbf{x}(\textsf{mod }p):\,

\displaystyle F(\mathbf{x})\equiv j\,(\textsf{mod }p),\,\exists~1\leq t\leq n,\,\,s.t.~F_{t}(\mathbf{x})\not\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p),

\displaystyle m_{t}\not\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p),m_{i}F_{t}(\mathbf{x})\equiv m_{t}F_{i}(\mathbf{x})\,(\textsf{mod }p)\,(1\leq i\leq n)\big\}\leq c_{3}p^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.

Let
r
be any positive integer and
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
be subsets of
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
. Assume that

\delta_{E_{1},E_{2}}\,>\,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}Cp^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}},

where
C=(1-c_{1})^{-1}\max\{c_{2},c_{3}\}
. Then

\#\{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in E_{1}\times E_{2}:\,F(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})\equiv j\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})\}>0.

Remark 1.1
.

In applications, the constants
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
depend only on the polynomial
F
(degree, coefficients, number of variables), and the prime
p
needs to be sufficiently large compared to the degree and the coefficients of
F
. So the result is uniform in all natural numbers
r\geq 1
and all integers
j
with
(j,p)=1
. Here and throughout, by depending on
F
we mean in terms of the degree, coefficients, and number of variables.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
F(\mathbf{x})=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}^{k}
be a polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{x}]
with
n\geq 2
,
k\geq 2
, and
a_{i}\neq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Then, for any sufficiently large prime
p
and the density threshold at least
Cp^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}
, the conclusion of Theorem
1.4
holds for all integers
r,j
with
r\geq 1
and
(j,p)=1
, and with the constant
C
depending only on
F
.

It also worth noting that when
k=3
and
r=1
, Corollary
1.5
recovers a result established by Iosevich and Koh in
[
6
]
. Moreover, Example
8.1
in the last section will show that this distance result is sharp in odd dimensions.

We mention that condition (iii) in Theorem
1.4
is equivalent to

\Big|\sum\limits_{\mathbf{x}\,(\textsf{mod }p)}\sum\limits_{s\not\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p)}\,\,e_{p}\left(-\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{x}+sF(\mathbf{x})-sj\right)\Big|\ll p^{\frac{n+1}{2}}

for
\mathbf{m}\not\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p)
. However, the above bound is not applicable to polynomials of the form
F(\mathbf{x})=\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}x_{j}^{k_{j}}
with distinct exponents. In
[
11
]
, Koh and Shen worked with a weaker exponential sum

\Big|\sum\limits_{\mathbf{x}\,(\textsf{mod }p)}\,\,e_{p}\left(-\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{x}+sF(\mathbf{x})\right)\Big|\ll p^{\frac{n}{2}}

for
\mathbf{m}\not\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p)
and
s\not\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p)
. And they proved that
|\Delta_{n,1}(E_{1},E_{2})|\gg p
whenever
E_{1},E_{2}
are subsets of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
satisfying

\delta_{E_{1},E_{2}}\gg p^{-(n-1)/2}.

(1)

We observe that the approach in
[
11
]
will not lead to
result which is uniform in
r
. With the method developed in this paper, we are able to provide a generalization of the above result in the setting of finite
p
-adic rings.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
F(\mathbf{x})=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}^{k_{i}}
be a polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{x}]
with
n\geq 2
,
k_{i}\geq 2
and
a_{i}\neq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Denote
k_{\ast}=\min\nolimits_{1\leq i\leq n}k_{i}
. Then, for any sufficiently large prime
p
, for any natural number
r\geq 1
, and any subsets
E_{1},E_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z}
, we have

|\Delta_{n,r}(E_{1},E_{2})|\gg\min\left\{p^{r},\,\frac{|E_{1}||E_{2}|}{p^{r(2n-2/k_{\ast})-n+1}}\right\}.

In particular, one has
|\Delta_{n,r}(E_{1},E_{2})|\gg p^{r}
if

\delta_{E_{1},E_{2}}\gg p^{r\big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{k_{\ast}}\big)-\frac{n-1}{2}}.

(2)

Here, the implied constants depend only on
F
.

If
k_{\ast}=2
, then we can see that the lower bound of the density is independent of
r
. However, if
k_{\ast}\geq 3
, then the formula (
2
) is meaningful only when
r\leq\frac{k_{\ast}(n-1)}{k_{\ast}-2}
.

1.2
The conjecture in even dimensions

In even dimensions, one might ask about the conjectured densities to guarantee that
U_{r}\subset\Delta_{n,r}(E)
or
|\Delta_{n,r}(E)|\gg p^{r}
. In this paper, we propose the following.

Conjecture 1.7
.

Let
F(\mathbf{x})=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}x_{i}^{k}
be a polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{x}]
with
n\geq 2
even,
k\geq 2
, and
a_{i}\neq 0
for all
1\leq i\leq n
. Then, for any sufficiently large prime
p
, if
r
is a positive integer and
E_{1},E_{2}
are subsets of
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
with
the density
\delta_{E_{1},E_{2}}\gg p^{-\frac{n}{2}}
, we have
|\Delta_{n,r}(E_{1},E_{2})|\gg p^{r}
.

Example
8.2
in the last section will support this conjecture for the case
k=2
.

In the setting of finite fields, i.e.
r=1
, as mentioned above, Chapman, Erdogan, Hart, Iosevich, and Koh
[
2
]
proved the exponent
4/3
by using an extension theorem associated to circles in the plane. Another proof by using geometric properties of rigid-motions in the plane can be found in
[
5
]
. Alex Iosevich asked in several conferences/workshops if the
4/3
-parallel result exists
in the setting of finite p-adic rings. In this paper, we give an affirmative answer to his question.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
F
be the distance function,
p
be an odd prime, and
r\geq 1
be an integer.
Let
E\subset(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
with
\delta_{E}\gg p^{-\frac{2}{3}}
. Then
|\Delta_{2,r}(E)|\gg p^{r}
. Here the implied constant is independent of
r
.

Compared to Theorem
1.4
, we have a smaller threshold density, namely, of
p^{-\frac{2}{3}}
. Regarding the proof of Theorem
1.8
, it is challenging if we just want to follow the methods in
[
2
]
or in
[
5
]
. On the one hand, in a recent exposition, Liao showed that the method in
[
5
]
implies too many degenerate cases, even with
r=2
, which are very hard to deal with. On the other hand, a direct computation shows that the same happens with the approach developed in
[
2
]
, namely, the main difficulty arises when finding the explicit form of the sum
\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{1_{C_{r,j}}}(\mathbf{m})\widehat{1_{C_{r,j}}}(\mathbf{m}^{\prime})
for
\mathbf{m}
and
\mathbf{m}^{\prime}
in
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
, where
C_{r,j}
is the circle centered at the origin of radius
j
.

In this paper, to prove Theorem
1.8
, we first use a group theoretic argument, which we learned from
[
20
, Appendix]
, to reduce the theorem to an extension type question for circles, then the rest is devoted to study such type estimate in the p-adic setting. The extension theorems, we obtain in Section 4, are of independent interest and are expected to have many other applications.

Unlike the finite field case, in finite p-adic rings, we are able to provide a large family of sets
E
such that Conjecture
1.7
is true, namely, the family of sets
E
satisfying the property that the density of the fibre of the natural projection over each element in
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{2}
is not too large in
E
. The precise statement reads as follows.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
F
be the distance function,
p
be an odd prime, and
r\geq 2
be an integer.
Let
E\subset(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
with
\delta_{E}\gg p^{-1}
. Assume that

\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\#\{(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2})\in E^{2}:\,\mathbf{x}_{1}\equiv\mathbf{x}_{2}\,(\textsf{mod }p)\}\ll p^{2r-\frac{7}{3}}|E|.

Then
|\Delta_{2,r}(E)|\gg p^{r}
.

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
F
be the distance function,
p
be an odd prime, and
r\geq 2
be an integer.
Let
E\subset(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
with
\delta_{E}\gg p^{-1}
. Assume that

\#\{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in E\colon\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\equiv\mathbf{x}\,(\textsf{mod }p)\}\ll p^{2r-\frac{7}{3}}

for each
\mathbf{x}\in E
. Then
|\Delta_{2,r}(E)|\gg p^{r}
.

The implied constants in
Theorem
1.9
and Corollary
1.10
are independent of
r
.

In addition to the restriction-theoretic approach developed above, the paper also introduces
an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
–to–
\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z}
transfer
principle for the standard quadratic distance in arbitrary dimensions. This principle allows us to lift results on the number of isosceles triangles from
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
to
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
. As a consequence, we obtain the following results by using the currently available estimates in dimensions two and four from the literature.

1.3
Results on geometric/graph configurations

Let
F(\mathbf{x})
be a given polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[\mathbf{x}]
in
n\geq 2
variables. We call
F(\mathbf{x})

good
if it satisfies the conditions of Theorem
1.4
. In the following, we prove several extensions of the distance result in the setting of geometric/graph configurations in which the polynomials
F(\mathbf{x})
are assumed to be good.

The first result is on the existence of rectangles of given side-length
j\in U_{r}
, which is an extension of an earlier result due to Lyall and Magyar in
[
18
]
.

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
F
be a good polynomial and
0<\delta^{\prime}<\delta<1
be given numbers. There exists
\epsilon>0
such that the following holds. For any odd prime
p
, any positive integer
r
, and any integer
j
with
(j,p)=1
, if
E\subset(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2n}
, with
p^{-\frac{2n-1}{2}}\leq\epsilon
, satisfies
\delta_{E}\geq\delta
, then
E
contains at least
\delta^{\prime}p^{4rn-2}
tuples

(\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{1}),~(\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{2}),~(\mathbf{u}_{2},\mathbf{v}_{1}),~(\mathbf{u}_{2},\mathbf{v}_{2})

that form rectangles of side-length
j
, i.e.
F(\mathbf{u}_{1}-\mathbf{u}_{2})\equiv F(\mathbf{v}_{1}-\mathbf{v}_{2})\equiv j\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})
.

If we only want to count the number of quadruples
(\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{u}_{2},\mathbf{u}_{3},\mathbf{u}_{4})
in
E
that form a cycle, i.e.,
F(\mathbf{u}_{1}-\mathbf{u}_{2})=F(\mathbf{u}_{2}-\mathbf{u}_{3})=F(\mathbf{u}_{3}-\mathbf{u}_{4})=F(\mathbf{u}_{4}-\mathbf{u}_{1})\equiv j\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})
, then a weaker condition is sufficient.

Theorem 1.14
.

Let
F
be a good polynomial and
p
be an odd prime. There is some
C>0
such that, if
r
is a positive integer,
j
is an integer with
(j,p)=1
, and
E
is a subset of
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
with
\delta_{E}\geq Cp^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}
, then
E
contains cycles of length
4
with distinct vertices and of side-length
j
.

The next result is on the existence of
k
-chains, i.e. a graph of
k+1
vertices
\mathbf{u}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{u}_{k+1}
such that
F(\mathbf{u}_{i}-\mathbf{u}_{i+1})\equiv j\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})
for all
1\leq i\leq k-1
.

Theorem 1.15
.

Let
F
be a good polynomial,
p
be an odd prime, and
k\geq 1
be an integer. There is some
C>0
such that, if
r
is a positive integer,
j
is an integer with
(j,p)=1
, and
E
is a subset of
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
with
\delta_{E}\geq Cp^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}
, then
E
contains
k
-chains of side-length
j
.

We note that this theorem improves the density of
p^{-\frac{n-1}{3}}
due to Lichtin in a recent paper
[
17
]
.

Our last result is on the distribution of pinned trees. We first need to introduce some notations.

Let
T
be an arbitrary tree with
k+1
vertices and
k
edges. Assume that
V(T)=\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{k+1}\}\subset E
, then the edge set of
T
can be ordered as follows:

\mathcal{E}(T)=\{(\mathbf{v}_{i_{1}},\mathbf{v}_{i_{2}}),(\mathbf{v}_{i_{3}},\mathbf{v}_{i_{4}}),\ldots,(\mathbf{v}_{i_{2k-1}},\mathbf{v}_{i_{2k}})\},

where
i_{1}\leq i_{3}\leq\cdots\leq i_{2k-1}
, and
i_{2s}<i_{2t}
if both
s<t
and
i_{2s-1}=i_{2t-1}
.

For such a tree
T
, the vector

\left(|\mathbf{v}_{i_{1}}-\mathbf{v}_{i_{2}}|,|\mathbf{v}_{i_{3}}-\mathbf{v}_{i_{4}}|,\ldots,|\mathbf{v}_{i_{2k-1}}-\mathbf{v}_{i_{2k}}|\right)\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{k}

is called the edge-length vector of
T
. Two trees
T
and
T^{\prime}
are called distinct if the corresponding edge-length vectors are not the same. We also recall that two trees
T
and
T^{\prime}
are isotropic if there exists a bijective map
\varphi
from
V(T)
to
V(T^{\prime})
such that the edges are preserved. For
\mathbf{x}\in V(T^{\prime})
and
\mathbf{v}\in V(T)
, we say
(T^{\prime},\mathbf{x})
is isomorphic to
(T,\mathbf{v})
if
T
is isomorphic to
T^{\prime}
under
\varphi
and
\varphi(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{v}
.

Theorem 1.16
.

Let
F
be a good polynomial,
p
be an odd prime, and
r
be a positive integer.
Let
E\subset(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
,
k\geq 1
be an integer, and
(T,\mathbf{v})
be a given tree with
k+1
vertices and
k
edges. If
\delta_{E}\gg_{k}p^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}
, then there exists
\mathbf{x}\in E
such that the number of distinct trees
(T^{\prime},\mathbf{x})
with vertices in
E
and isotropic to
(T,\mathbf{v})
is
\gg p^{rk}
.

2
Notations and Lemmas

In this paper, the letter
p
always denote a given prime
greater than
2
. The letter
n
denotes the dimension of the space under consideration, and
r
is always a positive integer.
The letters
m,k,l,i,j
always denote integers. The imaginary unit is denoted by
\mathsf{i}=\sqrt{-1}
.

When we write

The additive character modulo

is denoted by
e_{p^{r}}(x)=e^{\frac{2\pi\mathsf{i}x}{p^{r}}}
,
(x\,\textsf{mod }p^{r})
. For a function

, the Fourier transformation is defined by

\widehat{f}(\mathbf{m})=\frac{1}{p^{rn}}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{x}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})}f(\mathbf{x})e_{p^{r}}(-\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{x}),\quad\big(\mathbf{m}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{n}\big),

and the Fourier inverse is given by

The convolution of two functions

and

is defined by

(f_{1}\ast f_{2})(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{p^{rn}}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{y}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})}f_{1}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y})f_{2}(\mathbf{y}).

We have the property that
\widehat{f_{1}\ast f_{2}}=\widehat{f_{1}}\widehat{f_{2}}
. The Parseval’s identity is

\frac{1}{p^{rn}}\sum\limits_{\mathbf{x}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})}f_{1}(\mathbf{x})\overline{f_{2}(\mathbf{x})}=\sum\limits_{\mathbf{m}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})}\widehat{f_{1}}(\mathbf{m})\overline{\widehat{f_{2}}(\mathbf{m})}.

The next lemma is one version of Hensel’s lemma that will be regularly applied in this paper.

Lemma 2.1

(Hensel’s lemma)
.

Let

\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{x})=\big(G_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),\ldots,G_{m}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\big)

be a map from
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
to
\mathbb{Z}^{m}
, with
G_{i}
polynomials with integer coefficients. Let
l
be a positive integer and
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Suppose that
\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{y})\equiv\mathbf{0}

(\textsf{mod }p^{l})
. Let
R
be the rank of
J({\mathbf{G}})|_{\mathbf{y}}
modulo
p
, where
J({\mathbf{G}})|_{\mathbf{y}}
is the Jocobi matrix

J({\mathbf{G}})|_{\mathbf{y}}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial G_{1}}{\partial x_{1}}(\mathbf{y})&\frac{\partial G_{1}}{\partial x_{2}}(\mathbf{y})&\ldots&\frac{\partial G_{1}}{\partial x_{n}}(\mathbf{y})\\
\frac{\partial G_{2}}{\partial x_{1}}(\mathbf{y})&\frac{\partial G_{2}}{\partial x_{2}}(\mathbf{y})&\ldots&\frac{\partial G_{2}}{\partial x_{n}}(\mathbf{y})\\
\ldots&\ldots&\ldots&\ldots\\
\frac{\partial G_{m}}{\partial x_{1}}(\mathbf{y})&\frac{\partial G_{m}}{\partial x_{2}}(\mathbf{y})&\ldots&\frac{\partial G_{m}}{\partial x_{n}}(\mathbf{y})\end{bmatrix}.

Then

\#\big\{\mathbf{z}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{k}):\,\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{y}+p^{l}\mathbf{z})\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{l+k})\big\}\leq p^{k(n-R)}

(3)

for any integer
k\geq 1
. When
R=m
, the “
\leq
” can be replaced by “
=
”.

Proof.

Firstly, let us consider the case
k=1
. Let
\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{y})=p^{l}\mathbf{b}
, where
\mathbf{b}=(b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m})\in\mathbb{Z}^{m}
. Note that

G_{i}(\mathbf{y}+p^{l}\mathbf{z})\equiv G_{i}(\mathbf{y})+p^{l}(\nabla G_{i})(\mathbf{y})\cdot\mathbf{z}\quad(\textsf{mod }p^{l+1})

for any
i=1,2,\ldots,m
. Then
G_{i}(\mathbf{y}+p^{l}\mathbf{z})\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p^{l+1})

(i=1,2,\ldots,m)
if and only if
b_{i}+(\nabla G_{i})(\mathbf{y})\cdot\mathbf{z}\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p)

(i=1,2,\ldots,m)
, if and only if
J(\mathbf{G})|_{\mathbf{y}}\mathbf{z}\equiv-\mathbf{b}\,(\textsf{mod }p)
. The number of solutions to such a system of linear equations does not exceed
p^{n-R}
. Thus,

\#\big\{\mathbf{z}\,(\textsf{mod }p):\,\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{y}+p^{l}\mathbf{z})\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{l+1})\big\}\leq p^{n-R}.

Moreover, the system is consistent when
R=m
, and has exactly
p^{n-m}
solutions.

Secondly, let us consider the case
k=2
. For any
\mathbf{z}_{1}(\textsf{mod }p)
with
\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{y}+p^{l}\mathbf{z}_{1})\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{l+1})
, we write
\mathbf{y}_{1}=\mathbf{y}+p^{l}\mathbf{z}_{1}
. Noting that
J(\mathbf{G})|_{\mathbf{y}_{1}}\equiv J(\mathbf{G})|_{\mathbf{y}}\,(\textsf{mod }p)
, the rank of
J(\mathbf{G})|_{\mathbf{y}_{1}}
modulo
p
is also
R
. Hence

\#\big\{\mathbf{z}_{2}\,(\textsf{mod }p):\,\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{y}_{1}+p^{l+1}\mathbf{z}_{2})\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{l+2})\big\}\leq p^{n-R}.

It follows that

\#\big\{(\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{z}_{2})\,(\textsf{mod }p):\,\mathbf{G}\big(\mathbf{y}+p^{l}(\mathbf{z}_{1}+p\mathbf{z}_{2})\big)\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{l+2})\big\}\leq p^{2(n-R)}.

Finally, the conclusion follows by an induction on
k
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
a
be an integer with
(a,p)=1
. Let

be a natural number with
(k,p)=1
. Then for any

(7)

Moreover, for any natural number

\sum\limits_{s\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})}\left|\sum\limits_{x\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})}e_{p^{r}}(-mx+sax^{k})\right|^{2}\ll_{k}\begin{cases}p^{2r},\quad&\text{if }{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\textsf{ord}_{p}}(m)<r-\lceil\frac{r}{k}\rceil,\\

Note that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{s\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})}\left|\sum\limits_{x\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})}e_{p^{r}}(-mx+sax^{k})\right|^{2}=\sum\limits_{x,y\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})}\sum\limits_{s\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})}e_{p^{r}}\big(-m(x-y)+sa(x^{k}-y^{k})\big)

(9)

\ll_{k}1
. By Hensel’s lemma, we conclude that (

1\leq u,u^{\prime}\leq\infty

||(fd\sigma)^{\vee}||_{L^{u^{\prime}}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n},dm)}\leq C||f||_{L^{u}(V,d\sigma)},

(12)

holds for some constant
C>0
and all complex valued functions
f
on
V
. Here
dm
is the counting measure on
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
,
d\sigma
is the normalized surface measure on
V
, and

(fd\sigma)^{\vee}(m)=\frac{1}{|V|}\sum_{x\in V}f(x)e_{p}(m\cdot x),

||f||_{L^{u}(V,d\sigma)}=\left(\frac{1}{|V|}\sum_{x\in V}|f(x)|^{u}\right)^{1/u},

and

||g||_{L^{u^{\prime}}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n},dm)}=\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}}|g(m)|^{u^{\prime}}\right)^{1/u^{\prime}},

for any functions
f
and
g
. The
L^{u}\to L^{u^{\prime}}
Fourier extension problem has been studied intensively in the literature for paraboloids, spheres, cones, and homogeneous varieties with applications in several areas of Mathematics including
Discrete Geometry
and
Combinatorial Number Theory
. We refer the interested reader to a series of papers
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
for more discussions.

When
V
is a circle in the plane
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2}
, it has been proved in
[
2
]
that

||(fd\sigma)^{\vee}||_{L^{4}(\mathbb{F}_{p}^{2},dm)}\ll||f||_{L^{2}(V,d\sigma)}.

In this paper, to improve Corollary
1.5
in two dimensions, we need to study the finite p-adic ring analog of this estimate. The extension estimates are of independent interest and are expected to have more applications in other topics.

4.1
Extension theorems

In the rest of this section, we always assume that
n=2
. Recall that

C_{r,j}=\{\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}:\,\|\mathbf{x}\|\equiv j\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})\}.

We denote by
d\sigma_{r,j}
the normalised surface measure on
C_{r,j}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
p
be an odd prime, and

r\geq 1
be an integer. Let
j\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{\ast}
. Then

\Big(\sum_{m\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}}|(fd\sigma_{r,j})^{\vee}(m)|^{4}\Big)^{1/2}\ll p^{-\frac{r+1}{2}}\sum_{x\in C_{r,j}}|f(x)|^{2}.

We now define a more general extension problem.
Denote

G_{r}:=SO_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&-b\\
b&a\end{bmatrix}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r}):\,\,a^{2}+b^{2}\equiv 1\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})\right\}

for
r\geq 1
. The group
G_{r}
acts on
(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
naturally by
\mathbf{x}\mapsto\theta\mathbf{x}
, where
\theta\in G_{r}
and
\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
. Write the orbit of
\mathbf{x}
by

\textsf{orb}_{r}(\mathbf{x})=\big\{\theta\mathbf{x}:\,\theta\in G_{r}\big\},

and the stabilizer of
\mathbf{x}
is

\textsf{stab}_{r}(\mathbf{x})=\{\theta\in G_{r}:\,\theta\mathbf{x}\equiv\mathbf{x}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})\}.

For
\mathbf{m}=(m_{1},m_{2})\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
, recall that
v_{\mathbf{m}}=\min\{\textsf{ord}_{p}(m_{1}),\textsf{ord}_{p}(m_{2})\}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
p\equiv 3\,(\textsf{mod }4)
and
r\geq 1
be an integer. Let
\mathbf{m}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
be such that
\mathbf{m}\neq\mathbf{0}
. Denote by
d{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\sigma_{r}}
the normalized surface measure on
\textsf{orb}_{r}(\mathbf{m})
. Then

\Big(\sum_{x\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}}|(fd\sigma_{r})^{\vee}(x)|^{4}\Big)^{1/2}\ll p^{-\frac{r-3v_{\mathbf{m}}+1}{2}}\sum_{x\in\textsf{orb}_{r}(\mathbf{m})}|f(x)|^{2}.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
p\equiv 1\,(\textsf{mod }4)
and
r\geq 1
be an integer. Let
\mathbf{m}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
be such that
\mathbf{m}\neq\mathbf{0}
. Denote by
d\sigma_{r}
the normalized surface measure on
\textsf{orb}_{r}(\mathbf{m})
. Suppose that
\mathbf{m}=p^{v_{m}}\tilde{\mathbf{m}}
with
\tilde{\mathbf{m}}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r-v_{m}}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
and
\|\tilde{\mathbf{m}}\|\not\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p)
. Then

\Big(\sum_{x\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}}|(fd\sigma_{r})^{\vee}(x)|^{4}\Big)^{1/2}\ll p^{-\frac{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}r-3v_{\mathbf{m}}+1}{2}}\sum_{x\in\textsf{orb}_{r}(\mathbf{m})}|f(x)|^{2}.

4.2
Preliminary lemmas

To prove these extension theorems, we first need to collect and prove a number of preliminary results. All detailed proofs in this subsection are written under the assumption that
p\equiv 3\,(\textsf{mod }4)
. In the case
p\equiv 1\,(\textsf{mod }4)
,
the arguments are identical but more complicated, and will be omitted in this paper.

Let
p
be a prime with
p\equiv 3\,(\textsf{mod }4)
,
r\geq 1
be an integer. For any
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, one has
(l,p)=1
if and only if
(l,p^{r})=1
. For any
\theta\in G_{r}
and
\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
, it is not hard to verify that
\|\theta\mathbf{x}\|\equiv\|\mathbf{x}\|\quad(\textsf{mod }p^{r})
. Moreover, we have
v_{\theta\mathbf{x}}\geq v_{\mathbf{x}}
. Since
\theta
is invertible, one obtains that
v_{\theta\mathbf{x}}=v_{\mathbf{x}}
.

It is well-known (see
[
5
]
for example) that

|C_{1,j}|=|G_{1}|=p+1,\quad(j\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\ast}),\qquad|C_{1,0}|=1

when
p\equiv 3\,(\textsf{mod }4)
. In particular,
C_{1,0}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
. For any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z}
, it follows that
\|\mathbf{x}\|\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p)
, if and only if
\mathbf{x}\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p)
, if and only if
v_{\mathbf{x}}>0
. Or equivalently,
we have
\|\mathbf{x}\|\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{\ast}
if and only if
\|\mathbf{x}\|\not\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p)
, if and only if
\mathbf{x}\not\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p)
, if and only if
v_{\mathbf{x}}=0
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
p\equiv 3\,(\textsf{mod }4)
and
r\geq 1
.
Then
|G_{r}|=p^{r}(1+1/p)
.

Proof.

For
r=1
, we have
|G_{1}|=p+1
. Now consider the circumstances that
r\geq 2
. For any
\theta=\begin{bmatrix}a&-b\\
b&a\end{bmatrix}\in G_{r}
, there is some
\theta_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{0}&-b_{0}\\
b_{0}&a_{0}\end{bmatrix}\in G_{1}
such that
\theta\equiv\theta_{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p)
. Applying Lemma
2.1
to
(a_{0},b_{0})
and the polynomial
F(x,y)=x^{2}+y^{2}-1
. Then
(\nabla F)(a_{0},b_{0})=(2a_{0},2b_{0})\not\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p)
, since
a_{0}^{2}+b_{0}^{2}\equiv 1\,(\textsf{mod }p)
. Thus,

\#\left\{(z_{1},z_{2})\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r-1}):\,(a_{0}+pz_{1})^{2}+(b_{0}+pz_{2})^{2}\equiv 1\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})\right\}=p^{r-1}.

It follows that

|G_{r}|=p^{r-1}|G_{1}|=p^{r}(1+1/p).

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
p\equiv 3\,(\textsf{mod }4)
and
r\geq 1
.
For any
\mathbf{0}\neq\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
, let
\mathbf{x}=p^{v_{\mathbf{x}}}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
for some
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r-v_{\mathbf{x}}}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
with
v_{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}}=0
. Then

\textsf{orb}_{r}(\mathbf{x})=\big\{p^{v_{\mathbf{x}}}\theta_{0}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}:\,\theta_{0}\in G_{r-v_{\mathbf{x}}}\big\}=p^{v_{\mathbf{x}}}C_{r-v_{\mathbf{x}},\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\|}.

And

|\textsf{stab}_{r}(\mathbf{x})|=p^{v_{\mathbf{x}}},\qquad|\textsf{orb}_{r}(\mathbf{x})|=p^{r-v_{\mathbf{x}}}(1+1/p).

Proof.

Write
v=v_{\mathbf{x}}
for simplicity. Here
0\leq v\leq r-1
. Let
\tilde{\mathbf{x}}=(\tilde{x_{1}},\tilde{x_{2}})
. The equation
\theta\mathbf{x}\equiv\mathbf{x}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})
is equivalent to

\begin{bmatrix}a-1&-b\\
b&a-1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}p^{v}\tilde{x_{1}}\\
p^{v}\tilde{x_{2}}\end{bmatrix}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\end{bmatrix}\quad(\textsf{mod }p^{r}),

(13)

or equivalently,

\begin{bmatrix}\tilde{x_{1}}&-\tilde{x_{2}}\\
\tilde{x_{2}}&\tilde{x_{1}}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}a-1\\
b\end{bmatrix}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\end{bmatrix}\quad(\textsf{mod }p^{r-v}).

(14)

Since
v_{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}}=0
, one has
\tilde{x_{1}}^{2}+\tilde{x_{2}}^{2}\not\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p)
(recalling that
p\equiv 3\,(\textsf{mod }4)
). The coefficient matrix is invertible and

\begin{bmatrix}a\\
b\end{bmatrix}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}1\\
0\end{bmatrix},\quad(\textsf{mod }p^{r-v}).

By Lemma
2.1
, the number of
(a,b)\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
satisfying the above equivalence and
a^{2}+b^{2}\equiv 1\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})
is exactly
p^{v}
. So

\big|\textsf{stab}_{r}(\mathbf{x})\big|=p^{v}.

It then follows that
|\textsf{orb}_{r}(\mathbf{x})|=|G_{r}|/|\textsf{stab}_{r}(\mathbf{x})|=p^{r-v}(1+1/p)
.

Moreover, for any
\theta\in G_{r}
, there is some
\theta_{0}\in G_{r-v}
such that
\theta\equiv\theta_{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r-v})
. It can be verified that
\theta\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\equiv\theta_{0}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r-v})
. So

\textsf{orb}_{r}(\mathbf{x})=\big\{\theta(p^{v}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}):\,\theta\in G_{r}\big\}=\big\{p^{v}\theta_{0}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}:\,\theta_{0}\in G_{r-v}\big\}.

Note that
|G_{r-v}|=p^{r-v}(1+1/p)
by Lemma
4.4
, the elements on the right-hand side of above formula give different members of the orbit. Furthermore, one has
\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\|\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r-v}\mathbb{Z})^{\ast}
, since
v_{\tilde{\mathbf{x}}}=0
and
p\equiv 3\,(\textsf{mod }4)
. We have
\{\theta_{0}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}:\,\theta_{0}\in G_{r-v}\}=C_{r-v,\|\tilde{\mathbf{x}}\|}
. The proof is completed.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
p\equiv 3\,(\textsf{mod }4)
and
r\geq 1
.
Let
j
be an integer with
\text{ord}_{p}(j)=v
. Then

|C_{r,j}|=\begin{cases}p^{r}(1+1/p),\quad&\text{if }0\leq v<r\text{ and }v\text{ is even},\\
p^{2\lfloor r/2\rfloor},\quad&\text{if }v=r,\\
0,\quad&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

More concretely, we have

C_{r,j}=\begin{cases}\left\{p^{u}\mathbf{z}+p^{r-u}\mathbf{w}:\,\mathbf{z}\in C_{r-2u,\tilde{j}},\,\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{u}\mathbb{Z}\right\},\quad&\text{if }j=p^{2u}\tilde{j},\,0\leq u\leq\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\rfloor,\,\tilde{j}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r-2u}\mathbb{Z})^{\ast}\\
\left\{p^{\lceil r/2\rceil}\mathbf{w}:\,\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}\mathbb{Z}\right\},&\text{if }j=0,\\
\emptyset,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We first consider the case
j\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{\ast}
. By applying Lemma
2.1
, similar arguments as previous show that

|C_{r,j}|=p^{r-1}|C_{1,j}|=p^{r}(1+1/p).

Next, consider the circle
C_{r,0}

(r\geq 2)
. Since
C_{1,0}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
, we can write

C_{r,0}=\{p\mathbf{y}:\,\mathbf{y}\in C^{\prime}_{r,0}\},\quad C^{\prime}_{r,0}=\big\{\mathbf{y}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r-1}):\,\|p\mathbf{y}\|\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})\big\}.

When
r=2
, one sees that
C_{2,0}=\{p\mathbf{y}:\,\mathbf{y}\in(\mathbb{Z}/pZ)^{2}\}
and
|C_{2,0}|=p^{2}
. When
r\geq 3
, we further write
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{z}+p^{r-2}\mathbf{w}
with
\mathbf{z}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r-2}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
and
\mathbf{w}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{2}
. The condition
\|p\mathbf{y}\|\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})
holds if and only if
\|\mathbf{z}+p^{r-2}\mathbf{w}\|=\|\mathbf{y}\|\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r-2})
, if and only if
\|\mathbf{z}\|\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r-2})
.
It follows that

C_{r,0}=\big\{p(\mathbf{z}+p^{r-2}\mathbf{w}):\,\mathbf{z}\in C_{r-2,0},\,\mathbf{w}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{2}\big\},

and
|C_{r,0}|=p^{2}|C_{r-2,0}|
.

By induction, we conclude that

C_{r,0}=\left\{p^{\lceil r/2\rceil}\mathbf{w}:\,\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{\lfloor r/2\rfloor}\mathbb{Z}\right\}

and
|C_{r,0}|=p^{2\lfloor r/2\rfloor}
.

Now, let us consider the case
j=p^{v}\tilde{j}
, where
1\leq v\leq r-1
and
\tilde{j}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r-v}\mathbb{Z})^{\ast}
. For any
\mathbf{x}\in C_{r,j}
, it satisfies that
\|\mathbf{x}\|\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p)
. Recalling that
C_{1,0}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
, one has
\mathbf{x}\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p)
. So

C_{r,j}=\{p\mathbf{y}:\,\mathbf{y}\in C^{\prime}_{r,j}\},\quad C^{\prime}_{r,j}=\big\{\mathbf{y}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r-1}):\,\|p\mathbf{y}\|\equiv p^{v}\tilde{j}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})\big\}.

When
v=1
, it is easy to see that
C^{\prime}_{r,j}=\emptyset
. When
v\geq 2
, which means
r\geq 3
, similar arguments as previous show that

C_{r,p^{v}\tilde{j}}=\big\{p(\mathbf{z}+p^{r-2}\mathbf{w}):\,\mathbf{z}\in C_{r-2,p^{v-2}\tilde{j}},\,\mathbf{w}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{2}\big\},

and
|C_{r,p^{v}\tilde{j}}|=p^{2}\cdot|C_{r-2,p^{v-2}\tilde{j}}|
. By induction, it follows that

|C_{r,p^{v}\tilde{j}}|=\begin{cases}p^{r}(1+1/p),\quad&\text{if }v\text{ is even},\\
0,\quad&\text{if }v\text{ is odd}\end{cases}

when
1\leq v\leq r-1
. Indeed,

C_{r,p^{2u}\tilde{j}}=\left\{p^{u}\mathbf{z}+p^{r-u}\mathbf{w}:\,\mathbf{z}\in C_{r-2u,\tilde{j}},\,\mathbf{w}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{u}\mathbb{Z})^{2}\right\}

(15)

when
r\geq 3
and
1\leq u\leq(r-1)/2
.
∎

Remark:

When
p\equiv 3\,(\textsf{mod }4)
, we have the disjoint union

(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}=\left(\bigcup\limits_{u=0}^{\lfloor(r-1)/2\rfloor}\bigcup\limits_{\tilde{j}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r-2u}\mathbb{Z})^{\ast}}C_{r,p^{2u}\tilde{j}}\right)\,\cup\,C_{r,0},

whose cardinalities give

\displaystyle p^{2r}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{u=0}^{\lfloor(r-1)/2\rfloor}p^{r}(1+1/p)\cdot p^{r-2u}(1-1/p)+p^{2\lfloor r/2\rfloor}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{u=0}^{\lfloor(r-1)/2\rfloor}(p^{2r-2u}-p^{2r-2u-2})+p^{2\lfloor r/2\rfloor}.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
p\equiv 3\,(\textsf{mod }4)
and
r\geq 1
. Let
j\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{\ast}
. For any
\mathbf{0}\neq\mathbf{z}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
, we have

\#\big\{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in C_{r,j}^{2}:\,\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\equiv\mathbf{z}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})\big\}\,\leq\,2p^{r-1}.

Proof.

The set on the left-hand side involves system of congruences

\begin{cases}x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}\equiv j,\\
y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}\equiv j,\\
x_{1}-y_{1}\equiv z_{1},\\
x_{2}-y_{2}\equiv z_{2}.\end{cases}

(16)

Considering the system (
16
) modulo
p
, one sees that

2y_{1}z_{1}+2y_{2}z_{2}+z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p).

First, we consider the case
\mathbf{z}\not\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p)
. Assume without loss of generality that
z_{1}\not\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p)
. Inserting
y_{1}\equiv-(2z_{1})^{-1}(2y_{2}z_{2}+z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2})\,(\textsf{mod }p)
into
y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2}\equiv j\,(\textsf{mod }p)
, one obtains

z_{1}^{-2}(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2})y_{2}^{2}+z_{1}^{-2}z_{2}(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2})y_{2}+4^{-1}z_{1}^{-2}(z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2})^{2}\equiv j\quad(\textsf{mod }p).

When
p\equiv 3\,(\textsf{mod }4)
,
\mathbf{z}\not\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p)
implies that
z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}\not\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p)
. The left-hand side of above congruence has degree
2
. So there are at most two solutions in
y_{2}
. Moreover, each choice of
y_{2}
exactly determines the choices of
x_{1}
,
y_{1}
and
x_{2}
.

Now we apply Hensel’s lemma. The Jacobian matrix, in
(x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2})
, is given by

\begin{bmatrix}2x_{1}&0&2x_{2}&0\\
0&2y_{1}&0&2y_{2}\\
1&-1&0&0\\
0&0&1&-1\end{bmatrix}.

By elementary operations, we obtain

\begin{bmatrix}1&0&-1&0\\
0&1&0&-1\\
0&0&x_{1}&x_{2}\\
0&0&y_{1}&y_{2}\end{bmatrix}.

Since
j\not\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p)
, it satisfies that
\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\not\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p)
and the rank modulo
p
is at least
3
. Then the conclusion follows from Lemma
2.1
.

Second, let us deal with the case
\mathbf{z}\equiv\mathbf{0}

(\textsf{mod }p)
. Assume that
\mathbf{z}=p^{k}\tilde{\mathbf{z}}
with
k=v_{\mathbf{z}}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{z}}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r-k}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
. Here
1\leq k<r
, since
\mathbf{z}\neq\mathbf{0}
. Note that, for any solution
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
to the system (
16
) modulo
p^{r}
, it also satisfies (
16
) modulo
p^{k}
or
p^{k+1}
. For the former situation, one deduces that
\mathbf{x}\equiv\mathbf{y}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{k})
since
\mathbf{z}\equiv\mathbf{0}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{k})
. For the latter situation, similar arguments as previous shows that

(y_{1}+p^{k}\tilde{z_{1}})^{2}+(y_{2}+p^{k}\tilde{z_{2}})^{2}\equiv j\equiv y_{1}^{2}+y_{2}^{2},\,(\textsf{mod }p^{k+1}),

i.e.,
2y_{1}\tilde{z_{1}}+2y_{2}\tilde{z_{2}}\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p)
. Noting that
v_{\tilde{\mathbf{z}}}=0
, we assume without loss of generality that
\tilde{z_{1}}\not\equiv 0\,(\textsf{mod }p)
.
Then

x_{1}\equiv y_{1}\equiv y_{2}\tilde{z_{2}}\tilde{z_{1}}^{-1}\equiv x_{2}\tilde{z_{2}}\tilde{z_{1}}^{-1},\quad(\textsf{mod }p).

Now any solution
(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
to the system (
16
) modulo
p^{r}
satisfies that

\begin{cases}x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}\equiv j,\\
x_{1}\equiv x_{2}\tilde{z_{2}}\tilde{z_{1}}^{-1},\\
x_{1}\equiv y_{1},\\
x_{2}\equiv y_{2},\end{cases}\quad(\textsf{mod }p).

It is not hard to see that there are at most
2
solutions modulo
p
. Now we lift these solutions modulo
p
to solutions to (
16
) modulo
p^{r}
. By applying Hensel’s lemma with the rank of Jacobian matrix being
3
, we conclude that

\#\big\{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in C_{r,j}^{2}:\,\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\equiv\mathbf{z}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})\big\}\leq 2p^{r-1}.

∎

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
p\equiv 3\,(\textsf{mod }4)
and
r\geq 1
.
Let
\mathbf{m},\mathbf{z}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
be such that
\mathbf{m},\mathbf{z}\neq\mathbf{0}
. Then

\#\big\{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in(\textsf{orb}_{r}(\mathbf{m}))^{2}:\,\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\equiv\mathbf{z}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})\big\}\,\leq\,2p^{r-v_{\mathbf{m}}-1}.

Proof.

Write
v=v_{\mathbf{m}}
and
\mathbf{m}=p^{v}\tilde{\mathbf{m}}
. Here
0\leq v\leq r-1
,
\tilde{\mathbf{m}}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r-v_{\mathbf{m}}}\mathbb{Z})^{2}
and
v_{\tilde{\mathbf{m}}}=0
. Write
\|\tilde{\mathbf{m}}\|=\tilde{j}
for simplicity. Then
\tilde{j}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r-v}\mathbb{Z})^{\ast}
. By Lemma
4.5
, we have
\textsf{orb}(\mathbf{m})=p^{v}C_{r-v,\tilde{j}}
. Write
\mathbf{x}=p^{v}\tilde{\mathbf{x}}
and
\mathbf{y}=p^{v}\tilde{\mathbf{y}}
with
\tilde{\mathbf{x}},\tilde{\mathbf{y}}\in C_{r-v,\tilde{j}}
. When
\mathbf{z}\not\equiv\mathbf{0}(\textsf{mod }p^{v})
, it is obvious that

\#\big\{(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})\in(\textsf{orb}_{r}(\mathbf{m}))^{2}:\,\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\equiv\mathbf{z}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})\big\}=0.

When
\mathbf{z}\equiv\mathbf{0}(\textsf{mod }p^{v})
, we write
\mathbf{z}=p^{v}\tilde{\mathbf{z}}
for some

6
An
\mathbb{F}_{p}
–to–
\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z}
transfer principle in arbitrary dimensions

The purpose of this section is to formulate a prime field-to-ring
transfer principle in arbitrary dimensions. We work with the standard
quadratic form

\|\mathbf{x}\|:=x_{1}^{2}+\cdots+x_{n}^{2}.

The proof separates the finite field combinatorial input from the
higher-conductor analysis. The dependence on the dimension enters
through the finite field isosceles energy, the zero-sphere estimate,
and the Fourier transform of a multiplicative character composed with
the quadratic form.

For an odd prime
p
and an integer
j\geq 1
, write

R_{j}:=\mathbb{Z}/p^{j}\mathbb{Z},\qquad U_{j}:=R_{j}^{\times},

and let
\widehat{U_{j}}
denote the multiplicative character group of

U_{j}
. If
1\leq s\leq r
, let

\rho_{r,s}:U_{r}\longrightarrow U_{s}

be reduction modulo
p^{s}
. We say that

\chi\in\widehat{U_{r}}

factors through
U_{s}
if

\chi=\chi_{s}\circ\rho_{r,s}

for some
\chi_{s}\in\widehat{U_{s}}
. Since
\rho_{r,s}
is
surjective, the character
\chi_{s}
is unique. Characters that factor
through
U_{1}=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
will be called
low-conductor
characters
. For
2\leq s\leq r
, we say that
\chi
has

exact conductor
p^{s}
if it factors through
U_{s}
but not
through
U_{s-1}
. Equivalently, the induced character
\chi_{s}
is
nontrivial on

\ker(U_{s}\longrightarrow U_{s-1})=1+p^{s-1}R_{s}.

Throughout this section, every multiplicative character of
U_{j}
is
extended by zero to
R_{j}
.

There are two points that must be made precise. First, the input is a
full isosceles-energy estimate, rather than only a lower bound for the
cardinality of a finite field distance set. Second, in dimensions

n\geq 3
this energy cannot be replaced by the count obtained by
requiring the base to have nonzero squared length: two distinct
endpoints may have zero squared distance.

For a weight
h:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to[0,1]
, put

H:=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}}h(\mathbf{a})

and define

\mathcal{T}_{p,n}^{\times}(h):=\sum_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b},\mathbf{c}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}}h(\mathbf{a})h(\mathbf{b})h(\mathbf{c})\mathbf{1}_{\{\|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\|=\|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{c}\|\neq 0\}}.

(18)

For a set
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, we write

\mathcal{T}_{p,n}^{\times}(A):=\mathcal{T}_{p,n}^{\times}(1_{A}).

Thus, the superscript
\times
records that the common squared length
is nonzero. This is the full nonzero isosceles energy, including the
terms with
\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{c}
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Fix
n\geq 2
and

\left\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\right\rfloor\leq\beta<n.

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a collection of odd primes. Suppose that there

C_{0},C_{1}>0
, independent of
p\in\mathfrak{P}
, such
that, for every
p\in\mathfrak{P}
and every set

with
|A|\geq C_{0}p^{\beta}
, one has

\mathcal{T}_{p,n}^{\times}(A)\leq C_{1}\frac{|A|^{3}}{p}.

(19)

Then there are constants

C_{2}=C_{2}(n,C_{0},C_{1})>0
and

with the following property. For every

, every

, and every

satisfying

|E|\geq C_{2}p^{n(r-1)+\beta},\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad\delta_{E}\geq C_{2}p^{\beta-n},

one has

|\Delta_{n,r}(E)\cap U_{r}|\geq c_{2}p^{r}.

In particular,
|\Delta_{n,r}(E)|\gg p^{r}
, and all implied constants
are independent of both
p
and
r
.

Taking
\mathfrak{P}=\{p\}
gives the fixed-prime formulation. The
family formulation above merely records when the constants are
uniform as
p
varies.

In two dimensions, it follows from the paper
[
21
]
that for

, if

, then one has

\mathcal{T}_{p,n}^{\times}(A)\ll\frac{|A|^{3}}{p}.

In four dimensions
[
26
]
, we obtained the same conclusion under the assumption that

for any

.

Therefore, Theorems

and

follow immediately from Theorem

.

Sketch of ideas.

The transfer has three transparent steps. First, reduce
E
modulo

p
and record, at each point of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
, the proportion of its

p^{n(r-1)}
lifts that lie in
E
. This replaces the possibly very
irregular fibres of
E
by a weight
h_{E}
on the finite field. Second,
expand the unit-distance second moment by multiplicative characters.
The characters that see only reduction modulo
p
reproduce the
weighted finite field isosceles energy exactly, apart from the
predictable factor
p^{(3n-1)(r-1)}
. Third, the characters that see
higher
p
-adic digits are controlled at once by the

n
-dimensional quadratic Gauss transform; their Fourier multipliers
have size at most
p^{-sn/2}
at conductor
p^{s}
. Thus, all of the
combinatorial information is supplied at the first residue level, and
the remaining digits contribute only a uniform analytic error. A
short estimate for the zero sphere in
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
guarantees that, when

H_{E}\gg p^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
, a positive proportion of the pairs
have nonzero distance and hence lift to unit distances.

For

E\subset(\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{n}
, define

h_{E}(\mathbf{a}):=p^{-n(r-1)}\#\{\mathbf{x}\in E:\ \mathbf{x}\equiv\mathbf{a}\,(\textsf{mod }p)\},\qquad\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}.

(20)

Then,
0\leq h_{E}\leq 1
, and

H_{E}:=\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}}h_{E}(\mathbf{a})=\frac{|E|}{p^{n(r-1)}}.

(21)

For
\mathbf{x}\in E
and
j\in U_{r}
, let

\nu_{\mathbf{x}}(j):=\#\{\mathbf{y}\in E:\ \|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|=j\},

and define

\mathcal{N}_{n}^{\times}(E):=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in E}\sum_{j\in U_{r}}\nu_{\mathbf{x}}(j)^{2}.

(22)

The following two lemmas separate the combinatorial input from the
ring-theoretic argument.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
n\geq 2
and

\lfloor n/2\rfloor\leq\beta<n
, and let
p
be an odd prime.
Suppose that
C_{0}^{\prime},C_{1}^{\prime}>0
and that, whenever

h:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to[0,1]
has mass
H\geq C_{0}^{\prime}p^{\beta}
, one has

\mathcal{T}_{p,n}^{\times}(h)\leq C_{1}^{\prime}\frac{H^{3}}{p}.

Then, there are constants

C_{4}=C_{4}(n,C_{0}^{\prime},C_{1}^{\prime})>0
and

c_{4}=c_{4}(n,C_{0}^{\prime},C_{1}^{\prime})>0
, independent of
p
and
r
, such that
for every
r\geq 1
and every

with

, one has

|\Delta_{n,r}(E)\cap U_{r}|\geq c_{4}p^{r}.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
and

. Suppose that there are constants

C_{0},C_{1}>0
such that every
A\subset\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
with

satisfies

\mathcal{T}_{p,n}^{\times}(A)\leq C_{1}\frac{|A|^{3}}{p}.

Put

. Then, every weight

h:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to[0,1]
of mass

\mathcal{T}_{p,n}^{\times}(h)\leq(5C_{1}+1)\frac{H^{3}}{p}.

(23)

Theorem
6.1
follows directly from these two lemmas.

6.1
Proof of Lemma
6.2

We begin with the estimate that ensures the existence of sufficiently
many unit-distance pairs.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
h:\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}\to[0,1]
have mass
H
. Then

\sum_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}}h(\mathbf{a})h(\mathbf{b})\mathbf{1}_{\{\|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\|=0\}}\leq\bigl(p^{-1}+p^{-n/2}\bigr)H^{2}+p^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}H.

(24)

Consequently, if

H\geq 6p^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
, then

\sum_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}}h(\mathbf{a})h(\mathbf{b})\mathbf{1}_{\{\|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\|\neq 0\}}\geq\frac{H^{2}}{6}.

(25)

Proof.

Let

S_{0}:=\{\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}:\ \|\mathbf{z}\|=0\},

and let
\mathcal{A}
be the convolution matrix

\mathcal{A}_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}=1_{S_{0}}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})
.
The additive characters of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
are eigenvectors of

\mathcal{A}
. The constant eigenvector has eigenvalue
|S_{0}|
, and
the eigenvalue at
\mathbf{m}\neq\mathbf{0}
is

\lambda(\mathbf{m})=\sum_{\mathbf{z}\in S_{0}}e_{p}(-\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{z}).

Let
\eta
be the quadratic character of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
, and let

\mathfrak{g}_{p}:=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}e_{p}(x^{2}),\qquad|\mathfrak{g}_{p}|=p^{1/2}.

Additive orthogonality followed by completing the square gives, for

\mathbf{m}\neq\mathbf{0}
,

\lambda(\mathbf{m})=\frac{\mathfrak{g}_{p}^{n}}{p}\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}}\eta(t)^{n}e_{p}\left(-\frac{\|\mathbf{m}\|}{4t}\right).

(26)

If
n
is even, the last sum has modulus
p-1
when

\|\mathbf{m}\|=0
and modulus
1
otherwise. If
n
is odd, it is zero
when
\|\mathbf{m}\|=0
and is a quadratic Gauss sum of modulus
p^{1/2}

otherwise. Hence

\max_{\mathbf{m}\neq\mathbf{0}}|\lambda(\mathbf{m})|\leq p^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}.

(27)

The same calculation at
\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{0}
, now including the term
t=0
,
also gives

\frac{|S_{0}|}{p^{n}}\leq p^{-1}+p^{-n/2}.

(28)

For completeness, the evaluations just used follow after the change
of variables
u=t^{-1}
. When
n
is even, the sum in
(
26
) becomes

\sum_{u\neq 0}e_{p}(-\|\mathbf{m}\|u/4)
, which equals
p-1
if

\|\mathbf{m}\|=0
and
-1
otherwise. When
n
is odd, it becomes a
quadratic Gauss sum if
\|\mathbf{m}\|\neq 0
and vanishes if

\|\mathbf{m}\|=0
.

Write
h=H/p^{n}+h_{0}
, where
\sum_{\mathbf{a}}h_{0}(\mathbf{a})=0
.
By (
27
), the spectral theorem, and

0\leq h\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b}}h(\mathbf{a})h(\mathbf{b})1_{S_{0}}(\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b})

\displaystyle=\frac{|S_{0}|}{p^{n}}H^{2}+\langle\mathcal{A}h_{0},h_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|S_{0}|}{p^{n}}H^{2}+|\langle\mathcal{A}h_{0},h_{0}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(p^{-1}+p^{-n/2}\bigr)H^{2}+p^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\|h_{0}\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(p^{-1}+p^{-n/2}\bigr)H^{2}+p^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\sum_{\mathbf{a}}h(\mathbf{a})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigl(p^{-1}+p^{-n/2}\bigr)H^{2}+p^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}H.

Here we used

\|h_{0}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{\mathbf{a}}h(\mathbf{a})^{2}-H^{2}/p^{n}
.
This proves (
24
). Since
p\geq 3
and

n\geq 2
, one has
p^{-1}+p^{-n/2}\leq 2/3
. If

H\geq 6p^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}
, the last term in
(
24
) is at most
H^{2}/6
. Hence, the
zero-distance sum is at most
5H^{2}/6
, and subtracting it from
H^{2}

proves (
25
).
∎

Proof of Lemma
6.2
.

We first establish the following estimate

\mathcal{N}_{n}^{\times}(E)\leq p^{(3n-1)(r-1)}\mathcal{T}_{p,n}^{\times}(h_{E})+|E|q^{2n-1}p^{2-2n}.

(29)

Let us see first how this comparison proves the lemma.

Assume

|E|\geq C_{4}p^{n(r-1)+\beta},\qquad C_{4}:=\max\{C_{0}^{\prime},6\}.

Then

H_{E}\geq C_{4}p^{\beta}\geq\max\{C_{0}^{\prime}p^{\beta},\,6p^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor},\,p\}.

Thus, the hypothesis gives

\mathcal{T}_{p,n}^{\times}(h_{E})\leq C_{1}^{\prime}\frac{H_{E}^{3}}{p}.

Therefore

\displaystyle p^{(3n-1)(r-1)}\mathcal{T}_{p,n}^{\times}(h_{E})

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}^{\prime}p^{(3n-1)(r-1)}\frac{H_{E}^{3}}{p}=C_{1}^{\prime}\frac{|E|^{3}}{q}.

Moreover,

\frac{|E|q^{2n-1}p^{2-2n}}{|E|^{3}/q}=\frac{p^{2}}{H_{E}^{2}}\leq 1.

It follows from (
29
) that

\mathcal{N}_{n}^{\times}(E)\leq(C_{1}^{\prime}+1)\frac{|E|^{3}}{q}.

(30)

Arguing as the previous section, we obtain

|\Delta_{n,r}(E)\cap U_{r}|\geq\frac{q}{36(C_{1}^{\prime}+1)}.

It remains to prove (
29
). We use the
conductor decomposition introduced above. For

\chi\in\widehat{U_{r}}
, define

S_{\chi}(\mathbf{x}):=\sum_{\mathbf{y}\in E}\chi(\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}\|).

For
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z}
, multiplicative character orthogonality,
with every character extended by zero away from
U_{r}
, reads

\frac{1}{\varphi(p^{r})}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{U_{r}}}\chi(u)\overline{\chi(v)}=\mathbf{1}_{\{u=v\in U_{r}\}}.

Expanding
\nu_{\mathbf{x}}(j)^{2}
and applying this identity gives

\mathcal{N}_{n}^{\times}(E)=\frac{1}{\varphi(p^{r})}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{U_{r}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in E}|S_{\chi}(\mathbf{x})|^{2}.

(31)

Let
\mathcal{N}_{n,\mathrm{low}}^{\times}(E)
denote the part of
(
31
) coming from characters that
factor through
U_{1}=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
, and let

\mathcal{N}_{n,\mathrm{high}}^{\times}(E)
denote the sum over characters of
exact conductor
p^{s}
,
2\leq s\leq r
. Thus

\mathcal{N}_{n}^{\times}(E)=\mathcal{N}_{n,\mathrm{low}}^{\times}(E)+\mathcal{N}_{n,\mathrm{high}}^{\times}(E).

We first compute the low-conductor term. If

\mathbf{x}\equiv\mathbf{a}\,(\textsf{mod }p)
and
\chi
is induced by

\chi_{1}\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}}
, grouping
\mathbf{y}
by its residue class
gives

S_{\chi}(\mathbf{x})=p^{n(r-1)}\sum_{\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}}h_{E}(\mathbf{b})\chi_{1}(\|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\|).

(32)

There are
p^{n(r-1)}h_{E}(\mathbf{a})
choices of
\mathbf{x}\in E
above

\mathbf{a}
. Substituting (
32
) and using
orthogonality on
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
in the form

\sum_{\chi_{1}\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}}}\chi_{1}(u)\overline{\chi_{1}(v)}=(p-1)\mathbf{1}_{\{u=v\neq 0\}},\qquad u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{p},

we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{n,\mathrm{low}}^{\times}(E)

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{3n(r-1)}}{\varphi(p^{r})}\sum_{\chi_{1}\in\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}}}\sum_{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}}h_{E}(\mathbf{a})\left|\sum_{\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}}h_{E}(\mathbf{b})\chi_{1}(\|\mathbf{a}-\mathbf{b}\|)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{p^{3n(r-1)}(p-1)}{\varphi(p^{r})}\mathcal{T}_{p,n}^{\times}(h_{E})

\displaystyle=p^{(3n-1)(r-1)}\mathcal{T}_{p,n}^{\times}(h_{E}).

(33)

This identity is the precise point at which the finite field energy
enters the ring argument.

We next treat the high-conductor term. Fix
2\leq s\leq r
and a
character
\chi
of exact conductor
p^{s}
. Put

f:=1_{E},\qquad K_{\chi}(\mathbf{z}):=\chi(\|\mathbf{z}\|).

With the normalized convolution from Section 2,

S_{\chi}=q^{n}(f\ast K_{\chi}),\qquad\widehat{S_{\chi}}(\mathbf{m})=q^{n}\widehat{f}(\mathbf{m})\widehat{K_{\chi}}(\mathbf{m}).

Parseval’s identity and
Lemma
6.6
below give

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in E}|S_{\chi}(\mathbf{x})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\mathbf{x}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})}|S_{\chi}(\mathbf{x})|^{2}

\displaystyle=q^{3n}\sum_{\mathbf{m}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r})}|\widehat{f}(\mathbf{m})|^{2}|\widehat{K_{\chi}}(\mathbf{m})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq q^{3n}p^{-sn}\frac{|E|}{q^{n}}=q^{2n}p^{-sn}|E|.

(34)

The number of characters that factor through
U_{s}
is

|\widehat{U_{s}}|=\varphi(p^{s})
. Hence, the number of characters of
exact conductor
p^{s}
is

\varphi(p^{s})-\varphi(p^{s-1})=(p-1)^{2}p^{s-2}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{n,\mathrm{high}}^{\times}(E)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q^{2n}|E|}{\varphi(p^{r})}\sum_{s=2}^{r}(p-1)^{2}p^{s-2-sn}

\displaystyle=|E|q^{2n-1}p^{2-2n}\frac{(1-p^{-1})\bigl(1-p^{-(n-1)(r-1)}\bigr)}{1-p^{-(n-1)}}

\displaystyle\leq|E|q^{2n-1}p^{2-2n}.

(35)

Here we used

\sum_{s=2}^{r}p^{s-2-sn}=p^{-2n}\frac{1-p^{-(n-1)(r-1)}}{1-p^{-(n-1)}},

and the factor multiplying

|E|q^{2n-1}p^{2-2n}
in the middle line of
(
35
) is at most one because
n\geq 2
.
When
r=1
, the high-conductor sum is empty and the same conclusion
holds.

Combining (
33
) and
(
35
) proves
(
29
), and hence the lemma with

C_{4}=\max\{C_{0}^{\prime},6\}
and

c_{4}=1/(36(C_{1}^{\prime}+1))
.
∎

Quadratic Gauss transforms in arbitrary dimensions

We now prove the multiplier estimate used above. The argument is
uniform in the dimension: additive Fourier inversion in the value of
the quadratic form separates the
n
coordinates, and each coordinate
contributes a one-dimensional quadratic Gauss sum.

We first record the scalar multiplicative Gauss sum needed in the
argument. The modulus at a unit frequency is the specialization of

[
23
, Lemma 5.1(ii)]
to
R=\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z}
; we include the
elementary proof because it also gives the required vanishing at
nonunit frequencies.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
s\geq 2
, and let
\theta\in\widehat{U_{s}}
have exact conductor

p^{s}
. For
c\in R_{s}
, define

\mathfrak{G}_{s}(\theta;c):=\sum_{u\in U_{s}}\theta(u)e_{p^{s}}(cu).

Then

\mathfrak{G}_{s}(\theta;c)=\overline{\theta(c)}\,\mathfrak{G}_{s}(\theta;1)\qquad(c\in U_{s}),

whereas

\mathfrak{G}_{s}(\theta;c)=0\qquad(c\in pR_{s}).

Moreover,

\left|\mathfrak{G}_{s}(\theta;1)\right|=p^{s/2}.

Consequently,

\left|\mathfrak{G}_{s}(\theta;c)\right|=\begin{cases}p^{s/2},&c\in U_{s},\\
0,&c\in pR_{s}.\end{cases}

(36)

Proof.

Put

V_{s}:=\ker(U_{s}\longrightarrow U_{s-1})=1+p^{s-1}R_{s}.

Since
\theta
has exact conductor
p^{s}
, its restriction to
V_{s}
is
nontrivial.

Suppose first that
c\in pR_{s}
. Choose
\varepsilon\in V_{s}
with

\theta(\varepsilon)\neq 1
. For every
u\in U_{s}
, one has

c(\varepsilon-1)u=0\qquad\text{in }R_{s}.

Multiplication by
\varepsilon
permutes
U_{s}
, and hence

\mathfrak{G}_{s}(\theta;c)=\theta(\varepsilon)\mathfrak{G}_{s}(\theta;c).

Thus,
\mathfrak{G}_{s}(\theta;c)=0
.

If
c\in U_{s}
, the substitution
v=cu
gives

\mathfrak{G}_{s}(\theta;c)=\overline{\theta(c)}\,\mathfrak{G}_{s}(\theta;1).

It remains to evaluate the modulus at
c=1
. For
a\in R_{s}
, put

C_{s}(a):=\sum_{v\in U_{s}}e_{p^{s}}(av).

Writing the unit sum as the sum over all residue classes minus the sum
over the multiples of
p
gives

C_{s}(a)=p^{s}\mathbf{1}_{\{a=0\text{ in }R_{s}\}}-p^{s-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{a\in p^{s-1}R_{s}\}}.

Writing
u=tv
, we therefore obtain

\displaystyle\left|\mathfrak{G}_{s}(\theta;1)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in U_{s}}\theta(t)C_{s}(t-1)

\displaystyle=\varphi(p^{s})-p^{s-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in V_{s}\\
t\neq 1\end{subarray}}\theta(t).

Since
\theta|_{V_{s}}
is nontrivial,

\sum_{t\in V_{s}}\theta(t)=0.

The last restricted sum is therefore
-1
, and hence

\left|\mathfrak{G}_{s}(\theta;1)\right|^{2}=\varphi(p^{s})+p^{s-1}=p^{s}.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
p
be odd, let
n\geq 2
, let
2\leq s\leq r
, and let

\chi\in\widehat{U_{r}}
have exact conductor
p^{s}
. Define

K_{\chi}(\mathbf{z}):=\chi(\|\mathbf{z}\|),\qquad\mathbf{z}\in R_{r}^{n},

where
\chi
is extended by zero to
R_{r}
.
Then
\widehat{K_{\chi}}(\mathbf{m})=0
unless

\mathbf{m}=p^{r-s}\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\quad\text{for some}\quad\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\in(\mathbb{Z}/p^{s}\mathbb{Z})^{n}.

At such a frequency,

\left|\widehat{K_{\chi}}\bigl(p^{r-s}\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\bigr)\right|=\begin{cases}p^{-sn/2},&\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|\in U_{s},\\
0,&\|\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\|\in pR_{s}.\end{cases}

(37)

In particular,

\|\widehat{K_{\chi}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}((\mathbb{Z}/p^{r}\mathbb{Z})^{n})}\leq p^{-sn/2}.

Proof.

Since
\chi
factors through
U_{s}
, there is a unique

\chi_{s}\in\widehat{U_{s}}
such that

\chi=\chi_{s}\circ\rho_{r,s}.

The exact-conductor hypothesis says that
\chi_{s}
has exact conductor

p^{s}
. In particular,
K_{\chi}
depends only on

\mathbf{z}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{s})
. Summing over the lifts of each residue class
modulo
p^{s}
shows that its Fourier transform vanishes unless

\mathbf{m}=p^{r-s}\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}
. At a frequency of this form, the
normalized coefficient at level
r
is exactly the normalized
coefficient at level
s
formed with
\chi_{s}
. We may therefore
assume that
r=s
and, to simplify notation, write
\chi
for

\chi_{s}
.

Indeed, writing
\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{z}_{0}+p^{s}\mathbf{w}
, the sum over

\mathbf{w}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r-s})
contains the factor

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{w_{i}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{r-s})}e_{p^{r-s}}(-m_{i}w_{i}),

which vanishes unless every
m_{i}
is divisible by
p^{r-s}
. If

\mathbf{m}=p^{r-s}\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}
, the lift multiplicity

p^{n(r-s)}
cancels the corresponding part of the normalization

p^{-rn}
, leaving precisely the normalized level-
s
coefficient.

Let
\eta
denote the quadratic character of
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}
, and also its
lift
\eta\circ\rho_{s,1}
to
U_{s}
. For
a\in U_{s}
, put

g_{s}(a):=\sum_{x\,(\textsf{mod }p^{s})}e_{p^{s}}(ax^{2}).

We first record the elementary evaluation

g_{s}(a)=\eta(a)^{s}g_{s}(1),\qquad|g_{s}(1)|=p^{s/2}.

(38)

Indeed, writing
x=y+p^{s-1}z
, with

y\,(\textsf{mod }p^{s-1})
and
z\,(\textsf{mod }p)
, and summing first in
z
gives

\sum_{z\,(\textsf{mod }p)}e_{p}(2ayz)=\begin{cases}p,&p\mid y,\\
0,&p\nmid y.\end{cases}

Writing
y=py^{\prime}
in the surviving terms therefore gives

g_{s}(a)=p\,g_{s-2}(a\bmod p^{s-2})\qquad(s\geq 2),

where
g_{0}=1
. At level one,

g_{1}(a)=\eta(a)g_{1}(1),\qquad|g_{1}(1)|=p^{1/2},

by the finite field quadratic Gauss-sum calculation. For completeness,
extend
\eta
by
\eta(0)=0
. Since the number of solutions of

x^{2}=t
is
1+\eta(t)
and

\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}e_{p}(at)=0
for
a\neq 0
, one has

g_{1}(a)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}(1+\eta(t))e_{p}(at)=\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\eta(t)e_{p}(at)=\eta(a)\sum_{t\in\mathbb{F}_{p}}\eta(t)e_{p}(t).

If
\tau(\eta):=\sum_{t}\eta(t)e_{p}(t)
, then

\displaystyle|\tau(\eta)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}}\eta(u)\sum_{y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}}e_{p}((u-1)y)

\displaystyle=(p-1)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{*}\\
u\neq 1\end{subarray}}\eta(u)=p.

Thus,
|g_{1}(1)|=p^{1/2}
. Iterating the recurrence proves
(
38
).

Define the primitive multiplicative Gauss sum with positive additive
sign by

\tau_{+}(\overline{\chi}):=\sum_{a\in U_{s}}\overline{\chi(a)}e_{p^{s}}(a)=\mathfrak{G}_{s}(\overline{\chi};1).

The character
\overline{\chi}
also has exact conductor
p^{s}
, and
Lemma
6.5
gives

|\tau_{+}(\overline{\chi})|=p^{s/2}.

If
t\in U_{s}
, the change of variables
w=at
gives

\sum_{a\in U_{s}}\overline{\chi(a)}e_{p^{s}}(at)=\chi(t)\tau_{+}(\overline{\chi}).

If
t\in pR_{s}
, the sum on the left is

\mathfrak{G}_{s}(\overline{\chi};t)
, which vanishes by the same lemma.
Hence, under the zero-extension convention, one has for every

t\in R_{s}
the inversion formula

\chi(t)=\frac{1}{\tau_{+}(\overline{\chi})}\sum_{a\in U_{s}}\overline{\chi(a)}e_{p^{s}}(at).

(39)

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\chi}(\mathbf{m})
denote the unnormalized Fourier transform
of
K_{\chi}
at level
s
. Inserting
(
39
), separating the

n
coordinates, and completing the square in each coordinate, we
obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\chi}(\mathbf{m})

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\mathbf{z}\,(\textsf{mod }p^{s})}\chi(\|\mathbf{z}\|)e_{p^{s}}(-\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{z})

After the substitution

(41)

In particular,

(42)

We use the elementary identity

.

Example 8.1

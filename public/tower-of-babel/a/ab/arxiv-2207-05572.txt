Distributive FCP extensions
Gabriel Picavet and Martine Picavet-L’Hermitte

Abstract
. We are dealing with extensions of commutative rings R ⊆ S R\subseteq S whose chains of the poset [ R , S ] [R,S] of their subextensions are finite ( i.e. R ⊆ S R\subseteq S has the FCP property) and such that [ R , S ] [R,S] is a distributive lattice, that we call distributive FCP extensions. Note that the lattice [ R , S ] [R,S] of a distributive FCP extension is finite. This paper is the continuation of our earlier papers where we studied catenarian and Boolean extensions. Actually, for an FCP extension, the following implications hold: Boolean ⇒ \Rightarrow distributive ⇒ \Rightarrow catenarian. A comprehensive characterization of distributive FCP extensions actually remains a challenge, essentially because the same problem for field extensions is not completely solved. Nevertheless, we are able to exhibit a lot of positive results for some classes of extensions. A main result is that an FCP extension R ⊆ S R\subseteq S is distributive if and only if R ⊆ R ¯ R\subseteq\overline{R} is distributive, where R ¯ \overline{R} is the integral closure of R R in S S . A special attention is paid to distributive field extensions.

[

]

Actually for an FCP extension, the following implications hold: Boo-lean

Our main tool are the minimal (ring) extensions, a concept that was introduced by Ferrand-Olivier

[

Lattices that are catenarian and have a finite length are finite. It follows that the lattice of a distributive FCP extension is such that
[R,S]
is finite; that is, the extension has FIP (for the “finitely many intermediate algebras property”), a property also characterized in

We give a characterization of the distributivity of catenarian extensions.

1.1.
A summary of the main results

Any undefined material
is explained at the end of the section or in the next sections.

Section 2 is devoted to recalls and results on lattices, mainly in the context of FCP and FIP extensions, and to give first properties of distributive extensions. In this respect, we give rules concerning the usual algebraic constructions in commutative ring theory. We prove that an arbitrary Prüfer extension is distributive (Theorem
2.16
). Extensions of Nagata idealizations of modules are examined.

In Section 3, we look at the general properties of FCP distributive ring extensions related to various algebraic situations. A key result is Proposition
3.1

[

, which states that an FCP extension

is distributive if and only if, for any

and

are minimal, then
|[T\cap U,TU]|=4
and if

and

are minimal, then
|[T\cap U,TU]|=4
. We consider pinched FCP extensions, showing that their distributivity is equivalent to the distributivity of each of its paths. We give an application to the Loewy series of some special extensions by means of Boolean extensions. A length
2
extension is distributive if and only if its lattice has less than
4
elements. We also consider
B_{2}
-extensions (Boolean of length
2
), giving a characterization involving minimal extensions and other materials of ring theory recalled in Section 1.

As a consequence of the distributivity of arbitrary Prüfer extensions, we get the first main result: the non trivial Theorem
3.9

is distributive if and only if

is distributive, where

is the integral closure of

. This result allows us to reduce our work to the integral FCP extensions.

The complexity of this study needs three sections to get general results. It is known that there are three types of minimal extensions: inert, decomposed and ramified.

Let
R\subset S
be an FCP extension and
\mathcal{C}
be a maximal (necessarily finite) chain of
R
-subalgebras of
S
,
R=R_{0}\subset R_{1}\subset\cdots\subset R_{n-1}\subset R_{n}=S
. We say that

is

is said an

(IFCP) extension if all minimal subextensions of

are of the same type. It turns out that the subextensions of an FCP extension, determined by its seminormalization and
t
-closure, are IFCP extensions. Their distributivity admits simple characterizations explained in Section 4. If
R\subset S
is a subintegral (resp. seminormal and infra-integral) extension, then Proposition
4.2
(resp.;
4.4
) shows that

is distributive if and only if
R\subset S
is arithmetic (resp.; locally minimal). If the extension is

We then consider the case of a local base ring
R
of an integral FCP extension

. There are two cases determined by the finite maximal spectrum of

|\mathrm{Max}(S)|=1
and the branched case where
|\mathrm{Max}(S)|>1

Locally unbranched extensions are characterized in Section 5 through the

is arithmetic (Theorem

Branched extensions over a local ring are examined in Section 6. Note that when such an extension is distributive, then necessarily

. In
[
35
]
, we studied different types of closures of a ring extension, in particular, the u-closure and the co-subintegral closure. These closures play a main role in the characterization of distributive integral extensions as in Theorem

When an FCP extension

is integral and distributive, we prove in Section 7 that the fiber of each prime ideal of

has at most two elements (Proposition
7.1
). If this last condition is satisfied and in addition the extension is seminormal and infra-integral with a locally maximal conductor, the converse holds and the extension is locally minimal decomposed. Let an extension

In Section 8, we consider numerical properties of a distributive integral FCP (whence FIP) extension

, with the calculation of

.

The most complicated part, namely the t-closed part of an extension, can be reduced to the study of field extensions. This is the object of Section 9. We get simple results for two types of field extensions. Let

be an FCP field extension. If

The paper ends with Section 10 with an application of

2.

The lattice of an FCP extension

In the context of a lattice

An element

An extension

[
10
]
.

(b)

Since an FCP distributive extension is catenarian by Proposition

Proposition 3.1
.

An FCP extension

is distributive if and only if, for any

(1)

If

are minimal, then
|[T\cap U,TU]|=4
.

(2)

If
T\subset TU
and
U\subset TU
are minimal, then
|[T\cap U,TU]|=4
.

Proof.

We recall the result of
[
18
, Theorem, p. 16]
, given in our context: An FCP extension
R\subset S
is distributive if and only if, for any
T,U\in[R,S]
, the following conditions hold:

(1)

If
T\cap U\subset U
and
T\cap U\subset T
are minimal, then
T\cap U\subset TU
is a
B_{2}
-extension.

(2)

If
T\subset TU
and
U\subset TU
are minimal, then
T\cap U\subset TU
is a
B_{2}
-extension.

But, Proposition
2.1
says that an extension

is a
B_{2}
-extension if and only if
\ell[R^{\prime},S^{\prime}]=2
and
|[R^{\prime},S^{\prime}]|=4
. Moreover, in both cases (1) and (2),

implies that
\ell[T\cap U,T]=2
since
[T\cap U,TU]=\{T\cap U,T,U,TU\}
because
T\neq U
. Otherwise,
T=U
implies in both cases

, a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

An FCP extension
R\subset S

We have

is distributive for each

We use Proposition

Assume first that

cannot be both minimal Prüfer by
[
6
, Theorem 6.10]
, since they have the same crucial maximal ideal. So, the only possibility is that
V\subset U
and
V\subset T
are both minimal integral. In particular,
V\subset TU
is integral. If
V\in[R,\overline{R}]
, then
T,U\in[R,\overline{R}]
and
|[T\cap U,TU]|=4
according to Proposition
3.1
because
R\subseteq\overline{R}
is distributive.
Assume that
V\not\in[R,\overline{R}]
, and let
W
be the integral closure of
R\subset V
, so that
W\subset TU
is almost-Prüfer and
V
is the Prüfer hull of this extension. Let
W^{\prime}
be the integral closure of
W\subset TU
. We have the following commutative diagram:

\begin{matrix}{}&{}&{}&{}&W^{\prime}&{}&{}\\
{}&{}&{}&\nearrow&{}&\searrow&{}\\
R&\to&W&{}&{}&{}&TU\\
{}&{}&{}&\searrow&{}&\nearrow&{}\\
{}&{}&{}&{}&V&{}&{}\end{matrix}

Then, there is an order isomorphism
\varphi:[V,TU]\to[W,W^{\prime}]
defined by
\varphi(X):=X\cap W^{\prime}
for any
X\in[V,TU]
, by Lemma
3.6
. Set
T^{\prime}:=\varphi(T)
and
U^{\prime}:=\varphi(U)
. Since
W=\varphi(V)
, we get that
W\subset T^{\prime}
and
W\subset U^{\prime}
are minimal, so that
W=T^{\prime}\cap U^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime}U^{\prime}=\varphi(TU)=W^{\prime}
. But
[W,W^{\prime}]\subseteq[R,\overline{R}]
because
W\subseteq W^{\prime}
is integral as
R\subseteq W
. Hence,
W\subseteq W^{\prime}
is distributive. It follows that
|[W,W^{\prime}]|=4
because of Proposition
3.1
, whence
|[T\cap U,TU]|=4
.
∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FCP extension such that
R\subseteq\overline{R}
is distributive. For any distinct
T,U\in[R,S]
such that
T\subset TU
and
U\subset TU
are minimal, then
|[T\cap U,TU]|=4
.

Proof.

Of course,
T\neq U
.
Set
V:=T\cap U,\ M:=\mathcal{C}(T,TU)
and
N:=\mathcal{C}(U,TU)
. We are going to consider the different possibilities for the extensions
T\subset TU
and
U\subset TU
. Let
V^{\prime}
be the integral closure of
V
in
TU
.

(1)
T\subset TU
and
U\subset TU
are Prüfer minimal.

We claim that
V\subset TU
is Prüfer:
V^{\prime}\subset TU
is Prüfer implies that
T,U\in[V^{\prime},TU]
and we get that
V\subseteq V^{\prime}\subseteq T\cap U=V
, giving
V=V^{\prime}
. So,
V\subset TU
is Prüfer, and then distributive by Theorem
2.16
, which leads to
|[T\cap U,TU]|=4
by Proposition
3.1
.

(2)
T\subset TU
is Prüfer minimal and
U\subset TU
is minimal integral.

As in (1), we have
V^{\prime}\subseteq T
. Moreover,
V\subset U
is not integral, because in this case,
V\subset TU
would be integral, a contradiction with
T\subset TU
is Prüfer. Let
V^{\prime\prime}
be the integral closure of
V
in
U
, so that
V^{\prime\prime}\subseteq V^{\prime}\subseteq T
. But, we also have
V^{\prime\prime}\subseteq U
. To conclude,
V\subseteq V^{\prime\prime}\subseteq T\cap U=V
leads to
V^{\prime\prime}=V
. In particular,
V\subseteq U
is Prüfer and
V\subset TU
is almost-Prüfer, with
U
as Prüfer hull. An application of Lemma
3.6
yields that
N\cap V\in\mathrm{Supp}_{V}(V^{\prime}/V)=\mathrm{Supp}_{V}(TU/U)\subseteq\mathrm{Max}(V)
. Using
[
28
, Proposition 4.26]
, we show that
V\subset T
is minimal integral. Indeed,
V\subset T
is minimal integral if and only if
V\cap U=T\cap U\ (*)
and
VU\subset TU
is minimal integral
(**)
. But
(*)
is nothing but
V=V
while
(**)
is equivalent to
U\subset TU
is minimal integral, which both hold. Then,
V\subset T
is minimal integral,
so that
T
is the integral closure of
V\subset TU
. Because
V\subset TU
is almost-Prüfer and according to
[
34
, Corollary 6.2]
, we have an order-isomorphism
\psi:[V,TU]\to[V,T]\times[T,TU]
defined by
W\mapsto(W\cap T,WT)
. To conclude, we get
|[T\cap U,TU]|=4
.

(3)
T\subset TU
is minimal integral and
U\subset TU
is Prüfer minimal. The proof is the same as in (2) exchanging
T
and
U
.

(4)
T\subset TU
and
U\subset TU
are both minimal integral.

As in (2), let
V^{\prime\prime}
be the integral closure of
V
in
U
. Then,
V^{\prime\prime}\subseteq U
is Prüfer. As
U\subset TU
is integral, it follows that
V^{\prime\prime}\subset TU
is almost Prüfer, with
U
as Prüfer hull. By Lemma
3.6
, we get that
N\cap V^{\prime\prime}\in\mathrm{Supp}_{V^{\prime\prime}}(TU/U)\subseteq\mathrm{Max}(V^{\prime\prime})
, so that
N\cap V\in\mathrm{Max}(V)
since
V\subseteq V^{\prime\prime}
is integral. The same reasoning gives that
M\cap V\in\mathrm{Max}(V)
. A new discussion compares
M\cap V
and
N\cap V
.

(a)
M\cap V\neq N\cap V
.

According to
[
9
, Proposition 5.4]
, we get that
V\subset T
is minimal with crucial maximal ideal
N\cap V
and
V\subset U
is minimal with crucial maximal ideal
M\cap V
. Then, we infer from
[
6
, Lemma 2.7]
that
|[T\cap U,TU]|=4
.

(b)
M\cap V=N\cap V
.

To begin with, we are going to prove that
V\subset TU
is integral and then, use a reasoning similar to the one used in Proposition
3.7
.

(i)
M
and
N
are incomparable.

[
9
, Proposition 6.6(a)]
tells us that
V\subset U
is a minimal extension of the same type as
T\subset TU
, and then an integral extension, and so is
V\subset TU
.

(ii)
M\subset N
.

From
[
9
, Proposition 6.6(b)]
we infer that
V\subset T
is a minimal extension of the same type as
U\subset TU
, and then an integral extension, and so is
V\subset TU
.

(iii)
M=N
.

[
9
, Proposition 5.7]
implies that
M\in\mathrm{Max}(V)
. In particular,
M=(V:TU)
and
V/M
is a field, as
T/M
, so that
V/M\subset T/M
is an FCP field extension, and then an integral extension by
[
6
, Theorem 4.2]
, as
V\subset T
, and then as
V\subset TU
.

(iv)
N\subset M
: see (ii).

Then, in each case,
V\subset TU
is integral. We have the following commutative diagram, where
W
is the integral closure of
R
in
V
, and
W^{\prime}
is the integral closure of
W
in
TU
, so that
W\subseteq V
is Prüfer and
V\subset TU
is integral. Hence
W\subset TU
is almost-Prüfer.

\begin{matrix}{}&{}&{}&{}&W^{\prime}&{}&{}\\
{}&{}&{}&\nearrow&{}&\searrow&{}\\
R&\to&W&{}&{}&{}&TU\\
{}&{}&{}&\searrow&{}&\nearrow&{}\\
{}&{}&{}&{}&V&{}&{}\end{matrix}

Then, there is an order isomorphism
\varphi:[V,TU]\to[W,W^{\prime}]
defined by
\varphi(X):=X\cap W^{\prime}
for
X\in[V,TU]
, by Lemma
3.6
. Set
T^{\prime}:=\varphi(T)
and
U^{\prime}:=\varphi(U)
. Since
W^{\prime}=\varphi(TU)
, we get that
T^{\prime}\subset W^{\prime}
and
U^{\prime}\subset W^{\prime}
are minimal, so that
W^{\prime}=T^{\prime}U^{\prime}
and
T^{\prime}\cap U^{\prime}=\varphi(T\cap U)=\varphi(V)=W
. But
[W,W^{\prime}]\subseteq[R,\overline{R}]
because
W\subseteq W^{\prime}
is integral as
R\subseteq W
. Hence,
W\subseteq W^{\prime}
is distributive. It follows that
|[W,W^{\prime}]|=4
thanks to Proposition

, giving

.
∎

Theorem 3.9
.

An FCP extension
R\subset S
is distributive if and only if
R\subset\overline{R}
is distributive.

Proof.

One implication is obvious. For the converse, use Propositions
3.1
,
3.7
and
3.8
.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

An extension
R\subset S
is said
almost unbranched
if each
T\in[R,\overline{R}[
is a local ring. When
R\subset S
is an almost unbranched FCP extension, the result of Theorem
3.9
immediately follows. Indeed,
[
31
, Corollary 2.6]
shows that
R\subset S
is pinched at
\overline{R}
.
We may also remark that
\overline{R}\subseteq S
is Prüfer since FCP, and then distributive by Theorem
2.16
. It follows that Proposition
3.2
gives the result of Theorem
3.9
.

More generally, if
R\subset S
is a quasi-Prüfer extension pinched at
\overline{R}
, then
R\subset S
is distributive if and only if
R\subseteq\overline{R}
is distributive. Indeed,
[R,S]=[R,\overline{R}]\cup[\overline{R},S]
, with
\overline{R}\subseteq S
Prüfer, and then distributive by Theorem
2.16
.

According to Theorem
3.9
, we can limit our investigation to integral FCP distributive extensions. This is the subject of the following sections. Let
R\subset S
be an integral FCP extension. According to Proposition
2.6
,
R\subset S
is distributive if and only if
R_{M}\subset S_{M}
is distributive for any
M\in\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
.
Most of time, we only give a characterization when
R
is a local ring, because of the complexity of the conditions.

4.
Distributive integral IFCP extensions

We recall that an integral FCP extension is IFCP if all minimal subextensions are minimal of the same type. Let
R\subset S
be an integral FCP extension where
R
is a local ring. A characterization of distributivity is given along the paths of the canonical decomposition of
R\subset S
. In this section, we study the distributivity for these following paths, that is for IFCP integral FCP extensions: subintegral extensions (Proposition
4.2
), seminormal infra-integral extensions (Proposition
4.4
) and t-closed extensions (Proposition
4.5
). The reader is warned that deeper results will be given in the next sections, where the unbranched and branched contexts are separated.

To begin with, the following lemma is needed.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
R\subset S
be an infra-integral distributive FCP extension over the local ring
(R,M)
. Then two different minimal subextensions
R\subset T
and
R\subset U
of
R\subset S
cannot have the same type.

Proof.

Observe that
T\cap U=R
because
R\subseteq T\cap U\subset T,U
. Since
(R,M)
is a local ring and
R\subset S
is integral, then
R\subset S
is
M
-crucial because
\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)\subseteq\mathrm{Max}(R)
by
[
6
, Theorem 3.6]
. Using Proposition
3.1
, we get that
|[R,TU]|=4
, so that
\ell[R,TU]=2
. As
R\subset T
(resp.
R\subset U
) is either ramified or decomposed, Theorem
3.4
shows that the only possible case is that
{}_{TU}^{+}R\neq R,TU
, so that
R\subset T
and
R\subset U
are minimal extensions of different types.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

A subintegral FCP extension
R\subset S
is distributive if and only if it is arithmetic.

Proof.

One implication is Proposition
2.14
. Conversely, suppose that
R\subset S
is distributive. Assuming that
(R,M)
is a local ring by Proposition
2.6
, it is enough to show that
[R,S]
is a chain. Let
R=R_{0}\subset\cdots\subset R_{i}\subset\cdots\subset R_{n}=S
be a maximal chain, where
R_{i-1}\subset R_{i}
is minimal for each
i\in\mathbb{N}_{n}
. We claim that
[R,S]=\{R_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
. Otherwise, there exists
T\in[R,S]\setminus\{R_{i}\}_{i=0}^{n}
. In particular, there exists a greatest
k<n
such that
R_{k}\subset T
, and then some
T_{k}\in[R_{k},T]\setminus\{R_{i}\}_{i=k}^{n}
such that
R_{k}\subset T_{k}
is minimal ramified, as
R_{k}\subset R_{k+1}
, a contradiction by Lemma
4.1
. To conclude,
[R,S]
is a chain.
∎

We get as a corollary a characterization of distributive modules, by using our results gotten for ring extensions. An
R
-module
M
is said
distributive
if the lattice
\llbracket M\rrbracket
of its submodules is distributive. In
[
16
, Definition 1, p.119]
, a submodule
N
of an
R
-module
M
is called
distributive
if
N\cap(N^{\prime}+N^{\prime\prime})=(N\cap N^{\prime})+(N\cap N^{\prime\prime})
for any two submodules
N^{\prime},N^{\prime\prime}
of
M
. Of course,
M
is distributive if and only if any submodule
N
is distributive, which is equivalent, by
[
16
, Proposition 5.2, page 119]
to
\llbracket M_{P}\rrbracket
is a chain, for any
P\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
. We recover this result by a completely different way for modules of finite length (in order to deal with FCP extensions).

Corollary 4.3
.

An
R
-module
M
of finite length is distributive if and only if
\llbracket M_{P}\rrbracket
is a chain, for any
P\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
.

Proof.

Since
M
is of finite length,
[
26
, Proposition 2.2]
shows that
R\subseteq R(+)M
has FCP. According to
[
4
, Remark 2.9]
, there is a bijection
\varphi:\llbracket M\rrbracket\to[R,R(+)M]
defined by
\varphi(N)=R(+)N
for
N\in\llbracket M\rrbracket
. Obviously, for any
N,N^{\prime}\in\llbracket M\rrbracket
, we have
\varphi(N+N^{\prime})=\varphi(N)\varphi(N^{\prime})
and
\varphi(N\cap N^{\prime})=\varphi(N)\cap\varphi(N^{\prime})
. Then,
\varphi
is a lattice isomorphism. In particular,
M
is a distributive module if and only if
R\subseteq R(+)M
is a distributive ring extension. But, in
[
26
, Lemma 2.1]
, we proved that
R\subseteq R(+)M
is a subintegral extension. Then,
R\subseteq R(+)M
is distributive if and only if
R\subseteq R(+)M
is arithmetic according to Proposition
4.2
. Let
P\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
. Then
P(+)M
is the only maximal ideal of
R(+)M
lying over
P
. It follows that
(R(+)M)_{P}=(R(+)M)_{(P(+)M)}=R_{P}(+)M_{P}
, the last equality coming from
[
15
, Corollary 25.5]
. Hence,
R\subseteq R(+)M
is arithmetic if and only if
[R_{P},R_{P}(+)M_{P}]
is a chain, which is equivalent to
\llbracket M_{P}\rrbracket
is a chain. To conclude, we get that
M
is distributive if and only if
\llbracket M_{P}\rrbracket
is a chain, for any
P\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

A seminormal infra-integral FCP extension
R\subset S
is distributive if and only if it is locally minimal.

Proof.

One implication is Proposition
2.14
. Conversely, assume that
R\subset S
is distributive. We can assume that
(R,M)
is a local ring by Proposition
2.6
. Then, it is enough to show that
R\subset S
is minimal. Let
T\in[R,S]
be such that
R\subset T
is minimal decomposed. According to Lemma
4.1
and Proposition
1.8
, there does not exist any
U\in[R,S]\setminus\{T\}
such that
R\subset U
is minimal. If
T\neq S
, there exists some
V\in]T,S]
such that
T\subset V
is minimal decomposed, so that
|[R,V]|=3
and
\ell[R,V]=2
by
[
6
, Lemma 5.4]
, with
R\subset V
infra-integral seminormal according to Proposition
1.8
. But
[
29
, Theorem 6.1 (7)]
shows that
|[R,V]|=5
, a contradiction. To conclude,
T=S
and
R\subset S
is minimal.
∎

As we did in earlier papers for some other properties of t-closed FCP extensions, the case of distributive t-closed FCP extensions can be reduced to the study of distributive finite field extensions. This is shown in the next result. Distributive finite field extensions are examined in Section 8.

Proposition 4.5
.

A t-closed FCP extension is distributive if and only if its residual extensions are distributive.

Proof.

Let
R\subseteq S
be a t-closed FCP extension. By Proposition
2.6
,
R\subseteq S
is distributive if and only if
R_{M}\subseteq S_{M}
is distributive for each
M\in\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
. Hence we can assume that
(R,M)
is a local ring. Then,
M=(R:S)
and
(S,M)
is local by
[
8
, Lemma 3.17]
. Because of the lattice isomorphism
[R,S]\to[R/M,S/M]
defined by
T\mapsto T/M
, we get that
R\subseteq S
is distributive if and only if
R/M\subseteq S/M
is distributive.
∎

5.
Locally unbranched integral distributive FCP extensions

We recall that a ring extension
R\subset S
is called
unbranched
if
\overline{R}
is local; so that, if in addition the extension is integral,
R
is a local ring. When
R\subset S
is quasi-Prüfer and unbranched, all rings in
[R,S]
are local by
[
32
, Lemma 3.29]
. A
locally unbranched
extension is an extension
R\subset S
such that
R_{P}\subset S_{P}
is unbranched for each
P\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)
. A locally unbranched integral FCP extension is nothing but an integral
i
-extension (equivalently, a u-closed extension (Definition
1.5
)).

Theorem 5.1
.

A locally unbranched integral FCP extension
R\subset S
is distributive if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

R\subseteq{}_{S}^{t}R
is arithmetic;

(2)

[{}_{S}^{t}R,S]
is distributive;

(3)

R\subset S
is pinched at
{}_{S}^{t}R
.

Proof.

To begin with, we assume that
R
is a local ring, so that
R\subset S
is unbranched. Then
{}_{S}^{+}R={}_{S}^{t}R
by Corollary
1.10
.

Assume that
R\subset S
is distributive. Then (1) is a consequence of Proposition
4.2
and (2) is obvious. Moreover, in this case,
R\subset S
is catenarian. Then,
[
33
, Theorem 4.13]
gives (3).

Conversely, assume that (1), (2) and (3) hold. Then,
R\subset S
is distributive by Propositions
4.2
and
3.2
.

When
R
is not local, apply the beginning of the proof to
R_{M}\subset S_{M}
for any
M\in\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
, Definition
1.5
and Proposition
2.6
.
∎

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
R\subset S
be a distributive locally unbranched (equivalently u-closed) FCP-extension. If
{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R\to\overline{R}
is locally minimal, then
R\subset S
is arithmetic.

Proof.

By definition,
R\subset S
is arithmetic means that
R_{M}\subset S_{M}
is chained for each
M\in\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
. Then, it is enough to assume that
R
(and then
\overline{R}
) are local rings, and either
{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R=\overline{R}
, or
{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R\subset\overline{R}
is minimal. Moreover,
{}_{\overline{R}}^{+}R={}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R
according to Corollary
1.10
because
\mathrm{Spec}(\overline{R})\to\mathrm{Spec}(R)
is bijective.
By Proposition
4.2
, we know that
[R,{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R]
is a chain. If
{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R=\overline{R}
, then
[R,\overline{R}]
is a chain. Assume that
{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R\neq\overline{R}
. If
{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R=R
, then
R\subset\overline{R}
is minimal and then chained. If
{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R\neq R,\overline{R}
, assume that there exists some
T\in[R,\overline{R}]\setminus([R,{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R]\cup\{\overline{R}\})
. It follows that
R\subset T
is not subintegral. Set
U:={}_{T}^{t}R\in[R,{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R[
. Then
U\neq T
and there exist
V\in[U,{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R]
and
W\in[U,T]
such that
U\subset V
is minimal ramified and
U\subset W
is minimal inert, so that there exist two maximal chains from
U
to
VW
of different lengths by Proposition
2.4
, which is absurd because a distributive extension is catenarian (see Proposition
2.2
). Then,
[R,\overline{R}]=[R,{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R]\cup\{\overline{R}\}
is a chain. Using
[
29
, Lemma 1.5]
, a similar argumentation shows that there does not exist some
T^{\prime}\in[R,S]\setminus([R,\overline{R}]\cup[\overline{R},S])
. Then,
[R,S]
is a chain since so is
[\overline{R},S]
by
[
32
, Proposition 3.30]
.
∎

6.
Branched integral FCP distributive extensions

To get a general characterization of distributive extensions over a local ring, it remains to consider integral FCP extensions over a local ring which are not unbranched. Then, in case
R
is a local ring and
R\subset S
is not unbranched, we say that
R\subset S
is
branched
. In particular, since
|\mathrm{Max}(R)|=1
, it follows that
|\mathrm{Max}(S)|\geq 2
. In this case,
{}_{S}^{t}R\neq{}_{S}^{+}R
because of Proposition
1.8
.

We recall (Definition
1.5
) that for an infra-integral extension
R\subset S
, the u-closure
{}_{S}^{u}R
of
R\subset S
is the least
V\in[R,S]
such that
V\subseteq S
is subintegral.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
R\subset S
be an infra-integral branched FCP extension
such that
[R,{}_{S}^{+}R]
is a chain and
|\mathrm{Max}(S)|=2
. Then
R\subset S
is pinched at
{}_{S}^{u}R\cap{}_{S}^{+}R
with
[R,S]=[R,{}_{S}^{+}R]\cup[{}_{S}^{u}R,S]
and
[R,{}_{S}^{+}R]\cap[{}_{S}^{u}R,S]=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Let
T\in]R,S[
, so that
|\mathrm{Max}(T)|\leq 2
. It follows that
|\mathrm{Max}(T)|=2
if and only if
T\subseteq S
is subintegral if and only if
T\in[{}_{S}^{u}R,S]
. And
|\mathrm{Max}(T)|=1
if and only if
R\subseteq T
is subintegral, or equivalently,
T\in[R,{}_{S}^{+}R]
. To conclude,
[R,S]=[R,{}_{S}^{+}R]\cup[{}_{S}^{u}R,S]
and
[R,{}_{S}^{+}R]\cap[{}_{S}^{u}R,S]=\emptyset
.

We claim that
R\subset S
is pinched at
{}_{S}^{+}R\cap{}_{S}^{u}R
. Let
T\in[R,S]=[R,{}_{S}^{+}R]\cup[{}_{S}^{u}R,S]
. If
T\in[{}_{S}^{u}R,S]
, then
T\in[{}_{S}^{+}R\cap{}_{S}^{u}R,S]

Proposition 6.2
.

An infra-integral branched FCP extension

such that

is a chain.

(2)

.

(3)

, with
\mathrm{MSupp}_{{}_{S}^{u}R}(S/{}_{S}^{u}R):=\{M,M^{\prime}\}
and
\mathrm{MSupp}_{{}_{S}^{u}R}({}_{S}^{t}R/{}_{S}^{u}R):=\{M\}
. Let

.

Proposition 6.15
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FCP integral distributive branched extension such that
{}_{S}^{u}R\neq{}_{S}^{t}R
. Then
R\subset S
is pinched at
{}_{S}^{t}R
if and only if
|\mathrm{MSupp}_{{}_{S}^{u}R}(S/{}_{S}^{u}R)|=1
.

Proof.

Since
R\subset S
is an FCP integral distributive branched extension over the local ring
R
,

|\mathrm{Max}(S)|=|\mathrm{Max}({}_{S}^{t}R)|=|\mathrm{Max}({}_{S}^{u}R)|=2
. Moreover,
{}_{S}^{u}R\neq{}_{S}^{t}R
, so that
|\mathrm{MSupp}_{{}_{S}^{u}R}({}_{S}^{t}R/{}_{S}^{u}R)|=1
according to Proposition
6.2

is not chained.

Assume first that
R\neq{}_{S}^{+}R
and
{}_{S}^{t}R\neq S
. In particular,
R\subset{}_{S}^{t}R
is not pinched at
{}_{S}^{+}R
since
R\subset{}_{S}^{t}R
is not chained. Set
\mathrm{MSupp}_{{}_{S}^{u}R}({}_{S}^{t}R/{}_{S}^{u}R)=:\{M\}
and let

be the other maximal ideal of
{}_{S}^{u}R
.

If

is pinched at
{}_{S}^{t}R
, then
|\mathrm{MSupp}_{{}_{S}^{u}R}(S/{}_{S}^{u}R)|\neq 2
. Otherwise, let

be the splitter of
{}_{S}^{u}R\subset S
at
\{M^{\prime}\}

and

, it follows that

is t-closed, so that

Conversely, assume that

∎

7.
Extensions whose fibers have at most 2 elements

Our interest in extensions described by the title of the section is justified by the following result.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
R\subset S
be an integral distributive FCP extension. Then, for each
M\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)
, the fiber at
M
of
R\subset S
has at most two elements.

Proof.

Let
M\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)
. If
M\not\in\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)
, then
R_{M}=S_{M}
and the fiber at
M
of
R\subset S
has exactly one element. Since
R\subset S
is an integral FCP extension, we have
\mathrm{Supp}(S/R)=\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
. Now, let
M\in\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
. Then, the cardinality of the fiber at
M
of
R\subset S
is
|\mathrm{Max}(S_{M})|
. If
R_{M}\subset S_{M}
is unbranched, then
|\mathrm{Max}(S_{M})|=1
. If
R_{M}\subset S_{M}
is branched, then
|\mathrm{Max}(S_{M})|=2
by Theorem
6.12
.
∎

We sum up results on fibers gotten in the previous sections for an integral extension. First, we give a kind of converse of Proposition
7.1
that is satisfied in the case of integral distributive FCP extension.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
R\subset S
be an integral extension such that for each
P\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)
, the fiber at
P
of
R\subset S
has at most two elements. Then, there do not exist
T,U,V\in[R,S]
such that
T\subset U
and
U\subset V
are minimal decomposed, with
|\mathrm{MSupp}_{T}(V/T)|=1
.

Proof.

Let
M\in\mathrm{MSupp}_{T}(V/T)
and set
P:=M\cap R
.

Since
|\mathrm{MSupp}_{T}(V/T)|=1
, we get that
M=(T:U)
is the only element of
\mathrm{MSupp}_{T}(V/T)
and there exist
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathrm{Max}(U)
such that
M=M_{1}\cap M_{2}
with
M_{1}\neq M_{2}
. Moreover,
M=T\cap(U:V)
according to Lemma
1.4
. Since
U\subset V
is minimal decomposed, we deduce that
(U:V)=M_{i}
for some
i\in\{1,2\}
so that we may assume that
(U:V)=M_{1}
. In the same way, there exist
M_{11},M_{12}\in\mathrm{Max}(V)
such that
M_{1}=M_{11}\cap M_{12}
with
M_{11}\neq M_{12}
. As
(U:V)=M_{1}\neq M_{2}
, we get that
U_{M_{2}}=V_{M_{2}}
and there exists
M_{22}\in\mathrm{Max}(V)
lying above
M_{2}
. Of course,
M_{22}\neq M_{11},M_{12}
, and these three distinct maximal ideals of
V
are lying above
M
, and then also above
P
. They are all in the fiber of
P
in
V
, and there exists at least three maximal ideals of
S
in the fiber of
P
, a contradiction with the assumption.
∎

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
R\subset S
be an integral FCP extension such that for each
P\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)
, the fiber at
P
of
R\subset S
has at most two elements. Set
U:={}_{S}^{u}R
and
V:={}_{U}^{+}R
. Then
V\subseteq U
is locally minimal. For the same reason,
{}_{S}^{+}R\subseteq{}_{S}^{t}R
is locally minimal.

Proof.

Set
T:={}_{S}^{t}R
. According to Definition
1.5
and Proposition
1.7
,
U\subseteq S
is an i-extension and
U\subseteq T
. Moreover,
V\subseteq U
is seminormal infra-integral, so that any maximal chain is composed of minimal decomposed extensions. But for each
P\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)
, the fiber at
P
of
R\subseteq U
has at most two elements and
R\subseteq U
is infra-integral. Let
P\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)
. The fiber at
P
of
R\subseteq V
has exactly one element
Q\in\mathrm{Spec}(V)
and the fiber at
Q
of
V\subseteq U
has at most two elements since
U\subseteq S
is an i-extension. Using Proposition
7.2
and localizing
V\subseteq U
at
P
, we get that
V_{Q}\subseteq U_{Q}
is either an equality or a minimal decomposed extension. To conclude,
V\subseteq U
is locally minimal. In fact, there cannot be some
V_{1}\neq V_{2}\in[V_{Q},U_{Q}]
such that
V_{Q}\subset V_{i}
are both minimal decomposed for
i=1,2
, by
[
9
, Proposition 7.6]
, because in this case, there would be some
U_{i}\neq U_{Q}
in
[V_{Q},U_{Q}]
such that
V_{i}\subset U_{i}
would be minimal decomposed, a contradiction with Proposition
7.2
.

The last result is obvious.
∎

Let
I
be an ideal of a ring
R
. We say that
I
is
locally maximal
if, for each
P\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)
, either
I_{P}=R_{P}
or
I_{P}=PR_{P}
, that is
I_{P}
is the maximal ideal of
R_{P}
.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
R\subset S
be a seminormal infra-integral extension such that
(R:S)
is locally maximal and, for each
P\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)
, the fiber at
P
of
R\subset S
has at most two elements. Then
R\subset S
is a distributive
and locally minimal decomposed extension.

Moreover,
R\subset S
has FCP (and then FIP) if and only if
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
has finitely many elements.

Proof.

Assume first that
(R,M)
is a local ring, so that
(R:S)=M
. Since
R\subset S
is seminormal, then
M
is a radical ideal of
S

[
6
, Lemma 4.8]
, and necessarily an intersection of two maximal ideals
M_{1},M_{2}
of
S
. Otherwise,
M\in\mathrm{Max}(S)
leads to a contradiction, because in this case,
R/M=S/M
since
R\subset S
is infra-integral. Then, using again infra-integrality, we get
R/M\cong S/M_{i}
for
i=1,2
, which gives
S/M\cong S/(M_{1}\cap M_{2})\cong S/M_{1}\times S/M_{2}\cong(R/M)^{2}
. Then,
R\subset S
is a minimal decomposed extension according to Theorem
1.3
, and then distributive by Corollary
2.15
.

Now, we show that if
R\subset S
is a seminormal infra-integral extension such that
(R:S)
is locally maximal and, for each
P\in\mathrm{Spec}(R)
, the fiber at
P
of
R\subset S
has at most two elements, these properties are also satisfied for any extension

R_{M}\subseteq S_{M}
, where
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
.

So, let
M\in\mathrm{Max}(R)
and set
I:=(R_{M}:S_{M})
. First,
R_{M}\subseteq S_{M}
is a seminormal infra-integral extension by
[
37
, Corollary 2.10]
and
[
22
, Proposition 1.16]
with
(R:S)_{M}=MR_{M}
if
R_{M}\neq S_{M}
, which is equivalent to
(R:S)\subseteq M
. If
R_{M}=S_{M}
, then
R_{M}\subseteq S_{M}
is trivially distributive and
M\not\in\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
. Assume now that
R_{M}\neq S_{M}
, so that
M\in\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
. Hence, the fiber at
MR_{M}
of
R_{M}\subset S_{M}
has at most two elements. But,
(R:S)_{M}\subseteq(R_{M}:S_{M})=I
shows, using
[
21
, Lemma 4.7]
, that either
(R:S)_{M}=I\in\mathrm{Max}(R_{M})
or
(R_{M}:S_{M})=I=R_{M}
. In this last case,
R_{M}=S_{M}
, a contradiction. In the first case, it remains to show that for each
P\in\mathrm{Spec}(R_{M})
, the fiber at
P
of
R_{M}\subset S_{M}
has at most two elements. Let
P\in\mathrm{Spec}(R_{M})
. There exists
Q\in\mathrm{Spec}(R),\ Q\subseteq M
such that
P=QR_{M}
. Using
[
1
, Lemme 1, page 35]
, there is a bijection between the prime ideals of
S
lying over
Q
and the prime ideals of
S_{M}
lying over
P
. According to the assumption, for each
P\in\mathrm{Spec}(R_{M})
, the fiber at
P
of
R_{M}\subset S_{M}
has at most two elements. Then, the first part of the proof shows that
R_{M}\subset S_{M}
is a distributive minimal extension, so that
R\subset S
is a distributive locally minimal extension.

Assume that
R\subset S
has FCP (and then FIP because distributive). Then,
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
has finitely many elements by Lemma
1.4
. Conversely, assume that
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
has finitely many elements. Then,
R\subset S
has FCP according
[
6
, Proposition 3.7]
since each
R_{M}\subset S_{M}
is minimal for each
\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)
.
∎

Definition 7.5
.

[
21
, Definitions 1.1 and 1.2 and Proposition 1.6]
A ring extension
R\subset S
is

if there exists some

.

Let

be a u-elementary extension. For each

, the fiber at

of

has at most two elements.

Let

and

. Then

by

, Theorem 2.20]

.

8.
Combinatorics for the integral case

In this section we give the values of
\ell[R,S]
and
|[R,S]|
. Thanks to
[
6
, Theorem 3.6]
and
[
8
, Proposition 4.6]
, we have the equations
|[R,S]|=\prod_{M\in\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)}|[R_{M},S_{M}]|
and
\ell[R,S]=\sum_{M\in\mathrm{MSupp}(S/R)}\ell[R_{M},S_{M}]
.

Therefore, it is enough to get the values when
R
is a local ring, in particular because we have the description of
[R,S]
for a local ring
R
.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
R\subset S
be an integral distributive FCP extension over the local ring
R
. Then,

\ell[R,S]=\ell[R,{}_{S}^{+}R]+\ell[{}_{S}^{t}R,S]+|\mathrm{Max}(S)|-1.

Proof.

Since
R\subset S
is a distributive FCP extension,
R\subset S
is catenarian by Proposition
2.2
. Using the canonical decomposition, we get
\ell[R,S]=\ell[R,{}_{S}^{+}R]+\ell[{}_{S}^{+}R,{}_{S}^{t}R]+\ell[{}_{S}^{t}R,S]
. Moreover,
{}_{S}^{+}R\subseteq{}_{S}^{t}R
being distributive, we have either
{}_{S}^{+}R={}_{S}^{t}R\ (*)
or
{}_{S}^{+}R\subset{}_{S}^{t}R\ (**)
is minimal by Proposition
4.4
; that is, either
\ell[{}_{S}^{+}R,{}_{S}^{t}R]=0
or
\ell[{}_{S}^{+}R,{}_{S}^{t}R]=1
. In case
(*),\ |\mathrm{Max}(S)|=1
by Proposition
1.8
, and in case
(**),\ |\mathrm{Max}(S)|=2
by Theorem
1.3
, which gives the result.
∎

Theorem 8.2
.

Let
R\subset S
be an integral distributive FCP extension over the local ring

. Then,
|\mathrm{Max}({}_{S}^{u}R)|\leq 2
and
|\mathrm{Supp}_{{}_{S}^{u}R}({}_{S}^{t}R/{}_{S}^{u}R)|\leq 1
.

Consider the following cases:

(1)

|\mathrm{Supp}_{{}_{S}^{u}R}({}_{S}^{t}R/{}_{S}^{u}R)|=0
.
Then,
|[R,S]|=\ell[R,{}_{S}^{+}R]+|[{}_{S}^{t}R,S]|+\lambda
, with
\lambda=0
when
{}_{S}^{+}R=T
and
\lambda=1
when
{}_{S}^{+}R\neq T
.

(2)

|\mathrm{Supp}_{{}_{S}^{u}R}({}_{S}^{t}R/{}_{S}^{u}R)|=1
and
|\mathrm{Max}({}_{S}^{u}R)|=2
, in which case we set
\mathrm{Max}({}_{S}^{u}R)=:\{M,M^{\prime}\}
where
\mathrm{Supp}_{{}_{S}^{u}R}({}_{S}^{t}R/{}_{S}^{u}R)=:\{M\}
and denote by
V
the splitter of
{}_{S}^{u}R\subseteq S
at
\{M^{\prime}\}
.

(a) If
|\mathrm{Supp}_{{}_{S}^{u}R}(S/{}_{S}^{u}R)|=2
,
then,

9.
Distributive finite field extensions

According to Proposition
4.5
, the study of the distributivity of a t-closed FCP extension can be reduced to the study of the distributivity of a finite field extension.

We begin with the following lemmata.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
k\subset K\subset L
be a tower of two minimal field extensions, where
k\subset K
is radicial and
K\subset L
is separable. Then
k\subseteq L
is distributive if and only if
|[k,L]|=4
.

If these conditions hold, then
[k,L]=\{k,K,K^{\prime},L\}
where
K^{\prime}
is the separable closure of
k\subseteq L
.

Proof.

Clearly,
k\subset L
is a finite-dimensional field extension. Since
k\subset K
is minimal radicial, its degree is
p:=\mathrm{c}(k)
. Then, we also have
p=[L:K^{\prime}]
, with
K^{\prime}\neq k,K,L
, so that
K^{\prime}\subset L
is minimal and
|[k,L]|\geq 4
.

If
k\subseteq L
is distributive, it is also catenarian, so that
\ell[k,L]=2
, and
[
29
, Theorem 6.1 (8)]
shows that
|[k,L]|=4
because
k\subseteq L
is neither radicial, nor separable, nor exceptional. Proposition
3.1
gives the converse.
∎

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
k\subset L
be a finite field extension,
T
its separable closure and
U
its radicial closure and assume that
U\subset L
is separable. Then:

(1)

There exists an order isomorphism
\varphi:[k,T]\to[U,L]
defined by
\varphi(V):=UV
.

(2)

k\subset T
is distributive if and only if
U\subset L
is distributive.

Proof.

Define
\varphi:[k,T]\to[U,L]
by
\varphi(V):=UV
and

\psi:[U,L]\to[k,T]
by
\psi(V)=V\cap T
.

(1) Let
V\in[U,L]
and let
W\in[k,V]
be the separable closure of
k\subset V
. Then,
W=T\cap V=\psi(V)
. Now,
W\subseteq UW\subseteq V
, with
W\subseteq V
radicial implies that
UW\subseteq V
is also radicial. But
U\subseteq UW\subseteq V
, with
U\subseteq V
separable gives that
UW\subseteq V
is also separable, so that
V=UW=\varphi(W)=\varphi(\psi(V))
; then,
\varphi\circ\psi
is the identity on
[U,L]
.

Now, if
W\in[k,T]
, then
W\subseteq UW\cap T\subseteq T
implies that
W\subseteq UW\cap T
is separable. Since
k\subset U
is radicial, so is
W\subseteq UW
, and also
W\subseteq UW\cap T
. Then,
W=UW\cap T
, and
\psi\circ\varphi
is therefore the identity on
[k,T]
. Since
\varphi
and
\psi
preserve order,
\varphi
is an order isomorphism.

(2) Let
V,V^{\prime}\in[k,T]
and
W,W^{\prime}\in[U,L]
. Obviously,
\varphi(VV^{\prime})=\varphi(V)\varphi(V^{\prime})
, and
\psi(W\cap W^{\prime})=\psi(W)\cap\psi(W^{\prime})
. Assume that
W=\varphi(V)
and
W^{\prime}=\varphi(V^{\prime})
, so that
V=\psi(W)
and
V^{\prime}=\psi(W^{\prime})
. Then,
\varphi(V\cap V^{\prime})=\varphi[\psi(W)\cap\psi(W^{\prime})]=\varphi[\psi(W\cap W^{\prime})]=W\cap W^{\prime}=\varphi(V)\cap\varphi(V^{\prime})
. A similar proof shows that
\psi(WW^{\prime})=\psi(W)\psi(W^{\prime})
. It follows that
k\subset T
is distributive if and only if
U\subset L
is distributive.
∎

Proposition 9.3
.

Let
k\subset L
be an FIP field extension whose separable closure
T
is such that
k\subset T
is distributive. Then
|[U\cap V,UV]|=4
for any
U,V\in[k,L],\ U\neq V
such that
U\cap V\subset U,V
are minimal.

Proof.

Since
k\subset T
is distributive, it is catenarian, and so is
[k,L]
by
[
33
, Proposition 4.18]
.

Set
W:=U\cap V
and
W^{\prime}:=W\cap T
. We discuss along the different types of minimal extensions
W\subset U
and
W\subset V
.

(1) Assume that
W\subset U
and
W\subset V
are both radicial. Then,
W^{\prime}\subseteq W\subset U,V
are radicial, and so is
W^{\prime}\subset UV
. But, by
[
30
, Lemma 4.1]
, a radicial FIP extension is chained, so that
U=V
, a contradiction.

(2) Assume that
W\subset U
is radicial and
W\subset V
is separable, and set
p:=\mathrm{c}(k)
. Then, there is some
x\in U
such that
U=W[x]
, with
x^{p}\in W
. It follows that
UV=V[x]
, with
x^{p}\in V
, so that
V\subset UV
is minimal radicial. Since
k\subset L
is catenarian, we get that
\ell[W,UV]=2
. Then,
[
29
, Theorem 6.1 (8) (c)]
shows that
|[U\cap V,UV]|=4
because
W\subset UV
is neither radicial, nor separable, nor exceptional.

(3) Assume that
W\subset U
and
W\subset V
are both separable, then so is
W\subset UV
by
[
2
, Proposition 8, A.V. 40]
. Let
W^{\prime\prime}
be the separable closure of
W^{\prime}\subseteq UV
. We have the following commutative diagram
(only useful extensions appear):

\begin{matrix}k&\to&W^{\prime}&\to&W&\to&UV&\to&S\\
{}&{}&{}&\searrow&{}&\nearrow&{}&\nearrow&{}\\
{}&{}&{}&{}&W^{\prime\prime}&\to&T&{}&{}\end{matrix}

where
W^{\prime}\subseteq W
is radicial as
W^{\prime\prime}\subseteq UV
, while
W\subset UV
and
W^{\prime}\subseteq W^{\prime\prime}
are separable, so that
W
is the radicial closure of
W^{\prime}\subset UV
. Moreover,
[W^{\prime},W^{\prime\prime}]\subseteq[k,T]
implies that
W^{\prime}\subseteq W^{\prime\prime}
is distributive. Then, an application of Lemma
9.2
to the extension
W^{\prime}\subset UV
shows that
W\subset UV
is distributive, whence,
|[U\cap V,UV]|=4
by Proposition
3.1
.
∎

Lemma 9.4
.

Let
k\subset L
be an FIP distributive field extension and
U,V\in[k,L],\ U\neq V
. If
U,V\subset UV
are minimal separable extensions, then
U\cap V\subset UV
is separable.

Proof.

According to Proposition
3.1
, we have
|[U\cap V,UV]|=4
, so that
U\cap V\subset U,V
are minimal field extension. They are both separable, because if one of them is radicial, then
|[U\cap V,UV]|>4
, since
[U\cap V,UV]
does contain the separable closure of the extension, distinct from
U\cap V,U,V,UV
. It follows that
U\cap V\subset UV
is separable.
∎

Proposition 9.5
.

Let
k\subset L
be an FIP field extension whose separable closure
T
is such that
k\subset T
is distributive. Assume that
U\cap V\subset UV
is separable whenever
U,V\subset UV
are minimal separable for

|[U\cap V,UV]|=4
for any
U,V\in[k,L],\ U\neq V
such that
U,V\subset UV
are minimal.

Proof.

Set
W:=U\cap V
and
W^{\prime}:=W\cap T
, which is the separable closure of
k\subseteq W
. Arguing as in Proposition
9.3
, we discuss along the types of the minimal extensions
U\subset UV
and
V\subset UV
.

(1) Assume that
U\subset UV
and
V\subset UV
are both radicial. According to
[
9
, Proposition 3.8]
,
W\subset U
is radicial, so that
W\subset UV
is radicial.
But, by
[
30
, Lemma 4.1]
, a radicial FIP extension is chained, so that
U=V
, a contradiction.

(2) Assume that
U\subset UV
is radicial and
V\subset UV
is separable. Using again
[
9
, Proposition 3.8]
, we see that
W\subset V
is radicial. Let
T^{\prime}\in[W,V]
be such that
T^{\prime}\subset V
is minimal radicial. Since
k\subset T
is distributive, it is catenarian, and so is
k\subset L
by
[
33
, Proposition 4.18]
. In particular,
\ell[T^{\prime},UV]=2
, and an application of
[
29
, Theorem 6.1 (8) (c)]

|[U\cap V,UV]|=4

(3) Assume that
U\subset UV
and
V\subset UV
are both separable. It follows from the assumption that

is separable. Moreover,
W^{\prime}\subseteq W
is radicial because
W^{\prime}
is the separable closure of

, so that,

is the radicial closure of
W^{\prime}\subset UV
. Let
V^{\prime}
be the separable closure of

is radicial. But,

is separable, then

is the radicial closure of

be the separable closure of

\begin{matrix}k&\to&W^{\prime}&\to&W&\to&V&\to&UV\\

Since

to get the result.
∎

For a field
k
, we denote by
k_{u}[X]
the set of monic polynomials of
k[X]
. Let
L:=k[x]
be a finite separable (whence FIP) field extension of
k
and
f(X)\in k_{u}[X]
be
the
minimal polynomial of
x
over
k
. For any
K\in[k,L]
, we denote by
f_{K}(X)\in K_{u}[X]
the minimal polynomial of
x
over
K
. We set
\mathcal{D}:=\{f_{K}\mid K\in[k,L]\}
. The elements of
\mathcal{D}
have been characterized in
[
30
, Proposition 4.13]
. By
[
30
, Corollary 4.9]
, there is a reversing order bijection
[k,L]\to\mathcal{D}
defined by
K\mapsto f_{K}
, which gives that
\mathcal{D}
is an ordered set for the divisibility and a lattice. In particular,
\sup(f_{K},f_{K^{\prime}})=f_{K\cap K^{\prime}}
and
f_{KK^{\prime}}=\inf(f_{K},f_{K^{\prime}})
for
K,K^{\prime}\in[k,L]
.

Proposition 9.11
.

A finite separable field extension
k\subset L=k[x]
is distributive if and only if
\mathcal{D}
is a distributive lattice.

Proof.

The bijection
\varphi:[k,L]\to\mathcal{D}
defined by
\varphi(K)=f_{K}
gives the result.
∎

In case of Galois extension, the result is simpler.

Proposition 9.12
.

Let
k\subset L=k[x]
be a finite Galois extension. Then
k\subset L
is distributive if and only if
k\subset L
is cyclic.

Proof.

Using the Fundamental Theorem of Galois Theory, there is a reversing order isomorphism between
[k,L]
and the set
\mathcal{G}
of subgroups of the Galois group
G
of the extension. Then,
k\subset L
is distributive if and only if
\mathcal{G}
is distributive if and only if
G
is cyclic (see
[
36
, Example 4.2]
) if and only if
k\subset L
is cyclic.
∎

As we did for finite Boolean separable extensions in
[
30
, Theorem 4.19]
, using the normal closure allows to give a result for distributivity.

Proposition 9.13
.

Let
k\subset L
be a finite separable extension with normal closure
N
. If
k\subset N
is a cyclic extension, then
k\subset L
is distributive.

Proof.

In view of Proposition
9.12
, if
k\subset N
is a cyclic extension, then
k\subset N
is distributive, and so is
k\subset L
by Proposition
2.5
.
∎

(2) Set

10.
Applications of
\Delta
-extensions to distributive extensions

We know (Proposition
2.2
) that a distributive extension is catenarian. More precisely, a distributive extension
R\subseteq S
is obviously
modular
; that is,
T_{1}\cap(T_{2}T_{3})=T_{2}(T_{1}\cap T_{3})
for each
T_{1},T_{2},T_{3}\in[R,S]
such that
T_{2}\subseteq T_{1}
. In an earlier paper
[
31
]
, we studied
\Delta
-extensions. A ring extension
R\subset S
is a
\Delta
-extension
if
T+U\in[R,S]
for any
T,U\in[R,S]
. In particular, a
\Delta
-extension is modular
[
31
, Proposition 3.14]

and then catenarian.
We end this paper by showing the link between
\Delta
-extensions and distributive extensions.

Proposition 10.1
.

A distributive FCP extension
R\subset S
is a
\Delta
-extension if and only if
{}_{\overline{R}}^{t}R\subset\overline{R}
is arithmetic.

Proof.

Obvious by
[
31
, Corollary 4.30]
since a distributive extension is modular.
∎

Corollary 10.2
.

A distributive infra-integral extension is a
\Delta
-extension.

Proof.

Obvious by
[
31
, Theorem 4.18]
since a distributive extension is modular.
∎

Proposition 10.3
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FCP u-closed extension. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

R\subset S
is a distributive
\Delta
-extension;

(2)

R\subset S
is an arithmetic
\Delta
-extension;

(3)

R\subset S
is a distributive arithmetic extension;

Proof.

(1)
\Leftrightarrow
(3)
\Rightarrow
(2) is
[
31
, Proposition 5.4]
. At last, (2)
\Rightarrow
(1) by Proposition
2.14
.
∎

Proposition 10.4
.

Let
R\subset S
be an FCP integral seminormal
\Delta
-extension, where
R
is a local ring. Then,
R\subset S
is distributive if and only if
[R,S]=\{R\}\cup[{}_{S}^{t}R,S]
.

Proof.

Since
R\subset S
is an integral
\Delta
-extension,
[
31
, Theorem 4.11]
asserts that
R\subset{}_{S}^{t}R
is a
\Delta
-extension and
{}_{S}^{t}R\subseteq S
is arithmetic, so that
{}_{S}^{t}R\subseteq S
is distributive by Proposition
2.14
. Then,
R\subset S
is distributive if and only if
[R,S]=\{R\}\cup[{}_{S}^{t}R,S]
by Proposition
6.5
.
∎

We recall that an element
t
of a ring extension
S
is said
quadratic
over
R
if
t
is a zero of a quadratic monic polynomial of
R[X]
.

Proposition 10.5
.

An extension
R\subset R[t]
, where
t
is a quadratic element, and such that
R
is a SPIR, is an FIP distributive extension.

Proof.

In
[
31
, Proposition 5.11]
, we proved that
R\subset R[t]
is an FIP chained extension, then a distributive extension by Proposition
2.14
.
∎

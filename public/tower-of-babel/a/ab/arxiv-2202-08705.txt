Effects of isoscalar- and isovector-scalar meson mixing on neutron star structure
Fan Li, Bao-Jun Cai, Ying Zhou, Wei-Zhou Jiang, Lie-Wen Chen

Abstract
Based on the accurately calibrated interaction FSUGold, we show that including isovector scalar δ \delta meson and its coupling to isoscalar scalar σ \sigma meson in the relativistic mean field (RMF) model can soften the symmetry energy E sym ​ ( n ) E_{\rm{sym}}(n) at intermediate densities while stiffen the E sym ​ ( n ) E_{\rm{sym}}(n) at high densities. We find this new RMF model can be simultaneously compatible with (1) the constraints on the equation of state of symmetric nuclear matter at suprasaturation densities from flow data in heavy-ion collisions, (2) the neutron skin thickness of 208 Pb from the PREX-II experiment, (3) the largest mass of neutron star (NS) reported so far from PSR J0740+6620, (4) the limit of Λ 1.4 ≤ 580 \Lambda_{1.4}\leq 580 for the dimensionless tidal deformability of the canonical 1.4 M ⊙ M_{\odot} NS from the gravitational wave signal GW170817, (5) the mass-radius relation of PSR J0030+0451 and PSR J0740+6620 measured by NICER, and thus remove the tension between PREX-II and GW170817 observed in the conventional RMF model.



I
Introduction

A model-independent extraction of the neutron skin thickness of
208
Pb is reported recently by the updated Lead Radius EXperiment (PREX-II)
(
Adhikari et al. 2021
)
:

\Delta r_{\rm{np}}\equiv r_{n}-r_{p}=0.283\pm 0.071~\rm{fm}^{-3},

(1)

where
r_{n}~({r_{p}})
is the rms radii of the neutron (proton) distribution in the nucleus.
This value means a remarkably thick
\Delta r_{\rm{np}}
for
208
Pb compared to other results, and suggests a very stiff nuclear symmetry energy
E_{\rm{sym}}(n)
around nuclear saturation
density
n_{0}\sim 0.16
fm
-3
. The
E_{\rm{sym}}(n)
is an essential but poorly-known quantity that encodes the isospin dependence of nuclear
matter equation of state (EOS) and plays a key role in nuclear physics and astrophysics
(
Baran et al. 2005
;
Steiner et al. 2005
;
Li et al. 2008
;
Gandolfi et al. 2015
;
Özel & Freire 2016
;
Baldo & Burgio 2016
;
Zhang & Li 2019
)
. Indeed, it has been established that the symmetry energy density slope
L(n)
around
n_{0}
exhibits a strong positive linear correlation with the
\Delta r_{\rm{np}}
of finite nuclei
(
Brown 2000
;
Furnstahl 2002
;
Chen et al. 2005
;
Centelles et al. 2009
;
Chen et al. 2010
;
Roca-Maza et al. 2011
)
. In particular, there is an even stronger linear correlation between the
\Delta r_{\rm{np}}
and the
L(n_{c})
at a subsaturation cross density
n_{c}=0.11/0.16n_{0}\approx 2/3n_{0}
(roughly corresponding to the average density of finite nuclei), and the
\Delta r_{\rm{np}}
of heavy nuclei can be uniquely determined by
L(n_{c})

(
Zhang & Chen 2013
)
. The large
\Delta r_{\rm{np}}
of
208
Pb from PREX-II requires
L(n_{c})\gtrsim 52
MeV
(
Yue et al. 2021
)
or
L(2/3n_{0})\gtrsim 49
MeV
(
Reed et al. 2021
)
, suggesting a quite stiff
E_{\rm{sym}}(n)
at subsaturation density.

The stiff
E_{\rm{sym}}(n)
usually results in a large value of neutron star (NS) radius and tidal deformability. Using the
\Delta r_{\rm{np}}=0.283\pm 0.071~\rm{fm}^{-3}
of
208
Pb from PREX-II, Reed
et al.

(
Reed et al. 2021
)
have recently obtained a constraint of
642\leqslant\Lambda_{1.4}\leqslant 955
for the dimensionless tidal deformability of 1.4
M_{\odot}
NS within the non-linear relativistic mean field (RMF) model.
However, a limit of
\Lambda_{1.4}

\leq 580
has been extracted from the gravitational wave signal GW170817
(
Abbott et al. 2018
)
, indicating a serious tension between the PREX-II experiment and GW170817 in the RMF model, although this tension has not been observed in the non-relativistic energy density functional with the extended Skyrme interactions including the momentum dependence of effective many-body forces
(
Yue et al. 2021
)
.
Also, we note that the tension between PREX-II and GW170817 can be ameliorated in Bayesian analysis with parameterized EOSs of nuclear matter and NS matter
(
Biswas 2021
)
.
On the other hand, in the terrestrial lab, the EOS of symmetric
nuclear matter (SNM) at suprasaturation densities has been constrained
by collective flow data in heavy-ion collisions (HIC)
(
Danielewicz et al. 2002
)
, which strongly restricts the NS maximum mass
M_{\rm max}

with
M_{\rm max}\sim 2.3M_{\odot}

(
Zhou et al. 2019
;
Zhou & Chen 2019
;
Cai & Chen 2017
)
.
In addition, the heaviest NS with mass
2.08^{+0.07}_{-0.07}M_{\odot}
observed so far for PSR J0740+6620
(
Cromartie et al. 2020
;
Fonseca et al. 2021
)
can put strong constraints on the dense matter EOS and rule out the supersoft high density symmetry energy
(
Zhou & Chen 2019
)
.
Furthermore, the simultaneous mass-radius (M-R) determinations from Neutron Star Interior Composition Explorer (NICER) for PSR J0030+0451
(
Riley et al. 2019
;
Miller et al. 2019
)
with mass around
1.4M_{\odot}
as well as for PSR J0740+6620
(
Riley et al. 2021
;
Miller et al. 2021
)
with mass around
2.0M_{\odot}
have been obtained, which further put stringent constraints on the symmetry energy.
With so many constraints/data in the multimessenger era, it is of extreme importance to test the applicability of nuclear energy density functionals, including relativistic and non-relativistic models.

The RMF models are covariant effective theory and this relativistic covariant formulation has made great success during the last decades in understanding many nuclear phenomena (see, e.g., Refs.
(
Serot & Walecka 1986
;
Serot & Walecka 1997
;
Meng et al. 2006
)
).
Based on the Walecka model
(
Walecka 1974
)
and its extensions
(
Boguta & Bodmer 1977
;
Serot 1979
;
Müller & Serot 1996
)
, the RMF models usually involve the Dirac nucleons, the isoscalar scalar meson
\sigma
, isovector scalar meson
\omega
and isovector vector meson
\rho
. In addition, the
\sigma
meson self-interactions were
used
(
Boguta & Bodmer 1977
)
to reduce the nuclear incompressibility coefficient of symmetric nuclear matter while the
\omega
meson self-interactions were introduced to efficiently tune the NS maximum mass without sacrificing the agreement with other well-reproduced
observables
(
Müller & Serot 1996
)
.
Furthermore, the crossing term
\rho
-
\omega
is highly sensitive to the density dependence of the symmetry energy and is important for the properties of neutron-rich nuclei as well as the structure and cooling dynamics of
NSs
(
Horowitz &
Piekarewicz 2001a
;
Horowitz &
Piekarewicz 2001b
;
Carriere et al. 2003
)
.

According to relativistic covariance in the effective theory,
the isovector scalar meson
\delta
should also be included in the Lagrangian of the RMF model, and it may play an important role in understanding the properties of isospin asymmetric matter at high densities
(
Kubis & Kutschera 1997
;
Hofmann et al. 2001
;
Liu et al. 2002
;
Chen et al. 2007
)
.
Very recently, the coupling of
\delta
meson with
\sigma
meson has been studied by Zabari
et al.

(
Zabari et al. 2019a
;
Zabari et al. 2019b
)
, and it is found that the
\delta
-
\sigma
mixing can soften the
E_{\rm{sym}}(n)
at intermediate densities but stiffen the
E_{\rm{sym}}(n)
at high densities. This interesting feature provides the possibility to decrease the
\Lambda_{1.4}
to be compatible with the constraint of
\Lambda_{1.4}\leq 580

(
Abbott et al. 2018
)
since the
\Lambda_{1.4}
is essentially controlled by the density behaviors of
E_{\rm{sym}}(n)
at intermediate densities around
2n_{0}
.
Nevertheless, we note that
the parameter sets used in Ref.
(
Zabari et al. 2019a
)
violate the constraints on the EOS of SNM at suprasaturation densities from flow data in heavy-ion collisions
(
Danielewicz et al. 2002
)
, predict a
\Lambda_{1.4}
larger than
580
. Also in Ref.
(
Zabari et al. 2019a
)
, the
\omega
meson self-interactions and the crossing term
\rho
-
\omega
are not considered. A similar study has been performed very recently in Ref.
(
Miyatsu et al. 2022
)
.

In this work, based on the accurately calibrated interaction FSUGold
(
Todd-Rutel & Piekarewicz 2005
)
, we include the
\delta
meson and the crossing term
\delta
-
\sigma
in the RMF model, and find that the
\delta
-
\sigma
mixing can soften the
E_{\rm{sym}}(n)
at intermediate densities while stiffen the
E_{\rm{sym}}(n)
at high densities. Our results indicate that the new RMF model can remove the tension between the PREX-II and GW170817 in the conventional RMF model, and at the same time it is compatible with
the constraints on the EOS of SNM at suprasaturation densities from flow data in HIC, the largest NS mass reported so far from PSR J0740+6620, and the M-R relation of PSR J0030+0451 and PSR J0740+6620 measured by NICER.

II
Formalism

The EOS of isospin asymmetric matter, defined by its binding energy per nucleon, can be expressed as

E(n,\alpha)=E_{0}(n)+E_{\rm{sym}}(n)\alpha^{2}+O(\alpha^{4})

(2)

where
n=n_{n}+n_{p}
is the baryon density with
n_{n}
(
n_{p}
) denoting the neutron (proton) density,
\alpha=(n_{n}-n_{p})/(n_{n}+n_{p})
is the isospin asymmetry,
E_{0}(n)=E(n,\alpha=0)
is the EOS of SNM, and the symmetry energy
E_{\rm{sym}}(n)
is defined as

E_{\rm{sym}}(n)=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}E(n,\alpha)}{\partial\alpha^{2}}\mid_{\alpha=0}.

(3)

At nuclear matter saturation density
n_{0}
, the
E_{0}(n)
can be expanded in
\chi\equiv(n-n_{0})/3n_{0}
as

E_{0}(n)=E_{0}(n_{0})+\frac{K_{0}}{2!}\chi^{2}+\frac{J_{0}}{3!}\chi^{3}+O(\chi^{4})

(4)

where
E_{0}(n_{0})
is the binding energy per nucleon in SNM at
n_{0}
,
K_{0}=9n^{2}_{0}\frac{\partial^{2}E_{0}(n)}{\partial n^{2}}\mid_{n=n_{0}}
is the incompressibility coefficient, and
J_{0}=27n^{3}_{0}\frac{\partial^{3}E_{0}(n)}{\partial n^{3}}\mid_{n=n_{0}}
is the skewness coefficient. Similarly, the
E_{\rm{sym}}(n)
can be expanded at a reference density
n_{r}
as

E_{\rm{sym}}(n)=E_{\rm{sym}}(n_{r})+L(n_{r})\chi_{r}+O(\chi_{r}^{2})

(5)

with
\chi_{r}=(n-n_{r})/(3n_{r})
, and the symmetry energy density slope
L(n_{r})
at
n_{r}
can be obtained as

L(n_{r})=3n_{r}\frac{\partial E_{\rm{sym}}(n)}{\partial n}\mid_{n=n_{r}}.

(6)

The conventional
L\equiv L(n_{0})
is recovered by setting
n_{r}=n_{0}
.
In particular, the
L_{c}\equiv L(n_{c})
is obtained at a subsaturation cross density
n_{c}=0.11/0.16n_{0}
, which essentially determines the neutron skin thickness of
208
Pb
(
Zhang & Chen 2013
)
.

Based on the accurately calibrated interaction FSUGold
(
Todd-Rutel & Piekarewicz 2005
)
, we consider here the isovector scalar
\delta
meson and its coupling to the isoscalar scalar
\sigma
meson in the RMF model. The Lagrangian density
\mathcal{L}
of the nucleon system is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\psi}\left(i\partial_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}-m\right)\psi

(7)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle g_{\sigma}\sigma\overline{\psi}\psi-g_{\omega}\omega_{\mu}\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\psi-g_{\rho}\vec{\rho}_{\mu}\overline{\psi}\gamma^{\mu}\vec{\tau}\psi+g_{\delta}\vec{\delta}\overline{\psi}\tau\psi

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{\mu}\sigma\partial^{\mu}\sigma-m_{\sigma}^{2}\sigma^{2}\right)-\frac{1}{3}b_{\sigma}m\left(g_{\sigma}\sigma\right)^{3}-\frac{1}{4}c_{\sigma}\left(g_{\sigma}\sigma\right)^{4}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\omega_{\mu\nu}\omega^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}m_{\omega}^{2}\omega_{\mu}\omega^{\mu}+\frac{1}{4}c_{\omega}\left(g_{\omega}^{2}\omega_{\mu}\omega^{\mu}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\rho_{\mu\nu}\rho^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}m_{\rho}^{2}\vec{\rho}_{\mu}\vec{\rho}^{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{V}(g_{\rho}^{2}\vec{\rho}_{\mu}\vec{\rho}^{\mu})(g_{\omega}^{2}\omega_{\mu}\omega^{\mu})

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{\mu}\vec{\delta}\partial^{\mu}\vec{\delta}-m_{\delta}^{2}\vec{\delta}^{2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}C_{\delta\sigma}(g_{\sigma}^{2}\sigma^{2})(g_{\delta}^{2}\vec{\delta}^{2})

where
m
is the nucleon mass,
m_{\sigma}
,
m_{\omega}
,
m_{\rho}
and
m_{\delta}
are the meson masses, and
g_{\sigma}
,
g_{\omega}
,
g_{\rho}
and
g_{\delta}
represent coupling constants for nucleons with the corresponding mesons. In addition,
b_{\sigma}
and
c_{\sigma}
are dimensionless for the self-interaction of the
\sigma
meson,
c_{\omega}
is for the self-interaction of the
\omega
meson,
\Lambda_{V}
is the coupling coefficient between
\rho
meson and
\omega
meson, and
C_{\delta\sigma}
is the coupling coefficient between
\sigma
meson and
\delta
meson. We note that the Lagrangian density of the FSUGold interaction (without including the electromagnetic interactions) can be obtained by removing the terms associated with
\delta
meson in Eq. (
7
).

With the standard Euler-Lagrange equation

\partial_{\mu}(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial(\partial_{\mu}\phi)})-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\phi}=0,

(8)

one can obtain equations of motion for
the nucleon and meson fields from the Lagrangian density. The resulting Dirac equation for the nucleon field is

[\gamma^{\mu}(i\partial_{\mu}+\Sigma_{\mu}^{J})-(m+\Sigma_{s}^{J})]\Psi=0

(9)

with the nucleon scalar self-energy and vector self-energy given, respectively, by

\displaystyle\Sigma_{s}^{J}=

\displaystyle-g_{\sigma}\sigma-g_{\delta}\vec{\delta}\cdot\vec{\tau},

(10a)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\mu}^{J}=

\displaystyle g_{\omega}\omega_{\mu}+g_{\rho}\vec{\rho}_{\mu}\cdot\vec{\tau}.

(10b)

The equations of motion for the meson fields can be obtained as

\displaystyle(\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}+m^{2}_{\sigma})\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{\sigma}[\overline{\Psi}\Psi-b_{\sigma}M(g_{\sigma}\sigma)^{2}-c_{\sigma}(g_{\sigma}\sigma)^{3}

(11)

\displaystyle+C_{\delta\sigma}(g_{\sigma}\sigma)(g_{\delta}\vec{\delta})^{2}],

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\omega^{\mu\nu}+m^{2}_{\omega}\omega^{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{\omega}[\overline{\Psi}\gamma^{\nu}\Psi-c_{\omega}g_{\omega}^{3}\omega_{\mu}\omega^{\mu}\omega^{\nu}

(12)

\displaystyle-\Lambda_{V}(g_{\omega}\omega^{\nu})g_{\rho}^{2}\vec{\rho}_{\mu}\vec{\rho}^{\mu}],

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\vec{\rho}^{\mu\nu}+m^{2}_{\rho}\vec{\rho}^{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{\rho}[\overline{\Psi}\gamma^{\nu}\vec{\tau}\Psi-\Lambda_{V}(g_{\rho}\vec{\rho}^{\nu})g_{\omega}^{2}\omega_{\mu}\omega^{\mu}],

(13)

\displaystyle(\partial_{\mu}\partial^{\mu}+m^{2}_{\delta})\vec{\delta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{\delta}[\overline{\Psi}\vec{\tau}\Psi+C_{\delta\sigma}(g_{\delta}\vec{\delta})(g_{\sigma}\sigma)^{2}].

(14)

For a static and homogenous infinite nuclear matter, all derivative terms drop out and the expectation values of spacelike components of vector fields vanish (only zero components
\vec{\rho}_{0}
and
\omega_{0}
survive) due to translational invariance and rotational
symmetry of the nuclear matter. In addition, only the third component of isovector fields (
\delta^{(3)}
and
\rho^{(3)}
) needs to be considered because of the rotational invariance around the third axis in the isospin space. In the mean-field approximation, meson fields are replaced by their expectation values, i.e.,
\sigma\rightarrow\overline{\sigma}
,
\omega_{\mu}\rightarrow\overline{\omega}_{0}
,
\rho_{\mu}\rightarrow\overline{\rho}^{(3)}_{0}
, and
\delta\rightarrow\overline{\delta}^{(3)}
, and the equations of motion for the meson fields are reduced to

\displaystyle m^{2}_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{\sigma}[n^{s}-b_{\sigma}M(g_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma})^{2}-c_{\sigma}(g_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma})^{3}

(15)

\displaystyle+C_{\delta\sigma}(g_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma})(g_{\delta}\overline{\delta}^{(3)})^{2}],

\displaystyle m^{2}_{\omega}\overline{\omega}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{\omega}[n-c_{\omega}(g_{\omega}\overline{\omega}_{0})^{3}-\Lambda_{V}(g_{\omega}\overline{\omega}_{0})(g_{\rho}\overline{\rho}^{(3)}_{0})^{2}],

(16)

\displaystyle m^{2}_{\rho}\overline{\rho}^{(3)}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{\rho}[(n_{p}-n_{n})-\Lambda_{V}(g_{\rho}\overline{\rho}^{(3)}_{0})(g_{\omega}\overline{\omega}_{0})^{2}],

(17)

\displaystyle m^{2}_{\delta}\overline{\delta}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{\delta}[(n^{s}_{p}-n^{s}_{n})+C_{\delta\sigma}(g_{\delta}\overline{\delta}^{(3)})(g_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma})^{2}],

(18)

where the nucleon scalar density
n^{s}
and vector density are defined as

\displaystyle n^{s}=

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\Psi}\Psi\rangle=n^{s}_{n}+n^{s}_{p},

(19a)

\displaystyle n=

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\Psi}\gamma^{0}\Psi\rangle=n_{n}+n_{p}

(19b)

with neutron scalar density
n^{s}_{n}
and proton scalar density
n^{s}_{p}
given by

\displaystyle n^{s}_{J}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{3}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{k^{J}_{F}}\frac{m^{\ast}_{J}}{\sqrt{(\vec{k})^{2}+(m^{\ast}_{J})^{2}}}\,d\vec{k}

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m^{\ast}_{J}}{2\pi^{2}}[k^{J}_{F}\epsilon^{\ast,J}_{F}-(m^{\ast}_{J})^{2}\ln\frac{k^{J}_{F}+\epsilon^{\ast,J}_{F}}{m^{\ast}_{J}}],J=n,p,

where
\epsilon^{\ast,J}_{F}
and
k^{J}_{F}
are expressed as

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\ast,J}_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{(k^{J}_{F})^{2}+(m^{\ast}_{J})^{2}},

\displaystyle k^{J}_{F}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{F}(1+\tau^{J}_{3}\alpha)^{\frac{1}{3}},k_{F}=(\frac{3}{2}\pi^{2}n)^{\frac{1}{3}}

(21)

with
k_{F}
being the Fermi momentum of SNM. The effective (Dirac) masses of the neutron
m^{\ast}_{n}
and proton
m^{\ast}_{p}
can be expressed as

m^{\ast}_{J}=m-g_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma}-g_{\delta}\overline{\delta}^{(3)}\tau^{J}_{3},J=n,p.

(22)

In addition, the nucleon scalar self-energy and vector self-energy are given by

\displaystyle\Sigma_{s}^{J}=

\displaystyle-g_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma}-g_{\delta}\overline{\delta}^{(3)}\tau^{J}_{3},

(23a)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\mu}^{J}=

\displaystyle g_{\omega}\overline{\omega}_{0}+g_{\rho}\overline{\rho}^{(3)}_{0}\tau^{J}_{3}

(23b)

with
\tau_{3}=1(-1)
for protons (neutrons).

The set of coupled equations for the nucleon and meson
fields can be solved self-consistently using the iteration
method, and the properties of the nuclear matter can then
be obtained from these fields. According to the energy-momentum tensor

\mathcal{T}^{\mu\nu}=\sum_{\phi_{a}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{T}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}\phi_{a}}\partial^{\nu}\phi_{a}-\mathcal{T}g^{\mu\nu},

(24)

the energy density
\epsilon
and the pressure
P
can be obtained as

\displaystyle\epsilon

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{T}^{00}\rangle=\epsilon^{\rm{kin}}_{p}+\epsilon^{\rm{kin}}_{n}

(25)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}m^{2}_{\sigma}(\overline{\sigma})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}_{\omega}(\overline{\omega}_{0})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}_{\rho}(\overline{\rho}^{(3)}_{0})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}_{\delta}(\overline{\delta}^{(3)})^{2}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}mb_{\sigma}(g_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma})^{3}+\frac{1}{4}c_{\sigma}(g_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma})^{4}+\frac{3}{4}c_{\omega}(g_{\omega}\overline{\omega}_{0})^{4}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}\Lambda_{V}(g_{\rho}\overline{\rho}^{(3)}_{0})^{2}(g_{\omega}\overline{\omega}_{0})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}C_{\delta\sigma}(g_{\delta}\overline{\delta}^{(3)})^{2}(g_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma})^{2}

and

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\langle\mathcal{T}^{jj}\rangle=P^{\rm{kin}}_{p}+P^{\rm{kin}}_{n}

(26)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}m^{2}_{\sigma}(\overline{\sigma})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}_{\omega}(\overline{\omega}_{0})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}_{\rho}(\overline{\rho}^{(3)}_{0})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}m^{2}_{\delta}(\overline{\delta}^{(3)})^{2}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}mb_{\sigma}(g_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma})^{3}-\frac{1}{4}c_{\sigma}(g_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma})^{4}+\frac{1}{4}c_{\omega}(g_{\omega}\overline{\omega}_{0})^{4}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Lambda_{V}(g_{\rho}\overline{\rho}^{(3)}_{0})^{2}(g_{\omega}\overline{\omega}_{0})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}C_{\delta\sigma}(g_{\delta}\overline{\delta}^{(3)})^{2}(g_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma})^{2},

where
\epsilon^{\rm{kin}}_{J}
and
P^{\rm{kin}}_{J}
are the kinetic
contributions to the energy densities and pressure of the nuclear matter, respectively, and they are given as follow

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\rm{kin}}_{J}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{(2\pi)^{3}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{k^{J}_{F}}\sqrt{(\vec{k})^{2}+(m^{\ast}_{J})^{2}}\,d\vec{k}

(27)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\pi)^{2}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{k^{J}_{F}}k^{2}\sqrt{k^{2}+(m^{\ast}_{J})^{2}}\,dk

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}[3\epsilon^{\ast,J}_{F}n_{J}+m^{\ast}_{J}n^{s}_{J}]\,,J=n,p

and

\displaystyle P^{\rm{kin}}_{J}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{3(2\pi)^{3}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{k^{J}_{F}}\frac{(\vec{k})^{2}}{\sqrt{(\vec{k})^{2}+(m^{\ast}_{J})^{2}}}\,d^{3}k

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}[\epsilon^{\ast,J}_{F}n_{J}-m^{\ast}_{J}n^{s}_{J}]\,,J=n,p.

The binding energy per nucleon can be obtained from the
energy density via

E=E(n,\alpha=0)=\frac{\epsilon}{n}-m,

(29)

and the analytical expressions of
E_{\rm sym}(n)
and

can be obtained from their definitions Eqs. (

) and (

\displaystyle E_{\rm{sym}}(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k^{2}_{F}}{6\epsilon^{\ast}_{F}}+\frac{1}{2}(\frac{g_{\rho}}{m^{\ast}_{\rho}})^{2}n

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\frac{g_{\delta}}{m^{\ast}_{\delta}})^{2}\frac{(m^{\ast}_{0})^{2}n}{(\epsilon^{\ast}_{F})^{2}[1+(\frac{g_{\delta}}{m^{\ast}_{\delta}})^{2}A(k_{F},m^{\ast}_{0})]}

\displaystyle L(n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k^{2}_{F}[(\epsilon^{\ast}_{F})^{2}+(m^{\ast}_{0})^{2}]}{6(\epsilon^{\ast}_{F})^{3}}+\frac{g^{2}_{\sigma}(m^{\ast}_{0})^{2}k^{2}_{F}n}{2Q_{\sigma}(\epsilon^{\ast}_{F})^{4}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{3g^{2}_{\rho}n}{2Q_{\rho}}-\frac{3g^{3}_{\omega}g^{4}_{\rho}\Lambda_{V}\overline{\omega}_{0}n^{2}}{Q_{\omega}Q^{2}_{\rho}}

\displaystyle\frac{3g_{\sigma}g^{2}_{\delta}(m^{\ast}_{0})^{2}n^{2}}{2(\epsilon^{\ast}_{F})^{3}Q_{\sigma}Q_{\delta}}[\frac{2g_{\sigma}(m^{\ast}_{0})^{2}}{(\epsilon^{\ast}_{F})^{2}}+\frac{m^{\ast}_{0}\Gamma}{Q_{\delta}}-2g_{\sigma}]

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{3g^{2}_{\delta}(m^{\ast}_{0})^{2}n}{2(\epsilon^{\ast}_{F})^{2}Q_{\delta}}[1-\frac{2k^{2}_{F}}{3(\epsilon^{\ast}_{F})^{2}}-\frac{n\Phi}{2Q_{\delta}}]

where the
\Gamma
and
\Phi
are defined as

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3g_{\sigma}g^{2}_{\delta}[\frac{2n^{s}_{0}}{(m^{\ast}_{0})^{2}}-\frac{3n}{m^{\ast}_{0}\epsilon^{\ast}_{F}}+\frac{m^{\ast}_{0}n}{(\epsilon^{\ast}_{F})^{3}}]+2C_{\delta\sigma}g^{2}_{\delta}g^{2}_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma},

\displaystyle\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2g^{2}_{\delta}k^{2}_{F}}{(\epsilon^{\ast}_{F})^{3}}

(32)

and

\displaystyle Q_{\sigma}=

\displaystyle m^{2}_{\sigma}+g^{2}_{\sigma}A(k_{F},m^{\ast}_{0})+2mb_{\sigma}g^{3}_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma}+3c_{\sigma}g^{4}_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma}^{2}

(33a)

\displaystyle Q_{\omega}=

\displaystyle m^{2}_{\omega}+3c_{\omega}g^{4}_{\omega}\overline{\omega}_{0}^{2}

(33b)

\displaystyle Q_{\rho}=

\displaystyle(m^{\ast}_{\rho})^{2}=m^{2}_{\rho}+\Lambda_{V}g^{2}_{\rho}g^{2}_{\omega}\overline{\omega}_{0}^{2}

(33c)

\displaystyle Q_{\delta}=

\displaystyle(m^{\ast}_{\delta})^{2}+g^{2}_{\delta}A(k_{F},m^{\ast}_{0})

(33d)

In addition, the
(m^{\ast}_{\delta})^{2}
and
A(k_{F},m^{\ast}_{0})
are given by

\displaystyle(m^{\ast}_{\delta})^{2}=m^{2}_{\delta}-C_{\delta\sigma}g^{2}_{\delta}g^{2}_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma}^{2}

(34)

and

\displaystyle A(k_{F},m^{\ast}_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{(2\pi)^{3}}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{k_{F}}\frac{(\vec{k})^{2}}{\sqrt{[(\vec{k})^{2}+(m^{\ast}_{0})^{2}]^{\frac{3}{2}}}}\,d^{3}k

(35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 3(\frac{n^{s}}{m^{\ast}_{0}}-\frac{n}{\epsilon^{\ast}_{F}})

with
\epsilon^{\ast}_{F}=\sqrt{k^{2}_{F}+(m^{\ast}_{0})^{2}}
and
m^{\ast}_{0}=m-g_{\sigma}\overline{\sigma}
is the nucleon Dirac mass in symmetric nuclear matter.

The analytical expressions of
E_{\rm{sym}}(n)
(Eq. (
30
)) and
L(n)
(Eq. (
31
)) are very useful and convenient for exploring the properties of asymmetric nuclear matter. We note that the expression Eq. (
31
) is an extension of the analytical expression for
L(n)
in the nonlinear RMF model (e.g., the FSUGold model) in Refs.
(
Cai & Chen 2012a
)
by considering the contribution of
\delta
-
\sigma
mixing.

For NSs, we adopt here the conventional NS model, i.e., the NS contains core, inner crust and outer crust, and the core is assumed to be composed of only neutrons, protons, electrons and possible muons (
npe\mu
) and its EOS can be calculated within the RMF models. For the details, one can refer to Refs.
(
Zhou et al. 2019
;
Zhou & Chen 2019
;
Xu et al. 2009
;
Zhang & Chen 2016
)
. We would like to point out that in this work, the core-crust transition density
n_{t}
, which separates the liquid core from the solid inner crust of neutron stars, is obtained self-consistently by the thermodynamical method
(
Xu et al. 2009
;
Kubis 2007
;
Cai & Chen 2012b
)
. In addition, the causality condition is satisfied for all the EOSs used in the following NS calculations.

The FSUGold interaction
(
Todd-Rutel & Piekarewicz 2005
)
is an accurately calibrated relativistic parametrization in the non-linear RMF model and it can very successfully describe the ground state binding energy and charge radius of finite nuclei as well as their linear response.
In the FSUGold model
(
Todd-Rutel & Piekarewicz 2005
;
Fattoyev et al. 2010
)
,
the nucleon mass is fixed at
m=939.0~\rm{MeV}
, and the three meson masses are also fixed, namely,
m_{\sigma}=491.5~\rm{MeV}
,
m_{\omega}=782.5~\rm{MeV}
and
m_{\rho}=763.0~\rm{MeV}
. In this case, the FSUGold has thus seven model parameters, i.e.,

g_{\sigma}
,
g_{\omega}
,
g_{\rho}
,
b_{\sigma}
,
c_{\sigma}
,
c_{\omega}
and
\Lambda_{V}
, which are listed in TABLE
1
.
The bulk properties of infinite nuclear matter of FSUGold can be obtained as:
n_{0}=0.148~\rm{fm}^{-3}
, the binding energy per nucleon of SNM at
n_{0}
is
e_{0}\equiv E_{0}(n_{0})=-16.31~\rm{MeV}
,
K_{0}=229.2~\rm{MeV}
, the nucleon Dirac mass in SNM at
n_{0}
is
m^{\ast}_{\rm{Dirac}}=m^{\ast}_{0}/m=0.61
, the neutron (proton) Dirac mass in asymmetric nuclear matter at
n_{0}
and
\alpha=0.5
is
m^{\ast}_{\rm{Dirac,n,0.5}}=m^{\ast}_{n}(n=n_{0},\alpha=0.5)/m=0.61
(
m^{\ast}_{\rm{Dirac,p,0.5}}=m^{\ast}_{p}(n=n_{0},\alpha=0.5)/m=0.61
),

J_{0}=-521.6~\rm{MeV}
,
E_{{\rm{sym}},c}\equiv E_{\rm{sym}}(n_{c})=25.88~\rm{MeV}
and
L_{c}=48.02~\rm{MeV}
.
In addition, the FSUGold predicts
E_{\rm{sym}}(n_{0})=32.59
MeV,
L=60.5
MeV,
n_{t}=0.082~\rm{fm}^{-3}
, the central density of the maximum mass NS configuration
n_{\rm cen}=1.15~\rm{fm}^{-3}
,
R_{1.4}=12.66
km,
\Lambda_{1.4}=401
, and
M_{\rm max}=1.72M_{\odot}
.
TABLE
2
summarizes these results.
Also included in TABLE
2
are the FSUGold RMF predictions of some ground state properties of doubly magic nuclei
208
Pb and
48
Ca, i.e., the binding energy per nucleon
E^{A}_{B}
, the charge rms radius
R^{A}_{\rm ch}
, the neutron skin thickness
\Delta r^{A}_{\rm np}
with
A=208~(48)
for
208
Pb (
48
Ca), as well as the
208
Pb spin-orbit splittings of proton
2d
state
\epsilon^{208}_{ls,2d\pi}
, neutron
3p
state
\epsilon^{208}_{ls,3p\nu}
and neutron
2f
state
\epsilon^{208}_{ls,2f\nu}
.

One sees from TABLE
2
that while the FSUGold nicely reproduces the ground state properties of finite nuclei, it predicts a too small value of
M_{\rm max}=1.72M_{\odot}
, which is significantly smaller than the observed
2.08^{+0.07}_{-0.07}M_{\odot}
for PSR J0740+6620
(
Cromartie et al. 2020
;
Fonseca et al. 2021
)
.
In addition, we note the

\Delta r_{\rm{np}}
of
208
Pb is
0.207
fm with the FSUGold, which is smaller than the PREX-II measurement.
Noting the variation of
the higher-order bulk parameters (e.g.,
J_{0}
) essentially does not change the properties of finite nuclei
(
Zhou et al. 2019
)
, we thus increase
J_{0}
while keep
n_{0}
,
e_{0}
,
K_{0}
,
m^{\ast}_{\rm{Dirac}}
and
E_{\rm{sym},c}
unchanged to enhance the
M_{\rm max}
. At the same time, we increase
L_{c}
from original
48.02~\rm{MeV}
to
55~\rm{MeV}
to fit the constraint of
L_{c}\gtrsim 52~\rm{MeV}

(
Yue et al. 2021
)
from PREX-II. The
L_{c}=55~\rm{MeV}
corresponds to
L(2/3n_{0})=54~\rm{MeV}
, again consistent with the constraint of
L(2/3n_{0})\gtrsim 49
MeV
(
Reed et al. 2021
)
from PREX-II.
Indeed, the
L_{c}=55~\rm{MeV}
leads to
\Delta r_{\rm{np}}\approx 0.22
fm for
208
Pb according to the relation between
\Delta r_{\rm{np}}
of
208
Pb and
L_{c}
, i.e.,
\Delta r_{\rm{np}}=(0.0615\pm 0.0022)+(0.00282\pm 0.0000344)L_{c}

(
Yue et al. 2021
)
. In addition, we note that the
L_{c}=55~\rm{MeV}
produces
\Lambda_{1.4}=576
, and
L_{c}>56~\rm{MeV}
will violate the constraint
\Lambda_{1.4}\leq 580
.
Increasing
J_{0}
can lead to larger
M_{\rm max}
, but the
J_{0}
cannot be larger than
-322.0~\rm{MeV}
to fit the constraint on the SNM EOS from flow data
(
Danielewicz et al. 2002
)
. One then obtains a value of
M_{\rm max}=1.93M_{\odot}
with
J_{0}=-322.0~\rm{MeV}
, suggesting the FSUGold Lagrangian cannot simultaneously describe the observed
2.08^{+0.07}_{-0.07}M_{\odot}
for PSR J0740+6620
(
Cromartie et al. 2020
;
Fonseca et al. 2021
)
and the constraint on the SNM EOS from flow data
(
Danielewicz et al. 2002
)
.
From the seven bulk parameters
n_{0}=0.148~\rm{fm}^{-3}
,
e_{0}=-16.31~\rm{MeV}
,
K_{0}=229.2~\rm{MeV}
,
m^{\ast}_{\rm{Dirac}}=0.61
,
J_{0}=-322.0~\rm{MeV}
,
E_{\rm{sym},c}=25.88~\rm{MeV}
and
L_{c}=55~\rm{MeV}
, one can obtain the seven model parameters
g_{\sigma}
,
g_{\omega}
,
g_{\rho}
,
b_{\sigma}
,
c_{\sigma}
,
c_{\omega}
and
\Lambda_{V}
, and the corresponding parameter set is denoted as ‘FSU-J0’ and shown in TABLE
1
. Moreover, the nuclear matter bulk parameters and NS properties as well as the ground state properties of
208
Pb and
48
Ca with FSU-J0 are summarized in TABLE
2
.
As expected, one sees that compared to FSUGold, the FSU-J0 gives very similar ground state properties except a larger neutron skin thickness of
\Delta r^{208}_{\rm np}=0.227
fm due to the larger
L_{c}
and thus it is compatible with the PREX-II measurement.

Now we turn to the Lagrangian density
\mathcal{L}
given by Eq. (
7
), which additionally includes the
\delta
meson and the
\delta
-
\sigma
mixing compared with the FSUGold model and has nine model parameters, namely
g_{\sigma}
,
g_{\omega}
,
g_{\rho}
,
g_{\delta}
,
b_{\sigma}
,
c_{\sigma}
,
c_{\omega}
,
\Lambda_{V}
and
C_{\delta\sigma}
with the
\delta
meson mass fixed at
980.0~\rm{MeV}
.
To explore the effects of the
\delta
meson and the
\delta
-
\sigma
mixing, our strategy is to vary the
g_{\delta}
and
C_{\delta\sigma}
while fix the values of
n_{0}
,
e_{0}
,
K_{0}
,
J_{0}
,
m^{\ast}_{\rm{Dirac}}
,
E_{\rm{sym},c}
and
L_{c}
at their corresponding values as in FSU-J0, and this guarantees that the constraints from PREX-II and SNM EOS from flow data can be satisfied as well as the basic properties of finite nuclei can be reasonably described.
In such a way,
we find that increasing the value of
g_{\delta}
can stiffen the symmetry energy at suprasaturation densities and enhance the
M_{\rm max}
. However, the
g_{\delta}
cannot be too large since the
\Lambda_{1.4}
will also increase with
g_{\delta}
but it is limited by
\Lambda_{1.4}\leq 580
.
On the other hand, it is interesting to note that the
C_{\delta\sigma}
can soften the symmetry energy at intermediate densities around
2\sim 3n_{0}
but stiffen the symmetry energy at high densities, and this feature can decrease the
\Lambda_{1.4}
and increase the
M_{\rm max}
. As an example, we find the parameters
g_{\delta}=6.7
and
C_{\delta\sigma}=0.0385
lead to
\Lambda_{1.4}=578.5
and
M_{\rm max}=2.05M_{\odot}
, and we denote this parameter set as ‘FSU-
\delta 6.7
’ and the model parameters are listed in TABLE
1
. The nuclear matter bulk parameters and NS properties as well as the ground state properties of
208
Pb and
48
Ca with FSU-
\delta 6.7
are summarized in TABLE
2
.
One sees that the FSU-
\delta 6.7
predicts an isospin splitting of the nucleon Dirac mass with

m^{\ast}_{\rm{Dirac},p,0.5}>m^{\ast}_{\rm{Dirac},n,0.5}
due to the inclusion of the
\delta
meson, which is not observed in the FSUGold and FSU-J0.
As for the ground state properties, the FSU-
\delta 6.7
reproduces the experimental data on binding energies at the
2\%
level and charge rms radii better than
1\%
, which is already satisfactory in the present work without the best-fit procedure.
In addition, it is interesting to see that the inclusion of the
\delta
meson seems to slightly improve the description of the spin-orbit splittings and obviously enhance the neutron skin thickness.

IV
Summary

By including the isovector scalar
\delta
meson and its coupling to isoscalar scalar
\sigma
meson in the accurately calibrated interaction FSUGold, we have demonstrated that the new RMF model can
be simultaneously compatible with
the constraints on the EOS of SNM at suprasaturation densities from flow data in heavy-ion collisions,
the neutron skin thickness of
208
Pb from the PREX-II experiment,
the measured largest NS mass reported so far from PSR J0740+6620,
the limit of
\Lambda_{1.4}\leq 580
from GW170817,
and the M-R relation of PSR J0030+0451 and PSR J0740+6620 measured by NICER,
and thus remove the tension between PREX-II and GW170817 observed in the conventional RMF model.

The great success of the new RMF model is mainly due to the fact that
the
\delta
-
\sigma
mixing can soften the symmetry energy at intermediate densities but stiffen the symmetry energy at high densities,
which further leads to a larger
M_{\rm max}
but a smaller
\Lambda_{1.4}
.
Further investigation of the
\delta
-
\sigma
mixing effects on nuclear structure,
heavy-ion collisions and neutron stars is an extremely interesting topic.

Abstract
. This article is devoted to the study of solutions of non-homogenous linear differential equations having entire coefficients. We get all non-trivial solutions of infinite order of equation f ( n ) + a n − 1 ​ ( z ) ​ f ( n − 1 ) + … + a 1 ​ f ′ + a 0 ​ f = H ⁡ ( z ) f^{(n)}+a_{n-1}(z)f^{(n-1)}+\ldots+a_{1}f^{\prime}+a_{0}f=H(z) , by restricting the conditions on coefficients.

1.
Introduction

The results of this article depends heavily on the concepts of value distrbution theory. We discuss some basic notations here, for detailed theory, reader can see
[
9
]
. Let
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of non-zero roots of function
f(z)
with
\lim_{n\to\infty}a_{n}=\infty
, then exponent of convergence is defined as

\displaystyle{\lambda(f)=\inf\{\alpha>0\displaystyle{|}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{-\alpha}<\infty\}}
.

The order of growth for an entire function
f(z)
is given by

\displaystyle{\rho(f)=\overline{\lim_{r\to\infty}}\,\displaystyle{\frac{\log^{+}\log^{+}{M(r,f)}}{\log{r}}}},

where
M(r,f)
is the maximum of the function
f(z)
on the disc of radius
|z|=r.
The
n^{th}
order homogenous linear differential equation for function
f
with an entire coefficients
a_{0}(z),a_{1}(z),\ldots,a_{n-1}(z)
is

(1)

f^{(n)}+a_{n-1}(z)f^{(n-1)}+\ldots+a_{1}(z)f^{\prime}+a_{0}(z)f=0

and the associated non-homogenous linear differential equation is

(2)

f^{(n)}+a_{n-1}(z)f^{(n-1)}+\ldots+a_{1}f^{\prime}+a_{0}f=H(z),

where
H(z)
is an entire function. If all the coefficients of equation (
1
) are polynomials, then all solutions are of finite order. The solutions of associated non-homogenous equation (
2
) are of finite order if
H(z)
is of finite order(see
[
14
, Lemma 2]
). If
a_{p}
be the last coefficient which is transcendental entire function, then atmost
p
linearly independent solutions of equation (
1
) are of finite order. Thus, if atleast one of the coefficient is transcendental entire function, then atmost all solutions of equation (
1
) and (
2
) are of infinite order. Let
\rho
be the minimal order of solutions of equation (
1
), then there may exist atmost one solution of order
<\rho
of equation (
2
). Thus, if all non-trivial solution of equation (
1
) are of infinite order, there may exist finite order solution of equation (
2
), we have illustrated this by following example.

Example 1
.

The equation

f^{\prime\prime}+zf^{\prime}+e^{z}f=e^{-z}(1-z)+1

has a finite order solution that is
f(z)=e^{-z},
whereas the associated homogenous equation has all non-trivial solutions of infinite order.

Example 2
.

Let
b(z)
be a finite order entire function and has multiply connected Fatou component.
Then, the equation

f^{\prime\prime}-e^{z}f^{\prime}+b(z)f=0,

has all non-trivial solution of infinite order(see
[
11
, Theorem B]
). But the associated non-homogenous equation

f^{\prime\prime}-e^{z}f^{\prime}+b(z)f=e^{-z}(1+b(z))+1

has finite order solution
f(z)=e^{-z}.

The main aim of this article is to find such conditions on coefficients of non-homogenous equation (
2
) to ensure that all non-trivial solutions are of infinite order.

Relaxed Recovery Conditions for OMP/OLS by Exploiting both Coherence and Decay
C. Herzet, A. Drémeau, C. Soussen

Abstract
We propose extended coherence-based conditions for exact sparse support recovery using orthogonal matching pursuit (OMP) and orthogonal least squares (OLS). Unlike standard uniform guarantees, we embed some information about the decay of the sparse vector coefficients in our conditions. As a result, the standard condition μ < 1 / ( 2 ​ k − 1 ) \mu<1/(2k-1) (where μ \mu denotes the mutual coherence and k k the sparsity level) can be weakened as soon as the nonzero coefficients obey some decay, both in the noiseless and the bounded-noise scenarios. Furthermore, the resulting condition is approaching μ < 1 / k \mu<1/k for strongly decaying sparse signals. Finally, in the noiseless setting, we prove that the proposed conditions, in particular the bound μ < 1 / k \mu<1/k , are the tightest achievable guarantees based on mutual coherence.

3
, following convention (
5
), we proceed to a re-ordering of the atoms

{\mathbf{a}}_{i}
by decreasing values of their magnitudes
|x_{i}|
. Here, this convention is applied to the unselected atoms, which are therefore
indexed by:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\backslash\mathcal{Q}=\{1,2,\ldots,k-g\},

(10)

with

\displaystyle|x_{1}|\geq|x_{2}|\geq\ldots\geq|x_{k-g}|>0.

(11)

Theorem
3
jointly addresses both questions
of successful termination and partial support recovery.

Theorem 3

Assume that Oxx has selected

\mathcal{Q}\subsetneq{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
with
\mathrm{Card}(\mathcal{Q})=g
during the first
g

iterations and let
1\leq p\leq r\leq k-g
.

•

If

\displaystyle\mu<\frac{1}{k},

(12)

and the largest magnitudes of the unselected atoms after iteration
g
satisfy

\displaystyle|x_{i}|>\frac{2\mu(k-g-i)}{1-(g+i)\mu}\,|x_{i+1}|\hskip 20.00003pt\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,p\},

(13)

then Oxx is guaranteed to select atoms in
{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\backslash\mathcal{Q}

until all the elements in
\{1,\ldots,p\}
have been selected.

•

If

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\leq\mu<\frac{1}{g+r},

(14)

and the largest magnitudes of the unselected atoms after iteration
g
satisfy

\displaystyle|x_{i}|>\frac{2\mu(k-g-r)}{1-(g+r)\mu}\,|x_{i+1}|\hskip 20.00003pt\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,p\},

(15)

then Oxx is ensured to select atoms in
{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\backslash\mathcal{Q}

until all the elements in
\{1,\ldots,p\}
have
been selected or
g+r
iterations have been carried out.

Let us discuss the implications of Theorem

3
on both problems of
“successful
termination”
and
“partial support recovery”
. We
specifically elaborate on the corresponding choices of
g
,
p
and

r
.

The paradigm of
successful termination
corresponds to the
case
p=r=k-g
. In this setup, we note that conditions
(
14
)-(
15
) are irrelevant since

\frac{1}{g+r}=\frac{1}{k}
,
thus
(
14
) cannot be satisfied. On the other hand, the
conditions (
12
)-(
13
) can be rewritten in terms of
constraints on the mutual coherence
\mu

involving the decay of the nonzero elements:

\displaystyle\mu<\mu^{\star}=\min\left(\frac{1}{k},\mu^{\star}_{1},\ldots,\mu^{\star}_{p}\right),

with

\displaystyle\mu_{i}^{\star}=\frac{\frac{|x_{i}|}{|x_{i+1}|}}{2(k-g-i)+(g+i)\frac{|x_{i}|}{|x_{i+1}|}}.

Depending on the decay of the nonzero coefficients, it can thus be
seen that
\mu^{\star}\in[\frac{1}{2k-g-1},\frac{1}{k}]
. The strongest
condition corresponds to (
9
) and is obtained
for
|x_{1}|=|x_{2}|
; it ensures the
uniform
recovery of any

k
-sparse vector when
g
good atoms have been selected during the
first
g
iterations. The weakest condition,
\mu<\frac{1}{k}
, is
obtained as soon as
\frac{|x_{i}|}{|x_{i+1}|}\geq 2

\forall\,i\in\{1,\ldots,k-g\}
. We thus recover a result similar to
Corollary
1
. Here, the decay constraints only
apply to the elements in
{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\backslash\mathcal{Q}
since the elements in

\mathcal{Q}
have already been selected by
assumption.

Let us now discuss the particularization of Theorem

3
to the problem of
partial support
recovery
(here,
g
is set to 0). We focus on the case where
p=r\leq k
.
We note that both (
12
)-(
13
) and
(
14
)-(
15
) ensure the selection of elements of
{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
during the first
p
iterations of Oxx
provided that the
p
largest nonzero coefficients obey some “sufficient” decay (which is specified by either (
13
) or (
15
)). This leads to the following corollary for partial support recovery:

Corollary 2

Let
p\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. If
\mu<\frac{1}{p}
and the
p+1
largest coefficients of
{\mathbf{x}}
exhibit
a sufficient decay (specified by (
13
) or (
15
)), then Oxx selects atoms in
{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
during the first
p
iterations.

We note that Corollary
2

(which makes use of the mild assumpation
\mu<\frac{1}{p}
)
does not guarantee that the selected atoms correspond to the
p

largest
coefficients of
{\mathbf{x}}
. Such guarantee can be
obtained from (
12
)-(
13
) by imposing the stronger assumption
\mu<\frac{1}{k}
:

Corollary 3

Let
p\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
. If
\mu<\frac{1}{k}
and the
p+1

largest magnitudes in
{\mathbf{x}}
exhibit a sufficient decay (
13
), then Oxx selects atoms in
{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
until the
p

largest components of
{\mathbf{x}}
have been selected.

Note that Corollary
3
does not state that Oxx will select the
p

largest components of
{\mathbf{x}}
during the first
p
iterations. Their selection is however
guaranteed during the first
k
iterations.

II-C

Compressible and Noisy Signals

In many situations, the sought vector
{\mathbf{x}}
is not exactly
k
-sparse
but rather compressible
and possibly non-sparse. Furthermore,
the
observations are corrupted by some additive noise
(
{\mathbf{w}}\neq\mathbf{0}_{m}
).
We review hereafter some contributions of the literature dealing with
the success of Oxx in this particular setup and provide new strongest
results in Theorems
4
and
5
.
We will assume that the noise has a bounded
\ell_{2}
-norm,
that is
\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{2}\leq\epsilon
. In the following, a signal
{\mathbf{x}}

will be referred to as “
k
-compressible” as soon as the sum of the absolute values of
k

entries of
{\mathbf{x}}
is large with respect to the remaining entries. Denoting by
{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\star}}\triangleq\{1,\ldots,n\}\backslash{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
the complementary subset, the
k
-compressible assumption reads
\|{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\|_{1}\gg\|{\mathbf{x}}_{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\star}}\|_{1}
for some subset
{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
of
cardinality
k
.
{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
and
{\mathbf{x}}_{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\star}}
shall be thought of as the
head and tail of the signal
{\mathbf{x}}
, respectively.

Let us first consider the
k
-sparse setup
(
{\mathbf{x}}_{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\star}}=\mathbf{0}_{n-k}
) with noisy observations
(
\epsilon>0
). Although many researchers have emphasized that the
noiseless conditions can be generalized to the case where the noise
level is low in comparison to the smallest nonzero coefficient of

{\mathbf{x}}

[
36
,
37
,
38
,
39
,
40
]
,
no tight
condition of success for Oxx
has been proposed so far. Among the noticeable coherence-based guarantees, we can nevertheless mention the work by Donoho
et al.

[
36
, Th. 5.1]
(also rediscovered in
[
39
, Th. 1]
), stating that OMP succeeds if

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{2k-1},

(16)

\displaystyle|x_{i}|

\displaystyle>\frac{2\epsilon}{1-(2k-1)\mu},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}.

(17)

To the best of our knowledge, the extension of these results to the
success of OLS has never been made in the literature. We are neither aware of any contribution dealing with coherence-based
conditions ensuring the recovery of a particular support for

k
-compressible vectors
and
noisy observations. We address
these questions in the next theorems. The result stated in Theorem

4
implies, as a corollary, that
(
16
)-(
17
) are sufficient
conditions for
both
OMP and OLS. Theorem

5
is an extension of Theorem

2
to the noisy
k
-compressible setting. As in
subsection
II-A
, we assume that the elements of

{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
satisfy (
4
)-(
5
).

Theorem 4

If

\displaystyle\mu<\frac{1}{2k-1},

(18)

and

\displaystyle|x_{i}|

\displaystyle>\frac{2(\|{\mathbf{x}}_{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\star}}\|_{1}+\epsilon)}{1-(2k-i)\mu}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,k\},

(19)

then Oxx selects atoms in
{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
during the first
k
iterations.

The conditions of Theorem
4
take the same form as
those in (
16
)-(
17
) but depend on
the
\ell_{1}
-norm of
{\mathbf{x}}_{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\star}}
. Moreover,
condition (
19
)
depends on the position
i
of the ordered
coefficients: the larger
i
, the weaker the constraint on their amplitude. As a result, when
\|{\mathbf{x}}_{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\star}}\|_{1}=0
,
Theorem
4
leads to weaker conditions than those
previously proposed in
[
36
]
,

[
39
]
as soon as
{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
is not a flat vector.
For flat vectors, (
19
) obviously
reduces to (
17
).
In such a case, Theorem
4
leads to the standard conditions by Donoho
et al.

Let us mention that the conditions in Theorem
4
do
not enforce any constraint on the decay of the coefficients in
{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}

(but only between the elements in
{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\star}}
and each component of

{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
). In the next theorem, we state “horizon-1” conditions of the
same flavor as those presented in Theorems
2
and

3
. Let us first define the following quantity:

\displaystyle\gamma_{k}\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1-(k-2)\mu}{(\mu+1)(1-k\mu)}&\mbox{for OMP},\\
\sqrt{\frac{1-(k-2)\mu}{\mu+1}}\frac{\sqrt{1-(k-1)\mu}}{1-k\mu}&\mbox{for OLS}.\end{array}\right.

Our result then writes as follows:

Theorem 5

If

\displaystyle\mu<\frac{1}{k},

(22)

and
\forall i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
,

\displaystyle|x_{i}|

\displaystyle>\frac{2\mu(k-i)}{1-i\mu}|x_{i+1}|+2\gamma_{k}(\epsilon+\|{\mathbf{x}}_{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\star}}\|_{1}),

(23)

then Oxx selects atoms in
{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
from noisy data during the first
k
iterations.

Theorem
3
could be extended in a similar way to the
framework of compressible and noisy signals but we do not detail this
extension for conciseness.

III
Technical Details

In this section, we provide a proof of the theorems stated in section
II
. We first recall the main principles ruling OMP and OLS in section
III-A
. We then introduce some technical lemmas in section
III-B
. Finally the proof of the main results is exposed in section
III-C
.

III-A

OMP and OLS

In order to precisely describe the update rules characterizing Oxx,
let us first introduce some notations: given a set of indices
\mathcal{Q}
,

{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathcal{Q}}
represents the submatrix of
{\mathbf{A}}
specified by the columns
indexed in
\mathcal{Q}
; the projector onto the orthogonal complement of the
column span of
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathcal{Q}}
is defined as
\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\bot}\triangleq{\mathbf{I}}-{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathcal{Q}}{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\dagger}
, where
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\dagger}
is the pseudo-inverse of
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathcal{Q}}
; in particular,
{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{Q}}\triangleq\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\bot}{\mathbf{y}}
is the residual error when projecting
{\mathbf{y}}
onto the span of
{\mathbf{A}}_{\mathcal{Q}}
. Finally,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
represents the vector inner product and

\mathbf{0}_{m}
is the null vector of size
m\times 1
.

Oxx can be understood as an iterative procedure generating an estimate of
{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
by sequentially adding one new element to the current support estimate, say
\mathcal{Q}
. As detailed in Algorithm
1
, OMP and OLS differ in the way this new element is selected.
At each iteration, OLS selects the atom
{\mathbf{a}}_{j}
yielding the minimum
residual error
\|{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{Q}\cup\{j\}}\|_{2}
:

j\in\argmin_{i\notin\mathcal{Q}}\|{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{Q}\cup\{i\}}\|_{2},

and
n-\mathrm{Card}\{\mathcal{Q}\}
least-square problems have to be solved to compute

\|{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{Q}\cup\{i\}}\|_{2}
for all
i\notin\mathcal{Q}

[
6
]
. On the
contrary, OMP adopts the simpler rule

j\in\argmax_{i\notin\mathcal{Q}}|\langle{\mathbf{a}}_{i},{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{Q}}\rangle|,

to select the new atom
{\mathbf{a}}_{j}
, and then solves only one
least-square problem to update the new residual

{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{Q}\cup\{j\}}
.

The selection rules described above can also be expressed in terms of the (normalized) projected atoms of the dictionary
[
13
]
. This formulation will turn out to be convenient in our proofs below. More specifically, let

\displaystyle{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i}

\displaystyle\triangleq{\mathbf{P}}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\perp}{\mathbf{a}}_{i},

\displaystyle{\tilde{\mathbf{b}}}_{i}

\displaystyle\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i}}/{\|{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i}\|_{2}}&\mbox{if ${\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i}\neq\mathbf{0}_{m}$}\\
\mathbf{0}_{m}&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

With these notations, the selection rule of Oxx can be re-expressed as (see
e.g.
,
[
5
]
)

\displaystyle j\in\argmax_{i\notin\mathcal{Q}}|\langle{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{i},{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{Q}}\rangle|,

(26)

where

\displaystyle{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{i}\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i}&\textrm{for OMP},\\
{\tilde{\mathbf{b}}}_{i}&\textrm{for OLS}.\end{array}\right.

For simplicity, the dependence of
{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i}
,
{\tilde{\mathbf{b}}}_{i}
and
{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{i}
on
\mathcal{Q}
does not appear in our notations. The reader should however keep this dependence in mind in our subsequent derivations.

III-B

Some Useful Lemmas

We first state three useful lemmas, connecting different functions of the projected atoms to the mutual coherence of the dictionary.

Lemma 1

Let
\mathrm{Card}(\mathcal{Q})=g\geq 0
. If
\mu<\frac{1}{g}
, then

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\|{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i}\|^{2}_{2}\geq\frac{(\mu+1)\,(1-g\mu)}{1-(g-1)\mu}&\quad\forall i\notin\mathcal{Q},\\
|\langle{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i},{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{j}\rangle|\leq\frac{\mu\,(\mu+1)}{1-(g-1)\mu}&\quad\forall j\neq i.\end{array}

Proof:

The result is a direct consequence of Lemmas 4 and 10 in
[
14
]
.

\square

Lemma 2

Let
\mathrm{Card}(\mathcal{Q})=g\geq 0
. If
\mu<\frac{1}{g}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\langle{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{i},{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i}\rangle\geq\alpha_{g}>0&\quad\forall i\notin\mathcal{Q},\\
|\langle{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{i},{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{j}\rangle|\leq\mu_{g}&\quad\forall j\neq i,\end{array}

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{g}=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{(\mu+1)\,(1-g\mu)}{1-(g-1)\mu}&\mbox{for OMP}\\
\sqrt{\frac{(\mu+1)(1-g\mu)}{1-(g-1)\mu}}&\mbox{for OLS}\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\mu_{g}=

\displaystyle\;\min\left\{1,\,\frac{\mu}{1-g\mu}\;\alpha_{g}\right\}.

(35)

Proof:
The result immediately follows from Lemma
1
and
from
\|{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{j}\|_{2}\leq\|{\mathbf{a}}_{j}\|_{2}=1
and
\|{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{i}\|_{2}\leq 1
. Note that

\mu<\frac{1}{g}
implies that
{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i}\neq\mathbf{0}_{m}

(see (
1
)). Thus,
{\tilde{\mathbf{b}}}_{i}
reads
{{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i}}/{\|{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i}\|_{2}}
.

\square

Lemma 3

If
\mu\leq\frac{1}{g+1}
, then
(
35
) simplifies to:

\displaystyle\mu_{g}=\frac{\mu}{1-g\mu}\;\alpha_{g}.

(36)

Proof:

\mu\leq\frac{1}{g+1}
implies that
\mu\leq 1-g\mu
and then

\displaystyle\frac{\mu}{1-g\mu}\alpha_{g}\leq\alpha_{g}\leq 1,

where
\alpha_{g}\leq 1
follows from
\forall i\notin\mathcal{Q}
,

\alpha_{g}\leq\langle{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{i},{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i}\rangle\leq\|{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{i}\|_{2}\|{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i}\|_{2}\leq 1
.

\square

Lemmas
2
and
3
are the building blocks of the next lemma, which provides sufficient conditions for Oxx to select a good atom at a given iteration:

Lemma 4

Consider a (possibly non-sparse) signal
{\mathbf{x}}
and a subset
{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
of cardinality
k
.
Assume that Oxx, with
{\mathbf{y}}
defined as in (
1
) as input, has selected atoms in
\mathcal{Q}\subsetneq{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
during the first
g
iterations, with
0\leq g<k
.
Let

\alpha_{g}
,
\mu_{g}
be defined as in Lemma
2
.
If

\displaystyle\mu<\frac{1}{g},

(37)

\displaystyle(\alpha_{g}+\mu_{g})\|{\mathbf{x}}_{{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\backslash\mathcal{Q}}\|_{\infty}-2\mu_{g}\|{\mathbf{x}}_{{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\backslash\mathcal{Q}}\|_{1}>2\,(\epsilon+\|{\mathbf{x}}_{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\star}}\|_{1}),

(38)

then Oxx selects an atom in
{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\backslash\mathcal{Q}
at the next iteration.

Proof :
We want to show that (
37
)-(
38
) implies

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\max_{i\in{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\backslash\mathcal{Q}}}|\langle{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{i},{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{Q}}\rangle|>|\langle{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{l},{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{Q}}\rangle|,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall l\notin{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}.

(39)

First, using the definitions of the residual
{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{Q}}={\mathbf{P}}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\perp}{\mathbf{y}}
and the projected atoms
{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{r}}^{\mathcal{Q}}={\mathbf{s}}^{\mathcal{Q}}+\mathbf{P}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\bot}{\mathbf{w}},

where

\displaystyle{\mathbf{s}}^{\mathcal{Q}}=\sum_{i\notin\mathcal{Q}}{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i}\,x_{i}.

Noticing that
\|{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{i}\|_{2}\leq 1
and
\|{\mathbf{P}}_{\mathcal{Q}}^{\perp}{\mathbf{w}}\|_{2}\leq\|{\mathbf{w}}\|_{2}\leq\epsilon
, a sufficient condition for (
39
) is then as follows:

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\max_{i\in{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\backslash\mathcal{Q}}}|\langle{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{i},{\mathbf{s}}^{\mathcal{Q}}\rangle|-|\langle{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{l},{\mathbf{s}}^{\mathcal{Q}}\rangle|>2\epsilon,\hskip 10.00002pt\forall l\notin{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}.

(40)

Let
j\in\argmax_{i\in{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\backslash\mathcal{Q}}|x_{i}|
. Since
\mu<\frac{1}{g}
, we can apply Lemma
2
and bound the terms in the left-hand side of (
40
) as follows:

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\max_{i\in{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\backslash\mathcal{Q}}}|\langle{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{i},{\mathbf{s}}^{\mathcal{Q}}\rangle|

\displaystyle\geq|\langle{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{j},{\mathbf{s}}^{\mathcal{Q}}\rangle|

\displaystyle\geq|\langle{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{j},{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{j}\rangle|\,|x_{j}|-\sum_{i\notin\mathcal{Q}\cup\{j\}}|\langle{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{j},{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i}\rangle||x_{i}|

\displaystyle\geq\alpha_{g}\,|x_{j}|-\mu_{g}\,(\|{\mathbf{x}}_{{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\backslash(\mathcal{Q}\cup\{j\})}\|_{1}+\|{\mathbf{x}}_{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\star}}\|_{1}),

and
\forall l\notin{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
,

\displaystyle|\langle{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{l},{\mathbf{s}}^{\mathcal{Q}}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq|\langle{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{l},{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{l}\rangle|\,|x_{l}|+\sum_{i\notin\mathcal{Q}\cup\{l\}}|\langle{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{l},{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{i}\rangle||x_{i}|

\displaystyle\leq|x_{l}|+\mu_{g}\,(\|{\mathbf{x}}_{{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\backslash\mathcal{Q}}\|_{1}+\|{\mathbf{x}}_{{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\star}}\backslash\{l\}}\|_{1}),

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
|\langle{\tilde{\mathbf{c}}}_{l},{\tilde{\mathbf{a}}}_{l}\rangle|\leq 1
. Combining these two bounds, we easily obtain that

\displaystyle(\alpha_{g}+\mu_{g})|x_{j}|-2\mu_{g}

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{x}}_{{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\backslash\mathcal{Q}}\|_{1}

\displaystyle>2\,\epsilon+(1-\mu_{g})|x_{l}|+2\mu_{g}\|{\mathbf{x}}_{{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\star}}}\|_{1}

is a sufficient condition for
(
40
) and then
(
39
). Finally, noticing that

\mu_{g}\leq 1
(Lemma 2)
and
|x_{l}|\leq\|{\mathbf{x}}_{{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\star}}}\|_{1}
, we obtain (
38
).

\square

III-C

Proofs of the Sufficient Conditions

All the proofs of this part use Lemma

Proof of Theorem
1
:
We want to show that Oxx selects atoms in
{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
during the first
k
iterations for all dictionaries obeying
\mu<\mu^{\star}
, for some
\mu^{\star}>\frac{1}{2k-1}
, as long as
{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
is not a flat vector.

Let us first derive a condition on the mutual coherence ensuring that Oxx makes a correct decision at the
first
iteration. Particularizing the sufficient conditions of Lemma
4
to the case
\mathcal{Q}=\emptyset
(with
\epsilon=0
and
\|{\mathbf{x}}_{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\star}}\|_{1}=0
), we have that Oxx selects an element of
{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
provided that:

\displaystyle\mu<\frac{\rho}{2-\rho},

with
\rho=\|{\mathbf{x}}_{{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}}\|_{\infty}/\|{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\|_{1}
.
Now, since
{\mathbf{x}}_{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
is not flat, we have that
\rho>\frac{1}{k}
,
and therefore

\frac{\rho}{2-\rho}>\frac{1}{2k-1}
.

On the other hand, if Oxx has selected any
g\geq 1
atoms in
{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}

during the first
g
iterations, it was proved in

[
14
, Th. 3]
that Oxx makes good decisions during the
remaining
k-g
iterations provided that

\mu<\frac{1}{2k-g-1}
.
A sufficient condition for Oxx to select correct atoms during the first
k
steps thus simply writes

\displaystyle\mu<\mu^{\star}\ \mbox{ with }\ \mu^{\star}

\displaystyle=\min\left(\frac{\rho}{2-\rho},\frac{1}{2k-2}\right).

Clearly,
\mu^{\star}>\frac{1}{2k-1}
by definition.

\square

The direct part of Theorem
2
can be seen as a
special case of Theorem
5
when
\epsilon=0
and

{\mathbf{x}}_{\bar{\mathcal{Q}}^{\star}}=\mathbf{0}_{n-k}
,
and of Theorem
3
with
g=0,r=p=k
. Hence, we focus
on the latter proofs hereafter.

Proof of Theorem
5
:
Assume that Oxx has selected atoms in
\mathcal{Q}\subsetneq{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}
when
g\leq k-1
iterations have been completed; we apply Lemma
4
to show that, under the hypotheses of Theorem
5
, the next atom selected by Oxx belongs to
{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\backslash\mathcal{Q}
.

The first condition of Lemma
4
,
\mu<\frac{1}{g}
, is always verified since
\mu<\frac{1}{k}
by hypothesis and
g\leq k-1
.
Let
j
be the lowest index such that:

\displaystyle j

\displaystyle\in\argmax_{i\in{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\backslash\mathcal{Q}}|x_{i}|.

(41)

Clearly, (
5
) implies that
j\leq g+1
.

Because the nonzero
coefficients have been sorted in the decreasing order, see
(
5
), we have:

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{x}}_{{\mathcal{Q}^{\star}}\backslash\mathcal{Q}}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq|x_{j}|+(k-g-1)\,|x_{j+1}|.

(42)

IV
Conclusions

In this paper, we derived new guarantees of success for OMP and
OLS. First, we showed that there exists a sufficient condition of
success taking the form
\mu<\mu^{\star}
with

\mu^{\star}\in(\frac{1}{2k-1},\frac{1}{k}]
as soon as the nonzero
coefficients are not all equal (Theorem

1
). This result thus shows that the
traditional condition
\mu<1/(2k-1)
can be weakened for decaying
vectors. We then presented a new “horizon-1” condition of success
taking the decay of the nonzero coefficients into account (Theorem

2
). In this condition, the specific upper bound

\mu^{\star}
is related to the rate of decay: the faster the decay, the

\mu^{\star}

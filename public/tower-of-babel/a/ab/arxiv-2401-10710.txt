Classification with neural networks with quadratic decision functions
Leon Frischauf, Otmar Scherzer, Cong Shi

Abstract
Neural networks with quadratic decision functions have been introduced as alternatives to standard neural networks with affine linear ones. They are advantageous when the objects or classes to be identified are compact and of basic geometries like circles, ellipses etc. In this paper we investigate the use of such ansatz functions for classification. In particular we test and compare the algorithm on the MNIST dataset for classification of handwritten digits and for classification of subspecies. We also show, that the implementation can be based on the neural network structure in the software Tensorflow and Keras , respectively.

Binary classification

Clustering
refers to the task of identifying groups of data with similar properties in a large dataset. It is typically referred to
unsupervised learning
.
Classification
on the other hand uses

(such as for instance obtained by cluster analysis) and is referred to

3.1
Clustering

Definition 3.1

(Clustering)
.

Given a dataset
P=\left\{{\vec{x}}_{1},{\vec{x}}_{2},...,{\vec{x}}_{N}\right\}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a target number of clusters
k
, the aim is to find a
partition
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, that is

C_{i}\cap C_{j}=\emptyset\text{ for all }i\neq j\in\left\{1,\ldots,k\right\}\text{ and }\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}C_{i}=\mathbb{R}^{n},

with
centroids

{\vec{x}}_{i}^{c}:=\frac{1}{|C_{i}|}\sum_{{\vec{x}}_{j}\in C_{i}}{\vec{x}}_{j}\quad\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,k,

such that the functional

(C_{1},\ldots,C_{k})\mapsto\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{{\vec{x}}_{j}\in C_{i}}\left\|{\vec{x}}_{j}-{\vec{x}}_{i}^{c}\right\|^{2}

becomes minimal.

The most prominent clustering algorithms are
k
-means and hierarchical clustering (see for instance
[
18
]
).

–

The
k
-means algorithms solves the problem from
subsection 3.1
. However, this problem is NP-hard, and in practice heuristics, such as the Voronoy cell decomposition (see for instance
[
17
]
), have to be implemented.

–

Hierarchical clustering methods
[
18
]
aim at computing a hierarchy of clusters. There are two distinct approaches: agglomerative or divisive hierarchies. The first is a “bottom-up” approach, where each data point starts in its own cluster, and clusters are then merged together at each subsequent step. The second follows a “top-down” approach, where all data points start in the same cluster, and at each step the clusters are refined with subdivisions.

While these methods work rather well for diverse practical applications, they do not always provide optimal or even stable solutions. For instance, the
k
-means approach is very sensitive to the choices of initial centroids.

Classification

5
Conclusion

In this work we have show that shallow neural networks with quadratic decision functions are an alternative to deep neural network for binary classification. The algorithms can also be implemented easily based on existing software such as
Tensorflow
and
Keras
by using customized layers in the implementation. We see an enormous benefit in the problem of detecting subspecies. One should mention that currently different methodologies are used to prove approximation properties of
RQNN
s and
ALNN
s for arbitrary functions. In
[
7
]
we used wavelet constructions to prove approximation properties of
RQNN
s, while for
ALNN
s the standard way of proving approximation properties is via the universal approximation theorem (see
[
5
]
). The difference in these two approaches is necessary due to the compactness of the level sets of the according neural network functions.

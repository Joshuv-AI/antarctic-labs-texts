Fibonacci sums modulo 5


Abstract
We develop closed form expressions for various finite binomial Fibonacci and Lucas sums depending on the modulo 5 nature of the upper summation limit. Our expressions are inferred from some trigonometric identities.

Preliminaries

As usual, the Fibonacci numbers
F_{n}
and the Lucas numbers
L_{n}
are defined, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
by the following recurrence relations for
n\geq 2
:

\displaystyle F_{n}=F_{n-1}+F_{n-2},\quad F_{0}=0,\,\,\,F_{1}=1,

\displaystyle L_{n}=L_{n-1}+L_{n-2},\quad L_{0}=2,\,\,\,L_{1}=1.

For negative subscripts we have
F_{-n}=(-1)^{n-1}F_{n}
and
L_{-n}=(-1)^{n}L_{n}
.

Throughout this paper, we denote the golden ratio by
\alpha=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
and write
\beta=\frac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}=-\frac{1}{\alpha}
.
The Fibonacci and Lucas numbers possess the explicit formulas (Binet forms)

F_{n}=\frac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta},\quad L_{n}=\alpha^{n}+\beta^{n},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

The sequences
\{F_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
and
\{L_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
are indexed in the On-Line Encyclopedia of Integer Sequences

[
17
]
as entries A000045 and A000032, respectively.
For more information we refer to Koshy
[
12
]
and Vajda
[
18
]
who have written excellent books dealing with Fibonacci and Lucas numbers.

There exists a countless number of binomial sums involving Fibonacci and Lucas numbers.
For some new articles in this field we refer to the papers
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
6
]
.

In this paper, we introduce closed form expressions for finite Fibonacci and Lucas sums involving different kinds of binomial coefficients and depending on the the modulo 5 nature of the upper summation limit. Our expressions are derived from various trigonometric identities, particularly utilizing Waring formulas and Chebyshev polynomials of the first and second kinds. We also present some series involving Bernoulli polynomials.

We note that some of our results were announced without proofs in
[
5
]
.

Lemma 1
.

If
n
is a positive integer, then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}{\frac{{(-1)^{k-1}n}}{k}\binom{n-k-1}{k-1}2^{n-2k-1}\cos^{n-2k}x}=2^{n-1}\cos^{n}x-\cos nx,

(1)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor{(n-1)/2}\right\rfloor}{(-1)^{k}\binom{n-k-1}{k}2^{n-2k-1}\cos^{n-2k-1}x}=\frac{{\sin nx}}{\sin x}.

(2)

Lemma 2
.

If
n
is an integer, then

\displaystyle\cos{\Big(\frac{n\pi}{5}\Big)}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{n},&\text{\rm if $n\equiv 0\pmod{5}$};\\
(-1)^{n-1}\alpha/2,&\text{\rm if $n\equiv 1$ or $4\pmod{5}$};\\
(-1)^{n-1}\beta/2,&\text{\rm if $n\equiv 2$ or $3\pmod{5}$};\end{cases}

(3)

\displaystyle\cos{\Big(\frac{2n\pi}{5}\Big)}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{\rm if $n\equiv 0\pmod{5}$};\\
-\beta/2,&\text{\rm if $n\equiv 1$ or $4\pmod{5}$};\\
-\alpha/2,&\text{\rm if $n\equiv 2$ or $3\pmod{5}$}.\end{cases}

(4)

Proof.

Relations stated in (
3

For instance, they follow by applying the addition theorem for the cosine function

\cos(a+b)=\cos a\cos b-\sin a\sin b

combined with the special values

\cos\Big(\frac{\pi}{5}\Big)=\frac{\alpha}{2},\quad\cos\Big(\frac{2\pi}{5}\Big)=-\frac{\beta}{2},\quad\cos\Big(\frac{3\pi}{5}\Big)=\frac{\beta}{2},\quad\cos\Big(\frac{4\pi}{5}\Big)=-\frac{\alpha}{2}.

Relations stated in (
4

3
).

F_{n+t}-(-1)^{n}F_{t-1},&\text{\rm if $n\equiv 2$ or $3\pmod{5}$}.\end{cases}

3
Fibonacci sums modulo 5 from Waring formulas

This section is based on utilizing the following trigonometric identities with the use of Waring formulas.

Lemma 4
.

If
n
is a positive integer, then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}{(-1)^{k}\frac{n}{{n-k}}\binom{n-k}{k}2^{n-2k-1}\cos^{n-2k}x}=\cos nx,

(10)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(n-1)/2}{(-1)^{(n-1)/2-k}\frac{n}{{n-k}}\binom{n-k}{k}2^{n-2k-1}\sin^{n-2k}x}=\sin nx,\quad\text{\rm$n$ odd},

(11)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n/2}{(-1)^{n/2-k}\frac{n}{{n-k}}\binom{n-k}{k}2^{n-2k-1}\sin^{n-2k}x}=\cos nx,\quad\text{\rm$n$ even}.

(12)

Proof.

Consider the Waring formula

\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}{(-1)^{k}\frac{{n}}{{n-k}}\binom{n-k}{k}(x_{1}+x_{2})^{n-2k}(x_{1}x_{2})^{k}}=x_{1}^{n}+x_{2}^{n}.

Let
i
be the imaginary unit. The choice

x_{1}=e^{ix}/2
,
x_{2}=e^{-ix}/2
produces (
10
), while the choice

x_{1}=e^{ix}/(2i)
,
x_{2}=-e^{-ix}/{(2i)}
gives

x_{1}+x_{2}=\sin x
,
x_{1}x_{2}=1/4
, and

x_{1}^{n}+x_{2}^{n}=\begin{cases}(-1)^{(n-1)/2}2^{1-n}\sin nx,&\text{if $n$ is odd};\\
(-1)^{n/2}2^{1-n}\cos nx,&\text{if $n$ is even};\end{cases}

and hence (
11
) and (
12
).
∎

Lemma 5
.

If
n
is a positive integer, then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}{(-1)^{k}\binom{n-k}{k}2^{n-2k}\cos^{n-2k}x}=\frac{{\sin((n+1)x)}}{{\sin x}},

(13)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{(n-1)/2}{(-1)^{(n-1)/2-k}\binom{n-k}{k}2^{n-2k}\sin^{n-2k}x}=\frac{{\sin((n+1)x)}}{{\cos x}},\quad\text{\rm$n$ odd},

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n/2}{(-1)^{n/2-k}\binom{n-k}{k}2^{n-2k}\sin^{n-2k}x}=\frac{{\cos((n+1)x)}}{{\cos x}},\quad\text{\rm$n$ even}.

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Lemma
4
. We use the dual to the Waring formula

\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}{(-1)^{k}\binom{n-k}{k}(x_{1}+x_{2})^{n-2k}(x_{1}x_{2})^{k}}=\frac{{x_{1}^{n+1}-x_{2}^{n+1}}}{{x_{1}-x_{2}}}.

∎

Theorem 7
.

If
n
is a positive integer and
t
is any integer, then

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{n}{n-k}\binom{n-k}{k}F_{n-2k+t}=\begin{cases}2F_{t},&\text{\rm if $n\equiv 0$}\pmod{5};\\
-F_{t+1},&\text{\rm if $n\equiv 1$ or $4$}\pmod{5};\\
F_{t-1},&\text{\rm if $n\equiv 2$ or $3$}\pmod{5};\end{cases}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{n}{n-k}\binom{n-k}{k}L_{n-2k+t}=\begin{cases}2L_{t},&\text{\rm if $n\equiv 0$}\pmod{5};\\
-L_{t+1},&\text{\rm if $n\equiv 1$ or $4$}\pmod{5};\\
L_{t-1},&\text{\rm if $n\equiv 2$ or $3$}\pmod{5}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We apply equation (
10
). Inserting
x=\pi/5
and
x=3\pi/5
, respectively,
and keeping in mind the trigonometric identity

\cos 3x=4\cos^{3}x-3\cos x

we end with

\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{n}{n-k}\binom{n-k}{k}\alpha^{n-2k+t}=\begin{cases}2\alpha^{t},&\text{if $n\equiv 0$}\pmod{5};\\
-\alpha^{t+1},&\text{\rm if $n\equiv 1$ or $4$}\pmod{5};\\
\alpha^{t-1},&\text{\rm if $n\equiv 2$ or $3$}\pmod{5};\end{cases}

and

\sum_{k=0}^{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{n}{n-k}\binom{n-k}{k}\beta^{n-2k+t}=\begin{cases}2\beta^{t},&\text{\rm if $n\equiv 0$}\pmod{5};\\

-\beta^{t}(\beta^{3}-3\beta),&\text{\rm if $n\equiv 2$ or $3$}\pmod{5}.\end{cases}

To complete the proof simplify the terms in brackets and combine according the Binet form.

Fibonacci sums modulo 5 from Chebyshev polynomials

For any integer
n\geq 0
, the Chebyshev polynomials
\{T_{n}(x)\}_{n\geq 0}
of the first kind are defined
by the second-order recurrence relation
[
16
]

T_{n+1}(x)=2xT_{n}(x)-T_{n-1}(x),\quad n\geq 2,\quad T_{0}(x)=1,\,\,\,T_{1}(x)=x,

while the Chebyshev polynomials
\{U_{n}(x)\}_{n\geq 0}
of the second kind are defined by

U_{n+1}(x)=2xU_{n}(x)-U_{n-1}(x),\quad n\geq 2,\quad U_{0}(x)=1,\,\,\,U_{1}(x)=2x.

The Chebyshev polynomials possess the representations

\displaystyle T_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor}{n\choose 2k}(x^{2}-1)^{k}x^{n-2k},

\displaystyle U_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor{n}/{2}\rfloor}{n+1\choose 2k+1}(x^{2}-1)^{k}x^{n-2k},

and have the exact Binet-like formulas

\displaystyle T_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left((x+\sqrt{x^{2}-1})^{n}+(x-\sqrt{x^{2}-1})^{n}\right),

\displaystyle U_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x^{2}-1}}\left((x+\sqrt{x^{2}-1})^{n+1}-(x-\sqrt{x^{2}-1})^{n+1}\right).

The properties of Chebyshev polynomials of the first and second kinds have been studied extensively in the literature.
The reader can find in the recent papers
[
7
,
8
,
11
,
14
,
15
,
19
]
additional information about them, especially about their products, convolutions, power sums as well as their
connections to Fibonacci numbers and polynomials.

Concluding comments

In this paper, we presented new closed forms for some types of finite Fibonacci and Lucas sums involving different kinds of binomial coefficients and depending on the modulo 5 nature of the upper summation limit. To prove our results, we applied some trigonometric identities utilizing Waring formulas and Chebyshev polynomials of the first and second kinds.

Using similar techniques, we can generalize our findings to more common number sequences. Let us give, for example, a generalization of Theorems

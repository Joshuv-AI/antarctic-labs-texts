Abstract




Abstract.
In this paper, we initiate the study of backward doubly stochastic differential equations (BDSDEs, for short) with quadratic growth. The existence, comparison, and stability results for one-dimensional BDSDEs are proved when the generator
f(t,Y,Z)
grows in
Z
quadratically and the terminal value is bounded, by introducing some new ideas.
Moreover, in this framework, we use BDSDEs to give a probabilistic representation for the solutions of semilinear stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs, for short) in Sobolev spaces, and use it to prove the existence and uniqueness of such SPDEs, thus extending the nonlinear Feynman-Kac formula.

Key words:
backward doubly stochastic differential equation, stochastic partial differential equation, quadratic growth, Feynman-Kac formula, Sobolev solution.

AMS subject classifications.
60H10, 60H15, 60H30.

1
Introduction

The nonlinear Feynman-Kac formula was introduced by Pardoux–Peng
[
25
]
after they proved the existence and uniqueness of nonlinear backward stochastic differential equations (BSDEs, for short) in
[
24
]
. It provides a probabilistic representation for a large class of semilinear partial differential equations (PDEs, for short).
A few years later, in order to give a probabilistic representation for semilinear stochastic PDEs (SPDEs, for short), a new class of BSDEs called the backward doubly stochastic differential equations (BDSDEs, for short) were introduced by them too in
[
26
]
.
In order to present the work more clearly, we describe the problem in detail.

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
be a complete probability space on which two standard independent Brownian motions
\{W_{t};0\leqslant t<\infty\}
and
\{B_{t};0\leqslant t<\infty\}
, with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{R}^{l}
, respectively, are defined. Let
T>0
be a fixed terminal time and denote by
\mathcal{N}
the class of
\mathbb{P}
-null sets of
\mathscr{F}
, where

\mathscr{F}_{t}\triangleq\mathscr{F}_{t}^{W}\vee\mathscr{F}_{t,T}^{B},\quad\forall t\in[0,T].

In the above, for any process
\{\eta_{t};0\leqslant t\leqslant T\},

\mathscr{F}_{s,t}^{\eta}=\sigma\{\eta_{r}-\eta_{s};s\leqslant r\leqslant t\}\vee\mathcal{N}
and
\mathscr{F}_{t}^{\eta}=\mathscr{F}_{0,t}^{\eta}
.
For each
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let
\{X^{t,x}_{s};t\leqslant s\leqslant T\}
be
the solution of the following stochastic differential equations (SDEs, for short):

X^{t,x}_{s}=x+\int_{t}^{s}b(X^{t,x}_{r})dr+\int_{t}^{s}\sigma(X^{t,x}_{r})dW_{r},\quad~t\leqslant s\leqslant T,

and consider the following backward doubly stochastic differential equations: for
t\leqslant s\leqslant T
,

\displaystyle Y^{t,x}_{s}=h(X^{t,x}_{T})+\int_{s}^{T}f(r,X^{t,x}_{r},Y^{t,x}_{r},Z^{t,x}_{r})dr+\int_{s}^{T}g(r,X^{t,x}_{r},Y^{t,x}_{r},Z^{t,x}_{r})d\overleftarrow{B}_{r}-\int_{s}^{T}Z^{t,x}_{r}dW_{r},

(1.1)

where the
dW
integral is a forward Itô’s integral and the
d\overleftarrow{B}
integral is a backward one. The coefficient
h
is called the
terminal value
and the coefficient
f
is called the
generator
. The solution of (
1.1
) is the pair
(Y,Z)
of
\mathscr{F}
-measurable processes.
For convenience, hereafter, by a
quadratic
BDSDE, or BDSDE
with quadratic growth
, we mean that in (
1.1
), the generator
f
grows in
Z
quadratically. Meanwhile, we call
h
the
bounded terminal value
if it is bounded.

Pardoux–Peng
[
26
]
introduced BDSDEs (
1.1
), gave the well-posedness of solutions under globally Lipschitz coefficients, and used it to prove the existence and uniqueness of the following semilinear SPDEs:

\displaystyle\displaystyle u(s,x)=

\displaystyle h(x)+\int_{s}^{T}\left\{\mathcal{L}u(r,x)+f\big(r,x,u(r,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(r,x)\big)\right\}dr

(1.2)

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{T}g\big(r,x,u(r,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(r,x)\big)d\overleftarrow{B}_{r},\quad t\leqslant s\leqslant T,

where
\sigma^{\top}
denotes the transposed of
\sigma
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}},\quad~\left(a_{ij}\right)=\sigma\sigma^{\top}.

This result is summarized as the nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula,
which permits us to solve SPDE by BDSDE and, conversely, one can use SPDE to solve BDSDE too.
Since then, this important theory has attracted a lot of attention.
For example, Buckdahn–Ma
[
10
,
11
]
introduced the stochastic viscosity solution to the nonlinear SPDEs, which connect BDSDEs to extend the nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula. Bally–Matoussi
[
3
]
obtained the connection between the solution of BDSDEs and the Sobolev solution of related SPDEs. Zhang–Zhao
[
37
]
studied infinite horizon BDSDEs and got the stationary solutions of related SPDEs.
Xiong
[
33
]
solved a long standing open problem about the SPDE characterization of the super-Brownian motion by making use of the BDSDE.
Buckdahn–Li–Xing
[
9
]
studied mean-field BDSDEs and the associated nonlocal semilinear SPDEs.
Besides, Shi–Gu–Liu
[
28
]
obtained the comparison theorem of solutions of BDSDEs. Han–Peng–Wu
[
16
]
got the maximum principle for backward doubly stochastic control systems. Hu–Matoussi–Zhang
[
18
]
studied the Wong-Zakai approximations of BDSDEs.
For more recent developments of BDSDEs, we refer the readers to Boufoussi–Van Casteren–Mrhardy
[
6
]
,
Gomez et al.
[
15
]
, Shi–Wen–Xiong
[
29
]
, Wu–Zhang
[
31
]
, Zhang–Zhao
[
38
]
, etc.

Moreover, when the coefficient
g
is absent, BDSDEs (
1.1
) are essentially reduced to the following backward stochastic differential equations:

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad~0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

(1.3)

which were firstly introduced by Pardoux–Peng
[
24
]
, where they obtained the existence and uniqueness under Lipschitz coefficients. Since then, BSDE stimulates some significant developments in many fields, such as partial differential equation (see Pardoux–Peng
[
25
]
and Yong
[
34
]
), mathematical finance (see El Karoui–Peng–Quenez
[
14
]
), and stochastic optimal control (see Yong–Zhou
[
35
]
), to mention a few.
Meanwhile, based on the wide applications and motivated by the open problem proposed by Peng
[
23
]
, many efforts have been made to relax the conditions on the generator
f
of BSDE (
1.3
) for the existence and/or uniqueness of adapted solutions.
For example, Lepeltier–San Martin
[
21
]
proved the existence of adapted solutions for BSDE when the generator
f
is continuous and of linear growth in
(Y,Z)
.
In 2000, Kobylanski
[
20
]
established the well-posedness for one-dimensional BSDE (
1.3
) with
f
growing in
Z
quadratically and with bounded terminal value.
When the terminal value is unbounded, Briand–Hu
[
7
,
8
]
proved the existence and uniqueness of one-dimensional BSDEs with quadratic growth. Delbaen–Hu–Bao
[
13
]
studied BSDEs with the generator
f
growing in
Z
super-quadratically.
For multi-dimensional situation, Hu–Tang
[
19
]
proved the existence and uniqueness of BDSDEs with diagonally quadratic generators, and Xing–Zitkovic
[
32
]
established the existence and uniqueness for a large class of Markovian BSDEs with quadratic growth.
Some other recent developments of quadratic BSDEs can be found in
Bahlali–Eddahbi–Ouknine
[
1
]
,
Barrieu–El Karoui
[
5
]
, Cheridito–Nam
[
12
]
,
Hu–Li–Wen
[
17
]
, Richou
[
27
]
, Tevzadze
[
30
]
,
and references cited therein.

Note that in quadratic BSDEs, in order to overcome the difficulty of quadratic growth, one always would like to distinguish whether the terminal value is bounded or not. The reason is that when the terminal value is bounded, by involving bounded mean oscillation martingales (BMO martingale, for short), some nice estimates and regularities for the solution
(Y,Z)
could be obtained. In other words, in this case, one could prove that
Y
is bounded and
Z
belongs to the BMO martingale space, which are useful when proving the existence and uniqueness.
In addition, when proving the existence, the main idea is to use the exponential transformation, which implies that there is an essential difference between the study of the one-dimensional case and the multi-dimensional case.
In summary, when studying the quadratic BSDEs, it is better to distinguish whether the terminal value is bounded or not, and the framework is a one-dimensional case or a multi-dimensional case.

On the other hand, during the past two decades, stimulated by the broad applications and the open problem of Peng
[
23
]
,
tremendous efforts have been made to relax the conditions on the generator
f
of BDSDEs and extend the nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula.
However, to the best of our knowledge, there are few works concerning BDSDEs (
1.1
) when the generator
f
grows in
Z
quadratically. Some related studies are the works of Zhang–Zhao
[
39
]
and Bahlali et al.
[
2
]
, where they obtained the existence and uniqueness of BDSDEs when the generator
f
is of polynomial growth in
Y
and
is of super-linear (or sub-quadratic) in
Z
, respectively.
For quadratic BDSDEs, the main difficulty is that due to the collection
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathscr{F}_{t};0\leqslant t\leqslant T\}
is neither increasing nor decreasing, and it does not constitute a filtration.
So the main technique of BMO martingale used in quadratic BSDEs is useless here, and one cannot expect to study the regularity of
Z
in the BMO martingale space. Besides, the backward Itô’s integral appeared in BDSDE (
1.1
) will bring extra troubles when proving the existence and uniqueness of the solutions.

In this paper, we initiate the theory of BDSDEs with quadratic growth. In particular, we consider the one-dimensional framework of quadratic BDSDEs with bounded terminal value. First, we study the existence under a general assumption on the generator
f
. Borrowed some ideas from Kobylanski
[
20
]
,
we construct an artful exponential transformation (see
3.1
), which transforms the quadratic BDSDE into a classical one.
It should be pointed out that the solution
(Y,Z)
we considered is in the space

L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
(see Section
2
for detailed definitions), since
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathscr{F}_{t};0\leqslant t\leqslant T\}
is not a filtration.
Moreover, in order to overcome the troubles that come from the backward Itô’s integral, we introduce the idea of the comparison theorem of classical BDSDEs, and some restricted condition (see (
3.5
)) on the coefficient
g
is made, otherwise,
Y
cannot be bounded (see
3.2
).
Furthermore, based on a priori estimate (see
3.5
) and the monotone stability obtained (see
3.7
), we prove that BDSDE with quadratic growth admits a solution
(Y,Z)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
(see
Theorem 3.10
).
Second, in order to prove the uniqueness, we study a comparison theorem, due to the fact that the one-dimensional framework allows us to provide a comparison theorem which directly implies the uniqueness as a by-product (see
Theorem 4.4
).
However, a stronger assumption on the coefficients
f
and
g
than that for the existence result is used to prove the comparison theorem. In other words, we assume that the partial derivatives of the coefficients
f
and
g
with respect to
Z
are linear growth and bounded, respectively.
Moreover, based on the comparison theorem, we obtain a stability result, i.e.,
the solutions
(Y^{n},Z^{n})
of BDSDE with coefficients
(f^{n},g,\xi^{n})
converge to the unique solution
(Y,Z)
of BDSDE with coefficients
(f,g,\xi)
when the sequences
\{f^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
\{\xi^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
converge to
f
and
\xi
, respectively (see
Theorem 4.6
).
Finally, we use BDSDE (
1.1
) with quadratic growth to give a probabilistic representation for the solutions of SPDE (
1.2
) in Sobolev spaces, and use it to prove the existence and uniqueness of SPDE (
1.2
) when
f
grows in
\sigma^{\top}\nabla u
quadratically (see
Theorem 5.6
), thus extending the nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula of Pardoux–Peng
[
26
]
to quadratic growth situation.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, some preliminaries are presented. In Section
3
, a priori estimate, the monotonicity stability, and the existence of the solutions are proved. In Section
4
, we focus on the comparison theorem and thus derive the uniqueness of the solutions. The stability result is obtained in this section too. In Section
5
, we study the relationship between the solution of BDSDEs with quadratic growth and the Sobolev solution of related SPDEs. Section
6
concludes the results.

Repeat that the triple
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
is a complete probability space, and on which two standard independent Brownian motions
\{W_{t};0\leqslant t<\infty\}
and
\{B_{t};0\leqslant t<\infty\}
are defined with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{R}^{l}
, respectively. Let
T>0
be a fixed terminal time and denote by
\mathcal{N}
the class of
\mathbb{P}
-null sets of
\mathscr{F}
, where

\mathscr{F}_{t}\triangleq\mathscr{F}_{t}^{W}\vee\mathscr{F}_{t,T}^{B},\quad~\forall t\in[0,T].

In the above, for any process
\{\eta_{t};0\leqslant t\leqslant T\},

\mathscr{F}_{s,t}^{\eta}=\sigma\{\eta_{r}-\eta_{s};s\leqslant r\leqslant t\}\vee\mathcal{N}
and
\mathscr{F}_{t}^{\eta}=\mathscr{F}_{0,t}^{\eta}
.
Note that the collection
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathscr{F}_{t};0\leqslant t\leqslant T\}
is not a filtration since it
is neither increasing nor decreasing.

Let us introduce some notations that will be used below. Denote by
|\cdot|
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the Euclidean norm and dot product, respectively, throughout the paper.
For any
p\geqslant 1
and Euclidean space
\mathbb{H}
, denote by

C^{p}(\mathbb{H})
the set of functions valued in
\mathbb{H}
and of class
C^{p}
with the partial
derivations of order less than or equal to
p
are bounded.
In addition, we introduce the following spaces:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle L^{p}_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{H})=\Big\{\xi:\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\bigm|\xi\hbox{ is $\mathscr{F}_{T}$-measurable, }\|\xi\|_{L^{p}}\triangleq\big(\mathbb{E}|\xi|^{p}\big)^{1\over p}<\infty\Big\},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle L_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{H})=\Big\{\xi:\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\bigm|\xi\hbox{ is $\mathscr{F}_{T}$-measurable, }\|\xi\|_{\infty}\triangleq\mathop{\rm esssup}_{\omega\in\Omega}|\xi(\omega)|<\infty\Big\},\end{array}

and for any
t\in[0,T)
and
s\in[t,T]
, define

\displaystyle\displaystyle L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(t,T;\mathbb{H})\negthinspace=

\displaystyle\ \negthinspace\Big\{\varphi:[t,T]\negthinspace\times\negthinspace\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\bigm|\varphi_{s}\hbox{ is
$\mathscr{F}_{s}$-measurable},\ \|\varphi\|_{L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(t,T)}\triangleq\negthinspace\Big(\mathbb{E}\int^{T}_{t}\negthinspace|\varphi_{s}|^{p}ds\Big)^{1\over p}\negthinspace<\negthinspace\negthinspace\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle\displaystyle L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(t,T;\mathbb{H})=

\displaystyle~\Big\{\varphi:[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\bigm|\varphi_{s}\hbox{ is $\mathscr{F}_{s}$-measurable, }\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}\triangleq\mathop{\rm esssup}_{(s,\omega)\in[t,T]\times\Omega}|\varphi_{s}(\omega)|<\infty\Big\},

\displaystyle\displaystyle S_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(t,T;\mathbb{H})=

\displaystyle~\Big\{\varphi:[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\bigm|\varphi_{s}\hbox{ is $\mathscr{F}_{s}$-measurable, continuous, and }

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\ \|\varphi\|_{S_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(t,T)}\triangleq\Big\{\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|\varphi_{s}|^{p}\Big]\Big\}^{\frac{1}{p}}<\infty\Big\}.

Consider the following backward doubly stochastic differential equations:

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f\left(s,Y_{s},Z_{s}\right)ds+\int_{t}^{T}g\left(s,Y_{s},Z_{s}\right)d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad~0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

(2.1)

where
\xi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}
is a random variable,
f:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k}
and

g:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{k}\times\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{k\times l}
are two coefficients.
Note that the
dW
integral is a forward Itô’s integral and the
d\overleftarrow{B}
integral is a backward one, and both integrals are particular cases of Itô-Skorohod integral (see Nualart
[
22
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

A pair of measurable processes
(Y,Z)\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{k})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{k\times d})
is called a solution of BDSDE (
2.1
), if
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, it satisfies (
2.1
). In addition, if
Y
is bounded, then we call the pair
(Y,Z)
a bounded solution.

Now, we recall the following propositions concerning BDSDEs, which come from Pardoux–Peng
[
26
]
and Shi et al.
[
28
]
. The first one is the Itô’s formula for backward doubly stochastic differential equations, the second one is the existence and uniqueness for the classical BDSDEs, and the third one is the comparison theorem for one-dimensional classical BDSDEs.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\Phi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k})
,
\alpha\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{k})
,
\beta\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{k})
,
\gamma\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{k\times l})
,
\delta\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{k\times d})
be such that

\displaystyle\alpha_{t}=\alpha_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{s}ds+\int_{0}^{t}\gamma_{s}d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}+\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{s}dW_{s},\quad~0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

Then

\displaystyle\Phi(\alpha_{t})=

\displaystyle\ \Phi(\alpha_{0})+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\Phi^{\prime}(\alpha_{s}),\beta_{s}\rangle ds+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\Phi^{\prime}(\alpha_{s}),\gamma_{s}d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\langle\Phi^{\prime}(\alpha_{s}),\delta_{s}dW_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{Tr}[\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\alpha_{s})\gamma_{s}\gamma_{s}^{\top}]ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\mathrm{Tr}[\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\alpha_{s}))\delta_{s}\delta_{s}^{\top}]ds.

Assumption 1
.

The terminal value
\xi
comes from
L^{2}_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{k})
, and for any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}
,

f(\cdot,y,z)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{k})\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~g(\cdot,y,z)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{k\times l}).

Moreover, there exist two positive constants
C
and
\alpha
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that for any
t\in[0,T]
,
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times d}
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle|f(t,y_{1},z_{1})-f(t,y_{2},z_{2})|^{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle\ C\big[|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}+|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}\big],

\displaystyle\displaystyle|g(t,y_{1},z_{1})-g(t,y_{2},z_{2})|^{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle\ C|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}+\alpha|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2}.

(2.2)

Proposition 2.3
.

Under
1
, BDSDEs (
2.1
) admit a unique solution
(Y,Z)\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{k})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{k\times d})
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Suppose that the coefficients
(f^{1},g,\xi^{1})
and
(f^{2},g,\xi^{2})
satisfy
1
,
and let
(Y^{1},Z^{1})
and
(Y^{2},Z^{2})
be the solutions of one-dimensional BDSDEs (
2.1
) with parameters
(f^{1},g,\xi^{1})
and

3
Quadratic BDSDE

In this section, we study the existence of one-dimensional BDSDEs with quadratic growth, i.e.,
k=1
.
Before proving the existence, we study a priori estimate and the monotone stability, which are important for us to prove existence later.
In order to justify the assumptions that were used to prove the existence, we give two examples. It should be pointed out that the first example also implies the main idea to prove existence, which is the central technique throughout this section.

Example 3.1
.

Consider the following backward doubly stochastic differential equation,

Y_{t}=\xi+C\int_{t}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds+\alpha\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad~0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

(3.1)

where
C>0
and
\alpha\in(-1,1)
are two constants. Let
\beta
be a positive constant which will be given later. Applying the exponential change of variable to
y=\exp(\beta Y)
implies that

\displaystyle\displaystyle y_{T}=

\displaystyle~y_{t}-C\int_{t}^{T}\beta y_{s}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds-\alpha\int_{t}^{T}\beta y_{s}Z_{s}d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}+\int_{t}^{T}\beta y_{s}Z_{s}dW_{s}

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\int_{t}^{T}\beta^{2}y_{s}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{T}\beta^{2}y_{s}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~y_{t}-\Big[C-\frac{1-\alpha^{2}}{2}\beta\Big]\int_{t}^{T}\beta y_{s}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds-\alpha\int_{t}^{T}\beta y_{s}Z_{s}d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}+\int_{t}^{T}\beta y_{s}Z_{s}dW_{s}.

Let
\beta=\frac{2C}{1-\alpha^{2}}
and denote by
z=\beta yZ
, then the above equation can be rewritten as

\displaystyle y_{t}=\exp(\beta\xi)+\int_{t}^{T}\alpha z_{s}d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}z_{s}dW_{s},\quad~0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

(3.2)

which essential is a linear backward doubly stochastic differential equation. Then the classical existence and uniqueness (see
2.3
) implies that when

\exp(\beta\xi)\in L^{2}_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}),

(3.3)

there exists a unique solution
(y,z)\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
for BDSDE (
3.2
).
Note that,
\beta
is positive constant, so taking
\xi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
is a sufficient condition to insure that
\xi
satisfies (
3.3
). In fact, if
\xi\in L_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
, then
\exp(\xi)\in L_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
and (
3.3
) holds,
thus Eq. (
3.2
) admits a unique solution
(y,z)\in S^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, for BDSDE (
3.2
), by the comparison theorem (see
2.4
), one has

\exp(-\beta\|\xi\|_{\infty})\leqslant y_{t}\leqslant\exp(\beta\|\xi\|_{\infty}),\quad~\forall t\in[0,T],

which implies that
y_{t}
is positive and bounded. Now, we can define
(Y,Z)
by

Y_{t}=\frac{\ln(y_{t})}{\beta},\quad~Z_{t}=\frac{z_{t}}{\beta y_{t}},\quad~\forall t\in[0,T].

Then, it is easy to check that the pair
(Y,Z)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
defined above is a solution of BDSDE (
3.1
).
Finally, the uniqueness of (
3.1
) in
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
follows from the uniqueness of (
3.2
) and the fact that the exponential change of variable is no longer formal.

Example 3.2
.

Consider the following linear backward doubly stochastic differential equation,

Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}(H+d_{s}Y_{s})d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad~0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

(3.4)

where
H
is a constant and
d
is a bounded deterministic function.
From
2.3
, BDSDE (
3.4
) admits a unique solution
(Y,Z)
.
Moreover, Proposition 2.1 of Bally–Matoussi
[
3
]
implies that the solution
Y
is given explicitly by

Y_{t}=\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi(t,T)\xi+H\int_{t}^{T}d_{s}\Psi(t,s)ds\bigg|\mathscr{F}_{t}\right],\quad\ 0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

where

\Psi(t,s)=\exp\left(\int_{t}^{s}d_{r}d\overleftarrow{B}_{r}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{s}|d_{r}|^{2}dr\right),\quad\ t\leqslant s\leqslant T,

which is not bounded even if the terminal value
\xi
is bounded.

Remark 3.3
.

On the one hand,
3.1
tells us that why we require the boundedness of the terminal value
\xi
. On the other hand,
3.2
implies that even in the classical situation with bounded terminal value
\xi
, the normal Lipschitz condition of the coefficient
g
with respect to
y
(see (
2.2
)) could not guarantee that
Y
is bounded.

3.1
A priori estimate

In this subsection, we prove a priori estimate for the solution
(Y,Z)
of quadratic BDSDEs. First, we introduce the assumption.

Assumption 2
.

Suppose that the terminal value
\xi\in L^{\infty}_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
.
Let
a:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
b:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
be two functions, and
C
and
\alpha
be two positive constants with
\alpha\in(0,1)
. For all
(t,y,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle f(t,y,z)=a_{0}(t,y,z)y+f_{0}(t,y,z)

with

\displaystyle a_{0}(t,y,z)\leqslant a_{t},\quad~|f_{0}(t,y,z)|\leqslant b_{t}+C|z|^{2},\quad~\hbox{a.s.}

and

\displaystyle|g(t,y,z)|^{2}\leqslant\alpha|z|^{2},\quad~\hbox{a.s.}

(3.5)

Remark 3.4
.

We point out that the boundedness of the solution
Y
requires us to make the condition (
3.5
) concerning the coefficient
g
; if not, the solution
Y
may not be bounded even if the terminal value
\xi
is bounded (see
3.2
and
3.3
).
Here we give an example that satisfying (
3.5
):

g(t,y,z)=\sin(y)l(z)\quad~\hbox{with}\quad~|l(z)|^{2}\leqslant\alpha|z|^{2},\quad~\forall(t,y,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Proposition 3.5

(A priori estimate)
.

Let
(Y,Z)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a solution of BDSDE (
2.1
) with parameters
(f,g,\xi)
, which satisfy
2
with
a,b,C,\alpha
, such that

a^{+}=\max(a,0),\quad~b\in L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R}).

Then for every
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle Y_{t}\leqslant

\displaystyle\ \Big[\sup_{\Omega}(Y_{T})\Big]^{+}\cdot\exp\Big(\int_{t}^{T}a_{s}ds\Big)+\int_{t}^{T}b_{s}\exp\Big(\int_{t}^{s}a_{r}dr\Big)ds,\quad~\hbox{a.s.}

(3.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bigg(\hbox{resp. }Y_{t}\geqslant

\displaystyle\ \Big[\inf_{\Omega}(Y_{T})\Big]^{-}\cdot\exp\Big(\int_{t}^{T}a_{s}ds\Big)-\int_{t}^{T}b_{s}\exp\Big(\int_{t}^{s}a_{r}dr\Big)ds,\quad~\hbox{a.s.}\bigg).

(3.7)

Moreover, there exists a positive constant
K
depending only on
\|Y\|_{\infty},\ \|a^{+}\|_{L^{1}}
,
C
and
\alpha
such that

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\leqslant K.

(3.8)

Proof.

Consider the following linear ordinary differential equation,

\varphi_{t}=\Big[\sup_{\Omega}(Y_{T})\Big]^{+}+\int_{t}^{T}(a_{s}\varphi_{s}+b_{s})ds,\quad~0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

Then someone could compute that for
0\leqslant t\leqslant T
,

\varphi_{t}=\Big[\sup_{\Omega}(Y_{T})\Big]^{+}\cdot\exp\Big(\int_{t}^{T}a_{s}ds\Big)+\int_{t}^{T}b_{s}\exp\Big(\int_{t}^{s}a_{r}dr\Big)ds.

So the result (
3.6
) holds if we can prove that
Y_{t}\leqslant\varphi_{t}
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Applying Itô’s formula (see
2.2
) to the process
Y_{t}-\varphi_{t}
and to an increasing
C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
function
\Phi
, which will be determined later, deduce that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi(Y_{t}-\varphi_{t})=

\displaystyle\ \Phi(Y_{T}-\varphi_{T})+\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})[f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})-(a_{s}\varphi_{s}+b_{s})]ds

\displaystyle\ +\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})Z_{s}dW_{s}

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})|g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})|^{2}ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})|Z_{s}|^{2}ds.

We denote that for every
s\in[t,T]
,

\tilde{a}_{s}=a_{0}(s,Y_{s},Z_{s}).

From
2
, note that the function
\Phi
is increasing, we have that for every
s\in[t,T]
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}

\displaystyle(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})\big[f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})-(a_{s}\varphi_{s}+b_{s})\big]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ \Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})\big[\tilde{a}_{s}Y_{s}+b_{s}+C|Z_{s}|^{2}-(a_{s}\varphi_{s}+b_{s})\big]

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\ \Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})\big[\tilde{a}_{s}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})+(\tilde{a}_{s}-a_{s})\varphi_{s}+C|Z_{s}|^{2}\big],

and

\Phi^{\prime\prime}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})|g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})|^{2}\leqslant\alpha\Phi^{\prime\prime}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})|Z_{s}|^{2}.

Due to that
(\tilde{a}_{s}-a_{s})\varphi_{s}\leqslant 0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi(Y_{t}-\varphi_{t})\leqslant

\displaystyle\ \Phi(Y_{T}-\varphi_{T})+\int_{t}^{T}\tilde{a}_{s}\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})ds

\displaystyle\ +\int_{t}^{T}\Big(C\Phi^{\prime}-\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}\Big)(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})|Z_{s}|^{2}ds

(3.9)

\displaystyle\ +\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})Z_{s}dW_{s}.

Set
M=\|Y\|_{\infty}+\|\varphi\|_{\infty}
and define the function
\Phi
on the interval
[-M,M]
by

\Phi(u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle e^{\frac{2C}{1-\alpha}u}-1-\frac{2C}{1-\alpha}u-\frac{2C^{2}}{(1-\alpha)^{2}}u^{2},&u\in[0,M],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle 0,&u\in[-M,0].\end{array}\right.

Then it is easy to check that for all
u\in[-M,M]
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi(u)\geqslant 0,\ \hbox{ and }\ \Phi(u)=

\displaystyle\ 0\ \hbox{ if and only if }\ u\leqslant 0,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(u)\geqslant

\displaystyle\ 0,

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0\leqslant u\Phi^{\prime}(u)\leqslant

\displaystyle\ (M+1)\frac{2C}{1-\alpha}\Phi(u),

\displaystyle\displaystyle C\Phi^{\prime}-\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}\leqslant

\displaystyle\ 0.

Hence, if we set

k_{t}=a^{+}_{t}(M+1)\frac{2C}{1-\alpha},

then the function
k
is positive and deterministic.
Note that
Y_{T}-\varphi_{T}\leqslant 0
implies that
\Phi(Y_{T}-\varphi_{T})=0
, and thus for all
0\leqslant t\leqslant T
, one has

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0\leqslant

\displaystyle\Phi(Y_{t}-\varphi_{t})\leqslant\int_{t}^{T}k_{s}\Phi(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})ds

(3.10)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})Z_{s}dW_{s}.

Note that the term
\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})
is bounded on the interval
t\leqslant s\leqslant T
, so the terms
\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})Z_{s}
and
\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})
belong to the spaces

L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{l})
, respectively.
Taking expectations on both side of the inequality (
3.10
) implies that

0\leqslant\mathbb{E}\Phi(Y_{t}-\varphi_{t})\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}k_{s}\Phi(Y_{s}-\varphi_{s})ds.

Then Gronwall’s inequality yields that

\mathbb{E}\Phi(Y_{t}-\varphi_{t})=0,\quad~\forall t\in[0,T].

Therefore, note that
\Phi(u)\geqslant 0
, for all
t\in[0,T],

\Phi(Y_{t}-\varphi_{t})=0,\quad~\hbox{ a.s.}

Finally, again, note that
\Phi(u)=0
if and only if
u\leqslant 0
, we obtain that for every
t\in[0,T],

Y_{t}-\varphi_{t}\leqslant 0,\quad~\hbox{ a.s.},

which implies that the inequality (
3.6
) holds.
Similarly, by using the same computation, one can prove that (
3.7
) holds too.

Finally, in order to prove the estimate (
3.8
), we use again (
3.1
) with
t=0,\ \varphi=0
,
M=\|Y\|_{\infty}
,
and define
\Phi
on
[-M,M]
by

\Phi(u)=\frac{1-\alpha}{2C^{2}}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{2C}{1-\alpha}(u+M)\Big)-1-\frac{2C}{1-\alpha}(u+M)\Big].

It is easy to check that, for every
u\in[-M,M]
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi(u)\geqslant

\displaystyle\ 0,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(u)\geqslant

\displaystyle\ 0,

\displaystyle\displaystyle 0\leqslant u\Phi^{\prime}(u)\leqslant

\displaystyle\ \frac{M}{C}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{4CM}{1-\alpha}\Big)-1\Big],

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}-C\Phi^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\ 1.

Then (
3.1
) implies that

\displaystyle 0\leqslant\Phi(Y_{0})\leqslant

\displaystyle\ \Phi(Y_{T})+\int_{0}^{T}a_{s}^{+}\frac{M}{C}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{4CM}{1-\alpha}\Big)-1\Big]ds-\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\ +\int_{0}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s})g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}-\int_{0}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(Y_{s})Z_{s}dW_{s},

which leads that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|Z_{s}|^{2}ds\leqslant\Phi(M)+\frac{M}{C}\Big[\exp\Big(\frac{4CM}{1-\alpha}\Big)-1\Big]\|a^{+}\|_{L^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{R})}.

This completes the proof.
∎

An immediate consequence of this proposition is the following corollary.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
(Y,Z)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a solution of BDSDE with parameters
(f,g,\xi)
, where
\xi
belongs to
L^{\infty}_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
, and
f
and
g
satisfy the condition
2
with
a,b
,
C>0
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that
a^{+},b\in L^{1}(0,T;\mathbb{R})
, then

\|Y\|_{\infty}\leqslant\big(\|\xi\|_{\infty}+\|b\|_{L^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{R})}\big)\exp\big(\|a^{+}\|_{L^{1}([0,T];\mathbb{R})}\big).

3.2
Monotone stability

In this subsection, we would like to study the monotone stability of BDSDEs (
2.1
) with quadratic growth, which is also important for us to prove the existence later.

Proposition 3.7

(Monotone stability)
.

Let
(f,g,\xi)
be a set of parameters and let
(f^{n},g,\xi^{n})
be a sequence of parameters such that:

\rm(i)

There exist two positive constants
C
and
\alpha
with
0<\alpha<1
such that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle|f^{n}(t,y,z)|\leqslant C(1+|y|+|z|^{2}),\quad~|g(t,y,z)|^{2}\leqslant\alpha|z|^{2},\quad~\forall t\in[0,T],y\in\mathbb{R},z\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(3.11)

\displaystyle|g(t,y_{1},z_{1})-g(t,y_{2},z_{2})|^{2}\leqslant C|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}+\alpha|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2},\quad~\forall t\in[0,T],y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R},z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

\rm(ii)

The sequence
\{f^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
converges to
f
locally uniformly on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and for each
n\in\mathbb{N},
the sequence
\{\xi^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
converges to
\xi
in
L^{\infty}_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
.

\rm(iii)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the BDSDE with parameters
(f^{n},g,\xi^{n})
has a solution

(Y^{n},Z^{n})\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and the sequence
\{Y^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is monotonic such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\|Y^{n}\|_{\infty}\leqslant M
, where
M
is a positive constant.

Then there exists a pair of processes
(Y,Z)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle&\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}Y^{n}=Y\hbox{ uniformy on }[0,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle&\{Z^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}\hbox{ converges to $Z$ in }L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d}).\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover,
(Y,Z)
is a solution of BDSDE with parameters
(f,g,\xi)
. In particular, if the sequence
\{Y^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
has continuous paths for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
Y
has continuous paths too.

Proof.

From the assumptions we see that for all
t\in[0,T]
, the sequence
\{Y^{n}_{t};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is monotonic and bounded, and therefore it has a limit, which denoted by
Y
.
Without lose of generality, we consider that the sequence
\{Y^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is monotonic increasing to
Y
.
Moreover, from
3.5
, there is a positive constant
K
such that,

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|Z^{n}|^{2}ds\leqslant K,\quad~\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then there exists a process
Z\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that a subsequence of
\{Z^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
converges to
Z
weakly in
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
For simplicity presentation, by otherwise choosing a subsequence, we may assume that the whole sequence
\{Z^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
converges to
Z
weakly in
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Next, we would like to divide the proof into two steps, and denote the following notations:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Delta Y^{n}\triangleq Y-Y^{n},\quad\ \Delta Y^{m,n}\triangleq Y^{m}-Y^{n},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Delta Z^{n}\triangleq Z-Z^{n},\quad\ \Delta Z^{m,n}\triangleq Z^{m}-Z^{n}.

\emph{Step1.}
The sequence
\{Z^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
strongly converges to
Z
in
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, i.e.,

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\Delta Z^{n}_{t}|^{2}dt=0.

The main arguments of this step come from Zhang
[
36
]
and Kobylanski
[
20
]
.
It is easy to see that the pair
(\Delta Y^{m,n},\Delta Z^{m,n})
satisfies the following equation,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-d\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t}=[f^{m}(t,Y^{m}_{t},Z^{m}_{t})-f^{n}(t,Y^{n}_{t},Z^{n}_{t})]dt\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\ \ +[g(t,Y^{m}_{t},Z^{m}_{t})-g(t,Y^{n}_{t},Z^{n}_{t})]d\overleftarrow{B}_{t}-\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}dW_{t},\quad~t\in[0,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle\quad\ \Delta Y^{m,n}_{T}=\ \xi^{m}-\xi^{n}.\end{array}\right.

By assumptions, there exists a constant
C_{0}
depending on
C
and
M
, such that for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle|f^{m}(t,Y^{m}_{t},Z^{m}_{t})-f^{n}(t,Y^{n}_{t},Z^{n}_{t})|\leqslant

\displaystyle C_{0}[1+|\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}+|\Delta Z^{n}_{t}|^{2}+|Z_{t}|^{2}],

(3.12)

\displaystyle\displaystyle|g(t,Y^{m}_{t},Z^{m}_{t})-g(t,Y^{n}_{t},Z^{n}_{t})|^{2}\leqslant

\displaystyle C|\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}+\alpha|\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a smooth increasing function which will be specified later. For
m\geqslant n
, using Itô’s formula (see
2.2
) to
\Phi(\Delta Y^{m,n})
we have that

\displaystyle\displaystyle d\Phi(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})=

\displaystyle-\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})[f^{m}(t,Y^{m}_{t},Z^{m}_{t})-f^{n}(t,Y^{n}_{t},Z^{n}_{t})]dt

\displaystyle-\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})[g(t,Y^{m}_{t},Z^{m}_{t})-g(t,Y^{n}_{t},Z^{n}_{t})]d\overleftarrow{B}_{t}+\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}dW_{t}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})|g(t,Y^{m}_{t},Z^{m}_{t})-g(t,Y^{n}_{t},Z^{n}_{t})|^{2}dt+\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})|\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle-C_{0}\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})[1+|\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}+|\Delta Z^{n}_{t}|^{2}+|Z_{t}|^{2}]dt

\displaystyle-\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})[g(t,Y^{m}_{t},Z^{m}_{t})-g(t,Y^{n}_{t},Z^{n}_{t})]d\overleftarrow{B}_{t}+\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}dW_{t}

\displaystyle-\frac{C}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})|\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}dt-\frac{\alpha}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})|\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}dt+\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})|\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}dt,

where we have use the inequality (
3.12
).

In the following, we expect
\Delta Z^{m,n}
and
\Delta Z^{n}
to be closed. For this purpose, define

\Phi(u)=\frac{1-\alpha}{8C_{0}^{2}}\Big[\exp(\frac{4C_{0}}{1-\alpha}u)-\frac{4C_{0}}{1-\alpha}u-1\Big].

It is straightforward to check that for
u\geqslant 0
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(u)\geqslant 0\quad\hbox{with}\quad\Phi(0)=0,

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(u)\geqslant 0\quad\hbox{with}\quad\Phi^{\prime}(0)=0,

\displaystyle\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(u)=4C_{0}\Phi^{\prime}(u)+2.

Note that
Y^{m}\geqslant Y^{n}
when
m\geqslant n
. Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle d\Phi(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})\geqslant

\displaystyle-C_{0}\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})[1+|\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}+|\Delta Z^{n}_{t}|^{2}+|Z_{t}|^{2}]dt

\displaystyle-\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})[g(t,Y^{m}_{t},Z^{m}_{t})-g(t,Y^{n}_{t},Z^{n}_{t})]d\overleftarrow{B}_{t}+\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}dW_{t}

\displaystyle+[4C_{0}\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})+2]|\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}dt-\frac{C}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})|\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-C_{0}\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})[1+|\Delta Z^{n}_{t}|^{2}+|Z_{t}|^{2}]dt-\frac{C}{2}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})|\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}dt

\displaystyle-\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})[g(t,Y^{m}_{t},Z^{m}_{t})-g(t,Y^{n}_{t},Z^{n}_{t})]d\overleftarrow{B}_{t}+\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}dW_{t}

\displaystyle+[3C_{0}\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})+2]|\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}dt,

and thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Phi(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{0})+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}[3C_{0}\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})+2]|\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}dt-C_{0}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})|\Delta Z^{n}_{t}|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Phi(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{T})+C_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})[1+|Z_{t}|^{2}]dt+\frac{C}{2}\int_{t}^{T}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t})|\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}dt\Big].

Due to that
\{Z^{m};m\geqslant 1\}
converges to
Z
weakly in
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, so next we would like to
fix
n
and pass to the limit as
m\rightarrow\infty
.
The convergence of
Y^{m}\rightarrow Y
being pointwise, and
Y^{m}
being bounded by
M
, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, we have that

\displaystyle\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}[3C_{0}\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{n}_{t})+2]|\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}dt-C_{0}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{n}_{t})|\Delta Z^{n}_{t}|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Phi(\Delta Y^{n}_{T})+C_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{n}_{t})[1+|Z_{t}|^{2}]dt+\frac{C}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\Delta Y^{n}_{t})|\Delta Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}dt\Big],

and as

\displaystyle\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\bigg[-\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{n}_{t})|\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}dt\bigg]\leqslant-\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{n}_{t})|\Delta Z^{n}_{t}|^{2}dt,

we obtain that

\displaystyle\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}[2C_{0}\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{n}_{t})+2]|\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}dt

(3.13)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\Phi(\Delta Y^{n}_{T})+C_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{n}_{t})[1+|Z_{t}|^{2}]dt+\frac{C}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\Delta Y^{n}_{t})|\Delta Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}dt\Big].

Noting that by the convexity of the lower semi-continuous functional

J(Z)=\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\Delta Z^{n}_{t}|^{2}dt,

we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\Delta Z^{n}_{t}|^{2}dt\leqslant\liminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\Delta Z^{m,n}_{t}|^{2}dt.

(3.14)

Hence, combining (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) we have that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\Delta Z^{n}_{t}|^{2}dt\leqslant\mathbb{E}\Big[\Phi(\Delta Y^{n}_{T})+C_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\Phi^{\prime}(\Delta Y^{n}_{t})[1+|Z_{t}|^{2}]dt+\frac{C}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\Phi^{\prime\prime}(\Delta Y^{n}_{t})|\Delta Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}dt\Big].

By Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem, and note that
\Phi(0)=\Phi^{\prime}(0)=0
, we have that as
n\rightarrow\infty
, the right-hand side of the above inequality converges to 0, as well as the left-hand side. Now, passing to the limit as
n\rightarrow\infty
on the above inequality, we obtain that

\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\Delta Z^{n}_{t}|^{2}dt=0,

which implies that the sequence
\{Z^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
strongly converges to
Z
in
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

\emph{Step2.}
The uniform convergence of a subsequence of
\{Y^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
to
Y
.

At this stage we know that for all
t\in[0,T]
,
{\rm lim}_{n\rightarrow\infty}Y^{n}_{t}=Y_{t}
, and the sequence
\{Z^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
converges to
Z
in
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then from Lemma 2.5 of Kobylanski
[
20
]
, there exists a subsequence
\{Z^{n_{j}};n_{j}\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
\{Z^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that

Z^{n_{j}}
converges almost surely to
Z
and such that

\widetilde{Z}\triangleq\sup_{j}|Z^{n_{j}}|\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d}).

For simplicity presentation, by otherwise choosing a subsequence, we would like to still assume that the whole sequence
\{Z^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
converges almost surely to
Z
in
L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and thus we have

Z^{n}\rightarrow Z\ \hbox{ a.s. }dt\otimes d\mathbb{P}\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~\widetilde{Z}=\sup_{n}|Z^{n}|\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Recall that the sequence
\{f^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
converges locally uniformly to
f
, we obtain that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}f^{n}\left(t,Y_{t}^{n},Z_{t}^{n}\right)=f\left(t,Y_{t},Z_{t}\right),\quad~t\in[0,T],\ a.s.

In addition, due to that
f^{n}
satisfies the condition (
3.11
), one has

\left|f^{n}\left(t,Y_{t}^{n},Z_{t}^{n}\right)\right|\leqslant C(1+M+\sup_{n}\left|Z_{t}^{n}\right|^{2})\leqslant C_{0}(1+\widetilde{Z}_{t}^{2}).

Thus, Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem gives

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}f^{n}(t,Y^{n}_{t},Z^{n}_{t})dt=\int_{0}^{T}f(t,Y_{t},Z_{t})dt,\quad~a.s.

Similarly, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|g(t,Y^{n}_{t},Z_{t}^{n})-g(t,Y_{t},Z_{t})|^{2}dt\leqslant\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}[C|\Delta Y^{n}_{t}|^{2}+\alpha|\Delta Z^{n}_{t}|^{2}]dt=0,

and therefore

g(t,Y^{n},Z^{n})\rightarrow g(t,Y,Z)\ \hbox{ a.s. }dt\otimes d\mathbb{P}\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~\sup_{n}|g(t,Y^{n},Z^{n})|\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d}).

On the other hand, from the continuity properties of stochastic integral, we get that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\Big|\int_{t}^{T}Z^{n}_{s}dW_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s}\Big|=0\quad\hbox{in probability,}

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\Big|\int_{t}^{T}g(s,Y^{n}_{s},Z^{n}_{s})d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}\Big|=0\quad\hbox{in probability.}

(3.15)

Extracting a subsequence again if necessary, we may assume that the above convergence is
\mathbb{P}
-a.s.
Finally, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\Delta Y^{m,n}_{t}|\leqslant

\displaystyle~|\Delta Y^{m,n}_{T}|+\int_{t}^{T}|f^{m}(s,Y^{m}_{s},Z^{m}_{s})-f^{n}(s,Y^{n}_{s},Z^{n}_{s})|ds

\displaystyle+\Big|\int_{t}^{T}[g(s,Y^{m}_{s},Z^{m}_{s})-g(s,Y^{n}_{s},Z^{n}_{s})]d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}\Big|+\Big|\int_{t}^{T}\Delta Z^{m,n}_{s}dW_{s}\Big|.

Now fix
n
, and taking limits on
m
and supremum over
t\in[0,T]
, we obtain that for almost all
\omega\in\Omega
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}|\Delta Y^{n}_{t}|\leqslant

\displaystyle\ |\Delta Y^{n}_{T}|+\int_{t}^{T}|f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})-f^{n}(s,Y^{n}_{s},Z^{n}_{s})|ds

\displaystyle+\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\Big|\int_{t}^{T}[g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})-g(s,Y^{n}_{s},Z^{n}_{s})]d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}\Big|+\sup_{0\leqslant t\leqslant T}\Big|\int_{t}^{T}\Delta Z^{n}_{s}dW_{s}\Big|,

from which we deduce that the sequence
\{Y^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
converges to
Y
uniformly for
t\in[0,T]
. In particular,
Y
is continuous if the sequence
\{Y^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is.

Finally, we could pass to the limit in

\displaystyle Y^{n}_{t}=Y^{n}_{T}+\int_{t}^{T}f^{n}(s,Y^{n}_{s},Z^{n}_{s})ds+\int_{t}^{T}g(s,Y^{n}_{s},Z^{n}_{s})d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}Z^{n}_{s}dW_{s},\quad~0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

deducing that the pair
(Y,Z)
is a solution of BDSDE with parameters
(f,g,\xi)
.
∎

Remark 3.8
.

It should be point out that the solution here we obtain might not be unique, due to that the limit generator
f
is of quadratic growth in
z
, but not satisfies a comparison theorem. A stability result of BDSDE will be given (see
Theorem 4.6
) after the comparison theorem is proved in Section
4
.

3.3
Existence

Based on the results of a priori estimate and the monotone stability, now we can prove the existence of solutions of one-dimensional BDSDEs (
2.1
) with quadratic growth.

Assumption 3
.

Suppose that the terminal value
\xi
belongs to
L^{\infty}_{\mathscr{F}_{T}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R})
, and
c:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a continuous increasing function. Moreover, there exist some constants
a
,
b
,
C
,
d\in\mathbb{R}
and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that for all
t\in[0,T],y,\bar{y}\in\mathbb{R},z,\bar{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle f(t,y,z)=a_{0}(t,y,z)y+f_{0}(t,y,z)

with

\displaystyle d\leqslant a_{0}(t,y,z)\leqslant a,\quad~|f_{0}(t,y,z)|\leqslant b+c(|y|)|z|^{2},\quad~|g(t,y,z)|^{2}\leqslant\alpha|z|^{2},\quad~\hbox{a.s.}

\displaystyle|g(t,y_{1},z_{1})-g(t,y_{2},z_{2})|^{2}\leqslant C|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{2}+\alpha|z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2},\quad~\hbox{a.s.}

Remark 3.9
.

Under
3
, the coefficient
g
is bounded with respect to
y
and
z
. However, if the coefficient
g
is independent of
y
, then it is of linear growth and Lipschitz continuous with respect to
z
.
In fact,
3
implies that

g(t,y,0)=0,\quad~\forall(t,y)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}.

Here we present two examples that satisfying
3
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hbox{(i)}\quad~g(t,y,z)=

\displaystyle\ \alpha\sin(y)\cos(z),\quad~\forall(t,y,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d};

\displaystyle\displaystyle\hbox{(ii)}\quad~g(t,y,z)=

\displaystyle\ l(z),\quad~|l(z)|^{2}\leqslant\alpha|z|^{2},\quad~\forall(t,y,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

The following theorem is the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.10

(Existence)
.

Suppose that the parameters
(f,g,\xi)
satisfy
3
, then BDSDE (
2.1
) has at least one solution
(Y,Z)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
Y
is continuous.
Moreover, there exists a maximal solution
(Y^{*},Z^{*})

(
resp. a minimal solution
(Y_{*},Z_{*}))

such that for any set of parameters
(\hat{f},g,\hat{\xi})
, if

\hat{f}\leqslant f\quad\hbox{and}\quad\hat{\xi}\leqslant\xi\quad(\hbox{resp. }\hat{f}\geqslant f\quad\hbox{and}\quad\hat{\xi}\geqslant\xi),

one has

\hat{Y}\leqslant Y^{*}\quad(\hbox{resp. }\hat{Y}\geqslant Y_{*}),

where
(\hat{Y},\hat{Z})
is any solution of BDSDE with parameters
(\hat{f},g,\hat{\xi})
.

Proof.

The main idea here we used is that, first,
using an exponential change and a truncation argument in order to control the growth of the generator
f
in
z
and
y
, respectively. Second, finding a good approximation of
f
and constructing a sequence of uniformly Lipschitz continuous functions. Third, combine the previous result (
3.5
and
3.7
) to get the solution.

First, instead of
3
, we assume that the coefficient
f
satisfies the following condition:
There exist some constants
\tilde{a},\tilde{d}\in\mathbb{R}
and
B,C\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that for all
(t,y,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle f(t,y,z)=a_{0}(t,y,z)y+f_{0}(t,y,z)

(3.16)

with

\displaystyle\tilde{d}\leqslant a_{0}(t,y,z)\leqslant\tilde{a},\quad~|f_{0}(t,y,z)|\leqslant B+C|z|^{2},\quad~\hbox{a.s.}

(3.17)

Let
(F,g,\zeta)
be a set of parameters of BDSDE such that

F\leqslant f\quad\hbox{and}\quad\zeta\leqslant\xi,

(3.18)

and suppose that it has a solution
(\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
. The purpose is to find a solution
(Y,Z)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
of BDSDE with parameters
(f,g,\xi)
such that

\tilde{Y}\leqslant Y.

From
3.5
, if we set

\tilde{K}=(\|\xi\|_{\infty}+BT)\exp(\tilde{a}^{+}T),

then any solution
(Y,Z)
of BDSDE with parameters
(f,g,\xi)
has the following estimate,

\|Y\|_{\infty}\leqslant\tilde{K}.

Define

M=\max(\tilde{K},\|\tilde{Y}\|_{\infty}).

(3.19)

\emph{Step1.Theexponentialtransformation.}

In order to make a presentation more clearly, we rewrite BDSDE (
2.1
) as follows,

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}f(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds+\int_{t}^{T}g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad~0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

(3.20)

Let
\beta
be a positive constant which will be determined later.
Applying the exponential change of variable (see
2.2
) to
u=\exp(\beta Y)
transforms
formally BDSDE (
3.20
) with parameters
(f,g,\xi)
in a BDSDE with parameters
(\bar{f},\bar{g},\bar{\xi})
of the following,

\displaystyle u_{t}=\bar{\xi}+\int_{t}^{T}\bar{f}(s,u_{s},v_{s})ds+\int_{t}^{T}\bar{g}(s,u_{s},v_{s})d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}v_{s}dW_{s},\quad~0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

(3.21)

where

\displaystyle\bar{f}(t,u,v)=\beta uf(t,\frac{\ln u}{\beta},\frac{v}{\beta u})+\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}u\Big[g(t,\frac{\ln u}{\beta},\frac{v}{\beta u})^{2}-\Big(\frac{v}{\beta u}\Big)^{2}\Big],

(3.22)

\displaystyle\bar{g}(t,u,v)=\beta ug(t,\frac{\ln u}{\beta},\frac{v}{\beta u}),\qquad\bar{\xi}=\exp(\beta\xi),\qquad v=\beta uZ.

It is easy to check that
\bar{g}
and
\bar{\xi}
satisfy
3
. Similarly, the exponential change
u=\exp(\beta\tilde{Y})
transforms formally the BDSDE with parameters
(F,g,\zeta)
in a BDSDE with parameters

(\bar{F},\bar{g},\bar{\zeta})
where

\displaystyle\bar{F}(t,u,v)=\beta uF(s,\frac{\ln u}{\beta},\frac{v}{\beta u})+\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}u\Big[g(s,\frac{\ln u}{\beta},\frac{v}{\beta u})^{2}-\Big(\frac{v}{\beta u}\Big)^{2}\Big],\qquad\bar{\zeta}=\exp(\beta\zeta).

We consider a function
\Phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
such that

\Phi(u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle 1,\qquad\hbox{if }u\in[\exp(-\beta M),\exp(\beta M)],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle 0,\qquad\hbox{if }u\notin[\exp(-\beta(M+1)),\exp(\beta(M+1))],\end{array}\right.

and we set that for
(t,u,v)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\tilde{f}(t,u,v)=\Phi(u)\bar{f}(t,u,v),\quad~\tilde{F}(t,u,v)=\Phi(u)\bar{F}(t,u,v),\quad~l(u)=\Phi(u)\big(\tilde{a}u\ln u+B\beta u).

Now, let

\beta=\frac{2C}{1-\alpha},

(3.23)

then, note that (
3.17
), we have

\Phi(u)\Big(\tilde{d}u\ln u-B\beta u-(\frac{C}{\beta}+\frac{1}{2})\frac{|v|^{2}}{u}\Big)\leqslant\tilde{f}(t,u,v)\leqslant l(u).

We remark that
l
is a Lipschitz continuous function bounded from above by a
constant, denoted by
L
. Moreover, note that if we define

\tilde{y}=\exp(\beta\tilde{Y})\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~\tilde{z}=\beta\tilde{Z}\exp(\beta\tilde{Y}),

then the pair
(\tilde{y},\tilde{z})
is a solution of BDSDE with parameters
(\tilde{F},\bar{g},\bar{\zeta})
.

\emph{Step2.Theapproximation.}

We can approximate
\tilde{f}
by a decreasing sequence of uniformly Lipschitz continuous functions

\{\tilde{f}^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
such that for all
(t,u,v)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\tilde{F}(t,u,v)\leqslant\tilde{f}(t,u,v)\leqslant\tilde{f}^{n}(t,u,v)\leqslant l(u)+\frac{1}{2^{n}}.

Indeed, such a sequence exists. For instance,
denote by
m
an integral, and for each integral
n\geqslant m
, we would like to define the sequence of the functions
\{\tilde{f}^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
by

\tilde{f}_{n}(t,y,z)=\sup\left\{\tilde{f}(t,p,q)-n|p-y|-n|q-z|:(p,q)\in\mathbb{Q}^{1+d}\right\},

(3.24)

where
\mathbb{Q}
denotes the rational number.
It is easy to see that
\tilde{f}_{n}
is well defined and it is globally Lipschitz continuous in
y
and
z
with the constant

. In addition, the sequence
\{\tilde{f}^{n};n\geqslant m\}
is decreasing and converges pointwise to the function
\tilde{f}
.

Then the classical results of the existence and uniqueness (see
2.3
) and the comparison theorem (see
2.4
) for Lipschitz continuous coefficients give us that, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a unique solution
(y^{n},z^{n})

of the BDSDE with parameters
(\tilde{f}^{n},\bar{g},\bar{\xi})

\tilde{y}\leqslant y^{n+1}\leqslant y^{n}\leqslant y^{1}.

is bounded and positive. It should be point out that by the definition of

(see (

)) and

|\bar{g}(t,u,v)|^{2}\leqslant\alpha|v|^{2},\quad~\forall(t,u,v)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

(3.25)

which implies that
\bar{g}(t,u,v)=0
when

. Hence, again,
2.3
implies that, on the one hand, the pair of processes
\big\{\big(\exp(\beta M),0\big);t\in[0,T]\big\}

\big\{\big(\exp(-\beta M),0\big);t\in[0,T]\big\}
is the unique solution of BDSDE with parameters
\big(0,\bar{g},\exp(-\beta M)\big)
.
Moreover, since for
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough,

\exp(\beta\xi)\leqslant\exp(\beta M)\quad\text{ and }\quad\tilde{f}^{n}\big(t,\exp(\beta M),0\big)\leqslant 0,\quad~t\in[0,T],

and for all

,

then

gives us that for

large enough,

\exp(-\beta M)\leqslant y^{n+1}_{t}\leqslant y^{n}_{t}\leqslant\exp(\beta M),\quad~\hbox{a.s.}\ t\in[0,T].

(3.26)

Now, we come back to the previous problem by denoting

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{G}^{n}(t,y,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{f}^{n}(t,e^{\beta y},\beta e^{\beta y}z)}{\beta e^{\beta y}}+\frac{\beta}{2}\big[z^{2}-g(t,y,z)^{2}\big],

\displaystyle\displaystyle\tilde{G}(t,y,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\tilde{f}(t,e^{\beta y},\beta e^{\beta y}z)}{\beta e^{\beta y}}+\frac{\beta}{2}\big[z^{2}-g(t,y,z)^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=\Phi(e^{\beta y})f(t,y,z)+(1-\Phi(e^{\beta y}))\frac{\beta}{2}\big[z^{2}-g(t,y,z)^{2}\big].

Notice that
\beta

3.23
). Then, the pair
(Y^{n},Z^{n})
defined by

Y^{n}_{t}=\frac{\ln y^{n}_{t}}{\beta}\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~Z^{n}_{t}=\frac{z^{n}_{t}}{\beta y^{n}_{t}}

(\tilde{G}^{n},g,\xi)
. Let us recapitulate what we have obtained.

(i)

There exist two positive constants
K
and

such that for all

(iv)

.

Therefore, applying

(Y,Z)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})

\displaystyle Y_{t}=\xi+\int_{t}^{T}\tilde{G}(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})ds+\int_{t}^{T}g(s,Y_{s},Z_{s})d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}dW_{s},\quad~0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

In fact, it follows from

\displaystyle\tilde{G}(t,y,z)=\tilde{a}_{0}(t,y,z)y+\tilde{f}_{0}(t,y,z)

\displaystyle\tilde{a}_{0}(t,y,z)=\Phi(e^{\beta y})a_{0}(t,y,z)\leqslant a^{+},

). Then, notice that (

), we have

\displaystyle|\tilde{f}_{0}(t,y,z)|\leqslant B+\frac{3C}{1-\alpha}|z|^{2}.

So, by

, we obtain

\|Y\|_{\infty}\leqslant(\|\xi\|_{\infty}+BT)\exp(a^{+}T)=\tilde{K}\leqslant M.

Hence the pair
(Y,Z)
is also a solution of the BDSDE with parameters
(f,g,\xi)
.
In addition,

\tilde{Y}\leqslant Y\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~\|Y\|_{\infty}\leqslant M.

\emph{Step3.Thetruncation.}

Finally, we suppose that the generator
f
satisfies
3
. Let
(F,g,\zeta)
be a set of parameters of BDSDE such that the condition (
3.18
) hold true and denote by
(\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
a solution of BDSDE with the parameters

. For all
(t,y,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we set

\check{f}(t,y,z)=a_{0}(t,y,z)y+f_{0}(t,\phi_{M}(y)y,z)\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~\check{F}=F(t,\phi_{M}(y)y,z),

where

is defined in (

), and

\phi_{n}(u)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle 1,\qquad\hbox{if }|u|\leqslant n,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle 0,\qquad\hbox{if }|u|\geqslant n+1.\end{array}\right.

It is easy to check that the generator
\check{f}
satisfies the condition (
3.17
), and
(\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})
is also a solution of the BDSDE with parameters
(\check{F},g,\zeta)
. Then, by
Step 1
and

, we deduce that BDSDE with parameters

has a solution

such that

which implies that the pair of process

is also a solution of BDSDE with parameters

. then the existence of a maximal solution is proved.

Similarly, by using the same argument but with the change of variable

∎

Remark 3.11
.

Some ideas for proving
Theorem 3.10
are borrowed from Kobylanski
[
20
]
, however, due to the emergence of the backward Itô’s integral appeared in BDSDE (
2.1
), some new ideas and technics have been introduced and used. For example, the main idea of Kobylanski
[
20
]
is to take the exponential change to transform a quadratic BDSDE into a classical BDSDE essentially. While, this is not enough for us, since the presence of the backward Itô’s integral. In order to overcome the troubles, we introduce the idea of the comparison theorem of classical BDSDEs, which is evident from
3.1
.

4
Comparison Theorem

As we all know that the one-dimensional circumstance allows us to establish a comparison theorem for the solutions of BDSDEs, which implies the uniqueness of solutions of BDSDEs as a by-product. So in this section, we would like to prove a comparison theorem for the solutions of BDSDEs with quadratic growth. For
i=1,2
, we consider the following equation:

Y_{t}^{i}=\xi^{i}+\int_{t}^{T}f^{i}\left(s,Y_{s}^{i},Z_{s}^{i}\right)ds+\int_{t}^{T}g\left(s,Y_{s}^{i},Z_{s}^{i}\right)d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}Z_{s}^{i}dW_{s},\quad~0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

(4.1)

Then, under
3
, there exists a pair of measurable processes
(Y^{i},Z^{i})\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
that satisfies the corresponding BDSDE (
4.1
), where
i=1,2
. Moreover,
3.5
implies that there exists a positive constant
K
such that

\|Y^{i}\|_{\infty}+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|Z^{i}_{s}|^{2}ds\leqslant K,\quad~\forall t\in[0,T].

In order to prove the comparison theorem, we assume that the terminal value
\xi
is bounded, and the generator
f
is locally Lipschitz continuous and is of quadratic growth in
Z
in a strong sense.

Assumption 4
.

There exist three functions
l
,
l_{\varepsilon}
,
k:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and three positive constants
C
,
\varepsilon
, and
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that for all
t\in[0,T],\ y\in[-M,M]
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the coefficients
f
and
g
satisfy the following conditions:

\displaystyle\displaystyle|f(t,y,z)|

\displaystyle\leqslant l(t)+C|z|^{2},\qquad\ |g(t,y,z)|^{2}\leqslant\alpha|z|^{2},\quad~\text{ a.s., }

(4.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(t,y,z)

\displaystyle\leqslant l_{\varepsilon}(t)+\varepsilon|z|^{2},\quad~\Big|\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}(t,y,z)\Big|^{2}\leqslant C,\quad~\text{a.s.},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}(t,y,z)\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant k(t)+C|z|,\quad\ \ \Big|\frac{\partial g}{\partial z}(t,y,z)\Big|^{2}\leqslant\alpha.\quad~\text{ a.s. }

Similar to Kobylanski
[
20
]
, following the method used by Barles–Murat
[
4
]
for PDEs, we would like to divide the proof of the comparison theorem into two steps. First, we consider the comparison theorem under the condition that the generator
f
satisfies a structure condition (STR).
Then, we show the proof by giving a change of variable that transforms a BDSDE with the generator
f
satisfying
4
into a BDSDE with the generator
\widetilde{f}
satisfying this structure condition (STR).

Definition 4.1
.

We say that a generator
f
satisfies the structure condition (STR), if there exists a constant
a>0
and a function
b:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(t,y,z)+a\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\right|^{2}(t,y,z)\leqslant b(t),\quad~(t,y,z)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(4.3)

We have the following comparison theorem under the structure condition (STR).

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that both
\xi^{1}
and
\xi^{2}
are bounded, the coefficient
g
satisfies
4
, and either
f^{1}
or
f^{2}
satisfies the structure condition (STR) with the constant

and the function

.
For
i=1,2
, denote by
(Y^{i},Z^{i})
a solution of BDSDE (

) with parameters
(f^{i},g,\xi^{i})
, respectively.

(4.4)

then for any
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\displaystyle Y_{t}^{1}\leqslant Y_{t}^{2},\quad\hbox{a.s.}

.

(i) It should be point out that in the condition (STR), when

and the function

is bounded,

Proof of

.

For simplicity of presentation, we denote that for

\widehat{Y}_{t}=Y^{1}_{t}-Y^{2}_{t}\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~\widehat{Z}_{t}=Z^{1}_{t}-Z^{2}_{t},

Then, note that
\widehat{Y}^{+}=\max\{0,\widehat{Y}\}
,
using Itô’s formula (see

) to
\big(\widehat{Y}^{+}\big)^{p}
for

with

implies that

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\widehat{Y}_{t}^{+})^{p}=

\displaystyle p\int_{t}^{T}(\widehat{Y}^{+}_{s})^{p-1}\widehat{f}(s)ds+p\int_{t}^{T}(\widehat{Y}^{+}_{s})^{p-1}\widehat{g}(s)d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}-p\int_{t}^{T}(\widehat{Y}^{+}_{s})^{p-1}\widehat{Z}_{s}dW_{s}

(4.5)

\displaystyle+\frac{p(p-1)}{2}\int_{t}^{T}(\widehat{Y}^{+}_{s})^{p-2}|\widehat{g}(s)|^{2}ds-\frac{p(p-1)}{2}\int_{t}^{T}(\widehat{Y}^{+}_{s})^{p-2}|\widehat{Z}_{s}|^{2}ds.

For the term

in the above, note (

\displaystyle\displaystyle\widehat{f}(s)=

\displaystyle f^{1}(s,Y^{1}_{s},Z^{1}_{s})-f^{2}(s,Y^{2}_{s},Z^{2}_{s})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f^{1}(s,Y^{1}_{s},Z^{1}_{s})-f^{2}(s,Y^{1}_{s},Z^{1}_{s})+f^{2}(s,Y^{1}_{s},Z^{1}_{s})-f^{2}(s,Y^{2}_{s},Z^{2}_{s})

\displaystyle\displaystyle(\widehat{Y}^{+}_{s})^{p-1}\widehat{f}(s)\leqslant(\widehat{Y}^{+}_{s})^{p}\int_{0}^{1}\bigg(\frac{\partial f^{2}}{\partial y}+a\left|\frac{\partial f^{2}}{\partial z}\right|^{2}\bigg)(*)d\lambda+\frac{1}{4a}(\widehat{Y}^{+}_{s})^{p-2}|\widehat{Z}_{s}|^{2}{\bf 1}_{\{\widehat{Y}^{+}\geqslant 0\}},

For the term

in (

satisfied by the coefficient

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\widehat{g}(s)|^{2}=

\displaystyle\bigg\{\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}(*)d\lambda\Big)\widehat{Y}_{s}+\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial g}{\partial z}(*)d\lambda\Big)\widehat{Z}_{s}\bigg\}^{2}

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}(*)d\lambda\Big)^{2}|\widehat{Y}_{s}|^{2}+2\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}(*)d\lambda\Big)\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial g}{\partial z}(*)d\lambda\Big)|\widehat{Y}_{s}\widehat{Z}_{s}|+\Big(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\partial g}{\partial z}(*)d\lambda\Big)^{2}|\widehat{Z}_{s}|^{2}

\displaystyle\widetilde{C}|\widehat{Y}_{s}|^{2}+(\alpha+\varepsilon)|\widehat{Z}_{s}|^{2},

\displaystyle\displaystyle Y=\phi(\widetilde{Y}),\quad~w(Y)=\phi^{\prime}(\widetilde{Y}),\quad~Z=\phi^{\prime}(\widetilde{Y})\widetilde{Z}=w(Y)\widetilde{Z},

\displaystyle\quad+|z|\left[\frac{w^{\prime}}{w}\Big[k(t)+C\alpha\Big]+2ak(t)\Big(C+\frac{w^{\prime}}{w}(1+\alpha)\Big)\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\Big[\Big(\frac{w^{\prime}}{w}\Big)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{w^{\prime\prime}}{w}\Big]l(t)+\frac{w^{\prime}}{w}l(t)+l_{\varepsilon}(t)+ak(t)^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant|z|^{2}\left[\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\frac{w^{\prime\prime}}{w}+\frac{w^{\prime}}{w}2C+\Big(\frac{w^{\prime}}{w}\Big)^{2}+\varepsilon+2a\left(C+\frac{w^{\prime}}{w}(1+\alpha)\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\Big[\Big(\frac{w^{\prime}}{w}\Big)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{w^{\prime\prime}}{w}\Big]l(t)+\frac{w^{\prime}}{w}l(t)+l_{\varepsilon}(t)+ak(t)^{2}+\frac{1}{4(1-\alpha)}\Big[k(t)+C\alpha\Big]^{2}+ak(t)^{2},

where in the last inequality we have used the inequality
2\beta_{i}\gamma_{i}\leqslant|\beta_{i}|^{2}+|\gamma_{i}|^{2}
for
i=1,2
, with

\displaystyle\beta_{1}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-\alpha}\frac{w^{\prime}}{w}|z|,\quad~\gamma_{1}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{1-\alpha}}\Big[k(t)+C\alpha\Big];

\displaystyle\beta_{2}

\displaystyle=|z|\sqrt{a}\left(C+\frac{w^{\prime}}{w}(1+\alpha)\right),\quad~\gamma_{2}=\sqrt{a}k(t).

Thus, if we find
\phi
satisfying all the required assumptions and such that on
[-M,M]
,

\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\frac{w^{\prime\prime}}{w}+\frac{w^{\prime}}{w}2C+\Big(\frac{w^{\prime}}{w}\Big)^{2}<-\delta<0,

then choosing
a
and
\varepsilon
small enough, the coefficient before
|z|^{2}
is non-positive for all
y\in[-M,M]
. Therefore (STR) is satisfied. In fact, by setting

\phi(y)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\ln\left(\frac{e^{\lambda A\widetilde{y}}+1}{A}\right)-M,

then a straightforward yet tedious computation gives us that

w=A-\exp\{-\lambda(y+M)\},\quad~w^{\prime}=\lambda\exp\{-\lambda(y+M)\},\quad~w^{\prime\prime}=-\lambda^{2}\exp\{-\lambda(y+M)\},

which implies that
w^{\prime\prime}<0
for all
y\in[-M,M]
and
\lambda>0
. Moreover, when
A>1
and
\lambda>0
, it is easy to see that
w>0
and
w^{\prime}>0
on the interval
[-M,M]
. Furthermore, one could choose a proper
A
and
\lambda
such that

\displaystyle\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\frac{w^{\prime\prime}}{w}+\frac{w^{\prime}}{w}2C+\left(\frac{w^{\prime}}{w}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\exp(-\lambda(u+M))}{(A-\exp(-\lambda(u+M)))^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\Big[\lambda^{2}\Big(-(1-\alpha)A+(3-\alpha)\exp(-\lambda(u+M))\Big)+\lambda 4C(A-\exp(-\lambda(u+M)))\Big]

\displaystyle\leqslant-\delta<0.

The proof is complete.
∎

As an important application of the comparison theorem, we study the stability of BDSDEs.

Theorem 4.6

(Stability)
.

Let
(f,g,\xi)
be a set of parameters that satisfying the assumptions of
Theorem 4.4
, and let
(f^{n},g,\xi^{n})
be a sequence of parameters of BDSDEs such that:

(i)

There exist some constants
a
,
b
,
C
,
d\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and a continuous increasing function
c:\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the coefficients
(f^{n},g,\xi^{n})
satisfies
3
.

(ii)

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a solution
\left(Y^{n},Z^{n}\right)
to the BDSDE with parameters
(f^{n},g,\xi^{n})
.

(iii)

The sequence
\{f^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
converges to
f
locally uniformly on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and the sequence
\{\xi^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
converges to
\xi
in

.

Then there exists a pair of processes
(Y,Z)\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}Y^{n}=Y\hbox{ uniformy on }[0,T],\\
&\{Z^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}\hbox{ converges to $Z$ in }L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d}).\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover, the pair

is a solution of BDSDE with parameters
(f,g,\xi)
.

Proof.

For simplicity presentation, we define

\displaystyle\displaystyle H_{n}=

\displaystyle\ \inf_{m\geqslant n}f^{m},\quad~H^{n}=\sup_{m\geqslant n}f^{m},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\xi_{n*}=

\displaystyle\ \inf_{m\geqslant n}\xi^{m},\quad~\xi^{n*}=\sup_{m\geqslant n}\xi^{m},

and we consider the minimal solutions
\left(Y_{*}^{n}Z_{*}^{n}\right)
of BDSDE with parameters
\left(H^{n},\xi^{n*}\right)
, and the maximal solutions
\left(Y^{n*},Z^{n*}\right)
of BDSDE with parameters
\left(H_{n},\xi_{n*}\right)
, as both:

(i)

The sequence
\{H_{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is increasing and converges locally uniformly to

, and the sequence
\{\xi_{n*};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is increasing.

(ii)

The sequence
\{H^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is decreasing and converges locally uniformly to

, and the sequence
\{\xi^{n\ast};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is decreasing.

Then we have that

(i)

The sequence
\{Y^{n*};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded and decreasing, and for every

Hence, from

, there exists
\left(Y^{*},Z^{*}\right)
such that

, and

.

(ii)

The sequence
\{Y_{*}^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}

,

Hence, from

, there exists
\left(Y_{*},Z_{*}\right)
such that

, and

.

Finally, by

, for every

, one has that

therefore the sequence

converges uniformly to

. This completes the proof.
∎

5
Application to SPDE

When the generator
f
is of linear growth with respect to
y
and
z
, Pardoux–Peng
[
26
]
used BDSDEs to give a probabilistic representation for the classical solution of semilinear SPDEs; and Bally–Matoussi
[
3
]
and Zhang–Zhao
[
37
]
obtained the relationship between the solution of BDSDEs and the Sobolev solution of SPDEs.
Then, Wu–Zhang
[
31
]
got the Sobolev solution of related SPDEs when
f
is continuous and locally monotone in
y
.
Zhang–Zhao
[
39
]
and Bahlali et al.
[
2
]
used BDSDEs to prove the existence and uniqueness of related SPDEs when the generator
f
is of polynomial growth in
Y
and
grows in
Z
super-linearly (or sub-quadratically), respectively.
In this section, when the generator
f
is of quadratic growth in
z
, we use BDSDEs to give a probabilistic representation for the solutions of related semilinear SPDEs in Sobolev spaces, and use it to prove the existence and uniqueness of Sobolev solutions of the SPDEs, thus extending the nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula.

First, we recall some notations. For Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{H}
and
\mathbb{G}
, denote by
C_{l,b}^{k}(\mathbb{H};\mathbb{G})
the set of functions of class
C^{k}
from
\mathbb{H}
to
\mathbb{G}
, whose partial derivatives of order less than or equal to
k
are bounded.
Denote by
\mathcal{C}_{c}^{1,\infty}\left([0,T]\times\mathbb{H}\right)
the set of compactly supported functions
\varphi(t,x)
which are continuously derivable in the
t
-variable and infinitely continuously derivable in the
x
-variable.

Consider the following forward-backward doubly stochastic differential equation:

\displaystyle\displaystyle X^{t,x}_{s}=

\displaystyle\ x+\int_{t}^{s}b(X^{t,x}_{r})dr+\int_{t}^{s}\sigma(X^{t,x}_{r})dW_{r},\quad~t\leqslant s\leqslant T,

(5.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle Y^{t,x}_{s}=

\displaystyle\ h(X^{t,x}_{T})+\int_{s}^{T}f(r,X^{t,x}_{r},Y^{t,x}_{r},Z^{t,x}_{r})dr+\int_{s}^{T}g(r,X^{t,x}_{r},Y^{t,x}_{r},Z^{t,x}_{r})d\overleftarrow{B}_{r}-\int_{s}^{T}Z^{t,x}_{r}dW_{r},

(5.2)

where the coefficients
b
and
\sigma
come from
C_{l,b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
C_{l,b}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{n\times d})
, respectively. Then it is well known that the forward equation (
5.1
) admits a unique adapted solution, denoted by
\{X^{t,x}_{s};t\leqslant s\leqslant T\}
, which satisfying

\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{t\leqslant s\leqslant T}|X^{t,x}_{s}|^{p}\right]<\infty,\quad~\forall p>1.

Now, we would like to connect the forward-backward system (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) with quadratic growth to the following semilinear stochastic partial differential equation:

\displaystyle\displaystyle u(s,x)=

\displaystyle h(x)+\int_{s}^{T}\left\{\mathcal{L}u(r,x)+f\big(r,x,u(r,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(r,x)\big)\right\}dr

(5.3)

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{T}g\big(r,x,u(r,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(r,x)\big)d\overleftarrow{B}_{r},\quad t\leqslant s\leqslant T,

where
\sigma^{\top}
denotes the transposed of
\sigma
, and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}},\quad~\left(a_{ij}\right)=\sigma\sigma^{\top}.

(5.4)

Before introducing the definition of Sobolev solutions of SPDE (
5.3
), we let

\rho:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
be an integrable continuous positive function, and
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\rho^{-1}(x)\mathrm{d}x)
be the weighted
L^{2}
space endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{\rho}^{2}\triangleq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|u(x)|^{2}\rho^{-1}(x)dx.

Let us take the weight
\rho(x)=\exp\{F(x)\}
, where
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function and there is a positive constant
R>0
such that
F\in C_{l,b}^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}\right)
when
|x|>R
. For example, one can take
\rho(x)=\exp\{\delta|x|\}
with
\delta\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
or
\rho(x)=(1+|x|)^{q}
with
q>n+2
.

Let
\mathscr{H}
be the set of random fields
\left\{u(t,x);0\leqslant t\leqslant T,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}
such that
u(t,x)
is
\mathscr{F}_{t,T}^{B}
-measurable, and both
u
and
\sigma^{\top}\nabla u
belong to
L^{2}\left(\Omega\times(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathrm{d}\mathbb{P}\otimes\mathrm{d}t\otimes\rho^{-1}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)
. Then
\mathscr{H}
is a Banach space endowed with the following norm:

\|u\|_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}\triangleq\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{T}\left(|u(t,x)|^{2}+\left|(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(t,x)\right|^{2}\right)dt\rho^{-1}(x)dx\right].

Now we present the definition of Sobolev solution of SPDE (
5.3
).

Definition 5.1
.

We say that
u
is a Sobolev solution of SPDE (
5.3
), if
u\in\mathscr{H}
and for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}u(s,x)\partial_{s}\varphi(s,x)dsdx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}u(t,x)\varphi(t,x)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}h(x)\varphi(T,x)dx

(5.5)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(s,x)\cdot(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\varphi)(s,x)dsdx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}u\mathrm{div}[(b-\widetilde{A})\varphi](s,x)dsdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}f\big(s,x,u(s,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(s,x)\big)\varphi(s,x)dsdx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}g\big(s,x,u(s,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(s,x)\big)\varphi(s,x)dB_{s}dx,

where
A
is a
n
-vector whose coordinates are given by

\widetilde{A}_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial a_{ij}}{\partial x_{i}}
with
1\leqslant j\leqslant n.

It should be pointed out that, for the classical solution, indeed, if one supposes that
u
is a solution of SPDE (
5.3
) of class
C^{2}
, then, similar to the method of Pardoux–Peng
[
26
]
, applying
2.2
to
u(s,X^{t,x}_{s})
implies that the pair of processes
\{Y^{t,x}_{s},Z^{t,x}_{s};t\leqslant s\leqslant T\}
defined by

Y^{t,x}_{s}=u(s,X_{s}^{t,x})\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~Z^{t,x}_{s}=(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(s,X_{s}^{t,x})

is a solution of the backward doubly stochastic differential equation (
5.2
).

In the following, we discuss this relationship between the Sobolev solution of SPDEs and the solution of BDSDEs with quadratic growth. In particular, we first focus on a simple situation to better reflect the idea of solving the problem.

5.1
Simple situation

Consider the following type of backward doubly stochastic differential equations: for
t\leqslant s\leqslant T
,

Y^{t,x}_{s}=h(X^{t,x}_{T})+\int_{s}^{T}\big[f(r,X^{t,x}_{r},Y^{t,x}_{r},Z^{t,x}_{r})+C(Z^{t,x}_{r})^{2}\big]dr+\int_{s}^{T}\alpha Z^{t,x}_{r}d\overleftarrow{B}_{r}-\int_{s}^{T}Z^{t,x}_{r}dW_{r},

(5.6)

where
X^{t,x}
is the solution of (
5.1
),
C>0
and
\alpha\in(-1,1)
are two constants, and the functions
f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
h:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfy the following assumption.

Assumption 5
.

The function
h
is bounded, and there exist bounded functions
l:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
k:[0,T]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that for all
t\in[0,T],

x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
y\in\mathbb{R}
and
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the generator
f
satisfies the following condition:

\displaystyle\displaystyle|f(t,x,y,z)|\leqslant l(t),\quad~\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(t,x,y,z)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}(t,x,y,z)

\displaystyle\leqslant k(t),\quad~\text{a.s. }

Proposition 5.2
.

Under
5
, the following stochastic partial differential equation

\displaystyle\displaystyle u(s,x)=

\displaystyle h(x)+\int_{s}^{T}\left\{\mathcal{L}u(r,x)+f\big(r,x,u(r,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(r,x)\big)+C\big((\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(r,x)\big)^{2}\right\}dr

(5.7)

\displaystyle+\alpha\int_{s}^{T}(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(r,x)d\overleftarrow{B}_{r},\quad~t\leqslant s\leqslant T,

admits a unique Sobolev solution
u\in\mathscr{H}
, and
for every
t\in[0,T]
,

u(s,X_{s}^{t,x})=Y^{t,x}_{s}\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(s,X_{s}^{t,x})=Z^{t,x}_{s},\quad~a.s.,a.e.\ s\in[t,T],\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\{(Y_{s}^{t,x},Z_{s}^{t,x});t\leqslant s\leqslant T\}
is the unique solution of quadratic BDSDE (
5.6
).

Proof.

Applying the exponential transformation of variable to
\bar{Y}=\exp\{\beta Y\}
transforms
formally BDSDE (
5.6
) into the following BDSDE:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}=

\displaystyle\ \bar{Y}^{t,x}_{T}+\int_{s}^{T}\beta\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{r}f(r,X^{t,x}_{r},Y^{t,x}_{r},Z^{t,x}_{r})dr+\Big[C-\frac{1-\alpha^{2}}{2}\beta\Big]\int_{s}^{T}\beta\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{r}|Z^{t,x}_{r}|^{2}dr

\displaystyle\ +\alpha\int_{s}^{T}\beta\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{r}Z^{t,x}_{r}d\overleftarrow{B}_{r}-\int_{s}^{T}\beta\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{r}Z^{t,x}_{r}dW_{r},\quad~t\leqslant s\leqslant T.

By taking
\beta=\frac{2C}{1-\alpha^{2}}
, the above equation becomes

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}=\bar{h}(X^{t,x}_{T})+\int_{s}^{T}\bar{f}(r,X^{t,x}_{r},\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{r},\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{r})dr+\alpha\int_{s}^{T}\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{r}d\overleftarrow{B}_{r}-\int_{s}^{T}\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{r}dW_{r},\quad~t\leqslant s\leqslant T,

(5.8)

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{r}=\beta\bar{Y}^{t,x}Z^{t,x}_{r},\quad~\bar{h}(X^{t,x}_{T})=\exp\{\beta h(X^{t,x}_{T})\},

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{f}(r,X^{t,x}_{r},\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{r},\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{r})=\beta\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{r}f(r,X^{t,x}_{r},Y^{t,x}_{r},Z^{t,x}_{r}).

(5.9)

Under
5
, Eq. (
5.8
) is a classical BDSDE with a globally Lipschitz generator. Then Bally–Matoussi
[
3
]
(see also Zhang–Zhao
[
38
]
and Wu–Zhang
[
31
]
) implies that the following SPDE

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{u}(s,x)=

\displaystyle\bar{h}(x)+\int_{s}^{T}\left\{\mathcal{L}\bar{u}(r,x)+\bar{f}\big(r,x,\bar{u}(r,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(r,x)\big)\right\}dr

(5.10)

\displaystyle+\alpha\int_{s}^{T}(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(r,x)d\overleftarrow{B}_{r},\quad~t\leqslant s\leqslant T,

admits a unique Sobolev solution
\bar{u}\in\mathscr{H}
, and for every
t\in[0,T]
,

\bar{u}(s,X_{s}^{t,x})=\bar{Y}_{s}^{t,x}\quad~\text{and}\quad~\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}(s,X_{s}^{t,x})=\bar{Z}_{s}^{t,x},\quad~a.s.,a.e.\ s\in[t,T],\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

On the other hand, we see that

\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}=\exp(\beta Y^{t,x}_{s})\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s}=\beta\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}Z^{t,x}_{s},\quad~t\leqslant s\leqslant T,

where the pair
(Y^{t,x},Z^{t,x})\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the solution of BDSDE (
5.6
).
So we would like to define

\displaystyle u(s,X^{t,x}_{s})\triangleq\frac{\ln\bar{u}(s,X^{t,x}_{s})}{\beta}=\frac{\ln\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}}{\beta}=Y^{t,x}_{s},\quad~t\leqslant s\leqslant T,

(5.11)

and then one can compute that

\displaystyle(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(s,X^{t,x}_{s})=\frac{(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,X^{t,x}_{s})}{\beta\bar{u}(s,X^{t,x}_{s})}=\frac{\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s}}{\beta\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}}=Z^{t,x}_{s},\quad~t\leqslant s\leqslant T.

(5.12)

Hence, applying Itô’s formula (see
2.2
) to
u=\ln\bar{u}/\beta
could transforms formally SPDE (
5.10
) into SPDE (
5.7
). In fact, by using Itô’s formula to
u=\ln\bar{u}/\beta
, one has that

\displaystyle du(s,x)=

\displaystyle\frac{-1}{\beta\bar{u}(s,x)}\left\{\mathcal{L}\bar{u}(s,x)+\bar{f}\big(s,x,\bar{u}(s,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,x)\big)\right\}ds

(5.13)

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,x)}{\beta\bar{u}(s,x)}d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\frac{\big((\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,x)\big)^{2}}{\beta\bar{u}(s,x)^{2}}ds.

From the definitions (
5.4
) and (
5.11
), it is easy to compute that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\bar{u}(s,x)=\beta\bar{u}(s,x)\Big[\mathcal{L}u(s,x)+\frac{\beta}{2}\big((\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(s,x)\big)^{2}\Big].

(5.14)

Then, combining (
5.9
), (
5.11
), (
5.12
), (
5.13
), (
5.14
), and note that
\beta=\frac{2C}{1-\alpha^{2}}
, we deduce that

\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle du(s,x)=&-\left\{\mathcal{L}u(s,x)+f\big(s,x,u(s,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(s,x)\big)+C\big((\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(s,x)\big)^{2}\right\}ds\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle&-\alpha(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(s,x)d\overleftarrow{B}_{s},\quad~t\leqslant s\leqslant T,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle u(T,x)=&h(x).\end{aligned}\right.

(5.15)

In other words,
u
is a Sobolev solution of SPDE (
5.7
), and for every
t\in[0,T]
,

u(s,X_{s}^{t,x})=Y^{t,x}_{s}\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(s,X_{s}^{t,x})=Z^{t,x}_{s},\quad~a.s.,a.e.\ s\in[t,T],\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
(Y^{t,x},Z^{t,x})
is the unique solution of BDSDE (
5.6
).

Finally, the uniqueness of solutions of SPDE (
5.7
) comes from the uniqueness of BDSDE (
5.6
). This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The idea of the above proof is firstly to transform a simple quadratic BDSDE into a classical BDSDE, and then by using the relationship between the classical BDSDEs and SPDEs, one can conversely obtain the relationship between the BDSDE with quadratic growth and its related SPDE.

5.2
General situation

In this subsection, we use BDSDE (
5.2
) to give a probabilistic representation for the solutions of SPDE (
5.3
) in Sobolev space, and use it to prove the existence and uniqueness of Sobolev solutions of SPDE (
5.3
), thus obtaining the nonlinear stochastic Feynman-Kac formula in this framework. The following lemma is an extension of equivalence of norm principle given in Bally–Matoussi
[
3
]
and plays an important role later.

Lemma 5.4
.

There exist two positive constants
k_{1}
and
K_{1}
which depend on
T,\rho,b
and
\sigma
, such that for any
t\leqslant s\leqslant T
and
\phi\in L^{1}\left(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathrm{~d}\mathbb{P}\otimes\rho^{-1}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)
which is independent of
\mathscr{F}_{t,s}^{W}
,

k_{1}\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\phi(x)|\rho^{-1}(x)\mathrm{d}x\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\phi\left(X_{s}^{t,x}\right)\right|\rho^{-1}(x)\mathrm{d}x\leqslant K_{1}\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\phi(x)|\rho^{-1}(x)\mathrm{d}x.

Moreover, for any

such that

is independent of

, one has

\displaystyle k_{1}\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{t}^{T}|\Phi(s,x)|\mathrm{d}s\rho^{-1}(x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{t}^{T}\left|\Phi\left(s,X_{s}^{t,x}\right)\right|\mathrm{d}s\rho^{-1}(x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leqslant K_{1}\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{t}^{T}|\Phi(s,x)|\mathrm{d}s\rho^{-1}(x)\mathrm{d}x.

Now we are in a position to present the main result of this section.

Theorem 5.6

(Feynman-Kac formula)
.

Under
6
, SPDE (
5.3
) admits a unique Sobolev solution
u\in\mathscr{H}
, and for every
t\in[0,T]
,

u(s,X_{s}^{t,x})=Y^{t,x}_{s}\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(s,X_{s}^{t,x})=Z^{t,x}_{s},\quad~a.s.,a.e.\ s\in[t,T],\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
\{(Y_{s}^{t,x},Z_{s}^{t,x});t\leqslant s\leqslant T\}
is the unique solution of BDSDE (
5.2
) with quadratic growth.

Proof.

Uniqueness. The uniqueness of SPDE (
5.3
) follows from the uniqueness of BDSDE
(
5.2
). In fact, if
u^{1}
and
u^{2}
are two Sobolev solutions of SPDE (
5.3
), then the following two pairs

\big(u^{1}(s,X_{s}^{t,x}),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u^{1})(s,X_{s}^{t,x})\big)\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~\big(u^{2}(s,X_{s}^{t,x}),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u^{2})(s,X_{s}^{t,x})\big)

solve BDSDE (
5.2
). So the uniqueness of BDSDE (
5.2
) gives us that

u^{1}(s,X_{s}^{t,x})=u^{2}(s,X_{s}^{t,x}),\quad a.s.,a.e.\ s\in[t,T],\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and in particular

u^{1}(t,x)=u^{2}(t,x),\quad a.s.,a.e.,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Next, we prove the existence. Compared with the proof of the simple situation (see
5.2
), the main idea here we used is similar, however, the techniques are more complex and include an approximation. So the proof will be divided into six steps with some techniques borrowed from the proof of
Theorem 3.10
.

\emph{Step1.Theexponentialtransformation.}

Let
\beta=\frac{2C}{1-\alpha}
be a given positive constant.
Applying the exponential change of variable (see
2.2
) to
\bar{Y}^{t,x}=\exp(\beta Y^{t,x})
could transforms formally BDSDE (
5.2
) with parameters
(f,g,h)
into the following BDSDE with parameters
(\bar{f},\bar{g},\bar{h})
,

\displaystyle\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{t}=\bar{h}(X^{t,x}_{T})+\int_{t}^{T}\bar{f}(s,X^{t,x}_{s},\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s},\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s})ds+\int_{t}^{T}\bar{g}(s,X^{t,x}_{s},\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s},\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s})d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s}dW_{s},

(5.16)

where
\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s}=\beta\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}Z^{t,x}_{s}
and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{f}(s,X^{t,x}_{s},\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s},\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s})=

\displaystyle\beta\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}f\Big(s,X^{t,x}_{s},\frac{\ln\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}}{\beta},\frac{\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s}}{\beta\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\beta^{2}\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}\Big[g\Big(s,X^{t,x}_{s},\frac{\ln\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}}{\beta},\frac{\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s}}{\beta\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}}\Big)^{2}-\Big(\frac{\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s}}{\beta\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}}\Big)^{2}\Big],

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{g}(s,X^{t,x}_{s},\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s},\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s})=

\displaystyle\beta\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}g\Big(s,X^{t,x}_{s},\frac{\ln\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}}{\beta},\frac{\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s}}{\beta\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}}\Big),

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{h}(X^{t,x}_{T})=

\displaystyle\exp(\beta h(X^{t,x}_{T})),\quad~t\leqslant s\leqslant T.

Then the pair of processes
(\bar{Y}^{t,x},\bar{Z}^{t,x})
is the solution of BDSDE (
5.16
) with parameters
(\bar{f},\bar{g},\bar{h})
, where
\bar{h}
is bounded and
\bar{g}
satisfies
3
.

\emph{Step2.ApproximationofSPDE.}

Let the sequence
\{\bar{f}^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be defined as in (
3.24
). Then, the same arguments we developed in the proof of
Theorem 3.10
deduce that
\bar{f}^{n}
is globally Lipschitz continuous with the constant
n
and decreasing converges pointwise to the function
\bar{f}
.
Now, for each
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the processes
\bar{u}^{n}
and
\bar{v}^{n}
by

\bar{u}^{n}(t,x)=\bar{Y}^{t,x,n}_{t}\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~\bar{v}^{n}(t,x)=\bar{Z}^{t,x,n}_{t},

(5.17)

where
(\bar{Y}^{t,x,n},\bar{Z}^{t,x,n})
is the unique solution of the following BDSDE with parameters
(\bar{f}^{n},\bar{g},\bar{h})
: for
t\in[0,T]
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{Y}^{t,x,n}_{t}=

\displaystyle\bar{h}(X^{t,x}_{T})+\int_{t}^{T}\bar{f}^{n}(s,X^{t,x}_{s},\bar{Y}^{t,x,n}_{s},\bar{Z}^{t,x,n}_{s})ds

(5.18)

\displaystyle+\int_{t}^{T}\bar{g}(s,X^{t,x}_{s},\bar{Y}^{t,x,n}_{s},\bar{Z}^{t,x,n}_{s})d\overleftarrow{B}_{s}-\int_{t}^{T}\bar{Z}^{t,x,n}_{s}dW_{s}.

Note that the parameters
(\bar{f}^{n},\bar{g},\bar{h})
of the above BDSDE (
5.18
) satisfy that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{0}^{T}\Big(|\bar{h}(x)|^{2}+|\bar{f}^{n}(t,x,\bar{y},0)|^{2}+|\bar{g}(t,x,\bar{y},0)|^{2}\Big)dt\rho^{-1}(x)dx<\infty,\quad\forall\bar{y}\in\big[\exp\{-\beta M\},\exp\{\beta M\}\big].

Then Bally–Matoussi
[
3
]
(see also Zhang–Zhao
[
38
]
and Wu–Zhang
[
31
]
) deduce that

\bar{v}^{n}(s,x)=(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}^{n})(s,x),

(5.19)

and
\bar{u}^{n}
is the unique Sobolev solution to the following SPDE:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{u}^{n}(s,x)=

\displaystyle\bar{h}(x)+\int_{s}^{T}\left\{\mathcal{L}\bar{u}^{n}(r,x)+\bar{f}^{n}\big(r,x,\bar{u}^{n}(r,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}^{n})(r,x)\big)\right\}dr

(5.20)

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{T}\bar{g}\big(r,x,\bar{u}^{n}(r,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}^{n})(r,x)\big)d\overleftarrow{B}_{r},\quad s\in[t,T],

with

\bar{u}^{n}\left(s,X_{s}^{t,x}\right)=\bar{Y}_{s}^{t,x,n}\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~\big(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}^{n}\big)\left(s,X_{s}^{t,x}\right)=\bar{Z}_{s}^{t,x,n},\quad~a.s.,a.e.\ s\in[t,T],\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(5.21)

where
(\bar{Y}^{t,x,n},\bar{Z}^{t,x,n})
is the unique solution of BDSDE (
5.18
) with parameters
(\bar{f}^{n},\bar{g},\bar{h})
.

On the other hand, similar to (
3.26
), the comparison theorem for Lipschitz continuous coefficients (see
2.4
) implies that the sequence
\{\bar{Y}^{t,x,n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is bounded and decreasing in

such that

\exp(-\beta\|h\|_{\infty})\leqslant\bar{Y}_{s}^{t,x,n+1}\leqslant\bar{Y}_{s}^{t,x,n}\leqslant\exp(\beta\|h\|_{\infty}),\quad~\hbox{a.s.}\ s\in[t,T].

(5.22)

In addition, by using the same arguments we developed in the proofs of
3.5
and
3.7
, there exists a positive constant

such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|\bar{Z}^{t,x,n}_{s}|^{2}ds\leqslant K,

(5.23)

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle&\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{Y}^{t,x,n}_{s}=\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s},\quad~\hbox{a.s.}\ s\in[t,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle&\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|\bar{Z}^{t,x,n}_{s}-\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s}|^{2}ds=0,\end{aligned}\right.

(5.24)

where
(\bar{Y}^{t,x},\bar{Z}^{t,x})\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the solution of BDSDE (
5.16
) with parameters
(\bar{f},\bar{g},\bar{h})
.

\emph{Step3.ConvergenceofSPDE\eqref{SPDE.2}}

We point out that the limits which we consider below hold along with a subsequence, however for simplicity, the subsequence will still be indexed by
n
. From
5.4
, note (
5.19
), (
5.21
), (
5.22
) and (
5.23
), we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}\left(\left|\bar{u}^{n}(s,x)\right|^{2}+\left|\bar{v}^{n}(s,x)\right|^{2}\right)ds\rho^{-1}(x)dx

(5.25)

\displaystyle\leqslant K_{1}\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(T\|\bar{Y}^{t,x,n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{\mathbb{F}}(t,T)}^{2}+\int_{t}^{T}|\bar{Z}^{t,x,n}_{s}|^{2}ds\Big)\rho^{-1}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leqslant K_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big[T\exp(2\beta\|h\|_{\infty})+K\big]\rho^{-1}(x)dx<\infty.

Moreover, similar to (
3.25
), we have

|\bar{g}\big(s,x,\bar{u}^{n}(s,x),\bar{v}^{n}(s,x)\big)|^{2}\leqslant\alpha|\bar{v}^{n}(s,x)|^{2},\quad~a.s.,a.e.\ s\in[t,T],\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Then, note that
\bar{f}^{n}
is globally Lipschitz continuous, we deduce from (
5.25

\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}\Big[|\bar{f}^{n}\big(s,x,\bar{u}^{n}(s,x),\bar{v}^{n}(s,x)\big)|^{2}+|\bar{g}\big(s,x,\bar{u}^{n}(s,x),\bar{v}^{n}(s,x)\big)|^{2}\Big]ds\rho^{-1}(x)dx\leqslant\widetilde{K},

where

is some constant which depends on
\|h\|_{\infty}
,
K
,
K_{1}
and
T
.
Now, from
5.4
, (
5.17
), (
5.24
) and Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem, we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{n,m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}|\bar{u}^{n}(s,x)-\bar{u}^{m}(s,x)|^{2}ds\rho^{-1}(x)dx=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{n,m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}|(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}^{n})(s,x)-(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}^{m})(s,x)|^{2}ds\rho^{-1}(x)dx=0.

Then, using
5.4
again, and note that the space
\mathscr{H}
is complete, it follows that there exists
\bar{u}\in\mathscr{H}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}|\bar{u}^{n}(s,x)-\bar{u}(s,x)|^{2}ds\rho^{-1}(x)dx=0,

(5.26)

\displaystyle\lim_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}|(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}^{n})(s,x)-(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,x)|^{2}ds\rho^{-1}(x)dx=0,

(5.27)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}\Big(|\bar{u}(s,x)|^{2}+(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,x)|^{2}\Big)ds\rho^{-1}(x)dx<\infty.

Step 4. We show that for any
t\in[0,T]
,

\bar{u}(s,X^{t,x}_{s})=\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}\quad\hbox{and}\quad~(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,X^{t,x}_{s})=\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s},\quad~a.s.,a.e.\ s\in[t,T],\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(5.28)

By triangular inequality, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}|\bar{u}(s,X^{t,x}_{s})-\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}|^{2}ds\rho^{-1}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}|\bar{u}(s,X^{t,x}_{s})-\bar{u}^{n}(s,X^{t,x}_{s})|^{2}ds\rho^{-1}(x)dx

(5.29)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}|\bar{u}^{n}(s,X^{t,x}_{s})-\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}|^{2}ds\rho^{-1}(x)dx.

(5.30)

From
5.4
and (
5.26
), one has that the term (
5.29
) tends to

as
n
tends to
\infty
. In addition,
5.4
, (
5.21
) and (
5.24
) deduce that the term (
5.30
) tends to

as
n
tends to
\infty

So the first result in (
5.28
) holds. We next prove the second result in (
5.28

(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,X^{t,x}_{s})=\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s},\quad~a.s.,a.e.\ s\in[t,T],\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(5.31)

It is sufficient to show that the following term

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}|(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,X^{t,x}_{s})-\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s}|^{2}ds\rho^{-1}(x)dx

(5.32)

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}|(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,X^{t,x}_{s})-(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}^{n})(s,X^{t,x}_{s})|^{2}ds\rho^{-1}(x)dx

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}|(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}^{n})(s,X^{t,x}_{s})-\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s}|^{2}ds\rho^{-1}(x)dx

\displaystyle\longrightarrow 0\quad\hbox{as}\quad n\rightarrow\infty.

In fact, similarly, combining
5.4
, (
5.21
), (
5.24
), and (
5.27
), it is easy to see that (
5.32

5.31

Step 5. We show that
\bar{u}
is a Sobolev solution to the following SPDE:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\bar{u}(s,x)=

\displaystyle\bar{h}(x)+\int_{s}^{T}\left\{\mathcal{L}\bar{u}(r,x)+\bar{f}\big(r,x,\bar{u}(r,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(r,x)\big)\right\}dr

(5.33)

\displaystyle+\int_{s}^{T}\bar{g}\big(r,x,\bar{u}(r,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(r,x)\big)d\overleftarrow{B}_{r},\quad s\in[t,T].

First, it was shown in
Step 3
that
\bar{u}
belongs to the space
\mathscr{H}
, so by
5.1
, we only need to verify that
\bar{u}
satisfies Eq. (
5.5
) with parameters
(\bar{f},\bar{g},\bar{h})
.
Note that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, since
\bar{u}^{n}
is the Sobolev solution of SPDE (
5.20

\varphi\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}\bar{u}^{n}(s,x)\partial_{s}\varphi(s,x)dsdx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bar{u}^{n}(t,x)\varphi(t,x)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bar{h}(x)\varphi(T,x)dx

(5.34)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}^{n})(s,x)\cdot(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\varphi)(s,x)dsdx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}\bar{u}^{n}\mathrm{div}[(b-\widetilde{A})\varphi](s,x)dsdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}\bar{f}^{n}\big(s,x,\bar{u}^{n}(s,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}^{n})(s,x)\big)\varphi(s,x)dsdx

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}\bar{g}\big(s,x,\bar{u}^{n}(s,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}^{n})(s,x)\big)\varphi(s,x)dB_{s}dx.

Hence, if we can prove that along a subsequence (

\bar{u}
satisfies (
5.5
) with parameters
(\bar{f},\bar{g},\bar{h})

Due to that

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}\bar{u}(s,x)\partial_{s}\varphi(s,x)dsdx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bar{u}(t,x)\varphi(t,x)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\bar{h}(x)\varphi(T,x)dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,x)\cdot(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\varphi)(s,x)dsdx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}\bar{u}\mathrm{div}[(b-\widetilde{A})\varphi](s,x)dsdx.

Next, we compute the limit on the right hand side of (
5.34
).
We show that along a sequence

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}\bar{f}^{n}\big(s,x,\bar{u}^{n}(s,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}^{n})(s,x)\big)\varphi(s,x)dsdx

(5.35)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}\bar{f}\big(s,x,\bar{u}(s,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,x)\big)\varphi(s,x)dsdx.

In fact, note that the sequence
\{\bar{f}^{n};n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is globally Lipschitz continuous and decreasing converges pointwise to the function
\bar{f}
, and for every

,

\displaystyle\big(\bar{u}^{n}(s,X_{s}^{t,x}),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}^{n})(s,X_{s}^{t,x})\big)=\big(\bar{Y}_{s}^{t,x,n},\bar{Z}_{s}^{t,x,n}\big),\quad~a.s.,a.e.\ s\in[t,T],\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

\displaystyle\big(\bar{u}(s,X_{s}^{t,x}),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,X_{s}^{t,x})\big)=\big(\bar{Y}_{s}^{t,x},\bar{Z}_{s}^{t,x}\big),\quad~a.s.,a.e.\ s\in[t,T],\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

\left\{\begin{aligned} \displaystyle&\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bar{Y}^{t,x,n}_{s}=\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s},\quad~\hbox{a.s.}\ s\in[t,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr\displaystyle&\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|\bar{Z}^{t,x,n}_{s}-\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s}|^{2}ds=0.\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\Big|

\displaystyle\bar{f}^{n}\big(s,X_{s}^{t,x},\bar{u}^{n}(s,X_{s}^{t,x}),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}^{n})(s,X_{s}^{t,x})\big)

\displaystyle-\bar{f}\big(s,X_{s}^{t,x},\bar{u}(s,X_{s}^{t,x}),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,X_{s}^{t,x})\big)\Big|ds=0.

So combining
5.4
and the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem we have that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}\Big|

\displaystyle\bar{f}^{n}\big(s,x,\bar{u}^{n}(s,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}^{n})(s,x)\big)

\displaystyle-\bar{f}\big(s,x,\bar{u}(s,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,x)\big)\Big|ds\rho^{-1}(x)dx=0,

which implies that (
5.35
) holds. Finally, for the second term on the right hand side of (

) and the previous discussion, one has that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\bigg|

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}\bar{g}\big(s,x,\bar{u}^{n}(s,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u}^{n})(s,x)\big)\varphi(s,x)dB_{s}dx

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{t}^{T}\bar{g}\big(s,x,\bar{u}(s,x),(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,x)\big)\varphi(s,x)dB_{s}dx\bigg|=0\quad\hbox{in probability.}

Therefore
\bar{u}
satisfies Eq. (
5.5
) with parameters
(\bar{f},\bar{g},\bar{h})
, and thus

is a Sobolev solution to SPDE (

Step 6. The logarithmic transformation.

On the one hand, we see that SPDE (
5.33
) with parameters
(\bar{f},\bar{g},\bar{h})
has a Sobolev solution
\bar{u}
, and for every
t\in[0,T]
,

\bar{u}(s,X^{t,x}_{s})=\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}\quad\hbox{and}\quad(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,X^{t,x}_{s})=\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s},\quad~a.s.,a.e.\ s\in[t,T],\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where the pair
(\bar{Y}^{t,x},\bar{Z}^{t,x})
is the solution of BDSDE (
5.16
) with parameters
(\bar{f},\bar{g},\bar{h})
. On the other hand,

\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}=\exp(\beta Y^{t,x}_{s})\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s}=\beta\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}Z^{t,x}_{s},\quad~t\leqslant s\leqslant T,

where the pair
(Y^{t,x},Z^{t,x})\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{R})\times L_{\mathbb{F}}^{2}(0,T;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the solution of BDSDE (
5.2
) with parameters
(f,g,h)
.
Hence, similar to the computation from (
5.11
) to (
5.15
), if we define

\displaystyle u(s,X^{t,x}_{s})\triangleq\frac{\ln\bar{u}(s,X^{t,x}_{s})}{\beta}=\frac{\ln\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}}{\beta}=Y^{t,x}_{s},\quad~t\leqslant s\leqslant T,

with

\displaystyle(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(s,X^{t,x}_{s})=\frac{(\sigma^{\top}\nabla\bar{u})(s,X^{t,x}_{s})}{\beta\bar{u}(s,X^{t,x}_{s})}=\frac{\bar{Z}^{t,x}_{s}}{\beta\bar{Y}^{t,x}_{s}}=Z^{t,x}_{s},\quad~t\leqslant s\leqslant T,

then
u
is a Sobolev solution of SPDE (
5.3
) with parameters
(f,g,h)
, and for every
t\in[0,T]
,

u(s,X_{s}^{t,x})=Y^{t,x}_{s}\quad~\hbox{and}\quad~(\sigma^{\top}\nabla u)(s,X_{s}^{t,x})=Z^{t,x}_{s},\quad~a.s.,a.e.\ s\in[t,T],\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
(Y^{t,x},Z^{t,x})
is the unique solution of BDSDE (
5.2
) with parameters
(f,g,h)
. This completes the proof.
∎

6
Conclusion Remarks

We initiate the study of quadratic BDSDEs, where the existence, comparison, and stability results for one-dimensional BDSDEs with quadratic growth and bounded terminal value are obtained.
We remark that when proving a priori estimate, in order to ensure
Y
is bounded, we introduce the condition (
3.5
) concerning the coefficient
g
, which we think is necessary for the case of bounded terminal value. However, if someone discusses the solution of quadratic BDSDEs with unbounded terminal value, the condition (
3.5
) could be relaxed because the boundedness of
Y
is needn’t at that time. We hope to publish some follow-up works when the terminal value
\xi
is unbounded or/and
Y
is multi-dimensional.
Finally, in this framework, we used BDSDEs to prove the existence and uniqueness of the Sobolev solutions of semilinear SPDEs, which extend the nonlinear Feynman-Kac formula of Pardoux–Peng
[
26
]
. On the other hand, the existence and uniqueness of viscosity solutions of related SPDEs are interesting and merit further studies in the near future.

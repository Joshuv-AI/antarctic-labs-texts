Smith–Treumann theory and the linkage principle
Simon Riche, Geordie Williamson

Abstract
. We apply Treumann’s “Smith theory for sheaves” in the context of the Iwahori–Whittaker model of the Satake category. We deduce two results in the representation theory of reductive algebraic groups over fields of positive characteristic: (a) a geometric proof of the linkage principle; (b) a character formula for tilting modules in terms of the ℓ \ell -canonical basis, valid in all blocks and in all characteristics.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Geometric representation theory of reductive algebraic groups

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive algebraic group over an algebraically
closed field
\Bbbk
of characteristic
\ell>0
, and consider
the category
\Rep(\mathbf{G})
of finite-dimensional algebraic representations of
\mathbf{G}
. The
study of this category has
seen significant progress
over the last fifty years; however several
fundamental questions (e.g. dimensions and characters of simple and indecomposable
tilting modules) remain only partially understood.

One tempting avenue of pursuit is to find relationships to
\mathscr{D}
-modules
or constructible sheaves, and hence bring sheaf theory into play. The
archetypal example of the success of such an approach is the
Beĭlinson–Bernstein localization theorem, which establishes such an
link for modules over complex semi-simple Lie algebras.
Beĭlinson–Bernstein localization is an indispensable tool in modern
Geometric Representation Theory, leading to proofs of the
Kazhdan–Lusztig conjectures, character formulas for real reductive
groups, etc.

Back in the setting of
\Rep(\mathbf{G})
, the
geometric Satake
equivalence
provides such a connection to constructible sheaves. To

\mathbf{G}
we can associate the affine Grassmannian
{\mathcal{G}r}_{H}
of its complex Langlands dual
group
H
(an “infinite-dimensional algebraic variety”), and one has an equivalence
of tensor categories between
\Rep(\mathbf{G})
and a certain category of perverse sheaves with

\Bbbk
-coefficients on
{\mathcal{G}r}_{H}
.
The geometric Satake equivalence is central
to modern approaches to the Langlands program, and has become a
cornerstone of Geometric Representation Theory.

However, in contrast to Beĭlinson–Bernstein localization, the geometric
Satake equivalence has been surprisingly ineffective at answering questions about
\Rep(\mathbf{G})
.
For example, several basic statements and constructions involving
\Rep(\mathbf{G})
(e.g. the
linkage principle, or Frobenius twist) have no geometric
explanation. This is the more surprising, as several known or
conjectured formulas (e.g. Lusztig’s character formula) involve
Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials or their
\ell
-counterparts, which
encode dimensions of stalks of complexes on the affine Grassmannian and flag
variety of
H
. Nowadays we have several proofs of Lusztig’s character
formula for large
\ell
, however none of them pass through the
geometric Satake equivalence!

1.2.
Overview

The main result of the present paper is a proof of the linkage principle via the geometric
Satake equivalence. Our proof also explains that each “block” in the linkage
principle is controlled by a partial affine flag variety for the Langlands dual
group, which gives us new proofs of Lusztig’s conjecture on simple
characters (for large
\ell
) and of a conjecture of the authors on
tilting characters (for all
\ell
).

Our argument is a simple application of two new tools in Geometric
Representation Theory. The first one is Smith–Treumann theory, which is a variant of equivariant
localization for tori. In this theory the circle action is
replaced by the action of a cyclic group of order
\ell
, and the
coefficients are of characteristic
\ell
. We apply this
theory to the loop rotation action on the affine Grassmannian. Whilst
the fixed points under the full loop rotation action (infinitely many partial flag varieties for
H
) are rather boring, the fixed points under the subgroup
of
\ell
-th roots of unity (
affine
flag varieties for
H
,
which are finite in number if
H
is semi-simple) are rich.

The second ingredient is the Iwahori–Whittaker realisation of the
Satake category. This replaces the category of perverse
sheaves in the Satake equivalence with an equivalent category of perverse sheaves satisfying a certain
equivariance condition with respect to the pro-unipotent radical of
the Iwahori subgroup. (This condition, introduced—to our knowledge—by Bezrukavnikov, is inspired by the “Whittaker
conditions” in the representation theory of
p
-adic groups, hence the name.) It
turns out that in the Iwahori–Whittaker realisation, the components of
the fixed points discussed above match precisely the decomposition
of
\Rep(\mathbf{G})
given by the linkage principle. Our main theorem asserts
that the Smith restriction functor gives an equivalence between
tilting sheaves in the Iwahori–Whittaker realisation and a certain
category of parity complexes on the fixed points. It is then
straightforward to deduce the linkage principle. The character
formulas for simple and tilting modules alluded to above are also an
immediate consequence.

In the rest of the introduction, we give a more detailed overview of
the techniques and results of this paper.

1.5.
Smith–Treumann theory

A fundamental role in our proof is played by Treumann’s “Smith theory
for sheaves”
[
Tr
]
. The basic idea is that, when
dealing with coefficients of characteristic
\ell
, one should be able
to localize to fixed points of actions of cyclic groups of order

\ell
. (This theory should be compared with Borel’s “localization
theorem” for manifolds equipped with an action of the circle group;
in this analogy, finite cyclic groups become “discrete circles”;
see
[
W2
]
for more comments on this analogy.)

More precisely, let
X
be a variety endowed with an action of the group
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}
of

\ell
-th roots of unity, and denote by
X^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}}
the subvariety of
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}
-fixed points. One has two (Verdier dual)
restriction functors

D^{\mathrm{b}}_{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}}(X,\Bbbk)
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}}(X^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}},\Bbbk)
i^{!}
i^{*}

between the
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}
-equivariant derived categories of constructible

\Bbbk
-sheaves on
X
and on
X^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}}
.

A fundamental observation of Treumann is that the compositions of these
functors with the quotient functor to the
Smith category

\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathrm{Treu}}(X^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}},\Bbbk):=D^{\mathrm{b}}_{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}}(X^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}},\Bbbk)/\left\langle\text{${\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}$-perfect complexes}\right\rangle

become canonically isomorphic. Here an object in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}}(X^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}},\Bbbk)
is called
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}
-perfect
if
its stalks (naturally complexes of
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}
-modules) may be
represented by a bounded complex of free
\Bbbk[{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}]
-modules.
The resulting
Smith
restriction
functor

D^{\mathrm{b}}_{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}}(X,\Bbbk)
\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathrm{Treu}}(X^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}},\Bbbk)
i^{!*}

has remarkable properties. For example, it commutes with essentially
all sheaf theoretic functors
[
Tr
]
. It can be thought
of as an analogue of hyperbolic localization
[
Br
]
for
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}
-actions.

For technical reasons (most notably, to ensure that the Smith category of a point satisfies appropriate parity vanishing properties), we use a variant of Treumann’s construction,
proposed by the second author in
[
W2
]
. Namely, we assume that the
action of
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}
can be extended to an action of the
multiplicative group
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
on
X
and consider the
equivariant
Smith category

\mathsf{Sm}(X^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}},\Bbbk):=D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}},\Bbbk)/\left\langle\begin{array}[]{c}\text{complexes whose restriction}\\
\text{to
${\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}\subset\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}$ are ${\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}$-perfect}\end{array}\right\rangle.

With this definition,
the theory of parity complexes from
[
JMW
]
applies in the Smith quotient, which will be crucial for our arguments.

1.6.
Fixed points

To apply this idea in our setting, note that
{\mathcal{G}r}_{H}
has a
natural action of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
via “loop rotation,” induced by the action
of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
on
\mathbb{C}(\hskip-1.0pt(z)\hskip-1.0pt)
which “rescales”
z
. A beautiful
fact (that we first learned from R. Bezrukavnikov) is that, if

{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}\subset\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
denotes (as above) the subgroup of
\ell
-th roots of unity, we have a decomposition

(1.3)

({\mathcal{G}r}_{H})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}}=\bigsqcup_{\gamma\in\mathbf{X}/(W_{\mathrm{aff}},\square_{\ell})}{\mathcal{G}r}_{H,\gamma},

where the action
\square_{\ell}
of
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
on
\mathbf{X}
is defined by
(w\mathsf{t}_{\mu})\square_{\ell}\lambda=w(\lambda+\ell\mu)
for
w\in W_{\mathrm{f}}
,
\mu\in\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{R}
and
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}
. Moreover,
each component on the right-hand side of (
1.3
) is a partial affine flag
variety for the loop group

L_{\ell}H
representing
R\mapsto H(R(\hskip-1.0pt(z^{\ell})\hskip-1.0pt))
, whose “partiality” is governed by the stabilizer of an element in
\gamma
. For example, for
\gamma=W_{\mathrm{aff}}\square_{\ell}0
we obtain the “thin affine
Grassmannian” (defined as above for
{\mathcal{G}r}_{H}
, but now with
z

z^{\ell}
); and if
\gamma
has trivial stabiliser under
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
then
{\mathcal{G}r}_{H,\gamma}
is the full
affine flag variety for

.

The similarity between (
1.3
) and (
1.1
)
is rather striking; for example there are as many
components in the right-hand side of (
1.3
) as summands in the decomposition (
1.1
). However there is a fundamental difference: (
1.1
) involves the dot action (with
W_{\mathrm{f}}
fixing
-\rho
), whereas (
1.3
) involves the
unshifted action (with
W_{\mathrm{f}}
fixing

do not expect the Smith restriction functor to realise the linkage
principle in this setting.

1.7.
The Iwahori-Whittaker model

To get around this issue, we
replace the “traditional” Satake category
\mathsf{Perv}_{L^{+}H}({\mathcal{G}r}_{H},\Bbbk)

with the “Iwahori–Whittaker model” considered
in
[
BGMRR
]
. There it is proved that (under a mild assumption, satisfied e.g. if
H
is semisimple of adjoint type, which we assume from now on for simplicity) one has an equivalence of
abelian categories

(1.4)

\mathsf{Perv}_{L^{+}H}({\mathcal{G}r}_{H},\Bbbk)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{H},\Bbbk)

where the right-hand side denotes a category of perverse sheaves of
{\mathcal{G}r}_{H}
which satisfy
a certain equivariance condition with respect to the pro-unipotent
radical
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u}}^{+}
of an Iwahori subgroup; such
perverse sheaves are called
‘‘Iwahori--Whittaker.’’
5
5

5

One can make sense of this condition in
various ways. In this work (following Bezrukavnikov) we use étale
sheaves and the Artin–Schreier covering, which necessitates passing
to
{\mathcal{G}r}_{H}
defined over a field of characteristic
p>0
(with
p\neq\ell
). The
geometric Satake equivalence is also available in this setting.

A crucial point for us is that on simple objects the equivalence
(
1.4
) sends the intersection cohomology complex associated with the
L^{+}H
-orbit parametrized by
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{+}
(which corresponds to
\mathsf{L}(\lambda)
under (
1.2
)) to the Iwahori–Whittaker intersection cohomology complex associated with the
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u}}^{+}
-orbit parametrized by
\lambda+\rho
.
Thus, after passage to the Iwahori–Whittaker model, our issue with the
two distinct actions of
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
goes away, and the linkage principle is reflected
perfectly in the geometry of the
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}
-fixed points. In particular, two simple Iwahori–Whittaker perverse sheaves, parametrized by some weights
\lambda
and
\mu
,
lie in
the same summand in the linkage principle if and only if the
corresponding points
L_{\lambda}
and
L_{\mu}
lie in the same
component of the fixed points!

Another favorable property of the
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u}}^{+}
-action on
{\mathcal{G}r}_{H}
is that
each orbit is isomorphic to an affine space. This setting is known to
imply nice properties for categories of perverse sheaves (see
e.g.
[
BGS
]
), and in particular that this category admits a
transparent structure of a highest weight category. The situation is even more favorable here in that the “relevant” orbits (i.e. those which support a nonzero Iwahori–Whittaker local system) have dimensions of constant parity in each connected component of
{\mathcal{G}r}_{H}
. This implies that the tilting objects in
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{H},\Bbbk)
are parity in the sense of
[
JMW
]
; in particular the indecomposable tilting perverse sheaves coincide with the self-dual indecomposable parity objects.

1.8.
Main theorems

Recall that
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
acts on
{\mathcal{G}r}_{H}
via loop rotation. The Iwahori–Whittaker
condition and the loop rotation equivariance are compatible; we thus
obtain a Smith restriction functor

i^{!*}:D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{H},\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{H})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}},\Bbbk).

We will write
\mathsf{Parity}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{H},\Bbbk)
, resp.
\mathsf{SmParity}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{H})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}},\Bbbk)
, for the
additive category of parity sheaves in the source, resp. target, of this
functor, and
\mathsf{PerPar}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{H},\Bbbk)
for the full subcategory of
\mathsf{Parity}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{H},\Bbbk)
whose objects are the
perverse
parity complexes. Our first main result (stated more precisely in Theorem
7.4
) is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Smith restriction yields a fully-faithful functor

i^{!*}:\mathsf{PerPar}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{H},\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{SmParity}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{H})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}},\Bbbk).

Remarkably, the proof of this theorem is a few lines once one has the
appropriate technology in place. It is an easy consequence of
Beĭlinson’s lemma, once one knows that
i^{!*}
preserves standard and
costandard objects; this in turn follows because
i^{!*}
commutes
with
*

Recall from §

{\mathcal{G}r}_{H}
coincide with the indecomposable tilting perverse sheaves in
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{H},\Bbbk)
(which in turn
correspond to tilting

-modules under the geometric Satake
equivalence). Given
\lambda\in\rho+\mathbf{X}^{+}

by

By Theorem

, the image of any

under

supported on a single component. This has the following consequence,
from which one easily obtains the promised proof of the linkage principle (see §
8.2

Corollary 1.3
.

If
\Hom(\mathscr{E}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda},\mathscr{E}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\mu})\neq 0

W_{\mathrm{aff}}\square_{\ell}\lambda=W_{\mathrm{aff}}\square_{\ell}\mu

Theorem

This is the subject of our second main result on the geometric side.

\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{H})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}},\Bbbk)

Theorem 1.4
.

The functors

The first step
is the observation that

Remark 1.5
.

The isomorphism (
1.5
) shows that “
\mathsf{Q}\circ(\mathsf{For}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}})^{-1}
”
behaves like a degrading functor. Degrading functors are ubiquitous
in modern Geometric Representation Theory. In algebra, they are
often realised by forgetting the grading on a graded module; in geometry
they are often associated with forgetting a mixed structure. The
above shows that Smith–Treumann theory provides another possible
geometric realisation of degrading functors.

1.9.
Tilting characters

A fundamental question in the
representation theory of
\mathbf{G}
is to determine the characters of the
indecomposable tilting modules. In
[
RW1
]
we started advocating the
idea that character formulas for
\mathbf{G}
-modules should be expressed in
terms of the
\ell
-canonical basis of
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
, and illustrated this idea by a conjectural formula for
characters of indecomposable tilting modules in the principal block,
under the assumption that
\ell
is bigger than the Coxeter number
h
of
\mathbf{G}
. This formula was proved in case
\mathbf{G}=\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\Bbbk)
in
[
RW1
]
, and then for a general reductive
group in a joint work with P. Achar and S. Makisumi, see
[
AMRW
]
.
A simple consequence of the results of §
1.8
is a new and much simpler proof of this character
formula, along with an extension to a formula valid in all blocks of

\Rep(\mathbf{G})
, without any restriction of
\ell
.

To state this formula, recall that the summands on the right-hand side
of (
1.1
) can be parametrized by the weights in
the intersection
\mathcal{A}
of the weight lattice with the closure
of the fundamental alcove for the dot action of
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
. For
\lambda\in\mathcal{A}
we denote by
W_{\lambda}\subset W_{\mathrm{aff}}
the stabilizer of
\lambda
for

\bullet_{\ell}
(a standard parabolic subgroup), and by
W_{\mathrm{aff}}^{(\lambda)}
the subset of
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
consisting
of elements
w
which are both maximal in
wW_{\lambda}
and minimal in

W_{\mathrm{f}}w
. Then the
indecomposable tilting
\mathbf{G}
-modules in the block of
W_{\mathrm{aff}}\bullet_{\ell}\lambda
are
in a natural bijection with
W_{\mathrm{aff}}^{(\lambda)}
, and we denote by

\mathsf{T}(w\bullet_{\ell}\lambda)
the module of highest weight
w\bullet_{\ell}\lambda
.

The tilting character formula alluded to above can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.6
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathcal{A}
and
w\in W_{\mathrm{aff}}^{(\lambda)}

we have

\mathsf{ch}(\mathsf{T}(w\bullet_{\ell}\lambda))=\sum_{y\in W_{\mathrm{aff}}^{(\lambda)}}{}^{\ell}\hskip-1.0ptn_{y,w}(1)\cdot\chi_{y\bullet_{\ell}\lambda},

where
\chi_{\mu}
is the Weyl character formula attached to the
dominant weight
\mu
, and
{}^{\ell}\hskip-1.0ptn_{y,w}
is the antispherical

\ell
-Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial attached to
(y,w)
.

See
[
RW1
, §1.4]
for a comparison of this formula with an earlier formula conjectured by Andersen
[
A2
]
, which was one of our sources of inspiration. The proof of Theorem
1.6
proceeds as follows. By standard considerations, to compute the characters of indecomposable tilting modules it suffices to compute the dimensions of morphism spaces between such objects. In the present setting, thanks to Theorem
1.2
this boils down to computing the dimensions of morphism spaces between indecomposable parity objects in
\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{H})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}},\Bbbk)
. Formula (
1.5
) allows to translate this question into that of computing dimensions of morphism spaces between indecomposable parity objects in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{H})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}},\Bbbk)
, which can be done using (
1.3
) and the fact that the dimensions of stalks of indecomposable parity complexes on (partial) affine flag varieties are computed by
\ell
-Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials, as follows from the results of
[
RW1
, Part III]
.

1.10.
Simple characters

Using ideas of
Andersen
[
A2
]
recently refined by
Sobaje
[
Sob
]
, from the formula in Theorem
1.6
one can in theory deduce a character formula for simple
\mathbf{G}
-modules, in all blocks and all characteristics. This can be done in at least two ways. The first possibility is to use a “reciprocity formula” due to Andersen
[
A3
]
(based on earlier work of Jantzen) which expresses multiplicities of simple modules in Weyl modules in terms of multiplicities of induced modules in indecomposable tilting modules. This method has the advantage of allowing to deduce Lusztig’s conjectural formula
[
L1
]
in case the relevant
\ell
-Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials coincide with the corresponding standard Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials, but it requires the assumption that
\ell\geq 2h-2
, and does not produce a very natural formula in general, since it involves a certain “twist” (denoted
y\mapsto\hat{y}
below) on indices.

To explain this in more detail, let us assume that
\ell\geq 2h-2
and that
\mathbf{G}
is quasi-simple, and denote by
\alpha_{0}^{\vee}
the highest coroot. We then set

Y:=\{w\in W_{\mathrm{aff}}\mid\text{$w$ is minimal in $W_{\mathrm{f}}w$ and $\langle w\square_{\ell}\rho,\alpha_{0}^{\vee}\rangle<\ell(h-1)$}\}.

This subset does not depend on
\ell
, and is an ideal in the Bruhat order on the set of elements
w
minimal in
W_{\mathrm{f}}w
; in fact, in terms of the notation of
[
RW2
]
, it consists of the elements
w
sending the fundamental alcove
A_{\mathrm{fund}}
inside the portion of the dominant region delimited by the hyperplane orthogonal to
\alpha_{0}^{\vee}
and passing through
\rho
. Consider also the operation
y\mapsto\hat{y}
on
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
corresponding to the operation
A\mapsto\hat{A}
on alcoves considered in
[
Soe
]
or
[
RW2
]
(through the canonical bijection between
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
and the set of alcoves). Then, in view of
[
RW1
, Proposition 1.8.1]
, from Theorem
1.6
we obtain that for any
w\in Y
we have

[\nabla(w\bullet_{\ell}0)]=\sum_{y\in Y}{}^{\ell}\hskip-1.0ptn_{w,\hat{y}}(1)\cdot[\mathsf{L}(y\bullet_{\ell}0)]

in the Grothendieck group of
\Rep(\mathbf{G})
. In order to compare this formula with that in Lusztig’s conjecture, one needs to invert these equations. In general we do not know how to do that explicitly. However, if we assume that each polynomial
{}^{\ell}\hskip-1.0ptn_{w,\hat{y}}
in these formulas coincides with the corresponding “standard” Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial
n_{w,\hat{y}}
(as considered e.g. in
[
Soe
]
), then the inverse matrix is computed in
[
Soe
, Theorem 5.1]
; from this result we obtain that

[\mathsf{L}(w\bullet_{\ell}0)]=\sum_{y\in Y}(-1)^{\ell(w)+\ell(y)}h_{y,w}(1)\cdot[\nabla(y\bullet_{\ell}0)]

for any
w\in Y
, as predicted by Lusztig in
[
L1
]
. This property is well known to hold in large characteristic (without any known explicit bound), which explains why Theorem
1.6
provides a new proof of Lusztig’s conjecture in large characteristics.

Remark 1.7
.

The condition on
w
considered above is not the same as in Lusztig’s formulation of his conjecture. However, the two versions are known to be equivalent under the present assumptions, due to results of Kato; see
[
W1
, §§1.12–1.13]
for more details and references.

The other method to obtain a character formula for simple
\mathbf{G}
-modules out of a character formula for indecomposable tilting
\mathbf{G}
-modules, which works for all values of
\ell
thanks to the results of
[
Sob
]
, is to express multiplicities of the simple
\mathbf{G}
-modules whose highest weight is restricted in the baby Verma
\mathbf{G}_{1}\mathbf{T}
-modules. In this way one obtains a formula that may be compared with the “periodic” formulation of Lusztig’s conjecture, see
[
L2
]
. This formula was made explicit in
[
RW2
]
, under the assumption that
\ell\geq 2h-1
. The extension of the tilting character formula in Theorem
1.6
makes it desirable to extend the validity of these results to smaller values of
\ell
, and we plan to come back to this question in a future publication.

2.
Preliminaries on equivariant sheaves

2.1.
Equivariant sheaves

We start by recalling some generalities on étale sheaves on schemes endowed with an action of a finite group. We fix a (commutative) coefficient ring
\Bbbk
.

Let
X
be a scheme, and let
A
be a finite group acting on
X
(by scheme automorphisms). For any
g\in A
, we denote by
\alpha_{g}:X\xrightarrow{\sim}X
the action on
X
. Recall that an
A
-equivariant (étale) sheaf of
\Bbbk
-modules is the datum of an étale sheaf
\mathscr{F}
of
\Bbbk
-modules on
X
together with a collection
(\varphi_{g})_{g\in A}
where, for any
g\in A
,

\varphi_{g}:\alpha_{g}^{*}\mathscr{F}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathscr{F}

is an isomorphism of sheaves of
\Bbbk
-modules, this collection satisfying the condition that for
g,h\in A
we have

(2.1)

\varphi_{h}\circ\alpha_{h}^{*}(\varphi_{g})=\varphi_{gh}

as morphisms from
\alpha_{gh}^{*}\mathscr{F}
to
\mathscr{F}
. (We will often abuse notation, and omit the isomorphisms
(\varphi_{g})_{g\in A}
from the notation.) Morphisms of
A
-equivariant sheaves are defined as morphisms of sheaves compatible (in the natural way) with the isomorphisms
\varphi_{g}
. The (abelian) category of
A
-equivariant sheaves of
\Bbbk
-modules will be denoted
\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)
. We have a natural “forgetful” exact functor

(2.2)

\mathsf{For}_{A}:\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Sh}(X,\Bbbk)

(which simply forgets the collection of isomorphisms
(\varphi_{g})_{g\in A}
), where
\mathsf{Sh}(X,\Bbbk)
denotes the category of sheaves of
\Bbbk
-modules on
X
. If
A
acts trivially on
X
, we have a canonical identification

(2.3)

\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)=\mathsf{Sh}(X,\Bbbk[A]).

If
X,Y
are two schemes with actions of
A
(with actions denoted
\alpha^{X}_{-}
and
\alpha^{Y}_{-}
respectively), and
f:X\to Y
is an
A
-equivariant morphism, then for any
g\in A
we have a canonical isomorphism

(\alpha^{X}_{g})^{*}\circ f^{*}\cong f^{*}\circ(\alpha^{Y}_{g})^{*}.

As a consequence, the functor
f^{*}
induces an exact functor

\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(Y,\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk),

which will also be denoted
f^{*}
. Similarly, we have a canonical isomorphism

(\alpha^{Y}_{g})^{*}\circ f_{*}\cong f_{*}\circ(\alpha^{X}_{g})^{*}.

(Here we use the fact that
(\alpha^{Z}_{g})^{*}\cong(\alpha^{Z}_{g^{-1}})_{*}
for
Z=X
or
Y
.) As a consequence,
f_{*}
induces a functor

f_{*}:\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(Y,\Bbbk).

2.2.
Equivariant sheaves and injective resolutions

We consider again a scheme
X
endowed with an action of the finite group
A
.
For any
\mathscr{F}
in
\mathsf{Sh}(X,\Bbbk)
we set

\mathsf{Av}_{A}(\mathscr{F}):=\bigoplus_{g\in A}\alpha_{g}^{*}\mathscr{F}.

We endow
\mathsf{Av}_{A}(\mathscr{F})
with the structure of an
A
-equivariant sheaf by defining, for any
g\in A
, the isomorphism

\varphi_{g}:\alpha_{g}^{*}\mathsf{Av}_{A}(\mathscr{F})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathsf{Av}_{A}(\mathscr{F})

as the canonical identification

\alpha_{g}^{*}\left(\bigoplus_{h\in A}\alpha_{h}^{*}\mathscr{F}\right)=\bigoplus_{h\in A}\alpha_{hg}^{*}\mathscr{F}=\bigoplus_{a\in A}\alpha_{a}^{*}\mathscr{F}.

This construction extends in a natural way to an exact functor

\mathsf{Av}_{A}:\mathsf{Sh}(X,\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk).

Lemma 2.1
.

The functor
\mathsf{Av}_{A}
is left and right adjoint to the forgetful functor (
2.2
).

Proof.

To prove the lemma we have to define morphisms of functors

\mathsf{For}_{A}\circ\mathsf{Av}_{A}\to\mathrm{id},\quad\mathrm{id}\to\mathsf{For}_{A}\circ\mathsf{Av}_{A},\quad\mathsf{Av}_{A}\circ\mathsf{For}_{A}\to\mathrm{id},\quad\mathrm{id}\to\mathsf{Av}_{A}\circ\mathsf{For}_{A},

and check the appropriate zigzag relations. Here we have

\mathsf{For}_{A}\circ\mathsf{Av}_{A}=\bigoplus_{g\in A}\alpha_{g}^{*},

and the first two morphisms are defined as the projection to and embedding of the factor
\alpha_{e}^{*}=\mathrm{id}
. On the other hand, for
(\mathscr{F},(\varphi_{g})_{g\in A})
in
\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X)
, the morphisms

\mathscr{F}\to\mathsf{Av}_{A}\circ\mathsf{For}_{A}(\mathscr{F})\to\mathscr{F}

are defined as
\bigoplus_{g\in A}(\varphi_{g})^{-1}
and
\bigoplus_{g\in A}\varphi_{g}
respectively. (These morphisms of sheaves are morphisms of
A
-equivariant sheaves thanks to the cocyle condition (
2.1
).) The zigzag relations are all trivial to check.
∎

Lemma
2.1
implies that the functor
\mathsf{Av}_{A}
sends injective objects of
\mathsf{Sh}(X,\Bbbk)
to injective objects of
\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)
. Since the category
\mathsf{Sh}(X,\Bbbk)
has enough injectives (see
[
SP
,
Tag 01DU
]
), it follows that the same property holds in
\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)
. In fact, if
\mathscr{F}
belongs to
\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)
, and if
\mathscr{I}
is an injective object of
\mathsf{Sh}(X,\Bbbk)
such that we have an injection
\mathsf{For}_{A}(\mathscr{F})\hookrightarrow\mathscr{I}
, then the map
\mathscr{F}\to\mathsf{Av}_{A}(\mathscr{I})
deduced by adjunction is injective, and
\mathsf{Av}_{A}(\mathscr{I})
is injective in
\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)
.

In particular, if
f:X\to Y
is an
A
-equivariant morphism between schemes with
A
-actions, recall that we have the (non derived) pushforward functor

f_{*}:\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(Y,\Bbbk).

From the considerations on injective objects above we deduce that this functor admits a derived functor

Rf_{*}:D^{+}\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)\to D^{+}\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(Y,\Bbbk),

which can be computed by means of injective resolutions.

Since the functor
\mathsf{For}_{A}
admits an exact left adjoint, it sends injective objects to injective objects. It follows
that for an equivariant morphism
f:X\to Y
as above we have a natural commutative diagram

\textstyle{D^{+}\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{Rf_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\mathsf{For}_{A}}
\textstyle{D^{+}\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(Y,\Bbbk)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathsf{For}_{A}}
\textstyle{D^{+}\mathsf{Sh}(X,\Bbbk)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{Rf_{*}}
\textstyle{D^{+}\mathsf{Sh}(Y,\Bbbk),}

where the lower horizontal arrow is the standard pushforward functor. In particular, in case
X
and
Y
are of finite type over some field
\mathbb{F}
of finite cohomological dimension (e.g. algebraically closed), and
\Bbbk
is torsion (e.g. a field of positive characteristic), it is known that the “standard” functor
Rf_{*}
sends
D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}(X,\Bbbk)
into
D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}(Y,\Bbbk)
, see
[
SP
,
Tag 0F10
]
. It follows that the “equivariant” functor
Rf_{*}
considered above restricts to a functor

Rf_{*}:D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)\to D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(Y,\Bbbk).

Of course, since the functor

f^{*}:\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(Y,\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)

is exact, we have an induced functor

f^{*}:D\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(Y,\Bbbk)\to D\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)

which maps
D^{+}\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(Y,\Bbbk)
into
D^{+}\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)
and
D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(Y,\Bbbk)
into
D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)
(and is compatible with the usual pullback functor
f^{*}
in the obvious way). It is easily checked that the functor

f^{*}:D^{+}\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(Y,\Bbbk)\to D^{+}\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)

is left adjoint to

Rf_{*}:D^{+}\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)\to D^{+}\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(Y,\Bbbk).

Equivariant sheaves and quotient map

From now on we assume that
X
is of finite type over some base scheme

S
, that
A
is abelian,

6

By finiteness the functor

Remark 2.2
.

As explained in
[
SGA1
, Exposé V, Proposition 1.8]
, the action of

on

is admissible iff each orbit of

is included in an affine open subset of

for some field

Recall that a complex
\mathscr{G}\in D\mathsf{Sh}(X/A,\Bbbk[A])
is said to have

[a,b]
(for
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}

a\leq b

\mathscr{G}^{\prime}\in\mathsf{Sh}(X/A,\Bbbk[A])
we have

\mathscr{H}^{n}(\mathscr{G}^{\prime}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\mathop{\otimes}^{L}}_{\Bbbk[A]}}{\otimes^{L}_{\Bbbk[A]}}{\otimes^{L}_{\Bbbk[A]}}{\otimes^{L}_{\Bbbk[A]}}\mathscr{G})=0\quad\text{unless $n\in[a,b]$,}

[

Tag

. Recall also that
\mathscr{G}
has tor amplitude in

[a,b]
iff for any geometric point
\overline{x}
of

of
\Bbbk[A]

\mathscr{G}_{\overline{x}}
has tor amplitude in
[a,b]
,

[

\mathscr{G}\in D\mathsf{Sh}(X/A,\Bbbk[A])
is said to be

if it has tor amplitude in
[a,b]
for

.

The action of
A
on
X
induces an action on geometric points. Namely, if

\overline{x}:\Spec(K)\to X

is a geometric point and
g\in A
, then the geometric point
g\cdot\overline{x}
is the composition

\Spec(K)\xrightarrow{\overline{x}}X\xrightarrow{\alpha_{g}}X.

We will say that the
A
-action on
X
is

if each geometric point of
X
has trivial stabilizer for this action.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
\Bbbk
is a field, and that the
A
-action on
X
is free. Then for any
\mathscr{G}
in
\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)
, the sheaf
q_{*}\mathscr{G}\in\mathsf{Sh}(X/A,\Bbbk[A])
has finite tor dimension.

Proof.

By the comments above,
to prove the lemma it suffices to prove that there exist
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
such that for any geometric point

\overline{y}:\Spec(K)\to X/A
the
\Bbbk[A]
-module

\mathscr{G}_{\overline{y}}
has tor amplitude in
[a,b]
. In fact one can take
a=b=0
, as we now explain.
By
[
SP
,
Tag 03QP
]
we have

(q_{*}\mathscr{G})_{\overline{y}}=\bigoplus_{\overline{x}}\mathscr{G}_{\overline{x}},

where
\overline{x}
runs over the set
X_{\overline{y}}
of maps
\overline{x}:\Spec(K)\to X
such that
q\circ\overline{x}=\overline{y}
. The morphism
q
is surjective, and its fibers are the
A
-orbits (see
[
SGA1
, Exposé V, §1]
). Hence
X_{\overline{y}}
is nonempty, and
A
acts transitively on this set. Our assumption ensures on the other hand that this action has trivial stabilizers. Therefore, if we fix some
\overline{x}\in X_{\overline{y}}
, we deduce a bijection
A\xrightarrow{\sim}X_{\overline{y}}
determined by
g\mapsto g\cdot\overline{x}
. The
A
-equivariant structure on
\mathscr{G}
provides a canonical isomorphism

\mathscr{G}_{\overline{x}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathscr{G}_{g\cdot\overline{x}}

for each
g\in A
. Using these data we obtain an isomorphism

(q_{*}\mathscr{G})_{\overline{y}}=\Bbbk[A]\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathscr{G}_{\overline{x}},

which is easily seen to be
A
-equivariant. Hence
(q_{*}\mathscr{G})_{\overline{y}}
is free as a
\Bbbk[A]
-module, in particular of tor
amplitude in
[0,0]
.
∎

2.4.
Stalks at fixed points

We continue with the assumptions of §
2.3
. The closed subscheme
X^{A}\subset X
of
A
-fixed points is the scheme which represents the functor
Z\mapsto\Hom(Z,X)^{A}
, where
A
acts on
\Hom(Z,X)
via its action on
X
. (The representability of this functor is easy in our setting: since our action is admissible it suffices to treat the case
X=\Spec(R)
is affine; then
X^{A}
is the spectrum of the maximal quotient of
R
on which
A
acts trivially, i.e. the quotient of
R
by the ideal generated by the elements
x-g\cdot x
for
x\in R
and
g\in A
.) As a set, the closed subscheme
X^{A}\subset X
consists of the points
x\in X
which are fixed by
A
and such that the induced action on the residue field
k(x)
is trivial.

By definition, any geometric point of
X
which is stable under the
A
-action considered in §
2.3
factors through a geometric point of
X^{A}
. In particular, if
A
is a simple group then the
A
-action on the open subset
U:=X\smallsetminus X^{A}\subset X
is free.

Lemma 2.4
.

The
A
-action on
U
is admissible.

Proof.

Consider the closed subset
X^{A}\subset X
. Since the quotient morphism
q
is finite, hence closed, the subset
q(X^{A})\subset X/A
is closed. Now from the definition of the subset
X^{A}\subset X
given above and the fact that the fibers of
q
are the
A
-orbits in
X
, one sees that

U=q^{-1}(X/A\smallsetminus q(X^{A})).

Hence the claim follows from
[
SGA1
, Exposé V, Corollaire 1.4]
.
∎

From this lemma we obtain in particular that the quotient
U/A
exists as a scheme. In fact, in the proof of this lemma we have seen that the open embedding
j:U\hookrightarrow X
induces an open embedding
\overline{\jmath}:U/A\to X/A
, with complement
q(X^{A})
, and that the quotient morphism
q_{U}:U\to U/A
is the restriction of
q
to
U
.

We now denote by
i:X^{A}\to X
the closed embedding. Note that
i
is
A
-equivariant for the trivial
A
-action on
X^{A}
; we therefore have a functor

i^{*}:D\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)\to D\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X^{A},\Bbbk)\overset{\eqref{eqn:equ-sheaves-triv-action}}{\cong}D\mathsf{Sh}(X^{A},\Bbbk[A]).

In other words, if
\mathscr{F}
belongs to
D\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(X,\Bbbk)
, then
i^{*}(\mathsf{For}_{A}(\mathscr{F}))
admits a canonical “lift” as a complex of sheaves of
\Bbbk[A]
-modules. In particular, for any geometric point
\overline{x}
of
X^{A}

the complex

\mathscr{F}_{i(\overline{x})}=(i^{*}\mathscr{F})_{\overline{x}}

is in a natural way an object of
D(\Bbbk[A]\mathchar 45\relax\mathsf{Mod})
.

From now on we assume that
S=\Spec(\mathbb{F})
for some field
\mathbb{F}
of finite cohomological dimension, and that
X
is of finite type over
\mathbb{F}
. (This assumption implies that
U
is also of finite type over
\mathbb{F}
, see
[
GW
, Example 3.45]
. By
[
SGA1
, Corollaire 1.5]
we deduce that
X/A
and
U/A
also are of finite type.) We also assume that
\Bbbk
is a field of characteristic
\ell>0
which is nonzero in
\mathbb{F}
.
The proof of the following proposition was explained to us by L. Illusie and W. Zheng. (A different, longer and slightly less easy proof can also be deduced from
[
DL
, Proposition 3.7]
.) Recall that a bounded complex of
\Bbbk[A]
-modules is called perfect if it is isomorphic in
D^{\mathrm{b}}(\Bbbk[A]\mathchar 45\relax\mathsf{Mod})
to a bounded complex of finitely generated projective modules.

Proposition 2.5
.

Assume that
A
is a simple group.
If
\mathscr{F}\in D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}_{A}(U,\Bbbk)
is such that
\mathsf{For}_{A}(\mathscr{F})
has constructible cohomology sheaves (see
[
SP
,
Tag 03RW
]
), then for any geometric point
\overline{x}
of
X^{A}
the complex of
\Bbbk[A]
-modules

(Rj_{*}\mathscr{F})_{\overline{x}}

is perfect.

Proof.

Recall that the morphism
j
has finite cohomological dimension by
[
SP
,
Tag 0F10
]
. As a consequence the complex
Rj_{*}\mathscr{F}
is bounded, so that
(Rj_{*}\mathscr{F})_{\overline{x}}
is bounded. On the other hand, by
[
SGA4

1
2

, Th. finitude, Théorème 1.1]
, the complex
Rj_{*}\mathscr{F}
is constructible; hence its stalk
(Rj_{*}\mathscr{F})_{\overline{x}}
has finite-dimensional cohomology. By
[
SP
,
Tag 0658
]
, we deduce that to prove that this complex is perfect it suffices to prove that it has finite tor dimension. But since the image
x
of
\overline{x}
is fixed by
A
we have
q^{-1}(q(x))=\{x\}
, which implies that

(Rj_{*}\mathscr{F})_{\overline{x}}=\bigl(q_{*}(Rj_{*}\mathscr{F})\bigr)_{q(\overline{x})}

by
[
SP
,
Tag 03QP
]
. Now we have
q\circ j=\overline{\jmath}\circ q_{U}
, so that

q_{*}(Rj_{*}\mathscr{F})=R\overline{\jmath}_{*}((q_{U})_{*}\mathscr{F}).

By Lemma
2.3
the sheaf
(q_{U})_{*}\mathscr{F}
has finite tor dimension, hence by
[
SGA4
, Exposé XVII, Théorème 5.2.11]
and
[
SP
,
Tag 0F10
]
the complex
R\overline{\jmath}_{*}((q_{U})_{*}\mathscr{F})
also does, so that its stalk

\bigl(R\overline{\jmath}_{*}((q_{U})_{*}\mathscr{F})\bigr)_{q(\overline{x})}

must have finite tor dimension, which finishes the proof.
∎

3.
Smith theory for étale sheaves

3.1.
\varpi
-equivariant derived categories

The formalism of “Smith theory” that we will build will use the equivariant derived category of Bernstein–Lunts
[
BL
]
. This category is explicitly constructed only in a topological setting in
[
BL
]
, but it is well known that it applies also in the setting of étale sheaves under appropriate assumptions, see
[
BL
, §4.3]
.
In this subsection we briefly recall this construction in the particular case that we require.

So, from now on we fix an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
p
, and a finite field
\Bbbk
of characteristic
\ell\neq p
. We will consider (admissible) actions of the finite
\mathbb{F}
-group scheme
\varpi={\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}
of
\ell
-th roots of unity on
\mathbb{F}
-schemes of finite type. Here since
\mathbb{F}
is algebraically closed,
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}
is the constant group scheme associated with the finite group
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}(\mathbb{F})
, so that the constructions of Section
2
also apply in this setting. For simplicity, we will not explicitly distinguish the group scheme
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}
and the finite group
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}(\mathbb{F})
.

The construction of
[
BL
]
uses some “acyclic resolutions.” In this case these resolutions can be constructed explicitly as follows:
for any
n\geq 0
we set

V_{n}:=\mathbb{F}^{n}\smallsetminus\{0\},

with the (admissible) action of
\varpi
induced by the dilation action of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
. We have

\mathsf{H}^{m}(V_{n};\Bbbk)=\begin{cases}\Bbbk&\text{if $m=0$;}\\
0&\text{if $1\leq m\leq 2n-2$.}\end{cases}

From this we see that for any
\mathbb{F}
-scheme
X
of finite type the projection

p^{X}_{n}:V_{n}\times X\to X

is
(2n-2)
-acyclic, in the sense that for any
X
-scheme
Y
of finite type the morphism
p^{X,Y}_{n}:Y\times_{X}(V_{n}\times X)\to Y
is such that for any (étale)
\Bbbk
-sheaf
\mathscr{F}
the morphism

\mathscr{F}\to\tau_{\leq 2n-2}(R(p^{X,Y}_{n})_{*}(p^{X,Y}_{n})^{*}\mathscr{F})

induced by adjunction is an isomorphism. (In fact, here by the Künneth formula
[
SP
,
Tag 0F1N
]
we have
R(p^{X,Y}_{n})_{*}(p^{X,Y}_{n})^{*}\mathscr{F}\cong\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}(V_{n},\Bbbk)\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathscr{F}
.)

We now fix an
\mathbb{F}
-scheme
X
of finite type endowed with an admissible action of
\varpi
.
For any
n\geq 1
we set

P_{n}^{X}:=V_{n}\times X,

and consider the projection
p^{X}_{n}:P_{n}^{X}\to X
as above. Since the actions of
\varpi
on
V_{n}
and
X
are admissible, this property also holds for the product
V_{n}\times X
(with the diagonal action), so that we can consider the quotient

\overline{P}_{n}^{X}:=P_{n}^{X}/\varpi.

With the notation of §
2.4
, we have
(P_{n}^{X})^{\varpi}=\varnothing
; therefore, by
[
SGA1
, Exposé V, Corollaire 2.3]
the quotient map
q_{n}^{X}:P_{n}^{X}\to\overline{P}_{n}^{X}
is étale. In fact, in view of
[
SGA1
, Exposé V, Proposition 2.6]
this map is an étale locally trivial principal homogeneous space for
\varpi
in the sense of
[
SP
,
Tag 049A
]
.

For any
n
, we will denote by

D^{\mathrm{b}}(X,\varpi,n,\Bbbk)

the category whose

•

objects are triples
(\mathscr{F}_{n},\mathscr{F}_{X},\beta)
where
\mathscr{F}_{n}
is an object of
D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}(\overline{P}_{n}^{X},\Bbbk)
,
\mathscr{F}_{X}
is an object of
D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}(X,\Bbbk)
, and

\beta:(q_{n}^{X})^{*}\mathscr{F}_{n}\xrightarrow{\sim}(p_{n}^{X})^{*}\mathscr{F}_{X}

is an isomorphism;

•

morphisms from
(\mathscr{F}_{n},\mathscr{F}_{X},\beta)
to
(\mathscr{G}_{n},\mathscr{G}_{X},\gamma)
are pairs
(\varphi_{n},\varphi_{X})
with

\varphi_{n}:\mathscr{F}_{n}\to\mathscr{G}_{n},\qquad\varphi_{X}:\mathscr{F}_{X}\to\mathscr{G}_{X}

morphisms such that
\gamma\circ((q_{n}^{X})^{*}\varphi_{n})=((p_{n}^{X})^{*}\varphi_{X})\circ\beta
.

For any bounded interval
I\subset\mathbb{Z}
we denote by
D^{I}(X,\varpi,n,\Bbbk)
the full subcategory of
D^{\mathrm{b}}(X,\varpi,n,\Bbbk)
whose objects are the triples
(\mathscr{F}_{n},\mathscr{F}_{X},\beta)
where
\mathscr{H}^{m}(\mathscr{F}_{X})
vanishes unless
m\in I
. Then the category
D^{I}(X,\varpi,n,\Bbbk)
does not depend (up to canonical equivalence) on the choice of
n
, as long as
2n-2\geq|I|
, where
|I|
is the length of
I
; in fact, by the same arguments as in
[
BL
, §2.3.4]
,
if
n,m
satisfy this condition then the natural functors from
D^{I}(X,\varpi,n,\Bbbk)
and
D^{I}(X,\varpi,m,\Bbbk)
to the category defined similarly with
P_{n}^{X}
and
P_{m}^{X}
replaced by

V_{n}\times V_{m}\times X=P_{n}^{X}\times_{X}P_{m}^{X}

(with the diagonal
\varpi
-action) are equivalences of categories.

We can therefore define the
\varpi
-equivariant derived category

D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\varpi}(X,\Bbbk)

as the direct limit of the categories
D^{I}(X,\varpi,n,\Bbbk)
with
n\gg 0
, where
I
runs over the bounded intervals of
\mathbb{Z}
. This category admits a canonical structure of triangulated category, see
[
BL
, §§2.5.1–2.5.2]
. By construction, we have a canonical triangulated forgetful functor

(3.1)

\mathsf{For}_{\varpi}:D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\varpi}(X,\Bbbk)\to D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}(X,\Bbbk)

which sends a triple
(\mathscr{F}_{n},\mathscr{F}_{X},\beta)
to
\mathscr{F}_{X}
.

Remark 3.1
.

As explained in
[
BL
, Lemma 2.3.2]
, if
2n-2\geq|I|
the functor

sending

to
\mathscr{F}_{n}
is fully faithful. In particular, morphisms between objects in

can always be computed as morphisms in derived categories of quotients of “sufficiently acyclic” resolutions.

This construction is of course functorial in
X
.
Namely, consider
\mathbb{F}
-schemes of finite type
X,Y
endowed with admissible actions of
\varpi
, and a
\varpi
-equivariant morphism of
\mathbb{F}
-schemes

. (Note that
f
is automatically quasi-compact since

is Noetherian, see
[
SP
,
Tag 01P0
]
. It is also locally of finite type by
[

,
Tag 01T8
]
, hence of finite type. Finally,

,

and

are quasi-separated by
[

,
Tag 01T7
]
.)

(1)

We have a (
*
-)pullback functor

which can be explicitly described in terms of the pullback functors associated with

and the induced morphism
\overline{P}_{n}^{X}\to\overline{P}_{n}^{Y}
.

(2)

We have a (
*

(Here we use the fact that the usual pushforward functors respect the bounded derived categories, see

,

]
, and also that the morphisms

,

[

,

]
to “transport” the isomorphism

.)

(3)

If we assume that

is separated
then we also have a

-pushforward functor

see
[

, Exposé XVII]

(Here, the fact that the

(4)

By construction, all of these functors are compatible with the forgetful functor (

In practice all the schemes we will consider will be quasi-projective over

3.2.
Equivariant derived categories and equivariant sheaves

We continue with the setting of §
3.1
.
In Section
2
we have studied equivariant sheaves on schemes, and in §
3.1
we have considered the equivariant derived category. It is now time to explain the relation between these two constructions. This relation is based on the observation that (for any
\mathbb{F}
-scheme
X
of finite type with an admissible
\varpi
-action, and any
n\geq 1
) the natural pullback functor

\mathsf{Sh}(\overline{P}^{X}_{n},\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Sh}_{\varpi}(P^{X}_{n},\Bbbk)

is an equivalence of categories, by the sheaf condition applied to the étale covering
q_{n}^{X}:P_{n}^{X}\to\overline{P}_{n}^{X}
. Therefore, for any
\varpi
-equivariant sheaf
\mathscr{F}
on
X
the pullback
(p_{n}^{X})^{*}\mathscr{F}
admits a natural structure of
\varpi
-equivariant sheaf on
P_{n}^{X}
, hence descends to a sheaf
\mathscr{F}_{n}
on
\overline{P}_{n}^{X}
. Using this construction we define a canonical triangulated functor

(3.2)

D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}_{\varpi}(X,\Bbbk)\to D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\varpi}(X,\Bbbk).

Proposition 3.3
.

The functor (
3.2
) is an equivalence of categories.

Proof.

Let us first show that our functor is fully faithful. For this we need to show that for any
\varpi
-equivariant sheaves
\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
on
X
and any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, for
n\gg 0
the natural map

\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}_{\varpi}(X,\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}^{\prime}[m])\to\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}(\overline{P}^{X}_{n},\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{F}_{n},\mathscr{F}^{\prime}_{n}[m])

is an isomorphism, see Remark
3.1
. By construction, this amounts to proving that for
n\gg 0
the pullback functor induces an isomorphism

\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}_{\varpi}(X,\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}^{\prime}[m])\xrightarrow{\sim}\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}_{\varpi}(P_{n}^{X},\Bbbk)}((p_{n}^{X})^{*}\mathscr{F},(p_{n}^{X})^{*}\mathscr{F}^{\prime}[m]).

However by adjunction we have

\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}_{\varpi}(P_{n}^{X},\Bbbk)}((p_{n}^{X})^{*}\mathscr{F},(p_{n}^{X})^{*}\mathscr{F}^{\prime}[m])\\
\cong\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}_{\varpi}(X,\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{F},R(p_{n}^{X})_{*}(p_{n}^{X})^{*}\mathscr{F}^{\prime}[m]).

Now the right-hand side can be replaced by

\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}_{\varpi}(X,\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{F},\tau_{\leq 0}(R(p_{n}^{X})_{*}(p_{n}^{X})^{*}\mathscr{F}^{\prime}[m]))\\
\cong\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}_{\varpi}(X,\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{F},\tau_{\leq m}(R(p_{n}^{X})_{*}(p_{n}^{X})^{*}\mathscr{F}^{\prime})[m]).

If
2n-2\geq m
the morphism

\mathscr{F}^{\prime}\to\tau_{\leq m}(R(p_{n}^{X})_{*}(p_{n}^{X})^{*}\mathscr{F}^{\prime})

induced by adjunction is an isomorphism, which concludes the proof of fully faithfulness.

Once fully faithfulness is established, to conclude the proof it suffices to prove that images of
\varpi
-equivariant sheaves on
X
generate
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\varpi}(X,\Bbbk)
as a triangulated category. This is however clear from the construction of the “standard” t-structure in
[
BL
, §§2.5.1–2.5.2]
.
∎

In particular, in the special case where the
\varpi
-action on
X
is trivial, using Proposition
3.3
combined with (
2.3
), we obtain a canonical equivalence of triangulated categories

(3.3)

D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\varpi}(X,\Bbbk)\cong D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}(X,\Bbbk[\varpi]).

We now consider two
\mathbb{F}
-schemes
X
and
Y
of finite type, with admissible actions of
\varpi
, and a
\varpi
-equivariant morphism
f:X\to Y
. We have considered functors

\textstyle{D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}_{\varpi}(X,\Bbbk)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{Rf_{*}}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}_{\varpi}(Y,\Bbbk)}
\scriptstyle{f^{*}}

in §
2.2
, and functors

\textstyle{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\varpi}(X,\Bbbk)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{Rf_{*}}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\varpi}(Y,\Bbbk)}
\scriptstyle{f^{*}}

in §

. These functors are related in the natural way, as explained in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

The diagrams

\textstyle{D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Sh}_{\varpi}(Y,\Bbbk)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

3.3.
The crucial lemma

3.4.
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-equivariant derived categories

Later we will also need to consider equivariant derived categories for actions of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}
. The construction of this category is similar to, and in fact simpler than, the construction in §

. Namely, for any

is obtained by restriction from a natural
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action, and moreover we have a canonical map

which is a Zariski locally trivial principal
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-bundle. Therefore, given any
\mathbb{F}
-scheme
X
endowed with an action of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
, we consider the diagonal action on
V_{n}\times X
, and we have a Zariski locally trivial principal
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-bundle

V_{n}\times X\to V_{n}\times^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}X

for some scheme
V_{n}\times^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}X
which can be constructed by (Zariski) gluing over the natural cover which trivializes the map
V_{n}\to\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}
. If we assume
X
to be of finite type, then as in §
3.1
the map
p_{n}^{X}:V_{n}\times X\to X
is

-acyclic, which allows to define a category

, and check that the subcategory
D^{I}(X,\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},n,\Bbbk)
does not depend on the choice of

as long as
2n-2\geq|I|
. We can finally define the equivariant derived category
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X,\Bbbk)
as the direct limit of the categories

(with

) over the finite intervals

. These categories have the same functoriality properties as the categories

; in particular we have a natural (triangulated) forgetful functor

As in the
\varpi
-equivariant setting, we will denote by

the full subcategory of

whose objects are those

such that
\mathsf{For}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathscr{F})
has constructible cohomology sheaves.

The category

has a canonical object whose image under

is the constant sheaf

; it will also be denoted

In these terms, the

3.5.
The “Smith category” of a point

In this subsection we consider the special case of the constructions of §

where

whose objects are the complexes whose cohomology is finite-dimensional (or equivalently finitely generated over

We will denote by

the full triangulated subcategory whose objects are the complexes
\mathscr{F}
such that
\Res^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}_{\varpi}(\mathscr{F})
, considered as a complex of
\Bbbk[\varpi]
-modules through (

We then set

\mathsf{Sm}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk):=D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},c}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)/D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},c}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)_{\varpi\mathchar 45\relax\mathsf{perf}},

where we consider the Verdier quotient category.

The following lemma will be crucial for us below, in that it will allow to use some parity vanishing arguments in various variants of the category

Lemma 3.7
.

For any

we have

\Hom_{\mathsf{Sm}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)}(\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathrm{pt}},\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathrm{pt}}[n])\cong\begin{cases}\Bbbk&\text{if $n$ is even;}\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where we omit the quotient functor
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},c}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Sm}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)

The proof of Lemma
3.7
will require some preparation. We start with the following claim.

Lemma 3.8
.

For any
\mathscr{F}
in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},c}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)
, there exists a canonical isomorphism of graded
\Bbbk

\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\varpi}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)}\bigl(\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathrm{pt}},\Res^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}_{\varpi}(\mathscr{F})[m]\bigr)\cong\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathrm{pt},\mathscr{F})\oplus\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathrm{pt},\mathscr{F})[-1].

Proof.

We fix
\mathscr{F}
in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},c}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)
and

n\gg 0
the object
\mathscr{F}
is represented by a triple
(\mathscr{F}_{n},\mathscr{F}_{X},\beta)
in

and similarly for
\mathsf{H}^{m+1}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathrm{pt},\mathscr{F})
. If we denote by

\pi_{n}:V_{n}/\varpi\to\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}

the natural map, then
\Res^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}_{\varpi}(\mathscr{F})
is represented by the object
((\pi_{n})^{*}\mathscr{F}_{n},\mathscr{F}_{X},\beta)
in

, so that we have

\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\varpi}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)}\bigl(\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathrm{pt}},\Res^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}_{\varpi}(\mathscr{F})[m]\bigr)=\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}(V_{n}/\varpi,\Bbbk)}(\underline{\Bbbk}_{V_{n}/\varpi},(\pi_{n})^{*}\mathscr{F}_{n}[m]).

To prove the lemma, it therefore suffices to prove that for any
\mathscr{G}
in

\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}(V_{n}/\varpi,\Bbbk)}(\underline{\Bbbk}_{V_{n}/\varpi},(\pi_{n})^{*}\mathscr{G})\cong\\
\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1},\Bbbk)}(\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}},\mathscr{G})\oplus\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1},\Bbbk)}(\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{F}}^{n-1}},\mathscr{G}[-1]).

We start by proving that

Proof of Lemma
3.7
.

Lemma
3.9
shows in particular that
\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathrm{pt}}
does not belong to

\Hom_{\mathsf{Sm}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)}(\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathrm{pt}},\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathrm{pt}}[n])\neq 0

. Hence to conclude it only remains to prove that

\dim\Hom_{\mathsf{Sm}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)}(\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathrm{pt}},\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathrm{pt}}[n])\leq\begin{cases}1&\text{if $n$ is even;}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

A morphism
a
from
\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathrm{pt}}
to
\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathrm{pt}}[n]
in
\mathsf{Sm}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)
is represented by a diagram

\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathrm{pt}}\xleftarrow{f}\mathscr{F}\xrightarrow{g}\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathrm{pt}}[n]

in which
\mathscr{F}
belongs to
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},c}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)
,
f
and
g
are morphisms in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},c}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)
, and the cone of

belongs to

, i.e. has finite-dimensional equivariant cohomology (see Lemma

). In particular, from the long exact sequence in equivariant cohomology and (

) we obtain that there exists
N\in 2\mathbb{Z}
(which, for later use, we will assume to be at least

\mathsf{H}^{m}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathrm{pt},\mathscr{F})=\begin{cases}\Bbbk&\text{if $m$ is even;}\\

, considered as a morphism

in

by Lemma
3.9
. As a consequence,
a
can also be represented by the diagram

and

G
be a connected reductive algebraic group over
\mathbb{F}
, and choose a Borel subgroup
B\subset G
and a maximal torus
T\subset B
. The Weyl group of
(G,T)
will be denoted
W_{\mathrm{f}}
. (Here, the subscript stands for “finite,” and is here to avoid any confusion with the affine Weyl group introduced below.) We will also denote by
U
the unipotent radical of
B
, by
B^{+}
the Borel subgroup opposite to
B
with respect to
T
, and by
U^{+}
the unipotent radical of
B^{+}
.

We will denote by
\mathbf{X}:=X^{*}(T)
the character lattice of
T
, and by
\mathbf{X}^{\vee}:=X_{*}(T)
its cocharacter lattice. Let
\mathfrak{R}\subset\mathbf{X}
be the root system of
(G,T)
, and let
\mathfrak{R}^{+}\subset\mathfrak{R}
be the system of positive roots consisting of the
T
-weights in
\mathrm{Lie}(U^{+})
. Let also
\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{s}}
be the associated basis of
\mathfrak{R}
(the “simple roots”). These data define a set
S_{\mathrm{f}}
of Coxeter generators for
W_{\mathrm{f}}
, consisting of the reflections
s_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\in\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{s}}
.

The
affine Weyl group
is the semi-direct product

W_{\mathrm{aff}}:=W_{\mathrm{f}}\ltimes\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{R}^{\vee},

where
\mathfrak{R}^{\vee}\subset\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
is the coroot system, and
\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{R}^{\vee}
is the coroot lattice.
For
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{R}^{\vee}
we will denote by
\mathsf{t}_{\lambda}
the image of
\lambda
in
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
. The group
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
admits a natural structure of Coxeter group extending that of
W_{\mathrm{f}}
; the corresponding simple reflections
S_{\mathrm{aff}}\subset W_{\mathrm{aff}}
consist of
S_{\mathrm{f}}
together with the elements
\mathsf{t}_{\beta^{\vee}}s_{\beta}
with
\beta
a maximal root in
\mathfrak{R}
.

Given
n\in\mathbb{Z}\smallsetminus\{0\}
, we will consider two actions of
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
on
V:=\mathbf{X}^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
defined, for
w\in W_{\mathrm{f}}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{R}^{\vee}
, by

(w\mathsf{t}_{\lambda})\cdot_{n}\mu=w(\mu-n\lambda),\qquad(w\mathsf{t}_{\lambda})\square_{n}\mu=w(\mu+n\lambda)

for
\mu\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
, where in the right-hand side we consider the natural action of
W_{\mathrm{f}}
on
\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
.
(Here the action
\cdot_{n}
appears due to the sign conventions in Bruhat–Tits theory; but the action
\square_{n}
is closer to the action which will be relevant when considering Representation Theory.) Of course, these actions are related via

w\square_{n}\mu=-(w\cdot_{n}(-\mu))

for any
w\in W_{\mathrm{aff}}
and
\mu\in V
.

We set

\mathbf{a}_{n}:=\{\lambda\in V\mid\forall\alpha\in\mathfrak{R}^{+},\,-n<\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle<0\}.

Then the closure
\overline{\mathbf{a}}_{n}
of
\mathbf{a}_{n}
is a fundamental
domain for the action of
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
on
V
via
\cdot_{n}
and via
\square_{n}
. These actions stabilize
\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
, and a fundamental domain for the action of
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
on
\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
(for each of these actions) is therefore
\overline{\mathbf{a}}_{n}\cap\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
. (Compared to the conventions used in
[
dCHL
]
, our “fundamental alcove”
\mathbf{a}_{n}
is opposite. This is related to the fact that our fixed Borel subgroup
B
is chosen to be
negative
, while in
[
dCHL
]
it is chosen to be positive.)

The
affine roots
are the formal linear combinations
\alpha+m\hbar
with
\alpha\in\mathfrak{R}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. To such a combination we attach an affine function
f^{n}_{\alpha+m\hbar}
on
V
, determined by

f^{n}_{\alpha+m\hbar}(v)=\langle\alpha,v\rangle+nm,

and an element
s_{\alpha+m\hbar}\in W_{\mathrm{aff}}
determined by

s_{\alpha+m\hbar}=\mathsf{t}_{m\alpha^{\vee}}s_{\alpha}.

We then have

s_{\alpha+m\hbar}\cdot_{n}v=v-f^{n}_{\alpha+m\hbar}(v)\alpha^{\vee}

for any
v\in V
.

Remark 4.1
.

In practice, when considering these constructions in later sections, the integer
n
will be either
1
or a prime number different from
p
. As this assumption does not simplify the discussion in any way, we will not impose any restriction on
n
in this section.

4.2.
Some Bruhat–Tits theory

For any positive integer
n
, we set
\mathscr{K}_{n}:=\mathbb{F}(\hskip-1.0pt(z^{n})\hskip-1.0pt)
. We will consider
\mathscr{K}:=\mathscr{K}_{1}
as a valued field with its natural valuation (such that
z
has valuation
1
), and endow each
\mathscr{K}_{n}
with the valuation obtained by restriction. (In this way, all the fields
\mathscr{K}_{n}
are canonically isomorphic, but their valuations differ.) We will denote by
\mathscr{O}_{n}
the valuation ring of
\mathscr{K}_{n}
, so that
\mathscr{O}_{n}:=\mathbb{F}[\hskip-1.0pt[z^{n}]\hskip-1.0pt]
. For any
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
we have a point
z^{\lambda}\in G(\mathscr{K})
, defined as the image of
z
under the map
(\mathscr{K})^{\times}\to G(\mathscr{K})
induced by
\lambda
. If
\lambda\in n\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
, then
z^{\lambda}
belongs to
G(\mathscr{K}_{n})
.

The group scheme
G\times_{\Spec(\mathbb{F})}\Spec(\mathscr{K}_{n})
is a (split)
connected reductive group scheme over
\mathscr{K}_{n}
, so that one can
consider the associated “enlarged” Bruhat–Tits
building
\mathfrak{B}_{n}
(in the sense considered e.g. in
[
Pr
]
) which carries an action of
G(\mathscr{K}_{n})
. Our choice of maximal torus in
G
provides a split maximal torus
T\times_{\Spec(\mathbb{F})}\Spec(\mathscr{K}_{n})\subset G\times_{\Spec(\mathbb{F})}\Spec(\mathscr{K}_{n})
, which itself defines an apartment
A_{n}
in
\mathfrak{B}_{n}
. This apartment is an affine space with underlying vector space
V
, and it is stable under the action of
N_{G}(T)(\mathscr{K}_{n})
. The vectorial part of the action of
N_{G}(T)(\mathscr{K}_{n})
on
A_{n}
factors through the natural action of
N_{G}(T)(\mathscr{K}_{n})/T(\mathscr{K}_{n})=W_{\mathrm{f}}
on
V
, and for
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
the element
z^{n\lambda}\in T(\mathscr{K}_{n})
acts by translation by
-n\lambda
(see
[
Pr
, §1.8]
). Let us choose, for any
w\in W_{\mathrm{f}}
, a lift
\dot{w}
of
w
in
N_{G}(T)
. Then we will consider the map

\iota_{n}:W_{\mathrm{aff}}\to N_{G}(T)(\mathscr{K}_{n})

defined by
\iota_{n}(\mathsf{t}_{\lambda}w)=z^{n\lambda}\dot{w}
for
w\in W_{\mathrm{f}}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{R}^{\vee}
.

If we choose a
W_{\mathrm{f}}
-fixed point in
A_{n}
, then the action of
V
on this point defines an identification

(4.1)

V\xrightarrow{\sim}A_{n},

under which the action of
N_{G}(T)(\mathscr{K}_{n})
on
A_{n}
identifies with the action of
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
on
V
provided by
\cdot_{n}
. We will fix such an identification once and for all, and use it to identify all the data considered above related to
V
as data related to
A_{n}
. (None of our considerations below will depend on the choice of identification (
4.1
).)

The collection of fixed points of the reflections
s_{\alpha+m\hbar}
(or in other words of kernels of the functions
f^{n}_{\alpha+m\hbar}
) defines a hyperplane arrangement in
A_{n}
, hence a collection of
facets
(see
[
Bo
, Chap. V, §1.2]
). In particular,

\mathbf{a}_{n}

is a facet of maximal dimension, i.e. an alcove.
Another example of facet is the intersection
\mathbf{o}_{n}
of the reflection hyperplanes associated with all the reflections
s_{\beta}
with
\beta\in\mathfrak{R}
, i.e. the set of
W_{\mathrm{f}}
-fixed points. The facets we will mainly be interested in are those contained in the closure
\overline{\mathbf{a}}_{n}
of
\mathbf{a}_{n}
.

To any facet
\mathbf{f}
in
A_{n}
, Bruhat–Tits theory
associates a “parahoric group scheme”
P_{\mathbf{f}}
over
\Spec(\mathscr{O}_{n})
, a smooth affine group scheme over
\Spec(\mathscr{O}_{n})
with connected geometric fibers such that

(4.2)

P_{\mathbf{f}}\times_{\Spec(\mathscr{O}_{n})}\Spec(\mathscr{K}_{n})=G\times_{\Spec(\mathbb{F})}\Spec(\mathscr{K}_{n}),

and whose
\mathscr{O}_{n}
-points are of finite index in the pointwise stabilizer of
\mathbf{f}
in
G(\mathscr{K}_{n})
. (This group scheme is denoted similarly in
[
dCHL
]
; in
[
Pr
]
it is denoted
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbf{f}}^{\circ}
.) In particular we have

P_{\mathbf{o}_{n}}=G\times_{\Spec(\mathbb{F})}\Spec(\mathscr{O}_{n}),

and
P_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}
is an Iwahori group scheme, whose group of
\mathscr{O}_{n}
-points is the inverse image
of
B
under the map
G(\mathscr{O}_{n})\to G
of evaluation at
z^{n}=0
. This construction is compatible with inclusions of closures of facets in a natural way; in particular for any facet
\mathbf{f}
contained in
\overline{\mathbf{a}}_{n}
we have a closed embedding

(4.3)

P_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}\subset P_{\mathbf{f}}.

4.3.
Loop groups and partial affine flag varieties

As above we fix a positive integer
n
.
The
n
-th
loop group
associated with
G
is the ind-affine group ind-scheme
L_{n}G
over
\mathbb{F}
which represents the functor sending an
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
R
to
G(R(\hskip-1.0pt(z^{n})\hskip-1.0pt))
. The associated
arc group
(or positive loop group) is the affine group scheme
L^{+}_{n}G
over
\mathbb{F}
which represents the functor sending
R
to
G(R[\hskip-1.0pt[z^{n}]\hskip-1.0pt])
. (For basics about ind-schemes, see
[
Rz
, §1]
.)

The case we are mostly interested in is when
n=1
. In this case (here and in later related notation), we will usually omit the subscript from the notation. The case of a more general
n
however naturally appears when considering the action of
n
-th roots of unity by loop rotation. Namely, we have a natural action of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}
on
LG
by loop rotation. This action stabilizes the subgroup
L^{+}G
. Denote now by
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}\subset\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
the subgroup scheme of
n
-th roots of unity; we can then consider the fixed-points ind-scheme
(LG)^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}
and the fixed-points scheme
(L^{+}G)^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}
in the sense of §
2.4
.

Lemma 4.2
.

We have identifications

(LG)^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}=L_{n}G,\quad(L^{+}G)^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}=L_{n}^{+}G.

Proof.

For any
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
R
, the
R
-points
(LG)(R)
consist of the
\mathbb{F}
-scheme morphisms
\Spec(R(\hskip-1.0pt(z)\hskip-1.0pt))\to G
. Therefore, the
R
-points of
(LG)^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}
consist of the
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}
-invariant morphisms
\Spec(R(\hskip-1.0pt(z)\hskip-1.0pt))\to G
, i.e. the morphisms which factor through the quotient
\Spec(R(\hskip-1.0pt(z)\hskip-1.0pt))\to\Spec(R(\hskip-1.0pt(z)\hskip-1.0pt))/{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}=\Spec(R(\hskip-1.0pt(z^{n})\hskip-1.0pt))
. This proves the first identification. The proof of the second one is similar.
∎

It is well known (see e.g.
[
Rz
, §3]
) that the fppf sheafification of the functor

R\mapsto(LG)(R)/(L^{+}G)(R)

is represented by an ind-projective ind-scheme over
\mathbb{F}
, which is called the
affine Grassmannian
of
G
, and will be denoted
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
. The main goal of this section is to describe the ind-scheme
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}
, see Proposition
4.7
below. This will require discussing more general “partial affine flag varieties” attached to
L_{n}G
, as follows.

If
\mathbf{f}\subset\overline{\mathbf{a}}_{n}
is a facet, we can consider the affine group scheme
L^{+}_{n}P_{\mathbf{f}}
over
\mathbb{F}
which represents the functor sending
R
to
P_{\mathbf{f}}(R[\hskip-1.0pt[z^{n}]\hskip-1.0pt])
. In view of (
4.2
),
L^{+}_{n}P_{\mathbf{f}}
is a subgroup of
L_{n}G
. The partial affine flag variety

{\mathcal{F}l}^{n}_{\mathbf{f}}

associated with
\mathbf{f}
is the ind-projective ind-scheme over
\mathbb{F}
which represents the fppf sheafification of the presheaf

R\mapsto L_{n}G(R)/L_{n}^{+}P_{\mathbf{f}}(R).

These ind-schemes are the main object of study of
[
PR
]
. In particular, the connected components of
{\mathcal{F}l}^{n}_{\mathbf{f}}
are in a natural bijection with the algebraic fundamental group of
G
(see
[
PR
, Theorem 0.1]
); the component corresponding to the neutral element will be denoted
{\mathcal{F}l}^{n,\circ}_{\mathbf{f}}
.

If
\alpha\in\mathfrak{R}
, we will denote by
U_{\alpha}
the root subgroup of
G
attached to
\alpha
. Then, for an affine root
\alpha+m\hbar
, we will denote by
U_{\alpha+m\hbar}
the subgroup of
LG
which, for any isomorphism
u_{\alpha}:\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}\xrightarrow{\sim}U_{\alpha}
, identifies with the image of the morphism
x\mapsto u_{\alpha}(xz^{m})
.

The following statements are easily checked.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathfrak{R}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(1)

The subgroup
U_{\alpha+m\hbar}
is stable under the action of
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}
, and we have

(U_{\alpha+m\hbar})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}=\begin{cases}U_{\alpha+m\hbar}&\text{if $m\in n\mathbb{Z}$;}\\
\{1\}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(2)

If
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
, we have

z^{\lambda}\cdot U_{\alpha+m\hbar}\cdot z^{-\lambda}=U_{\alpha+(m+\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle)\hbar}.

(3)

If
\mathbf{f}\subset\overline{\mathbf{a}}_{n}
is a facet, we have
U_{\alpha+mn\hbar}\subset L_{n}^{+}P_{\mathbf{f}}
iff
f^{n}_{\alpha+m\hbar}
takes nonnegative values on
\mathbf{f}
.

4.4.
Fixed points on orbits

A crucial role in our discussion will be played by the following Iwahori subgroups of
L^{+}G
, for which we introduce special notation:

\mathrm{Iw}:=L_{1}^{+}P_{\mathbf{a}_{1}},\quad\mathrm{Iw}^{+}:=\dot{w}_{0}\cdot\mathrm{Iw}\cdot(\dot{w}_{0})^{-1}.

(Here,
w_{0}
is the longest element in
W_{\mathrm{f}}
.) More concretely,
\mathrm{Iw}
, resp.
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}
,
is the inverse image of
B
, resp.
B^{+}
, under the map
\mathrm{ev}_{0}:L^{+}G\to G
sending
z
to
0
. We also denote by
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u}}
and
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u}}^{+}
the pro-unipotent radicals of
\mathrm{Iw}
and
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}
, i.e. the inverse images of
U
and
U^{+}
under
\mathrm{ev}_{0}
.

Lor
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
, we denote by
L_{\lambda}
the coset of
z^{\lambda}
in
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
.
The group scheme
L^{+}G
acts on
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
(see §
4.3
), and the orbits of this action are parametrized by the subsemigroup
\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{+}\subset\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
of dominant cocharacters. More precisely,
we have

(4.4)

({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})_{\mathrm{red}}=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{+}}{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}^{\lambda}\quad\text{with ${\mathcal{G}r}_{G}^{\lambda}:=L^{+}G\cdot L_{\lambda}$,}

where the left-hand side denotes the reduced ind-scheme associated with
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
.
Moreover, for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{+}
the closure
\overline{{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}^{\lambda}}
is a projective
\mathbb{F}
-scheme, on which the action of
L^{+}G
factors through an action of a smooth quotient group scheme of finite type.

The orbits of
\mathrm{Iw}
and
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}
can be described similarly: we have

(4.5)

({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})_{\mathrm{red}}=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}}{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}\quad\text{with ${\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}:=\mathrm{Iw}\cdot L_{\lambda}$}

and

(4.6)

({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})_{\mathrm{red}}=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}}{\mathcal{G}r}^{+}_{G,\lambda}\quad\text{with ${\mathcal{G}r}^{+}_{G,\lambda}:=\mathrm{Iw}^{+}\cdot L_{\lambda}$.}

Moreover, each
\mathrm{Iw}
-orbit (resp.
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}
-orbit) is also an
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u}}
-orbit (resp.
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u}}^{+}
-orbit), and for any
\mu\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{+}
we have

{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}^{\mu}=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in W_{\mathrm{f}}\mu}{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in W_{\mathrm{f}}\mu}{\mathcal{G}r}^{+}_{G,\lambda}.

For
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
, the embedding of
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+}
in
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
will be denoted
j_{\lambda}^{+}
.

If
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we can also consider the
Iwahori subgroups
\mathrm{Iw}_{n},\mathrm{Iw}_{n}^{+}\subset L_{n}G
defined as above with
z
replaced by
z^{n}
, and their pro-unipotent radicals
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u},n},\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u},n}^{+}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

We have

\mathrm{Iw}^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}=\mathrm{Iw}_{n},\quad(\mathrm{Iw}^{+})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}=\mathrm{Iw}_{n}^{+},\quad(\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u}})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}=\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u},n},\quad(\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u}}^{+})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}=\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u},n}^{+}.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
we have

({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}=\mathrm{Iw}_{n}\cdot L_{\lambda},\quad({\mathcal{G}r}^{+}_{G,\lambda})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}=\mathrm{Iw}^{+}_{n}\cdot L_{\lambda}.

Proof.

The identifications in the first sentence are immediate consequences of Lemma
4.2
.

For the description of
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}
, for any
\alpha\in\mathfrak{R}
we set
\delta_{\alpha}=1
if
\alpha\in\mathfrak{R}^{+}
, and
\delta_{\alpha}=0
otherwise.
Using the notation introduced in §
4.3
, we set

\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u}}^{\lambda}:=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{R}}\left(\prod_{\delta_{\alpha}\leq m<\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle}U_{\alpha+m\hbar}\right),

where the products are taken in any chosen order.
Then it is well known that the map
u\mapsto u\cdot L_{\lambda}
induces an isomorphism

\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u}}^{\lambda}\xrightarrow{\sim}{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}
. Since
L_{\lambda}
is stable under the
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}
-action
we deduce an isomorphism
(\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u}}^{\lambda})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}\xrightarrow{\sim}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}
, and here by Lemma
4.3
(
1
) we have

(\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u}}^{\lambda})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}=\prod_{\alpha\in\mathfrak{R}}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\delta_{\alpha}\leq m<\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle\\
n\mid m\end{subarray}}U_{\alpha+m\hbar}\right).

It follows that
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}=\mathrm{Iw}_{n}\cdot L_{\lambda}
, as desired.
The proof that
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})^{{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{n}}=\mathrm{Iw}_{n}^{+}\cdot L_{\lambda}
is similar.
∎

4.5.
Big cells in partial affine flag varieties

Our arguments below will make use of the “big cell” in
{\mathcal{F}l}^{n}_{\mathbf{f}}
, whose construction we now recall following de Cataldo–Haines–Li
[
dCHL
]
. We first consider the ind-affine group ind-scheme
L^{(-1)}_{n}G
which represents the functor sending
R
to the kernel of the morphism

G(R[z^{-n}])\to G(R)

of evaluation at
z^{-n}=0
. Then
L^{(-1)}_{n}G
is a subgroup ind-scheme of
L_{n}G
, and we set

L^{--}_{n}P_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}=L^{(-1)}_{n}G\cdot U^{+}.

With this definition, it is well known (see e.g.
[
Fa
, §2]
) that the morphism

L^{--}_{n}P_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}\to{\mathcal{F}l}^{n,\circ}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}

induced by the action on the base point is representable by an open immersion,
and that from this one can obtain an “open cover” of
{\mathcal{F}l}^{n,\circ}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}
parametrized by
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
, where the open subset corresponding to
w
is the image of

\iota_{n}(w)\cdot L^{--}_{n}P_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}\cdot\iota_{n}(w)^{-1}

under the map
g\mapsto g\cdot[\iota_{n}(w)]
. (Here,
[\iota_{n}(w)]
is the image of
\iota_{n}(w)
in
{\mathcal{F}l}^{n,\circ}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}
.)

For a general facet
\mathbf{f}\subset\overline{\mathbf{a}}_{n}
, we denote by
W_{\mathrm{aff}}^{\mathbf{f}}
the pointwise stabilizer of
\mathbf{f}
in
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
(a finite parabolic subgroup) and set

L^{--}_{n}P_{\mathbf{f}}=\bigcap_{w\in W_{\mathrm{aff}}^{\mathbf{f}}}\iota_{n}(w)\cdot L_{n}^{--}P_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}\cdot\iota_{n}(w)^{-1}.

For instance, in the special case
\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{o}_{n}
, we have

(4.7)

L^{--}_{n}P_{\mathbf{o}_{n}}=L^{(-1)}_{n}G.

The following claim is easily checked (see
[
dCHL
, Proposition 3.6.4]
).

Lemma 4.5
.

For
\alpha\in\mathfrak{R}
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
U_{\alpha+nm\hbar}\subset L^{--}_{n}P_{\mathbf{f}}
iff
f^{n}_{\alpha+m\hbar}
takes negative values on
\mathbf{f}
.

With this definition, as explained in
[
dCHL
, §3.8.1]
, the morphism

L^{--}_{n}P_{\mathbf{f}}\to{\mathcal{F}l}^{n,\circ}_{\mathbf{f}}

defined by the action on the base point is representable by an open immersion.
One can obtain from this an “open cover” of
{\mathcal{F}l}^{n,\circ}_{\mathbf{f}}
parametrized by the quotient
W_{\mathrm{aff}}/W_{\mathrm{aff}}^{\mathbf{f}}
, where the open subset attached to a coset
wW_{\mathrm{aff}}^{\mathbf{f}}
is the image of the subgroup

\iota_{n}(w)\cdot L^{--}_{n}P_{\mathbf{f}}\cdot\iota_{n}(w)^{-1}

under the morphism of action on the image of
\iota_{n}(w)
. (These data do not depend on the choice of
w
in its coset, and this claim can be deduced from the corresponding fact for
\mathbf{a}_{n}
by using the morphism
{\mathcal{F}l}^{n,\circ}_{\mathbf{a}_{n}}\to{\mathcal{F}l}^{n,\circ}_{\mathbf{f}}
induced by (
4.3
).)

For
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, we will denote by
L^{(-1)}G(m)
, resp.
L_{n}^{(-1)}G(m)
, the subgroup of
L^{(-1)}G
, resp.
L_{n}^{(-1)}G
, which represents the functor sending

\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
. More precisely, for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) indecomposable object
\mathscr{T}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda}
in
\mathsf{Tilt}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
which is supported on
\overline{{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+}}
, and whose restriction to
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+}
is
\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{AS}}^{\lambda}[\dim({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})]
; then the assignment
\lambda\mapsto\mathscr{T}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda}
induces a bijection between
\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
and the set of isomorphism classes of indecomposable objects in
\mathsf{Tilt}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
.

5.2.
Loop rotation equivariant Iwahori–Whittaker sheaves

We will need to “add” the (loop rotation)
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-equivariance in the constructions of §
5.1
. We therefore consider a locally closed finite union of
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}
-orbits
X\subset{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
as above. The
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action by loop rotation on
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
stabilizes each
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}
-orbit, hence also
X
, so that we can consider the constructible
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-equivariant derived category of étale
\Bbbk
-sheaves
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},c}(X,\Bbbk)
, see §
3.4
. The quotient
J
of
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u}}^{+}
as in §
5.1
can be chosen in such a way that the
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action on
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u}}^{+}
induces an action on
J
. Since the morphism
\chi:\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u}}^{+}\to\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
is
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-equivariant (for the trivial
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action on
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
), so is
\chi_{J}
, and the local system
\chi_{J}^{*}\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{AS}}
is therefore
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-equivariant. We define the category

D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X,\Bbbk)

as the full subcategory of
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},c}(X,\Bbbk)
whose objects are the complexes
\mathscr{F}
such that

a_{J}^{*}\mathscr{F}\cong\chi_{J}^{*}\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{AS}}\boxtimes\mathscr{F}\quad\text{in }D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(J\times X,\Bbbk),

where
a_{J}:J\times X\to X
is the action morphism. (Here,
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
acts diagonally on
J\times X
.) Arguments similar to those for the case when the
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action is dropped
show that
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X,\Bbbk)
is a triangulated subcategory of
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},c}(X,\Bbbk)
; in fact this category is the essential image of the fully faithful functor

D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},c}(X,\Bbbk)\to D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X,\Bbbk)

sending a complex
\mathscr{F}
to
R(a_{J})_{!}\bigl(\chi_{J}^{*}\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{AS}}\boxtimes\mathscr{F}\bigr)
.
It is also easily checked that this category does not depend on the choice of
J
, and that the perverse t-structure on
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},c}(X,\Bbbk)
restricts to a t-structure on
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X,\Bbbk)
.

Taking the direct limit of the categories
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X,\Bbbk)
where
X
runs over the closed finite unions of
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}
-orbits, we obtain a triangulated category

D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)

with a natural perverse t-structure, whose heart will be denoted

\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
.
We have a natural
t-exact
forgetful functor

(5.1)

D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)\to D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk).

5.3.
Parity complexes

Let
X\subset{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
be a locally closed finite union of
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}
-orbits. Recall (see
[
JMW
,
RW1
]
) that an object
\mathscr{F}
in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}}(X,\Bbbk)
is called
*
-even, resp.
!
-even, if for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}_{++}^{\vee}
such that
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+}\subset X
the complex
(j_{\lambda}^{+})^{*}\mathscr{F}
, resp.
(j_{\lambda}^{+})^{!}\mathscr{F}
, is concentrated in even degrees, i.e. is a direct sum of objects of the form
\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{AS}}^{\lambda}[n]
with
n\in 2\mathbb{Z}
. (Here, by abuse we still denote by
j_{\lambda}^{+}
the embedding of
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+}
in
X
. Note also that if
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}\smallsetminus\mathbf{X}_{++}^{\vee}
is such that
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+}\subset X
, then as explained in §
5.1
we have
(j_{\lambda}^{+})^{*}\mathscr{F}=(j_{\lambda}^{+})^{!}\mathscr{F}=0
for any
\mathscr{F}
in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}}(X,\Bbbk)
, so that no condition is required for these strata.) We define similarly the
*
-odd and
!
-odd objects (requiring that
n
is odd in this case), and we say that
\mathscr{F}
is even, resp. odd, if it is both
*
-even and
!
-even, resp.
*
-odd and
!
-odd.

These notions can also be considered in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X,\Bbbk)
; more precisely an object
\mathscr{F}
in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X,\Bbbk)
is said to be
*
-even, resp.
!
-even, etc., if its image in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}}(X,\Bbbk)
(under the forgetful functor) is
*
-even, resp.
!
-even, etc. If
\mathscr{F}\in D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X,\Bbbk)
is
*
-even, for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}_{++}^{\vee}
such that
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+}\subset X
the complex
(j_{\lambda}^{+})^{*}\mathscr{F}
is a direct sum of objects of the form
\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{AS}}^{\lambda}[n]
with
n\in 2\mathbb{Z}
in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+},\Bbbk)
. A similar comment applies to
!
-even objects (with respect to
!
-restriction), and to
*
-odd and
!
-odd objects.

By definition, the category
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
, resp.
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
, is the direct limit of the categories
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}}(X,\Bbbk)
, resp.
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X,\Bbbk)
, where
X
runs over the closed finite unions of
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}
-orbits in
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
. Hence it makes sense to consider even and odd complexes in these categories. The general theory of
[
JMW
]
(see also
[
RW1
,
ACR
]
for some comments on the Iwahori–Whittaker case) guarantees that for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
there exists a unique (up to isomorphism) indecomposable object in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
, resp.
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
, which has the same parity as
\dim({\mathcal{G}r}^{+}_{G,\lambda})
, which is supported on
\overline{{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+}}
, and whose restriction to
{\mathcal{G}r}^{+}_{G,\lambda}
is
\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{AS}}^{\lambda}[\dim({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})]
. This object will be denoted

\mathscr{E}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda},\quad\text{resp.}\quad\mathscr{E}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda,\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}.

It is known also that the image of
\mathscr{E}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda,\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
under the forgetful functor (
5.1
) is
\mathscr{E}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda}
, see e.g.
[
MR
, Lemma 2.4]
. Moreover, any parity object in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
, resp.
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
, is a direct sum of cohomological shifts of objects of the form
\mathscr{E}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda}
, resp.
\mathscr{E}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda,\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
.

As remarked already in
[
BGMRR
]
, these objects have an alternative description, as follows.
It is known that the parity of
\dim({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})
(with
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
) is constant on each connected component of
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
. (This follows from the same property for
L^{+}G
-orbits, since each orbit
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+}
with
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
is dense in an
L^{+}G
-orbit.) As a consequence, a tilting object supported on a component where these dimensions are even, resp. odd, is even, resp. odd. In particular, by unicity, for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
we must have

(5.2)

\mathscr{T}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda}\cong\mathscr{E}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda}.

As a consequence, we obtain that the parity objects in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
are exactly the direct sums of cohomological shifts of tilting perverse sheaves.

Using these considerations we prove the following lemma, to be used later.

Lemma 5.2
.

The forgetful functor induces an equivalence of categories

\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk).

Proof.

It follows from the general theory of equivariant perverse sheaves
(recalled e.g. in
[
BR
, §1.16]
) that the forgetful functor

\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Perv}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
is fully faithful;
therefore, so is its restriction
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
. This general theory also implies that the
essential image of this functor is stable under subquotients (see
e.g.
[
J3
, §12.19]
).
Now from (
5.2
) and the fact that each object
\mathscr{E}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda}
belongs to the essential image of the forgetful functor
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)\to D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
(see the remarks above), we see that the essential image of our functor contains all the tilting objects. By the general theory of highest weight categories (see
[
Ri
, Proposition 7.17]
), the canonical functor provides an equivalence of triangulated categories

(5.3)

K^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Tilt}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)\xrightarrow{\sim}D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk).

In particular, it follows that
any object of
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
is a subquotient of a tilting object, hence that it belongs to this essential image, which finishes the proof.
∎

6.
Smith theory for Iwahori–Whittaker sheaves on
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}

We continue with the setting of Sections
4
–
5
.
Our goal in this section is to build a “Smith theory” for the category
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
, following Treumann
[
Tr
]
and Leslie–Lonergan
[
LL
]
.

6.1.
The Iwahori–Whittaker Smith category

As in Section
3
we consider the subgroup scheme
\varpi={\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{\ell}\subset\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
, and the fixed points
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}\subset{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
with respect to the loop rotation action. This subscheme is described in §
4.6
; in particular since
(\mathrm{Iw}^{+})^{\varpi}=\mathrm{Iw}_{\ell}^{+}
(see Lemma
4.4
), this group acts on
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}
, and each
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}_{\ell}
-orbit is also an
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u},\ell}^{+}
-orbit.

The
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action on
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
stabilizes
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}
, hence induces an action on this sub-ind-scheme. On the other hand, as explained above we also have an action of
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u},\ell}^{+}
on
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}
. The analysis in §
4.4
shows that the orbits of the latter action are naturally parametrized by
\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
, and that each orbit is stable under the action of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
. Repeating the construction in §
5.2
, now with the morphism
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u},\ell}^{+}\to\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{a}}
obtained by restricting
\chi
one can define for any locally closed finite union of
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}_{\ell}
-orbits
Y\subset({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}
the Iwahori–Whittaker loop rotation equivariant derived category

D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Y,\Bbbk).

As in §
5.1
, in case
Y=({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})^{\varpi}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
, this category vanishes unless
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
.

We define
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Y,\Bbbk)_{\varpi\mathchar 45\relax\mathsf{perf}}
as the full subcategory of
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Y,\Bbbk)
whose objects are the complexes
\mathscr{F}
such that
\Res^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}_{\varpi}(\mathscr{F})
has perfect geometric stalks in the sense of §
3.3
.
We then define
the
Iwahori–Whittaker Smith category
of
Y
as the Verdier quotient

\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk):=D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Y,\Bbbk)/D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Y,\Bbbk)_{\varpi\mathchar 45\relax\mathsf{perf}}.

This category has a natural structure of triangulated category; the (cohomological) shift functor will be denoted
[1]
as usual.

We now check that this construction is functorial in the following sense.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
Y,Z\subset({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}
be two locally closed finite unions of
\mathrm{Iw}_{\ell}^{+}
-orbits such that
Z\subset Y
. Denoting by
f
this inclusion, for
?\in\{*,!\}
there exist canonical functors

f_{?}^{\mathsf{Sm}}:\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Z,\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk),\quad f_{\mathsf{Sm}}^{?}:\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Z,\Bbbk)

such that the diagrams

\textstyle{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Z,\Bbbk)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{Rf_{?}}
\textstyle{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Y,\Bbbk)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Z,\Bbbk)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f_{?}^{\mathsf{Sm}}}
\textstyle{\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)}

and

\textstyle{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Y,\Bbbk)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{?}}
\textstyle{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Z,\Bbbk)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f^{?}_{\mathsf{Sm}}}
\textstyle{\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Z,\Bbbk)}

are commutative, where the vertical arrows are the quotient functors.

Proof.

By the universal property of Verdier quotients,
we need to show that the functors
Rf_{*}
,
Rf_{!}
, resp.
f^{*}
,
f^{!}
, send
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Z,\Bbbk)_{\varpi\mathchar 45\relax\mathsf{perf}}
into
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Y,\Bbbk)_{\varpi\mathchar 45\relax\mathsf{perf}}
, resp.
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Y,\Bbbk)_{\varpi\mathchar 45\relax\mathsf{perf}}
into
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Z,\Bbbk)_{\varpi\mathchar 45\relax\mathsf{perf}}
. For the functor
f^{*}
this claim is obvious from definition, and for
Rf_{*}
it follows from Lemma
3.6
. For the functor
Rf_{!}
, one can argue as follows. If
\overline{y}:\Spec(K)\to Y
is a geometric point of
Y
, then by
[
SGA4
, Exposé XVII, Proposition 5.2.8]
we have

(Rf_{!}\mathscr{F})_{\overline{y}}\cong R\Gamma_{c}(Z\times_{Y}\Spec(K),\mathscr{F}^{\prime}),

where
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
is the pullback of
\mathscr{F}
. Now
Z\times_{Y}\Spec(K)
is a locally closed subscheme of
\Spec(K)
, hence is either
\varnothing
or
\Spec(K)
. Hence
(Rf_{!}\mathscr{F})_{\overline{y}}
is either equal to
\mathscr{F}_{\overline{y}}
or to
0
, which shows that
Rf_{!}\mathscr{F}
must belong to
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Y,\Bbbk)_{\varpi\mathchar 45\relax\mathsf{perf}}
.

Finally we treat the case of
f^{!}
. For this we can assume that
f
is either a closed embedding or an open embedding. In the latter case we have
f^{!}=f^{*}
, hence the claim is known. In the former case, we denote by
g
the complementary open embedding. Then, given
\mathscr{F}
in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Y,\Bbbk)_{\varpi\mathchar 45\relax\mathsf{perf}}
we consider the distinguished triangle

f_{*}f^{!}\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{F}\to Rg_{*}g^{*}\mathscr{F}\xrightarrow{[1]}.

Here
\mathscr{F}
and
Rg_{*}g^{*}\mathscr{F}
belong to
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Y,\Bbbk)_{\varpi\mathchar 45\relax\mathsf{perf}}
, hence so does
f_{*}f^{!}\mathscr{F}
. This implies that
f^{!}\mathscr{F}
belongs to
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Z,\Bbbk)_{\varpi\mathchar 45\relax\mathsf{perf}}
, which completes the proof.
∎

It is easily seen that
(f^{*}_{\mathsf{Sm}},f^{\mathsf{Sm}}_{*})
and
(f^{\mathsf{Sm}}_{!},f_{\mathsf{Sm}}^{!})
are adjoint pairs of functors. In particular, if
f
is a closed embedding then the functor
f^{\mathsf{Sm}}_{*}=f^{\mathsf{Sm}}_{!}
is fully faithful, so that the category
\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Z,\Bbbk)
can (and will) be identified with a full triangulated subcategory in
\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
. It is also easily checked that, given a decomposition of
Y
as a disjoint union of a closed (in
Y
) finite union of
\mathrm{Iw}_{\ell}^{+}
-orbits and its open complement, we have canonical distinguished triangles as in the “recollement” setting of
[
BBDG
, §1.4]
.

The full faithfulness of pushforward under closed embeddings allows to define the category
\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
as the direct limit of the categories
\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
where
Y
runs over the closed finite unions of
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}_{\ell}
-orbits in
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}
.

6.2.
The Smith localization functor

We will be particularly interested in the construction of §
6.1
in the case
Y=X^{\varpi}
for some
locally closed finite union of
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}
-orbits
X\subset{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
. In this case, we denote by
i_{X}:X^{\varpi}\to X
the embedding. For any
\mathscr{F}
in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X,\Bbbk)
, we have objects
i_{X}^{!}\mathscr{F}
and
i_{X}^{*}\mathscr{F}
in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
, and a canonical morphism

i_{X}^{!}\mathscr{F}\to i_{X}^{*}\mathscr{F},

see (
3.4
).
It follows from Lemma
3.5
that the cone of this morphism is killed by the quotient functor

D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X^{\varpi},\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(X^{\varpi},\Bbbk).

We can therefore define the functor

i_{X}^{!*}:D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(X,\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(X^{\varpi},\Bbbk)

as the composition of either
i_{X}^{*}
or
i_{X}^{!}
with this quotient functor.

This functor is compatible with the push/pull functors associated with locally closed embeddings, in the following sense.

Proposition 6.2
.

If
X,Y\subset{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
are two locally closed finite unions of
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}
-orbits such that
X\subset Y
, and if we denote by
f:X\to Y
the embedding and by
f^{\varpi}:X^{\varpi}\to Y^{\varpi}
its restriction to
X^{\varpi}
, then we have canonical isomorphisms of functors

\displaystyle i_{Y}^{!*}\circ Rf_{*}\cong(f^{\varpi})^{\mathsf{Sm}}_{*}\circ i_{X}^{!*},\qquad i_{Y}^{!*}\circ Rf_{!}\cong(f^{\varpi})^{\mathsf{Sm}}_{!}\circ i_{X}^{!*},

\displaystyle i_{X}^{!*}\circ f^{*}\cong(f^{\varpi})_{\mathsf{Sm}}^{*}\circ i_{Y}^{!*},\qquad i_{X}^{!*}\circ f^{!}\cong(f^{\varpi})_{\mathsf{Sm}}^{!}\circ i_{Y}^{!*}.

Proof.

The first, resp. second, isomorphism on the first line follows from the base change theorem (see
[
SGA4
, Exposé XVIII, Corollaire 3.1.12.3]
and
[
SGA4
, Exposé XVII, Théorème 5.2.6]
respectively) if we see
i_{Y}^{!*}
and
i_{X}^{!*}
as the compositions of
i_{Y}^{!}
and
i_{X}^{!}
, resp. of
i_{Y}^{*}
and
i_{X}^{*}
, with the appropriate quotient functors. The isomorphisms on the second line follow similarly from the compatibility of pullback functors with composition.
∎

Taking the direct limit of the functors
i_{X}^{!*}
for
X
a closed finite union of
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}
-orbits in
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
, we also obtain a functor

i_{{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}}^{!*}:D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk).

6.3.
Some first properties of the Iwahori–Whittaker Smith category

Let us fix some
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
, and consider the
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u},\ell}^{+}
-orbit
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})^{\varpi}\subset({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}
. (Once again, the Iwahori–Whittaker category associated with an orbit labelled by a weight in
\mathbf{X}^{\vee}\smallsetminus\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
vanishes; these coweights can therefore be ignored.) We set

\mathscr{L}_{\mathsf{Sm}}^{\lambda}:=i^{!*}_{{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+}}(\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{AS}}^{\lambda}).

Lemma 6.3
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\Hom_{\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{L}_{\mathsf{Sm}}^{\lambda},\mathscr{L}_{\mathsf{Sm}}^{\lambda}[n])=\begin{cases}\Bbbk&\text{if $n$ is even;}\\
0&\text{if $n$ is odd.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u},\ell}^{+}
acts transitively on
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})^{\varpi}
(see Lemma
4.4
), we have an equivalence of triangulated categories

D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)\cong D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},c}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)

which matches
\mathscr{L}_{\mathrm{AS}}^{\lambda}
with
\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathrm{pt}}
. This equivalence induces an equivalence

\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)\cong\mathsf{Sm}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk),

where the right-hand side is defined in §
3.5
. The claim then follows from Lemma
3.7
.
∎

We consider once again a general locally closed finite union of
\mathrm{Iw}_{\ell}^{+}
-orbits
Y\subset({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}
.

Lemma 6.4
.

There exists a canonical isomorphism of endofunctors of
\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)

\mathrm{id}\xrightarrow{\sim}[2].

Proof.

As explained in Lemma
3.7
, there exists a canonical map
\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathrm{pt}}\to\underline{\Bbbk}_{\mathrm{pt}}[2]
in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},c}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)
whose cone has perfect geometric stalks. Pulling back to
Y
we deduce a canonical morphism
\underline{\Bbbk}_{Y}\to\underline{\Bbbk}_{Y}[2]
whose cone has perfect geometric stalks. Since the tensor product with
\underline{\Bbbk}_{Y}
, resp.
\underline{\Bbbk}_{Y}[2]
, defines an endofunctor of
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(Y,\Bbbk)
which is isomorphic to
\mathrm{id}
, resp. to
[2]
, and since the tensor product (over
\Bbbk
) of a perfect complex of
\Bbbk[\varpi]
-modules with any bounded complex is perfect, the desired claim follows.
∎

Proposition 6.5
.

For any
\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G}
in
\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
, the
\Bbbk
-vector space

\Hom_{\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G})

is finite-dimensional.

Proof.

The proof proceeds by induction on the number of
\mathrm{Iw}_{\ell}^{+}
-orbits in
Y
. In fact the distinguished triangles from the “recollement” setting (see §
6.1
) reduce the proof to the case
Y
consists of one orbit, which follows from Lemma
6.3
.
∎

7.
Parity objects in Smith categories

We continue with the setting of Sections
4
–
6
.

7.1.
Definition

As remarked already in
[
LL
]
(using slightly different definitions), the theory of parity complexes from
[
JMW
]
adapts easily to the Smith category
\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
, where
Y\subset({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}
is any locally closed union of
\mathrm{Iw}_{\ell}^{+}
-orbits. Namely, we will say
that an object
\mathscr{F}
in
\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
is
*
-
even
, resp.
!
-
even
, if for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}_{++}^{\vee}
such that
({\mathcal{G}r}^{+}_{G,\lambda})^{\varpi}\subset Y
, denoting by
j_{\lambda}^{+,\varpi}:({\mathcal{G}r}^{+}_{G,\lambda})^{\varpi}\to Y
the embedding, the object
(j^{+,\varpi}_{\lambda})^{*}_{\mathsf{Sm}}\mathscr{F}
, resp.
(j^{+,\varpi}_{\lambda})^{!}_{\mathsf{Sm}}\mathscr{F}
, is isomorphic to a direct sum of copies of
\mathscr{L}_{\mathsf{Sm}}^{\lambda}
. (In this case we do not need to consider even shifts because of Lemma
6.4
.) We will then say that
\mathscr{F}
is
even
if it is both
*
-even and
!
-even, and define similarly the notions of
*
-odd,
!
-odd, and odd objects (replacing
\mathscr{L}_{\mathsf{Sm}}^{\lambda}
by its shift by
1
). We will denote by
\mathsf{Sm}^{0}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
, resp.
\mathsf{Sm}^{1}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
, resp.
\mathsf{Sm}^{\mathrm{par}}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
, the full subcategory of
\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
whose objects are the even objects, resp. the odd objects, resp. the objects which are isomorphic to a direct sum of an even and an odd object.

Recall that an additive category is called
Krull–Schmidt
if any object can be written as a direct sum of indecomposable objects whose endomorphism rings are local. (If this property holds, then such a decomposition is unique up to isomorphism and permutation of factors.)

Lemma 7.1
.

The categories
\mathsf{Sm}^{0}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
,
\mathsf{Sm}^{1}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
and
\mathsf{Sm}^{\mathrm{par}}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
are Krull–Schmidt.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.5
and
[
CYZ
, Corollary A.2]
, to prove the lemma it suffices to prove that any idempotent in the category
\mathsf{Sm}^{\mathrm{par}}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
splits. We do this by induction on the number of
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}_{\ell}
-orbits in
Y
. If
Y=({\mathcal{G}r}^{+}_{G,\lambda})^{\varpi}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
, and if
\mathscr{F}
belongs to
\mathsf{Sm}^{\mathrm{par}}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
then either
\mathscr{F}=0
(in which case there is nothing to prove) or
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
and
\mathscr{F}=(\mathscr{L}_{\mathsf{Sm}}^{\lambda})^{\oplus n}\oplus(\mathscr{L}_{\mathsf{Sm}}^{\lambda})^{\oplus m}[1]
for some
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. In this case, by Lemma
6.3
we have

\mathrm{End}_{\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{F})\cong\mathrm{M}_{n}(\Bbbk)\times\mathrm{M}_{m}(\Bbbk),

so that any idempotent in
\mathrm{End}_{\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{F})
indeed splits.

To treat the induction step, we choose a closed
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}_{\ell}
-orbit
Z\subset Y
, and denote by

i:Z\hookrightarrow Y,\qquad j:Y\smallsetminus Z\hookrightarrow Y

the embeddings. For any
\mathscr{F}
in
\mathsf{Sm}^{\mathrm{par}}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
we then have a distinguished triangle

i^{\mathsf{Sm}}_{!}i^{!}_{\mathsf{Sm}}\mathscr{F}\to\mathscr{F}\to j_{*}^{\mathsf{Sm}}j^{*}_{\mathsf{Sm}}\mathscr{F}\xrightarrow{[1]},

and the objects
i^{!}_{\mathsf{Sm}}\mathscr{F}
and
j^{*}_{\mathsf{Sm}}\mathscr{F}
belong to
\mathsf{Sm}^{\mathrm{par}}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Z,\Bbbk)
and to
\mathsf{Sm}^{\mathrm{par}}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y\smallsetminus Z,\Bbbk)
respectively. If
e\in\mathrm{End}_{\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{F})
is an idempotent, then
i^{!}_{\mathsf{Sm}}(e)
and
j^{*}_{\mathsf{Sm}}(e)
are idempotents too, hence they split by the induction hypothesis. By
[
LC
, Proposition 2.3]
, this implies that
e
splits.
∎

We will also define the categories

\mathsf{Sm}^{0}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk),\quad\mathsf{Sm}^{1}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{Sm}^{\mathrm{par}}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)

as the direct limits of their counterparts for
Y
, where
Y
runs over closed finite unions of
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}_{\ell}
-orbits in
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}
. (Equivalently, these categories can be defined in terms of restrictions and corestrictions to
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}_{\ell}
-orbits, as for their counterparts above.) Of course, Lemma
7.1
implies that these categories
are Krull–Schmidt.

7.2.
Basic properties

The study of parity objects in
\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
is very similar to its counterpart in ordinary derived categories of sheaves performed in
[
JMW
]
; its essential ingredients are the parity vanishing property for one stratum proved in Lemma
6.3
, and standard distinguished triangles associated with a decomposition of a space into a closed part and its open complement. For this reason we will not give any proof in this subsection; these can be obtained by repeating the proofs of
[
JMW
]
essentially word-for-word.

The following is the analogue of
[
JMW
, Corollary 2.8 and Proposition 2.11]
.

Lemma 7.2
.

If
\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G}\in\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
are such that
\mathscr{F}
is
*
-even and
\mathscr{G}
is
!
-odd, then we have

\Hom_{\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G})=0.

As a consequence, if
Z\subset Y
is an open union of
\mathrm{Iw}^{+}_{\ell}
-orbits, the restriction of an indecomposable even (resp. odd) object of
\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
to
Z
is either indecomposable or zero.

Next, we define the
support
of an object
\mathscr{F}\in\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
as the closure of the union of the strata
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})^{\varpi}
where
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
is such that
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})^{\varpi}\subset Y
and
(j_{\lambda}^{+,\varpi})^{*}_{\mathsf{Sm}}\mathscr{F}
or
(j_{\lambda}^{+,\varpi})^{!}_{\mathsf{Sm}}\mathscr{F}
is nonzero. The following claim is the analogue of
[
JMW
, Theorem 2.12]
.

Proposition 7.3
.

If
\mathscr{F}\in\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
is even (resp. odd), nonzero, and indecomposable, then there exists exactly one
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
such that
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})^{\varpi}
is open in the support of
\mathscr{F}
.

Moreover, for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
such that
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})^{\varpi}\subset Y
, there exists at most one indecomposable even, resp. odd, object
\mathscr{F}
in
\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(Y,\Bbbk)
such that
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})^{\varpi}
is open in the support of
\mathscr{F}
and
(j^{+,\varpi}_{\lambda})^{*}\mathscr{F}\cong\mathscr{L}_{\mathsf{Sm}}^{\lambda}
, resp.
(j^{+,\varpi}_{\lambda})^{*}\mathscr{F}\cong\mathscr{L}_{\mathsf{Sm}}^{\lambda}[1]
.

7.3.
Comparison of parity objects in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
and
\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)

Proposition
7.3
implies that for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
there exists at most one indecomposable even, resp. odd, object in
\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
in the support of which
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})^{\varpi}
is open, and whose restriction to
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})^{\varpi}
is
\mathscr{L}_{\mathsf{Sm}}^{\lambda}
, resp.
\mathscr{L}_{\mathsf{Sm}}^{\lambda}[1]
. If it exists (which, as we shall see very soon, is always the case), this object will be denoted
\mathscr{E}^{\mathsf{Sm},0}_{\lambda}
, resp.
\mathscr{E}^{\mathsf{Sm},1}_{\lambda}
. (Of course, as soon as one of these objects exists the other one exists also, and we have
\mathscr{E}^{\mathsf{Sm},1}_{\lambda}\cong\mathscr{E}^{\mathsf{Sm},0}_{\lambda}[1]
.) With this notation, any indecomposable object in
\mathsf{Sm}^{\mathrm{par}}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
is isomorphic to an object
\mathscr{E}^{\mathsf{Sm},0}_{\lambda}
or
\mathscr{E}^{\mathsf{Sm},1}_{\lambda}
, and
Lemma
7.1
implies that any object of
\mathsf{Sm}^{\mathrm{par}}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
is a direct sum of such objects (in an essentially unique way).

Recall that the connected components of
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}
are the subvarieties
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,(\lambda)}
with
\lambda\in(-\overline{\mathbf{a}}_{\ell})\cap\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
, see (
4.14
).
Of course, each such connected component is contained in a connected component of
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
. Recall also (see §
5.3
) that the dimensions of the orbits
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\mu}^{+}
with
\mu\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
contained in a given connected component of
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
are of constant parity. We set
\mathsf{p}(\lambda)=0
, resp.
\mathsf{p}(\lambda)=1
, if all these orbits
contained in the connected component containing
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,(\lambda)}
are even-dimensional, resp. odd-dimensional. We then denote by
\mathsf{Sm}^{\natural}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
the full subcategory of
\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
whose objects are those whose restriction to
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,(\lambda)}
is even if
\mathsf{p}(\lambda)=0
, and odd if
\mathsf{p}(\lambda)=1
.

The following statement is
the crux of this paper.
(This statement is equivalent to Theorem
1.2
in the introduction since perverse parity objects in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
are the same as images of tilting perverse sheaves under the equivalence of Lemma
5.2
; see §
5.3
.)

Theorem 7.4
.

The composition

\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)\xrightarrow[\sim]{\text{Lemma~\ref{lem:IW-Gm-Perv}}}\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)\xrightarrow{i^{!*}_{{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}}}\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)

restricts to an equivalence of categories

\mathsf{Tilt}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Sm}^{\natural}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk).

Moreover, the objects
\mathscr{E}^{\mathsf{Sm},0}_{\lambda}
and
\mathscr{E}^{\mathsf{Sm},1}_{\lambda}
exist for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
.

Proof.

It easily follows from Proposition
6.2
and the considerations above that the functor
i^{!*}_{{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}}
sends even, resp. odd, objects to even, resp. odd, objects. Therefore, since any indecomposable object in
\mathsf{Tilt}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
is either even or odd (see (
5.2
)), our functor restricts to a functor

\mathsf{Tilt}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Sm}^{\natural}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk).

Next, standard arguments (based in particular on Lemma
7.2
) allow to prove by induction on the length of the filtrations that, for any
\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G}
in
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
such that
\mathscr{F}
admits a standard filtration and
\mathscr{G}
admits a costandard filtration, this functor induces an isomorphism

\Hom_{\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{G})\xrightarrow{\sim}\Hom_{\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(i^{!*}_{{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}}(\mathscr{F}),i^{!*}_{{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}}(\mathscr{G})).

(Here, the crucial case when
\mathscr{F}=\Delta^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda}
and
\mathscr{G}=\nabla^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda}
for some
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
is given by Lemma
6.3
.) Full faithfulness of our functor follows.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
, the object
i_{{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}}^{!*}(\mathscr{E}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda,\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}})
is indecomposable (by full faithfulness) and either even or odd. Moreover, since
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+}
is open in the support of
\mathscr{E}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda,\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
we see that
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})^{\varpi}
is open in the support of
i_{{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}}^{!*}(\mathscr{E}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda,\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}})
. Therefore the objects
\mathscr{E}^{\mathsf{Sm},0}_{\lambda}
and
\mathscr{E}^{\mathsf{Sm},1}_{\lambda}
exist, and we have

(7.1)

i_{{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}}^{!*}(\mathscr{E}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda,\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}})\cong\begin{cases}\mathscr{E}^{\mathsf{Sm},0}_{\lambda}&\text{if $\dim({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})$ is even;}\\
\mathscr{E}^{\mathsf{Sm},1}_{\lambda}&\text{if $\dim({\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\lambda}^{+})$ is odd.}\end{cases}

These considerations show that our functor is essentially surjective, hence an equivalence of categories.
∎

7.4.
Comparison of parity objects on
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}
and in the Smith category

Now we consider the Iwahori–Whittaker categories

D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)\quad\text{and}\quad D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk),

and the quotient functor

\mathsf{Q}:D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk).

The theory of parity complexes (as in §
5.3
) of course also applies in the categories
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
and
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
;
once again the indecomposable parity objects in these categories are classified (up to cohomological shift) by the
\mathrm{Iw}_{\ell}^{+}
-orbits in
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}
which support a nonzero Iwahori–Whittaker local system (i.e. by
\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
), and the forgetful functor

\mathsf{For}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}:D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)\to D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)

sends indecomposable parity complexes to indecomposable parity complexes. In particular, this functor induces a bijection between the sets of isomorphism classes of indecomposable parity objects in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
and in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
; up to replacing the category of parity objects in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
by an equivalent category (which we will omit from notation), one can therefore consider whenever convenient that the objects in these categories are the same.

The situation in this setting is even more favorable than in that of §
5.3
, due to the following property. (Here, if
\mathsf{D}
is a triangulated category, we write
\Hom^{\bullet}_{\mathsf{D}}(-,-)
for
\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Hom_{\mathsf{D}}(-,-[n])
.)

Lemma 7.5
.

For any parity complexes
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
, there exists a canonical isomorphism of graded
\Bbbk
-vector spaces

\Hom^{\bullet}_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{E}^{\prime})\\
\cong\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathrm{pt};\Bbbk)\otimes_{\Bbbk}\Hom^{\bullet}_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(\mathsf{For}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathscr{E}),\mathsf{For}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathscr{E}^{\prime})).

Moreover, these isomorphisms are compatible with composition in the obvious way.

Remark 7.6
.

The moral of this lemma is that the
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action on

({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}
“looks like the trivial action” as it factors through the map
t\mapsto t^{\ell}
and our
coefficients are of characteristic
\ell
.

Proof.

By definition, the
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action on
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}
factors through the quotient

\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\to\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}/\varpi=\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}},\quad t\mapsto t^{\ell}.

In other words, if we denote by
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}
another copy of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
, then there exists an action of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}
on
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}
from which the
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action we want to consider is deduced via the morphism
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\to\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}
defined by
t\mapsto t^{\ell}
. The
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}
-action on
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u},\ell}^{+}
is similarly obtained from an action of
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}
, so that one can consider the category
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
defined in the obvious way. With this notation introduced, the same considerations as in
[
MR
, Lemma 2.2]
show that for any parity complexes
\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
, the restriction functor

(7.2)

\Res^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}:D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)\to D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)

induces an isomorphism of graded
\Bbbk
-vector spaces

\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathrm{pt};\Bbbk)\otimes_{\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{pt};\Bbbk)}\Hom^{\bullet}_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}^{\prime})\\
\xrightarrow{\sim}\Hom^{\bullet}_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(\Res^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathscr{F}),\Res^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathscr{F}^{\prime})).

Now since
\Bbbk
has characteristic
\ell
, the morphism
\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{pt};\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathrm{pt};\Bbbk)
induced by our morphism
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\to\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}
vanishes in positive degrees, so that we have

\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathrm{pt};\Bbbk)\otimes_{\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{pt};\Bbbk)}\Hom^{\bullet}_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}^{\prime})\\
\cong\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathrm{pt};\Bbbk)\otimes_{\Bbbk}\left(\Bbbk\otimes_{\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{pt};\Bbbk)}\Hom^{\bullet}_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}^{\prime})\right).

As in
[
MR
, Lemma 2.2]
the forgetful functor
\mathsf{For}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}
induces an isomorphism

\Bbbk\otimes_{\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}(\mathrm{pt};\Bbbk)}\Hom^{\bullet}_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}^{\prime})\\
\xrightarrow{\sim}\Hom^{\bullet}_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(\mathsf{For}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{F}),\mathsf{For}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{F}^{\prime})),

so that we finally obtain a canonical isomorphism

\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathrm{pt};\Bbbk)\otimes_{\Bbbk}\Hom^{\bullet}_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(\mathsf{For}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{F}),\mathsf{For}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}(\mathscr{F}^{\prime}))\\
\xrightarrow{\sim}\Hom^{\bullet}_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(\Res^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathscr{F}),\Res^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathscr{F}^{\prime})).

To conclude it suffices to remark that the functor
\Res^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}
from (
7.2
) induces a (canonical) bijection between the isomorphism classes of parity complexes in the categories
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
and
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
; one can therefore replace
\Res^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathscr{F})
and
\Res^{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathscr{F}^{\prime})
in these isomorphisms by general parity complexes in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
.
∎

It is clear from definitions that the functor
\mathsf{Q}
sends parity complexes to parity complexes.

Proposition 7.7
.

For any complexes
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
which are either both even or both odd, there exists a canonical isomorphism of
\Bbbk
-vector spaces

\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}^{\bullet}(\mathsf{For}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathscr{E}),\mathsf{For}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathscr{E}^{\prime}))\cong\Hom_{\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(\mathsf{Q}(\mathscr{E}),\mathsf{Q}(\mathscr{E}^{\prime})).

Moreover, these isomorphisms are compatible with composition in the obvious way.

Proof.

Recall from
[
JMW
, Corollary 2.8]
that since
\mathscr{E},\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
are either both even or both odd,
the graded
\Bbbk
-vector space
\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}^{\bullet}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{E}^{\prime})

is concentrated in even degrees. Using Lemma
6.4
(and its proof), we see that the functor
\mathsf{Q}
induces a canonical morphism

\Hom^{\bullet}_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{E}^{\prime})\to\Hom_{\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(\mathsf{Q}(\mathscr{E}),\mathsf{Q}(\mathscr{E}^{\prime}))

which factors through a morphism

\Bbbk^{\prime}\otimes_{\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)}\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}^{\bullet}(\mathscr{E},\mathscr{E}^{\prime})\\
\to\Hom_{\mathsf{Sm}_{\mathcal{IW}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(\mathsf{Q}(\mathscr{E}),\mathsf{Q}(\mathscr{E}^{\prime})),

where
\Bbbk^{\prime}
means
\Bbbk
seen as an
\mathsf{H}^{\bullet}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathrm{pt},\Bbbk)
-module where
x
acts by multiplication by
1
under the identification (
3.5
). Standard arguments based on Lemma
6.3
, Lemma
7.2
and the distinguished triangles in the “recollement” setting show that the latter morphism is an isomorphism. The desired isomorphism follows, in view of Lemma
7.5
.
∎

Corollary 7.8
.

The functor
\mathsf{Q}
sends indecomposable parity complexes to indecomposable parity complexes.

Proof.

If
\mathscr{E}
is an indecomposable parity complex in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)
, then as explained above the complex
\mathsf{For}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathscr{E})
is indecomposable, so that the ring

\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(\mathsf{For}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathscr{E}),\mathsf{For}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathscr{E}))

is local. Since a finite-dimensional graded ring whose degree-
0
part is local is itself local (see
[
GG
, Theorem 3.1]
), it follows that the ring

\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell}}(({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi},\Bbbk)}(\mathsf{For}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathscr{E}),\mathsf{For}_{\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}(\mathscr{E})[n])

is also local. In view of Proposition
7.7
, this implies that
\mathsf{Q}(\mathscr{E})
is indecomposable.
∎

8.
Applications in representation theory of reductive algebraic groups

In this section, we finally use the constructions of Sections
4
–
7
to derive consequences on categories of representations of split connected reductive algebraic groups over
\Bbbk
.

8.1.
The geometric Satake equivalence and its Iwahori–Whittaker variant

The
Satake category
is the category

\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathrm{sph}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)

of étale
L^{+}G
-equivariant perverse sheaves on
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
. (By
definition, this category is the inductive limit of the categories

\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathrm{sph}}(X,\Bbbk)
where
X
runs over the closed finite unions of

L^{+}G
-orbits in
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
. Given such an
X
, the category
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathrm{sph}}(X,\Bbbk)
is defined as
\mathsf{Perv}_{H}(X,\Bbbk)
, where
H
is a smooth quotient of
L^{+}G
of finite type such that the
L^{+}G
-action on
X
factors through
H
, and such that the kernel of the surjection
L^{+}G\to H
is contained in
\ker(\mathrm{ev}_{0})
; the resulting category does not depend on the choice of
H
up to canonical equivalence.)
The natural convolution product
\star
on the equivariant derived category
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{L^{+}G}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
restricts to an exact monoidal product on the category
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathrm{sph}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
, see
[
MV1
]
.

The classification of the simple objects in
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathrm{sph}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
is given by the general theory of perverse sheaves from
[
BBDG
]
. Namely, in view of the description of the
L^{+}G
-orbits on
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
(see (
4.4
)) and since each of these orbits is simply connected, for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{+}
there exists a unique simple perverse sheaf
\mathscr{I}\hskip-1.0pt\mathscr{C}^{\lambda}
in
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathrm{sph}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
which is supported on
\overline{{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}^{\lambda}}
and whose restriction to
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}^{\lambda}
is
\underline{\Bbbk}_{{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}^{\lambda}}[\dim({\mathcal{G}r}_{G}^{\lambda})]
. Moreover, the assignment
\lambda\mapsto\mathscr{I}\hskip-1.0pt\mathscr{C}^{\lambda}
induces a bijection between
\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{+}
and the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects in
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathrm{sph}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
.

On the other hand, we denote by
G^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}
the unique split reductive group scheme over
\mathbb{Z}
whose base change to
\mathbb{C}
has root datum
(\mathbf{X}^{\vee},\mathbf{X},\mathfrak{R}^{\vee},\mathfrak{R})
, and then set

G^{\vee}_{\Bbbk}:=\Spec(\Bbbk)\times_{\Spec(\mathbb{Z})}G^{\vee}_{\mathbb{Z}}.

We will denote by
\Rep(G^{\vee}_{\Bbbk})
the category of finite-dimensional algebraic representations of the group scheme
G^{\vee}_{\Bbbk}
.

The following theorem is due (in this generality) to Mirković–Vilonen
[
MV1
,
MV2
]
.

Theorem 8.1
.

There exists an equivalence of monoidal categories

\mathsf{S}:(\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathrm{sph}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk),\star)\cong(\Rep(G^{\vee}_{\Bbbk}),\otimes_{\Bbbk}).

Remark 8.2
.

(1)

In fact, the proof of
[
MV1
]
gives slightly more than what is stated in Theorem
8.1
: the authors construct a
canonical

\Bbbk
-group scheme out of the category
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathrm{sph}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
, and then check that this group scheme is isomorphic to
G^{\vee}_{\Bbbk}
.

(2)

In addition to the category
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathrm{sph}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
, one can also consider the category
\mathsf{Perv}_{(L^{+}G)}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
of
\Bbbk
-perverse sheaves on
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
whose restriction to each
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}^{\lambda}
(
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{+}
) has constant cohomology sheaves. We then have a canonical forgetful functor
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathrm{sph}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Perv}_{(L^{+}G)}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
, which by
[
MV1
, Proposition 2.1]
is an equivalence of categories.

Once an equivalence as in Theorem
8.1
is fixed, the constructions in
[
MV1
]
provide a canonical embedding
T^{\vee}_{\Bbbk}\hookrightarrow G^{\vee}_{\Bbbk}
, where
T^{\vee}_{\Bbbk}
is the split
\Bbbk
-torus which is Langlands dual to
T
(i.e. whose character lattice is
\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
). We will denote by
B^{\vee}_{\Bbbk}
the Borel subgroup of
G^{\vee}_{\Bbbk}
containing (the image of)
T^{\vee}_{\Bbbk}
and whose roots are the negative coroots of
G
. For any
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{+}
we can then consider the “induced representation”

\mathsf{N}(\lambda):=\mathsf{Ind}_{B^{\vee}_{\Bbbk}}^{G^{\vee}_{\Bbbk}}(\lambda).

It is well known that
\mathsf{N}(\lambda)
contains a unique simple submodule, denoted
\mathsf{L}(\lambda)
, and that the assignment
\lambda\mapsto\mathsf{L}(\lambda)
induces a bijection between
\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{+}
and the set of isomorphism classes of simple
G^{\vee}_{\Bbbk}
-modules. It is well known also that for any
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{+}
we have

(8.1)

\mathsf{S}(\mathscr{I}\hskip-1.0pt\mathscr{C}^{\lambda})\cong\mathsf{L}(\lambda).

Below we will use an alternative geometric realization of
\Rep(G^{\vee}_{\Bbbk})
, in terms of the Iwahori–Whittaker derived category of §
5.1
, which was found in
[
BGMRR
]
.
The same construction as for the convolution product on
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{L^{+}G}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
defines a right action of the latter monoidal category on
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
. The corresponding bifunctor will also be denoted
\star
.

We will assume that there exists (and fix) an element
\varsigma\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
such that
\langle\varsigma,\alpha\rangle=1
for any
\alpha\in\mathfrak{R}^{\mathrm{s}}
.
Then there exists no orbit in
\overline{{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\varsigma}^{+}}\smallsetminus{\mathcal{G}r}_{G,\varsigma}^{+}
which supports a nonzero Iwahori–Whittaker local system. Therefore, the canonical map
\Delta^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\varsigma}\to\nabla^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\varsigma}
is an isomorphism, and this object is a simple perverse sheaf.

The following theorem is the main result of
[
BGMRR
]
.

Theorem 8.3
.

The functor sending
\mathscr{F}
to
\Delta^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\varsigma}\star\mathscr{F}
induces an equivalence of abelian categories

\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathrm{sph}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk).

Remark 8.4
.

Theorem
8.3
can also be used to give an alternative proof of Lemma
5.2
.
Namely, we see as in the proof of this lemma that our functor is fully faithful. If
\mathscr{F}
belongs to
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
, by Theorem
8.3
there exists an object
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
in
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathrm{sph}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
and an isomorphism

\mathscr{F}\cong\Delta^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\varsigma}\star\mathscr{F}^{\prime}.

By
[
MV1
, Proposition 2.2]
, the perverse sheaf
\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
is equivariant for the group
\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}\ltimes L^{+}G
. Therefore the perverse sheaf
\Delta^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\varsigma}\star\mathscr{F}^{\prime}
is the image of an object in
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW},\mathbb{G}_{\mathrm{m}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
, and we deduce the same property for
\mathscr{F}
.

8.2.
The linkage principle

We now come back to the setting where
G
is an arbitrary connected reductive algebraic group over
\mathbb{F}
. Recall the actions
\cdot_{\ell}
and
\square_{\ell}
of
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
on
\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
defined in §
4.1
. The action which is relevant in Representation Theory is the “dot action” defined by

(\mathsf{t}_{\lambda}v)\bullet_{\ell}\mu=v(\mu+\rho^{\vee})-\rho^{\vee}+\ell\lambda

for
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{R}^{\vee}
,
v\in W_{\mathrm{f}}
and
\mu\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
, where
\rho^{\vee}
is the halfsum of the positive coroots.
It is clear that for any
w\in W_{\mathrm{aff}}
and
\mu\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
we have

(8.2)

w\bullet_{\ell}\mu=w\square_{\ell}(\mu+\rho^{\vee})-\rho^{\vee}=w^{*}\cdot_{\ell}(\mu+\rho^{\vee})-\rho^{\vee},

where
(\mathsf{t}_{\lambda}v)^{*}:=\mathsf{t}_{-\lambda}v
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}\mathfrak{R}^{\vee}
and
v\in W_{\mathrm{f}}
.

The following statement is the first main result of this section.

Theorem 8.5
.

For
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{+}
, if
\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathrm{sph}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{I}\hskip-1.0pt\mathscr{C}^{\lambda},\mathscr{I}\hskip-1.0pt\mathscr{C}^{\mu})\neq 0
for some
n
, then
W_{\mathrm{aff}}\bullet_{\ell}\lambda=W_{\mathrm{aff}}\bullet_{\ell}\mu
.

Proof.

Note that if
\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathrm{sph}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{I}\hskip-1.0pt\mathscr{C}^{\lambda},\mathscr{I}\hskip-1.0pt\mathscr{C}^{\mu})\neq 0
for some
n
, then the orbits
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}^{\lambda}
and
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}^{\mu}
are contained in the same connected component of
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
.
If
Z
denotes the center of
G
, then the natural morphism
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})_{\mathrm{red}}\to({\mathcal{G}r}_{G/Z})_{\mathrm{red}}
restricts, on each connected component
X
of
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})_{\mathrm{red}}
, to a universal homeomorphism onto a connected component of
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G/Z})_{\mathrm{red}}
; see e.g.
[
BRR
, Proof of Theorem 8.1]
for references. By
[
SP
,
Tag 03SI
]
and Remark
8.2
(
2
) we then have a fully faithful functor
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathrm{sph}}(X,\Bbbk)\to\mathsf{Perv}_{(G/Z)_{\mathscr{O}}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G/Z},\Bbbk)
, which reduces the proof to the case
G
is semisimple of adjoint type, which we assume from now on.

In particular, under this assumption we can take
\varsigma=\rho^{\vee}
, and apply Theorem
8.3
. This result implies that the simple objects in
\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
are the perverse sheaves

\mathscr{I}\hskip-1.0pt\mathscr{C}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda}:=\Delta^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\varsigma}\star\mathscr{I}\hskip-1.0pt\mathscr{C}^{\lambda-\varsigma}

for
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}=\varsigma+\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{+}
. In view of (
8.2
), this shows that to prove the lemma it suffices to prove that for
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
, if
\Ext^{n}_{\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{I}\hskip-1.0pt\mathscr{C}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda},\mathscr{I}\hskip-1.0pt\mathscr{C}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\mu})\neq 0
for some
n
, then
W_{\mathrm{aff}}\cdot_{\ell}\lambda=W_{\mathrm{aff}}\cdot_{\ell}\mu
.

In view of Theorem
7.4
(see also (
5.2
) and (
7.1
)) and the decomposition of
({\mathcal{G}r}_{G})^{\varpi}
into its connected components (see Proposition
4.7
), if
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{++}
satisfy
W_{\mathrm{aff}}\cdot_{\ell}\lambda\neq W_{\mathrm{aff}}\cdot_{\ell}\mu
then we have

\Hom_{\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{T}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda},\mathscr{T}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\mu})=0.

This implies that the category
\mathsf{Tilt}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
breaks canonically as a direct sum of full subcategories parametrized by orbits of
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
on
\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
(for the action
\cdot_{\ell}
), where the subcategory corresponding to a given orbit has as objects the direct sums of indecomposable tilting perverse sheaves labelled by elements in this orbit. Using (
5.3
) we deduce a similar decomposition of
D^{\mathrm{b}}\mathsf{Perv}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{G}r}_{G},\Bbbk)
as a direct sum of full subcategories parametrized by
\mathbf{X}^{\vee}/(W_{\mathrm{aff}},\cdot_{\ell})
. For any
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
the object
\mathscr{I}\hskip-1.0pt\mathscr{C}_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}
is indecomposable, hence belongs to one of these subcategories; the existence of the nonzero maps

\mathscr{I}\hskip-1.0pt\mathscr{C}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda}\twoheadleftarrow\Delta^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{T}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{\lambda}

shows that this subcategory is the one attached to
W_{\mathrm{aff}}\cdot_{\ell}\lambda
, which finishes the proof.
∎

In view of Theorem
8.1
and (
8.1
), Theorem
8.5
is equivalent to the statement that if
\Ext^{n}_{\Rep(G^{\vee}_{\Bbbk})}(\mathsf{L}(\lambda),\mathsf{L}(\mu))\neq 0
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, then
W_{\mathrm{aff}}\bullet_{\ell}\lambda=W_{\mathrm{aff}}\bullet_{\ell}\mu
. This property is of course well known, and called the
Linkage Principle
, see
[
J2
, §II.6]
. (This statement was first conjectured by Verma, and proved by Andersen in full generality, after partial results of Humphreys, Kac–Weisfeiler and Carter–Lusztig; see
[
A1
]
for more details.)

8.3.
Antispherical
\ell
-canonical basis and parity complexes

Let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{aff}}
be the Hecke algebra of
(W_{\mathrm{aff}},S_{\mathrm{aff}})
, and let
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{asph}}
be its antispherical module, with “standard” basis
(N_{w}:w\in{}^{\mathrm{f}}W_{\mathrm{aff}})
parametrized by the subset
{}^{\mathrm{f}}W_{\mathrm{aff}}\subset W_{\mathrm{aff}}
of elements
w
which are minimal in
W_{\mathrm{f}}w
. (Here we follow the conventions of
[
Soe
]
.) Let us consider

{\mathcal{F}l}_{G}^{\circ}:={\mathcal{F}l}^{1,\circ}_{\mathbf{a}_{1}},

the connected component of the base point in the affine flag variety associated with
LG
.
We can then define, as for
{\mathcal{G}r}_{G}
, the Iwahori–Whittaker derived category
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{F}l}_{G}^{\circ},\Bbbk)
, and its full subcategory
\mathsf{Parity}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{F}l}_{G}^{\circ},\Bbbk)
of parity complexes. The
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u}}^{+}
-orbits on
{\mathcal{F}l}_{G}^{\circ}
are naturally parametrized by
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
, and those which support a nonzero Iwahori–Whittaker local system are the ones corresponding to elements in
{}^{\mathrm{f}}W_{\mathrm{aff}}
; we will denote by
\nabla^{\mathcal{IW}}_{w}
and
\mathscr{E}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{w}
the costandard perverse sheaf and indecomposable parity complex attached to
w\in{}^{\mathrm{f}}W_{\mathrm{aff}}
, respectively. (The latter object is characterized by properties similar to those considered in Proposition
7.3
, taking into account the dimension of the corresponding orbit.)
We
then have a canonical isomorphism

\mathrm{ch}:[\mathsf{Parity}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{F}l}_{G}^{\circ},\Bbbk)]\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{asph}}

determined by

\mathrm{ch}([\mathscr{F}])=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in{}^{\mathrm{f}}W_{\mathrm{aff}}\\
n\in\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\dim_{\Bbbk}\Hom_{D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{F}l}_{G}^{\circ},\Bbbk)}(\mathscr{F},\nabla_{w}^{\mathcal{IW}}[n])\cdot v^{n}N_{w},

where
[\mathsf{Parity}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{F}l}_{G}^{\circ},\Bbbk)]
is the split Grothendieck group of the additive category
\mathsf{Parity}_{\mathcal{IW}}({\mathcal{F}l}_{G}^{\circ},\Bbbk)
.

In terms of this isomorphism,
the
\ell
-canonical basis

({}^{\ell}\hskip-1.0pt\underline{N}_{w}:w\in{}^{\mathrm{f}}W_{\mathrm{aff}})
of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{asph}}
(see
[
RW1
,
AR3
]
) can be characterized by

(8.3)

{}^{\ell}\hskip-1.0pt\underline{N}_{w}:=\mathrm{ch}(\mathscr{E}^{\mathcal{IW}}_{w}).

The associated
\ell
-Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials

({}^{\ell}\hskip-1.0ptn_{y,w}:y,w\in{}^{\mathrm{f}}W_{\mathrm{aff}})
are characterized by the equality

{}^{\ell}\hskip-1.0pt\underline{N}_{w}=\sum_{y\in{}^{\mathrm{f}}W_{\mathrm{aff}}}{}^{\ell}\hskip-1.0ptn_{y,w}\cdot N_{y}.

Remark 8.6
.

It is easily seen that the computation of the
\ell
-canonical basis and
\ell
-Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials can be reduced to the case
G
is quasi-simple. In this case, the results of
[
RW1
, Part III]
show that this basis coincides with the basis with the same name studied in
[
JW
]
, for the Coxeter system
(W_{\mathrm{aff}},S_{\mathrm{aff}})
and the realization considered in
[
RW1
, Remark 10.7.2(2)]
. In particular, these data can be computed algorithmically using the procedure described in
[
JW
,
GJW
]
.

These considerations have been stated for the ind-variety
{\mathcal{F}l}^{1,\circ}_{\mathbf{a}_{1}}
, but in practice we will rather use them for the isomorphic variety
{\mathcal{F}l}^{\ell,\circ}_{\mathbf{a}_{\ell}}
, with respect to the action of
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u},\ell}^{+}
.
More generally we can consider a facet
\mathbf{f}\subset\overline{\mathbf{a}}_{\ell}
, and the basic connected component in the associated partial affine flag variety
{\mathcal{F}l}^{\ell,\circ}_{\mathbf{f}}
; see §
4.3
. Here again the Iwahori–Whittaker derived category (with respect to the action of
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u},\ell}^{+}
) makes sense, and so does the notion of parity complexes. This derived category will be denoted
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell}}({\mathcal{F}l}^{\ell,\circ}_{\mathbf{f}},\Bbbk)
.

The indecomposable parity objects in
D^{\mathrm{b}}_{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell}}({\mathcal{F}l}^{\ell,\circ}_{\mathbf{f}},\Bbbk)
can be described in terms of those on
{\mathcal{F}l}^{\ell,\circ}_{\mathbf{a}_{\ell}}
as follows.
As usual, the general theory of parity complexes ensures that there exists at most one indecomposable parity complex on
{\mathcal{F}l}^{\ell,\circ}_{\mathbf{f}}
associated with each
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u},\ell}^{+}
-orbit which supports a nonzero Iwahori–Whittaker local system, and that each indecomposable parity complex is isomorphic (up to cohomological shift) to such an object. As explained in Remark
4.8
(
2
), the
\mathrm{Iw}_{\mathrm{u},\ell}^{+}
-orbits on
{\mathcal{F}l}^{\ell,\circ}_{\mathbf{f}}
are parametrized in the natural way by the quotient
W_{\mathrm{aff}}/W_{\mathrm{aff}}^{\mathbf{f}}
, or in other words by the elements
w\in W_{\mathrm{aff}}
which are maximal in
wW_{\mathrm{aff}}^{\mathbf{f}}
. If
w\in W_{\mathrm{aff}}
is maximal in
wW_{\mathrm{aff}}^{\mathbf{f}}
, if the corresponding orbit supported a nonzero Iwahori–Whittaker local system, and if the associated indecomposable parity complex exists, we will denote it by
\mathscr{E}^{\mathcal{IW}_{\ell}}_{\mathbf{f},w}
.

In the following statement, the morphism

8.4.
The tilting character formula

Now we return to Representation Theory.
Recall that a
G^{\vee}_{\Bbbk}
-module
M
in
\Rep(G^{\vee}_{\Bbbk})
is called
tilting
if both
M
and
M^{*}
admit filtrations with subquotients of the form
\mathsf{N}(\lambda)
with
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{+}
. It is well known (see
[
J2
, §II.E]
) that the indecomposable tilting
G^{\vee}_{\Bbbk}
-modules are classified by their highest weight (a dominant weight), and that any tilting module is a direct sum of indecomposable tilting modules. The indecomposable tilting module of highest weight
\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{+}
will be denoted
\mathsf{T}(\lambda)
.

The same considerations as in §
4.1
show that a fundamental domain for the action of
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
on
\mathbf{X}^{\vee}
via
\bullet_{\ell}
is given by the subset

\overline{C}_{\ell}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbf{X}^{\vee}\mid\forall\alpha\in\mathfrak{R}^{+},\,0\leq\langle\lambda+\rho^{\vee},\alpha\rangle\leq\ell\}.

Below we will need to describe the subset
(W_{\mathrm{aff}}\bullet_{\ell}\lambda)\cap\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{+}
more explicitly for
\lambda\in\overline{C}_{\ell}
. For this we set
I_{\lambda}:=\{s\in S_{\mathrm{aff}}\mid s\bullet_{\ell}\lambda=\lambda\}
, so that the stabilizer in
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
of
\lambda
(for
\bullet_{\ell}
) is the parabolic subgroup
W_{\lambda}
of
W_{\mathrm{aff}}
generated by
I_{\lambda}
. We set

W_{\mathrm{aff}}^{(\lambda)}:=\{w\in W_{\mathrm{aff}}\mid\text{$w$ is maximal in $wW_{\lambda}$ and minimal in $W_{\mathrm{f}}w$}\}.

Then it is known that the assignment
w\mapsto w\bullet_{\ell}\lambda
induces a bijection

W_{\mathrm{aff}}^{(\lambda)}\xrightarrow{\sim}(W_{\mathrm{aff}}\bullet_{\ell}\lambda)\cap\mathbf{X}^{\vee}_{+};

see
[
AR2
, §10.1]
for similar considerations.
In view of these comments,
the following result gives an answer to the question of describing characters of
all
indecomposable tilting
G^{\vee}_{\Bbbk}
-modules, without any restriction on
\ell
.

Theorem 8.9
.

Let
\lambda\in\overline{C}_{\ell}
. Then for any
w\in W_{\mathrm{aff}}^{(\lambda)}
we have

[\mathsf{T}(w\bullet_{\ell}\lambda)]=\sum_{y\in W_{\mathrm{aff}}^{(\lambda)}}{}^{\ell}\hskip-1.0ptn_{y,w}(1)\cdot[\mathsf{N}(y\bullet_{\ell}\lambda)]

in the Grothendieck group of
\Rep(G^{\vee}_{\Bbbk})
.

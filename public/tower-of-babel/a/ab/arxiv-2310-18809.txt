Anatomy of Path Integral Monte Carlo: algebraic derivation of the harmonic oscillator’s universal discrete imaginary-time propagator and its sequential optimization
Siu A. Chin

Abstract
The direct integration of the harmonic oscillator path integral obscures the fundamental structure of its discrete, imaginary time propagator (density matrix). This work, by first proving an operator identity for contracting two free propagators into one in the presence of interaction, derives the discrete propagator by simple algebra without doing any integration. This discrete propagator is u ​ n ​ i ​ v ​ e ​ r ​ s ​ a ​ l universal , having the same two hyperbolic coefficient functions for all short-time propagators. Individual short-time propagator only modifies the coefficient function’s argument, its p ​ o ​ r ​ t ​ a ​ l portal parameter, whose convergent order is the same as the thermodynamic energy. Moreover, the thermodynamic energy can be given in a closed form for any short-time propagator. Since the portal parameter can be systematically optimized by matching the expansion of the product of the two coefficients, any short-time propagator can be optimized s ​ e ​ q ​ u ​ e ​ n ​ t ​ i ​ a ​ l ​ l ​ y sequentially , order by order, by matching the product coefficient’s expansion alone, without computing the energy. Previous empirical findings on the convergence of fourth and sixth-order propagators can now be understood analytically. An eight-order convergent short-time propagator is also derived.



I
Introduction

Current advances in Path Integral Monte Carlo (PIMC) continue to be the use of parametrized fourth-order short-time propagators to optimize PIMC’s convergence at the fewest number of
beads
jan01
;
sak09
;
chin15
;
kam16
;
lin18
;
wan22
.
This is especially important for ameliorating the sign problem in
fermion systems
chin15
. Since the harmonic oscillator’s discrete imaginary
time path integral can be computed analytically
kam16
;
wan22
;
sch81
,
it has been widely used as a testing ground for such optimization. However, even for the
harmonic oscillator, such multi-parameter optimization is non-trivial and has been done only
by trial and error in PIMC
sak09
, or by elaborate theoretical computations
wan22
.

In this work, we solve for, and optimize, the discrete harmonic oscillator path integral by a
completely novel approach, unique to the harmonic oscillator. The method therefore may not be
generalizable to more realistic interactions. However, what has achieved here for the harmonic oscillator,
will at least suggest what might be possible for future PIMC.

The conventional method of solving for the harmonic oscillator’s discrete path integral is by
direct integration
kam16
;
wan22
;
sch81
. Manipulating the resulting
tridiagonal matrix is akin to manipulating a black box, with no easy comprehension
of what’s inside. In this work, we completely abandon this opaque approach. Instead,
we first prove in Sect.
III
, an operator identity
for contracting two free propagators into one in the presence of the harmonic interaction.
Once proven, this identity eliminates the need for doing any integral.
By applying this identity to left-right symmetric short-time propagators in Sect.
IV
, this work
derives the discrete, imaginary-time harmonic oscillator propagator by simple algebra.
This discrete propagator has a
universal
structure, having the
same functional form, the same two hyperbolic coefficient functions, for all short-time propagators.
Different short-time propagator only changes the
portal
parameter, the argument of the
hyperbolic functions.

This universal discrete propagator now opens up new ways of optimizing any
short-time propagator due to two results in Sect.
V
. First,
the convergence of the portal parameter can be determined by matching the
series expansion of the product of the two coefficients to the exact result.
Second, the convergent order of the portal parameter can be shown to be the same
as the thermodynamic energy.
Therefore, the parameters in the short-time propagator, through the portal parameter,
can be optimized order-by-order, by matching the series expansion of the
product coefficient, without computing the energy. The advantage here is that
one does not need to optimize all short-time propagator parameters
simultaneously
in minimizing
the energy. Each parameter can be optimized
sequentially
, by matching the order conditions
one by one.

By applying this optimization procedure to fourth and sixth-order short-time-propagators
in Sect.
VI
, one can now derive analytically
the optimal parameter values empirically found by Sakkos
et al.
sak09

and reproduce those computed theoretically by Wang
et al.
wan22
. In order
to compare published results for these high-order propagators, the thermodynamic
energy is derived in a closed form for any short-time propagator.
Finally, in Sect.
VII
, the same optimization procedure can be exploited to yield
a new eight-order algorithm for solving the harmonic oscillator.

In Sect.
II
,
we give a brief review of PIMC, emphasizing its
the operator formulation.
Conclusions and future directions are stated in Sect.
VIII
.

II
Discrete Path integral

In suitable units, the one-dimensional imaginary time Schrödinger equation
can always be written as

-\frac{\partial\psi(x,\tau)}{\partial\tau}=(\hat{T}+\hat{V})\psi(x,\tau)=\left(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial{x}^{2}}+V(x)\right)\psi(x,\tau),

(1)

with dimensionless spatial variable
x
and imaginary time
\tau
(
\propto\beta\!=\!1/k_{B}T
).
The solution in operator form is just

|\psi(\tau)\rangle=\hat{G}(\tau)|\psi(0)\rangle,

(2)

where the imaginary time evoluation operator
\hat{G}(\tau)
is given by

\hat{G}(\tau)={\rm e}^{-\tau(\hat{T}+\hat{V})}.

(3)

The matrix element of
\hat{G}(\tau)
is the propagator, or density matrix:

G(x^{\prime},x,\tau)=\langle x^{\prime}|\hat{G}(\tau)|x\rangle=\sum_{n}\psi_{n}^{*}(x^{\prime})\psi_{n}(x){\rm e}^{-\tau E_{n}}.

(4)

In PIMC, one is
interested in extracting the square of the ground state wave function
\psi^{2}_{0}(x)
and energy
E_{0}

from the long time (or low temperature) limit of the imaginary time propagator:

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}G(x,x,\tau)\longrightarrow\psi_{0}^{2}(x){\rm e}^{-\tau E_{0}}+\cdots.

(5)

Since the matrix element of
\hat{G}(\tau)
is generally not known analytically, it is usually approximated by
a product of
N
short-time operators at imaginary time-step
{\epsilon}=\tau/N
,

\hat{G}_{N}=[\hat{G}_{1}({\epsilon})]^{N},

(6)

where
\hat{G}_{1}({\epsilon})
is a short-time approximation of
\hat{G}({\epsilon})

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{1}({\epsilon})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-{\epsilon}(\hat{T}+\hat{V})},

(7)

of which the simplest is the second-order
primitive approximation
(PA)

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{1}({\epsilon})

\displaystyle={\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{2}{\epsilon}\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-{\epsilon}\hat{T}}{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{2}{\epsilon}\hat{V}},

(8)

with matrix element

\displaystyle G_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},{\epsilon})=\langle x_{1}|\hat{G}_{1}({\epsilon})|x_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi{\epsilon}}}{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{2}{\epsilon}V(x_{1})}{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{2{\epsilon}}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{2}{\epsilon}V(x_{2})}.

(9)

The matrix element of (
6
) is then,

\displaystyle G_{N}(x^{\prime},x,\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle x^{\prime}|[\hat{G}_{1}({\epsilon})]^{N}|x\rangle

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx_{1}\cdots dx_{N-1}\,G_{1}(x^{\prime},x_{1},{\epsilon})G_{1}(x_{1},x_{2},{\epsilon})\cdots G_{1}(x_{N-1},x,{\epsilon}),

which is the conventional path integral for the discrete propagator
at
\tau=N{\epsilon}
.

In this work, because of the special nature of the harmonic oscillator, it is far more
transparent to work directly at the simpler operator level (
6
) than at the convoluted
path integral level (
10
). Moreover, this operator approach can easily accommodate
higher-order short-time operator
\hat{G}_{1}
more complex
than the primitive approximation (
8
).

III
Operator contractions

In terms of dimensionless length
x=r/\sqrt{\hbar/m\omega}
, energy
E={\cal E}/\hbar\omega

and imaginary time
\tau=\hbar\omega/k_{B}T
, the dimensionless harmonic potential operator is
\hat{V}(x)=x^{2}/2
.
In this case, the Gaussion integral below can be computed to give

\displaystyle\langle x_{1}|{\rm e}^{-a\hat{T}}{\rm e}^{-b\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-c\hat{T}}|x_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi a}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi c}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\,{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{2a}(x_{1}-x)^{2}-b\frac{1}{2}x^{2}-\frac{1}{2c}(x-x_{2})^{2}}

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\kappa}}{\rm e}^{-\nu\frac{1}{2}x_{1}^{2}}{\rm e}^{-\frac{1}{2\kappa}(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}}{\rm e}^{-\mu\frac{1}{2}x_{2}^{2}}

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle x_{1}|{\rm e}^{-\nu\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-\kappa\hat{T}}{\rm e}^{-\mu\hat{V}}|x_{2}\rangle

(13)

where

\kappa=a+abc+c,\quad\nu=\frac{bc}{\kappa},\quad\mu=\frac{ba}{\kappa}.

(14)

This means that (
13
) is actually an operator identity

{\rm e}^{-a\hat{T}}{\rm e}^{-b\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-c\hat{T}}={\rm e}^{-\nu\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-\kappa\hat{T}}{\rm e}^{-\mu\hat{V}},

(15)

where two
\hat{T}
operators, in the presence of interaction
\hat{V}
, have been
contracted
into one.
Therefore, any numbers of operators of
\hat{T}
and
\hat{V}
for the harmonic oscillator can be contracted down
to a single
\hat{T}
operator form, as in the RHS of (
15
), with obvious matrix element (
12
).

Applying (
15
) twice to the following three-operator case gives

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-a\hat{T}}{\rm e}^{-b\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-c\hat{T}}{\rm e}^{-b\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-a\hat{T}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-\nu\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-\kappa\hat{T}}{\rm e}^{-\mu\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-b\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-a\hat{T}},

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-\nu\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-\nu^{\prime}\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-\kappa^{\prime}\hat{T}}{\rm e}^{-\mu^{\prime}\hat{V}},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-\mu^{\prime}\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-\kappa^{\prime}\hat{T}}{\rm e}^{-\mu^{\prime}\hat{V}},

(17)

where the second application at (
16
) with
a^{\prime}=\kappa
,
b^{\prime}=\mu+b
and
c^{\prime}=a
yields

\displaystyle\kappa^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa+\kappa(\mu+b)a+a=(\kappa+a)+ba(\kappa+a)

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+ab)(\kappa+a)=(1+ab)(2a+abc+c)

\mu^{\prime}=\frac{(\mu+b)\kappa}{\kappa^{\prime}}=\frac{b(a+\kappa)}{(1+ab)(\kappa+a)}=\frac{b}{(1+ab)}

(19)

\displaystyle\nu+\nu^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{bc}{\kappa}+\frac{(\mu+b)a}{\kappa^{\prime}}=\frac{bc}{\kappa}+\frac{ab}{\kappa^{\prime}}\left(\frac{a+\kappa}{\kappa}\right)=\mu^{\prime}.

(20)

Note that the product

\kappa^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}=b(2a+abc+c),

(21)

is only a polynomial in the original coefficients.

It follows that any palindromic, left-right symmetric approximate short-time operator of the form

\hat{G}_{1}({\epsilon})=\prod_{i}{\rm e}^{-a_{i}{\epsilon}\hat{T}}{\rm e}^{-b_{i}{\epsilon}\hat{V}},

(22)

can be contracted down to a single
\hat{T}
-operator, palindromic form

\hat{G}_{1}({\epsilon})={\rm e}^{-\mu_{1}\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-\kappa_{1}\hat{T}}{\rm e}^{-\mu_{1}\hat{V}},

(23)

where
\mu_{1}
and
\kappa_{1}
are functions of
a_{i}
,
b_{i}
and
{\epsilon}
. Once
contracted to a single
\hat{T}
operator form, its matrix element is then easily given
by (
12
). Therefore we will refer to the short-time operator interchangeably
as the short-time propagator.

For the PA short-time propagator,
one has simply

\kappa_{1}={\epsilon}\quad{\rm and}\quad\mu_{1}=\frac{1}{2}{\epsilon}.

(24)

Other higher order short-time operators will be examined in Sects.
V
and
VI

IV
The universal discrete propagators

Starting with a palindromic short-time propagator
\hat{G}_{1}
, any product
\hat{G}_{1}^{N}
must also
be palindromic, contractable down to a single
\hat{T}
-operator form.
This is obvious because if
N
is even then operators on both sides are equal. If
N
is odd, then
there is central palindromic short-time operator with equal number of
\hat{G}_{1}
on both sides.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\hat{G}_{m+n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\hat{G}_{1}({\epsilon})]^{m}[\hat{G}_{1}({\epsilon})]^{n}

(25)

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-\mu_{m+n}\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-\kappa_{m+n}\hat{T}}{\rm e}^{-\mu_{m+n}\hat{V}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-\mu_{m}\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-\kappa_{m}\hat{T}}{\rm e}^{-\mu_{m}\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-\mu_{n}\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-\kappa_{n}\hat{T}}{\rm e}^{-\mu_{n}\hat{V}}

(26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-\mu_{m}\hat{V}}[{\rm e}^{-\kappa_{m}\hat{T}}{\rm e}^{-(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-\kappa_{n}\hat{T}}]{\rm e}^{-\mu_{n}\hat{V}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-\mu_{m}\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-\nu\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-\kappa\hat{T}}{\rm e}^{-\mu\hat{V}}{\rm e}^{-\mu_{n}\hat{V}}

where, by the contraction identity (
15
)

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa_{m}+\kappa_{n}+\kappa_{m}(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})\kappa_{n}=\kappa_{m+n},

(27)

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})\kappa_{n}}{\kappa_{m+n}},

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})\kappa_{m}}{\kappa_{m+n}}.

(28)

If one now defines an equally fundamental,
product variable
of
\kappa_{n}
and
\mu_{n}
via

\zeta_{n}=1+\kappa_{n}\mu_{n},

(29)

with
\zeta_{1}=1+\kappa_{1}\mu_{1}

\hat{G}_{1}
, then (

) reads

\displaystyle\kappa_{m+n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa_{m}+\kappa_{n}+\kappa_{n}(\zeta_{m}-1)+\kappa_{m}(\zeta_{n}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa_{n}\zeta_{m}+\kappa_{m}\zeta_{n}.

Since (
26

\displaystyle\mu_{m+n}=\mu_{m}+\frac{\kappa_{n}(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})}{\kappa_{n}\zeta_{m}+\kappa_{m}\zeta_{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{n}+\frac{\kappa_{m}(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})}{\kappa_{n}\zeta_{m}+\kappa_{m}\zeta_{n}}

The last equality can be true only if

\mu_{m}(\kappa_{n}\zeta_{m}+\kappa_{m}\zeta_{n})+\kappa_{n}(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n})=\mu_{n}(\kappa_{n}\zeta_{m}+\kappa_{m}\zeta_{n})+\kappa_{m}(\mu_{m}+\mu_{n}).

Substituting in

\displaystyle\mu_{m}\kappa_{n}\zeta_{m}+(\zeta_{m}-1)\zeta_{n}+\kappa_{n}\mu_{m}+(\zeta_{n}-1)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=(\zeta_{n}-1)\zeta_{m}+\mu_{n}\kappa_{m}\zeta_{n}+(\zeta_{m}-1)+\kappa_{m}\mu_{n},

(33)

which simplifies to

\mu_{m}\kappa_{n}\zeta_{m}+\kappa_{n}\mu_{m}=\mu_{n}\kappa_{m}\zeta_{n}+\kappa_{m}\mu_{n}.

(34)

Since

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_{n}(\zeta^{2}_{m}-1)}{\kappa_{m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa_{m}(\zeta^{2}_{n}-1)}{\kappa_{n}},

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\quad\frac{(\zeta^{2}_{m}-1)}{\kappa^{2}_{m}}

\displaystyle\frac{(\zeta^{2}_{n}-1)}{\kappa^{2}_{n}},

implying that

\gamma^{2}

Given (

(38)

rewrite (

In terms of the new variables (

V
Convergent order of short-time operators

For the PA propagator, as noted previously
\zeta_{1}=1+{\epsilon}^{2}/2
.
In the
convergent limit
of
{\epsilon}=\tau/N\rightarrow 0
while keeping
\tau
fixed,

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cosh^{-1}(\zeta_{1})

(59)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle{\epsilon}-\frac{1}{24}{\epsilon}^{3}+\cdots,

\displaystyle Nu

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle N{\epsilon}=\tau,

(60)

one obtains the coefficient (
45
)-(
46
):

\displaystyle\zeta(\tau)=\cosh(\tau),

(61)

\displaystyle\kappa(\tau)=\sinh(\tau),

(62)

reproducing the exact propagator
fey72

G(x^{\prime},x,\tau)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\kappa(\tau)}}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2\kappa(\tau)}\left(\zeta(\tau)(x^{2}+{x^{\prime}}^{2})-2xx^{\prime}\right)\right],

(63)

since

as
{\epsilon}\rightarrow 0
.

While (
63
) verifies the correct convergence of our discrete propagator, this exact result is
not the focus of this work. Instead, we are interested in optimizing

\hat{G}_{N}
so that it can approach this result at a minimum value of
N
.

Clearly the minimum value is
N=1
if

\zeta_{1}({\epsilon})=\cosh({\epsilon}),

(64)

forcing

in (

), so that
\hat{G}_{1}
is the exact propagator.
If
\hat{G}_{1}
is not exact, then its coefficient
\zeta_{1}({\epsilon})
must match the expansion

\cosh({\epsilon})=1+\frac{1}{2!}\epsilon^{2}+\frac{1}{4!}\epsilon^{4}+\frac{1}{6!}\epsilon^{6}+\cdots

(65)

as closely as possible, order by order in
\epsilon
.
By comparing PA’s
\zeta_{1}
from (
55
) to the above expansion
one sees that it is a second-order algorithm because it matches the above expansion
to second order in
{\epsilon}
. Therefore,
the order of the propagator is just the correct
expansion order of its product coefficient function
\zeta_{1}({\epsilon})
.
This is an
intrinsic
characterization of the convergence order of any short-time propagator
for the harmonic oscillator, with no reference to any calculations
extrinsic
to the algorithm.

However, by the following calculation of the thermodynamic energy,
this intrinsic characterization is the same as the conventional definition
that the order of the propagator is the order of its energy error.

First, we note that if a short-time propagator’s
\zeta_{1}
is correct up to order
2n
,

\zeta_{1}=1+\frac{1}{2!}\epsilon^{2}+\cdots+\frac{1}{(2n)!}\epsilon^{2n}+\frac{\delta_{2n+2}}{(2n+2)!}\epsilon^{2n+2}

but has error at order
2n+2
such that
\delta_{2n+2}<1
,
then the matching condition (
43
) would force
the portal parameter to converge as (note the minus sign)

u=\epsilon(1-C\epsilon^{2n}+\cdots)

(67)

with
C
determined by

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{\epsilon^{2k}}{(2k)!}+\frac{\delta_{2n+2}}{(2n+2)!}\epsilon^{2n+2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\epsilon^{2k}}{(2k)!}(1-C\epsilon^{2n})^{2k}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\epsilon^{2k}}{(2k)!}(1-2kC\epsilon^{2n}+\cdots)

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{2n+2}}{(2n+2)!}\epsilon^{2n+2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(2n+2)!}\epsilon^{2n+2}-C\epsilon^{2n+2}+\cdots

(68)

to be

C=\frac{1-\delta_{2n+2}}{(2n+2)!}.

(69)

so that (
67
) reads

u=\epsilon\left(1-\frac{(1-\delta_{2n+2})}{(2n+2)!}\epsilon^{2n}+O(\epsilon^{2n+2})\right).

(70)

Now the partition function at
N
discrete time step is

\displaystyle Z_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dx\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\kappa_{N}}}{\rm e}^{-\mu_{N}x^{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\kappa_{N}}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\mu_{N}}}

(71)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\kappa_{N}\mu_{N}}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(\zeta_{N}-1)}}=\frac{1}{2\sinh(Nu/2)}.

The partition function is therefore also
universal and depends on individual short-time propagators
only through the portal parameter
u
via (
43
). This was a very surprising finding
noted by Wang
et al.
wan22
, after integrating various short-time propagators.
It is unsurprising here because we have already shown that the discrete propagator itself is universal.

The
N
-bead thermodynamics energy is

\displaystyle E_{N}^{T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\frac{dZ_{N}}{d\tau}=\frac{du}{d\epsilon}E_{N}

(72)

where

E_{N}=\frac{1}{2}\coth(Nu/2)=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{{\rm e}^{Nu}-1}

(73)

is the universal discrete energy. For propagator with
u
given by (
67
),
in the convergent limit,

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}E_{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\frac{\tau}{\sinh(\tau)}C\epsilon^{2n})E(\tau)+\cdots

(74)

where
E(\tau)
is the exact free energy

E(\tau)=\frac{1}{2}\coth(\tau/2).

(75)

The thermodynamics energy therefore converges to leading order in
\epsilon
as

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}E^{T}_{N}(\tau)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{du}{d\epsilon}(1+\frac{\tau}{\sinh(\tau)}C\epsilon^{2n})E(\tau)+\cdots

(76)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-(2n+1)C\epsilon^{2n})(1+\frac{\tau}{\sinh(\tau)}C\epsilon^{2n})E(\tau)+\cdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle E(\tau)-E(\tau)\left(2n+1-\frac{\tau}{\sinh(\tau)}\right)C\epsilon^{2n}+\cdots.

This shows that if
\zeta_{1}
is
correct
to order
2n
, the thermodynamic energy has
error
of order
2n
.
Since
\tau/\sinh(\tau)\leq 1
and goes rapidly to zero with increasing
\tau

at any reasonably large
\tau
, the energy error is almost entirely
due to the convergent error of the portal parameter
u
:

E^{T}_{N}(\tau)\rightarrow E(\tau)\left(1-(2n+1)\frac{(1-\delta_{2n+2})}{(2n+2)!}\epsilon^{2n}+\cdots\right).

(77)

This result shows that for
\delta_{2n+2}\leq 1
, the thermodynamic energy
converges from below, in accordance with the Golden-Thompson inequality

;
tho65
.
Compare (
77
) to (

) shows that the convergent error of
the thermodynamic energy is not only of the same order as
the portal parameter, but is
(2n+1)
times larger. In the case of PA,

\delta_{4}=0

(78)

Thus for the harmonic oscillator,
any short-time propagator can be optimized by simply forcing its coefficient
\zeta_{1}(\epsilon)

to match the expansion of
\cosh(\epsilon)
to maximal order. There is no need to compute the energy separately.

VIII
Conclusions and future directions

This work has shown that, on the basis of a single operator contraction identity,
everything about the harmonic oscillator path integral can be known simply,
from deriving its universal discrete propagator, optimizing all short-time propagators, to
the exact formula for the thermodynamic energy.
The central role is played by the product coefficient
\zeta_{1}(\epsilon)
, out of which the
portal parameter
u
is derived, the discrete propagator defined, and whose derivative and
series expansion determined the thermodynamic energy and sequential steps of optimization. Because of
this detailed knowledge, all previous published results can now be understood analytically
and a new eight-order algorithm derived.

A natural follow up to this work would be a detailed study on the convergence of
the thermodynamic and Hamiltonian ground state energy as a function of
N
. Since the harmonic oscillator is
separable in any dimension, the discrete propagator derived here is applicable to harmonic oscillators in any dimension. In particular, it will be of interest to study the anti-symmetrized discrete fermion propagator
in two or three dimensions, to gain further insights on how to ameliorate the sign problem
chin15

in quantum dots using higher order propagators.

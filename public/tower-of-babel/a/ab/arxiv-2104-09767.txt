A new direct discontinuous Galerkin method with interface correction for two-dimensional compressible Navier-Stokes equations
Mustafa E. Danis, Jue Yan

Abstract
We propose a new formula for the nonlinear viscous numerical flux and extend the direct discontinuous Galerkin method with interface correction (DDGIC) of Liu and Yan [ 1 ] to compressible Navier-Stokes equations. The new DDGIC framework is based on the observation that the nonlinear diffusion can be represented as a sum of multiple individual diffusion processes corresponding to each conserved variable. A set of direction vectors corresponding to each individual diffusion process is defined and approximated by the average value of the numerical solution at the cell interfaces. The new framework only requires the computation of conserved variables’ gradient, which is linear and approximated by the original direct DG numerical flux formula. The proposed method greatly simplifies the implementation, and thus, can be easily extended to general equations and turbulence models. Numerical experiments with P 1 P_{1} , P 2 P_{2} , P 3 P_{3} and P 4 P_{4} polynomial approximations are performed to verify the optimal ( k + 1 ) t ​ h (k+1)^{th} high-order accuracy of the method. The new DDGIC method is shown to be able to accurately calculate physical quantities such as lift, drag, and friction coefficients as well as separation angle and Strouhal number.

Despite all the efforts mentioned above, the original DDGIC method
[
1
]
could not be applied to compressible NS equations directly. This is because an antiderivative of each nonlinear diffusion component is required in the numerical flux formula, and unfortunately, it does not exist for compressible NS equations. A similar difficulty arises for the interface correction terms. For general system of conservation equations, it is not clear how to define suitable interface correction terms for the test function. For this reason, we see different definitions for the viscous numerical flux and interface correction terms across the existing DDG methods, each of which is designed to be used in a specific application.

In this article, we propose a new DDG method with interface correction for compressible NS equations. In the new DDGIC method, antiderivatives of each nonlinear diffusion component are not required, and a suitable algorithm is developed for computing the interface correction terms. Therefore, the new DDGIC method can be conveniently used in different applications. The new idea is based on the following observation: For each equation of the system, we can decompose the complicated multi-variable entangled nonlinear diffusion into multiple individual diffusion processes corresponding to each conserved variable. We then explore the algebraic adjoint property of inner product and introduce a new direction vector on the element edge. Nonlinearity of each diffusion process essentially goes into the corresponding new direction vector defined at the cell interface, which can be approximated by the solution average of the two neighboring cells. To complete the computation of the viscous numerical flux, gradient of the conserved variables are approximated by the numerical flux formula of the original DDG method. Also, the new direction vectors are further applied to define the interface correction terms involving the test function.

One important advantage of the new DDGIC method is that it requires a rather simple implementation. Also, it can be extended to more general equations quite easily. For example, the new DDGIC method can be used as is when different turbulence models are implemented. This might improve the code reuse: The computation of the numerical fluxes of the conserved variables’ gradients are independent of the governing equation and the number of equations in that system. It is the definition of new direction vectors that reflects the physics of the underlying governing equation in the numerical solution. Therefore, the new DDGIC method can be seen as a general framework to apply DDGIC method to general conservation laws with nonlinear diffusion.

2
The new direct discontinuous Galerkin method with interface correction

In this section, we formally derive the new direct discontinuous Galerkin method with interface correction (DDGIC) for 2-D NS equations. First, we briefly discuss our new DDGIC method for the scalar nonlinear equations. We then explain the derivation of the new DDGIC method for 2-D compressible NS equations in detail.

2.1

2.2
The new DDGIC method for 2-D compressible Navier-Stokes equations

The compressible NS equations in an inertial frame with no external force is given as

\frac{\partial\textbf{Q}}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot\textbf{F}_{c}(\textbf{Q})=\nabla\cdot\textbf{F}_{v}(\textbf{Q},\nabla\textbf{Q}),\hskip 14.22636pt(\textbf{x},t)\in\Omega\times(0,T).

(11)

We have
\textbf{Q}=\left(\rho,\rho u,\rho v,E\right)^{T}
as the vector of conserved variables where
\rho
is the density,
u
is the velocity in x-direction,
v
is the velocity in y-direction and
E
is the total energy. We denote the velocity vector by
\textbf{u}=(u,v)^{T}
. Furthermore, we have
\textbf{F}_{c}
and
\textbf{F}_{v}
as the convective and viscous fluxes, respectively. The corresponding flux vectors
f
and
g
in x- and y- directions satisfy the following:

\nabla\cdot\textbf{F}_{c}(\textbf{Q})=\frac{\partial\textbf{f}_{c}(\textbf{Q})}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial\textbf{g}_{c}(\textbf{Q})}{\partial y},\quad\nabla\cdot\textbf{F}_{v}(\textbf{Q},\nabla\textbf{Q})=\frac{\partial\textbf{f}_{v}(\textbf{Q},\nabla\textbf{Q})}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial\textbf{g}_{v}(\textbf{Q},\nabla\textbf{Q})}{\partial y}.

The convective fluxes are defined as

\textbf{f}_{c}(\textbf{Q})=\begin{pmatrix}\rho u\\
\rho u^{2}+p\\
\rho uv\\
u(E+p)\end{pmatrix},\hskip 28.45274pt\textbf{g}_{c}(\textbf{Q})=\begin{pmatrix}\rho u\\
\rho uv\\
\rho v^{2}+p\\
u(E+p)\end{pmatrix},

where
p
is the static pressure. Also, we assume a calorically perfect gas and use the ideal gas law as the equation of state to calculate pressure
p=(\gamma-1)\rho e
.
Here,
\gamma
is the specific heat ratio, which is assumed to be
\gamma=1.4
throughout this study. In addition,
e
is the internal energy. It is also related to the total energy as well as the density and the velocity vector by the following identity

E=\rho e+\frac{1}{2}\rho\left(u^{2}+v^{2}\right).

Another important variable is the speed of sound, which is given by
a=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma p}{\rho}}
. Furthermore, the viscous fluxes are given by

\textbf{f}_{v}(\textbf{Q},\nabla\textbf{Q})=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\tau_{11}\\
\tau_{21}\\
u\tau_{11}+v\tau_{21}+\frac{\mu\gamma}{Pr}e_{x}\end{pmatrix},\hskip 28.45274pt\textbf{g}_{v}(\textbf{Q},\nabla\textbf{Q})=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\tau_{12}\\
\tau_{22}\\
u\tau_{12}+v\tau_{22}+\frac{\mu\gamma}{Pr}e_{y}\end{pmatrix}.

By subscripts

or

\boldsymbol{\tau}=\mu\begin{pmatrix}\frac{4}{3}u_{x}-\frac{2}{3}v_{y}&u_{y}+v_{x}\\
u_{y}+v_{x}&-\frac{2}{3}u_{x}+\frac{4}{3}v_{y}\\

where

\mu=\mu_{ref}\left(\frac{T}{T_{ref}}\right)^{3/2}\frac{T_{ref}+C_{s}}{T+C_{s}}

where

is the temperature,
T_{ref}
is the reference temperature,
C_{s}
is the Sutherland’s temperature, and
\mu_{ref}
is the reference viscosity at

. Note that temperature

is related to the internal energy by the thermodynamical identity

,
where

is the specific heat for a constant volume.
Finally,

In order to derive the DDG scheme formulation for 2-D compressible NS equations, we employ the same triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, numerical solution and test function space
\mathbb{V}_{h}^{k}
in (

As a discontinuous Galerkin method, the numerical solution is discontinuous at the cell interfaces. It is the numerical flux that determines how a cell exchanges information with its neighboring cells. Among the many methods in the literature to compute the convective numerical flux, we choose the local Lax-Friedrichs method, which is written out as

\widehat{\textbf{F}_{c}}\left(\textbf{Q}_{h}\right)\cdot\textbf{n}=\{\!\!\{\textbf{f}_{c}\left(\textbf{Q}_{h}\right)\}\!\!\}n_{1}+\{\!\!\{\textbf{g}_{c}\left(\textbf{Q}_{h}\right)\}\!\!\}n_{2}-\alpha\llbracket\textbf{Q}_{h}\rrbracket,\quad\alpha=\max\left(\lVert\textbf{u}\rVert+a\right).

(13)

where

is the speed of sound.

2.2.1
Adjoint property for the multi-variable system case of compressible Navier-Stokes

As discussed in section

\textbf{A}(u)\nabla u\cdot\textbf{n}=\nabla u\cdot\textbf{A}(u)^{T}\textbf{n},

of the nonlinear diffusion (

as the summation of the product of individual nonlinear diffusion matrix and the gradient of its state variable. Then, the new DDGIC scheme for 2-D compressible NS equations will be a straightforward extension of (
10
). Let’s use the x-momentum equation to illustrate the symbolic deduction. The second row of
(\textbf{f}_{v}(\textbf{Q},\nabla\textbf{Q}),\textbf{g}_{v}(\textbf{Q},\nabla\textbf{Q}))
consists of the stress tensor components
(\tau_{11},\tau_{12})
that can be decomposed into and laid out in column vector format as

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\tau_{11}\\
\tau_{12}\end{pmatrix}=\mu\begin{pmatrix}\frac{4}{3}u_{x}-\frac{2}{3}v_{y}\\
u_{y}+v_{x}\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\mu\begin{pmatrix}\frac{4}{3}u_{x}\\
u_{y}\end{pmatrix}+\mu\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{2}{3}v_{y}\\
v_{x}\end{pmatrix}=\mu\begin{pmatrix}\frac{4}{3}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\nabla u+\mu\begin{pmatrix}0&-\frac{2}{3}\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\nabla v

\displaystyle=-\frac{\mu}{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{4}{3}u&-\frac{2}{3}v\\
v&u\end{pmatrix}\nabla\rho+\frac{\mu}{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{4}{3}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\nabla(\rho u)+\frac{\mu}{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}0&-\frac{2}{3}\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}\nabla(\rho v).

We use the standard ordering and designate the x-momentum equation as the
second
equation, and adopt
\rho=\textbf{Q}^{(1)}
,
\rho u=\textbf{Q}^{(2)}
,
\rho v=\textbf{Q}^{(3)}
, and
E=\textbf{Q}^{(4)}
as the
first
,
second
,
third
and
fourth
conserved variables. For the x-momentum equation, we introduce the following nonlinear diffusion matrices:

\textbf{A}^{(21)}(\textbf{Q})=-\frac{\mu}{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{4}{3}u&-\frac{2}{3}v\\
v&u\end{pmatrix},\hskip 11.38092pt\textbf{A}^{(22)}(\textbf{Q})=\frac{\mu}{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{4}{3}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\hskip 11.38092pt\textbf{A}^{(23)}(\textbf{Q})=\frac{\mu}{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}0&-\frac{2}{3}\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},

(14)

and the corresponding viscous term can be rewritten as

\begin{pmatrix}\tau_{11}\\
\tau_{12}\end{pmatrix}=\mu\begin{pmatrix}\frac{4}{3}u_{x}-\frac{2}{3}v_{y}\\
u_{y}+v_{x}\end{pmatrix}=\textbf{A}^{(21)}(\textbf{Q})\nabla\rho+\textbf{A}^{(22)}(\textbf{Q})\nabla(\rho u)+\textbf{A}^{(23)}(\textbf{Q})\nabla(\rho v).

(15)

Similar to the scalar nonlinear diffusion equation, we explore the adjoint property of vector inner product

\textbf{A}^{(2m)}(\textbf{Q})\nabla\textbf{Q}^{(m)}\cdot\textbf{n}=\nabla\textbf{Q}^{(m)}\cdot\textbf{A}^{(2m)}(\textbf{Q})^{T}\textbf{n},\quad\mbox{for}\,\,m=1,2,3.

Correspondingly, the following new direction vectors are defined

\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(2m)}(\textbf{Q})=\textbf{A}^{(2m)}(\textbf{Q})^{T}\textbf{n},

(16)

and these lead to

\textbf{A}^{(2m)}(\textbf{Q})\nabla\textbf{Q}^{(m)}\cdot\textbf{n}=\nabla\textbf{Q}^{(m)}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(2m)}(\textbf{Q})\quad\mbox{for}\,\,m=1,2,3.

(17)

Since there are no viscous terms for the continuity equation, we have
\textbf{A}^{(1m)}(\textbf{Q})=\bf 0
with
m=1,\cdots,4
. For the y-momentum and the total energy equations, we refer to
A
where a complete description of the diffusion matrices
\textbf{A}^{(3m)}(\textbf{Q})
and
\textbf{A}^{(4m)}(\textbf{Q})
for
m=1,\cdots,4
is given, respectively.

Remark 2
.

The viscous numerical flux
\widehat{\textbf{F}_{v}}\cdot\textbf{n}
can be computed by the original DDG numerical flux formula (
6
) for each
\widehat{\textbf{A}^{(lm)}(\textbf{Q}_{h})\nabla\textbf{Q}_{h}^{(m)}}
term if and only if the compatibility condition

\frac{\partial\textbf{B}^{(l)}(\textbf{Q})}{\partial\textbf{Q}^{(m)}}=\textbf{A}^{(lm)}(\textbf{Q})\quad\mbox{for}\,\,m=1,\cdots,4

(18)

is satisfied. Here,
\textbf{B}^{(l)}(\textbf{Q})
is the antiderivative matrix corresponding to the diffusion matrices
\textbf{A}^{(lm)}(\textbf{Q})
.

Remark 3
.

For 2-D compressible NS equations, the diffusion matrices corresponding to x- and y-momentum equations of 2-D compressible NS equations are compatible, i.e.
\textbf{B}^{(2)}(\textbf{Q})
and
\textbf{B}^{(3)}(\textbf{Q})
exist. However, the matrix antiderivative
\textbf{B}^{(4)}(\textbf{Q})
does not exist for the energy equation. Therefore, the energy equation is not compatible and the original DDG method
[
28
]
cannot be used for 2-D compressible NS equation. Thus, another route will have to be taken.

2.2.2
The new DDGIC scheme formulation for 2-D compressible Navier-Stokes

We now present the derivation of the new DDGIC method for 2-D NS equations. For the sake of simplicity, we follow an equation-by-equation approach. Since there are no diffusion terms in the continuity equation, we begin by the x-momentum equation. The main idea of the new direct DG method is based on the adjoint property of vector inner product. Motivated by the continuous level adjoint property (
17
) with the new direction vector
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(2m)}(\textbf{Q})
in (
16
) and the corresponding diffusion matrices
\textbf{A}^{(2m)}(\textbf{Q})
in (
14
), the viscous numerical flux for x-momentum equation in (
12
) is defined as

\widehat{\textbf{F}^{(2)}_{v}}\cdot\textbf{n}=\mu\widehat{\begin{pmatrix}\frac{4}{3}u_{x}-\frac{2}{3}v_{y}\\
u_{y}+v_{x}\end{pmatrix}}\cdot\textbf{n}=\widehat{\nabla\rho_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(21)}(\{\!\!\{\textbf{Q}_{h}\}\!\!\})+\widehat{\nabla(\rho u)_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(22)}(\{\!\!\{\textbf{Q}_{h}\}\!\!\})+\widehat{\nabla(\rho v)_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(23)}(\{\!\!\{\textbf{Q}_{h}\}\!\!\}).

(19)

Following the adjoint property, the new direction vectors are defined as below

\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(2m)}(\{\!\!\{\textbf{Q}_{h}\}\!\!\}))=\textbf{A}^{(2m)}(\{\!\!\{\textbf{Q}_{h}\}\!\!\})^{T}\textbf{n},\quad m=1,2,3,\quad\forall\partial K.

(20)

Notice the notation
\{\!\!\{\textbf{Q}_{h}\}\!\!\}=\frac{\textbf{Q}^{-}_{h}+\textbf{Q}^{+}_{h}}{2}
denotes the solution average on
\partial K
with
\textbf{Q}_{h}^{-}
and
\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}
representing the values of the solution polynomial evaluated from the interior and exterior of the current cell
K
. The simple numerical flux formula of the direct DG method is then employed to compute
\widehat{\nabla\rho_{h}}=\widehat{\nabla\textbf{Q}_{h}^{(1)}}
,
\widehat{\nabla(\rho u)_{h}}=\widehat{\nabla\textbf{Q}_{h}^{(2)}}
,
\widehat{\nabla(\rho v)_{h}}=\widehat{\nabla\textbf{Q}_{h}^{(3)}}
and
\widehat{\nabla E_{h}}=\widehat{\nabla\textbf{Q}_{h}^{(4)}}
on
\partial K
as follows

\begin{aligned} \widehat{\partial_{x}\textbf{Q}^{(m)}_{h}}&=\frac{\beta_{0}}{h_{e}}\llbracket\textbf{Q}_{h}^{(m)}\rrbracket n_{1}+\{\!\!\{\partial_{x}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{(m)}\}\!\!\}+\beta_{1}h_{e}\llbracket\partial_{x}\partial_{x}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{(m)}n_{1}+\partial_{y}\partial_{x}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{(m)}n_{2}\rrbracket\\
\widehat{\partial_{y}\textbf{Q}^{(m)}_{h}}&=\frac{\beta_{0}}{h_{e}}\llbracket\textbf{Q}_{h}^{(m)}\rrbracket n_{2}+\{\!\!\{\partial_{y}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{(m)}\}\!\!\}+\beta_{1}h_{e}\llbracket\partial_{x}\partial_{y}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{(m)}n_{1}+\partial_{y}\partial_{y}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{(m)}n_{2}\rrbracket\end{aligned},\quad m=1,2,3,4,

(21)

which are the vector counterparts of the numerical flux (
9
) in the scalar case. Repeating the same process for y-momentum and total energy equations shows that this structure is maintained.
That is, the following new direction vectors are introduced on
\partial K

\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(lm)}=\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(lm)}(\{\!\!\{\textbf{Q}_{h}\}\!\!\})=\textbf{A}^{(lm)}(\{\!\!\{\textbf{Q}_{h}\}\!\!\})^{T}\textbf{n},\quad l=2,3,4,\quad m=1,\cdots,4,

(22)

and then employed along with the numerical flux formula (
21
) to complete the computation of the viscous numerical flux
\widehat{\textbf{F}_{v}}\cdot\textbf{n}
. Again, the reader is referred to
A
for a full description of the diffusion matrices
\textbf{A}^{(lm)}
.

It should be noted that the adjoint property (
22
) allows the essentially difficult nonlinear part of the numerical flux to be dealt with the direction vectors
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(lm)}
. Then, it is combined with the simple numerical flux formula of the direct DG method to approximate the gradient of conserved variables. In summary, the new DDGIC method defines multiple individual diffusion processes, each of which is combined to calculate the viscous numerical fluxes for 2-D compressible NS equations as

\widehat{\textbf{F}_{v}}(\textbf{Q}_{h},\nabla\textbf{Q}_{h})\cdot\textbf{n}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\widehat{\nabla\rho_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(21)}+\widehat{\nabla(\rho u)_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(22)}+\widehat{\nabla(\rho v)_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(23)}\\
\widehat{\nabla\rho_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(31)}+\widehat{\nabla(\rho u)_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(32)}+\widehat{\nabla(\rho v)_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(33)}\\
\widehat{\nabla\rho_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(41)}+\widehat{\nabla(\rho u)_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(42)}+\widehat{\nabla(\rho v)_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(43)}+\widehat{\nabla E_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(44)}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(23)

Similarly, the interface correction term can be simplified and computed as follows

\textbf{I}_{corr}(\textbf{Q}_{h},\phi_{j})\cdot\textbf{n}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\left(\llbracket\rho_{h}\rrbracket\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(21)}+\llbracket(\rho u)_{h}\rrbracket\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(22)}+\llbracket(\rho v)_{h}\rrbracket\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(23)}\right)\cdot\nabla\phi_{j}(x,y)\\
\left(\llbracket\rho_{h}\rrbracket\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(31)}+\llbracket(\rho u)_{h}\rrbracket\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(32)}+\llbracket(\rho v)_{h}\rrbracket\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(33)}\right)\cdot\nabla\phi_{j}(x,y)\\
\left(\llbracket\rho_{h}\rrbracket\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(41)}+\llbracket(\rho u)_{h}\rrbracket\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(42)}+\llbracket(\rho v)_{h}\rrbracket\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(43)}+\llbracket E_{h}\rrbracket\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(44)}\right)\cdot\nabla\phi_{j}(x,y)\end{pmatrix}.

(24)

Finally, we write the new DDGIC scheme for 2-D compressible NS equations

\displaystyle\frac{d\hat{\textbf{Q}}_{j}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\left(\textbf{F}_{c}(\textbf{Q}_{h})-\textbf{F}_{v}(\textbf{Q}_{h},\nabla\textbf{Q}_{h})\right)\cdot\nabla\phi_{j}(x,y)\;dxdy

(25)

\displaystyle-\int_{\partial K}\left(\widehat{\textbf{F}_{c}}(\textbf{Q}_{h})-\widehat{\textbf{F}_{v}}(\textbf{Q}_{h},\nabla\textbf{Q}_{h})\right)\cdot\phi_{j}(x,y)\textbf{n}~ds-\int_{\partial K}\textbf{I}_{corr}(\textbf{Q}_{h},\phi_{j})\cdot\textbf{n}~ds,\,\,\forall K\in T_{h}

One advantage of the proposed approach is that its implementation is very convenient and straightforward. It allows reuse of the code when simulating different equations, i.e turbulence simulations. The new DDG method only requires the computation of the numerical fluxes of the conserved variables’ gradients, which are completely independent of the governing equation. The information pertaining to the governing equation enters the numerical flux through the direction vectors
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(lm)}
. Therefore, the new DDGIC method puts forth a general framework for applying DDGIC method for general conservation laws with nonlinear diffusion.

Remark 4
.

For a general system of conservation laws of
N
equations with nonlinear diffusion, viscous numerical flux can be approximated as

\widehat{\textbf{F}_{v}}(\textbf{Q}_{h},\nabla\textbf{Q}_{h})\cdot\textbf{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{\nabla\textbf{Q}^{(n)}_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(1n)}(\{\!\!\{\textbf{Q}_{h}\}\!\!\})\\
\widehat{\nabla\textbf{Q}^{(n)}_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(2n)}(\{\!\!\{\textbf{Q}_{h}\}\!\!\})\\
\vdots\\
\widehat{\nabla\textbf{Q}^{(n)}_{h}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(Nn)}(\{\!\!\{\textbf{Q}_{h}\}\!\!\})\end{pmatrix},

while the interface correction term is given by

\textbf{I}_{corr}(\textbf{Q}_{h},\phi_{j})\cdot\textbf{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\llbracket\nabla\textbf{Q}^{(n)}_{h}\rrbracket\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(1n)}(\{\!\!\{\textbf{Q}_{h}\}\!\!\})\cdot\nabla\phi_{j}\\
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(2n)}(\{\!\!\{\textbf{Q}_{h}\}\!\!\})\cdot\nabla\phi_{j}\\
\vdots\\
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{(Nn)}(\{\!\!\{\textbf{Q}_{h}\}\!\!\})\cdot\nabla\phi_{j}\end{pmatrix}

2.3
Boundary Conditions

In this section, we describe the boundary condition implementation with the new DDGIC method. All boundary conditions are enforced weakly through the numerical fluxes of (
13
) and (
23
) and the interface correction (
24
).

For any edge
\partial K
falling on the domain boundary
\partial\Omega
, we denote the interior state by the superscript
-
and the ghost state by
+
. That is,
\textbf{Q}_{h}^{-},\partial_{x_{i}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{-}
and
\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{-}
are calculated from the corresponding interior element using the solution polynomial. On the other hand,
\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+},\partial_{x_{i}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}
and
\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}
are computed based on the particular boundary condition type, which are discussed below.

2.3.1
Periodic boundary

We simply take the solution and derivatives from the interior state of the corresponding periodic boundary. For instance, if the solution is periodic at
(x,y)
with
\textbf{Q}(x,y)=\textbf{Q}(x+L_{x},y+L_{y})
, then we set

\displaystyle\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\textbf{Q}_{h}^{-}(x+L_{x},y+L_{y}),

(26)

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\partial_{x_{i}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{-}(x+L_{x},y+L_{y}),

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{-}(x+L_{x},y+L_{y}).

2.3.2
Inflow and farfield boundaries

\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}
is determined from the free-stream conditions while
\partial_{x_{i}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}
and
\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}
are set equal to the corresponding interior values.

\textbf{Q}^{+}_{h}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{\infty}\\
\rho_{\infty}u_{\infty}\\
\rho_{\infty}v_{\infty}\\
\frac{p_{\infty}}{\gamma-1}+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{\infty}(u_{\infty}^{2}+v_{\infty}^{2})\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad\partial_{x_{i}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}=\partial_{x_{i}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{-},\quad\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}=\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{-}.

(27)

Remark 5
.

Note that this boundary condition is well-posed since the characteristic wave directions are taken into account by the local Lax-Friedrichs flux
(
13
). We refer to
[
46
]
for more details.

Remark 6
.

Since the first and second derivatives of the ghost state in (
27
) are the same as those of the interior state, the numerical flux (
21
) degenerates into

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial_{x}\textbf{Q}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta_{0}}{h_{e}}\llbracket\textbf{Q}_{h}\rrbracket n_{1}+\partial_{x}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{-},

(28)

\displaystyle\widehat{\partial_{y}\textbf{Q}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta_{0}}{h_{e}}\llbracket\textbf{Q}_{h}\rrbracket n_{2}+\partial_{y}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{-}.

2.3.3
Outflow boundary

\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}
is determined by the partially non-reflecting pressure outflow (PNR) method described in
[
46
]
while
\partial_{x_{i}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}
and
\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}
are set equal to the corresponding interior values.

\textbf{Q}^{+}_{h}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{h}^{-}\\
(\rho u)_{h}^{-}\\
(\rho v)_{h}^{-}\\
\frac{2p_{h}^{+}-p_{h}^{-}}{\gamma-1}+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{h}^{-}\left((u_{h}^{-})^{2}+(v_{h}^{-})^{2}\right)\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad\partial_{x_{i}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}=\partial_{x_{i}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{-},\quad\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}=\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{-}.

(29)

2.3.4
Adiabatic viscous wall

\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}
is computed by assuming no-slip velocity
(u_{h}^{+},v_{h}^{+})^{T}=(0,0)^{T}
and all other variables are the same as the interior state. On the other hand,
\partial_{x_{i}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}
is computed by assuming zero heat flux at the wall. For a calorically perfect gas, the wall heat flux is zero if the normal derivative of internal energy is zero, i.e.
\partial_{n}e_{h}^{+}=0
. This is achieved by taking projection of the internal energy gradient on the wall, i.e.
\partial_{x_{i}}e_{h}^{+}=\partial_{x_{i}}e_{h}^{-}-\partial_{n}e_{h}^{-}n_{i}
. All other variables are the same as the interior state. Moreover,
\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}
is set equal to the interior second derivative.

\displaystyle\textbf{Q}^{+}_{h}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{h}^{-}\\
0\\
0\\
\frac{2p_{h}^{+}-p_{h}^{-}}{\gamma-1}+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{h}^{-}\left((u_{h}^{-})^{2}+(v_{h}^{-})^{2}\right)\\
\end{pmatrix},

(30)

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_{x_{i}}\rho_{h}^{-}\\
\partial_{x_{i}}(\rho u)_{h}^{-}\\
\partial_{x_{i}}(\rho v)_{h}^{-}\\
(\partial_{x_{i}}\rho_{h}^{-})e_{h}^{-}+\rho_{h}^{-}(\partial_{x_{i}}e_{h}^{+})+\partial_{x_{i}}\left(\rho_{h}^{-}\left[(u_{h}^{-})^{2}+(v_{h}^{-})^{2}\right]\right)\\
\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{-}.

2.3.5
Symmetry plane

\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}
is computed by determining the mirror image of the velocity with respect to the wall. That is, the normal component of the velocity vector is negated while the tangential component is kept the same. Also, all other variables are extrapolated from the interior state. Furthermore,
\partial_{x_{i}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}
is computed as the velocity vector and
\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}
is equal to the interior second derivative.

\textbf{Q}^{+}_{h}=\begin{pmatrix}\rho_{h}^{-}\\
\rho_{h}^{-}(u_{h}^{-}-2(\textbf{u}_{h}^{-}\cdot\textbf{n})n_{1})\\
\rho_{h}^{-}(v_{h}^{-}-2(\textbf{u}_{h}^{-}\cdot\textbf{n})n_{2})\\
E_{h}^{-}\\
\end{pmatrix},\quad\partial_{x_{i}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}=\partial_{x_{i}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{-}-2(\partial_{n}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{-}\cdot\textbf{n})n_{i},\quad\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{+}=\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}\textbf{Q}_{h}^{-}.

(31)

Conclusions

In this article, we have proposed a new formula to compute the nonlinear viscous numerical flux at the element edge and develop a new direct DG method with interface correction for compressible Navier-Stokes equations. The nonlinear viscous flux is decomposed into multiple individual diffusion processes and a new direction vector for each conserved variable is defined. The numerical flux formula of the original direct DG method is applied only to the conserved variable’s gradient. The concept of multiple diffusion processes has been demonstrated to be useful for extending the new scheme to general equations and turbulence models. In a sequence of numerical examples, we have illustrated the optimal
(k+1)^{th}
order convergence with high order
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
,
P_{3}
and
P_{4}
polynomial approximations. It has thus been shown that the new DDGIC method is capable of calculating the associated physical quantities accurately.

Appendix A
The diffusion matrices for 2-D compressible Navier-Stokes equations

In this section, we lay out the definitions of individual diffusion matrices
\textbf{A}^{(lm)}(\textbf{Q})
for 2-D compressible NS equations. Here,
\textbf{A}^{(lm)}(\textbf{Q})
should be understood as the diffusion matrix corresponding to the
l^{th}
equation and the
m^{th}
conserved variable.

According to the standard convention, continuity equation is the first equation, therefore we have
l=1
. Since there is no viscous terms in the continuity equation, we have that

\textbf{A}^{(11)}(\textbf{Q})=\textbf{A}^{(12)}(\textbf{Q})=\textbf{A}^{(13)}(\textbf{Q})=\textbf{A}^{(14)}(\textbf{Q})=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(35)

For the x-momentum equation, we have
l=2
and

\textbf{A}^{(21)}(\textbf{Q})=-\frac{\mu}{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{4}{3}u&-\frac{2}{3}v\\
v&u\end{pmatrix},\,\textbf{A}^{(22)}(\textbf{Q})=\frac{\mu}{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}\frac{4}{3}&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\,\textbf{A}^{(23)}(\textbf{Q})=\frac{\mu}{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}0&-\frac{2}{3}\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\,\textbf{A}^{(24)}(\textbf{Q})=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(36)

Note that the total energy does not contribute to any of the diffusion matrices. In addition, one can easily show that all diffusion matrices for the x-momentum equation are compatible with

\textbf{B}^{(2)}(\textbf{Q})=\mu\begin{pmatrix}\frac{4}{3}u&-\frac{2}{3}v\\
v&u\end{pmatrix}.

(37)

For the y-momentum equation, we have
l=3
and

\textbf{A}^{(31)}(\textbf{Q})=-\frac{\mu}{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}v&u\\
-\frac{2}{3}u&\frac{4}{3}v\end{pmatrix},\,\textbf{A}^{(32)}(\textbf{Q})=\frac{\mu}{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-\frac{2}{3}&0\end{pmatrix},\,\textbf{A}^{(33)}(\textbf{Q})=\frac{\mu}{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&\frac{4}{3}\end{pmatrix},\,\textbf{A}^{(34)}(\textbf{Q})=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(38)

As in the x-momentum equation, the total energy does not contribute to any of the diffusion matrices, and the diffusion matrices are all compatible with

\textbf{B}^{(3)}(\textbf{Q})=\mu\begin{pmatrix}v&u\\
-\frac{2}{3}u&\frac{4}{3}v\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(39)

For the total energy equation, we have
l=4
and

\displaystyle\textbf{A}^{(41)}(\textbf{Q})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu}{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}\left(\frac{\gamma}{2Pr}-\frac{4}{3}\right)u^{2}+\left(\frac{\gamma}{2Pr}-1\right)v^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{Pr}e&-\frac{1}{3}uv\\
-\frac{1}{3}uv&\left(\frac{\gamma}{2Pr}-1\right)u^{2}+\left(\frac{\gamma}{2Pr}-\frac{4}{3}\right)v^{2}-\frac{\gamma}{Pr}e\end{pmatrix},

(40)

\displaystyle\textbf{A}^{(42)}(\textbf{Q})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu}{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}\left(\frac{4}{3}-\frac{\gamma}{Pr}\right)u&v\\
-\frac{2}{3}v&\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{Pr}\right)u\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\textbf{A}^{(43)}(\textbf{Q})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu}{\rho}\begin{pmatrix}\left(1-\frac{\gamma}{Pr}\right)v&-\frac{2}{3}u\\
u&\left(\frac{4}{3}-\frac{\gamma}{Pr}\right)v\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\textbf{A}^{(44)}(\textbf{Q})

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu\gamma}{\rho Pr}\mathbb{I}.

where
\mathbb{I}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
is the identity matrix. Unlike the momentum equations,
\textbf{B}^{(4)}(\textbf{Q})
does not exist. Therefore, the diffusion matrices of the total energy equation are not compatible.

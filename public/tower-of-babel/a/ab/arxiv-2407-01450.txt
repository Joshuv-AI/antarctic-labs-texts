Two-parameter quantum groups and R R -matrices: classical types
Ian Martin and Alexander Tsymbaliuk

Abstract
. We construct finite R R -matrices for the first fundamental representation V V of two-parameter quantum groups U r , s ​ ( 𝔤 ) U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g}) for classical 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} , both through the decomposition of V ⊗ V V\otimes V into irreducibles U r , s ​ ( 𝔤 ) U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g}) -submodules as well as by evaluating the universal R R -matrix. The latter generalizes the formulas of [ Jim ] for one-parametric quantum affine algebras.

Introduction

1.1.
Summary

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Kac-Moody Lie algebra of finite type. Then, it admits a root space decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{+}
with
\mathfrak{n}^{\pm}=\oplus_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\mathbb{C}\cdot e_{\pm\alpha}
corresponding to a polarization of the root system
\Phi=\Phi^{+}\sqcup(-\Phi^{+})
. The elements
e_{\pm\alpha}
are called
root vectors
. Thus,
U(\mathfrak{g})=U(\mathfrak{n}^{-})\otimes U(\mathfrak{h})\otimes U(\mathfrak{n}^{+})
and the ordered products in
\{e_{\pm\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}
form a basis of
U(\mathfrak{n}^{\pm})
for any total order on
\Phi^{\pm}
. The root vectors can actually be normalized so that

[e_{\alpha},e_{\beta}]=e_{\alpha}e_{\beta}-e_{\beta}e_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\times}\cdot e_{\alpha+\beta}\qquad\mathrm{for\ all}\quad\alpha,\beta\in\Phi^{+}\ \ \mathrm{such\ that}\ \ \alpha+\beta\in\Phi^{+}.

This inductively recovers all root vectors from the generators
\{e_{i}\}_{i\in I}
, corresponding to simple roots
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i\in I}
. When
\mathfrak{g}
is a Kac-Moody algebra of affine type, the root subspaces corresponding to imaginary roots are no longer one-dimensional. However, the theory of such algebras and their representations are well-understood due to their alternative realization as central extensions of the loop algebras
L\bar{\mathfrak{g}}
for
\bar{\mathfrak{g}}
of finite type:

(1.2)

0\to\mathbb{C}\to\mathfrak{g}\to L\bar{\mathfrak{g}}\to 0.

For any Kac-Moody algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, Drinfeld and Jimbo simultaneously introduced the quantum groups
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
which quantize the universal enveloping algebras of
\mathfrak{g}
. Similarly to
U(\mathfrak{g})
, the quantum groups admit a triangular decomposition
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})=U_{q}(\mathfrak{n}^{-})\otimes U_{q}(\mathfrak{h})\otimes U_{q}(\mathfrak{n}^{+})
. Furthermore,
U_{q}(\mathfrak{n}^{\pm})
admit PBW-type bases

(1.3)

U_{q}(\mathfrak{n}^{\pm})\ =\bigoplus_{\gamma_{1}\geq\dots\geq\gamma_{k}\in\Phi^{+}}\mathbb{C}(q)\cdot e_{\pm\gamma_{1}}\dots e_{\pm\gamma_{k}}

formed by the ordered products of
q
-deformed root vectors

e_{\pm\alpha}\in U_{q}(\mathfrak{n}^{\pm})
, defined via Lusztig’s braid group action, which requires one to choose a reduced decomposition of the longest element
w_{0}
in the Weyl group

\mathfrak{g}
. It is well-known (
[

) that this choice precisely ensures that the order
\geq

\Phi^{+}
is convex, in the sense of Definition

q
-deformed root vectors satisfy a

are any positive roots satisfying
\alpha<\alpha+\beta<\beta
and the minimality property (
5.9
):

(1.4)

[e_{\alpha},e_{\beta}]_{q}=e_{\alpha}e_{\beta}-q^{(\alpha,\beta)}e_{\beta}e_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{Z}[q,q^{-1}]^{\times}\cdot e_{\alpha+\beta}

where
(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes the scalar product corresponding to the root system of type
\mathfrak{g}
. Therefore, the
q
-deformed root vectors can be defined (up to scalar multiple) as iterated
q

, using the combinatorics of the root system and the chosen convex order on
\Phi^{+}

There is however a purely combinatorial approach to the construction of PBW-type bases of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{n}^{\pm})
, cf. (

\ell\colon\Phi^{+}\,\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}\,\Big\{\text{standard Lyndon words in}\ I\Big\}.

We note that in the context of (

of simple roots. Furthermore, (

\alpha<\beta\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\ell(\alpha)<\ell(\beta)\text{ lexicographically}.

It was shown in
[

for all

When
\mathfrak{g}
is of finite type and

is not a root of unity, the representation theory of

is completely parallel to that of
\mathfrak{g}
. On the other hand, to develop the representation theory of

for affine

from

\Psi\colon U^{\mathrm{Dr}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\,\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}\,U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\qquad\mathrm{for\ affine}\quad\mathfrak{g}

was constructed in
[

]
using an affine braid group action, while

was stated in
[

using

-bracketings

One of the key features of quantum groups is that they are actually quasitriangular Hopf algebras. The corresponding universal
R
-matrices
\mathcal{R}\in U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
(one needs to consider a completion here) satisfy

(1.7)

\mathrm{\textbf{quantum\ Yang-Baxter\ equation}}\quad\mathcal{R}_{12}\mathcal{R}_{13}\mathcal{R}_{23}=\mathcal{R}_{23}\mathcal{R}_{13}\mathcal{R}_{12}.

In particular, for any two finite-dimensional
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules
V,W
one obtains a
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-module intertwiner

(1.8)

\hat{R}_{VW}=(\rho_{W}\otimes\rho_{V})(\mathcal{R})\circ\tau\colon V\otimes W\,\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}\,W\otimes V,

where
\tau\colon V\otimes W\to W\otimes V
is the flip map
v\otimes w\mapsto w\otimes v
, and
\rho_{V}\colon U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\to\End(V),\rho_{W}\colon U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\to\End(W)
.

In fact, quantum groups first appeared in the
quantum inverse scattering method
, the study of exactly solvable statistical models and quantum integrable systems arising through the quantum Yang-Baxter equation (
1.7
). In this context, one starts with a solution of (
1.7
) or its version with a spectral parameter

(1.9)

\mathcal{R}_{12}(x)\mathcal{R}_{13}(xy)\mathcal{R}_{23}(y)=\mathcal{R}_{23}(y)\mathcal{R}_{13}(xy)\mathcal{R}_{12}(x),

and defines the algebra
U^{\mathrm{RTT}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
via the so-called
RTT-relations
, see
[
FRT
]
. The isomorphisms

(1.10)

\Upsilon\colon U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\,\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}\,U^{\mathrm{RTT}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\qquad\mathrm{for\ finite\ type}\ \ \mathfrak{g}

and

(1.11)

\Upsilon\colon U^{\mathrm{Dr}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\,\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}\,U^{\mathrm{RTT}}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\qquad\mathrm{for\ affine\ type}\ \ \mathfrak{g}

were first constructed in
[
DF
]
for types
A_{n},A^{(1)}_{n}
through the Gauss decomposition of the generating matrices. For other classical Lie algebras and their affinizations, such isomorphisms were first discovered in
[

]
and were revised much more recently in
[

,

The theory of multiparameter quantum groups goes back to the early 90s, see e.g.
[

,
Re
,
Ta
]
. However, the current interest in the subject stems from the papers
[
BW1
]

-matrix for the first fundamental

-representation, and used it to establish the Schur-Weyl duality between
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{gl}_{n})
and a two-parameter Hecke algebra.

The above works of Benkart and Witherspoon stimulated an increased interest in the theory of two-parameter quantum groups. In particular, the definition and the basic structural results on
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
for other classical simple Lie algebras

were provided in
[

BGH2
]

[

for a more uniform treatment and complete references.

The generalization of this theory from simple finite-dimensional Lie algebras to affine Lie algebras started with the work

(which however had some gaps in the exposition, see

for an alternative treatment of the PBW results stated in

was established on a case-by-case basis for

being of type

(see

), types

and

(see

of (

In this work (followed up by

is an orthogonal or symplectic Lie algebra. The present note is mostly concerned with the derivation of finite and affine

To derive the finite
R
-matrix
\hat{R}_{VV}
, we factorize the universal
R
-matrix into the “local” ones (one factor for each positive root of

) and then evaluate the result on the first fundamental representation

of

. Due to the absence of Lusztig’s braid group action on

(noted first in

through standard Lyndon words, which goes back to
[

,

,

,

in the one-parameter setup, to

in the super case, and to

for the two-parameter case. We note, however, that once the explicit formula is obtained, one can directly check that it coincides with the

by verifying that it intertwines the

-action on

and acts by the same scalars on the highest weight vectors of

To derive the affine

2.
Notations and definitions

Throughout the paper, we will work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}\supset\mathbb{C}(r,s)
, where
r
and
s
are indeterminates.

2.1.
Two-parameter finite quantum groups

Let
E
be a Euclidean space with a symmetric bilinear form
(\cdot,\cdot)
, and
\Phi\subset E
be an indecomposable reduced root system with an ordered set of simple roots
\Pi=\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the complex simple Lie algebra corresponding to this root system. Let
C=(c_{ij})_{i,j=1}^{n}
be the Cartan matrix of
\mathfrak{g}
, explicitly given by
c_{ij}=\frac{2(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})}
, and let
d_{i}=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i})
where
(\cdot,\cdot)
is normalized so that the short roots have square length
2
. We denote the root and weight lattices of
\mathfrak{g}
by
Q
and
P
, respectively:

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}\alpha_{i}=Q\subset P=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{Z}\varpi_{i}\qquad\mathrm{with}\quad(\alpha_{i},\varpi_{j})=d_{i}\delta_{ij}.

Having fixed above the order on the set of simple roots
\Pi
, we consider the (modified) Ringel bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
Q
, such that (unless
\{i,j\}=\{n-1,n\}
in type
D_{n}
) we have:

(2.1)

\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle=\begin{cases}d_{i}c_{ij}&\text{if}\ \ i<j\\
d_{i}&\text{if}\ \ i=j\\
0&\text{if}\ \ i>j\end{cases}\,,

while in the remaining case of
D_{n}
-type system, we set:

(2.2)

\langle\alpha_{n-1},\alpha_{n}\rangle=\langle\varepsilon_{n-1}-\varepsilon_{n},\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n}\rangle=-1,\qquad\langle\alpha_{n},\alpha_{n-1}\rangle=\langle\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n},\varepsilon_{n-1}-\varepsilon_{n}\rangle=1.

We note that
(\mu,\nu)=\langle\mu,\nu\rangle+\langle\nu,\mu\rangle
for any
\mu,\nu\in Q
.

Remark 2.1
.

The modification (
2.2
) is made to ensure that the algebra
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{so}_{2n})
defined below matches its original definition in
[
BGH1
]
.

We shall also need two-parameter analogues of
q
-integers and
q
-factorials (cf.
[
BW2
, (2.2)]
):

[m]_{r,s}=\frac{r^{m}-s^{m}}{r-s}=r^{m-1}+r^{m-2}s+\ldots+rs^{m-2}+s^{m-1}\quad\mathrm{for\ all}\quad m\in\mathbb{N},

[m]_{r,s}!=[m]_{r,s}[m-1]_{r,s}\cdots[1]_{r,s}\qquad\mathrm{for}\quad m>0,\qquad[0]_{r,s}!=1,

and most importantly two-parameter analogues of Gaussian binomial coefficients:

(2.3)

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{m}{k}_{r,s}=\frac{[m]_{r,s}!}{[m-k]_{r,s}![k]_{r,s}!}\qquad\mathrm{for\ all}\quad 0\leq k\leq m.

Finally, in analogy with the one-parameter case (see
[
Jan
, §4.2]
), we define

(2.4)

\begin{split}&r_{\gamma}=r^{(\gamma,\gamma)/2},\ \qquad s_{\gamma}=s^{(\gamma,\gamma)/2}\qquad\mathrm{for\ all}\quad\gamma\in\Phi,\\
&r_{i}=r_{\alpha_{i}}=r^{d_{i}},\qquad s_{i}=s_{\alpha_{i}}=s^{d_{i}}\qquad\mathrm{for\ all}\quad 1\leq i\leq n.\end{split}

We are ready now to introduce the main actor of this paper, the two-parameter quantum group of
\mathfrak{g}
, following
[

]
. While this definition is uniform for all types, we will make it more explicit for classical Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
in Subsection

.

Definition 2.2
.

The
two-parameter quantum group

U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
of a simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is the associative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra generated by
\{e_{i},f_{i},\omega_{i}^{\pm 1},(\omega_{i}^{\prime})^{\pm 1}\}_{i=1}^{n}
with the following defining relations (for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
):

(2.5)

[\omega_{i},\omega_{j}]=[\omega_{i},\omega^{\prime}_{j}]=[\omega^{\prime}_{i},\omega^{\prime}_{j}]=0,\qquad\omega_{i}^{\pm 1}\omega_{i}^{\mp 1}=1=(\omega_{i}^{\prime})^{\pm 1}(\omega_{i}^{\prime})^{\mp 1},

(2.6)

\omega_{i}e_{j}=r^{\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha_{i}\rangle}s^{-\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle}e_{j}\omega_{i},\qquad\omega_{i}f_{j}=r^{-\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha_{i}\rangle}s^{\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle}f_{j}\omega_{i},

(2.7)

\omega_{i}^{\prime}e_{j}=r^{-\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle}s^{\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha_{i}\rangle}e_{j}\omega_{i}^{\prime},\qquad\omega_{i}^{\prime}f_{j}=r^{\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle}s^{-\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha_{i}\rangle}f_{j}\omega_{i}^{\prime},

(2.8)

e_{i}f_{j}-f_{j}e_{i}=\delta_{ij}\frac{\omega_{i}-\omega^{\prime}_{i}}{r_{i}-s_{i}},

and quantum
(r,s)
-Serre relations

(2.9)

\begin{split}&\sum_{k=0}^{1-c_{ij}}(-1)^{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{1 - c_{ij}}{k}_{r_{i},s_{i}}(r_{i}s_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}k(k-1)}(rs)^{k\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha_{i}\rangle}e_{i}^{1-c_{ij}-k}e_{j}e_{i}^{k}=0\qquad\forall\,i\neq j,\\
&\sum_{k=0}^{1-c_{ij}}(-1)^{k}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{1 - c_{ij}}{k}_{r_{i},s_{i}}(r_{i}s_{i})^{\frac{1}{2}k(k-1)}(rs)^{k\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha_{i}\rangle}f_{i}^{1-c_{ij}-k}f_{j}f_{i}^{k}=0\qquad\forall\,i\neq j.\end{split}

Remark 2.3
.

We note that this definition does depend on the choice of an order of
\Pi
. We shall make a standard choice for classical
\mathfrak{g}
in the end of this section, thus matching the rest of the literature
[
BW1
,
BGH1
]
.

We note that the algebra
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
is
Q
-graded via

\deg(e_{i})=\alpha_{i},\quad\deg(f_{i})=-\alpha_{i},\quad\deg(\omega_{i})=0,\quad\deg(\omega_{i}^{\prime})=0\qquad\mathrm{for\ all}\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

For
\mu\in Q
, let
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})_{\mu}
(or simply
(U_{r,s})_{\mu}
) denote the degree
\mu
component of
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
under this
Q
-grading.

Analogously to the one-parameter case (cf.
[
Jan
, §4.11]
), there is a Hopf algebra structure on
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
, where the coproduct
\Delta
, counit
\epsilon
, and antipode
S
are defined on generators by the following formulas:

\displaystyle\Delta(\omega_{i}^{\pm 1})=\omega_{i}^{\pm 1}\otimes\omega_{i}^{\pm 1}

\displaystyle\epsilon(\omega_{i}^{\pm 1})=1

\displaystyle S(\omega_{i}^{\pm 1})=\omega_{i}^{\mp 1}

\displaystyle\Delta((\omega_{i}^{\prime})^{\pm 1})=(\omega_{i}^{\prime})^{\pm 1}\otimes(\omega_{i}^{\prime})^{\pm 1}

\displaystyle\epsilon((\omega_{i}^{\prime})^{\pm 1})=1

\displaystyle S((\omega_{i}^{\prime})^{\pm 1})=(\omega_{i}^{\prime})^{\mp 1}

\displaystyle\Delta(e_{i})=e_{i}\otimes 1+\omega_{i}\otimes e_{i}

\displaystyle\epsilon(e_{i})=0

\displaystyle S(e_{i})=-\omega_{i}^{-1}e_{i}

\displaystyle\Delta(f_{i})=1\otimes f_{i}+f_{i}\otimes\omega_{i}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\epsilon(f_{i})=0

\displaystyle S(f_{i})=-f_{i}(\omega_{i}^{\prime})^{-1}

Remark 2.4
.

The simplest way to see how the definition above generalizes the usual Drinfeld-Jimbo one-parametric quantum groups
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
(cf.
[
Jan
, §4]
) is to work in the numeric setup. To this end, let
r,s\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
with
r^{2}\neq s^{2}
and define
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
as in Definition
2.2
, but now viewed as an algebra over
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
. Then, for any
q\in\mathbb{C}
with
q^{4}\neq 1
, there is a natural Hopf algebra epimorphism

\pi\colon U_{q,q^{-1}}(\mathfrak{g})\twoheadrightarrow U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})\quad\mathrm{given\ by}\quad e_{i}\mapsto E_{i},\ f_{i}\mapsto F_{i},\ \omega_{i}\mapsto K_{i},\ \omega_{i}^{\prime}\mapsto K_{i}^{-1}\quad\mathrm{for\ all}\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

Moreover, the kernel of
\pi
is the two-sided ideal
\mathfrak{I}
generated by
\{\omega^{\prime}_{i}-\omega_{i}^{-1}\}_{i=1}^{n}
. Thus, we get:

(2.10)

\mathrm{Hopf\ algebra\ isomorphism}\quad U_{q,q^{-1}}(\mathfrak{g})/\mathfrak{I}\,\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}\,U_{q}(\mathfrak{g}).

We shall also realize
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
(with
q=r^{1/2}s^{-1/2}
) as a subalgebra of an extension of
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
in Appendix
A
.

Let us also define several subalgebras of
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
:

•

the “positive” subalgebra
U_{r,s}^{+}(\mathfrak{g})
, generated by
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
,

•

the “negative” subalgebra
U_{r,s}^{-}(\mathfrak{g})
, generated by
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
,

•

the “Cartan” subalgebra
U_{r,s}^{0}(\mathfrak{g})
, generated by

\{\omega_{i}^{\pm 1},(\omega^{\prime}_{i})^{\pm 1}\}_{i=1}^{n}
,

•

the “non-negative subalgebra”
U_{r,s}^{\geq}(\mathfrak{g})
, generated by

\{e_{i},\omega_{i}^{\pm 1}\}_{i=1}^{n}
,

•

the “non-positive subalgebra”
U_{r,s}^{\leq}(\mathfrak{g})
, generated by

\{f_{i},(\omega^{\prime}_{i})^{\pm 1}\}_{i=1}^{n}
.

When
\mathfrak{g}
is clear from the context, we will use
U_{r,s}
instead of
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
, and similarly for the above subalgebras.

For any
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}\alpha_{i}\in Q
, we define
\omega_{\mu},\omega^{\prime}_{\mu}\in U_{r,s}^{0}(\mathfrak{g})
via:

\omega_{\mu}=\omega_{1}^{k_{1}}\omega_{2}^{k_{2}}\cdots\omega_{n}^{k_{n}},\qquad\omega^{\prime}_{\mu}=(\omega_{1}^{\prime})^{k_{1}}(\omega_{2}^{\prime})^{k_{2}}\cdots(\omega_{n}^{\prime})^{k_{n}}.

2.2.
Hopf pairing

One of the basic structural properties of
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
is that it may be realized as a Drinfel’d double of its subalgebras
U^{\leq}_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
and
U^{\geq}_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
with respect to the Hopf algebra pairing. This was established case-by-case in the literature, and we shall rather just refer to
[
HP
]
:

Proposition 2.5
.

There exists a unique bilinear pairing

(2.11)

(\cdot,\cdot)\colon U_{r,s}^{\leq}(\mathfrak{g})\times U_{r,s}^{\geq}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathbb{K}

satisfying the following structural properties

(2.12)

(yy^{\prime},x)=(y\otimes y^{\prime},\Delta(x)),\qquad(y,xx^{\prime})=(\Delta(y),x^{\prime}\otimes x)\qquad\forall\,x,x^{\prime}\in U_{r,s}^{\geq}(\mathfrak{g}),\ y,y^{\prime}\in U_{r,s}^{\leq}(\mathfrak{g}),

as well as being given on the generators by:

(2.13)

\begin{split}&(f_{i},\omega_{j})=0,\qquad(\omega_{i}^{\prime},e_{i})=0,\qquad(f_{i},e_{j})=\delta_{ij}\frac{1}{s_{i}-r_{i}}\qquad\mathrm{for\ all}\quad 1\leq i,j\leq n,\\
&(\omega_{\lambda}^{\prime},\omega_{\mu})=r^{\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle}s^{-\langle\mu,\lambda\rangle}\qquad\mathrm{for\ all}\quad\lambda,\mu\in Q.\end{split}

We can also formally extend this pairing to the weight lattice
P
as follows:

(2.14)

(\omega_{\lambda}^{\prime},\omega_{\mu})=\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}(\omega_{i}^{\prime},\omega_{j})^{\lambda_{i}\mu_{j}}\qquad\mathrm{for\ any\ weights}\ \ \lambda=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\alpha_{i}\in P,\ \mu=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mu_{i}\alpha_{i}\in P.

Although
\lambda_{i},\mu_{i}
may not be integers, the expression above still makes sense because
\mathbb{K}
is algebraically closed.

Remark 2.6
.

As mentioned in the beginning of this Subsection, the above pairing allows for the realization of any two-parameter quantum group
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
as a Drinfel’d double of its Hopf subalgebras
U^{\leq}_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
,
U^{\geq}_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
with respect to the pairing
(\cdot,\cdot)
of (
2.11
).

Let us list several basic properties of this pairing that will be needed later.

•

First, if
x\in U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})_{\mu}
and
\nu\in Q
, then we have:

(2.15)

\omega_{\nu}x\omega_{\nu}^{-1}=(\omega_{\mu}^{\prime},\omega_{\nu})x,\qquad\omega_{\nu}^{\prime}x(\omega_{\nu}^{\prime})^{-1}=(\omega_{\nu}^{\prime},\omega_{\mu})^{-1}x.

•

Second, the pairing
(\cdot,\cdot)
is of homogeneous degree zero, i.e.

(2.16)

(y,x)=0\quad\mathrm{for}\quad x\in U_{r,s}^{\geq}(\mathfrak{g})_{\mu},\ y\in U_{r,s}^{\leq}(\mathfrak{g})_{-\nu}\quad\mathrm{with}\quad\mu\neq\nu.

•

Third, similarly to the one-parameter case (cf.
[
Jan
, §6.14–6.15]
), we have

(2.17)

\Delta(x)\in x\otimes 1+\bigoplus_{0<\nu<\mu}U_{r,s}^{+}(\mathfrak{g})_{\mu-\nu}\omega_{\nu}\otimes U_{r,s}^{+}(\mathfrak{g})_{\nu}+\omega_{\mu}\otimes x,

(2.18)

\Delta(y)\in y\otimes\omega_{\mu}^{\prime}+\bigoplus_{0<\nu<\mu}U_{r,s}^{-}(\mathfrak{g})_{-\nu}\otimes U_{r,s}^{-}(\mathfrak{g})_{-(\mu-\nu)}\omega_{\nu}^{\prime}+1\otimes y,

for any
x\in U_{r,s}^{+}(\mathfrak{g})_{\mu}
and
y\in U_{r,s}^{-}(\mathfrak{g})_{-\mu}
. Here, we use the
standard order
\leq
on
Q
:

(2.19)

\nu\leq\mu\Longleftrightarrow\mu-\nu=\sum_{i}k_{i}\alpha_{i}\quad\mathrm{with}\quad k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Then, combining the properties (
2.16
)–(
2.18
) with the defining properties (
2.12
,
2.13
), we obtain:

(2.20)

\begin{split}&(\omega_{\mu}^{\prime}y,x)=(\omega_{\mu}^{\prime},\omega_{\nu})(y,x),\qquad(y\omega_{\mu}^{\prime},x)=(y,x),\\
&(y,\omega_{\nu}x)=(\omega_{\mu}^{\prime},\omega_{\nu})(y,x),\qquad(y,x\omega_{\nu})=(y,x),\end{split}

for any
x\in U_{r,s}^{+}(\mathfrak{g})_{\nu}
and
y\in U_{r,s}^{-}(\mathfrak{g})_{-\mu}
.

2.3.
Classical types

Since in this paper we are only interested in the classical Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}
, it will be helpful to have more explicit formulas for the bilinear form, avoiding the use of the form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
Q
defined in (
2.1
,
2.2
). To this end, let us first recall the explicit realization of the classical root systems as well as specify the choice of simple roots for them:

•

A_{n}
-type
(corresponding to
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}
).

Let
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+1}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then, we have

(2.21)

\begin{split}&\Phi_{A_{n}}=\{\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j}\,|\,1\leq i\neq j\leq n+1\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1},\\
&\Pi_{A_{n}}=\{\alpha_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{i+1}\}_{i=1}^{n}.\end{split}

•

B_{n}
-type
(corresponding to
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}
).

Let
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be an orthogonal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
(\varepsilon_{i},\varepsilon_{i})=2
for all
i
. Then, we have

(2.22)

\begin{split}&\Phi_{B_{n}}=\{\pm\varepsilon_{i}\pm\varepsilon_{j}\,|\,1\leq i<j\leq n\}\cup\{\pm\varepsilon_{i}\,|\,1\leq i\leq n\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
&\Pi_{B_{n}}=\{\alpha_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{i+1}\}_{i=1}^{n-1}\cup\{\alpha_{n}=\varepsilon_{n}\}.\end{split}

•

C_{n}
-type
(corresponding to
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
).

Let
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, we have

(2.23)

\begin{split}&\Phi_{C_{n}}=\{\pm\varepsilon_{i}\pm\varepsilon_{j}\,|\,1\leq i<j\leq n\}\cup\{\pm 2\varepsilon_{i}\,|\,1\leq i\leq n\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
&\Pi=\{\alpha_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{i+1}\}_{i=1}^{n-1}\cup\{\alpha_{n}=2\varepsilon_{n}\}.\end{split}

•

D_{n}
-type
(corresponding to
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
).

Let
\{\varepsilon_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, we have

(2.24)

\begin{split}&\Phi_{D_{n}}=\{\pm\varepsilon_{i}\pm\varepsilon_{j}\,|\,1\leq i<j\leq n\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},\\
&\Pi_{D_{n}}=\{\alpha_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{i+1}\}_{i=1}^{n-1}\cup\{\alpha_{n}=\varepsilon_{n-1}+\varepsilon_{n}\}.\end{split}

Remark 2.7
.

We note that
(\varepsilon_{i},\varepsilon_{i})=2
in type
B_{n}
in agreement with our scaling of the form
(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Then we have the following respective formulas for the pairing of Cartan elements (we note that while the second formula follows from the first one in each case, it will be convenient to use both later on):

•

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\omega_{\lambda}^{\prime},\omega_{i})&=r^{(\varepsilon_{i},\lambda)}s^{(\varepsilon_{i+1},\lambda)},\\

•

B_{n}
-type

(2.26)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\omega_{\lambda}^{\prime},\omega_{i})&=\begin{cases}r^{(\varepsilon_{i},\lambda)}s^{(\varepsilon_{i+1},\lambda)}&\mathrm{if}\ \ 1\leq i<n\\
r^{(\varepsilon_{n},\lambda)}(rs)^{-\lambda_{n}}&\mathrm{if}\ \ i=n\end{cases}\,,\\

•

C_{n}
-type

(2.27)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\omega_{\lambda}^{\prime},\omega_{i})&=\begin{cases}r^{(\varepsilon_{i},\lambda)}s^{(\varepsilon_{i+1},\lambda)}&\mathrm{if}\ \ 1\leq i<n\\

s^{-2(\varepsilon_{n},\lambda)}(rs)^{2\lambda_{n}}&\mathrm{if}\ \ i=n\end{cases}\,.\end{split}

•

-type

(2.28)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\omega_{\lambda}^{\prime},\omega_{i})&=\begin{cases}r^{(\varepsilon_{i},\lambda)}s^{(\varepsilon_{i+1},\lambda)}&\mathrm{if}\ \ 1\leq i<n\\
r^{(\varepsilon_{n-1},\lambda)}s^{-(\varepsilon_{n},\lambda)}(rs)^{-2\lambda_{n-1}}&\mathrm{if}\ \ i=n\end{cases}\,,\\
(\omega_{i}^{\prime},\omega_{\lambda})&=\begin{cases}r^{-(\varepsilon_{i+1},\lambda)}s^{-(\varepsilon_{i},\lambda)}&\mathrm{if}\ \ 1\leq i<n\\
r^{(\varepsilon_{n},\lambda)}s^{-(\varepsilon_{n-1},\lambda)}(rs)^{2\lambda_{n-1}}&\mathrm{if}\ \ i=n\end{cases}\,.\end{split}

While the

Proposition 3.1

[

\displaystyle\rho(\omega_{i})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{rE_{ii}+sE_{i+1,i+1}+r^{-1}E_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}+s^{-1}E_{(i+1)^{\prime},(i+1)^{\prime}}+\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}^{j\neq i,i+1}\left(E_{jj}+E_{j^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right)}&\mathrm{if}\ \ 1\leq i<n\\
\displaystyle{s^{-1}E_{n-1,n-1}+rE_{nn}+sE_{(n-1)^{\prime},(n-1)^{\prime}}+r^{-1}E_{n^{\prime}n^{\prime}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-2}\left(r^{-1}s^{-1}E_{jj}+rsE_{j^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right)}&\mathrm{if}\ \ i=n\\
\end{cases}\,,

\displaystyle\rho(\omega^{\prime}_{i})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{sE_{ii}+rE_{i+1,i+1}+s^{-1}E_{i^{\prime}i^{\prime}}+r^{-1}E_{(i+1)^{\prime},(i+1)^{\prime}}+\sum_{1\leq j\leq n}^{j\neq i,i+1}\left(E_{jj}+E_{j^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right)}&\mathrm{if}\ \ 1\leq i<n\\
\displaystyle{r^{-1}E_{n-1,n-1}+sE_{nn}+rE_{(n-1)^{\prime},(n-1)^{\prime}}+s^{-1}E_{n^{\prime}n^{\prime}}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-2}\left(r^{-1}s^{-1}E_{jj}+rsE_{j^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\right)}&\mathrm{if}\ \ i=n\\

Proof.

The proof is analogous to that of Proposition

3.2.
Decomposition of the tensor square

In the rest of this section, we shall study the decomposition of the tensor square
V\otimes V
into irreducible
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
-submodules. In type
A_{n}
, this has been carried out in
[
BW2
, Proposition 5.3]
:

Proposition 3.7
.

[
BW2
]

The
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
-module
V\otimes V
decomposes into the direct sum of two irreducible modules
V\otimes V\simeq L(2\varepsilon_{1})\oplus L(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2})
with the highest weight vectors
v_{1}\otimes v_{1}
and
v_{1}\otimes v_{2}-rv_{2}\otimes v_{1}
.

In contrast, we shall show in Proposition
3.9
that
V\otimes V
actually decomposes into the direct sum of three irreducible
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
-submodules in the remaining classical types
B_{n},C_{n},D_{n}
(with
n\geq 2
for types
C_{n},D_{n}
). To this end, we start by establishing the following preliminary result (which is of independent interest):

Proposition 3.8
.

If
\lambda\in P^{+}
is a dominant integral weight, then the irreducible
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
-module
L(\lambda)
of the highest weight
\lambda
has the same dimension as the corresponding irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module.

Proof.

The idea of the proof is to specialize
r
to
q
and
s
to
q^{-1}
, and then appeal to the analogous result in the one-parameter case. The argument presented below closely follows that of
[
Jan
, §5.12–5.15]
.

Let us first set up some notation. Let
M(\lambda)=U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes_{U^{\geq}_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})}\mathbb{K}
be the Verma
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
-module of highest weight
\lambda\in P^{+}
, and let
V=L(\lambda)
be its finite-dimensional irreducible quotient, with highest weight vector denoted by
v_{\lambda}
(cf.
[
BW2
,
BGH2
]
). Let
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}(r)
and
A=\mathbb{F}[s,s^{-1}]
. For any sequence
\mathcal{I}=(i_{1},\ldots,i_{k})\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}^{k}
, set
f_{\mathcal{I}}=f_{i_{1}}\cdots f_{i_{k}}
, so that
V
is spanned by all the vectors
f_{\mathcal{I}}v_{\lambda}
. Since
V
is finite-dimensional and
Q
-graded, we note that
f_{\mathcal{I}}v_{\lambda}=0
for all but finitely many sequences
\mathcal{I}
. Therefore the
A
-submodule

V_{A}=\sum_{\mathcal{I}}Af_{\mathcal{I}}v_{\lambda}

is finitely generated, and is clearly also torsion-free. Thus,
V_{A}
is a free
A
-module since
A
is a PID. Furthermore, one can easily check that the natural map
\mathbb{K}\otimes_{A}V_{A}\to V
is an isomorphism (since
\mathbb{K}
contains the field of fractions of
A
), so that
\mathrm{rk}_{A}{V_{A}}=\dim_{\mathbb{K}}{V}
. On the other hand, we have an isomorphism
\mathbb{F}\,\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}\,\mathbb{C}(q)
given by
r\mapsto q
, which may be further extended to a homomorphism
A\to\mathbb{C}(q)
via
s\mapsto q^{-1}
, making
\mathbb{C}(q)
into an
A
-module. Consider the module
\overline{V}=\mathbb{C}(q)\otimes_{A}V_{A}
, which has an obvious
\mathbb{C}(q)
-vector space structure. Note that any basis of
V_{A}
over
A
yields a basis of
\overline{V}
over
\mathbb{C}(q)
. Thus, it suffices to make
\overline{V}
into a
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-module in such a way that
\overline{V}\simeq L(\lambda)
as
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules, where
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
is the Drinfeld-Jimbo quantum group over
\mathbb{C}(q)
(as the irreducible highest weight modules over
\mathfrak{g}
and
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
have the same dimension, cf.
[
Jan
, Theorem 5.15]
).

Let us first show that
V_{A}
is actually stable under the action of all the generators
f_{i},e_{i},\omega_{i}^{\pm 1},(\omega_{i}^{\prime})^{\pm 1}
. For
f_{i}
, this is obvious. Moreover, since all the pairings
(\omega_{\mu}^{\prime},\omega_{i})^{\pm 1}
,
(\omega_{i},\omega_{\mu}^{\prime})^{\pm 1}
belong to
A
, the fact that
V_{A}
is stable under
\omega_{i}^{\pm 1},(\omega_{i}^{\prime})^{\pm 1}
follows from the fact that
V_{A}
has a weight space decomposition. Finally, to prove stability of
V_{A}
under
e_{i}
, we first note that if
v\in V_{\mu}
, then

\frac{\omega_{i}-\omega_{i}^{\prime}}{r_{i}-s_{i}}\,v=\frac{r^{\langle\mu,\alpha_{i}\rangle}s^{-\langle\alpha_{i},\mu\rangle}-r^{-\langle\alpha_{i},\mu\rangle}s^{\langle\mu,\alpha_{i}\rangle}}{r_{i}-s_{i}}\,v=(rs)^{-\langle\alpha_{i},\mu\rangle}\frac{r^{(\alpha_{i},\mu)}-s^{(\alpha_{i},\mu)}}{r_{i}-s_{i}}\,v,

which belongs to
V_{A}
since
(\alpha_{i},\mu)
is divisible by
d_{i}
. Evoking
e_{j}v_{\lambda}=0
for all
j
, we thus get:

e_{j}f_{i_{1}}\cdots f_{i_{k}}v_{\lambda}=\sum_{1\leq s\leq k}^{i_{s}=j}f_{i_{1}}\cdots f_{i_{s-1}}\frac{\omega_{i_{s}}-\omega_{i_{s}}^{\prime}}{r_{i}-s_{i}}f_{i_{s+1}}\cdots f_{i_{k}}v_{\lambda},

which is in
V_{A}
by what we have already proved.

It remains to note that the factors
r^{\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha_{i}\rangle}s^{-\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle}
and
r^{-\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\rangle}s^{\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha_{i}\rangle}
specialize to
q^{(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}
and
q^{-(\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j})}
, respectively, under the specialization
r\mapsto q,s\mapsto q^{-1}
. Therefore, the operators
e_{i}
,
f_{i}
,
\omega_{i}
,
\omega_{i}^{\prime}
on
\overline{V}
satisfy the same relations as the generators
E_{i},F_{i},K_{i},K_{i}^{-1}
of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
, so that
\overline{V}
is a
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-module. Since
\overline{V}
is also a finite-dimensional highest weight module with the highest weight
\lambda
, we have
\overline{V}\simeq L(\lambda)
as
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
-modules.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

For
\mathfrak{g}
being one of the Lie algebras
\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1},\mathfrak{sp}_{2n},\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
(where
n\geq 2
if
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
or
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
) and
V
being the corresponding
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{g})
-representation from Propositions
3.2
,
3.3
,
3.4
, we have the following decomposition into irreducibles:

(3.2)

V\otimes V\simeq L(2\varepsilon_{1})\oplus L(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2})\oplus L(0).

Proof.

We shall only present complete details for
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1}
, the other cases being analogous.

Type
B_{n}
.

Let us first show that the following are highest weight vectors for the
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1})
-action on
V\otimes V
:

(3.3)

\begin{gathered}w_{1}=v_{1}\otimes v_{1},\quad w_{2}=v_{1}\otimes v_{2}-(\omega_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{\prime},\omega_{1})v_{2}\otimes v_{1}=\begin{cases}v_{1}\otimes v_{2}-rs^{-1}v_{2}\otimes v_{1}&\text{if}\ \ n=1\\
v_{1}\otimes v_{2}-r^{2}v_{2}\otimes v_{1}&\text{if}\ \ n>1\\
\end{cases}\,,\\
w_{3}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}r^{2(i-1)}v_{i}\otimes v_{i^{\prime}}+r^{2n-1}s^{-1}v_{n+1}\otimes v_{n+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}r^{2n-1}s^{2(i-n)-1}v_{i^{\prime}}\otimes v_{i}.\end{gathered}

The equality
e_{i}(w_{1})=0
for all
i
follows immediately from:

e_{i}\cdot v_{j}=\begin{cases}v_{i}&\text{if}\ \ j=i+1\\
-v_{(i+1)^{\prime}}&\text{if}\ \ j=i^{\prime}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}\,.

Likewise, since
e_{i}(v_{2})=0
unless
i=1
and
e_{1}(v_{2})=v_{1},\omega_{1}(v_{1})=(\omega_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{\prime},\omega_{1})v_{1}
, we also obtain:

e_{i}(w_{2})=\omega_{i}(v_{1})\otimes e_{i}(v_{2})-(\omega_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{\prime},\omega_{1})e_{i}(v_{2})\otimes v_{1}=0\qquad\mathrm{for\ all}\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

It remains to check that
w_{3}
is a highest weight vector. Indeed, for
k<n
we get:

e_{k}(w_{3})=r^{2k}v_{k}\otimes v_{(k+1)^{\prime}}-r^{2n-1}s^{2(k-n)-1}v_{(k+1)^{\prime}}\otimes v_{k}-r^{2(k-1)}\omega_{k}(v_{k})\otimes v_{(k+1)^{\prime}}+r^{2n-1}s^{2(k-n)+1}\omega_{k}(v_{(k+1)^{\prime}})\otimes v_{k}\\
=r^{2k}v_{k}\otimes v_{(k+1)^{\prime}}-r^{2(k-1)}r^{2}v_{k}\otimes v_{(k+1)^{\prime}}-r^{2n-1}s^{2(k-n)-1}v_{(k+1)^{\prime}}\otimes v_{k}+r^{2n-1}s^{2(k-n)+1}s^{-2}v_{(k+1)^{\prime}}\otimes v_{k}=0,

and similarly for
k=n
we have:

e_{n}(w_{3})=r^{2n-1}s^{-1}v_{n}\otimes v_{n+1}-r^{2n-1}s^{-1}v_{n+1}\otimes v_{n}-r^{2(n-1)}\omega_{n}(v_{n})\otimes v_{n+1}+r^{2n-1}s^{-1}\omega_{n}(v_{n+1})\otimes v_{n}\\
=r^{2n-1}s^{-1}v_{n}\otimes v_{n+1}-r^{2n-1}s^{-1}v_{n+1}\otimes v_{n}-rs^{-1}\cdot r^{2(n-1)}v_{n}\otimes v_{n+1}+r^{2n-1}s^{-1}v_{n+1}\otimes v_{n}=0.

It is clear that
w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}
are linearly independent, and since
w_{1}
has weight
2\varepsilon_{1}
,
w_{2}
has weight
\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}
, and
w_{3}
has weight
0
, the result of the Proposition will follow if we can match the corresponding dimensions:

(3.4)

\dim{L(\varepsilon_{1})}+\dim{L(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2})}+\dim{L(0)}=(2n+1)^{2}.

In view of Proposition
3.8
, we can prove this by using the Weyl dimension formula. In type
B_{n}
, we have

\rho=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\alpha\,=\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(n+\tfrac{1}{2}-i\right)\varepsilon_{i},

and therefore

(\rho,\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{j})=j-i,\qquad(\rho,\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{j})=2n+1-i-j,\qquad(\rho,\varepsilon_{i})=n+\tfrac{1}{2}-i.

Consequently, we obtain:

\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}(\rho,\alpha)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(n+\tfrac{1}{2}-i\right)\right)\left(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(j-i)(2n+1-j-i)\right).

A similar computation yields

\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}(2\varepsilon_{1}+\rho,\alpha)=\left(\prod_{i=2}^{n}(1+i)(2n+2-i)(n+\tfrac{1}{2}-i)\right)\left(n+\tfrac{3}{2}\right)\left(\prod_{2\leq i<j\leq n}(j-i)(2n+1-i-j)\right),

and thus we get

\dim{L(2\varepsilon_{1})}=\frac{\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}(2\varepsilon_{1}+\rho,\alpha)}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}(\rho,\alpha)}=n(2n+3).

Similarly, we obtain:

\dim{L(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2})}=n(2n+1).

This completes the proof of the equality (
3.4
) and hence of the Proposition, since
\dim{L(0)}=1
.

Type
C_{n}
.

The highest weight vectors for
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{sp}_{2n})
-action on
V\otimes V
are:

(3.5)

\begin{gathered}w_{1}=v_{1}\otimes v_{1},\quad w_{2}=v_{1}\otimes v_{2}-(\omega_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{\prime},\omega_{1})v_{2}\otimes v_{1}=v_{1}\otimes v_{2}-rv_{2}\otimes v_{1},\\
w_{3}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(r^{i-1}v_{i}\otimes v_{i^{\prime}}-r^{n}s^{i-n-1}v_{i^{\prime}}\otimes v_{i}\right),\end{gathered}

and we have

\dim{L(2\varepsilon_{1})}=n(2n+1),\qquad\dim{L(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2})}=(2n+1)(n-1),\qquad\dim{L(0)}=1.

Type
D_{n}
.

The highest weight vectors
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{so}_{2n})
-action on
V\otimes V
are:

(3.6)

\begin{gathered}w_{1}=v_{1}\otimes v_{1},\quad w_{2}=v_{1}\otimes v_{2}-(\omega_{\varepsilon_{1}}^{\prime},\omega_{1})v_{2}\otimes v_{1}=v_{1}\otimes v_{2}-rv_{2}\otimes v_{1},\\
w_{3}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(r^{i-1}v_{i}\otimes v_{i^{\prime}}+r^{n-1}s^{i-n}v_{i^{\prime}}\otimes v_{i}\right),\end{gathered}

and we have

\dim{L(2\varepsilon_{1})}=(2n-1)(n+1),\qquad\dim{L(\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2})}=n(2n-1),\qquad\dim{L(0)}=1.

∎

4.1.

Let us first recall the general construction (

Proof of Lemma

For
w_{1}

Likewise, if

\hat{R}(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}-r^{2}v_{2}\otimes v_{1})=rsv_{2}\otimes v_{1}-rs^{-1}v_{1}\otimes v_{2}-(s^{2}-r^{2})rs^{-1}v_{2}\otimes v_{1}=-rs^{-1}(v_{1}\otimes v_{2}-r^{2}v_{2}\otimes v_{1}).

Finally, for

\begin{split}\hat{R}&\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}r^{2(i-1)}v_{i}\otimes v_{i^{\prime}}+r^{2n-1}s^{-1}v_{n+1}\otimes v_{n+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}r^{2n-1}s^{-2(n-i)-1}v_{i^{\prime}}\otimes v_{i}\right)\\

&\quad+(rs^{-1}-r^{-1}s)\sum_{i=1}^{n}(r^{2(n-i)+1}s^{2(i-n)-1}-1)r^{2n-1}s^{2(i-n)-1}v_{i^{\prime}}\otimes v_{i}\\
&\quad+(rs^{-1}-r^{-1}s)\sum_{i=1}^{n}r^{2n-1}s^{2(i-n)-1}v_{i^{\prime}}\otimes v_{i}+(rs^{-1}-r^{-1}s)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}r^{2(i-1)}s^{2(j-i)}v_{j^{\prime}}\otimes v_{j}\\
&\quad+(rs^{-1}-r^{-1}s)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{1\leq j\leq(i+1)^{\prime}}^{j\neq i}t_{j}t_{i^{\prime}}^{-1}r^{2n-1}s^{2(i-n)-1}v_{j^{\prime}}\otimes v_{j}\,,\end{split}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{1\leq j\leq(i+1)^{\prime}}^{j\neq i}t_{j}t_{i^{\prime}}^{-1}r^{2n-1}s^{2(i-n)-1}v_{j^{\prime}}\otimes v_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}r^{4n-2i}s^{2(i+j-2n-1)}v_{j^{\prime}}\otimes v_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}r^{4n-2i}s^{4(i-n)-2}v_{i^{\prime}}\otimes v_{i}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}r^{2(j-i+n)-1}s^{2(i-n)-1}v_{j}\otimes v_{j^{\prime}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}r^{4n-2i}s^{2(i-n-1)}v_{n+1}\otimes v_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(r^{2n}s^{2j-4n}[n]_{r^{2},s^{2}}-r^{4n-2j}s^{4(j-n)-2})v_{j^{\prime}}\otimes v_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}r^{2n+1}s^{1-2n}[j-1]_{r^{2},s^{2}}v_{j}\otimes v_{j^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\quad+r^{2n}s^{-2n}[n]_{r^{2},s^{2}}v_{n+1}\otimes v_{n+1}.

Thus, the coefficient of
v_{i^{\prime}}\otimes v_{i}
in
\hat{R}(w_{3})
for

equals

\displaystyle r^{2i-1}s^{-1}+(rs^{-1}-r^{-1}s)(r^{2(n-i)+1}s^{2(i-n)-1}-1)r^{2n-1}s^{2(i-n)-1}+(rs^{-1}-r^{-1}s)r^{2n-1}s^{2(i-n)-1}

\displaystyle+r^{2i-1}s^{2(i-n)-1}(r^{2(n-i)}-s^{2(n-i)})+r^{2n-1}s^{2i-4n-1}(r^{2n}-s^{2n})-r^{4n-2i-1}s^{4(i-n)-3}(r^{2}-s^{2})

&=(r^{-1}s^{-3}-r^{-3}s^{-1})E_{k^{\prime}k^{\prime}}\otimes E_{k^{\prime},(k+1)^{\prime}}+(rs^{-1}-r^{-1}s)E_{k+1,(k+1)^{\prime}}\otimes E_{(k+1)^{\prime},k}\\
&\quad+(r^{-3}s^{-1}-r^{-1}s^{-3})E_{k^{\prime}k}\otimes E_{k,(k+1)^{\prime}}+(rs-r^{-1}s^{3})E_{k+1,k}\otimes E_{kk}\\
&\quad+(r^{-1}s^{-1}-rs^{-3})E_{k+1,k^{\prime}}\otimes E_{k^{\prime}k}+(rs^{-3}-r^{-1}s^{-1})E_{k^{\prime},k+1}\otimes E_{k+1,(k+1)^{\prime}}\\
&\quad+(r^{-5}s^{-1}-r^{-3}s^{-3})E_{k^{\prime},(k+1)^{\prime}}\otimes E_{(k+1)^{\prime},(k+1)^{\prime}}+(r^{-1}s-rs^{-1})E_{k+1,k+1}\otimes E_{k+1,k}\end{split}

and

(4.14)

\begin{split}&\Delta(f_{k})R_{5}-R_{5}\Delta(f_{k})+\Delta(f_{k})R_{4}+\Delta(f_{k})R_{6}-R_{4}\Delta(f_{k})-R_{6}\Delta(f_{k})\\
&=(r^{-1}s-rs^{-1})E_{k+1,k+1}\otimes E_{k+1,k}+(r^{-1}s^{-3}-r^{-3}s^{-1})E_{k^{\prime}k^{\prime}}\otimes E_{k^{\prime},(k+1)^{\prime}}\\
&\quad+(rs-r^{-1}s^{3})E_{k+1,k}\otimes E_{kk}+(r^{-5}s^{-1}-r^{-3}s^{-3})E_{k^{\prime},(k+1)^{\prime}}\otimes E_{(k+1)^{\prime},(k+1)^{\prime}}\\
&\quad+(rs^{-3}-r^{-1}s^{-1})E_{k^{\prime},k+1}\otimes E_{k+1,(k+1)^{\prime}}+(r^{-1}s^{-1}-rs^{-3})E_{k+1,k^{\prime}}\otimes E_{k^{\prime}k}\\
&\quad+(rs^{-1}-r^{-1}s)E_{k+1,(k+1)^{\prime}}\otimes E_{(k+1)^{\prime},k}+(r^{-3}s^{-1}-r^{-1}s^{-3})E_{k^{\prime}k}\otimes E_{k,(k+1)^{\prime}}.\end{split}

The right-hand sides of (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) are obviously equal, which implies
\hat{R}\Delta(f_{k})=\Delta(f_{k})\hat{R}
for
k<n
.

The computation for
k=n
is similar. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the main results of this section, Theorems

,
4.5
,
4.6
. We present full details only for the first one, since the other two are completely analogous (details are left to the interested reader).

Proof of Theorem
4.4
.

According to Theorem
4.1
and Proposition
3.9
, the action of
\hat{R}_{VV}
on the tensor product
V\otimes V
is uniquely determined by the eigenvalues of the highest weight vectors
w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}
from (
3.3
) under its action. We shall now verify that these eigenvalues are precisely equal to
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
,
\lambda_{3}
from Lemma
4.8
.

The eigenvalue
\widetilde{\lambda}_{1}
of the
\hat{R}_{VV}
-action on
w_{1}
is equal to
f(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{1})
. Since
\varepsilon_{1}=\alpha_{1}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}
,
f(\varepsilon_{1},\alpha_{i})=(\omega^{\prime}_{i},\omega_{\varepsilon_{1}})^{-1}
, and a computation using (
2.26
) yields
(\omega^{\prime}_{1},\omega_{\varepsilon_{1}})=s^{-2}
,
(\omega^{\prime}_{n},\omega_{\varepsilon_{1}})=rs
,
(\omega^{\prime}_{i},\omega_{\varepsilon_{1}})=1
if
1<i<n
, we thus obtain
\widetilde{\lambda}_{1}=r^{-1}s=\lambda_{1}
.

The eigenvalue
\widetilde{\lambda}_{2}
of the
\hat{R}_{VV}
-action on
w_{2}
equals the coefficient of
v_{1}\otimes v_{2}
in
\hat{R}_{VV}(w_{2})
, and the latter appears only from applying
\widetilde{f}\circ\tau
to the multiple of
v_{2}\otimes v_{1}
. Thus, we have
\widetilde{\lambda}_{2}=-rs^{-1}f(\varepsilon_{1},0)=-rs^{-1}=\lambda_{2}
if
n=1
. On the other hand, if
n>1
, then
\widetilde{\lambda}_{2}=-r^{2}f(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2})
, and since
\varepsilon_{2}=\alpha_{2}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}
, a similar calculation to the one above yields
\widetilde{\lambda}_{2}=-r^{2}\cdot(rs)^{-1}=-rs^{-1}=\lambda_{2}
.

The eigenvalue
\widetilde{\lambda}_{3}
of the
\hat{R}_{VV}
-action on
w_{3}
equals the coefficient of
v_{1}\otimes v_{1^{\prime}}
in
\hat{R}_{VV}(w_{3})
. The latter appears only from applying
\widetilde{f}\circ\tau
to the multiple of
v_{1^{\prime}}\otimes v_{1}
, so that
\widetilde{\lambda}_{3}=r^{2n-1}s^{1-2n}f(\varepsilon_{1},-\varepsilon_{1})
. As
f(\varepsilon_{1},-\varepsilon_{1})=f(\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{1})^{-1}=rs^{-1}
, we thus get
\widetilde{\lambda}_{3}=r^{2n}s^{-2n}=\lambda_{3}
.
∎

5.1.
Standard Lyndon words

Let
I=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
be a finite ordered alphabet parametrizing the simple roots of
\mathfrak{g}
, and let
I^{*}
be the set of all finite length words in the alphabet
I
. For
u=[i_{1}\dots i_{k}]\in I^{*}
, we define its
length
by
|u|=k
. We introduce the
lexicographical order
on
I^{*}
in a standard way:

[i_{1}\dots i_{k}]<[j_{1}\dots j_{l}]\quad\text{if }\ \begin{cases}i_{1}=j_{1},\dots,i_{a}=j_{a},i_{a+1}<j_{a+1}\text{ for some }a\geq 0\\
\text{\ or}\\
i_{1}=j_{1},\dots,i_{k}=j_{k}\text{ and }k<l\end{cases}.

For a word
w=[i_{1}\dots i_{k}]\in I^{*}
, the subwords:

(5.1)

w_{a|}=[i_{1}\dots i_{a}]\qquad\text{and}\qquad w_{|a}=[i_{k-a+1}\dots i_{k}]

with
0\leq a\leq k
will be called a
prefix
and a
suffix
of
w
, respectively.
We call such a prefix or a suffix
proper
if
0<a<k
. We start with the following important definition:

Definition 5.1
.

A word
w
is
Lyndon
if it is smaller than all of its proper suffixes:

(5.2)

w<w_{|a}\qquad\mathrm{for\ all}\quad 0<a<|w|.

We recall the following two basic facts from the theory of Lyndon words:

Proposition 5.2
.

(
[
Lo
, Proposition 5.1.3]
)
Any Lyndon word
\ell
has a factorization:

(5.3)

\ell=\ell_{1}\ell_{2}

defined by the property that
\ell_{1}
is the longest proper prefix of
\ell
which is also a Lyndon word.
Then,
\ell_{2}
is also a Lyndon word.

The factorization (
5.3
) is called a
standard factorization
of a Lyndon word.

Proposition 5.3
.

(
[
Lo
, Proposition 5.1.5]
)
Any word
w
has a unique factorization:

(5.4)

w=\ell_{1}\dots\ell_{k}

where
\ell_{1}\geq\dots\geq\ell_{k}
are all Lyndon words.

The factorization (
5.4
) is called a
canonical factorization
.

Let
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
be a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
generated by all
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
. The
standard bracketing
of a Lyndon word
\ell
(with respect to the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
) is defined inductively by the following procedure:

\bullet

e_{[i]}=e_{i}\in\mathfrak{n}^{+}
for
i\in I
,

\bullet

e_{[\ell]}=[e_{[\ell_{1}]},e_{[\ell_{2}]}]\in\mathfrak{n}^{+}
, where
\ell=\ell_{1}\ell_{2}
is
the standard factorization (
5.3
).

The following definition is due to
[
LR
]
:

Definition 5.4
.

A Lyndon word
\ell
is called
standard Lyndon
if
e_{\ell}
cannot be expressed as a linear combination of
e_{m}
for various Lyndon words
m>\ell
.

The major importance of this definition is due to the following result:

Theorem 5.5
.

(
[
LR
]
)
The set
\big\{e_{[\ell]}|\ell\mathrm{-standard\ Lyndon\ word}\big\}
provides a basis of
\mathfrak{n}^{+}
.

Due to a root space decomposition
\mathfrak{n}^{+}=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
with all
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
being one-dimensional, we get

(5.5)

\ell\colon\Phi^{+}\,\,\vphantom{j^{X^{2}}}\smash{\overset{\sim}{\vphantom{\rule{0.0pt}{1.99997pt}}\smash{\longrightarrow}}}\,\,\big\{\text{standard Lyndon words}\big\},

the so-called
Lalonde-Ram
’s bijection, mentioned in (
1.5
). This bijection was described explicitly in
[
Le
]
:

Proposition 5.6
.

(
[
Le
, Proposition 25]
)
The bijection
\ell
is inductively given as follows:

\bullet

for simple roots, we have
\ell(\alpha_{i})=[i]
,

\bullet

for other positive roots, the value of
\ell(\alpha)
is determined using Leclerc’s algorithm:

(5.6)

\ell(\alpha)=\max\left\{\ell(\gamma_{1})\ell(\gamma_{2})\,\Big|\Big.\,\alpha=\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}\,,\,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\Phi^{+}\,,\,\ell(\gamma_{1})<\ell(\gamma_{2})\right\}.

We shall also need one more important property of
\ell
. To the end, let us recall:

Definition 5.7
.

A total order on the set of positive roots
\Phi^{+}
is
convex
if:

(5.7)

\alpha<\alpha+\beta<\beta

for all
\alpha<\beta\in\Phi^{+}
such that
\alpha+\beta
is also a root.

The following result is
[
Le
, Proposition 28]
, where it is attributed to
[
Ro
]
(see also
[
NT
, Proposition 2.34]
):

Proposition 5.8
.

Consider the order on
\Phi^{+}
induced from the lexicographical order on
standard Lyndon words
:

(5.8)

\alpha<\beta\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\ell(\alpha)<\ell(\beta)\ \ \mathrm{lexicographically}.

This order is convex.

Finally, recall that given any convex order on
\Phi^{+}
, a pair
(\alpha,\beta)
of positive roots is called a
minimal pair
for
\gamma=\alpha+\beta\in\Phi^{+}
if

(5.9)

\alpha<\beta\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\nexists\,\alpha<\alpha^{\prime}<\gamma<\beta^{\prime}<\beta\quad\mathrm{such\ that}\quad\alpha^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime}=\gamma.

The following result goes back to
[
Ro
,
Le
]
(cf.
[
NT
, Proposition 2.38]
):

Proposition 5.9
.

For any
\gamma\in\Phi^{+}
, consider the standard factorization (
5.3
), so that
\ell_{1}=\ell(\alpha)
and
\ell_{2}=\ell(\beta)
. Then
(\alpha,\beta)
is a minimal pair for
\gamma=\alpha+\beta
.

Root vectors and the PBW theorem

The following construction of (quantum)
root vectors

e_{\gamma},f_{\gamma}
goes back to
[

,
K2
,

,

]
in the one-parameter setup, to
[

]
in the super setup, and to
[

,

,

,

]
in the two-parameter setup. For
\gamma=\alpha_{i}\in\Pi
, we set

e_{\alpha_{i}}=e_{i},\qquad f_{\alpha_{i}}=f_{i}.

By induction on the height of a root, for any
\gamma\in\Phi^{+}\backslash\Pi
, define (cf. (

(5.14)

e_{\gamma}=e_{\alpha}e_{\beta}-(\omega_{\beta}^{\prime},\omega_{\alpha})e_{\beta}e_{\alpha}\,,\qquad f_{\gamma}=f_{\beta}f_{\alpha}-(\omega_{\alpha}^{\prime},\omega_{\beta})^{-1}f_{\alpha}f_{\beta},

where the minimal pair

of

The following is the two-parameter version (which we shall address elsewhere) of the classical result of
[

,

,

,

]
(see also
[

]
where it was adapted to the super case):

Theorem 5.12
.

(a) The ordered products

(5.15)

\left\{\overset{\longleftarrow}{\underset{\gamma\in\Phi^{+}}{\prod}}e_{\gamma}^{m_{\gamma}}\,\Big|\Big.\,m_{\gamma}\geq 0\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad\left\{\overset{\longleftarrow}{\underset{\gamma\in\Phi^{+}}{\prod}}f_{\gamma}^{m_{\gamma}}\,\Big|\Big.\,m_{\gamma}\geq 0\right\}

are bases for

and

, respectively. Here and below, the arrow

over the product signs refers to the total order (

) on

.

(b) The Hopf pairing (

) is orthogonal with respect to these bases. More explicitly, we have:

(5.16)

\left(\overset{\longleftarrow}{\underset{\gamma\in\Phi^{+}}{\prod}}f_{\gamma}^{n_{\gamma}},\overset{\longleftarrow}{\underset{\gamma\in\Phi^{+}}{\prod}}e_{\gamma}^{m_{\gamma}}\right)=\prod_{\gamma\in\Phi^{+}}\Big(\delta_{n_{\gamma},m_{\gamma}}(f_{\gamma}^{m_{\gamma}},e^{m_{\gamma}}_{\gamma})\Big).

(c) For each

and

(f_{\gamma}^{m},e_{\gamma}^{m})=s_{\gamma}^{-m(m-1)/2}c_{\gamma}^{m}[m]_{r_{\gamma},s_{\gamma}}!

c_{\gamma}=\left(p_{\alpha,\beta}[p_{\alpha,\beta}+1]_{r_{\alpha},s_{\alpha}}^{2}\frac{(s_{\alpha}-r_{\alpha})(s_{\beta}-r_{\beta})}{(s_{\gamma}-r_{\gamma})}+(\omega_{\beta}^{\prime},\omega_{\alpha})-(\omega_{\alpha}^{\prime},\omega_{\beta})^{-1}\right)c_{\alpha}c_{\beta},

where

, and

\Theta=\overset{\longleftarrow}{\underset{\gamma\in\Phi^{+}}{\prod}}\left(\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{(f^{m}_{\gamma},e^{m}_{\gamma})}f^{m}_{\gamma}\otimes e^{m}_{\gamma}\right),

defined in (

Remark 5.14
.

In fact,

\exp_{r,s}(z)=\sum_{m\geq 0}s^{m(m-1)/2}\frac{z^{m}}{[m]_{r,s}!}.

\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{(f_{\gamma}^{m},e_{\gamma}^{m})}f_{\gamma}^{m}\otimes e_{\gamma}^{m}=\sum_{m\geq 0}\frac{s^{m(m-1)/2}_{\gamma}}{c_{\gamma}^{m}[m]_{r_{\gamma},s_{\gamma}}!}f_{\gamma}^{m}\otimes e_{\gamma}^{m}=\exp_{r_{\gamma},s_{\gamma}}(c_{\gamma}^{-1}f_{\gamma}\otimes e_{\gamma}).

Thus, the factorization formula (

5.4.1.
Factorized formula in type
A_{n}

We start by evaluating the action of
\{e_{\gamma},f_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Phi^{+}}
on the
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
-representation
V
from Proposition
3.1
:

Lemma 5.15
.

\rho(e_{\gamma_{ij}})=E_{i,j+1}
and
\rho(f_{\gamma_{ij}})=E_{j+1,i}
for any
1\leq i\leq j\leq n
.

Proof.

The proof is straightforward and proceeds by induction on the height
|\gamma_{ij}|=j-i+1
, where we use

e_{\gamma_{ij}}=e_{\gamma_{i,j-1}}e_{j}-(\omega_{j}^{\prime},\omega_{i}\ldots\omega_{j-1})e_{j}e_{\gamma_{i,j-1}},\qquad f_{\gamma_{ij}}=f_{j}f_{\gamma_{i,j-1}}-(\omega^{\prime}_{i}\ldots\omega^{\prime}_{j-1},\omega_{j})^{-1}f_{\gamma_{i,j-1}}f_{j}

and the explicit
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
-action on
V
, cf. (
5.14
) and Proposition
3.1
.
∎

Clearly,
\rho(e_{\gamma_{ij}})^{2}=\rho(f_{\gamma_{ij}})^{2}=0
for all
i\leq j
, so that

\Theta_{\gamma_{ij}}=1\otimes 1+(s-r)E_{j+1,i}\otimes E_{i,j+1}

on the
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})
-representation
V\otimes V
. To evaluate
\Theta
, let us first set

\Theta_{i}=\Theta_{\gamma_{in}}\Theta_{\gamma_{i,n-1}}\ldots\Theta_{\gamma_{i,i+1}}\Theta_{\alpha_{i}},

so that

\Theta=\Theta_{n}\Theta_{n-1}\ldots\Theta_{1}.

Since
E_{j+1,i}\otimes E_{i,j+1}\cdot E_{k+1,i}\otimes E_{i,k+1}=0
for any
i\leq k<j
, we thus obtain

\Theta_{i}=1\otimes 1+(s-r)\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}E_{j+1,i}\otimes E_{i,j+1}.

Moreover, since
E_{k+1,j}\otimes E_{j,k+1}\cdot E_{\ell+1,i}\otimes E_{i,\ell+1}=0
for any
k\geq j>i
and
\ell\geq i
, we get

(5.26)

\Theta=\Theta_{n}\ldots\Theta_{1}=1\otimes 1+(s-r)\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+1}E_{ji}\otimes E_{ij}.

For type
A_{n}
, the function
f
of (
4.3
) takes the following values on the weights of
V
,
see
[
BW1
, Lemma 4.4]
:

f(\varepsilon_{i},\varepsilon_{j})=\begin{cases}s^{-1}&\text{if}\ \ i<j\\
1&\text{if}\ \ i=j\\
r&\text{if}\ \ i>j\end{cases}\,.

Combining this formula with the formula (
5.26
) and the flip map
\tau
, we recover the explicit formula (
4.6
) for
\hat{R}_{VV}=\Theta\circ\widetilde{f}\circ\tau\colon V\otimes V\to V\otimes V
, thus providing an alternative proof of Theorem
4.3
, and giving a factorization of the
R
-matrix of
[
BW2
]
into “local” operators, parametrized by the set of positive roots
\Phi^{+}
.

5.4.2.
Factorized formula in type
B_{n}

We start by evaluating the action of
\{e_{\gamma},f_{\gamma}\}_{\gamma\in\Phi^{+}}
on the
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1})
-module
V
from Proposition
3.2
:

Lemma 5.16
.

(a) For
i\leq j\leq n
, we have

\begin{split}&\rho(e_{\gamma_{ij}})=E_{i,j+1}-s^{2(j-i)}E_{(j+1)^{\prime},i^{\prime}},\\
&\rho(f_{\gamma_{ij}})=\begin{cases}E_{j+1,i}-s^{2(i-j)}(rs)^{-2}E_{i^{\prime},(j+1)^{\prime}}&\text{if}\ \ j<n\\
(r^{-1}+s^{-1})(E_{n+1,i}-s^{2(i-n)}E_{i^{\prime},n+1})&\text{if}\ \ j=n\end{cases}\,.\end{split}

(b) For
i<j\leq n
, we have

\begin{split}&\rho(e_{\beta_{ij}})=(-1)^{n+1-j}(E_{ij^{\prime}}-s^{2(n-i)}r^{-2(n-j)}(rs)E_{ji^{\prime}}),\\
&\rho(f_{\beta_{ij}})=(-1)^{n+1-j}(r^{-1}+s^{-1})^{2}\cdot s^{2(j-n)}(r^{2(j-n)}E_{j^{\prime}i}-s^{2(i-n)}(rs)E_{i^{\prime}j}).\end{split}

Proof.

The proof is straightforward and proceeds by an increasing induction on
j
(from
j=i
up to
j=n
) for the roots
\gamma=\gamma_{ij}
, and then by a descending induction on
j
(from
j=n
till
j=i+1
) for the roots
\gamma=\beta_{ij}
. Here, we use the explicit
U_{r,s}(\mathfrak{so}_{2n+1})
-action on
V
from Proposition
3.2
, the explicit list of minimal pairs
(\alpha,\beta)
as specified in Subsection
5.2
, and finally the inductive construction (
5.14
).
∎

According to Lemma
5.16
(a), we have
\rho(e_{\gamma_{ij}})^{2}=\rho(f_{\gamma_{ij}})^{2}=0
for
1\leq i\leq j<n
, so that

(5.27)

\Theta_{\gamma_{ij}}=1\otimes 1+(s^{2}-r^{2})\left(E_{j+1,i}-s^{2(i-j)}(rs)^{-2}E_{i^{\prime},(j+1)^{\prime}}\right)\otimes\left(E_{i,j+1}-s^{2(j-i)}E_{(j+1)^{\prime},i^{\prime}}\right)

for any
1\leq i\leq j<n
. In contrast, for
j=n
, we have:

\rho(e_{\gamma_{in}})^{2}=-s^{2(n-i)}E_{ii^{\prime}},\quad\rho(f_{\gamma_{in}})^{2}=-s^{2(i-n)}(r+s)^{2}(rs)^{-2}E_{i^{\prime}i},\quad\rho(e_{\gamma_{in}})^{3}=\rho(f_{\gamma_{in}})^{3}=0.

Therefore, we obtain:

(5.28)

\begin{split}\Theta_{\gamma_{in}}=1\otimes 1&+(s^{2}-r^{2})(rs)^{-1}\left(E_{n+1,i}-s^{2(i-n)}E_{i^{\prime},n+1}\right)\otimes\left(E_{i,n+1}-s^{2(n-i)}E_{n+1,i^{\prime}}\right)\\
&+r^{-2}s^{-1}(r^{2}-s^{2})(r-s)E_{i^{\prime}i}\otimes E_{ii^{\prime}}.\end{split}

According to Lemma
5.16
(b), we also have
\rho(e_{\beta_{ij}})^{2}=\rho(f_{\beta_{ij}})^{2}=0
for all
i<j\leq n
, so that

(5.29)

\Theta_{\beta_{ij}}=1\otimes 1+(s^{2}-r^{2})(rs)^{-2}\left(r^{2(j-n)}E_{j^{\prime}i}-s^{2(i-n)}(rs)E_{i^{\prime}j}\right)\otimes\left(r^{2(n-j)}E_{ij^{\prime}}-s^{2(n-i)}(rs)E_{ji^{\prime}}\right).

To evaluate
\Theta
, let us first set

\Theta_{i}^{\prime\prime}=\Theta_{\beta_{i,i+1}}\Theta_{\beta_{i,i+2}}\ldots\Theta_{\beta_{in}},\quad\Theta_{i}^{\prime}=\Theta_{\gamma_{in}}\Theta_{\gamma_{i,n-1}}\ldots\Theta_{\gamma_{i,i+1}}\Theta_{\alpha_{i}},\quad\Theta_{i}=\Theta_{i}^{\prime\prime}\Theta_{i}^{\prime},

so that

\Theta=\Theta_{n}\Theta_{n-1}\ldots\Theta_{1}.

We will now show by induction on
n-k
that

(5.30)

\begin{split}&\Theta^{(k)}:=\Theta_{n}\Theta_{n-1}\ldots\Theta_{k}=1\otimes 1+c\sum_{i=k}^{n}\left(r^{-1}s-r^{2(n-i)}s^{2(i-n)}\right)E_{i^{\prime}i}\otimes E_{ii^{\prime}}\\
&+c\,\sum_{\mathclap{k\leq i<j\leq n}}\left(rsE_{ji}\otimes E_{ij}-r^{2(j-i)-1}sE_{ji}\otimes E_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}-r^{-1}s^{2(i-j)+1}E_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\otimes E_{ij}+(rs)^{-1}E_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\otimes E_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}\right)\\
&+c\,\sum_{i=k}^{n}\left(E_{n+1,i}\otimes E_{i,n+1}-r^{2(n-i)}E_{n+1,i}\otimes E_{n+1,i^{\prime}}-s^{-2(n-i)}E_{i^{\prime},n+1}\otimes E_{i,n+1}+E_{i^{\prime},n+1}\otimes E_{n+1,i^{\prime}}\right)\\
&+c\,\sum_{\mathclap{k\leq i<j\leq n}}\left((rs)^{-1}E_{j^{\prime}i}\otimes E_{ij^{\prime}}-r^{2(n-i)}s^{2(j-n)}E_{j^{\prime}i}\otimes E_{ji^{\prime}}-r^{2(n-j)}s^{2(i-n)}E_{i^{\prime}j}\otimes E_{ij^{\prime}}+rsE_{i^{\prime}j}\otimes E_{ji^{\prime}}\right),\end{split}

where
c=(s^{2}-r^{2})(rs)^{-1}
. For
k=1
, this provides the desired formula for
\Theta=\Theta^{(1)}
.

Let us start by computing a single
\Theta_{i}
. First, it is easy to see from (
5.27
) and (
5.28
) that the only non-zero products in
\Theta_{\gamma_{in}}\Theta_{\gamma_{i,n-1}}\ldots\Theta_{\alpha_{i}}
involve
1\otimes 1
, and thus

\displaystyle\Theta_{i}^{\prime}=1\otimes 1+c(r^{-1}s-1)E_{i^{\prime}i}\otimes E_{ii^{\prime}}

\displaystyle+c\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\left(rsE_{ji}\otimes E_{ij}-rs^{2(j-i)-1}E_{ji}\otimes E_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}-r^{-1}s^{2(i-j)+1}E_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\otimes E_{ij}+(rs)^{-1}E_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\otimes E_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle+c\left(E_{n+1,i}\otimes E_{i,n+1}-s^{2(n-i)}E_{n+1,i}\otimes E_{n+1,i^{\prime}}-s^{2(i-n)}E_{i^{\prime},n+1}\otimes E_{i,n+1}+E_{i^{\prime},n+1}\otimes E_{n+1,i^{\prime}}\right).

For similar reasons, we have

\displaystyle\Theta_{i}^{\prime\prime}=1\otimes 1

\displaystyle+c\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\left((rs)^{-1}E_{j^{\prime}i}\otimes E_{ij^{\prime}}-r^{2(j-n)}s^{2(n-i)}E_{j^{\prime}i}\otimes E_{ji^{\prime}}-r^{2(n-j)}s^{2(i-n)}E_{i^{\prime}j}\otimes E_{ij^{\prime}}+rsE_{i^{\prime}j}\otimes E_{ji^{\prime}}\right).

On the other hand, when computing the product
\Theta_{i}^{\prime\prime}\Theta_{i}^{\prime}
, there are some non-zero products not involving
1\otimes 1
, namely
cr^{2(n-j)}s^{2(i-n)}E_{i^{\prime}j}\otimes E_{ij^{\prime}}\cdot crs^{2(j-i)-1}E_{ji}\otimes E_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}
for
i<j\leq n
. The overall contribution of those equals

\displaystyle c^{2}r^{2n+1}s^{-2n-1}\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}r^{-2j}s^{2j}\right)E_{i^{\prime}i}\otimes E_{ii^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=c^{2}rs^{2(i-n)+1}[n-i]_{r^{2},s^{2}}E_{i^{\prime}i}\otimes E_{ii^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=c\left(1-r^{2(n-i)}s^{2(i-n)}\right)E_{i^{\prime}i}\otimes E_{ii^{\prime}},

and therefore we have

(5.31)

\begin{split}&\Theta_{i}=\Theta_{i}^{\prime\prime}\Theta_{i}^{\prime}=1\otimes 1+c\left(r^{-1}s-r^{2(n-i)}s^{2(i-n)}\right)E_{i^{\prime}i}\otimes E_{ii^{\prime}}\\
&+c\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\left(rsE_{ji}\otimes E_{ij}-rs^{2(j-i)-1}E_{ji}\otimes E_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}-r^{-1}s^{2(i-j)+1}E_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\otimes E_{ij}+(rs)^{-1}E_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\otimes E_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}\right)\\
&+c\left(E_{n+1,i}\otimes E_{i,n+1}-s^{2(n-i)}E_{n+1,i}\otimes E_{n+1,i^{\prime}}-s^{2(i-n)}E_{i^{\prime},n+1}\otimes E_{i,n+1}+E_{i^{\prime},n+1}\otimes E_{n+1,i^{\prime}}\right)\\
&+c\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}\left((rs)^{-1}E_{j^{\prime}i}\otimes E_{ij^{\prime}}-r^{2(j-n)}s^{2(n-i)}E_{j^{\prime}i}\otimes E_{ji^{\prime}}-r^{2(n-j)}s^{2(i-n)}E_{i^{\prime}j}\otimes E_{ij^{\prime}}+rsE_{i^{\prime}j}\otimes E_{ji^{\prime}}\right).\end{split}

In particular, for
i=n
we get

\Theta_{n}=1\otimes 1+c(r^{-1}s-1)E_{i^{\prime}i}\otimes E_{ii^{\prime}}\\
+c\left(E_{n+1,n}\otimes E_{n,n+1}-E_{n+1,n}\otimes E_{n+1,n^{\prime}}-E_{n^{\prime},n+1}\otimes E_{n,n+1}+E_{n^{\prime},n+1}\otimes E_{n+1,n^{\prime}}\right),

which agrees with the claimed formula (
5.30
) when
k=n
, thus establishing the base of our induction.

Let us now prove the step of induction in (
5.30
). It suffices to treat the
k=1
case, due to the telescopic structure of the action on
V
and order on
\Phi^{+}
, see Remark
5.10
. It thus remains to evaluate
\Theta^{(1)}=\Theta^{(2)}\cdot\Theta_{1}
, where
\Theta^{(2)}
is given by (
5.30
) and
\Theta_{1}
was just evaluated above. In addition to the terms of this product that involve
1\otimes 1
, we get the following extra summands:

(5.32a)

\left(-c\sum_{i=2}^{n}s^{2(i-n)}E_{i^{\prime},n+1}\otimes E_{i,n+1}\right)\left(-cs^{2(n-1)}E_{n+1,1}\otimes E_{n+1,1^{\prime}}\right)=(r^{2}-s^{2})^{2}\sum_{i=2}^{n}r^{-2}s^{2i-4}E_{i^{\prime}1}\otimes E_{i1^{\prime}},

(5.32b)

\begin{split}&\left(-c\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq n}r^{2(n-i)}s^{2(j-n)}E_{j^{\prime}i}\otimes E_{ji^{\prime}}\right)\left(-c\sum_{i=2}^{n}rs^{2i-3}E_{i1}\otimes E_{i^{\prime}1^{\prime}}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad=c^{2}\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}r^{2(n-i)+1}s^{2(j+i-n-1)-1}E_{j^{\prime}1}\otimes E_{j1^{\prime}}\\
&\qquad\qquad=c^{2}\sum_{j=3}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{j-1}r^{2(n-i)+1}s^{2(j+i-n-1)-1}\right)E_{j^{\prime}1}\otimes E_{j1^{\prime}}\\
&\qquad\qquad=(r^{2}-s^{2})^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{n}r^{2(n-j)+1}s^{2(j-n)-1}[j-2]_{r^{2},s^{2}}E_{j^{\prime}1}\otimes E_{j1^{\prime}},\end{split}

(5.32c)

\begin{split}&\left(-c\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq n}r^{2(n-j)}s^{2(i-n)}E_{i^{\prime}j}\otimes E_{ij^{\prime}}\right)\left(-c\sum_{j=2}^{n}rs^{2j-3}E_{j1}\otimes E_{j^{\prime}1^{\prime}}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad=(r^{2}-s^{2})^{2}\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}r^{2(n-j)-1}s^{2(i+j-n-2)-1}\right)E_{i^{\prime}1}\otimes E_{i1^{\prime}}\\
&\qquad\qquad=(r^{2}-s^{2})^{2}\sum_{i=2}^{n}r^{-1}s^{4i-2n-3}[n-i]_{r^{2},s^{2}}E_{i^{\prime}1}\otimes E_{i1^{\prime}},\end{split}

(5.32d)

\begin{split}&\left(c\sum_{i=2}^{n}(r^{-1}s-r^{2(n-i)}s^{2(i-n)})E_{i^{\prime}i}\otimes E_{ii^{\prime}}\right)\left(-c\sum_{i=2}^{n}rs^{2(i-1)-1}E_{i1}\otimes E_{i^{\prime}1^{\prime}}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad=(r^{2}-s^{2})^{2}\sum_{i=2}^{n}(r^{2(n-i)-1}s^{4i-2n-5}-r^{-2}s^{2(i-2)})E_{i^{\prime}1}\otimes E_{i1^{\prime}},\end{split}

(5.32e)

\begin{split}&\left(-c\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq n}r^{-1}s^{2(i-j)+1}E_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\otimes E_{ij}\right)\left(-c\sum_{j=2}^{n}r^{2(j-n)}s^{2(n-1)}E_{j^{\prime}1}\otimes E_{j1^{\prime}}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad=(r^{2}-s^{2})^{2}\sum_{i=2}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}r^{2(j-n-1)-1}s^{2(n+i-j-1)-1}\right)E_{i^{\prime}1}\otimes E_{i1^{\prime}}\\
&\qquad\qquad=(r^{2}-s^{2})^{2}\sum_{i=2}^{n}r^{2(i-n)-1}s^{2i-3}[n-i]_{r^{2},s^{2}}E_{i^{\prime}1}\otimes E_{i1^{\prime}},\end{split}

(5.32f)

\begin{split}&\left(-c\sum_{2\leq i<j\leq n}r^{2(j-i)-1}sE_{ji}\otimes E_{j^{\prime}i^{\prime}}\right)\left(-c\sum_{i=2}^{n}rs^{2i-3}E_{i1}\otimes E_{i^{\prime}1^{\prime}}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad=(r^{2}-s^{2})^{2}\sum_{i=2}^{n}\sum_{j=i+1}^{n}r^{2(j-i-1)}s^{2(i-2)}E_{j1}\otimes E_{j^{\prime}1^{\prime}}\\
&\qquad\qquad=(r^{2}-s^{2})^{2}\sum_{j=3}^{n}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{j-1}r^{2(j-i-1)}s^{2(i-2)}\right)E_{j1}\otimes E_{j^{\prime}1^{\prime}}\\
&\qquad\qquad=(r^{2}-s^{2})^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{n}[j-2]_{r^{2},s^{2}}E_{j1}\otimes E_{j^{\prime}1^{\prime}},\end{split}

(5.32g)

\begin{split}&\left(-c\sum_{i=2}^{n}r^{2(n-i)}E_{n+1,i}\otimes E_{n+1,i^{\prime}}\right)\left(-c\sum_{i=2}^{n}rs^{2i-3}E_{i1}\otimes E_{i^{\prime}1^{\prime}}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad=(r^{2}-s^{2})^{2}\left(\sum_{i=2}^{n}r^{2(n-i)-1}s^{2(i-2)-1}\right)E_{n+1,1}\otimes E_{n+1,1^{\prime}}\\
&\qquad\qquad=(r^{2}-s^{2})^{2}(rs)^{-1}[n-1]_{r^{2},s^{2}}E_{n+1,1}\otimes E_{n+1,1^{\prime}}.\end{split}

Thus, the overall contribution of the terms
\{E_{j1}\otimes E_{j^{\prime}1^{\prime}}\}_{1<j<n+1}
into
\Theta
equals

\sum_{j=2}^{n}(r^{2}-s^{2})s^{2(j-2)}E_{j1}\otimes E_{j^{\prime}1^{\prime}}+\sum_{j=3}^{n}(r^{2}-s^{2})^{2}[j-2]_{r^{2},s^{2}}E_{j1}\otimes E_{j^{\prime}1^{\prime}}=(r^{2}-s^{2})\sum_{j=2}^{n}r^{2(j-2)}E_{j1}\otimes E_{j^{\prime}1^{\prime}},

where the first summand arises from
1\otimes 1\cdot\Theta_{1}
and the second from (
5.32f
). Likewise, the overall coefficient of
E_{n+1,1}\otimes E_{n+1,1^{\prime}}
in
\Theta
is

(r^{2}-s^{2})(rs)^{-1}s^{2(n-1)}+(r^{2}-s^{2})^{2}(rs)^{-1}[n-1]_{r^{2},s^{2}}=(r^{2}-s^{2})(rs)^{-1}r^{2(n-1)},

where the first summand arises from
1\otimes 1\cdot\Theta_{1}
and the second from (
5.32g
).
Finally, the remaining terms (
5.32a
)–(
5.32e
) contribute to the coefficients of
\{E_{j^{\prime}1}\otimes E_{j1^{\prime}}\}_{2\leq j\leq n}
. Combining these with the corresponding terms from

, one eventually arrives at

(r^{2}-s^{2})(rs)^{-1}\sum_{i=2}^{n}r^{2(n-1)}s^{2(i-n)}E_{i^{\prime}1}\otimes E_{i1^{\prime}}.

Thus, (
5.32a
)–(
5.32g
) and their counterparts from

match the corresponding part in the right-hand side of (
5.30
) for
k=1
. On the other hand, it is easy to see that the remaining terms arising from
\Theta^{(2)}\cdot 1\otimes 1
and
1\otimes 1\cdot\Theta_{1}
exactly match with the remaining terms in the right-hand side of (
5.30
) for
k=1
. This completes our proof of the induction step, thus establishing (
5.30
).

To derive the formula for
\hat{R}_{VV}
it only remains to evaluate the values of
f
from (
4.3
) on the weights of
V
. In accordance with (
4.3
), we have

f(\lambda,\mu)=(\omega_{\mu}^{\prime},\omega_{\lambda})^{-1},

where we extend the Hopf pairing to the weight lattice as in (
2.14
).
From the formulas (
2.26
), the equality
\varepsilon_{k}=\alpha_{k}+\ldots+\alpha_{n}
, and the basic properties of the Hopf pairing, we derive:

(5.33)

f(\varepsilon_{i},\varepsilon_{j})=f(-\varepsilon_{i},-\varepsilon_{j})=\begin{cases}(rs)^{-1}&\text{if}\ \ i<j\\
r^{-1}s&\text{if}\ \ i=j\\
rs&\text{if}\ \ i>j\end{cases}\,,

while the remaining values are then determined by:

Definition 6.2

Proposition A.1
.

The elements (

) satisfy the defining relations of Drinfeld-Jimbo generators for

Proof.

The proof consists of a straightforward verification of all of the defining relations.

•

The commutativity
[\widetilde{\omega}_{i},\widetilde{\omega}_{j}]=0
is a direct consequence of (
2.5
).

•

Let us now check

\displaystyle\qquad 0

Cohomologies and generalized derivation extensions of n n -Lie algebras
B. Ateşli, O. Esen, S. Sütlü

Abstract
. A cohomology theory, associated to a n n -Lie algebra and a representation space of it, is introduced. It is observed that this cohomology theory is qualified to encode the generalized derivation extensions, and that it coincides, for n = 3 n=3 , with the known cohomology of n n -Lie algebras. The abelian extensions and infinitesimal deformations of n n -Lie algebras, on the other hand, are shown to be characterized by the usual cohomology of n n -Lie algebras. Furthermore, the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence of the Lie algebra cohomology is upgraded to the level of n n -Lie algebras, and is applied to the cohomology of generalized derivation extensions.



Introduction

In an effort to generalize the classical Hamiltonian mechanics to a theory that can accommodate two Hamiltonians, ternary Lie algebras were introduced in
[
16
]
, wherein it was hinted that the theory may be generalized into one that allows
(n-1)
-many Hamiltonians. This point of view was pursued further in
[
8
]
introducing a theory of
n
-Lie algebras, and then has further been developed in
[
19
]
.

It was in
[
10
]
, for the first time, a cohomology theory has been associated to
n
-ary Lie algebras to study their formal deformations. Then, in
[
2
]
, this cohomology theory has been considered further, in close connection with the Leibniz cohomology. This cohomology theory, associated to
n
-Lie algebras, upgraded recently to a cohomology theory with arbitrary coefficients in
[
1
]
and
[
20
]
in the case of
n=3
, and in
[
18
]
to a cohomology theory associated to a
n
-Hom-Lie algebra along with a representation space of it.

On the other hand, cohomology theories, more precisely the low dimensional cohomology groups, has intimate relations with the extensions/deformations of the algebraic/geometric objects,
[
11
,
12
]
. Regarding the
n
-ary Lie algebras, this point of view has been recently taken up by
[
20
]
both for the abelian extensions and the infinitesimal deformations, in the case of
n=3
. In the general case of
n
-Lie algebras, the analogue results were obtained in
[
3
]
, see also
[
15
, Prop. 4.3]
, for the central extensions, and in
[
15
, Thm. 4.2]
for (1-parameter) formal deformations. We shall hereby consider the cases of abelian extensions of
n
-Lie algebras, by a representation space, and the infinitesimal deformations.

The case of derivation extensions, on the other hand, has been considered in
[
17
]
for 3-Lie algebras, and it is argued that a derivation of a 3-Lie algebra
\mathfrak{G}
does not fit to define a 3-Lie algebra structure on the 1-dimensional extension
\mathfrak{G}\oplus k
. The authors, therefore, introduced the notion of a generalized derivation for a 3-Lie algebra, which has later been extended to
n
-Hom-Lie algebras in
[
18
]
.

It is very well-known that the derivations on
n
-Lie algebras, Leibniz algebras, etc. are in one-to-one correspondence with the 1st cohomology group of this algebraic object in question, with coefficients in itself. However, as is pointed out in
[
17
]
for 3-Lie algebras, such a characterization is missing for the case of generalized derivations. It is this gap that the present paper aims to fill in. More precisely, we realize a generalized derivations on a
n
-Lie algebra as a 1-cocycle in a cohomology theory associated to the very
n
-Lie algebra (along with a representation space) under consideration. Surprisingly enough, we observe that the cohomology theory classifying the generalized derivations on
n
-Lie algebras is not the usual one that classify the abelian extensions or the infinitesimal deformations. Nevertheless, these two cohomology theories coincides (only) in the case of
n=3
.

Finally, we upgrade the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence for Lie algebras to
n
-Lie algebras, allowing thus a thorough homological analysis on
n
-Lie algebras. We present explicitly the
E_{1}
-term relative to a subalgebra, and then the
E_{2}
-term relative to an ideal. As a manifestation of the structure of the cohomology of
n
-Lie algebras, the spectral sequence bears the Leibniz cohomology in addition to the
n
-Lie algebra cohomology. Furthermore, using this spectral sequence we show that the cohomology of the generalized derivation extension of a
n
-Lie algebra coincides with the cohomology of the
n
-Lie algebra itself.

-Lie algebras in Subsection

n
-Lie algebras in Proposition

of Subsection
2.3
, along with the infinitesimal deformations of
n
-Lie algebras in Subsection
2.4

2.5
, we introduce the cohomology theory encoding the generalized derivation extensions of
n
-Lie algebras. More precisely, we prove Proposition
2.13
. Finally, Section

n
-Lie algebra cohomology. As a direct application, we obtain Corollary

1.
Lie and Leibniz algebras

This introductory section is meant to review the basics of Lie and Leibniz algebras which will be needed in the sequel. To be more precise, we shall take a quick tour towards the cohomology of Lie algebras in the first subsection, whereas the second subsection will be reserved for the representations and the cohomology of Leibniz algebras.

1.1.
Derivation extensions of Lie algebras

In the present subsection we shall present a brief overview of derivation extensions of Lie algebras, through the Lie algebra cohomology.

Let us begin with the Lie algebra cohomology from
[
12
]
, as well as
[
9
]
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra, and let
V
be a (left) representation of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then, the
Chevalley-Eilenberg complex

C(\mathfrak{g},V):=\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 0}\,C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V),\qquad C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V):={\rm Hom}\left(\wedge^{n}\,\mathfrak{g},V\right),

together with
d:C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)\to C^{n+1}(\mathfrak{g},V)
which is given for any
f\in C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)
by

\displaystyle df(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n+1}):=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n+1}\,(-1)^{i+j}\,f([X_{i},X_{j}],X_{1},\ldots,\widehat{X}_{i},\ldots,\widehat{X}_{j},\ldots,X_{n+1})+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n+1}\,(-1)^{k+1}\,X_{k}\triangleright f(X_{1},\ldots,\widehat{X}_{k},\ldots,X_{n+1}),

form a differential complex. The homology
H(C(\mathfrak{g},V),d)
is called the
Lie algebra cohomology
of
\mathfrak{g}
, with coefficients in
V
, and is denoted by
H^{\ast}(\mathfrak{g},V)
. In other words,

Z^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\{f\in C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)\mid df=0\}

being the set of
n
-cocycles, and

B^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\{f\in C^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)\mid f=dg,\,\,{\rm for\,\,some}\,\,g\in C^{n-1}(\mathfrak{g},V)\}

being the set of
n
-coboundaries,

H^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V):=Z^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V)/B^{n}(\mathfrak{g},V),\qquad n\geqslant 0.

Now, a
derivation
of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is defined to be a linear operator
D:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
so that

D([X,Y])=[D(X),Y]+[X,D(Y)]

for any
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
. It follows at once from the
Jacobi identity
;

[X,[Y,Z]]+[Y,[Z,X]]+[Z,[X,Y]]=0

for all
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g}
, that the
adjoint action
yields a derivation
\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits_{X}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits_{X}(Y):=[X,Y]
, for any
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Given a derivation
D:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, if
D=\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits_{X}
for some
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, then it is called an
inner derivation
.

Given a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, represented over itself by the adjoint action, it is well-known that
Z^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
coincides with the set
Der(\mathfrak{g})
of derivations on
\mathfrak{g}
, while
B^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
represents the set
I(\mathfrak{g})
of inner derivations. Accordingly,

H^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=Z^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})/B^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=Der(\mathfrak{g})/I(\mathfrak{g}).

Furthermore, given a linear mapping
f:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, the space
\mathfrak{g}\oplus_{f}k:=\mathfrak{g}\oplus k
is a Lie algebra through

(1.1)

[(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta)]:=\Big([X,Y]+\alpha f(Y)-\beta f(X),\,0\Big)

for any
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
and any
\alpha,\beta\in k
, if and only if
f\in Z^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
.

If, on the other hand,
f\in B^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
; say
f=dZ=-\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits_{Z}
for some
Z\in\mathfrak{g}
, then
\mathfrak{g}\oplus_{f}k\cong\mathfrak{g}\rtimes kZ
.

As a result, following the terminology in
[
9
, Sect. I.4]
, the equivalence classes of
1-dimensional right extensions
of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
are in one-to-one correspondence with
H^{1}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})
.

1.2.
Representations and the cohomology of Leibniz algebras

In close connection with
n
-Lie algebras, for any
n\geqslant 2
, there are the Leibniz algebras. As such, we find it instructive to recall briefly the basics of Leibniz algebras, along with the Leibniz algebra cohomology. The concepts recalled here will be needed in the sequel.

Let us begin with the definition of a (left) Leibniz algebra following
[
17
]
. For the right handed version, we refer the reader to
[
14
]
. For a recent survey on Leibniz algebras, we refer the reader to
[
6
]
.

A
left Leibniz algebra
is a vector space
\mathfrak{L}
equipped with a bilinear map
[\,,]:\mathfrak{L}\otimes\mathfrak{L}\to\mathfrak{L}
satisfying

(1.2)

[x,[y,z]]=[[x,y],z]+[y,[x,z]]

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{L}
, whereas a
right Leibniz algebra
is one that

(1.3)

[[x,y],z]=[[x,z],y]+[x,[y,z]]

is satisfied.

Moreover, a Leibniz algebra is called
symmetric
if it is both a left and a right Leibniz algebra. It follows at once from the definition above that a Lie algebra is a symmetric Leibniz algebra. Other examples of (left) Leibniz algebras include associative algebras equipped with suitable endomorphisms, see for instance
[
14
, Ex. 1.2]
.

Following the ideas developed in
[
14
, Subsect. 1.5]
, the notion of a representation of a (left) Leibniz algebra is given as in
[
17
, Subsect. 2.1]
.

A
representation
of a Leibniz algebra
(\mathfrak{L},[\,,])
is defined to be a vector space
V
equipped with the linear maps
\lambda:\mathfrak{L}\to g\ell(V)
and
\rho:\mathfrak{L}\to g\ell(V)
such that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\lambda([x,y])=\lambda(x)\circ\lambda(y)-\lambda(y)\circ\lambda(x),

(1.5)

\displaystyle\rho([x,y])=\lambda(x)\circ\rho(y)-\rho(y)\circ\lambda(x),

(1.6)

\displaystyle\rho(y)\circ\rho(x)=-\rho(y)\circ\lambda(x),

for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{L}
.

Let us note also that, from the point of view of (left and right)
actions
, we may view
\lambda:\mathfrak{L}\to g\ell(V)
as a left action
\triangleright:\mathfrak{L}\otimes V\to V
by

x\triangleright v:=\lambda(x)(v),

and
\rho:\mathfrak{L}\to g\ell(V)
as a right action
\triangleleft:V\otimes\mathfrak{L}\to V
via

v\triangleleft x:=\rho(x)(v),

for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{L}
, and any
v\in V
. Accordingly,
\lambda:\mathfrak{L}\to g\ell(V)
may be called a
left representation
of the Leibniz algebra, while
\rho:\mathfrak{L}\to g\ell(V)
is referred as a
right representation
. Hence, the conditions (
1.4
)-(
1.6
) may be re-written as

(1.8)

\displaystyle[x,y]\triangleright v=x\triangleright(y\triangleright v)-y\triangleright(x\triangleright v),

(1.9)

\displaystyle v\triangleleft[x,y]=x\triangleright(v\triangleleft y)-(x\triangleright v)\triangleleft y,

(1.10)

\displaystyle(v\triangleleft x)\triangleleft y=-(x\triangleright v)\triangleleft y.

Given a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{L}
,

(1.11)

\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits^{L}:\mathfrak{L}\to g\ell(\mathfrak{L}),\qquad\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits^{L}(x)(y):=[x,y]

is a left representation of the Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{L}
onto itself, called the
left adjoint representation
of
\mathfrak{L}
on itself, while

(1.12)

\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits^{R}:\mathfrak{L}\to g\ell(\mathfrak{L}),\qquad\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits^{R}(x)(y):=[y,x]

is a right representation, called the
right adjoint representation
of
\mathfrak{L}
.

We shall make use of the notations

\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits^{L}_{x}:=\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits^{L}(x),\qquad\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits^{R}_{x}:=\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits^{R}(x)

for any
x\in\mathfrak{L}
.

A representation
V
of a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{L}
is called
symmetric
if

x\triangleright v+v\triangleleft x=0

for any
x\in\mathfrak{L}
, and any
v\in V
, and
V
is called
anti-symmetric
if

v\triangleleft x=0.

Given a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{L}
, and a representation
V
of
\mathfrak{L}
, let

V_{anti}:={\rm Span}\{x\triangleright v+v\triangleleft x\mid x\in\mathfrak{L},\,\,v\in V\}

be the
anti-symmetric kernel
of
V
,
[
6
,
14
]
. Then, the
symmetrization

V_{sym}:=V/V_{anti}

of
V
, is a symmetric representation of
\mathfrak{L}
.

On the other extreme, we may consider the symmetric sub-representations.

Example 1.1
.

Given a representation
V
of a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{L}
,

V^{sym}:=\{v\in V\mid x\triangleright v+v\triangleleft x=0,\,\,\forall\,x\in\mathfrak{L}\}

is also a representation. Indeed, given any
w\in V^{sym}
, and any
x,y\in\mathfrak{L}
,

\displaystyle y\triangleright(x\triangleright w)+(x\triangleright w)\triangleleft y=y\triangleright(x\triangleright w)+x\triangleright(w\triangleleft y)-w\triangleleft[x,y]=

\displaystyle y\triangleright(x\triangleright w)-x\triangleright(y\triangleright w)-w\triangleleft[x,y]=-[x,y]\triangleright w-w\triangleleft[x,y]=0,

that is,
x\triangleright w\in V^{sym}
. Then,

\displaystyle y\triangleright(w\triangleleft x)+(w\triangleleft x)\triangleleft y=(y\triangleright w)\triangleleft x+w\triangleleft[y,x]+(w\triangleleft x)\triangleleft y=

\displaystyle(y\triangleright w)\triangleleft x-[y,x]\triangleright w+(w\triangleleft x)\triangleleft y=(y\triangleright w)\triangleleft x-y\triangleright(x\triangleright w)+x\triangleright(y\triangleright w)+(w\triangleleft x)\triangleleft y=

\displaystyle-y\triangleright(x\triangleright w)+(w\triangleleft x)\triangleleft y=-y\triangleright(x\triangleright w)-(x\triangleright w)\triangleleft y=0,

where we used (
1.9
) in the first equality, and on the third equality we used the result that
y\triangleright w\in V^{sym}
. The fourth equality, on the other hand, follows from (
1.10
), and the last equality is a result of
x\triangleright w\in V^{sym}
. Accordingly, we conclude that
w\triangleleft x\in V^{sym}
.

Needless to say that
V^{sym}
is a symmetric representation of
\mathfrak{L}
. Furthermore, it follows from its very definition that
V^{sym}=V
if
V
is a symmetric representation, and that
V^{sym}
is trivial if
V
is an anti-symmetric representation.

Let us next recall the semi-direct sum construction on the Leibniz algebras. Given a Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{L}
, and a representation
V
of
\mathfrak{L}
, it takes a routine verification that
V\rtimes\mathfrak{L}:=V\oplus\mathfrak{L}
is also Leibniz algebra via

\Big[(v,x),(w,y)\Big]:=\Big(x\triangleright w+v\triangleleft y,\,[x,y]\Big),

called the
semi-direct sum
Leibniz algebra.

Given a (left) Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{L}
, a linear map
D:\mathfrak{L}\to\mathfrak{L}
is called a
derivation
if

D([x,y])=[D(x),y]+[x,D(y)]

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{L}
.

Given any left Leibniz algebra
\mathfrak{L}
, and any
x\in\mathfrak{L}
, the (left) adjoint action
\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits_{x}^{L}:\mathfrak{L}\to\mathfrak{L}
is a derivation.

It then follows at once from the definition that if
\mathfrak{L}
is a left Leibniz algebra, then (
1.11
) is a derivation. Similarly, in the case of
\mathfrak{L}
being a right Leibniz algebra, (
1.12
) is a derivation.

Let us finally recall the Leibniz algebra cohomology from
[
7
,
14
]
. Let
\mathfrak{L}
be a Leibniz algebra, and let
V
be a representation of
\mathfrak{L}
. Then,

CL(\mathfrak{L},V):=\bigoplus_{n\geqslant 0}\,CL^{n}(\mathfrak{L},V),\qquad CL^{n}(\mathfrak{L},V):={\rm Hom}\left(\mathfrak{L}^{\otimes\,n},V\right),

together with
d:CL^{n}(\mathfrak{L},V)\to CL^{n+1}(\mathfrak{L},V)
which is given by

(1.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&df(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1}):=\\
&\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n+1}\,(-1)^{i}\,f(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{i},\ldots,x_{j-1},[x_{i},x_{j}],\ldots,x_{n+1})+\\
&\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k+1}\,x_{k}\triangleright f(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{k},\ldots,x_{n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}\,f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\triangleleft x_{n+1}\end{split}

for any
f\in CL^{n}(\mathfrak{L},V)
, form a differential complex. The homology
H(CL(\mathfrak{L},V),d)
is called the
Leibniz algebra cohomology
of
\mathfrak{L}
, with coefficients in
V
, and is denoted by
HL^{\ast}(\mathfrak{L},V)
. Similar to the Lie algebra cohomology complex,

ZL^{n}(\mathfrak{L},V):=\{f\in CL^{n}(\mathfrak{L},V)\mid df=0\}

denotes the set of
n
-cocycles, and

BL^{n}(\mathfrak{L},V):=\{f\in CL^{n}(\mathfrak{L},V)\mid f=dg,\,\,{\rm for\,\,some}\,\,g\in CL^{n-1}(\mathfrak{L},V)\}

is the set of
n
-coboundaries. Accordingly,

HL^{n}(\mathfrak{L},V):=ZL^{n}(\mathfrak{L},V)/BL^{n}(\mathfrak{L},V),\qquad n\geqslant 0.

2.
Generalized derivation extensions of
n
-Lie algebras

In the present section we shall present the cohomological classification of generalized derivations of a
n
-Lie algebras. In the case of
n=2
, the result recovers the analogue result recalled briefly in Subsection
1.1
, while in the case of
n=3
it answers the question raised in
[
17
, Rk. 3.7]
. More precisely, we shall realize a generalized derivation of a
n
-Lie algebra as a 1-cocycle in a cohomology theory associated to
n
-Lie algebras, and an inner generalized derivation as a 1-coboundary.

2.1.
n
-Lie algebras

Let us first recall the very basics of
n
-Lie algebras from
[
2
,
16
,
19
]
.

A linear space
\mathcal{L}
, equipped with a linear operator
[\,,\ldots\,,\,]:\mathcal{L}_{n}\to\mathcal{L}
, is called an
n
-Lie algebra if it satisfies the
fundamental identity

(2.1)

[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},[x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-1}]]=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,[x_{n},\ldots,[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k}],\ldots,x_{2n-1}],

for any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n-1}\in\mathcal{L}
.

Along the lines of
[
2
]
, given an
n
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
, the space
\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
has the structure of a Leibniz algebra through

[x_{1},x_{2}]:=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\,x_{2}^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge[x_{1}^{1},\ldots,x_{1}^{n-1},x_{2}^{k}]\wedge\dots\wedge x_{2}^{n-1}

for any
x_{1}:=x_{1}^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{1}^{n-1},x_{2}:=x_{2}^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{2}^{n-1}\in\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
. Furthermore, as was shown in
[
2
, Thm. 2]
,
\mathcal{L}
happens to be a representation space for the Leibniz algebra
\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
.

2.1.1.
Representations

Let us recall the representations of
n
-Lie algebras from
[
4
,
5
]
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a
n
-Lie algebra. A vector space
V
with a linear map
\mu:\mathcal{L}_{n-1}\to g\ell(V)
is called a
representation
of
\mathcal{L}
, if

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mu(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})\mu(x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-2})-\mu(x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-2})\mu(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})=

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\,\mu(x_{n},\ldots,[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k}],\ldots,x_{2n-2}),

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mu([x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}],x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{k},\ldots,x_{n})\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k}),

for any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n-2}\in\mathcal{L}
.

Let us first note that in the particular case of
n=3
, the conditions (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) reduce precisely to those in, for instance,
[
1
, Def. 2.3]
.

As was mentioned in
[
4
,
5
]
, the conditions (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) are equivalent to
V\rtimes\mathcal{L}:=V\oplus\mathcal{L}
to be a
n
-Lie algebra through

[v_{1}+x_{1},\ldots,v_{n}+x_{n}]:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{k},\ldots,x_{n})(v_{k})+[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}].

In this case, the
n
-Lie algebra
V\rtimes\mathcal{L}
is called the “semi-direct sum” of
\mathcal{L}
and
V
.

The most immediate example of a representation is
\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits:\mathcal{L}_{n-1}\to g\ell(\mathcal{L})
, that is, the
adjoint representation
of a
n
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
on itself, which is given by

(2.4)

\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})(x_{n}):=[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

for any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{L}
.

A slightly more serious example, in the spirit of a dual representation, is the following. To this end, we shall first record the following generalization of
[
1
, Prop. 2.5(b)]
for the representations of
n
-Lie algebras.

Proposition 2.1
.

Given a
n
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
, and a representation
(V,\mu)
of
\mathcal{L}
,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n})\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k})+\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k}\,\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k})\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n})=0\end{split}

for any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n-2}\in\mathcal{L}
.

Proof.

To begin with, in view of (
2.2
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n})\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k})+

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k}\,\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k})\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n})=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k}\,\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k})\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n})+

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{n-2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k}\,\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,[x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+p}],\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k})+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k}\,\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},[x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n},x_{k}])+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n})\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k})+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant r<k\leqslant n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,[x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k},x_{r}],\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n})+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant k<r\leqslant n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,[x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k},x_{r}],\ldots,x_{n}).

That is,

\displaystyle n\,\mu([x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}],x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2})+

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{n-2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k+p-1}\,\mu([x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+p}],x_{n+1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{n+p}},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k})+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant r<k\leqslant n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k+r-1}\,\mu([x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k},x_{r}],x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{r}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n})+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant k<r\leqslant n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k+r}\,\mu([x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k},x_{r}],x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{r}},\ldots,x_{n})=0.

We next apply (
2.3
) to arrive

\displaystyle n\,\mu([x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}],x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2})+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant r<k\leqslant n}\sum_{p=1}^{n-2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{r+k+p-1}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{r}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+p})\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{n+p}},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k},x_{r})+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant k<r\leqslant n}\sum_{p=1}^{n-2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{r+k+p}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{r}},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+p})\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{n+p}},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k},x_{r})+

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{n-2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{n+k+p-1}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n})\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{n+p}},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k},x_{n+p})+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant r<k\leqslant n}\sum_{p=1}^{n-2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{p+k+r-1}\,\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots\widehat{x_{n+p}},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k},x_{r})\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{r}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+p})+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant r<k\leqslant n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{n+k+r}\,\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{r})\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{r}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n},x_{k})+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant r<k\leqslant n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{n+k+r-1}\,\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k})\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{r}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n},x_{r})+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant k<r\leqslant n}\sum_{p=1}^{n-2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k+r+p}\,\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{n+p}},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k},x_{r})\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{r}},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+p})+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant k<r\leqslant n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{n+k+r-1}\,\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{r})\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{r}},\ldots,x_{n},x_{k})+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant k<r\leqslant n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{n+k+r}\,\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k})\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{r}},\ldots,x_{n},x_{r})=0,

which, calling the left hand side of (
2.5
) as
F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n-2})
, may be arranged into

\displaystyle(2n-2)\,F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n-2})+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant r<k\leqslant n}\sum_{p=1}^{n-2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{r+k+p-1}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{r}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+p})\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{n+p}},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k},x_{r})+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant k<r\leqslant n}\sum_{p=1}^{n-2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{r+k+p}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{r}},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+p})\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{n+p}},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k},x_{r})+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant r<k\leqslant n}\sum_{p=1}^{n-2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{p+k+r-1}\,\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots\widehat{x_{n+p}},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k},x_{r})\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{r}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+p})+

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leqslant k<r\leqslant n}\sum_{p=1}^{n-2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k+r+p}\,\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{n+p}},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k},x_{r})(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,\widehat{x_{r}},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+p})=0.

Next, setting for any
a\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
b\in\{1,\ldots,n-2\}

F_{a,b}:=F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{a-1},x_{n+b},x_{a+1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{n+b-1},x_{a},x_{n+b+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2}),

the above expression may be presented as

(2.6)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{p=1}^{n-2}\,F_{k,p}=(n^{2}-2)F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n-2}).

The claim, now, follows from the iteration of (
2.6
), or equivalently the successive applications of (
2.2
) and (
2.3
).
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Let us note that in the case of
n=3
, (
2.5
) is precisely
[
1
, Prop. 2.5(b)]
for 3-Lie algebras.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a
n
-Lie algebra, together with a representation
(V,\eta)
. Then, the space
{\rm Hom}(V,W)
of linear maps from
V
to a vector space
W
is also a representation of
\mathcal{L}
via

(2.7)

\mu:\mathcal{L}_{n-1}\to g\ell({\rm Hom}(V,W)),\qquad\Big(\mu(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})(T)\Big)(v):=-T(\eta(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})(v)).

Indeed, we observe at once that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Big(\mu(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})\mu(x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-2})(T)-\mu(x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-2})\mu(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})(T)\Big)(v)=\\
&T(\eta(x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-2})\eta(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})(v)-\eta(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})\eta(x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-2})(v))=\\
&-\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\,T(\eta(x_{n},\ldots,[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k}],\ldots,x_{2n-2})(v))=\\
&\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\,\Big(\mu(x_{n},\ldots,[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k}],\ldots,x_{2n-2})(T)\Big)(v),\end{split}

where we employed (
2.2
) on the second equality.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\Big(\mu([x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}],x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2})(T)\Big)(v)=-T(\eta([x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}],x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2})(v))=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k}\,T(\eta(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{k},\ldots,x_{n})\eta(x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k})(v))=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k}\,T(\eta(x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k})\eta(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{k},\ldots,x_{n})(v))=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k}\,\Big(\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{k},\ldots,x_{n})\mu(x_{n+1},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{k})(T)\Big)(v),

where we applied (
2.5
) on the third equality.

For a direct proof, one that does not appeal to an analogue of
[
1
, Prop. 2.5(b)]
, we refer the reader to
[
18
, Thm. 3.9]
.

2.1.2.
Generalized derivations

In accordance with the 3-Lie algebras, given a
n
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
, a linear operator
D:\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}
is called a
derivation
if

D([x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}])=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,[x_{1},\ldots,D(x_{k}),\ldots,x_{n}]

for any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{L}
. Accordingly, it follows at once from the fundamental identity (
2.1
) that

\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}):\mathcal{L}\to\mathcal{L}

given by (
2.4
) is a derivation for any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}\in\mathcal{L}
. Such derivations are called
inner derivations
.

A
generalized derivation
of a
n
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
, on the other hand, is defined to be a linear map
D:\mathcal{L}_{n-1}\to\mathcal{L}
satisfying

(2.9)

\displaystyle D(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-2},[x_{n-1},\ldots,x_{2n-2}])=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,[x_{n-1},\ldots,D(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-2},x_{n-1+k}),\ldots,x_{2n-2}],

(2.10)

\displaystyle[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},D(x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-2})]+(-1)^{n}[D(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}),x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-2}]=

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\,D(x_{n},\ldots,[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k}],\ldots,x_{2n-2}),

(2.11)

\displaystyle D(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-2},D(x_{n-1},\ldots,x_{2n-3}))=\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\,D(x_{n-1},\ldots,D(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-2},x_{n-1+k}),\ldots,x_{2n-2}),

for any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}\in\mathcal{L}
, see
[
18
, Def. 5.1]
.

It follows at once, as is noted in
[
18
, Lemma 5.3]
, that for any
y\in\mathcal{L}

\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits_{y}:\mathcal{L}_{n-1}\to\mathcal{L}
,
\mathop{\rm ad}\nolimits_{y}(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-1}):=[y,z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-1}]
, is a generalized derivation. Such a generalized derivation is called an
inner generalized derivation
.

Let us note also that
D:\mathcal{L}_{n-1}\to\mathcal{L}
being a generalized derivation,
\mathcal{L}\oplus_{D}k:=\mathcal{L}\oplus k
is a
n
-Lie algebra through

(2.12)

[x_{1}+\alpha_{1},\ldots,x_{n}+\alpha_{n}]:=[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k+1}\alpha_{k}D(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{k}},\ldots,x_{n}).

Remarks on the conditions (
2.9
) and (
2.11
) are in order now.

Remark 2.4
.

Setting

(2.13)

D^{\sharp}:\mathcal{L}_{n-2}\to g\ell(\mathcal{L}),\qquad D^{\sharp}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-2})(y):=D(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-2},y),

we see at once that (
2.9
) is equivalent to
D^{\sharp}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-2})\in g\ell(\mathcal{L})
being a derivation of the
n
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
, for any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-2},y\in\mathcal{L}
.

Remark 2.5
.

The condition (
2.11
) is equivalent for the
n
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
to have the structure of a
(n-1)
-Lie algebra through

D:\mathcal{L}_{n-1}\to\mathcal{L},\qquad x_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{n-1}\mapsto D(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1}).

As for the condition (
2.10
), we have the following analogue of
[
17
, Lemma 3.5]
. Let us, however, record first the semi-direct sum Leibniz algebra
\mathcal{L}\rtimes\mathcal{L}_{n-1}:=\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
associated to a
n
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
.

To begin with,
\mathcal{L}
being an adjoint representation over itself via (
2.4
), it becomes a representation of the Leibniz algebra
\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
through

x\triangleright y:=[x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1},y],\qquad y\triangleleft x:=-[x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1},y],

for any
y\in\mathcal{L}
and any
x:=x^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x^{n-1}\in\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
. Accordingly,
\mathcal{L}\oplus\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
is a Leibniz algebra, the bracket on which being

[y_{1}+x_{1},y_{2}+x_{2}]:=[x_{1}^{1},\ldots,x_{1}^{n-1},y_{2}]-[x_{2}^{1},\ldots,x_{2}^{n-1},y_{1}]+[x_{1},x_{2}],

for any
y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathcal{L}
, and any
x_{1}:=x_{1}^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{1}^{n-1},x_{2}:=x_{2}^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{2}^{n-1}\in\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
. Let us note that

[x_{1},x_{2}]:=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\,x_{2}^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge[x_{1}^{1},\ldots,x_{1}^{n-1},x_{2}^{k}]\wedge\dots\wedge x_{2}^{n-1}\in\mathcal{L}_{n-1}.

Proposition 2.6
.

Given a
n
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
, let
D:\mathcal{L}_{n-1}\to\mathcal{L}
be a generalized derivation. Then,

D:\mathcal{L}\rtimes\mathcal{L}_{n-1}\to\mathcal{L}\rtimes\mathcal{L}_{n-1},\qquad D(y+x):=D(x),

for any
y\in\mathcal{L}
and any
x:=x^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x^{n-1}\in\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
, is a derivation of Leibniz algebras.

Proof.

On the one hand we have

\displaystyle D([y_{1}+x_{1},y_{2}+x_{2}])=D([x_{1}^{1},\ldots,x_{1}^{n-1},y_{2}]-[x_{2}^{1},\ldots,x_{2}^{n-1},y_{1}]+[x_{1},x_{2}])=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\,D(x_{2}^{1},\ldots,[x_{1}^{1},\ldots,x_{1}^{n-1},x_{2}^{k}],\ldots,x_{2}^{n-1}),

while on the other hand,

\displaystyle[D(y_{1}+x_{1}),y_{2}+x_{2}]+[y_{1}+x_{1},D(y_{2}+x_{2})]=

\displaystyle[D(x_{1}),y_{2}+x_{2}]+[y_{1}+x_{1},D(x_{2})]=

\displaystyle[x_{1}^{1},\ldots,x_{1}^{n-1},D(x_{2}^{1},\ldots,x_{2}^{n-1})]-[x_{2}^{1},\ldots,x_{2}^{n-1},D(x_{1}^{1},\ldots,x_{1}^{n-1})]=

\displaystyle[x_{1}^{1},\ldots,x_{1}^{n-1},D(x_{2}^{1},\ldots,x_{2}^{n-1})]+(-1)^{n}[D(x_{1}^{1},\ldots,x_{1}^{n-1}),x_{2}^{1},\ldots,x_{2}^{n-1}].

The equality, and hence the claim, now follows from (
2.10
).
∎

2.2.
Cohomology of
n
-Lie algebras

In order to classify the derivation extensions of
n
-Lie algebras, via cohomology, we shall now recall the cohomology theory for
n
-Lie algebras, developed in
[
2
]
, to a cohomology theory with arbitrary coefficients, see for instance
[
18
, Sect. 3]
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a
n
-Lie algebra, and let
(V,\mu)
be a representation of
\mathcal{L}
. Setting
x_{i}:=x_{i}^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{i}^{n-1}\in\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
,

(2.14)

C(\mathcal{L},V):=\bigoplus_{m\geqslant 0}\,C^{m}(\mathcal{L},V),\qquad C^{m}(\mathcal{L},V):={\rm Hom}\Big(\mathcal{L}_{n-1}^{\wedge\,(m-1)}\wedge\mathcal{L},V\Big),\quad m\geqslant 1,

where
C^{0}(\mathcal{L},V):=\mathcal{L}_{n-2}\otimes V
, is a differential complex via

(2.15)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\delta:C^{0}(\mathcal{L},V)\to C^{1}(\mathcal{L},V),\qquad\delta(z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes v)(y):=\mu(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-2},y)(v),\\
&\delta:C^{m}(\mathcal{L},V)\to C^{m+1}(\mathcal{L},V),\qquad m\geqslant 1\\
&\delta f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},y):=\\
&\sum_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant m}\,(-1)^{i}\,f(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,[x_{i},x_{j}],\ldots,x_{m},y)+\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt\sum_{i=1}^{m}\,(-1)^{i}\,f(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{m},[x_{i}^{1},\ldots,x_{i}^{n-1},y])+\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt\sum_{i=1}^{m}\,(-1)^{i+1}\,\mu(x_{i}^{1},\ldots,x_{i}^{n-1})\Big(f(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{m},y)\Big)+\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{n-1+m+i}\,\mu(x_{m}^{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{m}^{i}},\ldots,x_{m}^{n-1},y)\Big(f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-1},x_{m}^{i})\Big),\end{split}

for any
f\in C^{m}(\mathcal{L},V)
.

We shall now present, following
[
2
]
, the relation between the cohomology of a
n
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
, and the cohomology of the associated Leibniz algebra
\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
.

To this end, we shall need the following auxiliary result on the representation of the Leibniz algebra associated to the given
n
-Lie algebra.

Proposition 2.7
.

Given a
n
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
, together with a representation
(V,\mu)
, the linear space
\mathcal{L}_{n-2}\otimes V
is a representation of the Leibniz algebra
\mathcal{L}_{n-1}
through

(2.16)

\displaystyle x\triangleright(z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes v):=

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\,z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge[x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1},z_{k}]\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes v+z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes\mu(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1})(v),

(2.17)

\displaystyle(z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes v)\triangleleft x:=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{n+k}\,x^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{x^{k}}\wedge\dots\wedge x^{n-1}\otimes\mu(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-2},x^{k})(v).

Proof.

Let us begin with the verification of (
1.8
). To this end, we note from (
2.16
) that

\displaystyle y\triangleright(x\triangleright(z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes v))=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\,y\triangleright\Big(z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge[x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1},z_{k}]\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes v\Big)+

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pty\triangleright\Big(z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes\mu(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1})(v)\Big)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\underset{p\neq k}{\sum_{p=1}^{n-2}}\,z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge[y^{1},\ldots,y^{n-1},z_{p}]\wedge\dots\wedge[x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1},z_{k}]\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes v+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\,z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge[y^{1},\ldots,y^{n-1},[x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1},z_{k}]]\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes v+

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\,z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge[x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1},z_{k}]\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes\mu(y^{1},\ldots,y^{n-1})(v)+

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\sum_{p=1}^{n-2}\,z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge[y^{1},\ldots,y^{n-1},z_{p}]\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes\mu(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1})(v)+

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826ptz_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes\mu(y^{1},\ldots,y^{n-1})\mu(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1})(v).

Accordingly, it follows at once that

\displaystyle x\triangleright(y\triangleright(z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes v))-y\triangleright(x\triangleright(z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes v))=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\,z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge[x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1},[y^{1},\ldots,y^{n-1},z_{k}]]\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes v-

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\,z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge[y^{1},\ldots,y^{n-1},[x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1},z_{k}]]\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes v+

\displaystyle z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes\mu(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1})\mu(y^{1},\ldots,y^{n-1})(v)-

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055ptz_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes\mu(y^{1},\ldots,y^{n-1})\mu(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1})(v),

from which, and (
2.2
), we deduce (
1.8
).

As for (
1.9
) we compute, on one hand,

\displaystyle(x\triangleright(z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes v))\triangleleft y=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-2}\sum_{p=1}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{n+p}\,y^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{y^{p}}\wedge\dots\wedge y^{n-1}\otimes\mu(z_{1},\ldots,[x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1},z_{k}],\ldots,z_{n-2},y^{p})(v)+

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{n+p}\,y^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{y^{p}}\wedge\dots\wedge y^{n-1}\otimes\mu(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-2},y^{p})\mu(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1})(v),

and on the other hand

\displaystyle x\triangleright((z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes v)\triangleleft y)=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{n+p}\,x\triangleright\Big(y^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{y^{p}}\wedge\dots\wedge y^{n-1}\otimes\mu(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-2},y^{p})(v)\Big)=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{n-1}\underset{k\neq p}{\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}}\,(-1)^{n+p}\,y^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge[x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1},y^{k}]\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{y^{p}}\wedge\dots\wedge y^{n-1}\otimes\mu(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-2},y^{p})(v)+

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{n+p}\,y^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{y^{p}}\wedge\dots\wedge y^{n-1}\otimes\mu(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1})\mu(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-2},y^{p})(v).

We thus conclude (
1.9
), in view of (
2.2
).

Finally, we proceed onto (
1.10
). To this end, it suffices to compute

\displaystyle((z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes v)\triangleleft x)\triangleleft y=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{n+k}\,\Big(x^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{x^{k}}\wedge\dots\wedge x^{n-1}\otimes\mu(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-2},x^{k})(v)\Big)\triangleleft y=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{p=1}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{k+p}\,y^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{y^{p}}\wedge\dots\wedge y^{n-1}\otimes\mu(x^{1},\ldots,\widehat{x^{k}},\ldots,x^{n-1},y^{p})\mu(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-2},x^{k})(v),

since, in view of (
2.2

\displaystyle(x\triangleright(z_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge z_{n-2}\otimes v))\triangleleft y=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{n+p}\,y^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{y^{p}}\wedge\dots\wedge y^{n-1}\otimes\mu(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1})\mu(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-2},y^{p})(v)-

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{n+p}\,y^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{y^{p}}\wedge\dots\wedge y^{n-1}\otimes\mu(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-2},[x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1},y^{p}])(v)=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{n+p}\,y^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{y^{p}}\wedge\dots\wedge y^{n-1}\otimes\mu(x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1})\mu(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-2},y^{p})(v)+

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{p+1}\,y^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{y^{p}}\wedge\dots\wedge y^{n-1}\otimes\mu([x^{1},\ldots,x^{n-1},y^{p}],z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-2})(v).

The condition (
1.10
), then, follows from (
2.3
).
∎

Proposition 2.8
.

, together with a representation

\displaystyle\Delta^{m+1}(\delta f)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+1})=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{k}\,x_{m+1}^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{x_{m+1}^{k}}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{m+1}^{n-1}\otimes\delta f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m},x_{m+1}^{k})=

\displaystyle d(\Delta^{m}f)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m+1})=

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\cdots\wedge x_{m+1}^{n-1}\otimes f(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{m},x_{m+1}^{k})+

\displaystyle\underset{p\neq k}{\sum_{p=1}^{n-1}}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\,(-1)^{i+1+k}\,x_{m+1}^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{x_{m+1}^{k}}\wedge\dots\wedge[x_{i}^{1}\ldots,x_{i}^{n-1},x_{m+1}^{p}]\wedge\cdots

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\cdots\wedge x_{m+1}^{n-1}\otimes f(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{m},x_{m+1}^{k})+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\,(-1)^{i+1+k}\,x_{m+1}^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{x_{m+1}^{k}}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{m+1}^{n-1}\otimes\mu(x_{i}^{1},\ldots,x_{i}^{n-1})\Big(f(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{i},\ldots,x_{m+1}^{k})\Big),

\displaystyle(-1)^{m+1}\,(\Delta^{m}f)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\triangleleft x_{m+1}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{n+i+m+1+k}\,x_{m+1}^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{x_{m+1}^{i}}\wedge\cdots

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\cdots\wedge x_{m+1}^{n-1}\otimes\mu(x_{m}^{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\widehat{x_{m}^{k}}\wedge\dots\wedge x_{m}^{n-1},x_{m+1}^{i})\Big(f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m-1},x_{m}^{k})\Big).

2.3.
H^{2}(\mathcal{L},V)
and abelian extensions

The cohomology theory for
n
-Lie algebras is qualified to classify the abelian extensions of a
n
-Lie algebra with a representation space of it. More precisely
[
20
, Thm. 5.7]
, see also
[
13
]
, extends to
n
-Lie algebras as follows.

Proposition 2.9
.

Given a
n
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
, a representation
(V,\mu)
of
\mathcal{L}
, and any
f\in C^{2}(\mathcal{L},V)
, the space
V{}_{f}\rtimes\mathcal{L}:=V\oplus\mathcal{L}
is a
n
-Lie algebra through

(2.18)

[v_{1}+x_{1},\ldots,v_{n}+x_{n}]:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k+1}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{k},\ldots,x_{n})(v_{k})+(-1)^{n+1}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})+(-1)^{n+1}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

if and only if
f\in Z^{2}(\mathcal{L},V)
.

Proof.

Let us begin with the fundamental identity (
2.1
), that is,

(2.19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&[v_{1}+x_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}+x_{n-1},[v_{n}+x_{n},\ldots,v_{2n-1}+x_{2n-1}]]=\\
&\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,[v_{n}+x_{n},\ldots,[v_{1}+x_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}+x_{n-1},v_{n+k}+x_{n+k}],\ldots,v_{2n-1}+x_{2n-1}],\end{split}

where, on the left hand side we have

\displaystyle[v_{1}+x_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}+x_{n-1},[v_{n}+x_{n},\ldots,v_{2n-1}+x_{2n-1}]]=

\displaystyle[v_{1}+x_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}+x_{n-1},

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{p}\,\mu(x_{n},\ldots,\widehat{x_{n+p}},\ldots,x_{2n-1})(v_{n+p})+(-1)^{n+1}f(x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-1})+(-1)^{n+1}[x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-1}]]=

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{r+n}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{r},\ldots,[x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-1}])(v_{r})+

\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{p+n+1}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})\mu(x_{n},\ldots,\widehat{x_{n+p}},\ldots,x_{2n-1})(v_{n+p})+

\displaystyle\mu(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})\Big(f(x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-1})\Big)+f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},[x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-1}])+[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},[x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-1}]],

while on the right hand side

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,[v_{n}+x_{n},\ldots,[v_{1}+x_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}+x_{n-1},v_{n+k}+x_{n+k}],\ldots,v_{2n-1}+x_{2n-1}]=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,[v_{n}+x_{n},\ldots

\displaystyle\ldots,\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{r+1}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{r},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k})(v_{r})+(-1)^{n+1}\mu(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})(v_{n+k})+

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt(-1)^{n+1}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k})+(-1)^{n+1}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k}],\ldots

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\ldots,v_{2n-1}+x_{2n-1}]=

\displaystyle\underset{p\neq k}{\sum_{p=0}^{n-1}}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{p+n+1}\,\mu(x_{n},\ldots,\widehat{x_{n+p}},\ldots,[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k}],\ldots,x_{2n-1})(v_{n+p})+

\displaystyle\sum_{r=1}^{n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{k+r+1}\,\mu(x_{n},\ldots,\widehat{x_{n+k}},\ldots,x_{2n-1})\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{r},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k})(v_{r})+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{k+n+1}\,\mu(x_{n},\ldots,\widehat{x_{n+k}},\ldots,x_{2n-1})\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\ldots,x_{n-1})(v_{n+k})+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{k+n+1}\,\mu(x_{n},\ldots,\widehat{x_{n+k}},\ldots,x_{2n-1})\Big(f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k})\Big)+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,f(x_{n},\ldots,[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k}],\ldots,x_{2n-1})+\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,[x_{n},\ldots,[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k}],\ldots,x_{2n-1}].

Accordingly, in view of (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), the fundamental identity (
2.19
) holds if and only if

\displaystyle\mu(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})\Big(f(x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-1})\Big)+f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},[x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-1}])=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,(-1)^{k+n+1}\,\mu(x_{n},\ldots,\widehat{x_{n+k}},\ldots,x_{2n-1})\Big(f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k})\Big)+

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,f(x_{n},\ldots,[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k}],\ldots,x_{2n-1}),

which is equivalent to

(\delta f)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{2n-1})=0

for any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n-1}\in\mathcal{L}
.
∎

This
n
-Lie algebra
V{}_{f}\rtimes\mathcal{L}
is called the
abelian extension
of
\mathcal{L}
by
V
via
f\in Z^{2}(\mathcal{L},V)
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Given a
n
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
, a representation
(V,\mu)
of
\mathcal{L}
, and
f,g\in Z^{2}(\mathcal{L},V)
, we have
V{}_{f}\rtimes\mathcal{L}\cong V{}_{g}\rtimes\mathcal{L}
if and only if
f-g\in B^{2}(\mathcal{L},V)
.

Proof.

Let
H:V{}_{f}\rtimes\mathcal{L}\cong V{}_{g}\rtimes\mathcal{L}
be the
n
-Lie algebra isomorphism, which may be given by

H(v+x)=v+h(x)+x,

for some
h\in C^{1}(\mathcal{L},V)
, where
v\in V
and
x\in\mathcal{L}
. Then, it follows from

H([v_{1}+x_{1},\ldots,v_{n}+x_{n}])=[H(v_{1}+x_{1}),\ldots,H(v_{n}+x_{n})]

that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k+1}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{k},\ldots,x_{n})(v_{k})+(-1)^{n+1}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})+

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt(-1)^{n+1}h([x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}])+(-1)^{n+1}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k+1}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{k},\ldots,x_{n})(v_{k}+h(x_{k}))+(-1)^{n+1}g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})+(-1)^{n+1}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}],

end hence that

\displaystyle(-1)^{n+1}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})+(-1)^{n+1}h([x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}])=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,(-1)^{k+1}\,\mu(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{k},\ldots,x_{n})(h(x_{k}))+(-1)^{n+1}g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),

which is equivalent to

f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})-g(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=(\delta h)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),

for any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{L}
.
∎

As a result, we conclude that the abelian extensions of a
n
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
by
V
are in one-to-one correspondence with
H^{2}(\mathcal{L},V)
.

2.4.
H^{2}(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L})
and infinitesimal deformations

Let us next discuss briefly the infinitesimal deformations of a
n
-Lie algebra. More precisely, we shall upgrade
[
20
, Thm. 3.1]
to
n
-Lie algebras.

In accordance with the terminology for Lie algebras, let us define a
deformation
of a
n
-Lie algebra
\mathcal{L}
to be a smooth map

h:\mathcal{L}^{\times\,n}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{L},

so that
h(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},0):=[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
on
\mathcal{L}
, and that
[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]_{t}:=h(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},t)
determines a
n
-Lie algebra structure on
\mathcal{L}
, for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Similarly, an
infinitesimal deformation
of
\mathcal{L}
may be defined to be

\eta:\mathcal{L}^{\times\,n}\to\mathcal{L},\qquad\eta(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{{}_{t=0}}h(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},t),

which induces, by differentiating, that
\eta:\mathcal{L}_{n}\to\mathcal{L}
. In other words,
\eta\in C^{2}(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L})
. Furthermore, the derivative of the fundamental identity

[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},[x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-1}]_{t}]_{t}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,[x_{n},\ldots,[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k}]_{t},\ldots,x_{2n-1}]_{t}

yields

\displaystyle\eta(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},[x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-1}])+[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},\eta(x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-1})]=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,\eta(x_{n},\ldots,[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k}],\ldots,x_{2n-1})+

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\,[x_{n},\ldots,\eta(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n+k}),\ldots,x_{2n-1}]

that is

(\delta\eta)(x_{1},\ldots x_{n-1},x_{n},\ldots,x_{2n-2},x_{2n-1})=0,

or equivalently
\eta\in Z^{2}(\mathcal{L},\mathcal{L})
.

Let us further call two deformations
h_{1}:\mathcal{L}^{\times\,n}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{L}
and
h_{2}:\mathcal{L}^{\times\,n}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{L}
to be equivalent, if there is
u:\mathcal{L}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{L}
such that
u(x,0)=x
, and that

(2.20)

u(h_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},t),t)=h_{2}(u(x_{1},t),\ldots,u(x_{n},t),t)

for any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in\mathcal{L}
, and any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Differentiating (
2.20
) we see that the corresponding infinitesimal deformations

\eta_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{{}_{t=0}}h_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},t),\qquad\eta_{2}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}):=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{{}_{t=0}}h_{2}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},t)

satisfy

\displaystyle g([x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}])+\eta_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\eta_{2}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\,[x_{1},\ldots,g(x_{k}),\ldots,x_{n}],

that is,

\eta_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n})-\eta_{2}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n})=(\delta g)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1},x_{n}),

for

Neutron stars in massive scalar-Gauss-Bonnet gravity: Spherical structure and time-independent perturbations
Rui Xu, Yong Gao, Lijing Shao

Abstract
The class of scalar-tensor theories with the scalar field coupling to the Gauss-Bonnet invariant has drawn great interest since solutions of spontaneous scalarization were found for black holes in these theories. We contribute to the existing literature a detailed study of the spontaneously scalarized neutron stars (NSs) in a typical theory where the coupling function of the scalar field takes the quadratic form and the scalar field is massive. The investigation here includes the spherical solutions of the NSs as well as their perturbative properties, namely the tidal deformability and the moment of inertia, treated in a unified and extendable way under the framework of spherical decomposition. We find that while the mass, the radius, and the moment of inertia of the spontaneously scalarized NSs show very moderate deviations from those of the NSs in general relativity (GR), the tidal deformability exhibits significant differences between the solutions in GR and the solutions of spontaneous scalarization for certain values of the parameters in the scalar-Gauss-Bonnet theory. As a result, the celebrated universal relation between the moment of inertia and the tidal deformability of neutron stars breaks down. With the mass and the tidal deformability of NSs attainable in the gravitational waves from binary NS mergers, the radius measurable using the X-ray satellites, and the moment of inertia accessible via the high-precision pulsar timing techniques, future multi-messenger observations can be contrasted with the theoretical results and provide us necessary information for building up theories beyond GR.



I
Introduction

One aspect of the elegance in Einstein’s general relativity (GR) is that the geometric part of the field equations comes from the Einstein-Hilbert action, which merely consists of the simplest curvature invariant of the spacetime manifold, namely, the Ricci scalar. This simplicity is sacrificed in many alternative theories of gravity, where other curvature invariants are taken in to give modifications to GR, attempting to resolve its fundamental incompatibility with quantum field theory
Stelle 1977
, or to address issues in cosmology including the initial singularity of the Big Bang and the mystery of dark energy
Starobinsky 1980
;
Copeland et al. 2006
;
Nojiri and Odintsov 2011
. Among the quadratic curvature invariants, the Gauss-Bonnet term

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}=-G_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}R^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}=R_{{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}}R^{{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}}-4R_{\alpha\beta}R^{\alpha\beta}+R^{2},

(1)

is special in the sense that adding it multiplied by a constant to the action does not alter the field equations. In the above expression, the double dual of the Riemann curvature tensor is defined as

\displaystyle G_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}:=\frac{1}{4}E_{\alpha\beta\kappa\lambda}E_{\gamma\delta\rho\sigma}R^{\kappa\lambda\rho\sigma},

(2)

with
E_{{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}}
being the Levi-Civita tensor. Here the convention adopted for the Levi-Civita symbol
\epsilon_{{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}}
is
\epsilon_{0123}=1
.

This special property of the Gauss-Bonnet term is only true in the four-dimensional spacetime where the expression
g_{\mu\nu}E_{{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}}
, when antisymmetrized over any five indices, vanishes. Then, the identity

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}{\mathcal{G}}=-4G_{\mu\beta\gamma\delta}R_{\nu}^{\phantom{\mu}\beta\gamma\delta},

(3)

can be proved, and the variation of
{\mathcal{G}}
with respect to the metric can be calculated to obtain

\displaystyle\delta\left(\sqrt{-g}\,{\mathcal{G}}\right)=-4\sqrt{-g}\,D_{\gamma}\left(G_{\alpha}^{\phantom{\alpha}\beta\gamma\delta}\delta\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\phantom{\alpha}\beta\delta}\right),

(4)

where
g
is the determinant of the metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, and
\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\phantom{\alpha}\beta\delta}
are the Christoffel symbols with
D_{\alpha}
being the covariant derivative associated with them. The above variation contributes as a surface term in the action, and hence is dropped in the equations of motion
Lanczos 1938
.

The futility of adding
{\mathcal{G}}
multiplied by a constant to the action did not stop the interest in the Gauss-Bonnet term. On the contrary, it stimulated further investigations in dimensions higher than four
Cai and Guo 2004
;
Konoplya 2005
;
Cai et al. 2005
;
Camanho and Edelstein 2010
, motivated by string theory
Zwiebach 1985
;
Boulware and Deser 1985
. Remarkably, a nontrivial theory in the four-dimensional spacetime was recently established by setting the constant in front of
{\mathcal{G}}
proportional to
1/(D-4)
with
D
being the dimension of the spacetime
Glavan and Lin 2020
. When taking the limit
D\rightarrow 4
, the Gauss-Bonnet term gives a finite contribution to the Einstein field equations and thus modifies GR.

Another direction to investigate possible modifications to GR generated by the Gauss-Bonnet term is introducing a scalar field that couples to it
Torii and Maeda 2005
;
Nojiri et al. 2005
;
Boulware and Deser 1986
;
Koivisto and Mota 2007
;
Cognola et al. 2007
;
Leith and Neupane 2007
;
Akbarieh et al. 2021
. The idea also originates from string theory where a scalar field, called the dilaton, appears when the extra dimensions are compactified to recover the metric tensor of the four-dimensional spacetime
Scherk and Schwarz 1974
. One well-studied case is when the Gauss-Bonnet term couples to an exponential function of the scalar field in the action, and inherits the name dilatonic Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet theory
Kanti et al. 1996
;
Torii et al. 1997
;
Kanti et al. 1998
;
Torii and Maeda 1998
;
Pani and Cardoso 2009
;
Kanti et al. 2011
;
Ayzenberg and Yunes 2014
;
Cunha et al. 2017
;
Ayzenberg and Yunes 2018
;
Gonzalez et al. 2019
;
Blázquez-Salcedo et al. 2016
;
Konoplya et al. 2019
;
Pierini and Gualtieri 2021
;
Yagi et al. 2016
;
Witek et al. 2019
;
Wang et al. 2021
; another basic case has the scalar field directly coupled to the Gauss-Bonnet term so that the field equations are invariant under a constant shift of the scalar field, and is given the name shift-symmetric Gauss-Bonnet theory
Sotiriou and Zhou 2014
;
Saffer and Yagi 2021
. In these theories, the variation of the term involving
{\mathcal{G}}
no long contributes as a surface term with the help of a nontrivial scalar field, and therefore modifies the Einstein field equations. Notice that the scalar field equations in these theories do not admit a constant scalar as the solution. So the metric solutions in these theories are generally different from that in GR. In this work, we are interested in another type of theories where a phenomenon called “spontaneous scalarization” can happen
Damour and Esposito-Farèse 1993
;
Damour and Esposito-Farèse 1996
;
Harada 1998
;
Salgado et al. 1998
;
Ramazanoğlu and Pretorius 2016
;
Mendes and Ortiz 2016
;
Herdeiro et al. 2018
;
Cunha et al. 2019
;
Antoniou et al. 2018
;
Doneva and Yazadjiev 2018a
;
Silva et al. 2018
;
Doneva and Yazadjiev 2018b
;
Kuan et al. 2021
. This requires the theory to admit the trivial GR solution where the scalar field is a constant by assigning an even function of the scalar field coupled to
{\mathcal{G}}
in the action. The theories in Refs.
Antoniou et al. 2018
;
Doneva and Yazadjiev 2018a
;
Silva et al. 2018
;
Doneva and Yazadjiev 2018b
;
Kuan et al. 2021
;
Antoniou et al. 2021
;
Ventagli et al. 2021
, as proved therein, are such theories having solutions of spontaneous scalarization for both black holes and neutron stars (NSs). Here we refer them as scalar-Gauss-Bonnet theories of gravity with spontaneous scalarization, or simply scalar-Gauss-Bonnet theories.

Theories in which compact objects can be scalarized are compelling, because they make identical predictions as those of GR in the weak-field regime, guaranteeing their validity as alternative theories of gravity, but are ready to be distinguished from GR via astrophysical observations of strong-field systems
Damour and Esposito-Farèse 1996
;
Wex 2014
;
Shao and Wex 2016
;
Shao et al. 2017
. These observations include, for example, the shadow image of the supermassive black hole in the galaxy M87 obtained by the Event Horizon Telescope (EHT)
Akiyama et al. 2019
, the orbital decay of binary pulsars observed by pulsar timing arrays
Hulse and Taylor 1975
,
and the gravitational waves (GWs) from coalescences of compact binary systems detected by the LIGO and Virgo detectors
Abbott et al. 2019a
;
Abbott et al. 2021
.
The first comparison between the M87 shadow image and the numerically calculated black hole shadows in the typical scalar-Gauss-Bonnet theory proposed in Ref.
Doneva and Yazadjiev 2018b
has been done by
Cunha et al. 2019
, where a preliminary constraint on the parameter of the theory is obtained. A recent work by
Danchev et al. 2021
first constrains the same theory using the orbital data of three binary pulsars under the framework of Bayesian analysis and MCMC strategy
Shao et al. 2017
. As for using GW data to test scalar-Gauss-Bonnet theories, though it seems still early to talk about numerical bounds, the field is actively developing toward it. Particularly,
Shiralilou et al. 2021a
;
Shiralilou et al. 2021b
recently derives the post-Newtonian solution in scalar-Gauss-Bonnet theories to the first order beyond the quadrupole approximation, providing waveforms for the inspiralling stage of compact binary systems. Numerical simulations that required to build the waveforms for the merger stage of compact binary systems are unavailable but the investigation by
Kovács and Reall 2020
on the well-posedness of the initial-value problem in scalar-Gauss-Bonnet theories has set the first step in the direction.
The ringdown stage of a coalescence can be approximated using perturbation theory in a single-body background spacetime, and the quasinormal modes (QNMs) of nonrotating black holes, which might serve as the basis for constructing the GWs emitted by the remnant black hole, have been studied in scalar-Gauss-Bonnet theories by
Bryant et al. 2021
and
Staykov et al. 2021
. The QNMs of NSs, though in scalar-tensor theories without the Gauss-Bonnet term, have been studied by
Mendes and Ortiz 2018
.

Our work aims at giving a detailed account of the spontaneously scalarized NSs in a typical scalar-Gauss-Bonnet theory with a massive scalar field, which complements the existing literature on spontaneously scalarized black holes as well as the work by
Doneva and Yazadjiev 2018b
where NS solutions of spontaneous scalarization are obtained in a massless theory of such kind. In this paper, the study includes an analysis of the linearized scalar equation to find the onsets of spontaneous scalarization, illustrations of the numerical solutions to the group of nonlinear spherical equations, and a unified treatment of the slow-rotation and the tidal perturbations using the formalism of spherical decomposition introduced by
Regge and Wheeler 1957
and developed for NSs in Refs.
Thorne and Campolattaro 1967
;
Price and Thorne 1969
;
Thorne 1969
.
Our numerical solutions of the scalarized NSs are ready to use for constructing x-ray pulsar pulse profiles via the similar process demonstrated in Refs.
Silva and Yunes 2019
;
Xu et al. 2020
;
Hu et al. 2021
when adequate observational data, for example, from the NS Interior Composition Explorer (NICER) mission, are collected and analyzed to be compared with the theoretical predictions. The x-ray pulsar observations are also expected to measure the moment of inertia for NSs
Hu et al. 2020
;
Miao and Li 2021
, providing another avenue to constrain the theory when compared to the numerical results for the moment of inertia calculated here. The tidal deformability, extracted from GW observations of NS mergers like GW170817
Abbott et al. 2017
, provides a third way to test the theory, and certain values for the parameters of the theory have been excluded by a comparison between the measurement from GW170817 and the numerical results calculated here.

The paper is organized as follows. We start with a general setup for spherical NSs in scalar-Gauss-Bonnet theories in Sec.

II.2
and the group of nonlinear equations in Sec.

. Then numerical results for the tidal deformability and the moment of inertia of the scalarized NSs are presented in Sec.
III.2
and Sec.
III.3
. Finally, conclusions drawn from our study are summarized in Sec.

.
The appendices provide detailed equations and analyses in our study. Appendix

. Appendix

unless conventional units appear in numerical results for the intuitive perception of the physical quantities. The sign convention of the metric is

The linearized scalar field equation is the scalar field equation in Eqs. (
12

\displaystyle\Phi\rightarrow\Phi_{+}e^{r/\lambdabar_{\Phi}}+\Phi_{-}e^{-r/\lambdabar_{\Phi}},

(18)

where the functions
\Phi_{+}
and
\Phi_{-}

For calculating the interior solution, practical numerical integrations cannot begin exactly at the center of the star due to the

Once the interior and the exterior solutions to the linearized scalar field equation are computed separately, the condition for them to form an entire solution is that the values of

The parameters of these NSs, for example, their central energy densities or their masses, are critical in the sense that they mark possible onsets of spontaneous scalarization. \mu,\,\Phi
and
p
are coupled in the group of the ODEs in Eqs. (
12
), the search can be conducted through adjusting the value of
\Phi
at the center of the star (denoted as
\Phi_{0}
) while the value of
\mu
at the center of the star is set to zero and the value of
p
at the center is given by the EOS once the central energy density
\epsilon_{0}
is set to a value close to one of the critical values obtained in the linearized analysis. Similarly to the linearized case, the numerical integrations have to start at a tiny but nonzero radius. So corrections to the starting values of
\mu,\,\Phi
and
p
that can be approximated by their Taylor expansions at the center of the star are requisite. Appendix
A
discusses the expansions and gives the equations from which the expansion coefficients at the order of
r^{2}
can be numerically solved.

Differently from the linearized case, now it is unnecessary to integrate the interior and the exterior regions separately. The integration goes directly from the center of the star to a large enough radius, where
\Phi
likely blows up due to the same
e^{r/\lambdabar_{\Phi}}
factor unless the central value
\Phi_{0}
is properly chosen so that the
e^{r/\lambdabar_{\Phi}}
branch of the solution vanishes. By applying the shooting strategy to find out the appropriate values of
\Phi_{0}
for valid values of
\epsilon_{0}
, solutions of spontaneous scalarization with
\Phi\rightarrow 0
at infinity are attained.

Representative numerical results are plotted on the mass-radius relation diagram in Fig.

. The first thing to be noticed is that solutions of spontaneous scalarization are found around every critical central energy density given by the linearized analysis for the case of
\xi<0
while for the case of
\xi>0
, only the critical central energy density corresponding to the first intersection in the
\Phi^{\prime}/\Phi|_{s}
plot (see Fig.

) is an onset of spontaneous scalarization. Detailed numerical integrations show that around the critical central energy densities corresponding to the second and higher intersections in the
\Phi^{\prime}/\Phi|_{s}
plot for the case of
\xi>0
, solutions of spontaneous scalarization fail to develop because of an unexpected divergence soon after the integrations start. The divergence can only originate from the high nonlinearity in Eqs. (
12
).

III
Time-independent perturbations

In this section, we deal with a slowly rotating and tidally deformed NS under spontaneous scalarization using perturbation theory. The formalism that decomposes the metric perturbation into spherical harmonics introduced by
Regge and Wheeler 1957
and later developed for NSs by
Thorne and Campolattaro 1967
is adopted. We first give a brief review of the formalism.

III.1
Review of the perturbation theory in spherical background

Spherical harmonics form complete sets of orthogonal functions on a 2-sphere, and therefore are naturally the bases to expand the angular dependence of perturbations around a spherical background. Depending on the rotational properties of the quantities on the 2-sphere, scalar, vector, and tensor spherical harmonics may be used. Following the definitions of
Thorne 1980
, the transverse vector spherical harmonics and the traceless transverse tensor spherical harmonics with rank-2 used here are constructed from the usual scalar spherical harmonics
Y_{lm}
via,

\displaystyle\bm{Y}^{E}_{lm}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{l(l+1)}}r\bm{\nabla}Y_{lm}=\frac{r}{\sqrt{l(l+1)}}\left({\bm{d\theta}}\,\partial_{\theta}+{\bm{d\phi}}\,\partial_{\phi}\right)Y_{lm},

\displaystyle\bm{Y}^{B}_{lm}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{l(l+1)}}\bm{r}\times\bm{\nabla}Y_{lm}=\frac{r}{\sqrt{l(l+1)}}\left(-\frac{{\bm{d\theta}}}{\sin\theta}\,\partial_{\phi}+{\bm{d\phi}}\,\sin\theta\,\partial_{\theta}\right)Y_{lm},

\displaystyle\bm{T}^{E2}_{lm}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{(l-1)(l+2)}}\left[r\bm{\nabla}\bm{Y}^{E}_{lm}\right]^{\rm ST}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{l}\,r^{2}\left[{\bm{d\theta}}\otimes{\bm{d\theta}}\,\partial_{\theta}^{2}+{\bm{d\phi}}\otimes{\bm{d\phi}}\left(\sin\theta\cos\theta\,\partial_{\theta}+\partial_{\phi}^{2}\right)+\left({\bm{d\theta}}\otimes{\bm{d\phi}}+{\bm{d\phi}}\otimes{\bm{d\theta}}\right)\partial_{\phi}\left(\partial_{\theta}-\cot\theta\right)\right]^{\rm T}Y_{lm},

\displaystyle\bm{T}^{B2}_{lm}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{(l-1)(l+2)}}\left[r\bm{\nabla}\bm{Y}^{B}_{lm}\right]^{\rm ST}

(20)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{l}\,r^{2}\left[\left(-\frac{{\bm{d\theta}}\otimes{\bm{d\theta}}}{\sin\theta}+{\bm{d\phi}}\otimes{\bm{d\phi}}\,\sin\theta\right)\partial_{\phi}\left(\partial_{\theta}-\cot\theta\right)+{\bm{d\theta}}\otimes{\bm{d\phi}}\,\sin\theta\,\partial_{\theta}^{2}-{\bm{d\phi}}\otimes{\bm{d\theta}}\left(\cos\theta\,\partial_{\theta}+\frac{\partial_{\phi}^{2}}{\sin\theta}\right)\right]^{\rm S}Y_{lm},

where
\bm{\nabla}
is the gradient in three-dimensional Euclidean space,
N_{l}:=\sqrt{2/\left[(l-1)l(l+1)(l+2)\right]}
, and
\{{\bm{dr}},\,{\bm{d\theta}},\,{\bm{d\phi}}\}
is used as the basis for covariant components of a vector in the spherical coordinates
(r,\theta,\phi)
. The superscript “S” means symmetrizing the tensor and the superscript “T” means removing the trace. By dropping the factor
N_{l}\,r^{2}
, the covariant components of
\bm{T}^{E2}_{lm}
without removing the trace and the covariant components of
\bm{T}^{B2}_{lm}
can be recognized as
\psi_{l{\phantom{m}}{\mu\nu}}^{{\phantom{l}}m}
and
\chi_{l{\phantom{m}}{\mu\nu}}^{{\phantom{l}}m}
used by
Regge and Wheeler 1957
.

The perturbative quantities to be expanded using the spherical harmonics are the metric perturbation
\delta g_{{\mu\nu}}
, the fluid displacement
\xi_{\mu}
and the scalar perturbation
\delta\Phi
. On the 2-sphere, the metric perturbation can be decomposed as four scalars:

\displaystyle\delta g_{tt},\ \delta g_{tr},\ \delta g_{rr},\ \delta g_{\theta\theta}+\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta}\delta g_{\phi\phi},

two vectors:

\displaystyle\delta g_{t\theta}\,{\bm{d\theta}}+\delta g_{r\phi}\,{\bm{d\phi}},\ \delta g_{r\theta}\,{\bm{d\theta}}+\delta g_{r\phi}\,{\bm{d\phi}},

and a traceless tensor:

\displaystyle\Big[\delta g_{\theta\theta}\,{\bm{d\theta}}\otimes{\bm{d\theta}}+\delta g_{\theta\phi}\left({\bm{d\theta}}\otimes{\bm{d\phi}}+{\bm{d\phi}}\otimes{\bm{d\theta}}\right)+\delta g_{\phi\phi}\,{\bm{d\phi}}\otimes{\bm{d\phi}}\Big]^{\rm T},

while the fluid displacement can be decomposed as two scalars:

\displaystyle\xi_{t},\ \xi_{r},

and a vector:

\displaystyle\xi_{\theta}\,{\bm{d\theta}}+\xi_{\phi}{\bm{d\phi}}.

Furthermore, the expansions of all the 11 quantities can be conveniently divided into two parts according to the parity of the spherical harmonics: the so-called even-parity ones,
\{Y_{lm},\bm{Y}^{E}_{lm},\bm{T}^{E2}_{lm}\}
, that pick up a sign of
(-1)^{l}
under a parity transformation, and the so-called odd-parity ones,
\{\bm{Y}^{B}_{lm},\bm{T}^{B2}_{lm}\}
, that pick up a sign of
(-1)^{l+1}
under the same transformation. Because the perturbative field equations directly inherit the parity properties of
\delta g_{\mu\nu}
and
\xi_{\mu}
, perturbations with different types of parity do not mix. The same conclusion is also true for the angular momentum numbers
l
and
m
, allowing us to consider perturbations with specific values of
l
and
m
rather than a sum over all the values of them.

With the separation of even and odd parities, the 11 quantities become 22. But since there is no scalar spherical harmonics of odd parity, 7 of them trivially vanish. Moreover, the freedom to choose coordinates at the order of the perturbation can help us eliminate another 4 of them. Using the Regge-Wheeler gauge
Regge and Wheeler 1957
, the four quantities eliminated are all in the metric perturbation: the two vectors with even parity and the traceless tensor with even parity and with odd parity. This finally leaves us
11
nonvanishing quantities to express in terms of the spherical harmonics of a specific parity as well as specific
l
and
m
, namely, for even parity

\displaystyle\delta g_{tt}=e^{2\nu}H_{0}Y_{lm},\quad\delta g_{tr}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H_{1}Y_{lm},\quad\delta g_{rr}=e^{2\mu}H_{2}Y_{lm}

\displaystyle\delta g_{\theta\theta}+\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta}\delta g_{\phi\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2r^{2}KY_{lm},

\displaystyle\xi_{t}=W_{0}Y_{lm},\quad\xi_{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{1}Y_{lm}

\displaystyle\xi_{\theta}\,{\bm{d\theta}}+\xi_{\phi}\,{\bm{d\phi}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V\bm{Y}^{E}_{lm}\frac{\sqrt{l(l+1)}}{r},

\displaystyle\delta\Phi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle XY_{lm},

(21)

and for odd parity

\displaystyle\delta g_{t\theta}\,{\bm{d\theta}}+\delta g_{t\phi}\,{\bm{d\phi}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{0}\bm{Y}^{B}_{lm}\frac{\sqrt{l(l+1)}}{r},

\displaystyle\delta g_{r\theta}\,{\bm{d\theta}}+\delta g_{r\phi}\,{\bm{d\theta}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{1}\bm{Y}^{B}_{lm}\frac{\sqrt{l(l+1)}}{r},

\displaystyle\xi_{\theta}\,{\bm{d\theta}}+\xi_{\phi}\,{\bm{d\phi}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle h_{2}\bm{Y}^{B}_{lm}\frac{\sqrt{l(l+1)}}{r},

(22)

where
H_{0},\,H_{1},\,H_{2},\,K,\,W_{0},\,W_{1},\,V,\,X
and
h_{0},\,h_{1},\,h_{2}
are unknown functions of
t
and
r
to be solved from the perturbative field equations.

To obtain differential equations for the 11 perturbative functions using the field equations (
6
), the fluid displacement
\xi_{\mu}
needs to be related to the perturbation of the 4-velocity, the perturbation of the energy density, and the perturbation of the pressure. The relations are given, for example, by
Gittins et al. 2020
, as

\displaystyle\Delta u^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}u^{\mu}u^{\alpha}u^{\beta}\Delta g_{\alpha\beta},

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta n}{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(g^{\alpha\beta}+u^{\alpha}u^{\beta}\right)\Delta g_{\alpha\beta},

(23)

for the Lagrangian perturbation of the 4-velocity
\Delta u^{\mu}
and the Lagrangian perturbation of the particle number density
\Delta n
in terms of the Lagrangian perturbation of the metric
\Delta g_{\alpha\beta}
. The fluid displacement comes into Eqs. (
23
) when we change the Lagrangian perturbations to the Eulerian perturbations via the definition
\Delta:=\delta+{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}
, where
{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}
is the Lie derivative along the fluid displacement. Therefore, the Eulerian perturbation of the 4-velocity to be used in the perturbative field equations is

\displaystyle\delta u^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}u^{\mu}u^{\alpha}u^{\beta}\left(\delta g_{\alpha\beta}+D_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}+D_{\beta}\xi_{\alpha}\right)

(24)

\displaystyle-\left(\xi^{\lambda}D_{\lambda}u^{\mu}-u^{\lambda}D_{\lambda}\xi^{\mu}\right).

The Eulerian perturbations of the energy density and the pressure can be calculated from their Lagrangian perturbations

\displaystyle\Delta\epsilon=\left(\epsilon+p\right)\frac{\Delta n}{n},\quad\Delta p=\gamma p\frac{\Delta n}{n},

(25)

where the first equation is a result of the first law of thermodynamics under the assumption that the fluid elements are adiabatic, and the second equation can be regarded as the definition of the adiabatic index
\gamma
. Given an EOS relating
\epsilon
and
p
, the adiabatic index
\gamma
can be obtained via

\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{\epsilon+p}{p}\frac{dp}{d\epsilon}.

(26)

Now substituting
\delta g_{\alpha\beta}
and
\xi_{\mu}
from Eqs. (

) and (

), as well as the zeroth-order quantities, into Eqs. (

), (
24
) and (
25
), we can obtain the spherical decompositions for the perturbations of the 4-velocity, the energy density, and the pressure. The perturbative field equations then can be obtained. Once the angular dependence is detached, they give equations for
H_{0},\,H_{1},\,H_{2},\,K,\,W_{0},\,W_{1},\,V,\,X
and
h_{0},\,h_{1},\,h_{2}
. In Appendices
C
and
E
, these equations are displayed with the time dependence set to

, where the constant
\sigma
stands for the frequency of a normal or quasinormal mode of the star.

III.2
Tidal deformation: static even-parity
l=2

Our first application of the perturbation theory in spherical background is calculating the perturbation of the metric under a static external tidal field. The equations and the numerical approach to solve them are summarized in Appendix
C
. The quantity to be calculated is the tidal deformability
\lambda
, defined via
Hinderer 2008

\displaystyle Q_{2m}=-\lambda{\mathcal{E}}_{2m},

(27)

where
Q_{2m}
with
m=0,\pm 1,\pm 2
are the induced quadrupole moments of an originally spherical star and
{\mathcal{E}}_{2m}
are the spherical components of the static external tidal field. Both
Q_{2m}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{2m}
are defined in the asymptotic expansion of the metric perturbation
\delta g_{tt}
of even parity and angular momentum number
l=2
, namely

\displaystyle\delta g_{tt}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{2\nu}H_{0}\sum\limits_{m=-2}^{2}A_{m}Y_{2m}

(28)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\frac{3}{r^{3}}\sum\limits_{m=-2}^{2}Q_{2m}Y_{2m}-r^{2}\sum\limits_{m=-2}^{2}{\mathcal{E}}_{2m}Y_{2m},

where
A_{m}
are
m
-related constants to give angular dependence that matches the second line. Because the perturbative function
H_{0}
is a solution to Eqs. (
54
) that are independent of
m
, its leading asymptotic terms

\displaystyle H_{0}\rightarrow\frac{Q_{2m}}{A_{m}}\frac{3}{r^{3}}-\frac{{\mathcal{E}}_{2m}}{A_{m}}r^{2},

(29)

read off from Eq. (
28
), must be
m
-independent.
Therefore, the tidal deformability
\lambda=-Q_{2m}/{\mathcal{E}}_{2m}
does not depend on
m
.

To calculate
H_{0}
and extract its leading asymptotic expansion coefficients as shown in Eq. (
29
), we set
\sigma=0
in Eqs. (
54
) so they eventually simplify to three coupled ODEs for
H_{0},\,K
and
X
that can be numerically integrated from the center of the star. As usual, practical integrations start at a tiny radius rather than exactly at the center of the star to avoid numerical zeros in denominators. The values of
H_{0},\,K,\,X
and
X^{\prime}
at the tiny radius where the integrations start are then necessary inputs in the integrator. They are given by the Taylor expansions in Eqs. (
55
) with two of the expansion coefficients, say
H_{00}
and
X_{0}
as used in Eqs. (
56
), free to choose for the moment. The two coefficients are actually not free as soon as the physical asymptotic behavior is required for the solution. Similarly to the spherical solution, the asymptotic expression of a general solution to the perturbative equations contains both the factors of
e^{-r/\lambdabar_{\Phi}}
and the factors of
e^{r/\lambdabar_{\Phi}}
, while the contributions from the latter is supposed to be eliminated in the physical solution by adjusting the inputs of the integration through
H_{00}
and
X_{0}
. The problem of generating the physical solution is much simplified since the perturbative equations are homogeneous in the perturbative functions. Following
Pani and Berti 2014
, the physical solution can be found by linearly combining any two independent solutions to make
X
vanish at infinity instead of employing the shooting strategy that aims at finding suitable values of
H_{00}
and
X_{0}
.

In our numerical approach, two independent solutions are found with
(H_{00},\,X_{0})=(1,\,0)
and
(H_{00},\,X_{0})=(1,1)
, and then after the physical solution is constructed from them, two methods for computing the tidal deformability are available. The first one is extracting the coefficients
Q_{2m}/A_{m}
and
{\mathcal{E}}_{2m}/A_{m}
shown in Eq. (
29
) by directly fitting the numerical solution of
H_{0}
in the asymptotic region and then computing
\lambda
. The second one is using the formula developed by
Hinderer 2008
with all the quantities defined on the surface of the star therein substituted by the corresponding values at a sufficiently large
r
where the terms containing the factors of
e^{-r/\lambdabar_{\Phi}}
can be safely dropped so the expansion of
H_{0}
is exactly the same as that in GR for the formula to apply
Hu et al. 2021
. The two methods are no doubt theoretically equivalent but the second one turns out to be too sensitive to numerical errors when
\lambdabar_{\Phi}
is large. The first method is adopted in this work. The issue of the second method with large
\lambdabar_{\Phi}
is discussed in detail in Appendix
D
.

Figure
6
shows the results of the tidal deformability for the NSs in Fig.
3
. For
\xi<0
, the deviations are anyhow tiny so we only plotted the results for the scalarized NSs in the lowest mass bands. The results for
\xi>0
however are quite appealing. First and foremost, the scalarized NSs can have the dimensionless tidal deformability
\lambda/M^{5}
an order of magnitude larger than that of the NSs in GR. As a matter of fact, it is counterintuitive that the tidal deformability of the scalarized NSs is larger than that of the NSs in GR at all since according to the
M
-
R
diagram in Fig.
3
, these scalarized NSs are more compact than their GR counterparts. Secondly, the plots for
a=1
show jumps where the sign of the tidal deformability changes, suggesting that the tidal deformability of the scalarized NSs approaches infinities near the jumps. By investigating the numerically fitted expansion coefficients of
H_{00}
, we find that the coefficient in front of
r^{2}
decreases to zero for the special scalarized NSs that have masses at the discontinuities in the plots for
a=1,\,b=3,10
in Fig.
6
. For scalarized NSs near the special masses, the sign of the
r^{2}
-coefficient in the asymptotic expansion of
H_{00}
changes. This feature neither shows up in GR nor in the nonminimal scalar-tensor theories studied before
Hu et al. 2021
. It is a qualitative difference between the scalar-Gauss-Bonnet gravity studied here and GR.

The large deviations in the tidal deformability of the scalarized NSs from their GR counterparts are no doubt most helpful in testing the scalar-Gauss-Bonnet gravity in astrophysical observations. Simply taking the result from the observation of GW170817:
\lambda/M^{5}\lesssim 720
for
M\approx 1.4M_{\odot}

Abbott et al. 2019b
, we can already exclude the point
(a,\,b)=(0.1,\,3)
in the parameter space for the two popular EOSs AP4 and SLy4. To rule out a region in the parameter space of
(a,\,b)
, numerical results of the tidal deformability with
a
and
b
taking values near the point
(0.1,3)
are necessary to compare with the observational constraint. The implementation of the comparison algorithm into numerical code is beyond the scope of the present work but certainly worth further researching as more and more constraints on the tidal deformability of NSs are expected from future GW observations
Abbott et al. 2018b
.

The second application of the perturbation theory in spherical background is calculating the moment of inertia of slowly rotating scalarized NSs. The problem boils down to solving the first equation in Eqs. (

and

while the product
\sigma h_{2}
keeps finite and represents the radial part of the angular velocity of the star via the match (

). Following
Damour and Esposito-Farèse 1996
, we assume the angular velocity
\Omega
to be constant so that the moment of inertia of the star can be defined as

\displaystyle I:=\frac{J}{\Omega},

(30)

where the angular momentum

is extracted from the leading asymptotic term of

. The match (

) indicates
l=1
for a constant

IV
Summary

In this work which complements the study of spontaneous scalarization of NSs in the massless scalar-Gauss-Bonnet theory of gravitation by
Doneva and Yazadjiev 2018b
, the same phenomenon is investigated in detail for the massive scalar-Gauss-Bonnet theory. A linearized analysis on the scalar field equation is conducted to discover the parameter space as well as the onsets of spontaneous scalarization first. Then, numerical solutions of spontaneous scalarization are computed and their properties are discussed. To gain more insights into the scalarized NSs, their static perturbative properties are investigated. These properties include the tidal deformability and the moment of inertia, both of which we treat under the unified framework of spherical decomposition.

To summarize the results, we find that our solutions share similar qualitative characteristics with the solutions in the massless theory. First, solutions of spontaneous scalarization exist for both positive and negative coupling constants. Second, the scalar field at the center of the star emerges from zero at the onsets of spontaneous scalarization, and develops to finite values while the mass of the star changes (see Fig.
4
). But it does not drop back to zero again, namely that the onsets of spontaneous scalarization obtained by the linearized analysis are one-sided. Solutions of spontaneous scalarization cease to exist on the other end due to the nonlinearity of the leading-order recurrence equations (
36
) at the center of the star. Equations (
36
) only have real solutions for a certain range of the central parameters
\epsilon_{0},p_{0}
and
\Phi_{0}
, restricting the possible combinations of the star mass
M
and the central scalar field
\Phi_{0}
for solutions of spontaneous scalarization. Third, for negative coupling constants, spontaneous scalarization can happen in more than one interval along the
M
-
R
curve (see Fig.
3
). The scalarization interval corresponding to the lowest mass band has scalar solutions possessing zero node. The scalar solutions in the second lowest mass band possess one node, and so on.

More importantly, the quantitative details of our results show differences from those in Ref.
Doneva and Yazadjiev 2018b
since we use a different type of coupling function and consider a massive scalar field. The coupling function used here is quadratic in
\Phi
, which is intuitively weaker than the exponential coupling function used by
Doneva and Yazadjiev 2018b
. Therefore, the ranges of the star mass for spontaneous scalarization in our theory are narrow compared to the results in Ref.
Doneva and Yazadjiev 2018b
. We notice that increasing the absolute value of the coupling constant
\xi
in our theory does not lead to wider bands of the star mass for spontaneous scalarization, indicating that the widths of the mass bands depend on the type of coupling function rather than the magnitude of the coupling constant. Because of the smallness of the spontaneous scalarization bands, we have to zoom in on their
M
-
R
diagrams to observe so that we find that the
M
-
R
segments of the scalarized NSs can cross the
M
-
R
curves in GR when the coupling constant
\xi
is sufficiently negative, a fact that might be easily ignored if the
M
-
R
segments of the scalarized NSs spread out longer.

The mass of the scalar field, according to Fig.
3
, drives the spontaneously scalarized NSs to the higher end along the
M
-
R
curves in GR. Apart from that, its effect is mainly the Yukawa suppression factor
e^{-r/\lambdabar_{\Phi}}
that once dropped, makes the asymptotic behavior of the scalarized solutions identical to that of the solutions in GR. In case the exponential terms are desired, for example, when the reduced Compton wavelength of the scalar field
\lambdabar_{\Phi}
is much larger than the radius of the region that one is interested in, we employ the idea of double expansion, namely expanding the asymptotic solution in factors of
M/r
and factors of
e^{-r/\lambdabar_{\Phi}}
, to come up with the asymptotic expressions (
46
) for the first time to our knowledge. Unexpectedly, we find that Eqs. (
46
) do not have limits when
\lambdabar_{\Phi}\rightarrow\infty
, invalidating our attempt of matching them to the asymptotic expansions (
47
) in the massless scalar-tensor theory.

The scalarized NSs found in our work show very moderate deviations from NSs in GR on the
M
-
R
diagram, making the theory very easy to escape constraints from astrophysical observations of the mass and radius of NSs, especially with the uncertainty from various EOSs. Therefore, we further calculated their time-independent perturbative properties, including the tidal deformability and the moment of inertia. While the moment of inertia of the scalarized NSs only has insignificant differences from that of NSs in GR, remarkable deviations in the tidal deformability of the scalarized NSs from their GR counterparts are found for the case of a positive coupling constant. Not only can the magnitude of the tidal deformability of these scalarized NSs be significantly higher than that of the NSs in GR, but also the sign of the tidal deformability changes to negative where a discontinuity pops up at a special NS mass when the mass of the scalar field in the theory is large (see the panels of
a=1,\,b=3,10
in Fig.
6
). The discontinuity is caused by the vanishing of the
r^{2}
-coefficient in the asymptotic expansion of the metric perturbation
\delta g_{tt}
, a feature that does not occur in GR.

Distinguishing GR and the scalar-Gauss-Bonnet theory with a positive coupling constant becomes feasible with the discrepancies in the tidal deformability of the NSs. For the scalar-Gauss-Bonnet theory with a negative coupling constant, the quasinormal modes of the scalarized NSs might shed light on the way to distinguish them from NSs in GR using observations, which will be studied in a follow-up work. In addition, as the moment of inertia barely shows differences between the scalarized NSs and the NSs in GR, the renowned universal relation between the dimensionless tidal deformability
\lambda/M^{5}
and the dimensionless moment of inertia
I/M^{3}
discovered by
Yagi and Yunes 2017
in GR no longer holds for the scalarized NSs (see Fig.
8
).
The tidal deformability of NSs has begun to be accessible in the gravitational wave data by the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA Collaboration
Abbott et al. 2017
;
Abbott et al. 2019b
;
Abbott et al. 2018b
, and the moment of inertia of binary pulsars is expected to be extractable via pulsar timing in the coming era of the Square Kilometre Array (SKA)
Hu et al. 2020
;
Shao et al. 2015
;
Weltman et al. 2020
. Once they are measured for a sufficient group of NSs with necessary precision, we might be able to tell whether GR should be regarded as an approximation of a fundamentally motivated scalar-Gauss-Bonnet theory
Zwiebach 1985
;
Boulware and Deser 1985
;
Scherk and Schwarz 1974
, or at least, to exclude an appreciable region in the parameter space of it.

Numerical dynamics of integrodifference equations: Hierarchies of invariant bundles in L p ​ ( Ω ) L^{p}(\Omega)
Christian Pötzsche

Abstract
We study how the ”full hierarchy” of invariant manifolds for nonautonomous integrodifference equations on the Banach spaces of p p -integrable functions behaves under spatial discretization of Galerkin type. These manifolds include the classical stable, center-stable, center, center-unstable and unstable ones, and can be represented as graphs of C m C^{m} -functions. For kernels with a smoothing property, our main result establishes closeness of these graphs in the C m − 1 C^{m-1} -topology under numerical discretizations preserving the convergence order of the method. It is formulated in a quantitative fashion and technically results from a general perturbation theorem on the existence of invariant bundles (i.e. nonautonomous invariant manifolds).

Appendix B
Fredholm and Nemytskii operators on
L^{p}
-spaces

Let
(\Omega,{\mathfrak{A}},\mu)
be a measure space with
\sigma
-algebra
{\mathfrak{A}}
and a finite measure
\mu
, while
(Z,\left|\cdot\right|)
denotes a finite-dimensional Banach space over
{\mathbb{K}}
. For
p\in[1,\infty)
we define the space

\displaystyle L^{p}(\Omega,Z)

\displaystyle:=\biggl\{u:\Omega\to Z\biggl|u\text{ is $\mu$-measurable with }\int_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu<\infty\biggr\}

of
Z
-valued
p
-integrable functions and equip it with
\left\|u\right\|_{p}:=\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|u\right|^{p}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu\right)^{1/p}
as norm.

Our analysis requires preparations on Fredholm integral operators

{\mathscr{K}}v:=\int_{\Omega}k(\cdot,y)v(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y).

First, for the kernel
k:\Omega\times\Omega\to{\mathbb{K}}^{d\times n}
we suppose
Hille-Tamarkin conditions
: There exist
p,q\in(1,\infty)
such that

(h1)

k
is
\mu\otimes\mu
-measurable,

(h2)

if
q^{\prime}>1
with
\tfrac{1}{q}+\tfrac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
, then

\int_{\Omega}\biggl(\int_{\Omega}\left|k(x,y)\right|^{q^{\prime}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\biggr)^{p/q^{\prime}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(x)<\infty.

Proposition B.1
.

If
(h1
–
h2)
hold with
p,q>1
, then
{\mathscr{K}}\in L(L^{q}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{n}),L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d}))
is well-defined and compact with

\left\|{\mathscr{K}}\right\|_{L(L^{q}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{n}),L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d}))}\leq\biggl(\int_{\Omega}\biggl(\int_{\Omega}\left|k(x,y)\right|^{q^{\prime}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\biggr)^{\tfrac{p}{q^{\prime}}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(x)\biggr)^{\tfrac{1}{p}}.

(34)

Proof.

See
[
2
, pp. 149–150, 5.12]
and
[
2
, p. 340, 10.15]
for compactness.
∎

Second, let us furthermore consider the Nemytskii operator

[{\mathscr{G}}(u)](x):=g(x,u(x))\quad\text{for $\mu$-a.a.\ }x\in\Omega

induced by a growth function
g:\Omega\times{\mathbb{K}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{K}}^{n}
. Given
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
we assume that for all
0\leq\ell\leq m
one has the following
Carathéodory conditions
:

(c1)

D_{2}^{\ell}g(x,\cdot):{\mathbb{K}}^{d}\to L_{\ell}({\mathbb{K}}^{d},{\mathbb{K}}^{n})
exists and is continuous for
\mu
-a.a.
x\in\Omega
,

(c2)

D_{2}^{\ell}g(\cdot,z):\Omega\to L_{\ell}({\mathbb{K}}^{d},{\mathbb{K}}^{n})
is
\mu
-measurable on
\Omega
for all
z\in{\mathbb{K}}^{d}
.

Proposition B.2
.

Let
p,q\geq 1
,
c\in L^{q}(\Omega)
,
c_{0}\geq 0
and suppose
(c1
–
c2)
hold with
m=0
. If the growth estimate

\left|g(x,z)\right|\leq c(x)+c_{0}\left|z\right|^{\tfrac{p}{q}}\quad\text{for $\mu$-a.a.~$x\in\Omega$ and all }z\in{\mathbb{K}}^{d}

is satisfied, then
{\mathscr{G}}:L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d})\to L^{q}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{n})
is well-defined, bounded and continuous.

Proof.

See
[
20
, p. 63, Thm. 5.1]
.
∎

Corollary B.3
.

Let
1\leq q<p
. If there exists a
\mu
-measurable function
\lambda:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
satisfying

\left|g(x,z)-g(x,\bar{z})\right|\leq\lambda(x)\left|z-\bar{z}\right|\quad\text{for $\mu$-a.a.\ }x\in\Omega\text{ and all }z,\bar{z}\in{\mathbb{K}}^{d}

and
\int_{\Omega}\lambda(y)^{\tfrac{p}{p-q}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)<\infty
, then
{\mathscr{G}}:L^{q}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d})\to L^{q}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{n})
is globally Lipschitz with

\lip{\mathscr{G}}\leq\biggl(\int_{\Omega}\lambda(y)^{\tfrac{p}{p-q}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\biggr)^{\tfrac{p-q}{p}}.

(35)

Proof.

Given
u,\bar{u}\in L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d})
, we obtain

\left\|{\mathscr{G}}(u)-{\mathscr{G}}(\bar{u})\right\|_{q}^{q}=\int_{\Omega}\left|g(y,u(y))-g(y,\bar{u}(y))\right|^{q}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\leq\int_{\Omega}\lambda(y)^{q}\left|u(y)-\bar{u}(y)\right|^{q}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)

and the Hölder inequality
|\int_{\Omega}v_{1}(y)v_{2}(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)|\leq\left\|v_{1}\right\|_{r^{\prime}}\left\|v_{2}\right\|_{r}
for
\tfrac{1}{r}+\tfrac{1}{r^{\prime}}=1
leads to

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathscr{G}}(u)-{\mathscr{G}}(\bar{u})\right\|_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\lambda^{q}\right\|_{r^{\prime}}^{1/q}\left\|\left|u(\cdot)-\bar{u}(\cdot)\right|^{q}\right\|_{r}^{1/q}

\displaystyle=\biggl(\int_{\Omega}\lambda(y)^{qr^{\prime}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\biggr)^{\tfrac{1}{qr^{\prime}}}\biggl(\int_{\Omega}\left|u(y)-\bar{u}(y)\right|^{qr}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\biggr)^{\tfrac{1}{qr}}.

We now choose
r>0
such that
p=rq
(note that
q<p
guarantees
1<r
) and obtain
qr^{\prime}=\tfrac{p}{p-q}
, as well as
r^{\prime}=\tfrac{r}{r-1}
, thus

\left\|{\mathscr{G}}(u)-{\mathscr{G}}(\bar{u})\right\|_{q}\leq\biggl(\int_{\Omega}\lambda(y)^{\tfrac{p}{p-q}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\biggr)^{\tfrac{p-q}{p}}\left\|u-\bar{u}\right\|_{p},

which guarantees the Lipschitz condition (
35
).
∎

Proposition B.4
.

Let
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
p,q\geq 1
with
mq<p
,
c\in L^{\tfrac{pq}{p-mq}}(\Omega)
,
c_{0},\ldots,c_{m}\geq 0
and suppose
(c1
–
c2)
hold. If for
0\leq\ell\leq m
the growth conditions

\left|D_{2}^{\ell}g(x,z)\right|_{L_{\ell}({\mathbb{K}}^{d},{\mathbb{K}}^{n})}\leq c(x)+c_{\ell}\left|z\right|^{\tfrac{p-\ell q}{q}}\quad\text{for $\mu$-a.a.\ }x\in\Omega\text{ and all }z\in{\mathbb{K}}^{d}

(36)

are satisfied, then
{\mathscr{G}}:L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d})\to L^{q}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{n})
is of class
C^{m}
and for each
0\leq\ell\leq m
,
u\in L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d})
the following holds:

(a)

[D^{\ell}{\mathscr{G}}(u)v_{1}\cdots v_{\ell}](x)=D_{2}^{\ell}g(x,u(x))v_{1}(x)\cdots v_{\ell}(x)
for
\mu
-a.a.
x\in\Omega
and functions
v_{1},\ldots,v_{\ell}\in L^{q}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d})
,

(b)

\left\|D^{\ell}{\mathscr{G}}(u)\right\|_{L_{\ell}(L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d}),L^{q}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{n}))}\leq\left\|c\right\|_{\tfrac{pq}{p-\ell q}}+c_{\ell}\left\|u\right\|_{p}^{\tfrac{p-\ell q}{q}}
.

Due to the lack of a suitable reference we provide an explicit proof.

Proof.

Throughout, let
u\in L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d})
and formally define for
\mu
-a.a.
x\in\Omega
that

\displaystyle{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell}(u)(x)

\displaystyle:=D_{2}^{\ell}g(x,u(x))\in L_{\ell}({\mathbb{K}}^{d},{\mathbb{K}}^{n})\quad\text{for all }0\leq\ell\leq m,

\displaystyle[\bar{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell}(u)h](x)

\displaystyle:=D_{2}^{\ell}g(x,u(x))h(x)\in L_{\ell-1}({\mathbb{K}}^{d},{\mathbb{K}}^{n})\quad\text{for all }0<\ell\leq m

and
h\in L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d})
.
First, being
p
-integrable,
u
is
\mu
-measurable on
\Omega
. Since
D_{2}^{\ell}g
satisfy Carathéodory conditions, because of
[
20
, p. 62, Lemma 5.1]
also
x\mapsto D_{2}^{\ell}g(x,u(x))
is
\mu
-measurable on
\Omega
. Second, if we set
q_{\ell}:=\tfrac{pq}{p-\ell q}\geq 1
, then (
36
) become

\left|D_{2}^{\ell}g(x,z)\right|\leq c(x)+c_{\ell}\left|z\right|^{p/q_{\ell}}\quad\text{for all }0\leq\ell\leq m,\text{$\mu$-a.a.~$x\in\Omega$ and all }z\in{\mathbb{K}}^{d}.

(I) Claim:
{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell}:L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d})\to L^{q_{\ell}}(\Omega,L_{\ell}({\mathbb{K}}^{d},{\mathbb{K}}^{n}))
is well-defined and bounded for all
0\leq\ell\leq m
.

Using the growth condition (
36
) and the
L^{q_{\ell}}
-triangle inequality we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|D_{2}^{\ell}g(y,u(y))\right|^{q_{\ell}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\right)^{1/q_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|c(y)+c_{\ell}\left|u(y)\right|^{p/q_{\ell}}\right|^{q_{\ell}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\right)^{1/q_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|c\right\|_{q_{\ell}}+c_{\ell}\left\|u\right\|_{p}^{p/q_{\ell}}

and consequently
\left\|{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell}(u)\right\|_{q_{\ell}}\leq\left\|c\right\|_{q_{\ell}}+c_{\ell}\left\|u\right\|_{p}^{p/q_{\ell}}
.

(II) Claim:
{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell}:L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d})\to L^{q_{\ell}}(\Omega,L_{\ell}({\mathbb{K}}^{d},{\mathbb{K}}^{n}))
is continuous for all
0\leq\ell\leq m
.

Let
(u_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence in
L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d})
converging to
u
w.r.t.
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{p}
. Because of
[
2
, p. 57, 3.22(1)]
this implies pointwise convergence of a subsequence
\mu
-a.e., that is, w.l.o.g. we can suppose
\lim_{j\to\infty}u_{j}(x)=u_{j}(x)
for
\mu
-a.a.
x\in\Omega
, which shows

{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell}(u_{j})(x)=D_{2}^{\ell}g(x,u_{j}(x))\xrightarrow[j\to\infty]{}D_{2}^{\ell}g(x,u(x))={\mathscr{G}}^{\ell}(u)(x)\quad\text{for $\mu$-a.a.\ }x\in\Omega.

The necessity part of Vitali’s convergence theorem
[
2
, p. 57, 3.23]
applies to
(u_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and the growth assumptions on
D_{2}^{\ell}g
guarantee that
[
2
, p. 57, 3.23]
can be applied to
{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell}(u_{j})
with the exponent
q_{\ell}
rather than
p
. Thus, the sufficiency part of
[
2
, p. 57, 3.23]
shows that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\left\|{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell}(u_{j})-{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell}(u)\right\|_{q_{\ell}}=0
, i.e.
{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell}
is continuous.

(III) Claim:
\bar{\mathscr{G}}^{l+1}:L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d})\to L\bigl(L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d}),L^{q_{\ell}}(\Omega,L_{\ell}({\mathbb{K}}^{d},{\mathbb{K}}^{n}))\bigr)
is well-defined and continuous for all
0\leq\ell<m
.

For
h\in L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d})
we obtain from Hölder’s inequality that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|D_{2}^{\ell+1}g(y,u(y))h(y)\right|^{q_{\ell}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\right)^{1/q_{\ell}}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|D_{2}^{\ell+1}g(y,u(y))\right|^{q_{\ell}}\left|h(y)\right|^{q_{\ell}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\right)^{1/q_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|D_{2}^{\ell+1}g(y,u(y))\right|^{q_{\ell+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\right)^{q_{\ell}/q_{\ell+1}}\left\|h\right\|_{p}^{q_{\ell}}\right)^{1/q_{\ell}}=\left\|{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell+1}(u)\right\|_{q_{\ell+1}}\left\|h\right\|_{p}

and consequently
\bar{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell+1}(u)h\in L^{q_{\ell}}(\Omega,L_{\ell}({\mathbb{K}}^{d},{\mathbb{K}}^{n}))
results. The assumptions on
g
yield the inclusion
\bar{\mathscr{G}}^{(\ell+1)}(u)\in L\bigl(L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d}),L^{q_{\ell}}(\Omega,L_{\ell}({\mathbb{K}}^{d},{\mathbb{K}}^{n}))\bigr)
, while the continuity of
\bar{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell}
is shown as in step (II).

(IV) Claim:
{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell}:L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d})\to L^{q_{\ell}}(\Omega,L_{\ell}({\mathbb{K}}^{d},{\mathbb{K}}^{n}))
is continuously differentiable for all
0\leq\ell<m
.

Because
D_{2}^{\ell}g
is assumed to be continuously differentiable in the second argument, the Mean Value Theorem and Jensen’s Inequality imply

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell}(u+h)-{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell}(u)-\bar{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell+1}(u)h\right\|_{q_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|D_{2}^{\ell}g(y,u(y)+h(y))-D_{2}^{\ell}g(y,u(y))-D_{2}^{\ell+1}g(x,u(y))h(y)\right|^{q_{\ell}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\right)^{1/{q_{\ell}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left|D_{2}^{\ell+1}g(y,u(y)+\vartheta h(y))-D_{2}^{\ell+1}g(x,u(y))]h(y)\right|\,{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta\right)^{q_{\ell}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\right)^{1/{q_{\ell}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}\left|D_{2}^{\ell+1}g(y,u(y)+\vartheta h(y))-D_{2}^{\ell+1}g(x,u(y))]h(y)\right|^{q_{\ell}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\right)^{1/{q_{\ell}}}

and we obtain from Fubini’s theorem along with Hölder’s inequality

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell}(u+h)-{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell}(u)-\bar{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell+1}(u)h\right\|_{q_{\ell}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\left|D_{2}^{\ell+1}g(y,u(y)+\vartheta h(y))-D_{2}^{\ell+1}g(x,u(y))]h(y)\right|^{q_{\ell}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta\right)^{1/{q_{\ell}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\left|D_{2}^{\ell+1}g(y,u(y)+\vartheta h(y))-D_{2}^{\ell+1}g(x,u(y))\right|^{q_{\ell}}\left|h(y)\right|^{q_{\ell}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\,{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta\right)^{1/{q_{\ell}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{\Omega}\left|D_{2}^{\ell+1}g(y,u(y)+\vartheta h(y))-D_{2}^{\ell+1}g(x,u(y))\right|^{q_{\ell+1}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\mu(y)\right)^{q_{\ell}/{q_{\ell+1}}}\left\|h\right\|_{p}^{q_{\ell}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta\right)^{1/{q_{\ell}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{1}\left\|{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell+1}(u+\vartheta h)-{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell+1}(u)\right\|_{L^{q_{\ell+1}}(\Omega,L_{\ell}({\mathbb{K}}^{d},{\mathbb{K}}^{n})))}^{q_{\ell}}\,{\mathrm{d}}\vartheta\right)^{1/q_{\ell}}\left\|h\right\|_{p}

for
h\in L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d})
.
Due to the continuity of
{\mathscr{G}}^{\ell+1}:L^{p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{K}}^{d})\to L^{q_{\ell+1}}(\Omega,L_{\ell+1}({\mathbb{K}}^{d},{\mathbb{K}}^{n}))
the integral tends to zero in the limit

, and hence

is Frechét differentiable in

by uniqueness of derivatives. Its continuity was already shown in step (III).

(V) In particular, this establishes that

Abstract
. We prove that Picard-Lindelöf iterations for an arbitrary smooth normal Cauchy problem for PDE converge if we assume a suitable Weissinger-like sufficient condition. This condition includes both a large class of non-analytic PDE or initial conditions, and more classical real analytic functions. The proof is based on a Banach fixed point theorem for contractions with loss of derivatives. From the latter, we also prove an inverse function theorem for locally Lipschitz maps with loss of derivatives in arbitrary graded Fréchet spaces.

2.
A Banach fixed point theorem with loss of
derivatives

The idea to extend the classical Banach fixed point theorem to sequentially
complete subsets

(E,(|-|)_{\alpha\in\Lambda})

\forall\alpha\in\Lambda\,\exists k_{\alpha}\in[0,1)\,\forall x,y\in X:\ \left|P(x)-P(y)\right|_{\alpha}\leq k_{\alpha}|x-y|_{\alpha}.

The notion of contraction has also been extended to uniform spaces
(
[
29
,
30
]
) and to condensing maps on Hausdorff locally
convex linear spaces via the notion of measure of non-compactness
(see e.g.
[
2
]
and references therein). See also
[
1
]

for a recent survey, and
[
10
,
33
,
6
,
34
]
for updated
references framed in locally convex linear spaces.

In the present section, we want to prove a Banach fixed point theorem
for contractions with loss of derivatives in graded Fréchet spaces.
In this paper, by a
graded Fréchet space

\left(\mathcal{F},\left(\|-\|_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\right)

we mean a Hausdorff, complete topological vector space whose topology
is defined by an increasing sequence of seminorms:
\|-\|_{k}\leq\|-\|_{k+1}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We denote by
B_{r}^{k}(x):=\left\{y\in X\mid\|x-y\|_{k}<r\right\}

the ball of radius
r\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
defined by the
k
-norm.

A first trivial and well known result we will use is the following:

Lemma 1
.

Let
(\mathcal{F},\tau)
be a topological
space,
P:X\rightarrow\mathcal{F}
be a continuous function, and
assume that there is
y_{0}\in X
such that
\exists\,\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}P^{n}(y_{0})\in X

Then
\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}P^{n}(y_{0})
is a fixed point of

P.
In particular, this applies if
\mathcal{F}
is a Fréchet space
and
\left(P^{n}(y_{0})\right)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence of
points of
X

is a Cauchy complete
subspace.

Proof.

The usual proof works:
\overline{y}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}P^{n}(y_{0})\in X

exists by assumption, and since
P:X\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}
is continuous
we have

P(\overline{y})=P\left(\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}P^{n}(y_{0})\right)=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}P^{n+1}(y_{0})=\overline{y}.\qed

In particular, this general Lem.
1

to
contractions with loss of derivatives
in Fréchet spaces:
A key idea in defining this notion is that it has to depend on the

Definition 2

Let
\left(\mathcal{F},\left(\|-\|_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\right)

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

For all

\left\|P^{n+1}\left(y_{0}\right)-P^{n}\left(y_{0}\right)\right\|_{k}\leq\alpha_{kn}\left\|P\left(y_{0}\right)-y_{0}\right\|_{k+nL},

\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\alpha_{kn}\left\|P(y_{0})-y_{0}\right\|_{k+nL}<+\infty.

Note that if we actually have only one norm

We first note that condition (

(i)

We have a loss

(ii)

We will see only in Sec.

2

(iii)

(iv)

Since we want to take

3.
Solutions of equations

In this Section, we want to use the Banach fixed point Thm.

We have already mentioned that the first inequality in (

We now introduce the following definition of Lipschitz map:

Definition 10
.

Let

.
We say that a map

B

with loss of derivatives
(LOD)

and Lipschitz

\left(\Lambda_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

\forall y\in B:\ G\left(-,-,y\right)\in\mathcal{C}_{t}^{p}\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\infty}\left(T\times S,\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)
;

(ii)

\Lambda_{k}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(T\times S)
for all

(iii)

If
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
,
|\nu|\leq k
,
h=1,\ldots,m
,

,

, then

\left|\partial_{x}^{\nu}G^{h}(t,x,u)-\partial_{x}^{\nu}G^{h}(t,x,v)\right|\leq\Lambda_{k}(t,x)\cdot\max_{l=1,\ldots,m}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|\leq k+L\\
\gamma\leq p\end{subarray}}\left|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}(u^{l}-v^{l})(t,x)\right|.

(4.10)

We simply say that
G

is Lipschitz on

B

with LOD

if the previous conditions
(ii)
and
(iii)

hold for some
\left(\Lambda_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

In the next theorem, we prove that if

is defined by (

)
and all the radii

, then

is always Lipschitz
with respect to
constant
factors
\left(\Lambda_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}

in the space
\bar{B}_{R}(i_{0})\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{t}^{p}\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\infty}\left(T\times S,\mathbb{R}^{m}\right)

defined in (

) and with loss of derivatives

given, as in (

), by the maximum order of derivatives
in

suitable for the proof of the PLT if we are also able to prove that

.
On the other hand, in Sec.
5
we will show examples
of PDE with constant Lipschitz factors
\Lambda_{k}
but where we
are free to also take

result is only a sufficient condition.

Theorem 11
.

Let
r_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
for all

.
Set

,

as in (

(4.11)

Then the function
G
defined in (
4.9
) is Lipschitz in

\bar{B}_{R}(i_{0})
with loss of derivatives
L
and constant Lipschitz
factors.

Proof.

We only have to prove condition
(iii)
of Def.
10
,
so that we consider
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
,
|\nu|\leq k
,

h=1,\ldots,m
,
u
,
v\in\bar{B}_{R}(i_{0})
,
(t,x)\in T\times S
.
Note that

\partial_{x}^{\nu}G^{h}(t,x,u)=\partial_{x}^{\nu}\left\{F^{h}\left[t,x,\left(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}u\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|\leq L\\
\gamma\leq p\end{subarray}}\right]\right\}.

(4.12)

We first prove the case
\left|\nu\right|=0
. Since

,
we have

\gamma\leq p\end{subarray}}C_{0\alpha,\gamma}=:C_{0}\Subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
for all

and
\gamma\leq p

Similarly,

.
Thereby, using (

), we have

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{x}^{\nu}G^{h}(t,x,u)-\partial_{x}^{\nu}G^{h}(t,x,v)\right|

\gamma\leq p\end{subarray}}\left|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}(u^{l}-v^{l})(t,x)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\leq\|F\|_{1}\cdot\max_{l=1,\ldots,m}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|\leq k+L\\
\gamma\leq p\end{subarray}}\left|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}(u^{l}-v^{l})(t,x)\right|,

,
and now consider the case

From Faà di Bruno’s formula

\partial_{x}^{\nu}G^{h}(t,x,u)=\sum_{1\leq|\eta|\leq|\nu|}\partial^{\eta}F^{h}\left[t,x,\left(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}u\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|\leq L\\
\gamma\leq p\end{subarray}}\right]\cdot B_{\eta\nu}\left[\left(\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}u(t,x)\right)_{\mu\gamma}\right],

(4.13)

where

are Bell’s like
polynomials such that
|\mu|\leq|\nu|+|\alpha|\leq k+L
for all

.
For simplicity, set

\displaystyle\partial^{\eta}F^{h}\left(t,x,u\right)

\displaystyle:=\partial^{\eta}F^{h}\left[t,x,\left(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}u\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|\leq L\\
\gamma\leq p\end{subarray}}\right]

(4.14)

\displaystyle Q_{\eta\nu}(t,x,u)

\displaystyle:=B_{\eta\nu}\left[\left(\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}u(t,x)\right)_{\mu\gamma}\right],

(4.15)

so that we can estimate

\left|\partial_{x}^{\nu}G^{h}(t,x,u)-\partial_{x}^{\nu}G^{h}(t,x,v)\right|=\\
=\left|\sum_{\eta}\partial^{\eta}F^{h}(t,x,u)\cdot Q_{\eta\nu}(t,x,u)-\sum_{\eta}\partial^{\eta}F^{h}(t,x,v)\cdot Q_{\eta\nu}(t,x,v)\right|\leq\\
\leq\left|\sum_{\eta}\partial^{\eta}F^{h}(t,x,u)\cdot Q_{\eta\nu}(t,x,u)-\sum_{\eta}\partial^{\eta}F^{h}(t,x,u)\cdot Q_{\eta\nu}(t,x,v)\right|+\\
+\left|\sum_{\eta}\partial^{\eta}F^{h}(t,x,u)\cdot Q_{\eta\nu}(t,x,v)-\sum_{\eta}\partial^{\eta}F^{h}(t,x,v)\cdot Q_{\eta\nu}(t,x,v)\right|.\qquad

For some
L_{\eta\nu}>0
depending on

, the first summand
yields

\left|\partial^{\eta}F^{h}(t,x,u)\right|\cdot\left|Q_{\eta\nu}(t,x,u)-Q_{\eta\nu}(t,x,v)\right|\leq\\
\leq\|F\|_{k}\cdot L_{\eta\nu}\cdot\max_{l=1,\ldots,m}\max_{|\mu|\leq k+L}\left|\partial_{x}^{\mu}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}(u^{l}-v^{l})(t,x)\right|.

The second summand gives

\left|\partial^{\eta}F^{h}(t,x,u)-\partial^{\eta}F^{h}(t,x,v)\right|\cdot\left|Q_{\eta\nu}(t,x,v)\right|\leq\\
\leq\|F\|_{k+1}\cdot\max_{l=1,\ldots,m}\max_{|\alpha|\leq k+L}\left|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}(u^{l}-v^{l})(t,x)\right|\cdot N_{k},

where
N_{k}:=\max_{|\eta|\leq|\nu|\leq k}\max_{(t,x,v)\in T\times S\times C_{\eta\nu}^{k}}\left|Q_{\eta\nu}(t,x,v)\right|

and, as we did above,
v\in\bar{B}_{R}(i_{0})
yields some
C_{\eta\nu}^{k}\Subset\mathbb{R}^{m}

such that
\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}v(t,x)\in C_{\eta\nu}^{k}

for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{s}
such that
|\alpha|\leq k+L
. We finally
obtain

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{x}^{\nu}G^{h}(t,x,u)-\partial_{x}^{\nu}G^{h}(t,x,v)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq|\eta|\leq|\nu|}\left(\|F\|_{k}\cdot\max_{|\eta|\leq|\nu|\leq k}L_{\eta\nu}+\|F\|_{k+1}\cdot N_{k}\right)\cdot

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq}\cdot\max_{l=1,\ldots,m}\max_{|\alpha|\leq k+L}\left|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}(u^{l}-v^{l})(t,x)\right|.

Setting

\displaystyle\tilde{\Lambda}_{k}(\nu)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{1\leq|\eta|\leq|\nu|}\left(\|F\|_{k}\cdot\max_{|\eta|\leq|\nu|\leq k}L_{\eta\nu}+\|F\|_{k+1}\cdot N_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k}

\displaystyle:=\max_{|\nu|\leq k}\tilde{\Lambda}_{k}(\nu),

we get the conclusion.
∎

4.3.
The Picard-Lindelöf theorem for smooth normal PDE

A natural method to solve PDE is to transform it into an infinite-dimensional
ODE and then apply a PLT, see e.g.
[
27
]
. On the other hand,
our approach can be considered simpler because we do not transform
partial derivatives into ordinary ones in infinite dimensional spaces.

We can now state our main local existence result for smooth normal
systems of PDE.

Theorem 12
.

Let
\Lambda_{k}\in\mathcal{C}^{0}(T\times S)
,
r_{k}\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\cup\{+\infty\}

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and assume that
\mathring{S}
is dense
in

\bar{B}_{R}(i_{o})
as in
(
4.11

Assume that

is Lipschitz on
\bar{B}_{R}(i_{0})

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k,0}^{j}

\displaystyle:=1,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{k,n+1}^{j}(t,x)

(4.16)

\displaystyle:=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in S\\
0<j\leq d\end{subarray}}\Lambda_{k,n}^{j}(t_{0}+\max(a,b),x).

all

(ii)

{\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}}\bar{\Lambda}_{k,n}\cdot\left\|P\left(i_{0}\right)-i_{0}\right\|_{k+nL}<+\infty

of the problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{d}y(t,x)=F\left[t,x,\left(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}y\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|\leq L\\
\gamma\leq p\end{subarray}}\right],\\
\partial_{t}^{j}y(t_{0},x)=y_{0j}(x)\ j=0,\ldots,d-1,\end{cases}

given by

\left(\mathcal{C}_{t}^{p}\mathcal{C}_{x}^{\infty}\left(T\times S,\mathbb{R}^{m}\right),\left(\left\|-\right\|_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\right)

which satisfies

\forall k,m\in\mathbb{N}:\ \left\|y-P^{m}\left(i_{0}\right)\right\|_{k}\leq\sum_{n=m}^{+\infty}\bar{\Lambda}_{k,n}\cdot\left\|P\left(i_{0}\right)-i_{0}\right\|_{k+nL}.

, we set

\bar{M}_{kj}(t,x):=\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\,\hbox{\rm d}s_{j}\displaystyle\mathop{\,\ldots\,}^{j}\int_{t_{0}}^{s_{2}}M_{k}(s_{1},x)\,\,\hbox{\rm d}s_{1}\right|,

and we also assume

(iii)

\left|\partial_{x}^{\nu}G(t,x,u)\right|\leq M_{k}(t,x)

for all

(t,x)\in T\times S
and all
\nu\in\mathbb{N}^{s}

such that

;

(iv)

{\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in S\\
0<j\leq d\end{subarray}}}\bar{M}_{k,j}(t_{0}+\max(a,b),x)\leq r_{k}

We prove that

We firstly show, by induction on

\gamma\leq p\end{subarray}}\left|\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}\left[P^{n}(u)^{l}-P^{n}(v)^{l}\right](s_{1},x)\right|\,\,\hbox{\rm d}s_{1}=:(3^{*}).

(ii)
and Thm.

\displaystyle y(t,x)

\displaystyle=i_{0}(t,x)+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\,\hbox{\rm d}s_{d}\displaystyle\mathop{\,\ldots\,}^{d}\int_{t_{0}}^{s_{2}}G(s_{1},x,y)\,\,\hbox{\rm d}s_{1}

\displaystyle=i_{0}(t,x)+\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\,\hbox{\rm d}s_{d}\displaystyle\mathop{\,\ldots\,}^{d}\int_{t_{0}}^{s_{2}}F\left[s_{1},x,\left(\partial_{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{t}^{\gamma}y\right)_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|\leq L\\

\displaystyle M_{k}

\displaystyle:=\max_{(t,x,z)\in C_{k}}\max_{|\nu|\leq k}\left|\partial_{x}^{\nu}G(t,x,z)\right|

\max(a,b)\leq\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{r_{k}}{M_{k}}

y(t,x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\gamma-1}\frac{y_{0j}(x)}{j!}(t-t_{0})^{j}+\sum_{h=0}^{+\infty}\sum_{j=\gamma}^{d-1}\frac{\partial_{x}^{h\mu}y_{0j}(x)}{(j-\gamma)!}\mu_{j-\gamma,h}(t)+\sum_{h=0}^{+\infty}\eta_{h}(t,x).

\displaystyle y(t,x)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+q\sum_{h=0}^{+\infty}p^{h}\frac{(t-t_{0})^{(h+1)(d-\gamma)}}{\left[(h+1)(d-\gamma)\right]!}.

corresponding to initial conditions

, where

.
If we can exchange

As we discussed in the previous sections, Def.

Definition 21

We call

Lemma 22
.

Let
\left(\mathcal{F},\left(\|-\|_{k}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\right)

be a Fréchet space,
X
be a closed subset of
\mathcal{F}
,
y_{0}\in X

and
P:X\longrightarrow X
be a continuous map. Assume that
\left\|-\right\|_{0}

(hence,
\left\|-\right\|_{k}
for every

) is a
norm. If there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
such that

is a fixed point of

, then
P\in\LoD\left(X,0,y_{0}\right)
,

is a contraction with
0

.

Proof.

Let
N
be the smallest number such that

If

, so our claim follows just by setting each

If

,
\left\|P\left(y_{0}\right)-y_{0}\right\|_{0}\neq 0

since
\left\|-\right\|_{0}
is a norm, therefore
\left\|P\left(y_{0}\right)-y_{0}\right\|_{k}\neq 0

for every

, as the norms
\left\|-\right\|_{k}

are increasing. For every

set

\alpha_{kn}:=\begin{cases}\frac{\left\|P^{n+1}\left(y_{0}\right)-P^{n}\left(y_{0}\right)\right\|_{k}}{\left\|P\left(y_{0}\right)-y_{0}\right\|_{k+nL}},&\text{if}\,n<N;\\
\frac{1}{n^{2}\left\|P\left(y_{0}\right)-y_{0}\right\|_{k+nL}},&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(6.1)

With this choice, Def.

, hence

.
∎

7.
Conclusions

Starting from the classical Kowalevski counter-example for the heat
equation or Hadamard’s results on the Cauchy problem for the Laplace
equation, one can think that a PDE links in a given relation
\partial_{t}y

and
\partial_{x}y
and hence it necessarily forces the solution,
in general, in a space of functions whose derivatives growth in a
restricted way, these constraints being related to the PDE itself.
This implies that the initial conditions cannot be freely chosen but
must be taken into another constrained space. We could say that we
do not have to find a suitable space of generalized solutions for
our PDE, but conditions stating when it has a solution or not; only
at the philosophical level, this is similar to the point of view of
nonlinear differential Galois theory, see e.g.
[
23
]
, or the
formal theory of differential equations, see e.g.
[
28
]
.

The PLT we proved in this paper goes exactly in this direction, by
showing that the existence of a solution (by Picard-Lindelöf iterations)
depends on the initial conditions we start with: Def.
2

of contraction with loss of derivatives, the closure with respect
to iterations
(ii)
, Weissinger condition (
W
),
the definition of the radii (
5.6
), all go in this direction.
Examples considered in Sec.
5
show a first link
between the syntax of the PDE (in the term
(nd)!
) and the order
of growth of the derivatives of the initial conditions
\|y_{0j}\|_{k+nL}
.
On the other hand, exactly as up to fourth order algebraic equations
are solvable in radicals, if the order
d=1
the method of characteristics
allows one to solve a large class of PDE for
any
initial condition.

It is now natural to ask for a generalization to more singular normal
(nonlinear) PDE, e.g. where the right hand side
F
or some of the
initial conditions
y_{0j}
are some kind of generalized functions.
In order to get this generalization by following the ideas of the
present work, we would need a space of generalized functions which
is closed with respect to composition and with a complete topology
generated by norms; this space must clearly be non-trivial, e.g. containing
all Sobolev-Schwartz distributions. In our opinion, this target can
be fully accomplished in a beautiful and simple setting by considering
the Grothendieck topos of non-Archimedean generalized smooth function,
see e.g.
[
13
,
22
,
14
]
. We plan to realize this
goal in future works.

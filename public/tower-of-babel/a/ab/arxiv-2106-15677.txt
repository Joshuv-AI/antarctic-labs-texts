Abstract
. 2.
Congruences for Hecke Trace Forms

The main results of this section, which will be used to prove Theorems

and
1.3
in Section

Theorem 2.1
.

If

is prime and
k_{1},k_{2}
are even integers such that

, then

(2.1)

T_{k_{2}}(z)\equiv m\cdot T_{k_{1}}(z)\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)

for some
m\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
whenever
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
are related by one of the following conditions:

(i)

k_{2}=k_{1}+cp(p^{2}-1)
for
c\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
(in which case
m=1
),

(ii)

k_{2}=k_{1}+c(p^{2}-1)
for some
c\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
c+1\not\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)
, where
p\leq 11
and
k_{1}=(p^{2}-1)+0,4,6,8,10,14
(in which case
m=c+1
), or

(iii)

k_{2}=k_{1}+c(p-1)
for
c\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, where
p\leq 11

and
\dim S_{k_{1}}=\dim S_{k_{2}}
(in which case
m=1
).

Remark
.

For any prime
p\geq 5
, the theory of modular forms mod
p
shows that congruences of the form (
2.1
) can only occur for pairs of even weights
k_{2}>k_{1}\geq 4
that differ by a multiple of
p-1

[
6
, Proposition 2.43]
. Apart from this, it is natural to ask whether the conditions for such congruences given in Theorem
2.1
are comprehensive. This is the case for
p\in\{5,7,11\}
, and a general search of trace forms of weight up to 300 did not reveal any congruences except those predicted by Theorem
2.1
for
p\geq 13
.

Theorem 2.2
.

If
p\geq 5
is prime,
k\in\{0,4,6,8,10,14\}
, and
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, then

(2.2)

\widehat{T}_{k+m(p^{2}-1)}^{(p)}(z)=m\cdot\widehat{T}_{k+p^{2}-1}^{(p)}(z).

Remark
.

Note that given any even integer
k\geq 4
, we have that
\widehat{T}_{k}(z)
is the
q
-expansion obtained by applying
\Theta^{p-1}
to
T_{k}(z)
and reducing each coefficient mod
p
, where
\Theta=q\,\frac{d}{dq}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\,\frac{d}{dz}
. Strictly speaking, we obtain a relation as in (

) by applying
\Theta
to
T_{k+(p^{2}-1)}(z)
and

only once and reducing mod
p
. The effect of applying this operator
p-1
times instead
is that the resulting
q
-expansion is identical to the original one, except that all coefficients whose order is divisible by
p
are annihilated.

In Section
2.1
, we recall the Eichler-Selberg trace formula, which we use to prove Theorem

. The proofs of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
then follow in Sections

2.1.
The Eichler-Selberg Trace Formula

Following Zagier’s appendix in
[
5
]
, we define the Hurwitz class numbers
H(n)
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
as follows: let
H(n)=0
whenever
n<0
, and
H(0)=-\frac{1}{12}
. For
n>0
, define
H(n)
to be the number of equivalence classes with respect to
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
of positive definite binary quadratic forms

f(x,y)=ax^{2}+bxy+cy^{2},\quad a,b,c\in\mathbb{Z},

with discriminant
\Delta(f):=b^{2}-4ac=-n
, where we count forms equivalent to a scalar multiple of
x^{2}+y^{2}
with multiplicity
\frac{1}{2}
, and forms equivalent to a scalar multiple of
x^{2}+xy+y^{2}
with multiplicity
\frac{1}{3}
.

Moreover, for any even integer
k>0
, we define the polynomial
P_{k}(t,n)
to be the coefficient of
x^{k-2}
in the series expansion of
(1-tx+nx^{2})^{-1}.

Then we have that

(2.3)

P_{k}(t,n)=\frac{\rho^{k-1}-\bar{\rho}^{k-1}}{\rho-\bar{\rho}}\,,

where
\rho,\bar{\rho}
are defined by the relations
\rho+\bar{\rho}=t
and
\rho\bar{\rho}=n
. With these definitions, the Eichler-Selberg Trace Formula can be stated as follows:

Eichler-Selberg Trace Formula
.

If
k\geq 4
is an even integer and
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, then the trace of the Hecke operator
T(n)
on the space of cusp forms
S_{k}
is given by

(2.4)

\operatorname{Tr}T(n)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}P_{k}(t,n)H(4n-t^{2})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{dd^{\prime}=n}\min(d,d^{\prime})^{k-1},

where the second sum is taken over all factorizations
dd^{\prime}=n
with
d,d^{\prime}\geq 1
.

Note that the first sum is in fact finite since
H(4n-t^{2})
is non-zero only when
\absolutevalue{t}\leq 2\sqrt{n}
.

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
2.1

To prove (i), we use the Eichler-Selberg trace formula to show that
\operatorname{Tr}_{k}(n)\equiv\operatorname{Tr}_{k+p(p^{2}-1)}(n)\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)
for each
n\geq 1
.
Using Fermat’s little theorem, we see that
\min(d,d^{\prime})^{(k+p(p^{2}-1))-1}\equiv\min(d,d^{\prime})^{k-1}\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)
,
hence we need only to consider the first term in (
2.4
). In particular, it will suffice to prove that

P_{k}(t,n)\equiv P_{k+p(p^{2}-1)}(t,n)\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)

for each
n\geq 1
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\absolutevalue{t}\leq 2\sqrt{n}
.

If
t^{2}=4n

i.e.,
P_{k}(t,n)=(k-1)2^{2-k}t^{k-2}
. The desired congruence
P_{k}(t,n)\equiv P_{k+p(p^{2}-1)}(t,n)\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)
then follows by Fermat’s little theorem.

From now on, we can assume that

Case 1:

, i.e.,

Note that
\rho-\bar{\rho}=\sqrt{t^{2}-4n}\notin(p)
by assumption that

2.3.
Proof of Theorem
2.2

The proof of Theorem
2.2
relies on the following congruence relations for the polynomials
P_{k}(t,n)
mod
p
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Consider some fixed
(t,n)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}^{+}
so that
t^{2}\leq 4n
, and any prime
p\geq 5
such that
\gcd(p,n)=1
. Let
k\neq 2
be a non-negative even integer. If
p
is unramified in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{t^{2}-4n})
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, then

P_{k+m(p^{2}-1)}(t,n)\equiv\begin{cases}-n^{-1}\,\,\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)&\text{if }k=0,\\
P_{k}(t,n)\,\,\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

If
p
is ramified in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{t^{2}-4n})
or
t^{2}-4n=0
, then

P_{k+m(p^{2}-1)}(t,n)\equiv(k-m-1)n^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup).

Proof.

Suppose first that
p
is not ramified in
K:=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{t^{2}-4n})
. By the periodicity established in Cases 1 and 2 of the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we have
P_{k}(t,n)\equiv P_{k+m(p^{2}-1)}(t,n)\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)
if
k\geq 4
, and
P_{m(p^{2}-1)}(t,n)\equiv P_{p^{2}-1}(t,n)
if
k=0
. For
k\geq 4
, this is the desired statement; for
k=0
, we note that if
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
above
p
, then
\rho-\bar{\rho}=\sqrt{t^{2}-4n}\notin\mathfrak{p}
by the assumption that
p
does not ramify in
K
. Also,
\rho,\bar{\rho}\notin\mathfrak{p}
by assumption that
\gcd(p,n)=1
.
Hence, we have that
\rho^{p^{2}-1},\bar{\rho}^{p^{2}-1}\equiv 1\in\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}
, and so

P_{p^{2}-1}(t,n)=\frac{\rho^{p^{2}-2}-\bar{\rho}^{p^{2}-2}}{\rho-\bar{\rho}}\equiv\frac{\rho^{-1}-\bar{\rho}^{-1}}{\rho-\bar{\rho}}=\frac{-n^{-1}\sqrt{t^{2}-4n}}{\sqrt{t^{2}-4n}}=-n^{-1}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}.

Now, suppose that
p
is ramified in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{t^{2}-4n})
, so
p
divides
t^{2}-4n
. Then with notation as before, we have that
\sqrt{t^{2}-4n}\in\mathfrak{p}
, and as we are assuming that
n\notin(p)
, we must have that
t\notin(p)
as well. In particular, this means that
\rho,\bar{\rho}\equiv\frac{t}{2}\neq 0\in\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}
.
Consequently, it follows from (
2.5
) that

\displaystyle P_{k+m(p^{2}-1)}(t,n)

\displaystyle\equiv(k+m(p^{2}-1)-1)\rho^{k+m(p^{2}-1)-2}

\displaystyle\equiv(k-m-1)\rho^{k-2}\equiv(k-m-1)n^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p},

where we use that
\rho\equiv\frac{t}{2}
and
(\frac{t}{2})^{2}\equiv n\in\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}
.
Similarly, if
t^{2}-4n=0
, then the proof of Theorem
2.1
shows that

P_{k+m(p^{2}-1)}(t,n)=(k+m(p^{2}-1)-1)2^{2-k-m(p^{2}-1)}t^{k+m(p^{2}-1)-2}\equiv(k-m-1)n^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup).

∎

Furthermore, we will need the following important property of the Hurwitz class numbers
[
2
]
:

Theorem

(Kronecker-Hurwitz Relation)
.

For any positive integer
n
, we have that

(2.7)

\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}H(4n-t^{2})=\sum_{dd^{\prime}=n}\max(d,d^{\prime}),

where the second sum is taken over all factorizations
dd^{\prime}=n
with
d,d^{\prime}\geq 1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

We use the Eichler-Selberg trace formula (
2.4
) to show that

m\cdot\operatorname{Tr}_{k+(p^{2}-1)}(n)\equiv\operatorname{Tr}_{k+m(p^{2}-1)}(n)\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)

for
k\in\{0,4,6,8,10,14\}
and
n\geq 1
such that
\gcd(p,n)=1
.
The proof of Theorem
2.1
(i) shows that
P_{k+(p^{2}-1)}(t,n)\equiv P_{k+m(p^{2}-1)}(t,n)\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)
whenever
p
is unramified in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{t^{2}-4n})
. Thus, denoting by
S
(resp.
T
) the set of
t\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
t^{2}\leq 4n
and
p
is ramified (resp. unramified) in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{t^{2}-4n})
(where
t\in S
if
t^{2}=4n
), we need to show that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\sum_{t\in S}&\Big(m\,P_{k+p^{2}-1}(t,n)-P_{k+m(p^{2}-1)}(t,n)\Big)H(4n-t^{2})\\
&+\sum_{t\in T}(m-1)P_{k+p^{2}-1}(t,n)H(4n-t^{2})+\sum_{dd^{\prime}=n}(m-1)\min(d,d^{\prime})^{k+p^{2}-2}\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup),\end{split}

using that we have
P_{k+p^{2}-1}(t,n)\equiv P_{k+m(p^{2}-1)}(t,n)\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)
for
t\in T
by Lemma
2.3
, and that
\min(d,d^{\prime})^{k+p^{2}-2}\equiv\min(d,d^{\prime})^{k+m(p^{2}-1)-1}\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup).

Suppose first that
k\neq 0
. We note that for
t\in S
, by Lemma
2.3
we have

m\,P_{k+p^{2}-1}(t,n)-P_{k+m(p^{2}-1)}(t,n)\equiv m(k-2)n^{\frac{k-2}{2}}-(k-m-1)n^{\frac{k-2}{2}}

=(m-1)(k-1)n^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\equiv(m-1)P_{k}(t,n)\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup).

Similarly, for
t\in T
, Lemma
2.3
shows that
P_{k+p^{2}-1}(t,n)\equiv P_{k}(t,n)
. Moreover, since
d,d^{\prime}\not\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)
, we have

\min(d,d^{\prime})^{k+p^{2}-2}\equiv\min(d,d^{\prime})^{k-1}\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)
.
Thus (
2.8
) simplifies to

(m-1)\Big(\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}P_{k}(t,n)H(4n-t^{2})+\sum_{dd^{\prime}=n}\min(d,d^{\prime})^{k-1}\Big)\equiv-2(m-1)\operatorname{Tr}_{k}(n)\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup),

where the last relation holds since
S_{k}=0
and therefore

\operatorname{Tr}_{k}(n)=0
for each
k\in\{4,6,8,10,14\}
.

Now, consider the case in which
k=0
. By Lemma
2.3
, we have that

P_{m(p^{2}-1)}(t,n)\equiv-n^{-1}\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)

whenever
t\in T
, and that

mP_{p^{2}-1}(t,n)-P_{m(p^{2}-1)}(t,n)\equiv m\cdot(-2)n^{-1}-(-m-1)n^{-1}=(1-m)n^{-1}\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)

for
t\in S
. Hence (
2.8
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\sum_{t\in S}(m-1)(-n^{-1})H(4n-t^{2})+\sum_{t\in T}

\displaystyle(m-1)(-n^{-1})H(4n-t^{2})

\displaystyle+\sum_{dd^{\prime}=n}(m-1)\min(d,d^{\prime})^{p^{2}-2}\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup),

which is equivalent to the statement that

\displaystyle(m-1)\Big(-n^{-1}\sum_{t=-\infty}^{\infty}H(4n-t^{2})+\sum_{dd^{\prime}=n}\min(d,d^{\prime})^{p^{2}-2}\Big)\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup).

By the Kronecker-Hurwitz relation (
2.7
), we can write the above as

(m-1)\Big(-n^{-1}\sum_{dd^{\prime}=n}\max(d,d^{\prime})+\sum_{dd^{\prime}=n}\min(d,d^{\prime})^{p^{2}-2}\Big)\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup).

For any
d,d^{\prime}
such that
dd^{\prime}=n
, we have that
\min(d,d^{\prime})^{p^{2}-2}\equiv\min(d,d^{\prime})^{-1}\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)
, since
\min(d,d^{\prime})\not\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)
. Moreover, if we assume without loss of generality that
d\leq d^{\prime}
, then

This shows that
\big(-n^{-1}\sum_{dd^{\prime}=n}\max(d,d^{\prime})+\sum_{dd^{\prime}=n}\min(d,d^{\prime})^{p^{2}-2}\big)
vanishes term-wise mod
p
, and so we conclude that (

) holds for

.
∎

F(E_{p-1}^{n};x)\equiv F(E_{p-1};x)F(E_{p-1}^{n-1};x)x^{a_{p-1,(n-1)(p-1)}}(x-1728)^{b_{p-1,(n-1)(p-1)}}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x],

where the values of

Proposition 3.3
.

Let

Theorem 4.1
.

If

and

are as defined in (

), and

are given by

\widetilde{S}_{p}(x)^{n}
divides
F(T_{k};x)
mod

With

satisfying

2.1
, we have that
T_{k}(z)\equiv T_{k^{\prime}}(z)\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)
. Now, by Proposition
3.3
,

F(T_{k};x)\equiv F(T_{k^{\prime}};x)\widetilde{S}_{p}(x)^{n}x^{\delta_{p}\lfloor n/3\rfloor+a}(x-1728)^{\varepsilon_{p}\lfloor n/2\rfloor+b}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x],

where
(a,b)=(a_{k^{\prime},n(p-1)},b_{k^{\prime},n(p-1)})=(a_{k,n(p-1)},b_{k,n(p-1)})
since

Corollary 4.2

and

), and

are given by

in Proposition

.

Proof.

By assumption,
k-n(p-1)\in\{12,16,18,20,22,26\}
, so in particular,
F(T_{k-n(p-1)};x)=1
, as

must be a constant multiple of the generator

4.2.
Divisor Polynomials of Modified Trace Forms

Theorem
1.3
is a consequence of the following more general result, which uses Theorem
2.2
and Proposition
3.3
.

Theorem 4.3
.

If
p\geq 5
is prime and
k\geq p^{2}-1
is an even integer such that
k\equiv 0,4,6,8,10,14\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup^{2}-1)
, then

F(\widehat{T}_{k}^{(p)};x)\equiv m\cdot F(\widehat{T}_{k-n(p-1)}^{(p)};x)\widetilde{S}_{p}(x)^{n}x^{\delta_{p}\lfloor n/3\rfloor+a}(x-1728)^{\varepsilon_{p}\lfloor n/2\rfloor+b}\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup),

where
m=\lfloor\frac{k}{p^{2}-1}\rfloor
,
n=(p+1)(m-1)
, and
a,b\in\{0,1\}
are again given by
a_{k,n(p-1)},b_{k,n(p-1)}
in Proposition
3.1
. In particular,
\widetilde{S}_{p}(x)^{n}
divides
F(\widehat{T}_{k}^{(p)};x)
.

Proof.

Let
p,k,m
be as in the statement of the theorem.
Theorem
2.2
gives that

\widehat{T}_{k}^{(p)}(z)=m\cdot\widehat{T}_{k-(m-1)(p^{2}-1)}^{(p)}(z).

As in the remark following Theorem
2.2
, we note that
\widehat{T}_{k}^{(p)}(z)\equiv\Theta^{p-1}T_{k}(z)\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mup)
. Since for
f\in M_{k}
with integral coefficients,
\Theta(f)
is the mod
p
reduction of a modular form of weight
k+p+1
, it follows that
\widehat{T}_{k}^{(p)}
is the mod
p
reduction of a modular form of weight
k+p^{2}-1

[
6
, Proposition 2.44]
. Similarly,
\widehat{T}_{k-(m-1)(p^{2}-1)}^{(p)}
is the reduction of a form of weight
k-(m-2)(p^{2}-1)
.
Using these weights to compute divisor polynomials for
\widehat{T}_{k}^{(p)}(z)
and
\widehat{T}_{k-(m-1)(p^{2}-1)}^{(p)}(z)
, we apply Proposition
3.3
to get

F(\widehat{T}_{k}^{(p)};x)\equiv m\cdot F(\widehat{T}_{k-n(p-1)}^{(p)};x)\widetilde{S}_{p}(x)^{n}x^{\delta_{p}\lfloor n/3\rfloor+a}(x-1728)^{\varepsilon_{p}\lfloor n/2\rfloor+b}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x],

where
n=(m-1)(p+1)
, and
(a,b)=(a_{k-(m-2)(p^{2}-1),n(p-1)},b_{k-(m-2)(p^{2}-1),n(p-1)})=(a_{k,n(p-1)},b_{k,n(p-1)})
since
p^{2}-1\equiv 0\allowbreak\mkern 8.0mu({\operator@font mod}\mkern 6.0mu12)
.
∎

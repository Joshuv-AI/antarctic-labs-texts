Abstract
. We prove a bias towards the zero residue class in the distribution of the integers represented by binary quadratic forms. In most cases, we prove that the bias comes from a secondary term in an associated asymptotic expansion. This is unlike Chebyshev’s bias, which exists somewhere at the level of O ⁡ ( x 1 / 2 + ε ) . O(x^{1/2+\varepsilon}). We make a conjecture on the general situation which includes all cases not proven. Results on the distribution of the integers represented by a quadratic form–some of which are of independent interest–are proven along the way. The paper concludes with some numerical data that is illustrative of the bias.

B_{f}(x)\sim C(f)\frac{x}{\sqrt{\log x}}\quad(x\to\infty),

Above, and below,

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

(q,2D)=1

(a,q)=1

B_{f}(x;q,a)=\frac{1}{q}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p|q\\
\left(\frac{D}{p}\right)=-1\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)B_{f}(x)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2/3}}\right).

In particular, when
q

B_{f}(x;q,a)=c(q,a)B_{f}(x)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2/3}}\right),

where

c(q,a):=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{q}\quad&\textit{ if }\left(\frac{D}{q}\right)=1\\
\frac{q+1}{q^{2}}\quad&\textit{ if }\left(\frac{D}{q}\right)=-1,a\not\equiv 0\bmod q\\
\frac{1}{q^{2}}\quad&\textit{ if }\left(\frac{D}{q}\right)=-1,a\equiv 0\bmod q\end{cases}

Theorem

1.1

1/q

1.2

Let

Let

B_{f}(x;q,a)=B_{R}(x;q,a)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2/3}}\right)\quad(x\to\infty).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
D
be a discriminant for which

is cyclic of odd order

(q,2D)=1

B^{\prime}_{f}(x;q,a)
deenote the number of squarefree integers not exceeding

2D

Then, one has, for every

(a,q)=1

B^{\prime}_{f}(x;q,a)\sim A_{1}(f,q,a)\frac{x}{(\log x)^{1/2}},

for some constant
A_{1}(f,q,a)

B^{\prime}_{f}(x;q,a)=A_{1}(f,q,a)\frac{x}{(\log x)^{1/2}}-N^{\prime}_{f}(x;q,a),

where

N^{\prime}_{f}(x;q,a)=c^{\prime}(q,a)A_{2}(f)\frac{x}{(\log x)^{1-1/(2p_{0})}}(\log\log x)^{r}(1+o(1)),

A_{2}(f)

f

r=\begin{cases}p_{0}-2,\quad&[f]\in H,\\
p_{0}-3,\quad&\textit{otherwise,}\end{cases}

c^{\prime}(q,a)=\frac{1}{\phi(q)}\prod_{p|q}\left(1+\frac{\nu_{H}(p)}{p}\right)^{-1},

and

\frac{q}{q^{2}-1},\quad&\textit{if }a\not\equiv 0\bmod q\textit{ and }\nu_{H}(q)=1\\
o(1),\quad&a\equiv 0\bmod q\textit{ and }\nu_{H}(q)=0\\
\frac{1}{q-1},\quad&\textit{if }a\not\equiv 0\bmod q\textit{ and }\nu_{H}(q)=0.\end{cases}

Summary of main theorems:

•

If
G(D)\cong C(D)
, then the biased secondary term will be of size
x/(\log x)^{3/2}
, and will arise from a result on the equidistribution of arithmetic progressions for integers represented by a given genus (
Theorem

2.1
).

•

If
C(D)
is cyclic and of odd order, then the biased secondary term will be of size
x(\log\log x)^{\beta}/(\log x)^{\alpha}
, for some
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, and
\alpha\in[5/6,1)
. The secondary term in this case arises from a characterisation of integers which are represented by some form in the genus of
f

but not
by
f
itself, which will generalise the work of
[
Gol01
]

Several recent works have been concerned with some aspects of the behaviour of the form
x^{2}+y^{2}
in arithmetic progressions. In
[
Gor22
]

x^{2}+y^{2}

Inspired by the earlier work of Lemke Oliver and Soundararajan
[

]

[

]

[

The function field analogue of Landau’s theorem has also been explored. In
[
GR21
]
, Gorodetsky and Rodgers explore the variance of sums of two squares in short intervals for

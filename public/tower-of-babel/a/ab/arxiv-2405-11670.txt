On z z -elements of multiplicative lattices
Themba Dube, Amartya Goswami

Abstract
. The aim of this paper is to investigate further properties of z z -elements in multiplicative lattices. We utilize z z -closure operators to extend several properties of z z -ideals to z z -elements and introduce various distinguished subclasses of z z -elements, such as z z -prime, z z -semiprime, z z -primary, z z -irreducible, and z z -strongly irreducible elements, and study their properties. We provide a characterization of multiplicative lattices where z z -elements are closed under finite products and a representation of z z -elements in terms of z z -irreducible elements in z z -Noetherian multiplicative lattices.

a\neq 1
. We consider the chain
(\mathcal{E},\leqslant)
, where

\mathcal{E}=\left\{b\mid a\leqslant b,\;b\in\irs(L)\right\}.

By Lemma
2.2
(
2
), every proper
z
-element is below a maximal element. Since every maximal element
m
of
L
is prime (see Lemma
2.2
(
1
)), and hence, is strongly irreducible (follows Lemma
2.1
(
2
)), by Proposition
5.2
, it follows that
m\in\irs(L)
. Therefore,
\mathcal{E}\neq\emptyset
. By applying Zorn’s lemma, we conclude that
\mathcal{E}
has a minimal element, which is our desired minimal
z
-strongly irreducible element.
∎

The following result is going to tell us what happens to
\mathcal{Z}(L)
, when all
z
-elements of
L
are
z
-strongly irreducible. This result extends
[
Azi08
, Lemma 3.5]
.

Proposition 5.4
.

If
L
is a multiplicative lattice in which every
z
-element is
z
-strongly irreducible, then
\mathcal{Z}(L)
is totally ordered

Proof.

Suppose for any
a
,
b\in\mathcal{Z}(L)
are
z
-strongly irreducible. Since
a\wedge b\leqslant a\wedge b
, we have either
a\leqslant a\wedge b\leqslant b
or
b\leqslant a\wedge b\leqslant a
. This implies that
\mathcal{Z}(L)
is totally ordered.
∎

Next, we aim to present a result on the representation of
z
-elements in terms of
z
-irreducible elements in a special type of multiplicative lattices. We say that a multiplicative lattice
L
is
z
-Noetherian
if every ascending chain of
z
-elements in
L
is eventually stationary. The following proposition provides an extension of
[
Nas18
, Proposition 7.3]
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
L
be a
z
-Noetherian multiplicative lattice. Then every
z
-element of
L
can be represented as a meet of a finite number of
z
-irreducible elements of
L
.

Proof.

Let us suppose

\mathcal{F}=\left\{b\in\mathcal{Z}(L)\mid b\neq\bigwedge_{i=1}^{n}q_{i},\;q_{i}\in\irr(L)\right\}.

It suffices to show that
\mathcal{F}=\emptyset
.
Since
L
is
z
-Noetherian,
\mathcal{F}
must have a maximal element, denoted by
a
. Since
a\in\mathcal{F}
,
a
is not a finite
meet of
z
-irreducible elements of
L
. This implies that
a
is not
z
-irreducible. Hence, there are
z
-elements
b
and
b^{\prime}
such that
a<b
,
a<b^{\prime}
, and
a=b\wedge b^{\prime}.
Since
a
is
a maximal element of
\mathcal{F}
, we must have
b,

b^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{F}.
Therefore,
b
and
b^{\prime}
are a finite meet of

z
-irreducible elements of
L
. This, in turn, implies that
a
is also a finite meet of
z
-irreducible
elements of
L
, a contradiction.
∎

We shall now introduce prime-type
z
-elements. A proper
z
-element
p
of a multiplicative lattice
L
is called
z
-prime
if for all
a,

b\in\mathcal{Z}(L)
with
ab\leqslant p
implies that
a\leqslant p
or
b\leqslant p
. We shall denote by
\mathcal{P}_{z}(L)
the set of all
z
-prime elements of
L
. From Lemma
2.1
(
2
), it follows that every
z
-prime element is
z
-strongly irreducible.
Under an additional assumption on
L
, we now show that the equivalence formulation (
1.2
) holds for the
z
-prime elements.

Proposition 5.6
.

Suppose
L
is a
szi
-multiplicative lattice. An element
p
of
L
is
z
-prime if and only if
p\in\mathcal{Z}(L)
and
p\in\mathcal{P}(L)
.

Proof.

It is evident that if
p\in\mathcal{Z}(L)
as well as
p\in\mathcal{P}(L)
, then
p
is a
z
-prime element.
For the converse, suppose
p\in\mathcal{P}_{z}(L)
and
xy\leqslant p
, for some
x
,
y\in L
. Then from Lemma
4.4
(
9
), it follows that

\cz(x)\cz(y)\leqslant\cz(xy)\leqslant\cz(p))=p.

Since
p\in\mathcal{P}_{z}(L)
, we have
x\leqslant\cz(x)\leqslant p
or
y\leqslant\cz(y)\leqslant p.
This implies
p\in\mathcal{P}(L)
.
∎

Proposition 5.7
.

Every non-trivial multiplicative lattice
L
contains a minimal
z
-prime element.

Proof.

The existence of minimal prime elements is guaranteed by the supposition of that finite products of compact elements are compact (see
[
AAJ95
]
).
Suppose
L
is a non-trivial multiplicative lattice. Then by Lemma
2.2
(
2
), it follows that
L
has a maximal element, say
m
, which by Lemma
2.2
(
1
) and Lemma
3.2
(
3
) is a prime element as well as a
z
-element. Hence, by definition,
m
is a
z
-prime element, implying that the set
\mathcal{P}_{z}(L)
is nonempty. The claim now follows from a routine application of Zorn’s lemma. ∎

For
z
-Noetherian multiplicative lattices, the following proposition provides information regarding the cardinality of minimal
z
-prime elements. This result extends
[
Les15
, Proposition 6.7]
.

Proposition 5.8
.

If
L
is a
z
-Noetherian multiplicative lattice, then the set of minimal
z
-prime elements of
L
is finite.

Proof.

If
L
is
z
-Noetherian, then the topological space
\mathcal{P}_{z}(L)
(endowed with Zariski topology) is also Noetherian, and thus
\mathcal{P}_{z}(L)
has finitely many irreducible
components. Now every irreducible closed subset of
\mathcal{P}_{z}(L)

is of the form

\mathcal{V}_{z}(p)=\left\{q\in\mathcal{P}_{z}(L)\mid p\leqslant q\right\},

where
p
is a minimal
z
-prime element. Thus
\mathcal{V}_{z}(p)
is irreducible component if and only if
p
is a minimal
z
-prime element. Hence, the number of minimal
z
-prime elements of
L
is finite.
∎

We now focus in introducing another class of prime-type elements of multiplicative lattices. A proper
z
-element
p
of a multiplicative lattice
L
is called
z
-semiprime
if for all
a\in\mathcal{Z}(L)
with
a^{2}\leqslant p
implies
a\leqslant p
.
Similarly to
z
-prime elements, we also establish an equivalent formulation (
1.2
) for
z
-semiprime elements under the same additional assumption.

Proposition 5.9
.

An element
q
of a
szi
-multiplicative lattice
L
is
z
-semiprime if and only if
q
is a
z
-element and also a semiprime element of
L
.

Proof.

If
q\in\mathcal{Z}(L)
and also a semiprime element of
L
, then obviously
q
is
z
-semiprime. For the converse, let us suppose that
q
is a
z
-semiprime element and
a^{2}\leqslant q
for some
a\in L.
Then

(\cz(a))(\cz(a))\leqslant\cz(a^{2})\leqslant\cz(q)=q,

where the first and second inclusions respectively follow from Lemma
4.4
(
9
) and Lemma
4.4
(
5
), whereas the last equality follows from the fact that
q\in\mathcal{Z}(L)
and Lemma
4.4
(
2
). Since
q
is
z
-semiprime,
\cz(a)\leqslant q
, and by Lemma
4.4
(
1
), we have
a\leqslant q.

∎

We shall now see a relation between prime-type and irreducible-type
z
-elements, and the following results extends
[
Gol99
, Proposition 7.36]
.

Proposition 5.10
.

A
z
-element of a
szi
-multiplicative lattice
L
is
z
-prime if and only if it is
z
-semiprime and
z
-strongly irreducible.

Proof.

Let
p\in\mathcal{P}_{z}(L)
. Then by Proposition
5.6
,
p\in\mathcal{Z}(L)
and
p\in\mathcal{P}(L)
. This implies that
p
is
z
-semiprime by Proposition
5.9
. From Lemma
2.1
(
2
) and Proposition
5.2
, it follows that
p\in\irs(L)
. Conversely, let
p
be a
z
-semiprime as well as a
z
-strongly irreducible element. Let
a
,
b\in\mathcal{Z}(L)
satisfying
ab\leqslant p
. Then

(a\wedge b)^{2}\leqslant ab\leqslant p.

Since
p
is
z
-semiprime, this implies
a\wedge b\leqslant p
. But
p
is also
z
-strongly irreducible, and so
a\leqslant p
or
b\leqslant p.

∎

.

In a

Concluding remarks 5.12
.

Here are some questions on
z
-elements of multiplicative lattices that may be worth exploring.

\bullet

In Lemma
3.2
(
7
), we observed that
0\in\mathcal{Z}(L)
, whenever
L
is semisimple. It would be interesting to know whether the converse is also true.

\bullet

A sufficient condition for the identity
\cz(a)=\mathsf{m}_{a}
to hold is provided in Proposition
4.4
(
12
). We are not aware of any necessary and sufficient condition for this.

\bullet

Although Proposition
4.6
provides some equivalent conditions on the closedness of
\cz
under joins, it does not provide a characterization for multiplicative lattices to possess this property for
\cz
. This may be worth investigating.

\bullet

In this paper, we have proved the equivalence formulation (
1.2
) for
z
-prime,
z
-semiprime, and
z
-primary elements under the assumption that
L
is a
szi
-multiplicative lattice. Whether this assumption is necessary remains unknown to us.

\bullet

It could be interesting to know for which other distinguished classes of elements the equivalence formulation (
1.2
) holds, potentially with suitable assumptions on the underlying lattice.

\bullet

The notion of
z
-ideals has been extended to arbitrary noncommutative rings in
[
MJ20
]
. A similar extension for
z
-elements is worth it to study for arbitrary quantales.

\bullet

The notion of higher order
z
-ideals of (commutative) rings has been introduced and studied in
[
DI16
]
. It is easy to see that the definition of a
z
-element can also be extended to higher order
z
-elements. Therefore, studying these higher-order
z
-elements of multiplicative lattices would be of interest.

\bullet

In
[
DG23
]
, it has been discussed that the class of strongly irreducible ideals is the largest class on which a hull-kernel topology can be imposed. Considering
z
-strongly irreducible elements as the corresponding class of elements, it could be intriguing to explore the topological properties of this class and its subspaces, such as
z
-prime,
z
-semiprime,
z
-minimal prime, maximal elements,
etc
.

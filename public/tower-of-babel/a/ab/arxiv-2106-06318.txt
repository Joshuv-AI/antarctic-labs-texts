ON THE SIZE OF MINIMAL SURFACES IN ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4}
ARI AIOLFI, MARC SORET AND MARINA VILLE

Abstract
The Gauss map g g of a surface Σ \Sigma in ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} takes its values in the Grassmannian of oriented 2-planes of ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} : G + ​ ( 2 , 4 ) G^{+}(2,4) . We give geometric criteria of stability for minimal surfaces in ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} in terms of g g . We show in particular that if the spherical area of the Gauss map | g ⁡ ( Σ ) | |g(\Sigma)| of a minimal surface is smaller than 2 ​ π 2\pi then the surface is stable by deformations which fix the boundary of the surface. This answers the question of [ BDC3 ] in ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} .



Keywords—
Gauss map, Grassmannian, minimal surface, stability

Lemma 1
.

Left (resp. right) multiplication by
\mathfrak{g}_{L}
(resp. by
\mathfrak{g}_{R}
) on
\mathbb{H}
both define rotations of
\mathbb{H}
such that the angle between any quaternion and its image
is of absolute value
\pi/2
. Their respective actions are equal on
T\Sigma
and of opposite sign on
N\Sigma

Proof.

Suppose
q\in\mathbb{H}_{U}
. Then left or right multiplication by
q
defines an isometry:

\langle q\cdot x,q\cdot x\rangle=\Re(qx\bar{x}\bar{q})=|x|^{2}
.
And
\langle q\cdot x,x\rangle=\Re(qx\bar{x})=\Re(q)|x|^{2}
. Hence the isometry is a rotation and the absolute angle between any nonzero quaternion and its image is constant.
If
q\in\mathbb{H}_{UP}^{*}
then then absolute angle is
\pi/2
. By construction, left multiplication by
\mathfrak{g}_{L}
and right multiplication by
\mathfrak{g}_{R}
restricted to
T\Sigma
are equal to the same
\pi/2
- rotation.
Since both are isometries, the normal bundle is stable and the restriction of both rotations to
N\Sigma
are
\pi/2
-rotations. If they were equal,
then
\mathfrak{g}_{L}x=x\mathfrak{g}_{R}
for any
x\in\mathbb{H}
. Hence
\mathfrak{g}_{L}=\mathfrak{g}_{R}

and
\mathfrak{g}_{L}
commutes with any quaternion, which is impossible since
\mathfrak{g}_{L}
is a nonzero pure quaternion. Hence the rotation of
\mathfrak{g}_{L}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{R}
on
N\Sigma
are of opposite angle
so
\mathfrak{g}_{L}x=x\mathfrak{g}_{R}^{-1}=-x\mathfrak{g}_{R}
for any
x\in N\Sigma
since the angle is
\pi/2
.
∎

We start with a minimal surface whose Gauss map in Weierstrass coordinates -following the notations of Section
23
- are of the form

G:\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}U&\longrightarrow G(U)\subset Q_{2}\subset P(\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{C})\\
z&\longrightarrow[e^{\prime}(z),f^{\prime}(z),g^{\prime}(z),h^{\prime}(z)]\end{array}\right)

(30)

NB. If the Riemann surface
U
is not simply-connected then we replace it by its universal cover; the image by the Gauss map of
U
will not be affected.

From Lemma
4
, there exists a continuous path
\gamma:[0,1]\lx@nobreakspace\longrightarrow\mathcal{J}
with
\gamma_{0}=Id
and
\gamma_{1}=S
.

This generates a continuous family of maps
G_{t}:=\gamma_{t}.G
. Since
\gamma_{t}\in\mathcal{J}
then
\gamma_{t}G(U)\subset Q_{2}
.
In terms of Weierstrass coordinates, let
G_{t}:=[e_{t,1},e_{t,2},e_{t,3},e_{t,4}]

then
e_{t,1}.e_{t,2}+e_{t,3}e_{t,4}=0
.
The
G_{t}
are then the Gauss maps of a family of new minimal surfaces - so called associate minimal surfaces to
\Sigma
.

X_{t}:\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}U&\longrightarrow\Sigma_{t}\subset\mathbb{H}\\
z&\mapsto\int_{z}e_{1,t}+\overline{\int_{z}e_{2,t}}+(\int_{z}e_{3,t}+\overline{\int_{z}e_{4,t}})J\end{array}\right)

(31)

where
\Sigma_{0}=\Sigma
and by the defintion of (
28
) such that the Gauss map of
\Sigma_{1}
is given by :

(\mathfrak{g}_{L,1},\mathfrak{g}_{R,1})=(\sigma_{a}\circ\mathfrak{g}_{R},\mathfrak{g}_{L})

(32)

where
\sigma_{a}
is the antipodal symmetry.

Let us describe some invariants of this family of minimal surfaces.

Lemma 5
.

For any minimal surface
\Sigma
there is an associate family of minimal surfaces to
\{\Sigma_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
defined by (
31
)
such that the following conditions are satisfied:

\forall p\in U\ {\rm and}\ \forall t\in[0,1]:

1.

\Sigma_{t}
is locally isometric to
\Sigma_{0}=\Sigma
:

ds^{2}_{t}(p):=\lambda^{2}_{t}(p)|dz|^{2}=\lambda^{2}(p)|dz|^{2}

2.

The tangent curvature is invariant by deformation :

K_{t}^{T}(p)=K^{T}(p)

3.

The images of the Gauss maps have the same area :

|g_{t}(\Sigma_{t})|=|g(\Sigma)|

4.

If the operator
\Delta_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}}-2K^{T}_{t_{0}}
is positive for some
t_{0}\in[0,1]
then
\Delta_{\Sigma_{t}}-2K^{T}_{t}
is positive for all
t\in[0,1]
.

Proof.

1.

We consider the deformation of
\Sigma
defined in (
31
).In local isothermal coordinates
z
, in a neighborhood of some
p\in U
,
ds^{2}=\lambda|dz|^{2}
. where
\lambda=|e^{\prime}|^{2}+|f^{\prime}|^{2}+|g^{\prime}|^{2}+|h^{\prime}|^{2}=(G,G)

where
(,)
is the hermitian metric in
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}
.
As
\gamma_{t}\in U(n)
, the metrics
ds_{t}^{2}=\lambda_{t}|dz|^{2}
of
\Sigma_{t}
are all locally isometric to
\Sigma
since

\lambda_{t}=(G_{t},G_{t})=|e_{1,t}|^{2}+|e_{2,t}|^{2}+|e_{1,2}|^{2}+|e_{3,t}|^{2}+|e_{4,t}|^{2}=(\gamma_{t}G,\gamma_{t}G)=(G,G).

2.

The metric is invariant, so is the tangent curvature.

3.

|g_{t}(\Sigma)|=|\gamma_{t}g(\Sigma)|=|g(\Sigma)|

4.

If for some
t_{0}
:

\int_{U}|\nabla\phi|_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}}^{2}da_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}}\geq-2\int_{U}K_{t_{0}}^{T}\phi^{2}da_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}}\ \forall\phi\in C_{0}(U),

(33)

then, as the
\Sigma_{t}
are all isometric,
|\nabla\phi|_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}}^{2}=|\nabla\phi|_{\Sigma_{t}}^{2}
and
da_{\Sigma_{t_{0}}}=da_{\Sigma_{t}}
and by the second point,
the stability inequality (
33
) is true for any
t\in[0,1]
.

∎

.

Let
\Sigma

.

Then the projected area verifies

3
.

Suppose
|\mathfrak{g}_{L}(\Sigma)|=|\mathfrak{g}_{R}(\Sigma)|
then by the previous inequality,
|\mathfrak{g}_{L}(\Sigma_{t_{0}})|
and
|\mathfrak{g}_{R}(\Sigma_{t_{0}})|
are each less than
\pi
ie
the proportionate area of the left and right Gauss map are less than
\frac{1}{2}

and by Proposition
3
,
\Sigma
is stable.

Suppose that
|\mathfrak{g}_{L}(\Sigma)|<|\mathfrak{g}_{R}(\Sigma)|

) :

|\mathfrak{g}_{L}(\Sigma_{1})|=|\mathfrak{g}_{R}(\Sigma)|>|\mathfrak{g}_{L}(\Sigma)|=|\mathfrak{g}_{R}(\Sigma_{1})|

-3 and Corollary

\pi
whence

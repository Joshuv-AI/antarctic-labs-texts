Cut-Preserving Vertex Sparsifiers for Planar and Quasi-bipartite Graphs

Abstract

The construction of cut sparsifiers with good (small) quality and size has been a central problem in graph compression for years. Planar graphs and quasi-bipartite graphs are two important special families studied in this research direction. 1.2
Technical Overview

Planar cut sparsifiers.

Our construction of planar cut sparsifiers follows a similar framework as the planar distance emulator construction in
[
CKT22
]
, based on the intuition that, if we take the dual graph, then min-cuts become min-length partitioning cycles and therefore can be approximated by distance-based approaches. The algorithm consists of three steps:

1.

reduce general planar graphs to
O(1)
-face instances (where terminals lie on
O(1)
faces in the planar embedding of the graph);

2.

reduce
O(1)
-face instances to
1
-face instances (where all terminals lie on the boundary of a single face; such graphs are also known as
Okamura-Seymour instances
); and

3.

construct quality-
(1+{\varepsilon})
planar cut sparsifiers for
1
-face instances.

Step
1
and Step
3
are implemented in a similar way as
[
CKT22
]
. Step
2
requires several new ideas. The goal is to preserve the min-cuts for all terminal partitions. As terminals may lie on
O(1)
faces, for different terminal partitions, the dual partitioning cycle corresponding to the min-cuts may go around these
O(1)
faces in different ways, and therefore it is not enough to just preserve pairwise shortest paths in the dual graph. Indeed, as observed by
[
KR17
]
, we need to preserve, for each pair of terminals, and for each pattern (how a path going around the
O(1)
faces, e.g., on the left side of face
1
, on the right side of face
2
, etc), the shortest path following the pattern and connecting the terminal pair. We call such a sparsifier a
pattern distance emulator
, which a generalization of the standard distance emulators.
As there are
O(1)
faces, there are
2^{O(1)}
different patterns (left/right for each face). We manage to construct a near-linear size pattern emulator, incurring an unharmful factor
2^{O(1)}
loss in its size.

We believe that the notion of pattern emulators is of independent interest, and should play a role in solving other algorithmic problems on planar graphs.

Upper bounds for quasi-bipartite graphs.

For exact (quality-
1
) contraction-based cut sparsifiers, a central notion in previous constructions and lower bound proofs is the
profiles
of vertices. Specifically, the profile of a vertex
v
specifies, for each terminal partition
(S,T\setminus S)
, on which side of the cut
\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S,T\setminus S)
lies the vertex
v
.
Vertices of the same profile can be safely contracted together, and the number of different profiles is therefore an upper bound on the size of exact cut sparsifiers. For general graphs, a doubly-exponential upper bound of
2^{2^{k}}

[
HKNR98
]
on the number of distinct profiles was established, and this was later improved to
2^{\binom{k}{k/2}}

[
KR14
]
. For quasi-bipartite graphs, we will utilize its structure to proved a better (single-exponential) upper bound.

By the definition of quasi-bipartite graphs, there are no edge between non-terminal vertices, so non-terminal vertices form separate stars with terminals, and therefore contribute to terminal min-cuts independently. For example, in a star centered at
v
with
6
edges
e_{1},\ldots,e_{6}
connecting to terminal
t_{1},\ldots,t_{6}
, respectively. The profile of vertex
v
only depends on the weights of the edges
e_{1},\ldots,e_{6}
: for the terminal partition
(S=\left\{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\right\},T\setminus S=\left\{t_{4},t_{5},t_{6}\right\})
,

\text{$v$ lies on the $S$ side of $\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S,T\setminus S)$ iff }w(e_{1})+w(e_{2})+w(e_{3})>w(e_{4})+w(e_{5})+w(e_{6}).

This gives us some power to reveal properties of profiles in quasi-bipartite graphs.
For example, if we consider subsets
S\subseteq T
being
\left\{1,2\right\},\left\{3,4\right\},\left\{1,3\right\},\left\{2,4\right\}
, then
v
cannot lie on the
S
side for all these terminal min-cuts. Since otherwise, letting
w=\sum_{1\leq i\leq 6}w(e_{i})
, we get
w(e_{1})+w(e_{2})>w/2
,
w(e_{3})+w(e_{4})>w/2
,
w(e_{1})+w(e_{3})\leq w/2
, and
w(e_{2})+w(e_{4})\leq w/2
, a contradiction. This means that there are some “configurations” in the family of terminal subsets that are simply not realizable by any vertex profiles in quasi-bipartite graphs. We implement this idea through the notion of VC-dimension, realizing “configurations” by “set systems” and “not realizable” by “non-shatterable”, and obtain a bound of
k^{O(k^{2})}=2^{O(k^{2}\log k)}
on the number of profiles in quasi-bipartite graphs.

For quality-
(1+{\varepsilon})
cut sparsifiers, since we allow a small multiplicative error in accuracy, we are not restricted to contracting vertices with the same profile, but are allowed to moderately manipulate vertex profiles so as to reduce their number. We implement this idea by “projections onto
O(1/{\varepsilon}^{2})
-size stars”. On the one hand, consider for example a full star centered at
v
(that is, a star containing edges
(v,t)
for all
t\in T
) with uniform edge weights, and a subset
S\subseteq T
with
|S|=|T|/2-1
, so
v
lies on the
T\setminus S
side of
\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S,T\setminus S)
. If we uniformly at random sample
c=O(1/{\varepsilon}^{2})
edges from it and obtain a “mimicking substar”
H_{v}
, then by central limit theorem, with high probability
H_{v}
contains at most
c/2+O(1/{\varepsilon})
edges to
S
and at least
c/2-O(1/{\varepsilon})
edges to
T\setminus S
. Therefore, even if
H_{v}
fails in mimicking the behavior of the full star on the cut
(S,T\setminus S)
, in that it mistakenly selects more edges to
S
than to
T\setminus S
and therefore place
v
on the
S
side of
\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S,T\setminus S)
rather than the
T\setminus S
side, it only causes an error of
\frac{c/2+O(1/{\varepsilon})}{c/2-O(1/{\varepsilon})}=1+O({\varepsilon})
in the min-cut size, which is allowed.

On the other hand, we have shown in the exact case that the number of profiles for stars on a fixed
O(1/{\varepsilon}^{2})
-size terminal set is
O(1/{\varepsilon}^{2})^{O(1/{\varepsilon}^{4})}
(replacing
k
with
O(1/{\varepsilon}^{2})
in the
k^{O(k^{2})}
bound). Since the number of
O(1/{\varepsilon}^{2})
-size terminal subsets is
k^{O(1/{\varepsilon}^{2})}
, we can bound the total number of profiles produced by
O(1/{\varepsilon}^{2})
-size starts by

k^{O(1/{\varepsilon}^{2})}\cdot O(1/{\varepsilon}^{2})^{O(1/{\varepsilon}^{4})}
, obtaining a size bound of
k^{O(1/{\varepsilon}^{2})}f({\varepsilon})
for quality-
(1+{\varepsilon})
contraction-based cut sparsifiers for quasi-bipartite graphs.

Lower bounds for quasi-bipartite graphs.

As shown in
[
CT24b
,
CT24a
]
, contraction-based sparsifiers are closely related to the Steiner node version of the classic
0
-Extension problem
[
Kar98
]
. Specifically,
[
CT24b
]
showed that the best quality achievable by contraction-based flow sparsifiers is bounded by the integrality gap of the semi-metric LP relaxation. For cut sparsifiers, we observe that the best achievable quality is controlled by an even more restricted case of the
0
-Extension with Steiner Nodes problem, where the underlying graph is a boolean hypercube. We focus on this special case, construct a hard hypercube-instance which is also a quasi-bipartite graph, and prove a size lower bound for its
(1+{\varepsilon})
-approximation, leading to a same size lower bound for quality-
(1+{\varepsilon})
contraction-based cut sparsifiers for quasi-bipartite graphs.

Independent of work, Das, Kumar, and Vaz, showed in their work
[
DKV24
]
that quasi-bipartite graphs admit exact non-contraction-based cut sparsifiers of size

[
BSS12
]
to
O(n/{\varepsilon}^{2})
.

]

[
GHP17
]
, and graphs with bounded treewidth
[

]

,

Flow sparsifiers are highly correlated with cut sparsifiers.
Given a graph
G
and a set
T
of
k
terminals, a graph
G^{\prime}
is a

of

with respect to
T
with
quality

, iff every

, and every
G^{\prime}
-feasible multicommodity flow on

are increased by factor

. Flow sparsifiers are stronger than cut sparsifiers, in the sense that quality-

When no Steiner nodes are allowed,
Leighton and Moitra
[
LM10
]
showed the existence of quality-

flow sparsifiers.
On the lower bound side, the first quality lower bound in
[

]
is

, and this was later improved to

[

]
.

In the setting where Steiner nodes are allowed,

[
Chu12
]
showed

-quality contraction-based flow sparsifiers with size

).
On the lower bound side, Krauthgamer and Mosenzon

By default, all logarithms are to the base of

Let

In this paper, weights can be either representing capacities (in the context of cut values) or lengths (in the context of shortest-path distances).
For a vertex

the degree of

in
G

For a pair

of vertices in

(or

in

We may omit the subscript

Let
G
be a graph and let
T
be a subset of its vertices called

.
Let
H
be a graph with

H
is a

of

with

,

\mathsf{mincut}_{H}(T_{1},T_{2})\leq\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(T_{1},T_{2})\leq q\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{H}(T_{1},T_{2}).

We will use the following observation, which is immediate from the definition of cut sparsifiers.

.

If
(G_{1},T)
is a quality-

cut sparsifier of
(G,T)
, and
(G_{2},T)
is a quality-

cut sparsifier of

(G_{2},T)
is a quality-

cut sparsifier of
(G,T)
.

We say that a graph

is a

with respect to

, iff there exists a partition

of vertices in

Cuts in planar graphs are cycles in their dual graphs. We will exploit this property in a similar way as

Observation 28

, there exists a quasi-bipartite graph with
k
terminals whose exact contraction-based cut sparsifier must contain
\Omega(2^{k})
vertices.

We construct a graph
G
as follows. The terminal set contains
k
vertices and is denoted by
T
. For every subset
\emptyset\subsetneq S\subsetneq T
with even size, we add a new vertex
v_{S}
and connect it to every vertex of
S
.
Every edge is given a capacity that is chosen uniformly at random from the interval
(1-2^{k},1+2^{-k})
. In this way we can guarantee that with high probability, sets
E^{\prime},E^{\prime\prime}
of edges of
G
have the same total weight iff
E^{\prime}=E^{\prime\prime}
.
And clearly,
G
is a quasi-bipartite graph with
|V(G)|=\Omega(2^{k})
.

The proof of the
\Omega(2^{k})
size lower bound is completed by the following claims.

Claim 30
.

Every pair of distinct vertices have different profiles.

Proof.

Consider a pair
v=v_{S},v^{\prime}=v_{S^{\prime}}
of vertices in
G
.
As
G
is a quasi-bipartite graph, the profile of
v
only depends on incident edges of
v
.
We will show that there always exists a subset
U\subseteq T
of terminals, such that
v,v^{\prime}
lie on different sides of the
(U,T\setminus U)
min-cut in
G
.

Recall that
S,S^{\prime}
are distinct subsets of
T
with even size. Assume w.l.o.g. that
S\setminus S^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
. If
|S\cap S^{\prime}|
is even, then we let
U
contains exactly half of the elements in
S\cap S^{\prime}
. Now if
v
lies on the
U
side of the
(U,T\setminus U)
min-cut in
G
, then we are done, as
v^{\prime}
lies on the
T\setminus U
size of the min-cut, then we are done we add to
U
. Otherwise, we add all elements in
S\setminus S^{\prime}
, ensuring that
v
lies on the
T\setminus U
side of the
(U,T\setminus U)
min-cut while
v^{\prime}
lies on the
U
side.
Similarly, if
|S\cap S^{\prime}|
is even, then we let
U
contains
\frac{|S\cap S^{\prime}|+1}{2}
elements in
S\cap S^{\prime}
, and we can adjust
U
similarly (by optionally adding to it all elements of
S\setminus S^{\prime}
) to ensure that
v,v^{\prime}
lie on different sides of the
(U,T\setminus U)
min-cut in
G
.
∎

Claim 31
.

No pair of vertices can be contracted in an exact contraction-based cut sparsifier of
G
.

Proof.

Assume for contradiction that vertices
v_{S},v_{S^{\prime}}
are contracted. We have shown in
Claim

30
that there exists a subset
U
of
T
, such that vertices
v_{S},v_{S^{\prime}}
lie on different sides of the
(U,T\setminus U)
min-cut in
G
.
Therefore, if
v_{S},v_{S^{\prime}}
are merged in the sparsifier, then the set
E^{\prime\prime}
of edges constituting the
(U,T\setminus U)
min-cut in the cut sparsifier
H
must be different from the set
E^{\prime}
of edges constituting the
(U,T\setminus U)
min-cut in
G
, and therefore they must have different total weight (meaning that
H
cannot be an exact cut sparsifier for
G
), since we have assumed that sets
E^{\prime},E^{\prime\prime}
of edges of
G
have the same total weight iff
E^{\prime}=E^{\prime\prime}
.
∎

5
Proof of
Theorem

4

In this section, we provide the proof of
Theorem

4
, showing that every quasi-bipartite graph with
k
terminals admits a quality-
(1+{\varepsilon})
contraction-based cut sparsifier of size
k^{O(1/{\varepsilon}^{2})}\cdot(1/{\varepsilon})^{O(1/{\varepsilon}^{4})}
.

5.1
Sparsifying stars by sampling

Throughout, we use the parameter
c=\left\lceil 100/{\varepsilon}^{2}\right\rceil
.

For every non-terminal
v
in
G
, denote by
G_{v}
the star induced by all incident edges of
v
. We will construct another star
H_{v}
that contains at most
c
edges to mimick the behaviors of
G_{v}
, as follows.

Denote by
w
the total weight of all edges in
G_{v}
.
We say an edge
e
is
heavy
iff
w(e)\geq w/c
, otherwise we say it is
light
.
Denote by
h
the number of heavy edges in
G_{v}
and by
w^{h}
the total weight of them, so
h\leq c
.
We define a distribution
{\mathcal{D}}
on light edges as follows: the probability for a light edge
e
is

\text{Pr}_{{\mathcal{D}}}(e)=\frac{w(e)}{w-w^{h}}.

If
|V(G_{v})|\leq c
, then we let
H_{v}=G_{v}
.
Otherwise, we sample
c-h
light edges from
{\mathcal{D}}
with replacement. For each sampled edge, we assign to it a new weight of
\frac{w-w^{h}}{c-h}
.
The graph
H_{v}
simply contains all heavy edges in
G_{v}
and all sampled light edges (with their new weight).

For a subset
S\subseteq T
, let
w_{S}(v)
be the total weight of edges in
G_{v}
connecting
v
to a vertex in
S
, so
w=w_{T}(v)
, and similarly let
w^{\prime}_{S}(v)
be the total weight of edges in
H_{v}
connecting
v
to a vertex in
S
.
In this subsection we will omit
v
and only write
w_{S},w^{\prime}_{S}
for notational simplicity.
The following observation is immediate.

Observation 32
.

Graph
H_{v}
contains at most
c
edges with a total weight of
w
. Moreover, for every subset
S\subseteq T
,
{\mathbb{E}}\left[w^{\prime}_{S}\right]=w_{S}
.

We now measure the similarity of graphs
H_{v}
and
G_{v}
in terms of terminal cuts.
For a subset
S\subseteq T
, if
w^{\prime}_{S}>w/2
, then in graph
H_{v}
,
v
lies on the
S
side of the
(S,T\setminus S)
min-cut, otherwise
v
lies on the
T\setminus S
side.
We prove the following claim.

Claim 33
.

For a subset
S\subseteq T
with
w_{S}<w/2
,
\big(w-2\cdot w_{S}\big)\cdot\mathrm{Pr}\left[w^{\prime}_{S}>w/2\right]\leq{\varepsilon}\cdot w_{S}
.

Proof.

Assume by scaling that
w=1
.
We use the following Chernoff bound on negative associated random variables.

Lemma 34

(
[
DR96
]
)
.

Suppose
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are negatively associated random variables taking values in
\{0,1\}
. Let
X=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}X_{i}
and
\mu={\mathbb{E}}\left[X\right]
. Then for any
\delta>0
,

•

\mathrm{Pr}\left[X>(1+\delta)\mu\right]\leq e^{-\frac{\delta^{2}\mu}{(2+\delta)}}
; and

•

\mathrm{Pr}\left[X>(1+\delta)\mu\right]\leq\frac{e^{-\mu}}{(1+\delta)^{1+\delta}}
.

Let
X_{e}
be the random variable for the new weight of edge
e
in
H_{v}
(that is, if
e
is heavy, then
X_{e}=w(e)
, if
e
is light and sampled,
X_{e}=\frac{w-w^{h}}{c-h}
, and if
e
is light and not sampled,
X_{e}=0
). According to our sampling process, variables
\left\{X_{e}\right\}_{e\in E(G_{v})}
are negatively associated.
From
Lemma

34
, letting
\alpha=1/2-w_{S}
and
\delta=\alpha/w_{S}
, we have

•

\mathrm{Pr}\left[w^{\prime}_{S}>1/2\right]\leq e^{-\frac{c\delta^{2}w_{S}}{(2+\delta)}}=e^{-\frac{c\delta\alpha}{(2+\delta)}}
; and

•

\mathrm{Pr}\left[w^{\prime}_{S}>1/2\right]\leq\frac{e^{-cw_{S}}}{(1+\delta)^{1+\delta}}\leq\frac{e^{-cw_{S}}}{\big(\frac{1}{2w_{S}}\big)^{\frac{1}{2w_{S}}}}
;

(we have assumed that all edges are light, as the heavy edges have weights unchanged and can only help the concentration property of
w^{\prime}_{S}
).
We consider the following three cases.

Case 1:
1/2-{\varepsilon}/5<w_{S}\leq 1/2
.

In this case,
(1-2\cdot w_{S})<2\cdot{\varepsilon}/5<{\varepsilon}\cdot w_{S}
.

Case 2:
1/4<w_{S}<1/2-{\varepsilon}/5
.

In this case, since
\delta<1
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pr}\left[w^{\prime}_{S}>1/2\right]\leq e^{-\frac{c\delta\alpha}{3}}<e^{-\frac{2c\alpha^{2}}{3}}<\frac{3}{2c\alpha^{2}}<\frac{{\varepsilon}}{12\alpha},

where the second inequality is because
w_{S}<1/2
; the third one is becuase for any
x>0
,
e^{-x}<1/x
; and the last one is because
c=\left\lceil 100/{\varepsilon}^{2}\right\rceil
and
\alpha>{\varepsilon}/5
. Therefore,
2\alpha\cdot\mathrm{Pr}\left[w^{\prime}_{S}>1/2\right]<{\varepsilon}/6<{\varepsilon}\cdot w_{S}
.

Case 3:
w_{S}\leq 1/4
.

In this case,
2\cdot w_{S}\leq 1/2
, so
\mathrm{Pr}\left[w^{\prime}_{S}>1/2\right]\leq\frac{e^{-cw_{S}}}{(1/2w_{S})^{2}}\leq\frac{4w_{S}}{c}
. Therefore,
2\alpha\cdot\mathrm{Pr}\left[w^{\prime}_{S}>1/2\right]<\frac{12w_{S}}{c}<{\varepsilon}\cdot w_{S}
, since
\alpha<1/2
.
∎

The following statement is a byproduct of the Case 3 above and will be useful later.

Claim 35
.

If
w_{S}\leq 1/(2e)
,
\mathrm{Pr}\left[w^{\prime}_{S}>1/2\right]\leq e^{-\frac{w}{2w_{S}}}
.

5.2
Construction of the cut sparsifier

Step 1. Computing special cuts and keeping important vertices

For every pair
t,t^{\prime}
of terminals in
T
, we compute a min-cut in
G
separating
t
and
t^{\prime}
. This min-cut induces a terminal partition
(S_{t,t^{\prime}},T\setminus S_{t,t^{\prime}})
where
t\in S_{t,t^{\prime}}
and
t^{\prime}\in T\setminus S_{t,t^{\prime}}
. We call such partitions
special cuts
.

Claim 36
.

For every vertex
v
and every subset
S\subseteq T
, there is a special terminal cut
(\tilde{S},T\setminus\tilde{S})
, such that

\frac{\min\{w_{\tilde{S}}(v),w(v)-w_{\tilde{S}}(v)\}}{\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(\tilde{S})}\geq\frac{1}{k}\cdot\frac{\min\{w_{S}(v),w(v)-w_{S}(v)\}}{\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)}.

Proof.

Denote
\alpha=\frac{\min\{w_{S}(v),w(v)-w_{S}(v)\}}{\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)}
, so
\frac{w_{S}(v)}{\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)},\frac{w(v)-w_{S}(v)}{\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)}\geq\alpha
. Therefore, there exist terminals
t\in S
and
t^{\prime}\notin S
such that both
(v,t),(v,t^{\prime})
have weight at least
\frac{\alpha\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)}{k}
. Consider the special cut
(S_{t,t^{\prime}},T\setminus S_{t,t^{\prime}})
which is the min-cut in
G
separating terminals
t,t^{\prime}
, so
\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S_{t,t^{\prime}})\leq\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)
.
Denote
\tilde{S}=S_{t,t^{\prime}}
.
On the other hand, since the cut
(\tilde{S},T\setminus\tilde{S})
separates
t,t^{\prime}
,
(v,t),(v,t^{\prime})
cannot both lie in
E(v,\tilde{S})
or
E(v,T\setminus\tilde{S})
. Therefore,
w_{\tilde{S}}(v),w(v)-w_{\tilde{S}}(v)\geq\frac{\alpha\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)}{k}
.
Altogether,

\frac{\min\left\{w_{\tilde{S}}(v),w(v)-w_{\tilde{S}}(v)\right\}}{\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(\tilde{S})}\geq\frac{\alpha\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)}{k\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(\tilde{S})}=\frac{\alpha}{k}=\frac{1}{k}\cdot\frac{\min\{w_{S}(v),w(v)-w_{S}(v)\}}{\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)}.

∎

Let
\eta=\frac{{\varepsilon}^{4}}{1000k}
. We call a vertex
v

important
if there is a special terminal cut
(S,T\setminus S)
such that the contribution of
v
on it (that is, the value of
\mathsf{mincut}_{G_{v}}(S,T\setminus S)
) is at least
(\eta/k)\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)
. There are at most
k\cdot k/\eta=O(k^{3}/{\varepsilon}^{4})
important vertices. We keep all important vertices and their incident edges in the cut sparsifier. That is, important vertices are not contracted with any other vertices.

According to
Claim

36
, every non-important vertex
v
contributes at most
\eta\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)
to any cut
\mathsf{mincut}(S,T\setminus S)
. We handle them next.

Step 2. Contracting non-important vertices based on their sparsified stars

Recall that for each non-important non-terminal
v
, we have constructed in
Section

5.1
a sparsified star
H_{v}
mimicking the original star
G_{v}
in
G
. Let
\pi_{v}
be the profile of vertex
v
in graph
H_{v}
. For each profile
\pi
, we contract all non-important vertices
v
with
\pi_{v}=\pi
into a single node. This completes the construction of the contraction-based sparsifier. Denote the resulting graph by
G^{\prime}
.

Clearly,
G^{\prime}
is obtained from
G
by contractions only, so it is indeed a contraction-based sparsifier. The next observation shows that it contains at most

k^{O(1/{\varepsilon}^{2})}\cdot(1/{\varepsilon})^{O(1/{\varepsilon}^{4})}
vertices.

Observation 37
.

|V(G^{\prime})|\leq k^{O(1/{\varepsilon}^{2})}\cdot(1/{\varepsilon})^{O(1/{\varepsilon}^{4})}
.

Proof.

We have shown that the number of important vertices is
O(k^{3}/{\varepsilon}^{4})
. From
Theorem

2
, the number of different profiles of non-important vertices is at most
\binom{k}{c}\cdot c^{O(c^{2})}\leq k^{O(1/{\varepsilon}^{2})}\cdot(1/{\varepsilon})^{O(1/{\varepsilon}^{4})}
.
∎

In the rest of this section, we prove that
G^{\prime}
is indeed a quality-
(1+O({\varepsilon}))
cut sparsifier.

Fix a subset
S\subseteq T
and we analyze the difference between
\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)
and
\mathsf{mincut}_{G^{\prime}}(S)
.
For a non-terminal
v
, let
x_{v}
and
x^{\prime}_{v}
be the contribution of the vertex
v
for the cut
S
in
G
and
G^{\prime}
respectively, so
\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)=\sum_{v}x_{v}
.

Observation 38
.

If
v
is an important vertex, then
x^{\prime}_{v}=x_{v}
. If
v
is a non-important vertex, then
x^{\prime}_{v}
is either
x_{v}
or
w(v)-x_{v}
. Moreover,
{\mathbb{E}}\left[x^{\prime}_{v}\right]\leq(1+{\varepsilon})x_{v}
and
x_{v}\leq\eta\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)
.

Proof.

Since we have kept all important vertices in Step 1, their contribution to every terminal min-cut does not change.
For a non-important vertex
v
, since we constructed a sparsified star
H_{v}
for
G_{v}
, and decide the side of
v
for each terminal cut using the profile of
v
in
H_{v}
instead of
G_{v}
.
Therefore, when
w_{S}(v)\leq w(v)/2
and
w^{\prime}_{S}(v)\leq w(v)/2
, then
x^{\prime}_{v}=x_{v}
, otherwise
x^{\prime}_{v}=w(v)-x_{v}
, and the difference is
x^{\prime}_{v}-x_{v}=w(v)-2x_{v}
.
We have proved in
Claim

33
that for a subset
S\subseteq T
with
w_{S}(v)<w(v)/2
,
\big(w(v)-2\cdot w_{S}(v)\big)\cdot\mathrm{Pr}\left[w^{\prime}_{S}(v)>w(v)/2\right]\leq{\varepsilon}\cdot w_{S}(v)
, and so
{\mathbb{E}}\left[x^{\prime}_{v}\right]\leq(1+{\varepsilon})x_{v}
. Finally, from our discussion in Step 1,
x_{v}\leq\eta\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)
.
∎

We classify all non-important vertices into two types.
Let
V_{1}
be the set that contains all vertices
v
such that
x_{v}/w(v)\geq{\varepsilon}^{2}/k^{2}
, and let
V_{2}
contain the rest of non-important vertices.
The proof is completed by the following claims.

Claim 39
.

\mathrm{Pr}\left[\sum_{v\in V_{1}}(x^{\prime}_{v}-{\mathbb{E}}\left[x^{\prime}_{v}\right])\leq{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)\right]\leq 2^{-2k}
.

Proof.

We use the following version of Hoeffding’s inequality.

Lemma 40
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be independent random variables such that
a_{i}\leq X_{i}\leq b_{i}
. Let
S_{n}=\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}X_{i}
. Then for any
t>0
,

\mathrm{Pr}\left[S_{n}-{\mathbb{E}}\left[S_{n}\right]\geq t\right]\leq e^{-\frac{2t^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(b_{i}-a_{i})^{2}}}.

Let
\gamma=\frac{{\varepsilon}}{10}
.
For any vertex
v
in
V_{1}
,
x^{\prime}_{v}
is bounded by
x_{v}/\gamma
, so we can upper and lower bound
x^{\prime}_{v}
by
b_{v}=x_{v}/\gamma
and
a_{v}=0
. Also note that

\sum_{v\in V_{1}}(b_{v}-a_{v})^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\sum_{v\in V_{1}}x^{2}_{v}.

Since for any
v\in V_{1}
,
x_{v}\leq\eta\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)
,
\sum_{v\in V_{1}}x^{2}_{v}\leq\eta\cdot(\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S))^{2}
, which means

\sum_{v\in V_{1}}(b_{v}-a_{v})^{2}\leq\frac{\eta}{\gamma^{2}}\cdot(w(v))^{2}\leq\frac{{\varepsilon}^{2}}{10k}\cdot(\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S))^{2}.

From
Lemma

40
,

\mathrm{Pr}\left[\sum_{v\in V_{1}}(x^{\prime}_{v}-{\mathbb{E}}\left[x^{\prime}_{v}\right])\leq{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)\right]\leq e^{-\frac{2{\varepsilon}^{2}(\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S))^{2}}{\frac{{\varepsilon}^{2}}{10k}\cdot(\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S))^{2}}}\leq 2^{-2k}.

∎

Claim 41
.

\mathrm{Pr}\left[\sum_{v\in V_{2}}x^{\prime}_{v}>\sum_{v\in V_{2}}x_{v}+2{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)\right]<e^{-2k}
.

Proof.

We use the approach for proving the Chernoff bound, i.e. applying Markov’s inequality on the moment generate function of
\sum_{v\in V_{2}}x^{\prime}_{v}
. Since for every
v\in V_{2}
,
x_{v}\leq\gamma\cdot w(v)
, from
Claim

35
,
\mathrm{Pr}\left[x^{\prime}_{v}=w(v)-x_{v}\right]<e^{-\frac{w(v)}{2x_{v}}}
. For any
t>0
,

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}}\left[e^{tx^{\prime}v}\right]<e^{tx_{v}}+e^{-\frac{w(v)}{2x_{v}}}(e^{tw(v)}-e^{tx_{v}})<e^{tx_{v}}(1+e^{-\frac{w(v)}{2x_{v}}}(e^{tw(v)}-1))<e^{tx_{v}+e^{-\frac{w(v)}{2x_{v}}}(e^{tw(v)}-1)}.

Let
t=\frac{2k}{{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)}
. Since
x_{v}\leq\eta\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)
,
t\leq\frac{2\eta k}{{\varepsilon}x_{v}}
, which means
tw(v)\leq\frac{2\eta kw(v)}{{\varepsilon}x_{v}}\leq\frac{w(v)}{4x_{v}}
. This means
e^{-\frac{w(v)}{2x_{v}}}(e^{tw(v)}-1)<e^{-\frac{w(v)}{4x_{v}}}
. Since
\frac{w(v)}{x_{v}}\geq(1/\gamma)
and
\gamma=\frac{{\varepsilon}}{10}
, we have
\frac{w(v)}{x_{v}}>4(\log(w(v))-\log(x_{v})+\log(1/{\varepsilon}))
. So
e^{-\frac{w(v)}{4x_{v}}}<\frac{{\varepsilon}x_{v}}{w(v)}
. Therefore, if
tw(v)>1
,

{\mathbb{E}}\left[e^{tx^{\prime}_{v}}\right]<e^{tx_{v}+e^{-\frac{w(v)}{2x_{v}}}(e^{tw(v)}-1)}<e^{tx_{v}+{\varepsilon}x_{v}/w(v)}<e^{(1+{\varepsilon})tx_{v}}.

On the other hand, if
tw(v)\leq 1
,
e^{tw(v)-1}<2tw(v)
. Since
\frac{w(v)}{x_{v}}\geq(1/\gamma)
and
\gamma=\frac{{\varepsilon}}{10}
, we have
\frac{w(v)}{2x_{v}}>\log w(v)-\log x_{v}+\log(1/{\varepsilon})
, which means
e^{-\frac{w(v)}{2x_{v}}}<{\varepsilon}\cdot\frac{x_{v}}{w(v)}
. Therefore,

{\mathbb{E}}\left[e^{tx^{\prime}_{v}}\right]<e^{tx_{v}+e^{-\frac{w(v)}{2x_{v}}}(e^{tw(v)}-1)}<e^{tx_{v}+{\varepsilon}\frac{x_{v}}{w(v)}\cdot tw(v)}<e^{(1+{\varepsilon})tx_{v}}.

Therefore in either case, we have
{\mathbb{E}}\left[e^{tx^{\prime}_{v}}\right]<e^{(1+{\varepsilon})tx_{v}}
, and so

{\mathbb{E}}\left[e^{t\sum_{v\in V_{2}}x^{\prime}_{v}}\right]<e^{(1+{\varepsilon})t\sum_{v\in V_{2}}x_{v}}.

Therefore,

\begin{split}\mathrm{Pr}\left[\sum_{v\in V_{2}}x^{\prime}_{v}>\sum_{v\in V_{2}}x_{v}+2{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)\right]&<e^{(1+{\varepsilon})t\sum_{v\in V_{2}}x_{v}-t(\sum_{v\in V_{2}}x_{v}+2{\varepsilon}\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S))}\\
&<e^{-{\varepsilon}t\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)}<e^{-2k}.\end{split}

∎

Therefore,
\mathrm{Pr}\left[\sum_{v}x^{\prime}_{v}>(1+3{\varepsilon})\cdot\mathsf{mincut}_{G}(S)\right]<2^{-2k+1}
. Taking the union bound over all subsets
S\subseteq T
, with probability at least
1-2^{k-1}
,
G^{\prime}
is indeed a quality-
(1+3{\varepsilon})
cut sparsifier.

6
Proof of
Theorem

3

In this section we prove
Theorem

3
, showing that for every
k\geq 1
and
{\varepsilon}>0
, there exists a quasi-bipartite graph with
k
terminals, whose quality-
(1+{\varepsilon})
contraction-based cut sparsifier must contain
k^{\Omega\big(\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}\log(1/{\varepsilon})}\big)}
vertices. We will make use of the following intermediate problem, which is a variant of the
0
-Extension problem.

Boolean Hypercube Contraction (
BHC
).

In an instance of
BHC
, the input consists of

•

a graph
G=(V,E)
with
V=\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}
, namely each vertex is a
d
-dimensional
0/1
-string;

•

a set
T\subseteq V
of
k
terminals, such that every edge is incident to some terminal.

A solution is a mapping
f:V\to V
, such that for each terminal
t\in T
,
f(t)=t
. For solution
f
, its

•

cost
is defined as
\mathsf{cost}(f)=\sum_{(u,v)\in E}||f(u)-f(v)||_{1}
; and

•

size
is defined as
|\left\{f(v)\mid v\in V\right\}|
, namely the size of its image set.

The goal is to compute a solution
f
with small size and cost. Specifically, the following ratio called
stretch
, is to be minimized:

\rho=\frac{\sum_{(u,v)\in E}||f(u)-f(v)||_{1}}{\sum_{(u,v)\in E}||u-v||_{1}}.

In fact, the
BHC
problem is the special case of the
0
-Extension problem first introduced by Karzanov
[
Kar98
]
, as a generalization of the multi-way cut problem and a special case of the metric labeling problem.
The
0
-Extension problem and some of its variants were shown to be concretely connected to cut/flow sparsifiers
[
Moi09
,
LM10
,
AGK14
,
CT24a
,
CT24b
]
. Specifically,
BHC
focuses on searching for “canonical solutions” of the
0
-Extension with Steiner Nodes
problem studied
[
CT24a
]

Lemma 43
.

For every
k\geq 1
and
{\varepsilon}>0
, there exists a
k
-terminal quasi-bipartite instance of
BHC
, such that any solution of stretch
(1+{\varepsilon})
and must have size at least
k^{\Omega(1/({\varepsilon}\log(1/{\varepsilon})))}
.

Proof.

We construct the hard instance
(G,T)
of
BHC
as follows. The vertex set of
G
is
V=\left\{0,1\right\}^{d}
. The terminal set is
T=T_{0}\cup T_{1}\cup\left\{t^{*}_{0},t^{*}_{1}\right\}
, where
t^{*}_{0}
is the all-
0
vector,
t^{*}_{1}
is the all-
1
vector, and

T_{0}=\left\{v\mid||v||_{1}={\varepsilon}d\right\};\quad\quad T_{1}=\left\{v\mid||v||_{1}=(1-{\varepsilon})d\right\}.

So
k=|T|=2\cdot\binom{d}{{\varepsilon}d}+2
. Define
V^{\prime}=\left\{v\mid||v||_{1}=d/2\right\}
. The edge set is defined as follows:

E(G)=\bigcup_{v\in V^{\prime}}\bigg(\bigg\{(v,t)\text{ }\bigg|\text{ }||v-t||_{1}=(1/2-{\varepsilon})d\bigg\}\cup\bigg\{(v,t^{*}_{0}),(v,t^{*}_{1})\bigg\}\bigg),

(we assume that
{\varepsilon}d
and
d/2
are integers for convenience; the proof also holds if we replace
{\varepsilon}d
by
\left\lfloor{\varepsilon}d\right\rfloor
everywhere)
where the edges
(v,t^{*}_{0}),(v,t^{*}_{1})
have capacity
\binom{d/2}{{\varepsilon}d}
, or equivalently we can simply add
\binom{d/2}{{\varepsilon}d}
parallel edges connecting
v
to
t^{*}_{0}
and add
\binom{d/2}{{\varepsilon}d}
parallel edges connecting
v
to
t^{*}_{1}
. Therefore, every vertex
v\in V^{\prime}
has
4\cdot\binom{d/2}{{\varepsilon}d}
edges incident to it. We can pair these edges as follows: each edge connecting
v
to a terminal
t\in T_{0}
is paired with a copy of
(v,t^{*}_{1})
, and each edge connecting

to a terminal

is paired with a copy of
(v,t^{*}_{0})
. Note that each pair of edges form a shortest path connecting either
t^{*}_{0}
to a terminal in

or

to a terminal in
T_{0}
, with total
\ell_{1}
-length
(1-{\varepsilon})d
.

Clearly, every edge is incident to some terminal, and so

is indeed a quasi-bipartite graph.

We now show that any solution
f
to this instance of
BHC
with size at most
2^{d/5}
must have stretch at least
(1+\Omega({\varepsilon}))
. Since

k=2\cdot\binom{d}{{\varepsilon}d}+2=O(e/{\varepsilon})^{{\varepsilon}d}
,
2^{d/5}=k^{\Omega(1/({\varepsilon}\log(1/{\varepsilon})))}

For each vertex in the image set of
f
, the number of vertices in

that is at distance at most
d/100
away from it is at most
\sum_{i=0}^{d/100}\binom{d}{i}\leq(100e)^{d/100}\leq 2^{d/5}
.
As
|V^{\prime}|=\binom{d}{d/2}=\Omega(2^{d}/\sqrt{d})>2^{d/2}
, the number of vertices

with
||f(v)-v||_{1}\geq d/100

|V^{\prime}|-2^{d/5}\cdot 2^{d/5}>0.9\cdot|V^{\prime}|

For any such vertex
v
, either there are at least

coordinates with value

in

and
0
in
f(v)
, or there are at least
d/200
coordinates with value
0
in

and
1
in
f(v)
. Assume the former holds (the arguments for the latter is symmetric).
If we randomly sample a terminal

in
T_{0}

{\varepsilon}d/100
coordinate with value

\sum_{t\in T:t\sim v}||t-f(v)||_{1}-\sum_{t\in T:t\sim v}||t-v||_{1}\geq 2\cdot\frac{{\varepsilon}d}{100}\cdot 2\cdot\binom{d/2}{{\varepsilon}d}\geq\Omega({\varepsilon})\cdot\sum_{t\in T:t\sim v}||t-v||_{1}.

\begin{split}\frac{\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{t\in T:t\sim v}||t-f(v)||_{1}}{\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{t\in T:t\sim v}||t-v||_{1}}&=1+\frac{\sum_{v\in V}\big(\sum_{t\in T:t\sim v}||t-f(v)||_{1}-\sum_{t\in T:t\sim v}||t-v||_{1}\big)}{\sum_{v\in V}\sum_{t\in T:t\sim v}||t-v||_{1}}\\
&\geq 1+0.9\cdot\Omega({\varepsilon})=1+\Omega({\varepsilon}).\end{split}

This completes the proof.

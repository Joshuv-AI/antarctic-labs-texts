Rumors with Changing Credibility
Charlotte Out, Nicolás Rivera, Thomas Sauerwald, John Sylvester

Abstract
Randomized rumor spreading processes diffuse information on an undirected graph and have been widely studied. In this work, we present a generic framework for analyzing a broad class of such processes on regular graphs. Our analysis is protocol-agnostic, as it only requires the expected proportion of newly informed vertices in each round to be bounded, and a natural negative correlation property. This framework allows us to analyze various protocols, including PUSH , PULL , and PUSH-PULL , thereby extending prior research. Unlike previous work, our framework accommodates message failures at any time t ≥ 0 t\geq 0 with a probability of 1 − q ⁡ ( t ) 1-q(t) , where the credibility q ⁡ ( t ) q(t) is any function of time. This enables us to model real-world scenarios in which the transmissibility of rumors may fluctuate, as seen in the spread of “fake news” and viruses. Additionally, our framework is sufficiently broad to cover dynamic graphs.

Introduction

The rise of online social networks has facilitated a way for network users to rapidly obtain information, express their opinion, and stay in touch with friends and family. However, at the same time the large scale information cascades enabled by these new social technologies provide fertile ground for the spread of misinformation, rumors and hoaxes. This in turn can have severe consequences such as public panic, growing polarization, the manipulation of political events, and also economic damage. For instance, in 2013 a rumor that President Obama was injured in two explosions at the White House led to
\$90
billion USD being temporarily wiped off the value of United States stock market
[
33
]
. In the same year the World Economic Forum report
[
24
]
listed “massive digital misinformation” as one of the main risks for the modern society. More recently we have seen the spread of misinformation surrounding the Covid-19 pandemic
[
5
]
. Consequently, there has been a growing body of work aiming to gain insights into the rumor spreading dynamics
[
14
,
29
,
34
,
39
]
.

For a long time, randomized rumor spreading protocols such as the
PUSH
,
PULL
and
PUSH-PULL
protocols have been used to model the dissemination of information on graphs, e.g.,
[
3
,
15
,
26
]
. Both by mathematical analysis on “scale free” graphs in addition to experimental results on real-world social networks, it has been demonstrated that these protocols (in particular,
PUSH-PULL
) spread a rumor to a large fraction of vertices in a very short time (e.g.,
[
16
]
).

However, one shortcoming of the previous works that analyze these protocols is the assumption that the probability with which an individual believes the rumor, when receiving it, is constant over time – in fact, in many studies it is assumed that this
credibility
is equal to one in all rounds. In real world settings, one can imagine that the occurrence of emergent events (such as an earthquake or a new possibly lethal decease) can intensify the formation and propagation of rumors due to their suddenness and urgency, followed by a decrease in credibility once more information has become available. A related example is the spread of viruses, where counter-measures such as vaccination or social distancing, but also seasonal effects may affect the transmissibility over time, potentially even periodically/non-monotonically.

Moreover, it is often assumed that the graph is fixed throughout the execution of randomized rumor spreading protocols, which is rather restrictive since many networks, e.g., social networks, P2P networks or communication networks, are subject to frequent changes.

To address these issues, we introduce a new methodology for analyzing randomized rumor spreading protocols that allows us to study
PUSH
,
PULL
, and
PUSH-PULL
processes under the presence of a time-changing credibility (or transmissibility) function
q(t)
and dynamic graphs
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. However, our method is more general and allows us to study a broader class of spreading processes on dynamic graphs. To show the effectiveness of our analysis, we recover known results for the
PUSH
,
PULL
, and
PUSH-PULL
protocols in the context of a constant credibility function
q
, and provide analysis for specific time-dependent credibility functions
q(t)
.

1.1
Our Contribution

In this work, we present a general framework for analyzing a large class of randomized rumor spreading models. Our main results give concentration for the number of vertices informed after a certain stopping time. These results are very general however we show in detail how they can be applied to several models.

•

Broad Class of Spreading Processes.
Instead of using protocol specific characteristics, our framework only requires some mild conditions on the spreading process (i.e., bounded expected growth and a natural negative correlation property; see
Definition

2.1
).
This allows our setting to cover many models of randomized rumor spreading, beyond the standard
PULL
and
PUSH
models (see
Lemma

2.8
, the final bullet point below, and
Section

2.5
).

•

Credibility Function
q(t)
.
Our model allows for a time-dependent credibility function
q(t)\in[0,1]
, which specifies how transmissible the rumor is in each step. This can be seen as a major generalization of the prevalent notion of “robustness” in the literature, which usually refers to the uniform fault model with
q
fixed over
t
. Unlike in previous models, our credibility functions can be arbitrary, in particular they do not need to be monotone.

•

Stopping time Criterion.

We introduce a new technical tool based on a stopping time criterion. Roughly, for some desired number of vertices
B
to be informed, the stopping time triggers when a sum of expected growth factors of the process exceeds a threshold depending on
B
. The aforementioned growth factors are conditional expectations of the proportion of new vertices informed in the next step. We show that if this stopping criterion is met, then
B
vertices are informed with high probability (see
Theorem

3.1
). This is complemented by
Theorem

3.5
with a dual statement on the shrinking of the uninformed vertices.
Both results are significantly more general than previous analyses, which usually rely on a growth factor “target” that is independent of
t
and the set of informed vertices.

•

Dynamic Graphs.
Due to the general nature of our framework and stopping criteria, our analysis “abstracts away” the graph and the specific spreading process. Hence, we can cover sequences of dynamic regular graphs
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
instead of a fixed graph
G
. This flexibility comes from the fact that the connectivity of each
G_{t}
is captured by the growth factor of the process at round
t
, which in turn determines the stopping criterion. In particular, we do not require the graph to be connected at each step (see
Remark

3.2
).

•

Applications.
We prove several new results for general and specific credibility functions.
First, for general credibility functions, we combine our stopping time criterion with a simple lower bound based on sub-martingales. Together, they reveal a threshold phenomenon, very roughly saying that for expander graphs the quantity
\sum_{k=0}^{t}\log(1+q(k))
approximates
\log(|I_{t}|)
, where
I_{t}
is the set of vertices informed by time
t
(see
Section

4.1
).

After that, we turn to some specific credibility functions, including additive, multiplicative and Power-Law
(see
Sections

4.3
,
4.4
and
4.2
for the respective definitions and results). There, we prove several dichotomies in terms of the decay of
q(t)
.

Despite the generality and abstract nature of our main results,
we also recover some previous results for
static
graphs (and time-invariant
q(t)
) as a special case; however, our results for
PUSH
,
PULL
and
PUSH-PULL
additionally apply to
dynamic
graphs (see, e.g., the results in
Section

4.5
).

Related Work

Classical Protocols and Robustness.

Given a rumor spreading process on an
n
-vertex graph, define the spreading time by

as the first time all vertices are informed. The spreading time of

[
21
]

[

[

investigated the

Doerr and Kostrygin
[
17

derived a bound on the expected spreading time

of

, replicating the bound from
[
35
]
but only with an additive
O(1)
error instead of

.
Furthermore,
[
17
]
also considered
PULL
and
PUSH-PULL

O(1)
error. They also presented a more general result for the uniform fault model, where the leading factors are delicate functions of the (time-invariant) credibility
q\in(0,1]
. We are able to recover a with high probability version of the upper bounds from
[
17
]
for
PUSH
,
PULL
and
PUSH-PULL
(see
Section

4.5
).

Fountoulakis, Huber and Pangiotou
[
19
]
considered the uniform fault setting of
PUSH
on random graphs with
n
vertices where each edge is present w.p.
p=\omega(\log n/n)
. They proved that, up to lower-order terms, the same bound as for the complete graph holds. For the model without faults, Fountoulakis and Panagiotou
[
20
]
presented a tight analysis for
PUSH
on random
d
-regular graph for any constant
d\geq 3
. Panagiotou, Perez-Gimenez, Sauerwald and Sun
[
30
]
analyzed
PUSH
on almost-regular strong expanders, recovering the runtime bound for complete graphs up to low order terms (see
Eq.

2.1
for the definition of strong expander for regular graphs).

Finally, Daknama, Panagiotou and Reisser
[
12
]
greatly extended and unified these lines of works in terms of the graph classes considered, and the uniform fault model. Among other results, they proved that the aforementioned results from
[
17
]
(for
PUSH
,
PULL
and
PUSH-PULL
) also hold for almost-regular strong expanders, without any change in the leading factor. Our framework allows us to recover the upper bounds in
[
12
]
for regular graphs as well as dynamic sequences of regular graphs (see
Section

4.5
).

For general graphs (including highly non-regular ones), Chierichetti, Giakkoupis, Lattanzi and Panconesi
[
7
]
proved an upper bound of
O(\log n/\varphi)
on the time to inform all vertices for

[

]
for the

and

Dynamic Graphs.

Extending the aforementioned bounds for conductance and vertex expansion, Giakkoupis, Sauerwald and Stauffer

2.3
Our Class of Spreading Processes

We now introduce two general spreading processes, that are crucial to our framework. This is an abstraction of the aforementioned examples of
PUSH
,
PULL
and
PUSH-PULL
with credibility function
q(t)
, since we are now only considering the expected growth (or shrinking) factors. We point out that these may depend on several quantities such as the conductance of the informed set

Definition 2.1

(Growing and Shrinking Processes)
.

Let
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a sequence of graphs. Let
\mathcal{P}
be a stochastic process on
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
with a sequence of informed vertices
(I_{t})_{t\geq 0}\subseteq V(G_{t})
and uninformed vertices

for all
t\geq 0
. We begin by defining the following property of such a process

•

\mathcal{P}_{1}
(
Negative Correlation
): For any round
t\geq 0
and any subset
S\subseteq U_{t}
,

\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,\bigcap_{u\in S}\{u\in I_{t+1}\}\,\right]\leq\prod_{u\in S}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,u\in I_{t+1}\,\right].

For some time-independent value
C_{\mathrm{grow}}>0
we say that
\mathcal{P}
is a

-growing process if it satisfies
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and

•

\mathcal{P}_{2}
(
Monotonicity
): For any round
t\geq 0
, it holds deterministically that
I_{t}\subseteq I_{t+1}
(and
|I_{0}|\geq 1
),

•

\mathcal{P}_{3}
(
Bounded Expected Growth
): For any round
t\geq 0
the
expected growth factor satisfies
,

\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\,\right]\leq C_{\mathrm{grow}}.

Similarly, for some time-independent
C_{\mathrm{shrink}}<1
,
\mathcal{P}
is a
C_{\mathrm{shrink}}
-shrinking process if it satisfies
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and

•

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{2}
(

): For any round

, it holds deterministically that

(and
|U_{0}|\leq n/2
),

•

\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{3}
(

): For any round

\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|U_{t}|}~\,\right]\leq C_{\mathrm{shrink}}.

For convenience, we also define for all rounds

\delta_{t}:=\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{\min\left(|I_{t}|,|U_{t}|\right)}\,\right]=\max\left(\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\,\right],\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|U_{t}|}\,\right]\right).

. We note that,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Cov}_{t}\left[\,X_{u},X_{z}\,\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,X_{u}\cdot X_{z}\,\right]-\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,X_{u}\,\right]\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,X_{z}\,\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{u}=1\cap X_{z}=1\,\right]-\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{u}=1\,\right]\cdot\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{z}=1\,\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{u}=1\,\right]\cdot\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{z}=1\,\right]-\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{u}=1\,\right]\cdot\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{z}=1\,\right]

\displaystyle=0,

where
(a)
uses the negative correlation property (
\mathcal{P}_{1}
).
Moreover, since
X_{u}
is a Bernoulli random variable, we get that
\mathbf{Var}_{t}\left[\,X_{u}\,\right]=\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{u}=1\,\right]\cdot\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{u}=0\,\right]\leq\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{u}=1\,\right].

Returning to
Eq.

2.2
, we obtain

\mathbf{Var}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]\leq\sum_{u\in U_{t}}\mathbf{Var}_{t}\left[\,X_{u}\,\right]\leq\sum_{u\in U_{t}}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{u}=1\,\right]=\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\Delta_{t+1}\,\right].\qed

2.4
Specific Protocols and Growth Factors

In this subsection, we analyze specific protocols (in particular,
PUSH
,
PULL
and
PUSH-PULL
with credibility function
q(t)
) and verify that they are
C_{\mathrm{grow}}
-growing and
C_{\mathrm{shrink}}
-shrinking processes in the sense of
Definition

2.1
.

Let
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a sequence of regular graphs. Recall that in our setting
|I_{0}|=1
and
\Delta_{t+1}=I_{t+1}\setminus I_{t}
. In order to capture the progress of the rumor spreading process between the rounds
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
, we observe the following identities,

\displaystyle\frac{|I_{t_{2}}|}{|I_{t_{1}}|}=\prod_{t=t_{1}}^{t_{2}-1}\frac{|I_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}=\prod_{t=t_{1}}^{t_{2}-1}\frac{|I_{t}|+|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}=\prod_{t=t_{1}}^{t_{2}-1}\left(1+\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{|U_{t_{2}}|}{|U_{t_{1}}|}=\prod_{t=t_{1}}^{t_{2}-1}\frac{|U_{t+1}|}{|U_{t}|}=\prod_{t=t_{1}}^{t_{2}-1}\frac{|U_{t}|+|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|U_{t}|}=\prod_{t=t_{1}}^{t_{2}-1}\left(1-\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|U_{t}|}\right).

As such, we prove upper and lower bounds on the expectation of the growth factor,
\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{\min\left(|I_{t}|,|U_{t}|\right)}
of the
PUSH
,
PULL
and
PUSH-PULL
protocols.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
t\geq 0
be any round,
G_{t}
a
d_{t}
-regular graph with
n
vertices and
d_{t}\geq 1
, and
q(t)
an arbitrary credibility. Then,

(i)

for the
PUSH
protocol,

q(t)\cdot\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{2}\right)\cdot\varphi_{t}(I_{t})\leq\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{\min(|I_{t}|,|U_{t}|)}\,\right]\leq q(t)\cdot\varphi(I_{t}),

(ii)

for the
PULL

\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{\min\left(|I_{t}|,|U_{t}|\right)}\,\right]=q(t)\cdot\varphi(I_{t}),

(iii)

PUSH-PULL

\frac{3}{2}\cdot q(t)\cdot\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{2}\right)\cdot\varphi(I_{t})\leq\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{\min\left(|I_{t}|,|U_{t}|\right)}\,\right]\leq 2\cdot q(t)\cdot\varphi(I_{t}).

Proof.

For Item (

\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]=\sum_{u\in U_{t}}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,u\in I_{t+1}\,\right]=\sum_{u\in U_{t}}\left(1-\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,u\in U_{t+1}\,\right]\right).

(2.3)

Further, as
1-x\leq e^{-x}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,u\in U_{t+1}\,\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{v\in N(u)\cap I_{t}}\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}\right)=\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}\right)^{\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}\leq\exp\left(-\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}\right).

Since
\exp(x)\leq 1+x+\frac{1}{2}x^{2}
for any
x\in[-1,0]
(which can be applied since
\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}\leq 1
) we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,u\in U_{t+1}\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq 1-\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\left(\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}\right)^{2}\leq 1-\frac{q(t)\cdot\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{2}\right)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}},

(2.4)

where the second inequality follows since

\frac{\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}\leq 1.
Thus, by (
2.3
)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{u\in U_{t}}\frac{q(t)\cdot\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{2}\right)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{t}}\cdot q(t)\cdot\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{2}\right)\cdot e(I_{t},U_{t})

\displaystyle=q(t)\cdot\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{2}\right)\cdot\varphi(I_{t})\cdot\min(|I_{t}|,|U_{t}|).

To prove the upper bound on
\mathbf{E}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{\min(|I_{t}|,|U_{t}|)}\,\right]
we will bound
|\Delta_{t+1}|
from above. First, we note that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,u\in U_{t+1}\,\right]=\prod_{v\in N(u)\cap I_{t}}\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}\right)^{\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}\geq 1-\deg_{I_{t}}(u)\cdot\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}},

where the last step follows by Bernoulli’s inequality. Therefore,
by (
2.3
)

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]\leq\sum_{u\in U_{t}}\deg_{I_{t}}(u)\cdot\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}=\frac{1}{d_{t}}\cdot e(I_{t},U_{t})\cdot q(t)=q(t)\cdot\varphi_{t}(I_{t})\cdot\min(|I_{t}|,|U_{t}|).

Proof of (
ii
):
Let
u\in U_{t}
and let
X_{u}
be the indicator variable which is
1
if
u
gets informed in round
t+1
through
PULL
, and
0
otherwise. We note that
\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{u}=1\,\right]=\frac{\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}\cdot q(t)
. Hence,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in U_{t}}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{u}=1\,\right]=\sum_{u\in U_{t}}q(t)\cdot\frac{\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}=q(t)\cdot\frac{1}{d_{t}}\cdot e(I_{t},U_{t})=q(t)\cdot\varphi(I_{t})\cdot\min\left(|I_{t}|,|U_{t}|\right).

Proof of (
iii
):
The upper bound follows by adding up the two upper bounds on
|\Delta_{t+1}|
for
PUSH
and
PULL
.
Observe that
\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,u\in U_{t+1}\,\right]=\left(1-\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}{(u)}}{d_{t}}\right)\cdot\prod_{v\in N(u)\cap I_{t}}\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}\right)
. Thus, similarly to
Eq.

2.4
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|U_{t+1}|\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{u\in U_{t}}\left(1-\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}{(u)}}{d_{t}}\right)\cdot\left(1-\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\left(\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in U_{t}}\left(1-\frac{2\cdot q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}+\frac{3}{2}\cdot\left(\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}\right)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\left(\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}\right)^{3}\right).

Since for
z\in[0,1]
,
1-2\cdot z+\frac{3}{2}\cdot z^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\cdot z^{3}\leq 1-\frac{3}{2}\cdot z\left(1-\frac{z}{2}\right)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|U_{t+1}|\,\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\sum_{u\in U_{t}}\left(1-\frac{\frac{3}{2}\cdot q(t)\cdot\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{2}\right)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}|U_{t}|-\frac{3}{2}\cdot q(t)\cdot\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{2}\right)\cdot\varphi_{t}(I_{t})\cdot\min\left(|I_{t}|,|U_{t}|\right),

where
(a)
used that
\frac{\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}\leq 1
and
(b)
that
e(I_{t},U_{t})=d_{t}\cdot\varphi_{t}(I_{t})\cdot\min\left(|I_{t}|,|U_{t}|\right)
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Consider the
PUSH
protocol, and let

be any round where with

and
G_{t}
a
d_{t}
-regular graph with
n

\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\,\right]\geq q(t)\cdot\left(1-7\sqrt{\beta}\right).

For the same setting in the

\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\,\right]\geq q(t)\cdot\left(2-12\sqrt{\beta}\right).

Proof.

Let us first prove the result for the
PUSH
protocol.
We follow a similar approach to
[
30

.
We define

A:=\left\{u\in U_{t}\colon\deg_{I_{t}}(u)\geq 2d_{t}\cdot\sqrt{\beta}\right\}.

By definition of
A

|A|\cdot 2d_{t}\sqrt{\beta}\leq e(A,I_{t})\leq\frac{d_{t}|A|\cdot|I_{t}|}{n}+\lambda d_{t}\sqrt{|A|\cdot|I_{t}|}\leq\frac{d_{t}|A|\cdot|I_{t}|}{n}+d_{t}\beta\sqrt{|A|\cdot|I_{t}|}.

(2.5)

This implies

|A|\cdot 2\sqrt{\beta}\leq\frac{|A|\cdot|I_{t}|}{n}+\beta\sqrt{|A|\cdot|I_{t}|}.

Dividing by
|A|
and rearranging this, we can upper bound
|A|
as follows

\displaystyle|A|

\displaystyle\leq\beta^{2}\cdot|I_{t}|\cdot\left(\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\beta}-\frac{|I_{t}|}{n}}\right)^{2}.

We assume that
\beta\leq 1/49
(as otherwise the lower bound would trivial). Therefore, we have
2\sqrt{\beta}\geq 2\beta\geq 2\cdot\frac{|I_{t}|}{n}

\beta:=\lambda+\frac{|I_{t}|}{n}

. Thus, the denominator is bounded from below by

(2.6)

Next define

) and (

),

\displaystyle e(B,I_{t})

\displaystyle=e(U_{t},I_{t})-e(A,I_{t})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\geq}}(1-\lambda)\cdot d_{t}\cdot\frac{|U_{t}|\cdot|I_{t}|}{n}-\frac{d_{t}|A||I_{t}|}{n}-d_{t}\cdot\beta\sqrt{|A||I_{t}|}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{\geq}}\left(1-2\left(\lambda+\frac{|I_{t}|}{n}\right)-2\beta^{3/2}\right)\cdot d_{t}\cdot\frac{|U_{t}|\cdot|I_{t}|}{n}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{\geq}}(1-4\beta)\cdot d_{t}\cdot\frac{(n-|I_{t}|)\cdot|I_{t}|}{n}

\displaystyle\geq(1-5\beta)\cdot d_{t}\cdot|I_{t}|,

(2.7)

where
(a)
holds by applying
Lemma

A.10
to

and
Eq.

2.5
,
(b)
by
Eq.

2.6
,
(c)

and
(d)
by the definition of
\beta

\beta\leq 1/49\leq 1

\beta^{3/2}\leq\beta
).
Therefore, by
Eq.

2.4

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|U_{t+1}\cap B|\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{u\in U_{t}\cap B}\left(1-\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\left(\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{u\in U_{t}\cap B}\left(1-\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}\cdot\left(1-\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\sum_{u\in U_{t}\cap B}\left(1-\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}\cdot\left(1-2\sqrt{\beta}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=|U_{t}\cap B|-\left(1-2\sqrt{\beta}\right)\cdot\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}\cdot e(B,I_{t})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}|U_{t}\cap B|-\left(1-2\sqrt{\beta}\right)\cdot\left(1-5\beta\right)\cdot q(t)\cdot|I_{t}|,

where
(a)
used the definition of

and

, and
(b)
used
Eq.

2.7

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]\geq\left(1-7\sqrt{\beta}\right)\cdot q(t)\cdot|I_{t}|.

PUSH
.

We now turn to the
PUSH-PULL
protocol, where the derivation is almost identical to the one of
PUSH
. Here we use that in the

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,u\in U_{t+1}\,\right]

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}\right)^{\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}\cdot\left(1-\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)+\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}\deg_{I_{t}}(v)\right)\cdot\left(1-\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(v)}{d_{t}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 1-2\cdot\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}+\left(\frac{q(t)\cdot\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}{d_{t}}\right)^{2}.

Now the analogous derivation as for

yields the claim.

The next lemma improves over the lower and upper bound in
Lemma

2.3
(
i
) if
|I_{t}|\geq n/2
.
Concerning the lower bound, we have
q(t)\cdot(1-\frac{q(t)}{2})\leq 1-e^{-q(t)}
since
e^{-z}\leq 1-z+\frac{1}{2}z^{2}=1-z\cdot\left(1-\frac{z}{2}\right)
for
z\in[0,1]
. Further, if
d_{t}=\omega(1)
and
q(t)\cdot\varphi_{t}(I_{t})
is bounded below by a constant, then the upper bound below is tighter as
1-\exp(-x)\leq x
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Consider the
PUSH
protocol, and let
t\geq 0
be any round,
G_{t}
is a
d_{t}
-regular graph with
n
vertices and
q(t)
an arbitrary credibility. Then,

(i)

\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|U_{t}|}\,\right]\geq\left(1-e^{-q(t)}\right)\cdot\varphi(I_{t}).

(ii)

If
G_{t}
is connected, then,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|U_{t}|}\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq 1-e^{-\varphi(I_{t})\cdot q(t)}\cdot\left(1-\frac{\varphi(I_{t})\cdot(q(t))^{2}}{d_{t}}\right).

Proof.

We proceed by case distinction. We first consider the case where
d_{t}=1
and
q(t)=1
. We note that for
d_{t}=1
the graph
G_{t}
is a perfect matching. We note that then,

\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|U_{t+1}|\,\right]=|U_{t}|-|\Delta_{t+1}|=|U_{t}|-e(U_{t},I_{t}).

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|U_{t}|}\,\right]=\frac{e(U_{t},I_{t})}{|U_{t}|}=\frac{\varphi(I_{t})\cdot|U_{t}|}{|U_{t}|}=\varphi(I_{t}).

Clearly,
\varphi(I_{t})\geq(1-e^{-1})\cdot\varphi(I_{t})
, proving statement
(i)
. Moreover,
1-e^{-\varphi(I_{t})}\cdot(1-\varphi(I_{t}))\geq 1-(1-\frac{\varphi(I_{t})}{2})\cdot(1-\varphi(I_{t}))\geq\varphi(I_{t})
and thus statement
(ii)
holds as well for this case. Now, we consider the scenario where
d_{t}>1
or (
q_{t}<1
and
d_{t}\geq 1
). For the
PUSH
protocol,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|U_{t+1}|\,\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in U_{t}}\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}\right)^{\deg_{I_{t}}(u)}=:g.

(2.8)

For fixed
\sum_{u\in U_{t}}\deg_{I_{t}}(u)=e(I_{t},U_{t})
, our goal is to estimate the last expression, viewed as a function over
(\deg_{I_{t}}(u))_{u\in U_{t}}\in\{0,1,\ldots,d_{t}\}
, using a Schur-convexity argument. To avoid discretization issues, we will first define a more “generous” function
which has
|U_{t}|\cdot d_{t}
real-valued variables
(z_{k}(u))_{u\in U_{t},1\leq k\leq d_{t}}\in[0,d_{t}]
:

\displaystyle h:=\frac{1}{d_{t}}\cdot\sum_{u\in U_{t}}\sum_{k=1}^{d_{t}}\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}\right)^{z_{k}(u)},

(2.9)

where
\sum_{u\in U_{t}}\sum_{k=1}^{d_{t}}z_{k}(u)=e(I_{t},U_{t})\cdot d_{t}
. Clearly, the maximum of
g
is upper bounded by the maximum of
h
(by choosing for all
u\in V
and
1\leq k\leq d_{t}
,
z_{k}(u)=\deg_{I_{t}}(u)
). Let us consider the function
f(x,z):=(1-x)^{z}
where
x
corresponds to
\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}
in
h
and
z
to
z_{k}(u)
.
We have that
\frac{\partial f(x,z)}{\partial z}=(1-x)^{z}\cdot\log\left(1-x\right)
and
\frac{\partial^{2}f(x,z)}{\partial^{2}z}=(1-x)^{z}\cdot\left(\log\left(1-x\right)\right)^{2}
. We note that the second derivative is greater or equal to
0
for any
x\leq 1
. This proves that
f(x,z)
is convex in
z
. Since
h
(as a function in
(z_{k})_{u\in U_{t},1\leq k\leq d_{t}}
) is a sum of convex functions,
h
itself is also convex. Further, as
h
is symmetric in
(z_{k})_{u\in U_{t},1\leq k\leq d_{t}}
, it follows that
h
is Schur-convex.

Proof of (
i
) for
d_{t}>1
or
(q_{t}<1
and
d_{t}\geq 1)
: Since
z_{k}(u)\in[0,d_{t}]
, the function
h
is maximized if all
z_{k}(u)\in\{0,d_{t}\}
, and therefore

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|U_{t+1}|\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq\max_{(z_{k}(u))_{u\in U_{t},1\leq k\leq d_{t}}}h

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{d_{t}}\cdot\left(\frac{e(I_{t},U_{t})\cdot d_{t}}{d_{t}}\cdot\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}\right)^{d_{t}}+\left(|U_{t}|\cdot d_{t}-\frac{e(I_{t},U_{t})\cdot d_{t}}{d_{t}}\right)\cdot\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}\right)^{0}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e(I_{t},U_{t})}{d_{t}}\cdot\exp\left(-q(t)\right)+\left(|U_{t}|-\frac{e(I_{t},U_{t})}{d_{t}}\right)\cdot 1

\displaystyle=|U_{t}|-\frac{e(I_{t},U_{t})}{d_{t}}\cdot\left(1-e^{-q(t)}\right).

Since
e(I_{t},U_{t})=d_{t}\cdot\varphi(I_{t})\cdot|U_{t}|
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-e^{-q(t)}\right)\cdot\varphi(I_{t})\cdot|U_{t}|.

Proof of (
ii
) for
d_{t}>1
or
(q_{t}<1
and
d_{t}\geq 1)
: Now, we assume that
G_{t}
is connected, and thus
d_{t}\geq 2
. We note that the Schur-convex function
h
is minimized if all arguments are equal, and therefore

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|U_{t+1}|\,\right]

\displaystyle\geq\min_{(z_{k}(u))_{u\in U_{t},1\leq k\leq d_{t}}}h

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{d_{t}}\sum_{u\in U_{t}}\sum_{k=1}^{d_{t}}\left(1-\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}\right)^{\frac{e(U_{t},I_{t})}{|U_{t}|}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\geq}}|U_{t}|\cdot\exp\left(-\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}\cdot\frac{e(I_{t},U_{t})}{|U_{t}|}\cdot\left(1+\frac{q(t)}{d_{t}}\right)\right)

3
Lower Bounding the Number of Informed vertices

Our analysis will be split into two phases, a “growing” phase where
|I_{t}|\leq n/2
, and a “shrinking” phase where
|I_{t}|\geq n/2
.

3.1
Growing phase:
I_{t}\in[A,B]

In this section, we prove a lower bound on the number of informed vertices after a stopping time
\tau_{2}
, which aggregates over the expected growth factors between round
1
and
\tau_{2}-1
. In the following theorem (and throughout the rest of this paper) we use the convention that
\min\left\{\emptyset\right\}=\infty
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be any sequence of
d_{t}
-regular
n
-vertex graphs and consider a
C_{\mathrm{grow}}
-growing process
\mathcal{P}
with expected growth factors
\delta_{t}
. Let
t_{1}\geq 0
be any round, and let
A,B
be thresholds satisfying
1\leq A<B\leq n/2
and
\xi:=10^{-30}
. Define the stopping time
\tau_{2}
as

\tau_{2}:=\min\left(s\geq t_{1}\colon\sum^{s-1}_{t=t_{1}}\log\left(1+\delta_{t}\right)\geq\frac{\log\left(\frac{B}{A}\right)+\left(\log\left(\frac{B}{A}\right)+\log(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}})+1\right)^{2/3}}{\left(1-(1-\xi)\cdot|I_{t}|^{-\xi}\right)^{2}}\right).

(3.1)

Then there is a constant
C_{2}>0
such that

\mathbf{P}_{t_{1}}\left[\,|I_{\tau_{2}}|<B~\Big|~|I_{t_{1}}|\geq A\,\right]\leq\exp\left(-C_{2}\cdot\left(\log\left(\frac{B}{A}\right)\right)^{1/3}\right)+\mathbf{P}_{t_{1}}\left[\,\tau_{2}=\infty~\Big|~|I_{t_{1}}|\geq A\,\right].

Recall that the growth factors
\delta_{t}
are conditional expectations given by
\delta_{t}=\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t}|}{|I_{t}|}\,\right]
in the growing phase, where
|I_{t}|\leq n/2
. Intuitively, the stopping time
\tau_{2}
in
Corollary

4.3
can be viewed as a partial observer who does not know the sequence
I_{t}
, but only gets to know the expected growth factors in each round.

Remark 3.2
.

At first it might look challenging to apply
Theorem

3.1
, as one would need to control the probability that the stopping time is unbounded. However, in most applications we have a deterministic lower bound on the expected growth in each step and then, provided this bound is sufficiently large, this probability vanishes. See
Corollary

4.3
for a weaker but easier to apply variant of
Theorem

3.1
which leverages this idea. The use of this stopping time also allows
Theorem

3.1
to be very general. For instance, notice that
G_{t}
is not required to always be connected; this gives flexibility when handling dynamic graphs. This works because if it is not possible to spread the rumor (say due to connectivity issues), then this implies
\tau_{2}
is unbounded.

We will now give a brief overview of the proof of
Theorem

3.1
, followed by some helper lemmas and claims, and then complete the proof. The starting point is to analyze the growth rate of the number of informed vertices. To this end, we recall the following formula involving growth factors:

\displaystyle\frac{|I_{\tau_{2}}|}{|I_{t_{1}}|}

\displaystyle=\prod_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}\frac{|I_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}=\prod_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}\left(1+\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\right).

(3.2)

In order to transform this product into a sum of random variables, we first define for any
t\geq 0
,

X_{t}:=\log\left(1+\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\right).

Then, by taking logarithms in
Eq.

3.2
we obtain that

\log\left(\frac{|I_{\tau_{2}}|}{|I_{t_{1}}|}\right)=\sum_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}X_{t}.

Our approach will be to lower bound the sum of these
X_{t}
’s. Therefore, we will consider the expected (logarithmic) growth in each step (i.e.
\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}\,\right]
) (note that due to the dependence on
\mathcal{F}_{t}
it is also a random variable). We then show that
\sum_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}X_{t}
is tightly concentrated around
\sum_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}\,\right]
, using a variant of Azuma’s concentration inequality (
Lemma

A.7
). In doing so, we face the following difficulty of relating the expectation of
X_{t}
to the expected growth factor
\delta_{t}
. Specifically, we would like to apply the following approximation:

\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\log\left(1+\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\right)\,\right]\approx\log\left(1+\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\,\right]\right)=\log\left(1+\delta_{t}\right).

One direction in this approximation is immediate; since
\log(\cdot)
is concave, Jensen’s inequality gives us

\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\log\left(1+\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\right)\,\right]\leq\log\left(1+\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\,\right]\right).

It thus remains to bound the other direction, which amounts to proving an “approximate reverse version” of Jensen’s inequality. This is fairly involved, but we manage to establish the following general lemma:

Lemma 3.3
.

For a fixed round
t\geq 0
, let
G_{t}
be a regular
n
-vertex graph and consider a
C_{\mathrm{grow}}
-growing process
\mathcal{P}
.
If
|I_{t}|\in[A,n/2]
, then, for
\xi:=10^{-30}
, we have

\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\log\left(1+\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\right)\,\right]\geq\left(1-(1-\xi)\cdot|I_{t}|^{-\xi}\right)^{2}\cdot\log\left(1+\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\,\right]\right).

Note that the first factor on the right-hand side of the inequality above is
(1-o(1))
in the case
|I_{t}|=\omega(1)
(i.e., a super-constant number of vertices are informed).

Proof.

For brevity, we will denote

Z:=\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}.

We proceed by a case distinction dependent on the size of
\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right]
. Recall that as
\mathcal{P}
is a
C_{\mathrm{grow}}
-growing process, by
\mathcal{P}_{3}
and
\mathcal{P}_{4}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right]=:\delta_{t}\leq C_{\mathrm{grow}},

\displaystyle\mathbf{Var}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right]=\frac{1}{|I_{t}|^{2}}\cdot\mathbf{Var}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]\leq\frac{1}{|I_{t}|^{2}}\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]\leq\frac{\delta_{t}}{|I_{t}|}.

(3.3)

Case 1:
Let us assume
\delta_{t}\geq 8\cdot|I_{t}|^{-1/4}
.
We will first establish a concentration inequality, stating that
Z
is not much smaller than its expectation.
By Chebyshev’s inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,\left|Z-\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right]\right|\geq\left(|I_{t}|\cdot\delta_{t}\right)^{-1/3}\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right]\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathbf{Var}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right]}{\left(|I_{t}|\cdot\delta_{t}\right)^{-2/3}\cdot(\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right])^{2}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\frac{\delta_{t}/|I_{t}|}{\left(|I_{t}|\cdot\delta_{t}\right)^{-2/3}\cdot(\delta_{t})^{2}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{=}}\left(|I_{t}|\cdot\delta_{t}\right)^{-1/3},

(3.4)

where
(a)
used the upper bound on the variance of
Z
, and the identity for
\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right]
from
Eq.

3.3
. Observe that

\left(|I_{t}|\cdot\delta_{t}\right)^{-1/3}\leq\left(|I_{t}|\cdot 8|I_{t}|^{-1/4}\right)^{-1/3}=\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot|I_{t}|^{-1/4},

(3.5)

by the condition for Case 1. Therefore, as
Z\geq 0
and
\log(1+Z)\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\log(1+Z)\,\right]

\displaystyle\geq\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,Z\geq\left(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot|I_{t}|^{-1/4}\right)\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right]\,\right]\cdot\log\left(1+\Bigl(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot|I_{t}|^{-1/4}\Bigr)\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right]\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\geq}}\left(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot|I_{t}|^{-1/4}\right)\cdot\left(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot|I_{t}|^{-1/4}\right)\cdot\log(1+\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right])

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{=}}\left(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot|I_{t}|^{-1/4}\right)^{2}\cdot\log(1+\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right]),

(3.6)

where
(a)
used
Eq.

3.4
,
Eq.

3.5
, and
A.14
with
z:=\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right]
and
a:=\tfrac{1}{2}\cdot|I_{t}|^{-1/4}
. This completes Case 1.

Case 2:
The other case is
\delta_{t}\leq 8\cdot|I_{t}|^{-1/4}
.
In this case
Z
may not be concentrated (it was in Case 1) . However,
\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right]
is so small that
\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\log(1+Z)\,\right]
is close to
\log(1+\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right])
as even “outliers” cannot contribute too much to
\log(1+Z)
.
Recall that
\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]=\delta_{t}\cdot|I_{t}|
. Thus, for any
\eta\geq 0
,

\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,Z\geq(1+\eta)\cdot\delta_{t}\,\right]=\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\geq(1+\eta)\cdot\delta_{t}\cdot|I_{t}|\,\right]=\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\geq(1+\eta)\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\Delta_{t+1}\,\right]\,\right].

Applying the general form of the Chernoff bound (
Lemma

A.6
statement (iv)),

\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,Z\geq(1+\eta)\cdot\delta_{t}\,\right]\leq\left(\frac{e}{1+\eta}\right)^{(1+\eta)\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]}=\left(\frac{e}{1+\eta}\right)^{(1+\eta)\cdot\delta_{t}\cdot|I_{t}|}.

(3.7)

Set
\eta
satisfying
1+\eta=\zeta\cdot(|I_{t}|\cdot\delta_{t})^{-1}
for any
\zeta\geq 128|I_{t}|^{3/4}
, and observe that

1+\eta\geq 128|I_{t}|^{3/4}\cdot(|I_{t}|\cdot\delta_{t})^{-1}=128\cdot|I_{t}|^{-1/4}\cdot\delta_{t}^{-1}\geq 16\geq e^{5/2},

(3.8)

Now returning to the bound from
Eq.

3.7
,

\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,Z\geq\zeta\cdot|I_{t}|^{-1}\,\right]\leq\left(\frac{e}{1+\eta}\right)^{\zeta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\left(\frac{1}{1+\eta}\right)^{3\zeta/5}=\left(\frac{1}{1+\eta}\right)^{\zeta/10}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{1+\eta}\right)^{\zeta/2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{1+\eta}\cdot\left(\frac{1}{1+\eta}\right)^{\zeta/2},

where
(a)
holds by
Eq.

3.8
and
(b)
since
\zeta/10\geq 1
and
1+\eta\geq 1
. Inserting
1+\eta=\zeta\cdot(|I_{t}|\cdot\delta_{t})^{-1}
gives

\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,Z\geq\zeta\cdot|I_{t}|^{-1}\,\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\leq}}\frac{|I_{t}|\cdot\delta_{t}}{\zeta}\cdot\left(\frac{|I_{t}|\cdot\delta_{t}}{\zeta}\right)^{\zeta/2}.

(3.9)

We now define the set

\mathcal{I}:=\left\{\frac{0}{|I_{t}|},\frac{1}{|I_{t}|},\frac{2}{|I_{t}|},\ldots,\frac{\lfloor 128|I_{t}|^{3/4}\rfloor}{|I_{t}|}\right\}\subseteq\operatorname{Supp}(Z).

(3.10)

Then, by the definition of expectation,

\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\log(1+Z)\,\right]=\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Supp}(Z)}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,Z=x\,\right]\cdot\log\left(1+x\right)\geq\sum_{x\in\mathcal{I}}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,Z=x\,\right]\cdot\log\left(1+x\right),

(3.11)

In the following, we would like to estimate
\log(1+x)
by
x
, for each
x\in\mathcal{I}
. To that end, we do a case distinction:

•

Case 2a:
x=0
. In this case
\log(1+x)=\log(1)=0
and
x=0
, so we have

\log(1+x)=x\cdot 1.

•

Case 2b:
|I_{t}|^{-1}\leq x\leq 128|I_{t}|^{-1/4}
. For any
z\geq 0
,
\log(1+z)\geq z-z^{2}/2=z\cdot(1-z/2)
.
Hence for any
x>0
,

\log(1+x)\geq x\cdot\left(1-64|I_{t}|^{-1/4}\right).

•

Case 2c:
|I_{t}|^{-1}\leq x\leq 128|I_{t}|^{-1/4}

and
128|I_{t}|^{-1/4}\geq 1
.
Rearranging the second precondition implies

x\geq|I_{t}|^{-1}\geq 128^{-4}.

(3.12)

Now, we have

\displaystyle\log(1+x)

\displaystyle=x\cdot\frac{\log(1+x)}{x}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\geq}}x\cdot\frac{\log(1+128^{-4})}{128|I_{t}|^{-1/4}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\geq}}x\cdot\frac{\log(1+128^{-4})}{128}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{:=}}x\cdot C_{1},

where
(a)
used the upper bound on
x
from Case
2c
in the denominator and
Eq.

3.12
within the
\log(\cdot)
in the numerator, and
(b)
used that
|I_{t}|^{-1/4}\leq 1
, and
(c)
defines the constant
C_{1}:=\frac{\log(1+128^{-4})}{128}\in(0,1/1000)
.

Since when
128|I_{t}|^{-1/4}\leq 1
holds, the estimate from Case 2b is tighter than the estimate we would get from Case 2c (if we were allowed to apply it), and the estimate in Case 2a is always tighter than the one from Case 2b and 2c, we conclude that for any
x\in\mathcal{I}

\log(1+x)\geq x\cdot\left(1-\min\left(64|I_{t}|^{-1/4},1-C_{1}\right)\right).

Hence, by
Eq.

3.11
we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\log(1+Z)\,\right]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{x\in\mathcal{I}}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,Z=x\,\right]\cdot x\cdot\left(1-\min\left(64|I_{t}|^{-1/4},1-C_{1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\min\left(64|I_{t}|^{-1/4},1-C_{1}\right)\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Supp}(Z)}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,Z=x\,\right]\cdot x-\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Supp}(Z)\setminus\mathcal{I}}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,Z=x\,\right]\cdot x\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\min\left(64|I_{t}|^{-1/4},1-C_{1}\right)\right)\cdot\left(\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right]-\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Supp}(Z)\setminus\mathcal{I}}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,Z=x\,\right]\cdot x\right).

(3.13)

We proceed to bound the sum in the second factor on the right-hand side of
Eq.

3.13
, as follows

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Supp}(Z)\setminus\mathcal{I}}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,Z=x\,\right]\cdot x

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in\operatorname{Supp}(Z)\setminus\mathcal{I}}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,Z\geq x\,\right]\cdot x

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\sum_{\zeta=\lfloor 128|I_{t}|^{3/4}\rfloor+1}^{\max(\operatorname{Supp}(Z)\setminus\mathcal{I})\cdot|I_{t}|}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,Z\geq\frac{\zeta}{|I_{t}|}\,\right]\cdot\frac{\zeta}{|I_{t}|}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}\sum_{\zeta=\lfloor 128|I_{t}|^{3/4}\rfloor+1}^{\infty}\frac{|I_{t}|\cdot\delta_{t}}{\zeta}\cdot\left(\frac{|I_{t}|\cdot\delta_{t}}{\zeta}\right)^{\zeta/2}\cdot\frac{\zeta}{|I_{t}|}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{=}}\delta_{t}\cdot\sum_{\zeta=\lfloor 128|I_{t}|^{3/4}\rfloor+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{|I_{t}|\cdot\delta_{t}}{\zeta}\right)^{\zeta/2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{\leq}}\delta_{t}\cdot\sum_{\zeta=\lfloor 128|I_{t}|^{3/4}\rfloor+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{|I_{t}|\cdot 8|I_{t}|^{-1/4}}{128|I_{t}|^{3/4}}\right)^{\zeta/2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{=}}\delta_{t}\cdot\sum_{\zeta=\lfloor 128|I_{t}|^{3/4}\rfloor+1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{16}\right)^{\zeta/2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(d)}}{{\leq}}\delta_{t}\cdot 2^{-128|I_{t}|^{3/4}}

(3.14)

where
(a)
holds by the definition of
\mathcal{I}
from
Eq.

3.10
,
(b)
holds by
Eq.

3.9
,
(c)
holds since
\gamma\geq 128|I_{t}|^{3/4}
, and
\delta_{t}\leq 8|I_{t}|^{-1/4}
as we are in Case 2, and finally
(d)
holds by comparison with a geometric series. Recall that
\delta_{t}:=\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right]
, and so combining
Eq.

3.13
and
Eq.

3.14
gives

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\log(1+Z)\,\right]

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\min\left(64|I_{t}|^{-1/4},1-C_{1}\right)\right)\cdot\left(1-2^{-128|I_{t}|^{3/4}}\right)\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\geq}}\left(1-\min\left(64|I_{t}|^{-1/4},1-C_{1}\right)\right)^{2}\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right]

where
(a)
holds since
2^{-128|I_{t}|^{3/4}}\leq 2^{-128}\leq 1-C_{1}
as
C_{1}<1/1000
, and
2^{-128|I_{t}|^{3/4}}\leq 64|I_{t}|^{-1/4}
. Now,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\log(1+Z)\,\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\geq}}\left(1-(1-C_{1}^{2})\cdot|I_{t}|^{-C_{1}^{2}}\right)^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{\geq}}\left(1-(1-10^{-30})\cdot|I_{t}|^{-10^{-30}}\right)^{2},

(3.15)

where
(b)
holds due to
A.13
as
C_{1}\in(0,1/1000)
, and
(c)
since
C_{1}:=\frac{\log(1+128^{-4})}{128}\geq 10^{-15}
.

Now taking the worst-case lower bound over
Eq.

3.6
and
Eq.

3.15
, as well as noting that
|I_{t}|\geq A
and
\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right]\geq\log(1+\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,Z\,\right])
, completes the proof.
∎

Lastly, before beginning the proof of
Theorem

3.1
, we first state the following helper claim.

Claim 3.4
.

For
\tau_{2}
and
\xi:=10^{-30}
as in
Theorem

3.1
and
1\leq A\leq B\leq n/2
, the following holds,

\sum_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}\delta_{t}\leq\frac{4}{\xi^{2}}\cdot\left(\log\left(\frac{B}{A}\right)+\log(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}})+1\right).

Proof.

As the round
\tau_{2}
was chosen to be minimal,
A\geq 1
and
\xi:=10^{-30}
, we have,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}\log(1+\delta_{t})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\log(\frac{B}{A})+\left(\log\left(\frac{B}{A}\right)+\log(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}})+1\right)^{2/3}}{\left(1-(1-\xi)\cdot A^{-\xi}\right)^{2}}+\log(1+\delta_{\tau_{2}-1})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\log(\frac{B}{A})+\left(\log\left(\frac{B}{A}\right)+\log(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}})+1\right)^{2/3}}{\xi^{2}}+\log(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\xi^{2}}\cdot\left(\log\left(\frac{B}{A}\right)+\log(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}})+1\right).

Lastly, as
\log(1+z)\geq z/2
for any
z\in[0,1]
we get that

\sum_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}\delta_{t}\leq\frac{4}{\xi^{2}}\cdot\left(\log\left(\frac{B}{A}\right)+\log(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}})+1\right),

as claimed.
∎

We are now ready to prove our lower bound on the informed set during the growing phase.

Proof of
Theorem

3.1
.

Recall that,

X_{t}:=\log\left(1+\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\right).

and if
|I_{t}|\leq n/2

\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\,\right]:=\delta_{t}.

Moreover, let us define

Y_{t}:=\sum_{s=t_{1}}^{t-1}\left(X_{s}-\mathbf{E}_{s}\left[\,X_{s}\,\right]\right).

By construction,
(Y_{t})_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}
is a zero-mean martingale with respect to
I_{t_{1}},I_{t_{1}+1},\ldots,I_{\tau_{2}-1}
. To apply concentration inequalities, we need to provide a bound (
M
) on
Y_{t}-Y_{t+1}
when
|I_{t}|\leq n/2
. In this case,

\displaystyle Y_{t}-Y_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\sum^{t-1}_{s=t_{1}}\left(X_{s}-\mathbf{E}_{s}\left[\,X_{s}\,\right]\right)-\sum^{t}_{s=t_{1}}\left(X_{s}-\mathbf{E}_{s}\left[\,X_{s}\,\right]\right)=-\left(X_{t}-\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}\,\right]\right).

Now, using in
(a)
that
X_{t}\geq 0
deterministically, Jensen’s inequality in
(b)
, and in
(c)
the fact that
\mathcal{P}
is a
C_{\mathrm{grow}}
-growing process, we obtain

\displaystyle Y_{t}-Y_{t+1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\log\left(1+\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\right)\,\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}\log\left(1+\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\,\right]\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{\leq}}\log\left(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}}\right):=M.

(3.16)

We seek concentration for
Y_{\tau_{2}}
, however
\tau_{2}
may be very large (even unbounded). Thus, we cannot use a standard version of Azuma’s inequality, and we need to additionally consider the conditional variances,
\mathbf{Var}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}\,\right]
. To this end, we bound the variance for any round
t
with
|I_{t}|\leq n/2
, by using
Lemma

A.2
in
(a)
,

\mathbf{Var}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}\,\right]=\mathbf{Var}_{t}\left[\,\log\left(1+\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\right)\,\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\mathbf{Var}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\,\right]=\frac{1}{|I_{t}|^{2}}\cdot\mathbf{Var}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right].

By Using
Lemma

2.2
and by recalling the definition
\delta_{t}=\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\,\right]
, assuming
|I_{t}|\leq n/2
, we get

\mathbf{Var}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}\,\right]\leq\frac{1}{|I_{t}|}\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\,\right]=\frac{1}{|I_{t}|}\cdot\delta_{t}.

(3.17)

Note that by
3.4
, and using that
|I_{t}|\geq A
for all
t\geq t_{1}
,

\sum_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}\frac{1}{|I_{t}|}\cdot\delta_{t}\leq\frac{1}{A}\cdot\frac{4}{\xi^{2}}\left(\log\left(\frac{B}{A}\right)+\log\left(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}}\right)+1\right).

We are almost in a position to apply the concentration inequality (
Lemma

A.7
) to
Y_{\tau_{2}}
. The only slight tweak is that we will work with a martingale also stopped by
\tau:=\min\{t\geq t_{1}:|I_{t}|\geq n/2\}
, namely

{\widehat{Y}}_{t}:=Y_{t\wedge\tau_{2}\wedge\tau},

which is also a zero-mean martingale that satisfies
Eqs.

3.16
and
3.17
. The reason for this is that the bound (
3.17
) assumes the inequality
|I_{t}|\leq n/2
holds; we loose nothing doing this because
|B|\leq n/2
.

Now, applying
Lemma

A.7
to
\widehat{Y}_{t}
yields that for any
h>0
and
t\geq t_{1}

\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,\widehat{Y}_{t}<-h\,\right]<\exp\left(-\frac{h^{2}}{2\cdot\left(\frac{1}{A}\cdot\frac{4}{\xi^{2}}\left(\log\left(\frac{B}{A}\right)+\log\left(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}}\right)+1\right)+\frac{1}{3}\left(\log\left(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}}\right)\cdot h\right)\right)}\right).

(3.18)

Let us set,

h:=\left(\log\left(\frac{B}{A}\right)+\log(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}})+1\right)^{2/3}\geq 1.

(3.19)

Thus, for this
h
and any round
t\geq t_{1}
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{t_{1}}\left[\,\widehat{Y}_{t}<-h\,\right]

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{h^{2}}{2\left(\frac{4h^{3/2}}{A\cdot\xi^{2}}+\frac{\log(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}})h}{3}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{h^{2}}{2\left(\frac{4h^{3/2}}{A\cdot\xi^{2}}+\frac{\log(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}})h^{3/2}}{3}\right)}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-C_{2}\cdot h^{1/2}\right),

(3.20)

where
C_{2}
is given by
\left(\frac{8}{A\cdot\xi^{2}}+\frac{2}{3}\cdot\log\left(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}}\right)\right)^{-1}>0
. Observe that the right-hand side of (
3.20
) is independent of
t
, this will be important later. However, at this point we must make the following claim:

\text{Conditional on $|I_{t_{1}}|\geq A$, we have }\{Y_{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau}\geq-h\}\cap\{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau<\infty\}\subseteq\{|I_{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau}|\geq B\}\cap\{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau<\infty\}.

(3.21)

We prove this later, first we show how this, together with our earlier estimates, will establish the theorem.

Returning to the proof, by (
3.20
), we have that for any integer
t\geq 0
,

\mathbf{P}_{t_{1}}\left[\,Y_{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau}<-h,\;\tau_{2}\wedge\tau\leq t\,\right]\leq\exp\left(-C_{2}\cdot h^{1/2}\right).

Since the above bound holds for any integer
t\geq 0
, it follows that

\mathbf{P}_{t_{1}}\left[\,Y_{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau}<-h,\;\tau_{2}\wedge\tau<\infty\,\right]\leq\exp\left(-C_{2}\cdot h^{1/2}\right).

(3.22)

Observe that
|I_{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau}|\leq|I_{\tau_{2}}|
by monotonicity (
\mathcal{P}_{2}
). Using this fact, then (
3.21
), and finally (
3.22
), we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{t_{1}}\left[\,|I_{\tau_{2}}|<B~\Big|~|I_{t_{1}}|\geq A\,\right]

(3.23)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}_{t_{1}}\left[\,|I_{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau}|<B~\Big|~|I_{t_{1}}|\geq A\,\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}_{t_{1}}\left[\,|I_{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau}|<B,\tau_{2}\wedge\tau<\infty~\Big|~|I_{t_{1}}|\geq A\,\right]+\mathbf{P}_{t_{1}}\left[\,|I_{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau}|<B,\tau_{2}\wedge\tau=\infty~\Big|~|I_{t_{1}}|\geq A\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}_{t_{1}}\left[\,Y_{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau}<-h,\tau_{2}\wedge\tau<\infty~\Big|~|I_{t_{1}}|\geq A\,\right]+\mathbf{P}_{t_{1}}\left[\,\tau_{2}\wedge\tau=\infty~\Big|~|I_{t_{1}}|\geq A\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-C_{2}\cdot h^{1/2}\right)+\mathbf{P}_{t_{1}}\left[\,\tau_{2}=\infty~\Big|~|I_{t_{1}}|\geq A\,\right],

which, recalling the definition (
3.19
) of
h
, gives the bound in the statement.

It remains to prove the claimed containment in (
3.21
). For that we analyze the behavior of
|I_{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau}|
when the event
\{Y_{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau}\geq-h\}\cap\{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau<\infty\}
holds. We will split into two cases.

In the first case

\{Y_{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau}\geq-h\}\cap\{\tau<\infty,\tau\leq\tau_{2}\}
. Hence,
|I_{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau}|=|I_{\tau}|\geq n/2\geq B
.

In the second case

\{Y_{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau}\geq-h\}\cap\{\tau_{2}<\infty,\tau_{2}<\tau\}
. Thus,
Y_{\tau_{2}\wedge\tau}=Y_{\tau_{2}}
, and deterministically we have,

Y_{\tau_{2}}=\sum_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}\left(X_{t}-\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}\,\right]\right)\geq-h.

Rearranging this, we get that,

\displaystyle\sum_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}X_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}\,\right]-h=\sum^{\tau_{2}-1}_{t=t_{1}}\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\log\left(1+\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\right)\,\right]-h\geq\gamma\cdot\sum_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}\log\left(1+\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\,\right]\right)-h,

where the last inequality follows from
Lemma

3.3
, and
\gamma:=\left(1-(1-\xi)\cdot|I_{t}|^{-\xi}\right)^{2}
for
\xi:=10^{-30}
. Since
\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\,\right]=\delta_{t}
for rounds
t
with
|I_{t}|\leq n/2
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}X_{t}

\displaystyle\geq\gamma\cdot\sum_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}\log\left(1+\delta_{t}\right)-h=\gamma\cdot\sum_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}\log\left(1+\delta_{t}\right)-\left(\log\left(\frac{B}{A}\right)+\log(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}})+1\right)^{2/3}.

Finally, by using that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}\log\left(1+\delta_{t}\right)\geq\frac{\log(\frac{B}{A})+\left(\log\left(\frac{B}{A}\right)+\log(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}})+1\right)^{2/3}}{\gamma},

we conclude that
\sum_{t=t_{1}}^{\tau_{2}-1}X_{t}\geq\log(\frac{B}{A})
, i.e. that
|I_{\tau_{2}}|-|I_{t_{1}}|\geq B-A
, and thus
|I_{\tau_{2}}|\geq B
. ∎

3.2
Shrinking phase:
|U_{t}|\in[C,D]

In this section we consider the shrinking of the number of informed vertices. We prove an upper bound on the number of uninformed vertices after a stopping time
\tau_{3}\geq t_{2}
, which now aggregates over the expected shrinking factors between round
t_{2}
and
\tau_{3}-1
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be any sequence of
d_{t}
-regular
n
-vertex graphs and consider a
C_{\mathrm{shrink}}
-shrinking process
\mathcal{P}
with expected shrinking factors
\delta_{t}
. Let
C,D
be thresholds satisfying
n/2\geq C\geq D\geq\frac{3}{4}
and
t_{2}\geq 0
be a round such that
|U_{t_{2}}|\leq C
. We define a stopping time
\tau_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
as

\tau_{3}:=\min\left\{s\geq t_{2}\colon\sum^{\tau_{3}-1}_{t=t_{2}}\log\left(1-\delta_{t}\right)\leq-\frac{1}{\gamma}\cdot\left(\log\left(\frac{C}{D}\right)+\left(\log\left(\frac{C}{D}\right)-\log\left(1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}}\right)+1\right)^{2/3}\right)\right\},

(3.24)

where

\gamma:=\left(1-\min\left(\frac{1}{2(1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}})\cdot D},\frac{1}{2}\right)\right).

Then there is a constant
C_{2}>0
such that

\mathbf{P}_{t_{2}}\left[\,|U_{\tau_{3}}|>D~\Big|~|U_{t_{2}}|\leq C\,\right]\leq\exp\left(-C_{2}\cdot\left(\log\left(\frac{C}{D}\right)\right)^{1/3}\right)+\mathbf{P}_{t_{2}}\left[\,\tau_{3}=\infty~\Big|~|U_{t_{2}}|\leq C\,\right].

The proof of
Theorem

3.5
follows a similar flow as the proof of
Theorem

3.1
. We note that, just like in the growing phase,

\frac{|U_{\tau_{3}}|}{|U_{t_{2}}|}=\prod^{\tau_{3}-1}_{t=t_{2}}\frac{|U_{t+1}|}{|U_{t_{2}}|}=\prod^{\tau_{3}-1}_{t=t_{2}}\left(1-\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|U_{t}|}\right).

However, unlike in the growing phase, we cannot take the logarithm here, as both the numerator
|U_{t+1}|
and the denominator
|U_{t}|
may be zero. To avoid this, we define a different sequence with an artificial offset
\alpha
should
|U_{t}|
become zero:

\widetilde{U}_{t}:=\max\left(|U_{t}|,\alpha\right),

where we choose
\alpha:=\frac{1}{2}
. We also define

\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}:=\min\left(|\Delta_{t+1}|,|U_{t}|-\alpha\right).

(3.25)

Similarly to the growing phase, we will now introduce the logarithmic shrinking factor, defined as

X_{t}:=\log\left(1-\frac{\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}}{|U_{t}|}\right)\leq 0.

(3.26)

and set,

\widehat{U}_{t}:=\widetilde{U}_{t\wedge\tau},

where
\tau:=\min\{t\geq t_{2}\colon|U_{t}|\leq D\}
. Now taking the logarithm yields, as long as
\tau_{3}<\tau
,

\displaystyle\log\left(\frac{\widehat{U}_{\tau_{3}}}{U_{t_{2}}}\right)=\sum_{t=t_{2}}^{\tau_{3}-1}\log\left(1-\frac{\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}}{|U_{t}|}\right)=\sum_{t=t_{2}}^{\tau_{3}-1}X_{t}

(3.27)

Recall that
|U_{t_{2}}|\leq C
, hence rearranging
Eq.

3.27
yields

\log\left(\widehat{U}_{\tau_{3}}\right)=\log(|U_{t_{2}}|)+\sum_{t=t_{2}}^{\tau_{3}-1}X_{t}\leq\log(C)+\sum_{t=t_{2}}^{\tau_{3}-1}X_{t}.

Furthermore if
\sum_{t=t_{2}}^{\tau_{3}-1}X_{t}<-\log(C)+\log(D)
, then it follows that

\widehat{U}_{\tau_{3}}\leq D\quad\Rightarrow\quad|U_{\tau_{3}}|\leq\widehat{U}_{\tau_{3}}\leq D.

Hence our goal is to prove that

\displaystyle\sum_{t=t_{2}}^{\tau_{3}-1}X_{t}=\sum_{t=t_{2}}^{\tau_{3}-1}\log\left(1-\frac{\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}}{|U_{t}|}\right)\leq\log\left(\frac{D}{C}\right),

(3.28)

with
D<C
. Note that in the last expression, both sides are negative.

Before we can start with the proof of
Theorem

3.5
, we will state one crucial lemma that lower bounds the expectation and upper bounds the variance of the
X_{t}
, and subsequently, additional helper results.

Lemma 3.6
.

Consider a regular graph
G_{t}
and a
C_{\mathrm{shrink}}
-shrinking process
\mathcal{P}
with expected shrinking factors
\delta_{t}
and round
t\geq 0
with
1\leq|U_{t}|\leq\frac{n}{2}
. Recall that
X_{t}=\log\left(1-\frac{\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}}{|U_{t}|}\right)
. Then, there is a constant
\kappa<0
such that,

\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}\,\right]\geq\kappa.

Furthermore, there is a constant
\nu>0
such that,

\mathbf{Var}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}\,\right]\leq\nu\cdot\delta_{t}=\nu\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|U_{t}|}\,\right]

Proof of
Lemma

3.6
.

Recall that
\alpha=1/2
.
In order to lower bound the expectation, note that for any
\chi\leq 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}\,\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\geq}}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}>\chi\,\right]\cdot\chi+\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}\leq\chi\,\right]\cdot\log\left(\frac{\alpha}{|U_{t}|}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 1\cdot\chi+\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}\leq\chi\,\right]\cdot\log\left(\frac{\alpha}{|U_{t}|}\right),

(3.29)

where
(a)
holds since
\log\left(\frac{\alpha}{|U_{t}|}\right)
is the smallest (i.e., most negative) value the random variable

X_{t}
can attain, and in the last inequality we use that
\chi\leq 0
. Therefore, it remains to find a suitable constant
\chi\leq 0
such that
\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}\leq\chi\,\right]
is of the order
-1/\left(\log\left(\frac{\alpha}{|U_{t}|}\right)\right)
for any
|U_{t}|\geq 1
. Now, for any
\chi\leq 0
we have,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}\leq\chi\,\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,\log\left(1-\frac{\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}}{|U_{t}|}\right)\leq\chi\,\right]=\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,1-\frac{\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}}{|U_{t}|}\leq e^{\chi}\,\right]=\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}\geq(1-e^{\chi})\cdot|U_{t}|\,\right].

Further, due to the capping,
\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}\,\right]\leq\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]=\delta_{t}\cdot|U_{t}|
. We will assume (with foresight) that from now on that the constant
\chi
satisfies

\chi\leq\min\left(2\log(1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}}),-1\right).

(3.30)

To show that such a
\chi
satisfies the desired properties above, we continue with a case distinction:

Case 1:

\delta_{t}\leq\left(\frac{1-e^{\chi}}{e}\right)^{2}
.
Choosing
\eta:=\frac{1-e^{\chi}}{\delta_{t}}-1
,
the general Chernoff bound (
Lemma

A.6
(iv)) implies

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\,\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}\geq(1-e^{\chi})\cdot|U_{t}|\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\geq(1+\eta)\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{e}{1+\eta}\right)^{(1+\eta)\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\delta_{t}\cdot\frac{e}{1-e^{\chi}}\right)^{(1-e^{-\chi})\cdot|U_{t}|}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\left(\delta_{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}\cdot(1-e^{-\chi})\cdot|U_{t}|}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}\left(\delta_{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}\cdot|U_{t}|},

where
(a)
used that by the condition of Case
1
,

\frac{e}{1-e^{\chi}}\leq(\delta_{t})^{-1/2}
,
and
(b)
used that for
\chi\leq-1
,
1-e^{\chi}\geq 1/2
.

Case 2:

\delta_{t}\geq\left(\frac{1-e^{\chi}}{e}\right)^{2}
.
We choose again
\eta:=\frac{1-e^{\chi}}{\delta_{t}}-1
. Since
|U_{t}|\leq n/2
by assumption, and
\mathcal{P}
is a
C_{\mathrm{shrink}}
-shrinking process, by
Definition

2.1
(
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{3}
),
\delta_{t}\leq C_{\mathrm{shrink}}
. Moreover, for
\chi\leq 2\log(1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}})
by
Eq.

3.30
, we have

\frac{1-e^{\chi}}{\delta_{t}}\geq\frac{2C_{\mathrm{shrink}}-C_{\mathrm{shrink}}^{2}}{C_{\mathrm{shrink}}}=2-C_{\mathrm{shrink}},

and consequently,
\eta\geq 1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}}
.

Here, by another version of the Chernoff bound (
Lemma

A.6
,
(iii)
).

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\,\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}\geq(1-e^{\chi})\cdot|U_{t}|\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\geq(1+\eta)\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\eta^{2}}{2+\eta}\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\exp\left(-\frac{\eta^{2}}{3\eta/(1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}})}\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}\exp\left(-\frac{1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}}}{3/(1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}})}\cdot\delta_{t}\cdot|U_{t}|\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{=}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left(1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}}\right)^{2}}{3}\cdot\delta_{t}\cdot|U_{t}|\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\delta_{t}\right)^{-1/\log(\delta_{t})\cdot\frac{\left(1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}}\right)^{2}}{3}\cdot\delta_{t}\cdot|U_{t}|},

(3.31)

where
(a)
used that since
\eta\geq 1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}}
(and
1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}}<1
), we have
2+\eta\leq 2\eta/(1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}})+\eta\leq 3\eta/(1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}})
. In
(b)
we used again that
\eta\geq 1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}}
as well as the fact that
\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]=\delta_{t}\cdot|U_{t}|
, since we assumed that
|U_{t}|\leq n/2
.
Next, in order to lower bound
\frac{\delta_{t}}{\log(\delta_{t})}
, define the function

f(z):=\frac{z}{\log(z)},

for the range
z\in[\left(\frac{1-e^{\chi}}{e}\right)^{2},C_{\mathrm{shrink}}]
. This function is non-decreasing in
z
, and therefore is minimized for
z=\left(\frac{1-e^{\chi}}{e}\right)^{2}\geq\left(\frac{1-e^{-1}}{e}\right)^{2}\geq e^{-4}
, having used
\chi\leq-1
. Applying this to
Eq.

3.31
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\,\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}\geq(1-e^{\chi})\cdot|U_{t}|\,\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\delta_{t}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\log\left(e^{-4}\right)}\cdot\frac{\left(1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}}\right)^{2}}{3}\cdot e^{-4}\cdot|U_{t}|}=\left(\delta_{t}\right)^{\frac{\left(1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}}\right)^{2}}{12e^{4}}\cdot|U_{t}|}.

Combining the two cases, it follows that there is a constant
c_{1}=c_{1}(C_{\mathrm{shrink}}):=\frac{\left(1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}}\right)^{2}}{12e^{4}}>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\,X_{t}\leq\chi\,\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left[\,\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}\geq(1-e^{\chi})\cdot|U_{t}|\,\right]\leq\left(\delta_{t}\right)^{c_{1}\cdot|U_{t}|}.

(3.32)

Therefore, returning to the crucial term in
Eq.

3.29
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}\leq\chi\,\right]\cdot\log\left(\frac{\alpha}{|U_{t}|}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\delta_{t}\right)^{c_{1}\cdot|U_{t}|}\cdot\log\left(\frac{\alpha}{|U_{t}|}\right)

\displaystyle=-\left(\delta_{t}\right)^{c_{1}\cdot|U_{t}|}\cdot\log\left(\frac{|U_{t}|}{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\geq}}-\left(\delta_{t}\right)^{c_{1}\cdot|U_{t}|/2}\cdot\left(C_{\mathrm{shrink}}\right)^{c_{1}\cdot|U_{t}|/2}\cdot\log\left(2\cdot|U_{t}|\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{=}}-\left(\delta_{t}\right)^{c_{1}\cdot|U_{t}|/2}\cdot\exp\left(\log(C_{\mathrm{shrink}})c_{1}\cdot|U_{t}|/2+\log\log\left(2\cdot|U_{t}|\right)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\geq}}-\left(1\right)^{c_{1}\cdot|U_{t}|/2}\cdot c_{2}=-c_{2},

(3.33)

where
(a)
holds as
\delta_{t}\leq C_{\mathrm{shrink}}
,
\alpha=1/2
, and
(b)
as
\delta_{t}\leq 1
and
\exp\left(-\log\left(\frac{1}{C_{\mathrm{shrink}}}\right)c_{1}\cdot|U_{t}|/2+\log\log\left(2\cdot|U_{t}|\right)\right)
can be upper bounded by some constant
c_{2}>0
. Using this in
Eq.

3.29
yields the first statement,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,X_{t}\,\right]

\displaystyle\geq\chi-c_{2}=:\kappa.

For the upper bound on the variance, recall that the definition
\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}:=\min\left(|\Delta_{t}|,|U_{t}|-\alpha\right)
. Let
X_{1},X_{2}\sim\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}
be two independent random variables with the same distribution as
\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbf{Var}_{t}\left[\,\log\left(1-\frac{\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}}{|U_{t}|}\right)\,\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mathbf{E}\left[\,\left(\log\left(1-\frac{X_{1}}{|U_{t}|}\right)-\log\left(1-\frac{X_{2}}{|U_{t}|}\right)\right)^{2}\,\right]

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sum_{k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathrm{Supp}(\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1})}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}=k_{1}\,\right]\cdot\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}=k_{2}\,\right]\cdot\left(\log\left(1-\frac{k_{1}}{|U_{t}|}\right)-\log\left(1-\frac{k_{2}}{|U_{t}|}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\underbrace{\frac{1}{2}\left(\cdot\sum_{0\leq k_{1},k_{2}\leq\min\left((1-e^{\chi})\cdot|U_{t}|,|U_{t}|-\alpha\right)}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|=k_{1}\,\right]\cdot\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|=k_{2}\,\right]\cdot\left(\log\left(1-\frac{k_{1}}{|U_{t}|}\right)-\log\left(1-\frac{k_{2}}{|U_{t}|}\right)\right)^{2}\right)}_{:=A}

\displaystyle\quad\underbrace{+1\cdot\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}\geq\min\left((1-e^{\chi})\cdot|U_{t}|,|U_{t}|-\alpha\right)\,\right]\cdot\left(\log\left(\frac{|U_{t}|}{\alpha}\right)\right)^{2}}_{:=B},

where
(a)
follows from
Lemma

A.1
,
and
(b)
follows from the definition of expectation, and the fact that
X_{1},X_{2}\sim\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}
are independent.
We will now first bound
A
, and then
B
.
Regarding
A
,
Since
\log(.)
is concave, we have for any
k_{1}\geq k_{2}
,

\log\left(1-\frac{k_{2}}{|U_{t}|}\right)\leq\log\left(1-\frac{k_{1}}{|U_{t}|}\right)+\left(\frac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{|U_{t}|}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{1-\frac{k_{1}}{|U_{t}|}}\leq\log\left(1-\frac{k_{1}}{|U_{t}|}\right)+\left(\frac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{|U_{t}|}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{e^{\chi}},

where the last inequality used that
k_{1}\leq(1-e^{\chi})\cdot|U_{t}|
. Therefore,

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\cdot e^{2\chi}}\cdot\sum_{0\leq k_{1},k_{2}\leq\min\left((1-e^{\chi})\cdot|U_{t}|,|U_{t}|-\alpha\right)}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|=k_{1}\,\right]\cdot\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|=k_{2}\,\right]\cdot\left(\frac{k_{1}}{|U_{t}|}-\frac{k_{2}}{|U_{t}|}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\cdot e^{2\chi}}\cdot\sum_{0\leq k_{1},k_{2}\leq|U_{t}|}\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|=k_{1}\,\right]\cdot\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|=k_{2}\,\right]\cdot\left(\frac{k_{1}}{|U_{t}|}-\frac{k_{2}}{|U_{t}|}\right)^{2}.

For the next step we use
Lemma

A.1
in
(a)
and the bounded variance property (
Lemma

2.2
) in
(b)
, giving

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\cdot e^{2\chi}}\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{\left(X_{1}-X_{2}\right)^{2}}{|U_{t}|}\,\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\frac{1}{e^{2\chi}}\cdot\mathbf{Var}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|U_{t}|}\,\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}\frac{1}{e^{2\chi}}\cdot\frac{1}{|U_{t}|}\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\right]=\frac{1}{e^{2\chi}}\cdot\delta_{t}.

In order to upper bound
B
, we perform a case distinction.

First
, if
(1-e^{\chi})\cdot|U_{t}|<|U_{t}|-\alpha
(which by rearranging, yields
|U_{t}|>\frac{1}{2e^{\chi}}
), then

Eq.

3.32
gives

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}_{t}\left[\,\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}\geq(1-e^{\chi})\cdot|U_{t}|\,\right]\cdot\left(\log\left(\frac{|U_{t}|}{\alpha}\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\delta_{t}\right)^{c_{1}\cdot|U_{t}|/2}\cdot\left(\delta_{t}\right)^{c_{1}\cdot|U_{t}|/2}\cdot\left(\log\left(2\cdot|U_{t}|\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\delta_{t}\cdot\left(\delta_{t}\right)^{c_{1}\cdot|U_{t}|/2}\cdot\left(\log\left(2\cdot|U_{t}|\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}\delta_{t}\cdot\left(C_{\mathrm{shrink}}\right)^{c_{1}\cdot|U_{t}|/2}\cdot\left(\log\left(2|U_{t}|\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\delta_{t}\cdot\exp\left(\log\left(C_{\mathrm{shrink}}\right)\cdot c_{1}/2\cdot|U_{t}|+\log\log\left(2\cdot|U_{t}|\right)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{\leq}}\delta_{t}\cdot c_{3},

(3.34)

where in
(a)
we used that
c_{1}\cdot|U_{t}|/2\geq\frac{c_{1}}{4e^{c}}\geq 1
as
c_{1}:=\frac{\left(1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}}\right)^{2}}{12e^{4}}
, in
(b)
that
\delta_{t}\leq C_{\mathrm{shrink}}
since
\mathcal{P}
is a
C_{\mathrm{shrink}}
-shrinking process and
|U_{t}|\leq n/2
. Lastly, in
(c)
we used that
\exp\left(-\log\left(\frac{1}{C_{\mathrm{shrink}}}\right)c_{1}/2\cdot|U_{t}|+\log\log\left(2\cdot|U_{t}|\right)\right)
can be upper bounded by some constant
c_{3}>0
.

Secondly
, if
(1-e^{\chi})\cdot|U_{t}|\geq|U_{t}|-\alpha=|U_{t}|-\frac{1}{2}
holds (i.e.,
|U_{t}|\leq\frac{1}{2e^{\chi}}
), then by Markov’s inequality.

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\,\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}\geq|U_{t}|-\alpha\,\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left[\,\Delta_{t+1}\geq|U_{t}|\,\right]\leq\frac{\mathbf{E}\left[\,\Delta_{t+1}\,\right]}{|U_{t}|}=\delta_{t},

where the last equality holds since we assume that
|U_{t}|\leq n/2
. Therefore, in the second case,

\displaystyle B\leq\delta_{t}\cdot\left(\log(2\cdot|U_{t}|)\right)^{2}\leq\delta_{t}\cdot\frac{1}{\chi^{2}}.

Combining our bounds on
A+B
we finally conclude,

The next lemma is based on Jensen’s inequality and the fact that

Lemma 3.8
.

Consider the setting of

\displaystyle\leq\log\left(1-\frac{1}{|U_{t}|}\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\widetilde{\Delta}_{t+1}\,\right]\right)

4
Applications

In this section we will apply our general results to more concrete credibility functions, protocols and graph classes. We do not give an exhaustive list of all results that could be derived from our analysis framework, but instead choose to analyze some natural models with decaying credibility, and show that despite the flexible and abstract nature of the framework, we can recover some known results. Roughly speaking, in this section we will first present results that are very general but not necessarily tight, followed by more specific results that are asymptotically tight up to lower order terms.

We will now outline the general approach followed in this section. To control the growth of
|I_{t}|
we break the process into
j
phases defined by time steps
[t_{i},t_{i+1})
for
1\leq i\leq j
. With each phase
i
we associate two values
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
, where
A_{i}<B_{i}
, such that at the beginning of the
i
-th phase the informed set has size at least
A_{i}
and w.h.p. when the phase ends the informed set has size at least
B_{i}
. We use the size of the informed set at the end of the previous phase as a lower bound on the size of the informed set throughout the current phase (i.e.
B_{i-1}=A_{i})
. The w.h.p. guarantees on the length and growth of phases are provided by
Corollary

4.3
and
Corollary

4.4
(which are direct consequences of
Theorem

3.1
and
Theorem

3.5
respectively). These results also give us expressions for the time to finish the phase i.e.
t_{i+1}-t_{i}
.

Definition 4.1
.

For a round
t\geq 0
and any subset
I\subseteq V
with
1\leq|I|\leq n-1
, let

\delta_{t}(I):=\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\delta_{t}\,\mid\,I_{t}=I\,\right]=\frac{1}{\min(|I_{t}|,|U_{t}|)}\cdot\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|\Delta_{t+1}|\,\mid\,I_{t}=I\,\right],

(4.1)

be the expected growth factor, conditional on
I_{t}=I
(this is in fact, a deterministic quantity).
Further, for a fixed range of
[A,B]
, we define a worst-case lower bound on the expected growth factor (which only depends on
t
) by

\delta_{t}^{[A,B]}:=\min_{I\subseteq V\colon A\leq|I|\leq B}\delta_{t}(I).

(4.2)

Note that
\delta_{t}(I)
depends on the structure of the set
I
(e.g., the conductance), as well as on
q(t)
. However, for the more coarse quantity
\delta_{t}^{[A,B]}
, we only need
A\leq|I|\leq B
. In order to separate these two factors, we also define the following deterministic quantities,

\Phi(t):=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq V\colon\\
1\leq|I|\leq n-1\end{subarray}}\frac{\delta_{t}(I)}{q(t)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Psi(t):=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq V\colon\\
1\leq|I|\leq n-1\end{subarray}}\frac{\delta_{t}(I)}{q(t)}.

(4.3)

Moreover, we define
\Phi:=\min_{t\geq 0}\Phi(t)
and
\Psi:=\max_{t\geq 0}\Psi(t)
.

Definition 4.2
.

For any subset
I\subseteq V
with
1\leq|I|\leq k\leq n-1
,

\phi_{k}:=\min_{1\leq|I|\leq k}\varphi(I).

The following corollary is a direct consequence of
Theorem

3.1
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be any sequence of regular
n
-vertex graphs and consider a
C_{\mathrm{grow}}
-growing process
\mathcal{P}
. Let
A,B
be thresholds satisfying

. Moreover, let

(4.4)

where,

. Assume that

, then there is a constant

such that

\mathbf{P}_{t^{\prime}}\left[\,|I_{t^{*}}|\geq B~\Big|~|I_{t^{\prime}}|\geq A\,\right]\geq 1-\exp\left(-C_{2}\cdot\left(\log\left(\frac{B}{A}\right)\right)^{1/3}\right).

Theorem

3.5
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be any sequence of regular
n

-shrinking process
\mathcal{P}

4.1
Arbitrary Credibility

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be any sequence of regular
n
-vertex graphs, and
q(t)
be an arbitrary credibility function. Let
T\geq 1
be a deterministic number of rounds such that for some small
\rho\in(0,1)
(not necessarily constant) such that,

\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\log\Bigl(1+\Psi(t)\cdot q(t)\Bigr)\leq\log n+\log\rho.

Then,

\mathbf{E}[\,|I_{T}|\,]\leq\rho\cdot n,

and hence by Markov’s inequality, for any
\eta>0
(not necessarily constant),

\mathbf{P}\left[\,|I_{T}|\leq\rho\cdot n^{1+\eta}\,\right]\leq n^{-\eta}.

Proof.

Observe that, for any
T\geq 1
, by the definition of
\Psi(t)
from (
4.3
) we have

\mathbf{E}_{T-1}\left[\,|\Delta_{T}|\,\right]\leq|I_{T-1}|\cdot\max_{I\subseteq V\colon 1\leq|I|\leq n-1}\frac{\mathbf{E}_{T-1}\left[\,|\Delta_{T}|\,\big|\,I_{T-1}=I\,\right]}{\min(|I_{T-1}|,|U_{T-1}|)}=|I_{T-1}|\cdot\Psi(T-1)\cdot q(T-1).

Consequently, for any
T\geq 1
, the above bound gives us

\mathbf{E}_{T-1}\left[\,|I_{T}|\,\right]=|I_{T-1}|+\mathbf{E}_{T-1}\left[\,|\Delta_{T}|\,\right]\leq|I_{T-1}|\cdot(1+\Psi(T-1)\cdot q(T-1)).

(4.6)

Now, for
T=0
, we set
Y_{T}=Y_{0}:=1
, and for
T\geq 1
we define the random variable

Y_{T}:=\frac{|I_{T}|}{\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}\left(1+\Psi(t)\cdot q(t)\right)}.

We will verify that
Y_{T}
is a super-martingale. Indeed, for any
T\geq 1
,
Eq.

4.6
gives us

\mathbf{E}_{T-1}\left[\,Y_{T}\,\right]=\frac{\mathbf{E}_{T-1}\left[\,|I_{T}|\,\right]}{\prod^{T-1}_{t=0}\left(1+\Psi(t)\cdot q(t)\right)}\leq\frac{|I_{T-1}|\cdot(1+\Psi(T-1)\cdot q(T-1))}{\prod^{T-1}_{t=0}\left(1+\Psi(t)\cdot q(t)\right)}=\frac{|I_{T-1}|}{\prod^{T-2}_{t=0}\left(1+\Psi(t)\cdot q(t)\right)}=Y_{T-1}.

Additionally, we note that
Y_{T}
is bounded, as
Y_{T}\leq|I_{T}|\leq n
. Therefore, by the optional stopping theorem (
Theorem

A.5
),

\mathbf{E}[\,Y_{T}\,]\leq Y_{0}=1.

Rearranging,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[\,|I_{T}|\,]

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}\left(1+\Psi(t)\cdot q(t)\right)=\exp\left(\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\log\bigl(1+\Psi(t)\cdot q(t)\bigr)\right)\leq\rho\cdot n,

where the last inequality follows by the precondition.
∎

Next, we state two central results lower bounding the number of informed vertices, which both hold for arbitrary credibility functions.
We start with a rather crude bound, which is simple to prove.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be any sequence of regular
n
-vertex graphs and
\kappa>0
be any constant. Consider a process
\mathcal{P}
which is both a
C_{\mathrm{grow}}
-growing process and a
C_{\mathrm{shrink}}
-shrinking process, where
C_{\mathrm{shrink}}\leq 1-n^{-\kappa}
, with an arbitrary credibility function
q(t)
. If
T
is a number of rounds satisfying,

\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\log\left(1+\delta_{t}^{[1,n-1]}\right)\geq(2/\xi+\kappa)\cdot\log n,

where
\xi:=10^{-30}
then, we have

\mathbf{P}\left[\,|I_{T}|=n\,\right]\geq 1-o(1).

Proof.

We divide the process into two phases by defining (inductively) the following two deterministic times

\displaystyle t_{1}:=

\displaystyle\min\left\{t\geq 0:\sum^{t_{1}-1}_{t=0}\log\left(1+\delta_{t}^{[1,n/2-1]}\right)\geq\frac{3\log n}{2\xi}\right\}

\displaystyle t_{2}:=

\displaystyle\min\left\{t\geq t_{1}:\sum^{t_{2}-1}_{t=t_{1}}\log\left(1-\delta_{t}^{[n/2,n-1]}\right)\leq-\frac{2\log n}{\widetilde{\gamma}}\right\},

Recall that
\xi:=10^{-30}>0
(as in
Corollary

4.3
) and
\widetilde{\gamma}=\widetilde{\gamma}(C_{\mathrm{shrink}})>1/2
is the constant from
Corollary

4.4
. Assuming that
t_{1}\leq t_{2}<\infty
, we apply
Corollary

4.3
for the first phase (rounds
[0,t_{1})
) and
Corollary

4.4
for the second phase (rounds
[t_{1},t_{2})
). By
Corollary

4.3
for
A=1,B=n/2
and
\nu_{t}^{[A,B]}=\delta_{t}^{[1,n/2-1]}
(which trivially satisfies the requirement of
Corollary

4.3
on
\nu_{t}^{[A,B]}
), we get that

\mathbf{P}\left[\,|I_{t_{1}}|<n/2\,\right]=o(1).

In the same way, by
Corollary

4.4
for
C=n/2,D=3/4
and
\nu_{t}^{[C,D]}=\delta_{t}^{[n/2,n-1]}
,

\mathbf{P}_{t_{1}}\left[\,|U_{t_{2}}|>0\,\mid\,|U_{t_{1}}|\leq n/2\,\right]=o(1).

Thus by the Union Bound,

\mathbf{P}\left[\,|I_{t_{2}}|=n\,\right]\geq 1-o(1).

To prove the theorem statement, it remains to prove that
t_{2}\leq T
.

Note that since
\mathcal{P}
is
C_{\mathrm{grow}}
-growing process, by
Definition

2.1
(
\mathcal{P}_{3}
) we have
\delta_{t}^{[1,n/2-1]}\leq C_{\mathrm{grow}}
for any
t\geq 0
. Therefore, by minimality in the definition of
t_{1}
,

\sum_{t=0}^{t_{1}-1}\log\left(1+\delta_{t}^{[1,n/2-1]}\right)\leq\frac{3\log n}{2\xi}+\log(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}})\leq\frac{3\log n}{2\xi}+\log 2\leq\frac{5\log n}{3\xi}.

(4.7)

Similarly, since
\mathcal{P}
is a
C_{\mathrm{shrink}}
-shrinking process, by
Definition

2.1
(
\widetilde{\mathcal{P}}_{3}
),
\delta_{t}^{[n/2,n-1]}\leq C_{\mathrm{shrink}}
. Moreover, as
C_{\mathrm{shrink}}\leq 1-n^{-\kappa}
by hypothesis, and as
\widetilde{\gamma}\geq 1/2
, we have

\sum^{t_{2}-1}_{t=t_{1}}\log\left(1-\delta_{t}^{[n/2,n-1]}\right)\geq-\frac{2\log n}{\widetilde{\gamma}}+\log\left(1-C_{\mathrm{shrink}}\right)\geq-4\log n-\kappa\log n=-(4+\kappa)\log n.

Since
1+z\leq\frac{1}{1-z}
for
z\in[0,1)
, we get that

\sum^{t_{2}-1}_{t=t_{1}}\log\left(1+\delta_{t}^{[n/2,n-1]}\right)\leq-\sum^{t_{2}-1}_{t=t_{1}}\log\left(1-\delta_{t}^{[n/2,n-1]}\right)\leq(4+\kappa)\log n.

(4.8)

Since
\delta_{t}^{[1,n-1]}=\min\left(\delta_{t}^{[1,n/2-1]},\delta_{t}^{[n/2,n-1]}\right)
by (
4.2
), by combining
Eq.

4.7
and
Eq.

4.8
, we obtain,

\displaystyle\sum^{t_{2}-1}_{t=0}\log\left(1+\delta_{t}^{[1,n-1]}\right)\leq\sum_{t=0}^{t_{1}-1}\log\left(1+\delta_{t}^{[1,\frac{n}{2}-1]}\right)+\sum^{t_{2}-1}_{t=t_{1}}\log\left(1+\delta_{t}^{[\frac{n}{2},n-1]}\right)\leq\left(\tfrac{5}{3\xi}+4+\kappa\right)\log n<\left(\tfrac{2}{\xi}+\kappa\right)\log n,

thus
t_{2}\leq T
since
\xi:=10^{-30}
.
∎

Theorem 4.7

Proof.

4.2
Power-Law Credibility

In this part we consider a natural credibility function with a polynomial decay.

Definition 4.8

(Power-law credibility)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
be any constant . Then, the power-law credibility function is defined for any round
t\geq 0
as

q_{\alpha}(t):=(t+1)^{-\alpha}.

In particular, in the first round the credibility function is
1
.

We first observe that if
\alpha>1
, we only inform a constant number of vertices in expectation.

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be any sequence of regular graphs, and consider a Growing Process such that
\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\,\right]\leq C_{\mathrm{grow}}\cdot q(t)
for all
t\geq 0
. Then, for any constant
\alpha>1
, there is a constant
\kappa=\kappa(\alpha)>0
, such that for any
T\geq 0
,

\mathbf{E}[\,|I_{T}|\,]\leq\kappa.

The condition
\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\,\right]\leq C_{\mathrm{grow}}\cdot q(t)
is a refinement of
\mathcal{P}_{2}
in
Definition

2.1
, and is satisfied by the
PULL
,
PUSH
,
PUSH-PULL
processes as shown in
Lemma

2.3
by choosing
C_{\mathrm{grow}}
as 1, 1, and 2, respectively.

Proof.

Recall that
|I_{t+1}|=\left(1+\frac{|\Delta_{t+1}|}{|I_{t}|}\right)\cdot|I_{t}|
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}_{t}\left[\,|I_{t+1}|\,\right]\leq(1+C_{\mathrm{grow}}\cdot q(t))\cdot|I_{t}|\leq e^{C_{\mathrm{grow}}\cdot q(t)}\cdot|I_{t}|

and recursively we have
\mathbf{E}[\,|I_{T}|\,]\leq e^{C_{\mathrm{grow}}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}q(t)}
. Since
q(t)=(t+1)^{-\alpha}
with
\alpha>1
, it holds that
\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}q(t)<\infty
. By choosing
\kappa=e^{C_{\mathrm{grow}}\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}q(t)}
we obtain the desired result.
∎

The next result considers the regime
\alpha\leq 1
, and proves that after a sufficiently long time, the rumor reaches all
n
vertices. In particular, when
\alpha=1
, the spreading time becomes polynomial in
n
(even if
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
was a sequence of expander graphs).

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be any sequence of regular
n
-vertex graphs, and consider a process
\mathcal{P}
that is both a
C_{\mathrm{grow}}
-growing process and a
C_{\mathrm{shrink}}
-shrinking process, where
C_{\mathrm{shrink}}<1
is constant, with a power law credibility function. Then, for any constant
\alpha<1
, there are constants
0<\kappa_{1}:=\kappa_{1}(\alpha)<\kappa_{2}:=\kappa_{2}(\alpha)

such that for any
T_{1}\leq\kappa_{1}\cdot(\frac{1}{\Psi}\cdot\log n)^{1/(1-\alpha)}
,
T_{2}\geq\kappa_{2}\cdot(\frac{1}{\Phi}\cdot\log n)^{1/(1-\alpha)}
and any
\eta>0
we have,

(i)

\mathbf{P}\left[\,|I_{T_{1}}|<n^{1/2+\eta}\,\right]\geq 1-n^{-\eta}
,

(ii)

\mathbf{P}\left[\,|I_{T_{2}}|=n\,\right]\geq 1-o(1)
.

Further, if
\alpha=1
, then there are constants
0<\kappa_{1}<\kappa_{2}
, such that
for any
T_{1}\leq\left(\frac{1}{\Psi}\cdot n\right)^{\kappa_{1}}
and
T_{2}\geq\left(\frac{1}{\Phi}\cdot n\right)^{\kappa_{2}}
,

(iii)

\mathbf{P}\left[\,|I_{T_{1}}|<n^{1/2+\eta}\,\right]\geq 1-n^{-\eta}
,

(iv)

\mathbf{P}\left[\,|I_{T_{2}}|=n\,\right]\geq 1-o(1)
.

Proof.

We begin with the proof of
(i)
. Observe that

\prod_{t=0}^{T_{1}-1}\left(1+\Psi(t)\cdot q(t)\right)\leq\exp\left(\Psi\cdot\sum_{t=1}^{T_{1}}t^{-\alpha}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\exp\left(\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\cdot\Psi\cdot T_{1}^{1-\alpha}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{=}}\exp\left(\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\cdot\kappa_{1}\log n\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{\leq}}\sqrt{n},

where
(a)
holds by
A.16

(b)
holds by the definition of
T_{1}
and
(c)
holds if
\kappa_{1}\leq(\frac{1-\alpha}{2})^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}
. The result then follows from
Theorem

4.5
, where we take
\rho=1/\sqrt{n}
.

Proof of
(ii)
: We seek to apply
Theorem

4.6
. Since
1+x\geq e^{x/4}
for
0\leq x\leq 1
, and by
A.16
, we have

\prod_{t=0}^{T_{2}-1}\left(1+\Phi(t)\cdot q(t)\right)\geq\exp\left(\frac{1}{4}\cdot\Phi\cdot\sum_{t=1}^{T_{2}}t^{-\alpha}\right)\geq\exp\left(\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\cdot\Phi\cdot\left(T_{2}^{1-\alpha}-1\right)\right).

Now, by the definition of
T_{2}
and if
\kappa_{2}\geq(\tfrac{16(1-\alpha)}{\Phi\xi})^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}
, where

, then we have

\prod_{t=0}^{T_{2}-1}\left(1+\Phi(t)\cdot q(t)\right)\geq\exp\left(\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\cdot\Phi\cdot\left(\tfrac{16(1-\alpha)}{\Phi\xi}\log n-1\right)\right)\geq\exp\left(\left(\frac{2}{\xi}+1\right)\cdot\log n\right),

The proofs of statements

.∎

4.3
Additive Credibility

Definition 4.11

(Additive credibility)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then, the additive credibility function is defined for any round
t\geq 0
as

q_{\alpha}(t)=q(t):=(1-t\cdot\alpha)^{+},

where
z^{+}=\max(z,0)
.
In particular, in the first round (when
t=0
) the credibility function is
1
.

In comparison to the power-law credibility function, the additive credibility function has a time-independent decrease. As we will see below, the interesting regime (for expanders) is when
\alpha=\Theta(1/\log n)
. That means, unlike the power-law-credibility, the additive credibility function remains close to
1
for a significant number of rounds. However, after
O(\log^{2}n)
steps, the credibility becomes polynomially small; much smaller than any power-law credibility at this point.

Let us consider the additive credibility function in the
PUSH
and
PULL
model for regular graphs.
We also observe that if we let
T=1/\alpha
,
I_{T}
is the maximal set of informed vertices in every execution, as
q(t)=0
for
t\geq T
. We start by proving an upper bound on
I_{T}
for
T=1/\alpha
, followed by a lower bound. We remark that, due to the specific nature of
q(t)
, we can use Stirling’s approximation to determine a rather precise threshold for the parameter
\alpha
.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be a sequence of regular
n
-vertex strong expander graphs, and consider the
PUSH
or
PULL
protocol with an additive credibility function. Let
\mathcal{P}\in\{{\rm{\texttt{PUSH}}},{\rm{\texttt{PULL}}}\}
.

(i)

Let
\alpha\geq\frac{\log\left(\frac{4}{e}\right)}{\log n+\log\zeta}
, where
\frac{1}{n}<\zeta<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\cdot 2}
. Then, for any
T:=1/\alpha
and for any
\eta>0
(not necessarily constant),

\mathbf{P}\left[\,|I_{T}|\leq\sqrt{2}\cdot\zeta\cdot n^{1+\eta}\,\right]\geq 1-n^{-\eta}.

(ii)

Further, let
\alpha\leq\frac{\log\left(\frac{4}{e}\right)}{\log\left(2\sqrt{2}\cdot\exp\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}\cdot\frac{\log n+7(\log n)^{2/3}}{\left(1-(1-\xi)\cdot(\log n)^{-\xi}\right)^{2}}\right)\right)}
, for
\gamma_{\mathcal{P}}
as in
Theorem

4.7
. Then, for
T:=1/\alpha
,

\mathbf{P}\left[\,|I_{T}|\geq n\cdot\left(1-\exp(-\sqrt{\log n})\right)\,\right]\geq 1-o(1).

Proof.

We begin with Item
(i)
, where we seek to apply
Theorem

4.5
. We note that by
Lemma

2.3
(
i
) and
Lemma

2.3
(
ii
), for both
PUSH
and
PULL
,
\delta_{t}\leq q(t)\cdot\varphi(I_{t})
. Thus, for
PUSH
and
PULL

\Psi(t):=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq V\colon\\
1\leq|I|\leq n-1\end{subarray}}\frac{\delta_{t}(I)}{q(t)}\leq\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq V\colon\\
1\leq|I|\leq n-1\end{subarray}}\frac{q(t)\cdot\varphi(I)}{q(t)}\leq 1.

(4.22)

Hence, by using
Eq.

4.22
in
(a)
,
A.18
in
(b)
, and the definition of
\alpha
in
(c)
, we have

\displaystyle\prod^{T-1}_{t=0}\left(1+\Psi(t)\cdot q(t)\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\prod^{T-1}_{t=0}(1+q(t))\leq\prod^{1/\alpha-1}_{t=0}(1+(1-t\cdot\alpha))=\prod_{t=0}^{1/\alpha-1}\left(2-t\cdot\alpha\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\leq}}\sqrt{2}\cdot\left(\frac{4}{e}\right)^{1/\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{\leq}}\sqrt{2}\cdot\zeta n.

Taking logarithms yields,

\sum^{T-1}_{t=0}\log(1+\Psi(t)\cdot q(t))\leq\log\left(\sqrt{2}\cdot\zeta\cdot n\right).

By
Theorem

4.5
,
we obtain

\mathbf{E}\left[\,|I_{T}|\,\right]\leq\sqrt{2}\cdot\zeta\cdot n
as well as the tail bound by Markov’s inequality.

Proof of Item
(ii)
: For the second statement, we seek to apply
Theorem

4.7
. By a simple coupling argument, we may assume that
\alpha=\log\left(\frac{4}{e}\right)/\log\left(2\sqrt{2}\cdot\exp\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}\cdot\frac{\log n+7(\log n)^{2/3}}{\left(1-(1-\xi)\cdot(\log n)^{-\xi}\right)^{2}}\right)\right)
, since by making
\alpha
smaller,

increases and therefore only more vertices get informed. Recall that as in

\varepsilon:=1-\max_{t\geq\frac{1}{2\log(2)}\cdot\log(n)}q(t).

To satisfy the precondition on

of

, we need that
\varepsilon\geq\frac{1}{\log n}
for all

. By definition of the additive credibility function (and the fact it is non-increasing in

), it holds for any

\varepsilon=1-q\left(\frac{1}{2\log 2}\cdot\log n\right)\geq 1-(1-\frac{1}{2\log 2}\cdot\log n\cdot\alpha).

Since the choice of
\alpha
implies that
\alpha\geq\frac{\log(4/e)}{\frac{1}{2}\cdot\log n}
, it follows that

\varepsilon\geq\frac{\log(4/e)}{4\log 2}>\frac{1}{10}.

Therefore the precondition on

of

holds. Furthermore, by using

in
(a)
,

\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}(1+q(t))=\prod^{1/\alpha-1}_{t=0}(1+q(t))=\prod^{1/\alpha-1}_{t=0}\left(2-t\cdot\alpha\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\geq}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{4}{e}\right)^{1/\alpha}\cdot e^{-1/2\cdot\alpha}

Now, using in
(b)
that
e^{-1/2\cdot\alpha}\geq\frac{1}{2}
for our choice of
\alpha=O(\frac{1}{\log n})

(c)
again our specific choice of

\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}(1+q(t))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(b)}}{{\geq}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{4}{e}\right)^{1/\alpha}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(c)}}{{\geq}}\exp\left(\frac{1}{\gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}\cdot\frac{\log n+7\left(\log n\right)^{2/3}}{\left(1-(1-\xi)\cdot(\log)^{-\xi}\right)^{2}}\right),

Taking logarithms yields,

\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\log(1+q(t))\geq\frac{1}{\gamma_{\mathcal{P}}}\cdot\frac{\log n+7\left(\log n\right)^{2/3}}{\left(1-(1-\xi)\cdot(\log)^{-\xi}\right)^{2}},

and hence also the precondition

holds. Therefore, statement

follows by

4.7
.
∎

4.4
Multiplicative Credibility

Definition 4.13

(Multiplicative credibility)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then, the multiplicative credibility function is defined for any round
t\geq 0
as

q_{\alpha}(t):=(1-\alpha)^{t}.

In particular, in the first round the credibility function is
1
.

The next result is the multiplicative analogue of
Theorem

4.12
.

Theorem 4.14
.

Let
(G_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be any sequence of regular
n
-vertex strong expander graphs, and consider the
PUSH
or
PULL
protocol with a multiplicative credibility function . Then, there are constants
\kappa_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
\kappa_{2}\geq\frac{1}{8}

,

, and hence for any

\mathbf{P}\left[\,|I_{T}|\leq n^{1/2+\eta}\,\right]\geq 1-n^{-\eta}.

(ii)

Further, if
\alpha\leq\frac{\kappa_{2}}{\log n}

\mathbf{P}\left[\,|I_{T}|\geq n\cdot\left(1-\exp(-(\log n)^{1/2})\right)\,\right]\geq 1-o(1).

We believe that with a more refined analysis it would be also possible to show that

\Psi(t):=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq V\colon\\

Proof of
(i)
: Moreover, we set

5
Conclusions

In this work, we presented a general framework for analyzing spreading processes with a time-dependent credibility function. The key idea is to link the spreading progress to an aggregate sum of growth (or shrinking) factors over consecutive rounds. In that way, our approach generalizes various previous works that were based on estimating the worst-case growth across all sets via the conductance of the graph. We also obtained several dichotomy results in terms of the number of vertices that get informed, both for general and more concrete credibility functions (see
Section

4
).

In terms of open problems, a natural direction is to generalize our main technical results from regular graphs to arbitrary graphs, which we believe to be doable. Another avenue for future research is to allow more complex interactions between the credibility function
q(t)
and the evolving set of informed vertices
I_{t}
, which could more accurately model an external influence on the network (e.g., fact-checkers). Lastly, one could consider more general spreading processes including other epidemic models (e.g., SIR model or independent cascade model), majority dynamics or variants of the voter model, in which informed vertices may also become uninformed in future steps.

Auxiliary Probabilistic Tools

For convenience, we add several well known claims.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
Y
be any random variable, and
Y_{1},Y_{2}
be two independent copies with the same distribution as
Y
. Then,

\mathbf{Var}\left[\,Y\,\right]=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\mathbf{E}\left[\,(Y_{1}-Y_{2})^{2}\,\right].

Proof.

If
Y_{1},Y_{2}\sim Y
are independent samples from the same distribution
Y
, then

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}\left[\,(Y_{1}-Y_{2})^{2}\,\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\left[\,(Y_{1}-\mathbf{E}\left[\,Y\,\right]+\mathbf{E}\left[\,Y\,\right]-Y_{2})^{2}\,\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\left[\,(Y_{1}-\mathbf{E}\left[\,Y\,\right])^{2}\,\right]+2\cdot\mathbf{E}\left[\,(Y_{1}-\mathbf{E}\left[\,Y\,\right])\cdot(\mathbf{E}\left[\,Y\,\right]-Y_{2})\,\right]+\mathbf{E}\left[\,(Y_{2}-\mathbf{E}\left[\,Y\,\right])^{2}\,\right]

\displaystyle=2\cdot\mathbf{Var}\left[\,Y\,\right].\qed

Lemma A.2
.

Let
Z
be a non-negative random variable. Then,

\mathbf{Var}\left[\,\log(1+Z)\,\right]\leq\mathbf{Var}\left[\,Z\,\right].

Proof.

Let
Y
be any random variable, and
Y_{1},Y_{2}
be two independent copies with the same distribution as
Y
. Then, by
Lemma

A.1
,
2\cdot\mathbf{Var}\left[\,Y\,\right]=\mathbf{E}\left[\,(Y_{1}-Y_{2})^{2}\,\right]
. If
x,y\geq 0
then
|\log(1+x)-\log(1+y)|\leq|x-y|
. To see the previous inequality, assuming that
x>y
, the mean value theorem yields
\log(1+x)-\log(1+y)=\frac{1}{1+z}(x-y)
, where
z
lies between

and

, then clearly

, so

, proving the inequality. Now

\displaystyle 2\cdot\mathbf{Var}\left[\,\log(1+Z)\,\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{E}\left[\,\left(\log(1+Z_{1})-(\log(1+Z_{2})\right)^{2}\,\right]\leq\mathbf{E}\left[\,\left(Z_{1}-Z_{2}\right)^{2}\,\right]=2\cdot\mathbf{Var}\left[\,Z\,\right].\qed

Lemma A.3
.

Let
Z
be a non-negative random variable . Then, for any

,

\mathbf{Var}\left[\,\max\{Z,a\}\,\right]\leq\mathbf{Var}\left[\,Z\,\right],\qquad\text{as well as}\qquad\mathbf{Var}\left[\,\min(Z,a)\,\right]\leq\mathbf{Var}\left[\,Z\,\right].

This is identical to the proof of

.

If

\mathbf{E}\left[\,f(X)\,\right]\leq f\left(\mathbf{E}\left[\,X\,\right]\right).

.

Claim A.13
.

For all
x\geq 1
and
\kappa\in(0,1/1000)
we have,

\min\left(64\cdot x^{-1/4},1-\kappa\right)\leq(1-\kappa^{2})\cdot x^{-\kappa^{2}}.

Proof.

Observe that for any
x<\left(\frac{64}{1-\kappa}\right)^{4}
we have
1-\kappa<64\cdot x^{-1/4}
, and

(1-\kappa^{2})\cdot x^{-\frac{\log(1+\kappa)}{4\log(\frac{64}{1-\kappa})}}>(1-\kappa^{2})\cdot\left(\left(\frac{64}{1-\kappa}\right)^{4}\right)^{-\frac{\log(1+\kappa)}{4\log(\frac{64}{1-\kappa})}}=(1-\kappa^{2})\cdot\frac{1}{1+\kappa}=1-\kappa.

Conversely, for any
x\geq\left(\frac{64}{1-\kappa}\right)^{4}
we have
64\cdot x^{-1/4}\leq 1-\kappa
, and so we clam that also

64\cdot x^{-1/4}\leq(1-\kappa^{2})\cdot x^{-\frac{\log(1+\kappa)}{4\log(\frac{64}{1-\kappa})}}.

To see this, note that any such
x
can be expressed as
c\cdot\left(\frac{64}{1-\kappa}\right)^{4}
for some
c\geq 1
. After this change of variables the inequality above reads

(1-\kappa)\cdot c^{-1/4}\leq(1-\kappa)\cdot c^{-\frac{\log(1+\kappa)}{4\log(\frac{64}{1-\kappa})}},

which holds since
1/4\geq\frac{\log(1+\kappa)}{4\log(\frac{64}{1-\kappa})}
. The result follows by using the inequalities
\frac{x}{1+x}\leq\log(1+x)\leq x
since

\frac{\log(1+\kappa)}{4\log(\frac{64}{1-\kappa})}\geq\frac{\frac{\kappa}{1+\kappa}}{\frac{4\cdot 64}{1-\kappa}}\geq\kappa^{2},

as
\kappa\in(0,1/1000)
.
∎

Claim A.14
.

For any
z\geq 0
and any
a\in[0,1]
, the following holds:

\log\left(1+(1-a)\cdot z\right)\geq(1-a)\cdot\log\left(1+z\right).

Proof.

To begin observe that if
z=0
then the statement holds, for any
a\in[0,1]
. So from now on we can assume
z>0
. For any fixed
z>0
, we consider the function

f_{z}(a):=\log\left(1+(1-a)\cdot z\right)-(1-a)\cdot\log\left(1+z\right).

Note that for
a=0
, we have

f_{z}(0)=\log(1+z)-\log(1+z)=0,

and for
a=1
, we have

f_{z}(1)=\log(1)-0\cdot\log(1+z)=0.

Taking the derivatives,

\frac{\partial f_{z}(a)}{\partial a}=\frac{z}{(a-1)z-1}+\log(z+1),

and

\frac{\partial^{2}f_{z}(a)}{\partial^{2}a}=\frac{-z^{2}}{((a-1)z-1)^{2}}.

Thus we see that
\frac{\partial^{2}f_{z}(a)}{\partial a}<0
always, since
z>0
. Therefore, all stationary points must be local maxima and so
f_{z}(a)\geq 0
for all
a\in[0,1]
, which yields the claim.
∎

Claim A.15
.

For any
x<1
and
a<1
,

\log\left(1-(1-a)\cdot x\right)\leq\left(1-\frac{a}{1-x}\right)\cdot\log\left(1-x\right).

Proof.

The derivative of
f(x)=\log(x)
at point
1-x
is
\frac{1}{1-x}
. Therefore, as
\log(.)
is concave,

\log\left(1-x+a\cdot x\right)\leq\log\left(1-x\right)+\frac{a\cdot x}{1-x}.

Further, since
\log(1-x)\leq-x
, we have
x\leq-\log(1-x)
and thus

\log\left(1-x+a\cdot x\right)\leq\log(1-x)\cdot\left(1-\frac{a}{1-x}\right).\qed

Claim A.16
.

For any
0<\alpha<1
and
T\geq 1
,

\frac{T^{1-\alpha}-1}{1-\alpha}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{T}k^{-\alpha}\leq\frac{T^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}.

Proof.

Since the function
x^{1-\alpha}
is decreasing on
[1,\infty)
and
0<1-\alpha<1
we have

\sum_{k=1}^{T}k^{-\alpha}\leq 1+\int_{1}^{T}x^{-\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}x=1+\left[\frac{x^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\right]_{x=1}^{T}=1+\frac{T^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}-\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\leq\frac{T^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}.

The lower bound follows similarly as
\sum_{k=1}^{T}k^{-\alpha}\geq\int_{1}^{T}x^{-\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}x
.
∎

Lemma A.17

(Stirling’s approximation upper and lower bound)
.

For every integer
n\geq 1
,

\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n}e^{\frac{1}{12n+1}}<n!<\sqrt{2\pi n}\left(\frac{n}{e}\right)^{n}e^{\frac{1}{12n}}.

Claim A.18
.

For any
\alpha>0
,

\prod_{i=0}^{1/\alpha-1}(2-i\cdot\alpha)\leq\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{4}{e}\right)^{1/\alpha}.

Proof.

We have

\prod^{1/\alpha}_{i=1}(2-(i-1)\cdot\alpha)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{1/\alpha}(2/\alpha-(i-1))}{(1/\alpha)^{1/\alpha}}=\frac{(2/\alpha)!}{(1/\alpha)!(1/\alpha)^{1/\alpha}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{\leq}}\frac{\sqrt{2\pi\cdot 2/\alpha}\left(\frac{2/\alpha}{e}\right)^{2/\alpha}e^{\frac{1}{12\cdot 2/\alpha}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\cdot 1/\alpha}\left(\frac{1/\alpha}{e}\right)^{1/\alpha}e^{\frac{1}{12\cdot 1/\alpha+1}}(1/\alpha)^{1/\alpha}},

where
(a)
holds by applying the upper bound in
Lemma

A.17
to the factorial in the numerator and the lower bound in
Lemma

A.17
to the factorial in the denominator. With some factoring and cancelling we now have

\prod^{1/\alpha}_{i=1}(2-(i-1)\cdot\alpha)\leq\frac{\sqrt{2/\alpha}\cdot 2^{2/\alpha}\left(1/\alpha\right)^{2/\alpha}\left(1/e\right)^{2/\alpha}e^{\frac{1}{24/\alpha}}}{\sqrt{1/\alpha}\cdot(1/\alpha)^{1/\alpha}(1/\alpha)^{1/\alpha}\left(1/e\right)^{1/\alpha}e^{\frac{1}{12/\alpha+1}}}=\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{4}{e}\right)^{1/\alpha}\cdot\frac{e^{1/(24/\alpha)}}{e^{1/(12/\alpha)+1}}\leq\sqrt{2}\left(\frac{4}{e}\right)^{1/\alpha},

as claimed.
∎

Claim A.19
.

For any
\alpha>0
,

\prod^{1/\alpha-1}_{i=0}(2-i\cdot\alpha)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{4}{e}\right)^{1/\alpha}\cdot e^{-\frac{1}{2}\alpha}.

Proof.

Shifting the indices by one gives us

\prod^{1/\alpha-1}_{i=0}(2-i\cdot\alpha)=\prod^{1/\alpha}_{i=1}(2-i\cdot\alpha)=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{1/\alpha}(2/\alpha-i)}{(1/\alpha)^{1/\alpha}}=\frac{(2/\alpha-1)!}{(1/\alpha-1)!(1/\alpha)^{1/\alpha}}=\frac{(2/\alpha)!(1/\alpha)}{(1/\alpha)!(2/\alpha)(1/\alpha)^{1/\alpha}}.

By applying the Stirling’s inequalities
Lemma

A.17
to the factorials in the numerator denominator we have

\prod^{1/\alpha-1}_{i=0}(2-i\cdot\alpha)\geq\frac{\sqrt{2\pi\cdot 2/\alpha}\left(\frac{2/\alpha}{e}\right)^{2/\alpha}e^{\frac{1}{12\cdot 2/\alpha+1}}(1/\alpha)}{\sqrt{2\pi\cdot 1/\alpha}\left(\frac{1/\alpha}{e}\right)^{1/\alpha}e^{\frac{1}{12\cdot 1/\alpha}}(1/\alpha)^{1/\alpha}(2/\alpha)}=\frac{\sqrt{2/\alpha}(1/\alpha)2^{2/\alpha}\left(1/\alpha\right)^{2/\alpha}\left(1/e\right)^{2/\alpha}e^{\frac{1}{24/\alpha+1}}}{\sqrt{1/\alpha}(2/\alpha)(1/\alpha)^{1/\alpha}(1/\alpha)^{1/\alpha}\left(1/e\right)^{1/\alpha}e^{\frac{1}{12/\alpha}}}.

\displaystyle\prod^{1/\alpha-1}_{i=0}(2-i\cdot\alpha)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{4}{e}\right)^{1/\alpha}\cdot e^{\frac{1}{24/\alpha+1}-\frac{1}{12/\alpha}}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{4}{e}\right)^{1/\alpha}\cdot e^{\frac{1}{25}\alpha-\frac{1}{12}\alpha}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\frac{4}{e}\right)^{1/\alpha}\cdot e^{-\frac{1}{2}\alpha},

as claimed.
∎

Claim A.20
.

For the constants
c_{1}:=\frac{1}{2}
and
c_{2}:=\frac{1}{8}
we have,

\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(1+\left(1-\frac{c_{1}}{\log n}\right)^{i}\right)\leq\sqrt{n},\qquad and\qquad\prod_{i=0}^{4\log n-1}\left(1+\left(1-\frac{c_{2}}{\log n}\right)^{i}\right)\geq n^{3/2}.

Proof.

For the first statement,

\prod_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(1+\left(1-\frac{c_{1}}{\log n}\right)^{i}\right)\leq\exp\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{c_{1}}{\log n}\right)^{i}\right)\leq\exp\left(\frac{\log n}{c_{1}}\right)=\sqrt{n},

since
c_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
. For the second statement, recall
c_{2}:=\frac{1}{8}
, and note that for any
i\leq 4\log n
,

1+\left(1-\frac{c_{2}}{\log n}\right)^{i}\geq 2-4c_{2}\geq\frac{3}{2},

where we have used Bernoulli’s inequality. Therefore,

\prod_{i=0}^{4\log n-1}\left(1+\left(1-\frac{c_{2}}{\log n}\right)^{i}\right)\geq\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{4\log n-1}\geq n^{3/2},

as
(3/2)^{4}>e^{3/2}
.
∎

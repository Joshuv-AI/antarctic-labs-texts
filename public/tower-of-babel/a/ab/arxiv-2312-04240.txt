General teleportation channel in Fermionic Quantum Theory Preprint: APS/123-QED
Sanam Khan, R. Jehadeesan, Sibasish Ghosh

Abstract
Quantum Teleportation is a very useful scheme for transferring quantum information. Given that the quantum information is encoded in a state of a system of distinguishable particles, and given that the shared bi-partite entangled state is also that of a system of distinguishable particles, the optimal teleportation fidelity of the shared state is known to be ( F m ​ a ​ x ​ d + 1 ) / ( d + 1 ) (F_{max}d+1)/(d+1) with F m ​ a ​ x F_{max} being the ‘maximal singlet fraction’ of the shared state. In the present work, we address the question of optimal teleportation fidelity given that the quantum information to be teleported is encoded in Fermionic modes while a 2 ​ N 2N -mode state of a system of Fermions (with maximum 2 ​ N 2N no. of Fermions – in the second quantization language) is shared between the sender and receiver with each party possessing N N modes of the 2 ​ N 2N -mode state. Parity Superselection Rule (PSSR) in Fermionic Quantum Theory (FQT) puts constraint on the allowed set of physical states and operations, and thereby, leads to a different notion of Quantum Teleportation. Due to PSSR, we introduce restricted Clifford twirl operations that constitute the Unitary 2-design in case of FQT, and show that the structure of the canonical form of Fermionic invariant shared state differs from that of the isotropic state – the corresponding canonical invariant form for teleportation in Standard Quantum Theory (SQT). We provide a lower bound on the optimal teleportation fidelity in FQT and compare the result with teleportation in SQT. Surprisingly, we find that, under separable measurements on a bipartite Fermionic state, input and output states of the Fermionic teleportation channel cannot be distinguished operationally, even if a particular kind of resource state with ‘maximal singlet fraction’ being less than unity is used.



I
Introduction

Quantum Teleportation (QT) is a quantum information transmission task, first introduced by Bennett et al.
Bennett et al. 1993
where the sender and the receiver utilizes a pre-shared EPR-correlated pairs
Einstein et al. 1935
of particles. The first experimental demonstration of quantum teleportation was due to Bouwmeester et. al.
Boumeester et al. 1997
where the resource state used is entangled photon generated using parametric down conversion. Apart from sending unknown quantum state of a particle, QT also find its application in quantum computation. Gottesman and Chuang, in their seminal paper
Gottesman and Chuang 1999
, showed the essence of QT towards achieving fault-tolerant quantum computing. Also, the success probability of quantum gate operations in the linear optics quantum computation (LOQC) setup can be increased using non-deterministically prepared entangled states and quantum teleportation
Knill et al. 2001
. Recent advancement in QT
Langenfeld et al. 2021
also finds it’s application towards quantum networks
Avis et al. 2023
.

The question of finding optimal QT fidelity was first answered by Horodecki et al.
Horodecki et al. 1999
where the authors have shown that the optimal teleportation fidelity is related to the maximal singlet fraction of the resource state (generally, noisy) shared between sender and receiver. The authors utilized local twirling operations to reduce any bi-partite state to the form of isotropic state which is also physically isomorphic to a depolarizing channel in SQT – the corresponding reduced channel under local twirling operations on the channel operation. Such optimal teleportation has recently been used for benchmarking the performance of NISQ devices
Bharti et al. 2022
.

In case, information, encoded in a system of indistinguishable particles, needs to be teleported efficiently, one generally needs to consider an ‘entangled’ state of a bi-partite system consisting of a system of indistinguishable particles (see, for example, ref.
Lohmann and Karl 2019
). Entanglement of indistinguishable particles has been at the centre of debate
Benatti et al. 2020
since last two decades. Based on the definition of subsytem accessible to local observers, two different perspectives have been put forward –
particle local
and
mode local
. In
particle local
picture, it was pointed out that due to the symmetrized and antisymmetrized structures of the respective Hilbert spaces, the von-neumann entropy of the reduced density matrix obtained through partial trace, behaves differently for Bosons and Fermions
Ghirardi and Marinatto 2004
. However, this problem can be overcome in the
C^{\ast}
-algebraic formalism by substituting the partial trace operation with the restrictions of state to the corresponding subalgebras, and applying GNS construction
Balachandran et al. 2013
. Analysis of mode entanglement utilizes the second quantization method, and hence, the total number of particles need not be fixed. In mode local picture, the entanglement is defined relative to a given partioning of subsystems. This can be demonstrated using Bogoliubov transformation
Zanardi 2002
. Such mode local picture has also found it’s application to quantify the quantum mutual information between orbitals in quantum chemistry
Ding et al. 2021
.

Fermionic entanglement has been extensively studied both in particle picture
Ghirardi and Marinatto 2004
;
Schliemann et al. 2001
;
Li et al. 2001
;
Iemini and Vianna 2013
as well as mode picture
Moriya 2002
;
Bañuls et al. 2009
;
Friis et al. 2013
;
Benatti et al. 2014
. In mode picture – which, we are going to use in the present work – the set of allowed fermionic states and operations must adhere to the Parity Superselection Rule (PSSR)
Friis 2016
;
Vidal et al. 2021
;
D’Ariano et al. 2014
. Fermionic entanglement as a resource has been utilised in various quantum information processing tasks such as fermionic teleportation, superdense coding, thermodynamic processes and quantum metrology
Debarba et al. 2020
;
Gigena and Rossignoli 2017
;
Ptaszyński and Esposito 2023
;
Benatti et al. 2014
. Experimental demonstration of fermionic teleportation has become a reality using Solid-state system
Steffen et al. 2013
;
Qiao et al. 2020
. Recently graphene nanoribbons has been identfied as a quantum channel for teleportating Werner states with average teleportation fidelity more than
80\%

Tan et al. 2023
. Apart from quantum informational tasks, Fermionic Linear Optics(FLO) has been shown to be advantageous over classical simulation for quantum computation when initialized with proper input states
Oszmaniec et al. 2022
.

In this work, we attempt to answer the question of finding out the ‘optimal teleportation fidelity’ in FQT using the help of mode local picture and wedge product formalism
Vidal et al. 2021
. We follow the similar approach as that of finding the optimal teleportation fidelity in SQT
Horodecki et al. 1999
. However, we adhere to the operational notion of teleportation where one of the two
N
-modes subsystems of an
N\times N
-mode arbitrary bi-partite fermionic state is being teleported, and the fidelity of this teleportation is measured operationally. In this regard, we introduce the notion of ‘separable fidelity’ (to be defined later) for fermionic system. The main results of this paper include the structure of shared fermionic state under PSSR-respected local twirling operations and lower bound on the separable fidelity – considering the shared fermionic state (
N\times N
-mode) as a resource. We also compare this fidelity bound with the well-known optimal teleportation fidelity in SQT with the help of an example of a bi-partite state.

The rest of the paper is structured as follows. In Sec.
II
, we introduce the mathematical structure of fermionic Hibert space and the associated Fock space. We discuss the importance of PSSR in FQT, and the restrictions on states and operations.We also discuss the operational notion of Entanglement in FQT. In Sec.
III
, we prove the exact teleportation scheme for an
N\times N
-mode resource state. In Sec.
IV
, we derive the structure of the ‘reduced’ fermionic state under local twirling operation by introducing a Haar measure over a PSSR-respected Unitary group. In Sec.
V
, we construct an Unitary-2 design for this restricted Haar measure using PSSR-respected Clifford group operations. We prove the fermionic State-Channel Isomorphism in Sec.
VI
and introduce the notion of fermionic channel twirling operation. Finally, in Sec.
VII
, we define the average separable fidelity for fermionic channel and derive a lower bound for optimal fermionic teleportation scheme.

II.1
Fermionic Hilbert Space

For an
N
-mode fermionic system, consider
f_{j}

\{f_{i},f_{j}\}=0\ \ \ \{f_{i}^{\dagger},f_{j}^{\dagger}\}=0\ \ \ \{f_{i},f_{j}^{\dagger}\}=\delta_{ij}\mathbb{I}

In this work, we will utilize wedge-product Hilbert space instead of tensor-product Hilbert space for a bipartite system. Details about the relevant properties of wedge-product Hilbert spaces can be found in
Vidal et al. 2021

\mathcal{H_{PQ}}
denotes the wedge-product Hilbert space of
\mathcal{H_{P}}
and
\mathcal{H_{Q}}

\mathcal{H_{PQ}}=\mathcal{H_{P}}\wedge\mathcal{H_{Q}}

Consider the Hilbert space

of an
N
-mode fermionic system A with each mode spanning a 2-dimensional subspace. Let us assume that the basis for each of these subspaces be given by
\{|\Omega\rangle,|j\rangle\}

|j\rangle=f_{j}^{\dagger}|\Omega\rangle

and
|\Omega\rangle
being vacuum state (for

\{|\Omega\rangle,\ |i\rangle,\ |j\rangle,\ |i\rangle\wedge|j\rangle\}

correspond to the Hilbert spaces of
i
th and
j
th mode respectively along with the constraint

.

Thus, the global Hilbert space of an
N
-mode fermionic system is given by

\mathcal{H_{A}}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\wedge\mathcal{H}_{2}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathcal{H}_{N}

i.e. the wedge-product of all single mode subspaces. One can define a basis for
\mathcal{H_{A}}

Vidal et al. 2021
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B_{A}}=\{|j_{1}\rangle\wedge|j_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\wedge\dots\wedge|j_{k}\rangle

(1)

\displaystyle\ |\ j_{1}<j_{2}<\dots<j_{k},\ k\in[0,N]\}.

Each

in Eq. (
1
) is associated with a particular mode. Notice,

gives us the state
|\Omega{\rangle}
. In terms of fermionic creation operators, the aforesaid basis elements can also be written as

|j_{1}\rangle\wedge|j_{2}\rangle\wedge\dots\wedge|j_{k}\rangle=f_{j_{1}}^{\dagger}f_{j_{2}}^{\dagger}\dots f_{j_{k}}^{\dagger}|\Omega\rangle.

Dimension of this
N
-mode fermionic Hilbert space

is
2^{N}
.

As per Pauli exclusion principle, each mode can be occupied by maximum one fermion. Basis elements generated by even number of fermions are denoted by

while elements generated by odd number of fermions are denoted by
|O_{i}\rangle
with
i
ranging from
0
to
2^{N-1}
. In this new notation, the basis
\mathcal{B_{A}}
can be rewritten as

\mathcal{B_{A}^{P}}=\{|E_{1}\rangle,|E_{2}\rangle,\dots,|E_{2^{N-1}}\rangle,|O_{1}\rangle,|O_{2}\rangle,\dots,|O_{2^{N-1}}\rangle\}

(2)

Here,

, i.e. the ground state.

II.2
Parity superselection rule and physical states

Parity superselection rule (PSSR) imposes constraint on the form of a physical fermionic state. It states that, coherent superposition of fermionic state with even number of fermions and fermionic state with odd number of fermions is prohibited. For example, states
f_{i}^{\dagger}|\Omega\rangle
+
|\Omega\rangle
is not allowed due to having superposition of odd and even number of particles respectively. PSSR for spinor field was initially discussed in the context of special relativity
Wick et al. 1952
. Recently, PSSR has been imposed on the states of fermionic systems to remove the problem of non-symmetric bipartitions in terms of eigenvalues
Friis 2016
using the spin-statistics connection. Howerver it was argued that the necessity of imposing PSSR is profoundly related to the no-signalling principle
Johansson
.

PSSR has also been
derived
in the framework of operational probabilistic theories for fermionic quantum systems following a set of assumptions
D’Ariano et al. 2014
.

Now consider a mixed state
\rho
of an
N
-mode fermionic system. Due to PSSR, the most general form of
\rho
in the basis
\mathcal{B_{A}^{P}}
is given by

\rho=\sum_{i=1,j=1}^{i=2^{N-1},j=2^{N-1}}A_{ij}|E_{i}\rangle\langle E_{j}|+B_{ij}|O_{i}\rangle\langle O_{j}|

(3)

with suitable on A, B so that
\rho
satisfies positivity and the trace condition
{\rm Tr}{\rho}=1
.

To describe a physical state of a

-mode bipartite fermionic system, where
N
corresponds to local fermionic modes of subsystem A and

corresponds to local fermionic modes of subsystem B, we extend the Basis
\mathcal{B_{A}^{P}}
over the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H_{A}}\wedge\mathcal{H_{B}}
. Accordingly, the extended basis
\mathcal{B_{AB}^{P}}
will have the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{B_{AB}^{P}}=

\displaystyle\bigg\{|E_{i}^{A}\rangle\wedge|E_{j}^{B}\rangle,|E_{i}^{A}\rangle\wedge|O_{j}^{B}\rangle,|O_{i}^{A}\rangle\wedge|E_{j}^{B}\rangle,

(4)

\displaystyle|O_{i}^{A}\rangle\wedge|O_{j}^{B}\rangle\ \bigg|\ |E_{i(j)}^{A(B)}\rangle,|O_{i(j)}^{A(B)}\rangle\in\mathcal{B_{A(B)}^{P}}\bigg\}

.

with
i
ranging from
0
to
2^{N-1}
and
j
ranging from
0
to
2^{M-1}
. Using this extended basis, one can then express a generic PSSR-respected density matrix

as

\displaystyle W=

\displaystyle\sum_{ijkl}a_{ijkl}|E_{i}\rangle\langle E_{k}|\wedge|E_{j}\rangle\langle E_{l}|+b_{ijkl}|E_{i}\rangle\langle O_{k}|\wedge|E_{j}\rangle\langle O_{l}|+c_{ijkl}|O_{i}\rangle\langle E_{k}|\wedge|O_{j}\rangle\langle E_{k}|+d_{ijkl}|O_{i}\rangle\langle O_{k}|\wedge|O_{j}\rangle\langle O_{l}|

(5)

\displaystyle+A_{ijkl}|E_{i}\rangle\langle E_{k}|\wedge|O_{j}\rangle\langle O_{l}|+B_{ijkl}|E_{i}\rangle\langle O_{k}|\wedge|O_{j}\rangle\langle E_{l}|+C_{ijkl}|O_{i}\rangle\langle E_{k}|\wedge|E_{j}\rangle\langle O_{l}|+D_{ijkl}|O_{i}\rangle\langle O_{k}|\wedge|E_{j}\rangle\langle E_{l}|

with suitable conditions on the coefficients

and

and

II.3
Operations on fermionic states

As described in
Vidal et al. 2021
, the most generic PSSR-respected unitary operation assumes block-diagonal form
U_{BD}
in the basis
B^{\prime}=\{B_{e},B_{o}\}
due to no-signalling principle where,
B_{e}
(
B_{o}
) is the basis of even-parity (odd-parity) states of the fermionic system. However, one can always embed any anti-block diagonal unitary
U_{ABD}
into the form of a block-diagonal unitary using an ancilla system
Debarba et al. 2020
. We explicitly prove this fact in the following lemma for dimension
2^{N}
.

Lemma 1.
Any anti-block diagonal unitary in the basis
\mathcal{B_{A}^{P}}\equiv\{B_{e},B_{o}\}
can always be embedded into the form of a block-diagonal unitary using a single ancilla system.

Proof.
Consider a single mode fermionic ancilla system
C
, with creation and annihilation operators described by
f_{c}^{\dagger}
and
f_{c}
respectively. Hence the basis can be written as
\{|\Omega_{c}\rangle,f_{c}^{\dagger}|\Omega_{c}\rangle\}\equiv\{|E_{c}\rangle,|O_{c}\rangle\}
. Let the structure of
U_{BD}
and
U_{ABD}
in the basis
\mathcal{B_{A}^{P}}
be given by

\displaystyle U_{BD}=

\displaystyle\sum_{ij}u_{ij}|E_{i}\rangle\langle E_{j}|+v_{ij}|O_{i}\rangle\langle O_{j}|

(6)

\displaystyle U_{ABD}=

\displaystyle\sum_{ij}u^{\prime}_{ij}|E_{i}\rangle\langle O_{j}|+v^{\prime}_{ij}|O_{i}\rangle\langle E_{j}|

(7)

Consider the following unitary
U_{c}=(f_{c}^{\dagger}+f_{c})
on the ancillary mode. Then, any unitary of the form
U_{C}\wedge U_{ABD}
will have the form of
U_{BD}
.
\blacksquare

Fermionic Observables

In general, any observable for system

II.5
Entanglement in indistinguishable fermions

Entanglement for the system of indistinguishable particles has been a topic of debate since the last decade

Definition 1.
A fermionic state
\rho^{AB}
of a bipartite system
\mathcal{AB}
is said be a product state, if for any PSSR-respected observable
\hat{O}_{P}^{A}
and
\hat{O}_{P}^{B}

Tr(\rho^{AB}(\hat{O}_{P}^{A}\wedge\hat{O}_{P}^{B}))=Tr(\rho^{A}\hat{O}_{P}^{A})\ Tr(\rho^{B}\hat{O}_{P}^{B})

(9)

satisfied, where
\rho^{A(B)}
is a legitimate PSSR-respected density matrix for system
A(B)
. Notice that, in general, for any operational product state
{\rho}^{AB}
, we have

due to the absence of local discriminability
D’Ariano et al. 2014
;
D’Ariano et al. 2014
. However, the following is always true for any local PSSR-respected product state.

Lemma 2.
For any local PSSR-respected fermionic state
\rho^{AB}
of a bipartite system,

\displaystyle Tr(\rho^{AB}(\hat{O}_{P}^{A}\wedge\hat{O}_{P}^{B}))=

\displaystyle Tr(\rho^{A}\hat{O}_{P}^{A})\ Tr(\rho^{B}\hat{O}_{P}^{B})

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\rho^{AB}=

\displaystyle\rho^{A}\wedge\rho^{B}\ \ \ \ \forall\hat{O}_{P}^{A(B)}

(10)

with
\rho^{A(B)}
being legitimate PSSR-respected density matrix for system
A(B)
.

The proof of the
Lemma 2
is given in Appendix A.

A

is the one which can be represented as a convex combination of product fermionic states
\{\rho_{i}^{AB}\}
:

\rho_{sep}^{AB}=\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i}^{AB}

(11)

One can write the most general form of fermionic separable state as

\displaystyle\rho_{sep}^{AB}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}p_{i}\rho_{i}^{A}\wedge\rho_{i}^{B}+\sum_{abcd}A^{\prime}_{abcd}|E_{a}^{A}\rangle\langle O_{c}^{A}|\wedge|E_{b}^{B}\rangle\langle O_{d}^{B}|

Transformation of a separable fermionic state into an entangled fermionic state is forbidden under the operation of local anti-block diagonal unitary. In other words,

III
Fermionic teleportation for N
\times
N mode systems

Two different notions of fermionic teleportation have been discussed in
Debarba et al. 2020
:

1.

Fermionic single-mode exact teleportation
is associated with the procedure in which Alice starts with a bipartite fermionic state
|\psi_{\tilde{A}\tilde{A}^{\prime}}\rangle
(possibly entangled) and the information about the reduced state of Alice’s side
\rho_{\tilde{A}}
is transferred to Bob’s side such that the final state becomes
|\psi_{B\tilde{A}^{\prime}}\rangle
. This process utilizes a single copy of a maximally entangled two-mode fermionic state
|\psi_{AB}\rangle
.

2.

Fermionic two-mode teleportation
is associated with the procedure to transfer one qubit of information encoded in terms of complex amplitude
\alpha
and
\beta
(
|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}=1
). However, to encode the information of
\alpha
and
\beta
using fermionic state, one need minimum two number of modes. Hence, one has to perform the
Fermionic single-mode exact teleportation
twice to transmit the information encoded in terms of
\alpha
and
\beta
.

In this paper, we generalize the concept of
Fermionic single-mode teleportation
for N
\times
N mode system where we assume each fermionic system
\tilde{A},\tilde{A}^{\prime},A
and
B
consists of
N
local fermionic modes (LFM). But before delve into the fermionic teleportation, let us briefly discuss the teleportation protocol in d-dimensional Hilbert space as described in
Werner 2001
in the context of teleporting subsystem.

III.1
Teleportation in d-dimensional Hilbert space in SQT

In this quantum teleportation protocol, four quantum systems are used. Consider the teleportation channel being applied to a part of the bipartite state
\rho_{\tilde{A}^{\prime}\tilde{A}}
. Also, consider a pre-shared maximally entangled state
|\omega\rangle\langle\omega|_{AB}
between Alice and Bob. Hence the initial state can be written as

\rho_{\tilde{A}^{\prime}\tilde{A}}\otimes|\omega\rangle\langle\omega|_{AB}

Then a successful teleportation is represented by the equation

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}\text{tr}((\rho_{\tilde{A}^{\prime}\tilde{A}}\otimes|\omega\rangle\langle\omega|_{AB})

\displaystyle(\hat{O}_{\tilde{A}^{\prime}}\otimes M_{x}\otimes N_{x}(\hat{O}_{B})))

(13)

\displaystyle=\text{tr}(\rho_{\tilde{A}^{\prime}B}\hat{O}_{\tilde{A}^{\prime}}\otimes\hat{O}_{B})

with
M_{x}=|\Psi_{x}\rangle\langle\Psi_{x}|_{\tilde{A}A}
and
\langle\Psi_{x}|\Psi_{y}\rangle=\delta_{xy}
. Here,
\Psi_{x}
constitutes the basis of maximally entangled states and
X
denotes a set of
d^{2}
elements.
Also,

N_{x}(\hat{O}_{B})=U_{x}^{\dagger}(\hat{O}_{B})U_{x}

and

|\Psi_{x}\rangle\langle\Psi_{x}|=(U_{x}\otimes\mathbb{I})|\omega\rangle\langle\omega|(U_{x}^{\dagger}\otimes\mathbb{I})

defines the
U_{x}
for each x. Hence,
U_{x}
also forms the bases of unitary operators. To prove Eq. (
13
), let us proceed, as given in
Werner 2001
, by choosing
\rho_{\tilde{A}^{\prime}\tilde{A}}=|\phi_{2}\rangle_{\tilde{A}^{\prime}}\langle\phi_{4}|\otimes|\phi_{1}\rangle_{\tilde{A}}\langle\phi_{3}|
,
\hat{O}_{B}=|\psi_{1}\rangle_{B}\langle\psi_{3}|
and
\hat{O}_{\tilde{A}^{\prime}}=|\psi_{2}\rangle_{\tilde{A}^{\prime}}\langle\psi_{4}|
. Then, considering the

term in Eq. (
13

\displaystyle Term_{x}=\langle\phi_{4}\otimes

\displaystyle\phi_{3}\otimes\omega|\psi_{2}\otimes\Psi_{x}\otimes U_{x}^{\dagger}\psi_{1}\rangle\times

(14)

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{4}\otimes\Psi_{x}\otimes U_{x}^{\dagger}\psi_{3}|\phi_{2}\otimes\phi_{1}\otimes\omega\rangle

Now substituting
|\Psi_{x}\rangle=(U_{x}\otimes\mathbb{I})|\omega\rangle
and utilizing the inner product definition of tensor product Hilbert space we get

Term_{x}=d^{-2}\langle\phi_{3}|\psi_{1}\rangle\langle\psi_{3}|\phi_{1}\rangle\langle\phi_{4}|\psi_{2}\rangle\langle\psi_{4}|\phi_{2}\rangle

Hence, summing over

Notice, SQT satisfies local distinguishability of states

III.2
Teleportation in d-dimensional Hilbert space in FQT

In
2^{N}
-dim, teleportation of complex amplitudes of even-parity and odd-parity vectors at the same time is prohibited. This makes a difference between the teleportation protocol in SQT and FQT. However, incoherent mixtures of even-parity vectors and odd parity vectors are allowed. Fermionic partial trace of a bi-partite fermionic state may result in a reduced density matrix with incoherent mixtures of even-parity vectors and odd parity vectors. In this work, we have demonstrated the teleportation in the sense of subsystem swapping. To see this, consider a fermionic state

of an

bipartite system where
N
denotes LFMs. Our objective is to faithfully teleport the information about the state of subsystem

Teleportation in FQT. In this scheme

We define the resource state to be used for this teleportation as,

Theorem 1.
A successful fermionic teleportation of a subsystem of a bipartite fermionic state can be described by the equation

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}\text{tr}((\rho_{\tilde{A}^{\prime}\tilde{A}}^{f}\wedge\omega_{AB}^{f})

\displaystyle(\hat{O}_{\tilde{A}^{\prime}}\wedge M_{x}\wedge N_{x}(\hat{O}_{B})))

State-Channel isomorphism in FQT

A Quantum Operations for FQT in it’s most general form or Fermionic Channel has already been introduced in
Vidal et al. 2021
where the equivalence of operator-sum representation and completely Positive map has been proved. In this section, following the procedure mentioned in
Horodecki et al. 1999
, we will establish an isomorphism given by

\rho^{f}_{\mathcal{E}}=(\mathbb{I}\wedge\mathcal{E})(|\psi^{+}\rangle\langle\psi^{+}|_{f})

(25)

Here,
|\psi^{+}\rangle\langle\psi^{+}|_{f}=\Psi^{|E\rangle\langle E|}_{N}+\Psi^{|E\rangle\langle O|}_{N}
, represented in basis
\mathcal{B_{AB}^{P}}
and the first reduction of
\rho^{f}_{\mathcal{E}}
following fermionic partial tracing procedure gives
\mathbb{I}/d
with
\mathcal{E}
representing a fermionic channel. To prove the surjective property of this isomorphism. let us assume that the spectral decomposition of
\rho^{f}_{\mathcal{E}}
is given by

\rho^{f}_{\mathcal{E}}=\sum_{m}p_{m}^{E}|\psi^{E}_{m}\rangle\langle\psi^{E}_{m}|+\sum_{m}p_{m}^{O}|\psi^{O}_{m}\rangle\langle\psi^{O}_{m}|

where
|\psi^{E}_{m}\rangle
and
|\psi^{O}_{m}\rangle
represents eigenvectors of
\rho^{f}_{\mathcal{E}}
for all
m
. Let,

\displaystyle|\psi^{E}_{1}\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{ij}A_{ij}^{EE}|E_{i}\rangle\wedge|E_{j}\rangle+A_{ij}^{OO}|O_{i}\rangle\wedge|O_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle|\psi^{O}_{1}\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{ij}A_{ij}^{EO}|E_{i}\rangle\wedge|O_{j}\rangle+A_{ij}^{OE}|O_{i}\rangle\wedge|E_{j}\rangle

One can then represent
|\psi^{E}_{1}\rangle
in terms of a fermionic operator
K_{1}^{BD}
as

|\psi^{E}_{1}\rangle=(\mathbb{I}\wedge K_{1}^{BD})(|\psi^{+}\rangle\langle\psi^{+}|_{f})

with,
K_{1}^{BD}
being block diagonal in the basis
\mathcal{B^{P}_{A}}
. To see this, let us assume

K_{1}^{BD}=\sum_{ij}S_{ij}|E_{i}\rangle\langle E_{j}|+T_{ij}|O_{i}\rangle\langle O_{j}|

Then,
(\mathbb{I}\wedge K_{1}^{BD})(|\psi^{+}\rangle\langle\psi^{+}|_{f})
is given by

=\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\sum_{ij}S_{ij}|E_{j}\rangle\wedge|E_{i}\rangle+T_{ij}|O_{j}\rangle\wedge|O_{i}\rangle

Fixing
\langle E_{i}|K_{1}^{BD}|E_{j}\rangle=\sqrt{d}A_{ij}^{EE}
and
\langle O_{i}|K_{1}^{BD}|O_{j}\rangle=\sqrt{d}A_{ij}^{OO}
we get
|\psi^{E}_{1}\rangle=(\mathbb{I}\wedge K_{1}^{BD})(|\psi^{+}\rangle\langle\psi^{+}|_{f})
. Similarly one can find anti-block diagonal
K_{1}^{ABD}
and show that
|\psi^{O}_{1}\rangle=(\mathbb{I}\wedge K_{1}^{ABD})(|\psi^{+}\rangle\langle\psi^{+}|_{f})
. Hence,
\rho^{f}_{\mathcal{E}}
can be written as

\displaystyle\rho^{f}_{\mathcal{E}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{m}p_{m}^{E}(\mathbb{I}\wedge K_{m}^{BD})(|\psi^{+}\rangle\langle\psi^{+}|_{f})(\mathbb{I}\wedge{K_{m}^{BD}}^{\dagger})+

(26)

\displaystyle p_{m}^{O}(\mathbb{I}\wedge K_{m}^{ABD})(|\psi^{+}\rangle\langle\psi^{+}|_{f})(\mathbb{I}\wedge{K_{m}^{ABD}}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=(\mathbb{I}\wedge\mathcal{E})(|\psi^{+}\rangle\langle\psi^{+}|_{f})

Then
(\mathbb{I}\wedge\mathcal{E})
is a positive map with
\{K_{m}^{BD},K_{m}^{ABD}|\ \forall m\}
representing the Kraus operator for the channel
\mathcal{E}
.

To see the trace-preservion condition of the channel
\mathcal{E}
, we need to show that the operator

Z^{f}=\sum_{m}p_{m}^{E}{K_{m}^{BD}}^{\dagger}K_{m}^{BD}+p_{m}^{O}{K_{m}^{ABD}}^{\dagger}K_{m}^{ABD}=\mathbb{I}

. For this, recall that the consistency condition for partial trace in FQT
Friis et al. 2013
is given by

\text{tr}(\mathcal{O}_{A}\rho^{f}_{A})=\text{tr}((\mathcal{O}_{A}\wedge\mathbb{I}_{B})\rho^{f}_{AB})

for any bi-partite fermionic states
\rho^{f}_{AB}
with reduced state
\rho^{f}_{A}
. For
\rho^{f}_{A}=\mathbb{I}/d
and letting
\rho^{f}_{AB}=\rho^{f}_{\mathcal{E}}
, we get

\displaystyle\text{tr}(\mathcal{O}_{A})=d\text{tr}((\mathcal{O}_{A}\wedge\mathbb{I}_{B})\rho^{f}_{AB})=

\displaystyle d\sum_{m}p_{m}^{E}\text{tr}((\mathcal{O}_{A}\wedge\mathbb{I}_{B})(\mathbb{I}\wedge K_{m}^{BD})(|\psi^{+}\rangle\langle\psi^{+}|_{f})(\mathbb{I}\wedge{K_{m}^{BD}}^{\dagger}))

\displaystyle+p_{m}^{O}\text{tr}((\mathcal{O}_{A}\wedge\mathbb{I}_{B})(\mathbb{I}\wedge K_{m}^{ABD})(|\psi^{+}\rangle\langle\psi^{+}|_{f})(\mathbb{I}\wedge{K_{m}^{ABD}}^{\dagger}))

\displaystyle=d\text{tr}((|\psi^{+}\rangle\langle\psi^{+}|_{f})(\mathcal{O}_{A}\wedge Z^{f}))

\displaystyle=d\text{tr}((|\psi^{+}\rangle\langle\psi^{+}|_{f})(\mathcal{O}_{A}(Z^{f})^{T}\wedge\mathbb{I}_{B}))

\displaystyle=\text{tr}(\mathcal{O}_{A}(Z^{f})^{T})\ \ \ \ \ \forall\mathcal{O}_{A}

where we have used the properties
(\mathbb{I}\wedge Z^{f})|\psi^{+}\rangle_{f}=((Z^{f})^{T}\wedge\mathbb{I})|\psi^{+}\rangle_{f}
and partial trace of
|\psi^{+}\rangle\langle\psi^{+}|_{f}
is given by
\mathbb{I}/d
. Hence,
(Z^{f})^{T}=Z^{f}=\mathbb{I}
. This completes the proof of surjectivity.

VIII
Conclusion

In this work, we characterized general teleportation channel in FQT by introducing a fidelity measure based on separable measurements (also called as ‘product’ measurements). The motivation behind the use of this measure is the physical scenario where Alice and Bob are far away from each other. Although, due to local discriminability in SQT
Chiribella et al. 2011
, product measurements result in different probablities for different state, the lack of local discriminability in FQT may result in the same probablity for different states when measured with product measurements. Hence, in general, Alice and Bob may not be able to distinguish the bi-partite state before and after the teleportation operationally even though the mathematical structures of the initial and final bi-partite states are different. We considered this situation as the exact teleportation channel with respect to the separable fidelity measure.

At first, we derived the fermionic invariant state under local twirling operation and found that the structure of this invariant state differ from that of Werner state - the corresponding invariant state in SQT. We introduced a Haar measure on the restricted Unitary group
U_{res}
and showed that any state can be reduced to the invariant form using restricted Unitary group twirling. We constitute the Unitary-2 design of the restricted Unitary group twirling by introducing a restricted Clifford set. Introducing the partial transpose operation on fermionic modes, we derived the fermionic isotropic state - a linear combination of noise in even subspace, noise in odd subspace and the fermionic resource state equivalent to the exact teleportation channel. We introduced the state-channel isomorphism and channel twirling operation in FQT, and provided a lower bound on the optimal teleportation fidelity. The lower bound depends only on the parameters invariant under the local-twirling operations. Finally, we compared this lower bound with the optimal teleportation fidelity in SQT. However, the difference in the operational teleportation fidelity in SQT and FQT can be considered to be the consequence of local discriminability. While utilizing the operational definition of teleportation fidelity in FQT, Fermionic channel twirling operation need careful attention due to the non-linearity involved in the definition of the separable fidelity.

Even though we have provided the lower bound on the optimal teleportation fidelity calculated operationally, finding the exact expression for the optimal teleportation fidelity still remains open. Also, the effect of filtering on the twirling invariant parameters in FQT is yet to be figured out. In this paper, we establish the fermionic state-channel isomorphism by applying the channel on one half of the resource state with
\Upsilon
being unity. In this regard, possibility to extend the fermionic state-channel isomorphism with
\omega_{AB}^{f}
still remains open. Apart from FQT, one may also try to find the optimal teleportation fidelity for quantum information encoded in Bosonic modes. Also, general quantum teleportation channel may find its application in Fermionic Quantum Computation in the NISQ-like settings.

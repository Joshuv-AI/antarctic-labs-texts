Euler’s Reflection Formula, Infinite Product Formulas, and the Correspondence Principle of Quantum Mechanics


Abstract
We generalize the derivation of the Wallis formula for π \pi from a variational computation of the spectrum of the Hydrogen atom. We obtain infinite product formulas for certain combinations of gamma functions, which include irrational numbers such as 2 \sqrt{2} as well as some nested radicals. We also derive Euler’s reflection formula for reciprocals of positive even integers. We show that Bohr’s correspondence principle allows us to derive our product formulas and the reflection formula without the need for the limit definition of the gamma function.

Introduction

In
[
1
]
, the Wallis formula for
\pi
,

{\pi\over 2}={2\cdot 2\over 1\cdot 3}\;{4\cdot 4\over 3\cdot 5}\;{6\cdot 6\over 5\cdot 7}\cdots,

(1)

was derived from a variational computation of the spectrum of the hydrogen atom.
A trial wave function was used to approximate the energy levels of the hydrogen atom. Then Bohr’s correspondence principle was used to take a classical limit in which the approximate and exact results approach each other, ultimately leading to the Wallis formula.

It was shown in
[
2
]
that the Wallis formula for
\pi
obtained in
[
1
]
can be obtained using a different trial wave function than the one used there; it was also shown that the sum of some infinite series involving
\pi
can be obtained in a similar approach. It was then shown in
[
3
]
that the Wallis formula can also be obtained from a variational computation of the spectrum of a different potential that appears in quantum mechanics, namely the harmonic oscillator potential.

In this paper, we generalize the trial wave function of
[
1
]
to obtain a systematic set of infinite product formulas for certain combinations of gamma functions.
While these infinite product formulas can be obtained from previously-known mathematical formulas, here they are being derived for the first time by using a variational computation and a trial wave function within the framework of quantum mechanics. We follow
[
1
]
closely in presenting the derivation, first in three dimensions and then in
N
dimensions. It appears that some of the formulas obtained in the present paper can also be obtained from a similar computation involving the harmonic oscillator potential, using the observations made in
[
3
]
.

Using these product formulas, we also derive Euler’s reflection formula,
\Gamma(1-z)\Gamma(z)={\pi\over\sin\pi z}
, for
z=1/2s
,
s
a positive integer. Again, this formula has not been derived from physics until now.

We conclude by discussing the role of different definitions of
\Gamma(z)
in mathematical derivations of the product formulas and their relation to the role played by Bohr’s correspondence principle in our derivation.

Product formulas from the 3-dimensional hydrogen atom

The Schrödinger equation for the hydrogen atom is given by

H\psi=\left(-{\hbar^{2}\over 2m}\nabla^{2}-{e^{2}\over r}\right)\psi=E\psi,

with the corresponding radial equation obtained by separation of variables being

H(r)R(r)=\left[-{\hbar^{2}\over 2m}\left({d^{2}\over dr^{2}}+{2\over r}{d\over dr}-{\ell(\ell+1)\over r^{2}}\right)-{e^{2}\over r}\right]R(r)=ER(r).

Now we use the following trial wave function:

\psi_{\alpha b\ell m}=r^{\ell}e^{-\alpha r^{b}}Y^{m}_{\ell}(\theta,\phi),

(2)

where
\alpha>0
and
b>0
are real parameters and the
Y^{m}_{\ell}(\theta,\phi)
are the usual spherical harmonics. In
[
1
]
,
b
was fixed at
b=2
.
The expectation value of the Hamiltonian is found to be given by

\displaystyle\langle H\rangle_{\alpha b\ell}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle{\langle\psi_{\alpha b\ell m}|H(r)|\psi_{\alpha b\ell m}\rangle\over\langle\psi_{\alpha b\ell m}|\psi_{\alpha b\ell m}\rangle}

(3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left.{{\hbar^{2}\over 2m}\Gamma\left(2\ell+1+b\over b\right)\left(b(2\ell+b+1)\over 4\right)(2\alpha)^{2/b}-e^{2}{\Gamma\left(2\ell+2\over b\right)}(2\alpha)^{1/b}\;\over\Gamma\left(2\ell+3\over b\right)}\right.,

where we used the orthonormality of spherical harmonics. Minimizing with respect to
\alpha
gives

\langle H\rangle_{min}^{\ell,b}=-{me^{4}\over b\hbar^{2}}{1\over({2\ell+b+1\over 2})}\;{\left(\Gamma({2\ell+2\over b})\right)^{2}\over\Gamma({2\ell+3\over b})\Gamma({2\ell+b+1\over b})},

(4)

which is an upper bound for the lowest energy state with the given value of
\ell
.

The exact value for the lowest energy eigenstate for a given
\ell
is

E_{0,\ell}=-{me^{4}\over 2\hbar^{2}}{1\over(\ell+1)^{2}}\;.

(5)

This is also, famously, the value obtained in the Bohr model.
As in
[
1
]
, before we apply Bohr’s correspondence principle, we check that in the large
\ell
limit, the trial wave function – like the exact result – corresponds to strictly circular orbits, as in the Bohr model. To that end, we compute the uncertainty in
r^{2}
, measured in units of mean square radius. We obtain

{[\langle r^{4}\rangle_{\alpha b\ell}-(\langle r^{2}\rangle_{\alpha b\ell})^{2}]^{1\over 2}\over\langle r^{2}\rangle_{\alpha b\ell}}=\left({{\Gamma\left({2\ell+7\over b}\right)\Gamma\left({2\ell+3\over b}\right)\over{\Gamma\left({2\ell+5\over b}\right)}^{2}}-1}\right)^{1\over 2},

(6)

a quantity which approaches 0 at large
\ell
(see computation of the limit in Appendix
A
), so the orbits are circular at large
\ell
.
In addition, since our trial system approximates a quantum system, it
has quantized energy and angular momenta. Therefore, our trial system satisfies the postulates of the (semi-classical) Bohr model: circularity of orbits and quantization of energy and angular momenta.
According to Bohr’s correspondence principle, in the limit of large quantum number, quantum quantities approach classical ones
[
4
, p. 31]
,
[
5
, p. 117]
. It now follows that in the limit
\ell\rightarrow\infty
, our approximate quantum system approaches the classical model, as does the exact quantum system. Therefore, the ratio of the approximate value of the energy level given by equation (
3
) to the classical (Bohr) value
given by equation (
5
) approaches 1 in this limit. That is,

\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}{\langle H\rangle_{min}^{\ell,b}\over E_{0,\ell}}=\lim_{\ell\rightarrow\infty}{4\over b}{(\ell+1)^{2}\over(2\ell+b+1)}\;{\left(\Gamma({2\ell+2\over b})\right)^{2}\over\Gamma({2\ell+3\over b})\Gamma({2\ell+b+1\over b})}=1.

(7)

This limit leads to infinite product formulas for combinations of gamma functions of the form

{\Gamma(x)\Gamma(y)\over\Gamma\left({x+y\over 2}\right)^{2}}

for certain values of
x
and
y
which we now discuss.

Let
b
be a positive even integer, and let us consider the subsequence of integers
\ell
which are equivalent
modulo
b/2
, the reciprocal of the coefficient of
\ell
in the arguments of
\Gamma
in equation (
7
); the limit of the expression in equation (
7

Since we have
\ell\equiv a\mbox{ mod }{b/2}
for some

, we also have
\ell=a+{kb\over 2}
for some non-negative integer

\Gamma\left({2\ell+2\over b}\right)=\Gamma\left({2a+2\over b}+k\right),

and similarly for the other values of
\Gamma(x)
that appear in equation (
7
).
We can now use the recursion relation
\Gamma(z+k)=(z+k-1)(z+k-2)\cdots(z+1)z\Gamma(z)
and some algebraic manipulations to rewrite our limit as an infinite product formula for a combination of
\Gamma

{\Gamma\left({2a+1+2b\over b}\right)\Gamma\left({2a+3\over b}\right)\over{\Gamma\left({2a+2+b\over b}\right)}^{2}}=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}{(kb+2a+2)^{2}\over(kb+2a-b+3)(kb+2a+1+b)}\;,

(8)

or equivalently,

{\Gamma\left({2a+1\over b}\right)\Gamma\left({2a+3\over b}\right)\over{\Gamma\left({2a+2\over b}\right)}^{2}}={(2a+2)^{2}\over(2a+1)(2a+1+b)}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}{(kb+2a+2)^{2}\over(kb+2a-b+3)(kb+2a+1+b)}\;.

(9)

When
b=2
and

, we have the case of
[

and the above expression is the Wallis formula for

, equation (

Euler’s reflection formula from product formulas

In our infinite product formula equation (
9
), let
a={b\over 2}-1
, with
b
a positive even integer as before. After using the identity
\Gamma(1+{1\over b})={1\over b}\Gamma({1\over b})
, we have

\Gamma\left(1-{1\over b}\right)\Gamma\left({1\over b}\right)={b^{3}\over(b-1)(2b-1)}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}{(kb+b)^{2}\over(kb+1)(kb+2b-1)}.

(13)

Let
z={1\over b}
, take the reciprocal of the above equation, and rewrite it as a power series in
z-1
. This equation then becomes

{1\over\Gamma(1-z)\Gamma(z)}=-(z-1)\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(1-{(z-1)^{2}\over k^{2}}\right).

The right hand side turns out to be Euler’s formula for
{-\sin\pi(z-1)\over\pi}={\sin\pi z\over\pi}
, leading us to the formula

\Gamma(1-z)\Gamma(z)={\pi\over\sin\pi z}.

(14)

This formula is known as Euler’s reflection formula and holds for any complex number
z
that is not an integer.

Relation between definitions of
\Gamma(z)
and the correspondence principle

The gamma functions that appear in equations (
3
), (
4
), and (
10
) arise from the integrals used in the computation of the expectation value of the Hamiltonian and the integral formula definition for the gamma function,

\Gamma(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}\,t^{z-1}dt,\hskip 28.45274pt\mbox{Re}(z)>0\;.

(23)

Then, the correspondence principle gives us the limit in equation (
7
) or (
11
), which leads to our infinite product formulas and in turn to Euler’s reflection formula. Can the same infinite product formulas be obtained in any other way? The answer is “yes,” but
Euler’s limit-based definition of the gamma function,

\Gamma(z)=\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}{m^{z}m!\over z(z+1)(z+2)\cdots(z+m)}\,,\hskip 28.45274ptz\in\mathbf{C},\;z\neq 0,-1,-2,\ldots\;,

(24)

is required. The details are carried out in Appendix
B
.

The mathematical equivalence of Euler’s limit-based definition of the gamma function, equation (
24
), and the integral definition, equation (
23
), requires proof (see
[
14
]
), and is necessary for the mathematical derivation of the infinite product formulas as shown in Appendix
B
.
In our derivation,
we do not need the limit-based definition. Bohr’s correspondence principle provides the crucial link between the integral definition and the infinite product formulas we obtained.

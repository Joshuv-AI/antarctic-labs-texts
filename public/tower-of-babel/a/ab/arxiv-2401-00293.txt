Representation formulas for maximal monotone operators of type (D) in Banach spaces whose dual spaces are strictly convex
Nguyen B. Tran, Tran N. Nguyen, Huynh M. Hien

Abstract
This work deals with a maximal monotone operator A A of type (D) in a Banach space whose dual space is strictly convex. We establish some representations for the value A ​ x Ax at a given point x x via its values at nearby points of x x . We show that the faces of A ​ x Ax are contained in the set of all weak ∗ convergent limits of bounded nets of the operator at nearby points of x x , then we obtain a representation for A ​ x Ax by use of this set. In addition, representations for the support function of A ​ x Ax based on the minimal-norm selection of the operator in certain Banach spaces are given. Keywords: Maximal monotone operator, monotone operator of type (D), minimal-norm selection, w-Kadec-Klee-property, w ∗ -Kadec-Klee-property, strictly convex space MSC 2020: 47H05, 47H04, 47N10



1
Introduction

The theory of maximal monotone operators has had a great development for over sixty years. The concept of maximal monotone operators is a generalization of subdifferentials of lower semi-continuous convex functions. Maximal monotone operators rapidly found uses for subgradients, optimization, algorithms, financial mathematics, and much more. The closedness of graphs of maximal monotone operators plays a significant role in the theory of variational analysis. It is well-known that the graph of a maximal monotone operator is norm
\times
weak
∗
sequentially closed. However, the graph of such an operator might be not norm
\times
weak
∗
closed. This occurs due to the fact that a weak
∗
convergent net might be unbounded.

In 2003, Borwein et al.
[
3
]
gave an example showing that the subdifferential of a lower semi-continuous function in a separable Hilbert space is not norm
\times
weak
∗
closed.
In 2012, Borwein and Yao
[
4
]
provided an explicit structure formula for maximal monotone operators whose domains have nonempty interiors. Consequently, the graphs of these operators are
norm
\times
weak
∗
closed. The authors considered a maximal monotone operator in a subset of the interior of its domain in which the operator is locally bounded. The support function of the operator at a given point
x
equals the support function in the set consisting of the weak
∗
convergent limits of the operator at nearby points of
x
. Recently, Hantoute and Nguyen
[
11
]
considered maximal monotone operators in Hilbert spaces whose domains may have empty interiors
and established a relation between the faces of its values at a given point
x
and the set of strong convergent limits of the operator at nearby points of
x
.
Then, the authors provided an explicit representation for maximal monotone operators.
These results were extended to maximal monotone operators in reflexive Banach spaces
which are locally bounded together with their dual spaces by Khanh and Nguyen
[
14
]
. Furthermore, based on the minimal-norm selections,
the authors discovered another formula for maximal monotone operators in that class of spaces.

The main approach in
[
11
,
14
]
is that the authors used Minty surjectivity theorem in reflective spaces. A class of maximal monotone operators in nonreflexive Banach spaces which share several properties with these operators in reflexive Banach spaces was introduced by Gossez in 1971 and named ‘type (D)’; see
[
9
]
. A typical property of such a maximal monotone operator is that the range of the sum of it and the duality mapping is dense in the dual space. In 1992, Fitzpatrick and Phelps
[
7
]
defined a class of maximal monotone operators which includes the class of type (D) and was later called ‘type (FP)’. Four years later, Simons
[
19
]
introduced the class (NI) which was seemed to generalize the class of type (D). However, in 2010, Marques and Svaiter
[
12
]
showed that these two classes are identical. One year later, Bauschke et al.
[
2
]
discovered that these three of types coincide.

In this paper we consider a maximal monotone operator of type (D) in a nonreflexive Banach space whose dual space is strictly convex and try to extend the results in
[
11
,
14
]
. We represent the value of the operator at a point
x
via the set
consisting of weak
∗
convergent limits of its bounded nets at nearby points of
x
. We introduce the concepts ‘w-Kadec-Klee property’ and ‘w
∗
-Kadec-Klee property’ for Banach spaces and their dual spaces, respectively.
These classes of spaces include locally uniformly convex spaces. Respective results are given for the case that
X
has the w-Kadec-Klee property and
X^{*}
has the w
∗
-Kadec-Klee property.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we introduce the necessary background material which is well-known in principle
[
1
,
16
,
20
]
. Since the field requires a substantial amount of notation and concepts, we nevertheless provide some details. In Section
3
we consider a maximal monotone operator
A
of type (D) in a nonreflexive Banach space whose dual space is strictly convex. In Theorem
3.1
we represent the value
Ax
via the values of
A
at nearby points of
x
. Theorem
3.3
deals with lower semicontinuous convex functions.
In the final section we give a representation formula for the support function of the value of a maximal monotone operator in a nonreflexive Banach space whose dual space has the w
∗
-Kadec-Klee property by means of its minimal-norm selections.

Definition 2.1

Let

It is clear that a locally uniformly convex space is strictly convex.
The

are respectively defined by

\displaystyle J(x)

\displaystyle:=\{x^{*}\in X^{*}:\,\frac{1}{2}\|y\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}+\langle x^{*},y-x\rangle,\forall y\in X\},

(2.1)

\displaystyle J^{*}(x^{*})

\displaystyle:=\{x^{**}\in X^{**}:\langle x^{**},x^{*}\rangle=\|x^{*}\|^{2}=\|x^{**}\|^{2}\}.

(2.2)

For

is nonempty and closed convex. If

Let

Let

.

(i) An operator

Representation formulas for maximal monotone operators of type (D)

In this section we consider a maximal monotone operator
A
of type (D) in a Banach space
X

whose dual space is strictly convex. We represent the value
Ax
via the values at nearby points of
x
. We define new properties for
X
and
X^{*}
, which are generalizations of the Kadec-Klee property, named the w-Kadec-Klee and
\operatorname{w}^{*}
-Kadec-Klee, respectively. A respective representation was obtained in the case that
X^{*}
has the
\operatorname{w}^{*}
-Kadec-Klee property.

We first observe a property of the minimal-norm selection.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a maximal monotone operator. Then the minimal-norm
A^{\circ}x
is nonempty for all
x\in{D}(A).

Proof.

Let
x\in{D}(A)
. Then
\alpha:=\inf_{x^{*}\in Ax}\|x^{*}\|<+\infty
. For every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we take
x^{*}_{n}\in Ax
such that

\|x^{*}_{n}\|\leq\alpha+\frac{1}{n}.

(3.1)

By Banach-Alaoglu theorem, there exist a subnet
(x^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}
of
(x^{*}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
x^{*}\in X^{*}
such that
x^{*}_{i}\overset{\operatorname{w}^{*}}{\rightharpoonup}x^{*}.
It follows from inequality (
3.1
) that
\|x^{*}\|\leq\displaystyle\liminf_{i}\|x^{*}_{i}\|\leq\alpha.
On the other hand, also from inequality (
3.1
), the subnet
(x^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}
is eventually bounded. Owing to
[
3
, Section 2]
, we have
x^{*}\in Ax
. This implies
\|x^{*}\|\geq\alpha
and hence
\|x^{*}\|=\alpha
, so we get
x^{*}\in A^{\circ}x
, proving the lemma.
∎

The following properties of
J_{\varepsilon}
are useful later.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
x\in X
and
\varepsilon\geq 0,\lambda>0
. Then

(i)

J_{\varepsilon}(-x)=-J_{\varepsilon}(x),\,\,\lambda J_{\varepsilon}(x)=J_{\lambda^{2}\varepsilon}(\lambda x);

(ii)

\underset{x^{*}\in J_{\varepsilon}(x)}{\sup}|\|x\|-\|x^{*}\||\leq\sqrt{2\varepsilon};

(iii)

\underset{x^{*}\in J_{\varepsilon}(x)}{\sup}|\langle x^{*},x\rangle-\|x\|^{2}|\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon}(1+\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}).

Proof.

(i) Let
x\in X,\varepsilon\geq 0,\lambda>0
and
x^{*}\in J_{\varepsilon}(-x).

It follows from the definition of
J_{\varepsilon}
that

\frac{1}{2}\|y\|^{2}+\varepsilon\geq\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}+\langle x^{*},y+x\rangle,\,\forall y\in X.

(3.2)

This implies

\frac{1}{2}\|y\|^{2}+\varepsilon\geq\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}+\langle-x^{*},y-x\rangle,\forall y\in X,

which gives
-x^{*}\in J_{\varepsilon}(x)
. Hence, we have the inclusion

J_{\varepsilon}(-x)\subset-J_{\varepsilon}(x),\forall x\in X.

Switching the roles of
x
and
-x
, we also get
-J_{\varepsilon}(x)\subset J_{\varepsilon}(-x)
, implying the former. For the latter, multiplying both side of (
3.2
) by
\lambda^{2}
, we get

\frac{1}{2}\|\lambda y\|^{2}+\lambda^{2}\varepsilon\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\lambda x\|^{2}+\langle\lambda x^{*},\lambda y-\lambda x\rangle,\forall y\in X.

This yields

\frac{1}{2}\|y\|^{2}+\lambda^{2}\varepsilon\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\lambda x\|^{2}+\langle\lambda x^{*},y-\lambda x\rangle,\forall y\in X.

(3.3)

Then
\lambda x^{*}\in J_{\lambda^{2}\varepsilon}(\lambda x)
implies that

\lambda J_{\varepsilon}(x)\subset J_{\lambda^{2}\varepsilon}(\lambda x),\,\,\forall x\in X,\forall\varepsilon\geq 0,\forall\lambda>0.

(3.4)

This leads to

\frac{1}{\lambda}J_{\lambda^{2}\varepsilon}(\lambda x)\subset J_{(\frac{1}{\lambda})^{2}(\lambda^{2}\varepsilon)}(\frac{1}{\lambda}\lambda x)=J_{\varepsilon}(x),\,\,\forall x\in X,\forall\varepsilon\geq 0,\forall\lambda>0.

(3.5)

Combining (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we get the latter.

(ii) This follows from

|\|x^{*}\|-\|x\||\leq\sqrt{2\varepsilon}

due to

[
13
]
.

(iii) This is obvious for
\varepsilon=0.
For
\varepsilon>0
the definition of
J_{\varepsilon}
implies that

\frac{1}{2}\|(1+t)x\|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}+t\langle x^{*},x\rangle-\varepsilon,\,\,\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

This is equivalent to

t(\langle x^{*},x\rangle-\|x\|^{2})\leq\varepsilon+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\|x\|^{2},\,\,\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

In particular, for
t=\sqrt{\varepsilon}
we get

|\langle x^{*},x\rangle-\|x\|^{2}|\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon}\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}\right),

which proves (iii).
∎

The following lemma is an extension of
[
1
, Proposition 2.2]

on the Yosida approximation of maximal monotone operators in reflexive Banach spaces.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a Banach space whose dual space is strictly convex,
A:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a maximal monotone operator of type (D). For every
x\in{D}(A)
and a sequence
\lambda_{n}\downarrow 0
there exist
a subnet
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in I}
of
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, a net
(x_{i})_{i\in I}\subset{D}(A)
converging to
x
, a net
(z^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}\subset X^{*}
with
z^{*}_{i}\in J_{\lambda^{6}_{i}}(x-x_{i})
such that

(\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}
is bounded and

\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i}\in Ax_{i},\,\,\,\,\|\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i}\|\to\|A^{\circ}x\|,\,\,\,\lambda_{i}^{-1}z^{*}_{i}\overset{\operatorname{w}^{*}}{\rightharpoonup}A^{\circ}x.

(3.6)

Proof.

Let
x\in{D}(A)
and a sequence
\lambda_{n}\downarrow 0
. By Theorem
2.1
, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
we can find
x_{n}\in X
such that

0\in\lambda^{-1}_{n}J_{\lambda^{6}_{n}}(x_{n}-x)+Ax_{n}.

By Lemma
3.2
(i), there exists a sequence
(z^{*}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X^{*}
such that
z^{*}_{n}\in J_{\lambda^{6}_{n}}(x-x_{n})
and

\lambda^{-1}_{n}z^{*}_{n}\in Ax_{n},\,\,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

(3.7)

Due to the monotonicity of
A
and Lemma
3.1
, the set
A^{\circ}(x)
is nonempty and

\langle\lambda^{-1}_{n}z^{*}_{n}-A^{\circ}x,x-x_{n}\rangle\leq 0,

(3.8)

which implies that

\lambda^{-1}_{n}\langle z^{*}_{n},x-x_{n}\rangle\leq\langle A^{\circ}x,x-x_{n}\rangle\leq\|A^{\circ}x\|\left\|x_{n}-x\right\|.

(3.9)

Since
z^{*}_{n}\in J_{\lambda^{6}_{n}}(x-x_{n})
, Lemma
3.2
(iii) gives us

\langle z^{*}_{n},x-x_{n}\rangle\geq\|x-x_{n}\|^{2}-\lambda^{3}_{n}\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\|x-x_{n}\|^{2}\right)\geq\|x-x_{n}\|^{2}-\lambda^{3}_{n}(1+\|x-x_{n}\|^{2}).

It follows from (
3.9
) that

\lambda^{-1}_{n}\Big(\|x-x_{n}\|^{2}-\lambda^{3}_{n}(1+\|x-x_{n}\|^{2})\Big)\leq\|A^{\circ}x\|\left\|x-x_{n}\right\|.

Multiplying both sides by
\lambda^{-1}_{n}
and then adding
\lambda_{n}
, we get

(1-\lambda^{3}_{n})(\lambda^{-1}_{n}\|x-x_{n}\|)^{2}\leq\|A^{\circ}x\|(\lambda^{-1}_{n}\|x-x_{n}\|)+\lambda_{n},

which yields

\lambda^{-1}_{n}\|x-x_{n}\|\leq\frac{\|A^{\circ}x\|+\sqrt{\|A^{\circ}x\|^{2}+4\lambda_{n}(1-\lambda^{3}_{n})}}{2(1-\lambda^{3}_{n})}

(3.10)

for sufficiently large
n
,
and hence

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim\sup}\,\,\lambda^{-1}_{n}\|x-x_{n}\|\leq\|A^{\circ}x\|.

(3.11)

Furthermore, by Lemma
3.2
(ii),

\|z^{*}_{n}\|\leq\|x-x_{n}\|+\sqrt{2}\lambda^{3}_{n},\,\,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Together with (
3.11
), we get

\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim\sup}\,\,\|\lambda^{-1}_{n}z^{*}_{n}\|\leq\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim\sup}\,\,(\lambda^{-1}_{n}\|x-x_{n}\|+\sqrt{2}\lambda^{2}_{n})\leq\|A^{\circ}x\|.

(3.12)

This shows that
(\lambda^{-1}_{n}z^{*}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded. Using Banach-Alaoglu theorem, there exist
y^{*}\in X^{*}
and a subnet
(\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}
of
(\lambda^{-1}_{n}z^{*}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying

\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i}\overset{\operatorname{w}^{*}}{\rightharpoonup}y^{*}.

(3.13)

It follows from (
3.12
) that

\|y^{*}\|\leq\underset{i}{\lim\inf}\,\|\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i}\|\leq\underset{i}{\lim\sup}\,\|\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i}\|\leq\|A^{\circ}x\|.

(3.14)

On the other hand, owing to (
3.10
),
x_{n}\to x\text{ as }n\to\infty
, and hence

x_{i}\to x.
It follows from (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) that
(\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}
is bounded and

\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i}\in Ax_{i},\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i}\overset{\operatorname{w}^{*}}{\rightharpoonup}y^{*},\,\,x_{i}\to x.

(3.15)

Then
y^{*}\in Ax
by the maximal
monotonicity of
A
. From (
3.14
), we get
y^{*}=A^{\circ}x
since
A^{\circ}x
is a singleton set due to the strict convexity of
X^{*}
. Hence, we obtain from (
3.14
) that
\|\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i}\|\to\|A^{\circ}x\|
, completing the proof.
∎

Recall that if
A
is a maximal monotone operator, then
Ax
is convex and closed in
X^{*}
for
x\in{D}(A)
. In order to represent
Ax
by means of the values at nearby points of
x
, we establish a relation between the values at nearby points of
x
and the faces
A(x;v)
of
Ax
with respect to vector
v\in X\setminus\{0\},
which is defined by

A(x;v):=\Big\{y^{*}\in Ax:\langle y^{*},v\rangle=\underset{x^{*}\in Ax}{\sup}\langle x^{*},v\rangle\Big\}.

This relation depends on the properties of
X
.
Next we introduce a new class of Banach spaces.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space.

(a)

X
is said to have the
w-Kadec-Klee property
if for any net
(x_{i})_{i\in I}
,
x_{i}\overset{\operatorname{w}}{\to}x
and
\|x_{i}\|\to\|x\|
, then
x_{i}\to x
.

(b)

X^{*}
is said to have the
w^{*}
-Kadec-Klee property
if for any net
(x^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}
,
x^{*}_{i}\overset{\operatorname{w}^{*}}{\rightharpoonup}x^{*}
and
\|x^{*}_{i}\|\to\|x^{*}\|
, then
x^{*}_{i}\to x^{*}
.

The following lemma shows that the class of Banach spaces with
w^{*}
-Kadec-Klee property
includes locally uniformly convex Banach spaces.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. Then,

(a) If
X
is locally uniformly convex, then
X
has w-Kadec-Klee property;

(b) If
X^{*}
is locally uniformly convex, then
X^{*}
has
w^{*}
-Kadec-Klee property.

See
[
21
, Proposition 3.7.6]
for a proof of (a). The proof of (b) is similar.

The next result discovers a relation between the faces of
Ax

and the set consisting of the weak
∗
convergent limits of bounded nets of
A
at nearby points of
x
.
In what follows
j_{\lambda}(w)
means an element of
J_{\lambda}(w)
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space whose dual space is strictly convex and let
A:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a maximal monotone operator of type (D). Then
for
x\in{D}(A)
and
v\in X\setminus\{0\}

A(x;v)\subset\operatorname{bw}^{*}{-}\underset{\lambda\to 0\atop\underset{j_{\lambda}(w)\overset{\operatorname{w}^{*}}{\rightharpoonup}J(v)}{\|j_{\lambda}(w)\|\to\|J(v)\|}}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,\,A(x+\lambda w).

(3.16)

If in addition that
X^{*}
has the
w^{*}
-Kadec-Klee property, then for
x\in{D}(A)
and
v\in X\setminus\{0\}

A(x;v)\subset\operatorname{s}-\underset{\lambda\to 0\atop j_{\lambda}(w)\to J(v)}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}A(x+\lambda w).

(3.17)

Proof.

Let
x\in{D}(A)
,
v\in X\setminus\{0\}
and
x^{*}\in A(x;v).
Since
X^{*}
is strictly convex,
J(v)
is a singleton set. We consider a maximal monotone operator defined by

By:=Ay-J(v)-x^{*},\,\,\forall\,y\in X.

It is obvious that
{D}(B)={D}(A)
, and hence
x\in{D}(B)
. We first claim that

B^{\circ}x=-J(v).

(3.18)

Indeed, observe that

-J(v)=x^{*}-J(v)-x^{*}\in Ax-J(v)-x^{*}=Bx,

(3.19)

Moreover, for
y^{*}\in Ax
,
\langle x^{*}-y^{*},v\rangle\geq 0
since
x^{*}\in A(x;v)
. This implies

\begin{split}\|-J(v)\|&=\|v\|^{-1}\langle J(v),v\rangle\\
&\leq\|v\|^{-1}\langle J(v)+x^{*}-y^{*},v\rangle\\
&\leq\|v\|^{-1}\|J(v)+x^{*}-y^{*}\|\|v\|\\
&=\|y^{*}-J(v)-x^{*}\|.\end{split}

Now (
3.18
) follows from (
3.19
).

Next, we apply Lemma
3.3
to have nets
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in I}\to 0
,
(x_{i})_{i\in I}\subset{D}(B)
with
x_{i}\to x
and
(z^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}\subset X^{*}
with
z^{*}_{i}\in J_{\lambda^{6}_{i}}(x-x_{i})
such that
(\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}
is bounded
and

\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i}\in Bx_{i},\,\,\,\,\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i}\overset{\operatorname{w}^{*}}{\rightharpoonup}B^{\circ}x=-J(v),\|\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i}\|\to\|J(v)\|.

Define
w_{i}:=\lambda^{-1}_{i}(x_{i}-x),i\in I
. We then have

(\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i}+J(v)+x^{*})_{i\in I}\,\,\text{ is bounded},

\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i}+J(v)+x^{*}\in Bx_{i}+J(v)+x^{*}=Ax_{i}=A(x+\lambda_{i}w_{i}),

(3.20)

\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i}+J(v)+x^{*}\overset{\operatorname{w}^{*}}{\rightharpoonup}x^{*},

(3.21)

-\lambda_{i}^{-1}z^{*}_{i}\overset{\operatorname{w}^{*}}{\rightharpoonup}J(v),\|-\lambda_{i}^{-1}z^{*}_{i}\|\to\|J(v)\|.

(3.22)

It follows from Lemma
3.2
(i) that

-\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i}\in J_{\lambda^{4}_{i}}(\lambda^{-1}_{i}(x_{i}-x))\subset J_{\lambda_{i}}(\lambda^{-1}_{i}(x_{i}-x))=J_{\lambda_{i}}(w_{i}).

(3.23)

Thus

x^{*}\in{\rm bw}^{*}-\underset{t\to 0\atop\underset{j_{t}(w)\overset{\operatorname{w}^{*}}{\rightharpoonup}J(v)}{\|j_{t}(w)\|\to\|J(v)\|}}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,\,A(x+tw),

and hence (
3.16
) holds.

Now, suppose in addition that
X^{*}
has the
\operatorname{w}^{*}
-Kadec-Klee property and
x^{*}\in A(x;v)
with
x\in{D}(A)
and
v\neq 0
. By the above result, we find nets
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in I}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+},

(x_{i})_{i\in I}\subset{D}(A)
,
(w_{i})_{i\in I}\subset X
and
(z^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}\in X^{*}
satisfying (
3.20
)-(
3.22
). It follows from (
3.22
) that
-\lambda_{i}^{-1}z^{*}_{i}\to J(v)
, and hence
\lambda^{-1}_{i}z^{*}_{i}+J(v)+x^{*}\to x^{*}.
This leads to

x^{*}\subset\operatorname{s}-\underset{t\to 0\atop j_{t}(w)\to J(v)}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}A(x+tw),

completing the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space whose dual space is strictly convex and let
A:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a maximal monotone operator of type (D).
Then, for
x\in{D}(A)
and
v\in X\setminus\{0\}

A(x;v)\subset{\rm bw}^{*}-\underset{y\to x,y\neq x}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,Ay.

(3.24)

If in addition that
X^{*}
has the
{w}^{*}
-Kadec-Klee property, then for
x\in{D}(A)
and
v\in X\setminus\{0\},

A(x;v)\subset\operatorname{s}-\underset{y\to x,y\neq x}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,Ay.

(3.25)

Proof.

Let
x\in{D}(A)
,
v\in X\setminus\{0\}
and
x^{*}\in A(x;v)
. By (
3.16
), there exist nets
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in I}\to 0,

(w_{i})_{i\in I}\subset X,

(z^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}\subset X^{*}
with
z^{*}_{i}\in J_{\lambda_{i}}(w_{i})
,
z^{*}_{i}\overset{\operatorname{w}^{*}}{\rightharpoonup}J(v),

\|z^{*}_{i}\|\to\|J(v)\|
,
(x^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}\subset X^{*}
with
x^{*}_{i}\in A(x+\lambda_{i}w_{i})
, such that

(x^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}\text{ is bounded and }x^{*}_{i}\overset{\operatorname{w}^{*}}{\rightharpoonup}x^{*}.

Next we verify that
x+\lambda_{i}w_{i}\to x
, which would imply

x^{*}\in{\rm bw}^{*}-\underset{y\to x,y\neq x}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,Ay

and hence (
3.24
) holds. By Lemma
3.2
(ii) and
z_{i}\in J_{\lambda_{i}}(w_{i}),
we have
\|w_{i}\|\leq\|z^{*}_{i}\|+\sqrt{2\lambda_{i}}
for
i\in I
. Therefore

\underset{i\in I}{\limsup}\|w_{i}\|\leq\underset{i\in I}{\sup}(\|z^{*}_{i}\|+\sqrt{2\lambda_{i}})=\|J(v)\|=\|v\|

implies
x+\lambda_{i}w_{i}\to x.

The same argument applies to yield (
3.25
).
∎

Now we are in a position to derive the first representation for the value of a maximal monotone operator of type (D) at a point in its domain.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space whose dual space is strictly convex, and let
A:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a maximal monotone operator of type (D).
Then for
x\in X

Ax=\operatorname{co}\overline{\{\operatorname{bw}^{*}-\underset{y\to x,y\neq x}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,Ay\}}+N_{\overline{{D}(A)}}(x).

(3.26)

If in addition that
X^{*}
has the
{w}^{*}
-Kadec-Klee property, then for
x\in X

Ax=\operatorname{co}\{\operatorname{s}-\underset{y\to x,y\neq x}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,Ay\}+N_{\overline{{D}(A)}}(x).

(3.27)

Proof.

Let
x\in X
and

K:=\operatorname{bw}^{*}-\underset{y\to x,y\neq x}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,Ay.

Owing to the maximal monotonicity of
A
,
K\subset Ax
and the fact that
Ax
is closed convex and satisfies
Ax=Ax+N_{\overline{{D}(A)}}(x)
, we deduce the inclusion

\operatorname{co}\overline{\{\operatorname{bw}^{*}-\underset{y\to x,y\neq x}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,Ay\}}+N_{\overline{{D}(A)}}(x)\subset Ax.

To justify the reverse inclusion, we will show the following two inclusions

Ax\subset\operatorname{co}(\operatorname{bd}Ax)+N_{\overline{{D}(A)}}(x)

(3.28)

and

\operatorname{bd}Ax\subset\overline{K}.

(3.29)

For the case
x\notin{D}(A)
, one has
K=Ax=\operatorname{bd}Ax=\varnothing
and so the above inclusions are trivial. Let
x\in{D}(A).
To clarify (
3.28
), we pick any
x^{*}\in Ax
. If
x^{*}\in\operatorname{bd}Ax
, then

x^{*}\in\operatorname{bd}Ax\subset\operatorname{co}(\operatorname{bd}Ax)+N_{\overline{{D}(A)}}(x).

(3.30)

If
x^{*}\in\operatorname{int}Ax
, we take
x^{*}_{0}\in\operatorname{bd}Ax
and define

\bar{\rho}:=\sup\big\{\rho>0:x^{*}+t(x^{*}-x^{*}_{0})\subset Ax,\forall t\in[0,\rho)\big\}>0.

We consider two cases of
\bar{\rho}
.

Case 1:

\bar{\rho}=+\infty
. It means that
x^{*}+t(x^{*}-x^{*}_{0})\in Ax
for every
t\geq 0
. Hence, for
(y,y^{*})\in{Gr}(A)
by the monotonicity of
A
, we have

\langle x^{*}+t(x^{*}-x^{*}_{0})-y^{*},y-x\rangle\leq 0,\forall t\geq 0.

This implies
\langle x^{*}-x^{*}_{0},y-x\rangle\leq 0
. Since this holds for any
y\in{D}(A),
we get
x^{*}-x^{*}_{0}\in N_{\overline{{D}(A)}}(x)
, which implies that

x^{*}\in x^{*}_{0}+N_{\overline{{D}(A)}}(x)\subset\operatorname{co}(\operatorname{bd}Ax)+N_{\overline{{D}(A)}}(x).

Case 2:

\bar{\rho}<+\infty.
Since
Ax
is closed,
z^{*}:=x^{*}+\bar{\rho}(x^{*}-x^{*}_{0})\in Ax.
Furthermore,
z^{*}\in\operatorname{bd}Ax
by the definition of
\bar{\rho}
. Therefore

x^{*}=\frac{1}{1+\bar{\rho}}z^{*}+\frac{\bar{\rho}}{1+\bar{\rho}}x^{*}_{0}\in\operatorname{co}(\operatorname{bd}Ax)\subset\operatorname{co}(\operatorname{bd}Ax)+N_{\overline{{D}(A)}}(x).

Combining the results above, we conclude that (
3.28
) holds.

Finally, let
x^{*}\in\operatorname{bd}Ax
. According to
[
15
, Theorem 1]
, since
Ax
is weak
∗
closed convex, there exists a sequence
(x^{*}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\operatorname{bd}Ax
such that
x^{*}_{n}\to x^{*}
, where
x^{*}_{n}
are weak
∗
support points of
Ax
, i.e., for each
x^{*}_{n}
, there exists
v_{n}\in X\setminus\{0\}
such that

\langle x^{*}_{n},v_{n}\rangle=\sigma_{Ax}(v_{n}).

The equality means that
x^{*}_{n}\in A(x;v_{n})
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. By Corollary
3.1
,
x^{*}_{n}\in K
, which implies
x^{*}\in\overline{K}
. Hence we get (
3.29
) and the proof of (
3.26
) is complete.

Now suppose in addition that
X^{*}
has the
\operatorname{w}^{*}
-Kadec-Klee property. The proof of (
3.27
) is similar to that of (
3.26
), where
K
is replaced by the closed set
K^{\prime}:=\operatorname{s}-\underset{y\to x,y\neq x}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,Ay.

∎

Remark 3.1
.

With the setting in the previous theorem, for
x\in X

Ax=\operatorname{co}\,\overline{\{\operatorname{bw}^{*}-\underset{y\to x,y\neq x}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,Ay\}}+N_{\overline{{D}(A)}}(x)\subset\operatorname{co}\overline{\{\operatorname{w}^{*}-\underset{y\to x,y\neq x}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,Ay\}}+N_{\overline{{D}(A)}}(x).

(3.31)

It is worth noting that in the case that
X
is a Hilbert space, the above inclusion is still strict; see
[
3
, Example 1]
. Furthermore, the inclusion becomes equality if the nets are replaced by sequences, and by
[
11
, Theorem 3.3]

Ax=\operatorname{co}{\{\operatorname{ss}-\underset{y\to x,y\neq x}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,Ay\}}+N_{\overline{{D}(A)}}(x),

where

\displaystyle{\operatorname{ss}-}\underset{y\to x,y\neq x}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,Ay=\Big\{x^{*}\in X^{*}:

\displaystyle\exists\,(x_{n},x^{*}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\in{Gr}(A)\text{ with }x_{n}\neq x,\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

\displaystyle x_{n}\to x,\,x^{*}_{n}\to x^{*}\Big\}.

The next theorem is a representation for maximal monotone operators in reflexive spaces.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X
be a reflexive Banach space and let
A:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a maximal monotone operator. Then for
x\in X

Ax=\operatorname{co}\{\operatorname{s}-\underset{y\to x,y\neq x}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,Ay\}+N_{\overline{{D}(A)}}(x).

Proof.

Let
x\in X
. We first observe that the maximal monotonicity of
A
and the terms

\operatorname{co}\{\operatorname{s}-\underset{y\to x,y\neq x}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,Ay\},N_{\overline{{D}(A)}}(x)

are not affected by equivalently renorming of
X
and
X^{*}.
By Troyanski’s renorming theorem (
[
8
, Theorem 5.192]
),
X
admits an equivalent norm so that both
X
and
X^{*}
are locally uniformly convex. Therefore, we may assume that both
X
and
X^{*}
are locally uniformly convex. Then
X^{*}
has the
\operatorname{w}^{*}
-Kadec-Klee property due to Lemma
3.4
. Since the space
X
is reflexive, the operator
A
is of type (D), owing to
[
18
, Proposition 1]
. Using the second part of Theorem
3.1
, we derive the conclusion of the theorem.
∎

The next result is a representation formula for the subdifferentials of a lower semicontinuous convex function.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
X
be a Banach space whose dual space is strictly convex and let
f:X\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a lower semicontinuous function. Then for
x\in X

\partial f(x)=\operatorname{co}\overline{\{\operatorname{bw}^{*}-\,\,\underset{y\to x,y\neq x}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,\partial f(y)\}}+N_{\overline{\operatorname{dom}f}}(x).

(3.32)

If in addition that
X^{*}
has the
{w}^{*}
-Kadec-Klee property, then for
x\in X

\partial f(x)=\operatorname{co}\{\operatorname{s}-\,\,\underset{y\to x,y\neq x}{\operatorname{Lim}\sup}\,\partial f(y)\}+N_{\overline{\operatorname{dom}f}}(x).

(3.33)

Proof.

Using
[
10
, Lemma 1]
and
[
17
, Corollary 2]
one has

{R}(\lambda J_{\varepsilon}(\cdot-x)+\partial f)=X^{*},\forall x\in X,\forall\lambda,\varepsilon>0.

Now the theorem follows from Theorem
3.1
.
∎

4
A representation formula for the support functions of the values of maximal monotone operators

In this section we consider a maximal monotone operator
A
of type (D) in a Banach space which has the w-Kadec-Klee property and whose dual space has the w
∗
-Kadec-Klee property. We provide an explicit formula for the support function of
Ax
by use of the minimal-norm selection of
Ay
for nearby points
y
of
x
.

We first present a technical result for the theorem below.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space such that
X^{*},X^{**}
are strictly convex and
X
has the w-Kadec-Klee property,
X^{*}
has the
w^{*}
-Kadec-Klee property. Let
v\in X\setminus\{0\}
,
(t_{i})_{i\in I}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
with
t_{i}\to 0
and
(w_{i})_{i\in I}\subset X
. If there exists a net
(z^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}\subset X^{*}
with
z^{*}_{i}\in J_{t_{i}}(w_{i})
,

z^{*}_{i}\to J(v)
, then there exists a subnet of
(w_{i})_{i\in I}
converging to
v
.

Proof.

Assume that
(z^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}\subset X^{*}
with
z^{*}_{i}\in J_{t_{i}}(w_{i})
converging to
J(v)
.
According to
[
5
, p.608]
, for every
i\in I
, there exists
v_{i}\in X
such that

\|w_{i}-v_{i}\|\leq\sqrt{t_{i}},\|z^{*}_{i}-J(v_{i})\|\leq\sqrt{t_{i}}.

(4.1)

This implies
J(v_{i})\to J(v),
and hence
\|v_{i}\|\to\|v\|
, which shows that
(v_{i})_{i\in I}\subset X\subset X^{**}
is eventually bounded. Using Banach-Alaoglu theorem, by passing to a subnet, if necessary, that
v_{i}\overset{\operatorname{w}}{\to}z^{**}\in X^{**}
. Furthermore, since
X^{**}
is strictly convex, we have
v_{i}=J^{*}(J(v_{i}))
and
v=J^{*}(J(v))
. By the maximal monotonicity of
J^{*}
, we deduce
z^{**}=J^{*}(J(v))
and hence

\|v_{i}\|\to\|v\|,\,\,\text{ and }\,\,v_{i}\overset{\operatorname{w}}{\rightarrow}v.

Owing to the assumption that
X
has the
\operatorname{w}
-Kadec-Klee property, we get
v_{i}\to v
. It follows from (
4.1
) that
w_{i}\to v
, which completes the proof.
∎

The following theorem represents the support function of the value
Ax
via its minimal-norm selections.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space such that
X^{*},X^{**}
are strictly convex and
X
has the w-Kadec-Klee property,
X^{*}
has the
w^{*}
-Kadec-Klee property. Let
A:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a maximal monotone operator of type (D). Then for
x\in{D}(A),\,v\in X\setminus\{0\}

\sigma_{Ax}(v)=\underset{t\downarrow 0\atop w\to v}{\lim\inf}\langle A^{\circ}(x+tw),w\rangle.

(4.2)

Proof.

Let
x\in{D}(A)
and
v\in X\setminus\{0\}
. We first show that

\sigma_{Ax}(v)\leq\underset{t\downarrow 0\atop w\to v}{\lim\inf}\langle A^{\circ}(x+tw),w\rangle.

(4.3)

Indeed, by the monotonicity of
A
, for
x^{*}\in Ax
we have

\langle x^{*},w\rangle=\frac{1}{t}\langle x^{*},(x+tw)-x\rangle\leq\langle A^{\circ}(x+tw),w\rangle

for all
t\geq 0,w\in X\setminus\{0\}
,
with the convention
\langle A^{\circ}(x+tw),w\rangle=+\infty
if
x+tw\notin{D}(A)
. It follows that

\langle x^{*},v\rangle\leq\underset{t\downarrow 0\atop w\to v}{\lim\inf}\langle A^{\circ}(x+tw),w\rangle.

Since this holds for any
x^{*}\in Ax
, we get inequality (
4.3
). It remains to show

\sigma_{Ax}(v)\geq\underset{t\downarrow 0\atop w\to v}{\lim\inf}\,\langle A^{\circ}(x+tw),w\rangle.

(4.4)

This obviously holds for
\sigma_{Ax}(v)=+\infty
. If
\sigma_{Ax}(v)<+\infty
, then
v\in\operatorname{dom}(\sigma_{Ax})\subset\overline{{D}(\partial\sigma_{Ax})}
. We consider two cases of
v
as follows.

Case 1:

v\in D(\partial\sigma_{Ax})
. Pick any
x^{*}\in\partial\sigma_{Ax}(v).
Involving (
4.2
) that
\partial\sigma_{Ax}(v)=A(x;v)
, we have
x^{*}\in A(x;v)
.
According to Proposition
3.1
, we can find nets
(t_{i})_{i\in I}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
(w_{i})_{i\in I}\subset X
and
(z^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}\subset X^{*},(x^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}\subset X^{*}
with
t_{i}\downarrow 0,

z^{*}_{i}\in J_{t_{i}}(w_{i})
, and
x^{*}_{i}\in A(x+t_{i}w_{i})
such that

z^{*}_{i}\to J(v),\,\,\,\,x^{*}_{i}\to x^{*}.

(4.5)

On one hand, since
x^{*}_{i}\to x^{*}
,
(x^{*}_{i})_{i\in I}
is eventually bounded. Hence
(A^{\circ}(x+t_{i}w_{i}))_{i}
is also eventually bounded due to
\|A^{\circ}(x+t_{i}w_{i})\|\leq\|x^{*}_{i}\|
for all
i\in I
.
By Banach-Alaoglu theorem, passing to the limit
i\to\infty

along a subnet, one has

A^{\circ}(x+t_{i}w_{i})\overset{\operatorname{w}^{*}}{\rightharpoonup}z^{*}

(4.6)

for some
z^{*}\in X^{*}
. Note that
(w_{i})_{i}
is eventually bounded since
\|w_{i}\|\leq\|z^{*}_{i}\|+\sqrt{2t_{i}},\forall i\in I
and
\|z^{*}_{i}\|\to\|J(v)\|=\|v\|
. This gives
x+t_{i}w_{i}\to x
. It follows from the maximal monotonicity of
A
, the eventual boundedness of
(A^{\circ}(x+t_{i}w_{i}))_{i}
and (
4.6
) that
z^{*}\in Ax.
This yields

\sigma_{Ax}(v)\geq\langle z^{*},v\rangle.

(4.7)

On the other hand, using Lemma
4.1
, we may suppose, by passing to a subnet
if necessary, that
w_{i}\to v.

Owing to the fact that
(A^{\circ}(x+t_{i}w_{i}))_{i\in I}
is eventually bounded and (
4.6
), we get

\langle A^{\circ}(x+t_{i}w_{i}),w_{i}\rangle\to\langle z^{*},v\rangle.

Together with (
4.7
), we derive

\sigma_{Ax}(v)\geq\underset{i}{\lim}\,\langle A^{\circ}(x+t_{i}w_{i}),w_{i}\rangle\geq\underset{t\to 0\atop j_{t}(w)\to J(v)}{\lim\inf}\langle A^{\circ}(x+tw),w\rangle.

(4.8)

It follows from Lemma
4.1
that

\underset{t\to 0\atop j_{t}(w)\to J(v)}{\lim\inf}\langle A^{\circ}(x+tw),w\rangle\geq\underset{t\to 0\atop w\to v}{\lim\inf}\langle A^{\circ}(x+tw),w\rangle.

(4.9)

Combining (
4.8
) and (
4.9
), we obtain (
4.4
).

Case 2:

v\in\operatorname{dom}\sigma_{Ax}\setminus\operatorname{D}(\partial\sigma_{Ax})
. This means that
\partial\sigma_{Ax}(v)=\varnothing.
According to
[
5
, Theorem 2]
, there exists
v_{n}\to v
such that

\sigma_{Ax}(v_{n})\to\sigma_{Ax}(v)
and
\partial\sigma_{Ax}(v_{n})\neq\varnothing.
From Case 1, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}

\sigma_{Ax}(v_{n})=\underset{t\downarrow 0,\atop w\to v_{n}}{\lim\inf}\langle A^{\circ}(x+tw),w\rangle.

Hence, for every
n
there exist
t_{n}>0,w_{n}\in X
such that

t_{n}\leq\frac{1}{n},\,\,\|w_{n}-v_{n}\|\leq\frac{1}{n},\,\,|\langle A^{\circ}(x+t_{n}w_{n}),w_{n}\rangle-\sigma_{Ax}(v_{n})|\leq\frac{1}{n}.

This implies that
t_{n}\to 0,w_{n}\to v
and
\underset{n\to\infty}{\lim}\langle A^{\circ}(x+t_{n}w_{n}),w_{n}\rangle=\sigma_{Ax}(v)
. Hence we get (
4.4
). The proof is complete.
∎

The next result follows directly from Lemma
3.4
and the previous theorem.

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space such that
X,X^{*}
are locally uniformly convex and
X^{**}
is strictly convex and let
A:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be a maximal monotone operator of type (D).
Then for
x\in{D}(A)
and
v\in X\setminus\{0\}

\sigma_{Ax}(v)=\underset{t\downarrow 0,\atop w\to v}{\lim\inf}\,\langle A^{\circ}(x+tw),w\rangle.

(4.10)

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
X
be a Banach space such that
X^{*},X^{**}
are strictly convex and
X
has the w-Kadec-Klee property,
X^{*}
has the
w^{*}
-Kadec-Klee property. Let
A_{1},A_{2}:X\rightrightarrows X^{*}
be maximal monotone operators of type (D). If
{D}(A_{1})={D}(A_{2})=:D
and
A^{\circ}_{1}x\in A_{2}x
for all
x\in D,

then
A_{1}=A_{2}.

Proof.

We first show that for
x\in D
and
v\in X\setminus\{0\}

\sigma_{A_{1}x}(v)\geq\sigma_{A_{2}x}(v),

(4.11)

which would imply
A_{2}x\subset A_{1}x
for
x\in{D}
. This is trivial for
\sigma_{A_{1}x}=+\infty
. We consider
\sigma_{A_{1}x}(v)<+\infty
. According to Theorem
4.1
, there exist nets
(t_{i})_{i\in I}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+},(w_{i})_{i\in I}\in X
with
t_{i}\to 0
and
w_{i}\to v
such that

\sigma_{A_{1}x}(v)=\underset{i}{\lim}\langle A^{\circ}_{1}(x+t_{i}w_{i}),w_{i}\rangle.

(4.12)

By assumption,
A^{\circ}_{1}(x+t_{i}w_{i})\subset A_{2}(x+t_{i}w_{i})
for all
i\in I
. The monotonicity of
A_{2}
implies that

\sigma_{A_{2}x}(w_{i})\leq\langle A^{\circ}_{1}(x+t_{i}w_{i}),w_{i}\rangle\ \ \mbox{for all }i\in I.

Combining this with the lower semicontinuity of
\sigma_{A_{2}x}
and (
4.12
), we obtain

\sigma_{A_{2}x}(v)\leq\underset{i}{\lim}\,\sigma_{A_{2}x}(w_{i})\leq\underset{i}{\lim}\langle A^{\circ}_{1}(x+t_{i}w_{i}),w_{i}\rangle\leq\sigma_{A_{1}x}(v),

which means that (
4.11
) holds. This yields
A^{\circ}_{2}x\in A_{2}x\subset A_{1}x
for all
x\in D.
Similarly, we have
A_{1}x\subset A_{2}x
for every
x\in D
. Therefore
A_{1}=A_{2}
, as was to be shown.∎

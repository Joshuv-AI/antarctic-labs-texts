Global well-posedness for fractional Sobolev-Galpern type equations
Huy Tuan Nguyen, Nguyen Anh Tuan, Chao Yang

Abstract
This article is a comparative study on an initial-boundary value problem for a class of semilinear pseudo-parabolic equations with the fractional Caputo derivative, also called the fractional Sobolev-Galpern type equations. The purpose of this work is to reveal the influence of the degree of the source nonlinearity on the well-posedness of the solution. By considering four different types of nonlinearities, we derive the global well-posedness of mild solutions to the problem corresponding to the four cases of the nonlinear source terms. For the advection source function case, we apply a nontrivial limit technique for singular integral and some appropriate choices of weighted Banach space to prove the global existence result. For the gradient nonlinearity as a local Lipschitzian, we use the Cauchy sequence technique to show that the solution either exists globally in time or blows up at finite time. For the polynomial form nonlinearity, by assuming the smallness of the initial data we derive the global well-posed results. And for the case of exponential nonlinearity in two-dimensional space, we derive the global well-posedness by additionally use of Orlicz space. Keywords: fractional pseudo-parabolic, globally Lipschitz source, exponential nonlinearity, global well-posedness. 2020 MSC Classification: 35K20, 35K58.

2.1
Basic materials

We first establish some functional space concepts. Suppose that

\left\|\cdot\right\|_{X}

\displaystyle\left\|w\right\|_{C(I\rightarrow X)}:=\sup_{t\in I}\big\|w(t)\big\|_{X}<\infty.

Suppose that

is a Young function, i.e, a convex function which is even, continuous on

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{z\to\infty}\frac{\Xi(z)}{z}=\infty\qquad\text{and}\qquad\lim\limits_{z\to 0}\frac{\Xi(z)}{z}=0.

Then, we define the Orlicz space

as the space of all measurable functions

such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Xi\left(\frac{\left|w(x)\right|}{\kappa}\right){\mathrm{d}}x<\infty\quad\text{for some }\kappa>0.

The space

is a Banach space with respect to the Luxemburg norm

\displaystyle\left\|w\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}:=\inf\left\{\kappa\in\mathbb{R}~\Big|~\kappa>0,\int_{\Omega}\Xi\left(\frac{\left|w(x)\right|}{\kappa}\right){\mathrm{d}}x\leq 1\right\}.

Remark 2.1
.

Note that, we can use the framework of Orlicz space to cover the definitions of some well-known Lebesgue spaces as below

is the usual Lebesgue space

Lemma 2.1

2.2
Mild solution

For purpose of formulating a mild solution of Problem (
1
)-(
3
), the following lemma on the Laplace transform of the Caputo derivative operator plays an important role.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that the Laplace transform and the derivative of order
\alpha
in the Caputo sense of a function
w
exist. Then, the equation below holds

\displaystyle\widetilde{\partial_{t}^{\alpha}w}(s)=\mathcal{L}\{\partial_{t}^{\alpha}w\}(s)=s^{\alpha}\widetilde{w}(s)-s^{\alpha-1}w(0),

where
\mathcal{L}
stands for the Laplace transform operator.

Integrating the both sides of Equation (
1
) with an arbitrary function
\phi_{k}
and then applying the Laplace transform, we have

\displaystyle s^{\alpha}\int_{\Omega}[I+\mathcal{A}]\widetilde{u}({z},s)\phi_{k}({z})\mathrm{d}{z}+\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{A}\widetilde{u}({z},s)\phi_{k}({z})\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s^{\alpha-1}\int_{\Omega}[I+\mathcal{A}]u_{0}({z})\phi_{k}({z})\mathrm{d}{z}+\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{H(u)}({z},s)\phi_{k}({z})\mathrm{d}{z}.

This equation is equivalent to

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{u}({z},s)\phi_{k}({z})\mathrm{d}{z}=

\displaystyle\frac{s^{\alpha-1}(1+\theta_{k})}{s^{\alpha}(1+\theta_{k})+\theta_{k}}\int_{\Omega}u_{0}({z})\phi_{k}({z})\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{s^{\alpha}(1+\theta_{k})+\theta_{k}}\int_{\Omega}\widetilde{H(u)}({z},s)\phi_{k}({z})\mathrm{d}{z}.

Then by the inverse Laplace transform, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}u({z},t)\phi_{k}({z})\mathrm{d}{z}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}E_{\alpha}\left(\frac{-\theta_{k}t^{\alpha}}{1+\theta_{k}}\right)u_{0}({z})\phi_{k}({z})\mathrm{d}{z}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\frac{(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}}{1+\theta_{k}}E_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(\frac{-\theta_{k}(t-\tau)^{\alpha}}{1+\theta_{k}}\right)H(u({z},\tau))\phi_{k}({z})\mathrm{d}{z}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Since
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
, we can formulate the mild solution to Problem
(
1
)-(
3
)
in the following way

\displaystyle u(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}G_{1}(x,{z},t)u_{0}({z})\mathrm{d}{z}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{2}(x,{z},t-\tau)H(u({z},\tau))\mathrm{d}{z}\mathrm{d}\tau,

where, the Green kernels
G_{1},G_{2}
are given by

\displaystyle\qquad G_{1}(x,{z},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}E_{\alpha}\left(\frac{-\theta_{k}t^{\alpha}}{1+\theta_{k}}\right)\phi_{k}(x)\phi_{k}({z})

and

\displaystyle\qquad G_{2}(x,{z},t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{\alpha-1}}{1+\theta_{k}}E_{\alpha,\alpha}\left(\frac{-\theta_{k}t^{\alpha}}{1+\theta_{k}}\right)\phi_{k}(x)\phi_{k}({z}).

For purpose of simplifying notations, we set

\displaystyle\mathscr{S}(t)w(x):=\int_{\Omega}G_{1}(x,{z},t)w({z})\mathrm{d}{z}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{R}(t)w(x):=\int_{\Omega}G_{2}(x,{z},t)w(z)\mathrm{d}{z}.

Then, we can rewrite the formula for the mild solution in the following way

\displaystyle u(x,t)=\mathscr{S}(t)u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(u(x,\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau.

From the standpoint of this formulation concept, we derive some fundamental linear estimate through the following lemma

Lemma 2.5
.

Let

(

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\theta^{\nu}_{k}\left(\frac{\mathcal{M}}{1+\frac{\theta_{k}t^{\alpha}}{1+\theta_{k}}}\right)^{2}\big\langle w,\phi_{k}\big\rangle^{2}

\mu=1

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\theta^{\nu}_{k}\left(E_{\alpha}\left(\frac{-\theta_{k}t^{\alpha}}{1+\theta_{k}}\right)\right)^{2}\big\langle w,\phi_{k}\big\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{2\mathcal{M}^{2}\theta^{\nu}_{k}(1+\theta_{k}^{2})}{\theta_{k}^{2}t^{2\alpha}}\big\langle w,\phi_{k}\big\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mathcal{M}^{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{\theta^{2}_{1}}\right)t^{-2\alpha}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}.

Case 3:

\displaystyle\big\|\mathscr{S}(t)w\big\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\theta_{k}^{\nu}\left(E_{\alpha}\left(\frac{-\theta_{k}t^{\alpha}}{1+\theta_{k}}\right)\right)^{2}\big\langle w,\phi_{k}\big\rangle^{2}.

3.1
The time-fractional pseudo-parabolic equation with advection term

Throughout the current subsection, we assume that
F
is the vector
0
in
H(u)=(\eta\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla\cdot F(u)
. Then,

becomes a globally Lipschitz source function. Indeed, by Cauchy–Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\eta_{j}u_{x_{j}}(x)\right)^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x\leq|\eta|^{2}\int_{\Omega}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}u^{2}_{x_{j}}(x)\right){\mathrm{d}}x=|\eta|^{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}{\mathrm{d}}x.

It means that we can find a positive constant

independent of

(7)

\displaystyle\big\|H(w)-H(v)\big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\mathscr{C}_{1}\left\|w-v\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}.

Our main principle is the successive approximation in some reasonable Banach spaces. To this end, for any
a,\sigma>0
we denote by

\displaystyle\bullet~\sup_{t\in I\setminus\{0\}}t^{\kappa}e^{-\sigma t}\left\|w(t)\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}<\infty,

inductively in the following way

\displaystyle w_{1}(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\mathscr{S}(t)u_{0}(x),

\displaystyle w_{n+1}(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\mathscr{S}(t)u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(x,\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau.

Besides, we introduce some necessary lemmas to help us present the main points of the proof more clearly.

Lemma 3.1
.

(

Lemma 3.2
.

3.2
The non-trivial case of the vector field

In this subsection, we assume that the vector field

\displaystyle\Big\|\big|\nabla u\big|u\Big\|_{D^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq 2^{\frac{2d+2}{d+2}}C_{\Omega}\big\|u\big\|^{2}_{D^{1}(\Omega)}.

such that for any

\displaystyle\Big\|\nabla\cdot F(u)-\nabla\cdot F(v)\Big\|_{D^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq C(\Omega,d)\left(\big\|u\big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}+\big\|v\big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\right)\left\|u-v\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}.

In addition, from (
7
) and the fact that

, for any

, there exists a positive constant

such that the following estimate holds

(14)

\displaystyle\big\|H\left(u\right)-H\left(v\right)\big\|_{D^{-1}(\Omega)}\leq\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}\left(\big\|u\big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}+\big\|v\big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}+1\right)\left\|u-v\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}

The main aim of this part is to investigate the well-posed results that either the mild solution exists globally or blows up in finite maximal time.

Theorem 3.5
.

(Local existence)
Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
. If
u_{0}\in D^{1}(\Omega)
, Problem (
1
)-(
3
) posesses a locally unique mild solution.

Proof.

Thanks to the assumption
u_{0}\in D^{1}(\Omega)
, Lemma
2.5
(i)
implies

(15)

\displaystyle\big\|\mathscr{S}(t)u_{0}\big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}.

For any
w,v\in C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))
, from Lemma
2.5
(ii)
and inequality (
14
) we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\big\|\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)\Big(H(w(\tau))-H(v(\tau))\Big)\big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\left\|H(w(\tau))-H(v(\tau))\right\|_{D^{-1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}C_{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\left(\left\|w(\tau)\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\|v(\tau)\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}+1\right)\left\|w(\tau)-v(\tau)\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau.

This implies that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\big\|\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)\Big(H(w(\tau))-H(v(\tau))\Big)\big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau

(16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}C_{2}\alpha^{-1}\mathscr{T}^{\alpha}\Bigg(\left\|w\right\|_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))}+\left\|v\right\|_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))}+1\Bigg)\left\|w-v\right\|_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))}.

Then, if we consider the Picard sequence

\displaystyle w_{1}(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\mathscr{S}(t)u_{0}(x),

\displaystyle w_{n+1}(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\mathscr{S}(t)u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(x,\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau,

Inequality (
15
) immediately shows that
w_{1}\in C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))
. Besides, for
w_{n}\in C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))
, Estimate (
3.2
) gives us the following result

\displaystyle\left\|w_{n+1}(\tau)-w_{1}(\tau)\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\big\|\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(\tau))\big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}C_{2}\alpha^{-1}\mathscr{T}^{\alpha}\Big(\left\|w_{n}\right\|^{2}_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))}+\left\|w_{n}\right\|_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))}\Big),

i.e.,

\displaystyle\left\|w_{n+1}-w_{1}\right\|_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))}\leq\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}C_{2}\alpha^{-1}\mathscr{T}^{\alpha}\Big(\left\|w_{n}\right\|^{2}_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))}+\left\|w_{n}\right\|_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))}\Big).

Then, we can conclude that
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega)).
Furthermore, if
\mathscr{T}
is small enough, Estimate (
3.2
) infers for
w_{n-1},w_{n},w_{n+1}\in C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))
that

\displaystyle\left\|w_{n+1}-w_{n}\right\|_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))}\leq\frac{3}{4}\left\|w_{n}-w_{n-1}\right\|_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))}.

It means
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))
. Consequently, there exists a locally unique mild solution
u
of Problem (
1
)-(
3
) in
C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))
.
∎

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
u_{0}
be in
D^{1}(\Omega)
. Suppose that
u
is a unique mild solution to Problem (
1
)-(
3
) on
[0,\mathscr{T}]
. Then this solution can be extended to
[0,\mathscr{T}+T],
for some
T>0.

Proof.

Assume that
u
is the unique mild solution of Problem (
1
)-(
3
) on
[0,\mathscr{T}]
and
I=[0,\mathscr{T}+T]
, where
T>0
will be chosen later. For a constant
R>0
, we consider the space below

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}:=\left\{w\in C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))\quad\Biggl|\begin{array}[]{*{11}{ll}}&w(t,\cdot)=u(t,\cdot),&\forall t\in\left[0,\mathscr{T}\right],\\
&\left\|w(t,\cdot)-u(T,\cdot)\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\leq R,&\forall t\in\left[\mathscr{T},\mathscr{T}+T\right]\end{array}\right\}.

We aim to to show that Problem (
1
)-(
3
) has a unique mild solution that belongs to
\mathbb{E}
, then it is the extension of
u
to
I
. To this end, we take an element
\overline{w}
of
\mathbb{E}
and define the sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
as

\displaystyle w_{1}(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\mathscr{S}(t)u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(\overline{w}(x,\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau,

\displaystyle w_{n+1}(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\mathscr{S}(t)u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(x,\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau.

We can check easily for every
t\in[0,\mathscr{T}]
, that

\displaystyle w_{1}(x,t)=w_{2}(x,t)=...=w_{n}(x,t)=u(x,t),\quad\forall x\in\Omega.

On the contrary, when
t\in[\mathscr{T},\mathscr{T}+T]
, a simple calculation shows that

\displaystyle\left\|u_{1}(t)-u(\mathscr{T})\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big\|\mathscr{S}(t)u_{0}-\mathscr{S}(\mathscr{T})u_{0}\big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathscr{T}}^{t}\big\|\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(x,\tau))\big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\mathscr{T}}\big\|\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(x,\tau))-\mathscr{R}(\mathscr{T}-\tau)H(w_{n}(x,\tau))\big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Similar to Lemma
3.2
, we derive

\displaystyle\big\|\mathscr{S}(t)u_{0}-\mathscr{S}(\mathscr{T})u_{0}\big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\alpha^{-1}\mathcal{M}(t-\mathscr{T})^{\alpha}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\alpha^{-1}\mathcal{M}T^{\alpha}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\mathscr{T}}\big\|\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(x,\tau))-\mathscr{R}(\mathscr{T}-\tau)H(w_{n}(x,\tau))\big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{M}\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}C_{2}\big|t^{\alpha}-(t-\mathscr{T})^{\alpha}-\mathscr{T}^{\alpha}\big|}{\alpha(1-\alpha)\theta^{\frac{1}{2}}_{1}}\Big(\left\|w_{n}\right\|^{2}_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))}+\left\|w_{n}\right\|_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))}\Big).

In addition, the following estimate also holds

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{T}}^{t}\left\|\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(x,\tau))\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}C_{2}\int_{\mathscr{T}}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\Big(\left\|w_{n}\right\|^{2}_{D^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\|w_{n}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\Big)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}C_{2}(t-\mathscr{T})^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\Big(\left\|w_{n}\right\|^{2}_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))}+\left\|w_{n}\right\|_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}C_{2}T^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\Big(\left\|w_{n}\right\|^{2}_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))}+\left\|w_{n}\right\|_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))}\Big).

From the above estimates, by taking
T
small enough, one has

\displaystyle\left\|u_{1}(t)-u(\mathscr{T})\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\leq R.

It follows that
w_{1}
is in
\mathbb{E}
. The same arguments show that if
w_{n}\in\mathbb{E}
for
n\geq 1
, then
w_{n+1}
also belongs to
\mathbb{E}
. To complete the proof, we have to show that
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{E}
. Indeed, for
w_{n-1},w_{n}\in\mathbb{E}
and
t\geq\mathscr{T}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|w_{n+1}(t)-w_{n}(t)\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{T}}^{t}\big\|\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(\tau))-w_{n-1}(x,\tau)\big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{T}}^{t}\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}C_{2}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\Bigg(\left\|w_{n-1}(\tau)\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\|w_{n}(\tau)\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}+1\Bigg)\left\|w_{n}(\tau)-w_{n-1}(\tau)\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}C_{2}T^{\alpha}\alpha^{-1}\Bigg(\left(\left\|u(\mathscr{T})\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}+R\right)+1\Bigg)\left\|w_{n}-w_{n-1}\right\|_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))},

provided that for any
w\in\mathbb{E}
, by the triangle inequality, the following estimate holds for any
t\in[\mathscr{T},\mathscr{T}+T]

\displaystyle\left\|w(t)\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\left\|u(\mathscr{T})\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}+R.

Hence, if
T
is sufficiently small, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|w_{n+1}-w_{n}\right\|_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))}\leq\frac{3}{4}\left\|w_{n}-w_{n-1}\right\|_{C(I\to D^{1}(\Omega))},

the same arguments as (
12
) show that
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{E}
. As we said before,
\mathbb{E}
is a Banach space, then we can find a limit function of
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
which is the unique mild solution to Problem (
1
)-(
3
) on
[0,\mathscr{T}+T].
The proof is completed.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

(Global and finite time blowup solution)
Let
u_{0}
be in
D^{1}(\Omega)
and
u
be the unique mild solution to Problem (
1
)-(
3
). If we define

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{\max}:=\sup\Big\{\mathscr{T}>0~\big|~u\text{ exists uniquely on }[0,\mathscr{T}]\Big\},

then either
\mathscr{T}_{\max}=\infty
or

\lim\limits_{t\to\mathscr{T}_{\max}^{-}}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}=\infty
.

Proof.

Assume that the maximal time point
\mathscr{T}_{\max}
is finite and
I=[0,\mathscr{T})
. Let us make a contradict assumption that there exists a finite constant
\mathscr{M}
such that

(17)

\displaystyle\left\|u(t)\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\mathscr{M},\quad\forall t\in[0,\mathscr{T}_{\max}).

Let
\{t_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence in
I
satisfying
t_{n}\xrightarrow{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathscr{T}_{\max}
. We aim to show that
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}=\{u(t_{n})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
D^{1}(\Omega)
. In fact, for
t_{n}<t_{m}
, we find that

\displaystyle\left\|u_{m}-u_{n}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big\|\mathscr{S}(t_{m})u_{0}-\mathscr{S}(t_{n})u_{0}\big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{m}}\big\|\mathscr{R}(t_{m}-\tau)H(u(\tau))\big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\big\|\mathscr{R}(t_{m}-\tau)H(u(\tau))-\mathscr{R}(t_{n}-\tau)H(u(\tau))\big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau=:\sum_{j\in\{1,2,3\}}\mathcal{I}_{j}.

On one hand, since
u_{0}
is in
D^{1}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}\leq\alpha^{-1}\mathcal{M}(t_{m}-t_{n})^{\alpha}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\alpha^{-1}\mathcal{M}\Big((t_{m}-\mathscr{T}_{\max})^{\alpha}+(\mathscr{T}_{\max}-t_{n})^{\alpha}\Big)\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}.

Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we can find a number
n_{1}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{1}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3},\quad\forall m,n\geq n_{1}.

On the other hand, the use of Lemma
2.5
(ii)
shows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{m}}(t_{m}-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\big\|H(u(\tau))\big\|_{D^{-1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}C_{2}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{m}}(t_{m}-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\Big(\left\|u(\tau)\right\|^{2}_{D^{1}(\Omega)}+\left\|u(\tau)\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\Big)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\alpha^{-1}\big(\mathscr{M}^{2}+\mathscr{M}\big)\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}C_{2}\Big((t_{m}-\mathscr{T}_{\max})^{\alpha}+(\mathscr{T}_{\max}-t_{n})^{\alpha}\Big).

Then, we can also find a number
n_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{2}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3},\quad\forall m,n\geq n_{2}.

Also, it’s obviously that

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathscr{R}(t_{m}-\tau)H(u(\tau))-\mathscr{R}(t_{n}-\tau)H(u(\tau))\Big\|^{2}_{D^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\theta_{k}\mathscr{E}^{2}(k,t_{n},\tau,t_{m}-t_{n})}{(1+\theta_{k})^{2}}\Big\langle H(u(\tau)),\phi_{k}\Big\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\theta_{k}\mathscr{E}^{2}(k,t_{n},\tau,t_{m}-t_{n})}{(1+\theta_{k})^{2}}\Big\langle H(u(\tau)),\phi_{k}\Big\rangle^{2}.

Then, from (
9
), the following inequality holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\Big\|\mathscr{R}(t_{m}-\tau)H(u(\tau))-\mathscr{R}(t_{n}-\tau)H(u(\tau))\Big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{M}\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}C_{2}}{(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t_{n}}\Big((t_{n}-\tau)^{\alpha-1}-(t_{m}-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\Big)\big\|H(u(\tau))\big\|_{D^{-1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{M}\big(\mathscr{M}^{2}+\mathscr{M}\big)\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}C_{2}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\big|t_{m}^{\alpha}-(t_{m}-t_{n})^{\alpha}-t_{n}^{\alpha}\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{M}\big(\mathscr{M}^{2}+\mathscr{M}\big)\overline{\mathscr{C}}_{1}C_{2}}{\alpha(1-\alpha)}\Big(\big|t_{n}^{\alpha}-\mathscr{T}_{\max}^{\alpha}\big|+\big|t_{n}-\mathscr{T}_{\max}\big|^{\alpha}+\big|t_{m}-\mathscr{T}_{\max}\big|^{\alpha}+\big|t_{m}^{\alpha}-\mathscr{T}_{\max}^{\alpha}\big|\Big).

The convergence of
\{t_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
gives us a large number
n_{3}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{3}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3},\quad\forall m,n\geq n_{3}.

Hence, for every
\varepsilon>0
, we can always find a number
n_{0}=\max\left\{n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\right\}
that whenever
m,n\geq n_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|u_{m}-u_{n}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\varepsilon.

Accordingly, we can conclude that
\{u_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
D^{1}(\Omega)
. Thanks to the completeness of
D^{1}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique function
\overline{u}
which is the limit of
u_{n}
as
n
to infinity, i.e.

\displaystyle\overline{u}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}u(x,t_{n})=u(x,\mathscr{T}_{\max}).

Thus we have extended
u
over the interval
[0,\mathscr{T}_{\max}]
. Now, Lemma
3.6
is available for extending
u
to some large time interval. This fact leads us to a contradiction. Then, we can finish our proof by making the conclusion that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\to\mathscr{T}_{\max}^{-}}\left\|u(t)\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}=\infty,

provided
\mathscr{T}_{\max}<\infty.

∎

4
The other Lipschitz cases of the source functions

Throughout this section, we concern in the following form of the initial value problem (
1
)-(
3
)

(18)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u-\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\Delta u-\Delta u=H\left(u\right),\quad&\text{in}\quad\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\[4.26773pt]
\displaystyle u=0,&\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega\times(0,\infty),\\[4.26773pt]
\displaystyle u=u_{0},&\text{in}\quad\Omega\times\{0\}.\end{cases}

In particular, our main results for a mild solution
u:[0,\infty)\to\Omega
are going to revolve around the following two hypotheses for the source functions
H
.

(H_{1})
The nonlinearity of polynomial type

\displaystyle\quad~H(u)=|u|^{p-1}u,\quad p>2.

(H_{2})
The nonlinearity of exponential type

\displaystyle\quad~H(u)=u^{3}e^{u^{2}}.

4.1
The polynomial nonlinearity

For the hypothesis
(H_{1})
for the source function, we aim to establish the global-in-time well-posedness result for the mild solution to Problem (
1
)-(
3
). To this end we first provide some nonlinear control on
H
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
p>2
. Then, if

\displaystyle 0<\nu<\min\left\{1,\frac{d+2-2(p-1)d}{2-4(p-1)}\right\},

we can find a independent constant
\mathscr{C}_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\big\|H(u)-H(v)\big\|_{D^{\nu-1}(\Omega)}\leq\mathscr{C}_{2}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}^{p-1}\right)\left\|u-v\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)},

for every
u,v\in D^{\nu}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Firstly, for any
q\geq 1
, Hölder’s inequality gives us the following estimate

\displaystyle\big\||u|^{p-1}(u-v)\big\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\left\|u-v\right\|_{L^{2q}(\Omega)}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2(p-1)q}(\Omega)}^{p-1}.

Then, if we choose
q=\frac{2d}{d-2(\nu-1)}>1
, Lemma
5.3
shows that the Lebesgue space
L^{q}(\Omega)
embeds into the Hilbert space
D^{\nu-1}(\Omega)
. Also, by another use of Lemma
5.3
and the assumptions for
d,p,\nu
and
\Omega
, we have

\displaystyle D^{\nu}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2(p-1)q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2q}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega).

Combining the above result with the fact
(a+b)^{m}\leq 2^{m}(a^{m}+b^{m}),~a,b,m>0
and the triangle inequality, for any
u,v\in D^{\nu}(\Omega)
we have

\displaystyle\big\|H(u)-H(v)\big\|_{D^{\nu-1}(\Omega)}\leq 2^{q}C_{\Omega}\left(\left\|u\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}^{p-1}+\left\|v\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}^{p-1}\right)\left\|u-v\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)},

where
C_{\Omega}
comes from the embeddings. The proof is completed.
∎

Definition 4.2
.

Let
I=[0,\mathscr{T}]
and
\sigma,M>0
. Then, we define a subset of
C\left(I\to D^{\nu}(\Omega)\right)
as follows

\displaystyle\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{X}(\sigma,M,\nu):=\left\{w\in C(I\rightarrow D^{\nu}(\Omega))~\Big|~w(0)=u_{0}~\text{ and }\sup_{t\in I\setminus\{0\}}t^{\sigma}\left\|w(t)\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}<M\right\}.

Remark 4.1
.

Observe that
\mathbb{X}
is a Banach space involving the norm

\displaystyle\left\|w\right\|_{\mathbb{X}}=\sup_{t\in I\setminus\{0\}}t^{\sigma}\left\|w(t)\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
p>2
such that
\frac{p}{p-1}<\alpha^{-1}
. For a given datum
u_{0}\in D^{\nu}(\Omega)
sufficiently small and
I=[0,\mathscr{T}]
. Problem
(
1
)
-
(
3
)
admits a unique mild solution that belongs to the space
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{X}\left(\frac{\alpha}{p-1},2C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)},\nu\right)
.

Proof.

In the same spirit with Section
3
, we consider again the sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
established by

\displaystyle w_{1}(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\mathscr{S}(t)u_{0}(x),

\displaystyle w_{n+1}(x,t)

\displaystyle:=\mathscr{S}(t)u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(x,\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau.

By analogous argument as Lemma
3.2
, we can prove that
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a subset of
C(I\rightarrow D^{\nu}(\Omega))
. So, we need only to focus on deriving that
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a convergent sequence in
\mathbb{X}=\mathbb{X}\left(\alpha\mu,2C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)},\nu\right)
, where
\mu=\frac{1}{p-1}
. The Picard iteration process for this proof includes three main steps.

\bullet

Step 1
. We show that
w_{1}
is in
\mathbb{X}
.

Indeed, since
\mu\in(0,1)
and
u_{0}\in D^{\nu}(\Omega)
, thanks to Lemma
2.5
(
i
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|w_{1}(t)\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}t^{-\alpha\mu}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)},

which implies

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\mu}\left\|w_{1}(t)\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}.

\bullet

Step 2
. Assume that
w_{n}
belongs to
\mathbb{X}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
w_{n+1}\in\mathbb{X}
.

Indeed, Lemma
2.5
(
ii
) provides us the following estimate

(19)

\displaystyle\left\|w_{n+1}(t)-w_{1}(t)\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}\leq C_{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\|H(w_{n}(\tau))\|_{D^{\nu-1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Using Lemma
4.1
with
v=0
and the fact that
w_{n}\in\mathbb{X}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|w_{n+1}(t)-w_{1}(t)\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{C}_{2}C_{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\left\|w_{n}(\tau)\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}^{p}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{C}_{2}C_{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\tau^{-p\alpha\mu}\mathrm{d}\tau\right)\left\|w_{n}\right\|_{\mathbb{X}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{C}_{2}C_{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\tau^{-p\alpha\mu}\mathrm{d}\tau\right)\left(2C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}\right)^{p}.

Also, from our assumptions, we find that

\displaystyle p\alpha\mu<1\quad\text{and}\quad 1-p\mu+\mu=0,

which tells that

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\mu}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\tau^{-p\alpha\mu}\mathrm{d}\tau=\beta(\alpha,1-p\alpha\mu),

where the Beta function is defined in Definition
5.5
. From the standpoint of this result, we infer

(20)

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\mu}\left\|w_{n+1}(t)\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}+\mathscr{C}_{2}C_{2}\beta(\alpha,1-p\alpha\mu)\left(2C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}\right)^{p}.

Hence, the statement that
w_{n+1}\in\mathbb{X}
follows from the small data
u_{0}D^{\nu}(\Omega)
, then Step 2 is completed.

\bullet

Step 3
. We show that
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence.

Suppose that
w_{n}
and
w_{n-1}
are in
\mathbb{X}
. Our techniques are not too different from Step 2. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle\left\|w_{n+1}(t)-w_{n}(t)\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}

(21)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{2}C_{2}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\left(\left\|w_{n}(\tau)\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}^{p-1}+\left\|w_{n-1}(\tau)\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}^{p-1}\right)\left\|w_{n}(\tau)-v_{n-1}(\tau)\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Since
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\in\mathbb{X}
, multiplying the both sides of (
4.1
) by
t^{\alpha\mu}
, we have

\displaystyle t^{\alpha\mu}\left\|w_{n+1}(t)-w_{n}(t)\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathscr{C}_{2}C_{2}\left(t^{\alpha\mu}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\tau^{-p\alpha\mu}\mathrm{d}\tau\right)\left(2C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}\right)^{p-1}\left\|w_{n}(t)-w_{n-1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{X}}

(22)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\mathscr{C}_{2}C_{2}\beta(\alpha,1-p\alpha\mu)\left(2C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}\right)^{p-1}\left\|w_{n}(t)-w_{n-1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{X}}.

Then, we can take the supremum over the interval
I\setminus\{0\}
on both sides of (
4.1
) to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|w_{n+1}(t)-w_{n}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{X}}\leq 2\mathscr{C}_{2}C_{2}\beta(\alpha,1-p\alpha\mu)\left(2C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}\right)^{p-1}\left\|w_{n}(t)-w_{n-1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{X}}.

Therefore, if
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{\nu}(\Omega)}
is sufficiently small,
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{X}
.

From the above three steps, we can use again the arguments performed as in the proofs of Section
3
to find the limit function
u
of
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
which is the unique mild solution of Problem (
1
)-(
3
). The proof is completed.
∎

4.2
Exponential nonlinearity

This subsection concerns the global well-posedness of Problem (
1
)-(
3
) with the source function
H(u)=u^{3}e^{u^{2}}
. Here for any
w,v\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle|H(w)-H(v)|\leq\mathscr{C}_{3}\left|w-v\right|\left(w^{2}e^{w^{2}}+v^{2}e^{v^{2}}\right),

for some indepent
\mathscr{C}_{3}>0.
We recall the following theorem in
[
35
, Theorem 2]
or
[
29
, Section 1]
which is used to control the solution operator in the framework of Orlicz space.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a cone domain. Then,
W_{0}^{1,2}(\Omega)
embeds continuously into the Orlicz space
L^{\Xi}(\Omega)
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\alpha\in\left(0,\frac{2}{3}\right),

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a bounded domain with sufficiently smooth boundary, and
M
be a finite constant. Suppose that
I=[0,\mathscr{T}]
and
w,v
are functions in
C\left(I\to L^{\Xi}(\Omega)\right)
such that

\displaystyle\max\left(\sup_{t\in I}\left\|w(t)\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)},\sup_{t\in I}\left\|t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}w(t)\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)},\sup_{t\in I}\left\|w(t)\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)},\sup_{t\in I}\left\|t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}v(t)\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\right)<M.

If
M
is small enough, we can derive the following inequalities

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)\big(H(w(\tau))-H(v(\tau))\big)\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}

(23)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\mathscr{C}_{3}\mathscr{T}^{\alpha}}{\alpha}\left\|w-v\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}M^{2}\left(\left(\Gamma\left(3\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\Gamma\left(4\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(6M^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\right)

and

\displaystyle t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)\big(H(w(\tau))-H(v(\tau))\big)\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}

(24)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\mathscr{C}_{3}\left\|w-v\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}}{\big(\beta\left(\alpha,1-\frac{3\alpha}{2}\big)\right)^{-1}}M^{2}\left(\left(\Gamma\left(3\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\Gamma\left(4\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(6M^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\right).

Proof.

Assume that
w
and
v
are functions in
L^{\Xi}(\Omega)
. Then, we can use Theorem
4.4
to obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)\left(H(w(\tau))-H(v(\tau))\right)\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\Big\|\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)\left(H(w(\tau))-H(v(\tau))\right)\Big\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau.

Using Lemma
2.5
, we have

(25)

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)\left(H(w(\tau))-H(v(\tau))\right)\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\leq\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\Big\|H(w(\tau))-H(v(\tau))\Big\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau.

To continuous, we make a nonlinear estimate for the source function with
w=w(t),v=v(t),~t\in I
, as follows

(26)

\displaystyle\left\|H(w)-H(v)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{3}\left\|(w-v)(u^{2}+v^{2})\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\mathscr{C}_{3}\sum_{u\in\{w,v\}}\left\|\left|w-v\right||u|^{2}\left(e^{u^{2}}-1\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{3}\sum_{u\in\{w,v\}}\left(\left\|w-v\right\|_{L^{4}(\Omega)}\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{4}(\Omega)}+\left\|w-v\right\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\left\|e^{u^{2}}-1\right\|_{L^{6}(\Omega)}\right),

here, we have used Hölder’s inequality. If
\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}<(\frac{1}{6})^{\frac{1}{2}}
, it follows from the techniques used in
[
23
, Lemma 3.2]
that

(27)

\displaystyle\left\|e^{u^{2}}-1\right\|^{6}_{L^{6}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(e^{6u^{2}(x)}-1\right){\mathrm{d}}x\leq\int_{\Omega}\left(\exp\left({\frac{6\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}u^{2}(x)}{\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}}}\right)-1\right){\mathrm{d}}x\leq 6\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)},

where we have used the fact that

\displaystyle\left\{\kappa\in\mathbb{R}~\Big|~\kappa>0,\int_{\Omega}\Xi\left(\frac{|w(x)|}{\kappa}\right)\mathrm{d}x\leq 1\right\}=\left[\left\|w\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)},\infty\right).

According to (
26
), (
27
) and Lemma
2.1
, we find that

(28)

\displaystyle\left\|H(w)-H(v)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{C}_{3}\sum_{u\in\{w,v\}}\left\|w-v\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\left(\left(\Gamma\left(3\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\Gamma\left(4\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(6\left\|u\right\|^{2}_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\right).

Inclusion inequalities (
25
) and (
28
) allow us to deduce the inequality (
4.5
). Furthermore, by using (
4.5
) and

\displaystyle t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{\alpha-1}\tau^{-\frac{3\alpha}{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau=\beta\left(\alpha,1-\frac{3\alpha}{2}\right),

we get the result (
4.5
).
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with sufficiently smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
and
H(u)=u^{3}e^{u^{2}}
. Assume that
I=[0,\mathscr{T}]
and
u_{0}\in D^{1}(\Omega)
is small enough. Then, there exists a unique mild solution of Problem (
1
)-(
3
) in
C\left(I\to L^{\Xi}(\Omega)\right).

Proof.

We start by considering again the sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
as in Theorem
4.3
and the following space

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}:=\left\{w\in C\left(I\to L^{\Xi}(\Omega)\right)~\Big|~\left\|w\right\|_{\mathbb{U}}<2C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\right\},

where, the
\mathbb{U}
-norm is given by

\displaystyle\left\|w\right\|_{\mathbb{U}}:=\max\left\{\sup_{t\in I}\left\|w\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)},\sup_{t\in I}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|w\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\right\}.

Using Theorem
4.4
and considering Lemma
2.5
in two cases:
\mu=0
and
\mu=\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\left\|w_{1}(t)\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\leq\left\|\mathscr{S}(t)u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)},\qquad&\mu=0,~t\in I\\[5.69046pt]
\displaystyle t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|w_{1}(t)\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\leq t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|\mathscr{S}(t)u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)},&\mu=\frac{1}{2},~t\in I.\end{cases}

This result yields that
w_{1}\in\mathbb{U}.
Next we assume that
w_{n}
is in
\mathbb{U}
, then we can show that
w_{n+1}
is also in
\mathbb{U}
In fact, for given small data
u_{0}
, set

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{*}:=\left(\frac{32\mathscr{C}_{3}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\left(\left(\Gamma\left(3\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\Gamma\left(4\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(6(2\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\right)}{\alpha C_{1}}\right)^{\frac{-1}{\alpha}}.

On the one hand, when
t\leq\mathscr{T}_{*}
, we apply Lemma
4.5
with
v\equiv 0
to find that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\mathscr{C}_{3}\mathscr{T}_{*}^{\alpha}}{\alpha(2\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)})^{-3}}\left(\left(\Gamma\left(3\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\Gamma\left(4\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(6(2\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\right)

\displaystyle=2^{-1}C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}.

On the other hand, if
t
is larger than
\mathscr{T}_{*}
defined above, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathscr{T}_{*}}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{*}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\mathscr{C}_{3}\mathscr{T}_{*}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(2\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)})^{3}\left(\left(\Gamma\left(3\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\Gamma\left(4\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(6(2\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\right)}{\left(\beta\left(\alpha,1-\frac{3\alpha}{2}\right)\right)^{-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{128\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}^{4}\left(\mathscr{C}_{3}\left(\left(\Gamma\left(3\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\Gamma\left(4\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(6(2\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\right)\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\left(\beta\left(\alpha,1-\frac{3\alpha}{2}\right)\right)^{-1}}.

Hence, we can choose
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}
small enough to obtain

(29)

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathscr{T}_{*}}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}}{2}.

Combining all of the above arguments, whether
t\leq\mathscr{T}_{*}
or
t>\mathscr{T}_{*},
we can see that

(30)

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\leq C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}.

In addition, apply again Lemma
25
, for any
w_{n}\in\mathbb{U}
, we obtain

\displaystyle t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|\int_{\mathscr{T}_{*}}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}

(31)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\mathscr{C}_{3}(2C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)})^{3}}{\left(\beta\left(\alpha,1-\frac{3\alpha}{2}\right)\right)^{-1}}\left(\left(\Gamma\left(3\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\Gamma\left(4\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(6(2C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\right).

Therefore, for any
w_{n}\in\mathbb{U}
, we deduce the following two claims.

Claim 1
. Combining the conclusions that
w_{1}\in\mathbb{U}
and the inequality (
30
) gives us

\displaystyle\left\|w_{n+1}(t)\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\leq\left\|\mathscr{S}(t)u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\leq 2C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}.

Claim 2
. Similarly, since
w_{1}\in\mathbb{U}
and the estimate (
4.2
) holds, we have

\displaystyle t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|w_{n+1}(t)\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\leq t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|\mathscr{S}(t)u_{0}\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}+t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)H(w_{n}(\tau))\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\leq 2C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)},

as long as
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}
is sufficiently small.

These claims show that for any
t\in I
,
\big\|w_{n+1}\big\|_{\mathbb{U}(t)}
is less than or equals to
2C_{1}\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}
. It means that
w_{n+1}
belongs to
\mathbb{U}
whenever
w_{n}
is in
\mathbb{U}
, provided that the continuity of
w_{n+1}
is inferred by Lemma
3.2
. The remaining work is to show that
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence with respect to the
\mathbb{U}
norm. Based on this result, we can easily obtain the unique mild solution
u
of Problem (
1
)-(
3
) which is the limit function of the sequence
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
. To this end, we take two elements of the sequence,
w_{n-1},w_{n}\in\mathbb{U}
. Analogous to the way we find the estimate (
30
), we set

\displaystyle\mathscr{T}_{**}:=\left(\frac{64\mathscr{C}_{3}\left\|u_{0}\right\|^{2}_{D^{1}(\Omega)}\left(\left(\Gamma\left(3\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\Gamma\left(4\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(6(2\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\right)}{\alpha C_{1}}\right)^{\frac{-1}{\alpha}}.

On the one hand, for any
t>\mathscr{T}_{**}
, we have

\displaystyle\Bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)\big(H(w_{n}(\tau))-H(w_{n-1}(\tau))\big)\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigg\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{0}^{\mathscr{T}_{**}}\Big\|\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)\big(H(w_{n}(\tau))-H(w_{n-1}(\tau))\big)\Big\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau}_{\mathrm{I}_{1}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\int_{\mathscr{T}_{**}}^{t}\Big\|\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)\big(H(w_{n}(\tau))-H(w_{n-1}(\tau))\big)\Big\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\mathrm{d}\tau}_{\mathrm{I}_{2}}.

In view of Lemma
4.5
, one finds that

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\mathscr{C}_{3}\mathscr{T}_{**}^{\alpha}}{\alpha(2\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)})^{-2}}\left(\left(\Gamma\left(3\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\Gamma\left(4\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(6(2\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\right)\left\|w_{n}(t)-w_{n-1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{U}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\|w_{n}(t)-w_{n-1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{U}}.

Applying Lemma
4.5
in the same way as in (
29
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{I}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\mathscr{C}_{3}\mathscr{T}_{**}^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}(2\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)})^{2}\left(\left(\Gamma\left(3\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\Gamma\left(4\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(6(2\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\right)}{\left(\beta\left(\alpha,1-\frac{3\alpha}{2}\right)\right)^{-1}}\left\|w_{n}(t)-w_{n-1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{U}}

\displaystyle=\frac{64\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}^{3}\left(\mathscr{C}_{3}\left(\left(\Gamma\left(3\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\Gamma\left(4\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(6(2\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\right)\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\left(\beta\left(\alpha,1-\frac{3\alpha}{2}\right)\right)^{-1}}\left\|w_{n}(t)-w_{n-1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{U}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\|w_{n}(t)-w_{n-1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{U}},

provided that
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}
is sufficiently small. In addition, for any
t<\mathscr{T}_{**}
, we note that

\displaystyle\Bigg\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)\big(H(w_{n}(\tau))-H(w_{n-1}(\tau))\big)\mathrm{d}\tau\Bigg\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\leq\mathrm{I}_{1}\leq\mathrm{I}_{1}+\mathrm{I}_{2}.

Wherefore, for any
t\in I
the following estimate holds

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in I}\left\|w_{n+1}(t)-w_{n}(t)\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{t\in I}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)\big(H(w_{n}(\tau))-H(w_{n-1}(\tau))\big)\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}

(32)

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{I}_{1}+\mathrm{I}_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|w_{n}(t)-w_{n-1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{U}}.

On the other hand, by replacing respectively
w,v
by
w_{n},w_{n-1}\in\mathbb{U}
in Lemma
4.5
, the following inequality holds

\displaystyle t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{R}(t-\tau)\big(H(w_{n}(\tau))-H(w_{n-1}(\tau))\big)\mathrm{d}\tau\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2\mathscr{C}_{3}(2\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)})^{2}}{\left(\beta\left(\alpha,1-\frac{3\alpha}{2}\right)\right)^{-1}}\left(\left(\Gamma\left(3\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\Gamma\left(4\right)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(6(2\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)})^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}}\right)\left\|w_{n}(t)-w_{n-1}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{U}}.

As a result, if
\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{D^{1}(\Omega)}
is small enough, we deduce

(33)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in I\setminus\{0\}}t^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\left\|w_{n+1}(t)-w_{n}(t)\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|w-v\right\|_{\mathbb{U}}.

On account of (
4.2
), (
4.2
) and (
33
), we can conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|w_{n+1}(t)-w_{n}(t)\right\|_{\mathbb{U}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|w_{n}(t)-w_{n-1}(t)\right\|_{L^{\Xi}(\Omega)},

for every
w_{n},w_{n-1}\in\mathbb{U}
. It means that
\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{U}
. The proof is completed.
∎

.

(

.

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
p,q>0
. Then, the Beta function and the Gamma function can be defined respectively by

\displaystyle\beta(p,q)=\int_{0}^{1}(1-z)^{p-1}z^{q-1}\mathrm{d}z\quad\text{ and }\quad\Gamma(p)=\int_{0}^{\infty}z^{p-1}e^{-z}\mathrm{d}z.

The spine of the T T -graph of the Hilbert scheme of points in the plane
Diane Maclagan, Rob Silversmith

Abstract
. The torus T T of projective space also acts on the Hilbert scheme of subschemes of projective space. The T T -graph of the Hilbert scheme has vertices the fixed points of this action, and edges connecting pairs of fixed points in the closure of a one-dimensional orbit. In general this graph depends on the underlying field. We construct a subgraph, which we call the spine, of the T T -graph of Hilb m ⁡ ( 𝔸 2 ) \Hilb^{m}(\mathbb{A}^{2}) that is independent of the choice of infinite field. For certain edges in the spine we also give a description of the tropical ideal, in the sense of tropical scheme theory, of a general ideal in the edge. This gives a more refined understanding of these edges, and of the tropical stratification of the Hilbert scheme.



1.
Introduction

The torus
T\cong(K^{*})^{n}

\Hilb_{P}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
of subschemes of

-graph of the Hilbert scheme has

There is an edge between two vertices if there is a one-dimensional

The

for example, the proof that

[

[

]

[

]

[

]
. More generally,

[

]

equivariant cohomology ring of a variety with

-action.

The

-graph of the Hilbert scheme

of

The first result of this paper is the construction of a subgraph of

A

if and only if

In this latter case, denote by

the Hilbert function of

with respect to this grading:

.
Then

lies on the multigraded Hilbert scheme

parametrizing homogeneous

[

]
*Theorem 1

]
*Theorem 1.1, and has

The

Theorem 1.3

Let

There are comparatively few explicit examples of tropical ideals; see

Let

\mathbb{Z}
-grading
by

\deg(x)=a
and
\deg(y)=b
.
The closure of a
one-dimensional
T
-orbit has either one or two
T
-fixed points; if
there are two
T
-fixed points in the closure we have
ab>0
.

Notation 2.1
.

We set
S=K[x,y]
. We grade

by

\deg(x)=a
,
\deg(y)=b

The point

\Hilb_{S}^{h}

[

3.1.
Tropicalizations of ideals

We first recall the concept of tropicalization of ideals, and the
tropical stratification of the Hilbert scheme.

Let
\mathbb{B}=(\{0,\infty\},\mathrel{\oplus},\hskip 1.13809pt{\mathchoice{{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\displaystyle\circ$\cr\hfil$\displaystyle\cdot$\hfil\cr}}}}{{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\textstyle\circ$\cr\hfil$\textstyle\cdot$\hfil\cr}}}}{{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptstyle\circ$\cr\hfil$\scriptstyle\cdot$\hfil\cr}}}}{{\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\scriptscriptstyle\circ$\cr\hfil$\scriptscriptstyle\cdot$\hfil\cr}}}}}\hskip 1.13809pt)
be the Boolean
semiring, with the operations of tropical addition (minimum) and
tropical multiplication (addition). The tropicalization of
f=\sum c_{ij}x^{i}y^{j}\in K[x,y]
is

\trop(f)=\mathrel{\oplus}_{c_{ij}\neq 0}x^{i}y^{j}\in\mathbb{B}[x,y]
. The tropicalization of an ideal
I\subseteq K[x,y]
is

\trop(I)=\langle\trop(f):f\in I\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{B}[x,y].

This is the trivial valuation case of tropicalizing ideals in the
sense of tropical scheme theory

[
Giansiracusa2
,
TropicalIdeals
,
MaclaganRinconValuations
,
Balancing
]
.

Note that a polynomial in the semiring
\mathbb{B}[x,y]
can be
identified with its support. When
I\subseteq S
is graded, the
polynomials in
\trop(I)
of degree
d
of minimal support are the

circuits
of a matroid
\mathcal{M}(I_{d})
on the ground set

\mathrm{Mon}_{d}
of degree-
d
monomials. We call this the degree-
d
matroid
of
I
. See, for example,
[
Oxley
]
for more on matroids.

We will primarily focus on the
basis
characterization of
matroids. When
I\subseteq S
is homogeneous with Hilbert function

h
, a collection
E
of
h(d)
monomials of degree
d
is a basis for

\mathcal{M}(I_{d})
if there is no polynomial in
I
with support in

E
. The matroid
\mathcal{M}(I_{d})
is
uniform
if every
collection of
h(d)
monomials of degree
d
is a basis. In this case
we write
\mathcal{M}(I_{d})=U_{h(d),\mathrm{mon}_{d}}
, where
\mathrm{mon}_{d}=|\mathrm{Mon}_{d}|
.

The assignment
I\mapsto\trop(I)
defines a stratification of

\Hilb_{S}^{h}
, called the
matroid stratification
or

tropical stratification
. A stratum of this stratification
consists of all ideals with a fixed tropicalization. If
\sum_{d}h(d)<\infty
(as will always
be true in this paper), then there are finitely many strata, and they
are Zariski-locally closed. In general, there may be countably many
strata; see
[
SilversmithTropicalIdeal
]
.

(1)

(2)

and a fixed positive significant arrow

is the

(3)

If

from

. This uses

Remark 3.12
.

In the standard grading
\deg(x)=\deg(y)=1,
the lex-most ideal

is the

, also known as the

, with respect to the order

; see

[

]
*Chapter 4. This is the monomial ideal whose

part is the span of the

largest monomials in
lexicographic order. The lex-least ideal

is the lexicographic
ideal for the opposite order of the variables
x\prec y
. A
monomial ideal is lex-least with respect to the standard grading if
and only if the rows of its Young diagram are
strictly

decreasing in length, and similarly is lex-most if and only if the
columns of its Young diagram are strictly decreasing in length.
This means that for
M^{-}
we have
m_{k}=x^{i}y^{k}
for some

, so

m_{k}r^{\ell}\in S
for
0\leq\ell\leq k
. We also have by
symmetry that if

and
M^{+}
are the lex-least and lex-most
monomial ideals with a given Hilbert function
h
respectively, then
the Young diagrams of
M^{-}
and
M^{+}
are transposes of each other.

Another standard-graded fact about
M^{-}
that we need, which is not
true for nonstandard gradings, is that
w_{k}/m_{k-1}=y
for all

.

Proposition 3.13
.

Fix the standard
(1,1)
-grading for
S
. Fix
k\geq 0
with
m_{k}r\in S\setminus M^{-}
. If for some

we have that

m_{k}r^{\ell}\in S\setminus M^{-}
, then there is a direct path of length

\ell
from
m_{k}
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
k
. When
k=0
, there is no such

\ell
, so the claim holds. Now assume that the claim is true for
all
k^{\prime}<k
. Let
\ell^{\prime}
be maximal such that
(k,\ell^{\prime})\in T^{+}(M^{-})
. If
\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell
, then we claim that
(k,\ell)\in T^{+}(M^{-})
. This follows from the fact that
w_{k}r^{\ell^{\prime}}\in M^{-}
,
so since
w_{k}r^{\ell^{\prime}}\preceq w_{k}r^{\ell}
, we have
w_{k}r^{\ell}\in M^{-}
. In this case
c_{k}^{\ell}
is the required direct path.
Otherwise,
(k,\ell)\not\in T^{+}(M^{-})
, so
w_{k}r^{\ell}\in S\setminus M^{-}
. Let
k^{\prime}=j^{+}(w_{k}r^{\ell^{\prime}})=k-\ell^{\prime}
. We
have
w_{k}r^{\ell^{\prime}}=x^{i}m_{k^{\prime}}
for some

. Thus

m_{k^{\prime}}r^{\ell-\ell^{\prime}}x^{i}=w_{k}r^{\ell}
, so since
w_{k}r^{\ell}\in S\setminus M^{-}
, the same is true for
m_{k^{\prime}}r^{\ell-\ell^{\prime}}
, and
\ell-\ell^{\prime}\leq k^{\prime}
. Since
\ell^{\prime}<\ell\leq k
, we have
k^{\prime}>1
and

\ell^{\prime}+1\leq k
. This means that
w_{k}r^{\ell^{\prime}+1}\in S
, and

m_{k^{\prime}}r\in S\setminus M^{-}
, as otherwise we would have

m_{k^{\prime}}x^{i}r=w_{k}r^{\ell^{\prime}+1}\in M^{-}
, so

would be in

. By induction there is a direct path

from
m_{k^{\prime}}

of length

, so
c_{k}^{\ell^{\prime}}c_{P}
is a direct path of
length
\ell
from
m_{k}
.

3.3.
The structure of the Macaulay
matrix

For the rest of this section, we fix the standard
(1,1)
grading on

S=K[x,y]

Let

as in Theorem

. Note that there

.

For any

, and for any basis of

, we may write the coefficients of the basis as

for

if and only if the minor
indexed by rows corresponding to monomials not in
E

is zero. The matroid

\mathcal{M}(I_{d})
is thus exactly characterized by which

We begin by choosing a basis for

.
For each of the

.

As the polynomials

\{g_{m}\}_{m\in M^{-}_{d}}

\dim(I_{d})=d+1-h(d)=\left\lvert M^{-}_{d}\right\rvert,

conclude that
\{g_{m}\}_{m\in M^{-}_{d}}

Let
R
be the matrix with columns the coefficient vectors of the

. This is a degree-

. We
index the rows by
\mathrm{Mon}_{d}
in increasing order with respect to

, and index the columns by the monomials in

, so

3.4.
Proof of Theorem

We now prove:

Theorem 3.19
.

Fix the standard
(1,1)

Then every

Proof.

For convenience, in this proof let

Property
3.16
, we define

to be the
smallest monomial (with respect to

) in
M^{-}_{d}
. Again as
in Property
3.16
, we work with the Macaulay

\bar{R}
over
K[C_{\prec}(M^{-})]/\langle{Y}\rangle
; if a

.

Fix an

of

. By Property

, the
(i,j)
th entry

of

is
of the form

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{$P$ a path from}\\
\text{$m_{j^{-}(m^{*})}$ of

for some

. Note that the sum is zero if
\ell_{i,j}<0
. We have

\ell_{j,j}\geq 0
for all

by

.

The chosen minor is then

(1)

\displaystyle\det(R^{\prime})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n_{0}}}(-1)^{\sgn(\sigma)}\prod_{j=1}^{n_{0}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{$P$ a path from}\\
\text{$m_{j^{-}(m^{*})}$ of

By Proposition

, the path

. Hence we may

Then

is a monomial in

,
and

appears as a term of the right side of (

) when

.

Claim 3.20
.

Suppose

C=\prod_{j=1}^{s}c_{p_{k,\ell_{i}}}
, with

\ell_{1}\geq\ell_{2}\geq\dots\geq\ell_{s}\geq 0
, and we also

have the following inequality with respect to the lexicographic

(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{s})\succeq(l(P_{1}),\dots,l(P_{s})).

Proof of Claim
3.20
.

If

now assume that

. The proof is by induction on

. The

, in which case we must have

, so

that every variable

as defined there. Let

be the variable with

minimal dividing

c_{P_{1}}
. Since

, we have
c_{i}^{\ell^{\prime}}

for some

. We claim that the

before the step

is

before

,

. To see this, note that the part of

where

is the index of

, while the part of

Claim 3.21
.

Suppose

Let

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n_{0}}R^{\prime}_{j,j}

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

For an ideal

, we have

and hence of

This implies the main claim of Theorem

; the second claim in Theorem

follows from the standard fact that if

∎

Remark 3.22
.

The proofs of Theorems

Remark 3.23
.

If

, then necessarily

.

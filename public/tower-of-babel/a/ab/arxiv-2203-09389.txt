Classes of Baire spaces defined by semi-neighborhoods of the diagonal
Evgenii Reznichenko

Abstract
With the help of semi-neighborhoods of the diagonal, classes of Baire spaces are defined: Δ \Delta , Δ h \Delta_{h} and Δ s \Delta_{s} Baire spaces. These classes of spaces are studied with the help of topological games. They are useful in studying continuity in groups: paratopological Δ \Delta -Baire groups, quasi-topological Δ h \Delta_{h} -Baire groups, and semitopological Δ s \Delta_{s} -Baire groups are topological groups.

A space

(U_{n})_{n}
of open dense subsets of
X

G=\bigcap_{n}U_{n}
is dense in

Baire spaces play an important role in mathematics. The strongest and most interesting results are obtained when additional constraints on the space are imposed, such as metrizability.

The concepts of a Baire space and nonmeager space are closely related to each other.

Theorem 1.1
.

(1)

Baire spaces are nonmeager.

(2)

An open subset of a Baire space is a Baire space.

(3)

A space

X
is a nonmeager space.

(4)

A space
X
is nonmeager if and only if there exists an open nonempty Baire subspace of
X

(5)

A homogeneous nonmeager space is a Baire space.

5.1
Strategies vs tactics

Let
\Gamma
be a topological game played by players
\alpha
and
\beta
. In a typical topological game, the game
\Gamma(X)
is a
sequential infinite game of perfect information
. The game consists of players taking turns making moves. After a countable number of moves, after analyzing which players made moves, the winner is determined. When making a move, a player makes a choice of move based on all previous moves of both players.
These choices determine the player’s
strategy

A strategy in a sequential game is called a
tactic

[
32
]
if the player takes into account only the opponent’s previous move in his choice. Such strategies are also called
1
-tactics or stationary strategies.
A strategy is called a

if the player takes into account not all, but only some previous moves of the opponent and himself.
Thus, a tactic is a limited information strategy. In addition to tactics, other limited information strategies are also considered
[
31
,
33
]
.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
and
\mathcal{Q}
is some topological properties. An important role when using topological games is played by theorems of the form:

(
\Gamma_{1}
)

if
X
holds

, then
\beta
has a winning strategy in the game

.

A typical proof of theorems of the form (

) is that the
{\mathcal{P}}
property is used to construct a strategy for
\beta

That is, for
\beta
there is some limited information strategy. Sometimes a winning tactic is built for
\beta
. In the next section, we will find winning tactics for player
\beta
in the games we are interested in.

Theorem

(

)

if

-favorable.

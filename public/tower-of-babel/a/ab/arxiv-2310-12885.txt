Abstract
Multi-species BGK models describe the dynamics of rarefied gases with constituent particles of different elements or compounds with potentially non-trivial velocity distributions. In this paper, moment equations for the bulk velocities, energies, and temperatures of a spatially homogeneous multi-species BGK model are examined. A key challenge in analyzing these equations is the fact that the collision frequencies are allowed to depend on the species temperatures, which allows for more realistic simulations of dilute gas flow. Therefore, a positive lower bound is established for the species temperatures. With this lower bound, a global existence and uniqueness of solutions to the coupled velocity-energy ODE system is established. The lower bound also enables a proof of exponential decay to a unique steady-state solution. Numerical results are presented to demonstrate how the bulk velocities and temperatures relax for large times.

Multispecies BGK, Moment Equations, Kinetic Theory, Rarefied Gas Dynamics

Introduction

The Bhatnagar-Gross-Krook (BGK) equation
[
3
]
is a well-known and frequently employed model for simulating dilute gases.
It has been used in a variety of applications, including
hypersonics

[
1
,
9
]
,
micro-channel flows

[
10
,
20
]
,
nozzle flows

[
17
,
18
,
21
]
,
and plasma transport

[
2
,
13
]
.

The BGK equation prescribes the evolution of a position-velocity phase-space distribution that characterizes the state of a dilute gas and is typically employed as a simpler alternative to the Boltzmann equation.
The BGK equation can be derived via an ad-hoc approximation of the Boltzmann collision operator by a much simpler nonlinear relaxation operator
[
19
, Section 4.2]
, where the rate of relaxation is determined by a scalar collision frequency.
Like the Boltzmann collision operator, the BGK relaxation operator conserves mass, momentum, and energy.
It also dissipates entropy, and satisfies an H-Theorem that characterizes local thermodynamic equilibrium; see e.g.
[
24
, Section 3.6.1]
.

Recently, multi-species versions of the BGK equation have been derived that satisfy the conservation and entropy dissipation properties of the multi-species Boltzmann equation
[
5
,
16
,
14
,
11
]
.
In most of these cases, the collision frequencies are assumed to be independent of the phase-space velocity (
[
11
]
being an exception) and dependent on the kinetic distributions only through their respective number densities (
[
14
]
being an exception).
Conservation of the number density implies that collision frequencies that depend only on the number densities will be constant in time.
However, for general collision models, these frequencies will often depend on the species temperature or on the phase-space velocity
[
4
]
, and relaxing these commonly used assumptions can significantly alter the behavior of solutions
[
11
]
.
In addition, the analysis and simulation of the BGK equations becomes more difficult when the collision frequencies depend on time and/or phase-space velocity.

In the current paper, we study the space-homogeneous, multi-species BGK equations from
[
14
]
, which can be understood as a special, illustrative case of the equations in
[
5
]
or
[
16
]
.
We do so in the context of collision frequencies that are independent of the phase-space velocity, but depend on both number density and temperature.
In particular, we analyze the ODE system for the momentum and energy moments (the number density evolution is trivial), which is important for asymptotic analysis and for the development of numerical methods.
Indeed, many numerical simulations of the full BGK system (including phase-space advection) rely on implicit-explicit (IMEX) methods that treat the space-homogeneous component implicitly.
In this setting, the result of solving the associated moment equations first is to effectively linearize and diagonalize (with respect to the phase-space velocity) the required implicit solve, yielding a significant reduction in computational cost.
(See
[
7
,
22
]
for applications to the single species case and
[
23
, Section 4]
for an extension to the multi-species setting when the collision frequencies depend only on the number densities.)

In this paper, we first establish existence and uniqueness of solutions to the momentum-energy ODE system, which follows from standard theory once a lower positive bound can be established on the species temperatures.
We show monoticity of the minimum temperature envelope as well as upper and lower bounds on the bulk velocity.
We then prove exponential decay of the species momenta and energies to their steady state values, thereby generalizing some of the results in
[
8
]
to a system with an arbitrary number of species.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows.
In
Section

2
, we introduce the basic equations.
In
Section

3
, we prove existence and uniqueness results and show that the species temperatures remain positive if initially so. We also establish bounds on the bulk velocities.
In
Section

4
, we establish the main results about the long-time behavior of the moment equations.
In
Section

5
, we provide some examples to demonstrate this behavior numerically.
Conclusions are provided in
Section

6
.
The Appendix contains a statement of the Picard-Lindelöf Theorem, which is used for the existence/uniqueness result, as well as several technical results that are used in the proofs in

Section

4
.

2
Model Equations

We denote by
f_{i}
the kinetic distribution of particles of species
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
having mass
m_{i}
.
More specifically,
f_{i}(\boldsymbol{x},\boldsymbol{v},t)
is the density of species
i
particles at the point
\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, with microscopic velocity
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, at time
t\geq 0,
with respect to the measure
\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{x}
.
Associated to each
f_{i}
are the species number density
n_{i}
, mass density
\rho_{i}
, bulk velocity
\boldsymbol{u}_{i}
, and temperature
T_{i}
, defined by

\rho_{i}=m_{i}n_{i}=m_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{i}\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v},\qquad\boldsymbol{u}_{i}=\frac{1}{\rho_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}m_{i}\boldsymbol{v}f_{i}\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v},\qquad T_{i}=\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\right|^{2}f_{i}\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}.

(1)

The species momentum densities
\boldsymbol{k}_{i}
and species energy densities
E_{i}
are given by

\boldsymbol{k}_{i}=\rho_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\qquad\text{and}\qquad E_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\rho_{i}|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2}+\frac{d}{2}n_{i}T_{i}.

(2)

BGK models are expressed in terms of Maxwellian distributions that depend on these moments.

Definition 2.1
.

Given
n>0,\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\theta>0,
a Maxwellian
M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta}
is a function of the form

\displaystyle M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta}(\boldsymbol{v})=\frac{n}{(2\pi\theta)^{\frac{d}{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{\left|\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{u}\right|^{2}}{2\theta}\right).

(3)

Straightforward computations show that the moments of a Maxwellian satisfy

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta}\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}=n,\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\boldsymbol{v}M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta}\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}=n\boldsymbol{u},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\boldsymbol{v}|^{2}M_{n,\boldsymbol{u},\theta}\,\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}=n|\boldsymbol{u}|^{2}+dn\theta.

(4)

We consider in this paper the multi-species BGK equation from
[
14
]
, given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial t}+\boldsymbol{v}\cdot\nabla_{\boldsymbol{x}}f_{i}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\lambda_{i,j}(M_{i,j}-f_{i}),\quad\forall i\in\{1,\cdots,N\},

(5)

where
\varepsilon>0
is the Knudsen number,
\lambda_{i,j}>0
is the (nondimensional) frequency of collisions between species
i
and
j
(independent of
\boldsymbol{v}
), and

M_{i,j}(\boldsymbol{v})=M_{n_{i},\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j},T_{i,j}/m_{i}}(\boldsymbol{v})

is a Maxwellian defined by (
3
), using the mixture velocities and temperatures

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}\boldsymbol{u}_{j}}{\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}},

(6a)

\displaystyle T_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}T_{i}+n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}T_{j}}{n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}}+\frac{1}{d}\frac{\rho_{i}\rho_{j}\lambda_{i,j}\lambda_{j,i}}{\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}}\frac{|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}-\boldsymbol{u}_{j}|^{2}}{n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}}.

(6b)

The existence and uniqueness of nonnegative mild solutions to (a more general) multi-species BGK equation was proved in
[
15
]
for periodic spatial domains and collision frequencies that depend on the number densities.

2.1
Model Properties

The BGK collision operators in (
5
) satisfy the following invariance properties:
For any
i,j,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lambda_{i,j}(M_{i,j}-f_{i})\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}

\displaystyle=0,

(7)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lambda_{i,j}(M_{i,j}-f_{i})m_{i}\boldsymbol{v}\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lambda_{j,i}(M_{j,i}-f_{j})m_{j}\boldsymbol{v}\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{0},

(8)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lambda_{i,j}(M_{i,j}-f_{i})m_{i}|\boldsymbol{v}|^{2}\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\lambda_{j,i}(M_{j,i}-f_{j})m_{j}|\boldsymbol{v}|^{2}\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}

\displaystyle=0,

(9)

corresponding to conservation of species mass, total momentum, and total energy, respectively.
Furthermore, an entropy dissipation condition is satisfied:
For a spatially homogeneous mixture, the entropy function
\mathcal{H}=\sum_{i}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{i}\log f_{i}\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{v}
satisfies
\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\mathcal{H}\leq 0
.
Moreover,
\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\mathcal{H}=0
if and only if

f_{i}=M_{n_{i},\boldsymbol{u}_{\textnormal{eq}},T_{\textnormal{eq}}/m_{i}}(\boldsymbol{v})

for all

i\in\{1,\cdots,N\},

where
\boldsymbol{u}_{\text{eq}}
and
T_{\text{eq}}
are the equilibrium bulk velocity and temperature, respectively, which are common to all species
[
14
]
.

The collision frequencies
\lambda_{i,j}
are typically expressed as functions of the species moments given in (
1
).
To define these collision frequencies,
[
14
]
presents a recipe based on matching either momentum or energy relaxation rates of the Boltzmann equation, given either a differential cross section or a momentum transfer cross section.
In this paper, we consider the case of hard sphere collisions, for which the momentum transfer cross section is independent of microscopic velocity and given by
[
4
]
:

\sigma_{\textnormal{MT}}=\pi\left(\frac{d_{i}+d_{j}}{2}\right)^{2}=\pi d_{i,j}^{2},

(10)

where
d_{i}
and
d_{j}
are reference diameters for the particles of species
i
and
j
; reference diameters for several species are given in
[
4
]
and
[
6
]
.
Following the recipe for matching energy relaxation rates from Section 4.3 of
[
14
]
, we obtain the following collision frequencies for hard sphere interactions:

\lambda_{i,j}^{\textnormal{HS}}=\frac{32\pi^{2}}{3(2\pi)^{3/2}}\frac{m_{i}m_{j}}{(m_{i}+m_{j})^{2}}(d_{i}+d_{j})^{2}n_{j}\sqrt{\frac{T_{i}}{m_{i}}+\frac{T_{j}}{m_{j}}}.

(11)

While the formula in (
11
) is specific to the physically relevant case
d=3
, collision frequencies can be derived in arbitrary dimensions in a similar fashion.

The dependence of the collision frequencies on the temperature makes the BGK model considered here more complicated than many models considered in the literature, which assume that
\lambda_{i,j}
is a constant or depends only on
n_{j}
.
This is largely due to the non-Lipschitz nature of the square root function near zero and the fact that the collision frequencies are time-dependent functions.
While we focus on the hard spheres model in this paper for simplicity, the analysis provided here is readily applied to other models in the literature
[
4
]
.

2.2
Moment Equations

The focus of the present work is the space homogeneous multi-species BGK equation, obtained by removing the advection terms from (
5
).
In this case,
f_{i}=f_{i}(\boldsymbol{v},t)
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\lambda_{i,j}(M_{i,j}-f_{i}),\quad\forall i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}.

(12)

Multiplication of (
12
) by
m_{i}
,
m_{i}\boldsymbol{v}
, and
m_{i}|\boldsymbol{v}|^{2}
, followed by integration with respect to
\boldsymbol{v}
, gives equations for the mass density, momentum density, and energy density of each species

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}\rho_{i}}{\textnormal{d}t}

\displaystyle=0,

(13a)

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}(\rho_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i})}{\textnormal{d}t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}A_{i,j}(\boldsymbol{u}_{j}-\boldsymbol{u}_{i}),

(13b)

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}E_{i}}{\textnormal{d}t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}B_{i,j}\left(\frac{E_{j}}{n_{j}}-\frac{E_{i}}{n_{i}}\right)+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\sum_{j}B_{i,j}S_{i,j}(m_{i}-m_{j}),

(13c)

where the equations for
\rho_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}
and
E_{i}
are derived using the definitions for
\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}
and
T_{i,j}
from (
6
), and the
{N\times N}
matrices
A
,
B
,
C
,
D
,
F
,
G
, and
S
are given by

\displaystyle\left[A\right]_{i,j}=\frac{\rho_{i}\rho_{j}\lambda_{i,j}\lambda_{j,i}}{\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}},\;\;\left[B\right]_{i,j}=\frac{n_{i}n_{j}\lambda_{i,j}\lambda_{j,i}}{n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}},\;\;\left[S\right]_{i,j}=\left|\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}\right|^{2},\;\;\left[C\right]_{i,j}=B_{i,j}S_{i,j},

(14a)

\displaystyle\left[D\right]_{i,j}=\Big(\sum_{k}A_{i,k}\Big)\delta_{i,j},\quad\left[F\right]_{i,j}=\Big(\sum_{k}B_{i,k}\Big)\delta_{i,j},\quad\left[G\right]_{i,j}=\Big(\sum_{k}C_{i,k}\Big)\delta_{i,j}.

(14b)

According to (
13a
), each individual species mass density
\rho_{i}
(or, equivalently, number density
n_{i}
) is conserved (i.e., constant in time).
However, only the
total
momentum and energy are conserved.
Indeed, summing (
13b
) and (
13c
) over all species yields:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\textnormal{d}(\rho_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i})}{\textnormal{d}t}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\textnormal{d}E_{i}}{\textnormal{d}t}=0,

(15)

respectively.

In terms of the vectorized quantities

\boldsymbol{U}=\left[\boldsymbol{u}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{u}_{N}\right]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\boldsymbol{E}=[E_{1},\dots E_{N}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(16)

the equations in
(
13
)
take the form

\displaystyle P\frac{\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{U}}{\textnormal{d}t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(D-A)\boldsymbol{U},

(17a)

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{E}}{\textnormal{d}t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(F-B)Q^{-1}\boldsymbol{E}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}(G-C)M\mathbf{1},

(17b)

where

P=\text{diag}\{\rho_{k}\}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
,

Q=\text{diag}\{n_{k}\}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
,
and
M=\text{diag}\{m_{k}\}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
.
To simplify the analysis later, it will be convenient to introduce variables

\boldsymbol{W}=P^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{U}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}\quad\text{ and }\quad\boldsymbol{\xi}=Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{E}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

(18)

In terms of
\boldsymbol{W}
and
\boldsymbol{\xi}
, (
17
) takes the form

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{W}}{\textnormal{d}t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}Z\boldsymbol{W},

(19a)

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{\xi}}{\textnormal{d}t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\widehat{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}(G-C)M\mathbf{1},

(19b)

where

\displaystyle Z=P^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D-A)P^{-\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}\quad\text{ and }\quad\widehat{Z}=Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}(F-B)Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}.

(20)

3
Properties of the ODE system

The main result of this section is to show that there is a unique solution to the ODE system (
17
) for all physically meaningful initial conditions.
When the collision frequencies are constant in time, such a result follows directly from the standard ODE theory for systems with Lipschtiz dynamics.
However, because the collision frequencies in (
11
) depend on
\sqrt{T_{i}}
, only a local Lipschitz condition can be obtained for the right-hand side of (
17
).
Thus the key step is to show that the species temperatures are bounded below away from zero.
This bound will also be important for establishing exponential decay to steady-state in
Section

4
.
We also establish upper and lower bounds on the bulk velocity components that will be used in the decay estimates.

3.1
Positivity of the Temperature

To make the following proofs easier to manage, we introduce the parameters

\displaystyle\alpha_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}}{\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+\rho_{j}\lambda_{j,i}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta_{i,j}=\frac{n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}}{n_{i}\lambda_{i,j}+n_{j}\lambda_{j,i}},

(21)

which satisfy the conditions
\alpha_{i,j}+\alpha_{j,i}=1=\beta_{i,j}+\beta_{j,i}
.
With these parameters, the mixture values in (
6
) can be expressed as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}=\alpha_{i,j}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}+\alpha_{j,i}\boldsymbol{u}_{j}\quad\text{and}\quad T_{i,j}=\beta_{i,j}T_{i}+\beta_{j,i}T_{j}+\frac{1}{d}m_{i}\alpha_{j,i}\beta_{i,j}\left|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}-\boldsymbol{u}_{j}\right|^{2}.

(22)

Lemma 3.1
.

The temperatures
T_{i}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,N\}
, satisfy the ODE

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}T_{i}}{\textnormal{d}t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\lambda_{i,j}\beta_{j,i}(T_{j}-T_{i})+\frac{1}{\varepsilon d}\sum_{j}\lambda_{i,j}m_{i}\alpha_{j,i}(\alpha_{j,i}+\beta_{i,j})|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}-\boldsymbol{u}_{j}|^{2}.

(23)

Proof.

The proof is a direct calculation.
From (
13b
) and the fact that
A_{i,j}=\alpha_{j,i}\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}
, it follows that

\rho_{i}\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\rho_{i}\lambda_{i,j}(2\alpha_{j,i}\langle\boldsymbol{u}_{i},\boldsymbol{u}_{j}\rangle-2\alpha_{j,i}|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2}),

(24)

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the standard Euclidean inner product on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
The equation in (
23
) can be obtained by
(i) using (
2
) to express (
13c
) in terms of the temperatures and velocities;
(ii) invoking (
24
) to eliminate the time derivative of
|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2}
;
(iii) using the formula in (
22
) to replace
\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}
wherever it appears;
and (iv) applying the elementary relations

\alpha_{j,i}^{2}=\alpha_{j,i}-\alpha_{i,j}\alpha_{j,i}=2\alpha_{j,i}+\alpha_{i,j}^{2}-1
.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that for some
t_{\textnormal{f}}>0
, there exists a local solution
\boldsymbol{U}\in C^{1}\left([0,t_{\textnormal{f}}];\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}\right)

and
\boldsymbol{E}\in C^{1}\left([0,t_{\textnormal{f}}];\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
to the system given by (
17
), with initial conditions
\boldsymbol{U}(0)
and
\boldsymbol{E}(0)
such that
T_{i}(0)>0
for all
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
.
Then
T_{i}(t)\geq T_{\min}\coloneqq\min_{k}\{T_{k}(0)\}
for all
t\in[0,t_{\textnormal{f}}]
and all
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
.

Proof.

The proof relies on an integrating factor technique that yields a Grönwall-type estimate.
Define a lower temperature envelope

T_{\star}(t)=\min_{k}\{T_{k}(t)\}
.
From (
23
) it follows that, for
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}T_{i}}{\textnormal{d}t}+c_{i}(t)T_{i}(t)

\displaystyle\geq c_{i}(t)T_{\star}(t),\quad\text{ where }\quad c_{i}(t)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\lambda_{ij}(t)\beta_{ji}(t).

(25)

Let
a_{i}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}c_{i}(s)\textnormal{d}s
.
Then
a^{\prime}_{i}(t)=c_{i}(t)
and

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\left(e^{a_{i}(t)}T_{i}(t)\right)\overset{\eqref{EQ:diff_ineq_1}}{\geq}e^{a_{i}(t)}c_{i}(t)T_{\star}(t)=\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\left(e^{a_{i}(t)}\right)T_{\star}(t).

(26)

For each
\gamma>0
, define the set

\displaystyle S_{\gamma}=\left\{t\in[0,t_{\textnormal{f}}]\;\middle|\;T_{\star}(t)<\frac{1}{1+\gamma}T_{\star}(0)\right\}.

(27)

We now argue by contradiction.
Assume that
S_{\gamma}
is nonempty and let
t_{\star}=\inf S_{\gamma}
.
Then by definition,

\displaystyle T_{\star}(t_{\star})\leq\frac{1}{1+\gamma}T_{\star}(0)<T_{\star}(0).

(28)

However, because
T_{i}
is continuous on
[0,t_{\textnormal{f}}]
, it follows that

T_{\star}(t)\geq T_{\star}(t_{\star})
for every
t\in[0,t_{\star}]
, which, along with (
26
), implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\left(e^{a_{i}(t)}T_{i}(t)\right)\geq\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\left(e^{a_{i}(t)}\right)T_{\star}(t_{\star}),\qquad\forall t\in[0,t_{\star}].

(29)

Integrate both sides of (
29
) on
[0,t_{\star}]
, multiply the result by
e^{-a_{i}(t_{\star})}\geq 0
, and use
T_{i}(0)\geq T_{\star}(0)
, to get

\begin{split}T_{i}(t_{\star})&\geq T_{i}(0)e^{-a_{i}(t_{\star})}+T_{\star}(t_{\star})\left(1-e^{-a_{i}(t_{\star})}\right)\\
&\geq T_{\star}(0)e^{-a_{i}(t_{\star})}+T_{\star}(t_{\star})\left(1-e^{-a_{i}(t_{\star})}\right).\end{split}

(30)

Define
i_{\star}
such that
T_{i_{\star}}(t_{\star})=T_{\star}(t_{\star})
.
If
i=i_{\star}
, then (
30
) becomes

\displaystyle T_{\star}(t_{\star})\equiv T_{i_{\star}}(t_{\star})\geq T_{\star}(0)e^{-a_{i_{\star}}(t_{\star})}+T_{\star}(t_{\star})\left(1-e^{-a_{i_{\star}}(t_{\star})}\right),

(31)

which, after some simple algebra, implies that
T_{\star}(t_{\star})\geq T_{\star}(0)
.
However this results contradicts
(
28
)
which means that no such
t_{\star}
exists, and the set
S_{\gamma}
must be empty.
As a consequence,
T_{\star}(t)\geq\frac{1}{1+\gamma}T_{\star}(0)
for all
t\in[0,t_{\textnormal{f}}]
.
Since
\gamma>0
is arbitrary, the limit
\gamma\to 0
yields
T_{\star}(t)\geq T_{\star}(0)=T_{\textnormal{min}}
for all
t\in[0,t_{\textnormal{f}}]
.
The proof is complete.
∎

3.2
Velocity Bounds

For each component
k\in\{1,\cdots,d\}
, define the lower and upper velocity envelopes

\displaystyle\underline{u}_{k}(t)=\min_{j}\{U_{j,k}(t)\},\qquad\overline{u}_{k}(t)=\max_{j}\{U_{j,k}(t)\},

(32)

and the components of the vector
\boldsymbol{u}_{\max}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle\left[\boldsymbol{u}_{\max}(t)\right]_{k}

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\left|\underline{u}_{k}(t)\right|,\left|\overline{u}_{k}(t)\right|\right\}.

(33)

Theorem 3.3
.

Suppose that the assumptions of
Theorem

3.2
hold.
Then for each
k\in\{1,\cdots,d\}
, the velocity components follow the inequalities

\displaystyle\underline{u}_{k}(0)\leq\underline{u}_{k}(t)\leq U_{i,k}(t)\leq\overline{u}_{k}(t)\leq\overline{u}_{k}(0)

(34)

for all
t\in[0,t_{\textnormal{f}}]
and all
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
.
Further, for any
t\in[0,t_{\textnormal{f}}]
, and all
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
,

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}(t)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\max}(t)\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\max}(0)\right\|_{2}\eqqcolon u_{\max}.

(35)

Proof.

The proofs of the inequalities in (
34
) are similar to the proof of the temperature lower bound given in
Theorem

3.2
.
From the velocity equation, (
13b
),

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}U_{i,k}+\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\lambda_{i,j}\alpha_{j,i}\right)U_{i,k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\lambda_{i,j}\alpha_{j,i}U_{j,k},

(36)

we obtain, using the definitions

U_{j,k}(t)\leq\overline{u}_{k}(t)
, and
c_{i}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\lambda_{i,j}\alpha_{j,i}
,

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}U_{i,k}+c_{i}(t)U_{i,k}\leq c_{i}(t)\overline{u}_{k}(t).

(37)

From this point the proof of the upper bound in (
34
) follows that of
Theorem

3.2
, with (
37
) being the analog of (
25
).
The proof of the lower bound is even more similar.

Finally, the bound in (
35
) follows from (
34
).
Specifically, if
a
,
b
, and
c
are real-valued scalars such that
a\leq b\leq c
, then
|b|\leq\max\{|a|,|c|\}
.
Hence

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\right\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}U_{i,k}^{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{d}\max\left\{\left|\underline{u}_{k}(0)\right|,\left|\overline{u}_{k}(0)\right|\right\}^{2}=\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\max}(0)\right\|_{2}^{2}=u_{\max}^{2}.

(38)

∎

3.3
Existence and Uniqueness

According to (
13a
), the number densities
n_{i}
and mass densities
\rho_{i}
are non-negative constants in time:

n_{i}(0)=n_{i}(t),\quad\rho_{i}(0)=\rho_{i}(t)=m_{i}n_{i}(t),\quad\forall\,i\in\{1,\cdots,N\},\quad t\in[0,t_{\textnormal{f}}].

(39)

To avoid any degenerate cases, we assume that
n_{\textnormal{min}}\coloneqq\min_{j}n_{j}(0)>0
and
\rho_{\textnormal{min}}\coloneqq\min_{j}\rho_{j}(0)>0
.
Then, given any velocity-energy pair
(\boldsymbol{u}_{i},E_{i})
, the associated temperature
T_{i}
is given by (cf. (
2
))

T_{i}=\vartheta_{i}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i},E_{i})\coloneqq\frac{2}{dn_{i}}E_{i}-\frac{m_{i}}{d}|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2},

(40)

and the set of all realizable velocity and energy states is given by

\mathcal{R}=\left\{({\boldsymbol{U}},\boldsymbol{E})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\;\Big|\;\vartheta_{i}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i},E_{i})\geq 0,\;\forall\,i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}\right\},

(41)

where
\vartheta_{i}
is defined in (
40
).
Given positive scalars
T_{\textnormal{min}}\in(0,\infty)
and
E_{\textnormal{tot}}\in(0,\infty)
, let

\mathcal{D}(T_{\textnormal{min}},E_{\textnormal{tot}})=\left\{({\boldsymbol{U}},\boldsymbol{E})\in\mathcal{R}\;\Bigg|\;\sum_{i=1}^{N}E_{i}=E_{\textnormal{tot}},\;\vartheta_{i}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i},E_{i})\geq T_{\textnormal{min}},\;\forall\,i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}\right\}

(42)

and recall that by
Theorem

3.3
,

|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|\leq u_{\max}\eqqcolon\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{\max}(0)\right\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{\frac{2E_{\textnormal{tot}}}{\rho_{\textnormal{min}}}}

for all
(\boldsymbol{U},\boldsymbol{E})\in\mathcal{D}(T_{\textnormal{min}},E_{\textnormal{tot}})
.
Thus
\mathcal{D}(T_{\textnormal{min}},E_{\textnormal{tot}})
is a closed and bounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
T_{\textnormal{min}}\in(0,\infty)
and
E_{\textnormal{tot}}\in(0,\infty)
be given.
Suppose that for some
t_{\textnormal{f}}>0
, there exists a local solution
\boldsymbol{U}\in C^{1}\left([0,t_{\textnormal{f}}];\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}\right)
and
\boldsymbol{E}\in C^{1}\left([0,t_{\textnormal{f}}];\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
of the system in (
17
) with initial condition
(\boldsymbol{U}^{0},\boldsymbol{E}^{0})\in\mathcal{D}(T_{\textnormal{min}},E_{\textnormal{tot}})
.
Then for all
t\in[0,t_{\textnormal{f}}],

(\boldsymbol{U}(t),\boldsymbol{E}(t))\in\mathcal{D}(T_{\textnormal{min}},E_{\textnormal{tot}})
.
In particular,
T_{\textnormal{min}}\leq T_{i}(t)\leq T_{\textnormal{max}}\coloneqq\frac{2}{dn_{\textnormal{min}}}E_{\textnormal{tot}}
for all
t\in[0,t_{\textnormal{f}}]
.

Proof.

To conclude that
(\boldsymbol{U}(t),\boldsymbol{E}(t))\in\mathcal{D}
for all
t\in[0,t_{\textnormal{f}}]
, two conditions must be satisfied.
The first condition:

\sum_{i=1}^{N}E_{i}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}E_{i}(0)=E_{\textnormal{tot}},\quad\forall\,t\in[0,t_{\textnormal{f}}],

(43)

follows immediately from (
15
), and the upper bound
T_{i}(t)\leq T_{\textnormal{max}}
follows:

T_{i}(t)=\frac{2}{dn_{i}}E_{i}-\frac{m_{i}}{d}|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2}\leq\frac{2}{dn_{\textnormal{min}}}E_{\textnormal{tot}}=T_{\textnormal{max}}.

(44)

The second condition:

T_{\textnormal{min}}\leq T_{i}(t)=\vartheta(\boldsymbol{u}_{i}(t),E_{i}(t)),\quad\forall\,t\in[0,t_{\textnormal{f}}],

(45)

is a direct consequence of
Theorem

3.2
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose that
m_{i}>0
and
n_{i}>0
for all
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
.
Then for any
(\boldsymbol{U}^{0},\boldsymbol{E}^{0})\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{R}
, there exists a global, unique solution
(\boldsymbol{U},\boldsymbol{E})\in C^{1}([0,\infty);\mathcal{R})
of the system (
17
) with the initial conditions
(\boldsymbol{U}(0),\boldsymbol{E}(0))=(\boldsymbol{U}^{0},\boldsymbol{E}^{0})
.
Moreover, the associated temperatures
T_{i}(t)=\vartheta_{i}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i}(t),E_{i}(t))
are bounded below by their initial values; that is

\min_{i}T_{i}(t)\geq\min_{i}T_{i}(0),\qquad\forall t>0.

(46)

Proof.

The system (
17
) can be written as

\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\begin{pmatrix}\boldsymbol{U}\\
\boldsymbol{E}\end{pmatrix}=\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{U},\boldsymbol{E})\quad\text{where}\quad\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{U},\boldsymbol{E})\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}P^{-1}(D-A)\boldsymbol{U}\\
-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}(F-B)Q^{-1}\boldsymbol{E}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}(G-C)M\mathbf{1}\end{pmatrix}.

(47)

Let
T_{\textnormal{min}}=\min_{i}T_{i}(0)
and
E_{\textnormal{tot}}=\sum_{i}E_{i}(0)
.
Since
(\boldsymbol{U},\boldsymbol{E})\in\operatorname{int}\mathcal{R}
, it follows that
T_{\textnormal{min}}>0
and
E_{\textnormal{tot}}>0
.
Moreover, there exists an
\epsilon>0
such that the closed
\epsilon
-neighborhood of
\mathcal{D}(T_{\textnormal{min}},E_{\textnormal{tot}})
, denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}(T_{\textnormal{min}},E_{\textnormal{tot}})
, is contained in
\operatorname{int}\mathcal{R}
, i.e.,
\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}(T_{\textnormal{min}},E_{\textnormal{tot}})\subset\operatorname{int}\mathcal{R}
.
In particular,

\min_{i}T_{i}=\min_{i}\vartheta(\boldsymbol{u}_{i},E_{i}).

is bounded below on
\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}(T_{\textnormal{min}},E_{\textnormal{tot}})
.
Hence,
f
is Lipschitz on
\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}(T_{\textnormal{min}},E_{\textnormal{tot}})
with Lipschitz constant
L>0
and bound
\|\boldsymbol{f}(\boldsymbol{U},\boldsymbol{E})\|_{2}\leq\mathcal{M}<\infty
.
Since
(\boldsymbol{U}^{0},\boldsymbol{E}^{0})\in\mathcal{D}
, there is a number
\beta=\beta(\epsilon)>0
, independent of the point
(\boldsymbol{U}^{0},\boldsymbol{E}^{0})
, such that the closed ball
\overline{B}\left((\boldsymbol{U},\boldsymbol{E}),\beta\right)\subset\mathcal{D}_{\epsilon}
.
Appealing to the Picard-Lindelöf Theorem
A.1
, there exists a unique solution on the interval
[0,\delta]
, where
\delta=\min\left(\frac{1}{2L},\frac{\beta}{\mathcal{M}}\right)
.
By
Lemma

3.4
,
(\boldsymbol{U}(t),\boldsymbol{E}(t))\in\mathcal{D}
, for all
t\in[0,\delta]
.
Since
(\boldsymbol{U}(\delta),\boldsymbol{E}(\delta))\in\mathcal{D}
, we can apply the Picard-Lindelöf Theorem to (
17
) again, but with the initial condition
=(\boldsymbol{U}(\delta),\boldsymbol{E}(\delta))
, and thereby extend the unique solution to the interval
[0,2\delta]
.
Again,
(\boldsymbol{U}(2\delta),\boldsymbol{E}(2\delta))\in\mathcal{D}
, and the process can be continued indefinitely, with, crucially, no degradation of
\delta>0
, since
\mathcal{M}
and
L
cannot grow larger and
\beta
does not shrink.
The result follows.
∎

4
Long-time Behavior

Having proven the existence of unique, global, physically realizable solutions for
(
17
), we now seek to characterize the asymptotic behavior of those solutions.
In particular, we want to show that solutions converge to

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{U}^{\infty}(t)=\mathbf{1}\left(\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}\right)^{\top}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\boldsymbol{E}^{\infty}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}(t)|^{2}}{2}P\mathbf{1}+\frac{dT^{\infty}(t)}{2}Q\mathbf{1},

(48)

where

\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}(t)=\frac{\sum_{i}\rho_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}(t)}{\sum_{i}\rho_{i}}\quad\text{and}\quad T^{\infty}(t)=\frac{\sum_{i}n_{i}T_{i}(t)}{\sum_{i}n_{i}}+\frac{\sum_{i}\rho_{i}(|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}(t)|^{2}-|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}(t)|^{2})}{d\sum_{i}n_{i}}

(49)

are constant functions of time.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
(\boldsymbol{U},\boldsymbol{E})\in C^{1}\left([0,\infty);\mathcal{R}\right)
is a unique global-in-time solution to the system (
17
) with assumptions as given in
Theorem

3.5
.
Further let
\boldsymbol{T}\in C^{1}\left([0,\infty);\mathbb{R}^{N}\right)
be the associated temperature vector, whose components satisfy (
23
).
Then
\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}
,
T^{\infty}
, and
\boldsymbol{E}^{\infty}
are time invariant quantities, and
T^{\infty}>0
.

Proof.

By conservation of mass, (
13a
), and total momentum, (
15
),
\sum_{i}\rho_{i}
and
\sum_{i}\rho_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}
are constant.
Thus
\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}
is time invariant.
To show that
T^{\infty}
is constant in time, multiply (
49
) by the expression
\frac{d}{2}\sum_{i}n_{i}
, which is constant by (
13a
), to obtain

\begin{split}\frac{d}{2}\left(\sum_{i}n_{i}\right)T^{\infty}&=\sum_{i}\left[\frac{d}{2}n_{i}T_{i}+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{i}|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{i}\rho_{i}\right)|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2}\\
&=\sum_{i}E_{i}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{i}\rho_{i}\right)|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2}.\end{split}

(50)

The first term on the right-hand side of (
50
) is constant by conservation of total energy, (
15
); the second term is constant by conservation of species mass, (
13a
), and the fact that
\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}
is constant.
Thus
T^{\infty}
is time invariant.
It follows immediately from (
48
) that
\boldsymbol{E}^{\infty}
is also time invariant.

Since
(\boldsymbol{U}(t),\boldsymbol{E}(t))\in\mathcal{R}
for all
t>0
, it follows that
T_{i}(t)=\vartheta(\boldsymbol{u}_{i}(t),E_{i}(t))\geq 0
for all
t>0
.
To show that
T^{\infty}>0,
it is sufficient to show that
\sum_{i}\rho_{i}(|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2}-|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2})\geq 0
.
Let
r_{i}=\frac{\rho_{i}}{\sum_{k}\rho_{k}}>0
.
Then
\sum_{i}r_{i}=1
and
|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2}=(\sum_{i}r_{i}\boldsymbol{u}_{i})^{\top}(\sum_{j}r_{j}\boldsymbol{u}_{j})=\sum_{i}\sum_{j}r_{i}r_{j}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{u}_{j}
.
Therefore

\begin{split}\sum_{i}\rho_{i}(|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2}-|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2})&=\sum_{k}\rho_{k}\left[\sum_{i,j}r_{i}r_{j}|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2}-\sum_{i,j}r_{i}r_{j}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{u}_{j}\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k}\rho_{k}\sum_{i,j}r_{i}r_{j}\left|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}-\boldsymbol{u}_{j}\right|^{2}\geq 0.\end{split}

(51)

∎

Remark 4.2
.

The quantities
\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}
and
T^{\infty}
were referred to as the mixture mean velocity and mixture temperature, respectively, in
[
12
]
.
However, in this paper, we reserve these terms for the quantities
\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}
and
T_{i,j}
, respectively.

The convergence of
\boldsymbol{u}_{i}
,
T_{i}
, and
\boldsymbol{E}
as
t\to\infty
is established by the following result, the proof of which is the focus of the rest of this section.

Theorem 4.3
.

Under the assumptions of
4.1
,
for all
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}(t)=\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty},\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}T_{i}(t)=T^{\infty},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\lim_{t\to\infty}\boldsymbol{E}(t)=\boldsymbol{E}^{\infty},

(52)

and bounds for the decay rates of the bulk velocities and energies are given by

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}(t)-\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\boldsymbol{U}}e^{-\frac{z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t},\qquad i\in\{1,\dots,N\}

(53)

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{E}(t)-\boldsymbol{E}^{\infty}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}e^{-\frac{\widehat{z}_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}+C_{2}\frac{e^{-\frac{z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}-e^{-\frac{\widehat{z}_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}}{\widehat{z}_{\min}-z_{\min}},

(54)

where
z_{\min}>0
and
\widehat{z}_{\min}>0
are lower bounds on the positive eigenvalues of
Z
and
\widehat{Z}
, respectively, and
C_{\boldsymbol{U}}>0
,
C_{1}>0
, and
C_{2}>0
are constants depending only on the initial conditions of the system.

4.1
Null Spaces of
D-A
,
Z
,
F-B
, and
\widehat{Z}

To prove

Theorem

4.3
, we first characterize the null spaces of the matrices
D-A
,
Z
,
F-B
, and
\widehat{Z}
, which are defined in (
14
), and (
20
).

Lemma 4.4
.

The matrices
D-A
and
Z
are symmetric positive semi-definite (SPSD), each with a one dimensional null space.
In particular,
\mathcal{N}(D-A)={\textnormal{span}}(\{\mathbf{1}\})
and
\mathcal{N}(Z)={\textnormal{span}}\left(\left\{P^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{1}\right\}\right)
.
Moreover, the null space and range space of
Z
are invariant with respect to time.

Proof.

Clearly
D-A
and
Z
are symmetric by inspection.
For any
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

\boldsymbol{y}^{\top}(D-A)\boldsymbol{y}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}A_{i,j}(y_{i}-y_{j})^{2}\geq 0.

(55)

Moreover, since
A_{i,j}>0
,
\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}A_{i,j}(y_{i}-y_{j})^{2}=0
if and only if
y_{i}=y_{j}
for all
i
and
j
, in which case
\boldsymbol{y}=c\mathbf{1}
for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
Thus
D-A
is SPSD, with a one-dimensional null space spanned by the eigenvector
\mathbf{1}
.
Similarly,
\boldsymbol{y}^{\top}Z\boldsymbol{y}=(P^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{y})^{\top}(D-A)(P^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{y})\geq 0
, and
Z\boldsymbol{y}=\mathbf{0}\iff P^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{y}=c\mathbf{1}\iff\boldsymbol{y}=cP^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{1}
, for some
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
Thus
Z
is SPSD, with a one dimensional null space spanned by the eigenvector
P^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{1}
.
By conservation of mass, (
13a
),
P^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{1}
is indepedent of time.
Thus, the null space of
Z
is invariant with respect to time.
The symmetry of
Z
implies that
\mathcal{R}(Z)=\mathcal{N}(Z)^{\perp}
is also invariant.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

The matrices
F-B
and
\widehat{Z}
are symmetric positive semi-definite (SPSD), with
\mathcal{N}(F-B)={\textnormal{span}}\left(\left\{\mathbf{1}\right\}\right)
and
\mathcal{N}(\widehat{Z})={\textnormal{span}}\left(\left\{Q^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{1}\right\}\right)
.
Moreover, the null space and range space of
\widehat{Z}
are invariant with respect to time.

Proof.

The proof follows closely that of
Lemma

4.4
.
Details are left to the reader.
∎

4.2
Velocity Relaxation Proof

Theorem 4.6
.

Under the assumptions of
4.1
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}(t)=\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}

and

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}(t)-\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\boldsymbol{U}}e^{-\frac{z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t},\quad i=1,\cdots,N,

(56)

where
C_{\boldsymbol{U}}>0
is a constant that depends on the initial conditions of the system and
z_{\min}>0
is a lower bound on the positive eigenvalues of
Z
.

Proof.

The proof is based on (
19a
) which is equivalent to (
17a
).
Let
\boldsymbol{W}^{\infty}=P^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{U}^{\infty}
, where
\boldsymbol{U}^{\infty}=\mathbf{1}\left(\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}\right)^{\top}
is given in (
48
).
Then
Lemma

4.4
implies that
Z\boldsymbol{W}^{\infty}=\mathbf{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times d}
.
Moreover, since
P
and
\boldsymbol{U}^{\infty}
are independent of time, so too is
\boldsymbol{W}^{\infty}
.
Thus in terms of
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{W}}\coloneqq\boldsymbol{W}-\boldsymbol{W}^{\infty}
, (
19a
) takes the form

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{W}}}{\textnormal{d}t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}Z\widetilde{\boldsymbol{W}}.

(57)

Write the element
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{W}}
as a sum of the null and range space components with respect to
Z
:
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{W}}=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{W}}_{\mathcal{R}}+\widetilde{\boldsymbol{W}}_{\mathcal{N}}
.
As shown in
Appendix

C
,
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{W}}_{\mathcal{N}}=0
.
Hence
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{W}}=V\boldsymbol{A}
, where
the columns of
V=[\boldsymbol{v}_{1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{v}_{N-1}]\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times(N-1)}
are a set of orthonormal eigenvectors of
Z
that span
\mathcal{R}(Z)
, and
\boldsymbol{A}=[\boldsymbol{a}_{1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{a}_{d}]\in\mathbb{R}^{(N-1)\times d}
.
Using this formulation, the ODE system (
57
) becomes

\frac{\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{A}}{\textnormal{d}t}=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}V^{\top}ZV\boldsymbol{A}.

(58)

The eigenvalues of
V^{\top}ZV
are strictly positive and can be bounded below by
z_{\min}\coloneqq\frac{N\min_{i,j}A_{i,j}}{\max\{\rho_{k}\}}

(see
Appendix

B
).
Thus
\left(\boldsymbol{A},V^{\top}ZV\boldsymbol{A}\right)_{\text{F}}\geq z_{\min}\left\|\boldsymbol{A}\right\|_{\text{F}}^{2}
, where
\|\cdot\|_{\text{F}}
is the Frobenius norm.
Let
s(t)=\left\|\boldsymbol{A}\right\|_{\text{F}}^{2}
.
Then, using (
58
),

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textnormal{d}s}{\textnormal{d}t}=\left(\boldsymbol{A},\frac{\textnormal{d}\boldsymbol{A}}{\textnormal{d}t}\right)_{\text{F}}=\left(\boldsymbol{A},-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}V^{\top}ZV\boldsymbol{A}\right)_{\text{F}}\leq-\frac{z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}\left\|\boldsymbol{A}\right\|_{\text{F}}^{2}=-\frac{z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}s,

(59)

which implies that

\left\|\boldsymbol{A}(t)\right\|_{\text{F}}^{2}\leq\left\|\boldsymbol{A}(0)\right\|_{\text{F}}^{2}e^{-\frac{2z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}.

(60)

Since
V^{\top}V=I_{N-1}
, it follows that
\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{W}}\|_{\text{F}}^{2}=\left\|V\boldsymbol{A}\right\|_{\text{F}}^{2}=\left\|\boldsymbol{A}\right\|_{\text{F}}^{2}.

Thus, (
60
) becomes

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{W}(t)-\boldsymbol{W}^{\infty}\right\|_{\text{F}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\boldsymbol{W}(0)-\boldsymbol{W}^{\infty}\right\|_{\text{F}}^{2}e^{-\frac{2z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}.

(61)

Since
\boldsymbol{W}=P^{\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{U}
, it follows that

\begin{split}\left\|\boldsymbol{W}-\boldsymbol{W}^{\infty}\right\|_{\text{F}}^{2}&=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left[\rho_{j}\left((\boldsymbol{u}_{j})_{k}-\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}_{k}\right)^{2}\right]\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\rho_{j}\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{j}-\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}\right\|_{2}^{2}\geq\min\{\rho_{k}\}\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}-\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}\right\|_{2}^{2},\end{split}

(62)

for any
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
.
Thus, (
61
) gives the decay bound

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}(t)-\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\min\{\rho_{k}\}}\left\|\boldsymbol{W}(t)-\boldsymbol{W}^{\infty}\right\|_{\text{F}}^{2}\leq C_{\boldsymbol{U}}^{2}e^{-\frac{2z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t},

(63)

where

C_{\boldsymbol{U}}=\frac{1}{\min\{\rho_{k}\}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left\|\boldsymbol{W}^{0}-\boldsymbol{W}^{\infty}\right\|_{\text{F}}.

Thus,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\boldsymbol{u}_{i}(t)=\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty},
as desired.
∎

4.3
Energy Relaxation Proof

Theorem 4.7
.

With the same assumptions as in
4.1
,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\boldsymbol{E}(t)=\boldsymbol{E}^{\infty}

and

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{E}(t)-\boldsymbol{E}^{\infty}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}e^{-\frac{\widehat{z}_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}+C_{2}\frac{e^{-\frac{z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}-e^{-\frac{\widehat{z}_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}}{\widehat{z}_{\min}-z_{\min}},

(64)

where
C_{1}>0
and
C_{2}>0
are constants that depend on the initial conditions of the system (
17
) and
\widehat{z}_{\min}
is a lower bound on the positive eigenvalues of
\widehat{Z}
.

Proof.

The proof is based on (
19
), which is equivalent to (
17
).
Set
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\infty}\coloneqq Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\boldsymbol{E}^{\infty}
, and note that

\displaystyle\widehat{Z}\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\infty}=\frac{|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2}}{2}Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}(F-B)M\mathbf{1}.

(65)

Since
Q
and
\boldsymbol{E}^{\infty}
are time invariant, so too is
\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\infty}
.
Hence
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\coloneqq\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\infty}
satisfies the ODE

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}}{\textnormal{d}t}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\widehat{Z}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[(G-C)-|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2}(F-B)\right]M\mathbf{1}.

(66)

Consider the null and range space components of
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}
with respect to
\widehat{Z}
:
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\mathcal{R}}+\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\mathcal{N}}
.
It can be shown that
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\mathcal{N}}\equiv\mathbf{0}
.(See
Appendix

C
.)
Therefore

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}=\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}_{\mathcal{R}}=\widehat{V}\boldsymbol{b},

where

\widehat{V}=\left[\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\boldsymbol{v}}_{N-1}\right]\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times(N-1)}

is a matrix whose columns form an orthonormal basis for the range space of
\widehat{Z}
, and
\boldsymbol{b}=\widehat{V}^{\top}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
.
Multiplication of (
66
) by
\boldsymbol{b}^{\top}\widehat{V}^{\top}
gives

\frac{1}{2}\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\left\|\boldsymbol{b}\right\|_{2}^{2}=-\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{b}^{\top}\widehat{V}^{\top}\widehat{Z}\widehat{V}\boldsymbol{b}+\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\boldsymbol{b}^{\top}\widehat{V}^{\top}Q^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[(G-C)-|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2}(F-B)\right]M\mathbf{1}.

(67)

The eigenvalues of
\widehat{V}^{\top}\widehat{Z}\widehat{V}
are strictly positive and can be bounded below by

\widehat{z}_{\min}\coloneqq\frac{N\min_{i,j}B_{i,j}}{\max\{n_{k}\}}

(see
Appendix

B
),
which gives the bound
\boldsymbol{b}^{\top}\widehat{V}^{\top}\widehat{Z}\widehat{V}\boldsymbol{b}\geq\widehat{z}_{\min}\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{2}^{2}
for any
\boldsymbol{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
.
With this bound and an application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
67
) becomes

\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\left\|\boldsymbol{b}\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq-\frac{2\widehat{z}_{\min}}{\varepsilon}\left\|\boldsymbol{b}\right\|_{2}^{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\frac{\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{2}}{\min\{n_{k}\}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left\|\left[(G-C)-|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2}(F-B)\right]M\mathbf{1}\right\|_{2}.

(68)

The next step is to bound the source term in (
68
).
Standard norm inequalities and the definitions in (
14
), and
Appendix

B
, give

\left\|\left[\left(G-C\right)-|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2}\left(F-B\right)\right]M\mathbf{1}\right\|_{2}\leq 2\max\{m_{k}\}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{N}B_{\max}\left||\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}|^{2}-|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2}\right|.

(69)

Meanwhile, to bound
\left||\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}|^{2}-|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2}\right|
, we use (i) the decay bound in (
56
), (ii) the definitions of
\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}
and
\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}
, (iii) the triangle inequality and Cauchy Schwarz inequality, (iv) the fact that
\alpha_{i,j}+\alpha_{j,i}=1
, and (v) the fact that
|\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}|\leq\max_{i,j}\{|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|,|\boldsymbol{u}_{j}|\}\leq u_{\max}
to obtain

\left||\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}|^{2}-|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2}\right|=\left|(\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}+\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty})^{\top}(\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}-\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty})\right|\leq 2u_{\max}\left\|\boldsymbol{u}_{i,j}-\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}\right\|_{2}\\
\leq 2u_{\max}\left(\alpha_{i,j}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}-\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}\|_{2}+\alpha_{j,i}\|\boldsymbol{u}_{j}-\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}\|_{2}\right)\leq 2C_{\boldsymbol{U}}u_{\max}e^{-\frac{z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}.

(70)

Together (
69
)
and (
70
) give

\displaystyle\left\|\left[(G-C)-|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2}(F-B)\right]M\mathbf{1}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4N(N-1)C_{\boldsymbol{U}}u_{\max}B_{\max}\max\{m_{k}\}e^{-\frac{z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t},

(71)

and (
68
) becomes, after dividing through by
\left\|\boldsymbol{b}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\left\|\boldsymbol{b}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\widehat{z}_{\min}}{\varepsilon}\left\|\boldsymbol{b}\right\|_{2}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}C_{0}e^{-\frac{z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t},

(72)

where
C_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{4N(N-1)C_{\boldsymbol{U}}u_{\max}B_{\max}\max\{m_{k}\}}{\min\{n_{k}\}^{\frac{1}{2}}}
, or equivalently,

\displaystyle\frac{\textnormal{d}}{\textnormal{d}t}\left(\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{2}e^{\frac{\widehat{z}_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{0}}{\varepsilon}e^{\frac{\widehat{z}_{\min}-z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}.

(73)

Integrating both sides above in

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{b}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\boldsymbol{b}^{0}\|_{2}e^{-\frac{\widehat{z}_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}+\frac{C_{0}}{\varepsilon}\frac{\varepsilon}{\widehat{z}_{\min}-z_{\min}}\left(e^{-\frac{z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}-e^{-\frac{\widehat{z}_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}\right).

(74)

Since
\widehat{V}^{\top}\widehat{V}=I_{N-1}
, it follows that
\|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\widehat{V}\boldsymbol{b}\|_{2}^{2}=\left\|\boldsymbol{b}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Thus, (

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\infty}\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{0}-\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\infty}\right\|_{2}e^{-\frac{\widehat{z}_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}+C_{0}\frac{e^{-\frac{z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}-e^{-\frac{\widehat{z}_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}}{\widehat{z}_{\min}-z_{\min}}.

(75)

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{\xi}-\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\infty}\right\|_{2}^{2}

(76)

it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\boldsymbol{E}(t)-\boldsymbol{E}^{\infty}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max\{n_{k}\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\left\|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{0}-\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\infty}\right\|_{2}e^{-\frac{\widehat{z}_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}+C_{0}\frac{e^{-\frac{z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}-e^{-\frac{\widehat{z}_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}}{\widehat{z}_{\min}-z_{\min}}\right),

(77)

which recovers (

C_{1}\coloneqq\max\{n_{k}\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|\boldsymbol{\xi}^{0}-\boldsymbol{\xi}^{\infty}\right\|_{2}
and

.

\lim_{t\to\infty}\boldsymbol{E}(t)=\boldsymbol{E}^{\infty}

∎

4.4
Temperature Relaxation

Corollary 4.7.1
.

With the same assumptions as in
4.1
, for each species
i\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}T_{i}(t)=T^{\infty},
and

\begin{split}\left|T_{i}-T^{\infty}\right|&\leq\frac{2}{d\min\{n_{k}\}}\left[C_{1}e^{-\frac{\widehat{z}_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}+C_{2}\frac{e^{-\frac{z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}-e^{-\frac{\widehat{z}_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}}{\widehat{z}_{\min}-z_{\min}}\right]\\
&\qquad+\frac{\max\{m_{k}\}}{d}2u_{\max}C_{\boldsymbol{U}}e^{-\frac{z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}.\end{split}

(78)

Proof.

For each
i
,

\displaystyle T_{i}=\frac{2}{dn_{i}}E_{i}-\frac{m_{i}}{d}|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad T^{\infty}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{dn_{i}}E^{\infty}_{i}-\frac{m_{i}}{d}|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2}.

(79)

Therefore

\begin{split}\left|T_{i}-T^{\infty}\right|&\leq\frac{2}{d\min\{n_{k}\}}\left|E_{i}-E^{\infty}_{i}\right|+\frac{\max\{m_{k}\}}{d}\left||\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2}-|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2}\right|.\end{split}

(80)

The velocity decay bound (
56
),
gives

\begin{split}\left||\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}|^{2}-|\boldsymbol{u}_{i}|^{2}\right|\leq 2u_{\max}\|\boldsymbol{u}^{\infty}-\boldsymbol{u}_{i}\|_{2}\leq 2u_{\max}C_{\boldsymbol{U}}e^{-\frac{z_{\min}}{\varepsilon}t}.\end{split}

(81)

Using this and the energy decay bound (
64
), the result follows.

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we have studied the moment equations associated to a recently developed multi-species BGK kinetic model for rarefied gas dynamics in the spatially homogeneous setting.
The model includes collision frequencies that, unlike most models in the literature, depend on the species temperatures.
This fact complicates the analysis of the moment equations and subsequent numerical tools.
We have proven that all species temperatures are bounded below by a positive, non-decreasing temperature envelope, thus establishing that species temperatures always remain positive.
We have also established upper and lower bounds on the components of the bulk velocity that are in terms of the initial data.

Using the lower bound on the species temperatures, we have shown that the moments always stay within a bounded, time-invariant set of physically realizable states, which, in turn, leads to the existence of global, unique solutions.
Finally, we have proved that unique equilibria exist for the moment equations, and solutions converge to these equilbria exponentially in time.
In addition, we have established bounds on the convergence rates to equilibria.
We concluded the paper with some basic numerical simulations that demonstrate some of the established theoretical behavior.

The numerical results in this paper are computed with a backward Euler method, using an iterative scheme to solve the relevant non-linear algebraic equations at each time-step.
In future work, we will describe the scheme more fully and prove convergence under a suitable time step restriction that does not require resolution of the parameter
\varepsilon
.
We will use the backward Euler method as a component in an IMEX scheme for simulating multi-species BGK models that include phase-space advection.

.

Suppose that

\boldsymbol{f}\in C(\overline{B}(\boldsymbol{u}_{0},\beta);\mathbb{R}^{d})

Theorem B.1

(Eigenvalue Bounds)
.

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=z_{0}<z_{\min}\leq z_{1}\leq\cdots\leq z_{N-1}\leq z_{\max},

(90)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\widehat{z}_{0}<\widehat{z}_{\min}\leq\widehat{z}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\widehat{z}_{N-1}\leq\widehat{z}_{\max},

(91)

where

\displaystyle z_{\min}=\frac{A_{\min}N}{\max\{\rho_{k}\}}

\displaystyle,\quad z_{\max}=\frac{A_{\max}(N-1)}{\min\{\rho_{k}\}},\quad

(92)

\displaystyle\widehat{z}_{\min}=\frac{B_{\min}N}{\max\{n_{k}\}}

\displaystyle,\quad\widehat{z}_{\max}=\frac{B_{\max}(N-1)}{\min\{n_{k}\}}.

(93)

The proofs of (

(D-A)

\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathcal{R}(D-A)=\mathcal{N}(D-A)^{\perp}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{y}^{\top}(D-A)\boldsymbol{y}\leq\frac{A_{\max}}{2}\sum_{i,j}(y_{i}-y_{j})^{2}=\frac{A_{\max}}{2}\sum_{i}\sum_{j\neq i}\left(y_{i}^{2}+y_{j}^{2}\right)={A_{\max}}(N-1)\left\|\boldsymbol{y}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(95)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{z}^{\top}Z\boldsymbol{z}

\displaystyle\geq NA_{\min}\boldsymbol{z}^{\top}P^{-1}\boldsymbol{z}\geq\frac{NA_{\min}}{\max\{\rho_{k}\}}\left\|\boldsymbol{z}\right\|_{2}^{2}\eqqcolon z_{\min}\left\|\boldsymbol{z}\right\|_{2}^{2}.

(97)

Minimization of Dirichlet energy of j − j- degree mappings between annuli
David Kalaj

Abstract
. Let 𝔸 \mathbb{A} and 𝔹 \mathbb{B} be circular annuli in the complex plane and consider the Dirichlet energy integral of j − j- degree mappings between 𝔸 \mathbb{A} and 𝔹 \mathbb{B} . Then we minimize this energy integral. The minimizer is a j − j- degree harmonic mapping between annuli 𝔸 \mathbb{A} and 𝔹 \mathbb{B} provided it exits. If such a harmonic mapping does not exist, then the minimizer is still a j − j- degree mapping which is harmonic in 𝔸 ′ ⊂ 𝔸 \mathbb{A}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{A} and it is a squeezing mapping in its complementary annulus 𝔸 ′′ = 𝔸 ∖ 𝔸 \mathbb{A}^{\prime\prime}=\mathbb{A}\setminus\mathbb{A} . Such a result is an extension of the certain result of Astala, Iwaniec and Martin [ 1 ] .

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we continue to study the minimization problem of the
Dirichlet energy
of Sobolev mappings belonging to the
W^{1,2}
class, between domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Such research is related to the principle of non-interpenetration of matter in the mathematical theory
of Nonlinear Elasticity (NE) see for example
[
15
]
. We will minimize the energy of
j-
degree mappings between certain doubly connected domains.

1.1.
Dirichlet integral

Let
D
and
\Omega
be two bounded planar domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and let
h:D\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{onto}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega
be a mapping that belongs to the Sobolev class
W^{1,2}
.
The
Dirichlet integral
(also called
conformal energy
) of
h
is given as follows:

\mathcal{E}[h]=\int_{D}|Dh(z)|^{2}dxdy,

where
z=(x,y)\in D
and
|Dh(z)|^{2}=|f_{z}|^{2}+|f_{\bar{z}}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(|f_{x}|^{2}+|f_{y}|^{2})
.

Let
j
be a positive integer and assume that
\mathcal{H}_{j}(D,\Omega)
is the class of
j-
degree smooth mappings between
D
and
\Omega
mapping the inner/outer boundary onto inner/outer boundary. Recall that we say that a mapping
f
has degree
j
in a regular point
y\in\Omega
if

j=\deg(f,\Omega,y)=\sum_{x\in f^{-1}(y)}\mathrm{sign}(\det(Df(x))).

Here regular means that
\det(Df(x))\neq 0
for
x\in f^{-1}(y)
. For non-regular points, the degree is defined throughout a sequence of regular points
y_{n}
converging to
y
. This sequence exists according to the Sard theorem. Then we say that the mapping has a
j-
degree if
g
has degree
j
in every point from the image domain. The notation of degree can be also extended to Sobolev mappings of
W^{1,2}
class, or even to the continuous mappings.

If
D
and
\Omega
are two simply connected domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
different from
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and let
\mathbb{D}
be the unit disk. Then the Riemann mapping theorem asserts that there exist conformal bijections
g:D\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{onto}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{D}
and
k:\mathbb{D}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{onto}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Omega
. Then the mapping
f(z)=k(g^{j}(z))
is
j-
defree conformal mapping between
D
and
\Omega
.

Moreover, this map is a minimizer of the Dirichlet integral throughout the
j-
degree mappings. Indeed, for every
h
we have

\mathcal{E}[h]=\int_{D}|Dh(z)|^{2}dxdy\geq\int_{D}\det(Dh(z))dxdy=j\mbox{Area}(\Omega).

The equality holds if and only if
h
is
j-
degree conformal (because in this case, one has
|Dh|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(|h_{x}|^{2}+|h_{y}|^{2}\right)=\det(Dh(z))
).

If
D
and
\Omega
are doubly connected domains, then the situation is very different.
Namely, if the doubly connected domains are not conformally equivalent, then such a conformal minimizer does not exist. However, in this case, the harmonic diffeomorphisms can be an essential replacement for the conformal diffeomorphisms. The existence problem has been studied in a large number of papers. See for example the references
[
1
,
3
,
9
,
10
,
12
]
, which deal with the existence of minimisers for two-dimensional domains and surfaces and with their properties. The authors of
[
2
]
considered a similar problem for the so-called
\sigma_{2}
energy between annuli in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
.

The problems of this kind are related to the so called
Nitsche conjecture
for harmonic mappings.
Let us briefly explain what is its formulation.

1.2.
Nitsche conjecture

Let
\mathbb{A}:=\{z:r<|z|<R\}
and
\mathbb{B}=\{w:r_{\ast}<|w|<R_{\ast}\}
be two annuli.
In 1962 J.C.C. Nitsche announced, in a short article
[
14
]
, that the existence of a harmonic homeomorphism

h\colon\mathbb{A}\overset{\textnormal{\tiny{onto}}}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{B}
, whether or not it comes from a minimal graph, yields a lower bound on
\mathrm{Mod}\,(\mathbb{B})
in terms of
\mathrm{Mod}\,(\mathbb{A})
. He conjectured that the necessary and sufficient condition for such a mapping to exist is the following inequality, now known as the
Nitsche bound

(1.1)

\frac{R_{\ast}}{r_{\ast}}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{R}{r}+\frac{r}{R}\right).

Various lower bounds for
R_{\ast}/r_{\ast}
have been obtained by Lyzzaik
[
13
]
, Weitsman
[
16
]
, Kalaj
[
8
]
.
Finally, this conjecture was solved by Kovalev, Onninen, and Iwaniec
[
4
]
who give an affirmative answer to it.

1.3.
Motivations

An indirect evidence of Nitsche conjecture has been given previously by Astala, Iwaniec and Martin in
[
1
]
. Namely they showed that the minimizer of Dirichlet energy of
W^{1,2}
- of homeomorphisms exists and present itself a
W^{1,2}
- of homeomorphism precisely when the Nitsche bound (
1.1
) is satisfied.

On the other hand side, assume that reference annulus
\mathbb{A}
is substantially fatter than
\mathbb{A}_{*}
, precisely

(1.2)

\frac{R}{r}>\frac{R_{\ast}}{r_{\ast}}+\sqrt{\frac{R^{2}_{\ast}}{r^{2}_{\ast}}-1},\ \ \ \mbox{or equivalently}\ \ \ \frac{R_{\ast}}{r_{\ast}}<\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{R}{r}+\frac{r}{R}\right).

Let
\rho
be determined by the so-called critical Nitsche equation

\frac{R_{\ast}}{r_{\ast}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{R}{\rho}+\frac{\rho}{R}\right).

Then in
[
1
]
it is showed that the minimizer within all weak
W^{1,2}
-limits of homeomorphisms:
h:\mathbb{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{onto}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{A}^{\ast}

takes the form:

h(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69054ptr_{\ast}\frac{z}{|z|},&r<|z|<\rho\ \ \ \hbox{-not harmonic, squeezing map}\\
r_{\ast}\left(\frac{z}{2\rho}+\frac{\rho}{2\bar{z}}\right),&\rho<|z|<R\ \ \ \hbox{-critical harmonic Nitsche map}.\end{array}\right.

Note that
\det(Dh(z))=|h_{z}|^{2}-|h_{\bar{z}}|^{2}=\frac{r_{\ast}^{2}}{4|z|^{2}}-\frac{r_{\ast}^{2}}{4|z|^{2}}\equiv 0
for
r\leq|z|\leq\rho
. This minimizer is unique up to the rotation of annuli as it is shown in
[
1
]
.

1.4.
Statement of the problem and the formulation of the main result

Let
\mathbb{X}
and
\mathbb{Y}
be two doubly connected domains in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

This paper aims to minimize the Dirichlet energy integral
\mathcal{E}[h]
for mappings
h
belonging to the following class:

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{j}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y}):=

\displaystyle\big\{\mbox{the $j-$degree mappings in the Sobolev class}\ {W}^{1,2}(\mathbb{X},\mathbb{Y}),

\displaystyle\mbox{which map the inner/outer boundary onto the inner/outer boundary}\big\}.

In this paper we assume that
\mathbb{Y}=\mathbb{B}(r^{\ast},R^{\ast})
.
It should be noted that for every doubly connected domain
\mathbb{X}
there is a conformal diffeomorphism
\zeta:\mathbb{X}\mapsto\mathbb{A}(r,R)
. In this case, the conformal modulus of
\mathbb{X}
is defined by
\mathrm{Mod}(\mathbb{X})=\log\frac{R}{r}
. Without losing of generality we assume that
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}(1,r)
and
\mathbb{B}=A(1,R)
, where
r,R>1
, because the minimizations of Dirichlet energy of mappings
g
and their transformations
h(z)=\alpha g(\beta z)
is equivalent.

The following theorem is the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a
j-
degree radial harmonic mapping between
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
if and only if the condition (
2.5
) is satisfied. In this case, the harmonic mapping
g^{\circ}:\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{B}
is given by (
2.4
).

Moreover the minimum of the Dirichlet energy

(1.3)

\mathcal{E}[f]=\int_{\mathbb{A}}|Df|^{2}dxdy,

for
f\in\mathcal{H}_{j}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})
is attained for the
j-
degree radial harmonic mapping
g^{\circ}
provided that (
2.5
) is satisfied. If (
2.5
) is not satisfied, then the minimum of (
1.3
) is attained for a
j-
degree mapping
g^{\diamond}
(see (
2.6
) below) which is harmonic in a sub-annulus of
\mathbb{A}
but it is an ”squeezing” in rest of
\mathbb{A}
.
The minimum on both cases is unique up to a rotation.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.1
is a generalization of the similar result obtained by Astala, Iwaniec, and Martin in
[
1
]
for smooth homeomorphismsm. It must be emphasized that Theorem
1.1
however cannot be deduced from such a result, because
j-
degree mappings cannot be decomposed as the composition of a homeomorphism and the power function
z^{j}
as one can guess. It seems that such a result is new even for degree one mappings, which are not necessarily homeomorphisms. The proof is an adaptations of the corresponding proofs in
[
11
,
5
]
by employing the free Lagrangian for
j-
degree mappings mapping the inner/outer boundary to inner/outer boundary.

2.
Radial harmonic mappings and radial minimizers

Set
f(z)=e^{j\imath\tau}G(\rho)
, and call this mapping
j-
degree radial mapping
where
z=te^{\imath\tau}
and solve the Laplace equation
\Delta f=0
. Then we get the second order differential equation

(2.1)

-{j^{2}G(t)}+t{G^{\prime}(t)}+t^{2}G^{\prime\prime}(t)=0.

By taking the substitution
t=e^{x}
,
H(x)=G(e^{x})
, we obtain
H^{\prime}(x)=e^{x}G^{\prime}(e^{x})=tG^{\prime}(t)
and

H^{\prime\prime}(x)=H^{\prime}(x)+e^{2x}G^{\prime\prime}(e^{x})=F^{\prime}(x)+t^{2}G^{\prime\prime}(t).

Thus (
2.1
) is reduced to

(2.2)

-j^{2}H(x)+H^{\prime\prime}(x)=0.

Now take

s=H(x)
,
H^{\prime}(x)=h(s)
. Then we obtain
H^{\prime\prime}(x)=h^{\prime}(s)h(s)
. And (
2.2
) is reduced to the equation

2j^{2}s=(h^{2}(s))^{\prime}.

Thus

h(s)=\pm\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}+c}.

After returning back the variable
x=\log t
and the function
G
, we obtain the equation

(2.3)

\pm G^{\prime}(t)\frac{1}{\sqrt{j^{2}G(t)^{2}+c_{1}}}+\frac{1}{t}=0,

where plus sign is in the case when
G
is decreasing and minus sign is for the case when
G
is increasing.

Then the general solution of (
2.1
) is given by

G(t)=a\cosh(j^{2}\log(t))+b\sinh(j^{2}\log(t)).

Assuming that it maps
\mathbb{A}(1,r)
onto
\mathbb{A}(1,R)
, mapping the inner boundary onto the inner boundary and the outer boundary onto the outer boundary we get

(2.4)

g^{\circ}(z)=\left(\frac{r^{j}R-r^{2j}}{1-r^{2j}}\right)\bar{z}^{-j}+\frac{\left(1-r^{j}R\right)z^{j}}{1-r^{2j}}

and the given function (
2.4
) maps
\mathbb{A}(1,r)
onto
\mathbb{A}(1,R)
if and only if it is satisfied the Nitsche type bound

(2.5)

R\geq\frac{1}{2}r^{-j}\left(1+r^{2j}\right).

Then the previous inequality can be written as

r\leq r_{\circ}:=\left(R+\sqrt{-1+R^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{j}}.

Indeed,
g^{\circ}(\rho)
is increasing if and only if

r^{j}\left(R-r^{j}\right)+\rho^{2j}\left(r^{j}R-1\right)\geq 0,\ \ \ \rho\in[1,r],

and this inequality is equivalent with (
2.5
). Now if
r>r_{\circ}
, then there is an annulus
\mathbb{A}(\rho,r_{\circ})
having the conformal modulus equal to the modulus of
\mathbb{A}(1,r)
. Indeed we have
\rho=r_{\circ}/r
.
Let

(2.6)

g^{\diamond}(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}g^{\circ}(z),&\hbox{if $1\leq|z|<r_{\circ}$;}\\
\frac{z^{j}}{|z|^{j}},&\hbox{if $\rho<|z|\leq 1$.}\end{array}\right.

This is a
j-
degree mapping. Namely for every
1<|w|<R
, there exist exact
j
points from
1<|z|<r_{\circ}
so that
g^{\circ}(z)=w
. Moreover the
\mathrm{sign}(\det(Dg^{\circ}(z)))=1
. We can also define the degree for points
|w|=1
by using a sequence of points
1<|w_{n}|<R
converging to
w
. See Figure 1 for a
2
-degree mapping
g^{\diamond}:\mathbb{A}(1/2,2)\to\mathbb{A}(1,17/8).

Figure 1
.
The annulus
\mathbb{A}(1/2,2)=\mathbb{A}(1/2,1]\cup\mathbb{A}[1,2)
is mapped onto the annulus
\mathbb{A}(1,17/8)
in such a way that
\mathbb{A}(1/2,1)
is squeezed in the unit circle and wrapped two times, while the other part is
2-
degree harmonic mapping of
\mathbb{A}(1,2)
onto
\mathbb{A}(1,17/8)
.

2.1.
The energy of the stationary mapping

Elementary calculations lead to

|Dg^{\circ}|^{2}=\frac{j^{2}G^{2}}{t^{2}}+\dot{G}^{2}.

Thus it follows from (
2.3
) that

|Dg^{\circ}|^{2}=\frac{2j^{2}G^{2}+c_{1}}{t^{2}},

where

c_{1}=\frac{4j^{2}\left(1+R^{2}-R(r^{j}+r^{-j})\right)}{(r^{j}-r^{-j})^{2}}.

Observe that
c_{1}=0
if and only if
R=r^{j}
or
R=r^{-j}
. In this case
f(z)=z^{\pm j}
is a conformal mapping between
\mathbb{A}(r)
and
\mathbb{A}(R)
.

(2.7)

\dot{G}^{2}=\frac{(j^{2})G^{2}+c_{1}}{t^{2}},

Let

F=G^{-1}

then

(2.8)

\frac{F^{\prime}(s)}{F(s)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}+c_{1}}}.

The energy of
g^{\circ}
is now as follows

(2.9)

\mathcal{E}[g^{\circ}]=\int_{\mathbb{A}}|Dg^{\circ}|^{2}=4\pi j^{2}\int_{1}^{r}\frac{G^{2}(t)}{t}dt+2\pi c_{1}\log r.

Now we calculate
\mathcal{E}[g^{\diamond}]
. In this case
c_{1}=-j^{2}
. We have

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}[g^{\diamond}]&=\int_{\mathbb{A}(1,r)}|Dg^{\circ}|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{A}(\rho,1)}|Dg^{\diamond}|^{2}\\
&=4\pi j^{2}\int_{1}^{r}\frac{G^{2}(t)}{t}dt+2\pi-j^{2}\log r+2\pi j^{2}\int_{\rho}^{1}\frac{1}{t}dt\\
&=4\pi j^{2}\int_{1}^{r}\frac{G^{2}(t)}{t}dt-2\pi j^{2}\log\rho r.\end{split}

3.
Free Lagrangians

Assume that
\mathcal{H}_{j}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})
is a class of
j-
degree mappings, mapping
\mathbb{A}
onto
\mathbb{B}
and mapping the inner/outer boundary to the inner/outer boundary.
Motivated by the paper of Iwaniec and Onninen
[
7
,
5
,
6
]
we consider the following free Lagrangians.

a) A function in
t=|z|
;

(3.1)

L(z,h,Dh)dx\wedge dy=M(t)dx\wedge dy

Thus, for all
h\in\mathcal{H}_{j}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})
we have

(3.2)

\int_{\mathbb{A}}L(z,h,Dh)dx\wedge dy=\int_{\mathbb{A}}M(|z|)dx\wedge dy=2\pi\int_{r}^{R}tM(t)dt.

b) Pullback of a form in
\mathbb{B}
via a given mapping
h\in\mathcal{H}_{j}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})
;

(3.3)

L(z,h,Dh)dx\wedge dy=N(|h|)J(z,h)dx\wedge dy,\text{where}\ \ \ N\in L^{1}(r,R)

Thus, for all
h\in\mathcal{H}_{j}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})
we have

(3.4)

\int_{\mathbb{A}}L(z,h,Dh)dx\wedge dy=j\int_{\mathbb{B}}N(|w|)du\wedge dv=2\pi j\int_{r_{\ast}}^{R_{\ast}}sN(s)ds.

c) A radial free Lagrangian

(3.5)

L(z,h,Dh)dx\wedge dy=A(|h|)\frac{|h|_{N}}{|z|}dx\wedge dy

where
A\in L^{1}(r_{\ast},R_{\ast})
.
Thus, for all
h\in\mathcal{H}_{j}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})
we have

(3.6)

\int_{\mathbb{A}}L(z,h,Dh)dx\wedge dy=2\pi\int_{r}^{R}A(|h|)\frac{\partial|h|}{\partial\rho}d\rho=2\pi\int_{r_{\ast}}^{R_{\ast}}A(s)ds

d) An angular free Lagrangian

(3.7)

L(z,h,Dh)dx\wedge dy=B(|z|)\Im\left[\frac{h_{T}}{h}\right]dx\wedge dy,

where
B\in L^{1}(r,R)
.
Thus, for all
h\in\mathcal{H}_{j}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})
we have

(3.8)

\int_{\mathbb{A}}L(z,h,Dh)dx\wedge dy=\int_{r}^{R}\frac{B(t)}{t}\int_{|z|=t}\left(\frac{\partial\mathrm{Arg}\,h}{\partial\tau}d\tau\right)dt=2\pi j\int_{r}^{R}{B(t)}dt

The idea of using these free Lagrangians is to establish a general sub-gradient
type inequality for the integrand with two independent parameters:
t\in(r,R)
and

s\in(r^{\ast},R^{\ast})
, for the functions:
g

as follows:

|g_{T}|^{2}+|g_{N}|^{2}\geq X(s)\frac{|g|_{N}}{t}+Y(t)\frac{|g|}{s}\mbox{Im}\frac{g_{T}}{g}+Z(s)\det\left[Dg\right]+W(t),

where the coefficients
X
and
Z
are functions in the interval
(r^{\ast},R^{\ast})
, while
Y
and

W
are functions in the interval
(r,R)
. We will choose
t=|z|
and
s=|g(z)|
to
obtain the corresponding free Lagrangians on the right-hand side of the above inequality.

Suppose
g\in H_{j}(\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B})
and consider the following formula for Dirichlet energy

\mathcal{E}[g]=\int_{\mathbb{A}}(|g_{N}|^{2}+|g_{T}|^{2})dxdy.

Here for
z=\rho e^{\imath\tau}
,

g_{N}(z)=\frac{\partial g(z)}{\partial\rho}

and

g_{T}=\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial g(z)}{\partial\tau}.

Then the Jacobian of a mapping
g
can be written as

\det[Dg(z)]=\Im(g_{T}\overline{g_{N}}).

4.
The proof of Theorem
1.1

Since we already proved the existence of radial minimizers, it remains to prove inequality and equality statement. The proof consists of three parts.

4.1.
Under Nitsche bound

Let us consider three subcases
c_{1}=0
, i.e. the case
R=r^{j}
,
c_{1}>0
and
c_{1}<0
.
In what follows, we let
g(z)=(g_{1}(z),g_{2}(z))
to be the
j-
degree mapping between annuli
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
mapping inner/outer boundary onto the inner/outed bounrary.

(i):

Conformal case,
c_{1}=0
, i.e.
R=r^{j}
.

From the following trivial inequality

|g_{N}|^{2}+|g_{T}|^{2}\geq\det(Dg(z)),

it follows that

\mathcal{E}[g]\geq\int_{\mathbb{A}}\det(Dg(z))dxdy.

Now because
g
is a
j-
degree mapping we obtain the inequality

\mathcal{E}[g]\geq j|\mathbb{B}|=\int_{\mathbb{A}}\det(Dg^{\circ}(z))(z)dxdy.

Namely in this case
g^{\circ}(z)=z^{j}
and so
\det(Dg^{\circ}(z))(z)=j^{2}|z|^{2j-2}
. Thus

\int_{\mathbb{A}}\det(Dg^{\circ}(z))(z)dxdy=2\pi j^{2}\int_{1}^{r}\rho^{2j-1}d\rho=\pi j(r^{2j}-1)=j|\mathbb{B}|.

(ii):

Elastic case,
c_{1}>0
, i.e.
R>r^{j}
.

For every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, the following trivial inequality

(\alpha|g_{N}|-\beta|g_{T}|)^{2}\geq 0

is equivalent to

(4.1)

|g_{N}|^{2}+|g_{T}|^{2}\geq(1-\alpha^{2})|g_{N}|^{2}+(1-\beta^{2})|g_{T}|^{2}+2\alpha\beta|g_{T}||g_{N}|.

If we take
\beta=1
, then it reduces to the following simple inequality

|g_{N}|^{2}+|g_{T}|^{2}\geq(1-\alpha^{2})|g_{N}|^{2}+2\alpha|g_{T}||g_{N}|,

where the equality holds if and only if

(4.2)

\alpha|g_{N}|=|g_{T}|.

Furthermore, the following inequality holds:

(4.3)

(1-\alpha^{2})|g_{N}|^{2}+2\alpha|g_{T}||g_{N}|\geq 2(1-\alpha^{2})\gamma{|g_{N}|}+2\alpha\det(Dg(z))-{(1-\alpha^{2})\gamma^{2}},

where
\gamma
is an arbitrary constant.
It is easy to see that the equality is attained in (
4.3
), if
\gamma=|g_{N}|
.

Let
t=|z|
,
s=|g(z)|
. Suppose

\alpha=\frac{js}{\sqrt{c_{1}+j^{2}s^{2}}},

\alpha_{\ast}=\frac{jG(t)}{\sqrt{c_{1}+j^{2}G^{2}(t)}},

and

A=\dot{G}(t)\sqrt{\frac{1-{\alpha^{2}_{\ast}}}{1-\alpha^{2}}},

where
G
is a solution to the differential equation (
2.3
)
Moreover, in view of (
2.8
)

(4.4)

\begin{split}|Dg|^{2}&\geq 2\left[(1-\alpha^{2})A\right]{|g_{N}|}+\frac{2jsF^{\prime}(s)}{F(s)}\det[Dg]-(1-\alpha^{2})A^{2}\\
&\geq 2\left[(1-\alpha^{2})A\right]{|g|_{N}}+2j\frac{sF^{\prime}(s)}{F(s)}\det[Dg]-(1-\alpha^{2})A^{2}.\end{split}

Furthermore, in view of (
2.7
),

(4.5)

\begin{split}\gamma(s)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}2t(1-\alpha^{2})A\\
&=2t\sqrt{(1-{\alpha^{2}_{\ast}})(1-\alpha^{2})}\dot{G}(t)\\
&=\frac{2c_{1}t\dot{G}(t)}{\sqrt{j^{2}G^{2}(t)+c_{1}}}\cdot\frac{1}{\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}+c_{1}}}\\
&=2c_{1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}+c_{1}}},\end{split}

and

(4.6)

\begin{split}\delta(t)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}-(1-\alpha^{2})A^{2}\\
&=-\dot{G}^{2}(t)(1-\alpha^{2}_{\ast})\\
&=-c_{1}\frac{\dot{G}^{2}(t)}{\sqrt{c_{1}+j^{2}G(t)^{2}}}\\
&=-\frac{c_{1}}{t^{2}}.\end{split}

So for
t=|z|
and
s=|g(z)|
, by using the relations (
4.3
), (
4.4
), and (
4.5
), we obtain

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}[g]&=\int_{\mathbb{A}}(|g_{N}|^{2}+|g_{T}|^{2})dx\wedge dy\\
&\geq\int_{\mathbb{A}}\left\{2\left[(1-\alpha^{2})A\right]{|g|_{N}}+2j\frac{sF^{\prime}(s)}{F(s)}\det\left[Dg\right]-(1-\alpha^{2})A^{2}\right\}dx\wedge dy\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{A}}\gamma(s)\frac{|g|_{N}}{t}dx\wedge dy+2j\int_{\mathbb{A}}\frac{|g(z)|F^{\prime}(|g(z)|)}{F(|g(z)|)}\det\left[Dg\right]dx\wedge dy+\int_{\mathbb{A}}\delta(t)dx\wedge dy.\end{split}

By using the formulas (
3.4
), (
3.6
), (
3.8
), and (
4.5
) we obtain

\mathcal{E}[g]\geq 2\pi\int_{1}^{R}\gamma(s)ds+4\pi j^{2}\int_{1}^{R}\frac{s^{2}F^{\prime}(s)}{F(s)}ds+2\pi\int_{1}^{r}t\delta(t)dt.

Then

\mathcal{E}[g]\geq 4\pi c_{1}\int_{1}^{R}\frac{1}{\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}+c_{1}}}ds+4\pi j^{2}\int_{1}^{R}\frac{s^{2}F^{\prime}(s)}{F(s)}ds-2\pi c_{1}\int_{1}^{r}\frac{1}{t}dt.

According to

(4.7)

\int_{1}^{G(t)}\frac{1}{\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}+c_{1}}}\,ds=\log t,

we see that

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}[g]&\geq 4\pi c_{1}\log r-2\pi c_{1}\log r+4\pi j^{2}\int_{1}^{R}\frac{s^{2}F^{\prime}(s)}{F(s)}ds\\
&=2\pi c_{1}\log r+4\pi j^{2}\int_{1}^{R}\frac{s^{2}F^{\prime}(s)}{F(s)}ds.\end{split}

Changing the variable
t=F(s)
in the last integral, we get

(4.8)

\int_{1}^{R}\frac{s^{2}F^{\prime}(s)}{F(s)}ds=\int_{1}^{r}\frac{G^{2}(t)dt}{t}.

In view of (
2.9
), we see from (
4.8
) that

\mathcal{E}[g]\geq 2\pi c_{1}\log r+4\pi j^{2}\int_{1}^{r}\frac{G^{2}(t)dt}{t}=\mathcal{E}[g^{\circ}].

This finishes the proof of this case up to the equality statement.

(iii):

Non-elastic case,
-j^{2}\leq c_{1}<0
, i.e.
R<r^{j}

Suppose
g
is a smooth
j-
degree mapping between two annuli
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
. Let
z\in\mathbb{A}
,
g(z)\in\mathbb{B}
and let
t=|z|
,
s=|g(z)|
. If we put
\alpha=1
in (
4.1
), then we have the following inequality

(4.9)

|g_{N}|^{2}+|g_{T}|^{2}\geq(1-\beta^{2})|g_{T}|^{2}+2\beta|g_{T}||g_{N}|,

where the equality holds if and only if

(4.10)

\beta|g_{T}|=|g_{N}|.

Furthermore,

\det[Dg]=\mbox{Im}(g_{T}\overline{g_{N}})=|g_{T}\overline{g_{N}}|

if and only if

(4.11)

g_{T}\overline{g_{N}}\in\imath\mathbb{R}.

We continue with the following inequality

|g_{N}|^{2}+|g_{T}|^{2}\geq\mathcal{R}:{=}2(1-\beta^{2})B{|g_{T}|}+2\beta\det(Dg)-{(1-\beta^{2})B^{2}},

where
B
is a constant.

Since
\mbox{Im}\left[\frac{g_{T}}{g}\right]\leq\left[\frac{|g_{T}|}{|g|}\right]
, we infer that

|g_{T}|\geq|g|\mbox{Im}\left[\frac{g_{T}}{g}\right].

The above equality is attained if and only if

(4.12)

g_{T}\overline{g}\in\imath\mathbb{R}.

Now

\mathcal{R}\geq[2(1-\beta^{2})B]{|g|\mbox{Im}\left[\frac{g_{T}}{g}\right]}+2\beta\det\left[Dg\right]-{(1-\beta^{2})B^{2}}.

The above equality is attained if and only if the conditions (
4.11
), (
4.10
), (
4.12
) and the following equality

(4.13)

B=|g_{T}|

are satisfied.

Moreover, these conditions imply that
g
is radial. Now, we choose

(4.14)

\beta=\frac{\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}+c_{1}}}{js}

and

(4.15)

B=\frac{js}{t}.

Then, for
t=|z|
and
s=|g(z)|
, we get

(4.16)

\begin{split}-{(1-\beta^{2})B^{2}}&=\mu(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}\frac{c_{1}}{t^{2}}.\end{split}

and

(4.17)

\begin{split}2s(1-\beta^{2})B=\nu(t)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}-\frac{2c_{1}}{jt}.\end{split}

By using the relations (
4.9
), (
4.16
), and (
4.17
) we obtain

(4.18)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}[g]&=\int_{\mathbb{A}}(|g_{N}|^{2}+|g_{T}|^{2})dx\wedge dy\\
&\geq\int_{\mathbb{A}}\bigg[[2(1-\beta^{2})B]{{|g|\mbox{Im}\left[\frac{g_{T}}{g}\right]}}\\
&+2\frac{\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}+c_{1}}}{js}\det\left[Dg\right]-{(1-\beta^{2})B^{2}}\bigg]dx\wedge dy\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{A}}\nu(t)\Im\left[\frac{g_{T}}{g}\right]dx\wedge dy\\
&+2\int_{\mathbb{A}}\frac{\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}+c_{1}}}{js}\det\left[Dg\right]dx\wedge dy+\int_{\mathbb{A}}\mu(t)dx\wedge dy.\end{split}

By using the formulas (
3.4
), (
3.6
), (
3.8
), and (
4.18
) we obtain

\mathcal{E}[g]\geq 2\pi j\int_{1}^{r}\nu(t)dt+4\pi\int_{1}^{R}\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}+c_{1}}ds+2\pi\int_{1}^{r}t\mu(t)dt.

Now we use the equality

\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}+c_{1}}=\frac{j^{2}s^{2}+c_{1}}{\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}+c_{1}}}

to deduce that

\int_{1}^{R}\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}+c_{1}}ds=I_{1}+I_{2}=c_{1}\int_{1}^{R}\frac{1}{\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}+c_{1}}}ds+\int_{1}^{R}j^{2}s^{2}\frac{1}{\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}+c_{1}}}ds.

By using (
4.7
) we get

I_{1}=c_{1}\log r.

By using change of variables
t=F(s)
, in view of

(4.19)

F(\tau)=r\exp\left[\int_{1}^{\tau}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s^{2}+c_{1}}}ds\right],

we get

I_{2}=j^{2}\int_{1}^{R}s^{2}\frac{F^{\prime}(s)}{F(s)}ds=j^{2}\int_{1}^{r}\frac{G^{2}(t)}{t}dt.

Moreover, we have

2\pi j\int_{1}^{r}\nu(t)dt=-4\pi c_{1}\log r.

We also have

2\pi\int_{1}^{r}t\mu(t)dt=2\pi c_{1}\log r.

Summing all those formulas and again from (
2.9
), we see that

\mathcal{E}[g]\geq 2\pi c_{1}\log r+4\pi j^{2}\int_{1}^{r}\frac{G^{2}(t)dt}{t}=\mathcal{E}[g^{\circ}].

4.2.
Below Nitsche bound

In this case we assume that
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}(\rho,1]\cup\mathbb{A}[1,r)
. We repeat the previous argument (the case
\mathrm{(iii)}
). In this case
c_{1}=-j^{2}
and we have

(4.20)

\mathcal{E}[g]\geq 2\pi j\int_{\rho}^{r}\nu(t)dt+4\pi\int_{1}^{R}\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}-j^{2}}ds+2\pi\int_{\rho}^{r}t\mu(t)dt.

Further as before we obtain

4\pi\int_{1}^{R}\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}-j^{2}}ds=4\pi j^{2}\int_{1}^{r}\frac{G^{2}(t)dt}{t}-4\pi j^{2}\log r,

and in view of (
4.16
) and (
4.17
)

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}[g]&\geq 4\pi j^{2}\log\frac{r}{\rho}+4\pi j^{2}\int_{1}^{r}\frac{G^{2}(t)dt}{t}-4\pi j^{2}\log r-2\pi j^{2}\log\frac{\rho}{r}\\
&=4\pi j^{2}\int_{1}^{r}\frac{G^{2}(t)}{t}dt-2\pi j^{2}\log r-2\pi j^{2}\log\rho\\
&=\mathcal{E}[g^{\diamond}].\end{split}

4.3.
The equality part

To finish the proof of the theorem, we need to consider the equality case. Consider only the non-elastic case, and notice that the proof of the remaining case is similar. Assume that the equality is attained in our theorem for a certain mapping
g
. Let
z=te^{\imath\tau}
, and write
g(t,\tau)=g(z)
. Assume that
g(t,\tau)=u(t,\tau)e^{\imath v(t,\tau)}
for some real functions
u
and
v
, with
u(1,\tau)=1
,
u(r,\tau)=R
,
u(t,\tau+2\pi)=u(t,\tau)
,
v(t,\tau+2\pi)=v(t,\tau)
for every
t,\tau\in[1,r]\times\mathbb{R}
. Then from (
4.11
) and (
4.12
), we see that

\frac{\partial_{t}g(t,\tau)}{g(t,\tau)}=\imath\partial_{t}v(t,\tau)+\frac{\partial_{t}u(t,\tau)}{u(t,\tau)}\in\mathbb{R}.

Since
g
is
j-
degree mapping it follows that
v(t,\tau)=\phi(\tau)
for a certain
j-
degree mapping
\phi
of the unit circle onto itself. Now from (
4.11
), we have

\frac{\left(\imath u(t,\tau)\phi^{\prime}(\tau)+\partial_{\tau}u(t,\tau)\right)}{t}\in\imath\mathbb{R}.

Thus
u(t,\tau)=G(t)
. Now from (
4.10
) we see that

\beta\frac{G(t)\phi^{\prime}(\tau)}{r}=G^{\prime}(t),

where, as in (
4.14
),

(4.21)

\beta=\frac{\sqrt{j^{2}s^{2}+c_{1}}}{js}=\frac{\sqrt{G(t)^{2}+c_{1}}}{jG(t)}.

This implies that
\phi^{\prime}(\tau)=\mathrm{const}=j
, and thus
\phi(\tau)=j\tau+c
. Now from (
4.10
) and (
4.21
) we see that
G
satisfies the equation (
2.3
), which means that
g
is a
j-
degree harmonic mapping.

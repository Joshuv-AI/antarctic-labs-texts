1.
Introduction

We consider the anisotropic elliptic problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\nabla\big(a(x)\nabla\upsilon\big)=a(x)\big[e^{\upsilon}-s\phi_{1}-4\pi\alpha\delta_{q}-h(x)\big]\,\,\,\,\,\textrm{in}\,\,\,\,\,\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
&\upsilon=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\ \textrm{on}\,\ \,\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where

\Omega
is a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

s>0
is a large parameter,
q\in\Omega
,
\alpha\in(-1,+\infty)\setminus\mathbb{N}
,

\delta_{q}
denotes the Dirac measure with pole at point
q
,

h\in C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
is given,

a(x)
is a smooth function over
\overline{\Omega}
satisfying

\displaystyle a_{1}\leq a(x)\leq a_{2}

(1.2)

for some constants
0<a_{1}<a_{2}<+\infty
,
\phi_{1}
is a positive eigenfunction of
the anisotropic Laplacian operator

\displaystyle-\Delta_{a}:=-\frac{1}{a(x)}\nabla\big(a(x)\nabla\cdot\big)=-\Delta-\nabla\log a(x)\nabla

(1.3)

with Dirichlet boundary condition corresponding to the
first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
.
Clearly, if we set
\rho(x)=(-\Delta_{a})^{-1}h
in
H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
and
let
G(x,y)
be the Green’s function satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta_{a}G(x,y)=8\pi\delta_{y}(x),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,x\in\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
&G(x,y)=0,\,\qquad\qquad\quad\,\,\quad\,x\in\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.4)

and
H(x,y)
be its regular part given by

\displaystyle H(x,y)=G(x,y)-4\log\frac{1}{|x-y|},

(1.5)

then equation (
1.1
) is equivalent to solving for

u=\upsilon+\frac{s}{\lambda_{1}}\phi_{1}+\frac{\alpha}{2}G(\,\cdot,\,q)+\rho
, the problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta_{a}u=|x-q|^{2\alpha}k(x)e^{-t\phi_{1}}e^{u}\,\,\,\,\,\textrm{in}\,\,\,\,\,\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
&u=0\,\,\qquad\,\,\qquad\,\,\quad\quad\ \,\qquad\qquad\,\textrm{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.6)

where
k(x)=e^{-\rho(x)-\frac{\alpha}{2}H(x,q)}

.
The point
q
with Dirac measure in equation (
1.1
)
is called vortex point or singular source.
The term
|\cdot|^{2\alpha}
in problem (
1.6
) is called
the Hardy weight if
-1<\alpha<0
, whereas the Hénon weight if
\alpha>0
.
In this paper, we are interested in the existence of solutions of problem (
1.6
)
(or (
1.1
))
which exhibit the
multiple concentration behavior
around singular source
q
when
the parameter
t
tends to infinity.

If
a(x)=1
and
\alpha=0
, equation (
1.1
) is the classical
Ambrosetti-Prodi type problem
[
1
]
with exponential nonlinearity

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta\upsilon=g(\upsilon)-s\phi_{1}-h(x)\,\,\,\,\,\,\textrm{in}\,\,\,\,\,\Omega,\\[2.84526pt]
&\upsilon=0\,\,\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\,\qquad\,\,\,\,\textrm{on}\,\,\,\,\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.7)

as
s\rightarrow+\infty
, where
\Omega
is a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}

(
N\geq 2
),

h\in C^{0,\gamma}(\overline{\Omega})
is given,

\phi_{1}
is a positive eigenfunction of
-\Delta
with Dirichlet
boundary condition corresponding to the first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
,
and

g:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function such that

-\infty\leq\nu=\lim_{t\rightarrow-\infty}\frac{g(t)}{t}<\lim_{t\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{g(t)}{t}=\overline{\nu}\leq+\infty
.
Here
\nu=-\infty

and
\overline{\nu}=+\infty
are allowed.
The condition that
(\nu,\overline{\nu})
contains some eigenvalues of
-\Delta

subject to Dirichlet boundary condition
has great influence on the existence and multiplicity
of solutions for problem (
1.7
),
which has been widely considered since the early 1970s
so that many interesting results have been obtained
(see
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
and references therein).
Moreover, in the early 1980s Lazer and McKenna
[

conjectured that the

unbounded as
s\rightarrow+\infty
when

\nu<\lambda_{1}<\overline{\nu}=+\infty
and
g(t)
has
an appropriate growth at infinity.

In fact,
the Lazer-McKenna conjecture
holds true for problem (
1.7
) with many different types of nonlinearities,
which has been founded
in
[
7
,
8
,
9
]
for the exponential nonlinear case

g(t)=e^{t}-4\pi\alpha\delta_{q}
,
-1<\alpha\not\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and
N\geq 2
,
in
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
for the asymptotically linear case

g(t)=\overline{\nu}t_{+}-\nu t_{-}
with
\overline{\nu}
large enough,
in
[
13
,
14
]
for the subcritical case

g(t)=t_{+}^{p}+\lambda t
,
\lambda<\lambda_{1}
,
1<p<\frac{N+2}{N-2}
if
N\geq 3
,

1<p<+\infty
if
N=2
,
in
[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
for the critical case
g(t)=t_{+}^{\frac{N+2}{N-2}}+\lambda t
,

0<\lambda<\lambda_{1}
and
N\geq 6
, in
[
19
]
for the superlinear
nonhomogeneous case
g(t)=t_{+}^{p}+t_{-}^{q}
,

1<q<p<\frac{N+2}{N-2}
and
N\geq 4
,
in
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
for the
superlinear
homogeneous case
g(t)=|t|^{p}
,
1<p<\frac{N+2}{N-2}

N\geq 3
,

1<p<+\infty
if
N=2
,
and in
[

,

where

.
In particular,
when
g(t)=e^{t}
and
N=2

del Pino and Muñoz in
[

proved the Lazer-McKenna conjecture

with the accumulation of

\phi_{1}

Recently, in
[
8
]
, Dong, Hu and Zhang extended this result in
[
7
]

to the case
g(t)=e^{t}-4\pi\alpha\delta_{q}
,
-1<\alpha\not\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
and

,
and showed that if singular source

1.7

.

It is necessary to point out that the anisotropic
equation (
1.1
)
|_{\alpha=0}
is a special case
of problem (
1.7
) with
g(t)=e^{t}
in higher dimension

.
Indeed, let a standard
N
-dimensional torus be

\mathbb{T}=\{(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\,(\|x^{\prime}\|-1)^{2}+x_{N}^{2}\leq r_{0}^{2}\}

with
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N-1})
,

\|x^{\prime}\|=\sqrt{x_{1}^{2}+\ldots+x_{N-1}^{2}}
and
0<r_{0}<1
.
If we look for some special solutions of problem (
1.7
) with
g(t)=e^{t}
in the
axially symmetric
torus
\mathbb{T}
,
i.e. solutions
\upsilon
of the form
\upsilon(x^{\prime},x_{N})=\upsilon(r,x_{N})

with

1.7
) with

in higher dimension

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\nabla\big(r^{N-2}\nabla\upsilon\big)=r^{N-2}\big[e^{\upsilon}-s\phi_{1}-h(x)\big]\ \,\,\,\,\textrm{in}\,\,\,\,\,\Omega_{\mathbb{T}},\\[5.69054pt]
&\upsilon=0\,\,\ \quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\,\,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\,\,\,\,\,\,\quad\quad\,\,\textrm{on}\,\ \,\partial\Omega_{\mathbb{T}},\end{aligned}\right.

where
\Omega_{\mathbb{T}}=\{(r,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\,(r-1)^{2}+x_{N}^{2}<r_{0}^{2}\}
.
This is just the equation (
1.1
)
|_{\alpha=0}
with
anisotropic coefficient

.
In this direction,
Yang and Zhang in
[
9
]

proved that the Lazer-McKenna conjecture is also true

1.1

in the domain as

to give a positive answer to the Lazer-McKenna conjecture
for the singular case of the anisotropic equation

and an isolated local maximum

) (or (

.
In particular, we recover and extend the results in
[

,

,
9
]

This can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1.

Let
\alpha\in(-1,+\infty)\setminus\mathbb{N}
and assume

(A)
Singular source

\phi_{1}

and an isolated local maximum

.

Then for any integer

, there exists

, problem

)
has

\displaystyle u_{t}(x)=\left[\log\frac{1}{(\varepsilon_{0,t}^{2}\mu_{0,t}^{2}+|x-q|^{2(1+\alpha)})^{2}}+(1+\alpha)H(x,q)\right]+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left[\,\log\frac{1}{(\varepsilon_{i,t}^{2}\mu_{i,t}^{2}+|x-\xi_{i,t}|^{2})^{2}}+H(x,\xi_{i,t})\,\right]+o(1),

where

, as

, uniformly on

,

,
\varepsilon_{i,t}

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0,t}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}t\phi_{1}(q)},\,\,\ \quad\ \,\,\varepsilon_{i,t}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}t\phi_{1}(\xi_{i,t})},\,\,\ \quad\ \,\,\frac{1}{C}\leq\mu_{0,t}\leq Ct^{\frac{m(m+1+\alpha)^{2}a_{2}}{a_{1}}},\,\,\ \quad\ \,\,\frac{1}{C}\leq\mu_{i,t}\leq Ct^{\frac{(2m+\alpha)(m+1+\alpha)^{2}a_{2}}{2a_{1}}},

The corresponding result for problem (
1.1
) can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.2.

Let
\alpha\in(-1,+\infty)\setminus\mathbb{N}
.
If
(A)
of Theorem
1.1
holds, then for any integer

and any

of problem

then for any integer

there exists a family of solutions of problem (

) which exhibits

and

The same strategy
has been applied in

]
to

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u=|x-q|^{2\alpha}k(x)e^{-t\phi_{1}}e^{u}\,\,\,\,\,\textrm{in}\,\,\,\,\,\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
&u=0\,\,\qquad\,\,\qquad\,\,\quad\quad\qquad\qquad\,\textrm{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.8)

as
t\rightarrow+\infty
, where

\Omega
is a bounded smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\alpha\in(-1,+\infty)\setminus\mathbb{N}
,

,

is a given positive smooth function and

\phi_{1}
is a positive eigenfunction of

-\Delta
with Dirichlet boundary condition corresponding to the

\lambda_{1}
.
Just like that in equation (

the presence of Hardy-Hénon weight
has significant influence not only on

of equation (

2.
Approximating solutions

For notational convenience we always fix singular source

q\in\Omega
as an isolated local maximum
point of
a(x)\phi_{1}

and also a local maximum point of
\phi_{1}
, and further assume

\displaystyle 2\inf_{x\in\overline{B}_{d}(q)}\phi_{1}(x)>\sup_{x\in\overline{B}_{d}(q)}\phi_{1}(x)=\phi_{1}(q)=1,

(2.1)

where
d>0
is a small but fixed number, independent of
t
.
For points
\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})
with
\xi_{i}\in\overline{B}_{d}(q)
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{t}(q):=\left\{\,\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})\in\big(\overline{B}_{d}(q)\big)^{m}\left|\,a(q)\phi_{1}(q)-a(\xi_{i})\phi_{1}(\xi_{i})\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}},\,\,\,\,\,|\xi_{i}-q|\geq\frac{1}{t^{\beta}},\,\,\,\,\,|\xi_{i}-\xi_{j}|\geq\frac{1}{t^{\beta}},\right.\right.

\displaystyle i,j=1,\ldots,m,\,\,\,i\neq j\Big\},\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\,\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\,

(2.2)

where

\beta
is given by

\displaystyle\beta=\frac{(m+1+\alpha)^{2}a_{2}}{2a_{1}}.

(2.3)

We thus fix
\xi\in\mathcal{O}_{t}(q)
. For numbers
\mu_{0}>0
and

\mu_{i}>0
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, yet to
be chosen, we define

\displaystyle u_{0}(x)=\log\frac{8\mu_{0}^{2}(1+\alpha)^{2}}{k(q)(\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\mu_{0}^{2}+|x-q|^{2(1+\alpha)})^{2}},\qquad\qquad u_{i}(x)=\log\frac{8\mu_{i}^{2}}{k(\xi_{i})|\xi_{i}-q|^{2\alpha}(\varepsilon_{i}^{2}\mu_{i}^{2}+|x-\xi_{i}|^{2})^{2}},

(2.4)

which, respectively, solve

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}^{2}k(q)|x-q|^{2\alpha}e^{u_{0}}\qquad\textrm{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2},\,\qquad\quad\quad\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varepsilon_{0}^{2}k(q)|x-q|^{2\alpha}e^{u_{0}}=8\pi(1+\alpha),

(2.5)

and

\displaystyle-\Delta u_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}^{2}k(\xi_{i})|\xi_{i}-q|^{2\alpha}e^{u_{i}}\quad\,\,\textrm{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{2},\,\qquad\quad\quad\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varepsilon_{i}^{2}k(\xi_{i})|\xi_{i}-q|^{2\alpha}e^{u_{i}}=8\pi,

(2.6)

where

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(t)\equiv e^{-\frac{1}{2}t},\,\qquad\qquad\,\varepsilon_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}(t)\equiv e^{-\frac{1}{2}t\phi_{1}(\xi_{i})}.

(2.7)

Furthermore, we set

\displaystyle\rho_{0}:=\varepsilon_{0}^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}}=\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2(1+\alpha)}t\right\},\,\,\,\quad\,\,\,\,v_{0}:=\mu_{0}^{\frac{1}{1+\alpha}},\,\,\,\quad\,\,\,\gamma_{i}:=\frac{1}{\varepsilon_{0}}\varepsilon_{i}\mu_{i}=\mu_{i}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{2}t\big[\phi_{1}(\xi_{i})-1\big]\right\}.

(2.8)

We defined the approximate solution of problem (
1.6
) by

\displaystyle U(x):=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}\,U_{i}(x)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m}\,\big[u_{i}(x)+H_{i}(x)\big],

(2.9)

where
H_{i}(x)
is a correction term defined as the solution of

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{a}H_{i}+\nabla\log a(x)\nabla u_{i}=0\,\,\,\,\textrm{in}\,\,\,\,\,\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
&H_{i}=-u_{i}\,\quad\,\,\,\,\,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\textrm{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

(2.10)

Lemma 2.1.

For
any
0<\sigma<\min\{1,\,2(1+\alpha)\}
, any points

\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})\in\mathcal{O}_{t}(q)

and any
t
large enough,

\displaystyle H_{0}(x)=(1+\alpha)H(x,q)-\log\frac{8\mu_{0}^{2}(1+\alpha)^{2}}{k(q)}+O\left(\rho_{0}^{\sigma}v_{0}^{\sigma}\right),

(2.11)

\displaystyle H_{i}(x)=H(x,\xi_{i})-\log\frac{8\mu_{i}^{2}}{k(\xi_{i})|\xi_{i}-q|^{2\alpha}}+O\left(\varepsilon_{i}^{\sigma}\mu_{i}^{\sigma}\right),\,\quad\,i=1,\ldots,m,

(2.12)

uniformly in
\overline{\Omega}
, where
H
is the regular part of Green’s function defined in

(
1.5
)
.

Proof.

According to Lemma 2.4 in
[
28
]
, we need only to prove the expansion for
H_{0}
.
Observe that, on
\partial\Omega
,

\displaystyle H_{0}(x)=-u_{0}(x)=4(1+\alpha)\log|x-q|-\log\frac{8\mu_{0}^{2}(1+\alpha)^{2}}{k(q)}+O\left(\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\mu_{0}^{2}\right).

From (
1.4
)-(
1.5
) it follows that the regular part of Green’s function
H(x,y)
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta_{a}H(x,y)=-4\frac{(x-y)\cdot\nabla\log a(x)}{|x-y|^{2}},\,\,\,\,\,\,x\in\Omega,\\
&H(x,y)=4\log|x-y|,\,\qquad\qquad\qquad\,\,\,\,\quad\,x\in\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

If we set
Z_{0}(x)=H_{0}(x)-(1+\alpha)H(x,q)+\log\frac{8\mu_{0}^{2}(1+\alpha)^{2}}{k(q)}
,
then
Z_{0}(x)
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta_{a}Z_{0}(x)=\frac{4(1+\alpha)\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\mu_{0}^{2}}{\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\mu_{0}^{2}+|x-q|^{2(1+\alpha)}}\frac{(x-q)\cdot\nabla\log a(x)}{|x-q|^{2}}\,\ \ \,\,\textrm{in}\,\,\,\,\,\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
&Z_{0}(x)=O\left(\varepsilon_{0}^{2}\mu_{0}^{2}\right)\,\ \quad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\,\qquad\qquad\qquad\,\textrm{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Using the polar coordinates with center
q
, i.e.
r=|x-q|
and
making the change of variables

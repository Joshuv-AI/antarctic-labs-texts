1.
Introduction

The practical solution of integer points on hyperelliptic curves,
C:y^{2}=F(x)
for
F\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]

(\deg{F}\geq 3)
not a square, is a well studied problem. For instance if
F(x)
is monic and quartic, we have very sharp (upper) bounds for
\max\{|x|,|y|\}
or explicit methods to solve them
[
11
]
. For
\deg{F}
even, again we have polynomial bounds (but not very sharp), which in some cases we can just enumerate its integer points
[
27
]
. What is more, in this case we have specific algorithm to solve them, see
[
26
]
. If
F(x)
is of odd degree, the problem is harder. One reason for this, it is that the even degree can be treated with the so called Runge’s method
[
11
, Appendix]
. The same if
F(x)
is not monic (even for even degree), the problem is more difficult. In general, the case where
F(x)
is irreducible
and
\deg{F}\in\{3,4\}
leads to an elliptic curve, in which case we have a very good knowledge to practically solve it, for instance see
[
29
]
. The case where
F
is irreducible and
\deg{F}\geq 5,
was studied in
[
5
]
, where two assumptions must be satisfied so that their method be successful. They need a rational point on
C
and a basis for the jacobian of
C
over the rationals. Another method for
y^{2}=F(x)
and
\deg{F}
odd is the so called
quadratic Chabauty

[
1
]
. In
[
1
]
they combined Mordell-Weil sieve and
p-
adic approximations techniques to attack the problem.

Now, let
F(x)
be a reducible polynomial over the rationals. In this paper we address this case. The
quadratic Chabauty
method of
[
1
]
is still valid. Now there is also another method, the so called
descent method
. Fot instance in
[
9
]
, they use covers of
C
(in their paper they call such covers, heterogeneous spaces) in order to find rational points on curves of genus
2,
where
F(x)
is factorized in
{\mathbb{Q}}
as
f(x)g(x).
In
[
10
]
we used such covers to study the integer points on families of elliptic curves
y^{2}=x(x^{2}\pm p)
(
p
prime). This method is used in the current work to study hyperelliptic equations. We study the integer points on the hyperellipitc curve

y^{2}=(x^{3}+x+1)(x^{4}+2x^{3}-3x^{2}+4x+4),

which it was also studied in
[
1
, Section 9]
(using the
quadratic Chabauty
method). It was proved that the integer solutions
(x,y)
with
y>0,
are

\Sigma=\{(-1,2),(0,2),(-2,12),(3,62)\}.

It is easy to show that the set of integer solutions is indeed
\Sigma
using descent method, without the heavy computations on jacobian varieties.

We provide a more general framework, where superelliptic curves
{\mathcal{M}}:y^{p}=f(x)g(x),
also fits. For instance, in
[
10
, Section 8]
we tried this method in
y^{3}=x^{4}+kx.
In the present paper we study
y^{p}=(\alpha x^{p}+\beta)g(x)
(
p
prime). Therefore, in part, we generalize the results of
[
10
, Section 8]
.

In the bibliography, for superelliptic curves we have the following method (see
[
23
, Chapter IV, Section 4]
), which we call it
reduction to S-unit equations
. Under some conditions it may be practical. It is based on factorization over a number field. Say,
y^{n}=f(x),

n\geq 3,\ d=\deg{f}\geq 2
and
\alpha,\beta
are simple roots of
f(x).
If
(x,y)
is an integer point with
y\not=0,
then,
x-\alpha=\kappa\xi^{n}
where it is proved that
\kappa
belongs to a finite set of integral elements of the number field
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha),
for the proof of this see
[
23
, Lemma 4B]
. Similar if
\beta
is another simple root we get an equation of the form
x-\beta=\kappa^{\prime}\xi^{\prime n}.
If
d\geq 3
, then we end up with finitely many Thue equations
\kappa\xi^{n}-\kappa^{\prime}\xi^{\prime n}=\beta-\alpha
in
{\mathbb{Q}}(\alpha,\beta)
with unknowns
\xi,\xi^{\prime}.
If it occurs
\alpha,\beta,\xi,\xi^{\prime},\kappa,\kappa^{\prime}
be rational integers, we can solve the Thue equations over the integers.

For further information on descent method see
[
9
]
,
[
14
, Section 2.3]
,
[
16
, Section 1.3]
,
[
20
, Section 5.5]
and their references.

Our Contribution

We study the following families of superelliptic curves

Corollary 3.14
.

Let

The Artin–Rees lemma and size of spaces over nonassociative complete filtered rings
Jayanta Manoharmayum

Abstract
. This paper studies nonassociative filtered rings using associated gradations. We show that a complete filtered ring R R with affine associated graded ring generated in degree 1 1 is a local ring, and prove that the Artin–Rees lemma holds for R R . Assuming finiteness of the residue field, we derive asymptotics for abelian groups with an operation of R R , and identify classes of torsion elements for spaces over a central extension of R R .

1.
Introduction

A particularly effective technique in the study of filtered rings is to exploit the natural synergy between the filtration and its associated graded ring. In many instances, the associated graded ring is a simpler object to understand and more tractable; in the presence of a good filtration, we can utilise this knowledge to derive results about the original ring. A striking demonstration of this strategy is provided by the work of Huishi and van Oystaeyen on Zariskian filtrations in the setting of non-commutative rings. (For details, see
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
; for a different application, see
[
17
]
.).
This paper illustrates that the idea of linking filtered and graded problems can be effective even in the feral world of nonassociative rings.

Before proceeding to a description of the main results, we record our conventions for rings and filtrations. First, all rings are assumed to be unital. An unqualified ring always refers to a nonassociative (that is, not necessarily associative) ring; by contrast, unless indicated otherwise, a commutative ring/algebra is automatically assumed to be associative. Second,
all filtrations and gradings that we consider are assumed to be indexed over the monoid
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
. We will use straight-forward adaptations of terminology from the associative setting in this section; formal descriptions are given in later sections.

The results of this paper are obtained in the setting of complete filtered rings with affine associated graded rings which are generated in degree
1
. This is a broad class of rings, encompassing Iwasawa algebras and complete local Noetherian commutative rings. In detail, and for the remainder of this section, we assume the following.

R

(F^{*}(R))

2.
Finiteness conditions for spaces over a ring

2.1.
Generalities

Let
R
be a unital nonassociative ring. By a (left)
space over

, or simply an
R
-space
, we mean an abelian group

together with a bilinear pairing of additive groups

which is also unital in the sense that
(1,m)\to m
for all
m\in M

-space pairing will always be written multipicatively: thus
(r,m)\to rm
for all elements

(r_{1}+r_{2})m=r_{1}m+r_{2}m

and

Subspaces, quotients and morphisms of

-spaces are defined in the obvious way, and (can be checked to) have the expected properties.
Two equivalent descriptions of spaces over rings are noteworthy here. First, an

-space

is
an abelian group

with operators in
R
(see
[
2
, Ch. I, §4.2]
) such that the induced map from

is a morphism of abelian groups and the element

operates as the identity endomorphism on

-spaces is equivalent to the category of
(left)

-modules where

is the tensor algebra of the
\mathbb{Z}
-module

, and

is the ideal of

generated by

.

Remark 2.1
.

It would be desirable to have ‘modules’ rather than spaces, so that ring multiplication is involved. This can be done using the tensor algebra for rings satisfying polynomial identities, or from a variety (in the sense of universal algebras). See
[
19
, Ch. 11, §1]
) for details. Note that
even left ideals—which are left spaces and have ring multiplication built in—need to be appended before they can be used effectively as in the associative setting. (See
[
9
]
).

Now let

be an
R
-space. As in usual module theory, we say that

is
(left) Noetherian
if the ascending chain condition holds for

-subspaces of

: that is, if
M_{1}\subseteq M_{2}\subseteq\dots
is a sequence of
R
-subspaces of

, then we have
M_{k}=M_{k+1}=\dots
for some integer

. The ring

is left Noetherian if the ascending chain condition holds for left ideals.

We now consider finiteness conditions on spaces over a ring. Let
M
be an
R
-space. Recall that if
\Delta
is a subset of
M
, then
the
subspace generated by
\Delta
is the smallest subspace of

containing
\Delta
. A subspace of

is

if it is generated by a finite subset of

. Unfortunately, finite generation of spaces is a weaker condition than finite generation of associative modules; this difference is highlighted in the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Let

be an
R
-space.

(1)

Let
A
and
\Delta
be subsets of
R
and
M
, respectively. The
product

A\Delta
is the abelian subgroup of

consisting of all finite sums

\sum ax
with
a\in A
and
x\in\Delta
. The product
R\Delta
is the
the
R
-span
of
\Delta
.

(2)

We say that

is

if it is the
R
-span of a finite set: that is, we can find finitely many elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
in

such that
M=Rx_{1}+\dots+Rx_{n}
.

(3)

We say that

is

if every

-subspace of

The ring

is strongly (left) Noetherian if every (left) ideal of

is finitely spanned.

Note that if

is an
R
-space, then the

-subspace of

generated by a subset

is
R\Delta+R(R\Delta)+R(R(R\Delta))+\dots
. Also, as the ring

R\Delta+R(R\Delta)+R(R(R\Delta))+\dots=\bigcup R(R(\dots(R\Delta)\dots)).

This can well fail to be finitely spanned (e.g., consider free nonassociative rings).

Remark 2.3
.

It is clear that a strongly Noetherian ring/space is Noetherian. An associative ring is strongly Noetherian if and only if it is Noetherian. More generally, suppose the
R
-space

has the following property: for all elements

, the
R
-span

is a subspace of

. Then

is strongly Noetherian if and only it is Noetherian.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
R
be a ring and let

be an
R
-space. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

The space
M
is Noetherian if, and only if, every
R
-subspace of

is finitely generated.

(2)

Let

be an
R
-subspace. Then

is Noetherian (resp. strongly Noetherian) if and only if both

and

are Noetherian (resp. strongly Noetherian).

Proof.

Proposition
2.4
is proved in exactly the same way as in the setting of associative rings and modules, and so we omit the proof. To illustrate one case, let us show that if

and

must be strongly Noetherian.

So let

be an
R
-subspace. Now, both

(being a subspace of

) and

(viewed as a subspace of

) are finitely spanned. If we now take

x_{1},\dots x_{n},y_{1},\dots y_{m}\in N

such that

and the images of

.∎

Remark 2.5
.

Let

be a strongly Noetherian ring. Then, by Proposition

, we obtain a good supply of strongly Noetherian

-spaces by taking subquotients of finite free

-modules from the construction.

2.2.
Hilbert’s basis theorem

We now consider persistence of finiteness conditions under central extensions.
If
R
is a ring, then
R[X]
denotes the polynomial ring in a central indeterminate

; if

is an

-space, then we write

for the

-space of polynomials in

Proposition 2.6

(Hilbert’s basis theorem)
.

Let

be a ring and let

be an
R
-space. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

If

is Noetherian, then

is a Noetherian

-module.

(2)

If

Both parts of Proposition

We start with the following construction. Let

-subspace of

. For each integer

, we define a subset

by

(1)

N(j):=\left\{\,m\in M\;\middle|\;\begin{tabular}[]{c}$N$ contains a polynomial of the form\\
$mX^{j}+\text{lower degree terms}$\end{tabular}\,\right\}.

It is then easy to check that

is an

for all

.

.

Let

Since

So we are reduced to showing that if

Suppose this last claim is not true. Take

. Thus

and, as

, we can find an element

3.
Filtered rings and spaces

In this section, we set out notation and terminology for filtrations and associated gradations, and consider finiteness conditions (from Section
2
) in the context of filtered spaces. In Section
3.1
, we recall some properties of filtered abelian groups
and discuss two lemmas (Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.2
) that underpin many of the constructions in this paper. Specialising to rings and spaces over rings, we show that the property of being strongly Noetherian can be deduced from properties of associated gradations (Proposition
3.7
) in Section
3.2
.

3.1.
Filtered abelian groups

As indicated in Section
1
, we shall only consider decreasing filtrations indexed over
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
. We will denote the accompanying decreasing filtration on a filtered object by
(F^{i}(-))
when no confusion can arise. All abelian groups considered are additive.

By a
filtered abelian group
, we mean an abelian group
A
together with an exhaustive decreasing filtration
(\,F^{i}(A)\mid i=0,1,2,\dots)
of subgroups: that is, we have
F^{0}(A)=A
, and the inclusion
F^{i}(A)\supseteq F^{i+1}(A)
holds for all integers
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
The filtered abelian group
A
is said to be
separated
if its filtration is separated, i.e., we have
\bigcap F^{i}(M)=(0)
; it is
complete
if the natural map
M\to\varprojlim M/F^{i}(M)
is an isomorphism.

We now record some basic properties of filtered abelian groups. (For general references, see
[
7
]
,
[
12
]
.)

(FilAb1)

Let
A
be a filtered abelian group with filtration
(F^{i}(A))
.
The filtration
(F^{i}(A)))
allows us to define the
associated graded abelian group

\text{gr}(A)
given by

\text{gr}(A):=\frac{F^{0}(A)}{F^{1}(A)}\oplus\frac{F^{1}(A)}{F^{2}(A)}\oplus\dots.

The filtration also gives rise to two maps: the
valuation
map
v\colon A\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
and the
principal part
map
\sigma\colon A\to\text{gr}(A)
. These are defined as follows. If
a\in A
, then:

(a)

the valuation of
a
is given by
v(a):=\max\{\,i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\mid a\in F^{i}(A)\,\}
;

(b)

the principal part
\sigma(a)
is the image of
a
in
\text{gr}(A)
under the natural map

F^{v(a)}(A)\to F^{v(a)}(A)/F^{v(a)+1}(A).

By convention
F^{\infty}(A):=\bigcap F^{i}(A)
. Thus an element
a\in A
has infinite valuation
v(a)=\infty
if and only if the principal part
\sigma(a)=0
.

(FilAb2)

Let
A=A_{0}\oplus A_{1}\oplus\dots
be an
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded abelian group. Then
A
is naturally filtered with filtration
F^{i}(A)=A_{i}\oplus A_{i+1}\oplus\dots
, and we have
\text{gr}(A)\cong A
canonically.

(FilAb3)

Subgroups and quotients of a filtered abelian group inherit natural filtrations. Let
A
be a filtered abelian group with filtration
(F^{i}(A))
and
let
B
be a subgroup of
A
. Then we have induced filtrations on
B
and
A/B
given by

F^{i}(B)=B\cap F^{i}(A)\quad\text{and}\quad F^{i}(A/B)=(F^{i}(A)+B)/B.

The natural map
F^{i}(B)/F^{i+1}(B)\to F^{i}(A)/F^{i+1}(A)
is an injection for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and
\text{gr}(B)
is naturally a graded subgroup of
\text{gr}(A)
. The valuation and principal part of an element in
B
does not depend on whether we use the filtration
(F^{i}(B))
or
(F^{i}(A))
.

We now consider products. Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{n}
are filtered abelian groups. Then the induced filtration on
A_{1}\times\dots\times A_{n}
is
given by

(2)

F^{i}(A_{1}\times\dots\times A_{n})=F^{i}(A_{1})\times\dots\times F^{i}(A_{n}).

We have the following equality for the induced valuation: if
(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
is an element of
A_{1}\times\dots\times A_{n}
, then

(3)

v(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})=\min\{v(a_{1}),\dots,v(a_{n})\}.

(FilAb4)

The abelian group
A
is a topological group with its associated filtration
(F^{i}(A))
forming a basis of open neighbourhoods of
0
. If the filtration is separated, then the topology on
A
is the metric topology induced by the valuation map. If
A
is complete, then the natural map
A\to\varprojlim A/F^{i}(A)
is an isomorphism of topological groups with each factor
A/F^{i}(A)
in the product having the discrete topology, and
A
is a complete metric space.

We now state two lemmas that are key tools in the construction of elements in complete filtered abelian groups. The first of these (Lemma
3.1
) shows how to handle problems of convergence using the valuation; the proof is standard and omitted (see, for instance,
[
7
, Ch. I, §3.3]
). The second (Lemma
3.2

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
A
be a filtered abelian group, and let
v\colon A\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
be its valuation map. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

Suppose

is separated. Then a sequence
(x_{n})
in
A
converges to

if and only
v(x_{n}-x)\to\infty
.

(2)

Suppose
A
is complete. If
(x_{n})
is a sequence in
A
, then the series
x_{1}+x_{2}+\dots
converges precisely when
v(x_{n})\to\infty
(i.e., when
x_{n}\to 0
).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
A
and
B
be filtered abelian groups, and let
\phi:A\to B
be a continuous map. Assume the following two hypotheses.

(1)

A
is complete and

is separated.

(2)

There exists a constant

with the following property: given an element
b\in B
, we can find an element
a\in A
such that

v(b)<v(b-\phi(a))\quad\text{and}\quad v(b)\leq v(a)+K.

Then
\phi:A\to B
is surjective.

Proof.

Let
b\in B
. We construct sequences
a_{1},a_{2},\dots
(in
A
) and
b_{0},b_{1},b_{2},\dots
(in
B
) as follows. Set
b_{0}:=b
. Then, using hypothesis
2
, choose an element
a_{1}\in A
such that

v(b_{0})<v(b_{0}-\phi(a_{1}))
and
v(b_{0})\leq v(a_{1})+K
,

and set
b_{1}:=b_{0}-\phi(a_{1})
. Then iterate.

More formally, having constructed
a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in A
and
b_{1},\dots,b_{k}\in B
, we use hypothesis

to choose an element

and set

v(b_{k})<v(b_{k+1})\quad\text{and}\quad v(b_{k})\leq v(a_{k+1})+K.

Since

and

is separated, the sequence
(\phi(a_{1})+\dots+\phi(a_{k}))
converges to

by Lemma

and

converges in

∎

3.2.
Filtered rings and filtered spaces

By a
filtered ring
, we mean a ring
R
together with a decreasing filtration
(\,F^{i}(R)\mid i=0,1,2,\dots)

of ideals satisfying the following conditions.

•

The filtration is exhaustive: that is, we have
F^{0}(R)=R
.

•

The inclusions
F^{i}(R)F^{j}(R)\subseteq F^{i+j}(R)
hold for all
i,j\geq 0
.

If
R
is a filtered ring, then a
filtered
R
-space
is an
R
-space
M
together with a decreasing filtration of
R
-subspaces
(\,F^{i}(M)\mid i=0,1,2,\dots)
such that
F^{0}(M)=M
and

F^{i}(R)F^{j}(M)\subseteq F^{i+j}(M)
for all
i,j\geq 0
.

Let
R
is a filtered ring. Then the graded abelian group

\text{gr}(R):=\frac{F^{0}(R)}{F^{1}(R)}\,\oplus\,\frac{F^{1}(R)}{F^{2}(R)}\,\oplus\,\dots.

is a ring. We refer to
\text{gr}(R)
as the
associated graded ring
. Furthermore, if
M
is a filtered
R
-space then the associated graded abelian group

\text{gr}(M):=\frac{F^{0}(M)}{F^{1}(M)}\,\oplus\,\frac{F^{1}(M)}{F^{2}(M)}\,\oplus\,\dots

is a graded
\text{gr}(R)
-space. The multiplicative structures in both cases are induced by the inclusions
F^{i}(R)F^{j}(R)\subseteq F^{i+j}(R)
and

F^{i}(R)F^{j}(M)\subseteq F^{i+j}(M)
.

Remark 3.3
.

The products
F^{i}(R)F^{j}(R)
and
F^{i}(R)F^{j}(M)
are additive groups (Definition
2.2
); they are not assumed to be ideals or subspaces.

Filtered rings and filtered spaces are filtered abelian groups of course, and we shall use the terminology introduced in Section
3.1
without explicit reference. Note that valuation maps and principal part maps for filtered rings and filtered spaces do not preserve neither addition nor multiplication. We record the following specific case for later use, and briefly discuss two examples of filtrations and associated gradations.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

be a filtered

-module. Suppose

and

\sigma(r)\sigma(m)\neq 0
in

and
\sigma(rm)=\sigma(r)\sigma(m)
.

Proof.

Set
v(r)=i
and
v(m)=j
(both valuations are necessarily integers). Since

\sigma(r)\sigma(m)=rm\mod{F^{i+j+1}}
by definition, the product

4.
The Artin–Rees lemma for complete local-filtered rings

We will now prove assertions
(A1)
to
(A4)
in the setting of filtered spaces. As noted in Section

, the key idea is to consider rings with good filtrations and derive the sought results from associated gradations. Motivated by Zariskian filtrations in the associative setting, we consider filtered rings with strongly Noetherian Rees rings in Section

4.1.
The Rees ring

Let
R
be a filtered ring with filtration
(F^{i}(R))

\mathbf{R}(R)
of

to be the subring of

given by

(6)

\mathbf{R}(R)=R\oplus F^{1}(R)X\oplus F^{2}(R)X^{2}\oplus\dots.

If

(7)

\mathbf{R}(M):=F^{0}(M)\oplus F^{1}(M)X\oplus F^{2}(M)X^{2}\oplus\dots.

It is easily checked that
\mathbf{R}(M)
is, indeed, an
\mathbf{R}(R)
-space.

Let
M
be a filtered
R
-space, and let

A
denote the ring
R
or the space
M

has a natural filtration with associated valuation extending the valuation on

having valuation

F^{n}(\mathbf{R}(A))=\big\{\,f\in\mathbf{R}(A)\;|\;v(f)\geq n\,\big\}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\leq i\\
n\leq i+j\end{subarray}}F^{i}(A)X^{j}

We can now state a proposition on

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
R
be a complete filtered ring and let
M
be a separated filtered
R

\text{gr}(\mathbf{R}(M))
is strongly Noetherian, then
\mathbf{R}(M)
is strongly Noetherian.

We begin the proof of Proposition

with a definition. Let
M

R

A
denote
M
or the
R
-space

\varphi(\alpha)=\begin{cases}\sigma(a_{n})X^{n},&\text{if $\alpha=a_{0}+\dots+a_{n}X^{n}$ has degree $n$};\\

As in the case of the principal part map

, the leading monomial part map preserves multiplication only in specific cases, one of which is covered by the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2

Let
\alpha\in\mathbf{R}(R)
and

\varphi(\alpha\beta)=\varphi(\alpha)\varphi(\beta)

Proof.

Immediate from Lemma
3.4
.
∎

We now return to the proof of Proposition
4.1
. Let
\mathcal{N}
be an
\mathbf{R}(R)
-subspace
\mathbf{R}(M)

\overline{\varphi(\mathcal{N})}
be the
\text{gr}(\mathbf{R}(R))
-subspace of
\text{gr}(\mathbf{R}(M))
generated by

. Then by Lemma

\overline{\varphi(\mathcal{N})}=\text{gr}(\mathbf{R}(R))\varphi(\mathcal{N})=\left\{\,\text{finite sums $\sum\varphi(\eta)\,\Big|\,\eta\in\mathcal{N}$}\,\right\}.

Hence, since
\text{gr}(\mathbf{R}(M))
is strongly Noetherian, we can find finitely many non-zero elements

in

such that

\overline{\varphi(\mathcal{N})}=\text{gr}(\mathbf{R}(R))\varphi(\eta_{1})+\dots+\text{gr}(\mathbf{R}(R))\varphi(\eta_{n}).

Our aim then is to show that

.

Note that we cannot hope to use the method of Lemma

directly as the Rees ring

For each non-negative integer

Also, for each index

and let

\eta_{i}=a_{i}X^{d_{i}}+\text{lower degree terms},\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi(\eta_{i})=\sigma(a_{i})X^{d_{i}}.

We then have the following claim.

Claim 4.3
.

Let
\eta_{i}=a_{i}X^{d_{i}}+\text{lower degree terms}
,

, be as above. If

. Fix a non-negative integer

Proof of Claim
4.3
.

We give

It is clear that

Consider a non-zero element

4.2.
Complete local-filtered rings

From here on, we will be primarily interested in the following class of filtered rings.

Definition 4.4
.

We say that a filtered ring
R
is a

complete local-filtered ring
, or that
R
has a complete local-filtration, if its filtration
(F^{i}(R))
is complete and satisfies the following properties.

(C1)

The quotient
R/F^{1}(R)
is a field, which we refer to as the
residue field
of

.

(C2)

The associated graded ring
\text{gr}(R)
is a finitely generated commutative
R/F^{1}(R)
-algebra and generated in degree
1
.

We then have the following structure theorem for complete local-filtered rings.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
R
be a complete local-filtered ring. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

R
is a local ring with maximal ideal
F^{1}(R)
.

(2)

R
is strongly left noetherian.

(3)

For all integers
i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have
F^{i}(R)F^{j}(R)=F^{i+j}(R)
.

(4)

The Rees ring
\mathbf{R}(R)
is strongly left Noetherian.

Before proceeding to the proof of Theorem
4.5
, we record the following note on parts
1
and
3
of Theorem
4.5
(thereby justifying our terminology in Definition
4.4
). Here and subsequently, we write ‘
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a complete local-filtered (resp. local) ring
’ to express the condition that
R
is a complete local-filtered (resp. local) ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
.

Note 4.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring, and let
n
be a positive integer. For each choice of placing parenthesis in an

n
-fold product of
\mathfrak{m}
. In general, different choices of inserting parenthesis will lead to different additive subgroups of

.

Suppose now that the local ring
(R,\mathfrak{m})
has a complete local-filtration. Then part
1
and part

of Theorem

imply that all choices of inserting parenthesis will give the same additive subgroup and we obtain a uniquely defined

-fold product
\mathfrak{m}^{n}
. For definiteness, recursively set
\mathfrak{m}^{n+1}:=\mathfrak{m}(\mathfrak{m}^{n})
for integers

with
\mathfrak{m}^{0}:=R
. Then each
\mathfrak{m}^{n}
is an ideal of

and

(\mathfrak{m}^{n}\mid n=0,1,2,\dots)
is
the
complete local-filtration. Furthermore, the equality
\mathfrak{m}^{i}\mathfrak{m}^{j}=\mathfrak{m}^{i+j}
holds for all non-negative integers

and

.

Proof of Theorem
4.5
.

We denote the residue field

by

throughout this proof.

(Part 1)

To show that
R
is a local ring, consider an element

induces an isomorphism on each homogeneous component of

. The continuous additive map

(Part 2)

This follows from Proposition

(Part 3)

4.3.
The Artin-Rees lemma

Just as the in the case modules over local rings, the following version of the Artin–Rees lemma establishes when the topology on filtered spaces over a complete local-filtered ring is induced by powers of the maximal ideal.

Theorem 4.7

(Artin–Rees lemma)
.

Let
R
be a complete local-filtered ring, and let
M
be a separated filtered
R
-space. Assume that the associated space

\text{gr}(M)
is a finitely generated
\text{gr}(R)
-module
.
Then there exists an integer
D\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

F^{n+d}(M)=F^{n}(R)F^{d}(M)

for all integers
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
d\geq D
.

For the proof, we need the following lemma which constructs spanning sets that preserve each step of the filtration. (The integer
D
in Theorem
4.7
is derived from the valuations of elements in these spanning sets.)

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
R
be a complete filtered ring and let
M
be a separated filtered
R
-space. Suppose the associated Rees space
\mathbf{R}(M)
is a strongly Noetherian
\mathbf{R}(R)
-space. We can then find finitely many non-negative integers
d_{1},\dots,d_{k}
in
\mathbb{N}_{0}
and elements
m_{i}\in F^{d_{i}}(M)
for
i=1,\dots,k
such that the following holds: for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

F^{n}(M)=F^{n-d_{1}}(R)m_{1}+\dots+F^{n-d_{k}}(R)m_{k}

where we take
F^{i}(R):=(0)
when
i<0
.

Proof.

Replacing each element of a finite spanning set with its monomial parts, we see that we can find finitely many non-negative integers

d_{1},\dots,d_{k}
in
\mathbb{N}_{0}
and monomials
m_{1}X^{d_{1}},\dots,m_{k}X^{d_{k}}
in
\mathbf{R}(M)
such that

\mathbf{R}(M)=\mathbf{R}(R)m_{1}X^{d_{1}}+\dots+\mathbf{R}(R)m_{k}X^{d_{k}}.

Then each
m_{i}\in F^{d_{i}}(M)
and
F^{n}(M)=F^{n-d_{1}}(R)m_{1}+\dots+F^{n-d_{k}}(R)m_{k}
.∎

Proof of Theorem
4.7
.

We view
\text{gr}(M)
as a filtered
\text{gr}(R)
-module with their natural filtrations (induced from the gradings; see
(FilAb2)
). Since
\text{gr}(R)
is affine and generated in degree
1
, it follows that
\mathbf{R}(\text{gr}(M))
is a Noetherian
\mathbf{R}(\text{gr}(R))
-module. Using the canonical isomrphisms

\text{gr}(\mathbf{R}(R))\cong\mathbf{R}(\text{gr}(R)),\quad\text{gr}(\mathbf{R}(M))\cong\mathbf{R}(\text{gr}(M))

(see the displayed equalities (
10
)) and Proposition
4.1
, we deduce that
\mathbf{R}(M)
is a strongly Noetherian
\mathbf{R}(R)
-space.

By Lemma
4.8
, we can then find
k
non-negative integers
d_{1},\dots,d_{k}
in
\mathbb{N}_{0}
and elements
m_{i}\in F^{d_{i}}(M)
for each
i=1,\dots,k
such that

(12)

F^{n}(M)=F^{n-d_{1}}(R)m_{1}+\dots+F^{n-d_{k}}(R)m_{k}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Set
D:=\max\{d_{1},\dots,d_{k}\}
, and let
d\geq D
. Then, using Theorem
4.5
4
, we obtain

(13)

F^{n+d}(M)=\left(F^{n}(R)F^{d-d_{1}}(R)\right)m_{1}+\dots+\left(F^{n}(R)F^{d-d_{k}}(R)\right)m_{k}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

For each
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, let
n_{i}
be the dimension of
F^{d-d_{i}}(R)/F^{d-d_{i}+1}(R)
as a vector space over the residue field of
R
. Choose
elements
x(i,1),\dots,x(i,n_{i})
in
F^{d-d_{i}}(R)

such that the principal parts

\sigma(x(i,1)),\dots,\sigma(x(i,n_{i}))
form a basis of
F^{d-d_{i}}(R)/F^{d-d_{i}+1}(R)
.

Now let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and let

. Using the displayed equality (
13
), we can find elements
r(i,j)\in F^{n}(R)
, where

and

for each

such that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}r(i,j)(x(i,j)\Big)m_{i}\equiv m\pmod{F^{n+d+1}(M)}.

The assumption that
\text{gr}(M)
is a
\text{gr}(R)
-module then implies that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}r(i,j)((x(i,j)m_{i})\equiv m\pmod{F^{n+d+1}(M)}.

It follows that for a fixed

, the map

(F^{N}(R))^{n_{1}}\times\dots\times(F^{N}(R))^{n_{k}}\to F^{N+d}(M)

given by

\big(r(1,1),\dots,r(1,n_{1}),\dots,r(k,1),\dots,r(k,n_{k})\big)\to\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{i}}r(i,j)((x(i,j)m_{i})

satisfies the hypotheses of Lemma

Since

we obtain

.∎

As an application of Theorem
4.7
, we now discuss the extent to which the filtration on spaces with cyclic associated gradation is determined by the maximal ideal. The example below will be particularly relevant in Section

when we discuss torsion elements.

Example 4.9

(Cyclic spaces)
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete filtered ring, and let
M
be a separated filtered
R
-space. We assume that
\text{gr}(M)
is a cyclic
\text{gr}(R)
-module, and fix an element

whose principal part generates
\text{gr}(M)
. Then, by Lemma

, the element

spans

: that is, we have

. By re-indexing the filtration on

if necessary, we can assume that

has valuation

. We will now show that the following statements hold.

(CS1)

The filtration on

is given by

F^{n}(M)=\mathfrak{m}^{n}x
for all integers

.

(CS2)

For all integers

, we have
\mathfrak{m}^{i}(\mathfrak{m}^{j}x)=\mathfrak{m}^{i+j}x
.

Proof.

For part

, we need to check that the map

given by

is surjective. Now

, then the map

.

For part

against the displayed relation (

) shows that Theorem

.
∎

5.
Dimensions and sizes of permissible spaces

As we will now be considering consequences of the Artin–Rees lemma, let us denominate the class of spaces satisfying the hypotheses of Theorem
4.7
.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
R
be a complete local-filtered ring.

(1)

A filtered space
M
is said to be
permissible
if it is separated and the associated graded space

\text{gr}(M)
is a finitely generated
\text{gr}(R)
-module
. If
M
is permissible, then we call the Krull dimension of
\text{gr}(M)
the
associated dimension
of
M
and denote it by
\dim(M)
.

(2)

We say that a filtration is a
permissible filtration
if it is the filtration of a permissible
R
-space.

Let
R
be a complete local-filtered ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
. We will now show that the topology and the associated dimension of a permissible
R
-space are independent of the (choice of) permissible filtration. When
R
has finite residue field, the independence of filtration allows us to determine the cardinality of
M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M
asymptotically for any permissible
R
-space
M
. Finally, in Section
5.3
, we relate associated dimensions of permissible spaces to torsion.

5.1.
Independence of permissible filtrations

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local-filtered ring, and let
M
be a permissible filtered
R
-space. Then, the topology on
M
is induced by the filtration
(\mathfrak{m}^{i}M\mid i=0,1,\dots)
of subgroups.

Proof.

By Theorem
4.7
, we can find a non-negative integer
d
such that
F^{n+d}(M)=F^{n}(R)F^{d}(M)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Consequently, we have

(14)

F^{n+d}(R)F^{0}(M)\subseteq F^{n+d}(M)=F^{n}(R)F^{d}(M)\subseteq F^{n}(R)F^{0}(M)\subseteq F^{n}(M)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Since the topology on each quotient
M/F^{i}(M)
is discrete, the subgroup
F^{i}(R)M
is open. The proposition then follows from Theorem
4.5
and the inclusions (
14
) above. ∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
R
be a complete local-filtered ring, and let
M
be an
R
-space. If
(G^{i}(M)\mid i=0,1,\dots)
and
(H^{i}(M)\mid i=0,1,\dots)
are two permissible filtrations on
M
, then the
\text{gr}(R)
-modules

\frac{G^{0}(M)}{G^{1}(M)}\oplus\frac{G^{1}(M)}{G^{2}(M)}\oplus\dots\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{H^{0}(M)}{H^{1}(M)}\oplus\frac{H^{1}(M)}{H^{2}(M)}\oplus\dots

have the same Krull dimension. In other words, the dimension of a permissible space is independent of the choice of permissible filtration.

The justification makes use of the Hilbert–Samuel polynomial of the associated graded module, and we recall this now. Let
R
be complete local-filtered ring, and let
M
be an
R
-space. We write
\ell(M)
for the length of the
R
-space
M
. Thus
\ell(M)=n
if there is a saturated chain

0=M_{0}\subsetneq M_{1}\subsetneq\dots\subsetneq M_{n}=M

of
R
-subspaces; if no such chain exists, then
\ell(M)=\infty
. The length of an
R
-space is well defined since, as indicated in Section
2.1
,
R
-spaces are abelian groups with operators in the set
R
(see
[
8
, Ch. 3, §3.3]
).

Let
\boldsymbol{k}
denote the residue field of
R
, and let
M
be a permissible
R
-space. Then, for any integer
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

\ell\big(M/F^{n}(M)\big)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\dim_{\;\boldsymbol{k}}F^{k-1}(M)/F^{k}(M).

If
\mathcal{P}(x)
is the Hilbert–Samuel polynomial of the
\text{gr}(R)
-module
\text{gr}(M)
, then the degree of
\mathcal{P}
is the Krull dimension of
\text{gr}(M)
and
\ell\big(M/F^{n}(M)\big)=\mathcal{P}(n)
for all but finitely many
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. (See §13 of
[
11
]
.) We will often refer to
\mathcal{P}(x)
as the Hilbert–Samuel polynomial of the (permissible) filtration
(F^{*}(M))
.

Note 5.4
.

Let
0\to L\to M\to N\to 0
be a short exact sequence of
R
-spaces. We view
L
as a subspace and
N
as the quotient
M/L
. Let
(F^{*}(M))
be a permissible filtration on
M
. Then, with the induced filtrations (see
(FilAb3)
) on
L
and
N
, the sequence
0\to\text{gr}(L)\to\text{gr}(M)\to\text{gr}(N)\to 0
is an exact sequence of graded spaces: that is, we have

(15)

0\to\frac{F^{i}(L)}{F^{i+1}(L)}\to\frac{F^{i}(M)}{F^{i+1}(M)}\to\frac{F^{i}(N)}{F^{i+1}(N)}\to 0

for all
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Thus the induced filtration on
L
is permissible. If
L
is topologically closed, then the quotient
M/L
is also permissible with the induced filtration.

Proof of Proposition
5.3
.

We begin by setting out some notation. If
L
is a permissible
R
-space with filtration
(\Lambda^{*}(L))
, then we denote its Krull dimension by
\dim(\Lambda^{*}(L))
.

Consider the filtrations on
M
and
M\times M
given by

F^{i}(M)=G^{i}(M)\cap H^{i}(M)\quad\text{and}\quad F^{i}(M\times M)=G^{i}(M)\times H^{i}(M),

respectively. Note that the product
M\times M

is a permissible filtered module with respect to the filtration

(F^{*}(M\times M))
. Using the diagonal embedding
m\to(m,m)
to identify
M
with the diagonal of
M\times M
, we see that
(F^{*}(M))
is the induced filtration of
(F^{*}(M\times M))
on the diagonal, and therefore
(F^{*}(M))
is a permissible filtration on
M
.

Now, using the exact sequence (
15
), we have

\ell(M/F^{i}(M))\leq\ell\big((M\times M)/F^{i}(M\times M)\big)=\ell\big(M/G^{i}(M)\big)+\ell\big(M/H^{i}(M)\big).

By considering associated Hilbert–Samuel polynomials, we deduce that

(16)

\dim\big(F^{*}(M)\big)\leq\max\big\{\dim\big(G^{*}(M)\big),\,\dim\big(H^{*}(M)\big)\,\}.

For inequalities in the other direction, note that the inclusions
F^{i}(M)\subseteq G^{i}(M)
and
F^{i}(M)\subseteq H^{i}(M)
imply that

\ell(M/G^{i}(M))\leq\ell(M/F^{i}(M))\quad\text{and}\quad\ell(M/G^{i}(M))\leq\ell(M/F^{i}(M)).

Hence, from the associated Hilbert–Samuel polynomials, we see that

(17)

\dim(G^{*}(M))\leq\dim(F^{*}(M))\quad\text{and}\quad\dim(H^{*}(M))\leq\dim(F^{*}(M)).

Combining the displayed inequalities (
16
) and (
17
) above, we obtain

\dim(F^{*}(M))=\dim(G^{*}(M))=\dim(H^{*}(M)),

and this completes the proof of Proposition
5.3
.∎

The following corollary is now immediate from Note
5.4
and the result that the dimension is independent of choice of permissible filtration.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
R
be a complete local-filtered ring, let
M
be a permissible
R
-space, and let
L\subseteq M
be an
R
-subspace of
M
. Then
L
is a permissible space and
\dim(L)\leq\dim(M)
. Furthermore, if
L
is (topologically) closed, then
M/L
is a permissible space and
\dim(M)=\max\{\,\dim(L),\dim(M/L)\,\}
.

5.2.
Size of permissible spaces

We will now specialise to the case of finite residue field, and prove the following result on the asymptotic size of spaces.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local-filtered ring with finite residue field, let
M
be a permissible
R
-space, and set

q:=|\,R/\mathfrak{m}\,|,\quad\delta:=\dim(M).

Also, choose a permissible filtration
(F^{*}(M))
on
M
, and let
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
be the leading coefficient of the Hilbert–Samuel polynomial of
(F^{*}(M))
. Then

(18)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log_{q}\left|M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M\right|}{n^{\delta}}=\alpha.

Consequently, the Hilbert–Samuel polynomial of any permissible filtration on
M
is of the form
\alpha x^{\delta}+\text{lower degree terms}
.

Proof.

Using Theorem
4.7
and arguing as in the displayed inclusions (
14
), we can find a non-negative integer
d
such that

F^{n+d}(M)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{n}M\subseteq F^{n}(M)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. The cardinalities of the quotients then satisfy

(19)

\log_{q}\left|M/F^{n}(M)\right|\leq\log_{q}\left|M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M\right|\leq\log_{q}\left|M/F^{n+d}(M)\right|.

Let
\alpha x^{\delta}+Q(x)
be the Hilbert–Samuel polynomial associated to the filtration
(F^{*}(M))
. Thus
Q(x)
has degree at most
\delta-1
, and
\alpha
is a positive rational number. Since

\log_{q}\left|M/F^{i}(M)\right|
is the length of the
R
-space
M/F^{i}(M)
, the displayed inequalities (
19
) imply that

\alpha n^{\delta}+Q(n)\leq\log_{q}\left|M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M\right|\leq\alpha(n+d)^{\delta}+Q(n+d)

for sufficiently large
n
. The theorem is now immediate.∎

5.3.
Torsion elements in permissible spaces

The preceding discussion can be applied to identify classes of torsion elements in permissible spaces over a complete local-filtered ring with finite residue field. The class of elements which we consider is covered by the following definition.

Definition 5.7
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local-filtered ring, and let
M
be an
R
-space.
An element
x\in M
is said to be

R
-distinguished
, or simply
distinguished
, if
\mathfrak{m}^{i}(Rx)=\mathfrak{m}^{i}x
for all integers
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. If the stronger condition
\mathfrak{m}^{i}(\mathfrak{m}^{j}x)=\mathfrak{m}^{i+j}x
holds for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then we say that
x
is
\mathfrak{m}
-adically distinguished
.

Example 5.8
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local-filtered ring and let
M
be an
R
-space.
Then any element of
M
on which
R
operates associatively is
R
-distinguished. More precisely, if
m\in M
and
r(sm)=(rs)m
for all
r,s\in R
, then
m
is
\mathfrak{m}
-adically distinguished. In particular, if
R
is associative and
M
is an
R
-module, then every element of
M
is
\mathfrak{m}
-adically distinguished.

Note 5.9
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local-filtered ring and let
M
be an
R
-space. Then the following holds:
An element
x\in M
is
\mathfrak{m}
-adically distinguished if and only if
Rx
has a permissible filtration with cyclic associated gradation.

The forward implication holds by definition. Conversely, if
Rx
has a permissible filtration with cyclic associated gradation, then we must have
\text{gr}(Rx)=\text{gr}(R)\sigma(x)

where
\sigma(x)
is the principal part of
x
. Hence
x
is
\mathfrak{m}
-adically distinguished (by Example
4.9

(CS2)
).

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local-filtered ring with finite residue field and let
M
be a permissible
R
-space. Assume that
\text{gr}(R)
is an integral domain, and that
M
is spanned by
\mathfrak{m}
-adically distinguished elements with non-zero annihilators. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(S1)

\dim(M)\leq\dim(R)-1
.

(S2)

Every distinguished element of
M
has a non-zero annihilator.

(S3)

M
is spanned by
\mathfrak{m}
-adically distinguished elements with non-zero annihilators.

Remark 5.11
.

Theorem
5.10
implies the following in an associative setting.

Suppose
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is an associative complete local Noetherian ring with finite residue field and associated graded ring a commutative domain, and let
M
be finitely generated
R
-module. We assume that
M=Rx_{1}+\dots+Rx_{n}
with each
x_{i}
having non-zero annihilator. Then every element of
M
has a non-zero annihilator
.

The conclusion is not obvious: there is no clear way of producing an annihilator of
x_{1}+x_{2}
out of relations
r_{1}x_{1}=r_{2}x_{2}=0
.

Returning to the proof of Theorem
5.10
, we recall the following result from commutative algebra which plays a significant role in the ensuing discussion.

Theorem 5.12

(height–dimension formula)
.

If
A
is an affine domain and
I
is an ideal of
A
, then
\h(I)+\dim(A/I)=\dim(A)
.

Proof.

See
[
10
, Ch. 5, 14.H]
. The formula stated there (
loc. cit.
) covers prime ideals. The result for a general ideal
I
follows on choosing a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\supseteq I
with
\h(\mathfrak{p})=\h(I)
, and then applying the cited result together with the inequalities
\dim(A/\mathfrak{p})\leq\dim(A/I)
and
\h(I)+\dim(A/I)\leq\dim(A)
.∎

Proof of Theorem
5.10
.

The implication
\text{\ref{torsion2}}\implies\text{\ref{torsion3}}
is immediate from the hypothesis that
M
is spanned by
\mathfrak{m}
-adically distinguished elements with non-zero annihilators. The implications
\text{\ref{torsion1}}\implies\text{\ref{torsion2}}
and
\text{\ref{torsion3}}\implies\text{\ref{torsion1}}
are covered by Lemma
5.13
and Lemma
5.14
below, respectively. ∎

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local-filtered ring with finite residue field and let
M
be a permissible
R
-space.
If
\dim(M)\leq\dim(R)-1
, then every
R
-distinguished element in
M
has a non-zero annihilator.

Proof.

Let
x\in M
be
R
-distinguished. Then
Rx
is an
R
-subspace of
M
. By Corollary
5.5
, the
R
-space
Rx
is permissible and
\dim(Rx)\leq\dim(M)
. In particular,
\dim(Rx)\neq\dim(R)
. Suppose now, for a contradiction, that
x
has no non-zero annihilator. Then the map
R\to Rx
defined by
r\to rx
is an ismorphism of abelian groups. Moreover, from the defining property of being
R
-distinguished, we have
R/m^{n}\cong Rx/\mathfrak{m}^{n}(Rx)
for all integers
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. By Theorem
5.6
, we must have
\dim(R)=\dim(Rx)
, giving a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local-filtered ring with finite residue field and let
M
be a permissible
R
-space. Assume that
\text{gr}(R)
is an integral domain and that
M
is spanned by
\mathfrak{m}
-adically distinguished elements with non-zero annihilators. Then
\dim(M)\leq\dim(R)-1
.

Proof.

Suppose
x\in M
is
\mathfrak{m}
-adically distinguished and killed by a non-zero element
r
in
R
. Since
\text{gr}(Rx)=\text{gr}(R)\sigma(x)
(from Note
5.9
), we see that the principal part
\sigma(r)
is a non-trivial annihilator of the
\text{gr}(R)
-module
\text{gr}(Rx)
, and hence
\text{ht}(\text{Ann}\,\text{gr}(Rx))\geq 1
. Hence we obtain
\dim(Rx)\geq\dim(R)-1
from the height–dimension formula.

Using the strong Noetherian property if necessary, we can find finitely many
\mathfrak{m}
-adically distinguished elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
with non-zero annihilators such that
M=Rx_{1}+\dots+Rx_{n}
. It then suffices to show that

\dim(M)=\max\{\dim(Rx_{1}),\dots,\dim(Rx_{n})\}.

The desired equality follows from Corollary
5.5
and the following observations:

\bullet

Rx_{1},\dots,Rx_{n}
are
R
-subspace of
M
;

\bullet

\dim(Rx_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus Rx_{n})=\max\{\dim(Rx_{1}),\dots,\dim(Rx_{n})\}
;

\bullet

the map
Rx_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus Rx_{n}\to M
given by
(r_{1}x_{1},\dots,r_{n}x_{n})\to r_{1}x_{1}+\dots+r_{n}x_{n}
is a surjective morphism of
R
-spaces. ∎

6.
Central extensions and torsion elements in permissible spaces

Motivated by the theory of pseudo-null modules over Iwasawa algebras, we shall now consider implications of the preceding discussion for central extensions of complete local-filtered rings. The setting is follows. Given a central extension
R\subseteq S
of complete local-filtered rings (that is,
S
is topologically generated over
R
by central elements of
S
) and a space
M
over
S
, determine elements of
M
which are killed by
R
(under some assumptions on the
S
-space
M
, of course).

It will be convenient at this point to record an assumption which occurs as part of the hypotheses in a number of propositions that follow. But first, a definition. We say that an element
a\in R

operates centrally
on the
R
-space
M
if

(ar)m=a(rm)=r(am)=(ra)m

for all
r\in R
,
m\in M
.

Assumption 6.1
.

(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a complete local-filtered ring with finite residue field;
M
is an
R[[T]]
-space on which
T
operates centrally, and
M
is permissible as a space over
R
.

The central extensions that we consider will be spaces over formal power series rings, and they arise as follows. Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local-filtered ring with finite residue field, and let
M
be a permissible space over
R
. Suppose we are given a morphism
\phi:M\to M
of
R
-spaces such that the induced linear map
M/\mathfrak{m}M\to M/\mathfrak{m}M
is nilpotent. Now
R
is compact (as it is profinite), and consequently
M
is also compact. We can therefore extend the
R
-space structure on
M
to an
R[[T]]
-space structure by making
T
operate centrally on
M
via
\phi
. In subsequent subsections, we will show that the construction produces a permissible
R[[T]]
-space (Proposition
6.2
) and that its dimension is independent of the operator ring (Theorem
6.5
), and finally obtain an identification of classes of
R
-torsion elements (Theorem
6.7
).

6.1.
Central extensions and permissibility

In this subsection, we show that Assumption
6.1
already implies permissibility over the power series ring.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
and
M
be as in Assumption
6.1
. Then

M
is a permissible
R[[T]]
-space.

The proof of Proposition
6.2
requires the following two lemmas.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
M
be an
R
-space. Suppose
a\in R
is invertible, central, and operates centrally on
M
. Then the inverse of
a
also operates centrally on
M
.

Proof.

Let
b\in R
be the inverse of
a
: that is, we have
ab=ba=1
. Then
b
is also in the centre of
R
. It is easy to check that if
r\in R
and
m\in M
, then we have equalities

a((br)m)=a(b(rm))=a(r(bm))=a((rb)m)=rm.

The lemma then follows from the observation that map
M\to M
given by
m\to am
is injective.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
and
M
be as in Assumption
6.1
. Given a positive integer
j
, we can find a positive integer
k
such that
T^{k}M\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{j}M
.

Proof.

Since
T(\mathfrak{m}^{j}M)=\mathfrak{m}^{j}(TM)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{j}M
and
M/\mathfrak{m}^{j}M
is finite (by Proposition
5.2
), it is enough to show the following: if
v\in M
, then
T^{k}v\in\mathfrak{m}^{j}M
for some positive integer
k
.

So let us consider an element
v\in M
. Then we must have
T^{n+k}v\equiv T^{k}v\pmod{\mathfrak{m}^{j}M}
for some positive integers
k
and
n
. Thus
(1-T^{n})(T^{k}v)\in\mathfrak{m}^{j}M
. Since
1-T^{n}
is central, invertible and operates centrally, Lemma
6.3
implies that
T^{k}v\in\mathfrak{m}^{j}M
. ∎

Proof of Proposition
6.2
.

Let
X
be a central indeterminate, and consider the formal power series objects
R[[X]]
and
M[[X]]
with their canonical filtrations (see Example
3.6
). It is then easy to see that
R[[X]]
is complete local-filtered, and that
M[[X]]
is a permissible
R[[X]]
-space.

We view
M
as an
R[[X]]
-space by making
X
operate as
T
and consider the map
\theta:M[[X]]\to M
given by

\theta(m_{0}+m_{1}X+\dots)=m_{0}+Tm_{1}+\dots.

Since the topology on
M
is induced by the filtration
(\mathfrak{m}^{*}M)
, Lemma
6.4
implies that the map
\theta
is a well defined homomorphism of
R[[X]]
-spaces. Also given a positive integer
k
, we can find—by Theorem
4.7
and Lemma
6.4
—an integer
N
such that

F^{n}(M)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{k}M
and
T^{n}M\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{k}M
for all
n\geq N
. Thus if
i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
i+j\geq 2N
, then

\theta(F^{i}(M)X^{j})=T^{j}F^{i}(M)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{k}M.

Now each subgroup
\mathfrak{m}^{n}M
is topologically closed (because
M/\mathfrak{m}^{n}M
is finite), and the filtration on
M[[X]]
is given by
F^{n}(M[[X]])=\sum F^{i}(M)X^{j}
where the summation runs over non-negative integers
i,j
with
i+j\geq n
(see Example
3.6
). It follows that

\theta(F^{2N}(M[[X]]))=\sum_{i+j\geq 2N}T^{j}F^{i}(M)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{k}M,

and therefore the map
\theta:M[[X]]\to M
is continuous. Consequently, the
R[[T]]
-space
M
is permissible and
(\,\theta(F^{i}(M[[X]]))\mid i=0,1,\dots)
is a permissible filtration on
M
(see Note
5.4
). ∎

6.2.
Central extensions and invariance of dimension

Proposition
6.2
raises the possibility of two different ways of calculating the dimension of a permissible space, either over the base ring or the central extension. The following result asserts that both calculations will give the same dimension.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
and
M
be as in Assumption
6.1
. Then the dimension of
M
as an
R
-space is equal to the dimension of the
R[[T]]
-space
M
.

We will first proof Theorem
6.5
under assumptions on the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic filtration, and then derive the general case using the Artin–Rees lemma.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
and
M
be as in Assumption
6.1
. We also assume that
F^{i}(M)=\mathfrak{m}^{i}M
is a permissible filtration, and that
\mathfrak{m}^{i}(\mathfrak{m}^{j}M)=\mathfrak{m}^{i+j}M
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
Then the dimension of
M
as an
R
-space is equal to the dimension of the
R[[T]]
-space
M
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{n}
be the maximal ideal of
R[[T]]
. We write
d_{\mathfrak{m}}
and
d_{\mathfrak{n}}
for the dimension of
M
as an
R
-space and
R[[T]]
-space, respectively. By Theorem
5.6
, we can find constants
a,b\in\mathbb{Q}
such that

(20)

\log_{q}|M/\mathfrak{m}^{k}M|\sim ak^{d_{\mathfrak{m}}}\quad\text{and}\quad\log_{q}|M/\mathfrak{n}^{k}M|\sim bk^{d_{\mathfrak{n}}}.

Since
\mathfrak{m}^{k}\subseteq\mathfrak{n}^{k}
, we have
|M/\mathfrak{n}^{k}M|\leq|M/\mathfrak{m}^{k}M|
and so
d_{\mathfrak{n}}\leq d_{\mathfrak{m}}
.

For inequality in the other direction, we first fix a positive integer
N
such that
T^{N}M\subseteq\mathfrak{m}M
. This is possible by Lemma
6.4
. A straightforward inductive argument using Theorem
4.5
then shows that

(21)

T^{kN}M\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{k}M

for all positive integers
k
.

Now let
k
be a positive integer, and let
n
be a non-negative integer such that
n<kN
. We can then write
n=iN+j
where the integers
i,j
satisfy

0\leq i<k
and
0\leq j<N
. Using the inclusion (
21
) and Theorem
4.5
, we then have

(T^{n}\mathfrak{m}^{kN-n})M=\mathfrak{m}^{kN-n}(T^{n}M)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{kN-n}(T^{iN}M)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{kN-n}(\mathfrak{m}^{i}M)=\mathfrak{m}^{kN-n+i}M.

A stimple calculation shows that
kN-n+i\geq k
, and so
(T^{n}\mathfrak{m}^{kN-n})M\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{k}M
for
0\leq n<kN
. Since

\mathfrak{n}^{kN}=\mathfrak{m}^{kN}+\mathfrak{m}^{kN-1}T+\dots+\mathfrak{m}T^{kN-1}+T^{kN}R[[T]],

we obtain
\mathfrak{n}^{kN}M\subset\mathfrak{m}^{k}M
. So
\log_{q}|M/\mathfrak{m}^{k}M|\leq\log_{q}|M/\mathfrak{n}^{kN}M|
for all positive integers
k
. The displayed estimates (
20
) then imply that

ak^{d_{\mathfrak{m}}}\leq b(Nk)^{d_{\mathfrak{n}}}
for
k\gg 0
. Hence
d_{\mathfrak{m}}\leq d_{\mathfrak{n}}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.5
.

By Theorem
4.7
, there is a positive integer
D
such that
F^{n+d}(M)=F^{n}(R)F^{d}(M)
for all integers
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
d\geq D
. Using Theorem
4.5
, we see that
(\mathfrak{m}^{i}F^{D}(M)\mid i=0,1,\dots)
is a permissible filtration on
F^{D}(M)
and that

\mathfrak{m}^{i}(\mathfrak{m}^{j}F^{D}(M))=\mathfrak{m}^{i+j}F^{D}(M)

for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

Since
M
is a strongly Noetherian
R
-space, the ascending chain of
R
-subspaces

F^{D}(M)\subseteq F^{D}(M)+TF^{D}(M)\subseteq F^{D}(M)+TF^{D}(M)+T^{2}F^{D}(M)\subseteq\dots

stabilises, say from
N:=F^{D}(M)+\dots+T^{k}F^{D}(M)
onwards. Then, since
N
is a closed
R
-subspace of
M
and
TN\subseteq N
, Lemma
6.4
implies that
N
is an
R[[T]]
-space. Furthermore, as
T
operates centrally, the filtration

(\mathfrak{m}^{i}N\mid i=0,1,\dots)
is a permissible filtration on the
R
-space
N
and

\mathfrak{m}^{i}(\mathfrak{m}^{j}N)=\mathfrak{m}^{i+j}N
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Hence, by Proposition
6.6
, the dimension of the
R
-space
N
is equal to the dimension of the
R[[T]]
-space
N
.

Finally, since
M/N
has finite cardinality, the dimensions of
M
and
N
are equal as
R
-spaces, or as
R[[T]]
-spaces. Hence the dimension of
M
as an
R
-space is the same as the dimension of the
R[[T]]
-permissible space
M
.
∎

6.3.
Central extensions and torsion

Our aim is to relate presence of torsion in permissible spaces admitting a central extension to a property of associated gradations.
To this end, let
R
be a complete local-filtered ring, and define a
pseudo-null filtration
on an
R
-space
M
to be a permissible filtration on
M
such that the associated gradation is a pseudo-null
\text{gr}(R)
-module. We recall that for a commutative ring
A
, a
finitely generated
A
-module
N
is said to be
pseudo-null
if it satisfies one of the following equivalent conditions.

(PN1)

If
\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Spec}(A)
has height at most
1
, then the localisation
N_{\mathfrak{p}}=(0)
.

(PN2)

If
\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Spec}(A)
contains the annihilator of
N
, then
\text{ht}(\mathfrak{p})\geq 2
.

See, for instance,
[
13
, Definition 5.1.4 ]
. If
A
is an affine domain, then the pseudo-nullity of
N
is equivalent to the following condition:

(PN3)

\dim(A)-\dim(N)\geq 2
.

The equivalence follows from the height–dimension formula (Theroem
5.12
) and condition
(PN2)
.

Theorem 6.7
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
and
M
be as in Assumption
6.1
. Assume that
M
is spanned by
\mathfrak{m}
-adically distinguished elements as an
R
-space, and that
\text{gr}(R)
is an integral domain. Then the following statements are equivalent.

(T1)

Every
R
-distinguished element of
M
has a non-zero annihilator in
R
.

(T2)

The
R[[T]]
-space
M
has a pseudo-null filtration.

(T3)

All permissible filtrations on the
R[[T]]
-space
M
are pseudo-null filtrations.

Proof.

Note that
M
is a permissible
R[[T]]
-space by Proposition
6.2
. Since
the dimension of a permissible space is independent of the choice of permissible filtration (Proposition
5.3
), condition
(PN3)
implies the equivalences

\text{\ref{CET3}}\iff\text{\ref{CET4}}\iff\dim(R[[T]]-\dim(M)\geq 2.

Now, the dimension of
M
is independent of the operator ring (Theorem
6.5
). Also, since
\text{gr}(R[[T]])\cong\text{gr}(R)[T]
(see Example
3.6
, equation (
5
)), we have
\dim(R[[T]])=\dim(R)+1
. Thus

\dim(R[[T]]-\dim(M)\geq 2\iff\dim(R)-\dim(M)\geq 1.

The result now follows from Theorem
5.10
.
∎

Remark 6.8
.

In the classical setting of modules over commutative rings, Theorem
6.7
revisits the equivalence between pseudo-null
R[[T]]
-modules and torsion
R
-modules. The theory of pseudo-null modules in the general associative setting is due to Venjakob (
[
18
]
), and it can be shown that the existence of a pseudo-null filtration with certain regularity conditions imply pseudo-nullity of the module. It is then tempting to speculate on a structure theory for permissible spaces over complete local-filtered rings; that story though is outside the scope of this paper.

Abstract
. We compute the F F -pure threshold of some non-principal ideals which satisfy a geometric generic condition about their Newton polyhedron. We also contribute some evidence in favor of the conjectured equality between the F F -pure threshold and the log canonical threshold of ideals for infinitely many primes p p . These results are obtained by generalizing the theory of splitting polytopes to the case of ideals. As an application of our results we obtain geometric lower bounds for the recently introduced F F -volume of a collection of ideals.

1.
Introduction

The problem of quantifying singularities of an algebraic variety
X
at a given point
x
can be approached in several ways, but the first thing to consider is the characteristic of the ground field. Over fields of characteristic zero it is common to use the log canonical threshold of
X
at the point
x
,
{\operatorname{lct}}_{x}(X)
, which is a numerical invariant that can be defined analytically (via integration when available, e.g. over
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
) or algebro-geometrically (via resolution of singularities). Over fields of prime characteristic, the corresponding numerical invariant is called the
F
-pure threshold of
X
at the point
x
; it is denoted by
\fpt_{x}(X)
and its definition uses the Frobenius map. While defined in an entirely different way, it turns out that both invariants share several properties and moreover, if
X_{p}
denotes the reduction of the
variety
X
to characteristic
p
, it is known that
\fpt_{x}(X_{p})
approaches
{\operatorname{lct}}_{x}(X)
as long as
p
increases. Furthermore, there is a largely open conjecture asserting that
\fpt_{x}(X_{p})={\operatorname{lct}}_{x}(X)
for infinitely many primes
p
.

For definiteness, consider a polynomial ring
R
over a field
\mathbb{k}
of positive characteristic
p
. The
F
-pure threshold (at the origin) of an ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
is defined as the limit when
e\to\infty
of
p^{-e}(\max\{c\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid\mathfrak{a}^{c}\not\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}\})
. Here
\mathfrak{m}
denotes the homogeneous maximal ideal of
R
and
\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}
is the
p^{e}
-th Frobenius power of
\mathfrak{m}
. This definition was given by
Takagi and Watanabe
[
TW04
]
and since those days many properties and connections with other objects have been elucidated as it is summarized in the surveys
[
BFS13
,
TW18
]
and the references therein. However it is acknowledged by the community that the task of computing
\fpt_{x}(X)
is hard in general and few examples of explicit calculations are known, among them
[
ST09
,
Her14
,
MSV14
,
BS15
,
Her16
,
HNWZ16
,
Pag18
,
Mül18
,
Pag22
,
Tri22
,
GJN22
,
SV23
]
. In fact, the first computational routine for testing some examples was developed just a few years ago
[
HSTW21
]
.

Another serious difficulty that emerges when it comes to computing explicit examples of
F
-pure thresholds is that the existing methods are designed mostly for the case of principal ideals; the situation in this regard is similar to the one for test ideals
[
ST14
]
. To the best of our knowledge the more general results in this direction are due to Shibuta and Takagi
[
ST09
]
, who present several explicit calculations of

F
-pure thresholds of binomial ideals by using linear programming. The authors generalize there the notable case of monomial ideals that can be deduced from the work of Howald on multiplier ideals
[
How01
]
. In this work we attack the case of non-principal ideals by extending the techniques of splitting polytopes developed by Hernández
[
Her14
,
Her16
]
. The splitting polytope
\mathcal{P}_{f}
of a polynomial
f\in R
is a compact convex polytope that resembles the more classical notion of the Newton polyhedron. In fact there are several useful connections between these two objects, allowing one to formulate some algebro-geometric conditions of the hypersurface defined by
f
in terms of
\mathcal{P}_{f}
. By using the definition of the Newton polyhedron of a polynomial mapping
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})

[
VZ08
,
LVZ13
]
we define and study a splitting polytope
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
for an ideal
\mathfrak{a}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})
. The term ideal
\mathfrak{a}^{\circ}
of a non necessarilly principal ideal is a monomial ideal that has been used to study for example colength of ideals
[
BFS02
]
. In our first main result we determine some geometric conditions under which
\fpt(\mathfrak{a})
is equal to
\fpt(\mathfrak{a}^{\circ})
. In this way we provide an extensive list of new examples of
F
-pure thresholds for non-principal ideals that moreover can be computed explicitly, see Example
3.2
and Proposition
3.4
(2). Given a prime number
p
and
\rho\in(0,1]
,
\rho^{(e)}
denotes the
e
-th term in the
p
-expansion of
\rho
, while
\langle\rho\rangle_{e}
is the
e
-th truncation of such expansion. Moreover, for
\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{l}
, the symbol
|\rho|
denotes the sum of all the entries of
\rho
.

Theorem A

(Theorem
3.6
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
be a nonzero ideal with term ideal
\mathfrak{a}^{\circ}
, and take a list

of minimal generators of
\mathfrak{a}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
has a unique maximal point
\rho=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t})
with
\rho_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{l_{i}}
and set for

S_{i}=\sup\left\{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid\sum_{j=1}^{l_{i}}\rho_{i,j}^{(e)}\leq p-1\quad\text{for every}\quad 0\leq e\leq\ell\right\}.

(2)

On our second main result we contributed several cases in favor of a famous conjecture of Mustaţă, Tagaki, and Watanabe

2.
Splitting polytopes and Newton polyhedra of polynomial mappings

In this section we associate two polytopes to a given ideal
\mathfrak{a}
in a polynomial ring
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
, with
\mathbb{k}
an arbitrary field. One object is the usual Newton polytope of
\mathfrak{a}
, the other is the splitting polytope, see Definition
2.7
for the details. The key result of this section is Proposition
2.12
which allow us to control the coefficients of relevant monomials in precise powers of polynomials, by means of the splitting polytope. The terminology of
p
-expansions, that we describe next, will be useful in Section
3

Basics on

The following construction was introduced by Rényi in

under the more general definition of

Definition 2.1
.

Let

be an integer with

. Any real number

can be uniquely written as

(2.1.1)

\alpha={\sum}_{k\geq 1}\frac{\alpha^{(k)}}{p^{k}},

with

, and the further assumption that the sequence
\{\alpha^{(k)}\}_{k\geq 1}
does not eventually take only the value 0. We will say that the sequence
\{\alpha^{(k)}\}_{k\geq 1}
represents
\alpha
and call (
2.1.1
) the
p
-expansion of
\alpha
1

1

Note that the

-expansion of
\alpha
is different from its

-adic expansion, which is not defined for general real numbers.
.

The representation

is done through an infinite sequence which is obtained by using the ‘greedy’ algorithm of Rényi when
\alpha\neq\frac{a}{p^{e}}
, for an integer
a
. When
\alpha=\frac{a}{p^{e}}
we choose the representation with

in all the places after

. We denote by
\langle\alpha\rangle_{e}:=\sum_{k=1}^{e}\frac{\alpha^{(k)}}{p^{k}},
the

of

.

Definition 2.2
.

For
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
we define

as the sum
\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m}
of the entries of
\alpha
. When
\alpha\in[0,1]^{m}
we denote by
\langle\alpha\rangle_{e}
the vector

.

If moreover

is assumed to be a prime number, then the classical Lucas’s Lemma
[

]
asserts that
\binom{|r|}{r}\not\equiv 0\bmod p
if and only if the expansions in base

of the entries of

Corollary 2.3
.

The entries of a vector
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})\in[0,1]^{m}
add without carrying mod

if and only if, for every

\binom{p^{e}|\langle\alpha\rangle|}{p^{e}\langle\alpha\rangle_{e}}\not\equiv 0\bmod p.

Lemma 2.4

(
[

)

Consider a vector
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})
in

.

(1)

If

\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}

(2)

If

Example 2.5

If

2.2.
Splitting polytopes of polynomial mappings

Introduced first by Mustaţă, Takagi and Watanabe
[
MTW05
]
for the case of one polynomial, the splitting polytope has been studied further by Hernández
[
Her14
,
Her16
]
. Our construction for polynomial mappings follows closely the one given by Hernández.

Given
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, we denote by
\alpha\preceq\beta
the component-wise inequality, and denote by
\boldsymbol{1}_{m}
the vector
(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Let
f=\sum_{a\in{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}}}c_{a}x^{a}
be a non-constant polynomial in
R
. The support of
f
is the set

\operatorname{Supp}(f)=\{a\in{\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}}\mid c_{a}\neq 0\}.

If
\operatorname{Card}(\operatorname{Supp}(f))=n
, then the
m\times n
matrix
\mathcal{E}_{f}
having as columns the elements of
\operatorname{Supp}(f)
is called the
exponent matrix
of
f
.

Definition 2.6
.

The set
\mathcal{P}_{f}:=\{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{n}\mid\mathcal{E}_{f}\cdot\gamma\preceq\textbf{1}_{m}\}
, is called the
splitting polytope of

f
.

Consider now a finite list
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{t}\}
of polynomials in
R
belonging to
\mathfrak{m}
. In order to define a splitting polytope for the polynomial mapping
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})\,:\mathbb{k}^{m}\,\mapsto\mathbb{k}^{t}
we need some technical assumptions. For any
1\leq i\leq t
, assume that
f_{i}
has exactly

monomials, i.e.
\operatorname{Card}(\operatorname{Supp}(f_{i}))=n_{i}
, and

(2.2.1)

f_{i}=c_{a_{1}(i)}x^{a_{1}(i)}+\cdots+c_{a_{n_{i}}(i)}x^{a_{n_{i}}(i)}.

Now we set
\hat{f}_{1}=f_{1}
and define
\hat{f}_{2}
, as the polynomial obtained from

by removing the terms with monomials already appearing in
f_{1}
regardless of the corresponding coefficients. We proceed with this construction and define inductively
\hat{f}_{i}
, as the polynomial obtained from

by removing the terms with monomials already appearing in any of the polynomials

Definition 2.7
.

Given a polynomial mapping
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})
we set

(1)

An

(2)

The

\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}=\{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}\mid\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\cdot\gamma\preceq\textbf{1}_{m}\}.

Remark 2.10
.

Given a list of polynomials
\boldsymbol{f}
, one may be inclined to think that

, where

is the polynomial obtained by adding the different terms of each

For the following technical lemma we fix an integer

Lemma 2.11
.

Assume that
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
has a unique maximal point
\rho\in[0,1]^{N}
, which is written as
\rho=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t})
with
\rho_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{l_{i}}
, according to Definition
2.7
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

If for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}
,
|\gamma|=|\langle\rho\rangle_{e}|
and
\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\gamma=\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\langle\rho\rangle_{e}
, then
\gamma=\langle\rho\rangle_{e}
. Moreover, if
there exist vectors
\phi,\psi\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}
, with
\psi\preceq\langle\rho\rangle_{e}
and such that
|\phi|=|\psi|
and
\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\phi=\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\psi
; then
\phi=\psi
.

(2)

Let

be a fixed index in
\{1,\ldots,t\}
. If for some
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{l_{i}}
,
|\delta|=|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|
and
\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\delta=\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}
, then
\delta=\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}
. Moreover, if there exist vectors
\phi,\psi\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{l_{i}}
, with
\psi\preceq\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}
and such that

and
\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\phi=\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\psi

.

Proof.

The case of just one polynomial is stated and proved in
[
Her16
, Lemma 24]
. The proof given there can be easily extended to the present setting to justify the first part. Note, nevertheless, that a different argument is required to prove the second part, since our hypotheses do not imply in general that
\rho_{i}
is a maximal point of
\mathcal{P}_{\hat{f}_{i}}
(see Remark
2.13
).

So we start by considering the vector

\gamma=(\langle\rho_{1}\rangle_{e},\ldots,\langle\rho_{i-1}\rangle_{e},\delta,\langle\rho_{i+1}\rangle_{e},\ldots,\langle\rho_{t}\rangle_{e})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}.

It is clear that
|\gamma|=|\langle\rho\rangle_{e}|
, since
|\delta|=|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|
. From
\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\delta=\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}
follows

\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\gamma=\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{1}\langle\rho_{1}\rangle_{e}+\cdots+\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i-1}\langle\rho_{i-1}\rangle_{e}+\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\delta+\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i+1}\langle\rho_{i+1}\rangle_{e}+\cdots+\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{t}\langle\rho_{t}\rangle_{e}=\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\langle\rho\rangle_{e},

and the first part of the lemma gives
\gamma=\langle\rho\rangle_{e}
, hence
\delta=\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}
. For the second claim assume that there exist
\phi,\psi\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{l_{i}}
, with
\psi\preceq\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}
and such that
|\phi|=|\psi|
and
\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\phi=\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\psi
. Taking
\phi^{\prime}=\phi+\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}-\psi
gives
|\phi^{\prime}|=|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|
and
\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\phi^{\prime}=\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}
. The first claim of part (2) implies
\phi^{\prime}=\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}
, and consequently
\phi=\psi
.
∎

The following proposition provides an explicit formula for the coefficient of a relevant monomial in a polynomial
f
raised to the power
p^{e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|
. Here we assume that in
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})
, every member can be written as in (
2.2.1
), i.e.
\operatorname{Card}(\operatorname{Supp}(f_{i}))=n_{i}
.

Proposition 2.12
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
2.11
, the following statements hold.

(1)

The coefficient of the monomial
x^{p^{e}\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}}
in
f_{i}^{p^{e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|}
is
\binom{p^{e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|}{p^{e}\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}}c_{a(i)}^{b_{e}(i)}
, where
b_{e}(i)
is the vector in
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n_{i}}
having
p^{e}\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}
in the first
l_{i}
entries and
0
in the remaining ones. Moreover, the coefficient of the monomial
x^{p^{e}\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\langle\rho\rangle_{e}}
in the polynomial
g:=\prod_{i=1}^{t}f_{i}^{p^{e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|}
is
\prod_{i=1}^{t}\binom{p^{e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|}{p^{e}\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}}c_{a(i)}^{b_{e}(i)}
.

(2)

Let
v
be an element of
\frac{1}{p^{e}}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N}
, satisfying
v\preceq\langle\rho\rangle_{e}
and written in the form
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{t})
with
v_{i}\in\frac{1}{p^{e}}\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{l_{i}}
. Then, the coefficient of the monomial
x^{p^{e}\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}v_{i}}
in
f_{i}^{p^{e}|v_{i}|}
is
\binom{p^{e}|v_{i}|}{p^{e}v_{i}}c_{a(i)}^{d_{e}(i)}
, where
d_{e}(i)
is a vector of the form
p^{e}(v_{i},0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n_{i}}
. Moreover, the coefficient of the monomial
x^{p^{e}\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}v}
in the polynomial
g=\prod_{i=1}^{t}f_{i}^{p^{e}|v_{i}|}
is
\prod_{i=1}^{t}\binom{p^{e}|v_{i}|}{p^{e}v_{i}}c_{a(i)}^{d_{e}(i)}
.

Proof.

Consider a polynomial
h
with
\operatorname{Card}(\operatorname{Supp}(h))=n
and write
h=c_{a_{1}}x^{a_{1}}+\cdots+c_{a_{n}}x^{a_{n}}
. The multinomial theorem implies that for a positive integer
Q
,

(2.2.2)

h^{Q}=(c_{a_{1}}x^{a_{1}}+\cdots+c_{a_{n}}x^{a_{n}})^{Q}=\sum_{|k|=Q}\binom{Q}{k}c_{a}^{k}x^{\mathcal{E}k},

where we use the convention
\mathcal{E}:=\mathcal{E}_{h}
. We denote the set of indices in the sum (
2.2.2
) by
I=\{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}\ \text{;}\ |k|=Q\}
. Let
\widehat{I}=\widehat{I}(l)
denote the subset of
I
composed by vectors of the form
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{l},0,\ldots,0)
, for some
l\leq n
. Moreover, denote by
\hat{k}
the projection of
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
onto its first
l
entries. Then (
2.2.2
) becomes:

h^{Q}=\sum_{k\in\widehat{I}}\binom{Q}{k}c_{a}^{k}x^{\mathcal{E}k}+\sum_{k\in I\setminus\widehat{I}}\binom{Q}{k}c_{a}^{k}x^{\mathcal{E}k}=\sum_{k\in\widehat{I}}\binom{Q}{\hat{k}}c_{a}^{\hat{k}}x^{\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}\hat{k}}+\sum_{k\in I\setminus\widehat{I}}\binom{Q}{k}c_{a}^{k}x^{\mathcal{E}k},

where
\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}
is the matrix obtained from
\mathcal{E}
by deleting the last
n-l
columns. We will further split the first sum over
\widehat{I}
by fixing a vector
v\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{m}
, namely

h^{Q}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\widehat{I}\\
\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}\hat{k}=v\end{subarray}}\binom{Q}{\hat{k}}c_{a}^{\hat{k}}x^{\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}\hat{k}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\widehat{I}\\
\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}\hat{k}\neq v\end{subarray}}\binom{Q}{\hat{k}}c_{a}^{\hat{k}}x^{\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}\hat{k}}+\sum_{k\in I\setminus\widehat{I}}\binom{Q}{k}c_{a}^{k}x^{\mathcal{E}k}.

In order to show the first part of the Proposition, we fix
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
and use the previous formulation with
h=f_{i}
,
Q=p^{e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|
and
v=p^{e}\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}
, obtaining

\displaystyle f_{i}^{p^{e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|}

\displaystyle=\binom{p^{e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|}{p^{e}\langle\rho_{i,1}\rangle_{e},\ldots,p^{e}\langle\rho_{i,l_{i}}\rangle_{e}}c_{a}^{\hat{k}}x^{p^{e}\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\widehat{I}\\
\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\hat{k}\neq p^{e}\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}\end{subarray}}\binom{p^{e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|}{\hat{k}}c_{a}^{\hat{k}}x^{\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\hat{k}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\in I\setminus\widehat{I}}\binom{p^{e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|}{k}c_{a}^{k}x^{\mathcal{E}_{f_{i}}k}.

The first term in the right hand side contains just one summand due to Lemma
2.11
(2) with
\delta=\frac{1}{p^{e}}\hat{k}
. The first part will be proved once we show that the sum over
I\setminus\widehat{I}
does not contribute to the monomial
x^{p^{e}\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}}
. Assume otherwise that there exists
k\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{i}}
verifying
p^{e}k\in I\setminus\widehat{I}
and
\mathcal{E}_{f_{i}}k=\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}
. Suppose without loss of generality that
k=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{l_{i}},k_{l_{i+1}},0,\ldots,0)
with
k_{l_{i+1}}\neq 0
. Hence,
\mathcal{E}_{f_{i}}k=\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\hat{k}+\mathcal{E}^{\prime}k_{l_{i+1}}
, where
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is a column matrix. In fact, due to the construction of
\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
,
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is a column in the exponent matrix of some
\{f_{1},\ldots,f_{i-1}\}
, so we will safely assume that
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is the first column of
\mathcal{E}_{f_{1}}=\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{1}
. Consider now the following vector

\gamma=(\langle\rho_{1}\rangle_{e}+(k_{l_{i+1}},\overbrace{0,\ldots,0}^{l_{1}-1}),\langle\rho_{2}\rangle_{e},\ldots,\langle\rho_{i-1}\rangle_{e},\hat{k},\langle\rho_{i+1}\rangle_{e},\ldots,\langle\rho_{t}\rangle_{e})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}.

Clearly
|\gamma|=|\langle\rho\rangle_{e}|
, and since
\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}=\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\hat{k}+\mathcal{E}^{\prime}k_{l_{i+1}}
, we get

\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\gamma=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}^{t}\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{j}\langle\rho_{j}\rangle_{e}+\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\hat{k}+\mathcal{E}^{\prime}k_{l_{i+1}}=\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\langle\rho\rangle_{e}.

But then the first part of Lemma
2.11
implies
\gamma=\langle\rho\rangle_{e}
, in particular
|\hat{k}|=|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|
, which contradicts the fact that
|k|=|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|
, since
k_{l_{i+1}}\neq 0
.

The proof for the coefficient of
g:=\prod_{i=1}^{t}f_{i}^{p^{e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|}
now follows from a straightforward calculation and again the first part of Lemma
2.11
. Finally, similar considerations lead to the proof of the second part.
∎

2.3.
Newton polyhedra of polynomial mappings

Our definition of
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
was inspired by the construction of the Newton polyhedron
\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{f}}=\mathcal{N}_{(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})}
of a polynomial mapping
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})\,:\mathbb{k}^{m}\,\mapsto\mathbb{k}^{t}
that we address next assuming the notations of Section
2.2
.

Definition 2.14
.

Let
A
be a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}
. The Newton polyhedron

\mathcal{N}_{A}
associated to
A
is the convex hull in

\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{m}
of the set

\bigcup_{a\in A}\left(a+\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{m}\right).

Classically one associates a Newton polyhedron
\mathcal{N}_{f}
to a polynomial
f=\sum_{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{m}}c_{a}x^{a}
in the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
by setting
A=\operatorname{Supp}(f)
. There are two customary notions of ‘genericity’ for a fixed
\mathcal{N}_{f}
. We say that
f
is
non degenerated with respect to

\mathcal{N}_{f}
if for any compact face
\eta
of
\mathcal{N}_{f}
the system of equations

(2.3.1)

f_{\eta}=\partial f_{\eta}/\partial x_{1}=\cdots=\partial f_{\eta}/\partial x_{m}=0

has no solution in
(\mathbb{k}^{\times})^{m}
, where
f_{\eta}
denotes the polynomial obtained from
f
by restricting the support to
\eta
. For the second genericity condition,
\mathcal{N}_{f}
is called
convenient
when the support of
f
contains a point in every coordinate axis. A third condition is given by Hernández
[
Her16
, Definition 29]
where he says that
\mathcal{N}_{f}
is in
diagonal position
if the ray spanned by the vector
\boldsymbol{1}_{m}
intersects a compact face of
\mathcal{N}_{f}
. It is elementary to show, following Kouchnirenko’s ideas
[
Kou76
]
, that this condition is also generic (in the sense of Kouchnirenko). Note moreover that every convenient
\mathcal{N}_{f}
will be automatically in diagonal position.

Consider now a polynomial mapping
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})\,:\mathbb{k}^{m}\,\mapsto\mathbb{k}^{t}
. We denote by
\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
the Newton polyhedron of
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})
, which is given by using Definition
2.14
with
A=\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}\operatorname{Supp}(f_{i})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}\operatorname{Supp}(\hat{f}_{i})
. Moreover we consider the ideal
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}_{\boldsymbol{f}}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})
generated by the elements of the polynomial mapping
\boldsymbol{f}
and define
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{a}}
as the Newton polyhedron of the set
A=\bigcup_{f\in\mathfrak{a}}\operatorname{Supp}(f)
. Obviously
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
, which in particular shows that
\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
depends only on
\mathfrak{a}
and not on a system of generators, see
[
CNV15
, Section 2.1]
. Discussions, comparisons and examples of these definitions (and the corresponding non degeneracy conditions that they imply) are given in
[
BFS02
,
Tei04
,
VZ08
,
LVZ13
,
Ros15
,
Ste21
]
.

Some relations between
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
for principal ideals have been treated by Hernández in
[
Her16
, Section 4.4]
. It is not difficult to adapt those ideas for non-principal ideals as we discuss in the remainder of this Section.

Proposition 2.15
.

The following equality holds

\left\{|\gamma|\mid\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\setminus\{(0,\ldots,0)\}\right\}=\left\{\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\mid(1/\tau,\ldots,1/\tau)\in\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\}.

In particular,
\max\left\{|\gamma|\mid\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\setminus\{(0,\ldots,0)\}\right\}=\max\left\{\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\mid(1/\tau,\ldots,1/\tau)\in\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\right\}.

Proof.

For
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\setminus\{(0,\ldots,0)\}
we have
\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\cdot\gamma\preceq\textbf{1}_{m}
and then we choose
\delta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{m}
such that
\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\gamma+\delta=\textbf{1}_{m}
. By writing
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{l_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{l_{t}}
, we get

\textbf{1}_{m}=\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{1}\gamma_{1}+\cdots+\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{t}\gamma_{t}+\delta=\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{l_{1}}\gamma_{1,j_{1}}a_{1,j_{1}}+\cdots+\sum_{j_{t}=1}^{l_{t}}\gamma_{t,j_{t}}a_{t,j_{t}}+\delta.

Setting
\tau=|\gamma|>0
yields
\frac{1}{\tau}\textbf{1}_{m}=(1/\tau,\ldots,1/\tau)=\sum_{i=1}^{t}\sum_{j_{i}=1}^{l_{i}}\frac{\gamma_{i,j_{i}}}{\tau}a_{i,j_{i}}+\frac{1}{\tau}\delta
, but
a_{i,j_{i}}\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}\operatorname{Supp}(f_{i})
and
\sum_{i=1}^{t}\sum_{j_{i}=1}^{l_{i}}\frac{\gamma_{i,j_{i}}}{\tau}=\frac{|\gamma|}{\tau}=1
, which forces
(1/\tau,\ldots,1/\tau)\in\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
. The other inclusion follows by similar considerations.
∎

Under the assumption that
\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
is in diagonal position, Proposition
2.15
offers a very useful tool for determining the maximal face of the splitting polytope
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
.

Proposition 2.16
.

Assume that
\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
is in diagonal position and let
\eta
be the face of
\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
that is intersected by the ray spanned by
\textbf{1}_{m}
. Then the maximal face of
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
is given by

\{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{N}\mid\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\cdot\gamma=\textbf{1}_{m}\;\text{and}\;\gamma_{j}=0\;\text{if the j-th column of }\;\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\;\text{does not belongs to}\;\eta\}.

Proof.

The proof is an easy variation of the one given for the case of a principal ideal
[
Her16
, Lemma 32]
.
∎

Example 2.17
.

Let
\boldsymbol{f}
be the polynomial mapping given by
(y^{5}(x+y^{m})^{4},x^{n})
with
m\geq 1
and
n>4+5/m
. Then

\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}=\begin{pmatrix}4&3&2&1&0&n\\
5&m+5&2m+5&3m+5&4m+5&0\end{pmatrix},

and
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}=\{(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3},\gamma_{4},\gamma_{5},\gamma_{6})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{6}\mid 4\gamma_{1}+3\gamma_{2}+2\gamma_{3}+\gamma_{4}+n\gamma_{6}\leq 1,\text{ and }5\gamma_{1}+(m+5)\gamma_{2}+(2m+5)\gamma_{3}+(3m+5)\gamma_{4}+(4m+5)\gamma_{5}\leq 1\}
. The ray spanned by the vector

intersects
\mathcal{N}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
in the face formed by the line segment joining
(4,5)
and

gives that
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
has a unique maximal point with equations

.

3.
F
-pure thresholds of non-principal ideals

In this section we present two of the main results of this work Theorems
3.6
and
3.12
. A relevant role is played here by the term ideal of a given ideal
\mathfrak{a}
which is presented in Definition
3.3
. Then we discuss the relations between the log canonical threshold and the
F
-pure threshold of
\mathfrak{a}
. Several examples are presented to illustrate the scope of the discussed techniques.

3.1.
F
-pure thresholds and term ideals

Take a polynomial ring
R
over a field
\mathbb{k}
of prime characteristic
p
, and set
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
for the homogeneous maximal ideal of
R
. For
e\geq 1
we denote by
\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}
the
p^{e}
-th Frobenius power of
\mathfrak{m}
, i.e. the ideal
(x_{1}^{p^{e}},\ldots,x_{m}^{p^{e}})
.

Definition 3.1
.

For a nonzero ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
, the following integer is well defined

\nu_{\mathfrak{a}}(p^{e})=\max\{c\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid\mathfrak{a}^{c}\not\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}\}.

We also define the
F
-pure threshold of
\mathfrak{a}
(at the origin) by

\fpt(\mathfrak{a})=\lim\limits_{e\rightarrow\infty}{\frac{\nu_{\mathfrak{a}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}}.

The existence of the previous limit is proved in
[
MTW05
]
, while the non obvious fact that
\fpt(\mathfrak{a})
is a rational number is proved in
[
BMS08
]
.

Example 3.2
.

It is not difficult to show that if
\mathfrak{a}
defines a non singular variety of codimension
r
, then
\fpt(\mathfrak{a})=r
. Also it is known
[
How01
,
HY03
]
that for a monomial ideal
\mathfrak{a}
its
F
-pure threshold is given by the formula

\fpt(\mathfrak{a})=\max\{\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mid\boldsymbol{1}_{m}\in\tau\cdot\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{a}}\}.

This is exactly the same number obtained by computing the log canonical threshold of the monomial ideal (in characteristic zero, lets say over
\mathbb{Q}
) generated by the monomials in
\mathfrak{a}
. The equality is a consequence of the stronger fact that in the monomial case, the multiplier and test ideals of
\mathfrak{a}
coincide.

Definition 3.3

(
[
BFS02
]
)
.

For a nonzero ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
, the term ideal
\mathfrak{a}^{\circ}
of
\mathfrak{a}
is the ideal generated by all the monomials
x^{a}
with
a\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{a}}
.

Take a non necessarily principal ideal
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
, and consider the polynomial mapping
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})
formed by a list of minimal generators of
\mathfrak{a}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

The following properties hold.

(1)

\fpt(\mathfrak{a})\leq\min\{t,\fpt(\mathfrak{a}^{\circ})\}
.

(2)

\fpt(\mathfrak{a}^{\circ})=\max_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}}|\gamma|.

Proof.

The first property is well known, see e.g.
[
MTW05
]
. The second property follows from the discussion in Example
3.2
and Proposition
2.15
, alternatively one may adapt the proof for a principal ideal
[
Her16
, Proposition
36
]
.
∎

Our next goal is to make explicit the conditions required to get
\fpt(\mathfrak{a})=\fpt(\mathfrak{a}^{\circ})
. Our formulation extends previous results only available for principal ideals
[
Her16
, Theorem 42]
and
[
Her14
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Definition 3.5
.

Given a vector
\rho=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t})
with
\rho_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{l_{i}}
, we set for
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}

S_{i}=\sup\left\{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid\sum_{j=1}^{l_{i}}\rho_{i,j}^{(e)}\leq p-1\quad\text{for every}\quad 0\leq e\leq\ell\right\}.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
be a nonzero ideal with term ideal
\mathfrak{a}^{\circ}
, and take a list
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})
of minimal generators of
\mathfrak{a}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
has a unique maximal point
\rho=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t})
with
\rho_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{l_{i}}
.

If
I
denotes the set of indices for which
S_{i}
is finite, then the following assertions hold.

(1)

If
I=\emptyset
then
\fpt(\mathfrak{a})=\fpt(\mathfrak{a}^{\circ})
.

(2)

If
I\neq\emptyset
then

\fpt(\mathfrak{a})\geq\sum_{i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}\setminus I}|\rho_{i}|+\sum_{i\in I}\left(|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{S_{i}}|+\frac{1}{p^{S_{i}}}\right).

Proof.

For the case
I=\emptyset
, it will be enough to show that
|\rho|\leq\fpt(\mathfrak{a})
, according to Proposition
3.4
. Our first goal is to find an exponent
Q(e)
such that
\mathfrak{a}^{Q(e)}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}
. In order to check that this exponent is
p^{e}|\langle\rho\rangle_{e}|
we start by recalling from Proposition
2.12
(1) that

(3.1.1)

\prod_{i=1}^{t}\binom{p^{e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|}{p^{e}\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}}c_{a(i)}^{b_{e}(i)}x^{p^{e}\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\langle\rho\rangle_{e}}

appears as a summand of the polynomial
g=\prod_{i=1}^{t}f_{i}^{p^{e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|}
. Since
I=\emptyset
, Corollary
2.3
implies
\binom{p^{e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|}{p^{e}\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}}\not\equiv 0
mod
p
for each
\rho_{i}
, making the coefficient of (
3.1.1
) not zero and thus
p^{e}\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\langle\rho\rangle_{e}\in\operatorname{Supp}(g)
. However, from
\langle\rho\rangle_{e}\prec\rho
we have

p^{e}\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\langle\rho\rangle_{e}\prec p^{e}\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\rho\preceq p^{e}\textbf{1}_{m},

meaning that
x^{p^{e}\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\langle\rho\rangle_{e}}
does not belong to

\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}
. In turn this implies that
g\notin\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}
and finally
\mathfrak{a}^{p^{e}|\langle\rho\rangle_{e}|}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}
because
g
was already a member of
\mathfrak{a}^{p^{e}|\langle\rho\rangle_{e}|}
. We conclude that
|\langle\rho\rangle_{e}|\leq\frac{\nu_{\mathfrak{a}}(p^{e})}{p^{e}}
, which take us to
|\rho|\leq\fpt(\mathfrak{a})
by taking the limit as
e\to\infty
.

We now address the second part of the proof. We assume without lost of generality that
I=\{1,\ldots,r\}
and define
S=\max_{1\leq i\leq r}S_{i}
.
Our strategy here is to find again a suitable exponent
Q(e)
verifying
\mathfrak{a}^{Q(e)}\nsubseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}
, but in this case the construction of the corresponding monomial is more involved. Note first that the definition of
S_{i}
gives

\begin{matrix}\rho_{1,1}^{(S_{1}+1)}+\cdots+\rho_{1,l_{1}}^{(S_{1}+1)}\geq p\\
\rho_{2,1}^{(S_{2}+1)}+\cdots+\rho_{2,l_{2}}^{(S_{2}+1)}\geq p\\
\vdots\\
\rho_{r,1}^{(S_{r}+1)}+\cdots+\rho_{r,l_{r}}^{(S_{r}+1)}\geq p.\end{matrix}

There should exist then non negative integers
n_{1,1},\ldots,n_{1,l_{1}},

n_{2,1},\ldots,n_{2,l_{2}},

\ldots,n_{r,1},\ldots,n_{r,l_{r}}
such that

\begin{matrix}n_{1,1}+\cdots+n_{1,l_{1}}=p-1&\mathrm{and}&0\leq n_{1,j_{1}}\leq\rho_{1,j_{1}}^{(S_{1}+1)}\\
n_{2,1}+\cdots+n_{2,l_{2}}=p-1&\mathrm{and}&0\leq n_{2,j_{2}}\leq\rho_{2,j_{2}}^{(S_{2}+1)}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
n_{r,1}+\cdots+n_{r,l_{r}}=p-1&\mathrm{and}&0\leq n_{r,j_{r}}\leq\rho_{r,j_{r}}^{(S_{r}+1)},\end{matrix}

with at least one index in each row verifying the strict inequality, say
n_{i,1}<\rho_{i,1}^{(S_{i}+1)}
.
For
e
big enough we define the vectors,

\begin{cases}v_{1}=\langle\rho_{1}\rangle_{S_{1}}+\left(\frac{n_{1,1}}{p^{S_{1}+1}}+\frac{p-1}{p^{S_{1}+2}}+\cdots+\frac{p-1}{p^{S_{1}+e}},\frac{n_{1,2}}{p^{S_{1}+1}},\ldots,\frac{n_{1,l_{1}}}{p^{S_{1}+1}}\right)\\
v_{2}=\langle\rho_{2}\rangle_{S_{2}}+\left(\frac{n_{2,1}}{p^{S_{2}+1}}+\frac{p-1}{p^{S_{2}+2}}+\cdots+\frac{p-1}{p^{S_{2}+e}},\frac{n_{2,2}}{p^{S_{2}+1}},\ldots,\frac{n_{2,l_{2}}}{p^{S_{2}+1}}\right)\\
\vdots\\
v_{r}=\langle\rho_{r}\rangle_{S_{r}}+\left(\frac{n_{r,1}}{p^{S_{r}+1}}+\frac{p-1}{p^{S_{r}+2}}+\cdots+\frac{p-1}{p^{S_{r}+e}},\frac{n_{r,2}}{p^{S_{r}+1}},\ldots,\frac{n_{r,l_{r}}}{p^{S_{r}+1}}\right).\end{cases}

In this way
v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{r})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{r}}
verifies
p^{S+e}v\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{r}}
and moreover

\displaystyle|v|

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{S_{i}}|+\frac{p^{e}-1}{p^{S_{i}+e}}\right).

Observe also that for
i\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
,
v_{i}\preceq\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{S_{i}+e}\preceq\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{S+e}
and by Proposition
2.12
(2)

\prod_{i=1}^{r}\binom{p^{S+e}|v_{i}|}{p^{S+e}v_{i}}c_{a(i)}^{d_{e}(i)}\cdot\prod_{i=r+1}^{t}\binom{p^{S+e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{S+e}|}{p^{S+e}\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{S+e}}c_{a(i)}^{b_{e}(i)}

appears as the coefficient of the monomial

(x^{p^{S+e}\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{1}v_{1}}\cdots x^{p^{S+e}\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{r}v_{r}})\cdot(x^{p^{S+e}\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{r+1}\langle\rho_{r+1}\rangle_{S+e}}\cdots x^{p^{S+e}\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{t}\langle\rho_{t}\rangle_{S+e}})

in the polynomial
g
given by the product

\prod_{i=1}^{r}f_{i}^{p^{S+e}|v_{i}|}\cdot\prod_{i=r+1}^{t}f_{i}^{p^{S+e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{S+e}|}
. By the definitions of
S
and
S_{i}
, we conclude from Lucas’s Lemma that
\binom{p^{S+e}|v_{i}|}{p^{S+e}v_{i}}\not\equiv 0
mod
p
for each
v_{i}
. Since
S_{i}=\infty
,
for
i\in\{r+1,\ldots,t\}
, then
\binom{p^{S+e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{S+e}|}{p^{S+e}\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{S+e}}\not\equiv 0
mod
p
. In conclusion one can set
Q(e)=p^{S+e}|v|+p^{S+e}|\langle(\rho_{r+1},\ldots,\rho_{t})\rangle_{S+e}|
to verify that

g\in\mathfrak{a}^{Q(e)}
but
g\notin\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{S+e}]}
. In this way
Q(e)\leq\nu_{\mathfrak{a}}(p^{S+e})
. Dividing by
p^{S+e}
and letting
e\rightarrow\infty
yields finally

\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{S_{i}}|+\frac{1}{p^{S_{i}}}\right)+\sum_{i=r+1}^{t}|\rho_{i}|\leq\fpt(\mathfrak{a}).

∎

Example 3.7
.

Take
R=\mathbb{F}_{p}[x,y,z]
and let
\mathfrak{a}
be the ideal generated by
\boldsymbol{f}=(x^{2}+xy^{2},yz^{3})
. Then
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
is the splitting polytope of Example
2.9
with
a=b=2
, and
c=3
which has a unique maximal point
\rho=\left(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3}\right)
.
For
p=2
,

\frac{1}{3}=\sum_{e\geq 1}\frac{\alpha^{(e)}}{2^{e}},\quad\text{where }\,\alpha^{(e)}=0\,\text{ for }\,e\text{ odd and }\,\alpha^{(e)}=1\text{ for even }e,

thus
S_{1}=1,\;S_{2}=\infty
and
\langle\rho_{1}\rangle_{1}=(0,0)
. By Theorem
3.6
(2)
\fpt(\mathfrak{a})\geq|\langle\rho_{1}\rangle_{1}|+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{3}=\frac{5}{6}
.
If
p=3
, then
\frac{1}{3}=\sum_{e\geq 2}\frac{2}{3^{e}}
, again
S_{1}=1,\;S_{2}=\infty
and
\fpt(\mathfrak{a})\geq\frac{2}{3}
.

Assume now that
p=6w+1
for some
w\geq 1
, then
\frac{1}{3}=\sum_{e\geq 1}\frac{2w}{p^{e}}
and
S_{1}=S_{2}=\infty
, hence
\fpt(\mathfrak{a})=|\rho|=1
by Theorem
3.6
(1). Finally if
p=6w+5
for some
w\geq 0
, then
\frac{1}{3}=\sum_{e\geq 1}\frac{\alpha^{(e)}}{p^{e}}
, where
\alpha^{(e)}=2w+1
for
e
odd and
\alpha^{(e)}=4w+3
for
e
even. In this case
S_{1}=1,\;S_{2}=\infty
and
\langle\rho_{1}\rangle_{1}=\left(\frac{2w+1}{p},\frac{2w+1}{p}\right)
, giving
\fpt(\mathfrak{a})\geq\frac{4w+2}{p}+\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{3}=1-\frac{1}{3p}
. Summarizing,

\begin{cases}\fpt(\mathfrak{a})\geq\frac{5}{6}&\text{if}\quad p=2\\
\fpt(\mathfrak{a})\geq\frac{2}{3}&\text{if}\quad p=3\\
\fpt(\mathfrak{a})=1&\text{if}\quad p\equiv 1\mod 6\\
\fpt(\mathfrak{a})\geq 1-\frac{1}{3p}&\text{if}\quad p\equiv 5\mod 6.\end{cases}

2.17

\boldsymbol{f}=(y^{5}(x+y^{m})^{4},x^{n}),\quad\text{with}\quad m\geq 1\quad\text{and}\quad n>4+5/m.

. It is not difficult to show that for any prime

3.2.
Relations with the log canonical threshold

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal in
\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
and consider a log resolution of
\mathfrak{a}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. We mean by this, a proper birational map
\pi:Y\to\mathbb{A}^{m}
, with
Y
smooth, and such that
\mathfrak{a}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{Y}=\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-D)
is invertible and
\operatorname{Exc}(\pi)\cup\operatorname{Supp}(D)
is a simple normal crossing divisor. Denote by
K_{\pi}
the relative canonical divisor on
Y
and set
D
for the effective divisor given by
\pi^{-1}(\mathfrak{a})
, then the
log canonical threshold of

\mathfrak{a}
(at the origin) is given by

{\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a})=\sup\{\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\mid\lceil K_{\pi}-\alpha D\rceil\,\text{ is effective}\},

where
\lceil H\rceil
denotes the round up divisor of
H
. Given a prime number
p
, one may consider the reduction
\mathfrak{a}_{p}=\mathfrak{a}\cdot\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]_{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})}
of
\mathfrak{a}
mod
p
, and then it is very natural to ask about the relationship among the corresponding thresholds. Following Hara and Yosida
[
HY03
]
, Mustaţǎ, Tagaki, and Watanabe
[
MTW05
, Theorem 3.3]
observe that for
p
large enough, one has

(3.2.1)

\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{p})\leq{\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a})\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{p\to\infty}\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{p})={\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a}).

The authors also posed the following conjecture which still represents an interesting open challenge. For historical comments and some references of the proven cases consult the survey
[
BFS13
]
.

Conjecture 3.9

(
[
MTW05
, Conjecture 3.6]
)
.

There are infinitely many primes
p
for which
\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{p})={\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a})
.

Example 3.10
.

Following Example
3.7
, let
\mathfrak{a}
be the ideal generated by
\boldsymbol{f}=(x^{2}+xy^{2},yz^{3})
. It is not difficult to show that
{\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a})=1
which coincides with

p

Notation 3.11
.

(1)

For a given ideal
\mathfrak{a}
, we denote by
P_{\mathfrak{a}}
the set of prime numbers for which
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{a}_{p}}
.

(2)

For a given vector
\rho=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
having at least one index
i
with
|\rho_{i}|>1
, assume (reordering if necessary) that for
k\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
,
|\rho_{k}|>1
. Denote by
P_{\rho}^{0}
the set of prime numbers satisfying the condition

(3.2.2)

\rho_{k,1}^{(1)}+\cdots+\rho_{k,l_{k}}^{(1)}\geq p

for every
k\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, c.f. Lemma
2.4
.

(3)

For
\rho=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
we denote by
P_{\rho}^{\infty}
the set of prime numbers satisfying the condition that
\rho_{k}
adds without carrying for every
k\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
.

Note that condition
3.2.2
holds for a sufficiently large prime number
p
, more precisely it holds for
p\geq\frac{l_{k}}{|\rho_{k}|-1}
.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}
be a nonzero ideal with term ideal
\mathfrak{a}^{\circ}
, and take a list
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})
of minimal generators of
\mathfrak{a}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
has a unique maximal point
\rho=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t})
with
\rho_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{l_{i}}
.

(1)

Assume that
\rho
verifies the following property: whenever there is one
k_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
with
|\rho_{k_{0}}|>1
, then
|\rho_{k}|>1
for every
k\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
. If
{\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a}^{\circ})>t
, then
\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{p})={\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a})=t
for big enough primes in
P_{\rho}^{0}\cap P_{\mathfrak{a}}
.

(2)

Assume that
\rho
verifies the following property: whenever there is one
k_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
with
|\rho_{k_{0}}|\leq 1
, then
|\rho_{k}|\leq 1
for every
k\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
. Assume moreover that
P_{\rho}^{\infty}
is an infinite set. If
{\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a}^{\circ})\leq t
, then
\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{p})={\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a})
for big enough primes in
P_{\rho}^{\infty}\cap P_{\mathfrak{a}}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{a}_{p}^{\circ}
is a monomial ideal it follows from Proposition
3.4
(2) and Example
3.2
that

|\rho|=\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{p}^{\circ})={\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a}_{p}^{\circ})
. To prove the first part of the theorem, note that for
p\in P_{\mathfrak{a}}
, the equality
\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathcal{N}_{\mathfrak{a}_{p}}
guarantees that the log canonical threshold of the corresponding term ideals are equal, yielding

(3.2.3)

|\rho|=\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{p}^{\circ})={\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a}_{p}^{\circ})={\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a}^{\circ}).

From the hypothesis
{\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a}^{\circ})>t
, there should be at least one
k_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
with
|\rho_{k_{0}}|>1
, which also implies that
|\rho_{k}|>1
for every
k\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
. Now we use Lemma
2.4
(2) to conclude that
\rho_{k,1}^{(1)}+\cdots+\rho_{k,l_{k}}^{(1)}\geq p
, for
p\in P_{\rho}^{0}\cap P_{\mathfrak{a}}
, meaning that
S_{k}=0
(recall Definition
3.5
). Summarizing, we have shown that
S_{1}=\cdots=S_{t}=0
, and then Theorem
3.6
(2) gives

t\leq\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{p}).

In fact this inequality is an equality thanks to property
\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{p})\leq t
from Proposition
3.4
(1). Finally note that for
p
big enough in
P_{\rho}^{0}\cap P_{\mathfrak{a}}
we have, see (
3.2.1
),

t=\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{p})\leq{\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a}).

The conclusion then follows from the well known inequality
{\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a})\leq t
.

For the second part of the theorem we observe that
{\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a}^{\circ})\leq t
, implies that
|\rho_{1}|+\cdots+|\rho_{t}|\leq t
. Then there should be an index
k_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
for which
|\rho_{k_{0}}|\leq 1
, which in turn implies
|\rho_{k}|\leq 1
for every
k\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
. Lemma
2.4
(1) implies that
\rho_{k}
adds without carrying for infinitely many
p
. Now we use the fact that

P_{\rho}^{\infty}
is an infinite set, in particular this is saying that the intersection of the sets of primes in which each
\rho_{k}
adds without carrying is non-empty and infinite. We conclude that
S_{1}=\cdots=S_{t}=\infty
for any prime in
P_{\rho}^{\infty}
. This time we use Theorem
3.6
(1) to conclude
\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{p})=\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{p}^{\circ})
. Note that for
p
big enough in
P_{\rho}^{\infty}\cap P_{\mathfrak{a}}
we have
\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{p})\leq{\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a})
from the left hand side of (
3.2.1
), implying

\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{p})\leq{\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a})\leq{\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a}^{\circ}).

Finally we may complete the chain of inequalities as

\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{p}^{\circ})=\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{p})\leq{\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a})\leq{\operatorname{lct}}(\mathfrak{a}^{\circ})=\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{p}^{\circ}),

finishing this way the proof.
∎

4.
Mixed Generalized Test Ideals and
F
-Volumes

In this section we present a final application of our construction of the splitting polytope of a polynomial mapping. We first introduce the
definition of the
F
-volume of a collection of ideals
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{t}
and some of its properties. Then we use the proof of Theorem
3.6
for the construction of a lower bound for this volume.

Test ideals were introduced by Hochster and Huneke
[
HH90
,
HH94
]
as the characteristic
p
analogues of multiplier ideals. We follow the description of Bickle, Mustaţă, and Smith
[
BMS08
]
and consider
R
a polynomial ring over a field
\mathbb{k}
of prime characteristic
p
. Assume further that
R
is
F
-finite, meaning that the Frobenius map is finite.

Definition 4.1
.

(1)

Given an ideal
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq R
, the
p^{e}
-th
Frobenius root
of
\mathfrak{b}
, denoted
\mathfrak{b}^{[1/p^{e}]}
, is the smallest ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
such that
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}^{[p^{e}]}
.

(2)

Given a collection
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{t}
of ideals in
R
and
c=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{t}_{\geq 0}
, the
mixed generalized test ideal
with exponents
c_{1},\ldots,c_{t}
is defined as

\tau(\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{t}^{c_{t}})=\bigcup_{e>0}(\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{\lceil c_{1}p^{e}\rceil}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{t}^{\lceil c_{t}p^{e}\rceil})^{[1/p^{e}]}.

Since
R
is Noetherian, the ascending family of ideals
\left\{(\mathfrak{a_{1}}^{\lceil c_{1}p^{e}\rceil}\cdots\mathfrak{a_{t}}^{\lceil c_{t}p^{e}\rceil})^{[1/p^{e}]}\right\}_{e>0}
stabilizes, therefore
\tau(\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{t}^{c_{t}})=(\mathfrak{a_{1}}^{\lceil c_{1}p^{e}\rceil}\cdots\mathfrak{a_{t}}^{\lceil c_{t}p^{e}\rceil})^{[1/p^{e}]}
for
e
big enough. Note that for a given collection of ideals
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{t}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{t}
, there always exist points
d\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{t}
, other than
c
such that
\tau(\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{t}^{c_{t}})=\tau(\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{t}^{d_{t}})
. The subset of
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{t}
consisting of all such
d
is called the
constancy region
of
\tau(\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{c_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{t}^{c_{t}})
, see for instance
[
HY03
,
Tak04
,
BMS08
,
Pér13
]
. The measure of a related set in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{t}
was introduced under the name of
F
-volumes by Badilla-Céspedes, Núñez-Betancourt and Rodríguez-Villalobos
[
BNR22
]
.

Definition 4.2
.

Consider a collection
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{t},\mathfrak{b}
of ideals in
R
and assume that for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
,
\mathfrak{a}_{i}\subseteq\sqrt{\mathfrak{b}}
. Given
e\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
we define

\operatorname{V}_{\mathfrak{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{t}}^{\mathfrak{b}}(p^{e})=\{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{t})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{t}\ \mid\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\mathfrak{a}_{t}^{n_{t}}\not\subseteq\mathfrak{b}^{[p^{e}]}\}.

Theorem 4.3

(
[
BNR22
, Theorem
2.13
]
)
.

The limit

\lim_{e\to\infty}\frac{1}{{p^{et}}}\cdot\operatorname{Card}(\operatorname{V}_{\mathfrak{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{t}}^{\mathfrak{b}}(p^{e}))

converges.

The previous limit is called the
F
-volume of
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{t}
with respect to
\mathfrak{b}
and it is denoted by
\operatorname{Vol}_{F}^{\mathfrak{b}}(\mathfrak{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{t})
. The
F
-volume measures the sum of the volumes of the constancy regions where
\tau(\mathfrak{a}^{n_{1}}_{1}\cdots\mathfrak{a}^{n_{t}}_{t})\not\subseteq\mathfrak{b}
. It is known
[
BNR22
]
that the
F
-volume of
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{t}
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
is bounded above by the minimum between
\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{1})\cdots\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{t})
and the volume of the pyramid in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{t}
bounded by the coordinate hyperplanes and the hyperplane defined by
x_{1}+\ldots+x_{t}=\fpt(\mathfrak{a}_{1}+\ldots+\mathfrak{a}_{t})
. However, no trivial lower bounds are known for the
F
-volume of
\mathfrak{a}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{t}
with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
so we provide some bounds in terms of our splitting polytopes
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
. First we start with an easy calculation.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})
be a polynomial mapping of elements in
\mathfrak{m}
, and take
\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{\circ},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{t}^{\circ}
the collection formed by the term ideals of the principal ideals
(f_{1}),\ldots,(f_{t})
. Assume that a point
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
is written as
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{t})
with
\gamma_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{l_{i}}
, then

\max_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}}|\gamma_{1}|\cdots|\gamma_{t}|\leq\operatorname{Vol}_{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{\circ},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{t}^{\circ}).

Proof.

By definition
\gamma\in\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
means that
\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\langle\gamma\rangle_{e}\prec\mathcal{E}_{\boldsymbol{f}}\gamma\preceq\textbf{1}_{m}
, implying
x^{p^{e}\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{1}\langle\gamma_{1}\rangle_{e}}\cdots x^{p^{e}\operatorname{\widehat{\mathcal{E}}}_{t}\langle\gamma_{t}\rangle_{e}}\in(\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{\circ})^{p^{e}|\langle\gamma_{1}\rangle_{e}|}\cdots(\mathfrak{a}_{t}^{\circ})^{p^{e}|\langle\gamma_{t}\rangle_{e}|}\setminus\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}
. Therefore

(p^{e}|\langle\gamma_{1}\rangle_{e}|,\ldots,p^{e}|\langle\gamma_{t}\rangle_{e}|)
is a point of
\operatorname{V}_{\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{\circ},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{t}^{\circ}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})
, and we have the bound

p^{et}\prod_{i=1}^{t}|\langle\gamma_{i}\rangle_{e}|\leq\operatorname{Card}\left(\operatorname{V}_{\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{\circ},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{t}^{\circ}}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})\right).

Dividing by
p^{et}
and letting
e\rightarrow\infty
yields
\prod_{i=1}^{t}|\gamma_{i}|\leq\operatorname{Vol}_{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathfrak{a}_{1}^{\circ},\ldots,\mathfrak{a}_{t}^{\circ})
,
from which the conclusion easily follows.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Consider a polynomial mapping
\boldsymbol{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{t})
of elements in
\mathfrak{m}
. Suppose that
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
has a unique maximal point
\rho=(\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{t})
with
\rho_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{l_{i}}
, and set for
i\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
,

S_{i}=\sup\left\{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mid\sum_{j=1}^{l_{i}}\rho_{i,j}^{(e)}\leq p-1\quad\text{for every}\quad 0\leq e\leq\ell\right\}.

If
I
denotes the set of indices for which
S_{i}
is finite, then

\prod_{i\in I}\left(|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{S_{i}}|+\frac{1}{p^{S_{i}}}\right)\prod_{i\notin I}|\rho_{i}|\leq\operatorname{Vol}_{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}((f_{1}),\ldots,(f_{t})).

Proof.

Following the proof of Theorem
3.6
(1) we note that for
I=\emptyset
, the polynomial

g=\prod_{i=1}^{t}f_{i}^{p^{e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|}\notin\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}.

This implies that
(p^{e}|\langle\rho_{1}\rangle_{e}|,\ldots,p^{e}|\langle\rho_{t}\rangle_{e}|)
belongs to
\operatorname{V}_{(f_{1}),\ldots,(f_{t})}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})
, and also

p^{et}\prod_{i=1}^{t}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{e}|\leq\operatorname{Card}\left(\operatorname{V}_{(f_{1}),\ldots,(f_{t})}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{e})\right).

Dividing by
p^{et}
and letting
e\rightarrow\infty
concludes the proof of this case.

We follow the same strategy when
I\neq\emptyset
, but using this time the polynomial

g=\prod_{i=1}^{r}f_{i}^{p^{S+e}|v_{i}|}\cdot\prod_{i=r+1}^{t}f_{i}^{p^{S+e}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{S+e}|},

from the second part of the proof of Theorem
3.6
. Therefore

p^{(S+e)t}\left(\prod_{i=1}^{r}|v_{i}|\cdot\prod_{i=r+1}^{t}|\langle\rho_{i}\rangle_{S+e}|\right)\leq\operatorname{Card}\left(\operatorname{V}_{(f_{1}),\ldots,(f_{t})}^{\mathfrak{m}}(p^{S+e})\right).

The conclusion is achieved as before, dividing by
p^{(S+e)t}
and taking the limit.
∎

Example 4.6
.

Take
R=\mathbb{F}_{p}[x,y,z]
and
\boldsymbol{f}=(x^{2}+xy^{2},yz^{3})
as in Example
3.7
. Then

\begin{cases}\operatorname{Vol}_{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}((x^{2}+xy^{2}),(yz^{3}))\geq\frac{1}{6}&\text{if}\quad p=2\\
\operatorname{Vol}_{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}((x^{2}+xy^{2}),(yz^{3}))\geq\frac{1}{9}&\text{if}\quad p=3\\
\operatorname{Vol}_{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}((x^{2}+xy^{2}),(yz^{3}))\geq\frac{2}{9}&\text{if}\quad p\equiv 1\mod 6\\
\operatorname{Vol}_{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}((x^{2}+xy^{2}),(yz^{3}))\geq\frac{2}{9}-\frac{1}{9p}&\text{if}\quad p\equiv 5\mod 6.\end{cases}

Of course the inequality of Theorem
4.5
can be strict as we will show next. This demonstrates the need to continue looking for bounds for the
F
-volume.

Example 4.7
.

Let
R=\mathbb{F}_{2}[x,y]
and consider the polynomial mapping
\boldsymbol{f}=(x,x+y^{2})
.Then
\operatorname{Vol}_{F}^{\mathfrak{m}}((x),(x+y^{2}))=3/4

[
BNR22
, Example
2.15
]
.
On the other hand,
\mathcal{P}_{\boldsymbol{f}}
is exactly
[0,1]\times\left[0,\frac{1}{2}\right]
with maximal point
(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})=(1,1/2)
. Since
S_{1}=S_{2}=\infty
, we obtain
|\rho_{1}|\cdot|\rho_{2}|=1/2
.

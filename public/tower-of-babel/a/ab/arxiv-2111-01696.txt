Coulomb phase corrections to the transverse analyzing power A N ​ ( t ) A_{\text{N}}(t) in high energy forward proton-proton scattering
A. A. Poblaguev

Abstract
Study of polarized proton-proton elastic scattering in the Coulomb-nuclear interference region allows one to measure the forward hadronic single spin-flip amplitude including its phase. However, in a precision experimental data analysis, a phase-shift correction δ C \delta_{C} due to the long distance Coulomb interaction should be taken into account. For unpolarized scattering, δ C \delta_{C} is commonly considered as well established. Here, we evaluate the Coulomb phase shifts for the forward elastic proton-proton single spin-flip electromagnetic and hadronic amplitudes. Only a small discrepancy between the spin-flip and nonflip phases was found which can be neglected in the high energy forward elastic 𝑝𝑝 \mathit{pp} studies involving transverse spin. Nonetheless, the effective alteration of the hadronic spin-flip amplitude by the long-distance electromagnetic corrections can be essential for interpretation of the experimental results.



I
Introduction

Since electromagnetic amplitude can substantially contribute to the elastic forward proton-proton (
\mathit{pp}
) scattering at high energies, experimental study of the Coulomb-nuclear interference (CNI) in the
{pp}
scattering allows one to reveal the hadronic amplitude structure. For the unpolarized scattering, the CNI
{pp}
amplitude can be approximated as
Block and Cahn 1985

\phi_{pp}^{\text{CNI}}(s,t)=\text{Im}\,\phi(s,0)\left[(i\!+\!\rho)e^{Bt/2}\!+\!\frac{t_{c}}{t}e^{i\delta_{C}+\widetilde{B}t/2}\right]\!.

(1)

Here,
\phi(s,t)
stands for the hadronic amplitude and the electromagnetic component is identified by
t_{c}/t
term, where
t_{c}\!=\!-8\pi\alpha/\sigma_{\text{tot}}(s)
,
\alpha
is the fine structure constant and
\sigma_{\text{tot}}
is the total
pp
cross section.
Generally,
\phi_{pp}^{\text{CNI}}
and the parameters used are functions of total energy squared
s
and momentum transfer squared
t
.

In this paper, numerical estimates will be done for a 100 GeV proton beam (typical for the Relativistic Heavy Ion Collider and the future Electron Ion Collider) scattering off a fixed proton target. Therefore,
\rho\!=\!-0.079

Fagundes et al. 2017
,

\sigma_{\text{tot}}\!=\!39.2\,\text{mb}

Fagundes et al. 2017
,

t_{c}\!=\!-1.86\!\times\!10^{-3}\,\text{GeV}^{2}
,
and
B\!=\!11.2\,\text{GeV}^{-2}

Bartenev et al. 1973a
;
*Bartenev:1973kk
.
The electromagnetic form factor is expressed,

\widetilde{B}=2r_{E}^{2}/3=12.1\,\text{GeV}^{-2},

(2)

via rms charge radius of a proton,
r_{E}\!=\!0.841\,\text{fm}

Zyla et al. 2020
.

For the unpolarized scattering, a theoretical understanding of the Coulomb phase shift
\delta_{C}(t)
was developed in many works, particularly in
Akhiezer and Pomeranchuk 1946
;
*Bethe:1958zz
;
*West:1968du
;
*Cahn:1982nr
. Following Ref.
Kopeliovich and Tarasov 2001
and neglecting terms
\sim\!t^{2}\ln{t}
,

\displaystyle\delta_{C}(t)/\alpha

\displaystyle=-\ln{\left[(B+\widetilde{B})|t|/2\right]}-\gamma

(3)

\displaystyle+\frac{\widetilde{B}t}{2}\,\left(\ln{\frac{\widetilde{B}|t|}{2}}\!+\!\gamma\!+\!\ln{2}\!-\!1\right)-\frac{B}{B\!+\!\widetilde{B}}\frac{Bt}{2},

(4)

where
\gamma\!=\!0.5772
is Euler’s constant. The leading order approximation (
3
) is commonly used in experimental data analysis for many years. The next to leading order corrections (
4

It was shown in Ref.
Buttimore et al. 1978
that the Coulomb phase should be independent of the helicity structure of the experimentally measured scattering amplitudes though subsequent determination of the pure hadronic amplitudes may involve order-
\alpha

Recently, long-distance electromagnetic corrections, including the absorption, to the spin-flip amplitudes were derived
Kopeliovich et al. 2021
in the eikonal model. However, the results were presented as Fourier integrals (which were calculated numerically) and, thus, cannot be implemented, in a simple way, to an experimental data analysis. Also, it was noted
Kopeliovich et al. 2021
that the corrections to the spin-flip Coulomb phase “are so large, that hardly can be treated as a phase shift”, which may be understood as a suggestion to obsolete a commonly used expression for the analyzing power
A_{\text{N}}(t)
given in Eq. (
7
) and, consequently, to reanalyze all previous measurements of
A_{\text{N}}(t)
.

Here, we found compact algebraic approximations for the long-distance electromagnetic corrections, separately, to the spin-flip Coulomb phase and to the hadronic spin-flip amplitude parameter
r_{5}
[Eq. (
6
)]. It was shown that the difference between the spin-flip and nonflip Coulomb phases is small. Although, the effective correction to
\text{Re}\,r_{5}
was found to be essential for the experimental accuracy already achieved
Poblaguev et al. 2019
, it can be applied directly to the value of
r_{5}
obtained in experimental data fit. The corresponding changes in the Regge fit
Poblaguev et al. 2019
of the measured values of
r_{5}(s)
will be discussed.

(5)

\phi

\widetilde{\phi}_{\textit{sf}}/\widetilde{\phi}_{\textit{nf}}=\frac{\kappa_{p}}{2}\,\sqrt{-t}/m_{p},\quad\phi_{\textit{sf}}/\phi_{\textit{nf}}=\frac{r_{5}}{i+\rho}\,\sqrt{-t}/m_{p},

\displaystyle(t)=\frac{\sqrt{-t}}{m_{p}}\times

\displaystyle\frac{\left[\kappa_{p}(1\!-\!\delta_{C}^{em}\rho)\!-\!2(I_{5}\!-\!\delta_{C}^{h}R_{5})\right]{t_{c}/t}\!-\!2(R_{5}\!+\rho{I_{5}})}{\left({t_{c}/t}\right)^{2}-2\left(\rho+\delta_{C}\right){t_{c}/t}+1+\rho^{2}}\!,

3

All recent experimental studies

III.1
Theoretical approach used
Kopeliovich and Tarasov 2001
to calculate Coulomb corrections to the nonflip amplitudes

Considering multiple photon exchange in the elastic
pp
scattering and neglecting the higher order corrections
{\cal O}(\alpha^{3})
, the net long-range Coulomb (C) amplitude (see Fig.
1
) can be presented as
Kopeliovich and Tarasov 2001

\displaystyle f_{C}(q_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int{d^{2}b\,e^{i\vec{q}_{T}\vec{b}}\left[1-e^{i\chi_{C}^{\textit{nf}}(b)}\right]},

(8)

\displaystyle=\hat{f}_{C}(q_{T})+\frac{i}{2\pi}\int{d^{2}b\,e^{i\vec{q}_{T}\vec{b}}\left[\chi_{C}(b)\right]^{2}/2}

(9)

where
q_{T}\!\approx\!\sqrt{-t}
is transverse momentum and
the eikonal phase

\chi_{C}^{\textit{nf}}(b)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int{d^{2}q_{T}\hat{f}_{C}(q_{T})e^{-i\vec{q}_{T}\vec{b}}}

(10)

is a Fourier transform of the Coulomb part of the amplitude calculated in Born approximation
Kopeliovich and Tarasov 2001

\hat{f}_{C}(q_{T})=\frac{-2\alpha}{q_{T}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\,e^{-\widetilde{B}q_{T}^{2}/2}.

(11)

Here,
\hat{f}_{C}
is defined as sum of two nonflip helicity amplitudes

\langle\!+\>\!\!+\!|\!+\>\!\!+\!\rangle
and
\langle\!+\>\!\!-\!|\!+\>\!\!-\!\rangle

Buttimore et al. 1999
. A small photon mass
\lambda
was included to Eq. (
11
) to keep the integrals finite.

The multiphoton exchange results in an acquired Coulomb phase
\Phi_{C}(q_{T})

f_{C}(q_{T})=\hat{f}_{C}(q_{T})\,e^{i\Phi_{C}(q_{T})}.

(12)

Assuming
\Phi_{C}(q_{T})\!\ll\!1
, one finds

\displaystyle\!\!\Phi_{C}(q_{T})

\displaystyle=-i\left[f_{C}(q_{T})/\hat{f}_{C}(q_{T})-1\right]

(13)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int{d^{2}q_{1}d^{2}q_{2}\delta(\vec{q}_{T}\!-\!\vec{q}_{1}\!-\!\vec{q}_{2})\frac{\hat{f}_{C}(q_{1})\hat{f}_{C}(q_{2})}{\hat{f}_{C}(q_{T})}}\!,

(14)

Similarly, to calculate the Coulomb corrections to the hadronic amplitude,

\hat{f}_{N}(q_{T})=\frac{(i+\rho)\sigma_{\text{tot}}}{4\pi}\,e^{-Bq_{T}^{2}/2},

(15)

one can use the following relations,

\displaystyle f_{\text{NC}}(q_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int{d^{2}b\,e^{i\vec{q}_{T}\vec{b}}\gamma_{N}^{\textit{nf}}(b)e^{i\chi_{C}^{\textit{nf}}(b)}}

(16)

\displaystyle=\hat{f}_{N}(q_{T})+\frac{i}{2\pi}\int{d^{2}b\,e^{i\vec{q}_{T}\vec{b}}\gamma_{N}^{\textit{nf}}\chi_{C}^{\textit{nf}}},

(17)

\displaystyle\gamma_{N}^{\textit{nf}}(b)

\displaystyle=\frac{-i}{2\pi}\int{d^{2}q_{T}e^{-i\vec{q}_{T}\vec{b}}\,\hat{f}_{N}(q_{T})},

(18)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{NC}}(q_{T})

\displaystyle=-\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\int{\frac{d^{2}q_{1}}{q_{1}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}}

\displaystyle\times\exp{\left[-(B+\widetilde{B})q_{1}^{2}/2+B\vec{q}_{1}\vec{q_{T}}\right]}.

(19)

Equations (
14
) and (
19
) were analytically integrated in Ref.
Kopeliovich and Tarasov 2001
.
Both,
\Phi_{C}(q_{T})
and
\Phi_{\text{NC}}(q_{T})
, contain the divergent term
\ln{q^{2}/\lambda^{2}}
which, however, cancels in final expression (
3
) for the Coulomb phase difference

\delta_{C}(t)=\Phi_{C}(t)-\Phi_{\text{NC}}(t).

(20)

III.2
Calculation of the spin-flip Coulomb phase

To find the Coulomb corrected spin-flip amplitudes
f_{C}^{\textit{sf}}(q_{T})
and
f_{\text{NC}}^{\textit{sf}}(q_{T})
, one can use the following eikonal phases
Lapidus 1978

\chi_{C}^{\textit{sf}}(b)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int{d^{2}qe^{-i\vec{q}\vec{b}}\times\frac{\kappa_{p}}{2m_{p}}\,(\vec{n}\vec{q})\,\hat{f}_{C}(q)/2}

(21)

and

\gamma_{N}^{\textit{sf}}(b)=\frac{-i}{2\pi}\int{d^{2}qe^{-i\vec{q}\vec{b}}\times\frac{r_{5}}{(i+\rho)m_{p}}\,(\vec{n}\vec{q})\,\hat{f}_{N}(q)/2}

(22)

respectively. Here,

\hat{f}_{C}/2
and
\hat{f}_{N}/2
correspond to the nonflip amplitudes used in Eq. (
6
), and the azimuthal dependence of the scattering is defined by

\vec{n}
, a unit vector orthogonal to the beam momentum and the proton spin.

Considering the spin flip amplitudes for
\vec{q}_{T}\!=\!\vec{n}q_{T}
, one can readily determine the spin-flip phase
\Phi_{C}^{\textit{sf}}(q_{T})
by including factor
2(\vec{q}_{T}\vec{q}_{1})/q_{T}^{2}
or
2(\vec{q}_{T}\vec{q}_{2})/q_{T}^{2}
to integral (
14
).
Since
\vec{q}_{1}\vec{q}_{T}\!+\!\vec{q}_{2}\vec{q}_{T}\!=\!q_{T}^{2}
, we immediately find

\Phi_{C}^{\textit{sf}}(q_{T})\!=\!\Phi_{C}(q_{T}),

(23)

which leads to

\delta_{C}^{\textit{em}}(t)=\delta_{C}(t).

(24)

To calculate
\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\textit{sf}}(q_{T})
, factor
(\vec{q}_{2}\vec{q}_{T})/q_{T}^{2}=1-(\vec{q}_{1}\vec{q}_{T})/q_{T}^{2}
should be applied in Eq. (
19
), which gives

\displaystyle\!\!\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\textit{sf}}(q_{T})

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\text{NC}}(q_{T})+\frac{\alpha B}{B+\widetilde{B}}\times\Delta_{\text{NC}}^{\textit{sf}}(\eta),

(25)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\text{NC}}^{\textit{sf}}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\frac{du}{\eta}e^{-u^{2}/4\eta}}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\frac{d\varphi}{2\pi}\cos{\varphi}\,e^{u\cos{\varphi}}},

(26)

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{B+\widetilde{B}}\times\frac{Bq_{T}^{2}}{2}\approx Bq_{T}^{2}/4.

(27)

Expanding

e^{u\cos{\varphi}}\cos{\varphi}\to\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\frac{u^{k}}{k!}\cos^{k+1}{\varphi}}

(28)

and using following definite integrals
Gradshteyn et al. 2014

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\cos^{2n+1}{x}\,dx}

\displaystyle=0,

(29)

\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\cos^{2n}{x}\,dx}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2^{2n-2}}\,\frac{(2n-1)!}{(n-1)!n!},

(30)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}{x^{2n+1}e^{-px^{2}}\,dx}

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{2p^{n+1}},

(31)

one arrives to

\Delta_{\text{NC}}^{\textit{sf}}(\eta)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\frac{\eta^{k}}{(k+1)!}}=\frac{e^{\eta}-1}{\eta}.

(32)

Thus,

\delta_{C}^{h}(t)=\delta_{C}(t)-\frac{\alpha B}{B+\widetilde{B}}\,\frac{e^{\eta}-1}{\eta}.

(33)

Evaluating
\delta_{C}^{\textit{em}}
and
\delta_{C}^{h}
, we did not distinguish between nonflip
B
and spin-flip
B_{\textit{sf}}
hadronic slopes as well as between
\widetilde{B}
[Eq. (
2
)] and

\widetilde{B}_{\textit{sf}}=\left(r_{E}^{2}+r_{M}^{2}\right)/3=12.25\pm 0.25\,\text{GeV}^{-2},

(34)

where
r_{M}=0.851\!\pm\!0.026\,\text{fm}

Lee et al. 2015
is rms magnetic radius of a proton.

For small
t
, i.e., omitting terms approaching zero if
t\to 0
, one finds

\displaystyle\!\!\delta_{C}^{\textit{em}}(t,\widetilde{B}_{\textit{sf}},B)

\displaystyle=\delta_{C}(t)+\alpha\ln{\frac{B+\widetilde{B}}{B+\widetilde{B}_{\textit{sf}}}}

(35)

\displaystyle\delta_{C}^{h}(t,\widetilde{B},B_{\textit{sf}})

\displaystyle=\delta_{C}(t)-\frac{\alpha B_{\textit{sf}}}{B_{\textit{sf}}+\widetilde{B}}+\alpha\ln{\frac{B+\widetilde{B}}{B_{\textit{sf}}+\widetilde{B}}}

(36)

Since
r_{M}\!=\!r_{E}
within the current experimental accuracy of about 2%, we cannot distinguish between
\widetilde{B}
and
\widetilde{B}_{\textit{sf}}
. Also, there are arguments

to assume that

\delta_{C}^{h}R_{5}
in (
7

III.3
The electromagnetic correction to
\boldsymbol{r}_{5}

In Ref.
Kopeliovich et al. 2021
, it was pointed out that the hadronic spin-flip amplitude should also include the spin-flip photon exchange, i.e., one should replace

\gamma_{N}^{\textit{sf}}(b)\to\gamma_{N}^{\textit{sf}}(b)+i\chi_{C}^{\textit{sf}}(b)\gamma_{N}^{\textit{nf}}(b).

(37)

The effective spin-flip amplitude
r_{5}^{\gamma}
can be related to the integral

\frac{r_{5}^{\gamma}-r_{5}}{i+\rho}\,\frac{\vec{n}\vec{q}}{2m_{p}}=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int{d^{2}be^{i\vec{q}_{T}\vec{b}}\,\chi_{C}^{\textit{sf}}(b)\gamma_{N}^{\textit{nf}}(b)},

(38)

which is similar to that of calculated in (
25
)–(
32
). Thus,

\displaystyle r_{5}^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=r_{5}+i(i+\rho)\Delta_{\gamma}\approx r_{5}-\Delta_{\gamma},

(39)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa_{p}}{2}\frac{\alpha B}{(B+\widetilde{B}_{\textit{sf}})}\approx 0.003.

(40)

For the modified
r_{5}^{\gamma}
, Coulomb phase
\Phi_{\text{NC}}^{\textit{sf}}(t)
is the same as in Eq. (
25
).

Actually
r_{5}^{\gamma}
had being determined in all previous measurements of

IV
Summary

The technique developed in Ref.
Kopeliovich and Tarasov 2001
was adapted for calculation of the Coulomb phase shifts in the spin-flip terms
\widetilde{\phi}_{\textit{sf}}\phi_{\textit{nf}}^{*}
(
24
) and
\phi_{\textit{sf}}\widetilde{\phi}_{\textit{nf}}^{*}
(
33
) of
A_{\text{N}}(t)
.

Small difference,
\delta_{C}^{h}\!-\!\delta_{C}\sim-\alpha/2
, was found for the forward elastic
\mathit{pp}
scattering,
|t|\lesssim 0.05\,\text{GeV}^{2}
(
\eta<0.1
). Since
|r_{5}|\!\lesssim 0.02
, such a discrepancy can be neglected in Eq. (
7
).

Thus, assuming spin-dependent measurements at high energies, we can agree with the approximation for the Coulomb phases

\delta_{C}^{\textit{em}}=\delta_{C}^{\textit{h}}=\delta_{C}=-\alpha\times\left[\ln{\frac{(B\!+\!\widetilde{B})|t|}{2}}+\gamma\right]

(41)

suggested in Ref.
Buttimore et al. 1999
.

Spin-flip photon exchange (
37
) results in an effective correction
\Delta_{\gamma}\!\approx\!0.003

to real part of the hadronic spin-flip parameter
r_{5}
in Eq. (
7
). The correction found is about triple of the experimental accuracy for
R_{5}
in the HJET measurements
Poblaguev et al. 2019
.
Since
\Delta_{\gamma}
is independent of the experimental data analysis, any already published experimental value
r_{5}^{\gamma}
of the hadronic spin-flip amplitude, in particular given in
Poblaguev et al. 2019
;
Adamczyk et al. 2013
, can be easily adjusted to a bare one,

{\rm Re}\,r_{5}={\rm Re}\,r_{5}^{\gamma}+\frac{\kappa_{p}}{2}\frac{\alpha B}{B+\widetilde{B}_{\textit{sf}}}.

(42)

This might be especially important for a study of
r_{5}(s)
dependence on energy, e.g., in the Regge fit
Poblaguev et al. 2019
;
Kopeliovich et al. 2021
.

To illustrate a possible effect of corrections (
42
), the Regge fit
Poblaguev et al. 2019
of the HJET values of
r_{5}
(for
\sqrt{s}\!=\!13.76~\text{and}~21.92\,\text{GeV}
),

\sigma_{\text{tot}}(s)\times r_{5}(s)=\sum_{{\cal{}R}=P,R^{\pm}}{f_{5}^{\cal{}R}\,{\cal{}R}(s)},

(43)

was revisited.
In Ref.
Poblaguev et al. 2019
, the spin-flip Reggeon,
R^{+}(s)
and
R^{-}(s)
, and Pomeron (in a Froissaron parametrization),
P(s)
, functions were approximated by the nonflip ones
Fagundes et al. 2017
.
After applying corrections (
42
), the fit
\chi^{2}\!=\!2.2
(NDF=1) was improved to
\chi^{2}\!=\!0.9
and the central values of couplings
f_{5}^{\cal{}R}
were shifted to

\displaystyle\phantom{-}f_{5}^{P^{\phantom{+}}}\!\!\!=\!0.045\!\pm\!0.002_{\text{stat}}\!\pm\!0.003_{\text{syst}}

\displaystyle\,\to\,0.053,

(44)

\displaystyle-f_{5}^{R^{+}}\!\!\!=\!0.032\!\pm\!0.007_{\text{stat}}\!\pm\!0.014_{\text{syst}}

\displaystyle\,\to\,0.069,

(45)

\displaystyle\phantom{-}f_{5}^{R^{-}}\!\!\!=\!0.622\!\pm\!0.023_{\text{stat}}\!\pm\!0.024_{\text{syst}}

\displaystyle\,\to\,0.654.

(46)

A thorough refit of the
r_{5}
measurements, including the STAR
\sqrt{s}\!=\!200\,\text{GeV}
result
Adamczyk et al. 2013
, will be done elsewhere.

It is interesting to note that an absorptive correction,

a_{\textit{sf}}=\alpha B/(B+\widetilde{B}_{\textit{sf}}),

(47)

to the electromagnetic spin-flip form factor,
{\cal F}_{pp}^{\textit{sf}}(t)\to{\cal F}_{pp}^{\textit{sf}}(t)\times\left(1+a_{\textit{sf}}\,t/t_{c}\right)
, results
Poblaguev 2019
in the same effective alteration of

as shown in Eq. (
42
).

In Ref.
Kopeliovich et al. 2021
, to calculate absorptive corrections, graphs in Fig.
1

37
] to hadronic spin-flip amplitude

was not considered. Nonetheless, the term
\chi_{C}^{\textit{sf}}(b)\gamma_{N}^{\textit{nf}}(b)
appeared as an absorptive correction to electromagnetic spin-flip amplitude

. The corresponding equation was integrated numerically in
Kopeliovich et al. 2021

38

to be in exact agreement with Eq. (

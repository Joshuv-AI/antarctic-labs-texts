Modelling of Vertical Dipole Above Lossy Dielectric Half-Space: Characteristic Mode Theory
Sandip Ghosal, Arijit De, Raed M. Shubair, Ajay Chakrabarty

Abstract
This work introduces a theoretical extension of the characteristic mode formulation for analysing the vertical electric dipole lying above a lossy dielectric half-space. As the conventional characteristic formulation fails to maintain the orthogonality of the characteristic field modes over the infinite sphere, an alternate modal formulation is proposed here to maintain the orthogonality for both the current and field modes. The modal results are found to match closely with its method of moment counterparts. Later, the modes of an isolated dipole with no ground plane have been used to predict the role of the lossy ground plane through a theory of the linear combination of the eigenvectors. The proposed formulations have been studied with different heights from the ground plane and are compared with the direct modal solutions to validate its accuracy. It helps to provide a thorough understanding of how the isolated modes interact among each other to constitute the perturbed modes in the presence of the lossy half-space. It can find application to include the lossy earth effect in the study of the lightning fields and the path loss modelling of the antennas over the lossy ground.

II-A

Conventional Formulation

If conventional CMA of
[
11
]
is followed to compute the eigenmodes, corresponding characteristic equation of the impedance matrix
[\text{Z}_{LG}]=[\text{R}_{LG}]+\text{j}[\text{X}_{LG}]
will be

\displaystyle[\text{X}_{LG}]\tilde{J}_{LG,n}=\Delta_{LG,n}[\text{R}_{LG}]\tilde{J}_{LG,n}.

(7)

The eigenvectors
\tilde{J}_{LG,n}
and eigenvalues
\Delta_{LG,n}
will be real in (
7
). Its orthogonal relationship can be written as

\displaystyle[\tilde{J}_{LG,m}]^{T}[\text{X}_{LG}][\tilde{J}_{LG,n}]

\displaystyle=\Delta_{LG,n}[\tilde{J}_{LG,m}]^{T}[\text{R}_{LG}][\tilde{J}_{LG,n}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{mn}\Delta_{LG,n}[\tilde{J}_{LG,n}]^{T}[\text{R}_{LG}][\tilde{J}_{LG,n}],

(8)

\displaystyle\text{where}~~\delta_{mn}=0,~\text{if}~m\neq n,~\text{else}~0.

(9)

Due to the presence of the lossy coefficient
K_{eps}
, the matrix
[\text{R}_{LG}]
will have both the free-space radiation and lossy dissipation counterparts. The radiation part (
[\text{R}_{o}]
) is due to the first term in (
2
). The other two terms contribute to the lossy part of
[\text{R}_{L}]=[\text{R}_{LG}]-[\text{R}_{o}]
.

The introduction of characteristic mode analysis is fundamentally rooted in the far-field scattering matrix where the far-field scattering matrix was decomposed into standard eigen decomposition as
[S]E_{n}=\lambda^{S}_{n}E_{n}
in
[
59
]
and
[
10
]
to obtain a set of characteristic field modes
E_{n}
which are orthogonal over the far-field radiation sphere. Later, the MoM implementation of the electric field integral equation on the surface (/volume) of the lossless objects were used in
[
11
]
to find out a set of characteristic current modes which can generate a set of characteristic field modes which maintain the orthogonality over the infinite radiation sphere. Such inter-relationship of the current based CMA and the initial field-based CMA can be noted in
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
. So, the succeeding discussion checks whether the conventional characteristic mode formulation of (
7
) can maintain such orthogonality properties in the set of characteristic field modes.

Let the
n^{th}
eigenvector
\tilde{J}_{LG,n}
be assumed to produce the the
n^{th}
characteristic field pattern
\tilde{E}_{LG,n}
in the far-field. If the complex power balance equation is followed using
[
60
]
and
[
11
]
, it can be written that

\displaystyle[\tilde{J}_{LG,m}]^{T}[\text{Z}_{LG}][\tilde{J}_{LG,n}]=\frac{1}{\eta_{o}}\oint_{S\infty}\tilde{E}_{LG,m}^{*}.\tilde{E}_{LG,n}dS

\displaystyle+[\tilde{J}_{LG,m}]^{T}[\text{R}_{L}][\tilde{J}_{LG,n}]+\text{j}\omega\int_{V^{\prime}}(\mu\tilde{H}_{LG,m}^{*}.\tilde{H}_{LG,n}

\displaystyle-\epsilon_{o}\tilde{E}_{LG,m}^{*}.\tilde{E}_{LG,n})dV.

(10)

Separating the real part of (
10
), it can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta_{o}}\oint_{S\infty}\tilde{E}_{LG,m}^{*}.\tilde{E}_{LG,n}dS+[\tilde{J}_{LG,m}]^{T}[\text{R}_{L}][\tilde{J}_{LG,n}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{mn}[\tilde{J}_{LG,n}]^{T}[\text{R}_{LG}][\tilde{J}_{LG,n}].

(11)

Here, the free-space intrinsic impedance is
\eta_{o}
. The inner product of the characteristic
field modes
\tilde{E}_{LG,m}
and
\tilde{E}_{LG,n}
over the far-field radiation sphere (
S\infty
) provides the radiated power. For
m\neq n
, (
11
) can be written using (
8
) as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta_{o}}\oint_{S\infty}\tilde{E}_{LG,m}^{*}.\tilde{E}_{LG,n}dS=-[\tilde{J}_{LG,m}]^{T}[\text{R}_{L}][\tilde{J}_{LG,n}],~m\neq n.

(12)

For the lossless case, as
[\text{R}_{L}]=[0]
, the right hand term of (
12
) will be zero, providing the orthogonality to the field modes
\tilde{E}_{LG,m}
and
\tilde{E}_{LG,n}
. However, in the presence of the lossy dielectric ground, the resistive power loss term
\text{P}_{RL~m,n}=|[\tilde{J}_{LG,m}]^{T}[\text{R}_{L}][\tilde{J}_{LG,n}]|
can be non-zero. As a consequence, the
orthogonal relationship of the modal field patterns
\tilde{E}_{LG,n}
at far-field location will not be maintained as shown in (

\tilde{J}_{LG,n}
fail to diagonalize the far-field scattering matrix. So, the least-square convergence property over infinite sphere is also lost

11
]
. The situation is very similar to eigenmodes of the lossy dielectric bodies in
[

]

II-B

Proposed Formulation

As noted in (
12
), the projection of the eigenvectors in the matrix space of
[\text{R}_{L}]
leads to the loss of orthogonality in the far-field components. To maintain the far-field orthogonal property
[
12
]
, an alternate formulation is provided in the present work for computing the characteristic modes of the PEC dipole above lossy ground. The modes can be determined using the following characteristic relation,

\displaystyle[\text{X}_{LG}][\text{J}_{LG,n}]

\displaystyle=\lambda_{LG,n}[\text{R}_{o}][\text{J}_{LG,n}].

(13)

As both
[\text{X}_{LG}]
and
[\text{R}_{o}]
are symmetric and
[\text{R}_{o}]
is positive definite, the eigenvectors
[\text{J}_{LG,n}]
and eigenvalues
\lambda_{LG,n}
will be real. The orthogonal property of the
n^{th}
characteristic mode can be expressed as

\displaystyle[\text{J}_{LG,m}]^{T}[\text{X}_{LG}][\text{J}_{LG,n}]=\delta_{mn}\lambda_{LG,n}[\text{J}_{LG,m}]^{T}[\text{R}_{o}][\text{J}_{LG,n}].

(14)

Similar to the conventional case, the complex power balance equation can be written using
[
60
]
and
[
11
]

\displaystyle[\text{J}_{LG,m}]^{T}[\text{Z}_{LG}][\text{J}_{LG,n}]=\frac{1}{\eta_{o}}\oint_{S\infty}\tilde{E}_{LG,m}^{*}.\text{E}_{LG,n}dS

\displaystyle+[\text{J}_{LG,m}]^{T}[\text{R}_{L}][\text{J}_{LG,n}]+\text{j}\omega\int_{V^{\prime}}(\mu\tilde{H}_{LG,m}^{*}.\text{H}_{LG,n}

\displaystyle-\epsilon_{o}\text{E}_{LG,m}^{*}.\text{E}_{LG,n})dV.

(15)

Separation of the real part of (
15
) will provide

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta_{o}}\oint_{S\infty}\text{E}_{LG,m}^{*}.\text{E}_{LG,n}dS+[\text{J}_{LG,m}]^{T}[\text{R}_{L}][\text{J}_{LG,n}]

\displaystyle=[\text{J}_{LG,m}]^{T}[\text{R}_{o}][\text{J}_{LG,n}]+[\text{J}_{LG,m}]^{T}[\text{R}_{L}][\text{J}_{LG,n}]

\displaystyle=\delta_{mn}[\text{J}_{LG,n}]^{T}[\text{R}_{o}][\text{J}_{LG,n}]+[\text{J}_{LG,n}]^{T}[\text{R}_{L}][\text{J}_{LG,n}].

(16)

For
m\neq n
, it can be seen from (
16
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta_{o}}\oint_{S\infty}\text{E}_{LG,m}^{*}.\text{E}_{LG,n}dS=\delta_{mn}[\text{J}_{LG,n}]^{T}[\text{R}_{o}][\text{J}_{LG,n}]=0.

(17)

Thus, the characteristic modes obtained using the proposed formulation of (
16
) provide a set of characteristic field modes
\text{E}_{LG,m}
and
\text{E}_{LG,n}
which are orthogonal over the far-field radiation sphere. As a comparison between the conventional CMA formulation of (
7
) and the proposed formulation of (
13
), it is worthy of mentioning the following points:

•

In both the methods of (
7
) and (
13
), the dimension of the total eigenspace does not change.

•

The characteristic modes and eigenvalues are real for both the methods of (
7
) and (
13
).

•

The conventional method of (
7
) fails to provide the far-field orthogonality. Therefore, the total scattered field in the far-field region can not be expressed as a weighted linear combination of the characteristic field modes obtained using the conventional characteristic relation of (
7
).

•

The proposed formulation of (
13
) can maintain the orthogonality relation of both the characteristic current modes and the characteristic field modes. Lastly, the formulation is also applicable for the lossless case too when
[\text{X}_{LG}]=[\text{X}_{L}]+[\text{X}_{o}]=[\text{X}_{o}]
and
[\text{R}_{LG}]=[\text{R}_{L}]+[\text{R}_{o}]=[\text{R}_{o}]
.

•

As the number of characteristic modes is the same (
7
) and (
13
); the convergence behaviour will be controlled by the modal weighting coefficients only. The convergence of the total current or field depends on the proper selection of dominant modes, as discussed in more detail in the previous works
[

]
and

) and (
13
) that the dimension of the eigenspaces of the isolated dipole and the dipole above the lossy ground are same, i.e.,
N\times N
. Therefore, the eigenmode
[\text{J}_{LG,m}]
has been approximated as a weighted linear combination of the isolated modes
[\text{J}_{on}]
as

\displaystyle\text{J}_{LG,m}\cong\sum_{n=1}^{N}k_{mn}~\text{J}_{on}.

(24)

The rationale behind such approximation is that only two separate terms are added in (
2
) compared to (
42
). This causes a linear translation of the Kernel of the EFIE of the isolated dipole in the free-space. As a consequence, the impedance matrix
[\text{Z}_{LG}]
is a linear perturbation of the isolated matrix
[\text{Z}_{o}]
while having the same dimension of eigenspace. On a different note, the alteration of the isolated modes due to the lossy ground plane is a physical phenomenon where the EFIE operator experiences a linear perturbation. Using (
24
) and (
13
), it can be written that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}k_{mn}[\text{X}_{o}][\text{J}_{on}]+\sum_{n=1}^{N}k_{mn}[\text{X}_{L}][\text{J}_{on}]=

\displaystyle\lambda_{LG,m}\sum_{n=1}^{N}k_{mn}[\text{R}_{o}][\text{J}_{on}],

(25)

where
[\text{X}_{L}]=[\text{X}_{LG}]-[\text{X}_{o}]
.
Applying inner product of (
24
) with the
i^{th}
isolated mode
[\text{J}_{oi}]
, it will be

\displaystyle[\text{J}_{oi}]^{T}\sum_{n=1}^{N}k_{mn}[\text{X}_{o}][\text{J}_{on}]+[\text{J}_{oi}]^{T}\sum_{n=1}^{N}k_{mn}[\text{X}_{L}][\text{J}_{on}]=

\displaystyle\lambda_{LG,m}[\text{J}_{oi}]^{T}\sum_{n=1}^{N}k_{mn}[\text{R}_{o}][\text{J}_{on}].

(26)

The orthogonal properties of the isolated modes of (
43
) in (
26
) will provide

\displaystyle k_{mi}\lambda_{oi}\text{P}_{Ro,i}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}k_{mn}\text{P}_{XL,in}=k_{mi}\lambda_{LG,m}\text{P}_{Ro,i}.

(27)

where
\text{P}_{Ro,i}=[\text{J}_{oi}]^{T}[\text{R}_{o}][\text{J}_{oi}]
represents the radiative modal power of the
i^{th}
isolated mode
[\text{J}_{oi}]
. Similarly, the reactive modal power will be
\text{P}_{Xo,i}=[\text{J}_{oi}]^{T}[\text{X}_{o}][\text{J}_{oi}]=\lambda_{oi}\text{P}_{Ro,i}
.
The other component
\text{P}_{XL,in}=[\text{J}_{oi}]^{T}[\text{X}_{L}][\text{J}_{on}]
denotes the reactive interaction power due to the lossy ground coupling. It can be noted from (
26
) that the absence of the ground plane indicates that
\lambda_{oi}=\lambda_{LG,m}
. For
i\in[1,N]
, there will be
N
number of linear equations that will satisfy (
27
).The set of all such
N
linear equations can be expressed as the following matrix form

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}(\lambda_{LG,m}-\lambda_{o1})\text{P}_{Ro,1}&0&\ldots\\
0&(\lambda_{LG,m}-\lambda_{o2})\text{P}_{Ro,2}&\ldots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ldots\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}k_{m1}\\
k_{m2}\\
\vdots\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\text{P}_{XL,11}&\text{P}_{XL,12}&\ldots\\
\text{P}_{XL,21}&\text{P}_{XL,22}&\ldots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ldots\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}k_{m1}\\
k_{m2}\\
\vdots\end{bmatrix}

(28)

As shown in (
28
), the left matrix will be a diagonal matrix whereas the right matrix is a dense matrix. As both the matrices
[\text{X}_{LG}]
and
[\text{X}_{o}]
are symmetric with the Galerkin’s matching, the matrix
[\text{X}_{L}]
will also be symmetric. Therefore, it can be written that
\text{P}_{XL,in}=[\text{J}_{oi}]^{T}[\text{X}_{L}][\text{J}_{on}]=[\text{J}_{on}]^{T}[\text{X}_{L}][\text{J}_{oi}]=\text{P}_{XL,ni}
. With suitable rearrangement, (
28
) can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}(\text{P}_{XL,11}+\text{P}_{Xo,1})&\text{P}_{XL,12}&\ldots\\
\text{P}_{XL,12}&(\text{P}_{XL,22}+\text{P}_{Xo,2})&\ldots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ldots\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}k_{m1}\\
k_{m2}\\
\vdots\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{LG,m}\begin{bmatrix}\text{P}_{Ro,1}&0&\ldots\\
0&\text{P}_{Ro,2}&\ldots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ldots\\
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}k_{m1}\\
k_{m2}\\
\vdots\end{bmatrix}

(29)

It is to be noted here that both the left and right side matrices are real symmetric in (
29
). Therefore, it will produce a set of
N
real eigenvalues
\lambda_{LG,m}
and
N
number of eigenvectors, each of which will give the ground coupled mode
\text{J}_{LG,m}
following (
24
). The main contribution of (
29
) is that it gives a detailed understanding of how the isolated modes are gradually transformed into the ground coupled mode. Similar to
[
21
]
, the general formulation of (
29
) can be simplified with the closed-form solution for the first order approximation as shown in the next.

For the first order approximation, let
\text{J}_{LG,m}
be approximated as

\displaystyle\text{J}_{LG,m}=k_{1}\text{J}_{on_{1}}+k_{2}\text{J}_{on_{2}}.

(30)

Replacing (
30
) in (
13
), it will be

\displaystyle[\text{X}_{LG}](k_{1}\text{J}_{on_{1}}+k_{2}\text{J}_{on_{2}})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{LG,m}[\text{R}_{o}](k_{1}\text{J}_{on_{1}}+k_{2}\text{J}_{on_{2}}).

(31)

Taking inner product of (
31
) with
\text{J}_{on_{1}}
, it will be

\displaystyle k_{1}\text{P}_{Xo,n_{1}}+k_{1}\text{P}_{XL,n_{1}n_{1}}+k_{2}\text{P}_{XL,n_{1}n_{2}}=\lambda_{LG,m}k_{1}\text{P}_{Xo,n_{1}}.

(32)

The inner product of (
31
) with
\text{J}_{on_{2}}
provides

\displaystyle k_{1}\text{P}_{Xo,n_{1}}+k_{1}\text{P}_{XL,n_{1}n_{1}}+k_{2}\text{P}_{XL,n_{1}n_{2}}=\lambda_{LG,m}k_{1}\text{P}_{Xo,n_{1}}.

(33)

Rearrangement of (
32
) and (
33
) will lead to the following quadratic relation

\displaystyle ak_{n}^{2}+bk_{n}+c=0.

(34)

where

\displaystyle k_{n}=k_{2}/k_{1},~a=\text{P}_{Ro,n_{2}},~c=\text{P}_{Ro,n_{1}},

\displaystyle b=\frac{a(\text{P}_{Xo,n_{1}}+\text{P}_{XL,n_{1}n_{2}})-c(\text{P}_{Xo,n_{2}}+\text{P}_{XL,n_{2}n_{2}})}{\text{P}_{XL,n_{1}n_{2}}}.

(35)

Solution of (
34
) will be

\displaystyle k_{n}=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a}.

(36)

Two values of
k_{n}
will give two pairs of
[k_{1}~~k_{2}]
, which determine two modes of the dipole above the lossy ground plane. It can be seen in (
35
) that the parameters
a
and
c
depend only on the isolated dipole without the ground plane. The impact of the lossy ground is reflected in
b
throw the terms
\text{P}_{XL,n_{1}n_{1}}
,
\text{P}_{XL,n_{2}n_{2}}
, and
\text{P}_{XL,n_{1}n_{2}}
. The self-interactions due to the ground plane are indicated by
\text{P}_{XL,n_{1}n_{1}}
and
\text{P}_{XL,n_{2}n_{2}}
. Alternately, the mutual interaction of two modes of the isolated dipole due to the presence of the ground plane is represented by
\text{P}_{XL,n_{1}n_{2}}
. In the next section, the modes of the same dipole with the same excitation vector will be studied for different separation distances from the lossy dielectric half-space.

V
Conclusion

Following the limitation of the conventional characteristic mode analysis, the present work proposed a new eigenmode formulation for the vertical dipole lying above the lossy dielectric half-space. Three different case studies were considered for comparison-an isolated dipole, a vertical dipole above the infinite PEC ground plane, and a vertical dipole above the infinite lossy ground plane. A systematic blending of the theory of complex image and the theory of characteristic modes of PEC antennas provided the motivation of the proposed modal analysis. Later, a linear combination of the isolated modes were used to identify the underlying physics behind the impacts of the lossy ground on the modal distribution. The proposed theory can find various applications like in lightning field analysis, wireless propagation modelling where the radiating sources lie above the lossy ground plane.

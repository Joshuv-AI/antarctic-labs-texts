The Relation between the Optical Fe ii Emission and the Dimensionless Accretion Rate for Active Galactic Nuclei
Yan-Sheng Liu, Wei-Hao Bian

Abstract
It was suggested that the prominent feature of the optical Fe ii emission has a connection with the accretion process in active galactic nuclei (AGN). For a large sample of 4037 quasars ( z < 0.8 z<0.8 ) with measured H β \beta line dispersion ( σ H ​ β \sigma_{\rm H\beta} ) selected from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) and 120 compiled reverberation-mapped (RM) AGN, we use σ H ​ β \sigma_{\rm H\beta} and the extended R BLR ​ ( H ​ β ) − L 5100 R_{\rm BLR}({\rm H\beta})-L_{\rm 5100} relation to calculate supermassive black holes masses ( M BH M_{\rm BH} ) from the single-epoch spectra for the SDSS subsample, and σ H ​ β \sigma_{\rm H\beta} from the mean spectra for the RM subsample. We find a strong correlation between the relative optical Fe ii strength R Fe R_{\rm Fe} and ℳ ˙ \dot{\mathscr{M}} for the SDSS subsample with the Spearman correlation coefficient r s r_{s} of 0.727 0.727 , which is consistent with that derived from the mean spectra for the RM subsample. The magnitude of velocity shift of the optical Fe ii emission has a strong anticorrelation with ℳ ˙ \dot{\mathscr{M}} , whenever there is inflow or outflow. These strong correlations show that the optical Fe ii emission has an intimate connection with the accretion process. Assuming that the difference of M BH M_{\rm BH} is due to the variable virial factor f f for adopting FWHM H ​ β \rm FWHM_{H\beta} as the velocity tracer, we find that there is a relation between f f and FWHM H ​ β \rm FWHM_{H\beta} , log ⁡ f = − ( 0.41 ± 0.002 ) ​ log ⁡ FWHM H ​ β + ( 1.719 ± 0.009 ) \log f=-(0.41\pm 0.002)\rm\log FWHM_{H\beta}+(1.719\pm 0.009) for the single-epoch spectrum. The relation between log ⁡ f \log f and σ H ​ β \sigma_{\rm H\beta} is not too strong, suggesting that σ H ​ β \sigma_{\rm H\beta} does not seem to depend much on the broad-line region inclination and a constant σ \sigma -based f f is suitable for σ H ​ β \sigma_{\rm H\beta} as the velocity tracer.



I
Introduction

Fe
ii
emissions shown in the optical and ultraviolet (UV) spectra are prominent features in most active galactic nuclei
(
Boroson & Green 1992
;
Hu et al. 2008
;
Shen & Ho 2014
;
Zhao et al. 2020
, AGN; e.g.,)
. With the principal component analysis of a low-
z
sample of 87 Palomar–Green quasars,
Boroson & Green 1992
found that principal component 1 (PC1) is related to the relative strength of optical Fe
ii
to the broad H
\beta
(
R_{\rm Fe}
, the ratio between the strength of Fe
ii
emission within
4434-4684
Å and the broad H
\beta
), the FWHM of the broad H
\beta
(
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
), [O
iii
]5007 strength, and principal component 2 (PC2) links optical luminosity and
\alpha_{\rm ox}
(optical-X-ray spectral index).
PC1 and PC2 have relations with the accretion process around the supermassive black holes (SMBHs). With
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
-based SMBH masses (
M_{\rm BH}
),
Boroson 2002
suggested that PC1 is mainly correlated with the Eddington ratio (
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
,
L_{\rm Bol}
is the bolometric luminosity and
L_{\rm Edd}
is the Eddington luminosity) and PC2 has a strong connection with
M_{\rm BH}
and
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
.
Using a large sample of the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) quasars,
Hu et al. 2008
did a detailed line-fitting technique and found the connections between the optical Fe
ii
strength, velocity shift, and the Eddington ratio.
Using a two-dimensional plane of
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
and
R_{\rm Fe}
for the SDSS quasars,
Shen & Ho 2014
suggested that the Eddington ratio is also a key factor to be considered in the orientation-based unification of quasar phenomenology
(
Urry & Padovani 1995
)
.

In order to investigate the relation between Fe
ii
features and the accretion process,
M_{\rm BH}
is a key parameter needing to be determined. With effort over nearly two decades, the reverberation-mapping (RM) method with the H
\beta
spectral monitoring has been successfully applied for about 120 AGN
(
Blandford & McKee 1982
;
Peterson et al. 2004
;
Du et al. 2016a
;
Grier et al. 2017
;
Yu et al. 2020a
;
Yu et al. 2020b
;
Hu et al. 2021
, e.g.,)
. The RM method can measure the time lag between the line variation ( e.g., H
\beta
, Mg
ii
, C
iv
) and the corresponding continuum variation, and give the broad-line region (BLR) distance from the central SMBH (
R_{\rm BLR}
). For type 1 AGN, the BLR clouds emitting the broad H
\beta
line can be used as a probe to calculate the virial mass
(
Peterson et al. 2004
;
Netzer 2013
, e.g.,)
:

M_{\rm BH}=f\times\frac{R_{\rm BLR}~(\Delta V)^{2}}{G}.

(1)

where
\Delta V
is the velocity of the BLR clouds,
f
is a corresponding virial factor, and
G
is the gravitational constant. Through the RM AGN, an empirical
R_{\rm BLR}-L_{5100}
relation (
L_{\rm 5100}
is the 5100 Å monochromatic luminosity,
R_{\rm BLR}\propto L_{5100}^{0.5}
) was given and used to estimate
R_{\rm BLR}
from a single-epoch spectrum
(
Kaspi et al. 2000
;
Bentz et al. 2013
;
Du et al. 2016b
;
Dalla Bontà et al. 2020
;
Khadka et al. 2022
;
Maithil et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
.
The changes of the spectral energy distribution, connected to changes in the accretion rate, would likely lead to the breadth (an increment in the scatter) in the empirical
R_{\rm BLR}-L_{5100}
relation in the first-order consideration
(
Wang et al. 2014
;
Martínez-Aldama et al. 2019
;
Du & Wang 2019
;
Yu et al. 2020a
;
Maithil et al. 2022
)
.
AGN with strong Fe
ii
emission show a smaller
R_{\rm BLR}
than expected
(
Du & Wang 2019
, e.g.,)
. Since
R_{\rm Fe}
has a relation with the accretion rate
(
Netzer & Trakhtenbrot 2007
;
Du et al. 2016a
;
Yu et al. 2020a
;
Maithil et al. 2022
, e.g.,)
, an extended
R_{\rm BLR}({\rm H\beta})-L_{\rm 5100}
relation (
R_{\rm BLR}\propto L_{5100}^{0.5}+R_{\rm Fe}
) was recently suggested

(
Du & Wang 2019
;
Yu et al. 2020a
;
Khadka et al. 2022
)
.
The reduction in the scatter in the extended
R_{\rm BLR}({\rm H\beta})-L_{\rm 5100}
relation
(
Yu et al. 2020a
)
is in agreement with the one (
\sim 0.13
dex) previously reported by
Kilerci Eser et al. 2015
, who used the UV luminosity instead of
L_{5100}
as a substitute for the ionizing luminosity in the empirical
R_{\rm BLR}({\rm H\beta})-L_{\rm 5100}
relation. The extended
R_{\rm BLR}({\rm H\beta})-L_{\rm 5100}
relation would result in a better measurement of
M_{\rm BH}
.

For the RM AGN,
\Delta V
is usually traced by four kinds of velocities, i.e., the broad H
\beta
FWHM or the line dispersion (
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
) measured from the mean/rms spectrum
(
Peterson et al. 2004
;
Ho & Kim 2014
;
Yu et al. 2020b
, e.g.,)
. From Equation
1
,
f=M_{\rm BH}/(R_{\rm BLR}(\Delta V)^{2}/G)
. For four kinds of velocities
\Delta V
, the corresponding four kinds of virial factors
f
are defined, which are usually calibrated through the
M_{\rm BH}-\sigma_{\ast}
relation (
\sigma_{*}
is the bulge stellar velocity dispersion) or other independent methods to derive the SMBH masses
(
Onken et al. 2004
;
Ho & Kim 2014
;
Yu et al. 2020b
, e.g.,)
. For several multiple RM AGN, it was found
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
is better than
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
used to calculate
M_{\rm BH}
with a constant factor
f

(
Peterson et al. 2004
)
. For the 120 RM AGN sample
(
Yu et al. 2020b
)
, with respect to
M_{\rm BH}
from
\sigma_{*}
or
\sigma_{\rm H\beta,rms}
, it was found that we can obtain
M_{\rm BH}
from
\sigma_{\rm H\beta,mean}
with the smallest scatter than from
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
.

Dalla Bontà et al. 2020
also suggested that the use of
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
gives better results, while the use of
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
introduces a bias, stretching the mass scale such that high masses are overestimated and low masses are underestimated, although both velocity tracers are usable.
A variable
\rm FWHM
-based
f
was suggested
(
Collin et al. 2006
;
Mejía-Restrepo et al. 2018
;
Yu et al. 2019
;
Yu et al. 2020b
)
.
The cumulative fraction of
f
was consistent with the a simple model of thick-diskBLRs, which implied that, as the tracer of BLRs velocity,
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
has some dependence on the BLRs inclination, while
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
is insensitive to the inclination
(
Collin et al. 2006
;
Yu et al. 2019
)
.
For AGN with the single-epoch spectrum,
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
or
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
is used to trace
\Delta V
, which was used in large surveys, such as SDSS
(
Shen et al. 2011
)
.
With respect to the H
\beta
FWHM,
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
was preferred to calculate
M_{\rm BH}
from the single-epoch spectrum
(
Yu et al. 2020b
)
. With Equation
1
,
M_{\rm BH}=f_{\sigma}\times\sigma_{\rm H\beta}^{2}R_{\rm BLR}({\rm H\beta})/G=f_{\rm FWHM}\times{\rm FWHM}_{H\beta}^{2}R_{\rm BLR}({\rm H\beta})/G
.
Considering a variable
f_{\rm FWHM}
in
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
-based
M_{\rm BH}
, it means

f_{\rm FWHM}=f_{\sigma}(\sigma_{\rm H\beta}/\rm FWHM_{H\beta})^{2}.

(2)

About the accretion strength, there are mainly two parameters, the Eddington ratio
L_{\rm Bol}
/
L_{\rm Edd}
and the dimensionless accretion rate
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
, where
\dot{\mathscr{M}}\equiv\dot{M}/\dot{M}_{\rm Edd}
,
\dot{M}_{\rm Edd}=L_{\rm Edd}/c^{2}
. The accretion rate
\dot{M}
can be derived from the disk model of
Shakura & Sunyaev 1973
, which has been extensively applied to fit the spectra of AGN
(
Collin et al. 2002
;
Bian & Zhao 2003
;
Davis & Laor 2011
;
Mejía-Restrepo et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
. Considering that the radius
R
distribution of the effective disk temperature is given by
T_{\rm eff}\propto R^{-3/4}
,
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
is
(
Du et al. 2016a
, e.g.,)

\dot{\mathscr{M}}\equiv\dot{M}/\dot{M}_{\rm Edd}=20.1\left(\frac{l_{44}}{\cos\,\it{i}}\right)^{3/2}m_{7}^{-2}.

(3)

where
l_{44}=L_{5100}/10^{44}{\rm erg\ s}^{-1}
,
m_{7}=M_{\rm BH}/10^{7}M_{\odot}
. An average value of
{\rm cos}~i=0.75
is adopted, which corresponds to the opening angle of the dusty torus
(
Davis & Laor 2011
;
Du et al. 2016a
, e.g.,)
.
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
is inversely proportional to
M_{\rm BH}
to the second power. This Equation applies to AGN that have
\log(\dot{\mathscr{M}}/M_{\odot})
between -2 and 3.5, namely excluding the regimes of advection-dominated accretion flows (
\log(\dot{\mathscr{M}}/M_{\odot})<-2
) and of flows with hyperaccretion rates (
\log(\dot{\mathscr{M}}/M_{\odot})>3.5
).
Using FWHM-based
M_{\rm BH}
and
L_{\rm Bol}
/
L_{\rm Edd}
for 63 RM AGN,
Du et al. 2016a
found
R_{\rm Fe}
correlated with
L_{\rm Bol}
/
L_{\rm Edd}
and
\dot{\mathscr{M}}

(
Netzer & Trakhtenbrot 2007
;
Hu et al. 2008
;
Maithil et al. 2022
, also see)
. They also suggested the BLRs ”fundamental plane”, i.e., a strong bivariate correlation of
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
with
R_{\rm Fe}
and the H
\beta
shape
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}/\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
.

In this paper, we use a large sample of 4037 SDSS quasars
(
Hu et al. 2008
,
z<0.8
;)
with measured broad H
\beta
line dispersion
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
and
R_{\rm Fe}
to investigate again the relation between Fe
ii
emission and the accretion process, as well as a subsample of 120 RM AGN. About the accretion strength, we use
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
instead of
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
. We also derive the relation between
f_{\rm FWHM}
and
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
for the single-epoch spectrum. This paper is organized as follows. Section 2 presents the adopted samples and data analysis. Section 3 is our results and discussions. Section 4 is our conclusions. All of the cosmological calculations in this paper assume
\Omega_{\Lambda}=0.7
,
\Omega_{\rm M}=0.3
, and
H_{0}=70~{\rm km\ s}^{-1}{\rm Mpc}^{-1}
.

From the general form of the equation of motion for a cloud of mass
M_{c}
, the acceleration
a(r)=a_{\rm rad}(r)-g(r)-\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{dP}{dr}+f_{d}/M_{c}

(
Netzer 2013
)
, where
g(r)
is the gravitational acceleration,
a_{\rm rad}(r)
is the acceleration due to radiation pressure force, and
f_{d}
is the drag force;
P
is the thermal pressure [
P=\rho v_{s}^{2}
, where
v_{s}=(2k_{B}T/\mu m_{\rm H})^{1/2}\sim 16.6(T/10^{4}K)^{1/2}{\rm km\ s}^{-1}
,
\mu m_{\rm H}
is the average mass per particle, and
\mu=0.6
]. Neglecting the gas pressure gradient and the drag force for pure wind flows,
a(r)=a_{\rm rad}(r)-g(r)\approx\frac{\sigma_{T}L}{4\pi r^{2}\mu m_{H}c}[M(r)-\frac{L_{\rm Edd}}{L}]=\frac{G}{r^{2}}M_{\rm BH}[M(r)\frac{L}{L_{\rm Edd}}-1]
.
L
is the total luminosity, and approximately equals
L_{\rm Bol}
.
M(r)
is force multiplier, which is the ratio of the total radiation pressure to the radiation pressure due to Compton scattering.
M(r)\geq 1
, and
M(r)=1
for fully ionized gas. Assuming the Fe
ii
clouds falls down from torus (at
r_{1}
) with an initial
v_{0}=0
to Fe
ii
region at
r_{2}
(
\sim

R_{\rm BLR}({\rm H\beta})
), for the cases of constant
[M(r)\frac{L}{L_{\rm Edd}}-1]
, we get

v^{2}=2GM_{\rm BH}(1-M\frac{L}{L_{\rm Edd}})(\frac{1}{r_{2}}-\frac{1}{r_{1}})
.
Assuming the size of the innermost part of the torus is
r_{1}=nr_{2}
, where
n\sim 3-4

(
Netzer 2013
)
, and
M_{\rm BH}=f\rm FWHM_{H\beta}^{2}r_{2}/G
, we get

v_{\rm Fe}^{2}\approx 2\frac{n-1}{n}f{\rm FWHM^{2}_{H\beta}}(1-M(r)\frac{L}{L_{\rm Edd}})
. The force multiplier
M(r)
was found to be inversely correlated with the ionization parameter
(
Chelouche & Netzr 2005
)
. Assuming
M(r)=M_{0}(L/L_{\rm Edd})^{-\alpha}
, we get
v_{\rm Fe}^{2}\approx 2\frac{n-1}{n}f{\rm FWHM^{2}_{H\beta}}[1-M_{0}(L/L_{\rm Edd})^{1-\alpha}]
.
A smaller
v
would be found for AGN with larger
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
, and smaller
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}

(
Hu et al. 2008
, also see Figure 9 in )
.

However, we find that it is not the case for the blueward shift of Fe
ii
, i.e., a smaller velocity of Fe
ii
outflow for larger
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
. The reason for the relation between the blueward velocity
v_{\rm Fe}
and
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
is not clear.

IV
Conclusions

For a large sample of 4037 SDSS quasars (
z<0.8
) with measured
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
and
R_{\rm Fe}
and 120 compiled RM AGN, we use
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
instead of
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
and the extended
R_{\rm BLR}({\rm H\beta})-L_{\rm 5100}
relation (including
R_{\rm Fe}
) to calculate
M_{\rm BH}
from the single-epoch spectra for the SDSS subsample, and
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
from the mean spectra for the RM subsample. We use
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
instead of
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
to indicate the accretion strength to investigate the relation with optical Fe
ii
emission (
R_{\rm Fe}
and
v_{\rm Fe}
). We also derive the relation between the virial factor
f
and
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
for the single-epoch spectrum.
The main conclusions can be summarized as follows:

•

For the subsample of 4037 SDSS quasars, with respect to
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
,
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
-based
M_{\rm BH}
are averagely larger by
0.25
dex;
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
are averagely smaller by
0.50
dex. There exists a nonlinear relationship between
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
and
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
,
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}\propto\dot{\mathscr{M}}^{0.52}
. For the subsample of 120 RM AGN, the results are similar.
With respect to
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
,
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
-based
M_{\rm BH}
(from the mean spectrum) for the RM subsample are averagely larger by
0.23
dex,
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
are averagely smaller by
0.43
dex, and
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}\propto\dot{\mathscr{M}}^{0.56}
. This relation of
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
with
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
can be derived assuming
L_{5100}
is calculated from the standard accretion disk model and
L_{\rm Bol}
is proportional to
L_{5100}
.

•

Adopting
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}\propto\dot{\mathscr{M}}^{0.5}L_{5100}^{0.25}
, it means that the converting efficiency
\eta\propto\dot{\mathscr{M}}^{-0.5}L_{5100}^{0.25}\propto(L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd})^{-1}L_{5100}^{0.5}
. The nonlinear relation implies that
\eta
is anticorrelated with
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
or
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
(including a relatively weaker dependence on
L_{5100}
), i.e., smaller efficiency with higher dimensionless accretion rate. The efficiency is related to the SMBH spin.

•

For the subsample of 4037 SDSS quasars, a strong relation between
R_{\rm Fe}
and
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
is found:
R_{\rm Fe}=-(0.936\pm 0.006)+(0.600\pm 0.008)\log\dot{\mathscr{M}}
with the Spearman correlation coefficient
r_{s}
of
0.727
. For the subsample of 120 RM AGN, the results are similar with a larger scatter;
R_{\rm Fe}=(0.713\pm 0.047)+(0.532\pm 0.066)\log\dot{\mathscr{M}}
with
r_{s}=0.423
. With respect to [O
iii
]
\lambda
5007, the velocity shift
v_{\rm Fe}
has an strong anticorrelation with
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
, whenever inflow (
r_{s}=0.612
) or outflow (
r_{s}=-0.419
). These strong correlations show that the optical Fe
ii
emission has an intimate connection with the SMBH accretion process.

•

Assuming the Fe
ii
clouds fall down from torus with an initial
v_{0}=0
, we get the inflow velocity of the optical Fe
ii

v_{\rm Fe}^{2}\propto{\rm FWHM^{2}_{H\beta}}(1-M(r)\frac{L}{L_{\rm Edd}})
. A smaller
v
would be found for AGN with larger
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
, smaller
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
. It is qualitatively consistent with the relation of the inflow velocity
v_{\rm Fe}
with
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
and
L_{\rm Bol}/L_{\rm Edd}
or
\dot{\mathscr{M}}
.

•

Assuming a variable
f
in
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
-based
M_{\rm BH}
from the single-epoch spectrum, we find a strong correlation between
\log f
and
\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
,
\log f=(1.719\pm 0.028)-(0.414\pm 0.008)\rm FWHM_{H\beta}
. It is flatter than that derived from the mean spectrum for the RM subsample or 17 RM AGN with measured
\sigma_{\rm\ast}
. However, the relation between
\log f
and
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
is not too strong, suggesting that
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
doesn’t seem dependent much on the BLR inclination and a constant
f_{\sigma}
is suitable for
\sigma_{\rm H\beta}
as the velocity tracer.

Analytic solutions for the motion of spinning particles near spherically symmetric black holes and exotic compact objects Preprint: APS/123-QED
Vojtěch Witzany, Gabriel Andres Piovano

Abstract
Rapidly rotating bodies moving in curved space-time experience the so-called spin-curvature force, which becomes important for the motion of compact objects in gravitational-wave inspirals. As a first approximation, this effect is captured in the motion of a spinning test particle. We solve the equations motion of a spinning particle to leading order in spin in arbitrary static and spherically symmetric space-times in terms of one-dimensional closed-form integrals. This solves the problem and proves its integrability in a wide range of modified gravities and near exotic compact objects. Then, by specializing to the case of bound orbits in Schwarzschild space-time, we demonstrate how to express the solution in the form of Jacobi elliptic functions.

Introduction.
–
After a track record of spectacular successes in their first three observing runs
Abbott et al. 2019
;
Abbott et al. 2021
;
Abbott et al. 2023
, in May 2023 the LIGO and Virgo instruments were joined for the first time by the Japanese detector KAGRA to start the fourth run of observing coalescing compact objects through gravitational waves
Abbott et al. 2018
. The so-called LVK collaboration is expected to detect more events in this run than have been amassed to date. Next-generation detectors on Earth and in space promise to multiply the sensitivity and reach of the detectors even further
Kalogera et al. 2021
;
LISA Consortium 2017
. As the number of events grow, we will observe binaries with different mass ranges, mass ratios, and dynamical setups and, consequently, we will need a more faithful and complete picture of the two-body dynamics in order to detect and interpret these signals correctly.

In particular, the space-based observatory Laser Interferometer Space Antenna (LISA) will detect inspirals of stellar-mass compact objects (henceforth dubbed as secondary) onto supermassive black holes
Babak et al. 2017
. This will allow to accurately test whether the supermassive objects in the centres of galaxies are truly black holes or perhaps some exotic compact objects, or whether their gravitational field is described by Einstein gravity
Arun et al. 2022
. These so-called extreme-mass-ratio inspirals (EMRIs) are best modelled in a mass-ratio expansion
Barack and Pound 2019
. At leading order, the two-body dynamics is approximated by the adiabatic inspiral of a test particle in the space-time of the massive compact object, where the inspiral is driven by fluxes sourced by the secondary
Hughes et al. 2021
;
Hughes et al. 2005
.
Subleading corrections to the motion are collectively called post-1-adiabatic, and include the effects of second-order in the mass-ratio fluxes, conservative self-force, and the spin of the smaller companion
van de Meent 2018
;
Warburton et al. 2021
;
Wardell et al. 2023
.
Post-1-adiabatic corrections are fundamental to model waveform suited for data analysis for LISA
Babak et al. 2017
;
Barack et al. 2019
, and the spin of the secondary plays a crucial role
Huerta et al. 2012
;
Huerta and Gair 2011
;
Piovano et al. 2021
.
The effects of the latter are fully captured by the motion of a spinning test particle in the space-time of the massive compact object (computed to linear order to spin), and the outgoing gravitational-wave flux it sources
Warburton et al. 2017
;
Akcay et al. 2020
;
Mathews et al. 2022
;
Skoupý and Lukes-Gerakopoulos 2021
;
Skoupý and Lukes-Gerakopoulos 2022
;
Skoupý et al. 2023
;
Piovano et al. 2020
;
Piovano et al. 2023
;
Drummond et al. 2023
. Here we present an analytical solution of this motion near spherically symmetric compact objects in terms of quadratures. In particular, our analysis implies that the spinning particle motion is integrable at linear order in spin in any static, spherically symmetric space-time. We then specialize to Schwarzschild space-time and re-express the solution in terms of Jacobi elliptic integrals. Even though a number of works have treated this and similar topics previously (see, e.g., Refs
Rietdijk and van
Holten 1993
;
Suzuki and Maeda 1998
;
Jefremov et al. 2015
;
Zelenka et al. 2020
;
Drummond and Hughes 2022a
;
Drummond et al. 2023
), this is the first time an analytic, closed-form solution of generic bound motion of a spinning test particle in Schwarzschild space-time is presented.

We use the
G=c=1
geometrized units and the (-+++) signature of the metric. Greek indices label coordinate components. The Riemann tensor is defined by
a_{\nu;\kappa\lambda}-a_{\nu;\lambda\kappa}\equiv R^{\mu}{}_{\nu\kappa\lambda}a_{\mu}
, where
;
denotes the covariant derivative and
a_{\mu}
is an arbitrary form.

Spinning particle.
–
The motion of a spinning particle is given by the equations of motion
Mathisson 1937
;
Papapetrou 1951
;
Dixon 1964

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{D}P^{\mu}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}=-\frac{1}{2}R^{\mu}{}_{\nu\kappa\lambda}\dot{x}^{\nu}S^{\kappa\lambda}\,,

(1)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{D}S^{\mu\nu}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}=P^{\mu}\dot{x}^{\nu}-P^{\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}\,,

(2)

where
P^{\mu}
and
\dot{x}^{\nu}
are the 4-momentum and tangent vector to the worldline of the spinning test-particle, while
S^{\mu\nu}=-S^{\nu\mu}
is the spin tensor.
We are only interested in the dynamics to linear order in spin
S^{\mu\nu}
and, unless specified, all formulas are to be assumed at most
\mathcal{O}(S)
accurate. This is justified by considering a comparably light compact object in the field of a heavy black hole, since the truncated terms can be shown to scale as higher order in the mass ratio, as discussed in the Supplemental material
Sup
. We fix the relation of the centroid
x^{\mu}(\tau)
and the momentum
P^{\mu}
by the Tulczyjew-Dixon supplemental spin condition
S^{\mu\nu}P_{\nu}=0
, to obtain
P^{\mu}=m\dot{x}^{\mu}+\mathcal{O}(S^{2})
where
m
is the particle mass
Tulczyjew 1959
;
Dixon 1964
;
Semerák 1999
. The spin tensor can then be expressed as
S^{\mu\nu}=m\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}\dot{x}_{\kappa}s_{\lambda}/2
, where
s^{\mu}
is the specific spin vector and
\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}
the Levi-Civita pseudo-tensor. The equations then reduce to

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{D}^{2}x^{\mu}}{\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}}=-\frac{1}{4}{R}^{\mu}{}_{\nu\gamma\delta}\varepsilon^{\gamma\delta}{}_{\kappa\lambda}\dot{x}^{\nu}\dot{x}^{\kappa}s^{\lambda}\,,

(3)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{D}s^{\lambda}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}=0\,.

(4)

Motion in static, spherically symmetric metric.
–
A general static spherically symmetric spacetime metric can be locally expressed in the form

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-f(r)\mathrm{d}t^{2}+h(r)\mathrm{d}r^{2}+r^{2}\left(\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}+\sin\theta^{2}\mathrm{d}\phi^{2}\right)\,.

(5)

The Schwarzschild metric is contained within this class by setting
f(r),h(r)
to
f(r)=1/h(r)=1-2M/r
. The Killing vectors of this metric are generators of time translations and rotations around the
x,y,z
axes:

\displaystyle\xi_{(t)}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\,,

(6)

\displaystyle\xi_{(x)}=-\sin\phi\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}-\cos\phi\cot\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\,,

(7)

\displaystyle\xi_{(y)}=\cos\phi\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}-\sin\phi\cot\theta\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\,,

(8)

\displaystyle\xi_{(z)}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\,.

(9)

Dixon
Dixon 1970
showed that for any Killing vector the spinning particle motion has a constant of the form
C_{(\xi)}=P^{\mu}\xi_{\mu}-\xi_{\rho;\sigma}S^{\rho\sigma}/2
. In this case the Killing vectors correspond to a conserved specific spin-orbital energy and a formal angular momentum vector (we normalize each
C_{(\xi)}
by
m
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=-f\dot{t}+\frac{r^{2}\sin\theta f^{\prime}s^{\phi}\dot{\theta}-s^{\theta}\dot{\phi}}{2\sqrt{fh}}\,,

(10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{J}_{x}=-r^{2}(\sin\phi\,\dot{\theta}+\cos\phi\cos\theta\sin\theta\,\dot{\phi})\\
&\phantom{\mathcal{J}_{x}=}+\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}\Big[\sin\theta\cos\phi\,h(s^{t}\dot{r}-s^{r}\dot{t})\\
&\phantom{\mathcal{J}_{x}=}+r\sin\phi\sin\theta(s^{\phi}\dot{t}-s^{t}\dot{\phi})\\
&\phantom{\mathcal{J}_{x}=}+r\cos\phi\cos\theta(s^{t}\dot{\theta}-s^{\theta}\dot{t})\Big]\,,\end{split}

(11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{J}_{y}=r^{2}(\cos\phi\,\dot{\theta}-\sin\phi\cos\theta\sin\theta\,\dot{\phi})\\
&\phantom{\mathcal{J}_{x}=}+\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}\Big[\sin\theta\sin\phi\,h(s^{t}\dot{r}-s^{r}\dot{t})\\
&\phantom{\mathcal{J}_{x}=}+r\cos\phi\sin\theta(s^{t}\dot{\phi}-s^{\phi}\dot{t})\\
&\phantom{\mathcal{J}_{x}=}+r\sin\phi\cos\theta(s^{t}\dot{\theta}-s^{\theta}\dot{t})\Big]\,,\end{split}

(12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{J}_{z}=r^{2}\sin^{2}\!\theta\,\dot{\phi}\\
&\phantom{\mathcal{J}_{x}=}+\sqrt{\frac{f}{h}}\Big[\cos\theta\,h(s^{t}\dot{r}-s^{r}\dot{t})\\
&\phantom{\mathcal{J}_{x}=}+r\sin\theta(s^{\theta}\dot{t}-s^{t}\dot{\theta})\Big]\,.\end{split}

(13)

The linearisation in spin means that these integrals of motion are conserved up to
\mathcal{O}(s^{2})
. Another integral of motion is obtained by noticing that the following vector is parallel-transported along geodesics in general spherically symmetric static space-times:

\displaystyle l=\frac{r\dot{\theta}}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}-r\sin\theta\dot{\phi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\,,

(14)

and for spinning particles
Dl^{\mu}/\mathrm{d}\tau=\mathcal{O}(s)
. As a result, the aligned component of the spin vector is an approximate constant of motion

\displaystyle s_{\parallel}\equiv\frac{l^{\mu}s_{\mu}}{\sqrt{l^{\nu}l_{\nu}}}\,,\;\frac{\mathrm{d}s_{\parallel}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}=0+\mathcal{O}(s^{2})\,.

(15)

Angular momentum aligned coordinates.
–
For general initial conditions, the formal angular-momentum vector
\vec{\mathcal{J}}=(\mathcal{J}_{x},\mathcal{J}_{y},\mathcal{J}_{z})
keeps pointing into a constant direction. Additionally, the orbital plane is always almost orthogonal to this vector up to
\mathcal{O}(s)
corrections.
Hence, without loss of generality, we can rotate into a new coordinate system
\theta,\phi\to\vartheta,\varphi
such that its axis of
\varphi
-rotation (
\vartheta=0,\pi
) points in the direction of
\vec{\mathcal{J}}
. When defined with respect to this new system, we obtain
\mathcal{J}_{x}^{\prime}=\mathcal{J}_{y}^{\prime}=0,\mathcal{J}_{z}^{\prime}=\sqrt{\mathcal{J}_{x}^{2}+\mathcal{J}_{y}^{2}+\mathcal{J}_{z}^{2}}\equiv\mathcal{J}
, and the position of the particle will fulfil
\vartheta(\tau)=\pi/2+\delta\vartheta(\tau)
, where

\displaystyle\delta\vartheta=\frac{\sqrt{fh}(s^{r}\dot{t}-s^{t}\dot{r})}{r^{2}\dot{\varphi}}+\mathcal{O}(s^{2})\,.

(16)

In other words, the
\vartheta
-motion of the particle is automatically expressed in terms of the other variables. This transformation was already implicitly used in the numerical studies of Refs
Suzuki and Maeda 1998
;
Zelenka et al. 2019
, even though the authors did not realise it also allows to analytically solve for the
\vartheta
degree of freedom at
\mathcal{O}(s)
. More details of the computation can be found in the Supplemental material.

The equations of motion for the other orbital variables
r(\tau),t(\tau),\varphi(\tau)
can now be expressed in first-order form up to
\mathcal{O}(s^{2})

\displaystyle\dot{r}^{2}=\frac{1}{h}\left(-1+\frac{\mathcal{E}^{2}}{f}-\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)+\frac{s_{\parallel}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{J}(2f-rf^{\prime})}{(fh)^{3/2}r^{2}}\,,

(17)

\displaystyle\dot{t}=\frac{\mathcal{E}}{f}+\frac{s_{\parallel}\mathcal{J}f^{\prime}}{2fr\sqrt{fh}}\,,

(18)

\displaystyle\dot{\varphi}=\frac{\mathcal{J}}{r^{2}}+\frac{s_{\parallel}\mathcal{E}}{r^{2}\sqrt{fh}}\,.

(19)

Solving parallel transport.
–
As for the spin degree of freedom, we need to solve the parallel transport equation at leading (geodesic) order. To do so, we construct a parallel-transported tetrad
e^{\mu}_{(A)},A=0,...,3
inspired by Marck
Marck 1983
.
We start with the zeroth and third leg (component order
t,r,\varphi,\vartheta
)

\displaystyle e^{\mu}_{(0)}=\left(\mathcal{E}/f,\dot{r},\mathcal{J}/r^{2},0\right)\,,

(20)

\displaystyle e^{\mu}_{(3)}=\left(0,0,0,-\frac{1}{r}\right)\,,

(21)

where
\dot{r}
is given by eq. (
17
). Note that the full expression for
\dot{r}
including
\mathcal{O}(s)
corrections avoids singularities in the definition of the tetrad at radial turning points.
Now the first and second legs are given as
e^{\mu}_{(1)}=\cos\psi\,\tilde{e}^{\mu}_{(1)}+\sin\psi\,\tilde{e}^{\mu}_{(2)}
and
e^{\mu}_{(2)}=-\sin\psi\,\tilde{e}^{\mu}_{(1)}+\cos\psi\,\tilde{e}^{\mu}_{(2)}
, where

\displaystyle\tilde{e}^{\mu}_{(1)}=\left(\frac{\dot{r}r\sqrt{h}}{\sqrt{f(\mathcal{J}^{2}+r^{2})}},\frac{\mathcal{E}r}{\sqrt{fh(\mathcal{J}^{2}+r^{2})}},0,0\right)\,,

(22)

\displaystyle\tilde{e}^{\mu}_{(2)}=\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}\mathcal{J}}{f\sqrt{\mathcal{J}^{2}+r^{2}}},\frac{\mathcal{J}\dot{r}}{r},\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{J}^{2}+r^{2}}}{r^{2}},0\right)\,,

(23)

and the precession angle
\psi(\tau)
is obtained by integrating

\displaystyle\dot{\psi}=\frac{\mathcal{E}\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{J}^{2}+r^{2}}\,.

(24)

The components of the spin with respect to the parallel-transported tetrad
e^{\mu}_{(A)}
are constant at leading order. Additionally, since the spin magnitude
s\equiv\sqrt{s^{\mu}s_{\mu}}
is conserved and
s_{\mu}e^{\mu}_{(3)}=s_{\parallel}+\mathcal{O}(s^{2})
, the solution for the evolution of the spin vector can be expressed as

\displaystyle s^{t}=\sqrt{\frac{s^{2}-s_{\parallel}^{2}}{f(\mathcal{J}^{2}+r^{2})}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{E\mathcal{J}}\cos\psi}{\sqrt{f}}+\dot{r}r\sin\psi\right)\,,

(25a)

\displaystyle s^{r}=\sqrt{s^{2}-s_{\parallel}^{2}}\left(\frac{\mathcal{J}\dot{r}\cos\psi}{r}+\frac{\mathcal{E}r\sin\psi}{\sqrt{fh(\mathcal{J}^{2}+r^{2})}}\right)\,,

(25b)

\displaystyle s^{\varphi}=\frac{\sqrt{(s^{2}-s_{\parallel}^{2})(\mathcal{J}^{2}+r^{2})}\cos\psi}{r^{2}}\,,

(25c)

\displaystyle s^{\vartheta}=-\frac{s_{\parallel}}{r}\,.

(25d)

One can now also re-express
\delta\vartheta
from eq. (
16
) as

\displaystyle\delta\vartheta=\frac{\sqrt{(s^{2}-s_{\parallel}^{2})(\mathcal{J}^{2}+r^{2})}\sin\psi}{\mathcal{J}r}\,.

(26)

Solution by quadrature.
–
The motion of the spinning test particle in any static, spherically symmetric metric is solved by quadrature as follows. First one integrates

\displaystyle\tau(r)-\tau(r_{0})=\pm\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{R}(r^{\prime})}}\,,

(27)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(r)\equiv\frac{1}{h}\left(-1+\frac{\mathcal{E}^{2}}{f}-\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)+\frac{s_{\parallel}\mathcal{E}\mathcal{J}(2f-rf^{\prime})}{(fh)^{3/2}r^{2}}\,,

(28)

and inverts this relation to obtain
r(\tau)
. This is then substituted into the
r
-parametrized solutions

\displaystyle t(r)-t(r_{0})=\pm\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{R}(r^{\prime})}}\left[\frac{\mathcal{E}}{f}+\frac{s_{\parallel}\mathcal{J}f^{\prime}}{2fr^{\prime}\sqrt{fh}}\right],

(29)

\displaystyle\varphi(r)-\varphi(r_{0})=\pm\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{R}(r^{\prime})}}\left[\frac{\mathcal{J}}{{r^{\prime}}^{2}}+\frac{s_{\parallel}\mathcal{E}}{{r^{\prime}}^{2}\sqrt{fh}}\right],

(30)

\displaystyle\psi(r)-\psi(r_{0})=\pm\int_{r_{0}}^{r}\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}}{\sqrt{\mathcal{R}(r^{\prime})}}\left[\frac{\mathcal{E}\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{J}^{2}+r^{\prime 2}}\right].

(31)

The aligned coordinate position
\vartheta=\pi/2+\delta\vartheta
and the spin components
s^{\mu}
are then obtained by substituting the solutions for
r(\tau)
and
\psi(\tau)
into equations (
25
) and (
26
).

Schwarzschild space-time.
–
Let us demonstrate how to use this solution near a Schwarzschild black hole by setting
f=1/h=1-2M/r
. We focus on bound motion between radial turning points
r_{1}>r_{2}
which are real roots of
\mathcal{R}(r)
. Following Darwin’s treatment of geodesics
Darwin 1959
, we parametrize the motion by eccentricity
e
and semi-latus rectum
p
such that
r_{1}=p/(1-e),\,r_{2}=p/(1+e)
. The relation between energy, angular momentum and the orbital elements
e,p
is obtained by inserting the geodesic relations from Darwin into the equation
\mathcal{R}(r)=0
and computing the spin corrections

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{E}^{2}=\frac{(p-2M)^{2}-4M^{2}e^{2}}{p\left[p-M(3+e^{2})\right]}\\
&+s_{\parallel}\frac{(e^{2}-1)^{2}M\sqrt{Mp\left[p^{2}-4Mp-4M^{2}(e^{2}-1)\right]}}{p^{2}\left[p-M(3+e^{2})\right]^{2}},\end{split}

(32)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathcal{J}^{2}=\frac{Mp^{2}}{p-M(3+e^{2})}\\
&-s_{\parallel}\frac{\left[2p-3M(3+e^{2})\right]\sqrt{Mp\left[p^{2}-4Mp-4M^{2}(e^{2}-1)\right]}}{\left[p-M(3+e^{2})\right]^{2}}.\end{split}

(33)

Interestingly, these shifts exactly agree with those for a particle with spin fully aligned with orbital angular momentum when
s_{\parallel}=s
(cf. Refs
(
Drummond and Hughes 2022b
;
Mukherjee and Tripathy 2020
)
). The bound motion exists for
e\in[0,1)
and
p\in(p_{\rm c}(e),\infty)
, where the last stable orbits
p_{\rm c}(e)
are determined by the vanishing of the Jacobian of the transform in eqs (
32
) and (
33
), which yields (cf. the geodesic case in Ref.
Cutler et al. 1994
)

\displaystyle p_{\rm c}=(6+2e)M+2s_{\parallel}\sqrt{\frac{2(1+e)}{3+e}}\,.

(34)

This generalises the results for innermost stable circular orbits of particles with aligned spin from Refs
Suzuki and Maeda 1998
;
Favata 2011
;
Jefremov et al. 2015
;
Tsupko et al. 2016
to fully generic motion.
The function
\mathcal{R}(r)
can now be re-expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=\frac{1-\mathcal{E}^{2}}{r^{4}}(r_{1}-r)(r-r_{2})(r-r_{3})(r-r_{4})\,,

(35)

\displaystyle r_{1}>r_{2}>r_{3}>r_{4}\,,\;r_{1,2}=\frac{p}{1\mp e}\,,\;r_{4}=0\,,

(36)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&r_{3}=\frac{2Mp}{p-4M}+\frac{2s_{\parallel}\sqrt{Mp\left[p^{2}-4Mp+4M^{2}(1-e^{2})\right]}}{(p-4M)^{2}}\,.\end{split}

(37)

Expression as Jacobi elliptic integrals.
–
In Schwarzschild space-time, the most elegant parametrization of the motion is through Carter-Mino time
Carter 1968
;
Mino 2003

\lambda,\,\mathrm{d}\tau/\mathrm{d}\lambda=r^{2}
so that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\lambda(r)-\lambda(r_{2})=\int_{r_{2}}^{r}\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}}{\sqrt{R(r)}}\\
&\phantom{\lambda(r)-\lambda(r_{2})}=\frac{2F(\chi,k)}{\sqrt{(1-\mathcal{E}^{2})(r_{1}-r_{3})r_{2}}}\,,\end{split}

(38)

\displaystyle\sin\chi\equiv\sqrt{\frac{(r_{1}-r_{3})(r-r_{2})}{(r_{1}-r_{2})(r-r_{3})}}\,,

(39)

\displaystyle k^{2}=\frac{(r_{1}-r_{2})r_{3}}{(r_{1}-r_{3})r_{2}}\,,

(40)

\displaystyle R(r)\equiv(1-\mathcal{E}^{2})(r_{1}-r)(r-r_{2})(r-r_{3})r\,,

(41)

where
F(\chi,k)
is the elliptic integral of the first kind. All elliptic integrals
F,K,E,\Pi
and their inverses
{\rm am},{\rm sn},{\rm cn}
will be defined in the angle-modulus convention of Byrd & Friedman
Byrd and Friedman 2013
(see also Supplemental material).
The
\lambda(r)
function can be inverted by the same arguments as given by van de Meent
van de Meent 2020
for corresponding formulas for Kerr geodesics to yield

\displaystyle r(\lambda)=\frac{r_{3}(r_{1}-r_{2})\mathrm{sn}^{2}\left(\frac{K(k)}{\pi}q^{r},k\right)-r_{2}(r_{1}-r_{3})}{(r_{1}-r_{2})\mathrm{sn}^{2}\left(\frac{K(k)}{\pi}q^{r},k\right)-(r_{1}-r_{3})}\,,

(42)

\displaystyle q^{r}\equiv\Upsilon^{r}\lambda+q^{r}_{0}\,,

(43)

\displaystyle\Upsilon^{r}\equiv\frac{\pi\sqrt{(1-\mathcal{E}^{2})(r_{1}-r_{3})r_{2}}}{2K(k)}\,,

(44)

where
K(k)
is the complete elliptic integral of the first kind and
q^{r}_{0}
is an integration constant determined by initial conditions. Again, following closely the notation and approach of Ref.
van de Meent 2020
we express the other orbital variables as

\displaystyle t(\lambda)=q^{t}+\tilde{T}_{r}\left(\mathrm{am}\left(\frac{q^{r}}{\pi}K(k),k\right)\right)-\frac{\tilde{T}_{r}\left(\pi\right)}{2\pi}q^{r},\,

(45)

\displaystyle\varphi(\lambda)=q^{\varphi}+\tilde{\Phi}_{r}\left(\mathrm{am}\left(\frac{q^{r}}{\pi}K(k),k\right)\right)-\frac{\tilde{\Phi}_{r}\left(\pi\right)}{2\pi}q^{r},

(46)

\displaystyle\psi(\lambda)=q^{\psi}+\tilde{\Psi}_{r}\left(\mathrm{am}\left(\frac{q^{r}}{\pi}K(k),k\right)\right)-\frac{\tilde{\Psi}_{r}\left(\pi\right)}{2\pi}q^{r},

(47)

\displaystyle q^{t}\equiv\Upsilon^{t}\lambda+q^{t}_{0},\,q^{\varphi}\equiv\Upsilon^{\varphi}\lambda+q^{\varphi}_{0},\,q^{\psi}\equiv\Upsilon^{\psi}\lambda+q^{\psi}_{0},\,

(48)

where
q^{t,\varphi,\psi}_{0}
are again integration constants and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\tilde{T}_{r}(\chi)=\frac{\mathcal{E}}{\sqrt{(1-\mathcal{E}^{2})r_{2}(r_{1}-r_{3})}}\Bigg[\frac{2M(r_{1}-r_{3})(r_{2}-r_{3})+r_{3}(r_{3}(r_{2}+r_{3})-r_{1}(r_{2}-r_{3}))+2\frac{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{E}}Ms_{\parallel}}{r_{3}-2M}F(\chi,k)\\
&\phantom{\tilde{T}_{r}(\chi)=}+(r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{3}+4M)(r_{2}-r_{3})\Pi\left(\chi,\frac{r_{1}-r_{2}}{r_{1}-r_{3}},k\right)+r_{2}(r_{1}-r_{3})E(\chi,k)\\
&\phantom{\tilde{T}_{r}(\chi)=}-\frac{2M(r_{2}-r_{3})\left(8M^{2}+\frac{\mathcal{J}}{\mathcal{E}}s_{\parallel}\right)}{(r_{2}-2M)(r_{3}-2M)}\Pi\left(\chi,\frac{(r_{3}-2M)(r_{1}-r_{2})}{(r_{2}-2M)(r_{1}-r_{3})},k\right)\\
&\phantom{\tilde{T}_{r}(\chi)=}-\frac{(r_{1}-r_{2})\sin 2\chi\sqrt{r_{2}(r_{1}-r_{3})\left[r_{2}(r_{1}-r_{3})-r_{3}(r_{1}-r_{2})\sin^{2}\!\chi\right]}}{r_{1}+r_{2}-2r_{3}+(r_{1}-r_{2})\cos 2\chi}\Bigg]\,,\end{split}

(49)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\tilde{\Phi}_{r}(\chi)=\frac{2(\mathcal{J}+s_{\parallel}\mathcal{E})}{\sqrt{(1-\mathcal{E}^{2})r_{2}(r_{1}-r_{3})}}F(\chi,k)\,,\end{split}

(50)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\tilde{\Psi}_{r}(\chi)=\frac{2\mathcal{E}\mathcal{J}}{\sqrt{(1-\mathcal{E}^{2})(r_{1}-r_{3})r_{2}}(\mathcal{J}^{2}+r_{3}^{2})}\Bigg[r_{3}^{2}F(\chi,k)+\frac{\mathcal{J}^{2}(r_{2}^{2}-r_{3}^{2})}{\mathcal{J}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}}\mathfrak{Re}\left(\Pi\!\left(\chi,\frac{r_{3}-i\mathcal{J}}{r_{2}-i\mathcal{J}}\frac{r_{1}-r_{2}}{r_{1}-r_{3}},k\right)\right)\\
&\phantom{\tilde{\Psi}_{r}(\chi)=}-\frac{\mathcal{J}(r_{2}-r_{3})(\mathcal{J}^{2}-r_{2}r_{3})}{\mathcal{J}^{2}+r_{2}^{2}}\mathfrak{Im}\left(\Pi\!\left(\chi,\frac{r_{3}-i\mathcal{J}}{r_{2}-i\mathcal{J}}\frac{r_{1}-r_{2}}{r_{1}-r_{3}},k\right)\right)\Bigg]\,,\end{split}

(51)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Upsilon^{t}=\frac{\Upsilon^{r}}{2\pi}\tilde{T}_{r}(\pi)=\frac{\mathcal{E}}{2K(k)}\Bigg[\frac{2M(r_{1}-r_{3})(r_{2}-r_{3})+r_{3}(r_{3}(r_{2}+r_{3})-r_{1}(r_{2}-r_{3}))}{r_{3}-2M}K(k)\\
&\phantom{\Upsilon^{t}=}+(r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{3}+4M)(r_{2}-r_{3})\Pi\!\left(\frac{r_{1}-r_{2}}{r_{1}-r_{3}},k\right)+r_{2}(r_{1}-r_{3})E(k)\\

(52)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Upsilon^{\varphi}=\frac{\Upsilon^{r}}{2\pi}\tilde{\Phi}_{r}(\pi)=\mathcal{J}+s_{\parallel}\mathcal{E}\,,\end{split}

(53)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\Upsilon^{\psi}=\frac{\Upsilon^{r}}{2\pi}\tilde{\Psi}_{r}(\pi)=\frac{\mathcal{E}\mathcal{J}r_{3}^{2}}{\mathcal{J}^{2}+r_{3}^{2}}+\frac{\mathcal{E}\mathcal{J}^{2}(r_{2}-r_{3})}{K(k)(\mathcal{J}^{2}+r_{2}^{2})(\mathcal{J}^{2}+r_{3}^{2})}\Bigg[\mathcal{J}(r_{2}+r_{3})\mathfrak{Re}\left(\Pi\!\left(\frac{r_{3}-i\mathcal{J}}{r_{2}-i\mathcal{J}}\frac{r_{1}-r_{2}}{r_{1}-r_{3}},k\right)\right)\\

Discussion and outlooks.
–
The presented analytical solution for the motion of spinning test particles in Schwarzschild space-time can be taken “as is” to source gravitational-wave fluxes or to compute the gravitational self-force on the particle
Warburton et al. 2017
;
Akcay et al. 2020
;
Mathews et al. 2022
;
Skoupý and Lukes-Gerakopoulos 2021
;
Skoupý and Lukes-Gerakopoulos 2022
;
Skoupý et al. 2023
;
Piovano et al. 2020
;
Piovano et al. 2021
;
Piovano et al. 2023
;
Drummond et al. 2023
. Another useful output will be to extend the solution to compute action variables and scattering orbits in order to compare to Effective one-body models
Buonanno and Damour 1999
;
Akcay et al. 2021
;
Albertini et al. 2024
;
Khalil et al. 2023
and other approaches to the relativistic two-body problem
Blanchet et al. 2013
;
Bern et al. 2021
. The formulas provided in the notebooks along with this Letter can also be straightforwardly implemented into existing code-bases such as the BHPToolkit
BHP a
, thus providing an immediate impact in gravitational-wave modeling. In particular, this solution is a springboard to understand the radiation-reaction on the fully generic motion of spinning particles in
Kerr
space-time
Witzany 2019
;
Drummond and Hughes 2022a
;
Drummond et al. 2023
. Indeed, we have recently constructed semi-analytical orbits of spinning particles near Kerr black holes by using the Hamilton-Jacobi formalism presented in Ref.
Witzany 2019
, and it is proving important to have independently derived analytical formulas for validation, even if just for the Schwarzschild case. An important application will also be to use the quadratures in equations (
27
)-(
31
) to study deviations of the particle motion in modified gravities such as in Einstein-Gauss-Bonnet gravity
Glavan and Lin 2020
;
Zhang et al. 2020
, Horndeski and beyond-Horndeski theories
Babichev et al. 2017
;
Bakopoulos et al. 2022
, or near boson stars
Kesden et al. 2005
;
Macedo et al. 2013
;
Diemer et al. 2013
;
Brihaye et al. 2014
and other exotic compact objects
Cardoso and Pani 2019
.

However, we can already use our results to draw general conclusions about the detectability of secondary spin in large-mass-ratio inspirals onto spherically symmetric compact objects. Our solution demonstrates that time-averaged observables depend only on the aligned component of spin
s_{\parallel}
, independently of the compact-object model, and the contributions of the orthogonal component
s_{\perp},s_{\perp}^{2}\equiv s^{2}-s_{\parallel}^{2}
to any dynamical variable oscillate with the characteristic frequency
\Omega^{\psi}
generically different from the orbital frequencies. The spin contributions to the post-1-adiabatic phase of the waveform depends only the spin shifts to quantities such as average orbital frequencies and the average energy and angular momentum fluxes, which are independent of
s_{\perp}
(see also Ref.
Skoupý et al. 2023
). Furthermore, the
\Omega^{\psi}
-oscillation due to
s_{\perp}\lesssim m
only appears in the
\mathcal{O}(m/M)
sub-dominant corrections to the waveform amplitude
Flanagan and Hughes 1998
;
Pound and Wardell 2021
.
As a result, waveform templates that neglect
s_{\perp}
contributions will have a mismatch with models that include
s_{\perp}
of order
\mathcal{O}(m^{2}/M^{2})
. Hence, according to the Lindblom criterion, the waveforms that either include or neglect
s_{\perp}
for EMRIs will be indistinguishable from each other in a matched filtering analysis unless the signal to noise ratio of the EMRI signal reaches the order of the large mass ratio
M/m\sim 10^{4}-10^{6}

Lindblom et al. 2008
;
Schutz 1991
. This provides a key insight that will vastly simplify the treatment of spin in modified-gravity waveforms at large mass ratios.

Another consequence of this work is that the motion of spinning particles in any static spherically symmetric space-time is integrable to linear order in spin. In other words, if there is any chaos to be observed by the numerical integration of spinning particle motion
Suzuki and Maeda 1998
;
Zelenka et al. 2020
, it is an
\mathcal{O}(s^{2})
effect that goes beyond the validity of the equations itself, which is in lines with the quantitative numerical scalings of resonances observed in Schwarzschild space-time
Zelenka et al. 2020
.

A.1
Truncation of equations of motion at
\mathcal{O}(s)

The Mathisson-Papapetrou-Dixon equations of motion valid at quadrupolar order in a given background are
(
Mathisson 1937
;
Papapetrou 1951
;
Dixon 1964
;
Dixon 1970
)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{D}P^{\mu}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}=-\frac{1}{2}R^{\mu}{}_{\nu\kappa\lambda}\dot{x}^{\nu}S^{\kappa\lambda}+\frac{1}{6}R_{\nu\kappa\lambda\gamma}{}^{;\mu}J^{\nu\kappa\lambda\gamma}+...\,,

(S1)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{D}S^{\mu\nu}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}=P^{\mu}\dot{x}^{\nu}-P^{\nu}\dot{x}^{\mu}-\frac{4}{3}\left(R^{\mu}{}_{\kappa\lambda\gamma}J^{\nu\kappa\lambda\gamma}-R^{\nu}{}_{\kappa\lambda\gamma}J^{\mu\kappa\lambda\gamma}\right)+...\,,

(S2)

where
J^{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}
is the Dixon’s quadrupole, which encodes the deformations of an extended body due to external tidal fields or its rotation
(
Bailey and Israel 1975
;
Ehlers and Rudolph 1977
;
Binnington and Poisson 2009
;
Steinhoff and Puetzfeld 2012
;
Steinhoff et al. 2016
, see, e.g.)
. The three dots denote octupole and higher order multipoles which are not explicitly discussed here for the sake of simplicity.
The pole-dipole-quadrupole equations of motion are no longer universal because the quadrupole depends on the matter dynamics of the considered body.
Adiabatic and dynamical tidal quadrupoles
(
Binnington and Poisson 2009
;
Steinhoff et al. 2016
)
turn out to be suppressed by the fifth power of the mass ratio in the large-mass ratio expansion, so we will not discuss them here.
However,
J^{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}
also includes a contribution to the quadrupole sourced by the rotation of the body. This term turns out to be second-order-in-mass-ratio as well as
\mathcal{O}(S^{2})

(
Steinhoff and Schäfer 2009
)
:

\displaystyle J^{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}=\frac{3m}{2\mathcal{M}^{3}}c_{\rm ES^{2}}P^{[\mu}S^{\nu]}{}_{\gamma}S^{\gamma[\kappa}P^{\lambda]}\,,

(S3)

where
[]
denotes antisymmetrization of indices,
\mathcal{M}^{2}\equiv-P_{\mu}P^{\mu}
,
m\equiv-P_{\mu}\dot{x}^{\mu}
, and
c_{ES^{2}}
is a deformation coefficient that can be assumed to be approximately constant and
\propto m^{3}
almost up to the mass-shedding limit
(
Chakrabarti et al. 2014
, see, e.g.,)
. The curvature is
R^{\mu}{}_{\nu\kappa\lambda}\sim 1/r_{\rm c}^{2}
, where
r_{\rm c}
is the curvature length, and a covariant derivative adds an additional
\sim 1/r_{\rm c}
factor. The Christoffel-symbol (parallel transport/geodesic) terms in the equations of motion scale as
\sim 1/r_{\rm c}
. The dipole terms thus scale with a relative factor
\sim S/r_{\rm c}
as compared to the leading order in the equations of motion, and the quadrupole terms as
\sim S^{2}/r_{\rm c}^{2}
relative to leading order. Similarly, higher multipoles contain higher powers of
S
can be shown to scale with increasing powers of the ratio of spin and the curvature length.

The linear momentum is related to the four-velocity in the following manner under the Tulczyjew-Dixon condition
S^{\mu\nu}P_{\nu}=0

(
Steinhoff and Puetzfeld 2012
)
:

\displaystyle\dot{x}^{\mu}=\mathcal{U}^{\mu}+\frac{2S^{\mu\nu}S^{\kappa\lambda}R_{\kappa\lambda\nu\gamma}\mathcal{U}^{\gamma}}{4\mathcal{M}^{2}+S^{\gamma\delta}S^{\omega\chi}R_{\gamma\delta\omega\chi}}\,,

(S4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\mu}\equiv\frac{m}{\mathcal{M}^{2}}P^{\mu}-\frac{4}{3\mathcal{M}^{2}}R^{[\mu}{}_{\nu\kappa\lambda}J^{\gamma]\nu\kappa\lambda}P_{\gamma}+\frac{1}{6\mathcal{M}^{2}}S^{\mu\nu}R_{\kappa\lambda\chi\xi;\nu}J^{\kappa\lambda\chi\xi}\,.

(S5)

One of the consequences of relation above is that
\mathcal{M}^{2}\dot{x}^{\mu}=mP^{\mu}+\mathcal{O}(S^{2})
so by projection into
\dot{x}_{\mu}
we obtain
\mathcal{M}^{2}=m^{2}+\mathcal{O}(S^{2})
and both definitions of mass agree at leading order in spin.
By taking a covariant time derivative of the relation above and of
s^{\mu}\equiv\varepsilon^{\mu\nu\kappa\lambda}P_{\nu}S_{\kappa\lambda}/(2\mathcal{M}^{2})
we then obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{D}^{2}x^{\mu}}{\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}}=-\frac{1}{4}{R}^{\mu}{}_{\nu\gamma\delta}\varepsilon^{\gamma\delta}{}_{\kappa\lambda}\dot{x}^{\nu}\dot{x}^{\kappa}s^{\lambda}+\mathcal{O}(s^{2}R\nabla R)\,,

(S6)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{D}s^{\lambda}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}=0+\mathcal{O}(s^{2}R^{2})\,.

(S7)

In particular, the terms like
s^{2}R\nabla R
and
s^{2}R^{2}
have comparable contribution from the quadrupole
\propto c_{ES^{2}}
coefficient as well from the non-trivial momentum-velocity relation in the Tulczyjew-Dixon supplementary spin condition. Nevertheless, all terms including the Riemann tensor and its derivatives scaled with the curvature length
r_{\rm c}
in the same way. The Riemann tensor scales as
1/r_{\rm c}^{2}
while the magnitude of the specific spin
s
is set by the Møller radius
r_{\rm M}
, the minimal physical size of the rotating body
(
Møller 1949
)
. Thus, for compact objects in the large mass ratio limit, the truncation of the equations of motion (
3
) and (
4
) are controlled by powers of
r_{\rm M}/r_{\rm c}
. A multipole expansion is valid only when
r_{\rm M}/r_{\rm c}\ll 1
, a condition that is always satisfied for extreme mass-ratio binaries. This is because for bound motion near a black hole
r_{c}\sim\sqrt{r^{3}/M}\gtrsim M
and thus also
1/r_{c}\lesssim 1/M
, and the Møller radius
r_{\rm M}\lesssim m
for compact objects. As such, the equations of motion (
3
) and (
4
) presented in the main text corresponds to the general form of the Mathisson-Papapetrou-Dixon equations of motion truncated at leading order in an expansion in
m/M
. Before adding the
\mathcal{O}(S^{2})
terms we would thus have to add leading-order gravitational self-force for self-consistence; the cross-interactions between self-force and spin would then be of the same importance as the
\mathcal{O}(S^{2})
terms in the equations of motion, which is beyond the scope of our Letter.

A.2
Aligning the coordinate frame

Given the components of the angular-momentum vector
\vec{\mathcal{J}}
computed from equations (
11
)-(
13

\iota\in(0,\pi)
of
\vec{\mathcal{J}}
with respect to the

\displaystyle\cos\iota=\frac{\vec{\mathcal{J}}\cdot\hat{z}}{\mathcal{J}}=\frac{\mathcal{J}_{z}}{\mathcal{J}}\,,

(S8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}\equiv\sqrt{\mathcal{J}_{x}^{2}+\mathcal{J}_{y}^{2}+\mathcal{J}_{z}^{2}}\,,

(S9)

where
\hat{z}
is the unit vector in the
z
direction.
Now the aligned frame is obtained by rotating by
\iota
around the axis
\vec{n}
given as

\displaystyle\vec{n}=\frac{\vec{\mathcal{J}}\times\hat{z}}{|\vec{\mathcal{J}}\times\hat{z}|}=(-\cos\xi,\sin\xi,0)\,,

(S10)

\displaystyle\xi\equiv\arctan\frac{\mathcal{J}_{x}}{\mathcal{J}_{y}}\,,

(S11)

where
\xi
is related to the longitude of the ascending node known from classical celestial mechanics
Morbidelli 2002
. The angles
\iota,\xi
can be used to parametrize the angular-momentum vector as

\displaystyle\vec{\mathcal{J}}=\mathcal{J}(\sin\xi\sin\iota,\cos\xi\sin\iota,\cos\iota)\,.

(S12)

The transformation of any vector into the aligned coordinates then reads

\displaystyle\vec{v}^{\prime}=\vec{n}(\vec{n}\cdot\vec{v})+\cos\iota(\vec{n}\times\vec{v})\times\vec{n}-\sin\iota(\vec{n}\times\vec{v})\,.

(S13)

It is now easy to show that this map yields
\vec{\mathcal{J}}^{\prime}=(0,0,\mathcal{J}_{z^{\prime}}=\mathcal{J})
. Note that the transformation also flips counter-rotating coordinate systems (
\mathcal{J}_{z}<0
) into co-rotating ones (
\mathcal{J}_{z^{\prime}}>0
), which corresponds to
\iota>\pi/2
. The inverse map is simply obtained by switching the sign on the angle of rotation
\iota

\displaystyle\vec{v}=\vec{n}(\vec{n}\cdot\vec{v}^{\prime})+\cos\iota(\vec{n}\times\vec{v}^{\prime})\times\vec{n}+\sin\iota(\vec{n}\times\vec{v}^{\prime})\,.

(S14)

From the transformation of the unit position vector
\hat{r}=(\sin\phi\sin\theta,\cos\phi\sin\theta,\cos\theta)
and the aligned vector
\hat{r}^{\prime}=(\sin\varphi\sin\vartheta,\cos\varphi\sin\vartheta,\cos\vartheta)
one can easily read off the aligned coordinate system and its inverse transformation. Specifically, one can take the
t,r,\varphi,\vartheta=\pi/2+\delta\vartheta
solution presented in the main text of the paper and map it back into the original coordinates (
t,r
are unchanged)

\displaystyle\cos\theta=\cos\vartheta\cos\iota-\sin\vartheta\sin\iota\cos(\xi-\varphi)\,,

(S15)

\displaystyle\tan\phi=\frac{\cos\vartheta\sin\iota\sin\xi+\sin\vartheta\left[\cos^{2}\!\frac{\iota}{2}\sin\varphi-\sin^{2}\!\frac{\iota}{2}\sin(2\xi-\varphi)\right]}{\cos\vartheta\sin\iota\cos\xi+\sin\vartheta\left[\cos^{2}\!\frac{\iota}{2}\cos\varphi-\sin^{2}\!\frac{\iota}{2}\cos(2\xi-\varphi)\right]}\,.

(S16)

A.4
Jacobi elliptic integrals

The convention of Byrd & Friedman
Byrd and Friedman 2013
for incomplete Jacobi elliptic integrals of the first, second and third kinds
F,E,\Pi
is as follows

\displaystyle F(\chi,k)\equiv\int_{0}^{\chi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{\sqrt{1-k^{2}\sin^{2}\!\xi}}\,,

(S20)

\displaystyle E(\chi,k)\equiv\int_{0}^{\chi}\sqrt{1-k^{2}\sin^{2}\!\xi}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\,,

(S21)

\displaystyle\Pi(\chi,n,k)\equiv\int_{0}^{\chi}\frac{\mathrm{d}\xi}{(1-n\sin^{2}\!\xi)\sqrt{1-k^{2}\sin^{2}\!\xi}}\,.

(S22)

The complete elliptic integrals are then defined by
K(k)\equiv F(\pi/2,k),\,E(k)\equiv E(\pi/2,k),\,\Pi(n,k)\equiv\Pi(\pi/2,n,k)
. The Jacobi amplitude is the inverse of the elliptic integral of the first kind
F({\rm am}(u,k),k)\equiv u
and we also use
{\rm sn}(u,k)\equiv\sin({\rm am}(u,k)),\,{\rm cn}(u,k)\equiv\cos({\rm am}(u,k))
.

This convention should be contrasted with the convention of
Maplesoft Maple
, which uses
\sin(\xi)
in the first argument of incomplete integrals, or
Wolfram Mathematica
, which uses
k^{2}
in the last argument and switches the order of the first two arguments in the incomplete elliptic integral of the third kind
\Pi
.

.
The precession frequency written explicitly to leading order in terms of
p,e
reads

\Upsilon^{\psi}=\frac{\sqrt{Mp}}{\sqrt{(p-2M)^{2}-4M^{2}e^{2}}K(k_{0})}\Bigg[4MK(k_{0})+(p-2M(e+3))\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{M}(1-e)}{\sqrt{p-M(e^{2}+3)}}\mathfrak{Im}\left[\Pi(\gamma_{0},k_{0})\right]+\mathfrak{Re}\left[\Pi(\gamma_{0},k_{0})\right]\bigg)\Bigg],

(S35)

which agrees with eq. (64) of Ref.
van de Meent 2020
.

Using the previous expressions, it is easy to compute the nodal and periastron precession
\nu_{\rm nodal}
and
\nu_{\rm peri}
, respectively, which are defined as

\displaystyle\nu_{\rm nodal}

\displaystyle=2\pi\bigg(\frac{\Upsilon^{\varphi}}{\Upsilon^{\psi}}-1\bigg)\,,\quad\nu_{\rm peri}=2\pi\bigg(\frac{\Upsilon^{\varphi}}{\Upsilon^{r}}-1\bigg)\ ,

(S36)

The average polar fundamental frequency is
\Upsilon^{\psi}
, as can be seen from eq. (
26
).
This leads to the explicit formulas

\displaystyle\nu_{\rm nodal}

\displaystyle=2\pi\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{K}(p,e)}\sqrt{\frac{p(p-2M(1-e))(p-2M(1+e))}{p-M(e^{2}+3)}}K(k_{0})-1\right)\,,

(S37)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}(p,e)

\displaystyle=4MK(k_{0})+(p-2M(e+3))\left(\frac{\sqrt{M}(1-e)}{\sqrt{p-M(e^{2}+3)}}\mathfrak{Im}\left[\Pi(\gamma_{0},k_{0})\right]+\mathfrak{Re}\left[\Pi(\gamma_{0},k_{0})\right]\right)\,.

(S38)

We stress that
\nu_{\rm nodal}
is evaluated at leading
s_{\parallel}=s=0
order, since the precession itself is an
\mathcal{O}(s)
effect. On the other hand, as can be seen from eq. (
46
),
\varphi
will evolve by exactly the same amount between two pericenter passages and no averaging needs to be invoked. The non-averaged periastron precession is then given by

\displaystyle\nu_{\rm peri}=\nu^{\rm g}_{\rm peri}+s_{\parallel}\delta\nu_{\rm peri}\ ,

(S39)

where

\displaystyle\nu^{\rm g}_{\rm peri}

\displaystyle=\frac{4pK(k_{0})}{\sqrt{p(p+2M(e-3))}}-2\pi\ \,,

(S40)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\delta\nu_{\rm peri}&=2\sqrt{\frac{(p-2M)^{2}-4M^{2}e^{2}}{p(p+2M(e-3))}}\bigg(\frac{E(k_{0})}{p-2M(e+3)}-\frac{K(k_{0})}{p}\bigg)\,.\end{split}

(S41)

A.6
Linear shift to the orbital motion

The expansion of the orbital motion in spin is non-unique. We choose to expand at fixed radial turning points (parametrized by
p,e
) and fixed angle variables (homogeneous orbital phases)
q^{t},q^{r},q^{\varphi},q^{\psi}
. This is because the angle variables smoothly parametrize the orbital motion and avoid singularities at turning points associated, e.g., with expansions at fixed velocities and coordinates
u^{\mu},x^{\mu}
. Furthermore, this separates the “oscillating” part of the spin corrections from the secular/averaged effect of the spin expressed by the frequency shifts
\delta\Upsilon^{t,r,\varphi}
. By this method we obtain
x=x_{(\rm g)}+s_{\parallel}\delta x
for
x=t,r,\varphi
. On the other hand,
\psi
is consistent to compute only to leading order, since it appears only in
\mathcal{O}(s)
observables. The resulting expressions read

\displaystyle r_{(0)}=\frac{p\left[p-2M(3-e)-4Me\sin^{2}\!\chi_{0}\right]}{p-2M(3-e^{2})+e(p-4M)\cos 2\chi_{0}}\,,

(S42)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&t_{(0)}=q^{t}+\sqrt{\frac{(p-2M)^{2}-4e^{2}}{M\left[p-2M(3-e)\right]}}\Bigg[\frac{p\left[p^{2}-14Mp+2Me^{2}(p-2M)+36M^{2}\right]\left(F(\chi_{0},k_{0})-\frac{q^{r}}{\pi}K(k_{0})\right)}{(e^{2}-1)(p-4M)^{2}}\\
&\phantom{t_{(0)}=}+\frac{2\left[p-2M(3-e)\right]\left[p^{2}-Mp(1+3e^{2})+8M^{2}(e^{2}-1)\right]\left(\Pi(\chi_{0},\alpha_{0},k_{0})-\frac{q^{r}}{\pi}\Pi(\alpha_{0},k_{0})\right)}{(1-e^{2})(1+e)(p-4M)^{2}}\\
&\phantom{t_{(0)}=}-\frac{2M\left[p-2M(3-e)\right]\left(\Pi(\chi_{0},\beta_{0},k_{0})-\frac{q^{r}}{\pi}\Pi(\beta_{0},k_{0})\right)}{p-2M(1-e)}+\frac{Mp\left[p-2M(3-e)\right]\left(E(\chi_{0},k_{0})-\frac{q^{r}}{\pi}E(k_{0})\right)}{(1-e^{2})(p-4M)^{2}}\\
&\phantom{t_{(0)}=}-\frac{ep\sin 2\chi_{0}\sqrt{\left[p-2M(3-e)\right]\left[p-2M(3-e\cos 2\chi_{0})\right]}}{(1-e^{2})\left[p-2M(3-e^{2})+e(p-4M)\cos 2\chi_{0}\right]}\Bigg]\,,\end{split}

(S43)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\varphi_{(0)}=q^{\varphi}+2\sqrt{\frac{p}{p-2M(3-e)}}\left(F(\chi_{0},k_{0})-\frac{q^{r}}{\pi}K(k_{0})\right)\,,\end{split}

(S44)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\psi=q^{\psi}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{\left[(p-2M)^{2}-4M^{2}e^{2}\right]\left[p-2M(3-e)\right]}}\Bigg[4M\sqrt{p-M(3+e^{2})}\left(F(\chi_{0},k_{0})-\frac{q^{r}}{\pi}K(k_{0})\right)\\
&\phantom{\psi=}+\sqrt{M}(1-e)\left[p-2M(3+e)\right]\mathfrak{Im}\left(\Pi(\chi_{0},\gamma_{0},k_{0})-\frac{q^{r}}{\pi}\Pi(\gamma_{0},k_{0})\right)\\
&\phantom{\psi=}+\sqrt{p-M(3+e^{2})}\left[p-2M(3+e)\right]\mathfrak{Re}\left(\Pi(\chi_{0},\gamma_{0},k_{0})-\frac{q^{r}}{\pi}\Pi(\gamma_{0},k_{0})\right)\Bigg]\,,\end{split}

(S45)

\displaystyle\delta r=-\frac{e\sqrt{p\left[(p-2M)^{2}-4e^{2}\right]}\left[p-2M(3-e)\right]\sin 2\chi_{0}\sqrt{1-k_{0}^{2}\sin^{2}\!\chi_{0}}\left(E(\chi_{0},k_{0})-\frac{q^{r}}{\pi}E(k_{0})\right)}{\left[p-2M(3-e^{2})+e(p-4M)\cos 2\chi_{0}\right]^{2}}\,,

(S46)

\displaystyle\chi_{0}\equiv\mathrm{am}\left(\frac{q^{r}}{\pi}K(k_{0}),k_{0}\right)\,.

(S47)

The
\delta t,\delta\varphi
functions are too long to be typed here explicitly, but they can be found in machine-readable form in the accompanying file
SchwarzschildSpinAnalytic.m
.

It should be noted that if one is only interested in the approximate orbit, it is computationally more efficient to use the non-expanded formulas (
42
)-(
54
) presented in the main text. On the other hand, in a two-timescale expansion of a gravitational-wave inspiral it is convenient to separate the various pieces contributing to the motion separately, which is the main reason to provide these formulas.

Finally, the expanded results can now be substituted to obtain
\delta\vartheta(q^{r},q^{\psi},p,e)
as

\displaystyle\delta\vartheta=\frac{\sqrt{M(s^{2}-s_{\parallel}^{2})\left[Mp^{2}+(p-M(3+e^{2}))r^{2}_{(0)}\right]}\sin\psi}{Mpr_{(0)}}\,.

(S48)

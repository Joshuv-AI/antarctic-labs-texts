Phases and phase transitions in a dimerized spin- 𝟏 𝟐 \mathbf{\frac{1}{2}} XXZ chain
Harsh Nigam, Ashirbad Padhan, Diptiman Sen, Tapan Mishra, Subhro Bhattacharjee

Abstract
We revisit the phase diagram of the dimerized XXZ spin- 1 2 \frac{1}{2} chain with nearest-neighbor couplings which was studied numerically in Phys. Rev. B 106, L201106 (2022) . The model has isotropic X ​ Y XY couplings which have a uniform value and Z ​ Z ZZ couplings which have a dimerized form, with strengths J a J_{a} and J b J_{b} on alternate bonds. We find a rich phase diagram in the region of positive J a , J b J_{a},~J_{b} . We provide a detailed understanding of the different phases and associated quantum phase transitions using a combination of mean-field theory, low-energy effective Hamiltonians, renormalization group calculations employing the technique of bosonization, and numerical calculations using the density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG) method. The phase diagram consists of two Ising paramagnetic phases called IPM 0 and IPM π , and a phase with Ising Neel order called IN; all these phases are gapped. The phases IPM 0 and IPM π are separated by a gapless phase transition line given by 0 ≤ J a = J b ≤ 1 0\leq J_{a}=J_{b}\leq 1 which is described by a conformal field theory with central charge c = 1 c=1 . There are two gapless phase transition lines separating IPM 0 from IN and IPM π from IN; these are described by conformal field theories with c = 1 2 c=\frac{1}{2} corresponding to quantum Ising transitions. The c = 1 c=1 line bifurcates into the two c = 1 2 c=\frac{1}{2} lines at the point J a = J b = 1 J_{a}=J_{b}=1 ; the shape of the bifurcation is found analytically using RG calculations. A symmetry analysis shows that IPM 0 is a topologically trivial phase while IPM π is a time-reversal symmetry-protected topological phase (SPT) with spin- 1 2 \frac{1}{2} states at the two ends of an open system. A gap opens in the low-energy spectrum whenever one moves away from one of the phase transition lines; the scaling of the gap with the distance from a transition line is found analytically using the RG method. The numerical results obtained by the DMRG method are in good agreement with the analytical results. Further, we show that useful insights into the nature of the phase diagram of the above Hamiltonian, particularly the SPT as well as the phase transitions, is obtained via Kramers-Wannier duality to a deformed quantum Ashkin-Teller model. Finally we propose experimental platforms for testing our results.

refers to various four-fermion interactions. In particular, it is made up of two terms,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{int}=\mathcal{H}_{1}+\mathcal{H}_{2},

VI
Summary and Outlook

We have performed a detailed analysis to understand the phases and phase transitions of a spin-
\frac{1}{2}
XXZ model with dimerized longitudinal interaction. Numerical studies based on the DMRG method have already revealed the appearance of topological (protected by time-reversal symmetry) and trivial IPM phases and the transition between them through a gap-closing point as a ratio of the dimerization strengths. The topological transition boundary later bifurcates to two such that a symmetry broken IN phase is sandwiched between the two IPM phases in the limit of strong longitudinal interactions
[
21
]
leading to the phase diagram shown in Fig.
2
. In this study, through DMRG calculations we systematically identified the criticalities associated with these phase transitions from the analysis of the central charge, neutral and charge gaps and the fidelity susceptibility measure. Along the undimerized (
J_{a}=J_{b}
) line our calculations reproduce the well known critical point separating the TLL and the symmetry broken (IN) phase via a critical point described by
c=1
CFT. A remarkable aspect, however, of our findings is the splitting up of the c=1 critical point into two
c=\frac{1}{2}
Ising critical lines between the topological/trivial paramagnet (IPM) to the symmetry broken (IN) phases.

A mean-field treatment of an equivalent fermionic Hamiltonian, obtained via a Jordan-Wigner transformation, captures the basic structure of the phase diagram where the IPMs maps to trivial and topological insulating phases of an effective SSH model while the IN maps to a charge-density-wave insulator. However, not surprisingly, the mean-field treatment fails to faithfully capture the nature of the phase transitions which then is captured via a renormalization group analysis of the effective low-energy continuum field theory obtained from bosonization of the spin-Hamiltonian. The resultant bosonized continuum form is given by the so-called
double-frequency sine-Gordon
field theory
[
39
]
which captures the bifurcation of the
c=1
critical point into two Ising lines, including the scaling of the excitation gap as well as the transition line near the
c=1
point. Further, we have shown that the appearance of the IPM phases can be explained from the limit of very strong longitudinal interactions and the SPT phase transition between them can be understood from the decorated domain wall picture which becomes most apparent via KW duality to cluster Ising models and deformed quantum AT models.

Our findings provide the impetus for further exploration of interaction-driven topological phase transition and non-trivial critical phenomena in related interacting models such as the one considered in Ref.
[
51
]
. One can also seek to understand the effect of next-nearest-neighbour couplings as such terms are known to stabilize dimerized phases
[
52
,
53
,
8
]
. As the model under consideration can be mapped to the hardcore bosonic Hubbard model, these findings can be tested using state-of-the art experiments involving Rydberg gases
[
54
]
. On the other hand, one can also consider set ups with dipolar quantum gases in optical lattices
[
55
]
to simulate these findings. It is also important to know the fate of these phases and phase transition by relaxing the hardcore constraint - a scenario which may exhibit a much richer phase diagram in this context.

We end by noting that while our lattice level KW duality shows that the present dimerized XXZ model is dual to the deformed QAT model with both models resulting in the same low-energy continuum double frequency sine-Gordon field theories, the implementation of the symmetries in the dual representations are rather non-trivial
[
26
]
– including their effect in the
low-energy field theories– and remain to be systematically understood.

Appendix B
Degenerate perturbation theory for the anisotropic limits

Here we provide the details leading up to Eq.
8
. For
J_{b}\rightarrow\infty
, the unperturbed Hamiltonian is given by Eq.
3
while the perturbation consists of the other two terms in Eq.
1
. These two terms with coupling constants
J_{a}
and
J_{\perp}
do not mix in the leading order of perturbation and have opposite effects.

J_{a}
connects two neighbouring (odd/blue) bonds (Fig.
1

\sigma^{z}
states

\displaystyle\{|+_{I}+_{I+1}\rangle,|+_{I}-_{I+1}\rangle,|-_{I}+_{I+1}\rangle,|-_{I}-_{I+1}\rangle\}.

(47)

The first-order effective Hamiltonian for the above two bond spins is explicitly given by

\tilde{H}_{p1}[I,I+1]=-\frac{J_{a}}{4}\sigma_{I}^{z}\sigma_{I+1}^{z},

(48)

which can be obtained by writing out the
4\times 4
matrix explicitly. Thus, it generates
ferromagnetic
Ising interactions between the two bond spins, which align the two
\sigma^{z}_{I}

8
. The ground state of the above Hamiltonian is two-fold degenerate and thus the effect of

is to lift the massive degeneracy of
H_{b}
by breaking the global

symmetry. Remarkably, in terms of the underlying
S_{i}
-spins this is a Ising Neel state that was obtained in the numerical calculations of Ref.
[
21
]
in the same regime.

Turning to the
J_{\perp}
perturbation, we note that unlike the
J_{a}
term, this term acts on both the even and the odd bonds. However, at the leading order, the contribution from each odd bond and its action, on each odd bond, of
|\uparrow,\downarrow\rangle\leftrightarrow|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle
in terms of the bond spin is
|+_{I}\rangle\leftrightarrow|-_{I}\rangle
; this leads to the transverse field term in Eq.
8
.

We now extend this calculation to first order in
1/J_{b}
.
We find that such a term arises from the action of the
J_{\perp}

term on the even bonds. Acting on the four possible states on sites

I
and
I+1
, the
J_{\perp}
term acting on the bond joining them
gives the following:

\displaystyle-~J_{\perp}(S_{2i}^{x}S_{2i+1}^{x}~+~S_{2i}^{y}S_{2i+1}^{y})~|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle_{I}~|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle_{I+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-~\frac{J_{\perp}}{2}~|\uparrow\uparrow\rangle_{I}~|\downarrow\downarrow\rangle_{I+1},

\displaystyle-~J_{\perp}(S_{2i}^{x}S_{2i+1}^{x}~+~S_{2i}^{y}S_{2i+1}^{y})~|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle_{I}~|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle_{I+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-~\frac{J_{\perp}}{2}~|\downarrow\downarrow\rangle_{I}~|\uparrow\uparrow\rangle_{I+1},

\displaystyle-~J_{\perp}(S_{2i}^{x}S_{2i+1}^{x}~+~S_{2i}^{y}S_{2i+1}^{y})~|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle_{I}~|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle_{I+1}~=~0,

\displaystyle-~J_{\perp}(S_{2i}^{x}S_{2i+1}^{x}~+~S_{2i}^{y}S_{2i+1}^{y})~|\downarrow\uparrow\rangle_{I}~|\uparrow\downarrow\rangle_{I+1}~=~0.

In the first two equations in Eq.
, the state on
the left hand side (
i.e.,
one of the ground states) has an energy equal to

-J_{b}/2
, while the state on the right hand side (
i.e.,
an excited
state) has an energy
J_{b}/2
. Hence, the energy denominator in

-(J_{b}/2)-(J_{b}/2)=-J_{b}
. Further, the matrix element to go between the
left and right hand sides is
-J_{\perp}/2
, so that the
matrix element squared is
J_{\perp}^{2}/4
. Finally, Eq.

shows that only the states
|+_{I}+_{I+1}\rangle
and

|-_{I}-_{I+1}\rangle
are affected at second order, while the

|+_{I}-_{I+1}\rangle

are not

\tilde{H}_{p2}[I,I+1]~=~-~\frac{J_{\perp}^{2}}{8J_{b}}~(\mathbb{I}~+~\sigma_{I}^{z}\sigma_{I+1}^{z}).

(50)

Thus at second order in perturbation theory, the coefficient of the first term

This gives

which is Eq.

in the main text.

C.1

To be precise, let us consider the anisotropic limit by taking

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=-\frac{J_{b}}{4}\sum_{I}\tau^{x}_{I}\sigma_{I}^{x}

\displaystyle-\frac{J_{\perp}}{4}\sum_{I}\left[\tau^{x}_{I}+\sigma_{I}^{x}+\tau^{z}_{I}\tau^{z}_{I+1}+\sigma^{z}_{I}\sigma^{z}_{I+1}\right]

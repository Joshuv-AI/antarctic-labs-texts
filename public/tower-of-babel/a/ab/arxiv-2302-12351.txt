On the Hardness of Robustness Transfer: A Perspective from Rademacher Complexity over Symmetric Difference Hypothesis Space
Yuyang Deng & Mehrdad MahdaviThe Pennsylvania State University {yzd82,mzm616}@psu.edu Junyuan HongMichigan State University hongju12@msu.edu, Nidham Gazagnadou & Lingjuan LyuSony AI {nidham.gazagnadou,lingjuan.lv}@sony.com

Abstract
Recent studies demonstrated that the adversarially robust learning under ℓ ∞ \ell_{\infty} attack is harder to generalize to different domains than standard domain adaptation. How to transfer robustness across different domains has been a key question in domain adaptation field. To investigate the fundamental difficulty behind adversarially robust domain adaptation (or robustness transfer), we propose to analyze a key complexity measure that controls the cross-domain generalization: the adversarial Rademacher complexity over symmetric difference hypothesis space ℋ ​ Δ ​ ℋ \mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H} . For linear models, we show that adversarial version of this complexity is always greater than the non-adversarial one, which reveals the intrinsic hardness of adversarially robust domain adaptation. We also establish upper bounds on this complexity measure. Then we extend them to the ReLU neural network class by upper bounding the adversarial Rademacher complexity in the binary classification setting. Finally, even though the robust domain adaptation is provably harder, we do find positive relation between robust learning and standard domain adaptation. We explain how adversarial training helps domain adaptation in terms of standard risk . We believe our results initiate the study of the generalization theory of adversarially robust domain adaptation, and could shed lights on distributed adversarially robust learning from heterogeneous sources, e.g., federated learning scenario.

Our contributions are summarized as follows:

•

We study the Rademacher complexity over
\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}
class, and propose the adversarial variant of it, which is a new complexity measure towards better understanding the domain adaptation in adversarial learning. In both linear classification and regression settings, we first show that adversarial Rademacher complexity over
\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}
class is greater than its non-adversarial counterpart.
We also show that adversarial complexity is bounded by its non-adversarial counterpart plus residual terms polynomially depending on data dimension, model norm and adversarial budget.

•

We generalize our results to ReLU neural networks, where we derive an upper bound of adversarial
\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}
Rademacher complexity of a 2-layer ReLU neural network for binary classification.

•

Although we proved that robust risk transfer is hard, we discover a positive result when we evaluate target domain standard risk of the robust trained model on source domain. We show that, small source robust risk will imply a small target standard risk with adversarial budget dependent variance. This establish the connection between robust learning and standard domain adaptation, which helps explain the widely-observed phenomena that adversarially trained models can have good generalization performance on different domains.

•

We support our theoretical analysis by providing experiments illustrating how adversarial training can help domain adaptation, especially with
\ell_{1}
regularization.
We also highlight numerically the difficulty of transferring adversarial robustness across domains.

Related Work

Here, we briefly discuss some relevant prior works. But before we would like to highlight the key differences between
robust learning
, standard
domain adaptation
, and
adversarially robust domain adaptation
. In adversarially robust learning, we are interested in the gap between population robust risk and empirical robust risk, on the same domain; while in standard domain adaptation, we consider the gap between the (standard) risk on the target domain and the risk on the source domain on which the model is trained on. In adversarially robust domain adaptation, we examine the relation between adversarially robust risks on target and source domains.

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{D}}}(f\circ\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H})=\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}\left[\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}\leq W,\\
\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{p}\leq W\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{w}^{\prime\top}\mathbf{x}_{i}\right]\kern 5.0pt.

(7)

Indeed, by Ledoux-Talagrand contraction property of Rademacher complexity

20
]
, we have that
{\mathfrak{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{D}}}(\ell\circ\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H})\leq L_{\phi}{\mathfrak{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{D}}}(f\circ\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H})
.
Thus, in the following lemma we aim at estimating
{\mathfrak{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{D}}}(f\circ\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H})

Lemma 3

(Rademacher complexity for binary classification under linear hypothesis)
.

Consider hypothesis class defined in (
6
). Assume that a set of data
\{\mathbf{x}_{1},...,\mathbf{x}_{n}\}
are draw from
\mathcal{D}
. Let
\mathfrak{R}(f\circ\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H})
be defined as in (
7
).
Then the following statement holds true for non-adversarial Rademacher complexity :

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{D}}}(f\circ\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H})\leq\frac{W^{2}}{n}\left(\!\!\sqrt{2\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top})^{2}\right\|_{2}\!\!\log(2d)}+\frac{\left\|{\bf X}\right\|^{2}_{2,\infty}\log(2d)}{3}\!\right)\!\cdot\!\begin{cases}1,&\hskip-10.00002pt1\leq p\leq 2\\
d^{1-2/p},&p>2\end{cases},

(8)

where
{\bf X}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}
is the data matrix where
i
-th row is
\mathbf{x}_{i}
.

The proof of

D.1
.
Lemma

shows that the magnitude of the non-adversarial Rademacher over
\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}
class depends on the spectral norm of data covariance matrix. It implies that a more diverse dataset will result in a larger Rademacher complexity, and hence harder to perform domain adaptation. We notice that

also gave an estimation of the upper bound of

(1) Our bound is tighter in terms of the dependency on covariance matrix, since our bound depends on

while their bound depends on
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\|(\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top})^{2}\right\|_{F}^{2}

p
-norm while they only consider
2
-norm.

Then, let us specify the class of functions involved in the definition of the adversarial Rademacher complexity of
\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}
in (

\displaystyle\tilde{\ell}\circ\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}\!\!\vcentcolon=\!\!\Big\{\mathbf{x}\mapsto\!\!\max_{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}\phi\big(h_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{x}+\boldsymbol{\delta})h_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x}+\boldsymbol{\delta})\big)\!:\!h_{\mathbf{w}},h_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}\!\!\in\!\!\mathcal{H}\Big\},

(9)

and let us define

\displaystyle\tilde{f}\circ\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}\!\!\vcentcolon=\!\!\Big\{\mathbf{x}\mapsto\!\!\min_{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}h_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{x}+\boldsymbol{\delta})h_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}(\mathbf{x}+\boldsymbol{\delta})\ :\ h_{\mathbf{w}},h_{\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{H}\Big\}.

(10)

With the above notations, we can characterize the adversarial counterpart of (
7
). Again by Ledoux-Talagrand’s property, we get that
{\mathfrak{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{D}}}(\tilde{\ell}\circ\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H})\leq L_{\phi}{\mathfrak{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{D}}}(\tilde{f}\circ\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{D}}}(\tilde{f}\circ\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H})=\mathbb{E}_{\sigma}\left[\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}\leq W,\\
\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{p}\leq W\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\min_{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}\mathbf{w}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta})\mathbf{w}^{\prime\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta})\right]\!.

(11)

Theorem 1

(Adversarial Rademacher complexity for binary classification under linear hypothesis)
.

Consider hypothesis class defined in (
6
). Assume a set of data
\hat{\mathcal{D}}=\{\mathbf{x}_{1},...,\mathbf{x}_{n}\}
are drawn from
\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{D}}}(\tilde{f}\circ\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H})\leq{\mathfrak{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{D}}}({f}\circ\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H})+\frac{cW^{2}\sqrt{d\log(n)}}{\sqrt{n}}\epsilon d^{1/p^{*}}\big(\epsilon d^{1/p^{*}}+\left\|{\bf X}\right\|_{p^{*},\infty}\big)\,,

over the number of samples

. It implies that models with suppressed norm can help adversarially robust domain adaptation since it reduces the Rademacher complexity, as we will see in the experiments.

The lower bound result
in (

) shows that, adversarial Rademacher complexity over
\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}
will be always larger than non-adversarial one, which implies that adversarial robust domain adaptation is
at least as hard as non-adversarial domain adaptation

Moreover, the gap between adversarial and non-adversarial complexity is controlled by the spectral norm of Rademacher variable induced covariance matrix. This dependence reveals that a more diverse dataset would be harder to transfer robustness, compared to the standard domain adaptation.

4.3
Technical Novelty

Here we explain our technical novelty compared to existing works regarding adversarial Rademacher complexity
[
35
,
1
]
. Taking classification setting for example,
[
35
,
1
]
consider the Rademacher complexity over the loss class between model predictions and labels, i.e.,
{\mathbb{E}}[\sup_{\mathbf{w}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\min_{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|\leq\infty}\mathbf{w}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}+\boldsymbol{\delta})]
, where the inner minimization problem is
linear
in
\mathbf{w}
and
\boldsymbol{\delta}
. We consider the loss between predictions among two models, hence the Rademacher complexity is
{\mathbb{E}}[\sup_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\min_{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|\leq\infty}\mathbf{w}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}+\boldsymbol{\delta})\mathbf{w}^{\prime\top}(\mathbf{x}+\boldsymbol{\delta})]
, where the inner problem is quadratic in terms of
\mathbf{w}
and
\boldsymbol{\delta}
. Hence, the existing techniques for upper bound and lower bound are not applicable. The heart of our proof is in the proof of results on lower bound. For proving lower bound, controlling the magnitude of Rademacher complexity with the inner problem being quadratic objective is significantly harder than linear objective. We derive the (complicated) closed form solution to inner quadratic programming, and leverage the symmetric property of Rademacher random variables to avoid heavy computation.

(14)

where
\mathbf{w}(r)^{\top}
are the rows of
{\bf W}

6
Adversarial Training Helps Transfer to Different Domain

Even though robust risk is provably harder to transfer in domain adaptation, adversarially robust model does bring benefits, if we consider standard risk on target domain as criteria.
In this section, we will provide a positive result connecting standard ERM learning and adversarially robust learning.
As observed by prior works
[
32
,
6
]
, if a model is adversarially trained on the source domain, then its standard accuracy on target domain is sometimes better than if it had been fitted via vanilla ERM on source domain.
In this section, we try to explain this phenomena from adversarially robust domain adaptation perspective.
We found that, when adversarial budget is large enough,
small source adversarial risk almost guarantees the small target domain standard risk
.
First, we need to introduce the following optimization problem.

Definition 6
.

Let
\mathbf{p}
and
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
be two vectors on the
N
-dimensional simplex and let
\boldsymbol{\ell}
be a 0-1 vector.
Let also
\Lambda
be an arbitrary subset of
[N]\vcentcolon=\{1,\ldots,N\}
.
The
Subset Sum Problem with Structural Objective
can be defined as solving the following combinatorial optimization problem:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} \min_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\in\{0,1\}^{N}}\quad&\left|\mathbf{p}^{\top}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\ell}}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime\top}\boldsymbol{\ell}\right|\\
\textrm{s.t.}\quad&\tilde{{\ell}}_{i}={\ell}_{i},\ \forall\ i\in[N]\setminus\Lambda\\
\end{aligned}\right.\kern 5.0pt.

We denote its optimal value as
V^{*}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p},\boldsymbol{\ell},\Lambda)
.

The above problem is a variant of Subset Sum Problem
[
12
]
, which is also
NP-complete
.
We look for a subset of coordinates of a simplex vector
\mathbf{p}
, such that their sum is closest to a given goal.
The given goal has special structure: it is defined as sum of a subset of coordinates in another simplex vector
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
.
If the constraint set
\Lambda_{\epsilon}
has more indices, the optimal value will be smaller since we can determine the value on more coordinates of
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
.
In the following lemma, we explain how this combinatorial measure helps us to connect adversarially robust and standard risks for the binary classification task.

Lemma 5
.

Consider binary classification task, with sign linear classifier class
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{\mathbf{w}}:h_{\mathbf{w}}=\sign(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}),\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}\leq W\}
and 0-1 loss function
\phi(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}|x-y|
.
Assume all domains share the same labeling function
y(\mathbf{x})\in\{-1,1\}
. The following statement holds for any
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(h_{\mathbf{w}},y)\leq\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{label}_{\mathcal{T}}(h_{\mathbf{w}},y)+V^{*}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p},\boldsymbol{\ell},\Lambda_{\epsilon}),

where
\boldsymbol{\ell}
is the loss vector such that
\ell_{i}=\frac{1}{2}|\sign(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{i})-y(\mathbf{x}_{i})|
, with
\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{X}
.
The vectors
\mathbf{p}
,
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}
are probability mass vectors of
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, i.e.,
\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbb{P}_{X\sim\mathcal{T}}(X=\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
and
\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbb{P}_{X\sim\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(X=\mathbf{x}),\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}

Moreover,
\Lambda_{\epsilon}=\{i:|\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{i}|\leq\epsilon\|\mathbf{w}\|_{1},\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{X},\forall\mathbf{w},\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}\leq W\}
.

The corresponding proof is given in Appendix
G
.
Lemma
5
shows that, the standard risk on domain
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
can be bounded by robust risk on domain
\mathcal{T}
, plus the quantity controlled by
\epsilon
.
Since the set
\Lambda_{\epsilon}
stores all indices
i
such that we can choose to flip
\tilde{\ell}_{i}
’s value between
0
and
1
,
then if we have larger adversarial budget
\epsilon
, there will be more indices in
\Lambda_{\epsilon}
, which means there are more coordinates in
\tilde{\boldsymbol{\ell}}
we can play with, and hence smaller value of
V^{*}
.

Comparison with ERM model

Lemma
5
also apply to standard risk on
\mathcal{T}
, which means
\epsilon=0
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(h_{\mathbf{w}},y)\leq\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(h_{\mathbf{w}},y)+V^{*}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p},\boldsymbol{\ell},\Lambda_{0}).

In this case,
V^{*}
become large, since for any
\epsilon>0
, we have
|\Lambda_{\epsilon}|\geq|\Lambda_{0}|
holding. It turns out that, a small source (standard) risk may not imply a small target risk. Hence the ERM model will yield looser generalization guarantee to different domain, than robust ERM model.

Conclusion

In this paper we propose and analyze the adversarial Rademacher complexity over

Basic lemmas

Lemma 6

(Basic squared norm inequality)
.

For any vector
a,b
, we have that
\displaystyle\left\|a-b\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq 2\left\|a\right\|_{2}^{2}+2\left\|b\right\|_{2}^{2}
.

Lemma 7

(Hölder inequality)
.

Let
p\in\mathbb{R}
such that
1<p<\infty
. Let
p^{*}
be its conjugate, that is if
1<p<\infty
,
p^{*}
is such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{*}}=1
.
Let
\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then the following inequality holds

|\langle\mathbf{v},\,\mathbf{w}\rangle|\leq\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{p}\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{p^{*}}\kern 5.0pt.

If
p=1
, we set
p^{*}=\infty
.

Lemma 8

(Equivalence of
p
-norms)
.

Let
q>p\geq 1
, then for all
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have
\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{q}\leq\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{p}\leq d^{1/p-1/q}\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{q}
.
It also holds for
q=\infty
, that is
\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{p}\leq d^{1/p}\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{\infty}
.

Lemma 9

(Maximum dot product over
\ell_{\infty}
ball)
.

The
\ell_{\infty}
and
\ell_{1}
norms are duals of each other. That is:

\max_{\left\|x\right\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}z^{\top}x=\epsilon\left\|z\right\|_{1}\kern 5.0pt,

(18)

and this maximum is attained for
x^{*}=\epsilon\mathsf{sgn}(z)
, where
\mathsf{sgn}
denotes the element-wise sign function.

Proof.

Hölder inequality implies that for all
z,x\in\real^{p}
,

z^{\top}x\leq|z^{\top}x|\leq\left\|x\right\|_{\infty}\left\|z\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon\left\|z\right\|_{1}\kern 5.0pt.

Finally, we notice this upper bound is reached for
x^{*}=\epsilon\mathsf{sgn}(z)
as

z^{\top}x^{*}=\epsilon z^{\top}\mathsf{sgn}(z)=\epsilon\sum_{i=1}^{p}z_{i}\mathsf{sgn}(z_{i})=\epsilon\left\|z\right\|_{1}\kern 5.0pt.

∎

(Minimum dot product over

, the solution of

\min_{\left\|x\right\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}z^{\top}x=-\epsilon\left\|z\right\|_{1}\kern 5.0pt,

(19)

is attained at
x^{*}=-\epsilon\mathsf{sgn}(z)
.

Proof.

Same reasoning as in
Lemma

9
.

Lemma 11

(Lower bound of minimum “quadratic” form over
\ell_{\infty}
ball)
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the solution of

\min_{\left\|x\right\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}\langle u,\,x\rangle\langle v,\,x\rangle\geq-\epsilon^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{1}\left\|v\right\|_{1}\kern 5.0pt,

(20)

Proof.

Let
x\in B_{\infty}(0_{d},\epsilon)
which denotes the
\ell_{\infty}
centered ball of radius
\epsilon>0
.
Hölder’s inequality for dual norms applied twice gives us:

|\langle u,\,x\rangle\langle v,\,x\rangle|\leq\left\|x\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{1}\left\|v\right\|_{1}\leq\epsilon^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{1}\left\|v\right\|_{1}\kern 5.0pt,

which directly implies that

\min_{\left\|x\right\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}\langle u,\,x\rangle\langle v,\,x\rangle\geq-\epsilon^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{1}\left\|v\right\|_{1}\kern 5.0pt.

∎

Remark 1
.

We make several comments on the above

:

•

Note that if

, then the objective becomes positive and
0
is a simpler and sharp lower bound as it is reached for

.

•

Else if

which means the lower bound in (

•

Else if

Lemma 12

(Maximum squared dot product over
\ell_{\infty}
ball)
.

We have that

\max_{\left\|x\right\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}(z^{\top}x)^{2}=\epsilon^{2}\left\|z\right\|_{1}^{2}\kern 5.0pt,

(21)

and this maximum is attained for
x\in\{\epsilon\mathsf{sgn}(z),-\epsilon\mathsf{sgn}(z)\}
, where

Proof.

Hölder inequality implies that for all

(z^{\top}x)^{2}\leq\left\|x\right\|_{\infty}^{2}\left\|z\right\|_{1}^{2}\leq\epsilon^{2}\left\|z\right\|_{1}^{2}\kern 5.0pt.

Lemma 13
.

Let
{\bf A}
be a symmetric matrix, we have that

\displaystyle\sup_{\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{2}\leq W,\left\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}\leq W}\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=\left\|{\bf A}\right\|_{2}\kern 5.0pt.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
with
\ell_{2}-
norm smaller than
W
.
By Cauchy-Schwarz’s inequality we directly get that

\mathbf{w}^{\top}{\bf A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\leq|\langle\mathbf{w},\,{\bf A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\rangle|\overset{\text{Cauchy-Schwarz}}{\leq}\left\|{\bf A}\right\|_{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right\|_{2}\leq W^{2}\left\|{\bf A}\right\|_{2}\kern 5.0pt.

(22)

We then perform eigendecomposition on
{\bf A}
:

\mathbf{w}^{\top}{\bf A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=\mathbf{w}^{\top}{\bf U}{\bf\Sigma}{\bf U}^{\top}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}=W^{2}\mathbf{y}^{\top}{\bf\Sigma}\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\kern 5.0pt,

where
{\bf\Sigma}
is a diagonal matrix containing eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
’s of
{\bf A}
,
{\bf U}
is an orthogonal matrix since
{\bf A}
is symmetric,
\mathbf{y}\vcentcolon=\frac{1}{W}{\bf U}^{\top}\mathbf{w}
and
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}\vcentcolon=\frac{1}{W}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
.
In this orthogonal basis, let
i^{*}
be the coordinate of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{i^{*}}
with largest magnitude in absolute value.
We denote by
(\mathbf{e}_{i})_{i\in[d]}
the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Let
\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{e}_{i^{*}}
and
\mathbf{y}^{\prime}=\mathsf{sgn}(\lambda_{i^{*}})\mathbf{y}
, we get

\mathbf{y}^{\top}{\bf\Sigma}\mathbf{y}^{\prime}=|\lambda_{i^{*}}|=\sqrt{\max_{i\in[d]}\lambda_{i}({\bf A})^{2}}=\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}({\bf A}^{2})}=\left\|{\bf A}\right\|_{2}\kern 5.0pt.

Thus, the upper bound in (
22
) is attained by inverting the change of variable from
\mathbf{y},\mathbf{y}^{\prime}
to
\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
.

∎

Lemma 14
.

Let
{\bf A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. Then the following statements hold:

\sup_{\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}\leq W,\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{p}\leq W}\mathbf{w}^{\top}{\bf A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\leq\sup_{\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}\leq W,\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{2}\leq W}\mathbf{w}^{\top}{\bf A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\cdot\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }1\leq p\leq 2\\
d^{1-2/p},&\text{else if }p>2\end{cases}\kern 5.0pt,

(23)

\sup_{\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}\leq W,\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{p}\leq W}\mathbf{w}^{\top}{\bf A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\geq\sup_{\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}\leq W,\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{2}\leq W}\mathbf{w}^{\top}{\bf A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\cdot\begin{cases}d^{1-2/p},&\text{if }1\leq p\leq 2\\
1,\quad&\text{else if }p>2\end{cases}\kern 5.0pt.

(24)

Proof.

We begin with proving the first inequality. If
1\leq p\leq 2
, we know that:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{2}(W).

Hence
\sup_{\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}\leq W,\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{p}\leq W}\mathbf{w}^{\top}{\bf A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\leq\sup_{\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}\leq W,\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{2}\leq W}\mathbf{w}^{\top}{\bf A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
.

If
p>2
, since
\frac{1}{d^{1/2-1/p}}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}\leq\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}
, we know that
\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}\leq W
implies
\frac{1}{d^{1/2-1/p}}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}\leq W
. So we have:

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)\vcentcolon=\{\mathbf{w}:\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{p}\leq W\}\subseteq\{\mathbf{w}:\frac{1}{d^{1/2-1/p}}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}\leq W\}\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{2}(Wd^{1/2-1/p}).

Hence:

\displaystyle\sup_{\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}\leq W,\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{p}\leq W}\mathbf{w}^{\top}{\bf A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}\leq Wd^{1/2-1/p},\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{2}\leq Wd^{1/2-1/p}}\mathbf{w}^{\top}{\bf A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}\leq W,\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{2}\leq W}\mathbf{w}^{\top}{\bf A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\cdot d^{1-2/p}\kern 5.0pt.

Now we switch to prove the second inequality.
If
1\leq p\leq 2
, then
\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}\geq\frac{1}{d^{1/p-1/2}}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}
, so we know

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{2}(W)\vcentcolon=\{\mathbf{w}:\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{2}\leq W\}\subseteq\{\mathbf{w}:\frac{1}{d^{1/p-1/2}}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}\leq W\}=\mathcal{B}_{p}(d^{1/p-1/2}W).

Hence:

\displaystyle\sup_{\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}\leq W,\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{2}\leq W}\mathbf{w}^{\top}{\bf A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}\leq d^{1/p-1/2}W,\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{p}\leq d^{1/p-1/2}W}\mathbf{w}^{\top}{\bf A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq d^{2/p-1}\sup_{\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}\leq W,\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{p}\leq W}\mathbf{w}^{\top}{\bf A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\kern 5.0pt.

If

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{2}(W)\subseteq\mathcal{B}_{p}(W),

so we can conclude the relation:

\displaystyle\sup_{\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}\leq W,\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{2}\leq W}\mathbf{w}^{\top}{\bf A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\leq\sup_{\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}\leq W,\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{p}\leq W}\mathbf{w}^{\top}{\bf A}\mathbf{w}^{\prime}.

Lemma 16
.

Let

denotes in the mean time a single element and a coordinate wise sign operator, i.e.,
\mathsf{sgn}(a)\in\real
but
\mathsf{sgn}(\mathbf{w})\in\real^{p}

(\mathbf{w}^{T}\boldsymbol{\delta}^{*}+a)^{2}=(\epsilon\mathsf{sgn}(a)\mathbf{w}^{T}\mathsf{sgn}(\mathbf{w})+a)^{2}=(\epsilon\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{1}+|a|)^{2}\kern 5.0pt.

Proof.

Let us give a first intuition and proof in dimension one and then extend this to larger dimensions.

•

Case

(
\mathbf{w}
becomes

).
In this setting the problem intuition is clear: one should select
\boldsymbol{\delta}
, with maximal amplitude, that makes
\boldsymbol{\delta}w
having the same sign as

If

and

have the same sign, then
\boldsymbol{\delta}=\epsilon
.
Else,
\boldsymbol{\delta}=-\epsilon

•

Case

For all

Let
\boldsymbol{\delta}\in\real^{p}

.
Then, this implies that on the feasible set

(27)

Thus,

\displaystyle(\mathbf{w}^{T}\boldsymbol{\delta}+a)^{2}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{w}^{T}\boldsymbol{\delta})^{2}+2a\mathbf{w}^{T}\boldsymbol{\delta}+a^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:dotprod_l1_upper_bound}}{\leq}\epsilon^{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{1}^{2}+2a\mathbf{w}^{T}\boldsymbol{\delta}+a^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{2}\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{1}^{2}+2|a||\mathbf{w}^{T}\boldsymbol{\delta}|+a^{2}

\displaystyle=(\epsilon\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{1}+|a|)^{2}\kern 5.0pt.

Finally, one can check that upper bound of the objective is attained for

Lemma 17
.

Let
\mathbf{w}\in\real^{p}
,
a\in\real
and
\epsilon\geq 0
.
Let us consider the problem

\boldsymbol{\delta}^{*}=\argmin_{\boldsymbol{\delta}\in\real^{p}:\left\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}(\mathbf{w}^{T}\boldsymbol{\delta}+a)^{2}\kern 5.0pt.

(28)

Let

.

•

If

\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}=-\frac{a}{\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{1}}\frac{\mathbf{w}_{i}}{|\mathbf{w}_{i}|}&\quad\quad\forall i\in I\\
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}=0&\quad\quad\forall i\in[p]\backslash I\end{cases}\kern 5.0pt.

•

Else

\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}=-\epsilon\frac{a}{|a|}\frac{\mathbf{w}_{i}}{|\mathbf{w}_{i}|}&\quad\quad\forall i\in I\\

This solution can be condensed in the following formulation:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}=-a\frac{w_{i}}{|w_{i}|}\min\left\{\frac{1}{\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{1}},\frac{\epsilon}{|a|}\right\}&\quad\quad\forall i\in I\\

(29)

The minimal value is given by:

\displaystyle\min_{\boldsymbol{\delta}\in\real^{p}:\left\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}(\mathbf{w}^{T}\boldsymbol{\delta}+a)^{2}=a^{2}\left(1-\min\left\{1,\frac{\epsilon\|\mathbf{w}\|_{1}}{|a|}\right\}\right)^{2}.

Remark 2
.

Note that in general there are an infinite number of solutions to (
28
) as in (
29
) one can choose arbitrarily the value of
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}
for all
i\in[p]\backslash I
(as soon as it is kept smaller than
\epsilon
in absolute value).

Proof.

Let
[p]\vcentcolon=\{1,\ldots,p\}
.
Let us try to build a solution which drives the dot product
\mathbf{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\delta}
towards
-a
.
Let
I\vcentcolon=\{i\in[p]:\mathbf{w}_{i}\neq 0\}
.

Case 1:

If

\boldsymbol{\epsilon\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{1}\geq|a|.}

Let us
\boldsymbol{\delta}^{*}\in\real^{p}
such that

\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}=-\frac{a}{\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{1}}\frac{\mathbf{w}_{i}}{|\mathbf{w}_{i}|}&\quad\quad\forall i\in I\\
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}=0&\quad\quad\forall i\in[p]\backslash I\end{cases}\kern 5.0pt.

This vector is in the feasible set as
\left\|\boldsymbol{\delta}^{*}\right\|_{\infty}=\max_{i\in I}\frac{|a|}{\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{1}}\frac{|\mathbf{w}_{i}|}{|\mathbf{w}_{i}|}=\frac{|a|}{\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{1}}\leq\epsilon
, as assumed.
Then,

\mathbf{w}^{T}\boldsymbol{\delta}^{*}+a=-\frac{a}{\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{1}}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\mathbf{w}_{i}^{2}}{|\mathbf{w}_{i}|}+a=0\kern 5.0pt.

This means that if the entries of vector
\mathbf{w}
are large enough (in absolute value), we can build a feasible vector
\boldsymbol{\delta}^{*}
such that the objective in (
28
) is zero.

Case 2:

If

\boldsymbol{\epsilon\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{1}<|a|.}

Let us
\boldsymbol{\delta}^{*}\in\real^{p}
such that

\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}=-\epsilon\frac{a}{|a|}\frac{\mathbf{w}_{i}}{|\mathbf{w}_{i}|}&\quad\quad\forall i\in I\\
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}=0&\quad\quad\forall i\in[p]\backslash I\end{cases}\kern 5.0pt.

This vector is in the feasible set as
\left\|\boldsymbol{\delta}^{*}\right\|_{\infty}=\max_{i\in I}\epsilon\frac{|a|}{|a|}\frac{|\mathbf{w}_{i}|}{|\mathbf{w}_{i}|}=\epsilon
, as assumed.
Then,

\mathbf{w}^{T}\boldsymbol{\delta}^{*}+a=-\epsilon\frac{a}{|a|}\sum_{i\in I}\frac{\mathbf{w}_{i}^{2}}{|\mathbf{w}_{i}|}+a=a\underbrace{\left(1-\epsilon\frac{\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{1}}{|a|}\right)}_{\in[0,1]}\kern 5.0pt.

This means that if the entries of vector
\mathbf{w}
are too small (in absolute value), we can only build a feasible vector
\boldsymbol{\delta}^{*}
such that
\mathbf{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\delta}^{*}
close too
-a
.
And the corresponding objective in (
28
) becomes
a^{2}\left(1-\epsilon\frac{\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{1}}{|a|}\right)^{2}
.

Finally, one just can show with Hölder inequality that
(\mathbf{w}^{T}\boldsymbol{\delta}+a)^{2}\geq a^{2}\left(1-\epsilon\frac{\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{1}}{|a|}\right)^{2}
for all
\boldsymbol{\delta}
in the feasible set, which concludes the proof.
If
a\geq 0
, the computation follows easily, else we can just replace
\boldsymbol{\delta}\leftarrow-\boldsymbol{\delta}
to get back to the former case.

∎

The following Lemma gives the bound if we estimate adversarial
\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}
discrepancy from standard
\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}
discrepancy.

Lemma 19
.

The following relations between adversarial discrepancy from standard discrepancy holds for linear model class with bounded norm:
\mathcal{H}=\{h_{\mathbf{w}}:\mathbf{x}\mapsto\langle\mathbf{w},\,\mathbf{x}\rangle,\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}\leq W\}
.
For
L_{\phi}
-Lipschitz binary classification loss, we have:

\displaystyle disc^{adv}_{\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}}(\hat{\mathcal{S}},\hat{\mathcal{T}})

\displaystyle\leq disc_{\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}}(\hat{\mathcal{S}},\hat{\mathcal{T}})

\displaystyle\quad+2W^{2}L_{\phi}\sqrt{d}\epsilon\left(\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}}\|\mathbf{x}_{i}\|_{2}+\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{S}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{S}}}\|\mathbf{x}_{i}\|_{2}\right)\cdot\begin{cases}1&\hskip-10.00002pt1\leq p\leq 2\\
d^{1-2/p}&p>2\end{cases}\kern 5.0pt.

For
\ell_{2}
regression loss, we have:

\displaystyle disc^{adv}_{\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}}(\hat{\mathcal{S}},\hat{\mathcal{T}})

\displaystyle\leq disc_{\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}}(\hat{\mathcal{S}},\hat{\mathcal{T}})

\displaystyle\quad+8\sqrt{d}\epsilon W^{2}\left(\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}}\|\mathbf{x}_{i}\|_{2}+\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{S}}}\|\mathbf{x}_{i}\|_{2}\right)\cdot\begin{cases}1&\hskip-10.00002pt1\leq p\leq 2\\
d^{1-2/p}&p>2\end{cases}\kern 5.0pt.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)\vcentcolon=\{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\left\|\mathbf{w}\right\|_{p}\leq W\}
be the
\ell_{p}
-norm centered ball with radius
W
.
By the definition of
disc^{adv}_{\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}}(\hat{\mathcal{S}},\hat{\mathcal{T}})
, we have:

\displaystyle disc^{adv}_{\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}}(\hat{\mathcal{S}},\hat{\mathcal{T}})\overset{\eqref{eq:def_adversarial_hdeltah_discrepancy}}{=}\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\{\mathbf{w}:\|\mathbf{w}\|_{p}\leq W\}}|\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})-\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{S}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}|\mathcal{R}_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})-\mathcal{R}_{\hat{\mathcal{S}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})+\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})-\mathcal{R}_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\hskip 250.00038pt-(\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{S}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})-\mathcal{R}_{\hat{\mathcal{S}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}))|

\displaystyle{\leq}disc_{\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}}(\hat{\mathcal{S}},\hat{\mathcal{T}})+\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}|\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})-\mathcal{R}_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\quad+\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}|\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{S}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})-\mathcal{R}_{\hat{\mathcal{S}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})|\kern 5.0pt.

Now we study the gap
\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}|\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})-\mathcal{R}_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})|
and
\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}|\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{S}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})-\mathcal{R}_{\hat{\mathcal{S}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})|
.
For linear classification, we have:

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}|\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})-\mathcal{R}_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=\!\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}\left|\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}}\max_{\boldsymbol{\delta}:\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}(\phi(\langle\mathbf{w},\,\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta}\rangle\cdot\langle\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\,\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta}\rangle)-\phi(\langle\mathbf{w},\,\mathbf{x}_{i}\rangle\cdot\langle\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\,\mathbf{x}_{i}\rangle))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}\left|\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}}\max_{\boldsymbol{\delta}:\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}L_{\phi}|(\langle\mathbf{w},\,\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta}\rangle\cdot\langle\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\,\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta}\rangle)-(\langle\mathbf{w},\,\mathbf{x}_{i}\rangle\cdot\langle\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\,\mathbf{x}_{i}\rangle)|\right|

\displaystyle\leq\!\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}\left|\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}}\max_{\boldsymbol{\delta}:\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}L_{\phi}|\mathbf{w}^{\top}\left((\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta})(\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta})^{\top}-\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}\right)\mathbf{w}^{\prime}|\right|

\displaystyle\leq\!L_{\phi}\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}}\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}\left|\mathbf{w}^{\top}\left((\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*})(\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*})^{\top}-\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}\right)\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\right|

\displaystyle{\leq}L_{\phi}\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}}\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}\|(\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*})(\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*})^{\top}-\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}\|_{2}\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq L_{\phi}\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}}\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}+\mathbf{x}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}}^{\top}\|_{2}\|\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\|_{2}\kern 5.0pt,

where
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}
is a maximizer of the
i

\boldsymbol{\delta}
over the
\ell_{\infty}
-ball of radius
\epsilon
.
We know that
\|\mathbf{w}\|_{2}\leq W\cdot\begin{cases}1&\hskip-10.00002pt1\leq p\leq 2\\
d^{1/2-1/p}&p>2\end{cases}
, and
\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}+\mathbf{x}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}}^{\top}\|_{2}\leq 2\sqrt{d}\epsilon\|\mathbf{x}_{i}\|_{2}
, so we have:

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}|\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})-\mathcal{R}_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leq W^{2}L_{\phi}\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}}2\sqrt{d}\epsilon\|\mathbf{x}_{i}\|_{2}\cdot\begin{cases}1&\hskip-10.00002pt1\leq p\leq 2\\
d^{1-2/p}&p>2\end{cases}.

which concludes the proof for linear classification setting. Now we switch to regression setting:

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}|\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})-\mathcal{R}_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}\left|\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}}\max_{\boldsymbol{\delta}:\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}\|\langle\mathbf{w},\,\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta}\rangle-\langle\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\,\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta}\rangle\|^{2}_{2}-\|\langle\mathbf{w},\,\mathbf{x}_{i}\rangle-\langle\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\,\mathbf{x}_{i}\rangle\|^{2}_{2}\right|

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}\left|\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}}\max_{\boldsymbol{\delta}:\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}\|\langle\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\,\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta}\rangle\|^{2}_{2}-\|\langle\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime},\,\mathbf{x}_{i}\rangle\|^{2}_{2}\right|

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}\left|\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}}{(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime})^{\top}}\left[(\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*})(\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*})^{\top}-\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}\right](\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle=\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}\left|{(\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime})^{\top}}\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}}\left[\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}+\mathbf{x}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}}^{\top}\right](\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime})\right|

Now, we let
\mathbf{v}:=\mathbf{w}-\mathbf{w}^{\prime}
, and re-write the above inequality as:

\displaystyle\max_{\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}_{p}(W)^{2}}

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})-\mathcal{R}_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{w}^{\prime})|

\displaystyle{\leq}\max_{\mathbf{v}:\|\mathbf{v}\|_{p}\leq 2W}\left\|{\mathbf{v}^{\top}}\right\|_{2}\left\|\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}}\left[\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}+\mathbf{x}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}}^{\top}\right]\right\|_{2}\left\|\mathbf{v}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}}\left[\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}+\mathbf{x}_{i}{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}^{*}}^{\top}\right]\right\|_{2}4W\cdot\begin{cases}1&p\leq 2\\
d^{1-2/p}&p>2\end{cases}

\displaystyle\leq 8\sqrt{d}W\frac{1}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}\sum_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\hat{\mathcal{T}}}\|\mathbf{x}_{i}\|_{2}\cdot\begin{cases}1&p\leq 2\\
d^{1-2/p}&p>2\end{cases}.

where we use norm equivalence (

.

Corollary 1

(Adversarially Robust Domain Adapation Learning Bound, Linear Regression)

\hat{\mathcal{S}}
and
\hat{\mathcal{T}}

\displaystyle+4disc^{adv}_{\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H}}(\hat{\mathcal{T}},\hat{\mathcal{S}})+3\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}_{\mathcal{T}}(h_{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}},h_{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{\mathcal{S}}})+3\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{label}_{\mathcal{T}}(h_{\mathbf{w}^{*}_{\mathcal{S}}},y_{\mathcal{T}})

\displaystyle+M\mathcal{O}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\log(2/c)}{n_{\mathcal{S}}}}+\sqrt{\frac{\log(2/c)}{n_{\mathcal{T}}}}\right)+3\hat{\mathfrak{R}}_{\mathcal{S}}({\ell}\circ\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H})+3\hat{\mathfrak{R}}_{\mathcal{T}}({\ell}\circ\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle+\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{W^{2}}{\sqrt{n_{\mathcal{S}}}}\left({d}\epsilon\left\|{\bf X}_{\mathcal{S}}\right\|_{2,\infty}+d^{3/2}\epsilon^{2}\right)+\frac{W^{2}}{\sqrt{n_{\mathcal{T}}}}\left({d}\epsilon\left\|{\bf X}_{\mathcal{T}}\right\|_{2,\infty}+d^{3/2}\epsilon^{2}\right)\!\right)\!\times\!\begin{cases}1,&\hskip-10.00002pt1\leq p\leq 2\\

and

Proof.

The following proof is almost identical to that of

2

∎

Proof.

d^{1-2/p},&\text{else if }p>2\end{cases}\kern 5.0pt,

due to equivalence of norms.
Now, we denote
\mathbf{v}^{*}
such that
\mathbf{v}^{*}=\arg\max_{\|\mathbf{v}\|_{2}\leq 2W}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{i})^{2}
.
One can verify that the maximum value of

\displaystyle\sup_{\|\mathbf{v}\|_{2}\leq 2W}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{i})^{2}=\frac{1}{n}\sup_{\|\mathbf{v}\|_{2}\leq 2W}\mathbf{v}^{\top}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}\right)\mathbf{v}\leq\frac{4W^{2}}{n}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{\top}\right\|_{2}

the maxima is attained when

.

Now, we switch to adversarial Rademacher:

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{D}}}(\tilde{\ell}\circ\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{\|\mathbf{v}\|_{p}\leq 2W}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}\max_{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}(\mathbf{v}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{\delta}))^{2}\right]

1,\quad&\text{else if }p>2\end{cases}

16
, we have:

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{R}}_{\hat{\mathcal{D}}}(\tilde{\ell}\circ\mathcal{H}\Delta\mathcal{H})=\mathbb{E}\left[\sup_{\|\mathbf{v}\|_{2}\leq 2W}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}(\epsilon\|\mathbf{v}\|_{1}+|\mathbf{v}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{i}|)^{2}\right]\cdot\begin{cases}d^{1-2/p},&\text{if }1\leq p\leq 2\\
1,\quad&\text{else if }p>2\end{cases}.

:

First, to avoid confusion in different Rademacher variables, let us use
\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}
and
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
to denote the Rademacher variables in

\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}^{\prime}}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma^{\prime}_{i}(\epsilon\|\mathbf{v}^{\prime*}\|_{1}+|{\mathbf{v}^{\prime*}}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{i}|)^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{i}({\mathbf{v}^{*}}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{i})^{2}\right]

\mathcal{T}
within the adversary set, the perturbed risk can approximate the standard risk on
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
with some error.
First, let us define a perturbed risk for any perturbation

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(h_{\mathbf{w}},y,{\boldsymbol{\delta}})

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{T}}\left[\ell(h_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{x}+\boldsymbol{\delta}),y(\mathbf{x})\right]

(64)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{x})\frac{1}{2}|\sign(\mathbf{w}(\mathbf{x}+\boldsymbol{\delta}(\mathbf{x}))-y(\mathbf{x})|

\displaystyle=\mathbf{p}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tilde{\ell}}\left(\{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{X}|}\right)\kern 5.0pt.

(65)

We then recall the definition of the standard risk on
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(h_{\mathbf{w}},y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\mathbf{p}^{\prime}(\mathbf{x})\frac{1}{2}|\sign(\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x})-y(\mathbf{x})|

\displaystyle=\mathbf{p}^{\prime\top}\boldsymbol{{\ell}}\kern 5.0pt.

(66)

We recall the definition of adversarially robust risk over domain
\mathcal{T}
for the labeling function
y(\cdot)

\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{label}_{\mathcal{T}}(h_{\mathbf{w}},y)=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim\mathcal{T}}\left[\max_{\left\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\right\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}\ell(h_{\mathbf{w}}(\mathbf{w}+\boldsymbol{\delta}),y(\mathbf{x}))\right]\kern 5.0pt.

(67)

So, for any
\boldsymbol{\delta}
such that
\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon
, we get that

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(h_{\mathbf{w}},y)-\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{label}_{\mathcal{T}}(h_{\mathbf{w}},y)\overset{\eqref{eq:perturbated_risk}-\eqref{eq:adversarially_robust_risk_over_T}}{\leq}\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(h_{\mathbf{w}},y)-\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}}(h_{\mathbf{w}},y,\boldsymbol{\delta})\overset{\eqref{eq:perturbated_risk_over_T_scalar_product}-\eqref{eq:standard_risk_over_Tprime_scalar_product}}{=}\mathbf{p}^{\prime\top}\boldsymbol{{\ell}}-\mathbf{p}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tilde{\ell}}(\{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{X}|})\kern 5.0pt.

Since the above inequality holds for any
\boldsymbol{\delta}
such that
\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon
, we must have

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}(h_{\mathbf{w}},y)-\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}^{label}_{\mathcal{T}}(h_{\mathbf{w}},y)\leq\min_{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}|\mathbf{p}^{\prime\top}\boldsymbol{{\ell}}-\mathbf{p}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tilde{\ell}}(\{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{X}|})|\kern 5.0pt.

Now, let’s examine the coordinates in
\Lambda
.
For
i
-th coordinate, if it is in
\Lambda
, we know that

\displaystyle-\epsilon\|\mathbf{w}\|_{1}\leq\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{i}\leq\epsilon\|\mathbf{w}\|_{1}\kern 5.0pt,

which implies that, there is a
\boldsymbol{\delta}
that can change the sign of
\sign(\mathbf{w}(\mathbf{x}+\boldsymbol{\delta}))
, and hence change the value of
\tilde{\ell}_{i}
. That is, if
\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}y(\mathbf{x})\geq 0
, there is a
\boldsymbol{\delta}^{*}=\arg\min_{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}\mathbf{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\delta}y(\mathbf{x})
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}^{\top}(\mathbf{x}+\boldsymbol{\delta}^{*})y(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}y(\mathbf{x})-\epsilon\|\mathbf{w}\|_{1}\leq 0\kern 5.0pt.

Similarly, if
\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}y(\mathbf{x})\leq 0
, there is a
\boldsymbol{\delta}^{*}=\arg\max_{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}\mathbf{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\delta}y(\mathbf{x})
, such that:

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}y(\mathbf{x})+\mathbf{w}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\delta}^{*}y(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{w}^{\top}\mathbf{x}y(\mathbf{x})+\epsilon\|\mathbf{w}\|_{1}\geq 0\kern 5.0pt.

Finally, we get

\displaystyle\min_{\|\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq\epsilon}|\mathbf{p}^{\prime\top}\boldsymbol{{\ell}}-\mathbf{p}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tilde{\ell}}(\{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{|\mathcal{X}|})|

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{aligned} \min_{\tilde{\boldsymbol{\ell}}\in\{0,1\}^{N}}\quad&\left|\mathbf{p}^{\top}\tilde{\boldsymbol{\ell}}-\mathbf{p}^{\prime\top}\boldsymbol{\ell}\right|\\
\textrm{s.t.}\quad&\tilde{{\ell}}_{i}={\ell}_{i},\ \forall\ i\in[N]\setminus\Lambda\\
\end{aligned}\right.

\displaystyle=V^{*}(\mathbf{p}^{\prime},\mathbf{p},\boldsymbol{\ell},\Lambda)\kern 5.0pt.

∎

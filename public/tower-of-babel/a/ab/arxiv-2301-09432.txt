Monoidal Properties of Franke’s Exotic Equivalence
Nikitas Nikandros, Constanze Roitzheim

Abstract
. Franke’s reconstruction functor ℛ \mathcal{R} is known to provide examples of triangulated equivalences between homotopy categories of stable model categories, which are exotic in the sense that the underlying model categories are not Quillen equivalent. We show that, while not being a tensor-triangulated functor in general, ℛ \mathcal{R} is compatible with monoidal products.



1.
Introduction

For several decades, Franke’s exotic equivalence has been fascinating to homotopy theorists, as it is a rare example of a machinery that provides an equivalence up to homotopy between two model categories which are not Quillen equivalent. In practice, the known situations where Franke’s construction can be applied to obtain the equivalence

\mathcal{R}\colon\mathrm{D}^{([1],1)}(\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow\Ho(\mathcal{M})

link an algebraic model category (
\mathrm{D}^{([1],1)}(\mathcal{A})
is the derived category of a flavour of chain complexes in a suitable abelian category
\mathcal{A}
) with a stable model category
\mathcal{M}
which is not necessarily algebraic.
Key examples include

•

\mathcal{A}
the category of
\pi_{*}(R)
-modules for a ring spectrum
R
and
\mathcal{M}
the category of modules over
R
, together with some extra assumption on the projective dimension of
\pi_{*}(R)
as well as
\pi_{*}(R)
being concentrated in degrees that are multiples of some
N>1
,

•

\mathcal{A}
the category of
E(1)_{*}E(1)
-comodules and
\mathcal{M}
the category of
K
-local spectra at an odd prime.

In this paper, we will always assume that
\mathcal{R}
exists and is an equivalence.

Both the algebraic side
\mathrm{D}^{([1],1)}(\mathcal{A})
and the topological side
\Ho(\mathcal{M})
are equipped with monoidal structures derived from the monoidal model category structures on
\mathrm{C}^{([1],1)}(\mathcal{A})
and
\mathcal{M}
, so it is only natural to consider whether
\mathcal{R}
is compatible with these. But as

is not derived from a Quillen functor

, this problem requires a different approach working closely with the construction of
\mathcal{R}
itself.

The example of
K
-local spectra at
p=3
tells us that we cannot expect
\mathcal{R}
to be a monoidal functor in general: the preimage of the mod-3 Moore spectrum is a chain complex that is a monoid, whereas the mod-3 Moore spectrum has no associative multiplication
[
Gan07
, Remark 1.4.2]
.
However, we obtain the following, which is the main result of this article.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we will introduce some of the terminology that we need for our result. We assume that the reader is familiar with the basic background regarding simplicial sets, homological algebra and model categories.

The category of simplicial sets is denoted by

. For

Similarly,

We will let

be a graded (

\mathcal{A}

[1]
which is an equivalence of categories, and

. The

2.1.
Model Categories

We will now set up our background on model categories. We write any cofibrant replacement functor
Q\colon\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
that comes with a natural weak equivalence
q\colon Q\rightarrow 1_{\mathcal{M}}
.

Convention 2.1.1
.

We let
\Ho(\mathcal{M})
denote the category
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cof}}[\mathcal{W}^{-1}]
where
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cof}}
denotes the full subcategory of cofibrant objects of
\mathcal{M}
, and we denote the set of morphisms in
\Ho(\mathcal{M})
by
[X,Y].

Convention
2.1.1
allows us to provide a very simple description of the
left derived functor

\mathbb{L}F
of a left Quillen functor
F:\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}
. Indeed, the functor

F\left.\right|_{\mathcal{M}_{\text{cof}}}\colon\mathcal{M}_{\text{cof}}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{\text{cof}}

preserves weak equivalences and, therefore, it induces a functor between the localizations. This functor is precisely
\mathbb{L}F
with our convention.

Finally, an important class of model categories is the class of
simplicial model categories
. These are model categories which are enriched, tensored and cotensored over
s\mathrm{Set}
and which satisfy the pushout-product axiom (SM7). If a
simplicial model category is pointed, i.e., the terminal object is isomorphic to the initial one, then
\mathcal{M}
is enriched over the category
s\mathrm{Set}_{*}
of pointed simplicial sets. In particular, we have functors

-\otimes-\colon s\mathrm{Set}_{*}\times\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M},\ \ \ \Map_{\mathcal{M}}(-.-):\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{M}\rightarrow s\mathrm{Set}_{*},

and the adjunction

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{M}}(K\wedge X,Y)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{s\mathrm{Set}}(K,\Map_{\mathcal{M}}(X,Y)),

see
[
BR20
, Definition 6.1.28]
and
[
Rie14
, Section 11.4]
.

2.1.1.
Diagram Categories

We will use model structures on diagram categories throughout the paper. Below we introduce the definition of a direct category which is a generalization of the concept of a poset, see
[

Definition 2.1.2
.

Let

Definition 2.1.3

Suppose

We can now describe the

Proposition 2.1.4

Given a model category

2.1.2.
Homotopy Colimits in Simplicial Model Categories

In Definition
2.1.10
we recalled the definition of the homotopy colimit as a derived functor. Here, we will present an alternative construction via simplicial techniques. After introducing some definitions we briefly explain how this method provides a good theory of homotopy colimits, see also
[
Rie14

[

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a model category and consider the category of simplicial objects

as a simplicial category with tensors defined objectwise, i.e., for

and

Now, let
\mathcal{M}
be a simplicial model category. Given a simplicial object

we can construct an object in
\mathcal{M}
via

[

Definition 2.1.12

(Geometric Realization)
.

Let

The geometric realization
of

is defined as the coequalizer

\operatorname{coeq}\left(\coprod_{\sigma\colon[n]\rightarrow[k]\in\Delta}\Delta^{k}\otimes X_{n}\rightrightarrows\coprod_{[n]\in\Delta}\Delta^{n}\otimes X_{n}\right).

This is an example of a functor tensor product (coend). In this case, the geometric realization is the functor tensor product of

and the
functor
\Delta^{\bullet}\colon\Delta\rightarrow s\mathrm{Set},[n]\mapsto\Delta^{n}.
In other words, the realization

is the object

\Delta^{\bullet}\otimes_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}X=\int^{n}\Delta^{n}\otimes X_{n}.

The following theorem is the cornerstone of our exposition of homotopy colimits using geometric realizations, see
[

Theorem 2.1.13
.

If

\left|-\right|\colon\mathcal{M}^{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}\to\mathcal{M}

At this level of generality, this is the strongest result possible. It is not true that geometric realization preserves all objectwise weak equivalences. However, the above will suffice for our purposes. We can now start to work our way to the homotopy colimit of a diagram

as the coequilizer

\coprod_{i}X_{i}\leftleftarrows\coprod_{j\leftarrow i}X_{i},

but this is precisely the colimit of
X
. Therefore in this case,
\srep(X)
has the augmentation

\srep(F)\rightarrow\colim_{I}F,

where we regard the object
\colim_{I}F
as a constant simplicial object.

We therefore reach the following result.

Lemma 2.1.16
.

Given a diagram
X\in\mathcal{M}^{I}
and its simplicial replacement
\srep(X)\in\mathcal{M}^{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}
, there is a canonical isomorphism

\colim_{I}X\cong\colim_{\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}}\left(\srep(X)\right).

The proof can be found in
[
Rie14
, Lemma 4.4.2]
. The following lemma will also be of importance, see
[
Rie14
, Lemma 5.1.2]
,
[
Shu06
, Lemma 8.7]
.

Lemma 2.1.17
.

Let
I
be a small category and let
\mathcal{M}
be a simplicial model category. If
F\in\mathcal{M}^{I}
is objectwise cofibrant, then
\srep(F)\in s\mathcal{M}
is Reedy cofibrant.

The above Lemma
2.1.16
and Theorem
2.1.13
essentially mean that geomemetric realization of objectwise cofibrant diagrams is a good model for calculating homotopy colimits. For details see
[
Rie14
, Theorem 6.6.1]
.

2.2.
Homotopy Kan Extensions

In this subsection we will introduce
homotopy Kan extensions
, the homotopy invariant version of ordinary Kan extensions, see e.g.
[
Hir03
, Section 11.9]
.

Now, let
\mathcal{M}
be a model category. Furthermore, let
I,J
be direct categories and
f\colon I\rightarrow J
a functor. The pullback functor

f^{*}\colon\mathcal{M}^{J}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{I}

preserves weak equivalences, so it defines a functor between homotopy categories, which we denote by the same letter. Recall the functor
\Lan_{f}=f_{!}
, left adjoint to
f^{*}
.
We have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a model category and let
f\colon I\rightarrow J
be a map of direct categories. Then the adjunction

f_{!}\colon\mathcal{M}^{I}\rightleftarrows\mathcal{M}^{J}\colon f^{*}

is a Quillen adjunction.

Proof.

This follows from the definition of the projective model structure, see Proposition
2.1.4
. The functor
f^{*}
is a right adjoint by construction. It preserves weak equivalences and projective fibrations, which means that
f^{*}
is also a right Quillen functor.
∎

Thus, the derived functors of the adjoint pair
(f_{!},f^{*})
define an adjoint pair on the level of homotopy categories

\mathbb{L}\mathrm{Lan}_{f}:=\mathbb{L}f_{!}\colon\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{I})\rightleftarrows\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{J})\colon\mathbb{R}f^{*}.

A useful fact about homotopy Kan extensions is that they does not change the homotopy colimit of a diagram, which is similar to the properties of ordinary Kan extensions.

Corollary 2.2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a model category,
f\colon I\rightarrow J
a map of direct categories and let
X\in\mathcal{M}^{I}
. Then there is a canonical isomorphism in
\Ho(\mathcal{M})

\hocolim_{J}\mathbb{L}f_{!}X\cong\hocolim_{I}X.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that for every pair of left Quillen functors
F
and
G
there is a natural isomorphism

\mathbb{L}F\circ\mathbb{L}G\rightarrow\mathbb{L}(F\circ G),

see
[
Hov99
, Theorem 1.37]
, together with the natural isomorphism

\colim_{J}\Lan_{f}X\cong\colim_{I}X.

∎

To conclude this section, we will shortly discuss how one calculates the values and edges of a homotopy Kan extension. Recall the notion of a
slice category
for given posets
C
and
D
and a functor
f\colon C\rightarrow D
, namely

(2.2.3)

f/d=\left\{c\in C\colon f(c)\leq d\right\}

for
d\in D
.
The following is
[
Cis09
, Proposition 1.14]
, which tells us that homotopy Kan extensions can be computed pointwise.

Proposition 2.2.4
.

Let
f\colon I\to J
be a map of posets and let
X
be any functor
I\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
. For any object
j\in J
there is a canonical isomorphism in
\Ho(\mathcal{M})

(\mathbb{L}f_{!}F)_{j}\cong\hocolim\left(f/j\xrightarrow{\pi}I\xrightarrow{X}\mathcal{M}\right).

2.3.
Monoidal Model Categories

Let us now turn to some results concerning monoidal model categories,
see e.g.
[
Hov99
, Definition 4.2.6]
,
[
BR11
, Definition 6.1.9]
or
[
Rie14
, Definition 11.4.6]
for definitions.

Remark 2.3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\wedge)
be a closed symmetric monoidal category and let
f\colon X_{0}\rightarrow X_{1}
and
g\colon Y_{0}\rightarrow Y_{1}
be maps in
\mathcal{C}
. The pushout-product map is the universal arrow

f\mathbin{\square}g\colon X_{0}\wedge Y_{1}\coprod_{X_{0}\wedge Y_{0}}X_{1}\otimes Y_{0}\rightarrow X_{1}\wedge Y_{1}.

Another way to see the pushout-product map is as a left Kan extension. Again, consider a cocomplete, (closed) monoidal category
(\mathcal{C},\wedge)
. Let
[1]=\left\{0\leq 1\right\}
. Furthermore, consider the following map of posets.

\displaystyle\mathrm{pr}\colon[1]\times[1]

\displaystyle\rightarrow[1]

\displaystyle(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)

\displaystyle\mapsto 0

\displaystyle(1,1)

\displaystyle\mapsto 1

Now let
f
and
g
be morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
. We can consider them as as objects in the arrow category
f,g\in\mathcal{C}^{[1]}
. The functors
f\colon[1]\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
and
g\colon[1]\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
give rise to their objectwise tensor product
f\wedge g
, see Definition
2.3.2
. That is, the functor

f\wedge g\colon[1]\times[1]\rightarrow\mathcal{C}

is the following commutative diagram.

\textstyle{X_{0}\wedge Y_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X_{1}\wedge Y_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X_{0}\wedge Y_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X_{1}\wedge Y_{0}.}

Note that the slice category
\mathrm{pr}/0
is the poset
\ulcorner
and the slice
\mathrm{pr}/1
is the whole square. It follows that the map

\colim_{\ulcorner}\left(f\wedge g\right)\to\colim_{[1]\times[1]}\left(f\wedge g\right)

induced by the inclusion
\ulcorner\hookrightarrow[1]\times[1]
is exactly the map

f\mathbin{\square}g\colon X_{0}\wedge Y_{1}\coprod_{X_{0}\wedge Y_{0}}X_{1}\wedge Y_{1}\rightarrow X_{1}\wedge Y_{1}.

So indeed,
(\Lan_{\mathrm{pr}}(f\wedge g))=\mathrm{pr}_{!}(f\wedge g)=f\mathbin{\square}g
.

2.3.1.
Smash Products for Diagram Categories

A monoidal category
(\mathcal{M},\wedge)
gives rise to more monoidal categories by considering diagrams from small categories into
\mathcal{M}
. In our next example we discuss how this is related to model category theory.

Definition 2.3.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\wedge)
be a monoidal category and let
I
and
J
be direct categories. We define the external product, which is the bifunctor

-\wedge-\colon\mathcal{M}^{I}\times\mathcal{M}^{J}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{I\times J}

sending
(X,Y)
to the diagram

X\wedge Y\colon I\times J\rightarrow\mathcal{M},\ \ (i,j)\mapsto X_{i}\wedge Y_{j}.

The external product is part of a two-variable adjunction. Since we do not use the extra structure will not define the other two functors in the two-variable adjunction. We have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.3.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\wedge)
be a monoidal model category. Then, the bifunctor

-\wedge-\colon\mathcal{M}^{I}\times\mathcal{M}^{J}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{I\times J}

is a Quillen bifunctor, that is to say, it has a total left derived functor

-\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}-\colon\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{I})\times\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{J})\rightarrow\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{I\times J}).

Proof.

Suppose that the
injective
model structures
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{inj}}^{I},\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{inj}}^{J}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{inj}}^{I\times J}
exist, e.g., if

\mathcal{M}
is a combinatorial model category. Since in the injective model structures the cofibrations are the objectwise cofibrations, the above proposition follows directly. The universal property of
-\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}-
implies that up to canonical isomorphism both constructions give the same result.
∎

We have the following corollary.

Corollary 2.3.4
.

In the context of Proposition
2.3.3
, there is a functor isomorphism

\hocolim_{I\times J}(X\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}Y)\cong(\hocolim_{I}X)\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}(\hocolim_{J}Y).

Proof.

From Proposition
2.3.3
, it follows that the external product preserves diagram cofibrant objects and preserves trivial diagram cofibrations between diagram cofibrant objects. The result now follows from the strict formula

\colim_{I\times J}(X\wedge Y)\cong(\colim_{I}X)\wedge(\colim_{J}Y)

as all the objects involved are cofibrant.
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
2.3.3
, we also obtain the following.

Example 2.3.5
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\wedge)
be a monoidal model category and let
J
be a direct category. Consider the diagram category
\mathcal{M}^{J}
with the model structure
2.1.4
. The category
\mathcal{M}^{J}
inherits a monoidal structure

\mathcal{M}^{J}\times\mathcal{M}^{J}\to\mathcal{M}^{J},\ \ (X,Y)\mapsto X\wedge Y,

where
X\wedge Y
is the diagram
j\mapsto X_{j}\wedge Y_{j}
. By a proof analogous to Proposition
2.3.3
,
(\mathcal{M}^{J},\wedge)
is a monoidal model category.

Corollary 2.3.6
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\wedge)
be a pointed symmetric monoidal model category, and let
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
and
g\colon U\rightarrow V
be morphisms in
\mathcal{M}
. There is a canonical isomorphism

\hocofib(f)\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}\hocofib(g)\cong\hocofib(f\mathbin{\square}^{\mathbb{L}}g).

2.3.2.
Stable Model Categories and Triangulated Categories

Recall that the homotopy category
\Ho(\mathcal{M})
of a pointed model category
\mathcal{M}
supports a
suspension
functor

\Sigma\colon\Ho(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\Ho(\mathcal{M})

given by

\Sigma X:=\hocolim(\ast\leftarrow X\rightarrow\ast),

with a right adjoint functor

\Omega\colon\Ho(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\Ho(\mathcal{M})

given by

\Omega X=\holim(*\rightarrow X\leftarrow*).

Definition 2.3.9
.

A
stable model category
is a pointed model category for which the functors
\Sigma
and
\Omega
are inverse equivalences.

Example 2.3.10
.

The prototypical example of a stable model category is the category of spectra,
\mathrm{Sp}
. There are of course many variants of spectra, but as our result does not depend on a choice of suitable, monoidal model category, we will not need to specify this further.

Example 2.3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a graded abelian category with enough projectives, and let
\mathrm{C}^{([1],1)}(\mathcal{A})
denote the category of
twisted

([1],1)
-chain complexes or
differential
objects. An object of
\mathrm{C}^{([1],1)}(\mathcal{A})
is a pair
(M_{*},d)
with
M_{*}\in\mathcal{A}
together with a morphism
(the differential)

d\colon M_{*}\rightarrow M_{*}[1],

such that
d[1]\circ d=0
. The category
\mathrm{C}^{([1],1)}(\mathcal{A})
admits a stable model structure, the
projective model structure
, where the weak equivalences are the homology isomorphisms and the fibrations are the surjections. In particular, the cofibrant objects are the projective objects of
\mathcal{A}
. We let
\mathrm{D}^{([1],1)}(\mathcal{A})
denote the homotopy
category of
\mathrm{C}^{([1],1)}(\mathcal{A})
. For an object
(M_{*},d)\in\mathrm{C}^{([1],1)}(\mathcal{A})
we define the homology
H(M)=\ker d/\operatorname{im}d
, and so we have the homology functor

H_{*}\colon\mathrm{D}^{([1],1)}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}.

In the following we will let
(\mathcal{A},\otimes,\mathbf{1})
be an abelian symmetric monoidal category with enough projectives. In this case
(\mathrm{C}^{([1],1)}(\mathcal{A}),\otimes)
is a monoidal stable model category. Finally, we mention the homology functor
H_{*}\colon\mathrm{D}^{([1],1)}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
is a lax symmetric monoidal functor via the Künneth morphism.

We note that our methods throughout this paper also work in a setting where
\mathcal{A}
does not have enough projectives. In the case of
\mathcal{A}=E(1)_{*}E(1)\mbox{-comod}
,
\mathrm{C}^{([1],1)}(\mathcal{A})
can be equipped with a model structure where the cofibrant twisted chain complexes are degreewise projective as
E(1)_{*}
-modules. This
relative projective model structure
is also monoidal, see
[
BR11
, Section 5]
.

If
\mathcal{M}
is a pointed simplicial model category, then the suspension functor

\Sigma\colon\Ho(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\Ho(\mathcal{M})

admits a simple description. Indeed, by the simplicial model category axioms, the functor

S^{1}\wedge-\colon\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}

defined using the tensor with simplicial sets
is a left Quillen functor. Then,
\Sigma
can be defined as the left derived functor of
S^{1}\wedge-
, i.e.,

\Sigma X:=S^{1}\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}X=S^{1}\wedge QX,

see
[
Hov99
, 6.1.1]
. Note that if
\mathcal{M}
is stable, then the homotopy category
\Ho(\mathcal{M})
is a triangulated category with
\Sigma
a shift functor, see
[
BR11
, Theorem 4.2.1]
and
[
Hov99
, 7.1.6]
.

In a simplicial model category
\mathcal{M}
we can choose a particular
model
for the homotopy cofiber (
2.1.11
) of a morphism, which will help with computations. It is called the
mapping cone
construction.

Definition 2.3.12
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}
is a simplicial stable model category and
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
a morphism in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cof}}
. Let
\cone(f)
be the pushout of
f

along the canonical morphism

\operatorname{incl}\otimes 1\colon S^{0}\otimes X\rightarrow(I,0)\otimes X=CX,

that is,
\cone(f)
comes with the pushout square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces CX}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cone(f).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{incl}\otimes 1}

Here
CX=(I,0)\otimes X
denotes the
cone
of
X
. The natural map

\pi\colon(I,0)\otimes X\rightarrow S^{1}\otimes X

and the trivial map

\ast\colon Y\rightarrow S^{1}\otimes X

induce, using the universal property of pushput, a map

\partial\colon\cone(f)\rightarrow S^{1}\otimes X.

The fact that the mapping cone construction represents the homotopy cofiber and further details can be found in
[
BR20
, Section 4.3]
.

Definition 2.3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a simplicial stable model category and
f\colon X\rightarrow Y
a morphism in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{cof}}
. The
elementary triangle
associated to
f
is the triangle

X\xrightarrow{f}Y\xrightarrow{\iota}\cone(f)\xrightarrow{\partial}S^{1}\otimes X.

A triangle
(f,g,h)

A\xrightarrow{f}B\xrightarrow{g}C\xrightarrow{h}\Sigma A

in
\Ho(\mathcal{M})
is called
distinguished
if it is isomorphic to an elementary one.

2.4.
Homology of a Category with Coefficients in a Functor

In this subsection we will introduce one our main tools, namely homology of a category with coefficients in a functor. It is a particular case of functor homology that assigns the groups

\operatorname{Tor}^{I}_{*}(F,G)
to functors

F\colon I\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
and
G\colon I^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
with
\mathcal{A}
an abelian category. Since we do not need such generality, we will introduce it in a more down-to-earth way using simplicial techniques that dates back to Quillen. Traditional references include

[
Obe67
]
and
[
Obe68
]
, more contemporary references include
[
GCNT13
]
and
[
Ric20
, Chapters 15, 16]
.

Before we define the homology of a category with coefficients in a functor we will define the associated complex of a simplicial object
in an abelian category.

Definition 2.4.1
.

Let
D\in s\mathcal{A}
be a simplicial object in
\mathcal{A}
. We define the
associated complex

(C_{\bullet}(U),\partial)\in\Ch_{\geq 0}(\mathcal{A})
by

C_{n}(D)=D_{n},\ \ \ \ \partial_{n}=\sum^{n}_{i=0}(-1)^{n}d_{i}\colon C_{n}(D)\rightarrow C_{n-1}(D).

Note that the simplicial identities imply
\partial^{2}=0
, so
C_{\bullet}(D)
is indeed
a chain complex. Moreover, this evidently defines a functor
C\colon s\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\Ch_{\geq 0}(\mathcal{A})
. In other words, the associated complex to a simplicial object
D\in s\mathcal{A}
is the following chain complex.

(2.4.2)

D_{0}\xleftarrow{d_{0}-d_{1}}D_{1}\xleftarrow{d_{0}-d_{1}+d_{2}}D_{2}\leftarrow\ldots.

Definition 2.4.3
.

Let
I
be a small category and consider a diagram
D\colon I\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
. The
homology of the category

I

with coefficients in the functor

D
is defined as the homology of the complex
C_{\bullet}(D)
, i.e., the homology of the
associated complex of the simplicial replacement
\srep(D)\in s\mathcal{A}
.

So, unwinding the definition, we start by first taking the simplicial replacement
\srep(D)\colon\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}
of
D
, see Definition
2.1.14
, that is, the diagram

\textstyle{\displaystyle{\bigoplus_{i_{0}}}D_{i_{0}}}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\displaystyle{\bigoplus_{i_{0}\rightarrow i_{1}}}D_{i_{0}}}
\textstyle{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\displaystyle{\bigoplus_{i_{0}\rightarrow i_{1}\rightarrow i_{2}}}D_{i_{0}}\cdots.}

Then, we consider the associated chain complex (
2.4.2
)
C_{\bullet}(D)
. Then we defined
H_{p}(I;D)
to be the

p
th homology group of the chain complex
C_{\bullet}(D)
.

Now we will investigate how these constructions help us calculate homotopy colimits. First, recall the following.

Definition 2.4.4
.

We call a functor
F_{*}\colon\Ho(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}

homological
if it satisfies the following conditions.

(i)

F_{*}
is a graded functor, that is to say, it commutes with suspensions, so there are natural equivalences

F_{*}(\Sigma X)\cong F_{*}(X)[1]:=F_{*-1}(X)

which are part of the structure.

(ii)

F_{*}
is additive, i.e., it commutes with arbitrary coproducts.

(iii)

F_{*}
converts distinguished triangles into long exact sequences.

(iv)

Furthermore, if
(\mathcal{M},\wedge)
is a monoidal model category and
(\mathcal{A},\otimes)
is a monoidal abelian category, we require that
F_{*}
is lax symmetric monoidal, that is, there is a natural Künneth morphism

\kappa_{X,Y}\colon F_{*}X\otimes F_{*}Y\rightarrow F_{*}(X\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}Y).

Now let
\mathcal{M}
be a simplicial stable model category, let
I
be a direct category and let
X\in\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{I})
. Further, let

F_{*}\colon\Ho(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}

be a homological functor into an (graded) abelian category. Then there is a spectral sequence

(2.4.5)

E^{2}_{pq}=H_{p}(I;F_{q}X)\Rightarrow F_{p+q}(\hocolim_{J}X),

see
[
Ric20
, 16.3.1]
. The construction of the spectral sequence (
2.4.5
) arises from the skeletal filtration of a simplicial object. This spectral sequence will play a central role in our calculations for the monoidal properties of
\cQ

in Section
3
.

2.5.
Franke’s Realization Functor

In this subsection we will recall the construction of Franke’s equivalence

\mathcal{R}\colon\mathrm{D}^{([1],1)}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\Ho(\mathcal{M}).

For a detailed exposition we refer to
[
Pat12
, Section 3.3]
and
[
Roi08
]
.
Recall that
C_{N}
is the crown-shaped poset from Example
2.1.7
, and that the category
\mathrm{D}^{([1],1)}(\mathcal{A})
above is the derived category of twisted chain complexes from Example
2.3.11
, where
\mathcal{A}
is a graded symmetric monoidal hereditary abelian category with enough projectives,
\mathcal{M}
is a simplicial stable model category, and
F:\Ho(\mathcal{M})\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}
is a homological functor. Also, we assume
\mathcal{A}
splits into shifted copies of another abelian category
\mathcal{B}
,

\mathcal{A}=\bigoplus\limits_{i=0}^{N-1}\mathcal{B}[i]

for
N>1
. Under these assumptions,
\mathcal{R}
exists and is an equivalence.

For an object
X\in\mathcal{M}^{C_{N}}
we the structure morphisms of
X
as follows.

l_{i}\colon X_{\beta_{i}}\rightarrow X_{\zeta_{i}},\ \ k_{i}\colon X_{\beta_{i-1}}\rightarrow X_{\zeta_{i}},\ \ i\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}

Furthermore, let

\displaystyle Z^{(i)}(X)=F_{*}(X_{\zeta_{i}}),\ \ B^{(i)}(X)=F_{*}(X_{\beta_{i}}),\ \ C^{(i)}(X)=F_{*}(\cone(k_{i})),

\displaystyle\lambda^{(i)}\colon=F_{*}l_{i}\colon B^{(i)}(X)\longrightarrow Z^{(i)}(X),i\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z},

where
\cone(k_{i})
denotes the cone construction from Definition
2.3.12
. We will now list some additional assumptions that we need in order to assemble the
C^{(i)}
into a chain complex
C_{*}
.

Definition 2.5.1
.

Consider the full subcategory
\mathcal{L}
of
\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{C_{N}})
consisting of those diagrams
X\in\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{C_{N}})
which satisfy the following conditions.

(i)

The objects
X_{\beta_{i}}
and
X_{\zeta_{i}}
are cofibrant in
\mathcal{M}
for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
.

(ii)

The objects
F_{*}(X_{\beta_{i}})
and
F_{*}(X_{\zeta_{i}})
are contained in
\mathcal{B}[i]
for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
.

(iii)

The map
\lambda^{(i)}\colon F_{*}(X_{\beta_{i}})\to F_{*}(X_{\zeta_{i}})
is a monomorphism for any
i\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
.

Next we construct a functor

\cQ\colon\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathrm{C}^{([1],1)}(\mathcal{A}).

Let
X
be an object of
\mathcal{L}
. As the functor

F_{*}\colon\Ho(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\mathcal{A}

is homological, the distinguished triangles

X_{\beta_{i-1}}\xrightarrow{k_{i}}X_{\zeta_{i}}\rightarrow\cone(k_{i})\rightarrow\Sigma X_{\beta_{i-1}}

induce long exact sequences

\ldots\rightarrow B^{(i-1)}(X)\rightarrow Z^{(i)}(X)\xrightarrow{\iota^{(i)}}C^{(i)}(X)\xrightarrow{\rho^{(i)}}B^{(i-1)}(X)[1]\rightarrow Z^{(i)}(X)[1]\rightarrow\ldots.

Note that
B^{(i-1)}(X)\in\mathcal{B}[i-1]
and
Z^{(i)}(X)\in\mathcal{B}[i]
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
, since
X\in\mathcal{L}
. Therefore, the morphisms
B^{(i-1)}(X)\to Z^{(i)}(X)
and

4.
Main Result

4.1.
Homotopy Colimit Calculations

In this section we discuss how the functor
i^{*}\mathbb{L}\mathrm{pr}_{!}
interacts with the homotopy colimits of the various diagram categories, giving us the right hand side of the main diagram
1
. The main result of the section is the following.

Theorem 4.1.1
.

For any pair of diagrams
(X,Y)\in\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{C_{N}})\times\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{C_{N}})
, the homotopy colimit of the diagram
i^{*}\mathbb{L}\mathrm{pr}_{!}(X\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}Y)\in\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{C_{N}})
is naturally isomorphic to the smash
product of the homotopy colimits of
X
and
Y
, that is,

\hocolim_{C_{N}}\left(i^{*}\mathbb{L}\mathrm{pr}_{!}(X\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}Y)\right)\cong\hocolim_{C_{N}}X\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}\hocolim_{C_{N}}Y.

Recall that the functor

i^{*}\mathbb{L}\mathrm{pr}_{!}(-\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}-)\colon\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{C_{N}})\times\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{C_{N}})\to\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{C_{N}})

is the composition

\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{C_{N}})\times\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{C_{N}})\xrightarrow{\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}}\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{C_{N}\times C_{N}})\xrightarrow{\mathbb{L}\mathrm{pr}_{!}}\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{D_{N}})\xrightarrow{i^{*}}\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{C_{N}}).

In order to prove Theorem
4.1.1
we will break it apart into smaller pieces. Consider the following diagram.

\textstyle{\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{C_{N}})\times\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{C_{N}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}}
\textstyle{\Ho(\mathcal{M})}

\textstyle{\Ho(\mathcal{M}^{D_{N}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

The top horizontal functor is the smash product of homotopy colimits of crowned diagrams, that is,
\hocolim_{C_{N}}X\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}\hocolim_{C_{N}}Y.

The three other functors are the homotopy colimit functors

(ii)

Lemma 4.1.2
.

The top triangle and the middle triangle commute, that is,

\hocolim_{C_{N}}X\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}\hocolim_{C_{N}}Y\cong\hocolim_{C_{N}\times C_{N}}(X\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}Y)

\hocolim_{C_{N}\times C_{N}}(X\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}Y)\cong\hocolim_{D_{N}}\mathrm{pr}_{!}(X\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}Y).

Proof.

The first assertion follows from Corollary
2.3.4
as a direct application for

. The second assertion follows from the fact that the homotopy colimit of a homotopy left Kan
extension of a diagram is isomorphic to the homotopy colimit of the diagram itself

∎

We will prove the above proposition by proving that the functor

using the following definition, see
[
Rie14
, Definition 8.5.1]
.

Definition 4.1.3
.

A functor between small categories

is
homotopy final

(or
homotopy terminal
) if for every object

, the simplicial set

is contractible .

A convenient way to check whether a poset is contractible
is given by Quillen
[

: a poset

if there is an object
c_{0}\in C
and a map of posets

such that
c\leq f(c)\geq c_{0}
for every

. In this
case one can show that the identity
1_{C}
, the map
f
, and the constant map with value
c_{0}
from
C
to itself are homotopic (that is to say, their realizations are homotopic), and hence
C

is contractible. So, given a diagram

, to check that the canonical morphism

\phi_{i}\colon\hocolim_{C_{N}}i^{*}E\rightarrow\hocolim_{D_{N}}E

is an isomorphism it suffices to check that the slice categories

are contractible for any
\alpha\in\left\{\zeta,\gamma,\beta\right\}
and any

.

We will now apply this to our functor

).

Lemma 4.1.4

The functor

Proof.

We will prove the above proposition by applying Quillen’s criterion of conical contractible posets. First, we identity the slice categories

Since this poset contains only one element it is is obviously contractible. The next slice categories are of the form

\gamma_{n}/i=\left\{\alpha_{n}\in C_{N}\colon i(\alpha_{n})\geq\gamma_{n}\right\},

that is,

\textstyle{\zeta_{n}}
\textstyle{\zeta_{n+1}.}

4.2.

Finally, we are in a position to assemble all our work into our main theorem.

Theorem 4.2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a hereditary abelian category, and

be a monoidal stable model category such that Franke’s functor

We assemble our proof along the lines of the diagram

.
Let
M_{*}
and

2.1.1

Since

\mathcal{A}
. This

The essential l l -dimension of finite groups of Lie type, l ≠ 2 l\neq 2
Hannah Knight

Abstract
In this paper, we compute the essential l l -dimension of the finite groups of classical Lie type for odd primes l l not equal to the defining prime, specifically the linear groups, the symplectic groups, the orthogonal groups, and the unitary groups, and the simple factors in their Jordan-Hölder series.



1
Introduction

The goal of this paper is to compute the essential
l
-dimension of the finite groups of classical Lie type, for odd primes
l
not equal to the defining prime. Fix a field
k
. The essential dimension of a finite group
G
, denoted
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(G)
, is the smallest number of algebraically independent parameters needed to define a Galois
G
-algebra over any field extension
F/k
(or equivalently
G\text{-torsors over }\text{Spec}F
). In other words, the essential dimension of a finite group
G
is the supremum taken over all field extensions
F/k
of the smallest number of algebraically independent parameters needed to define a Galois
G
-algebra over
F
. The essential
l
-dimension of a finite group, denoted
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(G,l)
, is similar except that before taking the supremum, we allow finite extensions of
F
of degree prime to
l
and take the minimum of the number of parameters needed. In other words, the essential
l
-dimension of a finite group is the supremum taken over all fields
F/k
of the smallest number of algebraically independent parameters needed to define a Galois
G
-algebra over a field extension of
F
of degree prime to
l
. See Section
2
for more formal definitions. See also
[
3
]
and
[
7
]
for more detailed discussions. For a discussion of some interesting applications of essential dimension and essential
p
-dimension, see
[
14
]
.

What is the essential dimension of the finite simple groups? This question is quite difficult to answer. A few results for small groups (not necessarily simple) have been proven. For example, it is known that
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(S_{5})=2
,
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(S_{6})=3
for
k
of characteristic not
2

[
2
]
, and
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(A_{7})=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(S_{7})=4
in characteristic
0

[
4
]
. It is also known that for
k
a field of characteristic
0
containing all roots of unity,
\operatorname{ed}_{k}(G)=1
if and only if
G
is isomorphic to a cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}
or a dihedral group
D_{m}
where
m
is odd (
[
3
]
, Theorem 6.2). Various bounds have also been proven. See
[
3
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
13
]
, among others. For a nice summary of the results known in 2010, see
[
14
]
.

We can find a lower bound to this question by considering the corresponding question for essential
p
-dimension. In my previous article (
[
8
]
), I calculated the essential
p
-dimension of the split finite quasi-simple groups of classical Lie type at the defining prime
p
. In this article, we calculate the essential
l

l\neq 2
and
l\neq p
(where

Essential Dimension and Representation Theory Background

For completeness, we recall the relevant background. Fix a field
k
. Let
G
be a finite group,
p
a prime.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
T:\text{Fields}/k\to\text{Sets}
be a functor. Let
F/k
be a field extension, and
t\in T(F)
. The
essential dimension of

\mathit{t}
is

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(t)=\min_{F^{\prime}\subset F\text{ s.t. }t\in Im(T(F^{\prime})\to T(F))}\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(F^{\prime}).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
T:\text{Fields}/k\to\text{Sets}
be a functor. The
essential dimension of

\mathit{T}
is

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(T)=\sup_{t\in T(F),F/k\in\text{Fields}/k}\operatorname{ed}_{k}(t).

Definition 2.3
.

For
G
be a finite group, let

H^{1}(-;G):\text{Fields}/k\to\text{Sets}

be defined by

H^{1}(-;G)(F/k)=\{\text{the isomorphism classes of }G\text{-torsors over }\text{Spec}F\}.

Definition 2.4
.

The
essential dimension of

\mathit{G}
is

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(G)=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(H^{1}(-;G)).

Definition 2.5
.

Let
T:\text{Fields}/k\to\text{Sets}
be a functor. Let
F/k
be a field extension, and
t\in T(F)
. The
essential

\mathit{p}
-dimension of

\mathit{t}
is

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(t,p)=\min\operatorname{trdeg}_{k}(F^{\prime\prime})

where the minimum is taken over all

\displaystyle F^{\prime\prime}\subset F^{\prime}\text{ a finite extension},\text{ with }F\subset F^{\prime}

\displaystyle[F^{\prime}:F]\text{ finite }\text{ s.t. }p\nmid[F^{\prime}:F]\text{ and }

\displaystyle\text{the image of }t\text{ in }T(F^{\prime})\text{ is in }\text{Im}(T(F^{\prime\prime})\to T(F^{\prime}))

Note
.

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(t,p)=\min_{F\subset F^{\prime},p\nmid[F^{\prime}:F]}\operatorname{ed}_{k}(t|_{F^{\prime}}).

Definition 2.6
.

Let
T:\text{Fields}/k\to\text{Sets}
be a functor. The

\mathit{p}
-dimension of

\mathit{T}
is

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(T,p)=\sup_{t\in T(F),F/k\in\text{Fields}/k}\operatorname{ed}_{k}(t,p).

Definition 2.7
.

The

\mathit{p}

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
H
be a finite group. For any prime

P=H^{N}\rtimes P_{l}(S_{N})

\operatorname{ed}_{k}(P,l)=N\operatorname{ed}_{k}(H,l).

Proof.

Since

N\operatorname{ed}_{k}(H,l)=\operatorname{ed}_{k}(H^{N},l)\leq\operatorname{ed}_{k}(P,l).

On the other hand, let

\displaystyle\operatorname{ed}_{k}(G,l)

\displaystyle\leq\dim(V^{N})=N\dim(V)=N\operatorname{ed}_{k}(H,l).\qed

Lemma 2.22
.

A representation of a finite
p
-group,
H
, is faithful if and only if its restriction to
Z(H)
is faithful and if and
only if its restriction to
Z(H)[p]
is faithful.

Proof.

Let
H
be a finite
p
-group.

Claim
.

For
N
a nontrivial normal subgroup of
H
,
N\cap Z(H)
and
N\cap Z(H)[p]
are not trivial.

Granting this claim, we can complete the proof.

\Rightarrow
: The forward direction is obvious.

\Leftarrow
: Let
\rho
be a representation of
H
whose restriction to
Z(H)[p]
is faithful. Let
K=\text{ker}\rho
. Then
K
is normal in
H
and since the restriction of
\rho
to
Z(H)[p]
is faithful, we have
K\cap Z(H)[p]=\{1\}
. Then by the Claim,
K
must be trivial. Thus
K=\{1\}
. An identical proof works for if
\rho
restricted to
Z(H)
is faithful.
∎

Proof of the Claim.

We will first show that
N\cap Z(H)
is not trivial. Applying the class equation to
H\curvearrowright N
by conjugation, we have

|N|=|N^{H}|+\sum_{n_{i}}[H:\text{Stab}_{n_{i}}],

where the
n_{i}
are chosen one from each conjugacy class,
N^{H}=\{n\in N:hnh^{-1}=n,\forall h\in H\}\subseteq Z(H)
are the fixed points, and
\text{Stab}_{n_{i}}=\{h\in h:hn_{i}h^{-1}=n_{i}\}
is the stabilizer of
n_{i}\in N
. Since the stabilizer is a subgroup of
H
, a
p
-group, we have

p\bigm|[H:\text{Stab}_{n_{i}}],\text{ for all }i.

Thus we must have
p\bigm||N^{H}|
. And since
1\in N^{H}
,
N^{H}
is not empty. Therefore,
|N^{H}|\geq p
. And
N^{H}\subseteq Z(H)
. Therefore,
N\cap Z(H)
is not trivial.

Since
N\cap Z(H)
is not trivial and is contained in a
p
-group, we must have an element of order
p
by Cauchy’s Lemma. Thus
N\cap Z(H)[p]
is not trivial.
∎

Lemma 2.24

For an invertible matrix

Lemma 5.5

Induction Step:
Assume that the lemma is true for

For

Lemma 6.2
.

Let

,
\Gamma=\text{Gal}(k(\zeta_{l^{s}})/k)
,
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
\mathbf{a}\in(\mathbb{Z}/l^{s}\mathbb{Z})^{l}

invertible and

a_{i}=\gamma^{i}a_{l}+x\sum_{k=1}^{i}\gamma^{k-1}\text{ }\forall i.

Then
\sum_{i=1}^{l}ia_{i}
is not invertible.

Proof.

Write
\gamma=c+bl
for
l\nmid c
. Then

\displaystyle a_{l}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{l}a_{l}+x\sum_{k=1}^{l}\gamma^{k-1}

\displaystyle=c^{l}a_{l}+x\sum_{k=1}^{l}c\mod l

\displaystyle=c^{l}a_{l}\mod l

So we must have
c^{l}=1\mod l
. Thus
\gamma^{l}=1\mod l
. Hence
0=\gamma^{l}-1=(\gamma-1)^{l}\mod l.
Therefore
\gamma=1\mod l
and so
c=1
. Then

\displaystyle a_{i+1\text{ mod }l}-a_{i}

\displaystyle=(\gamma^{i+1}a_{l}+x\sum_{k=1}^{i+1}\gamma^{k-1})-(\gamma^{i}a_{l}+x\sum_{k=1}^{i}\gamma^{k-1})

\displaystyle=a_{l}(\gamma^{i+1}-\gamma^{i})+x\gamma^{i}

\displaystyle=a_{l}((1+bl)^{i+1}-(1+bl)^{i})+(1+bl)^{i}x

\displaystyle=a_{l}(1+bl)^{i}(1+bl-1)+(1+bl)^{i}x

\displaystyle=a_{l}bl(1+bl)^{i}+(1+bl)^{i}x

\displaystyle=(1+bl)^{i}(a_{l}bl+x)

Note that
\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{i+1\text{ mod }l}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{i}
. So

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{i+1\text{ mod }l}-a_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{l}(1+bl)^{i}(a_{l}bl+x)

\displaystyle=(a_{l}bl+x)\sum_{i=1}^{l}(1+bl)^{i}

\displaystyle=(a_{l}bl+x)\sum_{i=1}^{l}(1+ibl)\mod l^{2}

\displaystyle=(a_{l}bl+x)l+bl\frac{l(l+1)}{2}\mod l^{2}

\displaystyle=lx\mod l^{2}\text{ since }l\neq 2

So we can conclude that
l\bigm|x
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{l}ia_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{l}i\left(\gamma^{i}a_{l}+x\sum_{k=1}^{i}\gamma^{k-1}\right)

\displaystyle=a_{l}\sum_{i=1}^{l}i\mod l,\text{ since }l\bigm|x\text{ and }\gamma=1\mod l

\displaystyle=a_{l}\frac{l(l+1)}{2}\mod l

\displaystyle=0\mod l\text{ since }l\neq 2

Therefore,
\sum_{i=1}^{l}ia_{i}
is not invertible.

Remark 9

Definition 9.2

Theorem 9.8

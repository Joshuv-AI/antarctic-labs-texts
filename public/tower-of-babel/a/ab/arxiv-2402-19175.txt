Abstract
. The paper concerns the coarse flag Hilbert-Poincaré series of [ 4 ] in the case of the braid arrangement ℬ n \mathcal{B}_{n} associated to the symmetric group 𝔖 n + 1 {\mathfrak{S}}_{n+1} . We explicitly construct a companion statistic ino : 𝔖 n + 1 × Sym ⁡ ( n ) → ℕ \operatorname{ino}:{\mathfrak{S}}_{n+1}\times\operatorname{Sym}(n)\rightarrow\mathbb{N} for the descent statistic on Sym ⁡ ( n ) \operatorname{Sym}(n) using reverse ( P , ω ) (P,\omega) -partitions and quasisymmetric functions.



1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Background

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a central and essential hyperplane arrangement in
V\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Its
lattice of flats

\mathcal{L}
is given by the intersections of hyperplanes in
\mathcal{A}
ordered by reverse inclusion, so that its unique bottom element is
V
and its unique top element is
\mathbf{0}
.
The
chain Poincaré series
of a chain
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1}<C_{2}<\dots<C_{k}\}
in
\mathcal{L}
is given by the product of the Poincaré series of the intervals, with bottom and top attached,

\operatorname{Poin}_{\mathcal{C}}(y)=\prod_{i=0}^{k}\operatorname{Poin}([C_{i},C_{i+1}];y)\,,

where we set
C_{0}=V
and
C_{k+1}=\mathbf{0}
.
The
coarse flag Hilbert-Poincaré series
is defined by Maglione–Voll in
[
4
]
as

\operatorname{cfHP}_{\mathcal{A}}(y,t)=\sum_{\mathcal{C}}\operatorname{Poin}_{\mathcal{C}}(y)\cdot\left(\frac{t}{1-t}\right)^{\#\mathcal{C}}=\frac{\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(y,t)}{(1-t)^{n}}\,,

where the sum ranges over all chains in the intersection poset
\mathcal{L}
not containing the bottom element
V
.
Its numerator polynomial has nonnegative coefficients
[
2
]
and the Chow polynomial of the associated matroid is given by the evaluation
\operatorname{cfHP}_{\mathcal{A}}(-x,x)

[
8
]
.
It is moreover shown in
[
3
]
for simplicial arrangements that

\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(1,t)=\#\mathcal{R}\cdot\operatorname{E}_{n}(t)\,,

where
\operatorname{E}_{n}(t)=\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)}t^{\operatorname{des}(\sigma)}
is the
n
-th
Eulerian polynomial
, and where
\mathcal{R}
denotes the set of regions of the arrangement
\mathcal{A}
given by the connected components of its complement.
This raises the following natural question.

Question 1.1
.

Find a
companion statistic

\operatorname{st}:\mathcal{R}\times\operatorname{Sym}(n)\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
for the descent number on
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
such that

\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(y,t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}R\in\mathcal{R}\\
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)\end{subarray}}y^{\operatorname{st}(R,\sigma)}\cdot t^{\operatorname{des}(\sigma)}\,.

If
\mathcal{A}
is the reflection arrangement of a real reflection group
W
, the regions are in bijection with the elements of the group
W
, so this becomes the question of finding a companion statistic on
{W\times\operatorname{Sym}(n)}
.
We emphasize that it already appears to be a challenging task to
find such a companion statistic
, and that this statistic would in particular provide a new approach towards generalizing left-to-right minima on permutations to real reflection groups, see
Remark

1.5
.

The purpose of this paper is to introduce a new statistic on permutations which answers
1.1
for the symmetric group.

Main result

Let

and in

come
in o
rder, meaning that

.

Theorem 1.4
.

For the braid arrangement
\mathcal{B}_{n}
, we have

\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{B}_{n}}(y,t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n+1}\\
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)\end{subarray}}y^{\operatorname{ino}(w,\sigma)}\cdot t^{\operatorname{des}(\sigma)}\,.

We further refine this theorem in the remainder of this introduction and then prove it in
Section

2
.
Beside its natural appearance, the motivation for studying this companion statistic for the symmetric group is its connection to left-to-right minima for general real reflection groups.

Remark 1.5

(Motivation)
.

For a real reflection group
W
,
1.1
becomes the question of providing a companion statistic for descents on
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
in terms of the group
W
,

\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(y,t)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w\in W\\
\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)\end{subarray}}y^{\operatorname{st}(w,\sigma)}\cdot t^{\operatorname{des}(\sigma)}\,.

It is known that
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{A}}(y,0)=\operatorname{Poin}(\mathcal{A},y)
, where the latter is the Poincaré polynomial of the arrangement, see
[
4
]
.
For reflection arrangements
\mathcal{A}_{W}
, this corresponds to

\operatorname{Poin}(\mathcal{A}_{W},y)=\sum_{w\in W}y^{\ell_{R}(w)}\,,

where
\ell_{R}:W\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
denotes the
reflection length
on
W
given by the minimal number
k
such that
w=r_{1}\cdots r_{k}
for reflections
r_{1},\dots,r_{k}\in\penalty\ W
.
We thus obtain that the
yet-to-be-found
companion statistic on
W\times\operatorname{Sym}(n)
needs to be equidistributed with the reflection length on the slice
W\times\{\operatorname{id}\}
for the identity permutation
\operatorname{id}=[1,\dots,n]\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)
,

\sum_{w\in W}y^{\operatorname{st}(w,\operatorname{id})}=\sum_{w\in W}y^{\ell_{R}(w)}\,.

For the symmetric group, it is immediate from the definition that the companion statistic
\operatorname{ino}(w,\operatorname{id})
records exactly the
non-left-to-right minima
of the permutation
w
.
It is therefore a generalization of non-left-to-right minima.
For general
W
, a companion statistic
\operatorname{st}:W\times\operatorname{Sym}(n)
would thus necessarily be a version of generalized non-left-to-right minima that is equidistributed on
W\times\{\operatorname{id}\}
with reflection length.

Lemma 2.4

Let

\lambda(\overleftarrow{w},\sigma_{\uparrow})=(-{\lambda_{1}},\dots,-{\lambda_{n}})\,.

Proof.

The

The merging blocks only come in opposite order, swapping the sign of

Lemma 2.5
.

For

\big\{|\lambda_{1}|,\dots,|\lambda_{n}|\big\}=\big\{2,\dots,n+1\big\}\,.

2.4.
The poset
\boldsymbol{P_{w,Y}}

Throughout this section, fix
w\in{\mathfrak{S}}_{n+1}
and
Y\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
such that

•

Y
does not contain any left-to-right minima of
w
, while

•

Y
contains all right-to-left minima of
w
, except the number
1
.

Based on the properties of the edge-labeling
\lambda(w,\sigma)
provided in the previous section, we define a poset
P=P_{w,Y}
on the set
\{1,\dots,n\}
and an injective vertex-labeling
\Lambda
of
P
.
This poset is built so that

\mathsf{Lin}(P)=\{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)\mid\operatorname{Ino}(w,\sigma)=Y\}\,,

where
\mathsf{Lin}(P)
denotes the set of all linear extensions.
In particular, this implies that
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
is equal to the disjoint union of the linear extensions of the posets
P_{w,Y}
for all possible subsets
Y\subseteq\penalty\ \{1,\dots,n\}
with the two given properties.

We construct the poset
P
together with its injective vertex-labeling
\Lambda
by iterating through the numbers
n+1,\dots,2
in this order.
At each iteration step
w_{i}\in\{n+1,\dots,2\}
, we set the label
\Lambda
of one vertex.
If
w_{i}
is neither a left-to-right nor right-to-left minimum of
w
, we add an additional cover relation to
P
, based on the already added cover relations.

1.

If
w_{i}
is not a left-to-right minimum, we have
\ell\geq 1
and the set
\{\ell,\dots,i-1\}
forms a connected subposet of
P
with unique maximum. Denote this maximum by
a
.

2.

If
w_{i}
is not a right-to-left minimum, we have
r\leq n
and the set
\{i,\dots,r-1\}
forms a connected subposet of
P
with unique maximum.
Denote this maximum by
b
.

3.

If
w_{i}
is a left-to-right minimum, it is not a right-to-left minimum. Set
\Lambda(b)=\overline{w_{i}}
.

4.

If
w_{i}
is a right-to-left minimum, it is not a left-to-right minimum. Set
\Lambda(a)=w_{i}
.

5.

If
w_{i}
is neither a left-to-right nor a right-to-left minimum, add the cover relation together with the vertex label of the minimum given by

\displaystyle a\prec_{P}b\text{ and }\Lambda(a)=w_{i}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ if }w_{i}\in Y\,

\displaystyle b\prec_{P}a\text{ and }\Lambda(b)=\overline{w_{i}}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{ if }w_{i}\notin Y\,.

Observe that this iterative process is well-defined since the constructed Hasse diagram is a forest at every step.

2.5.
Quasisymmetric functions and a proof of the main result

In order to prove
Theorem

2.2
, we use the following implication of the theory of reverse
(P,\omega)
-partitions from
[
6
, §7.19]
.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
P
be a poset on
\{1,\dots,n\}
and let
\omega,\omega^{\prime}:P\to\mathbb{Z}
be two injective vertex-labelings having the same relative orders on the cover relations.
This is, for each cover relation
a\prec_{P}\penalty\ b
we have

\omega(a)<\omega(b)\Leftrightarrow\omega^{\prime}(a)<\omega^{\prime}(b)\,.

Then,
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
have the same descent set generating function on the set of linear extensions,

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathsf{Lin}(P)}\boldsymbol{t}^{\operatorname{Des}(\omega(\sigma))}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathsf{Lin}(P)}\boldsymbol{t}^{\operatorname{Des}(\omega^{\prime}(\sigma))}\,,

where
\operatorname{Des}\big(\omega(\sigma)\big)
denotes the descent set of
\big(\omega(\sigma(1)),\dots,\omega(\sigma(n))\big)
and analogously for
\operatorname{Des}(\omega^{\prime}(\sigma))
.

Before proving this proposition, we deduce
Theorem

2.2
as follows.

Lemma 2.14
.

For a cover relation
a\prec_{P}b
in
P=P_{w,Y}
, we have

a<b\Longleftrightarrow\Lambda(a)>\Lambda(b)\,,

where both comparisons are as integers.

Proof.

Within the construction of
P
, a cover relation
a\prec_{P}b
is introduced for
a<b
if
a
and
b
are the last left and right bars for some
w_{i}\in\{n+1,\dots,2\}
and
\Lambda(a)=w_{i}
.
If a cover relation
a\prec_{P}b
is introduced for
b<a
instead, the label of
a
is set to
\Lambda(a)=\overline{w_{i}}
.
In both cases,
\Lambda(b)
will be determined at a later iteration step.
Thus, we have
\overline{w_{i}}<\Lambda(b)<w_{i}
,
so that
\Lambda(a)>\Lambda(b)
if
a<b
, and
\Lambda(a)<\Lambda(b)
if
a>b
as desired.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

2.2
.

Using
Proposition

2.11
and
Lemma

2.14
, it follows with
Proposition

2.13
that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\mathsf{Lin}(P_{w,Y})}\boldsymbol{t}^{\operatorname{Des}(\sigma)}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathsf{Lin}(P_{w,Y})}\boldsymbol{t}^{\operatorname{Asc}(\lambda(w,\sigma))}\,.

Since
\operatorname{Sym}(n)
is the disjoint union over all linear extensions of the posets
P_{w,Y}
, the observation that
\operatorname{Ino}(w,\sigma)=Y
for
\sigma\in\mathsf{Lin}(P_{w,Y})
applied to (
2.4
) implies

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)}\boldsymbol{y}^{\operatorname{Ino}(w,\sigma)}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}^{\operatorname{Asc}(\lambda(w,\sigma))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{Y}\boldsymbol{y}^{Y}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathsf{Lin}(P_{w,Y})}\boldsymbol{t}^{\operatorname{Asc}(\lambda(w,\sigma))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{Y}\boldsymbol{y}^{Y}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathsf{Lin}(P_{w,Y})}\boldsymbol{t}^{\operatorname{Des}(\sigma)}=\sum_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(n)}\boldsymbol{y}^{\operatorname{Ino}(w,\sigma)}\cdot\boldsymbol{t}^{\operatorname{Des}(\sigma)}\,,

where the two outer sums on the right-hand side range over all subsets
Y\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}
that do not contain left-to-right minima of
w
while containing all right-to-left minima of
w
except the number
1
.
∎

We are left to provide a proof of
Proposition

2.13
.
Let
P
be a finite poset on
\{1,\dots,n\}
and let
\omega:P\to\mathbb{Z}
be an injective vertex-labeling of
P
.
Following
[
6
, §7.19]
, a
reverse
\boldsymbol{(P,\omega)}
-partition
is a map
\sigma:P\to\mathbb{N}
satisfying

1.

if
a\prec_{P}b
, then
\sigma(a)\leq\sigma(b)
, and

2.

if
a\prec_{P}b
and
\omega(a)>\omega(b)
,
then
\sigma(a)<\sigma(b)
.

Denote the set of reverse
(P,\omega)
-partitions by
\mathcal{A}^{r}(P,\omega)
,
and define the quasisymmetric function

K_{P,\omega}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{A}^{r}(P,\omega)}\boldsymbol{x}^{\sigma}\in\mathbb{N}[\![\boldsymbol{x}]\!]=\mathbb{N}[\![x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\dots]\!]\,,

where
\boldsymbol{x}^{\sigma}=\prod_{a\in P}x_{\sigma(a)}
.
Observe that
K_{P,\omega}
only depends on the relative order of
\omega
on the cover relations of
P
.
We exploit this property to prove
Proposition

2.13
.

There is an alternative formula for
K_{P,\omega}
in terms of linear extensions and the fundamental quasisymmetric functions

L_{\operatorname{Des}(\sigma)}=\sum x_{i_{1}}\cdots\ x_{i_{n}}\,,

where the sum ranges over all sequences
1\leq i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{n}\leq n
with
i_{k}<i_{k+1}
if
k\in\operatorname{Des}(\sigma)
.
The quasisymmetric function
K_{P,\omega}
then has the description

(2.5)

\displaystyle K_{P,\omega}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathsf{Lin}(P)}L_{\operatorname{Des}\omega(\sigma)}\,,

see
[
6
, Corollary 7.19.5]
.

Proof of
Proposition

2.13
.

Let
\omega,\omega^{\prime}:P\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
be two injective vertex-labelings such that for each cover relation
a\prec_{P}b
, we have

\omega(a)<\omega(b)\Longleftrightarrow\omega^{\prime}(a)<\omega^{\prime}(b)\,.

Since
K_{P,\omega}
only depends on the relative orders of
\omega
on the cover relations, we obtain
K_{P,\omega}=K_{P,\omega^{\prime}}
.
It thus follows from (
2.5
) that

\sum_{\sigma\in\mathsf{Lin}(P)}L_{\operatorname{Des}\omega(\sigma)}=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathsf{Lin}(P)}L_{\operatorname{Des}\omega^{\prime}(\sigma)}\,.

Since
L_{\operatorname{Des}(\sigma)}
only depends on the descent set of
\sigma
, this implies
Proposition

2.13
.
More precisely, for any subsets
A,B\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}(n)
, the equalities of fundamental quasisymmetric function generating functions and of descent set generating functions are equivalent,

\sum_{\sigma\in A}L_{\operatorname{Des}(\sigma)}=\sum_{\sigma\in B}L_{\operatorname{Des}(\sigma)}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\sum_{\sigma\in A}\boldsymbol{t}^{\operatorname{Des}(\sigma)}=\sum_{\sigma\in B}\boldsymbol{t}^{\operatorname{Des}(\sigma)}\,.\qed

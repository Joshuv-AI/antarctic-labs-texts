Relative adjoint algebras
Martín Mombelli

Abstract
. Given a finite tensor category 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} , an exact indecomposable 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} -module category ℳ {\mathcal{M}} , and a tensor subcategory 𝒟 ⊆ 𝒞 ℳ ∗ {\mathcal{D}}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{\mathcal{M}} , we describe a way to produce exact commutative algebras in the center Z ⁡ ( 𝒞 ) Z({\mathcal{C}}) , measuring this inclusion. The construction of such algebras is done in an analogous way as presented by Shimizu [ 20 ] , but using instead the relative (co)end , a categorical tool developed in [ 1 ] in the realm of representations of tensor categories. We provide some explicit computations.

Introduction

Assume
{\mathcal{M}}
is a module category over a finite tensor category
{\mathcal{C}}
. In
[
20
]
, the author introduces the notion of
adjoint algebra

{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{M}}
and the space of class functions
\rm{CF}({\mathcal{M}})
associated to
{\mathcal{M}}
, generalizing the definitions given in
[
19
]
. This algebra is an interesting object associated to
{\mathcal{M}}
. In fact, this algebra is a complete invariant of
{\mathcal{M}}
.

If
\triangleright:{\mathcal{C}}\times{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
is the action of
{\mathcal{C}}
on
{\mathcal{M}}
, then we can consider the action functor

\rho_{\mathcal{M}}:{\mathcal{C}}\to\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{M}}),

\rho_{\mathcal{M}}(X)(M)=X\triangleright M,\,\,X\in{\mathcal{C}},M\in{\mathcal{M}}.

The right adjoint of this functor is
\rho_{\mathcal{M}}^{\rm{ra}}:\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{M}})\to{\mathcal{C}}
, wich can be explicitly described as an end

\rho_{\mathcal{M}}^{\rm{ra}}(F)=\int_{M\in{\mathcal{M}}}\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(M,F(M)),

for any
F\in\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{M}})
, see
[
20
, Thm. 3.4]
. Here for any
M\in{\mathcal{M}}
,
\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(M,-)
is the right adjoint of the functor
{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
,
X\mapsto X\triangleright M
. It is called the
internal Hom
of the module category
{\mathcal{M}}
. The
adjoint algebra
is defined as

{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{M}}=\rho_{\mathcal{M}}^{\rm{ra}}(\mbox{\rm Id\,}_{\mathcal{M}}).

This object has structure of commutative algebra in the Drinfeld center
Z({\mathcal{C}})
. A key ingredient of the construction is the end of the functor

\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(-,-):{\mathcal{M}}^{\rm{op}}\times{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{C}}.

In this paper we investigate what kind of structure it is obtained, when we replace the usual end by the relative end, a new categorical tool introduced in
[
1
]
.

Assume
{\mathcal{A}}
is any category, and
S:{\mathcal{M}}^{\rm{op}}\times{\mathcal{M}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
is a functor equipped with natural isomorphisms

\beta^{X}_{M,N}:S(M,X\triangleright N)\to S(X^{*}\triangleright M,N).

We call this isomorphism a
{\mathcal{C}}
-
prebalancing
of
S
. Any additive
{\Bbbk}
-linear functor
S
posses a
\mbox{\rm vect\,}_{\Bbbk}
-prebalancing. However, in the general case, the prebalancing is an extra structure of the functor
S
. The
relative end
of
S

is an object
E\in{\mathcal{A}}

that comes with dinatural transformations
\pi_{M}:E\xrightarrow{..}S(M,M)
such that the equation

(0.1)

S(\mbox{ev}_{X}\triangleright\mbox{\rm id\,}_{M},\mbox{\rm id\,}_{M})\pi_{M}=S(m_{X^{*},X,M},\mbox{\rm id\,}_{M})\beta^{X}_{X\triangleright M,M}\pi_{X\triangleright M},

is fulfilled, and it is universal among all objects in
{\mathcal{A}}
with dinatural transformations that satisfy (
0.1
). Unlike the case
{\mathcal{C}}=\mbox{\rm vect\,}_{\Bbbk}
, it may happen that a dinatural transformation does not satisfy (
0.1
). The relative end of
S
is denoted by
\oint_{M\in{\mathcal{M}}}(S,\beta)
. When
{\mathcal{C}}=\mbox{\rm vect\,}_{\Bbbk}
, the relative end coincides with the usual end. Relative coends are defined similarly.

If
{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{\mathcal{M}}
is a tensor subcategory, then
{\mathcal{D}}
acts on the left and on the right on
\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{M}})
making it a
{\mathcal{D}}
-bimodule category. The action functor

\rho_{\mathcal{M}}:{\mathcal{C}}\to\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{M}})

lands in
Z_{\mathcal{D}}(\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{M}}))
, the relative center of
\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{M}})
. Thus we can consider the functor

(0.2)

\rho:{\mathcal{C}}\to Z_{\mathcal{D}}(\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{M}})).

Given any
F\in Z_{\mathcal{D}}(\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{M}}))
, the internal Hom of the module category
{\mathcal{M}}
posses a canonical
{\mathcal{D}}
-prebalancing, see Lemma
6.3
. Thus we can consider the relative end

\oint_{M\in{\mathcal{M}}}\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(-,F(-))\in{\mathcal{C}}.

The
adjoint algebra relative to

{\mathcal{D}}
is then defined as

(0.3)

{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{M}}}:=\oint_{M\in{\mathcal{M}}}\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(-,-)\in{\mathcal{C}}.

See Definition
6.6
. The algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{M}}}
seems to measure very well the inclusion
{\mathcal{D}}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{\mathcal{M}}
. The object
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{M}}}
is in fact a commutative connected algebra in the Drinfeld center
Z({\mathcal{C}})
such that
Z({\mathcal{C}})_{{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{M}}}}
is a rigid monoidal category. This last condition is equivalent to
Z({\mathcal{C}})_{{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{M}}}}
being an
exact
module category over
Z({\mathcal{C}})
, that is
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{M}}}
is an
exact
algebra. We called this type of algebras
étale
, generalizing the notion of étale algebras in the semisimple case. See
[
3
, Section 3]
. In our case, separability of the algebra is replaced by the condition of exactness.

The key step to prove all these facts, is the computation of a right adjoint of the functor
\rho
, given in (
0.2
). A right adjoint is described as

\bar{\rho}:Z_{\mathcal{D}}(\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{M}}))\to{\mathcal{C}},

\bar{\rho}(F)=\oint_{M\in{\mathcal{M}}}\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}(M,F(M)).

In Theorem
6.4
we prove this fact, and we also prove that
\bar{\rho}
is exact, faithful and dominant. This implies that the adjunction
(\rho,\bar{\rho})
is monadic, and this fact implies that there is a monoidal equivalence

Z({\mathcal{C}})_{A_{{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{M}}}}\simeq Z_{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{\mathcal{M}}).

Hence we can compute its Frobenius-Perron dimension as

\mbox{\rm FPdim\,}({\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{M}}})=\frac{\mbox{\rm FPdim\,}({\mathcal{C}})}{\mbox{\rm FPdim\,}({\mathcal{D}})}.

These assertions are stated in Proposition
6.10
.

The correspondence between tensor subcategories of
{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{\mathcal{M}}
and certain commutative algebras in the center is not new, at least in the semisimple case. In
[
3
, Thm. 4.10]
, the authors described a bijective correspondence between tensor subcategories of a fixed tensor category
{\mathcal{C}}
and étale subalgebras of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathcal{C}}.
This subalgebra is also described as
I(\mathbf{1})
, where
I
is the right adjoint of a certain functor. What we present new in this paper is an explicit description of the functor
I
, and the means to compute the algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{M}}}
.

One of the advantages of the description presented in (
0.3
), is that it provides a formula for its computation. In Section
7
we present explicit calculations. We provide examples when
{\mathcal{C}}
is an equivariantization by a finite group, when
{\mathcal{C}}
is the tensor category of representations of a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra, and when
{\mathcal{C}}
is the Deligne tensor product of two tensor categories.

1.1.
Monads

A monad over a category
{\mathcal{C}}
is an algebra object in the monoidal category
\operatorname{End}({\mathcal{C}})
. That is, a functor
T:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
equipped with natural morphisms

\mu:T\circ T\to T,\,\,\,\eta:\mbox{\rm Id\,}\to T,

the multiplication and the unit of
T
, such that they satisfy associativity and unity axioms. The category of
T
-modules,
{\mathcal{C}}^{T}
is the category whose objects are pairs
(M,s)
, where
M
is an object of
{\mathcal{C}}
,
s:T(M)\to M
is a morphism in
{\mathcal{C}}
such that

sT(s)=s\mu_{M},\quad s\eta_{M}=\mbox{\rm id\,}_{M}.

The forgetful functor
{\mathcal{U}}:{\mathcal{C}}^{T}\to{\mathcal{C}}
has left adjoint given by the
free
functor
{\mathcal{L}}:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{T}
,
{\mathcal{L}}(M)=(T(M),\mu_{M}).

If
(G:{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathcal{C}},F:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{D}})
is an adjunction, with unit and counit
e:G\circ F\to\mbox{\rm Id\,},

c:\mbox{\rm Id\,}\to F\circ G
, then
T=F\circ G
is a monad. The
comparison functor

\kappa:{\mathcal{D}}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{T},

is defined by
\kappa(M)=(F(M),F(e_{M})),
for any
M\in{\mathcal{D}}
. If the comparison functor
\kappa
is an equivalence of categories, the adjunction
(G,F)
is said to be
monadic
. Several results concerning monadicity of an adjunction are given by Beck’s Theorem. See for example
[
15
]
. As a consequence of Beck’s Monadicity Theorem, it follows that, if
F:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{D}}
is faithful and exact, then
(G,F)
is monadic.

2.1.
Étale algebras in finite tensor categories

We propose the following definition of étale algebras in the non-semisimple case, generalizing
[
3
, Definition 3.1]
. Assume that
{\mathcal{C}}
is a braided tensor category.

Definition 2.2
.

An algebra
A\in{\mathcal{C}}
is
étale
if:

\bullet

A
is commutative;

\bullet

the category
{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is an exact indecomposable left module category over
{\mathcal{C}}
;

\bullet

A
is simple as a right
A
-module; that is
A\in{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is a simple object.

It is
connected
if
\dim_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{1},A)=1
.

Remark 2.3
.

The exactness and indecomposability of
{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
as a left
{\mathcal{C}}
-module category may be a superfluous condition. We expect that
A
being right simple implies that
{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is an exact and indecomposable module category.

Remark 2.4
.

Condition of
{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
being exact and indecomposable module category is equivalent to
{}_{A}{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
being a rigid monoidal category. Indeed, there is a monoidal equivalence
{}_{A}{\mathcal{C}}_{A}\simeq{\mathcal{C}}^{*}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{A}}
. One can prove that this is also equivalent to
{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
being a rigid monoidal category, since rigidity of objects in
{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is iherited by rigidity in
{}_{A}{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
.

There is a canonical correspondence between étale algebras and dominant tensor functors with an exact faithful right adjoint. The proof of the next result follows from Proposition
1.2
. See also
[
3
, Lemma 3.5]
.

Corollary 2.5
.

If
F:Z({\mathcal{C}})\to{\mathcal{D}}
is an exact dominant tensor functor with an exact faithful right adjoint
R:{\mathcal{D}}\to Z({\mathcal{C}})
, then
(A,\sigma)=R(\mathbf{1})
is a connected étale algebra in the center
Z({\mathcal{C}})
.∎

Exploring a new approach to Hadronic Parity Violation from Lattice QCD
Marcus Petschlies, Nikolas Schlage, Aniket Sen, Carsten Urbach

Abstract
The long-range, parity-odd nucleon interaction generated by single pion exchange is captured in the parity-odd pion-nucleon coupling h π 1 h^{1}_{\pi} . Its calculation in lattice QCD requires the evaluation of 4-quark operator nucleon 3-point functions. We investigate a new numerical approach to compute h π 1 h^{1}_{\pi} based on nucleon matrix elements of parity-even 4-quark operators and related to the parity-violating electro-weak theory by PCAC and chiral perturbation theory. This study is performed with 2+1+1 dynamical flavors of twisted mass fermions at pion mass m π ≈ 260 ​ MeV m_{\pi}\approx 260\,\mathrm{MeV} in a lattice box of L ≈ 3 ​ fm L\approx 3\,\mathrm{fm} and with a lattice spacing of a ≈ 0.091 ​ fm a\approx 0.091\,\mathrm{fm} . From a calculation excluding fermion loop diagrams we find a bare coupling of h π 1 = 8.08 ​ ( 98 ) ⋅ 10 − 7 h^{1}_{\pi}=8.08\,(98)\cdot 10^{-7} .

The aforementioned first ab-initio lattice QCD determination and the
non-perturbative estimate of the nucleon matrix elements with the
parity-violating (PV) effective Lagrangian has been presented in
Ref.
Wasem 2012a
. In this work the actual transition matrix
elements
N\pi\overset{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PV}}}{\longrightarrow}N

for a transition of a nucleon-pion state to a nucleon state mediated
by the
\Delta I=1
, parity-violating Lagrangian was
considered. Though this calculation is pioneering, it is
also exploratory in many regards as also discussed in detail in
Refs.
Wasem 2012b
;
Guo and Seng 2019
. Challenges are the rigorous
treatment of the pion-nucleon state in finite volume and energy
non-conservation between initial and finial state on the lattice,
which were circumvented in Ref.
Wasem 2012a
.
Apart from this the calculation considered also only a certain quark
flow diagram topology, arguing that the neglected diagrams are
expected to have a contribution only within the statistical
accuracy. Moreover, renormalization of the 4-quark operators was not
included. Still, the obtained value on a coarse lattice with a
heavier-than-physical pion
m_{\pi}\approx 390\,\mathrm{MeV}
is consistent
with the recent NPDGgamma experimental analysis.

An alternative theoretical ansatz has been put forward anew in
Refs.
Feng et al. 2018
;
Guo and Seng 2019
,
by proposing a joint effort of chiral effective field theory (
\chi
EFT)
and lattice QCD. Based on the PCAC relation the transition via the
parity-violating interaction Lagrangian with a soft pion in the
initial or final state is equivalent to a transition via a
parity-conserving Lagrangian without a soft pion.
Details on the relevant PCAC relation

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{p_{\pi}\to 0}\,\langle n\,\pi^{+}\,|\,\mathcal{L}^{w}_{\mathrm{PV}}(0)\,|\,p\rangle

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{\sqrt{2}\,i}{F_{\pi}}\,\langle p\,|\,\mathcal{L}^{w}_{\mathrm{PC}}\,|p\rangle

(2)

are provided in Refs.
Feng et al. 2018
;
Guo and Seng 2019
.
The PCAC relation Eq. (
2
) is,
however, exact only in the limit of exact chiral symmetry. At
non-zero pion mass it receives higher order corrections in

\chi
EFT. But these corrections can be argued to be numerically small
Guo and Seng 2019
,
at the level of
\mathcal{O}\left({1\%}\right)
at the physical pion mass,
and can in principle also be calculated by studying the
pion mass dependence with lattice QCD, based on known low-energy constants
from meson and heavy-baryon chiral perturbation theory
Feng et al. 2018
.

This alternative theoretical ansatz leads to a major simplification in
the lattice computation: one now considers a transition amplitude
between single nucleon states
N\overset{\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PC}}}{\longrightarrow}N
with a parity-conserving (PC) Lagrangian,
which from a numerical point of view is more straightforward to handle
in a lattice calculation. In particular, the complication arising from
the pion-nucleon state is absent since the matrix element is computed
for single nucleon initial and final states.

In this work we investigate the computational concepts proposed in
Ref.
Guo and Seng 2019
in practice and propose a concrete numerical
implementation to evaluate the nucleon 3-point functions with the
4-quark operator insertions of
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{PC}}
.
Of course the ensuing Wick contractions still comprise fermion loop
diagrams, which were neglected in the first work
Wasem 2012b
. We will argue that these diagrams and the
renormalization procedure are intricately linked: in the lattice
calculation these particular fermion loop diagrams generate
power-divergent mixing with lower-dimensional operators, and we add an
initial discussion about such power divergent terms and our future
strategy for renormalizing the 4-quark operators.

Preliminary results of this work have been reported in
Sen et al. 2022
;
Schlage et al. 2023
.

II
Operators and coupling

The matching between the parity-violating interaction in the
electro-weak sector of the SM and the effective nucleon and pion
degrees of freedom at energy scale
\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}\sim m_{\mathrm{proton}}
has been worked out in Ref.
Dai et al. 1991
.
Here, we largely follow the notation of the recent
Ref.
Guo and Seng 2019
. The
\Delta I=1
, parity-conserving
Lagrangian is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{w}_{\mathrm{PC}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}}\,\frac{\sin^{2}\left(\theta_{w}\right)}{3}\,\sum\limits_{i}\,\left(C^{(1)}_{i}\,\theta^{(\ell)\prime}_{i}+S^{(1)}_{i}\,\theta^{(s)\prime}_{i}\right)\,,

(3)

C^{(1)}
and
S^{(1)}
denote the Wilson coefficients obtained in
1-loop perturbation theory
Dai et al. 1991
;
Kaplan and Savage 1993
.

The parity-even 4-quark operators
\theta^{(\ell)\prime}
with only light quarks

\ell
contributing and
\theta^{(s)\prime}
with light and strange

s
quarks contributing read

\displaystyle\theta^{(\ell)\prime}_{1}

\displaystyle=\bar{q}_{a}\,\gamma_{\mu}\,\mathbb{1}\,q_{a}\,\bar{q}_{b}\,\gamma^{\mu}\,\tau^{3}\,q_{b}\,,

\displaystyle\theta^{(\ell)\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle=\bar{q}_{a}\,\gamma_{\mu}\,\mathbb{1}\,q_{b}\,\bar{q}_{b}\,\gamma^{\mu}\,\tau^{3}\,q_{a}\,,

\displaystyle\theta^{(\ell)\prime}_{3}

\displaystyle=\bar{q}_{a}\,\gamma_{\mu}\,\gamma_{5}\,\mathbb{1}\,q_{a}\,\bar{q}_{b}\,\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}\,\tau^{3}\,q_{b}\,,

\displaystyle\theta^{(s)\prime}_{1}

\displaystyle=\bar{s}_{a}\,\gamma_{\mu}\,s_{a}\,\bar{q}_{b}\,\gamma^{\mu}\,\tau^{3}\,q_{b}\,,

\displaystyle\theta^{(s)\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle=\bar{s}_{a}\,\gamma_{\mu}\,s_{b}\,\bar{q}_{b}\,\gamma^{\mu}\,\tau^{3}\,q_{a}\,,

\displaystyle\theta^{(s)\prime}_{3}

\displaystyle=\bar{s}_{a}\,\gamma_{\mu}\,\gamma_{5}\,s_{a}\,\bar{q}_{b}\,\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}\,\tau^{3}\,q_{b}\,,

\displaystyle\theta^{(s)\prime}_{4}

\displaystyle=\bar{s}_{a}\,\gamma_{\mu}\,\gamma_{5}\,s_{b}\,\bar{q}_{b}\,\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}\,\tau^{3}\,q_{a}\,.

(4)

In the operator list Eq. (
4
)
q=(u,d)^{T}
is the up and down quark doublet and
\tau^{3}
is the third Pauli matrix, which gives the iso-vector combination
\bar{u}u-\bar{d}d
.
As a perturbative addition to pure QCD, the interaction Lagrangian
Eq. (
3
) induces a proton-neutron
mass splitting
(\delta m_{N})_{4q}
due
to the 4-quark operators,

\displaystyle\left(\delta m_{N}\right)_{4q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{N}}\,\langle p\,|\,\mathcal{L}^{w}_{\mathrm{PC}}(0)\,|\,p\rangle=-\frac{1}{m_{N}}\,\langle n\,|\,\mathcal{L}^{w}_{\mathrm{PC}}(0)\,|\,n\rangle\,,

(5)

and with the PCAC relation the leading contribution to the coupling comes from the mass splitting

\displaystyle h^{1}_{\pi}\approx-\frac{1}{F_{\pi}}\,\frac{(\delta m_{N})_{4q}}{\sqrt{2}}\,,

(6)

where
F_{\pi}
denotes the pion decay constant in the chiral limit.

In the following this work is mainly concerned with the lattice QCD estimate of the operator matrix elements

\langle N\,|\,\theta^{(f)}_{i}(0)\,|\,N\rangle
for
f=\ell,\,s
and
N
the proton and neutron.

III
4-quark operator matrix elements from the lattice

To determine the nucleon matrix elements of the 4-quark operators in Eq. (
4
) we follow
the Feynman-Hellmann-Theorem
technique advocated for in
Ref.
Bouchard et al. 2017
. The relevant correlation functions
result from inserting the individual operators

\theta^{(f)\prime}_{i}(x)
into the proton or neutron 2-point function,
summed over all lattice sites
x

The nucleons are interpolated by the usual zero-momentum, positive parity proton and neutron 3-quark operators

\displaystyle N^{+}_{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle=P^{(+)}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\,\sum\limits_{\vec{y}}\,\epsilon_{abc}\,u_{a}(t,\vec{y})^{T}\,C\gamma_{5}\,d_{b}(t,\vec{y})\,\,u_{\alpha^{\prime}c}(t,\vec{y})

\displaystyle N^{0}_{\alpha}(t)

\displaystyle=P^{(+)}_{\alpha\alpha^{\prime}}\,\sum\limits_{\vec{y}}\,\epsilon_{abc}\,d_{a}(t,\vec{y})^{T}\,C\gamma_{5}\,u_{b}(t,\vec{y})\,\,d_{\alpha^{\prime}c}(t,\vec{y})

(7)

with (positive) parity projector
P^{(+)}=\frac{1}{2}\,\left(\mathbb{1}+\gamma_{0}\right)
and
C

\displaystyle C_{3pt}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{x}\,\,\mathrm{Tr}\left[\langle N(t+t_{i})\,\,\theta^{(f)\prime}_{i}(x)\,\,\bar{N}(t_{i})\rangle\right]\,.

\displaystyle\qquad+\dots

Evaluation of diagrams

We evaluate the Wick contractions by a combination of point-to-all, stochastic and sequential quark propagators.
The point-to-all propagators
\psi^{(x_{i},\alpha,a)}
result from solving the lattice Dirac equation for a spin-color diluted source with
support at a single lattice site

\displaystyle S^{\beta\alpha}_{ba}(x;x_{i})

\displaystyle={D^{-1}}_{\beta,\beta^{\prime}}^{b,b^{\prime}}(x,y)\,\left[\delta_{x_{i},y}\,\delta_{\alpha,\beta^{\prime}}\,\delta_{a,b^{\prime}}\right]\,.

(15)

From the point-to-all propagators the nucleon 2-point functions are
evaluated in the usual way.

The quark loop in diagrams
B
and
D
in Fig.
2
is constructed by a fully time, spin and color diluted stochastic timeslice
propagator,

\displaystyle L(x)^{ab}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{t,\gamma,c}\,{D_{u}^{-1}}_{\alpha\kappa}^{ad}(x;y)\,\,\left(\eta(t,\vec{y})\,\delta_{t_{y},t}\,\delta_{\gamma,\kappa}\,\delta_{d,c}\right)\times

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\eta(t,\vec{x})\,\delta_{t_{x},t}\,\delta_{\gamma,\beta}\,\delta_{b,c}\right)\,,

(16)

where
\eta(t,\vec{x})\,\in\,\left\{\pm 1\right\}
are independent and identically with zero mean and unit variance

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\left[\eta(t,\vec{x})\right]

\displaystyle=0\,,\quad\mathrm{E}\left[\eta(t,\vec{x})\,\eta(t,\vec{y})\right]=\delta^{(3)}_{\vec{x},\vec{y}}\,.

(17)

\displaystyle T^{(K)}(y;x_{i})

Fierz rearrangement

The operators in Eq. (
4
) fall into two classes with respect to their spin-color structure:

\theta^{(f)\prime}_{1}
,
\theta^{(f)\prime}_{3}
for
f=\ell,\,s
consist of products of quark bilinear terms, i.e.
\bar{q}_{1}\,\Gamma\,q_{1}\times\bar{q}_{2}\,\Gamma\,q_{2}
.
The remaining operators
\theta^{(\ell)\prime}_{2}
and
\theta^{(s)\prime}_{2},\,\theta^{(s)\prime}_{4}
have cross-linked color and spinor indices. We refer to those
as “color-crossed” operators for short.

These matrix elements of the color-crossed operators can be computed
in two different ways. The first one is to compute the contractions
corresponding to the color-crossed operators. This is achieved
by using color dilution of the sequential sources
S^{(W),r}
in Eq. (
22
):
adding color indices
a,b,c
we thus have

\displaystyle S^{(W),r,a}_{bc}(x;x_{i})

\displaystyle=\Gamma\,\eta^{r}(x)\,S_{ac}(x;x_{i})\,\delta_{a,b}\,.

(23)

The second way is to apply a Fierz rearrangement in order to transform
the color-crossed operators into products of standard quark bilinear factors,
thereby avoiding the need for color dilution.
Then, the analogous methods discussed above for the non color-crossed operators
are applied.
For instance
\theta^{(\ell)\prime}_{2}
is equivalently represented as

\displaystyle\theta^{(\ell)\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle=u_{\alpha,a}^{*}\,\left(\gamma_{\mu}\right)_{\alpha\beta}\,u_{\beta,b}\,u_{\gamma,b}^{*}\,\left(\gamma_{\mu}\right)_{\gamma\delta}\,u_{\delta,a}-\left[u\leftrightarrow d\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\bar{u}\,\gamma_{\mu}\,u\,\,\bar{u}\,\gamma_{\mu}\,u+\frac{1}{2}\,\bar{u}\,\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}\,u\,\,\bar{u}\,\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}\,u

\displaystyle\quad-\bar{u}\,\mathbb{1}\,u\,\,\bar{u}\,\mathbb{1}\,u+\bar{u}\,\gamma_{5}\,u\,\,\bar{u}\,\gamma_{5}\,u-\left[u\leftrightarrow d\right]\,,

(24)

where by
\left[u\leftrightarrow d\right]
we denote the same term as written explicitly, but up replaced by down quark flavor. The strange operators
are treated accordingly.

Thus, instead of the list of operators in Eq. (
4
), we
consider only the quark bilinear forms

\displaystyle\bar{q}\,\Gamma\,q\,\,\bar{q}\,\Gamma\,q\,,\quad\bar{s}\,\Gamma\,q\,\,\bar{q}\,\Gamma\,s\,,\quad\bar{q}\,\Gamma\,q\,\,\bar{s}\,\Gamma\,s\,,\quad q=u,\,d

(25)

with as before
\Gamma=\left\{\gamma_{\mu}\right\},\,\left\{\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}\right\}
,
but also in addition

\Gamma=\mathbb{1},\,\gamma_{5}
.

For our numerical study we use a gauge field ensemble from the Extended Twisted Mass Collaboration
Alexandrou et al. 2018
with dynamical

of
32^{3}\times 64
, a lattice spacing of

and,
thus, a spatial lattice extend of

\displaystyle w_{\mathrm{fit}}

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\,(\chi^{2}+2\,N_{\mathrm{param}}-N_{\mathrm{data}})\right)

The ranges for

\displaystyle 1\leq t_{\mathrm{min}}/a\leq 10\,,\quad 10\leq t_{\mathrm{max}}/a\leq 17\,,\quad 2\leq\tau/a\leq 6\,.

VI
Comments on mixing and outline of renormalization

The renormalization of the set of 4-quark operators in Eq. (
4
) in continuum QCD
has been discussed in Refs.
Dai et al. 1991
;
Kaplan and Savage 1993
, and finds
its expression in the Wilson coefficients
\left\{C^{(1)}_{i},\,S^{(1)}_{i}\right\}
which are calculated in renormalized QCD perturbation
theory in the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme, together with their anomalous dimension matrix. In continuum QCD with the mass-independent
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme
and dimensional regularization, mixing with operators of lower dimension is excluded.

Here we comment on the situation in the practical lattice QCD calculation, with Wilson-type fermions and non-zero quark mass.
The explicit breaking of proper Lorentz symmetry down to discrete 3-rotations and to non-equivalence of spatial and temporal direction (due to
T\neq L
)
does not play a significant role for the 4-quark operators, which are invariant under a 3- and 4-dimensional rotation.

Of practical importance in this numerical calculation with Wilson-type fermions is the breaking of chiral symmetry, of up-down SU(2) flavor
and parity symmetry. We focus in these introductory remarks on the light quark propagators
\theta^{(\ell)\prime}
.

problems, especially towards physical pion mass and large lattice volume.

VII
Conclusion

In this work we investigated the numerical implementation of a new method to calculate nucleon matrix elements of parity-even
flavor-conserving 4-quark operators from lattice QCD, that pertain to the fully theoretical prediction of the long-range nucleon-pion coupling
h^{1}_{\pi}
.
This constitutes the first step towards a full-fledged calculation of the coupling from a combination of chiral perturbation theory
and non-perturbative lattice matrix elements.
For one ensemble at pion mass
260\,\mathrm{MeV}
and lattice spacing
a\approx 0.091\,\mathrm{fm}
we demonstrated
for the first time the calculation of all relevant (bare) matrix elements at the level of
10\%
combined statistical and systematic
uncertainty. The specific implementation shown here is readily and feasibly scalable towards physical pion mass, continuum and infinite volume.

We achieve this result due to the simplified representation of the coupling by application of the soft-pion-theorem and the implied sufficiency
to calculate (single-hadron) nucleon matrix elements of parity-even operators. Of course, corrections to this leading order
defining relation in
\chi
PT, though expected to be small, can in
principle be computed and, therefore, the approximation is
systematically improvable.

However, renormalization of lattice matrix elements poses a challenge due to mixing with lower-dimensional operators.

Appendix A
Lattice action and symmetries

Light quark lattice action

The lattice action of up and down quark for twisted mass fermions with a clover term is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(\ell)}

\displaystyle=a^{4}\;\sum\limits_{f=u,d}\;\sum\limits_{x}\;\bar{\psi}_{f}(x)\;\left(\gamma_{\mu}\,\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}-i\gamma_{5}\,W_{\mathrm{cr}}+m_{f}+r_{f}\,\frac{ac_{\mathrm{SW}}}{4}\,\gamma_{5}\,\sigma\,G\right)\;\psi(x)\,,

(38)

with Wilson parameters
r_{u}=1=-r_{d}
.

In Eq. (
38
) we have covariant derivative

\displaystyle\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\,\left(\nabla^{f}_{\mu}+\nabla^{b}_{\mu}\right)\,,

the subtracted Wilson term of dimension 5

\displaystyle W_{\mathrm{cr}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{a\,r_{f}}{2}\,\nabla^{f}_{\mu}\,\nabla^{b}_{\mu}+M_{\mathrm{cr}}(r_{f})\,,

and the Sheikholeslami-Wohlert term again of dimension 5 and with the clover-plaquette-based lattice field strength tensor
G

Sheikholeslami and Wohlert 1985
.

The form of the twisted mass lattice action (
38
) is valid in the physical basis of the quark fields, i.e. where the
mass term is real and diagonal, at maximal twist
Shindler 2008
, such that automatic
\mathcal{O}\left({a}\right)
improvement is
realized for physical observables.

Discretization of the strange quark fermion action

For the strange quark we employ the mixed action technique, with different fermion discretization used for the sea quarks pertaining to
gauge field sampling and to the valence quark, used for the actual calculations of correlation functions.

The sea quark action is given in Ref.
Frezzotti and Rossi 2004c
and to realize a mass splitting and automatic
\mathcal{O}\left({a}\right)
improvement
features mixing strange and charm sea quark flavor by lattice artifacts.

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{(s,c)}_{\mathrm{sea}}

\displaystyle=a^{4}\;\sum\limits_{x}\;\bar{\psi}_{h}(x)\;\left(\gamma_{\mu}\,\bar{\nabla}_{\mu}-i\gamma_{5}\,\tau^{1}\,W_{\mathrm{cr}}+\mu_{\sigma}+\mu_{\delta}\,\tau^{3}+\frac{ac_{\mathrm{SW}}}{4}\,\tau^{1}\,\gamma_{5}\,\sigma\,G\right)\;\psi_{h}(x)\,,

(39)

where
\psi_{h}=(c,\,s)^{T}
denotes the strange-charm doublet,
\mu_{\sigma}
the average bare quark mass of the doublet and
\mu_{\delta}
the mass

The bare mass parameters

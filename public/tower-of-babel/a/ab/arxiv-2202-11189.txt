Abstract
In this paper, we study the spectral estimation problem of estimating the locations of a fixed number of point sources given multiple snapshots of Fourier measurements in a bounded domain. We aim to provide a mathematical foundation for sparsity-based super-resolution in such spectral estimation problems in both one- and multi-dimensional spaces. In particular, we estimate the resolution and stability of the location recovery when considering the sparsest solution under the measurement constraint, and characterize their dependence on the cut-off frequency, the noise level, the sparsity of point sources, and the incoherence of the amplitude vectors of point sources. Our estimate emphasizes the importance of the high incoherence of amplitude vectors in enhancing the resolution of multi-snapshot spectral estimation. Moreover, to the best of our knowledge, it also provides the first stability result in the super-resolution regime for the well-known sparse MMV problem in DOA estimation.

Introduction

In this paper, we study the spectral estimation problem of estimating the locations of a fixed number of point sources given multiple snapshots of Fourier measurements in a bounded domain. This problem appears in many imaging

,

[

. A particular core of such problems in spectral estimation is named super-resolution, which seeks to reconstruct point sources separated less than the Rayleigh limit
[

. Over the past few years, the capability of super-resolution in single-snapshot spectral estimation has already been established by several mathematical theories
[

,
32
,
33
,
4
,

,

,

We analyze such cases in this paper, aiming to lay a mathematical foundation for sparsity-based multi-snapshot spectral estimation.

1.1

Our contribution and related work

Our main result (Theorem
2.1
) for one-dimensional multi-snapshot spectral estimation states that: Suppose
n
point sources located at
y_{j},j=1,\cdots,n,
and satisfy the separation condition

d_{\min}:=\min_{p\neq j}\left|{y_{p}-y_{j}}\right|_{\frac{n\pi}{\Omega}}\geqslant\frac{2.2e\pi}{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n}},

(1.1)

where
\Omega
is the cut-off frequency,
\sigma
is the noise level,
\left|{\cdot}\right|_{\frac{n\pi}{\Omega}}
is a certain wrap-around distance,
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}
is the amplitude matrix defined by (
2.6
), and
\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)
characterizes the correlation between the amplitude vectors of different sources. Then any solution to an
l_{0}
optimization problem (problem (
2.4
)) contains exactly
n
points. Moreover, for
\{\widehat{y}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y}_{n}\}
being a corresponding solution, after reordering
\widehat{y}_{j}
’s, we have

\Big|\widehat{y}_{j}-y_{j}\Big|_{\frac{n\pi}{\Omega}}<\frac{d_{\min}}{2},

(1.2)

and

\Big|\widehat{y}_{j}-y_{j}\Big|_{\frac{n\pi}{\Omega}}<\frac{C(n)}{\Omega}\mathrm{SRF}^{n-1}\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)},\quad 1\leqslant j\leqslant n,

(1.3)

where
C(n)=2\sqrt{2\pi}ne^{n}
and
\mathrm{SRF}=\frac{\pi}{\Omega d_{\min}}
is the super-resolution factor. In particular, (
1.1
) estimates the resolution for the sparse recovery in the super-resolution problem and (
1.3
) estimates the stability of the location recovery in such a scenario. It emphasizes the importance of the sparsity of sources, cut-off frequency, noise level, and particularly the high incoherence of amplitude vectors in enhancing the resolution and stability of multi-snapshot spectral estimation. The result was generalized to the multi-cluster case in Theorem
2.2
and the multi-dimensional case in Theorem
3.1
. Also, the result for a discrete model was established by Theorem
2.4
, giving a stability estimate for the sparse multiple measurement vectors (MMV) problem
[
10
,
8
,
12
]
in DOA estimation.

The most relevant work to our paper is
[
25
]
, in which the authors analyzed the stability and super-resolution of MUSIC
[
46
]
and ESPRIT
[
42
]
for multi-snapshot spectral estimation and derived estimates akin to ours. The MUSIC and ESPRIT methods are not directly sparsity-based methods. They reconstruct the source locations through subspace-based techniques and are usually called subspace methods. Under assumptions on the randomness of the noise, the authors estimated in
[
25
]
respectively the perturbation of the noise-space correlation function (NSC) for the MUSIC algorithm and the location recovery stability for the ESPRIT algorithm. Leveraging the notation in the current paper, their estimate for the output
\widehat{y}_{j}
’s of the ESPRIT algorithm reads:

a) Moderate SNR regime. If

\sigma\lesssim\sigma_{n}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Phi}})\sqrt{\lambda(X)},

then the averaged location recovery error satisfies

\mathbb{E}\left(\min_{\psi}\max_{j}\left|\widehat{y}_{\psi(j)}-y_{j}\right|_{\mathbb{T}}\right)\lesssim\frac{\Omega\sigma}{\sqrt{\lambda(X)}\sigma_{n}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Phi}})},

(1.4)

where
\psi
is a permutation on
\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
\left|{\cdot}\right|_{\mathbb{T}}
is a certain wrap-around distance,
\sigma_{n}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Phi}})
denotes the
n
-th largest singular value of the Vandermonde matrix
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Phi}}
that is similar to (
2.24
) and generated by
y_{j}
’s, and
\lambda(X)
is the minimum eigenvalue of the amplitude covariance matrix,

X:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\alpha_{t}\alpha_{t}^{*},

with
\alpha_{t}:=(a_{1,t},\cdots,a_{n,t})^{\top}
being the amplitude vector in the
t
-th snapshot.

b) Large SNR regime. If

\sigma\lesssim\frac{\sigma_{n}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Phi}})\sqrt{\lambda(X)}}{\sqrt{\Omega}}\min\left(1,\frac{\sigma_{n}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Phi}})\sqrt{d_{\min}}}{\sqrt{\Omega}},\frac{\sigma_{n}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Phi}})^{2}d_{\min}}{\Omega}\right),

then it holds that

\mathbb{E}\left(\min_{\psi}\max_{j}\left|\widehat{y}_{\psi(j)}-y_{j}\right|_{\mathbb{T}}\right)\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{\Omega}\sigma}{\sqrt{\lambda(X)}\sigma_{n}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Phi}})}.

(1.5)

In particular, it is well-known
[
22
, Theorem 2.7]
that for
n
point sources with minimum separation
d_{\min}
in a single cluster,
\sigma_{n}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Phi}})\gtrsim\sqrt{\Omega}(\Omega d_{\min})^{n-1}
, whence (
1.5
) becomes

\mathbb{E}\left(\min_{\psi}\max_{j}\left|\widehat{y}_{\psi(j)}-y_{j}\right|_{\mathbb{T}}\right)\lesssim\mathrm{SRF}^{n-1}\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{\lambda(X)}},

(1.6)

which is nearly the same as our estimate (
1.3
), except for the missing of cut-off frequency
\Omega
. Note that, as
\sqrt{\lambda(x)}=\sqrt{\lambda\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{*}\right)}=\sigma_{\min}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{*})
,
\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)
and
\sqrt{\lambda(x)}
are comparable; see (
2.11
) and (
2.12
). The drawback of the stability estimate (
1.6
) is the noise level requirement

\sigma\lesssim\frac{\sigma_{n}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Phi}})^{3}\sqrt{\lambda(X)}d_{\min}}{\sqrt{\Omega}\Omega},

which is far more restrictive. In comparison, as seen from (
1.1
), our requirement is only

\sigma\lesssim(\Omega d_{\min})^{n}\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right),

which is consistent with the stability estimate (
1.3
). We also conjecture that this requirement is already optimal to ensure a stable location reconstruction. On the other hand, our estimate (
1.3
) also holds in the moderate SNR regime, giving a better estimate than (
1.4
). For other comparisons regarding the results in the multi-cluster case, we leave them to Section
2.4
. We also notice a recent work
[
58
]
on the nonasymptotic performance analysis of ESPRIT, obtaining an estimate similar to (
1.4
).

It is worth emphasizing that there were already many mathematical theories for estimating the stability of super-resolution in the single measurement case. To the best of our knowledge, the first work for the stability of super-resolving multiple sources was by Donoho
[
16
]
. He considered a grid setting where a discrete measure is supported on a lattice (spacing by
\Delta
) and regularized by a so-called "Rayleigh index". His main contribution is estimating the corresponding minimax error in the recovery, which emphasizes the importance of the sparsity of sources for super-resolution. It was improved in recent years for the case when super-resolving
n
-sparse on-the-grid sources
[
13
]
, where the minimax error of amplitude recovery was shown to scale as
\mathrm{SRF}^{2n-1}\sigma
with
\mathrm{SRF}:=\frac{1}{\Delta\Omega}
being the super-resolution factor. The case of resolving multi-cluster sources was considered in
[
22
,
3
]
and similar minimax error estimations were established.

In
[
1
,
4
]
, the authors considered the minimax error for recovering off-the-grid point sources. They showed that for
\sigma\lesssim(\mathrm{SRF})^{-2p+1}
, where
p
is the number of point sources in a cluster, the minimax error for the amplitude and the location recoveries scale respectively as
(\mathrm{SRF})^{2p-1}\sigma
and
(\mathrm{SRF})^{2p-2}{\sigma}/{\Omega}
. Moreover, for the isolated non-cluster point sources, the corresponding minimax error for the amplitude and the location recoveries scale respectively as
\sigma
and
{\sigma}/{\Omega}
. These estimates were generalized to the case of resolving positive sources in
[
27
]
recently.

On the other hand, to analyze the resolution for recovering multiple point sources, in
[
34
,
32
,
33
,
29
]
the authors defined "computational resolution limits" which characterize the minimum required distance between point sources so that their number and locations can be stably resolved under certain noise level. By developing a non-linear approximation theory in a so-called Vandermonde space, they derived bounds for computational resolution limits for a deconvolution problem
[
34
]
and a spectral estimation problem
[
33
]
. In particular, they showed in
[
33
]
that the computational resolution limit for number and location recovery should be respectively
\frac{C_{\mathrm{num}}}{\Omega}\left(\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2n-2}}
and
\frac{C_{\mathrm{supp}}}{\Omega}\left(\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2n-1}}
, where
C_{\mathrm{num}}
and
C_{\mathrm{supp}}
are constants and
m_{\min}
is the minimum magnitude of the source amplitude. Their results demonstrate that when the point sources are separated larger than
\frac{C_{\mathrm{supp}}}{\Omega}\left(\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2n-1}}
, we can stably recover the source locations. Conversely, when the point sources are separated by a distance less than
O\left(\frac{C_{\mathrm{supp}}}{\Omega}\left(\frac{\sigma}{m_{\min}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2n-1}}\right)
, stably recovering the source locations is impossible in the worst case. This resolution limit indicates that super-resolution is possible for the single measurement case but requires a very high signal-to-noise ratio (according to the exponent
\frac{1}{2n-1}
). These estimates were later generalized to the problem of resolving positive sources
[
31
]
and moving sources
[
28
]
. As we have seen, the mathematics for the super-resolution from a single snapshot was well-established. Nevertheless, the multi-snapshot super-resolution still lacks or even is without any
mathematical foundation, despite its prominent importance. This strongly motivates the current paper.

For the super-resolution algorithms, the subspace methods such as MUSIC
[
46
,
51
,
22
,
24
]
, ESPRIT
[
42
,
23
]
, and Matrix Pencil Method
[
20
]
are widely used in applications due to their excellent performance. They can be dated back to Prony’s method
[
40
]
. In recent years, the rise in popularity of sparse modeling and compressive sensing has led to the creation of numerous sparsity-promoting super-resolution algorithms. In the groundbreaking work of Candès and Fernandez-Granda
[
7
]
, it was demonstrated that off-the-grid sources can be exactly recovered from their low-pass Fourier coefficients by total variation minimization under a minimum separation condition. Other well-known sparsity-promoting methods include the BLASSO algorithm
[
2
,
17
,
39
]
and the atomic norm minimization method
[
54
,
53
]
. These two algorithms were proved to be able to stably recover the sources under a minimum separation condition or a non-degeneracy condition. The resolution of these convex algorithms is limited by a distance on the scale of the Rayleigh limit
[
52
,
11
]
for recovering generic signed point sources. When super-resolving positive sources
[
36
,
35
,
5
,
15
]
, such constraint on resolution can be eased and the algorithm performance is nearly-optimal. However, most of the sparsity-promoting algorithms are designed for single-snapshot super-resolution, with scarce stability estimates for multi-snapshot equivalents. In a recent paper on a new multi-snapshot super-resolution algorithm
[
19
]
, the authors estimated a resolution characterizing when the focused source (a single point) was closed to one of the underlying sources to provide the theory for partial steps of their method. In direct comparison, the focus of our paper diverges considerably as we consider the recovery of all point sources. Furthermore, their estimate does not include terms related to amplitude vector incoherence.

One-dimensional case

2.1

Problem setting

Let
n
be the number of point sources and
y_{j}\in\mathbb{R},j=1,\cdots n,
be the corresponding locations. The point sources are denoted by
\delta_{y_{j}}
’s with
\delta
being the Dirac measure. We denote the source amplitude of
\delta_{y_{j}}
at
t
-th snapshot by
a_{j,t}
. Thus the collection of point sources in each snapshot writes

\mu_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j,t}\delta_{y_{j}},\quad t=1,\cdots,T,

(2.1)

where
T
is the total number of snapshots. The available measurements (snapshots) are the noisy Fourier data of
\mu_{t}
in a bounded interval. More precisely, they are given by

\mathbf{Y}_{t}(\omega)=\mathcal{F}[\mu_{t}](\omega)+\mathbf{W}_{t}(\omega)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j,t}e^{iy_{j}\omega}+\mathbf{W}_{t}(\omega),\ 1\leqslant t\leqslant T,\ \omega\in[-\Omega,\Omega],

(2.2)

where
\mathcal{F}[\mu_{t}]
denotes the Fourier transform of
\mu_{t}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{W}}_{t}(\omega)
is the noise. Here,
\Omega
is called the cut-off frequency, it represents the cut-off frequency in the imaging problem or the boundary of the sensor array in the DOA estimation. We assume

\left|{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{W}}_{t}(\omega)}\right|<\sigma,\quad 1\leqslant t\leqslant T,\ \omega\in[-\Omega,\Omega],

(2.3)

with
\sigma
being the noise level.

The inverse problem we are concerned with is to recover the sparsest location set
\{\widehat{y}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y}_{k}\}
that could generate these snapshots
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}_{t}
’s. In particular, we consider the following
l_{0}
-minimization problem:

\displaystyle\min_{\{\widehat{y}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y}_{k}\}}\left(\#\{\widehat{y}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y}_{k}\}\right)

(2.4)

\displaystyle\text{subject to the existence of $\widehat{a}_{j,t}$'s such that}\ \left|\left|{\mathcal{F}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{k}\widehat{a}_{j,t}\delta_{\widehat{y}_{j}}\right]-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}_{t}}\right|\right|_{\infty}<\sigma,\quad 1\leqslant t\leqslant T,

where
\left|\left|{f}\right|\right|_{\infty}:=\max_{\omega\in[-\Omega,\Omega]}|f(\omega)|
for a function
f
.

Our main result in the next section gives an estimation of the resolution and stability of the sparse recovery problem (
3.2
) in dimension one.

2.2

Main results

Let us first introduce some notation. For
\Lambda>0
, we define the wrap-around distance for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
by

\Big|x-y\Big|_{\Lambda}=\min_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|{x-y-k\Lambda}\right|.

(2.5)

We define the amplitude matrix as

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}:=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1,1}&\cdots&a_{1,T}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
a_{n,1}&\cdots&a_{n,T}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(2.6)

where
a_{j,t}
’s are the source amplitudes in (
2.1
) and the vector
(a_{j,1},\cdots,a_{j,T})^{\top}
is called the amplitude vector of point source
\delta_{y_{j}}
. For a
q\times k
matrix
B
, we define
\sigma_{\infty,\min}(B)
as

\sigma_{\infty,\min}(B):=\min_{x\in\mathbb{C}^{k},\mathbb{|}|x||_{\infty}\geqslant 1}||Bx||_{\infty},

(2.7)

characterizing the correlation between the columns of
B
. Throughout this paper, for a complex matrix
B
, we denote
B^{\top}
its transpose and
B^{*}
its conjugate transpose.

We have the following result on the stability of the problem (
2.4
), whose proof is given in Section
5
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that the following separation condition for the point sources holds:

d_{\min}:=\min_{p\neq j}\left|{y_{p}-y_{j}}\right|_{\frac{n\pi}{\Omega}}\geqslant\frac{2.2e\pi}{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n}},

(2.8)

with
\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top})}\leqslant 1
. Then any solution to (
2.4
) contains exactly
n
points. Moreover, for
\{\widehat{y}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y}_{n}\}
being a corresponding solution, after reordering the
\widehat{y}_{j}
’s, we have

\Big|\widehat{y}_{j}-y_{j}\Big|_{\frac{n\pi}{\Omega}}<\frac{d_{\min}}{2},

(2.9)

and

\Big|\widehat{y}_{j}-y_{j}\Big|_{\frac{n\pi}{\Omega}}<\frac{C(n)}{\Omega}\mathrm{SRF}^{n-1}\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)},\quad 1\leqslant j\leqslant n,

(2.10)

where
C(n)=2\sqrt{2\pi}ne^{n}
and
\mathrm{SRF}=\frac{\pi}{\Omega d_{\min}}
is the super-resolution factor.

Remark
.

If we consider that the point sources (as well as the solutions to (
2.4
)) are supported in an interval of length of several Rayleigh resolution limits, then the wrap-around distance in Theorem
2.1
can be replaced by the Euclidean distance.

Remark
.

For the case when
n=2
, the minimal separation distance in Theorem
2.1
,

\frac{2.2e\pi}{\Omega}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

applies for any
k
-dimensional space. It means that for multi-snapshot spectral estimation in a general
k
-dimensional space, the two-point resolution
[
47
,
49
,
48
,
9
,
14
,
26
]
for sparse recoveries like (
2.4
) is less than
\frac{2.2e\pi}{\Omega}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Remark
.

Note that the stability result in Theorem
2.1
holds for any algorithm that can recover the sparsest solution (solution with
n
point sources) satisfying the measurement constraint.

Theorem
2.1
demonstrates that when the point sources are separated by the distance
d_{\min}
in (
2.8
), each of the recovered locations from (
2.4
) lies in a neighborhood of the ground truth, with an estimated deviation also provided. Thus the resolution of the
l_{0}
-minimization problem (
2.4
) is less than

\frac{2.2e\pi}{\Omega}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top})}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Based on this formula, we demonstrate that the incoherence (encoded in
\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)
) between the amplitude vectors is crucial to the sparsity-based super-resolution. Recall that
\frac{C}{\Omega}
for a constant
C\approx\pi
is the so-called Rayleigh resolution limit. The estimate (
2.8
) also indicates that super-resolution from multiple snapshots is possible provided a sufficiently small noise level and large enough
\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)
. In particular, the resolution for the multi-snapshot spectral estimation with highly uncorrelated amplitude vectors is significantly better than the one for single-snapshot spectral estimation; see Section
2.5
.

We next present a simple example computing explicitly the resolution (
2.8
) and leave other detailed discussions to the following subsections.

Example
.

We consider two point sources with the amplitude matrix

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0.7\\
0.7&1\end{pmatrix}.

Suppose also that the noise level is
\sigma=10^{-3}
. By Lemma
D.5
,
\sigma_{\infty,\min}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top})
, defined in (
2.7
), is equal to
0.3
. Hence, by Theorem
2.1
, the resolution limit
d_{\min}
in solving problem (
2.4
) is smaller than

\frac{2.2e\pi}{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n}}\approx 0.34\frac{\pi}{\Omega},

where
\frac{\pi}{\Omega}
, as said before, is approximately the classical Rayleigh resolution limit.

2.3

Properties of
\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)

Adding same signals will not enhance the resolution:

Let

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1,1}&\cdots&a_{1,T}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
a_{n,1}&\cdots&a_{n,T}\end{pmatrix},\quad\widehat{\mathbf{A}}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1,1}&\cdots&a_{1,T}&a_{1,T+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
a_{n,1}&\cdots&a_{n,T}&a_{n,T+1}\\
\end{pmatrix}

with
(a_{1,T},\cdots,a_{n,T})^{\top}=(a_{1,T+1},\cdots,a_{n,T+1})^{\top}
. By the definition of
\sigma_{\infty,\min}(\cdot)
, it is clear that
\sigma_{\infty,\min}(\widehat{\mathbf{A}}^{\top})=\sigma_{\infty,\min}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top})
. Thus, adding the same signal cannot increase the resolution in Theorem
2.1
.

The incoherence is crucial:

The value of
\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)
is related to the correlation between the columns of the matrix
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}
. In particular, we have the following rough estimation of
\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)
:

\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)\geqslant\frac{\sigma_{\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)}{\sqrt{T}},

(2.11)

where
\sigma_{\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)
is the minimum singular value of
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}
. This clearly illustrates that the correlation between the columns of
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}
(i.e., the amplitude vectors of the sources) is crucial to
\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)
.

In particular, suppose that the amplitudes are independent Gaussian variables, i.e.,
a_{j,t}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
. We have

\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)\rightarrow 1

(2.12)

as
T\rightarrow\infty
, which can be seen from (
2.11
) and the fact that the minimum eigenvalue of
\left(\frac{1}{T}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)
tends to
1
.

2.4

Multi-cluster model

In this subsection, we consider a specific scenario where the point sources have a multi-cluster structure and present a result for the corresponding stability estimate. In particular, we consider the following
l_{0}
-minimization problem:

\displaystyle\min_{\{\widehat{y}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y}_{k}\},y_{j}\in\mathcal{O},1\leqslant j\leqslant k}\left(\#\{\widehat{y}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y}_{k}\}\right)

(2.13)

\displaystyle\text{subject to the existence of $\widehat{a}_{j,t}$'s such that}\ \left|\left|{\mathcal{F}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{k}\widehat{a}_{j,t}\delta_{\widehat{y}_{j}}\right]-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}_{t}}\right|\right|_{\infty}<\sigma,\ 1\leqslant t\leqslant T.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
s\geqslant 2
. Assume that the measurements
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}_{t}
’s are generated by
s
point sources located at
y_{j}
’s in
\mathcal{O}
with length
\frac{s\pi}{2\Omega}
with a cluster structure:
D_{1}^{c}=\left(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n_{1}}\right),D_{2}^{c}=\left(y_{n_{1}+1},\cdots,y_{n_{1}+n_{2}}\right)
,
\cdots,D_{K}^{c}=\left(y_{\sum_{q=1}^{K-1}n_{q}+1},\cdots,y_{s}\right)
. Assume that
\ \max_{q=1}^{K}n_{q}=n
,
\min_{y_{p},y_{j}\in D_{q}^{c}}\left|{y_{p}-y_{j}}\right|=d_{\min}
, and

\min_{y_{p}\in D_{q_{1}}^{c},y_{j}\in D_{q_{2}}^{c},q_{1}\neq q_{2}}\left|{y_{p}-y_{j}}\right|=d_{\max}
with
d_{\max}\geqslant 2d_{\min}
. Suppose that

\sigma<C_{1}(s)\left(\Omega d_{\max}\right)^{s-n}\left(\Omega d_{\min}\right)^{n}\sigma_{\infty,\min}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}),

(2.14)

for a certain
C_{1}(s)
sufficiently small. Then any solution to (
2.13
) contains exactly
s
points. Moreover, for
\{\widehat{y}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y}_{s}\}
being a corresponding solution, after reordering the
\widehat{y}_{j}
’s,

\left|{\widehat{y}_{j}-y_{j}}\right|<\frac{d_{\min}}{2},

(2.15)

and

\Big|\widehat{y}_{j}-y_{j}\Big|<\frac{C_{2}(s)}{\Omega}\left(\frac{\pi}{\Omega d_{\max}}\right)^{s-n}\mathrm{SRF}^{n-1}\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)},\quad 1\leqslant j\leqslant s,

(2.16)

where
C_{2}(s)
is a constant depending on
s
and
\mathrm{SRF}:=\frac{\pi}{\Omega d_{\min}}
. In particular, for
d_{\max}\geqslant\frac{c_{0}\pi}{\Omega}
with
c_{0}\geqslant 1
, the noise constraint becomes

\sigma<C_{1}(s)\left(c_{0}\pi\right)^{s-n}\left(\Omega d_{\min}\right)^{n}\sigma_{\infty,\min}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}),

(2.17)

and (
2.16
) becomes

\Big|\widehat{y}_{j}-y_{j}\Big|<\frac{C_{2}(s)}{\Omega}\frac{1}{c_{0}^{s-n}}\mathrm{SRF}^{n-1}\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)},\quad 1\leqslant j\leqslant s.

(2.18)

Theorem
2.2
and in particular, equations (
2.17
) and (
2.18
), indicate that when the clusters are separated by at least a Rayleigh length, the resolution and stability for the location recovery of multi-cluster sources are mainly determined by the sparsity and the structure of sources in the largest cluster.

Our estimate (
2.16
) is similar to the result in
[
25
]
, indicating that the ESPRIT and the sparsity-based method have similar performance even in resolving clustered sources. On the other hand, despite the exponents of
\Omega d_{\min}
in (
2.14
) and
\mathrm{SRF}
in (
2.16
) are only
n
(i.e., the size of the largest cluster), which is optimal, the
\sigma_{\infty,\min}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top})
inside (as well as the
\lambda(X)
in
[
25
]
) is still determined by the amplitude vectors of all point sources, which is not optimal. We conjecture that the stability of resolving point sources is determined only by the sparsity and the amplitude vectors of the sources in each cluster providing that the clusters are well-separated. We hope to improve such estimate in future work.

2.5

Comparison with the single snapshot case

In this subsection, we compare the resolution in the single snapshot case with that in the multiple snapshots case, whereby we illustrate the effect of multiple snapshots in enhancing the resolution.

In
[
33
]
, the authors estimate the so-called computational resolution limit for the line spectral estimation problem (or DOA estimation) of the single measurement case. The results in
[
33
]
show that, for the single measurement case, when the point sources are separated by

\tau=\frac{c_{0}}{\Omega}\sigma^{\frac{1}{2n-1}},

for some positive constant
c_{0}
, there exists a discrete measure
\mu=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\delta_{y_{j}}
with
n
point sources located at
\{-\tau,-2\tau,-n\tau\}
and another discrete measure
\widehat{\mu}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widehat{a}_{j}\delta_{\widehat{y}_{j}}
with
n
point sources located at
\{0,\tau,\cdots,(n-1)\tau\}
such that

\left|\left|{\mathcal{F}[\widehat{\mu}]-\mathcal{F}[\mu]}\right|\right|_{\infty}<\sigma,

and the minimum magnitude of amplitudes of
\widehat{\mu}
and
\mu
are of order one.

This result demonstrates that when the point sources are separated by
\frac{c_{0}}{\Omega}\sigma^{\frac{1}{2n-1}}
, the solution of the
l_{0}
-minimization problem in the single measurement case

\min_{\widehat{\mu}}\left|\left|{\widehat{\mu}}\right|\right|_{0}\quad\text{subject to}\quad\left|\left|{\mathcal{F}[\widehat{\mu}]-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}}\right|\right|_{\infty}<\sigma,

(2.19)

is not stable. In particular, the recovered point sources by (
2.19
) may be located in an interval completely disjoint from that of the ground truth.

Therefore, for the single snapshot case, when the point sources are separated by
O(\frac{\sigma^{\frac{1}{2n-1}}}{\Omega})
, the

l_{0}
-minimization may be unstable. However, for the multi-snapshot spectral estimation, when the point sources are separated by
O(\frac{(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)})^{\frac{1}{n}}}{\Omega})
, the
l_{0}
-minimization (
2.4
) is still stable.

For example, in the DOA estimation, suppose we have a high degree of incoherence in the amplitude vectors
a_{j,t}
’s (such as Gaussian random variables in (
2.12
)), making
\frac{1}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)}
or
\left(\frac{1}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}
be of constant order, the resolution now is of order
O(\frac{\sigma^{\frac{1}{n}}}{\Omega})
. Compared with resolution in the single measurement case, say of order
O(\frac{\sigma^{\frac{1}{2n-1}}}{\Omega})
, this clearly shows a significant enhancement and illustrates the effect of multiple snapshots in improving the resolution.

2.6

Lower bound for the resolution estimate

Theorem
2.1
states that when we have a high degree of incoherence for columns of
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}
so that
\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)
is of order one, the resolution of the sparse recovery (
2.4
) should be less than
\frac{c_{0}}{\Omega}\sigma^{\frac{1}{n}}
for some positive constant
c_{0}
. The following proposition, proven in Appendix
C
, illustrates that this resolution order is the best achievable.

Proposition 2.3
.

Given
n\geqslant 2
, and source amplitudes
a_{j,t},1\leqslant j\leqslant n,1\leqslant t\leqslant T
, let
\tau
be given by

\tau=\frac{0.044}{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{\left|\left|{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}\right|\right|_{1}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

(2.20)

with
\frac{\sigma}{\left|\left|{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}}\right|\right|_{1}}\leq 1
. Then there exist
n
points
y_{1}=-\tau,y_{2}=-2\tau,\ldots,y_{n}=-n\tau
and
n
points
\widehat{y}_{1}=0,\widehat{\tau},\cdots,\widehat{y}_{n}=(n-1)\tau
, such that there exist
\widehat{a}_{j,t}
’s so that

\left|\left|{\mathcal{F}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widehat{a}_{j,t}\delta_{\widehat{y}_{j}}\right]-\mathcal{F}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j,t}\delta_{y_{j}}\right]}\right|\right|_{\infty}<\sigma,\ t=1,\cdots,T.

In Proposition
2.3
, the recovered location and the ground truth are in two intervals that are completely disjoint from each other, rendering stable location recovery impossible. In particular, normalizing the magnitude of the amplitude vector (or signal) such that
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left|{a_{j,t}}\right|=O(1),t=1,\cdots,T
, (
2.20
) simplifies to

\tau\approx\frac{0.044}{\Omega}\sigma^{\frac{1}{n}},

which corroborates the sharpness of estimate (
2.8
) for highly uncorrelated amplitude vectors.

We also conjecture that, for a given amplitude matrix
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}
, the optimal resolution lower-bound estimate should be of order
O\left(\frac{1}{\Omega}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\right)
, validating the sharpness of (
2.8
) in general cases. We hope to prove it in future research.

2.7

Discrete model and MMV problems

In this subsection, we introduce the discrete version of the model (
2.2
) and the problem (
2.4
). Specifically, we assume the
n
sources
\delta_{y_{j}}
’s are supported on
N
evenly spaced grid points
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}\}
in
[-d,d]
. The collection of point sources in each snapshot is still

\mu_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j,t}\delta_{y_{j}},\quad t=1,\cdots,T,

(2.21)

where
T
is the total number of snapshots. We sample the Fourier transform of
\mu_{t}
at
M>n
equispaced points:

\mathbf{Y}_{t}\left(\omega_{q}\right)=\mathcal{F}[\mu_{t}]\left(\omega_{q}\right)+\mathbf{W}_{t}\left(\omega_{q}\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j,t}e^{iy_{j}\omega_{q}}+\mathbf{W}_{t}\left(\omega_{q}\right),\quad 1\leqslant q\leqslant M,t=1,\cdots,T,

(2.22)

where
\omega_{1}=-\Omega,\omega_{2}=-\Omega+h,\cdots,\omega_{M}=\Omega
. Here
h=\frac{2\Omega}{M-1}
is the sampling spacing and
\mathbf{W}_{t}\left(\omega_{q}\right)
’s are the noise. Throughout, we assume that
M>n
and that
h\leqslant\frac{\pi}{2d}
. The latter assumption excludes the non-uniqueness of the point source due to shifts by multiples of
\frac{2\pi}{h}
. Denote

\mathbf{Y}_{t}=\left(\mathbf{Y}_{t}\left(\omega_{1}\right),\cdots,\mathbf{Y}_{t}\left(\omega_{M}\right)\right)^{\top},\text{ and }\mathbf{W}_{t}=\left(\mathbf{W}_{t}\left(\omega_{1}\right),\cdots,\mathbf{W}\left(\omega_{M}\right)\right)^{\top}.

Then (
2.22
) can be rewritten as

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{S}}=\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Phi}}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}+\boldsymbol{\mathbf{W}},

(2.23)

where
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{S}}=\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}_{1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}_{T}\right)
,
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{W}}=\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{W}}_{1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{W}}_{T}\right)
,
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}
is the amplitude matrix defined by (
2.6
), and

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Phi}}:=\left(\phi_{M-1}(e^{ix_{1}}),\cdots,\phi_{M-1}(e^{ix_{N}})\right)

(2.24)

with
\phi_{s}(z):=z^{-\Omega}(1,z,\cdots,z^{s})^{\top}
and
x_{j}
’s being the grid points. The problem (
2.23
) is known as the multiple measurement vectors (MMV) problem
[
10
,
8
,
12
]
, which is common in DOA estimation, machine learning, and compressive sensing. The sparse recovery associated with MMV
[
12
,
56
,
55
]
reads

\min_{\mathbf{\widehat{A}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times T}}\left|\left|{\mathbf{\widehat{A}}}\right|\right|_{\text{row}-0}\quad\text{ subject to }\left|\left|{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{S}}-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Phi}}\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\widehat{A}}}}\right|\right|_{\mathrm{F}}<\sigma,

(2.25)

where the matrix
\mathbf{\widehat{A}}=\left(\widehat{a}_{j,t}\right)_{j=1,\cdots,N,t=1,\cdots,T}
and
\left|\left|{\mathbf{\widehat{A}}}\right|\right|_{\text{row}-0}
is the row-
l_{0}
quasi-norm
[
56
,
55
]
defined as

\left|\left|{\mathbf{\widehat{A}}}\right|\right|_{\text{row}-0}:=\#\operatorname{rowsupp}(\mathbf{\widehat{A}})

with
\operatorname{rowsupp}\left(\mathbf{\widehat{A}}\right):=\left\{j\in\{1,\cdots,N\}:\widehat{a}_{j,t}\neq 0\text{ for some }t\in\{1,\cdots,T\}\right\}
. Note that this is also a transformation of the optimization (
2.4
) in the discrete setting. The convex relaxation of this combinatorial problem is

\min_{\mathbf{\widehat{A}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times T}}\left|\left|{\mathbf{\widehat{A}}}\right|\right|_{\mathrm{rx}}\quad\text{ subject to }\left|\left|{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{S}}-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Phi}}\mathbf{\widehat{A}}}\right|\right|_{\mathrm{F}}<\sigma,

where
\left|\left|{\mathbf{\widehat{A}}}\right|\right|_{\mathrm{rx}}:=\sum_{1\leqslant j\leqslant N}\max_{t}\left|\widehat{a}_{j,t}\right|
. See
[
56
,
55
]
for a detailed discussion of these problems and the proposed algorithms. In particular,
[
56
,
55
]
focus on the simultaneous sparse approximation of signals consisting of general elementary atoms, and their stability findings (Theorem 5.1) do not apply to the super-resolution problem, due to the ill-condition of the corresponding dictionary.

As a consequence of Theorem
2.1
, we derive the following stability result for the optimization problem (
2.25
).

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose that the following separation condition for the point sources holds:

d_{\min}:=\min_{p\neq j}\left|{y_{p}-y_{j}}\right|_{\frac{n\pi}{\Omega}}\geqslant\frac{4.4e\pi}{\Omega}\Big(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n}},

(2.26)

with
\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top})}\leqslant 1
. Then any solution
\mathbf{\widehat{A}}
to (
2.25
) contains exactly
n
nonzero rows. Moreover, let
\widehat{y}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y}_{n}
be points in the grid
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}\}
corresponding to the nonzero rows of a solution of (
2.25
). After reordering the
\widehat{y}_{j}
’s, we have

\Big|\widehat{y}_{j}-y_{j}\Big|_{\frac{n\pi}{\Omega}}<\frac{d_{\min}}{2},

(2.27)

and

\Big|\widehat{y}_{j}-y_{j}\Big|_{\frac{n\pi}{\Omega}}<\frac{C(n)}{\Omega}\mathrm{SRF}^{n-1}\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}\right)},\quad 1\leqslant j\leqslant n,

(2.28)

where
\mathrm{SRF}=\frac{\pi}{\Omega d_{\min}}
is the super-resolution factor and
C(n)
is a constant depending on
n
.

Proof.

Let
\widehat{y}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y}_{k}
be points in the grid
\{x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}\}
corresponding to the nonzero rows of
\mathbf{\widehat{A}}
. Denote

\widehat{\mu}_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\widehat{a}_{j,t}\delta_{\widehat{y}_{j}},\quad\mu_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j,t}\delta_{y_{j}}.

Observe that the measurement constraint
\left|\left|{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{S}}-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\Phi}}\mathbf{\widehat{A}}}\right|\right|_{\mathrm{F}}<\sigma
implies

\max_{t=1,\cdots,T}\max_{j=1,\cdots,M}\left|{\mathcal{F}\left[\widehat{\mu}_{t}\right](\omega_{j})-\mathcal{F}\left[\mu_{t}\right](\omega_{j})}\right|<\sigma.

Then by similar arguments as those in the proof of Theorem
2.1
, we can prove the theorem. The constant factor
4.4e\pi
here, which is different from the one in Theorem
2.1
, is due to the fact that the discrete sampling results in a slight modification of the proof.
∎

Similarly, Proposition
2.3
(with a slight modification) gives a lower bound estimation for the problem (
2.25
). To the best of our knowledge, our theory is the first stability result for the sparse MMV recovery in the super-resolution problem.

\left|\left|{\mathbf{W}_{t}}\right|\right|_{\infty}<\sigma

\sigma

\left|\left|{f}\right|\right|_{\infty}=\max_{\left|\left|{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}}}\right|\right|_{2}\leqslant\Omega}\left|{f(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\omega}})}\right|

-minimization problem) under the measurement constraint. We suppose that the point sources are located in a sphere

\displaystyle\min_{\{\mathbf{\widehat{y}}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{\widehat{y}}_{k}\},\ \mathbf{\widehat{y}}_{j}\in\mathcal{O},1\leqslant j\leqslant k}\left(\#\{\mathbf{\widehat{y}}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{\widehat{y}}_{k}\}\right)

\displaystyle\text{subject to the existence of $\widehat{a}_{j,t}$'s such that}\ \left|\left|{\mathcal{F}\left[\sum_{j=1}^{n}\widehat{a}_{j,t}\delta_{\mathbf{\widehat{y}}_{j}}\right]-\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}_{t}}\right|\right|_{\infty}<\sigma,\ 1\leqslant t\leqslant T.

3.2

Main results for the stability of sparse recoveries in multi-dimensions

The amplitude matrix is still

\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{1,1}&\cdots&a_{1,T}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
a_{n,1}&\cdots&a_{n,T}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

(3.3)

Define

\gamma(d)=\begin{cases}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1}{j},&d\geqslant 1,\\
0,&d=0.\end{cases}

(3.4)

We have the following theorem. We refer to Section
6
for its proof.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
n\geqslant 2
and let the sphere
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be of radius
\frac{c_{0}n\pi}{4\Omega}
with
c_{0}\geqslant 1
. Let
\boldsymbol{\mathbf{Y}}_{t}
’s be the measurements that are generated by
n
point sources at
\{\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{n}\},\boldsymbol{\mathbf{y}}_{j}\in\mathcal{O}

d_{\min}:=\min_{p\neq j}\Big|\Big|\mathbf{y}_{p}-\mathbf{y}_{j}\Big|\Big|_{2}\geqslant\frac{2.2c_{0}e\pi 4^{d-1}\left(\frac{(n+2)(n+1)}{2}\right)^{\gamma(d-1)}}{\Omega}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\infty,\min}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}.

(3.5)

Then any solution to (
3.2
) contains exactly
n
points. Moreover, for
\{\mathbf{\widehat{y}}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{\widehat{y}}_{n}\}

Non-linear approximation theory in Vandermonde space

In this section, we present the main technique that is used in the proofs of the main results of the paper, namely the approximation theory in Vandermonde space. This theory was first introduced in
[
34
,
33
]
.
Instead of considering the non-linear approximation problem there, we consider a different approximation problem, which is relevant to the stability analysis of (

s\in\mathbb{N},s\geqslant 1,
and
z\in\mathbb{C}

\phi_{s}(z)=(1,z,\cdots,z^{s})^{\top}.

(4.1)

We consider the following non-linear problem:

\min_{\widehat{\theta}_{j}\in\mathbb{R},j=1,\cdots,k}\max_{t=1,\cdots,T}\min_{\widehat{a}_{j,t}\in\mathbb{C},j=1,\cdots,k}\Big|\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{k}\widehat{a}_{j,t}\phi_{s}(e^{i\widehat{\theta}_{j}})-v_{t}\Big|\Big|_{2},

(4.2)

where
v_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}a_{j,t}\phi_{s}(e^{i\theta_{j}})

\theta_{j}

s=k
. The main results are presented in Section

.

4.1

Notation and Preliminaries

We first introduce some notation and preliminaries. We denote for

\zeta(k)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}(\frac{k-1}{2}!)^{2},&\text{$k$ is odd,}\\

\frac{(\frac{k-2}{2}!)^{2}}{4},&\text{$k$ is even}.\end{array}\right.

(4.3)

We also define for

, and

\eta_{p,q}(z_{1},\cdots,z_{p},\widehat{z}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{z}_{q})=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}|(z_{1}-\widehat{z}_{1})|\cdots|(z_{1}-\widehat{z}_{q})|\\

(4.4)

.

Lemma 4.1

For

\min_{p\neq j}|\theta_{p}-\theta_{j}|_{2\pi}=\theta_{\min}

\widehat{\theta}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\theta}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}

\left|\left|{\eta_{k+1,k}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\cdots,e^{i\theta_{k+1}},e^{i\widehat{\theta}_{1}},\cdots,e^{i\widehat{\theta}_{k}})}\right|\right|_{\infty}\geqslant\xi(k)\left(\frac{2\theta_{\min}}{\pi}\right)^{k}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\epsilon>0
. For
\theta_{j},\widehat{\theta}_{j}\in\mathbb{R},j=1,\cdots,k
, assume that

\left|\left|{\eta_{k,k}(e^{i\theta_{1}},\cdots,e^{i\theta_{k}},e^{i\widehat{\theta}_{1}},\cdots,e^{i\widehat{\theta}_{k}})}\right|\right|_{\infty}<\left(\frac{2}{\pi}\right)^{k}\epsilon,

(4.5)

where
\eta_{k,k}
is defined as in (

), and that

\theta_{\min}=\min_{q\neq j}|\theta_{q}-\theta_{j}|_{2\pi}\geqslant\Big(\frac{4\epsilon}{\lambda(k)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{k}},

\widehat{\theta}_{j}

\left|{\widehat{\theta}_{j}-\theta_{j}}\right|_{2\pi}<\frac{\theta_{\min}}{2},\quad j=1,\cdots,k,

(4.8)

and moreover,

\left|{\widehat{\theta}_{j}-\theta_{j}}\right|_{2\pi}<\frac{2^{k-1}\epsilon}{(k-2)!(\theta_{\min})^{k-1}},\quad j=1,\cdots,k.

(4.9)

4.2

Approximation theory in Vandermonde space

Before presenting a lower bound for problem (
4.2
), we introduce a basic approximation result in Vandermonde space. This result was first derived in
[
33

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
k\geqslant 1
. For fixed

be the

be the one-dimensional orthogonal complement of

in

. Denote by

the orthogonal projection onto

in

Theorem 4.4

\theta_{j}\in\mathbb{R},1\leqslant j\leqslant k+1,

distinct points with

Proof.

Step 1.

So, we only need to consider the case when

k
distinct points
\widehat{\theta}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\theta}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}

\max_{t=1,\cdots,T}\min_{\widehat{\alpha}_{t}\in\mathbb{C}^{k}}\left|\left|{\widehat{A}(k)\widehat{\alpha}_{t}(k)-A\alpha_{t}}\right|\right|_{2}\geqslant\frac{\sigma_{\infty,\min}(B)\xi(k)(\theta_{\min})^{k}}{\pi^{k}}.

V

Lemma A.1
.

For

and sufficiently small

|(1-e^{ip-i\Delta})(1-e^{iq+i\Delta})|-|(1-e^{ip})(1-e^{iq})|<0.

Proof.

We only prove (

) is equivalent to showing

Thus, the case when
p>\pi
can be reduced to the case

. We first prove the result for
p\neq q
. Introduce the function

We calculate that

\displaystyle\frac{dg(\Delta)}{d\Delta}\Big|_{\Delta=0}=\frac{d|(1-e^{i(p-\Delta)})(1-e^{i(q+\Delta)})|}{d\Delta}\Big|_{\Delta=0}=\frac{d|1-e^{i(p-\Delta)}-e^{i(q+\Delta)}+e^{i(p+q)}|}{d\Delta}\Big|_{\Delta=0}=\frac{d\sqrt{g_{1}(\Delta)}}{d\Delta}\Big|_{\Delta=0},

Step 2.

By the claim in Step 1, the minimizer
(\widehat{\theta}_{1},\cdots,\widehat{\theta}_{k})
and
(\theta_{1},\cdots,d_{k+1})

\displaystyle 0\leqslant\theta_{1}\leqslant\widehat{\theta}_{1}<\theta_{2}\leqslant\widehat{\theta}_{2}<\cdots<\theta_{k}\leqslant\widehat{\theta}_{k}\leqslant\theta_{k+1}<2\pi.

, if

. It is clear that by the interlacing relation (

\widehat{\theta}_{j_{0}}
be the first point (starting from

) such that

(A.6)

Without loss of generality, we suppose that
\theta_{j_{0}}>\pi
. Then, we decompose the rest of the
\theta_{j}
’s into two sets:
set_{1}=\big\{\theta_{j_{-1}},\cdots,\theta_{j_{-p}}\big\}
contains all the

|e^{i\theta_{j_{-(q+1)}}}-e^{i\theta_{j_{0}}}|>|e^{i\theta_{j_{-q}}}-e^{i\theta_{j_{0}}}|,\quad|e^{i\theta_{j_{q+1}}}-e^{i\theta_{j_{0}}}|>|e^{i\theta_{j_{q}}}-e^{i\theta_{j_{0}}}|.

(A.7)

We also denote the corresponding point
\widehat{\theta}_{j}
in

for

\widehat{\theta}_{j_{-(q+1)}}

\widehat{\theta}_{j}

\displaystyle|\Pi_{q=1}^{k}(e^{i\theta_{j_{0}}}-e^{i\widehat{\theta}_{q}})|

\displaystyle=|e^{i\theta_{j_{0}}}-e^{i\widehat{\theta}_{j_{0}}}||\Pi_{q=1}^{p}(e^{i\theta_{j_{0}}}-e^{i\widehat{\theta}_{j_{-q}}})||\Pi_{s=1}^{k-1-p}(e^{i\theta_{j_{0}}}-e^{i\widehat{\theta}_{j_{s}}})|

\displaystyle|\Pi_{q=1}^{k}(e^{i\theta_{j_{0}}}-e^{i\widehat{\theta}_{q}})|>(\frac{\theta_{\min}}{\pi})^{2}|\Pi_{q=2}^{p}(e^{i\theta_{j_{0}}}-e^{i\widehat{\theta}_{j_{-q}}})||\Pi_{s=1}^{k-1-p}(e^{i\theta_{j_{0}}}-e^{i\widehat{\theta}_{j_{s}}})|.

|\theta_{j_{0}}-\widehat{\theta}_{-q}|_{2\pi}>|\theta_{j_{0}}-\theta_{-q+1}|_{2\pi}

and for

. Thus, we obtain the following estimate:

\displaystyle|\Pi_{q=1}^{k}(e^{i\theta_{j_{0}}}-e^{i\widehat{\theta}_{q}})|>(\frac{\theta_{\min}}{\pi})^{2}|\Pi_{q=2}^{p}(e^{i\theta_{j_{0}}}-e^{i\theta_{j_{-q+1}}})||\Pi_{s=1}^{k-1-p}(e^{i\theta_{j_{0}}}-e^{i\theta_{j_{s}}})|.

\displaystyle|\Pi_{q=1}^{k}(e^{i\theta_{j_{0}}}-e^{i\widehat{\theta}_{q}})|>(p-1)!(k-1-p)!(\frac{2\theta_{\min}}{\pi})^{k-2}(\frac{\theta_{\min}}{\pi})^{2}.

\min_{p=1,\cdots,k}(p-1)!(k-1-p)!\geqslant\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}(\frac{k-1}{2})!(\frac{k-3}{2})!&\text{$k$ is odd},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\cr(\frac{k-2}{2}!)^{2}&\text{$k$ is even}.\end{array}\right.

(A.9)

Thus, for

||\eta_{k+1,k}||_{\infty}\geqslant\xi(k)(\frac{2\theta_{\min}}{\pi})^{k}
.
∎

Proof.

\displaystyle||\eta_{k,k}||_{\infty}\geqslant\frac{\xi(k-2)}{4}(\theta_{\min})^{k}\geqslant\epsilon,

Step 3.

). For each

, we decompose the rest of the
\theta_{j}
’s into two sets:

’s in

\theta_{j}
’s. For

4.9
) in the same manner. The points in the two sets are arranged to satisfy that for

Paired operators and paired kernels
M. Cristina Câmara, André Guimarães, Jonathan R. Partington

Abstract
This paper is concerned with paired operators in the context of the Lebesgue Hilbert space on the unit circle and its subspace, the Hardy space. By considering when such operators commute, generalizations of the Brown–Halmos results for Toeplitz operators are derived. Further, the kernels of such operators are described, giving results on invariant and nearly-invariant subspaces, together with a generalization of Coburn’s theorem on Toeplitz kernels.



Keywords:

paired operator; paired kernel; Toeplitz operator; invariant subspace; nearly-invariant subspace;
kernel; Brown–Halmos theorem; Coburn’s theorem.

MSC (2010):
30H10, 47B35, 47B38

1
Introduction

Let
X
be a Hilbert space and
P,Q

complementary orthogonal projections on
X
. For
A,B
bounded linear operators on
X
we define the paired operators

S_{A,B}=AP+BQ
and
\Sigma_{A,B}=PA+QB
(see
[
15
]
). These
first appeared in the context of singular integral equations, and have been the object of
renewed interest because of the recently-established
connection with problems in diffraction theory
[
17
]
, dual truncated Toepliz operators

[
4
]
, and the characterization of block Toeplitz operators whose kernels present a scalar-type structure
[
7
, Cor. 3.4]
.

Here we work in the particular context of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, where
\mathbb{T}
is the unit circle, and

P
is
P^{+}
, the orthogonal projection onto the Hardy space
H^{2}
of the unit disc and
Q
is

P^{-}
, the complementary projection onto
(H^{2})^{\perp}=\overline{H^{2}_{0}}
.
The operators
A
and
B
are multiplication operators
A=M_{a}
and
B=M_{b}

with
a,b\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
This is the most common setting where these operators are studied, and also that with the most applications. They have been studied usually under an extra condition
that either
a
or
b
is invertible in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
or,
at most, has zeros of “integer order” at a finite
set of points of
\mathbb{T}
(see
[
15
, Sec. VI.8]
for a more precise definition).

Paired operators are also closely connected with Toeplitz operators: in fact, they are dilations of
Toeplitz operators.
Moreover, it can be shown that if
b^{\pm 1}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
, then the paired operator

aP^{+}+bP^{-}
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
is equivalent after extension (as defined in
[
1
]
) to
the Toeplitz operator
T_{a/b}
on
H^{2}
(see
[
3
]
), and an analogous
result can be obtained for dual Toeplitz operators if
a^{\pm 1}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
Thus it is natural to ask whether various well known properties of Toeplitz operators have analogues for paired operators.

In this paper we explore new connections of paired operators
with Toeplitz and Hankel operators. We study their
algebraic properties, such as when two such operators commute,
providing a generalization of the Brown–Halmos results for Toeplitz operators.
Then we consider the kernels of paired operators: this theme
leads off in two directions, one of which concerns invariant and nearly-invariant subspaces,
while in the other direction we arrive at a generalization of Coburn’s theorem
on Toeplitz kernels.

2
Paired operators: basic properties

From now on we write
L^{p}
to denote
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
for
p=2
and
p=\infty
.

Let
a,b\in L^{\infty}
. The
paired operator

S_{a,b}:L^{2}\to L^{2}
is
defined by

S_{a,b}f=aP^{+}f+bP^{-}f,\qquad(f\in L^{2}).

(2.1)

We call
\Sigma_{a,b}:L^{2}\to L^{2}
, defined by

\Sigma_{a,b}f=P^{+}af+P^{-}bf,\qquad(f\in L^{2}),

(2.2)

a
transposed paired operator
. We say that
S_{a,b}
and
\Sigma_{a,b}
are
dual
to each other,
while
S_{a,b}
and
\Sigma_{b,a}
are said to be
transposed
to each other (see
[
15
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a pair
\{a,b\}
is nondegenerate
if
a,b\in L^{\infty}
are nonzero a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
, and
a-b
is too.

Nondegeneracy will be a common hypothesis in our work, although sometimes
the weaker condition that
a-b\neq 0
will be sufficient.

Note that we identify
a
with
M_{a}
, the operator of multiplication by
a
whenever the context
is clear. In particular, we write
P^{+}a+P^{-}b
instead of

P^{+}aI+P^{-}bI
.

Using the orthogonal decomposition
L^{2}=H^{2}\oplus\overline{H^{2}_{0}}
we may write down
the matrix representation of
S_{a,b}
. For convenience, write
P^{\pm}a=a_{\pm}
and similarly for
b
.
Now since for
h_{+}\in H^{2}
we have
S_{a,b}h_{+}=ah_{+}
, the matrix form
of
S_{a,b}
is a matrix of Hankel and Toeplitz operators:

S_{a,b}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{+}+P^{+}a_{-}&P^{+}b_{+}\\
P^{-}a_{-}&P^{-}b_{+}+b_{-}\end{pmatrix}:H^{2}\oplus\overline{H^{2}_{0}}\to H^{2}\oplus\overline{H^{2}_{0}}.

Note that initially this matrix is only densely defined, but since
it corresponds to a bounded operator it can be extended
to the whole of
L^{2}
.

These various operators are related as follows.

Proposition 2.2
.

(i)
S_{a,b}^{*}=\Sigma_{\bar{a},\bar{b}}
, and

(ii)
\overline{S_{a,b}f}=zS_{\bar{b},\bar{a}}\bar{z}\bar{f}=z\Sigma_{b,a}^{*}\bar{z}\bar{f}

for all
f\in L^{2}
.

Proof.

(i) is clear; (ii) follows from the relations
\overline{P^{\pm}f}=zP^{\mp}\bar{z}\bar{f}
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
M=\max(\|a\|_{\infty},\|b\|_{\infty})
. Then

M\leq\|S_{a,b}\|\leq\min(\sqrt{2}M,\|a\|_{\infty}+\|b\|_{\infty}).

(2.3)

Proof.

Restricting to
f\in H^{2}
, we have
S_{a,b}f=af
and so
\|S_{a,b}\|\geq\|T_{a}\|=\|a\|_{\infty}
,
where
T_{a}
is the usual Toeplitz operator defined by
T_{a}f=P^{+}af
.
Analogously,
\|S_{a,b}\|\geq\|b\|_{\infty}
and so
\|S_{a,b}\|\geq M
.

Next,
\|S_{a,b}\|\leq\|a\|_{\infty}+\|b\|_{\infty}
is clear. Finally,
from

\|\phi\|_{2}^{2}=\|P^{+}\phi\|^{2}_{2}+\|P^{-}\phi\|_{2}^{2},\qquad(\phi\in L^{2}),

we know that for each such
\phi
there is a
\theta\in[0,\pi/2]
such that

\|P^{+}\phi\|_{2}=\|\phi\|_{2}\cos\theta
and
\|P^{-}\phi\|_{2}=\|\phi\|_{2}\sin\theta
.

Thus

\displaystyle\|S_{a,b}\phi\|_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|aP^{+}\phi+bP^{-}\phi\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\|a\|_{\infty}\cos\theta+\|b\|_{\infty}\sin\theta)\|\phi\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\{\|a\|_{\infty},\|b\|_{\infty}\}(\cos\theta+\sin\theta)\|\phi\|_{2}\leq M\sqrt{2}\|\phi\|_{2}.

So we also have

\|S_{a,b}\|\leq\sqrt{2}M,

(2.4)

and the second inequality in (
2.3
) follows.
∎

As a consequence, we can characterise the zero paired operator:

Corollary 2.4
.

S_{a,b}=0
if and only if
a=b=0
in
L^{\infty}
.

Remark 2.5
.

The estimates obtained in Proposition
2.3
are optimal. We have nontrivial cases where

\|S_{a,b}\|=M
, e.g., if
a=b=1
, and cases where
\|S_{a,b}\|=\sqrt{2}M
, e.g. if

a=1
and
b=z
. In the latter case, taking
\phi(z)=1+\dfrac{1}{z}
, we have

S_{a,b}\phi=2
and
\|\phi\|=\sqrt{2}
, so

\|S_{a,b}\|\geq\sqrt{2}
, and, from (
2.4
),
\|S_{a,b}\|=\sqrt{2}=\sqrt{2}M
.

Proposition 2.6
.

\|S_{a,b}\|=\|a\|_{\infty}+\|b\|_{\infty}

if and only if either
a
or
b
is
0
a.e.

Proof.

First if
b=0
then
S_{a,b}\phi=aP^{+}\phi
and since
\|S_{a,0}\|\geq\|T_{a}\|=\|a\|_{\infty}

(where
T_{a}
is the Toeplitz operator) it is clear that
\|S_{a,0}\|=\|a\|_{\infty}
too. Similarly if
a=0
.

For the converse, suppose that there is a
\phi\in L^{2}
with
\|\phi\|_{2}=1
and
for some
\epsilon
with
0<\epsilon<1
we have

\|aP^{+}\phi+bP^{-}\phi\|\geq(1-\epsilon)(\|a\|_{\infty}+\|b\|_{\infty}).

For what we are trying to prove we may assume that
a
and
b
are both nonzero.
There is a
\theta\in[0,\pi/2]
such that

\|P^{+}\phi\|_{2}=\cos\theta
and
\|P^{-}\phi\|_{2}=\sin\theta
.
Thus, with
A=\|a\|_{\infty}
and
B=\|b\|_{\infty}
we have

A\cos\theta+B\sin\theta\geq(1-\epsilon)(A+B),

and hence by Cauchy–Schwarz

A^{2}+B^{2}\geq(1-\epsilon)^{2}(A^{2}+2AB+B^{2})

or
(2\epsilon-\epsilon^{2})(A^{2}+B^{2})\geq 2(1-\epsilon)^{2}AB
.
Now suppose that
A\geq B
(the other way round is similar). Then we have

2(2\epsilon-\epsilon^{2})A^{2}\geq 2(1-\epsilon)^{2}AB

and so

B\leq(2\epsilon-\epsilon^{2})A/(1-\epsilon)^{2}
, and
since we can take
\epsilon>0
arbitrary, this means that
B=0
.
∎

3
Brown–Halmos type theorems

Paired operators are closely related to Toeplitz operators in
various ways. For instance, Toeplitz operators
T_{a}
,
with
a\in L^{\infty}
, can be seen as compressions to
H^{2}
of
paired operators of the form
S_{a,b}=aP^{+}+bP^{-}

or their duals
\Sigma_{a,b}=P^{+}a+P^{-}b
, with
b\in L^{\infty}
.
Given that, by the Brown–Halmos theorems
[
2
]
we have
for
a,\tilde{a}\in L^{\infty}
that

T_{a}T_{\tilde{a}}=T_{a\tilde{a}}\iff a\in\overline{H^{\infty}}\hbox{ or }\tilde{a}\in H^{\infty},

one may ask, similarly, when the composition of two paired operators is a paired operator
or, more precisely, when the equations

\displaystyle(aP^{+}+bP^{-})(\tilde{a}P^{+}+\tilde{b}P^{-})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\tilde{a}P^{+}+b\tilde{b}P^{-}

(3.1)

\displaystyle(P^{+}a+P^{-}b)(P^{+}\tilde{a}+P^{-}\tilde{b})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P^{+}(a\tilde{a})+P^{-}(b\tilde{b})

(3.2)

hold.
It is clear that (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) hold if
a=b
and
\tilde{a}=\tilde{b}

respectively. Otherwise we have the following (recall that nondegeneracy was defined in
Definition
2.1
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
a,\tilde{a},b,\tilde{b}\in L^{\infty}
and suppose that

\{a,b\}
and
\{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}\}
are nondegenerate. Then

\displaystyle S_{a,b}S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}=S_{a\tilde{a},b\tilde{b}}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\tilde{a}\in H^{\infty}\hbox{ and }\tilde{b}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{a,b}\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}=\Sigma_{a\tilde{a},b\tilde{b}}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle a\in\overline{H^{\infty}}\hbox{ and }b\in H^{\infty}.

(3.4)

Proof.

We have that

(aP^{+}+bP^{-})(\tilde{a}P^{+}+\tilde{b}P^{-})=a\tilde{a}P^{+}+b\tilde{b}P^{-}+T,

where

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-aP^{-}\tilde{a}P^{+}+aP^{+}\tilde{b}P^{-}+bP^{-}\tilde{a}P^{+}-bP^{+}\tilde{b}P^{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a-b)(P^{+}\tilde{b}P^{-}-P^{-}\tilde{a}P^{+}).

If
\tilde{a}\in H^{\infty}
and
\tilde{b}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
then
T=0
. Conversely,
suppose that
T=0
. Then, for all
f\in L^{2}
we have
P^{+}\tilde{b}P^{-}f=P^{-}\tilde{a}P^{+}f=0
and
therefore, taking
f=1
, we have
P^{-}\tilde{a}=0
, i.e.,
\tilde{a}\in H^{2}\cap L^{\infty}=H^{\infty}
.
Similarly, taking
f=\bar{z}
, we have
P^{+}\tilde{b}\bar{z}=0
, i.e.,
\tilde{b}\bar{z}\in\overline{H^{2}_{0}}\cap\bar{z}L^{\infty}=\bar{z}\overline{H^{\infty}}
.

The equivalence in (
3.4
) is proved similarly, or by taking adjoints.
∎

It follows from Proposition
3.1
that
S_{a,b}S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}=S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}S_{a,b}

when
a,\tilde{a}\in H^{\infty}
and
b,\tilde{b}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
, and
that
\Sigma_{a,b}\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}=\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}\Sigma_{a,b}
whenever

a,\tilde{a}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
and
b,\tilde{b}\in H^{\infty}
. More generally, we have
the following.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
a,\tilde{a},b,\tilde{b}\in L^{\infty}
with
\{a,b\},\{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}\}
nondegenerate.
Then

S_{a,b}S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}=S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}S_{a,b}\iff(a-b)(P^{-}\tilde{a}P^{+}-P^{+}\tilde{b}P^{-})=(\tilde{a}-\tilde{b})(P^{-}aP^{+}-P^{+}bP^{-}).

Proof.

S_{a,b}S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}-S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}S_{a,b}

\displaystyle=-aP^{-}\tilde{a}P^{+}+aP^{+}\tilde{b}P^{-}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle bP^{-}\tilde{a}P^{+}-bP^{+}\tilde{b}P^{-}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\tilde{a}P^{-}aP^{+}-\tilde{a}P^{+}bP^{-}\quad-\quad\tilde{b}P^{-}aP^{+}+\tilde{b}P^{+}bP^{-}

=-(a-b)(P^{-}\tilde{a}P^{+}-P^{+}\tilde{b}P^{-})-(\tilde{a}-\tilde{b})(P^{+}bP^{-}-P^{-}aP^{+}).

∎

Corollary 3.3
.

For
\eta\in L^{\infty}
and
\{a,b\}
nondegenerate,

\eta S_{a,b}=S_{a,b}\eta\iff\eta\in\mathbb{C}.

Proof.

\eta I=S_{\eta,\eta}
, and applying the calculation in the
proof of Proposition
3.2
we have

\eta S_{a,b}=S_{a,b}\eta\iff P^{-}\eta P^{+}=P^{+}\eta P^{-},

which is true if and only if
\eta\in\mathbb{C}
.
∎

One may then ask for which functions
f\in L^{2}
do multiplication by
\eta
and
S_{a,b}
commute.

Proposition 3.4
.

For
\eta\in L^{\infty}
,
\{a,b\}
nondegenerate, and
f\in L^{2}
,

\displaystyle\eta S_{a,b}f=S_{a,b}(\eta f)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\eta}\oplus\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\tilde{H}_{\eta}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\eta f_{+}\in H^{2},\eta f_{-}\in\overline{H^{2}_{0}}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\eta f_{+}=P^{+}(\eta f)

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\eta f_{-}=P^{-}(\eta f),

where the Hankel operators
H_{\eta}
and
\tilde{H}_{\eta}
are defined by

H_{\eta}=P^{-}\eta P^{+}
and
\tilde{H}_{\eta}=P^{+}\eta P^{-}
,
and we write
f_{\pm}=P^{\pm}f
.

Proof.

From Proposition
3.2
we have

\eta S_{a,b}f=S_{a,b}(\eta f)\iff P^{-}\eta f_{+}=P^{+}\eta f_{-},

which happens if and only if
P^{-}\eta f_{+}=P^{+}\eta f_{-}=0
,
or equivalently
\eta f_{+}\in H^{2}
and
\eta f_{-}\in\overline{H^{2}_{0}}
.

Now
P^{+}(\eta f)=P^{+}(\eta f_{+})+P^{+}(\eta f_{-})
;
if
\eta f_{+}\in H^{2}
and
\eta f_{-}\in\overline{H^{2}_{0}}
, then we have
P^{+}(\eta f)=\eta f_{+}
.

Conversely, if
\eta f_{+}=P^{+}(\eta f)
then
\eta f_{+}\in H^{2}
; on the other hand,
\eta f_{+}=P^{+}(\eta f)=P^{+}(\eta f_{+})+P^{+}(\eta f_{-})=\eta f_{+}+P^{+}(\eta f_{-})
,
so
P^{+}(\eta f_{-})=0
and we conclude
that
\eta f_{-}\in\overline{H^{2}_{0}}
.

The last equivalence is proved analogously.
∎

At this point we require the theory of inner and outer functions, and
the inner–outer factorization in
H^{2}
. A suitable reference is
[
16
, Chap. 1]
.

As an example of a class of functions
\eta\in L^{\infty}
and
f\in L^{2}
such that

P^{\pm}(\eta f)=\eta P^{\pm}f
we have the following, where
\theta
denotes an
inner function and
K_{\theta}
is its associated model space
K_{\theta}=H^{2}\ominus\theta H^{2}
.

Proposition 3.5
.

If
\eta\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
is such that
\eta=\alpha\bar{\theta}h
with
h\in H^{\infty}

and
\alpha
an inner function, then for all
f\in K^{\perp}_{\theta}
we have

P^{\pm}(\eta f)=\eta P^{\pm}f
.

Proof.

Since
f\in K^{\perp}_{\theta}
can be written in the form

f=f_{-}+\theta f_{+}
with
f_{-}\in\overline{H^{2}_{0}}
and
f+\in H^{2}
, we have that

\eta f=\eta(f_{-}+\theta f_{+})=\eta f_{-}+\alpha\bar{\theta}h\theta f_{+}=\eta f_{-}+\alpha hf_{+},

where
\eta f_{-}\in\overline{H^{2}_{0}}
and
\alpha hf_{+}\in H^{2}
. Therefore

P^{+}(\eta f)=\alpha hf_{+}=\eta\theta f_{+}=\eta P^{+}f
and
P^{-}(\eta f)=\eta f_{-}=\eta P^{-}f
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

We have that
w\in L^{\infty}
is a multiplier from a model space
K_{\theta_{1}}
onto
another model space
K_{\theta_{2}}
, i.e.,
wK_{\theta_{1}}=K_{\theta_{2}}
,
if and only if
w\in\mathop{\rm Hol}\nolimits(\mathbb{D})
with
w=\gamma\theta_{2}\overline{\theta_{1}}\bar{w}

where
|\gamma|=1

(see
[
9
]
and
[
6
]
). Thus conjugates of the surjective multipliers
between two model spaces provide examples of the functions
\eta
described in Proposition

3.5
.

4
Paired kernels

We denote the kernel of a paired operator, which we call a
paired kernel
, by

{\rm ker}_{a,b}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}.

(4.1)

Paired kernels are closely connected with Toeplitz kernels. To obtain this relation, we start
by recalling that two operators
T
and
S
, on the Banach spaces
X
and
Y
respectively,
are said to be
equivalent
if and only if there exist invertible operators

E
and
F
such that
T=ESF
. Clearly, in this case we have the isomorphism
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T\simeq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S
since we have

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T=F^{-1}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S
. The operators
T
and
S
are said to be

equivalent after extension
if and only if there are Banach spaces
\tilde{X}
and
\tilde{Y}

such that the operators

\tilde{T}=\left[\begin{matrix}T&0\\
0&I_{\tilde{X}}\end{matrix}\right]\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\tilde{S}=\left[\begin{matrix}S&0\\
0&I_{\tilde{Y}}\end{matrix}\right]

are equivalent (see
[
1
]
).

If
b\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}
(that is, if it is invertible in
L^{\infty}
), then it is clear
that
S_{a,b}
is equivalent to
S_{G,1}
, where
G=a/b
. On the other hand,
letting again
T_{G}
denote the Toeplitz operator with symbol
G\in L^{\infty}
,

T_{G}:H^{2}\to H^{2},\qquad T_{G}={P^{+}GP^{+}}_{|H^{2}},

(4.2)

we have that
T_{G}
is equivalent after extension to the paired operator

S_{G,1}
(see
[
3
]
). This means in particular that the kernels of the two
operators are isomorphic,

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}\simeq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{G,1}={\rm ker}_{G,1},

(4.3)

the isomorphism in this case being induced by the mapping
P^{+}
, and we have

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}=P^{+}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits_{G,1}=:{\rm ker}_{G,1}^{+}.

(4.4)

More generally, we denote

{\rm ker}^{\pm}_{a,b}=P^{\pm}{\rm ker}_{a,b}=P^{\pm}\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}.

(4.5)

As happens with Toeplitz kernels, paired kernels and their projections (
4.5
)
possess many interesting properties which, in the case of
{\rm ker}^{\pm}_{a,b}
, generalize those of Toeplitz kernels.

It is well known that, for
a\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
we have

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}=\{0\}
if
a\in H^{\infty}
and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}\not=\{0\}
if
a\in\overline{H^{2}_{0}}
.
A corresponding result for paired kernels is the following.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
a,b\in L^{\infty}\setminus\{0\}
. Then:

(i)

{\rm ker}_{a,b}=\{0\}
if
a\in H^{\infty}
and
b\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
;

(ii)

{\rm ker}_{a,b}\neq\{0\}
if
a\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
and
b\in H^{\infty}
has a nontrivial inner factor;

(iii)

{\rm ker}_{a,b}\neq\{0\}
if
a\in\overline{H^{\infty}_{0}}
and
b\in H^{\infty}
.

Proof.

(i)
aP^{+}f+bP^{-}f=0\iff aP^{+}f=-bP^{-}f
and,
since the left-hand side of this equation is in
H^{2}
while the right-hand side is in
\overline{H^{2}_{0}}
,
both are zero and it follows that
P^{\pm}f=0
, so
f=0
.

(ii) Let
b=b_{i}b_{o}
be the inner-outer factorization of
b
. Then we have

af_{+}+bf_{-}=0
for
f_{+}=\dfrac{b_{i}-b_{i}(0)}{z}b_{o}
and
f_{-}=-a\dfrac{1-b_{i}(0)\overline{b_{i}}}{z}
.

(iii) Clearly, taking
f_{+}=b
and
f_{-}=-a
gives a function
f=f_{+}+f_{-}
in
{\rm ker}_{a,b}
.
Alternatively, the result follows from part (ii), since
{\rm ker}_{a,b}={\rm ker}_{za,zb}
.
∎

We may see (iii) as a generalization of the property that

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}\neq\{0\}
if
a\in\overline{H^{\infty}_{0}}
, as follows:

If
a\in\overline{H^{\infty}_{0}}
, then
saying that
\phi_{+}\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}
is equivalent to saying that there is a
\phi_{-}\in\overline{H^{2}_{0}}
such that

a\phi_{+}=\phi_{-}
. That is,
a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0
, where
a\in\overline{H^{2}_{0}}
and
b=-1\in H^{\infty}
.

We now use the notation

\phi_{\pm}=P^{\pm}\phi,\qquad(\phi\in L^{2}).

(4.6)

Remark 4.2
.

Note that, with the assumption that
\{a,b\}
is nondegenerate, if
\phi\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}
and either
\phi_{+}=0
or

\phi_{-}=0
on a set of positive measure then, by the Luzin–Privalov theorem, we must have
\phi=0
.

Clearly, for any measurable complex-valued function
\eta
defined a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
such
that
\eta\neq 0
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
, we have the implication

\tilde{a}=\eta a,\quad\tilde{b}=\eta b\quad\implies\quad{\rm ker}_{a,b}={\rm ker}_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}.

(4.7)

It is thus natural to ask when two paired kernels are equal or related by inclusion, and whether the converse of (
4.7
) holds.

Proposition 4.3
.

For a nondegenerate pair
\{a,b\}

if
{\rm ker}_{a,b}\neq\{0\}
, then, for any
\tilde{a},\tilde{b}\in L^{\infty}
,
it holds that

{\rm ker}_{a,b}={\rm ker}_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}\quad\iff\quad a\tilde{b}=\tilde{a}b.

Proof.

Suppose that
{\rm ker}_{a,b}\neq\{0\}
and
{\rm ker}_{a,b}={\rm ker}_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
. Then,
for every
\phi\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}
with
\phi\neq 0
, we have

a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=\tilde{a}\phi_{+}+\tilde{b}\phi_{-}=0,

(4.8)

which implies that
(a\tilde{b}-\tilde{a}b)\phi_{+}\phi_{-}=0
.

If
a\tilde{b}-\tilde{a}b\neq 0
on a set of positive measure, then

\phi_{+}
or
\phi_{-}
must vanish on a set of positive measure, and by Remark
4.2
we have
\phi=0
, which is a contradiction. We
conclude then that
a\tilde{b}-\tilde{a}b=0
.

Conversely, suppose that
a\tilde{b}=\tilde{a}b
. Taking
\eta=\tilde{a}/a=\tilde{b}/b
we have

{\rm ker}_{a,b}={\rm ker}_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
by (
4.7
).
∎

This shows that the converse of (
4.7
) holds. We also have the following.

Corollary 4.4
.

Each nonzero
{\rm ker}_{a,b}

with
\{a,b\}
nondegenerate is uniquely determined by any of its nonzero elements and

{\rm ker}_{a,b}\cap{\rm ker}_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}\neq\{0\}\quad\iff\quad{\rm ker}_{a,b}={\rm ker}_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}.

if
\tilde{a}
and
\tilde{b}
are also in
L^{\infty}
.

Corollary 4.5
.

With the same assumptions as in Proposition
4.3
,
we have

{\rm ker}_{a,b}\subseteq{\rm ker}_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}\quad\iff\quad\left\{{\rm ker}_{a,b}={\rm ker}_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}\quad\vee\quad{\rm ker}_{a,b}=\{0\}\right\}.

The following result shows that, in its turn, each
nonzero
\phi\in L^{2}
uniquely determines a paired kernel.

Theorem 4.6
.

For each
\phi\in L^{2}\setminus\{0\}
, there is one and only one paired kernel to which
\phi
belongs.

Proof.

Take
\phi=\phi_{+}+\phi_{-}\in L^{2}\setminus\{0\}
and let

\phi_{+}=I_{+}O_{+}
and
\phi_{-}=I_{-}O_{-}
, where

I_{+}
and
\overline{I_{-}}
are inner functions and
O_{+}
and
\bar{z}\overline{O_{-}}

are outer functions in
H^{2}
. Then we have

\frac{I_{-}O_{-}}{I_{+}O_{+}}\phi_{+}=\phi_{-};\qquad\hbox{that is,}\qquad\overline{I_{+}}\frac{\phi_{+}}{O_{+}}=\overline{I_{-}}\frac{\phi_{-}}{O_{-}}.

(4.9)

Now
1/O_{+}
belongs to the Smirnoff class
{\mathcal{N}}_{+}
, so that there are

H_{1+}
and
H_{2+}
in
H^{\infty}
such that

\frac{1}{O_{+}}=\frac{H_{1+}}{H_{2+}}

(4.10)

(see, for example,
[
16
]
). Analogously,

\frac{1}{\bar{z}\overline{O_{-}}}=\frac{h_{1+}}{h_{2+}}

(4.11)

with
h_{1+},h_{2+}\in H^{\infty}
.
Thus, from (
4.9
)–(
4.11
) we have

(\overline{I_{+}}H_{1+}\overline{h_{2+}})\phi_{+}-(\overline{I_{-}}z\overline{h_{1+}}H_{2+})\phi_{-}=0

so
\phi\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}
with
a=\overline{I_{+}}H_{1+}\overline{h_{2+}}
and

b=-\overline{I_{-}}z\overline{h_{1+}}H_{2+}
, which are
L^{\infty}
functions.
By Corollary
4.4
,
{\rm ker}_{a,b}
is the only paired kernel containing
\phi
.
∎

5
Invariance and near invariance

Toeplitz kernels are
S^{*}
-invariant, where
S^{*}
is the backward shift
S^{*}=T_{\bar{z}}
, whenever the associated symbol is in
\overline{H^{\infty}}
, that is

S^{*}(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\phi})\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\phi}\quad\hbox{for}\quad\phi\in\overline{H^{\infty}}.

(5.1)

These Toeplitz kernels are the
model spaces
, and they are the
nontrivial subspaces of
H^{2}
that are invariant for
S^{*}
.

Recall that a subspace
M\subseteq H^{2}
is said to be

nearly
S^{*}
-invariant
if

f\in M,\quad f(0)=0\implies S^{*}f\in M.

(5.2)

While not all
Toeplitz kernels are
S^{*}
-invariant subspaces of
H^{2}
, they are all

nearly
S^{*}
-invariant
(see
[
5
]
for more on this subject).
Note that for
f\in H^{2}
we have
f(0)=0
if and only if
f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\bar{z}}
, where
H_{\bar{z}}
is the Hankel operator

H_{\bar{z}}=P^{-}\bar{z}P^{+}_{|H^{2}}
. Thus (
5.2
) for
M=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\phi}
can equivalently be
written as

f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\phi}\cap\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\bar{z}}\implies T_{\bar{z}}f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\phi}.

(5.3)

This is an important property of Toeplitz kernels which can be seen as describing, in the case
when
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{G}
is not invariant for
S^{*}
, the part of
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\phi}
that is mapped onto

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\phi}
by
T_{\bar{z}}=S^{*}
.

More generally, Toeplitz kernels are nearly
T_{\eta}
-invariant for every
\eta\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
, i.e.,

f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\phi}\cap\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\eta}\implies T_{\eta}f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\phi}

(5.4)

(see
[
5
, Sec. 3]
), and if in addition
\phi\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
, then
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{\phi}
is
invariant for
T_{\eta}
. It is natural to ask whether similar invariance properties hold for paired kernels.
We start by recalling that, for two operators
A
and
B
,

AB=BA\implies\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits A\hbox{ is invariant for }B,

(5.5)

since, if
f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits A
, then
A(Bf)=B(Af)=0
and so
Bf\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits A
. From this and
Proposition
3.2
we get the following.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
a,\tilde{a},b,\tilde{b}\in L^{\infty}
with
\{a,b\},\{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}\}
nondegenerate.
Then
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}
is invariant for
S_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
if
a,\tilde{a}\in H^{\infty}
and

b,\tilde{b}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
; also
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\Sigma_{a,b}
is invariant
for
\Sigma_{\tilde{a},\tilde{b}}
if
a,\tilde{a}\in\overline{H^{\infty}}
and
b,\tilde{b}\in H^{\infty}
.

Regarding the invariance properties with respect to
S_{\eta,\eta}=\eta I

we have:

Proposition 5.2
.

For
\eta\in L^{\infty}
and
\{a,b\}
nondegenerate we have
that if
f\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}
then we have
\eta f\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}
if and only if

f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\eta}\oplus\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\tilde{H}_{\eta},

(5.6)

where
H_{\eta}=P^{-}\eta P^{+}{}_{|H^{2}}
and
\tilde{H}_{\eta}=P^{+}\eta P^{-}{}_{|\overline{H^{2}_{0}}}
.

Proof.

Let
f,\eta f\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}
. Then

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle aP^{+}\eta f+bP^{-}\eta f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle aP^{+}\eta f_{+}+aP^{+}\eta f_{-}+bP^{-}\eta f_{+}+bP^{-}\eta f_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\eta f_{+}-aP^{-}\eta f_{+}+aP^{+}\eta f_{-}+bP^{-}\eta f_{+}+b\eta f_{-}-bP^{+}\eta f_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(a-b)(P^{-}\eta f_{+}-P^{+}\eta f_{-}),

so
P^{-}\eta f_{+}=P^{+}\eta f_{-}=0
, which is equivalent to

f\in\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\eta}\oplus\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\tilde{H}_{\eta}
.

The converse follows from the same calculation.
∎

We get an analogue of (
5.4
) as follows:

Proposition 5.3
.

For
\eta\in L^{\infty}
and
\{a,b\}
nondegenerate
and
f\in L^{2}
,

f\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}\cap(\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits H_{\eta}\oplus\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits\tilde{H}_{\eta})\implies S_{\eta,\eta}f\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}.

6
Coburn’s lemma

Coburn’s lemma for Toeplitz operators
[
8
]
asserts that for
g\in L^{\infty}

at least one of
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{g}
and
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T^{*}_{g}

is
\{0\}
.
To obtain a generalization of this lemma we study the relations between the kernels of

S_{a,b}
,
S_{\bar{b},\bar{a}}
, and
\Sigma_{\bar{a},\bar{b}}=S^{*}_{a,b}
.

Lemma 6.1
.

P^{\pm}(\bar{z}\bar{\phi})=\bar{z}\overline{\phi_{\mp}}
.

Proof.

Observe that
\bar{z}\bar{\phi}=\underbrace{\bar{z}\overline{\phi_{+}}}_{\in\overline{H^{2}_{0}}}+\underbrace{\bar{z}\overline{\phi_{-}}}_{\in H^{2}}

and the result follows.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

\overline{{\rm ker}_{a,b}}=z{\rm ker}_{\bar{b},\bar{a}}
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\phi\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle a\phi_{+}+b\phi_{-}=0

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\bar{a}\bar{z}\overline{\phi_{+}}+\bar{b}\bar{z}\overline{\phi_{-}}=0

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\bar{b}P^{+}(\bar{z}\bar{\phi})+\bar{a}P^{-}(\bar{z}\bar{\phi})=0

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle\bar{z}\bar{\phi}\in{\rm ker}_{\bar{b},\bar{a}},

where the third equivalence follows from Lemma
6.1
.
∎

We can thus define an antilinear isomorphism between
{\rm ker}_{a,b}
and
{\rm ker}_{\bar{b},\bar{a}}
,
as follows.

Proposition 6.3
.

The antilinear operator

J:{\rm ker}_{a,b}\to{\rm ker}_{\bar{b},\bar{a}},\qquad J\phi=\bar{z}\bar{\phi}

(6.1)

is well defined and bijective.

Next, we construct an isomorphism from

{\rm ker}^{*}_{a,b}:=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}^{*}
onto

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits_{\bar{a},\bar{b}}
.

Proposition 6.4
.

For nondegenerate
\{a,b\}

the operator
\tilde{J}:{\rm ker}^{*}_{a,b}\to\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits_{\bar{a},\bar{b}}
defined by

\tilde{J}\psi=(\bar{a}-\bar{b})\psi,\qquad(\psi\in{\rm ker}^{*}_{a,b})

(6.2)

is injective, and if

a\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty},\hbox{ or }b\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty},\hbox{ or }(a-b)\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty},

(6.3)

then
\tilde{J}
is an isomorphism from
{\rm ker}^{*}_{a,b}
onto
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits_{\bar{a},\bar{b}}
.

Proof.

We begin by showing that the operator is well defined, i.e.,

(\bar{a}-\bar{b})\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}^{*}\subseteq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{\bar{a},\bar{b}}.

(6.4)

Let
\psi\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{*}=\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits S_{a,b}^{*}
, which means that

P^{+}(\bar{a}\psi)=-P^{-}(\bar{b}\psi)=0,

(6.5)

since
H^{2}\cap\overline{H^{2}_{0}}=\{0\}
. Then

\displaystyle S_{\bar{a},\bar{b}}(\bar{a}-\bar{b})\psi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bar{a}P^{+}(\bar{a}\psi)-\bar{a}P^{+}(\bar{b}\psi)+\bar{b}P^{-}(\bar{a}\psi)-\bar{b}P^{-}(\bar{b}\psi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bar{a}P^{+}(\bar{b}\psi)+\bar{b}P^{-}(\bar{a}\psi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bar{a}(I-P^{-})(\bar{b}\psi)+\bar{b}(I-P^{+})(\bar{a}\psi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\bar{a}\bar{b}\psi+\bar{b}\bar{a}\psi=0,

so
(\bar{a}-\bar{b})\psi\in{\rm ker}_{\bar{a},\bar{b}}
.

Now the operator
\tilde{J}
is injective because we assume that
a-b\neq 0
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
, so it is left to show that
it is also surjective when (
6.3
) holds.

This is obvious if
a-b\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}
; suppose now
that
a\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}
or
b\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}
. We have, for

\psi\in{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{*}
, which must satisfy (
6.5
),

(\bar{a}-\bar{b})\psi=\phi\iff\underbrace{\bar{a}\psi}_{\in\overline{H^{2}_{0}}}-\underbrace{\bar{b}\psi}_{\in H^{2}}=\phi\iff\bar{a}\psi=\phi_{-}\hbox{ and }\bar{b}\psi=-\phi_{+}.

Thus we have, since
\bar{a}\phi_{+}+\bar{b}\phi_{-}=0
,

\psi=\frac{\phi_{-}}{\bar{a}}=\tilde{J}^{-1}\phi\qquad\hbox{if}\quad a\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}

and

\psi=-\frac{\phi_{+}}{\bar{b}}=\tilde{J}^{-1}\phi\qquad\hbox{if}\quad b\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}.

∎

Corollary 6.5
.

With the same assumptions as in Proposition
6.4
, if (
6.3
) holds, then

\tilde{J}^{-1}:{\rm ker}_{\bar{a},\bar{b}}\to{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{*}

is defined by

\tilde{J}^{-1}\phi=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\phi}{\bar{a}-\bar{b}}&\hbox{if}\quad a-b\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty},\\
\\
\dfrac{P^{-}\phi}{\bar{a}}&\hbox{if}\quad a\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty},\\
\\
-\dfrac{P^{+}\phi}{\bar{b}}&\hbox{if}\quad b\in\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}.\end{cases}

Corollary 6.6
.

If
{\rm ker}_{\bar{a},\bar{b}}=\{0\}
, then
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{*}=\{0\}
.

Taking Proposition
6.3
into account, we also have:

Corollary 6.7
.

The following are equivalent:

(i)
{\rm ker}_{b,a}=\{0\}
;

(ii)
{\rm ker}_{\bar{a},\bar{b}}=\{0\}
;

and, if at least one of
a,b
and
a-b
lies in
\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}
,

(iii)
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{*}=\{0\}
.

We can now formulate a generalization of Coburn’s lemma for Toeplitz operators
[
8
]
.

Theorem 6.8
.

Suppose that
a,b\neq 0
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
. Then

(i) We have
{\rm ker}_{a,b}=\{0\}
or
{\rm ker}_{b,a}=\{0\}
;
equivalently
{\rm ker}_{a,b}=\{0\}
or
{\rm ker}_{\bar{a},\bar{b}}=\{0\}
.

(ii) If at least one of
a,b
and
a-b
lies in
\mathcal{G}L^{\infty}
, then

{\rm ker}_{a,b}=\{0\}
or
{\rm ker}_{a,b}^{*}=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exist
\phi,\psi\neq 0
with
\phi\in{\rm ker}_{a,b},\psi\in{\rm ker}_{b,a}
. Then
clearly

a\phi_{+}=-b\phi_{-}\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad b\psi_{+}=-a\psi_{-}.

Therefore
ab\phi_{+}\psi_{+}=ab\phi_{-}\psi_{-}
, and, since

a,b\neq 0
a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
,
we have

\underbrace{\phi_{+}\psi_{+}}_{\in H^{1}}=\underbrace{\phi_{-}\psi_{-}}_{\in\overline{H^{1}_{0}}},

so both the terms above are
0
. This implies that either
\phi_{+}=0
or
\psi_{+}=0

and therefore, by Remark
4.2
,
\phi=0
or
\psi=0
, which
is a contradiction.
Therefore we cannot have both
{\rm ker}_{a,b}\neq\{0\}
and
{\rm ker}_{b,a}\neq\{0\}
;
by Corollary
6.7
,
{\rm ker}_{b,a}\neq\{0\}
is equivalent to
{\rm ker}_{\bar{a},\bar{b}}\neq\{0\}
.

Finally, (ii) follows from Corollary
6.7
(iii).
∎

Clearly the theorem does not apply to the cases
a=0
a.e. or
b=0
a.e., as is
easily verified.

We recover Coburn’s lemma when
b=1
. In that case we have

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits(aP^{+}+P^{-})=\{0\}

or
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits(\bar{a}P^{+}+P^{-})=\{0\}
,
where
\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits(aP^{+}+P_{-})\simeq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}
and

\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits(\bar{a}P^{+}+P^{-})\simeq\mathop{\rm ker}\nolimits T_{a}^{*}
(cf. (
4.3
)).

7
Final comments

In
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
, a new class of Toeplitz-like operators
T_{\omega}
is presented,
whose symbols
are complex rational functions
\omega
. The domain of
T_{\omega}
is

\{g_{+}\in H^{p}:\omega g_{+}=f+\rho\},

where
f\in L^{p}
and
\rho
is a strictly proper (i.e., zero at
\infty
) rational function with all poles on
\mathbb{T}
.
Then the authors write
T_{\omega}g_{+}=P^{+}f
. The operator is densely-defined as its domain
contains all polynomials.
Its kernel is then defined to be those
g_{+}
with
\omega g_{+}=f_{-}+\rho
.

Now, in the context of paired operators, we may take a more general
\omega=\phi/p
where
\phi\in L^{\infty}

and
p
is a polynomial with all zeros on
\mathbb{T}
.
We would naturally consider the kernel associated with the
bounded
paired
operator
S_{\phi,-p}
, since we then have, for
S_{\phi,-p}\,g=0
,

\phi g_{+}=pg_{-},\qquad\hbox{that is,}\qquad\omega g_{+}=g_{-}.

In general this gives a different outcome, as can be seen with the example

\omega(z)=1/(z-1)
. Its
T_{\omega}
kernel is the space of all
g_{+}
with

g_{+}/(z-1)=f_{-}+\rho
.

However, the kernel of
S_{1,1-z}
is the space of all
g
with

g_{+}+(1-z)g_{-}=0
.
Thus the kernels are different, namely
\mathbb{C}
in the first case and

\{0\}
in the second.

Finally, another recent paper that studies pairs of projections
(but in the context of model spaces, giving insights into
truncated Toeplitz operators) is
[
10
]
.

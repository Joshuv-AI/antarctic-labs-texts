Proper p p -harmonic functions and harmonic morphisms on the classical non-compact semi-Riemannian Lie groups
Elsa Ghandour, Sigmundur Gudmundsson

Abstract
. We apply the method of eigenfamilies to construct new explicit complex-valued p p -harmonic functions on the non-compact classical Lie groups, equipped with their natural semi-Riemannian metrics. We then employ this same approach to manufacture explicit complex-valued harmonic morphisms on these groups.

1.
Introduction

For a positive integer
p
, the complex-valued
p
-harmonic functions are solutions to a partial differential equation of order
2\,p
. This equation arises in various contexts, see for example the extensive analysis in
[
5
]
and a historic account in
[
15
]
. The best known applications are in physics e.g. for
p=2
in the areas of continuum mechanics, including elasticity theory and the solution of Stokes flows.
The literature on
2
-harmonic functions is vast, but until quiet recently, the domains were either surfaces or open subsets of flat Euclidean space, with only very few exceptions. For this see the regularly updated online bibliography
[
8
]
maintained by the second author.

In their recent article
[
11
]
, the authors produce
p
-harmonic functions on the classical Lie groups equipped with their standard Riemannian metrics. The primary goal of this work is to extend the study to the semi-Riemannian situation. By Theorem
3.2
we show how the problem can be reduced to finding an
eigenfamily
, i.e. a collection of complex-valued functions which are eigen both with respect to the
Laplace-Beltrami operator

\tau
and the
conformality operator

\kappa
, on the semi-Riemannian manifolds involved. The main part of this paper is devoted to the construction of such families on the following classical Lie groups equipped with their natural semi-Riemannian metrics

\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}}),\,\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{R}}),\,\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{H}}),\,\text{\bf SL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}}),\,\text{\bf SL}_{n}({\mathbb{R}}),\,\text{\bf SL}_{n}({\mathbb{H}}),

\text{\bf SO}(n,{\mathbb{C}}),\,\text{\bf Sp}(n,{\mathbb{C}}),\,\text{\bf Sp}(n,{\mathbb{R}}),\,\text{\bf SO}^{*}(2n),\,\text{\bf SU}(p,q),\,\text{\bf SO}(p,q),\,\text{\bf Sp}(p,q).

Our eigenfamilies can also be used to manufacture complex-valued harmonic morphisms on these manifolds as explained in Theorem
4.3
. They can therefore be seen as an interesting byproduct of the process presented here.

For semi-Riemannian geometry we recommend O’Neill’s standard text
[
16
]
. Readers not familiar with harmonic morphisms are advised to consult the standard text
[
2
]
by Baird and Wood,
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[

]
and the regularly updated online bibliography
[
7
]
. For the details from Lie theory, used in this paper, we refer the reader to
[
12
]
and
[

]
.

2.
Eigenfunctions and Eigenfamilies

In this paper we manufacture explicit complex-valued proper
p
-harmonic functions and harmonic morphisms on semi-Riemannian manifolds. For this we apply two different construction techniques which are presented in Theorem
3.2
and in Theorem
4.3
, respectively. The main ingredients for both these recipes are the common eigenfunctions for the tension field
\tau
and the conformality operator
\kappa
which we now describe.

Let
(M,g)
be an
m
-dimensional semi-Riemannian manifold and
T^{{\mathbb{C}}}M
be the complexification of the tangent bundle
TM
of
M
. We extend the metric
g
to a complex-bilinear form on
T^{{\mathbb{C}}}M
. Then the gradient
\nabla\phi
of a complex-valued function
\phi:(M,g)\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a section of
T^{{\mathbb{C}}}M
. In this situation, the well-known complex linear
Laplace-Beltrami operator
(alt.
tension field
)
\tau
on
(M,g)
acts locally on
\phi
as follows

\tau(\phi)=\Div(\nabla\phi)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\Big(g^{ij}\,\sqrt{|g|}\,\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{i}}\Big).

For two complex-valued functions
\phi,\psi:(M,g)\to{\mathbb{C}}
we have the following well-known fundamental relation

\tau(\phi\cdot\psi)=\tau(\phi)\cdot\psi+2\cdot\kappa(\phi,\psi)+\phi\cdot\tau(\psi),

where the complex bilinear
conformality operator

\kappa
is given by

\kappa(\phi,\psi)=g(\nabla\phi,\nabla\psi).

Locally this satisfies

\kappa(\phi,\psi)=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}g^{ij}\cdot\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x_{j}}.

Definition 2.1
.

[
10
]

Let
(M,g)
be a semi-Riemannian manifold. Then a complex-valued function
\phi:M\to{\mathbb{C}}
is said to be an
eigenfunction
if it is eigen both with respect to the Laplace-Beltrami operator
\tau
and the conformality operator
\kappa
i.e. there exist complex numbers
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that

\tau(\phi)=\lambda\cdot\phi\ \ \text{and}\ \ \kappa(\phi,\phi)=\mu\cdot\phi^{2}.

A set
\mathcal{E}=\{\phi_{i}:M\to{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,i\in I\}
of complex-valued functions is said to be an
eigenfamily
on
M
if there exist complex numbers
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{E}
we have

\tau(\phi)=\lambda\cdot\phi\ \ \text{and}\ \ \kappa(\phi,\psi)=\mu\cdot\phi\cdot\psi.

The following Theorem
2.2
shows that, given an eigenfamily
\mathcal{E}
, one can employ this to produce an extensive collection
\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\mathcal{E}}
of further such objects. The result is a semi-Riemannian version of Theorem 2.2 proven in
[
6
]
for the Riemannian case.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a semi-Riemannian manifold and the set of complex-valued functions

\mathcal{E}=\{\phi_{k}:M\to{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,k=1,2,\dots,n\}

be an eigenfamily on
M
i.e. there exist complex numbers
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathbb{C}}
such that for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{E}

\tau(\phi)=\lambda\cdot\phi\ \ \text{and}\ \ \kappa(\phi,\psi)=\mu\cdot\phi\cdot\psi.

Then the set of complex homogeneous polynomials of degree
d

\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\mathcal{E}}=\{P:M\to{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,P\in{\mathbb{C}}[\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{n}],\ P(\alpha\cdot\phi)=\alpha^{d}\cdot P(\phi),\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{C}}\}

is an eigenfamily on
M
such that for all
P,Q\in\mathcal{H}^{d}_{\mathcal{E}}
we have

\tau(P)=(d\cdot\lambda+d(d-1)\cdot\mu)\cdot P\ \ \text{and}\ \ \kappa(P,Q)=d^{2}\cdot\mu\cdot P\cdot Q.

Proof.

The statement can be proven with exactly the same arguments as its Riemannian counterpart, see Theorem 2.2 in
[
6
]
.
∎

3.
Proper
p
-Harmonic Functions

In this section we describe a method for manufacturing complex-valued proper
p
-harmonic functions on semi-Riemannian manifolds. This method was recently introduced for the Riemannian case in
[
11
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a semi-Riemannian manifold. For a positive integer
p
, the iterated Laplace-Beltrami operator
\tau^{p}
is given by

\tau^{0}(\phi)=\phi\ \ \text{and}\ \ \tau^{p}(\phi)=\tau(\tau^{(p-1)}(\phi)).

We say that a complex-valued function
\phi:(M,g)\to{\mathbb{C}}
is

(i)

p
-harmonic
if
\tau^{p}(\phi)=0
and

(ii)

proper
p
-harmonic
if
\tau^{p}(\phi)=0
and
\tau^{(p-1)}(\phi)
does not vanish identically.

The following Theorem
3.2
can be proven in exactly the same way as its Riemannian counterpart found as Theorem 3.1 in
[
11
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\phi:(M,g)\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a complex-valued function on a semi-Riemannian manifold and
(\lambda,\mu)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
be such that the tension field
\tau
and the conformality operator
\kappa
satisfy

\tau(\phi)=\lambda\cdot\phi\ \ \text{and}\ \ \kappa(\phi,\phi)=\mu\cdot\phi^{2}.

Then for any positive integer
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{+}
the non-vanishing function

\Phi_{p}:W=\{x\in M\,|\,\phi(x)\not\in(-\infty,0]\}\to{\mathbb{C}}

with

\Phi_{p}(x)=\begin{cases}c_{1}\cdot\log(\phi(x))^{p-1},&\text{if }\;\mu=0,\;\lambda\not=0\\[5.69046pt]
c_{1}\cdot\log(\phi(x))^{2p-1}+c_{2}\cdot\log(\phi(x))^{2p-2},&\text{if }\;\mu\not=0,\;\lambda=\mu\\[5.69046pt]
c_{1}\cdot\phi(x)^{1-\frac{\lambda}{\mu}}\log(\phi(x))^{p-1}+c_{2}\cdot\log(\phi(x))^{p-1},&\text{if }\;\mu\not=0,\;\lambda\not=\mu\end{cases}

is a proper
p
-harmonic function. Here
c_{1},c_{2}
are complex coefficients not both zero.

4.
Complex-Valued Harmonic Morphisms

In this section we describe a method for constructing complex-valued harmonic morphisms
\phi:(M,g)\to{\mathbb{C}}
from semi-Riemannian manifolds. This is a special case of the much studied harmonic morphisms
\phi:(M,g)\to(N,h)
between semi-Riemannian manifolds. They are maps which pull back local real-valued harmonic functions on
(N,h)
to harmonic functions on
(M,g)
. The standard reference for the extensive theory of harmonic morphisms is the book
[
2
]
, but we also recommend the updated online bibliography
[
7
]
.

The following result is a direct consequence of Theorem 3 of the paper
[
4
]
by B. Fuglede.

Proposition 4.1
.

A function
\phi:(M,g)\to{\mathbb{C}}
from a semi-Riemannian manifold to the standard Euclidean complex plane, is a harmonic morphism if and only if it is harmonic and horizontally conformal i.e.

\tau(\phi)=0\ \ \text{and}\ \ \kappa(\phi,\phi)=0.

The following Theorem
4.2
is a semi-Riemannian version of Theorem 5.2 of
[
1
]
, see also
[
2
]
. It gives the theory of complex-valued harmonic morphisms a strong geometric flavour and provides a useful tool for the construction of minimal submanifolds of codimension two. This is our main motivation for studying these maps.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\phi:(M,g)\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a horizontally conformal map from a semi-Riemannian manifold to the standard Euclidean complex plane. Then
\phi
is harmonic if and only if its fibres are minimal at regular points of
\phi
.

The next result shows that eigenfamilies can be utilised to manufacture a variety of harmonic morphisms.

Theorem 4.3
.

[
10
]

Let
(M,g)
be a semi-Riemannian manifold and

\mathcal{E}=\{\phi_{k}:M\to{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,k=1,2,\dots,n\}

be an eigenfamily of complex-valued functions on
M
. If
P,Q:{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{C}}
are linearily independent homogeneous polynomials of the same positive degree then the quotient

\frac{P(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n})}{Q(\phi_{1},\dots,\phi_{n})}

is a non-constant harmonic morphism on the open and dense subset

\{p\in M\,|\,Q(\phi_{1}(p),\dots,\phi_{n}(p))\neq 0\}.

5.
The Semi-Riemannian Lie Group
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})

The complex general linear group
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
of invertible
n\times n
matrices is given by

\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}\,|\,\det z\neq 0\}.

Its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
of left-invariant vector fields on
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
can be identified with
{\mathbb{C}}^{n\times n}
i.e. the linear space of complex
n\times n
matrices. We equip
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
with its natural semi-Riemannian metric
g
induced by the semi-Euclidean inner product
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\times\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
on
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
satisfying

g(Z,W)\mapsto-\,\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{e}\trace(Z\cdot W).

For
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
we then have the orthogonal decomposition

\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})=\mathfrak{gl}^{+}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\oplus\mathfrak{gl}^{-}_{n}({\mathbb{C}}),

where

\mathfrak{gl}^{+}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})=\mathfrak{u}(n)=\{Z\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\,|\,Z+\bar{Z}^{t}=0\}

is the set of skew-Hermitian matrices and

\mathfrak{gl}^{-}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})=i\cdot\mathfrak{u}(n)=\{Z\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\,|\,Z-\bar{Z}^{t}=0\}

the set of the Hermitian ones. Here
\mathfrak{u}(n)
is the Lie algebra of the unitary group
\text{\bf U}(n)
which is the maximal compact subgroup of
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
satisfying

\text{\bf U}(n)=\{z\in\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\,|\,z\,\bar{z}^{t}=I_{n}\}.

For
1\leq r,s\leq n
, we shall by
E_{rs}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
denote the matrix given by

(E_{rs})_{\alpha\beta}=\delta_{r\alpha}\delta_{s\beta}

and for
r<s
let
X_{rs},Y_{rs}
be the symmetric and skew-symmetric matrices

X_{rs}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(E_{rs}+E_{sr}),\ \ Y_{rs}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(E_{rs}-E_{sr}),

respectively. Further, let
D_{r}
be the diagonal elements with
D_{t}=E_{tt}
.
By
\mathcal{B}^{+}
we denote the orthonormal basis for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}(n)
satisfying

\mathcal{B}^{+}=\{Y_{rs},i\,X_{rs}\,|\,1\leq r<s\leq n\}\cup\{i\,D_{t}\,|\,t=1,2,\dots,n\}

and
\mathcal{B}^{-}=i\,\mathcal{B}^{+}
. Then
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}^{+}\cup\mathcal{B}^{-}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
such that
g(Z,Z)=1
if
Z\in\mathcal{B}^{+}
and
g(Z,Z)=-1
for
Z\in\mathcal{B}^{-}
. For later use we define the two standard matrices
J_{n}
and
I_{pq}
by

J_{n}=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_{n}\\
-I_{n}&0\end{bmatrix}\ \ \text{and}\ \ I_{pq}=\begin{bmatrix}-I_{p}&0\\
0&I_{q}\end{bmatrix}.

Let
G
be a subgroup of
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
inheriting the induced left-invariant semi-Riemannian metric from
g
. Then employing the Koszul formula for the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla

on
(G,g)
, we see that for all
Z,W\in\mathfrak{g}
we have

\displaystyle g(\hbox{$\nabla$\kern-3.00003pt\lower 4.30554pt\hbox{$Z$}\kern-1.00006pt{$Z$}},W)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g([W,Z],Z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{e}\trace(WZ-ZW)Z

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{e}\trace W(ZZ-ZZ)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

If
Z\in\mathfrak{g}
is a left-invariant vector field on
G
and
\phi:U\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a local complex-valued function on
G
then the first and second order derivatives satisfy

Z(\phi)(p)=\frac{d}{ds}\bigl(\phi(p\cdot\exp(sZ))\bigr)\Big|_{s=0},

Z^{2}(\phi)(p)=\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}\bigr(\phi(p\cdot\exp(sZ))\bigr)\Big|_{s=0}.

This implies that the tension field
\tau
and the conformality operator
\kappa
on
G
fulfill

\displaystyle\tau(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}}g(Z,Z)\cdot\bigl(Z^{2}(\phi)-\hbox{$\nabla$\kern-3.00003pt\lower 4.30554pt\hbox{$Z$}\kern-1.00006pt{$Z$}}(\phi)\bigr)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}}g(Z,Z)\cdot Z^{2}(\phi)

\displaystyle\kappa(\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}}g(Z,Z)\cdot Z(\phi)\cdot Z(\psi),

where
\mathcal{B}_{\mathfrak{g}}
is any orthonormal basis for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

The restriction of the semi-Riemannian metric
g
on
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
to its maximal compact subgroup
\text{\bf U}(n)
is its standard Riemannian metric. For this we have the following result, see Lemma 5.1 of
[
10
]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
z_{j\alpha}:\text{\bf U}(n)\to\mathbb{C}
be the complex-valued matrix elements of the standard representation of the unitary group
\text{\bf U}(n)
. Then the tension field
\tau
and the conformality operator
\kappa
on
\text{\bf U}(n)
satisfy the following relations

\displaystyle\tau(z_{j\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,n\cdot z_{j\alpha},

\displaystyle\kappa(z_{j\alpha},z_{k\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,z_{k\alpha}\cdot z_{j\beta}.

With this at hand we yield the following statement.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
z_{j\alpha}:\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{C}
be the complex-valued matrix elements of the standard representation of the general linear group
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. Then the tension field
\tau
and the conformality operator
\kappa
on
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
fulfill the following relations

\displaystyle\tau(z_{j\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\,n\cdot z_{j\alpha},

\displaystyle\kappa(z_{j\alpha},z_{k\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,2\cdot z_{k\alpha}\cdot z_{j\beta}.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Lemma
5.1
and how the semi-Riemannian metric is defined on the complex Lie algebra

\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})=\mathfrak{u}(n)\oplus i\cdot\mathfrak{u}(n).

∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
v
be a non-zero element of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Then the complex
n
-dimensional vector space

\mathcal{E}_{v}=\{\phi_{a}:\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\to\mathbb{C}\,|\,\phi_{a}(z)=\trace(v^{t}az^{t}),\ a\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\}

is an eigenfamily on
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
such that for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{E}_{v}
we have

\tau(\phi)=-\,2\,n\cdot\phi\ \ \text{and}\ \ \kappa(\phi,\psi)=-\,2\cdot\phi\cdot\psi.

Proof.

This result can be proven in exactly the same way as Theorem 5.2 presented in
[
10
]
.
∎

6.
The Semi-Riemannian Lie Group
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})

The real general linear group
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
of invertible
n\times n
matrices is given by

\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}\,|\,\det x\neq 0\}.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
of the complex general linear group
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
has a natural orthogonal decomposition

\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})\oplus i\cdot\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{R}}),

where
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
is the Lie algebra of
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
consisting of the real
n\times n
matrices.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
x_{j\alpha}:\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
be the real-valued matrix elements of the standard representation of the general linear group
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
. Then the tension field
\tau
and the conformality operator
\kappa
on
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
satisfy the following relations

\displaystyle\tau(x_{j\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,n\cdot x_{j\alpha},

\displaystyle\kappa(x_{j\alpha},x_{k\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,x_{k\alpha}\cdot x_{j\beta}.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Proposition
5.2
and how the semi-Riemannian metric is defined on the complex Lie algebra

\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})=\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})\oplus i\cdot\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{R}}).

∎

As an immediate consequence of Proposition
6.1
we have the following result.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
v
be a non-zero element of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. Then the complex
n
-dimensional vector space

\mathcal{E}_{v}=\{\phi_{a}:\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})\to{\mathbb{C}}\,|\,\phi_{a}(x)=\trace(v^{t}ax^{t}),\ a\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\}

is an eigenfamily on
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{R}})
such that for all
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{E}_{v}
we have

\tau(\phi)=-\,n\cdot\phi\ \ \text{and}\ \ \kappa(\phi,\psi)=-\,\phi\cdot\psi.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Proposition
6.1
and Theorem
5.3
.
∎

7.
The Semi-Riemannian Lie Group
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})

In this section we consider the quaternionic general linear group
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})
. Its standard complex representation
\pi:\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})\to\text{\bf GL}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})
is given by

\pi:(z+jw)\mapsto g=\begin{bmatrix}z_{11}&\dots&z_{1n}&w_{11}&\dots&w_{1n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
z_{n1}&\dots&z_{nn}&w_{n1}&\dots&w_{nn}\\
-\bar{w}_{11}&\dots&-\bar{w}_{1n}&\bar{z}_{11}&\dots&\bar{z}_{1n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-\bar{w}_{n1}&\dots&-\bar{w}_{nn}&\bar{z}_{n1}&\dots&\bar{z}_{nn}\\
\end{bmatrix}.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})
of
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})
clearly satisfies

\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})=\Big\{\begin{bmatrix}Z&W\\
-\bar{W}&\bar{Z}\end{bmatrix}\Big|\ Z,W\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\Big\}.

As a subgroup of
\text{\bf GL}_{2n}({\mathbb{C}})
the quaternionic general linear group
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})
inherits its natural semi-Riemannian metric
g
induced by the
semi-Euclidean inner product
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})\times\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})\to\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})
on
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})
given by

g(Z,W)=-\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{e}\trace(Z\cdot W).

For the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})
we have the orthogonal splitting

\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})=\mathfrak{gl}^{+}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})\oplus\mathfrak{gl}^{-}_{n}({\mathbb{H}}),

where

\mathfrak{gl}^{+}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})=\mathfrak{sp}(n)=\Big\{\begin{bmatrix}Z&W\\
-\bar{W}&\bar{Z}\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2n\times 2n}\,\Big|\,Z+\bar{Z}^{t}=0,W-W^{t}=0\Big\}.

By
\mathcal{B}^{+}
we denote the following orthonormal basis for the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sp}(n)
of the quaternionic unitary group
\text{\bf Sp}(n)
which is the maximal compact subgroup of
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})
. This satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&iX_{rs}\\
iX_{rs}&0\end{bmatrix},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&X_{rs}\\
-X_{rs}&0\end{bmatrix},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}iX_{rs}&0\\
0&-iX_{rs}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}Y_{rs}&0\\
0&Y_{rs}\end{bmatrix},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&D_{t}\\
-D_{t}&0\end{bmatrix},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}iD_{t}&0\\
0&-iD_{t}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&iD_{t}\\
iD_{t}&0\end{bmatrix}\Big|\,1\leq r<s\leq n,\ 1\leq t\leq n\Big\}\,.

For the orthogonal complement
\mathfrak{gl}^{-}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})
of
\mathfrak{sp}(n)
in
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})
we have the orthonormal basis

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}^{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&Y_{rs}\\
-Y_{rs}&0\end{bmatrix},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}0&iY_{rs}\\
iY_{rs}&0\end{bmatrix},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}iY_{rs}&0\\
0&-iY_{rs}\end{bmatrix},

\displaystyle\qquad\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}X_{rs}&0\\
0&X_{rs}\end{bmatrix},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}D_{t}&0\\
0&D_{t}\end{bmatrix}\Big|\,1\leq r,s\leq n,\ 1\leq t\leq n\Big\}\,.

Then
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}^{+}\cup\mathcal{B}^{-}
is an orthonormal basis for
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})
such that
g(Z,Z)=1
if
Z\in\mathcal{B}^{+}
and
g(Z,Z)=-1
if
Z\in\mathcal{B}^{-}.

With this at our disposal we can now prove the following statement.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
z_{j\alpha},w_{j\alpha}:\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})\to{\mathbb{C}}
be the complex-valued matrix elements of the standard representation of the quaternionic general linear group
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})
. Then the tension field
\tau
and the conformality operator
\kappa
on
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})
satisfy the following relations

\tau(z_{j\alpha})=-\,2n\cdot z_{j\alpha},\ \ \tau(w_{j\alpha})=-\,2n\cdot w_{j\alpha},

\kappa(z_{j\alpha},z_{k\beta})=-\,z_{k\alpha}\cdot z_{j\beta},\ \ \kappa(w_{j\alpha},w_{k\beta})=-\,w_{k\alpha}\cdot w_{j\beta},

\kappa(z_{j\alpha},w_{k\beta})=-\,z_{k\alpha}\cdot w_{j\beta}.

Proof.

For the tension field
\tau
on
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})
we have

\displaystyle\tau(z_{j\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{B}}g(Z,Z)\cdot Z^{2}(z_{j\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{B}^{+}}Z^{2}(z_{j\alpha})-\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{B}^{-}}Z^{2}(z_{j\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,e_{j}z\Big\{3\sum_{r<s}\begin{bmatrix}-X_{rs}^{2}&0\\
0&-X_{rs}^{2}\end{bmatrix}+\sum_{r<s}\begin{bmatrix}Y_{rs}^{2}&0\\
0&Y_{rs}^{2}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+3\sum_{t=1}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}-D_{t}^{2}&0\\
0&-D_{t}^{2}\end{bmatrix}\Big\}e_{\alpha}^{t}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\,e_{j}z\Big\{\sum_{r<s}\begin{bmatrix}X_{rs}^{2}&0\\
0&X_{rs}^{2}\end{bmatrix}+3\sum_{r<s}\begin{bmatrix}-Y_{rs}^{2}&0\\
0&-Y_{rs}^{2}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\sum_{t=1}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}D_{t}^{2}&0\\
0&D_{t}^{2}\end{bmatrix}\Big\}e_{\alpha}^{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\,e_{j}z\Big\{-4\sum_{r<s}\begin{bmatrix}X_{rs}^{2}&0\\
0&X_{rs}^{2}\end{bmatrix}+4\sum_{r<s}\begin{bmatrix}Y_{rs}^{2}&0\\
0&Y_{rs}^{2}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-4\sum_{t=1}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}D_{t}^{2}&0\\
0&D_{t}^{2}\end{bmatrix}\Big\}e_{\alpha}^{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\,e_{j}z\begin{bmatrix}n\cdot I_{n}&0\\
0&n\cdot I_{n}\end{bmatrix}e_{\alpha}^{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2n\cdot z_{j\alpha}.

For the conformal operation
\kappa
on
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{H}})
we similarly we yield

\displaystyle\kappa(z_{j\alpha},z_{k\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{B}}g(Z,Z)\cdot Z(z_{j\alpha})\cdot Z(z_{k\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{B}^{+}}Z(z_{j\alpha})\cdot Z(z_{k\beta})-\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{B}^{-}}Z(z_{j\alpha})\cdot Z(z_{k\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{j}z\Big\{\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{B}^{+}}Z\begin{bmatrix}E_{\alpha\beta}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}Z^{t}-\sum_{Z\in\mathcal{B}^{+}}Z\begin{bmatrix}E_{\alpha\beta}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}Z^{t}\Big\}z^{t}e_{k}^{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e_{j}z\Big\{-\sum_{r<s}\begin{bmatrix}X_{rs}E_{\alpha\beta}X_{rs}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}+\sum_{r<s}\begin{bmatrix}Y_{rs}E_{\alpha\beta}Y_{rs}^{t}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\sum_{t=1}^{n}\begin{bmatrix}D_{t}E_{\alpha\beta}D_{t}&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\Big\}z^{t}e_{k}^{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-e_{j}z(E_{\beta\alpha})z^{t}e_{k}^{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-z_{k\alpha}\cdot z_{j\beta}.

The other identities can be proven in exactly the same way.
∎

The Semi-Riemannian Lie group
\text{\bf SL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})

In this section we construct eigenfamilies on the semisimple non-compact complex special linear group
\text{\bf SL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})=\{z\in\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\,|\,\det z=1\}

equipped with its semi-Riemannian metric inherited from
\text{\bf GL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
. For the Lie algebra

\mathfrak{sl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})=\{Z\in\mathfrak{gl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})\,|\,\trace Z=0\}

we have the orthogonal decomposition
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})=\mathfrak{su}(n)\oplus i\cdot\mathfrak{su}(n)
. Here

\mathfrak{su}(n)=\{Z\in\mathfrak{u}(n)\,|\,\trace Z=0\}

is the Lie algebra of the special unitary group

\text{\bf SU}(n)=\{z\in\text{\bf U}(n)\,|\,\det z=1\}

which is the maximal compact subgroup of
\text{\bf SL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
z_{j\alpha}:\text{\bf SU}(n)\to\mathbb{C}
be the complex-valued matrix elements of the standard representation of the special unitary group
\text{\bf SU}(n)
. Then the tension field
\tau
and the conformality operator
\kappa
on
\text{\bf SU}(n)
satisfy the following relations

\displaystyle\tau(z_{j\alpha})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{(n^{2}-1)}{n}\cdot z_{j\alpha},

\displaystyle\kappa(z_{j\alpha},z_{k\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,(z_{k\alpha}\cdot z_{j\beta}-\frac{1}{n}\cdot z_{j\alpha}\cdot z_{k\beta}).

Proof.

For the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{u}(n)
of the unitary group
\text{\bf U}(n)
we have the orthogonal splitting

\mathfrak{u}(n)=\mathfrak{su}(n)\oplus\mathfrak{l},

where
\mathfrak{l}
is the real line generated by the unit vector
E_{n}=i\,I_{n}/\sqrt{n}
.
Hence the tension field
\hat{\tau}
on the unitary group
\text{\bf U}(n)
satisfies

\displaystyle\hat{\tau}(\phi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau(\phi)+E_{n}^{2}(\phi),

so we have

\tau(z_{j\alpha})=\hat{\tau}(z_{j\alpha})-E_{n}^{2}(z_{j\alpha})=-(n\cdot z_{j\alpha}-\frac{1}{n}\cdot z_{j\alpha})=-\,\frac{(n^{2}-1)}{n}\cdot z_{j\alpha}.

For the conformality operator
\hat{\kappa}
on
\text{\bf U}(n)
we similarily yield

\displaystyle\hat{\kappa}(\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kappa(\phi,\psi)+E_{n}(\phi)\cdot E_{n}(\psi).

Hence

\displaystyle\kappa(z_{j\alpha},z_{k\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\kappa}(z_{j\alpha},z_{k\beta})-E_{n}(z_{j\alpha})\cdot E_{n}(z_{k\beta})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,(z_{k\alpha}\cdot z_{j\beta}-\frac{1}{n}\cdot z_{j\alpha}\cdot z_{k\beta}).

∎

For the special linear group
\text{\bf SL}_{n}({\mathbb{C}})
we have the following statement.

Proposition 8.2
.

Let

on

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of Lemma
8.1
and how the semi-Riemannian metric is defined on the complex Lie algebra

\displaystyle\sum_{j,\alpha,k,\beta=1}^{n}a_{j\alpha}b_{k\beta}\,\kappa(z_{j\alpha},z_{k\beta})+\frac{2\,(n-1)}{n}\sum_{j,\alpha,k,\beta=1}^{n}a_{j\alpha}b_{k\beta}\,z_{j\alpha}z_{k\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,2\sum_{j,\alpha,k,\beta=1}^{n}a_{j\alpha}b_{k\beta}\,z_{j\beta}z_{k\alpha}+\frac{2}{n}\sum_{j,\alpha,k,\beta=1}^{n}a_{j\alpha}b_{k\beta}\,z_{j\alpha}z_{k\beta}

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j,\alpha,k,\beta=1}^{n}\left(a_{j\alpha}b_{k\beta}\,z_{j\alpha}z_{k\beta}-a_{j\alpha}b_{k\beta}\,z_{j\beta}z_{k\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j,\alpha,k,\beta=1}^{n}(a_{j\alpha}b_{k\beta}-a_{k\alpha}b_{j\beta})\,z_{j\alpha}z_{k\beta}.

v

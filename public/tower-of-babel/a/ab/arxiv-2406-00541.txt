Abstract
. The celebrated Bishop theorem states that an operator is subnormal if and only if it is the strong limit of a net (or a sequence) of normal operators. By the Agler-Stankus theorem, 2 2 -isometries behave similarly to subnormal operator in the sense that the role of subnormal operators is played by 2 2 -isometries, while the role of normal operators is played by Brownian unitaries. In this paper we give Bishop-like theorems for 2 2 -isometries. Two methods are involved, the first of which goes back to Bishop’s original idea and the second refers to Conway and Hadwin’s result of general nature. We also investigate the strong and ∗ * -strong closedness of the class of Brownian unitaries.

1.
Introduction

We begin by providing the concepts and facts necessary
to understand the content of the article. In what
follows,
\mathbb{C}
stands for the set of complex numbers.
Given two (complex) Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{K},
we
denote by
\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})
the Banach space of all
bounded linear operators from
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{K}
. The
kernel and the range of an operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{K})
are denoted by
\mathcal{N}(T)
and
{\mathcal{R}}(T)
,
respectively. We regard
\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}):=\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H})
as
a
C^{*}
-algebra. The identity operator on
\mathcal{H}
is
denoted by
I
. An operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is said
to be
normal
if
T^{*}T=TT^{*}
, and
subnormal

if there exists a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
and a normal
operator
N\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K})
such that
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
(isometric embedding) and
N
is an extension of

T
, that is,
Th=Nh
for all
h\in\mathcal{H}
. If
\mathcal{H}
is
infinite-dimensional, then the class of normal
operators on
\mathcal{H}
is strictly contained in the class
of subnormal operators on
\mathcal{H}
(for more information,
see
[
6
]
). The celebrated Bishop theorem gives
the following topological characterization of
subnormal operators, where the “strong closure”
refers to the “strong operator topology” on

\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
(see Section
2
).

Theorem 1.1

(
[
3
, Theorem 3.3]
; see also

[
6
, Theorem II.1.17]
)
.

The strong closure of
the set of all normal operators on a Hilbert space

\mathcal{H}
is equal to the set of all subnormal operators
on
\mathcal{H}
.

In other words, an operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is
subnormal if and only if there exists a net of normal
operators on
\mathcal{H}
strongly convergent to
T
.

There is another well-known class of operators called

2
-isometries introduced by Agler in
[
1
]
and
intensively studied by Agler and Stankus in the
trilogy
[
2
]
. It resembles the class of
subnormal operators in the sense that each

2
-isometry has an extension to a Brownian unitary.
In other words,
2
-isometries play the role of
subnormal operators, while Brownian unitaries play the
role of normal operators. Following
[
1
]
, we
say that an operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a
2
-isometry
if

\displaystyle I-2T^{*}T+T^{*2}T^{2}=0.

Before defining Brownian unitary, recall that the
covariance
of an operator
A\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is defined
by
\mathrm{cov}(A)=\sqrt{\|A^{*}A-I\|}
. An operator
T
is
said to be a
Brownian unitary of covariance

\sigma\in(0,\infty)
if
T
has a
matrix representation
T=\big[\begin{smallmatrix}V&\sigma E\\
0&U\end{smallmatrix}\big]
relative to some
nontrivial orthogonal decomposition
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2}
, where
V\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1})
and

E\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}_{2},\mathcal{H}_{1})
are isometries such that

\mathcal{H}_{1}={\mathcal{R}}(V)\oplus{\mathcal{R}}(E)
, and
U\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}_{2})
is
unitary (see
[
2
, Proposition 5.12]
).
Clearly, we have

1\leqslant\dim\mathcal{H}_{2}\leqslant\dim\mathcal{H}_{1}=\dim\mathcal{H}
and

\dim\mathcal{H}\geqslant\aleph_{0}.

By a
Brownian unitary of covariance

\sigma=0
,
we mean any unitary operator
T
. In both cases,

\mathrm{cov}(T)=\sigma
. Apparently, the class of Brownian
unitaries is strictly contained in the class of

2
-isometries. That the inclusion is strict can be
demonstrated with the help of a
2
-isometric
unilateral weighted shift
T
with the weight sequence

\{\sigma_{n}(\lambda)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, where

\lambda\in(1,\infty)
and

\displaystyle\sigma_{n}(\lambda)=\sqrt{\frac{1+(n+1)(\lambda^{2}-1)}{1+n(\lambda^{2}-1)}},\quad n\geqslant 0,

(see
[
15
, Lemma 6.1]
), by showing that
T

does not satisfy condition (iii) of
Theorem
3.1
.

The aforementioned fundamental relationship between

2
-isometries and Brownian unitaries is outlined
below (cf. Lemma
2.4
).

Theorem 1.2

(
[
2
, Proposition 5.79 & Theorem 5.80]
)
.

An operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a
2
-isometry if and
only if
T
extends to a Brownian unitary
R\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K})
, i.e.,
\mathcal{H}\subseteq\mathcal{K}
and
Th=Rh
for
all
h\in\mathcal{H}
. Moreover, for
\sigma\in[0,\infty)
,
any
2
-isometry of covariance less than or equal to

\sigma
extends to a Brownian unitary of covariance

\sigma
.

The main goal of this paper is to prove a Bishop-like
theorem (or theorems) for
2
-isometries with Brownian
unitaries playing the role of normal operators. The
most general result of this kind, without dimensional
constraints, is as follows:

Theorem 1.3
.

If
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
, then the following conditions are
equivalent:

(i)

T
is a
2
-isometry,

(ii)

T
is the
strong limit of a net
\{T_{\tau}\}_{\tau\in\varSigma}\subseteq\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
of Brownian unitaries
such that
\mathrm{cov}(T_{\tau})=\mathrm{cov}(T)
for every

\tau\in\varSigma
,

(iii)

T
is the
strong limit of a net
\{T_{\tau}\}_{\tau\in\varSigma}\subseteq\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
of Brownian unitaries
such that
\sup_{\tau\in\varSigma}\mathrm{cov}(T_{\tau})<\infty
.

Moreover, for every
\sigma\in[0,\infty)
, the class
of
2
-isometries on
\mathcal{H}
of covariance less than or
equal to
\sigma
is equal to the strong closure of
the class of Brownian unitaries on
\mathcal{H}
of covariance
less than or equal to
\sigma
.

For separable Hilbert spaces, the above topological
characterization of
2
-isometries takes the
following form:

Theorem 1.4
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{H}
is a separable Hilbert space and

T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Then the following conditions are
equivalent:

(i)

T
is a
2
-isometry,

(ii)

T
is the
strong limit of a sequence
\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
of Brownian unitaries such that

\mathrm{cov}(T_{n})=\mathrm{cov}(T)
for every integer
n\geqslant 1
,

(iii)

T
is the
strong limit of a sequence
\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
of Brownian unitaries.

Using the Conway-Hadwin theorem (see

[
7
, Theorem]
), we can obtain Bishop-like
theorems not only for strong topology, but also for
weak and
∗
-strong topology.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert
space,
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
\sigma\in[0,\infty)
.
Then

(i)

T
is the
strong limit of a sequence
\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
of Brownian unitaries of
covariance
\sigma
if and only if
T
is a

2
-isometry with
\mathrm{cov}(T)\leqslant\sigma
,

(ii)

T
is the
weak limit of a sequence
\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
of Brownian unitaries of
covariance
\sigma
if and only if
T
is the
compression of a Brownian unitary of covariance

\sigma
,

(iii)

T
is the
∗
-strong limit of a
sequence
\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})

of Brownian unitaries of covariance
\sigma
if and
only if
T
is either a unitary operator or a Brownian
unitary of covariance
\sigma
.

Note that equivalence (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) of
Theorem
1.4
follows from the statement (i)
of Theorem
1.5
applied to
\sigma=\mathrm{cov}(T)

and the fact that
2
-isometries on finite-dimensional
Hilbert spaces are unitary.

Regarding the Conway-Hadwin theorem, note that one of
its two main assumptions is that the considered
abstract class of operators is closed under countable
orthogonal sums. In our case, this is the class of
Brownian unitaries. Surprisingly, it is not closed
under this operation. However, the class of Brownian
unitaries of a fixed covariance is closed under
countable orthogonal sums. This narrower class is also
closed under approximate equivalence, which is the
second main assumption of the Conway-Hadwin theorem
(see Proposition
3.8
).

Proposition 1.6
.

The orthogonal sum
T=\bigoplus_{\omega\in\varOmega}T_{\omega}
of operators
T_{\omega}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\omega})
is a Brownian unitary if and only
if for every
\omega\in\varOmega
, the operator

T_{\omega}
is a Brownian unitary and

\mathrm{cov}(T_{\omega})
equals either
0
or
\mathrm{cov}(T)
.

It turns out that the class of Brownian unitaries
behaves quite well with respect to the
∗
-strong
topology.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a
sequence of Brownian unitaries which converges

∗
-strongly to
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
. Then
T
is either
an isometry or a Brownian unitary. Moreover, if

\mathrm{cov}(T_{n})=\mathrm{cov}(T_{1})
for every
n\geqslant 1
, then
T
is
either a unitary operator or a Brownian unitary with

\mathrm{cov}(T)=\mathrm{cov}(T_{1})
.

Note that if
\mathcal{H}
is separable, then the “only if”
part of statement (iii) of Theorem
1.5
is
equivalent to the “moreover” part of
Theorem
1.7
.

It is worth pointing out that Theorem
1.7
, as
well as Corollary
3.3
, are no longer valid if
the
∗
-strong topology is replaced by the strong
topology (see Remark
3.4
). It may be the case
that the
∗
-strong limit of a sequence of Brownian
unitaries, each with positive covariance, is a
non-unitary isometry, meaning that the class of
Brownian unitaries is not
∗
-strongly closed. In
fact, it is not closed even in the operator norm
topology (see Example
3.5
).

The question of the strong and weak closedness of the
class of
2
-isometries has the following partial
answer.

Theorem 1.8
.

The class of
2
-isometries on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}

is sequentially strongly closed, and if
\mathcal{H}
is
infinite-dimensional, it is not sequentially weakly
closed.

Taking into account the above, the following problem
arises.

Problem 1.9
.

Is the class of
2
-isometries on an
infinite-dimensional Hilbert space strongly
closed?

Let us recall that the strong closure of the set of
unitary (resp. normal) operators is equal to the set
of isometric (resp. subnormal) operators (see

[
12
, Problem 225]
and

[
3
, Theorem 3.3]
, respectively).
Consequently, the sets of isometric and subnormal
operators are strongly closed. It turns out that the
set of hyponormal operators is strongly closed as well
(see
[
12
, Problems 226]
). The question of weak
closedness of these sets of operators is discussed in
Remark
4.4
.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2

provides some basic properties of the covariance map,
including its strong lower semicontinuity (see
Lemma
2.3
). Sections
3
and
4

establish the proofs of the results stated in
Introduction. Section
5
gives two examples
showing that unitary operators can be
∗
-strong
limits of sequences of Brownian unitaries of a fixed
positive covariance.

In this paper, we focus on the study of

2
-isometries. It is natural to ask what can be done
in the context of
m
-isometries where
m\geqslant 3
. It
turns out that the situation is much more complex even
for
3
-isometries. The techniques used here for

2
-isometries are no longer applicable for
m\geqslant 3
.
The main reason for this is the lack of algebraic
characterizations of
m
-Brownian unitaries playing a
role somewhat similar to that played by Brownian
unitaries in the case of
2
-isometries (see

[
8
,
19
]
).

2.
The covariance map

We begin by recalling the three basic operator
topologies that are explored in this paper. Denote by

\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
the
C^{*}
-algebra of all bounded linear
operators on a complex Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. The
weak
, the
strong
and the
∗
-strong

topologies are defined as the locally convex
topologies on
\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
induced by the following
families of seminorms

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})\ni T

\displaystyle\longmapsto|\langle Tf,g\rangle|,\quad f,g\in\mathcal{H},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})\ni T

\displaystyle\longmapsto\|Tf\|,\quad f\in\mathcal{H},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})\ni T

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sqrt{\|Tf\|^{2}+\|T^{*}f\|^{2}},\quad f\in\mathcal{H}.

For more information on the relationship between the
different operator topologies, we refer the reader to

[
21
, Chapter II]
. In this paper, we will
repeatedly use the following fact, called the
sequential joint strong continuity of multiplication
of operators (see
[
18
, Lemma 3]
; see also

[
7
, Lemma 1]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
and

\{R_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
are
sequences converging strongly to
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
and

R\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
, respectively, then

\{T_{n}R_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges strongly to

TR
.

Recall that the
covariance
of an operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
, denoted by
\mathrm{cov}(T)
, is defined by

\mathrm{cov}(T)=\sqrt{\|\triangle_{T}\|}
, where

\triangle_{T}=T^{*}T-I
. It is easy to see that

\displaystyle\|T\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{1+\mathrm{cov}(T)^{2}},

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{cov}(T)

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{1+\|T\|^{2}}.

(2.2)

The proof of the following observation is left to the
reader.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
T=\bigoplus_{\omega\in\varOmega}T_{\omega}
is
the orthogonal sum of operators
T_{\omega}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}_{\omega})
, then
\mathrm{cov}(T)=\sup_{\omega\in\varOmega}\mathrm{cov}(T_{\omega})
.

As shown below, the covariance map
\mathrm{cov}(\cdot)
is
strongly lower semicontinuous. However, it is not
strongly continuous; worse, it is not
∗
-strongly
continuous (see Examples
5.1
and
5.2
).

Lemma 2.3
.

The map
\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})\ni T\mapsto\mathrm{cov}(T)\in[0,\infty)

is strongly lower semicontinuous and norm continuous.
Moreover, if
\{T_{\tau}\}_{\tau\in\varSigma}\subseteq\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a net converging strongly to

T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
, then

\displaystyle\mathrm{cov}(T)\leqslant\liminf_{\tau\in\varSigma}\mathrm{cov}(T_{\tau}).

Proof.

Since the norm continuity of
\mathrm{cov}(\cdot)
is obvious,
we can focus on proving the strong lower
semicontinuity. Denote by
\mathbb{S}
the unit sphere
of
\mathcal{H}
centered at
0
. For a vector
h\in\mathbb{S}
, define the function
\varphi_{h}\colon\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})\to[0,\infty)
by

\displaystyle\varphi_{h}(T)=\big|\|Th\|^{2}-\|h\|^{2}\big|,\quad T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}).

Clearly, each
\varphi_{h}
being strongly continuous is
strongly lower semicontinuous. This implies that

\varphi=\sup_{h\in\mathbb{S}}\varphi_{h}
is
strongly lower semicontinuous. However,

\displaystyle\varphi(T)=\sup_{h\in\mathbb{S}}|\langle\triangle_{T}h,h\rangle|=r_{\mathrm{n}}(\triangle_{T}),\quad T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}),

where
r_{\mathrm{n}}(S)
stands for the numerical
radius of an operator
S
. Since the numerical radius
and the norm of a selfadjoint operator coincide (see

[
10
, Theorem 1.4-2]
), we conclude that

\varphi(T)=\mathrm{cov}(T)
. Hence, the covariance map is
strongly lower semicontinuous.

The “moreover” part is a direct consequence of

[
17
, Proposition 1.5.11]
.
∎

The covariance map
\mathrm{cov}(\cdot)
is monotonically
increasing with respect to the inclusion of operators.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
\mathcal{H}
is a closed space invariant for

R\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K})
, then

\displaystyle\mathrm{cov}(R|_{\mathcal{H}})\leqslant\mathrm{cov}(R).

Proof.

Set
T=R|_{\mathcal{H}}
. Noting that for every
h\in\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle|\langle\triangle_{T}h,h\rangle|=\big|\|Th\|^{2}-\|h\|^{2}\big|=\big|\|Rh\|^{2}-\|h\|^{2}\big|=\big|\langle\triangle_{R}h,h\rangle\big|\leqslant\mathrm{cov}(R)^{2}\|h\|^{2},

we deduce that (cf. the proof of Lemma
2.3
)

\displaystyle\mathrm{cov}(T)^{2}=\|\triangle_{T}\|=r_{\mathrm{n}}(\triangle_{T})\leqslant\mathrm{cov}(R)^{2},

which completes the proof.
∎

How the covariance map behaves when restricting

2
-isometries to invariant or reducing subspaces can
be found in
[
2
, Lemma 5.32, Corollary 5.57 and
Lemma 5.90]
.

3.
Proofs of Proposition
1.6
and
Theorems
1.7
and
1.8

Brownian
unitaries can be characterized completely
algebraically as follows.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
2
, Theorem 5.20]
)
.

Let
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
and

\sigma=\mathrm{cov}(T)
. Suppose that
\sigma>0
. Then
T

is a Brownian unitary if and only if the following
hold:

(i)

T^{*2}T^{2}-2T^{*}T+I=0
,

(ii)

\triangle_{T}(TT^{*}-I)\triangle_{T}=0
,

(iii)

\sigma^{-2}\triangle_{T}
is an orthogonal projection,

(iv)

there exists an orthogonal projection
Q\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
such that

\displaystyle(\sigma^{2}-\triangle_{T})(TT^{*}-I)(\sigma^{2}-\triangle_{T})=\sigma^{4}(\sigma^{2}-1)Q.

Below we will show that the class of all

2
-isometries is sequentially strongly closed, but
not sequentially weakly closed if the underlying
Hilbert space is infinite-dimensional. For this we
need the following definition. An operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is said to be
weakly
,
strongly

or
uniformly stable
if the power sequence

\{T^{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges to
0
weakly,
strongly or in norm, respectively. We refer the reader
to
[
9
,
16
]
for more information on this
topic.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

That
the class of
2
-isometries on
\mathcal{H}
is sequentially
strongly closed follows easily from the definition and
Lemma
2.1
. It remains to prove that this class
is not sequentially weakly closed if
\dim\mathcal{H}\geqslant\aleph_{0}
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be any infinite-dimensional
Hilbert space. Decompose it as
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2}
,
where
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
are Hilbert spaces of the
same dimension. Fix a real number
q\in(0,1)
. Let

V\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1})
be an isometry with
\dim\mathcal{H}_{1}\ominus{\mathcal{R}}(V)=\dim\mathcal{H}_{1}
whose unitary part is
weakly stable (the case in which the unitary part is
absent is not excluded), let
E_{0}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}_{2},\mathcal{H}_{1})
be an isometry with range
orthogonal to the range of
V
(this is always
possible due to the assumption on dimensions of

\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
), and let
X_{0}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}_{2})
be
an isometry. Set
T=\big[\begin{smallmatrix}V&E\\
0&X\end{smallmatrix}\big]\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2})
with
E=\sqrt{1-q}E_{0}
and
X=\sqrt{q}X_{0}
.
Clearly,
X
commutes with
E^{*}E
and
X
is
quasinormal, that is,
X
commutes with
X^{*}X
. Since

E^{*}E+X^{*}X-I=0
, we infer from

[
4
, Theorem 7.1(iii)]
that
T
is a

2
-isometry. Because powers of a
2
-isometry are

2
-isometries (see
[
14
, Theorem 2.3]
), we see
that
\{T^{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of

2
-isometries. We claim that
\{T^{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

converges weakly to the zero operator. Indeed, it
follows from
[
5
, Lemma 3.3]
that
T^{n}=\big[\begin{smallmatrix}V^{n}&E_{n}\\
0&X^{n}\end{smallmatrix}\big]
for all integers
n\geqslant 1
, where

\displaystyle E_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}V^{j}EX^{n-1-j},\quad n\geqslant 1,

(3.1)

and

\displaystyle E_{n}^{*}E_{n}=E^{*}E\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}X^{*j}X^{j}\bigg),\quad n\geqslant 1.

(3.2)

This implies that

\displaystyle\|E_{n}\|^{2}=\|E_{n}^{*}E_{n}\|=(1-q)\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}q^{j}=1-q^{n},\quad n\geqslant 1.

Since
\{q^{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges to
0
, we
conclude that
\sup_{n\geqslant 1}\|E_{n}\|<\infty
.
Because the unitary part of
V
is weakly stable and,
by
\|X\|<1
,
X
is uniformly stable, we deduce from

[
5
, Corollary 2.2]
that the sequence

\{T^{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of
2
-isometries converges
weakly to the zero operator which is obviously not a

2
-isometry. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Regarding the proof of the second conclusion of
Theorem
1.8
, it is worth mentioning that a
Brownian unitary of positive covariance is never
weakly stable. Indeed, if
T=\big[\begin{smallmatrix}V&\sigma E_{0}\\
0&U\end{smallmatrix}\big]\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2})

is a Brownian unitary of covariance
\sigma\in(0,\infty)
, then
T^{n}=\big[\begin{smallmatrix}V^{n}&E_{n}\\
0&U^{n}\end{smallmatrix}\big]
for all integers
n\geqslant 1
, where
E_{n}
is as in (
3.1
) with
E=\sigma E_{0}
and
X=U
. Hence, by (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\|E_{n}\|=\sigma\sqrt{n},\quad n\geqslant 1,

which means that the sequence
\{E_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

is not norm bounded. Therefore, by

[
5
, Corollary 2.2]
,
T
is not weakly
stable. However, if
T
is a Brownian unitary of
covariance
\sigma=0
on an infinite-dimensional
Hilbert space, then it may happen that
T
is weakly
stable (see

, we can
define the “coordinate embedding”
V\colon\mathcal{H}\ni h\mapsto(h,0)\in\hat{\mathcal{K}}
of
\mathcal{H}
into

\hat{\mathcal{K}}:=\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{M}
. Set
\hat{S}:=\hat{V}S\hat{V}^{*}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\hat{\mathcal{H}})
with

\hat{V}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\hat{\mathcal{H}}:={\mathcal{R}}(V)
given by

\hat{V}h=Vh
for
h\in\mathcal{H}
, and
\hat{R}:=\hat{U}\tilde{R}\hat{U}^{*}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\hat{\mathcal{K}})
with
\hat{U}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\tilde{\mathcal{K}},\hat{\mathcal{K}})
given by
\hat{U}:=U\oplus W
. Using the identity
P_{\tilde{\mathcal{H}}}=\hat{U}^{*}P_{\hat{\mathcal{H}}}\hat{U}
, we conclude that

\hat{S}=P_{\hat{\mathcal{H}}}\hat{R}|_{\hat{\mathcal{H}}}
, and

\tilde{\mathcal{H}}
is invariant (resp., reducing) for

\tilde{R}
if and only if
\hat{\mathcal{H}}
is invariant
(resp., reducing) for
\hat{R}
. Clearly,
\tilde{R}

is of class
\mathscr{E}
if and only if
\hat{R}
is
of class
\mathscr{E}
(provided
\mathscr{E}
is as
in Theorem
4.2
).
\diamondsuit

Remark 4.4
.

Note that if
\mathcal{H}
is finite-dimensional, then
assertion (ii) of Theorem
4.2
is not true in
general. For example, it is not true for the classes

\mathscr{U}
and
\mathscr{N}
of unitary and normal
operators. Indeed, if it were true for

\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{U}
, then since
\mathscr{U}
is
closed in operator norm topology, the compression

P_{\mathcal{H}}U|_{\mathcal{H}}
of any unitary operator
U\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K})
to any finite-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{H}
of

\mathcal{K}
would be unitary, so any unitary operator would
be reduced by any finite-dimensional subspace, which
is not the case if
\dim\mathcal{H}\geqslant 2
. In turn, if it
were true for
\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{N}
, then since

\mathscr{N}
is closed in operator norm topology, the
compression of any normal operator
N\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{K})
to
any finite-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{H}
of
\mathcal{K}
would
be normal. However, this is not the case. Indeed, the
operator
B=[\begin{smallmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}]
is a non-normal compression of the unitary
operator
A=\Big[\begin{smallmatrix}0&0&1\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{smallmatrix}\Big]
to

\mathbb{C}^{2}\oplus\{0\}
. This example also shows that
assertion (ii) of Theorem
4.2
is not true for

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{U}
if
2\leqslant\dim\mathcal{H}<\aleph_{0}
. We refer the reader to
[
13
]
, where
the question of when compressions of normal matrices
are normal is investigated.

It is worth pointing out that by the Halmos’s dilation
theorem (see
[
12
, Problem 222 and Corollary]
),
the set of compressions of unitary (resp., normal)
operators to a fixed Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is equal to
the set of contractions (resp., operators) on
\mathcal{H}
.
On the other hand, if
\dim\mathcal{H}\geqslant\aleph_{0}
, then
the weak closure of the set
\mathbb{U}_{\mathcal{H}}
(resp.,

\mathbb{N}_{\mathcal{H}}
) of unitary (resp., normal)
operators on
\mathcal{H}
is equal to the set of contractions
(resp., operators) on
\mathcal{H}
(see

[
12
, Problem 224]
). As a consequence, if
\dim\mathcal{H}\geqslant\aleph_{0}
, then the weak closure of

\mathbb{U}_{\mathcal{H}}
(resp.,
\mathbb{N}_{\mathcal{H}}
) is
equal to the set of compressions of unitary (resp.,
normal) operators to
\mathcal{H}
. However, if
2\leqslant\dim\mathcal{H}<\aleph_{0}
, this equality is false.

\diamondsuit

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

(i) By Proposition
3.8
, the class

\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}
of all Brownian unitaries of
covariance
\sigma
satisfies the assumptions of
Theorem
4.2
. Hence, by the statement (i) of
this theorem, the operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
is the
strong limit of a sequence
\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
of Brownian unitaries of
covariance
\sigma
if and only if
T
is the
restriction of a Brownian unitary of covariance

\sigma
to its invariant subspace
\mathcal{H}
. In turn, by
Lemma
2.4
and Theorem
1.2
, the latter holds
if and only if
T
is a
2
-isometry with
\mathrm{cov}(T)\leqslant\sigma
.

(ii) This is a direct consequence of the statement
(ii) of Theorem
4.2
applied to

\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}
.

(iii) Applying the statement (iii) of
Theorem
4.2
to the class

\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{E}_{\sigma}
, we see that
T
is
the
∗
-strong limit of a sequence

\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
of
Brownian unitaries of covariance
\sigma
if and only
if
T
is the restriction of a Brownian unitary of
covariance
\sigma
to its reducing subspace
\mathcal{H}
. By
Corollary
3.7
, the latter holds if and only
if
T
itself is a Brownian unitary of covariance
0

or
\sigma
. This completes the proof.
∎

The following two facts are direct consequences of
Theorem
1.5
.

Corollary 4.5

(cf. Remark
4.4
)
.

If
\mathcal{H}
is an infinite-dimensional separable
Hilbert space and
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
, then
T
is the
weak limit of a sequence
\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
of unitary operators if and only
if
\|T\|\leqslant 1
.

Corollary 4.6
.

For
\sigma\in[0,\infty)
, any unitary operator on an
infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}

is a
∗
-strong limit of a sequence of Brownian
unitaries of covariance
\sigma
.

5.
Examples

In this section we provide some explicit examples of
unitary operators that are
∗
-strong limits of
sequences of Brownian unitaries of constant positive
covariance. In particular, this shows that the
covariance function
T\mapsto\mathrm{cov}(T)
is not

∗
-strongly continuous.

Example 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert
space and let
\{e_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an
orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}
. Fix a real number
\sigma>0
and two sequences
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
and

\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of complex numbers of modulus

1
. Set
\mathcal{H}_{2,n}=\langle e_{n+1}\rangle
and

\mathcal{H}_{1,n}=\mathcal{H}_{2,n}^{\perp}
, where
\langle f\rangle=\mathbb{C}\cdot f
for
f\in\mathcal{H}
. Clearly

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1,n}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2,n}
and

\mathcal{H}_{1,n}=\bigoplus_{j\neq n+1}\langle e_{j}\rangle
.
Define the unitary operator
W_{n}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\langle e_{j}\rangle)
by

\displaystyle W_{n}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\xi_{j}e_{j}\Big)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}\xi_{j}e_{j},\quad(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

and the unilateral shift
\widetilde{V}_{n}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\bigoplus_{j=n+2}^{\infty}\langle e_{j}\rangle)

of multiplicity
1
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}_{n}\Big(\sum_{j=n+2}^{\infty}\xi_{j}e_{j}\Big)=\sum_{j=n+2}^{\infty}\xi_{j}e_{j+1},\quad\{\xi_{j+n+1}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{2}.

Set
V_{n}=W_{n}\oplus\widetilde{V}_{n}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1,n})
. Define the operators
E_{n}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}_{2,n},\mathcal{H}_{1,n})
and
U_{n}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}_{2,n})
by
E_{n}(\xi e_{n+1})=\xi e_{n+2}
and

U_{n}(\xi e_{n+1})=z_{n}\xi e_{n+1}
for
\xi\in\mathbb{C}
. Clearly,
V_{n}
and
E_{n}
are isometries such
that
\mathcal{H}_{1,n}={\mathcal{R}}(V_{n})\oplus{\mathcal{R}}(E_{n})
. Define the
Brownian unitary
T_{n}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
by
T_{n}=\big[\begin{smallmatrix}V_{n}&\sigma E_{n}\\
0&U_{n}\end{smallmatrix}\big]
relative to

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1,n}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2,n}
. The covariance of
each
T_{n}
is equal to
\sigma
. Finally, let
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
be the unitary operator defined by

\displaystyle Th=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}\langle h,e_{j}\rangle e_{j},\quad h\in\mathcal{H}.

We claim that the sequence
\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

converges
∗
-strongly to
T
. First, we show that

\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges strongly to
T
.
For, take a vector
h\in\mathcal{H}
and decompose it as
follows

\displaystyle h=h_{n}\oplus g_{n+1}\oplus\langle h,e_{n+1}\rangle e_{n+1},\quad n\geqslant 1,

where
h_{n}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\langle h,e_{j}\rangle e_{j}
and

g_{n}=h-h_{n}
. Clearly,
h_{n}\oplus g_{n+1}\in\mathcal{H}_{1,n}
and
\langle h,e_{n+1}\rangle e_{n+1}\in\mathcal{H}_{2,n}
.
Note that
T=W_{n}\oplus\widetilde{W}_{n}
, where

\widetilde{W}_{n}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\bigoplus_{j=n+1}^{\infty}\langle e_{j}\rangle)
is the unitary operator given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}_{n}\bigg(\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}\xi_{j}e_{j}\bigg)=\sum_{j=n+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{j}\xi_{j}e_{j},\quad\{\xi_{j+n}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}\in\ell^{2}.

Since
Th=W_{n}h_{n}\oplus\widetilde{W}_{n}g_{n}
, we get

\displaystyle\|T_{n}h-Th\|

\displaystyle=\big\|\widetilde{V}_{n}g_{n+1}\oplus\sigma\langle h,e_{n+1}\rangle e_{n+2}\oplus z_{n}\langle h,e_{n+1}\rangle e_{n+1}-\widetilde{W}_{n}g_{n}\big\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|g_{n+1}\|+(\sigma+1)|\langle h,e_{n+1}\rangle|+\|g_{n}\|,\quad n\geqslant 1.

However,
\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{n}=h
and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle h,e_{n}\rangle=0
, so
Th=\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{n}h
.

It remains to prove that
\{T_{n}^{*}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

converges strongly to
T^{*}
. First, observe that

T_{n}^{*}=\big[\begin{smallmatrix}V_{n}^{*}&0\\
\sigma E_{n}^{*}&U_{n}^{*}\end{smallmatrix}\big]
and

E_{n}^{*}(h_{n}\oplus g_{n+1})=\langle h,e_{n+2}\rangle e_{n+1}
.
Hence, we have

\displaystyle\|T_{n}^{*}h-T^{*}h\|

\displaystyle=\big\|\widetilde{V}_{n}^{*}g_{n+1}\oplus\sigma\langle h,e_{n+2}\rangle e_{n+1}+\bar{z}_{n}\langle h,e_{n+1}\rangle e_{n+1}-\widetilde{W}_{n}^{*}g_{n}\big\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|g_{n+1}\|+\sigma|\langle h,e_{n+2}\rangle|+|\langle h,e_{n+1}\rangle|+\|g_{n}\|,\quad n\geqslant 1,

which implies that
T^{*}h=\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{n}^{*}h
.

Summarizing, we have proved that the sequence

\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of Brownian unitaries of
covariance
\sigma
converges
∗
-strongly to the
unitary operator
T
.

\diamondsuit

The Brownian unitaries
T_{n}
appearing in
Example
5.1
resemble Brownian shifts (cf.
[
2
, Proposition 5.2]
). In particular,

\dim{\mathcal{H}_{2,n}}=1
for all
n
. However, if

\mathfrak{n}
is any cardinal number, then taking the
orthogonal sum of such operators with
\mathfrak{n}

summands and using Proposition
1.6
, we can
obtain a sequence of Brownian unitaries of constant
positive covariance, which
∗
-strongly converges to
a unitary operator and has the property that

\dim{\mathcal{H}_{2,n}}=\mathfrak{n}
for all
n
. The next
example is not an orthogonal sum as above, and

\dim{\mathcal{H}_{2,n}}=\aleph_{0}
for all
n
.

Example 5.2
.

In this example, given an isometry
V
on a Hilbert
space
\mathcal{K}
, we write
\widehat{V}
for the operator

V
regarded as an operator from
\mathcal{K}
onto
{\mathcal{R}}(V)
.
Clearly,
\widehat{V}
is a unitary isomorphism. Let

\sigma
be a fixed positive real number and

T_{0}=\big[\begin{smallmatrix}V&\sigma E\\
0&U\end{smallmatrix}\big]
be a Brownian unitary of
covariance
\sigma
relative to an orthogonal
decomposition
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2}
with
\dim\mathcal{H}_{1}=\dim\mathcal{H}_{2}=\aleph_{0}
. Assume that

\{\mathcal{M}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of closed
subspaces of
\mathcal{H}_{2}
reducing
U
and such that

\mathcal{H}_{2}=\bigoplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{n}
and
\dim\mathcal{M}_{n}=\aleph_{0}
for all integers
n\geqslant 1
. Since

\dim\mathcal{M}_{j}=\dim E(\mathcal{M}_{j})\oplus\mathcal{M}_{j}
, there
exists a unitary isomorphism
W_{j}
from
\mathcal{M}_{j}
onto

E(\mathcal{M}_{j})\oplus\mathcal{M}_{j}
. Then
W^{(n)}:=W_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus W_{n}
is a unitary isomorphism from

\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{j}
onto

\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}E(\mathcal{M}_{j})\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{j}
(use the fact that
E
is
isometric). For
n\geqslant 1
, we define
\mathcal{H}_{1,n}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{j}
and

\mathcal{H}_{2,n}=\bigoplus_{j=n+1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{j}
.
Clearly,
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1,n}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2,n}
and

\dim{\mathcal{H}_{1,n}}=\dim{\mathcal{H}_{2,n}}=\aleph_{0}
. Using
the fact that
{\mathcal{R}}(V)\perp{\mathcal{R}}(E)
, we can define the
isometry
V_{n}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1,n})
as follows

\displaystyle V_{n}(h_{1}\oplus\hat{h}_{n})=Vh_{1}\oplus W^{(n)}\hat{h}_{n},\quad h_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{1},\,\hat{h}_{n}\in\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{j}.

Set
E_{n}=E|_{\mathcal{H}_{2,n}}
and
U_{n}=U|_{\mathcal{H}_{2,n}}
.
Then
E_{n}
is an isometry from
\mathcal{H}_{2,n}
into

\mathcal{H}_{1,n}
and
U_{n}
is a unitary operator on

\mathcal{H}_{2,n}
. Since

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}(V_{n})={\mathcal{R}}(V)\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}E(\mathcal{M}_{j})\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{j},\quad n\geqslant 1,

(5.1)

and
{\mathcal{R}}(E_{n})=\bigoplus_{j=n+1}^{\infty}E(\mathcal{M}_{j})
,
we infer from
\mathcal{H}_{1}={\mathcal{R}}(V)\oplus{\mathcal{R}}(E)
that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}(V_{n})\oplus{\mathcal{R}}(E_{n})={\mathcal{R}}(V)\oplus{\mathcal{R}}(E)\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{j}=\mathcal{H}_{1,n},\quad n\geqslant 1.

This means that
the operator
T_{n}\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
given by
T_{n}=\big[\begin{smallmatrix}V_{n}&\sigma E_{n}\\
0&U_{n}\end{smallmatrix}\big]
relative to

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1,n}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{2,n}
is a Brownian unitary
of covariance
\sigma
. Since
\mathcal{H}={\mathcal{R}}(V)\oplus{\mathcal{R}}(\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\infty}W_{j})
, we can define the
unitary operator
T\in\boldsymbol{B}(\mathcal{H})
by

T=\widehat{V}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\infty}W_{j}
.

First, we show that
\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges
strongly to
T
. Fix a vector
h\in\mathcal{H}
and decompose
it as
h=h_{1,n}\oplus h_{2,n}
, where
h_{1,n}\in\mathcal{H}_{1,n}
and
h_{2,n}\in\mathcal{H}_{2,n}
. Noting that

T=\widehat{V_{n}}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=n+1}^{\infty}W_{j}
, we have

\displaystyle\|T_{n}h-Th\|

\displaystyle=\Big\|V_{n}h_{1,n}\oplus\sigma E_{n}h_{2,n}\oplus U_{n}h_{2,n}-\Big[V_{n}h_{1,n}\oplus\Big(\bigoplus_{j=n+1}^{\infty}W_{j}\Big)h_{2,n}\Big]\Big\|

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sigma E_{n}h_{2,n}+\Big(U_{n}-\bigoplus_{j=n+1}^{\infty}W_{j}\Big)h_{2,n}\Big\|\leqslant(\sigma+2)\|h_{2,n}\|,\quad n\geqslant 1.

Since
h_{2,n}
converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
,
this implies that
Th=\lim_{n\to\infty}T_{n}h
.

Next, we prove that
\{T_{n}^{*}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

converges strongly to
T^{*}
. Decompose a vector
h\in\mathcal{H}
as
h=h_{1}\oplus g_{n}\oplus h_{2,n}
, where

h_{1}\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
,
g_{n}\in\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{M}_{j}

and
h_{2,n}\in\mathcal{H}_{2,n}
. Then
h_{1,n}:=h_{1}\oplus g_{n}\in\mathcal{H}_{1,n}
. Denote by
Q_{n}
(resp.,
P_{n}
) the
orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{H}
(resp.,
\mathcal{H}_{1,n}
)
onto
{\mathcal{R}}(V_{n})
and set
Q_{n}^{\bot}:=I-Q_{n}
. Since

V_{n}^{*}=\widehat{V_{n}}^{*}P_{n}
and
Q_{n}h=Q_{n}h_{1,n}=P_{n}h_{1,n}
, we have

\displaystyle V_{n}^{*}h_{1,n}=\widehat{V_{n}}^{*}P_{n}h_{1,n}=\widehat{V_{n}}^{*}Q_{n}h,\quad n\geqslant 1.

(5.2)

Noting that
T_{n}^{*}=\big[\begin{smallmatrix}V_{n}^{*}&0\\
\sigma E_{n}^{*}&U_{n}^{*}\end{smallmatrix}\big]
and
T^{*}=\widehat{V_{n}}^{*}\oplus\bigoplus_{j=n+1}^{\infty}W_{j}^{*}
, we get

\displaystyle\|T_{n}^{*}h-T^{*}h\|

\displaystyle=\Big\|V_{n}^{*}h_{1,n}\oplus(\sigma E_{n}^{*}h_{1,n}+U_{n}^{*}h_{2,n})

\displaystyle\hskip 43.05542pt-\Big[\widehat{V_{n}}^{*}Q_{n}h\oplus\Big(\bigoplus_{j=n+1}^{\infty}W_{j}^{*}\Big)Q_{n}^{\perp}h\Big]\Big\|

\displaystyle\hskip-4.30554pt\overset{\eqref{vnwj}}{=}\Big\|\sigma E_{n}^{*}h_{1,n}+U_{n}^{*}h_{2,n}-\Big(\bigoplus_{j=n+1}^{\infty}W_{j}^{*}\Big)Q_{n}^{\perp}h\Big\|

\displaystyle\leqslant\sigma\|E_{n}^{*}h_{1,n}\|+\|h_{2,n}\|+\|Q_{n}^{\perp}h\|,\quad n\geqslant 1.

(5.3)

Since
E_{n}
is an isometry,
E_{n}E_{n}^{*}
is the
orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{H}_{1,n}
onto
{\mathcal{R}}(E_{n})
.
Observing that
h_{1,n}-h_{1}=g_{n}\perp{\mathcal{R}}(E_{n})
,
we see that

\displaystyle E_{n}E_{n}^{*}h_{1,n}=E_{n}E_{n}^{*}h_{1}=R_{n}h_{1},\quad n\geqslant 1,

where
R_{n}
is the orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{H}_{1}

onto
{\mathcal{R}}(E_{n})
. Recalling that
{\mathcal{R}}(E_{n})=\bigoplus_{j=n+1}^{\infty}E(\mathcal{M}_{j})
, we deduce that

\{R_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges strongly to
0
. As
a consequence,
\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{n}E_{n}^{*}h_{1,n}=0
,
which implies that
\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{n}^{*}h_{1,n}=0
. Since
\mathcal{H}_{1}={\mathcal{R}}(V)\oplus{\mathcal{R}}(E)
and

{\mathcal{R}}(Q_{n})={\mathcal{R}}(V_{n})
, we infer from (
5.1
) that

\{Q_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges strongly to
I
.
Hence,
\lim_{n\to\infty}Q_{n}^{\perp}h=0
. Knowing
that
h_{2,n}
converges to
0
as
n\to\infty
and
using (
5.3
), we conclude that

\{T_{n}^{*}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
converges strongly to

T^{*}
.

Thus, we have proved that the sequence

\{T_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of Brownian unitaries of
covariance
\sigma
converges
∗
-strongly to the
unitary operator
T
.

\diamondsuit

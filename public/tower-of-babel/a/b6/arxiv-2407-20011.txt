An implicit two-phase obstacle-type problem for the p p -Laplacian with the fractional gradient
Pedro Miguel Campos, José Francisco Rodrigues

Abstract
In this work we study an inhomogeneous two-phase obstacle-type problem associated to the s s -fractional p p -Laplacian. Besides the existence and uniqueness of solutions, we study the convergence of the solutions when s → 1 s\to 1 to the classical problem. We also study the continuous dependence with respect to the level-set function v v , yielding an existence result for a two-phase obstacle-type problem with an implicit level-set function.

1
Introduction

The aim of this work is to study the continuous dependence and some of its consequences of the following two-phase obstacle-type problem in an arbitrary open bounded set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, formally in the form

\begin{cases}-\Delta^{s}_{p}u+\lambda_{+}\chi_{\{u>v\}}-\lambda_{-}\chi_{\{u<v\}}=f&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
u=0&\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega,\end{cases}

(1)

where
-\Delta^{s}_{p}
is the fractional
p
-Laplacian, with
p\in(1,\infty)
and
s\in(0,1)
. This operator arises naturally in the fractional setting of the Riesz fractional gradient
D^{s}
as a generalization of the classical
p
-Laplacian
-\Delta_{p}
, formally corresponding to the limit case
s=1
, with
D^{1}=D
being the classical gradient. Here
f=f(x)
and
\lambda=\lambda_{\pm}(x)\geq 0
a.e. in
\Omega
are functions in
L^{q}(\Omega)
for suitable
q
, and
\chi_{A}
is the characteristic function of the set
A\subset\Omega
. The level set function
v=v(x)
may be directly given in
L^{1}(\Omega)
or implicitly given as a function
v=\Phi[u]
depending on the solution
u
.

For each given
v
, the solution
u=u^{s}
to (
22
) can be obtained as the minimizer in the Lions-Calderón space
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, a fractional Sobolev-type space for
0<s<1
, to the functional

J^{s}_{p}(w)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{|D^{s}w|^{p}}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{[\lambda_{+}(w-v)^{+}+\lambda_{-}(w-v)^{-}]}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}{fw}\,dx,

(2)

with
w^{\pm}=\max\{\pm w,0\}
. We shall show that
u=u^{s}
converges in the limit case
s=1
, to
u^{1}
, which is the minimizer of the functional
J^{1}_{p}(w)
in the classical Sobolev space
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)=\Lambda^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, for
1<p<\infty
. However, these variational solutions do not always satisfy the equation (1) a.e. in
\Omega
but rather the following weaker form of that equation

-\Delta^{s}_{p}u+\lambda_{+}\chi_{{}_{>}}-\lambda_{-}\chi_{{}_{<}}=f\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(3)

with the characteristic functions
\chi_{\{u>v\}}
and
\chi_{\{u<v\}}
replaced, respectively, by quasi-characteristic functions
\chi_{{}_{>}}
and
\chi_{{}_{<}}
, such that

\chi_{\{u>v\}}\leq\chi_{{}_{>}}\leq\chi_{\{u\geq v\}},\quad\chi_{\{u<v\}}\leq\chi_{{}_{<}}\leq\chi_{\{u\leq v\}}\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega.

(4)

This type of equations arise in stationary models of interior heat control
[
10
]
and in composite membranes, as referred in
[
19
]
, which presents an extended exposition, with further references, of the regularity of the free boundaries

\Gamma_{<v}=\partial\{u<v\}\cap\Omega\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\Gamma_{>v}=\partial\{u>v\}\cap\Omega,

(5)

in the classical case of the Laplacian, with
v=0
and
f=0
. The free boundaries
\Gamma_{<0}
and
\Gamma_{>0}
may, in general, present branching points when they start intersecting the boundary of the degenerate phase
\partial\{u=0\}
, whenever this intermediate phase, the inter-phases, has non empty interior. In this case the parts of the free boundaries
\Gamma_{<0}\cap\partial\{u=0\}
and
\Gamma_{>0}\cap\partial\{u=0\}
behave locally as the free boundaries of the upper and lower obstacle problems, respectively. For nonlinear operators of
p
-Laplacian type, in the classical two-phase problem for
v=f=0
and constant
\lambda_{\pm}>0
, it has been shown that the free boundaries have locally finite
(d-1)
-Hausdorff measure for
p
close to 2 (for
p>2
in
[
11
]
and for
p<2
in
[
27
]
). In a nondegenerate case for large
f\neq 0
and more general quasilinear operators when the inter-phases set has zero Lebesgue measure it has been shown that the free boundary is, up to a null subset, the union of an at most countable family of
C^{1}
-surfaces in
[
20
, Theorem 4.2]
.

It was suggested in
[
3
]
that the fractional
p
-Laplacian can be used to study nonlocal hyperelastic materials, so it might be of interest to consider the problem (
22
) to design certain nonlinear and nonlocal composite membranes. On the other hand, in the case
p=2
it can be used to model equilibrium situations of foraging, since it have been well documented
[
22
]
that some predators exhibit spatial Levy-like behaviour when searching for food, which correspond to the fractional Laplacian.

Besides proving the existence of solutions, we also study the continuous dependence of these solutions with respect to the given function
v
and with respect to
s
as
s\nearrow 1
. These results have interesting consequences. The continuous dependence of these solutions with respect to the given function
v
allows us to prove an existence result for implicit two-phase problems formally in the form

\begin{cases}-\Delta^{s}_{p}u+\lambda_{+}(x)\chi_{\{u>\Phi(u)\}}-\lambda_{-}(x)\chi_{\{u<\Phi(u)\}}=f&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
u=0&\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

(6)

where
\Phi
is an operator which depends on the solution of the problem and the characteristic functions are relaxed to quasi-characteristic functions as in (
4
).

A motivation for studying the convergence of the fractional problem to the local problem, when
s\to 1
, arises from the fact that, in general, the properties of the free boundaries
\Gamma_{<v}
and
\Gamma_{>v}
of the two-phase problems for the classical
p
-Laplacian and for fractional differential operators are far from being well-understood. In fact, as it was observed in
[
17
]
, for the linear fractional case, the structure of the free boundaries is only known for problems that can be transformed, using the Caffarelli-Silvestre (or Stinga-Torrea) extension method, see
[
7
,
23
]
, into a degenerate local problem. We remark that it is not known if there is also an extension method for the fractional differential operators that arise in the context of the Riesz fractional gradient, aside from the fractional Laplacian. Nevertheless, we prove here that the solutions to the two phase problem in the fractional framework approximate the classical solutions, which suggests that they may share, at least for
s
near 1, some of the properties of the classical problem.

After introducing the functional framework in Section
2
, we present in Section
3
the characterization of the solution to the two-phase obstacle-type problems for
0<s\leq 1
by using an approximation by
\varepsilon
-regularized semilinear problems. In particular, we give a precise new estimate for the
L^{p}
-norm of the gradients
D^{s}
.

In Section
4
, we prove the convergence of the solution
u^{s}
of the fractional problem to the solution of the classical problem
u^{1}
when
s\to 1
, as well as a weak stability of the quasi-characteristic functions
\chi^{s}_{{}_{>}}
and
\chi^{s}_{{}_{<}}
towards
\chi^{1}_{{}_{>}}
and
\chi^{1}_{{}_{<}}
, respectively. This convergence is in fact strong if the limit problem is non-degenerate, i.e., if
\chi^{1}_{{}_{>}}=\chi_{\{u_{1}>v\}}
and
\chi^{1}_{{}_{<}}=\chi_{\{u_{1}<v\}}
, which holds whenever the Lebesgue measure of the inter-phases
\{u_{1}=v\}
is zero.

Finally, in Section
5
, exploring the continuous dependence of the solution and of the quasi-characteristic functions of the Dirichlet problem (
3
), we prove the existence of a weak solution to the implicit two-phase problem (
6
), giving examples of possible continuous operators
\Phi
with interesting properties, including a special case with a vanishing in measure inter-phase
\{u=\Phi(u)\}
.

We observe that, although our results are developed in the framework of
-\Delta^{s}_{p}
, they can be easily generalized to more general quasi-linear operators of the
p
-Laplacian type
-D^{s}\cdot(\boldsymbol{a}(x,D^{s}u)
as in
[
8
, Section 3.3.1]
.

2
Preliminaries

We consider the fractional Riesz gradient for smooth functions with compact support
\varphi
as it was defined in
[
21
]
,

D^{s}\varphi=D(I_{1-s}\varphi)

where
I_{1-s}
is the Riesz potential of order
1-s
,
0<1-s<1
,

I_{1-s}\varphi(x)=(I_{1-s}*\varphi)(x)=\frac{2^{s}\Gamma\left(\frac{d+s+1}{2}\right)}{\pi^{d/2}(d-1+r)\Gamma\left(\frac{1-s}{2}\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\frac{\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{d+s-1}}}\,dy.

Although this definition requires a certain regularity of the function
\varphi
, it can be extended to more general classes of functions. In fact, due to the fractional integration by parts formula

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\varphi(x)D^{s}\cdot\Phi(x)}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\Phi(x)\cdot D^{s}\varphi(x)}\,dx,

(7)

which is first valid for all functions
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\Phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we can then extend it to functions
\varphi\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for which there exists a function
G\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\varphi(x)D^{s}\cdot\Phi(x)}\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\Phi(x)\cdot G(x)}\,dx,\quad\forall\Phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}).

This distributional characterization of the (weak) fractional gradient allows to identify
G=D^{s}\varphi
in the Banach space, the Lions-Calderón space,

\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)=\overline{C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)}^{\|\cdot\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}},

for
0<s<1
and
1<p<\infty
, with the norm

f\mapsto\|f\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left(\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}+\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

When
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, this space coincides with the Bessel potential or generalized Sobolev spaces, as they are called in the Harmonic Analysis literature
[
21
]
, and it also coincides with the space of functions
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for which there exists a (weak) fractional gradient
D^{s}f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, as it was proven in
[
6
,
15
]
. When
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an arbitrary bounded set, as it will be assumed in this work, the functions
f\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
will be considered extended to the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by zero, their fractional gradient
D^{s}f
is different from zero on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega
and we may consider
f\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
as a closed subspace of
f\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Moreover, the identity (
7
) can be used, as in the classical case
s=1
, to give a weak formulation to partial differential equations, since it can be extended when the functions on the right hand side,
\varphi
and
D^{s}\cdot\Phi
are in
L^{p}(\Omega)
and in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, respectively, and on the left-hand-side,
\Phi
and
D^{s}\varphi
are in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
and in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
, respectively. Consequently, this characterization provides a convenient framework to study fractional partial differential equations through variational methods. In particular, this is useful throughout this article to deal with the fractional
p
-Laplacian,

\Delta^{s}_{p}f=D^{s}\cdot(|D^{s}f|^{p-2}D^{s}f),

(8)

since, for
f\in\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
,

\langle-\Delta^{s}_{p}f,g\rangle_{\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\times\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{|D^{s}f|^{p-2}D^{s}f\cdot D^{s}g}\,dx,\quad\forall g\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega),

and
\Delta^{s}_{p}f
is a distribution belonging to the dual space
\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)=\left(\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\right)^{\prime}
.

This space has some properties that are similar to those observed in the classical Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, for
0<s<1
and
1<p<\infty
. In fact, as
D^{s}f\to Df
when
s\to 1
for
f\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, we shall denote the gradient
Df=D^{1}f
and
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)=\Lambda^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, by considering also
Df=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega
.

Proposition 1

(Poincaré’s inequality)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
p\in(1,\infty)
and
s\in(0,1]
. There exists a positive constant
C_{P}>0
depending only on
p
,
d
,
\Omega
and
R
, such that for all open sets
\Omega_{1}\supset B_{2R}(0)
(
\Omega_{1}
can be unbounded) and for all
f\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, we have

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C_{P}}{s}\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1};\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(9)

Proof.

See
[
4
]
and
[
8
, Theorem 2.4]
.
∎

As in the classical case, for bounded sets
\Omega
, in
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
we can work, as we shall do here, with the equivalent norm
f\mapsto\|f\|_{\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}=\left(\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{p}\right)^{1/p}
, for
0<s\leq 1
and
1<p<\infty
.

Proposition 2

(Sobolev embeddings)
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
,
1<p<\infty
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an arbitrary open set. Then,

1)

if
sp<d
, we have
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset L^{q}(\Omega)
for
1\leq q\leq p^{*}_{s}=\frac{dp}{d-sp}
. Moreover, when
q=p^{*}_{s}
we have

\|f\|_{L^{p^{*}_{s}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})};

(10)

2)

if
sp=d
, then
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset L^{q}(\Omega)
for every
q\in[1,+\infty)
; and

3)

if
sp>d
, then we have
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\subset C^{0,s-\frac{d}{p}}(\overline{\Omega})
with the estimate

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C|x-y|^{s-\frac{d}{p}}\|D^{s}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

See
[
21
, Theorems 1.8 and 1.11]
.
∎

As in the classical
s=1
case, the Sobolev’s embeddings are also compact for all
q
, such that
1\leq q<p^{*}_{s}
, that is the Relich-Kondrachov theorem also holds for
0<s<1
.

For future reference, we denote the conjugate exponent
p^{\#}_{s}=(p^{*}_{s})^{\prime}
, where
p^{\#}_{s}=dp/[d(p-1)+sp]
if
sp<d
,
p^{\#}_{s}=1
if
sp>d
and
p^{\#}_{s}>1
if
sp=d
, for
0<s\leq 1
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded open set, and let
0\leq t<s\leq 1
and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Then,

\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\Subset\Lambda^{t,p}_{0}(\Omega).

Moreover, for our results about the approximation of solutions and the continuous dependence of the solutions, as
s\to\sigma=1
, we shall use the following two properties.

Proposition 4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
. Let
s_{n}\in(0,1]
be a convergent sequence of numbers such that
s_{n}\to\sigma\in(0,1]
, and
u_{n}\in\Lambda^{s_{n},p}_{0}(\Omega)
be such that
\sup_{n}{\|D^{s_{n}}u_{n}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}}<\infty
. Then, there exists a function
u\in\Lambda^{\sigma,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and a subsequence also denoted by
u_{n}
satisfying

u_{n}\to u\mbox{ in }\Lambda^{t,p}_{0}(\Omega),\mbox{ for all }0\leq t<\sigma,\quad\mbox{ and }\quad D^{s_{n}}u_{n}\rightharpoonup D^{\sigma}u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Proof.

See
[
4
]
for the case
\sigma=1
and
t=0
. One can use Proposition
3
as in
[
8
]
to generalize to
0<t<\sigma\leq 1
.
∎

Proposition 5
.

Let
0<s,t\leq 1
,
1<p<\infty
and assume that
u\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
v\in\Lambda^{t,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then, with
\alpha_{p}=2^{2-p}
for
p\geq 2
and
\alpha_{p}=p-1
for
1<p\leq 2
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{(|D^{s}u|^{p-1}D^{s}u-|D^{t}v|^{p-1}D^{t}v)\cdot D^{s}u-D^{t}v)}\,dx\\
\geq\begin{cases}\alpha_{p}\|D^{s}u-D^{t}v\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}&\mbox{ if }p\geq 2\\
\alpha_{p}\frac{\|D^{s}u-D^{t}v)\|^{2}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{(\|D^{s}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|D^{t}v\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})})^{2-p}},&\mbox{ if }1<p\leq 2.\end{cases}

Proof.

This is a simple application of
[
12
, Lemma 1.11]
in the vectorial case.

3
Characterization of the solution to the two-phase problem

Throughout this section we shall assume

v\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)

(11)

with
q^{\prime}=q/(q-1)
,
1\leq q^{\prime}<p^{*}_{s}
, for some
q
,
p^{\#}_{s}<q\leq\infty
, and

\lambda_{+},\lambda_{-}\in L^{q}(\Omega)\mbox{ with }\lambda_{\pm}\geq 0\mbox{ a.e. }x\in\Omega,

(12)

where
p^{\#}_{s}=(p^{*}_{s})^{\prime}
is the Sobolev conjugate exponent, i.e.
p^{\#}_{s}=dp/[d(p-1)+sp]
if
sp<d
,
p^{\#}_{s}=1
if
sp>d
and
p^{\#}_{s}>1
if
sp=d
, for
0<s\leq 1
.

We define the convex functional
\Psi_{v}(w)
in
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, for
0<s<1
and in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)=\Lambda^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
for
s=1
,

\Psi_{v}(w)=\int_{\Omega}{(\lambda_{+}(x)(w-v)^{+}+\lambda_{-}(x)(w-v)^{-})}\,dx

Proposition 6
.

Assume
f\in\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)=(\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega))^{\prime}
. Then for each s,
0<s\leq 1
, there exists a unique solution
u=u_{s}\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
to

\Psi_{v}(w)-\Psi_{v}(u)\geq\langle f+\Delta^{s}_{p}u,w-u\rangle_{\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\times\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}\quad\forall w\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega).

(13)

We can express the variational inequality (
13
) in terms of subdifferentials, by stating that there exists
\zeta_{v}\in\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
such that

\zeta_{v}=f+\Delta^{s}_{p}u\in\partial\Psi_{v}(u)

(14)

Proof.

This is a simple application of the direct method of calculus of variations to the minimisation problem for the strictly convex and l.s.c. functional defined in (
2
), which is coercive by Poincaré inequality:

u\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega):\quad J^{s}_{p}(u)\leq J^{s}_{p}(w),\quad\forall w\in\Lambda^{s,p}(\Omega).

Decomposing the functional
J^{s}_{p}
in the differentiable component plus
\Psi_{v}
, we obtain the Euler variational inequality (
13
) for the minimal solution
u
.
∎

To characterize the function
\zeta_{v}
in the case
f\in L^{p^{\#}_{s}}(\Omega)\subset\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, we study the following regularised problem for
\varepsilon>0
:

\begin{cases}-\Delta^{s}_{p}u_{\varepsilon}+\lambda_{+}H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}-v)-\lambda_{-}H_{\varepsilon}(v-u_{\varepsilon})=f&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\
u_{\varepsilon}=0&\mbox{ on }\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

(15)

Here, the characteristic functions
\chi_{\{u>v\}}
and
\chi_{\{u<v\}}
that appear in (
22
) are replaced by
G^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(u-v)=H_{\varepsilon}(u-v)
and
G^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(v-u)=H_{\varepsilon}(v-u)
, respectively, where

H_{\varepsilon}(t)=\begin{cases}0,&t\leq 0\\
\frac{t}{\varepsilon},&0\leq t\leq\varepsilon\\
1,&t\geq\varepsilon\end{cases}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad G_{\varepsilon}(t)=\begin{cases}0,&t\leq 0\\
\frac{t^{2}}{2\varepsilon},&0\leq t\leq\varepsilon\\
t-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},&t\geq\varepsilon.\end{cases}

Theorem 1
.

Under the assumption
f\in L^{p^{\#}_{s}}(\Omega)
, for each s,
0<s\leq 1
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
the unique solution
u_{\varepsilon}\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
to (
15
) converges in
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
towards the unique solution
u
of (
13
) and satisfies the estimate

\|D^{s}(u_{\varepsilon}-u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/\max\{p,2\}}.

(16)

Proof.

Step 1: Approximating problem and a-priori estimates.

By introducing the differentiable convex functional

J_{p,\varepsilon}^{s}(w)=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{|D^{s}w|^{p}}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{[\lambda_{+}G_{\varepsilon}(w-v)+\lambda_{-}G_{\varepsilon}(v-w)]}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}{fw}\,dx,

we easily see that the unique minimising solution
u_{\varepsilon}
of
J_{p,\varepsilon}^{s}
in
\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfies a.e. the equation (
15
). Indeed this equation in variational form corresponds to the Euler equation of the regularised minimum problem, firstly in the duality
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\times\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}
. As a consequence, by the assumption on
f
and the Sobolev inbeddings, we have
\int_{\Omega}{fw}\,dx=\langle f,w\rangle_{\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\times\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}
for all
w\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, and the equation (
15
) also holds a.e. in
\Omega
.

Now we observe that
u_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
\varepsilon>0
. Indeed, denoting

\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x)=\lambda_{+}(x)H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}-v)(x)-\lambda_{-}(x)H_{\varepsilon}(v-u_{\varepsilon})(x),

(17)

then we have

-\lambda_{-}(x)\leq\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq\lambda_{+}(x)\mbox{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega,

which together with
f\in L^{p^{\#}_{s}}(\Omega)
, yields
-\Delta^{s}_{p}u_{\varepsilon}=\zeta_{\varepsilon}-f\in L^{p^{\#}_{s}}(\Omega)
uniformly for
\varepsilon>0
. Multiplying both sides of the previous identity with
u_{\varepsilon}
, extended by zero in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and applying the formula (
7
), with the help of Proposition
2
and the fractional Sobolev’s inequality (
10
), we obtain

\|D^{s}u_{\varepsilon}\|^{p-1}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C(\Omega,p,s)(\|\lambda_{+}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|\lambda_{-}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{p^{\#}_{s}}(\Omega)}).

(18)

From Proposition
3
, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we can extract a subsequence from
u_{\varepsilon}
such that

u_{\varepsilon}\to u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\Omega)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad D^{s}u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup D^{s}u\mbox{ in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})

for some function
u\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Step 2: Characterization of the limit.

We are going to show that there exists a function
\zeta\in L^{q}(\Omega)
such that
\zeta=f+\Delta^{s}_{p}u
. Indeed, we notice that the pointwise estimates
0\leq H_{\varepsilon}(t)\leq 1
, for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, together with weak-
*
compactness of closed balls in
L^{\infty}
(see, for instance, Theorem 3.16 and page 101 of
[
5
]
) implies the existence of functions
\chi_{{}_{>}},\chi_{{}_{<}}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, with
0\leq\chi_{{}_{>}}(x),\chi_{{}_{<}}(x)\leq 1
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
, such that

H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}-v)\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\chi_{{}_{>}}\mbox{ and }H_{\varepsilon}(v-u_{\varepsilon})\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\chi_{{}_{<}}\mbox{ in }L^{\infty}(\Omega).

(19)

Observe that
\chi_{{}_{>}}=0
almost everywhere in
\{u<v\}
because

0=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\int_{\Omega}{H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}-v)(u_{\varepsilon}-v)^{-}}\,dx}=\int_{\Omega}{\chi_{{}_{>}}(u-v)^{-}}\,dx=\int_{\{u<v\}}{\chi_{{}_{>}}(u-v)}\,dx.

In addition,
\chi_{{}_{>}}=1
almost everywhere in
\{u>v\}
since

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\int_{\{v<u_{\varepsilon}<v+\varepsilon\}}{(1-\frac{u_{\varepsilon}-v}{\varepsilon})(u_{\varepsilon}-v)}\,dx}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\int_{\Omega}{(1-H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}-v))(u_{\varepsilon}-v)^{+}}\,dx}=\int_{\Omega}{(1-\chi_{{}_{>}})(u-v)^{+}}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\{u>v\}}{(1-\chi_{{}_{>}})(u-v)}\,dx

A similar argument can be used to prove that
\chi_{{}_{<}}=0
almost everywhere in
\{u>v\}
and
\chi_{{}_{<}}=1
almost everywhere in
\{u<v\}
. Consequently,

\chi_{\{u>v\}}\leq\chi_{{}_{>}}\leq\chi_{\{u\geq v\}},\quad\chi_{\{u<v\}}\leq\chi_{{}_{<}}\leq\chi_{\{u\leq v\}}

and, since
q>p^{\#}_{s}
,

\zeta_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\zeta\mbox{ in }L^{q}(\Omega)\mbox{ weak and also in }\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\mbox{ strong },

where

\zeta=\lambda_{+}\chi_{{}_{>}}-\lambda_{-}\chi_{{}_{<}}.

(20)

Moreover, by using the weak formulation of (
15
) with

,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{|D^{s}u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}D^{s}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot D^{s}w}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{\zeta_{\varepsilon}w}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}{fw}\,dx.

and letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we deduce

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{|D^{s}u|^{p-2}D^{s}u\cdot D^{s}w}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{\zeta w}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}{fw}\,dx.

In order to show that
\zeta=\zeta_{v}\in\Psi_{v}(u)
, we define the functional

\Psi^{\varepsilon}(w)=\int_{\Omega}{[\lambda_{+}G_{\varepsilon}(w-v)+\lambda_{-}G_{\varepsilon}(v-w)]}\,dx.

On the one hand, we have
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\Psi^{\varepsilon}(w)}=\Psi_{v}(w)
, for all
w\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, while on the other we have

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\Psi^{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})}\geq\lim_{\delta\to 0}{\Psi^{\delta}(u)}=\Psi_{v}(u).

since
G_{\delta}(t)\leq G_{\varepsilon}(t)
for each
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
0<\varepsilon<\delta
, and

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\Psi^{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})}\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\Psi^{\delta}(u_{\varepsilon})}=\Psi^{\delta}(u).

Consequently, from the definition of
\zeta_{\varepsilon}\in\partial\Psi^{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\{(\Psi^{\varepsilon})^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\}
, we deduce

\Psi_{v}(w)+\langle\zeta,w-u\rangle=\lim{[\Psi^{\varepsilon}(w)+\langle\zeta^{\varepsilon},w-u_{\varepsilon}\rangle]}\geq\liminf{\Psi^{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})}\geq\Psi_{v}(u)

for all
w\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, implying that
\zeta=\zeta_{v}\in\partial\Psi_{v}(u)\subset\Lambda^{-s,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
.

Step 3: Strong convergence.

To conclude, we prove the convergence of
D^{s}u_{\varepsilon}
to
D^{s}u
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
. If to the identity (
14
), we subtract (
15
), and use the definitions (

) and (
20
), of
\zeta_{\varepsilon}
and
\zeta_{v}
respectively, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\left(|D^{s}u|^{p-2}D^{s}u-|D^{s}u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}D^{s}u_{\varepsilon}\right)\cdot D^{s}(u-u_{\varepsilon})}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}{(\zeta_{\varepsilon}-\zeta)(u-u_{\varepsilon})}\,dx\\
=\int_{\Omega}{\lambda_{+}[H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}-v)-\chi_{{}_{>}}](u-u_{\varepsilon})}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}{\lambda_{-}[H_{\varepsilon}(v-u_{\varepsilon})-\chi_{{}_{<}}](u-u_{\varepsilon})}\,dx.

Since for every
t\geq 0
,
(1-H_{\varepsilon}(t))t\leq\varepsilon
, and
0\leq\chi_{\{u>v\}}\leq\chi_{{}_{>}}\leq\chi_{\{u\geq v\}}
a.e. in
\Omega
, we deduce for the positive phase

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\lambda_{+}[H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}-v)-\chi_{{}_{>}}](u-u_{\varepsilon})}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{u>v\}}{\lambda_{+}[H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}-v)-1](u-u_{\varepsilon})}\,dx+\int_{\{u=v\}}{\lambda_{+}[H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}-v)-\chi_{{}_{>}}](v-u_{\varepsilon})}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\{u>v\}}{\lambda_{+}[H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}-v)-1](v-u_{\varepsilon})}\,dx+\int_{\{u=v\}\cap\{u_{\varepsilon}<v\}}{\lambda_{+}\chi_{{}_{>}}(u_{\varepsilon}-v)}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{\{u=v\}\cap\{u_{\varepsilon}>v\}}{\lambda_{+}[H_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}-v)-1](v-u_{\varepsilon})}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|\lambda_{+}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

For the negative-phase, we can apply the same reasoning and deduce that

\int_{\Omega}{\lambda_{-}[H_{\varepsilon}(v-u_{\varepsilon})-\chi_{{}_{<}}](u-u_{\varepsilon})}\,dx\leq\varepsilon\|\lambda_{-}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

Consequently, for

we get the uniform estimate

\alpha_{p}\|D^{s}(u_{\varepsilon}-u)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\left(|D^{s}u|^{p-2}D^{s}u-|D^{s}u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}D^{s}u_{\varepsilon}\right)\cdot D^{s}(u-u_{\varepsilon})}\,dx\\
\leq\varepsilon(\|\lambda_{+}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\lambda_{-}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}).

On the other hand, for

, we use the estimate (

, depending on

and

.

4
, we have that

in
\Lambda^{t,p}_{0}(\Omega)

Corollary 1
.

Proposition 7

(Continuous dependence with respect to
v
)
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
,
f\in L^{p^{\#}_{s}}(\Omega)
,
\lambda_{\pm}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
with
\lambda_{\pm}\geq 0
a.e.

, and a sequence of functions

in

as

and

as in (

) and (

the solution of (

and

the solution of (

) associated to

, then

\chi_{{}_{>}}[v_{n}]\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\chi_{{}_{>}},\chi_{{}_{<}}[v_{n}]\overset{\ast}{\rightharpoonup}\chi_{{}_{<}}\mbox{ in }L^{\infty}(\Omega)

and

u_{n}\to u\mbox{ in }\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega).

Proof.

This follows from a very similar argument to the proof of Theorem
2
. In fact, we use the uniform bound on
\zeta_{n}-f
to be able to extract a subsequence still denoted by

such that,
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{p}(\Omega)
,
D^{s}u_{n}\rightharpoonup D^{s}u
in

for some function

. Then, we make use of the strong convergence in

of
u_{n}
and

to

and
v
, respectively, to characterize the weak limit of
\zeta_{n}=\lambda_{+}\chi_{{}_{>}}[v_{n}]-\lambda_{-}\chi_{{}_{<}}[v_{n}]
to

in

. Together with the weak convergence of

Then, by Proposition (

in

With this result, we can prove, using fixed-point methods, an existence result for the quasi-variational two-phase problem (

Theorem 4
.

Let
0<s\leq 1
,
f\in L^{p^{\#}_{s}}(\Omega)
,
\lambda_{\pm}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
with
\lambda_{\pm}(x)\geq 0
a.e.

\Phi:L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\to L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)

(u,\chi_{{}_{>}}[\Phi(u)],\chi_{{}_{<}}[\Phi(u)])\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)\times L^{\infty}(\Omega)\times L^{\infty}(\Omega)
satisfying

\begin{cases}-\Delta^{s}_{p}u+\lambda_{+}\chi_{{}_{>}}[\Phi(u)]-\lambda_{-}\chi_{{}_{<}}[\Phi(u)]=f&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\

(36)

with

\chi_{\{u>\Phi(u)\}}\leq\chi_{{}_{>}}[\Phi(u)]\leq\chi_{\{u\geq\Phi(u)\}},\quad{and}\quad\chi_{\{u<\Phi(u)\}}\leq\chi_{{}_{<}}[\Phi(u)]\leq\chi_{\{u\leq\Phi(u)\}}

\Psi_{\Phi(u)}(w)-\Psi_{\Phi(u)}(u)\geq\int_{\Omega}{(f+\Delta^{s}_{p}u)(w-u)}\,dx\quad\forall w\in\Lambda^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega).

Proof.

Let
L^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega)\ni v\mapsto S(v)=u_{v}
be the solution operator to

\begin{cases}-\Delta^{s}_{p}u_{v}+\lambda_{+}\chi_{{}_{>}}[\Phi(v)]-\lambda_{-}\chi_{{}_{<}}[\Phi(v)]=f&\mbox{ in }\Omega\\

|\zeta_{\Phi(v)}|=|\lambda_{+}\chi_{{}_{>}}[\Phi(v)]-\lambda_{-}\chi_{{}_{<}}[\Phi(v)]|\leq\lambda_{+}+\lambda_{-}

\|D^{s}u_{v}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\langle-\Delta^{s}_{p}u_{v},u_{v}\rangle\\
=\int_{\Omega}{(f-\zeta_{\Phi(v)})u_{v}}\,dx\leq C\||f|+\lambda_{+}+\lambda_{-}\|_{L^{p^{\#}_{s}}(\Omega)}\|D^{s}u_{v}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})},

Let

solving the implicit problem (

∎

Remark 7
.

We can consider

to be a Nemytskii operator defined by

Another particularly interesting example is the case in which

with

and

. In fact, to prove that

is continuous, as an operator in

is a Carathéodory function. Moreover, for any measurable subset

\lim_{|\mathcal{C}|\to 0}{\int_{\mathcal{C}}{|\tau(x,y,w_{n}(y))|^{p}}\,dy}\leq\lim_{|\mathcal{C}|\to 0}2^{p}{\int_{\mathcal{C}}{[|\phi(x,y)|^{p}+C|w_{n}|^{p}]}\,dy}=0

that converges to

The continuity of

then follows from Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem.

Infinite-dimensional hierarchy of recursive extensions for all sub n -leading soft effects in Yang-Mills
Silvia Nagy, Javier Peraza, Giorgio Pizzolo

Abstract
Building on our proposal in [ 1 ] , we present in detail the construction of the extended phase space for Yang-Mills at null infinity, containing the asymptotic symmetries and the charges responsible for sub n -leading soft theorems at all orders. The generality of the procedure allows it to be directly applied to the computation of both tree and loop-level soft limits. We also give a detailed study of Yang-Mills equations under the radial expansion, giving a thorough construction of the radiative phase space for decays compatible with tree-level amplitudes for both light-cone and radial gauges. This gives rise to useful recursion relations at all orders between the field strength and the vector gauge coefficients. We construct the sub n -leading charges recursively, and show a hierarchical truncation such that each charge subalgebra is closed, and their action in the extended phase space is canonical. We relate these results with the infinite-dimensional algebras that have been recently introduced in the context of conformal field theories at null infinity. We also apply our method to the computation of non-universal terms in the sub-leading charges arising in theories with higher derivative interaction terms.

Now, we solve for

\displaystyle-i\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left([A^{(k-n-1)}_{r},\partial_{u}A_{z}^{(-k)}-F^{(-k)}_{rz}]+\gamma^{-1}[A^{(1+k-n)}_{z},F^{(-k)}_{z\bar{z}}]\right)\bigg].

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n}\bigg\{-\partial_{z}A_{r}^{(-n-1)}+i\sum_{k=2}^{n+1}[A_{z}^{(k-n-1)},A_{r}^{(-k)}]

\displaystyle+\partial_{u}^{-1}\bigg[nF^{(-n)}_{rz}+\partial_{z}(\gamma^{-1}F^{(1-n)}_{z\bar{z}})

\displaystyle A_{z}^{(-n)}

\displaystyle\quad\mathrm{depends}\ \mathrm{on}\quad A_{r}^{(-n-1)},A_{r}^{(-n)},\{A_{\mu}^{(-k)}\}_{k<n}.

For instance,

\displaystyle A_{z}^{(-1)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{z}A_{r}^{(-2)}-i[A_{z}^{(0)},A_{r}^{(-2)}]\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{u}^{-1}\left(\partial_{z}(\gamma^{-1}F^{(0)}_{z\bar{z}})-i[A^{(-2)}_{r},\partial_{u}A^{(0)}_{z}]-i\gamma^{-1}[A^{(0)}_{z},F^{(0)}_{z\bar{z}}]\right).

Recursive formula for

:
As will be seen in

, the

Consider
\{F_{ur}^{(-n,0)}\in C^{\infty}(S^{2})\}_{n\geq 0}
, a family of functions on the sphere, then, by (

), we can obtain
F_{ur}^{-n}
in terms of
F_{ur}^{(-n,0)}
and
\{F_{ur}^{-k}\}_{k<n}
, by using the

It immediately follows from (

) that

\displaystyle F_{ur}^{(-n)}

\displaystyle=\partial_{u}^{-1}\bigg((3-n)F^{(-n+1)}_{ur}-2\gamma^{-1}\partial_{(z}F^{(-n+2)}_{\bar{z})u}

(115)

\displaystyle-i\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\left([A^{(k-n)}_{r},F^{(-k)}_{ur}]-2\gamma^{-1}[A^{(2+k-n)}_{(z},F^{(-k)}_{\bar{z})u}]\right)\bigg).

\displaystyle F_{ur}^{(-n)}(u,z,\bar{z})

(116)

\displaystyle-i\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}\left([A^{(k-n)}_{r},F^{(-k)}_{ur}]-2\gamma^{-1}[A^{(2+k-n)}_{(z},F^{(-k)}_{\bar{z})u}]\right)\bigg).

3.2.2
Decays on null infinity in the radiative phase space

As it is well known in the sub
n
-theorems literature (see for example
[
75
,
76
,
47
]
, the
u
-behaviour of the fields translates as derivatives via a Fourier transform in
u
. Let us assume that we are working at tree-level. This implies that the decay of the
u
-derivative of the initial data (
\partial_{u}A_{z}^{(0)}
) is faster than any polynomial in
u
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}(A^{(0)}_{z})(\omega,z,\bar{z})
be the time-Fourier transform of
A_{z}(u,z,\bar{z})
. Then, the
\omega\rightarrow 0
expansion of the tree-level amplitude (see
[
75
,
76
]
) implies the following fall off in
\omega
for the radiative free data for the gauge vector,

\mathfrak{F}(A^{(0)}_{z})(\omega,\hat{x})=\omega^{-1}\mathfrak{F}(A^{(0)}_{z})^{-1}(\hat{x})+\mathfrak{F}(A^{(0)}_{z})^{0}(\hat{x})+\omega\mathfrak{F}(A^{(0)}_{z})^{1}(\hat{x})+\ldots,

(117)

where the upper index outside the bracket indicates the order in the
\omega
-expansion. Undoing the Fourier transform,
A_{z}(u,z,\bar{z})
behaves like

A^{(0)}_{z}(u,\hat{x})=\theta(u)A_{z}^{(\theta)}(\hat{x})+\delta(u)A_{z}^{(\delta)}(\hat{x})+\delta^{\prime}(u)A_{z}^{\left(\delta^{\prime}\right)}(\hat{x})+\ldots,

(118)

with
\theta
a step function. Thus, the
u\rightarrow\pm\infty
falloffs for the null infinity free data are as follows

A^{(0)}_{z}(u,\hat{x})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u\rightarrow\pm\infty}}{{=}}A^{(0,0)\pm}_{z}(\hat{x})+o(u^{-\infty}),

(119)

where
o(u^{-\infty})
is a remainder that goes to zero faster than any power of
1/u
.

In what follows we compute the
u
-behaviour of the functions
A_{r}^{(-n)},A_{z}^{(-n)}
and
F_{ru}^{(-n)}
as
u\rightarrow-\infty
. Let us start with the the following boundary conditions on the field strength at
\mathcal{I}^{+}_{+}
,

\lim_{u\rightarrow+\infty}F^{(-2)}_{ru}(u,z,\bar{z})=0,\quad\lim_{u\rightarrow+\infty}F_{rz}^{(-2)}(u,z,\bar{z})=0,\quad\lim_{u\rightarrow+\infty}F_{r\bar{z}}^{(-2)}(u,z,\bar{z})=0,

(120)

which arise due to the absence of massive colored fields. The standard
u\rightarrow-\infty
fall-off for the radiative data
A_{z}^{(0)}
is the following,

A_{z}^{(0)}(u,z,\bar{z})=A_{z}^{(0,0)}(z,\bar{z})+o(u^{-\infty}).

(121)

This translates to
F_{ru}^{(-2)}
via (
104
) as follows

F_{ru}^{(-2)}(u,z,\bar{z})=F_{ru}^{(-2,0)}(z,\bar{z})+o(u^{-\infty}).

(122)

We can write

\partial_{u}F_{ru}^{(-2)}=2\gamma^{-1}\partial_{(z}F_{\bar{z})u}^{(0)}-2i\gamma^{-1}[A_{(z}^{(0)},F_{\bar{z})u}^{(0)}]=:2\gamma^{-1}D^{(0)}_{(z}F_{\bar{z})u}^{(0)},

(123)

where
D^{(0)}_{z}:=\partial_{z}-i[A_{z}^{(0)},\cdot]
. Using the results in the previous subsections, we will show here how the integration from
A_{z}^{(0)}(u,z,\bar{z})
can be carried out to all the coefficients (with the corresponding
constants of integration
as functions on the sphere).

First, observe that
A_{r}^{(-2)}=O(u)
, since
\partial_{u}A_{r}^{(-2)}+F_{ru}^{(-2)}=0
. Then, if
A_{r}^{(-2,0)}
is the finite value of the limit

\lim_{u\rightarrow-\infty}A_{r}^{(-2)}+uF_{ru}^{(-2,0)}=A_{r}^{(-2,0)}(z,\bar{z}),

(124)

where the second term on the LHS above has the role of substracting the linear dependence in
u
, then

A_{r}^{(-2)}(u,z,\bar{z})=-uF_{ru}^{(-2,0)}(z,\bar{z})+A_{r}^{(-2,0)}(z,\bar{z})+o(u^{-\infty}).

(125)

This is consistent with (
104
). Next, the equation for
A_{z}^{(-1)}
is given by (
111
),

\displaystyle A_{z}^{(-1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}u\left(\partial_{z}F_{ru}^{(-2,0)}-i[A_{z}^{(0,0)},F_{ru}^{(-2,0)}]+\partial_{z}(\gamma^{-1}F^{(0,0)}_{z\bar{z}})-i\gamma^{-1}[A^{(0,0)}_{z},F^{(0,0)}_{z\bar{z}}]\right)

(126)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{z}A_{r}^{(-2,0)}-i[A_{z}^{(0,0)},A_{r}^{(-2,0)}]\right)+A_{z}^{(-1,0)}+o(u^{-\infty}).

Observe that
A_{z}^{(-1)}
contains up to linear terms in
u
. The coefficients
F_{zu}^{(0)}
,
F_{zu}^{(-1)}
and
F_{zr}^{(-2)}
are found directly

\displaystyle F_{zu}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{u}A_{z}^{(0)}=o(u^{-\infty}),\quad F_{zu}^{(-1)}=-\partial_{u}A_{z}^{(-1)}=O(1),

(127)

\displaystyle F_{zr}^{(-2)}

\displaystyle=\partial_{z}A_{r}^{(-2)}-A_{z}^{(-1)}-i[A_{z}^{(0)},A_{r}^{(-2)}]=O(u).

(128)

The second equation is consistent with the definition of
A_{z}^{(-1)}
via (
107
),

\partial_{u}F^{(-2)}_{rz}=\partial_{u}A_{z}^{(-1)}-F^{(-n)}_{rz}+\partial_{z}(\gamma^{-1}F^{(0)}_{z\bar{z}})-i\left([A^{(-2)}_{r},\partial_{u}A_{z}^{(0)}-F^{(0)}_{rz}]+\gamma^{-1}[A^{(0)}_{z},F^{(0)}_{z\bar{z}}]\right).

(129)

From (
116
) we have

\displaystyle F_{ur}^{(-3)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial_{u}^{-1}\left(2\gamma^{-1}D^{(0)}_{(z}F^{(-1)}_{\bar{z})u}-2i\gamma^{-1}[A^{(-1)}_{(z},F^{(0)}_{\bar{z})u}]\right)

(130)

From equations (
127
) and (
130
), we deduce that
F_{ru}^{(-3)}
is
O(u)
. Indeed, the first and third terms within the brackets are
O(1)
in
u
, while the second term contains a product of a linear term and
\partial_{u}A_{z}^{(0)}
, which is
o(u^{-\infty})
; therefore, after integration, the second term is
O(1)
. Then, we can proceed in an analogous way, since now we have
A_{r}^{(-3)}=O(u^{2})
. Therefore, inductively from (
105
), (
109
) and (
116
) we have that each
F_{ru}^{(-n-2)}
will be
O(u^{n})
.

(137)

\displaystyle\quad+i\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}[A^{(k-n)}_{u},F^{(-k)}_{r\bar{z}}]-i\gamma^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}[A^{(2+k-n)}_{\bar{z}},F^{(-k)}_{z\bar{z}}],\qquad n\geq 2\;.

3.3.1
Recursive formula for the gauge vector

As in the previous section, we will prove that given the initial data

First of all, it is useful to write explicitly the definition of the field strength tensor in radial gauge. From (
131
) and (

), at a given order

we have

\displaystyle F_{ur}^{(-n)}

\displaystyle=(n-1)A_{u}^{(1-n)},\qquad n\geq 2,

(138)

\displaystyle F_{uz}^{(-n)}

\displaystyle=2\partial_{[u}A_{z]}^{(-n)}-i\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}[A_{u}^{(k-n)},A_{z}^{(-k)}]=\partial_{u}A_{z}^{(-n)}-(D_{z}A_{u})^{(-n)},

\displaystyle F_{rz}^{(-n)}

\displaystyle=(1-n)A_{z}^{(1-n)},\qquad n\geq 2,

\displaystyle F_{z\bar{z}}^{(-n)}

\displaystyle A_{u}^{(-n)}

We note that effective actions with higher derivative terms also arise when considering loops of the gluon itself, and these were studied in the simplified context of SDYM in

9
Conclusions

Expanding on our proposal in
[
1
]
, we have shown how the phase space at null infinity can be enlarged in order to accommodate sub
n
-leading charges, associated to the soft theorems for Yang-Mills theory. The procedure is independent of gauge and coordinate choices, and we have allowed for a very general fall-off in the expansion coordinate, thus making our procedure potentially applicable to both tree- and loop-level effects.

We also provided expressions for the equations of motion for Yang-Mills theory in the
r
-expansion, order by order, in standard Bondi coordinates. As an example, we gave the explicit equations in both radial and light-cone gauges using the usual radiative fall-offs compatible with tree-level. In both cases, we showed the existence of a recursive relation for the sub-leading coefficients to all orders in the
r
-expansion for the field strength and the gauge vector in terms of the leading component, which establishes the usual convention of taking
A_{z}^{(0)}(u,z,\bar{z})
as free initial data. Of course, it is not clear whether it is possible to obtain such recursive formulas for other gauge choices. We leave this question for future work.

We generalized the Stueckelberg procedure

The Stueckelberg trick has previously been employed in seemingly very different contexts, but the unifying principle is the presence of a local broken symmetry, though in our case this is subtly related to the radial expansion at null infinity. In particular, the Goldstone-like modes that constitute the expansion of the Stueckelberg field naturally serve as the indicators of the power in the radial expansion, since they serve as the target of the action of over-leading large gauge transformations acting on the whole phase space. This, in turn, allowed us to construct an extended phase space that contains a clear hierarchy of subspaces. The tangent subspaces (where variations live) are determined by the successive terms in the perturbative expansion of the characteristic power series generating the so-called Todd polynomial. Several interesting future lines of work emerge from our results. \displaystyle=-\partial_{u}\text{tr}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{k=m}^{n}\lambda^{[n](k)}F_{ur}^{(-2-m)}(t-u)^{k-m}

(371)

\displaystyle=\partial_{u}\text{tr}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{k=m}^{n}f_{n,k}(z,\bar{z})F_{ur}^{(-2-m)}\sum_{s=0}^{k-m}\binom{k-m}{s}(-1)^{k-m-s-1}\,t^{s}u^{n-m-s}

where

is the binomial coefficient

21

In general, the binomial coefficient

\binom{n}{k}:=\begin{cases}\frac{n!}{k!(n-k)!}\quad n\geq k\\
0\quad\quad\quad\ \ \,n<k\end{cases}\;.

(372)

. In the last step, we make use of

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{k=m}^{n}\sum_{s=0}^{k-m}\binom{k-m}{s}\alpha_{n,s,m,k}=\sum_{m,s=0}^{n}\sum_{k=m}^{n}\binom{k-m}{s}\alpha_{n,s,m,k}

\alpha_{n,s,m,k}
. At this point, it is convenient to define the following objects:

\displaystyle\rho^{[n]}_{s}(u,z,\bar{z})

\displaystyle:=\partial_{u}\text{tr}\sum_{m=0}^{n-s}\tilde{f}_{n,s,m}(z,\bar{z})F_{ur}^{(-2-m)}u^{n-m-s}

\displaystyle\tilde{f}_{n,s,m}(z,\bar{z})

so that the charge

can be written as

\displaystyle\rho^{[n]}=\sum_{s=0}^{n}t^{s}\rho_{s}^{[n]}=\underbrace{\rho_{0}^{[n]}}_{\mathrm{finite}}+\underbrace{\sum_{s=1}^{n}t^{s}\rho_{s}^{[n]}}_{\mathrm{divergent}}\;.

(376)

When taking the limit

(378)

which is a system of

\displaystyle f_{n-s,n-s-j}

Appendix E
Sub-leading charges from the Ward identity perspective

In this section we derive the explicit form of the gauge transformation
\Lambda
under the hypothesis that its associated charge matches a Ward identity-type expression.

More specifically, we assume that the charge splits into two contributions, a soft part that is linear in both
A_{z}^{(0)}
and
A_{\bar{z}}^{(0)}
and a hard part with quadratic and higher terms in
A_{z}^{(0)}
and
A_{\bar{z}}^{(0)}
. In particular, the modes should correspond to a vanishing energy limiting process of a soft gluon,

\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\int due^{iu\omega}F_{uz}^{(0)}\rightarrow\int duF_{uz}^{(0)}.

(380)

We impose radial gauge on the gauge field, but recall that we do not restrict the over-leading gauge parameters, as they only act on the Stueckelberg fields (see
section 2
for more details).

E.1
Leading order charge

As a warm up, we first re-derive the
u
dependence of the large gauge transformation corresponding to the leading charge. Let us start with a general parameter,
\Lambda=\Lambda^{(0)}(u,z,\bar{z})
.

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}[\Lambda]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{2}}\int_{\partial\Sigma}\text{tr}(\Lambda*F)^{(0)}=\frac{1}{e^{2}}\int_{\partial\Sigma}dz^{2}\text{tr}(\sqrt{g}\Lambda F_{ru})^{(0)}

(381)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{2}}\int_{\partial\Sigma}dz^{2}\text{tr}\left(\gamma\Lambda^{(0)}F_{ru}^{(-2)}\right)=\frac{1}{e^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{I}^{+}}dudz^{2}\partial_{u}\text{tr}\left(\gamma\Lambda^{(0)}F_{ru}^{(-2)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{I}^{+}}dudz^{2}\gamma\text{tr}\left(\partial_{u}\Lambda^{(0)}F_{ru}^{(-2)}\right)+\frac{1}{e^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{I}^{+}}dudz^{2}\gamma\text{tr}\left(\Lambda^{(0)}\partial_{u}F_{ru}^{(-2)}\right)

From the e.o.m. (
134
) at order
n=2
we obtain

F_{ru}^{(-2)}=2\gamma^{-1}\int_{-\infty}^{u}d\tilde{u}\left(\partial_{(z}F_{\bar{z})u}^{(0)}-i[A_{(z}^{(0)},F_{\bar{z})u}^{(0)}]\right)\;,

(382)

so that

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}[\Lambda]

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{e^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{I}^{+}}dudz^{2}\text{tr}\left[\partial_{u}\Lambda^{(0)}\int_{-\infty}^{u}d\tilde{u}\left(\partial_{(z}F_{\bar{z})u}^{(0)}-i[A_{(z}^{(0)},F_{\bar{z})u}^{(0)}]\right)\right]

(383)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{e^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{I}^{+}}dudz^{2}\text{tr}\left[\Lambda^{(0)}\left(\partial_{(z}F_{\bar{z})u}^{(0)}-i[A_{(z}^{(0)},F_{\bar{z})u}^{(0)}]\right)\right]\;.

The top line in the previous equation does not have the right form corresponding to the insertion of a standard soft mode. Therefore, we need to set
\partial_{u}\Lambda^{(0)}=0
, so

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}[\Lambda]

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{e^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{I}^{+}}dudz^{2}\text{tr}\left[\partial_{(z}\Lambda^{(0)}F_{\bar{z})u}^{(0)}+i\Lambda^{(0)}[A_{(z}^{(0)},F_{\bar{z})u}^{(0)}]\right]\;.

E.2

\Lambda=r\Lambda^{(1)}(u,z,\bar{z})+\Lambda^{(0)}(u,z,\bar{z})

\Lambda^{(0)}

The sub-leading charge is

\displaystyle Q_{\Sigma}^{\mathrm{sub}}[\Lambda]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{e^{2}}\int_{\partial\Sigma}dz^{2}\;\text{tr}\left(r^{2}\gamma\Lambda F_{ru}\right)^{(0)}=\frac{1}{e^{2}}\int_{\partial\Sigma}dz^{2}\gamma\text{tr}\left(\Lambda^{(0)}F_{ru}^{(-2)}+\Lambda^{(1)}F_{ru}^{(-3)}\right)

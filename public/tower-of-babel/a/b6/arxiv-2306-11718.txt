MultiHypExp : A Mathematica Package For Expanding Multivariate Hypergeometric Functions In Terms Of Multiple Polylogarithms
Souvik Bera

Abstract
We present the Mathematica package MultiHypExp that allows for the expansion of multivariate hypergeometric functions (MHFs), especially those likely to appear as solutions of multi-loop, multi-scale Feynman integrals, in the dimensional regularization parameter. The series expansion of MHFs can be carried out around integer values of parameters to express the series coefficients in terms of multiple polylogarithms. The package uses a modified version of the algorithm prescribed in Bera:2022ecn . In the present work, we relate a given MHF to a Taylor series expandable MHF by a differential operator. The Taylor expansion of the latter MHF is found by first finding the associated partial differential equations (PDEs) from its series representation. We then bring the PDEs to the Pfaffian system and further to the canonical form, and solve them order by order in the expansion parameter using appropriate boundary conditions. The Taylor expansion so obtained and the differential operators are used to find the series expansion of the given MHF. We provide examples to demonstrate the algorithm and to describe the usage of the package, which can be found in this url . 2
Methodology

A method to obtain series expansion of any MHF around arbitrary values of Pochhammer parameters is prescribed in
Bera:2022ecn
, where the expansion parameter, say
\epsilon
, appears linearly in the Pochhammer parameters. Throughout this method, the hypergeometric structure of a given MHF remains intact. In other words, starting with a series presentation, one can find the series expansion of a given MHF, whose coefficients are expressed in terms of higher summation fold MHFs with the same domain of convergence as that of the given MHF. However, the higher fold MHFs appearing in the series coefficients contain the same number of independent variables as the given MHF. For instance, the one variable Gauss
{}_{2}F_{1}(\epsilon,-\epsilon;\epsilon-1;x)
is expanded in series in
Bera:2022ecn
as

{}_{2}F_{1}(\epsilon,-\epsilon;\epsilon-1;x)

\displaystyle=1-\epsilon^{2}x~\,_{3}F_{2}(1,1,1;2,2;x)+\epsilon^{3}\Bigg(\frac{2}{3}x\,_{3}F_{2}(1,1,1;2,2;x)

\displaystyle+\frac{x}{3}~\text{KdF}^{2:1;2}_{2:0;1}\left[\begin{array}[]{c@{{}:{}}c@{;{}}c}1,1&1&1,1\\[4.30554pt]
2,2&{-}\mkern-7.0mu\leaders{\hbox{$\mkern-2.0mu-\mkern-2.0mu$}}{\hfill}\mkern-7.0mu{-}&2\end{array}\;\middle|\;x,x\right]+\frac{x^{2}}{12}~\text{KdF}^{2:1;2}_{2:0;1}\left[\begin{array}[]{c@{{}:{}}c@{;{}}c}2,2&1&1,2\\[4.30554pt]
3,3&{-}\mkern-7.0mu\leaders{\hbox{$\mkern-2.0mu-\mkern-2.0mu$}}{\hfill}\mkern-7.0mu{-}&3\end{array}\;\middle|\;x,x\right]\Bigg)

\displaystyle+O(\epsilon^{4})

(5)

Here, the Kampé de Fériet functions (denoted as KdF) are double summation fold HFs with only one variable

The same Gauss
{}_{2}F_{1}(\epsilon,-\epsilon;\epsilon-1;x)

{}_{2}F_{1}(\epsilon,-\epsilon;\epsilon-1;x)=1-\frac{x\epsilon^{2}}{\epsilon-1}+\frac{1}{2}x^{2}\epsilon(\epsilon+1)-\frac{1}{6}x^{3}((\epsilon-2)\epsilon(\epsilon+2))+O\left(x^{4}\right)

In this article, we focus on the series expansion of MHFs about its Pochhammer parameters as in Eq. (
2

It is well-known that the function
{}_{3}F_{2}(1,1,1;2,2;x)
can be written in terms of simpler function

{}_{3}F_{2}(1,1,1;2,2;x)=\frac{\text{Li}_{2}(x)}{x}

Nonetheless, such a reduction formulae for the KdF functions in Eq. (

There are some advantages in expressing the coefficients of the series expansion of MHFs (whenever possible) in terms of simpler functions such as MPLs

This motivates us to modify the algorithm presented in
Bera:2022ecn

In order to achieve this, we must perform the series expansion around integer values of Pochhammer parameters for most of the HFs, because the series expansion around rational values of parameters often leads to functions beyond MPLs.

We now recapitulate the algorithm proposed in

•

)

•

•

by a differential operator

\displaystyle F(\epsilon)=H(\epsilon)\bullet G(\epsilon)

•

Step 4
: Find the corresponding differential operator

•

We discuss each of the steps in detail.

2.1
Step 1
: Determination of the type of the series expansion

By observing the structure of the Pochhammer parameters in the series representation of a given MHF, one may find the type of the series expansion of the same. The series expansion of a MHF may be of Laurent series if any of the following situations appear

1.

When one or more lower Pochhammer parameters (i.e., Pochhammer parameters in the denominator) are of the form

\displaystyle(n+q\epsilon)_{p}

2.

When one or more upper Pochhammer parameters (i.e., Pochhammer parameters in the numerator) are of the form

\displaystyle(p+q\epsilon)_{n}

where
n
is non positive integer and
p
is non negative integer. We call a Pochhammer parameter
singular
if it satisfies any of the above two conditions. For instance, the series expansion of
{}_{2}F_{1}(1,1;-1+\epsilon;x)
is of Laurent type because of the presence of the lower Pochhammer parameter
(-1+\epsilon)_{p}
, where
p
is the summation index running over non-negative integer values.

However, the above two criteria do not guarantee the fact that, the series expansion of the corresponding MHF must be of Laurent type. The expansion of
{}_{2}F_{1}(\epsilon,-\epsilon;-1+\epsilon;x)
(given in Eq. (
2
)) is of Taylor type even though the first condition is satisfied. This is due to the presence of
\epsilon
dependent upper Pochhammer parameters. Thus, the above two criteria are necessary but not sufficient conditions.

2.2
Step 2
: Taylor expansion of MHF

In
Bera:2022ecn
, the Taylor expansion of a MHF is found by taking successive derivatives of the function with respect to its Pochhammer parameters, which results in higher summation fold MHFs in the series coefficients. In order to express the series coefficients in terms of MPLs, we modify this step.

To proceed, we define a HF with
n
variables having Pochhammer parameters

\displaystyle\textbf{F}:=F(\textbf{a};\textbf{b};\textbf{x})=\sum_{\textbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}}\frac{\Gamma(\textbf{a}+\mu.\textbf{m})/\Gamma(\textbf{a})}{\Gamma(\textbf{b}+\nu.\textbf{m})/\Gamma(\textbf{b})}\frac{\textbf{x}^{\textbf{m}}}{\textbf{m}!}=\sum_{\textbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}}\frac{(\textbf{a})_{\mu.\textbf{m}}}{(\textbf{b})_{\nu.\textbf{m}}}\frac{\textbf{x}^{\textbf{m}}}{\textbf{m}!}=\sum_{\textbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}}A(\textbf{m})\textbf{x}^{\textbf{m}}

We have used vector notation here;

•

•

•

\displaystyle P_{i}=\frac{A(\textbf{m}+\textbf{e}_{\textbf{i}})}{A(\textbf{m})}=\frac{g_{i}(\textbf{m})}{h_{i}(\textbf{m})},\hskip 28.45274pt\text{~}i=1,\dots,n

Step 3
: Construction of the secondary function

The secondary function
G(\epsilon)
related to

can be obtained by performing the following replacements of the Pochhammer parameters of

1.

When one or more lower Pochhammer parameters of

\displaystyle(n+q\epsilon)_{p}

2.

When one or more upper Pochhammer parameters of

\displaystyle(p+q\epsilon)_{n}

\displaystyle\rightarrow(q\epsilon)_{n}

As an example, the function

{}_{2}F_{1}(\epsilon,-\epsilon;1+\epsilon;x)

Thus obtained secondary function

2.4
Step 4
: The differential operator

There exist differential operators that relate two MHFs with Pochhammer parameters differed by integer values. In
Takayama1989
, a general algorithm based on Gröbner basis techniques is provided by Takayama. These differential operators play crucial role in the differential reduction of MHFs
Hyperdire
;
Hyperdire2
;
Hyperdire3
;

. For our purpose, we only need two types of differential operators; the step-down operator for the lower Pochhammer parameters and step-up operator for the upper Pochhammer parameters. These two types of operators can be easily obtained from the series representation of a MHF. For a MHF with
n
variables (Eq. (
8
)), the step-down operators for the lower Pochhammer parameters are given by

\displaystyle F(\textbf{a};\textbf{b};\textbf{x})=\frac{1}{b_{i}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nu_{ij}\theta_{x_{j}}+b_{i}\right)\bullet F(\textbf{a};\textbf{b}+\mathbf{e_{i}};\textbf{x})=H_{-}(b_{i})\bullet F(\textbf{a};\textbf{b}+\mathbf{e_{i}};\textbf{x})

(22)

and the step-up operators for the upper Pochhammer parameters can be found as

\displaystyle F(\textbf{a},\textbf{b},\textbf{x})=\frac{1}{a_{i}-1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{ij}\theta_{x_{j}}+a_{i}-1\right)\bullet F(\textbf{a}-\mathbf{e_{i}},\textbf{b},\textbf{x})=H_{+}(a_{i})\bullet F(\textbf{a}-\mathbf{e_{i}},\textbf{b},\textbf{x})

(23)

Here, as before, the bullet means the action of the operator
H_{\pm}
on a function. When required, the step-up and the step-down operators may be applied multiple times to increase or decrease any upper or lower Pochhammer parameter by suitable integer. In such cases, the product of the unit operators modulo the ideal generated by
L_{i}

\displaystyle H=\left[\prod_{i,j}H_{-}(b_{i})H_{+}(a_{j})\right]/\langle L_{1},\dots,L_{n}\rangle

(24)

2.5

: Action of the differential operator

In the final step, we apply the differential operator found in

. To proceed, we obtain the PDE associated with the Appell
F_{2}(a,b_{1},b_{2};c_{1},c_{2};x,y)
function

\displaystyle L_{1}

\displaystyle=-ab_{1}+\left(c_{1}-x\left(a+b_{1}+1\right)\right)\partial_{x}-b_{1}y\partial_{y}-xy\partial_{x}\partial_{y}-(x-1)x\partial_{x}^{2}

(40)

\displaystyle L_{2}

\displaystyle=-ab_{2}+\left(c_{2}-y\left(a+b_{2}+1\right)\right)\partial_{y}-b_{2}x\partial_{x}-xy\partial_{x}\partial_{y}-(y-1)y\partial_{y}^{2}

(41)

The operators
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
annihilate the Appell
F_{2}
function.

Next, using the vector

\displaystyle\textbf{g}=\left(F^{\prime},\theta_{x}F^{\prime},\theta_{y}F^{\prime},\theta_{x}\theta_{y}F^{\prime}\right)^{T}

(42)

the above set of PDEs is brought to the Pfaff system

\displaystyle d\textbf{g}=[\Omega_{1}dx+\Omega_{2}dy]\textbf{g}

(43)

where,

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&\frac{1}{x}&0&0\\
-\frac{1}{x-1}&\frac{\epsilon-2}{x-1}-\frac{\epsilon}{x}&-\frac{1}{x-1}&-\frac{1}{x-1}\\
0&0&0&\frac{1}{x}\\
\frac{\epsilon}{x-1}-\frac{\epsilon}{x+y-1}&\frac{(2-\epsilon)\epsilon}{x-1}-\frac{(2-\epsilon)\epsilon}{x+y-1}&\frac{\epsilon}{x-1}-\frac{2\epsilon+1}{x+y-1}&\frac{-\epsilon-2}{x+y-1}-\frac{-\epsilon x+(\epsilon+1)x-x-\epsilon}{(x-1)x}\\
\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&0&\frac{1}{y}&0\\
0&0&0&\frac{1}{y}\\
-\frac{\epsilon}{y-1}&-\frac{\epsilon}{y-1}&\frac{\epsilon}{y}+\frac{-2\epsilon-1}{y-1}&-\frac{1}{y-1}\\
\frac{\epsilon}{y-1}-\frac{\epsilon}{x+y-1}&\frac{\epsilon}{y-1}+\frac{\epsilon(\epsilon+1)-3\epsilon}{x+y-1}&\frac{2\epsilon+1}{y-1}-\frac{2\epsilon+1}{x+y-1}&\frac{-\epsilon-2}{x+y-1}+\frac{\epsilon}{y}+\frac{1}{y-1}\\
\end{array}\right)

Here
\theta_{x}=x\partial_{x}
and
\theta_{y}=y\partial_{y}
are Euler operators. Further, the Pfaff system is brought to the canonical form by the transformation matrix
T

\displaystyle T=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}\frac{1}{x-1}&0&0&0\\
\frac{2\epsilon-x}{(x-1)^{2}}&\frac{\epsilon}{x-1}&-\frac{x\epsilon}{(x-1)(x+y-1)}&0\\
-\frac{\epsilon}{x-1}&0&\frac{\epsilon}{x+y-1}&0\\
\frac{\epsilon\left(x^{2}+(y-1)x-2y\epsilon\right)}{(x-1)^{2}(x+y-1)}&-\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{x-1}&\frac{x\epsilon(-x+y\epsilon+1)}{(x-1)(x+y-1)^{2}}&\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{x+y-1}\\
\end{array}\right)

Finally, the system is solved order by order in
\epsilon
with the boundary condition

\displaystyle\textbf{g}(x=0,y=0)=(1,0,0,0)^{T}

(56)

which is valid for all orders in
\epsilon
. Thus we find the series expansion of
F^{\prime}
to be

\displaystyle F^{\prime}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{x-1}+\epsilon\Bigg[\frac{(G(1-y;x)-2G(1;x)+G(1;y))}{x-1}\Bigg]+\epsilon^{2}\frac{1}{x-1}\Bigg[2G(1;x)G(1;y)

\displaystyle-2G(1;y)G(1-y;x)-G(0,1-y;x)+2G(1,1-y;x)+2G(1-y,1;x)

\displaystyle-G(1-y,1-y;x)+2G(0,1;x)-4G(1,1;x)+G(0,1;y)-2G(1,1;y)\Bigg]+O(\epsilon^{3})

(57)

Next in
step 4
, we find the differential operator that relates
F
with
F^{\prime}

\displaystyle F=H\bullet F^{\prime}

(58)

Using the package
HYPERDIRE

Hyperdire
or by constructing the step-down operators, we obtain

\displaystyle H=1+\frac{1}{\epsilon}\theta_{x}-\frac{1}{\epsilon}\theta_{y}-\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\theta_{x}\theta_{y}

(59)

In the final
step 5
, we perform the action of the operator
H
on the series expansion of
F^{\prime}
to find the Laurent series expansion of
F
,

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\frac{x}{\epsilon}\left(\frac{2}{(x-1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(x+y-1)^{2}}\right)+\epsilon^{0}\Bigg[x\left(\frac{4}{(x-1)^{2}}-\frac{2}{(x+y-1)^{2}}\right)G(1;x)

\displaystyle+2x\left(\frac{1}{(x+y-1)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(x-1)^{2}}\right)G(1;y)+x\left(\frac{1}{(x+y-1)^{2}}-\frac{2}{(x-1)^{2}}\right)G(1-y;x)

\displaystyle-\frac{(x+2y-1)(x(2x+3y-3)-y+1)}{(x-1)^{2}(x+y-1)^{2}}\Bigg]+O(\epsilon)

(60)

We crosschecked the above result numerically and it is found to be consistent.

4
Application to the Feynman integrals

In
Anastasiou:1999ui
, the one loop three-point function with two massive propagators and one off-shell leg is expressed in terms of double variable HFs using the NDIM method. The authors presented several HF representations (Eqs. (3.23) - (3.27) and Eq. (3.18) of the abovementioned article) for the same Feynman integral, which are related by analytic continuations. Furthermore, starting with a HF representation valid in a certain kinematic region (namely, region IIb ) and using the integral representations of MHFs, the authors found the series expansion of the Feynman integral in Eq. (3.34) of
Anastasiou:1999ui
.

In principle, any analytic continuation of the Feynman integral can be used to find the series expansion. In this Section, we start with the HF representation of the Feynman integral valid in region IIIb, which contains Kampé de Fériet and Horn function, and find the series expansion using the
MultiHypExp
package. By doing so, besides validating their results, we also examine if series expansions from different analytic continuations agrees or not.

In region IIIb, the Feynman integral can be expressed as

\displaystyle I_{3}^{D}\left(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3};Q_{1}^{2},0,0,M_{1}^{2},M_{2}^{2},0\right)=I_{3}^{\left\{m_{2},q_{1}\right\}}+I_{3}^{\left\{p_{1},q_{1}\right\}}

(61)

where

\displaystyle I_{3}^{\left\{m_{2},q_{1}\right\}}=(-1)^{\frac{D}{2}}\left(-M_{1}^{2}\right)^{\frac{D}{2}-\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}-\nu_{3}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-\frac{D}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{D}{2}-\nu_{2}-\nu_{3}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\nu_{1}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\times S_{1}\left(\nu_{2},\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-\frac{D}{2},\nu_{3},1+\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-\frac{D}{2},\frac{D}{2},-\frac{Q_{1}^{2}}{M_{1}^{2}},\frac{M_{2}^{2}}{M_{1}^{2}}\right)\text{, }

(62)

\displaystyle I_{3}^{\left\{p_{1},q_{1}\right\}}=(-1)^{\frac{D}{2}}\left(-M_{1}^{2}\right)^{-\nu_{1}}\left(-M_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{D}{2}-\nu_{2}-\nu_{3}}\frac{\Gamma\left(\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-\frac{D}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{D}{2}-\nu_{3}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\nu_{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\times H_{2}\left(\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}-\frac{D}{2},\nu_{3},\nu_{1},\frac{D}{2}-\nu_{3},\frac{D}{2},\frac{Q_{1}^{2}}{M_{2}^{2}},-\frac{M_{2}^{2}}{M_{1}^{2}}\right)

(63)

Here, the
\nu_{i}
’s denote the powers of the propagators and
S_{1}
is KdF function and Horn
H_{2}
function. In terms of our notation of KdF functions (see Appendix
B
)

\displaystyle S_{1}(a_{1},a_{2},b,c,d,x,y)=\text{KdF}^{2:1;0}_{1:1;0}\left[\begin{array}[]{c@{{}:{}}c@{;{}}c}a_{1},a_{2}&b&{-}\mkern-7.0mu\leaders{\hbox{$\mkern-2.0mu-\mkern-2.0mu$}}{\hfill}\mkern-7.0mu{-}\\[4.30554pt]
c&d&{-}\mkern-7.0mu\leaders{\hbox{$\mkern-2.0mu-\mkern-2.0mu$}}{\hfill}\mkern-7.0mu{-}\end{array}\;\middle|\;x,y\right]

To proceed, we set the dimension
D=4-2\epsilon
and the powers of the propagators to unity (i.e.
\nu_{i}=1,i=1,2,3
). The KdF function
S_{1}
takes a simpler form

\displaystyle S_{1}(1,\epsilon+1,1,\epsilon+1,2-\epsilon,x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(1)_{m}(1)_{m+n}}{(2-\epsilon)_{m}}\frac{x^{m}y^{n}}{m!n!}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{y-1}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(1,1;2-\epsilon;-\frac{x}{y-1}\right)

(64)

The second identity is obtained by performing the summation of the index
n
.

On the other hand, the Horn
H_{2}
function takes the form

\displaystyle H_{2}(\epsilon,1,1,1-\epsilon,2-\epsilon,x,y)

(65)

We can easily find the series expansion of the Gauss
{}_{2}F_{1}
(Eq. (
4
)) and Horn
H_{2}
function (Eq. (
65
)) in
\epsilon
using the presented package (see Section
5.3
), which reads

\displaystyle S_{1}(1,\epsilon+1

\displaystyle,1,\epsilon+1,2-\epsilon,x,y)=-\frac{1}{x}G\left(1;\frac{x}{1-y}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{\epsilon}{x}\Bigg[G\left(1;\frac{x}{1-y}\right)-G\left(0,1;\frac{x}{1-y}\right)+G\left(1,1;\frac{x}{1-y}\right)\Bigg]+O\left(\epsilon^{2}\right)

(66)

\displaystyle H_{2}(\epsilon,1,

\displaystyle 1,1-\epsilon,2-\epsilon,x,y)=-\frac{G\left(\frac{1}{y}+1;x\right)}{xy}

\displaystyle+\frac{\epsilon}{xy}\Bigg(G\left(\frac{y+1}{y};x\right)-G\left(0,\frac{y+1}{y};x\right)

\displaystyle+G\left(\frac{y+1}{y},1;x\right)+G\left(\frac{y+1}{y},\frac{y+1}{y};x\right)\Bigg)+O(\epsilon^{2})

(67)

Using the above two expressions, we find the series expansion of the Feynman integral in terms of MPLs as

\displaystyle I_{3}^{D}

\displaystyle(1,1,1;Q_{1}^{2},0,0,M_{1}^{2},M_{2}^{2},0)=\frac{1}{Q_{1}^{2}}\Bigg[G\left(0,1;-\frac{Q_{1}^{2}}{M_{1}^{2}-M_{2}^{2}}\right)-G\left(0,1-\frac{M_{1}^{2}}{M_{2}^{2}};\frac{Q_{1}^{2}}{M_{2}^{2}}\right)

(68)

\displaystyle-G\left(1,1;-\frac{Q_{1}^{2}}{M_{1}^{2}-M_{2}^{2}}\right)+G\left(1-\frac{M_{1}^{2}}{M_{2}^{2}},1;\frac{Q_{1}^{2}}{M_{2}^{2}}\right)+G\left(1-\frac{M_{1}^{2}}{M_{2}^{2}},1-\frac{M_{1}^{2}}{M_{2}^{2}};\frac{Q_{1}^{2}}{M_{2}^{2}}\right)

(69)

\displaystyle-\left(\log\left(-M_{1}^{2}\right)+\gamma+i\pi\right)G\left(1;-\frac{Q_{1}^{2}}{M_{1}^{2}-M_{2}^{2}}\right)+\left(\log\left(-M_{2}^{2}\right)+\gamma+i\pi\right)G\left(1-\frac{M_{1}^{2}}{M_{2}^{2}};\frac{Q_{1}^{2}}{M_{2}^{2}}\right)\Bigg]

(70)

which can further be written in terms of ordinary polylogarithms
as

\displaystyle I_{3}^{D}(1,1,1;Q_{1}^{2},0,0,M_{1}^{2},

\displaystyle M_{2}^{2},0)=\frac{1}{Q_{1}^{2}}\Bigg(\text{Li}_{1,1}\left(1-\frac{M_{1}^{2}}{M_{2}^{2}},-\frac{Q_{1}^{2}}{M_{1}^{2}-M_{2}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle+\text{Li}_{1}\left(-\frac{Q_{1}^{2}}{M_{1}^{2}-M_{2}^{2}}\right)\Big[\text{Li}_{1}\left(1+M_{2}^{2}\right)-\text{Li}_{1}\left(1+M_{1}^{2}\right)\Big]\Bigg)+O(\epsilon)

(71)

The result obtained above in Eq. (
4
) matches with Eq. (3.34) of
Anastasiou:1999ui
by analytic continuations. Thus we conclude that the series expansion coefficients of different analytic continuations of a Feynman integral are related by analytic continuations. In other words, the process of finding analytic continuation and the process of obtaining the series expansion of MHF commute with each other.

6
Mathematica

The presented method in Section
2
is implemented in the form of a
Mathematica

Mathematicav11.3
package
MultiHypExp
, which consists of two commands,
SeriesExpand
and

. The former command can be used to find the series expansion of a given MHF, and the latter can be used to yield reduction formulae of MHFs. The usage of these commands is demonstrated in Section

Mathematica
.

6.1
The
SeriesExpand
command

Let us start with the
SeriesExpand
command. In Section
2
, the procedure of finding the series expansion is divided into five parts.

•

Step 1
and
Step 3
: In the first step (Section
2.1
), the type of the series expansion of the given MHF is determined by its Pochhammer structure, and the singular Pochhammer parameters (if exist) are replaced by the non-singular ones according to the prescription given in
Step 3
(Section
2.3
). This is achieved using the
Mathematica
’s pattern matching and replacement rules commands.

•

Step 2
: The Taylor series of MHF is found in this step. At first, the PDE associated with the given MHF is obtained and brought to the Pfaffian form.

1.

When the
SeriesExpand
command is called with the input argument

FunctionName
(i.e., the first form in Section
5.1
), the pre-stored Pfaff systems of double variable Appell
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}
and Horn
H_{2}
and triple variable Lauricella
F_{A},F_{B},F_{D},F_{K}
,
F_{M}
,
F_{N}
, and
F_{S}
are used.

2.

When the
SeriesExpand
command is called with the input argument
exp
(i.e., in the second form in Section
5.1
), the PDE associated with the MHF is calculated using the expressions given in Eq. (
9
) and Eq. (
10
). Further, the system of PDE is brought to Pfaffian form using the Gröbner basis calculations, which is carried out by the
Mathematica
package
HolonomicFunctions

KoutschanPhD
;
koutschan2010fast
. It may be noted that, there are computer programs which allow one to perform the Gröbner basis calculations, e.g., Singular
DGPS
, Macaulay2
M2
, Maple
Chyzak1998
. However, we opt to utilize
HolonomicFunctions
in order to maintain the homogeneity of the package within the scope of
Mathematica
.

Then the Pfaffian system, wherever possible, is further brought to the canonical form using the
Mathematica
package
CANONICA

Meyer:2017joq
and solved using proper boundary condition and expressed in terms of MPLs. Extensive use of commands of
PolyLogTools

Duhr:2019tlz
are made to integrate and simplify the expressions containing MPLs.

•

Step 4
: The differential operator that relates the given MHF and the associated secondary function is found in this step. The

Hyperdire
;
Hyperdire3

22
) and Eq. (
23

HolonomicFunctions
package.

•

Step 5
: Finally, the obtained differential operator is made to act on the Taylor series of the secondary function. As the Taylor expansion coefficients contain MPLs, the
PolyLogTools
commands are used to calculate the derivatives and simplify those expressions.

The series expansion of other double variable Appell-Horn functions (i.e.,
F_{4},H_{1},H_{3}
,
H_{4}
,

It is possible to find the reduction formulae of MHFs as a byproduct of the methodology. For a given MHF, one can find a secondary MHF, whose Taylor expansion can be readily calculated and the differential operator associated with these two functions can be made to act on the series expansion of the secondary function to find the reduction of the given MHF. An example of such reduction of Appell
F_{2}
function is given in Appendix

. This procedure is implemented in the

,
F_{2}
,
F_{3}
,
F_{4}
and triple variable Lauricella

and
F_{S}
functions. The required differential operators are procured using the

Hyperdire
;
Hyperdire2
packages, and the Taylor expansions of the abovementioned MHFs from Appendix

are stored in the package and are called accordingly to fulfill the purpose. It should be noted that, the current version of the package does not provide reduction formulae for other Horn or Lauricella functions.

6.3
Comments on the implementation

This section addresses various observations concerning the current version of the
Mathematica
implementation.

1.

In principle, the procedure mentioned in
Bera:2022ecn
can be applied to find the series expansion of any MHFs. However, the package
MultiHypExp
is made with the objective of expressing the series coefficients in terms of well-known MPLs. This, not only restricts the reach of the package to the small number of double- and triple-variable HFs, but also restricts the Pochhammer parameters to integers for most of the accessible HFs. Thus, the Appell
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}
, and Horn
H_{2}
and triple variable Lauricella
F_{A}
,
F_{B}
,
F_{D}
,
F_{K}
,
F_{M}
,
F_{N}
, and
F_{S}
functions can be expanded in series around the integer values of their Pochhammer parameters.

2.

Since the series expansion of other double variable Appell-Horn functions (i.e.,
F_{4}
,
H_{1}
,
H_{3}
,
H_{4}
,
H_{6}
and
H_{7}
) are calculated using their connection formulae to Appell
F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}
or Horn
H_{2}
, the parameter
c
in
H_{4}
,
d
in
H_{7}
must be of the form
p/2+q\epsilon
, where
p
is integer or half integer, so that, the
F_{2}
and
H_{2}
function appearing on the right-hand side of their corresponding connection formula (see Appendix
D
), can be expanded in series around integer values of parameters.

3.

To the best of our knowledge, there do not exist connection formulae of Appell
F_{4}
, Horn
H_{1}
and
H_{5}
with general parameters to other Appell-Horn functions. Thus, the package can find the series expansion of Appell
F_{4}
and Horn
H_{1}
with restricted Pochhammer parameters (see Appendix
D
).

4.

The package in its current version can find, at most, the first six series coefficients. This limitation comes from the
ToFibrationBasis
command of
PolyLogTools
as it can handle MPLs with weight up to five. This restriction can be lifted with the introduction of the newer version of
PolyLogTooLs
when it becomes available.

5.

In certain situations, a non-linear change of variables is required to rationalize algebraic functions appearing in the Pfaffian system, which has not been implemented in the present version of the package. This topic has been studied in the literature (see e.g.,
Besier:2018jen
). We intend to incorporate these into our future work.

6.

On an ordinary personal computer, the process of finding series expansion takes a few minutes for double variable HFs. However, the computational time significantly increases when triple variable HFs are considered, as most of the time is devoted to bringing the Pfaffian system into canonical form.

7.

The series expansion of a given MHF is not applicable on its singular locus.

8.

Since the series expansion of the Appell
F_{4}
function with general values of Pochhammer parameters can not be found using this package, the
ReduceFunction
command can only find the reduction formulae of
F_{4}
for restricted parameters.

7
Summary and Conclusions

In this work, we have provided an implementation of an algorithm to perform series expansion of MHFs. The implemented algorithm is a slight modification of the one presented in
Bera:2022ecn
. The modifications are made for the requirement of expressing the series coefficients in terms of MPLs, rather than higher summation-fold MHFs. We have utilized publicly available packages as dependencies to build the package
MultiHypExp
, which, in its current version, is suitable for finding the series expansion of certain one, two, and three-variable HFs around the integer values of parameters. The restriction of the (integer-valued) parameters arises as we choose to express the series coefficients in terms of MPLs, which offers immediate numerical evaluation using well-established computer programs. We have described the steps of the algorithms in detail and explained how the dependencies are employed to accomplish those steps.
We have provided examples of the series expansion of MHFs that typically appears in Feynman integral calculus.
The package also allows one to find reduction formulae of certain MHFs with integer values of Pochhammer parameters to simpler functions.

In some cases, the MHFs are needed to series-expand around rational values of parameters, which may require functions beyond MPLs. The current version of the package is not suitable for that. We plan to explore the possibilities in near future.

Multiple polylogarithms
goncharov2001multiple

\displaystyle G(a_{1},\dots,a_{n};z)=\int_{0}^{z}\frac{dt}{t-a_{1}}G(a_{2},\dots,a_{n};t)

(73)

and

These can also be defined as nested sums,

\displaystyle\text{Li}_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{k}}\left(z_{1},\ldots,z_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<n_{1}<n_{2}<\cdots<n_{k}}\frac{z_{1}^{n_{1}}z_{2}^{n_{2}}\cdots z_{k}^{n_{k}}}{n_{1}^{m_{1}}n_{2}^{m_{2}}\cdots n_{k}^{m_{k}}}

Appendix E
Reduction formulae of MHFs

It is possible write a MHF with positive integer values of Pochhammer parameters in terms of simpler functions. These useful reduction formulae have applications in mathematics and physics.
As pointed out in
Hyperdire2
, some reduction formulae of Appell and Lauricella-Saran functions can be immediately derived using the results of their series expansions (Appendix
C
) and the differential reduction formulae from
HYPERDIRE

Hyperdire
;
Hyperdire2
packages. For instance,

\displaystyle F_{2}(1+\epsilon,1+\epsilon

\displaystyle,1+\epsilon;2+\epsilon,2+\epsilon;x,y)=

\displaystyle\Big[\frac{\epsilon+1}{x\epsilon^{2}}\theta_{x}+\frac{\epsilon+1}{y\epsilon^{2}}\theta_{y}+\frac{(\epsilon+1)(x+y-1)}{xy\epsilon^{3}}\theta_{y}\theta_{x}\Bigg]\bullet F_{2}(\epsilon,\epsilon,\epsilon;1+\epsilon,1+\epsilon;x,y)

(158)

Using the series expansion of the
F_{2}
from Appendix
C
, we find the right-hand side to be

\displaystyle F_{2}(1+\epsilon,1+\epsilon

\displaystyle,1+\epsilon;2+\epsilon,2+\epsilon;x,y)=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(x+y-1)(G(1;x)-G(1-y;x))}{xy}-\frac{G(1;x)}{x}-\frac{G(1;y)}{y}\right)+O\left(\epsilon^{1}\right)

(159)

Finally, setting
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, the reduction formula of Appell
F_{2}
reads

\displaystyle F_{2}(1,1,1;2,2;x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{(x+y-1)(G(1,x)-G(1-y,x))}{xy}-\frac{G(1,x)}{x}-\frac{G(1,y)}{y}

(160)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(1-x)\log(1-x)}{xy}-\frac{(x+y-1)\log\left(1-\frac{x}{1-y}\right)}{xy}-\frac{\log(1-y)}{y}

(161)

The above result differs from the expression obtained in Eq. (A.9) of
F2reduction
. However, we found that Eq. (
160
) is numerically consistent.

, Lauricella - Saran

and

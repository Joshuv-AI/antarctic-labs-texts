A noncommutative maximal inequality for Fejér means on totally disconnected non-Abelian groups
Fugui Ding, Guixiang Hong, Xumin Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we explore Fourier analysis for noncommutative L p L_{p} space-valued functions on G G , where G G is a totally disconnected non-abelian compact group. By additionally assuming that the value of these functions remains invariant within each conjugacy class, we establish a noncommutative maximal inequality for Fejér means utilizing the associated character system of G G . This is an operator-valued version of the classical result due to Gát in [ Gát06 ] . We follow essentially the classical sketch, but due to the noncommutativity, many classical arguments have to be revised. Notably, compared to the classical results. the bounds of our estimates are explicity calculated.



-

1.
Background and the main result

Over the past two decades, there has been significant attention on totally disconnected groups, especially in non-abelian cases. These groups have found applications in various areas, for instance, in topological group theory, Lie group theory, and operator algebras (see e.g.
[
Bur18
,
CM18
,
CW21
,
IS07
,
Rau19
,
RZ10
,
SS13
,
Laz65
,
Glö18
,
BM00a
,
BM00b
,
Pal01
,
SW13
,
Wil15
,
Wil98
]
).

In the context of Fourier analysis, our interest lies in the pointwise convergence of the character system of totally disconnected
compact
groups with coefficients in noncommutative
L_{p}
-spaces throughout this paper. The character system arise canonically from the fact that any totally disconnected compact group can be realized as the complete direct product of its factor structures (see e.g.
[
Pon66
]
).
In other words, a totally disconnected compact group can be constructed as follows.
Consider a sequence of number
m:=(m_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
where
m_{k}\geq 2,k\in\mathbb{N}
and denote
G_{k}
as finite groups of order
m_{k}
. Each group
G_{k}
is equipped with the discrete topology and the normlized Haar measure
\mu_{k}
such that
\mu_{k}(G_{k})=1
, that is, the measure of every singleton of each
G_{k}
is
{1}/{m_{k}}
. The totally disconnected compact group
G
is composed of the complete direct product of
G_{k}
and equipped with product topology, operation and the corresponding product measure
\mu
. We denote it by

G:=\bigtimes_{k=0}^{\infty}G_{k}.

By the definition of operation, it is evident that
G
is non-abelian if and only if there exists at least one
G_{k}
that is non-ablien. Denote
\rho(G):=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}m_{k}
. The group
G
is called bounded if
\rho(G)<\infty
and unbounded otherwise.

Let
p_{k}
denote the number of conjugacy classes in the group
G_{k}
. Define

P_{0}:=1,P_{k+1}=p_{k}P_{k}
.
Then there is a unique expansion for each natural number
n
,

(1.1)

n=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}n_{k}P_{k},\qquad 0\leq n_{k}\leq p_{k}-1.

Denote by

r_{k}^{0}(x_{k})=1,\quad r_{k}^{1}(x_{k}),\cdots,r_{k}^{p_{k}-1}(x_{k}),

the irreducible characters of the group
G_{k}
.
The domain of
r_{k}^{d}
extends to the whole group
G
by

r_{k}^{d}(x):=r_{k}^{d}(x_{k}),\qquad d\in\{0,\cdots,p_{k}-1\},\quad G\ni x=(x_{k})_{k\geq 0}.

Now the family of irreducible characters of
G
can be indexed by natural numbers as following: for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, using the unique expansion (
1.1
), we define

\chi_{n}(x)=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}r_{k}^{n_{k}}(x).

Based on these characters, one may define the Dirichlet/Fejér means for an integrable function
f
that is
constant
in each conjugacy class:

(1.2)

\displaystyle S_{n}f(y):=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{f}(k)\chi_{k}(y)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\sigma_{n}f(y):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}S_{k}f(y),

where the Fourier coefficients
(\widehat{f}(k))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are defined by
\widehat{f}(k):=\int_{G}f(x)\bar{\chi}_{k}d\mu(x).

When the group
G
is abelian, the pointwise convergence problem of the Dirichlet/Fejér means has been well studied. For instance, in the case where
G_{k}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for all
k\geq 0
, the resulting character system is the well-known Walsh system. In this scenario, the pointwise convergence of Fejér means of integrable functions was established by Fine in
[
Fin55
]
(see also Schipp
[
Sch75
]
for alternative proof), while that of Dirichlet means for square integrable functions was established by Billard
[
Bil67
]
after Carleson’s resolution of the famous Lusin conjecture for Fourier series on the circle.
The Walsh system was generalized to
p
-series fields (
G_{k}=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
where
p
is a prime), Vilenkin systems (
G_{k}=\mathbb{Z}_{m}
where
m
is any fixed integer) and general bounded Vilenkin systems (
G_{k}=\mathbb{Z}_{m_{k}}
).
Many Mathematicals have explored Fourier analysis on these system. For example, Pal and Simon
[
PS77
]
extended Taibleson’s result
[
Tai67
]
on pointwise convergence of Fejér means for integrable functions over
p
-series fields to all the bounded Vilenkin systems. Moreover, Gosselim
[
Gos73
]
obtained the almost everywhere (abbreviated as
a.e.
) convergence of Dirichlet means for functions in
L_{p}
with
p>1
for all bounded Vilenkin systems.
However, the problems on the unbounded Vilenkin systems become slightly more complicated. Firstly, Price’s result
[
Pri57
]
indicates that one can expect only the
a.e.
convergence even for the Fejér means of continuous functions. After Young’s result
[
You96
]
on
a.e.
convergence of any lacunary Dirichlet means, Gát
[
Gát99
]
obtained the
a.e.
convergence of the Fejér means for functions in
L_{p}
with
p>1
. Later on, Gát
[
Gát03
]
extended his previous result to the case
p=1
but only for canonical lacunary Fejér means. The
a.e.
convergence of Dirichlet means for
p>1
and that of Fejér means for
p=1
seem to remain open for unbounded Vilenkin systems.

In the non-abelian case of
G
, the problem of pointwise convergence becomes quite subtle with only one positive non-trivial result as far as the authors are aware of. The reason behind this complexity might be explained as follows: most of the harmonic analysis methods for abelian groups rely on the fact that the absolute value of characters is
1
. However, in non-abelien groups, the resulting character might be unbounded or could even take a value of zero. This was illustrated in
[
Gát06
, Page 29]
for the special case where
G_{k}=S_{3}
is the permutation group for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
In the aforementioned paper
[
Gát06
]
, Gát obtained
the only positive result
in the literature, namely, the Fejér means is
a.e.
convergent for square integrable functions that are constant on each conjugacy class. Additionally, in the same paper, he established that for any
1<p<2
, there exist some bounded non-abelian group
G
and some
f\in L_{p}(G)
such that the Fejér means of
f
is not
a.e.
convergent.

On the other hand, within the realm of noncommutative harmonic analysis, motivated by operator algebra, quantum probability and noncommutative ergodic theory, there has been extensive research on maximal inequalities and the almost uniform convergence. For a more detailed exploration of the development of noncommutative maximal inequalities, readers may find valuable insights in
[
Jun02
,
JX07
,
HLW21
,
HWWb
,
HLX23
,
HRW23
,
CXY13
]
.

Motivated by all above, we aim to establish a noncommutative analogue of Gát’s result
[
Gát06
]
for any totally disconnected compact bounded group. More precisely, we will establish a noncommutative maximal inequality for Fejér means of square integrable functions with values in noncommutative
L_{2}
-space, and subsequently deduce the almost uniform convergence in a standard manner.

In order to state the main result, we introduce additional notations. Consider a semifinite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
equipped with a normal semifinite faithful trace
\tau
. The associated noncommutative
L_{p}
-spaces are denoted by
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
. The vector-valued noncommutative
L_{p}
-spaces
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})
will enable us to formulate
L_{p}
-norm of the classical maximal function. Our focus in this paper is primarily on the tensor von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}=L_{\infty}(G,\mu)\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
with the canonical tensor trace. Any element in
L_{p}(\mathcal{N})
can be viewed as an
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
-valued function that is
p
-integrable on
G
. Let
S_{\mathcal{M}}
be the dense
\ast
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{M}
in which the trace of the support of every element is finite. For
S_{\mathcal{M}}
-valued simple function
f
that is constant in each conjugacy class, the Fejér means can be defined without any ambiguity as following

\sigma_{n}f(y):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}S_{k}f(y).

This definition then extends to the
L_{2}(\mathcal{N})
spaces thanks to the Hilbert-valued Plancherel theorem.

Because any character cannot distinguish between group elements within the same conjugacy class, in this paper,
we exclusively focus on functions that keep a constant value within each conjugacy class.

Our main result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
f\in L_{2}(\mathcal{N})
. There is a universal constant
C
such that

\left\|(\sigma_{n}f)_{n}\right\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{N};\ell_{\infty})}\leq C\rho(G)^{2}\|f\|_{2},

and
\sigma_{n}f\rightarrow f
bilaterally almost uniformly as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Recalling
\rho(G)=\sup_{k\geq 0}m_{k}
, it becomes intriguing to explore whether a similar maximal inequality holds for unbounded totally disconnected non-abelian groups, namely when
\rho(G)=\infty
. In a work in progress
[
HWWa
]
, inspired by geometric group theory, especially quantum groups, we are able to provide the Fejér type means for all the totally disconnected compact groups that uphold the noncommutative maximal inequalities for all
1<p\leq\infty
.

We will focus on the proof of the maximal inequality in Theorem
1.1
, since the bilateral uniform convergence follows from a standard density argument in the noncommutative setting (refer to, e.g.
[
CXY13
,
HWWb
]
) combined with the well-known fact that the character system constitutes a complete orthonormal basis. For this reason, we will not revisit the definition of bilateral almost uniform convergence here and refer the reader to e.g.
[
JX07
]
for information.

2.
Preliminaries and the key intermediate estimates

In this section, we first recall some preliminaries in noncommutative analysis, and then present the key intermediate estimates crucial for proving of Theorem
1.1
.

2.1.
Vector-valued non-commutative
L_{p}
-spaces

Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be a von Neumann algebra equipped with a normal semifinite faithful trace
\tau
, and
S_{{\mathcal{M}}}^{+}
be the set of all positive
x\in{\mathcal{M}}
such that

\tau(s(x))<\infty,
where
s(x)
denotes the support of
x
, that is, the least projection
e\in{\mathcal{M}}
such that
exe=x.

Define
S_{\mathcal{M}}
as the linear span of
S_{{\mathcal{M}}}^{+}
. For any
p\in(0,\,\infty)
, we define

\|x\|_{p}:=(\tau|x|^{p})^{1/p},\ \ \ x\in S_{\mathcal{M}},

where
|x|:=(x^{*}x)^{1/2}
. The completion
of
(S_{\mathcal{M}},\|\cdot\|_{p})
is denoted by
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})
which is the usual noncommutative
L_{p}
-space
associated with
({\mathcal{M}},\,\tau)
. For
p=\infty
, we typically define
L_{\infty}({\mathcal{M}})={\mathcal{M}}
equipped with the operator norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{M}}}
.
Let
L_{p}^{+}({\mathcal{M}})
denote the positive part of
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})
. Recalling that
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
is a Hilbert space with respect to inner product
\langle x,\,y\rangle=\tau(x^{*}y)
. We refer to
[
PX03
]
for more information on noncommutative
L_{p}
-spaces.

Next we recall briefly the definition and fundamental properties of the vector-valued noncommutative
L_{p}
-space
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{\infty})
and refer the reader to
[
Pis93
,
Jun02
,
JX07
]
for more details. Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
. This space consists of all the sequences
x=(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
in
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}})
which admit a factorization of the following form: there are
a,b
and a bounded sequence
y=(y_{k})_{k\geq 1}\subset L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
such that

x_{k}=ay_{k}b,\quad\forall k\geq 1.

We then define

\|(x_{k})_{k}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})}=\inf\left\{\sup_{k\geq 1}\|y_{k}\|_{\infty}\|a\|_{2p}\|b\|_{2p}\right\}.

where the infimum is taken over all factorizations of
x
as above. More general, for any index set
\mathcal{I}
, we can define
L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{\infty}(\mathcal{I}))
and

\|(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})}=\inf_{x_{\lambda}=ay_{\lambda}b}\left\{\sup_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}}\|y_{\lambda}\|_{\infty}\|a\|_{2p}\|b\|_{2p}\right\}.

It has been checked in
[
JX07
]
that if
x\in L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{\infty}(\mathcal{I}))
, then

\|(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})}=\sup_{\Lambda\subset\mathcal{I}}\left\{\left\|(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\right\|_{p}:\Lambda\penalty\ \text{is}\penalty\ \text{finite}\right\}.

Remark 2.1
.

In
[
JX07
]
, the authors also provided an intuitive description of the maximal norm for
x=(x_{k})_{k}\subset L_{p}^{+}({\mathcal{M}})
. Precisely,
x\in L_{p}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{\infty})
if and only if there exists some
y\in L_{p}^{+}({\mathcal{M}})
such that
x_{k}\leq y
for all
k\geq 1,

and moreover

\left\|(x_{k})_{k}\right\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})}=\inf\{\|y\|_{p}:x_{k}\leq y\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall k\geq 1\}.

For this reason, we will often use the notation
\left\|{\sup_{k}}^{+}x_{k}\right\|_{p}

to represent
\|(x_{k})_{k}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{\infty})}
. However, it is important to caution the reader that
\left\|{\sup_{k}}^{+}x_{k}\right\|_{p}
is just a notation since
\sup_{k}x_{k}
may not make sense in the noncommutative setting.

Remark 2.2
.

From Remark
2.1
, it follows easily that
0\leq x_{n}\leq y_{n}
implies

\left\|{\sup_{n}}^{+}x_{n}\right\|_{p}\leq\left\|{\sup_{n}}^{+}y_{n}\right\|_{p},

which will be used frequently in this paper.

2.2.
Martingale structure and the key estimates

In this subsection, we recall some necessary preliminaries for proving Theorem
1.1
(see e.g.
[
Gát06
,
GT96
,
Gát99
]
). Specifically, we include the construction of a martingale structure on the totally disconnected compact groups which will reduce Theorem
1.1
to an estimate for the “dyadic block", as detailed in Proposition
2.3
.

Consider
y=(y_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
as an element in
G
, where
y_{k}\in G_{k}
. We define
I_{0}(y)=G
and for
k\geq 1
,

I_{k}(y):=\{x\in G:x_{j}=y_{j},0\leq j\leq k-1\}.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{k}
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\{I_{k}(x):x\in G\}
, and
E_{k}
be the associated conditional expectation.
It is a well-known fact that
E_{k}
’s extend to conditional expectations associated to the von Neumann subalgebras
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{k})\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
’s and we continue to denote them by
E_{k}
without causing confusion. The resulting martingale difference is denoted by
d_{k}=E_{k}-E_{k-1}
with convention
E_{-1}=0
. Then from Doob’s maximal inequalities for noncommutative martingales
[
Jun02
]
, one has

\left\|{\sup_{k}}^{+}E_{k}f\right\|_{p}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{p},\quad\forall f\in L_{p}(\mathcal{N}),

where
C_{p}
is a constant only depending on
p
.
In particular, for
p=2
, after checking the related arguments in
[
Jun02
, Lemma 3.1, Lemma 3.9 and Remark 5.5]
, one gets

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\|{\sup_{k}}^{+}E_{k}f\right\|_{2}\leq 2\|f\|_{2},\quad\forall f\in L_{2}(\mathcal{N}).

Moreover, due to the orthogonality of martingale differences, we also have

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left\|\Big(\sum_{k}|d_{k}f|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{2}=\left\|\Big(\sum_{k}|(d_{k}f)^{*}|^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{2}=\|f\|_{2}.

For
1<p\neq 2<\infty
, a noncommutative version of Burkholder-Gundy inequality is presented in
[
PX97
]
, but it will not be utilized in this paper. For this reason,
in the sequel we will only state all necessary estimates for the case
p=2
.

By exploiting the martingale estimates (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), Theorem
1.1
will be reduced to the following estimate for each “dyadic block". For the details of the reduction, we refer to Section
3
. Recalling that
n=\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}n_{k}P_{k}
, we define

|n|:=\max\{k:n_{k}\not=0\},

representing the largest index
k
for which
n_{k}
is non-zero.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
A\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in L_{2}(\mathcal{N})
, then we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\sigma_{n}f\right\|_{2}\leq 640\rho(G)^{2}\|f\|_{2}.

Recall that
P_{n}
is defined by
P_{0}=1,P_{k+1}=p_{k}P_{k}
. It follows that
P_{|n|}\leq n<P_{|n|+1}
, thus

\{n\in\mathbb{N}:|n|=A\}=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:P_{A}\leq n<P_{A+1}\}.

When
p_{k}=2
for all
k\geq 0
, the set
\{n\in\mathbb{N}:|n|=A\}
corresponds precisely to the dyadic block
\{n\in\mathbb{N}:2^{A}\leq n<2^{A+1}\}
.

The estimation process for the ‘dyadic block’, as outlined in Proposition
2.3
, necessitates the introduction of additional notation and relies on a crucial intermediate estimate. Specifically, we define the Dirichlet kernels and Fejér kernels as follows:

(2.4)

\displaystyle D_{n}(y,x):=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\chi_{k}(y)\bar{\chi}_{k}(x)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad K_{n}(y,x):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}D_{k}(y,x)

for
n\geq 1
and set
D_{0}:=K_{0}:=0
for convenience.
Given two positive integers
m
and
n
, we then define

K_{m,n}(y,x)\coloneqq\sum_{k=m}^{m+n-1}D_{k}(y,x).

Furthermore, we introduce an additional notation

n^{(s)}=\sum_{k=s}^{\infty}n_{k}P_{k}.

Then the key estimate can be stated as follows.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
f\in L_{2}(\mathcal{N})
and
A\geq s\in\mathbb{N}
, then we have

(2.5)

\displaystyle\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\int_{G}f(x)K_{n^{(s)},P_{s}}(y,x)d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}\leq 40\rho(G)^{2}P_{s}((A-s)^{2}+1)\|f\|_{2}.

By invoking a nice formula referenced in (
4.1
), transitioning from Proposition
2.4
to Proposition
2.3
is straightforward. Detailed discussions of this transition can be found in Section
4
. However, the proof of Proposition
2.4
is extremely involved, and takes up a substantial part of the paper.

Before presenting the delicate arguments for Proposition
2.4
, which will be elaborated in Section
6
, we first introduce the following relatively easier intermediate estimate essential for the proof. Consider an element
y=(y_{\ell})_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
in
G
.
For each
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, the conjugacy class of an element
y_{\ell}\in G_{\ell}
is denoted by
j_{\ell}(y_{\ell})
, given by

j_{\ell}(y_{\ell}):=\{x_{\ell}\in G_{\ell}:\exists z_{\ell}\in G_{\ell},y_{\ell}=-z_{\ell}+x_{\ell}+z_{\ell}\}.

Furthermore, we set
J_{0}(y)=G
and for
n\geq 1
, define

J_{n}(y):=\{x\in G:x_{i}\in j_{i}(y_{i}),0\leq i\leq n-1\}=j_{0}(y_{0})\times\cdots\times j_{n-1}(y_{n-1})\times\bigtimes_{i=n}^{\infty}G_{i}.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
f\in L_{2}(\mathcal{N})
and
A\geq s\in\mathbb{N}
, then we have that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\frac{1}{\mu(J_{s}(\cdot))}\int_{J_{s}(\cdot)}f(x)\chi_{n^{(s)}}(\cdot)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(s)}}(x)d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}\leq\|f\|_{2}.

To prove the reductions or the estimates such as (
2.3
), (
2.5
), (
2.6
), we primarily adhere to the classical sketch outlined in
[
Gát06
]
. Nevertheless, due to the noncommutativity, we have to pay meticulous attention to the pointwise estimates, and it compels us to revise many classical arguments. Notably, in reverting to the classical case, our results exhibit enhanced precision, particularly in the bounds of certain estimates, which are more accurate, exemplified by being of order
\rho(G)^{2}
. This refinement in accuracy, especially in noncommutative settings, underscores the nuanced improvements our approach offers over traditional methodologies.

As described above, the paper is organized as follows. In Section
3
, we prove Theorem
1.1
by assuming Proposition
2.3
. In Section
4
, we derive Proposition
2.3
based on Proposition
2.4
.
Section
5
presents the relatively easier intermediate estimate (
2.5
). Finally, in Section
6
, we provide the proof of Proposition
2.4
.

In this paper, the letter
C
denotes a positive absolute constant that not inevitably the same one in each occurrence. The notation
Y\lesssim Z
or
Z\lesssim Y
indicates that there exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that
Y\leq CZ
or
Z\leq CY
, respectively.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
: based on Proposition
2.3

Assuming Proposition
2.3
, we conclude Theorem
1.1
in this section. While we fundamentally adhere to the classical argument presented in
[
Gát06
]
, adapting it to the noncommutative setting requires careful consideration. In particular, our final argument is slightly different from the classical one due to the noncommutativity. To support these adjustments, several lemmas prove to be indispensable.

The following lemma admits an
L_{p}
-version for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
. However, for the purposes of this paper, we focus exclusively on the case
p=2
. For additional results and their proofs, we refer to
[
HWWb
, Proposition 2.3]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(x_{n})\in\ell_{2}(L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))
, then

\left\|{\sup_{n}}^{+}x_{n}\right\|_{2}\leq\left(\sum_{n}\|x_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The following lemma is well-known to experts. But we include a proof here to ensure completeness.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\{a_{n}\}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
and
(f_{n})_{n}\in L_{2}(L_{\infty}(G)\overline{\otimes}{\mathcal{M}};\ell_{\infty})
, then one has

\left\|{\sup_{n}}^{+}a_{n}f_{n}\right\|_{2}\leq\sup_{n}|a_{n}|\left\|{\sup_{n}}^{+}f_{n}\right\|_{2}.

Proof.

According to the definition of
L_{2}({\mathcal{M}};\ell_{\infty})
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a decomposition
f_{n}=ay_{n}b
, for any
n\geq 1
such that

\|a\|_{4}\sup_{n}\|y_{n}\|_{\infty}\|b\|_{4}\leq\left\|{\sup_{n}}^{+}f_{n}\right\|_{2}+\varepsilon.

Hence
a_{n}f_{n}=aa_{n}y_{n}b
and we can further express it as:

\|a\|_{4}\sup_{n}\|a_{n}y_{n}\|_{\infty}\|b\|_{4}\leq\|a\|_{4}\sup_{n}|a_{n}|\sup_{n}\|y_{n}\|_{\infty}\|b\|_{4}\leq\sup_{n}|a_{n}|\left(\left\|{\sup_{n}}^{+}f_{n}\right\|_{2}+\varepsilon\right).

By letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, we have

\left\|{\sup_{n}}^{+}a_{n}f_{n}\right\|_{2}\leq\sup_{n}|a_{n}|\left\|{\sup_{n}}^{+}f_{n}\right\|_{2}.

∎

The following two lemmas have been established in
[
Gát06
]
in the scalar-valued case, we present them here with a proof to facilitate the reader’s understanding on the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
f\in L_{2}(\mathcal{N})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|n|=A
. Then
\sigma_{n}(f)=\sigma_{n}(E_{A+1}f).

Proof.

By Lemma
5.3
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle E_{A+1}f(x)=S_{P_{A+1}}f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{P_{A+1}-1}\widehat{f}(j)\chi_{j}(x).

Then by the complete orthogonality property of the character system and
P_{A}\leq n<P_{A+1}
, we have that
for
i<P_{A+1}
,

\displaystyle\int_{G}E_{A+1}f(x)\bar{\chi}_{i}(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\sum_{j=0}^{P_{A+1}-1}\widehat{f}(j)\chi_{j}(x)\bar{\chi}_{i}(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\widehat{f}(i)\chi_{i}(x)\bar{\chi}_{i}(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\widehat{f}(i)\|\chi_{i}\|_{L_{2}(G)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{G}f(x)\bar{\chi}_{i}(x)d\mu(x).

Recalling the definition of Fejér means (
1.2
), then we immediately obtain the desired identity.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f\in L_{2}(\mathcal{N})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|n|=A
, then

(3.2)

\displaystyle n\sigma_{n}(E_{A}f)=P_{A}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A}f)+(n-P_{A})E_{A}f.

Moreover,

P_{A}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A}f)=P_{A}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A}f-E_{A-1}f)+P_{A-1}\sigma_{P_{A-1}}(E_{A-1}f)+(P_{A}-P_{A-1})E_{A-1}f.

Proof.

From Identity (
3.1
), it is obvious that

\int_{G}E_{A}f(x)\bar{\chi}_{j}(x)d\mu(x)=0,\qquad\forall j\geq P_{A}.

So for
P_{A}\leq k\leq n-1
,
S_{k}(E_{A}f)=S_{P_{A}}E_{A}f=E_{A}f
, and thus

\displaystyle n\sigma_{n}(E_{A}f)

\displaystyle=\sum^{P_{A}-1}_{k=0}S_{k}(E_{A}f)+\sum^{n-1}_{k=P_{A}}S_{k}(E_{A}f)

\displaystyle=P_{A}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A}f)+(n-P_{A})E_{A}f.

This is Identity (
3.2
).

To obtain the second identity, first observe that

P_{A}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A}f)=P_{A}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A}f-E_{A-1}f)+P_{A}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A-1}f).

Then the application of (
3.2
) to
n=P_{A}
and
E_{A-1}f
yields the desired identity.
∎

We are now in the position to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

From Lemma
3.3
, we have

\left\|{\sup_{n\geq 1}}^{+}\sigma_{n}f\right\|_{2}=\left\|{\sup_{A\geq 0}}^{+}{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\sigma_{n}f\right\|_{2}=\left\|{\sup_{A\geq 0}}^{+}{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\sigma_{n}(E_{A+1}f)\right\|_{2}.

Based on Lemma
3.1
and Lemma
3.4
, it is established that

\displaystyle\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\sigma_{n}(E_{A+1}f)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\sigma_{n}(E_{A+1}f-E_{A}f)\right\|_{2}+\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\sigma_{n}(E_{A}f)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{A}\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\sigma_{n}(E_{A+1}f-E_{A}f)\right\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \quad+\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\frac{P_{A}}{n}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A}f)+\frac{n-P_{A}}{n}E_{A}f\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\eqqcolon I+II.

We proceed by estimating the term
I
first. According to Proposition
2.3
,

I\leq 640\rho(G)^{2}\left(\sum_{A}\|E_{A+1}f-E_{A}f\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Following the to the martingale result (
2.2
), we find

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{A}\|E_{A+1}f-E_{A}f\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\sum_{A}|E_{A+1}f-E_{A}f|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{2}.

Consequently,

I\leq 640\rho(G)^{2}\|f\|_{2}.

Next, we turn our attention to estimate the term
II
. By the triangle inequality, we get

II\leq\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\frac{n-P_{A}}{n}(E_{A}f)\right\|_{2}+\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\frac{P_{A}}{n}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A}f)\right\|_{2},

and utilizing noncommutative Doob’s inequality (
2.1
) and along with Lemma
3.2
, we find

II\leq 2\|f\|_{2}+\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A}f)\right\|_{2}=2\|f\|_{2}+\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A}f)\right\|_{2}.

Now it suffices to estimate the second term. By applying Lemma
3.2
, Lemma
3.4
, the triangle inequality and the fact that
p_{k}\geq 2
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A}f)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}\frac{P_{A-1}}{P_{A}}\sigma_{P_{A-1}}(E_{A-1}f)\right\|_{2}+\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}\frac{P_{A}-P_{A-1}}{P_{A}}E_{A-1}f\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A}f-E_{A-1}f)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}\sigma_{P_{A-1}}(E_{A-1}f)\right\|_{2}+\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}E_{A-1}f\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A}f-E_{A-1}f)\right\|_{2}.

Since
|P_{A}|=A,P_{A}\in\{n:|n|=A\}
, and according to the result obtained for the term
I
, we establish

\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A}f-E_{A-1}f)\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\sigma_{n}(E_{A}f-E_{A-1}f)\right\|_{2}\leq 640\rho(G)^{2}\|f\|_{2}.

This leads us to the following estimate:

\displaystyle\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A}f)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}\sigma_{P_{A-1}}(E_{A-1}f)\right\|_{2}+(2+640\rho(G)^{2})\|f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A}f)\right\|_{2}+(2+640\rho(G)^{2})\|f\|_{2}.

Therefore, we obtain

\left\|{\sup_{A}}^{+}\sigma_{P_{A}}(E_{A}f)\right\|_{2}\leq 2(2+640\rho(G)^{2})\|f\|_{2}.

Consequently,

\left\|{\sup_{n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}}}^{+}\sigma_{n}f\right\|_{2}\leq I+II\lesssim\rho(G)^{2}\|f\|_{2}.

∎

Proof of Proposition

: based on Proposition
2.4

Assuming the validity of Proposition
2.4
, we consequently deduce Proposition
2.3
in this section.

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

Observe that for
n
when
|n|=A
, the following equality holds

(4.1)

\displaystyle nK_{n}(y,x)=\sum_{s=0}^{A}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{s}-1}K_{n^{(s+1)}+jP_{s},P_{s}}(y,x).

This leads to

\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\sigma_{n}f\right\|_{2}\leq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{s=0}^{A}\sum_{j=0}^{n_{s}-1}\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\int_{G}f(x)K_{n^{(s+1)}+jP_{s},P_{s}}(y,x)d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}.

Considering each fixed
j

(j\in\{0,\cdots,n_{s}-1\})
, we obtain

\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\int_{G}f(x)K_{n^{(s+1)}+jP_{s},P_{s}}(y,x)d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}\leq\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\int_{G}f(x)K_{n^{(s)},P_{s}}(y,x)d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}.

Combining Proposition
2.4
and the fact that
n_{s}\leq p_{s}
for any

, we get

\displaystyle\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\sigma_{n}f\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 40\rho(G)^{2}\frac{n_{s}}{P_{A}}\sum_{s=0}^{A}P_{s}((A-s)^{2}+1)\|f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 40\rho(G)^{2}\sum_{s=0}^{A}\frac{p_{s}P_{s}}{P_{A}}((A-s)^{2}+1)\|f\|_{2}

\displaystyle=40\rho(G)^{2}\sum_{s=0}^{A}\frac{1}{p_{s+1}\cdots p_{A-1}}((A-s)^{2}+1)\|f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 80\rho(G)^{2}\sum_{s=0}^{A}\frac{1}{2^{A-s}}((A-s)^{2}+1)\|f\|_{2}

\displaystyle=80\rho(G)^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{A}\frac{i^{2}+1}{2^{i}}\|f\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 80\rho(G)^{2}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{i^{2}+1}{2^{i}}\|f\|_{2}\leq 640\rho(G)^{2}\|f\|_{2}.

∎

5.
Proof of Lemma
2.5

The purpose of this section is to prove Lemma
2.5
, which will play an important role in the next section to demonstrate Proposition
2.3
. To achieve this, we introduce three pivotal lemmas to manipulate the Dirichlet kernels. These lemmas can be found in the literature. For instance, in
[
Gát06
]
the author listed them and cited the original references. To enhance comprehension, we present their proofs herein.

Lemma 5.1
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there holds

D_{n}(y,x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{+\infty}D_{P_{\ell}}(y,x)\sum_{i=0}^{n_{\ell}-1}r_{\ell}^{i}(y)\bar{r}_{\ell}^{i}(x)\chi_{n^{(\ell+1)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(\ell+1)}}(x).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, one may assume that
|n|=A
, that is
n=n_{0}P_{0}+\cdots+n_{A}P_{A}
. Rewriting the Dirichlet kernel, we reformulate it as follows:

D_{n}(y,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\chi_{k}(y)\bar{\chi}_{k}(x)=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{P_{A}-1}+\sum_{k=P_{A}}^{2P_{A}-1}+\cdots+\sum_{k=(n_{A}-1)P_{A}}^{n_{A}P_{A}-1}+\sum_{k=n_{A}P_{A}}^{n-1}\right)\chi_{k}(y)\bar{\chi}_{k}(x).

From the second term to the penultimate one, the summing indices
k
can be rewritten as
k=k^{\prime}+1\cdot P_{A}
,
\dotsm
,
k=k^{\prime}+(n_{A}-1)\cdot P_{A}
, where
k^{\prime}=k_{0}P_{0}+\cdots+k_{A-1}P_{A-1}
ranging from
0
to
P_{A}-1
. Thus

\sum_{k=P_{A}}^{2P_{A}-1}\chi_{k}(y)\bar{\chi}_{k}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{P_{A}-1}\chi_{k}(y)\bar{\chi}_{k}(x)r_{A}^{1}(y)\bar{r}_{A}^{1}(x),

\dotsm,

\sum_{k=(n_{A}-1)P_{A}}^{n_{A}P_{A}-1}\chi_{k}(y)\bar{\chi}_{k}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{P_{A}-1}\chi_{k}(y)\bar{\chi}_{k}(x)r_{A}^{n_{A}-1}(y)\bar{r}_{A}^{n_{A}-1}(x).

Regarding the last term, by representing the summation index index
k
as
k=k^{\prime}+n_{A}P_{A}
with
k^{\prime}
ranging from
0
to
n-1-n_{A}P_{A}
, it is deduced that

\sum_{k=n_{A}P_{A}}^{n-1}\chi_{k}(y)\bar{\chi}_{k}(x)=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1-n_{A}P_{A}}\chi_{k}(y)\bar{\chi}_{k}(x)\right)r_{A}^{n_{A}}(y)\bar{r}_{A}^{n_{A}}(x)

Therefore, the following is obtained

D_{n}(y,x)=D_{P_{A}}(y,x)\sum_{i=0}^{n_{A}-1}r_{A}^{i}(y)\bar{r}_{A}^{i}(x)+\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1-n_{A}P_{A}}\chi_{k}(y)\bar{\chi}_{k}(x)\right)r_{A}^{n_{A}}(y)\bar{r}_{A}^{n_{A}}(x).

Note that the above summation inside the bracket can be addressed similarly. Repeating the procedure, we conclude finally that

D_{n}(y,x)=\cdots=\sum_{\ell=0}^{A}D_{P_{\ell}}(y,x)\sum_{i=0}^{n_{\ell}-1}r_{\ell}^{i}(y)\bar{r}_{\ell}^{i}(x)\chi_{n^{(\ell+1)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(\ell+1)}}(x).

∎

Lemma 5.2
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

D_{P_{n}}(y,x)=\mu^{-1}(J_{n}(y))\bm{1}_{J_{n}(y)}(x),\quad x,y\in G.

Proof.

This assertion follows from the corresponding identity for a finite group. Let
H
be a finite group equipped with the canonical Haar measure. Denote by
\Sigma
the equivalence classes of continuous irreducible unitary representations of
H
, which is still finite. We then claim

\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\chi_{\sigma}(y)\bar{\chi}_{\sigma}(x)=\mu^{-1}(j(y))\bm{1}_{j(y)}(x),

where
\chi_{\sigma}
is the character associated to
\sigma\in\Sigma
. Indeed, by the Plancherel theorem, for any complex-valued function
f
, one has

f(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\widehat{f}(\sigma)\chi_{\sigma}(x)=\int_{H}f(t)\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\chi_{\sigma}(x)\bar{\chi}_{\sigma}(t)d\mu(t).

Taking
f(x)=\bm{1}_{j(y)}(x)
, we obtains

\displaystyle\bm{1}_{j(y)}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{j(y)}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\chi_{\sigma}(x)\bar{\chi}_{\sigma}(t)d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\chi_{\sigma}(x)\int_{j(y)}\bar{\chi}_{\sigma}(t)d\mu(t)=\mu(j(y))\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma}\chi_{\sigma}(x)\bar{\chi}_{\sigma}(y).

Now, by applying the above claim repeatedly, we have

\displaystyle D_{P_{n}}(y,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{P_{n}-1}\chi_{k}(y)\bar{\chi}_{k}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{0}=0}^{p_{0}-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{n-1}=0}^{p_{n-1}-1}\chi_{k_{0}P_{0}+\cdots+k_{n-1}P_{n-1}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{k_{0}P_{0}+\cdots+k_{n-1}P_{n-1}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k_{0}=0}^{p_{0}-1}\cdots\sum_{k_{n-1}=0}^{p_{n-1}-1}\prod_{\ell=0}^{n-1}r_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}}(y)\bar{r}_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}}(x)=\prod_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\sum_{k_{\ell}=0}^{p_{\ell}-1}r_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}}(y)\bar{r}_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}}(x)

\displaystyle=\prod_{\ell=0}^{n-1}\mu_{\ell}^{-1}(j_{\ell}(y))\bm{1}_{j_{\ell}(y)}(x)=\mu^{-1}(J_{n}(y))\bm{1}_{J_{n}(y)}(x).

∎

Recall that
m_{k}
denotes the order of
G_{k}
. We define the sequence
(M_{k})
by

M_{0}:=1
and
M_{k+1}=m_{k}M_{k}
for
k\geq 1
.

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in L_{2}(\mathcal{N})
, the following equality holds:

S_{P_{n}}f(y)=E_{n}f(y).

Proof.

Firstly, invoking Lemma
5.2
yields

S_{P_{n}}f(y)=\int_{G}f(x)D_{P_{n}}(y,x)d\mu(x)=\mu^{-1}(J_{n}(y))\int_{J_{n}(y)}f(x)d\mu(x).

Recall that
I_{0}(y)=G
and for
k\geq 1
,

I_{k}(z):=\{x\in G:x_{j}=z_{j},0\leq j\leq k-1\},\quad z\in G.

This admits
J_{n}(y)
to be decomposed as

J_{n}(y)=\bigcup_{z:z_{i}\in j_{i}(y)\atop i\in\{0,\dotsm,n-1\}}I_{n}(z).

Since
f
is constant on each conjugacy class, for any
z
satisfying
z_{i}\in j_{i}(y),i<n
,

\int_{I_{n}(z)}f(x)d\mu(x)=\int_{I_{n}(y)}f(x)d\mu(x).

Therefore, the equation rewrites to

\displaystyle S_{P_{n}}f(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(J_{n}(y))}\sum_{z:z_{i}\in j_{i}(y)\atop i\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}}\int_{I_{n}(y)}f(x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=M_{n}\int_{I_{n}(y)}f(x)d\mu(x)=\frac{1}{\mu(I_{n}(y))}\int_{I_{n}(y)}f(x)d\mu(x)=E_{n}f(y).

∎

Now, we are ready to show Lemma
2.5
.

Proof of Lemma
2.5
.

From the proof of Lemma
5.3
, we have deduced the following equation

\mu^{-1}(J_{s}(y))\int_{J_{s}(y)}f(x)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(s)}}(x)d\mu(x)=M_{s}\int_{I_{s}(y)}f(x)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(s)}}(x)d\mu(x).

For any element
x\in G
, let us denote it as
x=(x_{0},x_{1},\cdots,x_{s},\cdots)=(x_{<s},x_{\geq s})
where
x_{<s}=(x_{0},\cdots,x_{s-1})
,
x_{\geq s}=(x_{s},x_{s+1},\cdots)
.
Applying the above identity, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\mu^{-1}(J_{s}(y))\int_{J_{s}(y)}f(x)\chi_{n^{(s)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(s)}}(x)d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}M_{s}\int_{I_{s}(y)}f(x)\chi_{n^{(s)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(s)}}(x)d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle(\text{Definition of $I_{s}(y)$})=

\displaystyle\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}M_{s}\int_{I_{s}(y)}f(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})\chi_{n^{(s)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(s)}}(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle(\text{Definition of $n^{(s)}$})=

\displaystyle\left\|{\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{s,A}}}^{+}M_{s}\int_{I_{s}(y)}f(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})\chi_{n^{(s)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(s)}}(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle(\text{By Lemma \ref{3.2}})\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{s,A}}\left\|M_{s}\int_{I_{s}(y)}f(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})\chi_{n^{(s)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(s)}}(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}^{2},

where

\mathbb{N}_{s,A}:=\left\{n\mid{n_{i}\in\{0,\cdots,p_{i}-1\},\ i\in\{s,\cdots,A\}}\right\}.

For convenience, we denote

(5.1)

\displaystyle G_{<t>}=

\displaystyle\bigtimes_{i=0}^{t-1}G_{i}\times\bigtimes_{i=t+1}^{\infty}G_{i},

\displaystyle G_{\geq A+1}=

\displaystyle\bigtimes\limits_{i=A+1}^{\infty}G_{i},

\displaystyle G_{<s}=

\displaystyle G_{0}\times\cdots\times G_{s-1},

\displaystyle G_{s,A}=

\displaystyle G_{s}\times\cdots\times G_{A}.

and set

\displaystyle d\mu(x_{<s})=

\displaystyle d\mu_{0}(x_{0})\times\cdots\times d\mu_{s-1}(x_{s-1}),

\displaystyle d\mu(x_{s,A})=

\displaystyle d\mu_{s}(x_{s})\times\cdots\times d\mu_{A}(x_{A}),

\displaystyle d\mu(x_{\geq A+1})=

\displaystyle d\mu_{A+1}(x_{A+1})\times\cdots\cdots.

Since
n\in\mathbb{N}_{s,A}
and the definition of
E_{A+1}f
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{s,A}}\left\|\int_{G_{\geq s}}f(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})\chi_{n^{(s)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(s)}}(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})d\mu(x_{\geq s})\right\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{s,A}}\left\|\int_{G_{s,A}}(E_{A+1}f)(y_{<s},x_{s}\cdots x_{A},y_{\geq A+1})\chi_{n^{(s)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(s)}}(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})d\mu(x_{s,A})\right\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}^{2}.

Noting
\mu(I_{s}(y))=1/M_{s}
, we can thus get

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{s,A}}\left\|M_{s}\int_{I_{s}(y)}f(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})\chi_{n^{(s)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(s)}}(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})d\mu(x)\right\|_{L_{2}(G;L_{2}({\mathcal{M}}))}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{s,A}}\left\|M_{s}\int_{I_{s}(y)}f(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})\chi_{n^{(s)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(s)}}(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})d\mu(x)\right\|_{L_{2}({{\mathcal{M}}})}^{2}d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{s,A}}\left\|\int_{G_{\geq s}}f(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})\chi_{n^{(s)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(s)}}(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})d\mu(x_{\geq s})\right\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}^{2}d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\int_{G}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{s,A}}\left\|\int_{G_{s,A}}(E_{A+1}f)(y_{<s},x_{s}\cdots x_{A},y_{\geq A+1})\chi_{n^{(s)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(s)}}(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})d\mu(x_{s,A})\right\|_{L_{2}({\mathcal{M}})}^{2}d\mu(y).

y_{0},\cdots,y_{s-1},y_{A+1}\cdots

\displaystyle\int_{G_{s,A}}(E_{A+1}f)(y_{<s},x_{s}\cdots x_{A},y_{\geq A+1})

Hence, by applying the Hilbert-valued Plancherel theorem on
G_{s,A}
and considering Equation (

), we arrive at

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{s,A}}\left\|M_{s}\int_{I_{s}(y)}f(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})\chi_{n^{(s)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(s)}}(y_{<s},x_{\geq s})d\mu(x)\right\|_{L_{2}(G;L_{2}({\mathcal{M}}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{G_{<s}}\int_{G_{\geq A+1}}\left\|(E_{A+1}f)(y_{<s},\cdots,y_{\geq A+1})\right\|_{L_{2}(G_{s,A};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))}^{2}\|\chi_{n^{(s)}}(y)\|_{L_{2}(G_{s,A})}^{2}d\mu(y)

\displaystyle\left\|E_{A+1}f\right\|_{2}^{2}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}.

Consequently,

\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\mu^{-1}(J_{s}(y))\int_{J_{s}(y)}f(x)\chi_{n^{(s)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(s)}}(x)d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}\leq\|f\|_{2}.

Proof of Proposition

.

For any

Applying the triangle inequality yields

\displaystyle\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\int_{G}f(x)K_{n^{(s)},P_{s}}(y,x)d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=0}^{s-1}\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\int_{J_{t}(y)\setminus J_{t+1}(y)}f(x)K_{n^{(s)},P_{s}}(y,x)d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=:\sum_{t=0}^{s-1}B_{1,t}+\sum_{t=s}^{A}B_{1,t}+B_{0}.

Without loss of the generality, we may assume

Step 1:

\displaystyle B_{0}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\int_{J_{A+1}(y)}f(x)K_{n^{(s)},P_{s}}(y,x)d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle(\text{By Remark}\,\ref{2.3})

\displaystyle(\text{By Lemma}\,\ref{4.3})

\displaystyle=\rho(G)\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\sum_{k=n^{(s)}}^{n^{(s)}+P_{s}-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{A}\mu^{-1}(J_{\ell}(y))\mu(J_{A+1}(y))|\chi_{k^{(\ell+1)}}(y)|^{2}E_{A+1}f(y)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle(\text{Triangle inequality})

\displaystyle\leq\rho(G)\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\sum_{k=n^{(s)}}^{n^{(s)}+P_{s}-1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{s-1}\mu^{-1}(J_{\ell}(y))\mu(J_{A+1}(y))|\chi_{k^{(\ell+1)}}(y)|^{2}E_{A+1}f(y)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}P_{s}\sum_{\ell=s}^{A}E_{A+1}f(y)\mu(J_{A+1}(y))\mu^{-1}(J_{\ell}(y))|\chi_{n^{(\ell+1)}}(y)|^{2}\right\|_{2}.

\displaystyle\leq(t-s+2)P_{s}\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\mu^{-1}(J_{t}(y))\int_{J_{t}(y)\setminus J_{t+1}(y)}f(x)\chi_{n^{(t)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(t)}}(x)d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq(t-s+2)P_{s}\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\mu^{-1}(J_{t}(y))\int_{J_{t}(y)}f(x)\chi_{n^{(t)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(t)}}(x)d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ +(t-s+2)P_{s}\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\mu^{-1}(J_{t}(y))\int_{J_{t+1}(y)}f(x)\chi_{n^{(t)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(t)}}(x)d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=:(t-s+2)P_{s}(E+F).

It follows from Lemma

that

in

and using Lemma

, we get that

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle=\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\mu^{-1}(J_{t}(y))\int_{J_{t}(y_{<t>})\times j_{t}(y_{t})}r_{t}^{n_{t}}(y_{t})\bar{r}_{t}^{n_{t}}(x_{t})f(x)\chi_{n^{(t)}}(y_{<t>})\bar{\chi}_{n^{(t)}}(x_{<t>})d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\sum_{k=n^{(s)}}^{n^{(s)}+P_{s}-1}\mu^{-1}(J_{t+1}(y_{<t>}))\times\right.

\displaystyle\leq P_{s}\sum_{i=0}^{k_{t}-1}\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\mu^{-1}(J_{t+1}(y_{<t>}))\int_{J_{t+1}(y_{<t>})\times(j_{t}(y))^{c}}r_{t}^{i}(y)\bar{r}_{t}^{i}(x)f(x)\chi_{n^{(t+1)}}(y)\bar{\chi}_{n^{(t+1)}}(x)d\mu(x)\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq P_{s}\sum_{i=0}^{k_{t}-1}\left\|\left\|{\sup_{|n|=A}}^{+}\int_{(j_{t}(y))^{c}}r_{t}^{i}(y_{t})\bar{r}_{t}^{i}(x_{t})\mu^{-1}(J_{t+1}(y_{<t>}))\times\right.\right.

\displaystyle\leq P_{s}\sum_{i=0}^{k_{t}-1}\left\|\int_{(j_{t}(y))^{c}}|\bar{r}_{t}^{i}(x_{t})|\left\|f(\cdots,x_{t},\cdots)\right\|_{L_{2}(G_{<t>};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))}d\mu_{t}(x_{t})|r_{t}^{i}(y_{t})|\right\|_{L_{2}(G_{t})}

Finally, validating that

We conclude that

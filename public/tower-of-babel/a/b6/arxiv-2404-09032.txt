Cauchy convergence in 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -normed categories
Maria Manuel Clementino, Dirk Hofmann, Walter Tholen

Abstract
Building on the notion of normed category as suggested by Lawvere, we introduce notions of Cauchy convergence and cocompleteness for such categories that differ from proposals in previous works. Key to our approach is to treat them consequentially as categories enriched in the monoidal-closed category of normed sets, i.e. , of sets which come with a norm function. Our notions lead to the anticipated outcomes for individual metric spaces or the additive groups of normed vector spaces considered as small normed categories. But they quickly become more challenging when we consider large categories, such as the categories of all semi-normed or normed vector spaces and all linear maps as morphisms, not just because norms of vectors need to be allowed to have value ∞ \infty in order to guarantee the existence of colimits of (sufficiently many) infinite sequences. These categories, along with categories of generalized metric spaces, are the key example categories discussed in detail in this paper. Working with a general commutative quantale 𝒱 \mathcal{V} as a value recipient for norms, rather than only with Lawvere’s quantale ℛ + \mathcal{R}_{+} of the extended real half-line, we observe that the categorically atypical, and perhaps even irritating, structure gap between objects and morphisms in the example categories is already present in the underlying normed category of the enriching category of 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -normed sets. To show that the normed category and, in fact, all presheaf categories over it, are Cauchy cocomplete, we assume the quantale 𝒱 \mathcal{V} to satisfy a couple of light alternative extra properties which, however, are satisfied in all instances of interest to us. Of utmost importance to the general theory is the fact that our notion of Cauchy convergence is subsumed by the notion of weighted colimit of enriched category theory. With this theory and, in particular, with results of Albert, Kelly and Schmitt, we are able to prove that all 𝒱 \mathcal{V} -normed categories have correct-size Cauchy cocompletions, for 𝒱 \mathcal{V} satisfying our light alternative assumptions. We also show that our notions are suitable to prove a Banach Fixed Point Theorem for contractive endofunctors of Cauchy cocomplete normed categories.

. Then
\gamma
makes

a normed colimit of

Proof.

The truncation at

, whence
\gamma
is an ordinary colimit cocone precisely when
\gamma_{|K}
is one. Furthermore, given

in
\mathbb{X}

one has
\bigvee_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\Phi(N)=\bigvee_{N\geq K}\Phi(N)
, where
\leq
holds since

is monotonely increasing, and where

holds trivially. Consequently, (C1) and (C2) hold for

over a sequence

-

Corollary 5.6

An object

in a
\mathcal{V}
-normed category

if, and only if,

is a colimit cocone in the ordinary category
\mathbb{X}
such that

\mathrm{k}\leq\bigvee_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigwedge_{n\geq N}|\gamma_{n}|

\mathrm{k}

(C2b)

|f|\geq\bigvee_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigwedge_{n\geq N}|f\cdot\gamma_{n}|
, for every morphism

in
\mathbb{X}
.

Corollary 5.7
.

A normed colimit of a sequence in a
\mathcal{V}
-normed category

\mathrm{k}
-isomorphism, i.e., up to an isomorphism in the category
\mathbb{X}_{\circ}

of
\mathrm{k}
-morphisms of
\mathbb{X}

Proof.

If

Here is a sufficient, but not necessary, condition on the

-normed category

Proposition 5.8
.

Let

\mathcal{V}
-normed category satisfying the condition

(S)

|f\cdot h|\otimes|h|\leq|f|
for all composable morphisms

and

Then an object

with a cocone

is a normed colimit of a sequence
s
in
\mathbb{X}
if, and only if, (C1)
\gamma
is a colimit cocone in the ordinary category
\mathbb{X}
, and (C2a)
\gamma
is a
\mathrm{k}
-cocone.

Proof.

First we note that, in (S), the morphism

may be replaced equivalently by any cocone

, for some diagram

with

Indeed, for all

|\Delta f\cdot\alpha|\otimes|\alpha|=\bigwedge_{i\in\mathbb{I}}|f\cdot\alpha_{i}|\otimes\bigwedge_{i\in\mathbb{I}}|\alpha_{i}|\leq\bigwedge_{i\in\mathbb{I}}|f\cdot\alpha_{i}|\otimes|\alpha_{i}|\leq\bigwedge_{i\in I}|f|=|f|\;.

Having (C2a) and this extended version of (S), we can now show (C2b) of Corollary

\displaystyle\bigvee_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigwedge_{n\geq N}|f\cdot\gamma_{n}|

\displaystyle\leq(\bigvee_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigwedge_{n\geq N}|f\cdot\gamma_{n}|)\otimes(\bigvee_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigwedge_{n\geq N}|\gamma_{n}|)

\displaystyle\leq\bigvee_{N\in\mathbb{N}}(\bigwedge_{n\geq N}|f\cdot\gamma_{n}|\otimes\bigwedge_{n\geq N}|\gamma_{n}|)

\displaystyle\leq\bigvee_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigwedge_{n\geq N}|f\cdot\gamma_{n}|\otimes|\gamma_{n}|\;\leq\;|f|\;.

8
Semi-normed and normed vector spaces

When a norm function
|\!|\text{-}|\!|
on a (real, say) vector space

\infty

\infty

\mathcal{R}_{\times}
of Examples

(2) and the adjunction

The ordinary categories of semi-normed and of normed vector spaces are defined as follows:

Definition 8.1
.

A

is a function
|\!|\text{-}|\!|:X\to[0,\infty]

(N0)

|\!|0|\!|=0
,

(N1)

|\!|ax|\!|=|a||\!|x|\!|
,

(N2)

|\!|x+y|\!|\leq|\!|x|\!|+|\!|y|\!|
,

for all

and
a\in\mathbb{R},a\neq 0
.
5
5

5

We exclude

since otherwise (N1) would contradict (N0), in light of

in the quantale
\mathcal{R}_{\times}
; see Examples
7.3
(2).
The thus defined
semi-normed vector spaces
are the objects of the category

\mathsf{SNVec}_{\infty}
whose morphisms are arbitrary linear maps. It contains the full subcategory
\mathsf{NVec}_{\infty}
of

They fall short of being normed vector spaces in the classical sense only insofar as here vectors are permitted to have infinite norms.

(1) The additive group

from satisfying the zero-to-zero condition—a contradiction.
∎

Example 8.10
.

A normed colimit in
\mathsf{NVec}_{\infty}
of a Cauchy sequence of Banach spaces need not be Banach. Indeed,
consider the sequence

\textstyle{\{0\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{R}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{R}^{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathbb{R}^{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{\mathrm{colim}_{n}\;\mathbb{R}^{n}=\bigoplus_{n}\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{R}^{(\infty)}}

of isometric embeddings of finite-dimensional spaces, normed by the

exists and is given by the direct sum (in

We continue to work with a quantale

of

\textstyle{(A_{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\gamma_{n}}

in
\mathsf{Set}{||}\mathcal{V}

that distinguishes itself by a special property, as follows.

|c|=\bigwedge_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigvee_{n\geq N}\bigvee_{a\in\gamma_{n}^{-1}c}|a|;

\gamma_{n}

\mathcal{V}

Lemma 9.1
.

Let the set
A
be provided with the
\gamma
-induced Cauchy norm as above. Then, for any mapping
f:A\to B
to a
\mathcal{V}
-normed set

|f|\geq\bigvee_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\bigwedge_{n\geq N}|f\cdot\gamma_{n}|\,.

Proof.

Since, in its first (contravariant) variable, the internal hom
[\text{-,-}]
of
\mathcal{V}
transforms arbitrary joins into meets, we have:

\displaystyle|f|=\bigwedge_{c\in A}[|c|,|fc|]

\displaystyle=\bigwedge_{c\in A}[\bigwedge_{N}\bigvee_{n\geq N}\bigvee_{a\in\gamma_{n}^{-1}c}|a|,\,|fc|]

\displaystyle\geq\bigwedge_{c\in A}\bigvee_{N}[\bigvee_{n\geq N}\bigvee_{a\in\gamma_{n}^{-1}c}|a|,\,|fc|]

\displaystyle\geq\bigvee_{N}\bigwedge_{c\in A}[\bigvee_{n\geq N}\bigvee_{a\in\gamma_{n}^{-1}c}|a|,\,|fc|]

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{N}\bigwedge_{n\geq N}\bigwedge_{c\in A}\bigwedge_{a\in\gamma_{n}^{-1}c}[|a|,\,|fc|]

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{N}\bigwedge_{n\geq N}\bigwedge_{a\in A_{n}}[|a|,\,|f(\gamma_{n}a)|]

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{N}\bigwedge_{n\geq N}|f\cdot\gamma_{n}|\;.

In order to strengthen the assertion of Lemma

\otimes
-neutral element
\mathrm{k}
of the quantale
\mathcal{V}
. Actually, we offer two alternative possibilities, (A)
or
(B), for suitably augmenting our general quantalic setting, as follows:

(A)
\mathrm{k}

is approximated from totally below
(see Theorem

), that is:
\mathrm{k}=\bigvee\{\varepsilon\in\mathcal{V}\mid\varepsilon\lll\mathrm{k}\}.

(B)
\mathrm{k}

\wedge
-
distributes over arbitrary joins
, that is:
\mathrm{k}\wedge\bigvee_{i\in I}v_{i}=\bigvee_{i\in I}\mathrm{k}\wedge v_{i}\;.

Remarks 9.2
.

(1) Condition (A) certainly holds when the lattice
\mathcal{V}
is
(constructively) completely distributive
in the sense of
[
45

.

(2) Condition (B) trivially holds when the quantale
\mathcal{V}
is

when

is a

since, in the latter case

\mathcal{V}

\wedge
-distributes over arbitrary joins by definition, whilst in the former case the map

\mathcal{V}

(3) With the exception of those mentioned in Example
3.5
(2), most quantales discussed in this paper satisfy
both
conditions, (A) and (B), and the others at least one. We examine the two conditions further in Section
15
and confirm their logical independence.

For distributors

for a

\mathbb{X}
and

. Hence, the presheaf

and the higher-universe

. Therefore we have natural isomorphisms

From the discussion above we obtain:

Lemma 10.2
.

An object

of
\mathbb{X}
is a
\phi
-weighted colimit of

if, and only if,

is a
(\phi\cdot F^{*})
-weighted colimit of the identity functor
\mathbb{X}\to\mathbb{X}
, and in that case we simply speak of a
(\phi\cdot F^{*})
-weighted colimit in
\mathbb{X}
.

For a
\mathcal{V}
-normed category
\mathbb{X}
we consider the Yoneda embedding

whose codomain (irrespective of potentially having to be formed in a higher universe) is

-normed again. Actually,
\yoneda_{\mathbb{X}}
preserves norms since, for every

in

, one has

|\yoneda_{\mathbb{X}}f|=|\mathbb{X}(\text{-},f)|=\bigwedge_{z\in\mathbb{X}}|\mathbb{X}(z,f)|=\bigwedge_{h:z\to x}[|h|,|f\cdot h|]=|f|.

Definition 10.3
.

For every
\mathcal{V}
-normed category
\mathbb{X}
, let
\mathcal{P}\mathbb{X}
denote the full
\mathcal{V}
-normed subcategory of

defined by all

[

]
).

By definition, the accessible presheaves are the small-weighted colimits of representables. Viewing a presheaf

as a distributor

, this means that

if, and only if, there is a fully faithful
\mathcal{V}
-normed functor

with
\mathbb{A}
small and a distributor
\psi\colon\mathbb{A}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\phantom{\to}\;}_{\;\;}}\mathbb{E}
with

. Of course, for
\mathbb{X}
small, one has

\mathcal{P}\mathbb{X}=[\mathbb{X}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{Set}{||}\mathcal{V}]
.

Proposition 10.4
.

For every
\mathcal{V}
-normed category
\mathbb{X}
, the
\mathcal{V}
-normed category
\mathcal{P}\mathbb{X}

\mathcal{V}
-normed functor

Let
\sigma\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathcal{P}\mathbb{X}

. Since

is a (countable) set, there is a small full
\mathcal{V}
-normed subcategory
\mathbb{A}
of
\mathbb{X}
(with inclusion functor

) such that

factors as

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}\mathbb{A}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}\mathbb{X},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}I=-\cdot I^{*}}

and

.

Let

be a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{X}
. Then

has a

-weighted colimit.

-normed) category

Facts 10.6
.

Let

be a Cauchy sequence in a
\mathcal{V}
-normed category

, and let

.

Let
\mathbb{X}
be a
\mathcal{V}
-normed category. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

\mathbb{X}
is Cauchy cocomplete.

(ii)

\mathbb{X}
has all weighted colimits of diagrams

,

, where

is small and

.

(iii)

\mathbb{X}
has all weighted colimits of diagrams

,
\phi\colon\mathbb{A}{\mathrel{\to}^{\;\phantom{\to}\;}_{\;\;}}\mathbb{E}
, where
\mathbb{A}
is countable and
\phi
is a normed colimit of a Cauchy sequence of representables in
\mathcal{P}\mathbb{A}
.

(iv)

\mathbb{X}
has all weighted colimits of diagrams

,

, where the underlying category of
\mathbb{N}
is given by its order and

is a normed colimit of a Cauchy sequence of representables in
\mathcal{P}\mathbb{N}
.

11
Cauchy cocompletion of
\mathcal{V}
-normed categories

In the previous section we have shown that normed colimits of Cauchy sequences can be equivalently described as weighted colimits, for a certain choice of weights – under the assumption that the quantale
\mathcal{V}
satisfies condition (A)
or
(B), which we also assume in this section. Following the nomenclature of
[
30
,
5
]
, for every small
\mathcal{V}
-normed category
\mathbb{A}
we consider the class

of presheaves

that are normed colimits of Cauchy sequences of representables as in Corollary

(ii)
, and put

A

-normed category

is called

-cocomplete

\mathcal{V}
-normed functors

in

exists. Moreover, a

-normed functor is called

-cocontinuous

By Corollary
10.7
, a normed category

is Cauchy cocomplete if, and only if,
\mathbb{X}
is

-cocomplete. Furthermore, since the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Y}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\yoneda_{\mathbb{Y}}}

-normed functor

, using the notation of Proposition

and writing ncolim instead of just colim to stress the normedness of a colimit, for every Cauchy sequence

\Phi
-cocontinuous.

We denote by

the 2-category of

-cocomplete small

-normed categories,

.

For each small normed category
\mathbb{X}
, the presheaf category

Consider

On the geometry of some algebras related to the Weyl groupoid.
Ian M. Musson

Abstract
Let 𝚔 \mathtt{k} be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Let 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} be a finite dimensional classical simple Lie superalgebra over 𝚔 \mathtt{k} or 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( m , n ) \mathfrak{gl}(m,n) . In the case that 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} is a Kac-Moody algebra of finite type with set of roots Δ \Delta , Sergeev and Veselov introduced the Weyl groupoid 𝔚 = 𝔚 ⁡ ( Δ ) \mathfrak{W}=\mathfrak{W}(\Delta) , which has significant connections with the representation theory of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} . Let 𝔥 \mathfrak{h} , W W and Z ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) Z(\mathfrak{g}) be a Cartan subalgebra of 𝔤 0 \mathfrak{g}_{0} , the Weyl group of 𝔤 0 \mathfrak{g}_{0} and the center of U ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) U(\mathfrak{g}) respectively. Also let G G be a Lie supergroup with Lie G = 𝔤 G=\mathfrak{g} . There are several important commutative algebras related to 𝔚 \mathfrak{W} . Namely • The image I ⁡ ( 𝔥 ) I(\mathfrak{h}) of the injective Harish-Chandra map Z ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) ⟶ S ​ ( 𝔥 ) W Z(\mathfrak{g})\longrightarrow S(\mathfrak{h})^{W} . • The supercharacter ℤ {\mathbb{Z}} -algebras J ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) J(\mathfrak{g}) and J ⁡ ( G ) J(G) of finite dimensional representations of 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} and G G . Let 𝒜 = 𝒜 ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) \mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{g}) be denote either I ⁡ ( 𝔥 ) I(\mathfrak{h}) or J ⁡ ( G ) ⊗ ℤ 𝚔 J(G)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\mathtt{k} . The purpose of this paper is to investigate the algebraic geometry of 𝒜 . \mathcal{A}. In many cases, the algebra 𝒜 \mathcal{A} satisfies the Nullstellensatz. This gives a bijection between radical ideals in 𝒜 \mathcal{A} and superalgebraic sets (zero loci of such ideals). Any superalgebraic set is uniquely a finite union of irreducible superalgebraic components. In the Kac-Moody case, we describe the smallest superalgebraic set containing a given (Zariski) closed set, and show that the superalgebraic sets are exactly the closed sets that are unions of groupoid orbits.

5.1
Actions of groupoids on varieties.

A groupoid
\mathfrak{G}
can be
defined as a small category with all morphisms invertible.
We denote the set of objects by
\mathfrak{B}
which we call the
base
.
As in
[
27
]
we use the same notation
\mathfrak{G}
for the set of morphisms as for the groupoid itself.
For a variety
X
consider the groupoid

\mathfrak{A}(X)
with base all subvarieties of
X
, and morphisms all isomorphisms between subvarieties. We say the groupoid
\mathfrak{G}
acts on
X
if there is a functor

F:\mathfrak{G}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{A}(X).

If
g:b\longrightarrow b^{\prime}
is a morphism in
\mathfrak{G}
, set
s(g)=b
.
Then for
\lambda\in X,
set
\mathfrak{B}_{\lambda}=\{b\in\mathfrak{B}|\lambda\in F(b)\}
and
\mathfrak{G}_{(\lambda)}=\{g\in\mathfrak{G}|s(g)\in\mathfrak{B}_{\lambda}\}.
If
g\in\mathfrak{G}_{(\lambda)}
, we say
g

is defined at

\lambda
and often write
g\lambda=F(g)\lambda
.
Set
\mathfrak{G}_{(0)}\lambda=\{\lambda\}
and for
i\geq 0
, define

\mathfrak{G}_{(i+1)}\lambda=\{g\mu|\mu\in\mathfrak{G}_{(i)}\lambda,g\in\mathfrak{G}_{(\mu)}\}
. The
\mathfrak{G}
-orbit
of
\lambda
is

\mathfrak{G}\lambda=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}\mathfrak{G}_{(i)}\lambda
. Informally,
\mathfrak{G}_{(i)}\lambda
is the set of points in
X
we can reach from
\lambda
using
i
morphisms from
\mathfrak{G}.

If
X
is affine, the
invariant ring
for the action of
\mathfrak{G}
on
X
is

\mathcal{O}(X)^{\mathfrak{G}}=\{f\in\mathcal{O}(X)|f\mbox{ is constant on }\mathfrak{G}\mbox{-orbits}\}.

Unlike the case of a group action on
X
, the groupoid
\mathfrak{G}
does not in general act on functions.

Lemma 5.1
.

The comorphism

is constant on
\mathfrak{G}
-orbits.

This follows from the definition of
\mathcal{O}(X)^{\mathfrak{G}}

i\in[k]
, set
Q_{i}=P_{i}\cap A
. Then set

{\operatorname{heart}}_{q}V=\mathcal{V}(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}Q_{i}).

If
k=0
, set
{\operatorname{heart}}_{q}V=\emptyset.

Theorem 6.12
.

The S-closure of
V
is

V^{S}=\bigcup_{i=0}^{r}{\operatorname{heart}}_{i}V=\bigcup_{i=0}^{r}V_{i}.
The set
V^{S}
is superalgebraic.

Proof.

As in Proposition
6.9
if
q<r
, we have
V_{q}^{S}={\operatorname{heart}}_{q}V\cup V_{q+1}^{S}=V_{q}\cup V_{q+1}^{S}
and
V_{r}^{S}={\operatorname{heart}}_{r}V
. This gives the equalities. Each
{\operatorname{heart}}_{i}V
is superalgebraic, so the result follows.
∎

Corollary 6.13
.

The superalgebraic sets are exactly the closed sets that are invariant under the Weyl groupoid
\mathfrak{G}
.

Proof.

If
V
is closed and

\mathfrak{G}
-invariant, then

V=V^{S}
is superalgebraic. Conversely a superalgebraic set has the form
V=\mathcal{V}(I)
, with
I
an ideal of
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{O}(X)^{\mathfrak{G}}
. Since elements of
\mathcal{A}
are constant on orbits,
V
is a union of
\mathfrak{G}
-orbits.
∎

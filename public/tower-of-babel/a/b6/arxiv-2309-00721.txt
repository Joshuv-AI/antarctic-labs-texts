Geometric Tracking on 𝒮 3 \mathcal{S}^{3} Based on Sliding Mode Control
Eduardo Espindola, Yu Tang ∗

Abstract
Attitude tracking on the unit sphere of dimension 3 3 based on sliding mode is considered in this paper. The tangent bundle of Lagrangian dynamics that describes the rotational motion of a rigid body is first shown to be a Lie group, and then a sliding surface that emerged on it is defined. Next, a sliding-mode controller is designed for attitude tracking that relies on an intrinsic error defined on the Lie group. Almost global asymptotic stability of the closed loop is demonstrated using the Lyapunov analysis. Numerical simulations are included to compare the performance of the sliding mode controller designed on the Lie group with that designed in the embedding Euclidean space.



keywords
Attitude control, Geometric tracking, Lagrangian dynamics, Lie Groups, Sliding subgroups.

Introduction

There has been a growing interest in designing controllers
(
Bullo & Lewis 2019
;
Bullo & Murray 1999
;
Spong & Bullo 2005
)
and observers
(
Aghannan & Rouchon 2003
;
Bonnabel et al. 2008
;
Lageman et al. 2009
;
Zlotnik & Forbes 2018
;
Wang et al. 2021
)
for nonlinear systems on Lie groups due to the stronger stability and robustness that can be achieved by exploiting geometric structures of the configuration manifold such as symmetries and invariance
(
Marsden & Ratiu 1999
)
.
Attitude tracking for rigid bodies using a geometric approach has been reported in
Markdahl et al. 2017
;
Lee 2011
;
Lee et al. 2011
;
Saccon et al. 2013
;
Vang & Tron 2019
and references cited therein. In Refs.
Maithripala et al. 2006
;
Maithripala & Berg 2015
an intrinsic configuration error is defined on a Lie group, and PD and PID controllers are developed with local exponential stability. These intrinsic controllers do not depend on a particular coordinate chosen, and therefore can be applied to any attitude representation in attitude tracking, e.g. rotation matrices or quaternions, with the established local exponential stability.

Sliding mode control is a widely known control methodology recognized for its ability to deal with uncertainties and external disturbances, and has been applied to design robust controller in Euclidean space
(
Utkin 1978
;
Fridman et al. 2011
)
. Its extension to nonlinear manifolds to address attitude control problems with a geometric approach has been studied in
Gómez et al. 2019
;
Ghasemi et al. 2020
;
Meng et al. 2023
. Under a sliding mode controller, the error dynamics first reaches a sliding surface, designed to be invariant and to describe the desired behavior. Upon arriving the sliding surface, the error dynamics slides to the origin according to the desired behavior assigned to the sliding surface.
Several sliding mode controllers applied to the attitude problem of a rigid body have been proposed in
Crassidis & Markley 1996
;
Lo & Chen 1995
;
Lee & Utkin 2007
;
Zhu et al. 2011
. One drawback of these schemes compared to the sliding mode control design with a geometric approach is that the non-Euclidean geometry of the manifold in which the system evolves is not considered in the control design, which could cause undesired behavior of the closed-loop system, such as the presence of undesired equilibria and discontinuity of the closed-loop dynamics
(
Gómez et al. 2019
)
.

To address these issues, in this paper we propose a sliding mode control design on the Lie group
\mathcal{S}^{3}
using unit quaternions. The proposed sliding subgroup enables us to define a new sliding variable with geometric interpretations and allows us to design tracking controllers with stronger robustness properties. The almost global asymptotic stability of the desired equilibrium in the closed-loop system is shown by using the Lyapunov analysis. Numerical simulations are also included to compare this geometric controller with a non-geometric controller designed based on a conventional sliding variable in Euclidean space under the uncertainty in the inertia matrix and noisy measurements. Compared to the group
SO(3)
, the unit quaternions have the advantage of easier manipulation for the implementation of control laws. The main contributions of this paper are the design of sliding mode controllers on the unit quaternion group to track a desired attitude and rigorous proof of the stability of the proposed scheme. Instead of
\mathcal{S}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}

(
Espindola & Tang 2023b
)
, where
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the tangent space at the group identity, the sliding mode control design is developed directly on the state space defined by the tangent bundle
\mathcal{S}^{3}\times T_{q}\mathcal{S}^{3}
. This allows us to explore the Lagrangian dynamics for unit quaternions in the controller design. Towards this end, the state manifold of the Lagrangian dynamics based on quaternions that describe the rotational motion of a rigid body is first shown to be a Lie group under a properly defined group operation. Then a Lie subgroup embedded in the Lie group is designed with the desired property to asymptotically stabilize the identity
\bar{1}\in\mathcal{S}^{3}
of the quaternions group. Finally, an attitude-tracking control law is devised based on the proposed sliding subgroup and the intrinsic error defined on the Lie groups.

Section
2
describes the rotational motion of a rigid body based on a 4-DOF (Degree of Freedom) Lagrangian dynamics using unit quaternions proposed in
Espíndola & Tang 2023a
. Some results and properties of the Lagrangian dynamics useful for the subsequent sliding mode control design are summarized. In Section

, the definition of the group operation is first defined, then the state space of the rotational Lagrangian dynamics is shown to be a Lie group. At last, a sliding surface is defined, which is shown to be embedded on the state space with desired sliding properties. In Section
4
, the evolution of the attitude error is described by a Lagrangian dynamical equation, and a tracking control law is designed.

6
draws the conclusions.

Rotational motion of the rigid body

Given an inertial reference frame
\mathbf{I}
and a body frame
\mathbf{B}
fixed to the center of mass of the body, a rotation matrix
R\in SO(3)
determines the attitude of the rigid body relative to the inertial frame. The rotational motion of the rigid body can be globally described by the following kinematic and dynamic equations

\displaystyle\dot{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}J(q)\omega,

(1)

\displaystyle M\dot{\omega}

\displaystyle=(M\omega)^{\wedge}\omega+\tau,

(2)

where
q=\left[q_{0},\vec{q}^{T}\right]^{T}\in\mathcal{S}^{3}

is the unitary quaternion, which is related to a rotation matrix through Rodriguez formula
R(q)=I_{3}+2q_{0}\vec{q}^{\wedge}+2\left(\vec{q}^{\wedge}\right)^{2}
, with
q_{0}\in[-1,1]
and
\vec{q}\in\mathcal{B}^{2}
denoting the scalar and vector part of the unit quaternion, respectively.
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the angular velocity,
\tau\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the torque control vector, and
M=M^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
is a constant and positive definite inertia tensor of the rigid body, all expressed in the body frame
\mathbf{B}
.
Map
J\vcentcolon\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}^{4\times 3}
is defined as

\displaystyle J(x)

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-\vec{x}^{T}\\
x_{0}I_{3}+\vec{x}^{\wedge}\end{array}\right],\;\forall x=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}x_{0}\\
\vec{x}\end{array}\right],\;x_{0}\in\mathbb{R},\;\vec{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Similarly, maps
L\vcentcolon\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}^{4\times 3}
, and
Q,W\vcentcolon\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}^{4\times 4}
, which will be used in the Lagrangian dynamics, are defined as follows

\displaystyle L(x)=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}-\vec{x}^{T}\\
x_{0}I_{3}-\vec{x}^{\wedge}\end{array}\right],\;\forall x=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}x_{0}\\
\vec{x}\end{array}\right],\;x_{0}\in\mathbb{R},\;\vec{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

\displaystyle Q(x)

\displaystyle=\left[x\;J(x)\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}x_{0}&-\vec{x}^{T}\\
\vec{x}&x_{0}I_{3}+\vec{x}^{\wedge}\end{array}\right],

\displaystyle W(x)

\displaystyle=\left[x\;L(x)\right]=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}x_{0}&-\vec{x}^{T}\\
\vec{x}&x_{0}I_{3}-\vec{x}^{\wedge}\end{array}\right].

Some useful properties of these maps are summarized in Appendix
A
.

The motion equations (
1
)-(
2
) can be described by a
4
-DOF Lagrangian dynamics proposed in

, given by

D(q)\ddot{q}+C(q,\dot{q})\dot{q}=\bar{\tau},

(15)

where

\displaystyle D(q)

\displaystyle=J(q)MJ^{T}(q)+m_{0}qq^{T},

(16)

\displaystyle C(q,\dot{q})

\displaystyle=-J(q)(M\omega)^{\wedge}J^{T}(q)-D(q)Q(\dot{q})Q^{T}(q),

(17)

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}J(q)\tau,

(18)

and
\lambda_{\min}(M)\leq m_{0}\leq\lambda_{\max}(M)
a virtual inertia parameter. Note that the configuration manifold of Lagrangian dynamics (
15
) is
\mathcal{S}^{3}
. Moreover, the system (
15
) verifies the basic properties of Lagrangian dynamics, related to the energy conservation property, summarized in the following lemma.

Lemma 1

(
Properties of the Lagrangian system
)
.

Let
D(q)
and
C(q,\dot{q})
defined in (
16
) and (
17
), respectively, then the following statements hold
(
Espíndola & Tang 2023a
)
:

1.

Matrix
D(q)
is symmetric and positive definite:

\lambda_{min}(M)I_{4}\leq D(q)\leq\lambda_{max}(M)I_{4},\ \ \forall q\in\mathcal{S}^{3}.

(19)

2.

Matrix
\dot{D}(q)-2C(q,\dot{q})
is skew-symmetric:

x^{T}\left(\dot{D}(q)-2C(q,\dot{q})\right)x=0,\ \ \forall q\in\mathcal{S}^{3},\;\forall x\in{R}^{4}.

(20)

This Lagrangian dynamics allows one to address the full attitude control problem, where the attitude may evolve on the whole group
SO(3)
, and to design control laws
\bar{\tau}
based on the energy shaping methodology in Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{4}

(
Espíndola & Tang 2023a
)

using Property

of matrix
J(x)
. In this paper, the geometric structure of the configuration manifold is explored, allowing one to define a sliding subgroup that inherits the geometric structure and acquires the salient features of the sliding mode through the proper design of the controller.

3
Lie Group and Sliding Subgroup

3.1
The State Manifold as a Lie Group

Consider the state manifold of the Lagrangian system (
15
), given by the tangent bundle
T\mathcal{S}^{3}\vcentcolon=\mathcal{S}^{3}\times T_{q}\mathcal{S}^{3}
,
T_{q}\mathcal{S}^{3}
being the tangent space at
q\in\mathcal{S}^{3}
. The following lemma establishes that
T\mathcal{S}^{3}
is a Lie group endowed with the binary operation

\displaystyle g_{1}\cdot g_{2}

\displaystyle=\Big(Q(q_{1})q_{2},\;Q(q_{1})p_{2}+W(q_{2})p_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda Q(q_{1})\big(q_{0,2}q_{2}-\bar{1}\big)+\lambda W(q_{2})\big(q_{0,1}q_{1}-\bar{1}\big)

\displaystyle\quad-\lambda\big(q_{0,12}Q(q_{1})q_{2}-\bar{1}\big)\Big)\in T\mathcal{S}^{3}

(21)

\forall g_{1}=(q_{1},p_{1})
and
g_{2}=(q_{2},p_{2})\in T\mathcal{S}^{3}
, where
\lambda>0
is a scalar,
\bar{1}\vcentcolon=[1,0,0,0]^{T}
is the identity element on
\mathcal{S}^{3}
, and
Q(q_{i})q_{j}=\left[q_{0,ij},\vec{q}^{T}_{ij}\right]^{T}\in\mathcal{S}^{3}
, for
i,j=1,2,3
. Note that
Q(q_{i})q_{j}=q_{i}\otimes q_{j}\in\mathcal{S}^{3}
corresponds to the binary operation for the Lie group
\mathcal{S}^{3}
. Moreover, with a little abuse of notation, it is denoted the triple product
q_{i}\otimes q_{j}\otimes q_{k}=\left[q_{0,ijk},\vec{q}^{T}_{ijk}\right]^{T}
, for all
i,j,k=1,2,3
.

Lemma 2

(
Lie group structure
)
.

The tangent bundle
T\mathcal{S}^{3}
is a Lie group under the binary operation (
3.1
), with

•

Group identity:
e=\left(\bar{1},0_{4\times 1}\right)\in T\mathcal{S}^{3}
,

•

Inverse:
g^{-1}=\left(q^{-1},p^{-1}\right)\in T\mathcal{S}^{3}
,
\forall g=(q,p)\in T\mathcal{S}^{3}
, where
q^{-1}=\left[q_{0},-\vec{q}^{T}\right]^{T}\in\mathcal{S}^{3}
, and
p^{-1}=\left[p_{0},-\vec{p}^{T}\right]^{T}\in T_{q}S^{3}
.

PROOF.

It is clear that
T\mathcal{S}^{3}
is a smooth manifold and that the group operation (
3.1
) is smooth. The group axioms are verified as follows:

1.

\forall g=(q,p)\in T\mathcal{S}^{3}
, it has

\displaystyle g\cdot e

\displaystyle=\Big(Q(q)\bar{1},\;Q(q)0_{4\times 1}+W(\bar{1})p+\lambda Q(q)(\bar{1}-\bar{1})

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\lambda W(\bar{1})(q_{0}q-\bar{1})-\lambda(q_{0}Q(q)\bar{1}-\bar{1})\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(Q(\bar{1})q,\;Q(\bar{1})p+W(q)0_{4\times 1}+\lambda Q(\bar{1})(q_{0}\vec{q}-\bar{1})

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\lambda W(q)(\bar{1}-\bar{1})-\lambda(q_{0}Q(\bar{1})q-\bar{1})\Big)

\displaystyle=e\cdot g=\left(q,p\right)=g.

2.

Let
g^{-1}=\left(q^{-1},p^{-1}\right)\in T\mathcal{S}^{3}
be the inverse of
g=(q,p)\in T\mathcal{S}^{3}
, then

\displaystyle g\cdot g^{-1}=

\displaystyle\Big(Q(q)q^{-1},\;Q(q)p^{-1}+W\left(q^{-1}\right)p+\lambda Q(q)(q_{0}q^{-1}-\bar{1})

\displaystyle\hskip 8.50012pt\hskip 8.50012pt+\lambda W\left(q^{-1}\right)(q_{0}q-\bar{1})-\lambda(Q(q)q^{-1}-\bar{1})\Big)

\displaystyle=\Big(Q\left(q^{-1}\right)q,\;Q\left(q^{-1}\right)p+W(q)p^{-1}+\lambda Q\left(q^{-1}\right)(q_{0}q-\bar{1})

\displaystyle\hskip 8.50012pt\hskip 8.50012pt+\lambda W(q)(q_{0}q^{-1}-\bar{1})-\lambda(Q\left(q^{-1}\right)q-\bar{1})\Big)

\displaystyle=g^{-1}\cdot g=\left(\bar{1},0_{4\times 1}\right)=e,

where the fact that
q^{T}p=0
is used, given that
p\in T_{q}\mathcal{S}^{3}
.

3.

Associativity:
\forall g_{1}=(q_{1},p_{1})
,
g_{2}=(q_{2},p_{2})
and
g_{3}=(q_{3},p_{3})\in T\mathcal{S}^{3}
,

\displaystyle g_{1}

\displaystyle\cdot\left(g_{2}\cdot g_{3}\right)=g_{1}\cdot\Big(Q(q_{2})q_{3},\;Q(q_{2})p_{3}+W(q_{3})p_{2}

\displaystyle+\lambda Q(q_{2})(q_{0,3}q_{3}-\bar{1})+\lambda W(q_{3})(q_{0,2}q_{2}-\bar{1})

\displaystyle-\lambda(q_{0,23}Q(q_{2})q_{3}-\bar{1})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(Q(q_{1})Q(q_{2})q_{3},\;Q(q_{1})\Big(Q(q_{2})p_{3}+W(q_{3})p_{2}

\displaystyle+\lambda Q(q_{2})(q_{0,3}q_{3}-\bar{1})+\lambda W(q_{3})(q_{0,2}q_{2}-\bar{1})

\displaystyle-\lambda(q_{0,23}Q(q_{2})q_{3}-\bar{1})\Big)+W\left(Q(q_{2})q_{3}\right)p_{1}

\displaystyle+\lambda Q(q_{1})(q_{0,23}Q(q_{2})q_{3}-\bar{1})

\displaystyle+\lambda W\left(Q(q_{2})q_{3}\right)(q_{0,1}q_{1}-\bar{1})

\displaystyle-\lambda(q_{0,123}Q(q_{1})Q(q_{2})q_{3}-\bar{1})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(Q\left(Q(q_{1})q_{2}\right)q_{3},\;Q\left(Q(q_{1})q_{2}\right)p_{3}+W(q_{3})\Big(Q(q_{1})p_{2}

\displaystyle+W(q_{2})p_{1}+\lambda Q(q_{1})(q_{0,2}q_{2}-\bar{1})

\displaystyle+\lambda W(q_{2})(q_{0,1}q_{1}-\bar{1})-\lambda(q_{0,12}Q(q_{1})q_{2}-\bar{1})\Big)

\displaystyle+\lambda Q\left(Q(q_{1})q_{2}\right)(q_{0,3}q_{3}-\bar{1})

\displaystyle+\lambda W(q_{3})(q_{0,12}Q(q_{1})q_{2}-\bar{1})

\displaystyle-\lambda(q_{0,123}Q\left(Q(q_{1})q_{2}\right)q_{3}-\bar{1})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(Q(q_{1})q_{2},\;Q(q_{1})p_{2}+W(q_{2})p_{1}

\displaystyle+\lambda Q(q_{1})(q_{0,2}q_{2}-\bar{1})+\lambda W(q_{2})(q_{0,1}q_{1}-\bar{1})

\displaystyle-\lambda(q_{0,12}Q(q_{1})q_{2}-\bar{1})\Big)\cdot g_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(g_{1}\cdot g_{2}\right)\cdot g_{3},

where Properties of matrices
Q(\cdot)
and
W(\cdot)
in Lemma
8
are used.

3.2
Sliding Subgroup

The sliding subgroup, defined below, inherits the structure of the Lie group
T\mathcal{S}^{3}
, allowing us to design sliding mode controllers on the Lie group.

Lemma 3

(
Sliding subgroup
)
.

For
g=(q,p)\in T\mathcal{S}^{3}
, let the sliding variable
s(g)
be defined as

s(g)=p+\lambda\left(q_{0}q-\bar{1}\right)

(22)

where
\lambda>0
is a scalar.

Then the set

H=\left\{g\in T\mathcal{S}^{3}\;|\;s(g)=0_{4\times 1}\right\}

(23)

is a Lie subgroup.

PROOF.

Its clear that (
22
) defines a smooth map. The associativity of
H
is inherited from
T\mathcal{S}^{3}
. Therefore, it remains to verify the following:

•

Identity
e\in H
:
s(e)=s(\bar{1},0_{4\times 1})=0
.

•

Inverse: Let
g=(q,p)\in H
. Then
s(g)=0\implies p=-\lambda\left(q_{0}q-\bar{1}\right)
. Then

\displaystyle s\left(g^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=p^{-1}+\lambda\left(q_{0}q^{-1}-\bar{1}\right)

\displaystyle=-\lambda\left(q_{0}q^{-1}-\bar{1}\right)+\lambda\left(q_{0}q^{-1}-\bar{1}\right)=0_{4\times 1}.

This shows that
g^{-1}\in H
.

•

Closure of the group operation:
\forall g_{1}=(q_{1},p_{1})
and
g_{2}=(q_{2},p_{2})\in H
, it has that
p_{1}=-\lambda\left(q_{0,1}q_{1}-\bar{1}\right)
and
p_{2}=-\lambda\left(q_{0,2}q_{2}-\bar{1}\right)
. Thus,

\displaystyle s\left(g_{1}\cdot g_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=s\Big(Q(q_{1})q_{2},\;Q(q_{1})p_{2}+W(q_{2})p_{1}

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\lambda Q(q_{1})(q_{0,2}q_{2}-\bar{1})+\lambda W(q_{2})(q_{0,1}q_{1}-\bar{1})

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt-\lambda(q_{0,12}Q(q_{1})q_{2}-\bar{1})\Big)

\displaystyle=Q(q_{1})p_{2}+W(q_{2})p_{1}+\lambda Q(q_{1})(q_{0,2}q_{2}-\bar{1})

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\lambda W(q_{2})(q_{0,1}q_{1}-\bar{1})-\lambda(q_{0,12}Q(q_{1})q_{2}-\bar{1})

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\lambda(q_{0,12}Q(q_{1})q_{2}-\bar{1})

\displaystyle=Q(q_{1})(-\lambda(q_{0,2}q_{2}-\bar{1}))

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+W(q_{2})(-\lambda(q_{0,1}q_{1}-\bar{1}))

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\lambda Q(q_{1})(q_{0,2}q_{2}-\bar{1})+\lambda W(q_{2})(q_{0,1}q_{1}-\bar{1})

\displaystyle=0_{4\times 1}.

Therefore,
g_{1}\cdot g_{2}\in H
.

3.3
Sliding Property of the Sliding Subgroup

One of the salient features of sliding mode control is that the behavior of a system on the sliding subgroup is independent of the system dynamics. Define
\mathcal{M}\vcentcolon=\left\{g=(q,p)\in T\mathcal{S}^{3}\;|\;p=\dot{q}\right\}
, then
\mathcal{M}\subset T\mathcal{S}^{3}
. The following lemma establishes the sliding property of the subgroup
\bar{H}\vcentcolon=H\cap\mathcal{M}
, where
H
is defined in (
23
), that is,
\bar{g}\to e
asymptotically provided that
\bar{g}\in\bar{H}
.

Lemma 4

(
Sliding property
)
.

Let
\bar{g}=(q,\dot{q})\in\bar{H}
, then
\bar{g}\to e=(\bar{1},0_{4\times 1})
asymptotically.

PROOF.

Since
\left(q,\dot{q}\right)\in H\;\implies\;s(\bar{g})=0_{4\times 1}
, it follows that

\frac{d}{dt}(q-\bar{1})=\dot{q}=-\lambda(q_{0}q-\bar{1}).

Consider the candidate Lyapunov function
V=\frac{1}{2}\|q-\bar{1}\|^{2}=1-q_{0}
. Its time derivative on the sliding subgroup is

\displaystyle\dot{V}

\displaystyle=(q-\bar{1})^{T}\dot{q}=(q-\bar{1})^{T}\left(-\lambda(q_{0}q-\bar{1})\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda\bar{1}^{T}(q_{0}q-\bar{1})=-\lambda(1-q_{0}^{2})=-\lambda(1+q_{0})V.

For all
q\in\mathcal{S}^{3}\backslash\{-\bar{1}\}
, it has
1+q_{0}<0
. Therefore,
\dot{V}<0
. This shows the almost global asymptotic stability of
q=\bar{1}
.

3.4
Stability of the Sliding Subgroup

Consider the Lagrangian dynamics (
15
) with the configuration manifold
\mathcal{M}
. Let
\bar{g}=\left(q,\dot{q}\right)\in\mathcal{M}
, then the following control law

\bar{\tau}=-\lambda\left(D(q)(q_{0}\dot{q}+\dot{q_{0}}q)+C(q,\dot{q})(q_{0}q-\bar{1})\right)-K_{r}s(\bar{g})

(24)

renders the sliding subgroup
\bar{H}
attractive, where
K_{r}=K^{T}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{4\times 4}
is a positive definite gain matrix.

Lemma 5

(
Stability of the Sliding Subgroup
)
.

The control law (
24
) in closed loop with the system (
15
) renders the equilibrium point
s(\bar{g})=0_{4\times 1}
globally exponentially stable.

PROOF.

By the definition of the sliding variable (
22
) for
s(\bar{g})
and the Lagrangian dynamics (
15
), it has

\displaystyle D(q)\dot{s}

\displaystyle=D(q)\ddot{q}+\lambda D(q)(q_{0}\dot{q}+\dot{q_{0}}q),

\displaystyle=-C(q,\dot{q})\dot{q}+\bar{\tau}+\lambda D(q)(q_{0}\dot{q}+\dot{q_{0}}q).

In closed loop with the control law (
24
) it yields

D(q)\dot{s}=-C(q,\dot{q})s-K_{r}s.

(25)

Consider the following Lyapunov function candidate

V_{r}(s)=\frac{1}{2}s^{T}D(q)s,

(26)

which is positive definite and radially unbounded
according to Property
19
of Lemma
1
.

Remark 6
.

Due to the double coverage of
SO(3)
by
\mathcal{S}^{3}
, both
\pm q
will give the same attitude. Thus, the sliding variable (
22

according to the sign of the scalar part

of the unit quaternion. Furthermore, to ensure robustness to arbitrarily small noise in measurements, a switching strategy may be used in the control law

4
Attitude tracking control

4.1
Control Objectives

Given a desired trajectory
\dot{q}_{d}=\frac{1}{2}J(q_{d})\omega_{d}
, where
\omega_{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a desired angular velocity that is twice differentiable and
q_{d}\in\mathcal{S}^{3}
is the desired attitude, the goal of tracking the attitude is to render
\left(q,\dot{q}\right)\to\left(q_{d},\dot{q}_{d}\right)
asymptotically.

Let
g_{d}:=\left(q_{d},\dot{q}_{d}\right)
,
g:=(q,\dot{q})\in\mathcal{M}
. Then the intrinsic error defined on the Lie group
T\mathcal{S}^{3}
is defined as

\displaystyle g_{e}

\displaystyle=g^{-1}_{d}\cdot g=\left(q_{e},p_{e}\right)\in T\mathcal{S}^{3},

(28)

\displaystyle q_{e}

\displaystyle=Q(q^{-1}_{d})q=Q^{T}(q_{d})q,

(29)

\displaystyle p_{e}

\displaystyle=Q(q^{-1}_{d})\dot{q}+W(q)\dot{q}^{-1}_{d}+\lambda Q(q^{-1}_{d})(q_{0}q-\bar{1})

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda W(q)(q_{0,d}q^{-1}_{d}-\bar{1})-\lambda\left((q^{T}_{d}q)Q(q^{-1}_{d})q-\bar{1}\right).

(30)

Note that by Eq. (
1
),

\dot{q}_{e}=Q(q^{-1}_{d})\dot{q}+W(q)\dot{q}^{-1}_{d}=\frac{1}{2}J(q_{e})\omega_{e},

(31)

where
\omega_{e}=\omega-R^{T}(q_{e})\omega_{d}

is the angular velocity error.

Therefore,
\bar{g}_{e}=(q_{e},\dot{q}_{e})\in\mathcal{M}
describes the intrinsic error on the tangent bundle of the Lagrangian system

D(q_{e})\ddot{q}_{e}+C(q_{e},\dot{q}_{e})\dot{q}_{e}=\bar{\tau}_{c},

(32)

with

\displaystyle D(q_{e})

\displaystyle=J(q_{e})MJ^{T}(q_{e})+m_{0}q_{e}q_{e}^{T},

(33)

\displaystyle C(q_{e},\dot{q}_{e})

\displaystyle=-J(q_{e})w^{\wedge}_{r}J^{T}(q_{e})-D(q_{e})Q(\dot{q}_{e})Q^{T}(q_{e}),

(34)

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}_{c}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}J(q_{e})\tau_{c},

(35)

where

\displaystyle w_{r}

\displaystyle=M\omega_{e}-\left(\mathrm{tr}(M)I_{3}-2M\right)R^{T}(q_{e})\omega_{d},

(36)

\displaystyle\tau_{c}

\displaystyle=S\left(MR^{T}(q_{e})\omega_{d}\right)R^{T}(q_{e})\omega_{d}-MR^{T}(q_{e})\dot{\omega}_{d}+\tau.

(37)

Therefore, it follows from the results of the previous section that the control objectives are achieved by designing a control law for
\bar{\tau}_{c}
that makes the sliding subgroup
\bar{H}
attractive. Subsequently,
\bar{g}_{e}\to e
as a consequence of the sliding property.

4.2
Controller Design

The intrinsic error
g_{e}\in T\mathcal{S}^{3}
can be expressed in the state manifold of the Lagrangian system (
32
) as
\bar{g}_{e}=(q_{e},\dot{q}_{e})\in\mathcal{M}
. The sliding variable therefore, according to (
22
), is given by

s\left(\bar{g}_{e}\right)=\dot{q}_{e}+\lambda(q_{0,e}q_{e}-\bar{1}),

(38)

\lambda>0
being a design parameter. The following control law will render the sliding subgroup
\bar{H}
attractive

\displaystyle\bar{\tau}_{c}

\displaystyle=-\lambda\left(D(q_{e})\left(q_{0,e}\dot{q}_{e}+\dot{q}_{0,e}q_{e}\right)+C(q_{e},\dot{q}_{e})\left(q_{0,e}q_{e}-\bar{1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-K_{r}s\left(\bar{g}_{e}\right),

(39)

where

K_{r}=K^{T}_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{4\times 4}
is a positive definite gain matrix.

Theorem 7

)

The control law (
39
) in closed loop with the Lagrangian dynamics (
32
) stabilizes the equilibrium point
\bar{g}_{e}=e
almost globally asymptotically.

PROOF.

It follows directly of the sliding variable
s(\bar{g}_{e})
as a consequence of Lemma
3
, Lemma
4
and Lemma
5

6
Conclusions

A geometric sliding mode controller on the quaternion group was developed in this paper for attitude tracking. The state manifold of the Lagrangian dynamics that describes the attitude using the unit quaternion was shown to be a Lie group. A sliding subgroup that heritages the Lie group structure was designed, which allows us to design a sliding mode control on the Lie group. The almost global asymptotic stability of the closed-loop system was achieved. The performance of the proposed geometric sliding mode controller was illustrated and compared with a non-geometric sliding mode controller in the simulations, showing that the geometric controller performed better than the non-geometric controller in the presence of uncertain inertia matrix and noise in the measurement. Furthermore, more energy efficiency was observed in the geometric controller.

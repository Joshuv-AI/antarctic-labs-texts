Operator Norm Bounds on the Correlation Matrix of the SK Model at High Temperature
Christian Brennecke, Changji Xu, Horng-Tzer Yau

Abstract
We prove that the correlation matrix M = ( ⟨ σ i ​ σ j ⟩ − ⟨ σ i ⟩ ​ ⟨ σ j ⟩ ) 1 ≤ i , j ≤ N ∈ ℝ N × N \textbf{M}=(\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle-\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle\langle\sigma_{j}\rangle)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N} of the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model has the property that for every ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 there exists K ϵ > 0 K_{\epsilon}>0 , that is independent of N N , such that ℙ ⁡ ( ‖ M ‖ op ≤ K ϵ ) ≥ 1 − ϵ \mathbb{P}\big(\|\textbf{M}\|_{\text{op}}\leq K_{\epsilon}\big)\geq 1-\epsilon for N N large enough, for suitable interaction and external field parameters ( β , h ) (\beta,h) in the replica symmetric region. In other words, the operator norm of M is of order one with high probability. Our results are in particular valid for all ( β , h ) ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) × ( 0 , ∞ ) (\beta,h)\in(0,1)\times(0,\infty) and thus complement recently obtained results in [ 20 , 9 ] that imply the operator norm boundedness of M for all β < 1 \beta<1 in the special case of a vanishing external field.

Setup and Main Results

We consider systems of
N

H_{N}(\sigma)=\beta\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}g_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}+h\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_{i}=\frac{\beta}{2}(\sigma,\textbf{G}\sigma)+h\,(\textbf{1},\sigma).

(1.1)

The symmetric matrix
\textbf{G}=(g_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
is a GOE matrix, that is, up to the symmetry constraint its entries are i.i.d. centered Gaussian random variables of variance
N^{-1}
for

and we set

for simplicity. The standard Euclidean inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and its induced norm are denoted by

and
\|\cdot\|
, respectively. We assume
\beta>0
,

and we assume the
\{g_{ij}\}
to be realized in some probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})

\mathbb{P}

Based on a novel representation of the entries of the two point correlation matrix

\textbf{M}=\textbf{M}_{\beta,h}=(m_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}=\big(\langle\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rangle-\langle\sigma_{i}\rangle\langle\sigma_{j}\rangle\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}

(1.2)

in probability. Here,
\|\textbf{A}\|_{\text{op}}=\sup_{\textbf{u}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\|\textbf{u}\|=1}\|\textbf{A}\textbf{u}\|
denotes the standard operator norm, for

, and

\langle f\rangle=\frac{1}{Z_{N}}\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}f(\sigma)\,e^{H_{N}(\sigma)}\hskip 14.22636pt\text{with}\hskip 14.22636ptZ_{N}=\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}e^{H_{N}(\sigma)}

for

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume
(\beta,\mu_{h},\sigma_{h}^{2})\in\mathcal{A}_{RS^{-}}
and
h\sim\mathcal{N}(\mu_{h},\sigma_{h}^{2})
. Set
A_{m}=m/2
if

and
A_{m}=m/2+1/2
if
m\geq 3
.
Then, we have for every
k\geq 0,p\geq 1
with

that

\displaystyle N^{-p}\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{p}=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\,m_{j_{1}\ldots j_{p};k}^{2}\leq C_{\beta,h}N^{-A_{2k+p}}

for some
C_{\beta,h}>0
that is locally bounded in
(\beta,\mu_{h},\sigma_{h}^{2})
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we give a detailed proof for the cases
k=0
and
k=1
; the remaining cases
k\geq 2
are proved with analogous arguments. We use the representation

m_{j_{1}\ldots j_{p}}=\Big\langle\sigma^{1}_{j_{1}}\prod_{u=1}^{p-1}\Big(\sum_{v=1}^{u}(\sigma^{v}_{j_{u+1}}-\sigma^{u+1}_{j_{u+1}})\Big)\Big\rangle,

(2.7)

where we recall that
(\sigma^{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
denote i.i.d. replicas sampled from
\langle\cdot\rangle
. Eq. (
2.7
) follows from

\displaystyle\partial_{h_{i}}\left\langle{f(\sigma^{1},\ldots,\sigma^{u})}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma^{1},\ldots,\sigma^{u}\in\{-1,1\}^{N}}f(\sigma^{1},\ldots,\sigma^{u})\,\partial_{h_{i}}\frac{e^{H_{N}(\sigma^{1})+\ldots+H_{N}(\sigma^{u})}}{Z_{N}^{u}}

\displaystyle=\Big\langle f(\sigma^{1},\ldots,\sigma^{u})\Big(\sum_{v=1}^{u}\sigma^{v}_{i}-u\sigma^{u+1}_{i}\Big)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\langle f(\sigma^{1},\ldots,\sigma^{u})\sum_{v=1}^{u}(\sigma^{v}_{i}-\sigma^{u+1}_{i})\Big\rangle

and induction. Observe that the previous identity implies in particular that

\Big\langle\prod_{u=1}^{p-1}\Big(\sum_{v=1}^{u}(\sigma^{v}_{j_{u+1}}-\sigma^{u+1}_{j_{u+1}})\Big)\Big\rangle=\partial_{h_{j_{2}}}\ldots\partial_{h_{j_{p}}}\left\langle{1}\right\rangle=0\,.

(2.8)

Now, consider first the case
k=0
. Recalling that
(\sigma^{l})_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}
denotes a sequence of i.i.d. replicas sampled from
\langle\cdot\rangle
, Eq. (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) imply that we can express
m_{j_{1}\ldots j_{p}}^{2}
as

\begin{split}m_{j_{1}\ldots j_{p}}^{2}&=\Big\langle\big(\sigma^{1}_{j_{1}}\sigma^{-1}_{j_{1}}-N^{-1}\|\textbf{m}\|^{2}\big)\prod_{u=1}^{p-1}\Big(\sum_{v=1}^{u}(\sigma^{v}_{j_{u+1}}-\sigma^{u+1}_{j_{u+1}})\Big)\Big(\sum_{v^{\prime}=-1}^{-u}(\sigma^{v^{\prime}}_{j_{u+1}}-\sigma^{-(u+1)}_{j_{u+1}})\Big)\Big\rangle\\
\end{split}

so that

\begin{split}&N^{-p}\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{p}=1}^{N}m_{j_{1}\ldots j_{p}}^{2}\\
&=\Big\langle\big(R_{1,-1}-N^{-1}\|\textbf{m}\|^{2}\big)\prod_{u=1}^{p-1}\sum_{v=1}^{u}\sum_{v^{\prime}=-1}^{-u}\big(R_{v,v^{\prime}}-R_{v,-(u+1)}-R_{u+1,v^{\prime}}+R_{u+1,-(u+1)}\big)\Big\rangle\\
&=\Big\langle(T_{1,-1}+T_{1}+T_{-1})\prod_{u=1}^{p-1}\sum_{v=1}^{u}\sum_{v^{\prime}=-1}^{-u}\big(T_{v,v^{\prime}}-T_{v^{\prime},-(u+1)}-T_{u+1,v^{\prime}}+T_{u+1,-(u+1)}\big)\Big\rangle.\end{split}

Here, we used in the second step that
R_{l,l^{\prime}}=T_{l,l^{\prime}}+T_{l}+T_{l^{\prime}}+N^{-1}\|\textbf{m}\|^{2}
and that

\begin{split}&R_{v,v^{\prime}}-R_{v,-(u+1)}-R_{u+1,v^{\prime}}+R_{u+1,-(u+1)}\\
&=T_{v,v^{\prime}}+T_{v}+T_{v^{\prime}}+N^{-1}\|\textbf{m}\|^{2}-T_{v,-(u+1)}-T_{v}-T_{-(u+1)}-N^{-1}\|\textbf{m}\|^{2}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt-T_{u+1,v^{\prime}}-T_{u+1}-T_{v^{\prime}}-N^{-1}\|\textbf{m}\|^{2}+T_{u+1,-(u+1)}+T_{u+1}+T_{-(u+1)}+N^{-1}\|\textbf{m}\|^{2}\\
&=T_{v,v^{\prime}}-T_{v^{\prime},-(u+1)}-T_{u+1,v^{\prime}}+T_{u+1,-(u+1)}.\end{split}

Now, applying and using the same notation as in Theorem
2.2
, we get

\Big|N^{-p}\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{p}=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\,m_{j_{1}\ldots j_{p}}^{2}-N^{-\frac{p}{2}}E\prod_{u=0}^{p-1}Z_{u}\Big|\leq CN^{-\frac{p+1}{2}},

(2.9)

where
(Z_{u})_{0\leq u\leq p-1}
is a Gaussian random vector whose entries are defined by

Z_{u}=\begin{cases}U_{1,-1}+U_{1}+U_{-1}&:u=0,\\
\sum_{v=1}^{u}\sum_{v^{\prime}=-1}^{-u}\big(U_{vv^{\prime}}-U_{v,-(u+1)}-U_{u+1,v^{\prime}}+U_{u+1,-(u+1)}\big)&:u\geq 1.\end{cases}

This proves the lemma for the case
k=0
and

. To obtain an additional decay factor

\begin{split}EZ_{u}Z_{u^{\prime}}=&\,E\,\sum_{v=1}^{u}\sum_{v^{\prime}=-1}^{-u}\sum_{w=1}^{u^{\prime}}\sum_{w^{\prime}=-1}^{-u^{\prime}}\big(U_{vv^{\prime}}-U_{v,-(u+1)}-U_{u+1,v^{\prime}}+U_{u+1,-(u+1)}\big)U_{ww^{\prime}}\\

for all

. Since

is a Gaussian vector, we can then write

where
\textbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{p-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{p-1}
and where

is a Gaussian random variable independent of the remaining entries

. This implies for

that

u^{\prime}\neq u\end{subarray}}^{p-1}Z_{u^{\prime}}=0

and combining this with (

), we conclude that

\Big| N^{-p}\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{p}=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\,m_{j_{1}\ldots j_{p}}^{2}\Big| \leq CN^{-\frac{p+1}{2}}.

Next, consider the case
k=1
. By (

\displaystyle m_{j_{1}\ldots j_{p};1}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{N}m_{i_{1}i_{1}j_{1}\ldots j_{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{N}\Big\langle\sigma_{i_{1}}^{1}(\sigma_{i_{1}}^{1}-\sigma_{i_{1}}^{2})\prod_{u=2}^{p+1}\Big(\sum_{v=1}^{u}\big(\sigma^{v}_{j_{u-1}}-\sigma^{u+1}_{j_{u-1}}\big)\Big)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\langle(1-R_{1,2})\prod_{u=2}^{p+1}\Big(\sum_{v=1}^{u}\big(\sigma^{v}_{j_{u-1}}-\sigma^{u+1}_{j_{u-1}}\big)\Big)\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\big(N^{-1}\|\textbf{m}\|^{2}-R_{1,2}\big)\prod_{u=2}^{p+1}\Big(\sum_{v=1}^{u}\big(\sigma^{v}_{j_{u-1}}-\sigma^{u+1}_{j_{u-1}}\big)\Big)\Big\rangle,

where we used (

) in the last step. Hence

\begin{split}m_{j_{1}\ldots j_{p};1}^{2}&=\Big\langle\big(N^{-1}\|\textbf{m}\|^{2}-R_{12}\big)\big(N^{-1}\|\textbf{m}\|^{2}-R_{-1,-2}\big)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt\times\prod_{u=2}^{p+1}\Big(\sum_{v=1}^{u}(\sigma^{v}_{j_{u-1}}-\sigma^{u+1}_{j_{u-1}})\Big)\Big(\sum_{v^{\prime}=-1}^{-u}(\sigma^{v^{\prime}}_{j_{u-1}}-\sigma^{-(u+1)}_{j_{u-1}})\Big)\Big\rangle\end{split}

and thus, arguing as in the first step,

\displaystyle N^{-p}\!\!\!\sum_{j_{1},\ldots,j_{p}=1}^{N}\!\!\!\mathbb{E}\,m_{j_{1}\ldots j_{p};1}^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\,\Big\langle(T_{1}+T_{2}+T_{12})(T_{-1}+T_{-2}+T_{-1,-2})

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\times\prod_{u=2}^{p+1}\Big(\sum_{v=1}^{u}\sum_{v^{\prime}=-1}^{-u}\big(T_{v,v^{\prime}}-T_{v,-(u+1)}-T_{u+1,v^{\prime}}+T_{u+1,-(u+1)}\big)\Big)\Big\rangle.

\mathbb{E}(\cdot)
over the previous two tail bounds to see that they hold true unconditionally. Thus, we conclude

\begin{split}\lim_{N\to\infty}\bigg|\!\sup_{(\textbf{u},\textbf{v})\in S_{N,\varepsilon,k}}\Big[\beta\big(\textbf{u},\textbf{G}^{(k+1)}\textbf{u}\big)+\textbf{f}\,(\textbf{u},\textbf{v})\Big]-\mathbb{E}_{k}\!\!\sup_{(\textbf{u},\textbf{v})\in S_{N,\varepsilon,k}}\Big[\beta\big(\textbf{u},\textbf{G}^{(k+1)}\textbf{u}\big)+\textbf{f}\,(\textbf{u},\textbf{v})\Big]\bigg|&=0\end{split}

and

\begin{split}&\lim_{N\to\infty}\bigg|\sup_{(\textbf{u},\textbf{v})\in S_{N,\varepsilon,k}}\bigg[\frac{2\beta}{\sqrt{N}}\|\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\mathbf{u}\|(\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\textbf{u},\xi)+\textbf{f}\,(\textbf{u},\textbf{v})\bigg]\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt-\mathbb{E}_{k}\sup_{(\textbf{u},\textbf{v})\in S_{N,\varepsilon,k}}\bigg[\frac{2\beta}{\sqrt{N}}\|\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\mathbf{u}\|(\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\textbf{u},\xi)+\textbf{f}\,(\textbf{u},\textbf{v})\bigg]\bigg|=0\end{split}

in the sense of probability. Combining these observations with (
4.5
), we finally arrive at

\begin{split}&\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\text{p-}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\bigg[\sup_{(\textbf{u},\textbf{v})\in S_{N,\varepsilon,k}}\Big(\textbf{u},\big(\beta\textbf{G}+\frac{2\beta^{2}}{N}\textbf{v}\otimes\textbf{v}-\textbf{D}_{\textbf{v}}-\beta^{2}(1-q)\big)\textbf{u}\Big)\bigg]\\
&\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\text{p-}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\bigg[\sup_{(\textbf{u},\textbf{v})\in S_{N,\varepsilon,k}}\text{F}\big(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\xi,\mathbf{M}^{(k)},\mathbf{Z}^{(k)}\big)\bigg],\end{split}

(4.6)

where
\textbf{M}^{(k)}
and
\textbf{Z}^{(k)}
denote the matrices

\mathbf{M}^{(k)}=(\mathbf{m}^{(1)}\,\mathbf{m}^{(2)}\,\ldots\,\mathbf{m}^{(k)})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times k},\hskip 14.22636pt\mathbf{Z}^{(k)}=(\mathbf{z}^{(2)},\ldots,\mathbf{z}^{(k+1)})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times k}

and where

\begin{split}\text{F}\big(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\xi,\mathbf{M}^{(k)},\mathbf{Z}^{(k)}\big)&=\frac{2\beta}{\sqrt{N}}\|\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\mathbf{u}\|(\textbf{u},\xi)+2\beta\big(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{Z}^{(k)}\big(\mathbf{M}^{(k)T}\mathbf{M}^{(k)}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{M}^{(k)T}\mathbf{u}\big)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\frac{2\beta^{2}}{N}(\textbf{u},\textbf{m}^{(k)})^{2}-(\textbf{u},\textbf{D}_{\textbf{v}}\textbf{u})-\beta^{2}(1-q).\end{split}

Here, we used additionally that

\sup_{\|\textbf{v}-\textbf{m}^{(k)}\|^{2}\leq N\varepsilon}N^{-1}\big\|\textbf{v}\otimes\textbf{v}-\textbf{m}^{(k)}\otimes\textbf{m}^{(k)}\big\|_{\text{op}}\leq 2\sqrt{\varepsilon}

and that, in the sense of probability, we have

\begin{split}\lim_{N\to\infty}\big|\sup_{\|\textbf{u}\|^{2}=1}N^{-1/2}\big(\textbf{P}^{(k)}\textbf{u},\xi\big)\big|\leq\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{-1/2}\big\|\textbf{P}^{(k)}\xi\big\|&=0,\\
\lim_{N\to\infty}\Big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\zeta^{(s)}\otimes\phi^{(s)}-\,\mathbf{Z}^{(k)}\big(\mathbf{M}^{(k)T}\mathbf{M}^{(k)}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{M}^{(k)T}\Big\|_{\text{op}}&=0.\end{split}

The last two identities readily follow e.g. from a simple second moment bound and, respectively, from Prop.
4.2
. Notice that both matrices
N^{-1/2}\sum_{s=1}^{k}\zeta^{(s)}\otimes\phi^{(s)}
and
\mathbf{Z}^{(k)}\big(\mathbf{M}^{(k)T}\mathbf{M}^{(k)}\big)^{-1}\mathbf{M}^{(k)T}
are at most of rank
k
(
\textbf{Q}^{(k)}\mathbb{R}^{N}
contained in their kernels) so that the norm convergence follows from pointwise convergence on the vectors
(\textbf{m}^{(s)})_{s=1}^{k}
.

Step 2:
The remainder of the proof is based on analyzing the optimization problem on the r.h.s. of Eq. (
4.6
). This can be done with the same arguments as in
[
12
, Sections 4.1 to 4.4]
. For completeness, we translate the arguments from
[
12
]
to the present setting. In particular, we point out at which steps the AT condition (
1.5
) enters the analysis.

First, we control the r.h.s. of Eq. (
4.6
) through an optimization problem that only involves the limiting distributions of
\textbf{M}^{(k)}
and
\textbf{Z}^{(k)}
, based on Theorem
4.3
. Setting

\mu^{(k)}_{N}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\sqrt{N}u_{i},v_{i},\xi_{i},\textbf{M}^{(k)}_{i\cdot}\!,\,\textbf{Z}^{(k)}_{i\cdot}}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2k+3}),

where by
\textbf{X}_{i\cdot}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we denote the
i
-th row of
\textbf{X}\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, and setting

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(f)=\int\mu(dx)\,f(x),\hskip 5.69046pt\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(S)=\int\mu(dx)\,\textbf{1}_{S}(x)=\mu(S),\hskip 5.69046pt\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}=\int\mu(dx)\,f(x)g(x)

for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2k+3})
, we have that

\text{F}\big(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\xi,\mathbf{M}^{(k)},\mathbf{Z}^{(k)}\big)=\Phi\big(\mu_{N}^{(k)}\big),

where for every
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2k+3})
with finite second moment, we define

\begin{split}\Phi(\mu)&=2\beta\,\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\text{U}\underline{\text{Z}}^{T})\big[\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\underline{\text{M}}\,\underline{\text{M}}^{T})\big]^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\underline{\text{M}}\text{U})+2\beta\|\textbf{Q}_{\underline{\text{M}}}\text{U}\|_{L^{2}(d\mu)}\langle\text{U},\Xi\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+2\beta^{2}\langle M_{k},\text{U}\rangle^{2}_{L^{2}(d\mu)}-\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\big((1-\text{V}^{2})^{-1}\text{U}^{2}\big)-\beta^{2}(1-q).\end{split}

Here, the coordinates in the integrals over
\mathbb{R}^{2k+3}
w.r.t. to
\mu
are denoted by
(\text{U},\text{V},\Xi,\underline{\text{M}},\underline{\text{Z}})
(or, in other words,
\mathcal{L}(\text{U},\text{V},\Xi,\underline{\text{M}},\underline{\text{Z}})=\mu
) and
\textbf{Q}_{\underline{\text{M}}}
denotes the projection onto the orthogonal complement of the coordinates of
\underline{\text{M}}=(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
, i.e.

\textbf{Q}_{\underline{\text{M}}}\text{U}=\text{U}-\underline{\text{M}}^{T}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\,\underline{\text{M}}\,\underline{\text{M}}^{T}\big)^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\,\underline{\text{M}}\text{U}.

To stay consistent with the construction in (
4.1
) and with the previous notation, we write in the following
\underline{\text{Z}}=(Z_{2},\dots,Z_{k+1})
. Now, applying
[
12
, Lemma 1]
(whose proof in
[
12
, Appendix B.2]
carries over directly to the present context upon replacing the functional
\text{F}_{\text{x},k}
from
[
12
]
by
\Phi
defined above, based on Prop.
4.2
), we obtain the upper bound

\begin{split}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\text{p-}\limsup_{N\to\infty}\sup_{(\textbf{u},\textbf{v})\in S_{N,\varepsilon,k}}\text{F}\big(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v},\xi,\mathbf{M}^{(k)},\mathbf{Z}^{(k)}\big)&\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon,k}}\Phi(\mu),\end{split}

(4.7)

where

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon,k}=\big\{&\mu=\mathcal{L}(\text{U},\text{V},\Xi,\underline{\text{M}},\underline{\text{Z}}):\,\|\text{U}\|_{L^{2}(d\mu)}^{2}=1,\;\|\text{V}-M_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(d\mu)}\leq\varepsilon,\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(|\text{V}|<1)=1,\\
&\,\underline{\text{Z}}=(Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{k+1})\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{K}_{\leq k}),\,\underline{\text{M}}=(M_{1},\ldots,M_{k})\text{ with }M_{1}=\sqrt{q},\\
&\,M_{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s>1}}{{=}}\tanh(h+\beta Z_{s-1}),\,\Xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)\text{ independent of }\underline{\text{Z}}\,\big\}.\end{split}

Next, using that
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\underline{\text{Z}}\,\underline{\text{Z}}^{T})=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\underline{\text{M}}\,\underline{\text{M}}^{T})=\textbf{K}_{\leq k}
, we observe that

\begin{split}&\big\|\underline{\text{Z}}^{T}\big[\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\underline{\text{M}}\,\underline{\text{M}}^{T})\big]^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\underline{\text{M}}\text{U})\big\|^{2}_{L^{2}(d\mu)}+\|\textbf{Q}_{\underline{\text{M}}}\text{U}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(d\mu)}\|\Xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(d\mu)}\\
&=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\underline{\text{M}}\text{U})^{T}\big[\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\underline{\text{M}}\,\underline{\text{M}}^{T})\big]^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\underline{\text{Z}}\,\underline{\text{Z}}^{T})\big[\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\underline{\text{M}}\,\underline{\text{M}}^{T})\big]^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\underline{\text{M}}\text{U})+\|\textbf{Q}_{\underline{\text{M}}}\text{U}\|_{L^{2}(d\mu)}^{2}=1\end{split}

so that, by the independence of
\underline{\text{Z}}
and
\Xi
, we have that under
\mu

\underline{\text{Z}}^{T}\big[\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\underline{\text{M}}\,\underline{\text{M}}^{T})\big]^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\underline{\text{M}}\text{U})+\|\textbf{Q}_{\underline{\text{M}}}\text{U}\|_{L^{2}(d\mu)}\Xi\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1).

Arguing similarly that

\big\langle Z_{k+1},\underline{\text{Z}}^{T}\big[\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\underline{\text{M}}\,\underline{\text{M}}^{T})\big]^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\underline{\text{M}}\text{U})\big\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}=\langle M_{k},\text{U}\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}

and using that
\|Z_{k+1}\|_{L^{2}(d\mu)}=\|M_{k}\|_{L^{2}(d\mu)}=\sqrt{q}
, we can thus write

\begin{split}\underline{\text{Z}}^{T}\big[\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\underline{\text{M}}\,\underline{\text{M}}^{T})\big]^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\underline{\text{M}}\text{U})+\|\textbf{Q}_{\underline{\text{M}}}\text{U}\|_{L^{2}(d\mu)}\Xi=q^{-1}\langle M_{k},\text{U}\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}Z_{k+1}+\|\textbf{Q}_{M_{k}}\text{U}\|_{L^{2}(d\mu)}\,\Xi^{\prime}\end{split}

for some Gaussian
\Xi^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, which is independent of
Z_{k+1}
, and where
Q_{M_{k}}
denotes the projection onto the orthogonal complement of
M_{k}
in
L^{2}(d\mu)
. In particular, we have

\begin{split}\Phi(\mu)&=\frac{2\beta}{q}\,\langle\text{U},M_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}\langle Z_{k+1},\text{U}\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}+2\beta\|\textbf{Q}_{M_{k}}\text{U}\|_{L^{2}(d\mu)}\langle\text{U},\Xi^{\prime}\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+2\beta^{2}\langle M_{k},U\rangle^{2}_{L^{2}(d\mu)}-\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\big((1-\text{V}^{2})^{-1}\text{U}^{2}\big)-\beta^{2}(1-q),\end{split}

and setting
M_{k+1}=\tanh(h+\beta Z_{k+1})
, such that under the AT condition (
1.5
) we have
\|M_{k+1}-M_{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(d\mu)}=2q-2\alpha_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
by Lemma
4.1
and Prop.
4.2
4), we find

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\limsup_{k\to\infty}\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon,k}}\Phi(\mu)\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}}\Psi(\mu),

(4.8)

where
\Psi
is defined by

\begin{split}\Psi(\mu)&=2\beta q^{-1/2}\,\langle\text{U},\text{M}\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}\langle\text{Z},\text{U}\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}+2\beta\sqrt{1-q^{-1}\langle\text{M},\text{U}\rangle^{2}_{L^{2}(d\mu)}}\langle\text{W},\text{U}\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+2\beta^{2}\langle\text{M},\text{U}\rangle^{2}_{L^{2}(d\mu)}-\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\big((1-\text{V}^{2})^{-1}\text{U}^{2}\big)-\beta^{2}(1-q),\end{split}

for every
\mu=\mathcal{L}(\text{M},\text{U},\text{V},\text{W},\text{Z})\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}
, with
\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}
defined by

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}=\big\{&\mathcal{L}(\text{M},\text{U},\text{V},\text{W},\text{Z}):\|\text{U}\|_{L^{2}(d\mu)}^{2}=1,\|\text{V}-\text{M}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(d\mu)}\leq\varepsilon,\\
&\;\;\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(|\text{V}|<1)=1,(\text{W},\text{Z})\sim\mathcal{N}\big(0,\text{id}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big),\text{M}=\tanh(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})\big\}.\end{split}

Step 3:
Finally, we need to analyze the optimization problem on the r.h.s. in Eq. (
4.8
). Here, we introduce Lagrange multipliers and simply upper bound the r.h.s. by

\begin{split}\Psi(\mu)&=2\beta q^{-1/2}\,\langle\text{U},\text{M}\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}\langle\text{Z},\text{U}\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}+2\beta\sqrt{1-q^{-1}\langle\text{M},\text{U}\rangle^{2}_{L^{2}(d\mu)}}\langle\text{W},\text{U}\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+2\beta^{2}\langle\text{M},\text{U}\rangle^{2}_{L^{2}(d\mu)}-\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\big((1-\text{V}^{2})^{-1}\text{U}^{2}\big)-\beta^{2}(1-q)\\
&=2\beta q^{-1/2}x\,\,\langle\text{Z},\text{U}\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}+2\beta\sqrt{1-q^{-1}x^{2}}\,\langle\text{W},\text{U}\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}+2\beta^{2}x^{2}-\beta^{2}(1-q)\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\lambda_{u}(\|U\|_{L^{2}(d\mu)}^{2}-1)+\lambda_{x}(\langle\text{U},\text{M}\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}-x)-\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\big((1-\text{V}^{2})^{-1}\text{U}^{2}\big)\\
&\leq 2\beta^{2}x^{2}-\beta^{2}(1-q)-\lambda_{u}-\lambda_{x}x+\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\Theta(\text{M},\text{V},\text{W},\text{Z}),\end{split}

where we set
x=x(\text{U},\text{M})=\langle\text{U},\text{M}\rangle_{L^{2}(d\mu)}
as well as

\begin{split}\Theta(m,v,w,z)=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}\Big[\big(2\beta q^{-1/2}xz+2\beta\sqrt{1-q^{-1}x^{2}}\,w+\lambda_{x}m\big)u+\big(\lambda_{u}-(1-v^{2})^{-1}\big)u^{2}\Big].\end{split}

Fixing from now on
\lambda_{u}<1
and assuming w.l.o.g.
|v|<1
(in accordance to the fact that
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}(|V|<1)=1
), strict concavity implies that

\Theta(m,v,w,z)=\frac{1}{4}\frac{\big(2\beta q^{-1/2}xz+2\beta\sqrt{1-q^{-1}x^{2}}\,w+\lambda_{x}m\big)^{2}}{\big((1-v^{2})^{-1}-\lambda_{u}\big)}.

Together with the global Lipschitz continuity of
[-1,1]\ni v\mapsto\big((1-v^{2})^{-1}-\lambda_{u}\big)^{-1}\in[0,\infty)
, we thus obtain for every fixed
\lambda_{u}<1
and
K>0
the simple upper bound

\begin{split}&\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}}\Psi(\mu)\\
&\leq\max_{|x|\leq 1}\min_{|\lambda_{x}|\leq K}\bigg[2\beta^{2}x^{2}-\beta^{2}(1-q)-\lambda_{u}-\lambda_{x}x\\
&\hskip 71.13188pt+\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\frac{\big(2\beta q^{-1/2}x\text{Z}+2\beta\sqrt{1-q^{-1}x^{2}}\,\text{W}+\lambda_{x}\text{M}\big)^{2}}{4(\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})-\lambda_{u})}\bigg]\\
&=\max_{|x|\leq 1}\min_{|\lambda_{x}|\leq K}\bigg[2\beta^{2}x^{2}-\beta^{2}(1-q)-\lambda_{u}+\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\frac{\beta^{2}(1-q^{-1}x^{2})}{(\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})-\lambda_{u})}\\
&\hskip 91.04872pt-\lambda_{x}x+\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\frac{\beta^{2}q^{-1}x^{2}\text{Z}^{2}+\beta q^{-1/2}x\lambda_{x}\text{M}\text{Z}+\lambda_{x}^{2}\text{M}^{2}/4}{(\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})-\lambda_{u})}\bigg].\end{split}

(4.9)

Notice that, in the last step, we used the independence of
W
of
Z
and, consequently, of
\text{M}=\tanh(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})
. Applying Gaussian integration by parts w.r.t.
Z
and rescaling
x
and
\lambda_{x}
by a factor
1/\sqrt{q}
and respectively
\sqrt{q}
, we find that

\begin{split}&\max_{|x|\leq 1}\min_{|\lambda_{x}|\leq K}\bigg[2\beta^{2}x^{2}-\beta^{2}(1-q)-\lambda_{u}+\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\frac{\beta^{2}(1-q^{-1}x^{2})}{(\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})-\lambda_{u})}\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt-\lambda_{x}x+\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\frac{\beta^{2}q^{-1}x^{2}\text{Z}^{2}+\beta q^{-1/2}x\lambda_{x}\text{M}\text{Z}+\lambda_{x}^{2}\text{M}^{2}/4}{(\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})-\lambda_{u})}\bigg]\\
&=\max_{|x|\leq q^{-1/2}}\bigg\{2\beta^{2}qx^{2}-\beta^{2}(1-q)-\lambda_{u}+\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\frac{\beta^{2}}{(\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})-\lambda_{u})}\\
&\hskip 62.59596pt-\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\frac{2\beta^{4}q(\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})+\sinh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z}))}{(\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})-\lambda_{u})^{2}}x^{2}\\
&\hskip 62.59596pt+\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\frac{8\beta^{4}q\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})\sinh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})}{(\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})-\lambda_{u})^{3}}x^{2}\\
&\hskip 62.59596pt+\min_{|\lambda_{x}|\leq\sqrt{q}K}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\bigg[\frac{\beta^{2}\,}{\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})(\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})-\lambda_{u})}x\lambda_{x}\\
&\hskip 136.5733pt-\frac{2\beta^{2}\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})\tanh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})}{(\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})-\lambda_{u})^{2}}x\lambda_{x}\\
&\hskip 176.407pt-x\lambda_{x}+\frac{1}{4}\frac{\text{M}^{2}}{(\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\text{Z})-\lambda_{u})}\lambda_{x}^{2}\bigg]\bigg\}\\
&=\max_{|x|\leq q^{-1/2}}\Sigma_{\lambda_{u}}(x).\end{split}

(4.10)

Now, evaluating the Hessian of
\Sigma_{\lambda_{u}}
for
\lambda_{u}=0
, one obtains with
q_{4}=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\,\text{M}^{4}
that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^{2}\Sigma_{0}}{dx^{2}}&=2\beta^{2}q-2\beta^{4}q\,(1-4q+3q_{4})-(q-q_{4})^{-1}\big(1-\beta^{2}(1-4q+3q_{4})\big)^{2}\\
&=\frac{(1-\beta^{2}(1-4q+3q_{4}))}{(q-q_{4})}\big(2\beta^{2}q(q-q_{4})-\big(1-\beta^{2}(1-2q+q_{4})\big)-2\beta^{2}(q-q_{4})\big)\\
&=\frac{\big(1-\beta^{2}(1-4q+3q_{4})\big)}{(q-q_{4})}\big(-\big(1-\beta^{2}(1-2q+q_{4})\big)-2\beta^{2}(1-q)(q-q_{4})\big).\end{split}

Finally, by the AT condition (
1.5
) and
q-q_{4}=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\big(\frac{\tanh^{2}}{\cosh^{2}}\big)(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\,\text{Z})\geq 0
, we get

\begin{split}1-\beta^{2}(1-2q+q_{4})&=1-\beta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\text{sech}^{4}(h+\beta{\sqrt{q}}\,\text{Z})>0,\\
1-\beta^{2}(1-4q+3q_{4})&=1-\beta^{2}(1-2q+q_{4})+2\beta^{2}(q-q_{4})>0,\end{split}

and together with
1-q=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\text{sech}^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\,\text{Z})\geq 0
and smoothness in
\lambda_{u}
, this implies

\frac{d^{2}\Sigma_{\lambda_{u}}}{dx^{2}}<0

for
\lambda_{u}\in(0,1)
sufficiently small. By concavity, we obtain for
\lambda_{u}>0
small enough that

\begin{split}&\max_{|x|\leq 1}\min_{|\lambda_{x}|\leq K}\bigg[2\beta^{2}x^{2}-\beta^{2}(1-q)-\lambda_{u}+\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\frac{\beta^{2}(1-q^{-1}x^{2})}{(\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\,\text{Z})-\lambda_{u})}\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt-\lambda_{x}x+\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\frac{\beta^{2}q^{-1}x^{2}\text{Z}^{2}+\beta q^{-1/2}x\lambda_{x}\text{M}\text{Z}+\lambda_{x}^{2}\text{M}^{2}/4}{(\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\,\text{Z})-\lambda_{u})}\bigg]\\
&=-\beta^{2}(1-q)-\lambda_{u}+\beta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\,\text{Z})-\lambda_{u})}\\
&=-\int_{0}^{\lambda_{u}}dt\,\bigg[1-\beta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\frac{1}{(\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\,\text{Z})-t)^{2}}\bigg]\leq-c<0\end{split}

for some positive constant
c=c_{\beta,h}>0
. Here, the last inequality follows from the assumption that
\lambda_{u}>0
is sufficiently small and from the AT condition (
1.5
), noting that

\begin{split}&-\bigg[1-\beta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\frac{1}{(\cosh^{2}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\,\text{Z})-t)^{2}}\bigg]_{|t=0}=-\big(1-\beta^{2}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\text{sech}^{4}(h+\beta\sqrt{q}\,\text{Z})\big)<0.\end{split}

Combining this with (
4.6
), (
4.7
), (
4.8
) and (
4.10
), this proves Prop.
4.5
.
∎

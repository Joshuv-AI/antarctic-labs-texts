Orbital magnetization of Floquet topological systems
Gabriel E. Topp 1, Päivi Törmä 1, Dante M. Kennes 2,3, Aditi Mitra 4

Abstract
A general expression for the orbital magnetization of a Floquet system is derived. The expression holds for a clean system, and is valid for any driving protocol, and arbitrary occupation of the bands. The orbital magnetization is shown to be large not only for Chern insulators, but also for anomalous phases where the Chern number does not fully account for the topology. In addition, the orbital magnetization is shown to take significant values both for a thermal equilibrium occupation of the Floquet bands, and for occupations determined by a quantum quench from an initial state with zero orbital magnetization. For the latter case, the orbital magnetization is shown to be highly sensitive to van Hove singularities of the Floquet bands.

II
System and derivation

Let us consider a general spatially periodic and time periodic Hamiltonian in 2D. If
{\bf k}
is
the quasi-momentum, and
T
is the period of the drive, the Hamiltonian may be written as
H({\bf k},t)
, where
H({\bf k},t+T)=H({\bf k},t)
.
According to Floquet theory
Shirley 1965
;
Sambe 1973
, the Floquet eigenstate
|\psi_{n,\bf{k}}(t)\rangle
can be decomposed
as
|\psi_{n,\bf{k}}(t)\rangle=e^{-i\epsilon_{n,\bf{k}}t}|\phi_{n,\bf{k}}(t)\rangle
where

|\phi_{n,\bf{k}}(t)\rangle
is the time-periodic Floquet quasimode, and
\epsilon_{n,\bf{k}}
is the quasi-energy, with

n
labeling the bands. The first Floquet Brillouin zone corresponds to
\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}\in\left[-\pi/T,\pi/T\right]
.
It is convenient to define the Floquet Hamiltonian

H_{F}=H({\bf k},t)-i\partial_{t}
, which obeys
H_{F}|\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)\rangle=\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}|\phi_{n,\bf{k}}(t)\rangle
.

Let us assume that the system has reached a steady-state characterized by
an occupation
f_{n,{\bf k}}
of the Floquet bands
Dehghani et al. 2014
;
Dehghani et al. 2015
;
Dehghani and Mitra 2016a
;
Esin et al. 2018
. Thus the system is described by a density matrix

\rho(t)=\sum_{n,{\bf k}}f_{n,{\bf k}}|\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)\rangle\langle\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)|
. Note that due to the time-periodicity of the Floquet quasimodes, the steady-state still involves oscillations that are periodic in time. Depending on the value of
f_{n,{\bf k}}
, we could be studying pure states or mixed states. The density matrix can also have off-diagonal components. However, when evaluating the average of typical observables, the off-diagonal terms lead to rapid oscillations at incommensurate frequencies. A summation over all the rapidly oscillating terms leads to a decay in time. Since we are interested in the steady-state behavior of the expectation value of observables, we will neglect the off-diagonal components of the density matrix.

We are interested in studying the linear response of the above system to a weak external magnetic field. It was shown that the orbital magnetization in a Floquet eigenstate time-averaged over one drive cycle equals the rate at which the quasi-energy of that eigenstate changes due to the applied magnetic field (see for example
Nathan et al. 2017
). With this as the starting point, we will compute the change in the average quasi-energy of each Floquet eigenstate due to the applied magnetic field, weighting the result by the occupation probability of each Floquet eigenstate.

The average quasi-energy for a system described by the density matrix
\rho(t)=\sum_{n,{\bf k}}f_{n,{\bf k}}|\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)\rangle\langle\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)|
is

\displaystyle E=\sum_{n,{\bf k}}f_{n,{\bf k}}\langle\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)|H_{F}|\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)\rangle=\sum_{n,{\bf k}}f_{n,{\bf k}}\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}.

(1)

Our goal is to study the linear response of the above system to a small magnetic field
{\bf B}
applied in the perpendicular
\hat{z}
direction
and varying slowly with an in-plane wave-vector
{\bf q}=q\hat{y}
,

{\bf B}=\hat{z}B\cos(qy)

Shi et al. 2007
. Using a gauge where the vector potential is
{\bf A}=-\hat{x}B\sin(qy)/q
,
the perturbing term corresponding to this magnetic field is (we set
e=1,\hbar=1
) (see Appendix
A
)

\displaystyle V(t)=\sum_{{\bf p}}{\bf A}_{{\bf p}}\cdot{\bf J}_{-{\bf p}}(t)={\bf A}_{q}\cdot{\bf J}_{-q}(t)+c.c.,

(2)

where the current operator is
{\bf J}_{\bf q}(t)
=
\sum_{\bf k}c^{\dagger}_{\bf{k+q/2}}{\bf v}({\bf k},t)c_{{\bf k-q/2}}
,
{\bf v}({\bf k},t)
=
\partial_{\bf k}H({\bf k},t)
,
with
{\bf A}_{q}=-\hat{x}B/(2iq)
.
Denoting
\delta|\psi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)\rangle
as the change in the eigenstate to
O(V)
, and assuming that the perturbation is switched on at time
t=0
, we have (see Appendix
A
)

\displaystyle\delta|\psi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)\rangle=-i\sum_{n^{\prime},{\bf k^{\prime}}}e^{-i\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}t}|\phi_{n^{\prime},{\bf k^{\prime}}}(t)\rangle\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}

\displaystyle\times\langle\phi_{n^{\prime},{\bf k^{\prime}}}(t^{\prime})|V(t^{\prime})|\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t^{\prime})\rangle e^{-i\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}(t^{\prime}-t)+i\epsilon_{n^{\prime},{\bf k^{\prime}}}(t^{\prime}-t)}.

(3)

Since the quasi-energy does not change to
O(V)
(or equivalently to
O(B)
, c.f. Appendix
A
), the leading change to the quasimode is

\delta|\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)\rangle=e^{i\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}t}\delta|\psi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)\rangle
. We also assume that
the perturbation is switched on slowly so that it does not change the occupation of the Floquet states. This is also justified from the fact that changing the occupation requires real inelastic processes that move particles from one eigenstate to another. At
O(B)
, only virtual processes are allowed, and therefore to this order, there is no change in the occupation.

Thus the change in the average quasi-energy due to the external magnetic field is

\displaystyle\!\!\delta E(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,{\bf k}}f_{n,{\bf k}}\biggl[\langle\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)|H_{F}|\delta\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)\rangle+c.c.\biggr]\!\!=\!M(t)B,

(4)

where
M(t)
is the induced magnetization.
We are interested in the limit of long wavelengths,
q\rightarrow 0
, and long times
t\rightarrow\infty
. Even in the long time limit,
the time-periodicity of the Floquet states gives rise to a residual time-dependence to the magnetization. We will explore the magnetization
averaged over one drive cycle i.e,
\overline{M}={\lim}_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{t}^{t+T}dt^{\prime}M(t^{\prime})/T
, where

\overline{O}
indicates average of a quantity over one drive cycle.
We introduce the following notation for the
m
-th Fourier component of various matrix elements

\displaystyle\!\!\left[\hat{O}\right]^{m}_{n{\bf k},n^{\prime}{\bf k+q}}\!\!=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}e^{-im\Omega t^{\prime}}\!\!\langle\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t^{\prime})|\hat{O}(t^{\prime})|\phi_{n^{\prime},{\bf k+q}}(t^{\prime})\rangle.

(5)

We find (see Appendix
A
for intermediate steps)

\displaystyle\overline{M}=\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2q}\sum_{n,n^{\prime},{\bf k},m\in{\rm int}}\left(f_{n,{\bf k}}-f_{n^{\prime},{\bf k+q}}\right)\biggl[\left[H_{F}\right]^{m}_{n{\bf k},n^{\prime}{\bf k+q}}

\displaystyle\times\left[v^{x}({\bf k})\right]^{-m}_{n^{\prime}{\bf k+q},n{\bf k}}\frac{i}{\epsilon_{n,\bf{k}}+m\Omega-\epsilon_{n^{\prime},\bf{k+q}}}+c.c.\biggr].

(6)

Above,
v^{x}

is the velocity operator along the
\hat{x}
direction.

Taking the long wavelength limit is subtle and the final expression depends on the
distribution function
f_{n,{\bf k}}
. In particular, we find (restoring
e,\hbar
) (see Appendix
A
),

\displaystyle\overline{M}=-\frac{e}{2\hbar}{\rm Im}\sum_{n,{\bf k}}

\displaystyle\times\biggl[f_{n,{\bf k}}\overline{\langle\partial_{{\bf k}}\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)|\left(\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}+H_{F}\right)\times|\partial_{\bf k}\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)\rangle}

\displaystyle-\biggl\{\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow 0}\frac{\left(f_{n,{\bf k}}-f_{n,{\bf k+q}}\right)}{\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}-\epsilon_{n,{\bf k+q}}}\biggr\}\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}

\displaystyle\times\overline{\langle\partial_{\bf k}\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)|\left(\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}-H_{F}\right)\times|\partial_{\bf k}\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)\rangle}\biggr].

(7)

The cross product indicates that the orbital magnetization depends on the Berry curvature of the bands.
Since the orbital magnetization is related to the operator
{\bf r}\times{\bf v}
, with
{\bf v}=\partial_{t}{\bf r}=-i\left[{\bf r},H(t)\right]
, and since
{\bf r}
in the momentum basis corresponds to
\partial_{{\bf k}}
, that explains why we have
two partial derivatives in momentum weighted by

H_{F},\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}

Ceresoli et al. 2006
.

In order to explore the physics, we need to make some assumptions about the occupations
f_{n,{\bf k}}
. This requires thinking carefully about the initial state before the periodic driving was switched on, and to also account for relevant dissipative processes. When the system is coupled to an ideal reservoir described by a Fermi-Dirac distribution function with a chemical potential
\mu
and temperature
\beta^{-1}
, and when the Floquet system is driven at frequencies that are large as compared to the bandwidth, it has been shown that the Floquet system acquires the ideal distribution function of the reservoir
Dehghani et al. 2014
;
Dehghani et al. 2015
. However, for driving frequencies that are resonant, i.e, comparable to the bandwidth, the occupation of the Floquet states can be complicated, and highly dependent on the details of the system-reservoir coupling
Dehghani et al. 2014
;
Dehghani et al. 2015
. Nevertheless one can imagine performing careful reservoir engineering to obtain desired results for the Floquet occupation.
For this reason, we first consider the case where all states are occupied by a Fermi-Dirac distribution
function at a temperature
\beta^{-1}
, and a chemical potential
\mu
. Moreover, we study corrections to the combination

E-\mu\langle N\rangle
, where

N
is the number operator. This involves shifting
\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}},H_{F}
by
\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}-\mu,H_{F}-\mu
thus
arriving at the following expression for the orbital magnetization
(see Appendix
A
)

\displaystyle\overline{M}=\overline{M}_{1}+\overline{M}_{2},

(8a)

\displaystyle\overline{M}_{1}=-\frac{e}{2\hbar}{\rm Im}\biggl[\sum_{n,{\bf k}}f_{n,{\bf k}}

\displaystyle\times\overline{\langle\partial_{{\bf k}}\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)|\left(\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}+H_{F}-2\mu\right)\times|\partial_{\bf k}\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)\rangle}\biggr],

(8b)

\displaystyle\overline{M}_{2}=\frac{e}{2\hbar}{\rm Im}\biggl[\sum_{n,{\bf k}}f^{\prime}_{n,{\bf k}}\left(\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}-\mu\right)

\displaystyle\times\overline{\langle\partial_{\bf k}\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)|\left(\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}-H_{F}\right)\times|\partial_{\bf k}\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)\rangle}\biggr].

(8c)

Above
f^{\prime}_{n,{\bf k}}=\partial_{\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}}f
.
Since
\overline{M}_{1}
is proportional to the occupation, it survives at zero temperature, while

\overline{M}_{2}
being proportional to the derivative of the occupation times the energy, contributes only at non-zero temperature.
Eq. (
8
) reduces to that for a static system
Shi et al. 2007
with
H_{F}
being replaced by the static Hamiltonian
H
.
However for static systems in thermal equilibrium,
the orbital magnetization is determined as a linear response
correction to the free energy
F=E-\mu\langle N\rangle-\beta^{-1}S
,
S
being the entropy and
\beta^{-1}
being the temperature.
In contrast, here we are considering the linear response of a closed quantum system described by a density matrix
\rho
. For the orbital magnetization, this corresponds to determining corrections to the quasi-energy of each Floquet eigenstate weighted by the occupation of that state
Nathan et al. 2017
. Thus the natural object that appears here is corrections to
E
or
E-\mu\langle N\rangle
, where
\mu\langle N\rangle
is a convenient shift.

For a two-band Floquet system with particle-hole symmetry, the orbital magnetization per unit area
A
when one of the bands is fully occupied and the other is empty, reduces to
\overline{M}/A=-(e/\hbar)C\mu/2\pi
(see Appendix
A
). Thus the magnetization vanishes for this ideal filling for anomalous phases with
C=0
.
Therefore, in order to probe the
C=0
phase, we need to either break particle-hole symmetry, or raise the temperature in order to occupy the other band, or simply have a nonequilibrium occupation of the Floquet bands.

As discussed above, the occupation of Floquet states is not guaranteed to be in thermal equilibrium even when they are coupled to an ideal reservoir

Dehghani et al. 2014
;
Dehghani et al. 2015
;
Dehghani and Mitra 2016a
except for some limiting cases of high frequency driving or for very careful reservoir engineering. The most natural distribution is one arising from
a quantum quench from an initial state where a drive was absent, and then the drive was switched on following a certain protocol.
For a sudden switch-on of the drive at a time
t=0
, the quench distribution function is

\displaystyle f_{n,{\bf k}}=\sum_{\alpha}|\langle\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(0)|\psi^{\rm in}_{\alpha,{\bf k}}\rangle|^{2}f^{\rm in}_{\alpha,{\bf k}},

(9)

where
|\psi^{\rm in}_{\alpha,{\bf k}}\rangle
are the eigenstates of the Hamiltonian in the absence of drive, and
f^{\rm in}_{\alpha,{\bf k}}

is the occupation of these states, which we take to be
a Fermi-Dirac distribution at a temperature
\beta^{-1}
and a chemical potential
\mu
.

The orbital magnetization following a quantum quench, and obtained from corrections to the average quasi-energy
E
is (see Appendix
A
)

\displaystyle\overline{M}_{Q}=-\frac{e}{2\hbar}{\rm Im}\sum_{n,{\bf k}}\biggl[f_{n,{\bf k}}\overline{\langle\partial_{{\bf k}}\phi_{nk}(t)|\left(\epsilon_{nk}+H_{F}\right)\times|\partial_{{\bf k}}\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle}

\displaystyle-\biggl\{\sum_{\alpha}|\langle\phi_{n,{\bf k}}|\psi^{\rm in}_{\alpha,{\bf k}}\rangle|^{2}\partial_{\epsilon}f^{\rm in}_{\alpha}\frac{v_{y}^{\rm in}(\alpha,{\bf k})}{v_{y}(n,{\bf k})}

\displaystyle+f^{\rm in}_{\alpha,{\bf k}}\frac{\partial_{k_{y}}\left(|\langle\phi_{n,{\bf k}}|\psi^{\rm in}_{\alpha,{\bf k}}\rangle|^{2}\right)}{v_{y}(n,{\bf k})}\biggr\}

\displaystyle\times\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}\overline{\langle\partial_{\bf k}\phi_{nk}(t)|\left(\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}-H_{F}\right)\times|\partial_{\bf k}\phi_{n,{\bf k}}(t)\rangle}\biggr].

(10)

There are two main differences between Eq. (

) and Eq. (

). One is that the occupation probabilities entering
in Eq. (
10
) are given by Eq. (
9
) rather than a Fermi-Dirac distribution function. Second, the term coming from

\lim_{q\rightarrow 0}\left(f_{n,{\bf k}}-f_{n,{\bf k+q}}\right)/(\epsilon_{n,{\bf k}}-\epsilon_{n,{\bf k+q}})
(c.f. Eq. (
7
)) is quite subtle for the quench
as the energies entering in
f_{n,{\bf k}}
are those of the pre-quench Hamiltonian before driving was switched on.
Thus a ratio of velocities of the pre and post
quench systems

, appear in the formula for the quench,
with the orbital magnetization becoming sensitive to

) of the Floquet bands. We emphasize that vHs are always important whenever a sum on all momenta is involved. Thus vHs play a role even for a thermal occupation of the bands. However, as Eq. (

III
Application to driven graphene

We now apply the above formulas for the orbital magnetization to periodically driven (solid-state or artificial) graphene. We
choose a driving protocol where
{\bf k}a_{0}\rightarrow{\bf k}a_{0}+A_{0}a_{0}\left(\cos(\Omega)\hat{x}-\sin(\Omega t)\hat{y}\right)
(corresponding to a circularly polarized laser), where

a_{0}
is the spacing between nearest-neighbor sites,
A_{0}a_{0}
is the dimensionless drive amplitude, and
\Omega
is the drive frequency.
The chosen driving protocol effectively breaks time-reversal symmetry, with the high frequency limit

Oka and Aoki 2009
;
Inoue and Tanaka 2010
;
Kitagawa et al. 2011
;
Jotzu et al. 2014
corresponding to the Haldane model
Haldane 1988
.
Denoting the hopping amplitude of graphene by
t_{0}
, and the velocity at the Dirac points by
v_{F}
, the gap at the Dirac points
in the high frequency limit is
\approx 2(A_{0}v_{F})^{2}/\Omega

Oka and Kitamura 2019
, where
v_{F}=(3/2)t_{0}a_{0}

Castro Neto et al. 2009
.

On varying the drive frequency and amplitude, a rich phase diagram is obtained which includes
conventional Chern insulators with edge modes completely characterized by the Chern number, as well as anomalous phases where
the Chern number is insufficient to characterize the topology
Kundu et al. 2014
;
Dehghani et al. 2015
;
Dehghani and Mitra 2015
;
Dehghani and Mitra 2016b
;
Yates et al. 2016
.
The bandwidth of graphene is
D\approx 6t_{0}
. In order to highlight the main physics, we will study three qualitatively different cases.
One is that of high frequency driving
\Omega\gg D
,
the second is that of a weak
A_{0}a_{0}\ll 1
but resonant drive
\Omega\lesssim D
, while the third is that of a strong
A_{0}a_{0}\gg 1
and resonant drive
\Omega<D
where the Floquet bands bear little resemblance to that of graphene.
The high frequency case
shows physics identical to the Haldane model.
The other two cases, for our parameters, correspond to anomalous phases.

In particular, the case of weak amplitude but resonant drive
(Fig.
1
, panel (b)
\Omega=5t_{0},A_{0}a_{0}=0.5
), has Chern number
C=3
. On a cylinder,
the system hosts 3 chiral edge modes, with two of them of the same chirality and traversing the Floquet zone boundary (red), while the third of the opposite chirality and traversing the zone center (yellow). The Chern number of
3
measures the change in the chirality from the zone center to the zone boundary,
but cannot determine the number of edge modes at the two gaps.
The second anomalous phase (Fig.
1
, panel (c),
\Omega=0.5t_{0},A_{0}a_{0}=10
) is an example of a low frequency and high amplitude drive for which

C=0
, but the system still hosts chiral edge modes on the cylinder. In particular, the Floquet zone center (yellow) and boundary (red) each host two chiral edge modes, with all edge modes of the same chirality.

While Fig.
1
is for a system with boundaries, the rest of the paper presents results for a spatially periodic system. The results for the orbital magnetization for the three cases are plotted in Fig.
2
.
The first, second and third columns correspond to
C=1,3,0
respectively, with results presented in units of
et_{0}a_{0}^{2}/\hbar

on the left axes, and in Bohr magnetons
\mu_{B}=e\hbar/(2m_{e})
on the right axes, where
m_{e}
is mass of the electron, and
t_{0},a_{0}
are for solid-state graphene.
We choose a temperature of
\beta^{-1}=0.05t_{0}
, with the
Floquet zone boundaries
\pm\Omega/2
indicated by vertical dashed lines, and the Floquet band edges by vertical solid lines.
The magnetization
\overline{M}
for a thermal equilibrium occupation is plotted in the first row, while the second row shows the quench orbital
magnetization
\overline{M}_{Q}

As expected, the high frequency case of
C=1

\mu
traverses the gap at zero quasi-energy. The steep linear rise in
\overline{M}
across
\mu=0
, is proportional to
C
. The
quench magnetization
\overline{M}_{Q}
(b), in addition to changing rapidly around
\mu=0
, also shows sharp features
due to the dependence of
\overline{M}_{Q}
on vHs (see coincidence with sharp structures in the density of states (DOS), purple dashed lines).

The
C=3
case shows the same behavior as
C=1
close to
\mu=0
(c), however the largest orbital magnetization appear near the Floquet zone boundaries
\mu=\pm\Omega/2
(in contrast, the
C=1
case has zero orbital magnetization near
\mu=\pm\Omega/2
). We associate the large peaks visible in
\overline{M},\overline{M}_{Q}
(see Fig.
4

\mu\approx\pm\Omega/2
for

as an example of bulk-boundary correspondence as
two additional edge modes of the
opposite chirality
appear at
\mu=\pm\Omega/2

relative to
\mu=0

\overline{M}_{Q}
coincide with the vHs (d).

The final case is
C=0
(e,f) where the bands have a non-zero
Berry-curvature, although it integrates to zero. The non-zero orbital magnetization arises from the non-zero Berry curvature.
Since the chirality does not change from the Floquet zone centers to the zone boundaries,
the strength of the orbital magnetization is of the same magnitude at these points. Note that a temperature of

\beta^{-1}=0.05t_{0}
is ”high” for this case, as a result the orbital magnetization is smoother, and gives non-zero contributions even when
\mu
is outside the band edges. Despite the Chern number being zero, the orbital magnetization takes comparable values to the
C=1
case, making it a good probe of anomalous Floquet phases.

The integrand of Eq. (
8
), namely the orbital magnetization density, is shown in Fig.
3

(and for Eq. (
10

6

2

peaks in the Berry curvature. A non-zero Berry curvature is a prerequisite for a non-zero orbital magnetization density. However, the distribution functions, defined by the chemical potential

\Gamma

3

\mu=0.1t_{0}
.

IV
Conclusions

We derived a general expression for the orbital magnetization of bulk Floquet systems and applied it to periodically driven graphene. The orbital magnetization has a lot more information than the Chern number, showing significant response even for anomalous phases with
C=0
. Thus, orbital magnetization is a more sensitive probe of Floquet induced topology than traditional metrics like the Chern number. Ref.
Nathan et al. 2017
showed that when the bulk states are localized, the orbital magnetization is quantized and related to a 3D winding number
Rudner et al. 2013
. However, in our setting where the bulk states are delocalized, the response is non-universal. In addition, while a
C=0
phase shows a non-zero orbital magnetization, its time-averaged value does not determine in any obvious way how many edge modes the
C=0
phase has. In contrast when
C\neq 0
, so that edge modes at the center and boundaries of the Floquet Brillouin zone have opposite chirality, this manifests very clearly in the orbital magnetization (c.f.
C=3
and
C=1
in Fig.
2
).
Despite this lack of complete information, our results will be useful for experiments in Floquet systems where bulk states are likely to be delocalized.

In this paper we studied the orbital magnetization averaged over one drive cycle. It will also be useful to study the time-dependent orbital magnetization, and extract its Fourier components. For this case we expect to see large response at values of
\mu
that coincide with
the vHs and also its Floquet sidebands i.e., for
\mu=\mu_{0},\mu=\mu_{0}\pm\Omega,\mu=\mu_{0}\pm 2\Omega\ldots
.

Appendix A
Intermediate steps in the derivation of Eq. (
7
)

The Floquet quasimodes
|\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle
are time-periodic
|\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle=|\phi_{nk}(t+T)\rangle
, and therefore can be expanded in
Fourier components as follows

\displaystyle|\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle=\sum_{m\in{\rm int}}e^{im\Omega t}|\phi_{nk}^{m}\rangle.

(11)

The Floquet quasimodes obey

\displaystyle H_{F}|\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle=\epsilon_{nk}|\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle;\,H_{F}=H(t)-i\partial_{t},

(12)

and the Floquet eigenstates are

\displaystyle|\psi_{nk}(t)\rangle=e^{-i\epsilon_{nk}t}|\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle.

(13)

Let us define the time-evolution operator for the Floquet system as

\displaystyle U(t,0)

\displaystyle={\cal T}e^{-i\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\left[H(t^{\prime})\right]}.

(14)

Thus the Floquet unitary translates the Floquet eigenstate forward in time as follows

\displaystyle U(t,0)|\psi_{nk}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=|\psi_{nk}(t)\rangle=e^{-i\epsilon_{nk}t}|\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle.

(15)

The perturbing term
V(t)
is (setting
e=\hbar=1
)

\displaystyle V(t)=\sum_{{\bf p}}{\bf A}_{{\bf p}}\cdot{\bf J}_{-{\bf p}}(t).

(16)

Since we choose a perturbation at a specific wavevector
q
,
{\bf A}({\bf r})=-\hat{x}B\sin(qy)/q
, the sum on
{\bf p}
only involves two terms,
{\bf p}=q\hat{y}
and
{\bf p}=-q\hat{y}
. Thus we arrive at
Eq. (
2
), which we rewrite below for convenience

\displaystyle V(t)={\bf A}_{q}\cdot{\bf J}_{-q}(t)+c.c.,

(17)

with
{\bf A}_{q}=-\hat{x}B/(2iq)
.

In the presence of a perturbation
V(t)
, the Floquet unitary expanded to
O(V)
is

\displaystyle{\cal T}e^{-i\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\left[H(t^{\prime})+V(t^{\prime})\right]}

\displaystyle\approx U(t,0)-i{\cal T}\biggl[\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}U(t,t^{\prime})V(t^{\prime})U(t^{\prime},0)\biggr].

(18)

The change in the wave-function to leading order in
V
is

\displaystyle\delta|\psi_{nk}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=-i\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}U(t,t^{\prime})V(t^{\prime})U(t^{\prime},0)|\psi_{nk}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=-i\sum_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}|\psi_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}(t)\rangle\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\langle\psi_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}(t)|U(t,t^{\prime})V(t^{\prime})U(t^{\prime},0)|\psi_{nk}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=-i\sum_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}|\psi_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}(t)\rangle\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\langle\psi_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}(0)|U(0,t)U(t,t^{\prime})V(t^{\prime})U(t^{\prime},0)|\psi_{nk}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=-i\sum_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}|\psi_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}(t)\rangle\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\langle\psi_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}(0)|U(0,t^{\prime})V(t^{\prime})U(t^{\prime},0)|\psi_{nk}(0)\rangle

\displaystyle=-i\sum_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}e^{-i\epsilon_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}t}|\phi_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}(t)\rangle\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})|V(t^{\prime})|\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})\rangle e^{-i\epsilon_{nk}t^{\prime}+i\epsilon_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=-i\sum_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}e^{-i\epsilon_{nk}t}|\phi_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}(t)\rangle\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}(t^{\prime})|V(t^{\prime})|\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})\rangle e^{-i\epsilon_{nk}(t^{\prime}-t)+i\epsilon_{n^{\prime}k^{\prime}}(t^{\prime}-t)}.

(19)

Above we have used that the Floquet states constitute a complete set of states, and we have employed the identity
U(0,t)U(t,t^{\prime})=U(0,t^{\prime})
.

Note that to
O(B)
the change in the quasi-energy is given by
\delta\epsilon_{n,k}=\langle\phi_{n,k}|V(t)|\phi_{n,k}\rangle
. Since
V(t)
is proportional to the velocity operator, and the average velocity vanishes in an an eigenstate,

\langle\phi_{n,k}|V(t)|\phi_{n,k}\rangle=0
.
Therefore the change in the quasimode comes entirely from the change in the Floquet eigenstate, i.e.,
\delta|\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle=e^{i\epsilon_{n,k}t}\delta|\psi_{nk}(t)\rangle
to
O(B)
.
Substituting for
V(t)
, the change in the quasimode to
O(B)
is

\displaystyle\delta|\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{2q}\sum_{n^{\prime}}|\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t)\rangle\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime})|v^{x}(k+q/2,t^{\prime})|\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})\rangle e^{-i\epsilon_{nk}(t^{\prime}-t)+i\epsilon_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime}-t)}

\displaystyle-\frac{B}{2q}\sum_{n^{\prime}}|\phi_{n^{\prime}k-q}(t)\rangle\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k-q}(t^{\prime})|v^{x}(k-q/2,t^{\prime})|\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})\rangle e^{-i\epsilon_{nk}(t^{\prime}-t)+i\epsilon_{n^{\prime}k-q}(t^{\prime}-t)}.

(20)

Above,

\displaystyle v^{x}_{k+q/2}(t)={v}^{x}(k_{x},k_{y}+q/2,t)=\frac{\partial}{\partial k_{x}}H(k_{x},k_{y}+q/2,t).

(21)

Thus the change in the average quasi-energy to
O(B)
is

\displaystyle\delta E(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,k}f_{nk}\biggl[\langle\phi_{nk}(t)|H_{F}|\delta\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle+c.c.\biggr]

\displaystyle=\frac{B}{2q}\sum_{n,n^{\prime},k}f_{nk}\biggl[\langle\phi_{nk}(t)|H_{F}|\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t)\rangle\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime})|v^{x}_{k+q/2}(t^{\prime})|\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})\rangle e^{-i\epsilon_{nk}(t^{\prime}-t)+i\epsilon_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime}-t)}

\displaystyle-\langle\phi_{nk}(t)|H_{F}|\phi_{n^{\prime}k-q}(t)\rangle\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k-q}(t^{\prime})|v^{x}_{k-q/2}(t^{\prime})|\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})\rangle e^{-i\epsilon_{nk}(t^{\prime}-t)+i\epsilon_{n^{\prime}k-q}(t^{\prime}-t)}

\displaystyle+\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t)|H_{F}|\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\langle\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})|v^{x}_{k+q/2}(t^{\prime})|\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime})\rangle e^{i\epsilon_{nk}(t^{\prime}-t)-i\epsilon_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime}-t)}

\displaystyle-\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k-q}(t)|H_{F}|\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\langle\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})|v^{x}_{k-q/2}(t^{\prime})|\phi_{n^{\prime}k-q}(t^{\prime})\rangle e^{i\epsilon_{nk}(t^{\prime}-t)-i\epsilon_{n^{\prime}k-q}(t^{\prime}-t)}\biggr].

(22)

In second and last terms we find it convenient to shift

k\rightarrow k+q
, and interchange
n,n^{\prime}
. This gives

\displaystyle\delta E(t)=\frac{B}{2q}\sum_{n,n^{\prime},k}\left(f_{nk}-f_{n^{\prime},k+q}\right)

\displaystyle\biggl[\langle\phi_{nk}(t)|H_{F}|\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t)\rangle\int_{0}^{t}dt^{\prime}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime})|v^{x}_{k+q/2}(t^{\prime})|\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})\rangle e^{-i\epsilon_{nk}(t^{\prime}-t)+i\epsilon_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime}-t)}

\displaystyle+c.c\biggr]=BM(t).

(23)

Note that the term multiplying
B/(2q)
is
O(q)
because it is a product of a term which is symmetric under exchange of
n,n^{\prime}
when
q=0
(term in square brackets for
q=0
) and another which is anti-symmetric under exchange of
n,n^{\prime}

when
q=0
(the term
\left(f_{n,k}-f_{n^{\prime},k}\right)
). Thus the prefactor of
B/2q
vanishes when
q=0
. Now the goal is to expand this prefactor to
O(q)
and determine
M(t)
. When
n=n^{\prime}
, the
O(q)
correction comes from Taylor expanding
(f_{nk}-f_{nk+q})
to
O(q)
, while for
the
n\neq n^{\prime}
term, the
O(q)
correction comes from Taylor expanding
\langle\phi_{nk}(t)|H_{F}|\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t)\rangle
. Thus, in what follows we may replace
v^{x}_{k+q/2}
by
v^{x}_{k}
.

We introduce the following notation for the Fourier transform of matrix elements between Floquet quasimodes

\displaystyle\left[\hat{O}\right]^{m}_{nk,n^{\prime}k+q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}e^{-im\Omega t^{\prime}}\langle\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})|\hat{O}|\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime})\rangle.

(24)

In Eq. (
23
) we perform the
t^{\prime}
integral by including
a small imaginary part, and extending the integral to
\infty
. This gives the following expression for the average orbital magnetization

\displaystyle\overline{M}=\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2q}\sum_{n,n^{\prime},k,m}\biggl[\left(f_{nk}-f_{n^{\prime}k+q}\right)\left[H_{F}\right]^{m}_{nk,n^{\prime}k+q}\left[v^{x}(k)\right]^{-m}_{n^{\prime}k+q,nk}\frac{i}{\epsilon_{nk}+m\Omega-\epsilon_{n^{\prime},k+q}}+c.c.\biggr],

(25)

where,

\displaystyle\left[H_{F}\right]^{m}_{nk,n^{\prime}k+q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}e^{-im\Omega t^{\prime}}\langle\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})|H_{F}|\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\epsilon_{n^{\prime},k+q}+\epsilon_{n,k}+m\Omega\right]\sum_{m_{1}}\langle\phi_{nk}^{(m_{1}-m)}|\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}^{(m_{1})}\rangle,

(26)

and,

\displaystyle\left[v^{x}(k)\right]^{-m}_{n^{\prime}k+q,nk}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}e^{im\Omega t^{\prime}}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime})|\partial_{k_{x}}H(t^{\prime})|\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}e^{im\Omega t^{\prime}}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime})|\partial_{k_{x}}\biggl[H(t^{\prime})|\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})\rangle\biggr]-\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}e^{im\Omega t^{\prime}}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime})|H(t^{\prime})|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}e^{im\Omega t^{\prime}}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime})|\partial_{k_{x}}\biggl[\epsilon_{nk}+i\partial_{t^{\prime}}|\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})\rangle\biggr]

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}e^{im\Omega t^{\prime}}\biggl[\epsilon_{n^{\prime}k+q}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime})|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})\rangle-i\langle\partial_{t^{\prime}}\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime})|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})\rangle\biggr]

\displaystyle=\left(\partial_{k_{x}}\epsilon_{nk}\right)\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}e^{im\Omega t^{\prime}}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime})|\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}e^{im\Omega t^{\prime}}\left(\epsilon_{nk}-\epsilon_{n^{\prime}k+q}+i\partial_{t^{\prime}}\right)\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}(t^{\prime})|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\partial_{k_{x}}\epsilon_{nk}\right)\sum_{m_{1}}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}^{(m+m_{1})}|\phi_{nk}^{(m_{1})}\rangle+\biggl(\epsilon_{nk}-\epsilon_{n^{\prime}k+q}+m\Omega\biggr)\sum_{m_{1}}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}^{(m+m_{1})}|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}^{(m_{1})}\rangle.

(27)

Eq. (
25
) corresponds to Eq. (
6
).

In order to Taylor expand in

, it is convenient to separate the terms into
n\neq n^{\prime}
and

as follows

\displaystyle\overline{M}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2q}\sum_{n\neq n^{\prime},k,m}\biggl[\left(f_{nk}-f_{n^{\prime}k+q}\right)\left[H_{F}\right]^{m}_{nk,n^{\prime}k+q}\left[v^{x}(k)\right]^{-m}_{n^{\prime}k+q,nk}\frac{i}{\epsilon_{nk}+m\Omega-\epsilon_{n^{\prime},k+q}}+c.c.\biggr]

(28)

Substituting for matrix elements of

\displaystyle\overline{M}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2q}\sum_{n\neq n^{\prime},k,m}\left(f_{nk}-f_{n^{\prime}k+q}\right)\biggl[\biggl\{\frac{1}{2}\left[\epsilon_{n^{\prime},k+q}+\epsilon_{n,k}+m\Omega\right]\sum_{m_{1}}\langle\phi_{nk}^{(m_{1}-m)}|\phi_{n^{\prime}k+q}^{(m_{1})}\rangle\biggr\}

\displaystyle\times\biggl\{\left(\partial_{k_{x}}\epsilon_{nk}\right)\sum_{m_{2}}\langle\phi_{nk+q}^{(m+m_{2})}|\phi_{nk}^{(m_{2})}\rangle+\biggl(\epsilon_{nk}-\epsilon_{nk+q}+m\Omega\biggr)\sum_{m_{2}}\langle\phi_{nk+q}^{(m+m_{2})}|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}^{(m_{2})}\rangle\biggr\}\frac{i}{\epsilon_{nk}+m\Omega-\epsilon_{n,k+q}}+c.c.\biggr].

In addition, for the

\displaystyle\sum_{m_{1}}\langle\phi_{nk}^{(m_{1}-m)}|\phi_{nk+q}^{(m_{1})}\rangle\times\sum_{m_{2}}\langle\phi_{nk+q}^{(m+m_{2})}|\phi_{nk}^{(m_{2})}\rangle

\displaystyle=\biggl[\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dte^{-im\Omega t}\langle\phi_{nk}(t)|\phi_{nk+q}(t)\rangle\biggr]\biggl[\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dt^{\prime}e^{im\Omega t^{\prime}}\langle\phi_{nk+q}(t^{\prime})|\phi_{nk}(t^{\prime})\rangle\biggr],

\displaystyle+\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2q}\sum_{n,k,m}\left(f_{nk}-f_{nk+q}\right)\biggl[\biggl\{\frac{1}{2}\left[\epsilon_{n,k+q}+\epsilon_{n,k}+m\Omega\right]\sum_{m_{1}}\langle\phi_{nk}^{(m_{1}-m)}|\phi_{nk+q}^{(m_{1})}\rangle\biggr\}\biggl\{i\sum_{m_{2}}\langle\phi_{nk+q}^{(m+m_{2})}|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}^{(m_{2})}\rangle\biggr\}+c.c.\biggr].

As before, since
\langle\phi(t)|\phi(t)\rangle=1
the term

\displaystyle\overline{M}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\neq n^{\prime},k,m}\left(f_{nk}-f_{n^{\prime}k}\right)\biggl[\biggl\{\frac{1}{2}\left[\epsilon_{n^{\prime},k}+\epsilon_{n,k}+m\Omega\right]\sum_{m_{1}}\langle\phi_{nk}^{(m_{1}-m)}|\partial_{k_{y}}\phi_{n^{\prime}k}^{(m_{1})}\rangle\biggr\}\biggl\{\sum_{m_{2}}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k}^{(m+m_{2})}|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}^{(m_{2})}\rangle\biggr\}i+c.c.\biggr]

\displaystyle+\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{2q}\sum_{n,k}\left(f_{nk}-f_{nk+q}\right)\frac{1}{2}\left[\epsilon_{n,k+q}+\epsilon_{n,k}\right]\biggl[i\sum_{m_{1}}\langle\phi_{nk+q}^{(m_{1})}|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}^{(m_{1})}\rangle+c.c.\biggr].

(32)

Now we divide and multiply by
\left(\epsilon_{nk}-\epsilon_{n,k+q}\right)
in the second term and use the identity

\displaystyle(\epsilon_{nk}-\epsilon_{n,k+q})\sum_{m_{1}}\langle\phi_{nk+q}^{(m_{1})}|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}^{(m_{1})}\rangle=\overline{\langle\phi_{nk+q}|\epsilon_{nk}-H_{F}|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}\rangle}\approx q\overline{\langle\partial_{k_{y}}\phi_{nk}|\epsilon_{nk}-H_{F}|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}\rangle},

(33)

to obtain

\displaystyle\overline{M}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\neq n^{\prime},k,m}\left(f_{nk}-f_{n^{\prime}k}\right)\biggl[\biggl\{\frac{1}{2}\left[\epsilon_{n^{\prime},k}+\epsilon_{n,k}+m\Omega\right]\sum_{m_{1}}\langle\phi_{nk}^{(m_{1}-m)}|\partial_{k_{y}}\phi_{n^{\prime}k}^{(m_{1})}\rangle\biggr\}\biggl\{\sum_{m_{2}}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k}^{(m+m_{2})}|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}^{(m_{2})}\rangle\biggr\}i+c.c.\biggr]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n,k}\biggl\{\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow 0}\frac{\left(f_{nk}-f_{nk+q}\right)}{\epsilon_{nk}-\epsilon_{n,k+q}}\biggr\}\epsilon_{n,k}\biggl[i\overline{\langle\partial_{k_{y}}\phi_{nk}|\epsilon_{nk}-H_{F}|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}\rangle}+c.c\biggr].

(34)

Since
\langle\phi_{n^{\prime},k}(t)|\phi_{n,k}(t)\rangle=
constant in time,

\partial_{k_{i}}\left[\langle\phi_{n^{\prime},k}(t)|\phi_{n,k}(t)\rangle\right]=0
.
This must hold for each Fourier component giving
\partial_{k_{i}}\left[\sum_{m_{1}}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime},k}^{(m_{1}-m)}|\phi_{n,k}^{(m_{1})}\rangle\right]=0
for each
m
. Using this, we can move the derivatives between the bras and kets to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\neq n^{\prime},k,m}\left(f_{nk}-f_{n^{\prime}k}\right)m\Omega\biggl[\biggl\{\sum_{m_{1}}\langle\phi_{nk}^{(m_{1}-m)}|\partial_{k_{y}}\phi_{n^{\prime}k}^{(m_{1})}\rangle\biggr\}\biggl\{\sum_{m_{2}}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k}^{(m+m_{2})}|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}^{(m_{2})}\rangle\biggr\}i+c.c.\biggr]=0.

(35)

Similar manipulations lead to

\displaystyle\overline{M}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Im}\biggl[\sum_{n,n^{\prime},k,m}f_{nk}\left(\epsilon_{n^{\prime},k}+\epsilon_{n,k}\right)\biggl\{\sum_{m_{1}}\langle\partial_{k_{y}}\phi_{nk}^{(m_{1}-m)}|\phi_{n^{\prime}k}^{(m_{1})}\rangle\biggr\}\biggl\{\sum_{m_{2}}\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k}^{(m+m_{2})}|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}^{(m_{2})}\rangle\biggr\}\biggr]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}{\rm Im}\biggl[\sum_{n,n^{\prime},k,m}f_{n^{\prime}k}\left(\epsilon_{n^{\prime},k}+\epsilon_{n,k}\right)\biggl\{\sum_{m_{1}}\langle\phi_{nk}^{(m_{1}-m)}|\partial_{k_{y}}\phi_{n^{\prime}k}^{(m_{1})}\rangle\biggr\}\biggl\{\sum_{m_{2}}\langle\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{n^{\prime}k}^{(m+m_{2})}|\phi_{nk}^{(m_{2})}\rangle\biggr\}\biggr]

(36)

\displaystyle+{\rm Im}\biggl[\sum_{n,k}\biggl\{\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow 0}\frac{\left(f_{nk}-f_{nk+q}\right)}{\epsilon_{nk}-\epsilon_{n,k+q}}\biggr\}\epsilon_{n,k}\overline{\langle\partial_{k_{y}}\phi_{nk}(t)|\epsilon_{nk}-H_{F}|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle}\biggr].

(37)

The above can be written as

\displaystyle\overline{M}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}{\rm Im}\biggl[\sum_{n,n^{\prime},k}f_{nk}\overline{\langle\partial_{k_{y}}\phi_{nk}(t)|\epsilon_{nk}+H_{F}|\phi_{n^{\prime}k}(t)\rangle\langle\phi_{n^{\prime}k}(t)|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle}\biggr]

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}{\rm Im}\biggl[\sum_{n,n^{\prime},k}f_{n^{\prime}k}\overline{\langle\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{n^{\prime}k}(t)|\epsilon_{n^{\prime}k}+H_{F}|\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle\langle\phi_{nk}(t)|\partial_{k_{y}}\phi_{n^{\prime}k}(t)\rangle}\biggr]

(38)

\displaystyle+{\rm Im}\biggl[\sum_{n,k}\biggl\{\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow 0}\frac{\left(f_{nk}-f_{nk+q}\right)}{\epsilon_{nk}-\epsilon_{n,k+q}}\biggr\}\epsilon_{n,k}\overline{\langle\partial_{k_{y}}\phi_{nk}(t)|\epsilon_{nk}-H_{F}|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle}\biggr].

(39)

Now we remove the complete set of states,

\sum_{n}|\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle\langle\phi_{nk}(t)|=1
, and by noting that the second line is simply the complex conjugate of the first, we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{M}

\displaystyle=-{\rm Im}\biggl[\sum_{n,k}f_{nk}\overline{\langle\partial_{k_{y}}\phi_{nk}(t)|\epsilon_{nk}+H_{F}|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle}\biggr]

\displaystyle+{\rm Im}\biggl[\sum_{n,k}\biggl\{\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow 0}\frac{\left(f_{nk}-f_{nk+q}\right)}{\epsilon_{nk}-\epsilon_{n,k+q}}\biggr\}\epsilon_{n,k}\overline{\langle\partial_{k_{y}}\phi_{nk}(t)|\epsilon_{nk}-H_{F}|\partial_{k_{x}}\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle}\biggr].

(40)

Multiplying by
-e/\hbar
, and writing
{\rm Im}(a)=(a-a^{*})/2i
, we obtain

\displaystyle\overline{M}

\displaystyle=-\frac{e}{2\hbar}{\rm Im}\sum_{n,k}\biggl[f_{nk}\overline{\langle\partial_{\vec{k}}\phi_{nk}(t)|\left(\epsilon_{nk}+H_{F}\right)\times|\partial_{\vec{k}}\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle}

\displaystyle-\biggl\{\lim\limits_{q\rightarrow 0}\frac{\left(f_{nk}-f_{nk+q}\right)}{\epsilon_{nk}-\epsilon_{n,k+q}}\biggr\}\epsilon_{n,k}\overline{\langle\partial_{\vec{k}}\phi_{nk}(t)|\left(\epsilon_{nk}-H_{F}\right)\times|\partial_{\vec{k}}\phi_{nk}(t)\rangle}\biggr].

(41)

The above is Eq. (
7
).

A.1
Simplified formulas for the two-band model

When there are only two bands
n=u,d
as in the case of periodically driven graphene, the formulas for the orbital magnetization simplify. For
the orbital magnetization under assumptions of thermal equilibrium, and taking into account corrections from
E-\mu\langle N\rangle
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{M}

\displaystyle=-\frac{e}{2\hbar}{\rm Im}\sum_{k}\biggl[\left(f_{dk}-f_{uk}\right)\left(\epsilon_{d,k}+\epsilon_{u,k}-2\mu\right)\overline{F_{xy}(k,t)}

\displaystyle-(\epsilon_{d,k}-\epsilon_{u,k})\overline{F_{xy}(k,t)}\sum_{n=d,u}f^{\prime}_{nk}(\epsilon_{nk}-\mu)\biggr].

(42)

For the orbital magnetization for a quench occupation of the bands, and looking at corrections only to
E
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{M}_{Q}

\displaystyle=-\frac{e}{2\hbar}{\rm Im}\sum_{k}\biggl[\left(f_{dk}-f_{uk}\right)\left(\epsilon_{d,k}+\epsilon_{u,k}\right)\overline{F_{xy}(k,t)}

(43)

\displaystyle-\left(\epsilon_{d,k}-\epsilon_{u,k}\right)\overline{F_{xy}(k,t)}\sum_{\alpha}\biggl\{|\langle\phi_{dk}|\psi^{\rm in}_{\alpha k}\rangle|^{2}\partial_{\epsilon}f^{\rm in}_{\alpha}\frac{v_{y}^{\rm in}(\alpha,k)}{v_{y}(d,k)}\epsilon_{d,k}+\langle\phi_{uk}|\psi^{\rm in}_{\alpha k}\rangle|^{2}\partial_{\epsilon}f^{\rm in}_{\alpha}\frac{v_{y}^{\rm in}(\alpha,k)}{v_{y}(u,k)}\epsilon_{u,k}

\displaystyle+f^{\rm in}_{\alpha,k}\frac{\partial_{k_{y}}\left(|\langle\phi_{dk}|\psi^{\rm in}_{\alpha k}\rangle|^{2}\right)}{v_{y}(d,k)}\epsilon_{d,k}+f^{\rm in}_{\alpha,k}\frac{\partial_{k_{y}}\left(|\langle\phi_{uk}|\psi^{\rm in}_{\alpha k}\rangle|^{2}\right)}{v_{y}(u,k)}\epsilon_{u,k}\biggr\}\biggr].

(44)

Above
\overline{F_{xy}}
is the time-average of the Berry curvature and
\alpha
are the bands of the system before the quench.
Note that when there is particle-hole symmetry
\epsilon_{d,k}=-\epsilon_{u,k}
, then the first term in the above equations proportional to

\epsilon_{d,k}+\epsilon_{u,k}
does not contribute. In addition, when the system is in thermal equilibrium at zero temperature, with one band fully occupied, and the other empty, then Eq. (
A.1
) gives

\overline{M}/A=-(e/\hbar)C\mu/2\pi
.

A free boundary problem for the p p -Laplacian with nonlinear boundary conditions
P. Acampora, E. Cristoforoni

Abstract
We study a nonlinear generalization of a free boundary problem that arises in the context of thermal insulation. We consider two open sets Ω ⊆ A \Omega\subseteq A , and we search for an optimal A A in order to minimize a non-linear energy functional, whose minimizers u u satisfy the following conditions: Δ p ​ u = 0 \Delta_{p}u=0 inside A ∖ Ω A\setminus\Omega , u = 1 u=1 in Ω \Omega , and a nonlinear Robin-like boundary ( p , q ) (p,q) -condition on the free boundary ∂ A \partial A . We study the variational formulation of the problem in SBV \sbv , and we prove that, under suitable conditions on the exponents p p and q q , a minimizer exists and its jump set satisfies uniform density estimates. MSC 2020: 35A01, 35J66, 35J92, 35R35. Keywords: p-Laplacian, free boundary, Robin.

Introduction

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set with smooth boundary, and let
A
be a set containing
\Omega
. Consider the functional

F(A,v)=\int_{A}\lvert\nabla v\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}+\beta\int_{\partial A}v^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+C_{0}\mathcal{L}^{n}(A),

(1.1)

with
v\in H^{1}(A)
,
v=1
in
\Omega
and
\beta,C_{0}>0
fixed positive constants. The problem of minimizing this functional arises in the environment of thermal insulation:
F
represents the energy of a heat configuration
v
when the temperature is maintained constant inside the body
\Omega
and there’s a bulk layer
A\setminus\Omega
of insulating material whose cost is represented by
C_{0}
and the heat transfer with the external environment is conveyed by convection. For simplicity’s sake in the following we will set
C_{0}=1
. The variational formulation in (
1.1
) leads to an Euler-Lagrange equation, which is the weak form of the following problem:

\begin{cases}\Delta u=0&\text{in }A\setminus\Omega,\\[3.0pt]
\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\beta u=0&\text{on }\partial A,\\[4.0pt]
u=1&\text{in }\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.2)

The problems we are interested in concern the existence of a solution and its regularity. In this sense, one could be interested in studying a more general setting in which it is possible to consider possibly irregular sets
A
. Specifically, we could generalize the problem into the context of
\sbv
functions, aiming to minimize the functional

F(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lvert\nabla v\rvert^{2}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}+\beta\int_{J_{v}}\left(\underline{v}^{2}+\overline{v}^{2}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\mathcal{L}^{n}(\set{v>0}\setminus\Omega)

with
v\in\sbv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
v=1
in
\Omega
. This problem has been studied by L. A. Caffarelli and D. Kriventsov in
[
5
]
, where the authors have proved the existence of a solution
u
for the problem and the regularity of its jump set. Another similar problem, in a non-linear context, has been deepened by D. Bucur and A. Giacomini in
[
4
]
with a boundedness constraint.

In this paper, our main aim is to generalize the problem and techniques employed in
[
5
]
to a nonlinear formulation. In detail, for
p,q>1
fixed, we consider the functional

\mathcal{F}(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\lvert\nabla v\rvert^{p}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}+\beta\int_{J_{v}}\left(\underline{v}^{q}+\overline{v}^{q}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\mathcal{L}^{n}(\set{v>0}\setminus\Omega),

(1.3)

and in the following we are going to study the problem

\inf\Set{\mathcal{F}(v)}{\begin{gathered}v\in\sbv(\mathbb{R}^{n})\\
v(x)=1\text{ in }\Omega\end{gathered}}.

Notice that if
v\in\sbv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
v=1
a.e. in
\Omega
, letting
v_{0}=\max\set{0,\min\set{v,1}}
we have that
v_{0}\in\sbv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
v_{0}=1
a.e. in
\Omega
and
\mathcal{F}(v_{0})\leq\mathcal{F}(v)
so it suffices to consider the problem

\inf\Set{\mathcal{F}(v)}{\begin{gathered}v\in\sbv(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\\
v(x)\in[0,1]\,\mathcal{L}^{n}\text{-a.e.},\\
v(x)=1\text{ in }\Omega\end{gathered}}.

(1.4)

In a more regular setting, problem (
1.4
) can be seen as a PDE. Let us fix
\Omega,A
sufficiently smooth open sets,
u\in W^{1,p}(A)
with
u=1
on
\Omega
, and let us define the functional

F(u,A)=\int_{\Omega}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{p}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}\lvert u\rvert^{q}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\mathcal{L}^{n}({A\setminus\Omega}).

(1.5)

minimizers u to (
1.5
) solve the following boundary value problem

\begin{cases}\vphantom{\Big(}\divv\left(\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=0\qquad\qquad&\text{in $A\setminus\Omega$,}\\[7.0pt]
\vphantom{\bigg(}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{p-2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}+\beta\frac{q}{p}\lvert u\rvert^{q-2}u=0&\text{on $\partial A$,}\\[7.0pt]
u=1&\text{in $\Omega$.}\end{cases}

(1.6)

In
Section 2
we give some preliminary tools and definitions, and then we will prove the existence of a minimizer
u
of (
1.4
), under a prescribed condition on
p
and
q
. Finally, we will prove density estimates for the jump set
J_{u}
.

We resume in the following theorems the main results of this paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set, and let
p,q>1
be exponents satisfying one of the following conditions:

•

1<p<n
, and

1<q<\dfrac{p(n-1)}{n-p}:=p_{*}
;

•

n\leq p<\infty
, and
1<q<\infty
.

Then there exists a solution
u
to problem (
1.4
) and there exists a constant
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\Omega,\beta,p,q)>0
such that

u>\delta_{0}

(1.7)

\mathcal{L}^{n}
-almost everywhere in
\set{u>0}
, and there exists
\rho(\delta_{0})>0
such that

\supp u\subseteq B_{\rho(\delta_{0})}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded open set, and let
p,q>1
be exponents satisfying the assumptions of
Theorem 1.1
. Then there exist positive constants
C(\Omega,\beta,p,q)
,
c(\Omega,\beta,p,q)
,

such that if

is a minimizer to problem (

), then

c\,r^{n-1}\leq\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(J_{u}\cap B_{r}(x))\leq C\,r^{n-1},

for every

with
B_{r}(x)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
.

In particular, this implies the essential closedness of the jump set

, namely

\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\overline{J_{u}}\setminus J_{u})=0.

In
Section 3
we prove that the a priori estimate (

) holds for inward minimizers (see

in

. Finally, in

.

Remark 1.3
.

Notice that the condition on the exponents is undoubtedly verified when

.
Furthermore, if
\Omega
is a set with Lipschitz boundary, the exponent

is the optimal exponent such that

W^{1,p}(\Omega)\subset\subset L^{q}(\partial\Omega)\qquad\forall q\in[1,p_{*}).

Definition 2.1

(
\bv
)
.

Let
u\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We say that
u
is a function of
bounded variation
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and we write
u\in\bv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if its distributional derivative is a Radon measure, namely

\int_{\Omega}u\,\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}=\int_{\Omega}\varphi\,dD_{i}u\qquad\forall\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

with
Du
a
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We denote with
\lvert Du\rvert
the total variation of the measure
Du
. The space
\bv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Banach space equipped with the norm

\lVert u\rVert_{\bv(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\lVert u\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\lvert Du\rvert(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a measurable set. We define the
set of points of density 1 for
E
as

E^{(1)}=\Set{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{r}(x)\cap E)}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{r}(x))}=1},

and the
set of points of density 0 for
E
as

E^{(0)}=\Set{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{r}(x)\cap E)}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{r}(x))}=0}.

Moreover, we define the
essential boundary
of
E
as

\partial^{*}E=\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus(E^{(0)}\cup E^{(1)}).

Definition 2.3

(Approximate upper and lower limits)
.

Let
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a measurable function. We define the
approximate upper and lower limits
of
u
, respectively, as

\overline{u}(x)=\inf\Set{t\in\mathbb{R}}{\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{r}(x)\cap\set{u>t})}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{r}(x))}=0},

and

\underline{u}(x)=\sup\Set{t\in\mathbb{R}}{\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{r}(x)\cap\set{u<t})}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{r}(x))}=0}.

We define the
jump set
of
u
as

J_{u}=\Set{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\underline{u}(x)<\overline{u}(x)}.

We denote by
K_{u}
the closure of
J_{u}
.

If
\overline{u}(x)=\underline{u}(x)=l
, we say that
l
is the approximate limit of
u
as
y
tends to
x
, and we have that, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\limsup_{r\to 0^{+}}\dfrac{\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{r}(x)\cap\set{\lvert u-l\rvert\geq\varepsilon)}}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{r}(x))}=0.

If
u\in\bv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the jump set
J_{u}
is a
(n-1)
-rectifiable set, i.e.
{J_{u}\subseteq\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}M_{i}}
, up to a
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-negligible set, with
M_{i}
a
C^{1}
-hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for every
i
. We can then define
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-almost everywhere on
J_{u}
a normal
\nu_{u}
coinciding with the normal to the hypersurfaces
M_{i}
. Furthermore, the direction of
\nu_{u}(x)
is chosen in such a way that the approximate upper and lower limits of
u
coincide with the approximate limit of
u
on the half-planes

H^{+}_{\nu_{u}}=\set{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\nu_{u}(x)\cdot(y-x)\geq 0}

and

H^{-}_{\nu_{u}}=\set{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\nu_{u}(x)\cdot(y-x)\leq 0}

respectively.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set, and
E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
a measurable set. We define the
relative perimeter
of
E

P(E;\Omega)=\sup\Set{\int_{E}\divv\varphi\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}}{\begin{aligned} \varphi\in&\>C^{1}_{c}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\\
&\lvert\varphi\rvert\leq 1\end{aligned}}.

If

.

Theorem 2.5

(Decomposition of
\bv

.

Let

dDu=\nabla u\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}+\lvert\overline{u}-\underline{u}\rvert\nu_{u}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\lfloor_{{\Huge J_{u}}}+dD^{c}u,

Definition 2.6
.

Let

Lower Bound

In the following, we assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded open set and that
p
and
q
are two positive real numbers such that

\dfrac{q^{\prime}}{p^{\prime}}>1-\dfrac{1}{n}

(3.1)

where
p^{\prime}
and
q^{\prime}
are the Hölder conjugates of
p
and
q
respectively.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
v\in\sbv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a function such that
v=1
a.e. in
\Omega
. We say that
v
is an
inward minimizer
if

\mathcal{F}(v)\leq\mathcal{F}(v\chi_{A}),

for every set of finite perimeter
A
containing
\Omega
, where
\chi_{A}
is the characteristic function of set
A
.

Let
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a set of finite perimeter such that
\Omega\subset A
, and let
v\in\sbv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. We will make use of the following expression

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}(v\chi_{A})=&\int_{A}\lvert\nabla v\rvert^{p}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}+\beta\int_{J_{v}\cap A^{(1)}}\left(\underline{v}^{q}+\overline{v}^{\,q}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\beta\int_{\partial^{*}A\setminus J_{v}}v^{q}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\[5.0pt]
&+\beta\int_{J_{v}\cap\partial^{*}A}\gamma_{\partial A}^{-}(v)^{q}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\mathcal{L}^{n}\left((\set{v>0}\cap A)\setminus\Omega\right),\end{split}

(3.2)

Let
B
be a ball containing
\Omega
, then
\chi_{B}\in\sbv(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\chi_{B}=1
in
\Omega
, we will denote
\mathcal{F}(\chi_{B})
by
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

There exists a positive constant
\delta=\delta(\Omega,\beta,p,q)
such that if
u
is an inward minimizer with
\mathcal{F}(u)\leq 2\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
, then

u>\delta

\mathcal{L}^{n}
-almost everywhere in
\Set{u>0}
.

Proof.

Let
0<t<1
and

f(t)=\int_{\set{u\leq t}\setminus J_{u}}u^{q-1}\lvert\nabla u\rvert\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}=\int_{0}^{t}s^{q-1}P(\Set{u>s};\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus J_{u})\,ds.

For every such
t
, we have

f(t)\leq\left(\int_{\set{u\leq t}}u^{(q-1)p^{\prime}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\set{u\leq t}\setminus J_{u}}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{p}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\mathcal{F}(u)\leq 2\tilde{\mathcal{F}}.

(3.3)

Let
u_{t}=u\chi_{\set{u>t}}
. Using (
3.2
) we have

\begin{split}0\leq&\,\mathcal{F}(u_{t})-\mathcal{F}(u)\\[5.0pt]
=&\beta\int_{\partial^{*}\set{u>t}\setminus J_{u}}\overline{u}^{q}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{\set{u\leq t}\setminus J_{u}}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{p}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}-\beta\int_{J_{u}\cap\partial^{*}\set{u>t}}\underline{u}^{q}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\\[5.0pt]
&-\beta\int_{J_{u}\cap\set{u>t}^{(0)}}\left(\overline{u}^{q}+\underline{u}^{q}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\mathcal{L}^{n}(\set{0<u\leq t}),\end{split}

and rearranging the terms,

\begin{split}\int_{\set{u\leq t}\setminus J_{u}}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{p}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}&+\!\beta\int_{J_{u}\cap\partial^{*}\set{u>t}}\underline{u}^{q}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\!\beta\int_{J_{u}\cap\set{u>t}^{(0)}}\left(\overline{u}^{q}+\underline{u}^{q}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\\[7.0pt]
&+\mathcal{L}^{n}(\set{0<u\leq t})\leq\beta t^{q}P(\set{u>t};\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus J_{u})=\beta tf^{\prime}(t).\end{split}

(3.4)

On the other hand,

\begin{split}f(t)&=\int_{\set{u\leq t}\setminus J_{u}}u^{q-1}\lvert\nabla u\rvert\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\\[5.0pt]
&\leq\left(\int_{\set{u\leq t}}u^{(q-1)p^{\prime}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\!\!\left(\int_{\set{u\leq t}\setminus J_{u}}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{p}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\[7.0pt]
&\leq\!\Biggl(\mathcal{L}^{n}(\set{0<u\leq t})\Biggr)^{\!\!\frac{1}{p^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}}}\!\!\left(\int_{\set{u\leq t}}u^{q1^{*}}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)^{\!\!\frac{1}{q^{\prime}1^{*}}}\!\!\left(\int_{\set{u\leq t}\setminus J_{u}}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{p}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},\end{split}

where we used

1^{*}=\dfrac{n}{n-1},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\gamma=\dfrac{q1^{*}}{(q-1)p^{\prime}},

and
\gamma>1
by (
3.1
). By classical BV embedding in
L^{1^{*}}
applied to the function
(u\chi_{\set{u\leq t}})^{q}
and the estimate (
3.4
), we have

f(t)\leq C(n,\beta)\biggl(tf^{\prime}(t)\biggr)^{1-\frac{n-1}{q^{\prime}n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,d\left\lvert D(u\chi_{\set{u\leq t}})^{q}\right\rvert\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}.

We can compute

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\,d\left\lvert D(u\chi_{\set{u\leq t}})^{q}\right\rvert&\leq q\Biggl(\mathcal{L}^{n}(\set{0<u\leq t})\Biggr)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\set{u\leq t}\setminus J_{u}}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{p}\,d\mathcal{L}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\\[5.0pt]
&+\!\int_{J_{u}\cap\set{u>t}^{(0)}}\left(\overline{u}^{q}+\underline{u}^{q}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\!\int_{J_{u}\cap\partial^{*}\set{u>t}}\underline{u}^{q}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\\[5.0pt]
&+t^{q}P(\set{u>t};\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus J_{u})\leq(2+q\beta)tf^{\prime}(t).\end{split}

We therefore get

f(t)\leq C(n,\beta,q)\left(tf^{\prime}(t)\right)^{1+\frac{1}{nq^{\prime}}}.

Let
0<t_{0}<1
such that
f(t_{0})>0
, then for every
t_{0}<t<1
, we have
f(t)>0
and

\dfrac{f^{\prime}(t)}{f(t)^{\frac{nq}{q(n+1)-1}}}\geq\dfrac{C(n,\beta,q)}{t},

integrating from
t_{0}
to
1
, we have

f(1)^{\frac{q-1}{q(n+1)-1}}-f(t_{0})^{\frac{q-1}{q(n+1)-1}}\geq C(n,\beta,q)\log\dfrac{1}{t_{0}},

so that, using (
3.3
),

f(t_{0})^{\frac{q-1}{q(n+1)-1}}\leq(2\tilde{\mathcal{F}})^{\frac{q-1}{q(n+1)-1}}+C(n,\beta,q)\log t_{0}.

Let

\delta=\exp\left(-\dfrac{(2\tilde{\mathcal{F}})^{\frac{q-1}{q(n+1)-1}}}{C(n,\beta,q)}\right),

for every

\mathcal{L}^{n}
-almost everywhere on

.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

From

, if

is an inward minimizer with

, we have that

Indeed, on

we have that, by definition,

and that, since

\mathcal{L}^{n}
-almost everywhere in

.

Proposition 3.4
.

There exists a positive constant

such that if

is an inward minimizer with
\mathcal{F}(u)\leq 2\tilde{\mathcal{F}}
, then
u
is supported on

, where
\rho(\delta_{0})=\delta_{0}^{1-q}
and
B_{\rho(\delta_{0})}
is the ball centered at the origin with radius
\rho(\delta_{0})
. Moreover there exist positive constants

such that, for any

(3.5)

and if

, then

\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{r}(x)\cap\set{u>0})\geq C_{1}(\Omega,p,q)r^{n}.

(3.6)

Proof.

By
Theorem 3.2
, if
u
is an inward minimizer, we have

\int_{J_{u}\cap B_{r}(x)}\left(\overline{u}^{q}+\underline{u}^{q}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\geq\delta^{q}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(J_{u}\cap B_{r}(x)),

on the other hand, using
u\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x)}
as a competitor for

, we have

\int_{J_{u}\cap B_{r}(x)}\left(\overline{u}^{q}+\underline{u}^{q}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq\int_{\partial B_{r}(x)\cap\set{u>0}^{(1)}}\left(\overline{u}^{q}+\underline{u}^{q}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq C(n)r^{n-1}.

Let now
x\in K_{u}
and consider

. Using the isoperimetric inequality and inequality (

), we have that for almost every

\begin{split}0<\mu(r)&\leq K(n)\,P\!\left(B_{r}(x)\cap\set{u>0}^{(1)}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}\\
&\leq K(\Omega,\beta,p,q)\,P\!\left(B_{r}(x);\set{u>0}^{(1)}\right)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}.\end{split}

Notice that we used

in the last inequality. We have

\mu(r)\leq K\mu^{\prime}(r)^{\frac{n}{n-1}}.

\mathcal{L}^{n}(B_{r}(x)\cap\set{u>0})\geq C_{1}(\Omega,\beta,p,q)r^{n}.

Finally, let

and

such that

which leads to a contradiction if

is too small, hence there exists a positive value
\delta_{0}(\Omega,\beta,p,q)
such that
\set{u>0}\subset B_{\rho(\delta_{0})}

∎

Notice that if

Proposition 4.1
.

Let

. Then

if and only if

\mathcal{F}(u)\leq\mathcal{F}(v)\qquad\forall v\in H_{\delta_{0}}.

Proof.

As we observed before, if

is a minimizer over
H_{0}
then
u
is in

, hence it is a minimizer over
H_{\delta_{0}}
. Conversely, let us take

a minimizer over
H_{\delta_{0}}
, and let us consider in addition

. Without loss of generality assume

. We will prove that there exists a sequence
w_{k}
of inward minimizers such that

\mathcal{F}(w_{k})\leq\mathcal{F}(v)+\frac{C}{k^{q-1}}.

We first construct a family of functions

such that

\mathcal{F}(v_{a})\leq\mathcal{F}(v)+r(a),

with
\lim_{a\to 0}r(a)=0
. Let

, and let
{v_{a}=v\chi_{\set{v\geq a}\cap B_{\rho(a)}}}
, where

, we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}(v_{a})-\mathcal{F}(v)&\leq\beta\int_{\partial^{*}(\set{v\geq a}\cap B_{\rho(a)})\setminus J_{v}}v^{q}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\[5.0pt]
&\leq\beta a^{q}P(\set{v\geq a})+\beta\int_{(\partial B_{\rho(a)}\cap\set{v\geq a})\setminus J_{v}}v^{q}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\[5.0pt]
&\leq\beta a^{q}\left(P(\set{v\geq a})+\frac{1}{a^{q}}\int_{(\partial B_{\rho(a)}\cap\set{v\geq a})\setminus J_{v}}v\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right).\end{split}

(4.1)

In order to estimate the right-hand side, fix

, and observe that by the coarea formula

(4.2)

while, with a change of variables,

By mean value theorem, for every

we can find

such that

We now construct the aforementioned sequence of inward minimizers: let us consider the functional

\mathcal{G}_{k}(A)=\mathcal{F}(v_{a_{k}}\chi_{A}),

with

containing
\Omega
and contained in

a minimizing sequence for

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{k}(A_{j})&\geq\mathcal{L}^{n}(A_{j}\setminus\Omega)+\beta\int_{J_{\chi_{A_{j}}v_{a_{k}}}}\left(\underline{\chi_{A_{j}}v_{a_{k}}}^{q}+\overline{\chi_{A_{j}}v_{a_{k}}}^{\,q}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\[5.0pt]
&\geq\mathcal{L}^{n}(A_{j})+\beta a_{k}^{q}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(J_{\chi_{A_{j}}v_{a_{k}}})-\mathcal{L}^{n}(\Omega).\end{split}

Notice in addition that since

. We now have that

satisfies the conditions of

, and eventually extracting a subsequence we can suppose that

with a suitable

\mathcal{F}(w_{k})\leq\inf_{\Omega\subseteq A\subseteq\set{v_{a_{k}}>0}}\mathcal{G}_{k}(A)\leq\mathcal{F}(v_{a_{k}})\leq\mathcal{F}(v)+q\beta\frac{\lVert v\rVert_{\bv}}{k^{q-1}}.

By construction

Letting

∎

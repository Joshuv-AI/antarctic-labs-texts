JS is partially supported by NSF DMS-2012585 and AFOSR FA9550-20-1-0309. HY is partially supported by NSF CAREER grant DMS-1945029.
Jingwei Hu, Xiaodong Huang, Jie Shen, Haizhao Yang

Abstract
Numerical approximation of the Boltzmann equation presents a challenging problem due to its high-dimensional, nonlinear, and nonlocal collision operator. Among the deterministic methods, the Fourier-Galerkin spectral method stands out for its relative high accuracy and possibility of being accelerated by the fast Fourier transform. However, this method requires a domain truncation which is unphysical since the collision operator is defined in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . In this paper, we introduce a Petrov-Galerkin spectral method for the Boltzmann equation in the unbounded domain. The basis functions (both test and trial functions) are carefully chosen mapped Chebyshev functions to obtain desired convergence and conservation properties. Furthermore, thanks to the close relationship of the Chebyshev functions and the Fourier cosine series, we are able to construct a fast algorithm with the help of the non-uniform fast Fourier transform (NUFFT). We demonstrate the superior accuracy of the proposed method in comparison to the Fourier spectral method through a series of 2D and 3D examples.



Keywords.
Boltzmann equation; Petrov-Galerkin spectral method; mapped Chebyshev function; unbounded domain, NUFFT.

By tracking the probability density function, kinetic equations describe the non-equilibrium dynamics of the complex particle systems and have been widely used in disparate fields such as rarefied gas dynamics

In this paper, we consider the numerical approximation of the Boltzmann equation, one of the fundamental equations in kinetic theory
[
6
,
32
]
. The complete equation include both particle transport and collisions which are often treated separately by operator splitting. Since the collision part is the main difficulty when numerically solving the equation, we focus on the following spatially homogeneous Boltzmann equation:

\partial_{t}f=Q(f,f),\quad t>0,\quad{\bm{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad d=2,3,

(1.1)

where
f=f(t,{\bm{v}})
is the probability density function of time
t
and velocity
{\bm{v}}
, and
Q(f,f)
is the collision operator whose bilinear form is given by

Q(g,f)({\bm{v}})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{S^{d-1}}\mathcal{B}({\bm{v}},{\bm{v}_{*}},{\bm{\sigma}})\left[g({\bm{v}^{\prime}_{*}})f({\bm{v}}^{\prime})-g({\bm{v}_{*}})f({\bm{v}})\right]\,{\rm d}{\bm{\sigma}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}_{*}},

(1.2)

where the post-collisional velocities
({\bm{v}}^{\prime},{\bm{v}^{\prime}_{*}})
are defined in terms of the pre-collisional velocities
({\bm{v}},{\bm{v}_{*}})
as

\begin{cases}{\bm{v}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}({\bm{v}}+{\bm{v}_{*}})+\frac{1}{2}|{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}_{*}}|{\bm{\sigma}},\vskip 3.0pt\\
{\bm{v}^{\prime}_{*}}=\frac{1}{2}({\bm{v}}+{\bm{v}_{*}})-\frac{1}{2}|{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}_{*}}|{\bm{\sigma}},\end{cases}

(1.3)

with
{\bm{\sigma}}
being a vector over the unit sphere
S^{d-1}
. The collision kernel
\mathcal{B}
takes the form

\mathcal{B}({\bm{v}},{\bm{v}}_{*},{\bm{\sigma}})=B(|{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{*}|,\cos\theta),\quad\cos\theta=\left<\frac{{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{*}}{|{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{*}|},{\bm{\sigma}}\right>,

(1.4)

i.e., it is a function depending only on the relative velocity
|{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{*}|
and cosine of the scattering angle. The collision operator
Q(f,f)
satisfies many important physical properties, including conservation of mass, momentum, and energy:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(f,f)\,{\rm d}{{\bm{v}}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(f,f){\bm{v}}\,{\rm d}{{\bm{v}}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(f,f)|{\bm{v}}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{{\bm{v}}}=0,

(1.5)

and the Boltzmann’s H-theorem:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(f,f)\log f\,{\rm d}{{\bm{v}}}\leq 0.

(1.6)

In the physically relevant case (

Multi-dimensional mapped Chebyshev functions

In this section, we introduce the mapped Chebyshev functions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and discuss their approximation properties. These functions are extension of the one-dimensional mapped Chebyshev functions introduced in
[

]
based on tensor product formulation
[

,

]
. Later in Section
3
, they will serve as the trial functions and test functions in the Petrov-Galerkin spectral method for the Boltzmann equation.

2.1
Mapped Chebyshev functions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

To define the mapped Chebyshev functions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we start with the one-dimensional Chebyshev polynomials on the interval
I=(-1,1)
:

T_{0}(\xi)=1,\quad T_{1}(\xi)=\xi,\quad T_{k+1}(\xi)=2\xi T_{k}(\xi)-T_{k-1}(\xi),\quad k\geq 1.

(2.1)

Define the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\omega}
as

(F,G)_{\omega}:=\int_{I}F(\xi)G(\xi)\omega(\xi)\,{\rm d}{\xi},\quad\omega(\xi)=(1-\xi^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}},

(2.2)

then
\{T_{k}(\xi)\}_{k\geq 0}
satisfy the orthogonality condition

(T_{k},T_{l})_{\omega}=c_{k}\delta_{k,l},\quad\forall\ k,l\geq 0,

(2.3)

where
c_{0}=\pi
and
c_{k}=\pi/2
for
k\geq 1
.

We then introduce a one-to-one mapping
\xi\rightarrow v(\xi)
(its inverse is denoted as
v\rightarrow\xi(v)
) from
I
to
\mathbb{R}
such that

\frac{{\rm d}v}{{\rm d}\xi}=\frac{S}{(1-\xi^{2})^{1+\frac{r}{2}}}:=\frac{\omega(\xi)}{[\mu(\xi)]^{2}},\quad v(\pm 1)=\pm\infty,

(2.4)

where
S>0
is a scaling parameter,
r\geq 0
is the tail parameter, and the function
\mu
is given by

\mu(\xi)=\frac{(1-\xi^{2})^{\frac{1+r}{4}}}{\sqrt{S}}.

(2.5)

With this mapping we define two sets of mapped Chebyshev functions in
\mathbb{R}
as

\widetilde{T}_{k}(v):=\frac{[\mu(\xi(v))]^{4}}{\sqrt{c_{k}}}T_{k}(\xi(v)),\quad\widehat{T}_{k}(v):=\frac{[\mu(\xi(v))]^{-2}}{\sqrt{c_{k}}}T_{k}(\xi(v)).

(2.6)

Define the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathbb{R}}
as

(f,g)_{\mathbb{R}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(v)g(v)\,{\rm d}{v},

(2.7)

then it is easy to check that
\{\widetilde{T}_{k}(v)\}_{k\geq 0}
and
\{\widehat{T}_{k}(v)\}_{k\geq 0}
satisfy the orthonormal condition:

(\widetilde{T}_{k},\widehat{T}_{l})_{\mathbb{R}}=\delta_{k,l},\quad\forall\ k,l\geq 0.

(2.8)

Remark 2.1
.

Two one-to-one mappings between
I
and
\mathbb{R}
often used in practice are

•

logarithmic mapping

(r=0)
:

v=\frac{S}{2}\ln\left(\frac{1+\xi}{1-\xi}\right),\quad\xi=\tanh\left(\frac{v}{S}\right),\quad\mu(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{S}}(1-\xi^{2})^{\frac{1}{4}},

(2.9)

•

algebraic mapping

(r=1)
:

v=\frac{S\xi}{\sqrt{1-\xi^{2}}},\quad\xi=\frac{v}{\sqrt{S^{2}+v^{2}}},\quad\mu(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{S}}(1-\xi^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.10)

In the multi-dimensional case, we denote the multi-vector as
{\bm{v}}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{d})
and multi-index as
{\bm{k}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
, where
k_{j}
is a non-negative integer for each
j=1,\dots,d
;
0\leq{\bm{k}}\leq N
means
0\leq k_{j}\leq N
for each
j=1,\dots,d
. We define the mapped Chebyshev functions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
using (
2.6
) via the tensor product as

\widetilde{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({{\bm{v}}}):=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\widetilde{T}_{k_{j}}(v_{j}),\quad\widehat{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{v}}):=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\widehat{T}_{k_{j}}(v_{j}).

(2.11)

The inner products
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\bm{\omega}}
in
I^{d}=(-1,1)^{d}
and
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are defined, respectively, by

(F,G)_{\bm{\omega}}:=\int_{I^{d}}F({\bm{\xi}})G({\bm{\xi}}){\bm{\omega}}({\bm{\xi}})\,{\rm d}{\bm{\xi}},\quad(f,g)_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f({\bm{v}})g({\bm{v}})\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}},

(2.12)

with the weight function
{\bm{\omega}}({\bm{\xi}}):=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\omega(\xi_{j})
.
Then we have

\left(\widetilde{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}},\widehat{\bm{T}}_{\bm{l}}\right)_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(\widetilde{T}_{k_{j}},\widehat{T}_{l_{j}}\right)_{\mathbb{R}}=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\delta_{k_{j},l_{j}}=:{\bm{\delta}}_{{\bm{k}},{\bm{l}}}.

(2.13)

Therefore, any
d
-variate function
f({\bm{v}})
can be expanded using
\{\widetilde{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{v}})\}_{{\bm{k}}\geq 0}
as

f({\bm{v}})=\sum_{{\bm{k}}\geq 0}\widetilde{f}_{\bm{k}}\widetilde{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{v}})=\sum_{{\bm{k}}\geq 0}\widetilde{f}_{\bm{k}}\frac{[{\bm{\mu}}({\bm{\xi}})]^{4}}{\sqrt{\bm{c}_{\bm{k}}}}{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\xi}}),

(2.14)

and the expansion coefficients
\{\widetilde{f}_{\bm{k}}\}_{{\bm{k}}\geq 0}
are determined by

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{\bm{k}}=\left(f,\widehat{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}\right)_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bm{c}_{\bm{k}}}}\left([{\bm{\mu}}({\bm{\xi}})]^{-4}f({\bm{v}}({\bm{\xi}})),{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\xi}})\right)_{\bm{\omega}},

(2.15)

where

{\bm{\mu}}({\bm{\xi}}):=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\mu(\xi_{j}),\quad\bm{c}_{\bm{k}}:=\prod_{j=1}^{d}c_{k_{j}},\quad{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\xi}}):=\prod_{j=1}^{d}T_{k_{j}}(\xi_{j}),

(2.16)

and
{\bm{v}}({\bm{\xi}})
is the mapping from
I^{d}
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that each component
\xi_{j}
is mapped to
v_{j}
via the 1D mapping (
2.4
). The inverse mapping
{\bm{\xi}}({\bm{v}})
is understood similarly.

In Section
3
, we will introduce the Petrov-Galerkin spectral method for the Boltzmann equation in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where the trial function space and test function space are chosen, respectively, as

\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{d}:=\{\widetilde{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{v}})\}_{0\leq{\bm{k}}\leq N},\quad\widehat{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{d}:=\{\widehat{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{v}})\}_{0\leq{\bm{k}}\leq N}.

(2.17)

The choice of these functions is motivated by their decay/growth properties at large
|{\bm{v}}|
. The following
result is a straightforward extension of the 1D result in
[
18
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
\bm{k}\geq 0
and
|{\bm{v}}|\gg 1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\widetilde{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{v}})\right|\sim\begin{cases}e^{-\frac{2}{S}(\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|)},&r=0,\\
\prod_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|^{-4},&r=1;\end{cases}\quad\left|\widehat{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{v}})\right|\sim\begin{cases}e^{\frac{1}{S}(\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|)},&r=0,\\
\prod_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|^{2},&r=1,\end{cases}

(2.18)

where
r=0
corresponds to the logarithmic mapping (
2.9
) and
r=1
to the algebraic mapping (
2.10
).

2.2
Approximation properties

We describe below some approximation properties of the mapped Chebyshev functions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

For a function
f(\bm{v})
defined in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the transform
{\bm{v}}({\bm{\xi}})
maps it to a function in
I^{d}
. Hence, we introduce the linked function pair
(f,F)
such that
f({\bm{v}})=f({\bm{v}}(\bm{\xi}))\equiv F(\bm{\xi})
. In addition, we introduce another function pair
(\widehat{f}^{\alpha},\widehat{F}^{\alpha})
as

\widehat{f}^{\alpha}({{\bm{v}}}):={f}({\bm{v}})[\bm{\mu}({\bm{\xi}}(\bm{v}))]^{-\alpha}={F}({\bm{\xi}})[\bm{\mu}({\bm{\xi}})]^{-\alpha}=:\widehat{F}^{\alpha}({\bm{\xi}}).

(2.19)

We define the approximation space in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with a parameter
\alpha
as

\mathbb{V}_{N}^{\alpha,d}(\mathbb{R}^{d}):=\text{span}\left\{{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}^{\alpha}({\bm{v}}):=[{\bm{\mu}}({\bm{\xi}}({\bm{v}}))]^{\alpha}{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\xi}}({\bm{v}})),\ 0\leq{\bm{k}}\leq N\right\}.

(2.20)

Therefore, the trial function space
\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{d}
and test function space
\widehat{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{d}
introduced in the previous section correspond to
\mathbb{V}_{N}^{4,d}
and
\mathbb{V}_{N}^{-2,d}
, respectively.

In the following, the
L^{2}
space with a given weight
{\bm{w}}
is equipped with norm

\|f\|_{L_{\bm{w}}^{2}(I^{d})}=\left(\int_{I^{d}}|f({\bm{\xi}})|^{2}{\bm{w}}({\bm{\xi}})\,{\rm d}{{\bm{\xi}}}\right)^{1/2}\ \text{ or }\ \|f\|_{L_{\bm{w}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f({\bm{v}})|^{2}{\bm{w}}({\bm{v}})\,{\rm d}{{\bm{v}}}\right)^{1/2},

(2.21)

depending on the domain of interest.

Let
\mathbb{P}_{N}^{d}(I^{d})
denote the set of
d
-variate polynomials in
I^{d}
with degree
\leq N
in each direction, and
\Pi_{N}^{d}:L_{{\bm{\omega}}}^{2}(I^{d})\to\mathbb{P}_{N}^{d}(I^{d})
be the Chebyshev orthogonal projection operator such that

\left(\Pi_{N}^{d}{F}-{F},{\phi}\right)_{{\bm{\omega}}}=0,\quad\forall{\phi}\in{\mathbb{P}}_{N}^{d}(I^{d}).

(2.22)

Then we define another projection operator
\pi_{N}^{\alpha,d}:L_{\bm{\mu}^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\to{\mathbb{V}}_{N}^{\alpha,d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

\pi_{N}^{\alpha,d}f:=\bm{\mu}^{\alpha}\Pi_{N}^{d}(F\bm{\mu}^{-\alpha})=\bm{\mu}^{\alpha}\Pi_{N}^{d}\widehat{F}^{\alpha}.

(2.23)

One can verify using the definition that

\displaystyle\left(\pi_{N}^{\alpha,d}f-f,\bm{\mu}^{2-2\alpha}\bm{T}_{\bm{k}}^{\alpha}\right)_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\pi_{N}^{\alpha,d}f-f)\bm{\mu}^{2-\alpha}\bm{T}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\xi}}(\bm{v}))\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}}

(2.24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{I^{d}}\left[\bm{\mu}^{\alpha}\Pi_{N}^{d}\widehat{F}^{\alpha}-\bm{\mu}^{\alpha}\widehat{F}^{\alpha}\right]{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\xi}})\bm{\mu}^{2-\alpha}\frac{\bm{\omega}({\bm{\xi}})}{\bm{\mu}^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{\xi}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\Pi_{N}^{d}\widehat{F}^{\alpha}-\widehat{F}^{\alpha},{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}\right)_{\bm{\omega}}=0,\quad\forall\ 0\leq{\bm{k}}\leq N.

Next, we introduce the function space
\bm{B}_{\alpha}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
equipped with the norm

\left\|f\right\|_{\bm{B}_{\alpha}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left(\sum_{0\leq\bm{k}\leq m}\left\|{\bm{D}}_{\alpha,\bm{v}}^{\bm{k}}f\right\|_{L_{\bm{\varpi}^{\bm{k}+\frac{1+r}{2}{\bf 1}}}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(2.25)

and semi-norm

\left|f\right|_{\bm{B}_{\alpha}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|D_{\alpha,v_{j}}^{m}f\right\|_{L_{\bm{\varpi}^{m\bm{e}_{j}+\frac{1+r}{2}{\bf 1}}}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

(2.26)

where
\bf 1
is an all-one vector,
\bm{e}_{j}=(0,\dots,1,\dots,0)
with
1
in the
j
-th position and
0
elsewhere, and

\bm{D}_{\alpha,\bm{v}}^{\bm{k}}f:=D_{\alpha,v_{1}}^{k_{1}}\cdots D_{\alpha,v_{d}}^{k_{d}}f,\quad\bm{\varpi}^{{\bm{k}}}:=\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1-\xi(v_{j})^{2})^{k_{j}},

(2.27)

with

D_{\alpha,v_{j}}^{k_{j}}f:=\underbrace{a(v_{j})\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{j}}\left(a(v_{j})\frac{\partial}{\partial v_{j}}\left(\ldots\left(a(v_{j})\frac{\partial\widehat{f}^{\alpha}}{\partial v_{j}}\right)\ldots\right)\right)}_{k_{j}\text{ times derivatives}}=\frac{\partial^{k_{j}}\widehat{F}^{\alpha}}{\partial\xi_{j}},

(2.28)

where
a(v_{j}):=\frac{\mathrm{d}v_{j}}{\mathrm{d}\xi_{j}}
is determined by the mapping.

We have the following approximation result.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
r\geq 0
. If
f\in\bm{B}_{\alpha}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\left\|\pi_{N}^{\alpha,d}f-f\right\|_{L_{\bm{\mu}^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}\leq CN^{-m}\left|f\right|_{\bm{B}_{\alpha}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(2.29)

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\left\|\pi_{N}^{\alpha,d}f-f\right\|_{L_{\bm{\mu}^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\pi_{N}^{\alpha,d}f-f)^{2}\bm{\mu}^{2-2\alpha}\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{I^{d}}\left[\bm{\mu}^{\alpha}\Pi_{N}^{d}\widehat{F}^{\alpha}-\bm{\mu}^{\alpha}\widehat{F}^{\alpha}\right]^{2}\bm{\mu}^{2-2\alpha}\frac{\bm{\omega}({\bm{\xi}})}{\bm{\mu}^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{\xi}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\Pi_{N}^{d}\widehat{F}^{\alpha}-\widehat{F}^{\alpha}\right\|_{L_{\bm{\omega}}^{2}(I^{d})}^{2}.

By the multi-variate (full tensor product) Chebyshev approximation result (Theorem 2.1 in
[
30
]
), we know

\left\|\Pi_{N}^{d}\widehat{F}^{\alpha}-\widehat{F}^{\alpha}\right\|_{L_{{\bm{\omega}}}^{2}(I^{d})}\leq CN^{-m}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{m}\widehat{F}^{\alpha}\right\|_{L_{{\bm{\varpi}}^{m\bm{e}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}{\bf 1}}}^{2}(I^{d})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\left\|\pi_{N}^{\alpha,d}f-f\right\|_{L_{\bm{\mu}^{2-2\alpha}}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}=\left\|\Pi_{N}^{d}\widehat{F}^{\alpha}-\widehat{F}^{\alpha}\right\|_{L_{\bm{\omega}}^{2}(I^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CN^{-m}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|\partial_{\xi_{j}}^{m}\widehat{F}^{\alpha}\right\|_{L_{\bm{\varpi}^{m\bm{e}_{j}-\frac{1}{2}{\bf 1}}}^{2}(I^{d})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CN^{-m}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left\|D_{\alpha,v_{j}}^{m}f\right\|_{L_{\bm{\varpi}^{m\bm{e}_{j}+\frac{1+r}{2}{\bf 1}}}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle CN^{-m}\left|{f}\right|_{\bm{B}_{\alpha}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

∎

3
A Petrov-Galerkin spectral method for the Boltzmann equation

We consider the initial value problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f(t,{\bm{v}})=Q(f,f),&t>0,\quad{\bm{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
f(0,{\bm{v}})=f^{0}({\bm{v}}),\end{cases}

(3.1)

where
Q(f,f)
, in a strong form, is given by (
1.2
). To construct the Petrov-Galerkin spectral method, the following weak form of the collision operator is more convenient:

\left(Q(f,f),\phi\right)_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q(f,f)({\bm{v}})\phi({\bm{v}})\,{\rm d}{{\bm{v}}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{S^{d-1}}\mathcal{B}({\bm{v}},{\bm{v}_{*}},{\bm{\sigma}})f({\bm{v}})f({\bm{v}_{*}})[\phi({\bm{v}}^{\prime})-\phi({\bm{v}})]\,{\rm d}{{\bm{\sigma}}}\,{\rm d}{{\bm{v}}}\,{\rm d}{{\bm{v}_{*}}},

(3.2)

where
\phi({\bm{v}})
is a test function.

We look for an approximation of
f
in the trial function space
\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{d}
as

f(t,{\bm{v}})\approx f_{N}(t,{\bm{v}})=\sum_{0\leq{\bm{k}}\leq N}\widetilde{f}_{\bm{k}}(t)\widetilde{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{v}})\in\widetilde{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{d}.

(3.3)

Substituting
f_{N}
into (
3.1
) and requiring the residue of the equation to be orthogonal to the test function space
\widehat{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{d}
, we obtain

\left(\partial_{t}{f_{N}}-Q(f_{N},f_{N}),\widehat{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}\right)_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}=0\quad\text{for all }\ \widehat{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}\in\widehat{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{d}.

(3.4)

By the orthogonality condition (
2.13
), we find that the coefficients
\{\widetilde{f}_{\bm{k}}(t)\}
satisfy the following ODE system

\begin{cases}\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}\widetilde{f}_{\bm{k}}(t)=\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N},\\
\widetilde{f}_{\bm{k}}(0)=\widetilde{f}_{\bm{k}}^{0},\end{cases}\quad 0\leq{\bm{k}}\leq N,

(3.5)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N}

\displaystyle:=

3.1
Approximation property for the collision operator

In this subsection, we establish a consistency result of the spectral approximation for the collision operator. We will show that if

Lemma 3.4
.

Under the algebraic mapping (

) with

\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\eta}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|f\|_{L_{\bm{\mu}^{-2\eta}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}_{d\eta}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\quad\text{for any }\eta\geq 0.

Proof.

Note that

1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|^{2}\leq\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+|v_{j}|^{2})\leq(1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|^{2})^{d}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{\bm{\mu}^{-2\eta}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f({\bm{v}})|^{2}\bm{\mu}^{-2\eta}\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f({\bm{v}})|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+|v_{j}|^{2})^{\eta}\ {\rm d}{\bm{v}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f({\bm{v}})|^{2}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|^{2}\right)^{\eta}\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}}\right)^{1/2}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\eta}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Also,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{\bm{\mu}^{-2\eta}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f({\bm{v}})|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{d}(1+|v_{j}|^{2})^{\eta}\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f({\bm{v}})|^{2}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{d}|v_{j}|^{2}\right)^{d\eta}\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}}\right)^{1/2}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}_{d\eta}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Lemma 3.5
.

For any
\eta\geq 0
, there exist

and

\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\eta}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{\epsilon}\|f\|_{\mathcal{L}_{\eta+\frac{1+\epsilon}{2}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{\eta}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f({\bm{v}})|(1+|{\bm{v}}|^{2})^{\frac{\eta}{2}}\,{\rm d}{{\bm{v}}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f({\bm{v}})|^{2}(1+|{\bm{v}}|^{2})^{\eta+\frac{1+\epsilon}{2}}\,{\rm d}{{\bm{v}}}\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1+|{\bm{v}}|^{2})^{-\frac{1+\epsilon}{2}}\,{\rm d}{{\bm{v}}}

\displaystyle C_{\epsilon}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f({\bm{v}})|^{2}(1+|{\bm{v}}|^{2})^{\eta+\frac{1+\epsilon}{2}}\,{\rm d}{{\bm{v}}}

\displaystyle C_{\epsilon}\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{L}_{\eta+\frac{1+\epsilon}{2}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

\displaystyle\|Q(f,f)-\pi^{4,d}_{N}Q(\pi_{N}^{4,d}f,\pi_{N}^{4,d}f)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{3}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq

\begin{split}&\|\pi^{4,d}_{N}Q(f,f)-\pi^{4,d}_{N}Q(\pi_{N}^{4,d}f,\pi_{N}^{4,d}f)\|_{\mathcal{L}_{3}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|\pi^{4,d}_{N}Q(f,f)-\pi^{4,d}_{N}Q(\pi_{N}^{4,d}f,\pi_{N}^{4,d}f)\|_{L_{{\bm{\mu}}^{-6}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\

3.2
Approximation property for the moments

For the Boltzmann equation, the moments or macroscopic observables are important physical quantities. Still, under the algebraic mapping (
2.10
), we can show that the spectral method (
3.5
) preserves mass and energy.

Theorem 3.7
.

If using the algebraic mapping (
2.10
) with
N\geq 2
, the spectral method (
3.5
) preserves mass and energy, i.e.,
\rho(t)
and
E(t)
defined by

\displaystyle\rho(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{N}(t,{\bm{v}})\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}},\quad E(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{N}(t,{\bm{v}})|{\bm{v}}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}}

(3.19)

remain constant in time. Furthermore,

\rho(t)\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{0}({\bm{v}})\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}},\quad E(t)\equiv\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{0}({\bm{v}})|{\bm{v}}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{{\bm{v}}}.

(3.20)

Proof.

In 1D, the first few Chebyshev polynomials read

T_{0}(\xi)=1,\quad T_{1}(\xi)=\xi,\quad T_{2}(\xi)=2\xi^{2}-1.

With the algebraic mapping (
2.10
), we have

T_{0}(\xi(v))=1,\quad T_{1}(\xi(v))=\frac{v}{\sqrt{v^{2}+S^{2}}},\quad T_{2}(\xi(v))=\frac{v^{2}-S^{2}}{v^{2}+S^{2}},\quad\mu(\xi(v))=\frac{\sqrt{S}}{\sqrt{v^{2}+S^{2}}}.

Then

\widehat{T}_{k}(v)=\frac{[\mu(\xi(v))]^{-2}}{\sqrt{c_{k}}}T_{k}(\xi(v))=\frac{v^{2}+S^{2}}{\sqrt{c_{k}}S}T_{k}(\xi(v)).

Specifically,

\widehat{T}_{0}(v)=\frac{v^{2}+S^{2}}{\sqrt{c_{0}}S},\quad\widehat{T}_{1}(v)=\frac{v\sqrt{v^{2}+S^{2}}}{\sqrt{c_{1}}S},\quad\widehat{T}_{2}(v)=\frac{v^{2}-S^{2}}{\sqrt{c_{2}}S}.

Therefore,

1=\frac{\sqrt{c_{0}}}{2S}\widehat{T}_{0}(v)-\frac{\sqrt{c_{2}}}{2S}\widehat{T}_{2}(v),\quad v^{2}=\frac{\sqrt{c_{0}}S}{2}\widehat{T}_{0}(v)+\frac{\sqrt{c_{2}}S}{2}\widehat{T}_{2}(v).

Hence we can replace
(\widehat{T}_{0}(v),\widehat{T}_{2}(v))
by
(1,v^{2})
as basis functions, namely,

\widehat{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{1}=\text{span}\{1,\widehat{T}_{1},v^{2},\widehat{T}_{3},\widehat{T}_{4},\cdots,\widehat{T}_{N}\}.

In
d
dimensions, it is easy to see

1,\ v_{1}^{2},\ v_{2}^{2},\ \dots,\ v_{d}^{2}\in\widehat{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{d}\quad\text{for }N\geq 2.

In other words, we have shown that
1,|{\bm{v}}|^{2}\in\widehat{\mathbb{T}}_{N}^{d}
for
N\geq 2
.

On the other hand, by (
3.19
) and (
3.4
), we have

\begin{split}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}{t}}\rho(t)=&(\partial_{t}f_{N}(t,{\bm{v}}),1)_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}=\left(Q(f_{N},f_{N}),1\right)_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}=0;\\
\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}{t}}E(t)=&(\partial_{t}f_{N}(t,{\bm{v}}),|{\bm{v}}|^{2})_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}=\left(Q(f_{N},f_{N}),|{\bm{v}}|^{2}\right)_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}=0,\end{split}

where in the last equality we used the conservation property (
1.5
) of the collision operator.

It remains to show

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{N}(0,{\bm{v}})\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{0}({\bm{v}})\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}},\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{N}(0,{\bm{v}})|{\bm{v}}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f^{0}({\bm{v}})|{\bm{v}}|^{2}\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}}.

Note that
f_{N}(0,{\bm{v}})=\pi^{4,d}f^{0}
, it suffices to show

(\pi^{4,d}f^{0}-f^{0},1)_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}=(\pi^{4,d}f^{0}-f^{0},|{\bm{v}}|^{2})_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}=0,

which is true by (
2.24
) (with
\alpha=4
).
∎

4
Numerical realization

To implement the proposed spectral method, one needs to solve the ODE system (
3.5
). For time discretization, one can just use the explicit Runge-Kutta methods. Hence, the key is the efficient evaluation of
\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N}
as defined in (
3.6
).

In this section, we introduce two algorithms to compute
\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N}
. The first one is a direct algorithm that treats
\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N}
as a matrix/tensor-vector multiplication. Since the weight matrix/tensor does not depend on the numerical solution
f_{N}
, it can be precomputed and stored for repeated use. This approach is simple but will soon meet a bottleneck when
N
increases since the memory requirement as well as the online computational cost can get extremely high. To alleviate this, we propose a fast algorithm, where the key idea is to recognize the gain term of the collision operator as a non-uniform discrete Fourier cosine transform to be accelerated by the non-uniform FFT (NUFFT). Note that this is possible because we are using the mapped Chebyshev functions as a basis, which is related to the Fourier cosine series.

4.1
A direct algorithm

To derive the direct algorithm, we substitute (
3.3
) into (
3.6
) to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq\bm{i,j}\leq N}\widetilde{f}_{\bm{i}}\widetilde{f}_{\bm{j}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{S^{d-1}}\mathcal{B}({\bm{v}},{\bm{v}_{*}},{\bm{\sigma}})\,\widetilde{\bm{T}}_{\bm{i}}({\bm{v}})\widetilde{\bm{T}}_{\bm{j}}({\bm{v}_{*}})[\widehat{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{v}}^{\prime})-\widehat{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{v}})]\,{\rm d}{\bm{\sigma}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}_{*}}

(4.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq\bm{i,j}\leq N}\widetilde{f}_{\bm{i}}\widetilde{f}_{\bm{j}}\left[\widetilde{I}_{1}(\bm{i,j,k})-\widetilde{I}_{2}(\bm{i,j,k})\right],\quad 0\leq{\bm{k}}\leq N,

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{1}(\bm{i,j,k})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{S^{d-1}}\mathcal{B}({\bm{v}},{\bm{v}_{*}},{\bm{\sigma}})\,\widetilde{\bm{T}}_{\bm{i}}({\bm{v}})\widetilde{\bm{T}}_{\bm{j}}({\bm{v}_{*}})\widehat{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{v}}^{\prime})\,{\rm d}{\bm{\sigma}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}_{*}},

(4.2)

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{2}(\bm{i,j,k})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{S^{d-1}}\mathcal{B}({\bm{v}},{\bm{v}_{*}},{\bm{\sigma}})\,\widetilde{\bm{T}}_{\bm{i}}({\bm{v}})\widetilde{\bm{T}}_{\bm{j}}({\bm{v}_{*}})\widehat{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{v}})\,{\rm d}{\bm{\sigma}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}_{*}}.

(4.3)

Since the tensors
\widetilde{I}_{1}(\bm{i,j,k})
and
\widetilde{I}_{2}(\bm{i,j,k})
do not depend on coefficients
\{\widetilde{f}_{\bm{k}}\}_{0\leq{\bm{k}}\leq N}
, a straightforward way to evaluate
\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N}
is to precompute
\widetilde{I}_{1}(\bm{i,j,k})
and
\widetilde{I}_{2}(\bm{i,j,k})
, and then evaluate the sum in (
4.1
) directly in the online computation. This is what we refer to as the direct algorithm. We observe that this algorithm requires
\mathcal{O}(N^{3d})
memory to store the tensors
\widetilde{I}_{1}
and
\widetilde{I}_{2}
; and to evaluate (
4.1
), it requires
\mathcal{O}(N^{3d})
operations. Both the memory requirement and online computational cost can be quite demanding especially for
d=3
and large
N
.

We give some details on how to approximate
\widetilde{I}_{1}(\bm{i,j,k})
and
\widetilde{I}_{2}(\bm{i,j,k})
, though this step can be completed in advance and does not take the actual computational time. We first perform a change of variables
({\bm{v}},{\bm{v}_{*}})\rightarrow({\bm{v}}({\bm{\xi}}),{\bm{v}_{*}}({\bm{\eta}}))
to transform the integrals of
(\bm{v,v_{*}})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
into integrals of
({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}})\in I^{d}\times I^{d}
, using the mapping introduced in Section
2.1
:

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{1}(\bm{i,j,k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{I^{d}}\int_{I^{d}}G_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}})\frac{{\bm{T}}_{\bm{i}}({\bm{\xi}}){\bm{T}}_{\bm{j}}({\bm{\eta}})}{\sqrt{\bm{c_{i}c_{j}}}}{\bm{\omega}}({\bm{\xi}}){\bm{\omega}}({\bm{\eta}})\,{\rm d}{{\bm{\xi}}}\,{\rm d}{{\bm{\eta}}},

(4.4)

\displaystyle\widetilde{I}_{2}(\bm{i,j,k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{I^{d}}\int_{I^{d}}L_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}})\frac{{\bm{T}}_{\bm{i}}({{\bm{\xi}}}){\bm{T}}_{\bm{j}}({\bm{\eta}})}{\sqrt{\bm{c_{i}c_{j}}}}{\bm{\omega}}({{\bm{\xi}}}){\bm{\omega}}({{\bm{\eta}}})\,{\rm d}{{\bm{\xi}}}\,{\rm d}{{\bm{\eta}}},

(4.5)

where

\displaystyle G_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left[{\bm{\mu}}({{\bm{\xi}}}){\bm{\mu}}({{\bm{\eta}}})\right]^{2}\int_{S^{d-1}}\mathcal{B}({\bm{v}}({\bm{\xi}}),{\bm{v}_{*}}({\bm{\eta}}),{\bm{\sigma}})\frac{{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}\left({\bm{\zeta}}_{({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}},{\bm{\sigma}})}\right)}{\sqrt{\bm{c}_{k}}[{\bm{\mu}}\left({\bm{\zeta}}_{({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}},{\bm{\sigma}})}\right)]^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{\sigma}},

(4.6)

\displaystyle L_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({{\bm{\xi}}})\left[{\bm{\mu}}({{\bm{\eta}}})\right]^{2}}{\sqrt{\bm{c_{k}}}}\int_{S^{d-1}}\mathcal{B}({\bm{v}}({\bm{\xi}}),{\bm{v}_{*}}({\bm{\eta}}),{\bm{\sigma}})\,{\rm d}{\bm{\sigma}}.

(4.7)

Notice that in (
4.6
),
{\bm{\zeta}}_{({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}},{\bm{\sigma}})}\in I^{d}
is the value transformed from

{\bm{v}}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}({\bm{v}}({\bm{\xi}})+{\bm{v}_{*}}({\bm{\eta}}))+\frac{1}{2}|{\bm{v}}({\bm{\xi}})-{\bm{v}_{*}}({\bm{\eta}})|{\bm{\sigma}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

under the same mapping. To approximate the above integrals in
{\bm{\xi}}
,
{\bm{\eta}}
, and
{\bm{\sigma}}
, we choose
M_{\bm{v}}
Chebyshev-Gauss-Lobatto quadrature points in each dimension of
I^{d}
for both
{\bm{\xi}}
and
{\bm{\eta}}
; and
M_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
quadrature points on the unit sphere
S^{d-1}
(for
d=2
, this can be the uniform points in polar angle; for
d=3
, this can be the Lebedev quadrature
[
22
]
). Therefore, for each fixed index
{\bm{k}}
, (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) are forward Chebyshev transforms of the functions
G_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}})
and
L_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}})
, respectively. Thus, they can be evaluated efficiently using the fast Chebyshev transform.

4.2
A fast algorithm

To introduce the fast algorithm, we take the original form (
3.6
) and split
\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N}
into a gain term and a loss term as
\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N}=\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N,+}-\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N,-}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N,+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{S^{d-1}}\mathcal{B}({\bm{v}},{\bm{v}_{*}},{\bm{\sigma}})f_{N}({\bm{v}_{*}})\,\widehat{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{v}}^{\prime})\,{\rm d}{\bm{\sigma}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}_{*}}\right)f_{N}({\bm{v}})\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}},

(4.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N,-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{S^{d-1}}\mathcal{B}({\bm{v}},{\bm{v}_{*}},{\bm{\sigma}})f_{N}({\bm{v}_{*}})\,{\rm d}{\bm{\sigma}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}_{*}}\right)f_{N}({\bm{v}})\,\widehat{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{v}})\,{\rm d}{\bm{v}}.

(4.9)

We propose to evaluate
\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N,+}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N,-}
following the above expressions. To this end, given the coefficients
\{\widetilde{f}_{\bm{k}}\}_{0\leq{\bm{k}}\leq N}
at each time step, we first reconstruct
f_{N}
as in (
3.3
) at
M_{\bm{v}}
Chebyshev-Gauss-Lobatto quadrature points in each dimension of
{\bm{v}}
(for an accurate approximation we choose
M_{\bm{v}}=N+2
). This can be achieved by the fast Chebyshev transform in
\mathcal{O}(M_{{\bm{v}}}^{d}\log M_{{\bm{v}}})
operations.

To evaluate the gain term
\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N,+}
, we change the integrals of
(\bm{v,v_{*}})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
into integrals of
({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}})\in I^{d}\times I^{d}
in (
4.8
) (similarly as in the previous subsection for
\widetilde{I}_{1}(\bm{i,j,k})
):

\begin{split}\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N,+}&=\int_{I^{d}}\left(\int_{I^{d}}\int_{S^{d-1}}\mathcal{B}({\bm{v}}({\bm{\xi}}),{\bm{v}_{*}}({\bm{\eta}}),{\bm{\sigma}})f_{N}({\bm{v}_{*}}({\bm{\eta}}))\frac{{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}\left({\bm{\zeta}}_{({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}},{\bm{\sigma}})}\right)}{\sqrt{\bm{c}_{\bm{k}}}\left[{\bm{\mu}}\left({\bm{\zeta}}_{({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}},{\bm{\sigma}})}\right)\right]^{2}}\frac{\bm{\omega(\eta)}}{\left[{\bm{\mu}}(\bm{\eta})\right]^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{\sigma}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{\eta}}\right)f_{N}({\bm{v}}({\bm{\xi}}))\frac{\bm{\omega}({\bm{\xi}})}{[\bm{\mu}({\bm{\xi}})]^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{\xi}}\\
&=\int_{I^{d}}\left(\int_{S^{d-1}}F_{\bm{k}}({{\bm{\sigma}}},{\bm{\xi}})\,{\rm d}{\bm{\sigma}}\right)f_{N}({\bm{v}}({\bm{\xi}}))\frac{\bm{\omega}({\bm{\xi}})}{[\bm{\mu}({\bm{\xi}})]^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{\xi}},\end{split}

(4.10)

where

F_{\bm{k}}({{\bm{\sigma}}},{\bm{\xi}}):=\int_{I^{d}}\mathcal{B}({\bm{v}}({\bm{\xi}}),{\bm{v}_{*}}({\bm{\eta}}),{\bm{\sigma}})f_{N}({\bm{v}_{*}}({\bm{\eta}}))\frac{{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}\left({\bm{\zeta}}_{({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}},{\bm{\sigma}})}\right)}{\sqrt{\bm{c}_{\bm{k}}}\left[{\bm{\mu}}\left({\bm{\zeta}}_{({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}},{\bm{\sigma}})}\right)\right]^{2}}\frac{\bm{\omega(\eta)}}{\left[{\bm{\mu}}(\bm{\eta})\right]^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{\eta}}.

(4.11)

Suppose
M_{\bm{v}}
quadrature points are used in each dimension of
{{\bm{v}}}
and
{{\bm{v}_{*}}}
and
M_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
points are used on the sphere
S^{d-1}
, a direct evaluation of (
4.11
) would require
\mathcal{O}(M_{{\bm{\sigma}}}M_{{\bm{v}}}^{2d}N^{d})
operations. Given
F_{\bm{k}}({{\bm{\sigma}}},{\bm{\xi}})
, a direct evaluation of (
4.10
) would take
\mathcal{O}(M_{{\bm{\sigma}}}M_{{\bm{v}}}^{d}N^{d})
operations. Therefore, the major bottleneck is to compute
F_{\bm{k}}({{\bm{\sigma}}},{\bm{\xi}})
, which is prohibitively expensive without a fast algorithm. Our main idea is to recognize (
4.11
) as a non-uniform discrete Fourier cosine transform so it can be evaluated by the non-uniform fast Fourier transform (NUFFT). We will see that the total complexity to evaluate
F_{\bm{k}}({{\bm{\sigma}}},{\bm{\xi}})
can be brought down to
\mathcal{O}(M_{{\bm{\sigma}}}M_{{\bm{v}}}^{2d}|\log\epsilon|+M_{{\bm{\sigma}}}M_{{\bm{v}}}^{d}N^{d}\log N)
, where
\epsilon
is the requested precision in the NUFFT algorithm.

Applying the Chebyshev-Gauss-Lobatto quadrature
({\bm{\eta}}_{{\bm{j}}},w_{{\bm{j}}})_{1\leq{\bm{j}}\leq M_{{\bm{v}}}}
, (
4.11
) becomes

\displaystyle F_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\sigma}},{\bm{\xi}})

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq{\bm{j}}\leq M_{{\bm{v}}}}w_{\bm{j}}\frac{\mathcal{B}({\bm{v}}({\bm{\xi}}),{\bm{v}_{*}}({\bm{\eta}}_{\bm{j}}),{\bm{\sigma}})f_{N}({\bm{v}_{*}}({\bm{\eta}}_{\bm{j}}))}{\sqrt{c_{\bm{k}}}[{\bm{\mu}}({\bm{\zeta}}_{({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}}_{\bm{j}},{\bm{\sigma}})})]^{2}[{\bm{\mu}}({\bm{\eta}}_{\bm{j}})]^{2}}{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\zeta}}_{({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}}_{\bm{j}},{\bm{\sigma}})})

(4.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq{\bm{j}}\leq M_{{\bm{v}}}}w_{\bm{j}}\frac{\mathcal{B}({\bm{v}}({\bm{\xi}}),{\bm{v}_{*}}({\bm{\eta}}_{\bm{j}}),{\bm{\sigma}})f_{N}({\bm{v}_{*}}({\bm{\eta}}_{\bm{j}}))}{\sqrt{c_{\bm{k}}}[{\bm{\mu}}({\bm{\zeta}}_{({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}}_{\bm{j}},{\bm{\sigma}})})]^{2}[{\bm{\mu}}({\bm{\eta}}_{\bm{j}})]^{2}}\prod_{l=1}^{d}\cos\left(k_{l}\arccos( \bmzeta_{(\bmxi, \bmeta_\bmj, \bmsg),l})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{\bm{k}}}}\sum_{1\leq{\bm{j}}\leq M_{{\bm{v}}}}q_{\bm{j}}\prod_{l=1}^{d}\cos\left(k_{l}{\bm{z}}_{{\bm{j}},l}\right),

where
{\bm{\zeta}}_{({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}}_{\bm{j}},{\bm{\sigma}}),l}
is the
l
-th component of
{\bm{\zeta}}_{({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}}_{\bm{j}},{\bm{\sigma}})}
, and

q_{\bm{j}}:=w_{\bm{j}}\frac{\mathcal{B}({\bm{v}}({\bm{\xi}}),{\bm{v}_{*}}({\bm{\eta}}_{\bm{j}}),{\bm{\sigma}})f_{N}({\bm{v}_{*}}({\bm{\eta}}_{\bm{j}}))}{[{\bm{\mu}}({\bm{\zeta}}_{({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}}_{\bm{j}},{\bm{\sigma}})})]^{2}[{\bm{\mu}}({\bm{\eta}}_{\bm{j}})]^{2}},\quad{\bm{z}}_{{\bm{j}},l}:=\arccos( \bmzeta_{(\bmxi, \bmeta_\bmj, \bmsg),l}).

(4.13)

Note that
q_{\bm{j}}
and
{\bm{z}}_{\bm{j}}
depend on
{\bm{\sigma}}
and
{\bm{\xi}}
. (
4.12
) is almost like a non-uniform discrete Fourier cosine transform. Indeed, we propose to evaluate
F_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\sigma}},{\bm{\xi}})
as follows.

For each fixed
{\bm{\sigma}}
and
{\bm{\xi}}
, we compute

\widetilde{F}_{\bm{K}}:=\sum_{1\leq{\bm{j}}\leq M_{{\bm{v}}}}q_{\bm{j}}\exp(\bfi\bmK\cdot\bmz_{\bmj}),\quad-N\leq{\bm{K}}\leq N,

(4.14)

which is a non-uniform discrete Fourier transform mapping non-uniform samples
{\bm{z}}_{{\bm{j}}}\in[0,\pi]^{d}
into frequencies
{\bm{K}}\in[-N,N]^{d}
. This can be done efficiently using the NUFFT. In recent years, various NUFFT algorithms have been developed. In our numerical realization, we employ an efficient algorithm called FINUFFT
[
2
]
. The general idea is to apply an interpolation between non-uniform samples and an equispaced grid, and then perform the uniform FFT on the new grid. This algorithm only costs
\mathcal{O}(M_{{\bm{v}}}^{d}|\log\epsilon|+N^{d}\log N)
operations in computing (
4.14
) with the requested precision
\epsilon
. Once we obtain
\widetilde{F}_{\bm{K}}
,
F_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\sigma}},{\bm{\xi}})
can be retrieved as

•

in 2D

F_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\sigma}},{\bm{\xi}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{\bm{k}}}}\frac{1}{2}{\bf Re}\left(\widetilde{F}_{(k_{1},k_{2})}+\widetilde{F}_{(-k_{1},k_{2})}\right);

(4.15)

•

in 3D

F_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\sigma}},{\bm{\xi}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{c_{\bm{k}}}}\frac{1}{4}{\bf Re}\left(\widetilde{F}_{(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})}+\widetilde{F}_{(-k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})}+\widetilde{F}_{(k_{1},-k_{2},k_{3})}+\widetilde{F}_{(k_{1},k_{2},-k_{3})}\right).

(4.16)

This procedure needs to be repeated for every
{\bm{\sigma}}
and
{\bm{\xi}}
, hence the overall computational cost for getting
F_{\bm{k}}({\bm{\sigma}},{\bm{\xi}})
is
\mathcal{O}(M_{{\bm{\sigma}}}M_{{\bm{v}}}^{2d}|\log\epsilon|+M_{{\bm{\sigma}}}M_{{\bm{v}}}^{d}N^{d}\log N)
.

To evaluate the loss term
\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N,-}
, we change the integrals of
(\bm{v,v_{*}})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
into integrals of
({\bm{\xi}},{\bm{\eta}})\in I^{d}\times I^{d}
in (
4.9
) (similarly as in the previous subsection for
\widetilde{I}_{2}(\bm{i,j,k})
):

\mathcal{Q}_{\bm{k}}^{N,-}=\int_{I^{d}}\left(\int_{I^{d}}\int_{S^{d-1}}\mathcal{B}({\bm{v}}({\bm{\xi}}),{\bm{v}_{*}}({\bm{\eta}}),{\bm{\sigma}})f_{N}({\bm{v}_{*}}({\bm{\eta}}))\frac{\bm{\omega(\eta)}}{\left[{\bm{\mu}}(\bm{\eta})\right]^{2}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{\sigma}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{\eta}}\right)f_{N}({\bm{v}}({\bm{\xi}}))\,\frac{{\bm{T}}_{\bm{k}}({{\bm{\xi}}})\bm{\omega({\bm{\xi}})}}{\sqrt{\bm{c_{k}}}\left[{\bm{\mu}}({{\bm{\xi}}})\right]^{4}}\,{\rm d}{\bm{\xi}}.

(4.17)

Then one can just evaluate the terms in the parentheses directly with complexity
\mathcal{O}(M_{{\bm{v}}}^{2d}M_{{\bm{\sigma}}})
. The outer integral in
{\bm{\xi}}
can be viewed as the Chebyshev transform of some function thus can be evaluated efficiently by the fast Chebyshev transform in
\mathcal{O}(M_{{\bm{v}}}^{d}\log M_{{\bm{v}}})
. In particular, if we consider the Maxwell kernel, i.e.,
\mathcal{B}({\bm{v}},{\bm{v}_{*}},{\bm{\sigma}})\equiv\text{constant}
, terms in the parentheses only requires
\mathcal{O}(M_{{\bm{v}}}^{d})
complexity.

5
Numerical examples

In this section, we perform extensive numerical tests to demonstrate the accuracy and efficiency of the proposed Petrov-Galerkin spectral method in both 2D and 3D.

Recall that the main motivation of the current work is to obtain better accuracy by considering approximations in an unbounded domain. To illustrate this point, we will compare three methods to solve the Boltzmann equation:

(1) Fast Fourier-Galerkin spectral method proposed in
[
14
]
:

this method can achieve a good accuracy-efficiency tradeoff among the current deterministic methods for the Boltzmann equation. However, it requires the truncation of the domain to
[-L,L]^{d}
, where
L
is often chosen empirically such that the solution is close to zero at the boundary.

(2) Fast Chebyshev-0 method:

the method proposed in the current paper using the logarithmic mapping (
2.9
), where
r=0
and the scaling parameter
S
needs to be properly chosen.

(3) Fast Chebyshev-1 method:

the method proposed in the current paper using the algebraic mapping (
2.10
), where
r=1
and the scaling parameter
S
needs to be properly chosen.

In all three methods, the choice of truncation or mapping/scaling parameters has a great impact on the numerical accuracy. In the following tests, we first determine
L
in the Fourier spectral method. Then for the two Chebyshev methods, we propose an adaptive strategy to determine the scaling parameter
S
: for example, in 1D, the Chebyshev-Gauss-Lobatto quadrature points on the interval
[-1,1]
are given by

-1=\xi_{1}<\xi_{2}<\ldots<\xi_{N}=1.

We choose
S
such that the two quadrature points
\xi_{2}
and
\xi_{N-1}
are mapped to the boundary of
[-L,L]
, i.e.,

v(\xi_{1})=-\infty,\quad v(\xi_{2})=-L,\quad v(\xi_{N-1})=L,\quad v(\xi_{N})=-\infty.

Note that this
S
is adaptive in the sense that different
N
will correspond to different
S
.

5.1.1

5.1.2
Computing the moments

We next consider the time evolution problem and check the accuracy for moments approximation. Since the fast Chebyshev-0 method performs generally better than the fast Chebyshev-1 method, we will restrict to the former in the following tests. The comparison with the fast Fourier method will still be considered.

In (
3.1
), we choose the collision kernel
\mathcal{B}\equiv 1/(2\pi)
and the initial condition as

f^{0}({\bm{v}})=\frac{\rho_{1}}{2\pi T_{1}}\exp\left(-\frac{({\bm{v}}-V_{1})^{2}}{2T_{1}}\right)+\frac{\rho_{2}}{2\pi T_{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{({\bm{v}}-V_{2})^{2}}{2T_{2}}\right),

(5.3)

where
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}=1/2
,
T_{1}=T_{2}=1
and
V_{1}=(x_{1},y_{1})=(-1,2)
,
V_{2}=(x_{2},y_{2})=(3,-3)
.

Then for the momentum flow and energy flow defined as

6
Conclusion

We introduced a Petrov-Galerkin spectral method for the spatially homogeneous Boltzmann equation in multi-dimensions. The mapped Chebyshev functions in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
were carefully chosen to serve as the trial functions and test functions in the approximation. In the case of the algebraic mapping, we established a consistency result for approximation of the collision operator as well as the conservation property for the moments. Thanks to the close relation between the Chebyshev functions and the Fourier cosine series, we proposed a fast algorithm to alleviate the memory constraint in the precomputation and accelerate the online computation in the direct implementation. Through a series of numerical examples in 2D and 3D, we demonstrated that the proposed method can provide better accuracy (at least one or two digits for small
N
) in comparison to the popular Fourier spectral method.

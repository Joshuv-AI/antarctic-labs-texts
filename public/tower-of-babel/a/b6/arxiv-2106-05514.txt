Symmetric subcategories, tilting modules and derived recollements


Abstract
For any good tilting module T T over a ring A A , there exists an n n -symmetric subcategory ℰ \mathscr{E} of a module category such that the derived category of the endomorphism ring of T T is a recollement of the derived categories of ℰ \mathscr{E} and A A in the sense of Beilinson-Bernstein-Deligne. Thus the kernel of the total left-derived tensor functor induced by the tilting module is triangle equivalent to the derived category of ℰ \mathscr{E} .

Introduction

In representation theory of algebras and groups, finitely generated tilting modules have successfully been applied to understanding different aspects of algebraic and homological structure and properties of finite groups, finite-dimensional algebras and modules (for example, see
[
9
,
14
,
17
,
18
,
27
]
), while infinitely generated tilting modules not only have involved many important modules, such as adic modules, Fuchs divisible modules, generic modules, and Prüfer modules, but also have been of significant interest in studying derived categories and equivalences of general algebras and rings (see
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
10
,
11
,
31
]
). In this general context, a lot of results emerge with completely different features. So, the theory of infinitely generated tilting modules renews our view on finitely generated tilting modules, and gives us also surprising information about the whole tilting theory.

Let us first recall the definition of tilting modules.

2.1
Semi-orthogonal decompositions, recollements and homotopy (co)limits

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an additive category.

A full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
is always assumed to be closed under isomorphisms.
For an object

, we write
{\rm add}(X)
for the full
subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
consisting of all direct summands of
finite coproducts of copies of
X
. If
\mathcal{C}
admits
coproducts (that is, coproducts indexed over sets exist in

{\mathcal{C}}
), we write
{\rm Add}(X)
for the full subcategory of

\mathcal{C}
consisting of all direct summands of coproducts
of copies of
X
. Dually, we write
{\rm Prod}(X)
for the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}

consisting of all direct summands of products of copies of

X
if
\mathcal{C}
admits products (that is, products indexed over sets exist in

{\mathcal{C}}
).

We denote the composition of two morphisms
f:X\to Y
and
g:Y\to Z
in
\mathcal{C}
by
fg
. We write
f^{*}
for
{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(Z,f):{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(Z,X)\rightarrow{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(Z,Y)
and
f_{*}

for
{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(f,Z):{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(Y,Z)\rightarrow{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(X,Z)
. While for two functors
F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
and
G:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{E}
, the composition of
F
and

is denoted by

which is a functor from
\mathcal{C}
to

\mathcal{E}
. The kernel and image of

are defined as
{\rm Ker}(F):=\{X\in\mathcal{C}\mid FX\simeq 0\}
and

{\rm Im}(F):=\{Y\in\mathcal{D}\mid\exists\,X\in\mathcal{C},\;FX\simeq Y\}
, respectively.
Thus
{\rm Ker}(F)
and
{\rm Im}(F)
are closed under isomorphisms in
\mathcal{C}
.

For a complex
X^{\bullet}=(X^{i},d_{X}^{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
over
\mathcal{C}
, the morphism
d_{X}^{i}:X^{i}\rightarrow X^{i+1}
is called a

differential
of
X^{\bullet}
. For simplicity, we sometimes write

(X^{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
for

without mentioning

. For an integer

, we denote by

the complex by shifting

and

.

Let
{\mathscr{C}}(\mathcal{C})
be the category of all complexes over

\mathcal{C}
with chain maps, and
{\mathscr{K}}(\mathcal{C})
the homotopy
category of
{\mathscr{C}}(\mathcal{C})
. As usual, we denote by

{\mathscr{C}}^{-}(\mathcal{C})
the category of bounded-above complexes over
\mathcal{C}
, and
by

{\mathscr{C}}^{-}(\mathcal{C})
.
Note that

and

are triangulated
categories. For a triangulated category, its shift functor is
denoted by
[1]
universally.

Now, let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category and
X^{\bullet}\in{\mathscr{C}}(\mathcal{A})
. For

, there are two left-truncated complexes

X{{}^{\leq n}}:\;\;\cdots\longrightarrow X^{n-3}\xrightarrow{d_{X}^{n-3}}X^{n-2}\xrightarrow{d_{X}^{n-2}}X^{n-1}\xrightarrow{d_{X}^{n-1}}X^{n}\longrightarrow 0,

Dually, right-truncated complexes

(by taking cokernels on the left) can be defined.
Further, there are bi-truncated complexes for a pair of integers

Let

be the

in degree

The following is well known (for example, see

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

(1)

for each

is the unit adjunction morphism of

.

(2)

and

induces a triangle equivalence

of which a

Next, we recall the definition of homotopy colimits and limits in triangulated categories.

Definition 2.4
.

[
8
,
24
]

Let
\mathscr{D}
be a triangulated category such that countable coproducts exist in
\mathscr{D}
. Let

X_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}X_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}X_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{n}}}{{\longrightarrow}}X_{n+1}\longrightarrow\cdots

\mathscr{D}
. The
homotopy colimit
of this sequence, denoted by
\underrightarrow{{\rm Hocolim}}(X_{n})
,
is given, up to non-canonical isomorphism, by the triangle

\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}X_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(1-f_{*})}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}X_{n}\longrightarrow\underrightarrow{{\rm Hocolim}}(X_{n})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}X_{n}[1]

where the morphism
(1-f_{*})
is induced by
({\rm Id}_{X_{i}},-f_{i}):X_{i}\to X_{i}\oplus X_{i+1}\subseteq\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}X_{n}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.

Dually, the
homotopy limit
, denoted by
\underleftarrow{{\rm Holim}}
, of a sequence of objects and morphisms in a triangulated category with countable products can be defined.

Now, we consider homotopy colimits and limits in derived categories of abelian categories.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category. Recall that
\mathcal{A}

satisfies

satisfies

\rm{AB4^{\prime}}
if products indexed over sets exist in
\mathcal{A}
and products of short exact sequences in

and

is the module category of a ring.

A full subcategory

of the abelian category

is called an

of

if

is an abelian category and the inclusion

Finally, we mention a general fact on adjoint pairs of functors.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
F:\mathcal{C}\rightarrow\mathcal{D}
and
G:\mathcal{D}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}
be two functors of categories such that

forms a adjoint pair.

(1)
If

(2)

G
gives rise to an equivalence
\mathcal{D}\rightarrow{\rm Im}(G)
with the quasi-inverse given by the restriction of
F
to
{\rm Im}(G)
. In particular, if
C\in{\rm Im}(G)
, then the unit
\eta_{C}:C\rightarrow GF(C)
is an isomorphism.

2.2
Derived categories of exact categories

An
exact category
(in the sense of Quillen) is by definition an additive category endowed with a class of conflations
closed under isomorphism and satisfying certain axioms (for example, see
[
21
, Section 4]
). When the additive category is an abelian
category, the class of conflations coincides with the class of short exact sequences.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an exact category and
\mathcal{F}
a full subcategory of
\mathcal{E}
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is
closed under extensions
in
\mathcal{E}
,
that is, for any conflation
0\to X\to Y\to Z\to 0
in
\mathcal{E}
with both
X,Z\in\mathcal{F}
, we have
Y\in\mathcal{F}
. Then
\mathcal{F}
, endowed with the conflations in
\mathcal{E}
having their terms in
\mathcal{F}
, is an exact
category, and the inclusion
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
is a fully faithful exact functor. In this case,

\mathcal{F}
is called a
fully exact subcategory
of
\mathcal{E}
(see
[
21
, Section 4]
).

From now on, let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category and
\mathcal{E}
a fully exact subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
. A complex
X^{\bullet}\in{\mathscr{C}}(\mathcal{E})
is said to be
strictly exact

if it is exact in
{\mathscr{C}}(\mathcal{A})
and all of its boundaries belong to
\mathcal{E}
.
Let
\mathscr{K}_{\rm{ac}}(\mathcal{E})
be the full subcategory of
{\mathscr{K}}(\mathcal{E})
consisting of those complexes which are isomorphic to
strictly exact complexes. Then
\mathscr{K}_{\rm{ac}}(\mathcal{E})
is a full triangulated subcategory of
{\mathscr{K}}(\mathcal{E})
closed under direct summands.
The
unbounded derived category
of
\mathcal{E}
, denoted by
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{E})
, is defined to be the Verdier quotient of
{\mathscr{K}}(\mathcal{E})

by
\mathscr{K}_{\rm{ac}}(\mathcal{E})
. Similarly, the bounded-below, bounded-above and bounded derived categories

{\mathscr{D}}^{+}(\mathcal{E})
,
{\mathscr{D}}^{-}(\mathcal{E})
and
{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(\mathcal{E})
can be defined. Observe that the canonical functor
\mathscr{D}^{*}(\mathcal{E})\to{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{E})
is fully faithful
for
*\in\{+,-,b\}
.

If
F:\mathcal{E}_{1}\to\mathcal{E}_{2}
is an additive functor of exact categories, then
F
induces a functor
K(F):\mathscr{K}(\mathcal{E}_{1})\to\mathscr{K}(\mathcal{E}_{2})
of homotopy categories. Further, if
F
is an exact functor, that is,
F
sends conflations in
\mathcal{E}_{1}
to the ones in
\mathcal{E}_{2}
, then
F
induces a functor
D(F):\mathscr{D}(\mathcal{E}_{1})\to\mathscr{D}(\mathcal{E}_{2})
of derived categories.

Let
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{E}
be fully exact subcategories of
\mathcal{A}
with

\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
. Then
\mathcal{F}
can be regarded as a fully exact subcategory of
\mathcal{E}
.
We consider the following three conditions:

(a)
If
0\to X\to Y\to Z\to 0
is an exact sequence in
\mathcal{A}
with
X\in\mathcal{E}
and
Y,Z\in\mathcal{F}
, then
X\in\mathcal{F}
.

(b)
Each exact sequence
0\to E_{1}\to E_{0}\to F\to 0
in
\mathcal{A}
with
E_{1},E_{0}\in\mathcal{E}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}
can be completed into
an exact commutative diagram

\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{F_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{F_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{F\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}
\textstyle{0\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{E_{0}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{F\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{0}

with
F_{1},F_{0}\in\mathcal{F}
.

(c)
There is a natural number
n
such that, for each object
E\in\mathcal{E}
, there is a long exact sequence in
\mathcal{A}
:

0\longrightarrow F_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{n}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}F_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}F_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\longrightarrow 0

with
F_{i}\in\mathcal{F}
and
{\rm Im}(f_{i})\in\mathcal{E}
for all
0\leq i\leq n
.

The following result describes impacts of these conditions on derived categories.

Lemma 2.7
.

(1)
If
(a)
and
(b)
hold, then the inclusion
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
induces a fully faithful triangle functor
{\mathscr{D}}^{-}(\mathcal{F})\to{\mathscr{D}}^{-}(\mathcal{E})
.

(2)
Suppose that
\mathcal{E}
is closed under direct summands in
\mathcal{A}
. If
(a)
and
(c)
hold, then the inclusion
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
induces a triangle equivalence
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{E})
which restricts to an equivalence
\mathscr{D}^{*}(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\mathscr{D}^{*}(\mathcal{E})
for any
*\in\{+,-,b\}
.

Proof.

(1)
follows from the dual of
[
21
, Theorem 12.1]
(see also
[
26
, Proposition A.2.1]
), while
(2)
follows from

[
26
, Proposition A.5.6]
.
\square

If all arrows in
(a),(b)
and
(c)
are reversed, we get dual conditions of
(a),(b)
and
(c)
, respectively. So the dual version of Lemma
2.7
holds true.

2.3
Derived functors of the module categories of rings

Let
R
be a (unitary associative) ring. The full subcategories of projective and injective
R
-modules
are denoted by
R\mbox{{\rm-Proj}}
and
R\mbox{{\rm-Inj}}
, respectively.
If
M
is an
R
-module and
I
is a nonempty set, then
M^{(I)}
and
M^{I}
denote the direct sum and product of
I
copies of
M
,
respectively. The projective dimension and the endomorphism ring of
M
are denoted by
{\rm proj.dim}({{}_{R}}M)
and
{\rm End}_{R}(M)
, respectively.

A full subcategory
\mathcal{T}
of
R\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
is called a
thick subcategory
if it is closed under direct summands in
R\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
and has
the
two out of three property
: for any short exact sequence
0\to X\to Y\to Z\to 0
in
R\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
with two terms in
\mathcal{T}
, the third term belongs to
\mathcal{T}
as well.

In the sequel, we simply write
{\mathscr{C}}(R)
,
{\mathscr{K}}(R)
and
{\mathscr{D}}(R)
for

{\mathscr{C}}(R\mbox{{\rm-Mod}})
,
{\mathscr{K}}(R\mbox{{\rm-Mod}})
and
{\mathscr{D}}(R\mbox{{\rm-Mod}})
, respectively, and
regard
R\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
as the subcategory of
{\mathscr{D}}(R)
consisting of
all stalk complexes concentrated in degree zero.

Let
{\mathscr{K}}(R)_{P}
(respectively,
{\mathscr{K}}(R)_{I}
) be the smallest
full triangulated subcategory of
{\mathscr{K}}(R)
, which
contains all the bounded-above (respectively, bounded-below)
complexes of projective (respectively, injective)
R
-modules, and
is closed under arbitrary direct sums (respectively, direct
products).
Since
{\mathscr{K}}(R\mbox{{\rm-Proj}})
satisfies theses two properties, we have
{\mathscr{K}}(R)_{P}\subseteq{\mathscr{K}}(R\mbox{{\rm-Proj}})
.
Let
\mathscr{K}_{\rm{ac}}(R)
be the full subcategory of
{\mathscr{K}}(R)
consisting of all
exact complexes. Then
({\mathscr{K}}(R){{}_{P}},\mathscr{K}_{\rm{ac}}(R))
forms a semi-orthogonal decomposition
of
{\mathscr{K}}(R)
. Let
{}_{p}(-):{\mathscr{K}}(R)\to{\mathscr{K}}(R){{}_{P}}
be a right adjoint of the inclusion

{\mathscr{K}}(R)_{P}\to{\mathscr{K}}(R)
. Then, by Lemma
2.2
, the functor
{}_{p}(-)
induces a triangle equivalence
{\mathscr{D}}(R)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\mathscr{K}}(R){{}_{P}}
of which
a quasi-inverse is the composition of the inclusion
{\mathscr{K}}(R)_{P}\to{\mathscr{K}}(R)
with the localization functor
{\mathscr{K}}(R)\to{\mathscr{D}}(R)
.
Moreover, for each
X^{\bullet}
in
{\mathscr{K}}(R)
, the associated counit adjunction morphism
\alpha_{X^{\bullet}}:{{}_{p}}X^{\bullet}\to X^{\bullet}
is a quasi-isomorphism. Thus

\alpha_{X^{\bullet}}
or simply
{}_{p}X^{\bullet}
is called a
projective resolution
of
X^{\bullet}
in
{\mathscr{D}}(R)
. For example, if
X
is an

R
-module, then
{}_{p}X
can be chosen as a deleted projective resolution of
{}_{R}X
.
Dually,
(\mathscr{K}_{\rm{ac}}(R),{\mathscr{K}}(R){{}_{I}})
is a semi-orthogonal decomposition of
{\mathscr{K}}(R)
. In particular, there exists a quasi-isomorphism
\beta_{X^{\bullet}}:X^{\bullet}\to{{}_{i}}X^{\bullet}
in
{\mathscr{K}}(R)
with
{{}_{i}}X^{\bullet}\in{\mathscr{K}}(R)_{I}
. The
complex
{}_{i}X^{\bullet}
is called the
injective resolution
of

X^{\bullet}
in
{\mathscr{D}}(R)
. In particular,
{\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{K}}(R)}(P^{\bullet},X^{\bullet})\simeq{\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{D}}(R)}(P^{\bullet},X^{\bullet})
and
{\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{K}}(R)}(X^{\bullet},I^{\bullet})\simeq{\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{D}}(R)}(X^{\bullet},I^{\bullet})
for
P^{\bullet}\in\mathscr{K}^{-}(R\mbox{{\rm-Proj}}),I^{\bullet}\in\mathscr{K}^{+}(R\mbox{-Inj})
and
X^{\bullet}\in{\mathscr{K}}(R).

Now, let
S
be another ring. For a triangle functor
F:{\mathscr{K}}(R)\to{\mathscr{K}}(S)
, its
total left-derived functor

{\mathbb{L}}F:{\mathscr{D}}(R)\to{\mathscr{D}}(S)
is defined by

X^{\bullet}\mapsto F(_{p}X^{\bullet})
, and its
total right-derived
functor

{\mathbb{R}}F:{\mathscr{D}}(R)\to{\mathscr{D}}(S)
is defined by
X^{\bullet}\mapsto F(_{i}X^{\bullet})
. Further, if
F(X^{\bullet})
is exact whenever
X^{\bullet}
is exact, then
F

induces a triangle functor
D(F):{\mathscr{D}}(R)\to{\mathscr{D}}(S)
,

X^{\bullet}\mapsto F(X^{\bullet})
. In this case, up to natural
isomorphism,
{\mathbb{L}}F={\mathbb{R}}F=D(F)
, and

D(F)
is called the
derived functor
of
F
.

Given a complex
M^{\bullet}
of
R
-
S
-bimodules, we denote by
{\mathbb{R}}{\rm Hom}_{R}(M^{\bullet},-)
and
M^{\bullet}\otimes^{\rm\mathbb{L}}_{S}-
the
total right-derived functor of
{\rm Hom}^{\bullet}_{R}(M^{\bullet},-)
and the total left-derived functor of

M^{\bullet}\otimes^{\bullet}_{S}-
, respectively. Then
\big(M^{\bullet}\otimes^{\rm\mathbb{L}}_{S}-,{\mathbb{R}}{\rm Hom}_{R}(M^{\bullet},-)\big)
is an adjoint pair of triangle functors. In case of

Y\in S\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
and
X\in R\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
, we write
M^{\bullet}\otimes_{S}Y
and
{\rm Hom}_{R}(M^{\bullet},\,X)

for
M^{\bullet}\otimes^{\bullet}_{S}Y
and
{\rm Hom}^{\bullet}_{R}(M^{\bullet},\,X)
, respectively.

3
Symmetric subcategories of abelian categories

Now we introduce
n
-symmetric subcategories of an ableian category for
n\geq 0
.

Recall that an abelian category is
complete
(respectively,
cocomplete
) if it has products (respectively, coproducts) indexed over sets; and
bicomplete
if it is complete and cocomplete.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\mathcal{A}
be a bicomplete abelian category. An additive full subcategory
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathcal{A}
is said to be
n
-symmetric
if

(1)

\mathcal{B}
is closed under extensions, products and coproducts.

(2)
Given an exact sequence
0\rightarrow X\rightarrow M_{n}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow M_{1}\rightarrow M_{0}\rightarrow Y\rightarrow 0
in
\mathcal{A}
, we have
X,Y\in\mathcal{B}
whenever all
M_{i}\in\mathcal{B}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be an additive full subcategory of a bicomplete abelian category
\mathcal{A}
.

(1)
If
\mathcal{B}
is an
n
-symmetric subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
, then
\mathcal{B}
is an exact, thick subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
. It is also
(n+1)
-symmetric.

(2)
If
\mathcal{B}_{i}
is an
m_{i}
-symmetric subcategories of
\mathcal{A}
for
i=1,2
then
\mathcal{B}_{1}\cap\mathcal{B}_{2}
is a max
\{m_{1},m_{2}\}
-symmetric subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
.

(3)
If
\mathcal{B}
is closed under extensions and satisfies Definition
3.1
(2), then
\mathcal{B}
is an
n
-wide subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
in the sense of
[
22
, Definition 4.1]
.

(4)

\mathcal{B}
is
0
-symmetric if and only if
\mathcal{B}
is a Serre subcategory (that is, closed under subobjects, quotient objects and extensions) closed under coproducts and products if and only
\mathcal{B}
is a localizing subcategory (that is, a Serre subcategory closed under coproducts) closed under products.

\mathcal{B}
is
1
-symmetric if and only if

\mathcal{B}
is an abelian subcategory closed under extensions, coproducts and products.

In particular, if
R
is a ring and
\mathcal{A}=R\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
, then a full subcategory
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathcal{A}
is
0
-symmetric if and only if there is an ideal
I
of
R
with
I^{2}=I
such that
\mathcal{B}=(R/I)\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
=
\{M\in R\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}\mid IM=0\}
. This follows from
[
28
, Proposition 6.12]
. Moreover, a full subcategory
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathcal{A}
is
1
-symmetric if and only if there is a ring epimorphism
\lambda:R\rightarrow S
with
{\rm Tor}_{1}^{R}(S,S)=0
such that
\mathcal{B}
coincides with the image of the induced fully faithful functor
\lambda_{*}:S\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}\rightarrow R\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
, that is,
\mathcal{B}={\rm Im}(\lambda_{*})\simeq S\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}.
This can be seen by
[
10
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}
be bicomplete abelian categories satisfying AB4 and AB4
′
, and let
F:{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{C})
be a triangle functor commuting with coproducts and products. If there are integers
s\leq r
such that

H^{i}(FX)=0
for all
X\in\mathcal{A}
,
r<i
, or
i<s
, then
\mathscr{E}:=\mathcal{A}\cap{\rm Ker}(F)
is an
(r-s)
-symmetric subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.
It is easy to see that
\mathscr{E}
is an additive subcategory and closed under extensions, coproducts and products. Let
n:=r-s
and let
0\rightarrow X\rightarrow M_{n}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow M_{1}\rightarrow M_{0}\rightarrow Y\rightarrow 0
be an exact sequence in
\mathcal{A}
with
M_{j}\in\mathscr{E}
for all
j
. By assumption, we have
H^{i}(F(Y))=0
for
i>r
or
i<s
. Now, let
s\leq i\leq r
. Then
n+1+i\geq n+1+s=r+1
. It follows from
F(M_{j})=0
for
0\leq j\leq n
that
FY\simeq F(X)[n+1]
in
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathcal{C}).
This implies

H^{i}(F(Y))\simeq H^{i}(F(X)[n+1])\simeq H^{n+1+i}(F(X))
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In particular,
H^{i}(F(Y))=0
by assumption. Thus
H^{i}(F(Y))=0
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
and
Y\in{\rm Ker}(F)
. This also shows
X\in{\rm Ker}(F)
. Thus
\mathscr{E}
is an
n
-symmetric subcategory of
\mathcal{A}
.
\square

As an application of Proposition
3.3
, we consider the module categories of rings.

Example 3.4
.

Let
R
and
S
be rings, and let
{{}_{R}}M_{S}
be an

4.2

By the diagram

for each

In this recollement, the functors
D(i)
,
\Phi[-1]
,
H
and
G
can be restricted to functors between
\mathscr{D}^{-}
-derived categories by Remark
4.9
. So, to show that this recollement restricts to
\mathscr{D}^{-}
-derived categories, it suffices to show that the left adjoint
j_{!}:{\mathscr{D}}(A)\to{\mathscr{D}}(B)
of
G
restricts to a functor
{\mathscr{D}}^{-}(A)\to{\mathscr{D}}^{-}(B)
.

For any
m\in\mathbb{Z}
and
X\in{\mathscr{D}}(A)
, there are isomorphisms of abelian groups:

({\dagger})\quad{\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{D}}(A)}(X,G(B^{\vee})[m])\simeq{\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{D}}(B)}(j_{!}(X),B^{\vee}[m])\simeq{\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{K}}(B)}(j_{!}(X),B^{\vee}[m])

\;\;\quad\simeq H^{m}(j_{!}(X)^{\vee})\simeq(H^{-m}(j_{!}(X)))^{\vee}.

Let
X\in{\mathscr{D}}^{-}(A)
. Since
G(B^{\vee})\in{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(A)
by Lemma
4.15
, there is an integer
m_{X}
such that
{\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{D}}(A)}(X,

G(B^{\vee})[m])=0
for all
m\leq m_{X}
.
Observe that the functor
(-)^{\vee}
sends nonzero modules to nonzero modules. Thus
H^{-m}(j_{!}(X))=0
for all
m\leq m_{X}
. This implies
j_{!}(X)\in{\mathscr{D}}^{-}(B)
.

Recall that a ring homomorphism
\lambda:B\to C
is called a
ring epimorphism
if the restriction functor
\lambda_{*}:C\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}\to B\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
is fully faithful.
A ring epimorphism
\lambda:B\to C
is said to be
homological
if
{\rm Tor}^{B}_{m}(C,C)=0
for all
m\geq 1
. This is also equivalent to saying that the induced derived functor
D(\lambda_{*}):{\mathscr{D}}(C)\to{\mathscr{D}}(B)
is fully faithful. Following
[
11
]
, a good tilting module
{{}_{A}}T
is said to be
homological
if there exists a homological ring epimorphism
\lambda:B\to C
such that
D(\lambda_{*})
induces an equivalence
{\mathscr{D}}(C)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}{\rm Ker}(G)
of triangulated categories.

To prove Corollary
1.3
, we recall the definition of derived decompositions of abelian categories in
[
13
]
.

Definition 4.18
.

[
13
, Definition 1.1]

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category, and let
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
be full subcategories of
\mathcal{A}
. The pair
(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C})
is called a
derived decomposition
of
\mathcal{A}
if both
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
are abelian subcategories of
\mathcal{A}
such that

(1)
the inclusions
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{A}
induce fully faithful functors
{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(\mathcal{B})\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
and
{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(\mathcal{C})\hookrightarrow{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
, respectively.

(2)

\big({\mathscr{D}}^{b}(\mathcal{B}),{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(\mathcal{C})\big)
is a semi-orthogonal decomposition of
{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof of Corollary
1.3
. The equivalence of (1) and (2) is shown in
[
5
, Proposition 6.2]
, while the one of (1) and (3) is proved in
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
. Here, we prefer to giving a new and shorter proofs of these facts by the results in the previuos sections.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
: If
T
is homological, then, by definition, the associated homological ring epimorphism
\lambda:B\to C

induces an equivalence from
C\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
to
\mathscr{E}
. Thus
\mathscr{E}
is an abelian subcategory of
B\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
: Suppose
(2)
holds. Then
\mathscr{E}
is closed under direct sums, direct products, kernels and cokernels by Lemma
4.6
(1).
It follows that there exists a ring epimorphism
\lambda:B\to C
such that
\lambda_{*}

induces an equivalence
C\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{E}
of abelian categories (for example, see
[
10
, Lemma 2.1]
).
Since
\overline{D(i)}
is fully faithful by Proposition
4.17
, the functor
D(\lambda_{*})
is also fully faithful.
Thus
\lambda
is homological. Further, it follows from
{\rm Im}(\overline{D(i)})={\rm Ker}(G)
that
D(\lambda_{*})
induces an equivalence from
{\mathscr{D}}(C)
to
{\rm Ker}(G)
.
This shows
(1)
.

(2)\Rightarrow(3)
: Let
\rho^{\bullet}:{\rm Hom}_{A}(P^{\bullet},A)\otimes_{A}T\to{\rm Hom}_{A}(P^{\bullet},T)
be the chain map defined in Lemma
4.15
(2).
Associated with
B^{\vee}
, there exists the triangle in
{\mathscr{D}}(B):

(\bigtriangleup):\;X^{\bullet}[-1]\to B^{\vee}\to Y^{\bullet}\to X^{\bullet}

with

Y^{\bullet}:=({\rm Hom}_{A}(P^{\bullet},A)\otimes_{A}T)^{\vee}\in{\rm Im}(H)={\rm Im}(D(j))\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;X^{\bullet}:={\rm Coker}(\rho^{\bullet})^{\vee}[1]\in{\rm Ker}(G)={\rm Im}(D(i)).

Since
\mathcal{Y}\subseteq B\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
is an abelian subcategory, we have
H^{s}(Y^{\bullet})\in\mathcal{Y}
for any
s\in\mathbb{Z}
. Taking cohomologies on the triangle yields
H^{-s}(X^{\bullet})\simeq H^{-s}(Y^{\bullet})
whenever
s\geq 2
.
Moreover,
H^{t}(X^{\bullet})\simeq H^{t+1}\big({\rm Coker}(\rho^{\bullet})^{\vee}\big)\simeq\big(H^{-t-1}({\rm Coker}(\rho^{\bullet}))\big)^{\vee}=0
for any
t\geq 0
. In particular,
H^{0}(X^{\bullet})=0
.

Suppose
(2)
holds. Then
H^{s}(X^{\bullet})\in\mathscr{E}
for any
s\in\mathbb{Z}
.
By Lemma
4.10
,
H^{-s}(X^{\bullet})=0=H^{-s}(Y^{\bullet})
for any
s\geq 2
. It follows that

0=H^{-m}(Y^{\bullet})\simeq(H^{m}({\rm Hom}_{A}(P^{\bullet},A)\otimes_{A}T))^{\vee}
for any
m\geq 2
.
Now
(3)
holds by the fact that the functor
(-)^{\vee}
sends nonzero modules to nonzero modules.

(3)\Rightarrow(2)
: Suppose
(3)
holds. By Lemma
4.6
(1),
\mathscr{E}
is an abelian subcategory of
B\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
if and only if
it is closed under cokernels in
B\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
. Recall that
\mathcal{X}:=\{X\in B\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}\mid{\rm Ext}_{B}^{n}(X,Y)=0,\,Y\in\mathcal{Y},\,n=0,1\}
is always closed under cokernels in
B\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
. So it is enough to show

\mathscr{E}=\mathcal{X}
.

Clearly,
\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathcal{X}
. To show the converse, we first claim that each module
U\in\mathcal{X}
can be embedded into a module belonging to
\mathscr{E}
. Actually,
U
can be embedded into its injective envelop
J
in
B\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
. By the first isomorphism in Corollary
4.12
(2),
U
is isomorphic to a submodule of
X_{J}
. So, it suffices to show
X_{I}\in\mathscr{E}
for any injective
B
-module
I
.

Obviously,
H^{s}(Y^{\bullet})=0
for any
s\geq 1
. Combining this with
(3)
, we have
H^{-s}(Y^{\bullet})=0
for any
s\neq 0,1
. This implies

H^{-t}(X^{\bullet})=0
for any
t\neq 0,1
. As
H^{0}(X^{\bullet})=0
, we get
X^{\bullet}\simeq H^{-1}(X^{\bullet})[1]
in
{\mathscr{D}}(B)
.
Consequently,
H^{-1}(X^{\bullet})\in\mathscr{E}
by
X^{\bullet}\in{\rm Ker}(G)
. By Lemmas
4.3
(2) and
4.15
(2), the triangle
(\diamondsuit)
associated with
B^{\vee}
is isomorphic to the one
(\bigtriangleup)
. This shows
X_{B^{\vee}}\simeq H^{-1}(X^{\bullet})\in\mathscr{E}
. Recall that
D(i)
and
\Phi
commute with direct products by Lemma
4.7
(3), and that
{\rm Prod}(B^{\vee})
consists of all injective
B
-modules.
Thus
X_{I}\in{\rm Prod}(X_{B^{\vee}})\subseteq\mathscr{E}
by the diagram
(\sharp)
and Lemma
4.6
(1).

Now, it follows from the above claim that there is an injection
f_{0}:U\to E_{0}
with
E_{0}\in\mathscr{E}
. Since
\mathcal{X}
contains
\mathscr{E}
and is closed under cokernels in
B\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
, we have
{\rm Coker}(f_{0})\in\mathcal{X}
. By iterating this construction, we can get an infinitely long exact sequence

0\to U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{0}}}{{\longrightarrow}}E_{0}\to E_{1}\to\cdots\to E_{i}\to\cdots
of
B
-modules such that
E_{i}\in\mathscr{E}
for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
U\in\mathscr{E}
by Lemma
4.6
(2). Hence we have shown
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathscr{E}
. Thus
(2)
holds.

(2)
\Leftrightarrow
(4).
Note that
D^{b}(i):{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(\mathscr{E})\to{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(B)
and
D^{b}(j):{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(\mathcal{Y})\to{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(B)
are fully faithful by Proposition
4.17
and Lemma
4.7
(1), respectively. Since
{{}_{A}}T
and
T_{B}
have finite projective dimension,
H
and
G
can be restricted to functors between bounded derived categories. It follows that
({\mathscr{D}}^{b}(B)\cap{\rm Ker}(G),{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(B)\cap{\rm Im}(H))
is a semi-orthogonal decomposition of
{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(B)
. Further,
{\rm Im}({\mathscr{D}}^{b}(i))={\mathscr{D}}^{b}(B)\cap{\rm Ker}(G)
by Lemmas
4.3
(3) and
4.6
(2), while
{\rm Im}({\mathscr{D}}^{b}(j))={\mathscr{D}}^{b}(B)\cap{\rm Im}(H)
by Lemma
4.3
(3) and Corollary
4.5
(2).
Thus
\big({\rm Im}({\mathscr{D}}^{b}(i)),{\rm Im}({\mathscr{D}}^{b}(j))\big)
is a semi-orthogonal decomposition of
{\mathscr{D}}^{b}(B)
. This implies that
(\mathscr{E},\mathcal{Y})
is a derived decomposition of
B\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
if and only if
\mathscr{E}
is an abelian subcategory of
B\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
.
In other words,
(2)
and
(4)
are equivalent.

\square

Combining Corollary
1.3
with
[
13
, Corollary 1.3]
, we have the following result where our new contribution is
about the bound of projective dimension.

Corollary 4.19
.

If
{{}_{A}}T
is a homological tilting module, then there exists a homological ring epimorphism
\lambda:B\to C
such that

{\rm proj.dim}({{}_{B}}C)\leq 1
and
\lambda_{*}
induces an equivalence from
C\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
to
\mathscr{E}
.

Proof of Corollary
1.4
. By Lemma
4.6
(2), up to triangle equivalence, we have the identifications of categories:

\mathscr{D}^{b}(\mathscr{E})=\{X^{\bullet}\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathscr{E})\mid H^{i}(X^{\bullet})=0,|i|>>0\}
,
\mathscr{D}^{+}(\mathscr{E})=\{X^{\bullet}\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathscr{E})\mid H^{i}(X^{\bullet})=0,i<<0\}
and
\mathscr{D}^{-}(\mathscr{E})=\{X^{\bullet}\in{\mathscr{D}}(\mathscr{E})\mid H^{i}(X^{\bullet})=0,i>>0\}
. Since
{\rm proj.dim}(_{A}T)\leq n
and
{\rm proj.dim}(T_{B})\leq n
,
the functors
G
and
H
in Theorem
1.2
restrict to functors at
\mathscr{D}^{*}
-level for
*\in\{b,+,-\}
. So we get the half recollement

\textstyle{\mathscr{D}^{*}(\mathscr{E})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\mathscr{D}^{*}(B)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{G}
\textstyle{\mathscr{D}^{*}(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{H}

To show Corollary
1.4
, it suffices to show that the left adjoint
j_{!}:{\mathscr{D}}(A)\to{\mathscr{D}}(B)
of
G:{\mathscr{D}}(B)\to{\mathscr{D}}(A)
restricts to a functor
\mathscr{D}^{*}(A)\to\mathscr{D}^{*}(B)
whenever
A
is left coherent.

Since
A
is left coherent, the direct products of projective
A^{\rm op}
-modules are flat. As
(-)^{\vee}:A^{\rm op}\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}\to A\mbox{{\rm-Mod}}
sends flat modules to injective modules,
{\rm Hom}_{A}(P^{\bullet},A)^{\vee}
is a bounded complex of injective
A
-modules. By Lemma
4.15
(1) and the proof of Theorem
1.2
(see
({\dagger}))
, there are
isomorphisms

(H^{-m}(j_{!}(X)))^{\vee}\simeq{\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{D}}(A)}(X,G(B^{\vee})[m])\simeq{\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{D}}(A)}(X,{\rm Hom}_{A}(P^{\bullet},A)^{\vee}[m])\simeq{\rm Hom}_{{\mathscr{K}}(A)}(X,{\rm Hom}_{A}(P^{\bullet},A)^{\vee}[m])

for any
X\in{\mathscr{D}}(A)
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Note that the functor
(-)^{\vee}
sends nonzero modules to nonzero modules. Thus
X\in\mathscr{D}^{*}(A)
if and only if
j_{!}(X)\in\mathscr{D}^{*}(B)
. Further, the right (left) adjoint functor from
{\mathscr{D}}(B)
to
{\mathscr{D}}(\mathscr{E})
of
D(i)
restricts to
\mathscr{D}^{*}
-level follows from the first (second) triangle in Definition
2.3
(4).
\square

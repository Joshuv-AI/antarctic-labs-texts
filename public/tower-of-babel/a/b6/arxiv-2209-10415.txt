Prime geodesic theorem and closed geodesics for large genus
Yunhui Wu and Yuhao Xue

Abstract
. Let ℳ g \mathcal{M}_{g} be the moduli space of hyperbolic surfaces of genus g g endowed with the Weil-Petersson metric. In this paper, we show that for any ϵ > 0 \epsilon>0 , as g → ∞ g\to\infty , for a generic surface in ℳ g \mathcal{M}_{g} , the error term in the Prime Geodesic Theorem is bounded from above by g ⋅ t 3 4 + ϵ g\cdot t^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon} , up to a uniform constant multiplication. The expected value of the error term in the Prime Geodesic Theorem over ℳ g \mathcal{M}_{g} is also studied. As an application, we show that as g → ∞ g\to\infty , on a generic hyperbolic surface in ℳ g \mathcal{M}_{g} most closed geodesics of length significantly less than g \sqrt{g} are simple and non-separating, and most closed geodesics of length significantly greater than g \sqrt{g} are not simple, which confirms a conjecture of Lipnowski-Wright. A novel effective upper bound for intersection numbers on ℳ g , n \mathcal{M}_{g,n} is also established, when certain indices are large compared to g + n \sqrt{g+n} .



1.
Introduction

Let
X_{g}
be a closed hyperbolic surface of genus
g

(g\geq 2)
. The spectrum of Laplacian operator on
X_{g}
is a discrete closed subset in
\mathbb{R}^{\geq 0}
and consists of eigenvalues with finite multiplicity. We enumerate them, counted with multiplicity, in the following increasing order

0=\lambda_{0}(X_{g})<\lambda_{1}(X_{g})\leq\lambda_{2}(X_{g})\leq\cdots\to\infty.

For any
t>1
, we denote by
\pi_{X_{g}}(t)
the number of oriented primitive closed geodesics of length
\leq\ln t
. A remarkable application of Selberg’s trace formula
[
Sel56
]
is the Prime Geodesic Theorem (PGT) (see
e.g.
[
Hub61
,
Hej76
,
Ran77
]
) which states

(1)

\pi_{X_{g}}(t)=\mathop{\rm Li}(t)+\sum\limits_{\frac{3}{4}<s_{j}<1}\mathop{\rm Li}(t^{s_{j}})+\mathrm{O}_{X_{g}}\left(\frac{t^{\frac{3}{4}}}{\ln t}\right)

where
\mathop{\rm Li}(t)=\int_{2}^{t}\frac{dx}{\ln x}\sim\frac{t}{\ln t}\ \text{as}\ t\to\infty
,
s_{j}=\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-\lambda_{j}(X_{g})}
and the implied constant depends on the geometry of
X_{g}
. As an analog of the Riemann hypothesis for closed hyperbolic surfaces, it is a well-known conjecture that
the
\frac{3}{4}
in (
1
) can be replaced by
\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon
(see
e.g.
[
Ber16
, Page 190]
). Rewrite (
1
) as

(2)

\pi_{X_{g}}(t)=\mathop{\rm Li}(t)+\mathrm{Er}_{X_{g}}(t).

So the error term
\mathrm{Er}_{X_{g}}(t)
depends on the geometry and first non-zero eigenvalue of
X_{g}
. There are many works on the study of the error term
\mathrm{Er}_{X_{g}}(t)
, especially for special arithmetic surfaces. See
[
Sar80
,
Iwa84
,
LRS95
,
Cai02
,
SY13
]
and the references therein.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{g}
be the moduli space of closed hyperbolic surfaces of genus
g
endowed with the Weil-Petersson metric. Based on her celebrated thesis works
[
Mir07a
,
Mir07b
]
, Mirzakhani in
[
Mir13
]
initiated the study of random hyperbolic surfaces for large genus. In this paper, our aim is to study the average behavior of the error terms in (
1
) and (
2
) on

for large

. More precisely, let
\mathop{\rm Prob}\nolimits_{\rm WP}^{g}
and
\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}
be the probability measure and expectation on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
respectively given by the Weil-Petersson metric. We apply recent results of

]

]

in (
2

up to a universal constant multiplication. More precisely,

Theorem 1
.

There exists a universal constant

\epsilon>0
and all

which may depend on

\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\mathop{\rm Prob}\nolimits_{\rm WP}^{g}\left(X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g};\ \left|\pi_{X_{g}}(t)-\mathop{\rm Li}(t)\right|\leq c\cdot g\cdot t^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon}\right)=1.

Conjecture
.

Theorem

\frac{1}{2}

We also study the expected value
\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}\left[\left|\pi_{X_{g}}(t)-\mathop{\rm Li}(t)\right|\right]
for large genus. In light of (
1

\sum\limits_{\frac{3}{4}<s_{j}<1}\mathop{\rm Li}(t^{s_{j}})+\mathrm{O}_{X_{g}}\left(\frac{t^{\frac{3}{4}}}{\ln t}\right)

in (

Theorem 2

For any
X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
and all

\displaystyle\pi_{X_{g}}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle+

With the help of our previous work

\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}\left[\sum\limits_{0<\lambda_{j}(X_{g})\leq\frac{1}{4}}\mathop{\rm Li}(t^{s_{j}})\right]

\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}\left[\pi_{X_{g}}(t)\right]

Theorem 3
.

Let
t(g)
be a function such that

. Then for any

\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}\left[\ \left|1-\frac{\pi_{X_{g}}(t(g))}{\mathop{\rm Li}(t(g))}\right|\ \right]=O_{\epsilon}(g^{-1+\epsilon})

where the implied constant only depends on
\epsilon
.

For any
L>0
, denote by
N(X_{g},L)
the number of unoriented primitive closed geodesics in
X_{g}
of length
\leq L
. So
N(X_{g},L)=\frac{\pi_{X_{g}}(e^{L})}{2}.
As introduced above, by the Prime Geodesic Theorem

N(X_{g},L)\sim\frac{1}{2}\frac{e^{L}}{L}\ \textit{as $L\to\infty$}.

Denote by
N^{s}(X_{g},L)
,
N^{s}_{nsep}(X_{g},L)
,
N^{s}_{sep}(X_{g},L)
and
N^{ns}(X_{g},L)
the number of unoriented primitive
simple
,
simple and non-separating
,
simple and separating
, and
non-simple
closed geodesics in
X_{g}
of length
\leq L
respectively. It is clear that
N(X_{g},L)=N^{s}_{nsep}(X_{g},L)+N^{s}_{sep}(X_{g},L)+N^{ns}(X_{g},L)
and
N^{s}(X_{g},L)=N^{s}_{nsep}(X_{g},L)+N^{s}_{sep}(X_{g},L)
. A celebrated result of Mirzakhani
[
Mir08
]
states that

N^{s}(X_{g},L)\sim C(X_{g})\cdot L^{6g-6}\ \textit{as $L\to\infty$}

where
C(X_{g})
is a constant depending on
X_{g}
, and as a function on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
,
C:\mathcal{M}_{g}\to\mathbb{R}
is proper.

Based on Mirzakhani’s Integration Formula
[
Mir07a
]
, it is not hard to see that as
g\to\infty
, the expected values of
N^{s}(X_{g},L)
and
N^{s}_{nsep}(X_{g},L)
over
\mathcal{M}_{g}
satisfy that

\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}[N^{s}(X_{g},L)]\sim\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}[N^{s}_{nsep}(X_{g},L)]\sim\frac{1}{2}\frac{e^{L}}{L}\ \textit{as $L\to\infty$}.

It is quite mysterious that both
N(X_{g},L)
and
\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}[N^{s}_{nsep}(X_{g},L)]
have the same asymptotic leading term
\frac{1}{2}\frac{e^{L}}{L}
as
L\to\infty
. This suggests that for suitable
L=L(g)
depending on
g
, as
g\to\infty
, in the set of closed geodesics of length
\leq L
in a generic surface in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
, simple and non-separating ones are dominant. Meanwhile, as introduced above,
N^{s}(X_{g},L)\sim C(X_{g})\cdot L^{6g-6}
. This also seems to suggest that for suitable
L=L(g)
depending on
g
, as
g\to\infty
, in the set of closed geodesics of length
\geq L
in a generic surface in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
, non-simple ones are major part. Actually it was conjectured in
[
LW24
, Conjecture
1.2
]
that

Conjecture

(Lipnowski-Wright)
.

As
g\to\infty
, on most closed hyperbolic surfaces in
\mathcal{M}_{g}
most geodesics of length significantly less than
\sqrt{g}
are simple and non-separating, and most geodesics of length significantly greater than
\sqrt{g}
are not simple.

It is known by
[
Mir13
, Theorem 4.4]
that for any
\epsilon>0
,

\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\mathop{\rm Prob}\nolimits_{\rm WP}^{g}\left(X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g};\ N_{sep}^{s}(X_{g},(2-\epsilon)\ln g)\geq 1\right)=0.

And it is also known by
[
NWX23
, Theorem 4]
that for any
\epsilon>0
,

\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\mathop{\rm Prob}\nolimits_{\rm WP}^{g}\left(X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g};\ N^{ns}(X_{g},(1-\epsilon)\ln g)\geq 1\right)=0.

The two limits above imply that if
L(g)\leq(1-\epsilon_{0})\ln g
for some
\epsilon_{0}>0
, then

\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\mathop{\rm Prob}\nolimits_{\rm WP}^{g}\left(X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g};\ N(X_{g},L(g))=N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L(g))\right)=1.

This in particular tells that the first part of the conjecture of Lipnowski-Wright above is true when the length is less than
(1-\epsilon_{0})\ln g
. In this paper, we completely solve the conjecture above. More precisely, we prove

Theorem 4
.

The following two probabilities hold:

(1)

if
L(g)
satisfies that for some fixed
\epsilon_{0}>0
,

L(g)\geq(1-\epsilon_{0})\ln g\text{ and }\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\frac{L^{2}(g)}{g}=0,

then there exists a function
\delta(g)>0
satisfying
\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\delta(g)=0
such that

\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\mathop{\rm Prob}\nolimits_{\rm WP}^{g}\left(X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g};\ \left|1-\frac{N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L(g))}{N(X_{g},L(g))}\right|<\delta(g)\right)=1.

(2)

If
L(g)
satisfies

\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\frac{L^{2}(g)}{g}=\infty,

then

\lim\limits_{g\to\infty}\mathop{\rm Prob}\nolimits_{\rm WP}^{g}\left(X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g};\ \left|1-\frac{N^{ns}(X_{g},L(g))}{N(X_{g},L(g))}\right|<\frac{g}{L(g)^{2}}\right)=1.

In the proof of Part

of Theorem

, we establish a new bound on the Weil-Petersson volume

of moduli space of bordered hyperbolic surfaces with geodesic boundary components of length

, which relies on a novel bound on intersection numbers in this paper. Recall that on

the compactified moduli space of surfaces of genus

\left[\tau_{d_{1}}\cdots\tau_{d_{n}}\right]_{g,n}=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n}2^{2d_{i}}(2d_{i}+1)!!}{(3g-3+n-|d|)!}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}}\psi_{1}^{d_{1}}\cdots\psi_{n}^{d_{n}}\omega^{3g-3+n-|d|}.

In particular,
\left[\tau_{0}\cdots\tau_{0}\right]_{g,n}=\mathop{\rm Vol}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n})
, which is also denoted by

. There are deep connections between intersection numbers and Weil-Petersson volumes, especially for their asymptotics. Actually Mirzakhani

]

]

\frac{\left[\tau_{d_{1}}\cdots\tau_{d_{n}}\right]_{g,n}}{V_{g,n}}\sim 1\text{ \ as $g\to\infty$}.

Preliminaries

In this section, we include certain necessary backgrounds and tools used in this paper.

The Weil-Petersson metric

We denote
S_{g,n}
to be an oriented topological surface with
g
genus and
n
punctures or boundaries. Require
2g+n\geq 3
so that
S_{g,n}
admits hyperbolic metrics. Let
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
be the Teichmüller space of surfaces with
g
genus and
n
punctures. The moduli space of Riemann surfaces is
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}=\mathcal{T}_{g,n}/\mathop{\rm Mod}_{g,n}
where
\mathop{\rm Mod}_{g,n}
is the mapping class group of
S_{g,n}
which fixes the order of punctures. If
n=0
, write
\mathcal{T}_{g}=\mathcal{T}_{g,0}
and
\mathcal{M}_{g}=\mathcal{M}_{g,0}
for simplicity. Given
L=(L_{1},\cdots,L_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{\geq 0}
, the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(L)
consists of bordered hyperbolic surfaces with geodesic boundary components of length
L_{1},\cdots,L_{n}
. Note that a geodesic boundary component of length
0
is a puncture, so
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(0,\cdots,0)=\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
. The weighted moduli space is
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(L)=\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(L)/\mathop{\rm Mod}_{g,n}
.

Fix a pants decomposition
\{\alpha_{i}\}_{i=1}^{3g-3+n}
of
S_{g,n}
, the Fenchel-Nielsen coordinates
X\mapsto(\ell_{\alpha_{i}}(X),\tau_{\alpha_{i}}(X))_{i=1}^{3g-3+n}
are global coordinates for the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(L)
. Where
\ell_{\alpha_{i}}(X)
is the length of
\alpha_{i}
on
X
and
\tau_{\alpha_{i}}(X)
is the twist along
\alpha_{i}
(measured by length). Wolpert
[
Wol82
]
showed that the Weil-Petersson
symplectic form of
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(L)
has a good expression under Fenchel-Nielsen coordinates:

Theorem 7

(Wolpert)
.

The Weil-Petersson symplectic form
\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}
on
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(L)
is given by

\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}=\sum_{i=1}^{3g-3+n}d\ell_{\alpha_{i}}\wedge d\tau_{\alpha_{i}}.

The Weil-Petersson volume form is

d\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{\mathrm{WP}}=\tfrac{1}{(3g-3+n)!}\underbrace{\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega_{\mathrm{WP}}}_{3g-3+n\ \text{copies}}.

This is a mapping class group invariant measure on
\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(L)
, and hence a measure on
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(L)
, which still denoted by
d\mathop{\rm Vol}_{\mathrm{WP}}
. The total volume of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(L)
under the Weil-Petersson metric is finite and denoted by
V_{g,n}(L)
. This also implies a probability measure
\mathop{\rm Prob}\nolimits_{\rm WP}^{g}
on
\mathcal{M}_{g}
:

\mathop{\rm Prob}\nolimits_{\rm WP}^{g}(\mathcal{A}):=\frac{1}{V_{g}}\int_{\mathcal{M}_{g}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{A}}dX

where
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathcal{M}_{g}
is a Borel subset,
\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathcal{M}_{g}\to\{0,1\}
is its characteristic function, and
dX
is short for
d\mathop{\rm Vol}_{\mathrm{WP}}(X)
. One may see
[
Mir13
,
GPY11
,
MP19
,
NWX23
,

Mirzakhani’s integration formula

Now we introduce an integration formula in
[
Mir07a
,
Mir13
]
, which is an essential tool in the study of random hyperbolic surfaces of Weil-Petersson model.

Given any non-peripheral closed curve
\gamma
on topological surface
S_{g,n}
and
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g,n}
, denote
\ell(\gamma)=\ell_{\gamma}(X)
to be the hyperbolic length of the unique closed geodesic in
the homotopy class
\gamma
on
X
. Let
\Gamma=(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{k})
be an ordered k-tuple where the
\gamma_{i}
’s are distinct disjoint homotopy classes of nontrivial, non-peripheral, unoriented simple closed curves on
S_{g,n}
. Consider the orbit containing
\Gamma
under
\mathop{\rm Mod}_{g,n}
-action

\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}=\{(h\cdot\gamma_{1},\cdots,h\cdot\gamma_{k});\ h\in\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits_{g,n}\}.

Given a function
F:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define a function on
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}

\displaystyle F^{\Gamma}:\mathcal{M}_{g,n}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\mapsto

\displaystyle\sum_{(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{k})\in\mathcal{O}_{\Gamma}}F(\ell_{\alpha_{1}}(X),\cdots,\ell_{\alpha_{k}}(X)).

Assume
S_{g,n}-\cup_{j=1}^{k}\gamma_{j}=\cup_{i=1}^{s}S_{g_{i},n_{i}}
. For any
x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{k}
, we consider the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(S_{g,n}(\Gamma);\ell_{\Gamma}=x)
of the hyperbolic surfaces (possibly disconnected) homeomorphic to
S_{g,n}-\cup_{j=1}^{k}\gamma_{j}
with
\ell_{\gamma_{i}^{1}}=\ell_{\gamma_{i}^{2}}=x_{i}
for every
i=1,\cdots,k
, where
\gamma_{i}^{1}
and
\gamma_{i}^{2}
are two boundary components of
S_{g,n}-\cup_{j=1}^{k}\gamma_{j}
given by cutting along
\gamma_{i}
. Consider the Weil-Petersson volume

V_{g,n}(\Gamma,x)=\mathop{\rm Vol}\nolimits_{\mathrm{WP}}\left(\mathcal{M}(S_{g,n}(\Gamma);\ell_{\Gamma}=x)\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}V_{g_{i},n_{i}}(x^{(i)})

where
x^{(i)}
is the list of those coordinates
x_{j}
of
x
such that
\gamma_{j}
is a boundary component of
S_{g_{i},n_{i}}
. Using Theorem
7
, Mirzakhani proved the following integration
formula. See
[
Mir07a
, Theorem 7.1]
,
[
Mir13
, Theorem 2.2]
,
[
MP19
, Theorem 2.2]
,
[
Wri20
, Theorem 4.1]
for different versions.

Theorem 8

(Mirzakhani)
.

For any
\Gamma=(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{k})
, the integral of
F^{\Gamma}
over
M_{g,n}
with respect to Weil-Petersson metric is given by

\int_{\mathcal{M}_{g,n}}F^{\Gamma}dX=C_{\Gamma}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{k}}F(x_{1},\cdots,x_{k})V_{g,n}(\Gamma,x)x_{1}\cdots x_{k}dx_{1}\cdots dx_{k}

where the constant
C_{\Gamma}\in(0,1]
only depend on
\Gamma
. Moreover,
C_{\Gamma}=\frac{1}{2^{k}}
if
g>2
and
S_{g,n}-\cup_{j=1}^{k}\gamma_{j}\cong S_{g-k,n+2k}
.

The Weil-Petersson volume

We denote
V_{g,n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
to be the Weil-Petersson volume of
\mathcal{M}_{g,n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
and
V_{g,n}=V_{g,n}(0,\cdots,0)
. Recall that the intersection numbers
\left[\tau_{d_{1}}\cdots\tau_{d_{n}}\right]_{g,n}
are integrals of first Chern classes of tautological line bundles and Weil-Petersson symplectic form over
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}
. Mirzakhani
[
Mir07b
]
showed that this volume can be expressed by intersection numbers:

Theorem 9
.

For
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\geq 0
,

V_{g,n}(2x_{1},\cdots,2x_{n})=\sum_{|d|\leq 3g-3+n}\left[\tau_{d_{1}}\cdots\tau_{d_{n}}\right]_{g,n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}^{2d_{i}}}{(2d_{i}+1)!}.

Mirzakhani gave some estimates about
V_{g,n}
:

Theorem 10
.

(1)

[
Mir13
, Lemma 3.2]

For any
g,n\geq 0
,

b_{0}<\frac{(2g-2+n)V_{g,n}}{V_{g,n+1}}<b_{1}

where
b_{0}=\frac{10-\pi^{2}}{120}\approx 0.001
and
b_{1}=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{l\pi^{2l-2}}{(2l+1)!}\approx 0.401
.

(2)

[
Mir13
, Theorem 3.5]

\frac{(2g-2+n)V_{g,n}}{V_{g,n+1}}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}+O\left(\frac{1}{g}\right),

\frac{V_{g,n}}{V_{g-1,n+2}}=1+O\left(\frac{1}{g}\right).

Where the implied constants are related to
n
and independent of
g
.

Theorem 11

(
[
Mir13
, Corollary 3.7]
)
.

\sum_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}g_{1}+g_{2}=g\\
1\leq g_{1}\leq g_{2}\end{array}}\frac{V_{g_{1},1}V_{g_{2},1}}{V_{g}}\asymp\frac{1}{g}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \sum_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}g_{1}+g_{2}=g-1\\
1\leq g_{1}\leq g_{2}\end{array}}\frac{V_{g_{1},2}V_{g_{2},2}}{V_{g}}\asymp\frac{1}{g^{2}}.

The implied constants are independent of
g
.

The asymptotic behavior of
V_{g,n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
was first studied in
[
MP19
]
, also see
[
NWX23
]
and refined results in
[
AM22
]
.

Theorem 12
.

There exists a constant
c(n)>0
independent of
g
and
x_{i}
’s such that

\left(1-c(n)\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}}{g}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sinh(x_{i}/2)}{x_{i}/2}\leq\frac{V_{g,n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})}{V_{g,n}}\leq\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\sinh(x_{i}/2)}{x_{i}/2}.

Counting closed geodesics

Here we introduce some counting results. We want some upper bounds for the number of closed geodesics of length
\leq L
. On a hyperbolic surface, a closed geodesic is called
primitive
if it is not an iterate of any other closed geodesic at least twice. First, the collar lemma (see e.g.
[
Bus10
, Theorem 4.1.6]
) states that:

Theorem 13
.

In a hyperbolic surface
X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
, there are at most
3g-3
unoriented primitive closed geodesics of length
\leq 2\mathop{\rm arcsinh}1\approx 1.7627
. Moreover, they are simple and disjoint with each other.

Buser showed that:

Theorem 14

(
[
Bus10
, Theorem 6.6.4]
)
.

Let
X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
and
L>0
. In
X_{g}
there are at most
(g-1)e^{L+6}
oriented closed geodesics of length
\leq L
which are not iterates of closed geodesics of length
\leq 2\mathop{\rm arcsinh}1
.

For a hyperbolic surface
X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
, denote
\mathcal{P}(X_{g})
to be the set of all oriented primitive closed geodesics in
X_{g}
. Then combine Theorem
13
and
14
we have:

Corollary 15
.

For any
X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
and
L>0
,

\#\left\{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(X_{g});\ \ell(\gamma)\leq L\right\}\leq(g-1)e^{L+7}.

For compact hyperbolic surfaces with geodesic boundaries, the following counting result in our previous work
[
WX22
]
will be applied later.

Theorem 16

(
[
WX22
, Theorem 4]
)
.

For any
\epsilon_{1}>0
and
m=2g-2+n\geq 1
, there exists a constant
c(\epsilon_{1},m)>0
only depending on
m
and
\epsilon_{1}
such that for all
L>0
and any compact hyperbolic surface
X
of genus
g
with
n
boundary simple closed geodesics, we have

\#_{f}(X,L)\leq c(\epsilon_{1},m)\cdot e^{L-\frac{1-\epsilon_{1}}{2}\ell(\partial X)}.

Where
\#_{f}(X,L)
is the number of filling closed geodesics in
X
of length
\leq L
and
\ell(\partial X)
is the total length of the boundary closed geodesics of
X
.

Actually Theorem
16
also holds for filling multi-curves.

Definition 17
.

For
2g+n\geq 3
, let
\Gamma=(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{k})
be an ordered
k
-tuple where
\gamma_{i}
’s are homotopy classes of
nontrivial non-peripheral unoriented primitive closed curves on the topological surface
S_{g,n}
. We say
\Gamma
is
filling
in

if each component of the complement
S_{g,n}\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{k}\gamma_{i}
is homeomorphic to a disk or a punctured disk which is homotopic to a cusp of
S_{g,n}
.

In particular, a filling
1
-tuple is a filling closed curve in
S_{g,n}
.

On a hyperbolic surface
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(L_{1},\cdots,L_{n})
, we define the length of a
k
-tuple
\Gamma=(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{k})
to be the total length of
\gamma_{i}
’s, that is,

\ell_{\Gamma}(X):=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\ell_{\gamma_{i}}(X).

Define the counting function
N_{k}^{\text{fill}}(X,L)
for
L\geq 0
and
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(L_{1},\cdots,L_{n})
to be

N_{k}^{\text{fill}}(X,L):=\#\left\{\Gamma=(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{k});\ \begin{aligned} &\Gamma\ \text{is a filling}\ k\text{-tuple in}\ X\\
&\text{and}\ \ell_{\Gamma}(X)\leq L\end{aligned}\right\}.

Theorem 18
.

For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
,
0<\epsilon<\frac{1}{2}
and
m=2g-2+n\geq 1
, there exists
a constant
c(k,\epsilon,m)>0
only depending on
k,\epsilon
and
m
such that for all
L>0
and any compact hyperbolic surface
X
of genus
g
with
n
boundary simple closed geodesics, the following holds:

N_{k}^{\text{fill}}(X,L)\leq c(k,\epsilon,m)\cdot(1+L)^{k-1}e^{L-\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}\ell(\partial X)}.

Where
\ell(\partial X)
is the total length of the boundary closed geodesics of
X
.

is actually the same as the proof of Theorem

given in

.

Small eigenvalues

For
X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
, the spectrum of the Laplacian operator consists of discrete eigenvalues

0=\lambda_{0}(X_{g})<\lambda_{1}(X_{g})\leq\lambda_{2}(X_{g})\leq\cdots\to\infty.

Spectrum of hyperbolic surfaces are widely studied in the past century, see
[
Cha84
,
Bus10
,
Ber16
]
for example. Eigenvalues that are
\leq\frac{1}{4}
are called small eigenvalues. Buser
[
Bus77
]
showed that
\lambda_{4g-2}(X_{g})>\frac{1}{4}
and constructed
\mathcal{X}_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
which admits arbitrary small
\lambda_{2g-3}(\mathcal{X}_{g})
. Later, Otal and Rosas
[
OR09
]
proved that
\lambda_{2g-2}(X_{g})>\frac{1}{4}
. Here
2g-2
is sharp. One may also see Ballmann-Matthiesen-Mondal
[
BMM16
,
BMM17
]
for more general statements on
\lambda_{2g-2}(X_{g})
.

Theorem 19

(
[
OR09
]
)
.

There are at most
2g-2
eigenvalues in
[0,\frac{1}{4}]
for any
X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
.

Selberg’s trace formula

We introduce the Selberg’s trace formula
[
Sel56
]
of closed hyperbolic surfaces here, which is a useful tool when considering the spectrum of Laplacian and length spectrum. One may see
[
Bus10
, Theorem 9.5.3]
or
[
Ber16
, Theorem 5.6]
for the version we describe here.

Given a smooth function with compact support
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, its Fourier transform is defined as

\widehat{\varphi}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)e^{-\mathbf{i}tz}dt

for any
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

For
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, denote

(3)

r_{k}(X_{g})=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\lambda_{k}(X_{g})-\frac{1}{4}}\ &\ \text{if}\ \lambda_{k}(X_{g})>\frac{1}{4}\\
\mathbf{i}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-\lambda_{k}(X_{g})}\ &\ \text{if}\ \lambda_{k}(X_{g})\leq\frac{1}{4}\end{cases}

where
\lambda_{k}(X_{g})
is the
k
-th eigenvalue of
X_{g}
. Denote
\mathcal{P}(X_{g})
to be the set of all oriented primitive closed geodesics on
X_{g}
. Then the remarkable trace formula of Selberg states:

Theorem 20

(Selberg’s trace formula)
.

Let
X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
be a closed hyperbolic surface of genus
g
. For any even function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\widehat{\varphi}(r_{k}(X_{g}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(g-1)\int_{\mathbb{R}}r\tanh(\pi r)\widehat{\varphi}(r)dr

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(X_{g})}\frac{\ell(\gamma)}{2\sinh\left(\frac{k\ell(\gamma)}{2}\right)}\varphi(k\ell(\gamma)).

And both sides of the formula are absolutely convergent.

3.
PGT for general hyperbolic surfaces

In this section we finish the proof of Theorem
2
. Actually we show

Theorem 21
.

For any
X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
and
t>2
,

\pi_{X_{g}}(t)=\mathop{\rm Li}(t)+\sum_{0<\lambda_{j}\leq\frac{1}{4}}\mathop{\rm Li}(t^{s_{j}})+O\left(g\frac{t^{\frac{5}{6}}}{\ln t}+\frac{t^{\frac{2}{3}}}{\ln t}\sum_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(X_{g}),\\
\ell(\gamma)<t^{-1/6}\end{array}}\ln\left(\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)t^{\frac{1}{6}}}\right)\right)

where
s_{j}=s_{j}(X_{g})=\frac{1}{2}+\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-\lambda_{j}(X_{g})}
,
\mathcal{P}(X_{g})
is the set of all oriented primitive closed geodesics in
X_{g}
, and the implied constant is universal.

Theorem
2
is a direct consequence of Theorem
21
above.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

By Theorem
13
we know that there exist at most
(3g-3)
mutually disjoint primitive closed geodesics in
X_{g}
of length
\leq 2^{-1/6}
. So we have

\sum_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(X_{g}),\\
\ell(\gamma)<t^{-\frac{1}{6}}\end{array}}\ln\left(\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)t^{\frac{1}{6}}}\right)\prec g\cdot\max\left\{0,\ln\left(\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm sys}(X_{g})t^{\frac{1}{6}}}\right)\right\}

which together with Theorem
21
implies the conclusion.
∎

The error term in Theorem
21
is explicitly given in terms of the geometry of
X_{g}
. In order to achieve it, we will choose suitable test functions for Selberg’s trace formula such that we are able to distinguish all the geometric terms as shown in Theorem
21
. Our method is motivated by the proof of
[
BP22
, Corollary 1.4]
by Bourque and Petri.

Before showing Theorem
21
, we first make the following application.

Corollary 22
.

For any
X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g}
, if
L
satisfies
e^{e^{\frac{1}{3}L}}>\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm sys}(X_{g})}
, then

\#\left\{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(X_{g});\ \ell(\gamma)\leq L\right\}\prec g\frac{e^{L}}{L}.

Proof.

By Theorem
21
and
19
,

\#\left\{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(X_{g});\ \ell(\gamma)\leq L\right\}\prec g\frac{e^{L}}{L}+\frac{e^{\frac{2}{3}L}}{L}\sum_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(X_{g}),\\
\ell(\gamma)<e^{-L/6}\end{array}}\ln\left(\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)e^{\frac{1}{6}L}}\right).

When
e^{e^{\frac{1}{3}L}}>\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm sys}(X_{g})}
, we have

\sum_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(X_{g}),\\
\ell(\gamma)<e^{-L/6}\end{array}}\ln\left(\frac{1}{\ell(\gamma)e^{\frac{1}{6}L}}\right)\prec\max\left\{0,g\cdot\ln\left(\frac{1}{\mathop{\rm sys}(X_{g})}\right)\right\}\leq ge^{\frac{1}{3}L}.

Then the conclusion follows by the two inequalities above.
∎

Now we return to prove Theorem
21
.

For bounded
t
, the theorem holds naturally. So we only consider sufficiently large
t
. Let
\eta(x)\geq 0
be a non-negative smooth even function with

f_{\epsilon}(x)=(\eta_{\epsilon}*\eta_{\epsilon})(x),

.

For any

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{\tiny\begin{array}[]{c}\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(X_{g}),\\

. By Theorem

\displaystyle(2g-3)\mathop{\rm Li}(t^{\frac{1}{2}})+\sum_{j=1}^{2g-3}\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}\frac{t^{s}}{s}c(\epsilon)\frac{1}{j}g^{3-4s+\epsilon}ds

\ell_{\gamma_{1}}(X)+\ell_{\gamma_{2}}(X)\leq T
, and hence
Z(X_{g},L)\leq N^{ns,2}(X_{g},2L)
.
∎

Now we prove Theorem
59
.

Proof of Theorem
59
.

Now assume

\lim_{g\rightarrow\infty}L(g)=\infty\ \ \text{and}\ \ \limsup_{g\to\infty}\frac{L(g)}{\ln g}\leq C-1<\infty

for some constant
C>1
. So
L\leq C\ln g
for large enough
g
.

Then it follows by Lemma
48
,
61
,
62
,
63
and Equation (
192
) that for any
\epsilon_{1}>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}[N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L)^{2}]-\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}[N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L)]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}[N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L)]^{2}}

\displaystyle\prec_{\epsilon_{1}}

\displaystyle\frac{\mathop{\rm Li}(e^{L})+\frac{1}{g}e^{2L}+\frac{1}{g^{2}}\frac{e^{2L}}{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{g}e^{(2+\epsilon_{1})L}}{\mathop{\rm Li}(e^{L})^{2}}

\displaystyle\prec

\displaystyle Le^{-L}+\frac{1}{g}L^{2}e^{\epsilon_{1}L}

\displaystyle\prec_{C}

\displaystyle Le^{-L}+(\ln g)^{2}g^{\epsilon_{1}C-1}.

Now take
\epsilon_{1}=\frac{1}{2C}
. For any fixed
\epsilon>0
, by (
191
) as
g\to\infty
we have

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm Prob}\nolimits_{\rm WP}^{g}\left(X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g};\ N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L)\leq(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon)\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}[N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L)]\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm Prob}\nolimits_{\rm WP}^{g}\left(X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g};\ \left|1-\frac{N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L)}{\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}[N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L)]}\right|\geq\frac{1}{2}\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\epsilon^{2}}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}[N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L)^{2}]-\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}[N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L)]^{2}}{\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}[N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L)]^{2}}

\displaystyle\prec_{C}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\left(Le^{-L}+(\ln g)^{2}g^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right).

By Lemma
48
, we know that for
g
large enough,

\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}[N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L)]\geq\frac{1-\epsilon/2}{2}\mathop{\rm Li}(e^{L}).

Thus, we have that for large enough

,

\displaystyle\mathop{\rm Prob}\nolimits_{\rm WP}^{g}\left(X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g};\ N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L)>\frac{(1-\epsilon/2)^{2}}{2}\mathop{\rm Li}(e^{L})\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 1-\mathop{\rm Prob}\nolimits_{\rm WP}^{g}\left(X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g};\ N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L)\leq(1-\tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon)\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}[N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L)]\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-O_{C}\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\left(Le^{-L}+(\ln g)^{2}g^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right).

Then the conclusion follows by letting
g\to\infty
because
\frac{(1-\epsilon/2)^{2}}{2}\mathop{\rm Li}(e^{L})>\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}\mathop{\rm Li}(e^{L}).

∎

Proof of Theorem

Assume that

\lim_{g\rightarrow\infty}L(g)=\infty\ \ \text{and}\ \ \limsup_{g\to\infty}\frac{L(g)}{\ln g}\leq C-1<\infty

for some constant
C>1

for large enough
g
.

50
and Theorem

, for any
\epsilon_{1}>0
, when
g
is large enough we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}\left[\left|N(X_{g},L)-N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L)\right|\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\rm WP}^{g}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{[g/2]}N_{sep,k}^{s}(X_{g},L)+N^{ns}(X_{g},L)\right]

\displaystyle\prec_{C,\epsilon_{1}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{g}\mathop{\rm Li}(e^{L})+\frac{1}{g}e^{(1+\epsilon_{1})L}

\displaystyle\prec

\displaystyle\frac{1}{g}e^{(1+\epsilon_{1})L}.

Then by Markov’s inequality we have

\mathop{\rm Prob}\nolimits_{\rm WP}^{g}\left(X_{g}\in\mathcal{M}_{g};\ \left|N(X_{g},L)-N_{nsep}^{s}(X_{g},L)\right|\geq\frac{e^{(1+2\epsilon_{1})L}}{g}\right)\prec_{C,\epsilon_{1}}e^{-\epsilon_{1}L}

Appendix A
Counting filling
k
-tuples

In this Appendix, we give a brief sketch of the proof of Theorem
18
here. One may see
[
WX22
, Section 8]
for more details.

First, as a simple application of Corollary
15
we have

Lemma 64
.

For fixed
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
, there exists a constant
c_{k}>0
only depending on
k
such that for any hyperbolic surface
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(L_{1},\cdots,L_{n})
and
L>0
, we have

\#\left\{(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{k})\in\mathcal{P}(X)^{k};\ \sum_{i=1}^{k}\ell(\gamma_{i})\leq L\right\}\leq c_{k}\cdot(2g-2+n)^{k}(1+L)^{k-1}e^{L}

where
\mathcal{P}(X)
is the set of all oriented non-peripheral primitive closed geodesics in
X
.

Proof.

By doubling
X
along its boundaries, we get a closed hyperbolic surface
\overline{X}\in\mathcal{T}_{2g+n-1}
. Each non-peripheral primitive closed geodesic in
X
has two copies in
\overline{X}
. So by Corollary
15
, for any
T>0
we have

\displaystyle\#\{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(X);\ \ell(\gamma)\leq T\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\#\{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\overline{X});\ \ell(\gamma)\leq T\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2g-2+n)e^{T+7}.

Now we prove by induction on
k
. First the lemma holds for
k=1
by the above inequality. Now assume that the lemma holds for
k-1
. Then for
k\geq 2
, we get

\displaystyle\#\left\{(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{k})\in\mathcal{P}(X)^{k};\ \sum_{i=1}^{k}\ell(\gamma_{i})\leq L\right\}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{T=1}^{[L]+1}\#\left\{\gamma_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(X);\ T-1<\ell(\gamma_{1})\leq T\right\}

\displaystyle\times\#\left\{(\gamma_{2},\cdots,\gamma_{k})\in\mathcal{P}(X)^{k-1};\ \sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\ell(\gamma_{i})\leq L-T+1\right\}

\displaystyle\prec_{k}

\displaystyle\sum_{T=1}^{[L]+1}(2g-2+n)e^{T}\cdot(2g-2+n)^{k-1}(L-T+2)^{k-2}e^{L-T+1}

\displaystyle\prec

\displaystyle\sum_{T=1}^{[L]+1}(2g-2+n)^{k}(1+L)^{k-2}e^{L}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2g-2+n)^{k}(1+L)^{k-1}e^{L}

as desired.
∎

Similar to
[
WX22
, Theorem 38]
, we study counting filling
k
-tuples instead of filling geodesics. We prove

Theorem 65
.

There exists a universal constant
L_{0}>0
such that for any
0<\epsilon<1
,
m=2g-2+n\geq 1
,
\Delta\geq 0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\geq\Delta+L_{0}\cdot\frac{m\cdot n}{\epsilon}
, the following holds: for any hyperbolic surface
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
, one can always find a new hyperbolic surface
Y\in\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})
satisfying

(1)

y_{i}\leq x_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq n
;

(2)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}=\Delta
;

(3)

for any filling
k
-tuple
\Gamma=(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{k})
in
S_{g,n}
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\ell_{\gamma_{i}}(X)-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\ell_{\gamma_{i}}(Y)\geq\frac{1}{2}(1-\epsilon)\Delta.

Proof.

The proof is identical to the one of
[
WX22
, Theorem 38]
through replacing filling curves by filling
k
-tuples. We give a sketch about the proof here, and one may refer to
[
WX22
, Section 8]
for more details.

Consider a longest boundary component of
X
, denoted by
\alpha
. Let
w
be the width of the maximal embedded half-collar of
\alpha
in
X
, that is,

w=\sup\left\{d>0;\ \begin{aligned} &\{p\in X;\ \mathop{\rm dist}(p,\alpha)<d\}\\
&\text{is homeomorphic to a cylinder and}\\
&\text{does not intersect with}\ (\partial X\setminus\alpha)\end{aligned}\right\}.

Then the closed curve
\{p\in X;\ \mathop{\rm dist}(p,\alpha)=w\}
either touches another component of
\partial X
or has non-empty self-intersection. For both cases, it induces a pair of pants, denoted by
P
, in which
\alpha
is a boundary curve (see
[
WX22
, Page 382 Construction]
for details).

For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and filling
k
-tuple
\Gamma=(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{k})
of
X
, at least one of
\gamma_{i}
’s must intersect with the pair of pants
P
(actually this is the only place where we apply the filling property for
k
-tuples). Then as in
[
WX22
, Page 384]
we reduce the length of
\partial P\cap\partial X
and keep the part
X\setminus P
unchanged, the length of those
\gamma_{i}
’s which do not intersect with
P
are unchanged. For those
\gamma_{i}
’s which intersect with
P
, by
[
WX22
, Remark 41]
, length of
\gamma_{i}
reduce at least the multiplication of
\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}
and the reduction of
\partial P\cap\partial X
if
\ell(\alpha)
is large enough.

So if the initial total boundary length
\sum x_{i}
is large enough, we can always find a long boundary component and apply the above process to reduce the total boundary length. The process is workable unless all boundary components are bounded by some constant related to
\epsilon
and
m
. As argued above, similar as
[
WX22
, Proposition 40]
, for each time the total length of any filling
k
-tuple must reduce at least the multiplication of
\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}
and the reduction of the total boundary length. Then by using the same argument as in the proof of
[
WX22
, Theorem 38]
, one can get a desired new hyperbolic surface
Y
satisfying all the three required properties.
∎

Now applying Theorem
65
, we finish the proof of Theorem
18
.

Proof of Theorem
18
.

If
n=0
,
\ell(\partial X)=0
. Then the conclusion holds directly by Lemma
64
. Now assume
n\geq 1
and
X\in\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
.

Case 1
: the total boundary length
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}>L_{0}\frac{mn}{\epsilon}
. By Theorem
65
we may take
\Delta=\sum x_{i}-L_{0}\frac{mn}{\epsilon}
and then get a hyperbolic surface
Y\in\mathcal{T}_{g,n}(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n})
such that
\sum y_{i}=L_{0}\frac{mn}{\epsilon}
and for any filling
k
-tuple
\Gamma=(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{k})
in
S_{g,n}
we have

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\ell_{\gamma_{i}}(X)-\sum_{i=1}^{k}\ell_{\gamma_{i}}(Y)\geq\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}-L_{0}\frac{mn}{\epsilon}\right).

Thus

N_{k}^{\text{fill}}\left(X,L\right)\leq N_{k}^{\text{fill}}\left(Y,L-\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}\cdot\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}-L_{0}\frac{mn}{\epsilon}\right)\right)

which together with Lemma
64
and the fact that
n\leq m+2
implies that

\displaystyle N_{k}^{\text{fill}}\left(X,L\right)

\displaystyle\prec_{k}

\displaystyle m^{k}\left(1+L-\tfrac{1-\epsilon}{2}(\sum x_{i}-L_{0}\tfrac{mn}{\epsilon})\right)^{k-1}e^{L-\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}(\sum x_{i}-L_{0}\frac{mn}{\epsilon})}

\displaystyle\prec_{k,\epsilon,m}

\displaystyle(1+L)^{k-1}e^{L-\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}\sum x_{i}}.

Case 2
: the total boundary length
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\leq L_{0}\frac{mn}{\epsilon}
. Then by Lemma
64
directly we have

\displaystyle N_{k}^{\text{fill}}\left(X,L\right)

\displaystyle\prec_{k}

\displaystyle m^{k}(1+L)^{k-1}e^{L}

\displaystyle\prec_{k,\epsilon,m}

\displaystyle(1+L)^{k-1}e^{L-\frac{1-\epsilon}{2}\sum x_{i}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

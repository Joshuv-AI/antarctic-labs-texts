Abstract
: In this paper, we derive a soft photon theorem and a soft gluon theorem in the de Sitter spacetime from the Ward identity of the near cosmological horizon large gauge transformation. Taking the flat limit of the de Sitter spacetime, the soft theorems naturally recover the corresponding flat spacetime soft theorems.

Introduction

In the last few years there have been renewed interests on soft theorems. The new enthusiasm comes from a purely theoretical side which resides in the connection between soft theorems

Because any asymptotically flat spacetime shares the same boundary structure, Hawking, Perry, and Strominger (HPS) argued that black holes will carry soft hairs when acting asymptotic symmetry transformations on stationary black hole solutions

The newly discovered black hole soft degree of freedom provides a possible resolution of the black hole information paradox. \displaystyle\matrixelement{\mathrm{out}}{Q_{A}^{+}\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{S}Q_{A}^{-}}{\mathrm{in}}=0,

2
Soft photon theorem in de Sitter spacetime

In this section, we study the near horizon behavior of a Maxwell theory coupled with a background source in four dimensional dS spacetime, which is described by the action

\displaystyle S=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{1}{4}F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}+J_{\mu}A^{\mu}\right)\,,

(2)

and derive the soft theorem both in coordinates space and momentum space.

Figure 1:
Penrose diagram of de Sitter spacetime

We focus on the static patch in dS spacetime, see Fig.
1
for the Penrose diagram. The line element in static coordinates
(t,r,z,\bar{z})
is given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-f(r)\mathrm{d}t^{2}+f(r)^{-1}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\Omega(r)^{2}\gamma_{z\bar{z}}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}\bar{z}\,,

(3)

\displaystyle f(r)=\frac{r(2\ell-r)}{\ell^{2}}\,,\qquad\Omega=\ell-r\,,\qquad\gamma_{z\bar{z}}=\frac{2}{(1+z\bar{z})^{2}}\,,

(4)

where
\ell
is the dS radius which is related to the positive cosmological constant by
\Lambda=3\ell^{-2}
and we have shifted the cosmological horizon at
r=0
. To study the theory in the near horizon region, we introduce the retarded time coordinate
u=t-\frac{1}{2}\ell\log\left(\frac{r}{2\ell-r}\right)
. In the
(u,r,z,\bar{z})
coordinates, the line element becomes

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-f(r)\mathrm{d}u^{2}-2\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}u+\Omega(r)^{2}\gamma_{z\bar{z}}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}\bar{z}\,,

(5)

which covers the
\mathcal{H}^{-}
part of the horizon.

A similar analysis for
\mathcal{H}^{+}
could be generated by the time-reverse transformation of de Sitter spacetime near the bifurcation sphere
B
, and thus in the rest of this paper, we just show the detail on
\mathcal{H}^{-}
case. In the retarded coordinates all non-zero components of Christoffel are

\displaystyle\Gamma_{uu}^{u}

\displaystyle=-\Gamma_{ur}^{r}=-\frac{\Omega}{\ell^{2}}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{r}f

\displaystyle\Gamma_{z\bar{z}}^{u}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{z\bar{z}}\Omega=\gamma_{z\bar{z}}\Omega\partial_{r}\Omega

(6)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{uu}^{r}

\displaystyle=\frac{f\Omega}{\ell}=\frac{1}{2}f\partial_{r}f

\displaystyle\Gamma_{z\bar{z}}^{r}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{z\bar{z}}f\Omega=-\gamma_{z\bar{z}}\Omega\partial_{r}\Omega

(7)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{rz}^{z}

\displaystyle=\Gamma_{r\bar{z}}^{\bar{z}}=-\Omega^{-1}=\Omega^{-1}\partial_{r}\Omega

\displaystyle\Gamma_{zz}^{z}

\displaystyle=\left(\Gamma_{\bar{z}\bar{z}}^{\bar{z}}\right)^{*}=\gamma_{z\bar{z}}^{-1}\partial_{z}\gamma_{z\bar{z}}\,.

(8)

2.1
Gauge fixing, boundary condition and matching

In this section, we take the radial gauge and the near horizon fall-off conditions for the gauge fields and the conserved current are

\displaystyle A_{r}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle A_{u}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(r)

\displaystyle A_{z}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(1)

(9)

\displaystyle J_{r}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle J_{u}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(1)

\displaystyle J_{z}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(1).

It is easy to see that the only residual gauge transformation is
\delta A_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}\epsilon(z,\bar{z})
, where
\epsilon(z,\bar{z})
is an arbitrary differentiable function defined on the horizon. Because of the non-vanishing volume in phase space for large gauge symmetry
[
24
]
, this symmetry is non-trivial and leads to the infrared relation between near horizon physics and soft theorem.

The surface charge of the asymptotic symmetry could be constructed by the lower form through the generalized Noether’s second theorem
[
25
]
. We define the surface charge on the bifurcation sphere of the horizon

Q_{\epsilon}=\int_{B}\epsilon\star F,

(10)

which generates the large gauge transformation in the solution space. In the null infinity case
[
4
]
, the antipodal matching condition is imposed to preserve the conservation law between two null infinity, because the spatial infinity
i^{0}
is not a common boundary of
\mathcal{I}^{+}
and
\mathcal{I}^{-}
. But in this work, horizons live in a finite region, and the bifurcation sphere
B
is well defined in spacetime without any singularity nor ambiguity. One can consistently study the symmetries near the bifurcation sphere
[
26
]
. The conservation law of the gauge theories should hold automatically without any additional matching condition when
\mathcal{H}^{-}
deforms to
\mathcal{H}^{+}
as a Cauchy slice. However, we work in the retarded coordinates
(u,r,z,\bar{z})
or the advanced coordinates
(v,r,z,\bar{z})
on
\mathcal{H}^{+}
. Neither of them can really cover the bifurcation point
B
.
Thus, the charges computed in the two coordinates are not connected to each other automatically. To reveal the conservation law on the horizon, we introduce a horizon antipodal matching

F_{ru}^{0}(+\infty,0,z,\bar{z})=F_{rv}^{0}(-\infty,0,z,\bar{z})\,,

(11)

following the standard treatment at null infinity. As one can see from the solution space in the next section, the initial data of the gauge field is completely free. Thus, the matching condition can always be consistently imposed. Then the charge can be defined on the bifurcation sphere
B
as a limiting case

\displaystyle Q_{\epsilon}=

\displaystyle\lim_{u\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{B}\mathrm{d}^{2}z\ell^{2}\gamma_{z\bar{z}}\epsilon(z,\bar{z})F_{ru}(u,0,z,\bar{z})

(12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{v\rightarrow-\infty}\int_{B}\mathrm{d}^{2}z\ell^{2}\gamma_{z\bar{z}}\epsilon(z,\bar{z})F_{rv}(v,0,z,\bar{z})\,.

2.2
Solution space

Under the previously introduced gauge and near horizon fall-off conditions, we can arrange all equations of motion and constraints into several parts.

•

Continuity equation. We treat the matter current with no dynamics, as a consequence this current must be conserved.

\displaystyle\nabla^{\mu}J_{\mu}=0\,.

(13)

•

Hyper-surface equation

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu u}=0\,.

(14)

•

Standard equations

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu z}=J^{z}\,,\ \nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\bar{z}}=J^{\bar{z}}\,,

(15)

•

Supplementary equation

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu r}=J^{r}\,.

(16)

The previous gauge fixing does not eliminate all the gauge freedom, the remaining gauge symmetry also connects the equations of motion by the Noether identity

\displaystyle\nabla_{\nu}(J^{\nu}-\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu\nu})=0\,,

(17)

which indicates if the equations (
13
)–(
15
) are fulfilled, one have

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\left[\sqrt{-g}\left(J^{r}-\nabla_{\mu}F^{\mu r}\right)\right]=0\,.

(18)

The
r
-dependence parts of the supplementary equation are satisfied automatically and the only indeterminate equation is at
\mathcal{O}(1)
. All independent equations of motion yield the solutions as

\displaystyle J_{u}

\displaystyle=\Omega^{-2}\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}\gamma_{z\bar{z}}^{-1}(\partial_{z}J_{\bar{z}}+\partial_{\bar{z}}J_{z})+\Omega^{-2}J_{u}^{0}\,,

(19)

\displaystyle A_{u}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}\Omega^{-2}\int_{0}^{r^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}r^{\prime\prime}\gamma_{z\bar{z}}^{-1}\partial_{r^{\prime}}(\partial_{z}A_{\bar{z}}+\partial_{\bar{z}}A_{z})+\left(\Omega^{-1}-\ell^{-1}\right)A_{u}^{0}\,,

(20)

\displaystyle J_{u}^{0}

\displaystyle=-\gamma_{z\bar{z}}^{-1}\Omega^{-2}\partial_{u}\left(\partial_{z}A_{\bar{z}}^{0}+\partial_{\bar{z}}A_{z}^{0}\right)-\partial_{u}A_{u}^{0}\,,

(21)

\displaystyle J_{z}

\displaystyle=-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}A_{z}+\partial_{r}(f\partial_{r}A_{z})+\partial_{r}\partial_{z}A_{u}+\Omega^{-2}\partial_{z}\left[\gamma_{z\bar{z}}^{-1}(\partial_{\bar{z}}A_{z}-\partial_{z}A_{\bar{z}})\right]\,,

(22)

\displaystyle J_{\bar{z}}

\displaystyle=-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}A_{\bar{z}}+\partial_{r}(f\partial_{r}A_{\bar{z}})+\partial_{r}\partial_{\bar{z}}A_{u}+\Omega^{-2}\partial_{\bar{z}}\left[\gamma_{z\bar{z}}^{-1}(\partial_{z}A_{\bar{z}}-\partial_{\bar{z}}A_{z})\right]\,,

(23)

where
A^{0}_{u}
is an integral constant, defined by
A^{0}_{u}=\ell^{2}\partial_{r}A_{u}|_{r=0}
, and
A_{z}^{0}=A_{z}|_{r=0}
,
A_{\bar{z}}^{0}=A_{\bar{z}}|_{r=0}
,
J_{u}^{0}=\ell^{2}J_{u}|_{r=0}
. One can see from the standard equations that
A_{\bar{z}}^{0}
and
A_{z}^{0}
do not have constraints on their time evolution. They are the news functions of this system that indicating the local propagating degree of freedom. It is obvious that
A_{u}
is totally determined by the integration constant and
A_{z}
,
A_{\bar{z}}
which generate the whole solution space of the near horizon Maxwell theory. Any differentiable scalar function
\epsilon
defined on a
2
-sphere can be used to transform
A_{\mu}
through a large gauge transformation of
\delta A_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}\epsilon(z,\bar{z})
, resulting in an isomorphism of the solution space.

2.3
Surface charge of large gauge symmetry

We express the surface charge as

\displaystyle Q^{-}_{\epsilon}=\int_{\mathcal{H}^{-}}\mathrm{d}^{2}z\mathrm{d}u\ \gamma_{z\bar{z}}\epsilon\left[\gamma_{z\bar{z}}^{-1}\partial_{u}\left(\partial_{z}A_{\bar{z}}^{0}+\partial_{\bar{z}}A_{z}^{0}\right)+J_{u}^{0}\right]\,,

(24)

where the solutions of the equations of motion are inserted. One can split this charge into two parts

•

Soft Charge.

\displaystyle Q_{S}^{-}=\int_{\mathcal{H}^{-}}\mathrm{d}^{2}z\mathrm{d}u\ \epsilon\partial_{u}\left(\partial_{z}A_{\bar{z}}^{0}+\partial_{\bar{z}}A_{z}^{0}\right)\,.

(25)

If the symmetry parameter
\epsilon
is a constant at the boundary, the integrand is exactly a total derivative for some functions, and thus this charge is trivial. But for the nontrivial case where
\epsilon
is a function defined on the horizon, this charge serves a key role in the infrared relation between asymptotic symmetry and the soft theorem.

•

Hard Charge.

\displaystyle Q^{-}_{H}=\int_{\mathcal{H}^{-}}\mathrm{d}^{2}z\mathrm{d}u\ \gamma_{z\bar{z}}\epsilon J_{u}^{0}\,.

(26)

This part acts on
\ket{\mathrm{in}}
linearly, and the state of incoming particles defined on Hilbert space are eigenstates of the hard charge.

The action of the large gauge transformation on the gauge field can be recovered from the canonical commutation relation. The symplectic form of the free Maxwell field, defined on the
\mathcal{H}^{-}
as a Cauchy surface, is

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathcal{H}^{-}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{H}^{-}}\delta A\wedge\delta(\star F)

(27)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{H}^{-}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}^{2}z\delta\partial_{u}A_{\bar{z}}^{0}\wedge\delta A_{z}^{0}+\delta\partial_{u}A_{z}^{0}\wedge\delta A_{\bar{z}}^{0}\,,

which yields the canonical commutation relation

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{u}A_{\bar{z}}^{0}(u,z,\bar{z}),A_{z}^{0}(g,w,\bar{w})\right]=-i\delta(u-g)\delta^{2}(z-w)\,.

(28)

Thus

\displaystyle\left[Q_{\epsilon}^{-},A_{z}^{0}(u,z,\bar{z})\right]=i\partial_{z}\epsilon(z,\bar{z})\,,

(29)

which reveals that
Q_{\epsilon}^{-}
generates the gauge transformation of near horizon symmetry, as anticipated.

If we assume no magnetic monopole and long-range magnetic force
[
4
]
,
\mathrm{d}F=0
,
F_{z\bar{z}}|_{u\rightarrow-\infty}=0
, one can find a scalar field
N
such that

\displaystyle\partial_{z}N=\int\mathrm{d}u\partial_{u}A_{z}^{0}\,,\qquad\partial_{\bar{z}}N=\int\mathrm{d}u\partial_{u}A_{\bar{z}}^{0}\,,

(30)

and thus a useful form of soft charge is

\displaystyle Q_{S}^{-}=-2\int\mathrm{d}^{2}z\partial_{\bar{z}}\epsilon\partial_{z}N\,.

(31)

2.4
Mode expansion near the horizon

Now we derive the mode expansions of the gauge field which are the main ingredients for deriving a soft theorem. It is convenient to write down the mode expansion of the free field operator under the isotropic coordinates
(t,x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
, in which the line element of the dS spacetime is

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-\rho^{2}}{\ell^{2}+\rho^{2}}\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\mathrm{\left(\frac{2\ell^{2}}{\ell^{2}+\rho^{2}}\right)^{2}d}\vec{x}\cdot\mathrm{d}\vec{x}\,,

(32)

\displaystyle t=u+\frac{1}{2}\ell\log

\displaystyle\left(\frac{r}{2\ell-r}\right)\,,\quad x_{1}=\rho\frac{z+\bar{z}}{z\bar{z}+1}\,,\quad x_{2}=\rho\frac{z-\bar{z}}{i(z\bar{z}+1)}\,,\quad x_{3}=\rho\frac{1-z\bar{z}}{z\bar{z}+1}\,,

(33)

where

\displaystyle r=\ell\frac{(\ell-\rho)^{2}}{\ell^{2}+\rho^{2}}\,.

(34)

In the static patch of dS spacetime,
\partial_{t}
is a timelike Killing vector which suggests to define a positive energy of a particle as
\omega=-p_{0}
. The dispersion relation for massless particles in the isotropic coordinates is

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\rho^{2}+\ell^{2}}{\rho^{2}-\ell^{2}}\right)^{2}\omega^{2}+\left(\mathrm{\frac{\ell^{2}+\rho^{2}}{2\ell^{2}}}\right)^{2}\vec{p}^{2}=0\,.

(35)

One can find a covariant measure of one-particle phase space

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}\omega\mathrm{d}^{3}\vec{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta(p^{\mu}p_{\mu})\Theta(\omega)=\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-\rho^{2}}{\ell^{2}+\rho^{2}}\right)^{2}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\vec{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\omega}\,.

(36)

For the free massless scalar field
\phi(x)
, we can write this operator as

\displaystyle\phi(x^{\mu})=\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-\rho^{2}}{\ell^{2}+\rho^{2}}\right)^{2}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\vec{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\omega}\left[\mathfrak{a}(p)e^{ip\cdot x}+\mathfrak{a}(p)^{\dagger}e^{-ip\cdot x}\right]\,.

(37)

The extra factor
\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-\rho^{2}}{\ell^{2}+\rho^{2}}\right)^{2}
comes from the gravitational effect through the dispersion relation. Literally, this mode expansion is the flat spacetime one with the correction from the dispersion relation of the null momentum. The full mode expansion of scalar field in dS spacetime can be performed perturbatively, see, e.g., in
[
20
]
. However, for the gauge theories, they are the exact mode expansions since the dS spacetime is conformally flat and the gauge theories are conformal invariance in four dimensions at the classical level. The mode expansion for the Maxwell field
A_{\mu}
with two polarization vectors that are perpendicular to the direction of propagation is

\displaystyle A_{\mu}(x^{\mu})=\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-\rho^{2}}{\ell^{2}+\rho^{2}}\right)^{2}\sum_{\alpha=\pm}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\vec{p}}{(2\pi)^{3}2\omega}\left[\epsilon^{*\alpha}_{\mu}a_{\alpha}(p)e^{ip\cdot x}+\epsilon^{\alpha}_{\mu}a_{\alpha}^{\dagger}(p)e^{-ip\cdot x}\right]\,.

(38)

One can parameterize the photon momentum as

\displaystyle p_{\mu}=\frac{\omega}{1+\zeta\bar{\zeta}}\frac{2\ell^{2}}{\rho^{2}-\ell^{2}}\left(\frac{\rho^{2}-\ell^{2}}{2\ell^{2}}(1+\zeta\bar{\zeta}),(\zeta+\bar{\zeta}),-i(\zeta-\bar{\zeta}),(1-\zeta\bar{\zeta})\right)\,,

(39)

and similarly construct the polarization vectors as

\displaystyle\epsilon^{+\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{\ell^{2}+\rho^{2}}{2\ell^{2}}\left(\frac{2\ell^{2}}{\rho^{2}-\ell^{2}}\bar{\zeta},1,-i,-\bar{\zeta}\right)\,,

(40)

\displaystyle\epsilon^{-\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{\ell^{2}+\rho^{2}}{2\ell^{2}}\left(\frac{2\ell^{2}}{\rho^{2}-\ell^{2}}\zeta,1,i,-\zeta\right)\,,

(41)

which satisfies

\displaystyle\epsilon^{\alpha\mu}\epsilon^{\beta*}_{\mu}=\delta^{\alpha\beta}\,,\qquad p_{\mu}\epsilon^{\alpha\mu}=0\,,\qquad\epsilon^{\alpha}_{r}=0\,.

(42)

Projecting those two vectors to the sphere, we have

\displaystyle\epsilon_{\bar{z}}^{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sqrt{2}\ell^{2}\rho}{(1+\zeta\bar{\zeta})\left(\ell^{2}+\rho^{2}\right)}\,,

\displaystyle\epsilon_{z}^{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sqrt{2}\ell^{2}\rho}{(1+\zeta\bar{\zeta})\left(\ell^{2}+\rho^{2}\right)}\,.

(43)

To work on the energy representation, we should perform an integral to remove the degree of freedom with respect to the space angle

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}p}{2\omega}\epsilon^{*\alpha}_{\mu}a_{\alpha}(p)e^{ip\cdot x}=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{2\ell^{2}}{\rho^{2}-\ell^{2}}\right)^{3}\int\pi\omega\mathrm{d}\omega\epsilon^{*\alpha}_{\mu}(\omega\hat{x})a_{\alpha}(\omega\hat{x})

(44)

\displaystyle\times\int\sin\mathrm{\theta d}\theta\exp\left[i\omega\left(-t+\frac{2\ell^{2}}{\rho^{2}-\ell^{2}}\rho\cos\theta\right)\right]\,.

This allows for an exact computation of the integral,

\displaystyle\int\sin\mathrm{\theta d}\theta\exp

\displaystyle\left[i\omega\left(-t+\frac{2\ell^{2}}{\rho^{2}-\ell^{2}}\rho\cos\theta\right)\right]

(45)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho\omega}\left(\frac{r}{2\ell-r}\right)^{-i\ell\omega/2}\left(\frac{\rho^{2}-\ell^{2}}{2\ell^{2}}\right)\times e^{-i\omega u}\sin\left[\frac{2\ell^{2}}{\rho^{2}-\ell^{2}}\rho\omega\right]\,.

Finally, the near horizon transverse field operator
A^{0}_{z}
is expressed in terms of plane modes

\displaystyle A_{z}^{0}(x)=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\pi^{2}(1+z\bar{z})}

\displaystyle\frac{\ell^{6}}{\left[\ell^{2}+\left(R+\ell\right)^{2}\right]^{3}}\int\mathrm{d}\omega\left(\frac{r}{2\ell-r}\right)^{-i\ell\omega/2}

(46)

\displaystyle\times\sin\left[\frac{2\ell^{2}(R+\ell)\omega}{R(R+2\ell)}\right]\left[e^{\mathrm{-i\omega}u}a_{+}(\omega\hat{x})+e^{\mathrm{i\omega}u}a_{-}^{\dagger}(\omega\hat{x})\right]\,,

where
R=\rho-\ell
, and
R=0
at the horizon. Following the treatment in

,
8
]
, we introduce a near horizon regularization
R\rightarrow R+i\mathcal{R}
to deal with the divergence from the near horizon limit.

2.5
Soft photon theorem in coordinates space

Following previous treatment
[
4
,
7
]
, we choose
\epsilon=\frac{1}{z-w}
, which leads to

\displaystyle\partial_{\bar{z}}\epsilon(z,\bar{z})=2\pi\delta^{2}(z-w)\,.

(47)

Hence, the soft charge (
31
) has the form in terms of annihilation and creation operators

\displaystyle Q_{S}^{-}

\displaystyle=-8\pi^{2}i\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\left[\omega\widetilde{A_{z}^{0}}\right]\,,

(48)

where
\widetilde{A_{z}^{0}}
is defined by Fourier relation
A_{z}^{0}=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\mathrm{d}\omega e^{iu\omega}\widetilde{A_{z}^{0}}
, and thus

\displaystyle Q_{S}^{-}=-\frac{i2\sqrt{2}}{1+w\bar{w}}\frac{\ell^{2}}{R}\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\left(\omega^{2}a_{+}(\omega\hat{x})+\omega^{2}a_{-}^{\dagger}(\omega\hat{x})\right)\,.

(49)

The soft charge acts on the in-state as

Q_{S}^{-}\ket{\mathrm{in}}=-\frac{i2\sqrt{2}}{1+w\bar{w}}\frac{\ell^{2}}{R}\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\omega^{2}a_{-}^{\dagger}(\omega\hat{x})\ket{\mathrm{in}}\,.

(50)

Regarding to the action of the hard charge on the state, for simplicity, we consider a complex scalar field source. The conserved current in terms of complex scalar fields is

J^{0}_{u}=iQ\left(\phi^{0}\partial_{u}\bar{\phi}^{0}-\bar{\phi}^{0}\partial_{u}\phi^{0}\right)\,.

(51)

The symplectic form of a free scalar theory on the horizon is

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathcal{H}^{-}}=\int_{\mathcal{H}^{-}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}^{2}z\gamma_{z\bar{z}}\ell^{2}\left(\delta\partial_{u}\bar{\phi}^{0}_{k}\wedge\delta\phi^{0}_{k}+\delta\partial_{u}\phi^{0}\wedge\delta\bar{\phi}^{0}_{k}\right)\,,

(52)

which yields

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{u}\bar{\phi}^{0}_{k}(u,z,\bar{z}),\phi^{0}_{k}(g,w,\bar{w})\right]=-i\gamma^{-1}_{z\bar{z}}\ell^{-2}\delta^{2}(z-w)\delta(u-g)\,.

(53)

Thus the commutation relation between the hard charge and the scalar fields defined on
\mathcal{H}^{-}
is

\displaystyle\left[Q_{H}^{-},\phi^{0}_{k}(u_{k},z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})\right]=Q_{k}\epsilon(z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})\phi^{0}_{k}(u_{k},z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})\,.

(54)

Hence

\displaystyle Q_{H}^{-}\ket{\mathrm{in}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}Q_{k}\epsilon(z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})\ket{\mathrm{in}}\,.

(55)

Then the action of the hard charge on the in-state is

Q_{H}^{-}\ket{\mathrm{in}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{Q_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}}{z_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}-w}\ket{\mathrm{in}}\,,

(56)

Finally, combining those terms and considering also the out-state part, the Ward identity in the coordinates space yields the soft theorem as

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\matrixelement{\mathrm{out}}{a_{+}\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{S}a_{-}^{\dagger}}{\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle=i\frac{R}{2\ell^{2}\omega}\frac{1+w\bar{w}}{\sqrt{2}\omega}\left[\sum_{l=1}^{m}\frac{Q_{l}^{\mathrm{out}}}{w-z_{l}^{\mathrm{out}}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{Q_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}}{w-z_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}}\right]\matrixelement{\mathrm{out}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathrm{in}}\,.

(57)

2.6
Soft theorem in momentum space

From the null parameterization of the incoming hard momenta in the near horizon region,

\displaystyle p_{k\mu}^{\mathrm{in}}=\frac{E_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}}{1+z_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}\bar{z}_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}}\frac{2\ell^{2}}{\rho^{2}-\ell^{2}}\left(\frac{\ell^{2}-\rho^{2}}{2\ell^{2}}(1+z_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}\bar{z}_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}),(z_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}+\bar{z}_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}),-i(z_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}-\bar{z}_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}),(1-z_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}\bar{z}_{k}^{\mathrm{in}})\right)\,.

(58)

it is easy to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}Q_{k}\frac{p_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}\cdot\epsilon}{p_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}\cdot p^{\mathrm{in}}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{R}{\ell}\frac{(1+w\bar{w})}{\sqrt{2}\omega}\frac{Q_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}}{w-z_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}}\,.

(59)

Similar relation can be derived for the outgoing hard momenta. Those relations allow us to write the soft photon theorem in momentum space as

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\matrixelement{\mathrm{out}}{a_{+}\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{S}a_{-}^{\dagger}}{\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2\omega\ell}\left[\sum_{l=1}^{m}Q_{l}^{\mathrm{out}}\frac{p_{l}^{\mathrm{out}}\cdot\epsilon}{p_{l}^{\mathrm{out}}\cdot p}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}Q_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}\frac{p_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}\cdot\epsilon}{p_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}\cdot p}\right]\matrixelement{\mathrm{out}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathrm{in}}\,.

(60)

2.7
Flat limit

A limit of the spacetime geometries is in general a subtle issue
[
27
]
. For instance, the flat limit
\ell\to\infty
is not well defined for the line element (
5
). While, the flat limit is well defined for the case using
\Omega
as the radial coordinate, where the line element is

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=(\frac{\Omega^{2}}{\ell^{2}}-1)\mathrm{d}u^{2}-2\mathrm{d}\Omega\mathrm{d}u+\Omega^{2}\gamma_{z\bar{z}}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}\bar{z}\,,\quad\Omega=\ell-r\,.

(61)

The location of the cosmological horizon in term of
\Omega
is simply
\Omega=\ell
. Hence, the flat limit yields that the cosmological horizon becomes the null infinity of the flat spacetime.

Regarding to the flat limit of the soft theorem in dS spacetime, it is more reasonable to consider the momentum space one due to the subtleties from the coordinates in the spacetime limit. It is obvious from (
60
) that the soft theorem in the flat limit recovers the flat spacetime soft photon theorem. The only condition is that the dS radius multiplied by the soft energy is a finite constant in the flat limit, see also
[
28
]
for relevant discussions about the flat limit of AdS spacetime and soft limit. This constraint can be understood as follows. In terms of the cosmological constant
\Lambda
, the flat limit is only physically reasonable when
\Lambda
approaching zero as fast as the soft energy
\omega
. Note that any constant prefactor can be absorbed in the null parametrization (
39
) and the imaginary unit
i
would not change the physical observable cross section. The imaginary unit
i
can also be understood as a regularization in the flat limit in terms of
\Lambda
. Similar to the black hole cases
[
7
,
8
]
,
\Lambda=0
means there is no horizon in the spacetime at all. Since our derivation is based on the near horizon symmetry, the flat limit should not be taken directly, but by introducing a regularization
\Lambda\to\Lambda+i\lambda
. Then the condition for
\lambda
in the flat limit is that it should be proportional to the soft energy
\omega
.

3
Soft gluon theorem in de Sitter spacetime

In this section, we explore the soft gluon theorem from the Ward identity with respect to the near horizon symmetry of a scalar matter field coupled Yang-Mills theory in dS spacetime. For generality, we do not specify the
gauge group. The theory is described by the action

\displaystyle S=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\,\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{1}{4}F^{a\mu\nu}F^{a}_{\mu\nu}+D_{\mu}\phi^{a}D^{\mu}\bar{\phi}^{a}\right)\,.

(62)

The scalar fields
\phi^{a}
are in the adjoint representation of the gauge group,

\displaystyle\left[T^{a},T^{b}\right]=C^{abc}T^{c}\,,\qquad\left(T^{a}\right)_{bc}=C^{abc}\,,\qquad\ (D_{\mu}\phi)^{a}=\nabla_{\mu}\phi^{a}+ig_{YM}QC^{abc}A_{\mu}^{b}\phi^{c}\,

(63)

the gauge field strength is defined by

\displaystyle F^{a}_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}^{a}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}^{a}+ig_{YM}C^{abc}A_{\mu}^{a}A_{\nu}^{b}\,.

(64)

The gauge transformation satisfies the symmetry algebra

\displaystyle\left[\delta_{\epsilon_{1}},\delta_{\epsilon_{2}}\right]A^{a}_{\mu}=\delta_{\left[{\epsilon_{1}},{\epsilon_{2}}\right]}A^{a}_{\mu}\,,\qquad\left[\delta_{\epsilon_{1}},\delta_{\epsilon_{2}}\right]\phi^{a}=\delta_{\left[{\epsilon_{1}},{\epsilon_{2}}\right]}\phi^{a}\,.

(65)

3.1
Gauge fixing and boundary Condition

We choose the radial gauge and the following near horizon fall-off conditions,

\displaystyle A_{r}^{a}=0\,,\quad A_{u}^{a}=\mathcal{O}(r)\,,\quad A_{z}^{a}=\mathcal{O}(1)\,,\quad\phi^{a}=\mathcal{O}(1)\,,\quad\bar{\phi}^{a}=\mathcal{O}(1)\,.

(66)

The only residual gauge transformation is
\delta A_{\mu}^{a}=D_{\mu}\epsilon^{a}(z,\bar{z})
where
\epsilon^{a}(z,\bar{z})
are arbitrary differentiable functions defined on
S^{2}
. The surface charge is constructed by
[
29
]

\displaystyle Q_{\epsilon}=

\displaystyle 2\int_{B}\Tr[\epsilon\star F]

(67)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B}\mathrm{d}^{2}z\Omega^{2}\gamma_{z\bar{z}}\epsilon^{a}F^{a}_{ru}\,,

which generates the nontrivial large gauge transformation in the solution space of the near horizon scalar fields coupled Yang-Mills theory.

3.2
Solution space

Following the analysis of the Abelian case, we divide equations of motion into three parts

•

Hyper-surface equation

\displaystyle D_{\mu}F^{a\mu u}=0\,.

(68)

•

Standard equations

\displaystyle D_{\mu}F^{a\mu z}=J^{az}\,,\ D_{\mu}F^{a\mu\bar{z}}=J^{a\bar{z}}\,,\ D^{\mu}D_{\mu}\phi^{a}=D^{\mu}D_{\mu}\bar{\phi}^{a}=0\,.

(69)

•

Supplementary equation

\displaystyle D_{\mu}F^{\mu r}=J^{r}\,.

(70)

The conserved current is given by

\displaystyle J_{\mu}^{a}=iQC^{abc}\left(\phi^{b}D_{\mu}\bar{\phi}^{c}-\bar{\phi}^{b}D_{\mu}\phi^{c}\right)\,.

(71)

The Noether identity

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\left(g_{YM}J^{a\mu}-D_{\nu}F^{a\nu\mu}\right)

\displaystyle=ig_{YM}QC^{abc}\left[\phi^{b}D_{\mu}D^{\mu}\bar{\phi}^{c}-\bar{\phi}^{b}D_{\mu}D^{\mu}\phi^{c}\right]\,,

(72)

yields that, once equations (
68
) and (
69
) are satisfied, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\left[\sqrt{-g}\left(D_{\mu}F^{a\mu r}-g_{YM}J^{ar}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=0\,,

(73)

which means only the
\mathcal{O}(1)
equation is indeterminate. We present all necessary equations

•

Standard equations

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{A}\mathcal{D}_{A}\bar{\phi}^{a}+\Omega^{-1}

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{r}\left(\Omega f\partial_{r}\bar{\phi}^{a}\right)+f\partial_{r}\Omega\partial_{r}\bar{\phi}^{a}\right]-2\Omega^{-1}\partial_{r}\partial_{u}\left(\Omega\bar{\phi}^{a}\right)

(74)

\displaystyle-ig_{YM}C^{abc}\left(h^{AB}A_{A}^{b}D_{B}\bar{\phi}^{c}+fA_{r}^{b}\partial_{r}\bar{\phi}^{c}-A_{u}^{b}\partial_{r}\bar{\phi}^{c}\right)

\displaystyle+ig_{YM}C^{abc}\left[\mathcal{D}_{A}\left(A^{bA}\bar{\phi}^{c}\right)-\Omega^{-2}\partial_{r}\left(\Omega^{2}A_{u}^{b}\bar{\phi}^{c}\right)\right]=0\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{A}\mathcal{D}_{A}\phi^{a}+\Omega^{-1}

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{r}\left(\Omega f\partial_{r}\phi^{a}\right)+f\partial_{r}\Omega\partial_{r}\phi^{a}\right]-2\Omega^{-1}\partial_{r}\partial_{u}\left(\Omega\phi^{a}\right)

(75)

\displaystyle+ig_{YM}C^{abc}\left(h^{AB}A_{A}^{b}D_{B}\phi^{c}+fA_{r}^{b}\partial_{r}\phi^{c}-A_{u}^{b}\partial_{r}\phi^{c}\right)

\displaystyle-ig_{YM}C^{abc}\left[\mathcal{D}_{A}\left(A^{bA}\phi^{c}\right)-\Omega^{-2}\partial_{r}\left(\Omega^{2}A_{u}^{b}\phi^{c}\right)\right]=0\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\partial_{z}A_{u}^{a}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}A_{z}^{a}+\partial_{r}\left(f\partial_{r}A_{z}^{a}\right)

\displaystyle-\partial_{z}\left(F_{\ \ \ z}^{a\bar{z}}\right)-g_{YM}J_{z}^{a}

(76)

\displaystyle=ig_{YM}C^{abc}

\displaystyle\left[A_{\bar{z}}^{b}\partial_{r}A_{z}^{c}+A_{z}^{b}F_{\ \ \ z}^{c\bar{z}}+A_{z}^{b}\partial_{r}A_{\bar{z}}^{c}-2A_{u}^{b}\partial_{r}A_{z}^{c}\right]\,,

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\partial_{\bar{z}}A_{u}^{a}-2\partial_{u}\partial_{r}A_{\bar{z}}^{a}+\partial_{r}\left(f\partial_{r}A_{\bar{z}}^{a}\right)

\displaystyle-\partial_{\bar{z}}\left(F_{\ \ \ \bar{z}}^{az}\right)-g_{YM}J_{\bar{z}}^{a}

(77)

\displaystyle=ig_{YM}C^{abc}

\displaystyle\left[A_{z}^{b}\partial_{r}A_{\bar{z}}^{c}+A_{\bar{z}}^{b}F_{\ \ \ \bar{z}}^{cz}+A_{\bar{z}}^{b}\partial_{r}A_{z}^{c}-2A_{u}^{b}\partial_{r}A_{\bar{z}}^{c}\right]\,,

where
\mathcal{D}^{A}
and
h_{AB}
are the covariant derivative and metric defined on the horizon respectively. It is clear that
A^{a0}_{z}=A^{a}_{z}|_{r=0}
,
A^{a0}_{\bar{z}}=A^{a}_{\bar{z}}|_{r=0}
,
\phi^{a0}=\phi^{a}|_{r=0}
do not have constraints for their time evolution. They are the news functions of the system that characterize the local propagating degree of freedom.

•

Hyper-surface equation

\displaystyle A_{u}^{a}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}\Omega^{-2}\int_{0}^{r^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}r^{\prime\prime}\gamma_{z\bar{z}}^{-1}\partial_{r^{\prime\prime}}\left(\partial_{\bar{z}}A_{z}^{a}+\partial_{z}A_{\bar{z}}^{a}\right)+\left(\ell^{-1}-\Omega^{-1}\right)A_{u}^{a0}

(78)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{r}\mathrm{d}r^{\prime}\Omega^{-2}\int_{0}^{r^{\prime}}\mathrm{d}r^{\prime\prime}\gamma_{z\bar{z}}^{-1}ig_{YM}C^{abc}\left(A_{z}^{b}\partial_{r^{\prime\prime}}A_{\bar{z}}^{c}+A_{\bar{z}}^{b}\partial_{r^{\prime\prime}}A_{z}^{c}\right)\,,

where
A^{a0}_{u}
is an integral constant and defined by
A^{a0}_{u}=\ell^{2}\partial_{r}A^{a}_{u}|_{r=0}
.

•

Supplementary equation

\displaystyle\partial_{u}A_{u}^{a0}=-\gamma_{z\bar{z}}^{-1}\partial_{u}

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{\bar{z}}A_{z}^{a0}+\partial_{z}A_{\bar{z}}^{a0}\right)

(79)

\displaystyle-ig_{YM}C^{abc}\gamma_{z\bar{z}}^{-1}\left(A_{z}^{0b}\partial_{u}A_{\bar{z}}^{0c}+A_{\bar{z}}^{0b}\partial_{u}A_{z}^{0c}\right)

\displaystyle-iQg_{YM}C^{abc}\Omega^{2}\left(\phi^{0b}\partial_{u}\bar{\phi}^{0c}-\bar{\phi}^{0b}\partial_{u}\phi^{0c}\right)\,.

The full solution space of the non-Abelian theory is generated by the integration constant
A^{a0}_{u}
and the initial data
\{A_{z}^{a},A_{\bar{z}}^{a},\phi^{a},\bar{\phi}^{a}\}
and the news functions at any time.

3.3
Surface charge of large gauge symmetry

Inserting the near horizon solution, the boundary charge is reduced to

\displaystyle Q_{\epsilon}=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{H}^{-}}\mathrm{d}^{2}z\mathrm{d}u\epsilon^{a}\partial_{u}\left(\partial_{\bar{z}}A_{z}^{a0}+\partial_{z}A_{\bar{z}}^{a0}\right)

(80)

\displaystyle+ig_{YM}C^{abc}\int_{\mathcal{H}^{-}}\mathrm{d}^{2}z\mathrm{d}u\epsilon^{a}\left[\left(A_{z}^{a0}\partial_{u}A_{\bar{z}}^{0b}+A_{\bar{z}}^{a0}\partial_{u}A_{z}^{0b}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+Q\gamma_{z\bar{z}}\Omega^{2}\left(\phi^{0b}\partial_{u}\bar{\phi}^{0c}-\bar{\phi}^{0b}\partial_{u}\phi^{0c}\right)\right]\,.

We decompose this charge into two parts

•

Soft Charge

\displaystyle Q_{S}^{-}=-2\int_{B}\mathrm{d}^{2}z\partial_{\bar{z}}\epsilon^{a}\partial_{z}N^{a}\,,

(81)

where

\displaystyle\partial_{z}N^{a}=\int\mathrm{d}u\partial_{u}A_{z}^{a0}\,,\ \partial_{\bar{z}}N^{a}=\int\mathrm{d}u\partial_{u}A_{\bar{z}}^{a0}\,.

(82)

The soft charge is very similar to the case of the Maxwell theory. The only adaption is the group index on the gauge field.

•

Hard Charge

\displaystyle Q_{H}^{-}=

\displaystyle ig_{YM}C^{abc}\int_{\mathcal{H}^{-}}\mathrm{d}^{2}z\mathrm{d}u\epsilon^{a}\left[\left(A_{z}^{0b}\partial_{u}A_{\bar{z}}^{0c}+A_{\bar{z}}^{b0}\partial_{u}A_{z}^{0c}\right)\right.

(83)

\displaystyle\left.+Q\gamma_{z\bar{z}}\Omega^{2}\left(\phi^{0b}\partial_{u}\bar{\phi}^{0c}-\bar{\phi}^{0b}\partial_{u}\phi^{0c}\right)\right]

The first line is zero in the Abelian case since a linearization will turn off the self-interacting pieces.

The canonical commutation relations of the Abelian case on
\mathcal{H}^{-}
can be generalized to the present case as

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{u}A_{\bar{z}}^{0a}(u,z,\bar{z}),A_{z}^{0b}(g,w,\bar{w})\right]=-i\delta^{2}(z-w)\delta(u-g)\delta^{ab}\,,

(84)

thus

\displaystyle\left[Q^{-}_{\epsilon},A_{z}^{0d}(u,z,\bar{z})\right]=i\left[\partial_{\mu}\epsilon^{d}(z,\bar{z})+ig_{YM}C^{abd}A^{0a}_{z}\epsilon^{b}(z,\bar{z})\right]\,,

(85)

which is the infinitesimal gauge transformation of the gauge field.

3.4
Mode expansion near the horizon

The mode expansion of the Yang-Mills field is almost the same as the Maxwell field with the only adaption of a group index. We just give the final results of the mode expansion here without repeating the same type of computation. The mode expansion near the horizon field is given by

\displaystyle A_{z}^{0a}(x)=\frac{2\sqrt{2}}{\pi^{2}(1+z\bar{z})}

\displaystyle\frac{\ell^{6}}{\left[\ell^{2}+\left(R+\ell\right)^{2}\right]^{3}}\int\mathrm{d}\omega\left(\frac{r}{2\ell-r}\right)^{-i\ell\omega/2}

(86)

\displaystyle\times\sin\left[\frac{2\ell^{2}(R+\ell)\omega}{R(R+2\ell)}\right]\left[e^{\mathrm{-i\omega}u}a_{+}^{a}(\omega\hat{x})+e^{\mathrm{i\omega}u}a_{-}^{a\dagger}(\omega\hat{x})\right]\,.

3.5
Soft gluon theorem and flat limit

Following the choice in
[
8
]
, we choose
\epsilon^{a}(z,\bar{z})=\frac{1}{z-w}
where
a
denotes a particular component of
\epsilon
and the rest components are set to zero. Then, the soft charge of the Yang-Mills theory in terms of the annihilation and creation operators defined in the mode expansion is

\displaystyle Q_{S}^{-}=-\frac{i2\sqrt{2}}{1+w\bar{w}}\frac{\ell^{2}}{R}\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\left(\omega^{2}a_{+}^{a}(\omega\hat{x})+\omega^{2}a_{-}^{a\dagger}(\omega\hat{x})\right)\,.

(87)

Acting the soft charge on in-state yields

\displaystyle Q_{S}^{-}\ket{\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i2\sqrt{2}}{1+w\bar{w}}\frac{\ell^{2}}{R}\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\omega^{2}a_{-}^{a\dagger}(\omega\hat{x})\ket{\mathrm{in}}\,.

(88)

Regarding to the hard part, one needs to compute the commutation relation from the symplectic form of
\phi^{a}
, which is given by

\displaystyle\Omega_{\mathcal{H}^{-}}=\int_{\mathcal{H}^{-}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}^{2}z\gamma_{z\bar{z}}\ell^{2}\left(\delta\partial_{u}\bar{\phi}^{0a}\wedge\delta\phi^{0a}+\delta\partial_{u}\phi^{0a}\wedge\delta\bar{\phi}^{0a}\right)\,.

(89)

Hence,

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{u}\bar{\phi}^{0a}(u,z,\bar{z}),\phi^{0b}(g,w,\bar{w})\right]=-i\gamma^{-1}_{z\bar{z}}\ell^{-2}\delta^{2}(z-w)\delta(u-g)\delta^{ab}\,,

(90)

which leads to

\displaystyle\left[Q^{-}_{H},\phi^{0d}_{k}(u,z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})\right]=Q_{k}g_{YM}C^{abd}\epsilon^{a}(z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})\phi^{0b}_{k}(u,z_{k},\bar{z}_{k})\,.

(91)

One can find that the hard charge acts on in-state of the scalar particle linearly

\displaystyle Q_{H}^{-}\ket{\mathrm{in}}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}Q^{\mathrm{in}}_{k}T^{a}_{k}\epsilon^{a}(z_{k}^{\mathrm{in}},\bar{z}_{k}^{\mathrm{in}})\ket{\mathrm{in}}\,.

(92)

Note that the commutation relation in (
84
) will yield the same result for the hard charge acting on in-state of the gluon. Finally, combining the actions of the soft and hard charges, the Ward identity in the coordinates space leads to a soft gluon theorem in dS spacetime

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\omega^{2}\matrixelement{\mathrm{out}}{a_{+}^{b}\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{S}a_{-}^{b\dagger}}{\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle=i\frac{1+w\bar{w}}{2\sqrt{2}}\frac{R}{\ell^{2}}\left[\sum_{l=1}^{m}\frac{Q_{l}^{\mathrm{out}}T^{b}_{l}}{w-z_{l}^{\mathrm{out}}}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{Q_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}T^{b}_{k}}{w-z_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}}\right]\matrixelement{\mathrm{out}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathrm{in}}\,.

(93)

Considering the null parametrization in (
39
), the soft theorem in momentum space is

\displaystyle\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\matrixelement{\mathrm{out}}{a_{+}^{b}\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{S}a_{-}^{b\dagger}}{\mathrm{in}}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2\omega\ell}\left[\sum_{l=1}^{m}Q_{l}^{\mathrm{out}}\frac{T^{b}_{l}p_{l}^{\mathrm{out}}\cdot\epsilon}{p_{l}^{\mathrm{out}}\cdot p}-\sum_{k=1}^{n}Q_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}\frac{T^{b}_{k}p_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}\cdot\epsilon}{p_{k}^{\mathrm{in}}\cdot p}\right]\matrixelement{\mathrm{out}}{\mathcal{S}}{\mathrm{in}}\,.

(94)

It is amusing to see that the dS radius has exactly the same type of correction to soft photon and soft gluon theorem in dS spacetime. The flat limit of the soft gluon theorem is just the same as the soft photon theorem.

4
Conclusion

In this paper, a soft photon theorem and a soft gluon theorem are derived in dS spacetime from the Ward identity of the near horizon symmetry. Our results indicate that the cosmological constant affects the soft theorems universally. Both the soft photon and soft gluon theorem have a well defined flat limit. The soft theorems after the flat limit recover exactly the flat spacetime soft photon and soft gluon theorems.

As an ending remark, it is worth mentioning that we focus on massless hard particles in the soft theorem. While, the soft photon theorem derived in
[
20
]
involves only massive hard particles and the zero mass limit of the hard particles seems not well defined. So it is not clear to us how to connect our results in the present form to the ones in
[
20
]
.

Ricci Reheating on the Lattice
Daniel G. Figueroa, Toby Opferkuch, Ben A. Stefanek

Abstract
We study the dynamics of a non-minimally coupled (NMC) scalar spectator field in non-oscillatory inflationary scenarios, where there is a transition from inflation to kination domination (KD). Engineering a realistic finite-duration transition through a CMB-compatible inflaton potential, we calculate the initial tachyonic growth of the NMC field during KD and perform lattice simulations of the subsequent non-linear dynamics. We characterize the regularization effect on the tachyonic growth, either due to self-interactions, or via gravitational backreaction when the NMC field grows to dominate the energy of the universe. Our study provides the first realistic treatment of the dynamics, with significant improvements compared to previous work, where one or more of the following aspects were assumed: ( i i ) the background expansion can be neglected during the tachyonic growth, ( i ​ i ii ) coherence of the NMC field, ( i ​ i ​ i iii ) coherence of the inflaton, ( i ​ v iv ) instantaneous transition, and ( v v ) a KD equation of state of exactly w = 1 w=1 . Using our methodology, which requires none of the above assumptions, we determine the conditions to achieve proper reheating, i.e. energetic dominance of the NMC field over the inflaton. We characterize the time and energy scales of the problem, either for backreaction due to self-interactions, or (as a novelty of this work) due to gravitational effects. Finally, we calculate 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) \mathcal{O}(1) lattice correction factors to analytic scaling relations derived by some of us in previous work. This enables simple future studies without the need to run lattice simulations.

We consider a realistic transition of finite duration from quasi-de Sitter inflation to KD, which will be referred to, for short, as the qdS-KD transition. Our initial conditions are set deep inside inflation when the relevant fluctuations of the NMC scalar are sub-Hubble. As the qdS-KD transition proceeds, for certain modes there is a change of sign of the effective mass-squared, which flips from positive to negative, signifying the onset of a tachyonic instability. As long as the evolution of the
\chi

The tachyonic instability leads to exponential growth of the NMC field’s energy density. This growth eventually ends due to a backreaction of the NMC field itself on the dynamics. In particular, if self-interactions of
\chi

\chi

II
Model: setup, initial conditions, and dynamics

We consider a model involving a minimally coupled inflaton field
\phi
and a non-minimally coupled (NMC) spectator scalar field,
\chi
, which we will refer to as the
reheaton

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\int\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits^{4}\!x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{m_{p}^{2}}{2}R+\mathscr{L}_{\chi}+\mathscr{L}_{\text{inf}}\right]\,,

(1)

where

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\chi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\chi\partial_{\nu}\chi-\frac{1}{2}\xi R\chi^{2}-V(\chi)\,,

(2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\text{inf}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\mu}\phi\partial_{\nu}\phi-V_{\text{inf}}(\phi)\,,

(3)

with

Crucial to the Ricci reheating mechanism is an inflaton potential that allows for a transition from a quasi-de Sitter inflationary phase to a kination-dominated (KD) universe. A simple example that exhibits slow-roll inflation with a rapid transition to kination thereafter is a double plateau-like potential, as considered e.g. in
[

to flip sign and become tachyonic. This tachyonic mass results in an exponential growth of the reheaton field amplitude until it is regulated by either self-interactions in the potential
V(\chi)
, or by conservation of energy occurring if the reheaton field comes to dominate the energy budget in the universe. In the latter case, the non-minimally coupled reheaton sources the expansion of the universe, which we handle via the techniques developed in Ref.
[
94

1.

Self-interaction backreaction
: This corresponds to the case of a quartic potential,
V(\chi)=\frac{1}{4}\lambda\chi^{4}

\chi

Equations of motion

ds^{2}=-a(\eta)^{2\alpha}d\eta^{2}+a(\eta)^{2}\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}\,,

(9)

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime\prime}+(3-\alpha)\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\phi^{\prime}-a^{-2(1-\alpha)}\nabla^{2}\phi

(10)

\displaystyle=-a^{2\alpha}\Big[\xi R\chi+V^{\prime}(\chi)\Big]\,,

(11)

II.2
Initial conditions and lattice computation

Regarding the initial conditions of our problem, the inflaton field is set in a standard manner consistent with random initial fluctuations. We assume the inflaton is dominantly homogeneous and thus it evolves according to the homogeneous Klein-Gordon equation. Fluctuations are then added on top as random realizations of Fourier modes, following a Gaussian distribution with zero mean and variance given by a spectrum mimicking quantum vacuum fluctuations, see either of Refs.
[
92
,
91
]
for details on this standardized procedure. The initial spectrum of the field
\chi
requires however extra care, due to the presence of the non-minimal coupling. Fixing for this discussion
\alpha=1
, namely conformal time, we observe that
the kinetic term for
\chi
can be made canonical via the field redefinition
\chi\rightarrow\sigma/a
which, after removing total derivatives, leads to the following action for
\sigma
(where we neglect for the moment self-interactions of the NMC field),

\mathcal{S}_{\sigma}=\frac{1}{2}\int\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits\!\tau\mathop{\mathrm{\mathstrut d}}\nolimits^{3}\!x\left[(\sigma^{\prime})^{2}-(\nabla\sigma)^{2}-a^{2}\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)R\,\sigma^{2}\right]\,,

(18)

where we have used that in a FLRW background,
a^{\prime\prime}/a^{3}=R/6
. Quantization of
\sigma
then proceeds as in Minkowski space
[
101
]

\hat{\sigma}(\tau,x)=\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\left[\sigma_{k}(\tau)\hat{a}_{k}e^{ikx}+\sigma_{k}^{*}(\tau)a^{\dagger}_{k}e^{-ikx}\right]\,,

(19)

where the creation and annihilation operators satisfy the usual algebra
[\hat{a}_{k},\hat{a}_{k^{\prime}}^{\dagger}]=(2\pi)^{3}\delta(k-k^{\prime})
and the mode fluctuations are normalized as
\sigma_{k}\sigma_{k}^{\prime*}-\sigma_{k}^{\prime}\sigma_{k}^{*}=i
. The mode functions
\sigma_{k}(\tau)
satisfy the following equation of motion

\sigma^{\prime\prime}_{k}+\left[k^{2}+a^{2}\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)R\right]\,\sigma_{k}=0\,.

(20)

The coincident, equal-time two point function can be computed as

\langle\sigma^{2}\rangle=\langle 0|\hat{\sigma}(\tau,{\bf x})\hat{\sigma}(\tau,{\bf x})|0\rangle=\int\frac{dk}{k}\Delta_{\sigma}(k,\tau)\,,

(21)

where we have introduced a power spectrum defined as

\displaystyle\Delta_{\sigma}(k,\tau)=\frac{k^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(k,\tau)\,,~~~~~{\rm with}~~\langle\sigma_{{\bf k}}\sigma_{{\bf k}^{\prime}}^{*}\rangle=(2\pi)^{3}\mathcal{P}_{\sigma}(k,\tau)\delta({\bf k}-{\bf k}^{\prime})\,.

(22)

The power spectrum for
\chi
is simply given by
\Delta_{\chi}(k,\tau)=a^{-2}\Delta_{\sigma}(k,\tau)
. As shown in Fig.
3
, such power spectra typically exhibit a large excitation within a finite range of momenta. In order to understand such a
bump
in the spectra, we note that during the period following immediately after inflation,
R
is only sourced by the homogeneous inflaton, which initially dominates the energy budget. Introducing
K(\phi)\equiv\frac{1}{2a^{2\alpha}}{\phi^{\prime}}^{2}
,
G(\phi)\equiv\frac{1}{2a^{2}}(\vec{\nabla}\phi)^{2}
and
V_{\rm inf}(\phi)
as the inflaton’s kinetic, gradient and potential energy densities, we observe that the equation of state of the universe becomes, as the inflaton enters into KD (
K>2V
),

\displaystyle w\equiv{\left\langle K(\phi)-V_{\rm inf}(\phi)\right\rangle\over\left\langle K(\phi)+V_{\rm inf}(\phi)\right\rangle}>1/3\,,~~~({\rm KD})

(23)

gradually approaching
w\rightarrow 1
. The transition to unity occurs faster for larger
\beta
, namely when the qdS-KD transition is fast compared to the Hubble scale. As a result the Ricci scalar become negative, as

\displaystyle R={2\over m_{p}^{2}}\Big\langle 2V_{\rm inf}(\phi)-K(\phi)\Big\rangle<0\,,~~~({\rm KD})\,.

(24)

We note that
\langle...\rangle
in Eqs. (
23
) and (
24
) represents volume averaging, whereas in
Eq.

21
and
Eq.

22
it represents quantum-mechanical ensemble averaging.

At times when the gravitational backreaction of
\chi
can be ignored, and its self-interactions can be neglected,
Eq.

20
represents a linear equation for
\sigma_{k}
, for which
R<0
during KD. In this linear regime,
Eq.

20
indicates that, as long as
\xi>1/6
, infrared modes below a certain cutoff
k\lesssim k_{*}
, experience a
tachyonic
instability leading to exponential growth as
\sigma_{k}\propto e^{|\omega_{k}|\tau}
. From
Eq.

20
, we see that
\omega_{k}^{2}\equiv(k^{2}+a^{2}(\xi-\frac{1}{6})R)<0
, and hence we deduce that the tachyonic cutoff scale is

k_{*}=\sqrt{(\xi-1/6)|R|a^{2}}\>.

(25)

Using the universal relation in a FLRW background
[
94
]
,
R=3(1-3w)H^{2}
, with
H^{2}
the Hubble rate, we also conclude that
R<0
for
w>1/3
. As the sound speed of a fluid must not exceed the speed of light,
c_{\text{s}}^{2}=\partial p/\partial\rho\leq 1
, the equation of state during KD is also bounded as
w\leq 1
. Consequently, the most negative value of
R
is also bounded from below as
R\geq-6H^{2}
. This implies that if
\xi\gg 1
, sub-horizon modes of the NMC field will be excited, up to a maximum scale
k_{*}\simeq\sqrt{6\xi}aH
, as long as KD with
w\simeq 1
is sustained.

It is precisely the tachyonic instability during the linear regime, that actually explains the form of the spectra shown in Fig.
3
. The initial single bump shape is simply a consequence of the fact that only infrared modes up to a cutoff scale
k\lesssim k_{*}
, are excited during the linear regime. Above such cutoff, i.e. for
k\gtrsim k_{*}
, modes remain in vacuum. These vacuum fluctuations actually give rise to UV divergences in the power spectrum, which technically need to be regularized
[
102
,
103
,
104
]
. However, in practice on the lattice, the amplitude of the excited bump is much larger than the UV tail due to vacuum fluctuations. This is ensured thanks to the fact that the natural UV cutoff on the lattice,
k_{\rm UV}=\pi/\delta x
, is typically of the same order as (though slightly larger than)
k_{*}
. Therefore, as it is customary in lattice simulations
[
92
]
, we simply allow vacuum fluctuations to remain present in the simulation, as they do not affect the dynamics of the excited modes. In practice we ensure the above by choosing to start the simulation some time after inflation such that a clearly defined bump has formed, initializing the lattice simulation with such a peaked spectrum,
c.f.
the earliest time spectra in
Fig.

3
. From this point onwards, we run the lattice version of the equations of motion previously introduced, Eqs. (
10
)-(
12
), which will capture the dynamics of the two-field system, even when non-linearities develop later on. We present our results on the non-linear dynamics in the following section.

III
Results

As explained above, the tachyonic instability during the linear regime leads to exponential growth of the NMC field amplitude, and hence of its energy density. However, due to energy conservation, such growth cannot be sustained indefinitely. Basically, once the NMC energy surpasses a given threshold, the backreaction of
\chi
cannot be longer ignored, and the dynamics become non-linear from then on. As a result, the tachyonic instability is gradually switched off. The nature of the backreaction, and hence of the non-linear dynamics, depends crucially on whether the NMC field sustains large self-interactions or not. If self-interactions of
\chi
are negligible, the NMC field eventually comes to dominate the energy budget of the universe, driving
R
to zero, and thus switching off the instability. If, on the contrary, sufficiently strong self-interactions of the NMC field are present, these eventually screen the tachyonic mass, and as a result the instability is also switched off (even though
\chi
is still typically energetically very subdominant). We refer to the first regularizing effect as
gravitational backreaction
, and to the second one as
self-interaction backreaction
. In what follows, we describe both non-linear regimes in light of our lattice results, with the aid of analytic expressions.

IV
Summary and conclusions

In this paper, we examine the dynamics of a non-minimally coupled (NMC) scalar spectator field in non-oscillatory inflationary scenarios transitioning from inflation to kination domination (KD) in a finite time, while allowing the field dynamics to be fully inhomogeneous and non-linear. Unlike previous work, which often neglected background expansion during the transition or
enforced a rigid equation of state (EOS) of
w=1
during KD, our approach incorporates a precise evolution of this transition from the inflationary phase with
w\gtrsim-1
to KD with
w\lesssim+1
. We employ a CMB-compatible inflaton potential to simulate a realistic, finite-duration transition to KD, enabling us to investigate the tachyonic instability of the NMC field during this phase. Utilizing lattice simulations, we assess the non-linear dynamics of the NMC field, focusing on how its tachyonic growth is regulated. Our findings provide insights into the conditions necessary for efficient reheating, characterizing the pivotal timescales and energy scales involved. In this work we considered two main mechanism for regulating the tachyonic growth of the NMC field:

Gravitational backreaction

Scenarios where the NMC field is either free or contains extremely weak interactions reheat extremely efficiently, typically within two e-folds after the end of inflation. Here, the growth in the NMC field is exponential until its energy density becomes a sub-percentage fraction of the inflaton’s. At that point the NMC field contributions drive the Ricci scalar to zero, hence regularizing the tachyonic instability. We provide approximate analytic expressions describing this scenario in
Eq.

32
, as well as supporting lattice simulations in
Fig.

6
where we are able to self-consistently simulate the full system of equations in
Eqs.

10
,
11
and
12
. In contrast to the self-interaction backreaction case, here the analytic expressions do not accurately capture the reheating time necessitating the use lattice methods.

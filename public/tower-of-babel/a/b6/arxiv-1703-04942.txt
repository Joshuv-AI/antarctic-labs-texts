Abstract
. Tempered fractional derivatives originated from the tempered fractional diffusion equations ( TFDEs ) modeled on the whole space ℝ \mathbb{R} (see [ 23 ] ). For numerically solving TFDEs, two kinds of generalized Laguerre functions were defined and some important properties were proposed to establish the approximate theory. The related prototype tempered fractional differential problems was proposed and solved as the guidance. TFDEs are numerically solved by two domains Laguerre spectral method and the numerical experiments show some properties of the TFDEs and verify the efficiency of the spectral scheme.

1.
Introduction

The normal diffusion equation
\partial_{t}p(x,t)=\partial_{x}^{2}p(x,t)
can be derived from the Brownian motion which describes the particle’s random walks. Over the last few decades, a large body of literature has demonstrated that anomalous diffusion, in which the mean square variance grows faster (super-diffusion) or slower (sub-diffusion) than in a Gaussian process, offers a superior fit to experimental data observed in many important practical applications,
e.g., in physical science
[
14
,
17
,
18
,
19
]
, finance
[
11
,
16
,
25
]
, biology
[
13
,
5
]
and hydrology
[
4
,
8
,
9
]
.
The anomalous diffusion equation takes the form

\partial^{\nu}_{t}p(x,t)=\partial_{x}^{\mu}p(x,t),

(1.1)

where
0<\nu\leq 1
and
0<\mu<2
(cf.
[
17
]
for a review on this subject), whose solution exhibits heavy tails, i.e., power law decays at infinity. In order to ”temper” the power law decay, the authors of
[
23
]
applied an exponential factor
e^{-\lambda|x|}
to the particle jump density, and showed that the Fourier transform of the tempered probability density function
p(x,t)
takes the form

{\mathscr{F}}[p](\omega,t)=e^{-[pA_{+}^{\mu,\lambda}(\omega)+qA_{-}^{\mu,\lambda}(\omega)]Dt},\quad 0<\mu<2,

where
0\leq p\leq 1,~q=1-p
,
D
is a constant and

A_{\pm}^{\mu,\lambda}(\omega):=\begin{cases}(\lambda\pm i\omega)^{\mu}-\lambda^{\mu},&0<\mu<1,\\
(\lambda\pm i\omega)^{\mu}-\lambda^{\mu}-\pm i\omega\mu\lambda^{\mu-1},&1<\mu<2.\end{cases}

Moreover, they defined tempered fractional derivative operators
\partial_{\pm,x}^{\mu,\lambda}
through Fourier transform:
\mathscr{F}{[\partial_{\pm,x}^{\mu,\lambda}u]}(\omega)=A_{\pm}^{\mu,\lambda}(\omega)\mathscr{F}[u](\omega)
, and derived the tempered fractional diffusion equation (
TFDE
):

\partial_{t}u(x,t)=(-1)^{k}C_{T}\{p\partial_{+,x}^{\mu,\lambda}+q\partial_{-,x}^{\mu,\lambda}\}u(x,t),\quad\mu\in(k-1,k),~k=1,2.

(1.2)

It has been argued that tempered anomalous diffusion models have advantages over the normal dissuasion models in some applications of geophysics
[
15
,
31
]
and finance
[
6
]
.

It is a challenging task to numerically solve the tempered fractional diffusion equation (
1.2
), due particularly to (i) the non-local nature of tempered fractional derivatives; and (ii) the unboundedness of the domain. In
[
23
]
, the authors used a finite-difference approach on a truncated domain. In
[
29
]
, the authors considered tempered derivatives on a finite interval and derived an efficient Petrov-Galerkin method for solving tempered fractional ODEs by using the eigenfunctions of tempered fractional Sturm-Liouville problems. In
[
12
]
, the authors used Laguerre functions to approximate the substantial fractional ODEs, which are similar to those we consider in Section 3, on the half line.
In order to avoid the difficulty of assigning boundary conditions at the truncated boundary, we shall deal with the unbounded domain directly in this paper.

Since the tempered factional diffusion equation
is derived from the random walk on the whole line, one is tempted to use Hermite polynomials/functions which are suitable for many problems on the whole line
[
26
]
. Unfortunately,
due to the exponential factor
e^{\lambda|x|}
in the tempered fractional derivatives, Hermite polynomials/functions are not suitable basis functions. Instead,
as we will show in Section 3, properly defined generalized Laguerre functions (
GLFs
) enjoy particularly simple form under the action of tempered fractional derivatives, just as the relations between generalized Jacobi functions and usual fractional derivatives
[
7
]
. Hence, the main goal of this paper is to design efficient spectral methods using
GLFs
to solve the tempered fractional diffusion equation (
1.2
) in various situations.
However, Laguerre polynomials/functions are mutually orthogonal on the half line,
how do we use them to deal with (
1.2
) on the whole line? We shall first consider special cases of (
1.2
) with
p=1,\,q=0
or
p=0,\,q=1
. In these cases, we can reduce
(
1.2
) to the half line, and the
GLFs
can be naturally used. For the general case,
we shall employ a two-domain spectral-element method, and use
GLFs
as basis functions on each subdomain.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we present the definition of the tempered fractional derivatives, and recall some useful properties of Laguerre polynomials. In Sections
3
, we define two classes of generalized Laguerre functions, study their approximation properties, and apply them for solving simple one sided tempered fractional equations. In Section
4
, we develop a spectral-Galerkin method for solving a tempered fractional diffusion equation on the half line. Finally, we present a spectral-Galerkin method for solving the tempered fractional diffusion equation on the whole line in Section
5
. Some concluding remarks are given in the last section.

Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{R}
be respectively the sets of positive integers and real numbers. We further denote

{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:=\{0\}\cup{\mathbb{N}},\;\;{\mathbb{R}}^{+}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}:x>0\},\;\;{\mathbb{R}}^{-}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}:x<0\},\;\;\mathbb{R}_{0}^{\pm}:=\mathbb{R}^{\pm}\cup\{0\}.

(2.1)

2.1.
Usual (non-tempered) fractional integrals and derivatives

Recall the definitions of the fractional integrals and fractional derivatives in the sense of Riemann-Liouville (see e.g.,
[
21
]
).

Definition 2.1

(
Riemann-Liouville fractional integrals and derivatives).

For
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
or
a=-\infty,b=\infty,
and
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+},
the left and right fractional integrals are respectively defined as

{}_{a}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu}u(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\mu)}\int_{a}^{x}\frac{u(y)}{(x-y)^{1-\mu}}{\rm d}y,\;\;{}_{x}{\rm I}_{b}^{\mu}u(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\mu)}\int_{x}^{b}\frac{u(y)}{(y-x)^{1-\mu}}{\rm d}y,\;\;\;x\in\Lambda:=(a,b).

(2.2)

For real
s\in[k-1,k)
with
k\in{\mathbb{N}},
the left-sided Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative
(LRLFD)
of order
s
is defined by

{{}_{a}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s}u(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(k-s)}\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}\int_{a}^{x}\frac{u(y)}{(x-y)^{s-k+1}}{\rm d}y,\;\;\;x\in\Lambda,

(2.3)

and the right-sided Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative
(RRLFD)
of order
s
is defined by

{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{b}^{s}u(x)=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\Gamma(k-s)}\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}\int_{x}^{b}\frac{u(y)}{(y-x)^{s-k+1}}{\rm d}y,\;\;\;x\in\Lambda.

(2.4)

From the above definitions, it is clear that for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{b}^{k}=(-1)^{k}{}{\rm D}^{k},\quad{{}_{a}}{\rm D}_{x}^{k}={}{\rm D}^{k},\;\;\;{\rm where}\;\;\;{}{\rm D}^{k}:=\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}.

(2.5)

Therefore, we can express the RLFD as

{{}_{a}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s}u(x)={}{\rm D}^{k}\big\{{}_{a}{\rm I}_{x}^{k-s}u(x)\big\};\quad\;{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{b}^{s}u(x)=(-1)^{k}{}{\rm D}^{k}\big\{{}_{x}{\rm I}_{b}^{k-s}u(x)\big\}.

(2.6)

According to
[
10
, Thm. 2.14]
, we have that for any finite
a
and any
v\in L^{1}(\Lambda),
and real
s\geq 0,

{{}_{a}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s}\,{}_{a}{\rm I}_{x}^{s}f(x)=f(x),\quad\text{a.e. in}\;\;\Lambda.

(2.7)

Note that by commuting the integral and derivative operators in (
2.6
), we define the Caputo fractional derivatives:

{}_{a}^{C}{\rm D}_{x}^{s}u(x)={}_{a}{\rm I}_{x}^{k-s}\big\{{}{\rm D}^{k}u(x)\big\};\quad\;{}_{x}^{C}{\rm D}_{b}^{s}u(x)=(-1)^{k}{}_{x}{\rm I}_{b}^{k-s}\big\{{}{\rm D}^{k}u(x)\big\}.

(2.8)

For an affine transform
x=\lambda t,~\lambda>0
, on account of

\displaystyle{}_{a}{\rm I}_{t}^{\mu}v(\lambda t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\mu)}\int_{a}^{t}\frac{v(\lambda s)}{(t-s)^{1-\mu}}ds=\frac{\lambda^{-\mu}}{\Gamma(\mu)}\int_{a}^{t}\frac{v(\lambda s)}{(\lambda t-\lambda s)^{1-\mu}}\lambda ds

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{-\mu}}{\Gamma(\mu)}\int_{\lambda a}^{x}\frac{v(y)}{(x-y)^{1-\mu}}dy=\lambda^{-\mu}{}_{\lambda a}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu}v(x),

and
\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}=\lambda\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}x},
we derive from Definition
2.1
that

{}_{a}{\rm I}_{t}^{\mu}v(\lambda t)=\lambda^{-\mu}{}_{\lambda a}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu}v(x),\quad{{}_{a}}{\rm D}_{t}^{s}v(\lambda t)=\lambda^{s}{{}_{\lambda a}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s}v(x),\quad s,\mu,\lambda>0.

(2.9)

Similarly, we have the following identities for the right fractional derivative:

{}_{t}{\rm I}_{b}^{\mu}v(\lambda t)=\lambda^{-\mu}{}_{t}{\rm I}_{\lambda b}^{\mu}v(x),\quad{{}_{t}}{\rm D}_{b}^{s}v(\lambda t)=\lambda^{s}{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\lambda b}^{s}v(x),\quad s,\mu,\lambda>0.

(2.10)

2.2.
Tempered fractional integrals and derivatives on
{\mathbb{R}}

Recently, Sabzikar et al.
[
23
, (19)-(23)]
introduced
the tempered fractional integrals and derivatives on the whole line.

Definition 2.2

(
Tempered fractional integrals).

For
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}_{0}
, the left tempered fractional integral of a suitable function
u(x)
of
order
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
is defined by

{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\mu)}\int_{-\infty}^{x}\frac{e^{-\lambda(x-y)}}{(x-y)^{1-\mu}}\,u(y)\,{\rm d}y,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

(2.11)

and the right tempered fractional integral of order
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
is defined by

{}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\mu)}\int^{\infty}_{x}\frac{e^{-\lambda(y-x)}}{(y-x)^{1-\mu}}\,u(y)\,{\rm d}y,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.12)

It is evident that by (
2.2
) and (
2.11
)-(
2.12
), we have

{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu}={}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu,0},\quad{}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{\mu}={}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{\mu,0},

(2.13)

and

{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)=e^{-\lambda x}{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu}\big\{e^{\lambda x}u(x)\big\},\quad{}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)=e^{\lambda x}{}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{\mu}\big\{e^{-\lambda x}u(x)\big\}.

(2.14)

As shown in
[
23
]
, the tempered fractional derivative can be characterized by its Fourier transform. Recall that, for any
u\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),
its Fourier transform and inverse Fourier transform are defined by

{\mathscr{F}}[u](\omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u(x)e^{-{\rm i}\omega x}\,{\rm d}x;\quad u(x)={\mathscr{F}}^{-1}\big[{\mathscr{F}}[u](\omega)\big](x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\mathscr{F}}[u](\omega)e^{{\rm i}\omega x}\,{\rm d}\omega.

(2.15)

There holds the well-known Parseval’s identity:

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}u(x)\,\bar{v}(x)\,{\rm d}x=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\mathscr{F}}[u](\omega)\,\overline{{\mathscr{F}}[v]}(\omega)\,{\rm d}\omega,

(2.16)

where
\bar{v}
is the complex conjugate of
v
.
Let
H(x)
be the Heaviside function, i.e.,
H(x)=1
for
x\geq 0,
and vanishing for all
x<0.
Then we can reformulate the left tempered fractional integral as

\begin{split}{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)&=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\mu)}\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\mu-1}e^{-\lambda y}u(x-y)\,{\rm d}y=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\mu)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}y^{\mu-1}e^{-\lambda y}H(y)\,u(x-y)\,{\rm d}y\\
&=\big({K}\ast u\big)(x),\quad{\rm where}\;\;\;{K}(x):=x^{\mu-1}e^{-\lambda x}H(x)/{\Gamma(\mu)}.\end{split}

(2.17)

Note that
{K}(x)
is related to the particle jump density
(cf.
[
23
, (8)]
).
Using the formula:
{\mathscr{F}}[K](\omega)={(\lambda+{\rm i}\omega)^{-\mu}},
and the convolution property of Fourier transform (see, e.g.,
[
24
,
27
]
), we derive

{\mathscr{F}}[{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u](\omega)={\mathscr{F}}[K\ast u](\omega)={\mathscr{F}}[K](\omega)\,{\mathscr{F}}[u](\omega)={(\lambda+{\rm i}\omega)^{-\mu}}{\mathscr{F}}[u](\omega).

(2.18)

Similarly, the Fourier transform of the right tempered fractional integral is

{\mathscr{F}}[{}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}u](\omega)={(\lambda-{\rm i}\omega)^{-\mu}}{\mathscr{F}}[u](\omega).

(2.19)

In view of (
2.18
)-(
2.19
), Sabzikar et al.
[
23
]
then introduced the left and right tempered fractional derivatives as follows.

Definition 2.3

(
Tempered fractional derivatives).

For
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}_{0}
, the left and right tempered fractional derivatives of order
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
of a suitable function
u(x),
are defined by

{\mathscr{F}}\big[{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u\big](\omega)=(\lambda+{\rm i}\omega)^{\mu}{\mathscr{F}}[u](\omega),\quad{\mathscr{F}}\big[{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}u\big](\omega)=(\lambda-{\rm i}\omega)^{\mu}{\mathscr{F}}[u](\omega),

(2.20)

that is, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\big[{(\lambda+{\rm i}\omega)^{\mu}}{\mathscr{F}}[u](\omega)\big](x),\quad{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\big[{(\lambda-{\rm i}\omega)^{\mu}}{\mathscr{F}}[u](\omega)\big](x).

(2.21)

Introduce the space

W^{\mu,2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}):=\Big\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\,:~\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\lambda^{2}+\omega^{2})^{\mu}\,\big|\mathscr{F}[u](\omega)\big|^{2}{\rm d}\omega<\infty\Big\},\quad\mu,\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}.

(2.22)

Thanks to the Parseval’s identity (
2.16
), the above tempered fractional derivatives are well-defined for any
u\in W^{\mu,2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}).
Moreover, one verifies from (
2.18
)-(
2.21
) that

\displaystyle

\displaystyle{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)=u(x),\;\;\;\;{}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)=u(x),\quad\forall\,u\in W^{\mu,2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R});\;\;

(2.23)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)=u(x),\;\;\;\;{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}{}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)=u(x),\quad\forall\,u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Similar to (
2.14
), we have the following explicit representations.

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
u\in W^{\mu,2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}),
with

\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}_{0}
, the left and right tempered fractional derivatives of order
\mu\in[k-1,k)
with
k\in{\mathbb{N}},
have the explicit representations:

{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)=e^{-\lambda x}{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu}\big\{e^{\lambda x}u(x)\big\},\quad{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)=e^{\lambda x}{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu}\big\{e^{-\lambda x}u(x)\big\},

(2.24)

where
{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu}
and
{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu}
are the Riemann-Liouville fractional derivative operators in Definition
2.1
.
Alternatively, we have

\begin{split}{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)&=({}{\rm D}+\lambda)^{k}\,\big\{{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{k-\mu,\lambda}u(x)\big\}=({}{\rm D}+\lambda)^{k}\,\big\{e^{-\lambda x}{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{k-\mu}\big\{e^{\lambda x}u(x)\big\}\big\};\\
{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)&=({}{\rm D}-\lambda)^{k}\,\big\{{}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{k-\mu}u(x)\big\}=({}{\rm D}-\lambda)^{k}\,\big\{e^{\lambda x}{}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{k-\mu}\big\{e^{-\lambda x}u(x)\big\}\big\}.\end{split}

(2.25)

Proof.

Using the properties of Fourier transform:

{\mathscr{F}}[e^{-\lambda x}D^{k}v]=(\lambda+{\rm i}\omega)^{k}{\mathscr{F}}[e^{-\lambda x}v],
and

\begin{split}{\mathscr{F}}\big[e^{-\lambda x}&{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu}\big\{e^{\lambda x}u(x)\big\}\big](\omega)={\mathscr{F}}\big[e^{-\lambda x}{}{\rm D}^{k}{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{k-\mu}\big\{e^{\lambda x}u(x)\big\}\big](\omega)\\
&=(\lambda+{\rm i}\omega)^{k}{\mathscr{F}}\big[e^{-\lambda x}{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{k-\mu}\big\{e^{\lambda x}u(x)\big\}\big](\omega)\\
&=(\lambda+{\rm i}\omega)^{k}{\mathscr{F}}\big[{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{k-\mu,\lambda}u(x)\big](\omega)=(\lambda+{\rm i}\omega)^{\mu}{\mathscr{F}}[u](\omega).\end{split}

(2.26)

This verifies (
2.20
)-(
2.21
).
Similarly, we can derive the second representation of
{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}u
in (
2.24
).

The alternative form (
2.25
) can be derived by induction. Here we only
verify the left tempered derivative.

•

For
\mu\in[0,1)
, we derive from (

\displaystyle{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda x}{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu}\big\{e^{\lambda x}u(x)\big\}=e^{-\lambda x}\,{\rm D}{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{1-\mu}\big\{e^{\lambda x}u(x)\big\}

\displaystyle=e^{-\lambda x}\,{\rm D}\big[e^{\lambda x}{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{1-\mu,\lambda}\big\{e^{\lambda x}u(x)\big\}\big]=({\rm D}+\lambda)\big\{{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{1-\mu,\lambda}\big\{e^{\lambda x}u(x)\big\}\big\},

Lemma 2.1
.

Given
\lambda>0
and
\mu\in[k-1,k),~k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the tempered fractional derivative

{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)={{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{k,\lambda}{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{k-\mu,\lambda}u(x),\quad{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x)={{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{k,\lambda}{}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{k-\mu,\lambda}u(x).

(2.27)

In addition, we have

\displaystyle{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu+\nu,\lambda}u(x)={}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{\nu,\lambda}u(x),\quad\quad{}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{\mu+\nu,\lambda}u(x)={}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}{}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{\nu,\lambda}u(x),

(2.28)

\displaystyle{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu+\nu,\lambda}u(x)={{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\nu,\lambda}u(x),\quad{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu+\nu,\lambda}u(x)={{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\nu,\lambda}u(x),

(2.29)

\displaystyle({{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u,v)=(u,{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}v),\qquad\qquad\qquad({{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}u,v)=(u,{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}v),

(2.30)

where
\mu,\nu\geq 0
.

Remark 2.1
.

For a suitable function
f(x),~x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, its reflection
g(y)=f(-y),~y\in\mathbb{R}^{-}
satisfies

\displaystyle{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{y}^{\mu,\lambda}g(y)=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{-\lambda y}}{\Gamma(\mu)}\int_{-\infty}^{y}e^{\lambda\tau}(y-\tau)^{\mu-1}f(-\tau){\rm d}\tau\overset{t=-\tau}{=}\frac{e^{-\lambda y}}{{\Gamma(\mu)}}\int^{\infty}_{-y}e^{-\lambda t}(y+t)^{\mu-1}f(t)\rm{\rm d}t

(2.31)

\displaystyle\overset{x=-y}{=}

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\lambda x}}{{\Gamma(\mu)}}\int^{\infty}_{x}e^{-\lambda t}(t-x)^{\mu-1}f(t){\rm d}t={}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}f(x).

Hence, we can use (

) and derivative relation
\dfrac{{\rm d}^{k}}{{\rm d}y^{k}}=(-1)^{k}\dfrac{{\rm d}^{k}}{{\rm d}x^{k}}
to obtain the tempered derivative relation

{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{y}^{\mu,\lambda}f(-y)={{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}f(x),\quad y=-x,~x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(2.32)

2.3.
Laguerre polynomials and some useful formulas

For any
a\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},
we recall that the rising factorial in the Pochhammer symbol and the Gamma function have the relation:

(a)_{0}=1;\;\;\;(a)_{j}:=a(a+1)\cdots(a+j-1)=\frac{\Gamma(a+j)}{\Gamma(a)},\;\;{\rm for}\;\;j\geq 1.

(2.33)

Recall the hypergeometric function (cf.
[
1
]
):

{}_{1}F_{1}(a;b;x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{j}}{(b)_{j}}\frac{x^{j}}{j!},\;\;\;a,b,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+},\;\;-b\not\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

(2.34)

If
b-a>0,
then
{}_{1}F_{1}(a;b;x)
is absolutely convergent for all
x\in\mathbb{R}.
If
a
is a negative integer, then it reduces to a polynomial.

The Laguerre polynomial
with parameter
\alpha>-1
is defined as in Szegö
[
28
, (5.3.3)]
:

L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)=\frac{(\alpha+1)_{n}}{n!}~{}_{1}F_{1}\big(-n;\alpha+1;x\big),\;\;\;n\geq 1,\;\;x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+},

(2.35)

and
L_{0}^{(\alpha)}(x)\equiv 1.

Note that

L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(0)=\dfrac{(\alpha+1)_{n}}{n!},

(2.36)

and the Laguerre polynomials (with
\alpha>-1
) are orthogonal with respect to the weight function
x^{\alpha}e^{-x},
namely,

\int_{0}^{\infty}{L}_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)~{L}_{m}^{(\alpha)}(x)~x^{\alpha}e^{-x}\,{\rm d}x=\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}\,\delta_{mn},\quad\gamma_{n}^{\alpha}=\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)}.

(2.37)

They are eigenfunctions of the Sturm-Liouville problem:

x^{-\alpha}e^{x}\partial_{x}\big(x^{\alpha+1}e^{-x}\partial_{x}L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)\big)+\lambda_{n}L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)=0,\quad\lambda_{n}=n.

(2.38)

We have the following relations:

L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)=\partial_{x}L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)-\partial_{x}L_{n+1}^{(\alpha)}(x),

(2.39)

x\partial_{x}L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)=nL_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)-(n+\alpha)L_{n-1}^{(\alpha)}(x),

(2.40)

\partial_{x}L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)=-L_{n-1}^{(\alpha+1)}(x)=-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}L_{k}^{(\alpha)}(x).

(2.41)

In particular, for
\alpha=-k,~k=1,2,\ldots
(See Szegö
[
28
, (5.2.1)]
),

L_{n}^{(-k)}(x)=(-1)^{k}\frac{\Gamma(n-k+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)}x^{k}L_{n-k}^{(k)}(x),\quad n\geq k.

For notational convenience, we denote

{h}_{n}^{a,b}:=\frac{\Gamma(n+1+a)}{\Gamma(n+1+a-b)}.

(2.42)

We present below some formulas related to Laguerre polynomials and fractional integrals and derivatives, which play an important role in the algorithm development and analysis later. We provide their derivations in Appendix
A
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+},
we have

\displaystyle{}_{0}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu}\{x^{\alpha}L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)\}=h^{\alpha,-\mu}_{n}\,x^{\alpha+\mu}L_{n}^{(\alpha+\mu)}(x),\quad\alpha>-1;

(2.43)

\displaystyle{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu}\{x^{\alpha}L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)\}=h^{\alpha,\mu}_{n}\,x^{\alpha-\mu}L_{n}^{(\alpha-\mu)}(x),\quad\alpha>\mu-1,

(2.44)

and

\displaystyle{}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{\mu}\{e^{-x}L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)\}=e^{-x}L_{n}^{(\alpha-\mu)}(x),\quad\alpha>\mu-1;

(2.45)

\displaystyle{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu}\{e^{-x}L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)\}=e^{-x}L_{n}^{(\alpha+\mu)}(x),\quad\alpha>-1.

(2.46)

Moreover, we have that for
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha>k-1
,

{}{\rm D}^{k}\big\{x^{\alpha}e^{-x}L_{n}^{(\alpha)}(x)\big\}=\frac{\Gamma(n+k+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)}x^{\alpha-k}L^{(\alpha-k)}_{n+k}(x)e^{-x}.

(2.47)

3.
Generalized Laguerre functions

In this section, we introduce the generalized Laguerre functions (GLFs), and study its approximation properties.
In what follows, the operators
{}_{0}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda},{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}
on the half line should be understood as
0
in place of
-\infty
in (
2.11
) and
(
2.24
)-(
2.25
).

3.1.
Definition and properties

We first introduce the GJFs and their associated properties related to tempered fractional integrals/derivatives.

Definition 3.1

(
GLFs).

For real
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda>0,
we define the GLFs as

{\mathcal{L}}^{(\alpha,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}:=\begin{cases}x^{-\alpha}e^{-\lambda x}\,{L}_{n}^{(-\alpha)}(2\lambda x),\quad\alpha<0,\\
e^{-\lambda x}\,{L}_{n}^{(\alpha)}(2\lambda x),\qquad\quad\,\alpha\geq 0\end{cases}

(3.1)

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

Remark 3.1
.

It’s noteworthy that Zhang and Guo
[
30
]
introduce the GJFs

\widetilde{\mathscr{L}}_{l}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)=\begin{cases}x^{-\alpha}e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}x}L^{(-\alpha)}(\beta x),\quad&\alpha\leq-1,\;\;l\geq\bar{l}_{\alpha}=[-\alpha],\\
e^{-\frac{\beta}{2}x}L_{l}^{(\alpha)}(x),&\alpha>-1,\;\;l\geq\bar{l}_{\alpha}=0,\end{cases}

(3.2)

where the scaling factor
\beta>0.
It is seen that
we modified the definition in the range of
0<\alpha<1
(with
\beta=2\lambda
). This turns out to be essential for the numerical solution of FDEs of order
\mu\in(0,1),
as we shall see in the subsequent sections.
∎

We next present the basic properties of GLFs.
Firstly, one verifies readily from the orthogonality (
2.37
) and Definition
3.1
that for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
\lambda>0,

\int_{0}^{\infty}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\alpha,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\alpha,\lambda)}_{m}{(x)}\,x^{\alpha}dx=\gamma_{n}^{{|\alpha|},\lambda}\delta_{nm},\;\;\;\gamma_{n}^{|\alpha|,\lambda}=\frac{\gamma_{n}^{|\alpha|}}{(2\lambda)^{|\alpha|+1}},

(3.3)

where
\gamma_{n}^{|\alpha|}
is defined in (
2.37
).

We have the following important (left) “tempered” fractional integral and derivative rules.

Lemma 3.1
.

For
\mu,\nu,\lambda,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}_{0},
we have

\displaystyle{}_{0}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}=h^{\nu,-\mu}_{n}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu-\mu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)},

(3.4)

\displaystyle{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}=h^{\nu,\mu}_{n}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\mu-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)},\quad\nu\geq\mu,

(3.5)

and

{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu+k,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\mu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}=(-2\lambda)^{k}h^{\mu,\mu}_{n}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k,\lambda)}_{n-k}{(x)},\quad n\geq k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

(3.6)

where
h^{a,b}_{n}
is defined in (
2.42
).

Proof.

We obtain from (
2.11
) and
(
2.24
)-(
2.25
) (with replacing
-\infty
by
0
)
that

{}_{0}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}=e^{-\lambda x}{}_{0}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu}\{e^{\lambda x}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}\}=e^{-\lambda x}{}_{0}{\rm I}_{x}^{\mu}\{x^{\nu}L^{(\nu)}_{n}(2\lambda x)\},

and

{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}=e^{-\lambda x}{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu}\{e^{\lambda x}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}\}=e^{-\lambda x}{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu}\{x^{\nu}L^{(\nu)}_{n}(2\lambda x)\}.

Thus, from (
2.9
) and Lemma
2.2
, we obtain (
3.4
)-(
3.5
).

Using (
3.5
) and the derivative relation (
2.41
) (with
\alpha=\mu
), we obtain

\displaystyle{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu+k,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\mu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}

\displaystyle={{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{k,\lambda}{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\mu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}=e^{-\lambda x}{}{\rm D}^{k}\big\{e^{\lambda x}h^{\mu,\mu}_{n}{\mathcal{L}}^{(0,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}\big\}

\displaystyle=h^{\mu,\mu}_{n}\,e^{-\lambda x}{}{\rm D}^{k}\big\{L_{n}^{(0)}(2\lambda x)\big\}=(-2\lambda)^{k}h^{\mu,\mu}_{n}\,L_{n-k}^{(k)}(2\lambda x)\,e^{-\lambda x}.

This leads to (
3.6
).
∎

Similarly, we have the following rules of the (right) “tempered” fractional integrals and derivatives.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
\mu,\nu,\lambda,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\quad{}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}=(2\lambda)^{-\mu}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\nu-\mu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)},\quad\nu\geq\mu,

(3.7)

\displaystyle{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}=(2\lambda)^{\mu}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\mu+\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}.

(3.8)

Proof.

Identities (
3.7
) and (
3.8
) can be easily derived from (
2.9
), (
2.10
) and Lemma
2.2
. ∎

We highlight the fractional derivative formulas, which play an important role in the forthcoming algorithm and analysis.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
k-\nu\leq 0
,

\displaystyle{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{k,\lambda}\big\{{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}\big\}=\frac{\Gamma(n+\nu+1)}{\Gamma(n+\nu-k+1)}~{\mathcal{L}}^{(k-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)},

(3.9)

\qquad{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{k,\lambda}\big\{{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}\big\}=(-1)^{k}\frac{\Gamma(n+k+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)}{\mathcal{L}}^{(k-\nu,\lambda)}_{n+k}{(x)}.\qquad

(3.10)

Proof.

From Lemma
2.2

and relations

{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{k,\lambda}u=e^{-\lambda x}{}{\rm D}^{k}\big\{e^{\lambda x}u\big\},\quad{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{k,\lambda}u=e^{\lambda x}(-1)^{k}{}{\rm D}^{k}\big\{e^{-\lambda x}u\big\},

(3.11)

we obtain that for
k-\nu\leq 0
,

\displaystyle{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{k,\lambda}

\displaystyle\big\{x^{\nu}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}\big\}=e^{-\lambda x}{}{\rm D}^{k}\big\{(2\lambda)^{-\nu}(2\lambda x)^{\nu}L^{(\nu)}_{n}(2\lambda x)\big\}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{lagupolyderi}}{=}

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(n+1+\nu)}{\Gamma(n+\nu-k+1)}x^{\nu-k}L^{(\nu-k)}_{n}(2\lambda x)e^{-\lambda x}=\frac{\Gamma(n+1+\nu)}{\Gamma(n+\nu-k+1)}~{\mathcal{L}}^{(k-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)},

and

\displaystyle{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{k,\lambda}\big\{x^{\nu}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}\big\}=

\displaystyle e^{\lambda x}(-1)^{k}{}{\rm D}^{k}\big\{(2\lambda)^{-\nu}(2\lambda x)^{\nu}L^{(\nu)}_{n}(2\lambda x)e^{-2\lambda x}\big\}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{lagufunderi}}{=}

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\frac{\Gamma(n+k+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)}x^{\nu-k}L^{(\nu-k)}_{n+k}(2\lambda x)e^{-\lambda x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{k}\frac{\Gamma(n+k+1)}{\Gamma(n+1)}~{\mathcal{L}}^{(k-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}.

This ends the proof.
∎

Another attractive property of GLFs is that they are eigenfunctions of Sturm-Liouville problem.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
s,\nu,x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Then,

\qquad x^{\nu}{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{s,\lambda}\{x^{s-\nu}{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}\,{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}\}=\lambda_{n,-}^{s,\nu}\,{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)},\quad\nu-s\geq 0,

(3.13)

and

x^{-\nu}{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}\{x^{s+\nu}{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{s,\lambda}\,{\mathcal{L}}^{(\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}\}=\lambda_{n,+}^{s,\nu}\,{\mathcal{L}}^{(\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)},\qquad\qquad\,

(3.14)

where the corresponding eigenvalues
\lambda_{n,-}^{s,\nu}=(2\lambda)^{s}h_{n}^{\nu,s}
and
\lambda_{n,+}^{s,\nu}=(2\lambda)^{s}h_{n}^{\nu+s,s}
.

Proof.

Due to (
3.5
) and (
3.8
),

{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}=h^{\nu,s}_{n}{\mathcal{L}}^{(s-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)},\quad{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{s,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\nu-s,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}=(2\lambda)^{s}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}.

It’s straightforward to obtain that

\displaystyle x^{\nu}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{s,\lambda}\{x^{s-\nu}{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}\,{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}\}=x^{\nu}{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{s,\lambda}\{x^{s-\nu}{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}\}

\displaystyle=h^{\nu,s}_{n}x^{\nu}{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{s,\lambda}\{x^{s-\nu}{\mathcal{L}}^{(s-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}\}=h^{\nu,s}_{n}x^{\nu}{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{s,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\nu-s,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}=(2\lambda)^{s}h_{n}^{\nu,s}\,{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle x^{-\nu}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}\{x^{s+\nu}{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{s,\lambda}\,{\mathcal{L}}^{(\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}\}=x^{-\nu}{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}\{x^{s+\nu}{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{s,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}\}

\displaystyle=(2\lambda)^{s}x^{-\nu}{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}\{x^{s+\nu}{\mathcal{L}}^{(s+\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}\}=(2\lambda)^{s}x^{-\nu}{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu-s,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}=(2\lambda)^{s}h_{n}^{\nu+s,s}\,{\mathcal{L}}^{(\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}.

This ends the derivation.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The above results can be viewed as an extension of the standard Sturm-Liouville problem of generalized Laguerre functions (cf. (
2.38
)) to the tempered fractional derivative. We derive immediately from (
3.13
), (
3.14
) and the Stirling’s formula (see (
3.23
)) that for fixed
s
and
\nu
,

\lambda_{n,-}^{s,\nu}=\lambda_{n,+}^{s,\nu}=O\big((2\lambda n)^{s}\big),\quad n\gg 1.

When
s\rightarrow 1
and
\lambda=1/2
, it recovers the
O(n)
growth of eigenvalues of the standard Sturm-Liouville problem.∎

3.2.
Approximation by GLFs

3.2.1.
Approximation by
\big\{{\mathcal{L}}^{(\alpha,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}:~\alpha=-\nu<0\big\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.

Denote by
\mathcal{P}_{N}
the set of all polynomials of degree at most
N
, and define the finite dimensional
space

\mathcal{F}^{\nu,\lambda}_{N}({\mathbb{R}}^{+}):=\big\{x^{\nu}e^{-\lambda x}p(x)\,:\,p\in\mathcal{P}_{N}\big\},\quad N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

(3.15)

Define the
L_{\omega}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{+})
with the inner product and norm:

(f,g)_{\omega}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{+}}f\,\bar{g}\,\omega\,{\rm d}x,\quad\|f\|_{\omega}^{2}=(f,f)_{\omega},

(3.16)

where
\omega(x)
be a generic weight function and
\bar{g}
is the conjugate of the function
g
.
In particular, we omit
\omega
when
\omega\equiv 1.

To characterize the approximation errors, we define the non-uniformly weighted Sobolev space

A_{\nu,\lambda}^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{+}):=\Big\{u\in L^{2}_{\omega^{-\nu}}({\mathbb{R}}^{+}):{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\nu+k,\lambda}u\in L^{2}_{\omega^{k}}({\mathbb{R}}^{+}),\;k=0,\cdots,m\Big\},\quad m\in\mathbb{N}_{0},

(3.17)

equipped with the norm and semi-norm

\|u\|_{A_{\nu,\lambda}^{m}}:=\Big(\|u\|^{2}_{\omega^{-\nu}}+\sum_{k=0}^{m}\|{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\nu+k,\lambda}u\|^{2}_{\omega^{k}}\Big)^{{1}/{2}},\quad|u|_{A_{\nu,\lambda}^{m}}:=\|{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\nu+m,\lambda}u\|_{\omega^{m}},

(3.18)

where the weight function
\omega^{a}(x)=x^{a}.

Consider the orthogonal projection
\pi_{N}^{-\nu,\lambda}:\,{L^{2}_{\omega^{-\nu}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{+})\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{\nu,\lambda}_{N}({\mathbb{R}}^{+})
defined by

(\pi_{N}^{-\nu,\lambda}u-u,~\phi)_{\omega^{-\nu}}=0,\quad\forall\phi\in\mathcal{F}^{\nu,\lambda}_{N}({\mathbb{R}}^{+}).

(3.19)

Then, by the orthogonality (
3.3
),
u
and its
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection can be expanded as

u(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}~\hat{u}_{n}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)},\quad(\pi_{N}^{-\nu,\lambda}u)(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}~\hat{u}_{n}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)},

(3.20)

where

\hat{u}_{n}=\big(u,~{\mathcal{L}}_{n}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}\big)_{\omega^{-\nu}}\big/\gamma^{\nu,\lambda}_{n}.

Theorem 3.3
.

For
\lambda,\nu>0
, we have that for any
u\in A^{m}_{\nu,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
with
m\leq N+1
,

\|\pi_{N}^{-\nu,\lambda}u-u\|_{\omega^{-\nu}}\leq~c~(2\lambda N)^{-\frac{\nu+m}{2}}~\|{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\nu+m,\lambda}u\|_{\omega^{m}},

(3.21)

and for any
k\leq m,

\big\|{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\nu+k,\lambda}\big(\pi_{N}^{-\nu,\lambda}u-u\big)\big\|_{\omega^{k}}\leq~c~({2\lambda}N)^{\frac{k-m}{2}}~\|{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\nu+m,\lambda}u\|_{\omega^{m}},

(3.22)

where
c\approx 1
for large
N.

Proof.

By (
3.20
), we have

(u-\pi_{N}^{-\nu,\lambda}u)(x)=\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\,\hat{u}_{n}\,{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}.

By the orthogonality (
3.3
) and (
3.6
),

\big\|{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\nu+k,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{}\big\|^{2}_{\omega^{k}}=(-2\lambda)^{2k}\,(h^{\nu,\nu}_{n})^{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(L^{(k)}_{n-k}(2\lambda x)\big)^{2}\,e^{-2\lambda x}{\omega^{k}}(x){\rm d}x=(d_{n,k}^{\nu,\lambda})^{2}\gamma_{n-k}^{k,\lambda},

where we denoted
d_{n,k}^{\nu,\lambda}:=(2\lambda)^{k}\,h^{\nu,\nu}_{n}
and used the fact:

\int_{0}^{\infty}L^{(m)}_{n-m}(2\lambda x)L^{(k)}_{n-k}(2\lambda x)\,e^{-2\lambda x}\omega^{k}dx=\frac{\gamma_{n-k}^{k}}{(2\lambda)^{k+1}}\delta_{km}=\gamma_{n-k}^{k,\lambda}\delta_{km},

Thus we can obtain

\displaystyle\|\pi_{N}^{-\nu,\lambda}u-u\|^{2}_{\omega^{-\nu}}=\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}(\hat{u}_{n})^{2}\gamma_{n}^{\nu,\lambda},\quad\big|\pi_{N}^{-\nu,\lambda}u-u\big|^{2}_{A^{k}_{\nu,\lambda}}=\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}(\hat{u}_{n}d_{n,k}^{\nu,\lambda})^{2}\gamma_{n-k}^{k,\lambda},

\displaystyle\big|u\big|^{2}_{A^{m}_{\nu,\lambda}}=\sum_{n=m}^{\infty}(\hat{u}_{n}d_{n,m}^{\nu,\lambda})^{2}\gamma_{n-m}^{m,\lambda}.

Then one verifies readily that

\displaystyle\|\pi_{N}^{-\nu,\lambda}u-u\|^{2}_{\omega^{-\nu}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\gamma_{N+1}^{\nu,\lambda}}{(d_{N+1,m}^{\nu,\lambda})^{2}\gamma_{N+1-m}^{m,\lambda}}\big|u\big|^{2}_{A^{m}_{\nu,\lambda}},

\displaystyle\quad\big|\pi_{N}^{-\nu,\lambda}u-u\big|^{2}_{A^{k}_{\nu,\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{d_{N+1,k}^{\nu,\lambda}}{d_{N+1,m}^{\nu,\lambda}}\right)^{2}\frac{\gamma_{N+1-k}^{k,\lambda}}{\gamma_{N+1-m}^{m,\lambda}}\big|u\big|^{2}_{A^{m}_{\nu,\lambda}}.

Recall the property of the Gamma function (see
[
1
, (6.1.38)]
):

\Gamma(x+1)=\sqrt{2\pi}x^{x+1/2}\exp\Big(-x+\frac{\theta}{12x}\Big),\quad\forall\,x>0,\;\;0<\theta<1.

(3.23)

One can then obtain that for any constants
a,b,
and for
n\geq 1,

n+a>1
and
n+b>1,

\frac{\Gamma(n+a)}{\Gamma(n+b)}\leq\nu_{n}^{a,b}n^{a-b},

(3.24)

where

\nu_{n}^{a,b}=\exp\Big(\frac{a-b}{2(n+b-1)}+\frac{1}{12(n+a-1)}+\frac{(a-b)^{2}}{n}\Big).

(3.25)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{\gamma_{N+1}^{\nu,\lambda}}{(d_{N+1,m}^{\nu,\lambda})^{2}\gamma_{N+1-m}^{m,\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(N+2-m)}{(2\lambda)^{\nu+m}\Gamma(N+2+\nu)}\leq(2\lambda)^{-\nu-m}\nu_{n}^{2-m,2+\nu}N^{-\nu-m},

\displaystyle\left(\frac{d_{N+1,k}^{\nu,\lambda}}{d_{N+1,m}^{\nu,\lambda}}\right)^{2}\frac{\gamma_{N+1-k}^{k,\lambda}}{\gamma_{N+1-m}^{m,\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\lambda)^{k}\Gamma(N+2-m)}{(2\lambda)^{m}\Gamma(N+2-k)}<(2\lambda)^{k-m}\nu_{n}^{2-m,2-k}N^{k-m},

where
\nu_{n}^{2-m,2+\nu}\approx 1
and
\nu_{n}^{2-m,2-k}\approx 1
for fixed
m
and
n\geq N\gg 1
.
Then (
3.21
)-(
3.22
) follow.
∎

3.2.2.
Approximation by
\big\{{\mathcal{L}}^{(\alpha,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}:~\alpha=\nu\geq 0\big\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.

Introduce the non-uniformly weighted Sobolev space:

B_{\nu,\lambda}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{+}):=\Big\{u\in L^{2}_{\omega^{\nu}}({\mathbb{R}}^{+}):{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{s,\lambda}u\in L^{2}_{\omega^{\nu+s}}({\mathbb{R}}^{+}),\;0\leq s\leq r\Big\},\quad r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}_{0},

(3.26)

endowed with norm and semi-norm

\|u\|_{B_{\nu,\lambda}^{r}}:=\Big(\|u\|^{2}_{\omega^{\nu}}+|u|^{2}_{B_{\nu,\lambda}^{r}}\Big)^{{1}/{2}},\quad|u|_{B_{\nu}^{r}}:=\|{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\nu+r,\lambda}u\|_{\omega^{\nu+r}}.

(3.27)

Consider
the orthogonal projection
\Pi_{N}^{\nu,\lambda}:~{L^{2}_{\omega^{\nu}}}({\mathbb{R}}^{+})\rightarrow\mathcal{F}^{0,\lambda}_{N}({\mathbb{R}}^{+}),
defined by

\big(\Pi_{N}^{\nu,\lambda}u-u,~\phi\big)_{\omega^{\nu}}=0,\quad\forall\phi\in\mathcal{F}^{0,\lambda}_{N}({\mathbb{R}}^{+}),\quad\nu>-1,

(3.28)

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\lambda,r,\nu>0
. For any
u\in B^{r}_{\nu,\lambda}({\mathbb{R}}^{+})
with
0\leq s\leq r\leq N,
we have

\big\|{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{s,\lambda}\big\{\Pi_{N}^{\nu,\lambda}u-u\big\}\big\|_{\omega^{\nu+s}}\leq~c~({2\lambda}{N})^{\frac{s-r}{2}}~\|{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{r,\lambda}u\|_{\omega^{\nu+r}},

(3.29)

where
c\approx 1
for large
N.

Proof.

Note that by definition,

u-\Pi_{N}^{\nu,\lambda}u=\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}~\hat{u}_{n}{\mathcal{L}}_{n}^{(\nu,\lambda)}(x),\quad\hat{u}_{n}=\big(u,~{\mathcal{L}}_{n}^{(\nu,\lambda)}\big)_{\omega^{\nu}}\big/\gamma^{\nu,\lambda}_{n}.

Then by (
3.8
), and the orthgogonality,

\|{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{s,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{}\|^{2}_{\omega^{\nu+s}}=\|{\mathcal{L}}^{(\nu+s,\lambda)}_{n}{}\|^{2}_{\omega^{\nu+s}}=\gamma_{n}^{\nu+s,\lambda},

we can derive

\displaystyle\big|\Pi_{N}^{\nu,\lambda}u-u\big|^{2}_{B^{s}_{\nu,\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}\hat{u}_{n}(2\lambda)^{s}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\nu+s,\lambda)}_{n}{}\|^{2}_{\omega^{\nu+s}}=\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}(\hat{u}_{n})^{2}(2\lambda)^{2s}\gamma_{n}^{\nu+s,\lambda},

\displaystyle\big|u\big|^{2}_{B^{r}_{\nu+r,\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\hat{u}_{n}(2\lambda)^{r}{\mathcal{L}}^{(\nu+r,\lambda)}_{n}{\|}^{2}_{\omega^{\nu+r}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\hat{u}_{n})^{2}(2\lambda)^{2r}\gamma_{n}^{\nu+r,\lambda}.

Then,

\displaystyle\big|\Pi_{N}^{\nu,\lambda}u-u\big|^{2}_{B^{s}_{\nu,\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}(\hat{u}_{n})^{2}\gamma_{n}^{\nu+s,\lambda}\leq(2\lambda)^{2s-2r}\frac{\gamma_{N+1}^{\nu+s,\lambda}}{\gamma_{N+1}^{\nu+r,\lambda}}\sum_{n=N+1}^{\infty}(\hat{u}_{n})^{2}\gamma_{n}^{\nu+r,\lambda},

where by (
3.24
)-(
3.25
), we obtain

\dfrac{\gamma_{N+1}^{\nu+s,\lambda}}{\gamma_{N+1}^{\nu+r,\lambda}}=\dfrac{(2\lambda)^{r}\Gamma(N+\nu+s+2)}{(2\lambda)^{s}\Gamma(N+\nu+r+2)}\leq c({2\lambda})^{r-s}N^{s-r}.

Consequently, we have

\|{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{s,\lambda}\big\{\Pi_{N}^{\nu,\lambda}u-u\big\}\|_{\omega^{\nu+s}}\leq~c\,({2\lambda}N)^{\frac{s-r}{2}}\,|u|_{B^{r}_{\nu,\lambda}}.

This ends the proof.
∎

3.3.
A model problem and numerical results

In what follows, we consider the GLF approximation to a model tempered fractional equation of order

s\in[k-1,k)
with
k\in\mathbb{N}:

{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}u(x)=f(x),\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\;\;\lambda>0;\;\;u^{(j)}(0)=0,\quad j=0,1,\ldots,k-1,

(3.30)

where
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
is a given function.
Using the fractional derivative relation (
2.7
), one can find

u(x)={}_{0}{\rm I}_{x}^{s,\lambda}f(x)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}c_{i}\,x^{s-i}e^{-\lambda x},

where
\{c_{i}\}
can be determined by the conditions at
x=0.

In fact, we have all
c_{i}=0,
and

u(x)={}_{0}{\rm I}_{x}^{s,\lambda}f(x)=\frac{e^{-\lambda x}}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{x}(x-\tau)^{s-1}e^{\lambda\tau}f(\tau){\rm d}\tau=\frac{x^{s}}{\Gamma(s)}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)^{s-1}e^{-\lambda(1-t)x}f(xt){\rm d}t.

(3.31)

We see that if
f(x)
is smooth, then
u(x)=x^{s}F(x),
where
F(x)
is smoother than
f(x).

With this understanding, we construct the GLF Petrov-Galerkin approximation as: find
u_{N}\in\mathcal{F}^{s,\lambda}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
(defined in (
3.15
)) such that

({{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}u_{N},v_{N})=(f,v_{N}),\quad\forall\,v_{N}\in\mathcal{F}^{0,\lambda}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{+}).

(3.32)

We expand
f
and
u_{N}
as

f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}~\hat{f}_{n}{\mathcal{L}}^{(0,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)},~\quad u_{N}=\sum_{n=0}^{N}~\hat{u}_{n}{\mathcal{L}}^{(s,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}.

(3.33)

Using the derivative relation (
3.5
), we find immediately that
\hat{u}_{n}=\frac{\gamma_{n}^{0,\lambda}}{h_{n}^{s,s}}\hat{f}_{n}
for
n=0,1,\cdots,N
,
which also implies
{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}u_{N}=\pi^{0,\lambda}_{N}f.

Moreover, we can show that the numerical solution
u_{N}
is precisely the orthogonal projection in the following sense:

(u_{N}-u\,,\,w_{N})_{\omega^{-s}}=0,\quad\forall\,w_{N}\in\mathcal{F}^{s,\lambda}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{+}).

(3.34)

To this end, we first show

(u_{N}-u\,,\,{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{s,\lambda}v_{N})=({{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}u_{N}-{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}u,v_{N})=0,\quad\forall\,v_{N}\in\mathcal{F}^{0,\lambda}_{N}(\mathbb{R}^{+}).

(3.35)

Indeed, thanks to
u^{(j)}(0)=0
for
j=0,\ldots,k-1
, we have

{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}\{u_{N}-u\}=e^{-\lambda x}{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s}\{e^{\lambda x}(u_{N}-u)\}=e^{-\lambda x}{}_{0}{\rm I}_{x}^{k-s}{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{k}\{e^{\lambda x}(u_{N}-u)\}.

Then,

\displaystyle\left({{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}u_{N}-{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}u\,,\,v_{N}\right)=\left({}_{0}{\rm I}_{x}^{k-s}{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{k}\{e^{\lambda x}(u_{N}-u)\},e^{-\lambda x}v_{N}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(e^{\lambda x}(u_{N}-u)\,,\,(-1)^{k}{\rm D}^{k}{}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{k-s,\lambda}v_{N}\right)=\left(u_{N}-u\,,\,{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{s,\lambda}v_{N}\right),

so (
3.35
) is valid.
In addition, thanks to Lemma
2.2
and (
2.10
), we have

{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{s,\lambda}{\mathcal{L}}^{(0,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}=e^{\lambda x}{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{s}\big\{e^{-2\lambda x}L^{(0)}_{n}(2\lambda{x})\big\}=(2\lambda)^{s}e^{-\lambda x}L^{(s)}_{n}(2\lambda{x})=(2\lambda)^{s}x^{-s}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-s,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}.

Hence, (
3.34
) is valid.

Thanks to (
3.34
), we derive from Theorem
3.3
the following estimate where the convergence rate only depends on the regularity of the source term.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
u
and
u_{N}
be respectively the solutions of (
3.30
) and (
3.32
). Then for
{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{m,\lambda}f\in L^{2}_{\omega^{m}}(I)
with
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},
we have

\|u-u_{N}\|_{\omega^{-s}}\leq\,c\,(2\lambda N)^{-\frac{s+m}{2}}\,\|{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s+m,\lambda}u\|_{\omega^{m}}=c\,(2\lambda N)^{-\frac{s+m}{2}}\,\|{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{m,\lambda}f\|_{\omega^{m}},

(3.36)

where
c\approx 1
for large

We provide some numerical results to illustrate the convergence behaviour. We take

e^{-\lambda x}{}{\rm D}^{m}\{e^{\lambda x}f\}=e^{-\lambda x}\sum_{k=0}^{m}{m\choose{k}}\lambda^{m-k}e^{\lambda x}{}{\rm D}^{k}f=\sum_{k=0}^{m}{m\choose{k}}\lambda^{m-k}{}{\rm D}^{k}f.

4.
Application to Tempered fractional diffusion equation on the half line

In this section, we apply the GLFs to approximate a tempered fractional diffusion equation on the half-line.

4.1.
The tempered fractional diffusion equation on the half line

Consider the tempered fractional diffusion equation of order
\mu\in(0,1)
on the half line:

\begin{split}\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(x,t)+{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u(x,t)-\lambda^{\mu}u(x,t)=f(x,t),\quad&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times(0,T],\\
u(0,t)=0,\quad\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow\infty}u(x,t)=0,\quad&0<t\leq T,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.\end{cases}\end{split}

(4.1)

This equation models the particles jumping on the half line
\mathbb{R}^{+}
with the probability density function (see
[
23
, (8)]
):

f_{\varepsilon}(x)=C^{-1}_{\varepsilon}x^{-\mu-1}e^{-\lambda x}\mathbf{1}_{({\varepsilon,\infty})}(x),\quad 0<\mu<1.

Remark 4.1
.

We note that equation (
4.1
) is equivalent to the half line form of the TFDE (
1.2
) in which

\partial_{+,x}^{\mu,\lambda}u={{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u-\lambda^{\mu}u,\quad 0<\mu<1.

Indeed, we can show that for
\mu\in(0,1)
and real
\lambda>0
,

{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u=e^{-\lambda x}{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu}\big\{e^{\lambda x}{{u}(x)}\big\}=e^{-\lambda x}{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu}\big\{e^{\lambda x}{\tilde{u}(x)}\big\},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

where
\tilde{u}=u
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
\tilde{u}=0
for
x\in(-\infty,0)
. Moreover, we have

\begin{split}{\mathscr{F}}\big[e^{-\lambda x}{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu}\big\{e^{\lambda x}{\tilde{u}(x)}\big\}\big](\omega)=&\int_{\mathbb{R}}{}{\rm D}{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{1-\mu}\big\{e^{\lambda x}{\tilde{u}(x)}\big\}~e^{-(\lambda+{\rm i}\omega)x}{\rm d}x\\
=(\lambda+{\rm i}\omega)&\int_{\mathbb{R}}{}_{-\infty}{\rm I}_{x}^{1-\mu}\big\{e^{\lambda x}{\tilde{u}(x)}\big\}~e^{-(\lambda+{\rm i}\omega)x}{\rm d}x\\
{=}(\lambda+{\rm i}\omega)&\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\lambda x}{\tilde{u}(x)}~{}_{x}{\rm I}_{\infty}^{1-\mu}e^{-(\lambda+{\rm i}\omega)x}{\rm d}x\\
=(\lambda+{\rm i}\omega)^{\mu}&{\mathscr{F}}[\tilde{u}](\omega)\overset{\eqref{Temdef2FourierB}}{=}{\mathscr{F}}\big[{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}{{\tilde{u}(x)}}\big](\omega).\end{split}

This implies
\tilde{u}\in W^{\mu,2}_{\lambda}(\mathbb{R})
and the extended tempered fractional derivative
{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u
can be understood
in the sense of the original definition in
[
23
]
. ∎

4.2.
Spectral-Galerkin scheme

Define

H^{\mu,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{+}):=\big\{v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+}):~{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),~0<s\leq\mu\big\},\;\;\;s,\lambda>0,\;\;\mu\in(0,1),

with the semi norm and norm

|v|_{\mu,\lambda}=\|{{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}v\|,\quad\|v\|_{\mu,\lambda}=\big(\|v\|^{2}+|v|^{2}_{\mu,\lambda}\big)^{1/2}.

Furthermore, let

H^{\mu,\lambda}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
be the closure of
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mu,\lambda}
.

Thanks to the homogeneous boundary condition and (
2.30
), a weak form of (
4.1
) is to find
u(\cdot,t)\in H^{\mu,\lambda}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+})

such that

\big(\partial_{t}u(\cdot,t),v\big)+a_{\mu}\big(u(\cdot,t),v\big)=\big(f(\cdot,t),v\big),\quad\forall v\in H^{\mu,\lambda}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+}),\\

(4.2)

with
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),
where

\begin{split}a_{\mu}(u,v):&=({{}_{0}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u,v)-\lambda^{\mu}(u,v).\end{split}

(4.3)

The semi-discrete Galerkin approximation scheme is to find

u_{N}(\cdot,t)\in\mathcal{F}^{\nu,\lambda}_{N}({\mathbb{R}}^{+})

such that

\big(\partial_{t}u_{N}(\cdot,t),v\big)+a_{\mu}\big(u_{N}(\cdot,t),v\big)=\big(f(\cdot,t),v\big),\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{F}^{\nu,\lambda}_{N}({\mathbb{R}}^{+}),

(4.4)

with

u_{N}(x,0)=u_{0,N}(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}c_{0,n}\,{\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}.

Here, we choose
\max\big\{0,\mu-\frac{1}{2}\big\}<\nu\leq 1
so that
u(0,t)=0
.

Now, we set

u_{N}(x,t)=\sum_{n=0}^{N}c_{n}(t)\,\varphi_{n}({x}),\quad\varphi_{n}({x}):={\mathcal{L}}^{(-\nu,\lambda)}_{n}{(x)}.

(4.5)

We derive from the scheme (
4.4
) that

\mathbf{M}\frac{d}{dt}\vec{\mathbf{c}}(t)+\mathbf{A}\vec{\mathbf{c}}(t)=\vec{\mathbf{f}}(t);\quad\vec{\mathbf{c}}(0)=\vec{\mathbf{c}}_{0}.

(4.6)

where for fixed
t>0
, vectors

\begin{split}&\vec{\mathbf{c}}(t)=\big(c_{0}(t),c_{1}(t),\ldots,c_{N}(t)\big)^{T},\quad\vec{\mathbf{{c}}}_{0}=\big(c_{0,0}(t),c_{0,1}(t),\ldots c_{0,N}(t)\big)^{T},\\
&\vec{\mathbf{f}}(t)=\big(f_{0}(t),f_{1}(t),\ldots f_{N}(t)\big)^{T},\qquad f_{n}(t)=(f,\varphi_{n}),\quad{0\leq n\leq N}.\end{split}

(4.7)

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{mn}

\displaystyle=(\varphi_{n},\varphi_{m}),\quad\mathbf{A}_{mn}

\displaystyle=a_{\mu}(\varphi_{n},\varphi_{m}),\quad m,n=0,1,2,\ldots,N.

(4.8)

5.
Tempered fractional diffusion equation on the whole line

In this section, we present a spectral-element method with two-subdomains for the tempered fractional diffusion equation on the whole line originally proposed by

[
23
]
.

5.1.
Tempered fractional diffusion equation

We consider the tempered fractional diffusion equation of order
\mu\in(k-1,k),~k=1,2
on the whole line:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(x,t)=(-1)^{k}C_{T}\big\{p\partial_{+,x}^{\mu,\lambda}+q\partial_{-,x}^{\mu,\lambda}\big\}u(x,t)+f(x,t),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}u(x,t)=0,\end{cases}

(5.1)

where
p,q
are constants such that
0\leq p,q\leq 1,~p+q=1,

C_{T}
is a constant and the fractional operators are

•

For
0<\mu<1,

\displaystyle\partial_{+,x}^{\mu,\lambda}u={{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u-\lambda^{\mu}u,\quad\partial_{-,x}^{\mu,\lambda}u={{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}u-\lambda^{\mu}u;

(5.2)

•

For
1<\mu<2
,

\partial_{+,x}^{\mu,\lambda}u={{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u-\mu\lambda^{\mu-1}\partial_{x}u-\lambda^{\mu}u,\quad\partial_{-,x}^{\mu,\lambda}u={{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{\mu,\lambda}u+\mu\lambda^{\mu-1}\partial_{x}u-\lambda^{\mu}u.

(5.3)

5.2.
A two-domain spectral-element method

Let
\Lambda:=(a,b),-\infty\leq a<b\leq\infty
, and
\omega>0
be a generic weight function. For any
m\in\mathbb{N}
and a given weight function
\omega
, we denote

H_{\omega}^{m}(\Lambda):=\big\{v\in L_{\omega}^{2}(\Lambda):~\partial_{x}^{k}v\in L_{\omega}^{2}(\Lambda),~0<k\leq m\big\}

with the semi-norm and norm

|v|_{m,\omega,\Lambda}=\|\partial_{x}^{m}v\|_{\omega,\Lambda},\quad\|v\|_{m,\omega,\Lambda}=\Big(\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}|v|^{2}_{k,\omega,\Lambda}\Big)^{1/2}.

In particular, we omit the subscript
\omega
when
\omega\equiv 1
.

Moreover, for real
r>0
, we define

H^{r,\lambda}(\mathbb{R}):=\big\{v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):~{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{r,\lambda}v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\big\}

with the semi norm and norm

|v|_{r,\lambda}=\|{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{r,\lambda}v\|,\quad\|v\|_{r,\lambda}=\big(\|v\|^{2}+|v|^{2}_{r,\lambda}\big)^{1/2}.

We decompose the whole line as follows

\mathbb{R}={\Lambda}_{1}\cup{\Lambda}_{2},\quad\Lambda_{1}=(-\infty,0),\quad\Lambda_{2}=[0,\infty),

and denote
u_{\Lambda_{j}}(x,t):=u(x,t)\big|_{\Lambda_{j}},~j=1,2
. Introduce the approximation space:

V^{\lambda}_{\boldsymbol{N}}({\mathbb{R}}):=\Big\{\phi\in C(\mathbb{R})\,:\,\phi_{\Lambda_{i}}(x)=e^{-\lambda|x|}p,\;\;p_{\Lambda_{i}}\in\mathcal{P}_{N_{i}}(\Lambda_{i}),\;\;i=1,2\Big\},\quad\boldsymbol{N}=(N_{1},N_{2}),

(5.4)

and define

\phi^{\ast}(x)=e^{-\lambda|x|},\;\;\phi^{-}_{n_{1}}(x)=\begin{cases}{\mathcal{L}}^{(-1,\lambda)}_{n_{1}}{(-x)},&x\leq 0,\\[4.0pt]
0,&x>0,\end{cases}\;\;\phi^{+}_{n_{2}}(x)=\begin{cases}0,&x\leq 0,\\[4.0pt]
{\mathcal{L}}^{(-1,\lambda)}_{n_{2}}{(x)},&x>0,\end{cases}

(5.5)

where
{\mathcal{L}}^{(-1,\lambda)}_{n}{(}x)=e^{-\lambda x}xL_{n}^{(1)}(2\lambda x)
. One verifies readily that

V^{\lambda}_{\boldsymbol{N}}({\mathbb{R}})={\rm span}\big\{\phi^{\ast}(x);\;\;\phi^{-}_{n_{1}}(x),\;\;0\leq n_{1}\leq N_{1}-1;\;\;\phi^{+}_{n_{2}}(x),\;\;0\leq n_{2}\leq N_{2}-1\big\}.

(5.6)

Then, our semi-discrete spectral-Galerkin method is to find

u_{\boldsymbol{N}}(\cdot,t)\in V^{\lambda}_{\boldsymbol{N}}({\mathbb{R}})

such that

\begin{cases}\big(\partial_{t}u_{\boldsymbol{N}}(\cdot,t),v\big)+a^{\mu}_{pq}\big(u_{\boldsymbol{N}}(\cdot,t),v\big)=\big(f(\cdot,t),v\big),\quad&\forall v\in V^{\lambda}_{\boldsymbol{N}}({\mathbb{R}}),\\[4.0pt]
\big(u_{\boldsymbol{N}}(\cdot,0),v)=(u_{0},v),\quad&\forall v\in V^{\lambda}_{\boldsymbol{N}}({\mathbb{R}}),\end{cases}

(5.7)

where the bilinear form
a^{\mu}_{pq}(\cdot,\cdot)
is defined by

a^{\mu}_{pq}(u,v):=\begin{cases}\quad p({{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}u,v)+q(u,{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu,\lambda}v)-\lambda^{\mu}(u,v),~\qquad&0<\mu<1,\\
-\big\{p({{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu-1,\lambda}u,{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{1,\lambda}v)+q({{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{1,\lambda}u,{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{\mu-1,\lambda}v)\big\}\\
\qquad\quad\,+\lambda^{\mu}(u,v)+(p-q)\mu\lambda^{\mu-1}(\partial_{x}u,v),~~\quad&1<\mu<2.\end{cases}

(5.8)

We provide below some details of the algorithm.

\displaystyle u_{\boldsymbol{N}}(x,t)

\displaystyle=c^{\ast}(t)\phi^{\ast}({x})+\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{N_{1}-1}c^{-}_{n_{1}}(t)\phi^{-}_{n_{1}}({x})+\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{N_{2}-1}c^{+}_{n_{2}}(t)\phi^{+}_{n_{2}}({x}),

(5.9)

\displaystyle u_{\boldsymbol{N}}(x,0)

\displaystyle=c^{\ast}_{0}\phi^{\ast}({x})+\sum_{n_{1}=0}^{N_{1}-1}c^{-}_{0,n_{1}}\phi^{-}_{n_{1}}({x})+\sum_{n_{2}=0}^{N_{2}-1}c^{+}_{0,n_{2}}\phi^{+}_{n_{2}}({x}).

Let
H(x)
be the Heaviside function as before.
Thanks to the tempered fractional derivative and integral relations with GLFs, and a reflected mapping from positive half line
\mathbb{R}^{+}
to negative half line
\mathbb{R}^{-}
, we can derive the following identities (see Appendix
B
):

\begin{split}&{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{1,\lambda}\phi^{\ast}(x)=-2\lambda e^{-\lambda x}H(x),\quad{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{1,\lambda}\phi^{-}_{n_{1}}(x)=(n_{1}+1){\mathcal{L}}^{(0,\lambda)}_{n_{1}}{(-x)}H(-x),\\[6.0pt]
&{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}\phi^{\ast}(x)=\begin{cases}(2\lambda)^{s}e^{\lambda x},&x\leq 0,\\[6.0pt]
\dfrac{2\lambda e^{\lambda x}}{\Gamma(1-s)}\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\infty}\dfrac{e^{-2\lambda t}}{t^{s}}{\rm d}t,&x>0,\end{cases}\\[6.0pt]
&{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}\phi^{-}_{n_{1}}(x)=\begin{cases}-(2\lambda)^{s-1}(n_{1}+1)L^{(s-1)}_{n_{1}+1}{(-2\lambda x)}e^{\lambda x},&x\leq 0,\\[6.0pt]
-e^{\lambda x}\dfrac{{n_{1}}+1}{\Gamma(1-s)}\displaystyle\int_{x}^{\infty}\dfrac{L^{(0)}_{{n_{1}}+1}{(2\lambda(t-x))}e^{-2\lambda t}}{t^{s}}{\rm d}t,&x>0,\end{cases}\\[6.0pt]
&{{}_{x}}{\rm D}_{\infty}^{1,\lambda}\phi^{+}_{n_{2}}(x)=-(n_{2}+1){\mathcal{L}}^{(0,\lambda)}_{n_{2}+1}{(x)}H(x),\quad\\[6.0pt]
&{{}_{-\infty}}{\rm D}_{x}^{s,\lambda}\phi^{+}_{n_{2}}(x)=\dfrac{\Gamma(n_{2}+2)}{\Gamma(n_{2}+2-s)}x^{1-s}{\mathcal{L}}^{(1-s,\lambda)}_{n_{2}}{(x)}H(x).\end{split}

(5.10)

Then (
5.7
) leads to the system

\mathbf{M}\frac{d}{dt}\overrightarrow{{\mathbf{C}}}(t)+\mathbf{A}\overrightarrow{{\mathbf{C}}}(t)=\overrightarrow{{\mathbf{F}}}(t),

(5.11)

where

\begin{split}&\overrightarrow{{\mathbf{C}}}(t)=\big(c^{\ast}(t),\overrightarrow{{\mathbf{C}}}^{-}(t),\overrightarrow{\mathbf{C}}^{+}(t)\big)^{T},\qquad\overrightarrow{{\mathbf{F}}}(t)=\big(f^{\ast}(t),\overrightarrow{{\mathbf{F}}}^{-}(t),\overrightarrow{\mathbf{F}}^{+}(t)\big)^{T},\\
&\overrightarrow{{\mathbf{C}}}^{-}(t)=\big(c^{-}_{0}(t),c^{-}_{1}(t),\ldots,c^{-}_{N_{1}-1}(t)\big)^{T},\quad\qquad\overrightarrow{{\mathbf{C}}}^{+}(t)=\big(c^{+}_{0}(t),c^{+}_{1}(t),\ldots,c^{+}_{N_{2}-1}(t)\big)^{T}.\\
&\overrightarrow{{\mathbf{F}}}^{-}(t)=\big(f^{-}_{0}(t),f^{-}_{1}(t),\ldots,f^{-}_{N_{1}-1}(t)\big)^{T},~\,\,\quad\quad\overrightarrow{{\mathbf{F}}}^{+}(t)=\big(f^{+}_{0}(t),f^{+}_{1}(t),\ldots,f^{+}_{N_{2}-1}(t)\big)^{T}.\\
&f^{\ast}(t)=(f,\phi^{\ast}),\quad f^{-}_{n_{1}}(t)=(f,\phi^{-}_{n_{1}}),\qquad f^{+}_{n_{2}}(t)=(f,\phi^{+}_{n_{2}}),\quad{0\leq n_{i}\leq N_{i}-1},~i=1,2,\end{split}

and the matrices

\mathbf{M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbf{M}_{1\times 1}^{(*,*)}&{\mathbf{M}}_{1\times N_{1}}^{(*,-)}&{\mathbf{M}}_{1\times N_{2}}^{(*,+)}\\
\\
{\mathbf{M}}_{N_{1}\times 1}^{(-,*)}&\mathbf{M}_{N_{1}\times N_{1}}^{(-,-)}&\mathbf{M}_{N_{1}\times N_{2}}^{(-,+)}\\
\\
{\mathbf{M}}_{N_{2}\times 1}^{(+,*)}&\mathbf{M}_{N_{2}\times N_{1}}^{(+,-)}&\mathbf{M}_{N_{2}\times N_{2}}^{(+,+)}\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\mathbf{A}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}{\mathbf{A}}_{1\times 1}^{(*,*)}&{\mathbf{A}}_{1\times N_{1}}^{(*,-)}&{\mathbf{A}}_{1\times N_{2}}^{(*,+)}\\
\\
{\mathbf{A}}_{N_{1}\times 1}^{(-,*)}&\mathbf{A}_{N_{1}\times N_{1}}^{(-,-)}&\mathbf{A}_{N_{1}\times N_{2}}^{(-,+)}\\
\\
\mathbf{A}_{{N_{2}}\times 1}^{(+,*)}&\mathbf{A}_{N_{2}\times N_{1}}^{(+,-)}&\mathbf{A}_{N_{2}\times N_{2}}^{(+,+)}\\
\end{array}\right),

(5.12)

with the entries

\displaystyle\mathbf{M}_{c\times d}^{(a,b)}(i+1,j+1)=(\phi^{b}_{j},\phi^{a}_{i}),\quad\mathbf{A}_{c\times d}^{(a,b)}(i+1,j+1)=a_{pq}^{\mu}(\phi^{b}_{j},\phi^{a}_{i}),

\displaystyle a,b=*,-,+,\quad c,d=1,N_{1},N_{2},\qquad 0\leq i\leq c-1,\quad 0\leq j\leq d-1,

and
\overrightarrow{{\mathbf{C}}}(0)
is determined by the initial data.

The proof of the tempered derivative relation (

), and the detail on the entries of the matrix

.

6.
Concluding remarks

We presented in this paper efficient spectral methods using the generalized Laguerre functions for solving the tempered fractional differential equations. Our numerical methods and analysis are based on an important observation that the tempered fractional derivative, when restricted to the half line,
is intrinsically related to the generalized Laguerre functions that we defined in Sections
3
. By exploring the properties of generalized Laguerre functions, we derived optimal approximation results in properly weighted Sobolev spaces, and showed that

we define two classes of generalized Laguerre functions, study their approximation properties, and apply them for solving simple one sided tempered fractional equations. In Section
4
, we develop a spectral-Galerkin method for solving a tempered fractional diffusion equation on the half line. Finally, we present a spectral-Galerkin method for solving the tempered fractional diffusion equation on the whole line

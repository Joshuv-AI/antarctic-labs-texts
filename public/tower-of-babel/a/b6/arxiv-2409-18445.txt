Abstract
. Considering potentials defined by Bessel kernel with Bessel convolution a Kerman-Sawyer type characterization of trace inequality is given. As an application an estimate on the least eigenvalue of Schrödinger-Bessel operators is derived.

31C45, 35P15

1.
introduction

After several authors we call trace inequality the next type of inequalities

\|Jf\|_{p,\mu}\leq C\|f\|_{p,\nu},

where
J
is a certain integral operator,
\mu
and
\nu
are positive measures. The reason is that
\mu
can be the restriction of
\nu
to a subspace see
[
11
]
and
[
21
]
, or considering the Sobolev type version it generalizes
\|u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega,d\mu)}\leq C\|\nabla_{m}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega,d\nu)}
, see
[
22
]
.
The notion is strongly related to non-linear potential theory and has several different applications. For instance it can be applied to characterize positive measures which are multipliers for a pair of potential spaces, embedding theorems for Sobolev spaces, see e.g.
[
15
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
12
]
, to obtain bounds
for the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function, see
[
18
]
. It is also useful in applications to criteria of solvability for non-linear partial differential equations, see e.g.
[
9
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
1
]
, and for estimation of eigenvalues of partial differential operators, see e.g.
[
11
]
.

Some connections with nonlinear capacity in case of general radially decreasing convolution kernels are described in
[
3
]
. In the celebrated paper of Maz’ya and Verbitsky (see
[
15
]
) the equivalence of the trace inequality to capacitary and potential-type inequalities is proved in Riesz and Bessel cases. In Bessel case, some additions and additional results can be found in
[
17
]
.

As potential theory developed by Gauss in order to describe gravitational and electrostatic fields, it is ball-symmetric and is strongly connected to the Laplace operator. So partial differential operators arise in the applications are connected to the Laplace operator as well. Below we change the symmetry of the space applying Bessel translation and convolution rather than the standard one. This implies that instead of Laplace, the Laplace-Bessel operator (see (
11
)) comes into the picture which is also an important tool in physics. Moreover it results investigations in weighted spaces and so the trace inequality takes on the general form above. (For weighted potential see e.g.
[
2
]
, for potential with Bessel convolution see
[
10
]
.) In Theorem
1
we show the equivalence of the trace inequality with a ”cube condition”, c.f.
[
11
]
. Since it is much simpler to check a condition on cubes than on general compact sets, it proved to be a useful tool for applications, see
[
11
]
and Theorem
2
below.

The paper is organized as follows. The following section contains the main results and the definitions that are essential for their formulation. In the third section we collected all the properties of the defined notions which are used later. Moreover this section contains the proofs of some technical lemmas. The fourth section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1
. As an application of Theorem
1
in the last section we deal with the Schrödinger-Bessel operator.

2.
Notation, main results

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}:=\{x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}):x_{i}\geq 0,\ i=1,\dots,n.\}
.
B(x,r)
and
Q(x,r)
stand for the positive part of balls and cubes centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and of radius (half-side)
r
(i.e. e.g.
Q(x,r)=\times_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i}-r,x_{i}+r)\cap\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
).
\lambda
is the n-dimensional Lebesgue measure.

a=a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
is a multiindex. Let
\mathrm{E}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
and
\mathcal{M}(E)
stands for Radon
measures supported on
\mathrm{E}
. If
\mu
is a measure and
K
is a set, then
\mu_{|_{K}}
is the restriction of
\mu
to
K
. If
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(E)
for some
E,

d\mu_{a}(x):=x^{a}d\mu(x).
A measure
\mu
is doubling if there is a constant
C
such that for all
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq C\mu(B(x,r))
.

Define the Banach space
L^{p}_{a}
as follows.

(1)

\|f\|^{p}_{p,a}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|f(x)|^{p}d\lambda_{a}(x).

and as usual

(2)

L^{p}_{a}:=L^{p}_{a}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})=\{f:\|f\|_{p,a}<\infty\},\ \ \ \mathrm{L}^{p+}_{a}:=\{f\in L^{p}:f\geq 0\}.

The dual index
p
is defined by
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1.

2.1.
Bessel Translation

Let
a_{i}=2\alpha_{i}+1,\ \alpha_{i}>-\frac{1}{2}
,
i=1,\dots,n
,

|a|=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(2\alpha_{i}+1)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.

The Bessel translation of a function,
f
(see e.g. [14], [16], [20]) is

(3)

T^{t}_{a}f(x)=T^{t_{n}}_{a_{n}}\dots T^{t_{1}}_{a_{1}}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),

where

T^{t_{i}}_{a_{i}}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+\frac{1}{2})}

(4)

\times\int_{0}^{\pi}f(x_{1},\dots,\sqrt{x_{i}^{2}+t_{i}^{2}-2x_{i}^{2}t_{i}^{2}\cos\vartheta_{i}},x_{i+1},\dots,x_{n})\sin^{2\alpha_{i}}\vartheta_{i}d\vartheta_{i}.

The generalized convolution with respect to Bessel translation is

(5)

f\ast_{a}g=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}T^{t}f(x)g(x)d\lambda_{a}(x).

2.2.
Radially decreasing and Bessel kernels

Let
g
be a non-negative lower semi-continuous, non-increasing function on
\mathbb{R}_{+}

for which

(6)

\int_{0}^{1}g(t)t^{n+|a|-1}dt<\infty.

Then
\kappa:=g(|x|)
is a radially decreasing kernel on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The modified Bessel function of the second kind,
K_{\alpha}
is defined as follows.

(7)

i^{-\alpha}J_{\alpha}(ix)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!\Gamma(k+\alpha+1)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2k+\alpha},

where
J_{\alpha}
is the Bessel function, and

(8)

K_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{i^{\alpha}J_{-\alpha}(ix)-i^{-\alpha}J_{\alpha}(ix)}{\sin\alpha\pi}.

The Bessel kernel is

(9)

G_{a,\nu}(x):=\frac{2^{\frac{n-a-\nu}{2}+1}}{\Gamma(\frac{\nu}{2})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma(\alpha_{i}+1)}\frac{K_{\frac{n+|a|-\nu}{2}}(|x|)}{|x|^{\frac{n+|a|-\nu}{2}}}.

2.3.
Schrödinger-Bessel differential operator

Schrödinger-Bessel operator is

(10)

H_{a}:=-\Delta_{a}-v,

where
\Delta_{a}
is the Laplace-Bessel operator, i.e.

(11)

\Delta_{a}u=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{x_{i}x_{i}}+\frac{2\alpha_{i}+1}{x_{i}}u_{x_{i}},

and
v
is nonnegative and locally integrable.

2.4.
Results

The next result gives a Kerman-Sawyer type characterization of trace inequality. It is proved for Riesz and Bessel kernels with respect to the standard convolution, see
[
11
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
17
]
. Here we state a similar result with Bessel convolution.

Theorem 1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
0<\nu
. Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}))
be a doubling measure. Then the following properties of
\gamma
are equivalent.

There is a constant
A_{1}
such that for all
f\in L_{a}^{p}

(12)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|G_{a,\nu}*_{a}f|^{p}d\gamma_{a}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq A_{1}\|f\|_{p,a}.

There is a constant
A_{2}
such that for all cubes
Q

(13)

\int_{Q}((G_{a,\nu}*_{a}\gamma|_{Q})(x))^{p^{\prime}}x^{a}dx\leq A_{2}\gamma_{a}(Q),

The potential
G_{a,\nu}*_{a}\gamma
is finite a. e. and there is a constant
A_{3}
such that

(14)

G_{a,\nu}*_{a}(G_{a,\nu}*_{a}\gamma)^{p^{\prime}}(x)\leq A_{3}\ (G_{a,\nu}*_{a}\gamma)(x).

Moreover the least possible constants
A_{i}
are all proportional with each other with constants that are independent of
\gamma
.

The class of measures characterized by (
14
) turned out to be useful in applications to nonlinear equations, see e.g.
[
9
]
. Below we apply characterization (
13
) to get some eigenvalue estimate to a Schrödinger-type operator.

Let
l(Q)
be the edge lengths of the cube
Q
, and
\mu(Q)
is the volume of
Q
with respect to the measure
\mu
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
H_{a}
be as defined by (
10
). Assume further that
v(x)
defines a doubling measure. Then there are two positive constants
A
and
B
depending only on
n
and
a
such that the least eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
of
H_{a}
satisfies

L\leq-\lambda_{1}\leq U,

where

L=\sup\left\{l(Q)^{-2}:\frac{1}{v_{a}(Q)}\int_{Q}G_{a,2}*_{a}v|_{Q}(x)x^{a}v(x)dx\geq B\right\};

U=\sup\left\{l(Q)^{-2}:\frac{1}{v_{a}(Q)}\int_{Q}G_{a,2}*_{a}v|_{Q}(x)x^{a}v(x)dx\geq A\right\}.

3.
Preliminaries

In this section some useful properties of the above defined notions are collected and derived.

3.1.
Bessel translation

Bessel translation can also be expressed as an integral with respect to a kernel function:

(15)

T^{t_{i}}_{a_{i}}f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\int_{0}^{\infty}K(x_{i},t_{i},z_{i})f(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})d\lambda_{a_{i}}(z_{i}),

where for any
\alpha>-\frac{1}{2},x,t\geq 0

(16)

K(x,t,z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\pi^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{2^{2\alpha-1}\Gamma(\alpha+\frac{1}{2})}\frac{[((x+t)^{2}-z^{2})(z^{2}-(x-t)^{2})]^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}}{(xtz)^{2\alpha}},\hskip 4.0pt|x-t|<z<|x+t|;\\
0,\hskip 4.0pt\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Since
a
is fixed subsequently we omit it from the notation of the translation.

The formula above shows that Bessel translation is positive operator. It is also symmetric and bounded, that is

(17)

T^{t}f(x)=T^{x}f(t).

(18)

\|T^{t}f(x)\|_{p,a}\leq\|f\|_{p,a},\ \ \ 1\leq p\leq\infty,

see e.g.
[
13
]
.

Subsequently we need the estimations below.

Lemma 1
.

[
10
]

\mathrm{supp}T^{t}\chi_{B_{+}(0,r)}(x)=\overline{B_{+}(x,r)}
,
\mathrm{supp}T^{t}\chi_{[0,r)^{n}}(x)=\times_{i=1}^{n}[x_{i}-r,x_{i}+r]_{+}=:T_{+}(x,r)
.
There is a
c>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
,
t\in B_{+}(x,r)

(19)

T^{t}\chi_{B_{+}(0,r)}(x)\leq c\prod_{i=1}^{n}\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{r}{x_{i}}\right)^{a_{i}}\right\}.

There is a
c>0
such that for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
,
t\in T_{+}\left(x,\frac{r}{2}\right)

(20)

T^{t}\chi_{[0,r)^{n}}(x)\geq c\prod_{i=1}^{n}\min\left\{1,\left(\frac{r}{x_{i}}\right)^{a_{i}}\right\}.

To formulate the next technical lemma we introduce the abbreviations:

d(x,t,\vartheta):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}+t_{i}^{2}-2x_{i}t_{i}\cos\vartheta_{i},

D:=\left|\sqrt{d(t,u,\vartheta)}-\sqrt{d(x,y,\vartheta)}\right|,\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0ptN:=d(t,u,\vartheta)+d(x,y,\vartheta).

Lemma 2
.

Let
\vartheta\in[0,\pi]^{n}
be fixed. Then we have

(21)

D\leq 2\max\{\|x-t\|,\|y-u\|\}\left(3+\sqrt{2}\frac{\|y-t\|}{\sqrt{N}}\right).

(22)

d(z,y,\vartheta)\leq Cd(x,y,\vartheta)\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt\mbox{if}\hskip 4.0pt\|z-y\|\leq B\|x-y\|.

Proof.

First we show (
21
).

D\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(t_{i}-x_{i})(t_{i}+x_{i}-2y_{i}\cos\vartheta_{i})+(u_{i}-y_{i})(u_{i}+y_{i}-2t_{i}\cos\vartheta_{i})}{\sqrt{d(t,u,\vartheta)+d(x,y,\vartheta)}}

\leq\sqrt{2}\max\{\|x-t\|,\|y-u\|\}\sqrt{\frac{N+\sum_{i=1}^{n}H_{i}}{N}},

where
H_{i}=4\cos^{2}\vartheta_{i}(y_{i}^{2}+t_{i}^{2})+2(t_{i}x_{i}+u_{i}y_{i})-8t_{i}y_{i}\cos\vartheta_{i}-2t_{i}u_{i}\cos\vartheta_{i}-2x_{i}y_{i}\cos\vartheta_{i}
. Thus

D\leq\sqrt{2}\max\{\|x-t\|,\|y-u\|\}\sqrt{\frac{2N+\sum_{i=1}^{n}S_{i}}{N}},

where

S_{i}:=4(\cos^{2}\vartheta_{i}(y_{i}^{2}+t_{i}^{2})-2\cos\vartheta_{i}t_{i}y_{i}).

Let
N_{i}
be the
i
th term in the denominator. If
S_{i}\leq 0
, then we estimate it by 0. If
\cos\theta_{i}<0
(
S_{i}>0
), then
S_{i}\leq 2\times 4(y_{i}^{2}+t_{i}^{2})\leq 8N_{i}
. If
S_{i}>0
and
\cos\theta_{i}>0

then
\cos\theta_{i}>\frac{2t_{i}y_{i}}{t_{i}^{2}+y_{i}^{2}}
and so
S_{i}
is increasing in
\cos\theta_{i}
i.e.
S_{i}\leq 4(t_{i}-y_{i})^{2}
. Thus

D\leq\sqrt{2}\max\{\|x-t\|,\|y-u\|\}\left(\sqrt{\frac{2N}{N}}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{\cos\theta_{i}<0}S_{i}}{N}}+\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{\cos\theta_{i}>0}S_{i}}{N}}\right)

\leq\sqrt{2}\max\{\|x-t\|,\|y-u\|\}\left(\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{8}+2\frac{\|y-t\|}{\sqrt{N}}\right).

To prove (
22
) observe that

d(z,y,\vartheta)=\|z-y\|^{2}+4\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}z_{i}\sin^{2}\frac{\vartheta_{i}}{2}\leq B^{2}\|x-y\|^{2}+4\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}z_{i}\sin^{2}\frac{\vartheta_{i}}{2}.

Let
A>1
. If
Ax_{i}>z_{i}
for some index
i
, then

(23)

4y_{i}z_{i}\sin^{2}\frac{\vartheta_{i}}{2}\leq A4y_{i}x_{i}\sin^{2}\frac{\vartheta_{i}}{2}.

If
Ax_{i}\leq z_{i}
for some
i
, then
(A-1)x_{i}\leq z_{i}-x_{i}
. In this case we take

y_{i}z_{i}=x_{i}y_{i}+(z_{i}-x_{i})(y_{i}-x_{i})+(z_{i}-y_{i})x_{i}+(y_{i}-x_{i})^{2}+(y_{i}-x_{i})x_{i}

\leq x_{i}y_{i}+(z_{i}-x_{i})(y_{i}-x_{i})+(z_{i}-y_{i})\frac{1}{A-1}((z_{i}-y_{i})+(y_{i}-x_{i}))+(y_{i}-x_{i})^{2}

+(y_{i}-x_{i})\frac{1}{A-1}((z_{i}-y_{i})+(y_{i}-x_{i})).

Thus

d(z,y,\vartheta)\leq B^{2}\|x-y\|^{2}+4\sum_{1\leq i\leq n\atop Ax_{i}\leq z_{i}}y_{i}z_{i}\sin^{2}\frac{\vartheta_{i}}{2}+4\sum_{1\leq i\leq n\atop Ax_{i}>z_{i}}(\cdot)

\leq B^{2}\|x-y\|^{2}+4\sum_{1\leq i\leq n\atop Ax_{i}\leq z_{i}}y_{i}x_{i}\sin^{2}\frac{\vartheta_{i}}{2}+4\|z-x\|\|y-x\|+\frac{1}{A-1}\|z-y\|^{2}

+\frac{1}{A-1}\|z-y\|\|x-y\|+\|x-y\|^{2}+\frac{1}{A-1}\|z-y\|\|x-y\|+\frac{1}{A-1}\|x-y\|^{2}

+4A\sum_{1\leq i\leq n\atop Ax_{i}>z_{i}}y_{i}x_{i}\sin^{2}\frac{\vartheta_{i}}{2}\leq(A+1)4\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}x_{i}\sin^{2}\frac{\vartheta_{i}}{2}+\|x-y\|^{2}\left(2B+\frac{(B+1)^{2}}{A-1}\right),

i.e. (
22
) is proved by
C=\max\left\{A+1,2B+\frac{(B+1)^{2}}{A-1}\right\}
.

Remark 1
.

In particular

D\leq 10M,\hskip 4.0pt\mbox{if}\hskip 4.0ptx=t,\hskip 4.0pt\|u-y\|<M.

Indeed,
\sqrt{N}\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\|y-x\|+\|u-t\|)
and
\|y-t\|=\|y-x\|
.

3.2.
Bessel convolution

The convolution defined by Bessel translation similarly to the standard one, is symmetric and it fulfils the Young’s inequality, i.e.

(24)

f\ast_{a}g=g\ast_{a}f,

and if
1\leq p,q,r\leq\infty
with
\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}-1
; if
f\in L^{p}_{a}
and

g\in L^{q}_{a},
then

(25)

\|f\ast_{a}g\|_{r,a}\leq\|f\|_{p,a}\|g\|_{q,a},

see
[
20
, (3.178)]
.

3.3.
Radially decreasing kernels with Bessel convolution

The next lemma with general radially decreasing kernel is useful tool of the investigation below.

Lemma 3
.

Let
\kappa(t)=g(|t|)
be a radially decreasing kernel. Then

\kappa*_{a}\mu(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\chi_{B(0,r)}*_{a}\mu(x)(-g^{\prime}(r))dr.

Proof.

Let
R>0
be arbitrary. Define the next measure:
d\Theta_{x,a}(t)=t^{a}dT^{x}\mu(t)
. Since
g
is differentiable a.e. we have

\int_{0}^{R}\chi_{B(0,r)}*_{a}\mu(x)(-g^{\prime}(r))dr=\int_{0}^{R}(-g^{\prime}(r))\int_{B(0,r)}1d\Theta_{x,a}(t)dr

=\int_{B(0,r)}\int_{|t|}^{R}(-g^{\prime}(r))drd\Theta_{x,a}(t)

=\int_{B(0,r)}g(|t|)d\Theta_{x,a}(t)-g(R)\int_{B(0,r)}1d\Theta_{x,a}(t).

Then commutativity of convolution implies that

\int_{0}^{R}\chi_{B(0,r)}*_{a}\mu(x)(-g^{\prime}(r))dr=(\kappa\chi_{B(0,R)})*_{a}\mu(x)-g(R)(\chi_{B(0,R)}*_{a}\mu(x)).

Similarly

(\kappa\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}\setminus B(0,R)})*_{a}\mu(x)=\int_{R}^{\infty}\chi_{B(0,r)}*_{a}\mu(x)(-g^{\prime}(r))dr-g(R)(\chi_{B(0,R)}*_{a}\mu(x)).

Added up the result follows.

In the main part of the paper we restrict the investigation to Bessel kernel. It has the next asymptotic properties, see e.g.
[
3
, page 12]
.

Around zero we have

(26)

K_{\alpha}(r)\sim\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-ln\frac{r}{2}-c,&\text{if}\ \alpha=0;\\
C(\alpha)r^{-\alpha},&\text{if}\ \alpha>0,\end{array}\right.

and around infinity

(27)

K_{\alpha}(r)\sim\frac{c}{\sqrt{r}}e^{-r}.

Recall the definition of Bessel kernel, (
9
).
G_{a,\nu}
has the next semi-group property (see
[
19
, Lemma 4.3]
)

(28)

G_{a,\nu}*_{a}G_{a,\mu}=G_{a,\nu+\mu},\hskip 4.0pt\hskip 4.0pt\nu,\mu>0.

3.4.
Hankel transformation

Let

j_{\alpha}(x)=\frac{2^{\alpha}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{x^{\alpha}}J_{\alpha}(x)

be the entire Bessel function. Then the Hankel transform of a function
f\in L^{1}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n})
is

\mathcal{H}_{a}f(\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}f(x)\prod_{i=1}^{n}j_{\alpha_{i}}(x_{i}\xi_{i})x^{a}dx.

Below we need the next properties of Hankel transformation.

(29)

\mathcal{H}_{a}f*_{a}g=\hat{f}\hat{g},

(30)

\mathcal{H}_{a}(-\Delta_{a}u)(\xi)=|\xi|^{2}\hat{u}(\xi)

and denoting by

\langle f,g\rangle_{a}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}}f(x)g(x)x^{a}dx,

(31)

\langle f,g\rangle_{a}=\frac{2^{n-|a|}}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Gamma^{2}(\alpha_{i}+1)}\langle\hat{f},\hat{g}\rangle_{a},

see e.g.
[
20
, (1.83), (1.95)]
.
Finally for
\nu>0
we have (see
[
19
, Lemma 4.1]
) that

(32)

G_{a,\nu}(x)=\mathcal{H}_{a}^{-1}\frac{1}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{\nu}{2}}}.

4.

4.1.
(

\Rightarrow
(

The first implication in Theorem

can be stated in more general form. For the corresponding statement with respect to standard convolution we refer to
[

Proposition 1
.

Let
\kappa\in L^{1}_{a}
be a radially decreasing convolution kernel, and let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+})
and
1<p\leq q<\infty
. Then if
there is a constant
B_{1}
such that for all
f\in L_{a}^{p}

(33)

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}|\kappa\ast_{a}f|^{q}d\mu_{a}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq B_{1}\|f\|_{p,a},

Then there is a constant
B_{2}
such that for all compact sets
K

(34)

\|\kappa\ast_{a}\mu|_{K}\|_{p^{\prime},a}\leq B_{2}\mu_{a}(K)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}.

Proof.

(
33
)
\Rightarrow
(
34
). Let
f\in L_{a}^{p}
. This implication is immediate from

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x)(\kappa\ast_{a}\mu|_{K})(x)x^{a}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}T^{t}\kappa(x)t^{a}d\mu_{|_{K}}(t)x^{a}dx

=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}t^{a}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x)T^{t}\kappa(x)x^{a}dxd\mu_{|_{K}}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}(\kappa\ast_{a}f)(t)t^{a}d\mu_{|_{K}}(t)

=\int_{K}(\kappa\ast_{a}f)d\mu_{a}(t).

Applying Hölder’s inequality then combining with (
33
) and (
18
) yields

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}f(x)(\kappa\ast_{a}\mu|_{K})(x)x^{a}dx\right|\leq\|\kappa\ast_{a}f\|_{q,\mu_{a}}\mu_{a}(K)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\leq B_{1}\|f\|_{p,a}\ \mu_{a}(K)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}},

for all
f\in L^{p}_{a}.

Finally we have

\|\kappa\ast_{a}\mu|_{K}\|_{p^{\prime},a}\leq B_{2}\mu_{a}(K)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}.

) implies (

\gamma
is doubling. We need some further observations.

Lemma 4
.

If
\gamma
is doubling, then
\gamma_{a}
is doubling as well and

(35)

(\chi_{B(0,r)}*_{a}\gamma)(x)\sim r^{|a|}\gamma(B(x,r)).

Proof.

The first statement is obvious. To see the second one notice that if
t\in B(x,r)
, then
\min\{1,\frac{r}{x_{i}}\}t_{i}\leq 2r
. By Lemma

\sum_{i,j}\int_{Q}(G_{a,1}^{\beta}*_{a}v|_{Q_{i}})(x)(G_{a,1}^{\beta}*_{a}v|_{Q_{j}})(x)x^{a}dx

\leq c\left(2+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}e^{-k}k^{n-1}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{L}\int_{Q_{i}}(G_{a,1}^{\beta}*_{a}v|_{Q_{i}})^{2}(u)u^{a}du.

Finally we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}(G_{a,1}^{\beta}*_{a}v|_{Q}(x))^{2}x^{a}dx

\leq c\sum_{i=1}^{L}\int_{Q_{i}}(G_{a,1}^{\beta}*_{a}v|_{Q_{i}})^{2}(u)u^{a}du\leq cM\sum_{i=1}^{L}v_{a}(Q_{i})=cMv_{a}(Q)

for each cube
Q
. Taking supremum we have

M_{0}\sim M.

Applying the Fourier-Bessel transformation again we have
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}(G_{a,1}^{\beta}*_{a}v|_{Q}(x))^{2}x^{a}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}(G_{a,2}^{\beta}*_{a}v|_{Q}(x))v_{Q}(x)x^{a}dx
. We finish the proof with the observation (again on the transformed side) that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}(G_{a,2}^{\beta}*_{a}v|_{Q}(x))v_{Q}(x)x^{a}dx\sim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}(G_{a,2}*_{a}v|_{Q}(x))v_{Q}(x)x^{a}dx,

which after a renormalization leads to the inequalities of Theorem
2
.

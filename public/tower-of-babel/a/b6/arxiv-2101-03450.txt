Abstract
. We study emergent dynamics of the Lohe hermitian sphere (LHS) model with the same free flows under the dynamic interplay between state evolution and adaptive couplings. The LHS model is a complex counterpart of the Lohe sphere (LS) model on the unit sphere in Euclidean space, and when particles lie in the Euclidean unit sphere embedded in ℂ d + 1 \mathbb{C}^{d+1} , it reduces to the Lohe sphere model. In the absence of interactions between states and coupling gains, emergent dynamics have been addressed in [ 22 ] . In this paper, we further extend earlier results in the aforementioned work to the setting in which the state and coupling gains are dynamically interrelated via two types of coupling laws, namely anti-Hebbian and Hebbian coupling laws. In each case, we present two sufficient frameworks leading to complete aggregation depending on the coupling laws, when the corresponding free flow is the same for all particles.

\Omega^{\dagger}=-\Omega,\quad j\in{\mathcal{N}}:=\{1,\cdots,N\},

where
\Omega^{\dagger}

\Omega

In this paper, we are interested in the following simple question:

“What if dynamics of coupling gains interacts with the dynamics of states? i.e., dynamic interplay between coupling gains and state evolution. In this case, under what conditions, can a coupled system exhibit emergent dynamics?”

The above question has been addressed in other aggregation models, e.g., the Kuramoto model with adaptive couplings

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\dot{z}_{j}=\Omega z_{j}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\kappa_{jk}\Big(\langle z_{j},z_{j}\rangle z_{k}-\langle z_{k},z_{j}\rangle z_{j}\Big)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\lambda_{jk}\Big(\langle z_{j},z_{k}\rangle-\langle z_{k},z_{j}\rangle\Big)z_{j},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
&\dot{\kappa}_{jk}=-\gamma_{0}\kappa_{jk}+\mu_{0}\Gamma_{0}(z_{j},z_{k}),\quad\dot{\lambda}_{jk}=-\gamma_{1}\lambda_{jk}+\mu_{1}\Gamma_{1}(z_{j},z_{k}),\quad t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\
&(z_{j},\kappa_{jk},\lambda_{jk})(0)=(z_{j}^{0},\kappa_{jk}^{0},\lambda_{jk}^{0})\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R},\quad j,k\in\mathcal{N},\end{aligned}

where
\mu_{k}

Throughout the paper, we use the following handy notation:

Z:=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{N})\in(\mathbb{HS}^{d})^{N},\quad K:=[\kappa_{ij}],\quad\Lambda:=[\lambda_{ij}],\quad\max_{i,j}:=\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N},\quad\min_{i,j}:=\min_{1\leq i,j\leq N}.

Before we discuss our main results, we recall the concept of “

” for the Cauchy problem (
1.2

Definition 1.1
.

Let
(Z,K,\Lambda)
be a solution to (
1.2
). Then, complete aggregation occurs asymptotically if and only if following relations hold.

\lim_{t\to\infty}\max_{1\leq i,j\leq N}\|z_{i}(t)-z_{j}(t)\|=0.

Recall that the primary purpose of this paper is to provide sufficient frameworks leading to complete aggregation for system (
1.2
). In general, there will be no functional dependence between
\kappa_{jk}
and
\lambda_{jk}
. From now on, we assume that the system parameters satisfy the following relations:

(1.3)

\Omega=0,\quad\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{1}=\gamma,\quad\mu_{0}=\mu_{1}=\mu.

Motivated by the reduction from the Stuart-Landau(SL) model to the LHS model in Section
2.3
, we call the following relation as the SL coupling gain pair:

(1.4)

\kappa_{jk}>0,\quad\lambda_{jk}=-\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{jk},\quad j,k\in{\mathcal{N}}.

Under the setting (
1.4
), due to Lemma
2.2

2.3

1.2
) becomes

(1.5)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{z}_{j}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\kappa_{jk}\left[z_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\langle z_{j},z_{k}\rangle+\langle z_{k},z_{j}\rangle\Big)z_{j}\right],\quad t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle\dot{\kappa}_{jk}=-\gamma\kappa_{jk}+\mu\Gamma_{0}(z_{j},z_{k}),\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle(z_{j},\kappa_{ij})(0)=(z_{j}^{0},\kappa_{ij}^{0})\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\quad i,j\in\mathcal{N}.\end{cases}

At the end of Section

1.5

\mathbb{C}^{(d+1)N}

\mathbb{R}^{2(d+1)N}

\tilde{\lambda}_{jk}:=\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{jk}+\lambda_{jk},\quad j,k\in{\mathcal{N}},\quad\tilde{\Gamma}(z,w):=\frac{1}{2}\Gamma_{0}(z,w)+\Gamma_{1}(z,w),\quad z,w\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{S}^{d}.

Then, under the setting (
1.3

1.2

1.5

(1.6)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{z}_{j}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\kappa_{jk}\left[z_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\langle z_{j},z_{k}\rangle+\langle z_{k},z_{j}\rangle\Big)z_{j}\right]+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\tilde{\lambda}_{jk}\left(\langle z_{j},z_{k}\rangle-\langle z_{k},z_{j}\rangle\right)z_{j},\vskip 5.69046pt\\

\displaystyle(z_{j},\kappa_{jk},\lambda_{jk})(0)=(z_{j}^{0},\kappa_{jk}^{0},\lambda_{jk}^{0})\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R},\quad j,k\in{\mathcal{N}}.\end{cases}

and we take the following ansatz for the coupling law
\Gamma_{0}

(1.7)

\Gamma_{0}(w,z)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\|w-z\|^{2}\quad&:\mbox{Anti-Hebbian coupling law},\\
\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{2}\|w-z\|^{2}\quad&:\mbox{Hebbian coupling law}.\end{cases}

\mathcal{L}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\|z_{i}-z_{j}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2}.

Our first result deals with (
1.5

\eqref{A-5}_{1}

\max_{i,j}\mathcal{L}_{ij}^{0}<1,

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|z_{i}(t)-z_{j}(t)\|=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\kappa_{ij}(t)=0,\quad i,j\in{\mathcal{N}}.

Our second result is conserved with (

{\mathcal{D}}(Z):=\frac{1}{2}\max_{i,j}\|z_{i}-z_{j}\|^{2},

If there exist a constant

0<\kappa<\min\left\{\frac{\mu}{\gamma},~\min_{i,j}\kappa_{ij}^{0}\right\},\quad\max\left\{\max_{i,j}\kappa^{0}_{ij},~\frac{\mu}{\gamma}\right\}\leq\frac{2\mu\kappa}{2\mu-\gamma\kappa},\quad{\mathcal{D}}(Z^{0})<1-\frac{\gamma}{\mu}\kappa,

{\mathcal{D}}(Z(t))\leq C_{0}e^{-C_{1}t},\quad t>0.

We refer to Theorem
3.2

Secondly, we study emergent behaviors of (
1.6

\kappa_{ij}>0,\quad\lambda_{ij}\in\mathbb{R},\quad\forall~i,j\in{\mathcal{N}}.

Our third result can be stated as follows. Suppose that system parameters and initial data satisfy

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}_{ij}^{0}={\tilde{\lambda}}^{0},\quad i,j\in\mathcal{N},\quad\tilde{\Gamma}(t)=0,\quad\forall t>0,\quad\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle\max_{i,j}\frac{2|\tilde{\lambda}^{0}|}{\kappa_{ij}^{0}}+\max_{k,l}\mathcal{L}_{kl}^{0}<1,\end{cases}

Then under anti-Hebbian coupling law

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|z_{i}(t)-z_{j}(t)\|=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{t\to\infty}\kappa_{ij}(t)=0.

Finally, suppose there exist a constant

2|\tilde{\lambda}^{0}|<\kappa<\min\left\{\frac{\mu}{\gamma},~\min_{i,j}\kappa_{ij}^{0}\right\},\quad\max\left\{\max_{i,j}\kappa^{0}_{ij},~\frac{\mu}{\gamma}\right\}\leq\frac{2\mu(\kappa-2|\tilde{\lambda^{0}}|)}{2\mu-\gamma\kappa},\quad{\mathcal{D}}(Z^{0})<1-\frac{\gamma}{\mu}\kappa,

2.1.
Basic estimates

In this subsection, we study basic properties of system (
1.2
) such as the positivities of coupling gains, conservation of modulus of
z_{i}

Lemma 2.1
.

(Positivity and symmetry of coupling gains)

Suppose that the coupling laws
\Gamma_{0}
and
\Gamma_{1}
take nonnegative values:

\Gamma_{0}(z,\tilde{z})\geq 0,\quad\Gamma_{1}(z,\tilde{z})\geq 0,\quad z,{\tilde{z}}\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{S}^{d},

and let
(Z,K,\Lambda)
be a solution to (
1.2
). Then, we have the following assertions:

(1)

If initial coupling gains satisfy

\kappa_{ij}^{0}>0,\quad\lambda_{ij}^{0}>0,\quad\forall~i,j\in\mathcal{N},

then one has positivities of coupling gains:

\kappa_{ij}(t)>0,\quad\lambda_{ij}(t)>0,\quad\forall~t>0,\quad\in i,j\mathcal{N}.

(2)

If initial coupling gains satisfy

\kappa_{ij}^{0}=\kappa_{ji}^{0},\quad\lambda_{ij}^{0}=\lambda_{ji}^{0},\quad\forall~i,j\in\mathcal{N},

then symmetries of the coupling gains are preserved:

\kappa_{ij}(t)=\kappa_{ji}(t),\quad\lambda_{ij}(t)=\lambda_{ji}(t),\quad\forall~t>0,\quad i,j\in\mathcal{N}.

Proof.

(i) For the first assertion, we use
\eqref{A-1}_{2}
and Duhamel’s principle to find the following representations: for
t\geq 0
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \kappa_{ij}(t)&=e^{-\gamma_{0}t}\left(\kappa_{ij}^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{0}e^{\gamma_{0}s}\Gamma_{0}(z_{i}(s),z_{j}(s))ds\right),\\
\lambda_{ij}(t)&=e^{-\gamma_{1}t}\left(\lambda_{ij}^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{1}e^{\gamma_{1}s}\Gamma_{1}(z_{i}(s),z_{j}(s))ds\right).\end{aligned}

Since system parameters
\mu_{k}
and
\gamma_{k}
are nonnegative, it follows from (
2.1
) that

\kappa_{ij}(t)>0,\quad\lambda_{ij}(t)>0,\quad t>0.

(ii) For the second assertion, we use the symmetry of
\kappa_{ij}^{0},~\lambda_{ij}^{0},~\Gamma_{0}
and
\Gamma_{1}
in the index exchange
i\longleftrightarrow j
to find the desired results.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

(Conservation of modulus)

Let
(Z,K,\Lambda)
be a solution to the Cauchy problem
(
1.2
). Then, the modulus
\|z_{i}\|
is a conserved quantity: for
i\in{\mathcal{N}}
,

\|z_{i}^{0}\|=1\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\|z_{i}(t)\|=1,\quad t>0.

Proof.

It follows from the symmetry of coupling strengths that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle z_{i},\dot{z_{i}}\rangle&=\langle z_{i},\Omega z_{i}\rangle+\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\kappa_{ik}\Big(\langle z_{i},z_{k}\rangle-\langle z_{k},z_{i}\rangle\Big)\langle z_{i},z_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\lambda_{ik}\Big(\langle z_{i},z_{k}\rangle-\langle z_{k},z_{i}\rangle\Big)\langle z_{i},z_{i}\rangle,\\
\langle\dot{z}_{i},z_{i}\rangle&=\langle\Omega z_{i},z_{i}\rangle+\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\kappa_{ik}\Big(\langle z_{k},z_{i}\rangle-\langle z_{i},z_{k}\rangle\Big)\langle z_{i},z_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\lambda_{ik}\Big(\langle z_{k},z_{i}\rangle-\langle z_{i},z_{k}\rangle\Big)\langle z_{i},z_{i}\rangle.\end{aligned}

Since
\Omega_{i}
is skew-hermitian, we have

(2.3)

\langle z_{i},\Omega z_{i}\rangle+\langle\Omega z_{i},z_{i}\rangle=0.

Finally, we combine (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) to obtain the desired estimate:

\frac{d}{dt}\|z_{i}\|^{2}=\frac{d}{dt}\langle z_{i},z_{i}\rangle=\langle z_{i},\dot{z}_{i}\rangle+\langle\dot{z}_{i},z_{i}\rangle=0.

∎

Now, we consider corresponding linear and nonlinear flows:

(2.4)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{f}_{j}=\Omega f_{j},\quad t>0,~~\forall~j\in{\mathcal{N}},\\
\displaystyle f_{j}(0)=f_{j}^{0},\end{cases}

and

(2.5)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{w}_{j}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\kappa_{jk}\big(w_{k}-\langle w_{k},w_{j}\rangle w_{j}\big)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\lambda_{jk}\big(\langle w_{j},w_{k}\rangle-\langle w_{k},w_{j}\rangle\big)w_{j},\vskip 2.84544pt\\
\displaystyle\dot{\kappa}_{jk}=-\gamma_{0}\kappa_{jk}+\mu_{0}\Gamma_{0}(w_{j},w_{k}),\quad\dot{\lambda}_{jk}=-\gamma_{1}\lambda_{jk}+\mu_{1}\Gamma_{1}(w_{j},w_{k}),\vskip 2.84544pt\\
(w_{j}(0),\kappa_{jk}(0),\lambda_{jk}(0))=(w_{j}^{0},\kappa_{jk}^{0},\lambda_{jk}^{0})\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R},\quad i,j\in{\mathcal{N}}.\end{cases}

Let
R
and
L_{j}
be solution operators to (
2.4
) and
(
2.5
)
, respectively. Then, solutions to (
2.4
) and
w_{j}
in
(
2.5
)
can be represented as follows.

f_{j}(t)=R(t)f_{j}^{0}=:e^{\Omega t}f_{j}^{0},\quad w_{j}(t):=L_{j}(t)(W^{0},K^{0},\Lambda^{0}),\quad\forall~j\in{\mathcal{N}}.

In next lemma, we show that the full solution operator to (
1.2
) can be expressed as a composition of
R
and
L_{j}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

(Solution splitting property)
Let
(Z,K,\Lambda)
be a solution to system (
1.2
) with initial data
(Z^{0},K^{0},\Lambda^{0})
satisfying

\Omega_{j}\equiv\Omega,\quad j\in\mathcal{N}.

Then,
z_{j}
can be decomposed as a composition of
f_{j}
and
w_{j}
:

z_{j}(t)=R(t)\circ L_{j}(t)(Z^{0},K^{0},\Lambda^{0}).

Proof.

We substitute
z_{j}=e^{\Omega t}w_{j}
into (
1.1
) to obtain

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} e^{\Omega t}\dot{w}_{j}+\Omega e^{\Omega t}w_{j}=\Omega e^{\Omega t}w_{j}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\kappa_{jk}\big(e^{\Omega t}w_{k}-\langle e^{\Omega t}w_{k},e^{\Omega t}w_{j}\rangle e^{\Omega t}w_{j}\big)\\
+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\lambda_{jk}\big(\langle e^{\Omega t}w_{j},e^{\Omega t}w_{k}\rangle-\langle e^{\Omega t}w_{k},e^{\Omega t}w_{j}\rangle\big)e^{\Omega t}w_{j}.\end{aligned}

On the other hand, we use the skew-hermitian property of
\Omega
to find

(2.7)

(e^{\Omega t})^{\dagger}=(e^{\Omega t})^{-1}.

Finally, we combine (
2.6
) and (
2.7
) to get

\dot{w}_{j}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\kappa_{jk}\big(w_{k}-\langle w_{k},w_{j}\rangle w_{j}\big)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\lambda_{jk}\big(\langle w_{j},w_{k}\rangle-\langle w_{k},w_{j}\rangle\big)w_{j},

so that
w_{j}(t)=L_{j}(t)(W^{0},K^{0},\Lambda^{0})
. This gives a desired result.
∎

Note that the LHS model
\eqref{A-1}_{1}
contains two terms involving with
\kappa_{jk}
and
\lambda_{jk}
. To see the role of each coupling gain separately, we consider the following subsystems:

\bullet
(Subsystem A): If we impose the following conditions on (

\displaystyle\lambda^{0}_{jk}=0\quad\text{for all}\quad j,k\in\mathcal{N},\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{1}=0,

then we have

\lambda_{jk}(t)=0,\quad\forall~t>0,\quad j,k\in\mathcal{N}.

In this case, we have Subsystem A:

(2.8)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{z}_{j}=\Omega z_{j}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\kappa_{jk}\big(\langle z_{j},z_{j}\rangle z_{k}-\langle z_{k},z_{j}\rangle z_{j}\big),\quad t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\dot{\kappa}_{jk}=-\gamma_{0}\kappa_{jk}+\mu_{0}\Gamma_{0}(z_{j},z_{k}),\quad j,k\in\mathcal{N},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
(z_{j}(0),\kappa_{jk}(0))=(z_{j}^{0},\kappa_{jk}^{0})\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.\end{cases}

\bullet
(Subsystem B): If we impose the following condition on (
1.2
):

\displaystyle\kappa_{jk}^{0}=0\quad\text{for all}\quad j,k\in\mathcal{N},\quad\text{and}\quad\mu_{0}=0,

then we have

\kappa_{jk}(t)=0\quad\forall~t>0,\quad j,k\in\mathcal{N}.

In this case, one has Subsystem B:

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{z}_{j}=\Omega z_{j}+\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\lambda_{jk}\big(\langle z_{j},z_{k}\rangle-\langle z_{k},z_{j}\rangle\big)z_{j},\quad t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\dot{\lambda}_{jk}=-\gamma_{1}\lambda_{jk}+\mu_{1}\Gamma_{1}(z_{j},z_{k}),\quad i,j\in\mathcal{N},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
(z_{j}(0),\lambda_{jk}(0))=(z_{j}^{0},\lambda_{jk}^{0})\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.\end{cases}

2.2.
Reductions to other aggregation models

In this subsection, we study the relations between (
1.2
) and other first-order aggregation models with adaptive couplings.

For a real vector-valued state
\{z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{N}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, the interaction terms in Subsystem B become zero:

\langle z_{j},z_{k}\rangle-\langle z_{k},z_{j}\rangle=0,\quad j,k\in{\mathcal{N}}

so that Subsystem B reduces to the free flow:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{z}_{j}=\Omega z_{j},\quad t>0,\quad j,k\in{\mathcal{N}},\\
\displaystyle\dot{\lambda}_{jk}=-\gamma_{1}\lambda_{jk}+\mu_{1}\Gamma_{1}(z_{j},z_{k}).\end{cases}

In next lemma, we show that the real-valuedness of the components of
z_{j}
and
\kappa_{ij}
are propagated along system (
2.8
).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(Z,K,\Lambda)
be a solution to (
2.8

2.3.
From the Stuart-Landau model to the LHS model

In this subsection, we explain how the special coupling gain relation
\lambda_{jk}=-\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{jk}
can arise in the reduction from the generalized Stuart-Landau model to the LHS model.

Consider a
generalized Stuart-Landau model
on
\mathbb{C}^{d+1}
:

(2.16)

\frac{dz_{j}}{dt}=\big((1-\|z_{j}\|^{2})I_{d+1}+\Omega\big)z_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(z_{k}-z_{j}),

where
z_{j}\in\mathbb{C}^{d+1}
for all
j\in\mathcal{N}
,
\Omega
is a skew-hermitian matrix with the size
(d+1)\times(d+1)
and
I_{d+1}
is the identity matrix with the size
(d+1)\times(d+1)
.

We substitute the ansatz:

z_{j}=r_{j}w_{j},\quad r_{j}=\|z_{j}\|\quad\mbox{and}\quad w_{j}=\frac{z_{j}}{\|z_{j}\|},\quad\forall~j\in\mathcal{N}

into (
2.16
) to see

(2.17)

\dot{r}_{j}w_{j}+r_{j}\dot{w}_{j}=(1-r_{j}^{2})r_{j}w_{j}+r_{j}\Omega w_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(r_{k}w_{k}-r_{j}w_{j}).

Then,
\langle w_{j},\eqref{B-10}\rangle
implies

(2.18)

\displaystyle\dot{r}_{j}+r_{j}\langle w_{j},\dot{w}_{j}\rangle=(1-r_{j}^{2})r_{j}+r_{j}\langle w_{j},\Omega w_{j}\rangle+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(r_{k}\langle w_{j},w_{k}\rangle-r_{j}).

If we take the real part of (
2.18
), one has

(2.19)

\displaystyle\dot{r}_{j}=(1-r_{j}^{2})r_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(r_{k}\mathrm{Re}(\langle w_{j},w_{k}\rangle)-r_{j}).

Here we used the relations:

\langle w_{j},\dot{w}_{j}\rangle+\langle\dot{w}_{j},w_{j}\rangle=\langle w_{j},\dot{w}_{j}\rangle+\overline{\langle w_{j},\dot{w}_{j}\rangle}=2\mbox{Re}~\langle w_{j},\dot{w}_{j}\rangle,\quad\langle w_{j},\Omega w_{j}\rangle=0.

Now, we combine (
2.18
) and (
2.19
) to get

\displaystyle\dot{w}_{j}=\Omega w_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{r_{k}}{r_{j}}\Big(w_{k}-\mathrm{Re}(\langle w_{k},w_{j}\rangle)w_{j}\Big).

Similarly, we impose
r_{j}\equiv 1
on (
2.19
) to obtain

(2.20)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{w}_{j}&=\Omega w_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big[w_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\langle w_{k},w_{j}\rangle+\langle w_{j},w_{k}\rangle\Big)w_{j}\Big]\\
&=\Omega w_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(w_{k}-\langle w_{k},w_{j}\rangle w_{j})-\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\langle w_{j},w_{k}\rangle-\langle w_{k},w_{j}\rangle)w_{j}\\
&=\Omega w_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big[w_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\langle w_{k},w_{j}\rangle+\langle w_{j},w_{k}\rangle\Big)w_{j}\Big].\end{aligned}

Note that this is the special case of the LHS model (
1.1
) with
\kappa_{1}=-\frac{\kappa_{0}}{2}
.

Next, we show that system (
2.20
) can be embedded as a system on the Euclidean space by extending
(d+1)
-dimensional complex-valued vector
w\in\mathbb{C}^{d+1}
to
2(d+1)
-dimensional real-valued vector
\tilde{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{2(d+1)}
with the following map:

w=(w^{1},\cdots,w^{d+1})\quad\mapsto\quad\tilde{w}=\big(\mathrm{Re}(w^{1}),\cdots,\mathrm{Re}(w^{d+1}),\mathrm{Im}(w^{1}),\cdots,\mathrm{Im}(w^{d+1})\big).

Now we will rewrite (
2.20
) in terms of
\{\tilde{w}_{j}\}
. First, it is easy to see that

(2.21)

\dot{\tilde{w}}_{j}=\tilde{\dot{w}}_{j}.

By simple calculation, we have

\displaystyle\Omega w_{j}

\displaystyle=\big(\mathrm{Re}(\Omega)+\mathrm{i}\mathrm{Im}(\Omega)\big)\big(\mathrm{Re}(w_{j})+\mathrm{i}\mathrm{Im}(w_{j})\big)

\displaystyle=\big(\mathrm{Re}(\Omega)\mathrm{Re}(w_{j})-\mathrm{Im}(\Omega)\mathrm{Im}(w_{j})\big)+\mathrm{i}\big(\mathrm{Im}(\Omega)\mathrm{Re}(w_{j})+\mathrm{Re}(\Omega)\mathrm{Im}(w_{j})\big).

This yields

(2.22)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{Re}(\Omega w_{j})=\mathrm{Re}(\Omega)\mathrm{Re}(w_{j})-\mathrm{Im}(\Omega)\mathrm{Im}(w_{j}),\\
&\mathrm{Im}(\Omega w_{j})=\mathrm{Im}(\Omega)\mathrm{Re}(w_{j})+\mathrm{Re}(\Omega)\mathrm{Im}(w_{j}).\end{aligned}

Since
\Omega
is a
(d+1)\times(d+1)
complex skew-hermitian matrix, we know that
\mathrm{Re}(\Omega)
and
\mathrm{Im}(\Omega)
are symmetric. From this, we can define
2(d+1)\times 2(d+1)
skew-symmetric matrix
\tilde{\Omega}
as follows:

\tilde{\Omega}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathrm{Re}(\Omega)&-\mathrm{Im}(\Omega)\\
\mathrm{Im}(\Omega)&\mathrm{Re}(\Omega)\end{bmatrix}.

Then we have

(2.23)

\widetilde{\Omega w_{j}}=\tilde{\Omega}\tilde{w}_{j}.

Next, we rewrite
\langle w_{k},w_{j}\rangle+\langle w_{j},w_{k}\rangle
in terns of
\tilde{w}_{k}
and
\tilde{w}_{j}
as follows. By definition of the complex inner-product, we have

(2.24)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \langle w_{k},w_{j}\rangle+\langle w_{j},w_{k}\rangle&=w_{k}^{\dagger}w_{j}+w_{j}^{\dagger}w_{k}=2\mathrm{Re}(w_{k})^{T}\mathrm{Re}(w_{j})+2\mathrm{Im}(w_{k})^{T}\mathrm{Im}(w_{j})\\
&=2\tilde{w}_{k}^{T}\tilde{w}_{j}=2\langle\tilde{w}_{k},\tilde{w}_{j}\rangle.\end{aligned}

Finally we can express system (
2.20
) with
\{\tilde{w}_{j}\}
and
\Omega
using (
2.21
), (
2.22
), (
2.23
) and (
2.24
) to get

\dot{\tilde{w}}_{j}=\tilde{\Omega}\tilde{w}_{j}+\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\tilde{w}_{k}-\langle\tilde{w}_{k},\tilde{w}_{j}\rangle\tilde{w}_{j}\right)

which is exactly the Lohe sphere model. In summary, from the proper map between
\mathbb{C}^{d+1}
and
\mathbb{R}^{2(d+1)}
, we can transform the special case of the LHS model (
1.1
) with
\lambda_{jk}=-\frac{\kappa_{jk}}{2}
to the LS model. Thus, we can see that system (
2.21
) is a gradient flow as in the LS model (see Proposition 5.1 in
[
18
]
).

3.
Frameworks for complete aggregation and main results

In this section, we briefly present our main results and sufficient frameworks leading to complete aggregation in the sense of Definition
1.1
. As noted in the previous section, we consider four different cases depending on the relations between coupling gains
\kappa_{jk},~\lambda_{jk}
and coupling law
\Gamma_{0}
(anti-Hebbian or Hebbian law).

\diamond
(Coupling gain pair): Depending on the relation between
\kappa_{jk}
and
\lambda_{jk}
, we consider the following two cases:

•

Stuart-Landau coupling gain pair
(\kappa_{jk},\lambda_{jk})
:

\kappa_{jk}>0,\quad\lambda_{jk}=-\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{jk},\quad j,k\in{\mathcal{N}}.

•

General coupling gain pair
(\kappa_{jk},\lambda_{jk})
:

\kappa_{jk}>0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\lambda_{jk}\in\mathbb{R},\quad j,k\in{\mathcal{N}}.

\diamond
(Coupling law
\Gamma_{0}
): Consider anti-Hebbian and Hebbian laws:

(3.1)

\Gamma_{0}(w,z)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\|w-z\|^{2},\quad&\mbox{Anti-Hebbian law}\\
\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{2}\|w-z\|^{2},\quad&\mbox{Hebbian law}\end{cases}

The motivation for (
3.1
) can be explained as follows. In literature
[
16
,
19
,
20
]
on the synchronization with an adaptive coupling law, the following coupling law

(3.2)

\Gamma(\theta,{\tilde{\theta}})=\cos(\theta-\tilde{\theta})

was often employed. Note that when the phase difference between the interactiong oscillators is small, it increases the mutual coupling strength. Thus, it is called Hebbian coupling law. In contrast, when the phase difference is small, there is a case in which the rate of increment in coupling strength becomes small. This is called “
anti-Hebbian coupling law
” and the ansatz:

(3.3)

\Gamma(\theta,{\tilde{\theta}})=|\sin(\theta-{\tilde{\theta}})|

was used in aforementioned literature. Note that on
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{S}^{d}
,

\Gamma_{0}(z,{\tilde{z}})=\|z-\tilde{z}\|^{2}=2(1-\mathrm{Re}\langle z,{\tilde{z}}\rangle)=2\left(1-\mathrm{Re}\big(\cos\theta(z,\tilde{z})\big)\right),

where
\theta(z,\tilde{z})
is the angle between
z
and
\tilde{z}
, and
\Gamma_{0}
becomes smaller when the angle is small. In this sense, it plays the same role as anti-Hebbian law (
3.3
). In contrast, real part of
\Gamma_{0}(z,{\tilde{z}})=1-\frac{1}{2}\|z-\tilde{z}\|^{2}
exhibits the same dynamics as (
3.2
).

3.1.
SL coupling gain pair

Consider the Stuart-Landau coupling gain pair:

(3.4)

\kappa_{jk}>0,\quad\lambda_{jk}=-\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{jk},\quad\forall~t\geq 0,\quad j,k\in{\mathcal{N}}.

In fact, one can show that once initial gain pair satisfy (

), then the relation (
3.4
) will be propagated along (
1.2
) under suitable conditions on system parameters and coupling laws (see Lemma

4.

In this section, we study emergent dynamics of system (

First, we show that if the initial SL coupling gain pair satisfies (

), then it is propagated along the dynamics (
1.2

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that system parameters and initial coupling strengths satisfy

(4.2)

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{1}=\gamma,\quad\mu_{0}=\mu_{1}=\mu,\quad\Gamma_{0}+2\Gamma_{1}=0,\quad\lambda_{ij}^{0}=-\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{ij}^{0},

and let
(Z,K,\Lambda)
be a solution of (
1.1
). Then we have

(4.3)

\lambda_{ij}(t)=-\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{ij}(t),\quad\forall~t\geq 0,\quad i,j\in\mathcal{N}.

Proof.

It follows from (
1.1
) and (
4.2
) that

\dot{\kappa}_{ij}=-\gamma\kappa_{ij}+\mu\Gamma_{0}(z_{i},z_{j}),\quad\dot{\lambda}_{ij}=-\gamma\lambda_{ij}-\frac{\mu}{2}\Gamma_{0}(z_{i},z_{j}),\quad t>0.

This yields

\frac{d}{dt}(\kappa_{ij}+2\lambda_{ij})=-\gamma(\kappa_{ij}+2\lambda_{ij}).

By integrating the above relation, one has the desired estimate:

(\kappa_{ij}+2\lambda_{ij})(t)=e^{-\gamma t}(\kappa_{ij}^{0}+2\lambda_{ij}^{0})=0,\quad t\geq 0.

∎

Next, we substitute (
4.3
) into (
1.1
) to get the dynamics for
(Z,K)
:

(4.4)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{z}_{j}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\kappa_{jk}\left[z_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\langle z_{j},z_{k}\rangle+\langle z_{k},z_{j}\rangle\Big)z_{j}\right],~~t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle\dot{\kappa}_{jk}=-\gamma\kappa_{jk}+\mu\Gamma_{0}(z_{j},z_{k}),\quad j,k\in\mathcal{N},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle(z_{j},\kappa_{jk})(0)=(z_{j}^{0},\kappa_{jk}^{0})\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.\end{cases}

In what follows, we consider two coupling laws for
\Gamma_{0}
as prototype examples for the anti-Hebbian and the Hebbian couplings between the coupling gain and state:

(4.5)

\Gamma_{0}(z,{\tilde{z}})=\|z-\tilde{z}\|^{2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Gamma_{0}(z,{\tilde{z}})=1-\frac{1}{2}\|z-\tilde{z}\|^{2}.

4.1.
Anti-Hebbian coupling law

Consider system (
4.4
) with anti-Hebbian coupling law
\eqref{D-0-3}_{1}
:

(4.6)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{z}_{j}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\kappa_{jk}\left[z_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\Big(\langle z_{j},z_{k}\rangle+\langle z_{k},z_{j}\rangle\Big)z_{j}\right],~~t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle\dot{\kappa}_{jk}=-\gamma\kappa_{jk}+\mu\|z_{j}-z_{k}\|^{2},\quad j,k\in\mathcal{N},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle(z_{j},\kappa_{jk})(0)=(z_{j}^{0},\kappa_{jk}^{0})\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.\end{cases}

To study emergent dynamics of (
4.6
), we recall a Lyapunov functional
{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}
in (
3.7
):

\mathcal{L}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\|z_{i}-z_{j}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{4\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2}.

On
\mathbb{H}\mathbb{S}^{d}
, the functional
{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}
can be rewritten as follows:

(4.7)

\mathcal{L}_{ij}=1-\mbox{Re}~\langle z_{i},z_{j}\rangle+\frac{1}{4\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2}.

Thus, it is natural to study the time-evolution of
\langle z_{i},z_{j}\rangle
. For notational simplicity, we use

(4.8)

h_{ij}:=\langle z_{i},z_{j}\rangle,\quad R_{ij}:=\mathrm{Re}~h_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}(h_{ij}+h_{ji}),\quad I_{ij}:=\mathrm{Im}~h_{ij}=\frac{1}{2\mathrm{i}}(h_{ij}-h_{ji}).

Then, it is easy to see

R_{ii}=1,\quad I_{ii}=0,\quad|R_{ij}|\leq|h_{ij}|\leq 1,\quad R_{ij}=R_{ji}\quad\mbox{and}\quad I_{ij}=-I_{ji},\quad i,j\in{\mathcal{N}}.

We can rewrite (
4.6
) and a Lyapunov functional in (
4.7
):

\dot{z}_{j}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\kappa_{jk}(z_{k}-R_{jk}z_{j}),\quad\mathcal{L}_{ij}=1-R_{ij}+\frac{1}{4\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2}.

To sum up, system (
4.6
) on
\mathbb{HS}^{d}
becomes

(4.9)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{z}_{j}=\frac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\kappa_{jk}(z_{k}-R_{jk}z_{j}),\quad t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle\dot{\kappa}_{jk}=-\gamma\kappa_{jk}+\mu\|z_{j}-z_{k}\|^{2},\quad j,k\in{\mathcal{N}},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
(z_{j},\kappa_{jk})(0)=(z_{j}^{0},\kappa_{jk}^{0})\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.\end{cases}

Next, we study the time-evolution of
{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}
in a series of lemmas.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(Z,K)
be a solution to (
4.9
). Then
\mathcal{L}_{ij}
satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{L}_{ij}=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}R_{ik}+\kappa_{jk}R_{jk})(1-R_{ij})-\frac{\gamma}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2},\quad t>0.

Proof.

By direct calculations, one has

(4.10)

\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{L}_{ij}=-\frac{1}{2}(\dot{h}_{ij}+\dot{h}_{ji})+\frac{1}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})(\dot{\kappa}_{ik}-\dot{\kappa}_{jk}).

Note that the terms in the R.H.S. of (
4.10
) can be estimated as follows.

\bullet
(Estimate of the first term in (
4.10
)): We use (
4.8
) and (
4.9
) to find

(4.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{h}_{ij}+\dot{h}_{ji}=&\langle z_{i},\dot{z}_{j}\rangle+\langle\dot{z}_{i},z_{j}\rangle+\langle z_{j},\dot{z}_{i}\rangle+\langle\dot{z}_{j},z_{i}\rangle\\
=&\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathrm{Re}(\langle z_{i},\dot{z}_{j}\rangle+\langle\dot{z}_{i},z_{j}\rangle)\\
=&\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathrm{Re}\Big(\kappa_{jk}(h_{ik}-R_{jk}h_{ij})+\kappa_{ik}(h_{kj}-R_{ik}h_{ij})\Big)\\
=&\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(\kappa_{jk}(R_{ik}-R_{jk}R_{ij})+\kappa_{ik}(R_{jk}-R_{ik}R_{ji})\Big)\\
=&\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big((\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})(R_{jk}-R_{ik})+(R_{ik}\kappa_{ik}+R_{jk}\kappa_{jk})(1-R_{ij})\Big).\end{aligned}

\bullet
(Estimate of the second term in (
4.10
)): Similar to the first term, one has

(4.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})(\dot{\kappa}_{ik}-\dot{\kappa}_{jk})\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt=\frac{1}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})(-\gamma\kappa_{ik}-\mu h_{ik}-\mu h_{ki}+\gamma\kappa_{jk}+\mu h_{jk}+\mu h_{kj})\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt=-\frac{\gamma}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2}-\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})(h_{ik}+h_{ki}-h_{jk}-h_{kj})\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt=-\frac{\gamma}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})(R_{jk}-R_{ik}).\end{aligned}

In (
4.10
), we combine (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) to find the desired result.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(Z,K)
be a solution to (
4.9
) with the initial data
(Z^{0},K^{0})
satisfying the following relations:

\max_{i,j}\mathcal{L}_{ij}^{0}<1.

Then, we have the following assertions:

(1)

R_{ij}
and
\kappa_{ij}
are strictly positive:

R_{ij}(t)>0,\quad\kappa_{ij}(t)>0,\quad t\geq 0,\quad i,j\in\mathcal{N}.

(2)

\mathcal{L}_{ij}
is non-increasing function:

\mathcal{L}_{ij}(t)\leq\mathcal{L}_{ij}^{0},\quad t\geq 0,\quad i,j\in\mathcal{N}.

Proof.

Let
(i,j)\in{\mathcal{N}}^{2}
be fixed. Since
\kappa_{ij}^{0}>0
, by Lemma
2.1
, one has

\kappa_{ij}(t)>0,\quad t>0.

Now, it follows from
\mathcal{L}_{ij}^{0}<1
that

(4.13)

R_{ij}^{0}>\frac{1}{4\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}^{0}-\kappa_{jk}^{0})^{2}\geq 0.

We claim:

R_{ij}(t)>0,\quad t\geq 0.

For this, we introduce a set
{\mathcal{T}}_{ij}
:

{\mathcal{T}}_{ij}:=\{\tau\in[0,\infty):R_{ij}(t)>0,\quad t\in[0,\tau)\}.

Then, by (
4.13
) and continuity of
R_{ij}
, one has

{\mathcal{T}}_{ij}\not=\emptyset.

Suppose that

t_{ij}^{*}:=\sup{\mathcal{T}}_{ij}<\infty.

Then, one has

\displaystyle\lim_{t\nearrow t^{*}_{ij}}R_{ij}(t)=0.

We choose the index
(i_{0},j_{0})
by

(i_{0},j_{0}):=\argmin_{(k,l)}t^{*}_{kl}.

By the minimality of
t^{*}_{i_{0}j_{0}}
and Lemma
4.2
, we have

1-R_{i_{0}j_{0}}(t)\leq\mathcal{L}_{i_{0}j_{0}}(t)<\mathcal{L}_{i_{0}j_{0}}^{0},\quad\text{so that}\quad R_{i_{0}j_{0}}(t)>1-\mathcal{L}_{i_{0}j_{0}}^{0}>0,\quad t\in(0,t^{*}_{i_{0}j_{0}}).

We take
t\nearrow t^{*}_{i_{0}j_{0}}
to derive a contradiction:

0=\lim_{t\nearrow t^{*}_{i_{0}j_{0}}}R_{i_{0}j_{0}}(t)\geq 1-\mathcal{L}_{i_{0}j_{0}}^{0}>0,

Hence, we verified the claim:

t^{*}_{i_{0}j_{0}}=\infty\quad\mbox{and}\quad R_{ij}(t)>0,\quad t\in(0,\infty).

By minimality, we have

t^{*}_{ij}=\infty\quad\mbox{for each index $(i,j)$}.

On the other hand, we have

1-R_{ij}\geq 0.

Therefore, it follows from Lemma
4.2
that the derivative of
\mathcal{L}_{ij}
is not positive for every
t\in[0,\infty)
which yields the desired result.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
: Let
(Z,K)

) with the initial data

satisfying

To apply Lemma
4.4
to
\kappa_{ij}

in order to verify uniform continuity of
\kappa_{ij}

(4.14)

\displaystyle\dot{\kappa}_{ij}=-\gamma\kappa_{ij}+\mu\|z_{i}-z_{j}\|^{2}\leq-\gamma\kappa_{ij}+4\mu,

\kappa_{ij}

1-R_{ij}(t)<\mathcal{L}^{0}_{ij},\quad\text{therefore}\quad R_{ij}(t)>1-\mathcal{L}^{0}_{ij}>1-\mathcal{L}^{0}_{IJ}>0,\quad t>0.

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{1-R_{ij}}{N}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}R_{ik}+\kappa_{jk}R_{jk})-\frac{\gamma}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2}\quad(\because\text{Lemma \ref{L4.2})}

for any index

and

. This implies

\dot{\kappa}_{ij}=-\gamma\kappa_{ij}+\mu\|z_{i}-z_{j}\|^{2},\quad t>0.

4.5

\bullet
Case B

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\big((1-\mathcal{L}^{0}_{IJ})(\kappa_{ik}+\kappa_{jk})\big)dt\\

(4.19)

\dot{R}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}(\dot{h}_{ij}+\dot{h}_{ji})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big((\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})(R_{jk}-R_{ik})+(R_{ik}\kappa_{ik}+R_{jk}\kappa_{jk})(1-R_{ij})\Big).

\ddot{\kappa}_{ij}=-\gamma\dot{\kappa}_{ij}-2\mu\dot{R}_{ij},

which leads to uniform continuity of
\dot{\kappa}_{ij}
. As integration of
\dot{\kappa}_{ij}
is finite from (
4.19
):

\int_{0}^{\infty}\dot{\kappa}_{ij}(s)ds=-\kappa_{ij}^{0},

again from Lemma
4.4
, we can conclude that

(4.20)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\dot{\kappa}_{ik}(t)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\dot{\kappa}_{jk}(t)=0.

Therefore, taking limit
t\to\infty
to the dynamics

\dot{\kappa}_{ij}=-\gamma\kappa_{ij}+\mu\|z_{i}-z_{j}\|^{2},

with (
4.19
) and (
5.21
) yield the desired result

\lim_{t\to\infty}\|z_{i}(t)-z_{j}(t)\|=0.

∎

4.2.
Hebbian coupling law

Consider system (
4.4
) with the Hebbian coupling law
\eqref{D-0-3}_{2}
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{z}_{j}=\frac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\kappa_{jk}(z_{k}-R_{jk}z_{j}),\quad t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle\dot{\kappa}_{jk}=-\gamma\kappa_{jk}+\mu\left(1-\displaystyle\frac{\|z_{j}-z_{k}\|^{2}}{2}\right),\quad j,k\in{\mathcal{N}},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle(z_{j},\kappa_{jk})(0)=(z_{j}^{0},\kappa_{jk}^{0})\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}.\end{cases}

For a given system parameters
\gamma
and
\mu
, we choose positive constants
\kappa_{m}
and
\kappa_{M}
such that

(4.21)

\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{M}<\kappa_{m}\leq\frac{2\mu}{\gamma}\left(1-\frac{\kappa_{m}}{\kappa_{M}}\right).

Proposition 4.1
.

For positive constants
\kappa_{m}
and
\kappa_{M}
satisfying (
4.21
), suppose initial data satisfy

(4.22)

{\mathcal{D}}(Z^{0})<\frac{2\kappa_{m}}{\kappa_{M}}-1,\quad\kappa_{m}<\min_{i,j}\kappa^{0}_{ij},

and let
(Z,K)
be a solution to system (
3.10
) satisfying a priori assumption:

(4.23)

\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}\max_{i,j}\kappa_{ij}(t)\leq\kappa_{M}.

Then, there exist positive constants
D_{0}
and
D_{1}
such that

{\mathcal{D}}(Z(t))\leq D_{0}e^{-D_{1}t},\quad t>0.

Proof.

By
\eqref{C-2-3}_{2}
and continuity of solution, the set

\mathcal{{\tilde{T}}}_{ij}:=\{\tau\in(0,\infty)~:~\kappa_{ij}(t)>\kappa_{m},~\forall t\in(0,\tau)\}\not=\emptyset.

Now, we set

\tilde{\mathcal{T}}:=\bigcap_{i,j}\mathcal{{\tilde{T}}}_{ij}=\Big\{\tau\in(0,\infty)~:~\min_{i,j}\kappa_{ij}(t)>\kappa_{m},~\forall t\in(0,\tau)\Big\},\quad{\tilde{t}}^{*}:=\sup\mathcal{{\tilde{T}}}.

In the course of proof of Lemma
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\dot{h}_{ij}+\dot{h}_{ji}=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\big((\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})(R_{jk}-R_{ik})+(R_{ik}\kappa_{ik}+R_{jk}\kappa_{jk})(1-R_{ij})\big).

In the sequel, for notational simplicity, we set

{\mathcal{D}}_{ij}:={\mathcal{D}}_{ij}(Z),\quad{\mathcal{D}}:={\mathcal{D}}(Z),\quad{\mathcal{D}}^{0}:={\mathcal{D}}(Z^{0}).

This and defining relation (
4.22
) of
{\mathcal{D}}
imply

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{{\mathcal{D}}}_{ij}&=\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}(2-h_{ij}-h_{ji})=-\frac{1}{2}(\dot{h}_{ij}+\dot{h}_{ji})\\
&=-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\big((\kappa_{ik}+\kappa_{jk}){\mathcal{D}}_{ij}\big)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\big(\kappa_{ik}{\mathcal{D}}_{ik}+\kappa_{jk}{\mathcal{D}}_{jk}\big){\mathcal{D}}_{ij}\\
&\hskip 5.69046pt+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})({\mathcal{D}}_{jk}-{\mathcal{D}}_{ik})\\
&=:{\mathcal{J}}_{11}+{\mathcal{J}}_{12}+{\mathcal{J}}_{13}.\end{aligned}

From now on, we will regard
i
and
j
as a function of
t
. For each
t
, we assume that
i,j
are indices such that

{\mathcal{D}}={\mathcal{D}}_{ij}.

It follows from (
4.22
) and the definition of
{\tilde{t}}^{*}
that

(4.24)

{\mathcal{J}_{11}\geq 2\kappa_{m}{\mathcal{D}}},\qquad\mathcal{J}_{12}\leq 2\kappa_{M}{\mathcal{D}}^{2},\qquad\mathcal{J}_{13}\leq 2(\kappa_{M}-\kappa_{m}){\mathcal{D}},\quad t\in[0,{\tilde{t}}^{*}).

This leads to a differential inequality:

(4.25)

\dot{{\mathcal{D}}}<-2(2\kappa_{m}-\kappa_{M}){\mathcal{D}}+2\kappa_{M}{\mathcal{D}}^{2},\quad t\in[0,{\tilde{t}}^{*}),

where the factor
(2\kappa_{m}-\kappa_{M})
is positive from the first inequality of (
4.22
).

We apply the comparison principle to (
4.25
) to get

{\mathcal{D}}(t)\leq\frac{1}{\left(\frac{1}{{\mathcal{D}}^{0}}-\frac{2\kappa_{m}-\kappa_{M}}{\kappa_{M}}\right)e^{(2\kappa_{m}-\kappa_{M})t}+\frac{2\kappa_{m}-\kappa_{M}}{\kappa_{M}}},\quad t\in[0,{\tilde{t}}^{*}),

i.e. exponential decay occurs in
t\in[0,{\tilde{t}}^{*})
. Hence the proof is done if we verify
{\tilde{t}}^{*}=\infty
.

Now we use the initial condition
{\mathcal{D}}^{0}<\frac{2\kappa_{m}}{\kappa_{M}}-1
and (
4.25
) to obtain

-2(2\kappa_{m}-\kappa_{M}){\mathcal{D}}+2\kappa_{M}{\mathcal{D}}^{2}<0,\quad\text{whenever}\quad{\mathcal{D}}\in\left(0,\frac{2\kappa_{m}}{\kappa_{M}}-1\right).

Therefore,
{\mathcal{D}}
is decreasing in
t\in[0,{\tilde{t}}^{*})
. Hence we have

\dot{\kappa}_{ij}=-\gamma\kappa_{ij}+\mu-\mu{\mathcal{D}}_{ij}\geq-\gamma\kappa_{ij}+\mu-\mu{\mathcal{D}}\geq-\gamma\kappa_{ij}+2\mu\frac{\kappa_{M}-\kappa_{m}}{\kappa_{M}},\quad t\in[0,{\tilde{t}}^{*}).

By comparison principle, one has

\displaystyle\kappa_{ij}\geq\left(\kappa_{ij}^{0}-\frac{2\mu}{\gamma}\frac{\kappa_{M}-\kappa_{m}}{\kappa_{M}}\right)e^{-\gamma t}+\frac{2\mu}{\gamma}\left(\frac{\kappa_{M}-\kappa_{m}}{\kappa_{M}}\right)\geq\left(\kappa_{ij}^{0}-\kappa_{m}\right)e^{-\gamma t}+\kappa_{m},\quad t\in[0,{\tilde{t}}^{*}),

where the last equality holds from the second inequality of (
4.21
). By definition of
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}
, there exist indices
k
and
l
such that

\kappa_{m}=\lim_{t\nearrow{\tilde{t}}^{*}}\kappa_{kl}.

Therefore if
{\tilde{t}}^{*}
is finite, one has

\displaystyle\kappa_{m}=\lim_{t\nearrow\tilde{t}^{*}}\kappa_{kl}\geq\lim_{t\nearrow\tilde{t}^{*}}\left(\kappa_{kl}^{0}-\kappa_{m}\right)e^{-\gamma t}+\kappa_{m}=\left(\kappa_{kl}^{0}-\kappa_{m}\right)e^{-\gamma\tilde{t}^{*}}+\kappa_{m}>\kappa_{m},

which is contradictory, and we obtain our desired result.
∎

Now we are ready to provide a proof of our second main result.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

Recall the conditions (
3.11
):

(4.26)

0<\kappa<\min\left\{\frac{\mu}{\gamma},~\min_{i,j}\kappa_{ij}^{0}\right\},\quad\max\left\{\max_{i,j}\kappa^{0}_{ij},~\frac{\mu}{\gamma}\right\}\leq\frac{2\mu\kappa}{2\mu-\gamma\kappa},\quad{\mathcal{D}}(Z^{0})<1-\frac{\gamma}{\mu}\kappa.

Now, it suffices to show that the above conditions satisfy (
4.22
) and (
4.23
):

(4.27)

\kappa_{m}<\min_{i,j}\kappa^{0}_{ij},\quad{\mathcal{D}}(Z^{0})<\frac{2\kappa_{m}}{\kappa_{M}}-1,\quad\quad\sup_{0\leq t<\infty}\max_{i,j}\kappa_{ij}(t)\leq\kappa_{M}.

We first figure out
\kappa_{m}
and
\kappa_{M}
satisfying (
4.21
):

(4.28)

\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{M}<\kappa_{m},\quad\kappa_{m}\leq\frac{2\mu}{\gamma}\left(1-\frac{\kappa_{m}}{\kappa_{M}}\right).

Since
\kappa
is a candidate of
\kappa_{m}
, we assume that
\kappa_{m}
satisfies (
4.26
). Rewriting
\eqref{D-4-1}_{2}
, we have

(4.29)

\displaystyle\kappa_{m}\leq\frac{2\mu}{\gamma}\left(1-\frac{\kappa_{m}}{\kappa_{M}}\right)\Longleftrightarrow\frac{2\mu\kappa_{m}}{2\mu-\gamma\kappa_{m}}\leq\kappa_{M}.

Optimizing
\kappa_{M}
under (

), we have

\frac{2\mu\kappa_{m}}{2\mu-\gamma\kappa_{m}}=\kappa_{M}.

Therefore, as we set

(4.30)

\kappa_{m}=\kappa\quad\mbox{and}\quad\kappa_{M}=\frac{2\mu\kappa}{2\mu-\gamma\kappa},

\eqref{D-4-1}_{2}
is achieved. In particular, as
\kappa
satisfies
\eqref{D-3}_{1}
, we have

\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{M}<\kappa_{m}\Longleftrightarrow\kappa<\frac{\mu}{\gamma},

which is true from
\eqref{D-3}_{1}

is achieved.

\bullet
(Verification of (

)): Clearly,
\eqref{D-3}_{1}

\eqref{D-4}_{1}
. By the setting (

\frac{2\kappa_{m}}{\kappa_{M}}-1=\frac{2\kappa}{\frac{2\mu\kappa}{2\mu-\gamma k}}-1=1-\frac{\gamma\kappa}{\mu}.

Hence

\bullet
(Verification of (

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \kappa_{ij}(t)&=e^{-\gamma t}\left(\kappa_{ij}^{0}+\int_{0}^{t}\mu e^{\gamma s}R_{ij}ds\right)\leq e^{-\gamma t}\left(\kappa^{0}_{ij}+\int_{0}^{t}\mu e^{\gamma s}ds\right)\\
&\leq\left(\kappa^{0}_{ij}-\frac{\mu}{\gamma}\right)e^{-\gamma t}+\frac{\mu}{\gamma}\leq\max\left\{\max_{i,j}\kappa^{0}_{ij},~\frac{\mu}{\gamma}\right\}\leq\kappa_{M}.\end{aligned}

∎

Recall that our governing system is given as follows:

(5.1)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{z}_{j}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\kappa_{jk}(z_{k}-R_{jk}z_{j})+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\tilde{\lambda}_{jk}(\langle z_{j},z_{k}\rangle-\langle z_{k},z_{j}\rangle)z_{j},\quad t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\

Before we proceed analysis on the emergent behavior of (

and

and

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that coupling gains and coupling law satisfy

for some constant
c>0
, and let
(Z,K,\tilde{\Lambda})
be a solution to (
3.17
). Then, one has

c\kappa_{ij}(t)\geq\tilde{\lambda}_{ij}(t),\quad\forall t>0.

Proof.

We use (
3.17
) to see

\frac{d}{dt}\big(c\kappa_{ij}-\tilde{\lambda}_{ij}\big)=-\gamma\big(c\kappa_{ij}-\tilde{\lambda}_{ij}\big)+\mu\big(c\Gamma_{0}(z_{i},z_{j})-\tilde{\Gamma}(z_{i},z_{j})\big)\geq-\gamma\big(c\kappa_{ij}-\tilde{\lambda}_{ij}\big).

Therefore, we have

c\kappa_{ij}(t)-\tilde{\lambda}_{ij}(t)\geq e^{-\gamma t}\big(c\kappa^{0}_{ij}-\tilde{\lambda}^{0}_{ij}\big)\geq 0.

and this is the desired result.
∎

Parallel to the presentation in Section

5.1.
Anti-Hebbian coupling law

In this subsection, we study emergent dynamics of (

(5.2)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{z}_{j}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\kappa_{jk}\Big(z_{k}-R_{jk}z_{j}\Big)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\tilde{\lambda}_{jk}\Big(\langle z_{j},z_{k}\rangle-\langle z_{k},z_{j}\rangle\Big)z_{j},\quad t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\

\displaystyle(z_{j},\kappa_{jk},{\tilde{\lambda}}_{jk})(0)=(z_{j}^{0},\kappa_{jk}^{0},{\tilde{\lambda}}_{jk}^{0})\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R},\quad j,k\in\mathcal{N}.\end{cases}

As in Section
4
, we study the temporal evolution of the Lyapunov functional
{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}
introduced in (
3.7
) .

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(Z,K,{\tilde{\Lambda}})
be a solution to (
5.2
). Then, the functional
{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}
satisfies

(5.3)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{d}{dt}{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}&=-\left(\frac{1-R_{ij}}{N}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}R_{ik}+\kappa_{jk}R_{jk})-\frac{\gamma}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2}\\
&\hskip 11.38092pt-\frac{2I_{ij}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\tilde{\lambda}_{ik}I_{ik}-\tilde{\lambda}_{jk}I_{jk}).\end{aligned}

Proof.

By definition of
{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}
, one has

(5.4)

\dot{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}=-\frac{1}{2}(\dot{h}_{ij}+\dot{h}_{ji})+\frac{1}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})(\dot{\kappa}_{ik}-\dot{\kappa}_{jk}).

Next, we estimate two terms in the R.H.S. of (
5.4
) separately.

\bullet
(Estimate of the first term in (
5.4
)): By straightforward calculations, one has

(5.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\dot{h}_{ij}+\dot{h}_{ji}=\langle z_{i},\dot{z}_{j}\rangle+\langle\dot{z}_{i},z_{j}\rangle+\langle z_{j},\dot{z}_{i}\rangle+\langle\dot{z}_{j},z_{i}\rangle=\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathrm{Re}(\langle z_{i},\dot{z}_{j}\rangle+\langle\dot{z}_{i},z_{j}\rangle)\\
&=\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mathrm{Re}\Big[\kappa_{jk}(h_{ik}-R_{jk}h_{ij})+\kappa_{ik}(h_{kj}-R_{ik}h_{ij})+\tilde{\lambda}_{jk}(h_{jk}-h_{kj})h_{ij}+\tilde{\lambda}_{ik}(h_{ki}-h_{ik})h_{ij})\Big]\\
&=\frac{2}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big[\kappa_{jk}(R_{ik}-R_{jk}R_{ij})+\kappa_{ik}(R_{jk}-R_{ik}R_{ji})\big)-2\tilde{\lambda}_{jk}I_{jk}I_{ij}-2\tilde{\lambda}_{ik}I_{ki}I_{ij})\Big].\end{aligned}

\bullet
(Estimate of the second term in (
5.4
)): Again, one has

(5.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\frac{1}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})(\dot{\kappa}_{ik}-\dot{\kappa}_{jk})\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt=\frac{1}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})(-\gamma\kappa_{ik}-\mu h_{ik}-\mu h_{ki}+\gamma\kappa_{jk}+\mu h_{jk}+\mu h_{kj})\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt=-\frac{\gamma}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2}-\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})(h_{ik}+h_{ki}-h_{jk}-h_{kj})\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt=-\frac{\gamma}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})(R_{ik}-R_{jk}).\end{aligned}

In (
5.4
), we combine (
5.5
) and (
5.6
) to obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that the following relations hold:

(5.7)

\displaystyle\tilde{\lambda}_{ij}^{0}={\tilde{\lambda}}^{0},\quad~i,j\in\mathcal{N}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{\Gamma}(t)\equiv 0,\quad\forall~t>0

for some constant
\tilde{\lambda}^{0}
, and let
(Z,K,{\tilde{\Lambda}})
be a solution to (
5.2
). Then, the following assertions hold:

(1)

There exists a function
\tilde{\lambda}={\tilde{\lambda}}(\cdot)
such that

\tilde{\lambda}_{ij}(t)={\tilde{\lambda}}(t),\quad t>0,\quad\forall~i,j\in\mathcal{N}.

(2)

The functional
{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}
satisfies

\dot{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}\leq-\left(\frac{1-R_{ij}}{N}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(\kappa_{ik}R_{ik}+\kappa_{jk}R_{jk}-4|\tilde{\lambda}|\Big)-\frac{\gamma}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2},\quad t>0.

Proof.

It follows from (
5.2
) and (
5.7
) that

(5.8)

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\dot{z}_{j}=\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\kappa_{jk}\Big(z_{k}-R_{jk}z_{j}\Big)+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\tilde{\lambda}_{jk}\Big(\langle z_{j},z_{k}\rangle-\langle z_{k},z_{j}\rangle\Big)z_{j},\quad t>0,\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle\dot{\kappa}_{jk}=-\gamma\kappa_{jk}+\mu\|z_{j}-z_{k}\|^{2},\quad\dot{\tilde{\lambda}}_{jk}=-\gamma\tilde{\lambda}_{jk},\vskip 5.69046pt\\
\displaystyle(z_{j},\kappa_{jk},{\tilde{\lambda}}_{jk})(0)=(z_{j}^{0},\kappa_{jk}^{0},{\tilde{\lambda}}_{jk}^{0})\in\mathbb{H}\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R},\quad j,k\in\mathcal{N}.\end{cases}

(i) By

, one has

\tilde{\lambda}_{ij}(t)={\tilde{\lambda}}^{0}e^{-\gamma t}=:{\tilde{\lambda}}(t),\quad t>0,

which yields the first assertion.

(ii) Now, we estimate the last term of (

\frac{2I_{ij}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\tilde{\lambda}_{ik}I_{ik}-\tilde{\lambda}_{jk}I_{jk})=\frac{2I_{ij}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\big(\tilde{\lambda}_{ik}(I_{ik}-I_{jk})-I_{jk}(\tilde{\lambda}_{jk}-\tilde{\lambda}_{ik})\big)=\frac{2I_{ij}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\tilde{\lambda}(I_{ik}-I_{jk}).

We use the triangle inequality and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to find

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &|I_{ij}|=\sqrt{1-R_{ij}^{2}}=\sqrt{(1-R_{ij})(1+R_{ij})}\leq\sqrt{2(1-R_{ij})},\\
&|I_{ik}-I_{jk}|=\left|\frac{1}{2i}(h_{ik}-h_{ki}-h_{jk}+h_{kj})\right|=\frac{1}{2}\left|\langle z_{i}-z_{j},z_{k}\rangle+\langle z_{k},z_{j}-z_{i}\rangle\right|\\
&\hskip 48.36958pt\leq\|z_{i}-z_{j}\|=\sqrt{2(1-R_{ij})}.\end{aligned}

Finally, we combine (

) to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}&=-\left(\frac{1-R_{ij}}{N}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}R_{ik}+\kappa_{jk}R_{jk})-\frac{\gamma}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2}-\frac{2I_{ij}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\tilde{\lambda}_{ik}(I_{ik}-I_{jk})\\
&\leq-\left(\frac{1-R_{ij}}{N}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}R_{ik}+\kappa_{jk}R_{jk})-\frac{\gamma}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2}+\left|\frac{2I_{ij}}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\tilde{\lambda}_{ik}(I_{ik}-I_{jk})\right|\\
&\leq-\left(\frac{1-R_{ij}}{N}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}R_{ik}+\kappa_{jk}R_{jk})-\frac{\gamma}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2}+4\left(\frac{1-R_{ij}}{N}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{N}|\tilde{\lambda}|\\
&=-\left(\frac{1-R_{ij}}{N}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(\kappa_{ik}R_{ik}+\kappa_{jk}R_{jk}-4|\tilde{\lambda}|\Big)-\frac{\gamma}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2},\end{aligned}

and this is the desired result.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose that the following relations

\tilde{\lambda}_{ij}^{0}={\tilde{\lambda}}^{0},\quad i,j\in\mathcal{N}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{\Gamma}(t)=0,\quad t>0

hold for some constant
{\tilde{\lambda}}^{0}
, and let
(Z,K,{\tilde{\Lambda}})
be a solution to (

) with the initial data satisfying

\quad 1>\max_{i,j}\frac{2|\tilde{\lambda}^{0}|}{\kappa_{ij}^{0}}+\max_{k,l}\mathcal{L}_{kl}^{0}.

Then,
\mathcal{L}_{ij}
is non-increasing.

Proof.

If
\tilde{\lambda}^{0}=0
, by the same argument in a proof of Lemma

, we are done. We choose a constant

satisfying

(5.10)

\displaystyle c\kappa_{ij}^{0}\geq|\tilde{\lambda}^{0}|,

for any indices

and

. Since
\tilde{\Gamma}\equiv 0
, by Lemma

c\kappa_{ij}(t)\geq|\tilde{\lambda}(t)|,\quad t>0.

Hence, one has

(5.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}&\leq-\left(\frac{1-R_{ij}}{N}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}R_{ik}+\kappa_{jk}R_{jk}-4|\tilde{\lambda}|)-\frac{\gamma}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2}\\
&\leq-\left(\frac{1-R_{ij}}{N}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{N}\big(\kappa_{ik}(R_{ik}-2c)+\kappa_{jk}(R_{jk}-2c)\big)-\frac{\gamma}{2\mu N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\kappa_{ik}-\kappa_{jk})^{2}.\end{aligned}

Now we recall that
\kappa_{ij}
is positive by Lemma

because
\kappa_{ij}^{0}>0
. Therefore if we assume

(5.12)

\displaystyle\min_{k,l}R_{kl}^{0}>2c,

then

\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{*}_{ij}:=\{\tau\in(0,\infty):R_{ij}(t)>2c,\quad t\in(0,\tau)\},\quad

is non-empty for each

and we can introduce

\tilde{t}^{*}_{ij}:=\sup\tilde{\mathcal{T}}^{*}_{ij},\quad(i_{1},j_{1}):=\argmin_{k,l}{\tilde{t}}^{*}_{kl}.

Therefore, it follows from the minimality of

\mathcal{L}_{i_{1}j_{1}}(t)<\mathcal{L}_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{0},\quad t\in(0,{\tilde{t}}^{*}_{i_{1}j_{1}}).

By definition (
4.7
), one has

1-R_{i_{1}j_{1}}(t)\leq\mathcal{L}_{i_{1}j_{1}}(t),\quad t\geq 0.

Hence we have

1-R_{i_{1}j_{1}}(t)\leq\mathcal{L}_{i_{1}j_{1}}(t)<\mathcal{L}_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{0},\quad\text{so that}\quad R_{i_{1}j_{1}}(t)>1-\mathcal{L}_{i_{1}j_{1}}^{0}>0\quad\text{for}\quad t\in(0,{\tilde{t}}^{*}_{i_{1}j_{1}}).

Therefore by taking

, from the continuity of

6.
Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied the emergent dynamics of the LHS model with adaptive coupling gains. When the dynamics of coupling gains are decoupled from the dynamics of state, say, they are simply constants, in previous literature, several sufficient frameworks were proposed for complete aggregation in which all states collapse to the same state. However, when coupling gains and state evolutions are intertwined via adaptive coupling laws, emergent dynamics are more delicate and interesting. In order to couple the dynamics of coupling gains and state, we employ two types of coupling laws, namely anti-Hebbian law and Hebbian law in analogy with the dynamics of brain neurons. The former causes the increment of coupling gain, as the state differences become larger, whereas the latter causes the opposite effect. In the case of the same free flow for all particles, states aggregate to the same state asymptotically for some class of initial data and system parameters. When rotational coupling gain is the minus of the half of the sphere coupling gain, our first result says that the relative state tends to zero and coupling gains tend to zero asymptotically. Since the coupling gain becomes smaller over time, analysis of complete aggregation is highly nontrivial and difficult to analyze.
Despite this apparent difficulty, we use the Lyapunov functional approach and Barbalat’s lemma to show that the relative states and coupling gains tend to zero for the anti-Hebbian case. For the Hebbian coupling case, we show that the square of the state diameter tends to zero exponentially fast for some admissible class of initial data and initial system parameters. The same things can be done for an asymptotically SL coupling gain pair. All presented results in this paper deal with the ensemble of particles with the same free flows. There are several issues that have not been addressed in this work. For example, for an ensemble of LHS particles with the same free flow, a bi-polar state can emerge as one of the resulting asymptotic patterns. Then, is this bi-polar configuration unstable as for the Lohe sphere model? For the ensemble of particles with heterogeneous free flows, emergent dynamics is completely unknown even for nonnegative coupling gains, not to mention adaptive coupling gains. These interesting issues will be left for future work.

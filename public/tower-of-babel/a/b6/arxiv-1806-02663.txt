Abstract

Definition 1
.

[
9
]
Let
E
be a nonempty set and
\rho:E\times E\rightarrow[0,\infty)
a function.
\left(E,\rho\right)
is called

-metric space if there exists a real number

\rho(u,w)=0

u=w
;

(2)

\rho(u,w)=\rho(w,u)

(3)

\rho(u,w)\leq s[\rho(u,v)+\rho(v,w)].

Clearly a
b
-metric space with

Definition 2
.

[
3
]
Let
E

\rho:E\times E\rightarrow[0,\infty)

\left(E,\rho\right)

(1)

u=w

\rho(u,u)=\rho(u,w)=\rho(w,w)

(2)

\rho(u,u)\leq\rho(u,w);

(3)

\rho(u,w)=\rho(w,u);

(4)

\rho(u,w)\leq\rho(u,v)+\rho(v,w)-\rho(v,v)

It is clear that every metric space is also a partial metric spaces.

Now we give an analogue of Banach contraction principle. Our proof is very

Theorem 1
.

Let
(E,\rho)

(2.1)

for all

where
\lambda\in[0,1)

has a unique

\rho(b,b)=0

Let

\left\{u_{n}\right\}

for all

n_{0}
such that
\lambda^{n_{0}}<\frac{\varepsilon}{4s}
for given

Then, for all
u,w\in E

\rho(Gu,Gw)=\rho(S^{n_{0}}u,S^{n_{0}}w)\leq\lambda^{n_{0}}\rho(u,w)\text{.}

(2.2)

as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Hence, there exists a

such that

\rho(u_{l+1},u_{l})<\frac{\varepsilon}{4s}\text{.}

Now, let

B_{\rho}[u_{l},\varepsilon/2]:=\left\{w\in E:\rho(u_{l},w)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\rho(u_{l},u_{l})\right\}\text{.}

We need to prove that
G
maps the set
B_{\rho}[u_{l},\varepsilon/2]

nonempty set. Let

\displaystyle\rho(Gz,u_{l})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s\left[\rho(Gz,Gu_{l})+\rho(Gu_{l}+Gu_{l+1})+\ldots+\rho(Gu_{l+v-2},Gu_{l+v-1})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\rho(Gu_{l+v-1},u_{l})\right]-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{v-1}\rho(Gu_{l+i},Gu_{l+i})

\displaystyle s\left[\rho(Gz,Gu_{l})+\rho(Gu_{l}+Gu_{l+1})+\ldots+\rho(Gu_{l+v-2},Gu_{l+v-1})+\rho(Gu_{l+v-1},u_{l})\right]

\displaystyle s[\lambda^{n_{0}}(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\rho(u_{l},u_{l}))+\rho(u_{l+1},u_{l+2})+\ldots+\rho(u_{l+v-1},u_{l+v})+\rho(u_{l+v},u_{l})

\displaystyle s\left\{\lambda^{n_{0}}(\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+\rho(u_{l},u_{l}))+(s+1)\rho(u_{l},u_{l+1})+(s+1)\rho(u_{l+1},u_{l+2})+\right.

\displaystyle\rho(u_{l},u_{l})\left[s\frac{\varepsilon}{4s}+s^{2}\frac{\varepsilon^{v}}{(4s)^{v}}\right]+s\frac{\varepsilon}{4s}\frac{\varepsilon}{2}+

(E,\rho)
be a complete partial

\displaystyle\left.\rho(u_{n+v},Sb)\right]-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{v}\rho(u_{n+i},u_{n+i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s\left[\rho(b,u_{n+1})+\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n+2})+\ldots+\rho(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v})+\rho(u_{n+v},Sb)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s\left[\rho(b,u_{n+1})+\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n+2})+\ldots+\rho(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v})+\rho(Su_{n+v-1},Sb)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s\left[\rho(b,u_{n+1})+\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n+2})+\ldots+\rho(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v})+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\lambda\left\{\rho(u_{n+v-1},Su_{n+v-1})+\rho(b,Sb)\right\}\right].

So, it follows from the last inequality that

\displaystyle\rho(b,Sb)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{s}{(1-s\lambda)}\left[\rho(b,u_{n+1})+\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n+2})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+...+\rho(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v})+\lambda\rho(u_{n+v-1},Su_{n+v-1})\right]\text{.}

Since
\lambda\neq\frac{1}{s}
and
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
is a Cauchy
and convergent sequence, we have
\rho(b,Sb)=0
, so
Sb=b
. It means that
b
is a fixed point of
S
.

Now we show the uniqueness of fixed point. But first, we need to show that
if
b\in E
is a fixed point of
S
, then
\rho(b,b)=0
. Let assume to the
contrary that
\rho(b,b)>0
. Then, from (
2.4
) we have

\rho(b,b)=\rho(Sb,Sb)\leq\lambda\left[\rho(b,Sb)+\rho(b,Sb)\right]=2\lambda\rho(b,b)<\rho(b,b)\text{,}

which is a contradiction. So, assumption is wrong, namely,
\rho(b,b)=0
.
Now, we can show that
S
has a unique fixed point. Suppose
a,b\in E
be
two distinct fixed points of
S
. Then we have
\rho(b,b)=\rho(a,a)=0
,
and it follows from (
2.4
) that

\displaystyle\rho(b,a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(Sb,Sa)\leq\lambda\left[\rho(b,Sb)+\rho(a,Sa)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\left[\rho(b,b)+\rho(a,a)\right]=0

Therefore, we have
\rho(b,a)=0
and so
b=a
. Thus
S
has a unique fixed
point. This completes the proof.

Theorem 3
.

Let
(E,\rho)
be a complete partial
b_{v}\left(s\right)

metric space and
S:E\rightarrow E
a mapping satisfying:

\rho(Su,Sw)\leq\lambda\max\left\{\rho(u,w),\rho(u,Su),\rho(w,Sw)\right\}

(2.6)

for all
u,w\in E
and
\lambda\in\left[0,\frac{1}{s}\right)
. Then,
S

has a unique fixed point
b\in E
and
\rho(b,b)=0
.

Proof.

We begin with the existence of fixed points of
S
. Let
u_{0}\in E
be an
arbitrary initial point and let
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
be a sequence
defined by
u_{n+1}=Su_{n}
for all

.

If
u_{n}=u_{n+1}
for at

n
, then it is clear that this point is a fixed

S
. So, let assume that
u_{n+1}\neq u_{n}
for all

. Now, it
follows from (

) that

\displaystyle\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(Su_{n},Su_{n-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda\max\left\{\rho(u_{n},u_{n-1}),\rho(u_{n},Su_{n}),\rho(u_{n-1},Su_{n-1})\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\max\left\{\rho(u_{n},u_{n-1}),\rho(u_{n},u_{n+1}),\rho(u_{n-1},u_{n})\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\max\left\{\rho(u_{n},u_{n-1}),\rho(u_{n},u_{n+1})\right\}\text{.}

Set
L=\max\left\{\rho(u_{n},u_{n-1}),\rho(u_{n},u_{n+1})\right\}
.
There exist two cases. If

, then we get
\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n})\leq\lambda\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n})<\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n})
which

and then we

\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n})\leq\lambda\rho(u_{n},u_{n-1})\text{.}

On repeating this process, we obtain

\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n})\leq\lambda^{n}\rho(u_{1},u_{0})

(2.7)

for all
n
. On the other hand, since
\lambda^{n}\rightarrow 0
for
n\rightarrow\infty
, there exists a natural number
n_{0}
such that
0<\lambda^{n_{0}}s<1
. For
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
with
m>n

2.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\rho(u_{n},u_{m})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s\left[\rho(u_{n},u_{n+1})+\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n+2})+\ldots+\rho(u_{n+v-3},u_{n+v-2})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\rho(u_{n+v-2},u_{n+n_{0}})+\rho(u_{n+n_{0}},u_{m+n_{0}})+\rho(u_{m+n_{0}},u_{m})\right]

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{v-2}\rho(u_{n+i},u_{n+i})-\rho(u_{n+n_{0}},u_{n+n_{0}})-\rho(u_{m+n_{0}},u_{m+n_{0}})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s\left[\rho(u_{n},u_{n+1})+\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n+2})+\ldots+\rho(u_{n+v-3},u_{n+v-2})\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\rho(u_{n+v-2},u_{n+n_{0}})+\rho(u_{n+n_{0}},u_{m+n_{0}})+\rho(u_{m+n_{0}},u_{m})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s\left(\lambda^{n}+\lambda^{n+1}+\cdots+\lambda^{n+v-3}\right)\rho(u_{0},u_{1})

\displaystyle+s\lambda^{n}\rho(u_{v-2},u_{n_{0}})+s\lambda^{n_{0}}\rho(u_{n},u_{m})+s\lambda^{m}\rho(u_{n_{0}},u_{0}).

So, we get

\displaystyle\left(1-s\lambda^{n_{0}}\right)\rho(u_{n},u_{m})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s\left(\lambda^{n}+\lambda^{n+1}+\cdots+\lambda^{n+v-3}\right)\rho(u_{0},u_{1})

\displaystyle+s\lambda^{n}\rho(u_{v-2},u_{n_{0}})+s\lambda^{m}\rho(u_{n_{0}},u_{0}).

By taking limit from both side, we have

\lim_{n,m\rightarrow\infty}\rho(u_{n},u_{m})=0

Therefore,
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
E
. By
completeness of

there exists

such that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rho(u_{n},b)=\lim_{n,m\rightarrow\infty}\rho(u_{n},u_{m})=\rho(b,b)=0\text{.}

(2.8)

Now, we show that
b
is a fixed point of
S
. From definition of partial
b_{v}\left(s\right)
metric space and inequality (

), we have

\displaystyle\rho(b,Sb)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s\left[\rho(b,u_{n+1})+\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n+2})+\ldots+\rho(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v})+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\rho(u_{n+v},Sb)\right]-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{v}\rho(u_{n+i},u_{n+i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s\left[\rho(b,u_{n+1})+\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n+2})+\ldots+\rho(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v})+\rho(u_{n+v},Sb)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s\left[\rho(b,u_{n+1})+\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n+2})+\ldots+\rho(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v})+\rho(Su_{n+v-1},Sb)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle s\left[\rho(b,u_{n+1})+\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n+2})+\ldots+\rho(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v})+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\lambda\max\left\{\rho(u_{n+v-1},b),\rho(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v}),\rho(b,Sb)\right\}\right].

Set
F=\max\left\{\rho(u_{n+v-1},b),\rho(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v}),\rho(b,Sb)\right\}
. There exists three cases:

1. If

\rho(b,Sb)\leq s\left[\rho(b,u_{n+1})+\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n+2})+\ldots+\rho(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v})+\lambda\rho(u_{n+v-1},b)\right].

So, it follows from (

2. If

.

3. If

\left(1-s\lambda\right)\rho(b,Sb)\leq s\left[\rho(b,u_{n+1})+\rho(u_{n+1},u_{n+2})+\ldots+\rho(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v})\right].

Since

, we obtain that
\rho(b,Sb)=0
, that is,

. Thus,

is a fixed poit of
S
.

Now we show the uniqueness of fixed point of

. Suppose on the contrary
that

and

are two distinct fixed points of

and

. It
follows from (

) that

\displaystyle\rho(a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(Sa,Sb)\leq\lambda\max\left\{\rho(a,b),\rho(a,Sa),\rho(b,Sb)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\max\left\{\rho(a,b),\rho(a,a),\rho(b,b)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\rho(a,b)<\rho(a,b)\text{,}

which is a cotradiction. Therefore, we must have

and so

. Hence,

has a unique fixed point.

2.2.
b_{v}\left(\theta\right)
Metric Spaces

In 2017, Kamran et al. introduced following generalized metric space which
they call extended
b
-metric space.

Definition 9
.

[
7
]
Let
E
be a nonempty set and let
\theta:E\times E\rightarrow[1,\infty)
be a function. A function
\rho_{\theta}:E\times E\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is called an extended
b
-metric if for all

u,v,w\in E
it satisfies:

(1)

\rho_{\theta}(u,w)=0
iff
u=w
;

(2)

\rho_{\theta}(u,w)=\rho(w,u)
;

(3)

\rho_{\theta}(u,w)\leq\theta\left(u,w\right)[\rho_{\theta}(u,v)+\rho_{\theta}(v,w)].

The pair
\left(E,\rho_{\theta}\right)
is called an extended
b
-metric
space.

It is clear that if
\theta\left(u,w\right)=s
for all
u,w\in E,
then
we obtain
b
-metric space.

From this point of view, we introduce following generalized metric space
called as
b_{v}(\theta)
(or extended
b_{v}(s)
) metric space.

Definition 10
.

Let
E
be a nonempty set,
\theta:E\times E\rightarrow[1,\infty)

a function and
v\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\rho_{\theta}:E\times E\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is called

u,z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{v},w\in E
, each of
them different from each other, it satisfies

(1)

\rho_{\theta}(u,w)=0
iff
u=w
;

(2)

\rho_{\theta}(u,w)=\rho_{\theta}(w,u)
;

(3)

\rho_{\theta}(u,w)\leq\theta\left(u,w\right)[\rho_{\theta}(u,z_{1})+\rho_{\theta}(z_{1},z_{2})+\cdots+\rho_{\theta}\left(z_{v},w\right)].

The pair
\left(E,\rho_{\theta}\right)
is called
b_{v}(\theta)\
metric space.

Remark 4
.

It is clear that if for all
u,w\in E

(1)

\theta\left(u,w\right)=s
, then we obtain
b_{v}(s)
metric space,

(2)

v=1
, then we obtain extended
b
-metric space,

(3)

\theta\left(u,w\right)=s
and
v=1
, then we obtain
b
-metric
space,

(4)

\theta\left(u,w\right)=s
and
v=2
, then we obtain rectangular
b
-metric space,

(5)

\theta\left(u,w\right)=1
and
v=2
, then we obtain rectangular
metric space,

(6)

\theta\left(u,w\right)=1
, then we obtain
v
-generalized metric
space,

(7)

\theta\left(u,w\right)=1
and
v=1
, then we obtain usual metric
space.

Example 1

Let

\theta:\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\rightarrow[1,\infty)

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+1},u_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n},u_{n-1}\right)+k_{1}c^{n+1}+k_{2}c^{n}

is a Cauchy sequence. Since
b_{v}\left(s\right)
metric space is a
b_{2v}\left(s^{2}\right)
metric space, if
v=1
, then
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
is Cauchy.

Now we can give Banach fixed point theorem in complete
b_{v}\left(\theta\right)
metric space.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\left(E,\rho_{\theta}\right)
be a complete
b_{v}(\theta)
metric space with a bounded function
\theta
and
S:E\rightarrow E
a
contraction mapping, i.e., there exists a constant
c\in\left[0,1\right)

such that

\rho_{\theta}\left(Su,Sw\right)\leq c\rho_{\theta}\left(u,w\right)

(2.11)

for all
u,w\in E
. Then
S
has a unique fixed point.

Proof.

Let
u_{0}\in E
be an arbitrary initial point and let
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
be a sequence defined by
u_{n+1}=Su_{n}=S^{n+1}u_{0}
and
u_{n}\neq u_{n+1}
for all
n\geq 0
. It follows from Lemma
1
that
u_{n}\neq u_{m}
for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
. Since
S
is a contraction mapping, we can write

\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n},u_{m}\right)=\rho_{\theta}\left(Su_{n-1},Su_{m-1}\right)\leq c\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n-1},u_{m-1}\right).

From Lemma
2
, we have
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
is a Cauchy sequence.
So, it follows from completeness of
E
that there exists an element
u\in E

such that
u_{n}\rightarrow u
. Now, we show that
u\in FixS
, i.e.,
u=Su
.

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}\left(u,Su\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\theta\left(u,Su\right)\left[\rho_{\theta}\left(u,u_{n+1}\right)+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+1},u_{n+2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\cdots+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v}\right)+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v},Su\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\left(u,Su\right)\left[\rho_{\theta}\left(u,u_{n+1}\right)+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+1},u_{n+2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\cdots+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v}\right)+\rho_{\theta}\left(Su_{n+v-1},Su\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\theta\left(u,Su\right)\left[\rho_{\theta}\left(u,u_{n+1}\right)+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+1},u_{n+2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\cdots+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v}\right)+c\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v-1},u\right)\right].

Since
\theta
is a bounded function and
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
is Cauchy
with
u_{n}\rightarrow u
, we have
\rho_{\theta}\left(u,Su\right)=0
.
This means that
u\in FixS
. Next, we need to show that
u
is a unique
fixed point. Let assume to the contrary that there exists another fixed
point
w
. Since

\rho_{\theta}\left(u,w\right)=\rho_{\theta}\left(Su,Sw\right)\leq c\rho_{\theta}\left(u,w\right)<\rho_{\theta}\left(u,w\right),

we get
u=w
that is
u
is the unique fixed point of
S
.

for all

(2)

if we take

for all

where

, then we derive Theorem 2.1 of
[

]
in

metric spaces.

(3)

and

for all
u,w\in E
, then we
derive Theorem 2.1 of
[
29
]
in
b
-metric spaces.

(4)

if
v=2
and
\theta\left(u,w\right)=s
for all

, then we
derive Theorem 2.1 of
[

]
and so main theorem of
[
4
]
in
rectangular
b
-metric spaces.

if
\theta\left(u,w\right)=1
for all

, then we derive

In literature, there exist various type of contraction mappings. Weakly
contractive mapping is one of this type of contractions which generalize
usual contractions. A mapping
S:E\rightarrow E
is called weakly
contractive if there exists a continuous and nondecreasing function
\psi\left(t\right)
defined from
\mathbb{R}^{+}\cup\left\{0\right\}
onto itself such that
\psi\left(0\right)=0,\psi\left(t\right)\rightarrow\infty
as
t\rightarrow\infty
and
for all
u,w\in E

\rho_{\theta}\left(Su,Sw\right)\leq\rho_{\theta}\left(u,w\right)-\psi\left(\rho_{\theta}\left(u,w\right)\right).

(2.12)

Now, we generalize Banach fixed point theorem for weakly contractive
mappings in
b_{v}\left(\theta\right)
metric space.

Theorem 5
.

Let
E
be a complete
b_{v}\left(\theta\right)
metric space
and
S
a weakly contractive mapping on
E
. Then
S
has a unique fixed
point.

Proof.

Let
u_{0}\in E
be an arbitrary initial point. Define sequence
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
by
u_{1}=Su_{0}
,
u_{2}=Su_{1}=S^{2}u_{0},\ldots,u_{n+1}=Su_{n}=S^{n}u_{0}
. If
u_{n}=u_{n+1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
where
\mathbb{N}
is the set of positive integer, then proof is trivial. So, let assume that

u_{n}\neq u_{n+1}
for all
n
. Moreover, the case that
u_{n}\neq u_{m}

for all different
n
and
m
can be easily proved. From (
2.12
), we can
write

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+1},u_{n+p+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}\left(Su_{n},Su_{n+p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n},u_{n+p}\right)-\psi\left(\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n},u_{n+p}\right)\right)

for all
n,p\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\alpha_{n}=\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n},u_{n+p}\right)
. Since
\psi
is nondecreasing, we have

\alpha_{n+1}\leq\alpha_{n}-\psi\left(\alpha_{n}\right)\leq\alpha_{n}.

(2.13)

Thus, the sequence
\left\{\alpha_{n}\right\}
has a limit
\alpha\geq 0
. Now we should show that
\alpha=0
. Assume to the contrary that
\alpha>0
. Using (
2.13
), we have

\psi\left(\alpha_{n}\right)\geq\psi\left(\alpha\right)>0.

So, we get

\alpha_{n+1}\leq\alpha_{n}-\psi\left(\alpha\right).

Hence, we obtain
\alpha_{N+m}\leq\alpha_{m}-N\psi\left(\alpha\right)

which is a contradiction for large enough
N
. This proves that
\alpha=0
.
This means that
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
is Cauchy. Completeness of
E

implies that there exists a point
u\in E
such that
u_{n}\rightarrow u
.
Now, we show that
u
is a fixed point of
S
. Using (
2.12
) and
definition of
\rho_{\theta}
, we get

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}\left(u,Su\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\theta\left(u,Su\right)\left[\rho_{\theta}\left(u,u_{n+1}\right)+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+1},u_{n+2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\ldots+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v}\right)+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v},Su\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta\left(u,Su\right)\left[\rho_{\theta}\left(u,u_{n+1}\right)+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+1},u_{n+2}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\ldots+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v}\right)+\rho_{\theta}\left(Su_{n+v-1},Su\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\theta\left(u,Su\right)\left[\rho_{\theta}\left(u,u_{n+1}\right)+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+1},u_{n+2}\right)+\ldots+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v}\right)+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v-1},u\right)-\psi\left(\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v-1},u\right)\right)\right].

Since
\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n},u_{n+p}\right)\rightarrow 0
and
u_{n}\rightarrow u
as
n\rightarrow\infty
and
\psi\left(0\right)=0
,
we have
u
is a fixed point of
S
.

To prove the uniqueness of fixed point, we can assume that there exist one
more fixed point
w
. Since
S
is a weakly contractive mapping, we have

\rho_{\theta}\left(u,w\right)=\rho_{\theta}\left(Su,Sw\right)\leq\rho_{\theta}\left(u,w\right)-\psi\left(\rho_{\theta}\left(u,w\right)\right)<\rho_{\theta}\left(u,w\right).

So
u=w
This finishes the proof.

Now, we give Reich fixed point theorem.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\left(E,\rho_{\theta}\right)
be a complete
b_{v}(\theta)
metric space with a bounded function
\theta
and
S:E\rightarrow E
a
mapping satisfying:

\rho_{\theta}\left(Su,Sw\right)\leq\alpha\rho_{\theta}\left(u,w\right)+\beta\rho_{\theta}\left(u,Su\right)+\gamma\rho_{\theta}\left(w,Sw\right)

(2.14)

for all
u,w\in E
where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
are nonnegative constants
with
\alpha+\beta+\gamma<1
and
\Gamma_{1}<\frac{1}{\Gamma_{2}}

where
\Gamma_{1}=\min\left\{\beta,\gamma\right\}
and
\Gamma_{2}=\max\left\{\theta\left(u,Su\right),\theta\left(Su,u\right)\right\}
. Then
S
has a unique fixed point. Moreover, sequence
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
defined by
u_{n}=Su_{n-1}
converges strongly to the unique
fixed point of
S
.

Proof.

Let
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
be a sequence defined by
u_{n+1}=Su_{n}=S^{n+1}u_{0}
where
u_{0}\in E
is an arbitrary initial
point. If
u_{n}=u_{n+1}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, it is easy to see that
u_{0}
is a fixed point of
S
. Now, we assume
that
u_{n}\neq u_{n+1}
for all
n
. From (
2.14
) and definition of
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+1},u_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}\left(Su_{n},Su_{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n},u_{n-1}\right)+\beta\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n},Su_{n}\right)+\gamma\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n-1},Su_{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n},u_{n-1}\right)+\beta\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n},u_{n+1}\right)+\gamma\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n-1},u_{n}\right).

Then, we get

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+1},u_{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha+\gamma}{1-\beta}\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n},u_{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\alpha+\gamma}{1-\beta}\right)^{n}\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{1},u_{0}\right).

Since
\alpha+\beta+\gamma<1
, then it is clear that
0\leq\frac{\alpha+\gamma}{1-\beta}<1
. So, we obtain

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+1},u_{n}\right)=0.

(2.15)

Also, since we assume that
u_{n}\neq u_{n+1}
for all
n
and
\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+1},u_{n}\right)\leq\frac{\alpha+\gamma}{1-\beta}\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n},u_{n-1}\right)
, then it follows from Lemma
1
that
u_{n}\neq u_{m}
for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
. So, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n},u_{m}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}\left(Su_{n-1},Su_{m-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n-1},u_{m-1}\right)+\beta\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n-1},Su_{n-1}\right)+\gamma\beta\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{m-1},Su_{m-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n-1},u_{m-1}\right)+\beta\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n-1},u_{n}\right)+\gamma\beta\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{m-1},u_{m}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\alpha\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n-1},u_{m-1}\right)+\left(\beta\left(\frac{\alpha+\gamma}{1-\beta}\right)^{n-1}+\gamma\left(\frac{\alpha+\gamma}{1-\beta}\right)^{m-1}\right)\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{1},u_{0}\right)\text{.}

It follows from Lemma
2
that
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
is a Cauchy
sequence. So, from the completeness of
E
, we obtain that there exists a
point
u\in E
such that
u_{n}\rightarrow u
. Now, we show that
u
is a
fixed point of
S
, i.e.,
\rho_{\theta}\left(u,Su\right)=0
. Since

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}\left(u,Su\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\theta\left(u,Su\right)\left[\rho_{\theta}\left(u,u_{n+1}\right)+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+1},u_{n+2}\right)+\cdots+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v}\right)+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v},Su\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\theta\left(u,Su\right)\left[\rho_{\theta}\left(u,u_{n+1}\right)+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+1},u_{n+2}\right)+\cdots+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v}\right)+\rho_{\theta}\left(Su_{n+v-1},Su\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\theta\left(u,Su\right)\left[\rho_{\theta}\left(u,u_{n+1}\right)+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+1},u_{n+2}\right)+\cdots+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\alpha\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v-1},u\right)+\beta\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v}\right)+\gamma\rho_{\theta}\left(u,Su\right)\right],

we have

\displaystyle\left(1-\gamma\theta\left(u,Su\right)\right)\rho_{\theta}\left(u,Su\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\theta\left(u,Su\right)\left[\rho_{\theta}\left(u,u_{n+1}\right)+\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+1},u_{n+2}\right)+\cdots\right.

\displaystyle+\left.\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v}\right)+\alpha\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v-1},u\right)+\beta\rho_{\theta}\left(u_{n+v-1},u_{n+v}\right)\right].

Since
\Gamma_{1}<\frac{1}{\Gamma_{2}}
, we get
\left(1-\gamma\theta\left(u,Su\right)\right)\in\left[0,1\right)
. So, it follows from (
2.15
) and convergence of
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
that
\rho_{\theta}\left(u,Su\right)=0
. This means that
u
is a fixed point of
S.
Now,
we need to show that
u
is a unique fixed point. Let assume that there
exist another fixed point
v
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}\left(u,v\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{\theta}\left(Su,Sv\right)\leq\alpha\rho_{\theta}\left(u,v\right)+\beta\rho_{\theta}\left(u,Su\right)+\delta\rho_{\theta}\left(v,Sv\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\rho_{\theta}\left(u,v\right).

Since
\alpha<1
, we obtain that
\rho_{\theta}\left(u,v\right)=0,

i.e.,
u
is the unique fixed point of
S
.

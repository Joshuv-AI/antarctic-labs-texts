Next-order correction to the Dirac exchange energy of the free electron gas in the thermodynamic limit and generalized gradient approximations
Thiago Carvalho Corso and Gero Friesecke

Abstract
We derive the next order correction to the Dirac exchange energy for the free electron gas in a box with zero boundary conditions in the thermodynamic limit. The correction is of the order of the surface area of the box, and comes from three different contributions: (i) a real-space boundary layer, (ii) a boundary-condition-induced small shift of Fermi momentum and bulk density, and (iii) a long-range electrostatic finite-size correction. Moreover we show that the LDA, in addition to capturing the bulk term exactly, also produces a correction of the correct order but not the correct size. GGA corrections are found to be capable of capturing the surface term exactly, provided the gradient enhancement factor satisfies a simple explicit integral constraint. For current GGAs such as B88 and PBE we find that the new constraint is not satisfied and the size of the surface correction is overestimated by about ten percent. The new constraint might thus be of interest for the design of future exchange functionals.

Introduction

We derive the next order correction to the Dirac exchange energy for the free electron gas in a box with zero boundary conditions in the thermodynamic limit. Like Dirac exchange, the correction is of significant interest for density functional theory (DFT). In particular, it yields a novel exact constraint on generalized gradient approximations (GGAs).

Motivation from DFT and main result.

Original Kohn-Sham DFT

(interaction energy) and

\rho(r)=N\sum_{s_{1},...,s_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\int_{Q_{L}^{N-1}}|\Psi(r,s_{1},r_{2},s_{2},...,r_{N},s_{N})|^{2}dr_{2}...dr_{N}

(1.7)

3
Open shell effects and Fermi momentum asymptotics in the thermodynamic limit

In this section we consider two important aspects of the FEG in the thermodynamic limit: (i) we justify the use of formulas (
2.9
) for determinantal ground states with a general number of particles
N\in\mathbb{N}
(Lemma

3.1
Open shell effects

Our goal now is to show that, in the thermodynamic limit, the single-particle density matrix (and its derivatives) for any determinantal ground state is pointwise close to the unique closed shell formulas in (
2.9
).

To this end, let us introduce the previous and the current shells for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
as

\displaystyle N_{-}\coloneqq\max\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n<N\mbox{ and }n/2=|B^{D}_{R}\cap\mathbb{N}^{3}|\mbox{ for some }R>0\}=\max_{\epsilon>0}2|B^{D}_{R_{N}-\epsilon}\cap\mathbb{N}^{3}|,

\displaystyle N_{+}\coloneqq\min\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n\geq N\mbox{ and }n/2=|B^{D}_{R}\cap\mathbb{N}^{3}|\mbox{ for some }R>0\}=2|B^{D}_{R_{N}}\cap\mathbb{N}^{3}|,

where again
\mathbb{N}
is replaced by
\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\mathbb{Z}
for Neumann and periodic boundary conditions, respectively. Then according to
[
7

, one can work with the unique (closed shell) ground state density matrix
\gamma_{N_{-},L}
up to a pointwise error proportional to
N^{-1/2}
(or
L^{-\frac{3}{2}}
). However, as previously remarked, these estimates are not enough to justify the use of the exact closed shell formulas on the analysis of the next-to-leading order term in the asymptotic expansion for the exact exchange. To improve on this error estimate and include the more general rectangular box case, we use our own estimate in Lemma
1.1
. More precisely, setting
\alpha=0
and
z=0
in Lemma
1.1
, we obtain
7
7

7

Note that for the case of the cubic box, finding the optimal (algebraic) coefficient on the remainder goes under the name of sphere problem and has been studied by many authors
[
23

4

3

13
]

\displaystyle|\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap B^{D}_{R}|-\frac{4\pi}{3}|D|R^{3}=\mathcal{O}(R^{\frac{34}{23}+\epsilon}).

(3.1)

As a consequence, by adapting the arguments in

Lemma 3.1

(Open shell control)
.

Let

and

-electron gas in

(under either Dirichlet, Neumann or periodic boundary conditions) we have

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}_{r}\partial^{\beta}_{\tilde{r}}\gamma_{N,L}(r,\tilde{r})-\partial^{\alpha}_{r}\partial^{\beta}_{\tilde{r}}\gamma_{N_{-},L}(r,\tilde{r})|\leq c\frac{N^{\frac{|\alpha|+|\beta|}{3}+\frac{34}{69}+\epsilon}}{L^{3+|\alpha|+|\beta|}}.

(3.2)

In particular, if
\bar{\rho}=\frac{N}{|Q^{D}|L^{3}}
is constant, one has

\displaystyle\partial^{\alpha}_{r}\partial^{\beta}_{\tilde{r}}\gamma_{N,L}(r,\tilde{r})=\partial^{\alpha}_{r}\partial^{\beta}_{\tilde{r}}\gamma_{N_{-},L}(r,\tilde{r})+\mathcal{O}(L^{-\frac{35}{23}+\epsilon}).

Proof.

For simplicity we disregard spin here. First, we note that by eq. (
3.1
), the degeneracy of the open shell can be controlled by

\displaystyle d(N)=N_{+}-N_{-}\lesssim_{\epsilon}R_{N}^{\frac{34}{23}+\epsilon}\lesssim_{\epsilon}N^{\frac{34}{69}+\epsilon},

(3.3)

where the
\epsilon
in the second inequality is different from the first by a factor of

. Next, if
\Psi
is a determinantal ground state of the free
N
-electron gas in

, then we know (see section

\displaystyle\Psi=\underbrace{\biggr(\bigwedge_{k\in B_{R_{N_{-}}}}\phi^{L}_{k}\biggr)}_{=\Psi_{N_{-},L}}\wedge\widetilde{\phi}_{1}\wedge...\wedge\widetilde{\phi}_{N-N_{-}},

where

is a set of orthonormal functions given by linear combinations of the orbitals in the open shell, i.e.

\displaystyle\widetilde{\phi}_{i}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{N}^{3}\\
|k|=R_{N}\end{subarray}}c_{ik}\phi_{k}^{L}\quad\mbox{ for some $\{c_{ik}\}_{i,j}\subset\mathbb{C}$ satisfying $\sum_{k}c_{ik}\overline{c_{jk}}=\delta_{ij}$.}

In particular,

is unitary (from

to

) and we have

\displaystyle\biggr|\sum_{i,j,k}c_{ij}\overline{c_{ik}}a_{j}b_{k}\biggr|\leq\biggr(\sum_{j}|a_{j}|^{2}\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}\biggr(\sum_{k}|b_{k}|\biggr)^{\frac{1}{2}}

(3.4)

for any

\displaystyle\gamma_{\Psi}(r,\tilde{r})=\underbrace{\sum_{k\in B_{R_{N_{-}}}\cap\mathbb{N}^{3}}\phi_{k}^{L}(r)\overline{\phi_{k}^{L}}(\tilde{r})}_{=\gamma_{N_{-},L}(r,\tilde{r})}+\sum_{i=1}^{N-N_{-}}\widetilde{\phi}_{i}(r)\overline{\widetilde{\phi}_{i}}(\tilde{r})

3.2
Fermi momentum asymptotics

Here we derive the asymptotics of the finite-size Fermi momentum appearing in (
1.17
).

For the non-interacting electron gas model, the finite-size Fermi momentum is defined as the momentum of the highest occupied orbital of the ground state wave function. For the free
N
-electron gas in
Q^{D}_{L}
, it is simply given (in atomic units) by

\displaystyle p_{N,L}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{2\pi R^{\textnormal{Per}}_{N}}{L},\quad&\mbox{ for periodic BCs,}\\
\frac{\pi R^{\textnormal{Dir}}_{N}}{L},\quad&\mbox{ for Dirichlet BCs,}\\
\frac{\pi R^{\textnormal{Neu}}_{N}}{L},\quad&\mbox{ for Neumann BCs,}\end{cases}

(3.5)

where
R_{N}
is the Fermi radius. It is well known that in the thermodynamic limit, the finite-size Fermi momentum converges to the (continuum) Fermi momentum, defined as

\displaystyle p_{F}=(3\pi^{2}\bar{\rho})^{\frac{1}{3}}.

The next lemma presents the next-order correction of the finite-size Fermi momentum, which is crucial for deriving the next-order corrections from Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 3.2

(Fermi momentum asymptotics)
.

Let
\bar{\rho}=\frac{N}{|Q^{D}|L^{3}}
be constant,
p_{F}
be the Fermi momentum and
p_{N,L}
be the finite-size Fermi momentum. Then

\displaystyle p_{N,L}=\begin{cases}p_{F}+\mathcal{O}(L^{-\frac{35}{23}+\epsilon}),&\mbox{ for periodic BCs,}\\
p_{F}+\frac{\pi|\partial Q^{D}|}{8|Q^{D}|}L^{-1}+\mathcal{O}(L^{-\frac{35}{23}+\epsilon}),&\mbox{ for Dirichlet BCs,}\\
p_{F}-\frac{\pi|\partial Q^{D}|}{8|Q^{D}|}L^{-1}+\mathcal{O}(L^{-\frac{35}{23}+\epsilon}),&\mbox{for Neumann BCs.}\end{cases}

(3.6)

Proof.

We present the proof only for the Dirichlet case and denote
R^{\textnormal{Dir}}_{N}
simply by
R_{N}
. First, from the previous section we already know that
R_{N}\lesssim N^{\frac{1}{3}}
and
0\leq N_{+}-N\leq d(N)\lesssim N^{\frac{34}{69}+\epsilon}
. Since
|Q^{D}|=|D|
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{\bar{\rho}L^{3}}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{2|D|}=\frac{|\mathbb{N}^{3}\cap B^{D}_{R_{N}}|}{|D|}+\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{34}{69}+\epsilon})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8|D|}(|\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap B^{D}_{R_{N}}|-\sum_{j=1}^{3}|\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cap\pi_{j}(B_{R_{N}}^{D})|)+\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{34}{69}+\epsilon})

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{6}R_{N}^{3}-\frac{\pi|\partial Q^{D}|}{16|D|}R_{N}^{2}+\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{34}{69}+\epsilon}),

which implies that

\displaystyle p_{F}^{3}=p_{N,L}^{3}-\frac{3\pi|\partial Q^{D}|}{8|D|}\frac{1}{L}p_{N,L}^{2}+\frac{\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{34}{69}+\epsilon})}{L^{3}}.

(3.7)

Then, with
\bar{\rho}=\frac{N}{|Q^{D}|L^{3}}
fixed, it is clear that
p_{N,L}^{3}\rightarrow p_{F}^{3}
. The correction proportional to
1/L
now follows from equation (
3.7
) by Taylor expansion of the (near the solution
p_{F}
for
1/L=0

p_{N,L}
. ∎

Remark
.

The above lemma can be viewed as a variant of the famous two term Weyl’s law
[
16
]
on the asymptotic behaviour of eigenvalues of the Laplacian (with an improved remainder).

. Suppose that

0<\lambda_{k}\leq f^{(k)}(s)\leq A\lambda_{k},\quad s\in(0,M),

for some
A>0
. Then

\sum_{n\leq M}e^{if(n)}\lesssim_{k,A,\epsilon}M^{1+\epsilon}\Bigl(\lambda_{k}^{\frac{1}{k(k-1)}}+M^{-\frac{1}{k(k-1)}}+M^{-\frac{2}{k(k-1)}}\lambda_{k}^{-\frac{2}{k^{2}(k-1)}}\Bigr).

Remark
.

Note that for any
c<1
, the estimate above still holds for the sum in the interval
cM\leq n\leq M
(with two times the implicit constant above). Indeed, one can simply consider the sum in this interval as the sum until
M
minus the sum until
cM
, where both can be controlled by the same factor.

We also recall an elementary partial summation lemma (see e.g.
[
10
,
15
]
for the proof) that is used to deal with the weight function in
S^{\alpha}_{M}(R,z)
.

Lemma 4.2

(Partial summation
[
15
]
)
.

Let
g\in C^{1}([a,b])
and denote the total variation
\int_{a}^{b}|g^{\prime}|
of
g
by
V_{g}[a,b]
. Then for any sequence
a_{n}
, one has

\biggr|\sum_{n\geq a}^{b}g(n)a_{n}\biggl|\leq(V_{g}[a,b]+|g(a)|)\max_{\gamma\leq b}\biggr|\sum_{\gamma}^{b}a_{n}\biggl|.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

First note that if
M
is small (compared to
R
), then the estimate is trivial. Therefore, we need to consider only the case where
M
is big. Next, defining

\displaystyle f(k)\coloneqq R|k+z|\quad\mbox{ and }\quad g(k)\coloneqq\frac{(k+z)^{\alpha}}{|k+z|^{2+|\alpha|}},

(4.3)

the idea is to see
f
and
g
as functions of one coordinate, estimate the inner sum by Theorem
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
, and then use the trivial estimate for the outer sums. To verify the assumptions of Theorem
4.1
, let us consider the sets

\displaystyle U^{j}_{M}\coloneqq\{k\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|k_{j}|\geq M\mbox{ and }\max_{\ell\neq j}\{|k_{\ell}|\}\geq M/10\}\cap Q_{M}^{h},

(4.4)

\displaystyle L^{j}_{M}\coloneqq\{k\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|k_{j}|\geq M\mbox{ and }\max_{\ell\neq j}\{|k_{\ell}|\}<M/10\}\cap Q_{M}^{h},

(4.5)

and observe that a straightforward calculation yields

\displaystyle\partial_{k_{j}}^{4}f(k)=-3R\frac{|\pi_{j}(k+z)|^{2}(|\pi_{j}(k+z)|^{2}-4(k_{j}+z_{j})^{2})}{|k+z|^{7}}.

(4.6)

Now the reason for our choice of
\tfrac{4}{3}
in the definition of
Q^{h}_{M}
(any number between
1
and
\sqrt{2}
would be enough) is that, since
|z|_{max}\leq\pi
is small compared to
M
, inside of

we have

\displaystyle 4(k_{j}+z_{j})^{2}-|\pi_{j}(k+z)|^{2}\sim M^{2},\quad\quad|k+z|\sim M,\quad\mbox{ and }\quad|\pi_{j}(k+z)|^{2}\sim M^{2}

(with implicit constants independent of
M
and
R
).
In particular, from (
4.6
), we find that

\displaystyle|\partial^{4}_{k_{j}}f(k)|\sim\frac{R}{M^{3}}

(4.7)

inside

. Moreover, note that
|g(k)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|k|^{2}}
and
|\partial_{k_{j}}g(k)|\lesssim\frac{1}{|k|^{3}}
. Hence, as
|k|\geq M
for any
k\in Q_{M}^{h}
, if we consider
g
as a function of only one coordinate, say

, then for any
\{k_{1}\}\times\{k_{2}\}\times I\subset Q_{M}^{h}
we have

\displaystyle V_{g(k_{1},k_{2},\cdot)}[I]+|g(k)|\lesssim M^{-2}

(4.8)

with implicit constant independent of
k_{1},k_{2},I,M
and

. Therefore, we can apply Lemma
4.2
and Theorem
4.1
(see (
4.7
),(
4.8
)) to conclude that, for any

,

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in I_{1}\times I_{2}\times I_{3}\\
k\in(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{3}\end{subarray}}g(k)e^{if(k)}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\epsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{\ell}\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}\cap I_{\ell}\\
\ell\neq j\end{subarray}}M^{-2}M^{1+\epsilon}(R^{\frac{1}{12}}M^{-\frac{1}{4}}+M^{-\frac{1}{12}}+R^{-\frac{1}{24}}M^{-\frac{1}{24}})

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{\epsilon}(R^{\frac{1}{12}}M^{\frac{3}{4}}+M^{\frac{11}{12}}+R^{-\frac{1}{24}}M^{\frac{23}{24}}).

(4.9)

On the other hand, if

, then from equation (

) and the definition of

(see (

)), one can show that
\partial_{k_{\ell}}^{4}f(k)\sim\frac{R}{M^{3}}
for

. In particular, by summing over

first and using the same arguments as before, we conclude that, as long as
I_{1}\times I_{2}\times I_{3}\subset L_{M}^{j}
, the sum over
I_{1}\times I_{2}\times I_{3}
is again bounded by the right hand side of (

).

Finally, note that we can split the summation over

as

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in Q_{M}^{h}\cap(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{3}}g(k)e^{if(k)}=\sum_{p=1}^{P}\biggr(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in I_{p}^{1}\times I_{p}^{2}\times I_{p}^{3}\\
k\in(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{3}\end{subarray}}g(k)e^{if(k)}\biggl),

where the

or

4.2
Proof of Lemma
1.1

To complete the proof of Lemma
1.1
, we shall use two classical results. The first one is the celebrated Poisson summation formula (see e.g.
[
22
]
), already used in the present context in
[
7
]
, which we write in a slightly different form to fit our goal.

Lemma 4.3

(Poisson summation formula)
.

Let
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, then (see the Fourier transform convention adopted (
1.19
)) the Poisson summation formula states that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\widehat{u}(k)e^{ik\bm{\cdot}z}-u(z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{n}\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}u(z+k).

(4.10)

The second one is an (optimal) estimate on the decay of the Fourier transform of the characteristic function of the ball and its derivatives. The proof is a straightforward calculation (at least in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
).

Lemma 4.4

(Fourier transform of the ball)
.

Let
\chi_{B_{1}}
be the characteristic function of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then

\widehat{\chi_{B_{1}}}(k)=\frac{4\pi}{3}h(|k|),

where
h(s)=3(\sin s-s\cos s)/s^{3}
. Moreover, for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3}
, there exists
c_{\alpha}>0
such that

\displaystyle|\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{\chi_{B_{1}}}(k)|\leq\frac{c_{\alpha}}{(1+|k|)^{2}},\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(4.11)

Proof of Lemma
1.1
.

Let
R>0
, then our goal now is to bound the error term

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(R,z)\coloneqq\sum_{k\in B_{R}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{3}}(ik)^{\alpha}e^{ik\bm{\cdot}z}-\int_{B_{R}}(ik)^{\alpha}e^{ik\bm{\cdot}z}dk.

For this, the main idea is to apply the Poisson summation formula to a smooth version of the characteristic function of the ball of radius
R
, use Lemma
4.1
to control the error on the Fourier side and then estimate the difference between our smoothed error and the error
\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}
defined above. So first, let
H>0
be a small parameter to be chosen later, pick any non-positive function
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(0,1)
with
\int\phi(s)ds=-1
, and define

\displaystyle f_{R,H}(r)=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if}\quad|r|\leq R,\\
\displaystyle{1+\int_{0}^{|r|-R}\phi^{H}(s)\mathrm{d}s},&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(4.12)

where
\phi^{H}(s)\coloneqq\frac{1}{H}\phi\bigr(\frac{s}{H}\bigr)
. One can check that
f_{R,H}
is smooth, assumes only values between 0 and 1, and vanishes outside
B_{R+H}
. Moreover, observing that
\widecheck{f}_{R,H}(k)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}}\widehat{f}_{R,H}(k)
(as
f_{R,H}
is symmetric) and applying the Poisson summation formula to
\widehat{u}=f_{R,H}
we obtain

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}f_{R,H}(k)e^{ik\bm{\cdot}z}-\widehat{f}_{R,H}(z)}_{\coloneqq\mathcal{E}_{H}(R,z)}=\underbrace{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{3}\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}_{R,H}(z+k)}_{\coloneqq\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{H}(R,z)}.

(4.13)

The next step is to use Lemma
4.1
to estimate the right-hand side of (
4.13
). For this, we first use spherical coordinates to obtain

\displaystyle\widehat{f}_{R,H}(z+k)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}f_{R,H}(r)e^{-ir\bm{\cdot}(z+k)}\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=4\pi\int_{0}^{R}\frac{\tau\sin(|k+z|\tau)}{|k+z|}\mathrm{d}\tau+4\pi\int_{R}^{R+H}\frac{\tau\sin(|k+z|\tau)}{|k+z|}\biggr(1+\int_{0}^{\tau-R}\phi^{H}(s)ds\biggl)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=-4\pi\int_{R}^{R+H}\frac{\sin(|k+z|\tau)-(|k+z|\tau)\cos(|k+z|\tau)}{|k+z|^{3}}\phi^{H}(\tau-R)\mathrm{d}\tau.

In particular, by partial integration and re-scaling we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{H}(R,z)

\displaystyle=-4\pi\int_{R}^{R+H}\biggr(3\dot{\phi}^{H}(\tau-R)+\tau\ddot{\phi}^{H}(\tau-R)\biggr)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{3}\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\cos(|k+z|\tau)}{|k+z|^{4}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=-4\pi\int_{0}^{1}\biggr(\overbrace{\frac{3}{H}\dot{\phi}(\tau)+\frac{\tau}{H}\ddot{\phi}(\tau)}^{=\mathcal{O}(\frac{1}{H})}+\frac{R}{H^{2}}\ddot{\phi}(\tau)\biggr)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{3}\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\cos\bigr((R+H\tau)|k+z|\bigr)}{|k+z|^{4}}\mathrm{d}\tau

(4.14)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}\biggr(\frac{1}{H}\biggr)-\frac{4\pi R}{H^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\ddot{\phi}(\tau)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{3}\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}\frac{\cos\bigr((R+H\tau)|k+z|\bigr)}{|k+z|^{4}}\mathrm{d}\tau,

where for the last equality we used that
\sum_{k\in(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{3}\setminus\{0\}}|k+z|^{-4}\leq c
(since
|z|_{\max}\leq\pi
). Here we slightly abused the big-O notation to denote terms bounded in absolute value by a constant times
H^{-1}
by
\mathcal{O}(H^{-1})
. Now let
M>0
, then by integrating by parts
n
times, throwing out the terms outside the box
Q_{M}\setminus Q_{1}=[-M,M]^{3}\setminus[-1,1]^{3}
, and integrating back by parts (recall that
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(0,1)
), we find that

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{H}(R,z)

\displaystyle=4\pi R\int_{0}^{1}\phi(\tau)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{3}\\
\cap Q_{M}\setminus Q_{1}\end{subarray}}\frac{\cos\bigr((R+H\tau)|(k+z)|\bigr)}{|k+z|^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\mathcal{O}\biggr(\frac{1}{H}+\frac{R}{H^{n+1}M^{n}}\biggr).

Next, after possibly replacing the implicit constant above by a factor independent of
M
,
H
, and
R
, we can assume that
M=(\frac{4}{3})^{q}
for some
q\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus by using the decomposition
Q_{M}\setminus Q_{1}=\bigcup_{k=1}^{q-1}Q_{(\frac{4}{3})^{k}}^{h}
, the facts that
H<1
and
|z|_{max}\leq\pi
(by periodicity), and Lemma
4.1
, we find that

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{H}(R,z)\lesssim_{\epsilon,n}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{H}+\frac{R}{H^{n+1}M^{n}}+\sum_{k=1}^{q-1}R\biggr(\frac{4}{3}\biggr)^{k\epsilon}\biggr(R^{\frac{1}{12}}\biggr(\frac{4}{3}\biggr)^{\frac{3k}{4}}+\biggr(\frac{4}{3}\biggr)^{\frac{11k}{12}}+R^{-\frac{1}{24}}\biggr(\frac{4}{3}\biggr)^{\frac{23k}{24}}\biggr)

\displaystyle\lesssim_{\epsilon,n}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{H}+\frac{R}{H^{n+1}M^{n}}+\log M\biggr(R^{\frac{13}{12}}M^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon}+RM^{\frac{11}{12}+\epsilon}+R^{\frac{23}{24}}M^{\frac{23}{24}+\epsilon}\biggr),

(4.15)

where the
\log M
can be absorbed in
M^{\epsilon}
. Moreover, we can estimate the error coming from the smoothing procedure as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}^{0}(R,z)-\mathcal{E}_{H}(R,z)|\leq\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap B_{R+H}\\
k\not\in B_{R}\end{subarray}}\!\!\!\!\!\!|f_{R,H}(k)|+|\widehat{f}_{R,H}(z)-\widehat{\chi_{B_{R}}}(z)|\lesssim R^{2}H.

(4.16)

Hence by summing estimates (
4.15
) and (
4.16
), taking into account (
4.13
), setting
M=R^{m}
and
H=R^{-h}
, and minimizing the exponents, one concludes that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{0}(R,z)\lesssim_{\epsilon,n}R^{2-h(n)+\epsilon m(n)}\log R,\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

where
h(n)\coloneqq\frac{12n+11}{23n+22}
and
m(n)\coloneqq\frac{12n+12}{23n+22}
. Thus, since
h\uparrow\frac{12}{23}
for
n\rightarrow\infty
, by choosing
n
big enough, the result for
\alpha=0
follows.

For the estimates with
\alpha\neq 0
, we repeat the same arguments by taking
f^{\alpha}_{R,H}(r)=(ir)^{\alpha}f_{R,H}(r)
. In this case, note that by the Leibniz rule and using that
\partial^{\alpha}(|r|^{-4})\lesssim|r|^{-4-|\alpha|}
away from the origin, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{k}^{\alpha}\biggr(\frac{\cos((R+H\tau)|k+z|)}{|k+z|^{4}}\biggl)-\frac{\bigr(iR(k+z)\bigr)^{\alpha}}{2|k+z|^{|\alpha|+4}}\biggr(e^{i(R+H\tau)|k+z|}+(-1)^{|\alpha|}e^{-i(R+H\tau)|k+z|}\biggr)

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{R^{|\alpha|-1}}{|k+z|^{4}}.

(4.17)

We also have, by Poisson summation and the symmetry of
f^{\alpha}_{R,H}
, that

\displaystyle\underbrace{\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}f^{\alpha}_{R,H}(k)e^{ik\bm{\cdot}z}-\widehat{f}^{\alpha}_{R,H}(z)}_{\coloneqq\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}_{H}(R,z)}=\underbrace{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{3}\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}\widehat{f}^{\alpha}_{R,H}(z+k)}_{\coloneqq\widehat{\mathcal{E}}^{\alpha}_{H}(R,z)}.

(4.18)

Hence using the identity
\widecheck{(i\cdot)^{\alpha}f}=\partial^{\alpha}\widecheck{f}
together with estimate (
4.17
), and repeating the same steps from before (see (
4.14
)), we conclude that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}_{H}(R,z)

\displaystyle=-4\pi\int_{0}^{1}\biggr(\frac{3}{H}\dot{\phi}(\tau)+\frac{R+H\tau}{H^{2}}\ddot{\phi}(\tau)\biggr)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{3}\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}\partial^{\alpha}\biggr(\frac{\cos((R+H\tau)|k+z|)}{|k+z|^{4}}\biggl)

\displaystyle=-\frac{4\pi R}{H^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\ddot{\phi}(\tau)\!\!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{3}\\
k\neq 0\end{subarray}}\!\!\frac{\bigr(iR(k+z)\bigr)^{\alpha}}{2|k+z|^{|\alpha|+4}}\biggr(e^{i(R+H\tau)|k+z|}+(-1)^{|\alpha|}e^{-i(R+H\tau)|k+z|}\biggr)\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle+\mathcal{O}\biggr(\frac{R^{|\alpha|}}{H^{2}}\biggr).

Thus we can again integrate by parts
n
times, remove all terms for
|k|\not\in Q_{M}\setminus Q_{1}
and use Lemma
4.1
to show that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}_{H}(R,z)\lesssim_{\epsilon}R^{|\alpha|}\biggr(\frac{1}{H^{2}}+\frac{R}{H^{n+1}M^{n}}+R^{\frac{13}{12}}M^{\frac{3}{4}+\epsilon}+RM^{\frac{11}{12}+\epsilon}+R^{\frac{23}{24}}M^{\frac{23}{24}+\epsilon}\biggr).

(4.19)

Finally, by combining (
4.19
) with (
4.18
) and the following simple estimate for the smoothing error

|\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}(R,z)-\mathcal{E}^{\alpha}_{H}(R,z)|\lesssim R^{2+|\alpha|}H

one obtains an overall bound which is just
R^{|\alpha|}
times the right hand sides of (
4.15
) plus (
4.16
), and
the result follows by optimizing the exponent and using the same
n\rightarrow\infty
argument as before. ∎

4.3
Continuum approximation of density matrices

We can now introduce continuum approximations for the density matrices and prove that they accurately approximate the exact ground state density matrices.

Recalling formula (
2.9
) and replacing the sum over
k
by an integral, we can define the continuum version of the density matrix as

\displaystyle\gamma_{N,L}^{\textnormal{ctm}}(r,\tilde{r})=\begin{cases}\bar{\rho}\frac{p_{N,L}^{3}}{p_{F}^{3}}h(p_{N,L}|r-\tilde{r}|_{D,L}),&\mbox{ for periodic BCs,}\\
\bar{\rho}\frac{p_{N,L}^{3}}{p_{F}^{3}}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\det\sigma\,h(p_{N,L}|r-\sigma\tilde{r}|_{D,2L}),&\mbox{ for Dirichlet BCs,}\\
\bar{\rho}\frac{p_{N,L}^{3}}{p_{F}^{3}}\sum_{\sigma\in G}h(p_{N,L}|r-\sigma\tilde{r}|_{D,2L}),&\mbox{ for Neumann BCs ,}\end{cases}

(4.20)

where
p_{N,L}
and
p_{F}
are the finite-size and continuum Fermi momentum (see (
3.5
)), the function
h
is defined in (
1.20
), and
|r|_{D,2L}=|r\mod D(2L\mathbb{Z})^{3}|
(the torus distance).
In fact, in the Dirichlet case this approximation was already introduced in
[
7
]
but the error estimates obtained there were insufficient to conclude that the approximation is accurate enough to reveal the surface correction to Dirac exchange.

The following new estimate will be sufficient to obtain the surface correction. It is a direct consequence of Lemma
1.1
.

Theorem 4.2

(Continuum approximation estimates)
.

Let
\bar{\rho}=\frac{N}{|Q^{D}|L^{3}}
be fixed. Let
\Psi_{N,L}
be any determinantal ground state of the free
N
-electron gas on
Q^{D}_{L}
, and let
\gamma_{N,L}
be its single-particle density matrix introduced in (
2.8
). Then for any
\epsilon>0
we have

\displaystyle|\gamma_{N,L}(r,\tilde{r})-\gamma_{N,L}^{\textnormal{ctm}}(r,\tilde{r})|\lesssim_{\epsilon}\bar{\rho}N^{-\frac{35}{69}+\epsilon}\lesssim_{\epsilon}\bar{\rho}^{\frac{34}{69}+\epsilon}L^{-\frac{35}{23}+3\epsilon}.

(4.21)

More generally, for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3}
,

\displaystyle|\partial_{r}^{\alpha}\partial_{\tilde{r}}^{\beta}\gamma_{N,L}(r,\tilde{r})-\partial^{\alpha}_{r}\partial_{\tilde{r}}^{\beta}\gamma_{N,L}^{\textnormal{ctm}}(r,\tilde{r})|\lesssim_{\epsilon,\alpha,\beta}\bar{\rho}^{1+\frac{|\alpha|+|\beta|}{3}}N^{-\frac{35}{69}+\epsilon}\lesssim_{\epsilon,\alpha,\beta}\bar{\rho}^{\frac{34}{69}+\frac{|\alpha|+|\beta|}{3}+\epsilon}L^{-\frac{35}{23}+3\epsilon}.

(4.22)

Moreover, one has

\displaystyle|\gamma^{\textnormal{Dir/Neu}}_{N,L}(r,\tilde{r})|\lesssim_{\epsilon}\bar{\rho}\biggr(N^{-\frac{35}{69}+\epsilon}+(1+|r-\tilde{r}|_{D,2L})^{-2}\biggl),

(4.23)

\displaystyle|\gamma^{\textnormal{Per}}_{N,L}(r,\tilde{r})|\lesssim_{\epsilon}\bar{\rho}\biggr(N^{-\frac{35}{69}+\epsilon}+(1+|r-\tilde{r}|_{D,L})^{-2}\biggl).

(4.24)

Proof.

According to Lemma
3.1
, up to an error
\lesssim\bar{\rho}^{\frac{34}{69}+\epsilon}L^{-\frac{35}{69}+3\epsilon}
, we can use the closed shell formulas for any determinantal ground state.

Next, we know that (i)
R\sim L
, (ii)
\widehat{\chi}_{B^{D}_{R}}(z)=|D|R^{3}\widehat{\chi}_{B_{1}}(RDz)
, (iii)
\widehat{\chi}_{B_{1}}(z)=\frac{4\pi}{3}h(|z|)
, and (iv) the sums
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap B_{R}}e^{i\frac{\pi}{L}k\bm{\cdot}D^{-1}w}
and
\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}\cap B_{R}}e^{i\frac{2\pi}{L}k\bm{\cdot}D^{-1}w}
are periodic (in
w
) with respect to
D((2L\mathbb{Z})^{3})
and
D((L\mathbb{Z})^{3})
respectively. Hence, estimate (
4.21
) follows directly from the closed shell formulas (
2.9
) by applying Lemma
1.1
with
z=\frac{\pi}{L}D^{-1}(r-\sigma\tilde{r})\mod(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{3}
for the Dirichlet and Neumann case and
z=\frac{2\pi}{L}D^{-1}(r-\tilde{r})\mod(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{3}
for the periodic case.

For the derivative estimates in (
4.22
), one can simply use Lemma
1.1
together with the identity
\widehat{(-i\cdot)^{\alpha}f}=\partial^{\alpha}\widehat{f}
. Note that each derivative gives an additional factor of
1/L
which compensates for the

Proof of Theorem

In this section we make the following simplification to the current notation: as we are dealing with the thermodynamic limit (

5.1
Semi-local functionals

Our goal now is to prove the following two-term asymptotic expansion for general semi-local functionals of the density and its gradient.

Theorem 5.1

(Asymptotics for general semi-local functionals)
.

Let
\frac{N}{|Q^{D}|L^{3}}=\bar{\rho}=constant
,

be any determinantal ground state of the free

be the associated single-particle density. Suppose that

\displaystyle F[\rho_{L}]=\begin{cases}f(\bar{\rho},0)|Q^{D}|L^{3}+\mathcal{O}(L^{-\frac{34}{23}+\epsilon}),&\mbox{ for periodic BCs,}\\

where the boundary layer and Fermi momentum corrections are given by

\displaystyle c_{BL}(\bar{\rho})=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2p_{F}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f\bigr(\bar{\rho}(1-h(s)),2\bar{\rho}p_{F}|\dot{h}(s)|\bigr)-f(\bar{\rho},0)\mathrm{d}s,&\mbox{ for Dirichlet BCs,}\\

In the periodic case, since

and a simple Lipschitz estimate.

As the Neumann and Dirichlet case are similar to each other, we give the details only for the Dirichlet case. The proof is somewhat technical, due to the fact that we have not assumed continuity of the derivative of

with respect to

at

(otherwise the small parameter

First note that, since

depends only on

Thus the continuum approximation from Theorem

\displaystyle\rho^{\textnormal{ctm}}_{L}(r)=\gamma^{\textnormal{ctm}}_{L}(r,r)=\bar{\rho}\frac{p_{L}^{3}}{p_{F}^{3}}\sum_{\sigma\in G}\det\sigma h(p_{L}\lVert r-\sigma r\rVert)

(5.1)

(since

\displaystyle C\geq\rho^{\textnormal{ctm}}_{L}(r)\geq\frac{c(\delta)}{2}\quad\mbox{for any $r\in Q_{L,\delta}$ and some $C>0$},

Next, note that by the continuum approximation estimate (see equation (

), we find that

\displaystyle 2C\geq\rho_{L}(r)\geq\frac{c(\delta)}{4}\quad\mbox{for any $r\in Q_{L,\delta}$ and $L$ big enough}.

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta L^{2}+R(\delta)^{2}L+C(\delta)L^{\frac{34}{23}+\epsilon}.

Therefore, by dividing the above by

\displaystyle F[\rho_{L}]=F[\rho^{\textnormal{ctm}}_{L}]+\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{O}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{O}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{O}}}{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{O}$}}(L^{2}).

(5.3)

The next step is to work with the continuum versions on

and use a Taylor expansion of

\displaystyle F[\rho^{\textnormal{ctm}}_{L}]

\displaystyle\frac{p_{L}^{3}}{p_{F}^{3}}-1=\frac{3\pi|\partial Q^{D}|}{8p_{F}|Q^{D}|}\frac{1}{L}+\mathcal{O}(L^{-\frac{35}{23}+\epsilon})

\displaystyle K_{j}(\delta)+J_{\sigma_{j}}(\delta)

\displaystyle=\frac{8\bar{\rho}p_{L}^{3}}{p_{F}^{3}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\chi_{Q_{L,\delta}^{j}}(r)\biggr(\frac{\bar{\rho}p_{L}^{3}}{p_{F}^{3}}\frac{f_{2}^{L}(r,t)}{|\nabla\rho^{\textnormal{ctm}}_{L}(r)|}\biggr(\sum_{\sigma_{jj}=-1}\frac{p_{L}\det\sigma\dot{h}(p_{L}\lVert r-\sigma r\rVert)4r_{j}}{\lVert(1-\sigma)(r_{j},L\pi_{j}r)\rVert}\biggr)^{2}

Remark

(on Theorem
5.1
)
.

•

Note that
c_{FM}(\bar{\rho})
above depends on
\bar{\rho}
which is in contrast with the constant
c_{FM}
defined in Theorem
5.2
below. The reason is that for the “physically” relevant cases (see the discussion before Theorem
1.1
), we have
c_{FM}(\bar{\rho})=-c_{FM}\bar{\rho}
, where
c_{FM}=\frac{3}{8}
is precisely the value defined there (see Corollary
5.1
below).

•

The same arguments can be used for semi-local functionals of higher order derivatives by considering generalized variables like
(\rho,\nabla\rho,...,\partial^{\alpha}\rho)
and applying estimate (
1.15
). As we are only interested in LDA and GGAs for the moment, we leave the asymptotics of functionals for higher order derivatives for future works

•

If
f
is more regular and one knows the ratio at which the derivatives diverge when
(a,b)\rightarrow 0
, one can further use the decay of
h
to improve the remainder term from
\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{O}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{O}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{O}}}{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{O}$}}(L^{2})
(for Dirichlet and Neumann cases) to, possibly,
\mathcal{O}(L^{\frac{34}{23}+\epsilon})
.

As straightforward corollaries of Theorem
5.1
, we obtain the two-term asymptotic expansion for LDA and GGAs from Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 5.1

(Asymptotics of LDA)
.

Let
\rho_{L}
be the single-particle density of any determinantal ground state of the free

-electron gas in

5.2
Exact exchange

Now we turn to the asymptotic expansion of the exact exchange. The analysis here is different as we are dealing with a functional of the density matrix, not the density.

Theorem 5.2

(Asymptotics of exact exchange)
.

Let
\bar{\rho}=\frac{N}{|Q^{D}|L^{3}}=constant
,
\Psi_{L}
be a determinantal ground state of the free
N
-electron gas in
Q_{L}^{D}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle E_{x}[\Psi_{L}]=\begin{cases}-c_{x}\bar{\rho}^{\frac{4}{3}}|Q^{D}|L^{3}+c_{FS}\bar{\rho}|\partial Q^{D}|L^{2}+\mathcal{O}(L^{\frac{45}{23}+\epsilon}),&\mbox{for periodic BCs,}\\
-c_{x}\bar{\rho}^{\frac{4}{3}}|Q^{D}|L^{3}-(c_{BL}^{\textnormal{Dir}}+c_{FM}-c_{FS})\bar{\rho}|\partial Q^{D}|L^{2}+\mathcal{O}(L^{\frac{45}{23}+\epsilon}),&\mbox{for Dirichlet BCs,}\\
-c_{x}\bar{\rho}^{\frac{4}{3}}|Q^{D}|L^{3}-(c_{BL}^{\textnormal{Neu}}-c_{FM}-c_{FS})\bar{\rho}|\partial Q^{D}|L^{2}+\mathcal{O}(L^{\frac{45}{23}+\epsilon}),&\mbox{for Neumann BCs,}\end{cases}

where the finite-size, Fermi momentum and boundary layer corrections are

\displaystyle c_{FS}=\frac{1}{8},\quad c_{FM}=\frac{3}{8},\quad c_{BL}^{\textnormal{Dir}}=-\frac{\log 2}{4}\mbox{, and}\quad c_{BL}^{\textnormal{Neu}}=\frac{3\log 2}{4}.

Proof.

As before, we prove the Dirichlet case in detail and outline the proof for the other two boundary conditions at the end.

The first step is again to justify the use of the continuum density matrices (
4.22
). For this, we use the identity

|\gamma_{L}(r,\tilde{r})|^{2}-|\gamma^{\textnormal{ctm}}_{L}(r,\tilde{r})|^{2}=Re\{\overline{(\gamma_{L}(r,\tilde{r})-\gamma^{\textnormal{ctm}}_{L}(r,\tilde{r}))}(\gamma_{N}(r,\tilde{r})+\gamma^{\textnormal{ctm}}_{N}(r,\tilde{r}))\}

together with estimates (
4.21
) and (
4.23
) from Theorem
4.2
to obtain

\displaystyle\biggr|\int_{Q_{L}^{D}\times Q_{L}^{D}}\frac{|\gamma_{L}(r,\tilde{r})|^{2}-|\gamma^{\textnormal{ctm}}_{L}(r,\tilde{r})|^{2}}{|r-\tilde{r}|}\biggl|\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\tilde{r}

\displaystyle\lesssim L^{-\frac{70}{23}+\epsilon}\int_{Q_{L}^{D}\times Q_{L}^{D}}|r-\tilde{r}|^{-1}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\tilde{r}

\displaystyle+L^{-\frac{35}{23}+\epsilon}\int_{Q_{L}^{D}\times Q_{L}^{D}}\frac{(1+|r-\tilde{r}|_{D,2L})^{-2}}{|r-\tilde{r}|}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\tilde{r}

\displaystyle\lesssim L^{\frac{45}{23}+\epsilon}+L^{\frac{34}{23}+\epsilon}\log L.

Therefore, by the continuum formulas we have

\displaystyle E_{x}[\rho_{L}]\approx-\sum_{\tau,\sigma\in G}\det(\sigma\tau)\frac{\bar{\rho}^{2}p_{L}^{6}}{4p_{F}^{6}}\underbrace{\int_{Q_{L}^{D}\times Q_{L}^{D}}\frac{h(p_{L}|r-\sigma\tilde{r}|_{D,2L})h(p_{L}|r-\tau\tilde{r}|_{D,2L})}{|r-\tilde{r}|}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\tilde{r}}_{\coloneqq J_{\sigma,\tau}(L)},

(5.9)

where
\approx
will be used throughout this proof to denote equality up to errors included in the remainder of Theorem

Overall, there is a good match between numerics and asymptotics as
N
gets large. More detailed observations are the following.

•

Asymptotically, the LDA underestimates the surface term by 12%, whereas B88 and PBE overestimate it by 13% respectively 8%.

•

Asymptotically, only PBEsol is much more accurate than the LDA, exhibiting an error of just 1.4%. This should not come as a surprise to experts, as certain surface data (although not the ones considered here) entered into the choice of the parameters. Thus one may say that the present work provides an alternative theoretical justification of the PBEsol parameters. The price to pay is that PBEsol is the least accurate of the GGAs for very small
N
.

•

B88 is the most accurate GGA for very small
N
. This is not unexpected given the fact that the parameter
\beta
was fitted to data for the first few noble gas atoms. The price to pay is that B88 does not improve on the LDA beyond a few hundred electrons.

•

In the regime of 20 to 100 electrons, which is certainly relevant in applications, particularly in chemistry, PBE fares best.

•

The slowest convergence to the asymptotic value, and the largest fluctuations, occur for exact exchange. Neither asymptotics up to second order nor any of the GGA functionals correctly reproduce these significant finite-
N
fluctuations.
Note that they would be captured exactly by the universal Hohenberg-Kohn functional.

In the context of our model system, the free electron gas in a box with zero boundary conditions,
our rigorous asymptotic results and the above observations illustrate
both the advances that have been made in the physics and chemistry literature in designing computationally simple exchange-correlation functionals, and the immense difficulties in improving on the current state of the art. For the latter, we hope that the new exact constraint on GGAs presented here (eq. (
1.14
)) will in the future turn out to be useful.

Q_{L}=[0,L]^{3}
,
N/L^{3}=1
, is unique and the exact exchange energy is, by rescaling to the fixed domain

\displaystyle E_{x}[\Psi_{N,L}]

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{L}\int_{Q\times Q}\frac{\left|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}^{3},\,|k|\leq R_{N}}\phi_{k}(x)\overline{\phi_{k}(y)}\right|^{2}}{|x-y|}dx\,dy

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{3}\\
|k|,|\ell|\leq R_{N}\end{subarray}}\underbrace{\int_{Q\times Q}\frac{\prod\limits_{i=1}^{3}4\sin(\pi k_{i}x_{i})\sin(\pi\ell_{i}x_{i})\sin(\pi k_{i}y_{i})\sin(\pi\ell_{i}y_{i})}{|x-y|}dx\,dy}_{=:I_{k,\ell}}

(A.2)

where
R_{N}
is the Dirichlet Fermi radius defined in (
2.6
).

The starting point of our numerical scheme is a simple calculation which reduces the above six-dimensional integral to the three-dimensional integral of a separable function times the Coulomb potential over a finite region. For periodic boundary conditions such a reduction is trivial because the system is translation invariant, which implies that the exchange integrand depends only on the relative coordinate
z=x-y
; but the zero boundary condition breaks the translation invariance. Nevertheless the following holds:

Lemma A.1
.

For
k
,
\ell\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
, and
I_{k,\ell}
as defined above,

\displaystyle I_{k,\ell}=8\int_{[0,1]^{3}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{3}f_{k_{i},\ell_{i}}(z_{i})}{|z|}dz

(A.3)

where for
a,b\in\mathbb{N}
,
f_{a,b}
is defined as

\displaystyle f_{a,b}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\tau}{2}\Bigl(\cos(\pi(a+b)\tau)+\cos(\pi(a-b)\tau)\Bigr)+\biggr(\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}-\frac{1}{(a+b)}\biggr)\frac{\sin(\pi(a+b)\tau)}{2\pi}

\displaystyle+\begin{cases}\Bigl(\frac{1}{a}-\frac{1}{b}-\frac{1}{a-b}\Bigr)\frac{\sin(\pi(a-b)\tau)}{2\pi}&\mbox{ if }a\neq b,\\
\;\;\;\;\frac{1-\tau}{2}&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(A.4)

We remark that there is a well known alternative reduction of any 6D Coulomb integral of the form
\int u(x)\frac{1}{|x-y|}v(y)\,dx\,dy
to a 3D integral over reciprocal space, by using the convolution theorem for the Fourier transform. But this leads to an integral over an unbounded domain, a stronger (
\sim 1/|k|^{2}
) singularity, and – in our case – a slow decay of the integrand, making the expression (
A.3
) numerically much more favourable.

Proof.

Using the identity

2\sin(A)\sin(B)=\cos(A-B)-\cos(A+B)
and the change of variables
w=x+y,\,z=x-y
, and noting that the volume element becomes
\mathrm{d}x_{i}\mathrm{d}y_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}w_{i}\mathrm{d}z_{i}
,

I_{k,\ell}=\int_{[-1,1]^{3}}\frac{1}{|z|}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\underbrace{\left\{\frac{1}{2}\int_{|z_{i}|}^{2-|z_{i}|}\bigl(\cos\pi k_{i}z_{i}-\cos\pi k_{i}w_{i}\bigr)\bigl(\cos\pi\ell_{i}z_{i}-\cos\pi\ell_{i}w_{i}\bigr)dw_{i}\right\}}_{=:f_{k_{i},\ell_{i}}(z_{i})}dz.

The integral in the definition of
f_{a,b}(z_{i})
is elementary to evaluate by using the identity
2\cos A\cos B=cos(A+B)+\cos(A-B)
for the term
\cos\pi aw_{i}\cos\pi bw_{i}
, yielding

\displaystyle f_{a,b}(z_{i})

\displaystyle=(1-|z_{i}|)\cos(\pi az_{i})\cos(\pi bz_{i})+\cos(\pi az_{i})\frac{\sin(\pi b|z_{i}|)}{\pi b}+\cos(\pi bz_{i})\frac{\sin(\pi a|z_{i}|)}{\pi a}

\displaystyle-\frac{\sin(\pi(a+b)|z_{i}|)}{2\pi(a+b)}\;\;\begin{cases}-\frac{\sin(\pi(a-b)|z_{i}|)}{2\pi(a-b)}&\mbox{if }a\neq b,\\
+\frac{1-|z_{i}|}{2}&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Next, we note that since
f_{a,b}(-z_{i})=f_{a,b}(z_{i})
and the Coulomb potential
1/|z|
is invariant under the transformations
z_{i}\mapsto-z_{i}
, we can replace
[-1,1]^{3}
by
[0,1]^{3}
by adding a factor of
8
in front of the integral. The final expression for

given in the lemma now follows from the trigonometric identities
2\cos A\cos B=\cos(A+B)+\cos(A-B)
and
\alpha\sin A\cos B+\beta\cos A\sin B=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}\sin(A+B)+\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\sin(A-B)
.
∎

Since the integrand in (

Such an approximation is provided by recent advances in low-rank tensor approximation; more specifically, we use results of Hackbusch (
[
12

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}\approx\sum_{j=1}^{M}\omega_{j}e^{-\alpha_{j}r^{2}}.

(A.5)

Plugging this approximation into equation (

\displaystyle I_{k,\ell}\approx 8\sum_{j=1}^{M}\omega_{j}\prod_{i=1}^{3}I_{k_{i},\ell_{i},j}\;\mbox{ with }\;I_{a,b,j}=\int_{0}^{1}f_{a,b}(t)e^{-\alpha_{j}t^{2}}dt,

which reduces the 3D integral in (

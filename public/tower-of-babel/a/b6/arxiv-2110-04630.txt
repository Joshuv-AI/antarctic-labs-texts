Abstract
. A convergence result for perturbed holomorphic cylinders in ℝ ⊭ ​ ⋉ \mathbb{R}^{2n} is proved; a sequence of perturbed holomorphic cylinders converges to a configuration of two holomorphic disks joined by a flow line. A key step in the computation is played by an a priori exponential estimate.

Remark 2
.

Other researchers have considered this problem previously. See
[
Oh05
]
for a similar compactness result and see
[
OZ10
]
and
[
OZ12
]
for an analysis of the reverse
gluing
process. The paper
[
OZ12
]
also shows exponential convergence to a gradient flow segment. See also
[
MT09
]
for a related compactness result which also exhibits the formation of gradient flow lines. These papers assume the energy of the holomorphic cylinders is small, while in this paper we assume that the image of
u_{n}
lies inside of a ball
\overline{B}(1)
(i.e., we assume a
C^{0}
bound whereas they assume an
L^{2}
bound on the derivative).

A similar compactness phenomenon in the context of harmonic mappings is proved in
[
CT99
]
, where geodesic segments appear rather than gradient flow lines.

Remark 3
.

In the course of our argument, we will need to pass to sub-cylinders a few times to apply the a priori estimates from Section
2
. As a consequence, our description of the limit is only valid on
[-r_{n},r_{n}]\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
. In applications, some other information will be needed to control the behavior of
u_{n}
on the ends (e.g.,
u_{n}
is the restriction of a map defined on a larger Riemann surface
\Sigma_{n}
equipped with an embedding
[-r_{n}-1,r_{n}+1]\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\to\Sigma_{n}
, or
u_{n}
has totally real boundary conditions on the two boundary circles).

Remark 4
.

If
\epsilon_{n}r_{n}
converges to
\infty
, then we expect any convergence statement to require topological restrictions on
V
(e.g., if
V
is a gradient vector field for a Morse function, then
u_{n}
will converge to a broken flow line; or, if
V_{n}
is a Morsification of a Morse-bott vector field
V
, then
u_{n}
will converge to a Morse-Bott broken flow line, etc).

Remark 5
.

The set-up in Definition
1
is a bit of a “toy problem” since we do not consider more general almost complex structures
J
. The author expects more general almost complex structures can be handled in a similar way. The estimates/computations will involve many additional junk terms involving the derivatives of
J
(i.e., some things which cancel exactly in this paper will not cancel exactly with a more general
J
). Hopefully the ideas conveyed in this paper will still be of use to readers interested in more general almost complex structures.

2.
A Priori Estimates

In this section we will prove various a priori estimates for maps
u_{n}
satisfying Definition
1
. Our first result is a
uniform a priori estimate
for
u_{n}
and its derivatives. The elliptic estimates for the “delbar” operator
\overline{\partial}=\partial_{s}+J_{0}\partial_{t}
imply that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta>0
there is a constant
C^{\mathrm{ell}}_{\delta,k}
so that for any smooth
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
-valued function
u
we have:

(2)

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{W^{k+1,2}(s+\Omega(\delta))}\leq C^{\mathrm{ell}}_{\delta,k}(\left\lVert(\partial_{s}+J_{0}\partial_{t})u\right\rVert_{W^{k,2}(s+\Omega(2\delta))}+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{W^{k,2}(s+\Omega(2\delta))}),

where
\Omega(\delta)=[-\delta,\delta]\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
. See
[
RS01
, Lemma C.1]
for a short proof. In particular, the fact that
V_{n}
converges in
C^{\infty}
and
\left|u_{n}\right|
is everywhere less than
1
, together with the Sobolev embedding theorem, implies the following following result:

Lemma 2
.

Assuming the set-up in Definition
1
, we have the following a priori estimates for the behavior of
u_{n}
on the sub-cylinder
[-r_{n},r_{n}]\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
:

(3)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{(s,t)\in[-r_{n},r_{n}]\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\left|\nabla^{k}u_{n}(s,t)\right|\leq C_{k},

where
C_{k}
depends only on
k
.

Proof
.

It is straightforward to show that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left\lVert u_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{k,2}(s+\Omega(2^{-k}))}\leq C_{k}^{\prime}.

Then the Sobolev embedding theorem (see
[
MS12
, Theorem B.1.11]
) gives

\left|\nabla^{k}u(s,t)\right|\leq\left\lVert u_{n}\right\rVert_{C^{k}(s+\Omega(2^{-k-2}))}\leq C^{\mathrm{Sob}}_{k}\left\lVert u_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{k+2,2}(s+\Omega(2^{-k-2}))}.

These two observations complete the proof.

Next, we will prove an
exponential type estimate
that shows
u_{n}
becomes very thin as we approach the center of the cylinder.

Definition 6
.

Introduce the
center of mass

q_{n}:[-r_{n}-1,r_{n}+1]\to B(1)
given by

q_{n}(s)=\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}u_{n}(s,t)\,\mathrm{d}t.

Assuming that
u_{n}
satisfies Definition
1
, then we compute

(4)

q_{n}^{\prime}(s)-\epsilon_{n}V_{n}(q_{n}(s))=\epsilon_{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}(u_{n}(s,t))-V_{n}(q_{n}(s))\,\mathrm{d}t.

We think of of (
4
) as a perturbation of the flow line equation for
V_{n}
.

Introduce the function

(5)

\gamma_{n}(s)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\left|u_{n}(s,t)-q_{n}(s)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Our goal is to show that
\gamma_{n}^{\prime\prime}(s)-\delta^{2}\gamma_{n}(s)\geq 0
for some
\delta>0
. As explained in the pre-print
[
Can21
, Section 4.4]
the estimate
\gamma^{\prime\prime}-\delta^{2}\gamma\geq 0
is related to exponential decay estimates.

Remark 7
.

For simplicity of notation, we introduce the norm:

\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\left|f(t)\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t.

Lemma 3
.

There is a constant
\delta>0
, independent of
n
and
u_{n}
, so that
\gamma_{n}
satisfies the following differential inequality

(6)

\gamma_{n}^{\prime\prime}(s)-\delta^{2}\gamma_{n}(s)\geq\frac{3}{4}\left\lVert\partial_{s}(u_{n}-q_{n})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert\partial_{t}(u_{n}-q_{n})\right\rVert^{2}

for
s\in[-r_{n},r_{n}]
and for
n
sufficiently large. More precisely, there is
N
depending on
\left\{\epsilon_{n}\right\}
so that (
6
) holds for
n\geq N
and
s\in[-r_{n},r_{n}]
.

Proof
.

We compute

\gamma_{n}^{\prime\prime}(s)=\left\lVert\partial_{s}(u_{n}-q_{n})\right\rVert^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\left\langle u_{n}-q_{n},\partial_{s}\partial_{s}(u_{n}-q_{n})\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t.

We have

(7)

\partial_{s}(u_{n}-q_{n})=-J_{0}\partial_{t}(u_{n}-q_{n})+\epsilon_{n}(V_{n}(u_{n})-\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}(u_{n})\,\mathrm{d}t),

hence

\partial_{s}\partial_{s}(u_{n}-q_{n})=-\partial_{t}\partial_{t}(u_{n}-q_{n})+\epsilon_{n}(\partial_{s}-J_{0}\partial_{t})[V_{n}(u_{n})-\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}(u_{n})\,\mathrm{d}t].

Integration by parts then yields

(8)

\gamma_{n}^{\prime\prime}(s)=\left\lVert\partial_{s}(u_{n}-q_{n})\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\partial_{t}(u_{n}-q_{n})\right\rVert^{2}+\epsilon_{n}\int\left\langle u_{n}-q_{n},\beta_{n}\right\rangle\mathrm{d}t,

where

\beta_{n}=(\partial_{s}-J_{0}\partial_{t})[V_{n}(u_{n})-\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}(u_{n})\,\mathrm{d}t].

We rewrite
\beta_{n}
as follows:

\displaystyle V_{n}(u_{n})-\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}(u_{n})

\displaystyle=V_{n}(u_{n})-V_{n}(q_{n})+\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}(u_{n})-V_{n}(q_{n})\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=A_{n}\cdot(u_{n}-q_{n}),

where

(9)

A_{n}=\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}V_{n}((1-\tau)q_{n}+\tau u_{n})\mathrm{d}\tau-\int_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}V_{n}((1-\tau)q_{n}+\tau u_{n})\mathrm{d}\tau\mathrm{d}t.

As a consequence of the a priori estimates (
3
) we conclude that
A_{n}
is uniformly bounded in
C^{1}
on the cylinder
[-r_{n},r_{n}]\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
. For
n
sufficiently large (say
n\geq N
), we can bound its derivatives by a constant independent of
u_{n}
.

Since

\beta_{n}=((\partial_{s}-J_{0}\partial_{t})A_{n})\cdot(u_{n}-q_{n})+A_{n}\cdot((\partial_{s}-J_{0}\partial_{t})(u_{n}-q_{n})),

we conclude that for
s\in[-r_{n},r_{n}]

\left|\beta_{n}\right|\leq C_{1}(\left|u_{n}-q_{n}\right|+\left|\partial_{s}(u_{n}-q_{n})\right|+\left|\partial_{t}(u_{n}-q_{n})\right|),

where
C_{1}
is independent of
n\geq N
and
u_{n}
. By applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and the inequality
2ab\leq a^{2}+b^{2}
, we conclude from (
8
) that

\displaystyle\gamma_{n}^{\prime\prime}(s)

\displaystyle\geq\left\lVert\partial_{s}(u_{n}-q_{n})\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\partial_{t}(u_{n}-q_{n})\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle-\epsilon_{n}C_{2}(\left\lVert u_{n}-q_{n}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\partial_{s}(u_{n}-q_{n})\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\partial_{t}(u_{n}-q_{n})\right\rVert^{2}),

where
C_{2}
is independent of
n\geq N
and
u_{n}
. The next step in the argument is to invoke the
Poincaré Inequality
, which states that there is a constant
c_{\mathrm{pc}}>0
so that

\text{$f:\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}$ has mean zero}\implies\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{2}\leq c_{\mathrm{pc}}\left\lVert\partial_{t}f\right\rVert^{2}.

A simple proof is available using Fourier analysis. Noting that
u_{n}-q_{n}
has mean zero (by construction), we conclude that we can pick
n
sufficiently large so that
\epsilon_{n}C_{2}
is less than
\min\left\{0.25c_{\mathrm{pc}}^{-1},0.25\right\}
, which yields

\gamma_{n}^{\prime\prime}(s)-\frac{1}{4c_{\mathrm{pc}}}\gamma_{n}(s)\geq\frac{3}{4}\left\lVert\partial_{s}(u_{n}-q_{n})\right\rVert^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\left\lVert\partial_{t}(u_{n}-q_{n})\right\rVert^{2},

provided
s\in[-r_{n},r_{n}]
. Setting
\delta^{2}=(4c_{\mathrm{pc}})^{-1}
yields the desired result.

[

on the

Lemma 4

Suppose that

satisfies Definition

C

\displaystyle\int_{s-0.5}^{s+0.5}

\displaystyle\left\lVert u_{n}-q_{n}\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\partial_{s}(u_{n}-q_{n})\right\rVert^{2}+\left\lVert\partial_{t}(u_{n}-q_{n})\right\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C(e^{-\delta(r_{n}+s)}+e^{-\delta(r_{n}-s)})

for
s\in[-r_{n}+1,r_{n}-1]
and for
n
sufficiently large. Notice that the left hand side is the
W^{1,2}
norm (squared) over the sub-cylinder
[s-0.5,s+0.5]\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}

\delta>0
is the constant appearing in the differential inequality (

Proof

Let
\gamma_{n}
be the quantity introduced in (

It is clear that
\gamma_{n}(-r_{n})

\beta_{n}(-r_{n})

As a consequence,

(10)

\gamma_{n}(s)\leq c(e^{-\delta(r_{n}+s)}+e^{-\delta(r_{n}-s)}),

for

. It remains to estimate the integrals of

3.
Convergence to the limiting configuration

Our goal in this section is to prove that the sequence
u_{n}
converges near the ends of the cylinder to holomorphic disks with removable singularities
x_{-}
and
x_{+}
, and, away from the ends,
u_{n}
converges to a flow line for
V=\lim_{n\to\infty}V_{n}
connecting
x_{-}
to
x_{+}
. See Figure
1
for an illustration of the idea.

As we will see below, in order to capture the limiting flow line, we will need to rescale the map
u_{n}
, i.e., we instead consider the limiting behavior of

v_{n}(s,t)=u_{n}(\epsilon_{n}^{-1}s,\epsilon_{n}^{-1}t)

defined on the rescaled cylinder
[-\epsilon_{n}r_{n},\epsilon_{n}r_{n}]\times\mathbb{R}/\epsilon_{n}\mathbb{Z}
.

The Helmholtz decomposition of a B ​ M ​ O BMO type vector field in a slightly perturbed half space
Yoshikazu Giga, Zhongyang Gu

Abstract
We introduce a space of L 2 L^{2} vector fields with bounded mean oscillation whose “normal” component to the boundary is well-controlled. We establish its Helmholtz decomposition in the case when the domain is a perturbed C 3 C^{3} half space in 𝐑 n \mathbf{R}^{n} ( n ≥ 3 ) (n\geq 3) with small perturbation.



Keywords: Helmholtz decomposition,
BMO
space, perturbed half space.

1
Introduction

This is a continuation of our paper
[
14
]
.
It is well known that the Helmholtz decomposition plays a key role in analyzing the Stokes and the Navier-Stokes equations
[
25
]
.
Such decomposition is well studied for
L^{p}
space with
1<p<\infty
.
It is a topological direct sum decomposition

L^{p}(\Omega)^{n}=L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)\oplus G^{p}(\Omega)

of the
L^{p}
vector fields in a domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
Here,
L^{p}_{\sigma}(\Omega)
denotes the
L^{p}
-closure of the space of all smooth div-free vector fields that are compactly supported and
G^{p}
denotes the space of all
L^{p}
gradient fields.
If
p=2
, such decomposition holds for any domain
\Omega
.
It is an orthogonal decomposition and often called Weyl’s decomposition.
For
1<p<\infty
, the decomposition still holds for various domain including the whole space
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, the half space
\mathbf{R}^{n}_{+}
, a perturbed half space, a bounded smooth domain
[
10
]
and an exterior smooth domain.
However, there are smooth unbounded domains which do not admit
L^{p}
-Helmholtz decomposition;
see e.g. a nice book of Galdi
[
11
]
.

If
p=\infty
, such a decomposition does not hold even when
\Omega=\mathbf{R}^{n}
since the projection operator is a kind of the Riesz operator which is unbounded in
L^{\infty}
, though it is bounded in
L^{p}
(
1<p<\infty
).
We replace
L^{\infty}
by
BMO
space.
It turns out that it is convenient to consider a subspace
vBMO
of
BMO
to have the Helmholtz decomposition, at least for a half space
[
12
]
and a bounded domain
[
14
]
.
Our goal is to extend such a result to a perturbed half space.
Unfortunately, it seems that a direct extension is difficult because the behavior at space infinity is not well controlled.
Thus we consider the
L^{2}
intersection of this space.
For
L^{p}
space, Farwig, Kozono, and Sohr
[
7
]
established the Helmholtz decomposition of
L^{p}\cap L^{2}
(
p\geq 2
) and
L^{p}+L^{2}
(
1<p<2
) for arbitrary uniformly
C^{2}
smooth domain in
\mathbf{R}^{3}
.
(Later, it is extended to arbitrary dimension
[
8
]
.)
Although we consider
vBMO\cap L^{2}
in a slightly perturbed half space in the present paper, our results extend to any uniformly
C^{3}
domain.
This will be discussed in a separate forthcoming paper.

Let us recall the
BMO
space of vector fields introduced in
[
13
]
,
[
14
]
.
For
\mu\in(0,\infty]
, we recall the
BMO
seminorm.
For a locally integrable function
f
in
\Omega
, i.e.,
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)
, we set

[f]_{BMO^{\mu}(\Omega)}:=\sup\left\{\frac{1}{\left|B_{r}(x)\right|}\int_{B_{r}(x)}\left|f(y)-f_{B_{r}(x)}\right|\,dy\biggm|B_{r}(x)\subset\Omega,\ r<\mu\right\};

here
f_{B}
denotes the average over
B
and
B_{r}(x)
denotes the open ball of radius
r
centered at
x
and
\lvert B\rvert
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
B
.
For
\nu\in(0,\infty]
, we also use a seminorm

[f]_{b^{\nu}(\Gamma)}:=\sup\left\{r^{-n}\int_{\Omega\cap B_{r}(x)}\left|f(y)\right|\,dy\biggm|x\in\Gamma,\ 0<r<\nu\right\},

where
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
denotes the boundary of
\Omega
.
The space

BMO^{\mu,\nu}_{b}(\Omega):=\left\{f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)\,\middle|\,[f]_{BMO^{\mu}(\Omega)}+[f]_{b^{\nu}(\Gamma)}<\infty\right\}

is essentially introduced in
[
2
]
and well studied in
[
5
]
.
The Stokes semigroup in such spaces was studied
[
2
]
,
[
4
]
and it is useful to prove that the analyticity of the Stokes semigroup still holds in some unbounded domains which do not admit
L^{p}
-Helmholtz decomposition
[
3
]
.

Our space
vBMO
requires a control only on the normal component.
Let
d_{\Gamma}
denote the distance function from
\Gamma
.
We set

\displaystyle[v]_{vBMO^{\mu,\nu}(\Omega)}:=[v]_{BMO^{\mu}(\Omega)}+[\nabla d_{\Gamma}\cdot v]_{b^{\nu}(\Gamma)},

\displaystyle vBMO^{\mu,\nu}(\Omega):=\left\{v\in L^{1}_{loc}(\Omega)^{n}\,\middle|\,[v]_{vBMO^{\mu,\nu}(\Omega)}<\infty\right\},

where
\cdot
denotes the standard inner product.
This is a seminorm.
If
\Omega=\mathbf{R}^{n}_{+}
, this is not a norm unless
n=1
,
\nu=\infty
.
However, if
\Gamma
has a fully curved part in the sense of
[
13
, Definition 7]
, then
[\cdot]_{vBMO^{\mu,\nu}(\Omega)}
becomes a norm
[
13
, Lemma 8]
.
In particular, when
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{2}
domain, this is a norm.
In this paper, we consider the case where
\Omega
is a perturbed
C^{k}
half space

\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}:=\left\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\,\middle|\,x_{n}>h(x^{\prime})\right\},

where
h\not\equiv 0
is in
C^{k}_{c}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
, i.e.,
h
is a compactly supported
C^{k}
function in
\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
;
here
x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})
for
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
A perturbed
C^{k}
(
k\geq 2
) half space
\mathbf{R}^{n}_{h}
is said to be type
(K)
if

\sup_{x^{\prime}\in\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}\left\lvert\nabla^{\prime 2}h(x^{\prime})\right\rvert<K

where
\nabla^{\prime}:=(\partial_{1},\partial_{2},\ldots,\partial_{n-1})
.
We note that the perturbed
C^{k}
half space has a fully curved part so that
[v]_{vBMO^{\mu,\nu}(\Omega)}
is a norm.
By definition, there always exists
R_{h}>0
such that the support
\operatorname{supp}h\subseteq\overline{B_{R_{h}}(0^{\prime})}
.
We say that the perturbed
C^{k}
half space
\mathbf{R}^{n}_{h}
has small perturbation if

\displaystyle R_{h}^{\frac{2n-1}{2n}}<\frac{1}{2},\quad C_{s}(h)^{\frac{3n}{2}+8}C_{1}(h)\left(C_{\ast,1}(h)+C_{\ast,2}(h)+R_{h}^{\frac{n}{2}}\right)<\frac{1}{2C^{\ast}(n)}

(1)

where
C^{\ast}(n)
is a specific constant depending only on the space dimension
n
,

\displaystyle C_{s}(h):=1+\lVert h\rVert_{C^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})},\quad C_{1}(h):=1+R_{h}\left\lVert\nabla^{\prime 2}h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})},

\displaystyle C_{\ast,1}(h):=C_{1}(h)^{3}\left(1+R_{h}^{\frac{1}{4}}\right)\left(R_{h}^{\frac{1}{2}}\lVert\nabla^{\prime 2}h\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}+R_{h}^{\frac{5}{2}}\lVert\nabla^{\prime 2}h\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{3}\right),

\displaystyle C_{\ast,2}(h):=\left(R_{h}+R_{h}^{\frac{1}{2h}}\right)\left\lVert\nabla^{\prime 2}h\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}+\left(R_{h}^{n-1}+1\right)\lVert h\rVert_{C^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}.

To simplify the behavior near the infinity, we consider

vBMOL^{2}(\Omega):=vBMO^{\mu,\nu}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega).

Note that this space is independent of the choice of
\mu,\nu
.
The purpose of this paper is to establish the Helmholtz decomposition for the space
vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)
in the case where
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
is a perturbed
C^{3}
half space that has small perturbation with
n\geq 3
. Here is our main theorem.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
be a perturbed
C^{3}
half space of type
(K)
that has small perturbation with
n\geq 3
and
\Gamma=\partial\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
.
Then for any
v\in vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)
, there exists a unique decomposition
v=v_{0}+\nabla q
with

\displaystyle v_{0}\in vBMOL^{2}_{\sigma}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)

\displaystyle:=\left\{f\in vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)\,\middle|\,\operatorname{div}f=0\ \text{in}\ \mathbf{R}_{h}^{n},\ f\cdot\mathbf{n}=0\ \text{on}\ \Gamma\right\},

\displaystyle\nabla q\in GvBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\nabla p\in vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)\,\middle|\,p\in L^{2}_{loc}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)\right\}

satisfying the estimate

\|v_{0}\|_{vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)}+\|\nabla q\|_{vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)}\leq C(K,R_{\ast},R_{h})\|v\|_{vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)},

where
C(K,R_{\ast},R_{h})
is a constant that depends only on the constant
K
which controls the second order derivative of
h
, the reach of the boundary
R_{\ast}
and the size
R_{h}
which characterizes the support of
h
.
In particular, the Helmholtz projection
P_{vBMOL^{2}}
, defined by
P_{vBMOL^{2}}(v)=v_{0}
, is a bounded linear map on
vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)
with range
vBMOL^{2}_{\sigma}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)
and kernel
GvBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)
.

Roughly speaking, the reach
R_{\ast}
represents the size of a small neighborhood of the boundary within which every point has a unique projection on the boundary. Here we would like to direct the readers to subsection
2.1
for its precise definition.

Our strategy to prove Theorem
1
follows from the potential theoretic strategy we used to establish the Helmholtz decomposition in a bounded
C^{3}
domain
[
14
]
.
Let
E
represents the fundamental solution of
-\Delta
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
By
[
21
]
, we see that as long as the boundary
\Gamma
is uniformly
C^{2}
, the space
BMO^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega)
allows the standard cut-off,
i.e., we can decompose
v
into two parts
v=v_{1}+v_{2}
with
v_{2}=\varphi v
and
v_{1}=v-v_{2}
with some
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
that is supported within a small neighborhood of
\Gamma
.
Thus, the support of
v_{2}
lies in a small neighborhood of
\Gamma
whereas the support of
v_{1}
is away from
\Gamma
.
For
v_{1}
, by extending
v_{1}
as zero outside
\Omega
, we just set
q^{1}_{1}=E*\operatorname{div}v_{1}
.
Then, the
L^{\infty}
bound for
\nabla q^{1}_{1}
is well controlled near
\Gamma
, which yields a bound for
b^{\nu}
semi-norm.
To estimate
v_{2}
, we use a normal coordinate system near
\Gamma
and reduce the problem to the half space.
We extend
v_{2}
to
\mathbf{R}^{n}
so that the normal part
(\nabla d\cdot\overline{v}_{2})\nabla d
is odd and the tangential part
\overline{v_{2}}-(\nabla d\cdot\overline{v_{2}})\nabla d
is even in the direction of
\nabla d
with respect to
\Gamma
.
In such type of coordinate system, the minus Laplacian can be transformed as

L=A-B+\text{lower order terms},\ A=-\Delta_{\eta},\ B=\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n-1}\partial_{\eta_{i}}b_{ij}\partial_{\eta_{j}},

where
\eta_{n}
is the normal direction to the boundary so that
\{\eta_{n}>0\}
is the half space.
We then use a freezing coefficient method to construct volume potential
q_{1}^{\mathrm{tan}}
and
q_{1}^{\mathrm{nor}}
, which corresponds to the contribution from the tangential part
\overline{v_{2}}^{\mathrm{tan}}
and the normal part
\overline{v_{2}}^{\mathrm{nor}}
, respectively. Since the leading term of
\operatorname{div}\overline{v_{2}}^{\mathrm{nor}}
in normal coordinate consists of the differential of
\eta_{n}
only,
if we extend the coefficient
b_{ij}
even in
\eta_{n}
,

q_{1}^{\mathrm{nor}}
is constructed so that the leading term of
\nabla d\cdot\nabla q_{1}^{\mathrm{nor}}
is odd in the direction of
\nabla d
.
On the other hand, as the leading term of
\operatorname{div}\overline{v_{2}}^{\mathrm{tan}}
in normal coordinate consists of the differential of
\eta^{\prime}=(\eta_{1},...,\eta_{n-1})
only, the even extension of
b_{ij}
in
\eta_{n}
gives rise to
q_{1}^{\mathrm{tan}}
so that the leading term of
\nabla d\cdot\nabla q_{1}^{\mathrm{tan}}
is also odd in the direction of
\nabla d
.
Disregarding lower order terms and localization procedure,
q_{1}^{\mathrm{tan}}
and
q_{1}^{\mathrm{nor}}
are constructed as

\displaystyle q_{1}^{\mathrm{tan}}

\displaystyle=-L^{-1}\operatorname{div}\overline{v}^{\mathrm{tan}}_{2}=-A^{-1}(I-BA^{-1})^{-1}\operatorname{div}\overline{v}^{\mathrm{tan}}_{2},

\displaystyle q_{1}^{\mathrm{nor}}

\displaystyle=-L^{-1}\operatorname{div}\overline{v}^{\mathrm{nor}}_{2}=-A^{-1}(I-BA^{-1})^{-1}\operatorname{div}\overline{v}^{\mathrm{nor}}_{2}.

One is able to arrange
BA^{-1}
small by working in a small neighborhood of a boundary point.
Then
(I-BA^{-1})^{-1}
is given as the Neumann series
\sum^{\infty}_{m=0}(BA^{-1})^{m}
.
The
BMO
-
BMO
estimates for
\nabla q_{1}^{\mathrm{tan}}
and
\nabla q_{1}^{\mathrm{nor}}
follow from
[
9
]
.
Since the leading term of
\nabla d\cdot(\nabla q_{1}^{\mathrm{tan}}+\nabla q_{1}^{\mathrm{nor}})
is odd in the direction of
\nabla d
with respect to
\Gamma
, the
BMO
bound implies
b^{\nu}
bound.
The
L^{2}
estimates for
\nabla q_{1}^{\mathrm{tan}}
and
\nabla q_{1}^{\mathrm{nor}}
hold as in the localization procedure, the partition of unity we consider for a small neighborhood of
\Gamma
is locally finite.
As a result, setting
q_{1}=q^{1}_{1}+q_{1}^{\mathrm{tan}}+q_{1}^{\mathrm{nor}}
would give us our desired volume potential corresponding to
\operatorname{div}v
.
Some results needed for the construction of volume potential
q_{1}
have already been established in
[
14
, Section 3]
and
[
21
]
, for these parts we omit their proofs and recall them directly.

Theorem 2

(Construction of a suitable volume potential)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
be a uniformly
C^{3}
domain of type
(\alpha,\beta,K)
with
n\geq 2
. Let
R_{\ast}
be the reach of the boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
.
Then, there exists a bounded linear operator
v\longmapsto q_{1}
from
vBMOL^{2}(\Omega)
to
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that

-\Delta q_{1}=\operatorname{div}v\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega

and that there exists a constant
C=C(\alpha,\beta,K,R_{\ast})>0
satisfying

\|\nabla q_{1}\|_{vBMOL^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v\|_{vBMOL^{2}(\Omega)}.

In particular, the operator
v\longmapsto\nabla q_{1}
is a bounded linear operator in
vBMOL^{2}(\Omega)
.

Here
(\alpha,\beta,K)
are parameters that characterize the regularity of
\Gamma
. We would like to direct the readers to subsection
2.1
for their precise definitions.
Although the construction of the suitable volume potential works for arbitrary uniformly
C^{3}
domain in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
, for the rest of the theory we need to focus back on perturbed half space in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 3
.
For
v\in vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)
, we observe that
w=v-\nabla q_{1}
is divergence free in
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
.
Unfortunately, this
w
may not fulfill the trace condition
w\cdot\mathbf{n}=0
on the boundary
\Gamma=\partial\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
.
We construct another potential
q_{2}
by solving the Neumann problem

\displaystyle\Delta q_{2}

\displaystyle=0\quad\quad\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial q_{2}}{\partial n}

\displaystyle=w\cdot\mathbf{n}\quad\;\text{on}\quad\Gamma.

We then set
q=q_{1}+q_{2}
.
Since
\partial q_{2}/\partial\mathbf{n}=\nabla q_{2}\cdot\mathbf{n}
,
v_{0}=v-\nabla q
gives the Helmholtz decomposition.
To complete the proof of Theorem
1
, it suffices to control
\|\nabla q_{2}\|_{vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)}
by
\|v\|_{vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)}
.

Lemma 3

(Estimate of the normal trace)
.

Let
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
be a perturbed
C^{2+\kappa}
half space of type
(K)
with
\kappa\in(0,1)
,
n\geq 3
and
\Gamma=\partial\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
.
Then, there is a constant
C=C(K,R_{\ast},R_{h})>0
such that

\|w\cdot\mathbf{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|w\|_{vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)}

for all
w\in vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)
with
\operatorname{div}w=0
in
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
.

Here
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
is a Hilbert space that is isomorphic to
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
, which turns out to be the dual space of the homogeneous fractional Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
. Here, the homogeneous Sobolev space of order
s\in\mathbf{R}
is defined as

\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1}):=\Bigg\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})\,\Bigg|\,\widehat{f}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})\quad\text{and}\\
\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2s}\big|\widehat{f}(\xi^{\prime})\big|^{2}\,d\xi^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty\Bigg\}

where
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
denotes the space of Schwartz’s tempered distributions and
\widehat{f}
denotes the Fourier transform of
f
, see e.g.
[
1
, Section 1.3]
.
Unfortunately, the space
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
is complete if and only if
n\geq 3
.
Thus, we assume that
n\geq 3
when the
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
norm appears.
The basic idea to establish Lemma
3
is as follows.
The
L^{\infty}
estimate of
w\cdot\mathbf{n}
follows directly from the trace theorem established in
[
13
, Theorem 22]
.
For the
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
estimate for
w\cdot\mathbf{n}
, we split the boundary into the straight part and the curved part. Since we have the
L^{\infty}
estimate for
w\cdot\mathbf{n}
and the curved part is compact, the contribution in the
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
estimate for
w\cdot\mathbf{n}
that comes from the curved part can be estimated by the
L^{\infty}
norm of
w\cdot\mathbf{n}
.
For the contribution in the
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
estimate of
w\cdot\mathbf{n}
that comes from the straight part, we invoke the
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
estimate of
w\cdot\mathbf{n}
in the case of the half space.
We finally need the estimate for the Neumann problem.

Lemma 4

(Estimate for the Neumann problem)
.

Let
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
be a perturbed
C^{2}
half space of type
(K)
that has small perturbation with
n\geq 3
. Let
R_{\ast}
be the reach of
\Gamma=\partial\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
.
For any
g\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
, there exists a unique (up to constant) solution
u\in L_{loc}^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)
to the Neumann problem

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Delta u&=0\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\mathbf{n}}&=g\quad\text{on}\quad\Gamma\end{aligned}

(2)

such that the operator
g\longmapsto u
is linear. Moreover, there exists a constant
C=C(K,R_{\ast},R_{h})>0
such that

\|\nabla u\|_{vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}.

To establish Lemma
4
, the basic strategy is the same as in
[
14
]
, we firstly show that

\left\|\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g\big)\right\|_{vBMO^{\infty,\nu}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)}

can be controlled for any
g\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
where

E\ast(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g)(x):=\int_{\Gamma}E(x-y)g(y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y),\quad x\in\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}

represents the single layer potential for
g
.
Since the boundary
\Gamma=\partial\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
is curved and not compact, we appeal a perturbation argument.
For
g\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
, we decompose
g
into the curved part
g_{1}
and the straight part
g_{2}
by setting
g_{1}\big(y^{\prime},h(y^{\prime})\big):=1^{\prime}_{B_{2R_{h}}(0^{\prime})}(y^{\prime})g\big(y^{\prime},h(y^{\prime})\big)
for
y^{\prime}\in\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
and
g_{2}:=g-g_{1}
, where
1^{\prime}_{B_{2R_{h}}(0^{\prime})}
represents the characteristic function for the open ball
B_{2R_{h}}(0^{\prime})
in
\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
. Since
g_{2}
vanishes in the curved part of
\Gamma
, we can define
g_{2}^{H}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
by setting
g_{2}^{H}(y^{\prime},0)=g_{2}\big(y^{\prime},h(y^{\prime})\big)
for any
y^{\prime}\in\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
. Note that

E\ast\big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g_{2}\big)(x)=E\ast\big(\delta_{\partial\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}}\otimes g_{2}^{H}\big)(x)

for any
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
By setting
g_{2}^{\mathbf{R}^{n}}(y^{\prime},y_{n}):=g_{2}^{H}(y^{\prime},0)
for any
(y^{\prime},y_{n})\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
, we can deduce the
BMO
estimate of
\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g_{2}\big)
by applying the
L^{\infty}-BMO
estimate for singular integral operator
[
19
, Theorem 4.2.7]
to
\nabla\partial_{x_{n}}E\ast 1_{\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}}g_{2}^{\mathbf{R}^{n}}
.
Since
g_{1}\big(\cdot^{\prime},h(\cdot^{\prime})\big)
is compactly supported in
\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
, we may extend
g_{1}
to some
g_{1,\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{e}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
such that
g_{1,\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{e}}
is compactly supported and
\nabla d\cdot\nabla g_{1,\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{e}}=0
within a small neighborhood of
\Gamma
. Then, we can rewrite
\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g_{1}\big)
as

\displaystyle\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g_{1}\big)=\nabla\operatorname{div}\big(E*(g_{1,\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{e}}1_{\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}}\nabla d)\big)-\nabla E*\big(1_{\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}}g_{1}^{\mathrm{e}}f_{\theta,\rho_{0}/4}\big)

(3)

with some compactly supported continuous function
f_{\theta,\rho_{0}/4}
(see Proof of Lemma
25
(i)) that is independent of
g
.
The
BMO
estimate for the first term on the right hand side of (
3
) can be controlled using the
L^{\infty}-BMO
estimate for singular integration operator. The second term on the right hand side of (
3
) can be controlled by the
L^{\infty}
norm as
\nabla E(x)
is a locally integrable kernel and
f_{\theta,\rho_{0}/4}
has compact support. We thus obtain the
BMO
estimate for
\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g\big)
for
g\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
.

For the
b^{\nu}
estimate of the normal component of
\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g\big)
with
g\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
, we also decompose
g
into the curved part
g_{1}
and the straight part
g_{2}
.
It is possible to show that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\Gamma^{\mathbf{R}^{n}}_{\rho_{0}}}\int_{\Gamma}\left\lvert\frac{\partial E}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{y}}(x-y)\right\rvert\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)<\infty

(4)

where
\Gamma^{\mathbf{R}^{n}}_{\rho_{0}}:=\left\{x\in\Omega\,\middle|\,d(x)<\rho_{0}\right\}
denotes the
\rho_{0}
-neighborhood of
\Gamma
in
\Omega
.
Since
g_{1}\big(\cdot^{\prime},h(\cdot^{\prime})\big)
is compactly supported in
\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
, estimate (
4
) allows us to show that

\left\lvert\nabla d(x)\cdot\nabla\big(E*(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g_{1})\big)(x)\right\rvert\leq C\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}

for any
x\in\Gamma^{\mathbf{R}^{n}}_{\rho_{0}}
with some constant
C=C(K,R_{h},R_{\ast})>0
.
On the other hand, for
x
close to the curved part of
\Gamma
,
\nabla d(x)
is not necessarily
(0,...,0,1)
, in this case

\left\lvert\nabla d(x)\cdot\nabla\big(E\ast(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g_{2})\big)(x)\right\rvert

would contain contributions from
\nabla^{\prime}\big(E\ast(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g_{2})\big)
, which cannot be estimated by the
L^{\infty}
bound of
g_{2}
(see Proposition
26
). As a result, we introduce the
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
bound of
g_{2}
.
Since
g_{2}\big(\cdot^{\prime},h(\cdot^{\prime})\big)
is supported in
B_{2R_{h}}(0^{\prime})^{\mathrm{c}}
,
\nabla d(x)\cdot\nabla E(x-\cdot^{\prime})
can be viewed as an element of
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
for any
x
close to the curved part of
\Gamma
. Hence, by the
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}-\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}
duality we can deduce that

\left\lvert\nabla d(x)\cdot\nabla\big(E\ast(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g_{2})\big)(x)\right\rvert\leq C\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}

with some constant
C=C(K,R_{h},R_{\ast})>0
.
We thus obtain an
L^{\infty}
estimate for the normal component of
\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g\big)
within a small neighborhood of
\Gamma
. The
b^{\nu}
estimate naturally follows.

For
g\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
, we prove that the trace of

\big(Qg\big)(x)=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial E}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{x}}(x-y)g(y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y),\quad x\in\Gamma^{\mathbf{R}^{n}}_{\rho_{0}}

is of the form

\gamma\big(Qg\big)\big(x^{\prime},h(x^{\prime})\big)=\frac{1}{2}g\big(x^{\prime},h(x^{\prime})\big)-\big(Sg\big)\big(x^{\prime},h(x^{\prime})\big),

where
S:L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\to L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
is a bounded linear operator satisfying

\displaystyle\|S\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\to L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\leq C^{\ast}(n)C_{s}(h)^{\frac{3n}{2}+8}C_{1}(h)\big(C_{\ast,1}(h)+C_{\ast,2}(h)+R_{h}^{\frac{n}{2}}\big)

with
C^{\ast}(n)
denoting a specific fixed constant which depends on dimension
n
only.
Therefore, if
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
is a perturbed
C^{2}
half space that has small perturbation with
n\geq 3
, the inverse of
I-2S
is well-defined as a bounded linear map from
L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
to
L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
by the Neumann series

(I-2S)^{-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(2S)^{i}.

The solution to the Neumann problem (
2
) is formally given by

u(x)=E\ast\Big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes\big(2(I-2S)^{-1}g\big)\Big)(x),\quad x\in\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}.

We finally need the
L^{2}
estimate for
\nabla u
in
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
.
In the case of a half space, for
g\in\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
, the single layer potential
E\ast\big(\delta_{\partial\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}}\otimes g\big)
is exactly half of the solution
u
to the Neumann problem. By directly considering the partial Fourier transform of
E(x^{\prime},x_{n})
with respect to
x^{\prime}
, we could easily deduce that

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n})}=2\big\|\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\partial\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}}\otimes g\big)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n})}=C(n)\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}.

In the case that
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
is a perturbed
C^{2}
half space with
n\geq 3
, for
g\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
, we still decompose
g
into the curved part
g_{1}
and the straight part
g_{2}
.
Since
L^{\frac{2n-2}{n}}(\Gamma)
is continuously embedded in
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
and the curved part
g_{1}
has compact support in
\Gamma
,
g\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
would imply that both
g_{1},g_{2}\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
.
Since for any
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
we have that

\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g_{2}\big)(x)=\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\partial\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}}\otimes g_{2}^{H}\big)(x)

and for any
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}
we have that

\left\lvert\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\partial\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}}\otimes g_{2}^{H}\big)(x^{\prime},-x_{n})\right\rvert=\left\lvert\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\partial\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}}\otimes g_{2}^{H}\big)(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right\rvert,

the
L^{2}
estimate of
\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g_{2}\big)
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
follows from the
L^{2}
estimate of
\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\partial\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}}\otimes g_{2}^{H}\big)
in
\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}
, i.e., we have that

\displaystyle\big\|\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g_{2}\big)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=2\big\|\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\partial\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}}\otimes g_{2}^{H}\big)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n})}\leq C(K,R_{h})\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}.

For the curved part
g_{1}
, we recall the argument which establishes the
vBMO^{\infty,\nu}
norm for
\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g\big)
. We extend
g_{1}
to
g_{1,\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{e}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
and consider equation (
3
).
Since
\nabla\operatorname{div}E
is
L^{p}
integrable for any
1<p<\infty
, see e.g.
[
18
, Theorem 5.2.7 and Theorem 5.2.10]
, the
L^{2}
norm of the first term on the right hand side of (
3
) can be estimated by the
L^{\infty}
norm of
g
. Whereas
\nabla E(x)
is an integration kernel that is dominated by a constant multiple of
\lvert x\rvert^{-(n-1)}
, by the famous Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality
[
1
, Theorem 1.7]
, we can also control the
L^{2}
norm of the second term on the right hand side of (
3
) by the
L^{\infty}
norm of
g
.
Combine with the
L^{2}
estimate for the contribution from the straight part
g_{2}
, we finally obtain our desired
L^{2}
estimate

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)}\leq C(K,R_{h},R_{\ast})\|(I-2S)^{-1}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\leq C(K,R_{h},R_{\ast})\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}

for

. This completes the proof of Lemma
4
.

When taking the normal trace and solving the Neumann problem, the reason why we need to require the dimension

to be greater than or equal to

is because when

, we indeed have the fact that
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
is continuously embedded in

and
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})

\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
, which further implies that

is continuously embedded in
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
. Based on these facts, we can show that in the case of any perturbed
C^{2}
half space
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
with boundary
\Gamma=\partial\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
,
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
is isomorphic to
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
and
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
is isomorphic to
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
. More importantly, we can estimate the

by its

to decompose it into the curved part

and the straight part

. In the case where

is no longer complete and

.
The completion of
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R})
cannot be embedded in the space of Schwartz’s tempered distributions.
Moreover, as a limit case,
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R})
is not continuously embedded in

.
As a result, at the present we lack of tools to estimate the

in this paper.
The problem of
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R})
is similar to
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{2})
. The space
\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{2})
is not complete. Its completion should be the quotient space

as discussed in
[
11
]
since

includes all smooth compactly supported functions. We have to study an appropriate dual space as in
[
16
]
.

Let us recall some uniform estimates established in

Let

Let

denotes the boundary of domain

There exists

\Omega

(5)

so that

for

.
For a uniformly
C^{k}
domain
\Omega
, there is
R^{\Omega}>0
such that for

with

\pi x\in\Gamma
such that

.

is called the reach of
\Gamma
in
\Omega
, we denote this supremum by

.
Let
\Omega^{\mathrm{c}}
be the complement of
\Omega
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
. Similarly, there is
R^{\Omega^{\mathrm{c}}}>0
such that for
x\in\Omega^{\mathrm{c}}
with
\left\lvert d(x)\right\rvert<R^{\Omega^{\mathrm{c}}}
, we can also find a unique point
\pi x\in\Gamma
such that
\lvert x-\pi x\rvert=\left\lvert d(x)\right\rvert
.
The supremum of such
R^{\Omega^{\mathrm{c}}}
is called the reach of
\Gamma
in
\Omega^{\mathrm{c}}
, we denote this supremum by
R_{\ast}^{\Omega^{\mathrm{c}}}
. We then define

R_{*}:=\min\left(R^{\Omega}_{*},R^{\Omega^{\mathrm{c}}}_{*}\right),

which we call it the reach of
\Gamma
.
Moreover,
d
is
C^{k}
in the
\rho
-neighborhood of
\Gamma
for any
\rho\in(0,R_{\ast})
, i.e.,
d\in C^{k}\left(\Gamma^{\mathbf{R}^{n}}_{\rho}\right)
for any
\rho\in(0,R_{\ast})
with

\Gamma^{\mathbf{R}^{n}}_{\rho}:=\left\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\,\middle|\,\left\lvert d(x)\right\rvert<\rho\right\};

see e.g.
[
20
, Chap. 14, Appendix]
,
[

There exists

such that for every
z_{0}\in\Gamma
,

U_{\rho}(z_{0}):=\left\{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\,\middle|\,(\pi x)^{\prime}\in\operatorname{int}B_{\rho}(0^{\prime}),\ \left\lvert d(x)\right\rvert<\rho\right\}

h_{z_{0}}(\eta^{\prime})+\eta_{n}(\partial_{x_{n}}d)\big(\eta^{\prime},h_{z_{0}}(\eta^{\prime})\big),\end{array}\right.

&\leq C(n)\bigg\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}^{\mathrm{tan}}\bigg\|_{BMOL^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\end{split}

(14)

holds for any
1<s<\infty
.
Since
\nabla E
is in
L^{p^{\prime}}\big(B_{6\rho}(0)\big)
for any
1<p^{\prime}<n/(n-1)
, it follows that

\sup_{x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}}\left\lvert\nabla E*\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}^{\mathrm{tan}}\bigg)(x)\right\rvert\leq C(\rho)\bigg\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}^{\mathrm{tan}}\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}

for all
p>n
.
Thus, we have that

\bigg\|\nabla E*\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}^{\mathrm{tan}}\bigg)\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\leq C(\alpha,\beta,K,\rho)\|v\|_{vBMOL^{2}(\Omega)}.

Since the convolution kernel
\nabla E(x)
is dominated by a constant multiple of
\lvert x\rvert^{-(n-1)}
, by the well-known Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 1.7]
, we deduce that

\bigg\|\nabla E\ast\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}^{\mathrm{tan}}\bigg)\bigg\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\leq C(n)\bigg\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}^{\mathrm{tan}}\bigg\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}

Hence by estimate (
14
), we see that

\bigg\|\nabla E\ast\bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}^{\mathrm{tan}}\bigg)\bigg\|_{BMOL^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\leq C(\alpha,\beta,K,\rho)\|v\|_{vBMOL^{2}(\Omega)}.

Combine with estimate (
13
), we finally obtain that

\|\nabla q_{1}^{\mathrm{tan}}\|_{BMOL^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\leq C(\alpha,\beta,K,\rho)\|v\|_{vBMOL^{2}(\Omega)}.

By Proposition
8
, the control on the boundary with respect to
q_{1}^{\mathrm{tan}}
is estimated by

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\Gamma,\,r<\rho}\,r^{-n}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|\nabla d\cdot\nabla q_{1}^{\mathrm{tan}}|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq C(K,\rho)N_{\ast}\sup_{i\in\mathbf{N}}\|v_{2,i}\|_{vBMOL^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(\alpha,\beta,K,\rho)\|v\|_{vBMOL^{2}(\Omega)}

as the partition
\mathcal{Q}_{\Gamma}
is a locally finite open cover of
\Gamma^{\mathbf{R}^{n}}_{\rho}
.

For the contribution coming from the normal component, we set in a similar way that

q^{\mathrm{nor}}_{1,i}:=\theta_{i}p_{i}^{\mathrm{nor}}+E*(p_{i}^{\mathrm{nor}}\Delta\theta_{i}+2\nabla\theta_{i}\cdot\nabla p_{i}^{\mathrm{nor}})

and

q_{1}^{\mathrm{nor}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}q_{1,i}^{\mathrm{nor}}.

By making use of Proposition
9
and repeating the whole argument above that treats the case for
q_{1}^{\mathrm{tan}}
, we can prove that

\|\nabla q_{1}^{\mathrm{nor}}\|_{BMOL^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}+[\nabla d\cdot\nabla q_{1}^{\mathrm{nor}}]_{b^{\rho}(\Gamma)}\leq C(\alpha,\beta,K,\rho)\|v\|_{vBMOL^{2}(\Omega)}

in the same way.
Then the volume potential
q_{1}
corresponding to
v
can be constructed as

q_{1}:=q_{1}^{1}+q_{1}^{\mathrm{tan}}+q_{1}^{\mathrm{nor}}

where
q_{1}^{1}
is the volume potential defined in Proposition
7
corresponding to
v_{1}
.
By our construction, it can be easily seen that

\displaystyle-\Delta q_{1}

\displaystyle=-\Delta q^{1}_{1}-\Delta q^{\mathrm{tan}}_{1}-\Delta q^{\mathrm{nor}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{div}v_{1}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\operatorname{div}w_{i}^{\mathrm{tan}}+\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}\operatorname{div}w_{i}^{\mathrm{nor}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{div}(v_{1}+v_{2})=\operatorname{div}v

in
\Omega
. We are done.

Normal trace for a
L^{2}
vector field with bounded mean oscillation

Let
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
be a perturbed
C^{2+\kappa}
half space of type
(K)
with
\kappa\in(0,1)
and
n\geq 3
. Let
R_{\ast}>0
denote the reach of boundary
\Gamma=\partial\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
.
We have already shown that there exists a constant
C=C(\alpha,\beta,K,R_{\ast})>0
such that the estimate

\|w\cdot\mathbf{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq C\|w\|_{vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)}

holds for any
w\in vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)
, see
[
13
, Theorem 22]
. In this section, we would like to further estimate the
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
norm of the normal trace of
v
on
\Gamma
for
w\in vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)
.

For simplicity of notations, from now on we define that for any
\delta>0
,

B^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\delta):=\left\{x\in\Gamma\,\middle|\,\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert<\delta\right\},\quad B^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\delta)^{\mathrm{c}}:=\left\{x\in\Gamma\,\middle|\,\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert\geq\delta\right\}

and
\Lambda_{\delta}
to be the surface area of
B^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\delta)
, i.e.,

\Lambda_{\delta}:=\int_{B^{\prime}_{\Gamma}(\delta)}1\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)=\int_{\lvert y^{\prime}\rvert<\delta}\big(1+\left\lvert\nabla^{\prime}h(y^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dy^{\prime}.

Moreover, for
h\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
, we define the constant
C_{s}(h):=1+\|h\|_{C^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}
.

3.1
Isomorphism between
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
and
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)

We would like to firstly give a characterization to the homogeneous fractional Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
before we define
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
.
Let us recall that if
f\in\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
, then
f\in L_{loc}^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
and the Gagliardo seminorm

[f]_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}\frac{\left\lvert f(x^{\prime})-f(y^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert^{n}}\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}<\infty.

More precisely, if
f\in\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
, then it holds that

\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}=C(n)[f]_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}

with some constant
C(n)
that depends only on dimension
n
; see e.g.
[
1
, Proposition 1.37]
.
However, the finiteness of the Gagliardo seminorm
[f]_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}
does not imply that
f\in\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
. Constant functions in
\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
are typical counterexamples.
Since we are considering the case where
n\geq 3
, we have a very important property that
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
can be continuously embedded in
L^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
, i.e., there exists a constant
C(n)>0
such that the estimate

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}\leq C(n)\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}

(15)

holds for any
f\in\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
, see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 1.38]
.
As a result, we expect that
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
can be identified with the space

\dot{G}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1}):=\Big\{f\in L^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})\;\Big|\;[f]_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}<\infty\Big\}.

Fortunately, this expectation is indeed true. The Gagliardo seminorm
[\cdot]_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}
turns out to be a norm on
\dot{G}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
. The space
\dot{G}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
is complete with norm
[\cdot]_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}
and it contains
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
as a dense subspace; see
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
.
Since
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})\subset\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
, we see that
\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}
and
[f]_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}
are equivalent for any
f\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
.
Hence, the space
\dot{G}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
coincide with the completion of
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
in norm
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}
, i.e., it holds that

\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})=\dot{G}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1}).

The reason why the completion of
C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
in norm
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}
is indeed
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
is as follows.
For
\rho>0
, we can construct a cut-off function
\theta_{2\rho}\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
such that
0\leq\theta_{2\rho}\leq 1
in
\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
,
\theta_{2\rho}=1
in
B_{\rho}(0^{\prime})
and
\theta_{2\rho}=0
in
B_{2\rho}(0^{\prime})^{\mathrm{c}}
.
We note that for any
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
where
\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
denotes the Schwartz space,
\theta_{2\rho}f
converges to
f
in norm
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}
as
\rho\to\infty
(This claim will be established within the proof of Proposition
17
which appears later in subsection
3.3
). Therefore, by recalling that the space
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
is dense in
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
where
\mathcal{S}_{0}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
denotes the subspace of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
whose Fourier transform vanishes near the origin, see e.g.
[
1
, Proposition 1.35]
, we can deduce that

\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})=\overline{C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{\|\cdot\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}}.

With respect to a function
f
defined on
\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
, we could define a corresponding function
f^{h}
that is defined on
\Gamma
by setting

f^{h}\big(y^{\prime},h(y^{\prime})\big):=f(y^{\prime}),\quad\text{for all}\quad y^{\prime}\in\mathbf{R}^{n-1}.

Let the mapping
f\mapsto f^{h}
be denoted by
T_{h}
, i.e.,
f^{h}=T_{h}(f)
.
Since it is not that natural to consider the Fourier transform on a surface, we follow the characterization of
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
above to define the homogeneous fractional Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
. We say that
f^{h}\in\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
if
f^{h}\in L^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}(\Gamma)
satisfies

[f^{h}]_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}^{2}:=\int_{\Gamma}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\left\lvert f^{h}(x)-f^{h}(y)\right\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{n}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)<\infty.

The space
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
is a Banach space (actually Hilbert space) equipped with the norm
[\cdot]_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}
. Without causing any ambiguity, we simply use the norm notation
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}
to denote
[\cdot]_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}
.
Finally, we would like to note that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}\lvert f(y^{\prime})\rvert^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}\big(1+\left\lvert\nabla^{\prime}h(y^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dy^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2n-2}}

\displaystyle=\|f^{h}\|_{L^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}(\Gamma)}\leq C_{s}(h)^{\frac{n-2}{2n-2}}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})},

i.e.,
f\in L^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
if and only if
f^{h}\in L^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}(\Gamma)
.

Lemma 11
.

The mapping
T_{h}:\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})\to\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
f\in\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
. We naturally have that

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\left\lvert f^{h}(x)-f^{h}(y)\right\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{n}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)

\displaystyle\ \ =\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}\frac{\left\lvert f^{h}\big(x^{\prime},h(x^{\prime})\big)-f^{h}\big(y^{\prime},h(y^{\prime})\big)\right\rvert^{2}}{\left\lvert\big(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},h(x^{\prime})-h(y^{\prime})\big)\right\rvert^{n}}\big(1+\left\lvert\nabla^{\prime}h(x^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(1+\left\lvert\nabla^{\prime}h(y^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}

\displaystyle\ \ \leq C_{s}(h)^{2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}\frac{\left\lvert f(x^{\prime})-f(y^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert^{n}}\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}.

Hence, the
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
estimate for
f^{h}
reads as

\displaystyle\|f^{h}\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\leq C_{s}(h)\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}.

(16)

Let
f^{h}\in\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
. With respect to
f^{h}
, we define that
f(y^{\prime}):=f^{h}\big(y^{\prime},h(y^{\prime})\big)
for any
y^{\prime}\in\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
. By the mean value theorem, we see that the estimate
\left\lvert h(x^{\prime})-h(y^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq\|\nabla^{\prime}h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert
holds for any
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
.
As a result, for any
x,y\in\Gamma
we have that

\frac{1}{\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert}\leq\frac{C_{s}(h)}{\big\lvert\big(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},h(x^{\prime})-h(y^{\prime})\big)\big\rvert}=\frac{C_{s}(h)}{\lvert x-y\rvert}.

Hence, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}\frac{\big\lvert f(x^{\prime})-f(y^{\prime})\big\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert^{n}}\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C_{s}(h)^{n}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}\frac{\big\lvert f^{h}\big(x^{\prime},h(x^{\prime})\big)-f^{h}\big(y^{\prime},h(y^{\prime})\big)\big\rvert^{2}}{\big\lvert\big(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime},h(x^{\prime})-h(y^{\prime})\big)\big\rvert^{n}}\big(1+\big\lvert\nabla^{\prime}h(x^{\prime})\big\rvert^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\big(1+\big\lvert\nabla^{\prime}h(y^{\prime})\big\rvert^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}

\displaystyle=C_{s}(h)^{n}\int_{\Gamma}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\left\lvert f^{h}(x)-f^{h}(y)\right\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x-y\rvert^{n}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(x)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y).

Therefore, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}\leq C(n)C_{s}(h)^{\frac{n}{2}}\|f^{h}\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}.

(17)

\Box

As an application of Lemma
11
, we have the following embedding result.

Corollary 12
.

\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
is continuously embedded in
L^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

Let
f^{h}\in\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
and
f=T_{h}^{-1}(f^{h})
. Since
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
is continuously embedded in
L^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
, by estimate (
17
) we see that

\displaystyle\|f^{h}\|_{L^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{s}(h)^{\frac{n-2}{2n-2}}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}\leq C(n)C_{s}(h)^{\frac{n-2}{2n-2}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)C_{s}(h)^{\frac{n^{2}-2}{2n-2}}\|f^{h}\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}.

\Box

3.2
Isomorphism between
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
and
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)

Let us further recall that a tempered distribution
g
is said to belong to the homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
if
\widehat{g}\in L_{loc}^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
satisfies

\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}\lvert\xi^{\prime}\rvert^{-1}\big|\widehat{g}(\xi)\big|^{2}\,d\xi^{\prime}<\infty.

Since
\frac{1}{2}<\frac{n-1}{2}
for all
n\geq 3
,
\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
is characterized as the dual space of
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
, i.e., it holds that

\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}=\sup_{\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}\leq 1}\,\left\lvert\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}g(y^{\prime})f(y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}\right\rvert,

see e.g.
[
1
, Proposition 1.36]
.
In order to establish an isomorphism between the dual spaces of
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
and
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
, we need the following multiplication rule for
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
.

Proposition 13
.

Let
\rho>0
and
\zeta\in C^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
satisfies that
\zeta
is identically a constant in
B_{\rho}(0^{\prime})^{\mathrm{c}}
. Then for any
f\in\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
, we have that
\zeta f\in\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
satisfying

\|\zeta f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}\leq C(n)\big(\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}+\rho\|\nabla^{\prime}\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}\big)\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}.

Proof.

Let
f\in\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
.
For
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
, by the triangle inequality we see that

\left\lvert\zeta(x^{\prime})f(x^{\prime})-\zeta(y^{\prime})f(y^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}\leq 2\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\left\lvert f(x^{\prime})-f(y^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}+2\left\lvert f(x^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\zeta(x^{\prime})-\zeta(y^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}.

Since
\zeta
is identically a constant in
B_{\rho}(0^{\prime})^{\mathrm{c}}
, it is sufficient to estimate

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}(0^{\prime})}\int_{B_{2\rho}(0^{\prime})^{\mathrm{c}}}\left\lvert f(x^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}\frac{\left\lvert\zeta(x^{\prime})-\zeta(y^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert^{n}}\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}+\int_{B_{2\rho}(0^{\prime})^{\mathrm{c}}}\int_{B_{\rho}(0^{\prime})}\left\lvert f(x^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}\frac{\left\lvert\zeta(x^{\prime})-\zeta(y^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert^{n}}\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}

\displaystyle\ \ +\int_{B_{2\rho}(0^{\prime})}\int_{B_{2\rho}(0^{\prime})}\left\lvert f(x^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}\frac{\left\lvert\zeta(x^{\prime})-\zeta(y^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert^{n}}\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}.

For
\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert\geq 2\rho
and
\lvert y^{\prime}\rvert<\rho
, we have that
\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert\geq\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert-\rho
. Hence, by H
\ddot{\text{o}}
lder’s inequality and the embedding of
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
in
L^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
, see estimate (
15
), we deduce that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\rho^{n-1}\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\left(\int_{B_{2\rho}(0^{\prime})^{\mathrm{c}}}\left\lvert f(x^{\prime})\right\rvert^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}\left(\int_{B_{2\rho}(0^{\prime})^{\mathrm{c}}}\frac{1}{(\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert-\rho)^{n^{2}-n}}\,dx^{\prime}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\rho^{n-1}\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{i}}{r^{n^{2}-2n+2+i}}\,dr\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}.

Similarly, for
\lvert y^{\prime}\rvert\geq 2\rho
and
\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert<\rho
, we also have that
\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert\geq\lvert y^{\prime}\rvert-\rho
.
By H
\ddot{\text{o}}
lder’s inequality and estimate (
15
) again, we see that

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\rho\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\int_{B_{2\rho}(0^{\prime})^{\mathrm{c}}}\frac{1}{(\lvert y^{\prime}\rvert-\rho)^{n}}\,dy^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)\rho\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2n-2}{n-2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-2}\int_{\rho}^{\infty}\frac{\rho^{n-2-i}}{r^{n-i}}\,dr\right)\leq C(n)\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}.

By the mean value theorem, it holds that
\left\lvert\zeta(x^{\prime})-\zeta(y^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq\|\nabla^{\prime}\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert
.
Therefore, by H
\ddot{\text{o}}
lder’s inequality and estimate (
15
) once more, we finally have that

\displaystyle I_{3}

u_{f,2\rho}\in H^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
satisfies

\|u_{f,2\rho}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\leq C(n)\rho\|u_{f,2\rho}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n})},

see e.g.
[
1
, Proposition 1.55]
. Let
B_{2\rho}^{+}:=B_{2\rho}(0)\cap\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}
. Since
u_{f,2\rho}\in H^{1}(B_{2\rho}^{+})
, by the Gauss-Green formula, see e.g.
[
27
, Lemma 1.2.3]
, it holds that

\displaystyle\left\lvert\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}\theta_{2\rho}(x^{\prime},0)f(x^{\prime})w_{n}(x^{\prime},0)\,dx^{\prime}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\int_{B_{2\rho}^{+}}\left\lvert\nabla u_{f,2\rho}\cdot w\right\rvert\,dx\leq\|\nabla u_{f,2\rho}\|_{L^{2}\big(B_{2\rho}^{+}\big)}\|w\|_{L^{2}\big(B_{2\rho}^{+}\big)}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{f,2\rho}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\|w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n})}\leq C(n)\|u_{f}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\|w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n})}

for any
\rho>0
and
w\in L^{2}(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n})^{n}
with
\operatorname{div}w=0
in
\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}
. Since we have already shown above that
\theta_{2\rho}(\cdot^{\prime},0)f
converges to
f
in
\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}
norm as
\rho\to\infty
, by Proposition
16
we conclude that for any
f\in\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
and
w\in L^{2}(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n})
with
\operatorname{div}w=0
in
\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}
,

\left\lvert\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}f(x^{\prime})w_{n}(x^{\prime},0)\,dx^{\prime}\right\rvert\leq C(n)\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}\|w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n})},

i.e.,

\|w\cdot\mathbf{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}\leq C(n)\|w\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n})}.

\Box

Remark 18
.

Instead, if the domain
\Omega\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}

for any
w\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{n}
with
\operatorname{div}w=0
in
\Omega
; see e.g.
[
26
]
,
[
28
]
.

Now we are ready to consider the perturbed half space problem. For
w\in vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)
with
\operatorname{div}w=0
in

Proof of Lemma
3
.

Let
w\in vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)
with
\operatorname{div}w=0
in
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
.
Let
\varphi_{\ast}\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n})

in

w_{2}:=w-w_{1}

By Proposition

, we see that
w_{1},w_{2}\in vBMOL^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)

with some constant

.
For

.

As a result, we deduce that

B_{\rho+\tau_{1}(h)}(0)\cap\Gamma=B^{\prime}_{\Gamma}\big(\rho+\tau_{1}(h)\big)

\displaystyle w_{2,H}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}w_{2}(x)\quad\text{if}\quad x\in\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}\cap\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n},\\

4
Estimates for some boundary integrals

Let
E
denotes the fundamental solution of
-\Delta
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, i.e.,

E(x):=\begin{cases}-\log\lvert x\rvert/2\pi&\quad(n=2),\\
\lvert x\rvert^{2-n}/\big(n(n-2)b_{1}(n)\big)&\quad(n\geq 3),\end{cases}

where
b_{1}(n)
denotes the volume of the unit ball
B_{1}(0)
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
In this section, we assume that
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
is a perturbed
C^{2}
half space of type
(K)
with boundary
\Gamma=\partial\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
.
The purpose of this section is to establish several estimates for the trace operator of

\big(Qg\big)(x):=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial E}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{x}}(x-y)g(y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},\quad x\in\Gamma^{\Omega}_{\rho_{0}}

for
g\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
where
\partial/\partial\mathbf{n}_{x}
denotes the exterior normal derivative with respect to
x
-variable.

Let us recall that for a perturbed
C^{2}
half space
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
with
h\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
that is not identically zero,
R_{h}>0
represents the smallest positive real number such that
\operatorname{supp}h\subseteq\overline{B_{R_{h}}(0^{\prime})}
.

4.1
Estimate for the normal derivative in
y
of
E

To be specific, we give an estimate for the boundary integral of
\frac{\partial E}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{y}}(x-\cdot)
for
x\in\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
that is close to the boundary
\Gamma
. We have a compatible result with
[
14
, Lemma 6]
, which deals with the case where the domain is bounded.
For
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0}]
, we define that
\Gamma^{\Omega}_{\rho}:=\Gamma^{\mathbf{R}^{n}}_{\rho}\cap\Omega
.

Lemma 19
.

Let
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
be a perturbed
C^{2}
half space of type
(K)
with boundary
\Gamma=\partial\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
and
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0}]
.
Then, it holds that

(i)

\int_{\Gamma}\frac{\partial E}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{y}}(x-y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)=-\frac{1}{2}\quad\text{for any}\quad x\in\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n},

(ii)

\sup_{x\in\Gamma^{\Omega}_{\rho}}\,\int_{\Gamma}\left\lvert\frac{\partial E}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{y}}(x-y)\right\rvert\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)<C(n)C_{\ref{EP}}(K,h,\rho)

where

C_{\ref{EP}}(K,h,\rho):=\big(R_{h}^{n-1}+\rho K+\rho+1\big)\|h\|_{C^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}+\|\nabla^{\prime 2}h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}+\rho+1.

Proof.

(i)

This follows from the Gauss divergence theorem. For a bounded piecewise
C^{1}
domain
D\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
, we have that

\int_{\partial D}\frac{\partial E}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{y}}(x-y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)=\int_{D}\Delta_{y}E(x-y)\,dy

for any
x\in D
.
Since
\Delta_{y}E(x-y)=-\delta(x-y)
, we obtain that

\int_{\partial D}\frac{\partial E}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{y}}(x-y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)=-1

for
x\in D
. Let
x\in\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
.
For
R>0
, we define the domain
D_{R}
by

D_{R}:=\left\{(y^{\prime},y_{n})\,\middle|\,h(y^{\prime})<y_{n}<\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}+2\lvert x_{n}\rvert,\,\lvert y^{\prime}\rvert<R\right\}.

We consider
R>R_{h}+\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert
. By applying the Gauss divergence theorem in
D_{R}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle-1

\displaystyle=\int_{y_{n}=\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}+2\lvert x_{n}\rvert,\,\lvert y^{\prime}\rvert<R}\frac{\partial E}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{y}}(x-y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)+\int_{y\in\Gamma,\,\lvert y^{\prime}\rvert<R}\frac{\partial E}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{y}}(x-y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)

\displaystyle\ \ +\int_{0<y_{n}<\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}+2\lvert x_{n}\rvert,\,\lvert y^{\prime}\rvert=R}\frac{\partial E}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{y}}(x-y)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y).

The last term tends to zero naturally as
R\to\infty
. For the first term, since
\mathbf{n}_{y}
is pointing straightly upward but
x
is located below
\left\{(y^{\prime},y_{n})\,\middle|\,y_{n}=\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}+2\lvert x_{n}\rvert\right\}
, the kernel
\big(\partial E/\partial\mathbf{n}_{y}\big)(x-y)
in this case is exactly the half of the Poisson kernel
P_{\delta_{h,x_{n}}}(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})
with
\delta_{h,x_{n}}:=\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}+2\lvert x_{n}\rvert-x_{n}
. Hence, the first integral on the right hand side tends to
-\frac{1}{2}
as
R\to\infty
. We therefore obtain (i).

(ii)

Let us observe that

-\mathbf{n}_{y}=-\mathbf{n}\big(y^{\prime},h(y^{\prime})\big)=\big(-\nabla^{\prime}h(y^{\prime}),1\big)/\omega(y^{\prime})

where
\omega(y^{\prime})=\big(1+\left\lvert\nabla^{\prime}h(y^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}\big)^{1/2}
and
\nabla^{\prime}
is the gradient in
y^{\prime}
variables.
This implies that

-C(n)\frac{\partial E}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{y}}(x-y)=\frac{\sigma(y^{\prime})}{\omega(y^{\prime})\left(\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert^{2}+\big(x_{n}-h(y^{\prime})\big)^{2}\right)^{n/2}}

for
y\in\Gamma
with

\sigma(y^{\prime}):=-\nabla^{\prime}h(y^{\prime})\cdot(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})+\big(x_{n}-h(y^{\prime})\big)\;\;\text{where}\;\;x_{n}>h(x^{\prime}),\ x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\mathbf{R}^{n-1}.

We set that

K(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},x_{n}):=\frac{\sigma(y^{\prime})}{\left(\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert^{2}+\big(x_{n}-h(y^{\prime})\big)^{2}\right)^{n/2}}.

By the Taylor expansion, for
\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert<1
we have that

h(x^{\prime})=h(y^{\prime})+\nabla^{\prime}h(y^{\prime})\cdot(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})+r(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})

with

r(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime})^{\mathrm{T}}\cdot\int^{1}_{0}(1-\theta)\Big(\nabla^{\prime 2}h\Big)\big(\theta x^{\prime}+(1-\theta)y^{\prime}\big)\,d\theta\cdot(x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}).

We obtain that

\sigma(y^{\prime})=x_{n}-h(x^{\prime})+r(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})

with an estimate

\left\lvert r(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq\|\nabla^{\prime 2}h\|_{L^{\infty}\big(B_{1}(x^{\prime})\big)}\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert^{2}.

(27)

We decompose
K
into the sum of a leading term and a remainder term

K(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},x_{n})=K_{0}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},x_{n})+R(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},x_{n})

with

\displaystyle K_{0}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},x_{n})

\displaystyle:=\frac{x_{n}-h(x^{\prime})}{\left(\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert^{2}+\big(x_{n}-h(y^{\prime})\big)^{2}\right)^{n/2}},

\displaystyle R(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},x_{n})

\displaystyle:=\frac{r(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})}{\left(\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert^{2}+\big(x_{n}-h(y^{\prime})\big)^{2}\right)^{n/2}}.

The term
R
is estimated as

\left\lvert R(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},x_{n})\right\rvert\leq\|\nabla^{\prime 2}h\|_{L^{\infty}\big(B_{1}(x^{\prime})\big)}\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert^{2-n}

for
\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert<1
by estimate (
27
).
Hence,

\int_{\underset{\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert<1}{y\in\Gamma,}}\left\lvert\frac{R(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},x_{n})}{\omega(y^{\prime})}\right\rvert\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)\leq C(n)\|\nabla^{\prime 2}h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}.

Since

\left\lvert\sigma(y^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\nabla^{\prime}h(y^{\prime})\right\rvert\cdot\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert+\lvert x_{n}\rvert+\left\lvert h(y^{\prime})\right\rvert

for any
y^{\prime}\in\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
, we have that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{\underset{\lvert y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\rvert\geq 1}{y\in\Gamma,}}\left\lvert\frac{K(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},x_{n})}{\omega(y^{\prime})}\right\rvert\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)&\leq\int_{\lvert y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\rvert\geq 1}\left\lvert\nabla^{\prime}h(y^{\prime})\right\rvert\,dy^{\prime}+\int_{\lvert y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\rvert\geq 1}\left\lvert h(y^{\prime})\right\rvert\,dy^{\prime}\\
&\ \ +\int_{\lvert y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\rvert\geq 1}\frac{\lvert x_{n}\rvert}{\lvert y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\rvert^{n}}\,dy^{\prime}.\end{aligned}

(28)

Since
\operatorname{supp}h\subseteq\overline{B_{R_{h}}(0^{\prime})}
, the first two terms of estimate (
28
) can be estimated by the constant
C(n)R_{h}^{n-1}\|h\|_{C^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}
. On the other hand, since the estimate

\left\lvert x_{n}-h(x^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq(nK+1)\rho\|h\|_{C^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}+\rho

holds for any
x\in\Gamma^{\Omega}_{\rho}
, The third term of estimate (
28
) can be controlled by the constant
C(n)\big((\rho K+\rho+1)\|h\|_{C^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}+\rho\big)
.
By (i), we observe that

\displaystyle\frac{C(n)}{2}=\int_{\underset{\lvert y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\rvert\geq 1}{y\in\Gamma,}}\frac{K(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},x_{n})}{\omega(y^{\prime})}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)

\displaystyle\ \ +\int_{\underset{\lvert y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\rvert<1}{y\in\Gamma,}}\frac{K_{0}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},x_{n})}{\omega(y^{\prime})}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)+\int_{\underset{\lvert y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\rvert<1}{y\in\Gamma,}}\frac{R(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},x_{n})}{\omega(y^{\prime})}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y).

The term
K_{0}
is very singular but it is positive for
x\in\Gamma^{\Omega}_{\rho}
. Hence, we have that

\int_{\underset{\lvert y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}\rvert<1}{y\in\Gamma,}}\frac{K_{0}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},x_{n})}{\omega(y^{\prime})}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)\leq C(n)C_{\ref{EP}}(K,h,\rho)

where the constant
C_{\ref{EP}}(K,h,\rho)
has the explicit expression

C_{\ref{EP}}(K,h,\rho):=\big(R_{h}^{n-1}+\rho K+\rho+1\big)\|h\|_{C^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}+\|\nabla^{\prime 2}h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}+\rho+1.

Therefore, we finally obtain the estimate

\int_{\Gamma}\left\lvert\frac{\partial E}{\partial\mathbf{n}_{y}}(x-y)\right\rvert\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(y)\leq C(n)C_{\ref{EP}}(K,h,\rho)

which holds for any
x\in\Gamma^{\Omega}_{\rho}
.
This completes the proof of Lemma
19
.

\Box

Before we end this subsection, we would like to give an estimate on the difference between gradients of the signed distance function near the boundary with explicit dependency on the boundary function
h
. This estimate plays an important role in later estimations of various boundary integrals.

Proposition 20
.

Let
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
be a perturbed
C^{2}
half space with boundary
\Gamma=\partial\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
and
n\geq 2
. Then, for any
x\in\Gamma^{\mathbf{R}^{n}}_{\rho_{0}}
and
y\in\Gamma
, it holds that

\left\lvert\nabla d(x)-\nabla d(y)\right\rvert\leq C(n)C_{s}(h)^{2}\|\nabla^{\prime 2}h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}\lvert x-y\rvert.

Proof.

Let
y\in\Gamma
,
x\in\Gamma^{\mathbf{R}^{n}}_{\rho_{0}}
and
\pi x
be the unique projection of
x
on
\Gamma
.
Note that

\displaystyle\nabla d(x)-\nabla d(y)

\displaystyle=\nabla d(\pi x)-\nabla d(y)=\left(\frac{\nabla^{\prime}h(y^{\prime})}{\omega(y^{\prime})}-\frac{\nabla^{\prime}h(\pi x^{\prime})}{\omega(\pi x^{\prime})},\frac{1}{\omega(\pi x^{\prime})}-\frac{1}{\omega(y^{\prime})}\right)

where
\omega(\cdot^{\prime})=\big(1+\big\lvert\nabla^{\prime}h(\cdot^{\prime})\big\rvert^{2}\big)^{1/2}
and
\pi x^{\prime}
denotes the first
n-1
component of
\pi x
.
By rewriting

\displaystyle\nabla^{\prime}h(y^{\prime})\omega(\pi x^{\prime})-\nabla^{\prime}h(\pi x^{\prime})\omega(y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\big(\nabla^{\prime}h(y^{\prime})-\nabla^{\prime}h(\pi x^{\prime})\big)\omega(\pi x^{\prime})+\nabla^{\prime}h(\pi x^{\prime})\big(\omega(\pi x^{\prime})-\omega(y^{\prime})\big)

and applying the mean value theorem to both
\nabla^{\prime}h(y^{\prime})-\nabla^{\prime}h(\pi x^{\prime})
and
\omega(\pi x^{\prime})-\omega(y^{\prime})
, we can deduce that

\left\lvert\nabla d(\pi x)-\nabla d(y)\right\rvert\leq C(n)\|\nabla^{\prime 2}h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}\big(1+\|\nabla^{\prime}h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\big)\lvert\pi x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert.

Since we must have
\lvert x-y\rvert>\lvert x-\pi x\rvert
for any
y\in\Gamma
such that
y\neq\pi x
, by the triangle inequality
\lvert x-\pi x\rvert\geq\lvert y-\pi x\rvert-\lvert x-y\rvert
, we can deduce that the inequality
\lvert y-\pi x\rvert\leq 2\lvert x-y\rvert
holds for any
y\in\Gamma
. Since obviously
\lvert\pi x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert\leq\lvert\pi x-y\rvert
, we obtain Proposition
20
.

\Box

4.2
Criterion for a class of functions to be in
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})

Let
n\geq 2
. We say that
f\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
if
f\in L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
and

\|f\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}:=\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}+\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}\frac{\left\lvert f(x^{\prime})-f(y^{\prime})\right\rvert^{2}}{\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert^{n}}\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}<\infty.

Our criterion is similar and compatible with
[
15
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Proposition 21
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
\rho>0
. Suppose that
f\in C^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
satisfies

\operatorname{supp}f\subseteq B_{2\rho}(0^{\prime})^{\mathrm{c}},\quad\left\lvert f(x^{\prime})\right\rvert\cdot\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert^{n-1}\leq c_{1},\quad\left\lvert\nabla^{\prime}f(x^{\prime})\right\rvert\cdot\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert^{n}\leq c_{2}

with some constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
independent of
x^{\prime}\in\mathbf{R}^{n-1}
. Then the estimate

\|f\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\leq C(n)\left(\frac{c_{1}^{2}}{\rho^{n-1}}+\frac{c_{1}^{2}+c_{2}^{2}}{\rho^{n}}\right)

holds with some constant
C(n)>0
depending on
n
only.

Proof.

By a direct calculation, we see that

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\leq c_{1}^{2}\int_{B_{2\rho}(0^{\prime})^{\mathrm{c}}}\frac{1}{\lvert y^{\prime}\rvert^{2n-2}}\,dy^{\prime}\leq\frac{C(n)c_{1}^{2}}{\rho^{n-1}}.

For
y^{\prime}\in B_{\rho}(0^{\prime})
and
x^{\prime}\in B_{2\rho}(0^{\prime})^{\mathrm{c}}
, we have that
\lvert x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\rvert\geq\rho

Proposition 26

For

Let

. Note that

We assume the contrary of Proposition
26
. Suppose that there exist a constant

such that

\left\lvert R_{j}(g)(x^{\prime})\right\rvert\leq C^{\prime}\lVert g\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}

for any
\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert<r_{B}/2
and

with

\displaystyle\lVert P(g)\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}\leq C^{\prime}\lVert g\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}

(39)

(40)

5.3
L^{2}
estimate for the gradient of the single layer potential

We begin with the half space case.

Proposition 27
.

Let
n\geq 3
. For any

Proof.

We consider the partial Fourier transform of

Since

is radial symmetric in
\mathbf{R}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\big\lVert\partial_{x_{n}}E\ast\big(\delta_{\partial\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}}\otimes g\big)\big\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n})}^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{n-1}}\big\lvert\partial_{x_{n}}E\ast\big(\delta_{\partial\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}}\otimes g\big)(x^{\prime},x_{n})\big\rvert^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\,dx_{n}

\Box

, Theorem 5.2.7 and Theorem 5.2.10]
, we can deduce that

\|I_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\leq C\|g_{1,\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{e}}1_{\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}}\nabla d\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\leq C(n)\rho_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}R_{h}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}

as
\operatorname{supp}g_{1,\mathrm{c}}^{\mathrm{e}}\subset\left\{x\in\Gamma^{\mathbf{R}^{n}}_{\rho_{0}/2}\,\middle|\,\left\lvert(\pi x)^{\prime}\right\rvert<2R_{h}\right\}
.
Since
\nabla E(x)
is an integration kernel that is dominated by
C(n)\lvert x\rvert^{1-n}
for
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, by the famous Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality, see e.g.
[
1
, Theorem 1.7]
, we have that

\|I_{2}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\leq C(n)\|1_{\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}}g_{1}^{\mathrm{e}}f_{\theta,\rho_{0}/4}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}

where
r=\frac{2n}{n+2}
. Since
\operatorname{supp}g_{1}^{\mathrm{e}}f_{\theta,\rho_{0}/4}\subset\left\{x\in\Gamma^{\mathbf{R}^{n}}_{\rho_{0}/2}\,\middle|\,\left\lvert(\pi x)^{\prime}\right\rvert<2R_{h}\right\}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|1_{\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}}g_{1}^{\mathrm{e}}f_{\theta,\rho_{0}/4}\|_{L^{r}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\ \ \leq C(n)\rho_{0}^{\frac{1}{r}}R_{h}^{\frac{n-1}{r}}\left(\|\nabla^{\prime 2}h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}\big(1+\|\nabla^{\prime}h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}^{2}\big)+\rho_{0}^{-1}\right)\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}.

Hence, we obtain the
L^{2}
estimate for
g_{1}
, i.e.,

\big\|\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g_{1}\big)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}\leq C(n)C_{\ref{L2EPH},1}(h,\rho_{0})\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}.

where

C_{\ref{L2EPH},1}(h,\rho_{0}):=\rho_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}R_{h}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}+\rho_{0}^{\frac{n+2}{2n}}C_{s}(h)^{2}R_{h}^{\frac{n^{2}+n-2}{2n}}\big(\|\nabla^{\prime 2}h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}+\rho_{0}^{-1}\big).

The
L^{2}
estimate of
\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g_{2}\big)
in
\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}
follows directly from Proposition
27
and estimate (
41
). We have that

\displaystyle\big\|\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\partial\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}}\otimes g_{2}^{H}\big)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n})}

\displaystyle=C(n)\|g_{2}^{H}\|_{\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)C_{s}(h)^{n+5}C_{1}(h)^{2}\big(1+R_{h}^{\frac{n}{2}}\big)\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}.

Note that for any
x\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
, it holds that

\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g_{2}\big)(x)=\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\partial\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}}\otimes g_{2}^{H}\big)(x).

Moreover, for any
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}
we have that

\left\lvert\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\partial\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}}\otimes g_{2}^{H}\big)(x^{\prime},-x_{n})\right\rvert=\left\lvert\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\partial\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}}\otimes g_{2}^{H}\big)(x^{\prime},x_{n})\right\rvert.

Therefore, the
L^{2}
estimate of
\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g_{2}\big)
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
reads as

\displaystyle\big\|\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes g_{2}\big)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=2\big\|\nabla E\ast\big(\delta_{\partial\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n}}\otimes g_{2}^{H}\big)\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C(n)C_{s}(h)^{n+5}C_{1}(h)^{2}\big(1+R_{h}^{\frac{n}{2}}\big)\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}.

\Box

5.4
Solution to the Neumann problem

Let
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
be a perturbed
C^{2}
half space with boundary
\Gamma=\partial\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
and
n\geq 3
. We further assume that
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
has small perturbation, i.e., we require that the boundary function
h\in C_{c}^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{n-1})
satisfies

C_{\ast}(h)<\frac{1}{2C^{\ast}(n)}

where
C^{\ast}(n)
is a specific constant that depends on dimension
n
only.
Under this setting, we are able to construct a solution to Neumann problem (
2
).

Proof of Lemma
4
.

Let
g\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
.
By Corollary
24
and Theorem
22
, for any
i\in\mathbf{N}
we have that

\|(2S)^{i}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\leq 2^{i}C^{\ast}(n)^{i}C_{\ast}(h)^{i}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}.

Since we are now assuming that
2C^{\ast}(n)C_{\ast}(h)<1
, the operator
I-2S
, which is bounded linear from
L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
to
L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
, admits a well-defined inverse constructed by the Neumann series

(I-2S)^{-1}:=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}(2S)^{i}

in the sense that
(I-2S)^{-1}:L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\to L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
is also bounded linear.
For
g\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
, we have that

\displaystyle\|(I-2S)^{-1}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\leq\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}2^{i}C^{\ast}(n)^{i}C_{\ast}(h)^{i}\right)\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\leq\frac{\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}}{1-2C^{\ast}(n)C_{\ast}(h)}.

Hence, with respect to
g\in L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
, we claim that the solution to Neumann problem (
2
) can be constructed as

u(x)=E\ast\Big(\delta_{\Gamma}\otimes\big(2(I-2S)^{-1}g\big)\Big)(x),\quad x\in\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}.

A simple check ensures that
u
satisfies Neumann problem (
2
) formally. It is sufficient to establish the
vBMOL^{2}
estimate for
\nabla u
.
The
vBMO^{\infty,\rho_{0}}
-norm for
\nabla u
in
\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}
is guaranteed by Lemma
25
. By estimate (
37
) and estimate (
38
), we have that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{vBMO^{\infty,\rho_{0}}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)}\leq\frac{C(n)C_{\ref{NM}}(K,h,\rho_{0})}{1-2C^{\ast}(n)C_{\ast}(h)}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}

where
C_{\ref{NM}}(K,h,\rho_{0}):=C_{\ref{NM},i}(h,\rho_{0})+C_{\ref{NM},ii}(K,h,\rho_{0})
. The
L^{2}
estimate of
\nabla u
follows directly from Lemma
28
, we have that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}\big(\mathbf{R}_{h}^{n}\big)}\leq C(n)C_{\ref{L2EPH}}(h,\rho_{0})\|(I-2S)^{-1}g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\leq\frac{C(n)C_{\ref{L2EPH}}(h,\rho_{0})}{1-2C^{\ast}(n)C_{\ast}(h)}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)\cap\dot{H}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}.

This completes the proof of Lemma
4
.

\Box

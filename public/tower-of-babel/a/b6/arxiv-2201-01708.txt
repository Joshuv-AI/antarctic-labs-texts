Local decay rates of best-approximation errors using vector-valued finite elements for fields with low regularity and integrable curl or divergence
Zhaonan Dong, Alexandre Ern, Jean-Luc Guermond

Abstract
We estimate best-approximation errors using vector-valued finite elements for fields with low regularity in the scale of fractional-order Sobolev spaces. By assuming that the target field enjoys an additional integrability property on its curl or its divergence, we establish upper bounds on these errors that can be localized to the mesh cells. These bounds are derived using the quasi-interpolation errors with or without boundary prescription derived in [A. Ern and J.-L. Guermond, ESAIM Math. Model. Numer. Anal., 51 (2017), pp. 1367–1385]. In the present work, a localized upper bound on the quasi-interpolation error is derived by using the face-to-cell lifting operators analyzed in [A. Ern and J.-L. Guermond, Found. Comput. Math., (2021)] and by exploiting the additional assumption made on the curl or the divergence of the target field. As an illustration, we show how to apply these results to the error analysis of the curl-curl problem associated with Maxwell’s equations.



1
Introduction

A central question in the finite element approximation theory is to establish
local upper bounds on the best-approximation error for functions
that satisfy some minimal regularity assumptions
typically quantified in the scale of fractional-order Sobolev spaces.
The goal of the present work is to derive some novel results in this context
when the approximation is realized
using Nédélec finite elements and Raviart–Thomas finite elements.
Most of our developments focus on the Nédélec finite elements
since they require more elaborate arguments. The corresponding results for
the Raviart–Thomas finite elements only improve marginally the state of the art from
the literature, and only a short discussion is provided in a specific
section of the paper.

We are interested in approximating fields with a smoothness Sobolev
index that is so low that one cannot invoke the canonical
interpolation operators associated
with the considered finite elements. In this case, the best-approximation error can
be bounded by considering quasi-interpolation errors, such as those
derived in
[
10
]
.
However, the resulting upper bound cannot be localized to the mesh cells
if the regularity of the target function is only measured in the scale
of fractional-order Sobolev spaces. The lack of localization is relatively mild
if no boundary conditions are prescribed in the finite
element spaces, since in this case the quasi-interpolation error
can still be bounded by local contributions involving the fractional-order
Sobolev seminorm of the target function over patches of mesh cells instead of just each
mesh cell individually.
The lack of localization is more significant if boundary conditions are
additionally prescribed in the finite element spaces since in this case
the upper bound on the quasi-interpolation error is global. This is not surprising
as in this situation the target function has not enough smoothness to
have a well-defined trace at the boundary.
The main contribution of this work is to show that the localization becomes
possible provided
some (mild) additional assumptions are made on the integrability of the curl or
the divergence of the target function. The main tool to achieve this result hinges on
the face-to-cell lifting operators
introduced in
[
15
]
.
The additional assumptions on the curl or the divergence allow us to give a
weak meaning to the trace of the target function
in the dual space of a suitable fractional-order Sobolev space.

In this work, the space dimension is
d=3
for the Nédélec finite elements
and
d\geq 2
for the Raviart–Thomas finite elements.
For
d=2
, the results for the Raviart–Thomas elements
can be transposed to the Nédélec elements by invoking a rotation of angle
\frac{\pi}{2}
;
details are omitted for brevity (see, e.g.,
[
13
, Sec. 15.3.1]
).
We consider a polyhedral Lipschitz domain
D\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Moreover, we use boldface for
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
-valued
fields and linear spaces composed of such fields. For instance, for real
numbers
r\geq 0
and
p\in[1,\infty]
(we assume
p\in[1,\infty)

if
r\not\in{\mathbb{N}}
),
{\bm{W}}^{r,p}(D)
denotes the (fractional-order) Sobolev space
equipped with the Sobolev–Slobodeckij norm.

Let
({\mathcal{T}}_{h})_{h\in{\mathcal{H}}}
denote a shape-regular family of affine, matching,
simplicial meshes
such that each mesh covers
D
exactly.
Let
{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
denote the
{{{\bm{H}}(\text{\rm curl};D)}}
-conforming finite element space
built on the mesh
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
using the Nédélec finite element of degree
k\in{\mathbb{N}}

(here, the superscript

refers to the curl operator).
Given a target field
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{W}}^{r,p}(D)
, with

our goal is to establish localized upper bounds
on the best-approximation error

\inf_{{\bm{v}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\|{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{p}(D)}.

(1)

The first natural idea is to invoke the canonical interpolation operator
for Nédélec finite elements, say
\mathcal{I}_{h}^{\mathrm{c}}
. Since this operator can only
act on those fields having an
integrable tangential trace along all the mesh edges, invoking the standard trace theory
in Sobolev spaces (see, e.g.,
[
16
]
) shows that a suitable domain
for the canonical interpolation operator
\mathcal{I}_{h}^{\mathrm{c}}
is
{\bm{W}}^{r,p}(D)

with
rp>d-1=2
(and
r\geq 2
if
p=1
).
Assume that the polynomial degree is such that

k\geq 1
if
p\in[1,2]
and
k\geq 0
otherwise, and let

r\in(\frac{2}{p},k+1]
if
p>1
or
r\in[2,k+1]
if
p=1
. Then,
it is well-known (see, e.g.,
[
18
]

or
[
13
, Sec. 16.2]
) that there is

c
such that for all
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{W}}^{r,p}(D)
,
all
K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, and all
{h\in{\mathcal{H}}}
, we have

\|{\bm{v}}-\mathcal{I}_{h}^{\mathrm{c}}({\bm{v}})\|_{{\bm{L}}^{p}(K)}\leq c\,h_{K}^{r}|{\bm{v}}|_{{\bm{W}}^{r,p}(K)},

(2)

where
h_{K}
denotes the diameter of the mesh cell
K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
.
Here, the symbol
c
denotes
a generic positive constant whose value can change at each occurrence provided it
only depends on the mesh shape-regularity, the space dimension, and the polynomial
degree
k
of the considered finite elements.
Notice that
c
is unbounded as
r\downarrow\frac{2}{p}
if
p>1
.
For instance, in the Hilbert setting where
p=2
, the minimal regularity requirement is

{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{H}}^{r}(D)
with
r>1
, and
c
is unbounded as
r\downarrow 1
.

The requirement
r>\frac{2}{p}
can be lowered
to
r>\frac{1}{2}
(with
p=2
) by invoking more sophisticated results on traces
derived in
[
2
]
which, however, hinge on some additional integrability assumption
on
\nabla{\times}{\bm{v}}
. To use these results, the edge-based
degrees of freedom of the Nédélec finite element are extended by defining them
using edge-to-cell lifting operators and an integration by parts formula
(see, e.g.,
[
13
, Sec. 17.3]
). One can then show
(see
[
6
]
; see also
[
1
,
8
,
3
]
for slight variants) that
for all
r\in(\frac{1}{2},1]
and all
p>2
, there is
c
such that
for all
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{H}}^{r}(D)
with
\nabla{\times}{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{L}}^{p}(D)
,
all
K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, and all
{h\in{\mathcal{H}}}
, we have

\|{\bm{v}}-\mathcal{I}_{h}^{\mathrm{c}}({\bm{v}})\|_{{\bm{L}}^{2}(K)}\leq c\,\Big(h_{K}^{r}|{\bm{v}}|_{{\bm{H}}^{r}(K)}+h_{K}^{1+d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\|\nabla{\times}{\bm{v}}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{p}(K)}\Big),

(3)

with
c
unbounded as
r\downarrow\frac{1}{2}
or
p\downarrow 2
.

Unfortunately, the regularity assumption
r>\frac{1}{2}
is often not realistic in applications.
To go beyond this assumption, one can invoke the quasi-interpolation operators devised
in
[
10
]
. Recall that the construction of
these quasi-interpolation operators consists of first
projecting the target
field
{\bm{v}}
onto a fully discontinuous finite element space and then stitching
together the projected
field to recover the desired conformity property by averaging the canonical degrees
of freedom of the projected field. Since the projected field is always piecewise smooth,
this construction is always meaningful, regardless of the regularity of the target field

{\bm{v}}
. Let
\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{c,av}}_{h}:{\bm{L}}^{1}(D)\to{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
denote the quasi-interpolation
operator thus constructed with the Nédélec finite elements.
Then,
[
13
, Thm. 22.6]
shows that there is

c
such that for all
r\in[0,k+1]
, all
p\in[1,\infty]
if
r\in{\mathbb{N}}

and
p\in[1,\infty)
otherwise,
all
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{W}}^{r,p}(D)
, all
K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, and all
{h\in{\mathcal{H}}}
, we have

\|{\bm{v}}-\mathcal{I}_{h}^{\mathrm{c,av}}({\bm{v}})\|_{{\bm{L}}^{p}(K)}\leq c\,h_{K}^{r}|{\bm{v}}|_{{\bm{W}}^{r,p}(D_{K}^{\mathrm{c}})},

(4)

where
D_{K}^{\mathrm{c}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\mathop{\mbox{\rm int}}\big(\bigcup_{K^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}^{\mathrm{c}}}K^{\prime}\big)
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{K}^{\mathrm{c}}

denotes the collection of mesh
cells sharing at least one edge with
K
. We notice that a slight loss of localization occurs
in (
4
) since the Sobolev–Slobodeckij seminorm on the right-hand side
is evaluated over the macroelement
D_{K}^{\mathrm{c}}
and not just over
K
.
In the present work, we show that provided some (mild)
additional integrability assumption is made on
\nabla{\times}{\bm{v}}
, the
estimate (
4
) can be localized to the mesh cells
in
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{K}
; see Theorem
2.1

and Corollary
2.2
.

The loss of localization is more striking if one wants to additionally enforce a
homogeneous boundary condition on the tangential component of the target field. We
assume for
simplicity that the condition is enforced over the whole boundary
{\partial D}
of
D
.
Recall that the tangential trace operator
\gamma^{\mathrm{c}}:{{{\bm{H}}(\text{\rm curl};D)}}\to{\bm{H}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}(D)

is defined through a global integration by parts formula (see, e.g.,
[
13
, Thm. 4.15]
) and that we have

\gamma^{\mathrm{c}}({\bm{v}})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}={\bm{v}}_{|{\partial D}}\times{\bm{n}}_{D}
whenever the field
{\bm{v}}
is smooth enough,
where
{\bm{n}}_{D}
denotes the unit outward normal to
D
. Then, setting

{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k,0}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{{\bm{v}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h}){\;|\;}\gamma^{\mathrm{c}}({\bm{v}}_{h})=\bm{0}\}
, one is interested in establishing local upper bounds
on the best-approximation error

\inf_{{\bm{v}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k,0}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\|{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{p}(D)}.

(5)

Let
\mathcal{I}_{h0}^{\mathrm{c,av}}:{\bm{L}}^{1}(D)\to{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k,0}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})

denote the quasi-interpolation operator with homogeneous boundary prescription
associated with the Nédélec finite elements. Then,

[
13
, Thm. 22.14]
) shows that
for all
r\in[0,\frac{1}{p})
, there is
c
such that
for all
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{W}}^{r,p}(D)
, and all
{h\in{\mathcal{H}}}
, we have

\|{\bm{v}}-\mathcal{I}_{h0}^{\mathrm{c,av}}({\bm{v}})\|_{{\bm{L}}^{p}(D)}\leq c\,h^{r}\ell_{D}^{-r}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{{\bm{W}}^{r,p}(D)},

(6)

where
h\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\max_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{K}
,
\ell_{D}
is a characteristic (global) length scale
associated with
D
, and
\|{\bm{v}}\|_{{\bm{W}}^{r,p}(D)}=\|{\bm{v}}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{p}(D)}+\ell_{D}^{r}|{\bm{v}}|_{{\bm{W}}^{r,p}(D)}
.
Notice that the target field
{\bm{v}}
has not sufficient regularity to have a well-defined
tangential trace on the boundary.
The loss of localization in (
6
) arises when bounding
the quasi-interpolation error over those mesh cells that have at least one edge located on the boundary
{\partial D}

(the upper bound (
4
) holds true for the other mesh cells).
The presence of the global length scale
\ell_{D}
and of the full
Sobolev–Slobodeckij norm of
{\bm{v}}
instead of just the seminorm in (
6
)
comes from the need to invoke a Hardy inequality near the boundary (see the proof of

[
10
, Thm. 6.4]
). In the present work, we show that provided some
(mild) additional integrability assumption is made on
\nabla{\times}{\bm{v}}
, the
estimate (
6
) can be fully localized to the mesh cells; see again
Theorem
2.1
and Corollary
2.2
.

2
Main results on Nédélec finite elements

In this section, we first state our main results and then present their proofs.

2.1
Statement of the main results

Let us first observe that the domain of the tangential trace operator can be
extended to
{\bm{Y}}^{\mathrm{c}}(D)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{{\bm{v}}\in{{\bm{L}}^{2}(D)}{\;|\;}\nabla{\times}{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{L}}^{q}(D)\}

for all
q\in(\frac{2d}{2+d},2]
. Indeed, for all
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{Y}}^{\mathrm{c}}(D)
,

\gamma^{\mathrm{c}}({\bm{v}})\in{\bm{H}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\partial D})
can still be defined by duality
by setting for all
{\bm{w}}\in{\bm{H}}^{\frac{1}{2}}({\partial D})
,

\langle\gamma^{\mathrm{c}}({\bm{v}}),{\bm{w}}\rangle_{{\partial D}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int_{D}\Big({\bm{v}}{\cdot}\nabla{\times}{\bm{l}}({\bm{w}})-(\nabla{\times}{\bm{v}}){\cdot}{\bm{l}}({\bm{w}})\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x,

(7)

where
{\bm{l}}({\bm{w}})
denotes a lifting of
{\bm{w}}
in

{\bm{H}}^{1}(D)
. Indeed, owing to the Sobolev embedding theorem,
and the fact that
q>\frac{2d}{2+d}
, we infer that

{\bm{H}}^{1}(D)\hookrightarrow{\bm{L}}^{q^{\prime}}(D)
with
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
,
so that the second term on the right-hand side of (
7
)
is meaningful owing to Hölder’s inequality.
We can now state our main result. For simplicity, we estimate the
quasi-interpolation error only in the
{\bm{L}}^{2}
-norm.

Theorem 2.1

(Localized quasi-interpolation error estimate for Nédélec elements)

For all
r\in(0,1]
and all
q\in(\frac{2d}{2+d},2]
,
there is
c
such that for
all
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{H}}^{r}(D)
with
\nabla{\times}{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{L}}^{q}(D)
, all
K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
,
and all
{h\in{\mathcal{H}}}
, we have

\|{\bm{v}}-\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{c,av}}_{h}({\bm{v}})\|_{{\bm{L}}^{2}(K)}\leq c\sum_{K^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}^{\mathrm{c}}}\Big\{h_{K^{\prime}}^{r}|{\bm{v}}|_{{\bm{H}}^{r}(K^{\prime})}+h_{K^{\prime}}^{1+d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\|\nabla{\times}{\bm{v}}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{q}(K^{\prime})}\Big\}.

(8)

Moreover, assuming that
\gamma^{\mathrm{c}}({\bm{v}})=\bm{0}
, we also have

\|{\bm{v}}-\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{c,av}}_{h0}({\bm{v}})\|_{{\bm{L}}^{2}(K)}\leq c\sum_{K^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}^{\mathrm{c}}}\Big\{h_{K^{\prime}}^{r}|{\bm{v}}|_{{\bm{H}}^{r}(K^{\prime})}+h_{K^{\prime}}^{1+d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\|\nabla{\times}{\bm{v}}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{q}(K^{\prime})}\Big\}.

(9)

Squaring the above inequalities, summing over the mesh cells, and observing that
the cardinality of the set
\{K^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}{\;|\;}K\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{K^{\prime}}\}
is uniformly
bounded for all
K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and all
{h\in{\mathcal{H}}}
, we infer the following result.

Corollary 2.2

(Localized best-approximation error for Nédélec elements)

For all
r\in(0,1]
and all
q\in(\frac{2d}{2+d},2]
,
there is
c
such that for
all
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{H}}^{r}(D)
with
\nabla{\times}{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{L}}^{q}(D)
,
and all
{h\in{\mathcal{H}}}
, we have

\inf_{{\bm{v}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\|{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{2}(D)}\leq c\,\bigg\{\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\Big\{h_{K}^{2r}|{\bm{v}}|_{{\bm{H}}^{r}(K)}^{2}+h_{K}^{2+2d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\|\nabla{\times}{\bm{v}}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{q}(K)}^{2}\Big\}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(10)

Moreover, assuming that
\gamma^{\mathrm{c}}({\bm{v}})=\bm{0}
, we also have

\inf_{{\bm{v}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}_{k,0}^{\mathrm{c}}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\|{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{2}(D)}\leq c\,\bigg\{\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\Big\{h_{K}^{2r}|{\bm{v}}|_{{\bm{H}}^{r}(K)}^{2}+h_{K}^{2+2d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\|\nabla{\times}{\bm{v}}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{q}(K)}^{2}\Big\}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(11)

Remark 2.3

(Exponent)

Observe that
2+2d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})=2d(\frac{d+2}{2d}-\frac{1}{q})>0

since
q\in(\frac{2d}{2+d},2]
. Moreover, we have

2+2d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})=2
for
q=2
.

To define a tangential trace that is localized to the mesh face

It is shown in

shows again that
for all
r\in[0,\frac{1}{p})
, there is
c
such that
for all
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{W}}^{r,p}(D)
, and
all
{h\in{\mathcal{H}}}
, we have

\|{\bm{v}}-\mathcal{I}_{h0}^{\mathrm{d,av}}({\bm{v}})\|_{{\bm{L}}^{p}(D)}\leq c\,h^{r}\ell_{D}^{-r}\|{\bm{v}}\|_{{\bm{W}}^{r,p}(D)}.

(30)

We now show that the estimates (
28
) and (
30
)
can be localized under suitable additional assumptions on the divergence of
the field
{\bm{v}}
.
Let us first observe that the domain of the normal trace operator can be
extended to
{\bm{Y}}^{\mathrm{d}}(D)\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{{\bm{v}}\in{{\bm{L}}^{2}(D)}{\;|\;}\nabla{\cdot}{\bm{v}}\in L^{q}(D)\}

for all
q\in(\frac{2d}{2+d},2]
. Indeed, for all
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{Y}}^{\mathrm{d}}(D)
,

\gamma^{\mathrm{d}}({\bm{v}})\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}({\partial D})
can still be defined by duality
by setting for all
w\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}({\partial D})
,

\langle\gamma^{\mathrm{d}}({\bm{v}}),w\rangle_{{\partial D}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int_{D}\Big({\bm{v}}{\cdot}\nabla l(w)+(\nabla{\cdot}{\bm{v}})l(w)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x,

(31)

where
l(w)
denotes a lifting of
w
in

H^{1}(D)
. This is again a consequence of

q>\frac{2d}{2+d}
and Hölder’s inequality.
We can now state our main result. For simplicity, we estimate the
quasi-interpolation error only in the
{\bm{L}}^{2}
-norm.

Theorem 3.1

(Localized quasi-interpolation error estimate for Raviart–Thomas elements)

For all
r\in(0,1]
and all
q\in(\frac{2d}{2+d},2]
, there is
c
such that for
all
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{H}}^{r}(D)
with
\nabla{\cdot}{\bm{v}}\in L^{q}(D)
, all
K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
,
and all
{h\in{\mathcal{H}}}
, we have

\|{\bm{v}}-\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{d,av}}_{h}({\bm{v}})\|_{{\bm{L}}^{2}(K)}\leq c\sum_{K^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}^{\mathrm{d}}}\Big\{h_{K^{\prime}}^{r}|{\bm{v}}|_{{\bm{H}}^{r}(K^{\prime})}+h_{K^{\prime}}^{1+d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\|\nabla{\cdot}{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{q}(K^{\prime})}\Big\}.

(32)

Moreover, assuming that
\gamma^{\mathrm{d}}({\bm{v}})=0
, we also have

\|{\bm{v}}-\mathcal{I}^{\mathrm{d,av}}_{h0}({\bm{v}})\|_{{\bm{L}}^{2}(K)}\leq c\sum_{K^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{K}^{\mathrm{d}}}\Big\{h_{K^{\prime}}^{r}|{\bm{v}}|_{{\bm{H}}^{r}(K^{\prime})}+h_{K^{\prime}}^{1+d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\|\nabla{\cdot}{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{q}(K^{\prime})}\Big\}.

(33)

Using the same arguments as above, we readily infer the following result.

Corollary 3.2

(Localized best-approximation error for Raviart–Thomas elements)

For all
r\in(0,1]
and all
q\in(\frac{2d}{2+d},2]
, there is
c
such that for
all
{\bm{v}}\in{\bm{H}}^{r}(D)
with
\nabla{\cdot}{\bm{v}}\in L^{q}(D)
,
and all
{h\in{\mathcal{H}}}
, we have

\inf_{{\bm{v}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{d}}_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\|{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{2}(D)}\leq c\,\bigg\{\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\Big\{h_{K}^{2r}|{\bm{v}}|_{{\bm{H}}^{r}(K)}^{2}+h_{K}^{2+2d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\|\nabla{\cdot}{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{q}(K)}^{2}\Big\}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(34)

Moreover, assuming that
\gamma^{\mathrm{d}}({\bm{v}})=0
, we also have

\inf_{{\bm{v}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{d}}_{k,0}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\|{\bm{v}}-{\bm{v}}_{h}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{2}(D)}\leq c\,\bigg\{\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\Big\{h_{K}^{2r}|{\bm{v}}|_{{\bm{H}}^{r}(K)}^{2}+h_{K}^{2+2d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\|\nabla{\cdot}{\bm{v}}\|_{L^{q}(K)}^{2}\Big\}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(35)

Remark 3.3

(Comparison with canonical interpolation)

The canonical Raviart–Thomas
interpolation operator and the quasi-interpolation operator achieve the same
convergence rates under the same
smoothness assumptions. Notice though that the estimates (
32
)-(
33
)
are localized over
{\mathcal{T}}_{K}^{\mathrm{d}}
whereas the estimate (
27
) is localized
over the single cell
K
.
The upper bounds in Corollary
3.2
can also
be derived by invoking the canonical Raviart–Thomas
interpolation operator.
This is in contrast with the Nédélec elements where
the estimates (
8
)-(
9
) on the quasi-interpolation error
are essential to prove
Corollary
2.2
. These estimates hold for the canonical Nédélec interpolation operator
only if
r>\frac{1}{2}
.

4
Application to Maxwell’s equations

In this section, we briefly present two applications of the above
results for the approximation of Maxwell’s equations using
Nédélec finite elements. We focus on simplified forms of Maxwell’s
equations obtained, e.g., in the time-harmonic regime and in the
eddy currents approximation.

4.1
Model problem

Given a source term
{\bm{f}}\in{\bm{L}}^{q}(D)
with
q\in(\frac{2d}{2+d},2]
,
the model problem we consider consists of seeking

{\bm{A}}\in{{{\bm{H}}_{0}(\text{\rm curl};D)}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{{\bm{v}}\in{{{\bm{H}}(\text{\rm curl};D)}}{\;|\;}\gamma^{\mathrm{c}}({\bm{v}})=\bm{0}\}
such that

a({\bm{A}},{\bm{b}})=\ell({\bm{b}})
for all
{\bm{b}}\in{{{\bm{H}}_{0}(\text{\rm curl};D)}}
, with the following
sesquilinear and antilinear forms:

a({\bm{a}},{\bm{b}})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int_{D}(\nu{\bm{a}}{\cdot}\bar{{\bm{b}}}+\kappa\nabla{\times}{\bm{a}}{\cdot}\nabla{\times}\bar{{\bm{b}}})\,\mathrm{d}x,\qquad\ell({\bm{b}})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\int_{D}{\bm{f}}{\cdot}\bar{{\bm{b}}}\,\mathrm{d}x.

(36)

We assume that the model parameters
\nu,\kappa
are both bounded in

D
and we set
\nu_{\sharp}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\|\nu\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}
,

\kappa_{\sharp}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\|\kappa\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}
. We also assume that
there are real numbers
\theta
,
\nu_{\flat}>0
,
\kappa_{\flat}>0
such that

\essinf_{{\bm{x}}\in D}\Re\big(e^{i\theta}\nu({\bm{x}})\big)\geq\nu_{\flat}\quad\text{and}\quad\essinf_{{\bm{x}}\in D}\Re\big(e^{i\theta}\kappa({\bm{x}})\big)\geq\kappa_{\flat}.

(37)

This condition ensures the coercivity of the sesquilinear form
a
.
Therefore, the model problem is well-posed owing to the Lax–Milgram lemma,
and its unique weak solution is such that
\nu{\bm{A}}+\nabla{\times}(\kappa\nabla{\times}A)={\bm{f}}
in

D
together with
\gamma^{\mathrm{c}}({\bm{A}})=\bm{0}
.
To obtain a regularity result on the weak solution,
we assume that there is a partition
\{D_{m}\}_{m\in\{1\hskip 0.40974pt{:}\hskip 0.81949ptM\}}

of
D
into
M\geq 1
disjoint polyhedral Lipschitz subsets such that

\nu_{|D_{m}}
and
\kappa_{|D_{m}}
are constant for all
m\in\{1\hskip 0.50003pt{:}\hskip 1.00006ptM\}
.
One can then show (see
[
17
,
7
]
, see also

[
4
,
9
,
2
]
) that there is
r\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
such that

{\bm{A}}\in{\bm{H}}^{r}(D),\qquad\nabla{\times}{\bm{A}}\in{\bm{H}}^{r}(D).

(38)

4.2
Classical approximation with prescribed boundary
conditions

In this setting,
the discrete problem approximated with Nédélec finite elements consists of seeking

{\bm{A}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k,0}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
such that
a({\bm{A}}_{h},{\bm{b}}_{h})=\ell({\bm{b}}_{h})
for all

{\bm{b}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k,0}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
. Recall that
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
denotes the
polynomial degree and that
({\mathcal{T}}_{h})_{h\in{\mathcal{H}}}
is a shape-regular family of affine, matching,
simplicial meshes such that each mesh covers
D
exactly.
Notice that the homogeneous boundary condition
on the tangential component of
{\bm{A}}_{h}
at the boundary is explicitly enforced in
the discrete problem.

The error analysis is performed by establishing suitable
stability, consistency, and boundedness properties.
To avoid distracting technicalities,
we do not track the dependency of the constants in
the error analysis on the nondimensional factors
\frac{\nu_{\sharp}}{\nu_{\flat}}
,

\frac{\kappa_{\sharp}}{\kappa_{\flat}}
, and
\nu_{\sharp}\kappa_{\sharp}^{-1}\ell_{D}^{2}

(recall that
\ell_{D}
denotes
a characteristic (global) length scale associated with
D
).
Referring to
[
12
]

(see also
[
14
, Chap. 44]
) for further insight, the main
error estimate states that there is
c
such that we have

\|{\bm{A}}-{\bm{A}}_{h}\|_{{{{\bm{H}}(\text{\rm curl};D)}}}\leq c\,\inf_{{\bm{b}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k,0}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\|{\bm{A}}-{\bm{b}}_{h}\|_{{{{\bm{H}}(\text{\rm curl};D)}}}.

(39)

Moreover, invoking commuting quasi-interpolation operators, it is also shown therein
that there is
c
such that we have

\|{\bm{A}}-{\bm{A}}_{h}\|_{{{{\bm{H}}(\text{\rm curl};D)}}}\leq c\,\Big(\inf_{{\bm{b}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k,0}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\|{\bm{A}}-{\bm{b}}_{h}\|_{{{\bm{L}}^{2}(D)}}+\ell_{D}\inf_{{\bm{d}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{d}}_{k,0}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\|\nabla{\times}{\bm{A}}-{\bm{d}}_{h}\|_{{{\bm{L}}^{2}(D)}}\Big).

(40)

The analysis performed in this paper allows us to derive the following result where the
best approximation error is fully localized even when
r\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
.

Corollary 4.1

(Localized error estimate)

There is
c
such that we have

\|{\bm{A}}-{\bm{A}}_{h}\|_{{{{\bm{H}}(\text{\rm curl};D)}}}\leq c\,\bigg\{\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{K}^{2r}\Big(|{\bm{A}}|_{{\bm{H}}^{r}(K)}^{2}+\ell_{D}^{2(1-r)}\|\nabla{\times}{\bm{A}}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{2}(K)}^{2}+\ell_{D}^{2}|\nabla{\times}{\bm{A}}|_{{\bm{H}}^{r}(K)}^{2}\Big)\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(41)

Proof.

We combine the error estimate (
40
) with the
results from Corollary
2.2
and
Corollary
3.2
. In particular, we apply the estimate
(
11
) with
{\bm{v}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}={\bm{A}}
(notice that indeed
\gamma^{\mathrm{c}}({\bm{A}})=\bm{0}
)
and
q\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=2
, and the estimate (
35
) with
{\bm{v}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\nabla{\times}{\bm{A}}

(notice that indeed
\gamma^{\mathrm{d}}(\nabla{\times}{\bm{A}})=0
and that
\nabla{\cdot}{\bm{v}}=0
).
This yields

\|{\bm{A}}-{\bm{A}}_{h}\|_{{{{\bm{H}}(\text{\rm curl};D)}}}\leq c\,\bigg\{\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\Big(h_{K}^{2r}|{\bm{A}}|_{{\bm{H}}^{r}(K)}^{2}+h_{K}^{2}\|\nabla{\times}{\bm{A}}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{2}(K)}^{2}+\ell_{D}^{2}h_{K}^{2r}|\nabla{\times}{\bm{A}}|_{{\bm{H}}^{r}(K)}^{2}\Big)\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}},

whence the assertion follows from
h_{K}^{2}\leq h_{K}^{2r}\ell_{D}^{2(1-r)}
.
∎

4.3
Nitsche’s boundary penalty technique

Let us now consider a variant of the above discrete problem where the boundary
condition is enforced by Nitsche’s boundary penalty technique; see

[
11
]
and also
[
14
, Chap. 45]
.
For all
K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, we set
\nu_{K}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=|\nu_{|K}|
,
\kappa_{K}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=|\kappa_{|K}|
,

\nu_{r,K}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\Re(e^{i\theta}\nu_{K})
, and
\kappa_{r,K}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\Re(e^{i\theta}\kappa_{K})
.
To avoid distracting technicalities,
we do not track the dependency of the constants in
the error analysis on the nondimensional factors

\frac{\nu_{K}}{\nu_{r,K}}
and
\frac{\kappa_{K}}{\kappa_{r,K}}
.
Recall that for every mesh boundary face
F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\partial}
, there is a mesh cell

K_{-}\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
such that
F=\partial K_{-}\cap{\partial D}
.
The discrete problem now consists of seeking
{\bm{A}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})

such that
a_{h}({\bm{A}}_{h},{\bm{b}}_{h})=\ell({\bm{b}}_{h})
for all
{\bm{b}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
,
with
a_{h}({\cdot},{\cdot})=a({\cdot},{\cdot})-n_{h}({\cdot},{\cdot})+s_{h}({\cdot},{\cdot})
,
with the following sesquilinear forms defined on
{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})\times{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
:

n_{h}({\bm{a}}_{h},{\bm{b}}_{h})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\partial}}\int_{F}((\kappa\nabla{\times}{\bm{a}}_{h})\times{\bm{n}}_{F}){\cdot}\bar{{\bm{b}}}_{h}\,\mathrm{d}s,\qquad s_{h}({\bm{a}}_{h},{\bm{b}}_{h})\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\partial}}\eta_{0}e^{-i\theta}\frac{\lambda_{F}}{h_{F}}\int_{F}({\bm{a}}_{h}\times{\bm{n}}_{F}){\cdot}(\bar{{\bm{b}}}_{h}\times{\bm{n}}_{F})\,\mathrm{d}s,

(42)

where
\eta_{0}
is a nondimensional parameter that must be chosen large enough
so that the sesquilinear form
a_{h}
is coercive on
{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})

(see
[
11
, Lem. 3.3]
or
[
14
, Lem. 45.1]
).

To perform the error analysis, it is convenient
to introduce the space
{\bm{V}}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=\{{\bm{a}}\in{{{\bm{H}}_{0}(\text{\rm curl};D)}}{\;|\;}\kappa\nabla{\times}{\bm{a}}\in{\bm{V}}^{\mathrm{c}}(D)\}
,
where the space
{\bm{V}}^{\mathrm{c}}(D)
is defined in (
16
).
We observe that the exact solution satisfies
{\bm{A}}\in{\bm{V}}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
; indeed,
{\bm{A}}\in{{{\bm{H}}_{0}(\text{\rm curl};D)}}

by construction,
\nabla{\times}(\kappa\nabla{\times}{\bm{A}})={\bm{f}}-\nu{\bm{A}}\in{\bm{L}}^{q}(D)
owing to
our assumption on the source term
{\bm{f}}
, and
\kappa\nabla{\times}{\bm{A}}\in{\bm{L}}^{p}(D)

for some
p>2
owing to the Sobolev embedding theorem (recall that
\nabla{\times}{\bm{A}}\in{\bm{H}}^{r}(D)
by (
38
) and that
\kappa
satisfies a suitable
multiplier property as shown in
[
17
,
7
]
).
Notice that the approximation error
{\bm{A}}-{\bm{A}}_{h}
belongs to the space

{\bm{V}}_{\sharp}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}={\bm{V}}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}+{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
which we equip with the norm

\displaystyle\|{\bm{b}}\|_{{\bm{V}}_{\sharp}}^{2}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\Big\{\nu_{K}\|{\bm{b}}\|^{2}_{{\bm{L}}^{2}(K)}+\kappa_{K}\|\nabla{\times}{\bm{b}}\|^{2}_{{\bm{L}}^{2}(K)}\Big\}+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\partial}}\frac{\kappa_{K_{-}}}{h_{K_{-}}}\|{\bm{b}}_{h}\times{\bm{n}}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{2}(F)}^{2}

(43)

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\partial}}\kappa_{K_{-}}\Big\{h_{K_{-}}^{2d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p})}\|\nabla{\times}{\bm{b}}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{p}(K_{-})}^{2}+h_{K_{-}}^{2+2d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{q})}\|\nabla{\times}(\nabla{\times}{\bm{b}})\|_{{\bm{L}}^{q}(K_{-})}^{2}\Big\},

for all
{\bm{b}}\mathrel{\mathop{:}}={\bm{b}}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}+{\bm{b}}_{h}\in{\bm{V}}_{\sharp}
with
{\bm{b}}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}\in{\bm{V}}_{\mathrm{\scriptscriptstyle S}}
and

{\bm{b}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})
.
The following error estimate is established in
[
11
, Sec. 7.2]
and

[
14
, Thm. 45.6]
(with
q=2
): There is
c
such that we have

\|{\bm{A}}-{\bm{A}}_{h}\|_{{\bm{V}}_{\sharp}}\leq c\,\inf_{{\bm{b}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\|{\bm{A}}-{\bm{b}}_{h}\|_{{\bm{V}}_{\sharp}}.

(44)

The analysis performed above allows us to derive the following error estimate where the
best approximation error is fully localized even when
r\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
.

Corollary 4.2

(Localized error estimate)

There is
c
such that we have

\displaystyle\|{\bm{A}}-{\bm{A}}_{h}\|_{{\bm{V}}_{\sharp}}\leq{}

\displaystyle c\,\kappa_{\sharp}^{\frac{1}{2}}\bigg\{\sum_{K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{K}^{2r}\Big(\ell_{D}^{-2}|{\bm{A}}|_{{\bm{H}}^{r}(K)}^{2}+\ell_{D}^{-2r}\|\nabla{\times}{\bm{A}}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{2}(K)}^{2}+|\nabla{\times}{\bm{A}}|_{{\bm{H}}^{r}(K)}^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\partial}}h_{K_{-}}^{2d(\frac{d+2}{2d}-\frac{1}{q})}\|{\bm{f}}-\nu{\bm{A}}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{q}(K_{-})}^{2}\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(45)

Proof.

Adapting the arguments from the proof of
[
14
, Thm. 45.6]

(setting
t=r
therein) where the infimum is realized by using a commuting
quasi-interpolant with prescribed boundary conditions, we infer that

\displaystyle\inf_{{\bm{b}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\|{\bm{A}}-{\bm{b}}_{h}\|_{{\bm{V}}_{\sharp}}\leq{}

\displaystyle c\,\bigg\{\nu_{\sharp}\inf_{{\bm{b}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{c}}_{k,0}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\|{\bm{A}}-{\bm{b}}_{h}\|_{{{\bm{L}}^{2}(D)}}^{2}+\kappa_{\sharp}\inf_{{\bm{d}}_{h}\in{\bm{P}}^{\mathrm{d}}_{k,0}({\mathcal{T}}_{h})}\|\nabla{\times}{\bm{A}}-{\bm{d}}_{h}\|_{{{\bm{L}}^{2}(D)}}^{2}

\displaystyle+\kappa_{\sharp}\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{h}^{\partial}}\Big(h_{K_{-}}^{2r}|\nabla{\times}{\bm{A}}|_{{\bm{H}}^{r}(K_{-})}^{2}+h_{K_{-}}^{2d(\frac{d+2}{2d}-\frac{1}{q})}\|{\bm{f}}-\nu{\bm{A}}\|_{{\bm{L}}^{q}(K_{-})}^{2}\Big)\bigg\}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Combining this estimate with the estimates
(
11
) and (
35
) proves the assertion (as above, we hide
the nondimensional factor
\nu_{\sharp}\kappa_{\sharp}^{-1}\ell_{D}^{2}
in the generic
constant
c
).
∎

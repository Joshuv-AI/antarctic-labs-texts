1
Introduction

The aim of this paper is to prove mean value formulas for degenerate second order partial differential equations in the form

\mathscr{L}u:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}X_{i}\left(a_{ij}X_{j}u\right)+X_{0}u+\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}X_{j}u+cu-\partial_{t}u=f,

(1.1)

in some open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
. In the following,
z=(x,t)=(x_{1},\dots,x_{N},t)
denotes the point in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
,
1\leq m\leq N
and the
X_{j}
’s in (
1.1
) are smooth vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, i.e.,

X_{j}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\varphi_{k}^{j}(x)\partial_{x_{k}},\qquad j=0,\dots,m,

(1.2)

\varphi_{k}^{j}
being
C^{\infty}
functions. Starting from the vector fields
X_{j}
, the operator
\mathscr{L}
in (
1.1
) is written through the
m\times m
symmetric matrix
A(z)=\left(a_{ij}(z)\right)_{i,j=1,\dots,m}
, which has continuous entries and satisfies the usual ellipticity condition: there exists a constant
\Lambda\geq 1
such that

\Lambda^{-1}|{\xi}|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}a_{ij}(z){\xi}_{i}{\xi}_{j}\leq\Lambda|{\xi}|^{2},

(1.3)

for every
z\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
and
{\xi}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. To simplify the notation in the sequel, we still denote by
A
the
(m+1)\times(m+1)
matrix with entries
a_{ij}
for
i,j=1,\ldots,m
and
a_{(m+1)\,j}=a_{j\,(m+1)}=0
, for
j=1,\dots,m+1
. We assume that the coefficients of the vector
b(z)=(b_{1}(z),\dots,b_{m}(z))
and the functions
c
and
f
are bounded and continuous. As we are dealing with classical solutions, we also assume that
X_{i}a_{ij}
and
X_{j}b_{j}
are bounded continuous functions for
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. The reason why we write the operator
\mathscr{L}
in its divergence form is that we need to consider the fundamental solution
\Gamma^{*}
to the adjoint equation
\mathscr{L}^{*}v=0
. In the sequel we denote

X=\left(X_{1},\dots,X_{m}\right),\qquad X_{m+1}=X_{0}-\partial_{t}.

(1.4)

and we let

{\mathfrak{g}}={\rm Lie}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},X_{m+1})

(1.5)

be the Lie algebra generated by
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},X_{m+1}
. The main assumptions on the operator
\mathscr{L}
are listed below.

[H.1]

The vector fields
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m+1}
satisfy the Hörmander rank condition

{\rm rank}\,{\mathfrak{g}}(z)=N+1\qquad\text{for every}\quad z\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}.

(1.6)

[H.2]

there exists a homogeneous Lie group

\mathbb{G}=\left({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1},\circ,{\delta}_{\lambda}\right)
such that

i)

X_{1},\dots,X_{m},X_{m+1}
are
left-translation invariant on
\mathbb{G}
;

ii)

X_{1},\dots,X_{m},X_{m+1}
are
{\delta}_{\lambda}
-homogeneous of degree one;

[H.3]

There exists a fundamental solution
\Gamma^{*}
for the adjoint operator
\mathscr{L}^{*}
:

\mathscr{L}^{*}u:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}X_{i}\left(a_{ij}X_{j}u\right)-X_{0}u-\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}X_{j}u+\Big(c-\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_{j}b_{j}\Big)u+\partial_{t}u,

(1.7)

as stated in Definition
2.1
, having the properties (
2.15
) and (
2.16
).

Let us briefly comment on our hypotheses. We first recall that the Hörmander condition [H.1] implies that the operator

\mathscr{L}_{0}:=\sum_{k=1}^{m}X_{k}^{2}+X_{m+1}

(1.8)

is hypoelliptic. This means that every distributional solutiont
u
to
\mathscr{L}_{0}u=f
in some open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
belongs to
C^{\infty}(\Omega)
and is a classical solution to
\mathscr{L}_{0}u=f
whenever
f
is
C^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Note that
\mathscr{L}_{0}
is an operator of the form (
1.1
) if we choose
A
to be the
m\times m
identity matrix, and
b=0,c=0
. From this point of view, in the setting of the degenerate operators,
\mathscr{L}_{0}
plays the role of the heat operator in the family of the uniformly parabolic operators.

In Section
2
we recall the notation of homogeneous Lie groups
\mathbb{G}=\left({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1},\circ,{\delta}_{\lambda}\right)
and we give necessary and sufficient conditions on the vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{m},X_{m+1}
for the validity of condition [H.2]. In Section
2
we also declare the properties of the
fundamental solution
we need in this note, so that the meaning of condition [H.3] will be clarified.

Mean value formulas are a very classical tool for the treatment of harmonic functions. The first extenstions to the heat operator are due to Pini
[
38
]
and to Watson
[
43
]
. Mean value formulas for uniformly parabolic operators with
C^{\infty}
smooth coefficients have been proved by Fabes and Garofalo
[
20
]
, and by Garofalo and Lanconelli
[
26
]
. More recently, mean value formulas have ben proved in large generality by Cupini and Lanconelli in
[
18
]
, who still consider operators with smooth coefficients. In the recent articles
[
35
]
on uniformly parabolic equations and
[
37
]
, on degenerate elliptic equations, Malagoli and the authors consider differential operators whose coefficients belong to a suitable space of functions
C^{1+\alpha}
and
C^{1+\alpha}_{\mathbb{G}}
, respectively. This reduction of regularity, from
C^{\infty}
to
C^{1+\alpha}
, reflects in weaker regularity of the fundamental solution. As a consequence, the integration by parts on its level sets, which is a crucial point in the proof and for
C^{\infty}
coefficients relies on Sard’s theorem, becomes a delicate issue. In the case of
uniformly parabolic
operators with
C^{1}
coefficients, and then
C^{1}
fundamental solution, in
[
35
]
it is shown that this difficulty can be overcome in two ways, either by using a divergence theorem valid for
almost
C^{1}
-regular boundaries
, or using De Giorgi’s theory of perimeters. In
[
37
]
and in the present case of
degenerate
operators with
C^{1}_{\mathbb{G}}
coefficients the
almost
C^{1}
-regularity
of the level sets of the fundamental solution is not guaranteed: indeed, it is not
C^{1}
in the Euclidean sense. So, we are led to rely on the theory of functions with bounded variation and sets with finite perimeter in stratified groups, see Section
3
, which provides us with the relevant formulation of the divergence theorem. In Section
4
we prove our main results, i.e., the mean value formulas and, as an important consequence, the strong maximum principle. Finally, in Section
5
we show that our assumptions are verified in several important cases, such as degenerate Kolmogorov operators and parabolic operators on Carnot groups.

We conclude this introduction with a remark on the the homogeneity property [H.2]
ii)
. The layers in the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
have different degrees of homogeneity and these are at some extent arbitrary. Assuming for simplicity
X_{0}=0
, the commonest choice when dealing with regularity theory is the
parabolic scaling

\delta_{\lambda}:(x,0)=(\lambda x,0)
for
x
in the first layer, and
\delta_{\lambda}:(0,t)\mapsto(0,\lambda^{2}t)
on the time variable. But, as already done in
[
35
]
for uniformly parabolic operators in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
, the condition [H.2]
ii)
requires that
\delta_{\lambda}(x,t)=(\lambda x,\lambda t)
and we do the same here, with
x
in the first layer. Indeed, the very important property of
\Gamma^{*}
encoded in the equality

u({\xi},{\tau})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Gamma^{*}({\xi},{\tau};x,t){\varphi}(x)dx

cannot be used in connection with the divergence formula when the boundary integral is computed with respect to the Hausdorff measure, if this last is defined through the distance generated by the parabolic scaling. In fact, the surfaces
\{t=constant\}
would have codimension 2 and therefore they would be negligible.

2
Preliminaries and the Main Results

In this section we describe the group structure related to our differential operators and the properties that we require to the fundamental solutions. After that, we collect our hypotheses and state our main results. Further preliminaries concerning
BV
functions and sets with finite perimeter are presented in Section
3
.

Let us specify the meaning of classical solution to the equation
\mathscr{L}u=f
in some open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
. With this aim, we first recall the notion of
Lie derivative
. For any
z_{0}\in{\Omega}
and
j=1,\dots,m+1
we consider a path
\gamma
defined in a neighborhood
I
of the origin, such that

\gamma^{\prime}(s)=X_{j}(\gamma(s)),\qquad\gamma(0)=z_{0}.

Then the Lie derivative
X_{j}u(z_{0})
of
u
at
z_{0}
is

X_{j}u(z_{0}):=\frac{d}{ds}u(\gamma(s))_{\mid s=0}.

(2.1)

We say that
u
is a classical solution to
\mathscr{L}u=f
in some open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
if the Lie derivatives
X_{i}X_{j}u,i,j=1,\dots,m
and
X_{m+1}u
are defined as continuous functions and the differential equation is satisfied at every point of
\Omega
. The meaning of the equation
\mathscr{L}^{*}u=g
is analogous.

Next subsections contain some facts about the notions of Lie group and fundamental solution, that are likely known to the expert readers. We first recall some notation and results, then we state the main goal of this paper.

2.1
Homogeneous Lie groups

A Lie group
\mathbb{G}=\left({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1},\circ\right)
is said
homogeneous
if a family of dilations
\left({\delta}_{\lambda}\right)_{\lambda>0}
exists on
\mathbb{G}
and it is an automorphism of the group:

{\delta}_{\lambda}(z\circ{\zeta})=\left({\delta}_{\lambda}z\right)\circ\left({\delta}_{\lambda}{\zeta}\right),\quad\text{for all}\ z,{\zeta}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}\ \text{and}\ \lambda>0.

The assumptions [H.1] and [H.2] induce a direct sum decomposition of
{\mathfrak{g}}

{\mathfrak{g}}=W_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus W_{\mu},

(2.2)

where

\begin{split}W_{1}=&\text{span}\big\{X_{1},\ldots X_{m},X_{m+1}\big\},\\
W_{k}=&\text{span}\big\{[X_{i},X_{j}]\mid X_{i}\in W_{1},X_{j}\in W_{k-1}\big\},\qquad k=2,\ldots,\mu.\end{split}

Moreover
[X_{i},X_{j}]=0
whenever
X_{i}\in W_{\mu}
, and
X_{j}\in W_{1}
. In the sequel we denote by
m_{j}
the dimension of
W_{j}
, for
j=1,\dots,\mu
. If we represent the dilation
\delta_{\lambda}
on
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
by the following matrix

\delta_{\lambda}={\text{\rm diag}}(\lambda{\mathbb{I}}_{m_{1}},\lambda^{2}{\mathbb{I}}_{m_{2}},\ldots,\lambda^{\mu}{\mathbb{I}}_{m_{\mu}}),

(2.3)

we call
homogeneous dimension
of
\mathbb{G}
the integer
\mathcal{Q}=m_{1}+2\mu_{2}+\cdots+\mu m_{\mu}
and we have

\det\delta_{\lambda}=\lambda^{\mathcal{Q}}.

(2.4)

We endow each fiber of the first layer
W_{1}
with an inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{z}
(and the associated norm
|\cdot|_{z}
) that makes
X_{1}(z),\ldots,X_{m+1}(z)
an orthonormal frame, see
[
24
]
.

We next give some general comments about the differential operators on homegeneous Lie groups considered in this note. An important consequence of the homogeneity of the Lie group is the
pyramid-shaped
structure of the coefficients of the vector fields
X_{j}
’s. In the following we write
x=x^{(1)}+x^{(2)}+\cdots+x^{(k)}
with
x^{(j)}\in W_{j}
, and we use the notation in (
1.2
). As a consequence of the homogeneity of the differential operators
X_{1},\ldots,X_{m+1}
we have that the coefficients
\varphi^{j}_{1},\ldots,\varphi^{j}_{m}
are constant and the coefficients
\varphi^{j}_{m+1},\ldots,\varphi^{j}_{m+m_{2}}
are linear functions of the variable
x^{(1)}
. In general, if
k
is such that
x_{k}\in W_{i}
, then the coefficient
\varphi^{j}_{k}
is a polynomial function of the variable
x^{(1)},\ldots,x^{(i-1)}
. This fact plainly implies that

X_{j}^{*}=-X_{j},\qquad j=1,\dots,m+1.

(2.5)

In particular, the adjoint operator
\mathscr{L}^{*}
acts on sufficiently smooth functions as follows

\mathscr{L}^{*}v:=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}X_{i}\left(a_{ij}X_{j}v\right)-X_{0}v-\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}X_{j}v+\Bigl(c-\sum_{j=1}^{m}X_{j}b_{j}\Bigr)v+\partial_{t}v.

(2.6)

For the same reason,
X_{1},\dots,X_{m+1}
are
complete vector fields
, that is the integral curve of
X_{j}
is defined on the whole of
\mathbb{R}
for
j=1,\dots,m+1
.

We next discuss the problem of the existence of a Lie group
\mathbb{G}
as required in condition [H.2]. In the Examples
5.1
and
5.6
both the Lie group and the differential operators are given. In general, when a stratified Lie group is given, a standard procedure provides us with a family of left-invariant vector fields satisfying the Hörmander condition [H.1].

Vice versa, suppose that a collection of vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{m+1}
satisfying the Hörmander condition [H.1] is given. Then, under some furhter assumptions, it is possible to build a stratified Lie group such that the condition [H.2] is satisfied. Specifically the following result holds true. Suppose that
{\rm Lie}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},X_{m+1})
has dimension
N+1
and that every vector field
X\in{\rm Lie}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},X_{m+1})
is complete. Then there exists a Lie group
\mathbb{G}\left(\mathbb{R}^{N+1},\circ\right)
such that the Lie algebra of
\mathbb{G}
agrees with
{\rm Lie}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m},X_{m+1})
. The abstract version of this result is known as
Third Fundamental Theorem of Lie
, see
[
41
, Theorem 3.15.1]
. We refer to Theorem 1.1 in the article
[
8
]
by Bonfiglioli and Lanconelli for the explicit construction of this Lie group on
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
in the case of vector fields with analytical coefficiens, while
C^{\infty}
vector fields are considered by Biagi and Bonfiglioli in the more recent articles
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
.

We next introduce the distance we use to define the Hausdorff measure related to the notion of perimeter on which the divergence formula we need is based. From
[
25
, Theorem 5.1]
we know that there are constants
\varepsilon_{j}\in]0,1],\ j=1,\ldots,\mu
, with
\varepsilon_{1}=1
, such that the function

z\mapsto\|z\|_{\infty}=\max_{j=1,\ldots,\mu}\{\varepsilon_{j}|z_{j}|^{1/j}\},

(2.7)

where
|z_{j}|
denotes the Euclidean norm of the vector
z_{j}\in W_{j}
, defines a norm and as a consequence the distance

d_{\infty}(z,w)=\|w^{-1}\circ z\|_{\infty}.

(2.8)

We notice that
d_{\infty}
is equivalent to the Carnot-Carathéodory distance (see (
5.18
) below) and that for every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
there exist two positive constants
c_{K}^{-}
and
c_{K}^{+}
, such that

c_{K}^{-}|z-w|\leq d_{\infty}(z,w)\leq c_{K}^{+}|z-w|^{\frac{1}{\mu}},\qquad\text{for all}\ z,w\in K.

(2.9)

The invariance properties

d_{\infty}(\zeta\circ z,\zeta\circ w)=d_{\infty}(z,w),\quad d_{\infty}(\delta_{\lambda}z,\delta_{\lambda}w)=\lambda\,d_{\infty}(z,w),

(2.10)

hold for every
z,w,\zeta
in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
and for every positive
\lambda
, see again
[
25
]
. We next recall the notion of Hölder continuous functions on Lie groups. For
\alpha\in]0,1]
, we say that a function
u
defined on
{\Omega}
is
\alpha
-Hölder continuous, and we write
u\in C_{\mathbb{G}}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
, if there exists a positive constant
M
such that

|u(z)-u(w)|\leq Md_{\infty}(z,w)^{\alpha},\quad\text{for every}\ z,w\in\Omega.

(2.11)

Let us come to some geometric measure theoretical issues. For every
\nu\in W_{1}
, denote by
\nu^{\bot}
the codimension 1 subspace of
W_{1}
orthogonal to
\nu
and introduce the hyperplane
\mathcal{N}=\nu^{\bot}\oplus W_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus W_{\mu}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
and the constant

\theta=\{\mathscr{H}_{e}^{N}(B(0,1)\cap\mathcal{N})\}

(2.12)

where
B(0,1)=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}|\ \|z\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}
and
\mathscr{H}_{e}^{N}
is the
N
-dimensional euclidean Hausdorff measure, see e.g.
[
3
, Section 2.8]
. The constant
\theta
is introduced in
[
34
]
, is called spherical factor and is denoted
\omega_{{\mathbb{G}},\mathcal{Q}-1}
there. Moreover, it is independent of
\nu
because
d_{\infty}
is
vertically symmetric
according to Definition 6.1 in
[
33
]
, see Remark 6.2, Theorem 6.3 and Theorem 5.2 in
[
33
]
. We then recall the definition of the
spherical (
{\mathcal{Q}}-1
)-dimensional Hausdorff measure
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{G}}^{{\mathcal{Q}}-1}
of a Borel set
E
:

{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{G}}^{{\mathcal{Q}}-1}(E)=\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\inf\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{\theta}{2^{{\mathcal{Q}}-1}}({\rm diam}_{\mathbb{G}}(B_{i}))^{{\mathcal{Q}}-1}:B_{i}\text{ balls },\ E\subset\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}B_{i},\ {\rm diam}_{\mathbb{G}}\,(B_{i})\leq r\right\},

where
\theta
is the constant in (
2.12
) and
{\rm diam}_{\mathbb{G}}(B):=\sup_{z,\zeta\in B}d_{\infty}(z,\zeta)
. Notice that we have normalized the measure
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{G}}^{{\mathcal{Q}}-1}
so that
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{G}}^{{\mathcal{Q}}-1}(B(0,1)\cap{\mathcal{N}})=\theta
.

2.2
The fundamental solution

In this subsection we give a precise definition of
fundamental solution

\Gamma^{*}
for the operator
{\mathscr{L}}^{*}
in (
1.7
), we fix the notation for its superlevel sets and we list some further assumptions on
\Gamma^{*}
.

Definition 2.1

We say that a function
{\Gamma}^{*}={\Gamma}^{*}({\zeta};z)
defined for every
({\zeta};z)=({\xi},{\tau};x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}\times\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
with
t>\tau
is a fundamental solution to
\mathscr{L}^{*}v=0
if it satisfies the following conditions for every
z=(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
.

1.

The function
{\Gamma}^{*}(\,\cdot\,;z)
is a classical solution to the equation
\mathscr{L}^{*}\,{\Gamma}^{*}(\cdot\,;z)=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times]-\infty,t[
;

2.

the function
{\Gamma}^{*}(\,\cdot\,;z)
belongs to
L^{1}(K)
for every bounded measurable set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}\times]-\infty,t[
. Moreover, for every
({\xi},{\tau})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times]-\infty,t[
and
{\varphi}\in C_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the function

u({\xi},{\tau}):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Gamma^{*}({\xi},{\tau};x,t){\varphi}(x)dx

is well defined and satisfies

\lim_{({\xi},{\tau})\to(x,t)}u({\xi},{\tau})=\varphi(x)\quad\text{for every}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

We introduce some further notation in order to state our last assumption on
\Gamma^{*}
. Recall that
\mathcal{Q}
denotes the homogeneus dimension of the Lie group
\mathbb{G}
. For every
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
and for every
r>0
, we set

\begin{split}\psi_{r}(z_{0})&:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times]-\infty,t_{0}[\mid\Gamma^{*}(z;z_{0})=\tfrac{1}{r}\right\},\\
\Omega_{r}(z_{0})&:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times]-\infty,t_{0}[\mid\Gamma^{*}(z;z_{0})>\tfrac{1}{r}\right\},\end{split}

(2.13)

and we call
\psi_{r}(z_{0})
and
\Omega_{r}(z_{0})
respectively the
sphere
and the
ball
with radius
r
and
center

z_{0}
. As in the uniformly parabolic setting, here
z_{0}
belongs to the topological boundary of
\Omega_{r}(z_{0})
. We finally set

\mathcal{I}_{r,\varepsilon}(z_{0}):=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid\Gamma^{*}(x,t_{0}-\varepsilon;z_{0})>\tfrac{1}{r}\right\}.

(2.14)

Note that
\mathcal{I}_{r,\varepsilon}(z_{0})\neq\emptyset
only for sufficiently small positive
\varepsilon
. We also rely on the following properties of the fundamental solution

•

For every
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
there exists
r_{0}>0
such that the set

\Omega_{r}(z_{0})\quad\text{is bounded for every}\ r<r_{0};

(2.15)

•

it holds

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\mathscr{H}}_{e}^{N}\left(\mathcal{I}_{r,\varepsilon}(z_{0})\right)=0,\qquad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\mathcal{I}_{r,\varepsilon}(z_{0})}\Gamma^{*}(x,t_{0}-\varepsilon;z_{0})dx=0.

(2.16)

Note that this assumptions is analogous to the
pointwise vanishing integral condition
stated in
[
18
]
as Property
(H(z_{0},\varrho))
.

2.3
Statement of the main results

We define the gradient
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}
and the divergence
{\rm div}_{\mathbb{G}}
as follows. We agree to identify a
section

F=\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}F_{j}X_{j}
with its canonical coodinates
F=\left(F_{1},\dots,F_{m},F_{m+1}\right)
. With this agreement, we denote the gradient of
f\in C^{1}_{\mathbb{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the divergence of
F\in C^{1}_{\mathbb{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}^{m+1})
by

\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f:=\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}(X_{j}f)X_{j}\quad{\rm and}\quad{\rm div}_{\mathbb{G}}F:=-\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}X_{j}^{*}F_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}X_{j}F_{j}.

(2.17)

Moreover

\langle A(z)\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f(z),\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f(z)\rangle_{z}:=\sum_{j,k=1}^{m}a_{jk}(z)X_{j}f(z)X_{k}f(z),

(2.18)

for any
f\in C^{1}_{\mathbb{G}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In the following,
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}
is fixed and
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\Gamma^{*}(z;z_{0})
denotes the gradient with respect to the variable
z
. With this notation, we set

\begin{split}K_{\mathbb{G}}(z_{0};z)&:=\frac{\langle A(z)\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\Gamma^{*}(z;z_{0}),\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\Gamma^{*}(z;z_{0})\rangle_{z}}{|\nabla_{{\mathbb{G}}}\Gamma^{*}(z_{0};z)|_{z}},\\

[H.4]

, and for every
\mathscr{L}
-admissible curve

3
Functions of bounded variation

In this section we introduce some notation and the basic results on functions of bounded variation and sets with finite perimeter that we need to prove our mean value formulas. To simplify the notation, we put
n=N+1
and denote by
\lambda_{n}
the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We also keep the notation
z=(x,t)
for points in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
and recall that the homogeneous dimension of
{\mathbb{G}}
is denoted by
{\mathcal{Q}}
, see (
2.3
), (
2.4
). If
\mu
is a Borel measure and
E
is a Borel set, we use the notation
\mu\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}E(B)=\mu(E\cap B)
.

For an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
we define the space
BV_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega)
of functions of bounded variation in
\mathbb{G}
following
[
14
]
. We refer to
[
3
]
and to
[
25
]
for more information on the Euclidean and the subriemannian case, respectively.

Definition 3.1

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
u\in L^{1}({\Omega})
we define

\left\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\right\|\left(\Omega\right)=\sup\left\{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}u(z)\,\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{G}}g(z)dz:\ g\in C_{c}^{1}\left(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1}\right),\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}\leq 1\right\}.

(3.1)

We say that
u\in BV_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega)
if
\left\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\right\|(\Omega)
is finite.

Remark 3.2

We point out (see
[
25
, Remarks 2.10, 2.19]
) that the (usual) notation
\left\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\right\|\left(\Omega\right)

is somehow misleading, as the total variation depends upon the fixed vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{m+1}
, whereas the functional class
BV_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega)
only depends on
\mathbb{G}
and
\Omega
.

With the same proof contained e.g. in
[
3
, Prop. 3.6]
, it is possible to show that if
u
belongs to
BV_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega)
then its total variation
\left\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\right\|
is a finite positive Radon measure and there is a
\left\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\right\|
-measurable function
\sigma_{u}:\Omega\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1}
such that
|\sigma_{u}(z)|_{z}=1
for
\left\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\right\|
-a.e.
z\in\Omega
and

\int_{{\Omega}}u(z)\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{G}}g(z)dz=\int_{{\Omega}}\langle g,\sigma_{u}\rangle_{z}\,d\left\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\right\|

(3.2)

for all
g\in C_{\mathbb{G},c}^{1}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1})
. We denote by
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u
the vector measure
-\sigma_{u}\left\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\right\|
, so that
X_{j}u
is the measure
(-\sigma_{u})_{j}\left\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\right\|
and the following integration by parts formula holds true

\int_{{\Omega}}u(z)X_{j}g(z)dz=-\int_{{\Omega}}g\left(z\right)d\left(X_{j}u\right)\left(z\right)

(3.3)

for all
g\in C_{\mathbb{G},c}^{1}(\Omega)
.

Definition 3.3
(Sets of finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter)

Let
\chi_{E}
be the characteristic function of the measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
; we say that
E
is a set of finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter in
\Omega
if
\left\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\chi_{E}\right\|(\Omega)
is finite, and we call (generalized inward)
\mathbb{G}
-normal the
(m+1)
-vector

\nu_{E}(z)=-\sigma_{\chi_{E}}(z),

which is defined
\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\chi_{E}\|
-a.e.

As customary, we write
P_{\mathbb{G}}(E,B)
instead of
\left\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\chi_{E}\right\|(B)
for any Borel set
B
. Also, notice that if
A
is open, then

P_{\mathbb{G}}(E\cap A,A)=P_{\mathbb{G}}(E,A),

(3.4)

see (2.25) in
[
25
]
, and recall that

|\nu_{E}(z)|_{z}=1
for
P_{\mathbb{G}}(E)
-a.e.
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
With this notation, (
3.2
) takes the form

\int_{E}\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{G}}g(z)dz=-\int_{{\Omega}}\langle g,\nu_{E}\rangle_{z}dP_{\mathbb{G}}(E),

(3.5)

for all
g\in C_{\mathbb{G},c}^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{m+1})
.

We refer to
[
23
, Theorem 2.3.5]
for a proof of the following statement that connects the total variation of a
BV_{\mathbb{G}}
function with the perimeter of its level sets.

Proposition 3.4
(Coarea formula)

If
u\in BV_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega)
for some open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
then for a.e.

\tau\in\mathbb{R}
the set
E_{\tau}=\{x\in{\Omega}:\ u(x)>\tau\}
has finite
\mathbb{G}
-perimeter in
\Omega
and

\left\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\right\|(\Omega)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}P_{\mathbb{G}}(E_{\tau},\Omega)d\tau.

(3.6)

Conversely, if
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}P_{\mathbb{G}}(E_{\tau},\Omega)d\tau<\infty
, then
u\in BV_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega)
and equality (
3.6
) holds. Moreover, if
g:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Borel function, then

\int_{\Omega}g(z)d\left\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\right\|(z)=\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\int_{\Omega}g(z)dP_{\mathbb{G}}(E_{\tau})(z)d\tau.

(3.7)

Let us come to some finer properties of
BV_{\mathbb{G}}
functions and perimeters. In order to put formula (
3.5
) in a form closer to the classical one we define the
measuretheoretic
or
essential
boundary.

Definition 3.5
(Essential boundary)

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a measurable set. We say that
z\in\partial_{\mathbb{G}}^{*}E
if

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\lambda_{n}(B_{r}(z)\cap E)}{\lambda_{n}(B_{r}(z))}>0,\qquad\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\lambda_{n}(B_{r}(z)\setminus E)}{\lambda_{n}(B_{r}(z))}>0

and we call
\partial_{\mathbb{G}}^{*}E
the measuretheoretic or essential boundary of
E
.

It is immediately checked that
\partial_{\mathbb{G}}^{*}E\subset\partial E
. Observe that two different but equivalent distances on
{\mathbb{G}}
give the same essential boundary.

Let us see that the divergence theorem (
3.5
) can be rewritten in a form much closer to the classical formula, see
[
2
, Theorems 5.3, 5.4]
, where the problem is settled in general metric measure spaces, and
[
4
, Theorem 4.16]
.

Theorem 3.6

Given a set of finite
{\mathbb{G}}
-perimeter
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for
P_{\mathbb{G}}(E,\cdot)
-a.e.
z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
there is
\bar{r}(z)>0
such that

\ell_{\mathbb{G}}r^{{\mathcal{Q}}-1}\leq P_{\mathbb{G}}(E,B(z,r))\leq L_{\mathbb{G}}r^{{\mathcal{Q}}-1}

for every
r<\bar{r}(z)
, where
0<\ell_{\mathbb{G}}\leq L_{\mathbb{G}}<\infty
are two constants depending only on the group. As a consequence,
P_{\mathbb{G}}(E,\cdot)
is concentrated on
\partial_{\mathbb{G}}^{*}E
, i.e.,
P_{\mathbb{G}}(E,{\mathbb{G}}\setminus\partial_{\mathbb{G}}^{*}E)=0
, and
there is a Borel function

\beta_{E}:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to[\ell_{\mathbb{G}},L_{\mathbb{G}}]
such that

P_{\mathbb{G}}(E,B)=\int_{B\cap\partial_{\mathbb{G}}^{*}E}\beta_{E}(z)\,d{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{G}}^{{\mathcal{Q}}-1}(z),\quad\forall B\text{\ \ Borel set}.

(3.8)

The above theorem allows us to rewrite formula (
3.5
) as an integral on the essential boundary with respect to the
({\mathcal{Q}}-1)
-dimensional spherical Hausdorff measure as follows:

\int_{E}\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{G}}g(z)dz=-\int_{\partial_{\mathbb{G}}^{\ast}E}\langle g,\nu_{E}\rangle_{z}\,\beta_{E}(z)\,d{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{G}}^{{\mathcal{Q}}-1}.

(3.9)

In the following remarks we collect some useful results proved by Franchi, Serapioni and Serra Cassano
[
23
, Theorem 2.3.5]
and V. Magnani
[
33
]
on functions belonging to
C^{1}_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega)
, for which much more information is available.

Remark 3.7

If
\Omega
is bounded, a function
u
in
C^{1}_{\mathbb{G}}(\Omega)
also belongs to
BV_{{\mathbb{G}},{\rm loc}}(\Omega)
and by (
3.3
) the equalities

\int_{\Omega}X_{j}^{*}g(z)u(z)dz=\int_{\Omega}g(z)X_{j}u(z)dz,\qquad j=1,\ldots,m+1,

hold for every

. Recalling (

), we find that the measure derivative of

is
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\,\lambda_{n}
. Moreover, we say that

is a
{\mathbb{G}}
-regular surface if for any
p\in S
there are an open neighborhood

of

and

such that

S\cap U=\{z\in U:\ f(z)=0\ \textrm{and}\ \nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f(z)\neq 0\}.

Let
\Omega
be an open subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

,
E=\{f<0\}
,
S=\{f=0\}
, and let

be such that

and

. Then, as proved in
[
24

, there is a neighborhood

of

such that

has finite perimeter and

\nu_{E}(z)=-\frac{\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f(z)}{|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}f(z)|_{z}},\qquad z\in S\cap U.

(3.10)

In such a situation the equality

holds, see
[
24

. Notice also that the topological dimension of a

-regular surface is

Remark 3.8

If

We end this section with a variant of the

Lemma 3.9

Let
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a set of finite
{\mathbb{G}}
-perimeter. Then, for
\lambda_{1}
-a.e.
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}
the set
E\cap\{t<\tau\}
has finite
{\mathbb{G}}
-perimeter and for every Borel set
B
we have

\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\chi_{E\cap\{t<\tau\}}(B)=\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\chi_{E}(B\cap\{t<\tau\})-\lambda_{N}(B\cap\{t=\tau\})e_{N+1}.

Proof.
Setting
u_{s}(x,t)=\{[(\tau-t)/s)\vee 0]\wedge 1\}\chi_{E}(x,t)
, we have
u_{s}\to\chi_{E\cap\{t<\tau\}}
in
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
as
s\downarrow 0
, whence by the semicontinuity of the total variation,

P_{\mathbb{G}}(E\cap\{t<\tau\})\leq\liminf_{s\downarrow 0}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u_{s}\|({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

Now we compute

\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u_{s}=\{[(\tau-t)/s)\vee 0]\wedge 1\}\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\chi_{E}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}\{t<\tau-s\}-\frac{1}{s}\lambda_{n}\mathbin{\vrule height=6.88889pt,depth=0.0pt,width=0.55974pt\vrule height=0.55974pt,depth=0.0pt,width=5.59721pt}(E\cap\{\tau-s<t<\tau\})e_{N+1}

hence, setting
m(\tau)=\lambda_{n}(E\cap\{t<\tau\})
, we have

\limsup_{s\downarrow 0}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u_{s}\|({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\leq\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\chi_{E}\|(\{t<\tau\})+m^{\prime}(\tau),

where
m^{\prime}
denotes the right derivative of
m
, which is finite for
\lambda_{1}
-a.e.
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. This proves that
E\cap\{t<\tau\}
has finite
{\mathbb{G}}
-perimeter for
\lambda_{1}
-a.e.
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
. Therefore, since by (
3.4
)

P_{\mathbb{G}}(E\cap\{t<\tau\},\{t<\tau\})=P_{\mathbb{G}}(E,\{t<\tau\}),

and
{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{G}}^{{\mathcal{Q}}-1}(\partial_{\mathbb{G}}^{*}E\cap\{t=\tau\})=0
for a.e.
\tau
, we have

\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\chi_{E\cap\{t<\tau\}}(B)=\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\chi_{E}(B\cap\{t<\tau\})+\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\chi_{E}(B\cap\{t=\tau\})

for any Borel set
B
and
\lambda_{1}
-a.e.
\tau
. But,
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\chi_{E}(z)=-e_{N+1}{\mathcal{S}}_{\mathbb{G}}^{{\mathcal{Q}}-1}=-e_{N+1}{\mathcal{L}}^{N}
for
z\in E\cap\{t=\tau\}
and the thesis follows.

\square

5
Examples

In this section we list several examples of well-known and important operators verifying the hypotheses of our results, that basically rely on a suitable group structure and on the existence and the properties of the fundamental solution. As said in the Introduction, we warn the reader that the natural dilation operator used in the regularity theory is not the same we use here to prove mean value formulas, and in each example both are described. The homogeneous Lie groups in the examples we are going to present are the Carnot groups and the Kolmogorov groups. We refer to the monograph
[
9
]
for a detailed treatment of the subject of Carnot groups, and to the survey article
[
5
]
for the homogeneous Kolmogorov groups. Next, we check that our hypotheses on the fundamental solutions hold true, recalling in each case the relevant known results on existence and estimates. Concerning the problem of its existence, we recall that Levi’s parametrix method provides us with the existence of a fundamental solution for non-divergence operators with Hölder continuous coefficients in the setting of uniformly elliptic and parabolic operators. This method has been extended to the case of
heat operators on Carnot groups
whose prototypical case is the Heisenberg group in Example
5.1
and to
degenerate Kolmogorov operators,
described in Example
5.6
, which are two large classes of operators to which our results apply.

Example 5.1

Sublaplacian and parabolic operator on the Heisenberg group.

The Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}=(\mathbb{R}^{2n+1},\cdot)
is defined by the composition law

(x,y,s)\cdot(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},s^{\prime})=\Big(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},s+s^{\prime}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}(x_{j}^{\prime}y_{j}-x_{j}y_{j}^{\prime})\Big).

(5.1)

The dilation

\widetilde{\delta}_{\lambda}(x,y,s)=\big(\lambda x,\lambda y,\lambda^{2}s\big)

(5.2)

is an automorphism of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
and induces a direct sum decomposition on
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}

\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2},

(5.3)

where
V_{1}=\{(x,y,0)\mid x,y,\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}
and
V_{2}=\{=(0,0,s)\mid s\in\mathbb{R}\}
. The vector fields

X_{j}=\partial_{x_{j}}+2y_{j}\partial_{s},\quad Y_{j}=\partial_{y_{j}}-2x_{j}\partial_{s},\quad j=1,\dots,n,

(5.4)

are left-invariant on
(\mathbb{H}^{n},\cdot)
. Moreover

[X_{j},Y_{j}]=-4\partial_{s},\quad j=1,\dots,n,

while

[X_{j},X_{k}]=[Y_{j},Y_{k}]=[X_{j},Y_{k}]=0,\quad j,k=1,\dots,n.

In particular,
X_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
evaluated at
(x,y,s)=(0,0,0)
is a basis of
V_{1}
and the Lie algebra generated
by
X_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
, evaluated at any point of
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
, agrees with
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
. The homogeneous dimension of the Heisenberg group is
\mathcal{Q}_{H}=2n+2
. The differential operator

\Delta_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(X_{j}^{2}+Y_{j}^{2}\big),

(5.5)

is said
sub-Laplacian
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

The homogeneous Lie group
\mathbb{G}=\left(\mathbb{R}^{2n+2},\circ,({\delta}_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}\right)
relevant to the heat operator on the Heisenberg group,

\mathcal{H}_{0}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\big(X_{j}^{2}+Y_{j}^{2}\big)-\partial_{t},

(5.6)

is defined by the composition

(x,y,s,t)\circ(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},s^{\prime},t^{\prime})=\Big((x,y,s)\cdot(x^{\prime}y^{\prime},s^{\prime}),t+t^{\prime}\Big),

(5.7)

and by the dilation

{\delta}_{\lambda}(x,y,s,t)=\big(\widetilde{\delta}_{\lambda}(x,y,s),\lambda t\big).

(5.8)

Here
W_{1}=\{(x,y,0,t)\mid x,y,\in\mathbb{R}^{n},t\in\mathbb{R}\}
and
W_{2}=\{=(0,0,s,0)\mid s\in\mathbb{R}\}
, and the homogeneous dimension is
\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}_{H}+1
.
Note that the
parabolic scaling

\hat{\delta}_{\lambda}(x,y,s,t)=\big(\widetilde{\delta}_{\lambda}(x,y,s),\lambda^{2}t\big)

(5.9)

is commonly used in the regularity theory for the solutions to
\mathcal{H}_{0}u=f
. In this framework, the vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{n},Y_{1},\dots,Y_{n}
are homogeneous of degree
1
with respect to the dilation
({\delta}_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}
, while the derivative
\partial_{t}
is homogeneous of degree
2
, as usual in the case of parabolic operators. The homogeneous dimension of the group, with respect to
\big(\hat{\delta}_{\lambda}\big)_{\lambda>0}
, is
\mathcal{Q}_{H}+2
, because of the different role played by the time variable
t
.

\square

(5.10)

Example 5.6

Degenerate Kolmogorov operators.

The simplest degenerate Kolmogorov operator defined for

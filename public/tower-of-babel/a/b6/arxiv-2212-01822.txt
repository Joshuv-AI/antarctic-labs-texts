Flow by Gauss curvature to the L p L_{p} -Gaussian Minkowski problem Thanks: This paper is a revised version of an earlier paper submitted on August 21, 2021. In this new version we added Theorem 1.4 for the case − n − 1 < p ≤ 0 -n-1<p\leq 0 and made some revisions.
Weimin Sheng, Ke Xue

Abstract
. In this paper we study the L p L_{p} -Gaussian Minkowski problem, which arises in the L p L_{p} -Brunn-Minkowski theory in Gaussian probability space. We use Aleksandrov’s variational method with Lagrange multipliers to prove the existence of the logarithmic Gauss Minkowski problem. We construct a suitable Gauss curvature flow of closed, convex hypersurfaces in the Euclidean space ℝ n + 1 \mathbb{R}^{n+1} , and prove its long-time existence and converges smoothly to a smooth solution of the normalized L p L_{p} Gaussian Minkowski problem in cases of p > 0 p>0 and − n − 1 < p ≤ 0 -n-1<p\leq 0 with even prescribed function respectively. We also provide a parabolic proof in the smooth category to the L p L_{p} -Gaussian Minkowski problem in cases of p ≥ n + 1 p\geq n+1 and 0 < p < n + 1 0<p<n+1 with even prescribed function, respectively.

1.
Introduction

The Minkowski problem is a characterization problem for a geometric measure generated
by convex bodies. The study of the Minkowski problem has a long history and its resolution has led to a series of influential works, such as Minkowski
[
19
]
, Lewy
[
15
]
, Nirenberg
[
21
]
, Pogorelov
[
22
]
and Cheng-Yau
[
6
]
, etc.. The
L_{p}
surface
area measure and its associated Minkowski problem in the
L_{p}
Brunn-Minkowski theory
were introduced by Lutwak
[
18
]
in 1993. Since then, the
L_{p}
-Minkowski problem
has been studied extensively in
[
11
,
7
,
2
,
8
,
12
,
25
,
3
,
24
]
, and so on.
Recently, Huang-Xi-Zhao in
[
10
]
introduced a Minkowski problem in Gaussian probability space. In order to explain the problem, we introduce some notations as follows.

We say
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is a convex body if it is a compact convex set with nonempty
interior. Denote by
\mathcal{K}
the set of all convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
containing the origin
O
,
\mathcal{K}_{0}
the convex bodies that contain the origin in interior, and
\mathcal{K}_{e}
the
O
-symmetry convex bodies.
For
K\in\mathcal{K}
, we define its radial function
r_{K}:S^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
and support function
h_{K}:S^{n}\rightarrow[0,\infty)
, respectively, by

r_{K}(x)=\max\{a\in\mathbb{R}:a\cdot x\in K\}\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,h_{K}(x)=\max\{x\cdot y:y\in K\},\,\,x\in S^{n},

where
x\cdot y
denotes the inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.

For
K\in\mathcal{K}
, let
\partial K
be its boundary.
The Gauss map of
\partial K
, denoted by
\nu_{K}:\partial K\rightarrow S^{n}
is defined as follows:
for
y\in\partial K
,

\nu_{K}(y)=\{x\in S^{n}:x\cdot y=h_{K}(x)\}.

Let
\nu_{K}^{-1}:S^{n}\rightarrow\partial K

be the reverse Gauss map such that

\nu_{K}^{-1}(x)=\{y\in S^{n}:x\cdot y=h_{K}(x)\},\,x\in S^{n}.

We often omit the subscript
K
in
r_{K}
,
h_{K}
,
\nu_{K}
,
\nu_{K}^{-1}
,
if no confusion occurs.

Now the Gaussian volume functional, denoted by
\gamma_{n+1}
, is defined by

\gamma_{n+1}(K)=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n+1}}\int_{K}e^{\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}}dx.

Using the variational formula from
[
9
]
, the variational formula of
\gamma_{n+1}
is
obtained as

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{\gamma_{n+1}(K+tL)-\gamma_{n+1}(K)}{t}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}h_{L}(v)dS_{\gamma_{n+1},K}(v),

for any convex bodies
K,L\in\mathcal{K}
, where
K+t\cdot L=\{x+ty:x\in K,y\in L\}
, and
S_{\gamma_{n+1},K}
is the Gaussian surface area measure,

S_{\gamma_{n+1},K}(\eta)=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n+1}}\int_{\nu_{K}^{-1}(\eta)}e^{\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}(x),\,\,\eta\subset S^{n}.

The Gaussian Minkowski problem asks that given a finite Borel measure
\mu
, what are
the necessary and sufficient conditions on
\mu
so that there exists a convex body
K
with the origin

o\in int(K)
such that

\mu=S_{\gamma_{n+1},K}?

If
K
exists, to what extent is it unique?

It is shown in
[
10
]
that for any finite even Borel measure
\mu
on
S^{n}
which is not concentrated
on any closed hemisphere and
|\mu|<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}
, there exists a unique origin-symmetry
convex body with
\gamma_{n+1}(K)>\frac{1}{2}
such that
S_{\gamma_{n+1},K}=\mu
.

Gaussian Minkowski problem is different from the Minkowski
problem in Lebesgue measure space in the following aspects. By the works of Ball
[
1
]

and Nazarov
[
20
]
, the allowable
\mu
in the Gaussian Minkowski problem cannot have an arbitrarily big total mass. In fact the Gaussian surface
area of any convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is not more than
4(n+1)^{\frac{1}{4}}
.
Furthermore, by the definition of Gaussian surface area, we can know that not only
small convex body, but also large one, can has smaller Gaussian surface areas. For instance, let
B_{r}
be a centered ball with radius
r
, the Gaussian area density of
B_{r}
is
f_{r}=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n+1}}e^{\frac{-r^{2}}{2}}r^{n}
. One can observe that
\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n+1}}e^{\frac{-r^{2}}{2}}r^{n}\rightarrow 0
as
r\rightarrow 0
or
r\rightarrow\infty
. It is easy to see
f_{r}<\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n+1}}
when
n<3
. Secondly, Gaussian probability measure has neither
translation invariance nor homogeneity.

It is natural to introduce the
L_{p}
-Brunn-Minkowski theory in Gaussian probability
space, see
[
17
]
. The
L_{p}
-Gaussian surface area measures can be defined as

S_{p,\gamma_{n+1},K}(\eta)=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n+1}}\int_{\nu_{K}^{-1}(\eta)}e^{\frac{-|x|^{2}}{2}}(x\cdot\nu_{K}(x))^{1-p}d\mathcal{H}^{n}(x).

The
L_{p}
-Gaussian Minkowski problem can be stated as follows:

Given a finite Borel measure
\mu
on

S^{n}
, what are the necessary and sufficient conditions on
\mu
does there exist a convex
body
K
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
such that
L_{p}
-Gaussian surface area measure
S_{p,\gamma_{n+1},K}(\cdot)=\mu(\cdot)
? If
K

exists, is it unique?

If
f
is the density function of the given measure
\mu
, the
L_{p}
-Gaussian Minkowski problem becomes the following Monge-Ampère equation on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
:

(1.1)

\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n+1}}e^{-{\frac{|\nabla h|^{2}+h^{2}}{2}}}h^{1-p}\det{(h_{ij}+h\delta_{ij})}=f,

where
h
,
f
are the support function and the prescribed function, respectively.

In
[
17
]
, Liu has proved the following result.

Theorem A

Let
\alpha\in(0,1)
, for
p\geq 1
,
f\in C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
is a positive even function and satisfies
|f|_{L_{1}}<\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}r^{-p}ae^{\frac{-a^{2}}{2}}
, where
r
and
a
are chosen such that
\gamma_{n+1}(rB)=\gamma_{n+1}(P)=\frac{1}{2}
, symmetry trip
P=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:|x_{1}|\leq a\}
. Then
there exists a unique
C^{4,\alpha}
convex body
K\in\mathcal{K}^{n+1}_{e}
with
\gamma_{n+1}(K)>\frac{1}{2}
, its support function
h_{K}
satisfies the equation (
1.1
).

In this paper, we will improve the result in Theorem
1.5
.

Since the
L_{p}
Gaussian surface area measures has neither translation invariance nor homogeneity, we may also consider the normalized
L_{p}
-Gaussian Minkowski problem in the following way:

Problem 1.1
.

Given a finite Borel measure
\mu
on

S^{n}
, what are the necessary and sufficient conditions on
\mu
does there exist a convex
body K in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and a positive constant
c
such that
\mu(\cdot)=cS_{p,\gamma_{n+1},K}(\cdot)
?

Obviously, this problem becomes the following Monge-Ampère equation on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
:

(1.2)

ce^{-{\frac{|\nabla h|^{2}+h^{2}}{2}}}h^{1-p}\det{(h_{ij}+h\delta_{ij})}=f,

where
f
is the density of the measure
\mu
.

In
[
17
]
, by use of Aleksandrov’s variational method, Liu has proved

Theorem B

For
p>0
, let
\mu
be a nonzero finite Borel measure on
S^{n}
and be not concentrated in any closed hemisphere. Then there exist a convex body
K\in\mathcal{K}_{0}
and a positive constant
\lambda
such that

\mu=\frac{\lambda}{p}S_{p,\gamma_{n+1},K}.

In this paper, we study the case
p=0
, i. e. the logarithmic Gauss Minkowski problem. We will use Aleksandrov’s variational method to prove the existence of solution with Lagrange
multipliers.

Definition 1.1
.

A finite Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is said to satisfy
the subspace concentration
inequality
if, for every subspace
\xi
of
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, such that
0<\dim\xi<n+1
,

(1.3)

\mu(\xi\cap\mathbb{S}^{n})\leq\frac{1}{n+1}\mu(\mathbb{S}^{n})\dim\xi.

The measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is said to satisfy
the strict subspace concentration inequality
if
the inequality in (
1.3
) is strict for each subspace
\xi\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and
0<\dim\xi<n+1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
p=0
, if
\mu
is even finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
that satisfies the strict subspace concentration inequality. Then there exist an
O
-symmetric convex body K and a positive constant
c
such that

\mu=cS_{p,\gamma_{n+1},K}.

We also use the following normalised Gauss curvature flow to solve the existence of smooth solution for (
1.2
). Let
M_{0}
be a smooth, closed and convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
which encloses the origin,
n\geq 1
. We study following curvature flow

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\partial X}{\partial t}(x,t)=-\theta(t)e^{\frac{r^{2}}{2}}K(x,t){\langle X,\nu\rangle}^{p}f(\nu)\nu+X(x,t),\\
X(x,0)=X_{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

where
K(\cdot,t)
is the Gauss curvature of hypersurface
M_{t}
, parametrized by
X(\cdot,t):\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
,
\nu(\cdot,t)
is the unit outer normal at
X(\cdot,t)
, and
f
is a given positive smooth function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
r=|X(x,t)|
the distance from the point
X(x,t)
to the origin, and
\theta(t)
is defined as

\theta(t)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}e^{-\frac{r^{2}}{2}}r^{n+1}(\xi,t)d\xi\bigg/\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}h^{p}(x,t)f(x)dx.

r
can be regarded as a function of
\xi=\xi(x,t):=X(x,t)/|X(x,t)|\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, which we call it as the radial function.
The following functional plays an important role in our argument

(1.5)

\Psi(M_{t})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f(x)h^{p}(x,t)dx,\ \text{if}\ p\neq 0.\\
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f(x)\log h(x,t)dx,\ \text{if}\ p=0.\end{array}\right.

where
h(\cdot,t)
is the support function of
M_{t}
.
We have the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M_{0}
be a smooth, closed, uniformly convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
enclosing the origin, and
\gamma_{n+1}(\Omega_{0})\geq\frac{1}{2}
, where
\Omega_{0}
denotes the convex body enclosed by
M_{0}
. Let
f
be a smooth positive function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
p>0
. Then the normalised flow (
1.4
) has a
unique smooth solution
M_{t}=X(\mathbb{S}^{n},t)
for any time, and a subsequence of
M_{t}
converge smoothly to the smooth solution of (
1.2
) with
\frac{1}{c}=\lim_{t_{i}\rightarrow\infty}\theta(t_{i})>0
.

When
-n-1<p\leq 0
, we can aslo obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
M_{0}
be as in Theorem
1.3
and
M_{0}
is origin-symmetric. Let
f
be a smooth positive even function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
-n-1<p\leq 0
. Then the normalised flow (
1.4
) has a
unique smooth solution
M_{t}=X(\mathbb{S}^{n},t)
for any time, and a subsequence of
M_{t}
converge smoothly to the smooth solution of (
1.2
) with
\frac{1}{c}=\lim_{t_{i}\rightarrow\infty}\theta(t_{i})>0
.

Next, we use the Gauss curvature flow (
1.4
) to study the
L_{p}
-Gaussian Minkowski problem (
1.1
). We take another value
\theta(t)=(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n+1}
, then the flow (

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\partial X}{\partial t}(x,t)=-(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n+1}e^{\frac{r^{2}}{2}}K(x,t){\langle X,\nu\rangle}^{p}f(\nu)\nu+X(x,t)\\
X(x,0)=X_{0}(x)\end{array}\right.

We consider the following functional in our argument.

(1.7)

\Phi(M_{t})=\frac{1}{p}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}f(x)h^{p}(x,t)dx-\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n+1}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}d\xi\int_{0}^{r(\xi,t)}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{2}}s^{n}ds,\ \text{if}\ p\neq 0,

where
h(\cdot,t)
and
r(\cdot,t)
are the support function and radial function of

, respectively.
By studying this functional and the a priori estimates of the solutions to the flow (

.

Let

be a smooth, closed, uniformly convex hypersurface in

is a smooth positive function on

or

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we continue to give a brief review of some relevant notions about convex bodies and recall some basic properties of convex hypersurfaces.
Let

C^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
and
C^{+}_{e}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
be the sets of the positive and positive even functions defined on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, repectively.
Let
h\in C^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
, the Wulff shape
[h]
generated by
h
is a convex body defined by

[h]=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:x\cdot v\leq h(v),\forall v\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\}.

Obviously, if
K\in\mathcal{K}_{o}
,
[h_{K}]=K
.

For a compact convex subset
K\in\mathcal{K}
and
v\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
, the supporting hyperplane
H(K,v)
of
K
at
v
is given by

H(K,v)=\{x\in K:x\cdot v=h_{K}(v)\}.

The boundary point of
K
which only has one supporting hyperplane called regularity
point, otherwise, it is a singular point. The set of singular points is denoted as
\sigma_{K}
, it
is well known that
\sigma_{K}
has spherical Lebesgue measure
0
.

For
x\in\partial K\backslash\sigma_{K}
, its Gauss map
\nu_{K}
is represented by

\nu_{K}(x)=\{v\in\mathbb{S}^{n}:x\cdot v=h_{K}(v)\}.

Corresponding, for a Borel set
\eta\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
, its reverse Gauss map is denoted by
\nu_{K}^{-1}
,

\nu_{K}^{-1}(\eta)=\{x\in\partial K:\nu_{K}(x)\in\eta\}.

For the Borel set
\eta\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
, its surface area measure is defined as

S_{K}(\eta)=\mathcal{H}^{n}(\nu_{K}^{-1}(\eta)),

where
\mathcal{H}^{n}
is
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure.

For a Borel set
\omega\subset\mathbb{S}^{n}
,
\bm{\alpha}_{K}(\omega)
denotes its radial Gauss image and is defined as

\bm{\alpha}_{K}(\omega)=\{v\in\mathbb{S}^{n}:r_{K}(u)(u\cdot v)=h_{K}(v)\quad\text{for some}\ u\in\omega\},

when the Borel set
\omega
has only one element
u
, we will abbreviate
\bm{\alpha}_{K}(\{u\})
as
\bm{\alpha}_{K}(u)
. The subset of
\mathbb{S}^{n}
which make
\bm{\alpha}_{K}(u)
contain more than one element denoted by
\omega_{K}
for each
u\in\omega
. The set
\omega_{K}
has spherical Lebesgue measure
0
.

The radial Guass map of
K
is a map denoted by
\alpha_{K}(u)
, the only difference between
\alpha_{K}(u)
and
\bm{\alpha}_{K}(u)
is that the former is defined on
\mathbb{S}^{n}\backslash\omega_{K}
not on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
which lead
to
\bm{\alpha}_{K}(u)
may have many elements but
\alpha_{K}(u)
has only one. In other words, if
\bm{\alpha}_{K}(u)=\{v\}
, then
\bm{\alpha}_{K}(u)=\alpha_{K}(u)
.

Let
M
be a smooth, closed, uniformly convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
Assume that
M
is parametrized by the inverse Gauss map
X:\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow M
. The support function
h:\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of
M
is defined by

(2.1)

h(x)=\sup\left\{{\langle x,y\rangle}:y\in M\right\}.

The supremum is attained at a point
y
such that
x
is the outer normal of
M
at
y
. it is easy to check that

(2.2)

y=h(x)x+\nabla h(x),

where
\nabla
is the covariant derivative with respect to the standard matric
e_{ij}
of the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Here

(2.3)

r=|y|=\sqrt{h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}}.

The second fundamental form of
M
is given that

(2.4)

b_{ij}=h_{ij}+he_{ij},

where
h_{ij}=\nabla_{ij}^{2}h
denotes the second order covariant derivative of
h
with respect to the spherical metric
e_{ij}
. By Weingarten’s formula,

(2.5)

e_{ij}={\langle\frac{\partial\nu}{\partial x^{i}},\frac{\partial\nu}{\partial x^{j}}\rangle}=b_{ik}g^{kl}b_{jl},

where
g_{ij}
is the metric of
M
and
g^{ij}
its inverse. It follows from (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) that the principal radii of curvature of
M
, under a smooth local orthonormal frame on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, are the eigenvalues of the matrix

(2.6)

b_{ij}=h_{ij}+h\delta_{ij}.

The Gauss curvature is given by

(2.7)

K=\frac{1}{\det(h_{ij}+h\delta_{ij})}=S_{n}^{-1}(h_{ij}+h\delta_{ij}),

where

S_{k}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdot\cdot\cdot<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdot\cdot\cdot\lambda_{i_{k}},

denotes the
k
-th elementary symmetric polynomial of
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},...,\lambda_{n})
.

Let
X(\cdot,t)
be a smooth solution to the flow (
1.4
) and
h(\cdot,t)
its support function, then the flow (
1.4
) can be reduced to the initial value problem of the support function
h
:

(2.8)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}(x,t)=-\theta(t)e^{\frac{r^{2}}{2}}K(x,t)h^{p}f+h(x,t),\\
h(x,0)=h_{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

where
r=\sqrt{h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}}(x,t)
,
h_{0}
is the support function of the initial hypersurface
M_{0}
.

Since
M
encloses the origin, it can be parametrized via the radial function
r:\mathbb{S}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

M=\left\{r(\xi)\xi:\xi\in\mathbb{S}^{n}\right\}.

Then the following formulea are well-known:

(2.9)

\nu=\frac{r\xi-\nabla r}{\sqrt{r^{2}+|\nabla r|^{2}}},

and

(2.10)

\begin{array}[]{l}\displaystyle g_{ij}=r^{2}e_{ij}+r_{i}r_{j},\\
\displaystyle b_{ij}=\frac{r^{2}e_{ij}+2r_{i}r_{j}-rr_{ij}}{\sqrt{r^{2}+|\nabla r|^{2}}}.\end{array}

Set

(2.11)

3.
The variational method to generate solution

By employing variational method, we study the
L_{p}
-Gaussian Minkowski problem for
p=0

S_{p,\gamma_{n+1},K}
for

Consider the following minimum problem:

\min\left\{\phi(Q):Q\in\mathcal{K}_{e},\gamma_{n+1}(Q)=\frac{1}{2}\right\},

where
\phi:\mathcal{K}_{e}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by

\phi(Q)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\log h_{Q}(v)d\mu(v).

Lemma 3.1
.

If
K\in\mathcal{K}_{e}
and satisfies

\phi(K)=\min\left\{\phi(Q):Q\in\mathcal{K}_{e},\gamma_{n+1}(Q)=\frac{1}{2}\right\},

then this fact is equivalent to

\varphi(h_{K})=\min\left\{\varphi(z):z\in C_{e}^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n}),\gamma_{n+1}([z])=\frac{1}{2}\right\},

where
\varphi:C_{e}^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is defined by

\varphi(z)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\log z(v)d\mu(v).

Proof.

It is easy to get
h_{[z]}\leq z
and
[h_{[z]}]=[z]
from the definition of the Wulff shape, thus we have

\varphi(z)\geq\varphi(h_{[z]}).

Clearly,
K\in\mathcal{K}_{e}
and satisfies

\phi(K)=\min\left\{\phi(Q):Q\in\mathcal{K}_{e},\gamma_{n+1}(Q)=\frac{1}{2}\right\},

if and only if

\varphi(h_{K})=\min\left\{\varphi(z):z\in C_{e}^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n}),\gamma_{n+1}([z])=\frac{1}{2}\right\}.

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mu
be a nonzero finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
and
p=0
. If
K\in\mathcal{K}_{e}
and satisfies

\phi(K)=\min\left\{\phi(Q):Q\in\mathcal{K}_{e},\gamma_{n+1}(Q)=\frac{1}{2}\right\},

such that
\mu=cS_{p,\gamma_{n+1},K}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.1
we get

(3.1)

\varphi(h_{K})=\min\left\{\varphi(z):z\in C_{e}^{+}(\mathbb{S}^{n}),\gamma_{n+1}([z])=\frac{1}{2}\right\}.

For any
g\in C^{+}_{e}(\mathbb{S}^{n})
and

where
\delta>0
is sufficiently small, let

h_{t}(v)=h_{K}(v)\cdot e^{tg(v)}.

So (
3.1
) implies that
h_{K}
being a minimizer. By the Lagrange multipliers argument, we may conclude that there exists a constant
c>0
such that

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\varphi(h_{t})=c\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\gamma_{n+1}([h_{t}]).

From the variational formula (

), we have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}g(v)d\mu(v)=c\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}g(v)dS_{0,\gamma_{n+1},K}.

By the arbitrariness of

Lemma 3.3

1.6
) and the equation (
2.15
) is uniformly parabolic. By the
L^{\infty}
-norm estimates and gradient estimates in Lemmata
5.1
,
5.2
and
5.3
, we can obtain the Hölder continuity of
\nabla^{2}h
and
h_{t}
by Krylov’s theory
[
14
]
. Estimate for higher derivatives follows from the standard regularity theory of uniformly parabolic equations. Hence we obtain the long time existence and regularity of solution for the flow (
1.6
). The uniqueness of the smooth solution to (
2.15
) follows by the parabolic comparison principle. We obtain the following theorem:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
M_{0}
be a smooth, closed, uniformly convex hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
enclosing the origin. Let
f
is a smooth positive function on
\mathbb{S}^{n}
, then flow (
1.6
) has a unique smooth, uniformly convex solution
M_{t}
for all time, if one of the following is satisfied

(i)

p>n+1
,

(ii)

p=n+1
and
f<\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n+1}}
,

(iii)

0<p<n+1
,
M_{t}
is origin-symmetric as long as the flow exists and
f
is even function satisfying
(
1.8
).

Moreover we have the a priori estimates

||h||_{C_{x,t}^{k,m}\big(\mathbb{S}^{n}\times[0,\infty)\big)}\leq C_{k,m},

where
C_{k,m}>0
depends only on
k,m,f,p
and the geometry of
M_{0}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

By the a priori estimates in Lemmata
5.1
and
5.2
, there is a constant
C>0
, independent of
t
, such that

(5.9)

|\Phi(X(\cdot,t))|\leq C,\quad\forall t\in[0,\infty).

By Lemma
2.3
, for any
T>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi(X(\cdot,T))-\Phi(X(\cdot,0))

\displaystyle=-(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n+1}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}e^{\frac{r^{2}}{2}}Kh(fh^{p-1}-\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n+1}}\frac{1}{K}e^{\frac{-r^{2}}{2}})^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq-C_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}(fh^{p-1}-\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n+1}}\frac{1}{K}e^{\frac{-r^{2}}{2}})^{2}dxdt.

By (
5.9
), the above inequality implies there exists a subsequence of times
t_{j}\rightarrow\infty
such that
M_{t_{j}}
converges to a limiting hypersurface which satisfies (
1.1
).

To complete the proof of Theorem
1.5
, it suffices to show that the solution of (
1.1
) is unique. The equation can be written as

(5.10)

fh^{p-1}=\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n+1}}\det(\nabla^{2}h+hI)e^{-\frac{{h^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}}}{2}}.

Let
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
be two smooth solutions of (
5.10
). Suppose
F=h_{1}/h_{2}
attains its maximum at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{S}^{n}
. Then at
x_{0}

(5.11)

0=\nabla\log F=\frac{\nabla h_{1}}{h_{1}}-\frac{\nabla h_{2}}{h_{2}},

and
\nabla^{2}\log F
is a negative-semidefinite matrix at
x_{0}

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\nabla^{2}\log F

\displaystyle=\frac{\nabla^{2}h_{1}}{h_{1}}-\frac{\nabla h_{1}\otimes\nabla h_{1}}{h_{1}^{2}}-\frac{\nabla^{2}h_{2}}{h_{2}}+\frac{\nabla h_{2}\otimes\nabla h_{2}}{h_{2}^{2}}

(5.12)

\displaystyle=\frac{\nabla^{2}h_{1}}{h_{1}}-\frac{\nabla^{2}h_{2}}{h_{2}}.

By (
5.10
) and (
5
) we get at
x_{0}

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{\frac{h^{2}_{1}+|\nabla h_{1}|^{2}}{2}}\det(\nabla^{2}h_{2}+h_{2}I)h_{2}^{1-p}}{e^{\frac{h_{2}^{2}+|\nabla h_{2}|^{2}}{2}}\det(\nabla^{2}h_{1}+h_{1}I)h_{1}^{1-p}}

(5.13)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{e^{\frac{h^{2}_{1}+|\nabla h_{1}|^{2}}{2}}}{e^{\frac{h_{2}^{2}+|\nabla h_{2}|^{2}}{2}}}F(x_{0})^{p-(n+1)}.

If
F(x_{0})>1
, we have
h_{1}(x_{0})>h_{2}(x_{0})
, then by (
5.11
), we obtain

\frac{e^{\frac{h^{2}_{1}+|\nabla h_{1}|^{2}}{2}}}{e^{\frac{h_{2}^{2}+|\nabla h_{2}|^{2}}{2}}}=e^{\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{|\nabla h_{1}|^{2}}{h_{1}^{2}}+1\right)(h_{1}^{2}-h_{2}^{2})}>1,

which contradicts to (
5
) since
p\geq n+1
. So we obtain
F(x_{0})=\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}F\leq 1
. Similarly we can show
\min_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}F\geq 1
. Therefore
h_{1}\equiv h_{2}
.
∎

In order to prove the convergence of the flow (
1.6
), we require the following better gradient estimate.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
f=\frac{1}{2(\sqrt{2\pi})^{n+1}}
and
p\geq n+1
,
X(\cdot,t)
be a smooth uniformly convex solution to the flow (
1.6
), then there exist positive constant
C
and
C_{0}
, depending only on the intial hypersurface and
p
, such that

\max_{\mathbb{S}^{n}}\frac{|\nabla r|}{r}\leq Ce^{-C_{0}t}

for all
t>0
.

Proof.

Let
w=\log r
, under a local orthonormal frame, we have

g_{ij}=e^{2w}(w_{i}w_{j}+\delta_{ij})

b_{ij}=e^{w}(1+|\nabla w|^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}(-w_{ij}+w_{i}w_{j}+\delta_{ij})

and

K=\frac{\det b_{ij}}{\det g_{ij}}=(1+|\nabla w|^{2})^{-\frac{n+2}{2}}e^{-nw}\det(a_{ij})

where
a_{ij}=-w_{ij}+w_{i}w_{j}+\delta_{ij}
.

It is not hard to verify that
w
satisfies the following PDE

(5.14)

w_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}(1+|\nabla w|^{2})^{-\frac{p+n+1}{2}}e^{\frac{e^{2w}}{2}}e^{(p-n-1)w}\det(a_{ij})+1

Consider the auxiliary function

Q=\frac{1}{2}|\nabla w|^{2}.

At the point where
Q
attains its spatial maximum, we have

0=\nabla_{i}Q=\sum w_{k}w_{ki}

0\geq\nabla_{ij}Q=\sum w_{kj}w_{ki}+\sum w_{k}w_{kij}

Denote
\varrho=\frac{1}{2}(1+|\nabla w|^{2})^{-\frac{p+n+1}{2}}e^{\frac{e^{2w}}{2}}e^{(p-n-1)w}
. By differentiating (
5.14
), we obtain, at the point achieves its spatial maximum,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Q

\displaystyle=\sum w_{k}w_{kt}=-\det(a_{ij})\sum w_{k}\varrho_{k}-\varrho\sum w_{k}\nabla_{k}\det(a_{ij})

\displaystyle=-\varrho\det(a_{ij})(p-n-1+e^{2w})|\nabla w|^{2}+\varrho\det(a_{ij})\sum a^{ij}\nabla_{k}w_{ij}w_{k}

By the Ricci identity, we have

\nabla_{k}w_{ij}=\nabla_{j}w_{ik}+\delta_{ik}w_{j}-\delta_{ij}w_{k}

Hence

\displaystyle\partial_{t}Q

\displaystyle=-\varrho\det(a_{ij})(p-n-1+e^{2w})|\nabla w|^{2}

\displaystyle+\varrho\det(a_{ij})\sum a^{ij}(Q_{ij}-w_{ik}w_{kj}+w_{i}w_{j}-\delta_{ij}|\nabla w|^{2})

\displaystyle\leq-\varrho\det(a_{ij})(p-n-1+e^{2w})|\nabla w|^{2}+\varrho\det(a_{ij})(\max_{i}a^{ii}-\sum a^{ii})|\nabla w|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-C_{0}Q

We therefore have
Q\leq Ce^{-C_{0}t},
wher
C_{0}
and
C
are two positive constants which depends only on
p
and the geometry of
M_{0}
.

∎

Proof of Corollary
1.6
.

By Lemma
5.6
, We have that
||\nabla r||\rightarrow 0
exponentially as
t\rightarrow\infty
. Hence by the interpolation and the a priori estimates, we can get that
r
converges exponentially to a constant in the
C^{\infty}
topology as
t\rightarrow\infty.

∎

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Now we consider the case
0<p<n+1
. Since
f
is even and
M_{0}
is origin-symmetric, the solution remains
origin-symmetric for
t>0
. The long time existence of the flow (
1.6
) now follows from
Theorem
5.1
. As in the proof of Theorem
1.5
,
M_{t}
converges by a subsequence to the solution of equation (
1.1
).

∎

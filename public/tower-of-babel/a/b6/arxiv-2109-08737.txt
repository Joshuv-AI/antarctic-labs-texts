Quantitative problems on the size of G G -operators
Gabriel Lepetit

Abstract
G G -operators, a class of differential operators containing the differential operators of minimal order annihilating Siegel’s G G -functions, satisfy a condition of moderate growth called Galochkin condition , encoded by a p p -adic quantity, the size. Previous works of Chudnovsky, André and Dwork have provided inequalities between the size of a G G -operator and certain computable constants depending among others on its solutions. First, we recall André’s idea to attach a notion of size to differential modules and detail his results on the behavior of the size relatively to the standard algebraic operations on the modules. This is the corner stone to prove a quantitative version of André’s generalization of Chudnovsky’s Theorem: for f ⁡ ( z ) = ∑ α , k , ℓ c α , k , ℓ ​ z α ​ log ⁡ ( z ) k ​ f α , k , ℓ ​ ( z ) f(z)=\sum_{\alpha,k,\ell}c_{\alpha,k,\ell}z^{\alpha}\log(z)^{k}f_{\alpha,k,\ell}(z) , where f α , k , ℓ ​ ( z ) f_{\alpha,k,\ell}(z) are G G -functions, we can determine an upper bound on the size of the minimal operator L L over ℚ ¯ ​ ( z ) \overline{\mathbb{Q}}(z) of f ⁡ ( z ) f(z) in terms of quantities depending on the f α , k , ℓ ​ ( z ) f_{\alpha,k,\ell}(z) , the rationals α \alpha and the integers k k . We give two applications of this result: we estimate the size of a product of two G G -operators in function of the size of each operator; we also compute a constant appearing in a Diophantine problem encountered by the author.

Size of a differential module

2.1
Reminder on differential modules

In this part, we will essentially synthetise the presentation of the theory of differential modules contained in
[
13
, Chapter 2]
, that will be useful in the rest of this paper. More details and proofs about differential modules can be found in the above-quoted book.

Definition 1
.

Let
(k,\delta)
be a differential field. The ring of differential operators over
k
is denoted by
k[\partial]
and defined as the set of noncommutative polynomials in
\partial
with the compatibility condition

\forall a\in k,\quad\partial a=a\partial+\delta(a).

This is a left (and right) euclidean ring for the euclidean function

\mathrm{ord}\,:L=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\partial^{k}\mapsto n\quad\quad(\text{where}\;\;a_{n}\neq 0).

Definition 2
.

Let
(k,\delta)
be a differential field. A differential module
\mathcal{M}
over
k
is a
k
-vector space of finite dimension which is a left
k[\partial]
-module, or equivalently a
k
-vector space of finite dimension endowed with a map
\partial:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
such that

\forall\lambda\in k,\;\;\forall a,b\in\mathcal{M},\quad\partial(a+b)=\partial(a)+\partial(b)\quad\text{and}\quad\partial(\lambda a)=\lambda\partial(a)+\delta(\lambda)a.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a
k
-differential module of dimension
n
endowed with a
k
-basis
(e_{1},\dots,e_{n})
. Then there exists a matrix
A=(a_{i,j})_{i,j}\in M_{n}(k)
such that
\forall 1\leqslant i\leqslant n,\;\partial e_{i}=-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}a_{j,i}e_{j}
. A direct computation shows that if
u=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}e_{i}\in\mathcal{M}
, then
\partial u=0\iff u^{\prime}=Au,u={}^{t}(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})
.

Remark 1
.

If we choose another
k
-basis
(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})
of
\mathcal{M}
such that
\partial f_{i}=-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}b_{j,i}f_{j}
, then there exists
P\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
such that
B=P[A]=P^{-1}P^{\prime}+P^{-1}AP
.

The differential systems defined by
A
and
B
are then said to be
equivalent
over
k
. This is equivalent to the fact that
y\mapsto Py
is a one-to-one correspondance between the solution sets of
y^{\prime}=Ay
and
y^{\prime}=By
.

Therefore, the matrix
A
obtained by the construction above doesn’t depend on the choice of basis we make, up to equivalence of differential systems.

We can also do the converse operation:

Definition 3
.

For
A=(a_{ij})_{i,j}\in M_{n}(k)
, we define the differential module
\mathcal{M}_{A}
associated with the differential system
y^{\prime}=Ay
by
\mathcal{M}_{A}=k^{n}
endowed with the derivation
\partial
such that
\forall i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
,
\partial e_{i}=-\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}a_{ji}e_{j}
, where
(e_{1},\dots,e_{n})
is the canonical basis of
k^{n}
.

We associate with a differential operator
L=\partial^{n}+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n}a_{k}\partial^{k}\in k[\partial]
the differential module
\mathcal{M}_{L}:=\mathcal{M}_{A_{L}}
, where
A_{L}
is the companion matrix of
L
:

A_{L}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&&(0)\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\\
(0)&&0&1\\
-a_{0}&\dots&&-a_{n-1}\end{pmatrix}.

Definition 4
.

A
morphism of differential modules
is a
k
-linear map
\varphi:(\mathcal{M},\partial_{\mathcal{M}})\rightarrow(\mathcal{N},\partial_{\mathcal{N}})
such that
\partial_{\mathcal{N}}\circ\varphi=\varphi\circ\partial_{\mathcal{M}}
.

Proposition 1
.

Let
A,B\in M_{n}(k)
. Then there is an isomorphism of differential modules betweeen
\mathcal{M}_{A}
and
\mathcal{M}_{B}
if and only if
A
and
B
are equivalent over
k
.

Thus, the classes of equivalences of differential systems over
k
classify the
k
-differential modules up to isomorphism.

We can perform usual algebraic constructions with the differential modules:

•

A
differential submodule
of
\mathcal{M}
is a left
k[\partial]
-submodule of
\mathcal{M}
.

•

If
\mathcal{N}
is a differential submodule of
\mathcal{M}
, then the
quotient differential module

\mathcal{M}/\mathcal{N}
is endowed with the quotient derivation
\overline{\partial}:\overline{m}\mapsto\overline{\partial(m)}
.

•

The
cartesian product
(or direct sum) of
(\mathcal{M}_{1},\partial_{1})
and
(\mathcal{M}_{2},\partial_{2})
can be endowed with the derivation
\partial:(m_{1},m_{2})\mapsto(\partial_{1}(m_{1}),\partial_{2}(m_{2}))
, which makes it a differential module.

•

The
tensor product

\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{M}_{2}
is a differential module when endowed with
\partial:m_{1}\otimes m_{2}\mapsto m_{1}\otimes\partial_{2}(m_{2})+\partial_{1}(m_{1})\otimes m_{2}
(extended on the whole vector space with the additivity of
\partial
). Note that the tensor product cannot be defined over the noncommutative ring
k[\partial]
, since
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
are only left
k[\partial]
-modules. We will therefore often denote
\mathcal{M}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{M}_{2}
without precising the base field.

•

The derivation
\partial^{*}:\varphi\mapsto\left(m\mapsto\varphi(\partial(m))-\delta(\varphi(m))\right)
makes the
dual

\mathcal{M}^{*}=\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(M,k)
a differential module

•

More generally, the preceding constructions enable us to define a structure of differential module on the
space of morphisms

\mathrm{Hom}_{k}(\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2})\simeq\mathcal{M}_{1}^{*}\otimes_{k}\mathcal{M}_{2}
.

Remark 2
.

Actually, for any
A\in M_{n}(k)
, we have
\mathcal{M}_{A}^{*}\simeq\mathcal{M}_{A^{*}}
, where
A^{*}=-{}^{t}A
. If
X
is a fundamental matrix of solutions of the system

, then
Y:={}^{t}X^{-1}
satisfies
Y^{\prime}=-{}^{t}AY
.

Lemma 1
.

There is an isomorphism of differential modules
\mathcal{M}_{L}\simeq\left(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}(z)[\partial]/\overline{\mathbb{Q}}(z)[\partial]L\right)^{*}
.

Definition 5
.

The
adjoint operator
of

is the operator

such that
\mathcal{M}_{L^{*}}\simeq\overline{\mathbb{Q}}(z)[\partial]/\overline{\mathbb{Q}}(z)[\partial]L
.

We can compute directly

, then
L^{*}=(-\partial)^{n}+\sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}(-\partial)^{k}a_{k}
. The adjoint operator has useful properties, for instance that

and
\left(L_{1}L_{2}\right)^{*}=L_{2}^{*}L_{1}^{*}
.

We finally mention that the
cyclic vector Theorem
, implies that any differential system

is equivalent to some system

,

. This yields

[

, pp. 42–43]
).

2.2
Size and operations on differential modules

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a number field, and
G\in M_{n}(\mathbb{K}(z))
. In this subsection, following André’s notations, we introduce a quantity
\sigma(G)
encoding an arithmetic condition of moderate growth on a differential system, called

.

For

, we define

as the matrix such that, if

is the identity matrix. The matrices

\forall s\in\mathbb{N},\quad G_{s+1}=G_{s}G+G^{\prime}_{s},

where

Definition 6

(Galochkin,

Let

be such that

, we consider

the least common denominator of all coefficients of the entries of the matrices

, when

. The system

if

\exists C>0:\;\forall s\in\mathbb{N},\quad q_{s}\leqslant C^{s+1}.

Chudnovsky’s Theorem (cf
[
4
]
) states that if

is the companion matrix of the minimal non-zero differential operator

G

G
-operator
(see

For every prime ideal

of

as the

-adic absolute value on

We recall that the

\begin{array}[t]{lrcl}|\cdot|_{\mathfrak{p},\mathrm{Gauss}}:&\mathbb{K}(z)&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}\\
&\dfrac{\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N}a_{i}z^{i}}{\sum\limits_{j=0}^{M}b_{j}z^{j}}&\longmapsto&\dfrac{\max\limits_{0\leqslant i\leqslant N}|a_{i}|_{\mathfrak{p}}}{\max\limits_{0\leqslant j\leqslant M}|b_{j}|_{\mathfrak{p}}}.\end{array}

The absolute value

naturally induces a norm on

is a norm of algebra on

(

]

Let

\sigma(G):=\limsup\limits_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\dfrac{1}{s}\sum\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Spec}\,(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}})}h(s,\mathfrak{p})

\sigma(Y):=\limsup\limits_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\dfrac{1}{s}\sum\limits_{\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Spec}\,(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}})}\sup_{m\leqslant s}\log^{+}\left\|Y_{m}\right\|_{\mathfrak{p}}.

, and not
\alpha
.

Fischler and Rivoal proved this theorem for
\beta=0
in
[
6
]
. With their method, the constant
C(F,0)
is computable in principle, but they didn’t give an explicit formula for it. In
[
10
]
, we express explicitly
C(F,\beta)
in function of quantities depending on
L
,
F
and the denominator of
\beta
using the theory of
G
-operators. Let us explain how Theorems
1
and
3
of the present paper can be used to compute
C(F,\beta)
.

By
[
6
, Lemma 1 p. 11]
, we can find polynomials
Q_{0}(X),\dots,Q_{\ell}(X)\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{K}}[X]
and
u\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that

uz^{\mu-\omega}L=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}z^{j}Q_{j}(\theta+j),

with
\theta=z\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}z
,
\mu
the order of
L
,
\omega
the multiplicity of
0
as a singularity of
L
and
\ell=\delta-\omega
where
\delta
is the degree in
z
of
L
. We can show that if
\ell=0
, then
F(z)\in\mathbb{K}[z]
, so that this case is excluded by the assumption of Theorem
4
.

Define, for
j\in\{0,\dots,\ell\}
,
Q_{j,\beta}(X):=Q_{j}(X-\beta)
. Then the differential operator of order
\mu

L_{\beta}:=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\ell}z^{j}Q_{j,\beta}(\theta+j)\in\mathbb{K}\left[z,\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\right]

(18)

is the minimal operator over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}(z)
of the Nilsson-Gevrey series of arithmetic and holonomic type
z^{\beta}F(z)
. We introduce the operator

\widetilde{L}_{\beta}=\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\right)^{\ell}z^{m-1}L_{\beta}.

In
[
10
]
, a crucial point, which we couldn’t solve without using the results of the present paper, is the evaluation of
\sigma(\widetilde{L}_{\beta})
in function of
\sigma(L)
or of
\overline{\sigma}(F)
. Indeed,
\sigma(\widetilde{L}_{\beta})
occur in the expression of
C(F,\beta)
.

We can now answer that question. Indeed, on the one hand, if
L_{0}=\left(\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}z\right)^{\ell}
, Theorem
3
implies that

\sigma(\widetilde{L}_{\beta})\leqslant\ell+2\sigma(L_{0})+\sigma(L_{\beta})

since
\sigma(z^{m-1}L_{\beta})=\sigma(L_{\beta})
. Moreover, a basis of solutions of the equation
L_{0}(y(z))=0
is
(1,z,\dots,z^{\ell-1})
so that a fundamental matrix of solution of the system
y^{\prime}=A_{L_{0}}y
is the wronskian matrix

Y=\begin{pmatrix}1&z&\dots&z^{\ell-1}\\
0&1&&(\ell-1)z^{\ell-2}\\
\vdots&0&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\dots&(\ell-1)!\end{pmatrix},

which satisfies
Y^{(s)}=0
for
s
large enough. Hence
(A_{L_{0}})_{s}=Y_{s}Y^{-1}=0
for
s
large enough and
\sigma(L_{0})=0
.

On the other hand, by applying Theorem
2
to the Nilsson-Gevrey series
z^{\beta}F(z)
, we obtain

\sigma(L_{\beta})\leqslant\big(1+\log(2)\big)\max\left(1,2\log(d(\beta)),\sigma(L)\right).

(19)

Finally, using the bound on
\sigma(L)
arising from Chudnovsky’s Theorem ((
14
) and (
15
)), we have

\sigma(L_{\beta})\leqslant\big(1+\log(2)\big)\max\big(1,2\log(d(\beta)),\left((5+\varepsilon_{\mu,1})\mu^{2}(\delta+1)-1-(\mu-1)(\delta+1)\right)\overline{\sigma}(F)\big),

(20)

where
\varepsilon_{x,y}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}\;x=y\\
0&\text{else}\end{cases}
denotes the Kronecker symbol, and

\sigma(\widetilde{L}_{\beta})\leqslant\ell+\big(1+\log(2)\big)\max\big(1,2\log(d(\beta)),\left((5+\varepsilon_{\mu,1})\mu^{2}(\delta+1)-1-(\mu-1)(\delta+1)\right)\overline{\sigma}(F)\big).

This is the desired bound.

Remark 7
.

In
[
11
]
, we tried another method to obtain an inequality of the type of (
20
): we adapted directly the proof of Chudnovsky’s Theorem to the case of a subclass of the set of Nilsson-Gevrey series of arithmetic and holonomic type. We now explain this approach.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a number field and

f(z)=\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{\lambda}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\kappa}z^{\alpha_{\ell}}\log(z)^{k}f_{k,\ell}(z)

(21)

be a Nilsson-Gevrey series of arithmetic and holonomic type of order
0
, where
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\lambda})\in\mathbb{Q}^{\lambda}
, and the
f_{k,\ell}(z)\in\mathbb{K}\llbracket z\rrbracket
are
G
-functions. Here,
f(z)
is a particular case of Nilsson-Gevrey series of arithmetic and holonomic type, which is not of the most general form (
1
) given in the introduction. Indeed, in (
1
), it is allowed to take
f_{k,\ell}
as a linear combination with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}
of
G
-functions, which is not permitted here. Then Proposition
8
below provides an estimate on the size of the minimal operator of
f(z)
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}(z)
. This result uses a stronger hypothesis than Theorem
2
, namely that the minimal operator of
f
over the fraction field
\mathcal{R}
of the ring of "
\mathcal{S}
-polynomials" is the same as the minimal operator of
f
over
\mathbb{K}(z)
.The
\mathcal{S}
-polynomials are defined as the functions of the form (
21
), where
(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{\lambda})\in\mathbb{Q}^{\lambda}
and the
f_{k,\ell}(z)\in\mathbb{K}[z]
.

Proposition 8
.

Let
f(z)
be a function of the form (
21
).
Assume that the minimal operator
L
of
f(z)
of order
\mu
over the fraction field
\mathcal{R}
of the ring of the
\mathcal{S}
-polynomials satisfies
L\in\mathbb{K}(z)\left[\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}z\right]
. Then we have

\limsup_{s\rightarrow+\infty}\dfrac{1}{s}\log(q_{s})\leqslant[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{Q}]\left(2\log(d(\boldsymbol{\alpha}))+\kappa+2\mu\left(2\mu\lambda(\kappa+1)+1\right)(\delta+1)\max_{0\leqslant k\leqslant\kappa\atop 1\leqslant\ell\leqslant\lambda}\overline{\sigma}(f_{k,\ell})\right)

(22)

with the notations of Definitions
6
and
7
(with
G
the companion matrix of
L
), where
\delta=\deg_{z}(L)
.

Let us compare the conclusion of Proposition
8
with the one of Theorem
2
in the particular case of
L_{\beta}
. We can prove that
L_{\beta}
is at the same time the minimal operator of
z^{\beta}F(z)
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}(z)
and over
\mathcal{R}
.

If we consider the family
(f_{1},\dots,f_{\mu}):=(F,F^{\prime},\dots,F^{(\mu-1)})
consisting of linearly independent functions over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}(z)
and
(g_{1},\dots,g_{\mu}):=(G,G^{\prime},\dots,G^{(\mu-1)})
, where
G(z)=z^{\beta}F(z)
, then we see that
L_{\beta}=z^{\beta}Lz^{-\beta}
is the minimal operator of
G(z)
over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}(z)
, so that
(g_{1},\dots,g_{\mu})
is free over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}(z)
.

The Leibniz formula then shows that
(g_{1},\dots,g_{\mu})\in z^{\beta-\mu}\mathbb{K}\llbracket z\rrbracket
. This implies that the family
(z^{-\beta+\mu}g_{1},\dots,z^{-\beta+\mu}g_{\mu})
is free over
\mathcal{R}
, using the linear independence over
\mathbb{K}\llbracket z\rrbracket
of the family
(z^{\gamma}\log(z)^{k})_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Q}\cap[0,1[\atop k\in\mathbb{N}}
. Consequently,
(g_{1},\dots,g_{\mu})
is free over
\mathcal{R}
.

Thus, since the operator
L_{\beta}
of order
\mu
with coefficients in
\mathcal{R}
annihilates
G(z)
, it is the minimal operator of
G(z)
over
\mathcal{R}
.

In that case, using Proposition
2
, Equation (
22
) yields

\sigma(L_{\beta})\leqslant[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{Q}]\left(2\log(d(\beta))+2\mu(2\mu+1)(\delta+1)\overline{\sigma}(F)\right).

(23)

To simplify, we assume that
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}
and
d(\beta)\geqslant 2
(
i.e.

\beta\not\in\mathbb{Z}
).

•

If
d(\beta)\geqslant\exp\big((5+\varepsilon_{\mu,1})\mu^{2}(\delta+1)-1-(\mu-1)(\delta+1))\overline{\sigma}(F)/2\big)
, then (
20
) yields

\sigma(L_{\beta})\leqslant 2\big(1+\log(2)\big)\log(d(\beta)),

which is a better estimate on
\sigma(L_{\beta})
than (
23
) if and only if

\overline{\sigma}(F)>\dfrac{\log\big(d(\beta)+2\big)}{\mu(2\mu+1)(\delta+1)}.

•

Else, we see that the function

\begin{array}[t]{lrcl}\mathrm{Comp}:&\mathbb{N}^{*}\times\mathbb{N}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{R}\\
&(\mu,\delta)&\longmapsto&(1+\log(2))\big((5+\varepsilon_{\mu,1})\mu^{2}(\delta+1)-1-(\mu-1)(\delta+1)\big)-2\mu(2\mu+1)(\delta+1)\end{array}

is strictly positive. Indeed, if
\mu\geqslant 2
,
\mathrm{Comp}(\mu,\delta)=A(\mu)(\delta+1)-(1+\log(2))
where

A(\mu):=(3+5\log(2))\mu^{2}-(3+\log(2))\mu+1+\log(2)

is strictly greater than
1+\log(2)
for all
\mu\geqslant 2
. Moreover,
\mathrm{Comp}(1,\delta)=6\log(2)(\delta+1)-1-\log(2)\geqslant 0
for all
\delta\in\mathbb{N}
.

Hence, if
(\mu,\delta)\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\times\mathbb{N}
and
d(\beta)\leqslant\exp\big(\overline{\sigma}(F)\mathrm{Comp}(\mu,\delta)/2\big)
, then (
23
) is a better estimate than (
20
).

•

Else, if
d(\beta)>\exp\big(\overline{\sigma}(F)\mathrm{Comp}(\mu,\delta)/2\big)
, then (
20
) is a better estimate than (
23
).

Let us finally consider the following explicit example: let
F(z)
be the hypergeometric
G
-function

F(z)={}_{2}F_{1}\left(\dfrac{1}{3},\dfrac{2}{11},\dfrac{1}{6};z\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{\left(\frac{1}{3}\right)_{k}\left(\frac{2}{11}\right)_{k}}{\left(\frac{1}{6}\right)_{k}k!}z^{k}

whose minimal operator over
\overline{\mathbb{Q}}(z)
is

L=z(z-1)\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\right)^{2}+\left(\left(\dfrac{1}{3}+\dfrac{2}{11}+1\right)z-\dfrac{1}{6}\right)\left(\dfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}z}\right)+\dfrac{1}{3}\times\dfrac{2}{11}.

We take
\beta=1/7
. It satisfies
\delta=\mu=2
. Hence we obtain with (
20
)
\sigma(L_{\beta})\leqslant 1232
and with (
23
)
\sigma(L_{\beta})\leqslant 784
. We see that our alternative method improves the bound (
20
) on
\sigma(L_{\beta})
.

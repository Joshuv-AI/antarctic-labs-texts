Revisiting Latent Space of GAN Inversion for Real Image Editing
Kai Katsumata, Duc Minh Vo, Bei Liu, Hideki Nakayama

Abstract
The exploration of the latent space in StyleGANs and GAN inversion exemplify impressive real-world image editing, yet the trade-off between reconstruction quality and editing quality remains an open problem. In this study, we revisit StyleGANs’ hyperspherical prior 𝒵 \mathcal{Z} and combine it with highly capable latent spaces to build combined spaces that faithfully invert real images while maintaining the quality of edited images. More specifically, we propose ℱ / 𝒵 + \mathcal{F}/\mathcal{Z}^{+} space consisting of two subspaces: ℱ \mathcal{F} space of an intermediate feature map of StyleGANs enabling faithful reconstruction and 𝒵 + \mathcal{Z}^{+} space of an extended StyleGAN prior supporting high editing quality. We project the real images into the proposed space to obtain the inverted codes, by which we then move along 𝒵 + \mathcal{Z}^{+} , enabling semantic editing without sacrificing image quality. Comprehensive experiments show that 𝒵 + \mathcal{Z}^{+} can replace the most commonly-used 𝒲 \mathcal{W} , 𝒲 + \mathcal{W}^{+} , and 𝒮 \mathcal{S} spaces while preserving reconstruction quality, resulting in reduced distortion of edited images.

Such an unbounded embedding space cannot guarantee that the edited embedding is always present in the embedding space, resulting in distortion after editing.
Unlike typically used spaces, StyleGAN’s prior space

To address the trade-off challenge, one solution is to flexibly use the rich embedding space and the original latent space, which is the main target of this work.

To begin with, we revisit the original latent space

\mathcal{Z}^{+}

Qualitative and quantitative evaluations show that our proposed method maintains image quality after performing editing operations without sacrificing reconstruction quality.
Our main contributions are as follows:

•

We revisit
\mathcal{Z}

\mathcal{Z}^{+}

\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{Z}^{+}

•

According to the experimental results, our
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{Z}^{+}

•

We extend the method by leveraging
\mathcal{Z}^{+}

Related work

GAN inversion
aims to project real images into low-dimensional latent codes, which can be mainly classified into two types of approaches: encoder-based and optimization-based approaches.
The former type of approach
[
31
,
22
]
trains an encoder network with a loss function that measures the distance between the input image and the reconstructed image obtained from the predicted latent code.
The latter one
[
1
,
2
,
23
]
directly optimizes the latent code to reconstruct a target input.
A hybrid approach
[
10
,
20
]
, which combines encoder-based and optimization-based approaches, initializes a latent code with the encoder prediction and then fine-tunes the latent code using an optimization method.

Pioneering studies of GAN inversion aim at the faithful reconstruction of target images.
For example, extending a latent space leads to high reconstruction quality
[
1
,
2
,
10
]
.
Recently, Kang
et al
.

[
10
]
and Feng
et al
.

[
7
]
improve the robustness to out-of-range images.
Another recent direction of GAN inversion is to increase robustness to downstream tasks
[
42
]
.
Zhu
et al
.

[
42
]
aims to increase the editing quality of found latent codes by the regularization that directs latent codes toward a high-density region.
Although our study has the same goal as
[
42
]
, our approach is to use a latent space where we know the bound of the codes, unlike
[
42
]
.

Semantic image editing

[
8
,
25
,
6
,
39
,
27
,
17
]
is one of the downstream tasks of embedding real images into latent space.
The task modifies a latent code along semantically meaningful directions to generate an intended image. Supervised
[
25
]
and unsupervised
[
8
,
27
]
approaches are investigated to explore semantic directions. GANSpace
[
8
]
finds useful directions by computing eigenvectors on the empirical distribution of latent code. SeFa
[
27
]
factorizes the weights of layers that feed on latent codes. The above methods explore global semantic directions, which are shared among the latent codes and are called global methods. Another research direction is local methods
[
6
,
39
]
. The local methods explore semantic directions with respect to each latent code.

\mathcal{Z}

Space
.
The generator
G:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{X}
learns to map a simple distribution, called latent space
\mathcal{Z}

where
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{X}

The primitive latent code of the StyleGAN family is with

AgileGAN

employ the extended space
\mathcal{Z}^{+}
, which provides a different latent code from
\mathcal{Z}
for each layer.
Unlike GAN inversion, AgileGAN
[
29
]
addresses stylizing portraits and does not aim at faithful reconstruction. StyleAlign
[
33
]
demonstrates
\mathcal{Z}^{+}
cannot achieve faithful reconstruction for real image inversion.
As shown in
[
29
,
33
]
,
while the
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{Z}^{+}

, hypersphere) leads to edited images with less deterioration of quality.

\mathcal{W}
,
\mathcal{W}^{+}
, and
\mathcal{S}
Space
.
StyleGANs also use the intermediate latent space
\mathcal{W}
where each
\bm{w}\in\mathcal{W}
is produced by a mapping network consisting of eight fully-connected layers denoted as
\mathcal{M}:\mathcal{Z}\to\mathcal{W}
.
Thanks to multiple affine transformations and non-linearity functions in the mapping network

, the features of
\mathcal{W}
are more disentangled than those of
\mathcal{Z}
.

Thereafter,
[
1
,
2
]
introduced
\mathcal{W}^{+}

Each element
\bm{w}^{+}
in
\mathcal{W}^{+}
is defined as
\bm{w}^{+}=\{\bm{w}_{1},\bm{w}_{2},\ldots,\bm{w}_{N}\}

\bm{w}_{i}\in\mathcal{W}
, and

is the number of layers in generator that takes
\bm{w}

as input.
For a
1024\times 1024
StyleGAN, we use 18 different 512-dimensional latent codes as
\bm{w}^{+}
.

\mathcal{S}
space
[
32
]
is spanned by style parameters, which is transformed from
\bm{w}\in\mathcal{W}
using different learned affine transformations for each layer of the generator.

Although the
\mathcal{W}
,
\mathcal{W}^{+}
, and
\mathcal{S}
spaces derive faithful reconstruction quality, distortions and artifacts may appear in edited images
[
34
,
42
,
29
]
.
This is because the embeddings with these spaces for the images may not correspond appropriately to
\mathcal{Z}^{+}
, the StyleGAN prior latent space,
and the space cannot guarantee that the edited latent code reaches the original space. In this study, we aim at latent editing without suffering quality deterioration with the aid of the nature of
\mathcal{Z}
or
\mathcal{Z}^{+}
.

\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\mathcal{N}}

Space
.
Zhu
et al
.

[
42
]
introduced a normalized space
\mathcal{P}
to explore the GAN inversion trade-offs.
The space is the deactivated space of
\mathcal{W}
, which is computed by using the inversion of Leaky ReLU.
Zhu
et al
.

[
42
]
also proposed the
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{N}}
space by whitening the
\mathcal{P}
space with the principal component analysis (PCA) parameter computed on a million latent codes in the
\mathcal{P}
space.
Since the distribution of
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{N}}
can be interpreted as the Gaussian distribution with zero mean and unit variance, we can calculate the density of the latent codes in
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{N}}
.
As discussed in
[
18
]
, the regularization on
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{N}}
space ensures realistic GAN inversion outcomes.
Since
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{N}}
space cannot achieve sufficient reconstruction quality, the
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{N}}
can be extended to
\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\mathcal{N}}
in the same manner as
\mathcal{W}^{+}

[
1
,
2
]
.

Although
\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\mathcal{N}}
improves the robustness of the reconstructed latent codes, editing operations are performed on
\mathcal{W}
or
\mathcal{W}^{+}
space.
This means that utilizing the regularization on
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{N}}
leads to maintaining the image quality only within the neighborhood of an inverted code.
Hence, the weaknesses of the unbounded spaces as discussed above remain.
We thus seek the latent space that also guarantees generation quality in the editing phase.

\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{W}^{+}

and

\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{S}

Space
. Kang
et al
.

[
10
]
proposed
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{W}^{+}
space for generalization performance to out-of-range images (
Fig.

1
), consisting of the
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{W}^{+}
spaces, and the space also investigated in SAM
[
20
]
and Barbershop
[
41
]
.
The coarse-scale feature map
\bm{f}\in\mathcal{F}
is an intermediate output of a generator before taking fine-scale latent codes
\bm{w}_{M+}=\{\bm{w}_{M},\bm{w}_{M+1}\ldots,\bm{w}_{N}\}
.
An element
\bm{w}^{\ast}=(\bm{f},\bm{w}_{M+})
of
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{W}^{+}
consists of a base code
\bm{f}
and a detail code
\bm{w}_{M+}
.
The information of a noise input and bottom latent codes
\{\bm{w}_{1},\bm{w}_{2},\ldots,\bm{w}_{M-1}\}
is contained in
\bm{f}
, and the feature map controls the geometric information.
Kang
et al
.

[
10
]
have integrated the regularization on the
\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{N}}
space into the
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{W}^{+}
space to obtain robust latent codes. The combined space has extended the range of images that can be inverted.

\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{S}
space
[
36
]
employs
\mathcal{S}
space
[
32
]
as an alternative to
\mathcal{W}^{+}
.

Whereas these spaces achieve faithful reconstruction quality, it has the same limitations as
\mathcal{W}
,
\mathcal{W}^{+}
,
\mathcal{S}
, and
\mathcal{P}^{+}_{\mathcal{N}}
.
This is because latent editing is performed on unbounded spaces. Thus, to continue using
\mathcal{Z}
space, we leverage the
\mathcal{F}
space, which complements the lack of representative capacity of the bounded latent space (

1

3.2
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{Z}^{+}
Space

Overall, there is still no existing latent space that can guarantee both reconstruction quality and editing quality. As discussed in
[
18
,
42
,
29
,
34
]
, leveraging
\mathcal{Z}
or
\mathcal{Z}^{+}
spaces lead to high editing quality in exchange for reconstruction quality.
To overcome their limitation, we propose an alternative latent space
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{Z}^{+}
by extending the StyleGAN prior
\mathcal{Z}
.
The proposed space consists of the feature space
\mathcal{F}
for increasing representative capacity and a bounded latent space
\mathcal{Z}^{+}
for maintaining editing quality. We use the combination of the spaces
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{Z}^{+}

because we cannot achieve sufficient reconstruction quality when using the space
\mathcal{Z}^{+}
to increase the editing quality.

The latent space
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{Z}^{+}
can do semantic editing safety while maintaining the reconstruction quality (
Fig.

1
).
The
\mathcal{Z}^{+}
space is a concatenation of the
N
latent code
\bm{z}
, and it is derived in the same fashion as
\mathcal{W}^{+}
.
Each element
\bm{z}^{+}
in
\mathcal{Z}^{+}
is defined as
\bm{z}^{+}=\{\bm{z}_{1},\bm{z}_{2},\ldots,\bm{z}_{N}\}
where
\bm{z}_{i}\in\mathcal{Z}
. A code
\bm{z}_{i}
is an input for each layer of a StyleGAN generator and is transformed by the mapping network and AdaIN
[
9
]
before being fed into the generator.
The number of layers is
N=18
for a
1024\times 1024
StyleGAN. We define
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{Z}^{+}
by combining the
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{Z}^{+}
spaces.
Each element
\bm{z}^{\ast}\in\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{Z}^{+}
is defined as
\bm{z}^{\ast}=(\bm{f},\bm{z}_{M+})
, where
\bm{z}_{M+}=\{\bm{z}_{M},\bm{z}_{M+1},\ldots,\bm{z}_{N}\}
is a set of latent codes for the fine scales of the generator. We also introduce the
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{Z}
space for comparison, consisting of
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{Z}
instead of
\mathcal{Z}^{+}
.

The
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{Z}^{+}
space has the desirable properties required for GAN inversion: high reconstruction quality and high editing quality.
Our proposed space is equipped with high reconstruction capability attributed to the feature space
\mathcal{F}
and high editing quality attributed to the primitive space
\mathcal{Z}
.
PULSE
[
18
]
discussed the importance of
considering a manifold of a latent space, which controls the content quality. Following this discussion, Zhu
et al
.

[
42
]
assumes that
the deactivated
\mathcal{W}
follows a Gaussian distribution and picks a latent code located in a high-density region.
To greatly benefit from considering the latent manifold, we employ bounded latent codes. Since we know the shape of
\mathcal{Z}
,
we completely utilize the information of the distribution of
\mathcal{Z}
.

3.3
Inversion to
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{Z}^{+}

We introduce an inversion algorithm that projects images to the
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{Z}^{+}
space.
Our method is a hybrid method that first projects a target image to
\mathcal{F}
by using a pre-trained encoder to obtain an initial
base code and then directly optimizes the base and detail codes.

Given an input image
\bm{x}
, we find a latent code
\bm{z}^{\ast}
that reconstructs
\bm{x}
by optimizing an objective function
L_{\textrm{recon}}
, which is
defined as:

\displaystyle L_{\textrm{recon}}(\bm{z}^{\ast})=L_{\textrm{MSE}}(\bm{z}^{\ast})+\lambda_{\textrm{per}}L_{\textrm{per}}(\bm{z}^{\ast}),

(1)

where
L_{\textrm{MSE}}
and
L_{\textrm{per}}
are mean squared error (MSE) and perceptual losses
[
16
,
38
]
, respectively. The hyperparameter
\lambda_{\textrm{per}}
controls the contributions
of
L_{\textrm{MSE}}
and
L_{\textrm{per}}
.
MSE loss is defined as
L_{\textrm{MSE}}(\bm{z}^{\ast})=\|\bm{x}-G(\bm{z}^{\ast})\|^{2}
, and perceptual loss is defined as
L_{\textrm{per}}(\bm{z}^{\ast})=\|\phi(\bm{x})-\phi(G(\bm{z}^{\ast}))\|^{2}
with an LPIPS network
\phi
,
which is a pre-training network with the VGG backbone and extracts high-level feature representation.
MSE loss and perceptual loss are the distances between the target and inverted images in the data space and feature space, respectively. We use perceptual loss to enhance
reconstruction quality and specially to avoid blurred images
[
19
,
16
,
38
]
.

For efficient optimization, we initialize the base code by employing an encoder. We first compute a rough base code using an encoder and then optimize
a precise base code. We train an encoder
E\!:\!\mathcal{X}\!\to\!\mathcal{F}
by optimizing the loss function:

\displaystyle L_{\textrm{enc}}=

\displaystyle\|G(E(\bm{x}_{\downarrow}),\bm{w}^{s}_{M+})-\bm{x}\|^{2}

(2)

\displaystyle+\lambda_{\textrm{enc}}\|F(G(E(\bm{x}_{\downarrow})),\bm{w}^{s}_{M+})-F(\bm{x})\|^{2},

where
G:\bm{f}\times\bm{w}_{M+}\mapsto\bm{x}
is a generator (we use a different notation than the above-mentioned
G
in Section 3 to emphasize the various inputs), and
\bm{f}^{s}
and
\bm{w}^{s}_{M+}
are sampled codes corresponding to sampled latent code
\bm{z}\in\mathcal{Z}
.
Training images
\bm{x}=G(\bm{f}^{s},\bm{w}^{s}_{M+})
are reconstructed from the sampled codes
\bm{f}^{s}
and
\bm{w}^{s}_{M+}
.
We train the encoder on only the pairs of sampled latent code and generated images (no real image is used) because
\bm{w}^{s}_{M+}
corresponding to given images is unavailable.
The downsampled images
\bm{x}_{\downarrow}
have a resolution
8\times
larger than the feature space
\mathcal{F}
.

To respect the encoded latent codes, we use a regularization that penalizes the distance between an initial latent code and a current latent code in optimization. The regularization term for
\bm{f}
is defined as:

\displaystyle L_{\textrm{reg}}(\bm{z}^{\ast})=\|\bm{f}^{0}-\bm{f}\|^{2},

(3)

where
\bm{f}^{0}\!=\!E(\bm{x})
. The loss prevents the latent code
\bm{f}
from straying to far from the encoded code
\bm{f}^{0}
.
The final objective function for GAN inversion on
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{Z}^{+}
is given by

\displaystyle L(\bm{z}^{\ast})=L_{\textrm{recon}}(\bm{z}^{\ast})+\lambda_{reg}L_{\textrm{reg}}(\bm{z}^{\ast}),

(4)

where
\lambda_{reg}
is a weight of the regularization loss.
We retract the latent codes
\bm{z}_{M+}
to the surface of the hypersphere of radius
\sqrt{512}
after every iteration to compute precise gradients of the latent
\bm{z}_{M+}
, and we update each latent code
\bm{z}_{i}\in\bm{z}_{M+}
independently by using:

\displaystyle\bm{z}_{i}=\sqrt{512}\frac{\bm{z}_{i}}{|\bm{z}_{i}|}.

(5)

We optimize the latent code
\bm{z}^{\ast}
in
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{Z}^{+}
with the aid of both the feature encoder and the optimization method with regularization.
Although employing
\mathcal{Z}
space alone limits representation capacity,
extending the limited space to the
\mathcal{Z}^{+}
space and complementing it with a highly capable
\mathcal{F}
space
lead to increase reconstruction capability without sacrificing editing quality.

Latent editing on
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{Z}^{+}

For latent editing on our
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{Z}^{+}
space, we apply the discovered semantic directions to
\bm{z}_{M+}
.
We move the latent code
\bm{z}_{M+}
to
\{\bm{z}_{M}+\alpha\bm{n},\bm{z}_{M+1}+\alpha\bm{n},\ldots,\bm{z}_{N}+\alpha\bm{n}\}
by a latent direction
\bm{n}
with a step size
\alpha
.
We use GANSpace
[
8
]
and InterfaceGAN
[
26
]

for finding semantic directions.

GANSpace
[
8
]
computes semantic directions based on PCA applied in the intermediate latent space.
For semantic directions on
\mathcal{Z}
, we first apply PCA to
\bm{w}=f(\bm{z})
because PCA cannot be applied in the prior latent space
\mathcal{Z}
.
This PCA gives a basis
\bm{V}
for
\mathcal{W}
. The PCA coordinates
\bm{x}_{j}
of each
\bm{w}
are computed by
\bm{x}_{j}=\bm{V}^{\top}(\bm{w}_{j}-\bm{\mu})

where
\bm{\mu}
is the mean of
\bm{w}
. By employing linear regression, the latent basis vector
\bm{n}_{k}
on
\mathcal{Z}
is calculated by:

\displaystyle\bm{n}_{k}=\argmin\sum_{j}\|\bm{n}_{k}\bm{x}^{k}_{j}-\bm{z}_{j}\|^{2}.

(6)

InterfaceGAN
[
26
]
obtains a linear hyperplane in the latent space that can well separate the data into positive and negative groups from latent codes and corresponding attributes by using a support vector machine.

4
Experiments

We evaluate the proposed latent space from two aspects: reconstruction quality and editing quality.
For the reconstruction quality comparison, we verify that our latent space does not underperform the compared spaces. For the editing quality comparison, we demonstrate that our latent space preserves the perceptual quality of edited images better than the other spaces.

Implementation details.

For the inversion, we iteratively update the latent code 1200 times with the Adam optimizer.
We set learning rate of 0.01 and
\lambda_{\textrm{enc}}=\lambda_{\textrm{per}}=\lambda_{reg}=10.0
.

5
Conclusion

We revisit
\mathcal{Z}
space for GAN inversion to yield a better trade-off between reconstruction quality and editing quality. We integrate bounded latent space
\mathcal{Z}^{+}
with the hyperspherical prior instead of
\mathcal{W}^{+}
into the space with rich representative capacity, resulting in the presented space (
e.g
.
,
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{Z}^{+}
). Our thorough experiments on PTI, SAM,
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{W}^{+}
, and
\mathcal{F}/\mathcal{S}
demonstrate that we can preserve perceptual quality of edited images while maintaining sufficient reconstruction quality on par with baseline methods by replacing unbounded space (
e.g
.
,
\mathcal{W}^{+}
) to
\mathcal{Z}^{+}
.

Broader impact.

In this study, we investigate the hyperspherical prior of unconditional 2D StyleGAN.
While diffusion models have undergone rapid development, StyleGAN-based have demonstrated competitiveness in 3D generation
[
5
,
28
]
and text-to-image generation
[
24
]
. Besides unconditional 2D StyleGAN, this study has the potential to contribute to these approaches.
Additionally, our approach offers a promising way to enhance inversion techniques for diffusion models with a hyperspherical prior such as the CLIP
[
21
]
embedding since such prior also has a bounded nature like
\mathcal{Z}^{+}
.

Limitation.

Our approach achieves a better trade-off between reconstruction quality and editing quality than popularly used latent spaces. However, since most attempts for GAN inversion and latent editing have focused on the
\mathcal{W}
,
\mathcal{W}^{+}
, and
\mathcal{S}
spaces, the semantic directions in
\mathcal{Z}
and
\mathcal{Z}^{+}
are less disentangled than in the well-explored spaces.

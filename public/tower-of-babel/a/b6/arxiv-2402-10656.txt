Local interpolation techniques for higher-order singular perturbations of non-convex functionals: free-discontinuity problems
Margherita Solci

Abstract
We develop a general approach, using local interpolation inequalities, to non-convex integral functionals depending on the gradient with a singular perturbation by derivatives of order k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 . When applied to functionals giving rise to free-discontinuity energies, such methods permit to change boundary values for derivatives up to order k − 1 k-1 in problems defining density functions for the jump part, thus allowing to prove optimal-profile formulas, and to deduce compactness and lower bounds. As an application, we prove that for k k -th order perturbations of energies depending on the gradient behaving as a constant at infinity, the jump energy density is a constant m k m_{k} times the k k -th root of the jump size. The result is first proved for truncated quadratic energy densities and in the one-dimensional case, from which the general higher-dimensional case can be obtained by slicing techniques. A wide class of non-convex energies can be studied as an envelope of these particular ones. Finally, we remark that an approximation of the Mumford–Shah functional can be obtained by letting k k tend to infinity. We also derive a new approximation of the Blake-Zisserman functional. MSC codes: 49J45, 49J10, 26B30, 35B25, 74A45. Keywords: Γ \Gamma -convergence, non-convex energies, free-discontinuity problems, Mumford-Shah functional.

Introduction

In this paper we propose a general approach to the treatment of variational problems with non-convex energies singularly perturbed by a term with higher-order derivatives. Such perturbations are commonly used to define particular solutions to problems that otherwise possess no solution or a multiplicity of solutions.
The use of higher-order derivatives, beside its theoretical interest, is justified as
a part of a continuum higher-order expansion of atomistic models with long-range interactions (such as in
[
18
,
15
]
) or of finite-difference continuum energies as in
[
29
,
30
]
. Moreover, it guarantees higher regularity of minimizers, and, as a by-product, more stable numerical approximations (for the corresponding phase-field analogs, see e.g.
[
5
,
10
,
26
]
). Leaving aside topological singularities arising for vector-valued functions such as in the Ginzburg–Landau theory
[
8
,
37
]
, there are two principal examples of this approach: perturbations of double-well energies leading to phase transitions
[
34
]
, and non-convex energies with a “well at infinity” such as Lennard-Jones or Perona–Malik energies, leading to free-discontinuity problems
[
2
,
7
,
12
,
31
]
.

Non-convex integrals of the gradient of a function
u
, with integrands bounded or with sublinear growth, cannot be directly used in variational theories, since their lower-semicontinuous envelope (in any useful topology) is a constant, but possess properties which are useful e.g. for theories of Image Reconstruction. They can be chosen to be equal (or close) to the Dirichlet integral for small values of the gradient, while their derivative is small for large values of the gradient, so that discontinuity sets for
u
, representing the contour of an image, formally do not move for the gradient flow of such energies, while they follow a regularizing heat flow elsewhere
[
32
]
. A prototypical such integrand is the truncated quadratic potential, for which, in order to use a variational approach, a scaling argument (formalized in
[
17
]
using a renormalization-group approach) suggests to introduce a small parameter
\varepsilon>0
and consider the family of functionals

\int_{\Omega}\min\Big\{|\nabla u|^{2},\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big\}\,dx,

(1)

after which, in order to overcome non-convexity, this energy can then be discretized as in the works of Chambolle
[
21
]
or transformed in a truncated version of a Gagliardo seminorm as in a late conjecture by De Giorgi proved by Gobbino
[
29
]
, or mollified by taking some local averaged gradient
[

]

Such energies are shown to

-norm, in this case considering functionals of the form

F_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{\Omega}\min\Big\{|\nabla u|^{2},\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big\}\,dx+\varepsilon^{2k-1}\int_{\Omega}\|\nabla^{k}u\|^{2}\,dx.

(2)

The power

The object of this paper are methods to address the asymptotic analysis of energies (

As an application and motivation for our interpolation techniques, we give an answer to a long-standing question arisen from

]

]

The central issue in this asymptotic analysis is the possibility of estimating the energy of optimal transitions at jump points. The core

(defined as maximal intervals where

is in the non-convexity zone; that is, where

is given by

\displaystyle\varphi_{\varepsilon}(z):=\min_{T,v}\bigg\{T+\int_{0}^{T}|v^{(k)}|^{2}\,dt:v(T)-v(0)=z,v^{\prime}(0)=v^{\prime}(T)\in\{\pm\sqrt{\varepsilon}\},

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826ptv^{(\ell)}(0)=v^{(\ell)}(T)=0\hbox{ for }\ell\in\{2,\ldots,k-1\}\Big\},

Note that this ansatz is trivial if

since in this case there is no condition on derivatives higher that

at the endpoints of a transition interval. That case has been studied in

, with

The main point in validating this ansatz for

is the possibility of modifying the scaling argument so as to obtain homogeneous conditions for the higher-order derivatives of test functions.
As for many problems in the Calculus of Variations, the
possibility of changing boundary values is of central importance since it allows to describe averaged or concentrating quantities by means of homogenization formulas or optimal-profile problems. A simple example is illustrated in the notes by Alberti
[
1
]
describing phase-transition energies of Modica–Mortola type with two wells in
-1
and
1
. That one is a somewhat simpler case since for phase transitions a single optimal-transition problem must be analyzed, while for free-discontinuity problems the analog of an optimal transition must be constructed for each possible value of the discontinuity. Alberti notes that one can study only the contribution of the energy on intervals where the functions take the value
-1+\eta
and
1-\eta
, respectively, at the endpoints, with
\eta
a small positive parameter. Test functions then are easily constructed by extending the functions to take the value
-1
and
1
, respectively, at the endpoints of a slightly larger interval, up to an additional energy tending to
0
as
\eta\to 0
. An optimal transition cost is then obtained by optimizing on the transition functions and the interval length. In the gradient theory of phase transitions, the existence of points with values close to the ‘wells’ is simply obtained from the finiteness of the energy. In our case, a similar argument would be possible if we prove that at the endpoint of a transition interval all derivatives up to order
k-1
are small (after scaling). This seems very difficult, if true, to prove directly. We then relax our
anstaz
transforming it in an asymptotic problem on a sequence
u_{\varepsilon}
with equibounded
F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
, and in the study of intervals where

(which play the role of the sets where

is ‘close to the wells’ for phase transitions).The main tool that we use is an interpolation inequality (applied to

) as follows (see Lemma
7

Lemma.

There exists a constant

depending only on

\varepsilon\in(0,1)

This interpolation allows, instead of considering the problems on transition intervals defined as above, to prove that slightly larger intervals exist at the endpoints of which all higher derivatives of

For this purpose a very careful analysis, which is the main and most technical part of the work, has to be carried on by iteratively using interpolation inequalities.
We note the local application of these techniques, which allows to exploit additional properties of
u_{\varepsilon}
on these intervals and does not require global assumptions. The analysis becomes quite complex if the transition intervals are of very small length, since at that scale the effect of the non-convexity and of the perturbation are difficult to untangle. In that case, we cannot directly prove that the measure of the part interior to the intervals where

is below the threshold is negligible, and a smaller threshold for

must be introduced.

The application of our interpolation techniques allows to use as a lower bound the functions

Finally, we note the flexibility of our local interpolation methods, which have already been adapted and simplified to treat phase-transition problems

Statement and preliminaries

As noted in the Introduction, the relevant analysis is carried on in dimension one, and for a special sequence of functionals, since the study all other functionals can be reduced to dimension one by slicing and to envelopes of limits of sequences as below.

For

The results are valid on a generic bounded interval.

The limit behaviour of

Theorem 1

The functionals (

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{-\frac{T^{N}_{j}}{2}}^{\frac{T^{N}_{j}}{2}}((v^{N}_{j})^{(k)})^{2}\,dt=0.

We scale the functions to
(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
by setting
w^{N}_{j}(t)=v^{N}_{j}(T^{N}_{j}t)
, and note that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}((w^{N}_{j})^{(k)})^{2}\,dt=0.

Using iteratively the Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality we deduce that there exist polynomials
P_{j}
or degree at most
k-1
such that

\lim_{j\to+\infty}\|w^{N}_{j}-P_{j}\|_{H^{k}(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})}=0.

Note that
(w^{N}_{j})^{(\ell)}(t)=(T^{N}_{j})^{\ell}(v^{N}_{j})^{(\ell)}(T^{N}_{j}t)
, so that, by the convergence in
C^{k-1}([-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}])
of
w^{N}_{j}-P_{j}
to
0
, in particular we have

\lim_{j\to+\infty}P^{(\ell)}_{j}\big(\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\big)=\lim_{j\to+\infty}(w^{N}_{j})^{(\ell)}\big(\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\big)=0\quad\hbox{ for all }\ell\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\,.

(14)

Note that (
14
) provides
a sequence of linear systems
in the
k-1
coefficients of
P_{j}^{\prime}
,
with the right-hand side tending to
0
.
Since
2n\geq k
, the number of independent equations in these systems is exactly
k-1
.
This shows that
P_{j}^{\prime}

tend to
0
as
j\to+\infty
,
giving a contradiction since

\lim_{j\to+\infty}P_{j}\big(\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\big)=\lim_{j\to+\infty}w^{N}_{j}\big(\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\big)=\pm\tfrac{1}{2}.

In the special case
n=k-1
, we set
m_{k}(N)=m^{k-1}_{k}(N)
; that is,

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76219ptm_{k}(N)=\inf_{T>0}\min\bigg\{T+\int_{-\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{T}{2}}(v^{(k)})^{2}\,dt:v\in H^{k}\big(-\tfrac{T}{2},\tfrac{T}{2}\big),\ v\big(\pm\tfrac{T}{2}\big)=\pm\tfrac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 68.28644pt\ \hbox{\rm and }\ \big|v^{(\ell)}\big(\pm\tfrac{T}{2}\big)\big|\leq\tfrac{1}{N}\ \hbox{\rm for all }\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}\Big\}.

(15)

Lemma 5

(Convergence of approximate surface energy densities)
.

The sequence
m_{k}^{n}(N)
increasingly converges to

\displaystyle\hskip-34.1433ptm_{k}^{n}:=\inf_{T>0}\min\bigg\{T+\int_{-\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{T}{2}}(v^{(k)})^{2}\,dt:v\in H^{k}\big(-\tfrac{T}{2},\tfrac{T}{2}\big),\ v\big(\pm\tfrac{T}{2}\big)=\pm\tfrac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\hbox{\rm and }\ v^{(\ell)}\big(\pm\tfrac{T}{2}\big)=0\ \hbox{\rm for all }\ell\in\{1,\ldots,n\}\Big\}.

(16)

as
N\to+\infty
; in particular, for
n=k-1
, we have that
m_{k}(N)\to m_{k}
defined by (
1
). As a consequence,
m^{n}_{k}
and

m_{k}
are strictly positive.

Proof.

By definition,
m^{n}_{k}(N)
is increasing in
N
, and
m^{n}_{k}(N)\leq m^{n}_{k}
, so that

\limsup_{N\to+\infty}m^{n}_{k}(N)\leq m^{n}_{k}.

Conversely, let
(T_{N},v_{N})
be a minimizing pair for
m^{n}_{k}(N)
.
By Remark
4
, up to subsequences we can suppose that
T_{N}\to T_{0}\in(0,+\infty)
.
Up to an asymptotically negligible scaling, we can suppose that
T_{N}=T_{0}
,
and
v_{N}
tend to some
v_{0}
weakly in
H^{k}(-\frac{T_{0}}{2},\frac{T_{0}}{2})
. By the convergence to
0
of the derivatives up to order
n
,
(T_{0},v_{0})
is a test pair for
m^{n}_{k}
.
By the lower semicontinuity of the
L^{2}
-norm we then have

m^{n}_{k}\leq T_{0}+\int_{-\frac{T_{0}}{2}}^{\frac{T_{0}}{2}}(v_{0}^{(k)})^{2}\,dt\leq\liminf_{N\to+\infty}m^{n}_{k}(N),

which proves that
m^{n}_{k}
is the limit of
m^{n}_{k}(N)
, as desired.
∎

3
Interpolation methods for higher-order derivatives ‘below the threshold’

In this section we develop a way to estimate the sets where a function
u_{\varepsilon}
is ‘below the threshold’; that is,
|u^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\leq 1/\varepsilon
, in terms of the value of their higher-order derivatives up to
k-1
, with the objective to show that sets where all derivatives are ‘small’ are sufficiently uniformly dense.

We assume
k\geq 3
since the case
k=2
has been dealt with in
[
2
]
.
We will use the following notation for the localized version of the functionals

F_{\varepsilon}(u;I)=\int_{I}\Big(f_{\varepsilon}(u^{\prime})+\varepsilon^{2k-1}(u^{(k)})^{2}\Big)\,dt

on a subset
I
of
(0,1)
.

We fix a family
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
such that

S:=\sup_{\varepsilon>0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})<+\infty.

(17)

Since
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
is continuous, the set where
u^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}
is below the threshold can be written as

\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}:=\Big\{t\in(0,1):|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)|<\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\Big\}=\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}}I_{j},

where
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
is a countable set of indices,
each
I_{j}=(a_{j},b_{j})
is a maximal open interval; that is,
either
|u^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}
at the endpoints or the endpoints belong to
\{0,1\}
, and the intervals are pairwise disjoint.

Note that by the equiboundedness of
F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
we have

|(0,1)\setminus\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}|=\Big|\Big\{t\in(0,1):|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)|\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\Big\}\Big|\leq\varepsilon S.

(18)

In this section we estimate the size of the intervals
I_{j}
that do not contain points where all derivatives up to order
k-1
are small.
This will be a technical tool used in the proof of the coerciveness and of the lower estimate for the
\Gamma
-limit.

To describe such intervals, for any fixed
N\geq 1
and
for any
\varepsilon>0
we introduce the set
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(N)
of the indices
j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
such that

\Big|\Big\{t\in I_{j}:|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(t)|<\frac{1}{N\varepsilon^{\ell}}\ \hbox{\rm for all }\ell\in\{2,\dots,k-1\}\Big\}\Big|>0.

(19)

We now estimate the length of intervals
I_{j}
such that
j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(N)
.

Lemma 6

(Estimate for intervals with “large derivatives”)
.

Let
N\geq 1
be fixed.
There exists a positive constant
R_{k}(N)
such that
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
I\subseteq(0,1)
open interval satisfying

(i)

|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)|<\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}
for all
t\in I
;

(ii)

the set
\{t\in I:|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(t)|<\frac{1}{N\varepsilon^{\ell}}\ \hbox{\rm for all }\ell\in\{2,\dots,k-1\}\}
has measure
0
,

it holds

|I|\leq R_{k}(N)\varepsilon^{\frac{2k-3}{2k-4}}.

In particular, the estimate holds for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
I_{j}
with
j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(N)
.

The proof is based on the following interpolation lemma.

Lemma 7

(Interpolation)
.

Let
k\geq 3
be fixed.
There exists a constant
R_{k}>0
(depending only on
k
) such that for any bounded interval
I
,
u\in H^{k}(I)
,
\ell\in\{2,\dots,k-1\}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
the following estimate holds

\varepsilon^{\gamma_{\ell}}\|u^{(\ell)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}\leq R_{k}\Big(\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}+\varepsilon^{2k-1}\|u^{(k)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}+\frac{\varepsilon^{\gamma_{\ell}}}{|I|^{2(\ell-1)}}\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}\Big),

(20)

where
\gamma_{\ell}=\frac{2k-1}{k-1}(\ell-1)
.

Proof.

We recall a classical interpolation inequality stating that,
given
n,r\in\mathbb{N}
with
n>r
, there exists a constant
C(r,n)>0
such that
for any
I
open bounded interval and
v\in H^{n}(I)
the following estimate holds

\|v^{(r)}\|_{L^{2}(I)}\leq C(r,n)\Big(\|v\|^{\theta}_{L^{2}(I)}\|v^{(n)}\|^{1-\theta}_{L^{2}(I)}+\frac{\|v\|_{L^{2}(I)}}{|I|^{r}}\Big),

where
\theta=\frac{n-r}{n}
(see for instance
[
33
, Theorem 7.41]
).
Applying this inequality with
v=u^{\prime}
and
n=k-1
, for all
\ell\in\{2,\dots,k-1\}
we have that

\|u^{(\ell)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}\leq R_{k}\|u^{\prime}\|^{2\theta}_{L^{2}(I)}\|u^{(k)}\|^{2(1-\theta)}_{L^{2}(I)}+R_{k}\frac{\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}}{|I|^{2(\ell-1)}},

(21)

where
\theta=\frac{k-\ell}{k-1}
and
R_{k}=2\max\limits_{2\leq\ell\leq k-1}\big(C(\ell-1,k-1)\big)^{2}
.
Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
p=\frac{1}{\theta}=\frac{k-1}{k-\ell}
and

q=\frac{1}{1-\theta}=\frac{k-1}{\ell-1}
. By convexity, it follows that

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\alpha}\|u^{(k)}\|^{2(1-\theta)}_{L^{2}(I)}\|u^{\prime}\|^{2\theta}_{L^{2}(I)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q}\Big(\varepsilon^{\alpha}\|u^{(k)}\|^{2(1-\theta)}_{L^{2}(I)}\Big)^{q}+\frac{1}{p}\Big(\|u^{\prime}\|^{2\theta}_{L^{2}(I)}\Big)^{p}

(22)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\alpha\frac{k-1}{\ell-1}}\|u^{(k)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}+\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}.

By multiplying (
21
) by
\varepsilon^{\gamma_{\ell}}
, and applying estimate (
22
) with the choice
\alpha=\gamma_{\ell}=\frac{2k-1}{k-1}(\ell-1)
, we obtain (
20
), concluding the proof.
∎

We can now prove Lemma
6
.

Proof of Lemma
6
.

Let
N\geq 1
be fixed, and
let
I
satisfy hypotheses (i) and (ii).
For any
\ell\in\{2,\dots,k-1\},
we set

I_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}=\Big\{t\in I:|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(t)|\geq\frac{1}{N\varepsilon^{\ell}}\Big\}.

Since (ii) holds, we deduce that there exists
\ell\in\{2,\dots,k-1\}
such that

|I^{\ell}_{\varepsilon}|\geq\frac{|I|}{k+1}
.
Indeed, otherwise the measure of the set where at least one of the derivatives is greater than the threshold would be strictly less than
|I|
, contradicting the hypothesis (ii).
We apply (
20
) for this value of
\ell
to the interval
I
and to the function
u_{\varepsilon}
; since
F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};I)\leq S
and
\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}<\frac{|I|}{\varepsilon}
, we obtain

\frac{|I|}{k}\frac{1}{N^{2}\varepsilon^{2\ell}}\leq\|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}\leq R_{k}\Big(S\varepsilon^{-\gamma_{\ell}}+\frac{1}{|I|^{2(\ell-1)}}\frac{|I|}{\varepsilon}\Big),

with
\gamma_{\ell}=\frac{2k-1}{k-1}(\ell-1)
.
By this inequality, we deduce that either

\frac{|I|}{k}\frac{1}{N^{2}\varepsilon^{2\ell}}\leq 2R_{k}S\varepsilon^{-\gamma_{\ell}},

which implies

|I|\leq 2R_{k}kSN^{2}\varepsilon^{2\ell-\gamma_{\ell}}=2R_{k}kSN^{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{2k-\ell-1}{k-1}}\leq 2R_{k}kSN^{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{k}{k-1}}.

or

\frac{|I|}{k}\frac{1}{N^{2}\varepsilon^{2\ell}}\leq 2R_{k}\frac{1}{|I|^{2(\ell-1)}}\frac{|I|}{\varepsilon}.

In this last case, we deduce that
|I|^{2(\ell-1)}\leq 2R_{k}kN^{2}\varepsilon^{2\ell-1}
, which gives

|I|\leq(2R_{k}kN^{2})^{\frac{1}{{2(\ell-1)}}}\varepsilon^{\frac{2\ell-1}{2(\ell-1)}}\leq(2R_{k}kN^{2})^{\frac{1}{{2(\ell-1)}}}\varepsilon^{\frac{2k-3}{2k-4}}

since
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
If we set, with a slight abuse of notation,

R_{k}(N)=\max\big\{2R_{k}kSN^{2},\max_{2\leq\ell\leq k-1}\{(2R_{k}kN^{2})^{\frac{1}{{2(\ell-1)}}}\}\big\},

we then get the claim.
∎

For each
j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(N)
, let
I_{j}=(a_{j},b_{j})
and define

\left.\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69054pta_{j}^{N}=\inf\big\{t\in[a_{j},b_{j}):|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(t)|\leq\frac{1}{N\varepsilon^{\ell}}\ \hbox{\rm for all }\ell\in\{2,\ldots,k-1\}\big\},\\
b_{j}^{N}=\sup\big\{t\in(a_{j},b_{j}]:|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(t)|\leq\frac{1}{N\varepsilon^{\ell}}\ \hbox{\rm for all }\ell\in\{2,\ldots,k-1\}\big\};\end{array}\right.

(23)

that is,
(a_{j}^{N},b_{j}^{N})
is the maximal subinterval of
I_{j}
such that
the inequality
|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}|\leq\frac{1}{N\varepsilon^{\ell}}

holds at the endpoints.
Note that by Lemma
6
we have the estimates

|(a_{j},a_{j}^{N})|\leq R_{k}(N)\varepsilon^{\frac{2k-3}{2k-4}}\ \ \ \hbox{\rm and }\ \ |(b_{j}^{N},b_{j})|\leq R_{k}(N)\varepsilon^{\frac{2k-3}{2k-4}}.

(24)

Let
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(N)
denote the union of these subintevals; that is,

\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(N)=\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(N)}(a_{j}^{N},b_{j}^{N}).

(25)

In the next two statements, we will prove that in the complement of
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(N)
the intervals where
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
is below the threshold are asymptotically negligible with respect to the rest.

Lemma 8
.

Let
N
be such that
2N^{2}S\geq 1
.
Let
I\subseteq(0,1)
be an open interval such that

(i)

|I|\leq\frac{1}{2N^{2}S}\varepsilon
;

(ii)

I\cap\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(N)=\emptyset
.

Then,

\#\{j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}:I_{j}\cap I\neq\emptyset\}\leq k.

Proof.

Set
I=(a,b)
and
I_{j}=(a_{j},b_{j})
for all
j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
.
Note that if
I\cap I_{j}\neq\emptyset
and
I_{j}\not\subseteq I
, then either
I_{j}\cap I=(a,b_{j})
or
I_{j}\cap I=(a_{j},b)
. Since
I\cap\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(N)=\emptyset
, this holds for all
j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(N)
such that
I\cap I_{j}\not=\emptyset
.
To prove the claim, it is then sufficient to show that

\#\{j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(N):I_{j}\subset I\neq\emptyset\}\leq k-2.

By contradiction, suppose that there exists a family
\{j_{1},\dots,j_{k-1}\}
of
k-1
indices in
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(N)
such that

I_{j_{n}}\subset I
for all
n\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}
and the families

a_{j_{n}}
and
b_{j_{n}}
are strictly increasing with
n
.
This implies the existence of at least
k-2
points in
I
such that
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}=0
. Indeed, let
n<k-1
. Either
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(a_{j_{n}})=u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(b_{j_{n}})
, or
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(b_{j_{n}})=u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(a_{j_{n+1}})
; in both cases,
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}
vanishes at some
t_{n}\in(a_{j_{n}},a_{j_{n+1}})
.
An iteration of the application of the Rolle Theorem allows us to deduce that
for any
\ell\in\{2,\dots,k-1\}
there exists a point
x_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}\in I
such that
u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(x_{\varepsilon}^{\ell})=0
.
By the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus we then obtain the estimate

\|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}\|_{L^{2}(I)}\leq|I|^{k-\ell}\|u_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}(I)}

(26)

for any
\ell\in\{2,\dots,k-1\}
.

On the other hand, since for all
n
we have that
|\{t\in(a_{j_{n}},b_{j_{n}}):|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(t)|<\frac{1}{N\varepsilon^{\ell}}\ \hbox{\rm for all }\ell\in\{2,\dots,k-1\}\}|=0
, there exist an integer
\ell\in\{2,\dots,k-1\}
and a point

y_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}\in I
satisfying
|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(y_{\varepsilon}^{\ell})|\geq\frac{1}{N\varepsilon^{\ell}}
.
Hence,

|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(x_{\varepsilon}^{\ell})-u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(y_{\varepsilon}^{\ell})|\geq\frac{1}{N\varepsilon^{\ell}}.

Note that the function
u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}
is Hölder-continuous with exponent
\frac{1}{2}
and
constant
C_{\varepsilon}=\|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell+1)}\|_{L^{2}(I)}
; that is,

|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(t)-u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(s)|\leq\|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell+1)}\|_{L^{2}(I)}\sqrt{|t-s|}

(27)

for all
t,s\in I
. By using (
26
) in (
27
) applied with
t=x_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}
and
s=y_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}
we then deduce

\frac{1}{N\varepsilon^{\ell}}\leq|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(x_{\varepsilon}^{\ell})-u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(y_{\varepsilon}^{\ell})|\leq|I|^{k-(\ell+1)}\|u_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}\|_{L^{2}(I)}\sqrt{|x_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}-y_{\varepsilon}^{\ell}|}.

By multiplying by
\varepsilon^{\ell}
, recalling that
\varepsilon^{2k-1}\|u_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I)}\leq F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};I)\leq S
we obtain the estimate

\frac{1}{N}\leq|I|^{k-\ell-\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon^{\ell-k+\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{S}.

The hypothesis
|I|\leq\frac{1}{2N^{2}S}\varepsilon
on the length of

then gives

\frac{1}{N}\leq\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{2N^{2}S}\Big)^{k-\ell-\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon^{\ell-k+\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{S}=\Big(\frac{1}{2N^{2}S}\Big)^{k-\ell-\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{S}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}N},

.
This is a contradiction, proving the claim.
∎

Proposition 9

(Bound for the relative measure of intervals below threshold)
.

Let
N\geq 1
be a fixed integer such that
2N^{2}S\geq 1
. Then, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and for any interval

I
such that

I\cap\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(N)=\emptyset,

(28)

the following estimate holds

|I\cap\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}|\leq kR_{k}(N)\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2(k-2)}}(2N^{2}S|I|+\varepsilon).

Proof.

Note that we can apply Lemma
6
to
I_{j}
with
j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(N)
, and to the intervals
(a_{j},a_{j}^{N})
and

as in (

),
obtaining that for each
I_{j}
such that

(29)

holds.

We now consider separately the cases
|I|\leq\frac{1}{2N^{2}S}\varepsilon
and
|I|>\frac{1}{2N^{2}S}\varepsilon
.

Case

.

Applying Lemma
8
we obtain that the number of

such that

.

3.1
Finer analysis of ‘small’ intervals

In the proof of the coerciveness, we will also need to asymptotically estimate this proportion in the case of a family of “small” intervals

; that is, intervals not satisfying

\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{r}=\Big\{j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}:\int_{I_{j}}(u^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})^{2}\,dt<\frac{r}{\varepsilon}|I_{j}|\Big\},

(35)

with

Lemma 12
.

There exist a threshold
r_{k}\in(0,1)

and a constant
\widetilde{C}_{k}>0

such that
for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and for any interval

(i)

in

|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}
at at least one of the endpoints;

(ii)

\int_{I}(u^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})^{2}\,dt<\frac{r_{k}}{\varepsilon}|I|

the following estimate holds

|I|\geq\widetilde{C}_{k}\varepsilon^{\frac{k}{k-1}}.

(36)

In particular, (
36
) holds for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
I_{j}
with
j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{r_{k}}
.

Proof.

Let
r\in(0,1)
be fixed, and let
I=(a,b)
be an interval as in the hypothesis, with
r
in the place of
r_{k}
and with
|u^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(a)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}

(the case when
|u^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(b)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}
can be treated exactly in the same way).
Then, there exists
t_{\varepsilon}\in(a,b)
(also depending on

) such that
|u^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(t_{\varepsilon})|<\frac{\sqrt{r}}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}
.
It follows that

\int_{I}(u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime})^{2}\,dt\geq\int_{a}^{t_{\varepsilon}}(u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime})^{2}\,dt\geq\frac{1}{|t_{\varepsilon}-a|}\Big(\int_{a}^{t_{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}\,dt\Big)^{2}\geq\frac{(1-\sqrt{r})^{2}}{\varepsilon(b-a)}.

(37)

Now, by using (
37
) and the interpolation inequality (

) with
\ell=2
, we can estimate

\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}(1-\sqrt{r})^{2}}{\varepsilon|I|}\leq R_{k}S+R_{k}\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}}{|I|^{2}}\frac{r}{\varepsilon}|I|

(38)

since

.
Then,

\hbox{\rm either }\ \ \frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}(1-\sqrt{r})^{2}}{\varepsilon|I|}\leq 2R_{k}S\ \ \hbox{\rm or }\ \ \frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}(1-\sqrt{r})^{2}}{\varepsilon|I|}\leq 2R_{k}\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{2k-1}{k-1}}}{\varepsilon|I|}r.

which is the claim with
\widetilde{C}_{k}=\frac{(1-\sqrt{r_{k}})^{2}}{2R_{k}S}

∎

We now prove that, except for a set with total measure of order strictly less than

and for any

Lemma 13
.

Let

In this section, we prove Theorem

Let
N\geq 1
be such that
2N^{2}S\geq 1
.
For any

a_{j}^{N}
and

23

(a_{j}^{N},b_{j}^{N})=I_{j}^{N}

of

such that
|u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}|\leq\frac{1}{N\varepsilon^{\ell}}

\ell\in\{2,\ldots,k-1\}

We define

a_{\varepsilon}=\inf\{a_{j}^{N}:\ j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(N)\}\ \ \hbox{\rm and }\ \ b_{\varepsilon}=\sup\{b_{j}^{N}:\ j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(N)\},

noting that by Remark
10
we have that

|(0,1)\setminus(a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon})|=O(\varepsilon)_{\varepsilon\to 0}.

(47)

We now describe the complementary set in
(a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon})
of
the set
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(N)
given by the union of the intervals
(a_{j}^{N},b_{j}^{N})
, as defined in (
25

To this end, for

\displaystyle\tau(t)=\sup\{b_{j}^{N}:b_{j}^{N}\leq t,\ j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(N)\};

\displaystyle\sigma(t)=\inf\{a_{j}^{N}:a_{j}^{N}\geq t,\ j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(N)\},

noting that the sets are not empty since

\tau(t)
and
\sigma(t)

From these definitions, the following properties hold for all

(i)

;

(ii)

if

for

I_{j}\subset[\tau(t),\sigma(t)]

(iii)

(a_{j},a_{j}^{N}]\subset[\tau(t),\sigma(t)]

[b_{j}^{N},b_{j})
;

(iv)

by the continuity of the derivatives of
u_{\varepsilon}
,

|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\tau(t))|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\ \ \hbox{\rm and }\ \ |u_{\varepsilon}^{(\ell)}(\tau(t))|\leq\frac{1}{N\varepsilon^{\ell}}\ \ \hbox{\rm for all }\ \ell\in\{2,\dots,k-1\},

and the same properties hold in
\sigma(t)

Moreover, the intervals
(\tau(t),\sigma(t))
either coincide or are disjoint; indeed,

s\in(\tau(t),\sigma(t))
for some

(\tau(t),\sigma(t))=(\tau(s),\sigma(s))
.
Consider now a point
t
such that the corresponding interval is a point; that is,

\tau(t)=\sigma(t)=t
. In this case,
the common value also coincides with
\sup\{b_{j}:b_{j}\leq t,\ j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(N)\}
and with

\inf\{a_{j}:a_{j}\geq t,\ j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(N)\}

|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(a_{j})|=|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(b_{j})|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}

u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
we also have

|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}
.
It follows that

J(\varepsilon):=\Big\{t\in(a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon})\setminus\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(N):\tau(t)=\sigma(t)\Big\}\subset\Big\{t:|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\Big\},

|J(\varepsilon)|\leq\varepsilon S
.
Then, we can write
the set
(a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon})\setminus\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(N)
as the union of a set with measure of order
\varepsilon
and of a disjoint union of intervals of the form
(\tau(t),\sigma(t))
; that is, there exists a countable set of indices
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}(N)
such that

(a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon})\setminus\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(N)=J(\varepsilon)\cup\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}(N)}(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda}),

where
the intervals
(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})
are pairwise disjoint and the endpoints
\tau_{\lambda}
and
\sigma_{\lambda}

belong to the sets
\{\tau(t):t\in(a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon})\setminus\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(N)\}
and
\{\sigma(t):t\in(a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon})\setminus\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}(N)\}
, respectively.

Moreover, by Remark
10
we deduce that for
\varepsilon
small enough; more precisely, for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{k}(N))
, where
\widetilde{\varepsilon}_{k}(N)
is given in Remark
10

|(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})|\leq 4S\varepsilon

(48)

holds for any
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}(N)
.

In the proof of the equicoerciveness and of the lower bound for the
\Gamma
-limit,
we will use some estimates for the energy in each
(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})
in dependence of the value

z_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}=u_{\varepsilon}(\sigma_{\lambda})-u_{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\lambda}),

(49)

and a consequent global estimate for the energy in the union of these sets.

To obtain these estimates, we introduce for any interval
I\subset(0,1)
the minimum problem

\mu_{\varepsilon}(I)=\min_{v}\Big\{\frac{|I|}{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{2k-1}\int_{I}(v^{(k)})^{2}\,dt\Big\},

(50)

where the minimum is taken over the set of functions
v\in H^{k}(I)
satisfying the same boundary conditions of the function
u_{\varepsilon}
.
In order to provide a lower estimate for
\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau,\sigma)
by changing the variable in the minimum problem, we will choose a scaling parameter
\beta_{\varepsilon}>0
in dependence of the value of
|z_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}|
, and for any
v
admissible test function for the problem (
50
), we will define the scaled function
w
by setting

v(t)=z_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}w\Big(\frac{t}{\beta_{\varepsilon}}-\frac{\tau+\sigma}{2\beta_{\varepsilon}}\Big).

(51)

In order to give a lower bound for
F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
, it is convenient to introduce a further parameter
\theta\in(0,1)
and separately consider the family of indices
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}(N)
such that
|z_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}|\geq\theta
and

|z_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}|<\theta
, respectively.

The first estimate concerns the energy on intervals
(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})
such that
|u_{\varepsilon}(\sigma_{\lambda})-u_{\varepsilon}(\sigma_{\lambda})|\geq\theta
. Let
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{+}(N,\theta)
denote the set of such indices in

Proposition 15

(Lower estimate in the case
|z_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}|\geq\theta
)
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1)
be fixed.
There exists an integer
N(\theta)
such that for all

N\geq N(\theta)
and
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{N^{2}})
,
the following estimate holds:

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{+}(N,\theta)}\!F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda}))\geq C^{N}_{\varepsilon}\,m_{k}(N\theta)\!\!\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{+}(N,\theta)}\!\!|u_{\varepsilon}(\sigma_{\lambda})-u_{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\lambda})|^{\frac{1}{k}},

(52)

where
C^{N}_{\varepsilon}=1-4kR_{k}(N)N^{2}S\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2(k-2)}}
and
m_{k}(\cdot)
is the minimum defined in (
2.2
).

Proof.

We start by proving that, if
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{+}(N,\theta)
, the measure of the intersection between
(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})
and
the set
\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}
of the points where
|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|<\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}

is asymptotically negligible with respect to
|(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})|
.
Indeed, for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{+}(N,\theta)
we define the scaled function

v_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}
such that
u_{\varepsilon}(t)=z_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}v^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}(\frac{t-\tau_{\lambda}}{\sigma_{\lambda}-\tau_{\lambda}})
, obtaining

\displaystyle S\geq F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{2k-1}(z_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda})^{2}}{(\sigma_{\lambda}-\tau_{\lambda})^{2k-1}}\int_{0}^{1}((v^{\lambda}_{\varepsilon})^{(k)})^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\varepsilon^{2k-1}(z_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda})^{2}}{(\sigma_{\lambda}-\tau_{\lambda})^{2k-1}}m_{\theta}(N),

where

\displaystyle m_{\theta}(N)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\Big\{\int_{0}^{1}(v^{(k)})^{2}\,ds:v\in H^{k}(0,1),v(1)-v(0)=1,

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt|v^{(\ell)}(0)|\leq\frac{1}{\theta N},|v^{(\ell)}(1)|\leq\frac{1}{\theta N}\ \hbox{\rm for all }\ell\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}\Big\}.

Note that
m_{\theta}(N)\to+\infty
as
N\to+\infty
. Then, there exists a positive integer
N(\theta)
such that
m_{\theta}(N)\geq 1
and

(\frac{\theta^{2}}{S})^{\frac{1}{2k-1}}\geq\frac{1}{2N^{2}S}
for
N\geq N(\theta)
.
We choose
N(\theta)
also satisfying
2N(\theta)^{2}S\geq 1
. It follows that for all
N\geq N(\theta)
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)

\sigma_{\lambda}-\tau_{\lambda}\geq\varepsilon\Big(\frac{\theta^{2}}{S}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2k-1}}\geq\frac{1}{2N^{2}S}\varepsilon.

By Proposition
9

we deduce that

\displaystyle\frac{|(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})\cap\mathcal{A}_{\varepsilon}|}{|(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle kR_{k}(N)\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2(k-2)}}\Big(2N^{2}S+\frac{\varepsilon}{|(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})|}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4kR_{k}(N)N^{2}S\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2(k-2)}}

for all
N\geq N(\theta)
and for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
.
Hence,

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{+}(N,\theta)}\int_{\tau_{\lambda}}^{\sigma_{\lambda}}f_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})\,dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{+}(N,\theta)}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\Big|(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})\cap\Big\{t:|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\Big\}\Big|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-4kR_{k}(N)N^{2}S\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2(k-2)}})\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{+}(N,\theta)}\frac{\sigma_{\lambda}-\tau_{\lambda}}{\varepsilon}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{+}(N,\theta)}\!\!F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C^{N}_{\varepsilon}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{+}(N,\theta)}\!\!\Big(\frac{\sigma_{\lambda}-\tau_{\lambda}}{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{2k-1}\int_{\tau_{\lambda}}^{\sigma_{\lambda}}\!\!(u_{\varepsilon}^{(k)})^{2}\,dt\Big)

(53)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle C^{N}_{\varepsilon}\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{+}(N,\theta)}\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda}),

where
\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})
is the minimum defined in (
50
).

For any
v
admissible test function for
\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})
, we define a scaled function
w
as in (

) by choosing the scaling factor
\beta_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon|z_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{k}}

v(t)=z_{\varepsilon}w\Big(\frac{2t-(\tau_{\lambda}+\sigma_{\lambda})}{\varepsilon|z_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{k}}}\Big).

Letting
T_{\varepsilon}=\frac{\sigma_{\lambda}-\tau_{\lambda}}{\beta_{\varepsilon}}
, we obtain

\frac{\sigma_{\lambda}-\tau_{\lambda}}{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{2k-1}\int_{\tau_{\lambda}}^{\sigma_{\lambda}}(v^{(k)})^{2}\,dt=|z_{\varepsilon}|^{\frac{1}{k}}\Big(T_{\varepsilon}+\int_{-\frac{T_{\varepsilon}}{2}}^{\frac{T_{\varepsilon}}{2}}(w^{(k)})^{2}\,dt\Big),

\displaystyle\Big|w^{\prime}\Big(\pm\frac{T_{\varepsilon}}{2}\Big)\Big|\leq\sqrt{\varepsilon}|z_{\varepsilon}|^{\frac{1}{k}-1}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}{\theta}

\displaystyle\Big|w^{(\ell)}\Big(\pm\frac{T_{\varepsilon}}{2}\Big)\Big|\leq\frac{|z_{\varepsilon}|^{\frac{\ell}{k}-1}}{N}\leq\frac{1}{\theta N}\ \ \hbox{\rm for all }\ \ell\in\{2,\dots,k-1\}.

(54)

For all
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{N^{2}})
and for all
\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}_{\varepsilon}(N,\theta)
we then have

\mu_{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})\geq m_{k}(N\theta)|u_{\varepsilon}(\sigma_{\lambda})-u_{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\lambda})|^{\frac{1}{k}}.

(55)

By using this inequality in (

), we obtain the claim.
∎

Before stating a lower estimate in the case
|z_{\varepsilon}^{\lambda}|<\theta

in

We will have then to consider different scaling factors.

Given

Proposition 16

\hskip-2.84526pt\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(N,\theta,<)}\!\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};(\tau^{i}_{\lambda},\sigma^{i}_{\lambda}))\geq\!\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(N,\theta,<)}\!\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(|u_{\varepsilon}(\sigma^{i}_{\lambda})-u_{\varepsilon}(\tau^{i}_{\lambda})|)-R^{N}_{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma-\tau}{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{2k-1}\int_{\tau}^{\sigma}(v^{(k)})^{2}\,dt

We may now prove the following compactness theorem.

Theorem 17

(Compactness)
.

Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
be such that
F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq S<+\infty
. Then, there exists
u\in SBV_{2}(0,1)
such that, up to subsequences and to addition of suitable constants,

converge in measure to

Proof.

We fix
\theta\in(0,1)
and
N\geq N(\theta)
.
Let
\eta\in(0,1)
be fixed. There exists
\varepsilon_{k}(\eta)>0
such that, if
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{k}(\eta)
, then

C_{\varepsilon}^{N}\geq(1-\eta)
and
R_{\varepsilon}^{N}\leq\eta
for all
N
.
Since
m_{k}(N\theta)\geq r_{k}m_{k}^{\ell(k)}(N\theta)
,
by (
52
) and (
60
) we deduce that for all
N\geq N(\theta)
and
\varepsilon<\min\{\varepsilon_{k}(\eta),\varepsilon_{k}(\theta,N)\}

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{+}(N,\theta)\\
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(N,\theta,\geq)\end{subarray}}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda}))\geq(1-\eta)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{+}(N,\theta)\\
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(N,\theta,\geq)\end{subarray}}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(|u_{\varepsilon}(\sigma_{\lambda})-u_{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\lambda})|)

(67)

and

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(N,\theta,<)}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};(\tau^{i}_{\lambda},\sigma^{i}_{\lambda}))

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(N,\theta,<)}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(|u_{\varepsilon}(\sigma^{i}_{\lambda})-u_{\varepsilon}(\tau^{i}_{\lambda})|)-\eta

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(N,\theta,<)}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}\Big(\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}(u_{\varepsilon}(\sigma^{i}_{\lambda})-u_{\varepsilon}(\tau^{i}_{\lambda}))\Big|\Big)-\eta,

(68)

where in the last inequality we used the subadditivity of
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}
.

We now construct a sequence of functions

v_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}\in SBV_{2}(0,1)

such that

(69)

and such that

(i)

for all

(70)

(ii)

Then, we consider the intervals
(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})
with
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(N,\theta,<)

\{u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\neq(v_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N})^{\prime}\}\cap(a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon})\subseteq\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{+}(\theta,N)\\

\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(\theta,N,\geq)\end{subarray}}|(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})|\leq\frac{S\varepsilon}{1-4kR_{k}(N)N^{2}S\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2(k-2)}}}.

(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})

with
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(N,\theta,<)
.
By Lemma
13
and Remark
14
, we obtain an estimate for the lengths of the subsets of the union of
(\tau_{\lambda}^{i},\sigma_{\lambda}^{i})
where the
L^{2}
-norm of
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
is “below the threshold”
r_{k}
; that is,

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(N,\theta,<)}\Big|(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})\cap\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{r_{k}}\setminus\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(N)}I_{j}\Big|\leq R_{k}k^{2}SN^{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{k}{k-1}}

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(N,\theta,<)}\Big|(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})\cap\bigcup_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{r_{k}}\cap\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}^{\ast}(N)}\big(I_{j}\setminus(a_{j}^{\ast,N},b_{j}^{\ast,N})\big)\Big|\leq R_{k}k^{2}SN^{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{k}{k-1}}.

Noting that the remaining part of each
(\tau_{\lambda}^{i},\sigma_{\lambda}^{i})
is given by sets
A_{\lambda}^{i}
such that
\int_{A_{\lambda}^{i}}(u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})^{2}\,dt\geq\frac{r_{k}|A_{\lambda}^{i}|}{\varepsilon}
,
recalling (
35
) we deduce that the sum of the lengths of all these sets is estimated by
\frac{S\varepsilon}{r_{k}}
.
Hence, we can conclude that

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(N,\theta,<)}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}|(\tau_{\lambda}^{i},\sigma_{\lambda}^{i})|\leq R_{k}k^{2}SN^{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{k}{k-1}}+\frac{S\varepsilon}{r_{k}}.

(76)

Recalling that
(0,1)\setminus(a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon})=O(\varepsilon)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we deduce that

|\{u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\neq(v_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N})^{\prime}\}|=o(1)

as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Hence, up to addition of constants and up to subsequences,
the sequence

\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
converges in measure to
u
, concluding the proof of the compactness.
∎

We now prove a sharp lower estimate for the
\Gamma
-limit by optimizing the use of the bounds given by Propositions
15
and
16
.

Theorem 18

(Lower bound)
.

Let
u\in SBV_{2}(0,1)
. For all sequences
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
such that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in measure,
we have

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\int_{0}^{1}(u^{\prime})^{2}\,dt+m_{k}\sum_{t\in S(u)}|u(t+)-u(t-)|^{\frac{1}{k}}.

Proof.

Up to subsequences, we can assume that
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=S<+\infty
.
For fixed
\theta\in(0,1)
and
N\geq N(\theta)
,
let
\{v^{\theta,N}_{\varepsilon}\}
be the sequence constructed in the proof of Theorem
17
,
and note that
v^{\theta,N}_{\varepsilon}\to u
in
L^{1}
.

Note preliminarily that
|\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}(u_{\varepsilon}(\sigma^{i}_{\lambda})-u_{\varepsilon}(\tau^{i}_{\lambda}))|=|v_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(\sigma_{\lambda}+)-v_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(\sigma_{\lambda}-)|
can possibly be greater than
\theta
for some
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(N,\theta,<)
.
In this case, we can estimate the variation in
(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda})\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}(\tau^{i}_{\lambda},\sigma^{i}_{\lambda})
using the fact that in this set

, obtaining

\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\lambda}}(u_{\varepsilon}(b_{j_{i}(\lambda)}^{\ast,N})-u_{\varepsilon}(a_{j_{i}(\lambda)}^{\ast,N}))\Big|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\varepsilon}}\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\lambda}}|(a_{j_{i}(\lambda)}^{\ast,N},b_{j_{i}(\lambda)}^{\ast,N})|<\frac{1}{2N^{2}S}\sqrt{\varepsilon},

where in the last inequality we used the fact that
\sigma_{\lambda}-\tau_{\lambda}<\frac{1}{2N^{2}S}\varepsilon

Since

u_{\varepsilon}(\sigma_{\lambda})-u_{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\lambda})=v_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(\sigma_{\lambda}+)-v_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(\sigma_{\lambda}-)+\sum_{i=1}^{n_{\lambda}}(u_{\varepsilon}(b_{j_{i}(\lambda)}^{\ast,N})-u_{\varepsilon}(a_{j_{i}(\lambda)}^{\ast,N})),

recalling that
|u_{\varepsilon}(\sigma_{\lambda})-u_{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\lambda})|\leq\theta

|v_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(\sigma_{\lambda}+)-v_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(\sigma_{\lambda}-)|\leq\theta+\frac{1}{2N^{2}S}\sqrt{\varepsilon}\leq 2\theta

(77)

for
\varepsilon<(2N^{2}S\theta)^{2}
.

In order to deduce an optimal bound by Propositions
15
and
16
, we set

\widehat{\psi}_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(z)=r_{k}m_{k}^{\ell(k)}(2N\theta)(2\theta)^{\frac{1}{k}-\frac{3}{4}}\min\{\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{4}}|z|,|z|^{\frac{3}{4}}\},

\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(z)=\begin{cases}\widehat{\psi}_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(z)&\hbox{\rm if }z<2\theta\\
m_{k}(N\theta)|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}&\hbox{\rm if }z\geq 2\theta.\end{cases}

(78)

Note that the function has a discontinuity in
2\theta
.

If
\lambda\in\Lambda^{-}(N,\theta)
; that is, if
|u_{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\lambda})-u_{\varepsilon}(\sigma_{\lambda})|<\theta<2\theta
, by (
4
) we deduce the estimates

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(N,\theta,\geq)}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};(\tau_{\lambda},\sigma_{\lambda}))\geq(1-\eta)\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(N,\theta,\geq)}\widehat{\psi}_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(|u_{\varepsilon}(\sigma_{\lambda})-u_{\varepsilon}(\tau_{\lambda})|)

and

\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(N,\theta,<)}\!\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};(\tau^{i}_{\lambda},\sigma^{i}_{\lambda}))\geq\!\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-}(N,\theta,<)}\!\!\widehat{\psi}_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}\Big(\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{k_{\lambda}}(u_{\varepsilon}(\sigma^{i}_{\lambda})-u_{\varepsilon}(\tau^{i}_{\lambda}))\Big|\Big)-\eta,

with
N\geq N(\theta)
,
\eta>0

17
, and
\varepsilon<\min\{\varepsilon_{k}(\eta),\varepsilon_{k}(\theta,N)\}
, with
\varepsilon_{k}(\eta)
and
\varepsilon_{k}(\theta,N)
the positive thresholds given therein.
By (
52
) and (
60
) we then obtain

F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq(1-\eta)\sum_{t\in S(v_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N})}\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(|v_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(t+)-v_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(t-)|)+\int_{0}^{1}((v_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N})^{\prime})\,ds-\eta.

(79)

We now define
L_{\theta}^{N}(z)
as the linear function such that
L_{\theta}^{N}(0)=0
and
L_{\theta}^{N}(\theta)=\widehat{\psi}_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(\theta)
; that is,

L_{\theta}^{N}(z)=r_{k}m_{k}^{\ell(k)}(2N\theta)(2\theta)^{\frac{1}{k}-1}z.

If
\varepsilon<\Big(\frac{1}{2r_{k}m_{k}^{\ell(k)}(2N\theta)}\Big)^{4}\theta
, then

\widehat{\psi}_{\varepsilon}^{2\theta,N}(z)\geq L_{\theta}^{N}(|z|)\ \ \ \hbox{\rm if }\ |z|\leq 2\theta,

so that

\phi_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N}(z)\geq\phi^{\theta,N}(z):=\min\{L_{\theta}^{N}(|z|),m_{k}(\lfloor N\theta\rfloor)|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}\}.

Setting
\varepsilon_{k}(\eta,\theta,N)=\min\{\varepsilon_{k}(\eta),\varepsilon_{k}(\theta,N),\big(\frac{1}{2r_{k}m_{k}^{\ell(k)}(2N\theta)}\big)^{4}\theta\},

by (
79
) we then obtain that,
for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{k}(\eta,\theta,N))
,

F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq(1-\eta)G^{\theta,N}(v_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N})-\eta,

where

G^{\theta,N}(v)=\sum_{t\in S(v)}\phi^{\theta,N}(|v(t+)-v(t-)|)+\int_{0}^{1}(v^{\prime})\,ds

if

and
+\infty
otherwise.
Applying again Lemma
3

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\eta)\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\overline{G}^{\theta,N}(v_{\varepsilon}^{\theta,N})-\eta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(1-\eta)\sum_{t\in S(u)}\phi^{\theta,N}(|u(t+)-u(t-)|)+\int_{0}^{1}\gamma^{\theta,N}(u^{\prime})\,ds-\eta,

\gamma^{\theta,N}(z)=\min\{z^{2},(\phi^{\theta,N})^{\prime}(0+)|z|\}^{\ast\ast}
.
Since

(\phi^{\theta,N})^{\prime}(0+)=r_{k}m_{k}^{\ell(k)}(N\theta)\theta^{\frac{1}{k}-1}

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq(1-\eta)\sum_{t\in S(u)}\phi^{\theta}(|u(t+)-u(t-)|)+\int_{0}^{1}\gamma^{\theta}(u^{\prime})\,ds-\eta,

\gamma^{\theta}(z)=\min\{z^{2},(\phi^{\theta})^{\prime}(0+)|z|\}^{\ast\ast}
.

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq(1-\eta)m_{k}\sum_{t\in S(u)}|u(t+)-u(t-)|^{\frac{1}{k}}+\int_{0}^{1}(u^{\prime})^{2}\,ds-\eta,

since
\sup_{\theta}\phi^{\theta}(z)=m_{k}|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}
and
(\phi^{\theta})^{\prime}(0+)=r_{k}m_{k}^{\ell(k)}\theta^{\frac{1}{k}-1}\to+\infty
as

.
By the arbitrariness of

, the claim follows.
∎

We now conclude the proof of Theorem
1
by showing the upper estimate.

Proposition 19

(Upper bound)
.

Let
u\in SBV_{2}(0,1)
. Then, there exists a sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
such that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in
L^{1}(0,1)
and

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq m_{k}\sum_{t\in S(u)}|u(t+)-u(t-)|^{\frac{1}{k}}+\int_{0}^{1}(u^{\prime})^{2}\,dt.

Proof.

We start by proving the upper estimate for a function
u
such that
S(u)=\{t_{0}\}
,
u\in C^{\infty}\big((0,1)\setminus\{t_{0}\}\big)
and
u^{(\ell)}(t_{0}+)=u^{(\ell)}(t_{0}-)=0
for any
\ell\geq 1
.
Let
T>0
and let
w\in H^{k}(-\frac{T}{2},\frac{T}{2})
be such that
w
is admissible for the minimum problem defining
m_{k}
, and

m_{k}=T+\int_{-\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{T}{2}}(w^{(k)})^{2}\,dt.

We set
z=u(t+)-u(t-)
and define for all
\varepsilon>0

v_{\varepsilon}(t)=z\,w\Big(\frac{t-t_{0}}{\varepsilon|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}}\Big).

(80)

Hence,

\displaystyle m_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T+\varepsilon^{2k}\int_{-\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{T}{2}}(v_{\varepsilon}^{(k)}(\varepsilon|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}t+t_{0}))^{2}\,dt

(81)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T+\frac{\varepsilon^{2k-1}}{|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}}\int_{t_{0}-\varepsilon|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{T}{2}}^{t_{0}+\varepsilon|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{T}{2}}(v_{\varepsilon}^{(k)})^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}}\bigg(T|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}+\varepsilon^{2k-1}\int_{t_{0}-\varepsilon|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{T}{2}}^{t_{0}+\varepsilon|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{T}{2}}(v_{\varepsilon}^{(k)})^{2}\,dt\bigg).

We can now define the recovery sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
by setting

u_{\varepsilon}(t)=\begin{cases}u(t+\varepsilon|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{T}{2})&\hbox{\rm if }t\in(0,t_{0}-\varepsilon|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{T}{2}]\\
v_{\varepsilon}(t)&\hbox{\rm if }t\in(t_{0}-\varepsilon|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{T}{2},t_{0}+\varepsilon|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{T}{2})\\
u(t-\varepsilon|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{T}{2})&\hbox{\rm if }t\in[t_{0}+\varepsilon\frac{T}{2},1),\end{cases}

(82)

so that, thanks to (
81
), we get

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{t_{0}-\varepsilon|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{T}{2}}^{t_{0}+\varepsilon|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{T}{2}}\big(f_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})\,dt+\varepsilon^{2k-1}(v_{\varepsilon}^{(k)})^{2}\big)\,dt

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{1}\big((u^{\prime})^{2}+\varepsilon^{2k-1}(u^{(k)})^{2}\big)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle T|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}+\varepsilon^{2k-1}\int_{t_{0}-\varepsilon|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{T}{2}}^{t_{0}+\varepsilon|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}\frac{T}{2}}(v_{\varepsilon}^{(k)})^{2}\,dt+\int_{0}^{1}(u^{\prime})^{2}\,dt+o(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{k}|u(t_{0}+)-u(t_{0}-)|^{\frac{1}{k}}+\int_{0}^{1}(u^{\prime})^{2}\,dt+o(1),

concluding the proof since
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in
L^{1}(0,1)
.

The construction is completely analogous if
u
is such that
\#S(u)<+\infty
,
u\in C^{\infty}\big((0,1)\setminus S(u)\big)
and
u^{(\ell)}(t+)=u^{(\ell)}(t-)=0
for any
\ell\geq 1
and
t\in S(u)
. We then can conclude by a density argument since such a class is dense in
SBV_{2}(0,1)
.
∎

5
Extensions

We conclude the paper by deriving a general convergence result as an envelope of functionals with truncated-quadratic potentials, and two applications showing new approximations for the Mumford–Shah and Blake–Zisserman functionals.

5.1
An extension to a general class of integrands

We first state an extension, whose proof is exactly the same as in the case
a=b=c=1
and is omitted. For notational simplicity we limit our analysis to functionals on
(0,1)
.

Proposition 20
.

Let
a,b,c>0
and define

F^{a,b,c}_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{(0,1)}\big(f^{a,b}_{\varepsilon}(u^{\prime})+c\,\varepsilon^{2k-1}(u^{(k)})^{2}\big)\,dt

for
u\in H^{k}(0,1)
, with

f^{a,b}_{\varepsilon}(z)=\min\big\{az^{2},\frac{b}{\varepsilon}\big\}
. Then the
\Gamma
-limit with respect to the convergence in measure and
the
L^{1}
-convergence of
F^{a,b,c}_{\varepsilon}
is

F^{a,b,c}(u)=a\int_{(0,1)}(u^{\prime})^{2}\,dt+m^{b,c}_{k}\sum_{t\in S(u)}|u(t+)-u(t-)|^{\frac{1}{k}},

with

\displaystyle m^{b,c}_{k}=\inf_{T>0}\min\bigg\{bT+c\int_{-\frac{T}{2}}^{\frac{T}{2}}(v^{(k)})^{2}\,dt:v\in H^{k}\big(-\tfrac{T}{2},\tfrac{T}{2}\big),\ v\big(\pm\tfrac{T}{2}\big)=\pm\tfrac{1}{2},

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826ptv^{(\ell)}\big(\pm\tfrac{T}{2}\big)=0\hbox{ for all }\ell\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}\Big\}.

(83)

Proposition 21
.

Let
\alpha,\beta,\gamma>0
, and let
f
be an increasing lower-semicontinuous function on
[0,+\infty)
such that

f(0)=0,\qquad f^{\prime}(0)=\alpha\quad\hbox{ and }\quad\lim\limits_{z\to+\infty}f(z)=\beta,

then the
\Gamma
-limit with respect to the convergence in measure and
the
L^{1}
-convergence of

F_{\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{(0,1)}\Big(\frac{1}{\varepsilon}f(\varepsilon|u^{\prime}|^{2})+\gamma\varepsilon^{2k-1}(u^{(k)})^{2}\Big)\,dt

defined on
H^{k}(0,1)
is
F^{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}
.

Proof.

Note preliminarily that if
f(z)=\min\{a|z|,b\}
then
F_{\varepsilon}=F^{a,b,\gamma}_{\varepsilon}
, so that the
\Gamma
-limit is given by

F^{a,b,\gamma}
. Hence, for a general
f
, if
a,b>0
are such that
\min\{a|z|,b\}\leq f(z)
for all
z
, then

\Gamma\hbox{-}\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}\geq F^{a,b,\gamma}
. We then have

\Gamma\hbox{-}\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u)\geq\sup\Big\{a\int_{(0,1)}(u^{\prime})^{2}\,dt+m^{b,\gamma}_{k}\sum_{t\in S(u)}|u(t+)-u(t-)|^{\frac{1}{k}}\Big\},

where the supremum is taken for all pairs
(a,b)
such that
\min\{a|z|,b\}\leq f(z)
for all
z
.
Now, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\{a>0:b>0\hbox{ exists}:\min\{a|z|,b\}\leq f(z)\hbox{ for all }z\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\{b>0:a>0\hbox{ exists}:\min\{a|z|,b\}\leq f(z)\hbox{ for all }z\}.

By the Supremum of Measures Lemma (see e.g.
[
14
, Section 1.1.1]
) we have

\displaystyle\hskip-42.67912pt\sup\Big\{a\int_{(0,1)}(u^{\prime})^{2}\,dt+m^{b,\gamma}_{k}\sum_{t\in S(u)}|u(t+)-u(t-)|^{\frac{1}{k}}\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}a\Big)\int_{(0,1)}(u^{\prime})^{2}\,dt+\Big(\sup_{b\in\mathcal{B}}m^{b,\gamma}_{k}\Big)\sum_{t\in S(u)}|u(t+)-u(t-)|^{\frac{1}{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\int_{(0,1)}(u^{\prime})^{2}\,dt+m^{\beta,\gamma}_{k}\sum_{t\in S(u)}|u(t+)-u(t-)|^{\frac{1}{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F^{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u).

To check the upper bound it suffices to consider the case of a function
u
such that
S(u)=\{t_{0}\}
,
u\in C^{\infty}\big((0,1)\setminus\{t_{0}\}\big)
and
u^{(\ell)}(t_{0}+)=u^{(\ell)}(t_{0}-)=0
for any
\ell\geq 1
. We take
u_{\varepsilon}
defined as in
(
82
), where
v_{\varepsilon}
is defined as in (
80
) with
(T,w)
a minimal pair for
m^{\beta,\gamma}_{k}
.
Since
f(z)\leq\beta
, we then have

F_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq\int_{(0,1)}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}f(\varepsilon|u^{\prime}|^{2})\,dt+m^{\beta,\gamma}_{k}|u(t_{0}+)-u(t_{0}-)|^{\frac{1}{k}}\,.

Since
\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon}f(\varepsilon|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2})=\alpha|u^{\prime}(t)|^{2}
for all
t
, we can pass to the limit by using the Dominated Convergence Theorem, and obtain that

\Gamma\hbox{-}\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}F_{\varepsilon}(u)\leq F^{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}(u).

For a general
u\in SBV_{2}(0,1)
we can use a density argument exactly as in the proof of Proposition
19
.
∎

5.2
An approximation of the Mumford–Shah functional

Using a diagonal argument we may prove an approximation of the Mumford–Shah functional as follows.

Proposition 22
.

Let
\mu>0
. There exist
\varepsilon_{k}
and
c_{k}
such that, if
\Phi_{k}=F^{1,1,c_{k}}_{\varepsilon_{k}}
, then
then the
\Gamma
-limit of
\Phi_{k}
with respect to the convergence in measure and
the
L^{1}
-convergence is given by

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{k\to+\infty}\Phi_{k}(u)=\int_{(0,1)}(u^{\prime})^{2}\,dt+\mu\#S(u)=:F^{MS}_{\mu}(u)

(84)

on
SBV_{2}(0,1)
.

Proof.

Let
c_{k}
be such that
m^{1,c_{k}}_{k}=\mu
. The existence of such
c_{k}
follows
from the fact that the function
c\mapsto m^{1,c}_{k}
is continuous and by (
20
) we have
\lim\limits_{c\to 0^{+}}m^{1,c}_{k}=0
and
\lim\limits_{c\to+\infty}m^{1,c}_{k}=+\infty
.
We then have

\displaystyle\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F^{1,1,c_{k}}_{\varepsilon}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}(u^{\prime})^{2}\,dt+\mu\sum_{t\in S(u)}|u(t+)-u(t-)|^{\frac{1}{k}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{(0,1)}(u^{\prime})^{2}\,dt+\mu\sum_{t\in S(u)}\min\{1,|u(t+)-u(t-)|^{\frac{1}{k}}\}.

Recalling that
|z|^{\frac{1}{k}}\to 1
as
k\to+\infty
for all
z\neq 0
, and this convergence is increasing for
|z|\leq 1
,
we obtain as a consequence that

\Gamma\hbox{-}\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\Big(\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F^{1,1,c_{k}}_{\varepsilon}\Big)(u)\geq\int_{(0,1)}(u^{\prime})^{2}\,dt+\mu\#S(u).

On the other hand, since the
\Gamma
-
\limsup
is estimated from above by the pointwise limit, it satisfies the converse inequality, so that there exists

\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{k\to+\infty}\Big(\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}F^{1,1,c_{k}}_{\varepsilon}\Big)(u)=\int_{(0,1)}(u^{\prime})^{2}\,dt+\mu\#S(u).

Since the family of functionals
\{F^{1,1,c_{k}}_{\varepsilon}\}
is equicoercive as
\varepsilon\to 0
, thanks to
[
25
, Theorem 17.14]
we can use a diagonal argument and conclude.
∎

5.3
Approximation of the Blake-Zisserman functional

We finally investigate the possibility of using the same type of approximation for the Blake–Zisserman functional
[
9
]
, defined on piecewise-
H^{2}
functions as

F^{BZ}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime\prime}|^{2}dt+2\#(S(u))+\#(S(u^{\prime})\setminus S(u))

(85)

(again taking
(0,1)
as the domain of the functions for simplicity).
In this notation,
u^{\prime}
and
u^{\prime\prime}
are the almost everywhere defined first and second weak derivative of
u
, and
S(u)
and
S(u^{\prime})
are the discontinuity sets of
u
and
u^{\prime}
, respectively. The reason why the coefficient of the number of points of the jump set
\#(S(u))
is twice that of
\#(S(u^{\prime})\setminus S(u))
(
crease set
) is a lower-semicontinuity issue, since jump points can be approximated by crease points.
The functional
F^{BZ}
can be seen to some extent as a higher-order version of the Mumford–Shah functional, and can also be defined in dimension
d>1
on suitably defined spaces (see the review paper
[
20
]
for details). We prove the following result.

Theorem 23
.

Let
c_{k}
be implicitly defined by

\displaystyle\inf_{T>0}\min\bigg\{T+c_{k}\int_{0}^{T}|v^{(k)}|^{2}dt:v(0)=0,v(T)=1,

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055ptv^{(\ell)}(0)=v^{(\ell)}(T)=0,\ \ell\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}\Big\}=1,

(86)

and let
f_{\varepsilon}(z)=\min\{z^{2},\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\}
.
Then there exists a family
\overline{\varepsilon}_{k}
tending to
0
such that if
\varepsilon_{k}\leq\overline{\varepsilon}_{k}
, then the functionals
G_{k}
defined by

G_{k}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}f_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u^{\prime\prime})dt+c_{k-1}{\varepsilon_{k}}^{2k-3}\int_{0}^{1}|u^{(k)}|^{2}dt

(87)

on
H^{k}(0,1)

\Gamma
-converge with respect to the
L^{2}
-convergence to
F^{BZ}

Proof.

We note that, replacing
k
with
k-1
, we can equivalently define the functionals
G_{k}
as

G_{k}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}f_{\varepsilon_{k}}(u^{\prime\prime})dt+c_{k}{\varepsilon_{k}}^{2k-1}\int_{0}^{1}|u^{(k+1)}|^{2}dt,

(88)

so that they coincide with the functional
F_{k,\varepsilon_{k}}
,
computed on
u^{\prime}
, where

\varepsilon_{k}

F^{MS}=F^{MS}_{1}
as defined in (

Upper bound.
Let
u
be a piecewise-
H^{2}
function with
S(u)=\emptyset
.
Then the function
v=u^{\prime}
is piecewise
H^{1}
and we can take a recovery sequence
v_{k}
for
u
along the sequence
F_{k}
, so that

F^{BZ}(u)=F^{MS}(v)=\lim_{k\to+\infty}F_{k}(v_{k})=\lim_{k\to+\infty}G_{k}(u_{k}),

where
u_{k}
is a primitive of
v_{k}
converging to
u
. We then have

\Gamma\hbox{-}\limsup_{k\to+\infty}G_{k}(u)\leq F^{BZ}(u)

for all piecewise-
H^{2}
functions
u
with
S(u)=\emptyset
.
Since the functional
F^{BZ}
is the lower-semicontinuous envelope of its restriction

\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime\prime}|^{2}dt+\#(S(u^{\prime}))

to piecewise-
H^{2}
functions
u
with
S(u)=\emptyset

H^{2}

Lower bound.

We note that, if we set

G_{k,\varepsilon}(u)=\int_{0}^{1}f_{\varepsilon}(u^{\prime\prime})dt+c_{k}{\varepsilon}^{2k-1}\int_{0}^{1}|u^{(k+1)}|^{2}dt,

(91)

then for all
k
the
\Gamma

-functions
u

u^{\prime}
piecewise
H^{1}

S(u)=\emptyset

\displaystyle\Gamma\hbox{-}\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}G_{k,\varepsilon}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime\prime}|^{2}dt+\sum_{t\in S(u^{\prime})}|u^{\prime}(t+)-u^{\prime}(t-)|^{1/k}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|u^{\prime\prime}|^{2}dt+\sum_{t\in S(u^{\prime})}\min\{1,|u^{\prime}(t+)-u^{\prime}(t-)|^{1/k}\}.

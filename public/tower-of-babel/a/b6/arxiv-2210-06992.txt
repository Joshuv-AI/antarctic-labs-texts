S 4 S_{4} -quartics with Prescribed Norms
Sebastian Monnet

Abstract
. Given a number field k k and a finitely generated subgroup 𝒜 ⊆ k ∗ \mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{*} , we study the distribution of S 4 S_{4} -quartic extensions of k k such that the elements of 𝒜 \mathcal{A} are norms. We show that the density of such extensions is the product of so-called “local masses” at every place of k k . We give these local masses explicitly in almost all cases and give an algorithm for computing the remaining cases.



1.
Introduction

Fix a number field
k
. An
S_{4}
-quartic extension of
k
is a quartic field extension
K/k
, such that the normal closure
\widetilde{K}
of
K
over
k
has
\Gal(\widetilde{K}/k)\cong S_{4}
. For a real number
X>0
, write
N(X)
for the number of isomorphism classes of
S_{4}
-quartic extensions
K/k
such that the norm of the relative discriminant of
K
over
k
is at most
X
. Given a finitely generated subgroup
\mathcal{A}
of the unit group
k^{*}
, write
N(X;\mathcal{A})
for the number of such isomorphism classes with
\mathcal{A}\subseteq N_{K/k}(K^{*})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every finitely generated subgroup
\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{*}
, we have

0<\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\mathcal{A})}{N(X)}\leq 1,

with equality if and only if
\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{*4}
.

Corollary 1.2
.

For every finitely generated subgroup
\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{*}
, there are infinitely many
S_{4}
-quartic extensions
K
of
k
with
\mathcal{A}\subseteq N_{K/k}(K^{*})
.

Let us unpack the content of Theorem
1.1
. It tells us that any finitely generated subgroup
\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{*}
is contained in the norm group of a positive proportion of
S_{4}
-quartic extensions. Moreover, it tells us that
\mathcal{A}
can only be contained in 100% of such norm groups if
\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{*4}
, in which case
\mathcal{A}\subseteq N_{K/k}(K^{*})
for every
S_{4}
-quartic extension
K
of
k
. In other words, if
\mathcal{A}\subseteq N_{K/k}(K^{*})
for almost all
K
, then
\mathcal{A}\subseteq N_{K/k}(K^{*})
for all
K
.

Remark 1.3
.

In the special case where
\mathcal{A}
is generated by one element, Corollary
1.2
can be obtained by classical methods, using Hilbert’s irreducibility theorem. See
[
FLN22
, Example 1.13]
for details.

In
[
FLN22
]
, Frei, Loughran, and Newton considered the same question for abelian extensions with a given Galois group. They used class field theory to solve the problem. In our setting, the extensions are nonabelian, so we are unable to use class field theory in the same way. Our approach is to use a local-global principle called the Hasse norm principle to reduce the global problem to a collection of local conditions. We then apply a result of Bhargava, Shankar, and Wang to count the
S_{4}
-quartics satisfying this collection of local conditions. Bhargava, Shankar, and Wang’s result is also valid for
S_{3}
-cubics and
S_{5}
-quintics, and we expect that our methods will generalise to such extensions.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we first obtain the following explicit formula for the density of number fields in question.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{*}
be a finitely generated subgroup. There exist positive rational numbers
m_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}
, indexed by the primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
k
, such that each
m_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}
is defined explicitly in terms of
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathfrak{p}
, and

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\mathcal{A})}{X}=\frac{1}{2}\operatornamewithlimits{Res}_{s=1}\big(\zeta_{k}(s)\big)\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}m_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}.

For an element
\alpha\in k^{*}
, write
N(X;\alpha)
to mean
N(X;\langle\alpha\rangle)
, where
\langle\alpha\rangle
is the subgroup of
k^{*}
generated by
\alpha
. Equivalently,
N(X;\alpha)
is the number of
S_{4}
-quartic extensions
K/k
with appropriately bounded discriminant such that
\alpha\in N_{K/k}(K^{*})
. Similarly, write
m_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}
for the number
m_{\langle\alpha\rangle,\mathfrak{p}}
from Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For
\alpha\in k^{*}
and finite primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
k
with odd norm, the values of
m_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}
are given by Tables
1
and
2
. If
\mathfrak{p}
is an infinite prime and
f:k\to\mathbb{C}
is an embedding corresponding to
\mathfrak{p}
, then

m_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}=\begin{cases}\frac{5}{12}\quad\text{if $f$ is real and $f(\alpha)>0$},\\
\frac{7}{24}\quad\text{if $f$ is real and $f(\alpha)<0$},\\
\frac{1}{24}\quad\text{if $f$ is complex.}\end{cases}

In the special case
k=\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathfrak{p}=(2)
, the values of
m_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}
are given by Table
3
.

We illustrate how to use our results, and in particular how to read Tables
1
,
2
, and
3
, with an example.

Example 1.6
.

We will give an exact expression for the proportion of
S_{4}
-quartic number fields
K/\mathbb{Q}
such that
2\in\mathbb{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{*})
. Since
2>0
, we have

m_{2,\infty}=m_{1,\infty}=\frac{5}{12}.

In Table
3
, we have
r=u=1
, so

m_{2,2}=\frac{6523}{8192}.

For an odd prime
p
, we know that
2
is a quadratic residue if and only if
p\equiv\pm 1\pmod{8}
. Therefore, if
p\equiv\pm 3\pmod{8}
, we have

m_{2,p}=\frac{(4p^{2}+4p+5)(p-1)}{4p^{3}}.

If
p\equiv 7\pmod{8}
, then Table
2
tells us that

m_{2,p}=\frac{(p^{3}+p^{2}+2p+1)(p-1)}{p^{4}}.

For
p\equiv 1\pmod{8}
, the situation is more complicated, and we have

m_{2,p}=\begin{cases}\frac{(p^{3}+p^{2}+2p+1)(p-1)}{p^{4}}\quad\text{if $2$ is a quartic residue modulo $p$},\\
\frac{(p^{2}+p+2)(p-1)}{p^{3}}\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

We have

m_{1,p}=\frac{(p^{3}+p^{2}+2p+1)(p-1)}{p^{4}},

for all finite primes (including
2
), and therefore

\frac{m_{2,p}}{m_{1,p}}=\begin{cases}1\quad\text{if $p=\infty$},\\
\frac{6523}{8704}\quad\text{if $p=2$},\\
\frac{4p^{3}+4p^{2}+5p}{4(p^{3}+p^{2}+2p+1)}\quad\text{if $p\equiv\pm 3\pmod{8}$},\\
1\quad\text{if $p\equiv 7\pmod{8}$},\\
1\quad\text{if $p\equiv 1\pmod{8}$ and $2$ is a quartic residue modulo $p$},\\
\frac{p^{3}+p^{2}+2p}{p^{3}+p^{2}+2p+1}\quad\text{if $p\equiv 1\pmod{8}$ and $2$ is a quartic nonresidue modulo $p$}\end{cases}

Finally then, the proportion of
S_{4}
-quartic extensions
K/\mathbb{Q}
with
2\in N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(K^{*})
is given by the Euler product

\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{6523}{8704}\cdot\prod_{p\equiv\pm 3\pmod{8}}\Big(1-\frac{3p+4}{4p^{3}+4p^{2}+8p+4}\Big)\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\equiv 1\pmod{8}\\
\text{$2$ is a quartic nonresidue modulo $p$}\end{subarray}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{p^{3}+p^{2}+2p+1}\Big),

which is approximately equal to
33.06\%
.

1.1.
Structure of the paper

In Section
2
, we explain our approach to the problem. Our first step is to introduce the
Hasse Norm Principle
in Section
2.1
, which lets us consider the situation locally at each prime of
k
. Subsequently, in Section
2.2
, we state Bhargava, Shankar, and Wang’s counting result, which requires our local conditions to be “acceptable”. In Section
2.3
, we demonstrate that our conditions are indeed acceptable, allowing us to prove Theorem
1.4
.

The goal of Section
3
is to prove Theorem
1.5
. That is, we compute the masses
m_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}
explicitly for
\alpha\in k^{*}
and primes
\mathfrak{p}
that are either infinite or have odd norm, as well as in the special case
k=\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathfrak{p}=(2)
. In Section
3.1
, we introduce some notation and outline the strategy of Sections
3.2
–
3.4
. Then, in Section
3.2
, we compute all possible norm groups of several classes of quartic étale algebras over
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
, where
\mathfrak{p}
is a finite prime with odd norm. We apply these norm group computations in Section
3.3
to compute all possible masses
m_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}
, for finite primes
\mathfrak{p}
with odd norm, as well as for infinite primes. In Section
3.4
, we present our MAGMA (
[
BCP97
]
) algorithm to compute masses in general and obtain the values in Table
3
. We then assemble our results to prove Theorem
1.5
.

Finally, Section
4
is dedicated to proving Theorem
1.1
. Along the way, we obtain an explicit finite bound on
\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\alpha)}{X}
for
\alpha\in k^{*}
, in terms of
\alpha
and
k
. This bound implies that
\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X,\mathcal{A})}{X}
is finite for any finitely generated subgroup
\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{*}
, which allows us to obtain an explicit expression for the proportion
\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\mathcal{A})}{N(X)}
. By considering this explicit expression, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Reducing to Local Conditions

In this section, we break our global problem into infinitely many local problems. In Section
2.1
, we recall the Hasse Norm Principle, which says that
\alpha
is a norm globally if and only if it is a norm everywhere locally. In Section
2.2
, we recall a result of Bhargava, Shankar, and Wang, which allows us to count number fields satisfying a given collection of local conditions. In a probabilistic sense, this counting result essentially says that the local conditions at each prime behave like independent random events. The result also tells us how to compute the “probabilities” of these independent events. There is one technical hitch, which is that this result only applies to so-called
acceptable
collections of local conditions. Therefore, we spend Section
2.3
proving that our local conditions are indeed acceptable, so that we can apply Bhargava, Shankar, and Wang’s result.

2.1.
The Hasse Norm Principle

As usual, let
K/k
be an extension of number fields and let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
k
. Write
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
for the étale
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
-algebra

K_{\mathfrak{p}}=K\otimes_{k}k_{\mathfrak{p}},

which we call the
completion of
K
at
\mathfrak{p}
. In this paper, we will refer to the following result as “the Hasse Norm Principle”. This terminology is slightly nonstandard; in general, the Hasse Norm Principle is a local-global principle that may or may not hold in a particular extension. Theorem
2.1
says that this principle holds for all
S_{4}
-quartic extensions.

Theorem 2.1

(Hasse Norm Principle)
.

Let
K/k
be an degree
n
extension whose normal closure has Galois group
S_{n}
, and let
\alpha\in k^{*}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\alpha\in N_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}(K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*})
for each prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
k
,

(2)

\alpha\in N_{K/k}(K^{*})
.

Proof.

This is
[
Vos88
, Corollary to Theorem 4]
.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
K/k
be a degree
n
extension whose normal closure has Galois group
S_{n}
, and let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{*}
be a finitely generated subgroup. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{A}\subseteq N_{K_{\mathfrak{p}}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}(K_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*})
for each prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
k
,

(2)

\mathcal{A}\subseteq N_{K/k}(K^{*})
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
k
. Define
\Sigma_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}
to be the set of isomorphism classes of quartic étale algebras
L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}
with
\mathcal{A}\subseteq N_{L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}(L^{*})
. Then Corollary
2.2
tells us that an
S_{4}
-quartic extension
K/k
has
\mathcal{A}\subseteq N_{K/k}(K^{*})
if and only if
K_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}
for all
\mathfrak{p}
. Given
\alpha\in k^{*}
, write
\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}
for
\Sigma_{\langle\alpha\rangle,\mathfrak{p}}
, where
\langle\alpha\rangle
is the subgroup of
k^{*}
generated by
\alpha
.

2.2.
Counting number fields using local conditions

The Hasse Norm Principle tells us that the number fields
K
we are looking for are precisely those satisfying a certain collection of local conditions, in the sense of the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
n
be a positive integer. A
degree
n
collection of local conditions on
k
is a collection
(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}}
, where
\mathfrak{p}
ranges over primes of
k
, and each
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a set of isomorphism classes of étale
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
-algebras of degree
n
.

Definition 2.4
.

An extension
K/k

satisfies
a collection of local conditions
(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}}
if
K_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all
\mathfrak{p}
.

Remark 2.5
.

We will refer extensively to the discriminant
\Disc(L/k_{\mathfrak{p}})
, where
L
is an étale
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
-algebra. This discriminant has the well-known property that

\Disc\big((L_{1}\times\ldots\times L_{r})/k_{\mathfrak{p}}\big)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Disc(L_{i}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}),

for étale
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
-algebras
L_{i}
.

Definition 2.6
.

A
p
-adic field
is a finite degree extension of the
p
-adic numbers
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
F
be a
p
-adic field with maximal ideal
P
and residue field of size
q
. Let
L
be an étale algebra over
F
. Write
\Disc(L/F)
for the relative discriminant of
L
over
F
, and define
\Disc_{P}(L/F)
by

\Disc_{P}(L/F)=q^{v_{P}(\Disc(L/F))}.

Definition 2.8
.

Following
[
BSW15
]
, a degree
n
collection of local conditions
(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}}
is said to be
acceptable
if, for all but finitely many
\mathfrak{p}
, the set
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
contains every degree
n
étale algebra
L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}
with
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Disc(L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}))<2
.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\Sigma=(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}}
be a degree
4
collection of local conditions on
k
. For a positive real number
X
, write
N(X;\Sigma)
for the number of
S_{4}
-quartic extensions of
k
satisfying
\Sigma
such that the norm of the relative discriminant of
K/k
is at most
X
.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
k
and let
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
be a set of isomorphism classes of étale
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
-algebras. Define the
mass of
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
to be

m_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}})=\begin{cases}\frac{N(\mathfrak{p})-1}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\sum_{L\in\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}}\frac{1}{\Disc_{\mathfrak{p}}(L/k_{\mathfrak{p}})}\cdot\frac{1}{\#\Aut(L/k_{\mathfrak{p}})}\quad\text{if $\mathfrak{p}$ is finite},\\
\sum_{L\in\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}}\frac{1}{\#\Aut(L/k_{\mathfrak{p}})}\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{ if $\mathfrak{p}$ is infinite}.\end{cases}

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
\Sigma=(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}}
be an acceptable collection of local conditions. Then

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\Sigma)}{X}=\frac{1}{2}\operatornamewithlimits{Res}_{s=1}\big(\zeta_{k}(s)\big)\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}m_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}).

Proof.

This is
[
BSW15
, Theorem 2]
, in the case where
n=4
and
F
is a number field.
∎

2.3.
Showing that our conditions are acceptable

In order to apply Theorem
2.11
, we need to show that the collection
(\Sigma_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}})_{\mathfrak{p}}
of local conditions is acceptable.

We start by defining the “splitting symbol” of a prime in an extension, which will allow us to consider extensions
K/k
separately depending on how the prime
\mathfrak{p}
splits in
K
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
K/k
be an extension of number fields and let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime of
k
. Suppose that

\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}_{K}=P_{1}^{e_{1}}\ldots P_{r}^{e_{r}},

for distinct prime ideals
P_{i}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
. For each
i
, let
f_{i}
be the inertia degree of
P_{i}
over
\mathfrak{p}
. Then the
splitting symbol of
\mathfrak{p}
in
K
is

(K,\mathfrak{p})=(f_{1}^{e_{1}}\ldots f_{r}^{e_{r}}).

If
e_{i}=1
for some
i
, we suppress the notation and write
f_{i}
instead of
f_{i}^{1}
. Note that this symbol is only well-defined up to permutation of the indices, so we identify the symbols
(f_{1}^{e_{1}}\ldots f_{r}^{e_{r}})
and
(f_{\sigma(1)}^{e_{\sigma(1)}}\ldots f_{\sigma(r)}^{e_{\sigma(r)}})
for each permutation
\sigma
of the set
\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
. For example, we say that
(1^{2}2)=(21^{2})
.

We are interested in the completion
K_{\mathfrak{p}}
of
K
at
\mathfrak{p}
, which is an étale
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
-algebra. There is a natural notion of splitting symbol for étale
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
-algebras. We first define this splitting symbol.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
F
be a
p
-adic field with maximal ideal
P
, and let
L/F
be an étale algebra. Then
L\cong L_{1}\times\ldots\times L_{r}
for finite field extensions
L_{i}/F
. Let
e_{i}
and
f_{i}
be respectively the ramification index and inertia degree of
L_{i}
over
F
. Then the
splitting symbol of
L
is

(L,P)=(f_{1}^{e_{1}}\ldots f_{r}^{e_{r}}).

Again, we identify splitting symbols related by permutation, in the sense of Definition
2.12
.

It is easy to see that our two definitions of splitting symbol agree, in the sense that

(K_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathfrak{p})=(K,\mathfrak{p}),

whenever
\mathfrak{p}
is a finite prime of
k
. The following result is standard algebraic number theory.

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
E/F
be an extension of
p
-adic fields, and let
P
be the maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
. Let
p
be the residue characteristic of
F
. Write
e
and
f
for the ramification index and inertia degree, respectively, of
E/F
. If
p\nmid e
, then

v_{P}(\Disc(E/F))=f(e-1).

Definition 2.15
.

Let
\sigma
be a splitting symbol. We say that
\sigma
is
overramified
if it is one of
(1^{2}1^{2}),(2^{2})
, and
(1^{4})
.

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
F
be a
p
-adic field with maximal ideal
P
, such that the residue characteristic is not
2
. Let
L/F
be a quartic étale algebra. If
v_{P}(\Disc(L/F))<2
, then the splitting symbol
(L,P)
is not overramified.

Proof.

It suffices to show that overramified extensions have
v_{P}(\Disc(L/F))\geq 2
. This follows from Lemma
2.14
and Remark
2.5
, since the residue characteristic is odd, hence does not divide any of the ramification indices in overramified symbols.
∎

We will use the following well-known fact.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
E/F
be an unramified extension of
p
-adic fields. Then

\mathcal{O}_{F}^{*}\subseteq N_{E/F}(E^{*}).

Proof.

This is precisely the corollary on Page 50 of
[
Lan94
]
.
∎

Lemma 2.18
.

Let
L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}
be a quartic étale algebra such that

(L,\mathfrak{p})\in\{(1111),(112),(13),(1^{2}11),(1^{2}2),(1^{3}1)\}.

We have

N_{L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}(L^{*})=k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}.

Proof.

Write
L=L_{1}\times\ldots\times L_{r}
as usual. If
\sigma\neq(1^{2}2)
, then one of the
L_{i}
is equal to
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
, so
N_{L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}(L^{*})=k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}
. Suppose that
\sigma=(1^{2}2)
. Then we may assume that
L_{1}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}
is quadratic and unramified, and
L_{2}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}
is quadratic and totally ramified. Since
L_{1}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}
is unramified, Lemma
2.17
tells us that the group
N_{L_{1}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}(L_{1}^{*})
contains all units of
\mathcal{O}_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}
. Since
L_{2}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}
is totally ramified,
N_{L_{2}/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}(L_{2}^{*})
contains an element of valuation
1
. The result follows.
∎

Definition 2.19
.

In light of Lemma
2.18
, write
\mathrm{triv}
for the set

\mathrm{triv}=\{(1111),(112),(13),(1^{2}11),(1^{3}1),(1^{2}2)\}.

We call the elements of this set the
trivial
splitting symbols.

Remark 2.20
.

We have chosen to call these symbols trivial because the problem we want to solve, namely determining whether
\mathcal{A}\subseteq N_{L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}(L^{*})
, is trivial whenever
(L,\mathfrak{p})\in\mathrm{triv}
, since the answer is always yes.

Lemma 2.21
.

Let
\alpha\in k^{*}
, and let
\mathfrak{p}
be a finite prime of
k
such that
N(\mathfrak{p})
is odd and
4\mid v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha)
. Then
\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}
contains every quartic étale algebra
L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}
with
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Disc(L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}))<2
.

Proof.

Let
L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}
be a quartic étale algebra with
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Disc(L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}))<2
. By Lemma
2.16
, the symbol
(L,\mathfrak{p})
is not overramified, which means that it is either trivial or one of

. Suppose that

The next result is immediate from the definitions of

and

is acceptable.

of

(2)

\mathfrak{p}

By Lemma

, for every prime

contains every quartic étale algebra

Write

for the mass

3.
Computation of Masses

In this section, given an
\alpha\in k^{*}
, we give explicit values for the masses
m_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}
, whenever
N(\mathfrak{p})
is odd. We also present an algorithm for computing these masses in general, which we use to calculate every possible
m_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}
in the case
k=\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathfrak{p}=(2)
. In Section
3.1
, we define some notation and outline our strategy for computing the masses
m_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}
. In Section
3.2
, we compute explicitly the norm groups of all possible quartic étale algebras over
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
, for
\mathfrak{p}
not lying over
2
. In Section
3.3
, we compute all possible sets
\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}
, again for
\mathfrak{p}
not lying over
2
, as well as their masses
m_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}})
. Finally, in Section
3.4
, we describe our algorithm for computing
m_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}})
when
\mathfrak{p}
lies over
2
. We conclude the section by assembling the pieces to prove Theorem
1.5
.

3.1.
Strategy

We start by introducing some auxiliary notation.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a finite prime of
k
, and let
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
be a set of quartic étale
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
-algebras. Define the
pre-mass of
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
, denoted
\widetilde{m}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}})
, by

\widetilde{m}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}})=\sum_{L\in\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}}\frac{1}{\Disc_{\mathfrak{p}}(L/k_{\mathfrak{p}})}\cdot\frac{1}{\#\Aut(L/k_{\mathfrak{p}})}.

The additional notation of pre-mass is perhaps a little cumbersome, but it is a natural quantity to consider and makes the results of the current section easier to state. Of course, the point is that

m_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}})=\frac{N(\mathfrak{p})-1}{N(\mathfrak{p})}\cdot\widetilde{m}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}})

for
\mathfrak{p}
and
\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
as in Definition
3.1
. As such, for finite primes
\mathfrak{p}
, we will compute
\widetilde{m}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}})
, and this information will immediately allow us to compute the values of
m_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}})
.

Definition 3.2
.

Recall that, for
\alpha\in k^{*}
, we defined
\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}
to be the set of isomorphism classes of quartic étale
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
-algebras
L
with
\alpha\in N_{L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}(L^{*})
. For a possible splitting symbol
\sigma
, write
\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}^{\sigma}
for the set

\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}^{\sigma}=\{L\in\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}:(L,\mathfrak{p})=\sigma\}.

Since the sets
\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}^{\sigma}
partition
\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}
we have

\widetilde{m}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}})=\sum_{\sigma}\widetilde{m}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}^{\sigma}).

Therefore, it suffices to compute the values of
\widetilde{m}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}^{\sigma})
for each
\sigma
. This is more tractable, since there are not many quartic étale algebras with a given splitting symbol, so we can consider each one in turn. In the current section, for each
\mathfrak{p}
with
N(\mathfrak{p})
odd, we explicitly compute the sets
\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}^{\sigma}
along with the norm group of each étale algebra in
\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}^{\sigma}
.

Lemma 3.23
.

Let

3.4.
Primes lying over

Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime with even norm. The methods above do not apply, because in the wildly ramified case there are many more extensions. However, we have implemented an algorithm in MAGMA that computes
\widetilde{m}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}})
for any number field
k
and any finite prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
k
. This has two applications:

(1)

The algorithm allows us to verify the results from Section
3.3
for specific primes
\mathfrak{p}
with odd norm. Simultaneously, agreement with our results provides evidence that the algorithm works correctly.

(2)

The algorithm allows us to compute explicit values for

for primes
\mathfrak{p}
lying over
2
. In particular, we used the algorithm to compute the values in Table

.

Our code is in the GitHub repository at
https://github.com/Sebastian-Monnet/S4-quartics-prescribed-norms
. The most important function in our code is

ComputePreMassOfNormSet
. This takes as input a number
alpha
, a
p
-adic field
BaseField
, and a list
Symbols
of splitting symbols. The function then explicitly computes the set of quartic étale algebras of
BaseField
such that
alpha
is a norm and returns the pre-mass of that set. In our notation, the function computes

\tilde{m}_{\mathfrak{p}}\Big(\bigcup_{\sigma\in S}\Sigma_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}^{\sigma}\Big),

given a set
S
of splitting symbols.

The MAGMA file in the repository executes two functions:
PrintPreMassesFor2()
and
TestUpTo(20,3)
. The first of these functions prints the value of

for
\alpha=2^{r}u
, as
r
ranges over
\{0,1,2,3\}
and
u
ranges over
\{1,3,5,\ldots,15\}
. We will see in the proof of Lemma
3.26
that this is precisely the set of values we need to compute in order to know
m_{\alpha,2}
for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}^{*}
. The second function checks, for each prime
p\in\{5,7,11,13,17,19\}
, that the values in Tables
1
and
2
are correct for all extensions
k_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
of degree at most
3
. The function returns
true
if the results agree with those we have proved, and
false
otherwise.

Lemma 3.25
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}
lie over
2
and let
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{*}
. Suppose that
\alpha\equiv 1\pmod{8\pi}
, for a uniformiser
\pi
of
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{*4}
.

Proof.

Let
f(X)=(2X+1)^{4}-\alpha
. Then we have

f(X)=16X^{4}+32X^{3}+24X^{2}+8X+1-\alpha,

so
g(X):=\frac{1}{8}f(X)\in\mathcal{O}_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}[X]
, and we have

\lvert g(0)\rvert_{\mathfrak{p}}=\Big\lvert\frac{1-\alpha}{8}\Big\rvert_{\mathfrak{p}}<1,

whereas

\lvert g^{\prime}(0)\rvert_{\mathfrak{p}}=\lvert 1\rvert_{\mathfrak{p}}=1.

By Hensel’s Lemma, the polynomial
g(X)
has a root in
\mathcal{O}_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}
, hence
f(X)
does too.
∎

4.
Proportion of Extensions with Prescribed Norms

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.1
. Recall that, given a finite prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
k
, we often write
q
for the norm
N(\mathfrak{p})
of
\mathfrak{p}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\alpha\in k^{*}
, and let
\mathfrak{p}
be a finite prime of
k
such that the following two conditions hold:

(1)

q
is odd,

(2)

4\mid\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha)
.

Then
m_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}<1+\frac{1}{q^{2}}
.

Proof.

This is immediate from Tables
1
and
2
.
∎

Let
\alpha\in k^{*}
. In the following lemma, we write
S
for the set of primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
k
such that at least one of the following holds:

(1)

\mathfrak{p}
is finite and
q
is even,

(2)

\mathfrak{p}
is finite and
4\nmid\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha)
,

(3)

\mathfrak{p}
is infinite.

Then
S
is a finite set, so we can explicitly compute the mass
m_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in S
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\alpha\in k^{*}
, and let
S
be the set of primes defined above. We have an explicit finite bound

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\alpha)}{X}\leq\frac{1}{2}\operatornamewithlimits{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_{k}(s)\cdot\zeta_{k,S}(2)\cdot\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}m_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}},

where
\zeta_{k,S}(s)=\prod_{p\not\in S}(1-(N\mathfrak{p})^{-s})^{-1}
is the partial zeta function.

Proof.

It is easy to see that
S
is a finite set, so we have

\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}m_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}<\infty.

By Theorem
1.4
, we have

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\alpha)}{X}=\frac{1}{2}\operatornamewithlimits{Res}_{s=1}\zeta_{k}(s)\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}m_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}\cdot\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\not\in S}m_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}},

so it suffices to show that

\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\not\in S}m_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}\leq\zeta_{k,S}(2).

Note that
S
is precisely the set of primes to which Lemma
4.1
does not apply, so we have

\displaystyle\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\not\in S}m_{\alpha,\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\not\in S}\Big(1+\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{2}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\not\in S}\Big(1+\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{2}}+\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{4}}+\ldots\Big)

\displaystyle=\zeta_{k,S}(2).

∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{*}
be a finitely generated subgroup and let
\mathfrak{p}
be any prime of
k
. Then
m_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}>0
.

Proof.

If
\mathfrak{p}
is archimedean, the result follows from Lemmas
3.22
and
3.23
.
If
\mathfrak{p}
is finite, then

m_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}})\geq m_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathrm{triv}})=\frac{q-1}{q}\cdot\frac{5q^{2}+8q+8}{8q^{2}},

by Lemma
3.15
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For any finitely generated subgroup
\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{*}
, we have

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\mathcal{A})}{X}>0.

Proof.

Let
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
be a finite generating set for
\mathcal{A}
, so that
\Sigma_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}=\cap_{i}\Sigma_{\alpha_{i},\mathfrak{p}}
by Lemma
2.22
. Let
S
be the set of primes of
k
such that at least one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

\mathfrak{p}
is finite and
q
is even,

(2)

\mathfrak{p}
is finite and
q=3
,

(3)

4\nmid\ord_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha_{i})
for some
i
,

(4)

\mathfrak{p}
is infinite.

It is easy to see that
S
is a finite set. Moreover, if
\mathfrak{p}\not\in S
, then

\Sigma_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}\supseteq\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathrm{triv}}\cup\Sigma_{1,\mathfrak{p}}^{(22)}\cup\Sigma_{1,\mathfrak{p}}^{(4)},

by Lemmas
2.18
,
3.6
, and
3.7
.
Lemmas
3.15
,
3.16
, and
3.17
tell us that, for all finite
\mathfrak{p}
, we have

\widetilde{m}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathrm{triv}}\cup\Sigma_{1,\mathfrak{p}}^{(22)}\cup\Sigma_{1,\mathfrak{p}}^{(4)})=\frac{q^{2}+q+1}{q^{2}}.

It follows that

m_{\mathfrak{p}}(\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathrm{triv}}\cup\Sigma_{1,\mathfrak{p}}^{(22)}\cup\Sigma_{1,\mathfrak{p}}^{(4)})=1-\frac{1}{q^{3}}.

Taking the product over all finite primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
k
, we have

\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\nmid\infty}\Big(1-\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{3}}\Big)=\zeta_{k}(3)^{-1},

which is greater than
0
. Since
S
is finite, it follows that

\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\not\in S}m_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}\geq\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\not\in S}\Big(1-\frac{1}{N(\mathfrak{p})^{3}}\Big)>0.

Since
S
is finite, Lemma
4.3
implies that

\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\in S}m_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}>0.

The result now follows from Theorem
1.4
.
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

For any finitely generated subgroup
\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{*}
, we have

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\mathcal{A})}{X}<\infty.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Lemma
4.2
, since
N(X;\mathcal{A})\leq N(X;\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
.
∎

The following lemma is elementary.

Lemma 4.6

(Elementary analysis lemma)
.

Let
(a_{n})
and
(b_{n})
be sequences of real numbers, and suppose that
A=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}
and
B=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n}
are conditionally convergent products. Then the product

\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{b_{n}}

is conditionally convergent to
\frac{A}{B}
.

Lemma 4.7
.

We have

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\mathcal{A})}{N(X)}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\frac{m_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}}{m_{1,\mathfrak{p}}}.

Proof.

This follows immediately from Theorem
1.4
and Lemma
4.6
, whose hypotheses are satisfied by Lemmas
4.5
and
4.4
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

For each finitely generated subgroup
\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{*}
, Lemmas
4.4
and
4.5
tell us that

0<\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\mathcal{A})}{X}<\infty.

It follows by the algebra of limits that

\Bigg(\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\mathcal{A})}{X}\Bigg)/\Bigg(\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\{1\})}{X}\Bigg)=\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\mathcal{A})}{N(X;\{1\})}.

Lemma
4.4
tells us that the left-hand side is a ratio of two positive numbers, so we have

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\mathcal{A})}{N(X;\{1\})}>0.

Clearly

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\mathcal{A})}{N(X;\{1\})}\leq 1,

with equality if and only if

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\mathcal{A})}{X}=\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\{1\})}{X}.

By Lemma
4.7
, this equality holds if and only if
m_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}=m_{1,\mathfrak{p}}
for all
\mathfrak{p}
, and this is the case if and only if
\Sigma_{\mathcal{A},\mathfrak{p}}=\Sigma_{1,\mathfrak{p}}
for all
\mathfrak{p}
, hence if and only if
\mathcal{A}\subseteq N_{L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}}(L^{*})
for every
\mathfrak{p}
and every quartic étale algebra
L/k_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Suppose that this is the case. Taking
L
to be the unramified quartic extension of
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
shows that
4\mid v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
and for each prime
\mathfrak{p}
of
k
. Let
T
be the set of primes
\mathfrak{p}
of
k
with
N(\mathfrak{p})
odd. Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{A}
and let
\mathfrak{p}\in T
. Write
\pi
for a uniformiser of
\mathcal{O}_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}
, so that
\alpha=\pi^{r}u
, where
4\mid r
and
u\in\mathcal{O}_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{*}
. Taking
L
to be a totally ramified quartic field extension of
k_{\mathfrak{p}}
, Lemmas
3.10
and
3.11
tell us that
u\in\mathcal{O}_{k_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{*4}
. Therefore,
\alpha
is in
k_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*4}
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in T
, so
[
NSW00
, Theorem 9.1.11]
tells us that
\alpha\in k^{*4}
. We have shown that

\lim_{X\to\infty}\frac{N(X;\mathcal{A})}{N(X;\{1\})}=1\implies\mathcal{A}\subseteq k^{*4}.

The converse implication is trivial, so we are done.
∎

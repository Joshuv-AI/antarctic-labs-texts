Stable Comodule Deformations and the Synthetic Adams-Novikov Spectral Sequence
Jake Francis Baer, Maxwell Johnson, and Peter Marek

Abstract
. We study the Adams-Novikov spectral sequence in 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}}_{p} -synthetic spectra, computing the synthetic analogs of BP \mathrm{BP} and its cooperations to identify the synthetic Adams-Novikov E 2 \mathrm{E}_{2} -page, computed in a range with a synthetic algebraic Novikov spectral sequence. We then identify deformations associated to the Cartan-Eilenberg and algebraic Novikov spectral sequences in terms of stable comodule categories, categorifying an algebraic Novikov spectral sequence result of Gheorghe-Wang-Xu. We then apply Isaksen-Wang-Xu methods in 𝔽 p {\mathbb{F}_{p}} -synthetic spectra to deduce differentials in the synthetic Adams-Novikov for the sphere, producing almost entirely algebraic computations through the 45-stem.

\infty

p

{\mathbb{C}}

[

]

[

]

[

]
,
[

]

[
ABBK23
]

1.5.
The IWX-Comparison Method

As was shown in
[
Ghe18
]
, the natural map
{\mathbb{S}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\to{\mathbb{S}}_{{\mathbb{C}}}/\tau
is one of
{\mathbb{E}}_{\infty}
-rings. The homotopy groups of the former encode the stable homotopy groups of spheres and the latter’s homotopy groups are isomorphic to the Adams-Novikov
\mathrm{E}_{2}
-page. Moreover, one can run the
{\mathbb{F}_{p}}^{\mathrm{mot}}
-Adams spectral sequence for both, and get a comparison map from the motivic Adams spectral sequence to the algebraic Novikov spectral sequence. Because inverting
\tau
in the motivic Adams spectral sequence recovers the classical Adams spectral sequence
[
DI10
]
, this innocuously constructed map allows one to transfer differentials back and forth between the two spectral sequences. This idea was first used, to great effect, in
[
IWX20
]
to drastically extend our understanding of the stable homotopy groups of spheres at the prime
2
. We show in Section
8
that our results allow for similar comparisons to be made between the Adams-Novikov spectral sequence and the Cartan-Eilenberg spectral sequence.

Recollections on Synthetic Spectra and Filtered Objects

2.1.
Synthetic Spectra

Given an Adams-type ring spectrum
E

[
Pst22
, Definition 3.14]
, one can categorify its Adams spectral sequence as Pstrągowski’s category of synthetic spectra
\mathrm{Syn}_{E}
, which is a presentably symmetric monoidal stable
\infty
-category. The synthetic construction comes equipped with a functor
\nu_{E}:{\mathrm{Sp}}\to{\mathrm{Syn}}_{E}
assigning to each spectrum
X
its synthetic analog
\nu_{E}X
(abbreviated
\nu X
where there is no ambiguity) whose bigraded homotopy groups record both the original homotopy groups
\pi_{*}(X)
and the
E
-Adams spectral sequence for
X
, in a sense made precise by
[
BHS19
, Appendix A]
. We recall some salient features of
\nu_{E}
below, which appear in
[
Pst22
]
as Lemmas 4.4 and 4.23 and Corollary 4.28.

Proposition 2.1.1

(
[
Pst22
]
)
.

The functor
\nu_{E}:{\mathrm{Sp}}\to{\mathrm{Syn}}_{E}

(a)

is fully faithful,

(b)

preserves filtered colimits,

(c)

preserves cofiber sequences
X\to Y\to Z
that induce
E_{*}(-)
short exact sequences,

(d)

and is lax symmetric monoidal.

Moreover, for any
X\simeq\operatorname{colim}X_{\alpha}
which is a filtered colimit of finite spectra with finitely generated projective
E_{*}
homology and any
Y\in{\mathrm{Sp}}
, the natural map
\nu_{E}X\otimes\nu_{E}Y\to\nu_{E}(X\otimes Y)
is an equivalence.

Notation 2.1.2
.

Such an
X_{\alpha}
is said to be
E
-finite projective
[
Pst22
, Definition 3.13]
; the full subcategory of such is denoted
{\mathrm{Sp}}^{\mathrm{fp}}_{E}
.

Notation 2.1.3
.

There is a naturally bigraded family of spheres in
{\mathrm{Syn}}_{E}
which we denote by
{\mathbb{S}}_{E}^{t,w}=\Sigma^{t-w}\nu{\mathbb{S}}^{w}
. They induce bigraded suspension functors
\Sigma^{t,w}=(-)\otimes{\mathbb{S}}^{t,w}_{E}
and their mapping groups are denoted
\pi_{t,w}X=[{\mathbb{S}}^{t,w},X]
. We often just write
{\mathbb{S}}_{E}
instead of
{\mathbb{S}}_{E}^{0,0}
. We will often restrict attention to the subcategory
{\mathrm{Syn}}_{E}^{\mathrm{cell}}\subset{\mathrm{Syn}}_{E}
which is the smallest subcategory of
{\mathrm{Syn}}_{E}
containing the bigraded spheres
{\mathbb{S}}^{t,w}
and closed under colimits.

Remark 2.1.4
.

It has recently been shown in
[
Law24
]
that when
E
is connective
{\mathrm{Syn}}_{E}\simeq{\mathrm{Syn}}_{E}^{\mathrm{cell}}
.

Because the functor
\nu
does not commute with the formal suspension in the stable
\infty
-categories
{\mathrm{Sp}}
and
{\mathrm{Syn}}_{E}
, there is an induced universal comparison map
\tau:\Sigma^{0,-1}{\mathbb{S}}_{E}\to{\mathbb{S}}_{E}
which controls much of the structure of the synthetic category. As
\tau
is a self-map of the monoidal unit, it acts all on
E
-synthetic spectra
\tau:\Sigma^{0,-1}X\to X
.

Remark 2.1.5
.

We use the notation
\tau:\Sigma^{0,-1}{\mathbb{S}}_{E}\to{\mathbb{S}}_{E}
in this section when working with a general Adams-type ring spectrum
E
. In Section
2.2
and throughout the rest of this paper, we use
\lambda
instead if
E={\mathbb{F}_{p}}
.

Theorem 2.1.6

(
[
Pst22
]
)
.

There is a symmetric monoidal and fully faithful functor
Y:{\mathrm{Sp}}\to{\mathrm{Syn}}_{E}
such that the map
\tau:\Sigma^{0,-1}Y(X)\to Y(X)
is an equivalence for all
X
. Moreover, all such
\tau
-invertible synthetic spectra are in the essential image of
Y
. As a result,
Y
provides an embedding of spectra inside of
{\mathrm{Syn}}_{E}
consisting of the
\tau
-invertible objects.

The functor
X\mapsto\tau^{-1}X
is known as the realization functor and is a symmetric monoidal left adjoint to
Y
. The effect of inverting
\tau
is often referred to as the
generic fiber
of such a deformation of spectra. We now turn to the special fiber obtained informally by setting
\tau=0
. Let
{\mathbb{S}}_{E}/\tau
denote the cofiber of
\tau:\Sigma^{0,-1}{\mathbb{S}}_{E}\to{\mathbb{S}}_{E}
. To state the result, we first recall that to a (graded) Hopf algebroid
(A,\Gamma)
there is an associated category of stable comodules
\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)
constructed by inverting the homotopy equivalences of chain complexes detected by projectives
[
Hov04
]
. The following is an amalgamation of results in Sections 4.5 and 4.6 of
[
Pst22
]
:

Theorem 2.1.7
.

There is an adjunction

\chi_{*}:\mathrm{Mod}({\mathrm{Syn}}_{E};{\mathbb{S}}_{E}/\tau)\leftrightarrows\mathrm{Stable}(E_{*}E):\chi^{*}

whose left adjoint is fully faithful. On synthetic spectra in the image of
\nu
, the composition
\chi_{L}(-\otimes{\mathbb{S}}_{E}/\tau)
corresponds to taking
E_{*}
homology and hence takes the bigraded shifts of the cofiber of
\tau
to those of the unit of
\mathrm{Stable}(E_{*}E)
, inducing an equivalence on cellular objects. In addition, there is an isomorphism of abelian groups

[\Sigma^{t,w}\nu X,\nu Y/\tau]\cong\mathrm{Ext}^{w-t,w}_{E_{*}E}(E_{*}X,E_{*}Y).

We see that the cofiber of
\tau
records the
\mathrm{E}_{2}
page of the
E
-Adams spectral sequence. In fact, the
\tau
-Bockstein spectral sequence encodes the entirety of the
E
-Adams, see
[
BHS19
]
[Thm. 9.19].

2.2.
Synthetic Spectra over
{\mathbb{F}_{p}}

When
E={\mathbb{F}_{p}}
, we can make significantly stronger statements than for general Adams-type homology theories. Because it is conventional to associate the map
\tau
with motivic (and therefore
\mathrm{MU}
- or
\mathrm{BP}
-synthetic) homotopy theory, we will write
\lambda
for the analogous map in
{\mathrm{Syn}}_{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}

below.

Proposition 2.2.1
.

The following stronger statements hold for the category of
{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
-synthetic spectra:

(a)

the functor
\nu_{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}
is symmetric monoidal,

(b)

and the inclusion
\mathrm{Mod}({\mathrm{Syn}}_{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}};{\mathbb{S}}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}/\lambda)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Stable}(\mathcal{A}_{*})
is an equivalence.

Proof.

The first observation is that
{\mathrm{Sp}}_{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}^{\mathrm{fp}}\simeq{\mathrm{Sp}}^{\mathrm{fin}}
where the right-hand side is the category of finite spectra: the projectivity requirement is trivial for

-vector spaces and finiteness of a spectrum implies the finite generation of its mod
p
homology. Because every spectrum is a filtered colimit of finite spectra, (a) follows from Proposition
2.1.1
. Statement (b) then follows from the fact that the inclusion is essentially surjective onto the cellular subcategory and

is cellular

HPS97
]
[Thm. 2.3.1].
∎

2.3.
Filtered Models

The results in this section are collected from
[
GIKR22
]
,
[
Hed20
]
, and
[
BHS20
]
. Any results not therein are certainly known to experts; we make no claims of originality. Let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq}
denote the category of integers with unique morphisms
m\to n
whenever
m\geq n
. Given a category
{\mathcal{C}}
, a filtered object in
{\mathcal{C}}
is the data of a functor

X_{*}:{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq}\to{\mathcal{C}},

and these compile into a category
{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{fil}}=\mathrm{Fun}({\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq},{\mathcal{C}})
. We may consider a filtered object to be a diagram in
{\mathcal{C}}
, and taking colimits yields a functor
\operatorname{Re}:{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{fil}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
known as realization. Assuming that
{\mathcal{C}}
has a zero object,
\operatorname{Re}
has a collection of sections
Y_{k}
, for
-\infty<k\leq\infty
, which are determined by:

Y_{k}X_{w}=\begin{cases}X&w\leq k\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

where the connecting maps are identity where possible and
0
otherwise. This is still perfectly well defined for
k=-\infty
, but is the
0
functor and, of course, no longer a section of
\operatorname{Re}
.

Remark 2.3.1
.

Where we feel notational confusion may be possible, we will write
\operatorname{Re}^{\text{fil}}
to distinguish the filtered-realization functor from that of other deformations.

Lemma 2.3.2

(
[
Gla16
]
)
.

If
{\mathcal{C}}
is symmetric monoidal and its tensor product preserves colimits in each variable, then via Day convolution the category
{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{fil}}
is again symmetric monoidal, with unit
Y_{0}\mathbf{1}
. The tensor product on
{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{fil}}
will then also preserve colimits in each variable.

The category of filtered objects also comes equipped with a distinguished autoequivalence
[1]:{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{fil}}\to{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{fil}}
determined by the shift
X[1]_{w}=X_{w+1}
distinct from the formal suspension
\Sigma
, which is computed levelwise in
{\mathcal{C}}^{\text{fil}}
. We will write
\Sigma^{t,w}:=\Sigma^{t}\circ[w]
.

3.
The Synthetic Adams-Novikov Spectral Sequence

For an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-synthetic spectrum
X
, we will let
X_{\star}:=\pi_{\star}(X)
and
X_{\star}X:=\pi_{\star}(X\otimes X)
. The goal in this section is to compute the bigraded Hopf algebroid
(\mathrm{BP}^{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}_{\star},\mathrm{BP}_{\star}\mathrm{BP}^{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}})
and to give tools for computing the
\mathrm{E}_{2}
-page of the synthetic Adams-Novikov spectral sequence in terms of this Hopf algebroid. Throughout this section, we fix a prime

.

3.1.
Homotopy of the synthetic analogs of
\mathrm{BP}
and
\mathrm{BP}\otimes\mathrm{BP}

We first compute
\mathrm{BP}^{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}_{\star}
and
\mathrm{BP}_{\star}\mathrm{BP}^{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}
as
{\mathbb{Z}}_{(p)}[\lambda]

(1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{\lambda}^{\wedge}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X[\lambda^{-1}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X_{\lambda}^{\wedge})[\lambda^{-1}]}

for an arbitrary

. Though the existence of this fracture square has likely been known by experts in synthetic spectra, we reproduce a proof of its existence generally in
{\mathrm{Syn}}_{E}
by imitating
[
DFHH14
, Section 6]
:

Lemma 3.1.1
.

The commutative square (1) for
X\in{\mathrm{Syn}}_{E}
is a pullback square.

Proof.

We first show that
X\simeq L_{{\mathbb{S}}_{E}}X\simeq L_{({\mathbb{S}}_{E}[\lambda^{-1}]\vee{\mathbb{S}}_{E}/\lambda)}
, where

denotes Bousfield localization. The first equivalence is obvious. For the second, we show that the class of
{\mathbb{S}}_{E}
-acyclics agrees with the class of
({\mathbb{S}}_{E}[\lambda^{-1}]\vee{\mathbb{S}}_{E}/\lambda)
-acyclics. Note that
X
is
{\mathbb{S}}_{E}
-acyclic if and only if
X\simeq 0
. So immediately,
{\mathbb{S}}_{E}
-acyclic implies
({\mathbb{S}}_{E}[\lambda^{-1}]\vee{\mathbb{S}}_{E}/\lambda)
-acyclic. For the other direction,

being
({\mathbb{S}}_{E}[\lambda^{-1}]\vee{\mathbb{S}}_{E}/\lambda)
-acyclic is equivalent to both
X\otimes{\mathbb{S}}_{E}[\lambda^{-1}]\simeq X[\lambda^{-1}]\simeq 0
and
X/\lambda\simeq 0
. If
X/\lambda\simeq 0
, then
\lambda:X\to\Sigma^{0,1}X
is an equivalence so that
X[\lambda^{-1}]\simeq X
. Hence,
X\simeq 0
.

Since
(X/\lambda)[\lambda^{-1}]\simeq 0
is true for any synthetic spectrum

, the fracture square follows from a synthetic version of
[
DFHH14
, Section 6, Prop. 2.2]
.
∎

The most mysterious part of this square is the
\lambda
-completion

. For synthetic analogs of ordinary spectra, the
\lambda
-completion is straightforward:

Lemma 3.1.2
.

If

\nu_{E}(Y)_{\lambda}^{\wedge}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\nu_{E}(Y_{E}^{\wedge})

Note that

with the diagonal map being induced by the

If

\nu_{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}(Y)_{\lambda}^{\wedge}\xrightarrow{\simeq}\nu_{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}(Y_{p}^{\wedge})

3.3.
Synthetic Adams-Novikov SS

As a consequence of Theorem

, the Hopf algebroid
(\mathrm{BP}^{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}_{\star},\mathrm{BP}_{\star}\mathrm{BP}^{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}})
is flat. In the usual way, this gives a nice identification of the
\mathrm{E}_{2}
-page of the
\mathrm{BP}^{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}
-Adams spectral sequence in terms of Ext groups:

Proposition 3.3.1
.

Suppose

is an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-synthetic spectrum. Then the
\mathrm{E}_{2}
-page of the
\mathrm{BP}^{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}
-Adams spectral sequence for

is isomorphic to

\mathrm{E}_{2}=\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{BP}_{\star}\mathrm{BP}^{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}}^{*,*,*}(\mathrm{BP}^{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}_{\star},\mathrm{BP}^{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}_{\star}X).

Whenever
X
is bounded below in the sense that there’s an

such
\pi_{k,w}X=0
for

, the
\mathrm{BP}^{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}
-Adams spectral sequence turns out to converge to the homotopy of the

-localization of

. This is analogous to the classical situation, where the
\mathrm{BP}
-Adams spectral sequence for a connective spectrum converges to the homotopy of its

-localization. We state the main result here, but defer the proof to Appendix
A
, which uses the machinery of
[

]
:

Theorem 3.3.2
.

(Theorem

)
For every bounded-below

,

X_{\mathrm{BP}^{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}}^{\wedge}\simeq X_{(p)}

where

denotes
\mathrm{BP}^{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}
-nilpotent completion and

-localization is taken with respect to

.

3.4.
A synthetic algebraic Novikov SS

Classically, the algebraic Novikov spectral sequence (aNSS) is a spectral sequence that tries to compute the
\mathrm{E}_{2}
-page

{}_{\mathrm{AN}}\mathrm{E}_{2}^{s,t}=\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{BP}_{*}\mathrm{BP}}^{s,t}(\mathrm{BP}_{*},\mathrm{BP}_{*}X)

of the Adams-Novikov spectral sequence (ANSS) for a spectrum
X
. The ANSS
\mathrm{E}_{2}
-page is the homology of the
\mathrm{BP}_{*}\mathrm{BP}
-cobar complex, which we can filter by powers of the ideal
I:=\ker(\mathrm{BP}_{*}\to{\mathbb{F}}_{p})=(p,v_{1},v_{2},\ldots)
. The resulting spectral sequence is the aNSS and it has
\mathrm{E}_{2}
-page

\mathrm{E}_{2}=\mathrm{Ext}_{\mathrm{Gr}_{*}(\mathrm{BP}_{*}\mathrm{BP})}^{*,*,*}(\mathrm{Gr}_{*}(\mathrm{BP}_{*}),\mathrm{Gr}_{*}(\mathrm{BP}_{*}X)),

where
\mathrm{Gr}_{*}(-)
is the associated graded functor. The pair
(\mathrm{Gr}_{*}(\mathrm{BP}_{*}),\mathrm{Gr}_{*}(\mathrm{BP}_{*}\mathrm{BP}))
is a Hopf algebroid satisfying
\mathrm{Gr}_{*}(\mathrm{BP}_{*})\cong{\mathbb{F}}_{p}[a_{0},a_{1},\ldots]
with
a_{i}
in bidegree
(u,t)=(1,2p^{i}-2)
and

Proof.

The proof is similar to the proof of

5.
Twisted
t
-structures of Stable
\infty
-Categories

Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a stable
\infty
-category with a compact object
K
. By a twist of
{\mathcal{C}}
we mean an automorphism
F:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
. We will notate by
\Sigma^{n,m}:{\mathcal{C}}\to{\mathcal{C}}
the composition
\Sigma^{n}\circ F^{m}
. Note that
\Sigma^{n,m}
will preserve compact objects for all
n,m
as a composition of autoequivalences.

Definition 5.1
.

We say that
{\mathcal{C}}
is generated by
K
under bigraded suspensions if the smallest subcategory of
{\mathcal{C}}
containing
\Sigma^{n,m}K
for all
n,m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and closed under colimits is
{\mathcal{C}}
itself.

Remark 5.2
.

We will mostly be working with cellular subcategories where the generation assumption is forcibly imposed.

The goal of this section is to construct a “twisted”
t
-structure on
{\mathcal{C}}
whose connective part is detected by the mapping groups
[\Sigma^{n,m}K,-]
. To this end, fix
{\mathcal{C}},F,K
as described above such that
K
generates under colimits and put

{\mathcal{C}}_{\geq 0}=\{X\in{\mathcal{C}}\mid[\Sigma^{n,m}K,X]=0\text{ whenever }n+m<0\}.

Lemma 5.3
.

The category
{\mathcal{C}}_{\geq 0}
is the connective part of a
t
-structure on
{\mathcal{C}}
as soon as
K\in{\mathcal{C}}_{\geq 0}
.

Proof.

Write
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{\geq 0}
for the closure of the objects
\Sigma^{n,m}K
,
n+m\geq 0
in
{\mathcal{C}}
under colimits and extensions. We claim that
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{\geq 0}\simeq{\mathcal{C}}_{\geq 0}
. Having shown this, the lemma follows from
[
Lur17
, 1.4.4.11]
. To prove the equivalence, first observe that
{\mathcal{C}}_{\geq 0}
contains all of the
\Sigma^{n+m}K
for
n+m\geq 0
. To prove it is closed under colimits it suffices to show it is closed under arbitrary coproducts and cofibers. For the former, finite coproducts are immediate. Because all coproducts are filtered colimits of finite coproducts, we have all coproducts by the compactness of
K
. That we have all cofibers follows from the induced long exact sequence. Closure under extensions follows similarly. As a result we have
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}_{\geq 0}\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{\geq 0}
. To conclude it suffices to show that the latter category is generated under colimits by the same shifts of
K
. We note that
{\mathcal{C}}
being generated by the shifts is equivalent to asking that
X\to Y
is an equivalence if and only if the induced

\mathrm{Map}(\Sigma^{n,m}K,X)\to\mathrm{Map}(\Sigma^{n,m}K,Y)

is an equivalence of spaces for all
n,m
. Replacing mapping spaces by mapping spectra it suffices to check that

\mathrm{Map}^{\mathrm{Sp}}(\Sigma^{0,m}K,X)\to\mathrm{Map}^{\mathrm{Sp}}(\Sigma^{0,m}K,Y)

is an equivalence for all
m
. But
\pi_{k}\mathrm{Map}^{\mathrm{Sp}}(\Sigma^{0,m}K,X)=\pi_{0}\mathrm{Map}^{\mathrm{Sp}}(\Sigma^{k,m}K,X)
so that it suffices to check for an isomorphism on the mapping groups
[\Sigma^{k,m}K,-]
. For
X,Y
connective, the latter only depends on those where
k+m\geq 0
.
∎

Definition 5.4
.

The
(K,F)
-twisted
t
-structure on
{\mathcal{C}}
is the
t
-structure with connective part

as described above.

In practice, we will drop

from the notation. We wish to use twisted

]
and for defining deformations in Section

. The remainder of this section will work to verify his assumptions laid out in Section 2.1.1 of loc. cit.

The

Proof.

By
[

, 1.2.1.19]
it suffices to show that the only object in
{\mathcal{C}}_{\leq\infty}:=\cap_{n}{\mathcal{C}}_{\geq n}
is
0
. But due to colimit generation, an object

is

as soon as

for all

as shown in the proof of Lemma

∎

Lemma 5.6
.

The truncations

in the

Proof.

Given a filtered diagram

we see that the compactness of

Our main use of Lemma

\mathrm{Ext}_{\Gamma}^{*,*}(A,-)

Lemma 5.12
.

Suppose
(A,\Gamma)
is a graded or bigraded Hopf algebroid such that
A\in\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)_{\geq 0}
. Then the functors

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}_{\Gamma}^{*,*}(A,-)

\displaystyle:(\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)^{\mathrm{cell}})^{\heartsuit}\to\mathrm{Ext}_{\Gamma}^{t=s}\text{-mod}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}_{\Gamma}^{*,*,*}(A,-)

\displaystyle:(\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)^{\mathrm{cell}})^{\heartsuit}\to\mathrm{Ext}_{\Gamma}^{t=s,*}\text{-mod}

are fully faithful.

Proof.

We first note that for
X,Y\in(\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)^{\mathrm{cell}})^{\heartsuit}
and
n>0
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\pi_{*,*}A}(\pi_{*,*}(\Sigma^{n,0}X),\pi_{*,*}(Y))

\displaystyle=0=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ext}^{t=s}_{\Gamma}}(\pi_{*,*}(\Sigma^{n,0}X),\pi_{*,*}(Y)),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\pi_{*,*,*}A}(\pi_{*,*,*}(\Sigma^{n,0,0}X),\pi_{*,*,*}(Y))

\displaystyle=0=\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ext}^{t=s,*}_{\Gamma}}(\pi_{*,*,*}(\Sigma^{n,0,0}X),\pi_{*,*,*}(Y)),

for degree reasons. Whenever
n=0
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\pi_{*,*}A}(\pi_{*,*}(X),\pi_{*,*}(Y))

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ext}^{t=s}_{\Gamma}}(\pi_{*,*}(X),\pi_{*,*}(Y)),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}_{\pi_{*,*,*}A}(\pi_{*,*,*}(X),\pi_{*,*,*}(Y))

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Ext}^{t=s,*}_{\Gamma}}(\pi_{*,*,*}(X),\pi_{*,*,*}(Y)),

by the assumption that
X
and
Y
are in the heart. So to show fully faithful, it suffices to show that

\displaystyle[\Sigma^{n,0}X,Y]_{\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)^{\mathrm{cell}}}

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\pi_{*,*}A}(\pi_{*,*}(\Sigma^{n,0}X),\pi_{*,*}(Y)),

\displaystyle[\Sigma^{n,0,0}X,Y]_{\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)^{\mathrm{cell}}}\to

\displaystyle\text{ }\mathrm{Hom}_{\pi_{*,*,*}A}(\pi_{*,*,*}(\Sigma^{n,0,0}X),\pi_{*,*,*}(Y)),

is an isomorphism for
n\geq 0
. Whenever
n>0
, both sides are equal to 0 since
X
and
Y
are assumed to be in the heart.

To show it’s an isomorphism whenever
n=0
, we use the spectral sequence of Lemma
5.11
. Note that
\mathrm{E}_{1}^{t,t,0}
and
\mathrm{E}_{1}^{t,t,0,0}
for
t\geq 0
are the respective
\mathrm{E}_{1}
-page degrees which compute the bigraded and trigraded versions of
[X,Y]_{\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)^{\mathrm{cell}}}
. Since we assume that
A
is connective, we can choose a free resolution such that
\pi_{a,b}(\Sigma^{t,0}F_{s})
and
\pi_{a,b,c}(\Sigma^{t,0,0}F_{s})
vanish for
a+b-t<0
. For
t>0
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}_{1}^{t,t,0}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Hom}_{\pi_{*,*}A}(\pi_{*,*}\Sigma^{t,0}F_{t},\pi_{*,*}Y)

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}_{1}^{t,t,0,0}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Hom}_{\pi_{*,*,*}A}(\pi_{*,*,*}\Sigma^{t,0,0}F_{t},\pi_{*,*,*}Y)

are both 0 for degree reasons since
Y
is in the heart. Then the
\mathrm{E}_{1}
-page is concentrated in
t=0
and, for degree reasons, the differentials entering and exiting
\mathrm{E}_{1}^{0,0,0}
and
\mathrm{E}_{1}^{0,0,0,0}
are 0. Hence the edge homomorphisms

\displaystyle[X,Y]_{\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)^{\mathrm{cell}}}

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\pi_{*,*}A}(\pi_{*,*}(X),\pi_{*,*}(Y))

\displaystyle[X,Y]_{\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)^{\mathrm{cell}}}

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Hom}_{\pi_{*,*,*}A}(\pi_{*,*,*}(X),\pi_{*,*,*}(Y))

are isomorphisms, which finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.13
.

The functors

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}_{\Gamma}^{*,*}(A,-)

\displaystyle:(\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)^{\mathrm{cell}})^{\heartsuit}\to\mathrm{Ext}_{\Gamma}^{t=s}\text{-}\mathrm{mod}

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}_{\Gamma}^{*,*,*}(A,-)

\displaystyle:(\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)^{\mathrm{cell}})^{\heartsuit}\to\mathrm{Ext}_{\Gamma}^{t=s,*}\text{-}\mathrm{mod}

are essentially surjective.

Proof.

We just need to show that every
M\in\mathrm{Ext}^{t=s}_{\Gamma}
-mod or
M\in\mathrm{Ext}^{t=s,*}_{\Gamma}
-mod can be realized as the homotopy of an object in
(\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)^{\mathrm{cell}})^{\heartsuit}
. Note that the Ext groups of the unit
F:=\pi_{0}^{\heartsuit}(A)\in(\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)^{\mathrm{cell}})^{\heartsuit}
satisfy

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}^{*,*}_{\Gamma}(A,F)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{\Gamma}^{t=s},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ext}^{*,*,*}_{\Gamma}(A,F)

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Ext}_{\Gamma}^{t=s,*}.

If we have a free resolution of
M

\cdots\to F_{2}\to F_{1}\to F_{0}\to M\to 0,

then by taking wedges of
F\in(\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)^{\mathrm{cell}})^{\heartsuit}
, each
F_{i}
can be realized as the homotopy of an object
Z_{i}\in(\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)^{\mathrm{cell}})^{\heartsuit}
. By Lemma
5.12
, the map
F_{1}\to F_{0}
can be realized as Ext groups applied to a map of stable comodules
Z_{1}\to Z_{0}
. Consider the cofiber
X_{1}
of this map. Its Ext groups satisfy

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{s=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{Ext}^{s-k,s}_{\Gamma}(A,X_{1})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathrm{coker}(F_{1}\to F_{0})=M,&k=0\\
\mathrm{ker}(F_{1}\to F_{0})&k=1\\
0&\mathrm{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{s=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathrm{Ext}^{s-k,s,*}_{\Gamma}(A,X_{1})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\mathrm{coker}(F_{1}\to F_{0})=M,&k=0\\
\mathrm{ker}(F_{1}\to F_{0})&k=1\\
0&\mathrm{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then the Ext groups of the truncation
\tau_{\leq 0}X_{1}
are exactly isomorphic to
M
, which finishes the proof.
∎

In this section, we explore deformations of
\mathrm{Stable}(\mathcal{A}_{*})
and
\mathrm{Stable}(\mathrm{BP}_{*}\mathrm{BP})
associated with the Cartan-Eilenberg spectral sequence for the extension
\mathcal{P}_{*}\to\mathcal{A}_{*}\to\mathcal{E}_{*}
and the algebraic Novikov spectral sequence respectively and identify them with categories of stable comodules over
\mathcal{A}_{*}^{\mathrm{BP}}
and
\mathrm{BP}_{\star}\mathrm{BP}^{{\mathbb{F}_{p}}}

Let
(A,\Gamma)
be a Hopf algebroid and let
\mathrm{Comod}(\Gamma)
denote its 1-category of comodules. A comodule
C
is said to be an

if it is isomorphic to one of the form
\Gamma\otimes_{A}M
for some
A
-module

relative injective

[

C

0\to C\to C^{0}\to C^{1}\to C^{2}\to...

which is split-exact over
A
and such that all of the
C_{i}
are relative injectives. Such a resolution always exists: one can take the cobar complex

C^{i}=\Gamma\otimes\bar{\Gamma}^{\otimes i}\otimes C

where
\bar{\Gamma}
is the kernel of the augmentation
\Gamma\to A
. Given such a
C^{*}
resolving
C

\mathrm{Comod}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)
of the 1-category of
\Gamma
-comodules into
\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)
as complexes concentrated in degree
0
induces an equivalence
C\simeq C^{*}
. As a result, given any filtration

C^{*}\leftarrow\text{fil}^{1}C^{*}\leftarrow\text{fil}^{2}C^{*}\leftarrow\text{fil}^{3}C^{*}\leftarrow...

of the resolution by chain complexes
C_{*}^{j}
yields a filtration of
C
in
\mathrm{Stable}(\Gamma)
. As is explained in Section 9 of
[
GWX21
]

(A,\Gamma)=(\mathrm{BP}_{*},\mathrm{BP}_{*}\mathrm{BP})
we may form the algebraic Novikov spectral sequence as the spectral sequence associated to the filtration by powers of the ideal
I=(p,v_{1},v_{2},...)
. Explicitly, we put:

(\text{fil}^{j}C^{*})^{i}=I^{j-i}C^{i}

where
C^{*}
is the cobar complex resolution of
\mathrm{BP}_{*}
. The authors of
[
GWX21
]
proceed to prove that the

-Adams spectral sequence for the cofiber of

-Adams spectral sequence in

A similar story can be told for the classical CESS. The CESS may be similarly constructed by taking the cobar resolution

of

Appendix A
Nilpotent Completion in Stable
\infty
-Categories

The classical
E
-Adams spectral sequence can be used to compute the homotopy groups
\pi_{*}(X_{E}^{\wedge})
of the
E
-nilpotent completion of a spectrum
X
. A celebrated result of
[
Bou79
]
relates the
E
-nilpotent completion to Bousfield localization at
E
; namely, the
E
-nilpotent completion is
E
-local and the natural map

from the
E
-localization of

is an equivalence when
E
is connective,
X
is bounded-below, and
\pi_{0}E
satisfies certain conditions. In addition, there is an equivalence
L_{E}X\simeq L_{M\pi_{0}E}X
with localization with respect to the Moore spectrum
M\pi_{0}E
of
\pi_{0}E
. This is useful because Bousfield localization can be easier to understand than nilpotent completion. For example, if
E={\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and
X
bounded-below, then
L_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}X\xrightarrow{\simeq}X_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}^{\wedge}
and
L_{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}X\simeq L_{{\mathbb{S}}/p}X\simeq X_{p}^{\wedge}
, the

-completion of

.

Recent work of
[
Man21
]
generalizes Bousfield’s results from spectra to presentable, stable
\infty
-categories. Roughly speaking,
E
is instead a homotopy commutative algebra, connectivity corresponds to the connective part of a
t
-structure, Moore spectra correspond to the cofibers and/or localizations of a set of maps, and conditions on
\pi_{0}E
correspond to conditions on

in the heart of the

-structure.

In this appendix, we recall the background and precise results of
[
Man21
]
and use them to study examples of nilpotent completion relevant to this paper.

and unit object

and

\lambda

\lambda^{2}

•

Purple

\lambda^{3}

B.2.3.
Extensions

Vertical lines and lines of positive slope indicate extensions. Lines that stay in the same stem indicate multiplication by
\lambda
or
h

\alpha_{1}
(
\eta
in homotopy). Lines that change stem by 3 indicate multiplication by
\alpha_{2/2}
(
\nu
in homotopy). The curvature of an extension has no mathematical meaning and is there for aesthetic reasons. The color of an extension indicates something particular about the extension:

•

Gray
,
red
,
blue
, and
dark green
lines indicate non-hidden extensions which hit
\lambda^{0}=1
times a generator. The color corresponds to the
\lambda
-torsion (or freeness) of the target.

•

Orange
indicates hidden extensions which hit
\lambda^{0}=1
times a generator.

•

Magenta
lines hit
\lambda
times a generator. These can be non-hidden or hidden extensions.

•

Lime green
lines hit
\lambda^{2}
times a generator. These can be non-hidden or hidden extensions.

•

Purple
lines hit
\lambda^{3}
times a generator. In this range, these are only non-hidden.

•

Cyan
indicate hidden
\lambda
-extensions. These all hit
\lambda^{0}=1
times a generator.

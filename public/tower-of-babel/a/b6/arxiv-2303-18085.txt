High Frobenius pushforwards generate the bounded derived category
Matthew R. Ballard, Srikanth B. Iyengar, Pat Lank, Alapan Mukhopadhyay, Josh Pollitz

Abstract
. This work concerns generators for the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves over a noetherian scheme X X of prime characteristic. The main result is that when the Frobenius map on X X is finite, for any compact generator G G of 𝖣 ⁡ ( X ) \mathsf{D}(X) the Frobenius pushforward F ∗ e ​ G F^{e}_{*}G generates the bounded derived category whenever p e p^{e} is larger than the codepth of X X , an invariant that is a measure of the singularity of X X . The conclusion holds for all positive integers e e when X X is locally complete intersection. The question of when one can take G = 𝒪 X G=\mathcal{O}_{X} is also investigated. For smooth projective complete intersections it reduces to a question of generation of the Kuznetsov component.

Introduction

This work concerns the existence of (strong) generators in the bounded derived category of a noetherian scheme and, in particular, a commutative noetherian ring. The notion of a strong generator for an (essentially small) triangulated category
{\mathsf{T}}
was introduced by Bondal and van den Bergh
[
20
]
. Roughly speaking, an object
G
in
{\mathsf{T}}
is a generator if each object
A
can be built from
G
using the operations in
{\mathsf{T}}
: finite direct sums, summands, and mapping cones. When there is an an upper bound, independent of
A
, on the number of mapping cones required,
G
is said to be a strong generator; see
1.1
.

It is proved in
[
20
]
that the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves on a scheme that is essentially of finite type and smooth over a field admits a strong generator. Subsequently Aoki
[
3
]
proved that
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}{X})
has a strong generator for any separated, quasi-excellent scheme
X
of finite Krull dimension; this also extends work of Rouquier
[
80
]
, Keller and van den Bergh
[
49
]
, Lunts
[
60
]
, Iyengar and Takahashi
[
47
]
, and Neeman
[
69
]
.

While this settles the question of the existence of a strong generator in great generality, there are not that many general results that identify them.

We focus first on the affine situation:
X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Spec}R
, where
R
is a commutative noetherian ring. In this case,
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
is equivalent to
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
, the bounded derived category of finitely generated
R
-modules. When
R
is regular and of finite Krull dimension,
R
itself is a strong generator for
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
. In fact, any
R
-complex in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
having full support is a strong generator. The latter is a consequence of a theorem of Hopkins
[
43
]
, and Neeman
[
66
]
; see
1.5
. One can also identify strong generators when
R
is a complete intersection ring with isolated singularities using a theorem of Stevenson
[
91
]
. When
R
is an artinian ring,
R/J
, where
J
is the Jacobson radical of
R
, is a strong generator for
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
.

The situation is even more complicated in the global case. Perhaps the most comprehensive result is due to Orlov
[
73
]
who proved that when
X
is quasi-projective, with very ample line bundle
L
, the direct sum
\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus L\oplus\cdots\oplus L^{\otimes d}
, where
d=\dim X
, is a generator for
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
, the subcategory of perfect complexes. This gives strong generators for
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
when
X
is regular. Outside this case, there are only a few results that identify explicit generators for the bounded derived category.

In light of these remarks, it is surprising that for any
F
-finite scheme of prime characteristic one can identify strong generators for the bounded derived category, at least in terms of generators for
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
. This is the content of the result below. The existence of a generator
E
, as below, for
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
is a result of Bondal and van den Bergh
[
20
, Theorem 3.1.1]
. We emphasize that
E
need not be a strong generator for
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
. Indeed, the latter has no strong generators when
X
is singular; see
[
80
,
70
]
.
As usual
F^{e}_{*}G
denotes the pushforward of a complex
G
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
along
F^{e}
, the
e
-fold composition of the Frobenius map on
X
.

Theorem A
.

Let
X
be a noetherian
F
-finite scheme of prime characteristic
p
, and
E
a generator for
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
. For any
G
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
with
\operatorname{supp}_{X}G=X
, the complex
F^{e}_{*}(E\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{X}G)
is a generator for
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
for any natural number
e>\log_{p}(\operatorname{codepth}X)
; it is a strong generator when
X
is separated.

This result is contained in
Corollary

3.9
. The invariant
\operatorname{codepth}X
is defined for any noetherian scheme
X
, in terms of the local rings at
x\in X
; see
Section

3
. When
X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{Spec}R
, the codepth of
X
is bounded above by the number of generators of the
R
-module
F_{*}R
; see
Lemma

3.2
, which also provides a better bound. In particular, the codepth is finite for any
F
-finite noetherian scheme. The codepth of
X
is equal to
0
if and only if
X
is regular, and so it can be viewed as a numerical measure of the singularity of
X
.

Two special cases are worth noting. When
X
is affine, the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X}
of
X
generates
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
, so the preceding result specializes to:

Corollary B
.

When
R
is an
F
-finite commutative noetherian ring of prime characteristic
p
, the
R
-module
F^{e}_{*}R
is a strong generator for
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
for any natural number
e>\log_{p}(\operatorname{codepth}R)
.

In fact, one can take the Frobenius pushforward of any
R
-complex with coherent cohomology and full support; see
Corollary

3.11
. Here is another special case of
A
; it reappears as
Corollary

3.10
.

Corollary C
.

When
X
is a quasi-projective scheme over an
F
-finite field of prime characteristic
p
, and
L
a very ample line bundle,
F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus F^{e}_{*}L\oplus\cdots\oplus F^{e}_{*}L^{\otimes\dim X}
is a strong generator of
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
, for any natural number
e>\log_{p}(\operatorname{codepth}X)
.

It is not hard to deduce from these results that if for some
G
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
with full support and integer
n\geq 1
, the complex
F^{n}_{*}G
is perfect, then
X
is regular; this recovers
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
. Thus one can view
A
as a structural refinement of Kunz’s theorem
[
52
]
that is the case
G=\mathcal{O}_{X}
. One can also deduce other results of this ilk, characterizing the singularity type of
X
in terms of homological properties of
F
; such as those from
[
8
,
41
,
79
,
94
]
.

There are two key ingredients that go into the proof of
A
. One is a local-to-global principle that says, roughly, that a complex
G
is a generator for
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
if the stalk complex
G_{x}
is a generator for
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{O}_{x})
for each
x\in X
; see
Corollary

1.10
. A subtle point here is that generation involves the structure of
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
as module over
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
viewed as a tensor-triangulated category. We deduce this local-to-global principle from a more general statement, involving the ind-completions of the categories. This builds on work of Stevenson
[
89
]
, and is explained in
Section

1
.

Once we are down to the level of stalks, the main result concerns a nilpotence property of the Frobenius endomorphism on a local ring of prime characteristic. It implies that, under appropriate conditions involving support, high Frobenius pushforwards of any complex generates the finite length complexes in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{O}_{x})
. This is the content of
Section

2
.

A
prompts a number of questions. One is what can be said, vis a vis generation, when
F
is not necessarily finite; see
Theorem

3.6
for what we have to offer in this regard. Another question, prompted by Kunz’s theorem, is whether already the first Frobenius pushforward of some object generates the bounded derived category. Here is the most decisive result we prove concerning this question.

Theorem D
.

When
X
is
F
-finite and locally complete intersection, for any generator
G
of
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
, the complex
F_{*}G
is a strong generator for
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
. In particular, if
X
is affine,
F_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
strongly generates
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
.

See
Theorem

6.3
, and also
Theorem

6.5
for a more general statement. We also prove that Veronese subrings of
k[x,y]
have this property. The results for affine varieties raises the question: Does
F_{*}R
generate
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
for any commutative noetherian ring
R
? We do not know of any counterexamples.

In light of these results it is natural to ask for the smallest number
e
such that
F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
strongly generates
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
. This is interesting even when
X=\operatorname{Spec}R
, for examples suggest that the upper bound for
e
given by
A
is far from optimal. Here are some results in this direction; see
Sections

4
and
6
.

•

e\leq\lceil\log_{p}(\mathrm{Loewy\,length}\,R)\rceil
when
R
is an artinian local ring;

•

e\leq\lceil\log_{p}(n+1)\rceil
when
X=\mathbb{P}^{n}
;

•

e\leq\lceil\log_{p}3\rceil
with
X
the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at
\leq 4
points in general position;

•

e\leq\lceil\log_{p}n\rceil
for some smooth quadrics
X
of dimension
n
.

There may be no such
e
when, for instance,
X
is an
F
-finite smooth curve of positive genus; see
Theorem

4.10
. This prompts a definition:
X
is
F
-thick
if
F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
itself generates
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
for some
e\geq 1
. The class of
F
-thick schemes includes all affine schemes and overlaps significantly with varieties possessing a full exceptional collection. For smooth projective complete intersections
F
-thickness is tantamount to generation of the Kuznetsov component; see
Theorem

4.16
.

Shifting the focus from the full derived category to specific objects, recent and not so recent developments suggest considering the number of steps required to build
\mathcal{O}_{X}
from
F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
, for some
e
. We are interested in this number partly because in the affine case it is one measure of a failure of the
F
-split property.

Corollary E
.

For any
F
-finite noetherian ring
R
one has that

\inf\{n\mid\text{$R$ is in $\operatorname{thick}^{n}(F^{e}_{*}R)$ for some $e\geq 1$}\}

is finite; it equals one if and only if
R
is
F
-split. It is bounded above by
p^{c}
when
R
is locally complete intersection and
c
is the codimension of
R
.

The finiteness is immediate from
B
, and the second part is essentially the definition of the
F
-split property. These results are proved in
Section

5
. Another reason for our interest in
E
is that it says, in the language of
[
27
]
, that the
R
-module
F^{e}_{*}R
is proxy-small for
e\gg 0
. Among other things, this has consequences for the derived endomorphism ring of
F^{e}_{*}R
; see
op. cit.
and also
[
26
]
.

1.
Generators for triangulated categories

In this section we recall some basic notions and results concerning triangulated categories. The main examples of interest are various categories constructed from (quasi-)coherent sheaves on a noetherian scheme. We take Krause’s book
[
51
]
as our standard reference on triangulated categories; for constructions and results specific to schemes see Huybrecht’s book
[
45
]
and the Stacks Project
[
25
]
.

1.1
.

Let
{\mathsf{K}}
be a triangulated category. A subcategory
{\mathsf{S}}
of
{\mathsf{K}}
is
thick
if it is a full triangulated subcategory closed under retracts. Given an object
G
(or a set of them) in
{\mathsf{K}}
, we write
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(G)
for the smallest thick subcategory, with respect to inclusion, containing
G
. The objects in
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(G)
are referred to as being
finitely built from
G
. This subcategory is also often denoted
\langle G\rangle
; see, for example,
[
20
]
. One can construct
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(G)
inductively as follows: set
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{K}}}^{1}(G)
to be the smallest full subcategory of
{\mathsf{K}}
containing
G
and closed under suspensions, finite sums and retracts. For
n>1
, let
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{K}}}^{n}(G)
be the smallest full subcategory of
{\mathsf{K}}
containing all objects
A
that fit into a triangle

A\to B\to C\to{\sf\Sigma}A\,

with
B
in
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{K}}}^{n-1}(G)
and
C
in
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{K}}}^{1}(G)
, and which is closed under suspensions, finite sums and retracts. One has a filtration

\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(G)=\bigcup_{n\geqslant 1}\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{K}}}^{n}(G)\,.

See
[
20
]
, and also
[
7
]
, for details. An object
G
is a
(classical) generator
for
{\mathsf{K}}
if
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(G)={\mathsf{K}}
; it is a
strong generator
provided that
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{K}}}^{d}(G)={\mathsf{K}}
for some integer
d
. Clearly, if
{\mathsf{K}}
admits a strong generator, any generator is a strong generator.

Suppose
{\mathsf{K}}
admits all coproducts. A triangulated subcategory of
{\mathsf{K}}
is
localizing
when it is closed under small coproducts. We write
\operatorname{Loc}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(G)
for the smallest localizing subcategory containing
G
, and speak of objects in this category as being
built
from
G
. An object
C
in
{\mathsf{K}}
is
compact
if
\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(C,-)
commutes with coproducts in
{\mathsf{K}}
, and
{\mathsf{K}}
is
compactly generated
if there exists a set of compact objects
G
such that
\operatorname{Loc}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(G)={\mathsf{K}}
. We write
{{\mathsf{K}}}^{\mathrm{c}}
for the compact objects in
{\mathsf{K}}
; this is a thick subcategory of
{\mathsf{K}}
. If
G
is a set of compact objects in
{\mathsf{K}}
, then

(1.1.1)

\operatorname{Loc}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(G)\cap{{\mathsf{K}}}^{\mathrm{c}}=\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(G)\,.

This is proved by Neeman
[
67
, Theorem 2.1]
; see also
[
51
, Proposition 3.4.15]
.

Many of the triangulated categories of interest in this manuscript come equipped with an action of another triangulated category, and our proofs exploit this additional structure. The relevant notions are recalled below. For details see
[
89
]
.

1.2
.

Let
({\mathsf{T}},\otimes,\mathbf{1})
be a tensor triangulated category that acts on
{\mathsf{K}}
(on the left), in the sense of Stevenson
[
89
, Section 3]
; see also
[
92
]
. We write
\odot
to denote this action. It is suggestive to think, and speak, of
{\mathsf{K}}
as a
{\mathsf{T}}
-module, as in
[
89
,
90
]
. Given an object
G
(or, as before, a set of them) in
{\mathsf{K}}
, we denote
\operatorname{thick}^{\odot}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(G)
the thick
{\mathsf{T}}
-submodule of
{\mathsf{K}}
generated by
G
, that is to say, the smallest thick subcategory of
{\mathsf{K}}
that is closed under the action of
{\mathsf{T}}
.

When
{\mathsf{T}}
and
{\mathsf{K}}
admit all coproducts, we consider also
\operatorname{Loc}^{\odot}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(G)
, the
localizing
{\mathsf{T}}
-submodule
of
{\mathsf{K}}
generated by
G
. If
\operatorname{Loc}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(U)={\mathsf{T}}
for a set of objects
U
, then

(1.2.1)

\operatorname{Loc}^{\odot}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(G)=\operatorname{Loc}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(A\odot B\mid A\in U\text{ and }B\in G)\,.

In particular, if
\operatorname{Loc}_{{\mathsf{T}}}(\mathbf{1})={\mathsf{T}}
, then
\operatorname{Loc}^{\odot}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(G)=\operatorname{Loc}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(G)
; see
[
89
, Lemma 3.13]
.

Suppose that
{\mathsf{T}}
is compactly generated. Then Brown representability yields that
{\mathsf{T}}
has an internal function object,
\mathrm{hom}(-,-)
, adjoint to
-\otimes-
; we assume that this is exact in each variable. An object
D
in
{\mathsf{T}}
is
rigid
if for each
E
in
{\mathsf{T}}
the natural map

\mathrm{hom}(D,\mathbf{1})\otimes E\longrightarrow\mathrm{hom}(D,E)

is an isomorphism. The category
{\mathsf{T}}
is
rigidly compactly generated
if it is compactly generated, and the set of compact objects and rigid objects coincide; see
[
89
, Defintion 4.1]
. In this case, the unit
\mathbf{1}
of
{\mathsf{T}}
is compact. When a rigidly compactly generated category
{\mathsf{T}}
acts on a compactly generated category
{\mathsf{K}}
, the action restricts to compact objects, that is to say,
{{\mathsf{T}}}^{\mathrm{c}}
acts on
{{\mathsf{K}}}^{\mathrm{c}}
; see
[
89
, Lemma 4.6]
.

These observations yield also a module version of
Eq.

1.1.1
, namely, when
{\mathsf{T}}
is rigidly compactly generated and
{\mathsf{K}}
is compactly generated, for any set
G
of compact objects in
{\mathsf{K}}
, one has

(1.2.2)

\operatorname{Loc}^{\odot}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(G)\cap{{\mathsf{K}}}^{\mathrm{c}}=\operatorname{thick}^{\odot}_{{\mathsf{K}}}(G)\,,

where the category on the right is the
{\mathsf{T}}^{\mathrm{c}}
-submodule of
{{\mathsf{K}}}^{\mathrm{c}}
generated by
G
. This follows from (
1.1.1
), given (
1.2.1
).

1.3
.

Let
X
be a noetherian scheme and
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
the derived category of quasi-coherent sheaves, viewed as a triangulated category with suspension functor
{\sf\Sigma}
. When
X
is affine, we identify
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
with
{\mathsf{D}}(\mathcal{O}_{X})
, the derived category of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules.

As a triangulated category
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
admits arbitrary coproducts, and is compactly generated.
The compacts objects in
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
are the bounded complexes of vector bundles; that is to say,
perfect
complexes. We write
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
for the full subcategory consisting of perfect complexes. Bondal and van den Bergh
[
20
, Theorem 3.1.1]
proved that
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
has a compact generator: a perfect complex
G
such that

{\mathsf{D}}(X)=\operatorname{Loc}_{{\mathsf{D}}(X)}(G)\,.

It follows that
\mathsf{Perf}\,X=\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(X)}(G)
; see
Eq.

1.1.1
.

Our main interest is in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
, the full subcategory of
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
consisting of bounded complexes with coherent cohomology, and in finding strong generators for this triangulated category. Aoki
[
3
]
proved that these exist whenever
X
is separated, quasi-excellent, and of finite Krull dimension.

1.4
.

The derived tensor product
-\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{X}-
endows
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
with a structure of a rigidly compactly generated tensor triangulated category. Moreover
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
is a tensor triangulated subcategory of
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
and the derived tensor product gives an action of
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
on
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
, in the sense of
1.2
. One has

\operatorname{Loc}^{\odot}_{{\mathsf{D}}(X)}(G)=\operatorname{Loc}^{\otimes}_{{\mathsf{D}}(X)}(G)

for any
G
in
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
, where the category on the left is the localizing
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
-submodule of
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
generated by
G
, and the category on the right is the localizing tensor ideal of
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
. Moreover, when
X
is affine, the unit of the tensor product-
\mathcal{O}_{X}
, generates
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
as a thick subcategory, so

\operatorname{Loc}^{\odot}_{{\mathsf{D}}(X)}(G)=\operatorname{Loc}_{{\mathsf{D}}(X)}(G)\,.

These observations will be used implicitly in the sequel.

The triangulated subcategory
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
of
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
is not closed under the tensor product, unless
X
is regular; see
1.11
. However, the tensor product on
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
restricts to an action of
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
on
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
, so we are in the context of
1.2
.

The next paragraph is a recap on some results involving support for objects in
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
; for details see
[
46
, Appendix A]
, or
[
25
]
. This builds on the theory of support for complexes over commutative noetherian rings developed by Foxby
[
34
]
.

1.5
.

Let
X
be a noetherian scheme as before, and fix
E
in
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
. Given
x\in X
we write
E_{x}
for the stalk of
E
at
x
, viewed as an object in the derived category of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{x}
. Let
k(x)
denote the residue field of
\mathcal{O}_{x}
. We identify
k(x)
with the coherent sheaf on
\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}_{x}
it defines, as well as with the pushforward of this coherent sheaf along the localizing immersion
\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{O}_{x}\to X
. The
support
of the complex
E
is the subset of
X
prescribed by

\operatorname{supp}_{X}E\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\{x\in X\mid\operatorname{H}(E\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{X}k(x))\neq 0\}\,.

This is sometimes referred to as the
small support
of
E
. When
E
is in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
its support coincides with the usual one:

\operatorname{supp}_{X}E=\{x\in X\mid\operatorname{H}(E_{x})\neq 0\}\,.

In particular, in this case it is a closed subset of
X
.

When
X=\operatorname{Spec}R
, with
R
a commutative noetherian ring and
E
is the sheafification
\widetilde{M}
of an
R
-complex
M
, this is the notion of support from
[
34
]
, denoted
\operatorname{supp}_{R}M
. When moreover
M
is in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
one has

\operatorname{supp}_{X}E=\operatorname{supp}_{R}M=V(\operatorname{ann}_{R}\operatorname{H}(M))\,.

Our interest in support stems from the following result, proved by Neeman
[
66
]
in the affine case, and extended to schemes by Alonso Tarrío, Jeremías López, and Souto Solario
[
2
]
: Given objects
E,G
in
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
, one has

(1.5.1)

E\in\operatorname{Loc}^{\otimes}_{{\mathsf{D}}(X)}(G)\Longleftrightarrow\operatorname{supp}_{X}E\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{X}G\,.

Using this result and
Eq.

1.1.1
one deduces that when
E,G
are in
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
one has

(1.5.2)

E\in\operatorname{thick}^{\otimes}_{\mathsf{Perf}\,X}(G)\Longleftrightarrow\operatorname{supp}_{X}E\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{X}G\,.

This result was proved by Thomason
[
95
]
. The affine case is a result of Hopkins
[
43
]
and Neeman
[
66
]
. From (
1.5.2
) it follows, for example, that when
E
in
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
has full support then it generates
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
as a module over itself.

1.6
.

Given
x\in X
let
K
be the Koszul complex on a finite generating set for the maximal ideal of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{x}
, and for each
E
in
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
set

{E}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}E_{x}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{x}}K\,,

viewed as an element in
{\mathsf{D}}(\mathcal{O}_{x})
. This notation is ambiguous, for it does not reflect the choice of a generating set for the maximal ideal. However, if
K^{\prime}
is the Koszul complex on a different generating set, then

\operatorname{thick}(K)=\operatorname{thick}(K^{\prime})
as subcategories of
{\mathsf{D}}(\mathcal{O}_{x})
; this follows from (
1.5.2
), but can also be checked directly, as is done in
[
44
, Lemma 6.0.9]
. Consequently
\operatorname{thick}({E}(x))
is well-defined, and this is what is relevant in the statement below, and also later on. Observe that, since
K
is a perfect
\mathcal{O}_{x}
-complex, when
E
is in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
, the complex
{E}(x)
is in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{O}_{x})
.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X
be a noetherian scheme. Fix objects
E,G
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
, viewed as a
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
-module. If
{E}(x)
is in
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(\mathcal{O}_{x})}(G_{x})
for each
x\in X
, then
E
is in
\operatorname{thick}^{\odot}_{{\mathsf{D}}(X)}(G)
.

This result is essentially contained in the work of Stevenson
[
91
]
. Further below, we sketch a proof, for completeness. Observe that the converse also holds: if
E
is in the
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
-submodule generated by
G
, then since both
(-)_{x}
and
-\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{x}}K
are exact,
{E}(x)
is generated by
{G}(x)
, and hence also by
G_{x}
, as
\mathcal{O}_{x}
finitely builds
K
.

1.8
.

Let
{\mathsf{K}}({\mathsf{Inj}}\,X)
denote the homotopy category of quasi-coherent injective sheaves on
X
. This is a compactly generated triangulated category and restricting the natural localization functor

\boldsymbol{q}\colon{\mathsf{K}}({\mathsf{Inj}}\,X)\longrightarrow{\mathsf{D}}(X)

to the subcategory of compact objects induces an equivalence

(1.8.1)

{\mathsf{K}}({\mathsf{Inj}}\,X)^{\mathsf{c}}\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)\,.

See
[
50
, Theorem 1.1]
, where these results are proved when
X
is separated, and
[
97
, Appendix B]
for the general case.

The tensor triangulated category
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
is rigidly compactly generated and acts on
{\mathsf{K}}({\mathsf{Inj}}\,X)
, in the sense of
1.2
. One can view this action as the ind-completion of the action of
{\mathsf{Perf}}(X)
on
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
. Here is a concrete description of this action, following
[
91
, Section 3]
.

Given a flat
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
F
and an injective
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
I
, the
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-module
F\otimes_{X}I
is injective since
X
is noetherian. Thus, the tensor product induces an action of the homotopy category of complexes of flat modules on
{\mathsf{K}}({\mathsf{Inj}}\,X)
. When
F
is an acyclic complex of flat modules with flat syzygies (also known as a pure acyclic complex), and
I
is a complex of injectives,
F\otimes_{X}I
is contractible. Thus the said action factors through the Verdier quotient of the homotopy category of flats by the subcategory of pure acyclic complexes. This category, introduced by Murfet
[
65
,
68
]
, is usually denoted
{\mathsf{N}}(X)
and called the Neeman category of
X
. When
X
is affine this is equivalent to the homotopy category of projective modules. A complex
F
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules is
K
-flat
if it consists of flat
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules and
F\otimes_{X}-
preserves quasi-isomorphisms. Taking
K
-flat resolutions gives a fully faithful embedding of
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
into
{\mathsf{N}}(X)
, compatible with tensor products and coproducts. Via this embedding one gets an action of
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
on
{\mathsf{K}}({\mathsf{Inj}}\,X)
.

In what follows, we write
\operatorname{Loc}^{\odot}_{{\mathsf{K}}(X)}(G)
, with
{\mathsf{K}}(X)
in the subscript rather than
{\mathsf{K}}({\mathsf{Inj}}\,X)
, for the localizing submodule of
{\mathsf{K}}({\mathsf{Inj}}\,X)
generated by an object
G
.

Fix
x\in X
and let
\varGamma_{x}
denote the exact functor from
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
to
{\mathsf{D}}(\mathcal{O}_{x})
defined by the assignment

E\longrightarrow\mathbf{R}\varGamma_{\overline{\{x\}}}(E_{x})\,.

See
[
89
, Definition 5.3]
. The local-to-global principle
[
89
, Theorem 6.9]
yields that given
E,G
in
{\mathsf{K}}({\mathsf{Inj}}\,X)
, viewed with the
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
-action, one has

(1.8.2)

E\in\operatorname{Loc}^{\odot}_{{\mathsf{K}}(X)}(G)\Longleftrightarrow\varGamma_{x}E\in\operatorname{Loc}_{{\mathsf{K}}(\mathcal{O}_{x})}(\varGamma_{x}G)\quad\text{for each $x\in X$}.

The condition on the right is equivalent to
\varGamma_{x}E
being in the localizing subcategory generated by
G_{x}
, for the functor
\varGamma_{x}(-)
commutes with coproducts. For another version of this local-to-global principle see
[
14
, Lemma 3.2]
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.7
.

The basic idea is to apply the local-to-global principle. We use the equivalence
Eq.

1.8.1
and identify
E
and
G
with their images in
{\mathsf{K}}({\mathsf{Inj}}\,X)
. The desired conclusion is that
E
is in the localizing
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
-submodule of
{\mathsf{K}}({\mathsf{Inj}}\,X)
generated by
G
; the statement about thick submodules follows by
Eq.

1.2.2
. For any
x
in
X
one has that

\operatorname{Loc}_{{\mathsf{K}}(\mathcal{O}_{x})}({E}(x))=\operatorname{Loc}_{{\mathsf{K}}(\mathcal{O}_{x})}(\varGamma_{x}E)\,.

This holds because
{\mathcal{O}}(x)
and
\varGamma_{x}\mathcal{O}_{x}
build each other in
{\mathsf{D}}(\mathcal{O}_{x})
; indeed both objects in question are supported on
\{x\}
, so
Eq.

1.5.1
applies. Thanks to the tensor action of
{\mathsf{D}}(\mathcal{O}_{x})
on
{\mathsf{K}}({\mathsf{Inj}}\,\mathcal{O}_{x})
the equality above holds. One can also give a direct, elementary, proof; see, for example,
[
14
, Proposition 2.11]
. Given this observation, the hypothesis implies

\varGamma_{x}E\in\operatorname{Loc}_{{\mathsf{K}}(\mathcal{O}_{x})}(G_{x})\quad\text{for each }x\in X.

At this point we can invoke the local-to-global principle (
1.8.2
) to deduce that
E
is in the
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
-submodule of
{\mathsf{K}}({\mathsf{Inj}}\,X)
generated by
G
, as desired.
∎

1.9
.

Fix
x\in X
, set
R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{O}_{x}
and
k\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}k(x)
. We write
\varGamma_{x}{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
for the subcategory of
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
consisting of
R
-complexes
M
supported at the closed point
x
of
\operatorname{Spec}R
; equivalently, the
R
-module
\operatorname{H}(M)
has finite length. This subcategory contains
{E}(x)
for all
E
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
. The residue field
k(x)
of
R
generates
\varGamma_{x}{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
; see, for example,
[
27
]
. However
k(x)
is a strong generator for this category if and only if
R
is artinian; see, for instance,
[
80
]
.

Given these observations the result below is a consequence of
Theorem

1.7
.

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
X
be a noetherian scheme and fix
G
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
. If for each
x\in X
, the complex
G_{x}
finitely builds
k(x)
,
then
G
generates
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
as a
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
-module. ∎

One reason for our interest in finding generators of derived categories is that they are test objects for finiteness of various homological invariants. A sample result along these lines is provided in
Lemma

1.12
; see also
[
27
]
. To illustrate this point, we recall a classical characterization of regularity.

1.11
.

Let
X
be a noetherian scheme. Clearly
\mathsf{Perf}\,X\subseteq{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
. Equality holds precisely when
X
is regular, that is to say, the local rings
\mathcal{O}_{x}
are regular for
x
in
X
; equivalently, at each closed point
x
in
X
. This characterization of regularity is due to Auslander, Buchsbaum, and Serre, given the following observation: A complex
E
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
is perfect if and only if
E_{x}
is perfect in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}\mathcal{O}_{x})
for each
x\in X
; equivalently, at each closed point
x\in X
. In the affine case, which implies the global version, this is due to Bass and Murthy
[
13
]
; see also
[
10
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Here is a related result: Any compact generator for
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
generates
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
, as a thick subcategory. However
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
has a strong generator if and only if
X
is regular and of finite Krull dimension; see
[
80
, Proposition 7.25]
.

Lemma 1.12
.

Let
X
be a noetherian scheme and
G
a generator for
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
. An object
E
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
is perfect if and only if
\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{i}(E,G)=0
for
i\gg 0
. In particular, if
\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{i}(G,G)=0
for all
i\gg 0
, then
X
is regular.

Proof.

The second assertion follows immediately from the first, where the only if direction is clear. Suppose
\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{i}(E,G)=0
for
i\gg 0
. Since
G
generates the bounded derived category, for each closed point
x\in X
, the coherent sheaf
k(x)
is finitely built from
G
in
{\mathsf{D}}(X)
, hence the hypothesis yields

\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{\mathcal{O}_{x}}(E_{x},k(x))\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{X}(E,k(x))=0\quad\text{for $i\gg 0$.}

It follows that the
\mathcal{O}_{x}
-module
E_{x}
is perfect. Thus
E
is perfect; see
1.11
.
∎

2.
Local rings

In this section we establish a nilpotence-type result concerning the Frobenius endomorphism on a local ring. This is one of the key inputs into our arguments, in the next section concerning generators for the bounded derived category of a scheme over a field of positive characteristic.

Let
R
be a noetherian ring of prime characteristic
p
; that is to say,
p
is a prime number and
R
contains the field
\mathbb{F}_{p}
as a subring. Let

F\colon R\to R\quad\text{given by}\quad r\mapsto r^{p}

be its Frobenius endomorphism. It is well-known that the action induced by the Frobenius on various homology modules is often trivial; this springs from the fact that the Frobenius endomorphism on any simplicial commutative ring induces the trivial map in homology; see
[
74
]
and also
[
17
, Section 11]
. The gist of the result below is that, on noetherian local rings, the Frobenius endomorphism is even essentially nilpotent; see also
Proposition

2.7
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
R
be a noetherian local ring of prime characteristic
p
. For any
R
-complex
M
and natural number
e>\log_{p}(\operatorname{codepth}R)
there is an isomorphism

F^{e}_{*}(K^{M})\simeq\operatorname{H}(F^{e}_{*}(K^{M}))\quad\text{in ${\mathsf{D}}(R)$.}

In particular, the residue field
k
is in
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}(F^{e}_{*}(K^{M}))
whenever
\operatorname{H}(K^{M})\neq 0
.

In the statement,
K^{M}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}K\otimes_{R}M
where
K
is the Koszul complex on minimal generating set for the maximal ideal of
R
, and
F^{e}_{*}
is the restriction of scalars functor along
F^{e}
. The
codepth
of
R
is the non-negative integer

\operatorname{codepth}R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{edim}R-0ptR=\sup\{i\mid\operatorname{H}_{i}(K^{R})\neq 0\}\,.

The equality on the right is by the depth sensitivity of the Koszul complex. Let
Q\twoheadrightarrow R
be a Cohen presentation, meaning that
Q
is a regular local ring and the map is surjective. One has

(2.1.1)

\operatorname{codepth}R\leq\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{Q}R\,,

and equality holds if (and only if)
\operatorname{edim}Q=\operatorname{edim}R
, that is to say, when
Q\twoheadrightarrow R
is a minimal Cohen presentation.
This holds because of the formula of Auslander and Buchsbaum
[
23
, Theorem 1.3.3]
.

Since complete local rings admit Cohen presentations, this remark can be applied after completing
R
at its maximal ideal, for the codepth of
R
coincides with that of its completion; indeed, both the embedding dimension and the depth remain unchanged in this process.

2.2
.

Compare the statement of
Theorem

2.1
with
[
9
, Theorem 6.2.2]
that gives the same conclusion but where the lower bound for
e
is the spread of
R
. This number can be computed in terms of the graded Betti-numbers of
\mathrm{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
, the associated graded ring of
R
at its maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, over the symmetric algebra on
\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}
, and is related to the regularity of
\mathrm{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)
. The spread is harder to control; for instance, when
R
is a (not necessarily local) noetherian ring essentially of finite type, we do not know whether there is a global bound on the spread of
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
as
\mathfrak{p}
varies over the prime ideals in
R
. Such a bound is clear for the codepth and this fact is important in the sequel.

Nevertheless the proof of
Theorem

2.1
is similar to that of
[
9
, Theorem 6.2.2]
, except that, to exploit the special properties of the Frobenius map we work in the category of simplicial algebras and simplicial modules over them. The pertinent constructions and results are recalled below; see
[
76
,
84
]
for proofs.

2.3
.

Given a simplicial ring
\mathcal{A}
we write
\mathrm{Simp}(\mathcal{A})
for the category of simplicial
\mathcal{A}
-modules, with the usual model structure, and
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathcal{A})
for the corresponding homotopy category.

Viewing a commutative ring
R
as a simplicial ring in the standard way, by the Dold-Kan theorem one gets an equivalence of categories

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Simp}(R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Ch}_{\geqslant 0}(R)\,,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varGamma}

where
N(-)
is the normalization functor. These are compatible with natural model structures on the source and target. In particular,
N
induces an equivalence on homotopy categories:

N\colon\mathrm{Ho}(R)\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }{\mathsf{D}}_{\geqslant 0}(R)\,.

Here
{\mathsf{D}}_{\geqslant 0}(R)
is the derived category on non-negatively graded chain complexes, which can be identified with the full-subcategory of
{\mathsf{D}}(R)
consisting of
R
-complexes with homology concentrated in non-negative degrees. It is not a triangulated subcategory, for it is not closed under negative suspensions.

Thanks to the equivalence above, one can work with simplicial
R
-modules, at least for objects in
{\mathsf{D}}_{\geqslant 0}(R)
.

For a general simplicial ring
\mathcal{A}
, its normalization
N(\mathcal{A})
is a graded-commutative differential graded algebra, concentrated in non-negative degrees, and the Dold-Kan functor induces an equivalence between
\mathrm{Ho}({\mathcal{A}})
and
{\mathsf{D}}_{\geqslant 0}(N(\mathcal{A}))
, the derived category of differential graded
N(\mathcal{A})
-modules with homology concentrated in non-negative degrees. This subsumes the case
\mathcal{A}=R
discussed above.

2.4
.

Let
R
be a ring. An
R
-complex
M
is said to be
formal
if there is an isomorphism
M\simeq\operatorname{H}(M)
in
{\mathsf{D}}(R)
. Given the Dold-Kan equivalence, we say a simplicial
R
-module is formal if its normalization is formal. For instance, when
k
is a field, any simplicial
k
-vector space is formal.

2.5
.

Given a simplicial ring
\mathcal{A}
and an integer
n\geq 0
, there is a simplicial ring
\mathcal{B}
and a map of simplicial rings
\varphi\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
with the following properties:

(1)

\operatorname{H}_{i}(\mathcal{B})=0
for
i\geq n+1
;

(2)

\operatorname{H}_{i}(\varphi)
is bijective for
i\leq n
.

The map can be obtained by a process of killing the homology in
\mathcal{A}
in degree
n+1
and higher; see also the discussion on
[
96
, pp. 162]
for the construction of
\varphi
. This is part of the data of a Postnikov tower for
\mathcal{A}
.

2.6
.

Given a map
{\varphi}\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
of simplicial rings, one has an adjoint pair of functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Ho}(\mathcal{A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Ho}(\mathcal{B})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\varphi}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\varphi}_{*}}

where
{\varphi}_{*}
is restriction along
{\varphi}
, and
{\varphi}^{*}
is induced by
\mathcal{B}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}-
. When
{\varphi}
is an equivalence,
{\varphi}^{*}
and
{\varphi}_{*}
are inverse equivalences of categories. The normalization
N(\mathcal{A})
is a differential graded algebra, and the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Ho}(\mathcal{A})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Ho}(\mathcal{B})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{D}}_{\geqslant 0}(N(\mathcal{A}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathsf{D}}_{\geqslant 0}(N(\mathcal{B}))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\varphi}_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\varphi}_{*}}

commutes up to isomorphism of functors. This observation is used in recasting the statement in
Theorem

2.1
in terms of simplicial algebras and modules.

Proof of
Theorem

2.1
.

One has that
\mathfrak{m}\cdot\operatorname{H}(K^{M})=0
, where
\mathfrak{m}
is the maximal ideal of
R
. Thus, with
\widehat{R}
the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic completion of
R
, the map

K^{M}\longrightarrow\widehat{R}\otimes_{R}K^{M}\cong K^{\widehat{R}\otimes M}

induced by the natural map
R\to\widehat{R}
, is a quasi-isomorphism. Thus passing to
\widehat{R}
, we can assume
R
is complete in the
\mathfrak{m}
-adic topology. Let
Q\to R
be a minimal Cohen presentation.

We view
R
as a simplicial
Q
-algebra in the usual way, and take a simplicial
R
-algebra model for
K^{R}
. Let
\rho\colon Q\{X\}\xrightarrow{\simeq}R
and
Q\{Y\}\xrightarrow{\simeq}k
be simplicial free resolutions of
R
and
k
, respectively, as
Q
-algebras. Then

Q\{X,Y\}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}Q\{X\}\otimes_{Q}Q\{Y\}

is a simplicial free resolution of
K^{R}
over
Q\{X\}
. Let
F^{e}\colon Q\{X\}\to Q\{X\}
be the Frobenius map applied degreewise. At this point we have constructed the top two squares of the following commutative diagram of simplicial
Q
-algebras:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K^{R}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q\{X\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q\{X\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q\{X,Y\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k\{X\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{e}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{e}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi}

The equivalence
Q\{X,Y\}\xrightarrow{\simeq}k\{X\}
is obtained by applying
Q\{X\}\otimes_{Q}-
to the equivalence
Q\{Y\}\xrightarrow{\simeq}k
. Next we construct the lower part of the diagram.

Let
J
be the kernel of the canonical augmentation
k\{X\}\to k
; this is a simplicial ideal in
k\{X\}
and satisfies
\operatorname{H}_{i}(J)=0
for
i\leq 0
. Since
k\{X\}
is free, as a simplicial
k
-algebra, for each integer
n\geq 0
one has
\operatorname{H}_{i}(J^{n+1})=0
for
i\leq n
, by Quillen’s theorem
[
75

. In particular the surjection
k\{X\}\to k\{X\}/J^{n+1}
is bijective in homology in degrees
\leq n
. Set
c\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\operatorname{codepth}R
;
thus

\operatorname{H}_{i}(k\{X\})\cong\operatorname{H}_{i}(K^{R})=0\quad\text{for $i>c$.}

Let
k\{X\}/J^{c+1}\to\mathcal{A}
be a map of simplicial rings such that the induced map in homology is bijective in degrees
\leq c
and
\operatorname{H}_{i}(\mathcal{A})=0
for
i\geq c+1
; see
2.5
. The map
\alpha
in the diagram is the composition of maps

k\{X\}\longrightarrow k\{X\}/J^{c+1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}

By construction
\operatorname{H}(\alpha)\colon\operatorname{H}(k\{X\})\to\operatorname{H}(\mathcal{A})
is an isomorphism; that is to say,
\alpha
is an equivalence. The map

is defined to ensure that the upper triangle commutes.
Let

be the canonical augmentation and set

, so

factors through
\varepsilon
yielding the map

in the diagram. This completes the construction of the commutative diagram. Observe that

, and hence also
\mathcal{A}
is a simplicial

-algebra, and that the map
\Psi\colon k\to\mathcal{A}
is the composition of
F^{e}\colon k\to k
with the structure map

.

At this point, we complete the proof under the additional assumption that the

-complex

satisfies

for

, for we can then work entirely in the simplicial context, given the Dold-Kan equivalence between non-negatively graded

-complexes and simplicial

-modules; see

and

. To tackle the case of a general

Since

is a field, any simplicial

-module, is formal, in the sense of

. Thus, for any simplicial

-module

, the simplicial

-module

is formal. It thus follows from the commutative diagram above that for any simplicial

-module

, the simplicial

-module

is formal. This applies in particular to the simplicial model for

As to the last part, if

, has

∎

The essence of the argument above is the existence of the commutative diagram of simplicial algebras, and this can be paraphrased as follows.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let

be a noetherian local ring of prime characteristic

. For any natural number

factors through the natural surjection
R\to k
to the residue field. ∎

Given

2.1
, and more so the result above, it follows that any property of the ring that can be characterized in terms of some homological property of

of

is the prototypical such result, where

3.13
.

When

and so also on

. However we get no concrete bounds from this source. We would like to have such a bound, at least for

below. To begin with, we record the following observation that means that for a local ring the codepth in the sense above is the same as the one introduced earlier.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any noetherian local ring
R
, one has
\operatorname{codepth}R\geq\operatorname{codepth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}
for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in

.

Proof.

The desired statement is clear when

has a Cohen presentation

, which one can arrange to be minimal, for then (

) yields (in)equalities

\operatorname{codepth}R=\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{Q}R\geq\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{Q_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\geq\operatorname{codepth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\,,

which is the desired inequality.

We reduce to this case by passing to the completion,
\widehat{R}
, of

at its maximal ideal. Pick a prime ideal, say
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
in
\operatorname{Spec}\widehat{R}
lying over
\mathfrak{p}
and minimal with that property. One then has

\operatorname{edim}(\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}})\geq\operatorname{edim}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\quad\text{and}\quad 0pt(\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}})=0ptR_{\mathfrak{p}}\,,

where the inequality holds by, for example,
[

and the equality by
[

. This gives the last of the following inequalities

\operatorname{codepth}R=\operatorname{codepth}\widehat{R}\geq\operatorname{codepth}(\widehat{R}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}})\geq\operatorname{codepth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\,.

The equality is clear, whereas the first inequality is by the discussion in the previous paragraph, for

has a Cohen presentation.
∎

In the statement below, for any finitely generated

-module

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

(1)

When

is a quotient of a regular ring

\operatorname{codepth}R\leq\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{Q}R<\infty\,.

(2)

When

-finite one has

\operatorname{edim}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\leq\beta^{R}(F_{*}R)\quad\text{for each $\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R$.}

In particular,
\operatorname{codepth}R<\infty
.

Proof.

(1) When
R
is a quotient of a regular ring

one has inequalities

\operatorname{codepth}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\leq\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{Q_{\mathfrak{p}\cap Q}}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\leq\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{Q}R<\infty\,,

for each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of

, where the first one is by (

), and the finiteness of

is by

. Thus

is finite, and bounded above by

.

(2) We can deduce this from (1) for any
F
-finite ring is a quotient of an

-finite regular ring, as was proved by Gabber

. Here is a direct argument, based on the proof of
[

, Proposition 1.1]
.

Since the number of generators does not go up under localization, the desired result is that when
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a noetherian local ring
\beta^{R}(F_{*}R)\geq\operatorname{edim}R
. This follows from the computation:

\displaystyle\beta^{R}(F_{*}R)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{rank}_{k}(k\otimes_{R}F_{*}R)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{rank}_{k}(F_{*}(R/\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}))

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{rank}_{k}(F_{*}k)\operatorname{rank}_{k}(R/\mathfrak{m}^{[p]})

\displaystyle\geq\operatorname{rank}_{k}(\mathfrak{m}/\mathfrak{m}^{2})\,.

The (in)equalities are all standard.
∎

The preceding result gives one family of schemes whose codepth is finite. This family includes schemes that may have infinite Krull dimension.

Corollary 3.3

If

For the present, the more pertinent result is the one below.

If

Since

These considerations are pertinent to the result below; it contains

Theorem 3.5

Let

-finite noetherian scheme over a field of prime characteristic

By

it suffices to check that

If

Suppose

in

Theorem 3.6

Let

be a noetherian scheme over a field of prime characteristic

By the local-to-global principle

, and

Next we discuss alternative formulations of the preceding results, in terms of strong generators of

Lemma 3.8
.

Let

\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(X)}(U)=\mathsf{Perf}\,X

\operatorname{thick}^{\odot}_{{\mathsf{D}}(X)}(F^{e}_{*}G)=\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(X)}(F^{e}_{*}(E\otimes G)\mid E\in U)\,.

It suffices to verify this when

Let

denote pullback along

Now it remains to observe that

\displaystyle=\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(X)}(F_{*}(F^{*}(E)\otimes G)\mid E\in U)

Corollary 3.13
.

Let

A key observation observation is that given finite maps of schemes

Given this observation it follows that if

Relation to tilting

The more stringent requirement that

is a tilting generator has been studied in
[
56
,
77
,
81
,
82
]
. The class of
F
-thick schemes is strictly larger. Indeed for toric varieties there are obstructions to the existence of full exceptional collections, which prevents the tilting condition from being satisfied; see
[
28
, Theorem 1.3]
, as well as
[
42
,
64
]
. The affine situation is simpler.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
R
be a commutative noetherian ring. If
G
is a tilting generator for
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
, then
G\cong{\sf\Sigma}^{s}P
, for some finitely generated, faithful, projective
R
-module
P
and integer
s
; moreover, the ring
R
is regular. Therefore
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
has a tilting generator if and only if
R
is regular.

Proof.

Lemma

1.12
implies
R
is regular, and hence also that
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}G<\infty
. Then Nakayama’s Lemma yields that

\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{g}(G,G)\neq 0\text{ when }g=\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}G-\inf\operatorname{H}_{*}(G)\,.

This implies the desired result.
∎

We record some examples of
F
-thick schemes.

Example 4.3
.

Let
X\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
denote projective
n
-space over
k
. In this case
X
is
F
-thick and, moreover,
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is a tilting generator whenever
p^{e}>n.

Indeed,
[
78
, Lemma 2.1]
yields, for each
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, a decomposition

F_{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(l))\cong\bigoplus_{i\geq-l/p}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-i)^{\oplus\alpha(i,l)}\,,

where
\alpha(i,l)
is the number of monomials of degree
l+ip
that are not divisible by any
p^{\text{th}}
-power of a variable. Also, by
[
15
]
,

G=\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(-1)\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(-n)

generates
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
. So combining these two facts yields that
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
generates
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
whenever
p^{e}>n
. Also, as
\operatorname{Ext}_{X}^{t}(G,G)=0
for
t>0
, it follows that
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is a tilting generator for
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
.

Fix
p^{e}\leq n
. For each

in
\mathbb{Z}
one has

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathsf{D}}(X)}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(1),{\sf\Sigma}^{l}F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathsf{D}}(X)}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(p^{e}),{\sf\Sigma}^{l}\mathcal{O}_{X})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{H}^{l}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-p^{e}))

\displaystyle=0\,.

Hence
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
does not generate
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
.

Example 4.4
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a semisimple algebraic group over an algebraically closed field of prime characteristic

. If

where

is the Coxeter number of

, then

is a

{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}{\mathbf{G}})
. Furthermore, if

\mathbf{P}
is a parabolic subgroup of

and

for
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}{\mathbf{G}/\mathbf{P}})

(or

\mathbf{B}_{2}

is a

[

, Corollary 3.2]
.

Example 4.5
.

The blowup

of

at four points in general position is

-thick. By
[

, Corollary 5.4 & Proposition 6.4]

contains as direct summands bundles that form a full strong exceptional collection on

.

Example 4.6
.

Let
\mathbb{G}=G(2,n)
be the Grassmanian over an algebraically closed field of prime characteristic

[

, Corollary 16.11]

F_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{G}}
contains a full exceptional collection as direct summands whenever

Example 4.7
.

If
X
is a Severi-Brauer variety over a perfect field

of prime characteristic, then it is

-thick. We can apply
[

, Corollary 5.1.5]
to obtain a finite Galois extension

such that

for some integer

]

where

.

Example 4.8
.

If

Example 4.9

In 2006, Bondal

Nonsingular curves

Here we focus on nonsingular projective curves, and prove that the only ones which are
F
-thick are those of genus zero, and in this case,
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is a tilting generator; see
Theorem

4.10
. This is in sharp contrast with the affine case; see
Corollary

3.11
. First, a bit of terminology.

Fix a nonsingular projective curve
X
, and let
E
denote a vector bundle on
X
of rank
r
. The slope of
E
is
\mu(E)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{\deg(E)}{r}
where
\deg(E)
is its degree. We say
E
is semistable (respectively, stable) if for all subbundles of positive rank
G\subseteq E
defined over
k
, one has
\mu(G)\leq\mu(E)
(respectively,
\mu(G)<\mu(E)
).

Theorem 4.10
.

A nonsingular projective curve over a field of prime characteristic is
F
-thick if and only if its genus is zero.

Proof.

The genus zero case is essentially
Example

4.3
.

Next, suppose that
X
is an
F
-thick projective curve, and take
e>0
so that
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
generates
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
.
If
X
has nonzero genus, then
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is semistable by
[
93
, Corollary 4.4]
, and so
[
18
, Theorem 1]
ensures there exist a nontrivial vector bundle
E
such that

\operatorname{H}^{i}(X,E\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{X}F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X})=0\quad\text{for all $i$.}

Denote by
E^{\vee}
the dual vector bundle of
E
. From Serre duality, one gets

\operatorname{H}^{i}(X,E\otimes^{\operatorname{L}}_{X}F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X})=\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{X}(E^{\vee},F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X})=0\,,

which is a contradiction for
E^{\vee}
is nontrivial and
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
generates
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
. Therefore, the genus of
X
must be zero.
∎

4.11
.

A direct sum decomposition of
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is known when
X
is an elliptic curve (that is, the genus of
X
is one). Namely, if
X
is ordinary, then
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
splits into
p^{e}
non-isomorphic
p^{e}
-torsion line bundles; cf.
[
30
, Theorem 5.5]
. On the other hand, when
X
is supersingular,
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is isomorphic to Atiyah’s indecomposable vector bundle
\mathcal{F}_{p^{e}}
of degree zero and rank
p^{e}
; see
[
4
]
.

For smooth projective curves
X
of genus
g\geq 2
, it follows from
[
55
, Proposition 1.2]
that the vector bundle
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is stable and hence indecomposable.

Nonexamples and obstructions

Here is family of nonsingular projective varieties that are not
F
-thick.

Theorem 4.12
.

If
\pi\colon X\to C
is a nonsingular projective ruled surface with
C
a nonsingular projective curve with positive genus over an algebraically closed field of prime characteristic, then
X
is not
F
-thick.

Proof.

Assume the contrary that
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is a generator for
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}{X})
for some
e>0
. We claim that
C
is
F
-thick, contradicting
Theorem

4.10
.

To this end, the base field being algebraically closed allows us to use
[
71
, Theorem 2.6]
to obtain the following semiorthogonal decomposition

{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}{X})=\langle\pi^{*}{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}{C}),\mathcal{B}\rangle\,.

The corresponding projection

(4.12.1)

{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}{X})\to{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}{C})

induces the isomorphism
\pi_{*}F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}\cong F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{C}
since
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{C}
as coherent
\mathcal{O}_{C}
-modules, and all higher derived pushforwards of
\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
vanish. Finally, as
Eq.

4.12.1
is essentially surjective it follows that
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{C}
is a generator for
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}{C})
, yielding the desired contradiction.
∎

The next result identifies obstructions to the
F
-thick property.

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over an
F
-finite field. If there exists a nonzero object
E
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}{X})
such that
F^{e,*}E
is acyclic for some positive integer
e
, then
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
cannot generate
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}{X})
.

Proof.

Standard isomorphisms using hom-tensor adjunction, the projection formula and Serre duality yield

0=\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathsf{D}}(X)}(\mathcal{O}_{X},{\sf\Sigma}^{n}F^{e,*}E)\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{d-n}_{X}(F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X},\mathcal{H}\kern-1.0pto\kern-1.0ptm\kern-1.0pt(E,\omega_{X}))^{\vee}

for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
d
is the dimension of
X
. From these isomorphisms and the assumption on
F^{e,*}E
, we conclude that
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
does not generate
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
.
∎

Let
X
be a smooth projective
F
-thick variety. From
Proposition

4.13
, it follows that for every
E
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
and
e\gg 0
, the complex
F^{e,*}E
is not acyclic. In particular, we have the following constraint on the cohomology of line bundles.

Corollary 4.14
.

If
X
is
F
-thick, then there is a nonnegative integer
n
such that
\operatorname{H}^{*}(X,L^{n})\neq 0
for any line bundle
L
.

Proof.

Since
F^{e,*}L=L^{p^{e}}
, we can apply Proposition
4.13
.
∎

Corollary 4.15
.

Abelian varieties over algebraically closed fields of prime characteristic are not
F
-thick.

Proof.

Let
X
be an abelian variety and pick
L\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(X)
that is not
p
-torsion. As
\mathcal{O}_{X}\neq L^{p^{e}}
, we have
\operatorname{H}^{*}(X,F^{e,*}L)=0
, so Corollary
4.14
applies.
∎

Projective complete intersection varieties

All varieties considered in the rest of this section are assumed to be defined over a fixed
F
-finite field
k
of characteristic
p
. For a projective complete intersection variety
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{k}^{n}
of degree
d
, as is well-known, there a semiorthogonal decomposition

(4.15.1)

{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)=\langle\mathcal{A}_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X}(d-n),\ldots,\mathcal{O}_{X}\rangle\,;

\mathcal{A}_{X}
is often referred to as the Kuznetsov component of
Eq.

4.15.1
due to the connection Kuznetsov
[
54
]
drew between the structure of
\mathcal{A}_{X}
and the rationality of
X
. The component was also studied by Orlov
[
72
, Theorem 2.3]
where he related it to the category of graded singularities associated to
X
. A scheme
X
is said to be
globally
F
-split
if
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is a summand of
F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}_{k}
be a projective complete intersection of degree at most
n
and such
X
is globally
F
-split. Then
X
is
F
-thick if and only if
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
generates the Kuznetsov component of
X
for some
e>0
.

Contrast this with
Theorem

6.3
where we prove
F_{*}G
generates
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
for some perfect complex
G
.
Theorem

4.16
follows from the following stronger result, describing
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
for such
X
. We thank Devlin Mallory for suggesting this formulation, and for supplying part of the proof.

Theorem 4.17
.

Let
X
be as in
Theorem

4.16
and let
d
be its degree. If
p^{e}>n-d
then there is a direct sum decomposition

F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}=M\oplus\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(i)^{a_{i}}\,,

where
M
has no twists of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
as direct summands, and

a_{i}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $i=0$}\\
\geq 1&\text{if $d-n\leq i\leq-1$}\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Moreover, no other line bundles show up in the direct sum decomposition of
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
when
\dim X\geq 3
.

4.18
.

Any
X
as in
Theorem

4.17
is Fano. If
X
is smooth it is well-known that it is
F
-split for
p\gg 0
. More generally for any rational singularity
\tilde{X}
with a model over a finitely-generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra, its reduction mod
p
is
F
-split for
p\gg 0
; see
[
86
]
.

We fix the following notation for the rest of the section. Let
S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}k[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
where each
x_{i}
has degree one, and write
\mathfrak{m}
for the homogeneous maximal ideal
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})
of
S
. Fix
I
a homogeneous ideal of
S
and set
R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}S/I
.

4.19
.

Write
I^{[p^{e}]}
for the ideal generated by all
r^{p^{e}}
where
r\in I
. The element
F^{e}_{*}r
of
F^{e}_{*}R
refers to the elements
r\in R
viewed in
F^{e}_{*}R
. We record a variation on Fedder’s criterion: For a homogeneous element
s
in
S
, with image
r
in
R
, and for any positive integer

, the inclusion of the cyclic
R
-submodule of
F_{*}^{e}R
generated by
F_{*}^{e}(r)
splits if and only if
s(I^{[p^{e}]}:I)\not\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}
.
The argument is similar that in
[
33
, Proposition 1.7]
; see also
[
37
]
.

Proposition 4.20
.

As graded
R
-modules
R(-j)
is a direct summand of
F_{*}^{e}R
if and only if there exists
s\in S_{p^{e}j}
such that

s(I^{[p^{e}]}:I)\not\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}\,.

Proof.

We consider the
\frac{1}{p^{e}}\mathbb{Z}
-graded structure on
F^{e}_{*}R
: for a homogeneous
r\in R
, the corresponding element in
F^{e}_{*}R
has degree
\frac{\text{deg}(r)}{p^{e}}
. The direct sum of all
\mathbb{Z}
-graded components of
F_{*}^{e}R
is denoted
(F_{\ast}^{e}R)_{\mathbb{Z}}
. It can be shown that a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
R
-module
M
is a direct summand of
(F_{\ast}^{e}R)_{\mathbb{Z}}
if and only if it is a direct summand of
F_{\ast}^{e}R
.

Suppose that
R(-j)
is a direct summand of
(F_{*}^{e}R)_{\mathbb{Z}}
as a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
R
-module, and let
\phi\colon R(-j)\to(F_{*}^{e}R)_{\mathbb{Z}}
be a splitting. The element
r\in R
where
F^{e}_{*}r=\phi(1)
has degree
p^{e}j
and from
4.19
any lift
s
of

satisfies
s(I^{[p^{e}]}:I)\not\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}
.

Conversely, suppose that there exists an
s\in S_{p^{e}j}
such that

s(I^{[p^{e}]}:I)\not\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p^{e}]}.

Write
r
for the image of
s
in
R_{p^{e}j}
. The element
F_{*}^{e}(r)
in
(F_{*}^{e}R)_{\mathbb{Z}}
has degree
d
, and so the map of
R
-modules
R\to(F_{*}^{e}R)_{\mathbb{Z}}
defined by
1\mapsto F_{*}^{e}(r)
induces a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded
R
-module map

\phi\colon R(-j)\to(F_{*}^{e}R)_{\mathbb{Z}}
. The assumption on

, combined with
4.19
ensures that
\phi
splits in the category of (ungraded)

-modules. Hence it also splits in the category of graded modules.
∎

Proof of
Theorem

4.17
.

Assume
q\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}p^{e}>n-d
. First we verify that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)
is a summand of
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
for each
a
such that
d-n\leq a\leq 0
; this part of the proof works also when
X
has degree
n+1
. Note
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)
is a summand of
F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
if and only if
R(a)
is an
R
-module summand of
F^{e}_{*}R
; see
[
87
, Theorem 3.10]
. We produce necessary
R
-module splittings using
Proposition

4.20
. Let
f_{1},\ldots,f_{t}
be a regular sequence in
S
that generates
I
, and defines
X
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Then

(I^{[q]}:I)=(f^{q-1})+I^{[q]}\quad\text{where}\,\,f=f_{1}\cdots f_{t}\,.

Since
X
, and hence also
R
, is
F
-split,
f^{q-1}\notin m^{[q]}
; see
[
33
, section 2]
,
[
85
, Prop 4.10]
. Let
g
be a nonzero monomial in

such that

gf^{q-1}\notin m^{[q]}\quad\text{but}\quad x_{i}gf^{q-1}\in m^{[q]}\quad\text{for each }i=0,\ldots,n\,.

Thus
(x_{0}\cdots x_{n})^{q-1}
appears in the homogeneous polynomial
gf^{q-1}
, and so its degree is
(q-1)(n+1)
. For

, one has

(n-d)q\leq\text{deg}(g)=(n+1)(q-1)-d(q-1)\,.

Thus for
j=0,\ldots,n-d
we can choose a monomial factor
s_{j}
of

with
s_{j}f^{q-1}\notin m^{[q]}
. Then
Proposition

4.20
yields that

is a summand of

.

We verify, by an induction on

, that
\oplus_{j=n-d-t}^{n-d}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-j)
is a summand of
F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
for

; this holds when

by the argument above.
Once we know

F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\cong E\oplus\bigoplus_{j=n-d-t}^{n-d}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-j)\,,

it follows that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(n-d-t-1)
is a summand of

since the former is a summand of
F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
and does not admit any nonzero map to
\oplus_{j=n-d-t}^{n-d}\mathcal{O}_{X}(-j)
. This completes the proof of the claim about the twists of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
occurring in
F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
.

We now show that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)
can appear in the direct sum decomposition of
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
only if
d-n\leq a\leq 0
and exactly one copy of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
splits. To that end, write

F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}=M\oplus\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{O}_{X}(i)^{a_{i}}

where
M
is some coherent sheaf. Since
X
is
F
-split,
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is a summand of
F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Since
\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=\operatorname{H}^{0}(X,F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X})
and the global sections on the right side are already accounted for by one
\mathcal{O}_{X}
summand,

and
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)
cannot appear as a direct summand of
F_{*}^{e}\mathcal{O}_{X}
for
a\geq 1
. As to the other summands, since
\omega_{X}
is invertible one has the first isomorphism below:

\mathcal{H}\kern-1.0pto\kern-1.0ptm\kern-1.0pt(F^{e}_{*}\omega_{X},\mathcal{O}_{X})\otimes\omega_{X}\cong\mathcal{H}\kern-1.0pto\kern-1.0ptm\kern-1.0pt(F^{e}_{*}\omega_{X},\omega_{X})\cong F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{H}\kern-1.0pto\kern-1.0ptm\kern-1.0pt(\omega_{X},F^{e,!}\omega_{X})\cong F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}\,.

The others are standard. So
\mathcal{O}_{X}(b)
appears as a summand of
F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
if and only if

\mathcal{O}_{X}(-b)\otimes\omega_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(-b)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(d-n-1)=\mathcal{O}_{X}(d-n-1-b)

is a summand of
F^{e}_{*}\omega_{X}
. Since
\omega_{X}
is anti-ample,
F^{e}_{*}\omega_{X}
has no nonzero global sections, whereas
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a)
does whenever
a\geq 0
. We conclude that if
\mathcal{O}_{X}(b)
is a summand of
F^{e}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
then
b\geq d-n
.

It remains to note that when
\dim X\geq 3
the Picard group of
X
is
\mathbb{Z}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
.
∎

5.
Numerical invariants

Motivated by the work in
Section

3
one can introduce new invariants coming from the derived category to measure singularity types. One such invariant is briefly studied in this section. Throughout
R
is an
F
-finite noetherian ring.

Definition 5.1
.

The
Frobenius level
(or simply
F
-
level
) of
R
is

F\!\operatorname{-level}(R)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\inf\{n\geq 0\mid R\text{ is in }\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}^{n}(F_{*}^{e}R)\text{ for some }e>0\}\,.

Corollary

3.11
implies that this number is finite. A natural question arises: Does
F_{*}R
already generate
R
? That is to say, is
F_{*}R
proxy-small? See
[
26
,
27
]
for a discussion on proxy-smallness, and some consequences of this property.

5.2
.

In the terminology of
[
7
]
, the
level
of an
R
-complex
M
with respect to an
R
-complex
N
, denoted
\mathsf{level}^{N}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}(M)
, is the infimum of the set of integers
n
such that
M
is in
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}^{n}(N)
. Therefore
F\!\operatorname{-level}(R)
is the least value of the level of
R
with respect to
F_{*}^{e}R
for a positive integer
e
.

A ring
R
is
F
-split
if the Frobenius map
F\colon R\to R
is split in
\operatorname{mod}R
. The Frobenius level is a measure of the failure of
F
-splitness. This follows from the result below; it is well-known, we sketch a proof for lack of an adequate reference.

Proposition 5.3
.

For any finite map
R\to S
, with
R
commutative noetherian, the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

R\to S
is split as
R
-modules;

(2)

f_{*}S
has
R
as a direct summand;

(3)

(f_{*}S)^{n}
has
R
as a direct summand for some
n\geq 1
;

(4)

\mathsf{level}^{f_{*}S}(R)\leq 1
.

Proof.

Clearly (1)
\Rightarrow
(2)
\Rightarrow
(3)
\Rightarrow
(4). Moreover, (4) means that
R
is a direct summand of an object of the form

\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sf\Sigma}^{n}(f_{*}S)^{\oplus s_{n}}\,;

equivalently,
R
is a direct summand of
(f_{*}S)^{\oplus s_{0}}
; thus (4)
\Rightarrow
(3).

It remains to verify
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
. As (1) can be checked locally on
\operatorname{Spec}R
, so we can assume
R
is local. Then (3) implies
(f_{*}S)^{n}
, and hence also
f_{*}S
is a faithful
R
-module, and moreover that this remains so under base change. Thus
R\to S
is pure; since it is finite, we deduce that it is split; see, for instance,
[
63
, Theorem 7.14]
.
∎

Example 5.4
.

Let
R
be an artinian
F
-finite local ring with residue field
k
, and let
\ell\ell(R)
denote the Loewy length of
R
. For
e\geq\log_{p}\ell\ell(R)
it is easy to see that
F_{*}^{e}R
is a nonzero finite
k
-vectorspace and hence,

\mathsf{level}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}^{F_{*}^{e}R}(R)=\mathsf{level}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}^{k}(R)=\ell\ell(R)\,;

see, for example,
[
7
, Theorem 6.2]
. Thus
F\!\operatorname{-level}(R)\leq\ell\ell(R)
.

Here is another family of rings for which we could bound
F
-levels. The bound is sharp, for the result below and
Proposition

5.3
, any
F
-finite non-
F
-split hypersurface over a field of characteristic two has Frobenius level exactly 2.

Proposition 5.5
.

If
R
is an
F
-finite locally complete intersection ring of prime characteristic
p
, then

F\!\operatorname{-level}(R)\leq p^{\operatorname{codepth}R}
.

Proof.

By
[
58
, Corollary 3.4]
,

\mathsf{level}^{F_{*}R}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}(R)=\sup\{\mathsf{level}^{F_{*}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\mid\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\}

so we can assume
R
is a local complete intersection ring. Applying
[
58
, Corollary 2.17]
, we can further assume
R
is a quotient of an
F
-finite regular local ring
S
by a regular sequence
f_{1},\ldots,f_{c}
. It suffices to show
F\!\operatorname{-level}(R)
is at most
p^{c}.

Factor
F
as
R\to R^{\prime}\to R

where
R^{\prime}=S/(f_{1}^{p},\ldots,f_{c}^{p})
and the first map is base change along the Frobenius of
S
. Since
S
is
F
-finite and regular,
R^{\prime}
is a nonzero finite free
R
-module. By
[
11
, Remark 2]
, one has a filtration

0=R^{\prime}_{p^{c}}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq R_{1}^{\prime}\subseteq R_{0}^{\prime}=R^{\prime}

by
R^{\prime}
-submodules where each subquotient is
F_{*}R
. In particular,

\mathsf{level}^{F_{*}R}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}(R^{\prime})\leq p^{c}\,,

since
R^{\prime}
is free over
R
.
∎

5.6
.

It seems interesting to consider the
F
-level of dualizing complexes.
Clearly, there are other invariants that one can introduce to study the singularity type, also in the global context. In future work, we hope to explore the relations between them and properties like finite Frobenius representation type and strong
F
-regularity.

6.
The first Frobenius pushforward

The results from
Section

3
also bring up the following question.

Question 6.1
.

Suppose
X
is
F
-finite. Is there a perfect complex
G
such that
F_{*}G
itself generates
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
?

We know this is so when
X
is regular. The main result of this section,
Theorem

6.3
, generalizes this to locally complete intersections schemes.

6.1
also brings to mind the result below, due to Mathew
[
62
]
.

6.2
.

Let
R
be a noetherian ring of prime characteristic and
F
its Frobenius endomorphism. Assume
F
is finite. Since the kernel of
F
consists of nilpotent elements, it follows from
[
62
, Theorem 3.16]
, see also
[
17
, Section 11]
, that for any integer
e\geq 1
the
R
-module
F^{e}_{*}R
generates
{\mathsf{D}}(R)
as a tensor ideal thick subcategory:

{\mathsf{D}}(R)=\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}^{\otimes}(F^{e}_{*}R)\,.

Locally complete intersections

A scheme
X
is
locally complete intersection
if it is noetherian and the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{x}
is complete intersection for each
x\in X
. This condition means that the completion of
\mathcal{O}_{x}
at its maximal ideal can be presented as a regular ring modulo a regular sequence; see
[
23
, Section 2.3]
for details.

Theorem 6.3
.

When
X
is
F
-finite and locally complete intersection, for any perfect complex
G
that generates
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
, the complex
F_{*}G
is a generator for
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
. In particular, if
X
is affine,
F_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}
is a strong generator for
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
.

The proof uses the theory of cohomological support varieties for complete intersection local rings. The results we need are recalled below; see
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
.

6.4
.

Let
R
be a local complete intersection ring of codimension
c
, and residue field
k
. For each
M
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
, one can associate a Zariski closed cone
\operatorname{V}_{R}(M)
in the homogeneous spectrum
\operatorname{Spec}^{*}\mathcal{S}
where
\mathcal{S}
is the symmetric algebra on the graded
k
-space
{\sf\Sigma}^{-2}k^{c}
; see
[
5
]
. This is the ring of cohomology operators of Gulliksen
[
38
]
and Eisenbud
[
29
]
. The (Krull) dimension of
\operatorname{V}_{R}(M)
is the complexity of
M
:

\operatorname{cx}_{R}(M)=\inf\{d\in\mathbb{N}:\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{Ext}_{R}^{n}(M,k)\leq an^{d-1}\text{ for some $a>0$ and all $n$}\}\,.

The relevance of these varieties to the problem at hand is because of
[
59
, Theorem 3.1]
: If
R
is locally complete intersection and
M,N
are in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
, then

N\text{ is in }\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}M\iff\operatorname{V}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}})\subseteq\operatorname{V}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\text{ for each }\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\,.

Theorem

6.3
follows from the result below with
G=\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and
Lemma

3.8
.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let
X
be an
F
-finite noetherian scheme. If
X
is locally complete intersection, each
G
in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
with
\operatorname{supp}_{X}G=X
satisfies

{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)=\operatorname{thick}^{\odot}_{{\mathsf{D}}(X)}(F_{*}G)

as a
\mathsf{Perf}\,X
-module.

Proof.

Fix
x\in X
, set
R\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathcal{O}_{x}
and
M\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}G_{x}
. Given
Corollary

1.10
, it is suffices to verify the
R
-complex
F_{*}M
generates
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
.

Let
N
be in
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
. By
[
9
, Theorem 12.2.4]
,

\operatorname{cx}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(F_{*}(M_{\mathfrak{p}}))=\operatorname{cx}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(k(\mathfrak{p}))=\mathrm{codim}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\,.

In particular,
\operatorname{V}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}})\subseteq\operatorname{V}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(F_{*}(M_{\mathfrak{p}}))
. Since
(F_{*}M)_{\mathfrak{p}}
is isomorphic to
F_{*}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})
we obtain that

\operatorname{V}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}})\subseteq\operatorname{V}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}((F_{*}M)_{\mathfrak{p}})\,.

As the previous equality holds for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R
we can apply
6.4
to conclude that
N
is in
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}(F_{*}M)
.
∎

The next result produces examples of rings
R
which are not locally complete intersection, yet
F_{*}R
generates
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
.

Veronese subrings

Let
k
be an
F
-finite field of prime characteristic and let
S\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
, the polynomial ring in indeterminates
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}
, each having degree one. The observation below is applied when
R
is a Veronese subring of
S
.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
\iota\colon R\to S
be a finite extension that is split as a map of
R
-modules.
Then
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)=\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}(\iota_{*}S)
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that any finitely generated
R
-module
M
is in
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}(S)
. Since
S
is regular,
S\otimes_{R}M
is in
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(S)}(S)
and hence in
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}(\iota_{*}S)
by restriction of scalars along
\iota
. Since
\iota
is split,
M
is an
R
-module summand of
S\otimes_{R}M
. Thus
M
is finitely built from
\iota_{*}S
in
{\mathsf{D}}(R)
.
∎

The minimal graded free resolution of the
S
-module
S/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})^{j}
has the form:

(6.6.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0\rightarrow S^{\oplus b_{d}(j)}(-j-d+1)\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow S^{\oplus b_{1}(j)}(-j)\rightarrow S^{\oplus b_{0}(j)}\rightarrow 0\,.}

The resolution is linear after the first term. The integers
b_{i}(j)
are given by

b_{i}(j)=\begin{cases}1&i=0\\
\frac{(j+d-1)!}{(j-1)!(d-i)!(i-1)!(j+i-1)}&i\leq d\\
0&i\geq d+1\,.\end{cases}

See, for instance,
[
24
]
. Fix an integer
\ell\geq 1
. For each integer
j
, let
G_{j}
denote the
k
-vector space spanned by all monomials in
S
whose total degree is congruent to
j
modulo
\ell
. Note that each
G_{j}
is a finitely generated
R
-module.
Taking strands of
Eq.

6.6.1
yields exact sequences of
R
-modules

(6.6.2)

0\rightarrow G_{\ell-d+1}^{\oplus b_{d}(j)}\rightarrow\cdots G_{l-1}^{\oplus b_{2}(j)}\rightarrow R^{\oplus b_{1}(j)}\rightarrow G_{j}\rightarrow 0

for
1\leq j\leq\ell-1
.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
R
be the
\ell
-th Veronese subring of
k[x,y]
. For each integer
1\leq j\leq\ell-1
the following equality holds

{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)=\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}(R\oplus G_{j})\,.

Proof.

Given
Lemma

6.6
and the isomorphism
S\cong G_{0}\oplus\ldots\oplus G_{\ell-1}

of
R
-modules, it suffices to verify that each
G_{i}
is in
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}(R\oplus G_{j})
for
0\leq i\leq\ell-1
. This becomes clear by applying
Equation

6.6.2
with
d=2
.
∎

Proposition 6.8
.

Let
k
be an
F
-finite field. If
R
is the
\ell
-th Veronese subring of
k[x,y]
, for some
\ell\geq 1
, then
F_{*}R
is a strong generator for
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
.

Proof.

By
Lemma

6.7
it suffices to verify that
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}(F_{*}R)
contains
R
and at least one
G_{j}
where
1\leq j\leq\ell-1
. The first containment is satisfied as the Frobenius map
R\rightarrow F_{*}R
splits as an
R
-module, as
R
is a direct summand of a regular ring.

To show some
G_{j}
is in
\operatorname{thick}_{{\mathsf{D}}(R)}(F_{*}R)
, we import results from the theory of conic modules over a toric ring; see, for example,
[
22
,
31
]
. Namely, the module
F_{*}R
is isomorphic to a direct sum of conic modules; see
[
31
, Proposition 4.15]
and
[
21
, Theorem 3.1]
. On the other hand, any conic
R
-module is isomorphic some
G_{i}
by
[
31
, Remark 7.6]
. Therefore since
G_{0}\cong R
and
R
is not regular,
F_{*}R
must have a summand isomorphic to
G_{j}
for some
1\leq j\leq\ell-1.

∎

6.9
.

Let
S=k[x,y]
and
R
be the
\ell
-th Veronese subring of
S
. For
\ell\geq 3
, the ring
R
is not a complete intersection ring. Moreover, if
\ell>p^{2}[k:k^{p}]
, then
F_{*}R
cannot have
S
as a free
R
-module summand; indeed, the rank of
S
and
F_{*}R
as
R
-modules are
\ell
and
p^{2}[k:k^{p}]
respectively, cf.
Lemma

6.6
.

We end with a few observations regarding
6.1
.

6.10
.

By the local-to-global principle from
[
58
, Corollary 3.4]
, to settle
6.1
in the affine setting, it suffices to check that when
R
is local
F_{*}R
generates the residue field of
R
. When
R
is also Cohen-Macaulay, one can reduce to
R
artinian.

Suppose
R
is an artinian local ring.
6.1
has a positive answer when
k
is a direct summand of
F_{*}R
. This property holds if and only if the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
satisfies
(0:\mathfrak{m}^{[p]})\not\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}
. For example, if
R=\mathbb{F}_{2}[x,y]/(x^{4},x^{2}y^{2},y^{4})
it is not immediately clear whether
F_{*}R
generates
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R)
since

is not a summand of

; in this example,

F_{*}R\cong(R/(x^{2},xy,y^{2}))^{\oplus 3}\,.

However a direct analysis shows that even for this ring

generates
{\mathsf{D}}^{\mathsf{b}}(\operatorname{mod}R).

Further Properties of PPT and Hyponormal Matrices
Hamid Reza Moradi, Ibrahim Halil Gümüş, and Mohammad Sababheh

Abstract
. This paper discusses further properties of positive partial transpose matrices, with applications towards hyponormal, semi-hyponormal, and ( α , β ) (\alpha,\beta) -normal matrices. The obtained results present extensions and improvements of many results in the literature.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 15A45, 47A30; Secondary 15A42, 47A63, 47B65

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

In the sequel,
\mathcal{M}_{n}
denotes the algebra of all
n\times n
complex matrices. When
A\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
is such that
\left<Ax,x\right>\geq 0
for all
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we say that
A
is positive semidefinite, and we write
A\geq O
. On the other hand, if
\left<Ax,x\right>>0
for all nonzero
x\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
A
is said to be positive definite, and we write
A>O
. Here,
O
denotes the zero element of
\mathcal{M}_{n}
.

Given
A,B,X\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
, the matrix
\left[\begin{matrix}A&X^{*}\\
X&B\end{matrix}\right]
is in
\mathcal{M}_{2n}.
It is well known that if
\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O
, then

(1.1)

\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|\leq\left\|A\right\|+\left\|B\right\|,

where
\|\cdot\|
is the usual operator norm. Indeed, (
1.1
) follows from the following useful decomposition
[
5
, Lemma 3.4]
: For every matrix
\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O
, we have

\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]=U\left[\begin{matrix}A&O\\
O&O\\
\end{matrix}\right]{{U}^{*}}+V\left[\begin{matrix}O&O\\
O&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]{{V}^{*}}

for some unitaries
U,V
.

However, if
\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O
and the off-diagonal block
X
is Hermitian, Hiroshima
[
12
]
showed a stronger inequality than (
1.1
), as follows

(1.2)

\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}A&X\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|\leq\left\|A+B\right\|.

The block matrix
\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]
has received the attention of numerous researchers in the literature due to its usability and applications. For example,
\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]
is said to be positive partial transpose (PPT) if both
\left[\begin{matrix}A&X\\
{{X}^{*}}&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]
and
\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]
are positive semidefinite.

If
\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]
is PPT, Lee
[
15
, Theorem 2.1]
showed that

(1.3)

\left|X\right|\leq\frac{A\sharp B+{{U}^{*}}(A\sharp B)U}{2},

where
X=U\left|X\right|
is the polar decomposition of
X
, and
\sharp
is the matrix geometric mean. Recall, here, that if
A,B>O
and
0<t<1
, the weighted geometric mean of
A
and
B
is defined by

\displaystyle A\sharp_{t}B=A^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(A^{-\frac{1}{2}}BA^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{t}A^{\frac{1}{2}}.

When
t=\frac{1}{2},
we simply write
A\sharp B
instead of
A\sharp_{\frac{1}{2}}B
. The geometric mean
\sharp
was first
introduced by Pusz and Woronowicz
[
16
]
, which was further developed into a general theory of operator means by Kubo and Ando
[
14
]
.

Fu et al.
[
6
, Theorem 2.3]
improved (
1.3
) as follows

(1.4)

\left|X\right|\leq\left(A\sharp B\right)\sharp\left({{U}^{*}}\left(A\sharp B\right)U\right).

The fact that (
1.4
) improves (
1.3
) follows from the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality that states

(1.5)

\displaystyle A\sharp B\leq\frac{A+B}{2},

for any
A,B\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
with
A,B>O.

Among those useful characterizations of the geometric mean, we have
[
4
, (4.15)]
)

(1.6)

X\sharp Y=\max\left\{Z:\text{ }Z={{Z}^{*}},\left[\begin{matrix}X&Z\\
Z&Y\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O\right\};X,Y>O.

Recently, the following lemma has been shown in
[
10
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Lemma 1.1
.

If
\left[\begin{matrix}A&X\\
{{X}^{*}}&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]
is PPT, then so is
\left[\begin{matrix}A{{\sharp}_{t}}B&X\\
{{X}^{*}}&A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\\
\end{matrix}\right]
,
0\leq t\leq 1
.

Further, it has been shown in
[
15
, Corollary 2.2]
that

(1.7)

{{\lambda}_{j}}\left(2\left|X\right|-A{{\sharp}_{t}}B\right)\leq{{\lambda}_{j}}\left(A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\right),

when
\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]
is PPT. Here
\lambda_{j}
denotes the
j^{\text{th}}
largest eigenvalue.

This paper discusses extensions of (
1.4
) and (
1.7
), where we extend both inequalities to the weighted geometric mean. We will also extend
[
6
, Theorem 2.2]
, where we show that when
\left[\begin{matrix}A&X\\
{{X}^{*}}&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O
, then
\left|X\right|\leq\left(A{{\sharp}_{t}}{{U}^{*}}BU\right)\sharp\left(A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}{{U}^{*}}BU\right)
, for example. Further, we give an improvement of (
1.1
) that is different from (
1.2
). Many other consequences for PPT matrices and positive semidefinite matrices of the form
\left[\begin{matrix}A&X\\
{{X}^{*}}&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]
will be presented.

After that, we present some applications that include semi-hyponormal and
(\alpha,\beta)
-normal matrices. Here we recall that if
T\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
is such that
|T^{*}|^{2}\leq|T|^{2}
, then
T
is said to be hyponormal. If
T
satisfies the weaker condition
|T^{*}|\leq|T|
, then
T
is said to be semi-hyponormal. More generally, if
0\leq\alpha\leq 1\leq\beta
are such that
\alpha^{2}|T|^{2}\leq|T^{*}|^{2}\leq\beta^{2}|T|^{2}
, then
T
is said to be
(\alpha,\beta)
-normal. For example, we will show that

\left[\begin{matrix}\left|T\right|&{{T}^{*}}\\
T&\left|T\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O

if and only if
T
is semi-hyponormal. Many other results and consequences will be shown for these classes. As a consequence, we will be able to present a possible reverse for the inequality
\|T^{2}\|\leq\|T\|^{2}
, when
T
is
(\alpha,\beta)
-normal.

2.
Main Results for positive and PPT block matrices

Our first result is an extension of
[
6
, Theorem 2.2]
, which states that if
\left[\begin{matrix}A&X\\
{{X}^{*}}&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O
, then

\left|X\right|\leq A\sharp{{U}^{*}}BU\text{ and }\left|{{X}^{*}}\right|\leq UA{{U}^{*}}\sharp B.

Once this has been shown, we use it to extend some results about PPT matrices.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\left[\begin{matrix}A&X\\
{{X}^{*}}&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O
with
A,B,X\in{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}
and let
X=U\left|X\right|
be the polar decomposition of
X
. Then for any
0\leq t\leq 1
,

(2.1)

\left|X\right|\leq\left(A{{\sharp}_{t}}{{U}^{*}}BU\right)\sharp\left(A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}{{U}^{*}}BU\right)

and

(2.2)

\left|{{X}^{*}}\right|\leq\left(UA{{U}^{*}}{{\sharp}_{t}}B\right)\sharp\left(UA{{U}^{*}}{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\right).

In particular,

\left|X\right|\leq A\sharp{{U}^{*}}BU\text{ and }\left|{{X}^{*}}\right|\leq UA{{U}^{*}}\sharp B.

Proof.

We prove (
2.1
). Since
X=U\left|X\right|
is the polar decomposition of
X
, we have

\left[\begin{matrix}I&O\\
O&{{U}^{*}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}I&O\\
O&U\\
\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}U\\
{{U}^{*}}X&{{U}^{*}}BU\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O,

which implies

\left[\begin{matrix}A&\left|X\right|\\
\left|X\right|&{{U}^{*}}BU\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O,

since
{{X}^{*}}U={{U}^{*}}X=\left|X\right|
. Lemma
1.1
gives

\left[\begin{matrix}A{{\sharp}_{t}}{{U}^{*}}BU&\left|X\right|\\
\left|X\right|&A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}{{U}^{*}}BU\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O.

Now, the result follows from (
1.6
). To prove (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\left|{{X}^{*}}\right|

\displaystyle=U\left|X\right|{{U}^{*}}\quad\text{(by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{9}{}{}, p. 58]})}

\displaystyle\leq U\left(\left(A{{\sharp}_{t}}{{U}^{*}}BU\right)\sharp\left(A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}{{U}^{*}}BU\right)\right){{U}^{*}}

\displaystyle=U\left(A{{\sharp}_{t}}{{U}^{*}}BU\right){{U}^{*}}\sharp U\left(A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}{{U}^{*}}BU\right){{U}^{*}}

\displaystyle=\left(UA{{U}^{*}}{{\sharp}_{t}}BU\right)\sharp\left(UA{{U}^{*}}{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\right).

This proves (
2.2
). Letting
t=\frac{1}{2}
in (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), and noting that
T\sharp T=T,
when
T>O
, imply
\left|X\right|\leq A\sharp{{U}^{*}}BU\text{ and }\left|{{X}^{*}}\right|\leq UA{{U}^{*}}\sharp B.
This completes the proof.
∎

Using Theorem
2.1
, we present the following extension of (
1.4
).

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
A,B,X\in{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}
be such that
\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]
is PPT, and let
X=U\left|X\right|
be the polar decomposition of
X
. Then for any
0\leq t\leq 1
,

\left|X\right|\leq\left(A{{\sharp}_{t}}B\right)\sharp\left({{U}^{*}}\left(A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\right)U\right),

and

\left|{{X}^{*}}\right|\leq\left(U\left(A{{\sharp}_{t}}B\right){{U}^{*}}\right)\sharp\left(A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\right).

Proof.

Lemma
1.1
ensures that
\left[\begin{matrix}A{{\sharp}_{t}}B&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\\
\end{matrix}\right]
is also PPT, for any
0\leq t\leq 1
. By Theorem
2.1
, we conclude the first desired result.

The second inequality can be shown using the method we used to show (
2.2
). This completes the proof.
∎

Interestingly, Corollary
2.1
can be used to present a weighted version of (
1.7
), as follows.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
A,B,X\in{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}
be such that
\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]
is PPT. Then for any
0\leq t\leq 1
,

{{\lambda}_{j}}\left(2\left|X\right|-A{{\sharp}_{t}}B\right)\leq{{\lambda}_{j}}\left(A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\right)

and

{{\lambda}_{j}}\left(2\left|{{X}^{*}}\right|-A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\right)\leq{{\lambda}_{j}}\left(A{{\sharp}_{t}}B\right)

for all
j=1,2,\ldots,n
.

Proof.

Corollary
2.1
means that

\displaystyle\left|X\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(A{{\sharp}_{t}}B\right)\sharp\left({{U}^{*}}(A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B)U\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{A{{\sharp}_{t}}B+{{U}^{*}}(A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B)U}{2}.

Thus,

2\left|X\right|-A{{\sharp}_{t}}B\leq{{U}^{*}}(A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B)U.

Therefore,

{{\lambda}_{j}}\left(2\left|X\right|-A{{\sharp}_{t}}B\right)\leq{{\lambda}_{j}}\left(A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\right)

as desired.
∎

Related to the discussion, we employ (
1.2
) to obtain another refinement of (
1.1
), as follows.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
A,B,X\in{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}
be such that
\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O
and let
X=U\left|X\right|
be the polar decomposition of
X
. Then

\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|\leq\left\|A+{{U}^{*}}BU\right\|.

Proof.

If
X=U\left|X\right|
is the polar decomposition of
X
, then

\left[\begin{matrix}I&O\\
O&{{U}^{*}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}I&O\\
O&U\\
\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}I&O\\
O&U\\
\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}I&O\\
O&{{U}^{*}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}I&O\\
O&I\\
\end{matrix}\right],

since
U
is unitary. The fact that
\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O
implies

\displaystyle\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}I&O\\
O&{{U}^{*}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}I&O\\
O&U\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}A&\left|X\right|\\
\left|X\right|&{{U}^{*}}BU\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|A+{{U}^{*}}BU\right\|,

where we have used (
1.2
) to obtain the last inequality. This completes the proof.
∎

The following is an interesting characterization related to PPT matrices.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
A,B,X\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
be such that
\left[\begin{matrix}A&X\\
X^{*}&B\end{matrix}\right]
is PPT and let
0\leq t\leq 1
. Then there are some isometries
\widetilde{U},\widetilde{V}\in{{\mathcal{M}}_{2n,n}}
(depending on
t
), such that

\left[\begin{matrix}A{{\sharp}_{t}}B&X\\
{{X}^{*}}&A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\\
\end{matrix}\right]=\widetilde{U}\left(A{{\sharp}_{t}}B\right){{\widetilde{U}}^{*}}+\widetilde{V}\left(A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\right){{\widetilde{V}}^{*}}.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.1
,
\left[\begin{matrix}A{{\sharp}_{t}}B&X\\
X&A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\\
\end{matrix}\right]
is PPT. From
[
5
, Lemma 3.4]
, there are two unitaries
U,V\in{{\mathcal{M}}_{2n}}
partitioned into equally sized matrices,

U=\left[\begin{matrix}{{U}_{11}}&{{U}_{12}}\\
{{U}_{21}}&{{U}_{22}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\text{ and }V=\left[\begin{matrix}{{V}_{11}}&{{V}_{12}}\\
{{V}_{21}}&{{V}_{22}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]

such that

\left[\begin{matrix}A{{\sharp}_{t}}B&X\\
{{X}^{*}}&A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\\
\end{matrix}\right]=U\left[\begin{matrix}A{{\sharp}_{t}}B&O\\
O&O\\
\end{matrix}\right]{{U}^{*}}+V\left[\begin{matrix}O&O\\
O&A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\\
\end{matrix}\right]{{V}^{*}}.

Hence,

\left[\begin{matrix}A{{\sharp}_{t}}B&X\\
{{X}^{*}}&A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\\
\end{matrix}\right]=\widetilde{U}\left(A{{\sharp}_{t}}B\right){{\widetilde{U}}^{*}}+\widetilde{V}\left(A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\right){{\widetilde{V}}^{*}}

where

\widetilde{U}=\left[\begin{matrix}{{U}_{11}}\\
{{U}_{21}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\text{ and }\widetilde{V}=\left[\begin{matrix}{{V}_{12}}\\
{{V}_{22}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]

are isometries. This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem
2.3
implies the following remarkable result.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
A,B,X\in{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}
be such that
\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]
is PPT. Then for any
0\leq t\leq 1
,

\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}A{{\sharp}_{t}}B&X\\
{{X}^{*}}&A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|\leq\left\|A{{\sharp}_{t}}B\right\|+\left\|A{{\sharp}_{1-t}}B\right\|.

In particular

\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}A\sharp B&X\\
{{X}^{*}}&A\sharp B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|\leq 2\left\|A\sharp B\right\|.

Remark 2.1
.

Ando
[
1
, Theorem 3.3]
proved that if
\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]
is PPT, then

\left\|X\right\|\leq\left\|A\sharp B\right\|.

We know that
[
18
]
if
\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O
, then

2\left\|X\right\|\leq\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|.

Consequently, if
\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]
is PPT, then

\left\|X\right\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}A\sharp B&X\\
{{X}^{*}}&A\sharp B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|\leq\left\|A\sharp B\right\|.

Remark 2.2
.

It is well-known that

{{\left|\left\langle T{{\left|T\right|}^{\alpha+\beta-1}}x,y\right\rangle\right|}^{2}}\leq\left\langle{{\left|T\right|}^{2\alpha}}x,x\right\rangle\left\langle{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2\beta}}y,y\right\rangle;\left(\alpha,\beta\in\left[0,1\right],\alpha+\beta\geq 1\right)

for any
T

[
8
]
. By
[
13
, Lemma 1]
, we infer that

\left[\begin{matrix}{{\left|T\right|}^{2\alpha}}&{{\left|T\right|}^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{{T}^{*}}\\
T{{\left|T\right|}^{\alpha+\beta-1}}&{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2\beta}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O.

So,

\displaystyle 2\left\|T{{\left|T\right|}^{\alpha+\beta-1}}\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}{{\left|T\right|}^{2\alpha}}&{{\left|T\right|}^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{{T}^{*}}\\
T{{\left|T\right|}^{\alpha+\beta-1}}&{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2\beta}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|{{\left|T\right|}^{2\alpha}}+{{U}^{*}}{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2\beta}}U\right\|

\displaystyle=2\left\|{{\left|T\right|}^{2\alpha}}+{{\left|T\right|}^{2\beta}}\right\|

i.e.,

\left\|T{{\left|T\right|}^{\alpha+\beta-1}}\right\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}{{\left|T\right|}^{2\alpha}}&{{\left|T\right|}^{\alpha+\beta-1}}{{T}^{*}}\\
T{{\left|T\right|}^{\alpha+\beta-1}}&{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2\beta}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|\leq\left\|{{\left|T\right|}^{2\alpha}}+{{\left|T\right|}^{2\beta}}\right\|.

In particular,

\left\|T\right\|=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|&T\\
{{T}^{*}}&\left|T\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
A,B,X\in{{\mathcal{M}}_{n}}
be such that
\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O
. Then

\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|\leq\left\|A+B+\left|X\right|+\left|{{X}^{*}}\right|\right\|.

Proof.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}A&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}A&O\\
O&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]+\left[\begin{matrix}O&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&O\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}A&O\\
O&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]+\left[\begin{matrix}\left|X\right|&O\\
O&\left|{{X}^{*}}\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}A+\left|X\right|&O\\
O&B+\left|{{X}^{*}}\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|A+B+\left|X\right|+\left|{{X}^{*}}\right|\right\|

where the first inequality follows from the fact that
\left[\begin{matrix}O&{{X}^{*}}\\
X&O\\
\end{matrix}\right]
is Hermitian, and for any Hermitiam matrix
T
, we have
T\leq\left|T\right|
. The second inequality is also obtained from (
1.2
). This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Applications towards hyponormal and
(\alpha,\beta)
-normal matrices

This section presents several results on hyponormal, semi-hyponormal, and
(\alpha,\beta)
-normal matrices. While some of these results can be considered as applications of the results of the previous section, other results are related but independent.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
. Then
\left[\begin{matrix}\left|T\right|&{{T}^{*}}\\
T&\left|T\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O
if and only if
T
is semi-hyponormal.

Proof.

It is easy to show that
[
17
]
if
T
is semi-hyponormal, then

\left[\begin{matrix}\left|T\right|&{{T}^{*}}\\
T&\left|T\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O.

We show that if
\left[\begin{matrix}\left|T\right|&{{T}^{*}}\\
T&\left|T\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O
, then
T
is semi-hyponormal. Indeed, if
T=U\left|T\right|
is the polar decomposition of
T
, then

\left[\begin{matrix}\left|T\right|&{{T}^{*}}U\\
{{U}^{*}}T&{{U}^{*}}\left|T\right|U\\
\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}I&O\\
O&{{U}^{*}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\left|T\right|&{{T}^{*}}\\
T&\left|T\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}I&O\\
O&U\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O.

Since
{{T}^{*}}U={{U}^{*}}T=\left|T\right|
, we get

\left[\begin{matrix}\left|T\right|&\left|T\right|\\
\left|T\right|&{{U}^{*}}\left|T\right|U\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O.

From (
1.6
), we get

\left|T\right|\leq\left|T\right|\sharp{{U}^{*}}\left|T\right|U.

By the definition of geometric mean, we have

\left|T\right|\leq{{\left|T\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{{\left({{\left|T\right|}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{{U}^{*}}\left|T\right|U{{\left|T\right|}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right)}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{{\left|T\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Multiplying both sides by
{{\left|T\right|}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}
, we infer that

I\leq{{\left({{\left|T\right|}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{{U}^{*}}\left|T\right|U{{\left|T\right|}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\right)}^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

This implies

\left|T\right|\leq{{U}^{*}}\left|T\right|U.

Thus,

\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|=U\left|T\right|{{U}^{*}}\leq\left|T\right|,

which shows that
T
is semi-hyponormal. This completes the proof.
∎

In
[
5
, (2.11)]
, it has been shown that if
A,B
are normal, then

(3.1)

|A+B|\leq\frac{|A|+|B|+U^{*}(|A|+|B|)U}{2},

where
U
is the unitary matrix in the polar decomposition of
A+B.
Since every normal matrix is necessarily semi-hyponormal, and because of (
1.5
), the following result significantly improves
[
5
, (2.11)]
.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
be semi-hyponormal and let
U
be the unitary part in the polar decomposition
A+B=U\left|A+B\right|
. Then

\left|A+B\right|\leq\left(\left|A\right|+\left|B\right|\right)\sharp\left({{U}^{*}}\left(\left|A\right|+\left|B\right|\right)U\right).

Proof.

Using Theorem
3.1
, we see that

(3.2)

\left[\begin{matrix}\left|A\right|+\left|B\right|&{{A}^{*}}+{{B}^{*}}\\
A+B&\left|A\right|+\left|B\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O.

By (
3.2
) and Theorem
2.1
, we get the desired result.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
A,B\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
be semi-hyponormal. Then

\left\|A+B\right\|\leq\left\|\left|A\right|+\left|B\right|\right\|.

Remark 3.1
.

We highlight that Corollary
3.2

A,B

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

(ii)

\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{\alpha}{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}&{{\left|T\right|}^{2}}\\
{{\left|T\right|}^{2}}&\frac{1}{\alpha}{{\left|T\right|}^{2}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O
and
\left[\begin{matrix}\beta{{\left|T\right|}^{2}}&{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}\\
{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}&\beta{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O
.

(iii)

\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{{{\alpha}^{2}}}{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}&{{\left|T\right|}^{2}}\\
{{\left|T\right|}^{2}}&\frac{1}{{{\alpha}^{2}}}{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}\\

and
\left[\begin{matrix}{{\beta}^{2}}{{\left|T\right|}^{2}}&{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}\\

.

\left(i\right)\Leftrightarrow\left(ii\right)

\displaystyle{{\displaystyle\left|T\right|}^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{{{\alpha}^{2}}}{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}

\displaystyle\text{ }\Leftrightarrow{{\left|T\right|}^{2}}{{\left(\frac{1}{\alpha}{{\left|T\right|}^{2}}\right)}^{-1}}{{\left|T\right|}^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{\alpha}{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}&{{\left|T\right|}^{2}}\\
{{\left|T\right|}^{2}}&\frac{1}{\alpha}{{\left|T\right|}^{2}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O\quad\text{(by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{11}{}{}, Theorem 1.3.3]})}.

Again, since

is

-normal, we have

\displaystyle{{\displaystyle\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}\leq{{\beta}^{2}}{{\left|T\right|}^{2}}

\displaystyle\text{ }\Leftrightarrow{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}{{\left(\beta{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}\right)}^{-1}}{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}\leq\beta{{\left|T\right|}^{2}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\left[\begin{matrix}\beta{{\left|T\right|}^{2}}&{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}\\
{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}&\beta{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{2}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O\quad\text{(by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{11}{}{}, Theorem 1.3.3]})}.

\left(i\right)\Leftrightarrow\left(iii\right)
See

We can establish the following theorem by employing the same arguments as in the proof of
\left(i\right)\Leftrightarrow\left(ii\right)
in Theorem
3.2
, but we present another proof.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
be
\left(\alpha,\beta\right)
-normal. Then

\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|&\left|T\right|\\
\left|T\right|&\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left|T\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O\text{ and }\left[\begin{matrix}\sqrt{\beta}\left|T\right|&\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\\
\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|&\sqrt{\beta}\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O.

Proof.

According to the assumption,

\left\langle\left|T\right|x,x\right\rangle\left\langle\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|y,y\right\rangle\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\left\langle\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|x,x\right\rangle\left\langle\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|y,y\right\rangle

and

\left\langle\left|T\right|x,x\right\rangle\left\langle\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|y,y\right\rangle\leq\beta\left\langle\left|T\right|x,x\right\rangle\left\langle\left|T\right|y,y\right\rangle

for any
x,y\in\mathcal{H}
. On the other hand, we know that (see, e.g.,
[
9
, p. 216]
)

{{\left|\left\langle Tx,y\right\rangle\right|}^{2}}\leq\left\langle\left|T\right|x,x\right\rangle\left\langle\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|y,y\right\rangle.

Consequently,

{{\left|\left\langle Tx,y\right\rangle\right|}^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\left\langle\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|x,x\right\rangle\left\langle\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|y,y\right\rangle

and

{{\left|\left\langle Tx,y\right\rangle\right|}^{2}}\leq\beta\left\langle\left|T\right|x,x\right\rangle\left\langle\left|T\right|y,y\right\rangle.

The last two inequalities are equivalent to

(3.3)

\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|&{{T}^{*}}\\
T&\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O\text{ and }\left[\begin{matrix}\sqrt{\beta}\left|T\right|&{{T}^{*}}\\
T&\sqrt{\beta}\left|T\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O,

thanks to
[
13
, Lemma 1]
.

Now assume that
T=U\left|T\right|
is the polar decomposition of
T
. Then

(3.4)

\left[\begin{matrix}I&O\\
O&{{U}^{*}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|&{{T}^{*}}\\
T&\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}I&O\\
O&U\\
\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|&{{T}^{*}}U\\
{{U}^{*}}T&\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}{{U}^{*}}\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|U\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O.

On the other hand,

\left[\begin{matrix}\sqrt{\beta}\left|T\right|&{{T}^{*}}\\
T&\sqrt{\beta}\left|T\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O\text{ }\Leftrightarrow\text{ }\left[\begin{matrix}\sqrt{\beta}\left|T\right|&T\\
{{T}^{*}}&\sqrt{\beta}\left|T\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O.

So,

(3.5)

\left[\begin{matrix}I&O\\
O&U\\
\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}\sqrt{\beta}\left|T\right|&T\\
{{T}^{*}}&\sqrt{\beta}\left|T\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\left[\begin{matrix}I&O\\
O&{{U}^{*}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]=\left[\begin{matrix}\sqrt{\beta}\left|T\right|&T{{U}^{*}}\\
U{{T}^{*}}&\sqrt{\beta}U\left|T\right|{{U}^{*}}\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O.

One can easily check that
{{U}^{*}}T={{T}^{*}}U=\left|T\right|
. Meanwhile,
\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|=U\left|T\right|{{U}^{*}}
(see
[
9
, p. 58]
), so
{{U}^{*}}\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|U=\left|T\right|
. Hence, by (
3.4
) and (
3.5
), we obtain

\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|&\left|T\right|\\
\left|T\right|&\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left|T\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O\text{ and }\left[\begin{matrix}\sqrt{\beta}\left|T\right|&\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\\
\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|&\sqrt{\beta}\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O,

as desired.
∎

The matrices in (
3.3
) are P.P.T. Therefore, by Corollary
2.2
, we have the following eigenvalue inequalities.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
be
\left(\alpha,\beta\right)
-normal. Then

{{\lambda}_{j}}\left(2\sqrt{\alpha}\left|T\right|-\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\right)\leq{{\lambda}_{j}}\left(\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\right)

and

{{\lambda}_{j}}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{\beta}}\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|-\left|T\right|\right)\leq{{\lambda}_{j}}\left(\left|T\right|\right)

for
j=1,2,\ldots,n
.

Remark 3.2
.

It is well-known that for any
T\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
,

\left\|\;\left|T\right|-\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\;\right\|\leq\left\|T\right\|.

From Corollary
3.3
, we infer that if
T
is
\left(\alpha,\beta\right)
-normal, then

\left\|\;2\sqrt{\alpha}\left|T\right|-\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\;\right\|\leq\left\|T\right\|,

and

\left\|\;\frac{2}{\sqrt{\beta}}\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|-\left|T\right|\;\right\|\leq\left\|T\right\|.

The inequality (
1.5
) is usually referred to as the operator arithmetic-geometric mean inequality. It is of great interest in the literature to find possible reverses for this inequality. Usually, such reverses are found under additional conditions, as seen in
[
7
,
11
]
. In the following, we present a reverse of (
1.5
) for
(\alpha,\beta)
-normal matrices.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
be
\left(\alpha,\beta\right)
-normal. Then

\frac{\left|T\right|+\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}{2}\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}},\sqrt{\beta}\right\}\left(\left|T\right|\sharp\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\right).

Proof.

Theorem
3.3
implies

(3.6)

\left|T\right|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left(\left|T\right|\sharp\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\right)\text{ and }\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\leq\sqrt{\beta}\left(\left|T\right|\sharp\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\right)

due to (
1.6
). Further,

(3.7)

\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left(\left|T\right|\sharp\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\right)\text{ and }\left|T\right|\leq\sqrt{\beta}\left(\left|T\right|\sharp\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\right)

by utilizing the same approach as in the proof of inequality (
2.2
).
Inequalities (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) say that

\left|T\right|\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}},\sqrt{\beta}\right\}\left(\left|T\right|\sharp\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\right)\text{ and }\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}},\sqrt{\beta}\right\}\left(\left|T\right|\sharp\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\right).

Adding the above two inequalities together implies the desired result.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Inequalities (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) can be shown in another way.
Since
f\left(t\right)=\sqrt{t}
is operator monotone on
\left(0,\infty\right)
, and since
\alpha^{2}|T|^{2}\leq|T^{*}|^{2},
we infer that

\left|T\right|\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|.

This implies

\left|T\right|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left(\left|T\right|\sharp\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\right),

where we have used the fact that if
A,B,C,D>O
are such that
A\leq B
and
C\leq D
, then
A\sharp C\leq B\sharp D.

For the following result, we remind the reader of positive linear maps. A linear map
\Phi:\mathcal{M})_{n}\to\mathcal{M}_{n}
is said to be positive if
\Phi(A)\geq O
whenever
A\geq O.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
be
\left(\alpha,\beta\right)
-normal and let
\Phi
be a positive linear map. If
\Phi\left(T\right)=U\left|\Phi\left(T\right)\right|
is the polar decomposition of
\Phi\left(T\right)
, then

\left|\Phi\left(T\right)\right|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left(\Phi\left(\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\right)\sharp{{U}^{*}}\Phi\left(\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\right)U\right)

and

\left|\Phi\left(T\right)\right|\leq\sqrt{\beta}\left(\Phi\left(\left|T\right|\right)\sharp{{U}^{*}}\Phi\left(\left|T\right|\right)U\right).

Proof.

First notice that every positive linear map is adjoint-preserving; i.e.,
{{\Phi}^{*}}\left(T\right)=\Phi\left({{T}^{*}}\right)
for all
T

[
3
, Lemma 2.3.1]
.
It follows from (
3.3
) that

\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\Phi\left(\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\right)&{{\Phi}^{*}}\left(T\right)\\
\Phi\left(T\right)&\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\Phi\left(\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\right)\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O\text{ and }\left[\begin{matrix}\sqrt{\beta}\Phi\left(\left|T\right|\right)&{{\Phi}^{*}}\left(T\right)\\
\Phi\left(T\right)&\sqrt{\beta}\Phi\left(\left|T\right|\right)\\

thanks to

The following result presents an interesting reverse of the well-known inequality
\|T^{2}\|\leq\|T\|^{2},
for any
T
. We recall that a contraction
K
satisfies
KK^{*}\leq I
, the identity. We also recall that the spectral radius
r(X)
coincides with the operator norm
\|X\|
when
X\geq O.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
be
\left(\alpha,\beta\right)
-normal. Then

{{\left\|T\right\|}^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}\left\|{{T}^{2}}\right\|\text{ and }{{\left\|T\right\|}^{2}}\leq\beta\left\|{{T}^{2}}\right\|.

Indeed,

{{\left\|T\right\|}^{2}}\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{\alpha},\beta\right\}\left\|{{T}^{2}}\right\|.

Proof.

Ando
[
2
]
proved that
\left[\begin{matrix}A&X\\
{{X}^{*}}&B\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O
if and only if there exists a contraction
K
such that
X={{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}}K{{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}}
. It has been revealed in the proof of Theorem
3.3
that
\left[\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|&\left|T\right|\\
\left|T\right|&\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left|T\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O
. Therefore, there exists a contraction
K
such that
\left|T\right|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}K{{\left|T\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\geq O
.
So, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\;\left|T\right|\;\right\|

\displaystyle=\left\|T\right\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left\|{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}K{{\left|T\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}r\left({{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}K{{\left|T\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\quad\text{(since $r\left(X\right)=\left\|X\right\|$ for positive $X$)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}r\left(K{{\left|T\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\quad\text{(since $r\left(XY\right)=r\left(YX\right)$)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left\|K{{\left|T\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|\quad\text{(since $r\left(X\right)\leq\left\|X\right\|$)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left\|K\right\|\left\|{{\left|T\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|\quad\text{(by the submultiplicative property of usual operator norm)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left\|{{\left|T\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|\quad\text{(since $K$ is contraction)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}{{\left\|\left|T\right|\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\right\|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\quad\text{(by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{4}{}{}, Theorem IX.2.1]})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left\|{{T}^{2}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The second inequality comes from
\left[\begin{matrix}\sqrt{\beta}\left|T\right|&\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\\
\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|&\sqrt{\beta}\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\\
\end{matrix}\right]\geq O

and the same method as above. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

We will give another method to prove Theorem
3.5
. The operator inequality

\left|T\right|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left(\left|T\right|\sharp\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\right)

implies the following norm inequality

\left\|T\right\|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left\|\left|T\right|\sharp\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|\right\|.

But, for any positive operators
A,B
, we know that

\left\|A\sharp B\right\|\leq\left\|{{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{{B}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|,

i.e.,

\left\|T\right\|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left\|{{\left|T\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{{\left|{{T}^{*}}\right|}^{\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\left\|{{T}^{2}}\right\|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

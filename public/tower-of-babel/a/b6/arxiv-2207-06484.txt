A Unified Recovery of Structured Signals Using Atomic Norm
Xuemei Chen

Abstract
In many applications we seek to recover signals from linear measurements far fewer than the ambient dimension, given the signals have exploitable structures such as sparse vectors or low rank matrices. In this paper we work in a general setting where signals are approximately sparse in a so called atomic set. We provide general recovery results stating that a convex programming can stably and robustly recover signals if the null space of the sensing map satisfies certain properties. Moreover, we argue that such null space property can be satisfied with high probability if each measurement is subgaussian even when the number of measurements are very few. Some new results for recovering signals sparse in a frame, and recovering low rank matrices are also derived as a result.

Remark 2.3

In the setting of Example

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|h\|_{\mathcal{W}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\|a\|_{\mathcal{W}}+\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq\|a_{s}\|_{\mathcal{W}}+\|a-a_{s}\|_{\mathcal{W}}+\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{W}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\|a_{s}\|_{\mathcal{W}}+\frac{1+\beta_{\mathcal{W}}}{\beta_{\mathcal{W}}}\sigma+\frac{1}{\beta_{\mathcal{W}}}\|a_{s}\|_{\mathcal{W}}+\frac{1+\beta_{\mathcal{W}}}{\beta_{\mathcal{W}}}\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{W}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+\beta_{\mathcal{W}}}{\beta_{\mathcal{W}}}\left[\frac{(1+\beta_{\mathcal{W}})\rho}{\beta_{\mathcal{W}}-\rho}\sigma+\frac{\rho}{\beta_{\mathcal{W}}-\rho}\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{W}}\right]+\frac{1+\beta_{\mathcal{W}}}{\beta_{\mathcal{W}}}(\sigma+\|\eta\|_{\mathcal{W}})

Definition 3.1
.

The atomic set

To understand the above definition better, we see that
\displaystyle\|\sum_{j\in J}c_{j}w_{j}\|_{\mathcal{W}}=|J|
is equivalent to
\displaystyle\sum_{j\in J}\frac{c_{j}}{|J|}w_{j}
being on the boundary of
\displaystyle\text{conv}(\mathcal{W}_{\text{sym}})
. On the convex geometry level, this is requiring every subset of
\displaystyle s
(or less) extreme points (the set
\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\text{sym}}

be on
a face of the polytope. If we require every subset of
\displaystyle s
extreme points to
form
a face of the polytope, this is the so called
\displaystyle s
-neighborly
polytope
[
3
]
.The remark below argues that the
\displaystyle s

\displaystyle s

Remark 3.2

In the context of Example

\displaystyle s
-even is for the matrix

Lemma 3.3

(
[
15
, Lemma 2.3]
)
.

Suppose
\displaystyle t<N
are integers. There exists a family
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}
of subsets of
\displaystyle[N]
such that:

1.

Every set in
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}
consists of exactly
\displaystyle t

2.

For all
\displaystyle I,J\in\mathcal{U}
with
\displaystyle I\neq J

\displaystyle|I\cap J|<\frac{t}{2}
.

3.

\displaystyle|\mathcal{U}|\geq\left(\frac{N}{4t}\right)^{t/2}
.

The above lemma is a famous combinatorial result, which is essential in proving both Theorem
3.4
and Theorem
3.5
. Theorem
3.4
focuses on the case where we have exact recovery of sparse signals from its precise measurement (no noise) via (

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{W}=\{w_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
be
\displaystyle s
-even. If for every
\displaystyle z_{0}\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{W},s}

\displaystyle y=\mathcal{A}z_{0}

where

W

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{U}
be a family of subsets of
\displaystyle[N]
given by Lemma
3.3
with
\displaystyle t=\lfloor s/2\rfloor
. For each
\displaystyle I\in\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle z_{I}:=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{i\in I}w_{i}

\displaystyle|I|=t

\displaystyle\|z_{I}\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq 1

For
\displaystyle I\neq J

\displaystyle z_{I}-z_{J}=\frac{1}{t}(\sum_{i\in I-J}w_{i}-\sum_{j\in J-I}w_{j})

\displaystyle|I-J|+|J-I|=|I|+|J|-2|I\cap J|

3.3

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}
is
\displaystyle s
-even,

\|z_{i}-z_{j}\|_{\mathcal{W}}=\frac{1}{t}(|I-J|+|J-I|)>1.

(28)

We claim that
\displaystyle\{\mathcal{A}(z_{I}+\frac{1}{2}B_{\mathcal{W}}),I\in\mathcal{U}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

Otherwise there exist

\displaystyle v,v^{\prime}\in B_{\mathcal{W}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(z_{I}+\frac{1}{2}v)=\mathcal{A}(z_{J}+\frac{1}{2}v^{\prime})

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(z_{I}-z_{J})=\mathcal{A}(\frac{1}{2}v^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}v)

\displaystyle z_{I}-z_{J}\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{W},s}

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\mathcal{U}|\mathrm{vol}(\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{A}B_{\mathcal{W}})))\leq\mathrm{vol}(\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{A}B_{\mathcal{W}}).

Taking log of both sides yields

4
Subgaussian Measurements

In this section we show that
\displaystyle\mathcal{W}
-sparse vectors can be recovered from few random measurements via (

W
,
ϵ

). The arguments presented here are similar to those in
[
5
]

Definition 4.1
.

The
Gaussian width
of a set
\displaystyle S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined as

w(S):=\mathbb{E}\sup_{x\in S}\langle g,x\rangle,

where
\displaystyle g\sim N(0,I_{d})
is a standard Gaussian random vector.

Definition 4.2
.

A random vector
\displaystyle{\varphi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called a
subgaussian vector
with parameters
\displaystyle(\alpha,\sigma)
if it satisfies the following.

(1)

It is centered, that is,
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[{\varphi}]=0
.

(2)

There exists a positive
\displaystyle\alpha
such that
\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\langle{\varphi},z\rangle|\right]\geq\alpha
for every
\displaystyle z\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

(3)

There exists a positive
\displaystyle\sigma
such that
\displaystyle\Pr\left(|\langle{\varphi},z\rangle|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp(-\frac{t^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}})
for every
\displaystyle z\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

The proof of the following lemma can be found in
[
26
, Section 6.5]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

If
\displaystyle{f}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a subgaussian vector with parameters
\displaystyle(\alpha,\sigma)
, then

\Pr\left[|\langle{x},{f}\rangle|\geq t\right]\geq\frac{(\alpha-t)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}

for any
\displaystyle 0<t<\alpha
and
\displaystyle{x}\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.

If
\displaystyle\{{\varphi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{q}
are independent copies of the random distribution
\displaystyle{\varphi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then we can define the
mean empirical width
of a set
\displaystyle S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
as

W_{q}(S;{\varphi}):=\mathbb{E}\sup_{x\in S}\bigg\langle x,\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{q}}\sum_{i=1}^{q}\varepsilon_{i}{\varphi}_{i}\bigg\rangle,

where
\displaystyle\{\varepsilon_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
are independent random variables taking values uniformly over
\displaystyle\{\pm 1\}
and are independent from everything else.

The mean empirical width
\displaystyle W_{q}(S;{\varphi})
is a distribution-dependent measure of the size of the set
\displaystyle S
.
Note that
\displaystyle W_{q}(S;{\varphi})
reduces to the usual Gaussian width
\displaystyle w(S)
when
\displaystyle{\varphi}
follows a standard Gaussian distribution.
Estimation of
\displaystyle W_{m}(S;{\varphi})
for any subgaussian vector
\displaystyle{\varphi}
is made in
[
26
]
, where
\displaystyle S
is required to be
\displaystyle\mathbb{S}^{d-1}\cup G
for some cone
\displaystyle G
. However, the bound can be relaxed to any subset
\displaystyle S
by the observation of the generic chaining bound and the majorizing measure theorem
[
27
, Theorem 2.2.18 and Theorem 2.4.1]
. We will state this as a lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

If
\displaystyle{\varphi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is subgaussian with parameters
\displaystyle(\alpha,\sigma)
and
\displaystyle S
is any subset of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then

W_{m}(S;{\varphi})\leq Cw(S)

(32)

for some universal constant
\displaystyle C
.

The constant
\displaystyle C
is a universal constant that does not rely on
the choice of subgaussian distribution. See
[
27
]
for precise computations of this constant, and
[
5
, Remark 2.7]
for the computation of
\displaystyle C
when
\displaystyle{\varphi}
is multivariate normal.

The mean empirical width appears in the following important result. This theorem was originally stated in
[
19
]
and coined as
Mendelson’s Small Ball Method
by Tropp
[
26
]
. This will be a primary tool in obtaining our main estimates.

Theorem 4.5

(
[
26
, Proposition 5.1]
, cf.
[
19
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Fix a set
\displaystyle S\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\displaystyle{\varphi}
be a random vector in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
\displaystyle A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
have rows
\displaystyle\{a_{i}^{T}\}_{i=1}^{m}
that are independent copies of
\displaystyle{\varphi}^{T}
. Define

Q_{\xi}(S;{\varphi}):=\inf_{{x}\in S}\Pr\bigg(|\langle{x},{\varphi}\rangle|\geq\xi\bigg).

Then for any
\displaystyle\xi>0
and
\displaystyle t>0
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\inf_{{x}\in S}\|A{x}\|_{2}\geq\xi\sqrt{m}Q_{2\xi}(S;{\varphi})-2W_{m}(S;{\varphi})-\xi t

(33)

with probability
\displaystyle\geq 1-e^{-t^{2}/2}
.

Let

S_{\rho}:=\{z:\|z_{s}\|_{\mathcal{W}}\geq\rho\|z-z_{s}\|_{\mathcal{W}}\}\cap\mathbb{S}^{d-1}.

It is easy to show that
\displaystyle\inf_{{x}\in S_{\rho}}\|\mathcal{A}{x}\|_{2}>0
implies
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
having the stable
\displaystyle\mathcal{W}
-
\displaystyle\rho
-NSP. We let
\displaystyle A
be the matrix representation of the map
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}
under an orthonormal basis.

Theorem 4.6
.

Assume
\displaystyle{\varphi}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a subgaussian vector with parameters
\displaystyle(\alpha,\sigma)
. If
\displaystyle A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
is a measurement matrix with rows that are independent copies of
\displaystyle{\varphi}^{T}
and that the number of measurements satisfies

m\geq\frac{4^{8}\sigma^{4}}{\alpha^{6}}C^{2}w^{2}(S_{\rho}),

then with probability at least

1-\exp\left({-m\dfrac{\alpha^{4}}{64^{2}\sigma^{4}}}\right),

we have

\displaystyle\inf_{{x}\in S_{\rho}}\|\mathcal{A}{x}\|_{2}\geq Cw(S_{\rho}).

(34)

Proof.

We first apply Lemma
4.4
and Theorem
4.5

\inf_{{x}\in S_{\rho}}\|A{x}\|_{2}\geq\xi\sqrt{m}Q_{2\xi}(S_{\rho};\,{\varphi})-2Cw(S_{\rho})-\xi t.

(35)

By Lemma
4.3
, provided we choose
\displaystyle\xi<\alpha/2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle Q_{2\xi}(S_{\rho};{\varphi})=\inf_{{x}\in S_{\rho}}\Pr\left(|\langle{x},{\varphi}\rangle|\geq 2\xi\right)\geq\frac{(\alpha-2\xi)^{2}}{4\sigma^{2}}.

Placing the bound for
\displaystyle Q_{2\xi}(S_{\rho};{\varphi})
into (

\displaystyle\xi=\alpha/4
gives

\inf_{{x}\in S_{\rho}}\|A{x}\|_{2}\geq\frac{\alpha^{3}\sqrt{m}}{4^{3}\sigma^{2}}-2Cw(S_{\rho})-\frac{\alpha t}{4}:=a-b-\frac{\alpha t}{4}

(36)

Picking
\displaystyle m
and
\displaystyle t
to satisfy
\displaystyle a\geq 2b
and

gives

\inf_{x\in S_{\rho}}\|A{x}\|_{2}\geq a-b-(a-b)/2=(a-b)/2\geq b/2=Cw(S_{\rho}).

All that is left is to rewrite these conditions in terms of
\displaystyle m
and
\displaystyle t

a\geq 2b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{\alpha^{3}}{4^{3}\sigma^{2}}\sqrt{m}\geq 4Cw(S_{\rho})\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad m\geq\frac{4^{8}\sigma^{4}C^{2}}{\alpha^{6}}w^{2}(S_{\rho})

and

\frac{\alpha}{4}t=\frac{a-b}{2}\geq\frac{a}{4}=\frac{\alpha}{4^{4}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sigma}\right)^{2}\sqrt{m}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad t\geq\frac{1}{64}\sqrt{m}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sigma}\right)^{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad-\dfrac{t^{2}}{2}\leq-m\dfrac{\alpha^{4}}{64^{2}\sigma^{4}},

proving the result.
∎

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle\displaystyle<\|z_{I}-z_{J}\|_{\mathcal{W}}=\|z_{I}+\rho v-\Delta(\mathcal{A}(z_{I}+\rho v))-z_{J}+\Delta(\mathcal{A}(z_{J}+\rho v^{\prime}))-\rho v+\rho v^{\prime}-\rho v^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{W}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq\|z_{I}+\rho v-\Delta(\mathcal{A}(z_{I}+\rho v))\|_{\mathcal{W}}+\|\rho v\|_{\mathcal{W}}+\|\rho v^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{W}}+\|\Delta(\mathcal{A}(z_{J}+\rho v^{\prime}))-(z_{J}+\rho v^{\prime})\|_{\mathcal{W}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leq C\sigma_{\mathcal{W},s}(z_{I}+\rho v)+2\rho+C\sigma_{\mathcal{W},s}(z_{J}+\rho v^{\prime})\leq C\|\rho v\|_{\mathcal{W}}+2\rho+C\|\rho v^{\prime}\|_{\mathcal{W}}\leq 1.

It is easy to see that
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(z_{I}+\rho B_{\mathcal{W}})\subset(1+\rho)\mathcal{A}B_{\mathcal{W}}
for any
\displaystyle I\in\mathcal{U}
. So

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\mathcal{U}|\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{A}(\rho(B_{\mathcal{W}})))\leq\mathrm{vol}((1+\rho)\mathcal{A}B_{\mathcal{W}})

(53)

Let
\displaystyle\mathcal{A}B_{\mathcal{W}}
have dimension
\displaystyle r\leq m
, so (
53
) becomes

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\frac{N}{4t}\right)^{t/2}\rho^{r}\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{A}B_{\mathcal{W}})\leq(1+\rho)^{r}\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{A}B_{\mathcal{W}})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Rightarrow

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\frac{N}{4t}\right)^{t/2}\leq(1+\frac{1}{\rho})^{r}\leq(2C+3)^{m}.

Taking log of both sides yields

m\ln(2C+3)\geq\frac{t}{2}\ln\frac{N}{4t}\geq\frac{s/2-1}{2}\ln\frac{N}{2s}\geq\frac{s}{8}\ln\frac{N}{2s},

if
\displaystyle s>2
.
∎

Lemma 7.5
.

The frame
\displaystyle\{f_{n}=(\cos\frac{2\pi(n-1)}{8},\sin\frac{2\pi(n-1)}{8})\}_{n=1}^{8}
of
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{2}
is 1-splittable with constant
\displaystyle\sqrt{2}-1
.

Proof.

We need to prove that

\|x+y\|_{\mathcal{F}}\geq\|x_{1}\|_{\mathcal{F}}-\|y_{1}\|_{\mathcal{F}}+(\sqrt{2}-1)(\|y-y_{1}\|_{\mathcal{F}}-\|x-x_{1}\|_{\mathcal{F}}).

(54)

Recall
\displaystyle x_{1}=\argmin\limits_{v\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{F},1}}\|x-v\|_{\mathcal{F}}
.

First, we notice that given any
\displaystyle a\in[0,1]
, we have
\displaystyle\|af_{n}+(1-a)f_{n+1}\|_{\mathcal{F}}=1
for any
\displaystyle n
since
\displaystyle af_{n}+(1-a)f_{n+1}
is on the boundary of the convex hull. Therefore with scaling, we have

\|af_{n}+bf_{n+1}\|_{\mathcal{F}}=a+b,\quad\text{for any }a\geq 0,b\geq 0.

(55)

Next, we establish that

\|af_{1}+bf_{3}\|_{\mathcal{F}}=\max\{|a|,|b|\}+\beta\min\{|a|,|b|\}\geq|a|+\beta|b|,

(56)

where
\displaystyle\beta=\sqrt{2}-1
. The inequality is a straightforward calculation. For the equality, due to symmetry, we only need to prove the case when
\displaystyle a>b>0
. In this case,
\displaystyle x=af_{1}+bf_{3}=af_{1}+b(\frac{2}{\sqrt{2}}f_{2}-f_{1})=(a-b)f_{1}+\frac{2b}{\sqrt{2}}f_{2}
. By (
55
), we have
\displaystyle\|x\|_{\mathcal{F}}=a-b+\sqrt{2}b=a+\beta b
. Note that (
56
) is also true if the vector is
\displaystyle af_{2}+bf_{4}
since these two vectors are also orthogonal.

Third, we will show for
\displaystyle x=af_{1}+bf_{3}=\frac{a+b}{\sqrt{2}}f_{2}+\frac{b-a}{\sqrt{2}}f_{4}
where
\displaystyle a>b>0
,

\begin{array}[]{lll}&x_{1}=af_{1},&\text{if }a>b/\beta,\\
&x_{1}=\frac{a+b}{\sqrt{2}}f_{2},&\text{if }a\leq b/\beta.\end{array}

(57)

The idea is that to find the best 1-sparse approximation of
\displaystyle x
, we simply find which frame vector is closest to
\displaystyle x
(biggest inner product). In the case of
\displaystyle a>\beta b
,
\displaystyle x
is closest to
\displaystyle f_{1}
, so the best 1-sparse approximation of
\displaystyle x
is
\displaystyle af_{1}
. To prove this case rigorously, we can compute that other 1-sparse approximations are providing bigger tails. If we try to take out a portion of
\displaystyle f_{2}
, then
\displaystyle\min_{c}\|x-cf_{2}\|_{\mathcal{F}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{equ:f1f3}}}{{=}}\|x-\frac{a+b}{\sqrt{2}}f_{2}\|_{\mathcal{F}}=\frac{a-b}{\sqrt{2}}>b
. Similarly,
\displaystyle\min_{c}\|x-cf_{2}\|_{\mathcal{F}}=a>b
, and
\displaystyle\min_{c}\|x-cf_{4}\|_{\mathcal{F}}=\frac{a+b}{\sqrt{2}}>b
. The
\displaystyle a\leq{b/\beta}
case in (
57
) can be shown similarly.

A byproduct of the above argument is that for
\displaystyle x=af_{1}+bf_{3}
,

\|x_{1}\|_{\mathcal{F}}\geq\max\{|a|,|b|\}.

(58)

Finally, to prove (
54
), we assume, without loss of generality, that
\displaystyle x=af_{1}+bf_{3}
with
\displaystyle a>{b/\beta}>0
, and
\displaystyle y=cf_{1}+df_{3}
with arbitrary
\displaystyle c,d
. Therefore
\displaystyle\|x_{1}\|_{\mathcal{F}}=a
, and
\displaystyle\|x-x_{1}\|_{\mathcal{F}}=b
. So

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|x+y\|_{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\|(a+c)f_{1}+(b+d)f_{3}\|_{\mathcal{F}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{equ:f1f3}}}{{\geq}}|a+c|+\beta|b+d|

\displaystyle\displaystyle\geq a-|c|+\beta(|d|-b)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{equ:x1}}}{{\geq}}\|x_{1}\|_{\mathcal{F}}-\|y_{1}\|_{\mathcal{F}}+\beta(\|y-y_{1}\|_{\mathcal{F}}-\|x-x_{1}\|_{\mathcal{F}})

as desired.
∎

Note that the equality in (
54
) can be achieved, for example, at
\displaystyle x=6f_{1}+f_{3},y=-5f_{1}-2f_{3}
.

Lemma 7.7
.

For any
\displaystyle Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n_{1}\times n_{2}}
,

\min_{V\in\Sigma_{\mathcal{M},s}}\left(\|Z-V\|_{*}-\|V\|_{*}\right)=\|Z-Z_{s}\|_{*}-\|Z_{s}\|_{*}=-\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sigma_{i}(Z)+\sum_{i=s+1}^{K}\sigma_{i}(Z).

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\sigma_{i}
be the singular values of
\displaystyle Z
.

\displaystyle\|Z-V\|_{*}-\|V\|_{*}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Lemma\ \ref{lem:sv}}}{{\geq}}\sum_{i=1}^{K}|\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{i}(V)|-\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sigma_{i}(V)=\sum_{i=1}^{s}|\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{i}(V)|+\sum_{i=s+1}^{K}\sigma_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sigma_{i}(V)
.

It is easy to show that
\displaystyle\min_{c\in\mathbb{R}}(|\sigma-c|-c)=-\sigma
, therefore we have

\|Z-V\|_{*}-\|V\|_{*}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{s}(|\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{i}(V)|-\sigma_{i}(V))+\sum_{i=s+1}^{K}\sigma_{i}\geq-\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sigma_{i}+\sum_{i=s+1}^{K}\sigma_{i}.

∎

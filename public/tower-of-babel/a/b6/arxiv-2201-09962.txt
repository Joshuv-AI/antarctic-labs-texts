BPS cohomology for rank 2 degree 0 Higgs bundles (and more)
Sebastian Schlegel Mejia

Abstract
. We give a formula comparing the E-series of the moduli stacks of rank 2 degree 0 semistable Higgs bundles in genus g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 to intersection E-polynomials of its coarse moduli space. A parallel formula holds in various 2-Calabi–Yau settings, for example for sheaves on K3 surfaces, or preprojective algebras of g g -loop quivers. As a consequence we provide evidence for a conjecture of Davison on the BPS cohomology of Higgs bundles, which has implications for non-abelian Hodge theory for stacks. We apply the formula to cohomological χ \chi -independence tests for BPS cohomology of Higgs bundles and K3 surfaces.

1.1.
Cohomology of moduli spaces of
rank 2 Higgs bundles

Let
C
be a smooth connected projective complex curve
of genus
g\geq 2
.
Let

\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Dol}}_{r,d}

be the moduli stack of rank
r
degree
d
semistable Higgs bundles on
C

and let

p_{r,d}\colon\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Dol}}_{r,d}\to\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Dol}}_{r,d}

be its coarse moduli space.

When
\gcd(r,d)=1
, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Dol}}_{r,d}
and
moduli stack
\mathfrak{M}_{r,d}^{\mathrm{Dol}}
are smooth
and the morphism
p_{r,d}
is the trivial
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe over
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Dol}}_{r,d}

p_{r,d}\colon\mathfrak{M}_{r,d}^{\mathrm{Dol}}=\mathcal{M}_{r,d}^{\mathrm{Dol}}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{r,d}^{\mathrm{Dol}}.

By the Künneth formula, the compactly supported cohomology of the moduli stack is determined from the compactly supported cohomology of the moduli space. The latter has been studied extensively (see for example
[
GHS14
]
).

When
\gcd(r,d)\neq 1
, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{r,d}^{\mathrm{Dol}}
and moduli

\mathfrak{M}_{r,d}^{\mathrm{Dol}}
are singular
and the morphism
p_{r,d}\colon\mathfrak{M}_{r,d}^{\mathrm{Dol}}\to\mathcal{M}_{r,d}^{\mathrm{Dol}}
is more complicated than in the coprime case.
In the literature the cohomology of the moduli stack and moduli space have been
studied separately.

The compactly supported intersection cohomology of the moduli spaces
IH_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{r,d}^{\mathrm{Dol}})

has been determined for rank 2 and degree 0
in genus
2
by Felisetti
[
Fel21
]

and for arbitrary genus
g\geq 2
by Mauri

[
Mau21
]
.
Building on work of Schiffmann
[
Sch16
]
and Mozgovoy–Schiffmann
[
MS20a
]
on point counts of the stacks
\mathfrak{M}_{r,d}^{\mathrm{Dol}}
over finite fields, Fedorov–Soibelman–Soibelman in
[
FSS19
]

find a formula for the class
[\mathfrak{M}_{r,d}^{\mathrm{Dol}}]
in the
Grothendieck ring of stacks
K_{0}(\mathbf{St})
.

Set
d=0
, guaranteeing
\gcd(r,d)\neq 1
for
r>1
. Consider the
compactly supported cohomology (with rational coefficients) for all ranks

\mathcal{A}_{0}=\bigoplus_{r\geq 0}H_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Dol}}_{r,0})\otimes\mathbb{L}^{(1-g)r^{2}}

as an object in
the symmetric monoidal abelian category of (graded) mixed Hodge structures.
Here
\mathbb{L}=H_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{A}^{1})
is the mixed Hodge structure
given by the compactly supported cohomology of the affine line.

Motivated by cohomological Donaldson–Thomas theory
and non-abelian Hodge theory
for stacks
Davison conjectured the following for

Conjecture 1.1

(
[
Dav21
, Conjecture 5.6]
)
.

There is an isomorphism of mixed Hodge structures

\mathcal{A}_{0}\cong\operatorname{Sym}\bigg(\operatorname{FreeLie}\bigg(\bigoplus_{r\geq 1}IH_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Dol}}_{r,0})\otimes\mathbb{L}^{(1-g)r^{2}-1}\bigg)\otimes H_{c}^{\bullet}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})\otimes\mathbb{L}\bigg)

where
\operatorname{Sym}
is taken in the graded sense and

\operatorname{FreeLie}(V^{\bullet})
denotes the free graded
Lie algebra generated by
the graded (super) vector space
V^{\bullet}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Conjecture 4.6 in
[
Dav21
]
is the corresponding conjecture on the other side of non-abelian Hodge theory. Conjecture 7.7 in
[
Dav20
]
is a related conjecture in the setting of preprojective algebras of quivers.

The original motivation for this work was to
provide evidence towards this conjecture.
Conjecture
1.1
predicts the following formula for the
compactly supported cohomology of the moduli stack of rank 2
degree 0 Higgs bundles.

Theorem 1.3
.

There is an isomorphism of graded mixed Hodge structures

\begin{split}H_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Dol}}_{2,0})\otimes\mathbb{L}^{4-4g}&\cong IH_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Dol}}_{2,0})\otimes\mathbb{L}^{3-4g}\otimes H_{c}^{\bullet}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})\otimes\mathbb{L}\\
&\qquad\oplus\Lambda^{2}(H_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}_{1,0}^{\mathrm{Dol}})\otimes\mathbb{L}^{-g})\otimes H_{c}^{\bullet}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})\otimes\mathbb{L}\\
&\qquad\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(H_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}_{1,0}^{\mathrm{Dol}})\otimes\mathbb{L}^{-g}\otimes H_{c}^{\bullet}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})\otimes\mathbb{L}).\end{split}

where the alternating square
\Lambda^{2}
and the symmetric square
\operatorname{Sym}^{2}

are taken in the symmetric monoidal abelian category of graded mixed Hodge structures.

Let
q=E(\mathbb{L})
. We deduce Theorem
1.3
from the following equality of E-series
(see Corollary
1.11
).

Theorem 1.4
.

\begin{split}\frac{E(\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{Dol}}_{2,0})}{q^{4g-4}}&=\frac{1}{q^{4g-3}}E(IH_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}_{2,0}^{\mathrm{Dol}}))E(H_{c}^{\bullet}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})\otimes\mathbb{L})\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{q^{2g}}E(\Lambda^{2}(H_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}_{1,0}^{\mathrm{Dol}})))E(H_{c}^{\bullet}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})\otimes\mathbb{L})\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{q^{2g}}E(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(H_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}_{1,0}^{\mathrm{Dol}})\otimes H_{c}^{\bullet}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})\otimes\mathbb{L}))\end{split}

Remark 1.5
.

The RHS can be made more explicit using
E(H_{c}^{\bullet}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})\otimes\mathbb{L})=q/q-1
and Lemma
2.8

1.3.
BPS cohomology

As mentioned, the motivation for Conjecture
1.1

comes from
cohomological Donaldson–Thomas theory for 2CY-categories.
In particular Conjecture
1.1
can be interpreted as a statement about the BPS-cohomology for Higgs bundles.
In this section we give a short introduction to BPS-cohomology of 2CY-categories.

The foundations of cohomological Donaldson–Thomas theory for 2-Calabi–Yau categories have been laid out in
[
Dav21a
,
Dav16a
,
Dav20
,
Dav21
,
Dav16
,
KK21
]

and we refer the reader to these papers for more details.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a 2-Calabi–Yau (2CY) abelian category,
i.e.,
there are natural non-degenerate graded-symmetric pairings

(1)

\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathscr{C}}^{\bullet}(E,F)\times\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathscr{C}}^{2-\bullet}(F,E)\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}

for all
E,F\in\mathscr{C}
.

By the 2CY-property, the Euler pairing
\chi(E,F)=\chi(\operatorname{Ext}_{\mathscr{C}}^{\bullet}(E,F))
,
for
E,F\in\mathscr{C}
,
defines a symmetric pairing on the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathscr{C})
.
Assume the Euler pairing is the pullback along a group homomorphism
\gamma\colon K_{0}(\mathscr{C})\to\mathbb{Z}^{n}

of a symmetric bilinear form on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,
which by abuse of notation we denote by
\chi
.

Let
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be a primitive element.
For every
r\geq 0
let
\mathfrak{M}_{rv}
be
the moduli stack of objects in
E\in\mathscr{C}
of class
rv

, that is,
\gamma(E)=rv
.

We require a notion of
semistable
objects in the category
\mathscr{C}
, which is additive in short exact sequences: if
E^{\prime}\hookrightarrow E\rightarrow\mathrel{\mkern-14.0mu}\rightarrow E^{\prime\prime}
is a short exact sequence with
E^{\prime},E^{\prime\prime}
semistable, then
E
is also semistable.
Imposing semistability must define open substacks of finite type

\mathfrak{M}_{rv}^{\mathrm{ss}}\subseteq\mathfrak{M}_{rv}
that admit a good moduli space

\mathfrak{M}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{rv}\to\mathcal{M}_{rv}^{\mathrm{ss}}
and have virtual dimension
\operatorname{vdim}(\mathfrak{M}_{rv}^{\mathrm{ss}})=r^{2}\chi(v,v)
.

The total cohomology

\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{C},v}^{\mathrm{ss}}=\bigoplus_{r\geq 0}H_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{M}_{rv}^{\mathrm{ss}})\otimes\mathbb{L}^{\operatorname{vdim}(\mathfrak{M}_{rv}^{\mathrm{ss}})/2}

is an important object of study in the subject of cohomological Donaldson–Thomas theory.

Definition 1.8
.

We say that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{C},v}^{ss}

satisfies
the cohomological integrality conjecture
if there
exists mixed Hodge structures
\mathrm{BPS}_{rv}

of finite total dimension
and an isomorphism of

\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
-graded mixed Hodge structures

\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{C},v}^{\mathrm{ss}}\cong\operatorname{Sym}\bigg(\bigoplus_{r\geq 1}\mathrm{BPS}_{rv}\otimes H_{c}^{\bullet}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})\otimes\mathbb{L}\bigg).

The graded mixed Hodge structure
\bigoplus_{r\geq 1}\mathrm{BPS}_{rv}
is
the
BPS cohomology
.

Remark 1.9
.

The cohomological integrality conjecture is known in
Setting
1
by
[
KK21
]
,
Setting
2
by
[
Dav16
]
, and
Setting
4
by
[
Dav16a
]
.

Theorem
1.6
can be rephrased as a formula for the E-polynomial of BPS cohomology.

Corollary 1.10
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}_{\mathscr{C},v}^{\mathrm{ss}}
satisfies the cohomological integrality conjecture. Then

E(\mathrm{BPS}_{2v})=\frac{E(IH_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{ss}}))}{q^{4g-3}}+E(\Lambda^{2}(H_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\mathrm{ss}})\otimes\mathbb{L}^{-g})).

Moreover, assuming purity of
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{\mathscr{C},v}
, we can upgrade
the statement for E-polynomials to a statement for cohomology.

Corollary 1.11
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{ss}}_{\mathscr{C},v}
satisfies the cohomological integrality conjecture.
If
H_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{ss}})
is pure, then there is a
canonical isomorphism of
mixed Hodge structures

\begin{split}H_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{ss}})\otimes\mathbb{L}^{4-4g}&\cong IH_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{ss}})\otimes\mathbb{L}^{3-4g}\otimes H_{c}^{\bullet}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})\otimes\mathbb{L}\\
&\qquad\oplus\Lambda^{2}(H_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\mathrm{s}}))\otimes H_{c}^{\bullet}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})\otimes\mathbb{L}\\
&\qquad\oplus\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(H_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\mathrm{s}})\otimes H_{c}^{\bullet}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})\otimes\mathbb{L}),\end{split}

equivalently, there is a canonical isomorphism

\mathrm{BPS}_{2v}\cong IH_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{ss}})\otimes\mathbb{L}^{3-4g}\oplus\Lambda^{2}(H_{c}(\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\mathrm{ss}}\otimes\mathbb{L}^{-g})).

Proof.

By the cohomological integrality theorem
H_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{M}_{2}^{\mathrm{ss}})\otimes\mathbb{L}^{4-4g}
must be the
second graded piece of

\operatorname{Sym}(\bigoplus_{r\geq 1}\mathrm{BPS}_{rv}\otimes H_{c}^{\bullet}(B\mathbb{G}_{m})\otimes\mathbb{L})
.
Thus by

[
Dav21a
, Theorem 6.6]

there is a canonical inclusion
of the RHS into

. Purity and equality of E-polynomials (Theorem

) implies that the inclusion is in
fact an isomorphism of mixed Hodge structures.
∎

1.4.
Notation

We work over the complex numbers.
For an algebraic group
G
acting
on a scheme
X
denote by
X/G
the quotient stack.

We always take (compactly supported) cohomology and intersection cohomology with rational coefficients.
We overload the notation
\mathbb{L}
which denotes the mixed Hodge structure

H_{c}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{A}^{1})
or the class
[\mathbb{A}^{1}]
in the Grothendieck ring of varieties.
The E-polynomial
q\coloneqq E(\mathbb{L})=uv
is unambiguous.

2.
E-series

2.1.
E-series of mixed Hodge structures

A
(cohomologically graded) mixed Hodge structure

is a triple
H=(H^{\bullet},W,F)
consisting of

•

a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space
H
over
\mathbb{Q}

•

an increasing
weight filtration

W
on the graded vector space
H

•

a decreasing
Hodge filtration

F

on the complexified graded vector space
H_{\mathbb{C}}

such that the filtration
F
on the complexification of each
associated graded piece
W_{k}H/W_{k-1}H
endows said piece with a rational weight
k
pure Hodge structure.

A cohomologically graded mixed Hodge structure
H
is said to be a
(cohomologically graded) pure Hodge structure
if
H^{n}=(H^{n},W\cap H^{n},F\cap H^{n})
is a (non-graded) pure Hodge structure of weight
n
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Henceforth all Hodge structures are assumed
to be cohomologically graded.

For every cohomologically graded mixed Hodge structure
H=(H^{\bullet},W,F)
such that

(2)

\dim(\operatorname{Gr}_{F}^{p}\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{p+q}H^{n})<\infty\text{, }H^{n}=0\text{ for }n\gg 0,\text{ and }\operatorname{Gr}_{p}^{W}H^{n}=0\text{ for }p>n

we define the
E-series
of
H
to be

E(H)=\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{n}\dim(\operatorname{Gr}_{F}^{p}\operatorname{Gr}^{W}_{p+q}H^{n})u^{p}v^{q}\in\mathbb{Z}((u^{-1},v^{-1})).

If
H
is finite dimensional, then
E(H)
is a Laurent-polynomial
in
u,v
and so we call it the
E-polynomial
of
H
.

Remark 2.1
.

The boundedness condition (
2
) is satisfied for the compactly supported cohomology for all finite type Artin stacks with affine stabilisers
[
Dav20
, Lemma 4.6]
. Moreover it guarantees that the E-series is well-defined.

Let
\mathbf{MHS}^{-}
be the category of
mixed Hodge structures

H=(H^{\bullet},W,F)
satisfying (
2
).
It is a symmetric monoidal abelian category.
The E-series defines a ring homomorphism

E\colon K_{0}(\mathbf{MHS}^{-})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}((u^{-1},v^{-1})).

2.2.
E-series of varieties

For every finite type separated scheme
X
over
\mathbb{C}

its compactly supported cohomology
H_{c}^{\bullet}(X)
is endowed with a mixed Hodge structure by

[
Del71
,
Del74
]
.
The
E-polynomial
of a finite type separated scheme
X

is the E-polynomial of its compactly supported cohomology

E(X)=E(H_{c}^{\bullet}(X)).

The E-polynomial is a motivic invariant: for every closed subscheme
Z\subseteq X
we have
E(X)=E(Z)+E(X\smallsetminus Z)
and
E(X\times Y)=E(X)E(Y)
.

Example 2.2
.

q\coloneqq E(\mathbb{L})=uv
,
E(\mathbb{G}_{m})=q-1
,
E(\mathbb{P}^{1})=q+1
,
E(\mathrm{GL}_{2})=q(q+1)(q-1)^{2}
, and
E(\mathrm{GL}_{n})=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}(q^{n}-q^{i})
.

2.3.
E-series of stacks

There are two equivalent approaches to defining
the E-series of a quotient stack.
The first uses the Grothendieck ring of stacks.
The second uses an algebro-geometric approximation of the Borel construction
to define a mixed Hodge
structure on the compactly supported cohomology of a quotient stack,
see
[
Dav21a
, Section 2.3]
for details.
We take the approach
via the Grothendieck ring of stacks.

Let
K_{0}(\mathbf{Var})
and
K_{0}(\mathbf{St})
, denote the
Grothendieck ring of varieties and
finite type Artin stacks with affine stabilizers, respectively.
See

[
Eke09
,
FSS19
,
GHS14
]

for introductions to these rings.
Let
\mathbb{L}=[\mathbb{A}^{1}]
be the class of the affine line.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
Eke09
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

The classes
\mathbb{L}=[\mathbb{G}_{a}]
,
[\mathrm{GL}_{n}]
in
K_{0}(\mathbf{St})
are invertible with inverses
[B\mathbb{G}_{a}]
, resp.,
[B\mathrm{GL}_{n}]
,
and the natural inclusion

\mathfrak{I}\colon{K_{0}}(\mathbf{Var})[\mathbb{L}^{-1},[\mathrm{GL}_{n}]^{-1}]\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow K_{0}(\mathbf{St})

is an isomorphism of rings.

We use this isomorphism to define the E-series of
a finite type stack with affine stabilizers.

Definition 2.4
.

By the Künneth formula and
the long exact sequence in compactly supported cohomology,
the E-polynomial of a variety defines a ring homomorphism

E\colon K_{0}(\mathbf{Var})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}[u,v],

which induces the

E-series
homomorphism

E=E\circ\mathfrak{I}^{-1}\colon K_{0}(\mathbf{St})\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}[u,v][(uv)^{-1},E(\mathrm{GL}_{n})^{-1}]\subseteq\mathbb{Z}((u^{-1},v^{-1})),

where we overload
E
in our notation.

Example 2.5

We aim to explicitly check
E(\mathrm{BPS}_{2,1}^{\mathrm{Dol}})=E(\mathrm{BPS}_{2,0}^{\mathrm{Dol}})
, which is already known by
[
KK21
, Corollary 5.15]
(where cohomological
\chi
-independence is shown in general for Gopakumar–Vafa invariants of local curves).

4.1.1.
Rank 2 degree 0 BPS cohomology

Combining Theorem
1.4
and the computation of the E-polynomial of the stable locus
E(\mathcal{M}_{2,0}^{\mathrm{Dol},\mathrm{s}})
in
[
KY08
, Theorem 3.7]
we determine the E-polynomial
E(\mathrm{BPS}_{2,0}^{\mathrm{Dol}})
in terms of the genus
g
.
We then simplify the expression to make apparent the equality to
E(\mathrm{BPS}_{2,1}^{\mathrm{Dol}})
, which is determined below.

Note that the E-polynomial in
[
KY08
, Theorem 3.7]

is for the stable locus of the
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
-Higgs bundles moduli space. We apply the method explained in

[
Mau21
, Section 4.2]
to convert the the E-polynomial from the
\operatorname{SL}_{2}
-Higgs bundles case to the
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-Higgs bundles case that we use.

Let
\operatorname{Jac}(C)
be the Jacobian of the curve
C
. As a first step
write
E(\mathcal{M}_{2,0}^{\mathrm{Dol},\mathrm{s}})
so that the contribution of
E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))=(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}
is clear.

\begin{split}\frac{E(\mathcal{M}_{2,0}^{\mathrm{st}})}{q^{4g-3}}&=\left(\frac{(1-u^{2}v)^{g}(1-uv^{2})^{g}-q^{g+1}E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))}{(q-1)^{2}(q+1)}\right)E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{(q-1)(q+1)}(qE(\Lambda^{2}[\operatorname{Jac}(C)])+E(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\operatorname{Jac}(C))))\\
&\qquad+\frac{(q^{g}-1)(q^{g-1}-1)}{q^{2g-3}(q-1)(q+1)}E(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\operatorname{Jac}(C)))+\frac{(q^{g-1}-1)(q^{g-2}-1)}{q^{2g-2}(q-1)(q+1)}E(\Lambda^{2}[\operatorname{Jac}(C)])\\
&\qquad+\frac{(q^{g-1}-1)(q^{g-2}-1)}{q^{g-2}(q-1)}(E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))^{2}-E(\operatorname{Jac}(C)))+\frac{(q^{g}-1)(q^{g-1}-1)}{q^{g-1}(q-1)}E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{2}E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))\big((1-u)^{g-1}(1-v)^{g-1}+(1+u)^{g-1}(1+v)^{g-1}-2q^{g-1}\big)\\
&\qquad+E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))\bigg(\frac{q^{g-1}E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))}{(q-1)^{2}(q+1)}\\
&\qquad\qquad-\frac{(1+u)^{g-1}(1+v)^{g-1}(1-u)(1-v)}{4(q+1)}-\frac{g-1}{2}\frac{(u+v-2uv)(1-u)^{g-1}(1-v)^{g-1}}{q-1}\\
&\qquad\qquad-\frac{4g-7}{4}\frac{E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))}{q-1}-\frac{qE(\operatorname{Jac}(C))}{2(q-1)^{2}}\bigg)\end{split}

Now separately gathering terms with factors
E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))^{2}
,
and a single factor of
E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))=(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}
we have

\begin{split}\frac{E(\mathcal{M}_{2,0}^{\mathrm{st}})}{q^{4g-3}}&=\left(\frac{1-q^{g-1}}{q^{g-2}(q-1)}-\frac{4g-3}{4(q-1)}-\frac{q}{2(q-1)^{2}}\right)E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))^{2}\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{(q-1)(q+1)}(qE(\Lambda^{2}[\operatorname{Jac}(C)])+E(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\operatorname{Jac}(C))))\\
&\qquad+\frac{(q^{g}-1)(q^{g-1}-1)}{q^{2g-3}(q-1)(q+1)}E(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\operatorname{Jac}(C)))+\frac{(q^{g-1}-1)(q^{g-2}-1)}{q^{2g-2}(q-1)(q+1)}E(\Lambda^{2}[\operatorname{Jac}(C)])\\
&\qquad+\bigg(\frac{(1-u^{2}v)^{g}(1-uv^{2})^{g}}{(q-1)^{2}(q+1)}-\frac{(q^{g-1}-1)(q^{g-2}-1)}{q^{g-2}(q-1)}+\frac{(q^{g}-1)(q^{g-1}-1)}{q^{g-1}(q-1)}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\big((1-u)^{g-1}(1-v)^{g-1}+(1+u)^{g-1}(1+v)^{g-1}-2q^{g-1}\big)\\
&\qquad\qquad-\frac{(1+u)^{g-1}(1+v)^{g-1}(1-u)(1-v)}{4(q+1)}\\
&\qquad\qquad-\frac{g-1}{2}\frac{(u+v-2uv)(1-u)^{g-1}(1-v)^{g-1}}{q-1}\bigg)E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))\end{split}

Substituting into (
6
) and gathering the
E(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\operatorname{Jac}(C)))
and
E(\Lambda^{2}[\operatorname{Jac}(C)])
terms
we have

\begin{split}E(\mathrm{BPS}_{2,0}^{\mathrm{Dol}})&=\left(\frac{-q^{4g-1}-q^{4g-2}+q^{3g}+q^{3g-1}}{q^{4g-3}(q-1)(q+1)}-\frac{4g-3}{4(q-1)}-\frac{q}{´2(q-1)^{2}}\right)E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))^{2}\\
&\qquad+\frac{q^{4g-1}+q^{4g-2}+q^{4g-3}-q^{3g}-q^{3g-1}}{q^{4g-3}(q-1)(q+1)}E(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\operatorname{Jac}(C)))\\
&\qquad+\frac{q^{4g-1}+2q^{4g-2}-q^{3g}-q^{3g-1}}{q^{4g-3}(q+1)(q-1)}E(\Lambda^{2}[\operatorname{Jac}(C)])\\
&\qquad+\bigg(\frac{(1-u^{2}v)^{g}(1-uv^{2})^{g}}{(q-1)^{2}(q+1)}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\big((1-u)^{g-1}(1-v)^{g-1}+(1+u)^{g-1}(1+v)^{g-1}\big)\\
&\qquad\qquad-\frac{(1+u)^{g-1}(1+v)^{g-1}(1-u)(1-v)}{4(q+1)}\\
&\qquad\qquad-\frac{g-1}{2}\frac{(u+v-2uv)(1-u)^{g-1}(1-v)^{g-1}}{q-1}\bigg)E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))\end{split}

where two
E(\operatorname{Jac})
terms cancelled out with the contribution
E(\mathcal{M}_{1,0}^{\mathrm{s}})/q^{2g-1}=E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))/q^{g-1}
.

Using the identity
E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))^{2}=E(\Lambda^{2}[\operatorname{Jac}(C)])+E(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\operatorname{Jac}(C)))
cancels out the first
E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))^{2}
term.

\begin{split}E(\mathrm{BPS}_{2,0}^{\mathrm{Dol}})&=\left(-\frac{4g-3}{4(q-1)}-\frac{q}{2(q-1)^{2}}\right)E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))^{2}\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{(q-1)(q+1)}E(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\operatorname{Jac}(C)))\\
&\qquad+\frac{q}{(q+1)(q-1)}E(\Lambda^{2}[\operatorname{Jac}(C)])\\
&\qquad+\bigg(\frac{(1-u^{2}v)^{g}(1-uv^{2})^{g}}{(q-1)^{2}(q+1)}\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\big((1-u)^{g-1}(1-v)^{g-1}+(1+u)^{g-1}(1+v)^{g-1}\big)\\
&\qquad\qquad-\frac{(1+u)^{g-1}(1+v)^{g-1}(1-u)(1-v)}{4(q+1)}\\
&\qquad\qquad-\frac{g-1}{2}\frac{(u+v-2uv)(1-u)^{g-1}(1-v)^{g-1}}{q-1}\bigg)E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))\end{split}

Expanding

\begin{split}E(\Lambda^{2}[\operatorname{Jac}(C)])&=\frac{1}{2}(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}\big((1-u)(1-v)(1-u)^{g-1}(1-v)^{g-1}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-(1+u)(1+v)(1+u)^{g-1}(1+v)^{g-1}\big)\\
E(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\operatorname{Jac}(C)))&=\frac{1}{2}(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}\big((1-u)(1-v)(1-u)^{g-1}(1-v)^{g-1}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+(1+u)(1+v)(1+u)^{g-1}(1+v)^{g-1}\big)\end{split}

and combining with the second, third and fourth
E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))=(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}
terms yields

(8)

\begin{split}E(\mathrm{BPS}_{2,0}^{\mathrm{Dol}})&=\frac{(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}(1-u^{2}v)^{g}(1-uv^{2})^{g}}{(q^{2}-1)(q-1)}+(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}\bigg(-\frac{(1+u)^{g}(1+v)^{g}}{4(q+1)}\\
&\qquad-\frac{g}{2}\frac{(u+v-2uv)(1-u)^{g-1}(1-v)^{g-1}}{q-1}-\frac{4g-3}{4}\frac{(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}}{q-1}\\
&\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\frac{q(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}}{(q-1)^{2}}\bigg).\end{split}

4.1.2.
Rank 2 degree 1 BPS cohomology

In degree 1 the BPS cohomology is the shifted cohomology of the
rank 2 degree 1 coarse moduli space.
A formula for its E-polynomial can be extracted from

[
GHS14
, Appendix]
and is given by

\begin{split}E(\mathrm{BPS}_{2,1}^{\mathrm{Dol}})&=\frac{E(\mathcal{M}_{2,1})}{q^{4g-3}}\\
&=E(\operatorname{Jac}(C))\left(\frac{(1-u^{2}v)^{g}(1-uv^{2})^{g}-q^{g}E(\operatorname{Jac})}{(q^{2}-1)(q-1)}+\sum_{d=1}^{g-1}E(\operatorname{Sym}^{2g-2d-1}(C))\right)\\
&=\frac{(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}(1-u^{2}v)^{g}(1-uv^{2})^{g}}{(q^{2}-1)(q-1)}-\frac{q^{g}(1-u)^{2g}(1-v)^{2g}}{(q^{2}-1)(q-1)}\\
&\qquad+(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}\sum_{d=1}^{g-1}\operatorname{Coeff}_{t^{2g-2d-1}}\left(\frac{(1-ut)^{g}(1-vt)^{g}}{(1-t)(1-qt)}\right).\end{split}

The third equality follows from Macdonald’s computation of the cohomology of symmetric powers of curves
[
Mac62
]
.
We evaluate

\begin{split}\sum_{d=1}^{g-1}&\operatorname{Coeff}_{t^{2g-2d-1}}\left(\frac{(1-ut)^{g}(1-vt)^{g}}{(1-t)(1-qt)}\right)=\frac{q^{g}(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}}{(q-1)^{2}(q+1)}-\frac{(1+u)^{g}(1+v)^{g}}{4(q+1)}\\
&-\frac{g}{2}\frac{(u+v-2uv)(1-u)^{g-1}(1-v)^{g-1}}{q-1}-\frac{4g-3}{4}\frac{(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}}{q-1}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{q(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}}{(q-1)^{2}}\end{split}

following Hitchin

[
Hit87
, Proof of Theorem 7.6]

(see also
[
KY08
, Section 3.3]
).
Altogether we have

\begin{split}E(\mathrm{BPS}_{2,1}^{\mathrm{Dol}})&=\frac{(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}(1-u^{2}v)^{g}(1-uv^{2})^{g}}{(q^{2}-1)(q-1)}-\frac{q^{g}(1-u)^{2g}(1-v)^{2g}}{(q^{2}-1)(q-1)}\\
&\qquad+(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}\bigg(\frac{q^{g}(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}}{(q-1)^{2}(q+1)}-\frac{(1+u)^{g}(1+v)^{g}}{4(q+1)}\\
&\qquad-\frac{g}{2}\frac{(u+v-2uv)(1-u)^{g-1}(1-v)^{g-1}}{q-1}-\frac{4g-3}{4}\frac{(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}}{q-1}\\
&\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\frac{q(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}}{(q-1)^{2}}\bigg).\end{split}

This simplifies slightly to

\begin{split}E(\mathrm{BPS}_{2,1}^{\mathrm{Dol}})&=\frac{(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}(1-u^{2}v)^{g}(1-uv^{2})^{g}}{(q^{2}-1)(q-1)}+(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}\bigg(-\frac{(1+u)^{g}(1+v)^{g}}{4(q+1)}\\
&\qquad-\frac{g}{2}\frac{(u+v-2uv)(1-u)^{g-1}(1-v)^{g-1}}{q-1}-\frac{4g-3}{4}\frac{(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}}{q-1}\\
&\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\frac{q(1-u)^{g}(1-v)^{g}}{(q-1)^{2}}\bigg),\end{split}

which agrees with the expression (
8
)
for
E(\mathrm{BPS}_{2,0}^{\mathrm{Dol}})
.

4.2.
Sheaves on K3 surfaces

We can reinterpret some results in
[
dCRS21
]

as a cohomological
\chi
-independence check for sheaves on K3 surfaces.

Let
S
be a K3 surface.
Let
H
be a sufficiently general polarization of
S
.
Suppose
(S,H)
is of genus 2, i.e.,
the curves in the linear system
\lvert H\rvert
are of genus 2.
Let
v\in H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{alg}}(S,\mathbb{Z})
be a primitive Mukai vector with
v^{2}=2
.
Consider the moduli stack and moduli spaces
of
H
-Gieseker-semistable sheaves
\mathfrak{M}_{S,2v}^{H\text{-sst}}
,

\mathcal{M}_{S,2v}^{H\text{-sst}}
,
and
\mathcal{M}^{H\text{-sst}}_{S,v}
.

Let
OG10
be O’Grady’s ten-dimensional sporadic example of a hyper-Kähler manifold. By
[
dCRS21
, Lemma 4.1.3]
we have

IE(\mathcal{M}_{S,2v}^{H\text{-sst}})=E(OG10)-qE(\operatorname{Sym}^{2}(\mathcal{M}_{S,v}^{H\text{-sst}}))-q^{3}H(\mathcal{M}_{S,v}^{H\text{-sst}}).

and by (
6
) we have

(9)

E(\mathrm{BPS}_{S,2v})=\frac{E(OG10)}{q^{5}}-\frac{E_{u^{2},v^{2}}(\mathcal{M}_{S,v}^{H\text{-sst}})}{q^{4}}-\frac{E(\mathcal{M}_{S,v}^{H\text{-sst}})}{q^{2}}.

Pick a primitive Mukai vector

w\in H^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{alg}}(S,\mathbb{Z})

satisfying
w^{2}=4=(2v)^{2}
.
The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{S,w}^{H\text{-sst}}
is smooth
and deformation equivalent to the Hilbert scheme of 5 points on a K3 surface.

Corollary 4.1
.

Suppose the cohomological integrality conjecture is true for

\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{Coh}(S),v}^{H\text{-sst}}
, then

E(\mathrm{BPS}_{S,2v})=E(\mathrm{BPS}_{S,w}).

Proof.

By definition we have
E(\mathrm{BPS}_{S,w})=q^{-5}E(\mathcal{M}_{S,w}^{H-\mathrm{ss}})
. By
[
dCRS21
, Proposition 6.1.2]
and (
9
) we have

E(\mathrm{BPS}_{S,2v})=q^{-5}E(\mathcal{M}_{S,w}^{H-\mathrm{ss}})
.
∎

Therefore, the polynomial
E(\mathrm{BPS}_{S,2v})
is determined by
the Hodge numbers of
\mathcal{M}_{S,w}^{H\text{-sst}}
,
which are recorded in
[
dCRS21
, (103)]
.

Remark 4.2
.

If we assume the
\chi
-independence conjecture for BPS cohomology, then equation (
9
) (which follows from a simple application of the decomposition theorem
[
dCRS21
, Lemma 4.1.3]
and Theorem
3.15
) together with
\chi
-independence, yields a conjectural computation of the Hodge numbers of
OG10
.

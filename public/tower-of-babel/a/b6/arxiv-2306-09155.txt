Definable Lipschitz selections for affine-set valued maps Thanks: Supported by FWF-Project P 32905-N and Oberwolfach Research Fellows (OWRF) ID 2244p
Adam Parusiński and Armin Rainer

Abstract
. Whitney’s extension problem, i.e., how one can tell whether a function f : X → ℝ f:X\to\mathbb{R} , X ⊆ ℝ n X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n} , is the restriction of a C m C^{m} -function on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} , was solved in full generality by Charles Fefferman in 2006. In this paper, we settle the C 1 , ω C^{1,\omega} -case of a related conjecture: given that f f is semialgebraic and ω \omega is a semialgebraic modulus of continuity, if f f is the restriction of a C 1 , ω C^{1,\omega} -function then it is the restriction of a semialgebraic C 1 , ω C^{1,\omega} -function. We work in the more general setting of sets that are definable in an o-minimial expansion of the real field. An ingenious argument of Brudnyi and Shvartsman relates the existence of C 1 , ω C^{1,\omega} -extensions to the existence of Lipschitz selections of certain affine-set valued maps. We show that if a definable affine-set valued map has Lipschitz selections then it also has definable Lipschitz selections. In particular, we obtain a Lipschitz solution (more generally, ω \omega -Hölder solution, for any definable modulus of continuity ω \omega ) of the definable Brenner–Epstein–Hochster–Kollár problem. In most of our results we have control over the respective (semi)norms.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we settle the
C^{1,\omega}
-case of an open problem raised by Bierstone and Milman (cf.
[
21
]
):

Given a compact semialgebraic subset
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a semialgebraic function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}

which is the restriction of a
C^{m}
-function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, does there exist a semialgebraic
C^{m}
-extension of
f
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
?

For general
m
, the answer to this question is known to be affirmative in dimension
n\leq 2
,
due to Fefferman and Luli
[
10
]
.
In arbitrary dimension,
Aschenbrenner and Thamrongthanyalak
[
2
]

proved the
C^{1}
-version of the statement.
A solution with loss of regularity is due to Bierstone, Campesato, and Milman

[
5
]
.

The philosophy of our approach is related to Aschenbrenner and Thamrongthanyalak’s
who prove and use a definable version of Michael’s selection theorem.
“Definable” means that the sets and maps belong to a fixed o-minimal expansion of the real field
(of which semialgebraic sets are a basic example). Michael’s theorem concerns the existence of
continuous selections of set-valued maps.
Already the existence of definable Lipschitz selections is in general not known, since the classical results
are based on transcendental methods (e.g. the Steiner point).

Nevertheless, for certain affine-set valued maps, that are relevant for the
C^{1,\omega}
-Whitney extension problem,
Lipschitz selections can be constructed in a way that preserves definability in the given o-minimal structure.
This construction is due to Brudnyi and Shvartsman
[
7
]
; see also the references therein
for precursors.

Let us now describe our results in more detail.

1.1.
Main results

Let an o-minimal expansion of the real field be fixed.
Throughout the paper, a set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called
definable
if it is definable in this fixed o-minimal structure.
A map
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is definable if its graph
\{(x,\varphi(x)):x\in X\}
is a definable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Cf.
Section

2.1
.

By a
modulus of continuity
we always mean a positive, continuous, increasing, and concave function
\omega:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)

such that
\omega(t)\to 0
as
t\to 0
. A modulus of continuity
\omega
is called
definable
if the function
\omega
is definable.

By definition,
C^{1,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of all
C^{1}
-functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
that are globally bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

and whose partial derivatives of first order are globally bounded and globally
\omega
-Hölder on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Equipped with its natural norm, this space is a Banach space.
We write
C^{1,\omega}_{\operatorname{def}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for the subspace of definable functions in
C^{1,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Given a definable subset
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
we denote by
C^{1,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{X}
and
C^{1,\omega}_{\operatorname{def}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{X}
the respective trace spaces on
X
.
Let
\mathbb{R}^{X}_{\operatorname{def}}
be the set of all definable functions
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
.
For precise definitions we refer to
Section

2.2
.

We will prove the following theorem.

Theorem A
.

Let
\omega
be a definable modulus of continuity.
Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed definable
set and
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
a definable function.
Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

f
is the restriction of a
C^{1,\omega}
-function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(2)

f
is the restriction of a definable
C^{1,\omega}
-function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

That means

(1.1)

\mathbb{R}^{X}_{\operatorname{def}}\cap C^{1,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{X}=C^{1,\omega}_{\operatorname{def}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{X}.

Moreover, a subset of the set (
1.1
) is bounded in
C^{1,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{X}

if and only if it is bounded in
C^{1,\omega}_{\operatorname{def}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{X}
.

In the Lipschitz case
\omega(t)=t
, for compact definable
X
we even have

(1.2)

\|f\|_{C^{1,1}_{\operatorname{def}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{X}}\approx\|f\|_{C^{1,1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{X}}.

In particular, (
1.1
) holds for all classical Hölder classes with rational Hölder exponent,
since
\omega(t)=t^{\alpha}
with rational
\alpha\in(0,1]
is semialgebraic.
We conjecture that

(1.3)

\|f\|_{C^{1,\omega}_{\operatorname{def}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{X}}\approx\|f\|_{C^{1,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{X}}

for
any
definable modulus of continuity
\omega
.

The meaning of “
\approx
” in (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) is
that either quotient of the two sides lies in the interval
[C^{-1},C]

for some constant
C\geq 1
depending only on
n
.

The proof of
A
is based on an ingenious argument of Brudnyi and Shvartsman
[
7
]

that relates the existence of
C^{1,\omega}
-extensions to the existence of Lipschitz selections for
certain maps which take affine subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as values.

The next result,
B
, states that, if these maps happen to be definable and admit a Lipschitz selection, then
they have a definable Lipschitz selection.

We denote by
\mathcal{A}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
the set of all affine subspaces
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of dimension at most
k
. A pseudometric space
(\mathcal{M},\rho)
is said to be definable if
\mathcal{M}
is a definable subset
of some
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and the pseudometric
\rho:\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty)
is a definable function.
A map
F:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{A}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called definable if its graph
\bigcup_{m\in\mathcal{M}}(\{m\}\times F(m))
is a
definable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
A
selection
of
F
is a map
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
f(m)\in F(m)
for all
m\in\mathcal{M}
.
Cf.
Section

2.3
and
Section

2.4
.

Theorem B
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\rho)
be a definable pseudometric space and
F:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{A}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
a definable map.
Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

F
has a Lipschitz selection.

(2)

F
has a definable Lipschitz selection.

Moreover, if there is a Lipschitz selection
f
of
F
with Lipschitz seminorm
|f|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{R}^{n})}
, then
there is a definable Lipschitz selection
g
of
F
with

(1.4)

|g|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(k,n)\,|f|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
C(k,n)>0
is a constant that only depends on
k
and
n
.

To deduce
A
from
B
we actually need a slightly stronger version of the latter which is
stated in
Theorem

3.3
.

Another application of
B
is an
\omega
-Hölder (in particular, Lipschitz)
solution of the definable Brenner–Epstein–Hochster–Kollár problem.

Theorem C
.

Let
\omega
be a definable modulus of continuity.
Let
A_{ij},b_{i}:X\to\mathbb{R}
, for
i=1,\ldots,N
and
j=1,\ldots,M
, be definable functions defined on a definable
subset
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Consider the linear system of equations

(1.5)

\sum_{j=1}^{M}A_{ij}f_{j}=b_{i},\quad i=1,\ldots,N,

in the unkowns
f_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,M
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The system (
1.5
) admits an
\omega
-Hölder solution.

(2)

The system (
1.5
) admits a definable
\omega
-Hölder solution.

Moreover, if there is an
\omega
-Hölder solution
f=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{M})
of (
1.5
) with
\omega
-Hölder seminorm

|f|_{C^{0,\omega}(X,\mathbb{R}^{M})}
,
then there is a definable
\omega
-Hölder solution
g=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{M})
of (
1.5
) such that

(1.6)

|g|_{C^{0,\omega}(X,\mathbb{R}^{M})}\leq C(M)\,|f|_{C^{0,\omega}(X,\mathbb{R}^{M})}.

A
C^{0}
-version of this theorem is due to Aschenbrenner and Thamrongthanyalak
[
2
]
;
for the semialgebraic setting see also Fefferman and Kollár
[
9
]
.

Let us emphasize that, due to
[
7
]
, the respective first conditions in
A
and
B

are characterized by
finiteness principles
:

•

An arbitrary function
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
,
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, belongs to

C^{1,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{X}
if and only if for each subset
Y\subseteq X
of cardinality
\#Y\leq 3\cdot 2^{n-1}

there is
F_{Y}\in C^{1,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that
F_{Y}=f
on
Y
and
\sup_{Y}\|F_{Y}\|_{C^{1,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty
.

•

An arbitrary map
F:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{A}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
\mathcal{M}
is any pseudometric space,
admits a Lipschitz selection if and only if
for each subset
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
of cardinality
\#\mathcal{N}\leq 2^{k+1}

the restriction
F|_{\mathcal{N}}
has a Lipschitz selection
f_{\mathcal{N}}
such that
\sup_{\mathcal{N}}|f_{\mathcal{N}}|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{N},\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty
.

As shown in
[
7
]
, the cardinalities of the finite test sets cannot be reduced.
Obviously, we immediately get a corresponding finiteness principle for
C
.

The proofs of the main results,
A
and
B
, are essentially a
careful verification that the constructions of
[
7
]
are done in a definable way.
We first prove
B
in
Section

3
and then deduce
A
from it
in
Section

4
.
C
is a simple consequence of
B
; see
Section

3.4
.

For the transition from a definable Whitney jet of class
C^{1,\omega}
on
X
to a definable
C^{1,\omega}
-function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
we use our recent paper
[
14
]
on the uniform extension of definable Whitney jets of class
C^{m,\omega}

(which is a variation of the definable
C^{m}
Whitney extension theorem
[
11
,
12
,
18
]
).
At this stage, we cannot control the norms in this extension
and thus are not able to prove (
1.3
), but we prove that the extension can be done in
a bounded way; see also
Section

4.4
.
Alternatively, in the
C^{1,1}
-case one can use
the explicit formula due to Azaga, Le Gruyer, and Mudarra
[
3
]

which is visibly definable and admits a control of the norms which leads to (
1.2
).
The explicit formula also yields an immediate proof of the definable Kirszbraun theorem; see
Section

4.3
.

Notation

We equip
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the maximum-norm
\|x\|:=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|x_{i}|
.
If a different norm is used, e.g., the Euclidean norm
\|x\|_{2}:=(\sum_{1\leq i\leq n}x_{i}^{2})^{1/2}
,
then it will be explicitly stated.
Note that
\|x\|\leq\|x\|_{2}\leq\sqrt{n}\,\|x\|
.
The closed
\|\cdot\|
-balls
Q(x,r):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|x-y\|\leq r\}
are cubes with sides parallel to the
coordinate axes. We also write
Q(r):=Q(0,r)
so that
Q(x,r)=x+Q(r)
.
If
\lambda>0
then
\lambda Q(x,r)
denotes the cube
x+Q(\lambda r)
.
Then standard scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
\langle x,y\rangle:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}y_{i}
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall definitions and fix notation.

2.1.
O-minimal expansions of the real field

An
o-minimal expansion of the ordered field of real numbers

is a family
\mathscr{S}=(\mathscr{S}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, where
\mathscr{S}_{n}
is a collection of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

•

\mathscr{S}_{n}
is a boolean algebra with respect to the usual set-theoretic operations,

•

\mathscr{S}_{n}
contains all semialgebraic subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

•

\mathscr{S}
is stable by cartesian products and linear projections,

•

each
S\in\mathscr{S}_{1}
has only finitely many connected components.

A set
S
that belongs to
\mathscr{S}
is said to be
definable
(in
\mathscr{S}
).
A map
f:S\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is called
definable
if its graph

\{(x,f(x)):x\in S\}
is a definable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

The basic example of an o-mimimal expansion of the real field is the family of semialgebraic sets.
Another important example is the family of globally subanalytic sets.
Many more interesting o-minimal structures have been identified in recent decades.
We refer to
[
19
]
and
[
20
]
for the fundamentals of the theory.

From now on, the attribute “definable” will refer to a fixed o-minimal expansion of the real field.

2.2.
Spaces of differentiable functions

Let
\omega
be a modulus of continuity,
i.e., a positive, continuous, increasing, and concave function
\omega:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)

such that
\omega(t)\to 0
as
t\to 0
.
Let
C^{0,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the set of all continuous bounded functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

|f|_{C^{0,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\inf\{C>0:|f(x)-f(y)|\leq C\,\omega(\|x-y\|)\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}<\infty.

For
m
a nonnegative integer,
C^{m,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
consists of all
C^{m}
-functions such that
\partial^{\alpha}f
is
globally bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, for all
|\alpha|\leq m
, and
\partial^{\alpha}f\in C^{0,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, for all
|\alpha|=m
.
Then
C^{m,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Banach space with the norm

(2.1)

\|f\|_{C^{m,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\sup_{|\alpha|\leq m}|\partial^{\alpha}f(x)|+\sup_{|\alpha|=m}|\partial^{\alpha}f|_{C^{0,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Let
C^{m,\omega}_{\operatorname{def}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the subspace consisting of the functions in
C^{m,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that are definable.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a subset.
The trace space
C^{m,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{X}
is the set of all
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
such that there exists
F\in C^{m,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
F|_{X}=f
.
It carries the norm

\|f\|_{C^{m,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{X}}:=\inf\{\|F\|_{C^{m,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:F\in C^{m,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,F|_{X}=f\}.

Similarly, we consider the space
C^{m,\omega}_{\operatorname{def}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{X}
of restrictions to
X
of functions in
C^{m,\omega}_{\operatorname{def}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

with the norm

\|f\|_{C^{m,\omega}_{\operatorname{def}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{X}}:=\inf\{\|F\|_{C^{m,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:F\in C^{m,\omega}_{\operatorname{def}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,F|_{X}=f\}.

If
X
is definable, then each element of
C^{m,\omega}_{\operatorname{def}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})|_{X}

is a definable function on
X
.

2.3.
Pseudometric spaces

By a
pseudometric space

(\mathcal{M},\rho)
we mean a non-empty set
\mathcal{M}
together with a non-negative
real valued function
\rho:\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty)

such that
\rho(x,x)=0
,
\rho(x,y)=\rho(y,x)
, and
\rho(x,y)\leq\rho(x,z)+\rho(z,y)
for all
x,y,z\in\mathcal{M}
.
The function
\rho
is called a
pseudometric
;
if additionally
\rho(x,y)=0
implies
x=y
, it is called a
metric
.
We say that
(\mathcal{M},\rho)
is an
extended pseudometric space
if the pseudometric
\rho
may also take the value
+\infty
.

A pseudometric space
(\mathcal{M},\rho)
is called
definable
if
\mathcal{M}
is a definable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, for some
N
,
and
\rho:\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty)
is a definable function.

A map
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
Lipschitz
if

|f|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\inf\{C>0:\|f(x)-f(y)\|\leq C\,\rho(x,y)\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathcal{M}\}<\infty.

Let
\omega
be a modulus of continuity.
A map
f:X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is
\omega
-Hölder
if

|f|_{C^{0,\omega}(X,\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=|f|_{\operatorname{Lip}(X_{\omega},\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty,

where
X_{\omega}
is the space
X
endowed with the metric
\rho(x,y):=\omega(\|x-y\|)
.

2.4.
Set-valued mappings

A
set-valued mapping
is a map
F:X\to 2^{Y}
. The
graph
of
F
is the subset
\bigcup_{x\in X}(\{x\}\times F(x))
of
X\times Y
.
A
selection
of
F
is a map
f:X\to Y
such that
f(x)\in F(x)
for all
x\in X
.

If
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
is definable and
Y=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then we say that a set-valued map
F:X\to 2^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is
definable
if
its graph is a definable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

3.
Definable Lipschitz selections

The purpose of this section is to formulate and prove
Theorem

3.3

from which
B
and
C
will follow easily.

3.1.
Definable Lipschitz selections for cube-valued maps

Let
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the family of all closed cubes
Q(x,r):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|x-y\|\leq r\}
, where
r\in[0,\infty]
;
thus
Q(x,0)=\{x\}
and
Q(r,\infty)=\mathbb{R}^{n}
count as cubes and belong to
\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Cubes centered at the origin will be denoted by
Q(r):=Q(0,r)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\rho)
be a definable pseudometric space.
Let
F:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a definable map.
Assume that for every
2
-point subset
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
there exists a Lipschitz selection
f_{\mathcal{N}}:\mathcal{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

of
F|_{\mathcal{N}}
with
|f_{\mathcal{N}}|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{N},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1
.
Then there exists a definable Lipschitz selection
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

of
F
with
|f|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1
.

Proof.

Cf.
[
6
, Proposition 1.24 and Corollary 1.25]
.
By projection to the coordinate axes of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
it is enough to consider the case
n=1
.
Then
F(m)
is a closed interval
[a(m),b(m)]
(either bounded, possibly a point, or
\mathbb{R}
).

Let us first show that we may assume that all
F(m)
are bounded.
Consider the definable set
\mathcal{M}_{0}:=\{m\in\mathcal{M}:F(m)\text{ is bounded}\}=b^{-1}(\mathbb{R})

and the definable map
F_{0}:=F|_{\mathcal{M}_{0}}
.
We may assume that
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is non-empty, since otherwise

F(m)=\mathbb{R}
for all
m\in\mathcal{M}
and
f:=0
is the required Lipschitz selection.
If we prove that
F_{0}:\mathcal{M}_{0}\to\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{R})
has a definable Lipschitz selection
f_{0}:\mathcal{M}_{0}\to\mathbb{R}

with
|f_{0}|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M}_{0},\mathbb{R})}\leq 1
,
then
f_{0}
admits a definable Lipschitz extension
f:\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}
with

|f|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{R})}\leq 1
defined by

(3.1)

f(m):=\inf_{m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}}(f_{0}(m^{\prime})+\rho(m,m^{\prime})),\quad m\in\mathcal{M},

which clearly is the required selection of
F
(since
F(m)=\mathbb{R}
if
m\not\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
).
Let us check that
f
in (
3.1
) has the desired properties.
It is finite, because

f_{0}(m_{0})-\rho(m,m_{0})\leq f(m)\leq f_{0}(m_{0})+\rho(m,m_{0})

for any
m_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
,
where the first inequality holds, since
|f_{0}|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M}_{0},\mathbb{R})}\leq 1
entails

f_{0}(x)+\rho(x,m)\geq f_{0}(m_{0})-\rho(x,m_{0})+\rho(x,m)\geq f_{0}(m_{0})-\rho(m,m_{0}),

for all
x\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
.
We have
f|_{\mathcal{M}_{0}}=f_{0}
, since
if
m\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
then

f(m)\leq f_{0}(m)\leq f_{0}(m^{\prime})+\rho(m,m^{\prime})

for all
m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}
, whence
f(m)=f_{0}(m)
.
To see
|f|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{R})}\leq 1
let
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathcal{M}
. Then

f(m_{1})\leq\inf_{m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{0}}(f_{0}(m^{\prime})+\rho(m_{2},m^{\prime})+\rho(m_{1},m_{2}))=f(m_{2})+\rho(m_{1},m_{2}),

and we are done.

Hence, it suffices to prove the lemma under the assumption that
F(m)
is bounded for all
m\in\mathcal{M}
.
We will show that

f(m):=\inf_{m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}}(b(m^{\prime})+\rho(m,m^{\prime}))=\inf_{m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}}(\sup F(m^{\prime})+\rho(m,m^{\prime})),\quad m\in\mathcal{M},

is a definable Lipschitz selection of
F
with
|f|_{\operatorname{Lip(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{R})}}\leq 1
; in particular,
f
is finite.
It is immediate from the definition that
f
is definable and satisfies
f(m)\leq b(m)
for every
m\in\mathcal{M}
.
To see
a(m)\leq f(m)
let
m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}
be arbitrary. By assumption, there exists
g:=f_{\{m,m^{\prime}\}}:\{m,m^{\prime}\}\to\mathbb{R}

with
a(m)\leq g(m)\leq b(m)
,
a(m^{\prime})\leq g(m^{\prime})\leq b(m^{\prime})
, and
|g(m)-g(m^{\prime})|\leq\rho(m,m^{\prime})
,
so that
a(m)\leq b(m^{\prime})+\rho(m,m^{\prime})
.

Finally, for any
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathcal{M}
,

f(m_{1})\leq\inf_{m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}}(b(m^{\prime})+\rho(m_{2},m^{\prime})+\rho(m_{1},m_{2}))=f(m_{2})+\rho(m_{1},m_{2}),

and we conclude that
|f|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M},\mathbb{R})}\leq 1
.
∎

3.2.
Definable Lipschitz selections for affine-set valued maps

Let
V\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a definable set.
Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a graph with set of vertices
V

and set of edges
E\subseteq\{(v,v^{\prime})\in V\times V:v\neq v^{\prime}\}
.
We assume that
E
is symmetric in the sense that
(v,v^{\prime})\in E
implies
(v^{\prime},v)\in E
.
In other words,
the graph is undirected and, strictly speaking,
the set of edges actually is
E/\!\sim
, where
(v,v^{\prime})\sim(v^{\prime},v)
, but this
inaccuracy in notation will cause no troubles.
Furthermore, we assume that
E
is definable.
Let us write
v\leftrightarrow v^{\prime}
if
v,v^{\prime}\in V
are joined by an edge, i.e.,

(v,v^{\prime})\in E
.

We say that a subset
W\subseteq V
is
admissible
if
(W,E_{W})
regarded as a subgraph of
\Gamma=(V,E)
,
where
(v,v^{\prime})\in E_{W}
if and only if
(v,v^{\prime})\in E
and
v,v^{\prime}\in W
,
has no isolated vertices. This means that each vertex is joined by an edge to another vertex unless
the graph consists of but one vertex.

Let us endow the graph
\Gamma=(V,E)
with a
weight
, i.e., a symmetric function

w:E\to[0,\infty]
.
This induces an extended pseudometric space
(V,\sigma)
,
where
\sigma:V\times V\to[0,\infty]
is defined
by

(3.2)

\sigma(v,v^{\prime}):=\inf\sum_{i=0}^{k}w(e_{i}),\quad v\neq v^{\prime},

where the infimum is taken over all finite paths
\{e_{i}\}_{i=0}^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, of edges in
\Gamma
joining
v
and
v^{\prime}
.
Moreover,

\sigma(v,v):=0
and
\sigma(v,v^{\prime}):=+\infty
if there is no path of edges joining
v\neq v^{\prime}
.

We say that
\Gamma=(V,E,w)
is a
definable weighted graph
if the sets
V
and
E
are definable as specified above
and,
additionally,
there exist a definable pseudometric
\rho:V\times V\to[0,\infty)
on
V
and an absolute constant
A\geq 1
such that

(3.3)

\frac{1}{A}\rho\leq\sigma\leq A\rho.

So
(V,\sigma)
is a pseudometric space, while
(V,\rho)
is even a definable pseudometric space.
In particular, in a definable weighted graph any two vertices are joined by a path of edges
and
V
is an admissible subset of
V
.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},\rho)
be a definable pseudometric space.
The full graph with set of vertices
\mathcal{M}
, set of edges
\{(m,m^{\prime})\in\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}:m\neq m^{\prime}\}
,
and weight
\rho
is a definable weighted graph (where
\sigma=\rho
).
Furthermore, any subset of
\mathcal{M}
is admissible.

Recall that
\mathcal{A}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the family of affine subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of dimension at most
k

and that a map
F:V\to\mathcal{A}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is called definable if its graph
is a definable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For a map
f:W\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
W\subseteq V
, we set

|f|_{\operatorname{Lip}(W,\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\inf\{C>0:\|f(v)-f(v^{\prime})\|\leq C\,\rho(v,v^{\prime})\text{ for all }v,v^{\prime}\in W\};

i.e., the Lipschitz seminorm is computed in terms of
\rho
(not
\sigma
).

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E,w)
be a definable weighted graph.
Let
F:V\to\mathcal{A}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a definable map.
Assume that for each admissible subset
W\subseteq V
of cardinality
\#W\leq 2^{k+1}

there exists a Lipschitz selection
f_{W}:W\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
F|_{W}

with
|f_{W}|_{\operatorname{Lip}(W,\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1
.
Then there exists a definable Lipschitz selection
f:V\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
F
with

|f|_{\operatorname{Lip}(V,\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C
, where
C\geq 1
is a constant depending only on
k
,
n
, and

A
from (
3.3
).

Note that
Theorem

3.3
implies
B
in view of
Remark

3.2
.
It is a definable version of
[
7
, Theorem 2.3]
.
We closely follow its proof making sure that all steps are definable.

Let us give a rough outline of the general strategy of the proof.

\textstyle{\boxed{(V,\rho)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle F}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{A}_{k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(1)}
\textstyle{\boxed{(\hat{V},\hat{\rho})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\hat{F}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(3)}
\textstyle{\boxed{(\overline{V},\overline{\rho})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\overline{F}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{A}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(2)}

It is by induction on
k
.
The induction step (
k\to k+1
)
is divided into the following three stages (which correspond to the bent arrows in the diagram).

(1)

From
F:V\to\mathcal{A}_{k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
one constructs a “doubling”
(\overline{V},\overline{\rho})

of the space
(V,\rho)
and a map
\overline{F}:\overline{V}\to\mathcal{A}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

in such a way that the induction hypothesis yields a Lipschitz selection
\overline{f}
of
\overline{F}
.

(2)

The Lipschitz selection
\overline{f}
of
\overline{F}

is used to define a new space
(\hat{V}=\overline{V},\hat{\rho})
and
a cube-valued map
\hat{F}:\hat{V}\to\mathcal{Q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
;
the center of the cube
\hat{F}(\overline{v})
is
\overline{f}(\overline{v})
.
Thanks to
Lemma

3.1
, there is a Lipschitz selection
\hat{f}
of
\hat{F}

which moreover can be interpreted as a Lipschitz map defined on
(V,\rho)
.

(3)

The desired Lipschitz selection
f
of
F
is finally found
by defining
f(v)
to be the orthogonal projection of
\hat{f}(v)

to the affine subspace
F(v)
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

3.3.
Proof of
Theorem

3.3

We prove
Theorem

3.3
by induction on
k
.

The base case
k=0
is trivial:
\mathcal{A}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cong\mathbb{R}^{n}
so that
f=F:V\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the desired definable
Lipschitz selection.
Indeed, let
v,v^{\prime}\in V
be any two distinct vertices.
Then there is a path of edges
v=:v_{0}\leftrightarrow v_{1}\leftrightarrow\cdots\leftrightarrow v_{\ell}:=v^{\prime}

joining
v
and
v^{\prime}
so that, by assumption (as
\{v_{i},v_{i+1}\}
is admissible) and (
3.3
),

(3.4)

\|f(v)-f(v^{\prime})\|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\rho(v_{i},v_{i+1})\leq A\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}w(v_{i},v_{i+1}).

Taking the infimum over all paths joining
v
and
v^{\prime}
and using again (
3.3
),
we conclude that
|f|_{\operatorname{Lip}(V,\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq A^{2}
.

Let us assume that the result holds for some
k<n
and prove it for
k+1
.
Let
F:V\to\mathcal{A}_{k+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a definable map and assume that
for each admissible subset
W\subseteq V
of cardinality
\#W\leq 2^{k+2}

there exists a Lipschitz selection
f_{W}:W\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
F|_{W}

with
|f_{W}|_{\operatorname{Lip}(W,\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 1
.

Let
v_{1},v_{2}\in V
be such that
v_{1}\leftrightarrow v_{2}
.
Then
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
is an admissible subset of
V
and
the assumption
implies that
there exist points
x_{1}\in F(v_{1})
,
x_{2}\in F(v_{2})
such that

(3.5)

\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|\leq\rho(v_{1},v_{2}).

It follows that the set

\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in F(v_{1})\times F(v_{2}):\|x_{1}-x_{2}\|\leq\rho(v_{1},v_{2})\}

is non-empty and definable, since
F
and
\rho
are definable.
Therefore, by definable choice, we may assume that
x_{i}=x_{i}(v_{1},v_{2})

, depend in a definable way on
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
.
We may conclude that

(3.6)

P(v_{1},v_{2}):=F(v_{1})\cap\big(F(v_{2})+Q(2\rho(v_{1},v_{2}))+x_{1}(v_{1},v_{2})-x_{2}(v_{1},v_{2})\big)

is a non-empty convex definable set that is symmetric with respect to the point
x_{1}
.

Let us understand better the geometry of
P(v_{1},v_{2})
.
For ease of notation let
U_{1}:=F(v_{1})
and
U_{2}:=F(v_{2})+x_{1}-x_{2}
.
Then
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
are affine subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
through
x_{1}
of dimension at most
k+1
.
Set

,

.
Moreover, the “slab”
S_{2}:=U_{2}+Q(2\rho(v_{1},v_{2}))
is the thickening of
U_{2}
by
the cube
Q(2\rho(v_{1},v_{2}))
.

If
d_{1}=0
, then

\displaystyle P(v_{1},v_{2})=U_{1}\cap S_{2}=\{x_{1}\}=U_{1}\cap\big(\{x_{1}\}+Q(0)\big).

Now assume that
d_{1}>0
.
If

,
then

is a polytope in

centered at

with pairwise parallel faces.
In fact, the pairs of parallel faces result as the intersections with

of
opposite parallel faces of the slab

.
Let
p
be the number of pairs of parallel faces of

.
Thus

.
(Note that

may occur if

so that

is
a cube.)
Let

be the affine subspaces of
U_{1}
of dimension

through

that are equidistant to the respective affine hulls of opposite parallel faces of

,

\|\cdot\|
and
having the respective values

.
Then

(3.7)

P(v_{1},v_{2})=\bigcap_{i=1}^{p}\big(U_{1}\cap(L_{i}+Q(r_{i}))\big).

Now suppose that
U_{1}\cap U_{2}\neq\{x_{1}\}
.
If
U_{1}\cap U_{2}\neq U_{1}
,
then
P(v_{1},v_{2})
is a polytope in
U_{1}
centered at
x_{1}

with pairwise parallel faces
and infinite extension in the directions of

.
The number of pairs of parallel faces

again satisfies

.
Let
L_{1},\ldots,L_{p}
be the affine subspaces of
U_{1}
of dimension
d_{1}-1

through
x_{1}

that are equidistant
to the respective affine hulls of

finite
opposite parallel faces of
P(v_{1},v_{2})
.
As before the distance is computed with respect to
\|\cdot\|
and
let
r_{1},\ldots,r_{p}
be the respective values.
Then we again have the representation (
3.7
).

It remains to consider the case
U_{1}\cap U_{2}=U_{1}
.
Then
P(v_{1},v_{2})=U_{1}
.
If
d_{1}\leq k
, we set
L_{1}:=U_{1}
and

so that (
3.7
)
remains valid with

.
If

, we define
L_{1}:=\{x_{1}\}
and

.
Again (
3.7
)
holds true with

.
Note that this is the only case, where we allow an infinite radius.
This case occurs if and only if
F(v_{1})
and

are parallel and both have dimension

.

Thus we proved that in any case

(3.8)

P(v_{1},v_{2})=\bigcap_{i\in I(v_{1},v_{2})}\big(F(v_{1})\cap(L_{i}+Q(r_{i}))\big),

where the index set

has cardinality at least

is an affine subspace of

through

of dimension at most
k
.
The radii

are finite unless

and
\dim F(v_{1})=\dim F(v_{2})=k+1
.

Let us consider the definable sets

\overline{V}_{\|}:=\{(v_{1},v_{2}):v_{1}\leftrightarrow v_{2},\,F(v_{1})\|F(v_{2}),\,\dim F(v_{1})=\dim F(v_{2})=k+1\}.

\overline{V}_{0}:=\{\overline{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},i):v_{1}\leftrightarrow v_{2},\,(v_{1},v_{2})\not\in\overline{V}_{\|},\,i\in I(v_{1},v_{2})\}.

We get a new definable pseudometric space

(\overline{V}_{0},\overline{\rho})
,
where, for
\overline{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},i)\neq\overline{v}^{\prime}=(v_{1}^{\prime},v_{2}^{\prime},i^{\prime})

We extend this data to an extended pseudometric space

by
defining

\overline{V}:=\{\overline{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},i):v_{1}\leftrightarrow v_{2},\,i\in I(v_{1},v_{2})\}

and putting

if at least one of
\overline{v}\neq\overline{v}^{\prime}

\overline{V}_{0}
.

Define
\overline{F}:\overline{V}\to\mathcal{A}_{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by setting
\overline{F}(v_{1},v_{2},i):=L_{i}
.
By the above discussion,
\overline{F}
is definable.

Claim 1
.

There exists a definable Lipschitz selection
\overline{f}:\overline{V}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
\overline{F}

with
|\overline{f}|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\overline{V},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(k,n)
.

We will first consider the restriction
\overline{F}_{0}:=\overline{F}|_{\overline{V}_{0}}
.
By

, we may view
(\overline{V}_{0},\overline{\rho})
as a definable weighted graph,

).
Thus, by the induction hypothesis,

.

Claim 4

The cube

Intersecting both sides with

By

Definable

We will now work towards the proof of

.

It will allow us to bring to bear

.

4.1.
Definable Whitney jets vs. definable Lipschitz selections

Let
\omega
be a modulus of continuity.
We will henceforth assume
that

(4.1)

\omega\leq 1.

This is no restriction regarding
A
, since replacing
\omega
by
\overline{\omega}:=\min\{1,\omega\}

in (
2.1
) gives equivalent norms:

\|f\|_{C^{m,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{C^{m,\overline{\omega}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 3\,\|f\|_{C^{m,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

The first inequality is immediate from
\overline{\omega}\leq\omega
.
For the second inequality, it is enough to consider
m=0
and
show that
|f|_{C^{0,\overline{\omega}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 2\,\|f\|_{C^{0,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.
To this end let
t_{0}:=\inf\{t>0:\omega(t)\geq 1\}
. If
\|x-y\|<t_{0}
, then

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq|f|_{C^{0,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\omega(\|x-y\|)=|f|_{C^{0,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\,\overline{\omega}(\|x-y\|),

and, if
\|x-y\|\geq t_{0}
,

\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{\overline{\omega}(\|x-y\|)}=|f(x)-f(y)|\leq 2\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|,

which implies the assertion.

Let
X
be a closed definable subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Set

\mathcal{M}_{X}:=\{(x,y)\in X\times X:x\neq y\}

and endow it with the metric
\rho_{\omega}
defined by

\rho_{\omega}((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})):=\omega(\|x-y\|)+\omega(\|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\|)+\omega(\|x-x^{\prime}\|),

if
(x,y)\neq(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
, and
0
otherwise. (At this stage, we do not assume that
\omega
is definable,
but eventually we will.)

Let
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
be a definable function.
Consider the definable map
L_{f}:\mathcal{M}_{X}\to\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
defined by

L_{f}(x,y):=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle z,x-y\rangle=f(x)-f(y)\}.

Let us show that
f
can be completed to a definable Whitney jet of class

C^{1,\omega}
on
X
if and only if
L_{f}
has a definable Lipschitz selection.

Recall that
(f,g)
is a
definable Whitney jet of class
C^{1,\omega}
on
X
if

f:X\to\mathbb{R}
and
g:X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
are definable maps and

\displaystyle\|(f,g)\|_{X,1,\omega}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{x\in X}|f(x)|+\sup_{x\in X}\|g(x)\|+|(f,g)|_{X,1,\omega}<\infty,

where

\displaystyle|(f,g)|_{X,1,\omega}

\displaystyle:=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in X\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)-\langle g(y),x-y\rangle|}{\|x-y\|\,\omega(\|x-y\|)}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in X\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{\|g(x)-g(y)\|}{\omega(\|x-y\|)}.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed definable set and let
f:X\to\mathbb{R}
be a bounded definable function.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

There exists a bounded definable function
g:X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(f,g)
is a definable Whitney jet of class
C^{1,\omega}
on
X
.

(2)

There exists a bounded definable Lipschitz selection
\ell:\mathcal{M}_{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
L_{f}
.

If the equivalent conditions
1
and
2
hold, then

(4.2)

\inf_{g}\|(f,g)\|_{X,1,\omega}\approx\sup_{x\in X}|f(x)|+\inf_{\ell}\Big\{\sup_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{M}_{X}}\|\ell(x,y)\|+|\ell|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M}_{X},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\Big\},

where the infimum on the left-hand side is taken over all
g
satisfying
1
and
the infimum on the right-hand side over all
\ell
satisfying
2
.

Note that “
\approx
” in (
4.2
) means that any of the two sides is
bounded by the other side up to a multiplicative factor which depends only on
n
.

Proof.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2)
Let
g
be as in (1) so that
(f,g)
is a definable Whitney jet of class
C^{1,\omega}
on
X
.
For
(x,y)\in\mathcal{M}_{X}
let
\ell(x,y)
be the point in
L_{f}(x,y)
closest to
g(x)
(with respect to the Euclidean metric), i.e.,

(4.3)

\|g(x)-\ell(x,y)\|_{2}=\operatorname{dist}_{2}(g(x),L_{f}(x,y)).

Then
\ell:\mathcal{M}_{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a definable selection of
L_{f}
. Let us check that it is bounded and Lipschitz.
We have

\displaystyle\|g(x)-\ell(x,y)\|_{2}=\frac{|f(x)-f(y)-\langle g(x),x-y\rangle|}{\|x-y\|_{2}}

which is bounded by

\displaystyle\|(f,g)\|_{X,1,\omega}\frac{\|x-y\|\,\omega(\|x-y\|)}{\|x-y\|_{2}}\leq\|(f,g)\|_{X,1,\omega},

in view of
\omega\leq 1
(cf. (
4.1
)).
Thus

\|\ell(x,y)\|\leq\|g(x)\|+\|g(x)-\ell(x,y)\|\leq 2\,\|(f,g)\|_{X,1,\omega},

i.e.,
\ell
is bounded with
\sup_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{M}_{X}}\|\ell(x,y)\|\leq 2\,\|(f,g)\|_{X,1,\omega}
.

For
(x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathcal{M}_{X}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\ell(x,y)-\ell(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\ell(x,y)-g(x)\|+\|g(x)-g(x^{\prime})\|+\|g(x^{\prime})-\ell(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|(f,g)\|_{X,1,\omega}\big(\omega(\|x-y\|)+\omega(\|x-x^{\prime}\|)+\omega(\|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}\|)\big)

\displaystyle=\|(f,g)\|_{X,1,\omega}\,\rho_{\omega}((x,y),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})),

consequently,
|\ell|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M}_{X},\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|(f,g)\|_{X,1,\omega}
.

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1)
Assume that
L_{f}
has a bounded definable Lipschitz selection

\ell:\mathcal{M}_{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
For ease of notation, set

C_{\ell}:=\sup_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{M}_{X}}\|\ell(x,y)\|+|\ell|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal{M}_{X},\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

If
x\in X
is an isolated point of
X
, let
\hat{x}
be a closest point in
X\setminus\{x\}
(with respect to
\|\cdot\|
).
Otherwise, let
\hat{x}:=x
. The point
\hat{x}
can be assigned to
x
in a definable way.
Define
g:X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
by

g(x):=\begin{cases}\ell(x,\hat{x})&\text{ if }x\text{ is an isolated point of }X,\\
\lim_{X\ni y\to x}\ell(x,y)&\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

Then
g
is definable and
\|g(x)\|\leq C_{\ell}
for all
x\in X
. The limit exists, since

\|\ell(x,y)-\ell(x,y^{\prime})\|\leq C_{\ell}\,\rho_{\omega}((x,y),(x,y^{\prime}))=C_{\ell}\big(\omega(\|x-y\|)+\omega(\|x-y^{\prime}\|)\big).

Let us check that
(f,g)
is a Whitney jet of class
C^{1,\omega}
on
X
.
For each
x\in X
let
(x_{i})
be a sequence in
X\setminus\{x\}
(possibly stationary) such that
x_{i}\to\hat{x}
and
\ell(x,x_{i})\to g(x)
.
Then

\displaystyle\|g(x)-g(x^{\prime})\|

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}\|\ell(x,x_{i})-\ell(x^{\prime},x^{\prime}_{i})\|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\ell}\lim_{i\to\infty}\rho_{\omega}((x,x_{i}),(x^{\prime},x^{\prime}_{i}))

\displaystyle\leq C_{\ell}\big(\omega(\|x-\hat{x}\|)+\omega(\|x^{\prime}-\hat{x}^{\prime}\|)+\omega(\|x-x^{\prime}\|)\big)\leq 3C_{\ell}\,\omega(\|x-x^{\prime}\|).

Since
\ell(x,y)\in L_{f}(x,y)
, we have

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(y)-\langle g(x),x-y\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\langle\ell(x,y)-g(x),x-y\rangle|

\displaystyle=\lim_{i\to\infty}|\langle\ell(x,y)-\ell(x,x_{i}),x-y\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq n\,\|x-y\|\limsup_{i\to\infty}\|\ell(x,y)-\ell(x,x_{i})\|

\displaystyle\leq n\,C_{\ell}\,\|x-y\|\limsup_{i\to\infty}\rho_{\omega}((x,y),(x,x_{i}))

\displaystyle\leq n\,C_{\ell}\,\|x-y\|\big(\omega(\|x-y\|)+\omega(\|x-\hat{x}\|)\big)

\displaystyle\leq 2n\,C_{\ell}\,\|x-y\|\,\omega(\|x-y\|).

Thus
|(f,g)|_{X,1,\omega}\leq 2n\,C_{\ell}
.
∎

By a little trick, the boundedness condition for
\ell
in
Proposition

4.1

may be absorbed by the Lipschitz condition.
To this end,
we add a point
*
to
\mathcal{M}_{X}
.
We define
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{X}:=\mathcal{M}_{X}\cup\{*\}
and extend the metric to
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{X}
by setting

\widetilde{\rho}_{\omega}|_{\mathcal{M}_{X}\times\mathcal{M}_{X}}:=\rho_{\omega}
and
\widetilde{\rho}_{\omega}((x,y),*)=\widetilde{\rho}_{\omega}(*,(x,y)):=2

for all
(x,y)\in\mathcal{M}_{X}

as well as
\widetilde{\rho}_{\omega}(*,*):=0
.
(Since
\omega\leq 1
, the triangle inequality holds: for
m,m^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}_{X}
we have
\rho_{\omega}(m,m^{\prime})\leq 3\leq 2+2=\rho_{\omega}(m,*)+\rho_{\omega}(*,m^{\prime})
.)

The map
L_{f}
is extended to
\widetilde{L}_{f}:\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{X}\to\mathcal{A}_{n-1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by setting
\widetilde{L}_{f}|_{\mathcal{M}_{X}}=L_{f}
and

\widetilde{L}_{f}(*):=\{0\}
.

.

Under the assumptions of

and

where the infimum on the left-hand side is taken over all
g
satisfying
1
and
the infimum on the right-hand side over all
\widetilde{\ell}
satisfying

.

Proof.

To see that (3) implies (2),
note that

\frac{\|\widetilde{\ell}(x,y)\|}{2}=\frac{\|\widetilde{\ell}(x,y)-\widetilde{\ell}(*)\|}{\widetilde{\rho}_{\omega}((x,y),*)}\leq|\widetilde{\ell}|_{\operatorname{Lip}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{X},\mathbb{R}^{n})}

, if

.

Conversely, if
\ell
is a bounded definable Lipschitz selection of

, then

.
∎

Next we endow

the restriction

to every subset

F_{Y}\in C^{1,\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\displaystyle h(x)

4.3.
Definable Kirszbraun theorem

As a consequence of the result of
[
3
]
used in the proof of
Proposition

4.7
,
a version of Kirszbraun’s theorem on the extension of Lipschitz functions by an explicit formula is given in
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]
:

Let
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be any set and
f:X\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
a Lipschitz map with Lipschitz constant
M
,
where
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbb{R}^{m}
carry the standard inner product (i.e., the Lipschitz constant
M
is computed
with respect to the Euclidean norms, whence no factor
\sqrt{n}
).
Then

\displaystyle F(x)

\displaystyle:=\nabla_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\operatorname{conv}(g)(x,0),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\text{ where}

\displaystyle g(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\inf_{z\in X}\big(\langle f(z),y\rangle+\frac{M}{2}\|x-z\|^{2}_{2}\big)+\frac{M}{2}\|x\|_{2}^{2}+M\|y\|_{2}^{2},\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m},

defines a Lipschitz extension
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
of
f
with the same Lipschitz constant
M
.

This follows easily by applying the mentioned theorem of
[
3
]

to the
1
-jet
((0,0),(0,f))
on
X\times\{0\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Actually, the result is valid for maps between Hilbert spaces.

As a corollary we get a
definable Kirszbraun theorem
(since all formulas are definable):

Theorem 4.9
.

If
f:X\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, is a definable Lipschitz map,
then the map
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
defined above is a definable Lipschitz extension of
f

preserving the Lipschitz constant.

The definable Kirszbraun theorem was first proved by Aschenbrenner and Fischer
[
1
]
,
but the explicit formula gives it immediately.

Remark 4.10
.

Actually, the definable Kirszbraun theorem
[
1
, Theorem A]
holds in
any expansion
\mathfrak{R}=(R,0,1,+,\cdot,<,\ldots)
of a real closed ordered field that is
definably complete
(i.e.,
each non-empty definable subset of
R
which is bounded from above has a least upper bound in
R
).
Definable completeness (which follows from o-minimality) is a necessary condition for the validity of the
definable Kirszbraun theorem;
see
[
1
, Proposition 5.2]
.
A careful inspection of the proofs of
[
3
, Theorem 3.4]
and
[
4
, Theorem 1.2]

shows that the explicit extension formula given above still holds and thus
gives a short alternative proof in this general setting.

4.4.
Remarks on (
1.3
)

The obstacle for obtaining (
1.3
) for all definable moduli of continuity
\omega

is that we do not know if a bound of the type (
4.6
) generally holds.
But, by a result of Pawłucki
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
,
Whitney jets of class
C^{m,\omega}
(not necessarily definable) on a definable closed set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}

extend to
C^{m,\omega}
-functions and the extension is by a continuous linear operator
which is a finite composite of operators that either preserve definability or
are defined by integration with respect to a parameter.

Integration may lead out of the o-minimal structure one started with.
For instance, if one starts with globally subanalytic sets and maps, due to a result of Lion ann Rolin
[
13
]
,
one lands in the algebra of real functions generated by globally subanalytic functions
and their logarithms.
For a class containing globally subanalytic functions and their complex exponentials (thus oscillatory functions)
that is stable under parameterized integration, see
[
8
]
.
Generally, for every o-minimal expansion
\mathscr{S}
of the real field there is an o-minimal expansion
\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}

in which the solutions of Pfaffian equations with
\mathscr{S}
-definable
C^{1}
-coefficients are

\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}
-definable,
by Speissegger
[
17
]
.
Thus we may conclude that
(
1.3
) at least holds if the trace norm on the left-hand side is
computed with respect to the functions definable in
\widetilde{\mathscr{S}}
instead of
those definable in the structure
\mathscr{S}
we started with.

In
[
15
]
, integration with respect to a parameter is used
for smoothing operators that are linear, continuous, and preserve moduli of continuity.
In the proof of the definable Whitney extension theorem of class
C^{m}

[
11
,
12
,
18
]
as well as of class
C^{m,\omega}

[
14
]
,
smooth cell decomposition
and subtle inequalities for the derivatives of definable functions are used instead of smoothing operators.

Notes on the Chernoff estimate
Valentin A.Zagrebnov ∗

Abstract
The purpose of the present notes is to examine the following issues related to the the Chernoff estimate: (1) For contractions on a Banach space we modify the n \sqrt{n} -estimate and apply it in the proof of the Chernoff product formula for C 0 C_{0} -semigroups in the strong operator topology. (2) We use the idea of a probabilistic approach, proving the Chernoff estimate in the strong operator topology, to uplift it to the operator-norm estimate for quasi-sectorial contraction semigroups. (3) The operator-norm Chernoff estimate is applied to quasi-sectorial contraction semigroups for proving the operator-norm convergence of the Dunford-Segal approximants.



keywords
Semigroup theory; Chernoff lemma; Quasi-sectorial contractions; Product formulae.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let bounded operator
C
on a Banach space
\mathfrak{X}

(
C\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{X})
)
be a contraction, that is,
\|C\|\leq 1
. Then

\{e^{t\,(C-\mathds{1})}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a norm-continuous contraction semigroup on

\mathfrak{X}
and one has the estimate

\|(C^{n}-e^{n\,(C-\mathds{1})})x\|\leq\ \sqrt{n}\ \|(C-\mathds{1})x\|\ \ ,

(1.1)

for all
x\in\mathfrak{X}
and natural
\ n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof 1.2
.

To prove the inequality (
1.1
) we use the representation

C^{n}-e^{n(C-\mathds{1})}=e^{-n}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{n^{m}}{m!}(C^{n}-C^{m})\ .

(1.2)

To proceed we insert

\|(C^{n}-C^{m})x\|\leq\left\|(C^{|n-m|}-\mathds{1})x\right\|\leq|m-n|\|(C-\mathds{1})x\|\ ,

(1.3)

into (
1.2
) to obtain by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality the estimate:

\begin{split}&\|(C^{n}-e^{n(C-\mathds{1})})x\|\leq\|(C-\mathds{1})x\|\ e^{-n}\ \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{n^{m}}{m!}|m-n|\leq\\
&\{\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}e^{-n}\ \frac{n^{m}}{m!}|m-n|^{2}\}^{1/2}\|(C-\mathds{1})x\|\ ,\ x\in\mathfrak{X}\ ,\end{split}

(1.4)

Note that the sum in the right-hand side of (
1.4
) can be calculated explicitly.
It is equal to
\sqrt{n}
, which yields (
1.1
).

2.5
) in

\sqrt[3]{n}
-Lemma

Lemma 2.1
.

(
\sqrt[3]{n}\
-Lemma)

Let
C
be a contraction on a Banach space
\mathfrak{X}
. Then

\{e^{t(C-\mathds{1}}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a norm-continuous
contraction semigroup on
\mathfrak{X}
and one has the estimate

\|(C^{n}-e^{n\,(C-\mathds{1})})\,x\|\leq\frac{n}{\epsilon_{n}^{2}}\ 2\,\|x\|+\epsilon_{n}\ \|(\mathds{1}-C)\,x\,\|\,,\quad\ n\in{\mathds{N}}\,,

(2.5)

for all
x\in\mathfrak{X}
and
\epsilon_{n}>0
. For the optimal value of the
parameter
\epsilon_{n}

:

\epsilon_{n}^{*}:=\left(\frac{4\,n\,\|x\|}{\|(\mathds{1}-C)\,x\,\|}\right)^{1/3},

(2.6)

in the right-hand side of
(
2.5
)
we obtain estimate

\|(C^{n}-e^{n\,(C-\mathds{1})})\,\|\leq\frac{3}{2}\,\sqrt[3]{n}\ \|2\ (\mathds{1}-C)\,\|^{2/3}\,,

(2.7)

which we call the
\sqrt[3]{n}\
-Lemma.

Proof 2.2
.

Since operator
C
is bounded and
\|C\|\leq 1
, the operator
(\mathds{1}-C)
is generator of a
norm-continuous contraction semigroup since:

\|\,e^{-t\,(\mathds{1}-C)}\,\|\leq e^{-t}\ \left\|\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}C^{m}\right\|\leq 1\,,\quad t\geq 0\,.

(2.8)

For proving the estimate (
2.5
) we use representation

C^{n}-e^{n(C-\mathds{1})}=e^{-n}\ \sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{n^{m}}{m!}\,(C^{n}-C^{m})\,.

(2.9)

Then we split the sum (
2.9
)
into two parts: the central part for
|m-n|\leq\epsilon_{n}
and the tails for

|m-n|>\epsilon_{n}
. Optimisation of the splitting parameter
\epsilon_{n}
in
(
2.5
) yields the best estimate (
2.7
).

For evaluation the tails we use the Tchebychëv inequality. Let

X_{n}\in\mathds{N}_{0}

be the Poisson random variable with the rate parameter
n\in\mathds{N}
,
that is, with the
probability distribution
\mathbb{P}\{X_{n}=m\}=n^{m}e^{-n}/m!\,
. Then one gets for
expectation:
{\mathbb{E}}(X_{n})=n
, and for variance:
\mbox{Var}(X_{n}):={\mathbb{E}}((X_{n}-{\mathbb{E}}(X_{n}))^{2})=n
. That being so, the Tchebychëv inequality
yields

\ \ \mathbb{P}\{|X_{n}-{\mathbb{E}}(X_{n})|>\epsilon\}\leq\frac{\mbox{Var}(X_{n})}{\epsilon_{n}^{2}}\,,\ \ {\rm{for\ any}\ }\ \epsilon_{n}>0.

(2.10)

Note that although for any
x\in\mathfrak{X}
there is
an evident bound:
\|(C^{n}-C^{m})\,x\|\leq 2\,\|x\|
, when estimating (
2.9
)
we shall also use below inequalities

\begin{split}\|(C^{\,n}-C^{\,m})\,x\|&=\|C^{\,n-k}(C^{k}-C^{\,m-n+k})\,x\|\\
&\leq|m-n|\ \|C^{\,n-k}(\mathds{1}-C)\,x\|,\ \ \ k=0,1,\ldots,n\ ,\end{split}

(2.11)

that keep difference:
(\mathds{1}-C)\,x
.
Then by
\|C\|\leq 1
and by the Tchebychëv inequality (
2.10
) we obtain the
estimate for
tails:

\begin{split}&e^{-n}\sum_{|m-n|>\epsilon_{n}}\frac{n^{m}}{m!}\|(C^{n}-C^{m})\,x\|\leq e^{-n}\sum_{|m-n|>\epsilon_{n}}\frac{n^{m}}{m!}\cdot\ 2\,\|x\|\,\\
&=\ \mathbb{P}\{|X_{n}-{\mathbb{E}}(X_{n})|>\epsilon_{n}\}\cdot\ 2\,\|x\|\leq\frac{n}{\epsilon_{n}^{2}}\ 2\,\|x\|\,.\end{split}

(2.12)

To evaluate the central part of the sum (
2.9
), when
|m-n|\leq\epsilon_{n}
,
note that by virtue of (
2.11
):

\displaystyle\|(C^{n}-C^{m})x\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|m-n|\ \|C^{n-[\epsilon_{n}]}\,(\mathds{1}-C)x\|

(2.13)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\epsilon_{n}\ \|(\mathds{1}-C)\,x\|.

Then we obtain:

e^{-n}\,\sum_{|m-n|\leq\epsilon_{n}}\frac{n^{m}}{m!}\ \|(C^{n}-C^{m})\,x\|\leq\epsilon_{n}\ \|(\mathds{1}-C)\,x\|\,,\quad x\in\mathfrak{X}\,,

(2.14)

for
n\in{\mathds{N}}
. Estimate (
2.14
), together with (
2.12
),
yield (
2.5
) for all
x\in\mathfrak{X}
and
\epsilon_{n}>0
.

Minimising the estimate (
2.5
) with respect to parameter
\epsilon_{n}>0
one gets
for
\epsilon_{n}
the optimal value (
2.6
) and

\frac{n}{{\epsilon_{n}^{*}}^{2}}\ 2\,\|x\|+\epsilon_{n}^{*}\ \|(\mathds{1}-C)\,x\,\|=\frac{3}{2}\,\sqrt[3]{n}\ (4\,\|x\|)^{1/3}\ \|(\mathds{1}-C)\,x\,\|^{2/3}\,,

(2.15)

for all
x\in\mathfrak{X}
and
n\in{\mathds{N}}
. As a consequence, (
2.5
)
and (
2.15
) yield estimate (
2.7
), which is the
\sqrt[3]{n}\
-Lemma.

Theorem 2.3
.

(Chernoff product formula
Cher68
;
Cher74
)

Let
\Phi:t\mapsto\Phi(t)
be a function from
\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
to contractions on
\mathfrak{X}

such that
\Phi(0)=\mathds{1}
. Let
\{U_{A}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
be a contraction
C_{0}
-semigroup,
and let domain
D\subset{\rm{dom}}(A)
be a core of related generator
A
.

If the function
\Phi(t)
has a strong right-derivative
\Phi^{\prime}(+0)
at
t=0

(
that is,
\Phi^{\prime}(+0)x
exists for any
x\in{\rm{dom}}(\Phi^{\prime}(+0))
)
and if

\Phi^{\prime}(+0)\,x:=\lim_{t\rightarrow+0}\frac{1}{t}(\Phi(t)-\mathds{1})\,x=-\,A\,x\ ,

(2.16)

for all
x\in D
, then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}[\Phi(t/n)]^{n}\,x=U_{A}(t)\,x\ ,

(2.17)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
and
x\in\mathfrak{X}
.

Proof 2.4
.

Consider the bounded approximations
\{A_{n}(s)\}_{n\geq 1}
of generator
A
:

A_{n}(s):=\frac{\mathds{1}-\Phi(s/n)}{s/n}\ ,\quad s\in{\mathds{R}}^{+}\,,\quad n\in{\mathds{N}}\,.

(2.18)

Note that these operators are
m
-accretive:

\|(A_{n}(s)+\zeta\,\mathds{1})^{-1}\|\leq({\rm{Re}}(\zeta))^{-1}
for
{\rm{Re}}(\zeta)>0

and for any
n\in{\mathds{N}}
. By
\|\Phi(t)\|\leq 1
together with (
2.18
)
and condition (
2.16
) we obtain
\|e^{-t\,A_{n}(s)}\|\leq 1
, but also

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}A_{n}(s)\,x=A\,x\ ,

(2.19)

for all
x\in D
and any
s\in{\mathds{R}}^{+}
.
Then, given that
D={\rm{core}}(A)
, by virtue of
the Trotter-Neveu-Kato generalised strong convergence theorem (see, e.g.,
Ref.
7
(Theorem 3.17), or Ref.
8
(Chapter III, Theorem 4.9)) one obtains

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}e^{-t\,A_{n}(s)}\,x=U_{A}(t)\,x\ ,\quad x\in\mathfrak{X}\,,\quad s>0\,,\quad t\in{\mathds{R}}_{0}^{+}\,.

(2.20)

So, (
2.20
) is the strong and uniform in
t
and in
s
convergence of
contractive approximants
\{e^{-t\,A_{n}(s)}\}_{n\geq 1}
for
t\in{[0,\tau]}
and

s\in{(0,s_{0}]}
.

Now, by Lemma
2.1
(
2.7
) we obtain for contraction
C:=\Phi(t/n)
:

\begin{split}&\|[\Phi(t/n)]^{n}\,x-e^{-t\,A_{n}(t)}\,x\|=\|([\Phi(t/n)]^{n}-e^{{n}(\Phi(t/n)-\mathds{1})})\ x\|\\
&\leq\frac{3}{2}\,\sqrt[3]{n}\ (4\,\|x\|)^{1/3}\ \|(\mathds{1}-\Phi(t/n))\,x\,\|^{2/3}\ ,\quad x\in\mathfrak{X}\ .\end{split}

(2.21)

Since by (
2.19
) one gets for any
x\in D
and uniformly on
(0,t_{0}]
:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sqrt[3]{n}\ \|\,(\mathds{1}-\Phi(t/n))\ x\,\|^{2/3}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}t^{2/3}\,n^{-1/3}\ \|A_{n}(t)\ x\|^{2/3}=0\ ,

(2.22)

equations (
2.21
) and (
2.22
) provide uniformly on
(0,t_{0}]

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|\,[\Phi(t/n)]^{n}\,x-e^{-t\,A_{n}(t)}\,x\|=0,\ \quad x\in D\ .

(2.23)

Then (
2.20
) and (
2.23
) yield uniformly in
t\in[0,t_{0}]
the limit:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}[\Phi(t/n)]^{n}\,x=U_{A}(t)\,x\ ,\quad x\in D\ .

(2.24)

Note that by density of
D
and by the uniform estimate

\|\,[\Phi(t/n)]^{n}\,x-e^{-t\,A_{n}(t)}x\|\leq 2\,\|x\|
the convergence in (
2.23
)
can be extended to all
x\in\mathfrak{X}
. Indeed, it is known that on the bounded
subsets of
\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{X})
the topology of point-wise convergence on a
dense subset
D\subset\mathfrak{X}
coincides with the strong operator topology,
see, e.g., Ref.
14
(Chapter III, Lemma 3.5).
As a consequence, the limit (
2.23
), when being extended to
x\in\mathfrak{X}
, and
limit (
2.20
) yield the extension of (
2.24
) to (
2.17
).

The limit (
2.17
) that involves derivative (
2.16
) is called the

Chernoff product formula
for contractive
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{U_{A}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}

in the
strong
operator topology, cf. Ref.
8
(Chapter III, Section 5a.).

Proposition 2.5
.

(Lie-Trotter product formula
Trot59
;
Cher74
)

Let
A
,
B
and
C
be generators of contraction
C_{0}
-semigroups on
\mathfrak{X}
. Suppose that
algebraic sum

Cx=Ax+Bx\ ,

(2.25)

is valid for all
x\in D
, where domain
D={\rm{core}}\,(C)
. Then the semigroup

\{U_{C}(t)\}_{t\geq 0}
can be
approximated on
\mathfrak{X}
in the strong operator topology by the
Lie-Trotter product formula:

e^{-tC}\,x=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}(e^{-tA/n}e^{-tB/n})^{n}\,x\ ,\ \ \ x\in\mathfrak{X}\ ,

(2.26)

for all
t\in\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
and
C:=\overline{(A+B)}
is closure of the
operator-sum in
(
2.25
)
.

Proof 2.6
.

Let us define the contraction
\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}\ni t\mapsto\Phi(t)
,
\Phi(0)=\mathds{1}
, by

\Phi(t):=e^{-tA}e^{-tB}\ .

(2.27)

Note that if
x\in D
, then derivative

\Phi^{\prime}(+0)x=\lim_{t\rightarrow+0}\frac{1}{t}(\Phi(t)-\mathds{1})\ x=-(A+B)\ x\ .

(2.28)

Now we are in position to apply Theorem
2.3
. This yields (
2.26
) for
generator
C:=\overline{(A+B)}
.

Extensions of the strongly convergent Lie-Trotter product formula of

)), which is of a

nature than variational estimate (
2.5
)
(
\sqrt[3]{n}
-Lemma
2.1

In fact, it is a kind of

)
(

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
C\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{X})
be contraction on a Banach space
\mathfrak{X}
. Then

\{e^{t(C-\mathds{1}}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a norm-continuous contractive semigroup on

\mathfrak{X}
and the following estimate

\|(C^{n}-e^{n(C-\mathds{1})})\,x\|\leq\ \frac{n}{2}\ \Big(\|(C-\mathds{1})^{2}\,x\|+\frac{e^{2}}{3}\,\|(C-\mathds{1})^{3}\,x\|\Big)\ ,

(3.29)

holds for all
\ n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in\mathfrak{X}
.

Proof 3.2
.

The first assertion is proven in Lemma
2.1
, see (
2.8
).

To prove inequality (
3.29
) we use the telescopic representation:

C^{n}-e^{n(C-\mathds{1})}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\ C^{n-k-1}\,(C-e^{(C-\mathds{1})})\,e^{k(C-\mathds{1})}\ .

(3.30)

To proceed we exploit that operator
C\in\mathcal{L}(\mathfrak{X})
is bounded and therefore

C-e^{(C-\mathds{1})}=-\,\frac{1}{2}\ (\mathds{1}-C)^{2}-(\mathds{1}-C)^{3}\,\sum_{m=3}^{\infty}\,\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}\,(\mathds{1}-C)^{m-3}\,,

(3.31)

for the operator-norm convergent series. Hence, owing to
\|C\|\leq 1
one gets estimate

\Big\|\sum_{m=3}^{\infty}\,\frac{1}{m!}\,(\mathds{1}-C)^{m-3}\Big\|\leq\frac{1}{6}\ e^{\|\mathds{1}-C\|}\leq\frac{e^{2}}{6}\,.

(3.32)

Then on account of (
3.30
) - (
3.32
) and (
2.8
)
we obtain inequality (
3.29
).

Corollary 3.3
.

(Chernoff product formula)

Let
\Phi:t\mapsto\Phi(t)
be a function from
\mathbb{R}_{0}^{+}
to contractions on
\mathfrak{X}

such that
\Phi(0)=\mathds{1}
, which satisfies conditions of Theorem
2.3
.
Then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|([\Phi(t/n)]^{n}-e^{{n}(\Phi(t/n)-\mathds{1})})\ x\|=0\,,\quad\ x\in\mathfrak{X}\,,

(3.33)

and as a result one gets the product formula
(
2.17
)
.

Proof 3.4
.

On account of Theorem
3.1
we obtain by (
3.29
) the estimate

\displaystyle\|([\Phi(t/n)]^{n}-e^{{n}(\Phi(t/n)-\mathds{1})})\ x\|\leq

(3.34)

\displaystyle\frac{t^{2}}{2n}\ \Big(\Big\|\frac{n^{2}}{t^{2}}\,(\mathds{1}-\Phi(t/n))^{2}\,x\Big\|+\frac{2\,e^{2}}{3}\ \Big\|\frac{n^{2}}{t^{2}}(\mathds{1}-\Phi(t/n))^{2}\,x\Big\|\Big),\quad\ x\in\mathfrak{X}\,.

Note that by (
2.19
) for any
t\in{\mathds{R}}^{+}
we have on the dense set

D={\rm{core}}(A)
:

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{n}{t}\,(\mathds{1}-\Phi(t/n))\,x=A\,x\ ,\quad x\in D\,.

(3.35)

Given that generator
A
of contractive
C_{0}
-semigroup is accretive,
the range of resolvent:
{\rm{ran}}((A+\zeta\mathds{1})^{-1})=\mathfrak{X}
,
for
{\rm{Re}}(\zeta)>0
. As a consequence Ref.

(Chapter III, Problem 2.9

where

),

.

By virtue of estimate (

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|([\Phi(t/n)]^{n}-e^{{n}(\Phi(t/n)-\mathds{1})})\ x\|=0\ ,\quad x\in D\,.

(3.37)

Then similarly to concluding arguments in Theorem

Now, given that

Operator-Norm Chernoff Estimate

Definition 4.1
.

CaZ01
;
Zag08

Contraction
C
on the Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
is called quasi-sectorial for
semi-angle
\alpha\in[0,\pi/2)
with respect to the vertex at
z=1
, if its numerical
range
W(C)\subseteq D_{\alpha}
. Here the subset of complex plane

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}:=

(4.38)

\displaystyle\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|z|\leq\sin\alpha\}\cup\{z\in{\mathbb{C}}:|\arg(1-z)|\leq\alpha\ {\rm{and}}\ |z-1|\leq\cos\alpha\}.

We comment that
D_{\alpha}\subset D_{\pi/2}={\mathbb{D}}
(unit disc) and recall
that a
genera
l contraction
C
satisfies a weaker condition:
W(C)\subseteq{\mathbb{D}}
.

Note that if operator
C
is a quasi-sectorial contraction, then
\mathds{1}-C
is an

m
-sectorial operator with vertex
z=0
and semi-angle
\alpha

Proposition 4.2
.

Let

-sectorial operator with semi-angle

Proof 4.3

Seeing that the spectrum

(4.39)

Next, for any

(

, the proof must take into account a
subordination

of generators
A
and
B
.
A variety of the
one-
and
two-step
methods (including the Chernoff estimate),
as well as of the corresponding conditions, that ensure the convergence of the Trotter product

in Ref.

(A+B)

5
Chernoff Estimate and Dunford-Segal Approximation

Here we continue with more comments about
optimal
results for the rate of
convergence for operator-norm approximants of
C_{0}
-semigroups mentioned in
Remark
4.16
. This approximation theory was advanced in Ref.
9

(and improved later in Ref.
10
) for the
Yosida
, the
Dunford-Segal

and the
Euler
approximations of
C_{0}
-semigroups.
In addition to standard analysis of approximations in the
strong operator topology (cf. Ref.
3
) the paper Ref.
9
proposed to
study a
vector-dependent
estimates of the convergence rate for approximants, see
Remark
3.5
.
For holomorphic
C_{0}
-semigroups this estimates of convergence can be uplifted to the
operator-norm topology.

This last aspect will be considered in the present section versus the Chernoff operator-norm
estimate. We start by citation of the optimal
O(1/n)
result for the rate of the operator-norm
convergence for a simple case of the Euler approximants (
Euler formula
(
4.55
)),
which also shows a sectorial dependence of the upper bound.

Proposition 5.1
.

(Ref.
1
)

Let
A
be an
m
-sectorial operator in Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
, with semi-angle

\alpha\in[0,\pi/2)
and vertex at
z=0
. Then

\{e^{-t\,A}\}_{\,t\geq 0}
is a holomorphic quasi-sectorial contraction semigroup and
one infers that

\left\|\left(\mathds{1}+{t}A/n\right)^{-n}-e^{-tA}\right\|\leq\frac{M_{\alpha}}{(\cos\alpha)^{2}\ n}\ ,\ t\geq 0\ ,\ n\in\mathbb{N}\ ,

(5.57)

where

\frac{\pi\sin\alpha}{2\alpha}\leq M_{\alpha}\leq\min\left(\frac{\pi-\alpha}{\alpha},\,M\right)\quad{\rm{and}}\quad\frac{\pi}{2}\leq M\leq 2+\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\ .

(5.58)

Note that estimate (
5.58
) of the coefficient
M_{\alpha}
in (
5.57
) is not

optimal
. To this aim we elucidate (
5.58
) for the case of a non-negative
self-adjoint operator
A\,
, that is, for
\alpha=0
, cf. Remark
4.11
.
As a result one obtains by the spectral calculus:

\left\|(\mathds{1}+tA/n)^{-n}-e^{-tA}\right\|\leq\frac{e^{-1}}{n}\,,\quad t\geq 0\ ,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}\ ,

(5.59)

cf. one of the error bound in (
4.52
).
In Ref.
9
(Corollary 1.6 c) the Proposition
5.1
was extended
to a Banach space and any bounded holomorphic
C_{0}
-semigroup.

We conclude this section by demonstration that the Chernoff estimate (
4.46
)

itself
, i.e., without the two-step construction (
4.56
),
yields for holomorphic
C_{0}
-semigroups the
Dunford-Segal
approximation
introduced in Ref.
9
, Theorem 1.1 b).

Theorem 5.2
.

If
A
is an
m
-sectorial operator in Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
, with semi-angle

\alpha\in[0,\pi/2)
and vertex at
z=0
, then

\left\|e^{-n\,(\mathds{1}-e^{-t\,A/n})}-e^{-t\,A}\right\|\leq\frac{N}{(\cos\alpha)^{2}\ n}\ ,\quad t\geq 0\ ,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}\ ,

(5.60)

for some bounded
N>0
.

Proof 5.3
.

By virtue of Proposition
5.1
one gets that

\{e^{-t\,A/n}\}_{n\geq 1}
for
t\geq 0
and each
n\in\mathbb{N}

is a holomorphic quasi-sectorial contraction
C_{0}
-semigroup
\{e^{-t\,A/n}\}_{t\geq 0}
.
Let
C:=e^{-t\,A/n}
. Then by the operator-norm Chernoff estimate
(Theorem
4.8
) we obtain

\left\|e^{-t\,A}-e^{-n\,(\mathds{1}-e^{-t\,A/n})}\right\|\leq\frac{L_{\alpha}}{n^{1/3}}\ ,\quad t\geq 0\ ,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}\ .

(5.61)

This yields the operator-norm approximation of quasi-sectorial contraction semigroup

\{e^{-t\,A}\}_{t\geq 0}
by the Dunford-Segal approximants

\{e^{-n\,(\mathds{1}-e^{-t\,A/n})}\}_{n\geq 1}
with the rate
O(1/n^{1/3})
on a
Hilbert space
\mathfrak{H}
.

The uplifting the estimate (
5.61
) to sectorial dependent optimal rate (
5.60
)
follows verbatim the arguments in the proof of Proposition
5.1
.

Note that our definition of the
Dunford-Segal
approximants is (slightly) different from
that in Refs.
9
(Corollary 1.6 b), where assertion was extended to a Banach space
for any bounded holomorphic
C_{0}
-semigroup.

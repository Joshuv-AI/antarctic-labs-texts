Introduction

A central problem in analytic number theory is the study of vanishing or non-vanishing of
L
-functions at the central point. Some arithmetic consequences arise for example due to the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture, which links the order of the central zero of an elliptic curve
L
-function with its rank (see for example
Wi
). Another application was provided by Iwaniec and Sarnak
IS2
, who proved that at least 50% of
L
-functions in certain families of cusp forms do not vanish at the central point, and showed that any improvement on this proportion would rule out the existence of Landau-Siegel zeros.

A conjecture attributed to Chowla states that
L(1/2,\chi)\neq 0
for all Dirichlet characters
\chi
(see
Ch
for the conjecture in the case of real Dirichlet characters). In this paper we study the non-vanishing of Dirichlet
L
-functions at the central point under the unlikely assumption that there exists an exceptional Dirichlet character.

Unconditionally, Balasubramanian and Murty
BM
were the first to show that for any sufficiently large prime
q
, one has that
L(1/2,\chi)\neq 0
for a positive proportion of Dirichlet characters
\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)
. This result was significantly improved by Iwaniec and Sarnak
IS
, who obtained the non-vanishing proportion
1/3-\varepsilon
(also for non-prime
q
). The best known result for prime moduli is
5/13-\varepsilon
due to Khan, Milićević, and Ngo
KMN
.

The works of Murty
Murty
and Bui, Pratt, and Zaharescu
BPZ
prove the non-vanishing proportion
1/2-o(1)
conditionally — Murty under the generalized Riemann hypothsesis, and Bui, Pratt, and Zaharescu under the existence of an exceptional character with modulus of suitable size.

In this paper we improve on the result of Bui, Pratt, and Zaharescu. In particular we obtain the following corollary, improving their proportion
1/2-o(1)
to
1-o(1)
.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed. Let
D>1
be a squarefree fundamental discriminant and let
\psi
be the associated primitive quadratic character modulo
D
. Assume that

L(1,\psi)\ll\frac{1}{(\log D)^{25+\varepsilon}}.

(1)

Then, for any fixed
C>300
and any prime
q
such that

D^{300}\leq q\leq D^{C},

we have

\left\lvert\{\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)\colon L(1/2,\chi)\neq 0\}\right\rvert=(1+o(1))\varphi(q),

where the rate of convergence of
o(1)
depends only on
\varepsilon,C
and the implied constant in (
1
).

Remark 1
.

It is feasible that it is possible to loosen the condition (
1
) to the condition that
L(1,\psi)=o(1/\log D)
. This would require reworking the arguments in
BPZ
with a more optimally chosen mollifier than (
5
) below, and being very careful about not losing any logarithmic factors. It might be possible to carry this out by adapting the arguments from
CI
. In
CI
Conrey and Iwaniec considered a related problem, showing that if
L(1,\psi)=o(1/\log D)
, then, for any Dirichlet
L
-function, almost all zeros, whose imaginary part is on a suitable range, are simple and lie on the critical line.

As in
BPZ
, we actually get a more quantitative result — the following holds unconditionally, but is non-trivial only in case an exceptional character exists.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed. Let
D>1
be a squarefree fundamental discriminant and let
\psi
be the associated primitive quadratic character modulo
D
. Let
C>300
be fixed and let
q
be a prime such that

D^{300}\leq q\leq D^{C}.

(2)

Then, for any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varphi(q)}\sum_{\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)}\mathbf{1}_{\left\lvert L(1/2,\chi)\right\rvert\geq\frac{\delta^{3/2}}{(\log q)^{9/2}}}=1+O\left(\delta^{-2}L(1,\psi)(\log q)^{25+\varepsilon}+\frac{\delta^{-2}}{(\log q)^{1-\varepsilon}}+\delta\right).

(3)

Corollary
1
immediately follows from applying Theorem
2
with
\delta=(\log q)^{-\varepsilon/4}
and
\varepsilon/3
in place of
\varepsilon
. In Theorem
2
and other statements, the implied constants are allowed to depend on
\varepsilon
and
C
(which are said to be fixed), but not on
D
or
q
.

Remark 2
.

We have not tried to optimize the lower bound we get for
\left\lvert L(1/2,\chi)\right\rvert
in Theorem
2
. By estimating the left hand side of (
14
) below more carefully, it would probably be possible to improve the power of
\log q
in the lower bound. Furthermore, similarly to Remark
1
, it might be possible to improve on the error term.

As in many works, we consider only the even primitive characters, the case of odd primitive characters being handled similarly (since
q
is prime, there is only one non-primitive character
\chi_{0}
so its contribution is negligible). We write
\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}
for a sum over primitive even characters modulo
q
, and
\varphi^{+}(q)
for the number of such characters.

Our proof is based on the work of Bui, Pratt and Zaharescu
BPZ
and the equidistribution of the product
\varepsilon(\chi)\varepsilon(\psi\chi)
of root numbers. Here and later,
\varepsilon(\chi)
denotes the sign of the functional equation of
L(s,\chi)
, which can also be written as a normalized Gauss sum

\varepsilon(\chi):=\frac{\tau(\chi)}{q^{1/2}}=\frac{1}{q^{1/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)\\
(a,q)=1\end{subarray}}\chi(a)e\left(\frac{a}{q}\right).

(4)

The following proposition which we will prove in Section
4
shows that
\varepsilon(\chi)\varepsilon(\psi\chi)
is equidistributed on the unit circle when
\chi
runs over even primitive characters modulo
q
.

Proposition 3
.

Let
D>1
be a squarefree fundamental discriminant and let
\psi
be the associated primitive quadratic character modulo
D
. Let
q
be a prime such that
q\nmid D
.

For each character
\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)
, let
\theta_{\chi}\in(0,2\pi]
be such that
\varepsilon(\chi)\varepsilon(\chi\psi)=e(\theta_{\chi})
. Then, for any interval
(\alpha,\beta]\subseteq(0,2\pi]
, we have

\frac{1}{\varphi^{+}(q)}\,\,\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)}\mathbf{1}_{\theta_{\chi}\in(\alpha,\beta]}=\beta-\alpha+O(q^{-1/4}),

where the implied constant is absolute.

2
The work of Bui, Pratt and Zaharescu

Let
\varepsilon,D,\psi,C,
and
q
be as in Theorem
2
. Following
BPZ
, for any character
\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)
, we write

L_{\chi}(s):=L(s,\chi)L(s,\chi\psi)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(1*\psi)(n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}}.

Note that Theorem
2
is non-trivial only when
\psi
is an exceptional character modulo
D
(in the sense that (
1
) holds for some
\varepsilon>0
), and in this case we expect
1*\psi(n)
to vanish often once
n>D^{2}
, say (see for example
(
BPZ
, formula (2.2))
).

Bui, Pratt and Zaharescu
BPZ
consider the mollified
L
-functions
L_{\chi}(1/2)M(\chi)
, where the mollifier is taken as

M(\chi):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X,\\
D\nmid n\end{subarray}}\frac{(\mu*\mu\psi)(n)\chi(n)}{\sqrt{n}}

(5)

with
X:=D^{20}
. In the classical setting, it is crucial to take the mollifier as long as possible to make many of the coefficients of the mollified
L
-function vanish; in the exceptional case, the coefficients
(1*\psi)(n)
of
L_{\chi}(1/2)
are lacunary once
n
is larger than a small power of
D
, so taking
X=q^{\kappa}
for a small
\kappa>0
is sufficient. This explains why we can obtain better non-vanishing results assuming the existence of exceptional characters.

For convenience, we make the same choice of parameters as
BPZ
, so that
X=D^{20}
and (
2
) holds.

Write
Q=q\sqrt{D}/\pi
. Then
(
BPZ
, formula (4.1))
yields

L_{\chi}(1/2)M(\chi)=V_{1}\left(\frac{1}{Q}\right)+\varepsilon(\chi)\varepsilon(\chi\psi)V_{2}\left(\frac{1}{Q}\right)+O\left(\left\lvert B_{1}(\chi)\right\rvert+\left\lvert B_{2}(\chi)\right\rvert\right),

(6)

where, for
j=1,2
,
V_{j}(x)
is a smooth weight as in
(
BPZ
, Section 3)
and

B_{j}(\chi):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\leq X,\\
D\nmid a,\\
an>1\end{subarray}}\frac{(\mu*\mu\psi)(a)(1*\psi)(n)\chi(an)}{\sqrt{an}}V_{j}\left(\frac{n}{Q}\right).

The formula (
6
) is obtained in
BPZ
using the approximate functional equation (see
(
BPZ
, Lemma 3.2)
) and isolating the first summand in each term. By
(
BPZ
, Lemma 3.4)
we have
V_{j}(1/Q)=1+O(Q^{-1/2+\varepsilon})
for
j=1,2
and hence (
6
) implies that, for some absolute constant
C_{0}\geq 1
,

\left\lvert L_{\chi}(1/2)M(\chi)-(1+\varepsilon(\chi)\varepsilon(\chi\psi))\right\rvert\leq C_{0}\left(\left\lvert B_{1}(\chi)\right\rvert+\left\lvert B_{2}(\chi)\right\rvert+Q^{-1/2+\varepsilon}\right).

(7)

Furthemore,
(
BPZ
, Proposition 4.1)
gives that, for
j=1,2
, any
\varepsilon>0
, and any prime
q
in the range (
2
),

\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)}\left\lvert B_{j}(\chi)\right\rvert^{2}\ll L(1,\psi)q(\log q)^{25+\varepsilon}+\frac{q}{(\log q)^{1-\varepsilon}}.

(8)

The strategy of Bui, Pratt and Zaharescu
BPZ
is to proceed with the usual method of applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to obtain that

\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)\\
L(1/2,\chi)\neq 0\end{subarray}}1\geq\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)\\
L_{\chi}(1/2)M(\chi)\neq 0\end{subarray}}1\geq\frac{\left\lvert\sideset{}{{}^{+}_{\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)}}{\sum}L_{\chi}(1/2)M(\chi)\right\rvert^{2}}{\sideset{}{{}^{+}_{\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)}}{\sum}\left\lvert L_{\chi}(1/2)M(\chi)\right\rvert^{2}}.

(9)

Bui, Pratt and Zaharescu then use (
6
) and (
8
) to compute the first and second moments of
L_{\chi}(1/2)M(\chi)
— this gives that (assuming that
\psi
is an exceptional character), on the right hand side of (
9
), the numerator equals
(1+o(1))\varphi^{+}(q)^{2}
whereas the denominator equals
(2+o(1))\varphi^{+}(q)
. This yields the non-vanishing proportion
1/2+o(1)
.

Note that the application of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in (
9
) is costly, because by (
7
) the mollified
L
-functions still oscillate — our strategy is to dispose of the use of the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and instead exploit the fact that (
7
) holds for individual L-functions. Actually, from (
7
), the equidistribution of the signs
\varepsilon(\chi)\varepsilon(\chi\psi)
(Proposition
3
) and (
8
), one can see that
L_{\chi}(1/2)M(\chi)
for even
\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)
are equidistributed in the circle
\left\lvert z-1\right\rvert=1
, which directly implies the non-vanishing of
L_{\chi}(1/2)M(\chi)
for almost all characters
\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)
.

The variation of the root number has been utilized also in earlier (unconditional) results that involved a two-piece mollifier (see e.g.
MV
and
KMN
) — in these works one used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, but optimized its application.

Proof of Theorem

In this section we prove Theorem
2
assuming Proposition
3
. Let
\varepsilon,D,\psi,C,q,
and
\delta
be as in Theorem
2
and let
C_{0}
be as in (
7
). Now (
8
) implies that

\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)}\mathbf{1}_{\left\lvert B_{j}(\chi)\right\rvert\geq\delta/(4C_{0})}\ll\delta^{-2}\left(L(1,\psi)q(\log q)^{25+\varepsilon/2}+\frac{q}{(\log q)^{1-\varepsilon/2}}\right).

Furthermore, by Proposition
3
we know that
\left\lvert 1+\varepsilon(\chi)\varepsilon(\chi\psi)\right\rvert\geq 2\delta
for all
\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)
apart from an exceptional set consisting of
\ll\delta\varphi(q)+q^{3/4}
characters.

Combining (
7
) with the triangle inequality and these observations we obtain that, apart from an exceptional set of size

\ll\delta^{-2}L(1,\psi)q(\log q)^{25+\varepsilon}+\delta^{-2}\frac{q}{(\log q)^{1-\varepsilon}}+\delta\varphi(q),

(10)

we have

\left\lvert L_{\chi}(1/2)M(\chi)\right\rvert\geq 2\delta-C_{0}\left(\frac{\delta}{4C_{0}}+\frac{\delta}{4C_{0}}+Q^{-1/2+\varepsilon}\right)>\delta.

(11)

This already yields (
3
) with
\mathbf{1}_{\left\lvert L(1/2,\chi)\right\rvert\geq\delta^{3/2}/(\log q)^{9/2}}
replaced by
\mathbf{1}_{\left\lvert L(1/2,\chi)\right\rvert\neq 0}
and is thus sufficient for obtaining Corollary
1
. We next proceed to showing a lower bound for
\left\lvert L(1/2,\chi)\right\rvert
outside an acceptable exceptional set.

Recall that

L_{\chi}(1/2)M(\chi)=L(1/2,\chi)L(1/2,\chi\psi)M(\chi).

Since (
11
) holds apart from an exceptional set of size (
10
), Theorem
2
follows if we can establish that there are at most
O(\delta\varphi(q))
characters
\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)
for which
\left\lvert L(1/2,\chi\psi)M(\chi)\right\rvert\geq\delta^{-1/2}(\log q)^{9/2}
. This follows if

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}\left\lvert L(1/2,\chi\psi)M(\chi)\right\rvert^{2}\ll\varphi(q)(\log q)^{9},

(12)

and so it suffices to establish (
12
).

By the approximate functional equation we have for any primitive character
\chi
(see e.g.
(
IS
, formula (2.2))
)

L(1/2,\chi\psi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)\psi(n)+\varepsilon(\chi\psi)\overline{\chi}(n)\overline{\psi}(n)}{\sqrt{n}}W\left(n\sqrt{\pi/(qD)}\right),

(13)

where
W
(denoted by
V
in
IS
) is such that
W(y)=1+O(y^{10})
and
W(y)\ll y^{-10}
. Using these bounds we see that

L(1/2,\chi\psi)=\sum_{n\leq(qD)^{\frac{3}{4}}}\frac{\chi(n)\psi(n)+\varepsilon(\chi\psi)\overline{\chi}(n)\overline{\psi}(n)}{\sqrt{n}}W\left(n\sqrt{\pi/(qD)}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{qD}\right).

Using also the definition of
M(\chi)
(see (
5
)) and the orthogonality of characters (adding back
\chi=\chi_{0}
), noting that
X(qD)^{3/4}\leq q
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}\left\lvert L(1/2,\chi\psi)M(\chi)\right\rvert^{2}\\
&\ll\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)\end{subarray}}|M(\chi)|^{2}\left|\sum_{n\leq(qD)^{\frac{3}{4}}}\frac{\chi(n)\psi(n)+\varepsilon(\chi\psi)\overline{\chi}(n)\overline{\psi}(n)}{\sqrt{n}}W\left(n\sqrt{\pi/(qD)}\right)\right|^{2}\\
&\qquad+\frac{1}{q^{2}D^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)\end{subarray}}|M(\chi)|^{2}\\
&\ll\varphi(q)\operatorname*{\sum\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1},k_{2}\leq(qD)^{3/4}\\
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\leq X\\
k_{1}\ell_{1}=k_{2}\ell_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left\lvert(\mu\ast\mu\psi)(\ell_{1})\right\rvert\left\lvert(\mu\ast\mu\psi)(\ell_{2})\right\rvert}{\sqrt{k_{1}\ell_{1}k_{2}\ell_{2}}}+\frac{\varphi(q)}{q^{2}D^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\leq X\\
D\nmid n\end{subarray}}\frac{|(\mu\ast\mu\psi)(n)|^{2}}{n}\\
&\ll\varphi(q)\sum_{n\leq X(qD)^{3/4}}\frac{d_{3}(n)^{2}}{n}+\frac{X^{\varepsilon}}{qD^{2}}\ll\varphi(q)\prod_{p\leq X(qD)^{3/4}}\left(1+\frac{3^{2}}{p}\right).\end{aligned}

(14)

Now (
12
) follows from Mertens’ theorem, and so the proof of Theorem
2
is completed.

4
Proof of Proposition
3

In this section we prove Proposition
3
. By the Erdős-Turán inequality (see e.g.
(
Montgomery
, Corollary 1.1)
with
K=\lfloor q^{1/4}\rfloor
),

\displaystyle\Bigg|\frac{1}{\varphi^{+}(q)}

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)}\mathbf{1}_{\theta_{\chi}\in(\alpha,\beta]}-(\beta-\alpha)\Bigg|\leq\frac{1}{q^{1/4}}+\frac{3}{\varphi^{+}(q)}\sum_{1\leq k\leq q^{1/4}}\frac{1}{k}\left\lvert\quad\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)}e(k\theta_{\chi})\right\rvert.

Since
e(k\theta_{\chi})=(\varepsilon(\chi)\varepsilon(\chi\psi))^{k}
, Proposition
3
follows immediately from the following lemma.

Lemma 4
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
D>1
be a square-free fundamental discriminant and let
\psi
be the associated primitive quadratic character modulo
D
. Let
q
be a prime with
q\nmid D
. Then

\left\lvert\ \,\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)}\left(\varepsilon(\chi)\varepsilon(\chi\psi)\right)^{k}\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{q^{k}}+2k\cdot\frac{\varphi(q)}{q^{1/2}}.

Proof.

Our argument generalizes an argument in
(
BPZ
, Section 4)
where the special case
k=1
was established (see
(
BPZ
, formula (4.6))
). Since
(q,D)=1
, one gets (as pointed out in
(
BPZ
, page 603)
) from (
4
) and the Chinese remainder theorem

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\chi\psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(Dq)^{1/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a\>(\mathrm{mod}\>Dq)\\
(a,Dq)=1\end{subarray}}\chi(a)\psi(a)e\left(\frac{a}{Dq}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(Dq)^{1/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\>(\mathrm{mod}\>D)\\
(b,D)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}c\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)\\
(c,q)=1\end{subarray}}\chi(bq+cD)\psi(bq+cD)e\left(\frac{bq+cD}{Dq}\right)

\displaystyle=\chi(D)\psi(q)\varepsilon(\psi)\varepsilon(\chi).

Hence

\varepsilon(\chi)\varepsilon(\chi\psi)=\chi(D)\psi(q)\varepsilon(\psi)\varepsilon(\chi)^{2}

and, using also (
4
),

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)\end{subarray}}\left(\varepsilon(\chi)\varepsilon(\chi\psi)\right)^{k}\\
&=\frac{\psi(q)^{k}\varepsilon(\psi)^{k}}{q^{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dotsc,a_{2k}\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)\\
(a_{j},q)=1\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{a_{1}+\dotsb+a_{2k}}{q}\right)\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)}\chi(D^{k}a_{1}\dotsm a_{2k}).\end{aligned}

(15)

By orthogonality of characters we have, for any prime
q
and integers
m,n
such that
(mn,q)=1
,

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)}\chi(m)\overline{\chi}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sideset{}{{}^{*}}{\sum}_{\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)}(1+\chi(-1))\chi(m)\overline{\chi}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)}(1+\chi(-1))\chi(m)\overline{\chi}(n)-1=\mathbf{1}_{q\mid(m\pm n)}\frac{\varphi(q)}{2}-1.

Applying this to (
15
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sideset{}{{}^{+}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)\end{subarray}}\left(\varepsilon(\chi)\varepsilon(\chi\psi)\right)^{k}&=\frac{\psi(q)^{k}\varepsilon(\psi)^{k}}{2q^{k}}\varphi(q)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dotsc,a_{2k}\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)\\
D^{k}a_{1}\dotsm a_{2k}\equiv\pm 1\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{a_{1}+\dotsb+a_{2k}}{q}\right)\\
&\qquad-\frac{\psi(q)^{k}\varepsilon(\psi)^{k}}{q^{k}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dotsc,a_{2k}\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)\\
(a_{j},q)=1\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{a_{1}+\dotsb+a_{2k}}{q}\right).\end{aligned}

(16)

The second term on the right hand side of (
16
) equals

-\frac{\psi(q)^{k}\varepsilon(\psi)^{k}}{q^{k}}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{q-1}e\left(\frac{a}{q}\right)\right)^{2k}=-\frac{\psi(q)^{k}\varepsilon(\psi)^{k}}{q^{k}},

and thus has absolute value at most
1/q^{k}
.

On the other hand, the first term on the right hand side of (
16
) equals

\displaystyle\frac{\psi(q)^{k}\varepsilon(\psi)^{k}}{2q^{k}}\varphi(q)\sum_{\ell=0}^{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1},\dotsc,a_{2k-1}\>(\mathrm{mod}\>q)\\
(a_{j},q)=1\end{subarray}}e\left(\frac{a_{1}+\dotsb+a_{2k-1}+(-1)^{\ell}\overline{D^{k}a_{1}\dotsm a_{2k-1}}}{q}\right).

(17)

Here we have a
2k-1
-dimensional Kloosterman sum and by a bound of Smith
(
Smith
, Theorem 6)
, the absolute value of (
17
) is

\leq\frac{\varphi(q)}{2q^{k}}\cdot 2\cdot q^{(2k-1)/2}d_{2k}(q)=\frac{\varphi(q)}{q^{1/2}}d_{2k}(q).

Now the claim follows since
q
is a prime, so
d_{2k}(q)=2k
.

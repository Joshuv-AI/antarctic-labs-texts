Guts and The Minimal Volume Orientable Hyperbolic 3-Manifold with 3 Cusps
Yue Zhang

Abstract
. The minimal volume of orientable hyperbolic manifolds with a given number of cusps has been found for 0 , 1 , 2 , 4 0,1,2,4 cusps, while the minimal volume of 3-cusped orientable hyperbolic manifolds remains unknown. By using guts in sutured manifolds and pared manifolds, we are able to show that for an orientable hyperbolic 3-manifold with 3 cusps such that every second homology class is libroid, its volume is at least 5.49 … = 6 × 5.49\ldots=6\times Catalan’s constant.



1.
Introduction

Jorgensen and Thurston
[
Thu79
, Section 6.6]
proved that the volumes of hyperbolic 3-manifolds are well-ordered. Moreover, if a volume is an
n
-fold limit point of smaller volumes (of order type
\omega^{n}
), then there is a corresponding hyperbolic manifold of finite volume with precisely
n
orientable cusps. This gives rise to the problem of determining the minimal volume orientable hyperbolic 3-manifolds with n cusps.

When
n=0
(closed manifolds), Gabai, Meyerhoff and Milley
[
GMM09
]
identified the smallest volume orientable hyperbolic 3-manifold to be the Fomenko-Matveev-Weeks manifold with volume
0.94\ldots
.

When
n=1
, Cao and Meyerhoff
[
CM01
]
showed that the figure-eight knot complement and the manifold obtained by the
(5,1)
-Dehn filling from the Whitehead link complement have the minimal volume. Their volume is
2.02...=2V_{3}
, where
V_{3}
is the volume of the ideal regular tetrahedron.

When
n=2
, Agol
[
Ago10
]
found that the Whitehead link complement and the
(-2,3,8)
pretzel link complement are the minimal volume orientable hyperbolic 3-manifolds with two cusps, with volume
V_{8}=3.66\ldots=4\times K
, where
K
is Catalan’s constant

K=1-1/9+1/25-1/49+\cdots+(-1)^{n}/(2n+1)^{2}+\cdots.

V_{8}
is also the volume of a regular ideal octahedron in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
.

When
n=4
, Yoshida
[
Yos13
]
showed that the
8^{4}_{2}
link complement is the unique minimal volume orientable hyperbolic manifold with
4
cusps.

In the case of 2 and 4 cusps, Agol and Yoshida used guts of pared manifolds to estimate the volume.

However the minimal volume orientable hyperbolic 3-manifolds with 3 cusps remain unknown. Here is a conjecture about the minimal volume.

Conjecture 1.1
.

The minimal volume of 3-cusped orientable hyperbolic manifold is the volume of the 3-chain link complement, which is
5.33\ldots
.

In this paper, we make some progress on this conjecture.

Organization.

Section
2
consists of a review of pared manifolds and pared guts.

In Section
3
, we classify sutured guts in manifolds whose interior is a hyperbolic manifold of finite volume. Moreover, we show some properties of the sutured guts related to a vertex element of the Thurston norm.

In Section
4
, we introduce the definition of sutured bundles and library bundles which is used to prove that we cannot exchange sutured guts components infinitely many times in a hyperbolic manifold (Lemma
4.7
). We also define pared sutured manifolds which have compatible sutured manifold structure and pared manifold structure which helps find the guts of library sutured manifolds.

In Section
5
, we show that for a 3-cusped hyperbolic manifold of finite volume, there is a sequence of 2 sutured decomposition for the sutured guts related to different 2-homology classes.

In Section
6
, we analyze different cases for a 3-cusped hyperbolic manifold under a non-isotopic condition and estimate the volume of it as in Theorem
1.2
.

In Section
7
, we define doubled guts for 2-homology classes. In Theorem
7.7
, we show that the doubled guts remain invariant in any open Thurston cone
\Delta
for a certain class of orientable hyperbolic 3-manifolds. By using doubled guts, we deduce Theorem
1.3
to the case in Section
6
.

2.
Pared Manifolds, Pared Guts and Volume Estimation

We call the guts in
[
Ago10
]

pared guts
since the notion comes from the characteristic manifold of a pared manifold.

Definition 2.1
.

A
pared manifold
is a pair
(M,P)
where

•

M
is a compact, orientable irreducible 3-manifold and

•

P\subset\partial M
is a union of essential annuli
and tori in
M
,

such that

•

every abelian, noncyclic subgroup of
\pi_{1}(M)
is peripheral
with respect to
P
(i.e., conjugate to a subgroup of the fundamental
group of a component of
P
) and

•

every map
\varphi:(S^{1}\times I,S^{1}\times\partial I)\to(M,P)
that
is injective on the fundamental groups deforms, as maps of pairs, into
P
.

P
is called the
parabolic locus
of the pared manifold
(M,P)
.
We denote by
\partial_{0}M
the surface
\partial M-int(P)
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
Ago10
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Let
M
be an orientable compact manifold such that
int(M)
is hyperbolic of finite volume and so that
P=\partial M
is the pared locus of
M
. Let
X\subset M
be an essential surface. Then
(M\backslash\backslash X,P\backslash\backslash\partial X)
is a pared manifold.

Let
(M,P)
be a pared manifold such that
\partial_{0}M
is incompressible.
There is a canonical set of essential annuli
(A,\partial A)\subset(M,\partial_{0}M)
, called the
characteristic annuli
,
such that
(P,\partial P)\subset(A,\partial A)
, and
characterized (up to isotopy) by the property that they are the maximal collection of non-parallel essential
annuli such that every other essential annulus
(B,\partial B)\subset(M,\partial_{0}M)

may be relatively isotoped to an annulus
(B^{\prime},\partial B^{\prime})\subset(M,\partial_{0}M)

so that
B^{\prime}\cap A=\emptyset
.
Each complementary component
L\subset M\backslash\backslash A
is
one of the
following types:

(1)

T^{2}\times I
, a neighborhood of a torus component of
P
,

(2)

(S^{1}\times D^{2},S^{1}\times D^{2}\cap\partial_{0}M)
, a solid torus with annuli in the boundary,

(3)

(I-bundles,\partial I-subbundles)
, where the
I
-bundles over
the boundary are subsets of
A
, or

(4)

all essential annuli in
(L,\partial_{0}M\cap L)
are parallel in
L
into

(L\cap A,\partial(L\cap A)).

The union
of components
of type
(3)
, denoted
(W,\partial_{0}W)\subset(M,\partial_{0}M)
, is called the
window
of
(M,\partial_{0}M)
.
It is unique up to isotopy of pairs.

The union of the components of type 4 is called the
pared guts
of
M
and denoted by
Guts(M,P)
(a bit different from
[
Ago10
]
). Note that the parabolic locus of
Guts(M,P)
will consist of characteristic annuli. If
M
is compact orientable and
int(M)
admits a metric of finite volume, and
(X,\partial X)\subset(M,\partial M)
is an essential surface,
then define
Guts(M,X)=Guts(M\backslash\backslash X,\partial M\backslash\backslash\partial X)
.
The components of type (4) are acylindrical pared manifolds, which have a complete hyperbolic
structure of finite volume with geodesic boundary
[
Mor84
]
.
We let
\mbox{\rm{Vol}}(Guts(M,P))
denote the volume of this hyperbolic metric. If
D(M,P)
is
obtained by taking two copies of
M
and gluing them along the corresponding surfaces

\partial_{0}M
, then
\mbox{\rm{Vol}}(Guts(M,P))=\frac{1}{2}\mbox{\rm{Vol}}(D(M,P))
, where
\mbox{\rm{Vol}}(D(M,P))
is
the simplicial volume of
D(M,P)
, i.e., the sum of the volumes of the hyperbolic
pieces of the geometric decomposition.

In this paper, we heavily use the following theorem to estimate the volume of a hyperbolic manifold of finite volume.

Theorem 2.3

(
[

, Theorem 5.5]
,
[

Let

\mathrm{Vol}(M)\geq\mathrm{Vol}(Guts(M,S))\geq\frac{V_{8}}{2}|\chi(\partial Guts(M,S))|.

The estimation of

Theorem 2.4

(
[
Miy94
, Theorem 5.2]
)
.

Let
M

\mathrm{Vol}(M)\geq\frac{V_{8}}{2}|\chi(\partial M)|
. Moreover,

is obtained
from ideal regular octahedra by gluing along their faces when the equality holds.

We also state the definition of an annular compression from

Sutured Manifolds and Sutured Guts

We call the guts defined in
[
AZ22
]

sutured guts
since they are related to sutured manifolds. In this section, we set up some notation and terminology for the theory of sutured decompositions and sutured guts.

We classify the sutured guts components of a hyperbolic manifold of finite volume.

Definition 3.1
.

We call a sutured manifold
(N,R_{+},R_{-},\gamma)

acylindrical
if
N
is atoroidal and every essential annulus in
(N,R_{+}\cup R_{-})
is parallel in
N
into
(\gamma,\partial\gamma)
rel
R_{+}\cup R_{-})
.

Proposition 3.2

(Cf.
[
Ago10
]
)
.

Let
M
be a compact 3-manifold with toral boundary such that its interior is a hyperbolic manifold of finite volume. Each guts component with respect to a facet surface is one of the following types:

(1)

a
T\times I
with a boundary component as a sutured torus and two annuli on the other boundary component as sutures,

(2)

a solid torus with 2 non-longitudinal sutures in the boundary,

(3)

a solid torus with 4 longitudinal sutures in the boundary, or

(4)

an acylindrical taut sutured manifold.

.

, 2-ST and 4-ST.

Proof.

Let
(G,R_{+},R_{-},\gamma)
be a guts component with respect to a facet surface

Suppose there is a non-product annulus

In the following we sometimes use the core of an annulus to represent the annulus.

Definition 3.3
.

We define a

.

We call an element in

We are interested in the sutured guts related to a vertex element because it has the most complexity.

Lemma 3.5
.

A norm-minimizing surface

presenting a primitive vertex element of the Thurston norm is connected.

Proof.

If
S

In this section, we introduce some general settings that will help with analyzing the volume of hyperbolic manifolds.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
M

\partial
-irreducible manifold with toral boundary and

as a union of components of

We say the structure defined here is a

be the

\chi_{-}(U\cap M^{\prime})
is smaller than
x(z)
. Note that
U\cap M
and the mirrored copy of
U\cap M^{\prime}
in
M
represent
z
in
H_{2}(M,\partial M)
. This contradicts the definition of Thurston norm.
∎

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
M
be an orientable hyperbolic 3-manifold of finite volume such that every element in
H_{2}(M,\partial M)
is a libroid class. Let
z
be an element in
H_{2}(M,\partial M)
and
P
be a boundary component of
M
. Then the horizontally prime guts of
D_{P}(\Gamma(z))
is equivalent to the guts of
D_{P}(z)
in
D_{P}(M)
.

Proof.

Note that
P
is the only essential torus in
D_{P}(M)
and it is null-homologous. Hence the Thurston norm of
D_{P}(M)
is non-degenerate. Therefore by
[
AZ22
, Theorem 1.1]
, the guts of
D_{P}(z)
in
D_{P}(M)
is well-defined.

Let
F(z)
be a facet surface representing
z
. Then
D_{P}(F(z))
will be a collection of norm-minimizing surfaces representing
D_{P}(z)
in
D_{P}(M)
. Since each component of
M\backslash\backslash F(z)
contains one component of
\Gamma(z)
by Lemma
3.6
, each component of
D_{P}(M)\backslash\backslash D_{P}(F(z))
contains the double of one component of
\Gamma(z)
.

Let
G
be a component of
\Gamma(z)
and
N
be the component of
M\backslash\backslash F(z)
containing
G
.

If
G
is a
T\times I
with 2 sutures on a boundary component and a sutured toral boundary,
D_{P}(G)
is a
T\times I
with 2 isotopic sutures on each boundary component. We take a taut surface
S^{\prime}_{N}
homologous to the
R_{+}
part of
D_{P}(G)
such that it decomposes
D_{P}(G)
into two 4-ST’s. If
G
is a 4-ST or a 2-ST, we can also have
S^{\prime}_{N}
that decomposes
D_{P}(G)
into two 4-ST’s or 2-ST’s. Then the horizontally prime guts of
D_{P}(G)
is the union of these two solid tori. By extending
S^{\prime}_{N}
to the product sutured manifold
D_{P}(N)\backslash D_{P}(G)
, we have a taut surface
S_{N}
which represents
z
. Furthermore,
\{S_{N}\}
is a maximal collection of taut surfaces representing
z
in
D_{P}(N)
and non-isotopic to
R_{\pm}(N)
.

The union of
D_{P}(F(z))
and
S_{N}
for each component
N
of
M\backslash\backslash F(z)
is a facet surface
F(D_{P}(z))
representing
D_{P}(z)
in
D_{P}(M)
. Since the guts of
F(D_{P}(z))
is equivalent to the horizontally prime guts of
D_{P}(\Gamma(z))
, we know the lemma is true.

∎

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
M
be an orientable hyperbolic 3-manifold of finite volume such that every element in
H_{2}(M,\partial M)
is a libroid class. Let
y,z
be in an open Thurston cone
\Delta
of
M
such that
y
vanishes on exactly one boundary component of
M
. Then there is a
w
in the open segment
(y,z)
such that the doubled guts of
y
is equivalent to the doubled guts of
u
in
D(M)
for every element
u
in
[y,w]
.

Proof.

Let
y
vanish on a boundary component
P
. We take
w
small enough so that every element in
[w,y]
does not vanish on any boundary component other than
P
.

Note that
P
is the only essential torus in
D_{P}(M)
and it is null-homologous. Hence the Thurston norm of
D_{P}(M)
is non-degenerate. Since
y
and
w
are in the same open Thurston cone, there is a class
w^{\prime}
such that we have
\chi_{-}(ky)=\chi_{-}(w^{\prime})+\chi_{-}(w)
and
w^{\prime}=ky-w
for some positive number
k
. Therefore
\chi_{-}(kD(y))=\chi_{-}(D(v))+\chi_{-}(D(w))
. By Lemma
7.4
, we know that
D_{P}(y)
and
D_{P}(w)
are both in an open Thurston cone of
D_{P}(M)
. Besides, there is an open segment
(a,b)
containing
D(y)
and
D(w)
in the open Thurston cone such that the restrictions of
a
and
b
on each boundary component are not in opposite orientations. By
[
AZ22
, Theorem 1.2]
, the guts of
D_{P}(y)
is equivalent to the guts of
D_{P}(u)
in
D_{P}(M)
for every element
u
in
[y,w]
.

By Lemma
7.5
, we know that the horizontally prime guts of
D_{P}(\Gamma(y))
is equivalent to the horizontally prime guts of
D_{P}(\Gamma(u))
. Let
S_{P}(y)
and
S_{P}(u)
be the union of a maximal collection of non-parallel horizontal surfaces in
D_{P}(\Gamma(y))
and
D_{P}(\Gamma(z))
, respectively. By gluing themselves along boundary components other than
P
, we have a union of non-parallel horizontal surfaces
S(y)
and
S(u)
in
D(\Gamma(y))
and
D(\Gamma(z))
, respectively. Because
y
and
u
do not vanish on any boundary component other than
P
, we know that each
S(y)
and
S(u)
is the union of a maximal collection of non-parallel horizontal surfaces. Therefore the horizontally prime guts of
D(\Gamma(y))
is equivalent to the horizontally prime guts of
D(\Gamma(u))
.

∎

By Lemma
7.6
and
[
AZ22
, Theorem 1.2]
, the following theorem holds.

Theorem 7.7
.

Let
M
be an orientable hyperbolic 3-manifold of finite volume such that every element in
H_{2}(M,\partial M)
is a libroid class and
\Delta
be an open Thurston cone. If no element in
\Delta
vanishes on two boundary component, then for two classes
y,z
in
\Delta
, the doubled guts of
y
is equivalent to the doubled guts of
z
in
D(M)
.

Proof.

Let
(v,w)
be an open segment containing
y,z
in
\Delta
. We divide
(v,w)
by finitely many points such that each resulting open component does not contain two elements whose restrictions on a boundary component are in opposite orientations. By
[
AZ22
, Theorem 1.2]
, guts are invariant for second homology classes in each open segment. By Lemma
7.6
, we know that each point on
(v,w)
has a neighborhood in
(v,w)
such that double guts for second homology classes in this neighborhood are invariant. Combining these, we know that Theorem
7.7
is true.
∎

Now we start proving Theorem
1.3
.

Proof of Theorem

.

By the well-orderness the volumes of hyperbolic 3-manifolds (see
[
Thu79
, Theorem 5.12.1]
), there is an orientable hyperbolic 3-cusped manifold

satisfying the condition in Theorem

and having the minimal volume among all such 3-manifolds. Suppose
\mbox{\rm{Vol}}(M_{book})<2V_{8}
.

There is a long exact sequence for
(M_{book},\partial M_{book})
:

\cdots\to H_{2}(M_{book})\to H_{2}(M_{book},\partial M_{book})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial}}{{\to}}H_{1}(\partial M_{book})\to\cdots

By Lemma
7.1
, we know that there is no non-separating closed surface in
M_{book}
and hence the map from
H_{2}(M_{book})
to
H_{2}(M_{book},\partial M_{book})
is 0. Therefore the map
\partial
is injective. Because the image of
\partial
is of rank 3 by Lemma
5.1
,
H_{2}(M_{book},\partial M_{book})
is 3-dimensional. Since the Betti number of

is 3 and, for each boundary component

, there is an element

in

such that

in the

summand of

vanishes on

If there is a

contains 2

’s. By Lemmma
6.1
,
\mbox{\rm{Vol}}(M_{book})\geq 2V_{8}
. So we assume there is no such class.

Let

If

of

2.2.
Cotriangular Hopf algebras

A key point for us will be the preservation of the following property under cocycle deformation.

Definition 2.4
.

(
[
EGNO
, Definition 8.3.19]
) A
coquasitriangular
Hopf algebra is a pair
(H,R)
, where
H
is a Hopf
\mathbb{C}
-algebra and
R:H\otimes H\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a convolution invertible bilinear map such that

(1)

R(h,\ell g)=\sum R(h_{1},g)R(h_{2},\ell)\,\,\textit{ and }\,\,R(gh,\ell)=\sum R(g,\ell_{1})R(h,\ell_{2});

(2)

\sum R(h_{1},g_{1})h_{2}g_{2}=\sum g_{1}h_{1}R(h_{2},g_{2})

for
g,h,\ell\in H
. Such a functional
R
is called an
R
-form
for
H
.

If in addition

\textrm{(3)}\quad\sum R(h_{1},g_{1})R(g_{2},h_{2})=\epsilon(g)\epsilon(h),

that is, if
R^{-1}=R_{21}
, then
(H,R)
is called
cotriangular
. ∎

There is a dual concept of a
(quasi)triangular Hopf algebra

(H,R)
, defined by Drinfeld
[
Dr1
]
,
[
EGNO
, Definition 8.3.1]
, where now

, the
universal
R
-matrix
, is an invertible element of

satisfying certain equations
[
EGNO
, (8.8), page 198]
, stemming from the braid relations. A finite dimensional Hopf algebra
(H,R)
is easily seen to be (quasi)triangular if and only if
(H^{*},R)
is co(quasi)triangular
[
EGNO
, page 201]
(noting that
R\in H\otimes H
can be regarded as an element of
(H^{*}\otimes H^{*})^{*}
).

The following classes of examples are key for us:

Example 2.5
.

(1)(
[
EGNO
, Example 8.3.4]
) If

is a commutative Hopf algebra, then
(H,\epsilon\otimes\epsilon)
is cotriangular. Equivalently, any cocommutative Hopf algebra is triangular with universal
R
-matrix
1\otimes 1
.

(2)(
[
EGNO
, Remark 8.3.24]
) Let
(H,R)
be co(quasi)triangular, and let
J
be a Hopf
2
-cocycle for
H
. Define
R^{J}:=J_{21}^{-1}\ast R\ast J
. Then

is an
R
-form for

, and we say that
R^{J}
is obtained from

by

. Thus,
({}_{J}H_{J},R^{J})
is a co(quasi)triangular Hopf algebra. ∎

The following properties of the

-forms obtained as in Example

(2) will be needed later.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let

be an affine algebraic group, so

-cocycle for

(1)

For all

(2)

Let

Proof.

(1) Let

and this equals 0 by equation (

(2) This is

∎

Notice that we can reformulate Definition

m^{\mathrm{op}}=R\ast m\ast R^{-1}

thus exposing the relevance of

-forms for

(
[

Let

(1)

The following lemma will be important in analyzing the structure of cotriangular Hopf algebras.

.

(
[

, Proposition 2.1]
)

Let

Definition 2.9
.

A cotriangular Hopf algebra

whose radical

is

Clearly, by Lemma

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
(H,R)
be a cotriangular Hopf algebra. Then there are maps
\Psi_{\ell},\Psi_{r}:H\to H^{\circ}
, defined respectively by
\Psi_{\ell}(a)=R(a,-)
and
\Psi_{r}(a)=R(-,a)
for

, mapping into (the isomorphic Hopf algebras)

and

Proof.

Clearly
\Psi_{\ell}
and
\Psi_{r}
are linear maps into

with kernel

. To see that their images lie in

, let

and fix a finite dimensional subcoalgebra

of

with

. Setting
I_{C}:=\{h\in H\mid R(h,d)=0,\,\forall d\in C\}
, one sees from the definition of

that

is an ideal of

containing

, and that

embeds into

via
\Psi_{\ell}
. Since
\Psi_{r}(c)(I_{C})=0
, this proves that
\mathrm{im}(\Psi_{r})\subset H^{\circ}
. Similarly,
\mathrm{im}(\Psi_{\ell})\subset H^{\circ}
.

Since for all

\Psi_{\ell}(ab)(x)=R(ab,x)=\sum R(a,x_{1})R(b,x_{2})=(\Psi_{\ell}(a)\ast\Psi_{\ell}(b))(x),

is an algebra homomorphism from

to

. Moreover, the first part of Definition

(1) shows that

is a bialgebra homomorphism into

Recall that by

has antipode

of

(S^{\ast})^{-1}f(x)=f(S^{-1}(x))

\Psi_{\ell}(S(h))=R(S(h),x)=R(h,S^{-1}(x))=(S^{\ast})^{-1}(\Psi_{\ell}(h)),

is a Hopf algebra map from

The argument for

is similar, noting that

has antipode

. Note that the two codomain Hopf algebras are isomorphic by
[

.
∎

Specialise now to the case where

is the coordinate ring

of an affine algebraic group

.

(

Let

be an affine algebraic group, and let

be a Hopf

-cocycle for

.

(

Let

2.4.
Hopf
2
-cocycles for unipotent groups

A
quasi-Frobenius Lie algebra
is a finite dimensional Lie algebra
\mathfrak{t}
equipped with a symplectic
2
-cocycle
\omega
. That is,
\omega:\mathfrak{t}\times\mathfrak{t}\to\mathbb{C}
is a non-degenerate skew-symmetric bilinear form such that for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{t}
,

(2.12)

\omega([x,y],z)+\omega([z,x],y)+\omega([y,z],x)=0.

Thus every quasi-Frobenius Lie algebra has even dimension.

Definition 2.14
.

Given a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
,
r\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
is a solution to the
classical Yang-Baxter equation (CYBE)
if

[r_{12},r_{13}]+[r_{12},r_{23}]+[r_{13},r_{23}]=0
. ∎

The equivalence of (3) and (4) below is due to Drinfeld
[
Dr2
]
and applies to all complex finite dimensional Lie algebras: for a pair
(\mathfrak{t},\omega)
as in (4) one takes
r:=\omega^{-1}\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{t}\subset\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
. Every solution to the CYBE arises in this way.

The equivalence between (1) and (3) was proved in
[
EG6
, Theorem 3.2]
, and later in
[
G2
, Theorem 5.1]
by an alternative proof avoiding the use of Etingof-Kazhdan deformation theory. Full details and references can be found in
[
G2
,
\lx@sectionsign
5]
(see also Remarks
2.16
(2) below).

Theorem 2.15
.

(Etingof-Gelaki,
[
EG6
]
)
Let

be a unipotent affine algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
, with
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathrm{Lie}(G)
. Then the following sets are in bijection with each other.

(1)

2
-cocycles for

(2)

(T,\omega)
, where

(3)

Equivalence classes of pairs

(4)

\omega

on every monomial in

.
∎

For Theorem

it thus remains only to show that

Lemma 4.8
.

u_{n}\in\mathrm{im}(\Psi)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
,
\Psi(y_{n})
lies in

, so we can fix an integer

such that
\Psi(y_{n})
vanishes on

Retain the notation (

, let

denote the linear form on

and

on every monomial
{\bf y}^{{\bf i}}
such that

. Let

{\bf y}^{{\bf b*}}

{\bf y}^{{\bf b*}}\in U(\mathfrak{k})\subset U(\mathfrak{g}).

Hence, by Lemmas

and

, for each

{\bf y}^{{\bf b*}}=\Psi({\bf z}_{{\bf b}}).

5.1.
Crossed product decomposition

Recall that since

is a closed subgroup of

,
\mathcal{O}(G/T)
and
\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G)
are left and right coideal subalgebras of
\mathcal{O}(G)
respectively, and hence are polynomial subalgebras in
\mathrm{dim}(G)-\mathrm{dim}(T)
variables; see for example
[
BG
]
.

Following Proposition
2.11
, there is an epimorphism
\pi
of Hopf algebras from
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
onto
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(T)_{J}
with kernel
I_{R^{J}}
, the radical of
R^{J}
. The notation (
2.4
) is used below for the algebras
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)
and
\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
, and extended in the obvious way to subalgebras of these algebras.
Finally, recall that
S^{J}
denotes the antipode of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

With the above notation, we have the following:

(1)

The algebra
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G/T)_{J}={}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G/T)
is a left coideal subalgebra of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
, and a subalgebra of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)
. Correspondingly,
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G)_{J}=\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G)_{J}
is a right coideal subalgebra of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
, and a subalgebra of
\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
.

(2)

I_{R^{J}}=(\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G)_{J})^{+}{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}=({}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G/T))^{+}{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}.

(3)

S^{J}(\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G)_{J})={}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G/T)
, and vice versa.

Proof.

(1) The maps
\pi:\mathcal{O}(G)\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{O}(T)
and
\Delta
are the same when applied to
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)
. Therefore, since
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G/T)_{J}
is defined using the coalgebra structure, we see that, as vector subspaces of
\mathcal{O}(G)
, we have

(5.1)

{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G/T)_{J}=\mathcal{O}(G/T)=\{f\in\mathcal{O}(G)\mid(\mathrm{id}\otimes\pi)\circ\Delta(f)=f\otimes\overline{1}\}.

In particular,
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G/T)_{J}
is a left coideal subalgebra of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
since
\mathcal{O}(G/T)
is a left coideal subalgebra of
\mathcal{O}(G)
. Hence, for

a,b\in{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G/T)_{J}
, we have

\displaystyle m_{J}(a\otimes b)

\displaystyle=\sum J^{-1}(a_{1},b_{1})a_{2}b_{2}J(a_{3},b_{3})

\displaystyle=\sum J^{-1}(a_{1},b_{1})a_{2}b_{2}\epsilon(a_{3})\epsilon(b_{3})

\displaystyle=\sum J^{-1}(a_{1},b_{1})a_{2}b_{2}.

That is,
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G/T)_{J}={}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G/T)
. This proves the first half of (1); the second half is exactly similar.

(2), (3) These follow from (
5.1
) by standard properties of Hopf ideals in connected Hopf algebras due to Masuoka, see e.g.
[
BG
, Proposition 2.3]
.
∎

The Hopf algebra

.

5.2.
Consequences of Theorem
5.2

Although we are unable to give a precise description of the centre of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
for unipotent affine algebraic groups
G
, we can at least identify an important central subalgebra:

Proposition 5.4
.

(
[
G3
, Lemma 3.7]
)

If
G
is a unipotent affine algebraic group with a Hopf
2
-cocycle

whose support is
T
, then the algebra
\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G/T)
of functions which are constant on the double cosets of
T
in

is contained in the centre of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
.

Proof.

By
[
G2
, Theorem 3.5]
(without needing the hypothesis of unipotence), there are algebra embeddings

\mathcal{O}(G/T)=\mathcal{O}(G/T)_{J}\subseteq Z(\mathcal{O}(G)_{J})\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G)={}_{J}\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G)\subseteq Z({}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)),

where the equalities emphasise that the multiplication is the same in the linked algebras. Hence, we have

(5.2)

\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G/T)\subseteq\mathcal{O}(G/T)=\mathcal{O}(G/T)_{J}\subseteq Z(\mathcal{O}(G)_{J})

and

(5.3)

\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G/T)\subseteq\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G)={}_{J}\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G)\subseteq Z(_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)).

Moreover (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) imply that

(5.4)

\Delta(\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G/T))\subseteq\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G)\otimes\mathcal{O}(G/T)\subseteq Z({}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)\otimes\mathcal{O}(G)_{J})),

since
\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G)
and
\mathcal{O}(G/T)
are right and left coideal subalgebras of
\mathcal{O}(G)
, respectively. Thus, the result follows from (
5.4
), and the fact that by Lemma
2.2
(3),
\Delta
embeds
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
in
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)\otimes\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
.
∎

Recall that an ideal
I
of a ring
A
is called
polycentral
if there is a finite subset
\{y_{1},\ldots,y_{t}\}\subset I
, which generate
I
as a right ideal, such that for all
i=1,\ldots,t
,

y_{i}+\left(\sum_{j<i}y_{j}A\right)\textit{ is central in }A/\left(\sum_{j<i}y_{j}A\right).

The ideal
I
and its corresponding generating set are called
regular polycentral
if in addition each
y_{i}
is not a zero divisor in
A/(\sum_{j<i}y_{j}A)
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
G
be a unipotent affine algebraic group of dimension
n
, and
J
a Hopf
2
-cocycle for
G
. Then the augmentation ideal
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}^{+}
of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
has a regular polycentral generating set of
n
elements.

Proof.

By Theorem
5.2
, with
T
as usual denoting the support of
J
, and
\mathfrak{t}
the Lie algebra of
T
, we have

(5.5)

{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}\cong{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G/T)_{J}\#_{\sigma}U(\mathfrak{t})^{J}.

If
T=G
then
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}\cong U(\mathfrak{t})
as an algebra, and the result follows by taking as generators of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}^{+}
a basis of
\mathfrak{t}
obtained from its upper central series. So we may suppose without loss of generality that
T\subsetneq G
. By standard properties of unipotent groups in characteristic
0
, the normaliser
N_{G}(T)
is a closed subgroup of
G
strictly containing
T
. Thus,
N_{G}(T)/T
is a non-trivial unipotent group, so we can find a chain of subgroups

1\subset T_{1}/T\subset T_{2}/T\subset\cdots\subset T_{\ell}/T=N_{G}(T)/T,

with each subgroup normal in the next, and successive factors isomorphic to
(\mathbb{C},+)
. Repeating with
N_{G}(T)
in place of
T
, we eventually obtain a normal subgroup
N
of
G
with
T\subseteq N
and
G/N\cong(\mathbb{C},+)
.

By the discussion at the start of
\lx@sectionsign
4.2
, the factor
G/N
of
G
corresponds to a Hopf subalgebra
H_{1}=\mathbb{C}[X]
of
\mathcal{O}(G)
. Here
X
is primitive, and for all
g\in G
,
X
is a constant function on the coset
gN=Ng
. Moreover, for all
g\in G
, we have

TgT\subset NgN=gN,

so that
X
is constant on the double coset
TgT
. In particular, by Proposition
5.4
,
X
is central in
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
. Since
X\in{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G/T)^{+}
, we can factor by the Hopf ideal
X{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
. The ideal
X{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
is, by construction, contained in
({}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G/T))^{+}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
. Hence, Lemma
5.1
(2) applies, and we obtain a quotient Hopf algebra of GK-dimension
n-1
, namely
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(N)_{J}
. Now one inducts on

to obtain the theorem.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

Let

be a unipotent affine algebraic group, and

a Hopf

-cocycle for

.

(1)

(S^{J})^{2}=\mathrm{id}
.

(2)

Suppose that

, and for brevity, denote

. Since

where the isomorphism is of

-bimodules.

Therefore, since

is Artin-Schelter regular by Corollary

(2), it follows from

that the Nakayama automorphism

On the other hand, since

5.3.
Stratification of representations by double cosets

We keep the notation as specified at the start of
\lx@sectionsign
5
. When
J
is trivial, so that
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}=\mathcal{O}(G)
is commutative and
T=\{1\}
, the simple
\mathcal{O}(G)
-modules are of course parametrized by the points of
G
. We generalize this to arbitrary
J
here.

The starting point is Proposition
5.4
. By
[
MR
, Proposition 9.1.7]
, the endomorphism algebra of each simple
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
-module
V
is
\mathbb{C}
, so
V
is killed by a maximal ideal of the central subalgebra
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G/T)_{J}
. Our target is to make this observation more precise. For
g\in G
, let
Z_{g}:=TgT
be the double coset of
T
in
G
containing
g
, so that
Z_{g}
is the orbit containing
g
under the left action of
T\times T
on
G
given by

(5.9)

(r,s)\cdot g:=rgs^{-1};\,\,\,r,s\in T.

Lemma 5.8
.

(
[
G3
, §4]
)

Fix
g\in G
, and set
T_{g}:=T\cap gTg^{-1}
.

(1)

Z_{g}
is an irreducible closed subset of
G
of dimension

2\mathrm{dim}(T)-\mathrm{dim}(T_{g}).

(2)

Define the embedding
\theta:T_{g}\rightarrow T\times T,\,\,\,a\mapsto(a,g^{-1}ag).
Then
\theta(T_{g})
acts on
T\times T
on the right, and

\mathcal{O}(Z_{g})\cong(\mathcal{O}(T)\otimes\mathcal{O}(T))^{\theta(T_{g})}.

(3)

Let
\mathcal{I}(Z_{g})
be the defining ideal of
Z_{g}
in
\mathcal{O}(G)
, and let
i:T\hookrightarrow G
be the inclusion morphism. Then there is an algebra epimorphism

m_{g}^{\ast}:=(i^{\ast}\otimes i^{\ast})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\lambda_{g^{-1}})\circ\Delta:\mathcal{O}(G)\twoheadrightarrow\left(\mathcal{O}(T)\otimes\mathcal{O}(T)\right)^{\theta(T_{g})}

with kernel
\mathcal{I}(Z_{g})
. ∎

Proof.

(1), (2) Since
T\times T
is unipotent its orbits on
G
under the action (
5.9
) are closed by the theorem of Kostant, Rosenberg and Rosenlicht,
[
Mi
, Theorem 17.64]
, so
Z_{g}
is an irreducible closed subset of
G
. The stabilizer of
g
in
T\times T
is
\{(a,g^{-1}ag)\mid a\in T\cap gTg^{-1}\}
, so that
Z_{g}\cong(T\times T)/(T\cap gTg^{-1})
. More precisely, the closed subgroup
\theta(T_{g})
acts on
T\times T
on the right by

(x,y)\cdot\theta(a)=(xa,g^{-1}a^{-1}gy),

for
x,y\in T
and
a\in T_{g}
, yielding an isomorphism of affine varieties

(T\times T)/\theta(T_{g})\rightarrow Z_{g},\quad\overline{(x,y)}\mapsto xgy.

This proves (1) and (2).

(3) The above right action of
\theta(T_{g})
induces a left action of
\theta(T_{g})
on
\mathcal{O}(T)\otimes\mathcal{O}(T)
, given, for
a\in T_{g}
,
x,y\in T
, and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{O}(T)
, by

\theta(a)(\alpha\otimes\beta)(x,y)=\alpha(xa)\beta(g^{-1}a^{-1}gy).

That is,
\theta(a)
acts via
\rho_{a}\otimes\lambda_{g^{-1}ag}
, where
\rho
and
\lambda
are the right and left regular actions of
G
on
\mathcal{O}(G)
as noted in (
2.7
). So there is an algebra isomorphism

\mathcal{O}(Z_{g})\cong(\mathcal{O}(T)\otimes\mathcal{O}(T))^{\theta(T_{g})}.

Now (3) is just a reformulation of the above.
∎

The value of Lemma
5.8
lies in the following result:

Proposition 5.9
.

(
[
G3
, Proposition 4.1]
)

Retain the above notation. The map

is also a surjective algebra homomorphism from

onto

.
Hence,
\mathcal{I}(Z_{g})
is also a two-sided ideal of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
. ∎

Proof.

Since

Keep the notation from the start of

Theorem 5.12
.

(
[
G3
, Theorem 5.1]
)

Every simple
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
-module factors through a unique quotient
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(Z_{g})_{J}
.

Proof.

Let
V
be a simple (left)
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
-module. By Proposition
5.4
and Schur’s lemma
[
MR
, Proposition 9.1.7]
,
V
is annihilated by
K{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
for a maximal ideal
K
of
\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G/\penalty T)
. Now,
K{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}=K\mathcal{O}(G)
is also an ideal of
\mathcal{O}(G)
, and is clearly
(T\times T)
-invariant under the action of the left and right winding automorphisms of
T
on
\mathcal{O}(G)
(
5.18
). Therefore, the same invariance is true of
\sqrt{K\mathcal{O}(G)}
, where
\sqrt{K\mathcal{O}(G)}/K\mathcal{O}(G)
denotes the nilradical of
\mathcal{O}(G)/K\mathcal{O}(G)
. Thus, the variety
\mathcal{V}(\sqrt{K\mathcal{O}(G)})
is a disjoint union of orbits
TgT
, all of them closed by Lemma
5.8
(1), as
g
runs through those elements of
G
whose maximal ideals
\mathfrak{m}_{g}
contain
K\mathcal{O}(G)
. Choose a set of representatives
\mathcal{S}\subset G
for these orbits. Thus, by the commutative Nullstellensatz, we have

(5.10)

\bigcap_{g\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{I}(Z_{g})=\sqrt{K\mathcal{O}(G)}.

Note that (
5.10
) is valid whether viewed in
\mathcal{O}(G)
or in
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
, and that for
g\in\mathcal{S}
, the ideals
\mathcal{I}(Z_{g})
of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
are comaximal, because they are by definition comaximal as ideals of
\mathcal{O}(G)
, and since addition of ideals involves only the vector space structure. Hence,
V
is annihilated by at most one of the ideals
\mathcal{I}(Z_{g})
for

. The theorem is thus equivalent to the claim that

V\textit{ is killed by }\mathcal{I}(Z_{g})\textit{ for some }g\in\mathcal{S}.

To prove (
5.11
), we first determine the
(T\times T)
-invariants of the strata: namely,

(5.12)

{}^{T}\left({}_{J}\mathcal{O}(Z_{g})_{J}\right)^{T}=\,{}^{T}\left({}\mathcal{O}(Z_{g})\right)^{T}=\mathbb{C}.

The first equality in (
5.12
) holds because the space of invariants is independent of the multiplication on the algebra, and the second equality holds because the maximal ideals of
\mathcal{O}(Z_{g})
form a single
(T\times T)
-orbit intersecting in
{0}
, so that every nonzero
(T\times T)
-invariant in
\mathcal{O}(Z_{g})
must be a unit, while the only units of
\mathcal{O}(Z_{g})
are the scalars
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
by Lemma
5.8
(2), noting that
\mathcal{O}(T)^{\otimes 2}
is a polynomial algebra.

Now observe that by (
5.10
), there is a canonical embedding of algebras

(5.13)

\varphi:{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}/\sqrt{K\mathcal{O}(G)}\hookrightarrow\Pi_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\,{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(Z_{s})_{J}.

Moreover,
\varphi
is clearly
(T\times T)
-equivariant, so the restriction of
\varphi
gives an embedding

(5.14)

\varphi:{}^{T}\left({}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}/\sqrt{K\mathcal{O}(G)}\right)^{T}\hookrightarrow\Pi_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\,{}^{T}({}_{J}\mathcal{O}(Z_{s})_{J})^{T}=\Pi_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{C},

where the final equality follows from (
5.12

5.13
) and (
5.14
) we see that

(5.15)

{}^{T}\left({}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}/\sqrt{K\mathcal{O}(G)}\right)^{T}\subset Z\left({}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}/\sqrt{K\mathcal{O}(G)}\right).

If
|\mathcal{S}|=1
, then
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(Z_{g})_{J}={}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}/\sqrt{K\mathcal{O}(G)}
, and (
5.11
) is immediate by definition of
K
. So suppose that
|\mathcal{S}|>1
, and let
TxT
be a double coset with
x\in\mathcal{S}
. Then
TxT
is preserved by
T\times T
, and so therefore is
\mathcal{I}(Z_{x})
. Therefore,
T\times T
acts rationally on the non-zero space
\mathcal{I}(Z_{x})/\sqrt{K\mathcal{O}(G)}

is unipotent, its only rational irreducible module is the trivial module, hence there exists

(T\times T)
-invariant.

By (
5.15
),

\alpha
is not constant on
\mathcal{V}(\sqrt{K\mathcal{O}(G)})

whereas

and

. Hence, by the noncommutative Nullstellensatz

, Proposition 9.1.7]
, there exists

such that

, and
\alpha-\lambda\notin\sqrt{K\mathcal{O}(G)}

so that

(T\times T)
-invariant ideal of both
\mathcal{O}(G)
and of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}

\sqrt{K\mathcal{O}(G)}\subsetneq I_{1}\textit{ and }I_{1}V=0.

Now
\mathcal{V}_{1}:=\mathcal{V}(\sqrt{I_{1}})
is a proper subvariety of
\mathcal{V}(\sqrt{K\mathcal{O}(G)})
and is a union of
(T\times T)
-orbits labelled by a proper subset
\mathcal{S}_{1}
of
\mathcal{S}

If on the other hand

are noetherian, this process must terminate after finitely many repeats, so that (

6.1.

We start with the following elementary observation.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
k
be a field,

, and
F=k\langle X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\rangle
the free
k
-algebra of rank
n
. Let
D:=\langle[X_{i},X_{j}]\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n\rangle
be the commutator ideal of
F
, so
F/D\cong k[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
. Let
S:=F/E
be any factor algebra of

. Then the commutator ideal
\langle[S,S]\rangle
of

is
I:=(D+E)/E
.

Proof.

Since
D\subseteq D+E
,
S/I
is commutative, so
\langle[S,S]\rangle\subseteq I
. On the other hand, the image in
S
of every generator
[X_{i},X_{j}]
of

lies in
\langle[S,S]\rangle
, so

.
∎

Now we apply Lemma
6.1
in the setting of Remarks
3.4
(1). That is, in the lemma we take

and
n=\mathrm{dim}(G)
, so
\mathcal{O}(G)\xrightarrow{\pi_{D}}F/D
,
X_{i}\mapsto X_{i}+D
, is an algebra isomorphism, and we define the ideal
E
of
F
to be the ideal
E
of Remarks
3.4
(1), so that
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}\xrightarrow{\pi_{E}}F/E
,
X_{i}\mapsto X_{i}+E
, is an algebra isomorphism. Then by Lemma
6.1
,
(D+E)/E
is the commutator ideal of

.

Now let
I:=\langle[{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J},{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}]\rangle
be the commutator ideal of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
. Since the commutator ideal of any Hopf algebra is a Hopf ideal,

is a Hopf ideal of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
, and
\pi_{E}
induces a canonical Hopf algebra isomorphism

{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}/I\xrightarrow{\overline{\pi_{E}}}(F/E)/((D+E)/E),\,\,\,X_{i}+I\mapsto\pi_{E}(X_{i})+(D+E)/E.

In particular, since the algebra
(F/E)/((D+E)/E)
is canonically isomorphic to the algebra
F/(D+E)
, we see that the algebra

is a factor Hopf algebra of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
in a canonical way.

Now set
\mathscr{I}:=\pi_{D}^{-1}\left((D+E)/D\right)
; it is an ideal of
\mathcal{O}(G)
, and
\pi_{D}
induces a canonical algebra isomorphism

\mathcal{O}(G)/\mathscr{I}\xrightarrow{\overline{\pi_{D}}}(F/D)/((D+E)/D),\,\,\,X_{i}+\mathscr{I}\mapsto\pi_{D}(X_{i})+(D+E)/D.

Since the algebra
(F/D)/((D+E)/D)
is canonically isomorphic to the algebra

, it follows from the above that
\mathscr{I}
is a Hopf ideal of
\mathcal{O}(G)
, so the algebra
F/(D+E)
is also a factor Hopf algebra of
\mathcal{O}(G)
in a canonical way.

It follows that the Hopf algebras
\mathcal{O}(G)/\mathscr{I}
and
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}/I
are canonically isomorphic via the “identity map”

\mathcal{O}(G)/\mathscr{I}\xrightarrow{{\rm id}}{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}/I,\,\,\,X_{i}+\mathscr{I}\mapsto X_{i}+I,

hence
I=\mathscr{I}
, so

is also a Hopf ideal of
\mathcal{O}(G)
.

Thus, combining the above with Remarks
3.4
(1), we have obtained the following.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
G
be a unipotent affine algebraic group,

a Hopf

-cocycle for

7.

7.1.
Abelian support

Let
G
be a unipotent affine algebraic group, and
J
a Hopf
2
-cocycle for
G
. When is
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
commutative? Notice that by Theorem
3.3
, this is the case if and only if all the derivations appearing in the presentation of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
as an IHOE are trivial, and hence holds if and only if
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}=\mathcal{O}(G)
via the identity map. By the definition (
2.5
) of the multiplication in
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
, this happens if and only if

(7.1)

m\ast J=J\ast m,

where
m
denotes the multiplication in
\mathcal{O}(G)
. By definition, see
[
EG4
,
\lx@sectionsign
2.2]
, (
7.1
) holds if and only if

belongs to the set
Z^{2}_{\mathrm{inv}}(G)
of
invariant
Hopf
2
-cocycles. Invariant Hopf
2
-cocycles were defined and studied in
[
Scha1
]
; see also
[
BC
]
where the adjective
lazy
is used for them. In
[
EG4
, Theorem 4.2]
, it is shown (for

affine algebraic group over
\mathbb{C}
) that if

then the support of

is a closed normal abelian subgroup of
G
. The outcome is the following result:

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
G
be an affine algebraic group, and
J
a Hopf
2
-cocycle for
G
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
is commutative.

(2)

{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}\cong\mathcal{O}(G)
as Hopf algebras.

(3)

{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}=\mathcal{O}(G)
; that is, the identity map is an isomorphism of Hopf algebras.

(4)

J\in Z^{2}_{\mathrm{inv}}(G)
.

(5)

The support of

is an abelian normal subgroup of
G
.

Proof.

The equivalence of (1)-(4), and the proof of
(4)\Rightarrow(5)
are explained before the proposition. That
(5)\Rightarrow(4)
follows from
[
EG4
, Theorem 6.1 and the first three lines of its proof]
.
∎

For the following corollary recall from Proposition
2.11
and Definition

that the support of the Hopf
2
-cocycle

of
G
is only defined up to conjugacy in
G
.

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
G
be an affine algebraic unipotent group,

-cocycle for

(1)

,
and

{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}/\langle[{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J},{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}]\rangle=\mathcal{O}(N).

(2)

The group
\Gamma
of finite dimensional simple
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
-modules is

.

Proof.

(1) By Theorem
5.14
(1), the right hand side of the displayed equality is a Hopf ideal of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
, and the factor of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
by this ideal is isomorphic as a Hopf algebra to

applied to it. It follows that

\langle[{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J},{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}]\rangle\subseteq\mathcal{O}(G/N)^{+}{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}.

Suppose now that the inclusion in (

\langle[{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J},{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}]\rangle
is always a Hopf ideal (in any Hopf algebra), so the factor by it, being commutative and in characteristic 0, is reduced
by

. Hence, the strict inclusion in (

-module

which is not killed by

(2) This is immediate from (1).
∎

{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G)_{J}=\mathcal{O}(T\backslash G)=\mathbb{C}[F_{34}-F_{23},2F_{23}F_{34}-2F_{24}-F_{23}^{2}].

By Theorem
5.2
, using the notation
\overline{X}
for images of elements
X
of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
under
\pi
, we obtain

\displaystyle{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}

\displaystyle\cong{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G/T)_{J}\#_{\sigma}{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(T)_{J}

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{C}[F_{34}-F_{23},2F_{24}-F_{23}^{2}]\#_{\sigma}\mathbb{C}\langle\overline{F_{12}},\overline{F_{13}},\overline{F_{23}},\overline{F_{14}}\rangle.

We have already seen in Example
7.6
that
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(T)_{J}\cong U(\mathfrak{t})
, as required by Theorem
4.9
.

Finite dimensional simple modules:
From the defining relations (
7.17
) of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
it is immediate that its commutator ideal
\langle[{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J},{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}]\rangle
contains the elements
A:=\{F_{34},F_{23},F_{12}-F_{24}\}
. Let
I
be the ideal of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
generated by
A
, and observe that the elements of
A
in the given order form a polycentral set of generators for
I
(in the terminology of
\lx@sectionsign
5.2
). This implies that
A
generates the same ideal
I
in
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
and in
\mathcal{O}(G)
, necessarily a Hopf ideal, with

{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}/I=\mathcal{O}(G)/I=\mathbb{C}[\overline{F_{12}},\overline{F_{13}},\overline{F_{14}}],

where the first equality (of Hopf algebras) is seen to hold by checking that the commutators of all the generators are
0
in
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}/I
. Thus,
I=\langle[{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J},{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}]\rangle
, and so, reading off from the generating set
A
, the group
\Gamma
of
1
-dimensional
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
-modules is

\Gamma:=\{I+x(E_{12}+E_{24})+vE_{13}+wE_{14}\mid x,v,w\in\mathbb{C}\}\cong(\mathbb{C},+)^{3}.

We check that
\Gamma=C_{0}
and
\Gamma\cap T=\{I+vE_{13}+wE_{14}\mid v,w\in\mathbb{C}\}=F
, and

N:=N_{G}(T)=\{T+zE_{24}\mid z\in\mathbb{C}\},

so that
C=\Gamma T=N
.

The strata
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(Z_{g})_{J}
:
There are two cases to consider.

Case I:

g\in N:=N_{G}(T)
. We may choose as a set of coset representatives for
N/T
the elements

\{\gamma(x):=I+x(E_{12}+E_{24})\mid x\in\mathbb{C}\}\subset C_{0}=\Gamma.

Since
\gamma(x)\in\Gamma
, the winding automorphism
\tau^{\ell}_{\gamma(x)}:\alpha\mapsto\sum\alpha_{1}\gamma(x)(\alpha_{2})
,
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}(G)
, is an algebra automorphism of
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
, as well as of
\mathcal{O}(G)
, and maps the ideal
\mathcal{I}(\gamma(0))=\langle F_{34}-F_{23},2F_{24}-F_{23}^{2}\rangle
to
\mathcal{I}(\gamma(x))
, hence showing that, as algebras,

{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(Z_{\gamma(x)})_{J}\cong{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(Z_{\gamma(0)})_{J}={}_{J}\mathcal{O}(T)_{J}\cong U(\mathfrak{t}).

Case II:

g\notin N_{G}(T)
. Consider the elements

\{\beta(u):=I+uE_{34}\mid u\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\}\subset G\setminus N.

One calculates that
T\beta(u)T=N\beta(u)
, so that, together with the Case I cosets
T\gamma(x)
, these yield all the double cosets of
T
in
G
. Since the defining ideal of
N
in
\mathcal{O}(G)
is
\langle F_{34}-F_{23}\rangle
, we see that

\mathcal{I}(Z_{\beta(u)})=\langle F_{34}-F_{23}-u\rangle.

The generator
F_{34}-F_{23}-u
is central in
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}
, so that

{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(Z_{\beta(u)})_{J}={}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}/(F_{34}-F_{23}-u){}_{J}\mathcal{O}(G)_{J}=\mathbb{C}\langle\overline{F_{12}},\overline{F_{13}},\overline{F_{34}},\overline{F_{24}},\overline{F_{14}}\rangle.

Define new generators for
{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(Z_{\beta(u)})_{J}
as follows:

P:=\overline{F_{24}}-\overline{F_{34}}(\overline{F_{34}}-u),\,\,Q:=\overline{F_{14}},\,\,Z:=\overline{F_{34}},\,\,X:=-\overline{F_{12}},

Y:=\overline{F_{13}}-(P-\overline{F_{12}})P+\frac{1}{2}(\overline{F_{34}}+1)P^{2}.

One can now easily check that

{}_{J}\mathcal{O}(Z_{\beta(u)})_{J}=\mathbb{C}[X,Y,Z]\otimes\mathbb{C}\langle P,Q\rangle\cong\mathbb{C}[X,Y,Z]\otimes A_{1}(\mathbb{C}).\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\qed

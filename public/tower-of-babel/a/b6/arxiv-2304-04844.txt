Introduction

The category of
m-
periodic complexes, for
m\geq 2,
has independent interest by itself but also it is related to the orbit categories of the bounded derived category. The connection between the orbit categories of the derived categories of an algebra and the
m-
periodic complexes was established by the compression functor.

In
[
20
]
, L. Peng and J. Xiao showed a relationship between these two categories. In particular, the mentioned authors dealt with the
2-
periodic chain complexes over a finite dimensional hereditary algebra
H
and proved that the root category
\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mbox{mod}\,H)/[2]
, studied by D. Happel in
[
16
]
, inherits a triangulated structure from
\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mbox{mod}\,H).

The orbit category of a triangulated category is not necessarily triangulated itself. However in 2005, B. Keller devised a triangulated hull for certain orbit categories of the derived categories. For any finite dimensional algebra
A
of finite global dimension, the author defined a triangulated functor from the derived category to the triangulated hull in such a way that the orbit category
\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mbox{mod}\,A)/[m]
embeds into its triangulated hull. The embedding is an equivalence in case that the orbit category admits a canonical triangle structure, see
[
18
]
.

Several authors studied the above problem and found that the category of
m-
periodic complexes is a good framework to deal with. In particular, the natural functor from the category of bounded complexes to the
m-
periodic category, called the compression functor, plays an important role because when it is dense is precisely the case that the orbit category inherits a triangulated structure from the derived category
\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mbox{mod}\,A).

For an additive category
{\mathcal{A}}
, the category of
m-
periodic complexes
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}})
, together with the relative homotopy category
\mathbf{K}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}})
and its derived category
\mathbf{D}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}})
in case that
{\mathcal{A}}
is abelian, became to have more and more interest by itself and also related to the compression functor, see between others
[
12
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
26
]
and
[
27
]
.

It is known that if
{\mathcal{A}}
is a hereditary additive category then the compression functor is dense. Moreover, the result is also true in case that the algebra
A
is derived equivalent to a hereditary category, see
[
18
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
26
]
. We observe that if this is the case, then the essential image of the compression functor is equivalent to the category of
m-
periodic complexes.

Some properties concerning the Auslander–Reiten theory for the category of
m-
periodic complexes when
H
is a hereditary algebra were studied in
[
6
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
and
[
21
]
.
In case that
H
is a hereditary algebra, the almost split sequences of
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,H)
were studied in
[
11
]
considering the almost split sequences in the category of complexes over the hereditary algebra and the fact that the compression functor is dense in that case.
Recently in
[
10
]
, the author got the Auslander-Reiten quiver for
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,H)
using an alternative approach.

In the general context of linear
k-
categories, the notion of Galois
G-
covering was defined in
[
3
]
and requires the density condition.
The authors showed that a Galois
G
-covering between Krull-Schmidt additive categories preserves almost split sequences. Inspired by this fact, a natural question is under which conditions over an additive category
\mathcal{A}
one can ensure that the compression functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}:\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
is a Galois
G-
covering?

The Auslander-Reiten quiver of a category provides important information of the category by considering only those morphisms and vertices from which one can build all others. The construction of this quiver is often hard and we need to manage the irreducible morphisms and indecomposable objects of our category. The existence of Auslander-Reiten sequences which allow us to construct the quiver is a previous step to have in mind.

For
A
a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra of finite global dimension, it was proved in
[
12
]
, Theorem 2.10, that the triangulated hull of the orbit category of
\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mbox{mod}\,A)/[m]
admits Auslander-Reiten triangles.

On the other hand, the triangulated hull
R
is triangle equivalent to
\mathbf{K}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
and also to
\mathbf{D}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{mod}\,A)
(see Theorem 2.10 in
[
27
]
). Therefore
\mathbf{K}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
admits Auslander-Reiten triangles. We point out that this fact is equivalent to the existence of almost split sequences in the category
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
because the relative homotopy category is the stable category of
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
, see
[
22
]
.

A generalization of finite dimensional
k-
algebras is the notion of dualizing
k-
categories introduced by M. Auslander and I. Reiten in
[
2
]
, where they proved that the category
\mbox{proj}\,A
is an example of a dualizing category. From
[
5
]
we know that if
\mathcal{A}
is a dualizing category, then the category
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}(\mathcal{A})
has almost split sequences. Moreover, if
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathcal{A}
has finite global dimension then
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
has almost split sequences (see
[
15
]
). Therefore, if we know that the compression functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}
is a Galois
G-
covering, the existence of almost split sequences in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
guarantee the existence of almost split sequences in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A}).

In this work, we are interested on the construction of the so called Auslander-Reiten quiver of the category of
m-
periodic complexes over a finite dimensional algebra with finite strong global dimension.
The category of complexes of fixed size plays an important role in our arguments and we reserve special attention to the restriction of the compression functor to this category.
In the particular case that
A
is an iterated tilted algebra, we deduce that the compression functor is a Galois
G-
covering in the sense of
[
3
]
. Then we use this fact to obtain the Auslander-Reiten quiver of the category
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
.

The work is organized as follows. We dedicate the first section to recall basic results on
m-
periodic complexes over an additive category
{\mathcal{A}}
and also some properties of the compression functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}:\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}})
, for any positive integer
m
. Furthermore, we collect a few results from
[
3
]
concerning Galois
G-
coverings, which are useful to prove some statements in the next section. In particular, we study properties of the compression functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}:\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
when
A
is a finite dimensional algebra of finite global dimension.
In Section 2, we prove some facts of the compression functor respect to indecomposable objects, irreducible morphisms and density, whenever
{\mathcal{A}}
is an additive category. Section 3, is devoted to show conditions over
\mathcal{A}
that allows us to ensure that the compression functor is a Galois
G-
covering in the sense of
[
3
]
. In the end of this section, we study the dualizing case and prove that under extra hypothesis, the category of
m-
periodic complexes has almost split sequences and that we can get them from almost split sequences of
\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
. More precisely, we can get them from almost split sequences of
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})
. In particular, we get the result for
\mathcal{A}=\mbox{proj}\,A
in case that
A
is an iterated tilted algebra. In Section 4, we show how to construct the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
, for
A
a finite dimensional algebra with finite strong global dimension. Finally, in the last section, we study the behaviour of sectional paths in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
, whenever
A
is any finite dimensional algebra.

1.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an additive
k-
category over a commutative ring
k

and
{\mathcal{P}}
be the full subcategory consisting of all the projective objects in
\mathcal{A}
. By Mod-
\mathcal{A}
we denote the category which consists of all additive functors from
\mathcal{A}^{op}
to the category of abelian groups. The Yoneda functor allows us to see
\mathcal{A}
as a full subcategory of the cocomplete abelian category Mod-
\mathcal{A}.

We denote by
\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{A})
the additive
k-
category which consists of unbounded chain complexes and by
\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
the full subcategory of
\mathbf{C}(\mathcal{A})
which consists of the bounded complexes.

Let
A
be a finite dimensional algebra and
\mbox{mod}\,A
be the category of finitely generated
A
-modules. We denote by
\mbox{proj}\,A
the full subcategory of
\mbox{mod}\,A
whose objects are the projective
A
-modules.

For the category of finitely generated modules over a finite dimensional algebra of finite global dimension,
\mbox{mod}\,A
, we consider the Frobenius category
\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mbox{proj}\,A})
and the homotopy category
\mathbf{K}^{b}({\mbox{proj}\,A})
which is triangle equivalent to the stable category.

With abuse of notation and by the triangle equivalences given in
[
16
]
, we identify the following triangulated categories

\underline{\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)}\simeq\mathbf{K}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)\simeq\mathbf{D}^{b}(\mbox{mod}\,A).

1.1
.

The
m-
periodic complexes.
Let
m
be a positive integer.

By definition an
m-
periodic complex over
\mathcal{A}
is a complex
X=(X^{i},d_{X}^{i})
such that
X^{i}=X^{j}
and
d_{X}^{i}=d_{X}^{j}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
where
i\equiv_{m}j
, that means the integer numbers
i
and
j
are congruent module
m.
The objects
X^{i}
are in
\mathcal{A}
and the differentials
d_{X}^{i}:X^{i}\rightarrow X^{i+1}
are morphisms in
\mathcal{A}
such that
d_{X}^{i+1}d_{X}^{i}=0
.

If
X=(X^{i},d_{X}^{i})
and
Y=(Y^{i},d_{Y}^{i})
are two
m-
periodic complexes, a morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
is a sequence of morphisms
f^{i}:X^{i}\rightarrow Y^{i}
of
\mathcal{A}
such that
f^{i}=f^{j}
for all
i,j\in\mathbb{Z}
with
i\equiv_{m}j
and such that

d_{i}f^{i+1}=f^{i}d_{i+1}
for all
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. We compose the morphisms from right to left.

We denote by
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
the category of
m
-periodic complexes over
{\mathcal{A}}.

The category
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
is a Frobenius category and the stable category
\underline{\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}})}
coincides with
\mathbf{K}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}})
the relative homotopy category of
m-
periodic complexes. If we consider the restriction to the full subcategory
{\mathcal{P}}
of
\mathcal{A}
, we get the category
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{P}})
and also the category
\mathbf{K}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{P}})
(see
[
20
]
).

For each integer
s
there is a canonical shift functor
[s]
on
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}})
which maps an
m-
periodic complex
(X,d_{X})
to
(X[s],\delta)
where
\delta^{i}=(-1)^{s}d_{X}^{s+i}.

In the particular case that
\mathcal{A}
is the category of finitely generated modules over a finite dimensional algebra of finite global dimension,
\mbox{mod}\,A
, we can consider the
m-
periodic derived category of
A
,
\mathbf{D}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{mod}\,A),
that is, the localization of the relative homotopy category with respect to quasi-isomorphisms. Moreover, the categories
\mathbf{K}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
and
\mathbf{D}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{mod}\,A)
are triangulated equivalent and the
m-
periodic derived category is invariant under derived equivalences (see
[
27
]
and
[
26
]
).

With abuse of notation, throughout this paper, for a finite dimensional algebra
A
with finite global dimension we identify the following triangulated categories:

\underline{\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)}\simeq\mathbf{K}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)\simeq\mathbf{D}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{mod}\,A).

1.2
.

The compression functor.

By
[
20
]
, we know that there is an exact functor, named the compression functor,
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}:\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
that induces an exact functor between the homotopy categories
\mathbf{K}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
and
\mathbf{K}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
. Moreover, it also induces an exact functor from the homotopy category
\mathbf{K}^{b}({\mathcal{P}})
and the relative homotopy category
\mathbf{K}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{P}}).

With abuse of notation, we also denote by
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}
the compression functor between the homotopy categories.

We recall the definition of the compression functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}:\mathbf{C^{b}}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
given in
[
20
]
.

Definition 1.3
.

For
X=(X^{i},d_{X}^{i})
a complex in
\mathbf{C^{b}}(\mathcal{A})
, we set
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X)=({\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X)^{i},d_{{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X)}^{i})
where
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X)^{i}=\oplus_{{}_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}}X^{i+tm}
and
d_{{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X)}^{i}=(d_{st}^{i})_{s,t\in\mathbb{Z}}
such that
d_{st}^{i}:X^{i+sm}\rightarrow X^{(i+1)+tm}
with
d_{st}^{i}=0
for
s\neq t
and
d_{ss}^{i}=d_{X}^{i+ms}
for all
s\in\mathbb{Z}
.

For a morphism
f=(f^{i}):X\rightarrow Y
in
\mathbf{C^{b}}(\mathcal{A})
, we set
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(f)=(g^{i}):\ {\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X)\rightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(Y)
where
g^{i}=(g_{st}^{i})
such that
g_{st}^{i}:X^{i+sm}\rightarrow Y^{i+tm}
with
g_{st}^{i}=0
for
s\neq t
and
g_{ss}^{i}=f^{i+ms}
for all
s\in\mathbb{Z}
.

In a more general context, if
\mathcal{A}
has coproducts then we can consider the compression functor
\Delta:\mathbf{C}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}})
given by

\Delta(X)=\oplus_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}X[mj]
for a complex
(X,d_{X})\,\in\mathbf{C}({\mathcal{A}}).
When we restrict to bounded complexes we get that

{\mathcal{F}}_{m}:\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow\mathbf{C}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}})
is the composition of the inclusion functor
\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow\mathbf{C}({\mathcal{A}})
with
\Delta
.

We observe that the inclusion functor
{\bigtriangledown}:\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow\mathbf{C}({\mathcal{A}})
is the right adjoint of
\Delta.
That means,

\mbox{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}})}(\Delta(X),\Delta(Y))\simeq\mbox{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}({\mathcal{A}})}(X,{\bigtriangledown}\Delta(Y)).

Now, we focus on the case that
\mathcal{A}=\mbox{proj}\,A
for
A
a finite dimensional algebra of finite global dimension.

The following result shows the link between the compression functor and the orbit categories, see
[
18
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
A
be a finite dimensional algebra with finite global dimension and
m\geq 1
. The compression functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}:\ \mathbf{K}^{b}(\emph{proj}\,A)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\equiv m}(\emph{proj}\,A)
yields an embedding from its essential image
\mathbf{K}^{b}(\emph{proj}\,A)/[m]
into its triangulated hull
\mathbf{K}_{\equiv m}(\emph{proj}\,A).

Moreover, the following statements are equivalent.

(a)

The category
\mathbf{K}^{b}(\emph{proj}\,A)/[m]
has a canonical triangulated structure.

(b)

The embedding
i:\mathbf{K}^{b}(\emph{proj}\,A)/[m]{\longrightarrow}\mathbf{K}_{\equiv m}(\emph{proj}\,A)
is an equivalence.

(c)

The compression functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}:\ \mathbf{K}^{b}(\emph{proj}\,A)\rightarrow\mathbf{K}_{\equiv m}(\emph{proj}\,A)
is dense.

Furthermore, if one of the above Statements holds for one choice of
m
then it holds for all positive integer
m.

Proof.

By
[
26
, Theorem 4.3]
and also
[
27
]
, we know that the compression functor provides an embedding
i
from its essential image
\mathbf{K}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)/[m]
to its triangulated hull
\mathbf{K}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
.
Moreover, by
[
27
]
, we know that the triangulated hull of
\mathbf{K}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)/[m]
is triangle equivalent to the category
\mathbf{K}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A).
Then we get that Statement (a) is equivalent to Statement (b).

On the other hand, we have that the embedding
i:\mathbf{K}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)/[m]{\longrightarrow}\mathbf{K}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
is an equivalence if and only if
i
is dense. Moreover,
i
is dense if and only if
the compression functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}
is dense. Then Statement (b) is equivalent to Statement (c).

By
[
25
, Theorem 1]
, we know that if the orbit category
\mathbf{K}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)/[m]
is triangulated for one choice of
m
then it is triangulated for each
m
, proving the result.
∎

1.5
.

Galois
G
-covering.

We recall several notions and results stated in
[
3
]
that shall be fundamental to prove some results throughout the next sections. Here a linear category stands for an additive skeletal
k-
linear category.

We recall the definition of
G
-precovering given in
[
3
]
, (also see
[
1
, 1.6]
).

Definition 1.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be linear categories with
G
a group acting on
\mathcal{A}
. A functor

F:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
is called a
G
-precovering provided that
F
has a
G
-stabilizer
\delta
such that, for
any
X,Y\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
, the following two maps are isomorphisms:

F_{X,Y}:\oplus\mathcal{A}(X,g.Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(F(X),F(Y)):(u_{g})_{g\in G}\rightarrow\Sigma_{g\in G}F(u_{g})\delta_{(g,Y)}.

F^{X,Y}:\oplus\mathcal{A}(g.X,Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(F(X),F(Y)):(v_{g})_{g\in G}\rightarrow\Sigma_{g\in G}\delta^{-1}_{(g,X)}F(v_{g}).

In the above definition, as observed by H. Asashiba in
[
1
, 1.6]
, it is sufficient to require that all
F_{X,Y}
or all
F^{X,Y}
be isomorphisms.

Next, we state the definition of an irreducible morphism in an additive category.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an additive category
and
f:X\rightarrow Y
a morphism in
\mathcal{A}
. The morphism

f
is said to be a section (respectively, a
retraction) if and only if there is a morphism
h:Y\rightarrow X
such that
hf=1_{X}
(respectively,
fh=1_{Y}
). Whenever one of
these conditions hold,
f
is said to be a split morphism.

The morphism
f
is said to be irreducible if
it is not a split morphism and, for any factorization
f=hg
, we
have that either
g
is a section or
h
is a retraction.

The next result is fundamental to prove that the compression functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}
reflects irreducible morphisms. Although the isomorphisms that we use to prove the result are well-known and moreover, since we can find a proof in Proposition 3.9 (2), in the pre-print
[
23
]
, for the convenience of the reader, bellow we state and present a proof that the functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}
is a
G-
precovering with
G
the cyclic group generated by
[m]
.

First observe that the cyclic group generated by
[m],
acts admissibly on
\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}}).
In fact,

(1)

the action is free because
X\neq X[mj]
for any indecomposable complex in
\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
and

(2)

it is locally bounded since
\mbox{Hom}(X,Y[mj])=0
for all but finitely many integers
j
and for any indecomposable complexes
X
and
Y
in
\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}}).

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an additive
k-
category. The functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}:\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}})
is a
G-
precovering with
G
the cyclic group generated by
[m]
and
\delta=id
a
G-
stabilizer.

Proof.

Consider
X
and
Y
in
\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}}).
Since the complexes are bounded then there is a finite set
I\subset\mathbb{Z}
such that
\mbox{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})}(X,Y[mj])=0
for all integer
j\notin I
.

Using this fact we have the following isomorphisms:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mbox{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}})}({\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X),{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(Y))&=&\mbox{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}({\mathcal{A}})}(X,{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(Y))\\
&=&\mbox{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}({\mathcal{A}})}(X,\oplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}Y[mj])\\
&=&\oplus_{j\in I}\mbox{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})}(X,Y[mj])\\
&=&\oplus_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\mbox{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})}(X,Y[mj])\end{array}

proving that the functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}
is a
G-
precovering, (see Definition
1.6
).
∎

As a consequence of Proposition
1.8
, we get the following corollary which is fundamental to prove the irreducibility of the morphisms in different categories.

We first observe that in
[
3
, Lemma 2.7]
the complexes
X,Y
and
Z
in
\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
are not necessarily indecomposable.

Corollary 1.9
.

(
[
3
]
, Lemma 2.7)
Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an additive category and
f:X\rightarrow Y
in
\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
be a morphism.
The following conditions hold.

(1)

If
g:X\rightarrow Z
or
g:Z\rightarrow Y
is a morphism in
\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
then
g
factorizes through
f
if and only if

{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(g)
factorizes through
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(f).

(2)

The morphism
f
is a section, retraction, or isomorphism if an only if
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(f)
is a section, retraction, or isomorphism, respectively.

A functor
F:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
between linear categories is called almost dense if each indecomposable object in
\mathcal{B}
is isomorphic to an object lying in the image of
F
.

Finally, we recall the definition of a Galois
G
-covering stated in
[
3
, Definition 2.8]
.

Definition 1.10
.

Consider
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
linear categories with
G
a group acting admissibly on
\mathcal{A}
. Following
[
3
, Definition 2.8]
we say that a
G
-precovering
F:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}
is called a Galois
G
-covering provided that the following
conditions are verified.

(a)

The functor
F
is almost dense.

(b)

If
X\in\mathcal{A}
is indecomposable, then

is indecomposable.

(c)

If

are indecomposable with

such that

.

1.11
.

The category of complexes of fixed size.
Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an additive category. The category of complexes of fixed sized, precisely of size
n
with
n\geq 2
, is the full subcategory of
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
whose objects are the complexes with cells zero
outside of the natural interval
[1,n].
We denote by
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}(\mathcal{A})
this category.

If
0<n\leq s
we can consider the full and faithful functor
\phi_{n,s}:\,\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{[1,s]}({\mathcal{A}})
that sends each
X\in\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})
to

X^{1}{\rightarrow}X^{2}\rightarrow\dots{\rightarrow}X^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\rightarrow}}0\stackrel{{\scriptstyle s-n}}{{\cdots}}{\rightarrow}0

in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,s]}({\mathcal{A}}).
Therefore we can consider
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})
as a full subcategory of
\mathbf{C}_{[1,s]}({\mathcal{A}}).

Without loss of generality, if
n\leq s
we shall identify a complex
X\in\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})
with
\phi_{n,s}(X)\in\mathbf{C}_{[1,s]}({\mathcal{A}}).

2.
The compression functor of bounded complexes

Throughout all this section, we consider
\mathcal{A}
an additive category.

2.1.
The compression functor and the indecomposable complexes

We start this section showing that the compression functor provides an equivalence between the category
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})
and a full subcategory of
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv s}({\mathcal{A}}),
in the case that
0<n\leq s
.

First, we fix some useful notation.

Notation 2.1
.

We denote by
\mathcal{X}_{s}^{\prime}
the full subcategory of
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv s}({\mathcal{A}})
which consists of the
s-
periodic complexes
Z^{\prime}
as follow

Z^{\prime}:\cdots\rightarrow Z^{s-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\rightarrow}}Z^{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0}}{{\rightarrow}}Z^{1}{\rightarrow}Z^{2}\rightarrow\dots{\rightarrow}Z^{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0}}{{\rightarrow}}Z^{1}{\rightarrow}Z^{2}\rightarrow\dots{\rightarrow}Z^{s}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0}}{{\rightarrow}}Z^{1}{\rightarrow}\dots

with differential
d^{sj}=0
for any
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Whenever it is convenience, we use
\mathcal{X}_{n,s}^{\prime}
to denote the full subcategory of
\mathcal{X}_{s}^{\prime}
which consists of the complexes
Z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{s}^{\prime}
such that

Z^{t+sj}=0
for any
t\in\{n+1,\dots,s\}
and
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

Note that
\mathcal{X}_{s,s}^{\prime}=\mathcal{X}_{s}^{\prime}.

By
(-)^{\prime}:\,\mathbf{C}_{[1,s]}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow\mathcal{X}_{s}^{\prime}
we denote the functor that sends
Z\in\mathbf{C}_{[1,s]}({\mathcal{A}})
to
Z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{s}^{\prime}.

Remark 2.2
.

The functor
(-)^{\prime}:\,\mathbf{C}_{[1,s]}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow\mathcal{X}_{s}^{\prime}
is an equivalence because it is essentially surjective and also a full and faithful functor. In fact, consider
X
and
Y
complexes in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,s]}({\mathcal{A}})
. Observe that
f:X\rightarrow Y
is a morphism of complexes if and only if
f^{\prime}:X^{\prime}\rightarrow Y^{\prime}
is a morphism in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv s}({\mathcal{A}}).
This means,

\mbox{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}_{\equiv s}({\mathcal{A}})}(X^{\prime},Y^{\prime})=\mbox{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})}(X,Y)
and therefore the functor
(-)^{\prime}

is full and faithful.

By Remark
2.2
, we get the following result.

Proposition 2.3
.

Consider
s\geq n.
The compression functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{s}:\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv s}({\mathcal{A}})
is a full and faithful functor and its essential image is the full subcategory
\mathcal{X}_{n,s}^{\prime}
of
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv s}({\mathcal{A}}).
Then we get the factorization
{\mathcal{F}}_{s}:\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{X}_{n,s}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathbf{C}_{\equiv s}({\mathcal{A}}).
Moreover,
{\mathcal{F}}_{s}
preserves and reflects indecomposable complexes.

Proof.

Consider
Z\in\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})
and
Z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{n,s}^{\prime}
the
s-
periodic complex defined in
2.1
.

We affirm that
{\mathcal{F}}_{s}(Z)=Z^{\prime}.
In fact, for each
{t\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
we can consider
{j_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}}
the unique integer such that
t\in[sj_{0}+1,sj_{0}+s].
Then we have that
{\mathcal{F}}_{s}(Z)^{t}=\oplus_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}Z^{t-sj}=Z^{t-sj_{0}}=(Z^{\prime})^{t}.
We infer that the essential image of
{\mathcal{F}}_{s}
is the full subcategory
\mathcal{X}_{n,s}^{\prime}
into
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv s}({\mathcal{A}}).

By Remark
2.2
, we get that
(-)^{\prime}\circ\phi_{n,s}:\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow\mathcal{X}_{n,s}^{\prime}
is an equivalence of categories.
Then the functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{s}
is the composition

\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(-)^{\prime}\circ\phi_{n,s}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathcal{X}_{n,s}^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathbf{C}_{\equiv s}({\mathcal{A}})

where
i
is the inclusion morphism.

Finally, consider
X
a complex in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})
. Note that
W
is a direct summand of

{\mathcal{F}}_{s}(X)=X^{\prime}
in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv s}({\mathcal{A}})
if and only if
W\in\mathcal{X}_{n}^{\prime}.
Indeed, if
s>n
we conclude the result using the fact that
(X^{\prime})^{j}=0
for each
j\in\{n+1,\dots,s\}.
If
s=n
since there is a section
u:W\rightarrow X^{\prime}
then
d_{W}^{n}=0
because
u^{1}d_{W}^{n}=d_{X^{\prime}}^{n}u^{n}=0
and
u^{1}
is a monomorphism.
Therefore,
W\in\mathcal{X}_{n}^{\prime}
and
W=Y^{\prime}={\mathcal{F}}_{s}(Y)
for some
Y
in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})
.
From this fact and using that the compression functor is additive we get the following equivalent statements:
{\mathcal{F}}_{s}(X)={\mathcal{F}}_{s}(Y)\oplus{\mathcal{F}}_{s}(L)
in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv s}({\mathcal{A}})
if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}_{s}(X)={\mathcal{F}}_{s}(Y)\oplus{\mathcal{F}}_{s}(L)
in
\mathcal{X}_{n}^{\prime}
and also
{\mathcal{F}}_{s}(X)={\mathcal{F}}_{s}(Y)\oplus{\mathcal{F}}_{s}(L)
in
\mathcal{X}_{n}^{\prime}
if and only if
X=Y\oplus L
in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}}).

∎

We observe that, up to shifts, a complex in
\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
belongs to a category
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})
, for some
n
.

Remark 2.4
.

Consider
l(X)
the size of the complex
X\in\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}}).

Then the following conditions hold.

(1)

For each complex
X\in\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
there is an integer
t
such that
X[t]\in\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})
where
n=l(X)
. Moreover,
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X[t])\in\mathcal{X}_{n}^{\prime}
for every
m\geq n=l(X)
, see Proposition
2.3
.

(2)

For every
X
and
Y
in
\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
there is an integer
t
and
n>1
such that
X[t]
and
Y[t]
in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})
. Moreover, the following isomorphisms

\mbox{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}})}({\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X[t]),{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(Y[t]))=\mbox{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})}(X[t],Y[t])=\mbox{Hom}_{\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})}(X,Y)

hold as a consequence of the equivalence given in Proposition
2.3
and the fact that the shift functor
[-]:\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
is an equivalence of categories.

In general, for bounded complexes we shall show that compression functor preserve indecomposable complexes.

First, we introduce the following notation.

Notation 2.5
.

We fix a
m>0
and a
n>1.

We denote by
w
the positive integer such that
w=\mbox{min}\{s\geq 1,\,\mbox{such that }ms\geq n\}.

We note that there is an integer
l\geq 0
such that
mw=n+l
.

Now, we collect some facts about the compression functor.

Lemma 2.6
.

For
m\geq 1
and
X\in\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})
the following conditions hold.

(1)

{\mathcal{F}}_{mw}(X)=\oplus_{j\equiv_{w}0}X[mj]=X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{n,mw}^{\prime}.

(2)

The
m-
periodic complex
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X)
decomposes as a finite sum of
mw-
periodic complexes as follows

{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X)=\oplus_{j\in[0,w-1]}{\mathcal{F}}_{mw}(X)[mj].

Proof.

If
s\geq n,
then by Proposiion
2.3
we know that
{\mathcal{F}}_{s}(X)=\oplus_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}X[sj]=X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}_{s}^{\prime}
. In particular,
mw\geq n
and we have that

{\mathcal{F}}_{mw}(X)=\oplus_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}X[mwj]=\oplus_{j\equiv_{w}0}X[mj]=X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{n,mw}.

For
m\geq 1
, we can express

\begin{array}[]{lcl}{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X)&=&\oplus_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}X[mj]\\
&=&\oplus_{j\equiv_{w}0}X[mj]\oplus\oplus_{j\equiv_{w}1}X[mj]\oplus\dots\oplus_{j\equiv_{w}(w-1)}X[mj]\\
&=&\oplus_{j\equiv_{w}0}X[mj]\oplus\oplus_{j\equiv_{w}0}X[mj+1]\oplus\dots\oplus_{j\equiv_{w}0}X[mj+(w-1)].\end{array}

Therefore, we get that

{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X)=\oplus_{j\in[0,w-1]}X^{\prime}[mj]=\oplus_{j\in[0,w-1]}{\mathcal{F}}_{mw}(X)[mj],

proving the result.
∎

Now, we prove that the functor

{\mathcal{F}}_{m}:\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
preserves indecomposable complexes.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Krull-Schmidt category. For any
m>1
we get that the compression functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}:\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
preserves and reflects indecomposable complexes.

Proof.

If
Z
is a direct summand of
X
in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
, that is,
X=Z\oplus L
in
\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
for some
L
then

{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X)={\mathcal{F}}_{m}(Z)\oplus{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(L)
in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}}),
since the compression functor is additive.

Consider an indecomposable complex
X\in\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
X\in\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}})
with
X^{1}\neq 0
and
X^{n}\neq 0.
Since
X
is indecomposable in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
, then so is in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}}).
If
m\geq n,
we get the result from Proposition
2.3
.

Now, we analyze the case that
m<n.
By Lemma
2.6
, the complex

{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X)=\oplus_{j\in[0,w-1]}{\mathcal{F}}_{mw}(X)[mj]

is a finite sum of
mw-
periodic complexes with
mw=n+l
where
0\leq l\leq m-1.
In fact, since
w=\mbox{min}\{s\geq 1,\,\mbox{such that}\,ms\geq n\}
then
mw\geq n
and by the minimality of
w
we have that
n>m(w-1)
. Therefore,
n+l\geq n
implies that
l\geq 0
. Furthermore,

n+l-m<n
then
l<m
and hence
l\leq m-1
. We observe that in this case
w\geq 2.

By Proposition
2.3
we know that
{\mathcal{F}}_{mw}(X)
is indecomposable in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv mw}({\mathcal{A}})
because
X
is indecomposable in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\mathcal{A}}).
Then also the complex
{\mathcal{F}}_{mw}(X)[mj]
is indecomposable in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv mw}({\mathcal{A}})
for any
{j\in[0,w-1]}.

We prove that
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X)
has only trivial direct summand in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}}).
In fact, if
Z
is a non-zero direct summand of
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X)
in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}})
then there is a section
u:Z\rightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(X)
in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}})
. Furthermore,
u
is also a section in
\mathbf{C}({\mathcal{A}})
and also a section in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv mw}({\mathcal{A}}).

Then we infer that there is
{J\subseteq[0,w-1]}
such that

as
mw-
periodic complex.
Since
Z\neq 0
then
J\neq\emptyset
. Consider

Since
Z
is a
m-
periodic complex we have that

for every
{t\in[0,w-1]}.
Then

\begin{array}[]{lcl}Z&=&\oplus_{j\in J}{\mathcal{F}}_{mw}(X)[m(j+t-j_{0})]\\
&=&{\mathcal{F}}_{mw}(X)[mt]\oplus\oplus_{j\neq j_{0}}{\mathcal{F}}_{mw}(X)[m(j+t-j_{0})],\end{array}

and then

is a direct summand of

for each

. Thus

proving that

is an indecomposable

∎

2.2.
Irreducible morphisms under the compression functor.

In the rest of this section, we focus our attention on the behaviour of the irreducible morphisms under the compression functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}:\mathbf{C}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mathcal{A}})

First, we prove that the compression functor reflects irreducible morphisms.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let

{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(f)

1.9
(2),

with
s.gl.\emph{dim}\,A=\nu
then the following statements hold.

(1)

The compression functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}:\mathbf{C}^{b}(\emph{proj}\,A)\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\emph{proj}\,A)
is a Galois
G
-covering in the sense of
[

]
.

(2)

There is a bijective correspondence between the sets
\,\emph{Ind}(\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\emph{proj}\,A))\,
and

\bigcup_{k=0}^{m-1}\emph{Ind}(\mathbf{C}_{[1,\nu+1]}(\emph{proj}\,A)[k])
.

3.1.
Dualizing
k-
categories

Dualizing
k-
categories were introduced by M. Auslander and I. Reiten in
[
2
]
as a generalization of artin
k
-algebras.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt
k
-category. By
\mbox{Mod}\,\mathcal{A}
we denote the category of contravariant functors
F:\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathrm{Mod}\,k
and by
\mbox{mod}\,\mathcal{A}
the full subcategory of
\mbox{Mod}\,\mathcal{A}
whose objects are the finitely presented functors.

The category
\mathcal{A}
is called dualizing if the finitely presented objects and the
finitely copresented objects in
\mathrm{Mod}\,\mathcal{A}
coincide, see
[
2
]
and
[
13
]
.
If
\mathcal{A}
is dualizing,
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathcal{A}
is an abelian category with enough projective objects. Therefore the category
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathcal{A})
is also abelian.

From
[
5
]
we know that if
\mathcal{A}
is a dualizing category, then the category
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathcal{A})
so is and the usual exact structure
\mathcal{F}
on
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathrm{mod}\,\mathcal{A})
has almost split sequences. Moreover,
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}(\mathcal{A})
has almost split sequences. If
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathcal{A}
has finite global dimension then
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
has almost split sequences, see for example
[
15
]
.

In all that follows,
\mathcal{A}
shall be a Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt
k-
category
verifying that there is a positive integer
n\geq 2
such that each indecomposable complex in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
is a shift of an indecomposable complex in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}(\mathcal{A}).

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a dualizing category such that
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathcal{A}
has finite global dimension. Then
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
has almost split sequences.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
, we know that
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}
is a Galois
G
-covering. Moreover, by
[
15
, Corollary 2]
if
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathcal{A}
has finite global dimension then
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
has almost split sequences.

By
[
3
, Theorem 3.7]
we know that a short sequence
\eta
in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
is an almost split sequence if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(\eta)
is an almost split sequence in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
.
∎

Remark 3.7
.

In
[
15
, Corollary 5]
, the authors proved that if
\mathcal{A}
is dualizing
and
\mathrm{mod}\,\mathcal{A}
has finite global dimension then
\mathbf{C}^{b}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
has almost split sequences. Here
\mathbf{C}^{b}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
denotes the smallest full subcategory of
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
containing all the stalk
m-
periodic complexes closed under finite extensions.

The mentioned authors proved that when
\mbox{mod}\,\mathcal{A}
has global dimension less than or equal to one then both categories
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
and
\mathbf{C}^{b}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
coincide.

By
[
5
]
, we know that there are almost split sequences in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}(\mathcal{A}),
whenever
\mathcal{A}
is dualizing. The next result shows that these sequences provide the ones in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A}).

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a dualizing
k-
category such that
\mbox{mod}\,\mathcal{A}
has finite global dimension. Then each almost split sequence in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
is the image under
\mathcal{F}_{m}
of the shift of an almost split sequence in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha:A^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}B^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\rightarrow}}C^{\prime}
be an almost split sequence in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
. By
Theorem
3.1
there is a complex
X\in\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}(\mathcal{A})
such that
\mathcal{F}_{m}(X[t])=A^{\prime}
for some integer
t.

By Proposition
2.13
, since
A^{\prime}
is not projective-injective then
X
neither so is. By Theorem
3.2
we know that the functor
\mathcal{F}_{m}:\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mathcal{A})
is a Galois
G
-covering thus we may consider
\eta:\ X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h}}{{\rightarrow}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle l}}{{\rightarrow}}Z
an almost split sequence in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
starting in
X
such that
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(\eta[t])=\alpha
. Observe that we can consider the shift such that
X^{1}\neq 0.
Then
X^{n+1}=0
because
X
is indecomposable. Hence
X\in\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}(\mathcal{A}).

Now, we prove that
\eta
is in fact an almost split sequence in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}(\mathcal{A}).

First, we affirm that
Y^{n+1}=0.
In fact, consider
f:X\rightarrow Y=\oplus_{i=1}^{r}Y_{i}
. We observe that if
Y^{n+1}\neq 0
then there is an indecomposable direct summand of
Y,
namely
Y_{t}
such that
Y_{t}^{n+1}\neq 0
and
f_{t}:X\rightarrow Y_{t}
is an irreducible morphism between indecomposable complexes. We point out that
Y_{t}
is not projective-injective, otherwise
Y_{t}=J_{n}(P)
and then
f=0
because
id_{P}f^{n}=0.

Then, the mapping cone
C_{f_{t}}
is an indecomposable complex. Therefore, we get a contradiction because
C_{f_{t}}^{1}=X[1]^{1}\oplus Y_{t}^{1}=X^{2}\neq 0
and
C_{f_{t}}^{n+1}=X[1]^{n+1}\oplus Y_{t}^{n+1}\neq 0.
Then we conclude that
Y^{n+1}=0.

From the fact that
Y^{n+1}=X^{n+1}\oplus Z^{n+1}=Z^{n+1}
we get that
Z^{n+1}=0
.

Therefore we only need to show that
Y
has length at most
n
so we can conclude that
X,Y,Z\in\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}(\mathcal{A}).

Assume that
Y^{0}\neq 0
, then there exists a indecomposable direct summand of
Y
,
Y_{r}
, with
Y^{0}_{r}\neq 0
and there also be an irreducible morphism
f_{r}:X\to Y_{r}
.
Again, since an indecomposable complex has at most
n
non-zero entries and
Y^{0}_{r}\neq 0
we have that
Y_{r}^{n}=0
. Now, since
f_{r}
is an irreducible morphism and
X^{0}=0
, then by
[
14
, Corollary 2, Proposition 3]
the irreducible morphism
f_{r}
is a split monomorphism and thus
X^{n}=0
.

Now, if this is the case we have that
Y^{0}=Z^{0}\neq 0
implies
Z^{n}=0
and thus
Y^{n}=0
because
Z
is an indecomposable object. Hence if
Y^{-1}=0
we can choose another interval
[1,n]
by shifting the complexes one entry to the left and we are done. If
Y^{-1}\neq 0
, we repeat the above argument to get that
X^{n-1},Y^{n-1}
and
Z^{n-1}
are zero and we proceed to analyze if
Y^{-2}=0
or not. We repeat this argument, until there exists
i<n
such that
Y^{-i}=0
. Observe that since
X^{1}\neq 0
this process stops.
Selecting the proper interval we get that
X,Y,Z\in\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}(\mathcal{A})
.
∎

4.
The Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
where the strong global dimension of
A
is finite.

Throughout this section, we consider
A
a finite dimensional
k
-algebra over a field
k
and with
s.gl.\mbox{dim}\,A=\nu<\infty
.

By
[
2
, Proposition 2.5]
, we know that
\rm{proj}\,A
is dualizing.

The aim of this section is to show how to build the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\rm{proj}\,A)
. By the result given in
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
, we shall give two ways of constructing this quiver. First applying the compression functor
\mathcal{F}_{m}
and secondly applying the same knitting technique used to obtain the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}(\rm{proj}\,A)
.

By Theorem
3.5
we know that if
A
is a finite dimensional
k
-algebra such that
s.gl.\mbox{dim}\,A<\infty
then
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}:\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
with
m\geq 2
is a Galois
G
-covering where
G
is the cyclic group generated by
[m]
.

By
[
3
, Proposition 3.3]
, if
u:X\rightarrow Y
is a morphism in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)

with
X
or
Y
indecomposable, then
u
is irreducible in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(u)
is irreducible
in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
.

Furthermore, by
[
3
, Proposition 3.4]
if
u:X\rightarrow Y
is a morphism in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
,
then
u
is a minimal left almost split morphism or a minimal right almost split morphism if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(u)
is a minimal left almost split morphism
or a minimal right almost split morphism in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
, respectively.

As a consequence of
[
3
, Theorem 3.7]
, we get the following result for
\mathcal{A}=\mbox{proj}\,A
and
m\geq 2
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A
be a finite dimensional
k
-algebra such that
s.gl.\emph{dim}\,A=\nu
.
The sequence
\eta
in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\emph{proj}\,A)
is almost split if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(\eta)
is an almost split sequence in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\emph{proj}}\,A)
for any
m\geq 2
.

Moreover, the additive function can be applied to build the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\rm{proj}\,A)
.

Proof.

By
[
3
, Theorem 3.7]
we have that
\eta
in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
is almost split sequence if and only if
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(\eta)
is an almost split sequence in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mbox{proj}}\,A)
for any
m\geq 2
.

On the other hand,
\mathcal{F}_{m}:\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mbox{proj}}\,A)
is a Galois
G
-covering, where
G
is the cyclic group generated by
[m]
.

In the category of complexes
\mathbf{C}^{b}({\rm proj}\,A)
the exact sequences are the ones of the form
\eta:X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\rightarrow}}Z
such that for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
the sequence
X^{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f^{i}}}{{\rightarrow}}Y^{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g^{i}}}{{\rightarrow}}Z^{i}
is exact and splits. Then
Y^{i}=X^{i}\oplus Z^{i}
for each
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Consider the almost split sequence
\mathcal{F}_{m}(\eta):\mathcal{F}_{m}(X)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{m}(Y)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{m}(Z)
in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}({\mbox{proj}}\,A)
. We have to prove that
\mathcal{F}_{m}(Y)^{i}=\mathcal{F}_{m}(X)^{i}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{m}(Z)^{i}
for each
i
.

Hence

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{F}_{m}(X)^{i}\oplus\mathcal{F}_{m}(Z)^{i}&=&(\oplus_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}X^{i+tm})\oplus(\oplus_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}Z^{i+tm})\\
&=&\oplus_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}(X^{i+tm}\oplus Z^{i+tm})\\
&=&\oplus_{t\in\mathbb{Z}}Y^{i+tm}\\
&=&\mathcal{F}_{m}(Y)^{i}\end{array}

proving the result.
∎

Consider
\mathcal{A}=\mbox{proj}\,A
with
A
a finite dimensional
k
-algebra with
s.gl.\mbox{dim}\,A=\nu
. Then
\mathcal{A}
is a Hom-finite Krull-Schmidt
k-
category verifying that each indecomposable complex in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
is a shift of an indecomposable complex in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,\nu+1]}(\mbox{proj}\,A).

By Theorem
3.8
we get the following result, which is fundamental to build the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\rm{proj}\,A)
for
m\geq 2
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
A
be a finite dimensional
k
-algebra such that
s.gl.\emph{dim}\,A=\nu
. Let
m\geq 2
. Consider
\mathcal{F}_{m}:\mathbf{C}^{b}(\emph{proj}\,A)\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\emph{proj}\,A)
the compression el functor. Then each almost split sequence in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\emph{proj}\,A)
is the image under
\mathcal{F}_{m}
of the shift of an almost split sequence in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,\nu+1]}(\emph{proj}\,A)
.

Now, we show how to build the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
, provided we know how to construct the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathbf{C}_{[1,\nu+1]}({\rm proj}\,A)
. For more details of how to construct the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\rm proj}\,A)
we refer the reader to
[
9
, Section 5]
. We observe that if
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\rm proj}\,A)
is of finite type then the technique given in
[
9
, Section 5]
allow us to construct the whole quiver.

We have two ways of constructing the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
.

By
[
7
, Theorem 4.1]
since
A
is a finite dimensional piecewise hereditary algebra then we know that all the almost split sequences in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
are the ones included in the subquiver
\Gamma(\Sigma)
between the section
\Sigma
and
\Sigma[1]
and containing both sections. By
\Sigma[1]
we mean a shift of
\Sigma
. More precisely, we can find that situation in the quivers of the categories
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\rm proj}\,A)
. In particular, in a category
\mathbf{C}_{[1,\nu+1]}({\rm proj}\,A)
, where
s.gl.\mbox{dim}\,A=\nu
.

For the first way of constructing the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
we can follow the next steps.

(1)

We construct the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathbf{C}_{[1,\nu+1]}({\rm proj}\,A)
.

(2)

We consider a section
\Sigma
and the subquiver
\Gamma(\Sigma)
in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,\nu+1]}({\rm proj}\,A)
.

(3)

Since
\mathcal{F}_{m}(X)=\mathcal{F}_{m}(X[m])
then it is enough to consider the almost split sequences in
\Gamma(\Sigma)
in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,\nu+1]}({\rm proj}\,A)
and the shifts of this almost split sequences in
\Gamma(\Sigma)[i]
for
i=1,\dots,m-1
in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
.

(4)

Applying the functor
\mathcal{F}_{m}
to the almost split sequences stated in step (3) we obtain the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
.

A second way of constructing the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
is as follows:

We may apply the knitting technique because of Theorem
4.1
. This technique has been used to build the Auslander-Reiten quiver of a module category and also for the categories of fixed size
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\rm proj}\,A)
, see
[
7
, Section 5]
.

First, we start this process with the same steps (1) and (2) stated above and we continue with steps (3)-(5) as we explain below.

(3)

We may apply the functor
\mathcal{F}_{m}
to the almost split sequences in
\Gamma(\Sigma)
.

(4)

We compute the radical of the projective-injective complexes in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
. We know that if
X
is the starting of an almost split sequence in
\Gamma(\Sigma)
with a direct summand projective-injective
Y
in its middle term then
\mathcal{F}_{m}(X)
is the starting of the almost split sequence in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
that has a projective-injective complex,
\mathcal{F}_{m}(Y)
, in its middle term. We call
\mathcal{F}_{m}(X)
the radical of
\mathcal{F}_{m}(Y)
and we denote it by
{\rm rad}\,\mathcal{F}_{m}(Y)
. Moreover,
\mathcal{F}_{m}(X[i])
is the starting of the almost split sequence in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
that has the projective-injective complex
\mathcal{F}_{m}(Y[i])
in its middle term, for
i=1,\dots,m-1
. In other words,
{\rm rad}\,\mathcal{F}_{m}(Y[i])=\mathcal{F}_{m}(X[i])
.

(5)

We continue building the quiver applying the knitting technique.

Bellow we present an example to show how we can apply both methods of constructing the Auslander-Reiten quiver.

5.
On sectional paths in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)

Throughout this section, we consider
A
a finite dimensional
k
-algebra over a field
k
.

The composition of irreducible morphisms on a sectional path in
{\rm mod}\,A
is non-zero, for any artin algebra
A
. This result was first proved by R. Bautista and S. Smalø in 1983, see
[
4
]
. Moreover, in
[
17
]
H. Igusa and G. Todorov also studied sectional paths in
{\rm mod}\,A
and proved that the composition of
m
irreducible morphisms on a sectional path does not belong to the power
m+1
of the radical of the category
\mbox{mod}\,A
and as a consequence does not vanish.

On the other hand, in
[
9
]
, the authors analyzed this problem for a category of complexes of fixed size. They proved that the composition of irreducible morphisms on a sectional path in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\rm proj}\,A)
can be zero and they studied conditions under which such compositions do not vanish. Precisely, the authors proved that if
H
is a finite dimensional hereditary algebra and
X_{0}\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow X_{m}
is a sectional path in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}(\mbox{proj}\,H)
then the composition of the irreducible morphisms in a sectional path does not vanish.

In this section, we shall prove that we have a similar result than the one mentioned above for the composition of irreducible morphisms on a sectional path in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
, whenever
A
is a finite dimensional
k
-algebra.

We recall the notion of sectional path.

Definition 5.1
.

A path
X_{0}\longrightarrow X_{1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow X_{m}
of irreducible morphisms between
indecomposable complexes in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\emph{proj}\,A)
is sectional if

\tau_{\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}}^{-1}X_{i}\not\simeq X_{i+2}
for every
i=0,\dots,m-2
.

S. Liu studied the behaviour of sectional paths in a left or a right Auslander-Reiten category,
\mathcal{A}
. Precisely, the mentioned author proved that any sectional path
X_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{1}}}{{\rightarrow}}X_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{2}}}{{\rightarrow}}\cdots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f_{m}}}{{\rightarrow}}X_{m}
in these categories verify that
f_{m}\cdots f_{1}\notin\Re^{m+1}_{\mathcal{A}}(X_{0},X_{m})
, see
[
19
, Lemma 2.7]
.

In
[
9
]
, the authors proved that
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\rm proj}\,H)
is a left Auslander-Reiten category if and only if
H
is hereditary. Therefore, as an immediate consequence they got that the composition of irreducible morphisms in a sectional path in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}({\rm proj}\,H)
is always non-zero.

Next, we shall see that
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,H)
is a left Auslander-Reiten category, whenever
H
is a finite dimensional hereditary algebra.

We recall the following definitions from
[
19
]
.

Definition 5.2
.

An object
X
in a Krull-Schmidt category
\mathcal{A}

is called pseudo-projective if there exists a right minimal almost split (sink)
monomorphism
M\rightarrow X
, and dually, it is called pseudo-injective
if there exists a left minimal almost split (source) epimorphism
X\rightarrow N
.

Definition 5.3
.

A Krull-Schmidt category
\mathcal{A}
is called
a left Auslander Reiten category if each indecomposable
object in
\mathcal{A}
is either pseudo-projective or the
end-term of an almost split sequence.
\mathcal{A}
is called
a right
Auslander-Reiten category if each indecomposable object in

\mathcal{A}
is either pseudo-injective or the starting term of
an almost split sequence.
\mathcal{A}
is called
an Auslander-Reiten
category if it is a left and a right Auslander-Reiten category.

Proposition 5.4
.

If
H
is a finite dimensional hereditary algebra over a field
k
, then
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\emph{proj}\,H)
is a left Auslander-Reiten category.

Conversely, if
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\emph{proj}\,H)
is a left Auslander-Reiten category for
m>2
, then
H
is a hereditary algebra.

Proof.

Let
H
be a hereditary algebra. Then s.gl.
\mbox{dim}\,H<\infty
. Consider
X
an indecomposable complex in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,H)
.

If
X
is not projective then by
[
11
, Proposition 2.6]

X
is the end-term of an almost split sequence in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,H)
. Otherwise, if
X
is projective since the compression functor
\mathcal{F}_{m}:\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,H)\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,H)
is dense because of Theorem

, then there is a complex

such that
\mathcal{F}_{m}(Z)=X
. Furthermore,
Z
is projective, because by
[
20
]
the functor
\mathcal{F}_{m}
sends projective complexes in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,H)
into projective complexes in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,H)
. Since
\rho:\mbox{rad}(Z)\rightarrow Z
is a left almost split (sink) morphism ending in
Z
then
\mathcal{F}_{m}(\rho):\mathcal{F}_{m}(\mbox{rad}(Z))\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{m}(Z)
is a left almost split (sink) morphism ending in
X
. Since

is hereditary then the above sink morphisms are monomorphisms, because for every

we have that the projective dimension of

is less than or equal to one.

Conversely. Assume that

if

On the other hand, there exists a non-zero morphism

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
H
be a finite dimensional hereditary algebra and
X_{0}\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow X_{r}
be a sectional path in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\emph{proj}\,H)
. If
f_{i}:X_{i-1}\rightarrow X_{i}
are irreducible morphisms for
i=1,\cdots,r

then
f_{r}\cdots f_{1}\notin\Re^{r+1}_{\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\emph{proj}\,H)}(X_{0},X_{r})
. In particular, the composition
f_{r}\cdots f_{1}
does not vanish.

Now, we show an example where we can see that there are sectional paths in

where the composition of the irreducible morphisms in it vanishes.

Lemma 5.7

Let

be a finite dimensional

\emph{dim}\,A<\infty
. If

with

an indecomposable projective-injective complex for some
i\in\{0,\cdots,r\}
then
i=0
or
i=r
.

Proof.

Consider
X_{0}\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow X_{r}
a sectional path in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
with
X_{i}
an indecomposable projective-injective complex for some
i\in\{0,\cdots,r\}
. Let
X_{j}=J_{i_{0}}(P)
be the projective-injective complex, where
P
is an indecomposable projective
A
-module. Since s.gl.
\mbox{dim}\,A<\infty
then the compression functor
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}:\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)\rightarrow\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
is dense. Moreover,
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}
preserves indecomposable complexes, projective complexes and irreducible morphisms. Then we have that
Z_{0}\rightarrow Z_{1}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow Z_{r}
is a path in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
, where
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(Z_{i})=X_{i}
for
i=0,\dots,r
. Moreover,
Z_{j}
is a projective-injective complex in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
. By Theorem
3.8
the above path is sectional in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
.

Now, by
[
5
, Proposition 8.7]
, we know that
\mbox{rad}(Z_{j})=R_{i_{0}}(P)
is an indecomposable complex in
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
, for
n
arbitrarily large. That is, we consider
n
such that the paths of irreducible morphisms
\mbox{rad}(Z_{j})\rightarrow Z_{j}\rightarrow\tau_{\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)}^{-1}Z_{j-1}
belongs to
\mathbf{C}_{[1,n]}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
, where
Z_{j}
is the projective-injective complex and
Z_{j-1}=\mbox{rad}(Z_{j})
. Therefore,
\mbox{rad}(Z_{j})
is also indecomposable in
\mathbf{C}^{b}((\mbox{proj}\,A))
and by Proposition
2.7
,
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(\mbox{rad}(Z_{j}))
is an indecomposable complex in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
.

On the other hand,
\mbox{rad}(Z_{j})\rightarrow Z_{j}
is irreducible in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
.
Therefore, applying the compression functor we have that
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(\mbox{rad}(Z_{j}))\rightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(Z_{j})
is irreducible in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
.

Moreover, by
[
9

, the irreducible morphism
\mbox{rad}(Z_{j})\rightarrow Z_{j}
in

Dually, we can see that

in

where

\mbox{rad}(Z_{j})\rightarrow Z_{j}
has the same property in
\mathbf{C}^{b}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
. Applying the compression functor we have that
{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(\mbox{rad}(Z_{j}))\rightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(Z_{j})
has dimension one in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)

{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(Z_{j})\rightarrow{\mathcal{F}}_{m}(\tau_{\mathbf{C}^{b}}^{-1}Z_{j-1})
in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
has dimension one.

Now, assume that
j\neq r
. If
X_{r-1}
is projective-injective then the path

is not sectional, since

∎

Proposition 5.8
.

Let
A
be a finite dimensional
k
-algebra with s.gl.
\emph{dim}\,A<\infty
. Let
X_{0}\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow X_{r}
be a non-zero sectional path in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\emph{proj}\,A)
with
m>1
. Then, one of the following statements hold.

(a)

The complexes
X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{r}
are not projective-injective.

(b)

The complexes
X_{0},X_{1},\cdots,X_{r-1}
are not projective-injective.

(c)

X_{0}
and
X_{r}
are projective-injective complexes and
X_{1},\cdots,X_{r-1}
are not projective-injective.

Proof.

Let
X_{0}\rightarrow X_{1}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow X_{r}
be a non-zero sectional path in
\mathbf{C}_{\equiv m}(\mbox{proj}\,A)
with
m>1
. If there are not projective-injective complexes in such a path then condition (a) and (b) hold.

Assume that there is an
i\in\{0,\dots,r\}
such that
X_{i}
is projective-injective. Furthermore, let
i\in\{0,\dots,m\}

be the least integer such that
X_{i}
is projective-injective. By Lemma
5.7
we have that
i=0
or
i=r
.

If
i=0
then
X_{0}
is projective-injective and for
j>0
we have the following two situations because of Lemma
5.7
: the path is of the form
X_{0}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow X_{r}
, where
X_{j}
are not projective-injective for
j=1,\dots,r-1
and
X_{r}
is projective-injective; or
of the form
X_{0}\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow X_{r}
with
X_{1},\dots,X_{r}
not projective-injective. Then conditions (c) and (a) hold, respectively. In case that
i=r
then
X_{0},\cdots,X_{r-1}
are not projective-injective and (b) holds.
∎

On the possible temperatures for flows on an AF-algebra
Klaus Thomsen

Abstract
. It is shown that there is a unital simple mono-tracial AF algebra A with the property that for every compact set K of real numbers containing zero there is a flow on A for which the set of inverse temperatures is exactly K.



1.
Introduction

The work
[
BEH
]
by Bratteli, Elliott and Herman demonstrated that the set of inverse temperatures for a one parameter group of automorphisms of a simple unital
C^{*}
-algebra can be an arbitrary closed set of real numbers. For general reasons the set must be closed, and their examples show that this is the only restriction. It follows also from
[
BEH
]
that the simplexes of equilibrium states can vary wildly with the temperature. However, in the quantum statistical models where the theory is used the underlying
C^{*}
-algebra is often an AF algebra or even a UHF algebra, and it was not before the work of Kishimoto, in
[
Ki2
]
and
[
Ki3
]
, that one could begin to suspect that a similar wild behaviour can occur for flows on simple AF algebras. Indeed, in
[
Ki2
]
Kishimoto constructed flows on simple unital AF algebras and UHF algebras with a non-trivial structure of KMS states and in
[
Ki3
]
he opened up for the possibility of having flows on simple unital AF algebras for which the set of possible inverse temperatures is not the entire real line. Recall that by results of Powers and Sakai,
[
PS
]
, any approximately inner flow on a unital
C^{*}
-algebra with a trace state must have
\beta
-KMS states for all
\beta
. In particular, any approximately inner flow on a unital AF algebra must have equilibrium states at every temperature. In addition, Powers and Sakai conjectured that any flow on a UHF algebra is approximately inner, which, if true, would imply that all flows on a UHF algebra have equilibrium states at every temperature. The AF version of the Powers-Sakai conjecture was refuted by Kishimoto in
[
Ki3
]
, and the actual conjecture by Matui and Sato in
[
MS
]
. The possibility of having other sets of inverse temperatures than
\mathbb{R}
for flows on a simple unital AF algebra and perhaps even on a UHF algebra has been actualized by these results. It can be shown that for the examples of Kishimoto in
[
Ki3
]
and Matui and Sato in
[
MS
]
, the flows that are shown not to be approximately inner have no
\beta
-KMS states when
\beta\neq 0
. (See Remark
3.2
below.) Hence, up to now, for all flows on AF algebras for which the set of possible inverse temperatures has been found, the set has either been
\mathbb{R}
or
\{0\}
. The main purpose of the present paper is to show that the ideas of Matui and Sato can be combined with the approach of Bratteli, Elliott and Herman to get an example of a simple unital AF algebra
A
such that for any compact set of real numbers containing zero there is a
2\pi
-periodic flow
\theta^{K}
on
A
with the set of inverse temperatures equal to
K
, and for any
\beta
in
K
the
\beta
-KMS state is unique. In fact, we will show that one of the AF algebras considered in
[
BEH
]
is such an algebra. In addition we combine the results with the authors recent work on the variation with
\beta
of the simplexes of
\beta
-KMS states for flows on UHF algebras.

The fundamental idea behind the new examples is the same as in
[
BEH
]
,
[
Ki3
]
and
[
MS
]
, namely to consider the dual action on well-chosen crossed products by the integers, and as in all these papers the success depends on results from the classification of simple
C^{*}
-algebras. Besides the classification of AF algebras, which was fundamental already in
[
BEH
]
, we depend here on the recent work by Castillejos, Evington, Tikuisis, White and Winter in
[
CETWW
]
.

2.
Statement of results

In this paper all
C^{*}
-algebras are assumed to be separable and all traces and weights on a
C^{*}
-algebra are required to be non-zero, densely defined and lower semi-continuous. Concerning weights and in particular KMS weights we shall use notation and results from Sections 1.1 and 1.3 in
[
KV
]
. Let
A
be a
C^{*}
-algebra and
\theta
a flow on
A
. Let
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. A
\beta
-KMS weight for
\theta
is a weight
\omega
on
A
such that
\omega\circ\theta_{t}=\omega
for all
t
, and

\omega(a^{*}a)\ =\ \omega\left(\theta_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(a)\theta_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(a)^{*}\right)\ \ \ \forall a\in D(\theta_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}})\ .

(2.1)

In particular, a
0
-KMS weight for
\theta
is a
\theta
-invariant trace. It was shown by Kustermans in Theorem 6.36 of
[
Ku
]
that this definition agrees with the one introduced by Combes in
[
C
]
.
It is because of the formulation given by (
2.1
), which was not available when
[
BEH
]
was written, that we are able to work with KMS weights throughout the present work. A bounded
\beta
-KMS weight is called a
\beta
-KMS functional and a
\beta
-KMS state when it is of norm
1
.

The elements of the additive group
\mathbb{Z}\left[t,t^{-1},(1-t)^{-1}\right]
of polynomials in
t,t^{-1}
and
(1-t)^{-1}
with integer coefficients will be considered as continuous functions on the open interval
]0,1[
. To simplify the notation we denote this group by
G_{0}
. Let
F
be a closed non-empty subset of
[0,1]
which neither contains
0
nor
1
. Set

G^{+}_{F}\ =\ \left\{f\in G_{0}:\ f(t)>0\ \forall t\in F\right\}\cup\{0\}\ .

With
G_{F}^{+}
as the semigroup of positive elements the group
G_{0}
is a simple dimension group by Corollary 2.2 in
[
BEH
]
. As in
[
BEH
]
we denote by
A_{F}
the unique AF algebra whose

-group with dimension scale is isomorphic to
(G_{0},G^{+}_{F})
,
[

]
. The main results of the paper are the following.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
F\subseteq[0,1]
and
F_{1}\subseteq[0,1]
be closed subsets of
[0,1]
such that

•

F\neq\emptyset
,

•

(F\cup F_{1})\cap\{0,1\}=\emptyset

\theta

The AF algebra

Let

Preparatory lemmas

The first lemma can be considered as an updated version of a part of the discussion in Remark 3.3 of
[
BEH
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
B
be a
C^{*}
-algebra and
\gamma\in\operatorname{Aut}(B)
an automorphism of
B
. Let
\widehat{\gamma}
be the dual action on
B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z}
considered as a
2\pi
-periodic flow. For
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
the restriction map
\omega\mapsto\omega|_{B}
is a bijection from the
\beta
-KMS weights for
\widehat{\gamma}
onto the traces
\tau
on
B
with the property that
\tau\circ\gamma=e^{-\beta}\tau
. The inverse is the map
\tau\mapsto\tau\circ P
, where
P:B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z}\to B
is the canonical conditional expectation.

Proof.

Let
p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\cdots
be an approximate unit in
B
from the Pedersen ideal
K(B)
of
B
with the additional property that
p_{k}^{2}\leq p^{2}_{k+1}
for all
k
, cf.
[
Pe
]
. Let
u
be the canonical unitary multiplier of
B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z}
such that
ubu^{*}=\gamma(b)
for
b\in B
, and let
\tau
be a trace on
B
with the property that
\tau\circ\gamma=e^{-\beta}\tau
. For
a,b\in B,\ n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
we have that

P(p_{k}au^{n}p_{k}^{2}bu^{m}p_{k})=P(p_{k}bu^{m}p_{k}^{2}\widehat{\gamma}_{i\beta}(au^{n})p_{k})=0

unless
m=-n
. Set
a^{\prime}=p_{k}a\gamma^{n}(p_{k}),\ b^{\prime}=p_{k}b\gamma^{-n}(p_{k})
. Then
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\in K(B)
, and by using Proposition 5.5.2 in
[
Pe
]
we find that

\displaystyle\tau\circ P(p_{k}bu^{-n}p_{k}\widehat{\gamma}_{i\beta}(p_{k}au^{n}p_{k}))=e^{-n\beta}\tau(p_{k}bu^{-n}p_{k}^{2}au^{n}p_{k})

\displaystyle=e^{-n\beta}\tau(b^{\prime}u^{-n}a^{\prime}u^{n})=\tau(\gamma^{n}(b^{\prime})a^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\tau(a^{\prime}\gamma^{n}(b^{\prime}))=\tau\circ P(p_{k}au^{n}p_{k}^{2}bu^{-n}p_{k})\ ,

showing that
\tau\circ P
is a
\beta
-KMS functional on
p_{k}(B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z})p_{k}
for the restriction of
\widehat{\gamma}
. We shall use repeatedly that when
b\geq 0
in
B
we have that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\tau(p_{k}bp_{k})=\tau(b)\ ,

(3.1)

which follows from the lower semi-continuity of
\tau
since
\tau(p_{k}bp_{k})=\tau(\sqrt{b}p_{k}^{2}\sqrt{b})
and
\sqrt{b}p_{k}^{2}\sqrt{b}
increases to
b
as
k\to\infty
. Let
x\in D(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}})
. Then

\displaystyle\tau\circ P\left(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*}\right)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\tau\circ P\left(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)p_{k}^{2}\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{l\to\infty}\tau\left(p_{l}P\left(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)p_{k}^{2}\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*}\right)p_{l}\right)\ .

We have shown above that
\tau\circ P
is a
\beta
-KMS functional on
p_{l}(B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z})p_{l}
and when
l\geq k
this gives

\displaystyle\tau\left(p_{l}P\left(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)p_{k}^{2}\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*}\right)p_{l}\right)=\tau\circ P\left(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(p_{l}xp_{k})\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(p_{l}xp_{k})^{*}\right)

\displaystyle=\ \tau\circ P((p_{l}xp_{k})^{*}p_{l}xp_{k})=\tau\circ P(p_{k}x^{*}p_{l}^{2}xp_{k})\leq\tau\left(p_{k}P(x^{*}x)p_{k}\right)\ .

By using (
3.1
) we conclude that

\tau\circ P\left(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*}\right)\ \leq\ \tau\circ P(x^{*}x)\ .

Similarly,

\displaystyle\tau\circ P(x^{*}x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\tau\circ P(x^{*}p_{k}^{2}x)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\lim_{l\to\infty}\tau\circ P(p_{l}x^{*}p_{k}^{2}xp_{l})\ .

When
l\geq k
,

\displaystyle\tau\circ P(p_{l}x^{*}p_{k}^{2}xp_{l})=\tau\circ P(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(p_{k}xp_{l})\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(p_{k}xp_{l})^{*})

\displaystyle=\tau\circ P(p_{k}\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)p_{l}^{2}\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*}p_{k}\ \leq\tau\left(p_{k}P(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*})p_{k}\right),

and we find therefore that

\displaystyle\tau\circ P(x^{*}x)\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\tau\left(p_{k}P(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*})p_{k}\right)=\tau\circ P(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*})\ .

We conclude therefore first that
\tau\circ P(x^{*}x)=\tau\circ P(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(x)^{*})
, and then that
\tau\circ P
is a
\beta
-KMS weight.

Let
\omega
be a
\beta
-KMS weight for
\widehat{\gamma}
. Then
\omega(b^{*}b)=\omega(bb^{*})
for all
b\in B
because
\widehat{\gamma}_{-i\frac{\beta}{2}}(b)=b
. Using Riemann sum approximations to the integral

P(a)=(2\pi)^{-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\widehat{\gamma}_{t}(a)\ \mathrm{d}t\ ,

it follows from the
\widehat{\gamma}
-invariance and lower semi-continuity of
\omega
that

\omega\circ P(a)\leq\omega(a)

(3.2)

when
a\geq 0
in
B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z}
. In particular,
\omega|_{B}
is densely defined since
\omega
is, and we conclude that
\tau=\omega|_{B}
is a trace on
B
. Let
a\geq 0
. Then

\omega\circ\gamma(a)=\omega(uau^{*})=e^{-\beta}\omega\left(\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(u\sqrt{a})\widehat{\gamma}_{-\frac{i\beta}{2}}(u\sqrt{a})^{*}\right)=e^{-\beta}\omega(a)\ .

It follows that
\tau\circ\gamma=e^{-\beta}\tau
. It remains now only to show that
\omega=\omega\circ P
. Note that because
\omega\circ\widehat{\gamma}_{t}=\omega
we find for all
b\in B,\ n\in\mathbb{Z}
, that

\omega(p_{k}bu^{n}p_{k})=\begin{cases}0,&\ n\neq 0\\
\omega(p_{k}bp_{k}),&\ n=0\end{cases}\ =\ \omega(p_{k}P(bu^{n})p_{k})\ ,

implying that
\omega(p_{k}\ \cdot\ p_{k})=\omega(p_{k}P(\ \cdot\ )p_{k})
. Let
a\in B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z}
,
a\geq 0
. Since
\omega|_{B}
is a trace we can use (
3.1
) to conclude that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\omega(p_{k}ap_{k})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\omega(p_{k}P(a)p_{k})=\ \omega(P(a))\ .

Since

the lower semi-continuity of

\omega(a)\leq\omega(P(a))
. Combined with (

) this yields the desired conclusion that
\omega\circ P=\omega
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
B
be a
C^{*}
-algebra and
\gamma\in\operatorname{Aut}(B)
an automorphism of
B
. The restriction map
\omega\mapsto\omega|_{B}
is a bijection from the
\widehat{\gamma}
-invariant traces
\omega
on
B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z}
onto the
\gamma
-invariant traces on
B
. The inverse is the map
\tau\mapsto\tau\circ P
, where
P:B\rtimes_{\gamma}\mathbb{Z}\to B
is the canonical conditional expectation.

We remark that in general

can have traces that are not invariant under the dual action

and hence are not determined by their restriction to

; also in cases where

is UHF and

[

]

]

Recall that a

-algebra

is stable when

, where
\mathbb{K}
is the
C^{*}
-algebra of compact operators on an infinite dimensional separable Hilbert space.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
B
be a stable simple AF-algebra not isomorphic to
\mathbb{K}
, and let
\gamma\in\operatorname{Aut}(B)
be an automorphism of
B
. There is an automorphism
\gamma^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Aut}(B)
such that

a)

\gamma^{\prime}_{*}=\gamma_{*}
on
K_{0}(B)
,

b)

the restriction map

\mu\ \mapsto\ \mu|_{B}

is a bijection from traces
\mu
on
B\rtimes_{\gamma^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}
onto the
\gamma^{\prime}
-invariant traces on
B
,

c)

B\rtimes_{\gamma^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}
is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable; i.e
(B\rtimes_{\gamma^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z})\otimes\mathcal{Z}\simeq B\rtimes_{\gamma^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}
where
\mathcal{Z}
denotes the Jiang-Su algebra,
[
JS
]
, and

B\rtimes_{\gamma^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}

Proof.

The first step is to show that there is an isomorphism
\phi:B\to B\otimes\mathcal{Z}
such that
\phi_{*}={(\operatorname{id}_{B}\otimes 1_{\mathcal{Z}})}_{*}
. Since
\mathcal{Z}
is strongly self-absorbing in the sense of Toms and Winter,
[
TW
]
, it suffices for this to show that
B
is
\mathcal{Z}
-stable. For a unital simple AF algebra this follows from Theorem A and Corollary C in
[
CETWW
]
, and
\mathcal{Z}
-stability is invariant under stable isomorphism by Corollary 3.2 in
[
TW
]
. It follows from
[
B
]
that
B
is stably isomorphic to a unital AF algebra, and we deduce in this way the existence of
\phi
.

For the second step we use
[
Sa
]
to obtain an automorphism
\theta
of
\mathcal{Z}
with the weak Rohlin property; that is, with the property that for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
there is a sequence
\{f_{n}\}
in
\mathcal{Z}
such that

•

0\leq f_{n}\leq 1_{\mathcal{Z}}
for all
n
,

•

\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}a-af_{n}=0
for all
a\in\mathcal{Z}
,

•

\lim_{n\to\infty}\theta^{j}(f_{n})f_{n}=0
for
j=1,2,3,\cdots,k
, and

•

\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau\left(1_{\mathcal{Z}}-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\theta^{j}(f_{n})\right)=0
,

where
\tau
is the trace state of

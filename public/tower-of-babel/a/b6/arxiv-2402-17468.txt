1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, all varieties are projective over the complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
.

1.1.
Background

Classifying algebraic varieties is a central problem in algebraic geometry. One way to attack this problem, especially for varieties of general type, is to study the relation among their birational invariants first, and then to obtain an explicit description using these numerical information. This is often refered as the geography of algebraic varieties in the literature.

It is via this approach that many classification results for varieties of general type have been proved. As a typical example, it was proved by Noether
[
27
]
that every smooth surface
S
of general type satisfies the following optimal inequality:

(1.1)

{\mathrm{vol}}(S)\geq 2p_{g}(S)-4,

which is now called the Noether inequality. In his celebrated paper
[
14
]
, Horikawa completely classified surfaces of general type which attain the equality in (
1.1
). The Noether inequality problem in dimension three also has attracted lots of attentions (e.g.
[
21
,
8
,
2
,
6
,
9
]
). Recently, J. Chen, M. Chen and C. Jiang
[
7
]
proved that every smooth
3
-fold
X
of general type, either having a Gorenstein minimal model or with
p_{g}(X)\geq 11
, satisfies the following optimal Noether inequality:

(1.2)

{\mathrm{vol}}(X)\geq\frac{4}{3}p_{g}(X)-\frac{10}{3}.

Parallel to Horikawa’s work
[
14
]
, they asked [Question 1.5, loc. cit] whether
3
-folds attaining the equality can be classified, and this question has been solved by the authors in
[
17
]
.

Motivated by the explicit classification of irregular varieties of general type, it is a fundamental question to what extent does the irregularity affect the distribution of birational invariants (see
[
30
, Introduction]
for a detailed description). In fact, if a smooth surface
S
of general type satisfies the Noether equality in (
1.1
), then
q(S)=0

[
1
, Theorem 10]
. Thus there must be a sharper Noether inequality for irregular surfaces of general type. This problem was studied extensively by Bombieri
[
1
, §10]
and later solved by Debarre
[
11
]
who proved that every irregular surface
S
of general type satisfies the following optimal Noether inequality:

(1.3)

{\mathrm{vol}}(S)\geq 2p_{g}(S).

Moreover, Debarre proved that if the equality holds, then
1\leq q(S)\leq 4
.
Based on the results of Horikawa, Debarre, Catanese, Ciliberto, Mendes Lopes and Pardini
[
15
,
11
,
3
,
10
]
, a complete classification of irregular surfaces of general type satisfying the equality in (
1.3
) has been established. We refer to
[
10
, §1]
for the history and details regarding this result.

Very similar to the surface case, it is proved recently that if a smooth
3
-fold
X
of general type with
p_{g}(X)\geq 11
satisfies the Noether equality in (
1.2
), then
q(X)=0

[
17
]
. Thus it is natural to ask:

Question
.

What is the optimal Noether inequality for irregular
3
-folds of general type? Once the desired inequality is obtained, can one classify the
3
-folds attaining the equality?

However, unlike the Noether inequality (
1.2
) which was known already two decades ago for smooth canonically polarized
3
-folds
[
8
]
, this question is unknown even for irregular
3
-folds with smooth canonical models.

1.2.
Main results

The main purpose of this paper is to give an answer to the above question for “almost all” irregular
3
-folds of general type. Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth irregular
3
-fold of general type. Then we have the following optimal Noether inequality:

(1.4)

{\mathrm{vol}}(X)\geq\frac{4}{3}p_{g}(X),

provided one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

X
has a Gorenstein minimal model;

(2)

q(X)=1
and
p_{g}(X)\geq 16
;

(3)

q(X)\geq 2
.

Moreover, if
X
satisfies the equality in (
1.4
) as well as any of the above three conditions (1)–(3), then
1\leq q(X)\leq 2
.

Note that minimal models of
3
-folds
X
with
{\mathrm{vol}}(X)<\frac{4}{3}p_{g}(X)\leq 20
form a bounded family
[
24
, Corollary 2]
. Thus Theorem
1.1
shows that the Noether inequality (
1.4
) holds for all minimal irregular
3
-folds of general type except possibly finitely many families.

We would like to remark on the optimality of the Noether inequality (
1.4
). Indeed, the authors in
[
18
]
described the canonical model of all smooth irregular
3
-folds
X
of general type with
{\mathrm{vol}}(X)=\frac{4}{3}\chi(\omega_{X})
and showed that
q(X)=1
and
h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
for such
X
. Thus we deduce that

{\mathrm{vol}}(X)=\frac{4}{3}\left(p_{g}(X)-h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})+q(X)-1\right)=\frac{4}{3}p_{g}(X)

for such
X
. A notable feature is that the general Albanese fibre of such
X
is a
(1,2)
-surface. Here and throughout the paper, a
(1,2)
-surface always denotes a smooth surface
S
with
{\mathrm{vol}}(S)=1
and
p_{g}(S)=2
.

Our second theorem shows that “almost all” irregular
3
-folds attaining the equality in (
1.4
) are exactly of the above form.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth irregular
3
-fold of general type with
{\mathrm{vol}}(X)=\frac{4}{3}p_{g}(X)
and
p_{g}(X)\geq 16
. Then
{\mathrm{vol}}(X)=\frac{4}{3}\chi(\omega_{X})
. Thus the canonical model of
X
can be explicitly described as in
[
18
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Interestingly, a similar phenomenon actually holds also in the surface case. Namely, let
S
be a smooth irregular surface of general type with
p_{g}(S)\geq 5
. Then
{\mathrm{vol}}(S)=2p_{g}(S)
if and only if
{\mathrm{vol}}(S)=2\chi(\omega_{S})

[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Results in
[
21
,
7
,
17
]
suggest that a
3
-fold
X
of general type with small
{{\mathrm{vol}}(X)}/{p_{g}(X)}

admits a pencil of
(1,2)
-surfaces.
Thus there should be a sharper Noether inequality for irregular
3
-folds of general type without pencils of
(1,2)
-surfaces. Our third theorem provides a strong evidence that the desired inequality may be just like Debarre’s inequality (
1.3
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth
3
-fold of general type with
q(X)\geq 2
whose general Albanese fibre is not a
(1,2)
-surface. Then we have the following optimal Noether inequality:

(1.5)

{\mathrm{vol}}(X)\geq 2p_{g}(X),

provided one of the following conditions holds:

(1)

X
has a Gorenstein minimal model;

(2)

p_{g}(X)\neq 2
or
3
.

The optimality of (
1.5
) can be seen from the following example. Take a minimal surface
S
of general type with
K_{S}^{2}=2
,
p_{g}(S)=3
and
q(S)=0
. Such a surface
S
exists and has been classified by Horikawa
[
14
]
. Take a smooth curve
C
of genus two. Let
X=S\times C
be the product
3
-fold. Then it is easy to compute that
q(X)=2
,
p_{g}(X)=6
and
{\mathrm{vol}}(X)=K_{X}^{3}=12
. In particular, we have
{\mathrm{vol}}(X)=2p_{g}(X)
.

1.3.
Sketch of the proof

The whole proof of the Noether inequality is subject to the irregularity, and is divided into two main cases. Since the canonical volume and the geometric genus are birational invariants, we may assume that
X
is a minimal irregular
3
-fold of general type.

Case 1
:
q(X)\geq 2
. In this case, when
{\rm alb}\dim X=1
, we apply the Noether-Severi inequality and the slope inequality in
[
16
,
18
]
to deduce the inequalities (
1.4
) and (
1.5
). When
{\rm alb}\dim X\geq 2
, up to a birational modification, we choose a smooth member
S\in{\mathrm{Mov}}|K_{X}|
and deduce a relation of the form
{\mathrm{vol}}(X)\geq\alpha{\mathrm{vol}}(S)
from Kawamata’s extension theorem (here
\alpha
depends on the canonical image of
X
). Since
S
is an irregular surface of general type, we apply the Severi inequality in
[
29
]
or the slope inequality in
[
34
]
to get another relation of the form
{\mathrm{vol}}(S)\geq\beta\chi(\omega_{S})
(here
\beta
depends on the Albanese dimension of
S
). Moreover, by a detailed analysis on the canonical image of
X
, we also prove that
p_{g}(X)
is bounded from above by
\chi(\omega_{S})
and
\chi(\omega_{X})
. Combining the above three inequalities with the Severi inequality in
[
35
]
, the inequality (
1.5
) in this case can be proved.

Case 2
:
q(X)=1
. In this case, the Albanese map of
X
induces a fibration
f:X\to B
over a genus one curve
B
. Let
F
be a general fibre of
f
. When
F
is not a
(1,2)
-surface, we may slightly modify the proof in Case 1 to get the inequality (
1.4
). However, dealing with the case when
F
is a
(1,2)
-surface and
h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})>0
requires new insight, mainly because the Noether-Severi inequality in
[
18
]
only provides a weaker Noether inequality. Our new input here is to use the positivity of the direct image sheaf
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(mK_{X})
to get more positivity of
K_{X}
. More precisely, we prove that
K_{X}-aF
is nef for some
a>\frac{4}{3}
when
p_{g}(X)\geq 16
. If
X
is Gorenstein, we prove a weaker result that
K_{X}-\frac{3}{4}F
is nef when
p_{g}(X)\geq 4
. These two positivity results plus a known estimate in
[
18
]
help us establish the inequality (
1.4
) in the corresponding cases.

As has been pointed out by Debarre
[
11
, §6]
, Bombieri
[
1
, Lemma 14]
proved that
{\mathrm{vol}}(S)\geq 2\chi(\omega_{S})
holds for every irregular surface
S
of general type. When
q(S)=1
, we have
p_{g}(S)=\chi(\omega_{S})
and Debarre’s inequality (
1.3
) follows directly from Bombieri’s result. As a dramatic difference, for an irregular
3
-fold
X
with
q(X)=1
, we only have
p_{g}(X)=\chi(\omega_{X})+h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
. Thus the above implication in dimension two fails in dimension three once
h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})>0
, which is the main difficulty in this paper.

1.4.
Notation and conventions

In this paper, we adopt the following notation and conventions.

Varieties and divisors

Let
V
be a normal variety of dimension
d
. We say that
V
is
minimal
, if
V
has at worst
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminal singularities and the canonical divisor
K_{V}
is nef. The
geometric genus

p_{g}(V)
of
V
is defined as

p_{g}(V):=h^{0}(V,K_{V}).

For a Weil divisor
L
on
V
, the
volume

{\mathrm{vol}}(L)
of
L
is defined as

{\mathrm{vol}}(L):=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{h^{0}(V,nL)}{n^{d}/d!}.

The volume
{\mathrm{vol}}(K_{V})
is called the
canonical volume
of
V
, and is denoted by
{\mathrm{vol}}(V)
. If
V
is birational to a smooth variety
V^{\prime}
with
{\mathrm{vol}}(V^{\prime})>0
, we say that
V
is of general type. Note that if
V
is minimal, then
{\mathrm{vol}}(V)=K_{V}^{d}
.

For a linear system
\Lambda
on
V
, we denote by
{\mathrm{Mov}}\Lambda
and
{\mathrm{Bs}}\Lambda
the movable part and the base locus of
\Lambda
, respectively. If
p_{g}(V)\geq 2
, the rational map

\phi_{K_{V}}:V\dasharrow\mathbb{P}^{p_{g}(V)-1}

induced by the linear system
|K_{V}|
is called the
canonical map
of
V
, and
\phi_{K_{V}}(V)
is called the
canonical image
.

Irregular varieties

Let
V
be a normal variety with at worst rational singularities. We say that
V
is
irregular
, if
q(V):=h^{1}(V,\mathcal{O}_{V})>0
. Note that
V
has a well-defined
Albanese map

a:V\to\mathrm{Alb}(V),

where
A:=\mathrm{Alb}(V)
is an abelian variety referred as the Albanese variety of
V
. The number
\dim a(V)
, denoted by
{\rm alb}\dim V
, is called the
Albanese dimension
of
V
. Let

V\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\rightarrow}}W\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\rightarrow}}A

be the Stein factorization of
a
. Then
f
is called the
Albanese fibration
of
V
, and a fibre of
f
is called an
Albanese fibre
. If
\phi:V^{\prime}\to V
is a resolution of singularities of
V
and
a^{\prime}:V^{\prime}\to\mathrm{Alb}(V^{\prime})
is the Albanese map of
V^{\prime}
, then
\mathrm{Alb}(V^{\prime})\cong\mathrm{Alb}(V)
and
a^{\prime}=a\circ\phi
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we list some preliminary results used in the later proof.

2.1.
Linear systems on
3
-folds

Let
V
be a normal
3
-fold. Let
L
be a Cartier divisor on
V
with
h^{0}(V,L)\geq 2
. Let

\phi_{L}:V\dasharrow\mathbb{P}^{h^{0}(V,L)-1}

be the rational map induced by the linear system
|L|
. Denote
\Sigma_{L}=\phi_{L}(V)
.

We first recall a lemma due to Reid.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
31
, Corollaries (i)]

Let
W\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
be an irreducible and non-degenerate variety of dimension
w
. Then

h^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}^{N},\mathcal{I}_{W}(2)\right)\leq\binom{N-w+2}{2}-\min\{\deg W,2(N-w)+1\}.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\dim\Sigma_{L}=3
, then

\displaystyle h^{0}(V,2L)

\displaystyle\geq 3h^{0}(V,L)-3+\min\{\deg\Sigma_{L},2h^{0}(V,L)-7\}\geq 4h^{0}(V,L)-6.

If
\dim\Sigma_{L}=2
, then

\displaystyle h^{0}(V,2L)\geq 2h^{0}(V,L)-1+\min\{\deg\Sigma_{L},2h^{0}(V,L)-5\}\geq 3h^{0}(V,L)-3.

Proof.

Up to a birational modification, we may assume that
V
is smooth and
|L|
is base point free. Write
N=h^{0}(V,L)-1
. By the assumption,
\Sigma_{L}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{N}
is non-degenerate.

If
\dim\Sigma_{L}=3
, then
h^{0}(V,L)\geq 4
and
\deg\Sigma_{L}\geq N-2=h^{0}(V,L)-3
. By Lemma
2.1
, we deduce that

\displaystyle h^{0}\left(\Sigma_{L},\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{L}}(2)\right)

\displaystyle\geq h^{0}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{N}}(2)\right)-h^{0}\left(\mathbb{P}^{N},\mathcal{I}_{\Sigma_{L}}(2)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\binom{N+2}{2}-\binom{N-1}{2}+\min\{\deg\Sigma_{L},2N-5\}

\displaystyle=3h^{0}(V,L)-3+\min\{\deg\Sigma_{L},2h^{0}(V,L)-7\}

\displaystyle\geq 4h^{0}(V,L)-6.

If
\dim\Sigma_{L}=2
, then
h^{0}(V,L)\geq 3
and
\deg\Sigma_{L}\geq N-1=h^{0}(V,L)-2
. By a similar argument via Lemma
2.1
, we deduce that

h^{0}\left(\Sigma_{L},\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{L}}(2)\right)\geq 2h^{0}(V,L)-1+\min\{\deg\Sigma_{L},2h^{0}(V,L)-5\}\geq 3h^{0}(V,L)-3.

Note that in either case, we have
h^{0}(V,2L)\geq h^{0}\left(\Sigma_{L},\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{L}}(2)\right)
. Thus the proof is completed.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
V
is minimal of general type and
\pi:V^{\prime}\to V
be a resolution of singularities of
V
. Let
S
be a semi-ample divisor on
V^{\prime}
which itself is a smooth surface of general type. If
\lambda\pi^{*}K_{V}-S
is
\mathbb{Q}
-linear equivalent to an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor for some
\lambda>0
, then

\lambda(1+\lambda)^{2}K_{V}^{3}\geq K_{S_{0}}^{2},

where
\sigma:S\to S_{0}
is the contraction onto the minimal model
S_{0}
of
S
.

Proof.

Since
K_{V}
is semi-ample, by
[
7
, Corollary 2.3]
which was deduced from Kawamata’s extension theorem, the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
(1+\lambda)\pi^{*}K_{V}|_{S}-\sigma^{*}K_{S_{0}}
is pseudo-effective. Thus

\lambda(1+\lambda)^{2}K_{V}^{3}\geq(1+\lambda)^{2}\left((\pi^{*}K_{V})^{2}\cdot S\right)\geq(\sigma^{*}K_{S_{0}})^{2}=K_{S_{0}}^{2}.

The proof is completed.
∎

2.2.
Positivity of divisors on fibred varieties

Let

f:V\to B

be a fibration from an
n
-dimensional normal variety
V
onto a smooth curve
B
. Denote by
F
a general fibre of
f
. Let
L
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier Weil divisor on
V
such that
L
is
f
-nef and that
L|_{F}
is Cartier. Suppose that
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V}(L)
is nonzero. Let

0=\mathcal{E}_{0}\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathcal{E}_{l}=f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V}(L)

be the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V}(L)
. For any
1\leq i\leq l
, set

r_{i}={\mathrm{rank}\,}\mathcal{E}_{i},\quad\mu_{i}=\frac{\deg(\mathcal{E}_{i}/\mathcal{E}_{i-1})}{{\mathrm{rank}\,}(\mathcal{E}_{i}/\mathcal{E}_{i-1})}.

Then we have
\mu_{1}>\mu_{2}>\cdots>\mu_{l}
.

Consider the evaluation morphism

{\mathrm{ev}}:f^{*}f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V}(L)\to\mathcal{O}_{V}(L).

It induces a rational map

\psi:V\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}_{B}(f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V}(L)).

Note that
\psi
is actually defined by the linear system
|L+tF|
for a sufficiently large
t\in\mathbb{N}
. Denote
\Delta=\mathrm{Sing}(V)\cup\mathrm{Supp}(\mathcal{O}_{V}(L)/\mathrm{Im}({\mathrm{ev}}))
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
C
be an integral curve on
V
not contained in the
f
-horizontal part of
\Delta
. Then

\left((L-\mu_{l}F)\cdot C\right)\geq 0.

Proof.

Suppose that
C
is
f
-vertical. Since
L
is
f
-nef, we have

\left((L-\mu_{l}F)\cdot C\right)=(L\cdot C)\geq 0.

Suppose that
C
is
f
-horizontal now. Choose a birational modification
\pi:V^{\prime}\to V
such that
V^{\prime}
is smooth,
\pi
is isomorphic over
V\backslash\Delta
, and
|M|={\mathrm{Mov}}|{\left\lfloor\pi^{*}(L+tF)\right\rfloor}|
is base point free for a sufficiently large
t\in\mathbb{N}
.
Write

\pi^{*}(L+tF)=M+Z.

Then we have
\pi(Z)\subseteq\Delta
. Since
C
is not contained in
\Delta
, the strict transform
C^{\prime}
of
C
under
\pi
is not contained in
{\mathrm{Supp}}(Z)
. Now by
[
25
, Corollary 3.5]
, the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
\pi^{*}(L-\mu_{l}F)-Z
is nef. It follows that

\left((L-\mu_{l}F)\cdot C\right)=\left(\pi^{*}(L-\mu_{l}F)\cdot C^{\prime}\right)\geq(Z\cdot C^{\prime})\geq 0.

Thus the proof is completed.
∎

2.3.
Vector bundles on elliptic curves

Let
E
be an elliptic curve. For a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
E
, we may write

\mathcal{E}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{E}_{i},

where each
\mathcal{E}_{i}
is indecomposable, i.e., not a direct sum of subbundles. By
[
32
, Page 3, Fact]
, every
\mathcal{E}_{i}
is semistable. Thus it is easy to obtain the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
\mathcal{E}
. Indeed, we may further assume that

\mu(\mathcal{E}_{1})=\cdots=\mu(\mathcal{E}_{i_{1}})>\cdots>\mu(\mathcal{E}_{i_{k-1}+1})=\cdots=\mu(\mathcal{E}_{i_{k}}),

where
\mu(\mathcal{E}_{i})=\deg\mathcal{E}_{i}/{\mathrm{rank}\,}\mathcal{E}_{i}
for each
i
and
i_{k}=n
. Then the Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
\mathcal{E}
is just

0=\mathcal{F}_{0}\subsetneq\mathcal{F}_{1}\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathcal{F}_{k}=\mathcal{E},

where
\mathcal{F}_{l}=\mathcal{E}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathcal{E}_{i_{l}}
for any
1\leq l\leq k
.

Definition 2.5
.

For a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
E
as above, the minimal (resp. maximal) slope
\mu_{\min}(\mathcal{E})
(resp.
\mu_{\max}(\mathcal{E})
) of
\mathcal{E}
is defined to be
\min\{\mu(\mathcal{E}_{i})|1\leq i\leq n\}
(resp.
\max\{\mu(\mathcal{E}_{i})|1\leq i\leq n\}
).

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{V}_{1}
and
\mathcal{V}_{2}
be two vector bundles on
E
. Then

\mu_{\min}(\mathcal{V}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{V}_{2})=\mu_{\min}(\mathcal{V}_{1})+\mu_{\min}(\mathcal{V}_{2}).

Proof.

See
[
19
, Page 29]
.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{V}_{1}
and
\mathcal{V}_{2}
be two vector bundles on
E
such that
\mathcal{V}_{2}
is indecomposable. If
{\mathrm{Hom}}(\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{2})\neq 0
, then

\mu(\mathcal{V}_{2})\geq\mu_{\min}(\mathcal{V}_{1}).

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{V}_{2}
is indecomposable, it is semistable, and
\mu_{\max}(\mathcal{V}_{2})=\mu(\mathcal{V}_{2})
. Thus this lemma follows from
[
19
, Lemma 1.3.3]
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

If there is a generically surjective morphism
\phi:\mathcal{V}_{1}\to\mathcal{V}_{2}
between vector bundles
\mathcal{V}_{1}
and
\mathcal{V}_{2}
on
E
, then

\mu_{\min}(\mathcal{V}_{2})\geq\mu_{\min}(\mathcal{V}_{1}).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a direct summand of
\mathcal{V}_{2}
with
\mu(\mathcal{F})=\mu_{\min}(\mathcal{V}_{2})
. Since
\phi
is generically surjective, the composition
\mathcal{V}_{1}\to\mathcal{F}
of
\phi
and the natural projection
\mathcal{V}_{2}\to\mathcal{F}
is nonzero. Then this lemma follows from
[
19
, Lemma 1.3.3]
.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

[
5
, Lemma 4.3]

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a vector bundle on
E
. Then
\mathcal{F}
is ample if and only if
H^{1}(E,\mathcal{F}\otimes P)=0
for all
P\in{\mathrm{Pic}}^{0}(E)
.

Corollary 2.10
.

Let
f:V\to E
be a fibration from a smooth variety
V
to
E
, and let
L
be a nef and big
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor with its fractional part being simple normal crossing. Then the sheaf
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V}(K_{V}+{\left\lceil L\right\rceil})
is ample.

Proof.

By the Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing theorem, for any
P\in{\mathrm{Pic}}^{0}(E)
, we have
h^{1}\left(V,\mathcal{O}_{V}\left(K_{V}+{\left\lceil L\right\rceil}\right)\otimes f^{*}P\right)=0
.
By the Leray spectral sequence, we deduce that
h^{1}(E,f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V}(K_{V}+{\left\lceil L\right\rceil})\otimes P)=0
. By Lemma
2.9
,
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{V}(K_{V}+{\left\lceil L\right\rceil})
is ample.
∎

3.
Irregular
3
-folds of general type with
q\geq 2

Throughout this section, we assume that
X
is a minimal
3
-fold of general type with
q(X)\geq 2
. The main result in this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be as above. Then

K_{X}^{3}\geq\frac{4}{3}p_{g}(X),

and the equality holds only when
{\rm alb}\dim X=2
and
q(X)=2
. Suppose furthermore that the general Albanese fibre of
X
is not a
(1,2)
-surface. Then

K_{X}^{3}\geq 2p_{g}(X)

if

is Gorenstein or
p_{g}(X)\neq 2,3
,

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof.

3.1.
The case when
{\rm alb}\dim X=1

Suppose first that
{\rm alb}\dim X=1
. Let

f:X\to B

be the Albanese fibration of
X
, where
B
is a smooth curve of genus
g(B)=q(X)\geq 2
. Let
F
be a general fibre of
f
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that
{\rm alb}\dim X=1
. Then
K^{3}_{X}>\frac{4}{3}p_{g}(X)
if
F
is a
(1,2)
-surface, and
K_{X}^{3}\geq 2p_{g}(X)
otherwise. In particular, Theorem
3.1
holds.

Proof.

We first assume that
F
is a
(1,2)
-surface. If
h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\leq 1
, by
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
, we have

K_{X}^{3}>\frac{4}{3}\chi(\omega_{X})=\frac{4}{3}p_{g}(X)+\frac{4}{3}\left(q(X)-h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})-1\right)\geq\frac{4}{3}p_{g}(X).

Now suppose that
h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\geq 2
. Since
q(F)=0
and
R^{1}f_{*}\omega_{X}
is torsion free by
[
22
, Theorem 2.1]
, we know that
R^{1}f_{*}\omega_{X}=0
. By the Leray spectral sequence, we have
h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=h^{1}(X,\omega_{X})=h^{1}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X})
. Since
f_{*}\omega_{X/B}
is of rank two and
h^{1}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X})\geq 2
, Fujita’s decomposition
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
forces
f_{*}\omega_{X/B}=\mathcal{O}_{B}^{\oplus 2}
. Thus
f_{*}\omega_{X}=\omega_{B}^{\oplus 2}
. In particular,
p_{g}(X)=2g(B)\geq 4
. Note that
K_{X/B}
is nef. It follows that

K_{X}^{3}=K_{X/B}^{3}+6\left(g(B)-1\right)\geq 6\left(g(B)-1\right)=3p_{g}(X)-6>\frac{4}{3}p_{g}(X).

We now assume that
F
is not a
(1,2)
-surface. By
[
16
, Theorem 1.6]
,
K_{X/B}^{3}\geq 2\deg f_{*}\omega_{X/B}
. Thus it follows that

\displaystyle K^{3}_{X}

\displaystyle=K_{X/B}^{3}+6\left(g(B)-1\right)K_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 2\deg f_{*}\omega_{X/B}+6\left(g(B)-1\right)K_{F}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\deg f_{*}\omega_{X}+\left(6K_{F}^{2}-4p_{g}(F)\right)\left(g(B)-1\right)

\displaystyle=2p_{g}(X)-2h^{1}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X})+\left(6K_{F}^{2}-2p_{g}(F)\right)\left(g(B)-1\right),

where the last equality follows from the Riemann-Roch theorem. By
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
,
h^{1}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X})\leq{\mathrm{rank}\,}f_{*}\omega_{X}=p_{g}(F)
. Moreover, since
K_{F}^{2}\geq 2p_{g}(F)-4
, it is easy to see that
3K_{F}^{3}\geq 2p_{g}(F)
unless
F
is a
(1,2)
-surface. Combine the above inequalities together, and we deduce that

K^{3}_{X}\geq 2p_{g}(X)-2p_{g}(F)+6K_{F}^{2}-2p_{g}(F)\geq 2p_{g}(X).

Thus the proof is completed.
∎

The case when

Proposition 3.3

Suppose that

Proposition 3.4

Since

3.2.2.
\dim\Sigma=2

Here we treat the case when
\dim\Sigma=2
. Thus
p_{g}(X)\geq 3

Lemma 3.7

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose that
{\rm alb}\dim X\geq 2
and
\dim\Sigma=2

{\rm alb}\dim S=2

3.5

3.11
, we may write

M\equiv bS,

where
S
is a general fibre of
\psi
. Let
\sigma:S\to S_{0}
be the contraction onto the minimal model
S_{0}
of
S
.

We first consider the case when
g(\Sigma^{\prime})>0
. In this case, since
q(X)=1
, we have
g(\Sigma^{\prime})=1
. Thus the universal property of the Albanese map implies that
\psi=f^{\prime}
, and we may assume that
S=F^{\prime}
and
\Sigma^{\prime}=B
. Moreover,
g(\Sigma^{\prime})=1
implies that
b=p_{g}(X)
. Thus it follows that

K_{X}^{3}=(\pi^{*}K_{X})^{3}\geq b\left((\pi^{*}K_{X})^{2}\cdot F^{\prime}\right)=p_{g}(X)K_{F}^{2}.

If
K_{F}^{2}\geq 2
, then the above inequalities implies that

K_{X}^{3}\geq 2p_{g}(X).

If
K_{F}^{2}=1
, since
F
is not a
(1,2)
-surface and
p_{g}(X)>0
, we deduce that
p_{g}(F)=1
. Now
f_{*}\omega_{X}
is a line bundle on
B
, and
h^{0}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X})=p_{g}(X)\geq 2
. Thus
h^{1}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X})=0
. By (
5.2
) and the Riemann-Roch theorem, we have

K_{X}^{3}\geq 2\deg f_{*}\omega_{X}=2p_{g}(X).

We now assume that
g(\Sigma^{\prime})=0
. In this case,
f^{\prime}|_{S}:S\to B
is non-trivial. Thus
q(S)>0
. Suppose first that
K_{S_{0}}^{2}\geq 4
. As in the proof of Proposition
3.11
, we have

K_{X}^{3}\geq\frac{\left(p_{g}(X)-1\right)^{3}}{\left(p_{g}(X)\right)^{2}}K_{S_{0}}^{2}\geq\frac{4\left(p_{g}(X)-1\right)^{3}}{\left(p_{g}(X)\right)^{2}},

and it is easy to check that the three inequalities in the proposition hold. It remains to treat the case when
K_{S_{0}}^{2}\leq 3
. By
[
11
, Théorème 6.1]
,
K_{S_{0}}^{2}\geq 2p_{g}(S)\geq 2q(S)
. This forces
p_{g}(S)=q(S)=1
. First,
p_{g}(S)=1
implies that
\psi_{*}\omega_{X^{\prime}}
is a line bundle, and
p_{g}(X)\geq 2
further implies that
\psi_{*}\omega_{X^{\prime}}
is ample. Second,
q(S)=1
implies that
R^{1}\psi_{*}\omega_{X^{\prime}}
is a line bundle. By the Leray spectral sequence,
h^{1}(\Sigma^{\prime},R^{1}\psi_{*}\omega_{X^{\prime}})=h^{2}(X^{\prime},\omega_{X^{\prime}})-g(\Sigma^{\prime})=1
. Thus
R^{1}\psi_{*}\omega_{X^{\prime}}=\omega_{\Sigma^{\prime}}
. By the Leray spectral sequence again, we deduce that

h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=h^{1}(X^{\prime},\omega_{X^{\prime}})=h^{1}(\Sigma^{\prime},\psi_{*}\omega_{X^{\prime}})+h^{0}(\Sigma^{\prime},R^{1}\psi_{*}\omega_{X^{\prime}})=0.

By (
5.1
), it follows that

K_{X}^{3}\geq 2\chi(\omega_{X})=2p_{g}(X)+2q(X)-2=2p_{g}(X).

The whole proof is completed.
∎

5.2.
The case when
\dim\Sigma=2

We now consider the case when
\dim\Sigma=2
. Thus
p_{g}(X)\geq 3
. Since
\dim\phi_{K_{X}}(F)\geq 1
, we have
p_{g}(F)\geq 2
. We still denote by
C
a general fibre of
\psi
, where
C
is a smooth curve of genus
g(C)\geq 2
.

As in §
3.2.2
, we may assume that
X^{\prime}
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
are smooth. Let
S\in|M|
be a smooth general member. Denote by
\sigma:S\to S_{0}
the contraction onto its minimal model. Then we have
M|_{S}\equiv dC
, where
d=(\deg\tau)\cdot(\deg\Sigma)\geq p_{g}(X)-2
. Let
H^{\prime}
be a smooth curve on
\Sigma^{\prime}
such that
S=\psi^{*}H^{\prime}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Suppose that
\dim\Sigma=2
and
C
is
f^{\prime}
-vertical. Then
K_{X}^{3}\geq 2p_{g}(X)-2
.

Proof.

By the assumption,
C
is contained in a general fibre of
f^{\prime}
. Thus we have

K_{X}^{3}\geq\left((\pi^{*}K_{X})\cdot M^{2}\right)=d\left((\pi^{*}K_{X})|_{F^{\prime}}\cdot C\right)=d(K_{F}\cdot C_{0}).

where
C_{0}=\pi_{*}C
. Note that
(K_{F}\cdot C_{0})\geq 2
. Otherwise, we would have
(K_{F}\cdot C_{0})=1
. By the Hodge index theorem, we deduce that
K_{F}^{2}=1
, and
F
is a
(1,2)
-surface, which is absurd. Now
f^{\prime}
factors through
\psi
. It implies that
\Sigma^{\prime}
is an irregular surface. By
[
26
, Theorem 7]
, we know that
d\geq p_{g}(X)-1
. Combine the above inequalities together, and it follows that

K_{X}^{3}\geq 2p_{g}(X)-2.

Thus the proof is completed.
∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose that
\dim\Sigma=2
and
C
is not
f^{\prime}
-vertical.

(1)

If
g(H^{\prime})\geq 1
, then
K_{X}^{3}\geq 2p_{g}(X)-2
;

(2)

If
g(H^{\prime})=0
, then

K_{X}^{3}\geq\min\left\{\frac{5}{2}p_{g}(X)-5,2p_{g}(X)-\frac{10}{3}\right\}.

Proof.

Since
S
admits a surjective morphism
f^{\prime}|_{S}:S\to B
, we know that
q(S)>0
. By the assumption,
f^{\prime}|_{S}
does not factor through
\psi|_{S}:S\to H^{\prime}
.

If
g(H^{\prime})\geq 1
, then we deduce that
{\rm alb}\dim S=2
. By the same argument as in the proof of Lemma
3.9
, we deduce that
K_{X}^{3}\geq 2d
. By
[
26
, Theorem 7]
, we have
d\geq p_{g}(X)-1
. Thus it follows that

K_{X}^{3}\geq 2p_{g}(X)-2.

The proof of (1) is completed.

In the following, we assume that
g(H^{\prime})=0
.
By
[
7
, Corollary 2.3]
, the
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor
2(\pi^{*}K_{X})|_{S}-\sigma^{*}K_{S_{0}}
is pseudo-effective. Thus we have

2\left((\pi^{*}K_{X})\cdot C\right)\geq\left((\sigma^{*}K_{S_{0}})\cdot C\right)=(K_{S_{0}}\cdot C_{0}),

where
C_{0}=\sigma_{*}C
. If
(K_{S_{0}}\cdot C_{0})\geq 5
, then
\left((\pi^{*}K_{X})\cdot C\right)\geq\frac{5}{2}
and we have

K_{X}^{3}\geq\left((\pi^{*}K_{X})\cdot M^{2}\right)=d\left((\pi^{*}K_{X})\cdot C\right)\geq\frac{5}{2}p_{g}(X)-5.

Thus we may assume that
(K_{S_{0}}\cdot C_{0})\leq 4
. Note that by the adjunction,
K_{S}-2M|_{S}\equiv K_{S}-2dC
is pseudo-effective. Thus
K_{S_{0}}-2dC_{0}
is pseudo-effective. It follows that

4\geq(K_{S_{0}}\cdot C_{0})\geq 2dC_{0}^{2}\geq 2C_{0}^{2}\left(p_{g}(X)-2\right)\geq 2C_{0}^{2}.

In particular,
C_{0}^{2}=0
,
1
or
2
. We claim that in all cases,

K_{S_{0}}^{2}\geq 4\chi(\omega_{S})-8.

Suppose the claim holds. Then as in the proof of Lemma
3.8
, we know that

\chi(\omega_{X})+\chi(\omega_{S})\geq 3p_{g}(X)-3.

Combine the above inequalities with (
3.2
) and (
5.1
). It follows that

K_{X}^{3}\geq\frac{1}{3}K_{X}^{3}+\frac{1}{6}K_{S_{0}}^{2}\geq\frac{2}{3}\left(\chi(\omega_{X})+\chi(\omega_{S})-2\right)\geq 2p_{g}(X)-\frac{10}{3}.

To prove the above claim, suppose first that
C_{0}^{2}=0
. Then
\psi|_{S}
descends to a fibration
h:S_{0}\to H^{\prime}
with
C_{0}
as a general fibre. By the adjunction and parity,
g(C_{0})=2
or
3
. Since
q(S_{0})>0
, by
[
34
, Corollary 1]
, we have

K_{S_{0}}^{2}+8\left(g(C_{0})-1\right)=K_{S_{0}/H^{\prime}}^{2}\geq 4\deg h_{*}\omega_{S_{0}/H^{\prime}}=4\chi(\omega_{S})+4\left(g(C_{0})-1\right).

That is,

K_{S_{0}}^{2}\geq 4\chi(\omega_{S})-4\left(g(C_{0})-1\right)\geq 4\chi(\omega_{S})-8.

Suppose that
C_{0}^{2}>0
. Then by parity, either
(K_{S_{0}}\cdot C_{0})=3
and
C_{0}^{2}=1
, or
(K_{S_{0}}\cdot C_{0})=4
and
C_{0}^{2}=2
. By the Hodge index theorem, we always have
K_{S_{0}}^{2}\leq 9
. By
[
1
, Lemma 14]
, we know that
\chi(\omega_{S})\leq 4
and

K_{S_{0}}^{2}\geq 2\chi(\omega_{S})\geq 4\chi(\omega_{S})-8.

Thus the claim holds, and the proof of the lemma is completed.
∎

and

In particular, if

.

and

5.3.
The case when
\dim\Sigma=3

We now treat the case when
\dim\Sigma=3
.

Proposition 5.6
.

Suppose that
\dim\Sigma=3
. Then
K_{X}^{3}\geq 2p_{g}(X)-3
.

Proof.

Let
S\in|M|
be a general member. Then
S
is a smooth surface of general type. Since
S
admits a surjective morphism
f^{\prime}|_{S}:S\to B
, we know that
q(S)>0
. Let
h:S\to B^{\prime}
be the Stein factorization of
f^{\prime}|_{S}
with a general fibre
C
. Then
g(C)\geq 2
. Let
\sigma:S\to S_{0}
be the contraction onto the minimal model of
S
. Since
g(B^{\prime})>0
,
h
naturally descends to a fibration
h_{0}:S_{0}\to B^{\prime}
with a general fibre
C_{0}=\sigma_{*}C
.

Since
\phi_{M}|_{C}
is a finite morphism, we know that
h^{0}(C,M|_{C})\geq 2
. In particular,
(M\cdot C)\geq 2
. By the adjunction, we deduce that

\deg K_{C}=(K_{S}\cdot C)=\left((K_{X^{\prime}}+M)\cdot C\right)\geq 2(M\cdot C)\geq 4.

Thus
g(C)\geq 3
. By
[
34
, Theorem 2]
, we know that

K_{S_{0}}^{2}\geq\left(\frac{4g(C_{0})-4}{g(C_{0})}\right)\chi(\omega_{S})\geq\frac{8}{3}\chi(\omega_{S}).

As in the proof of Proposition
3.4
, we still have

\chi(\omega_{X})+\chi(\omega_{S})\geq 4p_{g}(X)-6.

Combine the above inequalities with (
3.2
) and (
5.1
). It follows that

K_{X}^{3}\geq\frac{1}{4}K_{X}^{3}+\frac{3}{16}K_{S_{0}}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}(\chi(\omega_{X})+\chi(\omega_{S}))\geq 2p_{g}(X)-3.

Thus the proof is completed.
∎

5.4.
The case when
X
is Gorenstein

Finally, we treat the case when
X
is Gorenstein.

Lemma 5.7
.

Suppose that
X
is Gorenstein. If the map
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{2}H^{0}(F,K_{F})\to H^{0}(F,2K_{F})
is injective, then

K_{X}^{3}\geq\left(2p_{g}(F)-4\right)\deg f_{*}\omega_{X}+h^{1}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X})\left(h^{1}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X})+1\right).

Proof.

Denote
q_{f}=h^{1}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X})
. By
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
, we have

f_{*}\omega_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{B}^{\oplus q_{f}}\oplus\mathcal{V},

where
{\mathrm{rank}\,}\mathcal{V}=p_{g}(F)-q_{f}
and
h^{1}(B,\mathcal{V})=0
. By the assumption, we have
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{2}f_{*}\omega_{X}\subseteq f_{*}\omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}
. Denote by
\mathcal{F}
the saturation of
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{2}f_{*}\omega_{X}
in
f_{*}\omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}
. Then
we have the following short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{F}\to f_{*}\omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}\to\mathcal{Q}\to 0.

By the Kawamata-Viehweg vanishing theorem and the Leray spectral sequence, we know that
h^{1}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X}^{\otimes 2})=0
. Via the long exact sequence, we deduce that
h^{1}(B,\mathcal{Q})=0
and

h^{0}(B,\mathcal{Q})\geq h^{1}(B,\mathcal{F})
. Thus by the Riemann-Roch theorem, we have

\deg f_{*}\omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}=\deg\mathcal{F}+\deg\mathcal{Q}=h^{0}(B,\mathcal{F})-h^{1}(B,\mathcal{F})+h^{0}(B,\mathcal{Q})\geq h^{0}(B,\mathcal{F}).

Note that

\deg\left({\mathrm{Sym}}^{2}f_{*}\omega_{X}\right)=\left(p_{g}(F)+1\right)\deg f_{*}\omega_{X}

and

h^{1}\left(B,{\mathrm{Sym}}^{2}f_{*}\omega_{X}\right)\geq h^{1}\left(B,{\mathrm{Sym}}^{2}\mathcal{O}_{B}^{\oplus q_{f}}\right)=\frac{q_{f}(q_{f}+1)}{2}.

By the Riemann-Roch theorem, we know that

h^{0}(B,\mathcal{F})\geq h^{0}\left(B,{\mathrm{Sym}}^{2}f_{*}\omega_{X}\right)=\left(p_{g}(F)+1\right)\deg f_{*}\omega_{X}+\frac{q_{f}(q_{f}+1)}{2}.

On the other hand, since
h^{1}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X}^{\otimes 2})=0
, by the Riemann-Roch formula, we know that

\deg f_{*}\omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}=h^{0}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X}^{\otimes 2})=h^{0}(X,2K_{X})=\frac{1}{2}K_{X}^{3}+3\chi(\omega_{X}).

Moreover, since
q(X)=1
, by the Leray spectral sequence, we have

\chi(\omega_{X})=p_{g}(X)-h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\leq h^{0}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X})-h^{1}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X})=\deg f_{*}\omega_{X}.

Combine the above (in)equalities together, and it follows that

K_{X}^{3}\geq 2\deg f_{*}\omega_{X}^{\otimes 2}-6\chi(\omega_{X})\geq\left(2p_{g}(F)-4\right)\deg f_{*}\omega_{X}+q_{f}(q_{f}+1).

The proof is completed.
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

Suppose that
X
is Gorenstein with
p_{g}(X)=2
. Then
K_{X}^{3}\geq 4
.

Proof.

Since
K_{X}^{3}
is an even integer, to prove this lemma, it suffices to prove that
K_{X}^{3}>2
. Now
\chi(\omega_{X})=p_{g}(X)-h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})>0
. By the Leray spectral sequence, we know that
h^{1}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X})\leq h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})\leq 1
.

If
h^{1}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X})=0
, by (
5.2
) and the Riemann-Roch theorem, we have

K_{X}^{3}\geq 2\deg f_{*}\omega_{X}=2h^{0}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X})=2p_{g}(X).

From now on, we assume that
h^{1}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X})=1
. By the Riemann-Roch theorem,
\deg f_{*}\omega_{X}=h^{0}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X})-h^{1}(B,f_{*}\omega_{X})=1
. Thus
f_{*}\omega_{X}
cannot be a line bundle. In particular,
p_{g}(F)={\mathrm{rank}\,}f_{*}\omega_{X}\geq 2
. If
p_{g}(F)=2
, by
[
12
, Theorem 2.1]
,
f_{*}\omega_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{B}\oplus\mathcal{L}
where
\mathcal{L}
is a line bundle on
B
with
\deg\mathcal{L}=1
. By
[
25
, Corollary 3.5]
,
K_{X}-F
is pseudo-effective. By
[
28
, Theorem 1.4]
and
[
5
, Lemma 4.1]
,
K_{X}-\epsilon F
is nef for some
\epsilon>0
. Thus

K_{X}^{3}>\left((K_{X}-\epsilon F)^{2}\cdot K_{X}\right)\geq\left((K_{X}-\epsilon F)^{2}\cdot F\right)=K_{F}^{2}\geq 2,

where the last inequality holds since
F
is not a
(1,2)
-surface. If
p_{g}(F)=3
, then the natural map
{\mathrm{Sym}}^{2}H^{0}(F,K_{F})\to H^{0}(F,2K_{F})
is injective. Thus by Lemma
5.7
, we have

K_{X}^{3}\geq 2\deg f_{*}\omega_{X}+2=4.

If
p_{g}(F)\geq 4
, since
f_{*}\omega_{X}^{\otimes m}
is ample for any
m\geq 2

[
5
, Lemma 4.1]
, by
[
28
, Main Theorem 1]
and the argument in the proof of
[
28
, Proposition 2.1]
(see the inequality
(*)
there), we know that

K_{X}^{3}>\left(\frac{4p_{g}(F)-8}{p_{g}(F)}\right)\deg f_{*}\omega_{X}\geq 2\deg f_{*}\omega_{X}=2.

As a result, we always have
K_{X}^{3}>2
, and the proof is completed.
∎

3.1
. If

6.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

As in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we may assume that
X
is minimal with

p_{g}(X)\geq 16

6.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

We may again assume that
X
is minimal. Then the result just follows from Theorem

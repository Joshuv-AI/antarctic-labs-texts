On the index of maximally hypoelliptic differential operators
Omar Mohsen

Abstract
We give an index formula for the class of all ∗ * -maximally hypoelliptic differential operators on any closed manifold with vector bundle coefficients, generalising previous index formulas by Atiyah-Singer and van Erp. Using this formula, we give new explicit index computations for Hörmander’s sum of squares operators of arbitrary rank. † † AMS subject classification: 19K56, 22A22, 46L80, Secondary: 22E25, 53R30. Keywords: index theory, maximally hypoelliptic differential operators, singular foliations, C ∗ C^{*} -algebras, Rockland condition.



Introduction

Let
E,F\to M
be vector bundles on a closed manifold
M

D:\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(F)
a differential operator. Under suitable conditions on

\ker(D)
and

are finite dimensional, where

is the formal adjoint. One then defines the analytic index by

\Ind_{a}(D):=\dim(\ker(D))-\dim(\ker(D^{*})).

(1)

The index problem asks for a topological formula for the analytic index. For elliptic differential operators, such a formula was found by Atiyah and Singer
[
AS68a
]
. Nevertheless there is a very large class of non-elliptic operators for which the analytic index is finite.

For some non-elliptic operators called Toeplitz operators which are defined on the boundary of a complex domain, a topological formula for the analytic index was found by Boutet de Monvel
[
Bou78a
]
. Based on work by Epstein and Melrose
[
EMa
]
, the formula of Boutet de Monvel was generalised to arbitrary contact manifolds by van Erp for operators without vector bundle coefficients
[
Erp10b
,
Erp10c
]
. Later van Erp and Baum generalised van Erp’s formula to allow vector bundle coefficients
[
BE14a
]
(always on contact manifolds). Their formula allows them to compute the index of some Hörmander’s sum of squares
[
Hör67a
]
of rank
2
.

In this article, we consider the class of
*
-maximally hypoelliptic differential operator. A
*
-maximally hypoelliptic differential operator is a differential operator
D
on a manifold

and

such that

and Corollary

[

]
for an expository introduction to the topic. In this case, one can show that the analytic index is finite and it coincides with the Fredholm index of

, see Section
1
. Elliptic differential operators are
*
-maximally hypoelliptic because one can take

and

the classical Sobolev spaces. Hörmander’s sum of squares are also

-maximally hypoelliptic see
[

,
FS74a
,
HN85a
]
. The operators considered by Boutet de Monvel and more generally by van Erp and Baum are also

-maximally hypoelliptic, see
[

]
. We refer the reader to Example

, for concrete examples of
*
-maximally hypoelliptic differential operators on

which aren’t elliptic.

Atiyah-Singer topological formula is based on the classical principal symbol. In
[
AMY22a
]
, we defined a principal symbol for
*
-maximally hypoelliptic differential operators. Theorem

Let
D\in\Diff^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}(M,E,F)
be a
*

Let

In other words we only look at representations in the characteristic set. Even though the bundle

3.1
. So

\Ind_{a}(D^{\prime\prime})=\sum_{x\in f^{-1}_{k}(0)}\sum_{\lambda}\Ind_{AS}([\lambda-\sigma^{l}_{classical}(D^{\prime},y,\xi)]),

(18)

where

•

\sum_{\lambda}
is the sum over the spectrum of
\left(\partial_{z}^{2}-c(x)^{2}z^{2k}\right)^{\frac{l(k+1)}{2}}
.

•

\Ind_{AS}([\lambda-\sigma^{l}_{classical}(D^{\prime},y,\xi)])
means the image of
[\lambda-\sigma^{l}_{classical}(D^{\prime},y,\xi)]\in K^{1}(S_{x})=K^{0}(\ring{T}^{*}p^{-1}(x))
by
K^{0}(\ring{T}^{*}p^{-1}(x))\xrightarrow{K_{*}(\iota)}K^{0}(T^{*}p^{-1}(x))\xrightarrow{\Ind_{AS}}\mathbb{Z}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Consider the filtration of depth
k+2
on
M

\mathcal{F}^{1}=\langle X\rangle,\mathcal{F}^{2}=\langle X,(f\circ p)Y:Y\in\Gamma(\ker(dp))\rangle,\quad\mathcal{F}^{i+1}=\mathcal{F}^{i}+[\mathcal{F}^{i},\mathcal{F}^{1}]+[\mathcal{F}^{i-1},\mathcal{F}^{2}].

The computations we did above work almost word for word the same for this filtration, one just replaces the formula for
D^{\prime\prime}
with

(\nabla_{X}^{4}-((f\circ p)D)^{2})^{\frac{l(k+2)}{4}}-D^{\prime}

and
D,D^{\prime}
as above.

Return to
S^{1}\times S^{1}
.

From (
18
), one immediately deduces (
8
). We end this section two further examples.

•

for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the operator

D=(\partial_{x}^{2}+(f(x)\partial_{y})^{2})^{k+1}+g(x,y)\partial_{y}^{2}.

The operator is
*
-maximally hypoelliptic if and only if

g(x,y)\notin\mathrm{spec}\left(\left(\partial_{z}^{2}-c(x)^{2}z^{2k}\right)^{k+1}\right),\quad\forall(x,y)\in f^{-1}_{k}(0)\times S^{1}.

Its analytic index always vanishes by (
18
).

•

Using remark
3.2
, supposing
4|k+2
, consider the operator

D=(\partial_{x}^{4}-(f(x)\partial_{y})^{2})^{\frac{k+2}{4}}+g(x,y)\sqrt{-1}\partial_{y}.

The operator is
*
-maximally hypoelliptic if and only if

g(x,y)\notin\pm\mathrm{spec}\left(\left(\partial_{z}^{4}+c(x)^{2}z^{2k}\right)^{\frac{k+2}{4}}\right),\quad\forall(x,y)\in f^{-1}_{k}(0)\times S^{1}.

One then has

\Ind_{a}(D)=\sum_{x\in f^{-1}_{k}(0)}\sum_{\lambda}w(\lambda-g(x,\cdot))-w(\lambda+g(x,\cdot)),

where the sum is over the spectrum.

Proof of Theorem
A
.

The idea is to construct a
C^{*}
-algebra fibered over
[0,1]^{2}
which connects the index for
*
-maximally hypoelliptic operators with Atiyah-Singer index. The
C^{*}
-algebra is obtained as the
C^{*}
-algebra of the blowup of a singular foliation on
M\times[0,1]^{2}
, see
[
Moh22a
]
. By a singular foliation on a compact manifold, we mean a finitely generated module of vector fields which is closed under Lie brackets. We define a singular foliation on
M\times[0,1]^{2}
which is defined as the set of finite sums of vector fields of the form

t^{i}sf(x,t,s)X(x),

where
X\in\mathcal{F}^{i}
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M\times[0,1]^{2})
. It is denoted by
\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}
. This is a singular foliation by (
2
). The holonomy groupoid
\mathcal{H}(\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})\rightrightarrows M\times[0,1]^{2}
of
\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}
defined in
[
AS09a
]
is fibered over
[0,1]^{2}
with fiber over
(t,s)\in[0,1]^{2}
equal to

•

the pair groupoid
M\times M
if
(t,s)\in]0,1]^{2}
, whose
C^{*}
-algebra is
\mathcal{K}(L^{2}M)

•

the bundle of abelian groups
TM
if
(t,s)\in]0,1]\times\{0\}
, whose
C^{*}
-algebra is
C_{0}(T^{*}M)

•

the bundle of osculating groups
\mathcal{G}\mathrm{r}(\mathcal{F}):=\sqcup_{x\in M}\mathcal{G}\mathrm{r}(\mathcal{F})_{x}
if
(t,s)\in\{0\}\times]0,1]
, whose
C^{*}
-algebra is
C^{*}\mathcal{G}\mathrm{r}(\mathcal{F})
is equal to
I_{\phi^{*}(\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})^{*})}
, where
\phi
is the map (
12
). This follows from
[
AMY22a
, Proposition 2.18]
.

•

the bundle of abelian groups
\bigsqcup_{x\in M}\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}
if
(t,s)=(0,0)
. By
[
AS11c
, Theorem 3.7]
, the
C^{*}
-algebra of the fiber is equal to
C_{0}(\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})^{*})
, where
\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})^{*}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}^{*}
inherits the subspace topology defined in Section
1
.

We won’t use the maximal
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}
defined in
[
AS09a
]
as it doesn’t have the fibers we need. Instead we will use the
C^{*}
-algebra of the blowup
C^{*}_{z}\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}
defined in
[
Moh22a
, Section 3]
. By
[
Moh22a
, Theorem 3.7]
,
C^{*}_{z}\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}
can be defined as a quotient of
C^{*}\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}
by the formula

C^{*}_{z}\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}:=\frac{C^{*}\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}}{J},\quad J=\{a\in C^{*}\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}:a_{(t,s)}=0,\quad\forall(t,s)\in]0,1]^{2}\}.

By

Moh22a
, Theorem 3.7 and Example 3.8]
, we deduce that the fibers of
C^{*}_{z}\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}
are given by

•

\mathcal{K}(L^{2}M)
if
(t,s)\in]0,1]^{2}
.

•

C_{0}(T^{*}M)
if
(t,s)\in]0,1]\times\{0\}
.

•

C^{*}\mathcal{T}\mathcal{F}
if
(t,s)\in\{0\}\times]0,1]

•

C_{0}(\mathcal{T}^{*}\mathcal{F})
if

The restriction to each side of the square

gives a short exact sequence of the form (

Lemma 4.1
.

If
D\in\Diff^{k}_{\mathcal{F}}(M,E,F)
is
*
-maximally hypoelliptic, then
\Ind_{\mathcal{F}}([\sigma^{k}(D)])=\Ind_{a}(D)
.

Proof.

In
[
AMY22a
, Section 2.4]
, we constructed an unbounded multiplier
\Theta(D)
of the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}_{z}\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}_{|[0,1]\times\{1\}}
which when restricted to
t\neq 0
it acts on
\mathcal{K}(L^{2}M)
by
t^{k}D
and when restricted to
t=0
it acts on
C^{*}\mathcal{T}\mathcal{F}
by
\sigma^{k}(D)
. This unbounded multiplier is Fredholm. This follows from the construction of a parametrix in
[
AMY22a
, Section 3.10 and 3.11]
. It follows that
[\Theta(D)]\in K_{0}(C^{*}_{z}\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}_{|[0,1]\times\{1\})}
is well defined. The definition of the map

implies the result, because
K(\ev_{0})([\Theta(D)])=[\sigma^{k}(D)]
and

.
∎

The existence of the

-algebra
C^{*}_{z}\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}
on the square
[0,1]^{2}
implies that

\Ind_{\mathcal{F}}\circ\mu_{\mathcal{T}^{*}\mathcal{F}}=\Ind_{AS}\circ\Ex.

(19)

To see this

Lemma 4.2
.

The map
K(\ev_{(0,0)}):K_{*}(C^{*}_{z}\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})\to K^{*}(\mathcal{T}^{*}\mathcal{F})
is an isomorphism, where
\ev_{(0,0)}
is the evaluation map at
(0,0)
.

Proof.

The kernel of
\ev_{(0,0)}
fits into the short exact sequences

\displaystyle 0\to C^{*}_{z}\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}_{|]0,1]\times[0,1]}\to\ker(\ev_{(0,0)})\to C^{*}_{z}\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}_{|\{0\}\times]0,1]}\to 0

Since
C^{*}_{z}\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}_{|\{0\}\times]0,1]}\simeq C^{*}_{z}\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}_{|(0,1)}\otimes C_{0}(]0,1])

Proof of Theorem
B
.

In
[
Moh22a
, Section 2.2]
, a blowup space of any singular foliation is defined. In particular for
\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F}
, we get a blowup space
\blup(\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})_{(x,0,1)}
which is defined as a subspace of the Grassmannian manifold of subspaces of
\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}
of codimension
\dim(M)
. It is shown in
[
Moh22a
, Section 3.2]
that any element
\xi\in\mathcal{T}^{*}\mathcal{F}_{x}
must vanish on a subspace
V\subseteq\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}
such that
V\in\blup(\mathfrak{t}\mathfrak{a}\mathcal{F})_{(x,0,1)}
. By
[
Moh22a
, Proposition 2.7]
,
V
is a Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gr}(\mathcal{F})_{x}
. If
B_{\xi}:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{R}
is the anti-symmetric bilinear map
B_{\xi}(X,Y)=\xi([X,Y])
. Then it is well known that
\dim(Ad^{*}(\mathcal{G}\mathrm{r}(\mathcal{F})_{x})\cdot\xi)=\codim(\ker(B_{\xi}))
. But since
\xi
vanishes on
V
and
V
is a Lie subalgebra, it follows that
B_{\xi}
vanishes on
V\times V
. The result follows.
∎

Large isospin symmetry breaking in kaon production at high energies
Wojciech Brylinski, Marek Gazdzicki, Francesco Giacosa, Mark Gorenstein, Roman Poberezhnyuk, Subhasis Samanta, Herbert Stroebele

Abstract
It is well known that isospin symmetry is fulfilled to a good approximation in strong interactions, as confirmed in low-energy scattering experiments and in mass spectra of both light and heavy hadrons. In collisions of nuclei with an equal number of protons and neutrons, isospin symmetry imposes that the number of produced charged kaons should equal the number of neutral ones. The NA61/SHINE experiment at CERN recently reported an excess of charged over neutral kaon production in high-energy nucleus-nucleus collisions. Here, we argue that the measured charge-to-neutral kaon ratio of about 1.2 indicates an unexpectedly large violation of isospin symmetry. Using well-established models for hadron production, we demonstrate that known symmetry-breaking effects and the initial nuclei containing more neutrons than protons lead only to a small (few per cent) deviation from unity at high energies. Thus, they cannot explain the measurements. The significance of the isospin symmetry violation beyond the known effects is 5.5 ⋅ σ \cdot\sigma when errors quoted by the experiments are used and 8.1 ⋅ σ \cdot\sigma for the PDG-like scaled errors. New systematic, high-precision measurements and theoretical efforts are needed to establish the origin of the observed large isospin-symmetry breaking.

In 1932, Heisenberg suggested that the neutron and the proton could be seen as different manifestations of the same particle, the nucleon
Heisenberg 1932
, explaining their similar masses. Wigner referred to this feature as ‘isotopic spin’, or ‘isospin’, due to the formal similarity with the more familiar spin
Wigner 1937
. Today, isospin is an important element of nuclear physics and nuclei classification
Warner et al. 2006
.
The concept of isospin was promptly extended to the other strongly interacting particles, the hadrons. In particular, Kemmer
Kemmer 1939
applied it to the pion triplet postulated by Yukawa in 1935
Yukawa 1935
. For a historical outline, see Refs.
Kemmer 1982
;
Webb 2008
.

Nowadays, hadrons are viewed as emergent within
Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD)
Fritzsch et al. 1973
, the theory of strong interactions between the elementary carriers of colour charge, the quarks and the gluons.
Because of confinement, QCD is based on a local colour gauge symmetry
that is hidden in colourless hadrons.
Hadrons are classified into mesons with integer spin, typically
quark-anti-quark states such as pions and kaons, and baryons with semi-integer spin, typically three-quark states such as protons and neutrons.

Within QCD, the isospin symmetry could be understood as a part of a more general symmetry, denoted as flavour symmetry, which arises because the gluons interact ‘democratically’ with the light quark flavours
u
,
d
, and
s
.
Moreover, in the limit where the quarks can be considered massless (the so-called chiral limit), these interactions do not depend on their chirality, thus realizing a larger symmetry called chiral symmetry. The latter is spontaneously broken in the QCD vacuum generating (quasi-) Goldstone bosons, pions, kaons, and the
\eta
-meson.

In more specific terms, the isospin operator triplet

\hat{\mathbb{I}}=(\hat{I}_{x},\hat{I}_{y},\hat{I}_{z})
refers to an
SU(2)
rotation of the ‘isodoublet’
(u,d)
, where
u

has eigenvalue
I_{z}=1/2
and
d

I_{z}=-1/2
, thus an isospin transformation changes e.g. a
proton into a neutron or generates an
O(3)
rotation of the pion triplet. Kaons, which are grouped into the
isodoublet
(I=1/2)
states
(K^{+}\equiv u\bar{s},K^{0}\equiv d\bar{s})
and

(\bar{K^{0}}\equiv s\bar{d},K^{-}\equiv s\bar{u})
correspond naturally to the
underlying isospin doublets
(u,d)
and
(-\bar{d},\bar{u})
and shall play a
crucial role in the following.
Isospin symmetry implies that
only transitions preserving total isospin
I
and its
z
-component
I_{z}
are allowed. This property is referred to as the isospin conservation.

QCD would strictly obey the isospin symmetry, having equal light quark masses,
m_{u}=m_{d}
.
Since the current quark masses of
u
and
d
quarks differ only by
a few MeV
Workman
et al. 2022
, only small deviations from this symmetry
are expected.
The relative mass differences of charge states of pions and kaons are small,
\left(m_{\pi^{+}}-m_{\pi^{0}}\right)/\left(m_{\pi^{+}}+m_{\pi^{0}}\right)\simeq 0.017

and
\left(m_{K^{+}}-m_{K^{0}}\right)/\left(m_{K^{+}}+m_{K^{0}}\right)\simeq-0.004
.
The elastic cross sections for pion-pion, pion-nucleon, and nucleon-nucleon
scattering closely follow the predictions of isospin symmetry
Pennington 2005
;
Alarcon et al. 2012
.

Thus, one also expects that isospin symmetry is obeyed in good approximation in hadron production processes at high energies.
However, recently reported NA61/SHINE experimental results on producing charged and neutral kaons, supported by the compilation of the world data reported in Ref.
Adhikary et al. 2023
, seem to contradict those expectations
Gazdzicki and Hansen 1991
. Here, we address this question in detail.

Isospin symmetry and kaon production in nucleus-nucleus collisions.

Let us consider, for definiteness, an initial state with two identical nuclei having equal proton and neutron numbers (
Z=N=A/2
) in their ground states
1
1

1

Considering two distinct nuclei, each of them having an equal number of protons and neutrons, would lead to the same results.
. This initial state is denoted as
\left|A+A\right\rangle
. In conserved quantities, the condition
Z=N

is given by
Q/B=1/2,
where
Q=Z
and
B=A
are the nuclei’s electric charge and baryon number, respectively. Then, each nucleus’ third component of isospin is zero
2
2

2

For almost all nuclei of this type, the total isospin is also zero
Lenzi and Bentley 2009
, hence the total isospin of the
\left|A+A\right\rangle
state vanishes.
.

The isospin transformation is parameterized by the unitary operator
\hat{O}=e^{i\theta_{j}\hat{I}_{j}}
and, in general, (approximate) isospin symmetry of QCD means that
\hat{O}^{\dagger}\hat{H}_{QCD}\hat{O}\simeq\hat{H}_{QCD}
, where

\hat{H}_{QCD}
refers to the Hamiltonian of QCD.
A particularly useful unitary operator is the special isospin transformation, called charge symmetry transformation,
\hat{C}=e^{i\pi\hat{I}_{y}}
.
It corresponds to an inversion of the third component of isospin:
\hat{C}\left|u\right\rangle=-\left|d\right\rangle
and
\hat{C}\left|d\right\rangle=\left|u\right\rangle
, schematically
u\leftrightarrow d
.
At the hadronic level, the
\hat{C}
transformation implies
p\leftrightarrow n
,
\pi^{+}\leftrightarrow\pi^{-}
,
K^{+}\leftrightarrow K^{0}
,
\bar{K}^{0}\leftrightarrow K^{-}
, etc.

Without referring to a detailed mathematical formalism, charge symmetry means that strong interactions are invariant under the inversion of the third component of the isospin of every nucleus and hadron of the initial and final states.
Let us consider an ensemble of initial states being invariant under the charge transformation
\hat{C}
- probabilities of having initial states related by this transformation are equal. This is indeed the case of nucleus-nucleus collisions where each nucleus has an equal number of protons and neutrons (thus,
I_{z}=0
).
Then, the invariance under
\hat{C}
-transformation holds also for the final state ensemble, implying that the mean multiplicities of
\hat{C}
-related quantities, such
K^{+}
and
K^{0}
and
\bar{K}^{0}
and
K^{-}
, coincide:

\langle K^{+}\rangle=\langle K^{0}\rangle\text{ , }

(1)

and

\langle K^{-}\rangle=\langle\bar{K}^{0}\rangle\text{ . }

(2)

Note, since models predict only properties of ensembles of events, we need to consider mean multiplicities for testing charge invariance.

The relations (
1
) and (
2
) are in agreement with the result first formulated in 1955 by Shmushkevich
Shmushkevich 1955
;
Dushin and
Shmushkevich 1956
, see also Refs.
MacFarlane et al. 1965
;
Wohl 1982
;
Pal 2014
. This result states that an initial ‘uniform’ ensemble of hadronic state (that is, one with an equal mean number of each member of any isospin multiplet)
evolves into a uniform final-state ensemble.

The neutral kaons are eigenstates of strong interaction, oscillating (
K^{0}\leftrightarrow\bar{K}^{0}
) due to weak interaction. The weak eigenstates are the long-lived ‘K-long’
K_{L}^{0}
and short-lived ‘K-short’
K_{S}^{0}
, which decay into three and two pions with mean lifetimes of 15 m/
c
and 2.8 cm/
c
, respectively. By neglecting the small CP violation, the weak and strong kaonic eigenstates are related by:

\left(\begin{array}[c]{c}\left|K_{S}^{0}\right\rangle\\
\left|K_{L}^{0}\right\rangle\end{array}\right)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}1&-1\\
1&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[c]{c}\left|K^{0}\right\rangle\\
\left|\bar{K}^{0}\right\rangle\end{array}\right)\text{ ,}

(3)

hence, for the mean number of
K_{S}^{0}
and
K_{L}^{0}
one gets:

\langle K_{S}^{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\langle K^{0}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}\langle\bar{K}^{0}\rangle=\langle K_{L}^{0}\rangle\text{ .}

(4)

Thus, we have an equal number of long-lived and short-lived kaons, even if
\langle K^{0}\rangle
and

\langle\bar{K}^{0}\rangle
are different due to the non-zero baryon number of the initial state.

By combining Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
) and using Eq. (
4
) one finds:

\langle K^{+}\rangle+\langle K^{-}\rangle=2\langle K^{0}_{S}\rangle~,

(5)

where also the r.h.s. in accessible to experiments. Consequently, the exact isospin symmetry prediction for the charged-to-neutral kaon ratio in
nucleus-nucleus collisions with
Q/B=1/2
reads:

R_{K}\equiv\frac{\langle K^{+}\rangle+\langle K^{-}\rangle}{\langle K^{0}\rangle+\langle\bar{K}^{0}\rangle}=\frac{\langle K^{+}\rangle+\langle K^{-}\rangle}{2\langle K_{S}^{0}\rangle}=1~.

(6)

This prediction serves as the initial reference for testing the isospin symmetry with the results provided by the NA61/SHINE experiment
Adhikary et al. 2023

that measured
R_{K}=1.233\pm 0.014(stat)\pm 0.053(sys)
in collisions of argon and scandium nuclei at the energy of 11.9 GeV per nucleon pair.
This result is consistent with the compilation of the world data, even if each previous measurement, taken individually, was also consistent with
R_{K}=1
within its large uncertainty. Yet, as discussed below, all data taken together clearly show a significant deviation from unity.

Neither is the isospin symmetry exact nor does experimental data refer to
A+A
collisions with
Q/B=1/2
.
In the following, we estimate possible deviations of
R_{K}
from unity due to the known effects: isospin symmetry-breaking processes and
Q/B\neq 1/2
,
using popular and well-established statistical and dynamical models.
Then, we quantify the difference between the experimental results and the model predictions and discuss possible implications.

Known isospin symmetry-breaking effects.

The isospin symmetry is explicitly broken in weak and electromagnetic processes and is an approximate symmetry of strong interactions. Here, we consider the known isospin symmetry-breaking effects one by one.

(A)

Weak processes. The weak interaction does not obey the isospin symmetry.
The mean lifetimes
Workman
et al. 2022
of charged and neutral kaons are

\tau(K^{+})=\tau(K^{-})=(1.238\pm 0.001)\cdot 10^{-8}
s
and

\tau(K^{0}_{S})=(8.954\pm 0.004)\cdot 10^{-11}
s,

\tau(K^{0}_{L})=(5.116\pm 0.021)\cdot 10^{-8}
s.
The charged kaons are typically measured by reconstructing their trajectories in a detector. Due to the large mean lifetime, the corrections for their losses caused by weak decays are small.
In contrast,
the neutral
K^{0}_{S}
kaons are measured by reconstructing their decays into two charged pions. Typically, the corrections for the losses caused by weak decays are large. This is because the decay should be far enough from the interaction point to separate the decay point from the background in
high multiplicity
A+A
collisions.
Assuming that the
K^{0}_{S}
mesons are measurable when their proper lifetime is larger than the mean lifetime (typical for NA61/SHINE
Adhikary et al. 2023
), one estimates the maximum relative bias of the mean multiplicity as

\frac{\Delta(\langle K_{S}^{0}\rangle)}{\langle K_{S}^{0}\rangle}=\frac{3\cdot\sigma(\tau(K_{S}^{0})}{\tau(K_{S}^{0})}\approx 0.0013.

(7)

Thus, the maximum deviation of
R_{K}
from unity due to the uncertainty of the mean
K^{0}_{S}
lifetime is 0.13 %.

(B)

Electromagnetic process.
Electromagnetic interactions do not conserve isospin because the electromagnetic coupling constant differs for the quark flavours
u
and
d
. Yet, electromagnetic decays of hadrons are typically suppressed by a factor
\alpha\simeq 1/137
compared to strong ones. Consequently, processes that involve the production of virtual photons and their subsequent decay into charged kaons are suppressed by a factor
\alpha^{2}
and thus negligible.

(C)

Strong processes within QCD.
The isospin symmetry is not exact because
u
and
d
quark masses are different,
\approx 2.2
and
\approx 4.7
MeV, respectively.
The different quark masses lead to different masses of hadrons within the isospin multiplets, particularly different masses of charged and neutral kaons.
The ratio
R_{K}
can be expanded as a function of the mass difference of the
u
and
d
quarks as:

R_{K}=R_{K}^{(0)}+\frac{m_{d}-m_{u}}{\Lambda_{QCD}}R_{K}^{(1)}+...\text{ ,}

(8)

where
R_{K}^{(0)}
is the value of the ratio for the isospin symmetry (
m_{d}=m_{u}
) being equal to one for
Q/B=1/2
, and
\Lambda_{QCD}\simeq 250
MeV is the QCD energy scale
Kneur and Neveu 2012
;
Deur et al. 2016
.
The question is whether
R_{K}^{(1)}+...
terms can reproduce the experimental data.
We list below the considered effects and quantify their impact on
R_{K}

using the statistical hadron resonance gas model (HRG)
Vovchenko and
Stoecker 2019
.
The results are cross-checked with the microscopic,
UrQMD transport model
Bass et al. 1998
;
Bleicher et al. 1999
;
Bleicher and
Bratkovskaya 2022
.
For details, see Methods.

(i)

Smaller masses of charged kaons than neutral ones,
m_{K^{+}}=m_{K^{-}}=493.7
MeV and
m_{K^{0}}=m_{\bar{K}^{0}}=497.6

This was estimated by removing resonances from the particle list of HRG.

(ii)

Different kaon masses impact the branching ratios

(iii)

Mass differences of hadrons belonging to other isospin multiplets also break the isospin symmetry and affect

The largest effect comes from the mass difference between proton and neutron, which reduces

at the lowest collision energies.
At energies larger than 10 GeV, this effect is negligible.

Interestingly, HRG for

Closing remarks.

Several comments are in order here.

(I)

The known effects of the isospin-symmetry breaking fail to explain the experimental results on the charged-to-neutral kaon ratio reported recently by NA61/SHINE
Adhikary et al. 2023
.
A dedicated experimental and theoretical effort is needed to solve the puzzle of this unexpected large isospin symmetry breaking.

(II)

Concerning measurements, reviewing the validity of the past results and confirming them with new high-precision data is important.
Systematic results on the collision energy and nuclear mass dependence of the isospin-breaking effect should help us understand its nature.
Measurements of the charged-to-neutral kaon ratio in collisions of an equal
number of protons and neutrons would eliminate uncertainty in its interpretation. In particular, measurements of the ratio in
deuterium-deuterium interactions, the lightest nuclei with
I=0
, are important.

(III)

An interesting experimental test is also possible by considering
\pi^{-}+C
and
\pi^{+}+C
interactions. While the ensemble of only one of them is not invariant under charge transformation, the ensemble having an equal number of
\pi^{-}+C
and
\pi^{+}+C
interactions is invariant. Thus, for the joint ensemble, the exact charge symmetry predicts
R_{K}=1
.

(IV)

Kaons play a special role due to their simple isospin structure and easy measurement. This explains why the first results on a large isospin-symmetry breaking are reported for kaons. Yet, it is important to perform a similar study for other isospin multiplets in the future.
For example, using the same methods, we have checked that the (anti-)proton to (anti)-neutron ratio is even less sensitive to the known isospin symmetry-breaking effects and, thus, is predicted to be almost exactly one in nucleus-nucleus collisions with
Q/B=0.5
in a broad range of collision energies including the low energies. Considering collision-by-collision fluctuations of charged and neutral kaon numbers would also be interesting. A puzzling result was recently reported by ALICE at LHC
Acharya et al. 2022

and discussed in Ref.
Kapusta et al. 2023
.

(V)

The relationship between the production rates of charged and neutral kaons in hadronic collisions was used to estimate the flux of secondary neutral kaons produced in
p
+Be collisions at beam momenta of several 100 GeV/
c
in neutral kaon and neutrino beams
Bonesini et al. 2001
. Recently, the charged-to-neutral kaon ratio was studied in
p+p
interactions at SPS energies,
and no excess in comparison to a simple quark counting model
emerged
Stepaniak and
Pszczel 2023
. On the other hand,
\pi^{-}
+C interactions at SPS energies yield a ratio of charged-to-neutral kaons that is significantly larger than unity and predictions
of popular models
Adhikary et al. 2023
.

(VI)

Concerning models, one may argue that there are known isospin-breaking effects which are improperly implemented in the popular models.
For example, an isospin-breaking in production and decay of heavy resonances could sum up a significant result. While this seems hard to hold since we considered known effects of this type,
careful and independent verification is required.
Independently, one can extend the models by introducing new isospin-breaking processes and fitting their parameters to the data.
This can be done either for the quark-gluon processes or the hadron-resonance processes.
For example, within the statistical hadronization models, one can introduce the quark fugacity factors for
u
and
d
quarks separately
Petrán et al. 2013
;
Rafelski and Petran 2014
;
Rafelski and Petran 2015
. This could allow us to make predictions for other hadron ratios.

(VII)

Concerning unknown effects violating isospin symmetry,
the possibility of having a phase of strongly interacting matter with a significant isospin violation was suggested by Pisarski and Wilczek within a linear
\sigma
model of QCD
Pisarski and Wilczek 1984
.
They expect masses of
\pi^{0}
and
\eta
mesons to decrease if the
U_{A}(1)
symmetry is effectively restored at lower temperatures than the chiral phase transition. Can the chiral anomaly
Giacosa et al. 2018
;
Giacosa et al. 2023
or other non-perturbative phenomena within QCD significantly affect the charge-to-neutral kaon ratio?

Summary.

In this work, we have investigated the ratio
R_{K}
of charged-to-neutral
kaon yields in nucleus-nucleus collisions at high energies.
In the limit of exact isospin symmetry, this ratio is unity if the initial state of nuclei having an equal number of protons and neutrons (
Q/B=1/2
) is considered.
The known isospin-symmetry breaking effects included in popular models predict
R_{K}\approx 1.03
at high collision energy.
Many experimental results correspond to collisions of nuclei with more neutrons than protons (
Q/B<1/2
, with
Q/B\simeq 0.4
). This reduces
R_{K}
significantly
only at low collision energies, where the ratio is lower than one. We conclude that the known effects violating isospin symmetry cannot explain experimental results on nucleus-nucleus collisions, for which
R_{K}
is mostly within the range 1.1-1.2. The significance of the isospin symmetry violation beyond the known effects is 5.64
\sigma
when errors quoted by the experiments are used and 8.21
\sigma
when the PDG-like scaled errors are used.

The evidence for unexpected large isospin symmetry breaking for kaons that cannot be explained by otherwise successful models seems well upheld. Assuming it passes future experimental tests, the quest is to understand its origin.

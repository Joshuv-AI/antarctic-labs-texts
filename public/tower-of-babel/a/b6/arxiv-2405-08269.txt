On saturation of the discrepancy principle for nonlinear Tikhonov regularization in Hilbert spaces
Qinian Jin

Abstract
. In this paper we revisit the discrepancy principle for Tikhonov regularization of nonlinear ill-posed problems in Hilbert spaces and provide some new and improved saturation results under less restrictive conditions, comparing with the existing results in the literature.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
and
Y
be two Hilbert spaces whose inner products and norms are denoted by the same notations

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\|\cdot\|
. Let
F:\mbox{dom}(F)\subset X\to Y
be a nonlinear
operator with domain
\mbox{dom}(F)
. We consider the equation

F(x)=y

(1)

and assume (
1
) is solvable in
\mbox{dom}(F)
, i.e.
y\in\mbox{Ran}(F)
, the range of
F
.
Since the solution of (
1
) may not be unique, it is necessary to select a right one.
To this end, we make a first guess
x^{*}\in\mbox{dom}(F)
and try to find a solution closest to
x^{*}
.
We say
x^{\dagger}\in\mbox{dom}(F)
is a
x^{*}
-minimum-norm solution of (
1
) if

F(x^{\dagger})=y

and

\|x^{\dagger}-x^{*}\|=\min_{x\in\mbox{dom}(F)}\{\|x-x^{*}\|:F(x)=y\}.

We consider the situation that (
1
) is ill-posed in the sense that its solution does not depend
continuously on the data. The exact data
y
is usually unavailable in practical applications. Instead we
only have a noisy data
y^{\delta}
obtained by measurement which satisfies

\|y^{\delta}-y\|\leq\delta

(2)

with a known noise level
\delta>0
. One of the important questions is then to reconstruct
x^{\dagger}
by using

y^{\delta}
. This requires the regularization techniques. The most famous method is Tikhonov regularization in
which the solution of the minimization problem

\min_{x\in\mbox{dom}(F)}\left\{\|F(x)-y^{\delta}\|^{2}+\alpha\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}\right\}

(3)

is used to approximate
x^{\dagger}
, where
\alpha>0
is the so-called regularization parameter. The
existence of
x^{*}
-minimum-norm solution and the regularized solution
x_{\alpha}^{\delta}
can be guaranteed
when
F
is weakly closed; see
[
3
]
. The solution
of (
3
) may not be unique in general, we will use
x_{\alpha}^{\delta}
to denote any solution of (
3
).
When
x_{\alpha}^{\delta}
is used to approximate
x^{\dagger}
, the accuracy depends crucially on the choice of the
regularization parameter
\alpha
which can be selected by either
a priori
or
a posteriori

rules (
[
3
,
8
,
11
,
12
]
). One of the most prominent
a posteriori
parameter
choice rule is Morozov’s discrepancy principle which can be stated as follows.

Rule 1

(The discrepancy principle)
.

Let
\tau\geq 1
be a given number. Define
\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})>0
to be the number such that

\|F(x_{\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|=\tau\delta
.

One may refer to
[
7
]
for a discussion on the existence of
\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})
satisfying
Rule
1
. With
\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})
chosen by Rule
1
, it has been shown in
[
3
]

that
x_{\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})}^{\delta}\to x^{\dagger}
as
\delta\to 0
and if
F
is Fréchet differentiable in a
neighborhood of
x^{\dagger}
whose Fréchet derivative
F^{\prime}(x)
is Lipschitz continuous with constant

L
and if the source condition
x^{\dagger}-x^{*}=F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})^{*}u
holds with
L\|u\|<1
, then

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\|x_{\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|:\|y^{\delta}-y\|\leq\delta\right\}=O(\delta^{1/2})

(4)

for the worst case error. It is natural to ask if the rate in (
4
) can be improved under a higher
order source condition
x^{\dagger}-x^{*}=\mbox{Ran}((F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})^{*}F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger}))^{\nu})
with
\nu>1/2
. For
compact linear operator
F
, Groetsch showed in
[
4
,
5
]
that if the rate
O(\delta^{1/2})
in
(
4
) is replaced by
o(\delta^{1/2})
then it must have
x^{*}=x^{\dagger}
. Thus, for a general choice
of the initial guess
x^{*}
, the rate in (
4
) is the best possible in the worst case scenario which
is known as the saturation of the discrepancy principle for linear Tikhonov regularization in Hilbert spaces.
In
[
10
]
Scherzer gave a saturation result on Rule
1
for nonlinear Tikhonov regularization
in Hilbert spaces. His proof relies on several conditions on the nonlinear operator
F
some of which seem
restrictive. Furthermore, the argument in
[
10
]
requires
\tau
to be sufficiently large which seems
unnatural. In this note we will provide new and improved saturation results for Rule
1

for nonlinear Tikhonov regularization (
3
) in Hilbert spaces under less restrictive conditions.

2.
Main results

The saturation result proved in
[
10
]
on Rule
1
for nonlinear Tikhonov regularization in
Hilbert spaces is based on the following technical conditions on the operator
F
, where
B_{\rho}(x^{\dagger}):=\{x\in X:\|x-x^{\dagger}\|<\rho\}
.

Assumption 1
.

There is
\rho>0
such that
B_{\rho}(x^{\dagger})\subset\emph{dom}(F)
and
F
is Fréchet dfferentiable
on
B_{\rho}(x^{\dagger})
. Moreover, there exists
\kappa_{0}\geq 0
such that for any
x,z\in B_{\rho}(x^{\dagger})

there is a bounded linear operator
R(x,z):X\to X
such that

F^{\prime}(x)=F^{\prime}(z)R(x,z)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|I-R(x,z)\|\leq\kappa_{0}\|x-z\|.

Assumption 2
.

There exists
\kappa_{1}\geq 0
such that for any
x,z\in B_{\rho}(x^{\dagger})
there is a bounded linear
operator
S(x,z):Y\to Y
such that

F^{\prime}(x)=S(x,z)F^{\prime}(z)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|I-S(x,z)\|\leq\kappa_{1}\|x-z\|.

Theorem 1

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let Assumption
1
and Assumption
2
hold. Assume that
F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})

is compact with infinite rank and
\max\{\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1}\}\|x^{*}-x^{\dagger}\|
is
sufficiently small. Consider Tikhonov regularization (
3
) and let

\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})
be determined by Rule
1
with sufficiently large

\tau>1
. If

\sup\left\{\|x_{\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|:\|y^{\delta}-y\|\leq\delta\right\}=o(\delta^{1/2})

as
\delta\to 0
, then
x^{\dagger}=x^{*}
.

The proof idea of Theorem
1
given in
[
10
]
is to connect Rule
1

for the nonlinear Tikhonov regularization (
3
) with a discrepancy principle for its
linearization at the sought solution
x^{\dagger}
and then use the saturation result of Groetsch
(
[
4
,
5
]
) for linear Tikhonov regularization. More precisely, replacing
F(x)
in
(
3
) by its linearization
y+A(x-x^{\dagger})
at
x^{\dagger}
, where
A:=F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})
, one
may consider the linearized minimization problem

\displaystyle\min_{x\in X}\left\{\|Ax-q^{\delta}\|^{2}+\alpha\|x-x^{*}\|^{2}\right\},\quad q^{\delta}:=Ax^{\dagger}+y^{\delta}-y

(5)

whose unique minimizer is given by

\hat{x}_{\alpha}^{\delta}=x^{\dagger}+(\alpha I+A^{*}A)^{-1}\left(\alpha(x^{*}-x^{\dagger})+A^{*}(y^{\delta}-y)\right).

(6)

It is then shown that if
\alpha
is chosen by Rule
1
, there must exists
a constant
c>1
such that

\displaystyle\|A\hat{x}_{\alpha}^{\delta}-q^{\delta}\|=\hat{\tau}\delta

(7)

for some number
\hat{\tau}\in[1,c]
. Once this is achieved, the saturation result
on the discrepancy principle for linear Tikhonov regularization can be used directly
to conclude
x^{*}-x^{\dagger}=0
. In order to achieve the above connection between
Rule
1
for nonlinear Tikhonov regularization and a discrepancy
principle for its linearization,one needs to compare
\|F(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|
with

\|A\hat{x}_{\alpha}^{\delta}-q^{\delta}\|
and guarantee the existence of a constant
c
such that
(
7
) holds for some
\hat{\tau}\in[1,c]
. These require the number
\tau

in Rule
1
to be sufficiently large and the nonlinear operator
F
to satisfy
Assumption
1
and Assumption
2
with sufficiently small

\max\{\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1}\}\|x^{*}-x^{\dagger}\|
.

In the following we will present some new and improved saturation results on Rule
1

for nonlinear Tikhonov regularization (
3
) in Hilbert spaces under less restrictive
conditions. Our first result shows that the conclusion of Theorem
1
holds for
Rule
1
with any
\tau>0
under merely Assumption
1
and the smallness
of
\max\{\kappa_{0},\kappa_{1}\}\|x^{*}-x^{\dagger}\|
is not required.

Theorem 2
.

Let Assumption
1
hold. Assume that, for the operator
A:=F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})
, there
exists a sequence of positive numbers
\{\lambda_{k}\}
in the spectrum
\sigma(AA^{*})
of
AA^{*}

such that
\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{k}=0
. Consider the nonlinear Tikhonov regularization
(
3
) and assume that there is a constant
\tau>0
such that, for any small
\delta>0

and any
y^{\delta}
satisfying
\|y^{\delta}-y\|\leq\delta
, a number
\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})>0
can be chosen such
that
\|F(x_{\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|\leq\tau\delta
. If

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\|x_{\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|:\|y^{\delta}-y\|\leq\delta\right\}=o(\delta^{1/2})

(8)

as
\delta\to 0
, then
x^{\dagger}=x^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha:=\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})
. According to (
8
), we have
\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|<\rho

for all
y^{\delta}
satisfying
\|y^{\delta}-y\|\leq\delta
provided
\delta>0
is sufficiently small. Thus

x_{\alpha}^{\delta}
is an interior point of
\mbox{dom}(F)
and consequently there holds the Euler equation

F^{\prime}(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})^{*}(F(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})-y^{\delta})+\alpha(x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{*})=0.

Manipulating this equation we then obtain

\displaystyle(\alpha I+A^{*}A)(x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=\alpha(x^{*}-x^{\dagger})+F^{\prime}(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})^{*}(y^{\delta}-F(x_{\alpha}^{\delta}))+A^{*}A(x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=\alpha(x^{*}-x^{\dagger})+(F^{\prime}(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})^{*}-A^{*})(y^{\delta}-F(x_{\alpha}^{\delta}))

\displaystyle\quad\,-A^{*}(F(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}-A(x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}))

which gives

\displaystyle x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=(\alpha I+A^{*}A)^{-1}\left(\alpha(x^{*}-x^{\dagger})+A^{*}(y^{\delta}-y)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\,+(\alpha I+A^{*}A)^{-1}(F^{\prime}(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})^{*}-A^{*})(y^{\delta}-F(x_{\alpha}^{\delta}))

\displaystyle\quad\,-(\alpha I+A^{*}A)^{-1}A^{*}(F(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})-y-A(x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger})).

By using the definition of
\hat{x}_{\alpha}^{\delta}
, we thus have

\displaystyle x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-\hat{x}_{\alpha}^{\delta}

\displaystyle=-(\alpha I+A^{*}A)^{-1}A^{*}\left(F(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})-y-A(x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger})\right)

\displaystyle\quad\,+(\alpha I+A^{*}A)^{-1}\left(F^{\prime}(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})^{*}-A^{*}\right)\left(y^{\delta}-F(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})\right).

(9)

Since
x_{\alpha}^{\delta}\in B_{\rho}(x^{\dagger})
, we may use Assumption
1
to obtain

\displaystyle x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-\hat{x}_{\alpha}^{\delta}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}(\alpha I+A^{*}A)^{-1}A^{*}A\left(R(x^{\dagger}+t(x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}),x^{\dagger})-I\right)(x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger})dt

\displaystyle\quad\,+(\alpha I+A^{*}A)^{-1}\left(I-R(x^{\dagger},x_{\alpha}^{\delta})^{*}\right)F^{\prime}(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})^{*}(y^{\delta}-F(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})).

and consequently

\displaystyle\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-\hat{x}_{\alpha}^{\delta}\|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}t\kappa_{0}\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}dt+\frac{1}{\alpha}\|I-R(x^{\dagger},x_{\alpha}^{\delta})\|\|F^{\prime}(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})^{*}(y^{\delta}-F(x_{\alpha}^{\delta}))\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{0}\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\kappa_{0}\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{*}\|\leq C_{0}\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|,

where
C_{0}:=(3\rho/2+\|x^{*}-x^{\dagger}\|)\kappa_{0}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\|\hat{x}_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|\leq(1+C_{0})\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|.

By virtue of (
8
) we then have

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\|\hat{x}_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|:\|y^{\delta}-y\|\leq\delta\right\}=o(\delta^{1/2}).

(10)

Based on (
10
) and the assumption on
\sigma(AA^{*})
, we will show that
for
\delta_{k}:=\lambda_{k}
there exists
y^{\delta_{k}}
satisfying
\|y^{\delta_{k}}-y\|\leq\delta_{k}

such that

\delta_{k}/\alpha_{k}\to 0\quad\mbox{ as }k\to\infty,

where
\alpha_{k}:=\alpha(\delta_{k},y^{\delta_{k}})
. To see this, let
\{F_{\lambda}\}
denote the spectral
family generated by the self-adjoint operator
AA^{*}
and for each
k
we set

G_{\lambda_{k}}:=F_{3\lambda_{k}/2}-F_{\lambda_{k}/2}.

Then
G_{\lambda_{k}}
is an orthogonal projection on
Y
and commutes with
(\alpha I+AA^{*})^{-1}
.
We then follow an idea from
[
1
,
9
]
and consider the perturbed data defined by

y^{\delta_{k}}:=y-\delta_{k}G_{\lambda_{k}}z_{k},

where

z_{k}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\|G_{\lambda_{k}}A(x^{\dagger}-x^{*})\|^{-1}A(x^{\dagger}-x^{*}),&\mbox{ if }G_{\lambda_{k}}A(x^{\dagger}-x^{*})\neq 0,\\
\mbox{arbitrary with }\|G_{\lambda_{k}}z_{k}\|=1,&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

Since
\lambda_{k}\in\sigma(AA^{*})
, such
y^{\delta_{k}}
is well-defined. It is clear that

\|y^{\delta_{k}}-y\|=\delta_{k}
. Let

\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}:=x^{\dagger}+\alpha_{k}(\alpha_{k}I+A^{*}A)^{-1}(x^{*}-x^{\dagger}).

From the definitions of
\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}^{\delta_{k}}
and
\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}

it follows that

\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}^{\delta_{k}}-\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}=-\delta_{k}(\alpha_{k}I+A^{*}A)^{-1}A^{*}G_{\lambda_{k}}z_{k}.

Hence, by using
G_{\lambda_{k}}^{2}=G_{\lambda_{k}}
, the definition of
z_{k}
and
\|G_{\lambda_{k}}z_{k}\|=1
, we have

\displaystyle 2\left\langle\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}-x^{\dagger},\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}^{\delta_{k}}-\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\alpha_{k}\delta_{k}\left\langle(\alpha_{k}I+AA^{*})^{-1}A(x^{*}-x^{\dagger}),(\alpha_{k}I+AA^{*})^{-1}G_{\lambda_{k}}z_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2\alpha_{k}\delta_{k}\left\langle(\alpha_{k}I+AA^{*})^{-1}G_{\lambda_{k}}A(x^{\dagger}-x^{*}),(\alpha_{k}I+AA^{*})^{-1}G_{\lambda_{k}}z_{k}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2\alpha_{k}\delta_{k}\|G_{\lambda_{k}}A(x^{\dagger}-x^{*})\|\|(\alpha_{k}I+AA^{*})^{-1}G_{\lambda_{k}}z_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 0

and

\displaystyle\|\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}^{\delta_{k}}-\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\delta_{k}^{2}\|(AA^{*})^{1/2}(\alpha_{k}I+AA^{*})^{-1}G_{\lambda_{k}}z_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\delta_{k}^{2}\int_{\lambda_{k}/2}^{3\lambda_{k}/2}\frac{\lambda}{(\alpha_{k}+\lambda)^{2}}d\|F_{\lambda}G_{\lambda_{k}}z_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\delta_{k}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{k}/2}{(\alpha_{k}+3\lambda_{k}/2)^{2}}\|G_{\lambda_{k}}z_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\delta_{k}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{k}/2}{(\alpha_{k}+3\lambda_{k}/2)^{2}}.

Therefore, by using the identity

\displaystyle\|\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}^{\delta_{k}}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}=\|\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\|\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}^{\delta_{k}}-\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}\|^{2}+2\left\langle\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}-x^{\dagger},\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}^{\delta_{k}}-\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}\right\rangle,

we obtain

\displaystyle\|\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}^{\delta_{k}}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}\geq\|\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}^{\delta_{k}}-\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}\|^{2}\geq\delta_{k}^{2}\frac{\lambda_{k}/2}{(\alpha_{k}+3\lambda_{k}/2)^{2}}=\frac{\delta_{k}^{2}/\lambda_{k}}{2(\alpha_{k}/\lambda_{k}+3/2)^{2}}.

(11)

Since
\delta_{k}=\lambda_{k}
, it then follows from (
10
) and (
11
) that

\frac{\delta_{k}}{2(\alpha_{k}/\delta_{k}+3/2)^{2}}\leq\|\hat{x}_{\alpha_{k}}^{\delta_{k}}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}=o(\delta_{k})

which implies that
\alpha_{k}/\delta_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
.

Finally we show
x^{\dagger}=x^{*}
. Let

C_{1}:=\sup_{x\in B_{\rho}(x^{\dagger})}\|F^{\prime}(x)\|

which is finite by Assumption
1
. By the definition of
\alpha_{k}
and
the Euler equation for
x_{\alpha_{k}}^{\delta_{k}}
we have

\displaystyle\|x_{\alpha_{k}}^{\delta_{k}}-x^{*}\|=\frac{1}{\alpha_{k}}\|F^{\prime}(x_{\alpha_{k}}^{\delta_{k}})^{*}(F(x_{\alpha_{k}}^{\delta_{k}})-y^{\delta_{k}})\|\leq\frac{C_{1}}{\alpha_{k}}\|F(x_{\alpha_{k}}^{\delta_{k}})-y^{\delta_{k}}\|\leq\frac{C_{1}\tau\delta_{k}}{\alpha_{k}}\to 0

as
k\to\infty
. Since (
8
) also implies
\|x_{\alpha_{k}}^{\delta_{k}}-x^{\dagger}\|\to 0
as
k\to\infty
,
we therefore have
x^{\dagger}=x^{*}
.
∎

The saturation result given in Theorem
2
covers not only Rule
1
but also other
possible parameter choice rules including the sequential discrepancy principle (
[
6
]
) below.

Rule 2

(Sequential discrepancy principle)
.

Let
\tau>1
,
\alpha_{0}>0
and
0<\gamma<1
be given numbers, and let

\Delta_{\gamma}:=\{\alpha_{0}\gamma^{j}:j=0,1,\cdots\}.

Define
\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})
to be the largest number in
\Delta_{\gamma}
such that

\|F(x_{\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|\leq\tau\delta
.

We remark that for nonlinear ill-posed problems there are some issues concerning Rule
1

which require serious consideration. First, the existence of
\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})
in Rule
1

is not always guaranteed in general. Furthermore, even if
such an
\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})
exists, additional work needs to be executed to find it which can be
time-consuming. The sequential discrepancy principle can resolve these issues automatically.
Indeed, by the definition of
x_{\alpha}^{\delta}
it is easy to see that
\lim_{\alpha\to 0}\|F(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|\leq\delta

which implies the existence of
\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})
by Rule
2
. According to Theorem
2
,
under merely Assumption
1
, Tikhonov regularization (
3
) with Rule

2
exhibits the saturation.

Next we will provide a saturation result under the following Lipschitz continuity
of the Fréchet derivative of the forward operator
F
.

Assumption 3
.

There is
\rho>0
such that
B_{\rho}(x^{\dagger})\subset\emph{dom}(F)
and
F
is Fréchet
differentiable on
B_{\rho}(x^{\dagger})
. Moreover, there is
L\geq 0
such that

\|F^{\prime}(x)-F^{\prime}(z)\|\leq L\|x-z\|,\qquad\forall x,z\in B_{\rho}(x^{\dagger}).

We will show that, under Assumption
3
, Tikhonov regularization (
3
)
with the regularization parameter chosen by Rule
1
or Rule
2
does not
possess the convergence rate
o(\delta^{1/2})
in the worst case scenario even if

x^{\dagger}-x^{*}\in\mbox{Ran}((A^{*}A)^{\nu})
with
\nu\geq 1/2
. We need the following
result which is essentially contained in the proof of
[
3
, Theorem 2.4]
,
see also
[
2
,
12
]
.

Lemma 3
.

Let Assumption
3
hold and let
x_{\alpha}^{\delta}
be a solution of (
3
)
with
x_{\alpha}^{\delta}\in B_{\rho}(x^{\dagger})
. If
x^{*}-x^{\dagger}=F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})^{*}u
for some
u\in Y

with
L\|u\|<1
, then

\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|\leq\frac{\delta+\alpha\|u\|}{\sqrt{\alpha(1-L\|u\|)}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|F(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|\leq\delta+2\alpha\|u\|.

(12)

Theorem 4
.

Let Assumption
3
hold and assume, for the operator
A:=F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})
, there exists a
sequence of positive numbers
\{\lambda_{k}\}\subset\sigma(AA^{*})
such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{k}=0
. Consider the Tikhonov regularization (
3
)
and let
\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})
be determined by either Rule
1
or Rule
2
with
\tau>1
.
If
x^{\dagger}-x^{*}=F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})^{*}u
for some
u\in\mathcal{N}(F^{\prime}(x^{\dagger})^{*})^{\perp}
with
L\|u\|<1

and if

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\|x_{\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|:\|y^{\delta}-y\|\leq\delta\right\}=o(\delta^{1/2})

(13)

as
\delta\to 0
, then
x^{\dagger}=x^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha:=\alpha(\delta,y^{\delta})
. From (
13
) it follows that
x_{\alpha}^{\delta}\in B_{\rho}(x^{\dagger})
for
small
\delta>0
. Thus, by virtue of (
2
) and Assumption
3
we have

\displaystyle\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-\hat{x}_{\alpha}^{\delta}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\alpha}}\|F(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})-y-A(x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger})\|+\frac{1}{\alpha}\|F^{\prime}(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})-A\|\|y^{\delta}-F(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4\sqrt{\alpha}}L\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\alpha}L\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|\|y^{\delta}-F(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})\|.

With the help of Lemma
3
we then have

\displaystyle\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-\hat{x}_{\alpha}^{\delta}\|\leq\frac{L(\delta+\alpha\|u\|)}{4\alpha\sqrt{1-L\|u\|}}\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|+\frac{1}{\alpha}L\left(\delta+2\alpha\|u\|\right)\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|.

(14)

We next claim that

\displaystyle\alpha\geq\frac{(\tau-1)\gamma\delta}{2\|u\|}.

(15)

Indeed, if
\alpha
is determined by Rule
1
, then by using the second
inequality in Lemma
3
we have

\tau\delta=\|F(x_{\alpha}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|\leq\delta+2\alpha\|u\|

which implies that
\delta\leq 2\alpha\|u\|/(\tau-1)
and hence (
15
) holds as
0<\gamma<1
.
If
\alpha
is determined by Rule
2
, then by using again the second inequality in
Lemma
3
we can obtain

\tau\delta<\|F(x_{\alpha/\gamma}^{\delta})-y^{\delta}\|\leq\delta+\frac{2\alpha}{\gamma}\|u\|

which implies again (
15
). Therefore, it follows from (
14
) and (
15
) that

\displaystyle\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-\hat{x}_{\alpha}^{\delta}\|\leq C_{0}L\|u\|\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|,

where

C_{0}:=2+\frac{2}{(\tau-1)\gamma}+\frac{2+(\tau-1)\gamma}{4(\tau-1)\gamma\sqrt{1-L\|u\|}}.

Consequently

\|\hat{x}_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|\leq(1+C_{0}L\|u\|)\|x_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|.

and thus we may use (
13
) to conclude

\displaystyle\sup\left\{\|\hat{x}_{\alpha}^{\delta}-x^{\dagger}\|:\|y^{\delta}-y\|\leq\delta\right\}=o(\delta^{1/2}).

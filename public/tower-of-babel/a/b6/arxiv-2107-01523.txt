1.
Introduction

A
weight

\omega
in this paper is a positive and integrable function on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. For
0<p<\infty
, the
weighted Bergman space
L^{p}_{a}(\omega)
is a linear space of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
satisfying

\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}:=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{p}\omega(z)dA(z)\Big)^{1/p}<\infty,

where
dA(z)=dxdy/\pi
is the normalized area measure on
\mathbb{D}
.
If
\omega(z)=\omega_{\alpha}(z):=(1+\alpha)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha},-1<\alpha<\infty
, then
L^{p}_{a}(\omega_{\alpha})
is known as standard weighted Bergman space in literatures. Moreover,
L_{a}^{p}(\omega_{0})
is the classical Bergman space, and is denoted by
L_{a}^{p}

[
Zhu07
]
. Recall for
0<p<\infty
, the
Hardy space
consists of analytic functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
such that

\|f\|_{H^{p}}:=\sup_{0\leq r<1}\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta\Big)^{1/p}<\infty.

Let
g
be an analytic function on
\mathbb{D}
. We consider the
Volterra type integration operator

J_{g}
with symbol
g

J_{g}(f)(z):=\int_{0}^{z}f(\lambda)g^{\prime}(\lambda)d\lambda,\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

where
f
is an analytic function on
\mathbb{D}
.

Pommerenke
[
Pom77
]
proved that
J_{g}
is bounded on Hardy space
H^{2}
if and only if
g
is of bounded mean oscillation. The same characterization of the boundedness
of
J_{g}
on the general Hardy space
H^{p}
was obtained by Aleman and Siskakis
[
AS95
]
for
p\geq 1
and by Aleman and Cima
[
AC01
]
for
0<p<1
. In
[
AC01
]
, Aleman and Cima further considered any pair of
(p,q)\in(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)
and studied the symbol
g
such that
J_{g}:H^{p}\to H^{q}
is bounded. Analogous characterization of the symbol
g
for
J_{g}
to be bounded on Bergman spaces can be found in
[
Sis06
]
. Aleman and Siskakis
[
AS97
]
established the result on a large class of weighted Bergman spaces, which includes the class of standard weights
\omega_{\alpha}
as special examples. Pau and Peláez
[
PP10
]
described the
L^{p}_{a}(\omega)
-
L^{q}_{a}(\omega)
boundedness of
J_{g}
for
0<p,q<\infty
when
\omega
is rapidly decreasing, while Peláez and Rättyä
[
PR14
]
got the corresponding characterizations when
\omega
is regular or rapidly increasing weight.
Wu
[
Wu11
]
established boundedness criterions of
J_{g}
from
L_{a}^{p}(\omega_{\alpha})
to
H^{q}
for all
(p,q)\in(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)
but
0<q<\min\{2,p\}<\infty
. Very recently, this gap was filled by Miihkinen, Pau, Perälä and Wang in
[
MPPW20
]
using analysis on tent spaces and some other delicate techniques.
Moreover, by modifying the proofs of the boundedness results, one can get the corresponding compactness results (
[
Sis06
,
Wu11
,
CPW21
]
).

The goal of the present paper is to study Volterra type integration operators between weighted Bergman spaces
L_{a}^{p}(\omega)
and Hardy spaces
H^{q}
for
0<p,q<\infty
. Here the weight
\omega
is a
\mathcal{D}
weight. Recall that the class of
\mathcal{D}
weights

[
PR19
]
consists of positive integrable radial functions
\omega
on
\mathbb{D}
such that
\omega
satisfies
both forward and reverse doubling conditions
, i.e., there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}\in(1,\infty)
and
C_{3}\in[1,\infty)
,

C_{1}\widehat{\omega}\bigg(1-\frac{1-r}{C_{2}}\bigg)\leq\widehat{\omega}(r)\leq C_{3}\widehat{\omega}\bigg(\frac{1+r}{2}\bigg),\quad r\in[0,1),

where
\widehat{\omega}(z)=\int_{|z|}^{1}\omega(r)dr
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
. The class of
\mathcal{D}
weights can be regarded as the largest class of radial weights
\omega
in some sense that most of the results over
L_{a}^{p}(\omega_{\alpha})
can still hold on
L_{a}^{p}(\omega)
. For instance, the Littlewood-Paley estimate holds for weighted Bergman spaces induced by radial weights if and only if
\omega
is a
\mathcal{D}
weight
[
PR19
]
.

Let
H(\mathbb{D})
be the set of analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}
. To describe our results, let us introduce two function spaces, which can be regarded as generalized Bloch spaces (little Bloch spaces). For a radial weight
\omega
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
, let

\mathcal{B}^{\gamma,\omega}=\Big\{f\in H(\mathbb{D}):\sup\limits_{z\in\mathbb{D}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\gamma}}{\omega(z)}|f^{\prime}(z)|<\infty\Big\}

and

\mathcal{B}_{0}^{\gamma,\omega}=\Big\{f\in H(\mathbb{D}):\lim\limits_{|z|\to 1^{-}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\gamma}}{\omega(z)}|f^{\prime}(z)|=0\Big\}.

Let
\mathbb{T}
be the unit circle. For an interval
I\subset\mathbb{T}
, we define the
Carleson square

S(I)=\{z\in\mathbb{D}:z/|z|\in I,~1-|I|\leq|z|<1\},

where
|\cdot|
is the normalized arc measure on
\mathbb{T}
.
For
0<s<\infty
, a positive measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{D}
is a
s
-Carleson measure
(vanishing
s
-Carleson measure)

[
Zhu07
, Section 9.2]
if

\sup\limits_{I\subseteq\mathbb{T}}\frac{\mu(S(I))}{|I|^{s}}<\infty\quad\Big(\lim\limits_{|I|\to 0}\frac{\mu(S(I))}{|I|^{s}}=0\Big).

The (vanishing)
1
-Carleson measure is called (vanishing) Carleson measure.
Let
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
and
0<\alpha<\infty
, the
Stolz non-tangential approach region
is given by

\Gamma_{\alpha}(\xi)=\bigg\{z\in\mathbb{D}:|z-\xi|<(\alpha+1)(1-|z|)\bigg\}.

For convenience, we denote
\Gamma_{1}(\xi)
by
\Gamma(\xi)
. Let
0<p<\infty
,
0<q\leq\infty
and
\nu
be a positive Borel measure. The
tent space

T_{q,\nu}^{p}

consists of
\nu
-equivalence classes of
\nu
-measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{D}
such that

\|f\|_{T_{q,\nu}^{p}}^{p}:=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\int_{\Gamma_{\alpha}(\xi)}|f(z)|^{q}d\nu(z)\bigg)^{\frac{p}{q}}d\xi<\infty,\quad q\in(0,\infty)

and

\|f\|_{T_{q,\nu}^{p}}^{p}:=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\textmd{ess}\sup_{z\in\Gamma_{\alpha}(\xi)}|f(z)|\bigg)^{p}d\xi<\infty,\quad q=\infty,

where the essential supremum is taken with respect to
\nu
.
Note that the tent spaces
T_{q,\nu}^{p}
does not depend on the choice of
\Gamma_{\alpha}

[
MPPW20
]
.
We denote
T_{q,\nu}^{p}
by
T_{q,\omega,\beta}^{p}
, where
d\nu(z)=\Big(\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}{\omega(z)}\Big)^{\beta}dA(z)
and
0<\beta<\infty
.

Our main results are the followings. The first two results characterize the boundedness and compactness of Volterra type integration operator

J_{g}
from weighted Bergman spaces to Hardy spaces.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<p,q<\infty
,
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
. The following statements hold:

(A):

If
0<p\leq\min\{2,q\}
or
2<p<q<\infty
, then
J_{g}:L_{a}^{p}(\omega)\to H^{q}
is bounded if and only if

g\in\mathcal{B}^{\gamma,\widehat{\omega}^{\frac{1}{p}}},

where
\gamma=1+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}
;

(B):

If
2<p=q<\infty
, then
J_{g}:L_{a}^{p}(\omega)\to H^{q}
is bounded if and only if the positive measure

\mu_{g}

is a Carleson measure, where
d\mu_{g}(z)=|g^{\prime}(z)|^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{p+2}{p-2}}}{\widehat{\omega}(z)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}}dA(z)
;

(C):

If
0<q<\infty
and
p>\max\{2,q\}
, then

J_{g}:L_{a}^{p}(\omega)\to H^{q}
is bounded if and only if

g^{\prime}\in T_{\frac{2p}{p-2},\widehat{\omega},\frac{2}{p-2}}^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}
;

(D):

If
0<q<p\leq 2
, then
J_{g}:L_{a}^{p}(\omega)\to H^{q}
is bounded if and only if

B_{g,\omega}\in L^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}(\mathbb{T})
, where

B_{g,\omega}(\xi)=\sup\limits_{z\in\Gamma(\xi)}|g^{\prime}(z)|\frac{(1-|z|^{2})}{\widehat{\omega}(z)^{\frac{1}{p}}},~\xi\in\mathbb{T}.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
0<p,q<\infty
,
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
. The following statements hold:

(A):

If
0<p\leq\min\{2,q\}
or
2<p<q<\infty
, then
J_{g}:L_{a}^{p}(\omega)\to H^{q}
is compact if and only if

g\in\mathcal{B}_{0}^{\gamma,\widehat{\omega}^{\frac{1}{p}}},

where
\gamma=1+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}
;

(B):

If
2<p=q<\infty
, then
J_{g}:L_{a}^{p}(\omega)\to H^{q}
is compact if and only if the positive measure

\mu_{g}

is a vanishing Carleson measure, where
d\mu_{g}(z)=|g^{\prime}(z)|^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{p+2}{p-2}}}{\widehat{\omega}(z)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}}dA(z)
;

(C):

If
0<q<\infty
and
p>\max\{2,q\}
, then
J_{g}:L_{a}^{p}(\omega)\to H^{q}
is compact if and only if

g^{\prime}\in T_{\frac{2p}{p-2},\widehat{\omega},\frac{2}{p-2}}^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}
;

(D):

If
0<q<p\leq 2
, then
J_{g}:L_{a}^{p}(\omega)\to H^{q}
is compact if and only if

\lim\limits_{r\to 1^{-}}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sup_{z\in\Gamma(\xi)\backslash r\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}}{\widehat{\omega}(z)^{\frac{q}{p-q}}}d\xi=0.

The following result gives descriptions for the boundedness and compactness of
J_{g}
from Hardy spaces to weighted Bergman spaces. For a positive measure
\mu
, define

\widetilde{\mu}(\xi)=\int_{\Gamma(\xi)}\frac{d\mu(\lambda)}{1-|\lambda|^{2}},\quad\xi\in\mathbb{T}.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
0<p,q<\infty
. For
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
, let
d\mu_{g}(z)=|g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{q}\omega(z)dA(z)
. Then

(A):

If
0<p\leq q<\infty
, then
J_{g}:H^{p}\to L_{a}^{q}(\omega)
is bounded (compact) if and only if
\mu_{g}
is a
q/p
-Carleson measure (vanishing
q/p
-Carleson measure);

(B):

If
0<q<p<\infty
, then
J_{g}:H^{p}\to L_{a}^{q}(\omega)
is compact if and only if

J_{g}:H^{p}\to L_{a}^{q}(\omega)
is bounded if and only if
\widetilde{\mu_{g}}\in L^{\frac{p}{p-q}}(\mathbb{T})
.

Remark 1.1
.

Theorem
1.1
-Theorem
1.3
generalize the recent work of
[
MPPW20
,
CPW21
]
from standard weights
\omega_{\alpha}(z)=(1+\alpha)(1-|z|^{2})^{\alpha}dA(z),-1<\alpha<\infty
to
\mathcal{D}
weights.
The main strategies are based on a combination of the basic ideas in
[
Wu11
,
MPPW20
,
CPW21
]
. Nevertheless, there are two additional obstacles we need to overcome. The first one is the lack of explicit expressions for Bergman kernels of
\mathcal{D}
weighted Bergman spaces. In addition, the parameter
\alpha
plays important roles in
[
MPPW20
,
CPW21
]
for standard weighted Bergman spaces.
We use some delicate analysis of the weights to capture the suitable information from
\omega
instead of
\alpha
.

Remark 1.2
.

Since the proof of boundedness (Theorem
1.1
) and compactness (Theorem
1.2
) of
J_{g}
share several overlaps, we just present detailed proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Throughout this paper, for a radial weight
\omega
, we denote
\omega(E)=\int_{E}\omega(z)dA(z)
, for any measurable set
E\subseteq\mathbb{D}
. And we will use
a\lesssim b
to represent that there exists a constant
C=C(\cdot)>0
satisfying
a\leq Cb
,
a\gtrsim b
if
there exists a constant
C=C(\cdot)>0
satisfying
a\geq Cb
, where the constant
C(\cdot)
depends on the parameters indicated in the parenthesis, varying under different circumstances. Moreover, if
a\lesssim b
and
b\gtrsim a
, then we denote by
a\asymp b
. Besides, for a number
p>1
, let
p^{\prime}
be the conjugate index of
p
such that
\frac{1}{p}
+
\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}
=1.

2.
From Bergman space into Hardy space

The main goal in this section is to prove Theorem
1.2
. The whole proof is divided into four parts.

2.1.
Case A

First we are devoted to dealing with the case when
0<p\leq\min\{2,q\}
or
2<p<q<\infty
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume
0<p\leq\min\{2,q\}
or
2<p<q<\infty
. Let
\omega
be a
\mathcal{D}
weight and
\gamma=1+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}
. Then the Volterra type integration operator
J_{g}:L_{a}^{p}(\omega)\to H^{q}
is compact if and only if

g\in\mathcal{B}_{0}^{\gamma,\widehat{\omega}^{1/p}},

where
\widehat{\omega}(z)=\int_{|z|}^{1}\omega(r)dr
.

To show Proposition
2.1
, we first give some useful lemmas.
By a disk version of Calderón’s theorem, one has the following:

Lemma 2.2
.

[
Pav13
, Theorem 1.3]
Suppose
f
is analytic on
\mathbb{D}
with
f(0)=0
. If
0<p<\infty
, then

\|f\|_{H^{p}}^{p}\asymp\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\int_{\Gamma(\xi)}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{2}dA(z)\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}}d\xi.

The following Dirichlet-type embedding theorem can be found in
[
MPPW20
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
f
is analytic on
\mathbb{D}
with
f(0)=0
. If
0<q\leq 2
, then

(2.1)

\|f\|_{H^{q}}\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{L_{a}^{q}(\omega_{q-1})}.

If
0<p<q<\infty
, then

(2.2)

\|f\|_{H^{q}}\lesssim\|f^{\prime}\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega_{p+\frac{p}{q}-2})}.

Now we recall two classes of weights which have close relations with
\mathcal{D}
weights. A weight
\omega
is called
regular

[
PR14
]
if
\omega
is continuous and radial on
\mathbb{D}
such that

\psi_{\omega}(r)\asymp(1-r)
for
0\leq r<1
, where
\psi_{\omega}(r)=\frac{\widehat{\omega}(r)}{\omega(r)}
is the distortion function, which was introduced in
[
Sis00
]
. We denote the class of regular weights by
\mathcal{R}
. And a weight
\omega
is a
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
weight

[
PRS18
]
if there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\widehat{\omega}(r)\leq C\widehat{\omega}(\frac{1+r}{2}),\quad\textmd{for~all}~r\in[0,1).

Note that
\mathcal{R}\subsetneq\mathcal{D}\subsetneq\widehat{\mathcal{D}}

[
PR14
,
PRS18
,
PR19
]
. Here we list some useful properties of
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
,
\mathcal{D}
and regular weights which are important in our subsequent work.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
PRS18
, Theorem C]

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\omega
be a
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
weight. Then the reproducing kenel of
L_{a}^{2}(\omega)
at
z
has the following estimates,

\|K_{z}^{\omega}\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega)}^{p}\asymp\int_{|z|}^{1}\frac{dt}{\widehat{\omega}(t)^{p-1}(1-t)^{p}},\quad|z|\to 1^{-}.

From Lemma
2.4
, we get

(2.3)

\|K_{z}^{\omega}\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega)}^{p}\asymp\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(z)^{p-1}(1-|z|)^{p-1}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

By
[
PRS18
, Proposition 5]
and its proof, we have

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and
\omega
be a
\mathcal{D}
weight. Then
\kappa(z)=(1-|z|)^{-1}\widehat{\omega}(z)
is regular and
\|f\|_{L^{p}(\omega)}\asymp\|f\|_{L^{p}(\kappa)}
, for each analytic function
f
on
\mathbb{D}
.

Denote by
B(z,r)
and
D(z,r)
the Euclidean disk and the pseudo-hyperbolic disk centered at
z
with radius
r
respectively. The next result shows a nice property of regular weights, which can be found in page 8 of
[
PR14
]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\omega
be a regular weight.
Then for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
r\in(0,1)
,

\omega(z)\asymp\omega(u),\quad\textmd{for~all}~u\in D(z,r).

Combine Lemma
2.5
with Lemma
2.6
, we get the following result.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and
r\in(0,1)
. Then for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
,

\widehat{\omega}(z)\asymp\widehat{\omega}(u),\quad\textmd{for~all}~u\in D(z,r).

Now we are ready to prove Proposition
2.1
.

Proof of Proposition
2.1
.

Sufficiency. Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be any sequence in
L_{a}^{p}(\omega)
such that
\|f_{j}\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega)}\leq 1
and
f_{j}
uniformly converges to 0 on any compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
. If
g\in\mathcal{B}_{0}^{\gamma,\widehat{\omega}^{1/p}}
, then we will show

\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\|J_{g}(f_{j})\|_{H^{q}}=0.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
r\in(0,1)
such that
for any
z\in\mathbb{D}
with
r\leq|z|<1
, it holds that

(2.4)

\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\gamma}}{\widehat{\omega}(z)^{1/p}}|g^{\prime}(z)|<\varepsilon.

Moreover, we can find a constant
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
j>k
,
|f_{j}|<\varepsilon
on
r\mathbb{D}:=\{z:|z|<r\}
.
By Lemma
2.3
, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain

\|J_{g}(f_{j})\|_{H^{q}}\lesssim\|(J_{g}f_{j})^{\prime}\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega_{p\gamma-1})}.

Together with Lemma
2.5
and (
2.4
), for any
j>k
, we get

\displaystyle\|J_{g}(f_{j})\|_{H^{q}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\bigg(\int_{r\mathbb{D}}|f_{j}(z)|^{p}\frac{\widehat{\omega}(z)}{1-|z|}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{p}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{p\gamma}}{\widehat{\omega}(z)}dA(z)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle+\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{D}\backslash r\mathbb{D}}|f_{j}(z)|^{p}\frac{\widehat{\omega}(z)}{1-|z|}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{p}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{p\gamma}}{\widehat{\omega}(z)}dA(z)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon+\varepsilon\|f_{j}\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega)}\lesssim\varepsilon.

Necessity. Assume that
J_{g}:L_{a}^{p}(\omega)\to H^{q}
is compact.
Let
\alpha>\frac{1}{p}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
. Take testing functions

f_{\lambda}(z)=\frac{(K_{\lambda}^{\omega}(z))^{\alpha}}{\|K_{\lambda}^{\omega}\|_{L_{a}^{\alpha+1-\frac{1}{p}}(\omega)}^{\alpha+1-\frac{1}{p}}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Let
\omega_{2n+1}=\int_{0}^{1}\omega(r)r^{2n+1}dr,~n\in\mathbb{N}\bigcup\{0\}
. It follows from
[
PR16
, formula (20)]
that

|K_{\lambda}^{\omega}(z)|\leq\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|z|^{n}}{2\omega_{2n+1}}\asymp\frac{1}{\omega_{1}}+\int_{0}^{|z|}\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(t)(1-t)^{2}}dt\lesssim\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(z)(1-|z|)},\quad|z|\to 1^{-}.

Together with (
2.3
), we obtain

|f_{\lambda}(z)|\asymp\widehat{\omega}(\lambda)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}}(1-|\lambda|)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}}|K_{\lambda}^{\omega}(z)|^{\alpha}\lesssim\frac{\widehat{\omega}(\lambda)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}}(1-|\lambda|)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}}}{\widehat{\omega}(z)^{\alpha}(1-|z|)^{\alpha}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Then for any compact subset
E\subseteq\mathbb{D}
, we have

\lim\limits_{|\lambda|\to 1^{-}}\sup\limits_{z\in E}|f_{\lambda}(z)|=0.

Moreover, from (
2.3
), we know

\|f_{\lambda}\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega)}\asymp\widehat{\omega}(\lambda)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}}(1-\lambda)^{\alpha-\frac{1}{p}}\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{D}}|K_{\lambda}^{\omega}(z)|^{\alpha p}\omega(z)dA(z)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\asymp 1.

Therefore, we deduce

\lim\limits_{|\lambda|\to 1^{-}}\|J_{g}(f_{\lambda})\|_{H^{q}}=0.

For any
z,\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
,
by using a classical result of the pointwise estimate for
(J_{g}f_{\lambda})^{\prime}
(see
[
Gar07
, Page 85-86]
), we get

(2.5)

\displaystyle|f_{\lambda}(z)g^{\prime}(z)|=|(J_{g}f_{\lambda})^{\prime}(z)|\lesssim\frac{1}{(1-|z|^{2})^{1+\frac{1}{q}}}\|J_{g}f_{\lambda}\|_{H^{q}}.

By choosing
z=\lambda
in (
2.5
), together with (
2.3
), we deduce

\frac{(1-|\lambda|^{2})^{\gamma}}{\widehat{\omega}(\lambda)^{\frac{1}{p}}}|g^{\prime}(\lambda)|\lesssim\|J_{g}f_{\lambda}\|_{H^{q}},

from which it follows that

\lim\limits_{|\lambda|\to 1^{-}}\frac{(1-|\lambda|^{2})^{\gamma}}{\widehat{\omega}(\lambda)^{\frac{1}{p}}}|g^{\prime}(\lambda)|=0.

Thus, we complete the proof.
∎

2.2.
Case B

Now we turn to deal with the case
2<p=q<\infty
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, recall the Rademacher functions

r_{k}(t):=\textmd{sgn}\{\sin(2^{k}\pi t)\},~t\in[0,1].
The following Khinchine’s inequality and Kahane’s inequality
[
Lue93
]
are useful in our subsequent work.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
0<p<\infty
. Then for any sequence of complex numbers

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}h(\xi)\big(A_{\nu_{g},\alpha}^{2}(f)(\xi)\big)^{2}d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{1}{1-|z|}\int_{I_{\alpha}(z)}h(\xi)d\xi|f(z)|^{2}d\nu_{g}(z).

Notice that for any
I\subseteq\mathbb{T}
, there exists
a\in\mathbb{D}
such that
I=I_{\alpha}(a)
.
For any
a\in\mathbb{D}
, take testing functions

h_{a}(\xi)=\chi_{I_{\alpha}(a)}(\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{T}.

Notice that

(2.21)

\|h_{a}\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-2}}(\mathbb{T})}\asymp(1-|a|)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}

and

(2.22)

\int_{I_{\alpha}(z)}h_{a}(\xi)d\xi\asymp 1-|z|,\quad z\in\wedge_{\alpha}(I_{\alpha}(a)).

Combining (
2.20
), (
2.21
), (
2.22
) with
\textmd{H}\ddot{\textmd{o}}\textmd{lder's}
inequality, for any
a\in\mathbb{D}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\int_{\wedge_{\alpha}(I_{\alpha}(a))}|f(z)|^{2}d\nu_{g}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{1}{1-|z|}\int_{I_{\alpha}(z)}h_{a}(\xi)d\xi|f(z)|^{2}d\nu_{g}(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}}h_{a}(\xi)\big(A_{\nu_{g},\alpha}^{2}(f)(\xi)\big)^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\|h_{a}\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-2}}(\mathbb{T})}\|A_{\nu_{g},\alpha}^{2}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle\asymp(1-|a|)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}\|A_{\nu_{g},\alpha}^{2}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}^{2},

from which it follows that

(2.23)

\frac{\int_{\wedge_{\alpha}(I_{\alpha}(a))}|f(z)|^{2}d\nu_{g}(z)}{(1-|a|)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}}\lesssim\|A_{\nu_{g},\alpha}^{2}(f)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}.

For any
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
and
\beta>0
, consider test functions

f_{\zeta,\beta}(z):=\frac{(1-|\zeta|)^{\frac{\beta+1}{p}}}{(1-\bar{\zeta}z)^{\frac{\beta+1}{p}}\widehat{\omega}(\zeta)^{\frac{1}{p}}(1-|\zeta|)^{\frac{1}{p}}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Recall Lemma A(vi) in
[
PRS18
]
states that there exists
\gamma_{0}=\gamma_{0}(\omega)>0
such that for any
\gamma\geq\gamma_{0}
, it holds that

\int_{\mathbb{D}}\frac{\omega(z)}{|1-\bar{\zeta}z|^{\gamma+1}}dA(z)\asymp\frac{\widehat{\omega}(\zeta)}{(1-|\zeta|)^{\gamma}},\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{D}.

Hence, we can choose
\gamma
large enough such that
\|f_{\zeta,\gamma}\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega)}\asymp 1
and
f_{\zeta,\gamma}
converges uniformly on any compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
as
|\zeta|\to 1^{-}
. Combining with (
2.23
), we obtain that for any
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
and
a\in\mathbb{D}
, it holds that

(2.24)

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\wedge_{\alpha}(I_{\alpha}(a))}|f_{\zeta,\gamma}(z)|^{2}d\nu_{g}(z)}{(1-|a|)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}}\lesssim\|A_{\nu_{g},\alpha}^{2}(f_{\zeta,\gamma})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}.

In view of the compactness of
J_{g}
and (
2.13
), for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
r_{1}\in(0,1)
such that for any
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
with
r_{1}\leq|\zeta|<1
, it holds that
\|A_{\nu_{g},\alpha}^{2}(f_{\zeta,\gamma})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}<\varepsilon
.
Notice that there exists
r_{2}\in(r_{1},1)
such that for any
a\in\mathbb{D}
with
r_{2}\leq|a|<1
,

D(z,r)\subseteq\mathbb{D}\backslash r_{1}\mathbb{D},\quad\textmd{for~any}~z\in\wedge_{\alpha}(I_{\alpha}(a)).

For
a\in\mathbb{D}
with
r_{2}\leq|a|<1
, take
\zeta\in\mathbb{D}
with

\zeta\in\bigcup_{z\in\wedge_{\alpha}(I_{\alpha}(a))}D(z,r).

Together with (
2.24
), for any
a\in\mathbb{D}
with
r_{2}\leq|a|<1
, we deduce

(2.25)

\frac{\int_{\wedge_{\alpha}(I_{\alpha}(a))}\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(z)^{\frac{2}{p}}(1-|z|)^{\frac{2}{p}}}d\nu_{g}(z)}{(1-|a|)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}}\lesssim\varepsilon.

Next we show that for any
f\in L_{a}^{p}(\omega)
,
|f(z)|^{2}d\nu_{g}(z)
is a vanishing
\frac{p-2}{p}
-Carleson measure.
By the subharmonicity of
|f|^{p}
, Lemma
2.5
, Lemma
2.7
and (
2.25
), for any
a\in\mathbb{D}
with
r_{2}\leq|a|<1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{\wedge_{\alpha}(I_{\alpha}(a))}|f(z)|^{2}d\nu_{g}(z)}{(1-|a|)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\int_{\wedge_{\alpha}(I_{\alpha}(a))}\bigg(\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(z)(1-|z|)}\int_{D(z,r)}|f(u)|^{p}\frac{\widehat{\omega}(u)}{1-|u|}dA(u)\bigg)^{\frac{2}{p}}d\nu_{g}(z)}{(1-|a|)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega)}^{2}\frac{\int_{\wedge_{\alpha}(I_{\alpha}(a))}\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(z)^{\frac{2}{p}}(1-|z|)^{\frac{2}{p}}}d\nu_{g}(z)}{(1-|a|)^{\frac{p-2}{p}}}

(2.26)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon\|f\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega)}^{2}.

It follows from Proposition
2.10
and (
2.2
) that there exits

such that

is a vanishing Carleson measure.
By the subharmonicity of
|g^{\prime}|^{2}
, we deduce

|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\frac{(1-|z|)^{2}}{\widehat{\omega}(z)}\lesssim\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(z)(1-|z|)}\int_{D(z,t)}|g^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{2}(1-|\zeta|)dA(\zeta)=\frac{\nu_{g}(D(z,t))}{\widehat{\omega}(z)(1-|z|)},

which yields that

\bigg(|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}\frac{(1-|z|)^{2}}{\widehat{\omega}(z)}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{p-2}}\frac{\widehat{\omega}(z)}{1-|z|}dA(z)

is a vanishing Carleson measure.
Thus, we conclude that
\mu_{g}
is a vanishing Carleson measure.
∎

Case C

For a separated sequence

where

For

then we say the sequence

we say the sequence
\{b_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\in T_{\infty}^{p}(\{a_{k}\})
.

The following two results characterize the dual space and the factorization of tent spaces of sequences respectively (see
[
Ars99
]
and
[
MPPW20
]
).

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\{a_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a separated sequence.

•

If
1<q<\infty
, then
the dual space of

can be identified with
T_{q^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}(\{a_{k}\})
via the pairing,

\langle\{b_{k}\},\{d_{k}\}\rangle_{T_{2}^{2}(\{a_{k}\})}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}\bar{d_{k}}(1-|a_{k}|^{2}),\quad\{b_{k}\}\in T_{q}^{p}(\{a_{k}\}),~\{d_{k}\}\in T_{q^{\prime}}^{p^{\prime}}(\{a_{k}\}).

•

If
0<q\leq 1
, the the dual space of
T_{q}^{p}(\{a_{k}\})
can be identified with
T_{\infty}^{p^{\prime}}(\{a_{k}\})
via the pairing,

\langle\{b_{k}\},\{d_{k}\}\rangle_{T_{2}^{2}(\{a_{k}\})}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}\bar{d_{k}}(1-|a_{k}|^{2}),\quad\{b_{k}\}\in T_{q}^{p}(\{a_{k}\}),~\{d_{k}\}\in T_{\infty}^{p^{\prime}}(\{a_{k}\}).

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
0<p,q<\infty
and
\{a_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be an
r
-lattice. If
p<p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
,
q<q_{1},q_{2}<\infty

\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}=\frac{1}{p}
and
\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}=\frac{1}{q}

Then

. Moreover, for any

\inf\|\{c_{k}\}\|_{T_{q_{1}}^{p_{1}}(\{a_{k}\})}\cdot\|\{d_{k}\}\|_{T_{q_{2}}^{p_{2}}(\{a_{k}\})}\lesssim\|\{b_{k}\}\|_{T_{q}^{p}(\{a_{k}\})},

where the infimum above is taken over all possible factorizations of
\{b_{k}\}
.

We also need the following useful estimates.

Lemma 2.16
.

[

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
. Assume that
\{a_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{D}\backslash\{0\}

is a separated sequence. Then

F:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}c_{k}\frac{K_{a_{k}}^{\omega}}{\|K_{a_{k}}^{\omega}\|_{L_{a}^{2-\frac{1}{p}}(\omega)}^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\in H(\mathbb{D}).

Moreover,
\|F\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega)}\lesssim\|\{c_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\|_{l^{p}}
for all
\{c_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\in l^{p}
.

Proof.

Let
\kappa(z)=\frac{\widehat{\omega}(z)}{1-|z|},~z\in\mathbb{D}
and
\{c_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\in l^{p}
. From Lemma
2.5
, we know that
\kappa
is regular. Assume that
\rho\in(0,1)
. For any
z\in\mathbb{D}
with
0<|z|\leq\rho
, it follows from
Lemma
2.6
,
\textmd{H}\ddot{\textmd{o}}\textmd{lder's}
inequality and (
2.3
) that

\displaystyle|F(z)|\leq

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\bigg[\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{|K_{a_{k}}^{\omega}(z)|}{\|K_{a_{k}}^{\omega}\|_{L_{a}^{2-\frac{1}{p}}(\omega)}^{2-\frac{1}{p}}}\bigg)^{p^{\prime}}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\sup\limits_{k\geq 1}|K_{a_{k}}^{\omega}(z)|\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\widehat{\omega}(a_{k})(1-|a_{k}|)\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\asymp

\displaystyle\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}\sup\limits_{k\geq 1}|K_{a_{k}}^{\omega}(z)|\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\kappa(D(a_{k},r))\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\bigg(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|c_{k}|^{p}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
r\in(0,1)
and
C
is a constant which only depends on
p,\rho,r
and
\omega
.
Thus, we know that
F
is analytic on
\mathbb{D}
. Take Lemma
2.5
into account, we deduce

\|F\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega)}\asymp\|F\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\kappa)}.

Since
(L_{a}^{p}(\kappa))^{*}\simeq L_{a}^{p^{\prime}}(\kappa)
(see
[
PRS18
, Theorem D]
), together with the property of subharmonicity, similar analysis as above gives the desired result.
∎

Now we give the main result of this subsection.

Proposition 2.18
.

Let
0<q<\infty
,
p>\max\{2,q\}
,
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
and
g\in H(\mathbb{D})
. Then the Volterra type
integration operator
J_{g}:L_{a}^{p}(\omega)\to H^{q}
is compact if and only if
J_{g}
is bounded if and only if

g^{\prime}\in T_{\frac{2p}{p-2},\widehat{\omega},\frac{2}{p-2}}^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}
. Moreover,

\|J_{g}\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega)\to H^{q}}\asymp\|g^{\prime}\|_{T_{\frac{2p}{p-2},\widehat{\omega},\frac{2}{p-2}}^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}}
.

Proof.

We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1.
Let
g
be an analytic function on
\mathbb{D}
with satisfying

g^{\prime}\in T_{\frac{2p}{p-2},\widehat{\omega},\frac{2}{p-2}}^{\frac{pq}{p-q}},

we will show that

J_{g}:L_{a}^{p}(\omega)\to H^{q}
is a compact operator. Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be any sequence in
L_{a}^{p}(\omega)
with
\|f_{j}\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega)}\leq 1
and
f_{j}
converges to 0 uniformly on any compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
. We are devoted to proving

(2.27)

\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\|J_{g}(f_{j})\|_{H^{q}}=0.

Since
g^{\prime}\in T_{\frac{2p}{p-2},\widehat{\omega},\frac{2}{p-2}}^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}
, then
for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
r_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

(2.28)

\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\int_{\Gamma(\xi)\backslash r_{0}\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\bigg(\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}{\widehat{\omega}(z)}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}dA(z)\bigg)^{\frac{q(p-2)}{2(p-q)}}d\xi\bigg]^{\frac{p-q}{p}}<\varepsilon.

Moreover, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
j>k_{0}
,
|f_{j}|^{q}<\varepsilon
on
r_{0}\mathbb{D}
.
Combining Lemma
2.5
, (
2.6
) with Fubini’s theorem, for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we deduce

(2.29)

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\int_{\Gamma(\xi)\backslash r_{0}\mathbb{D}}|f_{j}(z)|^{p}\frac{\widehat{\omega}(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}dA(z)d\xi\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{j}(z)|^{p}\frac{\widehat{\omega}(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}\int_{I(z)}d\xi dA(z)\asymp\|f_{j}\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega)}^{p}.

By Lemma
2.2
, (
2.28
), (
2.29
) and
\textmd{H}\ddot{\textmd{o}}\textmd{lder's}
inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\|J_{g}(f_{j})\|_{H^{q}}^{q}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\int_{\Gamma(\xi)\bigcap r_{0}\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}|f_{j}(z)|^{2}dA(z)\bigg)^{\frac{q}{2}}d\xi+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\int_{\Gamma(\xi)\backslash r_{0}\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{2}|f_{j}(z)|^{2}dA(z)\bigg)^{\frac{q}{2}}d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon+\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg[\bigg(\int_{\Gamma(\xi)\backslash r_{0}\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\bigg(\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}{\widehat{\omega}(z)}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}dA(z)\bigg)^{\frac{q(p-2)}{2p}}

\displaystyle\cdot\bigg(\int_{\Gamma(\xi)\backslash r_{0}\mathbb{D}}|f_{j}(z)|^{p}\frac{\widehat{\omega}(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}dA(z)\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\bigg]d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\int_{\Gamma(\xi)\backslash r_{0}\mathbb{D}}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\bigg(\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}{\widehat{\omega}(z)}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}dA(z)\bigg)^{\frac{q(p-2)}{2(p-q)}}d\xi\bigg]^{\frac{p-q}{p}}

\displaystyle\cdot\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\int_{\Gamma(\xi)\backslash r_{0}\mathbb{D}}|f_{j}(z)|^{p}\frac{\widehat{\omega}(z)}{(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}dA(z)\bigg)d\xi\bigg]^{\frac{q}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon+\varepsilon\|f_{j}\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega)}^{q}\lesssim\varepsilon,\quad\textmd{for~any}~j>k_{0}.

Step 2.
Assume that
J_{g}
is bounded. For
r\in(0,1)
, let
\tilde{r}=\tanh r
and
\{a_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be an
\tilde{r}
-lattice.
It follows from Lemma
2.7
that

(2.30)

\widehat{\omega}(z)\asymp\widehat{\omega}(a_{k}),\quad z\in D(a_{k},\tilde{r}).

By the subharmonicity of

and (
2.30
), for any
\xi\in\mathbb{T}

\displaystyle\int_{\Gamma(\xi)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{4}{p-2}}}{\widehat{\omega}(z)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}}dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\Gamma(\xi)}\bigg(\int_{D(z,\tilde{r})}|g^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{2}dA(\zeta)\bigg)^{\frac{p}{p-2}}\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(z)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k}\int_{D(a_{k},\tilde{r})}\Big(\int_{D(z,\tilde{r})}|g^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{2}dA(\zeta)\Big)^{\frac{p}{p-2}}\frac{1}{\widehat{\omega}(z)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}(1-|z|^{2})^{2}}dA(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k}\Big(\sup_{\zeta\in D(a_{k},2\tilde{r})}|g^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\Big)\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}}{\widehat{\omega}(a_{k})^{\frac{2}{p-2}}},

where the summation runs
\{k:D(a_{k},\tilde{r})\bigcap\Gamma(\xi)\neq\emptyset\}
.
Let
\delta=3e^{2r}-2

2.11
,
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{T}

\displaystyle\begin{split}&~~~~\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\int_{\Gamma(\xi)}|g^{\prime}(z)|^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\frac{(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{4}{p-2}}}{\widehat{\omega}(z)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}}dA(z)\bigg)^{\frac{q(p-2)}{2(p-q)}}d\xi\\

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\sum\limits_{a_{k}\in\Gamma(\xi)}\bigg(\sup_{\zeta\in D(a_{k},2\tilde{r})}|g^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}\bigg)\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{\frac{2p}{p-2}}}{\widehat{\omega}(a_{k})^{\frac{2}{p-2}}}\bigg)^{\frac{q(p-2)}{2(p-q)}}d\xi\lesssim\|J_{g}\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega)\to H^{q}}^{\frac{pq}{p-q}}.

Define a sequence
\{b_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
by

b_{k}=\bigg(\sup_{\zeta\in D(a_{k},2\tilde{r})}|g^{\prime}(\zeta)|^{q}\bigg)\frac{(1-|a_{k}|^{2})^{q}}{\widehat{\omega}(a_{k})^{\frac{q}{p}}}.

Let

\eta=\frac{2p}{q(p-2)}
. We turn to prove that

s
such that

(2.32)

\|\{b_{k}^{\frac{1}{s}}\}\|_{T_{\eta s}^{\frac{ps}{p-q}}(\{a_{k}\})}\lesssim\|J_{g}\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega)\to H^{q}}^{\frac{q}{s}}.

Choose a constant

. From Lemma
2.14
, the dual space of

.

2.6
), for any sequence

(2.33)

|\langle e_{k},b_{k}^{\frac{1}{s}}\rangle_{T_{2}^{2}(\{a_{k}\})}|\leq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}|e_{k}b_{k}^{\frac{1}{s}}|(1-|a_{k}|^{2})\asymp\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}|e_{k}b_{k}^{\frac{1}{s}}|\int_{I(a_{k})}d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sum\limits_{a_{k}\in\Gamma(\xi)}|e_{k}b_{k}^{\frac{1}{s}}|d\xi.

By Lemma
2.15
, we obtain

(2.34)

T_{(\eta s)^{\prime}}^{\big(\frac{ps}{p-q}\big)^{\prime}}(\{a_{k}\})=T_{\frac{2s}{2s-q}}^{s^{\prime}}(\{a_{k}\})\cdot T_{\frac{ps}{q}}^{\frac{ps}{q}}(\{a_{k}\}),

from which there exist two sequences
\{x_{k}\}\in T_{\frac{2s}{2s-q}}^{s^{\prime}}(\{a_{k}\})
and

\{y_{k}\}\in T_{p}^{p}(\{a_{k}\})
such that

Together with (
2.33
) and
\textmd{H}\ddot{\textmd{o}}\textmd{lder's}

3.
From Hardy space into Bergman space

In this section, we are devoted to proving Theorem
1.3
.
To begin with, we state a result (cf.
[
PR19
, Theorem 5]
) which is vital to the proof of our main result in this section. It describes that the class of
\mathcal{D}
weights is the largest class of radial weights such that the Littlewood-Paley estimate holds.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
0<p<\infty
and
\omega
be a radial weight. Then

\|f\|_{L_{a}^{p}(\omega)}^{p}\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f^{\prime}(z)|^{p}(1-|z|^{2})^{p}\omega(z)dA(z)+|f(0)|^{p},\quad f\in H(\mathbb{D})

holds if and only if
\omega\in\mathcal{D}
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let

d\mu_{g}(z)=|g^{\prime}(z)|^{q}(1-|z|^{2})^{q}\omega(z)dA(z).

It follows from Lemma
3.1
that

(3.67)

\|J_{g}(f)\|_{L_{a}^{q}(\omega)}^{q}\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{q}d\mu_{g}(z).

By (
3.67
), the boundedness (compactness) of
J_{g}:H^{p}\to L^{q}_{a}(\omega)
is equivalent to that of
Id:H^{p}\to L^{q}(\mu_{g})
. For the case when
0<p\leq q<\infty
, the identity operator
Id:H^{p}\to L^{q}(\mu_{g})
is bounded (compact) if and only if
\mu_{g}
is a
\frac{q}{p}
-Carleson measure (vanishing
\frac{q}{p}
-Carleson measure) (cf.
[
Zhu07
, Section 9.2]
).

Now we are in a position to deal with the case when
0<q<p<\infty
. For
\xi\in\mathbb{T}
, recall

\widetilde{\mu_{g}}(\xi)=\int_{\Gamma(\xi)}\frac{d\mu_{g}(\lambda)}{1-|\lambda|^{2}}.

By
[
Pau16
, Theorem E]
and (
3.67
), we know that
J_{g}:H^{p}\to L_{a}^{q}(\omega)
is bounded if and only if
\widetilde{\mu_{g}}\in L^{\frac{p}{p-q}}(\mathbb{T})
.

It suffices to prove that
J_{g}:H^{p}\to L_{a}^{q}(\omega)
is compact if
\widetilde{\mu_{g}}\in L^{\frac{p}{p-q}}(\mathbb{T})
.
Let
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be any sequence in
H^{p}
with
\|f_{j}\|_{H^{p}}\leq 1
and
f_{j}
converges to 0 uniformly on any compact subset of
\mathbb{D}
.
By Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

(3.68)

\|J_{g}(f_{j})\|_{L_{a}^{q}(\omega)}^{q}\asymp\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{j}(z)|^{q}d\mu_{g}(z).

Then we only need to show

(3.69)

\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{j}(z)|^{q}d\mu_{g}(z)=0.

Since
\widetilde{\mu_{g}}\in L^{\frac{p}{p-q}}(\mathbb{T})
, then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
r_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

(3.70)

\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\int_{\Gamma(\xi)\backslash r_{0}\mathbb{D}}\frac{d\mu_{g}(z)}{1-|z|^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{p-q}}d\xi\bigg]^{\frac{p-q}{p}}<\varepsilon.

Moreover, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
j>k_{0}
,
|f_{j}|^{q}<\varepsilon
on
r_{0}\mathbb{D}
. It follows from

[
Gar07
, Theorem 3.1 in Chapter II]
,
\textmd{H}\ddot{\textmd{o}}\textmd{lder's}
inequality, Fubini’s theorem and (
2.6
), that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f_{j}(z)|^{q}d\mu_{g}(z)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon+\int_{\mathbb{D}\backslash r_{0}\mathbb{D}}\frac{|f_{j}(z)|^{q}}{1-|z|^{2}}\int_{I(z)}d\xi d\mu_{g}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon+\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\sup_{z\in\Gamma(\xi)}|f_{j}(z)|^{p}d\xi\bigg)^{\frac{q}{p}}\bigg[\int_{\mathbb{T}}\bigg(\int_{\Gamma(\xi)\backslash r_{0}\mathbb{D}}\frac{d\mu_{g}(z)}{1-|z|^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{p-q}}d\xi\bigg]^{\frac{p-q}{p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon+\varepsilon\|f_{j}\|_{H^{p}}^{q}\lesssim\varepsilon.

Thus, we get the desired conclusion and complete the proof.

Quasi-Keplerian parametrization for eccentric compact binaries in scalar-tensor theories at second post-Newtonian order and applications
David Trestini

Abstract
The generalized quasi-Keplerian parametrization for compact binaries on eccentric bound orbits is established at second post-Newtonian (2PN) order in a class of massless scalar-tensor theories. This result is used to compute the orbit-averaged flux of energy and angular momentum at Newtonian order, which means relative 1PN order beyond the leading-order dipolar radiation of scalar-tensor theories. The secular evolution of the orbital elements is then computed at 1PN order. At leading order, the closed form “Peters and Mathews” relation between the semimajor axis a a and the eccentricity e e is found to be independent of any scalar-tensor parameter, and is given by a ∝ e 4 / 3 / ( 1 − e 2 ) a\propto e^{4/3}/(1-e^{2}) . Finally, the waveform is obtained at Newtonian order in the form of a spherical harmonic mode decomposition, extending to eccentric orbits the results obtained in [JCAP 08 (2022) 008] .

Introduction

The LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA collaboration has so far detected more than 90 gravitational wave (GW) events
Abbott et al. 2021

III
The quasi-Keplerian parametrization

Let us now turn to the computation of the QK parametrization for eccentric orbits at second post-Newtonian order, which is the main result of this work. The goal is to obtain a closed form expression for the motion of bound orbits obeying the 2PN conservative equations of motion of scalar-tensor theory. The motion is parametrized only in terms of two free variables, e.g. the energy and angular momentum. In Section
III.1
, I define the QK parametrization and obtain some generic results that depend only on the functional relation linking the energy and angular momentum of the binary system to the relative trajectories and velocities. These results are then specialized in Section
III.2
to the case of the scalar-tensor theory considered in this work, and yield the expression of the PK parameters (e.g., the semimajor axis
a_{r}
and eccentricities

,
e_{t}
, and
e_{\phi}
) in terms of the energy and angular momentum of the binary. The reason for this split is that the generic results can be very useful to obtain the QK parametrization of many other alternative theories of gravity.

V
Evolution of the orbital elements

Thanks to both the QK parametrization of Section
III
and the fluxes obtained in Section
IV

V.1
, and applied at lowest
-1
PN order in Section
V.2
. At this order, it is possible to analytically solve the ODEs arising from the flux-balance equations and thus obtain expressions analogous to those of Peters and Mathews

;
Peters 1964
for GR. The relative 1PN expressions for the ODEs governing the orbital evolution are then presented in Section
V.3
.

V.1
General method

Consider an orbital element, which is denoted generically
\xi
. Its orbit-averaged evolution can be expressed as

\left\langle\frac{\differential\xi}{\differential t}\right\rangle=\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial E}\left\langle\frac{\differential E}{\differential t}\right\rangle+\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial J}\left\langle\frac{\differential J}{\differential t}\right\rangle\,.

(35)

where
J=|\mathbf{J}|
.
Since the expressions of the orbital elements in terms of
E
and
J
were computed in Section
III
and the energy and angular momentum fluxes were computed in Section
IV
, we now have all the ingredients to compute the expressions of the orbital elements, assuming that the following balance equations hold:

\left\langle\frac{\differential E}{\differential t}\right\rangle=-\left\langle\mathcal{F}\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathcal{F}^{s}\right\rangle\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\left\langle\frac{\differential J}{\differential t}\right\rangle=-\left\langle\mathcal{G}\right\rangle-\left\langle\mathcal{G}^{s}\right\rangle\,.

(36)

V.2

First, let us work at lowest Newtonian order in the orbital parameters, and reproduce in this case some results derived in GR by Peters and Mathews

VII
Conclusion

To my knowledge, this work is the first systematic analytical study of eccentric binaries in ST theories. The expressions of the 2PK orbital elements were computed, first in a generic setting, then specializing to ST theories. The flux of energy and angular momentum at next-to-leading order beyond dipolar radiation were then derived, i.e. at Newtonian order with respect to quadrupolar radiation. After a preliminary study of the leading order
-1
PN case, for which were obtained several closed form expressions analogous to the works of Peters and Mathews
Peters and Mathews 1963
;
Peters 1964
, the energy and angular momentum balance equations were then used to obtain the ODEs expressing the secular evolution of the orbital elements at Newtonian order. Finally, the expressions for waveform in terms of spherical harmonic modes were computed at Newtonian order. At all steps of these computations, I checked that these results were in agreement with the literature in the GR limit as well in limiting case of circular orbits (
e_{t}\rightarrow 0
).

Thanks to the 2PK parametrization computed in this work, it is in principle possible to obtain the fluxes, waveform and evolution of the orbital elements at 1.5PN order, just like in
1
. However, one difficulty for this is the treatment of the hereditary tail and memory integrals in the eccentric case. In GR, it is possible to treat these analytically
Arun et al. 2008a
;
Arun et al. 2008b
;
Arun et al. 2009
as an expansion in eccentricity for
e_{t}\ll 1
, and the generalization to the case of ST would not present any additional difficulty. Another possible extension of this work is the derivation of the 3PK parametrization. The main difficulty would be the treatment of the nonlocal tails in the equations and conserved energy. Extending the techniques developed for GR at 4PN order to treat these hereditary terms
Cho et al. 2022
seems possible as well.

Finally, I would like to emphasize that the results of Section
III.1
are not theory dependent: they can be used as soon as one obtains an expression for the energy and angular momentum that exhibits the right properties. These results are to be used as a facilitator in studying the behavior of compact binary systems on eccentric orbits for a range of different theories of gravity.

Appendix B
Details of the quasi-Keplerian construction at 2PN order

The starting point of the determination of the QK parametrization is (
13
). The periods
P
and
K
defined by (
16
) are obtained using the techniques of Appendix
A
, from which one trivially obtains the mean motion
n=2\pi/P
. The positions of the periastron
r_{p}
and apastron

are first obtained at lowest order by factorizing the
c\rightarrow\infty
limit of
\mathcal{R}(s)
. PN corrections to these roots are then obtained iteratively by requiring that they cancel
\mathcal{R}(s)
, up to higher-order PN corrections. The PK parameters
a_{r}=(s_{p}+s_{a})/(2s_{a}s_{p})
and
e_{r}=(s_{p}-s_{a})/(s_{p}+s_{a})
are then trivial to obtain, where we recall
s_{p,a}=1/r_{p,a}
.
Following
Memmesheimer et al. 2004
, it is now very useful to introduce the auxiliary variable

\tilde{v}\equiv 2\arctan\left(\sqrt{\frac{1+e_{r}}{1-e_{r}}}\tan\left(\frac{u}{2}\right)\right)\,,

(58)

which differs from
v
defined in (
III.1
) by the choice of eccentricity. Note that at this stage,
e_{\phi}
and
e_{t}
are undetermined, only
e_{r}
is known.

The starting point to determine the Kepler equation is to integrate (
13a
) and find

\left|t-t_{0}\right|=\int_{s}^{s_{p}}\frac{\differential s}{s^{2}\sqrt{\mathcal{R}(s)}}=\int_{s}^{s_{p}}\frac{\differential s\,\overline{P}(s)}{s^{2}\sqrt{(s-s_{a})(s_{p}-s)}}\,,

(59)

where
t_{0}
is some reference time at which the system is an periastron and where
\overline{P}(s)
arises [similarly to (
54
)] from the PN expansion of
1/\sqrt{\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}\smash{(s)}}
, see (
15
). The problem has now been reduced to computing master integrals of the form

\mathcal{J}_{p}\equiv\int^{s}\frac{\differential s\,s^{p-2}}{\sqrt{(s-s_{a})(s_{p}-s)}}\,,

(60)

Interestingly, these master integrals are most conveniently expressed using auxiliary variables that depend heavily on the value of
p
. They are given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{(s_{a}+s_{p})u-(s_{p}-s_{a})\sin(u)}{2(s_{a}s_{p})^{3/2}}

(61a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{u}{\sqrt{s_{a}s_{p}}}

(61b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{2}

\displaystyle=\tilde{v}

(61c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{3}

\displaystyle=\frac{(s_{a}+s_{p})\tilde{v}+(s_{p}-s_{a})\sin(\tilde{v})}{2}

(61d)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{3s_{a}^{2}+2s_{a}s_{p}+3s_{p}^{2}}{8}\tilde{v}+\frac{(s_{p}-s_{a})(s_{p}+s_{a})}{2}\sin(\tilde{v})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(s_{p}-s_{a})^{2}}{16}\sin(2\tilde{v})

(61e)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{5}

\displaystyle=\frac{(s_{p}+s_{a})(5s_{a}^{2}-2s_{a}s_{p}+5s_{p}^{2})}{16}\tilde{v}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{3(s_{p}-s_{a})(5s_{a}^{2}+6s_{a}s_{p}+5s_{p}^{2})}{32}\sin(\tilde{v})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{3(s_{p}-s_{a})^{2}(s_{a}+s_{p})}{32}\sin(2\tilde{v})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{(s_{p}-s_{a})^{3}}{96}\sin(3\tilde{v})

Keeping with the notation of
Memmesheimer et al. 2004
, and replacing
s_{p}
and
s_{a}
by their 2PN accurate expressions, I explicitly find an intermediate form for the Kepler equation,

where

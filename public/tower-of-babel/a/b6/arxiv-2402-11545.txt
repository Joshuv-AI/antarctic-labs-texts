Abstract
In a recent work (Dick et al, QMC sparse grid Galerkin finite element methods for linear elasticity equations with uncertainties, https:arxiv.org/pdf/2310.06187.pdf ), we considered a linear stochastic elasticity equation with random Lamé parameters which are parameterized by a countably infinite number of terms in separate expansions. We estimated the expected values over the infinite dimensional parametric space of linear functionals ℒ {\mathcal{L}} acting on the continuous solution 𝐮 {\bf u} of the elasticity equation. This was achieved by truncating the expansions of the random parameters, then using a high-order quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) method to approximate the high dimensional integral combined with the conforming Galerkin finite element method (FEM) to approximate the displacement over the physical domain Ω . \Omega. In this work, as a further development of aforementioned article, we focus on the case of a nearly incompressible linear stochastic elasticity equation. To serve this purpose, in the presence of stochastic inhomogeneous (variable Lamé parameters) nearly compressible material, we develop a new locking-free symmetric nonconforming Galerkin FEM that handles the inhomogeneity. In the case of nearly incompressible material, one known important advantage of nonconforming approximations is that they yield optimal order convergence rates that are uniform in the Poisson coefficient. Proving the convergence of the nonconforming FEM leads to another challenge that is summed up in showing the needed regularity properties of 𝐮 {\bf u} . For the error estimates from the high-order QMC method, which is needed to estimate the expected value over the infinite dimensional parametric space of ℒ ​ 𝐮 , {\mathcal{L}}{\bf u}, we are required here to show certain regularity properties of 𝐮 {\bf u} with respect to the random coefficients. Some numerical results are delivered at the end.

Introduction

In this work we explore and analyze the application of the quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) method combined with a new nonconforming Galerkin finite element method (FEM) to solve a linear elastic model with uncertainties
[
14

-\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{r}}))={\bf f}({\boldsymbol{x}})\quad\text{for }{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega,~~{\boldsymbol{r}}=({\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}),

(1.1)

subject to homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions

on
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
with
{\boldsymbol{y}}
and
{\boldsymbol{z}}
being parameter vectors describing randomness. The parametric Cauchy stress tensor

is defined as

\boldsymbol{\sigma}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}))=\lambda(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{z}})\Big(\nabla\cdot{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\Big)I+2\mu(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{y}}){\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}))\quad{\rm on}\quad\Omega

Here,

\nabla
) and the divergence (
\nabla\cdot

{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega

\mu({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{y}})=\mu_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}y_{j}\psi_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}})~~{\rm and}~~\lambda({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{z}})=\Lambda\widehat{\lambda}({\boldsymbol{x}},{\boldsymbol{z}})=\Lambda\Big(\widehat{\lambda}_{0}({\boldsymbol{x}})+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}z_{j}\phi_{j}({\boldsymbol{x}})\Big),

(1.2)

{\boldsymbol{y}}=(y_{j})_{j\geq 1}
and
{\boldsymbol{z}}=(z_{k})_{k\geq 1}

U:=(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})^{\mathbb{N}}

and
z_{k}
, respectively, which are assumed to be i.i.d. uniformly distributed.
Here, the constant

\mu_{0},\,\widehat{\lambda}_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega),\quad\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty~~{\rm and}~~\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty\,.

(A1)

As in

]
, in this work, we are interested in efficient approximation of the expected value

\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{L}({\bf u})):=\int_{U}\int_{U}\mathcal{L}\big({\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\big)\,d{\boldsymbol{r}}

(1.3)

of
\mathcal{L}({\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}))
for a certain linear functional

, with respect to the random vector variables

d{\boldsymbol{r}}=d{\boldsymbol{y}}\,d{\boldsymbol{z}}
,

and

(displacement) is the random vector function that solves problem (

]

3
Regularity properties

This section is devoted to showing some useful regularity properties of the weak solution with respect to both the physical variable
{\boldsymbol{x}}
and parametric variables
{\boldsymbol{y}}
and
{\boldsymbol{z}}
. Following
[
13
]
, the weak formulation of parameter-dependent equation (
1.1
) is: Find
{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\in{\bf V}
so that

\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}),{\bf v})=\ell({\bf v}),\quad\text{for all~~${\bf v}\in{\bf V}$ and for every ${\boldsymbol{r}}=({\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}})\in U\times U$,}

(3.1)

where the linear functional
\ell
and the bilinear operator
\mathcal{B}
are defined by

\ell({\bf v})=\int_{\Omega}{\bf f}\cdot{\bf v}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}),{\bf v})=\int_{\Omega}[2\mu\,{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})):{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf v})+\lambda\nabla\cdot{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\nabla\cdot{\bf v}]\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}.

The colon operator is the inner product between tensors. Since
\max\{\frac{\|\nabla\cdot{\bf w}\|}{\sqrt{2}},\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf w})\|\}\leq\|\nabla{\bf w}\|
,

\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf v},{\bf w})\leq C_{\lambda,\mu}\|\nabla{\bf v}\|\,\|\nabla{\bf w}\|\leq C_{\lambda,\mu}\|{\bf v}\|_{{\bf V}}\,\|{\bf w}\|_{{\bf V}},~~{\rm for}~~{\bf v},{\bf w}\in{\bf V}.

(3.2)

So,
\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{r}};\cdot,\cdot)
is bounded over
{\bf V}\times{\bf V}
. The coercivity property of
\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{r}};\cdot,\cdot)
on
{\bf V}
follows from the nonnegativity assumptions on
\mu
and
\lambda
and Korn’s inequality as

\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf v},{\bf v})\geq 2\mu_{\min}\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf v})\|^{2}\geq C\mu_{\min}\|{\bf v}\|_{{\bf V}}^{2},\quad{\bf v}\in{\bf V}.

(3.3)

Owing to these two properties, and since
|\ell({\bf v})|\leq\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}\|{\bf v}\|_{{\bf V}}
, an application of the Lax-Milgram theorem completes the proof of the next theorem.

Theorem 3.1

For every
f\in{\bf V}^{*}
, and for every
{\boldsymbol{r}}\in U\times U
, problem (
3.1
) has a unique solution.

3.1
Regularity in
{\boldsymbol{x}}

For the convergence of the nonconforming finite element solution, we derive next some regularity estimates of the parametric solution of (
3.1
). For the planar linear elasticity problem with constant Lamé parameters
\lambda
and
\mu
, these estimates were proved in
[
28
, Theorem A.1]
for domains with smooth boundary. Later on, some ideas were borrowed from
[
28
]
to show these results for the case of convex polygonal domains, see
[
7
, Lemma 2.2]
. Our results extend the ones in the existing literature to the case of variable random parameters
\lambda
and
\mu
. Some ideas from
[
7
, Lemma 2.2]
are used in our proof.

Theorem 3.2

Assume that (
A2
) is satisfied. Then, for every
f\in{\bf V}^{*}
, and for every
{\boldsymbol{r}}\in U\times U
, the parametric weak solution
{\bf u}={\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})
of problem (
3.1
) satisfies the estimate

\|{\bf u}\|_{{\bf V}}+\Lambda\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|\leq C\,\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}\,.

(3.4)

Furthermore, if
\Omega
is a convex domain (or if
\Omega
has a smooth boundary) and
{\bf f}\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
, then
{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\in{\bf V}\cap{\bf H}
, which is a strong solution of (
1.1
), and the following estimate holds true

\|{\bf u}\|_{\bf H}+\|(\lambda+\mu)\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|_{{\bf V}}\leq C\,\|{\bf f}\|,\quad{\rm for~every}~~{\boldsymbol{r}}\in U\times U\,.

(3.5)

The constant
C
in (
3.4
) depends on
\Omega
,
\mu
and
\widehat{\lambda}
, while in (
3.5
),
C
depends (linearly) on
\|\nabla\mu(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{y}})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
and
\|\nabla\widehat{\lambda}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{z}})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
as well. In both (
3.4
) and (
3.5
),
C
is independent of
\Lambda
.

Proof

From the coercivity property in (
3.3
) and the weak formulation in (
3.1
), we have

C\mu_{\min}\|{\bf u}\|_{{\bf V}}^{2}\leq 2\mu_{\min}\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u})\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u},{\bf u})=\ell({\bf u})\leq\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}\|{\bf u}\|_{{\bf V}},

(3.6)

for every
{\boldsymbol{r}}\in U\times U
. Since
{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\in{\bf V},
by
[
3
, Theorem 3.1 and (3.2)]
, there exists
{\bf u}^{*}\in{\bf V}
such that
\nabla\cdot{\bf u}=\nabla\cdot{\bf u}^{*}
and
\|{\bf u}^{*}\|_{{\bf V}}\leq C\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|
.
Using this in the weak formulation (
3.1
), after some calculations, we reach

\Lambda\widehat{\lambda}_{\min}\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}\lambda\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\nabla\cdot{\bf u}^{*}\,\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}=\ell({\bf u}^{*})-\int_{\Omega}2\mu{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u}):{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u}^{*})\,\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}\\
\leq C\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}\|{\bf u}^{*}\|_{{\bf V}}+2\mu_{\max}\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u})\|\,\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u}^{*})\|\leq C\Big(\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}+2\mu_{\max}\|{\bf u}\|_{{\bf V}}\Big)\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|\,.

(3.7)

Thus,
\Lambda\widehat{\lambda}_{\min}\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|\leq C\Big(\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}+2\mu_{\max}\|{\bf u}\|_{{\bf V}}\Big)
. Combining this estimate with the inequality
\|{\bf u}\|_{{\bf V}}\leq\frac{C}{\mu_{\min}}\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}
, which is due to
(
3.6
), completes the proof of the regularity estimate in (
3.4
).

We turn now to the case of a convex domain
\Omega
. Since
{\bf f}\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
and since the bilinear operator
\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{r}};\cdot,\cdot)
is
{\bf V}-
elliptic for every
{\boldsymbol{r}}\in U\times U
, the solution
{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})
of (
3.1
) is in the space
{\boldsymbol{H}}
owing to the elliptic regularity theory, and consequently,
{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})
is a strong solution of (
1.1
).
For more details about strongly elliptic systems and regularity, see
[
24
, Theorem 4.18]
.

To show the regularity estimate in (
3.5
), we use first the identity

\nabla\cdot(g{\bf A})=g\nabla\cdot{\bf A}+\nabla g\cdot{\bf A}
(with
g\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
,
{\bf A}=[a_{ij}]
is an
m
-by-
m
matrix-valued function, and so
\nabla g\cdot{\bf A}=[\nabla g\cdot{\bf A}_{i}]
is an
m
-by-
1
column vector where
{\bf A}_{i}
is the
i
th row of
{\bf A}
), and notice that

\nabla\cdot(\boldsymbol{\sigma}({\bf u},\lambda,\mu))=\lambda\nabla\cdot\Big(\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\,I\Big)+2\mu\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u})+\nabla\lambda\cdot\Big(\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\,I\Big)+2\nabla\mu\cdot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u})\,.

However
\nabla\cdot(\nabla\cdot{\bf u}I)=\nabla(\nabla\cdot{\bf u})
and
2\nabla\cdot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u})=\Delta{\bf u}+\nabla(\nabla\cdot{\bf u})
, so problem (
1.1
) can be reformulated as: for
{\boldsymbol{r}}\in U\times U,

-\mu\Delta{\bf u}-(\lambda+\mu)\nabla(\nabla\cdot{\bf u})={\bf f}+\nabla\lambda\,\Big(\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\Big)+2\nabla\mu\cdot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u})=:\mu\,{\bf G}.

(3.8)

For later use, it is not hard to observe that

\|\nabla(\nabla\cdot{\bf u})\|\leq\|\eta^{-1}\Delta{\bf u}\|+\|\eta^{-1}{\bf G}\|~~{\rm with}~~\eta=\frac{\lambda+\mu}{\mu}=\frac{\lambda}{\mu}+1\,.

(3.9)

By dividing now both sides of (
3.8
) by
\mu
and letting
p=-\eta\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, after some simplifications, we obtain

-\Delta{\bf u}+\nabla p={\bf\widetilde{G}}:={\bf f}/\mu+2\nabla\mu\cdot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u})/\mu+\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\,\Big(\lambda/\mu^{2}\Big)\nabla\mu\,.

(3.10)

We borrow now the idea used in
[
7
, Lemma 2.2]
, specifically (2.19) and (2.20) therein,

\exists~{\bf u}^{*}\in{\bf H}\cap{\bf V}~{\rm such~that}~~\nabla\cdot{\bf u}=\nabla\cdot{\bf u}^{*}~{\rm and}~\|{\bf u}^{*}\|_{\bf H}\leq C_{0}\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|_{{\bf V}},

(3.11)

where the generic constant
C_{0}
depends on
\Omega
only. (This can be guaranteed by using
[
3
, Theorem 3.1 and (3.2)]
.) Hence, by setting
{\bf w}={\bf u}-{\bf u}^{*}
, problem (
3.10
) can be reformulated in (homogeneous) Stokes form as: find
{\bf w}\in{\bf H}\cap{\bf V}
and
p\in H^{1}(\Omega)
such that

-\Delta{\bf w}+\nabla p={\bf\widetilde{G}}-\Delta{\bf u}^{*},\quad{\rm with}~~\nabla\cdot{\bf w}=0\,.

(3.12)

Thanks to the regularity estimates for the Stokes problem in
[
23
, Theorem 2]
, we have

\|{\bf w}\|_{\bf H}+\|\nabla p\|\leq C_{0}\|{\bf\widetilde{G}}-\Delta{\bf u}^{*}\|,~~\text{assuming that $\Omega$ is convex.}

If
\Omega
is a
{\mathcal{C}}^{1,1}
bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(with
d\in\{2,3\}
), a similar result was proved in
[
2
, Theorem 3]
. Furthermore, for a
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}
class domain, we refer the reader to
[
16
, Theorem IV.6.1]
for similar regularity results. Recall that
{\bf u}={\bf w}+{\bf u}^{*}
and
p=-\eta\nabla\cdot{\bf u}
, thence

\|{\bf u}\|_{\bf H}+\|\nabla(\eta\nabla\cdot{\bf u})\|\leq C_{0}\Big(\|{\bf\widetilde{G}}\|+\|{\bf u}^{*}\|_{\bf H}\Big)\,.

However, from (
3.11
), the regularity estimate in (
3.4
), and the pre-estimate in (
3.9
),
we have

\|{\bf u}^{*}\|_{\bf H}\leq C_{0}\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|_{{\bf V}}\leq C_{0}\|\nabla(\nabla\cdot{\bf u})\|+C\Lambda^{-1}\|{\bf f}\|\leq C_{0}(\|\eta^{-1}\Delta{\bf u}\|+\|\eta^{-1}{\bf G}\|)+C\Lambda^{-1}\|{\bf f}\|,

and consequently,

\|{\bf u}\|_{\bf H}+\|\nabla(\eta\nabla\cdot{\bf u})\|\leq C_{0}(\|\eta^{-1}\Delta{\bf u}\|+\|\eta^{-1}{\bf G}\|)+C\Lambda^{-1}\|{\bf f}\|+C\|{\bf\widetilde{G}}\|\,.

From the estimate in (
3.4
) together with the embedding of
{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
into
{\bf V}^{*}
, we have

\|\eta^{-1}{\bf G}\|\leq C\Big(1+\|\nabla\widehat{\lambda}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}/\widehat{\lambda}_{\min}\Big)\|{\bf f}\|~~{\rm and}~~\|{\bf\widetilde{G}}\|\leq C\Big(1+\widehat{\lambda}_{\max}/\widehat{\lambda}_{\min}\Big)\|{\bf f}\|\,.

Combining the above two equations, we conclude that

\|{\bf u}\|_{\bf H}+\|\nabla(\eta\nabla\cdot{\bf u})\|\leq C_{0}\|\eta^{-1}\Delta{\bf u}\|+C\|{\bf f}\|\leq C_{0}\frac{\mu_{\max}}{\widehat{\lambda}_{\min}\Lambda}\|\Delta{\bf u}\|+C\|{\bf f}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}\|{\bf u}\|_{\bf H}+C\|{\bf f}\|,

for a sufficiently large
\Lambda
. Canceling the common terms yields

\|{\bf u}\|_{\bf H}+2\|\nabla(\eta\nabla\cdot{\bf u})\|\leq C\|{\bf f}\|\,.

(3.13)

To finalize the proof of (
3.5
), it is sufficient to show that

\|\nabla((\lambda+\mu)\nabla\cdot{\bf u})\|\leq C\|{\bf f}\|\,,

(3.14)

and then, merging this with (
3.13
) and the regularity estimate in (
3.4
). In fact, the bound in (
3.14
) is a consequence of the inequality

\|\nabla((\lambda+\mu)\nabla\cdot{\bf u})\|=\|\nabla(\mu\eta\nabla\cdot{\bf u})\|\leq C\|\nabla(\eta\nabla\cdot{\bf u})\|+C\|\eta\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|\leq C\|\nabla(\eta\nabla\cdot{\bf u})\|+C\Lambda\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|\,,

and the achieved estimates in (
3.13
) and (
3.4
).

\Box

3.2
Regularity in random coefficients

For the QMC error bounds, we prove in the next theorem a mixed partial derivatives estimate of the parametric displacement
{\bf u}
with respect to the random variables
y_{j}
and
z_{k}
. Here,
{\mathcal{S}}
denotes the set of (multi-index) infinite vectors
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{j})_{j\geq 1}
with nonnegative integer entries such that
|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|:=\sum_{j\geq 1}\alpha_{j}<\infty.
For
\boldsymbol{\alpha}=(\alpha_{j})_{j\geq 1}
and
\boldsymbol{\beta}=(\beta_{j})_{j\geq 1}
in
\mathcal{S},
the mixed partial derivative with respect to
{\boldsymbol{y}}
and
{\boldsymbol{z}}
is denoted by

\partial_{{\boldsymbol{r}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}:=\partial_{{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\frac{\partial^{|\boldsymbol{\beta}|}}{\partial_{z_{1}}^{\beta_{1}}\partial_{z_{2}}^{\beta_{2}}\cdots}\frac{\partial^{|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|}}{\partial_{y_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{y_{2}}^{\alpha_{2}}\cdots},\quad{\boldsymbol{r}}=({\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}})\,.

Theorem 3.3

Under assumptions (
A1
) and (
A3
), for every
{\bf f}\in{\bf V}^{*}
, every
{\boldsymbol{r}}\in U\times U
and every
\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}\in\mathcal{S}
, the solution
{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})
of the parametric weak problem (
3.1
) satisfies

\big\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\big(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}})\big)\big\|+\Lambda\big\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\big(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}})\big)\big\|\leq(|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|+|\boldsymbol{\beta}|)!\ \widetilde{\bf b}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\widehat{\bf b}^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\Big(\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u})\|+\Lambda\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|\Big),

(3.15)

where

\widetilde{\bf b}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\prod_{i\geq 1}(\widetilde{b}_{i})^{\alpha_{i}},\quad\widetilde{b}_{j}=\Big(\frac{2C_{1}}{\widehat{\lambda}_{\min}}\Big(1+\frac{\mu_{\max}}{\mu_{\min}}\Big)+\frac{1}{\mu_{\min}}\Big)\|\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},

and

\widehat{\bf b}^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}=\prod_{i\geq 1}(\widehat{b}_{i})^{\beta_{i}},\quad\widehat{b}_{j}=\Big(\frac{1}{\widehat{\lambda}_{\min}}\Big(1+\sqrt{2}C_{1}\frac{\mu_{\max}}{\mu_{\min}}\Big)+\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\mu_{\min}}\Big)\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,.

The constant
C_{1}
occurring in the proof depends on
\Omega
only. Consequently,

\|\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}})\|_{{\bf V}}\leq C(|\boldsymbol{\alpha}|+|\boldsymbol{\beta}|)!\ \widetilde{\bf b}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\widehat{\bf b}^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}},

(3.16)

where the constant
C
depends on
\Omega
,
\mu
and
\widehat{\lambda}
, but is independent of
\Lambda
.

Proof
Differentiating both sides of (
3.1
) with respect to the variables
y_{j}
and
z_{k}
, we find the following recurrence after some calculations

\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{r}};\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u},{\bf v})=-\int_{\Omega}\Big[2\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\alpha_{j}\psi_{j}\,{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}-{\bf e}_{j},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u}):{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf v})+\Lambda\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\beta_{k}\phi_{k}\nabla\cdot\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}-{\bf e}_{k}}{\bf u}\nabla\cdot{\bf v}\Big]\,d{\boldsymbol{x}},

(3.17)

where
\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}=\sum_{j,\alpha_{j}\neq 0}
(that is, the sum over the nonzero indices of
\boldsymbol{\alpha}
), and
{\bf e}_{i}\in\mathcal{S}
denotes the multi-index with entry
1
in position
i
and zeros elsewhere. Choose
{\bf v}=\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u}
in (
3.17
), then from the coercivity property in (
3.3
) we have

2\mu_{\min}\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})\|^{2}\leq\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{r}};\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u},\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})\leq 2\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\alpha_{j}\|\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}-{\bf e}_{j},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})\|\,\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})\|\\
+\Lambda\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\beta_{k}\,\|\phi_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\cdot\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}-{\bf e}_{k}}{\bf u}\|\,\|\nabla\cdot\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u}\|\,,

and thus, using the inequality
\|\nabla\cdot{\bf v}\|\leq\sqrt{2}\,\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf v})\|
(
2
is the physical dimension) gives

\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})\|\leq\frac{1}{\mu_{\min}}\Big(\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\alpha_{j}\|\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}-{\bf e}_{j},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})\|+\Lambda\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\beta_{k}\,\frac{\|\phi_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{\sqrt{2}}\|\nabla\cdot\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}-{\bf e}_{k}}{\bf u}\|\Big)\,,

(3.18)

for every
{\boldsymbol{r}}\in U\times U
. To proceed in our proof, we argue as in (
3.7
) and deduce that there exists
(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})^{*}\in{\bf V}
such that

\nabla\cdot\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u}=\nabla\cdot(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})^{*}
and
\|(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})^{*}\|_{{\bf V}}\leq C_{1}\|\nabla\cdot\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u}\|
. Using this and the inequality
\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}((\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})^{*})\|\leq\|(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})^{*}\|_{{\bf V}}
in the weak formulation (
3.17
) with
{\bf v}=(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})^{*}
, we obtain

\Lambda\widehat{\lambda}_{\min}\|\nabla\cdot\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u}\|^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}\lambda\nabla\cdot\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u}\nabla\cdot(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})^{*}\,\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}\leq 2\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\alpha_{j}\|\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}-{\bf e}_{j},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})\|\,\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}((\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})^{*})\|\\
+\Lambda\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\beta_{k}\,\|\phi_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\cdot\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}-{\bf e}_{k}}{\bf u}\|\,\|\nabla\cdot(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})^{*}\|+2\mu_{\max}\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})\|\,\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}((\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})^{*})\|\\
\leq\Big(2C_{1}\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\alpha_{j}\|\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}-{\bf e}_{j},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})\|+\Lambda\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\beta_{k}\,\|\phi_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\cdot\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}-{\bf e}_{k}}{\bf u}\|\\
+2C_{1}\mu_{\max}\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})\|\Big)\,\|\nabla\cdot\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u}\|\,.

Dividing both sides by
\|\nabla\cdot\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u}\|
and using (
3.18
) yield

\Lambda\|\nabla\cdot\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u}\|\leq\frac{2C_{1}}{\widehat{\lambda}_{\min}}\Big(1+\frac{\mu_{\max}}{\mu_{\min}}\Big)\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\alpha_{j}\|\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}-{\bf e}_{j},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})\|\\
+\frac{\Lambda}{\widehat{\lambda}_{\min}}\Big(1+\sqrt{2}C_{1}\frac{\mu_{\max}}{\mu_{\min}}\Big)\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\beta_{k}\,\|\phi_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|\nabla\cdot\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}-{\bf e}_{k}}{\bf u}\|\,.

Combining this estimate with (
3.18
) leads to

\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})\|+\Lambda\|\nabla\cdot\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u}\|\leq\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\alpha_{j}\widetilde{b}_{j}\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}-{\bf e}_{j},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})\|+\Lambda\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\beta_{k}\,\widehat{b}_{k}\|\nabla\cdot\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}-{\bf e}_{k}}{\bf u}\|\,.

(3.19)

To proceed in our proof, we use the induction hypothesis on
n:=|\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\boldsymbol{\beta}|
. From (
3.19
), it is clear that (
3.15
) holds true when
|\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\boldsymbol{\beta}|=1
. Now, assume that (
3.15
) is true for
|\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\boldsymbol{\beta}|=n
, and the task is to show it for
|\boldsymbol{\alpha}+\boldsymbol{\beta}|=n+1.

From (
3.19
) and the induction hypothesis, we have

\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u})\|+\Lambda\|\nabla\cdot\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u}\|\leq n!\Big(\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\alpha_{j}\widetilde{b}_{j}\widetilde{\bf b}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}-{\bf e}_{j}}\widehat{\bf b}^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u})\|+\Lambda\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\beta_{k}\,\widehat{b}_{k}\widetilde{\bf b}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\widehat{\bf b}^{\boldsymbol{\beta}-{\bf e}_{k}}\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|\Big)\\
\leq n!\widetilde{\bf b}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\widehat{\bf b}^{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\Big(\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\alpha_{j}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\beta_{k}\Big)(\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u})\|+\Lambda\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|)\,.

Since
\sum_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\alpha_{j}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\beta_{k}=n+1
, the proof of (
3.15
) is completed.

Finally, since
\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}))\|\geq C\,\|\partial_{{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}}}^{\boldsymbol{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\beta}}{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}})\|_{{\bf V}}
(by Korn’s inequality) and since
\|{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}({\bf u})\|+\Lambda\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|\leq\|\nabla{\bf u}\|+\Lambda\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|\leq C\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}
(by (
3.4
)), we obtain (
3.16
) from (
3.15
).

4
A truncated problem and error estimates

In this section, we investigate the error from truncating the first and second sums in (
1.2
) at
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
terms, respectively, for some
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{N}.
First, let us define the truncated weak formulation problem: for every
{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\bf s}\in U\times U,
find
{\bf u}_{\bf s}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\bf s})\in{\bf V}

\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{r}}^{\bf s};{\bf u}_{\bf s}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\bf s}),{\bf v})=\ell({\bf v}),\qquad\forall~{\bf v}\in{\bf V}.

(4.1)

By Theorem
3.1
, the variational problem (
4.1
) has a unique solution. In the next theorem, we estimate
\mathcal{L}({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{\bf s})
. This is needed for measuring the QMC finite element error in (

).

Theorem 4.1

Let
{\bf u}
and
{\bf u}_{\bf s}
be the solutions of the parametric weak problems
(
3.1
) and (
4.1
), respectively. Assume that
|\mathcal{L}({\bf w})|\leq\|\mathcal{L}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}\|{\bf w}\|_{\bf V}
for any
{\bf w}\in{\bf V}
. Under Assumption (
A1
), for every
{\bf f}\in{\bf V}^{*}
, every
{\boldsymbol{r}}\in U\times U
, and
every
{\bf s}\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, we have

|\mathcal{L}({\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}))-\mathcal{L}({\bf u}_{\bf s}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\bf s}))|\leq C\,\widetilde{C}\,\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}\|\mathcal{L}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}},

(4.2)

where
C
depends on
\Omega,

\mu
, and
\widehat{\lambda}
, and

\widetilde{C}=\sum_{j\geq s_{1}+1}\|\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\sum_{j\geq s_{2}+1}\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,.

Moreover, if
\sum_{j\geq s_{1}+1}\|\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{p}s_{1}^{1-1/p}
and
\sum_{j\geq s_{2}+1}\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{q}s_{2}^{1-1/q}
for some
0<p,\,q<1,
then
\widetilde{C}\leq C_{p,q}\Big(s_{1}^{1-\frac{1}{p}}+s_{2}^{1-\frac{1}{q}}\Big).

Note that, by using the Stechkin inequality, a similar bound can be observed if
(
A3
) and (
A4
) are satisfied instead.

Proof

From the variational formulations in (
3.1
) and (
4.1
), we have

\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{r}}^{\bf s};{\bf u}_{\bf s}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\bf s})-{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}),{\bf v})=\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{r}}-{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\bf s};{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}),{\bf v})\,.

(4.3)

By following the steps in (
3.2
) and using the achieved estimate in (
3.4
), we have

|\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{r}}-{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\bf s};{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}),{\bf v})|\leq C\Big(\sum_{j\geq s_{1}+1}\|\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\|{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\|_{{\bf V}}+\Lambda\sum_{j\geq s_{2}+1}\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\|\Big)\,\|{\bf v}\|_{{\bf V}}\\
\leq C\,\widetilde{C}\Big(\|{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\|_{{\bf V}}+\Lambda\,\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\|\Big)\,\|{\bf v}\|_{{\bf V}}\leq C\,\widetilde{C}\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}\|{\bf v}\|_{{\bf V}}\,.

Now, by mimicking the proof of (
3.4
) with (
4.3
) instead of the weak formulation in (
3.1
), and the above estimate instead of
|\ell({\bf v})|\leq\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}\|{\bf v}\|_{{\bf V}},
we obtain

\|{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})-{\bf u}_{\bf s}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\bf s})\|_{{\bf V}}\leq C\,\widetilde{C}\|{\bf f}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}\,.

(4.4)

From the linearity and boundedness properties of
\mathcal{L}
,

|\mathcal{L}({\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})-{\bf u}_{\bf s}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\bf s}))|\leq C\|{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})-{\bf u}_{\bf s}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}^{\bf s})\|_{{\bf V}}\|\mathcal{L}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}\,,

and hence, to complete the proof, we insert (
4.4
) in this equation.

5
Nonconforming FEM

In this section, we develop and analyze a low-order nonconforming Galerkin FEM for approximating the solution of the model problem (
3.1
) (and consequently, of problem (
1.1
)) over the physical domain
\Omega
, and also investigate the numerical errors. We introduce a family of regular quasi-uniform triangulations
\mathcal{T}_{h}
of the domain
\overline{\Omega}
and let
h=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(h_{K})
, where
h_{K}
denotes the diameter of the element
K
. For convenience, we introduce the following notations:
{\mathcal{E}}=\{E_{1},E_{2},\ldots,E_{q_{h}}\}
is the set of all edges of the interelement boundaries of the elements in

{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, while
{\mathcal{F}}=\{F_{1},F_{2},\ldots,F_{b_{h}}\}
is the set of all remaining edges that are on the boundary of
\Omega.
The corresponding midpoints of the edges in
{\mathcal{E}}
and
{\mathcal{F}}
are denoted by
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots,\xi_{q_{h}}
and
\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\ldots,\eta_{b_{h}}
, respectively. The space of linear polynomials is denoted by
P_{1}.
The nonconforming finite element space is then defined by

{\bf V}_{h}=\{{\bf w}_{h}\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega):~{\bf w}_{h}|_{K}\in[P_{1}]^{2},~\forall~K\in\mathcal{T}_{h},\\
~{\bf w}_{h}{\rm~is~continuous~at}~\xi_{i}~{\rm and}~{\bf w}_{h}(\eta_{j})=0,~{\rm for}~~1\leq i\leq q_{h},~1\leq j\leq b_{h}\}.

Developing a well-posed nonconforming FEM that maintains the symmetric structure of the variational formulation of problem (
1.1
) (which is important at both a theoretical and practical level) becomes more technically challenging when the Lamé parameter
\mu
is
allowed to depend on the spatial variable
{\boldsymbol{x}}
, so an innovative touch is needed here. To elaborate, from the definitions of
\boldsymbol{\sigma}
and
{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}},
and by using the identity
\nabla\cdot(\mu\nabla{\bf u})^{T}=\nabla\mu\cdot(\nabla{\bf u})^{T}+\mu\nabla(\nabla\cdot{\bf u})
(which holds true provided that the mixed first derivatives of the entries of
{\bf u}
are commutative), we notice that

\nabla\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}({\bf u})=\nabla(\lambda\nabla\cdot{\bf u})+\nabla\cdot(\mu\nabla{\bf u})+\nabla\cdot(\mu\nabla{\bf u})^{T}\\
=\nabla(\lambda\nabla\cdot{\bf u})+\nabla\cdot(\mu\nabla{\bf u})+\nabla\mu\cdot(\nabla{\bf u})^{T}+\mu\nabla(\nabla\cdot{\bf u})\,.

Therefore, since
\mu\nabla(\nabla\cdot{\bf u})=\nabla(\mu\nabla\cdot{\bf u})-\nabla\mu\nabla\cdot{\bf u}
, we may reformulate (
1.1
) by rewriting the principle part in divergence form as: for every
{\boldsymbol{r}}\in U\times U
and every
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega,

-\nabla\cdot(\mu\nabla{\bf u})-\nabla((\lambda+\mu)\nabla\cdot{\bf u})+\nabla\mu\,\nabla\cdot{\bf u}-\nabla\mu\cdot(\nabla{\bf u})^{T}={\bf f}\,.

(5.1)

When the Lamé parameters
\mu
and
\lambda
are constants, (
5.1
) reduces to the following strong form:
-\mu\Delta{\bf u}-(\lambda+\mu)\nabla(\nabla\cdot{\bf u})={\bf f}
.
The weak formulation and the nonconforming FEM can then be defined accordingly; see for example
[
7
]
. For the weak formulation of (
5.1
) when
\mu
and
\lambda
vary with
{\boldsymbol{x}}
, we take the
{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
inner product with a test function
{\bf v}\in{\bf V}
, then use the divergence theorem and the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition on
{\bf v}
. The variational form of problem (
5.1
) is then defined as: find
{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\in{\bf V}
such that

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u},{\bf v})=\ell({\bf v}),\quad\text{for all}\quad{\bf v}\in{\bf V},

(5.2)

where

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u},{\bf v}):=\int_{\Omega}[\mu\,\nabla{\bf u}:\nabla{\bf v}+(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\nabla\cdot{\bf v}+(\nabla\mu\,\nabla\cdot{\bf u}-\nabla\mu\cdot(\nabla{\bf u})^{T})\cdot{\bf v}]\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}\,.

Here, the last term
\int_{\Omega}(\nabla\mu\,\nabla\cdot{\bf u}-\nabla\mu\cdot(\nabla{\bf u})^{T})\cdot{\bf v}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}
of the integrand equals

\int_{\Omega}\nabla\mu\cdot({\bf K}_{u_{1}}v_{2}+{\bf K}_{v_{1}}u_{2})\,d{\boldsymbol{x}},~~{\rm with}~~{\bf u}=\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}\\
u_{2}\end{bmatrix},~~{\bf v}=\begin{bmatrix}v_{1}\\
v_{2}\end{bmatrix},~~~{\boldsymbol{x}}=(x_{1},x_{2}),~~{\rm and}~~{\bf K}_{g}=\begin{bmatrix}-g_{x_{2}}\\
g_{x_{1}}\end{bmatrix},

where the sub index
x_{i}
denotes the partial derivative with respect to
x_{i}
for
i=1,2.
Then, by applying again the divergence theorem and using the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions
u_{2}=0
on
\Gamma
, assuming that the mixed first order derivatives of
\mu
exist and commute, we reformulate the operator
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}({\boldsymbol{r}};\cdot,\cdot)
in a symmetric form as:

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u},{\bf v}):=\int_{\Omega}[\mu\,\nabla{\bf u}:\nabla{\bf v}+(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\nabla\cdot{\bf v}+\nabla\mu\cdot({\bf K}_{u_{1}}v_{2}+{\bf K}_{v_{1}}u_{2})]\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}\,.

(5.3)

Alternatively,
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u},{\bf v})
can be extracted from
\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u},{\bf v})
by reformulating the latter as

\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u},{\bf v})=\int_{\Omega}[\mu\,\nabla{\bf u}:\nabla{\bf v}+(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\nabla\cdot{\bf v}-\mu\,{\bf K}_{u_{1}}\cdot\nabla v_{2}-\mu\,{\bf K}_{v_{1}}\cdot\nabla u_{2})]\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}\,,

then applying the divergence theorem to the last two terms to obtain
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u},{\bf v})
, assuming that the mixed derivatives of
u_{1}
and of
v_{1}
exist and commute.

Motivated by (
5.2
), we define the nonconforming FEM as: find
{\bf u}_{h}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\in{\bf V}_{h}
such that

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u}_{h},{\bf v}_{h})=\ell({\bf v}_{h}),\quad\text{for all ${\bf v}_{h}\in{\bf V}_{h}$ and for every ${\boldsymbol{r}}\in U\times U,$}

(5.4)

where the
broken
bilinear form
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};\cdot,\cdot)
is defined as
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}({\boldsymbol{r}};\cdot,\cdot)

in (
5.3
), but with
\nabla_{h}
and
\nabla_{h}\cdot
in place of
\nabla
and
\nabla\cdot
, respectively. The discrete gradient operator
\nabla_{h}
is defined as:
\nabla_{h}{\bf v}|_{K}=\nabla({\bf v}|_{K})
for any
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and the discrete divergence operator
\nabla_{h}\cdot
is defined similarly.

The existence and uniqueness of the solution
{\bf u}_{h}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})
of (
5.4
) is guaranteed if the operator
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};\cdot,\cdot)
is positive-definite on
{\bf V}_{h}
. To show this, we introduce the following broken norms on the finite dimensional space
{\bf V}_{h}
: for
{\bf v}\in V_{h},

\|{\bf v}\|_{h}^{2}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}\nabla{\bf v}:\nabla{\bf v}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}~~{\rm and}~~\|{\bf v}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{h}}^{2}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}[\mu\,\nabla{\bf v}:\nabla{\bf v}+(\mu+\lambda)(\nabla\cdot{\bf v})^{2}]\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}\,.

There exists a positive constant
B
(depending on
\Omega
) such that

\|{\bf v}\|\leq B\,\|{\bf v}\|_{h}\leq B/\sqrt{\mu_{\min}}\|{\bf v}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{h}},\quad{\rm for~all}~~{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h};

(5.5)

see
[
4
]
for the proof of the first inequality. Noting that
\|{\bf v}\|_{h}=0
means that
{\bf v}
is a piecewise constant vector on each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and hence, by using the zero boundary conditions together with continuity at midpoints, it follows that
{\bf v}\equiv{\bf 0}.

Assume that
\max\{|\mu_{x_{1}}|,|\mu_{x_{2}}|\}\leq c_{0}
for some positive constant
c_{0}
. By using (
5.5
), we get

2\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}|\nabla\mu\cdot{\bf K}_{v_{1}}v_{2}|\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}\leq 2c_{0}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}(|v_{1_{x_{1}}}|+|v_{1_{x_{2}}}|)|v_{2}|\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}\\
\leq c_{0}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{K}(|v_{1_{x_{1}}}|^{2}+|v_{1_{x_{2}}}|^{2}+2|v_{2}|^{2})\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}\\
\leq c_{0}\Big(\|{\bf v}\|_{h}^{2}+2\|{\bf v}\|^{2}\Big)\leq c_{0}(1+2B^{2})/\mu_{\min}\,\|{\bf v}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{h}}^{2}\,.

Consequently, for
{\bf v}\in{\bf V}_{h}
,

(1-c_{0}(1+2B^{2})/\mu_{\min})\,\|{\bf v}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{h}}^{2}\leq\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf v},{\bf v})\leq(1+c_{0}(1+2B^{2})/\mu_{\min})\,\|{\bf v}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{h}}^{2}\,.

(5.6)

Therefore,
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};\cdot,\cdot)
is positive-definite over
{\bf V}_{h}
whenever

c_{0}<\mu_{\min}/(1+2B^{2})
. Noting that
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};\cdot,\cdot)=\widehat{\mathcal{B}}({\boldsymbol{r}};\cdot,\cdot)
on
{\bf V}
, we can show with the help of the Poincaré inequality that
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}({\boldsymbol{r}};\cdot,\cdot)
is also positive-definite on
{\bf V}
for small
c_{0}
, and consequently,
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};\cdot,\cdot)
is positive-definite on
{\bf V}_{h}+{\bf V}
for small
c_{0}
. The associated energy norm is
\|\cdot\|_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}=\Big(\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};\cdot,\cdot)\Big)^{1/2}.

5.1
Convergence analysis

For the error analysis, following
[
10
]
, we use the projection operator
\Pi_{h}:{\bf V}\to{\bf V}_{h}
, defined by

\int_{E_{i}}[\Pi_{h}{\bf v}-{\bf v}]\,ds=0\iff\Pi_{h}{\bf v}(\xi_{i})=\frac{1}{|E_{i}|}\int_{E_{i}}{\bf v}\,ds,\quad{\rm for}~~1\leq i\leq q_{h}.

Thus, for any
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
\nabla_{h}\cdot(\Pi_{h}{\bf v})=|K|^{-1}\int_{K}\nabla\cdot{\bf v}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}
on
K
, see
[
10
]
for the proof. So,

\nabla\cdot{\bf v}=0\implies\nabla_{h}\cdot(\Pi_{h}{\bf v})=0.

By a standard Bramble–Hilbert argument, it is known that

\|{\bf v}-\Pi_{h}{\bf v}\|+h\|\nabla_{h}({\bf v}-\Pi_{h}{\bf v})\|\leq Ch^{2}\|{\bf v}\|_{\bf H}\,.

(5.7)

In the next theorem, we use some ideas from
[
7
,
10
]
to show the convergence of the nonconforming finite element solution of problem (
1.1
) over the domain
\Omega
.

Theorem 5.1

Assume that
\Omega
is convex,
{\bf f}\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
, and
\max\{|\mu_{x_{1}}|,|\mu_{x_{2}}|\}\leq c_{0}
on
\Omega\times U
for a sufficiently small
c_{0}
. For every
{\boldsymbol{r}}\in U\times U,
let
{\bf u}
and
{\bf u}_{h}
be the solutions of problems (
1.1
) and (
5.4
), respectively. Under Assumption (
A3
), we have
\|{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})-{\bf u}_{h}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\|_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}\leq Ch\|{\bf f}\|
, where
C
depends on
\Omega
,
\mu
and
\widehat{\lambda}
, and linearly on
\|\nabla\mu(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{y}})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
and
\|\nabla\widehat{\lambda}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{z}})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
, but is independent of the Lamé parameter factor
\Lambda
.

Proof

The bilinear operator
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};\cdot,\cdot)
is symmetric and positive-definite on
{\bf V}_{h}+{\bf V}
, and so, by the second Strang lemma
[
27
]
, we have

\|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h}\|_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}\leq\inf_{{\bf v}_{h}\in{\bf V}_{h}}\|{\bf u}-{\bf v}_{h}\|_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}+\sup_{{\bf v}_{h}\in{\bf V}_{h}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{|\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h},{\bf v}_{h})|}{\|{\bf v}_{h}\|_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}}\,.

(5.8)

Using (
5.6
), (
3.11
), the identity
\nabla_{h}\cdot\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*}=\nabla_{h}\cdot\Pi_{h}{\bf u}
(because
\nabla_{h}\cdot{\bf u}^{*}=\nabla_{h}\cdot{\bf u}
), the projection bound in (
5.7
), and the second regularity estimate in Theorem
3.2
, we reach

\displaystyle\inf_{{\bf v}_{h}\in{\bf V}_{h}}\|{\bf u}-

\displaystyle\bf{\displaystyle v}_{h}\|_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}^{2}\leq C\|{\bf u}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}\|_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}^{2}

(5.9)

\displaystyle\leq C\|{\bf u}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}\|_{h}+C\Lambda\|\nabla_{h}\cdot({\bf u}^{*}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}^{*})\|+C\|{\bf u}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}\|_{h}\|{\bf u}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}\|

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2}\Big(\|{\bf u}\|_{\bf H}+\Lambda\|{\bf u}^{*}\|_{\bf H}\Big)^{2}\leq Ch^{2}\Big(\|{\bf u}\|_{\bf H}+\Lambda\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|_{\bf V}\Big)^{2}\leq Ch^{2}\|{\bf f}\|^{2}\,.

To estimate the second term in (
5.8
), we make the splitting

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h},{\bf v}_{h})=\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u},{\bf v}_{h})-\langle{\bf f},{\bf v}_{h}\rangle=J_{1}+J_{2}-J_{3},

(5.10)

where, after
applying the divergence theorem over each element,

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial K}\mu\,(\nabla{\bf u}\,{\bf n})\cdot{\bf v}_{h}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}},\quad J_{2}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial K}(\mu+\lambda)(\nabla\cdot{\bf u})\,{\bf n}\cdot{\bf v}_{h}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}},

\displaystyle{\rm and}~~J_{3}=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial K}v_{1_{h}}u_{2}\,{\bf K}_{\mu}\cdot{\bf n}\,d{\boldsymbol{x}},

with
{\bf u}=(u_{1},u_{2})^{T}
and
{\bf v}_{h}=(v_{1_{h}},v_{2_{h}})^{T}
, and with
{\bf n}
denoting the outward unit normal vector.

The task now is to estimate
J_{1}
,
J_{2}
and
J_{3}.
For
J_{1},
define the jump matrix function
[\hskip-1.4457pt[{\bf v}_{h}]\hskip-1.4457pt]
across the edge
e\in{\mathcal{E}}
between the two elements
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
, with outward unit normal vectors
{\bf n}_{1}
and
{\bf n}_{2}
, respectively, as
[\hskip-1.4457pt[{\bf v}_{h}]\hskip-1.4457pt]={\bf v}_{h}|_{K_{1}}{\bf n}_{1}^{T}+{\bf v}_{h}|_{K_{2}}{\bf n}_{2}^{T}
. If
e\in{\mathcal{F}}
is a boundary edge that belongs to
K_{1}
, then
[\hskip-1.4457pt[{\bf v}_{h}]\hskip-1.4457pt]={\bf v}_{h}|_{K_{1}}{\bf n}_{1}^{T}
. Thus,
J_{1}
can be expanded as

J_{1}=\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}\cup{\mathcal{F}}}\int_{e}\mu\,\nabla{\bf u}:[\hskip-1.4457pt[{\bf v}_{h}]\hskip-1.4457pt]\,d{\boldsymbol{x}}\,.

Due to the continuity of the function
{\bf v}_{h}
at the midpoint of any edge
e\in{\mathcal{E}}
and since
{\bf v}_{h}={\bf 0}
at the midpoint of any edge
e\in{\mathcal{F}}
,
[\hskip-1.4457pt[{\bf v}_{h}]\hskip-1.4457pt]={\bf 0}
on the midpoint of every
e\in{\mathcal{E}}\cup{\mathcal{F}}
. Consequently, by using the Bramble-Hilbert Lemma and homogeneity arguments, and with the help of the Trace Theorem
(see
[
8
, Section 1.6]
or
[
26
]
),
we get

J_{1}\leq Ch\Big(\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}\cup{\mathcal{F}}}\|\mu\nabla{\bf u}\|_{H^{1}(\bar{K}_{e})}\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sum_{e\in{\mathcal{E}}\cup{\mathcal{F}}}|e|[\hskip-1.4457pt[{\bf v}_{h}]\hskip-1.4457pt]\|_{{\bf L}^{2}(e)}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

Here,
\bar{K}_{e}\in K_{e}
where
K_{e}
is the set of triangles in
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
having
e
as an edge. Hence, using the inequality
|e|\,\|[\hskip-1.4457pt[{\bf v}_{h}]\hskip-1.4457pt]\|^{2}_{{\bf L}^{2}(e)}\leq Ch^{2}\sum_{K\in K_{e}}\|\nabla{\bf v}_{h}\|^{2}_{{\bf L}^{2}(K)}
(because
[\hskip-1.4457pt[{\bf v}_{h}]\hskip-1.4457pt]={\bf 0}
at the midpoint of
e
) yields

|J_{1}|\leq Ch\|\mu\nabla{\bf u}\|_{\bf V}\|{\bf v}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{h}}\leq Ch\|{\bf u}\|_{\bf H}\|{\bf v}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{h}}\,.

To estimate
J_{2}
, we follow the above procedure, taking into account that the jump scalar function
[\hskip-1.4457pt[{\bf v}_{h}]\hskip-1.4457pt]={\bf v}_{h}|_{K_{1}}\cdot{\bf n}_{1}+{\bf v}_{h}|_{K_{2}}\cdot{\bf n}_{2}
across the edge
e\in{\mathcal{E}}
, while
[\hskip-1.4457pt[{\bf v}_{h}]\hskip-1.4457pt]={\bf v}_{h}|_{K_{1}}\cdot{\bf n}_{1}
on the boundary edge
e
that belongs to
K_{1}
. We get
|J_{2}|\leq Ch\|(\mu+\lambda)\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|_{\bf V}\|{\bf v}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{h}}.

Again, a similar procedure can also be followed to estimate
J_{3}
and obtain

|J_{3}|\leq Ch\|\nabla\mu\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}\|u_{2}\|_{\bf V}\|v_{1_{h}}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{h}}\leq Ch\|{\bf u}\|_{\bf V}\|{\bf v}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{h}}\,,

where
[\hskip-1.4457pt[{\bf v}_{h}]\hskip-1.4457pt]=v_{1_{h}}|_{K_{1}}{\bf n}_{1}+v_{1_{h}}|_{K_{2}}{\bf n}_{2}
on
e\in{\mathcal{E}}\cap\overline{K}_{1}\cap\overline{K}_{2}
and
[\hskip-1.4457pt[{\bf v}_{h}]\hskip-1.4457pt]=v_{1_{h}}|_{K_{1}}{\bf n}_{1}
on
e\in{\mathcal{F}}\cap\overline{K}_{1}
.
Now, inserting the above estimates in (
5.10
) and using Theorem
3.2
and (
5.6
) yield

|\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h},{\bf v}_{h})|\leq Ch\Big(\|{\bf u}\|_{\bf H}+\Lambda\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|_{{\bf V}}\Big)\|{\bf v}_{h}\|_{\mathcal{B}_{h}}\leq Ch\|{\bf f}\|\,\|{\bf v}_{h}\|_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}\,.

(5.11)

Using this and (
5.9
) together with (
5.8
) will complete the proof of this theorem.

For measuring the QMC finite element error in (
2.5
), we estimate
\mathcal{L}({\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h})
in the next theorem.

Theorem 5.2

In addition to the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
, we assume that
\mathcal{L}:{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
is a bounded linear functional (
|\mathcal{L}({\bf w})|\leq\|\mathcal{L}\|\,\|{\bf w}\|
), then

|\mathcal{L}({\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}))-\mathcal{L}({\bf u}_{h}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}))|\leq Ch^{2}\|{\bf f}\|\|\mathcal{L}\|,~~~{\rm for~every}~~{\boldsymbol{r}}=({\boldsymbol{y}},{\boldsymbol{z}})\in U\times U,

where
C
depends on
\Omega
,
\mu
,
\widehat{\lambda}
, and linearly on
\|\nabla\mu(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{y}})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
and
\|\nabla\widehat{\lambda}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{z}})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
, but is independent of the Lamé parameter factor
\Lambda
. Furthermore, by using the equality

\|{\bf v}\|=\sup_{{\bf w}\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega),{\bf w}\neq{\bf 0}}\frac{|\mathcal{L}({\bf v})|}{\|{\bf w}\|},\quad{\rm for~any}~~{\bf v}\in{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega),~~{\rm with}~~\mathcal{L}({\bf v})=\langle{\bf w},{\bf v}\rangle,

the above estimate leads to the following optimal convergence bound:

\|{\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})-{\bf u}_{h}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\|\leq C\,h^{2}\|{\bf f}\|,~~~{\rm for~every}~~{\boldsymbol{r}}\in U\times U\,.

Proof

We start our proof by replacing
\ell
with
\mathcal{L}
in (
3.1
), and then, by Theorem
3.1
, the new parametric variational problem: find
{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}\in{\bf V}
such that

\mathcal{B}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}},{\bf v})=\mathcal{L}({\bf v})\quad{\rm for~all}~~{\bf v}\in{\bf V},~~{\rm for~every}~~{\boldsymbol{r}}\in U\times U,

(5.12)

has a unique solution. Furthermore, using Theorem
3.2
(with
{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}
in place of
{\bf u}
), we deduce that

\|{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\|_{{\bf V}}+\Lambda\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\|\leq C\|\mathcal{L}\|_{{\bf V}^{*}}\,.

(5.13)

Then by proceeding as in the proof of (
3.5
) in Theorem
3.2
(with
{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}
in place of
{\bf u}
), and using that the linear functional
\mathcal{L}
is bounded in
{\bf L}^{2}(\Omega)
, we conclude that

\|{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\|_{\bf H}+\|(\lambda+\mu)\nabla\cdot{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\|_{{\bf V}}\leq C\|\mathcal{L}\|.

(5.14)

The nonconforming finite element solution of
{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}
is defined by: Find
{\bf\Theta}_{h}\in{\bf V}_{h}
such that

\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf\Theta}_{h},{\bf v}_{h})=\mathcal{L}({\bf v}_{h}),\quad\text{for all}\quad{\bf v}_{h}\in{\bf V}_{h}\,.

(5.15)

By following the proof of Theorem
5.1
and using (
5.14
), we obtain the following error estimate

\|{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})-{\bf\Theta}_{h}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\|_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}\leq Ch\|{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})\|_{\bf H}\leq Ch\|\mathcal{L}\|.

(5.16)

From the linearity property of
\mathcal{L}
, (
5.12
), (
5.15
), and the symmetric property of
\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};\cdot,\cdot)
,

\mathcal{L}({\bf u}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})-{\bf u}_{h}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}}))=\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}},{\bf u})-\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf\Theta}_{h},{\bf u}_{h})=G_{1}+G_{2}+G_{3},

(5.17)

where

G_{1}=\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}-{\bf\Theta}_{h},{\bf u}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u})+\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h},{\bf\Theta}_{h}-{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}})+\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h},{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}),

G_{2}=\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h},\Pi_{h}{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}})\quad{\rm and}\quad G_{3}=\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}({\boldsymbol{r}};{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}-{\bf\Theta}_{h},\Pi_{h}{\bf u})\,.

By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, Theorem
5.1
, (
5.16
), the bound in (
5.9
) in addition to a similar bound with
{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}
in place of
{\bf u}
where the regularity estimate in (
5.14
) is needed, we reach

|G_{1}|\leq\|{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}-{\bf\Theta}_{h}\|_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}\|{\bf u}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}\|_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}+\|{\bf u}-{\bf u}_{h}\|_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}(\|{\bf\Theta}_{h}-{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}\|_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}}+\|{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}-\Pi_{h}{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}\|_{\widehat{\mathcal{B}}_{h}})\leq Ch^{2}\|{\bf f}\|\,\|\mathcal{L}\|.

To estimate
G_{2}
, we follow the approach used for bounding (
5.10
), that is, the derivation between (
5.10
) and (
5.11
). To obtain a sharp bound, it is necessary to use the identity
[\hskip-1.4457pt[\Pi_{h}{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}]\hskip-1.4457pt]=[\hskip-1.4457pt[\Pi_{h}{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}-{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}]\hskip-1.4457pt]
across the skeleton of the finite element mesh, which is due to the continuity of
{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}
on
\Omega
and also to the fact that
{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}(\cdot,{\boldsymbol{r}})={\bf 0}
on
\Gamma
. In conclusion, we get

|G_{2}|\leq Ch\Big(\|{\bf u}\|_{\bf H}+\Lambda\|\nabla\cdot{\bf u}\|_{{\bf V}}\Big)\|\Pi_{h}{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}-{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}\|_{h}\leq Ch^{2}\|{\bf f}\|\,\|\mathcal{L}\|\,.

By arguing as above, we obtain a similar estimate of
G_{3}
with
{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}
and
{\bf u}
in place of
{\bf u}
and
{\bf u}_{\mathcal{L}}
, respectively, and so,
|G_{3}|\leq Ch^{2}\|{\bf f}\|\,\|\mathcal{L}\|.
Finally, inserting the achieved estimates of
G_{1}
,
G_{2}
and
G_{3}
in (
5.17
) will complete the proof.

\Box

We consider a purely deterministic problem with Lamé parameters

and

\Lambda

\lambda_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})=\Lambda(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\sin(2x_{1}))

\mu_{0}(\boldsymbol{x})=1+x_{1}+x_{2}
for
{\boldsymbol{x}}=(x_{1},x_{2})

u_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\bigl(\cos(2x_{1})-1\bigr)\sin(2x_{2})\\
\bigl(1-\cos(2x_{2})\bigr)\sin(2x_{1})\end{bmatrix}+\frac{\sin(x_{1})\sin(x_{2})}{\Lambda}\begin{bmatrix}1\\
1\end{bmatrix}.

Notice
{\bf u}=\mathbf{0}
on
\partial\Omega
. Table
1
compares the errors and convergence rates of our nonconforming scheme with those using the standard conforming, piecewise-linear Galerkin finite element method, when
\Lambda=1

h^{2}

\mathbf{L}^{2}

h

For the remaining examples, we chose the

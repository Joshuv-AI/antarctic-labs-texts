Introduction

Let
N=\binom{n}{2}
and
s\geq 2
. Let
e_{i,j},\,i=1,2,\ldots,N,\,j=1,2,\ldots,s
be
s
independent permutations of the edges
E(K_{n})
of the complete graph
K_{n}
. Let
{\bf e}_{i}=(e_{i,1},e_{i,2},\ldots,e_{i,s})
and for
I\subseteq[N]
let
E_{I,j}=\left\{e_{i,j}:i\in I\right\}
for
j=1,2,\ldots,s
. A
MultiForest
is a set
I\subseteq[N]
such that the edge sets
E_{I,j}
induce forests for
j=1,2,\ldots,s
. A
MultiTree
is a MultiForest in which each forest is a spanning tree. In this paper we study the following question: what is the smallest
m=m(n)
such that w.h.p.
[m]
contains a MultiTree.

This is a particular case of the following more general question: given matroids
{\mathcal{M}}_{1},{\mathcal{M}}_{2},\ldots,{\mathcal{M}}_{s}
over a common ground set
E=\left\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{M}\right\}
let

{\cal I}_{k}=\left\{I\in\binom{[M]}{k}:\;\left\{e_{i},i\in I\right\}\text{ is independent in }{\mathcal{M}}_{i},i=1,2,\ldots,s\right\}.

Then let
m^{*}=\max\left\{k:{\cal I}_{k}\neq\emptyset\right\}
. Then we can ask what is the smallest
m=m(n)
such that w.h.p.
[m]
contains a member of
{\cal I}_{m^{*}}
. In general this is a rather challenging question, mainly because the structure of randomly chosen matroids is not as well understood as the structure of random graphs.

There is at least one instance where we already have a precise answer to the above matroid question. We let
M=N
and let
{\mathcal{M}}_{1}
be the graphic matroid of
K_{n}
. For
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
we randomly color each edge

uniformly with

and
{\mathcal{M}}_{2}
is the partition matroid where a set
I\subseteq E(K_{n})
is independent if
e_{1},e_{2}\in I
implies that
c(e_{1})\neq c(e_{2})
. In more familiar terminology,
I
is
rainbow colored
. This problem was solved in Frieze and McKay
[
5
]
where it was shown that w.h.p.
m^{*}
is the smallest integer
m
such that the graph induced by
e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{m}
is (i) connected and (ii)
|\left\{c(e_{i}):i=1,2,\ldots,m\right\}|\geq n-1
.

Going back to MultiTree’s, we prove two theorems.

Theorem 1
.

We have w.h.p. that
m^{*}=O(n\log n)
.

When
s=2
we can use Edmond’s theorem
[
2
]
to prove the following: let
\Gamma_{j,m}=([n],E_{[m],j})
.

Theorem 2
.

W.h.p.
m^{*}=\max\left\{m_{1},m_{2}\right\}
where for
j=1,2
,
m_{j},=\min\left\{m:\Gamma_{j,m}\text{ is connected}\right\}
.

There is no actual need to restrict attention to matroid intersection. For example let
I
be a
Multimatching
if the sets
E_{I,j},j=1,2,\ldots,s
induce matchings and let
I
be a

Theorem 3
.

W.h.p.
[m]
contains a MultiPerfectMatching if

.

One thing missing from this paper is what might be called MultiHamiltonCycle, where the edge sets

We next consider as to how long we have to run this part of the process altogether. We first consider the time taken to get giant trees of size

s

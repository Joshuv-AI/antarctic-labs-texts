On the optimal shape of a thin insulating layer
P. Acampora, E. Cristoforoni, C. Nitsch, C. Trombetti

Abstract
We are interested in the thermal insulation of a bounded open set Ω \Omega surrounded by a set whose thickness is locally described by ε ​ h \varepsilon h , where h h is a non-negative function defined on the boundary ∂ Ω \partial\Omega . We study the problem in the limit for ε \varepsilon going to zero using a first-order asymptotic development by Γ \Gamma -convergence. Introduction

Energy efficiency has emerged as one of the most pressing issues in recent years, as it is critical to achieving sustainable development, lowering greenhouse gas emissions, and mitigating climate change, all while boosting economic growth and increasing quality of life.
Thermal insulation is important in the context of energy efficiency because it helps to reduce heat transfer and energy losses in buildings and industrial processes, resulting in significant energy and cost savings.

This paper addresses the topic of thermal insulation of a solid, kept at constant temperature, by displacing around it an insulator. Roughly speaking, the optimal insulation is the one minimizing, at thermal equilibrium, the heat rate loss per unit time across the exterior boundary. More specifically, in our model heat exchange with the environment occurs through convection, which is by far the most common mechanism in real world applications.

In order to contain costs of insulation, the volume of the insulator is prescribed. The resulting mathematical model gives rise to a free boundary problem which has been previously studied in
[
7
]
and for more general heat transfer mechanisms (including for instance also radiation) in
[
5
]
. In particular, the former paved the way on how to prove the existence of an optimal distribution of insulator and the regularity of its a priori unknown boundary.

However, although a solution always exists for every set to be insulated and every amount of insulator, very little is known about the optimal shape of the insulator, and the only completely solved case happens to be the radial one.

This work addresses the problem of qualitatively and quantitatively describing how to displace the insulator, and to do so, it restricts the analysis to "small insulation thickness", an approximation reasonable in many contexts, such as in the case of buildings and other big structures.

Throughout the paper,
\Omega
represents the body to insulate, in which the temperature is fixed. Since the problem is invariant under temperature scaling and translations, without loss of generality the temperature of the body will be
1
, while the environment temperature will be
0
.
If convection is the leading mechanism of thermal exchange with the environment, then the heat rate loss per unit time and unit surface is proportional to the temperature jump across the surface element separating the insulator (or the body) with the environment. The constant of proportionality will be denoted by
\beta>0
. If
\Sigma
denotes the insulator (see figure), then at equilibrium the temperature distribution in
\Sigma
is an harmonic function which we denote by
u
.

In summary,

\begin{cases}\Delta u=0&\text{in }\Sigma,\\[5.0pt]

We can also characterize the function

as the minimizer of the energy functional

\mathcal{E}(v)=\int_{\Sigma}\lvert\nabla v\rvert^{2}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial(\Omega\cup\Sigma)}v^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

among all functions

such that

in
\Omega

For a given
\Omega
, our ultimate goal would be to find the shape of
\Sigma
which minimizes

\int_{\partial(\Omega\cup\Sigma)}u\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

among all
\Sigma
of prescribed measure.

So far, such a problem seems to be out of reach, so we restricted our analysis to the case where the layer of the insulating material and the conductivity of the insulator are both very small.

More precisely, let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a smooth bounded, open set, and let
h\colon\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-negative function. Denoting by
\nu
the exterior unit normal to the boundary of
\Omega

\Sigma_{\varepsilon}=\Set{\sigma+t\nu(\sigma)}{\sigma\in\partial\Omega,\,0<t<\varepsilon h(\sigma)}

and we denote by
\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\overline{\Omega}\cup\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
.
Here, the non negative parameter
\varepsilon
is meant to be small and we want to investigate the limit as
\varepsilon
vanishes. But in order to have this limit non trivial, we also assume that the conductivity of the insulator is small, namely, the heat flux inside
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
is
-\varepsilon Du
. In practice, we are assuming that we use a "small quantity" of a "very good" insulator.

All at once we can consider the minimization of the following energy functional

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(v,h)=\varepsilon\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\lvert\nabla v\rvert^{2}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}}v^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

where

, with

in
\Omega
. Here, the small parameter
\varepsilon

, a minimum

of

\min\Set{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(v,h)}{v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}),\,v=1\,\text{in }\Omega}

(1.1)

solves the boundary value problem:

\begin{cases}\Delta u_{\varepsilon,h}=0&\text{in }\Sigma_{\varepsilon},\\[5.0pt]
u_{\varepsilon,h}=1&\text{in }\Omega,\\[5.0pt]
\varepsilon\dfrac{\partial u_{\varepsilon,h}}{\partial\nu_{\varepsilon}}+\beta u_{\varepsilon,h}=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}\setminus\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\nu_{\varepsilon}
is the exterior unit normal to the boundary of

The volume of insulator we displace is

\mathcal{H}_{m}=\mathcal{H}_{m}(\partial\Omega)=\Set{h\in L^{1}(\partial\Omega)}{\begin{aligned} &\int_{\partial\Omega}h\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq m\\[3.0pt]
&\,h\geq 0\end{aligned}}.

(1.2)

Our problem reduces to finding the best configuration of insulating material surrounding
\Omega

\min\Set{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(v,h)}{\begin{aligned} &v\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}),\\
&v=1\,\text{in
}\Omega,\\
&h\in\mathcal{H}_{m}\end{aligned}}.

Following argument similar to those used in

Then, in view of the convexity of the functional with respect to

\min\Set{\mathcal{F}_{0}(h)}{h\in\mathcal{H}_{m}}

is achieved by the constant

Displacing the insulator uniformly around the boundary is somehow the trivial solution, the one suggested by common sense, and very common when insulating buildings. However, it is mathematically not satisfactory at all. As we expect that portions of boundary with higher (mean) curvature are less convenient to insulate with respect to those with lower curvature. Such an idea is strongly suggested by the radial cases (see for example
[

But such a kind of evidence is lost when performing the
\Gamma
-limit and therefore we decided to push our analysis a bit further.
Let

K_{0}=\Set{v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}{\,v=1\,\text{in }\Omega},

our main result is a first-order asymptotic development by
\Gamma
-convergence (see

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, open set with

\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,h)=\dfrac{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\cdot,h)-\mathcal{F}_{0}(h)}{\varepsilon}

\mathcal{F}^{(1)}(v,h)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{Hh(2+\beta h)}{2(1+\beta h)^{2}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}&\text{if }v\in K_{0},\\[10.0pt]
+\infty&\text{if }v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\setminus K_{0}.\end{cases}

Finally, in

3.1
Setting of the problem

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded, open set with
C^{1,1}
boundary, and fix a positive Lispchitz function
h\colon\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
. We recall that

\Sigma_{\varepsilon}=\Set{\sigma+t\nu(\sigma)}{\sigma\in\partial\Omega,\,0<t<\varepsilon h(\sigma)},

and

\Omega_{\varepsilon}=\overline{\Omega}\cup\Sigma_{\varepsilon}.

Our assumptions on
\partial\Omega
ensure that there exists
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(\Omega,h)
such that, if
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, the map

(\sigma,t)\longmapsto\sigma+t\nu(\sigma)

is invertible, that is, for every
x\in\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
there exist unique
\sigma(x)\in\partial\Omega
and
t(x)
, with
0\leq t(x)\leq\varepsilon h(\sigma(x))
, such that

x=\sigma(x)+t(x)\nu(\sigma(x)).

Therefore, we can extend

and
\nu
on
\Sigma_{\varepsilon}
as
h(x)=h(\sigma(x))
, and
\nu(x)=\nu(\sigma(x))
respectively. Moreover, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega

d(x)=d(x,\partial\Omega)=\inf_{y\in\partial\Omega}\,\lvert x-y\rvert

be the distance from
\partial\Omega

for every

.

.

\begin{split}\varepsilon\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\lvert\nabla\varphi_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{2}\,dx\leq&\dfrac{\beta^{2}}{\varepsilon}\int_{\Sigma_{\varepsilon}}\left(\dfrac{1}{(1+\beta h)^{2}}+2\dfrac{Hd}{(1+\beta h)^{3}}\right)\,dx+\varepsilon C\left\lvert\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\right\rvert\\[10.0pt]
\leq&\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{\beta^{2}h}{(1+\beta h)^{2}}(1+\varepsilon\tau hH+\varepsilon^{2}Q)\,d\tau\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\\[10.0pt]
&+\int_{\partial\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}\dfrac{2\varepsilon\beta^{2}h^{2}H\tau}{(1+\beta h)^{3}}(1+\varepsilon\tau hH+\varepsilon^{2}Q)\,d\tau\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\varepsilon C\left\lvert\Sigma_{\varepsilon}\right\rvert\\[10.0pt]
=&\int_{\partial\Omega}\dfrac{\beta^{2}h}{(1+\beta h)^{2}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\varepsilon\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\dfrac{\beta^{2}h^{2}H}{2(1+\beta h)^{2}}+\dfrac{\beta^{2}h^{2}H}{(1+\beta h)^{3}}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+O(\varepsilon^{2}).\end{split}

(3.31)

On the other hand, for every
\sigma\in\partial\Omega
,

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\sigma+\varepsilon h(\sigma)\nu(\sigma))=\frac{1}{1+\beta h(\sigma)}-\frac{\varepsilon\beta h(\sigma)^{2}H}{2(1+\beta h(\sigma))^{2}},

from which we get

\begin{split}\beta\int_{\partial{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq\int_{\partial\Omega}\dfrac{\beta}{(1+\beta h)^{2}}(1+\varepsilon hH)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}-\int_{\partial\Omega}\dfrac{\varepsilon\beta^{2}h^{2}H}{(1+\beta h)^{3}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+O(\varepsilon^{2}).\end{split}

(3.32)

Finally, joining (
3.24
), (
3.31
), (
3.32
), and (
3.25
) we have

\begin{split}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})=\dfrac{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})-\mathcal{F}_{0}}{\varepsilon}&\leq\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\dfrac{\beta^{2}h^{2}H}{2(1+\beta h)^{2}}+\dfrac{\beta hH}{(1+\beta h)^{2}}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+O(\varepsilon)\\[10.0pt]
&=\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}\dfrac{hH(2+\beta h)}{2(1+\beta h)^{2}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+O(\varepsilon)\end{split}

so that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\delta\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{\varepsilon})\leq\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}\dfrac{hH(2+\beta h)}{2(1+\beta h)^{2}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

and the
\Gamma
-limsup inequality is proved.
∎

4

Let
\Omega

In the following, we drop the dependence on the set

For every

Remark 4.1
.

Assume that a non-zero continuous solution

The previous equation yields

\dfrac{c}{\beta}(1+\beta\mu)^{3}-(1+\beta\mu)+\dfrac{\varepsilon H}{\beta}=0,

for some constant

.

Let
\varepsilon>0
, in the following we will assume that

\sup_{\partial\Omega}\dfrac{\varepsilon H}{\beta}\leq\dfrac{2}{3}.

(4.2)

Let

For every

Proposition 4.2
.

Let (
4.2
) hold true. Then, for every

, and
\sigma\in\Gamma_{k}
, in the interval

(4.3)

and there exists

In particular, we have

\lim_{k\to k_{0}^{-}}z_{k}=1.

(4.5)

Moreover, for every

Proof.

For any fixed

we have that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y}P_{k}(y,\sigma)<0\qquad\text{if }y\in\left[1,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3k}}\right),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y}P_{k}(y,\sigma)>0\qquad\text{if }y\in\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{3k}},+\infty\right).

Therefore, in the interval
(1,+\infty)
there exists a unique zero
y_{k}(\sigma)
of the polynomial

, and

y_{k}(\sigma)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{3k}}.

Notice in addition that for every

.
Hence, if we choose

to be the unique real number in

We now prove (

). We first observe that

is decreasing in

, so that

k_{1}z_{k_{2}}^{3}-z_{k_{2}}+\dfrac{\varepsilon H_{0}}{\beta}<k_{2}z_{k_{2}}^{3}-z_{k_{2}}+\dfrac{\varepsilon H_{0}}{\beta}=0=k_{1}z_{k_{1}}^{3}-z_{k_{1}}+\dfrac{\varepsilon H_{0}}{\beta},

which ensures

since the polynomial

From (

Finally, in order to prove (

and

∎

Let

, and let

be as in

Notice that, by (

We have the following

Proposition 4.3
.

Let (
4.2
) hold true. Then, for every

,there exists a unique

We first prove that the function

is continuous. Fix

and let
\delta>0

\beta(M(k)-M(k+\delta))=\int_{\Gamma_{k+\delta}}(y_{k}-y_{k+\delta})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\Set{1-k-\delta\leq\frac{\varepsilon H}{\beta}<1-{k}}}(y_{{k}}-1)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

By definition, we have that for every

Let

Theorem 4.4
.

Let (

Proof.

for every

We claim that

is increasing in

.
By explicit computation, we have that

g^{\prime}(t)=\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}\dfrac{\varepsilon H-\beta(1+\beta h_{t})}{(1+\beta h_{t})^{3}}(h-\mu_{k})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

From (

) we have that, for every

is increasing on

\begin{split}g^{\prime}(t)&\geq\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}\dfrac{\varepsilon H-\beta(1+\beta\mu_{k})}{(1+\beta\mu_{k})^{3}}(h-\mu_{k})\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\\[10.0pt]

Fix

where

is the mean curvature of

\Omega\in\mathcal{K}_{P}

\mathcal{H}_{m}
is the set defined in (

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega,h)=\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}\left(\dfrac{1}{1+\beta h}+\varepsilon H_{\Omega}\dfrac{h(2+\beta h)}{2(1+\beta h)^{2}}\right)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

(4.15)

Definition 4.7

(Cookie Shape)

For any

For every

, We have that

Moreover,

P(C_{r,R})=2\omega_{n-1}\left(R^{n-1}+(n-1)r\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\left(r\rho+R\right)^{n-2}}{\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}}\,d\rho\right).

(4.16)

We observe that the function

is increasing in

and

.

Theorem 4.9
.

For every

we have

\inf\Set{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega,h)}{(\Omega,h)\in\mathcal{K}_{p}\times\mathcal{H}_{m}(\partial\Omega)}=\dfrac{\beta P^{2}}{P+\beta m}

and the infimum is asymptotically achieved by

For every
(\Omega,h)\in\mathcal{K}_{p}\times\mathcal{H}_{m}(\partial\Omega)

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega,h)\geq\beta\int_{\partial\Omega}\dfrac{1}{1+\beta h}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\geq\dfrac{\beta P^{2}}{P+\beta m}.

let

be such that, for every

Consider

h_{k}(\sigma)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{m}{2\omega_{n-1}R_{k}^{n-1}}&\text{if }H(\sigma)=0,\\[5.0pt]

4.2
Maximization with geometric constraints

Fix
\varepsilon,m>0
. For every bounded, open set
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
C^{1,1}
boundary, we let

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)=\inf\Set{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega,h)}{\,h\in\mathcal{H}_{m}},

where
\mathcal{H}_{m}
is the set defined in (
1.2
). This section will study the maximization of
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
with fixed quermassintegral. We refer to
[
13
,
6
]
for the following

Definition 4.10

(Quermassintegrals)
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty,
bounded, convex set. We define the
quermassintegrals
as the unique coefficients
W_{j}(\Omega)
such that

\lvert\Omega+tB\rvert=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}W_{j}(\Omega)t^{j},

where
B
is the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and

\Omega+tB=\Set{x+ty}{x\in\Omega,\>y\in B}.

In particular
W_{0}(\Omega)
is the measure of
\Omega
and
W_{n}(\Omega)=\omega_{n}
, the measure of the unit ball.

Theorem 4.11

(Alexandrov-Fenchel Inequality)
.

Let
0\leq i<j\leq n-1
, and let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a nonempty,
bounded, convex set, then

\left(\frac{W_{i}(\Omega)}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-i}}\leq\left(\frac{W_{j}(\Omega)}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-j}}.

Moreover, the inequality holds as equality if and only if
\Omega
is a ball.

Remark 4.12
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded, open, convex set with
C^{2}
boundary and nonzero Gaussian curvature, then the quermassintegral are related to the principal curvatures of the boundary of
\Omega
. Indeed we have that for every
j=1,\dots,n

W_{j}=\dfrac{1}{n}\int_{\partial\Omega}H_{j-1}(\sigma)\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}

Here
H_{j}
denotes the
j
-th normalized elementary symmetric function of the principal curvatures
of
\partial\Omega
, that is
H_{0}=1
and, for every
j=1,\dots n-1
,

H_{j}(\sigma)=\binom{n-1}{j}^{-1}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}\leq\dots\leq i_{j}\leq n-1}k_{i_{1}}(\sigma)\cdots k_{i_{j}}(\sigma),

where
k_{1}(\sigma),\dots,k_{n-1}(\sigma)
are the principal curvatures at a point
\sigma\in\partial\Omega
. In particular, we have that

W_{1}(\Omega)=\dfrac{1}{n}P(\Omega)

and

W_{2}(\Omega)=\dfrac{1}{n(n-1)}\int_{\partial\Omega}H_{\Omega}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

In the planar case, we have the following

Proposition 4.13
.

Let
n=2
, and let
\Omega
be a bounded, open, simply connected set
\Omega
with
C^{2}
boundary such that either

P(\Omega)\geq 3\pi\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\beta}

or

P(\Omega)\leq\pi\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\beta}.

Then

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)\leq\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega^{*}),

(4.17)

where
\Omega^{*}
is the ball having the same perimeter as
\Omega
.

Proof.

If

P(\Omega)\leq\pi\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\beta},

then

\dfrac{\varepsilon H_{\Omega^{*}}}{\beta}\geq 2,

and, by
Section 4
, we get

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega^{*})=\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega^{*},0)=\beta P(\Omega^{*}).

On the other hand,

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)\leq\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega,0)=\beta P(\Omega)=\beta P(\Omega^{*}),

which gives (
4.17
).
If

P(\Omega)\geq 3\pi\dfrac{\varepsilon}{\beta},

then

\dfrac{\varepsilon H_{\Omega^{*}}}{\beta}\leq\dfrac{2}{3},

and, by
Theorem 4.4
, we get

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega^{*})=\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega^{*},m/P(\Omega^{*})).

On the other hand,

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)&\leq\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega,m/P(\Omega^{*}))\\
&=\beta\left(\dfrac{P(\Omega^{*})P(\Omega)}{P(\Omega^{*})+\beta m}+\dfrac{\varepsilon m(2P(\Omega^{*})+\beta m)}{2(P(\Omega^{*})+\beta m)^{2}}\int_{\partial\Omega}H_{\Omega}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right).\end{split}

By Gauss-Bonnet theorem we have that

\int_{\partial\Omega}H_{\Omega}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}=2\pi=\int_{\partial\Omega^{*}}H_{\Omega^{*}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1},

so that

\begin{split}\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)&\leq\beta\left(\dfrac{P(\Omega^{*})^{2}}{P(\Omega^{*})+\beta m}+\dfrac{\varepsilon m(2P(\Omega^{*})+\beta m)}{2(P(\Omega^{*})+\beta m)^{2}}\int_{\partial\Omega^{*}}H_{\Omega^{*}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\right)\\[10.0pt]
&=\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega^{*},m/P(\Omega^{*})),\end{split}

which is (
4.17
).
∎

In the general case of possibly higher dimensions, we have

Proposition 4.14
.

Let
n\geq 3
,
2\leq k\leq n-1
, and let
\Omega
be a bounded, open, convex set with
C^{2}
boundary and nonzero Gaussian curvature such that either

W_{k}(\Omega)\geq\omega_{n}\left(\dfrac{3(n-1)\varepsilon}{2\beta}\right)^{n-k}

(4.18)

or

W_{k}(\Omega)\leq\omega_{n}\left(\dfrac{(n-1)\varepsilon}{2\beta}\right)^{n-k}.

(4.19)

Then

\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega)\leq\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega^{*})

where
\Omega^{*}
is the ball such that
W_{k}(\Omega)=W_{k}(\Omega^{*})
.

Proof.

Since
W_{k}(\Omega)=W_{k}(\Omega^{*})
, from the Alexandrov-Fenchel inequalities, we have that for every
0\leq i\leq k

\left(\frac{W_{i}(\Omega^{*})}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-i}}=\left(\frac{W_{k}(\Omega^{*})}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-k}}=\left(\frac{W_{k}(\Omega)}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-k}}\geq\left(\frac{W_{i}(\Omega)}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-i}},

that is

W_{i}(\Omega)\leq W_{i}(\Omega^{*}).

In particular, we have that

P(\Omega)\leq P(\Omega^{*})

and

\int_{\partial\Omega}H_{\Omega}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\leq\int_{\partial\Omega^{*}}H_{\Omega^{*}}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}.

Moreover, since

\left(\frac{W_{k}(\Omega^{*})}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-k}}=\left(\frac{W_{1}(\Omega^{*})}{\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}=\left(\dfrac{P(\Omega^{*})}{n\omega_{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n-1}}=\frac{n-1}{H_{\Omega^{*}}},

the conditions (
4.18
) and (
4.19
) read as

\dfrac{\varepsilon H_{\Omega^{*}}}{\beta}\leq\dfrac{2}{3},

and

\dfrac{\varepsilon H_{\Omega^{*}}}{\beta}\geq 2,

respectively. Therefore, the result can be obtained following the proof of
Subsection 4.2
.
∎

4.3
Final remarks

Let
\Omega,\Omega_{0}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be bounded open sets
with smooth boundary such that
\overline{\Omega}\subset\overline{\Omega}_{0}
and let

\mathcal{I}_{\beta}(\Omega,\Omega_{0})=\inf\Set{\int_{\Omega_{0}}\lvert\nabla u\rvert^{2}\,dx+\beta\int_{\partial\Omega_{0}}u^{2}\,d\mathcal{H}^{n-1}}{\begin{aligned} &\,u\in H^{1}(\Omega_{0}),\\
&\,u\geq 1\,\text{ in }\overline{\Omega}\end{aligned}}.

The results in
Subsection 4.2
and
Subsection 4.2
are coherent with the ones proved in
[
10
]
for the functional

\mathcal{I}_{\beta,\delta}(\Omega)=\mathcal{I}_{\beta}(\Omega,\Omega+\delta B),

where
\Omega_{0}=\Omega+\delta B
is the Minkowski sum of
\Omega
and the unit ball
B=B_{1}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Namely,
in
[
10
]
, the following theorems are proved

Theorem 4.15
.

Let
\Omega
be a connected, bounded, open set in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
with piecewise
C^{1}
boundary. Then

\mathcal{I}_{\beta,\delta}(\Omega)\leq\mathcal{I}_{\beta,\delta}(\Omega^{*}),

where
\Omega^{*}
is the ball having the same perimeter as
\Omega
.

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
n\geq 3
and let
\Omega
be a bounded, open, convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\mathcal{I}_{\beta,\delta}(\Omega)\leq\mathcal{I}_{\beta,\delta}(\Omega^{*}),

where
\Omega^{*}
is the ball such that
W_{n-1}(\Omega^{*})=W_{n-1}(\Omega)
.

Finally, in
[
5
]
, it is proved the following

Theorem 4.17
.

The solution to the problem

\min\Set{I_{\beta}(\Omega,\Omega_{0})}{\begin{aligned} &\lvert\Omega\rvert=\omega_{n},\\
&\lvert\Omega_{0}\rvert\leq M\end{aligned}}

consists of two concentric balls.

The previous theorem naturally leads to the following question

Open problem 1
.

Prove or disprove that the problem

\min\Set{\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(\Omega,h)}{\begin{aligned} &\lvert\Omega\rvert=\omega_{n},\\
&h\in\mathcal{H}_{m}\end{aligned}}

admits the couple
(B_{1},h^{*})
as a solution, where
h^{*}
is a constant function.

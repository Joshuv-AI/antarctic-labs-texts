On the difference between Fixed-Order and Contour-Improved Perturbation Theory
Maarten Golterman,

Abstract




Using standard mathematical methods for asymptotic series and the large-
\beta_{0}
approximation, we define a Minimum Distance between the Fixed-Order perturbative series and the Contour-Improved perturbative series in the strong coupling
\alpha_{s}
for finite-energy sum rules as applied to hadronic
\tau
decays.
This distance is similar, but not identical, to the Asymptotic Separation of Hoang and Regner, which is defined in terms of the difference of the two series after Borel resummation. Our results confirm a nonzero nonperturbative result in
\alpha_{s}
for this Minimum Distance as a measure of the intrinsic difference between the two series, as well as a conflict with the Operator Product Expansion for Contour-Improved Perturbation Theory.

I
Introduction and motivation

The determination of
\alpha_{s}
from an analysis of the hadronic
\tau
decay has a very long and successful history, which is linked to the use of Finite Energy Sum Rules (FESRs)
shankar
-
BNP
. These FESRs rely on the analyticity of the Vector (and Axial) two-point correlator to relate integrals of the spectral function to complex contour integrals of perturbation theory supplemented by the nonperturbative contribution from the Operator Product Expansion (OPE). Because the mass of the
\tau
lepton is not much larger than the scale of QCD, one of the problems that one has to face is that of the (non)convergence of the perturbative series, a property associated with renormalons
Beneke:1998ui
. Even the OPE as a whole has been recognized as
constituting a nonconvergent expansion, and this property is at the origin of
(quark-hadron) Duality Violations
PQW
-
BCGMP
.

Although the QCD perturbative series is expected to be only asymptotic, multiple ideas have been explored to compensate for this lack of convergence by a reorganization of the terms in the series in order to help approach the final result at the fastest possible rate
Caprini
-
Abbas
. Two of these organizations of the perturbative series have been predominant in analyses of the hadronic
\tau
decay: Fixed-Order Perturbation Theory (FOPT) and Contour-Improved Perturbation Theory (CIPT)
CIPT
-
CIPT2
.

The essential difference between FOPT and CIPT is in the choice of the renormalization scale
\mu^{2}
which is present in any perturbative calculation. Usually the natural choice for a QCD correlator with an external scale
s_{0}
has been to take
\mu^{2}
equal to this external scale to minimize the contribution of the ubiquitous logarithms of the form
\log(s_{0}/\mu^{2})
appearing in the perturbative series. This is the choice made in FOPT. However, the existence of the contour of integration in the complex plane in the FESRs also allows the possibility to make the choice
\mu^{2}=z
to kill the logarithmic contribution of the form
\log(z/\mu^{2})
along the whole contour of integration in the
z
complex plane, and this latter choice is the one made in CIPT.

The proponents of CIPT defend their position by arguing that this choice resums the perturbative series along the whole complex contour, resulting in a series which, at least for the lowest orders that have been calculated in
\tau
decay so far, seems to be better behaved,
i.e.
, with smaller term-by-term contributions. However, apart from its unique application,
1
1

1

To the best of our knowledge, a choice like CIPT has never been made in any QCD analysis apart from its use in FESR
analyses. It is far from obvious whether the CIPT
\alpha_{s}
is the same coupling as that extracted from an observable for which there exists no contour to consider.
the partial resummation carried out by CIPT is potentially suspect. As mentioned above, the QCD perturbative series is asymptotic and it turns out that this partial resummation disrupts a cancellation between the leading perturbative orders from the Adler function and the orders originating from the contour of integration. In fact, in the large-
\beta_{0}
approximation this cancellation is exact
BJ
.

Recently, the seminal work of Ref.
Hoang:2020mkw
has substantially contributed to a clarification of the difference between FOPT and CIPT. These authors have pointed out that the Borel transform which generates the CIPT series is different from that of the FOPT series, resulting in a irreducible difference, of
\mathcal{O}(e^{-c/\alpha_{s}})
(with
\alpha_{s}
the strong coupling and
c
some positive constant), which they refer to as the ‘‘Asymptotic Separation” (AS). Since the renormalon structure of the FOPT series, and in particular its associated ambiguities, match the contributions from the OPE as expected,
2
2

2

Any ambiguity generated by the perturbative series is expected to be cured by a contribution from the OPE, since the combined result should be unambiguous.
this extra nonperturbative contribution present in the CIPT series is in conflict with the OPE or, at least, with its standard version
Shifman:1978bx
, in that it suggests the existence of nonperturbative terms which go beyond the OPE.

However, in spite of this, in some analyses it has been standard practice to assume the same OPE for FOPT and for CIPT, taking an average of the results obtained for
\alpha_{s}
in both cases, and taking the difference as some sort of systematic error
Pich:2022tca
. In view of the results of Ref.
Hoang:2020mkw
, this procedure now has turned out to be inconsistent.

What makes sense, once the source for this irregular behavior of the CIPT series has been identified, is to design a cure for it. Reference
Benitez-Rathgeb:2022yqb
has presented a scheme to bring CIPT into agreement with the OPE, and the results obtained largely confirm the results obtained with the FOPT method.

The purpose of the present note is to discuss the difference between Fixed-Order Perturbation Theory (FOPT) and Contour-Improved Perturbation Theory (CIPT), following the analysis of Hoang and Regner
Hoang:2020mkw
. However, in contrast to the approach of Ref.
Hoang:2020mkw
, which considers this difference at the level of a Borel transform of the contour integration generally used in
\tau
-decay analyses, in this work we will consider the asymptotic series which results after the integration over this contour is carried out, order by order, at the level of the perturbative series expansion. The result of this analysis confirms that there is a nonperturbative difference between the FOPT and CIPT asymptotic series, as pointed out in Ref.
Hoang:2020mkw
, and we agree with the main message of that reference, namely, that this nonperturbative difference is at odds with the OPE. In addition, we show how to reproduce the results of Ref.
Hoang:2020mkw
in a different way, at least in the large-
\beta_{0}
approximation. However, our new result for the difference at the level of the series is not the same as the AS obtained at the level of Borel sums in Ref.
Hoang:2020mkw

In this work we will limit ourselves to the large-
\beta_{0}
case. We do this because this case is
considerably simpler while at the
same time containing the essential

discussion of the main results. Therefore, let us define the variable

(2)

The Renormalization Group equation in terms of

(3)

For future reference, let us note that

II
FOPT for the Adler function and arbitrary moment
x^{m}

The Adler function,
D(z)
posseses a renormalon structure
Beneke:1998ui
which admits a Borel representation
given by

\displaystyle D(z)=-z\frac{d\Pi(z)}{dz}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,B(\omega)\,e^{-\frac{\omega}{\alpha(-z)}}

(4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,B(\omega)\left(\frac{-z}{\mu^{2}}\right)^{-\omega}\,e^{-\frac{\omega}{\alpha(\mu^{2})}}

in terms of the Borel function,
B(\omega)
, whose singularities closest to the origin are located at integer values of
\omega
. The second equation in Eq. (
4
) follows from Eq. (
3
).
In Eq. (
4
),
\Pi(z)
stands for the scalar function associated with the vacuum polarization tensor of the two-point vector correlator.

One distinguishes the ultraviolet (UV) renormalons, as those sigularities located at negative values of
\omega
, and the infrared (IR) renormalons, with singularities located at positive values of
\omega
. In most of this work we will concentrate on the IR renormalons, as they will turn out to be mainly responsible for the FOP-CIPT difference, but we will also comment on the UV renormalons and clarify their role, if any, in regard to this difference.

In general, these singularities are branch points but, in the large-
\beta_{0}
approximation, the IR renormalons are simply poles in the Borel function
B(\omega)
: a simple pole at
\omega=2
plus an infinite sequence of double poles at
\omega=3,4,5,...
. Therefore, we may concentrate on the contribution of an individual pole to the Adler function by considering the integral

D(z)|_{p}=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\left(\frac{-z}{\mu^{2}}\right)^{-\omega}\,\frac{1}{(p-\omega)^{\gamma}}\,e^{-\frac{\omega}{\alpha(\mu^{2})}}\ .

(5)

where, as we have discussed,
\gamma=1
or
2
in the large-
\beta_{0}
approximation.

At this level the distinction between FOPT and CIPT lies in the choice for the scale
\mu
. In FOPT, this choice amounts to taking
\mu^{2}=s_{0}
, where
s_{0}
is a typical external Euclidean scale in the problem which, in the particular case of the hadronic
\tau
decay, may be simply identified with
m_{\tau}^{2}
. Because the
\tau
mass is not much larger than the hadronic scale, analyses of the decay of the
\tau
lepton traditionally use the so-called Finite Energy Sum Rules (FESRs), which consist of moments,
A_{m}(s_{0})
, of contour integrals of radius
|z|=s_{0}
around the origin of the complex plane. Therefore, we will study integrals of the type

A_{m}(s_{0})=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|x|=1}\frac{dx}{x}\,x^{m}\,D(xs_{0})\ .

(6)

Upon inserting (
5
) into (
6
), and using the identity

-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|x|=1}\frac{dx}{x}\,x^{m}\,(-x)^{-\omega}=\frac{\sin(\pi\omega)}{\pi(m-\omega)}\ ,

(7)

one easily obtains that

A_{m}(s_{0})=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|x|=1}\frac{dx}{x}\,x^{m}\,D(s_{0}x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\left(\frac{\sin(\pi\omega)}{(m-\omega)(p-\omega)^{\gamma}}\right)\,e^{-\frac{\omega}{\alpha(s_{0})}}\ .

(8)

The case
\gamma=1
is particularly simple as this integral is well-defined for all values of
m
except
m=p
, for
which it diverges. For
m\neq p
, the integrand in Eq. (
8
) is analytic everywhere in the complex
\omega
plane and has an
infinite
radius of convergence in
\omega
. This allows the integration to be carried out even after expanding the expression in parenthesis in powers of
\omega
, resulting in a convergent power expansion in
\alpha
for the integral, whose radius of convergence we show
to be equal to
1/\pi
in App.
A
. In this case, the Borel transform of FOPT in Eq. (
8
) defines an analytic function which we will assume to be the
exact
answer. This will be sufficient for our purposes.
3
3

3

Even when a convergent power expansion defines an analytic function in
\alpha
, this function might differ from the exact solution by terms
\mathcal{O}(e^{-1/\alpha})
.
The situation will be different in the CIPT case, which is key to the
arguments of Ref.
Hoang:2020mkw
and this paper.

In the general case, the pole at
\omega=p
is not simple but double (
i.e.
,
\gamma=2
), or even a branch point if one goes beyond the large-
\beta_{0}
approximation. In these cases, even FOPT needs to be regulated and it is common practice to do so according to the Principal Value prescription, defined as the average with respect to an
\pm i\epsilon
shift in both singularities at
\omega=p
and
\omega=m
, taking the limit
\epsilon\to 0
at the end. The fact that the result has a finite limit when
\epsilon\to 0
means that we are defining the Borel integral by analytic continuation.

The FOPT series, being an expansion in powers of
\alpha
, can therefore be obtained as

A_{m}(s_{0})=-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{|x|=1}\frac{dx}{x}\,x^{m}\,D(s_{0}x)=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\left[\frac{\sin(\pi\omega)}{(m-\omega)(p-\omega)^{\gamma}}\right]_{T}\,e^{-\frac{\omega}{\alpha(s_{0})}}\ ,

(9)

where the symbol
\left[H(\omega)\right]_{T}
denotes the Taylor expansion of the function
H(\omega)
around
\omega=0
, integrated term by term.

To study the difference between FOPT and CIPT and make contact with the Asymptotic Separation defined in Ref.
Hoang:2020mkw
, it will prove convenient to split the
\sin(\pi\omega)=\frac{1}{2i}(e^{i\pi\omega}-e^{-i\pi\omega})
, and
integrate each term in the difference separately. This requires the poles at
\omega=p
and
\omega=m
to be regulated and we will do so using the common Principal Value prescription mentioned above.

III
CIPT for the Adler function and arbitrary moment
x^{m}
and the Asymptotic Separation of Hoang and Regner

Unlike in the case of FOPT, the CIPT choice for the scale
\mu^{2}
in the contour moment (
6

\mu^{2}=z
in Eq. (
5

D(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\left[\frac{1}{(p-\omega)^{\gamma}}\right]_{T}\,e^{-\frac{\omega}{\alpha(-z)}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,n!\,\frac{B(-\gamma,n)}{p^{n+\gamma}}\,\alpha^{n+1}(-z)\ ,

(10)

where
B(-1,n)=1
,
B(-2,n)=n+1

To obtain

in CIPT, we again use the identity (
7

A_{m}(s_{0})=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\left[\frac{1}{(p-\omega)^{\gamma}}\right]_{T}\frac{\sin(\pi\omega)}{m-\omega}e^{-\omega/\alpha(s_{0})}\ ,

(11)

for its corresponding series expansion. This amounts to carrying out the contour integration of Eq. (
10

term by term, because
\frac{\sin(\pi\omega)}{m-\omega}
is an analytic function everywhere in the complex plane, and
thus equals its Taylor expansion everywhere. There is thus no need to deform the contour, as was done
in Ref.
Hoang:2020mkw
, where the series was Borel resummed first.

We note the difference between the FOPT series in Eq. (
9
) and the CIPT counterpart in Eq. (
11
). Resumming the series in Eq. (
11
) by removing the symbol
\left[\ \right]_{T}
immediately reproduces the FOPT result in Eq. (
8
). Why are these two series different then? In the particular case of a simple pole,
\gamma=1
, one can easily see where the difference comes from: The expansion of
\frac{1}{p-\omega}
in powers of
\omega
and consequent integration term by term is a mathematically illegal operation since this expansion converges only
for
|\omega|\leq p
, whereas
\omega
is integrated all the way to infinity. This causes the series (
11
) to be asymptotic, unlike the FOPT result in Eq. (
9
) since
\sin(\pi\omega)/((m-\omega)(p-\omega))
has no singularity in
\omega
for
m\neq p
, and therefore, an infinite radius of convergence in a power expansion in
\omega
. This leads to the finite radius of convergence of the
FOPT expansion in powers of
\alpha
found in App.
A
.

As an asymptotic expansion, CIPT in Eq. (
11
) will approach a certain value, in general different from Eq. (
8
), before it diverges. In Ref.
Hoang:2020mkw
, a Borel sum for the CIPT expansion
was defined, and an explicit expression for the difference between the CIPT and FOPT Borel sums, the “Asymptotic Separation” (AS) was obtained.

In order to calculate this AS we will follow a slightly different route from Ref.
Hoang:2020mkw
which, in our opinion, requires less mathematical complexity and may clarify some aspects of the final result, at least in the large-
\beta_{0}
approximation. To do this, it is useful to split the
\sin(\pi\omega)
in Eq. (
11

\gamma=1,2

\displaystyle\Delta(\alpha,p,m,\gamma,\epsilon,k)=

(12)

\displaystyle\hskip-28.45274pt\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\frac{1}{(p+i\epsilon-\omega)^{\gamma}}\,\ \frac{e^{-\frac{\omega}{\alpha}\left(1-ik\pi\alpha\right)}}{m+i\epsilon-\omega}-\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\left[\frac{1}{(p-\omega)^{\gamma}}\right]_{T}\,\ \frac{e^{-\frac{\omega}{\alpha}\left(1-ik\pi\alpha\right)}}{m+i\epsilon-\omega}\ ,

\alpha\equiv\alpha(s_{0})

\Delta\sim\mathcal{O}(e^{-p/\alpha})

If we could remove the symbol

Hoang:2020mkw
defines the moments with
(-x)^{m}
, so that their expressions differ
by
(-1)^{m}
from ours.
This shows the equivalence of both methods, at least for the large-
\beta_{0}
approximation considered here. Clearly, the case
m=p
is special as the above expressions for
\Delta_{\rm AS}(\alpha,p,p,\gamma)
are ill-defined. In this case, Hoang and Regner in Ref.
Hoang:2020mkw

IV
The Minimum Distance (MD) between the FOPT and the CIPT Series

In this section we would like to discuss another possible approach to the FOPT-CIPT difference which, unlike that of Ref.
Hoang:2020mkw
, is based on the CIPT series for the contour moments
A_{m}(s_{0})
themselves rather than on its Borel resummation. After all, it is the series one considers in the actual analyses of
hadronic
\tau
decays in practice.

The starting point of this discussion is the fact that the difference between the FOPT Borel representation in Eq. (
8
) and the sum of the first
n-1
terms of the CIPT
series expansion
in Eq. (
11
) can be written as (
\gamma=1,2
)

\Delta_{\rm SER}(\alpha,p,m,\gamma,n)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\!\!d\omega\,\frac{e^{-\omega/\alpha}\ \sin(\pi\omega)}{(m-\omega+i\epsilon)(p-\omega+i\epsilon)^{\gamma}}\left(\frac{\omega}{p}\right)^{n}\left(\frac{p+n(p-\omega)}{p}\right)^{\gamma-1}\ ,

(26)

where the limit
\epsilon\to 0
will always be taken in the end. The series
\Delta_{\rm SER}(\alpha,p,m,\gamma,n)
constitutes an asymptotic series as a function of the order
n
and, as such, it comes within a minimum distance of zero for some value of
n=n^{*}
, where it stabilizes for a few orders before it diverges. The value of this minimum distance at the order
n=n^{*}
is the difference between the FOPT Borel result and the CIPT series,
\Delta_{\rm SER}(\alpha,p,m,\gamma,n^{*})
, we are interested in. General properties of asymptotic expansions suggest that we should expect this value of
n^{*}
to be of
\mathcal{O}(1/\alpha)
, and this minimum distance of
\mathcal{O}(e^{-p/\alpha})
.

In order to verify that this is indeed the case, we carry out a saddle point approximation and express
\Delta_{\rm SER}
as

\Delta_{\rm SER}(\alpha,p,m,\gamma,n)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\omega\,\frac{e^{\Phi(\omega,n)}\ \sin(\pi\omega)}{(m-\omega+i\epsilon)(p-\omega+i\epsilon)^{\gamma}}\left(\frac{p+n(p-\omega)}{p}\right)^{\gamma-1}\ ,

(27)

where

\displaystyle\Phi(\omega,n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\omega}{\alpha}+n\log\frac{\omega}{p}

(28)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle-\frac{\omega_{0}}{\alpha}+n\log\frac{\omega_{0}}{p}-\frac{n}{2}\frac{(\omega-\omega_{0})^{2}}{{\omega_{0}}^{2}}\ ,

and
\omega_{0}=n\alpha
is the value at which the derivative vanishes,
\Phi^{\prime}(\omega_{0},n)=0
.
Setting
\omega_{0}=n\alpha
, and
imposing the asymptotic stability condition
\Phi(\omega,n^{*})\sim\Phi(\omega,n^{*}+1)
leads to
n^{*}=p/\alpha
and, consequently,
\omega_{0}=p
.
8
8

8

The factor
\frac{p+n(p-\omega)}{p}
is equal to one for
\omega=\omega_{0}=p
.
The exponential factor in (
27
), therefore, becomes

e^{\Phi(\omega,n^{*})}\approx e^{-\frac{p}{\alpha}}\ e^{-\frac{1}{2}\,\frac{(\omega-p)^{2}}{p\alpha}}\quad,\quad n^{*}=\frac{p}{\alpha}\ ,

(29)

which yields a nonperturbative correction
\mathcal{O}(e^{-\frac{p}{\alpha}})
, as expected, times a gaussian centered at
\omega=p
with a narrow width of
\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{p\,\alpha})
. In fact, for
\alpha
small enough, the gaussian is so narrow that, when the integrand in Eq. (
27
) is well defined at
\omega=p
(as it is the case for
\gamma=1
and
m\neq p
), this gaussian may be replaced by a Dirac delta,
i.e.
,
\sim\delta(\omega-p)
. In particular when
p=-|p|
is a negative integer, as it is the case of UV renormalons, the
i\epsilon
prescription is not necessary since the integrand is regulated by the
\sin(\pi\omega)
and the integral (
26
) vanishes like
\sin(\pi\omega)\,\delta(\omega-|p|)/(p-\omega)
.
If the gaussian is not approximated by the Dirac delta, it will still be very much suppressed relative to the natural scale
\mathcal{O}(e^{-|p|/\alpha})
. The net result is that UV renormalons are not the main cause for the FOPT-CIPT difference, in agreement with the conclusion based on the AS criterion of Ref.
Hoang:2020mkw
and our discussion in the previous section.

We have checked the results (
29
) in a number of cases and the agreement for small
\alpha
is very good, being optimal when the value of
n^{*}=p/\alpha
is employed in the initial integral
\Delta_{\rm SER}
in Eq. (
26
) rather than using the alternate expression
obtained using the approximation (
28
) in Eq. (
27
). A possible exception appears to be the double pole in the Adler function for the case with
m=p
, for which, as we will see below, the subleading terms in
n^{*}=p/\alpha+...
are not negligible.

In what follows, we will compare in a few cases the “Minimum Distance” (MD), defined as

\Delta_{\rm SER}(\alpha,p,m,\gamma,n^{*})
with
n^{*}=p/\alpha
, with

\Delta_{\rm SER}(\alpha,p,m,\gamma,n)

as a function of
n

Hoang:2020mkw

V
Conclusions

For many years the difference between FOPT and CIPT has been a source of systematic uncertainty that has plagued the determination of
\alpha_{s}
from hadronic
\tau
decay. Recently, the authors of Ref.
Hoang:2020mkw
have pointed out that there is a measure for this difference in terms of the corresponding Borel transforms, which they termed the Asymptotic Separation. This separation is nonperturbative, of
\mathcal{O}(e^{-1/\alpha_{s}})
, and therefore in conflict with the OPE, which makes CIPT-based results for
\alpha_{s}
inconsistent with the OPE. For example, for
m=1
the results in Eqs. (
25
) and (
31
) show a nonperturbative result which has no OPE counterpart as the residue theorem removes any power corrections to the corresponding moment.
10
10

10

This is true in the large-
\beta_{0}
approximation but there is no reason to believe this does not carry over to the case of full QCD.
Consequently, CIPT results should not be included in any average for
\alpha_{s}
, at least until schemes such as that proposed in Ref.
Benitez-Rathgeb:2022yqb
, designed to fix the problems of CIPT, lead to new determinations of
\alpha_{s}
consistent with the OPE.

In the present work, using the large-
\beta_{0}
approximation, we have confirmed the findings in Ref.
Hoang:2020mkw
. Using standard analysis tools for asymptotic series, we have introduced a measure of the distance between the FOPT Borel sum and the CIPT perturbative series directly, and not in terms of a Borel sum for the CIPT series, which we called the Minimum Distance. As in the case of the AS, we have found several examples where this Minimum Distance is not zero, and we believe this result to be generic. In the large-
\beta_{0}
approximation employed here, there are cases (
e.g.
the simple pole,
\gamma=1
and
m\neq p
) where the FOPT series is convergent to its Borel sum and, consequently, this sum can be taken to be the exact answer for this series.

The Minimum Distance characterizes the nonperturbative difference between the FOPT and CIPT series without the
need to define a Borel sum for the CIPT series. As such, unlike the AS, it relies on defining a Borel sum only
for the FOPT series, which is consistent with the OPE. It thus reinforces the conclusion that the CIPT
series is inconsistent with the OPE.

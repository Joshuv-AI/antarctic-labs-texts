Thinplate Splines on the Sphere
Rick K. BEATSON † and Wolfgang ZU CASTELL ‡§

Abstract
In this paper we give explicit closed forms for the semi-reproducing kernels associated with thinplate spline interpolation on the sphere. Polyharmonic or thinplate splines for ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} were introduced by Duchon and have become a widely used tool in myriad applications. The analogues for 𝕊 d − 1 {\mathbb{S}}^{d-1} are the thin plate splines for the sphere. The topic was first discussed by Wahba in the early 1980’s, for the 𝕊 2 {\mathbb{S}}^{2} case. Wahba presented the associated semi-reproducing kernels as infinite series. These semi-reproducing kernels play a central role in expressions for the solution of the associated spline interpolation and smoothing problems. The main aims of the current paper are to give a recurrence for the semi-reproducing kernels, and also to use the recurrence to obtain explicit closed form expressions for many of these kernels. The closed form expressions will in many cases be significantly faster to evaluate than the series expansions. This will enhance the practicality of using these thinplate splines for the sphere in computations.

Introduction

In this paper we give explicit closed forms for the semi-reproducing kernels associated with thinplate spline interpolation on the sphere. Polyharmonic or thinplate splines for
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
were introduced by Duchon in his classic papers
[
10
,
11
]
and have become a widely used tool in myriad applications. The analogues for
{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
are the thinplate splines for the sphere. The topic was first discussed by Wahba
[
22
,
23
]
in the early 1980’s, for the
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
case. Wahba presented the associated semi-reproducing kernels as infinite series. These semi-reproducing kernels play a central role in expressions for the solution of the associated spline interpolation and smoothing problems.

The main aims of the current paper are to give a recurrence for these semi-reproducing kernels, and also to use the recurrence to obtain explicit closed form expressions. Here we are building on previous work of Martinez-Morales
[
16
]
. Unfortunately, there are errors in the theory presented in
[
16
]
and consequently many of the expressions given there for the kernels are incorrect. The closed form expressions given here will usually be significantly faster to evaluate than the series expansions. This will enhance the practicality of using the thinplate splines for the sphere in computations.

The paper is laid out as follows. Section
2
discusses the central role played by semi-reproducing kernels in the solution of both interpolation and penalized least squares fitting problems. Section
3
develops semi-reproducing kernels associated with the thinplate splines on the sphere, that is, semi-reproducing kernels associated with minimum energy interpolation and penalized least squares fitting problems with a particular choice of energy. The energy chosen being that naturally associated with iterated Laplace–Beltrami operators. These semi-reproducing kernels are given in this section as infinite series. Section
3
also recalls known results concerning Fourier–Gegenbauer expansions that will be needed later. Section
4
motivates the construction of an operator
T
and its adjoint
T^{*}
. It also presents some fundamental properties of these operators. These operators were initially developed in Martinez-Morales
[
16
]
. Section
5
gives a recurrence for the various thinplate spline kernels
K_{d,m}(x,y)
, where
d
indicates the dimension and
m
is the power of the associated differential operator. More precisely, it gives a recurrence for the related functions
k_{d,m}\colon[-1,1]\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
where
k_{d,m}(\langle x,y\rangle)=K_{d,m}(x,y)
. Sections
6
and
7
give short closed form expressions for many of the functions
k_{d,m}
.

For ease of access to the relevant background we will base our notation on that used in Dai and Xu
[
8
]
. Occasionally, when the value for the dimension
d
is particularly important, we will supplement the symbol they use with a
d
.

Let us begin with summarising the main ideas of reproducing kernels in indefinite inner product spaces and their role in both interpolation and penalized least squares fitting problems. These results show the central role of reproducing kernels in the solution of these problems. We focus on the specific case of reproducing kernels

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a subset of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. Consider approximation from a vector space over the reals
{\mathcal{H}}\subset C({\mathcal{D}})
. Assume the space
{\mathcal{H}}

f\in{\mathcal{H}}

(f,f)=0

{\mathcal{H}}_{0}

\dim{\mathcal{H}}_{0}=m<\infty

(f,f)=0

A standard approach to deal with semi-inner products is to supplement the semi-inner product with an inner product on
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
thereby obtaining a definite inner product on the space
{\mathcal{H}}
(see
[
5

{\mathcal{H}}
into a direct sum
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}_{1}

{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
. Given
m

{\mathcal{H}}_{0}

{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
as the space of functions in
{\mathcal{H}}
which are mapped onto zero by all these functionals.

Let us recall this approach using point evaluations. Nevertheless, it is important to note that there are many choices for such a set of functionals. Clearly, the decomposition obtained for the space
{\mathcal{H}}
depends upon the choice made for the
m

Definition 2.1
.

A set of distinct points
{\mathcal{X}}
is said to be unisolvent for
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
if the only function in
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
which is zero at all points of
{\mathcal{X}}
is the zero function.

Given a unisolvent set
{\mathcal{X}}=\{z_{1},\ldots,x_{m}\}
for
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
, where
m=\dim({\mathcal{H}}_{0})
, the set of point evaluation functionals
\{\delta_{x}\colon x\in{\mathcal{X}}\}
is linearly independent on
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
. Hence, we can find a Lagrange basis
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
of
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
with respect to
{\mathcal{X}}
, i.e.,
u_{i}(x_{j})=\delta_{ij}
,
1\leq i,j\leq m
. Then

\displaystyle[f,g]_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}f(x_{j})g(x_{j}),\qquad f,g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0},

defines an inner product on
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
. The basis
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}
is orthogonal with respect to the inner product

[\cdot,\cdot]_{0}
. Furthermore, the mapping

\displaystyle P_{0}\colon\ {\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}_{0},\qquad f\mapsto f_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}f(x_{j})u_{j}

is a projection of
{\mathcal{H}}
onto
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
. The definition of inner product for the full space
{\mathcal{H}}
which follows will make
P_{0}
the orthogonal projection onto
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
. The subspace
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
can then be defined via the projector
P_{1}=I-P_{0}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{1}=\{f\in{\mathcal{H}}\colon f(x)=0\ \text{for all}\ x\in{\mathcal{X}}\}.

Since
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
is the kernel of the semi-inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
, i.e.,
(f,f)=0
iff
f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
, the semi-inner product is definite on
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
via construction. Therefore,

\displaystyle[f,g]=[P_{0}f,P_{0}g]_{0}+(P_{1}f,P_{1}g),\qquad f,g\in{\mathcal{H}},

defines a definite inner product on
{\mathcal{H}}
(see
[
5
]
for further details).

We are interested in Hilbert spaces carrying the special property of being reproducing kernel spaces. There is a one-to-one correspondence between reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces and positive definite kernels. A similar relation holds true for semi-reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces.

Definition 2.2
.

Given
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, a pair
({\mathcal{X}},{\boldsymbol{a}})
with
{\mathcal{X}}=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
a set of distinct points from
{\mathcal{D}}
and
{\boldsymbol{a}}=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})^{\rm T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is called an
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
-increment if

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}f(x_{j})=0\qquad\text{for all}\ f\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0}.

The set of all
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
-increments is denoted by
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}^{\perp}
.

Note that an
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
-increment can naturally be identified with a linear functional

\displaystyle\lambda_{{\mathcal{X}},{\boldsymbol{a}}}(f)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\delta_{x_{j}}(f),\qquad f\in{\mathcal{H}},

vanishing on
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
.

The structure of semi-reproducing kernels already shows that we can expect the reproducing kernel for a semi-Hilbert space to be positive definite only on a suitable subspace.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
be a finite dimensional subset of
C({\mathcal{D}})
and
K\colon{\mathcal{D}}\times{\mathcal{D}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
be a symmetric function.
K
is called conditionally positive definite with respect to
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
if for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{i}a_{j}K(x_{i},x_{j})\geq 0,

(2.1)

for all
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
-increments
({\mathcal{X}},{\boldsymbol{a}})
.
K
is said to be strictly conditionally positive definite with respect to
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
if the inequality in (
2.1
) is strict whenever in addition
{\boldsymbol{a}}
is nonzero.

There is a correspondence between conditionally positive definite kernels and semi-reproducing spaces (see
[
2
,
17
,
20
]
). The associated reproducing property can again be stated in terms of
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
-increments.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}\subset C({\mathcal{D}})
be a semi-Hilbert space, i.e.,
{\mathcal{H}}
is a semi-inner product space with semi-inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
the kernel
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
of which is finite-dimensional, and
{\mathcal{H}}
is complete with respect to the induced semi-norm. A symmetric kernel

is called a semi-reproducing kernel for
{\mathcal{H}}

reproduces
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
-increments, i.e., for all
\lambda_{{\mathcal{X}},{\boldsymbol{a}}}
annihilating
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}K(\cdot,x_{j})\in{\mathcal{H}},

(2.2)

and

\displaystyle\left(f,\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}K(\cdot,x_{i})\right)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}f(x_{j})\qquad\text{for all}\ f\in{\mathcal{H}}.

(2.3)

Given

\displaystyle u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\qquad\mbox{ such that }\quad\lambda_{j}(u_{i})=\delta_{ji},\quad 1\leq i,j\leq m,

\displaystyle K_{0}(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}(x)u_{j}(y),\qquad x,y\in{\mathcal{D}},

obviously provides a reproducing kernel for

\displaystyle[f,g]_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}(f)\lambda_{j}(g),\qquad f,g\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0}.

\displaystyle P_{0}f=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}(f)u_{j},\qquad f\in{\mathcal{H}},

we can again define

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}_{1}=\{f\in{\mathcal{H}}\colon\lambda_{j}(f)=0,\,1\leq j\leq m\}.

\displaystyle K_{1}(x,y)=K(x,y)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}(y)\lambda_{j}\big(K(x,\cdot)\big)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}u_{j}(x)\lambda_{j}(K\big(\cdot,y)\big)

is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space itself with reproducing kernel

\displaystyle K_{\mathcal{H}}(x,y)=K_{1}(x,y)+K_{0}(x,y),\qquad x,y\in{\mathcal{D}}.

3
Series representations for thinplate spline kernels

on the sphere

For functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
interpolating and smoothing with thinplate/polyharmonic splines associated with the energy

\displaystyle E_{\kappa}(f)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sum_{i_{1}=1,i_{2}=1,\ldots,i_{\kappa}=1}^{d}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_{1}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_{2}}}\cdots\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i_{\kappa}}}f(x)\right)^{2}{\rm d}x,

are very successful approximation methods. For sufficiently smooth functions
f
, decaying sufficiently fast at infinity, integration by parts gives

\displaystyle E_{\kappa}(f)=(-1)^{\kappa}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x)(\triangle^{\kappa}f)(x){\rm d}x,

where
\triangle
is the Laplacian. Analogues for the sphere come from considering instead of the Laplacian the Laplace–Beltrami operator
\triangle^{\star}
, and working on the “Fourier” side since the spherical harmonics
\{Y_{nj}\}
are both a complete orthonormal system for
L^{2}\big({{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}\big)
and also eigenfunctions of the Laplace–Beltrami operator. More precisely,

\displaystyle\triangle^{\star}Y_{j}^{n}=-n(n+d-2)Y_{j}^{n},\qquad j=1,\ldots,N_{d,n},\qquad n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

For the explicit value of the dimension
N_{d,n}
see (
3.1
), below.

This section will consider corresponding spaces of functions on the sphere and the relevant semi-reproducing kernels
K_{d,m}
. The central role played by these semi-reproducing kernels in minimal energy interpolation and regularised least squares fitting is clear from the discussion in Section
2
, and in particular Theorem
2.5
. This topic was first considered by Wahba
[
22
]
in the
{\mathbb{S}}^{2}
case. See also Wahba’s monograph
[
24
]
. The reader can find valuable additional relevant material in Freeden, Gervens and Schreiner
[
12
, Chapter 5]
, Levesley, Light, Ragozin and Sun
[
15
]
, and Cheney and Light
[
7
, Chapter 32]
. The material in these references differs somewhat from what appears here. Usually this is due to a treatment based on reproducing kernels rather than semi-reproducing kernels, or to a different choice of the energy. Gneiting
[
13
]
gives an excellent survey of recent work concerning kernels for the sphere.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}^{d}_{n}
denote the space of real harmonic polynomials homogeneous of degree
n
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
. The spherical harmonics are the restrictions of these to the sphere
{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}
. In a slight abuse of notation the space of spherical harmonics of degree
n
on
{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}
is also written
{\mathcal{H}}^{d}_{n}
. The dimension of the space is

\displaystyle N_{d,n}=\dim{\mathcal{H}}^{d}_{n}=\binom{n+d-1}{n}-\binom{n+d-3}{n-2}.

(3.1)

Spherical harmonics are a complete orthogonal system on
L^{2}\big({{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}\big)
with respect to the inner product

\displaystyle[f,g]_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}}=\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}}\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}}f(x)g(x){\rm d}\sigma(x),

(3.2)

where

\displaystyle\sigma_{d}=\frac{2\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)}=\frac{2\pi^{\lambda+1}}{\Gamma(\lambda+1)}

is the surface area of
{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}
(for
\lambda
see (
3.3
) below).

For the set
\{Y_{j}^{n}\colon 1\leq j\leq N_{d,n}\}
, being an orthonormal basis for
{\mathcal{H}}^{d}_{n}
, the addition formula

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d,n}}Y_{j}^{n}(x)Y_{j}^{n}(y)=\frac{n+\lambda}{\lambda}C^{\lambda}_{n}\big(x^{\rm T}y\big)=N_{d,n}W^{\lambda}_{n}\big(x^{\rm T}y\big),\qquad\lambda=\frac{d-2}{2},

(3.3)

shows that the reproducing kernels of the spaces
{\mathcal{H}}^{d}_{n}
are the zonal polynomials
W_{n}^{\lambda}
which are Gegenbauer polynomials normalized so that
W^{\lambda}_{n}(1)=1
. The
Gegenbauer polynomial
of order
\lambda\geq 0
and degree
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
is defined as the hypergeometric polynomial

\displaystyle C_{n}^{\lambda}(x)=\frac{\Gamma(n+2\lambda)}{n!\Gamma(2\lambda)}\,{{{}_{2}F_{1}}\left[\left.\begin{matrix}-n,n+2\lambda\\
\lambda+\frac{1}{2}\\
\end{matrix}\right|\frac{1-x}{2}\right]},\qquad x\in[-1,1].

The Gegenbauer polynomials are orthogonal with respect to the inner product

\displaystyle[f,g]_{\lambda}=\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)g(x)\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}x.

(3.4)

Indeed,

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}C_{n}^{\lambda}(x)C_{m}^{\lambda}(x)\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}x=h_{n}^{\lambda}\delta_{nm},

(3.5)

where

\displaystyle h_{n}^{\lambda}=\frac{\pi\Gamma(2\lambda+n)}{2^{2\lambda-1}n!(\lambda+n)\Gamma^{2}(\lambda)}=\frac{\sigma_{d}}{\sigma_{d-1}}\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+n}C_{n}^{\lambda}(1).

Note that since the convolution of zonal functions on the sphere remains zonal, the inner product (
3.2
) reduces to (
3.4
) for zonal functions, where
\lambda=\frac{d-2}{2}
. The remaining weight function has the integral

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}x=h^{\lambda}_{0}=\frac{\sigma_{d}}{\sigma_{d-1}}C^{\lambda}_{0}(1).

Although, Gegenbauer polynomials provide a complete orthogonal system for all
\lambda>-\frac{1}{2}
, we will fix
\lambda=\frac{d-2}{2}
throughout this paper.

The constant
C_{n}^{\lambda}(1)
relates to the dimension
N_{d,n}
given in (
3.1
); indeed,

\displaystyle C_{n}^{\lambda}(1)=\binom{n+d-3}{d-3}=\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+n}N_{d,n}.

Since
\{Y_{j}^{n}\colon 1\leq j\leq N_{d,n},\,n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\}
is a complete orthonormal system for

L^{2}\big({{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}\big)
, we can consider Fourier series

\displaystyle f~\sim\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d,n}}a_{nj}Y_{j}^{n},

where

\displaystyle a_{nj}=\langle f,Y_{j}^{n}\rangle,

converges to
f\in L^{2}\big({{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}\big)
in the
L^{2}
-sense.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}^{d}_{m}
be the subspace of
L^{2}\big({{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}\big)
formed by the functions
f\in L^{2}\big({{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}\big)
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[n(n+d-2)]^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d,n}}a_{nj}^{2}<\infty.

Further,
{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
is the space of all functions
f\in{\mathcal{F}}^{d}_{m}
with Fourier coefficients
a_{nj}=0
, for all
1\leq j\leq N_{d,n}
and
0\leq n\leq\ell
. In what follows we will consider approximations
s
to
f
whose smoothness is measured by an inner product on
{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
with an additional spherical polynomial part of degree
\ell
viewed as a trend. The case most frequently occurring in the literature is that of
{\mathcal{F}}^{d,0}_{m}
.

For
f,g\in{\mathcal{F}}^{d,0}_{m}
with
f\sim\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{d,n}}a_{nj}Y^{n}_{j}
and
g\sim\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{d,n}}b_{nj}Y^{n}_{j}
and
m
even,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}}\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}}\big(\triangle_{0,d}^{m/2}f\big)(x)\big(\triangle_{0,d}^{m/2}g\big)(x){\rm d}\sigma(x)

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}}\int_{{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}}\!\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d,n}}[n(n+d-2)]^{m/2}a_{nj}Y^{n}_{j}(x)\right)\!\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d,n}}[n(n+d-2)]^{m/2}b_{nj}Y^{n}_{j}(x)\right)\!{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[n(n+d-2)]^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d,n}}a_{nj}b_{nj},

(3.7)

by the extended Parseval identity in the space
L^{2}\big({{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}\big)
. In view of the equality between expressions (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) define an “energy” semi-inner product for
{\mathcal{F}}^{d}_{m}
by

\displaystyle(f,g)_{m,\ell}=\sum_{n=\ell+1}^{\infty}[n(n+d-2)]^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d,n}}a_{nj}b_{nj}.

(3.8)

It is clear from (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) that this is an analogue of the usual semi-inner product associated with smoothing splines on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

(\cdot,\cdot)_{m,\ell}
is an inner product for
{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
. It is easy to show that
{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
with norm
\|f\|_{m,\ell}=\sqrt{(f,f)_{m,\ell}}
is a Hilbert space. A proof could be based on the arguments in
[
7
, pp. 247–250]
.

Now for
x,y\in{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}
define

\displaystyle K_{d,m,\ell}(x,y)=\sum_{n=\ell+1}^{\infty}[n(n+d-2)]^{-m}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d,n}}Y^{n}_{j}(x)Y^{n}_{j}(y)

\displaystyle\hphantom{K_{d,m,\ell}(x,y}{}=\sum_{n=\ell+1}^{\infty}[n(n+d-2)]^{-m}N_{d,n}W^{\lambda}_{n}\big(x^{\rm T}y\big),

(3.9)

by the addition formula (
3.3
).
K_{d,m,\ell}
is clearly a zonal kernel since it depends only on the cosine of the angle between
x
and
y
. We will use the notation
K_{d,m}
for the kernels
K_{d,m,0}
, and call these kernels the
thinplate spline kernels for the sphere

{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
. The
K_{2,m}
and
K_{3,m}
kernels were initially introduced by Wahba
[
22
]
. Since
K_{d,m,\ell}(x,y)
is zonal we can sensibly define functions
k_{d,m,\ell}
by

\displaystyle k_{d,m,\ell}(\xi)=K_{d,m,\ell}(x,y),\qquad\text{where}\quad x,y\in{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\xi=x^{\rm T}y.

(3.10)

Explicitly,

\displaystyle k_{d,m,\ell}(\xi)=\sum_{n=\ell+1}^{\infty}[n(n+d-2)]^{-m}N_{d,n}W^{\lambda}_{n}(\xi),

(3.11)

We will refer to the functions
k_{d,m,\ell}
as
semi-reproducing functions
, and the functions
k_{d,m}=k_{d,m,0}
as
thinplate
spline functions
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\ell
be a non-negative integer and
2m\geq d\geq 2
.

(a)

K_{d,m,\ell}(x,y)
is the reproducing kernel for the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
.

(b)

K_{d,m,\ell}(x,y)
considered as a function in
C\big({\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}\big)
is strictly positive definite.

Proof.

Proof of part
(a)
.
Assume
2m\geq d\geq 2
. To show
K=K_{d,m,\ell}
is the reproducing kernel for
{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
it suffices to show that the following two properties hold,
[
9
, p. 317]
.

(i)

For each fixed
y\in{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}
,
f(\cdot)=K(\cdot,y)
is in
{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
.

(ii)

For each function
f\in{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
the reproducing property

\displaystyle\big(f(\cdot),K(\cdot,y)\big)_{m,\ell}=f(y),

holds.

To show property (i) let
y
be some fixed point in
{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}
and define
k_{y}(\cdot)=K(\cdot,y)
. From the definition of
K
,
k_{y}
has Fourier coefficients

\displaystyle a_{nj}=[n(n+d-2)]^{-m}Y^{n}_{j}(y),\qquad n\geq\ell+1\quad\text{and}\quad 1\leq j\leq N_{d,n}.

(3.12)

Hence,

\displaystyle(k_{y},k_{y})_{m,\ell}=\sum_{n=\ell+1}^{\infty}[n(n+d-2)]^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d,n}}(a_{nj})^{2}=\sum_{n=\ell+1}^{\infty}[n(n+d-2)]^{-m}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d,n}}\left(Y^{n}_{j}(y)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\hphantom{(k_y,k_y)_{m,\ell}}{}=\sum_{n=\ell+1}^{\infty}[n(n+d-2)]^{-m}N_{d,n}W^{\lambda}_{n}(1)=\sum_{n=\ell+1}^{\infty}[n(n+d-2)]^{-m}N_{d,n},

where in the second to last step the addition formula (
3.3
) has been used. The estimate
N_{d,n}={\mathcal{O}}\big(n^{d-2}\big)
, holding for
d>1
, shows that the sum above is finite when
2m\geq d
, and hence
k_{y}\in{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
as required.

To show the reproducing property let
f
be any function in
{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
. Suppose
f
has Fourier series
\sum\limits_{n=\ell+1}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{d,n}}a_{nj}Y^{n}_{j}
. Then, from (
3.8
) and (
3.12
)

\displaystyle(f,k_{y})_{m,\ell}=\sum_{n=\ell+1}^{\infty}[n(n+d-2)]^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d,n}}a_{nj}[n(n+d-2)]^{-m}Y^{n}_{j}(y)

\displaystyle\hphantom{( f, k_y )_{m,\ell}}{}=\sum_{n=\ell+1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{d,n}}a_{nj}Y^{n}_{j}(y)=f(y).

That is the reproducing property holds.

Proof of part
(b)
.
In view of the characterisations of strict positive definiteness of zonal kernels given by Chen, Menegatto and Sun
[
6
]
for
d>2
, and by Menegatto
[
18
]
for
d=2
, part (b) follows from the signs of the Gegenbauer coefficients of
K_{d,m}
displayed in equation (
3.9
).
∎

Above we have shown
K_{d,m,\ell}
is the reproducing kernel for the space
{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
which arises from using a Fourier projection onto spherical polynomials to split the space
{\mathcal{F}}^{d}_{m}
into a direct sum
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}\oplus{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
, where
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}=\cup_{n=0}^{\ell}{\mathcal{H}}^{d}_{n}
is the space of spherical polynomials of degree at most
\ell
.

For interpolation problems it is natural to use instead a projection onto polynomials interpolating at a certain finite set of points, and this results in a different direct sum decomposition. Fortunately, here the semi-reproducing kernel approach becomes especially convenient as
K_{d,m,\ell}
is a semi-reproducing kernel for the space
{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
with respect to the space of polynomials
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
. This is the content of the following easily shown lemma whose proof is included for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\ell
be a nonnegative integer and
2m\geq d\geq 2
. The reproducing kernel
K_{d,m,\ell}
for the space
{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
with semi-inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{m,\ell}
, defined above, is one choice of semi-reproducing kernel for the space
{\mathcal{F}}^{d}_{m}
with respect to the space of spherical polynomials of degree
\ell
,
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}=\cup_{n=0}^{\ell}{\mathcal{H}}^{d}_{n}
.

Proof.

{\mathcal{F}}^{d}_{m}={\mathcal{H}}
is considered as a semi-inner product space with semi-inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{m,\ell}
. Choose
P_{0}
as Fourier projection onto the kernel
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
of the semi-inner product, which is the space of spherical polynomials of degree not exceeding
\ell
. Set
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}=(I-P_{0}){\mathcal{F}}^{d}_{m}={\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
. Then clearly
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{F}}^{d}_{m}={\mathcal{H}}_{0}\oplus{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}={\mathcal{H}}_{0}\oplus{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
. Also, for each
x\in{{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}}
,
K_{d,m,\ell}(\cdot,x)\in{\mathcal{H}}_{1}={\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}\subset{\mathcal{F}}^{d}_{m}={\mathcal{H}}
by Lemma
3.1
(a). Therefore, considering an
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
-increment
({\mathcal{X}},{\boldsymbol{a}})

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}K_{d,m,\ell}(\cdot,x_{i})\in{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}\subset{\mathcal{F}}^{d}_{m},

the first property, i.e., property (
2.2
), of a semi-reproducing kernel. Also, given any
f\in K^{d}_{m}
the direct sum splitting allows us to write
f=f_{0}+f_{1}
where
f_{0}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
and
f_{1}\in{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
. Therefore, considering the
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
-increment
({\mathcal{X}},{\boldsymbol{a}})

\displaystyle\left(f(\cdot),\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}K_{d,m,\ell}(\cdot,x_{i})\right)_{m,\ell}=\left(f_{0}(\cdot)+f_{1}(\cdot),\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}K_{d,m,\ell}(\cdot,x_{i})\right)_{m,\ell}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\big(f_{0}(\cdot),K_{d,m,\ell}(\cdot,x_{i})\big)_{m,\ell}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\left(f_{1}(\cdot),\sum_{i=1}^{n}K_{d,m,\ell}(\cdot,x_{i})\right)_{m,\ell}=0+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}f_{1}(x_{i}),

which follows from
(h_{0},h_{1})_{m,\ell}=0
for all
h_{0}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
and
h_{1}\in{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
, and also from
K_{d,m,\ell}
being the reproducing kernel for
{\mathcal{F}}^{d,\ell}_{m}
. Continuing, using the vanishing property of
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
-increments,

\displaystyle\left(f(\cdot),\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}K_{d,m,\ell}(\cdot,x_{i})\right)_{m,\ell}

\displaystyle=0+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}f_{1}(x_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}f_{0}(x_{i})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}f_{1}(x_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}f(x_{i}),

the second property, i.e., property (
2.3
), of a semi-reproducing kernel. Therefore,
K_{d,m,\ell}
is a semi-reproducing kernel for
{\mathcal{F}}^{d}_{m}
with semi-inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{m,\ell}
, as required.
∎

In view of Lemma
3.2
, Theorem
2.5
concerning the solution of interpolation and penalized least squares fitting problems applies to interpolation and smoothing problems on the sphere. Applying the theorem the semi-reproducing kernel
K=K_{d,m,\ell}
, defined in equation (
3.9
), plays a central role in interpolation and penalized least squares fitting problems posed in the space
{\mathcal{H}}={\mathcal{F}}^{d}_{m}
, with semi-inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{m,\ell}
with respect to the finite dimensional subspace
{\mathcal{H}}_{0}=\cup_{n=0}^{\ell}{\mathcal{H}}^{d}_{n}
. In this section we have given series expansions for these kernels. In Section
5
, Theorem
5.1
, below, we will provide a recurrence relation for the particularly important thinplate spline kernels,
K_{d,m,}
, via a recurrence for the corresponding functions
k_{d,m}
. In Sections
6
and
7
the recurrence relation will be used to give short explicit expressions for many of the thinplate spline kernels.

4
The operator
\boldsymbol{T}
and its adjoint
\boldsymbol{T^{*}}

In this section we discuss an operator
T
, and its adjoint
T^{*}
, which will be crucial parts of the recurrence for the thinplate spline functions
k_{d,m}
. These operators were defined by Martinez-Morales in
[
16
]
.

In view of the series expansions for the kernels
k_{d,m}
given in equation (
3.11
) a multiplier operator with Fourier multiplier of
(n(n+2\lambda))^{-1}
would transform
k_{d,m}
into
k_{d,m+1}
. The operators
T
and
T^{*}
, are discussed below have some, but not quite all, the desired properties.

Note that the differential equation for the Gegenbauer polynomials is given by
[
1
, equation (22.6.5)]
or
[
19
, Table 18.8.1]

\displaystyle\big(1-x^{2}\big)y^{\prime\prime}-(2\lambda+1)xy^{\prime}+n(n+2\lambda)y=0.

Rewriting this we obtain

\displaystyle\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{-\lambda+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}\left(\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{\lambda+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}\right)y=-n(n+2\lambda)y.

Call the operator on the left hand side of the equation
D_{\lambda}
. Then, for
\lambda>0
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

\displaystyle D_{\lambda}C_{n}^{\lambda}(x)=-n(n+2\lambda)C_{n}^{\lambda}(x).

Let us formally invert the equation
D_{\lambda}f=g
using averages centered at 1. Dealing with the outer derivative in
D_{\lambda}
we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{1}\big(1-y^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}g(y){\rm d}y=\left.\big(1-y^{2}\big)^{\lambda+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}y}f(y)\right|_{x}^{1}.

If

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(y)\ \text{is continuous at}\ y=1,

(4.1)

the term on the right hand side for
y=1
vanishes. We can then proceed obtaining

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{1}\big(1-y^{2}\big)^{-\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{y}^{1}\big(1-z^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}g(z){\rm d}z{\rm d}y=f(x)-f(1).

Setting
f(x)=C_{n}^{\lambda}(x)
and
g(x)=-n(n+2\lambda)C_{n}^{\lambda}(x)
, condition (
4.1
) is clearly satisfied. We therefore obtain the following statement.

Proposition 4.1
.

For
\lambda>0
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}

\displaystyle\int_{x}^{1}\big(1-y^{2}\big)^{-\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{y}^{1}\big(1-z^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}C_{n}^{\lambda}(z){\rm d}z{\rm d}y=\frac{C_{n}^{\lambda}(1)-C_{n}^{\lambda}(x)}{n(n+2\lambda)}.

Proof.

We would like to give a direct proof of the statement. Towards this goal, we use an integral given in
[
1
, equation (22.13.2)]
(or
[
19
, equation (18.17.1)]
for the general Jacobi case)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x}\big(1-y^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}C_{n}^{\lambda}(y){\rm d}y=\frac{2\lambda}{n(2\lambda+n)}\big[C_{n-1}^{\lambda+1}(0)-\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{\lambda+\frac{1}{2}}C_{n-1}^{\lambda+1}(x)\big].

Decomposing the integral over
[0,1]
into two integrals over
[0,x]
and
[x,1]
, respectively, we can use the formula to obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{y}^{1}\big(1-z^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}C_{n}^{\lambda}(z){\rm d}z=\frac{2\lambda}{n(2\lambda+n)}\big(1-y^{2}\big)^{\lambda+\frac{1}{2}}C_{n-1}^{\lambda+1}(y).

Note that the function on the right hand side vanishes at
y=1
. Towards the claim, it remains to integrate the polynomial
C_{n-1}^{\lambda+1}
which readily follows from
[
19
, equation (18.9.19)]

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}x}C_{n}^{\lambda}(x)=2\lambda C_{n-1}^{\lambda+1}(x)\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad C_{n}^{\lambda}(x)=2\lambda\int C_{n-1}^{\lambda+1}(y){\rm d}y,

completing the proof.
∎

Similarly, we could have treated the average centered at
-1
giving the following result.

Proposition 4.2
.

For
\lambda>0
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{x}\big(1-y^{2}\big)^{-\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{-1}^{y}\big(1-z^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}C_{n}^{\lambda}(z){\rm d}z{\rm d}y=\frac{C_{n}^{\lambda}(-1)-C_{n}^{\lambda}(x)}{n(n+2\lambda)}.

The operators

\displaystyle T_{\lambda}f(x)=-\int_{-1}^{x}\big(1-y^{2}\big)^{-\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{-1}^{y}\big(1-z^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}f(z){\rm d}z{\rm d}y

and

\displaystyle T_{\lambda}^{*}f(x)=-\int_{x}^{1}\big(1-y^{2}\big)^{-\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{y}^{1}\big(1-z^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}f(z){\rm d}z{\rm d}y

have been defined in
[
16
]
, showing that
T_{\lambda}^{*}
is the adjoint of
T_{\lambda}
with respect to the inner product (
3.4
). To be precise, the following statement holds true (cf.
[
16
, Theorem 3]
).

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
f\in C[-1,1)\cap L^{1}_{\lambda}[-1,1]
and
g\in C(-1,1]\cap L^{1}_{\lambda}[-1,1]
. Then
gT_{\lambda}f,fT_{\lambda}^{*}g\in L^{1}_{\lambda}[-1,1]
and

\displaystyle[T_{\lambda}f,g]_{\lambda}=[f,T_{\lambda}^{*}g]_{\lambda}.

The proof exploits the fact that with the weight
\big(1-z^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}
in the inner integral, continuity of
f
suffices to cope with the singularity introduced by the weight
\big(1-y^{2}\big)^{-\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}
in the outer integral of
T_{\lambda}
. Based on this observation, the theorem follows from Fubini’s theorem.

Propositions
4.1
and
4.2
above thus show that the polynomials
C_{n}^{\lambda}
are basically – up to a constant – eigenfunctions of
T^{*}
and
T
, respectively. This is somewhat obvious from the fact that both
T
and
T^{*}
invert
D_{\lambda}
. Both propositions have been derived in
[
16
, Lemma 1]
via a different proof employing the Rodriguez formula of the Gegenbauer polynomials.

5
A recurrence for the thinplate spline functions for the sphere

This section concerns a recurrence for the thinplate spline functions
k_{d,m}
for
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
. The recurrence will be used in Sections
6
and
7
to give short explicit forms for many of the functions
k_{d,m}
. It is important to note that the corresponding recurrence given in
[
16
, Theorem 4]
is incorrect and does not yield the thinplate spline functions
k_{d,m}
. Consequently, many of the explicit formulas claimed for the functions
k_{d,m}
in the paper
[
16
]
are also incorrect.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
\lambda=\frac{d-2}{2}
,
x\in[-1,1]
, and
e_{0}(x)=1
for all
x\in[-1,1]
. The thinplate spline functions,
k_{d,m}
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, for the sphere
{\mathcal{S}}^{d-1}
, defined via the series (
3.11
), are alternatively generated by the recurrence

\displaystyle k_{d,m}(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{[e_{0},T_{\lambda}e_{0}]_{\lambda}}{[e_{0},e_{0}]_{\lambda}}-(T_{\lambda}e_{0})(x),&\text{when}\ m=1,\\
(T_{\lambda}k_{d,m-1})(x)-\dfrac{[e_{0},T_{\lambda}k_{d,m-1}]_{\lambda}}{[e_{0},e_{0}]_{\lambda}},&\text{when}\ m>1.\end{cases}

Proof.

Fix
d\geq 2
and define a sequence of functions
(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots)
by the recurrence

\displaystyle f_{m}(x)=\begin{cases}\dfrac{[e_{0},T_{\lambda}e_{0}]_{\lambda}}{[e_{0},e_{0}]_{\lambda}}-(T_{\lambda}e_{0})(x),&\text{when}\ m=1,\\
(T_{\lambda}f_{m-1})(x)-\dfrac{[e_{0},T_{\lambda}f_{m-1}]_{\lambda}}{[e_{0},e_{0}]_{\lambda}},&\text{when}\ m>1,\end{cases}

(5.1)

this recursion mirroring the one in the statement of the theorem.

Throughout this proof we view the series definition (
3.11
) as an orthogonal expansion

\displaystyle g\sim\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{g}_{n}C^{\lambda}_{n}

in terms of the Gegenbauer polynomials
C^{\lambda}_{n}
. The uniqueness theorem tells us that functions with the same coefficients are identical.

First, observe that the constant term in the definition of
f_{m}
ensures that the zeroth Fourier coefficient
\widehat{(f_{m})}_{0}
is zero. Therefore, consider in what follows Fourier coefficients of index
n\geq 1
.

Clearly,
e_{0}\in C[-1,1]\cap L^{1}_{\lambda}[-1,1]
. It therefore follows from Theorem
4.3
that
f_{1}
is continuous on
[-1,1]
and
f_{1}\in L^{1}_{\lambda}[-1,1]
. Using induction on
m
we can then conclude, again using Theorem
4.3
, that
f_{m}
is continuous on
[-1,1]
and
f_{m}\in L^{1}_{\lambda}[-1,1]
for
m>1
.

For a function
f\in L^{1}_{\lambda}[-1,1]
, and
n\geq 1
, Theorem
4.3
gives that

\displaystyle\widehat{T_{\lambda}f}_{n}=\frac{1}{h_{n}^{\lambda}}\int_{-1}^{1}T_{\lambda}f(x)C_{n}^{\lambda}(x)\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}x=\frac{1}{h_{n}^{\lambda}}\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)T_{\lambda}^{*}C_{n}^{\lambda}(x)\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}x,

which by Proposition
4.1
yields

\displaystyle\widehat{T_{\lambda}f}_{n}=\frac{1}{n(n+2\lambda)}\frac{1}{h_{n}^{\lambda}}\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)C_{n}^{\lambda}(x)\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\hphantom{\widehat{T_\lambda f}_n=}{}-\frac{1}{n(n+2\lambda)}\frac{C_{n}^{\lambda}(1)}{h_{n}^{\lambda}}\int_{-1}^{1}f(x)\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}x.

(5.2)

In the special case of
f=e_{0}
, the first integral vanishes due to orthogonality (
3.5
). Furthermore,

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}e_{0}(x)\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}x=\int_{-1}^{1}\big(1-x^{2}\big)^{\lambda-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm d}x=[e_{0},e_{0}]_{\lambda}=h_{0}^{\lambda}.

We therefore obtain that for
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\widehat{(T_{\lambda}e_{0})}_{n}=-\frac{1}{n(n+2\lambda)}\frac{C_{n}^{\lambda}(1)}{h_{n}^{\lambda}}h^{\lambda}_{0}=-\frac{1}{n(n+2\lambda)}\frac{n+\lambda}{\lambda}=-\frac{1}{n(n+2\lambda)}\frac{N_{d,n}}{C_{n}^{\lambda}(1)}.

Thus, the function
f_{1}
generated as specified in equation (
5.1
) has the same Fourier coefficients as the thinplate spline function
k_{d,1}
of equation (
3.11
). Thus, by uniqueness, it is
k_{d,1}
.

Now returning to general functions
f
we can rewrite (
5.2
) as

\displaystyle\widehat{T_{\lambda}f}_{n}=\frac{1}{n(n+2\lambda)}{\widehat{f}}_{n}-\frac{1}{n(n+2\lambda)}C_{n}^{\lambda}(1)\frac{h^{\lambda}_{0}}{h_{n}^{\lambda}}{\widehat{f}}_{0}.

(5.3)

From (
5.3
) and the definition of
f_{m}
we have that for
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 1

\displaystyle\big(\widehat{f_{m}}\big)_{n}=\frac{1}{n(n+2\lambda)}\big(\widehat{f_{m-1}}\big)_{n}-\frac{1}{n(n+2\lambda)}C_{n}^{\lambda}(1)\frac{h_{0}^{\lambda}}{h_{n}^{\lambda}}\big(\widehat{f_{m-1}}\big)_{0}=\frac{1}{n(n+2\lambda)}\big(\widehat{f_{m-1}}\big)_{n},

since
\big(\widehat{f_{m-1}}\big)_{0}=0
. Thus, by induction

\displaystyle\big(\widehat{f_{m}}\big)_{n}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{when}\ n=0,\\
(n(n+2\lambda))^{-m}\dfrac{N_{d,n}}{C^{\lambda}_{n}(1)},&\text{when}\ n\geq 1.\end{cases}

Hence
f_{m}

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\alpha
be an integer and
\beta
a nonnegative integer. If
\alpha
is nonnegative further suppose
\beta<a
. Then

\displaystyle J_{\beta,\alpha}=\sum_{\nu=0}^{\beta}\binom{\beta}{\nu}\frac{(-1)^{\nu}}{\alpha-\nu}=\frac{(-1)^{\beta}\beta!}{\alpha(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-\beta)}.

Proof.

The identity will be proven by induction on
\beta
. For
\beta=0
the result is immediate. Now assume that the identity is true for
\beta=\kappa-1
where
\kappa\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then

\displaystyle J_{\kappa,a}=\binom{\kappa}{0}\frac{1}{\alpha}+\binom{\kappa}{\kappa}\frac{(-1)^{\kappa}}{\alpha-\kappa}+\sum_{\nu=1}^{\kappa-1}\left\{\binom{\kappa-1}{\nu}+\binom{\kappa-1}{\nu-1}\right\}\frac{(-1)^{\nu}}{\alpha-\nu}

\displaystyle\hphantom{J_{\kappa,a}}{}=\sum_{\nu=0}^{\kappa-1}\binom{\kappa-1}{\nu}\frac{(-1)^{\nu}}{\alpha-\nu}+\left\{\frac{(-1)^{\kappa}}{\alpha-\kappa}+\sum_{\ell=0}^{\kappa-2}\binom{\kappa-1}{\ell}\frac{(-1)^{\ell+1}}{\alpha-1-\ell}\right\}

\displaystyle\hphantom{J_{\kappa,a}}{}=\sum_{\nu=0}^{\kappa-1}\binom{\kappa-1}{\nu}\frac{(-1)^{\nu}}{\alpha-\nu}-\sum_{\ell=0}^{\kappa-1}\binom{\kappa-1}{\ell}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\alpha-1-\ell}.

Applying the induction hypothesis twice

\displaystyle J_{\kappa,\alpha}=\frac{(-1)^{\kappa-1}(\kappa-1)!}{\alpha(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-\kappa+1)}-\frac{(-1)^{\kappa-1}(\kappa-1)!}{(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)\cdots(\alpha-\kappa)}=\frac{(-1)^{\kappa}\kappa!}{\alpha(\alpha-1)\cdots(\alpha-\kappa)},

showing that the identity also holds for
\beta=\kappa
.
∎

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
\kappa\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\left(1-x^{2}\right)^{\kappa}\ln\left(\frac{1-x}{2}\right){\rm d}x=2^{2\kappa+1}(-1)^{\kappa+1}\sum_{\nu=0}^{\kappa}\binom{\kappa}{\nu}\frac{(-1)^{\nu}}{(2\kappa-\nu+1)^{2}}.

Proof.

\displaystyle I=\int_{-1}^{1}\left(1-x^{2}\right)^{\kappa}\ln\left(\frac{1-x}{2}\right){\rm d}x=\sum_{\nu=0}^{\kappa}\binom{\kappa}{\nu}2^{\nu}(-1)^{\nu}\int_{-1}^{1}\ln\left(\frac{1-x}{2}\right)(1-x)^{2\kappa-\nu}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\hphantom{I}=\sum_{\nu=0}^{\kappa}\binom{\kappa}{\nu}2^{2\kappa}(-1)^{\nu}\int_{-1}^{1}\ln\left(\frac{1-x}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1-x}{2}\right)^{2\kappa-\nu}{\rm d}x.

(7.13)

Now

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\ln\left(\frac{1-x}{2}\right)\left(\frac{1-x}{2}\right)^{2\kappa-\nu}{\rm d}x=2\int_{0}^{1}\ln(t)t^{2\kappa-\nu}{\rm d}t=\frac{-2}{(2\kappa-\nu+1)^{2}}.

Substituting into equation (
7.13
) yields the result.
∎

Substituting

\displaystyle\hphantom{G_\lambda^\alpha(y)}{}=\int_{-1}^{y}(1+z)^{\kappa}\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\kappa-\alpha}\binom{\kappa-\alpha}{\gamma}2^{\gamma}(-1)^{\kappa-\alpha-\gamma}(1+z)^{\kappa-\alpha-\gamma}{\rm d}z

\displaystyle\hphantom{\widetilde{G}_\lambda(y)}{}=\sum_{\nu=0}^{\kappa}2^{\nu}\binom{\kappa}{\nu}\left\{\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\nu}\binom{\nu}{\gamma}(-1)^{\nu-\gamma}\frac{1}{2\kappa-\gamma+1}\right\}(1-y)^{\kappa-\nu}.

\displaystyle G_{\lambda}(y)=(1+y)^{\kappa+1}\sum_{\nu=0}^{\kappa}2^{\nu}\binom{\kappa}{\nu}\frac{\nu!(2\kappa-\nu)!}{(2\kappa+1)!}(1-y)^{\kappa-\nu},

and hence a representative value of

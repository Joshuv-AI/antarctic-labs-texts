Local-in-time existence of strong solutions to a class of the compressible non-Newtonian Navier-Stokes equations
Martin Kalousek, Václav Mácha, Šárka Nečasová

Abstract
The aim of this article is to show a local-in-time existence of a strong solution to the generalized compressible Navier-Stokes equations for arbitrarily large initial data. The goal is reached by L p L^{p} -theory for linearized equations which are obtained with help of the Weis multiplier theorem and can be seen as a generalization of the work of Shibata and Enomoto [ 13 ] (devoted to compressible fluids) to compressible non-Newtonian fluids.



Keywords:
non-Newtonian fluids, the Weis theorem,
L^{p}
-theory

AMS subject classification:
35A01, 35B65, 35P09, 35Q35

1
Introduction

In this paper, we analyze a boundary value problem for the Navier-Stokes equations describing the flow of a compressible non-Newtonian fluid that reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\varrho u)+\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\varrho u\otimes u)+\nabla\pi(\varrho)=

\displaystyle\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle\text{ in }Q_{T},

(1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varrho+\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits(\varrho u)=

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\text{ in }Q_{T},

\displaystyle u(0,\cdot)=u_{0},\varrho(0,\cdot)=

\displaystyle\varrho_{0}

\displaystyle\text{ in }\Omega.

Here
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},d\geq 2
is the domain occupied by the fluid,
T>0
is the time of evolution and
Q_{T}=(0,T)\times\Omega
. We denote by
u(t,x)
the fluid velocity, by
\varrho(t,x)
the fluid density and by
\mathcal{S}
the viscous stress. We assume the periodic boundary condition, i.e.,
\Omega
is a torus

\Omega=\left([-1,1]|_{\{-1,1\}}\right)^{d}.

We restrict ourselves to the constitutive relation

\mathcal{S}=2\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})\mathbb{D}^{D}u+\eta(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u\mathbb{I}_{d},

(2)

where
\mu
and
\eta
are viscosity coefficients whose properties will be specified later.

1.1
Discussion and main result

The system with
\mu\equiv\mu_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\eta\equiv\eta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
has been extensively studied. The mathematical analysis of compressible viscous fluids goes back to 1950s. The first result concerning uniqueness was given by Graffi
[
15
]
and Serrin
[
33
]
. A local in time existence in Hölder continuous spaces was proven by Nash
[
30
]
, Itaya
[
16
,
18
]
and Vol’pert and Hudjaev
[
17
]
. In Sobolev-Slobodetskii space the local existence was shown by Solonnikov
[
35
]
. The local-in-time existence and global-in-time existence for small data in Hilbert space traces back to Valli
[
37
]
. The optimal regularity of local in time solutions was obtained by Charve and Danchin
[
9
]
. The global solution with the initial data close to a rest state was shown by Matsumura and Nishida
[
28
,
27
]
in the whole space and the exterior domain. The case of bounded domain was treated in work of Valli and Zajączkowski
[
38
]
.

The global-in-time existence of a weak solution is much more recent and goes back to the half of 1990s. The existence is known due to nowadays standard Feireisl - Lions theory – we refer to
[
14
]
and to
[
22
]
.

However, there is an unsatisfactory number of articles for the system with a general
\mu
and
\eta
. In particular, Mamontov
[
25
]
,
[
26
]
considered an exponentially growing viscosity and isothermal pressure. For such case the global existence of a weak solution was shown. Recently, Abbatiello, Feireisl and Novotný in
[
1
]
provide a proof of the existence of so-called dissipative solution – a notion of solution which is ’weaker’ than a weak solution. The existence of weak solution is still unproven. The main obstacle seems to be the lack of compactness of velocity
u
which is caused by an insufficient control of
\partial_{t}u
in (and near) vacuum regions.

Let us mention that such type of problem was already studied in the incompressible case by Ladyzhenskaya
[
21
]
and then extensively studied by group of J. Nečas
[
6
,
5
,
24
]
. Further, basic properties of weak or measure-valued solution of incompressible non-Newtonian fluids are described in
[
23
,
4
]
. Concerning the compressible case the first attempt to solve this problem can be found in work of Nečasová and Novotný
[
29
]
where the existence of measure-valued solution was shown.

The concept of the maximal regularity is classical and the main achievements for abstract theory go back to the work of Ladyzhenskaya, Uraltzeva and Solonnikov
[
20
]
, Da-Prato and Grisvard
[
11
]
, Amann
[
2
]
and Prüss
[
31
]
. The notion of
\cal R
–sectoriality was introduced by Clement and Prüss see
[
10
]
. The fundamental result by Weis
[
39
]
about equivalency of description for the maximal regularity in terms of vector-valued Fourier multipliers and
{\cal R}
–sectoriality was a breakthrough for the applications.

The Weis multiplier theorem allows to deduce the
L^{p}
maximal regularity to problems connected with the fluid dynamics. We refer to
[
12
]
and
[
19
]
for the comprehensible decription of the method.
The maximal
L^{p}
regularity result for incompressible fluids can be found in work by Shibata and Shimizu
[
34
]
, for the non-Newtonian incompressible situation it was established in work by Bothe and Prüss
[
7
]
. Maximal regularity for the compressible case was shown by Enomoto and Shibata
[
13
]
.

The goal of this paper is to use the Weis multiplier theorem to show the short-time existence of a strong solution in
L^{p}
setting for the non-Newtonian compressible fluid.

Let us introduce our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu\in C^{3}([0,\infty))
and
\eta\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy
\mu(s)+2\mu^{\prime}(s)s>0
for all
s\geq 0
and
\eta(r)+\eta^{\prime}(r)r>0
for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
. Let, moreover,
\pi\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
,
q>d
and
p\in\left(\frac{2q}{q-d},\infty\right)
be given. Then for every
u_{0}\in W^{2,q}(\Omega)
and
\varrho_{0}\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
,
\frac{1}{\varrho_{0}}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
there is
T>0
such that there exists

(\varrho,u)\in L^{p}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\Omega))\times L^{p}(0,T;W^{2,q}(\Omega))

with

(\partial_{t}\varrho,\partial_{t}u)\in L^{p}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\Omega))\times L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))

which satisfies (
1
).

The proof of the main result relies on several steps. The first step is the use of Lagrange coordinates to get rid of the convective term.

Secondly, we linearize the resulting system and we show
L^{p}-L^{q}
regularity property of a solution. This is discussed in Section
3
and it is obtained by means of the Weis multiplier theorem.

The sufficient regularity of the velocity
u
allows to deduce appropriate bounds and one may use the Banach fix-point argument to deduce the existence of a strong solution assuming the time
T>0
is small enough. This is described in Section
2
.

The remaining part of this introductory section is devoted to necessary preliminary statements.

1.2
Notation

We denote by
\mathbb{I}_{d}
the
d\times d
identity matrix. For the
d\times d
–matrix valued mapping
G

\mathrm{sym}G
denotes the symmetric part of
G
, i.e.,
\mathrm{sym}G=\frac{1}{2}\left(G+G^{\top}\right)
and
G^{D}
is the traceless part of
G
, i.e.,
G^{D}=G-\frac{1}{d}\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits G\mathbb{I}_{d}
. Let
u
be a mapping with values in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
then
\mathbb{D}u=\mathrm{sym}(\nabla u)
is the symmetric part of the gradient and
\mathbb{D}^{D}u
stands for the traceless part of
\mathbb{D}u
.
For purposes of this paper we denote by
\mathcal{V}^{p,q}(Q_{T})
the following function space

\mathcal{V}^{p,q}(Q_{T})=L^{p}(0,T;W^{2,q}(\Omega))\cap W^{1,p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))

with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{V^{p,q}(Q_{T})}=\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(0,T;W^{2,q}(\Omega))}+\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))}
.
Starting with definition (
2
), using the fact that
\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\mathbb{D}^{D}u=0
and the symmetry of
\mathbb{D}^{D}u
we have

\begin{split}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\mathcal{S})_{j}=&2\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})\sum_{l=1}^{d}\partial_{l}\left((\mathbb{D}^{D}u)_{jl}-\frac{1}{d}\delta_{jl}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u\right)\\
&+4\mu^{\prime}(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})\sum_{k,l,m=1}^{d}(\mathbb{D}^{D}u)_{jk}(\mathbb{D}^{D}u)_{lm}\partial_{k}\left((\mathbb{D}u)_{lm}-\frac{1}{d}\delta_{lm}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u\right)\\
&+\sum_{l=1}^{d}\delta_{jl}\left(\eta(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)+\eta^{\prime}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u\right)\partial_{l}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u\\
=&\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})\sum_{l=1}^{d}(\partial^{2}_{l}u_{j}+\partial_{j}\partial_{l}u_{l})+2\mu^{\prime}(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})\sum_{k,l,m=1}^{d}(\mathbb{D}^{D}u)_{jk}(\mathbb{D}^{D}u)_{lm}\left(\partial_{k}\partial_{m}u_{l}+\partial_{k}\partial_{l}u_{m}\right)\\
&-\frac{2}{d}\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})\partial_{j}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u+\left(\eta(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)+\eta^{\prime}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u\right)\partial_{j}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u\\
=&\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})\sum_{l=1}^{d}(\partial^{2}_{l}u_{j}+\partial_{j}\partial_{l}u_{l})+4\mu^{\prime}(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})\sum_{k,l,m=1}^{d}(\mathbb{D}^{D}u)_{jl}(\mathbb{D}^{D}u)_{km}\partial_{l}\partial_{m}u_{k}\\
&-\frac{2}{d}\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})\partial_{j}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u+\left(\eta(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)+\eta^{\prime}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u\right)\partial_{j}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u.\end{split}

Hence we deduce that

(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\mathcal{S})_{j}=\sum_{k,l,m=1}^{d}a^{lm}_{jk}(\mathbb{D}u)\partial_{m}\partial_{l}u_{k},

(3)

where

\begin{split}a^{lm}_{jk}(\mathbb{D}u)=&\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})\left(\delta_{jk}\delta_{lm}+\delta_{jm}\delta_{kl}\right)+4\mu^{\prime}(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})(\mathbb{D}^{D}u)_{jl}(\mathbb{D}^{D}u)_{km}\\
&+\left(\eta(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)+\eta^{\prime}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u-\frac{2}{d}\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})\right)\delta_{km}\delta_{jl}.\end{split}

(4)

We note that
a_{jk}^{lm}
possesses the following symmetries

a_{jk}^{lm}=a_{kj}^{lm}=a^{jk}_{lm}=a^{jm}_{lk}=a^{lk}_{jm}\text{ for all }j,k,l,m=1,\dots,d.

(5)

We define for a given
u\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})^{d}

the quasilinear differential operator
\mathcal{A}
as

\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}u)(\mathbb{D}v)=\sum_{l,m=1}^{d}a^{lm}_{jk}(\mathbb{D}u)\partial_{l}\partial_{m}v,

(6)

whose ellipticity is ensured by certain identities satisfied by
\mu,\eta
and their derivatives as we now show.
Considering
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}_{sym}
we get

\begin{split}a^{lm}_{jk}\xi_{jl}\xi_{km}=&\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})(\xi_{jl}^{2}+\xi^{2}_{km})+4\mu^{\prime}(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})(\mathbb{D}^{D}u)_{jl}\xi_{jl}(\mathbb{D}^{D}u)_{km}\xi_{km}\\
&+\left(\eta(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)+\eta^{\prime}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u-\frac{2}{d}\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})\right)\xi_{jj}\xi_{ll}.\end{split}

Hence we obtain

\begin{split}\sum_{j,k,l,m=1}^{d}a^{lm}_{jk}\xi_{jl}\xi_{km}=&2\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})|\xi|^{2}+4\mu^{\prime}(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})|\mathbb{D}^{D}u\cdot\xi|^{2}\\
&+\left(\eta(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)+\eta^{\prime}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u-\frac{2}{d}\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})\right)(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\xi)^{2}.\end{split}

Applying the decomposition
\xi=\xi^{D}+\frac{tr\xi}{d}\mathbb{I}_{d}
, in particular the fact that
\xi^{D}\cdot\mathbb{I}_{d}=0
, we arrive at

\sum_{j,k,l,m=1}^{d}a^{lm}_{jk}\xi_{jl}\xi_{km}=2\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})|\xi^{D}|^{2}+4\mu^{\prime}(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})|\mathbb{D}^{D}u\cdot\xi^{D}|^{2}+\left(\eta(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)+\eta^{\prime}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u\right)(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\xi)^{2}.

(7)

We deal with the notion of strong ellipticity of the operator
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}u)
which means that there exists
C_{el}(\mathbb{D}u)>0
such that

\sum_{j,k,l,m=1}^{d}a^{lm}_{jk}\xi_{jl}\xi_{km}\geq C_{el}(\mathbb{D}u)|\xi|^{2}\text{ holds for all }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}.

(8)

Let investigate necessary conditions on
\mu
and
\eta
related to the strong ellipticity of
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}u)
.
We first choose
g\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
as an eigenvector of
\mathbb{D}^{D}u
and
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
being perpendicular to
g
. We consider the matrix
(\xi)_{jk}=\frac{1}{2}(g_{j}h_{k}-g_{k}h_{j})
,
j,k=1,\ldots d
and we obtain from (
7
) combined with (
8
)

2\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})|\xi|^{2}\geq C_{el}(\mathbb{D}u)|\xi|^{2}

as
\xi
is obviously traceless and
\xi^{D}=\xi
consequently. Hence one concludes that

2\mu(s)\geq C_{el}(\mathbb{D}u)\text{ for all }s\in[0,\|\mathbb{D}^{D}u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}].

As
u\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})^{d}
can be chosen arbitrarily, we get

\mu(s)>0\text{ for any }s\geq 0.

(9)

In order to get the next condition involving
\mu
, we set
\xi=\mathbb{D}^{D}u
in (
8
), which together with (
7
) imply

2\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2}+4\mu^{\prime}(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2}|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2}\geq C_{el}(u)|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2}.

Hence one concludes that

2\mu(s)+4\mu^{\prime}(s)s\geq C_{el}(\mathbb{D}u),\text{ for any }s\in[0,\|\mathbb{D}^{D}u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}]

and finally

\mu(s)+2\mu^{\prime}(s)s>0\text{ for any }s\geq 0

(10)

by repeating the arguments leading to (
9
). To determine a condition on
\eta
we choose
\xi=\mathbb{I}_{d}
in (
8
). It follows that
(\mathbb{I}_{d})^{D}=0
and we obtain from (
7
) that

\left(\eta(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)+\eta^{\prime}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u\right)d^{2}\geq C_{el}(\mathbb{D}u)d.

Repeating the arguments leading to (
9
), (
10
) respectively, we get

\eta(r)+\eta^{\prime}(r)r\geq\frac{C_{el}(u)}{d}\text{ for all }r\in[-\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)},\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}]

and then

\eta(r)+\eta^{\prime}(r)r>0\text{ for all }r\in\mathbb{R}.

(11)

We note that conditions (
9
), (
10
) and (
11
) are also sufficient for the ellipticity of
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}u)
. If
\mu^{\prime}(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})\geq 0
these conditions ensure that

\sum_{j,k,l,m=1}^{d}a^{lm}_{jk}\xi_{jl}\xi_{km}\geq 2\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})|\xi^{D}|^{2}+(\eta(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)+\eta^{\prime}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\xi)^{2}.

(12)

From the continuity of
\mu
and the function
r\mapsto\eta(r)+\eta^{\prime}(r)r
and from the assumption
u\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})^{d}
we conclude the existence of
s_{min}\in[0,\|\mathbb{D}^{D}u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}]
and
r_{min}\in[-\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u\|_{L^{\infty}}]
such that
\mu(s)\geq\mu(s_{min})>0
for all
s\in[0,\|\mathbb{D}^{D}u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}]
and
\eta(r)+\eta^{\prime}(r)r\geq\eta(r_{min})+\eta^{\prime}(r_{min})r_{min}>0
for all
r\in[-\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u\|_{L^{\infty}},\|\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u\|_{L^{\infty}}]
. It follows from (
12
) that

\sum_{j,k,l,m=1}^{d}a^{lm}_{jk}\xi_{jl}\xi_{km}\geq 2\mu(s_{min})|\xi^{D}|^{2}+(\eta(r_{min})+\eta^{\prime}(r_{min})r_{min})(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\xi)^{2}.

(13)

Moreover, since one can easily check that the mapping
\xi\mapsto\sqrt{|\xi^{D}|^{2}+(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\xi)^{2}}
is an equivalent norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, the ellipticity of
\mathcal{A}(u)
follows from (
13
) by setting
C_{el}=\min\{2\mu(s_{min}),\eta(r_{min})+\eta^{\prime}(r_{min})r_{min})\}
.

If
\mu^{\prime}(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})<0
one applies the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality in (
7
) to obtain

\sum_{j,k,l,m=1}^{d}a^{lm}_{jk}\xi_{jl}\xi_{km}\geq\\
2\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})|\xi^{D}|^{2}+4\mu^{\prime}(|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2})|\mathbb{D}^{D}u|^{2}|\xi^{D}|^{2}+\left(\eta(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)+\eta^{\prime}(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u)\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u\right)(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits\xi)^{2}.

(14)

The continuity of
s\mapsto\mu(s)+2\mu^{\prime}(s)s
and the assumption
u\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})^{d}
imply the existence of
\tilde{s}_{min}\in[0,\|\mathbb{D}^{D}u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}]
such that
\mu(s)+2\mu^{\prime}(s)s\geq\mu(\tilde{s}_{min})+2\mu^{\prime}(\tilde{s}_{min})\tilde{s}_{min}>0
for all
s\in[0,\|\mathbb{D}^{D}u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}]
. Going back to (
14
) the ellipticity of
\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}u)
follows by setting
C_{el}=\min\{\mu(\tilde{s}_{min})+2\mu^{\prime}(\tilde{s}_{min})\tilde{s}_{min},\eta(r_{min})+\eta^{\prime}(r_{min})r_{min}\}
.

2
Local-in-time well posedness of the compressible Navier-Stokes

In this section we present a proof of Theorem
1.1
. First, we transform system (
1
) into Lagrangian coordinates. Then a linearization of the transformed system is derived. Next, employing Theorem
3.1
we show that the solution operator to the linearized system is a contraction on a suitably chosen function space. Finally, by the Banach fixed point theorem we conclude the local-in-time well posedness of the transformed system and of (
1
) accordingly.

2.1
System in Lagrangian coordinates

We define the Lagrangian coordinates as

\begin{split}X_{u}(t,y)&=y+\int_{0}^{t}u(s,X_{u}(s,y))\ {\rm d}s.\end{split}

(15)

A general function
f(t,x):\Omega\mapsto\mathbb{R}
fulfills

\begin{split}\partial_{t}f(t,X_{u}(t,y))&=\partial_{1}f(t,X_{u}(t,y))+\nabla_{x}f(t,X_{u}(t,y))\cdot u\\
\nabla_{y}f(t,X_{u}(t,y))&=\nabla_{x}f(t,X_{u}(t,y))\nabla_{y}X_{u}(t,y)\\
\nabla_{x}f&=\nabla_{y}f(\nabla_{y}X_{u})^{-1}.\end{split}

The Jacobi matrix of the transformation
X_{u}
is
\mathbb{I}_{d}+\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{y}u(s,X_{u}(s,y))\;\mathrm{d}s
. We tacitly assume the invertibility of this matrix, which is ensured if

\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\nabla_{y}u(s,\cdot)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\;\mathrm{d}s<\sigma

(16)

for some small number
\sigma
. Since we will work with functions that possess Lipschitz regularity with respect to space variables, the latter condition is fulfilled if
T
is chosen suitably small.
In what follows we use the notation
E_{u}:=\mathbb{I}_{d}-(\nabla X_{u})^{-1}
. Let us note that we get from (
15
)

E_{u}(t,y)=W\left(\int_{0}^{t}\nabla u(s,X_{u}(s,y))\;\mathrm{d}s\right),

(17)

where
W
is a
d\times d
–matrix valued mapping that is smooth with respect to matrices
B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
with
|B|<2\sigma
and
W(0)=0
.
We define
\tilde{\varrho}(t,y)=\varrho(t,X(t,y))
and
\tilde{u}(t,y)=u(t,X(t,y))
and we rewrite (
1
) as

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\tilde{\varrho}+\tilde{\varrho}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{y}\tilde{u}&=G(\tilde{\varrho},\tilde{u})\\
\tilde{\varrho}\partial_{t}\tilde{u}+\nabla_{y}\pi(\tilde{\varrho})-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{y}\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{D}_{y}\tilde{u})&=F(\tilde{\varrho},\tilde{u})\end{split}

(18)

where

\begin{split}G(\tilde{\varrho},\tilde{u})&=-\nabla\tilde{u}\cdot E_{\tilde{u}}\tilde{\varrho}\\
F(\tilde{\varrho},\tilde{u})&=\nabla_{y}\pi(\tilde{\varrho})E_{\tilde{u}}+\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{y}\tilde{\mathcal{S}}(\tilde{u})-\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{y}\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{D}^{D}_{y}\tilde{u})-\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\nabla_{y}\tilde{\mathcal{S}}(\tilde{u})E_{\tilde{u}})\\
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{D}^{D}_{y}\tilde{u})&=\mu(|\mathbb{D}^{D}_{y}\tilde{u}|^{2})\mathbb{D}^{D}_{y}\tilde{u}+\eta(\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{y}\tilde{u})\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{y}\tilde{u}\mathbb{I}_{d}\\
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}(\tilde{u})&=\mu(|\mathcal{G}(\tilde{u})|^{2})\mathcal{G}(\tilde{u})+\eta(\mathcal{D}(\tilde{u}))\mathcal{D}(\tilde{u})\mathbb{I}_{d}\\
\mathcal{G}(\tilde{u})&=\mathbb{D}^{D}_{y}\tilde{u}-\mathrm{sym}(\nabla_{y}\tilde{u}E_{\tilde{u}})^{D},\ \mathcal{D}(\tilde{u})=\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{y}\tilde{u}-\nabla_{y}\tilde{u}\cdot E_{\tilde{u}}.\end{split}

2.2
Linearized system

Let
\tilde{\varrho}=\varrho_{*}+\theta_{0}+\theta
where
\varrho_{*}
is a constant and
\theta_{0}=\varrho_{0}-\varrho_{*}
is independent of
t
. Recall both
\varrho_{*}
and
\theta_{0}
are given. Consequently, (
18
) becomes (recall (
6
))

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\theta+(\varrho_{*}+\theta_{0})\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{y}(\tilde{u}-u_{0})&=\mathscr{G}(\theta,\tilde{u})\\
(\varrho_{*}+\theta_{0})\partial_{t}\tilde{u}-\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}_{y}u_{0})(\mathbb{D}_{y}(\tilde{u}-u_{0}))+\pi^{\prime}(\varrho_{*}+\theta_{0})\nabla_{y}\theta&=\mathscr{F}(\theta,\tilde{u})\end{split}

(19)

where

\begin{split}\mathscr{G}(\theta,\tilde{u})=&G(\varrho_{*}+\theta_{0}+\theta,\tilde{u})-\theta\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{y}\tilde{u}-(\varrho_{*}+\theta_{0})\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits_{y}u_{0}\\

We use this lemma to infer

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

Recall that, according to the standard decomposition of unity, for all
\varepsilon>0
there is a covering
\{B_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of the torus
\Omega
such that

•

\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}B_{j}\supset\Omega
.

•

There are functions
\zeta_{j}
and
\tilde{\zeta}_{j}
such that
\operatorname{supp}\zeta_{j}\subset B_{j}
,
\tilde{\zeta}_{j}=1
on
\operatorname{supp}\zeta_{j}
,
\operatorname{supp}\tilde{\zeta}_{j}\subset 2B_{j}
,
\|\zeta_{j}\|_{W^{2,\infty}}+\|\tilde{\zeta}_{j}\|_{W^{2,\infty}}\leq c
with
c
independent of
j
, and
\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{j}=1
on
\Omega
.

•

Since
\nabla u_{0},\theta_{0}\in BUC
, we may assume the following property: for every
j\in\mathbb{N}

|\mathbb{D}u_{0}(x)-\mathbb{D}u_{0}(x_{j})|+|\theta_{0}(x)-\theta_{0}(x_{j})|<\varepsilon

for all
x\in B_{j}
. Here
x_{j}
is the center of a ball
B_{j}
.

We emphasize that
\varepsilon>0
will be chosen later.

For every
j
we solve an equation

\lambda u_{j}-\frac{1}{\varrho_{*}+\theta_{0}(x_{j})}\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}u_{0}(x_{j}))(\mathbb{D}u)-\pi^{\prime}(\varrho_{*}+\theta_{0}(x_{j}))\frac{1}{\lambda}\nabla\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u_{j}\\
=\tilde{\zeta}_{j}\left(F+\left(\frac{1}{\varrho_{*}+\theta_{0}(x)}\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}u(x))(\mathbb{D}u)-\frac{1}{\varrho_{*}+\theta_{0}(x_{j})}\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{D}u_{0}(x_{j}))(\mathbb{D}u)\right)\right.\\
+\left.\left(\pi^{\prime}(\varrho_{*}+\theta_{0}(x))\frac{1}{\lambda}\nabla\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u_{j}\right)-\pi^{\prime}(\varrho_{*}+\theta_{0}(x_{j}))\frac{1}{\lambda}\nabla\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u_{j}\right).

(73)

Since
\mathbb{D}u
and
\theta
are bounded and uniformly continuous function, (
73
) can be written as

\lambda u_{j}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda}u_{j}=\tilde{\zeta}_{j}F+\tilde{\zeta}_{j}\mathscr{P}_{\lambda j}u_{j}

(74)

where
\mathscr{P}_{\lambda j}:W^{2,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a linear operator bounded independently of
j
. Note also that
\mathscr{B}_{\lambda}+\mathscr{P}_{\lambda j}=\mathcal{B}_{\lambda}
on
B_{j}
.

We claim that
\lambda(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda}-\mathscr{P}_{\lambda j})^{-1}
,
|\lambda|^{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{k}(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda}-\mathscr{P}_{\lambda j})^{-1}
and
\partial_{k}\partial_{l}(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda}-\mathscr{P}_{\lambda j})^{-1}
are
\mathcal{R}-
bounded on
\Sigma_{\beta,\nu}
assuming
\varepsilon
is small enough. To justify this claim it is enough to repeat the proof of
[
12
, Proposition 4.2]
. First, recall that
\mathscr{P}_{\lambda}
is
\mathcal{R}-
bounded on
\Sigma_{\beta,\nu}
by
c\varepsilon
according to Proposition
4.1
. The same proposition also yields
(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda})^{-1}
is
\mathcal{R}-
bounded as
\lambda(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda})^{-1}
is
\mathcal{R}-
bounded on
\Sigma_{\beta,\nu}
by Theorem
3.4
. We have

\lambda(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda}-\mathscr{P}_{\lambda j})^{-1}=\lambda(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda})^{-1}(\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{P}_{\lambda j}(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda})^{-1})^{-1}\\
=\lambda(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda})^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(\mathscr{P}_{\lambda j}(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda})^{-1})^{n}.

We use Proposition
4.2
to deduce

\mathcal{R}\left\{\lambda(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda})^{-1}(\mathscr{P}_{\lambda j}(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda})^{-1})^{n}\right\}\leq\mathcal{R}\left\{\lambda(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda})^{-1}\right\}\left(c\varepsilon\mathcal{R}\left\{(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda})^{-1}\right\}\right)^{n}

Now it is enough to take
\varepsilon
such that
\gamma:=c\varepsilon\mathcal{R}\left\{(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda})^{-1}\right\}
is less than
1
. This allows to claim that

\mathcal{R}\left\{\lambda(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda}-\mathscr{P}_{\lambda j})^{-1}\right\}\leq\mathcal{R}\left\{\lambda(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{B}_{\lambda})^{-1}\right\}\frac{1}{1-\gamma}.

The same true can be deduced also for the remaining two operators.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{j}(\lambda)
be a solution operator to (
74
). We define

\mathcal{W}(\lambda)(f)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{j}\mathcal{T}_{j}(\lambda)(\tilde{\zeta}_{j}f).

Then
u=\mathcal{W}(\lambda)(f)
fulfills

\lambda u-\mathcal{B}_{\lambda}u=f-\mathscr{K}_{\lambda}(f)

where
\mathscr{K}_{\lambda}:L^{q}(\Omega)\to L^{q}(\Omega)
is defined as
\mathscr{K}_{\lambda}(f)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{B}_{\lambda}(\zeta_{j}u_{j})-\zeta_{j}\mathcal{B}_{\lambda}(u_{j})
and
u_{j}=\mathcal{T}_{j}(\lambda)(\tilde{\zeta}_{j}f)
.
The operator
\mathscr{K}_{\lambda}
may be written as

\mathscr{K}_{\lambda}=\frac{1}{\varrho_{*}+\theta_{0}}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k,l,n=1}^{d}a_{mn}^{kl}(\mathbb{D}u_{0})\left(\partial_{k}\partial_{l}\zeta_{j}u_{j,n}+\partial_{l}\zeta_{j}\partial_{k}u_{j,n}+\partial_{k}\zeta_{j}\partial_{l}u_{j,n}\right)\\
+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\pi^{\prime}(\varrho_{*}+\theta_{0})\frac{1}{\lambda}\left(\nabla\zeta_{j}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits u_{j}+\nabla^{2}\zeta_{j}u_{j}+\nabla\zeta_{j}\nabla u_{j}\right)

(75)

and
\mathscr{K}_{\lambda}
is in particular
\mathcal{R}-
bounded on
\Sigma_{\beta,\nu^{\prime}}
. Indeed, we show this particular boundedness of one term of the right hand side of (
75
) since the others might be treated in the same way.

\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\sum_{l=1}^{n}r_{l}(v)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}(\nabla\zeta_{j})\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\mathcal{T}_{j}(\lambda_{l})(\tilde{\zeta}_{j}f)\frac{\pi^{\prime}(\varrho_{*}+\theta_{0})}{\lambda}\right)\right\|^{q}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\ {\rm d}v\\
\leq c\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\sum_{l=1}^{n}r_{l}(v)(\nabla\zeta_{j})\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\mathcal{T}_{j}(\lambda_{l})(\tilde{\zeta}_{j}f)\right\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{j}\cap\Omega)}\ {\rm d}v\\
\leq c\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\sum_{l=1}^{n}r_{l}(v)|\lambda_{l}|^{-1/2}|\lambda_{l}|^{1/2}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\mathcal{T}_{j}(\lambda_{l})(\tilde{\zeta}_{j}f)\right\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{j}\cap\Omega)}\ {\rm d}v\\
\leq c\nu^{\prime-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\sum_{l=1}^{n}r_{l}(v)|\lambda_{l}|^{1/2}\mathop{\mathrm{div}}\nolimits\mathcal{T}_{j}(\lambda_{l})(\tilde{\zeta}_{j}f)\right\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{j}\cap\Omega)}\ {\rm d}v\\
\leq c\nu^{\prime-1/2}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\left\|\sum_{l=1}^{n}r_{l}(v)f\right\|^{q}_{L^{q}(B_{j}\cap\Omega)}\ {\rm d}v.

Consequently, the
\mathcal{R}-

\mathscr{K}_{\lambda}
is
c\nu^{\prime-1/2}
and by a proper choice of

it is less or equal than

. We deduce that

\mathcal{R}_{\mathcal{L}(L^{q})}\{(\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{K}_{\lambda})^{-1},\lambda\in\Sigma_{\beta,\nu^{\prime}}\}\leq 2.

See also Proposition
4.2
.
We thus have
u=\mathcal{W}\circ(\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{K}_{\lambda})^{-1}(f)
and this solution operator fulfills all the demanded properties.

Note also that the solution to (
67
) is unique. This comes from the ellipticity of operator

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Due to Theorem
3.3
we have

\lambda(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{A})^{-1}\ \mathcal{R}-\text{bounded for all }\lambda\in\Sigma_{\beta,\nu}.

(76)

Especially,
\lambda(\lambda\mathbb{I}_{d}-\mathscr{A})^{-1}
is bounded and by the Hille-Yosida theorem (see
[
3
, Theorem 3.1, Chapter 1]
) there exists a semigroup of class
C^{0}
generated by
\mathscr{A}
. We denote this semigroup
\mathscr{T}
, i.e.

\partial_{t}\mathscr{T}-\mathscr{A}\mathscr{T}=0.

We set
U(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathscr{T}(t-s)F(s)\ {\rm d}s
where
F=(\mathscr{G},\mathscr{F})
and we immediately get

\|U\|_{L^{p}(0,T;(W^{1,q}(\Omega)\times L^{q}(\Omega)))}\leq c\|F\|_{L^{p}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\Omega)\times L^{q}(\Omega))}

(77)

where
c
depends also on the time interval
(0,T)
. Recall that
U
has two components and we denote them by
\theta
and
u
. Moreover
U
solves

\partial_{t}U-\mathscr{A}U=F

which is another form of (
65
).

Clearly,
U
solves also

\partial_{t}U+2\nu U-\mathscr{A}U=F+2\nu U.

We set
G=F+2\nu U
and we extend the definition of
G
and
U
in such a way that
U(t)=0
,
G(t)=0
for all
t<0
.

We may write

U(t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{(-2\nu\mathbb{I}_{d}+\mathscr{A})(t-s)}\chi_{[0,\infty)}(t-s)G(s)\ {\rm d}s=\left(e^{(-2\nu\mathbb{I}_{d}+\mathscr{A})(s)}\chi_{[0,\infty)}(s)\right)*\left(G(s)\right)(t).

By rules for Fourier transform we get

\widehat{\partial_{t}U}=i\xi\left((-i\xi-2\nu)\mathbb{I}_{d}+\mathscr{A}\right)^{-1}\widehat{G}

and
M(\xi)=i\xi\left((-i\xi-2\nu)\mathbb{I}_{d}+\mathscr{A}\right)^{-1}
is the corresponding multiplier as mentioned in Theorem
3.2
. Indeed, using (
76
), Proposition
4.1
and Proposition
4.2
we infer that
M(\xi)
and
\xi M^{\prime}(\xi)
are
\mathcal{R}
–bounded and Theorem
3.2
implies

\|\partial_{t}U\|_{L^{p}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\Omega)\times L^{q}(\Omega))}\leq c\|G\|_{L^{p}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\Omega)\times L^{q}(\Omega))}\leq c\|F\|_{L^{p}(0,T;(W^{1,q}(\Omega)\times L^{q}(\Omega)))}

having (
77
) in mind. Consequently,

\|\theta\|_{W^{1,p}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\Omega))}+\|u\|_{W^{1,p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))}\leq c\left(\|\mathscr{G}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\Omega))}+\|\mathscr{F}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))}\right).

(78)

Note also that
\theta,u
solves

2\nu\left(\begin{matrix}\theta\\
u\end{matrix}\right)-\mathscr{A}\left(\begin{matrix}\theta\\
u\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}\mathscr{G}\\
\mathscr{F}\end{matrix}\right)-\partial_{t}\left(\begin{matrix}\theta\\
u\end{matrix}\right)+2\nu\left(\begin{matrix}\theta\\
u\end{matrix}\right).

Theorem
3.3
yields that
\partial_{j}\partial_{k}\lambda^{-1}\mathcal{R}_{\lambda 2}
is bounded on
\Sigma_{\beta,\nu}
for every
j,k=1,\ldots,d
and thus we deduce (with help of (
78
))

\|u\|_{L^{p}(0,T;W^{2,q}(\Omega))}\leq c\left(\|\mathscr{G}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;W^{1,q}(\Omega))}+\|\mathscr{F}\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega))}\right).

This completes the proof.

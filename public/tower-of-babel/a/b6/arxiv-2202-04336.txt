Prospects for extra Higgs boson search via 𝒑 𝒑 → 𝑯 , 𝑨 → 𝝉 𝝁 , 𝝉 𝝉 pp\to H,A\to\tau\mu,\tau\tau at the High Luminosity Large Hadron Collider Preprint: OU-HEP-220208
Wei-Shu Hou, Rishabh Jain, Chung Kao

Abstract
We extend heavy Higgs searches at the Large Hadron Collider for H → τ ​ μ H\to\tau\mu by CMS, and H → τ ​ τ H\to\tau\tau by ATLAS and CMS, to study discovery prospects of extra Higgs states in p p → H , A → τ μ , τ τ pp\to H,A\to\tau\mu,\tau\tau with e ​ μ + E̸ T e\mu+\not{E}_{T} and j τ ​ μ + E̸ T j_{\tau}\mu+\not{E}_{T} final states, where j τ = π , ρ , a 1 j_{\tau}=\pi\,,\rho\,,a_{1} and E̸ T \not{E}_{T} is missing transverse energy. In a general two Higgs doublet model without Z 2 Z_{2} symmetry, extra Yukawa couplings ρ τ ​ τ \rho_{\tau\tau} and ρ τ ​ μ \rho_{\tau\mu} can drive H , A → τ ​ τ H,A\to\tau\tau and τ ​ μ \tau\mu channels at hadron colliders, following gluon-gluon fusion production with extra ρ t ​ t \rho_{tt} couplings. The light Higgs boson h ⁡ ( 125 ) h(125) is found to resemble closely the Standard Model Higgs boson; in the alignment limit of cos ⁡ γ → 0 \cos\gamma\to 0 for h h – H H mixing, flavor changing neutral Higgs couplings such as h → τ ​ μ h\to\tau\mu are naturally suppressed, but the couplings of the heavier H H is optimized by sin ⁡ γ → 1 \sin\gamma\to 1 . We define various signal regions for H , A → τ ​ μ H,A\to\tau\mu and τ ​ τ \tau\tau and evaluate physics backgrounds from dominant processes with realistic acceptance cuts and tagging efficiencies. Two different studies are presented. We first perform a parton level study without any hadronization and with minimal detector smearing. We then include hadronization using PYTHIA 8.2 and fast detector simulation using DELPHES to give event level simulation. Results for s = 13 \sqrt{s}=13 TeV appear promising, which we extend further to s = 14 \sqrt{s}=14 TeV for the High Luminosity LHC.

V
Collider prospects for
H,A\to\tau\tau

The
H,A\to\tau\tau
process is more challenging
than
H,A\to\tau\mu
to probe at LHC because
mass reconstruction is poorer.
The tau pairs from
gg\to H,A\to\tau\tau
are back to back.
It is difficult to determine the invariant mass of tau pairs
(
M_{\tau\tau}
).
Thus we rely on the transverse mass with visible particles and
missing transverse energy from tau decays.
Furthermore, for
\rho_{\tau\tau}=\rho_{\tau\mu}=\lambda_{\tau}
,
the
H,A\to\tau\tau
final state has half the event rate
compared to
H,A\to\tau^{+}\mu^{-}+\tau^{-}\mu^{+}
.
Although there is no flavor violation,
we consider the same final state as

\tau\tau\to e\mu+\not{E}_{T},\ j_{\tau}\mu+\not{E}_{T}
.

Contribution of
\tau\mu
in
\tau\tau
.

With the same final state, obviously
H,A\to\tau\mu

can contribute to
H,A\to\tau\tau
.
However, we find that
M_{T}(\mu,\not{E}_{T})

is a powerful variable in separating
the
\tau\tau
signal from
\tau\mu
.
This is because

M_{T}(\mu,\not{E}_{T})>
100 GeV for
\tau\mu
, while

M_{T}(\mu,\not{E}_{T})<50
GeV for
\tau\tau
,
as
\mu
from the former comes from Higgs decay whereas
for
\tau\tau
it comes from
\tau
decay.

For
H,A\to\tau\tau
, both leptons
(fully leptonic) and
j_{\tau}
+ lepton
(semileptonic) come from
\tau
decay,
which give 4 and 3 neutrinos, respectively,
in the final state. This makes the
collinear approximation weaker for mass reconstruction.
So we rely on cluster transverse mass of
two visibly decaying
\tau
’s and
\not{E}_{T}

(
M_{T}(\tau_{\rm vis1},\tau_{\rm vis2},\not{E}_{T})
),
which is given as
Barger:1987nn
,

\displaystyle M_{T}^{2}(\tau_{\rm vis1},\tau_{\rm vis2},\not{E}_{T})=

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{|\vec{p}_{T}(\tau_{\rm vis1},\tau_{\rm vis2})|^{2}+M^{2}(\tau_{\rm vis1},\tau_{\rm vis2})}+\not{E}_{T}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\ \ \ -\left(\vec{p}_{T}(\tau_{\rm vis1},\tau_{\rm vis2})+\vec{\not{E}}_{T}\right)^{2},

(15)

with
M(\tau_{\rm vis1},\,\tau_{\rm vis2})

and
\vec{p}_{T}(\tau_{\rm vis1},\,\tau_{\rm vis2})

the invariant mass and net transverse momentum of
the two visible
\tau
decays, respectively.

VII
Discussion and Conclusion

Extra
\tau
couplings
\rho_{\tau\tau}
and
\rho_{\tau\mu}

can act as good probes for exotic
H
,
A
scalars
below the
t\bar{t}
threshold at HL-LHC.
We have illustrated the prospects of discovering
either
H
or
A
in
\tau\mu
and
\tau\tau
final states.
We studied the constraint on relevant couplings
from various searches and estimated
the statistical significance at the HL-LHC with

3\,\rm ab^{-1}
for
\sqrt{s}=13
, and 14 TeV.

From our study, we offer the following comments.

•

CMS
H\to\tau\mu
with
35.9\,\rm fb^{-1}
data
puts stronger bound on
\rho_{\tau\mu}
than
the latest Belle limit on
\tau\to\mu\gamma
, which is
limited to the mass range considered in this study.
Intuitively, if
H
or
A
approach

\mathcal{O}(\rm TeV)
, we expect Belle to do better.
Limits from
h\to\tau\mu
depends on
c_{\gamma}
,
strengthening as
c_{\gamma}
increases from 0.01 to 0.1.

•

Constraints on
pp\to H\to\tau\tau

from ATLAS and CMS follow an interesting trend:
ATLAS is better for
m_{H}<330
GeV,
but after which CMS and ATLAS are comparable.
This again tells the amazing sensitivity of CMS,
as the data used is only a quarter that of ATLAS.
Constraints from
pp\to h\to\tau\tau

become important for
c_{\gamma}=0.1
.

•

If
A
is lighter, like in Cases B1 and B2,
we find that the limits on
\rho_{\tau\mu}
are
the most stringent below
2m_{t}
, but
becomes even weaker than Cases A1 and A2
(
H

From our PL study of

–

For Cases A1 and A2, lower value of

We see a slight bump for Case A2 around

We find that below 400 GeV,

Above 400 GeV, significance can be improved by combining leptonic and semileptonic channels.

The

We draw the following remarks at the parton level:

–

Just like Cases B1 and B2 for
\tau\mu
,
the statistical significance overlaps. We see an

Beyond

t\bar{t}

–

For Cases A1 and A2,

–

Another big motivation to study

and

As discussed in Ref.

On the other side, there is EWBG driven by

\rho_{tc}
and

In this article, we find that beyond
2m_{t}

it is quite challenging to probe either

H,A\to\tau\mu
or
H,A\to\tau\tau
.
However, we have to keep in mind that we have
not considered all
\tau
-lepton decay modes,

\tau

promising future for discovering

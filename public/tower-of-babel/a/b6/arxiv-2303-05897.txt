. Introduction

The theory of
{\rm C}^{*}
-algebra extensions began with the work of Brown, Douglas and Fillmore on classifying essentially normal operators
[
3
]
. But soon it develops into a theory related to many fields of mathematics. Absorbing extensions play an important role in the theory of
{\rm C}^{*}
-algebras. The noncommutative Weyl-von Neumann theorem of Voiculescu
[
13
]
shows that for any separable
{\rm C}^{*}
-algebra
A
, every (unital) essential extension of
A
by
\mathcal{K}
is (unital) absorbing.
For the more general separable case, the essential extensions of
A
by
B\otimes\mathcal{K}
, Thomsen shows that there always exists a (unital) trival extension which is (unital) absorbing
[
12
]
. It is a non-trivial task to decide that under which conditions any essential extension of separable
{\rm C}^{*}
-algebras is absorbing.

In this paper, we consider the
{\rm C}^{*}
-algebra extensions of
A
by
B
.
We say an extension
e:0\to B\to E\to A\to 0
has
trivial

index

maps
, if the induced six-term exact sequence

\textstyle{{\,\,\mathrm{K}_{0}(B)\,\,}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\,\,\mathrm{K}_{0}(E)\,\,}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\,\,\mathrm{K}_{0}(A)\,\,}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\delta_{0}}
\textstyle{{\,\,\mathrm{K}_{1}(A)\,\,}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\delta_{1}}
\textstyle{{\,\,\mathrm{K}_{1}(E)\,\,}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{{\,\,\mathrm{K}_{1}(B)\,\,}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

satisfies that both
\delta_{0}
and

are the zero maps.
Using the intrinsic characterizations established by Elliott and Kucerovsky
[
5
]
and techniques for stable C*-algebras, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 0.1
.

Let
A,B
be nuclear, separable
{\rm C}^{*}
-algebras of stable rank one and real rank zero. Suppose that
A
is simple,
B
is stable and
({\rm K}_{0}(B),{\rm K}_{0}^{+}(B))

0\to B\to E\to A\to 0.

Then

We apply this result to the stably strong Ext-groups and obtain the correspondence from the isomorphic classes of extensions of
{\rm K}_{*}
-groups to subgroup of strong Ext-groups.

Remark 1.6

Definition 1.8
.

(
[
10
, Definition 3.2]
)
A triple

(iii)

Let

be an ordered abelian group, and let

be upper directed, hereditary, full subsets of

. Define

Denote the

(
[

]

[

Now we prove the following result for the nonsimple ordered group case, which generalizes

Proposition 1.10
.

Let
(G,G_{+},\Sigma)
be a weakly unperforated, scaled, ordered, abelian
group, and suppose that
n\,\,\hat{\cdot}\,\Sigma=G_{+}

for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\Sigma=G_{+}
.

Proof.

Let
g\in G_{+}
, then
(n+1)g\in G_{+}
. Since
n\,\hat{\cdot}\,\Sigma=G_{+}
, there is an element
x\in\Sigma
with
nx\geq(n+1)g
. From

and Definition
1.9
(i), there exists
t\in{\rm tor}(G)
such that
x-g+t\in G^{+}
and

. Then
x+t\in G_{+}
, and hence,

n(x+t)+t=(n-1)x+x+t\in G_{+}.

By Definition
1.9
(ii), we have
x+2t\in G^{+}
. Then,

(nx-(n+1)g)+(x+2t)+nt=(n+1)(x-g+t)+t\in G^{+}.

Using Definition

(ii) again, we have

x-g=x-g+t-t\in G^{+}.

By the hereditary property of
\Sigma
, we obtain
g\in\Sigma
. Thus
\Sigma=G^{+}
.

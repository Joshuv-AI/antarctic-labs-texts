Buckling by disordered growth
Rahul G. Ramachandran, Ricard Alert, Pierre A. Haas

Abstract
Buckling instabilities driven by tissue growth underpin key developmental events such as the folding of the brain. Tissue growth is disordered due to cell-to-cell variability, but the effects of this variability on buckling are unknown. Here, we analyse what is perhaps the simplest setup of this problem: the buckling of an elastic rod with fixed ends driven by spatially varying growth. Combining analytical calculations for simple growth fields and numerical sampling of random growth fields, we show that variability can increase as well as decrease the growth threshold for buckling, even when growth variability does not cause any residual stresses. For random growth, we find that the shift of the buckling threshold correlates with spatial moments of the growth field. Our results imply that biological systems can either trigger or avoid buckling by exploiting the spatial arrangement of growth variability.

We begin by studying “growth islands”, where part of the rod grows uniformly, while the rest does not grow [Fig.
2
(a)
]. This includes the subcases in which the growth islands are (1) two symmetric axial segments spanning the whole thickness of the rod, and (2) an annulus of the cross-section of the rod spanning its entire length. In the most general case, the growth islands are (3) two symmetric annular segments. In case (1), our numerical calculations give a uniform elastic stretch of the compressed rod before buckling. The stretch remains piecewise uniform along the rod in case (2), while, in case (3), the elastic stretch field is more complex [Fig.
2
(b)
]. In all three cases, the buckling threshold changes compared to the case of uniform growth,
\langle g\rangle_{\ast}\neq g_{\ast}
. This is hardly surprising in cases (2) and (3) because the growth field is incompatible with the geometry of the rod. This incompatibility produces residual stresses which might be expected to affect the buckling threshold. The surprise, however, is in case (1), in which the growth field is compatible with the rod geometry. Hence, the rod is unstressed if its ends are not fixed, and it has the same grown length as a uniformly growing rod, and yet the buckling thresholds differ!

Once again, the buckling threshold does not vary much in case (1), but varies more strongly in cases (2) and (3), and this variation again correlates well with the variance

One might expect this correlation of

In turn, this implies that the probability distribution of buckling thresholds depends not only on the probability distribution from which the growth variability is sampled, but also on the distribution of the spatial moments that emerges from it at the scale of the rod.

In summary, we have shown how spatial variability in growth shifts the buckling transition of an elastic rod. To understand these effects, we have proposed analytical estimates for growth islands (Fig.
2
). For random growth fields, we found that the shift of the buckling threshold correlates with spatial moments of the growth field (Fig.
3
). Together with the probability distribution of the growth disorder, the spatial moments of the growth field thus control buckling and hence, more biologically, its robustness against microscopic, cell-scale disorder.

Our results have multiple additional implications for the robustness of buckling transitions in biological systems. First, we showed that variability can significantly decrease the buckling threshold, but we did not find any cases that would lead to a comparably large increase of the threshold [Fig.
3
(a)
], suggesting that variability favours morphogenetic instabilities. Next, case (1) in Fig.
3
(a)
might represent growth disorder in a cell monolayer. The buckling threshold varies but slightly in this case, which suggests that the buckling transition of cell monolayers is largely independent of in-plane disorder. Since the threshold varies both above and below that for uniform buckling, imposing the average growth required for uniform buckling is not sufficient to ensure buckling in the presence of disorder. Finally, the spatial moments that we have implicated in the buckling threshold [Fig.
3
(d)
] stress that robustness depends not only on the magnitude of cell-to-cell variability but also on its spatial distribution in the tissue.

Meanwhile, performing the asymptotic calculations of buckling thresholds
[
18
]
that would confirm our analytical estimates and numerical results remains an open challenge. Our work also opens the door to interweaving the ideas of “mechanical statistics” that we have introduced here with the more established concepts of stochastic elasticity
[

,

, which studies ensembles of elastic objects with
uniform

(S7b)

I.2
Rod elasticity

We describe the elasticity of the rod within the framework of morphoelasticity
[
9
,
10
]
, based on a multiplicative decomposition
[
11
]
of the deformation gradient
\mathbfsf{\tilde{F}}
into an intrinsic growth part
\mathbfsf{F_{g}}
and an elastic part
\smash{\mathbfsf{F}=\mathbfsf{\tilde{F}}\bigl(\mathbfsf{F_{g}}\bigr)^{-1}}
. With incompressible neo–Hookean constitutive relations
[
9
]
, the deformed configuration of the rod minimises the elastic energy

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\iiint_{\mathcal{V}_{g}}{e\,\mathrm{d}V_{g}},\quad\text{with }e=\dfrac{C}{2}\bigl[\operatorname{tr}\bigl(\mathbfsf{F}^{\top}\mathbfsf{F}\bigr)-3\bigr],

(S9)

subject to the incompressibility constraint
\det{\mathbfsf{F}}=1
. Here,
C>0
is a material parameter and the integration is over the grown virtual configuration
\mathcal{V}_{g}
of the rod, with volume element
\mathrm{d}V_{g}=\bigl(\det{\mathbfsf{F_{g}}}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}V
, where
\mathrm{d}V=\varepsilon^{2}\zeta\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}\zeta\,\mathrm{d}\phi
is the volume element of the undeformed configuration.

The morphoelastic analogue of the first Piola–Kirchhoff stress tensor
[
9
,
12
]
is

\displaystyle\mathbfsf{P}=C\bigl[\mathbfsf{F}\bigl(\mathbfsf{F_{g}}\bigr)^{-\top}-p\mathbfsf{\tilde{F}}^{-\top}\bigr],

(S10)

where
p
is the pressure that enforces incompressibility
[
7
]
. The Cauchy equation
[

,
12
]
then takes the form

\displaystyle\operatorname{Div}\mathbfsf{P}^{\top}=\boldsymbol{0},

(S11a)

subject to the force-free condition
\mathbfsf{P}\cdot\boldsymbol{N}=\boldsymbol{0}
on the surface
\zeta=h
of the undeformed rod, and where the divergence is taken with respect to the undeformed configuration of the shell. Using its definition
[
9
]
, we find

\displaystyle\operatorname{Div}\mathbfsf{P}^{\top}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(1-\varepsilon\kappa\sin{\phi})\boldsymbol{t}+\varepsilon\zeta\tau\boldsymbol{B}}{(1-\varepsilon\kappa\sin{\phi})^{2}+(\varepsilon\zeta\tau)^{2}}\cdot\dfrac{\partial\mathbfsf{P}^{\top}}{\partial s}

\displaystyle\qquad+\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon\zeta}\left[\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\zeta}\bigl(\zeta\mathbfsf{P}\cdot\boldsymbol{N}\bigr)-\dfrac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\bigl(\mathbfsf{P}\cdot\boldsymbol{B}\bigr)\right].

(S11b)

I.3
Asymptotic expansion

We now specialise to the case of an axially growing rod that we are interested in here, and which corresponds to

0&1&0\\

where

\displaystyle\tilde{f}=1+\varepsilon g+\varepsilon E,

(S15)

II
Buckling of a uniformly growing rod

We can now provide a more formal derivation of the buckling threshold of a uniformly growing rod that confirms the informal argument in the main text. This supports the use a similar argument in the calculation of the approximate buckling threshold for growth islands in the final section of this Supplemental Material.

We assume a planar buckled midline
y(x)
, where
{x\in[-1/2,1/2]}
is the coordinate along the straight undeformed midline of the rod. From definitions
(
S15
)
, the midline strain and curvature strain are

\displaystyle E=\sqrt{1+y^{\prime}(x)^{2}}-(1+g),

\displaystyle K=\dfrac{y^{\prime\prime}(x)}{[1+y^{\prime}(x)]^{3/2}}.

(S33)

Varying
\mathcal{E}
with respect to
y(x)
leads to the Euler–Lagrange equation for the midline of the buckled rod,

\displaystyle\dfrac{h^{2}}{4[1+y^{\prime}(x)^{2}]^{3}}\left(y^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(x)+\dfrac{6y^{\prime\prime}(x)^{3}}{[1+y^{\prime}(x)^{2}]^{2}}-\dfrac{6y^{\prime\prime}(x)y^{\prime\prime\prime}(x)}{1+y^{\prime}(x)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\left(\dfrac{1+g}{[1+y^{\prime}(x)^{2}]^{3/2}}-1\right)y^{\prime\prime}(x),

(S34)

subject to the boundary conditions
{y(\pm 1/2)=y^{\prime}(\pm 1/2)=0}
imposing the conditions of clamped, fixed ends. Anticipating a buckled solution of small amplitude
A\ll 1
, we expand
{y(x)=Ay_{1}(x)+O\bigl(A^{2}\bigr)}
, so that

\displaystyle y_{1}^{\prime\prime\prime\prime}(x)+\dfrac{4g}{h^{2}}y_{1}^{\prime\prime}(x)=0,

(S35)

subject to
y_{1}(\pm 1/2)=y_{1}^{\prime}(\pm 1/2)=0
. The general solution of Eq.
(
S35
)
is

\displaystyle y_{1}(x)=c_{1}+c_{2}x+c_{3}\cos{qx}+c_{4}\sin{qx},

(S36)

where
q^{2}=4g/h^{2}
and
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3},c_{4}
are constants of integration. The boundary conditions require

\displaystyle c_{1}+c_{3}\cos{\dfrac{\lambda}{2}}

\displaystyle=c_{3}\lambda\sin{\dfrac{\lambda}{2}}=0

(S37a)

\displaystyle\dfrac{c_{2}}{2}+c_{4}\sin{\dfrac{\lambda}{2}}

\displaystyle=c_{2}+c_{4}\lambda\cos{\dfrac{\lambda}{2}}=0.

(S37b)

In a
symmetric
buckled mode,
c_{2}=c_{4}=0
, so
{q\sin{(q/2)}=0}
from Eqs.
(
S37a
)
unless
c_{1}=c_{3}=0
and hence
y_{1}(x)\equiv 0
. The solution
q=0
again leads to
y_{1}(x)\equiv 0
, so the lowest non-trivial eigenvalue, i.e., the smallest non-trivial wavenumber, is
q_{\text{s}}=2\uppi
.
Asymmetric
buckled modes with
c_{2},c_{4}\not=0
are also possible, but the simultaneous equations
(
S37b
)
have a non-zero solution if and only if
\tan{(q/2)}=q/2
, with smallest non-trivial wavenumber
q_{\text{a}}\approx 8.99
. Thus
q_{\text{a}}>q_{\text{s}}
, so the symmetric mode is the dominant buckling mode.

By definition,
q^{2}=4g/h^{2}
, so the rod buckles when
{g=h^{2}q_{\text{s}}^{2}/4=h^{2}\uppi^{2}}
. With
q=q_{\text{s}}
, we also get
{c_{1}=c_{3}=1/2}
up to rescaling
A
from Eqs.
(
S37a
)
. Equation
(
S36
)
yields
y_{1}(x)=(1+\cos{2\uppi x})/2=\cos^{2}{\uppi x}
because
c_{2}=c_{4}=0
in a symmetric mode. Thus
y(x)=A\cos^{2}{\uppi x}
as assumed in the main text.

(S41)

. At each growth increment, we solve equilibrium equations linearised around the deformed configuration of the previous step. This linearisation of the weak form of the equations and its spatial discretisation, and the solution algorithm are provided in Refs.
[

Growth disorder

To introduce growth disorder, we partition the rod into
M

N

MN

S1

To each cell
c

G_{c}
such that the growth tensor takes the values

\mathbfsf{F}_{\mathbfsf{g},c}=\begin{pmatrix}1+\langle g\rangle(1+G_{c})&&&0&&&0\\
0&&&1&&&0\\
0&&&0&&&1\end{pmatrix}

(S42)

on cell
c
, where
\langle g\rangle
is the spatially averaged growth of the rod, which, because the cells have equal volumes, we impose by requiring

\displaystyle\sum_{c}{G_{c}}=0.

(S43)

Growth islands

In the case of “growth islands”, the growth vanishes (i.e.,

) on all cells save the ones that represents the growth islands. Thus, from Eq.

\displaystyle G_{c}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}-1&\text{if }c\notin\text{growth islands},\\
MN-1&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

IV
Buckling of a rod with growth islands

In this section, we provide the details of the derivation of the approximation, stated in the main text, of the buckling threshold of a rod in which the growth is concentrated in “growth islands”, as introduced in the main text. The growth islands [Fig. 2(d) of the main text] are two symmetric annular segments, each of length
\ell_{\text{g}}/2
and cross-sectional area
\smash{\uppi h_{\text{g}}^{2}}
, centred at
x=\pm x_{0}
and
\zeta=\zeta_{0}
and defined by

\displaystyle\pm x_{0}-\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{g}}}{4}\leqslant x\leqslant\pm x_{0}+\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{g}}}{4},

\displaystyle\zeta_{0}^{2}-\frac{h_{\mathrm{g}}^{2}}{2h^{2}}\leqslant\zeta^{2}\leqslant\zeta_{0}^{2}+\frac{h_{\mathrm{g}}^{2}}{2h^{2}},

(S46)

The growth islands thus occupy a fraction
\chi_{\mathrm{g}}={\ell_{\mathrm{g}}h^{2}_{\mathrm{g}}}/{h^{2}}
of the total volume of the rod. The growth field is therefore

\displaystyle g(x,\zeta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\vskip 4.2679pt\dfrac{\langle g\rangle}{\chi_{\mathrm{g}}}&\text{if }(x,\zeta)\in\text{growth islands},\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

where
\langle g\rangle
is the (spatially averaged) mean growth of the rod. Indeed, the quantity
{\chi_{\mathrm{g}}(1\!+\!\langle g\rangle/\chi_{\mathrm{g}})\!+(1\!-\!\chi_{\mathrm{g}})(1\!+\!0)=1\!+\!\langle g\rangle}
is then the total growth of the rod, as required.

Due to the expected symmetry of the lowest buckling mode, we can, similarly to the numerical solution, restrict our calculations to the half rod
0\leqslant x\leqslant 1/2
.

We now make our first assumption: We assume that the geometric stretch
\lambda(x,\zeta)
in the flat configuration of the rod is piecewise uniform along the rod and uniform in the radial direction. This is consistent with the simple elastic stretch fields observed in numerical solutions in cases (1) and (2) in Fig. 2(b) of the main text, but is clearly a simplification in case (3). We thus assume

\displaystyle\lambda(x,\zeta)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\lambda_{1}&\text{if }x_{0}-\dfrac{\ell_{\mathrm{g}}}{4}\leqslant x\leqslant x_{0}+\dfrac{\ell_{\mathrm{g}}}{4},\\
\lambda_{2}&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

where the constants
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
are to be determined in our calculation. In the flat configuration, the condition of fixed ends imposes

\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{g}}}{2}\lambda_{1}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{g}}}{2}\right)\lambda_{2}=\frac{1}{2}.

(S51)

We further assume that the deflection of the rod, in its buckled configuration, is still given by that of the uniform rod, viz.,

\displaystyle y(x)=A\hat{y}(x)+O\bigl(A^{2}\bigr),\quad\text{where }\hat{y}(x)=\cos^{2}{\uppi x}.

(S52)

Let
\lambda_{\text{g}}=1+g
denote the intrinsic growth stretch. The elastic stretch of the midline in the buckled configuration is then, from the multiplicative decomposition of morphoelasticity,

\displaystyle\Lambda=\dfrac{\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+y^{\prime}(x)^{2}}}{\lambda_{\text{g}}}=\dfrac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\text{g}}}+A^{2}\dfrac{\hat{y}^{\prime 2}}{2\lambda\lambda_{\text{g}}}+O\bigl(A^{4}\bigr).

(S53)

Because we seek to compute a correction to the leading-order buckling threshold, we need to replace the strain energy term
E^{2}
in the energy density
(
S32a
)
with a nonlinear form. We choose the neo-Hookean energy density corresponding to principal stretches
\Lambda,\Lambda^{-1/2},\Lambda^{-1/2}
, as required by volume conservation, for this purpose and thus replace

\displaystyle E^{2}

\displaystyle\to\Lambda^{2}+\dfrac{2}{\Lambda}-3

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\dfrac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\text{g}}}\right)^{2}+\dfrac{2\lambda_{\text{g}}}{\lambda}-3+A^{2}\dfrac{\hat{y}^{\prime}(x)^{2}}{\lambda\lambda_{\text{g}}}\dfrac{\left(\lambda/\lambda_{\text{g}}\right)^{3}\!-\!1}{\left(\lambda/\lambda_{\text{g}}\right)^{2}}+O\bigl(A^{4}\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad\approx\left(\dfrac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\text{g}}}\right)^{2}+\dfrac{2\lambda_{\text{g}}}{\lambda}-3+A^{2}\hat{y}^{\prime}(x)^{2}\dfrac{\lambda-\lambda_{\text{g}}}{\lambda}.

(S54)

In the final step, we have linearised, in the term at order
O\bigl(A^{2}\bigr)
,
\lambda\approx\lambda_{\text{g}}\approx 1
excepting one factor
1/\lambda
resulting from the midline stretch, and, of course, except in the difference
\lambda-\lambda_{\text{g}}
. We emphasise that we have no asymptotic justification for the use of this nonlinear midline strain in our calculation
2
2

2

This justification would be provided by extending the asymptotic derivation of the rod theory in the first section of this Supplemental Material to non-uniform growth and beyond leading order, which is beyond the scope of this analysis.
. Moreover, the curvature strain of the buckled rod is

\displaystyle K=\dfrac{\lambda y^{\prime\prime}(x)}{\bigl(\lambda^{2}+y^{\prime}(x)^{2}\bigr)^{3/2}}\approx A\dfrac{\hat{y}^{\prime\prime}(x)}{\lambda^{2}},

(S55)

which includes a factor
1/\lambda^{2}
resulting from the stretch of the midline; this provides an informal justification for not neglecting a corresponding factor
1/\lambda
above. Using Eq.
(
S32a
)
, we thus obtain an approximation for the energy of the rod,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\approx\int_{0}^{1}{\int_{0}^{h}{\left[\left(\dfrac{\lambda}{\lambda_{\text{g}}}\right)^{2}+\dfrac{2\lambda_{\text{g}}}{\lambda}-3+A^{2}\left(\hat{y}^{\prime}(x)^{2}\dfrac{\lambda-\lambda_{\text{g}}}{\lambda}+\dfrac{\zeta^{2}\hat{y}^{\prime\prime}(x)^{2}}{2\lambda^{4}}\right)\right]\lambda_{\text{g}}\,\mathrm{d}\zeta}\,\mathrm{d}x}=\mathcal{E}_{0}+A^{2}\mathcal{E}_{2}.

(S56)

In the double integral, we have included a factor of
\lambda_{\text{g}}\approx 1
that corresponds to the Jacobian for the transformation from the undeformed to the intrinsic configuration of the rod, motivated, like the use of the nonlinear form for the strain energy, by our quest to compute an estimate for a correction to the buckling threshold. Again, we do not have a formal asymptotic justification for including this term. In the final expression,
\mathcal{E}_{0},\mathcal{E}_{2}
are complicated functions of
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\langle g\rangle_{\ast}
as well as of the geometric parameters describing the growth islands. We determine
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
by minimising
\mathcal{E}_{0}
subject to Eq.
(
S51
)
. For this purpose, we write

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=1+L_{2}h^{2}+L_{4}h^{4}+O\bigl(h^{6}\bigr),

\displaystyle\langle g\rangle_{\ast}

\displaystyle=g_{2}h^{2}+g_{4}h^{4}+O\bigl(h^{6}\bigr),

(S57)

which yields an expansion for
\lambda_{2}
from Eq.
(
S51
)
. We thus obtain

\displaystyle L_{2}

\displaystyle=-g_{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{\ell_{\mathrm{g}}}-1\right),

\displaystyle L_{4}=\bigl(g_{2}^{2}-g_{4}\bigr)\left(\dfrac{1}{\ell_{\mathrm{g}}}-1\right).

(S58)

The buckling threshold is now determined from
\mathcal{E}_{2}=0
, which corresponds to
\partial\mathcal{E}/\partial A^{2}=0

A\neq 0

A=0

\displaystyle g_{2}

\displaystyle=\uppi^{2},

\displaystyle g_{4}=\dfrac{\uppi^{3}}{\ell_{\text{g}}^{2}}\left\{\uppi\ell_{\text{g}}\left[1-\ell_{\text{g}}+2\ell_{\text{g}}\left(\dfrac{\zeta_{0}}{h}\right)^{2}-\left(\dfrac{h_{\text{g}}}{h}\right)^{-2}\right]-\left[1+3\ell_{\text{g}}-2\ell_{\text{g}}\left(\dfrac{\zeta_{0}}{h}\right)^{2}-\left(\dfrac{h_{\text{g}}}{h}\right)^{-2}\right]\cos{\bigl(4\uppi x_{0}\bigr)}\sin{\bigl(\uppi\ell_{\text{g}}\bigr)}\right\}.

(S59)

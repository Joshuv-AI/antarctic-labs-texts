Quantum Principal Bundles and Yang–Mills Scalar Matter Fields Preprint: AIP/123-QED
Gustavo Amilcar Saldaña Moncada

Abstract
This paper’s aim is to develop a non–commutative geometrical version of the theory of Yang–Mills and space–time scalar matter fields. To accomplish this purpose, we will dualize the geometrical formulation of this theory, in which principal G G –bundles, principal connections, and linear representations play the most important role. In addition, we will present the non–commutative geometrical Lagrangian of the system as well as the non–commutative geometrical associated field equations . At the end of this work, we show an some examples.



I
Introduction

The Standard Model is one of the most successful and important theoretical achievement in modern physics. From a philosophical/mathematical point of view, it is another example of the intrinsic relation and interplay between physics and differential geometry, which in this case, is given by the geometrical framework of principal bundles, their connections and the associated structures.

Despite all of this, it presents some basic and fundamental problems that it cannot solve. For example, a consistent and coherent description of the space–time at the level of the Plank scale. The need to investigate further is evident. Non–commutative geometry, also known as

(7)

We say that the corepresentation is

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(V)<|\mathbb{N}|

of
\mathcal{G}

Given two

\textsc{Mor}(\delta^{V},\delta^{W}),

(9)

–corepresentations will be denoted by

(10)

A
\mathcal{G}
–corepresentation

is

(L\not=\{0\},V)
such that

Concluding Comments

This work was developed under Durdevich's theory of qpb's and as we have mentioned in Subsection II.B, there are mathematical reason to consider this approach and not the one presented in, for example, reference
32 32
. In order of importance, these reasons are the following.

1.

The curvature
R^{\omega}
of every qpc
\omega
is a linear map with domain
\mathfrak{qg}^{\#}
, as the dualization of the classical case indicates.

2.

The curvature is quantum differential form of type
\mathrm{ad}
, as in the classical case, i.e.,
R^{\omega}

\in

\textsc{Mor}(\mathrm{ad},\Delta_{\mathrm{Hor}})
. In terms of a physical interpretation, we can consider
R^{\omega}
as a non–-commutative geometrical tensor field.

3.

The Systematical use of left/right associated qvbs with their associated qlc's for
D^{\omega}
and
\widehat{D}^{\omega}
.

4.

When
\mathfrak{qg}^{\#}
is a finite–dimensional
\mathbb{C}
–vector space, we can apply all the theory of associated qvb's for the
\mathcal{G}
–corepresentation
\mathrm{ad}
. Particularly, we can use the inner products of equations (
126
), (
128
) and take the square norm of
R^{\omega}
, as in differential geometry.

Of course, there are other important aspects of Durdevich's theory that enabled the formulation of our framework. For instance, the use of universal differential envelope
\ast
–calculus as quantum differential forms of
\mathcal{G}
. This space not only allows us to extend the
\ast
–Hopf algebra structure of
G^{\infty}
to
\Gamma^{\wedge\,\infty}
, but it is also maximal with this property (
26 26
); moreover, it generalizes the
\ast
–algebra of
\mathbb{C}
–valued differential forms of a (compact matrix) Lie group (
31 31
). The universal differential envelope
\ast
–calculus and the extension of the coproduct
\Delta
play a central role in defining differential calculus on qpb's, the
\ast
–algebras
\mathrm{Hor}^{\bullet}P
,
\|^{\bullet}P
,
\Omega^{\bullet}(M)
and the
\mathcal{G}
–corepresentation
\Delta
.

It is worth mentioning that a necessary auxiliary map is required (the embedded differential
\Theta
) to define
R^{\omega}
and this implies the existence of the operator
S^{\omega}
. As we have seen, this operator naturally appears in the non–commutative geometrical Bianchi identity and in the non–commutative geometrical Yang–Mills equation. In differential geometry, every principal connection is regular and hence
S^{\omega}=0
(
10 10
).

In reference
13 13
we show that the operator
S^{\omega}
produces physical terms in the electromagnetic theory in the Moya–Weyl algebra: electric charges and currents; and magnetic charges and currents in vacuum. This fact is strong motivating reason to keep the research going and tested it for other sections of the Standard Model.

It is worth mentioning that in differential geometry, the Standard Model is developed in trivial principal
G
–bundles over
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Thus, trivial principal bundle are particularly important in physics (
16 16
). On the other hand, Subsection V.B proves that our theory can be applied in trivial quantum principal
\mathcal{G}
–bundles (for any quantum group
\mathcal{G}
) in the Moya–Weyl algebra. It is worth mentioning that the Moyal–Weyl algebra is one of the most widely used non-commutative spaces in the study of quantum gravity within the framework of non-commutative geometry (
40 40
).

On the other hand, in differential geometry, the theory of
U(1)
–instantons (
16 16
) is developed in the principal bundle

. The Non–commutative geometrical counterpart of this bundle is a particular case of the examples presented in Subsection V.C and the reader can check our theory applied in this qpb in reference
11 11
. How the operator
S^{\omega}
changes the theory of
SU(2)
–instantons will be explored in forthcoming publications.

It is worth mentioning that the class of examples presented in Subsection V.A allows to develop a gauge theory on classical spaces (compact manifolds) under the assumption that the structure group of the theory is a quantum group, for example, the quantum group associated with a Lie group (for which we obtain the gauge theory of differential geometry) or the quantum groups
SU_{q}(n)
.

Of course, the three classes of qpb's discussed in Section V are not the only ones suitable for our theory. For instance, if all the quantum spaces are finite–dimensional, it follows that all the operators in the theory are adjointable. The reader can check a concrete example of this in reference
12 12
. An interesting research project is to apply this theory to qpb's whose quantum base space
B
is the tensor product of a finite–dimensional
\ast
–algebra with the
\ast
–algebra of
\mathbb{C}
–valued smooth functions on a (compact) manifold. In these cases, the space
B
can be seen as a kind of non-commutative Kaluza–Klein model, as in references
1 1
;
34 34
. This will be explored in forthcoming publications.

To finalize our comments about the operator
S^{\omega}
, notice that we have asked for the existence of the adjoint operators of

and

, not for an specific form of them. For example, for the qpb and the differential calculus used in Sections 2, 3 of reference

As we have mentioned in the first section, this paper opens the door to other lines of research in order to get a geometrical formulation of the Standard Model in the framework of Non–Commutative Geometry and all that this entails. For instance, using spectral triplets can be a way to relate this theory with Connes' formulations as well as adding a kind of non–commutative geometrical spin geometry to our theory.

Finally, it is worth mentioning that the presented formalism can be easily generalized in order to add quantum Pseudo–Riemannian closed orientable spaces by weakening Definition

Proposition 46
.

Let

C:\Omega^{\bullet}(B)\otimes_{B}\textsc{Mor}(\mathrm{ad},\Delta_{P})\longrightarrow\Omega^{\bullet}(B)\otimes_{B}\textsc{Mor}(\mathrm{ad},\Delta_{P})

be a map formally adjointable with respect to
\langle-|-\rangle^{\bullet}_{\l}
. Then the map
\widehat{C}:=\ast^{\bullet}\circ C\circ\ast^{\bullet\,-1}
is formally adjointable with respect to
\langle-|-\rangle^{\bullet}_{\widehat{\r{}}}
.

Proof.

Let
\psi
,
\phi

\in

E^{\mathfrak{qg}^{\#}}_{\r{\otimes}}{}_{B}\Omega^{\bullet}(B)
. Then

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{C}(\psi)\mid\phi\rangle^{\bullet}_{\widehat{\r{}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\ast^{\bullet\,-1}(\widehat{C}(\psi))\mid\ast^{\bullet\,-1}(\phi)\rangle^{\bullet}_{\l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle C(\ast^{\bullet\,-1}(\psi))\mid\ast^{\bullet\,-1}(\phi)\rangle^{\bullet}_{\l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\ast^{\bullet\,-1}(\psi)\mid C^{\star_{\l}}(\ast^{\bullet\,-1}(\phi))\rangle^{\bullet}_{\l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\ast^{\bullet\,-1}(\psi)\mid\ast^{\bullet\,-1}(C^{\star_{\r{}}}(\phi))\rangle^{\bullet}_{\l}=\langle\psi\mid C^{\star_{\r{}}}(\phi)\rangle^{\bullet}_{\widehat{\r{}}},

where
C^{\star_{\l}}
is the formally adjoint operator of
C
and we have defined
C^{\star_{\r{}}}:=\ast^{\bullet}\circ C^{\star_{\l}}\circ\ast^{\bullet\,-1}
.
∎

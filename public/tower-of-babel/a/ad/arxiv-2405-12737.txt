Hyperuniformity of random measures on Euclidean and hyperbolic spaces
Michael Björklund, Mattias Byléhn

Abstract
. We investigate lower asymptotic bounds of number variances for invariant locally square-integrable random measures on Euclidean and real hyperbolic spaces. In the Euclidean case we show that there are subsequences of radii for which the number variance grows at least as fast as the volume of the boundary of Euclidean balls, generalizing a classical result of Beck. With regards to real hyperbolic spaces we prove that random measures are never geometrically hyperuniform and if the random measure admits non-trivial complementary series diffraction, then it is hyperfluctuating. Moreover, we define spectral hyperuniformity and stealth of random measures on real hyperbolic spaces in terms of vanishing of the complementary series diffraction and sub-Poissonian decay of the principal series diffraction around the Harish-Chandra Ξ \Xi -function.

1.
Introduction

In the field of statistical mechanics for low-temperature states of matter, the notion of
hyperuniformity
, or
superhomogeneity
, as introduced by Stillinger and Torquato in the seminal paper
[
21
]
in 2003, quantifies long-range order of an invariant point process in terms of the degree of suppression of large-scale density fluctuations. Equivalently, hyperuniformity is characterized by the suppression of small frequencies in the diffraction picture. Hyperuniformity and the associated number variances of invariant point processes have been studied extensively in Euclidean spaces, examples including invariant random lattice shifts, i.i.d. perturbations of such, certain determinantal point processes, most mathematical quasicrystals and more. See
[
20
]
for an extensive survey and
[
8
]
for details on mathematical quasicrystals.

In this article we investigate lower asymptotic bounds of number variances, i.e. the variance of the measure of asymptotically large balls, for invariant locally square-integrable random measures on Euclidean and real hyperbolic spaces. We prove for general invariant locally square-integrable random measures on Euclidean space that there are unbounded sequences of centered Euclidean balls such that the number variance grows at least as fast as the volume of the boundary of the balls, extending a Theorem of Beck in the case of a locally finite point set
[
5
, Theorem 2A]
.
Our methods are Fourier theoretic and utilizes the
diffraction measure
of a random measure, generalizing the notion of the
structure factor
of a point process.

As for random measures on real hyperbolic space, we investigate contending notions of hyperuniformity. This is also done in terms of the diffraction measure associated to such a random measure, which for hyperbolic spaces (and more generally, commutative spaces) is defined using the available
spherical transform
, generalising the Hankel transform in Euclidean space. We show that, contrary to the Euclidean setting, the large-scale number variance of a random measure on real hyperbolic space grows at least as fast as the volume of large metric balls along some unbounded sequence of metric balls. Nevertheless, we are able to define a notion of spectral hyperuniformity on the principal part of the relevant frequency domain that is distinctly different from measuring large-scale density fluctuations. Instead, our definition favours the property of having suppressed ”small scale” frequency fluctuations to characterize hyperuniformity.

1.1.
Random measures

Let
X
denote the space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the action of translations or real hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with the action of orientation-preserving isometries. By a
random measure
on
X
we mean a probability measure
\mu
on the cone
\mathscr{M}_{+}(X)
of positive locally finite measures on
X
, which can be thought of as the law of a
\mathscr{M}_{+}(X)
-valued random variable. One usually studies such random measures in terms of
linear statistics

Sf:\mathscr{M}_{+}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
associated to bounded measurable functions
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with bounded support, given by

\displaystyle Sf(p)=\int_{X}f(x)dp(x)\,.

Given an invariant locally square-integrable random measure
\mu
on
X
, we denote by
\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
the
number variance
with respect to
\mu
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)=\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(S\chi_{B_{r}})=\int_{\mathscr{M}_{+}(X)}|p(B_{r})-\iota_{\mu}\mathrm{Vol}_{X}(B_{r})|^{2}d\mu(p)

where
B_{r}=B_{r}(o)
is the metric ball of radius
r>0
centered at a fixed reference point
o\in X
and
\iota_{\mu}>0
is the intensity of
\mu
, i.e. the mean measure of a unit volume Borel set. More generally, one can consider variances of the form
\mathrm{Var}(S\chi_{rB})
for
r>0
, where
B\subset X
is any bounded Borel set with positive measure and
rB
is the
r
-neighbourhood of
B
with respect to the metric on
X
. We will in this paper only consider metric balls
B_{r}
.

1.2.
Number variances and diffraction measures in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

A uniform upper bound on the number variance of a random measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is obtained from an application of the mean ergodic theorem, yielding

\displaystyle\limsup_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(R)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(B_{R})^{2}}=0\,.

We provide a general lower bound of the number variance for maximizing sequences of radii, extending a celebrated result of Beck
[
5
, Theorem 2A]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu
be an invariant locally square-integrable random measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

(1.2)

\displaystyle\limsup_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(R)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{n-1}(\partial B_{R})}>0\,.

Remark 1.2
.

The question of whether there is a uniform lower bound on this quotient for a given random measure
\mu
is in general unclear. In Subsections
5.2
and
5.3
however, we show that the randomly shifted standard lattice in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
satisfies

(1.3)

\displaystyle\liminf_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{5}}}(R)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{4}(\partial B_{R})}=0\,.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on a spectral formulation of the number variances. More precisely, the
diffraction measure

\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}
of an invariant locally square-integrable random measure
\mu
is the unique positive Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfying

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu}(Sf_{1}\mathchar 59\relax Sf_{2})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widehat{f}_{1}(\xi)\overline{\widehat{f}_{2}(\xi)}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)

for all bounded measurable functions
f_{1}\mathchar 59\relax f_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with bounded support, where
\widehat{f}
denotes the Fourier transform of
f
. In particular, one computes the number variance to be

\displaystyle\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)=(2\pi)^{n}r^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}J_{\frac{n}{2}}(r\lVert\xi\rVert)^{2}\frac{d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)}{\lVert\xi\rVert^{n}}\,\mathchar 59\relax

where
J_{n/2}
denotes a Bessel function of the first kind. Classical asymptotic expansions of such Bessel functions allow for a proof of Theorem
1.1
.

1.3.
Hyperuniformity and stealth in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

An invariant locally square-integrable random measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
geometrically hyperuniform
if

\displaystyle\limsup_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(R)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(B_{R})}=0\,.

The volume term in the denominator coincides with the variance of the invariant Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with unit intensity, and one can interpret a hyperuniform random measure
\mu
as having ”sub-Poissonian mass fluctuations”. If the above upper limit is infinite, the random measure is
hyperfluctuating
. In
[
8
]
, the first author and Hartnick prove that a random measure
\mu
is geometrically hyperuniform if and only if it is
spectrally hyperuniform
, meaning that

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(B_{\varepsilon}(0))}{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(B_{\varepsilon}(0))}=0\,.

The denominator here can be interpreted as the diffraction measure of the invariant Poisson point process in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with unit intensity.

A particularly rigid class of hyperuniform invariant random measures
\mu
are
stealthy
random measures, meaning random measures for which the diffraction measure
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}
vanishes in a neighbourhood of the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Examples include invariant random shifts of lattices, and in upcoming work with A. Fish we will provide new families of stealthy random measures and point processes on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

1.4.
Number variances and diffraction measures for real hyperbolic spaces

For
n
-dimensional real hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}=\mathrm{SO}^{\circ}(1\mathchar 59\relax n)/\mathrm{SO}(n)
, we consider locally square-integrable random measures
\mu
that are invariant under the action of the orientation-preserving isometry group
\mathrm{SO}^{\circ}(1\mathchar 59\relax n)
in analogy with isotropic (i.e. translation and rotation invariant) random measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

On
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, there is a well-behaved generalization of the Fourier transform, the
spherical transform
, available for bi-
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-invariant functions
\varphi:\mathrm{SO}^{\circ}(1\mathchar 59\relax n)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
. It is given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathrm{SO}^{\circ}(1\mathchar 59\relax n)}\varphi(g)\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}(g)dg\,\mathchar 59\relax

where
dg
denotes a fixed Haar measure and
\omega^{(n)}_{\lambda}
are
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-
spherical functions
for
\mathrm{SO}^{\circ}(1\mathchar 59\relax n)
, that is, the bi-
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-invariant functions on
\mathrm{SO}^{\circ}(1\mathchar 59\relax n)
given by

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}(g)=\frac{2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}\sinh(d(g.o\mathchar 59\relax o))^{2-n}\int_{0}^{d(g.o\mathchar 59\relax o)}(\cosh(d(g.o\mathchar 59\relax o))-\cosh(s))^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\cos(\lambda s)ds\,\mathchar 59\relax

where
d
denotes the hyperbolic metric. With respect to the spherical transform, the diffraction measure of an invariant locally square-integrable random measure
\mu
is a pair of measures defined by the equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}_{\mu}(S\varphi_{1}\mathchar 59\relax S\varphi_{2})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{\varphi}_{1}(\lambda)\overline{\widehat{\varphi}_{2}(\lambda)}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(p)}(\lambda)+\int_{0}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\widehat{\varphi}_{1}(i\lambda)\overline{\widehat{\varphi}_{2}(i\lambda)}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(c)}(\lambda)\,.

We refer to the measures
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(p)}
on
(0\mathchar 59\relax+\infty)
and
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(c)}
on
i[0\mathchar 59\relax\frac{n-1}{2})
as the
principal-
and
complementary diffraction measure
respectively. We leave out
\lambda=0
corresponding to the
Harish-Chandra
\Xi
-function

\omega_{0}
from the support of the principal diffraction measure for reasons that will become clear from our results. The existence and uniqueness of such diffraction measures can be seen as part of important work by Krein, Gelfand-Vilenkin and later by Bopp in
[
9
, Part II, Theorem 3]
.

Remark 1.3
.

Diffraction measures can more generally be defined for random measures on a large class of
commutative spaces

X=G/K
with
K
compact, which includes higher rank symmetric spaces, regular trees, Bruhat-Tits buildings and products of such. These are positive measures on the
K
-spherical unitary dual
\widehat{G}^{K}
of the pair
(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
. We investigate the general framework and prove the existence and uniqueness of diffraction measures in an upcoming article.

In terms of the diffraction measure, the number variance can be written as

\displaystyle\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)=\frac{\pi^{n}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+1)^{2}}\,\sinh(r)^{2n}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\omega^{(n+2)}_{\lambda}(a_{r})|^{2}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(p)}(\lambda)+\int_{0}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}|\omega^{(n+2)}_{i\lambda}(a_{r})|^{2}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(c)}(\lambda)\Big)\,.

In this picture, it is crucial to understand the asymptotic behaviour of the spherical functions in order to understand the asymptotics of the number variance.

Also, the asymptotics of the number variance of a random measure
\mu
depends significantly on whether the complementary diffraction measure is trivial or not.

A first naive definition of geometric hyperuniformity of a random measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is to require that

\displaystyle\limsup_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(R)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(B_{R})}=0\,.

However, since
\mathrm{SO}^{\circ}(1\mathchar 59\relax n)
is non-amenable then
\mathrm{Vol}(\partial B_{R})\asymp\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(B_{R})
as
R\rightarrow+\infty
, so it is a priori unclear whether this definition admits examples or not. We prove that no random measures on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
are geometrically hyperuniform in the following hyperbolic analogue of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mu
be a locally square-integrable invariant random measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Then

(1.4)

\displaystyle\limsup_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(R)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(B_{R})}>0\,.

Moreover,

(1)

if
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(c)}(\{0\})>0
, then

(1.5)

\displaystyle\liminf_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(R)}{R^{2}\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(B_{R})}>0\,.

(2)

if
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(c)}([\delta\frac{n-1}{2}\mathchar 59\relax\frac{n-1}{2}))>0
for some
\delta\in(0\mathchar 59\relax 1)
, then

(1.6)

\displaystyle\liminf_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(R)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(B_{R})^{1+\delta}}>0\,.

For the special case of a randomly shifted lattice orbit in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, Theorem
1.4
implies that there is a sequence of radii such that the number variance grows at least as fast as the hyperbolic volume of large metric balls. Moreover, when the complementary diffraction measure is non-trivial, the random measure is hyperfluctuating in analogy with the Euclidean terminology.

There are related works to the above result: In
[
12
, Theorem 2]
, Hill and Parnovski derive explicit asymptotics of the number variance for randomly shifted lattice orbits in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Theorem
1.4
implies that the error term in their result is sharp. In
[
16
, Theorem 2]
, Magyar provides a positive lower bound on the
L^{\infty}
-norm of the discrepancy of infinite point sets over balls in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
that is proportional to the volume of the ball. For number variances (the
L^{2}
-norm of the discrepancy), we expect that there are examples of translation bounded point processes
\mu
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
for which

\displaystyle\liminf_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(R)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(B_{R})}=0\,.

1.5.
Hyperuniformity in real hyperbolic space

In light of Theorem
1.4
, there is no canonical ”geometric” definition of hyperuniformity of a random measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. We instead formulate a notion of
spectral hyperuniformity
in terms of the diffraction measure of
\mu
that is distinctly different from the geometric one.

Definition 1.5
.

An invariant locally square-integrable random measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is
spectrally hyperuniform
if
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(c)}=0
and

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(p)}((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon])}{\varepsilon^{3}}=0\,.

The denominator
\varepsilon^{3}
is asymptotic to the diffraction measure of the interval
(0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon]
for the unit intensity invariant Poisson point process as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, so this definition of hyperuniformity is in that way consistent with spectral hyperuniformity on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is worth noting that, with respect to this definition, it is no longer the case that all invariant randomly shifted lattice orbits are spectrally hyperuniform as there are lattices that admit complementary spectrum, even co-compact ones, see for example
[
13
, Section 1.2, Eq.B]
. With this notion of hyperuniformity in mind, we define what it means for a random measure to be stealthy.

Definition 1.6
.

An invariant locally square-integrable random measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is
stealthy
if
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(c)}=0
and there is a
\lambda_{o}>0
such that
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(p)}((0\mathchar 59\relax\lambda_{o}))=0
.

Stealthy random measures are spectrally hyperuniform. In
[
13
, Section 1.2, Eq.B]
, Jenni constructs a lattice
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})\cong\mathrm{SO}^{\circ}(1\mathchar 59\relax 2)
that does not admit complementary spectrum nor principal spectrum around
\lambda=0
in the sense that the smallest non-zero eigenvalue of the Laplace operator on
\Gamma\backslash\mathbb{H}^{2}
is strictly larger than
1/4
. The eigenvalue
1/4
corresponds to
\lambda=0
, so the resulting invariant random lattice orbit of
\Gamma
in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
is stealthy, and in particular spectrally hyperuniform.

The lack of other examples of spectrally hyperuniform point processes and random measures in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is however quite severe. In an upcoming article we will prove that determinantal point processes in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
(and more generally in any commutative space associated with a non-amenable Gelfand pair) are not spectrally hyperuniform, in contrast to the Euclidean case where determinantal point processes associated to kernels which define orthogonal projections in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are always hyperuniform. Here we show that locally square-integrable i.i.d. perturbations of an invariant random lattice orbit in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
are not hyperuniform. We note the apparent contrast to the setting in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where i.i.d. perturbations of an invariant random lattice are always locally square-integrable and always preserves hyperuniformity, see
[
1
, Appendix B]
and Proposition
4.5
. The following Proposition is proved in Section
10
.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
\Gamma<\mathrm{SO}^{\circ}(1\mathchar 59\relax n)
be a lattice and
d\nu(g)=\beta(g)dg
for some positive measurable bi-
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-invariant function
\beta
with
\int\beta(g)dg=1
such that

\displaystyle\beta(g)\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-2(n-1+\varepsilon)d(g.o\mathchar 59\relax o)}

for all
g\in G
and some
C>0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon>0
. Then the invariant random lattice orbit of
\Gamma
in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with independent
\nu
-distributed perturbations is locally square-integrable and not spectrally hyperuniform.

Remark 1.8

(Heat kernel hyperuniformity in Euclidean and hyperbolic spaces)
.

In future work, we will prove that spectral hyperuniformity is equivalent to what we refer to as
heat kernel hyperuniformity
. Denote the heat kernel by
h_{\tau}(x)
,
\tau>0
, the fundamental solution of the heat operator
\partial_{\tau}-\Delta
in the respective geometries. In
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define
\mu
to be heat kernel hyperuniform if

\displaystyle\limsup_{\tau\rightarrow+\infty}\,\tau^{\frac{n}{2}}\,\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(Sh_{\tau})=0\,\mathchar 59\relax

and similarly in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, if

\displaystyle\limsup_{\tau\rightarrow+\infty}\,\tau^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{2}\tau}\,\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(Sh_{\tau})=0\,.

One perk of this formulation is that the smoothness of

allows us to extend the notion of hyperuniformity to invariant random

on these spaces as well, for which we expect that one can construct hyperuniform and stealthy examples. Moreover, by uniform estimates of the heat kernel due to Anker and Ostellari in
[
3
]
, one should be able to formulate heat kernel hyperunifomity for a large class of non-compact symmetric spaces.

2.
Random measures on homogeneous spaces

We introduce invariant locally square-integrable random measures and their autocorrelation measures. The constructions work for a large family of proper homogeneous metric spaces, but we will only apply them to Euclidean and hyperbolic spaces in the subsequent sections.

In Subsection
2.1
we set up the metric spaces over which we will consider random measures. We proceed by defining invariant locally square-integrable random measures, related objects and provide examples in Subsection
2.2
. In Subsection

2.1.
Homogeneous metric spaces

Let

be a proper non-compact metric space and fix a point

. Suppose that there is a closed and unimodular subgroup
G
of the isometry group

. We will assume that

We will frequently make use of the fact that there is a Borel measurable

2.2.
Random measures

Let
\mathscr{M}_{+}(X)

\displaystyle=|J_{\alpha}(t)+M_{\alpha}(t)||J_{\alpha}(t)-M_{\alpha}(t)|

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\frac{C_{\alpha}}{(1+t)^{1/2}}+\Big(\frac{2}{\pi t}\Big)^{1/2}\Big)\frac{\kappa_{\alpha}}{t^{3/2}}\leq\frac{(C_{\alpha}+1)\kappa_{\alpha}}{t^{2}}\quad\mbox{ if }\,t\geq 1\,\mathchar 59\relax

so that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}t^{\beta}|J_{\alpha}(t)^{2}-M_{\alpha}(t)^{2}|dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}t^{\beta}|J_{\alpha}(t)^{2}-M_{\alpha}(t)^{2}|dt+\int_{1}^{T}t^{\beta}|J_{\alpha}(t)^{2}-M_{\alpha}(t)^{2}|dt

\displaystyle\leq(C^{2}_{\alpha}+1)\int_{0}^{1}t^{\beta-1}dt+(C_{\alpha}+1)\kappa_{\alpha}\int_{1}^{T}t^{\beta-2}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{\alpha}^{2}+1}{\beta}+(C_{\alpha}+1)\kappa_{\alpha}\frac{T^{\beta-1}-1}{\beta-1}\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad T\geq 1\,.

Note that the right hand side remains finite in the limiting case of
\beta=1^{+}
. Putting everything together, we define

\displaystyle\delta_{T}=T^{-\beta}\int_{0}^{T}t^{\beta}J_{\alpha}(t)^{2}dt-\frac{1}{\pi\beta}\,\mathchar 59\relax

so that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}t^{\beta}J_{\alpha}(t)^{2}dt=\Big(\frac{1}{\pi\beta}+\delta_{T}\Big)T^{\beta}\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad T\geq 1

and

\displaystyle|\delta_{T}|

\displaystyle\leq T^{-\beta}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}t^{\beta}M_{\alpha}(t)^{2}dt-\frac{T^{\beta}}{\pi\beta}+\int_{0}^{T}t^{\beta}|J_{\alpha}(t)^{2}-M_{\alpha}(t)^{2}|dt\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\Big|\int_{0}^{1}s^{\beta-1}\cos(2(Ts-\phi_{\alpha}))ds\Big|+\frac{C_{\alpha}^{2}+1}{\beta T^{\beta}}+(C_{\alpha}+1)\kappa_{\alpha}\frac{T^{\beta-1}-1}{(\beta-1)T^{\beta}}\longrightarrow 0

as
T\rightarrow+\infty
. We also note that this limit is preserved in the limiting case
\beta=1^{+}
.
∎

4.
Euclidean diffraction measures

We define the diffraction measure of an invariant locally square-integrable random measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and recall a known upper bound for its asymptotic volume growth.

The autocorrelation measure
\eta_{\mu}
of an invariant locally square-integrable random measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
positive-definite
in the sense of measures, that is

\displaystyle\eta_{\mu}(f^{*}*f)\geq 0\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad\forall\,f\in\mathscr{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{bnd}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,.

The positive-definiteness of
\eta_{\mu}
is the crucial property that will allow us to define the diffraction measure of
\mu
. Recall that a Radon measure
\eta
is
tempered
if it extends to a continuous linear functional on the Schwartz space
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of smooth functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
with sub-polynomial decay, that is,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+\lVert x\rVert)^{\alpha}|\partial^{\beta}f(x)|<+\infty

for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
and all multi-indices
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n}
. The following classical result establishes the existence and uniqueness of a measure-theoretic Fourier transform of positive-definite measures.

Theorem 4.1

(Bochner-Schwartz)
.

Let
\eta
be a positive-definite signed Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then there is a unique positive tempered Radon measure
\widehat{\eta}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\eta(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widehat{f}(\xi)d\widehat{\eta}(\xi)

for all functions
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In particular,
\eta
is tempered.

We refer to
\widehat{\eta}
as the
Fourier transform
of
\eta
.

Definition 4.2

(Euclidean diffraction measures)
.

The
diffraction measure
of an invariant locally square-integrable random measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the unique Fourier transform
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}\in\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of the autocorrelation measure
\eta_{\mu}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In particular, the
\mu
-variance of linear statistics
Sf
with
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
can written in terms of the diffraction measure as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(Sf)=\eta_{\mu}(f^{*}*f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)\,.

The linear span of the functions
f^{*}*f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are enough to uniquely determine the autocorrelation measure
\eta_{\mu}
, so the above formula for the
\mu
-variance uniquely determines the diffraction measure
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}
. Moreover, one can make use of the mean ergodic theorem to see that the diffraction measure satisfies
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\{0\})=0
.

We now justify the extension of this formula to all functions
f\in\mathscr{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{bnd}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, in particular for indicator functions
\chi_{B_{r}}
with
r>0
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\mu
be an invariant locally square-integrable random measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f\in\mathscr{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{bnd}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then

\displaystyle\eta_{\mu}(f^{*}*f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)\,.

In particular,
\widehat{f}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\mathchar 59\relax\widehat{\eta}_{\mu})
.

Proof.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, let
\beta_{\varepsilon}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the function satisfying
\widehat{\beta}_{\varepsilon}(\xi)=\mathrm{e}^{-\varepsilon^{2}\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Setting
f_{\varepsilon}=\beta_{\varepsilon}*f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then a standard argument shows that
f_{\varepsilon}
converges to
f
Lebesgue-almost everywhere. Since
f
is measurable and bounded with bounded support, the convolution
f^{*}*f
defines a continuous compactly supported function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It follows that
f_{\varepsilon}^{*}*f_{\varepsilon}
converges
uniformly
to
f^{*}*f
. In particular, since
\eta_{\mu}
is a Radon measure then

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\eta_{\mu}(f_{\varepsilon}^{*}*f_{\varepsilon})=\eta_{\mu}(f^{*}*f)\,.

On the other hand, by definition of
\beta_{\varepsilon}
and the diffraction measure we have that

\displaystyle\eta_{\mu}(f_{\varepsilon}^{*}*f_{\varepsilon})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2\varepsilon^{2}\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)\,.

Since
\mathrm{e}^{-2\varepsilon^{2}\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}}
is monotonely increasing as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
with limit
1
then the monotone convergence Theorem tells us that

\displaystyle\eta_{\mu}(f^{*}*f)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2\varepsilon^{2}\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)\,.

∎

The above Lemma imposes a growth condition on the diffraction measure
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}
, which one can give a more specific description of.

Lemma 4.4

(
[
6
]
, Proposition 4.9, p.21)
.

Let
\mu
be a random measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the diffraction measure of
\mu
satisfies

\displaystyle\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(B_{L})\ll_{n\mathchar 59\relax\mu}\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(B_{L})

for all sufficiently large
L>0
.

Example 4.1

(Poisson diffraction)
.

In Example
2.4
we computed the variance of linear statistics for the unit intensity invariant Poisson point process, which in the Euclidean case reads as

\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{Poi}}(Sf)=(f^{*}*f)(0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{2}dx\,.

By the Plancherel formula,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{Poi}}(Sf)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{2}dx=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\,\mathchar 59\relax

so the diffraction measure of the Poisson process is
d\widehat{\eta}_{\mathrm{Poi}}(\xi)=(2\pi)^{-n}d\xi
.

Example 4.2

(Diffraction of a random lattice)
.

Let
\Gamma<\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a lattice. Then there is a matrix
g_{\Gamma}\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\Gamma=g_{\Gamma}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. The covolume of
\Gamma
can then be written as
\mathrm{covol}(\Gamma)=\det(g_{\Gamma})
and the
dual lattice of
\Gamma
is the lattice

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\perp}=\Big\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle\gamma\mathchar 59\relax\xi\rangle\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}\quad\forall\,\gamma\in\Gamma\Big\}\,.

The
Poisson summation formula
states that

\displaystyle\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(\gamma)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{covol}(\Gamma)}\sum_{\xi\in\Gamma^{\perp}}\widehat{f}(\xi)

for
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. By the formula for the autocorrelation measure in Example
2.5
and the Poisson summation formula,

\displaystyle\eta_{\Gamma}(f)=\frac{1}{\mathrm{covol}(\Gamma)}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(\gamma)-\frac{1}{\mathrm{covol}(\Gamma)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)dx=\frac{1}{\mathrm{covol}(\Gamma)^{2}}\sum_{\xi\in\Gamma^{\perp}\backslash\{0\}}\widehat{f}(\xi)

for all
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Thus the diffraction measure of the random lattice
\mu_{\Gamma}
is

\widehat{\eta}_{\Gamma}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{covol}(\Gamma)^{2}}\sum_{\xi\in\Gamma^{\perp}\backslash\{0\}}\delta_{\xi}\,.

Example 4.3

(Diffraction of a random perturbed lattice)
.

Since all lattices in Euclidean spaces are cocompact, random perturbed lattices are locally square-integrable by Lemma
2.6
with a well-defined diffraction measure.

If
\nu\in\mathrm{Prob}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we define its Fourier transform
\widehat{\nu}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
to be

\displaystyle\widehat{\nu}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\mathrm{e}^{-i\langle x\mathchar 59\relax\xi\rangle}d\nu(x)\,.

Then
\widehat{\nu}
is a continuous function, but does not necessarily vanish at infinity as in the absolutely continuous case. Moreover, one computes
\widehat{\check{\nu}*\nu}=|\widehat{\nu}|^{2}
. With this Fourier transform in mind and the formula for the autocorrelation of a random perturbed lattice
(\Gamma\mathchar 59\relax\nu)
from Example
2.6
,

\displaystyle\eta_{\Gamma\mathchar 59\relax\nu}=\check{\nu}*\eta_{\Gamma}*\nu+\iota_{\Gamma}(\delta_{0}-\check{\nu}*\nu)=\check{\nu}*\nu*\eta_{\Gamma}+\iota_{\Gamma}(\delta_{0}-\check{\nu}*\nu)

where
\iota_{\Gamma}=\mathrm{covol}(\Gamma)^{-1}
, the corresponding diffraction measure is

\displaystyle d\widehat{\eta}_{\Gamma\mathchar 59\relax\nu}(\xi)=|\widehat{\nu}(\xi)|^{2}d\widehat{\eta}_{\Gamma}(\xi)+\iota_{\Gamma}(1-|\widehat{\nu}(\xi)|^{2})\frac{d\xi}{(2\pi)^{n}}\,.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\Gamma<\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a lattice and
\nu\in\mathrm{Prob}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
a probability measure. Then the perturbed lattice point process
\mu_{\Gamma\mathchar 59\relax\nu}
is hyperuniform.

Proof.

By the formula for the diffraction measure we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\eta}_{\Gamma\mathchar 59\relax\nu}(B_{\varepsilon})=\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}|\widehat{\nu}(\xi)|^{2}d\widehat{\eta}_{\Gamma}(\xi)+\frac{\iota_{\Gamma}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}(1-|\widehat{\nu}(\xi)|^{2})d\xi\,.

Note that
\widehat{\nu}(0)=\nu(\mathbb{R}^{n})=1
, so
1-|\widehat{\nu}(\xi)|^{2}=o(1)
as
\xi\rightarrow 0
. Moreover, since
\mu_{\Gamma}
is stealthy, then
\widehat{\eta}_{\Gamma}|_{B_{\varepsilon}}=0
for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
. Finally, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
we then have that

\displaystyle\widehat{\eta}_{\Gamma\mathchar 59\relax\nu}(B_{\varepsilon})=\frac{\iota_{\Gamma}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}}(1-|\widehat{\nu}(\xi)|^{2})d\xi=\frac{\iota_{\Gamma}}{(2\pi)^{n}}o(\varepsilon^{n})\,\mathchar 59\relax

so
\mu_{\Gamma\mathchar 59\relax\nu}
is spectrally hyperuniform and hence hyperuniform.
∎

5.
Beck’s Theorem

We will in this section investigate lower bounds for number variance
\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)
as
R\rightarrow+\infty
for invariant locally square-integrable random measures
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In Subsection
5.1
we refine and prove Theorem
1.1
, and in Subsections
5.2
and
5.3
we arrive at a proof of Equation
1.3
. Finally, we formulate the contents of Subsection
5.1
for isotropic random measures in terms of the associated powder diffraction measure in Subsection
5.4
.

5.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we will prove a lower bound on the average of the number variance that is similar to the inequality that Beck and Chen prove in
[
5
, p.4]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mu
be an invariant locally square-integrable random measure on Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then there is a constant
C=C(n)>0
such that for every
R_{o}>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)dr\geq C\Big(\int_{\lVert\xi\rVert\geq R_{o}^{-1}}\frac{d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)}{\lVert\xi\rVert^{n+1}}\Big)R^{n-1}\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad\forall\,R\geq R_{o}\,.

Proof.

From Lemma
3.1
we have that

\displaystyle\widehat{\chi}_{B_{r}}(\xi)=(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\Big(\frac{r}{\lVert\xi\rVert}\Big)^{\frac{n}{2}}J_{\frac{n}{2}}(r\lVert\xi\rVert)\,.

By Lemma
3.4
with
\alpha=n/2
and
\beta=n
, there is for every
T\geq 1
a
\delta_{T}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\delta_{T}\rightarrow 0
as
T\rightarrow+\infty
such that

(5.1)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}|\widehat{\chi}_{B_{r}}(\xi)|^{2}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi)^{n}}{\lVert\xi\rVert^{n}}\int_{0}^{R}r^{n}J_{\frac{n}{2}}(r\lVert\xi\rVert)^{2}dr=\frac{b_{n}^{2}}{\lVert\xi\rVert^{2n+1}}\int_{0}^{R\lVert\xi\rVert}t^{n}J_{\frac{n}{2}}(t)^{2}dt

(5.2)

\displaystyle=\frac{(2\pi)^{n}}{\lVert\xi\rVert^{n+1}}\Big(\frac{1}{\pi n}+\delta_{R\lVert\xi\rVert}\Big)R^{n}>0

for every
R\geq R_{o}
and every
\lVert\xi\rVert\geq R_{o}^{-1}
. Define

\displaystyle C=(2\pi)^{n}\inf_{T\geq 1}\Big(\frac{1}{\pi n}+\delta_{T}\Big)=(2\pi)^{n}\inf_{T\geq 1}T^{-n}\int_{0}^{T}t^{n}J_{\frac{n}{2}}(t)^{2}dt\,\mathchar 59\relax

which is positive since

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}t^{n}J_{\frac{n}{2}}(t)^{2}dt>0\quad\forall\,T\geq 1\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\lim_{T\rightarrow+\infty}T^{-n}\int_{0}^{T}t^{n}J_{\frac{n}{2}}(t)^{2}dt=\frac{1}{\pi n}>0

by Lemma
3.4
. An application of Fubini provides the lower bound

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big(\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}|\widehat{\chi}_{B_{r}}(\xi)|^{2}dr\Big)d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\lVert\xi\rVert\geq R_{o}^{-1}}\Big(\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}|\widehat{\chi}_{B_{r}}(\xi)|^{2}dr\Big)d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\lambda)\geq R^{n-1}C\int_{\lVert\xi\rVert\geq R_{o}^{-1}}\frac{d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)}{\lVert\xi\rVert^{n+1}}

as desired.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Since
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}
satisfies
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(B_{L})\ll_{n\mathord{\mathchar 59\relax}\mu}L^{n}
for sufficiently large
L>0
by Lemma
4.4
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\lVert\xi\rVert\geq R_{o}^{-1}}\frac{d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)}{\lVert\xi\rVert^{n+1}}\ll_{n}\int_{R_{o}^{-1}}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(B_{u})}{u^{n+2}}du\ll_{n\mathchar 59\relax\mu\mathchar 59\relax R_{o}}\int_{R^{-1}_{o}}^{\infty}\frac{du}{u^{2}}=R_{o}<+\infty\,.

Thus the right hand side in Theorem
5.1
is indeed finite.

Theorem
1.1
now follows form the fact that

\displaystyle\limsup_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(R)}{R^{n-1}}\geq\limsup_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{R^{n}}\int_{0}^{R}\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)dr\geq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)}{\lVert\xi\rVert^{n+1}}>0\,\mathchar 59\relax

which may or may not be infinite.

Before moving on we emphasize that if the diffraction measure vanishes in a neighbourhood around
\xi=0
, then the limit as
R\rightarrow+\infty
in Theorem
5.1
can be normalized and computed.

Definition 5.3

(Euclidean stealthy random measures)
.

An invariant locally square-integrable random measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
stealthy
if there is a
\lambda_{o}>0
such that
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(B_{\lambda_{o}})=0
.

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
\mu
be a stealthy random measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)}{r^{n-1}}dr=2^{n}\pi^{n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)}{\lVert\xi\rVert^{n+1}}\,.

Proof.

Let
\lambda_{o}>0
such that
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(B_{\lambda_{o}})=0
. Similarly to the proof of Theorem
5.1
we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)}{r^{n-1}}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{\lVert\xi\rVert\geq\lambda_{o}}\Big(\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{|\widehat{\chi}_{B_{r}}(\xi)|^{2}}{r^{n-1}}dr\Big)d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n}\int_{\lVert\xi\rVert\geq\lambda_{o}}\Big(\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}rJ_{\frac{n}{2}}(r\lVert\xi\rVert)^{2}dr\Big)\frac{d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)}{\lVert\xi\rVert^{n}}\,.

If
R\geq\lambda_{o}^{-1}
, then Lemma
3.4
with
\alpha=n/2
and
\beta=1
gives us

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}rJ_{\frac{n}{2}}(r\lVert\xi\rVert)^{2}dr=\frac{1}{R\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}}\int_{0}^{R\lVert\xi\rVert}tJ_{\frac{n}{2}}(t)^{2}dt\longrightarrow\frac{1}{\lVert\xi\rVert\pi}

as
R\rightarrow+\infty
. By Remark
5.2
we know that
\int_{\lVert\xi\rVert\geq\lambda_{o}}\lVert\xi\rVert^{-(n+1)}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)
is finite, so by dominated convergence we see that

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}(2\pi)^{n}\int_{\lVert\xi\rVert\geq\lambda_{o}}\Big(\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}rJ_{\frac{n}{2}}(r\lVert\xi\rVert)^{2}dr\Big)\frac{d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)}{\lVert\xi\rVert^{n}}=2^{n}\pi^{n-1}\int_{\lVert\xi\rVert\geq\lambda_{o}}\frac{d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\xi)}{\lVert\xi\rVert^{n+1}}\,.

∎

5.2.
Dynamics for the invariant random
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-lattice

We consider the invariant random
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-lattice and compute its diffraction measure. We also provide dynamical results on square roots of natural numbers that will be a key ingredient in proving the lower limit

\displaystyle\liminf_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{5}}}(R)}{R^{4}}=0\,.

5.2.1.
Number variance of the invariant random
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-lattice

Consider the standard lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{n}<\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the random lattice
\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
. Its dual lattice is
(\mathbb{Z}^{n})^{\perp}=2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, so by Example
4.2
, the diffraction measure
\widehat{\eta}_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
of the random lattice
\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
is

\displaystyle\widehat{\eta}_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(\widehat{f})=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}\widehat{f}(2\pi\gamma)

for any
f\in\mathscr{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{bnd}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. In particular,
\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
is stealthy. The number variance of the linear statistic
S\chi_{B_{R}}
can then be written using Example
3.1
as

(5.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}}(R)}{R^{n-1}}=\frac{1}{R^{n-1}}\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}|\widehat{\chi}_{B_{R}}(2\pi\gamma)|^{2}=(2\pi)^{n}R\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{r_{n}(\ell)}{\ell^{n/2}}J_{\frac{n}{2}}(2\pi R\sqrt{\ell})^{2}

where
r_{n}(\ell)=\#\{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:\lVert\gamma\rVert^{2}=\ell\}
is the sum-of-squares function. By Corollary
5.4
,

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}}(r)}{r^{n-1}}dr=2^{n}\pi^{n-1}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{r_{n}(\ell)}{\ell^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}\,.

Moreover, Remark
5.2
implies that the right hand side is finite. We now show the slightly stronger statement that the map
R\mapsto R^{-(n-1)}\mathrm{NV}_{\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}}(R)
is uniformly bounded in
R\geq 0
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
n\geq 1
. Then the Dirichlet series

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}(s;r_{n})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{r_{n}(\ell)}{\ell^{s}}

is absolutely convergent whenever
\mathrm{Re}(s)>\frac{n}{2}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
s
is real. Rewriting
\mathfrak{D}(s\mathchar 59\relax r_{n})
as a series over the integer lattice and partitioning into unit annuli, we get

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}(s;r_{n})=\sum_{\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{1}{\lVert\gamma\rVert^{2s}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{k\leq\lVert\gamma\rVert<k+1}\frac{1}{\lVert\gamma\rVert^{2s}}\Big)\,.

An upper bound is then

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}(s;r_{n})\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\#(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap B_{k+1}\backslash B_{k})}{k^{2s}}\,.

The number of lattice points in a ball can be bounded from above and below using a standard box covering argument,

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+1)}(k-\sqrt{n})^{n}\leq\#(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap B_{k})\leq\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+1)}(k+\sqrt{n})^{n}

for all sufficiently large
k\geq 1
. Thus the number of lattice points in the annulus
B_{k+1}\backslash B_{k}
is bounded as

\displaystyle\#(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap B_{k+1}\backslash B_{k})

\displaystyle=\#(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap B_{k+1})-\#(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap B_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi^{n/2}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+1)}\Big((k+1+\sqrt{n})^{n}-(k-\sqrt{n})^{n}\Big)\ll_{n}k^{n-1}

for all sufficiently large
k\geq 1
, and we extend this to a uniform bound
\#(\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap B_{k+1}\backslash B_{k})\ll_{n}k^{n-1}
for all
k\geq 1
. This means that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}(s;r_{n})\ll_{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2s-(n-1)}}\,.

The latter series is convergent if and only if
s>\frac{n}{2}
.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

The number variance of the invariant random
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-lattice satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{NV}_{\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}}(R)\ll_{n}R^{n-1}

for all
R\geq 0
.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.3
, there is a constant
C_{n/2}>0
such that
J_{\frac{n}{2}}(t)\leq C_{n/2}(1+t)^{-1/2}
for all
t\geq 0
. Thus

\displaystyle\mathrm{NV}_{\mu_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}}(R)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n}R^{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{r_{n}(\ell)}{\ell^{n/2}}J_{\frac{n}{2}}(2\pi R\sqrt{\ell})^{2}

By Lemma

, the right hand side is convergent and so we’re done.
∎

5.2.2.
Rational independence of square roots and ergodicity

The support of the diffraction measure
\widehat{\eta}_{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}
is
\{2\pi\xi:\xi\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and in light of Example

we will show that one can scale norms
\lVert\xi_{1}\rVert\mathchar 59\relax...\mathchar 59\relax\lVert\xi_{N}\rVert
for any finite collection
\xi_{1}\mathchar 59\relax...\mathchar 59\relax\xi_{N}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
such that they are simultaneously close to the integers. We will make crucial use of the following is a classical result due to Besicovitch.

Lemma 5.7

(
[
7
]
, Theorem 2, p.4)
.

Let
\ell_{1}\mathchar 59\relax...\mathchar 59\relax\ell_{J}
be distinct positive square-free integers. Then the roots
\sqrt{\ell_{1}}\mathchar 59\relax...\mathchar 59\relax\sqrt{\ell_{J}}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
.

A simultaneous consideration of the fractional parts of such square roots give rise to the following uniquely ergodic dynamical system.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
\alpha_{1}\mathchar 59\relax...\mathchar 59\relax\alpha_{J}\in\mathbb{R}
be linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then the translation action

\displaystyle v+\mathbb{Z}^{J}\longmapsto(v+\alpha)+\mathbb{Z}^{J}\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad\alpha=(\alpha_{1}\mathchar 59\relax...\mathchar 59\relax\alpha_{J})\in\mathbb{R}^{J}

on the torus
\mathbb{R}^{J}/\mathbb{Z}^{J}
is uniquely ergodic with the Lebesgue measure as the invariant measure. In particular, every orbit of the action is dense.

Proof.

Suppose that
\theta
is an invariant probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{J}/\mathbb{Z}^{J}
with respect to the prescribed action. It suffices to show that
\theta
is the Lebesgue measure. By invariance, the Fourier transform
\widehat{\theta}:\mathbb{Z}^{J}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
of
\theta
satisfies

\displaystyle\widehat{\theta}(\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{J}/\mathbb{Z}^{J}}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi i\langle\gamma\mathchar 59\relax x\rangle}d\theta(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{J}/\mathbb{Z}^{J}}\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi i\langle\gamma\mathchar 59\relax x+\alpha\rangle}d\theta(x)=\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi i\langle\gamma\mathchar 59\relax\alpha\rangle}\widehat{\theta}(\gamma)

for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}^{J}
. This forces
\widehat{\theta}(\gamma)=0
for all
\gamma\neq 0
, since otherwise there would be a non-zero
\gamma
for which
\langle\gamma\mathchar 59\relax\alpha\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}
, contradicting the assumed
\mathbb{Q}
-linear independence of
\alpha_{1}\mathchar 59\relax...\mathchar 59\relax\alpha_{J}
. Thus
\widehat{\theta}
is the unit mass on
0\in\mathbb{Z}^{J}
and hence
\theta
is the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{J}/\mathbb{Z}^{J}
by injectivity of the Fourier transform.
∎

Let

5.4.
Isotropic random measures on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

Given a random measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, one can require invariance under other subgroups of the Euclidean isometries rather than just translations. The special Euclidean group
G_{n}=K_{n}\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
K_{n}=\mathrm{SO}(n)
is the group of rotations and translations, and contains the subgroup of translations considered in the previous subsections. Random measures that are invariant under

are commonly referred to as

Given a translation invariant random measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

as the push-forward of
\mu

-averaging map

\mathrm{Av}_{K_{n}}(p)(B)=\int_{K_{n}}p(k.B)dm_{K_{n}}(k)

for every bounded Borel set

, where

is the Haar probability measure on
K_{n}
. Note however that this specific procedure does not produce isotropic point processes from translation invariant point processes.

If
\mu
is an isotropic and locally square-integrable random measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}

\eta_{\mu}
is a bi-

-invariant measure on

of radial functions. On this subalgebra the Fourier transform restricts to what we call the

. Given

, its Hankel transform is the function

given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}(x)dx\,\mathchar 59\relax

where

are the
\mathrm{SO}(n)
-spherical functions from Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle\omega^{(n)}_{\lambda}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{(\lambda\lVert x\rVert)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}}J_{\frac{n-2}{2}}(\lambda\lVert x\rVert)\,.

By construction,
\widehat{f}(\xi)=\widetilde{f}(\lVert\xi\rVert)
for all

and dualizing this to measures, we are able to define the Hankel transform of a positive-definite Radon measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 5.11

By the existence and uniqueness of the diffraction measure in Theorem

for all

. With this notation, the lower bound in Theorem

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)dr\geq C\Big(\int_{R_{o}^{-1}}^{\infty}\frac{d\widetilde{\eta}_{\mu}(\lambda)}{\lambda^{n+1}}\Big)R^{n-1}\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad\forall\,R\geq R_{o}

6.
Spherical harmonic analysis on real hyperbolic spaces

6.1.
Cartan decomposition and invariant reference measures

Consider the bilinear form

6.1.1.

A polar decomposition for

is available via the length map

\displaystyle A_{n}=\Big\{a_{t}=\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}c|c|c@{}}\cosh(t)&0&\sinh(t)\\

\hline\cr\sinh(t)&0&\cosh(t)\end{array}\right):t\in\mathbb{R}\Big\}<G_{n}

Note that if

6.1.4.
The algebra of bi-

As in the case of isotropic random measures on Euclidean spaces we will restrict our attention to linear statistics and autocorrelation measures on the subspace of radial functions in

Lemma 6.1

6.2.

A

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda}(g)=\int_{K_{n}}\xi_{\lambda\mathchar 59\relax u}(k^{-1}g.o)dm_{K_{n}}(k)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{2\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}[g.o\mathchar 59\relax(1\mathchar 59\relax v)]^{-i\lambda-\frac{n-1}{2}}d\sigma_{n-1}(v)

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}(z\mathchar 59\relax w)=\frac{\Gamma(z)\Gamma(w)}{\Gamma(z+w)}=\int_{0}^{1}s^{z-1}(1-s)^{w-1}ds\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad z\mathchar 59\relax w\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\mathbb{Z}_{\leq 0}\,.

The spherical function

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}(g)=a_{n}\sinh(\ell(g))^{2-n}\int_{0}^{\ell(g)}(\cosh(\ell(g))-\cosh(s))^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\cos(\lambda s)ds\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad a_{n}=\frac{2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{\mathrm{B}(\frac{n-1}{2}\mathchar 59\relax\frac{1}{2})}\,.

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})\sinh(\ell(g))}\int_{-\ell(g)}^{\ell(g)}\mathrm{e}^{-(i\lambda+\frac{n-3}{2})s}\Big(1-\Big(\frac{\cosh(\ell(g))-\mathrm{e}^{s}}{\sinh(\ell(g))}\Big)^{2}\Big)^{\frac{n-3}{2}}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})\sinh(\ell(g))^{n-2}}\int_{-\ell(g)}^{\ell(g)}\mathrm{e}^{-i\lambda s}\Big(\mathrm{e}^{-s}\big(\sinh^{2}(\ell(g))-(\cosh(\ell(g))-\mathrm{e}^{s})^{2}\big)\Big)^{\frac{n-3}{2}}ds\,.

Note that

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-s}\big(\sinh^{2}(\ell(g))-(\cosh(\ell(g))-\mathrm{e}^{s})^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-s}\big(\sinh^{2}(\ell(g))-\cosh^{2}(\ell(g))+2\cosh(\ell(g))\mathrm{e}^{s}-\mathrm{e}^{2s}\big)

\displaystyle=2(\cosh(\ell(g))-\cosh(s))\,\mathchar 59\relax

so the spherical function is

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}(g)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})\sinh(\ell(g))^{n-2}}\int_{-\ell(g)}^{\ell(g)}(\cosh(\ell(g))-\cosh(s))^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{i\lambda s}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})\sinh(\ell(g))^{n-2}}\int_{0}^{\ell(g)}(\cosh(\ell(g))-\cosh(s))^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\cos(\lambda s)ds\,.

∎

The following result is a recollection of some standard useful properties of the spherical functions.

Lemma 6.3
.

The spherical functions
\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}
,
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, satisfy the following:

(1)

\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}(e)=1
and
\omega_{-\lambda}^{(n)}=\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
.

(2)

\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}
is bounded if and only if
|\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)|\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
. Moreover, if
|\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)|<\frac{n-1}{2}
we have that

|\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}(g)|<1

for all
g\neq e
in
G_{n}
.

(3)

If
g\neq e
and
\lambda\geq 0
then
|\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}(g)|\leq\omega_{0}^{(n)}(g)
.

Proof.

For (1) we have that

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}(e)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{2\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}[o\mathchar 59\relax(1\mathchar 59\relax v)]^{-i\lambda-\frac{n-1}{2}}d\sigma_{n-1}(v)=1\,\mathchar 59\relax

and by Lemma
6.2
it is easy to see that
\omega_{-\lambda}^{(n)}=\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}
for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
using that
\cos(-\lambda s)=\cos(\lambda s)
.

For (2), the first claim is obvious and for the second one we first assume that
|\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)|\leq\frac{n-1}{2}
and use Lemma

to find that

\displaystyle|\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}(g)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})\sinh(\ell(g))^{n-2}}\int_{0}^{\ell(g)}(\cosh(\ell(g))-\cosh(s))^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\cosh(|\mathrm{Im}(\lambda)|s)ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\pi^{\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})\sinh(\ell(g))^{n-2}}\int_{0}^{\ell(g)}(\cosh(\ell(g))-\cosh(s))^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\cosh\Big(\frac{n-1}{2}s\Big)ds

\displaystyle=\omega_{i\frac{n-1}{2}}^{(n)}(g)=1

for all

. Moreover, it is not hard to see that the last inequality is strict if

and

(3) follows from a similar bound as in the proof of (2), using that

for all

∎

6.3.
A trigonometric expansion of spherical functions

The following Proposition can be viewed as a alternate formulation of the classical asymptotic expansions of spherical functions due to Harish-Chandra, see
[

, Theorem 5.5, p.430]
. For us however, we are in need of an expansion along the line

, which differs slightly from the referenced statement. We emphasize that our proof will rely entirely on elementary arguments.

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
\lambda>0
. Then there is an
r_{o}>0
and bounded continuous functions

\alpha_{n}(\cdot\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\mathchar 59\relax\beta_{n}(\cdot\mathchar 59\relax\lambda):[r_{o}\mathchar 59\relax+\infty)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

such that

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}(a_{r})=a_{n}\sinh(r)^{-\frac{n-1}{2}}\big(\alpha_{n}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\cos(\lambda r)+\beta_{n}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\sin(\lambda r)\big)\,\mathchar 59\relax

with

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\alpha_{n}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)=\mathrm{Re}\Big(\mathrm{B}\Big(i\lambda\mathchar 59\relax\frac{n-1}{2}\Big)\Big)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\beta_{n}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)=\mathrm{Im}\Big(\mathrm{B}\Big(i\lambda\mathchar 59\relax\frac{n-1}{2}\Big)\Big)\,.

6.3.1.
Some identities for the Beta function

For the proof of Proposition
6.4
in even dimensions, it will be useful to consider the the power series expansion

(6.2)

\displaystyle(1-x)^{-1/2}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}E_{\ell}x^{\ell}\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad E_{\ell}=\frac{(2\ell)!}{4^{\ell}(\ell!)^{2}}

for
0\leq x<1
. Using Stirling approximation, one gets the asymptotic
E_{\ell}\sim\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi\ell}}
as
\ell\rightarrow+\infty
.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0\}
such that
\mathrm{Re}(z)\geq 0
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}(z\mathchar 59\relax m+1)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k+z}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathrm{B}(z\mathchar 59\relax m+1/2)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}E_{\ell}\,\mathrm{B}(z+\ell\mathchar 59\relax m+1)\,.

Proof.

The first identity follows from binomial expansion,

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}(z\mathchar 59\relax m+1)=\int_{0}^{1}x^{z-1}(1-x)^{m}dx=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(-1)^{k}\int_{0}^{1}x^{z+k-1}dx=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k+z}\,.

For the second identity, we first note that

\displaystyle|\mathrm{B}(z+\ell\mathchar 59\relax m+1)|\leq\mathrm{B}(\mathrm{Re}(z)+\ell\mathchar 59\relax m+1)\leq\mathrm{B}(\ell\mathchar 59\relax m+1)=\frac{m!(\ell-1)!}{(\ell+m)!}\,\mathchar 59\relax

so

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}E_{\ell}|\mathrm{B}(z+\ell\mathchar 59\relax m+1)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}E_{\ell}\mathrm{B}(\ell\mathchar 59\relax m+1)

\displaystyle=m!\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}E_{\ell}\frac{(\ell-1)!}{(\ell+m)!}\leq m!\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{E_{\ell}}{\ell^{m+1}}<+\infty\,.

By dominated convergence, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}E_{\ell}\,\mathrm{B}(z+\ell\mathchar 59\relax m+1)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}x^{z-1}\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}E_{\ell}x^{\ell}\Big)(1-x)^{m}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}x^{z-1}\big((1-x)^{-1/2}-1\big)(1-x)^{m}dx

\displaystyle=\mathrm{B}(z\mathchar 59\relax m+1/2)-\mathrm{B}(z\mathchar 59\relax m+1)\,.

Rearranging this, we finally have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{B}(z\mathchar 59\relax m+1/2)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}E_{\ell}\,\mathrm{B}(z+\ell\mathchar 59\relax m+1)\,.

∎

6.3.2.
Some formulas for intermediate coefficients

Lemma 6.6
.

For every
q\in\mathbb{N}
and all
r\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\cosh(s)^{q}ds\leq 2^{q+\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{2\cosh(r)^{q}}{q}\,.

Proof.

Setting
u=\cosh(s)
we split our integral as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\cosh(s)^{q}ds=\int_{1}^{\cosh(r)}\frac{u^{q}}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}du=\int_{1}^{2}\frac{u^{q}}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}du+\int_{2}^{\cosh(r)}\frac{u^{q}}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}du\,.

For the first integral we make the estimate

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{2}\frac{u^{q}}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}du

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{2}\frac{u^{q}}{\sqrt{(u+1)(u-1)}}du\leq 2^{q-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{1}^{2}\frac{du}{\sqrt{u-1}}=2^{q+\frac{1}{2}}\,.

For the second integral, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{2}^{\cosh(r)}\frac{u^{q}}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}du

\displaystyle=\int_{2}^{\cosh(r)}\frac{u^{q}}{\sqrt{(u+1)(u-1)}}du\leq\int_{2}^{\cosh(r)}\frac{u^{q}}{u-1}du

\displaystyle=\int_{2}^{\cosh(r)}\Big(1+\frac{1}{u-1}\Big)u^{q-1}du\leq 2\int_{2}^{\cosh(r)}u^{q-1}du\leq\frac{2\cosh(r)^{q}}{q}\,.

Gathering the two estimates we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\cosh(s)^{q}ds=\int_{1}^{2}\frac{u^{q}}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}du+\int_{2}^{\cosh(r)}\frac{u^{q}}{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}du\leq 2^{q+\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{2\cosh(r)^{q}}{q}\,.

∎

Lemma 6.7
.

For every
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
r\geq 0
,
\lambda>0
,

(6.3)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\cosh(s)^{k}\cos(\lambda s)ds=a_{k}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\cos(\lambda r)+b_{k}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\sin(\lambda r)\,\mathchar 59\relax

where

\displaystyle a_{k}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)=\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{(2j-k)\sinh((2j-k)r)}{(2j-k)^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\,\mathchar 59\relax\,\,b_{k}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)=\frac{\lambda}{2^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{\cosh((2j-k)r)}{(2j-k)^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\,.

Moreover, if

\displaystyle a_{k}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\leq\int_{0}^{r}\cosh(s)^{k}ds\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad b_{k}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}+2\lambda\int_{0}^{r}\cosh(s)^{k}ds\,\mathchar 59\relax

and

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{a_{k}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)}{\cosh(r)^{k}}=\frac{k}{k^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{b_{k}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)}{\cosh(r)^{k}}=\frac{\lambda}{k^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\,.

A binomial expansion of

yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\cosh(s)^{k}\cos(\lambda s)ds=\frac{1}{2}\int_{-r}^{r}\cosh(s)^{k}\mathrm{e}^{i\lambda s}ds=\frac{1}{2^{k+1}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\int_{-r}^{r}\mathrm{e}^{(2j-k+i\lambda)s}ds\,.

The latter integral is

\displaystyle\int_{-r}^{r}\mathrm{e}^{(2j-k+i\lambda)s}ds

\displaystyle=2\,\mathrm{Re}\Big(\frac{\sinh((2j-k+i\lambda)r)}{2j-k+i\lambda}\Big)

\displaystyle=2\,\frac{(2j-k)\sinh((2j-k)r)\cos(\lambda r)+\lambda\cosh((2k-j)r)\sin(\lambda r)}{(2j-k)^{2}+\lambda^{2}}

which means that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\cosh(s)^{k}\cos(\lambda s)ds=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{(2j-k)\sinh((2j-k)r)}{(2j-k)^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\Big)\cos(\lambda r)

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{\cosh((2j-k)r)}{(2j-k)^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\Big)\sin(\lambda r)

as desired.

For the upper bound of

, we have

\displaystyle a_{k}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{(2j-k)\sinh((2j-k)r)}{(2j-k)^{2}+\lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{(2j-k)^{2}}{(2j-k)^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\frac{\sinh((2j-k)r)}{2j-k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{\sinh((2j-k)r)}{2j-k}=\int_{0}^{r}\cosh(s)^{k}ds\,.

For

, we isolate the worst case of

by

\displaystyle b_{k}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda}{2^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{\cosh((2j-k)r)}{(2j-k)^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{\lambda}{2^{k}}\sum_{2j-k\neq 0}\binom{k}{j}\frac{\cosh((2j-k)r)}{(2j-k)^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\,.

In the above inequality, we used that
\binom{k}{j}\leq 2^{k}
for all
j=0\mathchar 59\relax...\mathchar 59\relax k
when isolating the term corresponding to
2j-k=0
. Next we observe that

\frac{1}{2|2j-k|}\leq\frac{1}{2}\leq\tanh(r)\leq\tanh(|2j-k|r)

for all

such that

and all

, so that

\displaystyle\cosh((2j-k)r)\leq 2(2j-k)\sinh((2j-k)r)

for the same

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{k}}\sum_{2j-k\neq 0}\binom{k}{j}\frac{\cosh((2j-k)r)}{(2j-k)^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\leq\frac{2}{2^{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}\frac{(2j-k)\sinh((2j-k)r)}{(2j-k)^{2}+\lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 2a_{k}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\leq 2\int_{0}^{r}\cosh(s)^{k}ds\,\mathchar 59\relax

and so

\displaystyle b_{k}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}+\frac{\lambda}{2^{k}}\sum_{2j-k\neq 0}\binom{k}{j}\frac{\cosh((2j-k)r)}{(2j-k)^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}+2\lambda\int_{0}^{r}\cosh(s)^{k}ds

Lemma 6.8
.

Suppose that

,

. Then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\Big(\frac{\cosh(s)}{\cosh(r)}\Big)^{\ell}\Big(1-\frac{\cosh(s)}{\cosh(r)}\Big)^{m-1}\cos(\lambda s)ds=A_{m\mathchar 59\relax\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\cos(\lambda r)+B_{m\mathchar 59\relax\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\sin(\lambda r)\,\mathchar 59\relax

\displaystyle|A_{m\mathchar 59\relax\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)|\leq\frac{2^{m+1}}{\ell}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad|B_{m\mathchar 59\relax\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)|\leq\big(\frac{1}{\lambda}+8\lambda\big)\frac{2^{m-1}}{\ell}

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}A_{m\mathchar 59\relax\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\binom{m-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}(k+\ell)}{(k+\ell)^{2}+\lambda^{2}}=\mathrm{Re}(\mathrm{B}(i\lambda\mathchar 59\relax m))\,\mathchar 59\relax

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}B_{m\mathchar 59\relax\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)

Proof of Lemma
6.8
.

Using the binomial Theorem we can write

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{r}\Big(\frac{\cosh(s)}{\cosh(r)}\Big)^{\ell}

\displaystyle\Big(1-\frac{\cosh(s)}{\cosh(r)}\Big)^{m-1}\cos(\lambda s)ds=

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\binom{m-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\cosh(r)^{k+\ell}}\int_{0}^{r}\cosh(s)^{k+\ell}\cos(\lambda s)ds\,\mathchar 59\relax

which by Lemma

is

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}

\displaystyle\binom{m-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{\cosh(r)^{k+\ell}}(a_{k+\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\cos(\lambda r)+b_{k+\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\sin(\lambda r))=

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}

\displaystyle\binom{m-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}a_{k+\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)}{\cosh(r)^{k+\ell}}\Big)\cos(\lambda r)+\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\binom{m-1}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}b_{k+\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)}{\cosh(r)^{k+\ell}}\Big)\sin(\lambda r)\,.

\displaystyle|B_{m\mathchar 59\relax\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)|

Corollary 6.9

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}E_{\ell}\int_{0}^{r}\Big(\frac{\cosh(s)}{\cosh(r)}\Big)^{\ell}

\displaystyle\Big(1-\frac{\cosh(s)}{\cosh(r)}\Big)^{m-1}\cos(\lambda s)ds=

\displaystyle=\Big(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}E_{\ell}A_{m\mathchar 59\relax\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\Big)\cos(\lambda r)+\Big(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}E_{\ell}B_{m\mathchar 59\relax\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\Big)\sin(\lambda r)

is absolutely convergent. We may then apply Fubini’s Theorem to write the spherical function as

\displaystyle\omega^{(2m)}_{\lambda}(a_{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{2m}}{\sinh(r)^{m-\frac{1}{2}}}\coth(r)^{m-\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}E_{\ell}\int_{0}^{r}\Big(\frac{\cosh(s)}{\cosh(r)}\Big)^{\ell}\Big(1-\frac{\cosh(s)}{\cosh(r)}\Big)^{m-1}\cos(\lambda s)ds

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{2m}}{\sinh(r)^{m-\frac{1}{2}}}\coth(r)^{m-\frac{3}{2}}\Big(\Big(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}E_{\ell}A_{m\mathchar 59\relax\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\Big)\cos(\lambda r)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\Big(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}E_{\ell}B_{m\mathchar 59\relax\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\Big)\sin(\lambda r)\Big)\,.

Thus we are inclined to define

\displaystyle\alpha_{2m}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)=\coth(r)^{m-\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}E_{\ell}A_{m\mathchar 59\relax\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\beta_{2m}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)=\coth(r)^{m-\frac{3}{2}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}E_{\ell}B_{m\mathchar 59\relax\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)\,.

Since these series are absolutely convergent for all
r>\mathrm{arccosh}(2)
by Corollary
6.9
, we can compute their term-wise limit and use Lemma
6.5
to see that

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\alpha_{2m}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)=\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}E_{\ell}A_{m\mathchar 59\relax\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}E_{\ell}\mathrm{Re}(\mathrm{B}(i\lambda+\ell\mathchar 59\relax m))=\mathrm{Re}(\mathrm{B}(i\lambda\mathchar 59\relax m-1/2))

and

\displaystyle\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\beta_{2m}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)=\lim_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}E_{\ell}B_{m\mathchar 59\relax\ell}(r\mathchar 59\relax\lambda)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}E_{\ell}\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{B}(i\lambda+\ell\mathchar 59\relax m))=\mathrm{Im}(\mathrm{B}(i\lambda\mathchar 59\relax m-1/2))\,.

This concludes the proof of Proposition
6.4
.

Remark 6.10
.

The spherical functions
\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}
on real hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n}
are special cases of
Jacobi functions
.
For real rank one symmetric spaces, the spherical functions are essentially all Jacobi functions, admitting integral formulas similar to that of Lemma
6.2
, see
[
4
, Eq. 5.10, p.40]
. The explicit integral formulas for
n
-dimensional hyperbolic space over the real, complex and quarternionic field respectively are given by

\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}(g)=\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})^{-1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|[a_{\ell(g)}.o\mathchar 59\relax(1\mathchar 59\relax v)]|^{-i\lambda-\rho}d\sigma(v)

for
t\geq 0
, where
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is the unit sphere in the hyperbolic space with canonical surface measure
\sigma
and
\rho
is the half sum of positive roots with respect to a fixed root system. For the octonionic case,

and one must make some modifications for the integral formula, but we do not get into any details about this here. We expect that the proof of Proposition

can for the complex and quarternionic cases be carried out in a similar fashion for such real rank one symmetric spaces.

6.4.
Bounds and asymptotics of spherical functions

We will now derive the main asymptotic bounds of spherical functions that will lead us to the proof of Theorem

in the later sections.

Proposition 6.11
.

The spherical functions satisfy the following:

(1)

For every
r>0
and every
\lambda>0

\displaystyle|\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}(a_{r})|\leq\frac{a_{n}}{\lambda\sinh(r)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\,.

(2)

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}|\omega^{(n)}_{\lambda}(a_{r})|^{2}\sinh(r)^{n-1}dr=2^{n-2}\frac{|\mathrm{B}(i\lambda\mathchar 59\relax\frac{n-1}{2})|^{2}}{\mathrm{B}(\frac{1}{2}\mathchar 59\relax\frac{n-1}{2})^{2}}\,.

In particular, the convergence is uniform in

(3)

Moreover, if
\lambda\in[0\mathchar 59\relax\frac{n-1}{2}]

\displaystyle\omega_{i\lambda}^{(n)}(a_{r})\geq C\frac{\sinh(\lambda r)}{\lambda\sinh(r)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\,.

Remark 6.12

The limit formula in item (2) of the above Proposition has previously been computed by Strichartz in

To prove item (2) in Proposition

Lemma 6.13

Let

To prove (1) we use that

\displaystyle|\omega_{\lambda}^{(n)}(a_{r})|

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{n}}{\sinh(r)^{n-2}}\Big|\int_{0}^{r}(\cosh(r)-\cosh(s))^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\cos(\lambda s)ds\Big|

\displaystyle\leq a_{n}\frac{(\cosh(r)-1)^{\frac{n-3}{2}}}{\sinh(r)^{n-2}}\Big|\int_{0}^{r}\cos(\lambda s)ds\Big|\leq\frac{a_{n}}{\lambda\sinh(r)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}\,.

\displaystyle\omega_{i\lambda}(a_{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{n}}{\sinh(r)^{n-2}}\int_{0}^{r}(\cosh(r)-\cosh(s))^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\cosh(\lambda s)ds

6.5.
The spherical transform on
\mathbb{H}^{n}

Given a function
\varphi\in\mathscr{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{bnd}}(G_{n})
, its
Fourier-Helgason transform
is the function
\mathscr{F}\varphi:\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}\varphi(\lambda\mathchar 59\relax u)=\int_{G_{n}}\varphi(g)\overline{\xi_{\lambda\mathchar 59\relax u}(g)}dm_{G_{n}}(g)\,.

If
\varphi
happens to be bi-
K_{n}
-invariant, then

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}\varphi(\lambda\mathchar 59\relax u)=\int_{G_{n}}\varphi(g)\overline{\omega_{\lambda}(g)}dm_{G_{n}}(g)

for any choice of

.

Definition 6.14

(Hyperbolic spherical transform)
.

The
spherical transform
of a function
\varphi\in\mathscr{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{bnd}}(G_{n}\mathchar 59\relax K_{n})
is the function
\widehat{\varphi}:\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}(\lambda)=\int_{G_{n}}\varphi(g)\overline{\omega_{\lambda}(g)}dm_{G_{n}}(g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(a_{t})\overline{\omega_{\lambda}(a_{t})}\sinh(t)^{n-1}dt\,.

One shows that
\widehat{\varphi}
is even and analytic on
\mathbb{C}
for every
\varphi\in\mathscr{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{bnd}}(G_{n}\mathchar 59\relax K_{n})
and that
\widehat{\varphi_{1}^{*}*\varphi_{2}}(\lambda)=\overline{\widehat{\varphi}_{1}(\overline{\lambda})}\widehat{\varphi}_{2}(\lambda)
. In particular, if
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\cup i\mathbb{R}
then
\widehat{\varphi^{*}*\varphi}(\lambda)=|\widehat{\varphi}(\lambda)|^{2}
.

As for the
L^{2}
-theory of the spherical transform, we again have a Plancherel formula. To state it, we introduce the
Harish-Chandra
c
-function

\displaystyle c_{n}(\lambda)=\rho_{n}2^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\frac{\mathrm{B}(i\lambda\mathchar 59\relax\frac{n-1}{2})}{\mathrm{B}(\frac{1}{2}\mathchar 59\relax\frac{n-1}{2})}=\rho_{n}2^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})\Gamma(i\lambda)}{\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})\Gamma(i\lambda+\frac{n-1}{2})}\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\backslash i\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\,\mathchar 59\relax

where
\rho_{n}>0
is a normalizing constant such that the Plancherel formula below holds. The precise value of
\rho_{n}
is not crucial to us in proving the remaining main results. A computation of the

-function with an exact value of
\rho_{n}
can be found in
[

, Section 4, Thm 4.2. p.334]
, but we note that the value depends on the normalization of the Haar measure
m_{G_{n}}
. Note that
c_{n}
is meromorphic and does not have any zeros in
\mathbb{C}
. In particular,
|c_{n}|
is a strictly positive function.

Lemma 6.15

(Spherical Plancherel formula)
.

For every
\varphi\in\mathscr{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{bnd}}(G_{n}\mathchar 59\relax K_{n})
,

\displaystyle\int_{G_{n}}|\varphi(g)|^{2}dm_{G_{n}}(g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}|\widehat{\varphi}(\lambda)|^{2}\frac{d\lambda}{|c_{n}(\lambda)|^{2}}

Equivalently, the spherical transform extends to a unitary map

L^{2}(G_{n}\mathchar 59\relax K_{n})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mathchar 59\relax|c_{n}(\lambda)|^{-2}d\lambda)\,.

Before moving on, we will compute the spherical transform of indicator functions on balls.

Example 6.1

(Spherical transform of the indicator function on a centered Cartan ball)
.

The computation of the spherical transform for the indicator function

is similar to the Euclidean case. Using the formula

\displaystyle\widehat{\chi}_{B_{r}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r}\omega^{(n)}_{\lambda}(t)\sinh(t)^{n-1}dt

\displaystyle=a_{n}\int_{0}^{r}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}(\cosh(t)-\cosh(s))^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\cos(\lambda s)ds\Big)tdt

\displaystyle=a_{n}\int_{0}^{r}\Big(\int_{s}^{r}(\cosh(t)-\cosh(s))^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\sinh(t)dt\Big)\cos(\lambda s)ds

In terms of spherical functions, we have

7.
Hyperbolic diffraction measures

In this section we will introduce the diffraction measure associated to a random measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Furthermore, we provide an asymptotic upper bound on the diffraction measures of large intervals, similar to the Euclidean case. The support such diffraction measures will be confined to the subset

\displaystyle\Omega_{n}^{+}=(0\mathchar 59\relax+\infty)\cup i[0\mathchar 59\relax(n-1)/2]\subset\mathbb{C}\,.

The following Lemma ensures existence and uniqueness of the diffraction measure of a random measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, which we will prove in larger generality in upcoming work.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
\mu
be a locally square-integrable invariant random measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with autocorrelation measure
\eta_{\mu}
on
G_{n}
. Then there is a unique positive Radon measure
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}
on
\Omega_{n}^{+}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{G_{n}}\varphi(g)d\eta_{\mu}(g)=\int_{\Omega_{n}^{+}}\widehat{\varphi}(\lambda)d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\lambda)\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad\forall\,\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G_{n}\mathchar 59\relax K_{n})\,.

Moreover,
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\{i\frac{n-1}{2}\})=0
and the measures
\eta_{\mu}\mathchar 59\relax\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}
are tempered in the sense that there are constants
a\mathchar 59\relax b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{G_{n}}(1+\ell(g))^{-a}\omega_{0}^{(n)}(g)^{-\frac{n+1}{2}}d\eta_{\mu}(g)<+\infty\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\int_{\Omega_{n}^{+}}(1+|\lambda|)^{-b}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\lambda)<+\infty\,.

Since the spherical function
\omega_{0}^{(n)}
decays exponentially, the following family of functions are integrable with respect to the autocorrelation measure.

Corollary 7.2
.

If
\varphi:G_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a measurable function such that

|\varphi(g)|\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha d(g.o\mathchar 59\relax o)}\longrightarrow 0

as
g\rightarrow+\infty
for every
\alpha>0
, then
\varphi
is integrable with respect to
\eta_{\mu}
.

In fact,
\eta_{\mu}
extends to a continuous linear functional on a certain

-space
of smooth functions that are rapidly decaying with respect to a power of
\omega_{0}
, but for our purposes we will not need this here.

We define the

\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(p)}
and the

\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(c)}
to be the restrictions of
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}
to
(0\mathchar 59\relax+\infty)
and

respectively, so that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{n}^{+}}\widehat{\varphi}(\lambda)d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{\varphi}(\lambda)d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(p)}(\lambda)+\int_{0}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\widehat{\varphi}(i\lambda)d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(c)}(\lambda)\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G_{n}\mathchar 59\relax K_{n})\,.

Both measures
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(p)}\mathchar 59\relax\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(c)}
are positive Radon measures, and

is necessarily a finite measure. As in the Euclidean case, we can extend the defining identity

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(Sf)=\eta_{\mu}(\varphi_{f}^{*}*\varphi_{f})=\int_{0}^{\infty}|\widehat{\varphi}_{f}(\lambda)|^{2}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(p)}(\lambda)+\int_{0}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}|\widehat{\varphi}_{f}(i\lambda)|^{2}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(c)}(\lambda)

,

for

\displaystyle\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(p)}([0\mathchar 59\relax L])\leq\mathrm{e}^{1+(\frac{n-1}{2})^{2}}CL^{n}\int_{G_{n}}(2+\ell(g))^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\ell(g)^{2}}{4}}d\eta_{\mu}^{+}(g)\,.

Since

is tempered, the latter integral is finite by Corollary

.
∎

A hyperbolic analouge of Beck’s theorem

We prove Theorem

using the following hyperbolic analogue of Theorem

Theorem 8.1

Let
\mu
be an invariant locally square-integrable random measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
with diffraction measure

. The following holds:

(1)

if

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(R)}{\cosh(R)^{n-1}}\geq C\int_{0}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{\sinh^{2}(R\lambda)}{\lambda^{2}}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(c)}(\lambda)\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad\forall\,R\geq R_{o}\,.

By definition of the diffraction measure
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}
and Equation (

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)}{\cosh(r)^{n-1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{|\widehat{\chi}_{B_{r}}(\lambda)|^{2}}{\cosh(r)^{n-1}}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(p)}(\lambda)+\int_{0}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\frac{|\widehat{\chi}_{B_{r}}(i\lambda)|^{2}}{\cosh(r)^{n-1}}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(c)}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n+1}}{n^{2}}\tanh(r)^{n-1}\sinh(r)^{n+1}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\omega_{\lambda}^{(n+2)}(a_{r})|^{2}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(p)}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\int_{0}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}|\omega_{i\lambda}^{(n+2)}(a_{r})|^{2}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(c)}(\lambda)\Big)\,.

To prove (1), we use item (3) in Proposition
6.11
to find a constant

for

\displaystyle\omega_{i\lambda}^{(n+2)}(a_{R})\geq C_{o}\frac{\sinh(\lambda R)}{\lambda\sinh(R)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}\,\quad\forall\,R\geq R_{o}\,.

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(R)}{\cosh(R)^{n-1}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2^{n+1}}{n^{2}}\tanh(R)^{n-1}\sinh(R)^{n+1}\int_{0}^{\frac{n-1}{2}}|\omega_{i\lambda}^{(n+2)}(a_{R})|^{2}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(c)}(\lambda)

. An application of Fubini yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)}{\cosh(r)^{n-1}}dr

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2^{n+1}}{n^{2}}\int_{\lambda_{o}}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}|\omega_{\lambda}^{(n+2)}(a_{r})|^{2}\tanh(r)^{n-1}\sinh(r)^{n+1}dr\Big)d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(p)}(\lambda)\,.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}|\omega_{\lambda}^{(n+2)}(a_{r})|^{2}\tanh(r)^{n-1}

9.
Spectral hyperuniformity and stealth

We define spectral hyperuniformity of an invariant locally square-integrable random measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
in Subsection
9.1
and define the relative notion of stealth in Subsection
9.2
.

9.1.
Spectral hyperuniformity

By Theorem
1.4
, we know that random measures
\mu
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
are never geometrically hyperuniform in the sense that

(9.1)

\displaystyle\limsup_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(R)}{\cosh(R)^{n-1}}>0\,\mathchar 59\relax

and if
\mu
admits non-trivial complementary spectrum, then this upper limit is infinite. A useful definition of spectral hyperuniformity would then necessarily have to differ from this geometric definition. Having understood the large scale behaviour of diffraction measures, one is inclined to study the small scale/local behaviour of the diffraction measure in order to obtain information on the large scale behaviour of the number variance. We take the stance that one should interpret spectral hyperuniformity as comparing the diffraction measure of a given random measure to the diffraction measure of the invariant Poisson point process around
\lambda=0
. For the definition we shall use that

\displaystyle\widehat{\eta}_{\mathrm{Poi}}((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon])=\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{d\lambda}{|c_{n}(\lambda)|^{2}}\asymp\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\lambda^{2}d\lambda\asymp\varepsilon^{3}

for sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
. Note that the exponent
3
is the same in all dimensions
n
.

Definition 9.1

(Hyperbolic spectral hyperuniformity)
.

A random measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is
spectrally hyperuniform
if
\widehat{\eta}^{(c)}_{\mu}=0
and

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(p)}((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon])}{\varepsilon^{3}}=0\,.

Remark 9.2
.

There are many examples of point processes
\mu
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
such that
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(c)}\neq 0
, even for many lattices, including cocompact ones. These will be hyperfluctuating in the sense of Theorem
1.4
and not spectrally hyperuniform according to our definition.

Having defined spectral hyperuniformity, we have a point of reference for defining more rigid properties of random measures on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

9.2.
Stealthy random measures

Definition 9.3

(Stealthy random measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
)
.

A random measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is
stealthy
if
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(c)}=0
and there is a
\lambda_{o}>0
such that
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(p)}((0\mathchar 59\relax\lambda_{o}))=0
.

It is clear from this definition that every stealthy random measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is spectrally hyperuniform. As mentioned in the introduction, in
[
13
, Section 1.2., Eq.B]
Jenni provides a lattice
\Gamma<G_{2}\cong\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that the random lattice orbit
\mu_{\Gamma}
on
\mathbb{H}^{2}
is a stealthy point process, in particular spectrally hyperuniform.

An almost immediate consequence of Theorem
8.1
is that we can compute the average asymptotic number variance of a given stealthy random measure in terms of the diffraction measure.

Corollary 9.4
.

Let
\mu
be a stealthy random measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)}{\cosh(r)^{n-1}}dr=\frac{2^{n+1}}{n^{2}\rho_{n+2}^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|c_{n+2}(\lambda)|^{2}d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(p)}(\lambda)\,\mathchar 59\relax

which is strictly positive.

Proof.

If
\mu
is stealthy there is a
\lambda_{o}>0
such that the diffraction measure
\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}
is supported on
[\lambda_{o}\mathchar 59\relax+\infty)\subset\Omega_{n}^{+}
. Thus

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)}{\cosh(r)^{n-1}}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{n+1}}{n^{2}}\int_{\lambda_{o}}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}|\omega_{\lambda}^{(n+2)}(a_{r})|^{2}\tanh(r)^{n-1}\sinh(r)^{n+1}dr\Big)d\widehat{\eta}_{\mu}^{(p)}(\lambda)\,.

As shown in the proof of item (2) of Theorem
8.1
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{R}\int_{0}^{R}|\omega_{\lambda}^{(n+2)}(a_{r})|^{2}\tanh(r)^{n-1}\sinh(r)^{n+1}dr=\rho_{n+2}^{-2}|c_{n+2}(\lambda)|^{2}

uniformly in
\lambda\geq\lambda_{o}
, so by dominated convergence we’re done.
∎

as

10.3.
Random perturbed lattice orbits in
\mathbb{H}^{n}
are not spectrally hyperuniform

Let
\Gamma<G_{n}
be a lattice and
\nu\in\mathrm{Prob}(G_{n})
a bi-
K_{n}
-invariant probability measure that is absolutely continuous with respect to the Haar measure
m_{G_{n}}
, satisfying the exponential decay condition in Lemma
10.2
.

Proof of Proposition
1.7
.

From the formula for the diffraction measure
\widehat{\eta}_{\Gamma\mathchar 59\relax\nu}
in Equation
10.1
we cut off the first term to get the lower bound

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\widehat{\eta}_{\Gamma\mathchar 59\relax\nu}((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon])}{\varepsilon^{3}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\mathrm{covol}(\Gamma)}\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}(1-|\widehat{\nu}(\lambda)|^{2})\frac{d\lambda}{|c_{n}(\lambda)|^{2}}\,.

By item (3) of Lemma
6.3
we have that

\displaystyle\widehat{\nu}(\lambda)=\int_{G_{n}}\omega_{\lambda}(g)d\nu(g)\leq\int_{G_{n}}\omega_{0}(g)d\nu(g)=\widehat{\nu}(0)\,\mathchar 59\relax

so

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\widehat{\eta}_{\Gamma\mathchar 59\relax\nu}((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon])}{\varepsilon^{3}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\mathrm{covol}(\Gamma)}(1-|\widehat{\nu}(0)|^{2})\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{d\lambda}{|c_{n}(\lambda)|^{2}}\,.

Since
|c_{n}(\lambda)|^{-2}\asymp\lambda^{2}
when
\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}
then there is a constant
c_{o}>0
such that
|c(\lambda)|^{-2}\geq c_{o}\lambda^{2}
for all sufficiently small
\lambda\geq 0
, so that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon}\frac{d\lambda}{|c_{n}(\lambda)|^{2}}\geq\frac{c_{o}}{3}\,.

Moreover, by item (2) in Lemma
6.3
,
\omega_{0}(g)<1
for all
g\neq e
, and since
m_{G_{n}}(K_{n})=0
then
\nu(K_{n})=0
by absolute continuity, so

\displaystyle\widehat{\nu}(0)=\int_{G_{n}}\omega_{0}(g)d\nu(g)<\nu(G_{n})=1\,.

Gathering these lower bounds,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\widehat{\eta}_{\Gamma\mathchar 59\relax\nu}((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon])}{\varepsilon^{3}}\geq\frac{c_{o}}{3\,\mathrm{covol}(\Gamma)}(1-|\widehat{\nu}(0)|^{2})>0\,.

∎

Remark 10.4
.

Although we have not introduced diffraction measures for random measures on other homogeneous spaces, we do so in upcoming work for general commutative metric spaces
X=G/K
with
K
compact. This includes semisimple Lie groups
G
with finite center and maximal compact subgroup
K
, and there the same argument as in the above proof holds, so no perturbation of a stealthy lattice yields a hyperuniform point process.

As a consequence of Proposition
1.7
, we prove that the number variance of a perturbed lattice orbit grows as fast as the volume along
all

Corollary 10.5
.

\displaystyle\liminf_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu_{\Gamma\mathchar 59\relax\nu}}(R)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}(B_{R})}>0\,.

Proof.

By the formula for the diffraction measure of

we can bound the number variance form below using the spherical Plancherel formula as

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\mathrm{covol}(\Gamma)}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\widehat{\chi}_{B_{R}}(\lambda)|^{2}(1-|\widehat{\nu}(\lambda)|^{2})\frac{d\lambda}{|c_{n}(\lambda)|^{2}}

Let

be a commutative unital Banach algebra.
Then

Let

and componentwise operations. Then

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{v}\|_{2}^{2}:=\|v_{1}\|^{2}+\cdots+\|v_{n}\|^{2}

are given the induced subspace topology. We also state the following observation which will be used later.

Lemma 1.2
.

Let

be a commutative unital Banach algebra, and

is denoted by

\|M\|^{2}\leq\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|m_{ij}\|^{2}.

Proof.

Let
\mathbf{v}\in A^{n\times 1}
have components

. Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in

, we have

\displaystyle\|M\mathbf{v}\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big\|\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{ij}v_{j}\Big\|^{2}\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|m_{ij}v_{j}\|\Big)^{2}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|m_{ij}\|\|v_{j}\|\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|m_{ij}\|^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|v_{k}\|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\|m_{ij}\|^{2}\|\mathbf{v}\|_{2}^{2}.

As

Definition 2.1

Let

Given a Borel set

{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.81}{$\displaystyle\bigcup\limits_{n\;\!\in\;\!\mathbb{N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\textstyle\bigcup\limits_{n\;\!\in\;\!\mathbb{N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptstyle\bigcup\limits_{n\;\!\in\;\!\mathbb{N}}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bigcup\limits_{n\;\!\in\;\!\mathbb{N}}$}}}E_{n}=E

\phantom{AAAAAAAAA}\displaystyle|\mu|(E)=\sup_{(E_{n})_{n\;\!\in\;\!\mathbb{N}}\;\!\in\;\!\mathcal{P}(E)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\mu(E_{n})|,

\delta(E)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1&\textrm{if }\;0\in E,\\

|\widehat{\mu}(s)|\!=\!\Big|\int_{[0,\infty)}\!\!\!\!e^{-sx}d\mu(x)\Big|\!=\!\Big|\int_{[0,\infty)}\!\!\!\!e^{-\;\!iw(x)}e^{-sx}d|\mu|(x)\Big|\!\leq\!\int_{[0,\infty)}\!\!\!\!1\;\!d|\mu|(x)\!=\!\|\mu\|.

For
t\in[0,1)
,
s\in\mathbb{C}_{{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.81}{$\displaystyle\geq 0$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\textstyle\geq 0$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptstyle\geq 0$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptscriptstyle\geq 0$}}}}
and
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}
,
\widehat{\mu_{t}}(s)=\widehat{\mu}(s-\log(1\!-t))
. Indeed,

\widehat{\mu_{t}}(s)\!=\!\int_{[0,\infty)}\!\!\!\!e^{-sx}(1\!-t)^{x}d\mu(x)\!=\!\int_{[0,\infty)}\!\!\!\!e^{-sx}e^{x\log(1-t)}d\mu\!=\!\widehat{\mu}(s-\log(1\!-t)).

Recall that
\mu_{1}=\mu(\{0\})\delta
. As

\widehat{\delta}(s)=1
for all
s\in\mathbb{C}_{{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.81}{$\displaystyle\geq 0$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\textstyle\geq 0$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptstyle\geq 0$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptscriptstyle\geq 0$}}}}
,

\widehat{\mu_{1}}(s)=\mu(\{0\})
. As with the Laplace transforms of
{\textrm{L}}^{1}
functions, the same proof, mutatis mutandis, shows

\widehat{\mu\ast\nu}(s)=\widehat{\mu}(s)\;\!\widehat{\nu}(s)
for
s\in\mathbb{C}_{{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.81}{$\displaystyle\geq 0$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\textstyle\geq 0$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptstyle\geq 0$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptscriptstyle\geq 0$}}}}
and
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}
.

We provide some motivation for Definition
2.2
in the following remark.

Remark 3.3
.

We want to create a homotopy taking
\mu\in{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(\mathcal{M}^{+})
to
I_{n}

(with all diagonal entries equal to
\delta
).
In the case of disc algebra, one uses dilations
f\mapsto f(t\;\!\cdot)
,
t\in[0,1]
to take

I_{n}
to
f\in{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(A(\mathbb{D}))
.
Motivated by this, and bearing in mind that the Laplace transform
\widehat{\mu}
of
\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}

satisfies the following analogue of the Riemann-Lebesgue lemma (we include a proof of this in the Appendix)

\lim_{\mathbb{R}\;\!\owns\;\!s\;\!\rightarrow\;\!+\infty}\widehat{\mu}(s)=\mu(\{0\}),

it is natural to try and construct a homotopy by ‘translating’ the Laplace transform and then taking the inverse Laplace transform. As

\lim_{t\;\!\rightarrow\;\!1-}-\log(1-t)=+\infty,

we try

H(t):=\mu_{t}:=\textrm{Inverse Laplace transform of }\boldsymbol{(}s\mapsto\widehat{\mu}(s-\log(1-t))\boldsymbol{)}.

To determine
\mu_{t}
, we write

\int_{[0,+\infty)}\!\!\!\!e^{-(s-\log(1-t))x}d\mu(x)\!=\!\int_{[0,+\infty)}\!\!\!\!e^{-sx}(1\!-\!t)^{x}d\mu(x)\!=\!\int_{[0,+\infty)}\!\!\!\!e^{-sx}d\mu_{t}(x),

where
\mu_{t}
is as in Definition
2.2
.
\Asterisk

Finally, we are ready to prove Theorem
2.3
.

Proof.

(of Theorem
2.3
):
We need to show
{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(A)\subset{\textrm{E}}_{n}(A)
. Let

M=\left[\!\!\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mu_{11}&\cdots&\mu_{1n}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mu_{n1}&\cdots&\mu_{nn}\end{array}\!\!\right]\in{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(A).

For
t\in[0,1]
, define

H(t)=M_{t}=\left[\!\!\begin{array}[]{ccc}(\mu_{11})_{t}&\cdots&(\mu_{1n})_{t}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(\mu_{n1})_{t}&\cdots&(\mu_{nn})_{t}\end{array}\!\!\right].

Thanks to (
1
) and Lemma
3.1
,
as
M\in{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(A)
, we have
M_{t}\in{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(A)
for all
t\in[0,1]
,
since

\delta=\delta_{t}=(\det M)_{t}=\det(M_{t}).

The continuity of
[0,1]\owns t\mapsto M_{t}\in{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(A)
follows from
Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
1.2
. We note that
M_{1}=C\delta
,
where
C\in{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is the constant matrix

C=\left[\!\!\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mu_{11}(\{0\})&\cdots&\mu_{1n}(\{0\})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\mu_{n1}(\{0\})&\cdots&\mu_{nn}(\{0\})\end{array}\!\!\right].

But
{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is path connected, and so there exists a homotopy, say
h:[0,1]\rightarrow{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
,
taking
C
to the identity matrix
I_{n}\in{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
. Combining
H
with
h
,
we obtain a homotopy
\widetilde{H}:[0,1]\rightarrow{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(A)
that takes
M
to
I_{n}\in{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(A)
:

\widetilde{H}(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}H(2t)&\textrm{if }\;t\in\left[0,\displaystyle{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.81}{$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\textstyle\frac{1}{2}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptstyle\frac{1}{2}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptscriptstyle\frac{1}{2}$}}}\right],\\[8.5359pt]
h(2t-\!1)\delta&\textrm{if }\;t\in\left[{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.81}{$\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\textstyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptstyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}}{\scalebox{0.81}{$\scriptscriptstyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}$}}},1\right].\end{array}\right.

So
M\in{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(A)
is null-homotopic. By Proposition
1.3
,
M\in{\textrm{E}}_{n}(A)
.
∎

4.2.

-norm of

\displaystyle{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(\delta\;\!\mathbb{C}+{\textrm{L}}^{1}[0,+\infty))\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(\mathcal{W}^{+})\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

4.4.
The analytic almost periodic Wiener algebra

Define the subalgebra
\mathcal{A}^{+}_{0}
of
\mathcal{M}^{+}
to be the set of all

\mu\in\mathcal{M}^{+}
that are of the form

\mu=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!{\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.9}{$\displaystyle\sum_{\phantom{AAAAA}0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\cdots}$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\textstyle\sum_{\phantom{AAAAA}0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\cdots}$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptstyle\sum_{\phantom{AAAAA}0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\cdots}$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptscriptstyle\sum_{\phantom{AAAAA}0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\cdots}$}}}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!f_{n}\delta_{\{\lambda_{n}\}},

where the sequence of coefficients

is absolutely summable.
The
\mathcal{M}^{+}
-norm of

is
given by

\|\mu\|=\|(f_{n})_{n\geq 0}\|_{1}
for
\mu\in\mathcal{A}_{0}^{+}
.
The algebra
\mathcal{A}_{0}^{+}
is a Banach subalgebra of
\mathcal{M}^{+}
,
and has the property (
\mathbf{P}
).
Hence for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(\mathcal{A}^{+}_{0})={\textrm{E}}_{n}(\mathcal{A}^{+}_{0})

Recall the classical Banach algebra
\textrm{APW}^{+}
of almost periodic functions
f
,
whose Bohr-Fourier coefficients
(f_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}
, are summable,
and
f_{\lambda}=0
for
\lambda<0
. The algebra operations in
\textrm{APW}^{+}
are defined pointwise, and the norm is given by

\|f\|_{1}={\mathchoice{\scalebox{0.9}{$\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\geq 0}$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\textstyle\sum_{\lambda\geq 0}$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptstyle\sum_{\lambda\geq 0}$}}{\scalebox{0.9}{$\scriptscriptstyle\sum_{\lambda\geq 0}$}}}\;|f_{\lambda}|.

\mathcal{A}_{0}^{+}
is isomorphic as a Banach algebra to

4.5.
The algebra
\textrm{AP}^{+}
of analytic almost periodic functions

We refer the reader to
[
7
]
and
[
5
]
for details on almost periodic functions.
For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\mathbf{e}_{\lambda}:=e^{i\lambda\;\!\cdot}\in{\textrm{L}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
Let
\mathcal{T}
be the space of trigonometric polynomials, i.e.,

\mathcal{T}
is the linear span of
\{\mathbf{e}_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}
.
Define
AP
to be the closure of
\mathcal{T}
with respect to the
{\textrm{L}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
-norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
. Then

AP
is a Banach algebra with pointwise operations, and the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
.
The space
\mathcal{T}
is equipped with
the inner product

\langle p,q\rangle=\lim_{R\;\!\rightarrow\;\!+\infty}\frac{1}{2R}\int_{-R}^{R}p(x)\;\!\overline{q(x)}\;\!dx\quad(p,q\in\mathcal{T}),

where
\overline{\cdot}
denotes complex conjugation.
The limit exists since

\langle\mathbf{e}_{\lambda},\mathbf{e}_{\lambda^{\prime}}\rangle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&\textrm{if }\;\lambda=\lambda^{\prime},\\
0&\textrm{if }\;\lambda\neq\lambda^{\prime}.\end{array}\right.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in\textrm{AP}
, the
Bohr-Fourier coefficient

f_{\lambda}
is defined as follows:
If
(p_{n})_{n}
is a sequence in
\mathcal{T}
converging to
f
in
AP
, then

f_{\lambda}=\lim_{n\;\!\rightarrow\;\!\infty}\langle p_{n},\mathbf{e}_{-\lambda}\rangle.

Define the
Bohr spectrum of
f
to be the set

\sigma(f)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:f_{\lambda}\neq 0\},

which is at most countable.
Let
\textrm{AP}^{+}
be the subspace of
AP
given by

\textrm{AP}^{+}=\{f\in\textrm{AP}:\sigma(f)\subset[0,+\infty)\}.

Each
f\in\textrm{AP}^{+}
has a holomorphic extension to the upper half-plane

\mathbb{U}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\textrm{Im}\;\!z>0\}.

The set
\textrm{APW}^{+}
is a dense subset of
\textrm{AP}^{+}
(since the trigonometric polynomials
are dense in
\textrm{AP}^{+}
).
We will use this, and the following consequence Proposition
1.3
,
to show that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have

{\textrm{SL}}_{n}({\textrm{AP}}^{+})={\textrm{E}}_{n}({\textrm{AP}}^{+})
. We remark that the Bass stable rank of
{\textrm{AP}}^{+}
is
\infty
; see
[
18
]
. We have the following result:

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A
be a commutative unital Banach algebra.
Let
S
be a Banach algebra containing the unit of
A
such that

\begin{array}[]{l}\bullet\;\textrm{for all }n\in\mathbb{N},\;{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(S)={\textrm{E}}_{n}(S),\\
\bullet\;S\textrm{ is a full subalgebra of }A,\;(\textrm{i.e.},\textrm{ if }S\cap\textrm{GL}_{1}(A)=\textrm{GL}_{1}(S)),\\
\bullet\;\textrm{the inclusion map is continuous},\textrm{ and}\\
\bullet\;S\textrm{ is dense in }A.\end{array}

Then for all
n\in\mathbb{N},

{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(A)={\textrm{E}}_{n}(A)
.

Proof.

Let
f\in{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(A)
. As
S
is dense in
A
, we can find an element
g\in S^{n\times n}
such that

\|g-f\|<\frac{1}{\|f^{-1}\|}.

Then for all
t\in[0,1]
,

\|tf^{-1}(g-f)\|<1\cdot\|f^{-1}\|\cdot\frac{1}{\|f^{-1}\|}=1.

So
I_{n}+tf^{-1}(g-f)\in\textrm{GL}_{n}(A)
. As

g=f+g-f=f(I_{n}+f^{-1}(g-f)),

g\in\textrm{GL}_{n}(A)
. Thus
\det g\in S\cap\textrm{GL}_{1}(A)
, giving
\det g\in\textrm{GL}_{1}(S)
. Also,

(I_{n}+tf^{-1}(g-f))^{-1}g\in\textrm{GL}_{n}(A)
for all
t\in[0,1]
, which implies

\Delta(t):=\det((I_{n}+tf^{-1}(g-f))^{-1}g)\in\textrm{GL}_{1}(A)

for all
t\in[0,1]
. For
t\in[0,1]
, define the matrix
H(t)\in A^{n\times n}
to be
the matrix obtained by scaling (any one, say) the first column of
(I_{n}+tf^{-1}(g-f))^{-1}g

by
(\Delta(t))^{-1}
. Then

\displaystyle\det(H(t))\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!(\Delta(t))^{-1}\det((I_{n}+tf^{-1}(g-f))^{-1}g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!(\Delta(t))^{-1}\Delta(t)=1.

Hence
H(t)\in{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(A)
, and the map
[0,1]\owns t\mapsto H(t)\in{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(A)
is continuous.
We have
H(1)=f
. Also,
H(0)
is the matrix obtained by scaling the first column
of
g
by
(\det g)^{-1}
. So
H(0)\in{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(S)={\textrm{E}}_{n}(S)
. By Proposition
1.3
,
there exists a homotopy
h:[0,1]\rightarrow{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(S)
such that

and

.
Thanks to the fact that the inclusion map for

By combining

Consequently,
{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(A)={\textrm{E}}_{n}(A)

.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N},

{\textrm{SL}}_{n}({\textrm{AP}}^{+})={\textrm{E}}_{n}({\textrm{AP}}^{+})
.

Proof.

For

, we have

, showing that the inclusion map is continuous.
We have
\textrm{APW}^{+}
is a dense subset of
{\textrm{AP}}^{+}
.
The full subalgebra assumption can be seen to hold by using the corona theorem

).

.

4.6.
{\textrm{APW}}^{+}_{S}
and
{\textrm{AP}}^{+}_{S}

Recall that an

is a subset

, and
\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}\in S

Given an additive sub-semigroup

we define the Banach subalgebra
{\textrm{APW}}^{+}_{S}
of
{\textrm{APW}}^{+}
by

{\textrm{APW}}^{+}_{S}:=\{f\in{\textrm{APW}}^{+}:\sigma(f)\subset S\},

with the induced norm
\|\cdot\|_{1}
from
{\textrm{APW}}^{+}
. Similarly,
we define the Banach subalgebra
{\textrm{AP}}^{+}_{S}
of
{\textrm{AP}}^{+}
by

{\textrm{AP}}^{+}_{S}:=\{f\in{\textrm{AP}}^{+}:\sigma(f)\subset S\},

with the induced norm
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
from
{\textrm{AP}}^{+}
. Thus if

the
{\textrm{APW}}^{+}_{S}
and
{\textrm{AP}}^{+}_{S}
are

{\textrm{APW}}^{+}
and
{\textrm{AP}}^{+}
, respectively.

The Banach algebra
{\textrm{APW}}^{+}_{S}
is isomorphic to the
Banach subalgebra
\mathcal{A}_{0,S}^{+}
of
\mathcal{M}^{+}
consisting of all measures

\mu=\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\sum_{\phantom{AAAAAA}\lambda_{0},\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3},\cdots\;\!\in\;\!S}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!f_{n}\delta_{\{\lambda_{n}\}},

where the sequence of coefficients
(f_{n})_{n\geq 0}
is absolutely summable.
The Banach subalgebra
\mathcal{A}_{0,S}^{+}
of
\mathcal{M}^{+}
has the property (
{\mathbf{P}}
).
So for all
n\in\mathbb{N},

{\textrm{SL}}_{n}(\mathcal{A}_{0,S}^{+})={\textrm{E}}_{n}(\mathcal{A}_{0,S}^{+})
, and

{\textrm{SL}}_{n}({\textrm{APW}}_{S}^{+})={\textrm{E}}_{n}({\textrm{APW}}_{S}^{+})
.

To get the result for
{\textrm{AP}}_{S}^{+}
, we again use Theorem
4.1
.
The algebra
{\textrm{APW}}_{S}^{+}
is a dense subset of
{\textrm{AP}}_{S}^{+}
. Next we show that

is a full subalgebra of
{\textrm{APW}}_{S}^{+}
. Let

be such that there exists a

,
where

is the constant function taking value

everywhere on

is the constant function taking value

of

{\textrm{APW}}_{S}^{+}
is a full subalgebra of
{\textrm{APW}}_{S}^{+}
.
By Theorem

{\textrm{SL}}_{n}({\textrm{AP}}_{S}^{+})={\textrm{E}}_{n}({\textrm{AP}}_{S}^{+})

\mathcal{M}^{+}
generated by
\delta,\mu

. In fact, set

Proposition 4.5

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\Big|\int_{[0,+\infty)}\!\!e^{-sx}e^{-iw(x)}d|\mu|(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\Big|\int_{[0,r]}\!\!e^{-sx}e^{-iw(x)}d|\mu|(x)\Big|+\Big|\int_{(r,+\infty)}\!\!e^{-sx}e^{-iw(x)}d|\mu|(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\int_{[0,r]}\!\!1\;\!d|\mu|(x)+e^{-sr}\int_{(r,+\infty)}\!\!1\;\!d|\mu|(x)\leq\epsilon+e^{-sr}|\mu|((r,+\infty)).

So
\displaystyle\limsup_{s\;\!\rightarrow\;\!+\infty}|\widehat{\mu}|\leq\epsilon
. As
\epsilon
was arbitrary,

\displaystyle\limsup_{s\;\!\rightarrow\;\!+\infty}|\widehat{\mu}|\leq 0.

As
|\widehat{\mu}(s)|\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\lim_{s\;\!\rightarrow\;\!+\infty}|\widehat{\mu}(s)|=0,\textrm{ and }\displaystyle\lim_{s\;\!\rightarrow\;\!+\infty}\widehat{\mu}(s)=0.

If
\mu(\{0\})\neq 0
, we complete the proof by considering
\nu:=\mu-\mu(\{0\})\delta
, which satisfies

\nu(\{0\})=0
and
\widehat{\nu}=\widehat{\mu}-\mu(\{0\})
.
∎

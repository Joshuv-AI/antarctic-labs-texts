The sixth moment of the Riemann zeta function and ternary additive divisor sums
Nathan Ng

Abstract
Hardy and Littlewood initiated the study of the 2 ​ k 2k -th moments of the Riemann zeta function on the critical line. In 1918 Hardy and Littlewood established an asymptotic formula for the second moment and in 1926 Ingham established an asymptotic formula for the fourth moment. Since then no other moments have been asymptotically evaluated. In this article we study the sixth moment of the zeta function on the critical line. We show that a conjectural formula for a certain family of ternary additive divisor sums implies an asymptotic formula with power savings error term for the sixth moment of the Riemann zeta function on the critical line. This provides a rigorous proof for a heuristic argument of Conrey and Gonek [ 11 ] . Furthermore, this gives some evidence towards a conjecture of Conrey, Keating, Farmer, Rubinstein, and Snaith [ 8 ] on shifted moments of the Riemann zeta function. In addition, this improves on a theorem of Ivic [ 31 ] , who obtained an upper bound for the the sixth moment of the zeta function, based on the assumption of a conjectural formula for correlation sums of the triple divisor function.

The

I_{k}(T)=\int_{0}^{T}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2k}dt,

This article concerns the behaviour of (

Hardy and Littlewood initiated the study of the moments (

arose from the relationship of the

The motivation for studying the moments

is that it seems that

In 1918, Hardy and Littlewood

\int_{0}^{T}|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{4}dt\sim\frac{T}{2\pi^{2}}(\log T)^{4}.

In 1996, Conrey and Ghosh

10

In 1998,

Definition 1

Let

Observe that

\begin{split}\zeta_{\{0,\ldots,0\}}(s)&=\zeta(s)^{k}\text{ and }\\

Definition 2

Given finite multisets

The series

In fact,

Precise formulae for

For

Examples

(ii) Let

Definition 4

The approximate functional equation and the Dirichlet series
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}}(s)

One of the difficulties in evaluating mean values of the type (
1.1
) and (
1.10
) is that the
integration is on the line
\Re(s)=\frac{1}{2}
where
\zeta
does not possess an absolutely convergent
Dirichlet series. Instead, in the critical strip a standard tool is the approximate functional equation.
The approximate functional equation for
\zeta(s)^{k}
for
k=1,2
was derived by Hardy and Littlewood. Their result is of the form

\zeta(s)^{k}=\sum_{n\leq x}\frac{d_{k}(n)}{n^{s}}+\chi(s)^{k}\sum_{n\leq y}\frac{d_{k}(n)}{n^{1-s}}+\text{error}(s,x,y)

where
x
and
y
are an arbitrary pair of real numbers satisfying
x,y\gg 1
and

xy=(\frac{|t|}{2\pi})^{k}
(it being assumed that
s=\sigma+it
with
-\frac{1}{2}\leq\sigma\leq\frac{3}{2}

and
t^{2}\gg 1
).
There are several problems with this version of the approximate functional equation.
First, each of these sums have sharp cutoffs, that is, the sum over
n
does not decay smoothly.
In practice, it is convenient to sum over all integers with
a weight which is smooth. The sharp cutoff functions lead to poor error terms error
(s,x,y)
.
Another problem is the presence of the factor
\chi(s)^{k}
. Modern versions of the approximate functional
equation (see
[
48
, p.92, eq. (4.20.1)]
) have the shape

\zeta(s)^{k}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{k}(n)\nu_{x}(n)}{n^{s}}+\chi(s)^{k}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{d_{k}(n)\tilde{\nu}_{y}(n)}{n^{1-s}}+O(\exp(-ct^{2}))

(2.1)

where
c>0
and

\nu_{x}(m)
and
\tilde{\nu}_{y}(n)

are certain smooth weights essentially supported in
[0,x]
and
[0,y]
, while

xy=(\frac{|t|}{2\pi})^{k}
and
s=\sigma+it
with
0\leq\sigma\leq 1
and
t^{2}\gg 1
.
A classical approach to evaluating (
1.1
)

|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2k}=\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)^{k}\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}-it)^{k}
and then

) with

and then multiply out to give nine terms.
Some of the terms contain factors of the form
\chi(\tfrac{1}{2}\pm it)^{k}
. These have to be treated with stationary phase and
lead to unappealing oscillatory integrals.
One way to circumvent this problem is to develop an approximate functional equation
for
|\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)|^{2k}

\zeta(\tfrac{1}{2}+it)^{k}

Reintroducing the smooth partition of unity

We simplify the last sum by rearranging summation variables so that

\sum_{M,N}\tilde{I}_{M,N}=T\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{3}\sum_{i_{2}=1}^{3}{\bf c}_{i_{1},i_{2}}\sum_{0<|r|\leq R_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}u_{\ell,r;i_{1},i_{2}}\Bigg(\sum_{M,N}\frac{1}{\sqrt{MN}}i_{M,N,r;i_{1},i_{2}}\Bigg)

\begin{split}\sum_{M,N}\frac{1}{\sqrt{MN}}i_{M,N,r;i_{1},i_{2}}&=\int_{\max(0,r)}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{x(x-r)}}f^{*}(x,x-r)x^{-a_{i_{1}}}(x-r)^{-b_{i_{2}}}\,dx\\

\begin{split}&T\sum_{i_{1},i_{2}=1}^{3}|{\bf c}_{i_{1},i_{2}}|\sum_{1\leq|r|\leq R_{0}}\sum_{\ell\geq 1}|u_{\ell,r;i_{1},i_{2}}|\int_{\max(0,r)}^{\max(0,r)+1}|f^{*}(x,x-r)|(x-\min(0,r))^{-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}(x-\max(0,r))^{-\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\,dx\\

|y+t|\geq 1
, these combine to give

\begin{split}\Bigg|\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+a+it+s))\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+b-it+s))}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+a+it))\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+b-it))}\Bigg|&\ll\frac{|t+a^{\prime\prime}+y|^{\frac{a^{\prime}+\sigma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}|b^{\prime\prime}+y-t|^{\frac{b^{\prime}+\sigma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{4}(|t+y+a^{\prime\prime}|+|b^{\prime\prime}+y-t|)}}{(|t|^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\pi t}{4}})^{2}}\\
&\ll\frac{|t+y|^{\frac{a^{\prime}+\sigma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}|y-t|^{\frac{b^{\prime}+\sigma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{4}(|t+y|+|y-t|)}}{|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi t}{2}}}.\end{split}

In the case that
|y-t|\geq 1
and
|y+t|\geq 1
, we apply this with

a=a_{j}=a_{j}^{\prime}+ia_{j}^{\prime\prime}
and
b=b_{j}=b_{j}^{\prime}+ib_{j}^{\prime\prime}
to find that

\begin{split}|g_{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}}(s,t)|&\ll\prod_{j=1}^{3}\frac{|y+t|^{\frac{a_{j}^{\prime}+\sigma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}|y-t|^{\frac{b_{j}^{\prime}+\sigma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{4}(|t+y|+|y-t|)}}{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi t}{2}}}\\
&\ll\prod_{j=1}^{3}\frac{|y+t|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\delta}{2}}|y-t|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\delta}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{4}(|t+y|+|y-t|)}}{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi t}{2}}},\end{split}

(B.7)

since we are assuming the bound (
1.15
) for
\mathcal{I}
and
\mathcal{J}
.
It follows (given that
\delta
is positive) that if
y\geq t+1
then

\displaystyle|g_{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}}(s,t)|

\displaystyle\ll(t^{\frac{1}{2}}(y+t)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\delta}{2}}(y-t)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\delta}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}(y-t)})^{3}\ll t^{\frac{3}{2}}(r+t)^{\frac{3\sigma}{2}-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{3\delta}{2}}r^{\frac{3\sigma}{2}-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{3\delta}{2}}e^{-\frac{3\pi r}{2}},

where we have used that
y+t\asymp y
, and that
0\leq\sigma\leq A
, and have made the variable
change
r=y-t
. By considering separately, the cases
1\leq r\leq t
and
r>t
,
we obtain that
|g_{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}}(s,t)|\ll(r+t)^{2}t^{3\sigma-1}

which establishes (
B.5
) in the case
y\geq t+1
.
For
y\in[\sqrt{t},t-1]
and
\frac{1}{2}\leq\sigma\leq A
, we have by (
B.7
)

\displaystyle|g_{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}}(s,t)|

\displaystyle\ll\Big(\frac{(y+t)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\delta}{2}}(t-y)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\delta}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi t}{2}}}{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi t}{2}}}\Big)^{3}\ll\Big(\frac{t^{\sigma-\frac{1}{2}+\delta}}{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\Big)^{3}=t^{3(\sigma+\delta)}\ll y^{2}t^{3\sigma-1+3\delta},

since
y>\sqrt{t}
.
For
y\in[\sqrt{t},t-1]
and
\sigma\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
, we obtain

\displaystyle|g_{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}}(s,t)|

\displaystyle\ll\Big(\frac{(y+t)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\delta}{2}}(t-y)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\delta}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi t}{2}}}{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi t}{2}}}\Big)^{3}\ll\Big(\frac{t^{\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\delta}{2}}(t-y)^{\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\delta}{2}}}{t^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\Big)^{3}

\displaystyle\ll t^{\frac{3\sigma}{2}+\frac{3}{4}+\frac{3\delta}{2}}(t-y)^{\frac{3\sigma}{2}-\frac{3}{4}+\frac{3\delta}{2}}\ll y^{2}t^{3\sigma-1+3\delta}.

(B.8)

In the case that
\sigma\in[0,\frac{1}{2})
this can be checked by considering the function
h(y)=y^{2}(t-y)^{\frac{3}{4}-\frac{3\sigma}{2}}

on the interval
[\sqrt{t},t-1]
.
Elementary calculus shows that the minimum of
h
on this interval is
\gg t^{\frac{7}{4}-\frac{3\sigma}{2}}

and therefore (
B.8
) follows.
Now, if
y\in[t-1,t+1]
then, for
|b|\leq\delta
, one has

|\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+b-it+s))}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+b-it))}|\ll_{A}\frac{1}{|\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+b-it))|}\ll t^{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\delta}{2}}e^{\frac{\pi t}{4}}

(by (
B.6
)) and thus

\begin{split}\Bigg|\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+a+it+s))\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+b-it+s))}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+a+it))\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+b-it))}\Bigg|&\ll\frac{|t+a^{\prime\prime}+y|^{\frac{\sigma}{2}-\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\delta}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|\frac{t+y+a^{\prime\prime}}{2}|}}{|t|^{-\frac{1}{4}}e^{-\frac{\pi t}{4}}}\cdot(t^{\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\delta}{2}}e^{\frac{\pi t}{4}})\ll t^{\frac{\sigma}{2}+\frac{1}{4}+\delta}\end{split}

for
|a|,|b|\leq\delta
.
Therefore
|g_{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}}(s,t)|\ll(t^{\frac{\sigma}{2}+\frac{1}{4}+\delta})^{3}\ll y^{2}t^{3\sigma-1+3\delta}
, since

y\in[t-1,t+1]
and
\sigma\geq 0
. The cases for
y\leq-\sqrt{t}
are proven in a similar fashion.
∎

B.2
Partial derivative bounds. Proof of Lemma
2.2
(iii)

Proof.

For
1\leq j\leq 3
, let
p_{j}(s,t)=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+a_{j}+it+s))\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+b_{j}-it+s))}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+a_{j}+it))\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+b_{j}-it))}
,

\theta_{j}(s,t)=\frac{d}{dt}\log p_{j}(s,t)
, and

\Theta(s,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\theta_{j}(s,t)
.
Observe that

\frac{d}{dt}g_{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}}(s,t)=g_{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}}(s,t)\Theta(s,t)

(B.9)

and more generally, for
i\geq 1
,

\frac{d^{i}}{dt^{i}}g_{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}}(s,t)=\sum_{u+v=i-1}\binom{i-1}{u}\frac{d^{u}}{dt^{u}}g_{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}}(s,t)\frac{d^{v}}{dt^{v}}\Theta(s,t).

(B.10)

We shall now demonstrate that

\frac{d^{v}}{dt^{v}}\theta_{j}(s,t)\ll|s|t^{-v-1}\text{ for }j=1,2,3.

(B.11)

From this it follows directly that one has

\frac{d^{v}}{dt^{v}}\Theta(s,t)\ll|s|t^{-v-1}.

(B.12)

Using these facts, we can prove the Lemma by induction.
Observe that Lemma
2.2
(ii), (
B.12
) with
v=0
, and (
B.9
) imply

\frac{d}{dt}g_{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}}(s,t)\ll|s|T^{3\varepsilon+3\delta-1}
.
This, together with Lemma
2.2
(ii), establishes the Lemma in the cases
i=0
and
i=1
.
Now assume the inductive hypothesis that, for some
j\in\mathbb{N}
one has
\frac{d^{u}}{dt^{u}}g_{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}}(s,t)\ll|s|^{u}T^{3\varepsilon+3\delta-u}
for
u\leq j-1
.
Combining this with (
B.12
) and (
B.10
),
we obtain the case
i=j
of the bound in (
2.14
), and so (by induction) we find that
(
2.14
) holds for
for all
i\geq 0
,
\Re(s)=\varepsilon
, and
|\Im(s)|\leq\sqrt{T}
.
Thus to complete our proof of Lemma
2.2
(iii) we must establish (
B.11
).
Note that

\displaystyle\theta_{j}(s,t)=\frac{i}{2}

\displaystyle\Bigg(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+a_{j}+it+s))}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+a_{j}+it+s))}-\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+a_{j}+it))}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+a_{j}+it))}-\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+b_{j}-it+s))}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+b_{j}-it+s))}+\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+b_{j}-it))}{\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+b_{j}-it))}\Bigg).

However, we have the asymptotic expansion

\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}(z)=\log z+O(|z|^{-1})

and thus, when
y=\Im(s)
,

\displaystyle\theta_{j}(s,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\Big(-\log\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}+a_{j}+it\Big)\Big)+\log\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}+a_{j}+it+s\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle-\log\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}+b_{j}-it+s\Big)\Big)+\log\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}+b_{j}-it\Big)\Big)\Big)+O(t^{-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}(\log(t+y)-\log(t)-\log|y-t|+\log(t))+O(t^{-1})

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2}\Big(\log\Big(1+\frac{y}{t}\Big)-\log\Big(1-\frac{y}{t}\Big)\Big)+O(t^{-1})\ll\frac{1+|y|}{t}\ll\frac{|s|}{t}

since
|y|\leq\sqrt{T}
and
\Re(s)=\varepsilon>0
. We now study the derivatives of
\theta_{j}
.
We have

\displaystyle\frac{d^{v}}{dt^{v}}\theta_{j}(s,t)=\Big(\frac{i}{2}\Big)^{v+1}

\displaystyle\Big(\Big(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\Big)^{(v)}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}+a_{j}+it+s\Big)\Big)-\Big(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\Big)^{(v)}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}+a_{j}+it\Big)\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle+\Big(\Big(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\Big)^{(v)}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}+b_{j}-it+s\Big)\Big)-\Big(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma}\Big)^{(v)}\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{1}{2}+b_{j}-it\Big)\Big)\Big)\Big(\frac{-i}{2}\Big)^{v+1}.

It is known (see
[
1
]
) that

(\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}}{\Gamma})^{(v)}(z)=\frac{(-1)^{v-1}(v-1)!}{z^{v}}+O(\frac{1}{|z|^{v+1}})
for
\Re(z)>0

and thus

\begin{split}\frac{d^{v}}{dt^{v}}\theta_{j}(s,t)=(-1)^{v-1}(v-1)!\Big(\frac{i}{2}\Big)^{v+1}&\Bigg(\frac{1}{(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+a_{j}+it+s))^{v}}-\frac{1}{(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+a_{j}+it))^{v}}\\
&+\frac{1}{(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+b_{j}-it+s))^{v}}-\frac{1}{(\frac{1}{2}(\frac{1}{2}+b_{j}-it))^{v}}\Bigg)+O(t^{-v-1}).\end{split}

(B.13)

Note that
\alpha_{j}=\frac{\frac{1}{2}+a_{j}+it}{\frac{1}{2}+a_{j}+it+s}
satisfies

|\alpha_{j}^{-1}-1|=\frac{|s|}{|\frac{1}{2}+a_{j}+it|}<\frac{|s|}{t-\delta}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+T}}{t-\delta}<\frac{c_{1}}{\sqrt{T}}<\frac{1}{2},

where
c_{1}>0
and the last inequality holds
when
T
is sufficiently large. This implies that
|\alpha_{j}^{-1}|>\frac{1}{2}

and also
|1-\alpha_{j}|<\frac{4|s|}{t}<1
, for
T
sufficiently large. From these inequalities and the binomial theorem
it follows that
\alpha_{j}^{\nu}-1\ll_{\nu}\frac{|s|}{t}
for
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
.
Multiplying this last inequality by
2^{\nu}(\frac{1}{2}+a_{j}+it)^{-\nu}
and using

|\frac{1}{2}+a_{j}+it|>t/2
, we obtain that the first two terms in the
brackets in (
B.13
) sum to
\ll_{\nu}\frac{|s|}{t^{\nu+1}}
. The final two terms in the brackets
may be treated similarly. We may therefore deduce from (
B.13
) that

\frac{d^{v}}{dt^{v}}\theta_{j}(s,t)\ll_{\nu}\frac{|s|+1}{t^{\nu+1}}
. Since this shows that
(
B.12
) holds (given that we have
\Re(s)=\varepsilon>0
), the proof is now complete.
∎

B.3
Proof of Lemma
5.3

Next we provide a proof of Lemma
5.3
.
We aim to establish the following result:
Let
M\ll T^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}
,
N\asymp M
,
0\neq r\ll\frac{M}{T_{0}}T^{\varepsilon}
,
and
(x,y)\in[M,2M]\times[N,2N]
.
Then

x^{m}y^{n}f_{r}^{(m,n)}(x,y)\ll T^{\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon}P^{n}\text{ where }P=\Big(\frac{T}{T_{0}}\Big)T^{\varepsilon}.

(B.14)

Proof.

Recall that
f_{r}
is defined by (
5.18
). That is,
f_{r}(x,y)=W(\frac{x}{M})W(\frac{y}{N})\phi(x,y)

where

\phi(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\varepsilon)}\frac{G(s)}{s}\Big(\frac{1}{\pi^{3}xy}\Big)^{s}\frac{1}{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Big(1+\frac{r}{y}\Big)^{-it}g_{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}}(s,t)w(t)dt\,ds

when
x,y>0
(and is otherwise equal to
0
).
In order to prove this lemma it suffices to establish

x^{m}y^{n}\phi^{(m,n)}(x,y)\ll T^{\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon}P^{n}\text{ for }x\asymp M,y\asymp N\text{ where, }P=\Big(\frac{T}{T_{0}}\Big)T^{\varepsilon}.

(B.15)

Indeed, it follows from (
B.15
) (via two applications of the generalized product rule) that one has:

\begin{split}|f_{r}^{(m,n)}(x,y)|&=\Big|\sum_{i_{1}+i_{2}=m}\binom{m}{i_{1}}W^{(i_{1})}\Big(\frac{x}{M}\Big)M^{-i_{1}}\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=n}\binom{n}{j_{1}}W^{(j_{1})}\Big(\frac{y}{N}\Big)N^{-j_{1}}\phi^{(i_{2},j_{2})}(x,y)\Big|\\
&\leq\sum_{i_{1}+i_{2}=m}2^{m}O_{i_{1}}(1)\Big(\frac{x}{2}\Big)^{-i_{1}}\sum_{j_{1}+j_{2}=n}2^{n}O_{j_{1}}(1)\Big(\frac{y}{2}\Big)^{-j_{1}}\cdot x^{-i_{2}}y^{-j_{2}}\cdot O_{i_{2},j_{2}}(T^{\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon}P^{j_{2}})\\
&=\Big(\sum_{i_{1}=0}^{m}\sum_{j_{1}=0}^{n}O_{i_{1},j_{1},m,n}(1)\cdot P^{-j_{1}}\Big)T^{\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon}P^{n}x^{-m}y^{-n}\end{split}

where we have made use of
W(u)
being zero when
u\geq 2
.
Since
P\geq 1
(as follows by (
1.13
), for all sufficiently large
T
), this bound for
f_{r}^{(m,n)}(x,y)
implies that in (
B.14
).
We have now reduced the proof of the lemma to establishing (
B.15
).
We begin with

\phi^{(m,n)}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(\varepsilon)}\frac{G(s)}{s}\Big(\frac{1}{\pi^{3}}\Big)^{s}\frac{1}{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial x^{m}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial y^{n}}\Big(x^{-s}y^{-s}\Big(1+\frac{r}{y}\Big)^{-it}\Big)g_{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}}(s,t)\omega(t)dt\,ds.

(B.16)

It suffices to compute the partial derivatives in the last inner integral.
Observe that

\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}x^{-s}=P_{k}(s)x^{-s-k}\text{ where }P_{k}(s)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }k=0,\\
\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}(-s-j)&\text{if }k\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

(B.17)

It suffices to determine
\frac{d^{n}}{dy^{n}}y^{-s}(1+\frac{r}{y})^{-it}
for
n\geq 0
.
It may be shown by a straightforward induction proof that

\frac{d^{k}}{dy^{k}}\Big(1+\frac{r}{y}\Big)^{-it}=\Big(1+\frac{r}{y}\Big)^{-it}(y+r)^{-k}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\binom{k}{m}P_{k-m}(m)P_{m}(-it)\Big(\frac{r}{y}\Big)^{m}.

For
k\geq 1
, one gets (when
y\asymp N\asymp M
,
t\asymp T
, and

1\leq|r|\ll\frac{M}{T_{0}}T^{\varepsilon}=o(M)
and
P=(\frac{T}{T_{0}})T^{\varepsilon}\geq 1
):

\begin{split}\Big|\frac{d^{k}}{dy^{k}}\Big(1+\frac{r}{y}\Big)^{-it}\Big|&\leq|y(1+o(1)|^{-k}\sum_{m=1}^{k}2^{k}((k-1)!)\cdot O_{m}(T^{m})\cdot O_{m}\Big(\Big(\frac{P}{T}\Big)^{m}\Big)\\
&\ll_{k}y^{-k}P^{k}\cdot\sum_{m=1}^{k}O_{m}(1)\ll_{k}\Big(\frac{P}{y}\Big)^{k}.\end{split}

(B.18)

In the case
k=0
we have the same estimate, since
|u^{-it}|=1=(P/y)^{0}
for
u>0
.
By the generalized product rule, (
B.17
), and (
B.18
)

\begin{split}\frac{d^{n}}{dy^{n}}y^{-s}\Big(1+\frac{r}{y}\Big)^{-it}&=\sum_{j+k=n}\binom{n}{j}y^{-s-j}P_{j}(s)\frac{d^{k}}{dy^{k}}\Big(1+\frac{r}{y}\Big)^{-it}\ll\sum_{j+k=n}\binom{n}{j}N^{-\varepsilon-j}|s|^{j}\Big(\frac{P}{y}\Big)^{k}\\
&=N^{-\varepsilon}\Big(\frac{|s|}{N}+\frac{P}{y}\Big)^{n}\ll N^{-n-\varepsilon}(|s|+P)^{n}\end{split}

when
\Re(s)=\varepsilon
,
y\asymp N
,
t\asymp T
,

1\leq|r|\ll\frac{M}{T_{0}}T^{\varepsilon}=o(M)
and
P=(\frac{T}{T_{0}})T^{\varepsilon}\geq 1
, by (
1.13
).
By (
B.17
) it follows that

\begin{split}\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial x^{m}}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial y^{n}}\Big(x^{-s}y^{-s}\Big(1+\frac{r}{y}\Big)^{-it}\Big)&\ll x^{-\varepsilon-m}|s|^{m}\cdot N^{-n-\varepsilon}(P+|s|)^{n}\ll M^{-2\varepsilon}x^{-m}y^{-n}|s|^{m}(P+|s|)^{n}\\
&\ll M^{-2\varepsilon}(1+|s|)^{m+n}P^{n}x^{-m}y^{-n}.\end{split}

Using this, along with
\Big|\frac{G(s)}{s}\Big|\leq\frac{|G(s)|}{\varepsilon}
for
\Re(s)=\varepsilon
, and

|g_{\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}}(s,t)|\ll\Big(\frac{t}{2}\Big)^{3\varepsilon}(1+O(|s|^{2}+1))\ll(1+|s|)^{2}t^{3\varepsilon}\text{ for }\Re(s)=\varepsilon\text{ and }t\asymp T>1

(which follows trivially from Lemma
2.2
(ii))
we may bound (
B.16
). The three bounds,
and (
1.12
), (
1.13
),
combine to give

\begin{split}x^{m}y^{n}\phi^{(m,n)}(x,y)&\ll_{\varepsilon}P^{n}M^{-2\varepsilon}\int_{(\varepsilon)}|G(s)|\Big(\frac{1}{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(1+|s|)^{m+n+2}T^{3\varepsilon}|\omega(t)|dt\Big)|ds|\\
&\ll\Big(\frac{T^{3}}{M^{2}}\Big)^{\varepsilon}P^{n}\text{ for }x\asymp M,y\asymp N\text{ and }m,n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}.\end{split}

Finally, observe that
M\gg\frac{T_{0}}{T^{\varepsilon}}|r|\geq\frac{T_{0}}{T^{\varepsilon}}>T^{\frac{3}{4}}
(by (
1.11
)). Hence
\frac{T^{3}}{M^{2}}\ll T^{3-\frac{3}{2}}=T^{\frac{3}{2}}
and we establish Lemma
5.3
.
∎

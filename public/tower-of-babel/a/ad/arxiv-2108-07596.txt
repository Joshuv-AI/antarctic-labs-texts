Quantum electrodynamic effects on the two-stream instability
Antoine Bret

Abstract
We consider the quantum electrodynamic corrections to the two-stream instability. We find these corrections vanish at first order unless a guiding magnetic field 𝐁 0 \mathbf{B}_{0} is considered. With respect to the classical version of the instability, quantum electrodynamic effects reduce the most unstable wave vector and its growth rate by a factor 1 + ξ \sqrt{1+\xi} , with ξ = α 9 ​ π ​ ( B 0 / B c ​ r ) 2 \xi=\frac{\alpha}{9\pi}(B_{0}/B_{cr})^{2} , where α \alpha is the fine-structure constant and B c ​ r B_{cr} the Schwinger critical magnetic field. Although derived for a cold system, these results are valid for the kinetic case. The results are valid in the range ξ ≪ 1 \xi\ll 1 and, actually, up to linear corrections in ξ \xi .

The goal of this letter is to assess QED effects in relation to this instability. The system studied is sketched on Figure

A guiding magnetic field

We first briefly remind the derivation of the classical TSI in order to clearly see where QED corrections come into play.

How do QED effects modify this picture?

QED corrections to Eqs. (

3

[

\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E}=4\pi(\rho_{c}+\rho_{vac}),

(8)

where
\rho_{vac}

\rho_{vac}=-\frac{1}{180\pi^{2}}\frac{\alpha}{c^{2}B_{cr}^{2}}\nabla\cdot\left[2(E^{2}-c^{2}B^{2})\mathbf{E}+7c^{2}(\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B})\mathbf{B}\right],

(9)

B_{0}\ll B_{cr}

We can now establish the QED-modified dispersion equation for the TSI. The QED correction to the classical Poisson equation (
6

(14)

The strength of the QED corrections is therefore measured by,

\xi=\frac{\alpha}{9\pi}\frac{B_{0}^{2}}{B_{cr}^{2}}.

1+\xi=\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}}{\gamma_{0}^{3}(\omega-kv_{0})^{2}}+\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}}{\gamma_{0}^{3}(\omega+kv_{0})^{2}}.

In conclusion, QED corrections to the TSI require the presence of a guiding magnetic field to be non-zero at first order. This stands in contrast with the classical TSI where such a field has no effect. From Eqs. (
23
) we see that the most unstable
k
and the growth rate are both reduced by a factor
\sqrt{1+\xi}
, where
\xi\propto\alpha(B_{0}/B_{cr})^{2}
is given by Eq. (
15
).

To my knowledge, there is still no deep physical understanding of the TSI, a “Fermi-like picture”, beyond the mathematical resolution of its dispersion equation. It is then difficult to provide a full physical interpretation of the QED effects on TSI.

What can definitely be said is that in the linear regime, the perturbed field
\mathbf{B}_{1}
grows from 0. Therefore, for
\mathbf{B}_{0}+\mathbf{B}_{1}
to approach the Schwinger limit, a finite
B_{0}
is required, already close to
B_{cr}
. This is why QED effects for the instability vanish for
B_{0}=0
. If
B_{0}=0
, the field
B_{1}
alone will not reach
B_{cr}
in the linear regime of the instability. It would be interesting to determine in future works the conditions required for QED effects to play a role even if
B_{0}=0
.

The reduction of the growth rate could come from the shielding of charges by vacuum particles. Bare charges attract vacuum particles of the same charge and repel those of opposite charge, so that they are screened (
[
16
]
, p. 482). Indeed Eqs. (
23
) can be interpreted as a renormalization of the plasma frequency
\omega_{p}\rightarrow\omega_{p}/\sqrt{1+\xi}
, which in turn amounts to a renormalization of the elementary charge
q\rightarrow q/\sqrt{1+\xi}
, since
\omega_{p}\propto q
.

The present result can be straightforwardly extended to the case of electron beams of different densities as Eq. (
6
,
14
) read the same in that case. Also, Eqs. (
7
,
16
) allow to generalize the present results to the kinetic case, since the kinetic dispersion equation can be obtained from Eq. (
16
) by replacing the sum over the beams on the right-hand-side by an integral in velocity space
[
17
]
.

The general case where any orientations of
\mathbf{k}
are considered is more involved because Eq. (
11
) will bring more complex corrections to the dielectric tensor.

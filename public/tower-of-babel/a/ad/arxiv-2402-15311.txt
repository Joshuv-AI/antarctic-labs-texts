Scaling Limit of the Kuramoto Model on Random Geometric Graphs
Francisco Cirelli, Pablo Groisman, Ruojun Huang, Hernán Vivas

Abstract
. We consider the Kuramoto model on a graph with nodes given by n n i.i.d. points uniformly distributed on the d d dimensional torus. Two nodes are declared neighbors if they are at distance less than ϵ \epsilon . We prove a scaling limit for this model in compact time intervals as n → ∞ n\to\infty and ϵ → 0 \epsilon\to 0 such that ϵ d + 2 ​ n / log ⁡ n → ∞ \epsilon^{d+2}n/\log n\to\infty . The limiting object is given by the heat equation. On the one hand this shows that the nonlinearity given by the sine function disappears under this scaling and on the other hand, provides evidence that stable equilibria of the Kuramoto model on these graphs are, as n → ∞ n\to\infty , in correspondence with those of the heat equation, which are explicit and given by twisted states. In view of this, we conjecture the existence of twisted stable equilibria with high probability as n → ∞ n\to\infty .

Proposed model and main results

Given any positive integer

\epsilon=\epsilon(n)\in(0,1)

V=\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\}

n

\Lambda_{d}:=[0,2\pi]^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mathbb{T}^{d}=(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{d}

Let

be the quotient map to the torus and

d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(q(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}),q(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d}))=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|x_{i}-y_{i}\|^{2}_{\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}}}

2\pi
.

Remark 1.1
.

For technical reasons, we will extend the point cloud
V
on the box
\Lambda_{d}
to a point cloud
\tilde{V}
on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
periodically. To do that, we first copy the points

to every box of the form
\Pi_{\ell=1}^{d}[2\pi m_{\ell},2\pi(m_{\ell}+1)]

, and these boxes form a tiling of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Each point

x_{i}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}2\pi m_{\ell}\vec{e}_{\ell}
, where
\{\vec{e}_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{d}
is the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Clearly, if
\tilde{x}_{i}
is a copy of

.

Note that for each

among all other copies of

. An important fact to note is that
\|x_{i}-x^{i}_{j}\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}=d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(q(x_{i}),q(x_{j}))
.

From now on, we denote
|\cdot|=\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
. We denote with
\sigma_{d}
the volume of a unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
K:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a given non-negative,

function with compact support in the closure of the unit ball
\mathbb{B}(0,1)
such that
K(z)>0
for every

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K(z)dz=1.

We denote the finite constants

(1.2)

\displaystyle\kappa_{i}:=\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z|^{i}K(z)\,dz,\quad i=1,2.

We consider the homogeneous Kuramoto model on a weighted graph
G=(V,E)
, where
V=\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\}
and
\{x_{i},x_{j}\}\in E\Leftrightarrow d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(q(x_{i}),q(x_{j})=|x_{i}-x^{i}_{j}|<\epsilon
. Thus, two points are connected if and only if they are close when seen as points on the torus. The weights are given by
w_{ij}=K(\epsilon^{-1}(x^{i}_{j}-x_{i}))
. Note now, since
K
is radially symmetric, the weight is solely determined by the distance of the points in the torus. Let
u^{n}\colon[0,\infty)\times V\to\mathbb{R}
be the unique solution to a system of
n
Kuramoto equations

(1.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{d}{dt}}u^{n}(t,x_{i})=\displaystyle{\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}N_{i}}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sin\left(u^{n}(t,x_{j})-u^{n}(t,x_{i})\right)K\left(\epsilon^{-1}(x^{i}_{j}-x_{i})\right),\\[10.0pt]
u^{n}(0,x_{i})=u^{n}_{0}(x_{i}),\quad\quad i=1,2,...,n.\end{cases}

The random integer
N_{i}=|\mathcal{N}(i)|
denotes the cardinality of the set of neighbors of
x_{i}
, that we call

\mathcal{N}(i):=\{j\colon j\neq i,\,d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(q(x_{i}),q(x_{j}))=|x^{i}_{j}-x_{i}|<\epsilon\}\subset\{1,2,...,n\}\backslash\{i\}.

Our assumptions on the kernel
K
include the canonical choice of the indicator function on the unit ball (normalized to have integral
1
), in which case we get constant weight for points at distance smaller than
\epsilon
and zero otherwise. The Kuramoto model can be also interpreted as taking values in the unit circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}\cong\mathbb{T}
, where each
u^{n}(t,x_{i})
represents the phase of an oscillator, and this is done by performing

mod
2\pi
.

Remark 1.2
.

We extend the initial condition
u_{0}^{n}
from
V
to
\tilde{V}
in the following “pseudo-periodic” way: we will choose some
\{\tilde{k}_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

, then we set

u_{0}^{n}(y)=u_{0}^{n}(x_{i})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}2\pi\tilde{k}_{\ell}m_{\ell}.

.

Similarly, we extend
u^{n}
to
\tilde{V}
via

(1.4)

\displaystyle u^{n}(t,y):=u^{n}(t,x_{i})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}2\pi\tilde{k}_{\ell}m_{\ell},\quad\text{for all }t\geq 0.

Note that, with this extension, for
x_{i}\in V

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sin\left(u^{n}(t,x_{j})-u^{n}(t,x_{i})\right)K\left(\epsilon^{-1}(x^{i}_{j}-x_{i})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\sin\left(u^{n}(t,x^{i}_{j})-u^{n}(t,x_{i})\right)K\left(\epsilon^{-1}(x^{i}_{j}-x_{i})\right)

(1.5)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in\tilde{V}}\sin\left(u^{n}(t,y)-u^{n}(t,x_{i})\right)K\left(\epsilon^{-1}(y-x_{i})\right)

where the last equality stems from the fact that

and at most one copy of each

Throughout the paper we use the notation

(1.6)

\displaystyle[y]:=y\text{ mod }2\pi\quad\in\Lambda_{d},

the modulo being performed component-wise, for any

For any fixed

We are interested in the Kuramoto model in graphs with this structure, on the one hand since they are ubiquitous when modeling interacting oscillators with spatial structure, and on the other hand because they form a large family of model networks with persistent behavior (robust to small perturbations) for which we expect to have twisted states as stable equilibria. Definition 1.3

We say that a function

Lemma 1.4

To any continuous function
f:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{T}
corresponds a pseudo-periodic continuous function
\tilde{f}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, whose winding numbers are determined by

. We call
\tilde{f}

. If

\tilde{f}:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}

Proof.

Let
p:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{S}^{1}
be the standard universal cover of
\mathbb{S}^{1}

p(x)=e^{\mathbf{i}x}
where
\mathbf{i}

f\circ q:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{T}

Since

Given any other

\tilde{g}(0)\in p^{-1}(f\circ q(0))

For the converse result, given a continuous pseudo-periodic

Utilizing the fact that
{1_{[0,\infty)}\times p:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}

Twisted states are expected to be robust and persistent in several contexts (since they can be observed in nature), but situations in which they can be computed explicitly (and hence proving their existence) are not. In our model, we expect to have twisted states as a generic property, i.e. we expect them to exist with high probability as
n\to\infty
and to persist if one adds or removes one or a finite number of points; or if one applies small perturbations to the points in a generic way.

Although the existence of such steady-states cannot be deduced directly from our arguments, we think that our results provide evidence of their ubiquity as a robust phenomenon.

Twisted states and their stability have also been studied in small-world networks
[
32
]
and in the continuum limit
[
35
]
among others.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{x_{i}\in V}\left|N_{i}-\frac{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d}n}{(2\pi)^{d}}\right|>\lambda\right)\leq 2n\exp\left\{-\frac{2\lambda^{2}}{\frac{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d}n}{(2\pi)^{d}}+\frac{\lambda}{3}}\right\},\quad\lambda>0,

which implies the previous statement.

By means of this identification, we can compare solutions of (
1.3
) with solutions of (
1.8
) and that is the purpose of our main theorem below. Denote
\|u^{n}_{0}-u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}=\sup_{x_{i}\in V}|u^{n}_{0}(x_{i})-u_{0}(x_{i})|

Theorem 1.7
.

Let

be fixed,
u^{n}:[0,T]\times V\to\mathbb{R}
be the unique solution of (

) with initial condition

, and
u:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}

for some

. Assume Condition

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{x_{i}\in V,\,t\in[0,T]}|u^{n}(t,x_{i})-u(t,x_{i})|=0,\quad\text{almost surely.}

Proof.

The proof is a consequence of Proposition

∎

Remark 1.8
.

We can give a quantitative bound on the difference between Kuramoto solution and the heat equation. This is because most parts of our proof are non-asymptotic. Fixing
T
finite and pseudo-periodic
\tilde{u}_{0}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there exist some finite constants

,
C_{2}=C_{2}(\alpha,\tilde{u}_{0},T)
and

,

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\biggl(\sup_{x_{i}\in V,\,t\in[0,T]}|u^{n}(t,x_{i})-u(t,x_{i})|>\delta+C_{2}\epsilon^{\alpha}\biggr)\leq C_{1}n^{3}e^{-C_{1}^{-1}\epsilon^{d+2}n\delta^{2}}+2\mathbb{P}\left(\|u^{n}_{0}-u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}>\delta/4\right).

This bound can be seen by combining Proposition

and (

As a consequence we obtain evidence of the existence of patterns. In particular, we prove that the system (
1.3
) remains close to a continuous twisted state for times as large as we want by taking

Remark 1.10
.

From a scaling limits point of view (and moving away from the Kuramoto context), it may seem a bit “trivial” that our limit equation is the heat equation, which either could be read as good news or bad news. On the one hand, obtaining such a simple equation helps to easily identify the behavior of the system in this scale. On the other hand, one could aim for more interesting objects in the limit. is smooth). Identifying the limit equation, particularly the form of the differential operator, appears to be an intriguing and nontrivial problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\frac{d}{dt}}u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x)=\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\sin\left(u^{I,\epsilon}(t,y)-u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x)\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy,\\[10.0pt]

where

\epsilon

is fixed. We will prove:

(1)

and pseudo-periodic

;

(2)

comparison principles;

(3)

a spatial Lipschitz estimate
uniform in
\epsilon
(assuming
\tilde{u}_{0}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
);

(4)

uniform convergence of solutions of (

) to solutions of the heat equation (
1.8

for some

).

2.1.
Existence

The following proposition gives existence and uniqueness of solutions to (
2.1
). We will follow a fixed point procedure; let us integrate (
2.1
) with respect to time to get

(2.2)

u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x)=\tilde{u}_{0}(x)+\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\sin\left(u^{I,\epsilon}(s,y)-u^{I,\epsilon}(s,x)\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy\>ds.

We see that finding a solution of the integral equation (
2.1
) is equivalent to finding a pseudo-periodic
u^{I,\epsilon}:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
with

{u^{I,\epsilon}\in C\big([0,\infty);C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})\big)}

satisfying (

).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
F(x):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}g(x,y)\mathsf{K}(y-x)dy
, where

g(x,y)\in C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
(with bounded
C^{1}
-norm) and
\mathsf{K}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
with compact support, then the following derivative formula holds:

\displaystyle\nabla F(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\left[\nabla_{x}g(x,y)+\nabla_{y}g(x,y)\right]\mathsf{K}(y-x)dy.

Proof.

Consider a sequence of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
functions
\{\mathsf{K}_{n}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\mathsf{K}_{n}\to\mathsf{K}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Let

(2.3)

\displaystyle F_{n}(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(x,y)\mathsf{K}_{n}(y-x)dy

and clearly we have that

\displaystyle\nabla F_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\nabla_{x}g(x,y)\mathsf{K}_{n}(y-x)+g(x,y)\nabla_{x}(\mathsf{K}_{n}(y-x))\right]dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\nabla_{x}g(x,y)\mathsf{K}_{n}(y-x)-g(x,y)\nabla_{y}(\mathsf{K}_{n}(y-x))\right]dy

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\nabla_{x}g(x,y)+\nabla_{y}g(x,y)\right]\mathsf{K}_{n}(y-x)dy,

where the last step is due to integration by parts, and due to
\mathsf{K}_{n}
being compactly supported, there are no boundary terms. Since
g\in C^{1}_{b}
(with bounded
C^{1}
-norm), we have for any
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla F_{n}(x)-\nabla F_{m}(x)|\leq 2\|g\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\|\mathsf{K}_{n}-\mathsf{K}_{m}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

We also have that

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|F_{n}(x)-F_{m}(x)|\leq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\|\mathsf{K}_{n}-\mathsf{K}_{m}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

In other words,

\displaystyle\|F_{n}-F_{m}\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq 3\|g\|_{C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\|\mathsf{K}_{n}-\mathsf{K}_{m}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where the
C^{1}
-norm of
g
is defined as usual
\|g\|_{C^{1}}:=\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla_{x}g\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\nabla_{y}g\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

Since
\{\mathsf{K}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is Cauchy in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have that
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is Cauchy in
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and we call
\lim_{n\to\infty}F_{n}=:\widetilde{F}
the limit. Further, since
\mathsf{K}
is the
L^{1}
-limit of
\mathsf{K}_{n}
, passing to
n\to\infty
on both sides of (
2.3
) and comparing with the formula of
F
, we see that
\widetilde{F}=F
. Then passing to
n\to\infty
on both sides of (
2.4
) we see that the claimed formula for
\nabla F
holds.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
u(t,x):[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function in
(t,x)
, pseudo-periodic in
x
for every
t
, then the function

U(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sin\left(u(t,y)-u(t,x)\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy

is
2\pi
-periodic in
x
in each coordinate direction, for every
t
.

Proof.

Let
\{k_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
denote the winding numbers of
u(t,\cdot)
for every
t\geq 0
(which by Lemma
1.4
are time-independent and determined by
u|_{t=0}
). We have, for every
\ell=1,2,...,d
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle U(t,x+2\pi\vec{e}_{\ell})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sin\left(u(t,y)-u(t,x+2\pi\vec{e}_{\ell})\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x-2\pi\vec{e}_{\ell}}{\epsilon}\Big)dy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sin\left(u(t,y^{\prime}+2\pi\vec{e}_{\ell})-u(t,x+2\pi\vec{e}_{\ell})\right)K\Big(\frac{y^{\prime}-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sin\left(u(t,y^{\prime})+2\pi k_{\ell}-u(t,x)-2\pi k_{\ell}\right)K\Big(\frac{y^{\prime}-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sin\left(u(t,y^{\prime})-u(t,x)\right)K\Big(\frac{y^{\prime}-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy^{\prime}=U(t,x),

where in the second equality we made a change of variables
y=y^{\prime}+2\pi\vec{e}_{\ell}
, and in the third equality we used the pseudo-periodicity of
u(t,\cdot)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Fix any
\epsilon>0
. Given any pseudo-periodic
\tilde{u}_{0}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
, there exists a unique pseudo-periodic function
u^{I,\epsilon}\in C\left([0,\infty);C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})\right)
satisfying (
2.2
) and hence a unique solution of (
2.1
).

Proof.

Solutions of (
2.2
) are fixed points of the operator

(2.5)

\displaystyle F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}(u^{I,\epsilon})(t,x):=\tilde{u}_{0}(x)+\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}\sin\left(u^{I,\epsilon}(s,y)-u^{I,\epsilon}(s,x)\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy\>ds.

For a fixed pseudo-periodic initial condition
\tilde{u}_{0}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
and a positive
T
(to be chosen later) we consider the Banach space

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{T}:=\Big\{f:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}:\;

\displaystyle f(t,\cdot)\text{ is pseudo-periodic},\,\,f\in C\big([0,T];C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})\big),

(2.6)

\displaystyle f|_{t=0}=\tilde{u}_{0},\;\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{1}(\Lambda_{d})}\leq 1+\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{C^{1}(\Lambda_{d})}\Big\}

with the norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{X}_{T}}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{1}(\Lambda_{d})},

where by an abuse of notation, for a pseudo-periodic function
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, we write
\|g\|_{C^{1}(\Lambda_{d})}
for
\|g|_{\Lambda_{d}}\|_{C^{1}(\Lambda_{d})}
. We immediately remark that the map
F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}
(
2.5
) takes a pseudo-periodic continuous function to another pseudo-periodic continuous function, with the same winding numbers. This is a consequence of Lemma
2.2
and that
\tilde{u}_{0}
is pseudo-periodic.

We want to apply Banach’s fixed point theorem to
F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}
in
\mathcal{X}_{T}
. We must check:

(a)

F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}(\mathcal{X}_{T})\subset\mathcal{X}_{T}
;

(b)

F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}
is a contraction, i.e. there exists
\nu\in(0,1)
such that

\|F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}(u^{I,\epsilon})-F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}(v)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{T}}\leq\nu\|u-v\|_{\mathcal{X}_{T}}

for all
u,v\in\mathcal{X}_{T}
.

We first show (b). We first observe that
h(x,y):=\sin\left(u(t,y)-u(t,x)\right)
has bounded
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
-norm for each
t
. Indeed,
\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}\leq 1
and furthermore,

\nabla_{y}h(x,y)=\cos\left(u(t,y)-u(t,x)\right)\nabla_{y}u(t,y).

Since
u(t,y)
is pseudo-periodic,
\|\nabla_{y}u(t,y)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
is equal to
\|\nabla_{y}u(t,y)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Lambda_{d})}
, and therefore is bounded. Hence
\|\nabla_{y}h(x,y)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}
is bounded, and similarly
\|\nabla_{x}h(x,y)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})}
is bounded as well.
We are in a situation to apply Lemma
2.1
, whereby for any
x\in\Lambda_{d}
,

\displaystyle\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}|\nabla F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}(u)(t,x)-\nabla F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}(v)(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\cos\left(u(s,y)-u(s,x)\right)\nabla u(s,y)-\cos\left(v(s,y)-v(s,x)\right)\nabla v(s,y)\Big|K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy\>ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\cos\left(u(s,y)-u(s,x)\right)\nabla u(s,x)-\cos\left(v(s,y)-v(s,x)\right)\nabla v(s,x)\Big|K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy\>ds.

We notice that although we consider
x\in\Lambda_{d}
, there may be some
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
that are within Euclidean distance
\epsilon
from
x
but are outside of
\Lambda_{d}
. Since
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, such
y
must be in one of the adjacent boxes. We must have either
y_{\ell}=[y]_{\ell}\pm 2\pi
or
y_{\ell}=[y]_{\ell}
,
\ell=1,2,...,d
(see (
1.6
)). For
g
that is pseudo-periodic with winding numbers
\{k_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{d}
, we have
g([y])=g(y)+\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}a_{\ell}k_{\ell}\vec{e}_{\ell}
for some
a_{\ell}\in\{0,\pm 2\pi\}
and
\nabla g([y])=\nabla g(y)
. Hence, since the cosine is a
2\pi
-periodic function and
u,v
are pseudo-periodic, the previous display equals

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\cos\left(u(s,[y])-u(s,x)\right)\nabla u(s,[y])-\cos\left(v(s,[y])-v(s,x)\right)\nabla v(s,[y])\Big|K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy\>ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\cos\left(u(s,[y])-u(s,x)\right)\nabla u(s,x)-\cos\left(v(s,[y])-v(s,x)\right)\nabla v(s,x)\Big|K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy\>ds.

Then by the triangle inequality and
1
-Lipschitz property of sine function, we further bound it by

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(|\nabla u(s,[y])-\nabla v(s,[y])|

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\big(|u(s,[y])-v(s,[y])|+|u(s,x)-v(s,x)|\big)|\nabla v(s,[y])|\Big)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy\>ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(|\nabla u(s,x)-\nabla v(s,x)|

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+\big(|u(s,[y])-v(s,[y])|+|u(s,x)-v(s,x)|\big)|\nabla v(s,x)|\Big)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy\>ds.

Since
\|K\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=1
, applying Hölder’s inequality

\|fK(\frac{y-\cdot}{\epsilon})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|K(\frac{y-\cdot}{\epsilon})\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\epsilon^{d}\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

to the above integrals in
dy
, and noting that both
x,[y]\in\Lambda_{d}
, we get from above

(2.7)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\nabla F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}(u)(t,\cdot)-\nabla F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}(v)(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Lambda_{d})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(1+\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{C^{1}(\Lambda_{d})}\right)T\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|u(s,\cdot)-v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Lambda_{d})}+CT\sup_{s\in[0,T]}\|\nabla u(s,\cdot)-\nabla v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Lambda_{d})},

where the constant
C=C(\epsilon,d)
may change from line to line, and we used that since
v\in\mathcal{X}_{T}
(
2.1
), its

-norm in space is bounded by
1+\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{C^{1}(\Lambda_{d})}
up to time
T
.

Similarly, we can estimate

\displaystyle\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}|F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}(u)(t,x)-F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}(v)(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\sin\left(u(s,y)-u(s,x)\right)-\sin\left(v(s,y)-v(s,x)\right)\Big|K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy\>ds

\displaystyle={\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big|\sin\left(u(s,[y])-u(s,x)\right)-\sin\left(v(s,[y])-v(s,x)\right)\Big|K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy\>ds,}

which yields that

(2.8)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}(u)(t,\cdot)-F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}(v)(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Lambda_{d})}\leq C(\epsilon,d)T\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(s,\cdot)-v(s,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Lambda_{d})}.

Recalling the definition of
C^{1}(\Lambda_{d})
-norm, we get from (
2.7
)-(
2.8
)

\displaystyle\|F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}(u)-F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}(v)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{T}}\leq CT\left(1+\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{C^{1}(\Lambda_{d})}\right)\|u-v\|_{\mathcal{X}_{T}},

for some finite
C=C(\epsilon,d)
.

For requirement (a), a similar argument (just do not consider

) shows that

\displaystyle\|F_{\tilde{u}_{0}}(u)\|_{\mathcal{X}_{T}}\leq\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{C^{1}(\Lambda_{d})}+CT\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}_{T}}.

Choosing
T_{0}:=\frac{1}{2C\left(1+\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{C^{1}(\Lambda_{d})}\right)}
we get by Banach’s fixed point theorem local existence of a unique solution to (

) in the time interval
[0,T_{0}]
. We want to iterate the above argument and get global existence. We note that even though
T_{0}
depends inversely on
C^{1}
-norm of the initial condition, the dependence is linear. On the other hand, our construction (
2.1
) is such that each iteration only increases the norm of the solution by at most
1
. This means that, if we iterate the previous argument infinitely many times, the sum of the local existence intervals diverges and we can reach any
T
. Finally note that a continuous solution of (
2.2
) is in fact differentiable in
t
and as such, a solution of (
2.1
).
∎

2.2.
Comparison Principle and Lipschitz Estimate

This section is devoted to the proof of a comparison principle for (
2.1
) and the consequential uniform Lipschitz estimate. We start with the comparison principle.

Lemma 2.4
.

Fix
T
finite. Let
\Psi:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
be strictly positive and
v,w:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be two continuous functions, with continuous time derivative,
2\pi
-periodic in
x
that satisfy

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}v(t,x)-\mathcal{L}v(t,x)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{d}{dt}w(t,x)-\mathcal{L}w(t,x),

(2.10)

\displaystyle v(0,x)

\displaystyle\geq w(0,x),\quad x\in\Lambda_{d},\,t\in[0,T],

with

\mathcal{L}v(t,x):=\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi(t,x,y)\left(v(t,y)-v(t,x)\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy.

Then,

v(t,x)\geq w(t,x),\quad x\in\Lambda_{d},\,t\in[0,T].

Proof.

Assume that the conclusion of the lemma fails to hold, i.e. there exists
(t_{0},x_{0})\in[0,T]\times\Lambda_{d}
(not necessarily in the interior), such that

v(t_{0},x_{0})<w(t_{0},x_{0}).

We may assume that such a point is a minimum point for
v-w
on
[0,T]\times\Lambda_{d}
since
v,w
are continuous. Notice in particular that
t_{0}>0
, since
(v-w)|_{t=0}\geq 0
. By (
2.9
),

\frac{d}{dt}(v-w)(t_{0},x_{0})\geq\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi(t,x_{0},y)\left((v-w)(t_{0},y)-(v-w)(t_{0},x_{0})\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy.

But since
(t_{0},x_{0})
is a minimum for
v-w
on
[0,T]\times\Lambda_{d}
and
\Psi
is positive, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi(t,x_{0},y)\left((v-w)(t_{0},y)-(v-w)(t_{0},x_{0})\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi(t,x_{0},y)\left((v-w)(t_{0},[y])-(v-w)(t_{0},x_{0})\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy>0,

where we used the
2\pi
-periodicty of
v-w
and
[y]\in\Lambda_{d}
as defined in (
1.6
),
and

\frac{d}{dt}(v-w)(t_{0},x_{0})\leq 0,

thus we have a contradiction.
∎

From Lemma
2.4
we can deduce a Lipschitz estimate uniformly in
\epsilon
. Let us define

{\|f\|_{\rm Lip(\Lambda_{d})}:=\sup_{x\in\Lambda_{d},\,h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\}}\frac{|f(x+h)-f(x)|}{|h|}.}

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
T\in(0,\infty)
be fixed and
\tilde{u}_{0}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
be pseudo-periodic. Then there exist
\epsilon_{0}=\epsilon_{0}(T,\tilde{u}_{0})>0
and
C_{T}=C_{T}(\tilde{u}_{0})
finite such that if
\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
,
u^{I,\epsilon}
is the unique pseudo-periodic solution of (
2.1
) from Proposition
2.3
, then for every
0\leq t\leq T
,

\|u^{I,\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{\rm Lip(\Lambda_{d})}\leq C_{T}.

Remark 2.6
.

The uniform Lipschitz estimate will be used in the next Section
3
, but we point out that it is not needed for the proof of convergence of solutions of the integral equation to solutions of the heat equation.

Proof.

Fix
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\}
and consider
w_{h}(t,x):=u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x+h)-u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x)
. Since
u^{I,\epsilon}(t,\cdot)
is pseudo-periodic, we see that
w_{h}(t,\cdot)
is
2\pi
-periodic in each coordinate direction. This follows from (
1.9
). Using (
2.1
), we have that
w_{h}
satisfies for every
x\in\Lambda_{d}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle w_{h}(t,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\sin\left(u^{I,\epsilon}(t,y+h)-u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x+h)\right)-\sin\left(u^{I,\epsilon}(t,y)-u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x)\right)\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy\>dy,

by a change of variables, with
w_{h}|_{t=0}=\tilde{u}_{0}(x+h)-\tilde{u}_{0}(x)
.
Note that we can rewrite

\displaystyle\sin\left(u^{I,\epsilon}(t,y+h)-u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x+h)\right)-\sin\big(u^{I,\epsilon}(t,y)-u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x)\big)

\displaystyle=\Psi(t,x,y)\big(w_{h}(t,y)-w_{h}(t,x)\big),

with

\displaystyle\Psi(t,x,y)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{1}\cos\left(s(u^{I,\epsilon}(t,y+h)-u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x+h))+(1-s)(u^{I,\epsilon}(t,y)-u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x))\right)\>ds.

For some large constant
M
(to be specified), let us define a time

\displaystyle\tau_{M}:=\inf\big\{t\geq 0:\|u^{I,\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{\text{Lip}(\Lambda_{d})}\geq M\big\}.

We claim that, by taking
\epsilon
small enough (for instance
\epsilon<\pi/(4M)
), we can ensure that
\Psi(t,x,z)\geq c_{0}>0
for some constant
c_{0}=c_{0}(M)
, for any
t\in[0,\tau_{M})
. Indeed, the control of
\|u^{I,\epsilon}(t,\cdot)\|_{\text{Lip}(\Lambda_{d})}
up to
\tau_{M}
, together with the pseudo-periodicity of
u^{I,\epsilon}(t,\cdot)
, imply that for any
y
with
|y-x|\leq\epsilon
,

\displaystyle|u^{I,\epsilon}(t,y)

\displaystyle-u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x)|\leq M|y-x|\leq M\epsilon,

\displaystyle|u^{I,\epsilon}(t,y+h)

\displaystyle-u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x+h)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|u^{I,\epsilon}(t,y-x+[x+h])-u^{I,\epsilon}(t,[x+h])|\leq M|y-x|\leq M\epsilon.

In the last line, the difference of
u^{I,\epsilon}(t,\cdot)
at two different spatial points
y+h,x+h
is unchanged under a simultaneous translation by
[x+h]-(x+h)
(which is a multiple of
2\pi
in each coordinate direction), due to pseudo-periodicity of
u^{I,\epsilon}(t,\cdot)
, see (
1.9
). The argument of the cosine in
\Psi(x,z)
is thus uniformly close to
0
.

To apply the maximum principle, Lemma
2.4
, we next use the following barrier

\bar{w}(t,x):=(t+1)|h|\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{\rm Lip(\Lambda_{d})},\quad t\geq 0,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

(notice that this function is space independent). The function
\bar{w}
satisfies

\frac{d}{dt}\bar{w}(t,x)-\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi(t,x,y)\left(\bar{w}(t,y)-\bar{w}(t,x)\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy=\frac{d}{dt}\bar{w}=|h|\|u_{0}\|_{\rm Lip}\geq 0,

and as shown above,
w_{h}
satisfies

\frac{d}{dt}w_{h}(t,x)-\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi\left(t,x,y\right)\left(w_{h}(t,y)-w_{h}(t,x)\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dydy=0.

Also,
\bar{w}(0,x)\geq w_{h}(0,x)
for
x\in\Lambda_{d}
since due to
u^{I,\epsilon}|_{t=0}=\tilde{u}_{0}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

w_{h}(0,x)\leq|w_{h}(0,x)|=|\tilde{u}_{0}(x+h)-\tilde{u}_{0}(x)|\leq\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{\rm Lip(\Lambda_{d})}|h|=\bar{w}(0,x).

Hence, with both functions
w_{h},\bar{w}
continuous and
2\pi
-periodic, we satisfy the conditions of Lemma
2.4
which yields

\displaystyle w_{h}(t,x)\leq\bar{w}(t,x)=(t+1)|h|\|u_{0}\|_{\rm Lip(\Lambda_{d})},\quad t\in[0,\tau_{M}),\,x\in\Lambda_{d}.

An analogous reasoning for
-w_{h}
gives

\displaystyle-w_{h}(t,x)\leq(t+1)|h|\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{\rm Lip(\Lambda_{d})},\quad t\in[0,\tau_{M}),\,x\in\Lambda_{d}.

In other words, for
t\in[0,\tau_{M})
,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\Lambda_{d}}\frac{|u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x+h)-u^{I,\epsilon}(x)|}{|h|}\leq(t+1)\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{\rm Lip(\Lambda_{d})}.

Choosing
M=2(T+1)\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{\rm Lip(\Lambda_{d})}
then ensures that
\tau_{M}>T
, and hence (
2.11
) holds for any
t\in[0,T]
. Since the right-hand-side of (
2.11
) does not depend on
h
, we obtain the desired Lipschitz bound with
C_{T}:=(T+1)\|\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{\rm Lip(\Lambda_{d})}
.
∎

2.3.
Convergence to solutions of the heat equation

Next we show that solutions to the integral equation (
2.1
) converge to solutions of the heat equation (
1.8
) as
\epsilon\to 0
. Notice that we are assuming the same initial condition for the heat and integral equations.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
u:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be the unique pseudo-periodic solution of (
1.8
) and
u^{I,\epsilon}:[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
the unique pseudo-periodic solution of (
2.1
), with the same pseudo-periodic initial condition
u|_{t=0}=u^{I,\epsilon}|_{t=0}=\tilde{u}_{0}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then for any
T>0
there exists
C=C(T,\alpha,\tilde{u}_{0},K)>0
and
\epsilon_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for any
\epsilon<\epsilon_{0}
,

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|u^{I,\epsilon}(t,\cdot)-u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Lambda_{d})}\leq C\epsilon^{\alpha}.

Before proving Proposition
2.7
, we show pointwise convergence of the integral operator to the Laplacian.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
v\in C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
be pseudo-periodic, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, then there exists a finite constant
\tilde{C}
depending only on
K,d
and the
C^{2,\alpha}(\Lambda^{\prime}_{d})
-norm of
v
such that for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)

\sup_{x\in\Lambda_{d}}\Big|\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sin\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy-\frac{\kappa_{2}}{2d}\Delta v(x)\Big|\leq\tilde{C}\epsilon^{\alpha},

where
\Lambda^{\prime}_{d}:=[-1,2\pi+1]^{d}
is a
1
-enlargement of
\Lambda_{d}
.

Proof.

Note that for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
it holds

|\sin t-t|\leq t^{3}.

Indeed, let us check this for
t\geq 0
, and the case
t<0
follows since the involved functions are odd. Let
h(t):=t-\sin t-t^{3}
which satisfies
h(0)=0
and
h^{\prime}(t)=1-\cos t-3t^{2}=2\sin^{2}\frac{t}{2}-3t^{2}\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2}-3t^{2}\leq 0
, and thus
h(t)\leq h(0)=0
for all
t\geq 0
. Let us denote
E(t):=\sin t-t
and we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sin\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy+\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}E\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy

\displaystyle=:(A)+(B).

Regarding the first term (A), since
v\in C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have that

\Big|v(y)-v(x)-Dv(x)\cdot(y-x)-\frac{1}{2}D^{2}v(x)(y-x)\cdot(y-x)\Big|\leq\tilde{C}|x-y|^{2+\alpha}

for all
x\in\Lambda_{d}
,
|y-x|\leq 1
and some finite constant
\tilde{C}
that depends on the
C^{2,\alpha}(\Lambda^{\prime}_{d})
-norm of
v
. Hence,

\displaystyle\Big|(A)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Dv(x)\cdot(y-x)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{2}D^{2}v(x)(y-x)\cdot(y-x)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy\right)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{C}|x-y|^{2+\alpha}K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy=\frac{\epsilon^{\alpha}}{\sigma_{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{C}|z|^{2+\alpha}K(z)dz

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}\epsilon^{\alpha}.

Since
K
is radially symmetric, by symmetry of the integrands we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Dv(x)\cdot(y-x)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy=0,\quad\delta_{i\neq j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{2}D_{ij}v(x)(y-x)_{i}(y-x)_{j}K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy=0,

and recalling the constant
\kappa_{2}
(
1.2
)

\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{d}D_{ii}v(x)(y-x)^{2}_{i}K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy=\frac{\kappa_{2}}{2d}\Delta v(x).

This shows that

\Big|(A)-\frac{\kappa_{2}}{2d}\Delta v(x)\Big|\leq\tilde{C}\epsilon^{\alpha}.

Turning to the second term (B), for any
x\in\Lambda_{d}
and
|y-x|\leq 1
we have

\displaystyle|(B)|=\Big|\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}E\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|E\left(v(y)-v(x)\right)|K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|v(y)-v(x)|^{3}K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\|v\|_{C^{1}(\Lambda^{\prime}_{d})}^{3}|y-x|^{3}K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy=\frac{\epsilon}{\sigma_{d}}\|v\|_{C^{1}(\Lambda^{\prime}_{d})}^{3}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|z|^{3}K(z)dz

\displaystyle=\tilde{C}\epsilon,

for some possibly different
\tilde{C}
.
∎

We are ready to prove the convergence of
u^{I,\epsilon}
to
u
.

Proof of Proposition
2.7
.

Since
\tilde{u}_{0}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, parabolic regularity theory (Schauder estimates) guarantees that the unique solution of the heat equation
u(t,x)
is
C^{2,\alpha}
in space and
C^{1,\alpha/2}
in time, cf.
[
26
, Theorem 9.1.2]
. More precisely, since the supremum norm of
\tilde{u}_{0}
over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is infinite when the winding numbers are non-zero, to apply the standard Schauder theorem we can first subtract the “tilt”
\tilde{u}_{0}(x)-\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}k_{\ell}x\cdot\vec{e}_{\ell}
from the initial condition, solve the heat equation with such initial condition in the correct Hölder space, and then add back the “tilt”.
Let

w^{\epsilon}(t,x):=u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x)-u(t,x).

Since
u^{I,\epsilon},u
are both pseudo-periodic, continuous, with the same pseudo-periodic initial condition
\tilde{u}_{0}
, they have the same time-independent winding numbers. Hence,
w^{\epsilon}(t,\cdot)
is
2\pi
-periodic in each coordinate direction.
We write

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}w^{\epsilon}(t,x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\sin\left(u^{I,\epsilon}(t,y)-u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x)\right)-\sin\left(u(t,y)-u(t,x)\right)\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy+E_{\epsilon}(u)(t,x),

where

(2.12)

E_{\epsilon}(u)(t,x):=\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sin\left(u(t,y)-u(t,x)\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy-\frac{1}{2}\kappa_{2}\Delta u(x).

For the pseudo-periodic solution
u(t,x)
of the heat equation (
1.8
), during time interval
[0,T]
its spatial
C^{2,\alpha}(\Lambda^{\prime}_{d})
-norm is uniformly bounded by a constant that depends on
\tilde{u}_{0},T,d
, and hence thanks to Lemma
2.8
, the error term

(2.13)

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T],\,x\in\Lambda_{d}}|E_{\epsilon}(u)(t,x)|\leq\tilde{C}\epsilon^{\alpha}

for some finite constant
\tilde{C}=\tilde{C}(\tilde{u}_{0},T,d,K)
independent of
\epsilon
.

Now, for any
x\in\Lambda_{d}
,

\displaystyle\sin\left(u^{I,\epsilon}(t,y)-u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x)\right)-\sin\left(u(t,y)-u(t,x)\right)

\displaystyle=\Psi(t,x,y)\left(w^{\epsilon}(t,y)-w^{\epsilon}(t,x)\right)

where

\displaystyle\Psi(t,x,y)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{1}\cos\left(s\left(u^{I,\epsilon}(t,y)-u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x)\right)+(1-s)\left(u(t,y)-u(t,x)\right)\right)ds,

Notice that, due to the uniform (in
\epsilon
) Lipschitz bound on
u^{I,\epsilon}(t,\cdot)
(see Lemma
2.5
) and
u(t,\cdot)
in the time interval
[0,T]
, for
y
with
|y-x|\leq\epsilon
, the argument of the cosine in
\Psi(t,x,y)
can be chosen to be uniformly close to
0
(by making
\epsilon
small enough), and
\Psi(t,x,y)
bounded uniformly away from
0
.

We want to apply the comparison principle Lemma
2.4
. Let

\bar{w}(t,x):=C_{1}t\epsilon^{\alpha}+C_{2}\epsilon

(notice that this function is space-independent) so that

\frac{d}{dt}\bar{w}(t,x)-\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Psi(t,x,y)\left(\bar{w}(t,y)-\bar{w}(t,x)\right)K\Big(\frac{y-x}{\epsilon}\Big)dy=\frac{d}{dt}\bar{w}(t,x)=C_{1}\epsilon^{\alpha}.

Thanks to (
2.12
) we can choose
C_{1}>\tilde{C}
of (
2.13
) such that
\bar{w}
and
w^{\epsilon}
satisfy (
2.9
) and choosing
C_{2}>0
we can also get (
2.10
) (since
w^{\epsilon}|_{t=0}=0
). Then, since
w^{\epsilon},\bar{w}
are both continuous and
2\pi
-periodic, Lemma
2.4
yields

u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x)-u(t,x)=w^{\epsilon}(t,x)\leq C_{1}t\epsilon^{\alpha}+C_{2}\epsilon

for all
t\in[0,T]
. An analogous reasoning with
-w^{\epsilon}
gives the lower bound and hence proves the result.
∎

3.
Comparison between microscopic model and integral equation

In this section we prove the convergence of solutions to the discrete model (
1.3
) to solutions of the integral equation (
2.1
). This, combined with the results of the previous section, gives the proof of Theorem
1.7
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
V=\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\}
be a sample of i.i.d. points with uniform distribution in
\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
d\geq 1
and assume
\epsilon=\epsilon(n)
satisfies Condition
1.6
. Assume also

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\|u^{n}_{0}-\tilde{u}_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(V)}>\delta\right)<\infty

for any
\delta>0
. If
u^{n}
is the unique solution of (
1.3
) with initial condition
u^{n}_{0}
,
u^{I,\epsilon}
is the unique pseudo-periodic solution of (
2.1
) obtained in Proposition
2.3
with initial condition
\tilde{u}_{0}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
pseudo-periodic, we have that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|u^{n}-u^{I,\epsilon}\right\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times V)}=0,\quad a.s.

As described in Remark
1.2
, we extend the initial condition
u_{0}^{n}
from
V
to
\tilde{V}
pseudo-periodically. For the latter, we need to choose and fix the integers
\{\tilde{k}_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{d}
mentioned in that remark. Since our aim is to approximate the integral equation solution
u^{I,\epsilon}
which is pseudo-periodic with winding numbers
\{k_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{d}
(determined by its initial condition
\tilde{u}_{0}
), we set
\tilde{k}_{\ell}:=k_{\ell}
for
\ell=1,2,...,d
.

We also introduce a convention: For
x_{i}\in V
(original point in
\Lambda_{d}
), its neighbors in the extended cloud are those points
y\in\tilde{V}
within Euclidean distance
\epsilon
from it. Such
y
may be outside of
\Lambda_{d}
, but it is a copy of some
x_{j}\in V
inside
\Lambda_{d}
. Note that this
y
coincides with the
x^{i}_{j}
defined in Remark
1.1
for
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. For the rest of this section, for
x_{i}\in\Lambda_{d}
, we use
\{x_{j},\,j\in\mathcal{N}(i)\}
to denote neighbors of
x_{i}
in
\tilde{V}
, and in the case that
x_{j}
is outside of
\Lambda_{d}
, it is not the original point but its copy; we use instead the notation
[x_{j}]
(see (
1.6
)) to denote the original point in
\Lambda_{d}
.

We first state a discrete maximum principle similar to Lemma
2.4
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
a:[0,\infty)\times\tilde{V}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
satisfy
a(t,x_{i},x_{j})>0
whenever
j\in\mathcal{N}(i)
, for all
t>0
, and
u,v:[0,\infty)\times\tilde{V}\to\mathbb{R}
,
2\pi
-periodic in spatial variable in each coordinate direction, satisfy for any
x_{i}\in V

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}u(t,x_{i})-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}a(t,x_{i},x_{j})\left(u(t,x_{j})-u(t,x_{i})\right)

\displaystyle>\frac{d}{dt}v(t,x_{i})-\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}a(t,x_{i},x_{j})\left(v(t,x_{j})-v(t,x_{i})\right)

\displaystyle u(0,x_{i})

\displaystyle\geq v(0,x_{i}).

Then, we have that

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}N_{i}}\sum_{{x_{j}\in\tilde{V}}}\left[\sin\left(u^{n}(t,x_{j})-u^{n}(t,x_{i})\right)-\sin\left(u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x_{j})-u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x_{i})\right)\right]K\left(\epsilon^{-1}(x_{j}-x_{i})\right)

(given

), and for any fixed

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\Big(\sup_{x_{i}\in V}\Big|\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}N_{i}}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)}\xi_{j}^{i}(t)-\frac{1}{\sigma_{d}\epsilon^{d+2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sin\left((u^{I,\epsilon}(t,y)-u^{I,\epsilon}(t,x_{i})\right)K\left({\epsilon}^{-1}{(y-x_{i})}\right)dy\Big|\geq\delta\Big)

, namely (

.
Since the events
\mathsf{A}_{\delta}:=\big\{\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{x_{i}\in V}|e_{n}(t,x_{i})|>\delta,\,\text{i.o.}\big\}
are nested in
\delta
and non-decreasing as
\delta\downarrow 0
, we conclude that

\mathbb{P}\Big(\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{x_{i}\in V}|e_{n}(t,x_{i})|>0\Big)\leq\mathbb{P}(\cup_{\delta>0}\mathsf{A}_{\delta})=\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\mathbb{P}(\mathsf{A}_{\delta})=0.

This completes the proof of Proposition
3.1
and Theorem
1.7
.
∎

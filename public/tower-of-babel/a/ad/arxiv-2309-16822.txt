1
Determination of twist angle between the WSe
{}_{2}
and MoSe
{}_{2}
layers in heterostrucutres.

The twist angle between the constituent layer of the heterostructure samples was determined by performing polarisation resolved second harmonic generation (SHG) measurements. Fig. S1 shows results of the SHG measurements for sample 1 to 6 along with the determined twist angle between the monolayers. The laser excitation used for SHG measurement was at 1313 nm and SHG signal was measured at 656 nm. The intensity as function of excitation laser polarisation is recorded from the ML transferred on the substrate, which was excited with a linearly polarised light of a femtosecond mode-locked laser. Typical high intensity maxima are observed in six-fold symmetry, as each maximum indicates the armchair direction on hexagonal crystal lattice of TMDC ML. Comparing the SHG response from the two constituent MLs of the heterostructure system, the twist angle can be determined. To identify and distinguish between R or H-type stacking, we measured the SHG response of the heterostructures. Here, a reduction in intensity of SHG signal from the heterostructure region indicates the H-type stacking. We estimate the overall measurement and fitting error in the range of 1° to 3° on average.

Figure S 1:
SHG signal of TMDC heterostructure with different twist angle as function of excitation laser polarization angle. The determined twist angles for samples 1-5 are 56°, 54°, 25°, 3°, 12°, and 1°, respectively.

Photoluminescence excitation spectroscopy

Photoluminescence excitation spectroscopy provides important insight into the intralayer exciton resonances which contribute to the formation of interlayer excitons in twisted heterostructures. Fig. S2 shows the integrated intensity of interlayer exciton as a function of excitation wavelength for sample 1 to 6. We observed a systematic change in the PLE resonances of the intralayer exciton of the constituent layers of the heterostructure as shown below for six different twist angles.

3
Wavelength dependence of IX emission energy and linewidth

The IX emission as function of excitation wavelength is fitted with the Voigt function to account for inhomogeneous boarding and also for extraction of emission energy and lindwidth. Fig. S3 reveals that the IX energy and lindwidth exhibit an inverse correlation. Especially around the intralayer exciton wavelength (energy), the linewidth of the intralayer exciton reduces considerably, along with a blue shift in the emission energy.

In order to gain microscopic access to the moiré exciton energy landscape, we start with a Hamiltonian formulated in second quantization. Importantly, we start in a decoupled monolayer basis, were the moiré potential is added as a periodic modification to these energies

{}_{2}
-WSe
{}_{2}

[
5
]

[
3
,
2
]

\begin{split}&H_{0}=\sum_{L\bm{Q}\bm{\xi}}E^{\bm{\xi}}_{L\bm{Q}}X^{\bm{\xi}%
\dagger}_{L,\bm{Q}}X^{\bm{\xi}}_{L,\bm{Q}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}L\bm{Q}\bm%
{\xi}\\
\bm{g}\end{subarray}}V^{\bm{\xi}}_{L}(\bm{g})X^{\bm{\xi}\dagger}_{L,\bm{Q}+\bm%
{g}}X^{\bm{\xi}}_{L,\bm{Q}}+h.c,\end{split}

where
\bm{Q}

\bm{\xi}=(\xi_{e},\xi_{h})
is the exciton valley index, where
L=(l_{e},l_{h})
is a compound layer index and
\bm{g}=\bm{G}_{2}-\bm{G}_{1}

X^{(\dagger)}
are exciton annihilation (creation) operators. Here,

[
6

[

4

The matrix element of the moiré potential is given by

\begin{split}V^{\bm{\xi}}_{L}(\bm{g})=v^{c}_{l_{e}}(\bm{g})\mathcal{F}^{\bm{%
\xi}}_{LL}(\beta_{LL}\bm{g})-v^{v}_{l_{h}}(\bm{g})\mathcal{F}^{\bm{\xi}*}_{LL}%

\mathcal{F}^{\bm{\xi}}_{LL^{\prime}}(\bm{q})=\sum_{\bm{k}}\Psi^{\bm{\xi}*}_{L}%

The approximation of an infinite dimensional random variable or an infinite dimensional diffusion process by the corresponding discretized objects is an essential aspect of an interface model in statistical mechanics.
It captures the transition from a microscopic perspective to a macro- or mesoscopic point of view.
In this article, a discretization of

In this frame, the main interests of this survey are:

•

Can we approximate a diffusion, informally given by (

The relevant asymptotic statement, to which both questions refer, is the weak measure convergence of equilibrium fluctuations.
In the article, for technical reasons, the second problem has to be covered first.
In the first part of this survey, we address the imminent question, to what extent the height map influences the scaling limit. We give an answer under the assumption that

\Lambda_{N}x(z):=N^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{N}}x_{i}\,\Xi(Nz-I_{N}(i)),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}},\,z\in D,

K_{2}\subset H

\bigcup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(K_{1})\subseteq K_{2}.

(2.20)

To prove (
2.20
), we show that the set on the left-hand side is totally bounded.
It follows from Proposition
2.7
that
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{-1}
is a continuous linear operator from
\operatorname{Im}(\Lambda_{N})
to
\operatorname{Im}(\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N})
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
with

\big|\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{-1}h\big|_{H}\leq 3^{\frac{d}{2}}c^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,|h|_{H},\quad h\in\operatorname{Im}(\Lambda_{N}),

(2.21)

where
c:=

2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Xi(z)^{2}\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z-1

\in(0,\infty)
is the constant from that proposition.
In the following, the open
\delta
-ball
\{h^{\prime}\in H

|

|h-h^{\prime}|_{H}<\delta\}

with centre
h\in H
is denoted by
B(h,\delta)
for
\delta\in(0,\infty)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed.
Since
K_{1}
is totally bounded and
C(\overline{D})
is dense in
H
, there exist
M\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{M}\in C(\overline{D})
such that

K_{1}\subseteq\bigcup_{l=1}^{M}B(\varphi_{l},3^{-\frac{d}{2}-1}c^{\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon).

(2.22)

Due to (
2.18
) and (
2.19
), there exists
N_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
and
x_{N,l}\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}
for
l=1,\dots,M
,
N>N_{\varepsilon}
, such that

\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x_{N,l}\in B(\varphi_{l},\varepsilon/3)\quad\textnormal{and}\quad\Lambda_{N}x_{N,l}\in B(\varphi_{l},3^{-\frac{d}{2}-1}c^{\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon),\quad l=1,\dots,M,\,N>N_{\varepsilon}.

For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
the set
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(K_{1})
is a compact and therefore there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{k}\in C(\overline{D})
such that

\bigcup_{N=1}^{N_{\varepsilon}}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(K_{1})\subseteq\bigcup_{l=1}^{k}B(\eta_{l},\varepsilon).

Next, we show

\bigcup_{N=N_{\varepsilon}+1}^{\infty}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(K_{1})\subseteq\bigcup_{l=1}^{M}B(\varphi_{l},\varepsilon),

(2.23)

which would prove the total boundedness of
\bigcup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(K_{1})
, as the choice of
\varepsilon
is arbitrary.
To prove (
2.23
), we assume
h\in\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(K_{1})
for some
N>N_{\varepsilon}
. Using (
2.22
),
there exists
l\in\{1,\dots,M\}
and
y\in\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(B(\varphi_{l},3^{-\frac{d}{2}-1}c^{\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon))

with
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}y=h
.
Now, (
2.21
) and the choices of
x_{N,l}
yield

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}y-\varphi_{l}|_{H}

\displaystyle=\big|\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(\Lambda_{N}y-\Lambda_{N}x_{N,l})+\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x_{N,l}-\varphi_{l}\big|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{\frac{d}{2}}c^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\big|\Lambda_{N}y-\Lambda_{N}x_{N,l}\big|_{H}+\big|\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x_{N,l}-\varphi_{l}\big|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{\frac{d}{2}}c^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\big|\Lambda_{N}y-\varphi_{l}\big|_{H}+\big|\varphi_{l}-\Lambda_{N}x_{N,l}\big|_{H}\Big)+\big|\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x_{N,l}-\varphi_{l}\big|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq 3^{\frac{d}{2}}c^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(3^{-\frac{d}{2}-1}c^{\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon+3^{-\frac{d}{2}-1}c^{\frac{1}{2}}\varepsilon\big)+\varepsilon/3=\varepsilon.

This proves (
2.23
). The proof for the existence of a compact set
K_{2}\subset H
satisfying (
2.20
) for a fixed compact set
K_{1}
is complete.

Now, the tightness of
{(m_{N}\circ\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}^{-1})}_{N}
is an immediate consequence of the tightness of
{(m_{N}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{-1})}_{N}
. Indeed, for
0<\delta<1
and a compact set
K_{\delta}\subset H
such that

\inf_{N\in\mathbb{N}}m_{N}(\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(K_{\delta}))\geq 1-\delta,

we can choose a compact set
K_{\delta}^{\prime}\subset H
with
\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(K_{\delta})\subseteq\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}^{-1}(K_{\delta}^{\prime})
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
due to (
2.20
). In particular,

\inf_{N\in\mathbb{N}}m_{N}\circ\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}^{-1}(K_{\delta}^{\prime})\geq\inf_{N\in\mathbb{N}}m_{N}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(K_{\delta})\geq 1-\delta.

Identifying the accumulation points of
{(m_{N}\circ\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}^{-1})}_{N}
is the only thing left to do in this proof. We make use of
[
EK86
, Theorem 4.5 of Chapter 3]
.
It is sufficient to show

\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}}F(\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}m_{N}(x)=\int_{H}F(h)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}m(h)

(2.24)

for any
F:H\to\mathbb{R}
of the form
F(h)=g(\langle f_{1},h\rangle_{H},\dots,\langle f_{q},h\rangle_{H})
, where
q\in\mathbb{N}
,
g:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}
is a bounded, Lipschitz continuous function and
f_{1},\dots,f_{q}:

D\to\mathbb{R}
are continuous with compact support contained in
D
. The reason is that the family of all functions
F:H\to\mathbb{R}
of this form separates the points of
H
.
Let
F
,
g
and
f_{1},\dots,f_{q}
,
q\in\mathbb{N}
, be chosen in this manner. We have

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}}F(\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}m_{N}(x)-\int_{H}F(h)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}m(h)\Big|\\
\leq\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}}F(\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}m_{N}(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}}F(\Lambda_{N}x)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}m_{N}(x)\Big|\\
+\limsup_{N\to\infty}\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}}F(\Lambda_{N}x)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}m_{N}(x)-\int_{H}F(h)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}m(h)\Big|.

Due to the weak measure convergence
m_{N}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{-1}\Rightarrow m
, the second term of the right-hand side vanishes. Now, we address the first summand. Let
\varepsilon>0
.
There exists a compact set
K_{\varepsilon}\subset H
with

m_{N}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(K_{\varepsilon})\geq 1-\frac{\varepsilon}{4\sup_{h\in h}|F(h)|}

for
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
L
denote the Lipschitz constant of
g
.
We have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}}F(\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}m_{N}(x)-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}}F(\Lambda_{N}x)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}m_{N}(x)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(K_{\varepsilon})}\big|F(\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x)-F(\Lambda_{N}x)\big|\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}m_{N}(x)+2\|F\|_{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}\setminus\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(K_{\varepsilon})}\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}m_{N}

\displaystyle\leq L\int_{\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(K_{\varepsilon})}\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{q}\big|\langle f_{k},\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x-\Lambda_{N}x\rangle_{H}\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}m_{N}(x)+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

So, (
2.24
) follows, if there exists
M_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\big|\langle f_{k},\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x-\Lambda_{N}x\rangle_{H}\big|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2L\sqrt{q}}\quad\text{for }x\in\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(K_{\varepsilon}),\,k=1,\dots,q\text{ and }N\geq M_{\varepsilon}.

(2.25)

We define

\omega_{N}(z):=\max_{k=1,\dots,q}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z^{\prime}\in\overline{D}\\
|z-z^{\prime}|_{\textnormal{euc}}\leq\sqrt{d}/N\end{subarray}}|f_{k}(z^{\prime})-f_{k}(z)|,\quad z\in\overline{D}.

By virtue of (
2.20
) there exists a compact set
K^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}

such that

\bigcup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(K_{\varepsilon})\subseteq K^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}.

(2.26)

Then, we choose
M_{\varepsilon}^{(1)},M_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\omega_{M_{\varepsilon}^{(1)}}(z)\leq\varepsilon\big(4L\max_{h\in K_{\varepsilon}\cup K^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}}|h|_{H}\big)^{-1}\Big(q\int_{D}\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad z\in D

(2.27)

and

\bigcup_{k=1}^{q}\Big\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,\Big|\,|z-z^{\prime}|_{\textnormal{euc}}\leq{\sqrt{d}/M_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}}\text{ for some }z^{\prime}\in\operatorname{supp}[{f_{k}}]\Big\}\subset D.

We set
M_{\varepsilon}:=\max(\{M_{\varepsilon}^{(1)},M_{\varepsilon}^{(2)}\})
.
Let
k\in\{1,\dots,q\}
,
x\in\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(K_{\varepsilon})
and
N\geq M_{\varepsilon}
.

\big|\langle f_{k},\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x-\Lambda_{N}x\rangle_{H}\big|\\
\leq\Big|\langle f_{k},\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x\rangle_{H}-N^{-\frac{d}{2}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{N}}f_{k}\big(\frac{p_{i}}{N}\big)x_{i}\Big|+\Big|N^{-\frac{d}{2}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{N}}f_{k}\big(\frac{p_{i}}{N}\big)x_{i}-\langle f_{k},\Lambda_{N}x\rangle_{H}\Big|.

(2.28)

Next, since
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widetilde{\xi}_{N,i}\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z=N^{-d}
and

\operatorname{supp}[{\widetilde{\xi}_{N,i}}]\subseteq\Big\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,\Big|\,\big|z-\frac{p_{i}}{N}\big|_{\textnormal{euc}}\leq{\sqrt{d}/M_{\varepsilon}}\Big\}\subset D

for
i\in\{1,\dots,k_{N}\}
with
p_{i}/N\in\operatorname{supp}[{f_{k}}]
, it holds

\Big|\langle f_{k},\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x\rangle_{H}-N^{-\frac{d}{2}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{N}}f_{k}\big(\frac{p_{i}}{N}\big)x_{i}\Big|=\Big|\langle f_{k},\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x\rangle_{H}-N^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{N}}f_{k}\big(\frac{p_{i}}{N}\big)x_{i}\int_{D}\widetilde{\xi}_{N,i}(z)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z\Big|\\
=\Big|N^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{N}}\int_{D}\Big(f_{k}(z)-f_{k}\big(\frac{p_{i}}{N}\big)\Big)\,x_{i}\widetilde{\xi}_{N,i}(z)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z\Big|\leq\int_{D}\omega_{N}(z)\big|\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x(z)\big|\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z\\
\leq\varepsilon\big(4L\max_{h\in K_{\varepsilon}\cup K^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}}|h|_{H}\big)^{-1}\Big(q\int_{D}\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{D}\big|\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x(z)\big|\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4L\sqrt{q}}.

In the second to last inequality, we used (
2.27
), while the last inequality follows from (
2.26
),
x\in\Lambda_{N}^{-1}(K_{\varepsilon})
and Hölder’s inequality

\int_{D}\big|\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x(z)\big|\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z\leq|\widetilde{\Lambda}_{N}x(z)|_{H}|\boldsymbol{1}(\cdot)|_{H}
.
With the analogous argumentation

\Big|\langle f_{k},\Lambda_{N}x\rangle_{H}-N^{-\frac{d}{2}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{N}}f_{k}\big(\frac{p_{i}}{N}\big)x_{i}\Big|\leq\Big(\int_{D}w_{N}(z)^{2}\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\max_{h\in K_{\varepsilon}}|h|_{H}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4L\sqrt{q}}.

Hence (
2.25
) holds and thereby (
2.24
).
This concludes the proof.
∎

3
The dynamic model

3.1
Tightness of equilibrium laws

We continue to work in the setting of Section
2
,
with
(H,\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle)=L^{2}(D,\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z)
,
\Lambda_{N}
,
\Xi
,
D_{N}
,
G_{N}
,
k_{N}
and
p_{1},\dots p_{k_{N}}\in G_{N}
as in the previous section.
In this section, a dynamic version of Theorem
2.8
is derived.
By default, when referring to
C([0,\infty),Y)
as a topological space for a metric space
Y
, we address the topology corresponding to the locally uniform convergence of functions
[0,\infty)\to Y
.

We make use of the theory of Dirichlet forms for symmetric Markov processes.
The basic terminology and notions of the standard textbooks
[
FOT11
,
BH91
,
MR92
]
are used.
From here on, we have the following additional assumptions.

Condition 3.1
.

(i)

Let
H_{0}
be a separable Hilbert space, densely included in
H
, such that the inclusion
i:H_{0}\hookrightarrow H
has a finite Hilbert Schmidt norm

\|i\|_{\textnormal{HS}}
. Identifying
H
with its dual
H^{\prime}
we can interpret
H
as a subset of the dual
H_{0}^{\prime}
via

H_{0}\subset H=H^{\prime}\subset H_{0}^{\prime}.

(ii)

For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
let
m_{N}
be a Borel probability measure on
\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}
and

(\mathcal{E}^{N},\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{N}))
be a symmetric, regular, local and conservative Dirichlet form on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}},m_{N})
, which admits a Carré-du-Champs operator

\Gamma_{N}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{N})\times\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{N})\to L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}},m_{N}).

Moreover, each linear functional
\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}\ni x\mapsto a^{\textnormal{T}}x\in\mathbb{R}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}
,
is a representative of an element
l_{a}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{N})
with

\Gamma_{N}(l_{a},l_{a})(\cdot)\leq\gamma\,a^{\textnormal{T}}a\quad m_{N}\text{-a.e.},

(3.1)

where the constant
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
does not depend on
N
.

For each
N\in\mathbb{N}
let

\mathbf{M}_{N}=\big(\Omega_{N},\mathcal{F}^{N},{(\mathcal{F}^{N}_{t})}_{t\geq 0},(X^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0},(P^{N}_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}\cup\{\Delta\}}\big)

denote a special standard process with state space
\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}
and life time
\zeta_{N}
, which is properly associated with
(\mathcal{E}^{N},\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{N}))
.
Such a process exists, because
(\mathcal{E}^{N},\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{N}))
is regular. Then,
\mathbf{M}_{N}
is unique up to equivalence.
It holds

P^{N}_{x}(\{\omega\in\Omega_{N}\,|\,[0,\zeta_{N}(\omega))\ni t\mapsto X^{N}_{t}(\omega)\text{ is continuous}\})=1\quad\text{for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}},

due to the local property of
\mathcal{E}^{N}
.
The strongly continuous symmetric contraction semigroup on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}},m_{N})
corresponding to
(\mathcal{E}^{N},\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E}^{N}))
is denoted by
{(T^{N}_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
.
By conservativeness, it holds
T^{N}_{t}\boldsymbol{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}},=\boldsymbol{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}}
for
t\geq 0
. This means there exists a set
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}
of zero capacity (referring to the
1
-capacity associated with
\mathcal{E}^{N}
) such that
P^{N}_{x}(\{\zeta_{N}=\infty\})=1
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}\setminus\mathcal{N}
. W.l.o.g.
\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}\setminus\mathcal{N}
may be assumed to be
\mathbf{M}_{N}
-invariant, as argued in
[
MR92
, Chap. IV, Cor. 6.5]
. Considering the restriction
\mathbf{M}_{N}|_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}\setminus\mathcal{N}}
(as defined in
[
MR92
, Chap. IV, Rem. 6.2(i)]
)
and then applying
the procedure described in
[
MR92
, Chapt.IV, Sect.3, pp. 117f.]
, re-defining
\mathbf{M}_{N}
in such way that each element from
\mathcal{N}
is a trap, we may assume
P_{x}^{N}(\{\zeta_{N}=\infty\})=1
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}
.
Furthermore, after the procedure of weeding (restricting the sample space to a subset of
\Omega_{N}
as explained in
[
Dyn65
, Chap. III, Paragraph 2, pp. 86f.]
), we may w.l.o.g. assume that
\mathbf{M}_{N}
is non-terminating and continuous, i.e.
\zeta_{N}(\omega)=\infty
and
[0,\infty)\ni t\mapsto X^{N}_{t}(\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}
is continuous for every
\omega\in\Omega_{N}
.

As a conclusion of Remark
3.2
, the following corollary holds.

Corollary 3.3
.

There exists a diffusion process, i.e. a non-terminating, continuous special standard process

\mathbf{M}_{N}=\big(\Omega_{N},\mathcal{F}^{N},{(\mathcal{F}^{N}_{t})}_{t\geq 0},(X^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0},(P^{N}_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}}\big)

on
\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}
,
which is properly associated with
\mathcal{E}^{N}
. So, for every
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}},m_{N})
, the function

\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}\ni x\mapsto\int_{\Omega_{N}}u\circ X^{N}_{t}\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}P^{N}_{x}

is a (quasi-continuous) version of
T^{N}_{t}u
for
t\geq 0
. In particular, the measure
m_{N}
is an invariant measure and
\mathbf{M}_{N}
is
m_{N}
-symmetric.

We analyse the distribution of

{(u^{N}_{t})}_{t\geq 0}:\Omega_{N}\to C([0,\infty),H)
defined by

u^{N}_{t}:=\Lambda_{N}X^{N}_{N^{2}t},\quad t\geq 0,

(3.2)

for
N\in\mathbb{N}
. We are interested in the weak measure convergence
of the equilibrium law under
{(u^{N}_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
as
N\to\infty
.
A suitable path space to tackle this problem is
C([0,\infty),H_{0}^{\prime})
.
Denoting the
i
-th coordinate process of
{(X^{N}_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
with
{(X^{N,i}_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
for
i=1,\dots k_{N}
,
we have

u^{N}_{t}=N^{\frac{d}{2}-1}\sum_{i=1}^{k_{N}}X^{N,i}_{N^{2}t}\,\Xi(N\,\cdot\,-p_{i}),\quad t\geq 0.

(3.3)

Let

P_{N}(A):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}}P^{N}_{x}(A)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}m_{N}(x),\quad A\in\mathcal{F}^{N}.

The equilibrium law of
{(u^{N}_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
on
C([0,\infty),H_{0}^{\prime})
is denoted by
\mathbb{P}_{N}
, i.e.

\mathbb{P}_{N}(B):=P_{N}\big(\big\{\omega\in\Omega_{N}\,\big|\,{(u^{N}_{t}(\omega))}_{t\geq 0}\in B\big\}\big)

(3.4)

for a Borel measurable set
B\subseteq C([0,\infty),H_{0}^{\prime})
.

Proposition 3.4
.

If
{(m_{N}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{-1})}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
is a tight family of probability measures on
H
, then
{(\mathbb{P}_{N})}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
is tight on
C([0,\infty),H_{0}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

This proof follows a well-known idea for the derivation of a tightness result in the context of symmetric Markov processes, which has been used in
[
Gro+03
, Theorem 6.1]
,
[
GKR07
, Theorem 5.1]
or
[
DZ07
, Lemma 5.2]
among others.
As argued in each of the three cited references, the desired tightness property can be concluded from the tightness of the invariant measures
{(m_{N}\circ\Lambda_{N}^{-1})}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
together with the estimate

\sup_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\Big(\int_{C([0,\infty),H_{0}^{\prime})}|u_{t}-u_{s}|_{H^{\prime}_{0}}^{p}\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}\mathbb{P}_{N}(u)\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq c(t-s)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad 0\leq s<t<\infty,

(3.5)

for some constants
c\in(0,\infty)
and
p\in(1,\infty)
.

The rest of the proof is dedicated to the verification of (
3.5
). The strategy is analogous as
in
[
Gro+03
, Theorem 6.1]
,
[
GKR07
, Theorem 5.1]
or
[
DZ07
, Lemma 5.2]
.
At first we fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varphi\in H_{0}
.
Let
r_{T}
be the time-reversal operator
\Omega_{N}\to\Omega_{N}
w.r.t. time
0<T<\infty
.
There is a
P_{N}
-martingale
{(M_{t})}_{t\geq 0}
with
quadratic variation given by

{<\!{M}\!>}_{t}=2\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma_{N}\big(\langle\varphi,\Lambda_{N}\,\cdot\,\rangle,\langle\varphi,\Lambda_{N}\,\cdot\,\rangle\big)\big(X_{s}\big)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}s\quad P_{N}\text{-a.s.},\quad t\geq 0,

(3.6)

such that

\langle\varphi,\Lambda_{N}X_{t}\rangle-\langle\varphi,\Lambda_{N}X_{0}\rangle=\frac{1}{2}M_{t}+\frac{1}{2}(M_{T-t}\circ r_{T}-M_{T}\circ r_{T})\quad P_{N}\text{-a.s.},\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

(3.7)

The formula of (
3.7
) is called Lyons-Zheng decomposition and stated in
[
FOT11

+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\tfrac{1-e^{-\beta_{j}/N^{d}}}{1+e^{-\beta_{j}/N^{d}}}\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}l_{t}^{N,i,\tilde{y}_{j}},

\tilde{y}_{j}:=N^{1-\frac{d}{2}}y_{j}
, and
{(M_{t}^{u_{i}})}_{t\geq 0}
is the martingale additive functional with

{<\!{M^{u_{i}},M^{u_{j}}}\!>_{t}}=2\int_{0}^{t}\Gamma_{N}(u_{i},u_{j})\big(X_{s}\big)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}s=t\delta_{ij},\quad t\geq 0,\quad i,j\in\{1,\dots,k_{N}\}.

(4.21)

Step (4): Via compactness of the embedding
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{E})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}},m_{A_{N}})
the absolute continuity criterion for the corresponding semigroup can be shown. Therefore,
we have the stronger statement that (
4.20
) indeed holds
P_{x}^{N}
-a.s. for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{k_{N}}

∎

Lemma A2
.

Let
{(g_{N})}_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
either be a sequence of bounded, monotone decreasing functions, or a sequence of bounded, monotone increasing functions on
\mathbb{R}
such that there exists a bounded function
g:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
|r|\leq\delta\end{subarray}}|g(x)-g_{N}(x+r)|\overset{N\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\quad\text{for every }\delta\in(0,\infty).

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. There exists a minorante
g_{N,m}^{\text{min}}\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
and a majorante
g_{N,m}^{\text{maj}}\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
for
N\in\mathbb{N}
with

-\|g_{N}\|_{\infty}\leq g_{N,m}^{\text{min}}(x)\leq g_{N}(x)\leq g_{N,m}^{\text{maj}}\leq\|g_{N}\|_{\infty},\quad x\in\mathbb{R},

which meet the following properties:

There are asymptotic functions
g_{m}^{\text{min}}
,
g_{m}^{\text{maj}}\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\|g_{N,m}^{\text{min}}-g_{m}^{\text{min}}\|_{\infty}=0,\quad\lim_{N\to\infty}\|g_{N,m}^{\text{maj}}-g_{m}^{\text{maj}}\|_{\infty}=0.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}g_{m}^{\text{min}}(x)=g(x),\quad\lim_{m\to\infty}g_{m}^{\text{maj}}(x)=g(x),\quad\text{if }g\text{ is continuous at the point }x\in\mathbb{R}.

Proof.

We will construct the desired minorante and majorante for
g_{N}
via a mollifying technique with uses a partition of unity. W.l.o.g. let’s assume we are in the monotone increasing case. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\tau_{m,i}
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, be a partition of union, subordinate to
(\frac{i-1}{m},\frac{i+1}{m})
,
i\in\mathbb{Z}
. Choose
-\frac{1}{m}<r(N,m,i)<\frac{1}{m}
such that

\big|g(\frac{i}{m})-g_{N}\big(\frac{i}{m}+r(N,m,i)\big)\big|\leq\frac{1}{2N}+\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\\
|r|\leq 1/m\end{subarray}}\big|g\big(\frac{i}{m}\big)-g_{N}\big(\frac{i}{m}+r\big)\big|,\quad i,N\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.22)

We define

\displaystyle g_{N,m}^{\text{min}}:=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}g_{N}\big(\frac{i}{m}+r(N,m,i)\big)\tau_{m,i+2},\quad g_{N,m}^{\text{maj}}:=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}g_{N}\big(\frac{i}{m}+r(N,m,i)\big)\tau_{m,i-2}

for
N\in\mathbb{N}
and

\displaystyle g_{m}^{\text{min}}:=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}g\big(\frac{i}{m}\big)\tau_{m,i+2},\quad g_{m}^{\text{maj}}:=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}g\big(\frac{i}{m}\big)\tau_{m,i-2}.

We remark that
\tau_{m,i}(x)\neq 0
necessitates
i\in\{\lfloor mx\rfloor,\lfloor mx\rfloor+1\}
for
i\in\mathbb{Z}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and in particular,
\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor}(x)+\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor+1}(x)=1
. The functions defined above have the desired properties indeed.
It holds

\displaystyle g_{N,m}^{\text{min}}(x)

\displaystyle=g_{N}\Big(\frac{\lfloor mx\rfloor-2}{m}+r(N,m,\lfloor mx\rfloor-2)\Big)\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor}(x)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+g_{N}\Big(\frac{\lfloor mx\rfloor-1}{m}+r(N,m,\lfloor mx\rfloor-1)\Big)\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor+1}(x)

\displaystyle\leq g_{N}\Big(\frac{\lfloor mx\rfloor-1}{m}\Big)\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor}(x)+g_{N}\Big(\frac{\lfloor mx\rfloor}{m}\Big)\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor+1}(x)

\displaystyle\leq g_{N}(x)(\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor}(x)+\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor+1}(x))=g_{N}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},N\in\mathbb{N},

and also

\displaystyle g_{N,m}^{\text{maj}}(x)

\displaystyle=g_{N}\Big(\frac{\lfloor mx\rfloor+2}{m}+r(N,m,\lfloor mx\rfloor+2)\Big)\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor}(x)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+g_{N}\Big(\frac{\lfloor mx\rfloor+3}{m}+r(N,m,\lfloor mx\rfloor+3)\Big)\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor+1}(x)

\displaystyle\geq g_{N}\Big(\frac{\lfloor mx\rfloor+1}{m}\Big)\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor}(x)+g_{N}\Big(\frac{\lfloor mx\rfloor+2}{m}\Big)\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor+1}(x)

\displaystyle\geq g_{N}(x)(\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor}(x)+\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor+1}(x))=g_{N}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R},N\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
. We choose
N_{0}
is large enough such that
\inf_{r:|r|\leq 1/m}|g(x)-g_{N}(x+r)|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\geq N_{0}
. For
N\geq\max\{N_{0},\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\}
, using (
4.22
), it holds

\displaystyle|g_{N,m}^{\text{min}}(x)-g_{m}^{\text{min}}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|g_{N}\Big(\frac{\lfloor mx\rfloor-2}{m}+r(N,m,\lfloor mx\rfloor-2)\Big)-g\Big(\frac{\lfloor mx\rfloor-2}{m}\Big)\Big|\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor}(x)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\Big|g_{N}\Big(\frac{\lfloor mx\rfloor-1}{m}+r(N,m,\lfloor mx\rfloor-1)\Big)-g\Big(\frac{\lfloor mx\rfloor-1}{m}\Big)\Big|\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor+1}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\big(\frac{1}{2N}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\big)\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor}(x)+\big(\frac{1}{2N}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\big)\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor+1}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor}(x)+\varepsilon\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor+1}(x)=\varepsilon.

Analogously, we get
|g_{N,m}^{\text{maj}}(x)-g_{m}^{\text{maj}}(x)|\leq\varepsilon
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
N\geq\max\{N_{0},\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\}
.

Moreover, let
g
be continuous at
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
be large enough such that
|g(x)-g(y)|\leq\varepsilon
if
y\in\mathbb{R}
,
|y-x|<\frac{3}{m}
, then

\displaystyle|g_{N}^{\text{min}}(x)-g(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|g\Big(\frac{\lfloor mx\rfloor-2}{m}\Big)-g(x)\Big|\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor}(x)+\Big|g\Big(\frac{\lfloor mx\rfloor-1}{m}\Big)-g(x)\Big|\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor+1}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor}(x)+\varepsilon\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor+1}(x)=\varepsilon

and also

\displaystyle|g_{N}^{\text{maj}}(x)-g(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|g\Big(\frac{\lfloor mx\rfloor+2}{m}\Big)-g(x)\Big|\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor}(x)+\Big|g\Big(\frac{\lfloor mx\rfloor+3}{m}\Big)-g(x)\Big|\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor+1}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor}(x)+\varepsilon\tau_{m,\lfloor mx\rfloor+1}(x)=\varepsilon.

In the case, where
g_{N}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, are monotone decreasing, we define

\displaystyle g_{N,m}^{\text{min}}:=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}g_{N}\big(\frac{i}{m}+r(N,m,i)\big)\tau_{m,i-2},\quad g_{N,m}^{\text{maj}}:=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}g_{N}\big(\frac{i}{m}+r(N,m,i)\big)\tau_{m,i+2}

for
N\in\mathbb{N}
and

\displaystyle g_{m}^{\text{min}}:=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}g\big(\frac{i}{m}\big)\tau_{m,i-2},\quad g_{m}^{\text{maj}}:=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}g\big(\frac{i}{m}\big)\tau_{m,i+2},

instead. The values of
r(N,m,i)
for
N,m,i\in\mathbb{N}
are chosen exactly as in the first case. From here, the argumentation is analogous. This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma A3
.

Let
\mu
be a non-degenerate centred Gaussian measure on
H:=L^{2}(D,\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z)
for a domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
For each value
a\in\mathbb{R}
the level set
\{z\in D

|

h(z)=a\}
has Lebesgue measure zero for
\mu
-a.e.
h\in H
.

Proof.

The claim is that the
\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z
-class defined by the composition
\boldsymbol{1}_{\{a\}}\circ h

vanishes in
\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z
-a.e. sense for
\mu
-a.e.
h\in H
. This is shown as follows.

Let
\varphi\in H
such that the image measure of
\mu
under any shift
\tau_{s\varphi}h:=h+s\varphi
,
h\in H
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, is absolutely continuous w.r.t.
\mu
itself, i.e.
\mu\circ\tau_{s\varphi}^{-1}\ll\mu
. In that case, we immediately have
\mu\ll\mu\circ\tau_{s\varphi}^{-1}
, hence the equivalence (mutual absolute continuity)
\mu\sim\mu\circ\tau_{s\varphi}^{-1}
, for
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, defining the measure

\sigma_{\varphi}(B):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu\circ\tau_{s\varphi}^{-1}(B)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}s,\quad B\subseteq H\text{ Borel measurable},

it holds
\sigma_{\varphi}\sim\mu
. Moreover,

\int_{H}u\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}\mu=\int_{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(h+s\varphi)\frac{\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}\sigma_{\varphi}}(h+s\varphi)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}s\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}\mu(h)

for any Borel measurable function
u:H\to[0,\infty)
.
Now, let

\tilde{\varphi}:D\to\mathbb{R}
be a representative for
\varphi
and
A\subseteq D
be a Borel measurable set such that
\tilde{\varphi}(z)\neq 0
for
z\in A
. Since
any singleton is negligible w.r.t. the Lebesgue measure, Fubini’s theorem yields

\int_{H}\int_{A}\boldsymbol{1}_{a}(h(z))\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}\mu(h)=\int_{H}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{A}\boldsymbol{1}_{a}\big(h(z)+s\tilde{\varphi}(z)\big)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z\frac{\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}\sigma_{\varphi}}(h+s\varphi)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}s\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}\mu(h)\\
=\int_{H}\int_{A}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\tilde{\varphi}(z)^{-1}(a-h(z))}(s)\frac{\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}\mu}{\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}\sigma_{\varphi}}(h+s\varphi)\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}s\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}\mu(h)=0.

(4.23)

Due to the Cameron-Martin formula, the space
V:=\{\varphi\in H

|

\mu\circ\tau_{s\varphi}^{-1}\ll\mu
for
s\in\mathbb{R}\}
is dense in
H
, as is shown in

[
BH91
, Theorems 3.1.2 & 3.1.3 of Chapter II]
. Hence, we can find an orthonormal basis
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\dots
of elements from
V
and define Borel measurable subsets
A_{1},A_{2},\dots
of
D
as
A_{i}:=\{z\in D\,|\,\tilde{\varphi}_{i}(z)\neq 0\}
for some
\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z
-version
\tilde{\varphi}_{i}
of
\varphi_{i}
. The right-hand-side of (
4.23
) is independent of
\varphi
and in this case yields

\int_{H}\int_{A_{i}}\boldsymbol{1}_{a}(h(z))\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}\mu(h)=0,\quad i\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
Q:=D\setminus\big(\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}A_{i}\big)
. Since
\langle\boldsymbol{1}_{Q},\varphi_{i}\rangle=0
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, the set
Q
has Lebesgue measure zero. Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{H}\int_{D}\boldsymbol{1}_{a}(h(z))\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}\mu(h)

\displaystyle=\int_{H}\int_{\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}A_{i}}\boldsymbol{1}_{a}(h(z))\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}\mu(h)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\int_{H}\int_{A_{i}}\boldsymbol{1}_{a}(h(z))\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}z\mathop{}\!\textnormal{d}\mu(h)=0.

This concludes the proof.
∎

Non-standard linear recurring sequence subgroups and automorphisms of irreducible cyclic codes Thanks: This work was supported by the Estonian Research Council grant PRG49.
Henk D. L. Hollmann

Abstract
Let 𝒰 {\cal U} be the multiplicative group of order n n in the splitting field 𝔽 q m \mathbb{F}_{q^{m}} of x n − 1 x^{n}-1 over the finite field 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} . Any map of the form x → c ​ x t x\rightarrow cx^{t} with c ∈ 𝒰 c\in{\cal U} and t = q i t=q^{i} , 0 ≤ i < m 0\leq i<m , is 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -linear on 𝔽 q m \mathbb{F}_{q^{m}} and fixes 𝒰 {\cal U} set-wise; maps of this type will be called standard . Occasionally there are other, non-standard 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} -linear maps on 𝔽 q m \mathbb{F}_{q^{m}} fixing 𝒰 {\cal U} set-wise, and in that case we say that the pair ( n , q ) (n,q) is non-standard . We show that an irreducible cyclic code of length n n over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} has “extra” permutation automorphisms (others than the standard permutations generated by the cyclic shift and the Frobenius mapping that every such code has) precisely when the pair ( n , q ) (n,q) is non-standard; we refer to such irreducible cyclic codes as non-standard or NSIC-codes . In addition, we relate these concepts to that of a non-standard linear recurring sequence subgroup as investigated in a sequence of papers by Brison and Nogueira. We present several families of NSIC-codes, and two constructions called “lifting” and “extension” to create new NSIC-codes from existing ones. We show that all NSIC-codes of dimension two can be obtained in this way, thus completing the classification for this case started by Brison and Nogueira.



I
Introduction

The general problem of determining the automorphism group of any cyclic code is a difficult problem, and
[
1
, Section 3.5]
suggests to investigate special cases such as irreducible cyclic codes. Usually, the only permutation automorphisms of an irreducible cyclic code are those generated by the cyclic shift and the Frobenius mapping; we refer to these codes as
standard
and to the exceptional ones that possess “extra” permutation automorphisms as
non-standard
. For briefness, we will refer to non-standard irreducible cyclic codes as
NSIC-codes
. Examples include the
q
-ary simplex codes, certain even-weight codes, and the duals of the binary and ternary Golay codes.
The ultimate goal is to obtain a full classification of the NSIC-codes. We present several families of NSIC-codes, together with construction techniques called
lifting
and
extension
to construct new NSIC-codes from existing ones. As one of our main results, we show that
every NSIC-code of dimension two can be obtained in this way.

Interestingly, the notion of an NSIC-code can be related to various other notions. In a series of papers, see, e.g.,
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
, Brison and Nogueira investigated the concept of a
non-standard linear recurring sequence subgroup
or
NSLRS-group
,
a multiplicative subgroup in an extension of a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with the property that the elements can be represented by a non-cyclic linear recurring sequence with characteristic polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
(for precise definitions of these and other notions, we refer to the next two sections). We show that the notion of an NSIC-codes coincides with that of a NSLRS-group in the case where the characteristic polynomial of the linear recurrence relation
is required to be
irreducible
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Let
{\cal U}
be the multilicative subgroup of order

\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}

as

Notation and preliminaries

Throughout this paper,
q
denotes a power of a prime,

is a positive integer such that
\gcd(n,q)=1
, and

. The finite field of size

is denoted by
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and we use
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
to denote both the non-zero elements of the field and the multiplicative subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We let

denote the algebraic closure of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
For a subset
{\cal H}
of a group
{\cal G}
, we write
{\cal H}\leq{\cal G}
to denote that
{\cal H}
is a subgroup of
{\cal G}
.
There is a
unique
multiplicative subgroup in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of order

, consisting of the
n
-th roots of unity in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, which we denote by
{\cal U}_{n,q}
.
Note that
{\cal U}_{n,q}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{*}
, and by the definition of

,
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is the smallest extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
that contains
{\cal U}_{n,q}
. We let
\xi
denote a fixed element in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{*}
of order
n
, and we mostly use
g
to denote the minimal polynomial of
\xi
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
; note that the degree
\deg(g)
of

equals

. Then
{\cal U}_{n,q}=\mbox{$\langle\xi\rangle$}

\xi
.

An important notion in this paper is the
q
-order
. The

-order of
\xi
, denoted by
\delta_{q}(\xi)
, is the smallest positive integer

for which
\xi^{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. It is not difficult to show that
\delta_{q}(\xi)=\delta_{q}(n):=n/\gcd(n,q-1)
, so depends only on
n
.
Writing

and

, we have

with
e\mid q-1
and
\gcd(d,(q-1)/e)=1
; as a consequence, the number

depends only on
d
and is the smallest positive integer such that
d\mid(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)
.
For proofs and more information on the
q
-order, see
[
6
]
.

In this paper, we investigate the group
{\cal L}(n,q)
of all
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear maps

on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
that fix the group
{\cal U}_{n,q}
of order
n
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{*}

set-wise
, that is, such that
L({\cal U}_{n,q})={\cal U}_{n,q}
. Note that
{\cal L}(n,q)
is indeed a group: since
m={\rm ord}_{n}(q)
, the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-span of the elements

of
{\cal U}_{n,q}=\mbox{$\langle\xi\rangle$}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, so if
L\in{\cal L}(n,q)
, then
{\rm Im}(L)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, hence
L
is invertible. We refer to an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear map on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
of the form
L:x\rightarrow cx^{q^{j}}
, for some
j\in[m]:=\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, as

. Note that such a standard map is contained in
{\cal L}(n,q)
if and only if
c\in{\cal U}_{n,q}
. We write
{\cal L}_{\rm st}(n,q)
to denote the subgroup of
{\cal L}(n,q)
consisting of the standard maps in
{\cal L}(n,q)
. In certain exceptional cases,

{\cal L}(n,q)
is stricktly larger than
{\cal L}_{\rm st}(n,q)
; in that case, we refer to the pair
(n,q)
as
non-standard
and to the group
{\cal U}_{n,q}
as
non-standard (over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
); in the usual case where
{\cal L}(n,q)={\cal L}_{\rm st}(n,q)
, we refer to both
(n,q)
and
{\cal U}_{n,q}
as
standard
.

Let
{\cal S}_{n}
denote the symmetric group on
n
symbols,
the group of all permutations on
[n]
. Define the map

\Psi:{\cal L}(n,q)\rightarrow{\cal S}_{n}

by
\Psi(L)=\pi
if
L(\xi^{i})=\xi^{\pi(i)}
for
i=0,\ldots,n-1
.
It is easily seen that
\Psi
is a one-to-one group-homomorphism. We will write
{\cal S}(n,q)=\Psi({\cal L}(n,q))
and
{\cal S}_{\rm st}(n,q)=\Psi({\cal L}_{\rm st}(n,q))
to denote the images in
{\cal S}_{n}
under
\Psi
of
{\cal L}(n,q)
and
{\cal L}_{\rm st}(n,q)
.
By the above,

{\cal L}(n,q)\cong{\cal S}(n,q)

and
{\cal L}_{\rm st}(n,q)\cong{\cal S}_{\rm st}(n,q)=\{x\rightarrow q^{i}x+a\pmod{n}\mid a\in\mathbb{Z}_{n},i\in[m]\}
;
in particular,
we note that

has size
nm
.
For missing proofs and more details, we refer to
[
6
,
7
]
.

III
Linear recurring seqence subgroups

In this section, we establish the relation between non-standard pairs and non-standard linear recurring relation subgroups
[
3
]
. For more background on linear recurring sequence, see, e.g.,
[
9
]
or
[
10
]
.

A sequence
{\bf s}=s_{0},s_{1},\ldots
in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is called an
m
th order

linear recurring sequence
if it satisfies a (homogeneous) linear recurrence relation of the form

s_{k}=\sigma_{m-1}s_{k-1}+\cdots+\sigma_{1}s_{k-m+1}+\sigma_{0}s_{k-m}

(1)

for all integers
k\geq m
,
where
m\geq 1
,
\sigma_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, and

\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{m-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
The monic polynomial

f(x)=x^{m}-\sigma_{m-1}x^{m-1}-\cdots-\sigma_{1}x-\sigma_{0}

(2)

in
\mathbb{F}[x]
with
f(0)=-\sigma_{0}\neq 0
is called the
characteristic polynomial
of the recurrence
relation (
1
) and a sequence
{\bf s}
that satisfies (
1
) is called an
f
-sequence
.
Such a sequence is necessarily periodic, and we denote the (smallest) period of an
f
-sequence
{\bf s}
by
{\rm per}({\bf s})
. We say that an
f
-sequence
{\bf s}
in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is
cyclic
if there exists
\alpha\in\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
such that
s_{k+1}/s_{k}=\alpha
for all
k\geq 0
; note that then, necessarily,
f(\alpha)=0
.
Recall that
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
denotes an element of order
n
, and
{\cal U}_{n,q}=\mbox{$\langle\xi\rangle$}
is the group of
n
-th roots of unity.
If there exists an
f
-sequence
{\bf s}
in
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with
{\rm per}({\bf s})=n
such that
{\cal U}_{n,q}=\{s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{n-1}\}
, then we say that
{\cal U}_{n,q}
is
an
f
-subgroup
, and that
{\cal U}_{n,q}
is
represented
by
{\bf s}
. Sometimes, such a group is referred to as a
linear recurring sequence subgroup

[
2
]
.
A rather uninteresting way for
{\cal U}_{n,q}
to be an
f
-subgroup is when
f
is the minimal polynomial of
\xi
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, representing
{\cal U}_{n,q}
by the cyclic
f
-sequence
{\bf s}
with
s_{k}=\xi^{k}
(
k\geq 0
). But sometimes there exist
non-cyclic

f
-sequences that represent
{\cal U}_{n,q}
; in that case, if
f
is a polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we refer to
{\cal U}_{n,q}
as a
non-standard
f
-subgroup
or a
non-standard linear recurring sequence subgroup (NSLRS-group)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

In this paper, we will be mainly interested in the case where the characteristic polynomial of the linear recurrence relation is
irreducible
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Our approach is based on the following results. For precise proofs, we refer to
[
6
]
.

Theorem III.1

Let
g
be irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of degree
m
, and let
\xi
be a zero of
g
of order
n
in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, so that
m={\rm ord}_{n}(q)
. Then a sequence
{\bf s}
is a
g
-sequence in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
if and only if there are
L_{0},\ldots,L_{m-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
such that

s_{k}=L_{0}\xi^{k}+L_{1}\xi^{qk}+\cdots+L_{m-1}\xi^{q^{m-1}k}\qquad(k\geq 0).

(3)

Proof:

(Sketch)
Since
\xi,\xi^{q},\ldots,\xi^{q^{m-1}}
are all zeros of
g
, sequences of the form as in the theorem are indeed
g
-sequences. A dimension argument shows that these are indeed all the
g
-sequences over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
.
∎

Theorem III.2

With the assumptions as in Theorem
III.1
,
a
g
-subgroup
{\cal U}
in an extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is unique, and has the form
{\cal U}=\mbox{$\langle\xi\rangle$}={\cal U}_{n,q}
.

Proof:

(Sketch) All zeros
\xi^{q^{i}}
(
i\in[m]
) of
g
have the same period
n
, and so every non-zero
g
-sequence
{\bf s}
has minimal period
{\rm per}({\bf s})=n
.
∎For a generalization of this result to
f
-subgroups for general
f
, and for additional background and references, see
[
9
]
.
If we now combine the above results, we obtain the following.

Theorem III.3

With the assumptions as in Theorem
III.1
,
a group
{\cal U}
is a
g
-subgroup if and only if
{\cal U}={\cal U}_{n,q}=\mbox{$\langle\xi\rangle$}
.
In addition, there is a one-to-one correspondence between
g
-sequences representing
{\cal U}_{n,q}
and

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear maps
L\in{\cal L}(n,q)
,
where
{\bf s}
corresponds to
L
if
s_{k}=L(\xi^{k})
(
k\geq 0)
; moreover,
{\bf s}
is cyclic if and only if the corresponding map

is in
{\cal L}_{\rm st}(n,q)
.

Proof:

(Sketch) It is well-known that there is a one-to-one correspondence between
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear maps on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
and
q
-polynomials
on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, maps of the form
L(x)=L_{0}x+L_{1}x^{q}+\cdots+L_{m-1}x^{q^{m-1}}
with
L_{0},L_{1},\ldots,L_{m-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
. Now the result essentially is a direct consequence of the expression (
3
) for
g
-sequences in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
.
∎As a consequence, the notion of a non-standard pair
(n,q)
coincides with that of a non-standard
g
-subgroup over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
g
irreducible of order
n
.

IV
Automorphisms of cyclic codes

A linear
[n,k]_{q}
-code
C
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. We sometime refer to vectors in
C
as
code words
. The dual code
C^{\perp}
of
C
is the collection of all vectors
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
for which
({\bf a},{\bf c}):=a_{0}c_{0}+\cdots+a_{n-1}c_{n-1}=0
for all
{\bf c}\in C
. A permutation
\pi\in{\cal S}_{n}
induces a permutation on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
by mapping a vector
{\bf c}=(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})
to the vector

{\bf c}^{\pi}=(c_{\pi^{-1}(0)},c_{\pi^{-1}(1)},\ldots,c_{\pi^{-1}(n-1)}).

The group of permutation automorphisms of
C
, denoted by
{\rm PAut}(C)
, is the collection of all permutations
\pi\in{\cal S}_{n}
with the property that if
{\bf c}\in C
, then
{\bf c}^{\pi}\in C
. For later use, we remark that
{\rm PAut}(C^{\perp})={\rm PAut}(C)

[
11
, Lemma 1.3, (i)]
.
The
cyclic shift
is the permutation
\sigma=(0,1,\ldots,n-1)\in{\cal S}_{n}
, mapping
i
to
i+1\pmod{n}
(
i\in[n])
.
A linear code
C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
is
cyclic
if
\sigma\in{\rm PAut}(C)
. By identifying a vector
{\bf c}=(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with the polynomial
c(x)=c_{0}+c_{1}x+\cdots+c_{n-1}x^{n-1}
in
{\cal R}_{n,q}:=\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]\bmod x^{n}-1
, a code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
corresponds to a subset of
{\cal R}_{n,q}
. A linear code is cyclic if and only if the corresponding subset is an
ideal
in
{\cal R}_{n,q}
. Now any ideal of
{\cal R}_{n,q}
is
principal
, that is, generated by a unique monic polynomial. If
C=(g(x))
is a cyclic code of length
n
, then
g(x)
is called the
generator polynomial
of
C
and
h(x)=(x^{n}-1)/g(x)
is called the
parity-check polynomial
of
C
.

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
have order
n
and degree
m={\rm ord}_{n}(q)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Let
{\rm Tr}(x)=x+x^{q}+\cdots+x^{q^{m-1}}
denote the trace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
The code
C_{n,q}
consisting of all code words

\mu(a)=({\rm Tr}(a\xi^{0}),{\rm Tr}(a\xi^{1}),\ldots,{\rm Tr}(a\xi^{n-1}))

with
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is called the
irreducible
(or
minimal
) cyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Note that
C_{n,q}
is unique up to a permutation, and has dimension
m
.
The dual
C_{n,q}^{\perp}
of
C_{n,q}
has as generator polynomial the minimal polynomial
g(x)
of
\xi
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, which has degree
m
; such a code is called
maximal

[
12
]
.
It is well-known and easily verified that beside the cyclic shift
\sigma
, also the Frobenius mapping
\phi:x\rightarrow qx\pmod{n}
(considered as an element of
{\cal S}_{n}
) is an automorphisms of
C_{n,q}
(to see this, note that if

{\bf c}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, then
c(x)^{q}=c(x^{q})=c^{\phi}(x)
corresponds to
{\bf c}^{\phi}
). We write
{\rm PAut}_{\rm st}(C_{n,q})
to denote the group
\langle\sigma,\phi\rangle
generated by
\sigma
and
\phi
; note that
|{\rm PAut}_{\rm st}(C_{n,q})|=nm
. Recall that, by definition,
C_{n,q}
is a NSIC-code if and only if
{\rm PAut}(C_{n,q})\supsetneq{\rm PAut}_{\rm st}(C_{n,q})
.
The following result shows some unexpected connections between the concept of a NSIC-code
and various other notions of
non-standardness.

Theorem IV.1

An irreducible cyclic code
C_{n,q}
is a NSIC-code if and only if
(n,q)
is a non-standard pair.
More precise, we have that
{\rm PAut}(C_{n,q})={\rm PAut}(C_{n,q}^{\perp})={\cal S}(n,q)
and
{\rm PAut}_{\rm st}(C_{n,q})={\rm PAut}_{\rm st}(C_{n,q}^{\perp})={\cal S}_{\rm st}(n,q)
.

Proof:

First, let
L\in{\cal L}(n,q)
, with
\Psi(L)=\pi\in{\cal S}_{n}
. Recall that
C_{n,q}^{\perp}
is the cyclic code with defining zero
\xi
. So if
{\bf c}\in C_{n,q}^{\perp}
, then
c(\xi)=0
, hence

0=L(0)=L(\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{i}\xi^{i})=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{i}\xi^{\pi(i)}=c^{\pi}(\xi),

and we conclude that
{\bf c}^{\pi}\in C_{n,q}^{\perp}
. Since
{\bf c}\in C_{n,q}^{\perp}
was arbitrary,
\pi\in{\rm PAut}(C_{n,q}^{\perp})
.

On the other hand, let
\pi\in{\rm PAut}(C_{n,q}^{\perp})
. Since
(1,\xi,\ldots,\xi^{m-1})
is a basis for
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we can define an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear map
L
on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
by setting
L(\xi^{i})=\xi^{\pi(i)}
for
i=0,\ldots,m-1
, and then extending
L
by
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearity. We claim that
L\in{\cal L}(n,q)
with
\Psi(L)=\pi
. To see this, let
j\in\{m,\ldots,n-1\}
. There are
a_{0},\ldots,a_{m-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
\xi^{j}=a_{0}+a_{1}\xi+\cdots+a_{m-1}\xi^{m-1}
. This has two consequences. First, by definition of
L
,

L(\xi^{j})=a_{0}\xi^{\pi(0)}+\cdots+a_{m-1}\xi^{\pi(m-1)}.

(4)

Next, the vector

{\bf c}=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{m-1},0,\ldots,0,-1,0,\ldots,0)
, with the entry -1 in position
j
, has
c(\xi)=0
, so
{\bf c}\in C_{n,q}^{\perp}
, and since
\pi\in{\rm PAut}(C_{n,q}^{\perp})
, we have
{\bf c}^{\pi}\in{\rm PAut}(C_{n,q}^{\perp})
, that is,

0=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{i}\xi^{\pi(i)}=a_{0}\xi^{\pi(0)}+\cdots+a_{m-1}\xi^{m-1}-\xi^{\pi(j)}.

(5)

Combining (
4
) and (
5
), we conclude that
L(\xi)=\xi^{\pi(j)}
. Since
j
was arbitrary, we conclude that
L(\xi^{j})=\xi^{\pi(j)}
for all
j\in[n]
,
that is,
\Psi(L)=\pi
.
∎

V
Lifting and extension

In this section, we discuss two methods to create new non-standard pairs from existing ones.

Let
\xi
have order
n
and degree
m
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let
t
be a positive integer for which
\gcd(m,t)=1
.
Since
\gcd(n,q^{ti}-1)=\gcd(n,q^{ti}-1,q^{m}-1)=\gcd(n,q^{\gcd(m,ti)}-1)=\gcd(n,q^{\gcd(m,i)}-1)
, we have
m={\rm ord}_{n}(q)={\rm ord}_{n}(q^{t})
, hence the minimal polynomial
g(x)
of
\xi
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is also the minimal polynomial of
\xi
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
. So in view of the results in Section
III
, the following result is not too surprising.

Theorem V.1

(
Lifting
) Let
m
and
t
be as above.
Then

{\cal S}(n,q)={\cal S}(n,q^{t})
and
{\cal S}_{\rm st}(n,q)={\cal S}_{\rm st}(n,q^{t})
. So
(n,q)
is non-standard if and only if
(n,q^{t})
is non-standard.

Proof:

(Sketch)
Let
L\in{\cal L}(n,q)
, so let
L(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}L_{i}x^{q^{i}}
on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
with
L_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
(
i\in[m]
), where we consider the indices modulo
m
. Define
\tilde{L}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}L_{it}x^{q^{ti}}
(
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{mt}}
). Then
L=\tilde{L}
on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, and
\tilde{L}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
-linear on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{tm}}
. Moreover, since
\langle\xi\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
, we have
\tilde{L}(\xi^{i})=L(\xi^{i})=\xi^{\pi(i)}
for all
i\in[n]
, where
\pi=\Psi(L)
, so
\tilde{L}\in{\cal L}(n,q^{t})
and
\Psi(\tilde{L})=\Psi(L)
.
Conversely, if
\tilde{L}\in{\cal L}(n,q^{t})
, then
\tilde{L}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
-linear, hence
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear, on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{mt}}
, and since
\tilde{L}
fixes
\langle\xi\rangle
set-wise,
\tilde{L}
also fixes
\mathbb{F}_{q}(\xi)=\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
set-wise. So the restriction
L
of
\tilde{L}
to
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
is in
{\cal L}(n,q)
.
The claims are now obvious.
∎We will refer to the operation of passing from a non-standard pair
(n,q)
to a non-standard pair
(n,q^{t})
with
\gcd(m,t)=1
as
lifting
. In coding terms, note that if
\tilde{C}
is the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
-span of the code
C_{n,q}
, then
{\rm PAut}(\tilde{C})={\rm PAut}(C_{n,q})
. And we have
C_{n,q^{t}}\subseteq\tilde{C}
, with equality if and only if the parity-check polynomial
g
of
C_{n,q}
(and of
\tilde{C}
) is also irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
, which holds if and only if
\gcd(m,t)=1
.

The above operation on a non-standard pair
(n,q)
changed the value of
q
. Our next operation changes
n
.
Recall that if
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{*}
has order
n
, then
d=\delta_{q}(n)=n/\gcd(n,q-1)
is the smallest positive integer such that
\xi^{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
; in addition,
n=de
with
e=\gcd(n,q-1)
and
\gcd(d,(q-1)/e)=1
.
First, we prove a lemma.

Lemma V.2

Let
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
have order
n
and
q
-order
\delta_{q}(\xi)=\delta_{q}(n)=d=n/\gcd(n,q-1)
, and let
{\cal G}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
with
\xi^{d}\in{\cal G}
. Then
{\cal G}\langle\xi\rangle\leq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{*}
and
|{\cal G}\langle\xi\rangle|=d|{\cal G}|
.

Proof:

Obviously,
{\cal G}\mbox{$\langle\xi\rangle$}\leq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{*}
. Since
\xi^{d}\in{\cal G}
, every element of
{\cal G}\langle\xi\rangle
has the form
g\xi^{i}
with
g\in{\cal G}
and
i\in[d]
. By definition of the
q
-order, all these elements are distinct, hence
{\cal G}\langle\xi\rangle
has order
d|{\cal G}|
.
∎Now we have the following ‘extension” result.

Theorem V.3

(
Extension
)
Let
n=de
with
e=\gcd(n,q-1)
and
d=\delta_{q}(n)
, so that
\gcd(d,(q-1)/e)=1
. For a positive integer
f
, we have
d=\delta_{q}(n)=\delta_{q}(nf)
if and only if
f\mid(q-1)/e
. Let
f\mid(q-1)/e
and let
\theta
have order
nf
in an extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
\theta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{*}
, and
\langle\theta\rangle{\cal L}(n,q)\leq{\cal L}(nf,q)
;
in particular, if
(n,q)
is non-standard and
f\mid d(q-1)/n
, then
(nf,q)
is also non-standard.

Proof:

With the assumptions in the theorem, we have that
\delta_{q}(nf)=def/\gcd(def,q-1)=df/\gcd(df,(q-1)/e)=df/\gcd(f,(q-1)/e)=d
if and only if
f\mid(q-1)/e
. Next, assume that
f\mid(q-1)/e
. Let
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{*}
have order
n
, and let
\theta
have order
nf
.
Now
{\cal G}=\langle\theta^{d}\rangle\leq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
|{\cal G}|=ef
, hence
\langle\theta\rangle={\cal G}\langle\xi\rangle
by Lemma
V.2
. Finally, if
g\in{\cal G}
and
L\in{\cal L}(n,q)
, then
gL(\langle\theta\rangle)=gL({\cal G}\mbox{$\langle\xi\rangle$})\subseteq g{\cal G}\mbox{$\langle\xi\rangle$}\subseteq\mbox{$\langle\theta\rangle$}
, hence
gL\in{\cal L}(n,q)
. Obviously,
{\cal L}_{\rm st}(nf,q)=\langle\theta\rangle{\cal L}_{\rm st}(n,q)
, so the last conclusion is immediate.
∎Extension is related to the following code construction technique.
Let
C
be a cyclic code of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, with parity-check polynomial
g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, so that
g(x)\mid x^{n}-1
, and let
\nu\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
with
\nu^{nf}=1
. Let
\tilde{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{nf}
be the collection of words

{\tilde{{\bf c}}}=(c_{0},\nu c_{1},\ldots,\nu^{n-1}c_{n-1},\nu^{n}c_{0},\ldots,\nu^{nf-1}c_{n-1})

with
{\bf c}=(c_{0},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in C
. If
c(x)=a(x)(x^{n}-1)g(x)
for some
a(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
with
\deg(a)<\deg(g)
, then
{\tilde{c}}(x)=c(\nu x)((\nu x)^{nf}-1)/((\nu x)^{n}-1)=a(\nu x)(x^{nf}-1)/g(\nu x)
, hence
\tilde{C}
is a cyclic code of length
nf
over
{\bf F}_{q}
, with parity-check polynomial
(x^{nf}-1)/g(\nu x)
. Note that if
g
is irreducible with zero
\xi
, then
C=C_{n,q}
, and if the zero
\xi/\nu
of
g(\nu x)
has order
nf
, then
\tilde{C}=C_{nf,q}
.
Remark also that if
\tilde{\nu}
has order
f
, if
C_{0}
is the cyclic code consisting of all scalar multiples of the word
(1,\tilde{\nu},\tilde{\nu}^{2},\ldots,\tilde{\nu}^{f-1})
, and if
C_{1}
is the code with code words
(c_{0},\nu c_{1},\ldots,\nu^{n-1}c_{n-1})
with
(c_{0},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n-1})\in C
, then
C_{1}
is equivalent to
C
and
\tilde{C}
is equivalent to the product code
C_{0}\times C_{1}
.
For more information on this product code construction and its relation with extension, we refer to
[
6
]
.

In
[
6
]
we show that multiple lifts and extensions can always be obtained by a
single
lift, followed by a
single
extension.

VI
Equally-spaced polynomials

A polynomial
f(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is called
equally-spaced
if it is of the form
f(x)=g(x^{k})
for some integer
k\geq 2
. Then
\deg(f)=k\cdot\deg(g)
and if
g(0)\neq 0
, then
{\rm ord}(f)=k\cdot{\rm ord}(g)
and
{\rm ord}_{q}(f)=k\cdot{\rm ord}_{q}(g)
(see
[
6
]
). Equally-spaced polynomials form a rich source of non-standard examples
[
5
]
.

Theorem VI.1

Let
k\geq 2
be a positive integer with
\gcd(k,q)=1
, let
g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
be monic with
g(0)\neq 0
, of order
n\geq 1
and degree
m\geq 1
, and suppose that
f(x)=g(x^{k})
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then the pair
(nk,q)

is non-standard
except when
f(x)=x^{2}+1
.

For a proof, we refer to
[
5
]
or
[
6
]
. In
[
6
]
we also show that if
C
is cyclic of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, with generator polynomial
g(x)
, then the product code
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}\times C
is again cyclic, with generator polynomial
g(x^{k})
.

Note that under the conditions of Theorem
VI.1
, the polynomial
f(x)=g(x^{k})
is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if
{\rm ord}_{nk}(q)=mk
. The next theorem states when this occurs.

Theorem VI.2

Let
k
,
g(x)
,
n
, and
m
be as in Theorem
VI.1
, so with
\gcd(nk,q)=1
and with
g
irreducible, so with
m={\rm ord}_{n}(q)
.
Let
P(n,q)
be the set of numbers that have only prime factors
r

for which
r|n
and
\gcd(r,(q^{m}-1)/n)=1
. Then the polynomial
f(x)=g(x^{k})

is irreducible if and only if

k\in P(n,q)
, with
4\not\,\mid k
if
2\in P(n,q)
and
n\equiv 2\bmod 4
.

The attraction of Theorem
VI.2
is that it is
constructive
. For a proof, we refer to
[
6
]
.

VII
Known non-standard pairs and their codes

The following non-standard pairs
(n,q)
and corresponding irreducible (NSIC) and maximal cyclic codes are known to us.

1) Pairs
(n,q)
with
n\geq 5
prime and
m={\rm ord}_{n}(q)=n-1
. The corresponding codes are the
repetition codes
and their duals, the
even-weight codes
, with corresponding polynomials
f(x)=(x^{n}-1)/(x-1)
. Here,
{\cal L}(n,q)={\cal S}_{n}
.

2) Pairs
(n,q)
with
n=q^{m}-1
, where
m>2
or
m=2,q>2
. The corresponding codes are the
q
-ary simplex codes
and their duals, the primitive BCH codes with designed distance 2, with as polynomials the
primitive
polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Here,
{\cal L}(n,q)={\rm GL}(m,q)
.

3) The pair
(n,q)=(23,2)
, where
m={\rm ord}_{n}(q)=11
, with corresponding code the (dual of the)
binary Golay code
, with group
{\cal L}(23,2)\cong M_{23}
.

4) The pair
(n,q)=(11,3)
, where
m=5
, with corresponding codes the (dual of the)
ternary Golay code
, with group
{\cal L}(11,3)\cong{\rm PSL}(2,11)
.

5) Pairs
(n,q)=(kn_{0},q)
with
m={\rm ord}_{n}(q)=km_{0}
, where
n_{0}>1
and
k\geq 2
are integers with
k>2
if
n_{0}=2
and
m_{0}={\rm ord}_{n_{0}}(q)
.
(See Theorem
VI.2
for the conditions under which this is the case.)
The corresponding codes are
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}\times C_{n_{0},q}
and their duals, with group
{\cal L}(n,q)\cong S_{k}\wr{\rm PAut}(C_{n_{0},q})
(wreath product), and with corresponding polynomials the irreducible
equally-spaced
polynomials
f(x)=g(x^{k})
with
g(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
and
f(x)\neq x^{2}+1
.

6) In addition, for every non-standard pair
(n,q)
with
m={\rm ord}_{n}(q)
, we have non-standard pairs
(nf,q^{t})
for every positive integer
t
such that
\gcd(t,m)=1
and every positive integer
f
such that
f\mid(q^{t}-1)/\gcd(n,q^{t}-1)
that can be obtained from
(n,q)
by
lifting and extension
as described in Section
V
.

Possibly, the above examples exhaust all possibilities. In the next section, we show this to hold for the case where
m=2
.

VIII
Classification for
m=2

For degree
m=2
, we have the following result.

Theorem VIII.1

The non-standard pairs
(n,q)
with
m={\rm ord}_{n}(q)=2
and
q
-order
d=n/\gcd(n,q-1)
are the ones listed in Section
VII
for which
m=2
and are the following.

1) Pairs
(n,q)
where
n=2e>4
with
e\geq 1
integer for which
e\mid q-1
, with both
q
and
(q-1)/e
odd (the equally-spaced case); here
d=2
and
{\cal L}(2e,q)\cong\mathbb{Z}_{e}\wr S_{2}
(wreath product).

2) Pairs
(n,q)
where
n=q^{2}-1
and
q\geq 3
(the primitive case); here
d=q+1\geq 4
and
{\cal L}(n,q)={\rm GL}(2,q)
.
And in addition,
all pairs
(nf,q^{t})
obtained from a non-standard pair

(n=q^{2}-1,q)
as above
by lifting and extension, so with
t
odd and
f\mid(q^{t}-1)/(q-1)
; here
d=q+1
and

{\cal L}(f(q^{2}-1),q^{t})=\langle\theta\rangle{\rm GL}(2,q)
, where
\theta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}^{*}
has order
f(q^{2}-1)
.

Proof:

(Sketch) If
d=2
, then the corresponding polynomial is of the form
x^{2}-\sigma
with
\sigma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and is irreducible over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. A simple analysis, using Theorem
VI.2
,
quickly leads to the non-standard pairs as listed in case 1).
Next, let
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
be an element of degree
m=2
, order
n
, and
q
-order
d>2
, so with minimal polynomial
g(x)=x^{2}-\sigma_{1}x-\sigma_{0}
, where
\sigma_{0},\sigma_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. Let
T\in{\cal L}(n,q)
be the “cyclic shift” map defined by
T(x)=\xi x
on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
,
and let
L\in{\cal L}(n,q)
with
L(1)=1
and
L(\xi)=\omega+\nu\xi
, say. We proceed in several steps.

1. (Normalization) The element
\tilde{\xi}=\xi/\sigma_{1}
has minimal polynomial
\tilde{g}(x)=x^{2}-x-\lambda
, where
\lambda=\sigma_{0}/\sigma_{1}^{2}
. Obviously, the
q
-order of
\tilde{\xi}
again equals
d
.
Writing
\tilde{\omega}=\omega/\sigma_{1}
, we have
T(1)=\sigma_{1}\tilde{\xi},T(\tilde{\xi})=\sigma_{1}\tilde{\xi}^{2}=\sigma_{1}(\tilde{\xi}+\lambda)
and
L(1)=1,L(\tilde{\xi})=\nu\tilde{\xi}+\tilde{\omega}
. Put
D={\rm diag}(1,\sigma_{1}^{-1})
and define

\tilde{L}=L^{D}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\tilde{\omega}\\
0&\nu\end{array}\right],\qquad\tilde{T}=\sigma_{1}^{-1}T^{D}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\lambda\\
1&1\end{array}\right].

Note that
\tilde{L}
and
\tilde{T}
are the matrices of the maps
L
and
\sigma_{1}^{-1}T
on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, with respect to the basis
(1,\tilde{\xi})
.

2. (Subgroup of
{\rm PGL}(2,q)
) Next, we identify the points of the 1-dimensional projective geometry
{\rm PG}(1,q)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with the sets
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\alpha
(
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
), and we consider the group
{\cal G}=\langle\tilde{T},\tilde{L}\rangle
generated by the matrices
\tilde{T}
and
\tilde{L}
as a subgroup of
{\rm PGL}(2,q)
.
Since
\tilde{\xi}^{d}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, every power
\tilde{\xi}^{j}
of
\tilde{\xi}
is of the form
a\tilde{\xi}^{i}
for some
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,d-1\}
and some
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, hence
{\cal G}\leq{\rm PGL}(2,q)
fixes the set
O=\{1,\xi,\ldots,\xi^{d-1}\}
, considered as a subset of
{\rm PG}(1,q)
, set-wise.

3. (Subgroup structure of
{\rm PGL}(2,q)
) Now we use the known subgroup structure of
{\rm PGL}(2,q)
, see, e.g.,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
, and a separate analysis for the cases
3\leq d\leq 5
, to conclude that either
L
is standard, or
{\cal G}
is conjugate in
{\rm PGL}(2,q)
to one of its subgroups
{\rm PGL}(2,q_{0})
or
{\rm PSL}(2,q_{0})
. Assuming the last case, we can then show that
d=q_{0}+1
, where
q=q_{0}^{t}
with
t
odd, and
\lambda,\tilde{\omega},\nu\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}

with
\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}=\mathbb{F}_{p}(\lambda)
,
hence
\tilde{\xi}\in\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}^{2}
.

4. As a consequence,
L
fixes
\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}^{2}}
, and a simple analysis reveals that the restriction of
L
to
\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}^{2}}
is non-standard if
L
itself is non-standard. Moreover, since
L
fixes both
\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}^{2}}^{*}
and
\langle\xi\rangle
set-wise, it also fixes the intersection
\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}^{2}}^{*}\cap\langle\xi\rangle=\langle\xi^{\delta}\rangle
, where
\delta
is the
q_{0}
-order of
\xi
. So, writing
\theta=\xi^{\delta}
, we conclude that the group
\langle\theta\rangle
is non-standard over
\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
, as witnessed by the non-standard map
L
on
\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}^{2}}
.

5. We have shown that
\theta
is non-standard over
\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
, with (maximal)
q_{0}
-order
q_{0}+1
. Now by
[
4
, Theorem 2.4]
,
\theta
is primitive in
\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}^{2}}
.

6. Finally, it can be shown that, as expected, the non-standard group
\langle\xi\rangle
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
can be obtained for the non-standard group
\langle\theta\rangle
over
\mathbb{F}_{q_{0}}
by lifting and extension.
∎In
[
7
]
we shown that a nonstandard element
\xi
of order
n
and degree
m={\rm ord}_{n}(q)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with maximal
q
-order
d=(q^{m}-1)/(q-1)
is necessarily primitive, so has order
n=q^{m}-1
, thus generalizing
[
4
, Theorem 2.4]
for
m=2
to
all

m
. The proof of the generalization is completely different and uses the recently completed classification of 1/2-transitive linear groups
[
13
]
. We take this opportunity to remark that this result
does not
follow from
[
16
, Theorem 2.3]
since the given proof is only valid when the subgroup
G
in that theorem is
proper
.

IX
Discussion and conclusions

Let
n
and
q
be positive integers with
\gcd(n,q)=1
, and let
m
be the multiplicative order of
q
molulo
n
, the smallest positive integer such that
n\mid q^{m}-1
.
We say that the pair
(n,q)
is
non-standard
if the collection
{\cal L}(n,q)
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear maps on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
that fixes the group
{\cal U}_{n,q}
of
n
-th roots of unity set-wise contains maps not of the form
x\rightarrow cx^{q^{j}}
with
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
and
j\in[m]
.
In this paper, we have first linked this notion to that of
non-standard linear recurring sequence subgroups

[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
. Then we showed that a pair
(n,q)
is non-standard precisely when an irreducible cyclic code
of length
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}

has “extra” permutation automorphisms (others than those generated by the cyclic shift and some Frobenius mapping); in this paper, we refer to such codes as
NSIC-codes
.
A result by Brison and Nogueira from
[
4
]
for the case
m=2
states that a non-standard pair
(n,q)
with (maximal)
q
-order
d=\delta_{q}(n)=q+1
is necessarily primitive, so has
n=q^{2}-1
.
Using the known subgroup structure of
{\rm PG}(2,q)
in combination with this result, we have
finished the classification of the non-standard pairs and NSIC-codes
for the case of dimension
m=2

that was initiated by Brison and Nogueira in
[
2
,
3
,
4
]
.

Substantial information is available on the subgroup structure of
{\rm PGL}(m,q)
for
m=3
, and to a lesser extend also for
m=4
, see, e.g.,
[
17
]
.
By using similar methods, in combination with
our generalization of
[
4
, Theorem 2.4]

to
all

m
in
[
7
]
,
we expect that classification is also possible for the case
m=3
, and perhaps even for the case
m=4
.

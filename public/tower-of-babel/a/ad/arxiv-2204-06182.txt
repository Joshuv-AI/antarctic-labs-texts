The Convergence Properties of Infeasible Inexact Proximal Alternating Linearized Minimization
Yukuan Hu, Xin Liu

Abstract
The proximal alternating linearized minimization method ( PALM ) suits well for solving block-structured optimization problems, which are ubiquitous in real applications. In the cases where subproblems do not have closed-form solutions, e.g., due to complex constraints, infeasible subsolvers are indispensable, giving rise to an infeasible inexact PALM ( PALM-I ). Numerous efforts have been devoted to analyzing feasible PALM , while little attention has been paid to PALM-I . The usage of PALM-I thus lacks theoretical guarantee. The essential difficulty of analyses consists in the objective value nonmonotonicity induced by the infeasibility. We study in the present work the convergence properties of PALM-I . In particular, we construct a surrogate sequence to surmount the nonmonotonicity issue and devise an implementable inexact criterion. Based upon these, we manage to establish the stationarity of any accumulation point and, moreover, show the iterate convergence and the asymptotic convergence rates under the assumption of the Łojasiewicz property. The prominent advantages of PALM-I on CPU time are illustrated via numerical experiments on problems arising from quantum physics and 3D anisotropic frictional contact. Keywords. Example 1.1

(Linear constraints)

The feasible region

is the Birkhoff polytope

Example 1.2

(Nonlinear constraints)

The feasible region
\mathcal{S}_{i}
is an ellipsoid in

, namely,
\mathcal{S}_{i}:=\{\mathbf{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}:\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{w}^{\top}A_{i}\mathbf{w}+\mathbf{b}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{w}\leq\alpha_{i}\}

[

[
26
]

[
14

[
9

Owing to the infeasibility, the objective value sequence is not ensured to be monotonic, while the sufficient reduction of the objective value is presumably crucial in proving the stationarity of any accumulation point. The only work exploring the convergence properties of
PALM-I
goes to
[
10

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\|\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(k+1)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{j}^{(k+1)}\|+\sum_{j=i}^{n}\|\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(k)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{j}^{(k)}\|\leq\beta_{1}\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{(k+1)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{(k)}\|,\\
\left\langle\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{(k)},\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{(k+1)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{(k)}\right\rangle\leq\beta_{2}\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{(k+1)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{(k)}\|^{2},\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

where
\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{(k+1)}
is the unique solution of
equation

1.3
, defined as

\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{(k+1)}:=\argmin_{\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\mathcal{S}_{i}}~\left\langle\nabla_{i}f(\mathbf{x}_{<i}^{(k+1)},\mathbf{x}_{\geq i}^{(k)}),\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k)}\right\rangle+\frac{\sigma_{i}^{(k)}}{2}\|\mathbf{x}_{i}-\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k)}\|^{2}.

(1.5)

Based upon
equation

1.4
, they establish a sufficient reduction result over the objective value sequence
\{f(\bar{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)})\}
, where
\bar{\mathbf{z}}^{(k)}:=(\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{1}^{(k)},\ldots,\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{n}^{(k)})

cannot be computed, not to mention

\|\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{(k+1)}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{(k)}\|
is needed for obtaining
\{\mathbf{x}_{j}^{(k)}\}_{j=i}^{n}
. Unfortunately, the authors in
[
10
]
do not discuss these points. In consequence, the convergence properties of
PALM-I
remain to be investigated, particularly with
implementable inexact criteria
. This is essential in providing a theoretical guarantee for the usage of efficient infeasible subsolvers.

Notations and Organization

This paper presents scalars, vectors, and matrices by lower-case letters, bold lower-case letters, and upper-case letters, respectively. The notation
\mathbf{1}
stands for the all-one vector with proper dimension. The notations
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle

We use subscripts to denote the components or blocks of vectors or matrices; e.g.,
\mathbf{x}_{i}
is the
i
-th variable block. Occasionally for brevity, we make abbreviation for the aggregation of variable blocks; e.g.,

, and

(the latter four are also null if the index sets in the subscript are empty).

For a function

(resp.

) is the gradient (resp. subdifferential) of

at certain point where

is differentiable (resp. subdifferentiable). We add a subscript to indicate the block to which the derivative is taken with respect; e.g.,

Given a set

. If the set

Preliminaries

We collect several notions from convex analysis as well as the Łojasiewicz property in this section.

Definition 2.1

(
[
28
]
)
.

Let
G:\mathbb{E}\to(-\infty,\infty]
be a proper closed function, where
\mathbb{E}
is an Euclidean space. For a given
\mathbf{x}\in\mathrm{dom}(G)
, the Fréchet subdifferential of
G
at
\mathbf{x}
, denoted by
\partial G(\mathbf{x})
, is defined as

\partial G(\mathbf{x}):=\left\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{E}:\liminf_{\mathbf{y}\neq\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\to\mathbf{x}}\frac{G(\mathbf{y})-G(\mathbf{x})-\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\rangle}{\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\|}\geq 0\right\}.

When
\mathbf{x}\notin\mathrm{dom}(G)
, we simply set
\partial G(\mathbf{x})=\emptyset
. When
\partial G(\mathbf{x})
is a singleton, we say that
G
is Fréchet differentiable at
\mathbf{x}
and denote the derivative by
\nabla G(\mathbf{x})
.

Remark 2.1
.

(i)

If
G:\mathbb{E}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is proper closed convex, then,

\partial G(\mathbf{x})=\left\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathbb{E}:G(\mathbf{y})-G(\mathbf{x})\geq\left\langle\mathbf{u},\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x}\right\rangle,~\forall~\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{E}\right\},\quad\forall~\mathbf{x}\in\mathrm{dom}(G).

(ii)

If
G:\mathbb{E}\to(-\infty,\infty]
and
H:\mathbb{E}\to(-\infty,\infty]
are proper closed functions, and
G
is Fréchet differentiable at
\mathbf{x}
, then
\partial(G+H)(\mathbf{x})=\nabla G(\mathbf{x})+\partial H(\mathbf{x})
.

(iii)

If
G:\mathbb{E}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is proper closed and
0\in\partial G(\mathbf{x})
, we call
\mathbf{x}
a stationary point of
G
.

Definition 2.2

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let

is an Euclidean space. The function

S^{(t)}:=\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\|\bar{\mathbf{z}}^{(k+1)}-\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\|.

Under the assumptions made in
4.1
,
S^{(0)}<\infty
,
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|\Delta\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\|<\infty
, and
\{\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\}
converges to some stationary point
\bar{\mathbf{z}}
of
F
in
equation

1.2
. It is then easy to get that, for any
t\geq 0
,

\|\mathbf{z}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{z}}\|\leq\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\left\{\|\bar{\mathbf{z}}^{(k+1)}-\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\|+\|\bar{\mathbf{z}}^{(k+1)}-\mathbf{z}^{(k+1)}\|\right\}\leq S^{(t)}+\omega\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{(k+1)},

(5.1)

where the second inequality is from
3.1
.

If there exists
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that
v^{(K)}=\overline{F}
, the asymptotic convergence rates depends only on the choices of
\{\varepsilon^{(k)}\}
because
S^{(t)}\equiv 0
for all sufficiently large

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose the assumptions in
4.1
hold. Let
\bar{\mathbf{z}}
be the unique limit point of the sequence
\{\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\}
generated by
PALM-I
. Assume that there exists
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that
v^{(K)}=\overline{F}
.

(i)

If
\varepsilon^{(k)}=\bar{\varepsilon}\tilde{\rho}^{k}
for any
k\geq 0
, where
\tilde{\rho}\in(0,1)
, then
\|\mathbf{z}^{(k)}-\bar{\mathbf{z}}\|\leq\mathcal{O}(\tilde{\rho}^{k})
for any
k\geq K
.

(ii)

If
\varepsilon^{(k)}=\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{(k+1)^{\ell}}
for any
k\geq 0
, where
\ell>1
, then
\|\mathbf{z}^{(k)}-\bar{\mathbf{z}}\|\leq\mathcal{O}\left(k^{-(\ell-1)}\right)
for any
k\geq K
.

Due to the errors in solving
equation

3.1
,
v^{(k)}=\overline{F}
can hardly happen in implementation. In the remainder of this section, we focus on the cases where
v^{(k)}-\overline{F}>0
for any
k\geq 0
. We first derive a universal upper bound on
S^{(t)}
in this setting.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose the assumptions in
4.1
hold and
\bar{\theta}\in[\theta,1)
. Assume that
v^{(k)}-\overline{F}>0
for any
k\geq 0
. Then we have, for any
t\geq 1
,

S^{(t)}\leq\left[S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}\right]+C_{2}\left[S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}+\omega\varepsilon^{(t)}\right]^{\frac{1-\bar{\theta}}{\bar{\theta}}}+C_{3}E_{\bar{\theta}}^{(t)},

where

\displaystyle E_{\bar{\theta}}^{(t)}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{(k)}+\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}(e^{(k)})^{\bar{\theta}}+(e^{(t)})^{1-\bar{\theta}},

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle:=2\sqrt{\frac{c}{(\gamma-1)L}}\bar{M},\quad\>\>\>q:=\frac{\sqrt{c(\gamma-1)L}}{2},

(5.2)

\displaystyle C_{2}

\displaystyle:=\frac{cp\left(c\bar{M}\right)^{\frac{1-\bar{\theta}}{\bar{\theta}}}}{q(1-\bar{\theta})},\qquad\>C_{3}:=\max\left\{\omega,\frac{\bar{C}_{1}^{\bar{\theta}}}{pq},\frac{cp\bar{C}_{1}^{1-\bar{\theta}}}{q(1-\bar{\theta})}\right\}.

Proof.

In view of
equation

4.12
in the proof of
4.1
, one has the following upper bound on
S^{(t)}
:

\displaystyle S^{(t)}\leq

\displaystyle\|\bar{\mathbf{z}}^{(t)}-\mathbf{z}^{(t-1)}\|+\omega\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{(k)}+\frac{\bar{C}_{1}^{\bar{\theta}}}{pq}\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}(e^{(k)})^{\bar{\theta}}+\frac{cp}{q(1-\bar{\theta})}\left[F(\mathbf{z}^{(t)})-\overline{F}+2\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}C_{1}^{(t)}\|\Delta\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\|^{2}\right]^{1-\bar{\theta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}\right]+\omega\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{(k)}+\frac{\bar{C}_{1}^{\bar{\theta}}}{pq}\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}(e^{(k)})^{\bar{\theta}}+\frac{cp}{q(1-\bar{\theta})}\left[\left|F(\mathbf{z}^{(t)})-\overline{F}\right|^{1-\bar{\theta}}+\bar{C}_{1}^{1-\bar{\theta}}(e^{(t)})^{1-\bar{\theta}}\right],

(5.3)

where the second inequality follows from
equation

4.1
,
2.2
(iii),
3.1
, and the summability of
\{\varepsilon^{(k)}\}
. By
2.1
and
4.3
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\left|F(\mathbf{z}^{(t)})-\overline{F}\right|^{\bar{\theta}}\leq c\cdot\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial F(\mathbf{z}^{(t)}))\leq c\|\mathbf{w}^{(t)}\|

\displaystyle\leq c\bar{M}\left[\|\mathbf{z}^{(t)}-\mathbf{z}^{(t-1)}\|+\|\Delta\mathbf{z}^{(t)}\|\right]\leq c\bar{M}\left[S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}+\omega\varepsilon^{(t)}\right],

where the last inequality uses
3.1
. Therefore, since
\frac{1-\bar{\theta}}{\bar{\theta}}>0
,

\left|F(\mathbf{z}^{(t)})-\overline{F}\right|^{1-\bar{\theta}}\leq\left(c\bar{M}\right)^{\frac{1-\bar{\theta}}{\bar{\theta}}}\left[S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}+\omega\varepsilon^{(t)}\right]^{\frac{1-\bar{\theta}}{\bar{\theta}}}.

(5.4)

Plugging
equation

5.4
into
equation

5.3
, we conclude that

\displaystyle S^{(t)}\leq

\displaystyle\left[S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}\right]+\frac{cp\left(c\bar{M}\right)^{\frac{1-\bar{\theta}}{\bar{\theta}}}}{q(1-\bar{\theta})}\left[S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}+\omega\varepsilon^{(t)}\right]^{\frac{1-\bar{\theta}}{\bar{\theta}}}

\displaystyle+\omega\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{(k)}+\frac{\bar{C}_{1}^{\bar{\theta}}}{pq}\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}(e^{(k)})^{\bar{\theta}}+\frac{cp\bar{C}_{1}^{1-\bar{\theta}}}{q(1-\bar{\theta})}(e^{(t)})^{1-\bar{\theta}}.

This completes the proof by noting the definition of
C_{2}
,
C_{3}
, and
E_{\bar{\theta}}^{(t)}
in
equation

5.2
.
∎

Below, given
v^{(k)}-\overline{F}>0
for any
k\geq 0
, we present the asymptotic convergence rates of
PALM-I
under different values of
\theta
and choices of
\{\varepsilon^{(k)}\}
. Before that, we give a technical lemma, whose proof could be found in Lemma 4 of
[
25
, Chapter 2]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\{a^{(k)}\}
be a nonnegative sequence. If

a^{(k+1)}\leq\left(1-\frac{b}{k}\right)a^{(k)}+\frac{d}{k^{s+1}},

where
b
,
d
,
s
are positive scalars, and
b>s
, then

a^{(k)}\leq\frac{d}{b-s}\frac{1}{k^{s}}+o\left(\frac{1}{k^{s}}\right).

We begin with exponentially decreasing
\{\varepsilon^{(k)}\}
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Suppose the assumptions in
4.1
hold with
\varepsilon^{(k)}=\bar{\varepsilon}\tilde{\rho}^{k}
for any
k\geq 0
, where
\tilde{\rho}\in(0,1)
. Let
\bar{\mathbf{z}}
be the unique limit point of the sequence
\{\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\}
generated by
PALM-I
and
\theta\in[0,1)
is the Łojasiewicz exponent of
F
at
\bar{\mathbf{z}}
. Assume that
v^{(k)}>\overline{F}
for any
k\geq 0
.

(i)

If
\theta=0
, then there exists
\rho_{1}\in(0,1)
such that
\|\mathbf{z}^{(k)}-\bar{\mathbf{z}}\|\leq\mathcal{O}(\rho_{1}^{k})
for all sufficiently large
k
.

(ii)

If
\theta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, then there exists
\rho_{2}\in(0,1)
such that
\|\mathbf{z}^{(k)}-\bar{\mathbf{z}}\|\leq\mathcal{O}(\rho_{2}^{k})
for all sufficiently large
k
.

(iii)

If
\theta\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, then
\|\mathbf{z}^{(k)}-\bar{\mathbf{z}}\|\leq\mathcal{O}\left(k^{-\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}}\right)
for all sufficiently large
k
.

Proof of (i) and (ii).

By the choice of
\{\varepsilon^{(k)}\}
, any
\bar{\theta}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
:
\bar{\theta}\geq\theta
complies with
4.1
(b). Hence,
5.1
is valid for any
\bar{\theta}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
:
\bar{\theta}\geq\theta
. From the proof of
4.4
(i) and the choice of
\{\varepsilon^{(k)}\}
, there exists
k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
:
k_{2}\geq k_{1}
such that
S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}+\omega\varepsilon^{(t)}\in[0,1)
for any
t\geq k_{2}
. Since
\frac{1-\bar{\theta}}{\bar{\theta}}\geq 1
for any
\bar{\theta}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
, one has

\left[S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}+\omega\varepsilon^{(t)}\right]^{\frac{1-\bar{\theta}}{\bar{\theta}}}\leq S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}+\omega\varepsilon^{(t)},\quad\forall~t\geq k_{2}.

Combining the last inequality and
5.1
, we obtain that

S^{(t)}\leq\bar{\rho}S^{(t-1)}+\bar{C}_{2}\varepsilon^{(t)}+\bar{C}_{3}E_{\bar{\theta}}^{(t)},\quad\forall~t\geq k_{2},

where
\bar{\rho}:=\frac{1+C_{2}}{2+C_{2}}\in(0,1)
,
\bar{C}_{2}:=\frac{\omega C_{2}}{2+C_{2}}
,
\bar{C}_{3}:=\frac{C_{3}}{2+C_{2}}
. Invoking the above recursion repeatedly, together with the choice of
\{\varepsilon^{(k)}\}
, yields, for any
t\geq k_{2}
,

\displaystyle S^{(t)}\leq

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}^{t-k_{2}+1}S^{(k_{2}-1)}+\bar{C}_{2}\sum_{k=k_{2}}^{t}\bar{\rho}^{t-k}\tilde{\rho}^{k}+\bar{C}_{3}\sum_{k=k_{2}}^{t}\bar{\rho}^{t-k}E_{\bar{\theta}}^{(k)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}^{t-k_{2}+1}S^{(k_{2}-1)}+\bar{C}_{2}t\max\{\bar{\rho},\tilde{\rho}\}^{t}+\bar{C}_{3}\sum_{k=k_{2}}^{t}\bar{\rho}^{t-k}E_{\bar{\theta}}^{(k)}.

(5.5)

We then proceed with calculations on
E_{\bar{\theta}}^{(t)}
: for any
t\geq 1
,

E_{\bar{\theta}}^{(t)}=\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{(k)}+\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}(e^{(k)})^{\bar{\theta}}+(e^{(t)})^{1-\bar{\theta}}=\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}\tilde{\rho}^{t}}{1-\tilde{\rho}}+\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}^{2\bar{\theta}}\tilde{\rho}^{2\bar{\theta}t}}{(1-\tilde{\rho}^{2})^{\bar{\theta}}(1-\tilde{\rho}^{2\bar{\theta}})}+\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}^{2(1-\bar{\theta})}\tilde{\rho}^{2(1-\bar{\theta})t}}{(1-\tilde{\rho}^{2})^{1-\bar{\theta}}}.

Since
\bar{\theta}\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
,
2\bar{\theta}\leq\min\{2(1-\bar{\theta}),1\}
. Therefore, there exists a positive constant
\bar{M}_{1}
such that
E_{\bar{\theta}}^{(t)}\leq\bar{M}_{1}\tilde{\rho}^{2\bar{\theta}t}
for any
t\geq 1
. Putting this into
equation

5.5
, we achieve

S^{(t)}\leq\bar{\rho}^{t-k_{2}+1}S^{(k_{2}-1)}+\bar{C}_{2}t\max\{\bar{\rho},\tilde{\rho}\}^{t}+\bar{C}_{3}\bar{M}_{1}t\max\{\bar{\rho},\tilde{\rho}^{2\bar{\theta}}\}^{t},\quad\forall~t\geq k_{2}.

Since
\max\{\bar{\rho},\tilde{\rho}^{2\bar{\theta}}\}\in(0,1)
, there exists an integer
k_{3}\in\mathbb{N}
:
k_{3}\geq k_{2}
such that
S^{(t)}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(\max\{\bar{\rho},\tilde{\rho}^{2\bar{\theta}}\}^{\frac{t}{2}}\right)
for all
t\geq k_{3}
. In view of
equation

5.1
, we prove statements (i) and (ii).
∎

Proof of (iii).

By the choice of
\{\varepsilon^{(k)}\}
,
\bar{\theta}=\theta
just complies with
4.1
(b) and hence
5.1
is valid. From the proof of
4.4
(i) and the choice of
\{\varepsilon^{(k)}\}
, there exists
k_{4}\in\mathbb{N}
:
k_{4}\geq k_{1}
such that
S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}+\omega\varepsilon^{(t)}\in[0,1)
for any
t\geq k_{4}
. Since
\theta\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
,
\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}<1
. Therefore, invoking
2.2
(iii),

\left[S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}+\omega\varepsilon^{(t)}\right]^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}\leq\left[S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}\right]^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}+\omega(\varepsilon^{(t)})^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}.

Moreover, for any
t\geq k_{4}
,

S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}\leq\left[S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}\right]^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}.

Combining the above two relations with
5.1
, one has, for any
t\geq k_{4}
,

S^{(t-1)}=\left(S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}\right)+S^{(t)}\leq(2+C_{2})\left[S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}\right]^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}+\omega C_{2}(\varepsilon^{(t)})^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}+C_{3}E_{\theta}^{(t)}.

(5.6)

With simple calculations, we reach

\omega C_{2}(\varepsilon^{(t)})^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}+C_{3}E_{\theta}^{(t)}=\omega C_{2}(\bar{\varepsilon}\tilde{\rho}^{t})^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}+C_{3}\left[\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}\tilde{\rho}^{t}}{1-\tilde{\rho}}+\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}^{2\theta}\tilde{\rho}^{2\theta t}}{(1-\tilde{\rho}^{2\theta})(1-\tilde{\rho}^{2})^{\theta}}+\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}^{2(1-\theta)}\tilde{\rho}^{2(1-\theta)t}}{(1-\tilde{\rho}^{2})^{\theta}}\right].

Putting the last equality into
equation

5.6
, we have the existence of some
\bar{M}_{2}>0
for which

S^{(t-1)}\leq(2+C_{2})\left[S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}\right]^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}}+\bar{M}_{2}\tilde{\rho}^{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}t},\quad\forall~t\geq k_{4}.

Since
\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}>1
, invoking
2.2
(ii), we obtain from the last inequality that

(S^{(t-1)})^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}\leq 2^{\frac{2\theta-1}{1-\theta}}\left[(2+C_{2})^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}\big(S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}\big)+\bar{M}_{2}^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}\tilde{\rho}^{t}\right],\quad\forall~t\geq k_{4},

which further yields

S^{(t)}\leq S^{(t-1)}-C_{4}(S^{(t-1)})^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}+C_{5}\tilde{\rho}^{t},\quad\forall~t\geq k_{4},

(5.7)

where
C_{4}:=2^{-\frac{2\theta-1}{1-\theta}}(2+C_{2})^{-\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}
,
C_{5}:=\bar{M}_{2}^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}(2+C_{2})^{-\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}
.

Let
h_{\theta}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined as
h_{\theta}(x):=x^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}
. Since
\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}>1
,
h_{\theta}
is convex on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Hence, for a fixed
\xi\geq 0
,

\displaystyle(S^{(t-1)})^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}-(\xi t^{-\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}})^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}=h_{\theta}(S^{(t-1)})-h_{\theta}(\xi t^{-\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle h_{\theta}^{\prime}(\xi t^{-\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}})\left[S^{(t-1)}-\xi t^{-\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}}\right]=\frac{\theta\xi^{\frac{2\theta-1}{1-\theta}}}{(1-\theta)t}\left[S^{(t-1)}-\xi t^{-\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}}\right].

Plugging the last inequality into
equation

5.7
, one has, for any
t\geq k_{4}
(possibly after enlargement),

\displaystyle S^{(t)}

\displaystyle\leq S^{(t-1)}-C_{4}\left[(S^{(t-1)})^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}-(\xi t^{-\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}})^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}\right]-C_{4}(\xi t^{-\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}})^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}+C_{5}\tilde{\rho}^{t}

\displaystyle\leq S^{(t-1)}-\frac{C_{4}\theta\xi^{\frac{2\theta-1}{1-\theta}}}{(1-\theta)t}\left[S^{(t-1)}-\xi t^{-\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}}\right]-C_{4}(\xi t^{-\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}})^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}+C_{5}\tilde{\rho}^{t}

\displaystyle=\left[1-\frac{C_{4}\theta\xi^{\frac{2\theta-1}{1-\theta}}}{(1-\theta)t}\right]S^{(t-1)}+\frac{C_{4}\frac{2\theta-1}{1-\theta}\xi^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}}{t^{\frac{\theta}{2\theta-1}}}+C_{5}\tilde{\rho}^{t}

\displaystyle\leq\left[1-\frac{C_{4}\theta\xi^{\frac{2\theta-1}{1-\theta}}}{(1-\theta)t}\right]S^{(t-1)}+\frac{C_{4}\frac{2\theta-1}{1-\theta}\xi^{\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}+C_{5}}{t^{\frac{\theta}{2\theta-1}}}.

Thus, after choosing
\xi
such that
C_{4}\theta\xi^{\frac{2\theta-1}{1-\theta}}>\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}
, we conclude from
5.2
that

S^{(t)}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}}\right),\quad\forall~t\geq k_{4},

which completes the proof of statement (iii) after combination with
equation

5.1
.
∎

equation

3.1
, the finite termination of

when

seems to go beyond the reach of

, no matter how fast

When

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\theta\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and
\ell>\frac{\theta+1}{2\theta}
. Define

\tau(\theta,\ell):=\min\left\{\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}\ell,\ell-1,(2\ell-1)\theta-1,(2\ell-1)(1-\theta)\right\}.

Then
\tau(\theta,\ell)
has the following closed-form expression:

\tau(\theta,\ell)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1-\theta}{\theta}\ell,&\text{if }\ell\in\left[\frac{\theta}{2\theta-1},\infty\right),\\
(2\ell-1)\theta-1,&\text{if }\ell\in\left(\frac{\theta+1}{2\theta},\frac{\theta}{2\theta-1}\right].\end{array}\right.

Theorem 5.3
.

Suppose the assumptions in
4.1
hold with
\varepsilon^{(k)}=\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{(k+1)^{\ell}}
for any
k\geq 0
, where
\ell>1
. Let
\bar{\mathbf{z}}
be the unique limit point of the sequence
\{\mathbf{z}^{(k)}\}
generated by
PALM-I
and
\theta\in[0,1)
is the Łojasiewicz exponent of
F
at
\bar{\mathbf{z}}
. Assume that
v^{(k)}>\overline{F}
for any
k\geq 0
. Then, for all sufficiently large
k
,

\|\mathbf{z}^{(k)}-\bar{\mathbf{z}}\|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{O}\left(k^{-\frac{1-\theta}{2\theta-1}}\right),&\text{if }\ell\in\left[\frac{\theta}{2\theta-1},\infty\right)\text{ and }\theta\in(\frac{1}{2},1),\\
\mathcal{O}\left(k^{-(\ell-1)}\right),&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

(5.8)

Proof.

The proof of this theorem is analogous to that of statement (iii) in
5.2
. By
4.1
, any
\bar{\theta}\in[\theta,1)
:
\bar{\theta}>\max\{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\ell-1}},\frac{1}{2}\}
complies with
4.1
(b). For such
\bar{\theta}
,
5.1
is valid. From the proof of
4.4
(i) and the choice of
\{\varepsilon^{(k)}\}
, there exists
k_{5}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}+\omega\varepsilon^{(t)}\in[0,1)
for any
t\geq k_{5}
. By the choice of
\{\varepsilon^{(k)}\}
,

\displaystyle\omega C_{2}(\varepsilon^{(t)})^{\frac{1-\bar{\theta}}{\bar{\theta}}}+C_{3}E_{\bar{\theta}}^{(t)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega C_{2}(\varepsilon^{(t)})^{\frac{1-\bar{\theta}}{\bar{\theta}}}+C_{3}\left[\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{(k)}+\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}(e^{(k)})^{\bar{\theta}}+(e^{(t)})^{1-\bar{\theta}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\omega C_{2}}{(t+1)^{\frac{1-\bar{\theta}}{\bar{\theta}}\ell}}+C_{3}\left[\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{(k+1)^{\ell}}+\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}^{2\bar{\theta}}}{(2\ell-1)^{\bar{\theta}}k^{(2\ell-1)\bar{\theta}}}+\left(\sum_{k=t}^{\infty}\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}^{2}}{(k+1)^{2\ell}}\right)^{1-\bar{\theta}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\omega C_{2}}{t^{\frac{1-\bar{\theta}}{\bar{\theta}}\ell}}+C_{3}\left[\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}}{(\ell-1)t^{\ell-1}}+\frac{\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}^{2\bar{\theta}}}{(2\ell-1)^{\bar{\theta}}[(2\ell-1)\bar{\theta}-1]}}{(t-1)^{(2\ell-1)\bar{\theta}-1}}+\frac{\bar{\varepsilon}^{2(1-\bar{\theta})}}{(2\ell-1)^{1-\bar{\theta}}t^{(2\ell-1)(1-\bar{\theta})}}\right].

Noticing the definition of
\tau
in
5.3
and putting the last inequality into
equation

5.6
, we have the existence of some
\bar{M}_{3}>0
for which

S^{(t-1)}\leq(2+C_{2})\left[S^{(t-1)}-S^{(t)}\right]^{\frac{1-\bar{\theta}}{\bar{\theta}}}+\frac{\bar{M}_{3}}{t^{\tau(\bar{\theta},\ell)}},\quad\forall~t\geq k_{5}.

Following the similar arguments in the proof of statement (iii) in
5.2
, one could obtain

S^{(t)}\leq\left[1-\frac{C_{4}\bar{\theta}\xi^{\frac{2\bar{\theta}-1}{1-\bar{\theta}}}}{(1-\bar{\theta})t}\right]S^{(t-1)}+\frac{C_{4}\frac{2\bar{\theta}-1}{1-\bar{\theta}}\xi^{\frac{\bar{\theta}}{1-\bar{\theta}}}+C_{6}}{t^{\min\{\frac{\bar{\theta}}{2\bar{\theta}-1},\tau(\bar{\theta},\ell)\frac{\bar{\theta}}{1-\bar{\theta}}\}}},\quad\forall~t\geq k_{5},

where
C_{6}:=\bar{M}_{3}^{\frac{\bar{\theta}}{1-\bar{\theta}}}(2+C_{2})^{-\frac{\theta}{1-\theta}}
. Note that
\min\{\frac{\bar{\theta}}{2\bar{\theta}-1},\tau(\bar{\theta},\ell)\frac{\bar{\theta}}{1-\bar{\theta}}\}>1
due to
\bar{\theta}>\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\ell-1}}
. Thus, after choosing
\xi
such that
C_{4}\bar{\theta}\xi^{\frac{2\bar{\theta}-1}{1-\bar{\theta}}}>\min\{\frac{\bar{\theta}}{2\bar{\theta}-1},\tau(\bar{\theta},\ell)\frac{\bar{\theta}}{1-\bar{\theta}}\}-1
, we conclude from
5.2
that

S^{(t)}\leq\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-[\min\{\frac{\bar{\theta}}{2\bar{\theta}-1},\tau(\bar{\theta},\ell)\frac{\bar{\theta}}{1-\bar{\theta}}\}-1]}\right),\quad\forall~t\geq k_{5}.

Invoking
5.3
and combining
equation

5.1
, it is not difficult to derive

\|\mathbf{z}^{(t)}-\bar{\mathbf{z}}\|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-\frac{1-\bar{\theta}}{2\bar{\theta}-1}}\right),&\text{if }\ell\in[\frac{\bar{\theta}}{2\bar{\theta}-1},\infty),\\
\mathcal{O}\left(t^{-\frac{2\bar{\theta}^{2}\ell-\bar{\theta}^{2}-1}{1-\bar{\theta}}}\right),&\text{if }\ell\in(\frac{\bar{\theta}^{2}+1}{2\bar{\theta}^{2}},\frac{\bar{\theta}}{2\bar{\theta}-1}),\end{array}\right.\quad\forall t\geq k_{5}.

(5.9)

Since
equation

5.9
holds for any
\bar{\theta}\in[\theta,1):\bar{\theta}>\max\{\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\ell-1}},\frac{1}{2}\}
and
k_{5}
does not rely on its value, the best rate exponent must be attained at one of the following two:

\text{(A)}~\max_{\bar{\theta}\in[\frac{\ell}{2\ell-1},1),\bar{\theta}\geq\theta}~\frac{1-\bar{\theta}}{2\bar{\theta}-1};\quad\text{(B)}~\max_{\bar{\theta}\in(\sqrt{\frac{1}{2\ell-1}},\frac{\ell}{2\ell-1}],\bar{\theta}\geq\theta}~\frac{2\bar{\theta}^{2}\ell-\bar{\theta}^{2}-1}{1-\bar{\theta}}.

Note that
\bar{\theta}\geq\frac{\ell}{2\ell-1}
holds for any
\bar{\theta}\geq\theta
if and only if
\ell\geq\frac{\theta}{2\theta-1}
and
\theta\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. Suppose
\ell\geq\frac{\theta}{2\theta-1}
and
\theta\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
. Then the best rate exponent is achieved at (A) with just
\bar{\theta}=\theta
, establishing the first line in
equation

5.8
. Suppose otherwise, it is easy to check that the optimal values of both (A) and (B) are
\ell-1
with the minimizer
\bar{\theta}=\frac{\ell}{2\ell-1}
, leading to the second line of
equation

5.8
. The proof is complete.
∎

The first line of
equation

5.8
recovers the result for
PALM-E

[
5
,
31
]
. In view of this and statement (iii) in
5.2
, it appears that the solutions errors in solving
equation

3.1
do not affect the asymptotic rates of
PALM-I
at all if
\{\varepsilon^{(k)}\}
decreases fast enough.

6
Numerical Experiments

In this section, we use numerical results to validate the convergence of
PALM-I
and demonstrate its merits over
PALM-E
and
PALM-F
. All the numerical experiments presented are run in a platform with Intel(R) Xeon(R) Gold 6242R CPU @ 3.10GHz and 510GB RAM running
Matlab
R2018b under Ubuntu 20.04.

6.1
Optimization with Linear Constraints

The first class of problems under consideration is the one discussed in
[
12
]
, i.e., the
\ell_{1}
penalized discretized multi-marginal optimal transport problems in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
arising from quantum physics, which generally take the form

\begin{array}[]{cl}\min\limits_{\{X_{i}\}_{i=2}^{N}}&\sum\limits_{i=2}^{N}\left\langle X_{i},\Lambda C\right\rangle+\sum\limits_{i<j}\left(\left\langle X_{i},\Lambda X_{j}C\right\rangle+\beta\left\langle X_{i},X_{j}\right\rangle\right)\\
\mathrm{s.}~\mathrm{t.}&X_{i}\in\mathcal{S}:=\{W\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times K}:W\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1},~W^{\top}\varrho=\varrho,~\mathrm{Tr}(W)=0,~W\geq 0\},~~\forall~i.\end{array}

(6.1)

Here,
\beta>0
is the penalty parameter and
N
,
K\in\mathbb{N}
refer, respectively, to the number of electrons in the system and finite elements
\mathcal{T}:=\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
discretizing a bounded domain
\Omega
. The vector
\varrho:=[\varrho_{1},\ldots,\varrho_{K}]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{K}
is defined as
\varrho_{k}:=\int_{e_{k}}\rho(\mathbf{r})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}
for any
k\in\{1,\ldots,K\}
, where
p:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the single-electron density of the system. The diagonal matrix
\Lambda:=\mathrm{Diag}(\varrho)
and
C=(C_{ij})
denotes the discretized Coulomb cost matrix whose diagonal elements are all set to zero to avoid numerical instability:

C_{ij}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\|\mathbf{d}_{i}-\mathbf{d}_{j}\|^{-1},&\text{if }i\neq j,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}\end{array}\right.

with
\{\mathbf{d}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
being the barycenters of elements
\{e_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
. For brevity, let
\mathcal{B}:\mathbb{R}^{K\times K}\to\mathbb{R}^{2K+1}
be a linear operator defined as

\mathcal{B}(W):=[\mathbf{1}^{\top}W^{\top},~\varrho^{\top}W,~\mathrm{Tr}(W)]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{2K+1},\quad\forall~W\in\mathbb{R}^{K\times K},

and
\mathbf{b}:=[\mathbf{1}^{\top},~\varrho^{\top},~0]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{2K+1}
. Then the set
\mathcal{S}
can be expressed simply as
\mathcal{S}=\{W:\mathcal{B}(W)=\mathbf{b},~W\geq 0\}
. This type of constraints has been mentioned in
1.1
. In our experiments, we consider a 1D system with
N=3
electrons and the domain
\Omega=[-1,1]
; the density is a normalized Gaussian, namely,

\rho(x)\propto\exp\left(-x^{2}/\sqrt{\pi}\right),\quad\forall~x\in\mathbb{R}.

We adopt an equal-mass discretization so that all the entries in
\varrho
are identical.

We compare the performances of
PALM-E
and
PALM-I
when solving
equation

6.1
with
K=36
; that is to say, the number of variables equals
36^{2}\times 2=2592
. The proximal parameter is fixed at
\sigma_{i}^{(k)}\equiv\sigma=10^{-2}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and
k\geq 0
. In both
PALM-E
and
PALM-I
, we adapt the semismooth Newton-CG (
ssncg
) proposed in
[
18
]
to efficiently compute the projection
\mathscr{P}_{\mathcal{S}}
. As noted in
1.1
, the infeasibility is inevitable. In this context, particularly with
ssncg
as the subsolver, the residual function
r_{i}(X_{i}^{(k+1)},\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{(k+1)},\tilde{X}_{i}^{(k)})
becomes

\max\left\{-\left\langle\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{(k+1)},\mathcal{B}(X_{i}^{(k+1)})-\mathbf{b}\right\rangle,0\right\}+\|\mathcal{B}(X_{i}^{(k+1)})-\mathbf{b}\|_{\infty},

where
\bm{\lambda}_{i}^{(k+1)}\in\mathbb{R}^{2K+1}
is an approximate dual solution given by
ssncg
. In
PALM-E
, we set
\{\varepsilon^{(k)}\equiv 10^{-7}\}_{k\geq 0}
such that all the subproblems are solved to high accuracy, whereas in
PALM-I
, we pick a nonincreasing sequence
\big\{\varepsilon^{(k)}=\max\{\frac{10^{-1}}{(k+1)^{\ell}},10^{-7}\}\big\}_{k\geq 0}
with
\ell=0.75
. Note that by
4.2
, the stationarity point of
equation

6.1
can be characterized by the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions. The outer
PALM-E
or
PALM-I
framework is therefore stopped once the relative KKT violation is smaller than
10^{-6}
.

PALM-E
and
PALM-I
with random initializations. The built-in “
rand
” function in
Matlab
is invoked to generate 100 initial points and then we plot out the averaged history of the relative KKT violation for both

and

6.2
Optimization with Nonlinear Constraints

The second class of testing problems involves nonconvex quadratic objective functions and multiple ellipsoidal constraints, having the form

\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{z}}&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\mathbf{z},A\mathbf{z}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbf{b},\mathbf{z}\right\rangle\\
\mathrm{s.}~\mathrm{t.}&\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\mathbf{x}_{i},B_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbf{c}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{i}\right\rangle\leq 1,~i=1,\ldots,n\end{array}

(6.2)

where, for
i=1,\ldots,n
,
m_{i}=m\in\mathbb{N}
;
A\in\mathbb{S}^{mn}
, while
\{B_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{S}_{++}^{m}
;
\mathbf{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{mn}
and
\{\mathbf{c}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
. This problem class is related to several domains
[
11
,
15
,
19
]
, as noted in
1.2
. In our implementation,
A
and
\mathbf{b}
are generated by the built-in function “
randn
” in
Matlab
. To form
\{B_{i}=(b_{i,jk})\}_{i=1}^{n}
, we adopt the construction in
[
9
,
14
]
:

b_{i,jk}=10^{\frac{j-1}{m-1}\text{ncond}_{i}},~\text{if }j=k;~~0,~\text{otherwise}.

It is easy to see that
\text{ncond}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
controls the condition number and the spectrum of each
B_{i}
is spread in
[1,10^{\text{ncond}_{i}}]
.

We compare the performances of
PALM-E
,
PALM-F
, and
PALM-I
when solving
equation

6.2
with

and
m=500
; that is, the number of variables is
2500
. We select
\{\text{ncond}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}=\{3.00,3.25,3.50,3.75,4.00\}
. The vectors
\{\mathbf{c}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
are set to be all-zero so that all ellipsoids are concentric. The proximal parameter is fixed at
\sigma_{i}^{(k)}\equiv\sigma=1
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and
k\geq 0
. The three algorithms are armed with different subsolvers. Specifically, both

and

invoke the self-adative alternating direction methods of multiplier proposed in
[

]
(

);

uses the feasible hybrid projection algorithm in
[

]
(

are not necessarily feasible. For

and

, we terminate

if

\|\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k+1)}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{(k)}+\lambda_{i,\textup{{hp}}}^{(k+1)}(B_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k+1)}+\mathbf{c}_{i})\|\leq\frac{\eta}{2}\|\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k+1)}-\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k)}\|_{\infty}.

where

, and

estimates the multiplier associated with the ellipsoidal constraint. It is not difficult to verify that the above inexact criteria help produce iterates fulfilling the assumption in
[

. Regarding

in
PALM-E
and

, the residual function
r_{i}(\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k+1)},\lambda_{i,\textup{{sadmm}}}^{(k+1)},\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{(k)})
becomes

\displaystyle\max\left\{\left\langle\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k+1)},\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k+1)}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{(k)}+\lambda_{i,\textup{{sadmm}}}^{(k+1)}(B_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k+1)}+\mathbf{c}_{i})\right\rangle,0\right\}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left\|\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k+1)}-\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{i}^{(k)}+\lambda_{i,\textup{{sadmm}}}^{(k+1)}(B_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k+1)}+\mathbf{c}_{i})\right\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\lambda_{i,\textup{{sadmm}}}^{(k+1)}\max\left\{-\left[\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k+1)},B_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k+1)}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbf{c}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k+1)}\right\rangle-\alpha_{i}\right],0\right\}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\max\left\{\frac{1}{2}\left\langle\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k+1)},B_{i}\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k+1)}\right\rangle+\left\langle\mathbf{c}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{i}^{(k+1)}\right\rangle-\alpha_{i},0\right\},

where
\lambda_{i,\textup{{sadmm}}}^{(k+1)}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is an approximate dual solution given by
sadmm
. In
PALM-E
, we set

; for

\big\{\varepsilon^{(k)}=\max\{\frac{10^{-1}}{(k+1)^{\ell}},10^{-6}\}\big\}_{k\geq 0}

7
Conclusions

We recognize by examples the indispensability of infeasible subsolvers in
PALM
whenever constraints are complicated and illustrate through numerical simulations that
PALM-I
can be far more efficient than
PALM-E
and
PALM-F
. The shortage of existing works on
PALM-I
motivates us to analyze its convergence properties, particularly in the presence of objective value nonmonotonicity. We achieve this by constructing a monotonically decreasing surrogate sequence. Moreover, an implementable inexact criterion for subsolvers is devised for practical usage.

Futural improvements can be anticipated in several lines. For example, one could incorporate nonsmooth regularization terms into objective function and handle infeasibility and nonsmoothness simultaneously. Besides, the assumptions, such as Hoffman-like error bound, may appear to be restrictive and call for further relaxation. Last but not least, it is worth investigating the convergence properties of
PALM-I
on problems with nonconvex constraints and designing implementable inexact criteria for those contexts.

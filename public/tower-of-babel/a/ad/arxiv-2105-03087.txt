Pointwise Multiplication by the Characteristic Function of the Half-space on Anisotropic Vector-valued Function Spaces
Nick Lindemulder

Abstract
. We study the pointwise multiplier property of the characteristic function of the half-space on weighted mixed-norm anisotropic vector-valued function spaces of Bessel potential and Triebel-Lizorkin type.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 46E35, 46E40; Secondary: 46E30

1.
Introduction

The motivation for this paper comes from the close connection between spaces of vanishing traces and the pointwise multiplier property of the
characteristic function of the underlying domain under consideration, and more particularly, the importance of this connection for interpolation for spaces with vanishing boundary conditions.
The first interpolation theorem for such spaces is due to Grisvard
[
15
]
for
L_{2}
-based Sobolev-Slobodeckii spaces and the real interpolation functor,
which he subsequently extended to the
L_{p}
-based setting in
[
16
]
. The corresponding result for the Sobolev/Bessel potential scale and the complex interpolation functor was subsequently obtained by Seeley
[
56
]
, but also see the more recent works
[
4
]
and
[
32
]
and the references given therein.

The real interpolation results from
[
15
,
16
]
can be considered more elementary than the complex interpolation result from
[
56
]
. Whereas
[
15
,
16
]
has the pointwise multiplier property of
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}
on the Sobolev-Slobodeckii space
W^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the parameter range

(1)

\frac{1}{p}-1<s<\frac{1}{p}

as a byproduct (also see
[
4
, Chapter VIII]
),
[
56
]
has as one of its main ingredients the result due to Shamir
[
57
]
and Strichartz
[
61
]
that
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}
is a pointwise multiplier on the Bessel potential space
H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in the parameter range (
1
).
For a further discussion on this we refer the reader to
[
4
, Chapter VIII]
, in particular,

[
4
, p. 341, 342, 368]
.
Furthermore, extensions to Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
F^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
can be found in
[
13
,
36
,
42
,
43
,
58
,
59
,
60
,
62
]
, also see the monograph
[
51
]
.

Recently, Meyries and Veraar
[
42
]
extended the pointwise multiplier result from
[
57
,
61
]

to the vector-valued setting with Muckenhoupt weights. The weights considered are the power weights
w_{\gamma}
given by

(2)

w_{\gamma}(t,x^{\prime})=|t|^{\gamma},\qquad t\in\mathbb{R},x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1},

for
\gamma\in(-1,p-1)
. It was shown that
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}
is a pointwise multiplier on the weighted vector-valued Bessel potential space
H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w_{\gamma},X)
if
X
is a UMD Banach space (see Section
2.3
) and
s
satisfies

(3)

\frac{1+\gamma}{p}-1<s<\frac{1+\gamma}{p},

where
\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}=\{(t,x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}:t>0\}
.
Furthermore, analogous results were obtained for Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces, without any restrictions on the Banach space
X
.

The motivation to consider the weighted vector-valued setting in
[
42
]
is the

L_{p}
-
L_{q}
-maximal regularity approach to parabolic evolution equations. In this setting the use of temporal and/or spatial weights of the form (
2
) allow one to treat rougher initial and/or boundary data, respectively, and they provide an inherent smoothing effect of the solutions, see for instance the papers
[
5
,
8
,
17
,
28
,
33
,
41
,
45
,
48
]
and the monograph
[
46
]
.
Furthermore, temporal weights are of fundamental importance in the recently developed theory of critical spaces and its applications to nonlinear partial differential equations of quasi- and semilinear type, see
[
1
,
2
,
44
,
49
,
50
,
47
]
.

The pointwise multiplier property of
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}
on
H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w_{\gamma},X)
from
[
42
]
was first used by the author, Meyries and Veraar
[
32
]
to prove results on complex interpolation of weighted vector‐valued Sobolev/Bessel potential spaces on the half‐line with Dirichlet boundary conditions, with as an application the characterization of the fractional domain spaces of the first derivative operator on the half-line. It was subsequently used by Amann
[
4
]
to characterize complex interpolation spaces of Sobolev/Bessel potential spaces on half-spaces with normal boundary conditions.
These interpolation results are (partial) extensions of Seeley’s classical work in this direction
[
56
]
.

In
[
4
]
the above mentioned interpolation result is
[
4
, Theorem VIII.2.4.8]
. Whereas
[
4
]
is mostly concerned with so-called
anisotropic
function spaces,
[
4
, Theorem VIII.2.4.8]
restricts itself to the classical
isotropic
setting. As explainded in
[
4
, Remark VIII.2.4.9]
, the reason for this restriction is the unavailability of an anisotropic version of the pointwise multiplier result from
[
42
]
. A conjectured anisotropic analogue is left as an open problem on
[
4
, p. 342]
. In this paper we will solve this open problem (see Theorem

) and, furthermore, obtain an analogous result for anisotropic Triebel-Lizorkin spaces (see Theorem

).
As an application one could obtain results on interpolation with boundary conditions analogously to
[

. However, we will leave this to the future.

Anisotropic function spaces naturally appear in the study of parabolic partial differential equations, where they provide a description of the corresponding parabolic regularity in which the time and space derivatives contribute in different strength, see e.g.
[
10
,
17
,
24
,
28
,
33
]
. Loosely speaking, there are two approaches to anisotropy in the context of function spaces. On the one hand, there is a Fourier analytic approach, where the anisotropic nature of the function space is obtained by using a suitable anisotropic scaling structure in the Fourier domain, see e.g.
[
4
,
12
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
29
,
28
]
.
On the other hand, there is a functional analytic approach, where the anisotropic nature of the function space is obtained by taking intersections of function space-valued function spaces, see e.g.
[
9
,
24
,
37
,
38
,
46
]
. A bridge between the two approaches is provided by intersection representations, see e.g.
[

,
29
]
, which will play a major role in this paper.

In the maximal
L_{p}
-
L_{q}
-regularity problem for fully inhomogeneous parabolic boundary value problems, Triebel-Lizorkin spaces have turned out to naturally occur in the description of the sharp regularity of the boundary data (see
[

,
21
,

,
28
,

]

For instance, in the special case of the heat equation with Dirichlet boundary condition, the boundary datum has to be in anisotropic mixed-norm Triebel-Lizorkin space

F^{\delta,(\frac{1}{2},1)}_{(p,q),q}(J\times\partial\mathscr{O})=F^{\delta}_{p,q}(J;L_{q}(\partial\mathscr{O}))\cap L_{p}(J;B^{2\delta}_{q,q}(\partial\mathscr{O})),\qquad\delta=1-\frac{1}{2p},

where the identification between these two spaces follows from the intersection representation
[

, Example 5.7]
.
Triebel-Lizorkin spaces can furthermore be important as a technical tool in the study of Sobolev and Bessel potential spaces, see e.g.
[

,
33
,
39
,
41
,
42
,

,
54
,
55
]
for the Banach space-valued setting.
The latter even holds in the weighted setting with power weights
w_{\gamma}
(
2
) with
\gamma
outside the
A_{p}
-range
(-1,p-1)
, see
[

]
and
[

,

,

]
.

Motivated by
[

,

]
, we will extend the corresponding results in
[

]
for weighted Triebel-Lizorkin spaces beyond the

-setting considered there. Furthermore, motivated by the maximal

-

-regularity problem, we will simultaneously extend these results to the anisotropic mixed-norm setting.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Decompositions and Anisotropy

Let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and
\mathpzc{d}\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
. Consider the
\mathpzc{d}
-decomposition of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(4)

\mathbb{R}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{\ell}}.

We write
\mathbb{R}{d}
when we want to indicate that we view
\mathbb{R}^{d}
as being
\mathpzc{d}
-decomposed as in (
4
). For
x\in\mathbb{R}{d}
we accordingly write
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell})
with
x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{j}}
for each
j\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
.

For
\bm{a}\in(0,\infty)^{\ell}
we define the anisotropic quasi-norm
\lvert\,\cdot\,\rvert_{(\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}\colon\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
by

\lvert x\rvert_{(\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}:=\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\lvert x_{j}\rvert^{2/a_{j}}\Bigr)^{1/2},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},

which is a quasi-metric on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}}
.
We also define the anisotropic dilation
\delta_{\lambda}^{(\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}
on
\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}}
for
\lambda>0
by

\delta_{\lambda}^{(\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}x:=(\lambda^{a_{1}}x_{1},\cdots,\lambda^{a_{\ell}}x_{\ell}),\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}}.

2.2.
Weights

A reference for the general theory of Muckenhoupt weights is
[
14
, Chapter 9]
.

A
weight
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a measurable function
w:\mathbb{R}^{d}\longrightarrow[0,\infty]
that takes it values almost everywhere in
(0,\infty)
.
We denote by
\mathcal{W}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the set of all weights
w
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

For
w\in\mathcal{W}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
p\in[1,\infty)
we denote by
L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
the space of all
f\in L_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

||f||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/p}<\infty,

where
L_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the space of equivalence classes of complex-valued measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
\mathpzc{d}
-decomposed as in (
4
).
For
\bm{p}\in[1,\infty)^{\ell}
and
\bm{w}\in\prod_{j=1}^{l}\mathcal{W}(\mathbb{R}{\mathpzc{d}_{j}})
we denote by
L_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w})
the mixed-norm space

L_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w}):=L_{p_{l}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{\ell}},w_{\ell})[\ldots[L_{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{1}},w_{1})]\ldots],

that is,
L_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w})
is the space of all
f\in L_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with

||f||_{L_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{\ell}}}\ldots\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{1}}}|f(x)|^{p_{1}}w_{1}(x_{1})\mathrm{d}x_{1}\right)^{p_{2}/p_{1}}\ldots w_{\ell}(x_{\ell})\mathrm{d}x_{\ell}\right)^{1/p_{\ell}}<\infty.

We equip
L_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w})
with the norm
||\,\cdot\,||_{L_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w})}
, which turns it into a Banach space.

Given a Banach space
X
, we denote by
L_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)
the associated Bochner space

L_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X):=L_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w})(X)=\{f\in L_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X):||f||_{X}\in L_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w})\}.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
we denote by
A_{p}=A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the class of all Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
-weights, which are all the locally integrable weights on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for which the
A_{p}
-characteristic

[w]_{A_{p}}:=\sup_{Q\text{ cube in }\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(x)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)\in[1,\infty]

is finite,
where in the supremum we consider cubes
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes.
We furthermore set
A_{\infty}:=\bigcup_{p\in(1,\infty)}A_{p}
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and let
w
be a weight on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Denoting by
w^{\prime}_{p}=w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
the
p
-dual weight of
w
, we have

w\in A_{p}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w^{\prime}_{p}\in A_{p^{\prime}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w,w^{\prime}_{p}\in A_{\infty}.

The most important weight for this paper is the power weight

w_{\gamma}(x_{1},x^{\prime})=|x_{1}|^{\gamma},\qquad x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1},

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
.
For this weight we have

w_{\gamma}\in A_{p}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\gamma\in(-1,p-1)

and

w_{\gamma}\in A_{\infty}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\gamma\in(-1,\infty).

Furthermore,
(w_{\gamma})^{\prime}_{p}=w_{\gamma^{\prime}_{p}}
with
\gamma^{\prime}_{p}=-\frac{\gamma}{p-1}
.

2.3.
UMD Spaces

We refer the reader to
[
19
, Chapter 4]
for an introduction to the theory of UMD spaces.

Let us state some facts:

•

Every Hilbert space is a UMD space;

•

If
X
is a UMD space,
(S,\Sigma,\mu)
is
\sigma
-finite and
p\in(1,\infty)
, then
L_{p}(S;X)
is a UMD space.
A generalization of this to the setting of reflexive Musielak-Orlicz spaces can be found in
[
34
]
.

•

UMD spaces are reflexive. In particular,
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
are not UMD.

•

Closed subspaces and quotients of UMD spaces are again UMD spaces. In particular, reflexive Besov, Triebel-Lizorkin and Sobolev spaces are UMD.

2.4.
Function Spaces

For the theory of Banach space-valued distributions we refer the reader to

[
4
]
. Let us explicitely mention that, given a Banach space
X
, we denote by
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X)
the space of
X
-valued Schwartz functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and we denote by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X)=\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),X)
the space of
X
-valued tempered distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Furthermore, we denote by
\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{M}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X)
the space of slowly increasing smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This means that
f\in\mathscr{O}_{\mathrm{M}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X)
if and only if
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X)
and, for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, there exist
m_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{N}
and
c_{\alpha}>0
such that

||D^{\alpha}f(x)||_{X}\leq c_{\alpha}(1+|x|^{2})^{m_{\alpha}},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Let
X
be a Banach space, let
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathpzc{d}\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
.
For
\bm{a}\in(\frac{1}{\mathbb{N}})^{\ell}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
we define the anisotropic Bessel potential operator
J^{(\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}_{s}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X))
by

J^{(\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}_{s}f:=\left[\bigl((1+\lvert\xi\rvert_{(\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}^{2})^{s/2}\cdot\widehat{f}\bigr)\right]^{\vee},\qquad\qquad f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X),

and for
\bm{p}\in(1,\infty)^{\ell}
and
\bm{w}\in\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}A_{p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{j}})
we define the corresponding Bessel potential space
H^{s,\bm{a}}_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)
by

\displaystyle H^{s,\bm{a}}_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X):=\left\{f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X):J^{(\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}_{s}f\in L_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)\right\},

\displaystyle||f||_{H^{s,\bm{a}}_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)}:=||J^{(\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}_{s}f||_{L_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{p}},\bm{w};X)}.

Furthermore, for
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
,
\bm{\omega}\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
,
\bm{p}\in(1,\infty)^{\ell}
and
\bm{w}\in\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}A_{p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{j}})
we define the Bessel potential space
H^{\sigma/\bm{\omega}}_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)
by

H^{\sigma/\bm{\omega}}_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X):=H^{\sigma/\dot{\omega},\bm{\omega}/\dot{\omega}}_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X),

where
\dot{\omega}=\mathrm{lcm}(\bm{\omega})
, the least common multiple of
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{\ell}
.
The Bessel potential space
H^{\sigma/\bm{\omega}}_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)
is a weighted version of the Bessel potential space
H^{\sigma/\bm{\omega}}_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}};X)
from
[
4
]
, also see
[
24
]
.

Let
\bm{a}\in(\frac{1}{\mathbb{N}})^{\ell}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that
\mathrm{gcd}(\bm{a}^{-1})=1
, the greatest common divisor of
\frac{1}{a_{1}},\ldots,\frac{1}{a_{\ell}}
, and write
m=(\frac{1}{a_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot\frac{1}{a_{\ell}})
.
Then, setting
\bm{\omega}=m\bm{a}\in\mathbb{N}^{\ell}
and
\sigma=ms
, we have
m=\dot{\omega}=\mathrm{lcm}(\bm{\omega})
, so that
s=\sigma/\dot{\omega}
and
\bm{a}=\bm{\omega}/\dot{\omega}
, and thus

(5)

H^{s,\bm{a}}_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)=H^{\sigma/\bm{\omega}}_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X),\qquad\qquad\text{isometrically}.

The following proposition makes it possible to obtain a version of this for the case
\mathrm{gcd}(\bm{a}^{-1})>1
, see Corollary
2.2
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a UMD Banach space,
\bm{a}\in(\frac{1}{\mathbb{N}})^{\ell}
,
\bm{p}\in(1,\infty)^{\ell}
,
\bm{w}\in\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}A_{p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{j}})
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
k\bm{a}\in(\frac{1}{\mathbb{N}})^{\ell}
.
Then

(6)

H^{s,\bm{a}}_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)=H^{ks,k\bm{a}}_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X).

Proof.

We need to show that
J^{(\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}_{s}J^{(k\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}_{-ks}
and
J^{(k\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}_{ks}J^{(\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}_{-s}
are bounded operators on
L_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)
.
By relabeling the parameters, it suffices to consider the first. So we need to show that

m(\xi):=\frac{(1+\lvert\xi\rvert_{(\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}^{2})^{s/2}}{(1+\lvert\xi\rvert_{(k\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}^{2})^{ks/2}},\qquad\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},

defines a Fourier multiplier on
L_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)
.
By by using the weighted anisotropic mixed-norm Mikhlin multiplier theorem
[
35
, Theorem 7.1]
, this can be done through a standard scaling argument, as follows.

We define the function
M:\mathbb{R}^{d+1}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}
by

M(t,\xi):=\frac{(t^{2k}+\lvert\xi\rvert_{(\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}^{2})^{s/2}}{(t^{2}+\lvert\xi\rvert_{(k\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}^{2})^{ks/2}},\qquad\qquad(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}}\setminus\{(0,0)\},

which is a
C^{\infty}
-function as
\bm{a},k\bm{a}\in(\frac{1}{\mathbb{N}})^{\ell}
. Furthermore, note that
M
is homogeneous of order
0
with respect to the scaling
(t,\xi)\mapsto(\lambda t,\delta^{(\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}_{\lambda}\xi)
, that is,
M(t,\xi)=M(\lambda t,\delta^{(k\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}_{\lambda}\xi)
for all
\lambda>0
. This homogeneity implies that

D^{(0,\beta)}M(t,\xi)=\lambda^{k\bm{a}\cdot\beta}[D^{(0,\beta)}M](\lambda t,\delta^{(k\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}_{\lambda}\xi),\qquad\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\mathpzc{d}},\lambda>0,(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}}\setminus\{(0,0)\},

where
k\bm{a}\cdot\beta=k\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}a_{j}\beta_{j}
. Therefore, for each
\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{\mathpzc{d}}
,

M_{\beta}(t,\xi):=\lvert\xi\rvert_{(k\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}^{k\bm{a}\cdot\beta}D^{(0,\beta)}M(t,\xi),\qquad\qquad(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}}\setminus\{(0,0)\},

defines a
C^{\infty}
-function that is homogeneous of order
0
with respect to the scaling
(t,\xi)\mapsto(\lambda t,\delta^{(\bm{a},\mathpzc{d})}_{\lambda}\xi)
and thus is a bounded function.
In particular, taking
t=1

r_{E}=\sup\Big\{r\in(0,\infty):E^{[r]}\text{is a UMD Banach function space}\Big\}.

Suppose that
s>\max\{\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{p},\frac{1+\gamma}{p},\frac{1}{r_{E}}\}-1
. Let
u\in(0,\infty)
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
s>\max\{\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{p},\frac{1+\gamma}{p},\frac{1}{r_{E}}\}-\frac{1}{u}
and
m>s
. Then, for all
f\in L_{1,{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R};E(X))
,

(16)

||f||_{\mathbb{F}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};E;X)}\eqsim||f||_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};E(X))}+\Big|\Big|\left(\int_{0}^{1}[t^{-s}d^{m}_{t,u}f]^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)^{1/q}\Big|\Big|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};E(X))}

Proof.

Note that

\max\Big\{\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{p},\frac{1+\gamma}{p},\frac{1}{r_{E}}\Big\}=\inf\left\{\frac{1}{r}:r\in(0,p\wedge q),E^{[r]}\text{is a UMD Banach function space},w_{\gamma}\in A_{p/r}\right\}.

The discrete version of (
16
) thus follows from

[
29
, Corollary 4.7]
(and
[
29
, Examples 3.5
\&
3.20]
):
for all
f\in L_{1,{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R},E(X))
,

||f||_{\mathbb{F}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};E;X)}\eqsim||f||_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};E(X))}+\Big|\Big|\left(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}[2^{ns}d^{m}_{2^{-n},u}f]^{q}\right)^{1/q}\Big|\Big|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};E(X))}.

The equivalence (
16
) can be obtained from this by a standard monotonicty argument.
∎

3.
Pointwise Multiplication on Bessel Potential Spaces

In this subsection we will extend the multiplier property of
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}
on
H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w_{\gamma},X)
from
[
42
, Theorem 1.1]
to the anisotropic mixed-norm setting. The approach in
[
42
]
is based on the paraproduct technique as introduced by Bony (see e.g.
[
6
]
) and a randomized Littlewood-Paley decomposition. In the context of pointwise multipliers, the use of paraproduct techniques goes back to Peetre
[
43
]
and Triebel
[
62
]
in order to treat Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces in the full parameter range
p,q\in(0,\infty]
.

Alternative proofs of
[
42
, Theorem 1.1]
were afterwards obtained by the author
[
26
]
and by the author, Meyries and Veraar
[
32
]
.
The latter proof is based on the representation of fractional powers of the negative Laplacian as a singular integral and the Hardy–Hilbert inequality and is much simpler than the proofs in

[
26
,
42
]
.

In order to extend
[
42
, Theorem 1.1]
to the anisotropic mixed-norm setting, we could try to extend one of its proofs from
[
26
,
32
,
42
]
. However, we will not go into this direction. Instead, we will simply bootstrap the result itself to the anisotropic mixed-norm setting through the intersection representation Theorem
2.3
and the sum representation Corollary
2.4
.

For
i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
we write

(17)

\mathbb{R}{d}_{+,i}=[\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{1}}\times\cdots\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{i-1}}]\times\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{i}}_{+}\times[\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{i+1}}\times\cdots\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{\ell}}]=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}}:x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{i}}_{+}\}.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space,
\bm{a}\in(\frac{1}{\mathbb{N}})^{\ell}
,
\bm{p}\in(1,\infty)^{\ell}
,
\bm{w}\in\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}A_{p_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{j}})
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Let
i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
and suppose that
\mathpzc{d}_{i}=1
and
w_{i}=w_{\gamma}
for some
\gamma\in(-1,p_{i}-1)
.
If

a_{i}\left(\frac{1+\gamma}{p_{i}}-1\right)<s<a_{i}\frac{1+\gamma}{p_{i}},

then
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}{d}_{+,i}}
is a pointwise multiplier on the space
H^{s,\bm{a}}_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)
in the sense that

||\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}{d}_{+,i}}f||_{H^{s,\bm{a}}_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)}\lesssim||f||_{H^{s,\bm{a}}_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)},\qquad f\in H^{s,\bm{a}}_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)\cap L_{\bm{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X).

Proof.

Note that the case
s=0
is trivial. So we only need to treat the case
s\neq 0
. In the notation of Theorem
2.3
and Corollary
2.4
, it trivially holds that

(18)

\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}{d}_{+,i}}\in\mathcal{L}\left(L_{\check{\bm{p}}_{j}}\Big(\mathbb{R}^{\check{\mathpzc{d}}_{j}},\check{\bm{w}}_{j};H^{s/a_{j}}_{p_{j}}\big(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{j}},w_{j};L_{\hat{\bm{p}}_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{\hat{\mathpzc{d}}_{j}},\hat{\bm{w}}_{j};X)\big)\Big)\right)

for each
j\neq i
, as a pointwise multiplier. By Theorem
2.3
or Corollary
2.4
, depending on whether
s>0
or
s<0
, respectively, it thus suffices that (
18
) is also valid for
j=i
. Denoting
Y=L_{\hat{\bm{p}}_{i}}(\mathbb{R}^{\hat{\mathpzc{d}}_{i}},\hat{\bm{w}}_{i};X)
, the latter is equivalent to
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}
being a pointwise multiplier on
H^{s/a_{i}}_{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};Y)
. As the UMD property of
X
is inherited by
Y
(see
[
19
, Proposition 4.2.15]
) and as
\frac{1+\gamma}{p_{i}}-1<\frac{s}{a_{i}}<\frac{1+\gamma}{p_{i}}
,
[
42
, Theorem 1.1]
yields that
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is indeed a pointwise multiplier on
H^{s/a_{i}}_{p_{i}}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};Y)
, as desired.
∎

4.
Pointwise Multiplication on Triebel-Lizorkin spaces

The following theorem is an analogue of Theorem
3.1
for the Triebel-Lizorkin scale, also see Corollary
4.5
and Remark
4.7
below. In its formulation we use the notation
\mathbb{R}{d}_{+,i}
from (
17
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space,
\bm{a}\in(0,\infty)^{\ell}
,
\bm{p}\in(0,\infty)^{\ell}
,
q\in(0,\infty]
,
\bm{w}\in\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}A_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{j}})
and
s\in(0,\infty)
. Let
\bm{r}\in(0,\infty)^{\ell}
be such that
r_{j}<p_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge p_{j}\wedge q
for
j=1,\ldots,\ell
and
\bm{w}\in\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}A_{p_{j}/r_{j}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{j}})
.
Let
i\in\{1,\ldots,\ell\}
and suppose that
\mathpzc{d}_{i}=1
and
w_{i}=w_{\gamma}
for some
\gamma\in(-1,\infty)
.
If

(19)

a_{i}\left(\max\big\{\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{p_{1}},\ldots,\frac{1}{p_{i}},\frac{1+\gamma}{p_{i}}\big\}-1\right)_{+}\,+\,\sum_{j=1,\ldots,\ell;j\neq i}a_{j}\mathpzc{d}_{j}\big(\frac{1}{r_{j}}-1\big)_{+}<s<a_{i}\frac{1+\gamma}{p_{i}},

then
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}{d}_{+,i}}
is a pointwise multiplier on the space
F^{s,\bm{a}}_{\bm{p},q}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)\subset L_{1,{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X)
.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that, if
\bm{p}\in(1,\infty)^{\ell}
,
q\in[1,\infty]
and
w_{j}\in A_{p_{j}}
for each
j\neq i
, then each
r_{j}
with
j\neq i
can be taken arbitrarily close to
1
and the condition (
19
) reduces to

(20)

a_{i}\left(\frac{1+\gamma}{p_{i}}-1\right)_{+}<s<a_{i}\frac{1+\gamma}{p_{i}}.

Remark 4.3
.

Note that, if
\ell=2
,
\bm{a}=(1,1)
,
\bm{p}=(p,p)
,
i=1
and
w_{2}=\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}_{2}}}
, then
r_{2}
can be taken arbitrarily close to
q\wedge p\wedge\frac{p}{1+\gamma}
and condition (
19
) reduces to

(21)

\left(\max\big\{\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{p},\frac{1+\gamma}{p}\big\}-1\right)_{+}\,+\,(d-1)\left(\max\big\{\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{p}\big\}-1\right)_{+}<s<\frac{1+\gamma}{p}.

As a consequence, if
s
satisfies (
21
),
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}
is a pointwise multiplier on
F^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w_{\gamma};X)\subset L_{1,{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X)
. For
X=\mathbb{C}
,
p\in(0,1)
,
\gamma=0
this gives back the case
p\in(0,1)
of
[
62
, Theorem 2.8.7]
.

Remark 4.4
.

The corresponding version of Theorem
4.1
(and Corollary
4.5
below) for Besov spaces could be derived by means of real interpolation. However, to the best of the author’s knowledge, real interpolation results for weighted Triebel-Lizorkin spaces seem to be only present in the literature in the scalar-valued isotropic setting, see
[
7
,
52
]
.

In the following corollary we remove the positive part from the left-hand side of (
20
), which yields an extension of the

-case of

, Theorem 1.3]
to the anisotropic setting with
\gamma
beyond the

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
X
be a Banach space,
\bm{a}\in(0,\infty)^{\ell}
,
\bm{p}\in(1,\infty)^{\ell}
,

and

.
Let

\mathpzc{d}_{i}=1

Remark 4.6

Remark 4.7
.

Theorem

Proof of Corollary
4.5
.

If
\gamma\geq p_{i}-1

4.1
(through Remark
4.2

4.1
(and Remark
4.2

s<0

Pick

(and Remark

(24)

\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}{d}_{+,i}}f\in F^{s,\bm{a}}_{\bm{p},q}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X),\qquad\qquad f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X).

.
In this case we can apply Proposition
2.5
to obtain (

w_{j}^{\prime}\in A_{p^{\prime}_{j}}

(and Remark

Now, in order to derive (

where

\displaystyle\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}{d}_{+,i}}f,g\rangle

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:cor:thm:pm_F;dual;q-finite;norming;pairing}}}{{=}}\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}{d}_{+,i}}f,g\rangle_{\langle\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X),\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X^{*})\rangle}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}{d}_{+,i}}f(x),g(x)\rangle_{\langle X,X^{*}\rangle}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\langle f(x),\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}{d}_{+,i}}g(x)\rangle_{\langle X,X^{*}\rangle}\mathrm{d}x=\langle f,\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}{d}_{+,i}}g\rangle_{\langle\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X),\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d};X^{*})\rangle}

||\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}{d}_{+,i}}f||_{F^{s,\bm{a}}_{\bm{p},q}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)}\lesssim||f||_{F^{s,\bm{a}}_{\bm{p},q}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)}||\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}{d}_{+,i}}g||_{F^{-s,\bm{a}}_{\bm{p}^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w}^{\prime}_{\bm{p}};X^{*})}\lesssim||f||_{F^{s,\bm{a}}_{\bm{p},q}(\mathbb{R}^{\mathpzc{d}},\bm{w};X)}.

We will use the intersection representation from Theorem
2.8
to reduce Theorem
4.1
to the one-dimensional setting. The following lemma treats the corresponding one-dimensional case.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
X
be a Banach space,
E
a quasi-Banach function space,
p\in(0,\infty)
,
q\in(0,\infty]
,
\gamma\in(-1,\infty)
and
s\in(0,\infty)
.
Suppose that
E^{[r]}
is a UMD Banach function space for some
r\in(0,\infty)
and put

(26)

r_{E}=\sup\Big\{r\in(0,\infty):E^{[r]}\text{is a UMD Banach function space}\Big\}.

If
\max\{\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{p},\frac{1+\gamma}{p},\frac{1}{r_{E}}\}-1<s<\frac{1+\gamma}{p}
, then
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}
is a pointwise multiplier on the space

\mathbb{F}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};E;X)\subset L_{1,{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R};E(X))

.

In the proof of this lemma we will use a description of
\mathbb{F}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};E;X)
in terms of differences and a Hardy inequality. Such a strategy was used before in connection to the pointwise multiplier property of
1_{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}
by Strichartz
[
61
]
for scalar-valued Bessel potential spaces
H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, by Triebel
[
62
, Section 2.8.6]
for scalar-valued Triebel-Lizorkin spaces
F^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and by the author
[
26
, Theorem 1.4]
for weighted vector-valued Bessel potential spaces
H^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w;X)
.

Proof.

For convenience of notation we only treat the case
q<\infty
, the case
q=\infty
being completely similar (with some minor modifications). Let
u\in(0,\infty)
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
\frac{1}{u}>\max\{\frac{1}{q},\frac{1}{p},\frac{1+\gamma}{p},\frac{1}{r_{E}}\}
and
m>s
. Then the conditions of Theorem
2.9
are fulfilled and we thus have (
16
) at our disposal. In order to use (
16
), it will be convenient to introduce the notation

[f]_{\mathbb{F}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};E;X)}:=\Big|\Big|\left(\int_{0}^{1}[t^{-s}d^{m}_{t,u}f]^{q}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)^{1/q}\Big|\Big|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};E(X))},\qquad f\in L_{1,{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R},E(X)).

Fix
f\in\mathbb{F}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};E;X)\subset L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R};E(X))
and put
g:=1_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}f\in L_{1,{\rm loc}}(\mathbb{R};E(X))
.

Let
(x,\varsigma)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times S
. Then

\displaystyle d^{m}_{t,u}g(x,\varsigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{-t}^{t}||\Delta^{m}_{h}g(x,\varsigma)||_{X}^{u}dh\right)^{1/u}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{\max\{-x/m,-t\}}^{t}||\Delta^{m}_{h}g(x,\varsigma)||_{X}^{u}dh\right)^{1/u}+\left(\frac{1}{t}\int^{\max\{-x/m,-t\}}_{-t}||\Delta^{m}_{h}g(x,\varsigma)||_{X}^{u}dh\right)^{1/u}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{\max\{-x/m,-t\}}^{t}||\Delta^{m}_{h}f(x,\varsigma)||_{X}^{u}dh\right)^{1/u}+\left(\frac{1}{t}\int^{\max\{-x/m,-t\}}_{-t}||\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}(-1)^{m-j}{m\choose j}g(x+jh,\varsigma)||_{X}^{u}dh\right)^{1/u}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{t}\int_{\max\{-x/m,-t\}}^{t}||\Delta^{m}_{h}f(x,\varsigma)||_{X}^{u}dh\right)^{1/u}+\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\frac{1}{t}\int^{\max\{-x/m,-t\}}_{-t}||f(x+jh,\varsigma)||_{X}^{u}dh\right)^{1/u}

\displaystyle\eqsim

\displaystyle d^{m}_{t,u}f(x,\varsigma)+1_{\{t>x/m\}}t^{-1/u}(t-x/m)^{1/u}\,\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\left(\fint_{-t}^{-x/m}||f(x+jh,\varsigma)||_{X}^{u}dh\right)^{1/u}

\displaystyle d^{m}_{t,u}f(x,\varsigma)+1_{\{t>x/m\}}t^{-1/u}(t-x/m)^{1/u}\,\left(||f(x,\varsigma)||_{X}+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}M_{u}||f||_{X}\left(\frac{m-j}{m}x\right)\right),

from which it follows that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{1}[t^{-s}d^{m}_{t,u}g(x,\varsigma)]^{q}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{1}t^{-sq}d^{m}_{t,u}f(x,\varsigma)^{q}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)^{1/q}\>+\>\left(\int_{x/m}^{\infty}t^{-sq}t^{-q/u}(t-x/m)^{q}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\>\left[||f(x,\varsigma)||_{X}+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}M_{u}||f||_{X}\left(\frac{m-j}{m}x\right)\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{1}t^{-sq}d^{m}_{t,u}f(x,\varsigma)^{q}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\quad|x|^{-s}\left[||f(x,\varsigma)||_{X}+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}M_{u}||f||_{X}\left(\frac{m-j}{m}x\right)\right],

where we used

\int_{a}^{\infty}t^{\alpha}(t-a)^{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}t=a^{\alpha+\beta+1}\int_{1}^{\infty}\tau^{\alpha}(\tau-1)^{\beta}d\tau\lesssim_{\alpha,\beta}a^{\alpha+\beta+1},\quad\quad\alpha\in\mathbb{R},\beta\geq 0,\alpha+\beta<-1,

with
\alpha=-sq-q/u-1
and

.
Taking
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\gamma})[E]

\displaystyle\Big|\Big|\left(\int_{0}^{1}[t^{-s}d^{m}_{t,u}g]^{q}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)^{1/q}\Big|\Big|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\gamma})[E]}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle[f]_{\mathbb{F}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};E;X)}\>+\>||f||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w_{-sp+\gamma};E(X))}

\displaystyle\quad+\>\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\Big|\Big|(x,\varsigma)\mapsto M_{u}||f||_{X}\big(\frac{m-j}{m}x,\varsigma\big)\Big|\Big|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w_{-sp+\gamma})[E]}

\displaystyle[f]_{\mathbb{F}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};E;X)}\>+\>||f||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w_{-sp+\gamma};E(X))}

\displaystyle\quad+\>\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\big|\big|M_{u}||f||_{X}\big|\big|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w_{-sp+\gamma})[E]}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle[f]_{\mathbb{F}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};E;X)}\>+\>||f||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R},w_{-sp+\gamma};E(X))},

where we used that

40
, Theorem 1.2]
,
it follows that

(27)

\Big|\Big|\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}[t^{-s}d^{m}_{t,u}(1_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}f)]^{q}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)^{1/q}\Big|\Big|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}_{+},w_{\gamma})[E]}\lesssim[f]_{\mathbb{F}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};E;X)}+||f||_{\mathbb{F}^{s}_{p,q}(\mathbb{R},w_{\gamma};E;X)}.

d^{m}_{t,u}(1_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}f)(x,\varsigma)=d^{m}_{t,u}(f-1_{\mathbb{R}_{-}}f)(x,\varsigma)\lesssim d^{m}_{t,u}(f)(x,\varsigma)+d^{m}_{t,u}(1_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}[f(-\,\cdot\,)])(-x,\varsigma),

∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Note that

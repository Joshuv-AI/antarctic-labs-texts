Cohomologically smooth morphisms for étale 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} -sheaves in characteristic p p
Felix Lotter

Abstract
. We scrutinise the notions of cohomologically smooth morphisms and smooth objects for the six functor formalism of étale 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} -sheaves on schemes in characteristic p p . We show that only cohomologically étale morphisms are cohomologically smooth in this setting. This is complemented by a characterisation of cohomologically étale morphisms in arbitrary characteristic. In fact, we prove that such a morphism is already étale up to universal homeomorphism.



1.
Introduction

The main goal of this paper is to answer the following question:

Which morphisms admit Poincaré duality for étale
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-sheaves on schemes in characteristic
p
?

Let us clarify what we mean by this.

1.1.
The 6-functor formalism

For a scheme
X
, one can consider the category
\mathrm{Sh}(X,\Lambda)
of étale sheaves of
\Lambda
-modules on
X
, where
\Lambda
is some torsion ring. This category is equipped with a tensor product
\tensor
and an internal
\Hom
object
\iHom
; for a morphism
f:Y\to X
there is an induced pullback functor
f^{*}:\mathrm{Sh}(X,\Lambda)\to\mathrm{Sh}(Y,\Lambda)
which admits a right adjoint
f_{*}:\mathrm{Sh}(Y,\Lambda)\to\mathrm{Sh}(X,\Lambda)
. Deriving all of these functors yields four of the six operations: The tensor product
\tensor^{L}
, the internal
\Hom

R\iHom
, the pullback functor
f^{*}:\mathcal{D}(X,\Lambda)\to\mathcal{D}(Y,\Lambda)
and its right adjoint
Rf_{*}:\mathcal{D}(Y,\Lambda)\to\mathcal{D}(X,\Lambda)
. The remaining two are special in that they do not arise from deriving a functor on the abelian level and are not even defined for all morphisms: If
f
is a separated, finite type morphism of qcqs schemes, one defines a functor
f_{!}:\mathcal{D}(Y,\Lambda)\to\mathcal{D}(X,\Lambda)
and again it admits a right adjoint,
f^{!}:\mathcal{D}(X,\Lambda)\to\mathcal{D}(Y,\Lambda)
, called the exceptional pullback. These
6
functors are subject to certain relations, e.g. the projection formula and base change formula. Roughly, all of this data is what constitutes an abstract 6-functor formalism; but for a long time this term was an unprecise heuristic. However, a concise definition was found recently by L. Mann in
[
Man22a
]
, vastly streamlining the previous work of Liu-Zheng (
[
LZ17
]
) and Gaitsgory-Rozenblyum (
[
GR19
]
).

1.2.
Cohomologically smooth morphisms

Using Mann’s definition of an abstract six functor formalism, one can give meaning to the question if a morphism admits Poincaré duality by employing the notion of
cohomologically smooth
morphisms. Cohomological smoothness was first defined in the context of étale cohomology of diamonds by P. Scholze in
[
Sch17
]
to capture the cohomological properties that are expected from "smooth" morphisms. This notion makes sense in any abstract 6-functor formalism. Roughly, a cohomologically smooth morphism is a morphism
f
for which the functor
f^{!}
right adjoint to
f_{!}
is given by twisted pullback (in a universal way). If one considers the étale
6
-functor formalism of
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-sheaves on the category of separated, finite type schemes over a field
k=\bar{k}
with
\ell\in k^{\times}
, all smooth morphisms are cohomologically smooth and this amounts indeed to Poincaré duality in étale cohomology (cf.
Example

2.33
).

Let us now make our question more precise:

Main question
.

What are the cohomologically smooth morphisms for the 6-functor formalism of étale
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-sheaves on finite type, separated schemes over a field of characteristic
p

In this setting, it is clear that Poincaré duality can not be expected from smooth morphisms in a naive way. Consider for example the projective line

has no higher
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-cohomology even implies that

Finally, we address a generalisation of our main question:

1.5.
Relation to the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence

The formalism of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-sheaves in characteristic
p
is related to coherent cohomology in a certain sense by the
\mathrm{mod}\ p
Riemann-Hilbert correspondence proven by B. Bhatt and J. Lurie in
[
BL19
]
. Also see
[
Mat23
]
for an alternative exposition. They show that
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-sheaves in characteristic
p
correspond to
algebraic Frobenius modules
via an equivalence of categories which satisfies certain compatibilities with pullback, pushfoward and
!
-functors. However, coherent cohomology in the classic sense cannot be upgraded to a 6-functor formalism in a reasonable way. This problem can be avoided by using the theory of condensed mathematics developed by P. Scholze and D. Clausen, which might open up a possibility to turn the
\mathrm{mod}\ p
Riemann-Hilbert correspondence into a correspondence of 6-functor formalisms. Cohomological smoothness would transfer along such a correspondence which adds additional motivation to
Theorem

4.9
.

1.6.
Overview

This paper is organised into four sections.

In section 2 we recall the notion of 6-functor formalisms in the sense of
[
Man22a
]
and the formal groundwork that we will need later on. Following
[
Sch23
]
, we construct the étale
6
-functor formalism in a generality which allows us to pass to the absolute weak normalisation (which we also briefly recall from
[
Ryd10
]
in this context), using the results of P. Hamacher in
[
Ham19
]
. We then discuss the notions of smooth objects and cohomologically smooth and cohomologically étale morphisms and prove important criteria and stability properties. Here we essentially follow the arguments given in
[
Man22b
]
,
[
Sch23
]
and
[
Zav23
]
. This is also intended as a small overview over the different (but mostly equivalent) formal notions and their properties in these three sources, and the relations between them. While some of the statements do not seem to be recorded in the literature yet, there is hardly a claim of originality in this section.

Section 3 is concerned with cohomologically étale morphisms in the étale 6-functor formalism and in particular our geometric characterisation of them via the absolute weak normalisation. After observing some important properties of absolutely weakly normal schemes, we first give a description of the
cohomologically unramified
morphisms (i.e. morphisms whose diagonal is cohomologically étale, cf.
Definition

2.64
); namely, we prove that they are exactly the topologically unramified ones. We then show that topologically unramified morphisms are quasi-finite (
Corollary

3.13
) and that a quasi-finite cohomologically smooth morphism is open (
Proposition

3.14
). All in all, we see that cohomologically étale morphisms are universally open and topologically unramified as they are both cohomologically smooth and cohomologically unramified. We then use the descent results for perfect schemes from
[
BM21
]
to prove that a morphism with these properties is étale after absolute weak normalisation (
Theorem

3.20
). To finish the proof of
Theorem

3.27
, we prove that a quasi-finite morphism is cohomologically étale if and only if it is cohomologically smooth. We end the section with a digression on quasi-finite morphisms in positive characteristic. Namely, we show that they are cohomologically étale over a stratification of the target (
Corollary

3.33
) which will have an interesting consequence for the discussion of smooth objects (
Proposition

5.15
).

Using the results from section 3, we then answer our main question from above in section 4: For this, we first show that
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{n}\to\Spec k
is not
\mathcal{D}_{p}
-cohomologically smooth for any
n>0
by an explicit calculation and then use this fact to characterise the cohomologically smooth morphisms to a field. By stability of cohomologically smooth morphisms under base change, this shows that
\mathcal{D}_{p}
-cohomologically smooth morphisms are quasi-finite (
Proposition

4.8
).

Finally, we employ a similar strategy for the description of smooth objects in section 5: We first deal with the absolute case, i.e. the objects which are smooth for morphisms to the base field. By an explicit calculation, we prove that
\mathbb{F}_{p}
is not smooth for
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{1}\to\Spec k
(
Proposition

5.6
) and can deduce the characterisation of smooth objects from this (
Proposition

5.12
), mostly by formal arguments. By stability of smooth objects under base change, this readily implies the statement about the relative case (
Corollary

5.9
).

As smooth and proper objects are actually morphisms in a (2-)category, it is a natural question if and how they can be composed. Indeed, suppose we are given a commutative triangle

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

Let us again assume that

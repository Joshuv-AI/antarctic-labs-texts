Powers are easy to avoid
Gareth Jones, Olivier Le Gal

Abstract
. Suppose that ℝ ~ \widetilde{\mathbb{R}} is an o-minimal expansion of the real field in which restricted power functions are definable. We show that if ℝ ^ \widehat{\mathbb{R}} is both a reduct (in the sense of definability) of the expansion ℝ ~ ℝ \widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}} of ℝ ~ \widetilde{\mathbb{R}} by all real power functions and an expansion (again in the sense of definability) of ℝ ~ \widetilde{\mathbb{R}} , then, provided that ℝ ~ \widetilde{\mathbb{R}} and ℝ ^ \widehat{\mathbb{R}} have the same field of exponents, they define the same sets. This can be viewed as a polynomially bounded version of an old conjecture of van den Dries and Miller.



1.
Introduction

This paper deals with first order definability and more precisely inter-definability in o-minimal expansions of the field of reals, in the
spirit of Bianconi
[
Bia97
,
Bia05
]
, van den Dries, Macintyre and Marker
[
DMM97
, section 5]
, or Jones,
Kirby and Servi
[
JKS16
]
(see also Sfouli
[
Sfo10
]
, Jones, Kirby, Le Gal and Servi
[
JKLGS19
]
, Wilkie
[
Wil08
]
and
[
ES18
]
for considerations in the same vein, and Conant
[
Con18
]
for a very similar result in a very different setting). We assume that the reader is familiar with the basics of o-minimal geometry, and otherwise refer to Coste
[
Cos00
]
or van den Dries
[
Dri98b
]
. The question we address here is the analogue of a conjecture of van den Dries and Miller
[
DM96
]
concerning the exponential function. Our analogue considers power functions in place of the exponential function.

We begin by discussing the original conjecture of van den Dries and Miller. For this we denote by
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
the expansion of
the real field by restricted analytic functions, and by
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}^{\mathbb{R}}
the expansion of
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
by
all real power functions

x\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}x^{r},&x>0\\
0,&x\leq 0,\end{array}\right.

for real
x
and arbitrary
r
in
\mathbb{R}
. The field of reals with real power functions was first studied by Miller in
[
Mil94a
]
, and the survey
[
Mil12
]
highlights the central role of those functions in o-minimality. In particular, they form a complete scale for asymptotics in polynomially bounded structures. Finally, by
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an},\exp}
we denote the expansion of
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
by the
exponential function. The three structures
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
,
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}^{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an},\exp}
are known to be o-minimal (for instance the first two are reducts of the third, which is o-minimal by
[
DM94
]
), and to be genuine expansions one of another, in the sense that new sets are definable. The latter can easily be seen at the level of one variable functions, as follows. The field of exponents of
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
is known to be
\mathbb{Q}
, meaning that
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
only defines the power functions that have rational exponents, while by construction,
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}^{\mathbb{R}}
has field of exponents
\mathbb{R}
. Furthermore,
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}^{\mathbb{R}}
is polynomially bounded (
[
Mil94a
]
), meaning that any function definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}^{\mathbb{R}}
grows slower at infinity than a power function, whereas the exponential function is bounded by no power but is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an},\exp}
. In
[
DM96
, 2.5(6), p. 506]
van den Dries and Miller conjectured that
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}^{\mathbb{R}}
is maximal amongst polynomially bounded reducts of
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an},\exp}
. Let us clarify that here, and in general in this paper, reduct and expansion are meant in the sense of definability. So,
\mathcal{R}_{1}
is a reduct of
\mathcal{R}_{2}
and
\mathcal{R}_{2}
is an expansion of
\mathcal{R}_{1}
if
\mathcal{R}_{2}
defines all sets definable in
\mathcal{R}_{1}
. Also, definability is always meant with parameters.

We give a rough interpretation of the conjecture. The structure
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
defines the restriction of the exponential function to any
bounded interval, so definable sets of
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an},\exp}
are obtained by mixing global subanalytic sets with the germ of the exponential at infinity. This germ produces at least two phenomena that are not of subanalytic nature: irrational powers (by
x^{r}=e^{r\log(x)}
) and infinitely flat functions (such as
e^{-\frac{1}{|x|}}
at
0
). Flat functions define in return germs that are not polynomially bounded, and then the unrestricted exponential function is definable by Miller’s dichotomy theorem (
[
Mil94b
]
). Hence, if the conjecture were proven to be true, those irrational powers (and their mixing with global subanalytic sets) would be the only thing that the unbounded exponential adds to global subanalytic sets that does not, in return, define the unbounded exponential.
To the best of our knowledge, the conjecture remains open, despite the progress made by Soufflet
[
Sou02
]
, Kuhlmann and Kuhlmann
[
KK03
]
and Randriambololona
[
Ran14
]
. We discuss their results later.

We can ask similar questions at the polynomially bounded level. Here, having a certain field as field of exponents is the analogue of being polynomially bounded, and adding all real exponents is the analogue of adding the exponential function. In this setting we give a complete answer to the analogue of the question asked by van den Dries and Miller. We introduce some notation before stating our result. Given an o-minimal structure
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
we write
k(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}})
for the field of exponents of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
, and we write
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
for the expansion of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
by the real power functions with exponents in
\Lambda
. We say that
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
defines restricted power functions if, for any
r\in\mathbb{R}
, the restriction to some neighborhood of
0
of
x\mapsto(1+x)^{r}
is definable in
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
. Defining the restricted exponential function is sufficient to define restricted powers, so this condition is not rare. Following
[
Mil12
]
, if
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
is polynomially bounded and defines restricted power functions,
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
is polynomially bounded again. (The question of whether
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
is polynomially bounded assuming only that
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
is polynomially bounded remains open.) For brevity, the following statement of our main result does not mention the assumption of being polynomially bounded. But the interest is in this case, since otherwise
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
already define the same sets.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
be an o-minimal expansion of the real field that defines restricted power functions, and let
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
be an expansion of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
and a reduct of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
(all in the sense of definability). If
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
have the same field of exponents, then
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
have the same definable sets.

So
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
is maximal amongst reducts of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
with field of exponents
k(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}})
.

An alternative way to interpret Theorem
1.1
explains the title of the paper. Suppose
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
defines restricted power functions, and that
X
is definable in
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
. If the field of exponents of the expansion
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{X}
of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
by
X
is a certain field
\Lambda
, then Theorem
1.1
shows that
X
is definable in the expansion
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
by power functions with exponents in
\Lambda
. For
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{X}
is an expansion of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
and a reduct of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
, and it has the same field of exponents
\Lambda
as
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
(one has
k(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda})=\Lambda
in view of
[
Mil12
, Theorem 1.4]
). So, roughly, if
X
is defined from
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
and powers, then
X
admits a definition which avoids all those powers that are not in return defined by
X
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
. We illustrate this with an example.

Example
.

We define
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(1)

f(x,y)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle y^{\frac{1}{\pi}}g\left(\frac{y}{x^{\pi}}-1\right),&\text{if}\;0<x<1\;\text{and}\;x^{\pi}<y<x^{\pi}(1+x),\\
x&\text{otherwise},\end{array}\right.

where
g
is an analytic function on
(-1,+\infty)
that we reveal below. The function
f
is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}^{\mathbb{R}}
, being a composition of algebraic functions, powers, and restricted analytic functions (only the restriction of
g
to
(0,1)
intervenes) on domains defined using the same functions.
Notice that the given definition involves unrestricted irrational power functions, since the powers in
y^{\frac{1}{\pi}}
and
x^{\pi}
act on variables that approach zero.
However, with our particular
g
, the field of exponents of the expansion
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an},f}
of
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
by
f
is
\mathbb{Q}
. So the expansion
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an},f}
of
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
is a reduct of
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}^{\mathbb{R}}
with the same field of exponents as
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
. Then Theorem
1.1
asserts that
f
is definable in
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
; in other terms,
f
can be defined with no use of irrational powers.
As the reader may have guessed our function
g
is given by
g(t)=(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{\pi}}
, so
f
admits actually the (subanalytic) definition
f(x,y)=x
. In this example, irrational powers were indeed easy to avoid. The expression (
1
) of
f
now seems artificial, but has been chosen in view of our proof, because (
1
) is an admissible output of the Preparation Theorem of van den Dries, Speissegger (
[
DS02
]
) applied to
f
in the structure
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}^{\mathbb{R}}
.

In the aforementioned
[
Ran14
]
, Randriambololona shows that there are infinitely many different maximal polynomially bounded reducts of

\mathbb{R}_{\text{an},\exp}
. Randriambololona’s arguments for producing different maximal polynomially bounded reducts of

\mathbb{R}_{\text{an},\exp}
make heavy use of the exponential function, and do not adapt to the polynomially bounded setting. In our setting, we do not know whether
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
is the unique maximal reduct of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}

with field of exponents
k(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}})
. We leave it
as an open question, that we find exiting because we suspect the answer might be negative. Randriambololona also mention that, if van den Dries and Miller’s conjecture was true, then
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}^{\mathbb{R}}
would be the unique maximal polynomially bounded reduct of
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an},\exp}
that expands
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
(even,
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}^{\mathbb{R}}
would be the unique polynomially bounded reduct of
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an},\exp}
with field of exponents
\mathbb{R}
that expands
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
). Our theorem proves an analogue of this, with
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
playing the role of
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
, the expansion
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
the role of
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}^{\mathbb{R}}
and the structure
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
the role of
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an},\exp}
. Indeed, if
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
is an expansion of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
with
k(\widehat{\mathbb{R}})=\Lambda
, then
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
is an expansion of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
, so if
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
is also a reduct of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
, our theorem applies then
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
define the same sets. So Theorem
1.1
proves the following:

Corollary 1.2
.

Suppose
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
is an o-minimal expansion of the real field that defines restricted power functions, and
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{R}
is a field extension of
k(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}})
. Then
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
is the unique (and in particular, unique maximal) reduct of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
with field of exponents
\Lambda
that expands
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
.

Theorem
1.1
also extends partial results around van den Dries and Miller conjecture.
The main advances in proving this conjecture concern functions of one variable.
Thus, Soufflet shows in
[
Sou02
]
that any expansion of
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}^{\mathbb{R}}
that is a polynomially bounded reduct of
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an},\exp}
defines the same one variable functions as
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}^{\mathbb{R}}
(and so the same subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
by cell decomposition). So from the point of view of definable unary functions,
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}^{\mathbb{R}}
is maximal.
A similar result is obtained
independently by Kuhlman and Kuhlman in
[
KK03
]
, dealing with arbitrary
polynomially bounded expansions
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
of the field of reals in which the restricted exponential function is definable, rather
than with
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
– van den Dries and Miller focus on
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
but the generalized conjecture seems also natural (in 1996, few o-minimal structures were known that are not reducts or expansions of
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
; from Le Gal
[
LG10
]
, there are however polynomially bounded structures that are even not compatible with
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
).
Kuhlman and Kuhlman moreover work in an arbitrary model
M
of the complete theory
\text{Th}(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{\exp})
of the
expansion of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
by the exponential. Namely, they prove (with obvious notations)
that if
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
is a polynomially bounded structure on
M
that is an expansion of

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathbb{R}}
and reduct of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\exp}
, then
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
and

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathbb{R}}
have the same Hardy field. As shown by Randriambololona (
[
Ran14
]
), having the same Hardy field and having the same unary functions are not equivalent in general, whereas they are over the reals. So
[
KK03
]
answers, at the level of unary functions, a generalization of van den Dries and Miller conjecture for polynomially bounded expansions of the real field in which the restricted exponential is definable.
It is also possible to formulate a generalization of the conjecture of van den Dries and Miller to smaller fields of exponents. Our
theorem combines naturally with
[
KK03
]
to solve this question at the level of unary functions.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
be an o-minimal structure that defines the restricted exponential function, and let
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
be a polynomially bounded reduct of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{\exp}
that expands
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
. If
k(\widehat{\mathbb{R}})=\Lambda
, then
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
and
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
defines the same one variable functions (then have the same definable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
).

So, at the level of one variable functions,
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
is maximal amongst reducts of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}_{\exp}
with field of exponents
\Lambda
. Similarly, our theorem means that the original conjecture of van den Dries and Miller implies the generalization with smaller fields of exponents.

To complete this exposition we state a corollary of the preceding proposition, obtained with the field of reals with restricted exponential as
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
, the expansion of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
by
f
as
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
and
\mathbb{Q}
as
\Lambda
. This statement was the initial motivation for this work.

Corollary 1.4
.

Suppose that
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is definable in both
\mathbb{R}_{\text{an}}
and
\mathbb{R}_{\exp}
. Then
f
is definable in the expansion of the real field by the restricted exponential function.

The text is organized to follow the structure of the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
In the first step, a model-theoretic trick reduces the problem to one variable functions,
at the cost of working over non-archimedean models of
\text{Th}(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}})
. For notational purposes, given a real closed field
M
underlying a model of this theory, we decorate
\mathcal{M}
with the symbol decorating
\mathbb{R}
in the corresponding expansion (this is explained formally in Section
2
).
The model-theoretic reduction is given in Section
2
, and
the problem becomes to show that if
f:M\to M
is definable in a reduct

\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathbb{R}}
with
k(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}})=k(\widehat{\mathcal{M}})
,
then
f
is definable in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
. We prove the latter in the same section, using the lemmas which occur later in the paper (and form the core of the proof).

Those lemmas use the technical but simple Lemma
3.3
, based on the Preparation Theorem (
[
DS02
]
) of van den Dries and Speissegger. This technical lemma
gives uniforms pivots, in the following sense. Given various
p_{i}
, in different expressions involving powers of
x\mapsto|x-p_{i}|
, we can, piecewise,
simultaneously rewrite the expressions in a way that only depends on powers of
x\mapsto|x-p|
with a uniform
p
. We recall the statement of the Preparation Theorem (on which our whole proof heavily relies) and prove
Lemma
3.3
in Section
3
.

To show that
f
is definable in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
, we first prove that
f
has a particular form. Namely, it is piecewise given as the composition of a map definable in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
with real power functions. This is Lemma
4.1
in Section
4
. It is related to a result of Miller’s
[
Mil94a
, Proposition 4.5]
.
We use the fact that, being definable in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathbb{R}}
, the function
f
is piecewise given by terms, and reason inductively on the complexity of the terms. The proof requires rewriting powers of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
-definable maps, and this can be done after giving a monomial form to such expressions. This is Lemma
5.1
in Section
5
, related to Lion and Rolin
[
LR97
, Théorème 1 (together with §1 and §2 p. 862) and Théorème 3]
and Soufflet
[
Sou02
, Theorem 2.3]
. The lemma pays particular attention to the exponents that appear in the process, which we use later.

We then have to avoid the extra exponents involved in the composition of an
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
-definable map with powers, using the assumption that the resulting function is definable in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
. This is Lemma
6.1
, proven in Section
6
from the Preparation Theorem again and arguments involving valuations, including the strong version of our lemma
5.1
. It completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The version of Lemma
6.1
we prove is a bit more general than the one we use, since we think it could have some independent interest.

Finally, in Section
7
, we restate a version of Kuhlmann and Kuhlmann’s Theorem adapted to our framework, and combine it with Theorem
1.1
to show Proposition
1.3
.

2.
The model-theoretic setting and the proof

Let
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
be a polynomially bounded o-minimal expansion of the real ordered field
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
with field of exponents
k(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}})
. Let
\widetilde{L}
be the language of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
and assume, without loss of generality, that
\widetilde{T}=\text{Th}(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}})
has quantifier elimination and a universal axiomatisation. Suppose too that all restricted power functions are definable in
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
be the expansion of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
by all power functions. Suppose that
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
is a reduct (in the sense of definability) of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
. To prove Theorem
1.1
, we can assume without loss of generality that
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
is a syntactic expansion of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
. Recall that we write
k(\widehat{\mathbb{R}})
for the field of exponents of
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
.

So we have to show that if
k(\widehat{\mathbb{R}})=k(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}})
then
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
defines the same sets as
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
.

Let
\widetilde{L}^{\mathbb{R}}
be the language of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
and
\widetilde{T}^{\mathbb{R}}
be the theory of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
.

Note that it follows immediately from the o-minimality of the pfaffian closure of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
(due to Speissegger
[
Spe99
]
) that the structure
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
is o-minimal.

We will frequently use the following result, which is due to Chris Miller.

Theorem 2.1

(Miller
[
Mil94a
,
Mil12
]
)
.

The structure
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
is polynomially bounded. The theory
\widetilde{T}^{\mathbb{R}}
has quantifier elimination and a universal axiomatization. Definable functions in
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
are given piecewise by
\widetilde{L}^{\mathbb{R}}
terms.

Any model
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathbb{R}}
of
\widetilde{T}^{\mathbb{R}}
has a reduct
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
obtained as
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
is obtained from
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
. More precisely,
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
is an expansion of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
by some sets
X_{\iota}
, with each
X_{\iota}
the realisation in
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
of a formula
\phi_{\iota}
. We define
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
to be the expansion of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
by the interpretations of the formulas
\phi_{\iota}
in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathbb{R}}
. The structures
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
are elementarily equivalent.

By a result of van den Dries’s (
[
Dri98a
, Lemma 4.7]
), to reach our aim it is sufficient to establish the following.

Claim 1
.

Suppose that
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathbb{R}}
is an
\omega
-saturated model of
\widetilde{T}^{\mathbb{R}}
. If
f:M\to M
is definable in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
then
f
is definable in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
.

To see that our theorem follows from the claim, suppose that
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is definable in
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
. Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathbb{R}}
be an
\omega
-saturated model of
\widetilde{T}^{\mathbb{R}}
. Suppose that
X=\phi(\widehat{\mathbb{R}})
for some
\widehat{L}
-formula
\phi
with parameters from
\mathbb{R}
. By the claim, and Lemma 4.7 in
[
Dri98a
]
every set definable in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
is definable in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
. So there is an
\widetilde{L}
-formula
\psi
and parameters
a
in
M
such that
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}\models\forall x(\phi(x)\leftrightarrow\psi(x,a))
. So
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}\models\exists y\forall x(\phi(x)\leftrightarrow\psi(x,y))
and so
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}\models\exists y\forall x(\phi(x)\leftrightarrow\psi(x,y))
, so
X
is definable in
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
.

We now prove the Claim, using results from later sections.

Proof of Claim
1
.

From Theorem
2.1
,
f
is piecewise given by terms in the language
\widetilde{L}^{\mathbb{R}}
with parameters from
M
. By Lemma
4.1
, there is a partition of
M
into open intervals
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
and a finite set of points such that for each
i
there exist
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, an
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
-definable
F
with domain a subset of
M^{m}
and
\delta\in M
(all depending on
i
) such that

f(x)=F\left(|x-\delta|^{\alpha}\right)

on
I_{i}
. But our
f
is also definable in
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
. So by Lemma
6.1
we can refine the partition (but keep the notation) and get that

f(x)=G\left(|x-\eta|^{\beta}\right),

on
I_{i}
, for some
G:M^{n}\to M
definable in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
,
\beta\in k(\widehat{\mathcal{M}})^{n}
,
\eta\in M
.
Since
k(\widehat{\mathcal{M}})=k(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}})
the function
x\mapsto|x-\eta|^{\beta}
is in fact definable in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
. And then
f
is definable in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
.
∎

3.
The valuation, the Preparation Theorem and uniform pivots

In this section we recall some classical facts about the interaction of the natural valuation map, o-minimality and polynomial boundedness. We let
\mathcal{M}
be an o-minimal expansion of a real closed field, with underlying set
M
.

The natural valuation
v:M^{\times}\to M^{\times}/U
is the quotient mapping from the group of invertible elements
M^{\times}
to its quotient by the subgroup
U
of units

U:=\left\{x\in M:\;\text{there is some }n\in\mathbb{N}\text{ such that }\frac{1}{n}<|x|<n\right\}.

The value group
M^{\times}/U
is a totally ordered group which we write additively, where the order is conventionally chosen in such a way that
|x|\leq|y|\Rightarrow v(x)\geq v(y)
, and
v
is extended to
M
by setting
v(0)=\infty
.
The valuation is only useful if
M
is non-archimedean, for the value group is trivial otherwise. (Note that the model
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathbb{R}}
we introduced in Section 2, in reducing our theorem to the claim, is certainly non-archimedean since the type given by the sequence of formulas
x>n
, for natural numbers
n
, is realized in
M
, as
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathbb{R}}
is
\omega
-saturated).

Also, if
\mathcal{M}
defines real power functions, it induces an external multiplication on the value group that turns it into an ordered vector space over
\mathbb{R}
, by setting
\lambda\cdot v(x)=v(|x|^{\lambda})
for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

Combining o-minimality with the valuation gives rise to the following.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
f:X\to M
be definable in an o-minimal expansion
\mathcal{M}
of a non-archimedean real closed field
M
. Suppose that for all
x\in X
there is an
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
1/n<f(x)<n
. Then there is some
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
x\in X
we have
1/n<f(x)<n
.

Proof.

We show that
f
is bounded by below by a positive rational. The same argument for
1/f
gives the upper bound.
The function
f
is definable and positive, so
f(X)
is a finite union of points and intervals contained in
(0,+\infty)
. In particular,
f
admits a nonnegative infimum,
a
say. We claim that
v(a)\leq 0
. So suppose that
v(a)>0
. Then by assumption
\{a\}\not\subseteq f(V)
, so there is some interval
(a,b)\subseteq f(V)
. But then, as

is either
0
or a positive infinitesimal, there is some infinitesimal
c\in(a,b)
with
c\in f(V)
, which contradicts our assumption (note that the
M
has infinitesimals as it is non-archimedean). So
v(a)\leq 0
and hence
a>1/n
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

∎

The valuation of a function definable in a polynomially bounded o-minimal structure has strong regularities, as shown for instance by the preparation theorem of van den Dries and Speissegger
[

]
. We recall its statement, as we use it frequently in the following. Suppose now that
\mathcal{M}
is a polynomially bounded o-minimal structure, expanding a real closed field

, and suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is an elementary extension of an expansion of the real field. In the statement below, definable means definable in
\mathcal{M}
(and cells are cells definable in

).

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}:M^{n+1}\to M
are definable. Then there is a cell decomposition

of

such that for each cell

there are

and definable functions

and definable functions

such that the graph of
\theta
is disjoint from

, and for all

and

we have

f_{i}(x,y)=|y-\theta(x)|^{\lambda_{i}}a_{i}(x)u_{i}(x,y)

and

|u_{i}(x,y)-1|<\frac{1}{2}.

This is only stated in
[
DS02
]
in the case that the underlying field

is the real numbers. But it is clear that the proof there establishes the result above. (More care is needed in polynomially bounded structures whose theories have no archimedean model, see
[

]
.)

For simplicity, we will say that a function

By Proposition

, for a function that is definable in an o-minimal expansion of a non-archimedean real closed field, being a unit is equivalent to have values that are all units.

The previous lemma, and its proof, show how arguments involving cell decompositions, even in one variable, can easily produce rather heavy notation. In an attempt to alleviate this, we say that a property

holds piecewise on an interval

in
M
if there is a finite partition

of
(a,b)
made of points and intervals, such that, for all
i=1,\dots,n
, and all
x\in(a_{i},a_{i+1})
, we have
P(x)
. We omit
(a,b)
from the notation in the case that
(a,b)=M
, so saying

holds piecewise means
P(x)
holds piecewise on
M
. Whenever

is an existential formula, then of course the witnesses for the existentially quantified variables depend on the piece. For instance, the conclusion of the preceding lemma can now be stated as follows.

Then, piecewise on
(a,b)
, there exists

, and for

, there exist

and
\mathcal{M}_{i}

c_{i}|x-\delta_{i}|^{\ell_{i}(1,\alpha^{(i)})}u_{i}\left(|x-\delta_{i}|^{(1,\alpha^{(i)})}\right)=d_{i}|x-p|^{k_{i}(1,\alpha^{(i)})}v_{i}\left(|x-p|^{(1,\alpha^{(i)})}\right).

-definable function of

The case (b) is very similar, factoring out

We state the assumptions we need:

7.
Polynomially bounded unary functions in
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}},\exp)

In this section we prove Proposition
1.3
. For this we fix a polynomially bounded structure
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
that defines the restricted exponential function, and a unary function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
definable in
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}},\exp)
. It is sufficient to prove the next proposition.

Proposition 7.1
.

With the setting above, if
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}},f)
is polynomially bounded with field of exponents
\Lambda
, then
f
is definable in
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
.

To reduce to our main result, we need the following.

Theorem 7.2

(Kuhlmann and Kuhlmann)
.

Suppose that
\wideparen{\mathbb{R}}
is a polynomially bounded o-minimal structure which defines the restricted exponential function and has field of exponents
\mathbb{R}
. If
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
is a polynomially bounded reduct of
(\wideparen{\mathbb{R}},\exp)
expanding
\wideparen{\mathbb{R}}
then
\wideparen{\mathbb{R}}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
have the same Hardy field.

In the case that
\wideparen{\mathbb{R}}
is
\mathbb{R}_{\an}^{\mathbb{R}}
this is a special case of the second part of
[
KK03
, Theorem 4 on p. 687]
. The proof of that Theorem works in the general setting above (for this, the hypothesis
k(\wideparen{\mathbb{R}})=\mathbb{R}
is necessary; it is implicit in the statement of
[
KK03
, Corollary 2]
as its proof involves
[
KK03
, Lemma 15]
).

Proof of Proposition
7.1
.

It is enough to prove that the germ of
f
at each point (including
\pm\infty
) is definable in
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
. We work at infinity.
Denote by
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
the expansion
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}},f,\{x^{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\})
of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}
by
f
and power
functions, and let
\mathcal{H}
be the Hardy field of
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
. The structure
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
is polynomially
bounded by
[
Mil12
, Theorem 1.4]
since the restricted exponential is definable in
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}},f)
. Now
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
is
a reduct of
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}},\exp)
and
an expansion of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
. So by the Theorem of Kuhlmann and Kuhlmann above (with
\wideparen{\mathbb{R}}=\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
),
the field
\mathcal{H}
is the Hardy field of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
. In particular, since the germ at infinity of
f

belongs to
\mathcal{H}
, the restriction
f|_{(a,\infty)}
is definable in
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
for some real

.

The structure
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}},f|_{(a,\infty)},\{x^{\alpha}:\alpha\in\Lambda\})
has field of exponents
\Lambda
as a reduct of
(\widetilde{\mathbb{R}},f)
. As this structure is a reduct of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathbb{R}}
and an expansion of
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
our main result applies, and so

is definable in
\widetilde{\mathbb{R}}^{\Lambda}
.
∎

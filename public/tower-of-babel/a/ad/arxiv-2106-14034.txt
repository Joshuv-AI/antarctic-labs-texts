Abstract
We prove a general alternate circular summation formula of theta functions, which implies a great deal of theta-function identities. In particular, we recover several identities in Ramanujan’s Notebook from this identity. We also obtain two formulaes for ( q , q ) ∞ 2 ​ n (q;q)_{\infty}^{2n} . Key words: theta function, circular summation, elliptic function, Ramanujan, modular equation MSC 2010: 11F27, 11F20, 11F11, 33E05

Introduction

Throughout we put
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
, where
\mbox{Im}\ \tau>0
. As
usual, the Jacobi theta functions
\theta_{k}(z|\tau)
for
k=1,2,3,4
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle\theta_{1}(z|\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-iq^{1\over 8}\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{n(n+1)\over 2}e^{(2n+1)iz},

\displaystyle\theta_{2}(z|\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{1\over 8}\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n(n+1)\over 2}e^{(2n+1)iz},

\displaystyle\theta_{3}(z|\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}q^{n^{2}\over 2}e^{2niz},

\displaystyle\theta_{4}(z|\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q^{n^{2}\over 2}e^{2niz}.

To carry out our work, we need some notations and basic facts about
the Jacobi theta functions. We use the familiar notation

(z;q)_{\infty}=\prod_{n=0}^{\infty}(1-zq^{n})

and sometimes write

(a,b,\cdots,c;q)_{\infty}=(a;q)_{\infty}(b;q)_{\infty}\cdots(c;q)_{\infty}.

Using the well-known Jacobi product identity
[
2
, p. 35, Entry
19]

f(a,b):=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a^{n(n+1)\over 2}b^{n(n-1)\over 2}=(ab,-a,-b;ab)_{\infty},

(1)

we can deduce
the infinite product representations for theta functions, namely,

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\theta_{1}(z|\tau)=iq^{1\over 8}e^{-iz}(q,e^{2iz},qe^{-2iz};q)_{\infty}~,&\theta_{2}(z|\tau)=q^{1\over 8}e^{-iz}(q,-e^{2iz},-qe^{-2iz};q)_{\infty}~,\\
\theta_{3}(z|\tau)=(q,-q^{1\over 2}e^{2iz},-q^{1\over 2}e^{-2iz};q)_{\infty}~,&\theta_{4}(z|\tau)=(q,q^{1\over 2}e^{2iz},q^{1\over 2}e^{-2iz};q)_{\infty}~.\\
\end{array}

Employing the above identities, we can easily get the following
relations:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lcl}\theta_{1}(z+\pi|\tau)=-\theta_{1}(z|\tau),&&\theta_{2}(z+\pi|\tau)=-\theta_{2}(z|\tau),\\
\theta_{3}(z+\pi|\tau)=\theta_{3}(z|\tau),&&\theta_{4}(z+\pi|\tau)=\theta_{4}(z|\tau)\end{array}

and

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lcl}\theta_{1}(z+\pi\tau|\tau)=-q^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-2iz}\theta_{1}(z|\tau),&&\theta_{2}(z+\pi\tau|\tau)=q^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-2iz}\theta_{2}(z|\tau),\\
\theta_{3}(z+\pi\tau|\tau)=q^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-2iz}\theta_{3}(z|\tau),&&\theta_{4}(z+\pi\tau|\tau)=-q^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-2iz}\theta_{4}(z|\tau).\end{array}

We also have

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lcl}\theta_{1}(z+{\pi\over 2}|\tau)=\theta_{2}(z|\tau),&&\theta_{2}(z+{\pi\over 2}|\tau)=-\theta_{1}(z|\tau),\\
\theta_{3}(z+{\pi\over 2}|\tau)=\theta_{4}(z|\tau),&&\theta_{4}(z+{\pi\over 2}|\tau)=\theta_{3}(z|\tau)\\
\end{array}

and

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lcl}\theta_{1}(z+{\pi\tau\over 2}|\tau)=iq^{-{1\over 8}}e^{-iz}\theta_{4}(z|\tau),&&\theta_{2}(z+{\pi\tau\over 2}|\tau)=q^{-{1\over 8}}e^{-iz}\theta_{3}(z|\tau),\\
\theta_{3}(z+{\pi\tau\over 2}|\tau)=q^{-{1\over 8}}e^{-iz}\theta_{2}(z|\tau),&&\theta_{4}(z+{\pi\tau\over 2}|\tau)=iq^{-{1\over 8}}e^{-iz}\theta_{1}(z|\tau).\end{array}

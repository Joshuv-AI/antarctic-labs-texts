Cohernece in permutation-invariant state enhances permutation-asymmetry
Masahito Hayashi

Abstract
A Dicke state and its decohered state are invariant for permutation. However, when another qubits state to each of them is attached, the whole state is not invariant for permutation, and has a certain asymmetry for permutation. The amount of asymmetry can be measured by the number of distinguishable states under the group action or the mutual information. This paper investigates how the coherence of a Dicke state affects the amount of asymmetry. To address this problem asymptotically, we introduce a new type of central limit theorem by using several formulas on hypergeometric functions. We reveal that the amount of the asymmetry in the case with a Dicke state has a strictly larger order than that with the decohered state in a specific type of the limit.

Introduction

Asymmetry is a root of the variety of various physical phenomena.
Consider a perfectly symmetric state. Such a state does not reflect any changes so that
it can realize no variety.
To realize rich variety, the state needs to have sufficient asymmetry.
In physics, people consider that symmetry breaking is needed for our world.
Therefore, to explain various phenomena,
many researchers introduced spontaneous symmetry breaking, which plays
an important role in various areas in physics,
nuclear star, superfluidity, superconductivity, cold atoms, Higgs boson, Nambu-goldstone boson.
When the degree of symmetry breaking is large, it realizes rich phenomena.
Since the amount of symmetry breaking can be restated as
the amount of asymmetry, it is a central topic to study the amount of asymmetry.

Fortunately, quantum information theory has strong tools to evaluate the amount of asymmetry
[
2
,
1
]
.
The variety generated by group symmetry under a given state
can be measured by the number of distinguishable states generated by group action to describe the
symmetry.
This number is evaluated by
the difference between
the von Neumann entropy of the averaged state with respect to the group action
and the von Neumann entropy of the original state.
This fact can be shown by a simple application of quantum channel coding theorem
[
3
,
4
]

to the family of the states translated by the group action
although this idea can be backed to various studies
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
.
Therefore, the difference between the von Neumann entropies of these two states
can be considered as the amount of the asymmetry of the given state.
Now, we focus on the permutation symmetry on qubits.
When we apply the permutation of the state
\ket{1^{M}\,0^{N}}
,
the averaged state
\rho_{mix,N+M,M}
is permutation-invariant, and has no asymmetry.
However, it still works as a resource for asymmetry

Now, we discuss how quantum theory can improve the amount of asymmetry

To address this problem, we focus on the coherence, which is a key concept in

2
General theory for asymmetry

First, we discuss a general theory for asymmetry when
a unitary representation
f
of a finite group
G
is given
on
\mathcal{H}
.
Since the invariance of a state
\rho
under the
G
-action
f

is expressed as
f(g)\rho f(g)^{\dagger}=\left|\psi\right>\!\left<\psi\right|
for any
g\in G
,
the asymmetry of
\rho
is considered
as the degree of change caused by the
G
-action.
When a state
f(g)\rho f(g)^{\dagger}
is generated with equal probability
\frac{1}{\left|G\right|}
,
the averaged state with respect to the representation
f

is given as

\displaystyle\av_{f}[\rho]:=\sum_{g\in G}\frac{1}{\left|G\right|}f(g)\rho f(g)^{\dagger}.

(6)

In this case, the map
g\mapsto f(g)\rho f(g)^{\dagger}

is considered as a classical-quantum channel, and
the mutual information is given as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&S(\av_{f}[\rho])-\sum_{g\in G}\frac{1}{\left|G\right|}S(f(g)\rho f(g)^{\dagger})\\
=&S(\av_{f}[\rho])-S(\rho),\end{split}

(7)

where
the von Neumann entropy
S(\rho)
is defined as

-\tr\rho\log\rho
.
If we use the relative entropy
D(\rho\|\sigma)=\tr\rho(\log\rho-\sigma)
,
the mutual information is
rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{g\in G}\frac{1}{\left|G\right|}D(f(g)\rho f(g)^{\dagger}\|\av_{f}[\rho])=D(\rho\|\av_{f}[\rho])

(8)

because
D(f(g)\rho f(g)^{\dagger}\|\av_{f}[\rho])=D(\rho\|\av_{f}[\rho])
.
That is, the asymmetry is measured by

S(\av_{f}[\rho])-S(\rho)=D(\rho\|\av_{f}[\rho])
.
Therefore, the mutual information (
7
)
can be considered as the degree of asymmetry of

\rho
under the representation
f
.
This quantity was also introduced in the context of the resource theory of asymmetry

[
1
]
and other context
[
2
]
.
In particular, when
\rho
is a pure state
\left|\psi\right>\!\left<\psi\right|
,
it is simplified to

\displaystyle S(\av_{f}[\psi]):=-\tr_{\mathcal{H}}\bigl(\av_{f}[\psi]\log\av_{f}[\psi]\bigr).

(9)

Further, when a channel
\Gamma
satisfies the covariance condition:

\displaystyle\Gamma(f(g)\rho f(g)^{\dagger})=f(g)\Gamma(\rho)f(g)^{\dagger},

(10)

the information processing inequality for
\Gamma
implies

\displaystyle S(\av_{f}[\rho])-S(\rho)=D(\rho\|\av_{f}[\rho])

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle D(\Gamma(\rho)\|\Gamma(\av_{f}[\rho]))=D(\Gamma(\rho)\|\av_{f}[\Gamma(\rho)])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(\av_{f}[\Gamma(\rho)])-S(\Gamma(\rho)).

(11)

This fact shows that the application of a covariant channel
decreases the amount of asymmetry.

In fact, to connect the mutual information (
7
)
and the distinguishability, we need to consider the
repetitive use of the classical-quantum channel defined
by
\{f(g)\rho f(g)^{\dagger}\}_{g\in G}
.
When our interest is the case when the above classical-quantum channel is used only once,
we need to prepare another measure.
In this case, we evaluate the number
M(\rho,\epsilon)
of distinguished states among

\{f(g)\rho f(g)^{\dagger}\}_{g\in G}
with error probability
\epsilon
.

Assume that the state
\rho
is
a constant times of a projection to a
v
-dimensional subspace
and the set

\{f(g)\rho f(g)^{\dagger}\}_{g\in G}

is composed of orthogonal states.
In this case

\av_{f}[\rho]
is a constant times of a projection to a
w
-dimensional subspace.
In this case, the number
M(\rho,0)

of distinguishable state is
w/v
.
Also,
S(\av_{f}[\rho])-S(\rho)
equals
\log w/v
.
When we allow error probability

the number
of distinguishable inputs is
jw/v
.
This is because it is allowed that
j
informations are represented to the same state.
Hence, we have the formula

\displaystyle\log M(\rho,1-\frac{1}{j})=\log j+\log\frac{w}{v}.

(12)

Next, we prepare a general formula for
M(\rho,\epsilon)
for a general state
\rho

when
the set of states

\{f(g)\rho f(g)^{\dagger}\}_{g\in G}
is given.
We prepare the following notation.
Given a Hermitian matrix

and
\{A>0\}

express the projections to the eigenspace of
A
corresponding to non-negative

For two Hermitian matrices

B
,
the symbol
\{A\geq B\}
denotes the projection
\{A-B\geq 0\}
.
Other projections such as
\{A>B\}
and
\{A\leq B\}
are similarly defined.
Then, we consider the information spectrum relative entropy
D_{s}^{\delta}
,
which is given by
[
20
]

\displaystyle D_{s}^{\delta}(\rho\|\rho^{\prime}):=\max\bigl\{\lambda\ \mid\tr\rho\{\rho\leq e^{\lambda}\rho^{\prime}\}\leq\delta\bigr\}.

(13)

Since
\av_{f}[\rho]
is invariant for

applying
[

, we have

\displaystyle\log M(\rho,\epsilon)\leq D_{s}^{\epsilon+\delta}(\rho\|\av_{f}[\rho])+\log\frac{1}{\delta}.

(14)

The paper

derived the following evaluation:

\displaystyle\log M(\rho,\epsilon)\geq D_{s}^{\epsilon-\delta}(\rho\|\av_{f}[\rho]))-\log\frac{1}{\delta},

(15)

which is slightly better than
[

, Lemma 3]
in this special case.
We define

\displaystyle H_{s}^{\epsilon}(\rho):=\max\bigl\{\lambda\mid\tr\rho\{-\log\rho\leq\lambda\}\leq\epsilon\bigr\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max\bigl\{\lambda\mid\tr\rho\{\rho\geq e^{-\lambda}\}\leq\epsilon\bigr\},

(16)

When
\rho
is a pure state, as shown Theorem

in

\displaystyle M(\rho,\epsilon)

\displaystyle\geq H_{s}^{\epsilon-\delta_{1}-\delta_{2}}(\av_{f}[\rho])-\log\frac{1}{\delta_{1}\delta_{2}},

(17)

\displaystyle M(\rho,\epsilon)

\displaystyle\leq H_{s}^{\epsilon+\delta_{1}+2\delta_{2}}(\av_{f}[\rho])+\log\frac{1}{\delta_{1}\delta_{2}^{2}}.

(18)

3
Formulation

This paper investigates how an invariant state enhances the amount of asymmetry.
To address this question, we focus on
the
n
-fold tensor product

\mathcal{H}=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
of the qubit system
\mathbb{C}^{2}=\mathbb{C}\ket{0}\oplus\mathbb{C}\ket{1}
,
and consider it as the representation space of the permutation group
G=\mathfrak{S}_{n}

acting by permutation of tensor factors as

\displaystyle\pi(g)(\ket{i_{1}\dotsm i_{n}}):=\ket{i_{g^{-1}(1)}\dotsm i_{g^{-1}(n)}},\quad g\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}.

(19)

A Dicke state on
(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes(N+M)}

is a typical invariant state because
it is given as the permutation invariant state
with fixed weights
N
and
M
as

\displaystyle\ket{\Xi_{N+M,M}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\tbinom{N+M}{M}^{-1/2}\bigl(\ket{1^{M}\,0^{N}}+\text{permuted terms}\bigr),

(20)

where
\ket{1^{M}\,0^{N}}\in(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes k}
is defined as

\displaystyle\ket{1^{l}\,0^{k-l}}:=|\overbrace{1\dotsm 1}^{l}\,\overbrace{0\dotsm 0}^{k-l}\rangle=\ket{1}^{\otimes l}\otimes\ket{0}^{\otimes(k-l)}

(21)

while the state

\ket{1^{l}\,0^{k-l}}
is asymmetric with respect to the permutation.
Remember that the state
\ket{\Xi_{n,m|k,l}}
is defined as

\displaystyle\ket{\Xi_{n,m|k,l}}=\ket{1^{l}\,0^{k-l}}\otimes\ket{\Xi_{N+M,M}}\in(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}.

(22)

In contrast, the decohered state of the Dicke state
is given as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\rho_{mix,N+M,M}\\
:=&\tbinom{N+M}{M}^{-1}\bigl(\left|1^{M}\,0^{N}\right>\!\left<1^{M}\,0^{N}\right|+\text{permuted terms}\bigr).\end{split}

(23)

To study the asymmetry, we focus on the
\SU(2)
-
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
Schur-Weyl duality
on the tensor product system
\mathcal{H}=(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
depicted as

\displaystyle\SU(2)\curvearrowright(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}\curvearrowleft\mathfrak{S}_{n}.

(24)

The Schur-Weyl duality claims that we have the following decomposition of

(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}
into irreducible representations (irreps for short):

\displaystyle(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}=\bigoplus_{x=0}^{{n/2}}\mathcal{U}_{(n-x,x)}\boxtimes\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}.

(25)

Here
\boxtimes
denotes the tensor product of linear spaces, equipped with

\SU(2)
-action on the left and
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-action on the right factor,

\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}
denotes the
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-irrep corresponding to the partition
(n-x,x)
,
and
\mathcal{U}_{(n-x,x)}
denotes the highest weight
\SU(2)
-irrep of dimension

.
Using the hook formula (see
[
21
, I.5, Example 2; I.7, (7.6)]
for example),
we can compute the dimension of
\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}
as

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\dim\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}=\frac{k!}{\prod_{\square\in(n-x,x)}h(\square)}\\
=&\frac{n-2x+1}{n-x+1}\binom{n}{x}=\binom{n}{x}-\binom{n}{x-1},\end{split}

(26)

where
\square\in(n-x,x)
denotes a box in the Young diagram
corresponding to the partition
(n-x,x)
.
Using this formula, we have the completely mixed state

\rho_{\text{mix},\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}}=\frac{1}{\dim\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}}\id_{\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}}
.
The highest weight
\SU(2)
-irrep of dimension
2j+1
has the standard basis

\{\ket{j,m}\mid m=-j,-j+1,\dotsc,j\}
, where

j
and
m
express the total and z-direction angular momenta, respectively.

In our analysis, the projector
\mathsf{P}_{(n-x,x)}
onto the isotypical component
in the decomposition (
25
)
plays a key role, and is defined as

\displaystyle\mathsf{P}_{(n-x,x)}\colon(\mathbb{C}^{2})^{\otimes n}\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{U}_{(n-x,x)}\boxtimes\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}.

(27)

As the set of the projectors

\{\mathsf{P}_{(n-x,x)}\}_{x}
forms a projective measurement,
we define the probability
p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l)
as

\displaystyle p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l):=\bra{\Xi_{n,m|k,l}}\mathsf{P}_{(n-x,x)}\ket{\Xi_{n,m|k,l}},

(28)

and denote the corresponding probability distribution by

.
Since we can switch
\ket{0}\leftrightarrow\ket{1}
in the system,
the probability mass function (pmf) enjoys the system symmetry

\displaystyle p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l)=p(x\,|\,n,n-m,k,k-l),

(29)

Since the distribution

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\av_{\pi}[\Xi_{n,m|k,l}]\\
=&\sum_{x=0}^{{n/2}}p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l)\left|\tfrac{n}{2}-x,\tfrac{n-2m}{2}\right>\!\left<\tfrac{n}{2}-x,\tfrac{n-2m}{2}\right|\\
&\boxtimes\rho_{\text{mix},\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}},\end{split}

(30)

our analysis is concentrated in the distribution

.
We also denote by

a discrete random variable distributed by
the probability mass function (pmf for short)

.
We choose integers

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathfrak{N}\\
:=&\Big\{(n,m,k,l)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{4}\Big|\begin{array}[]{l}m,k\leq n,\\
\ m+k-n\leq l\leq m\wedge k\end{array}\Big\}.\end{split}

(31)

Then, the von Neumann entropy is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}&S\bigl(\av_{\pi}[\Xi_{n,m|k,l}]\bigr)\\
=&\sum_{x}p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l)\\
&\cdot\bigl(\log\dim\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}-\log p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l)\bigr),\end{split}

(32)

which expresses the amount of asymmetry of
\ket{\Xi_{n,m|k,l}}
.
Furthermore, we can also consider the number of approximately orthogonal elements among
the states
\{\pi(g)\ket{\Xi_{n,m|k,l}}\}_{g\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}
,
which is characterized by a slightly different quantity.
As explained in Section
7
, the number of distinguishable elements
with error probability
\varepsilon>0
is approximated by the following value:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\overline{H}_{s}^{\varepsilon}(\av_{\pi}[\Xi_{n,m|k,l}])\\
&:=\max\biggl\{\lambda\bigg|P_{n,m,k,l}[\{x\mid\log\dim\mathcal{V}_{(n-x,x)}\\
&\hskip 43.05542pt-\log p(x\,|\,n,m,k,l)\leq\lambda\}]\leq\epsilon\biggr\}.\end{split}

(33)

4
Asymmetry with decohered symmetric state

First, we discuss the case with the decohered state

\rho_{mix,N+M,M}
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle S(\av_{\pi}[\left|1^{l}\,0^{k-l}\right>\!\left<1^{l}\,0^{k-l}\right|\otimes\rho_{mix,N+M,M}])

\displaystyle-S(\left|1^{l}\,0^{k-l}\right>\!\left<1^{l}\,0^{k-l}\right|\otimes\rho_{mix,N+M,M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(\rho_{mix,k+N+M,l+M})-S(\rho_{mix,N+M,M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\binom{l+M}{k+N+M}-\log\binom{M}{N+M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\log\binom{m}{n}-\log\binom{m-l}{n-k}.

(34)

To consider the relation with
S\bigl(\av_{\pi}[\Xi_{n,m|k,l}]\bigr)
,
we consider the pinching map
{\cal E}

In Type I limit,

8
Conclusion and discussion

We have shown that the coherence in the Dicke state enhances
the asymmetry for the permutation when a qubits state
\ket{1^{l}\,0^{k-l}}

is attached.
To clarify the merit of the coherence, we have discussed two types of limits, Type I and II limits.
In these limits,
the case with the Dicke state has
a larger amount of degree than
the case with the decohered state.
In particular, under Type I limit,
the amount of the degree in the former has a strictly larger order than
that of the latter.
Under Type II limit,
both cases have different leading terms.
Their difference characterizes the effect of the existence of
the coherence.
This fact shows the importance of the coherence even in the
asymmetry for permutation.
Using this discussion, we have derived the inequality (
58
).
Since the inequality (
58
) can be considered without this type of limit,
it is possible to directly derive this inequality without considering this type of limit.
This kind of derivation is an interesting future study.

For our asymptotic derivations,
we have employed various asymptotic formulas for
the distribution
P_{n,m,k,l}
.
Since the distribution
P_{n,m,k,l}
has a highly complicated form,
this fact shows the usefulness of
these asymptotic formulas.
Since the distribution
P_{n,m,k,l}
is based on the
the Schur-Weyl duality, which is a key structure in quantum information,
we can expect that
these formulas can be used in other topics in quantum information.

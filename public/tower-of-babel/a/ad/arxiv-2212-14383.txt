Topology of 3-manifolds with uniformly positive scalar curvature
Jian Wang

Abstract
. In this article, we classify (non-compact) 3 3 -manifolds with uniformly positive scalar curvature. Precisely, we show that an orientable 3-manifold has a complete metric with uniformly positive scalar curvature if and only if it is homeomorphic to an (possibly) infinite connected sum of spherical 3 3 -manifolds and some copies of 𝕊 2 × 𝕊 1 \mathbb{S}^{2}\times\mathbb{S}^{1} . Further, we study a 3 3 -manifold with mean convex boundary and with uniformly positive scalar curvature. If the boundary is a disjoint union of closed surfaces, then the manifold is an (possibly) infinite connected sum of spherical 3 3 -manifolds, some copies of 𝕊 1 × 𝕊 2 \mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{S}^{2} and some handlebodies.



1.
Introduction

In a Riemannian manifold

When considering the topology of

[
Yau82
]
)

How to classify
3
-manifolds admitting complete Riemannian metrics of (uniformly) positive scalar curvature up to diffeomorphisms?

Extensive research has been devoted to this question by various authors, including Schoen-Yau’s works
[
SY79a
,
SY79b
,
SY82
]
, Gromov-Lawson’s
[

,

]
and other. In the case of closed

[

,

,

. Notably, he proved that a closed and oriented

(called spherical

. Bessières, Besson and Maillot extended his result to (non-compact)

However, the classification of open

]

]

&=\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega^{\tau}_{+}\setminus\Omega}d\mathcal{H}^{2}-\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega\cap\Omega^{\tau}_{+}}d\mathcal{H}^{2}-\int_{\Omega^{\tau}_{+}\setminus\Omega}h~d\mathcal{H}^{3}\\
&<\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega^{\tau}_{+}\setminus\Omega}d\mathcal{H}^{2}-\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega\cap\Omega^{\tau}_{+}}d\mathcal{H}^{2}-\int_{\Omega^{\tau}_{+}\setminus\Omega}\text{div}(\eta)~d\mathcal{H}^{3}\end{split}

(3.5)

We have that
\partial(\Omega^{\tau}_{+}\setminus\Omega)
consists of two parts,
\partial^{*}\Omega^{\tau}_{+}\setminus\Omega
and
\partial^{*}\Omega\cap\Omega^{\tau}_{+}
. It follows from the fact that
\Omega^{\tau}_{+}\setminus\Omega\subset\text{Int}~~M
. Moreover,

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega^{\tau}_{+}\setminus\Omega}\text{div}(\eta)&=\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega^{\tau}_{+}\setminus\Omega}\eta\cdot\nu~d\mathcal{H}^{2}-\int_{\partial*\Omega\cap\Omega^{\tau}_{+}}\eta\cdot\nu~d\mathcal{H}^{2}\\
&\geq\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega^{\tau}_{+}\setminus\Omega}d\mathcal{H}^{2}-\int_{\partial^{*}\Omega\cap\Omega^{\tau}_{+}}d\mathcal{H}^{2},\end{split}

(3.6)

where
\nu
is the unit normal vector and
\eta\cdot\nu=1
on
\partial^{*}\Omega^{\tau}_{+}\setminus\Omega
.

Thus,
\mathcal{A}(\Omega\cup\Omega^{\tau}_{+})<\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
. By an analogous calculation,
\mathcal{A}(\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\tau}_{-})<\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
. It follows that for any
\Omega\in\mathcal{C}
,

\mathcal{A}(\Omega)>\mathcal{A}((\Omega\cup\Omega^{\tau}_{+})\setminus\Omega^{\tau}_{-})\geq-C\mathcal{H}^{3}(M),

(3.7)

where
|h|\leq C
on
M\setminus(\Omega^{\tau}_{+}\cup\Omega^{\tau}_{-})
. Thus,

.

Take a sequence

with
\mathcal{A}(\Omega_{k})\rightarrow I
as

. From the above, we may assume that

\Omega_{k}\Delta\Omega_{0}\subset M\setminus(\Omega^{\tau}_{+}\cup\Omega^{\tau}_{-}).

(3.8)

We have that for

large enough,
\mathcal{A}(\Omega_{k})\leq I+1
and
|h|\leq C
on
M\setminus(\Omega^{\tau}_{+}\cup\Omega^{\tau}_{-})
. It follows that

\mathcal{H}^{2}(\partial^{*}\Omega_{k})\leq I+1+C\mathcal{H}^{3}(M)

By BV-compactness, taking a subsequence, the sets
\Omega_{k}
converges to a Caccioppoli set

. It follows that

is a minimizer of
\mathcal{A}
and thus has smooth boundary from the standard regularity theory
[
Tam84
]
. ∎

In the following, we will repeatedly use the geometric version of loop lemma.

Theorem 4.3

(See
[

and

i=1,2
. If

[\Sigma]\neq 0

It remains to show that one of components of

The Mayer-Vietoris sequence shows

Since

,
Hence,

.
One of

is isomorphic to

and generated by
[\Sigma]
.

We may assume that

\mathbb{Z}
and generated by
[\Sigma]
.
Thus,
H_{2}(\overline{M}_{2})
is trivial. From the above remark,
\overline{M}_{2}
is compact.
Since

\overline{M}_{1}

We finish the proof of the lemma.

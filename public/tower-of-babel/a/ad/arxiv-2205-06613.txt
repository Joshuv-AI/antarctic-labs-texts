Smooth l -Fano weighted complete intersections
Anastasia V. Vikulova

Abstract
. In this paper we prove that for n n -dimensional smooth l l -Fano well formed weighted complete intersections, which is not isomorphic to a usual projective space, the upper bound for l l is equal to ⌈ log 2 ⁡ ( n + 2 ) ⌉ − 1 . \lceil\log_{2}(n+2)\rceil-1. We also prove that the only l l -Fano of dimension n n among such manifolds with inequalities ⌈ log 3 ⁡ ( n + 2 ) ⌉ ⩽ l ⩽ ⌈ log 2 ⁡ ( n + 2 ) ⌉ − 1 \lceil\log_{3}(n+2)\rceil\leqslant l\leqslant\lceil\log_{2}(n+2)\rceil-1 is a complete intersection of quadrics in a usual projective space.



1.
Introduction

A smooth projective variety
X
is called a Fano variety, if the first Chern class of the tangent bundle
\text{c}_{1}(X)
is ample.
Varieties which are called
l
-Fano varieties and which generalize Fano varieties were introduced in the papers
[
1
]
and
[
18
]
.
In the sequel, we always work over the field of complex numbers.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that the Chern character
\text{ch}_{i}(X)
of a smooth projective variety
X
is positive if
\text{ch}_{i}(X)\cdot Z>0
for every effective
i
-cycle
Z.

Definition 1.2
.

A smooth Fano variety
X
is called an
l-Fano
variety if the Chern characters
\text{ch}_{i}(X)
are positive for all
2\leqslant i\leqslant l.

Example 1.3
.

It is obvious that a usual projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is an
l
-Fano variety for
l=1,\ldots,n
. Indeed, we have
\text{ch}_{k}(\mathbb{P}^{n})\,=\,\frac{n+1}{k!}H^{k},
where
H
is a hyperplane class in
\mathbb{P}^{n}.

Example 1.4
.

If
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
is a smooth complete intersection of hypersurfaces of degrees
d_{1},\ldots,d_{k},
then
X
is an
l
-Fano
variety if and only if
\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}^{l}<N+1
(see, for example,
[
17
, Section 2, Example 3]
).
In particular, we immediately get that a smooth complete intersection of
k
quadrics
X
in a projective space is
l
-Fano
if and only if
l\leqslant\lceil\log_{2}(\frac{N+1}{k})\rceil-1.
In other words, a complete intersection of
k
quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
is
l
-Fano variety if and only if
k\leqslant\lceil\frac{N+1}{2^{l}}\rceil-1.
In the same way we get that a smooth cubic hypersurface in a projective space is
l
-Fano
if and only if
l\leqslant\lceil\log_{3}(N+1)\rceil-1.

Definition
1.2
is motivated by the following observations. While a general point of a Fano variety is contained in a rational curve (see
[
8
, Chapter 5, Theorem 1.6.1]
), a general point of a 2-Fano variety is contained in a rational surface under some mild assumptions (see
[
18
]
). Another property of Fano varieties is Tsen theorem which states that a smooth Fano hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}_{K}
over a field
K/k
of transcendence degree
1
over an algebraically closed field
k
always has a
K
-point. At the same time, Tsen–Lang theorem states that a smooth
l
-Fano
hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}_{K}
over a field
K/k
of transcendence degree
l
over an algebraically closed field
k
always has a
K
-point (see
[
7
, Theorem 6]
).

The following conjecture was suggested in
[
1
]
:

Conjecture 1.5

(
[
1
, Conjecture 1.7]
)
.

If
X
is an n-dimensional smooth
l
-Fano
variety with
l\geqslant\lceil\log_{2}(n+2)\rceil,
then
X\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n}.

There is some progress in proving this conjecture. In
[
1
, Theorems 1.3 and 1.5]
there is a classification of
2
-Fano varieties among rational homogeneous spaces of Picard rank 1 and a classification of
3
-Fano varieties of dimension
n
and index at least
n-2
(here the index is the maximal positive integer which divides the canonical class), and it was shown that for these two classes of varieties Conjecture
1.5
is true. In the paper
[
14
]
by computer calculations it was shown that the only smooth toric varieties of dimension at most
8
with positive second Chern character is a projective space. Thus, for toric varieties of dimension at most
8
Conjecture
1.5
is true.

The purpose of this paper is to study Conjecture
1.5
in the case of smooth weighted complete intersections (see
[
6
]
or Section
2
below for definitions and basic properties of these varieties).

Remark 1.6
.

Note that we assume that codimension of a smooth weighted complete intersection is positive, i.e. a weighted projective space is not considered as a weighted complete intersection in itself. Note also that by
[
13
, Theorem 2.7]
a smooth well formed weighted complete intersection which is not an intersection with a linear cone is not isomorphic to a projective space.

We are going to prove the following theorem:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{N})
be a smooth
n
-dimensional well formed weighted complete intersection which is not an intersection with a linear cone.
Suppose that
X
is
l
-Fano. Then
l<\lceil\log_{2}(n+2)\rceil.

In other words, Theorem
1.7
states that Conjecture
1.5
holds for smooth well formed weighted complete intersections which are not intersections with a linear cone. Moreover, in this case there is the following strengthening of Theorem
1.7
:

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{N})
be a smooth n-dimensional well formed weighted complete intersection which is not an intersection with a linear cone.
Suppose that
X
is
l
-Fano, and

\lceil\log_{3}(n+2)\rceil\leqslant l<\lceil\log_{2}(n+2)\rceil.

Then

\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}(1,\dots,1)=\mathbb{P}^{N}

and
X\subset\mathbb{P}
is a complete intersection of at most
\lceil\frac{N+1}{2^{l}}\rceil-1
quadrics.

Remark 1.9
.

By
[
17
, Section 2, Example 3]
a smooth weighted complete intersection

X\subset\mathbb{P}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{N})
of multidegree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{k})
is
l
-Fano if and only if

(1.1)

\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{j}^{m}>\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}^{m}\;\;\text{for all}\;\;m=1,\ldots,l.

In Section
3
we show that in order to check
l
-Fano condition it is sufficient to show that the inequality (
1.1
) holds for
m=l.

The plan of the paper is as follows.
In Section
2
we collect some basic facts about weighted complete intersections.
In Section
3
we study the Chern characters of Fano varieties and prove Theorem
3.1
about monotonocity property of smooth
l
-Fano
varieties; namely, we show that if the
(l+1)
-th Chern character of smooth well formed weighted complete intersection which is not an intersection with a linear cone is positive, then the
l
-th one is positive as well.
In Section
4
we prove Theorem
1.7
.
In Section
5
we prove Theorem
1.8
.
In Appendices
A
and
B
we perform some auxiliary elementary computations.

2.
Preliminaries

First of all, we recall some definitions.

Definition 2.1
.

The variety
\mathbb{P}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{N})=\text{Proj}\,\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{N}],
where the weight of the variable
x_{i}
is equal to
a_{i},
is called a weighted projective space.

Definition 2.2
.

A weighted complete intersection
X\subset\mathbb{P}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{N})
of multidegree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}),
where
k\geqslant 1
is an intersection of hypersurfaces of degrees
d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}
such that the codimension of every irreducible component of
X
is equal to
k.

Definition 2.3
.

We say that a weighted projective space
\mathbb{P}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{N})
is well formed if
\gcd(a_{0},\ldots,\widehat{a_{i}},\ldots,a_{N})\,=\,1
for all
i.
We say that a weighted complete intersection
X
in a well formed projective space
\mathbb{P}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{N})
is well formed if

\text{codim}_{X}(X\cap\text{Sing}\,\mathbb{P}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{N}))\geqslant 2.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{N})
be a weighted complete intersection of multidegree
(
d_{1},\ldots,d_{k})
in a well formed weighted projective space. We say that
X
is not an intersection with a linear cone, if
d_{i}\neq a_{j}
for all
i
and
j.

Remark 2.5
.

It is reasonable to consider
X
such that it is not an intersection with a linear cone because of the following observation. Assume that
X\subset\mathbb{P}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{N})
is a degree
d
hypersurface which is an intersection with a linear cone, i.e.
d=a_{i}
for some
i.
Then the general hypersurface is the zero set of a polynomial
f=x_{i}+g.
Such hypersurfaces are parametrized by a Zariski open subset in the space of degree
d
hypersurfaces (see
[
6
, Chapter 6]
). Thus, if
X\subset\mathbb{P}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{N})
is a general smooth complete intersection of multidegree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{k})
and
d_{r}=a_{s}
for some
r
and
s,
then
X\simeq X^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{P}(a_{0},\ldots,\widehat{a_{s}},\ldots,a_{N}),
where
X^{\prime}
is a smooth weighted complete intersection of multidegree
(d_{1},\ldots,\widehat{d_{r}},\ldots,d_{k}).

The main auxiliary result used in the proofs of Theorems
1.7
,
1.8
and
3.1
is the following:

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{N})
be a smooth well formed Fano weighted complete intersection of multidegree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{k})
which is not an intersection with a linear cone. Assume that

d_{1}\leqslant d_{2}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant d_{k}\qquad\text{and}\qquad a_{0}\leqslant a_{1}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant a_{N}.

Then

(1)

d_{i}\,>\,a_{N-k+i}\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leqslant i\leqslant k-1;

(2)

\#\{j\mid a_{j}\,=\,1\}\geqslant k+1;

(3)

\#\{j\mid a_{j}\,=\,1\}\geqslant\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{j}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i};

(4)

d_{i}\neq a_{j}\quad\text{for all}\quad i,j;

(5)

d_{i}\neq 1\quad\text{for all}\quad i;

(6)

N\geqslant 2k;

(7)

\#\{j\mid b\;\;\text{divides}\;\;a_{j}\}\leqslant\#\{i\mid b\;\;\text{divides}\;\;d_{i}\}\quad\text{for all}\quad b\in\mathbb{N}_{>1};

(8)

d_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot d_{k}\;\;\text{is divisible by}\;\;a_{0}\cdot\ldots\cdot a_{N};

(9)

d_{k}\geqslant 2a_{N}.

Proof.

We refer to
[
6
, Lemma 18.14]
for
(1)
; we refer to
[
10
, Corollary 5.3 and 5.11]
for
(2)
and
(3)
; assertions
(4)
and
(5)
follow from assertion
(2)
and from the assumption that
X
is not an intersection with a linear cone; for
(6)
we refer to
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
; for
(7)
we refer to
[
11
, Lemma 2.15]
and
(8)
follows from
(7)
.

Assertion
(9)
can be proved using
(5)
and
(7)
. Indeed, if all
a_{j}\,=\,1,
then by
(5)
we have

d_{k}\geqslant 2=2a_{N}.

If
a_{N}\neq 1,
then according to
(7)
there is
d_{t}
such that
d_{t}=ma_{N}
for some integer
m>1.
Thus we obtain

d_{k}\geqslant d_{t}=ma_{N}\geqslant 2a_{N}.

∎

3.
Chern characters

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{N})
be a smooth well formed weighted complete intersection of multidegree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{k}).
It is well known (for example,
see
[
1
, Section 6]
) that the Chern characters can be represented in a simple way as

(3.1)

\text{ch}_{l}(X)\,=\,\frac{1}{l!}(\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{j}^{l}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}^{l})H^{l}|_{X},

where
H
is a generator of class group
\text{Cl}(\mathbb{P}).
This follows immediately from the exact sequence

(3.2)

0\to T_{X}\to T_{\mathbb{P}}|_{X}\to\bigoplus_{j=1}^{k}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(d_{j})|_{X}\to 0,

where
T_{\mathbb{P}}=\Omega_{\mathbb{P}}^{\vee}
is the dual sheaf of the sheaf of Kähler differentials. Let us remark that (
3.2
) is well defined, because we assume that
X
is well formed and thus by
[
11
, Proposition 2.11]
it does not pass through the singular locus of
\mathbb{P}.
So we have the following exact sequence (for example, see
[
5
, Theorem 8.1.6]
)

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}\to\bigoplus_{j=0}^{N}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}}(a_{j})\to T_{\mathbb{P}}\to 0,

which gives us the Chern characters of
T_{\mathbb{P}}.

We will also prove the following theorem for the Chern characters:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{N})
be a smooth well formed Fano weighted complete intersection which is not an intersection with a linear cone.
Then
X
is
l
-Fano
if and only if
\emph{\text{ch}}_{l}(X)
is positive.

Proof.

We should prove that if
\text{ch}_{l}(X)
is positive, then all
\text{ch}_{m}(X)
for
2\leqslant m\leqslant l-1
are positive as well. So we have to prove that
\text{ch}_{m}(X)\cdot Z>0
for every effective
m
-cycle
Z
and all
2\leqslant m\leqslant l-1.
But by (
3.1
) we know that
\text{ch}_{m}(X)
is a multiple of
H^{m}|_{X}
which is a power of an ample divisor. Thus, we have that
H^{m}|_{X}\cdot Z>0
for every effective
m
-cycle
Z.
So we have to prove that the coefficients at
H^{m}|_{X}
in the expression for
\text{ch}_{m}(X)
(see (
3.1
)) are positive for
m\leqslant l.
For this we will prove that

(3.3)

\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{j}^{m}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}^{m}>\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{j}^{m+1}-\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}^{m+1}

for all integers
m\geqslant 1.

From Theorem
2.6
(3)
and Fano condition we get that if

s\,=\,\#\{j\mid a_{j}\,=\,1\},

then

\sum_{j=s}^{N}a_{j}\,\leqslant\,\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}\,<\,\sum_{j=s}^{N}a_{j}+s.

As all assumptions of Lemma
A.4
hold in the theorem, thus we get that inequality (
3.3
) is true.

∎

It is not true in general that if the
l
-th Chern character of smooth Fano variety is positive, then the (
l-1
)-th one is positive as well. Let us consider the following example.

Example 3.2
.

Let
X
be a blow-up of a point on the projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}.
Its Picard group is
\text{Pic}(X)=\mathbb{Z}H\oplus\mathbb{Z}E,
where
H
is the pullback of the hyperplane class in
\mathbb{P}^{n},
and
E
is an exceptional divisor. We have
X\simeq\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}(-1))
and there is a natural
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-fibration

\pi:X\to\mathbb{P}^{n-1}.
If
H^{\prime}
is a hyperplane class in
\mathbb{P}^{n-1},
then
\pi^{*}(H^{\prime})=H-E.

According to
[
4
, Lemma 2.3]
the cone of effective
k
-cycles of
X
for
1\leqslant k<n
is generated by

•

X_{k}=H^{n-k}-(-1)^{n-k+1}E^{n-k},

•

Y_{k}=(-1)^{n-k+1}E^{n-k}.

Also note that the following relations on
H
and
E
hold:

(3.4)

H\cdot E=0,\;\;H^{n}=1,\;\;E^{n}=(-1)^{n+1}.

We have the exact sequence

0\to L\to T_{X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\pi^{*}T_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}\to 0,

where
L\simeq K_{X}^{-1}\otimes\pi^{*}K_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}
is an invertible sheaf on
X.
So we get that the Chern character of
X
is

\text{ch}(X)=\text{ch}(K_{X}^{-1})\text{ch}(\pi^{*}K_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}})+\text{ch}(\pi^{*}T_{\mathbb{P}^{n-1}}).

One we can rewrite it as

\text{ch}(X)=\exp(H+E)+n\exp(H-E)-1.

By (
3.4
) we get

\text{ch}_{k}(X)=\frac{(H+E)^{k}}{k!}+n\frac{(H-E)^{k}}{k!}=\frac{1}{k!}((n+1)H^{k}+(-1)^{k}(n+(-1)^{k})E^{k}).

So we can calculate the intersection of
\text{ch}_{k}
with effective
k
-cycles for
k<n
:

\text{ch}_{k}(X)\cdot X_{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\left((n+1)-(n+(-1)^{k})\right)

and

\text{ch}_{k}(X)\cdot Y_{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\left(n+(-1)^{k}\right).

For
k=n
we obtain

\text{ch}_{n}(X)=\frac{1}{n!}\left(1-(-1)^{n}\right).

Thus we get that
\text{ch}_{k}(X)
is positive for odd
k
and nef but not positive for even
k.
In particular,
X
is not a
2
-Fano variety.

Remark 3.3
.

There are other examples of varieties with the same behavior of the Chern characters (see, for example,
[
16
, Example 4.1]
). The is a conjecture which states that all smooth toric varieties except
\mathbb{P}^{n}
are not
2
-Fano. See, for example, the paper
[
16
]
. However, if we consider
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial terminal varieties, then there is an example of a toric
2
-Fano variety which is not isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{n}
(see
[
15
]
).

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.7

Without loss of generality we can assume that

d_{1}\leqslant d_{1}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant d_{k}\qquad\text{and}\qquad a_{0}\leqslant a_{1}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant a_{N}.

Also we can assume that
X
is a Fano variety, because otherwise there is nothing to prove. It is enough to prove that for
l=\lceil\log_{2}(N-k+2)\rceil

the
l
-th Chern
character is not positive, i.e.

\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}^{l}\geqslant\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{j}^{l}.

From Lemma
A.4
it is enough to prove this for
l=\log_{2}(N-k+2)
. Assuming that
\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}^{l}<\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{j}^{l}.
Taking into account assertions
(2)
,
(5)
and
(9)
of Theorem
2.6
and the fact that
a_{N}\geqslant a_{j}
for
k+1\leqslant j\leqslant N,
we get

k-1+2^{l}a_{N}^{l}\,<\,\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}^{l}\,<\,\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{j}^{l}\leqslant k+1+a_{N}^{l}(N-k).

We obtain

k-1+(N-k+2)a_{N}^{l}\,<\,k+1+a_{N}^{l}(N-k),

so we get
a_{N}^{l}<1,
which is impossible.

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.8

Let us prove some preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth well formed Fano weighted complete intersection of codimension
k
in
\mathbb{P}(a_{0},\ldots,a_{N}),
which is not an intersection with a linear cone. Assume that
\lceil\log_{3}(N-k+2)\rceil=1
. Then
N=2,

k=1
and
X
is a conic in
\mathbb{P}(1,1,1)=\mathbb{P}^{2}.

Proof.

As
\lceil\log_{3}(N-k+2)\rceil=1,
we have that
N-k+2\leqslant 3,
or, equivalently,
N-k\leqslant 1.
The case when
N-k=0
is impossible by Theorem
2.6
(6)
. Thus, we have
N-k=1.
According to assertions
(2)
and
(6)
of Theorem
2.6
this holds if and only if
N=2,

k=1
and
s\geqslant 2.
But the only smooth Fano variety in this case should be a curve
X\subset\mathbb{P}(1,1,a_{2})
of degree
d
such that
2+a_{2}>d.
However, by Theorem
2.6
(9)
we have
2+a_{2}>d\geqslant 2a_{2}.
Thus, we get
a_{2}=1
and
d=2,
i.e.
X
is a conic in
\mathbb{P}^{2}.

∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a smooth Fano complete intersection of codimension
k
which does not lie in a hyperplane. Assume that
X
is not an intersection of quadrics. Then for
l=\log_{3}(N-k+2)
the inequality

(5.1)

\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}^{l}\geqslant N+1

holds true.

Proof.

From our assumptions there is
d_{i}
such that
d_{i}>2.
Suppose that the inequality (
5.1
) is false, i.e.

\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}^{l}\,<\,N+1.

We have

(k-1)2^{l}+3^{l}\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}^{l}\,<\,N+1.

Thus we get

(k-1)2^{l}+N-k+2\,<\,N+1,

which is equivalent to
(k-1)\cdot 2^{l}\,<\,k-1,
but it is impossible, because
l>1
by Lemma
5.1
.

∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.8
.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}(a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})
be a smooth well formed weighted complete intersection of multidegree
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{k})
which is not an intersection with a linear cone. Without loss of generality we can assume that

d_{1}\leqslant d_{2}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant d_{k}\qquad\text{and}\qquad a_{0}\leqslant a_{1}\leqslant\ldots\leqslant a_{N}.

We can also assume that
X
is a Fano variety, because otherwise there is nothing to prove. Also we assume that
X
is not a complete intersection of quadrics in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
by Example
1.4
. It is enough to prove that for
l=\lceil\log_{3}(N-k+2)\rceil
, which is at least 2 by Lemma
5.1
, the
l
-th Chern character is not positive, i.e.

(5.2)

\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{i}^{l}\geqslant\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{j}^{l}.

Let
s\,=\,\#\{a_{j}\mid a_{j}\,=\,1\}.
In the case
s=N+1
Theorem
1.8
holds true by Lemma
5.2
. So assume that
s\leqslant N.

Suppose that
a_{N}=2.
Then we have
a_{j}=2
for all
s\leqslant j\leqslant N.
Thus, by Theorem
2.6
(7)
we get
k\geqslant N-s+1.
So by Lemma
B.1
the inequality (
5.2
) holds.

Otherwise, if
a_{N}\geqslant 3,
the assumptions of Lemma
B.3
hold by
Theorem
2.6
.
Thus, the inequality (
5.2
) is implied by this lemma.

∎

Appendix A
Monotonicity and boundedness

In this appendix we prove some elementary lemmas about monotonicity and boundedness of functions which are needed in Theorems
1.8
and
3.1
.

Lemma A.1
.

If
a\geqslant b\geqslant c>0
, then the function
f(l)=a^{l}+b^{l}
grows slower than the function
g(l)=(a+c)^{l}+(b-c)^{l}
for
l\geqslant 1
in
\mathbb{R};
in other words, the function
g(l)-f(l)
in non-increasing for
l\geqslant 1.

Proof.

We have

(A.1)

h(l)=g(l)-f(l)=(a+c)^{l}+(b-c)^{l}-a^{l}-b^{l}.

If
b=c,
then we are done, because

h(l)=(a+b)^{l}-a^{l}-b^{l}=b^{l}\left(\left(\frac{a+b}{b}\right)^{l}-\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{l}-1\right)

which is monotonically increasing, as a product of monotonically increasing non-negative functions. So assume that
b>c.
We have

(A.2)

h(l)=(b-c)^{l}\left(\left(\frac{a+c}{b-c}\right)^{l}-\left(\frac{a}{b-c}\right)^{l}-\left(\frac{b}{b-c}\right)^{l}+1\right).

Put
y=\frac{a}{b-c}
and
z=\frac{b}{b-c}.
So we can rewrite
h(l)
as

h(l)=(b-c)^{l}\left((y+z-1)^{l}-y^{l}-z^{l}+1\right)=(b-c)^{l}t(l,y,z).

The function
(b-c)^{l}
is monotonically increasing. So it is enough to prove that
t(l,y,z)
is monotonically increasing with respect to
l.
Let us note that

y+z-1>y\geqslant z>1

by our conditions. We have

(A.3)

s(l,y,z)=\frac{\partial t(l,y,z)}{\partial l}=\ln(y+z-1)\cdot(y+z-1)^{l}-\ln(y)\cdot y^{l}-\ln(z)\cdot z^{l}.

For the sake of proving that
t(l,y,z)
is monotonically increasing, we need to show that
s(l,y,z)
is monotonically increasing with respect to
z
and get that

s(l,y,z)>s(l,y,1)=0.

Indeed,

\frac{\partial s(l,y,z)}{\partial z}=(y+z-1)^{l-1}-z^{l-1}+l\ln(y+z-1)\cdot(y+z-1)^{l-1}-l\ln(z)\cdot z^{l-1}>0.

Thus,
h(l)
is monotonically increasing.

∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
1\leqslant d\leqslant a\leqslant b
be real numbers. Then we have

a+b\leqslant\frac{a}{d}+bd.

Proof.

Indeed, we have

bd+\frac{a}{d}-a-b=\frac{a}{d}\left(\frac{bd^{2}}{a}+1-d-\frac{bd}{a}\right)=\frac{a}{d}\left(d-1\right)\left(\frac{bd}{a}-1\right)\geqslant 0.

∎

Lemma A.3
.

Let

where
l\in\mathbb{R}.
Then
f(l)\,>\,0
for
l\geqslant 2.

Proof.

Indeed, the function

is monotonically increasing, because

\frac{\partial}{\partial l}\left(\frac{f(l)}{2^{l}}\right)=2^{l}\ln 2-\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{l}\ln\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)>\ln 2\left(2^{l}-\left(\frac{3}{2}\right)^{l}\right)>0.

Moreover, we have
f(2)=3>0.
Thus,
f(l)
is positive for all

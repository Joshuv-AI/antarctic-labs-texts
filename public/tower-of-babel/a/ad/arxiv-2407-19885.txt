Explicit formulas related to Euler’s product expansion for cosine function
Taekyun Kim, Dae San Kim

Abstract
. In this paper, we derive by using elementary methods some continued fractions, certain identities involving derivatives of tan ⁡ x \tan x , several expressions for log ⁡ cosh ⁡ x \log\cosh x and an identity for π 2 \pi^{2} , from a series expansion of tan ⁡ x \tan x , which gives the product expansion of the cosine function.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
11A55; 11B68

1.
Introduction and preliminaries

In this paper, from a series expansion of
\tan x
(see (
1
)), which gives the product expansion of the cosine function, we derive some continued fractions, certain identities involving derivatives of
\tan x
, several expressions for
\log\cosh x
and an identity for
\pi^{2}
.

In more detail, the outline of this paper is as follows. In Section 1, we recall the Bernoulli numbers, the Riemann zeta function together with their special values at even positive integers, and the product expansion of the sine function. We remind the reader of the Euler numbers and explicit expressions of them obtained by a contour integral. In fact, these yield a relationship between Euler and Bernoulli numbers (see (
14
)). We show that

(1)

\displaystyle\tan x

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{2}{\pi(2k+1)}\bigg)^{2}2x\frac{1}{1-\big(\frac{2x}{\pi(2k+1)}\big)^{2}}

\displaystyle=8x\bigg(\frac{1}{\pi^{2}-4x^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{((2k+1)\pi)^{2}-4x^{2}}\bigg),

by expressing
\tan x
as a series involving the Euler numbers and invoking the aforementioned explicit expressions of Euler numbers. Then we derive the product expansion of the cosine function from (
1
). Section 2 contains the main results of this paper. In Theorem 2.1, we derive a continued fraction for
\frac{\pi}{4}
. In Theorem 2.2, by successively applying differential operators to the expression
\frac{\tan x}{8x}+\frac{1}{4x^{2}-\pi^{2}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{((2k+1)\pi)^{2}-4x^{2}}
we show that it is equal to
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{((2k+1)\pi)^{2}-4x^{2}}\Big)^{n+1}
. By taking
(2n-2)
th derivative of the partial fraction expression,
\tan x=2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{(2k+1)\pi-2x}-\frac{1}{(2k-1)\pi+2x}\Big)
, of (
1
), dividing by
2^{2n-1}(2n-2)!
, and evaluating at
x=\frac{\pi}{4}
, we have the expression in Theorem 2.3, which is equal to
\frac{1}{4^{2n-1}}\Big(\zeta\Big(2n-1,\frac{1}{4}\Big)-\zeta\Big(2n-1,\frac{3}{4}\Big)\Big)
. Here
\zeta(s,x)
is the Hurwitz zeta function. By taking the derivative of (
1
), and letting
x=\frac{\pi}{2n+1}
we obtain an expression of
\pi^{2}
as an infinite series, which is valid for any integer
n
. Letting
n=0
, we get an expression of
\frac{\pi}{2}
in Corollary 2.5. From the product expansion of the cosine function, we show that
\frac{\tanh x-\tan x}{8x}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{(2k+1)^{2}\pi^{2}+4x^{2}}-\frac{1}{(2k+1)^{2}\pi^{2}-4x^{2}}\bigg)
. From the identity obtained by letting
x=\frac{\pi}{2(2n+1)}
in this, we get a continued fraction for
\bigg(\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\tanh(\frac{\pi}{2(2n+1)})-\tan(\frac{\pi}{2(2n+1)})}{2n+1}\bigg)^{-1}
in Theorem 2.6. Finally, from the observation
\tanh x=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{2n-1}\frac{2^{2n-1}x^{2n-1}}{(2n-1)!}
we have several expressions for
\log\cosh x
in Theorem 2.7. For the rest of this section, we recall the facts that are needed throughout this paper.

The Bernoulli numbers are defined by

(2)

\frac{z}{e^{z}-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}B_{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-20]).

The first few terms of
B_{n}
are given by:

\displaystyle B_{0}=1,\,B_{1}=-\frac{1}{2},\,B_{2}=\frac{1}{6},\,B_{4}=-\frac{1}{30},\,B_{6}=\frac{1}{42},\,B_{8}=-\frac{1}{30},\,B_{10}=\frac{5}{66},\,

\displaystyle B_{12}=-\frac{691}{2730},\,B_{14}=\frac{7}{6},\,B_{16}=-\frac{3617}{510},\,B_{18}=\frac{43867}{798},\,B_{20}=-\frac{174611}{330},\dots;

\displaystyle B_{2k+1}=0,\,\,(k\geq 1).

For
s\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}(s)>1
, the Riemann zeta function is defined by

(3)

\zeta(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{s}},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1,9,12]).

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(4)

\zeta(2n)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2n}}=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}(2\pi)^{2n}}{2(2n)!}B_{2n},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10]).

We observe from (
2
) that

(5)

z\cot z=\frac{2iz}{e^{2iz}-1}+iz=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}2^{2n}B_{2n}}{(2n)!}z^{2n}.

From (
4
) and (
5
), we get the following product expansion of the sine function:

\frac{\sin z}{z}=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{z}{k\pi}\bigg)^{2}\bigg),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,10,13]).

It is well known that a continued fraction for
\pi
is given by

(6)

\pi=\cfrac{4}{1+\cfrac{1^{2}}{2+\cfrac{3^{2}}{2+\cfrac{5^{2}}{2+\cfrac{7^{2}}{2+\cfrac{9^{2}}{2+\cdots}}}}}},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [5,14]),

In [14], another continued fraction for
\pi
is given by

(7)

\pi=3+\cfrac{1^{2}}{6+\cfrac{3^{2}}{6+\cfrac{5^{2}}{6+\cfrac{7^{2}}{6+\cfrac{9^{2}}{6+\cdots}}}}}

The Euler numbers are defined by

(8)

\frac{2}{e^{z}+1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{n}\frac{z^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [8,10,11]).

From (
8
), we note that

(9)

E_{0}=1,\quad E_{n}=-\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{n}{i}E_{i},\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10]).

The first few terms of
E_{n}
are given by:

\displaystyle E_{0}=1,\,E_{1}=-\frac{1}{2},\,E_{3}=\frac{1}{4},\,E_{5}=-\frac{1}{2},\,E_{7}=\frac{17}{8},\,E_{9}=-\frac{31}{2},\,E_{11}=\frac{691}{4},\,

\displaystyle E_{13}=-\frac{5461}{2},\,E_{15}=\frac{929569}{16},\,E_{17}=-\frac{3202291}{2},\,E_{19}=\frac{221930581}{4},\dots;

\displaystyle E_{2k}=0,\,\,(k\geq 1).

For
n\geq 2
, by (
8
), we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\oint_{C_{N}}\frac{2}{e^{z}+1}\frac{1}{z^{n}}dz=2\pi i\Big(\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{z=0}\frac{2}{e^{z}+1}\frac{1}{z^{n}}+\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_{z=(2k+1)i\pi}\frac{2}{e^{z}+1}\frac{1}{z^{n}}\Big)

\displaystyle=2\pi i\Big(\frac{E_{n-1}}{(n-1)!}+\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{-2}{((2k+1)i\pi)^{n}}\Big),

where the contour
C_{N}
is the (positively oriented) circle with radius
2N\pi
centered at the origin
(N=1,2,3,\dots)
. Thus we have

(10)

\displaystyle E_{n-1}

\displaystyle=2(n-1)!\frac{1}{(\pi i)^{n}}\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2k+1)^{n}}

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\displaystyle 4(n-1)!\frac{(-1)^{n/2}}{\pi^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2k+1)^{n}}\displaystyle,&\textrm{for $n$ even,}\\
0,&\textrm{for $n$ odd.}\end{array}\right.

From (
10
), we note that

(13)

\displaystyle E_{2n-1}

\displaystyle=4(2n-1)!\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\pi^{2n}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2k+1)^{2n}},

\displaystyle=4(2n-1)!\frac{(-1)^{n}}{\pi^{2n}}\Big(1-\frac{1}{4^{n}}\Big)\zeta(2n)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2^{2n}-1}{n}B_{2n},\quad(n\geq 1).

As
E_{2n}=B_{2n+1}=0
for
n\geq 1
, the following holds true:

(14)

E_{n}=-\frac{2(2^{n+1}-1)}{n+1}B_{n+1},\quad(n\geq 0).

We observe that

(15)

\displaystyle i\tan x

\displaystyle=i\frac{\frac{e^{ix}-e^{-ix}}{2i}}{\frac{e^{ix}+e^{-ix}}{2}}=1-\frac{2}{e^{2ix}+1}

\displaystyle=1-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{n}\frac{(2i)^{n}}{n!}x^{n}

\displaystyle=-i\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{2n+1}\frac{2^{2n+1}(-1)^{n}}{(2n+1)!}x^{2n+1}.

From (
13
) and (
15
), we have

(16)

\displaystyle\tan x

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{2n+1}x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}(-1)^{n-1}E_{2n+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{2^{2n+1}x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}\frac{4(2n+1)!}{\pi^{2n+2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2k+1)^{2n+2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{2}{\pi(2k+1)}\bigg)^{2}2x\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{2x}{\pi(2k+1)}\bigg)^{2n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{2}{\pi(2k+1)}\bigg)^{2}2x\frac{1}{1-\big(\frac{2x}{\pi(2k+1)}\big)^{2}}.

By (
16
), we get

(17)

\displaystyle-\frac{d}{dx}\log\cos x=\tan x

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{2}{(2k+1)\pi}\bigg)^{2}(-2x)\frac{1}{1-\big(\frac{2x}{(2k+1)\pi}\big)^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dx}\log\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{2x}{(2k+1)\pi}\bigg)^{2}\bigg).

From (
17
), we have

(18)

\displaystyle\log\cos x

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\log\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{x}{(2k+1)\pi}\bigg)^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\log\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{2x}{(2k+1)\pi}\bigg)^{2}\bigg).

Hence, by (
18
), we get the product expansion of the cosine function:

(19)

\cos x=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{2x}{(2k+1)\pi}\bigg)^{2}\bigg).

2.
Explicit formulas related to Euler product expansion for cosine function

From (
16
), we note that

(20)

\displaystyle\tan x

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{2}{(2k+1)\pi}\bigg)^{2}2x\bigg(\frac{1}{1-\big(\frac{2x}{(2k+1)\pi}\big)^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=8x\bigg(\frac{1}{\pi^{2}-4x^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{((2k+1)\pi)^{2}-4x^{2}}\bigg).

From (
20
), we have

(21)

\frac{1}{8x}\tan x=\frac{1}{\pi^{2}-4x^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{((2k+1)\pi)^{2}-4x^{2}}.

Let
x=\pi
in (
21
). Then we have

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{((2k+1)\pi)^{2}-4\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2k-1)(2k+3)\pi^{2}}.

Multiplying
\pi^{2}
on both sides of (
22
), we get

(23)

\frac{1}{3}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2k-1)(2k+3)}=\frac{1}{1\cdot 5}+\frac{1}{3\cdot 7}+\frac{1}{5\cdot 9}+\cdots

Let
x=\frac{\pi}{4}
in (
21
). Then we see that

(24)

\frac{1}{2\pi}-\frac{4}{3\pi^{2}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{4}{(4k+1)(4k+3)\pi^{2}}.

Multiplying
\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}
on both sides of (
24
), we get

(25)

\frac{\pi}{8}-\frac{1}{3}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{4k+1}-\frac{1}{4k+3}\bigg).

Thus, by (
25
), we get

(26)

\frac{3\pi-8}{12}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{4k+1}-\frac{1}{4k+3}\bigg)=\frac{1}{5}-\frac{1}{7}+\frac{1}{9}-\frac{1}{11}+\frac{1}{13}-\frac{1}{15}+\cdots.

From (
23
), we get

(27)

\displaystyle\frac{12}{3\pi-8}-1=\cfrac{1}{\cfrac{3\pi-8}{12}}-1=\cfrac{1-\frac{1}{5}+\frac{1}{7}-\frac{1}{9}+\frac{1}{11}-\frac{1}{13}+\frac{1}{15}-\cdots}{\frac{1}{5}-\frac{1}{7}+\frac{1}{9}-\frac{1}{11}+\frac{1}{13}-\frac{1}{15}+\cdots}

\displaystyle=\cfrac{\big(\frac{4}{5}-\frac{4}{7}+\frac{4}{9}-\frac{4}{11}+\frac{4}{13}-\frac{4}{15}+\cdots)+\big(\frac{5}{7}-\frac{5}{9}+\frac{5}{11}-\frac{5}{13}+\cdots\big)}{\frac{1}{5}-\frac{1}{7}+\frac{1}{9}-\frac{1}{11}+\frac{1}{13}-\frac{1}{15}+\cdots}

\displaystyle=4+\cfrac{5^{2}}{\cfrac{\big(1-\frac{5}{7}\big)+\frac{5}{9}-\frac{5}{11}+\frac{5}{13}-\frac{5}{15}+\cdots}{\frac{1}{7}-\frac{1}{9}+\frac{1}{11}-\frac{1}{13}+\frac{1}{15}-\cdots}}

\displaystyle=4+\cfrac{5^{2}}{\cfrac{\big(\frac{2}{7}-\frac{2}{9}+\frac{2}{11}-\frac{2}{13}+\cdots\big)+\big(\frac{7}{9}-\frac{7}{11}+\frac{7}{13}-\cdots\big)}{\frac{1}{7}-\frac{1}{9}+\frac{1}{11}-\frac{1}{13}+\frac{1}{15}-\cdots}}

\displaystyle=4+\cfrac{5^{2}}{2+\cfrac{7^{2}}{\cfrac{\big(1-\frac{7}{9}\big)+\frac{7}{11}-\frac{7}{13}+\frac{7}{15}-\cdots}{\frac{1}{9}-\frac{1}{11}+\frac{1}{13}-\frac{1}{15}+\cdots}}}

\displaystyle=\cdots

\displaystyle=4+\cfrac{5^{2}}{2+\cfrac{7^{2}}{2+\cfrac{9^{2}}{2+\cfrac{11^{2}}{2+\cfrac{13^{2}}{2+\cdots}}}}}.

Therefore, by (
27
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

We have the following continued fraction for
\frac{\pi}{4}
:

\frac{\pi}{4}=\frac{2}{3}+\cfrac{1}{5+\cfrac{5^{2}}{2+\cfrac{7^{2}}{2+\cfrac{9^{2}}{2+\cfrac{11^{2}}{2+\cdots}}}}}

By (
21
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8x}\frac{d}{dx}\bigg(\frac{\tan x}{8x}+\frac{1}{4x^{2}-\pi^{2}}\bigg)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\big(((2k+1)\pi)^{2}-4x^{2}\big)^{2}},

\displaystyle\frac{1}{16x}\frac{d}{dx}\frac{1}{8x}\frac{d}{dx}\bigg(\frac{\tan x}{8x}+\frac{1}{4x^{2}-\pi^{2}}\bigg)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\big(((2k+1)\pi)^{2}-4x^{2}\big)^{3}}.

Continuing this process, we have

(28)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{((2k+1)\pi)^{2}-4x^{2}}\bigg)^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8nx}\frac{d}{dx}\frac{1}{8(n-1)x}\frac{d}{dx}\cdots\frac{1}{16x}\frac{d}{dx}\frac{1}{8x}\frac{d}{dx}\bigg(\frac{\tan x}{8x}+\frac{1}{4x^{2}-\pi^{2}}\bigg),

where
n
is a positive integer.

From (
28
), we note that

\displaystyle\bigg[\frac{1}{8nx}\frac{d}{dx}\frac{1}{8(n-1)x}\frac{d}{dx}\cdots\frac{1}{16x}\frac{d}{dx}\frac{1}{8x}\frac{d}{dx}\bigg(\frac{\tan x}{8x}+\frac{1}{4x^{2}-\pi^{2}}\bigg)\bigg]_{x=\frac{\pi}{4}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4^{n+1}}{\pi^{2n+2}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{(4k+1)(4k+3)}\bigg)^{n+1}.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{((2k+1)\pi)^{2}-4x^{2}}\bigg)^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8nx}\frac{d}{dx}\frac{1}{8(n-1)x}\frac{d}{dx}\cdots\frac{1}{16x}\frac{d}{dx}\frac{1}{8x}\frac{d}{dx}\bigg(\frac{\tan x}{8x}+\frac{1}{4x^{2}-\pi^{2}}\bigg).

By (
20
), we get

(29)

\displaystyle\tan x

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{8x}{\big((2k+1)\pi\big)^{2}-4x^{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{8x}{\big((2k+1)\pi-2x)((2k-1)\pi+2x)}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{(2k+1)\pi-2x}-\frac{1}{(2k-1)\pi+2x}\bigg).

From (
29
), we have

(30)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{2n-1}}\frac{1}{(2n-2)!}\frac{d^{2n-2}}{dx^{2n-2}}\tan x

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{\big((2k+1)\pi-2x\big)^{2n-1}}-\frac{1}{\big((2k+1)\pi+2x\big)^{2n-1}}\bigg),

where
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Taking
n=1
and
x=\frac{\pi}{4}
in (
30
), we get

\frac{\pi}{4}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{4k+1}-\frac{1}{4k+3}\bigg)=1-\frac{1}{3}+\frac{1}{5}-\frac{1}{7}+\frac{1}{9}-\frac{1}{11}+\cdots,

which agrees with the result in (
26
).

Letting
x=\frac{\pi}{4}
in (
30
), we have

(31)

\frac{1}{2^{2n-1}}\frac{1}{(2n-2)!}\bigg[\frac{d^{2n-2}}{dx^{2n-2}}\tan x\bigg]_{x=\frac{\pi}{4}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{(4k+1)^{2n-1}}-\frac{1}{(4k+3)^{2n-1}}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{2}{\pi}\bigg)^{2n-1}.

Thus, by (
31
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
n\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\frac{\pi}{4}\bigg)^{2n-1}\frac{1}{(2n-2)!}\bigg[\frac{d^{2n-2}}{dx^{2n-2}}\tan x\bigg]_{x=\frac{\pi}{4}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(4k+1)^{2n-1}}-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(4k+3)^{2n-1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4^{2n-1}}\Big(\zeta\Big(2n-1,\frac{1}{4}\Big)-\zeta\Big(2n-1,\frac{3}{4}\Big)\Big),

where
\zeta(s,x)
is the Hurwitz zeta function given by

\zeta(s,x)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(n+x)^{s}},\quad s\in\mathbb{C}\ \ \mathrm{with}\quad\mathrm{Re}(s)>1.

By (
20
), we get

(32)

\displaystyle\sec^{2}x

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dx}\tan x=8\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\big((2k+1)\pi\big)^{2}-(2x)^{2}}+8x\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{8x}{\big((2k+1)\pi\big)^{2}-(2x)^{2}\big)^{2}}

\displaystyle=8\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\big((2k+1)\pi\big)^{2}+4x^{2}}{\big(\big((2k+1)\pi\big)^{2}-4x^{2}\big)^{2}}.

From (
32
), we note that

(33)

\frac{1}{8}\sec^{2}x=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2k+1)^{2}\pi^{2}+4x^{2}}{\big(4x^{2}-(2k+1)^{2}\pi^{2}\big)^{2}}.

Let
x=\frac{\pi}{2n+1},\ (n\in\mathbb{Z})
in (
33
). Then we have

(34)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8}\sec^{2}\bigg(\frac{\pi}{2n+1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2k+1)^{2}\pi^{2}+4\big(\frac{\pi}{2n+1}\big)^{2}}{\big(4\big(\frac{\pi}{2n+1}\big)^{2}-(2k+1)^{2}\pi^{2}\big)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{\big(4+(2n+1)^{2}(2k+1)^{2}\big)\big(\frac{\pi}{2n+1}\big)^{2}}{\big(4-(2n+1)^{2}(2k+1)^{2}\big)^{2}\big(\frac{\pi}{2n+1}\big)^{4}}.

Thus, by (
34
), we get the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\pi^{2}=8(2n+1)^{2}\cos^{2}\bigg(\frac{\pi}{2n+1}\bigg)\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{4+(2n+1)^{2}(2k+1)^{2}}{\big(4-(2n+1)^{2}(2k+1)^{2}\big)^{2}}.

Let
n=0
in Theorem 2.5. Then we have

(35)

\pi^{2}=8\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{4+(2k+1)^{2}}{\big(4-(2k+1)^{2}\big)^{2}}.

Thus, by (
35
), we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.5
.

\frac{\pi}{2}=\sqrt{2\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{4+(2k+1)^{2}}{\big(4-(2k+1)^{2}\big)^{2}}}.

From (
19
), we note that

(36)

\cos ix=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(1+\frac{4x^{2}}{(2k+1)^{2}\pi^{2}}\bigg).

As
\cos ix=\cosh x
, from (
36
) we have

(37)

\cosh x=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(1+\bigg(\frac{2x}{(2k+1)\pi}\bigg)^{2}\bigg).

By (
19
) and (
37
), we get

(38)

\displaystyle\cosh x\cos x

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(1+\bigg(\frac{2x}{(2k+1)\pi}\bigg)^{2}\bigg)\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{2x}{(2k+1)\pi}\bigg)^{2}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{2x}{(2k+1)\pi}\bigg)^{4}\bigg).

From (
38
), we have

(39)

\displaystyle\tanh x-\tan x

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dx}\Big(\log\cosh x+\log\cos x\Big)=\frac{d}{dx}\log\Big(\cosh x\cos x\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dx}\log\bigg(\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{2x}{(2k+1)\pi}\bigg)^{4}\bigg)=\frac{d}{dx}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\log\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{2x}{(2k+1)\pi}\bigg)^{4}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dx}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg[\log\bigg(1+\bigg(\frac{2x}{(2k+1)\pi}\bigg)^{2}\bigg)+\log\bigg(1-\bigg(\frac{2x}{(2k+1)\pi}\bigg)^{2}\bigg)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\cfrac{\frac{8x}{\big((2k+1)\pi\big)^{2}}}{1+\big(\frac{2x}{(2k+1)\pi}\big)^{2}}-\cfrac{\frac{8x}{\big((2k+1)\pi\big)^{2}}}{1-\big(\frac{2x}{(2k+1)\pi}\big)^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle=8x\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{(2k+1)^{2}\pi^{2}+4x^{2}}-\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(2k+1)^{2}\pi^{2}-4x^{2}}\bigg).

Thus, by (
39
), we get

(40)

\frac{\tanh x-\tan x}{8x}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{(2k+1)^{2}\pi^{2}+4x^{2}}-\frac{1}{(2k+1)^{2}\pi^{2}-4x^{2}}\bigg).

Letting
x=\frac{\pi}{2(2n+1)},\ (n\geq 1)
in (
40
), we have

\displaystyle\cfrac{\tanh\big(\frac{\pi}{2(2n+1)}\big)-\tan\big(\frac{\pi}{2(2n+1)}\big)}{\frac{4\pi}{2n+1}}

(41)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{(2k+1)^{2}\pi^{2}+\big(\frac{\pi}{2n+1}\big)^{2}}-\frac{1}{(2k+1)^{2}\pi^{2}-\big(\frac{\pi}{2n+1}\big)^{2}}\bigg).

Thus, by (
41
), we get

(42)

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{4(2n+1)}\bigg[\tanh\bigg(\frac{\pi}{2(2n+1)}\bigg)-\tan\bigg(\frac{\pi}{2(2n+1)}\bigg)\bigg]

3.
Conclusion

In this paper, we demonstrated that a series for
\tan x
, which gives a product expansion of the cosine function, is very useful in deriving various results. Indeed, by using this series we obtained some continued fractions, certain identities involving derivatives of

, several expressions for

Especially, we obtained two continued fractions of

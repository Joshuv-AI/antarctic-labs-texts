Convergence of Natural p p -Means for the p p -Laplacian in the Heisenberg group
András Domokos, Juan J. Manfredi, Diego Ricciotti, Bianca Stroffolini

Abstract
. In this paper we prove uniform convergence of approximations to p p -harmonic functions by using natural p p -mean operators on bounded domains of the Heisenberg group ℍ \mathbb{H} which satisfy an intrinsic exterior corkscrew condition. These domains include Euclidean C 1 , 1 C^{1,1} domains.

1.
Introduction

Solutions to a large class of elliptic and parabolic equations can be characterized by asymptotic mean value properties (see for example
[
MPR10
,
BCMR21
]
). Consider the case of
p
-harmonic functions for
1<p<\infty
. A smooth function with non-vanishing gradient satisfies at a point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the
p
-harmonic equation

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\{\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{u_{x_{i}}(x)u_{x_{j}}(x)}{|\nabla u(x)|^{2}}\right\}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}(x)=0,

if and only if

(1.1)

u(x)=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\sup_{B_{{\varepsilon}}(x)}u+\inf_{B_{{\varepsilon}}(x)}u\right)+\beta\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{{\varepsilon}}(x)}u(y)\,dy+o({\varepsilon}^{2}),

where
\alpha=\frac{p-2}{n+p}
and
\beta=\frac{n+2}{n+p}
.
For general viscosity solutions the same characterization prevails provided that we interpret (
1.1
) in the viscosity sense.

The expansion (
1.1
) suggests the consideration of solutions
u_{\varepsilon}
to the Dynamic Programming Principle (DPP)

(1.2)

u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\sup_{B_{{\varepsilon}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}+\inf_{B_{{\varepsilon}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}\right)+\beta\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{{\varepsilon}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy,

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain, the function
u_{\varepsilon}\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
and
B_{\varepsilon}(x)\subset\Omega
. Suppose that
g\colon\partial\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous. In order to consider (
1.2
) for all points
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and for all
0<{\varepsilon}\leq 1
, consider the
{\varepsilon}
-boundary strip

\Gamma_{{\varepsilon}}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega\,\colon\,\text{dist}(x,\Omega)\leq{\varepsilon}\}.

We extend the function
g
continuously to a function
G
to this boundary strip and consider the problem

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{*3{>{\displaystyle}c}l}u_{\varepsilon}(x)&=&\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\sup_{B_{{\varepsilon}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}+\inf_{B_{{\varepsilon}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}\right)+\beta\,\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{B_{{\varepsilon}}(x)}u_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy,&x\in{\Omega},\\
u_{\varepsilon}(x)&=&G(x),&{x\in\Gamma_{{\varepsilon}}}.\end{array}\right.

Since we are requiring that
u_{\varepsilon}=G
in
\Gamma_{\varepsilon}
, the expression (
1.2
) is now well defined for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.
When the domain
\Omega
is Lipschitz, one can solve the Dirichlet problem (
1.3
) and obtain a family
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{0<{\varepsilon}\leq 1}
of functions such that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
uniformly in
\overline{\Omega}
, where
u
is the unique viscosity solution to the Dirichlet problem

(1.4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cccl}\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\{\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{u_{x_{i}}u_{x_{j}}}{|\nabla u|^{2}}\right\}u_{x_{i}x_{j}}&=&0,&\text{ in }{\Omega}\\
u&=&g,&\text{ in }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

We note that viscosity solutions of the problem (
1.4
) are also weak solutions, when the homogeneous
p
-Laplacian is replaced by the variational

p
-Laplacian,
\operatorname{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=0
,
[
JLM01
]
. For a mean value property that applies directly to the variational
p
-Laplacian see
[
dTL21
]
.

The nonlinear mean value expression in the right-hand side of (
1.2
) is a
tug-of-war with noise
mean, suggested by connections with probability developed in
[
PSSW09
]
and
[
PS08
]
.

Let
\Omega_{E}=\Omega\cup\Gamma_{1}
and
{\mathcal{B}}(\overline{\Omega})
,
{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega_{E})
be the class of bounded real measurable functions defined on
\overline{\Omega}
and

\Omega_{E}
, respectively.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that an operator
A:{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega_{E})\to{\mathcal{B}}(\overline{\Omega})

is an average operator if it satisfies the following properties:

•

(Stability)
\inf_{\{y\in\Omega_{E}\}}\phi(y)\leq A[\phi](x)\leq\sup_{\{y\in\Omega_{E}\}}\phi(y),\forall x\in\overline{\Omega}
;

•

(Monotonicity)If
\phi\leq\psi
in
\Omega_{E}
then
A[\phi]\leq A[\psi]
in
\overline{\Omega}
;

•

(Affine invariance)
A[\lambda\phi+\xi]=\lambda A[\phi]+\xi,\forall\lambda>0,\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}
.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that a family of averages
\{A_{\varepsilon}\}_{{\varepsilon}>0}
satisfies the
asymptotic mean value property (AMVP)
for the
p
-Laplacian if for every
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\Omega_{E})
such that
\nabla\phi\not=0
, we have

A_{\varepsilon}[\phi](x)=\phi(x)+c\,{\varepsilon}^{2}\left(\Delta_{p}^{H}\phi(x)\right)+o({\varepsilon}^{2})

for some constant
c>0
independent of
{\varepsilon}
and
\phi
, and where the constant in
o({\varepsilon}^{2})
can be taken uniformly for all
x\in\overline{\Omega}
.

Associated to an average operator
A_{\varepsilon}
, we have a dynamic programming principle (DPP) at scale
{\varepsilon}
given by

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cccl}u_{\varepsilon}(x)&=&A_{\varepsilon}[u_{\varepsilon}](x)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{\varepsilon}(x)&=&G(x)&\text{ on }{\Gamma_{1}}.\end{array}\right.

Later in Section §
2
we will discuss existence and uniqueness for the DPP (
1.5
) associated to the natural
p
-means in the Heisenberg group.

We will say that a function
u\in{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega_{E})
is a subsolution (resp. supersolution) of (
1.5
) in
\Omega
with boundary datum
G
, if
u\leq G
(resp.
u\geq G
) on
\Gamma_{1}
and
u(x)\leq A_{{\varepsilon}}[u](x)
(resp.
u(x)\geq A_{{\varepsilon}}[u](x)
) for
x\in\Omega
.

Consider the following conditions on the family of averages
\{A_{\varepsilon}\}_{{\varepsilon}>0}
:

Uniform Stability:

(US)

\begin{split}&\textup{For all ${\varepsilon}>0$ there exists $u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{E})$, a solution of \eqref{eq:expDPP1}
with a}\\
&\textup{bound on $\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{E})}$ uniform in ${\varepsilon}$.}\end{split}

Uniform Boundedness:

(UB)

\begin{split}&\textup{For all ${\varepsilon}>0$ there exists $u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{E})$, a solution of \eqref{eq:expDPP1}, and }\\
&\inf_{\Gamma_{1}}G\leq u_{\varepsilon}(x)\leq\sup_{\Gamma_{1}}{G}\quad\textup{for all}\quad x\in\Omega.\end{split}

Comparison Principle:

(CP)

\begin{split}&\textup{Let $u^{1}_{\varepsilon}$ and $u^{2}_{\varepsilon}$ be a subsolution and a supersolution of \eqref{eq:expDPP1} }\\
&\textup{with boundary data $G_{1}$ and $G_{2}$ respectively.}\\
&\textup{If $G_{1}\leq G_{2}$ on $\Gamma_{1}$, then $u_{\varepsilon}^{1}\leq u_{\varepsilon}^{2}$ in $\Omega_{E}$.}\end{split}

Theorem 1.3

(Convergence of general mean approximations in the Euclidean case,
1<p\leq\infty
,
[
dTMP20
]
)
.

Let

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain and
g\in C(\partial\Omega)
. Let the family of averages
\{A_{\varepsilon}\}_{{\varepsilon}>0}
satisfy the
AMVP
with respect to the
p
-Laplacian. Let also
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{{\varepsilon}>0}
be a sequence of solutions of the corresponding DPP (
1.5
), where
G
is a continuous extension of
g
to
\Gamma_{1}
. Then, we have that

u_{\varepsilon}\to u\text{ uniformly in }\overline{\Omega}\text{ as }{\varepsilon}\to 0,

•

when the domain
\Omega
is of class
C^{2}
and the family of averages
\{A_{\varepsilon}\}_{{\varepsilon}>0}
satisfies (
US
), or

•

when the domain
\Omega
is Lipschitz and the family of averages
\{A_{\varepsilon}\}_{{\varepsilon}>0}
satisfies the uniform boundedness (
UB
) and the comparison principle
(
CP
) properties,

where
u
is the unique solution of the Dirichlet problem

(1.6)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cccl}-\Delta_{p}u&=&0&\text{ in }\Omega\\
u&=&g&\text{ on }\partial{\Omega},\end{array}\right.

The proof of this theorem for
C^{2}
domains is based on an extension of the method in
[
BS91
]
, once we have the
strong uniqueness theorem
for the
p
-Laplacian (Proposition 3.2 in
[
dTMP20
]
). The key observation is that the convergence of approximations that satisfy the asymptotic mean value property, the
uniform boundedness property (
UB
), and the comparison principle (
CP
) depends only on the strong uniqueness principle for the limit operator, which is the
p
-Laplacian in this case.

To apply the Barles-Souganidis method in smooth domains of the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}
, we need to establish the
strong uniqueness principle
for the homogeneous
p
-Laplacian in the Heisenberg group

\Delta^{N}_{\mathbb{H},p}
. In
[
MS21
]
we were able to establish it only when the domain
\Omega=B
, the Euclidean unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Thus, we concluded the convergence of general mean approximations only in this case.

For more general domains in
\mathbb{H}
an obstruction to the application of this method is the presence of characteristic points.
In the current paper, we pursue a different approach that does not rely on the strong uniqueness principle.
First, we use good properties of the fundamental solution of the
p
-Laplacian to establish convergence in smooth ring domains with
p
-harmonic boundary data. Second, we set up a boundary iteration suggested by the proof of sufficiency for the Wiener condition of boundary regularity. These steps are independent of each other. We will establish them for the case of the natural
p
-means in the Heisenberg group.

The notion of natural
p
-means in general topological measure spaces was introduced in
[
IMW17
]
.
Let
X
be a compact topological measure space endowed with a positive Radon measure
\nu
. Given a function
u\in L^{p}(X)
and
1<p\leq\infty
, there exists a unique real value
\mu_{p}^{X}(u)
such that

(1.7)

\|u-\mu_{p}^{X}(u)\|_{p}=\min_{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}}\|u-\lambda\|_{p}\,.

We will call
\mu_{p}^{X}(u)
the natural
p
-mean of
u
in
X
. Note that the above definition extends to the case
p=1
, provided that
u
is assumed to be continuous on
X
.
Existence, uniqueness, and several useful properties of the natural
p
-means were studied in
[
IMW17
]
, where the AMVP for the natural
p
-means is established in the Euclidean case.

While for general
p
there is no explicit formula for
\mu_{p}^{X}(u)
, for the cases
p=1,2
, and
p=\infty
we have:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\mu_{1}(u)&=&\text{med}(u),\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\mu_{2}(u)&=&\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{X}u(y)d\nu,\text{ and}\\
\mu_{\infty}(u)&=&\frac{1}{2}(\operatorname{ess\,inf}_{y\in X}u(y)+\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{y\in X}u(y)).\end{array}

Consider the family of natural
p
-mean operators
\{\mu_{p}(u,{\varepsilon})\}_{0<{\varepsilon}<1}
defined on functions

u\in{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega_{E})
as follows. For
x\in\overline{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
B_{{\varepsilon}}(x)
the Euclidean ball of radius
{\varepsilon}
centered at
x
, we set

(1.8)

\mu_{p}(u,{\varepsilon})(x)=\mu^{\overline{B_{{\varepsilon}}(x)}}_{p}(u).

Observe that for any
u\in L^{p}(\Omega_{E})

the function

is continuous in
\overline{\Omega}
. This property
is not shared by the tug-of-war means of type (
1.2

We can now rephrase Theorem 3.2 in
[
IMW17
]
:

Theorem 1.4
.

The family of natural p-mean operators

\mu_{p}(\phi,{\varepsilon})(x)=\phi(x)+\frac{{\varepsilon}^{2}}{2(n+p)}\Delta_{p}^{H}\phi(x)+o({\varepsilon}^{2}),\;\text{as}\;{\varepsilon}\to 0,

where the constant in

can be taken uniformly for all

.

In a recent paper, Chandra, Ishiwata, Magnanini, and Wadade
[
CIMW21
]
have also proved the convergence of the natural

-means in the Euclidean case. Their approach and our approach differ in the treatment at the boundary, but the main results are essentially the same in the case of

Recall that for the first Heisenberg group,
\mathbb{H}=(\mathbb{R}^{3},*)
, the group operation is given as

(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})*(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})=\left(x_{1}+y_{1},x_{2}+y_{2},x_{3}+y_{3}+\frac{1}{2}(x_{1}y_{2}-x_{2}y_{1})\right).

The vector fields

X_{1}=\partial_{x_{1}}-\frac{x_{2}}{2}\partial_{x_{3}},\quad X_{2}=\partial_{x_{2}}+\frac{x_{1}}{2}\partial_{x_{3}}\quad\text{and}\quad T=\partial_{x_{3}}

form a basis of the associated Lie algebra. We denote the horizontal gradient of a smooth function
u
by
\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}{u}=(X_{1}u)X_{1}+(X_{2}u)X_{2}
,
the horizontal Laplacian by

\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u=X_{1}^{2}u+X_{2}^{2}u,

the horizontal
p
-Laplacian by

\Delta_{\mathbb{H},p}u=X_{1}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}{u}|^{p-2}X_{1}u)+X_{2}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}{u}|^{p-2}X_{2}u),

and the normalized horizontal
\infty
-Laplacian by

\Delta_{\mathbb{H},\infty}u=\langle D^{2,*}_{\mathbb{H}}u\,\frac{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}{u}}{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}{u}|}\,,\,\frac{\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}{u}}{|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}{u}|}\rangle,

where
D^{2,*}_{\mathbb{H}}u
denotes the symmetrized Hessian
(D^{2,*}_{\mathbb{H}}u)_{ij}=(X_{i}X_{j}+X_{j}X_{i})/2
.
For a smooth function
u
, with non-vanishing horizontal gradient, we define the normalized
p
-Laplacian as

\Delta^{N}_{\mathbb{H},p}u=|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}{u}|^{2-p}\Delta_{\mathbb{H},p}u=(p-2)\Delta_{\mathbb{H},\infty}u+\Delta_{\mathbb{H}}u\,.

The Korányi smooth gauge, given by

|x|_{\mathbb{H}}=|(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})|_{\mathbb{H}}=\left((x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2})^{2}+16x_{3}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}},

induces the left-invariant metric

d_{\mathbb{H}}(x,y)=|x^{-1}*y|_{\mathbb{H}}.

The Korányi ball with center
x_{0}
and radius
r
will be denoted by

B^{\mathbb{H}}_{r}(x_{0})=\{x\in\mathbb{H}\,|\,d_{\mathbb{H}}(x_{0},x)<r\}.

The Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}
is unimodular and its Haar measure is the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
We also have a family of anisotropic dilations
(\rho_{\lambda})_{\lambda>0}
, that are group isomorphisms

(1.10)

\rho_{\lambda}(x)=\rho_{\lambda}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})=(\lambda x_{1},\lambda x_{2},\lambda^{2}x_{3}).

The homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{H}
is
Q=4
.

The natural
p
-means in the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}
were studied in
[
MS21
]
.
Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}
is a bounded domain. Fix

{\varepsilon}

\Gamma_{{\varepsilon}}^{\mathbb{H}}=\{x\in\mathbb{H}\setminus\Omega\,\colon\,\text{dist}_{\mathbb{H}}(x,\Omega)\leq{\varepsilon}\}.

Define

\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{E})
and

the set of real-valued bounded Lebesgue measurable functions on
\Omega_{E}

We define the natural

\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{p}(\,\cdot\,,{\varepsilon}):\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{E})\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}(\overline{\Omega}),

given by

\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{p}(\phi,{\varepsilon})(y)=\mu^{\overline{B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}(y)}}_{p}(\phi)\quad\text{for all}\,\,\phi\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{E})\,\,\text{and}\,\,y\in\overline{\Omega},

To consider more general domains, we define the following boundary regularity condition.

Definition 1.10

We say that a domain

satisfies the exterior
\mathbb{H}
-corkscrew condition if
there exists
\bar{\delta}>0
and
\mu\in(0,1)
such that for every
\delta\in(0,\bar{\delta})
and
y\in\partial\Omega
there exists a ball
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu\delta}(z)
strictly contained in
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta}(y)\setminus\Omega
.

It is known that domains with
C^{1,1}
boundary in the Euclidean sense satisfy the exterior
\mathbb{H}
-corkscrew condition (see
[
CG98
]
, Theorem 14 for domains in the Heisenberg group and
[
MM05
]
, Theorem 1.3 for the more general case of domains in step 2 Carnot groups). This regularity is optimal in the sense that for every
\alpha\in[0,1)
there exist domains with
C^{1,\alpha}
boundary in the Euclidean sense that do not satisfy the condition in Definition
1.10

(see Example 8.2 in
[
LMR20
]
).

For domains satisfying the exterior
\mathbb{H}
-corkscrew condition we first prove the following boundary estimate.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
\Omega
be an open, bounded subset of
\mathbb{H}
satisfying the exterior
\mathbb{H}
-corkscrew condition in Definition
1.10
and
G\in C(\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{1})
.
For
2\leq p<\infty
let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the solution of (
1.11
) in
\Omega
with boundary value
G
on
\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}
, for
0<{\varepsilon}<1
.
Given
\eta>0
there exist
\delta_{0}=\delta_{0}(\eta,\mu,p)
and
{\varepsilon}_{0}={\varepsilon}_{0}(\eta,\delta,\mu)
such that

|u_{\varepsilon}(x)-G(y)|\leq\eta,

for all
y\in\partial\Omega
,
x\in B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{0}}(y)\cap\Omega
and
{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0}
.

Once we have Theorem
1.11
, we are ready to state our main result.

Theorem 1.12

(For the range
2\leq p<\infty
)
.

Let
\Omega
be an open bounded subset of
\mathbb{H}
satisfying the exterior
\mathbb{H}
-corkscrew condition and let
\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}
be its outer
{\varepsilon}
-boundary, for
0<{\varepsilon}<1
. Let
g\in C(\partial\Omega)
,
G\in C(\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{1})
be a continuous extension of
g
and
u_{\varepsilon}
be the solution of the DPP (
1.11
) with boundary datum
G
. Then
u_{\varepsilon}
converges to
u
uniformly on
\overline{\Omega}
as
{\varepsilon}\to 0
, where
u
is the solution to the Dirichlet problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta^{N}_{\mathbb{H},p}u=0&\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega\\
u=g&\quad\text{in}\quad\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

The plan of the paper is as follows. In Section §2 we present various properties of the natural
p
-means in

\mathbb{H}
. The proofs of the perturbation Lemma
1.7
and of Proposition
1.8
are in Section §3, while the proofs of Lemma
1.7
and Proposition
1.9
are in Section §4. The proof of the key boundary continuity estimate Theorem
1.11
is in Section §5, which is the most technical section of the paper, and the proof of the main result is in Section §6.

The major technical difference between the results in
[
MS21
]
, this article, and
[
CIMW21
]
is that in the latter the DPP is modified at points close to
\partial\Omega
. As a consequence the solution of the DPP exists and is continuous provided that
\Omega
satisfies a regularity condition implied by the exterior sphere property. Thus, we both prove convergence of slightly different approximations to
p
-harmonic functions.
In our case, we get existence of possibly discontinuous solutions of the DPP for a general bounded domain
\Omega
, but need boundary regularity to prove convergence.

Finally, we note that the validity of Proposition
1.8
for
1<p<2
would imply that Theorem
1.12
also holds for
1<p<2
. However, our current proof of Proposition
1.8
requires
p\geq 2
.

2.
Natural
p
-Means in
\mathbb{H}

We now collect several results on the natural
p
-means in
\mathbb{H}
that we will need later. For all
u
,
v\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{E})
,
0<{\varepsilon}\leq 1
and
y\in\Omega
, the following properties hold:

•

Continuity in the
L^{p}
-norm (Theorem 2.4 in
[
IMW17
]
):

\left|\|u-\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{p}(u,{\varepsilon})(y)\|_{L^{p}(B_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{H}}(y))}-\|v-\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{p}(v,{\varepsilon})(y)\|_{L^{p}(B_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{H}}(y))}\right|\leq\|u-v\|_{L^{p}(B_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{H}}(y))}.

In particular, the function
y\mapsto\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{p}(u,{\varepsilon})(y)
is continuous in
\overline{\Omega}
.

•

Monotonicity (Theorem 2.5 in
[
IMW17
]
):

\text{if}\,\,u\leq v\,\,\text{a.e. on}\,\,B_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{H}}(y),\,\,\text{then}\,\,\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{p}(u,{\varepsilon})(y)\leq\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{p}(v,{\varepsilon})(y).

•

Affine invariance (Proposition 2.7 in
[
IMW17
]
): for
c,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
it holds

\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{p}(\alpha u+c,{\varepsilon})(y)=\alpha\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{p}(u,{\varepsilon})(y)+c.

•

Rescaling (Corollary 2.3 in
[
IMW17
]
): Let
x\in\Omega
. Defining
u_{{\varepsilon},x}(z)=u(x+{\varepsilon}z)
for
z\in B^{\mathbb{H}}_{1}(0)
, we have

\mu_{p}(u,{\varepsilon})(x)=\mu_{p}(u_{{\varepsilon},x},1)(0).

•

Integral characterization (Theorem 2.1 in
[
IMW17
]
): For
1<p<\infty
,
\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{p}(u,{\varepsilon})(y)
is the unique solution of

\int_{B_{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{H}}(y)}\left\lvert u(z)-\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{p}(u,{\varepsilon})(y)\right\rvert^{p-2}\left(u(z)-\mu^{\mathbb{H}}_{p}(u,{\varepsilon})(y)\right)\,\mbox{d}z=0,

with the convention that
0^{p-2}0=0
if
1<p<2
.

All the properties above extend to the case
p=1
if we assume that
u
and
v
are continuous on
\Omega_{E}
.

Existence and uniqueness of the solution of the DPP (
1.11
) can be proved as in the Euclidean case. Solutions are automatically continuous in the interior (see Lemma 5.6 and Theorem 3.4 in
[
MS21
]
):

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1<p\leq\infty
. There exists a unique
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{B}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
that satisfies (
1.11
). Moreover, we have that
u_{\varepsilon}\in C(\Omega)
.

Note, however, that
u_{\varepsilon}
might not be continuous on
\overline{\Omega}
.

Next, we provide a version of the comparison principle which will be needed later. This is a slight modification of Theorem 4.3 in
[
MS21
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega_{{\varepsilon}})
be a subsolution of (
1.11
) in
\Omega
with boundary datum
F
, and
v_{\varepsilon}\in{\mathcal{B}}(\Omega_{{\varepsilon}})
be a supersolution of (
1.11
) in
\Omega
with boundary datum
G
. Then

u_{\varepsilon}\leq v_{\varepsilon}+\sup_{\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}}(F-G)\quad\text{on}\quad\Omega_{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Let
\phi_{\varepsilon}=u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}
be defined on
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. Note that
\phi_{\varepsilon}\leq F-G
on
\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}
and therefore
\displaystyle{\sup_{\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}\leq\sup_{\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}}(F-G)}
. Assume by contradiction that

M_{\varepsilon}:=\sup_{\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}>\sup_{\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}}(F-G).

Note that this implies
\sup_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}\phi_{\varepsilon}=\sup_{\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}=M_{\varepsilon}
.
By definition of
M_{\varepsilon}
, there exists a sequence
x_{n}\in\Omega
such that
\phi_{\varepsilon}(x_{n})
converges to
M_{\varepsilon}
. We can assume that, up to a subsequence,
x_{n}
converges to some
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
. Passing to the limit in the inequality

\phi_{\varepsilon}(x_{n})\leq\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(u_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{n})-\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(v_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{n})\,,

we obtain

M_{\varepsilon}\leq\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(u_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0})-\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(v_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0}),

because the function
x\to\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(f,{\varepsilon})(x)
is continuous in
\overline{\Omega}
for
f\in L^{p}(\Omega_{\varepsilon})
. Therefore,

(2.1)

\begin{split}\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(u_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0})&\geq\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(v_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0})+M_{\varepsilon}\\
&=\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(v_{\varepsilon}+M_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0}).\end{split}

Using the notation
h(u,\lambda)=|u-\lambda|^{p-2}(u-\lambda)
, we have that
h
is increasing in
u
for fixed
\lambda
, and decreasing in
\lambda
for fixed
u
. Therefore, in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, we have

\begin{split}h\left(u_{\varepsilon},\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(u_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0})\right)&\leq h\left(u_{\varepsilon},\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(v_{\varepsilon}+M_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0})\right)\\
&\leq h\left(v_{\varepsilon}+M_{\varepsilon},\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(v_{\varepsilon}+M_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0})\right),\end{split}

because
u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\leq M_{\varepsilon}
on
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. We can rewrite the previous inequality as

h\left(u_{\varepsilon},\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(u_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0})\right)-h\left(v_{\varepsilon}+M_{\varepsilon},\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(v_{\varepsilon}+M_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0})\right)\leq 0

in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
. The integral characterization of the natural
p
-means implies that

\int_{B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})}h\left(u_{\varepsilon},\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(u_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0})\right)-h\left(v_{\varepsilon}+M_{\varepsilon},\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(v_{\varepsilon}+M_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0})\right)=0,

and therefore

h\left(u_{\varepsilon},\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(u_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0})\right)-h\left(v_{\varepsilon}+M_{\varepsilon},\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(v_{\varepsilon}+M_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0})\right)=0\quad\text{a.e. in}\,\,B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}).

Since
s\to|s|^{p-2}s
is injective, we obtain

u_{\varepsilon}-\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(u_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0})=v_{\varepsilon}+M_{\varepsilon}-\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(v_{\varepsilon}+M_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0})=0\quad\text{a.e. in}\,\,B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}),

which rewrites as

\begin{split}u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}&=M_{\varepsilon}+\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(u_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0})-\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(v_{\varepsilon}+M_{\varepsilon},{\varepsilon})(x_{0}).\end{split}

Recalling (
2.1
), we get

u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\geq M_{\varepsilon}=\sup_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}}(u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})
a.e. in
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})
, hence

u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}=M_{\varepsilon}\quad\text{a.e. in}\,\,B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x_{0}).

The previous argument shows that if
x\in\bar{\Omega}
is such that
\phi_{\varepsilon}(x)=M_{\varepsilon}
, then
\phi_{\varepsilon}=M_{\varepsilon}
a.e. in
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x)
. Since
\Omega
is bounded and connected, it is possible to find a finite chain of balls
\{B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x_{i})\}_{i=0}^{N}
starting from
x_{0}
, such that
x_{i+1}
is a point in
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x_{i})
with
\phi_{\varepsilon}(x_{i+1})=M_{\varepsilon}
for all
i=0,...,N-1
and
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x_{N})\cap\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}\neq\emptyset
. This means that
\phi_{\varepsilon}=M_{\varepsilon}=\sup_{\Omega}\phi_{\varepsilon}
a.e. on
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}(x_{N})\cap\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}
, contradicting
\phi_{\varepsilon}\leq F-G
on
\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}
.
∎

3.
Taylor Expansions

The goal of this section is to prove Proposition
1.8
.

First, we include details of the proof of Lemma
1.6
in the
relevant case for our analysis
p>2
, because similar arguments will be used later to prove the stronger version Proposition
1.8
.
We pay particular attention to the dependence of the estimate on
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
.

Proof.

Let
p>2
and denote
h(s)=|s|^{p-2}s
.
Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
0<{\varepsilon}<1
. Define
u_{{\varepsilon},x_{0}}(z)=u(x_{0}*\rho_{\varepsilon}(z))
for all
z=(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3})=(z_{h},z_{3})\in B
, where
B=B_{1}^{\mathbb{H}}(0)
denotes the Korányi ball centered at the origin with radius
1
. The Heisenberg Taylor expansion gives

u_{{\varepsilon},x_{0}}(z)=u(x_{0})+{\varepsilon}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0}),z_{h}\rangle+{\varepsilon}^{2}Tu(x_{0})z_{3}+\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}^{2}\langle D^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0})z_{h},z_{h}\rangle+O({\varepsilon}^{3}),

where the constant in
O({\varepsilon}^{3})
is uniform in
x_{0}\in\Omega
and depends on the
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
norm of the second and third derivatives of
u
.
Therefore, by the affine invariance and monotonicity of the natural means, it is enough to show the expansion for the quadratic function

q_{{\varepsilon},x_{0}}(z)=u(x_{0})+{\varepsilon}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0}),z_{h}\rangle+{\varepsilon}^{2}Tu(x_{0})z_{3}+\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}^{2}\langle D^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0})z_{h},z_{h}\rangle.

1.
First order term.
Let

v_{{\varepsilon},x_{0}}(z)=\frac{q_{{\varepsilon},x_{0}}(z)-q_{{\varepsilon},x_{0}}(0)}{{\varepsilon}}=\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0}),z_{h}\rangle+{\varepsilon}Tu(x_{0})z_{3}+\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}\langle D^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0})z_{h},z_{h}\rangle\,,

and note that
v_{{\varepsilon},x_{0}}
converges uniformly on
B
to the function
v_{x_{0}}(z)=\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0}),z_{h}\rangle
as
{\varepsilon}\to 0
. As a consequence,

\frac{\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(q_{{\varepsilon},x_{0}},1)(0)-q_{{\varepsilon},x_{0}}(0)}{{\varepsilon}}=\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(v_{{\varepsilon},x_{0}},1)(0)\longrightarrow\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(v_{x_{0}},1)(0)=0,

where the last equality is due to

\int_{B}h(v_{x_{0}}(z))\,\mbox{d}z=\int_{B}|\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0}),z_{h}\rangle|^{p-2}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0}),z_{h}\rangle\,\mbox{d}z=0,

which holds by symmetry of the integrand on
B
.

2.
Second order term.
Let

\delta_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})=\frac{\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(q_{{\varepsilon},x_{0}},1)(0)-q_{{\varepsilon},x_{0}}(0)}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}=\frac{\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(v_{{\varepsilon},x_{0}},1)(0)}{{\varepsilon}}.

By the integral characterization of the natural means we have

(3.1)

\begin{split}0&=\int_{B}h(v_{{\varepsilon},x_{0}}(z)-\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(v_{{\varepsilon},x_{0}},1))\,\mbox{d}z\\
&=\int_{B}h\left(\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0}),z_{h}\rangle+{\varepsilon}Tu(x_{0})z_{3}+\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}\langle D^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0})z_{h},z_{h}\rangle-{\varepsilon}\delta_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\right)\,\mbox{d}z\\
&=\int_{B}h(v_{x_{0}}(z))\,\mbox{d}z+{\varepsilon}\int_{B}\left(\int_{0}^{1}h^{\prime}(F(z,x_{0},{\varepsilon},t))\,\mbox{d}t\right)\psi(z,x_{0},{\varepsilon})\,\mbox{d}z,\end{split}

where we used a first order Taylor expansion and denoted

(3.2)

\psi(z,x_{0},{\varepsilon})=Tu(x_{0})z_{3}+\frac{1}{2}\langle D^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0})z_{h},z_{h}\rangle-\delta_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})

and

F(z,x_{0},{\varepsilon},t)=\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0}),z_{h}\rangle+t{\varepsilon}\psi(z,x_{0},{\varepsilon}).

Manipulating (
3.1
), we can explicitly compute

\delta_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})=\frac{\displaystyle{\int_{B}\int_{0}^{1}h^{\prime}(F(z,x_{0},{\varepsilon},t))\,\mbox{d}t\left(Tu(x_{0})z_{3}+\frac{1}{2}\langle D^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0})z_{h},z_{h}\rangle\right)\,\mbox{d}z}}{\displaystyle{\int_{B}\int_{0}^{1}h^{\prime}(F(z,x_{0},{\varepsilon},t))\,\mbox{d}t\,\mbox{d}z}},

which implies the uniform bound

|\delta_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})|\leq\left|\left|Tu\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left|\left|D^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}u\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}

for all
0<{\varepsilon}<1
and
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
. Since
p>2
, by the dominated convergence theorem, up to a subsequence, we obtain that

\delta_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\longrightarrow\frac{\displaystyle{\int_{B}\left(Tu(x_{0})z_{3}+\frac{1}{2}\langle D^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0})z_{h},z_{h}\rangle\right)h^{\prime}(\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0}),z_{h}\rangle)\,\mbox{d}z}}{\displaystyle{\int_{B}h^{\prime}(\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0}),z_{h}\rangle)\,\mbox{d}z}}=:\delta_{0}(x_{0}).

This integral can be explicitly computed (
[
IMW17
]
,
[
AKPW20
]
) to obtain

\delta_{0}(x_{0})=c_{p}\,\Delta^{N}_{\mathbb{H},p}u(x_{0}).

∎

We show now the proof of Proposition
1.8
.

Proof.

Using the notation in the proof of Lemma
1.6
, it is enough to
show that the quantity

\frac{\delta_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})-\delta_{0}(x_{0})}{{\varepsilon}}

is uniformly bounded in
0<{\varepsilon}<1
and
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
.

To this end, in (
3.1
) use a second order Taylor expansion to get

\begin{split}0&=\int_{B}h^{\prime}(\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0}),z_{h}\rangle)\psi(z,x_{0},{\varepsilon})\,\mbox{d}z\\
&+{\varepsilon}\int_{B}\left(\int_{0}^{1}h^{\prime\prime}(F(z,x_{0},{\varepsilon},t))\,\mbox{d}t\right)\psi^{2}(z,x_{0},{\varepsilon})\,\mbox{d}z.\end{split}

Therefore,

\frac{\delta_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})-\delta_{0}(x_{0})}{{\varepsilon}}=\frac{\displaystyle{\int_{B}\left(\int_{0}^{1}h^{\prime\prime}(F(z,x_{0},{\varepsilon},t))\,\mbox{d}t\right)\psi^{2}(z,x_{0},{\varepsilon})\,\mbox{d}z}}{\displaystyle{\int_{B}h^{\prime}(\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0}),z_{h}\rangle)\,\mbox{d}z}}.

First, we establish a lower bound on the denominator.
Let
R_{x_{0}}
be a rotation in the horizontal plane such that
R_{x_{0}}^{\tau}\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0})=|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0})|(1,0)=(|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0})|,0)
. Observe that
B
is invariant under the change of variables
z=R_{x_{0}}y
, so

\begin{split}\int_{B}h^{\prime}(\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0}),z_{h}\rangle)\,\mbox{d}z&=(p-1)\int_{B}|\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0}),z_{h}\rangle|^{p-2}\,\mbox{d}z\\
&=(p-1)|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0})|^{p-2}\int_{B}|y_{1}|^{p-2}\,\mbox{d}y\\
&\geq c_{p}\min_{\overline{\Omega}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u|^{p-2},\end{split}

because
0<\int_{B}|y_{1}|^{p-2}\,\mbox{d}y<\infty
for
p>2
.

Now we establish an upper bound on the numerator. Note that

\psi^{2}(z,x_{0},{\varepsilon})\leq 4\left(\left|\left|Tu\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left|\left|D^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}u\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)^{2}

for all
0<{\varepsilon}<1
,
z\in B
, and
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
. Since
h^{\prime\prime}(s)=(p-1)(p-2)|s|^{p-4}s
, it is enough to estimate the integral

\begin{split}\int_{B}\int_{0}^{1}\left\lvert\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0}),z_{h}\rangle+t{\varepsilon}\psi(z,x_{0},{\varepsilon})\right\rvert^{p-3}\,\mbox{d}t\,\mbox{d}z.\end{split}

After performing the change of variables
z=R_{x_{0}}y
, where
R_{x_{0}}
is the same rotation as above, the integral becomes

\begin{split}&\int_{B}\int_{0}^{1}\Big\lvert y_{1}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0})|+t{\varepsilon}\psi(R_{x_{0}}y,x_{0},{\varepsilon})\Big\rvert^{p-3}\,\mbox{d}t\,\mbox{d}y\\
&=|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0})|^{p-3}\int_{B}\int_{0}^{1}|y_{1}+t{\varepsilon}\Psi(y,x_{0},{\varepsilon})|^{p-3}\,\mbox{d}t\,\mbox{d}y,\end{split}

where we denoted

\begin{split}\Psi(y,x_{0},{\varepsilon})&=|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0})|^{-1}\psi(R_{x_{0}}y,x_{0},{\varepsilon})\\
&=|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0})|^{-1}\left(Tu(x_{0})y_{3}+\frac{1}{2}\langle R_{x_{0}}^{\tau}D^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}u(x_{0})R_{x_{0}}y_{h},y_{h}\rangle-\delta_{\varepsilon}(x_{0})\right).\end{split}

Observe that

|\Psi(y,x_{0},{\varepsilon})|\leq\frac{2\left|\left|Tu\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left|\left|D^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}u\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{{\min_{\overline{\Omega}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u|}}=c_{\Psi}

for all
y\in B
,
0<{\varepsilon}<1
and
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
.

For fixed
{\varepsilon}
,
t\in[0,1]
and
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
, consider the change of variables
\zeta=\Phi_{{\varepsilon},t,x_{0}}(y)=\Phi_{\varepsilon}(y)
, given by

\begin{cases}\zeta_{1}=y_{1}+{\varepsilon}t\Psi(y,x_{0},{\varepsilon})\\
\zeta_{i}=y_{i}\quad i=2,3\end{cases}

for
y\in B
.

We claim that there exists
\hat{{\varepsilon}}>0
such that for all
0<{\varepsilon}<\hat{{\varepsilon}}
the map
\Phi_{\varepsilon}:B\longrightarrow\Phi_{\varepsilon}(B)
is a diffeomorphism with
\min_{B}\mathcal{J}\Phi_{\varepsilon}\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
More exactly,
\hat{{\varepsilon}}
can be taken to be

\hat{{\varepsilon}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\left|\left|Tu\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left|\left|D^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}u\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{\min_{\overline{\Omega}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u|}+1\right)^{-1}\,,

so it is independent of
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
.
Indeed, the Euclidean Jacobian of the transformation is

\mathcal{J}\Phi_{\varepsilon}(y)=I_{3\times 3}+{\varepsilon}t\begin{pmatrix}\nabla_{y}\Psi(y,x_{0},{\varepsilon})\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix},

which implies

|\mathcal{J}\Phi_{\varepsilon}(y)|=|1+{\varepsilon}t\partial_{y_{1}}\Psi(y,x_{0},{\varepsilon})|\geq\frac{1}{2}

for all
y\in B
,
0<{\varepsilon}<\hat{{\varepsilon}}
and
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
, because

\left|\left|\partial_{y_{1}}\Psi(\cdot,x_{0},{\varepsilon})\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{\left|\left|Tu\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\left|\left|D^{2}_{\mathbb{H}}u\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{\min_{\overline{\Omega}}|\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}u|}.

Moreover, for all
0<{\varepsilon}<\hat{{\varepsilon}}
and
\eta
,
\xi\in B
we have

\begin{split}|\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\xi)-\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\eta)|\geq|\xi-\eta|-{\varepsilon}t|\Psi(\xi,x_{0},{\varepsilon})-\Psi(\eta,x_{0},{\varepsilon})|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\xi-\eta|,\end{split}

because
|\Psi(\xi,x_{0},{\varepsilon})-\Psi(\eta,x_{0},{\varepsilon})|\leq\left|\left|\nabla_{y}\Psi(\cdot,x_{0},{\varepsilon})\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}|\xi-\eta|
. This concludes the proof of the claim.

Now, for
y\in B
,
\zeta\in\Phi_{\varepsilon}(B)
we have
|\zeta_{2}|=|y_{2}|\leq 1
,
|\zeta_{3}|=|y_{3}|\leq 1
and
|\zeta_{1}|=|y_{1}+{\varepsilon}t\Psi(y,x_{0},{\varepsilon})|\leq|y_{1}|+c_{\Psi}{\varepsilon}\leq 1+c_{\Psi}{\varepsilon}
, therefore

\begin{split}\int_{B}|y_{1}+{\varepsilon}t\Psi(y,x_{0},{\varepsilon})|^{p-3}\,\mbox{d}y&=\int_{\Phi_{\varepsilon}(B)}|\zeta_{1}|^{p-3}|\mathcal{J}\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\zeta))|^{-1}\,\mbox{d}\zeta\\
&\leq 2\int_{\Phi_{\varepsilon}(B)}|\zeta_{1}|^{p-3},\mbox{d}\zeta\\
&\leq 2\int_{\{|\zeta_{1}|\leq 1+c_{\Psi}{\varepsilon}\}}|\zeta_{1}|^{p-3}\,\mbox{d}\zeta_{1}\\
&=4(1+c_{B}{\varepsilon})^{p-2},\end{split}

since
p>2
.
∎

4.
Convergence in the case of
p
-harmonic data

In this section we prove Lemma
1.7
and Proposition
1.9
.

In Lemma
1.7
we show that we can obtain supersolutions and subsolutions of the DPP (
1.11
) by small perturbations of
p
-harmonic functions with non-vanishing horizontal gradient. This will be used as a first step towards proving convergence of the approximation scheme to the appropriate solution of the Dirichlet problem for the
p
-Laplace equation, in the case its boundary datum is
p
-harmonic.

First we show the proof of Lemma
1.7
.

Proof.

Up to a left translation, we can assume that
\Omega
does not intersect the cylinder

\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{H}\,\,|\,\,x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}<1\},

so we can choose
q_{0}=0
and
v(x)=|{x}|_{\mathbb{H}}^{s}
.
From the calculations in the proof of Theorem 12.1 in
[
LMR20
]
there exist
s\geq 4
and
\hat{{\varepsilon}}
such that

\Delta^{N}_{\mathbb{H},p}(U+{\varepsilon}v)\geq s{\varepsilon}\quad\text{in}\,\,\Omega

for all
0<{\varepsilon}<\hat{{\varepsilon}}
.
From the expansion in Proposition
1.8
, there exists
C>0
such that

\begin{split}(U+{\varepsilon}v)(x)&\leq\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(U+{\varepsilon}v,{\varepsilon})(x)-c_{p}{\varepsilon}^{2}\Delta^{N}_{\mathbb{H},p}(U+{\varepsilon}v)(x)+C{\varepsilon}^{3}\\
&\leq\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(U+{\varepsilon}v,{\varepsilon})(x)-{\varepsilon}^{3}(c_{p}s-C)\\
&\leq\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(U+{\varepsilon}v,{\varepsilon})(x)\end{split}

for all
x\in\Omega
and
0<{\varepsilon}<\hat{{\varepsilon}}
, provided we choose
s>C/c_{p}
and further restrict
\hat{{\varepsilon}}
.

Analogous computations give

\Delta^{N}_{\mathbb{H},p}(U-{\varepsilon}v)\leq-s{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega
, which, again by Proposition
1.8
, implies
(U+{\varepsilon}v)(x)\leq\mu_{p}^{\mathbb{H}}(U+{\varepsilon}v,{\varepsilon})(x)
for all
x\in\Omega
and an appropriate choice of
s
.
∎

Next comes the proof of Proposition
1.9
.

Proof.

By Lemma
1.7
, there exist
\hat{{\varepsilon}}>0
,
s\geq 4
and
q_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
such that, denoting
v(x)=|q_{0}^{-1}*x|_{\mathbb{H}}^{s}
we have that
U+{\varepsilon}v
is a subsolution and
U-{\varepsilon}v
is a supersolution of (
1.11
) in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
for all
0<{\varepsilon}<\hat{{\varepsilon}}
.
By the comparison principle in Lemma
2.2
we get

U+{\varepsilon}v\leq u_{\varepsilon}+{\varepsilon}\sup_{\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}}(v)

and

u_{\varepsilon}\leq U-{\varepsilon}v+{\varepsilon}\sup_{\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}}(v)

on
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
, for all
0<{\varepsilon}<\hat{{\varepsilon}}
. Therefore,

|u_{\varepsilon}-U|\leq-{\varepsilon}v+{\varepsilon}\sup_{\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}}(v)\leq 2{\varepsilon}\sup_{\Omega_{1}}v

on
\Omega
, so

\left|\left|u_{\varepsilon}-U\right|\right|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\Omega}{\varepsilon},

where
C_{\Omega}=2\sup_{\Omega_{1}}v
. This uniform bound concludes the proof.
∎

5.
Boundary Estimate

In this section we prove Theorem
1.11
.

Proof.

Fix
\eta>0
. We prove the upper bound
u_{{\varepsilon}}(x)\leq G(y)+\eta
, the proof of the lower one being analogous.
By uniform continuity, there exists
\delta\in(0,\bar{\delta})
such that

(5.1)

G(x)\leq G(y)+\frac{\eta}{2}\quad\text{for all}\,\,y\in\partial\Omega,\,x\in B^{\mathbb{H}}_{5\delta}(y)\cap\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}\,\,\text{and}\,\,0<{\varepsilon}<1.

Introducing the notations

(5.2)

N^{\varepsilon}(y)=N^{{\varepsilon},\delta}(y):=\sup_{B^{\mathbb{H}}_{5\delta}(y)\cap\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}}G\quad\text{and}\quad M^{\varepsilon}:=\sup_{\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}}G,

inequality (
5.1
) rewrites as

(5.3)

N^{\varepsilon}(y)\leq G(y)+\frac{\eta}{2}\quad\text{for all}\,\,y\in\partial\Omega\,\,\text{and}\,\,0<{\varepsilon}<1.

Set
\xi=\frac{4-p}{p-1}
and

(5.4)

\theta=\frac{1-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\mu}{2-\mu}\right)^{\xi}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\mu}{2}\right)^{\xi}}{1-\left(\frac{\mu}{2}\right)^{\xi}}\in(0,1).

Note that
\theta
depends only on
\mu
and
p
.
For
k\geq 0
define

(5.5)

\delta_{k}=\frac{\delta}{4^{k-1}}

and

(5.6)

M_{k}^{\varepsilon}(y)=N^{\varepsilon}(y)+\theta^{k}(M^{\varepsilon}-N^{\varepsilon}(y)).

We have the following

Claim
Let
{\varepsilon}_{k}>0
and suppose that for all
{\varepsilon}\in(0,{\varepsilon}_{k})
we have

u_{\varepsilon}\leq M_{k}^{\varepsilon}(y)\quad\text{in}\quad B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{k}}(y)\cap\Omega.

Then, there exists
{\varepsilon}_{k+1}={\varepsilon}_{k+1}(\eta,\mu,\delta,p)\in(0,{\varepsilon}_{k})
such that

u_{\varepsilon}\leq M_{k+1}^{\varepsilon}(y)\quad\text{in}\quad B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{k+1}}(y)\cap\Omega

for all
{\varepsilon}\in(0,{\varepsilon}_{k+1})
.

Using the claim above, since
u_{\varepsilon}\leq M^{\varepsilon}=M_{0}^{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}
for all
{\varepsilon}\in(0,1)
, we can find
{\varepsilon}_{1}>0
such that
u_{\varepsilon}\leq M_{1}^{\varepsilon}(y)
in
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{1}}(y)\cap\Omega
for
{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{1}
. We now repeat this process, and after
k_{0}
iterations we find
{\varepsilon}_{k_{0}}>0
such that
u_{\varepsilon}\leq M_{k_{0}}^{\varepsilon}(y)
in
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{k_{0}}}(y)\cap\Omega
for
{\varepsilon}\in(0,{\varepsilon}_{k_{0}})
.
Choosing
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

k_{0}>\log_{\theta}\left(\frac{\eta}{2}\left(\sup_{\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{1}}G-\inf_{\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{1}}G+1\right)^{-1}\right)

we have

(5.7)

M_{k_{0}}^{\varepsilon}(y)-N^{\varepsilon}(y)=\theta^{k_{0}}(M^{\varepsilon}-N^{\varepsilon}(y))\leq\frac{\eta}{2},

for all
y\in\partial\Omega
, because
\displaystyle{M^{\varepsilon}\leq\sup_{\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}}G}
and
\displaystyle{N^{\varepsilon}(y)\geq\inf_{\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{\varepsilon}}G}
.
Combining with (
5.3
) we get

u_{\varepsilon}\leq G(y)+\eta\quad\text{in}\quad B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{k_{0}}}(y)\cap\Omega,

for all
{\varepsilon}\in(0,{\varepsilon}_{k_{0}})
.

To conclude, we now provide a sketch of the proof of the claim.
The key properties of having radial fundamental solutions in annular domains for
\Delta^{N}_{\mathbb{H},p}
with non-vanishing horizontal gradient,
together with the comparison principle for solutions of (
1.11
), the uniform convergence in Proposition
1.9
and the fact that a genuine triangle inequality holds for the Korányi norm, allow us to adapt the proof of Lemma 4.4 of
[
dTMP20
]
to the current setting with minor modifications.

Proof of claim:

1.

Since
\Omega
satisfies the exterior
\mathbb{H}
-corkscrew condition, there exists a sequence of balls
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu\delta_{k+1}}(z_{k})
strictly contained in
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{k+1}}(y)\setminus\Omega
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. To simplify the notation, let
N=N^{\varepsilon}(y)
and
M_{k}=M^{\varepsilon}_{k}(y)
.
The following boundary value problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta^{N}_{\mathbb{H},p}U_{k}=0&\quad\text{in}\quad B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{k}}(z_{k})\setminus\overline{B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu\delta_{k+1}}(z_{k})}\\
U_{k}=N&\quad\text{on}\quad\partial B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu\delta_{k+1}}(z_{k})\\
U_{k}=M_{k}&\quad\text{on}\quad\partial B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{k}}(z_{k})\end{cases}

has the radial solution

U_{k}(x)=\begin{cases}\frac{a_{k}}{|z_{k}^{-1}*x|_{\mathbb{H}}^{\xi}}+b_{k}&\quad\text{if}\,\,p\neq 4\\
a_{k}\log(|z_{k}^{-1}*x|_{\mathbb{H}})+b_{k}&\quad\text{if}\,\,p=4\end{cases},

for suitable coefficients
a_{k}
and
b_{k}
. Denote by
\tilde{\Omega}
the annulus
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{k}}(z_{k})\setminus\overline{B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu\delta_{k+1}}(z_{k})}
. Choosing
{\varepsilon}_{k+1}
sufficiently small, for
0<{\varepsilon}<{\varepsilon}_{k+1}
we let
U_{k}^{\varepsilon}
be the solution of the problem (
1.11
) in
\tilde{\Omega}
with boundary value
U_{k}
. Since
\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}U_{k}\neq 0
in
\tilde{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}
, Proposition
1.9
implies that
U_{k}^{\varepsilon}
converges uniformly to
U_{k}
in
\tilde{\Omega}
.

2.

Consider now
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{k}/2}(z_{k})
. Due to its radial nature, we have that
U_{k}\geq\alpha M_{k}+\beta N
on
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{k}/2}(z_{k})
, for appropriate coefficients satisfying
\alpha+\beta=1
. By hypothesis,
\alpha u_{\varepsilon}+\beta N\leq\alpha M_{k}+\beta N
on the
{\varepsilon}
-boundary of
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{k}/2}(z_{k})\cap\Omega
. Using the fact that
U_{k}^{\varepsilon}
is uniformly close to
U_{k}
and the comparison principle for solutions of (
1.11
), given
\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}>0
we get that

(5.8)

\alpha u_{\varepsilon}+\beta N\leq U_{k}+2\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}\quad\text{on}\,\,B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{k}/2}(z_{k})\cap\Omega,

for
{\varepsilon}\in(0,{\varepsilon}_{k+1})
sufficiently small.

3.

Now consider
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{k+1}}(y)
. Because a genuine triangle inequality holds for the Korányi gauge, we have
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{k+1}}(y)\subset B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{k}/2}(z_{k})
(so (
5.8
) holds in this ball) and
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{k+1}}(y)\subset B^{\mathbb{H}}_{(2-\mu)\delta_{k+1}}(z_{k})
. We have that
U_{k}\leq\beta^{\prime}N+\alpha^{\prime}M_{k}
on
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{(2-\mu)\delta_{k+1}}(z_{k})
for suitable coefficients satisfying
\alpha^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime}=1
. Combining with (
5.8
), in
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{\delta_{k+1}}(y)\cap\Omega
and for
{\varepsilon}\in(0,{\varepsilon}_{k+1})
we have

\begin{split}u_{\varepsilon}&\leq\frac{\beta^{\prime}-\beta}{\alpha}N+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{\alpha}M_{k}+\frac{2\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}}{\alpha}\\
&=N+\theta(M_{k}-N)=M_{k+1},\end{split}

with an appropriate choice of
\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}
. Keeping track of the coefficients, whose expressions can be explicitly computed, we find that
\theta
turns out to be as in (
5.4
).
∎

6.
The proof of Theorem
1.12

Recall the notion of viscosity solutions of the
p
-Laplace equation

(6.1)

-\Delta^{N}_{\mathbb{H},p}v=0\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}
be open and bounded. A bounded upper semicontinuous function
v:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a viscosity subsolution of (
6.1
) if for every
x_{0}\in\Omega
and for every
\phi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
such that
v-\phi
has a strict maximum at
x_{0}
,
v(x_{0})=\phi(x_{0})
and
\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}{\phi}(x_{0})\neq 0
, it holds

-\Delta^{N}_{\mathbb{H},p}\phi(x_{0})\leq 0.

A bounded lower semicontinuous function
v:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a viscosity supersolution of (
6.1
) if
for every
x_{0}\in\Omega
and for every
\phi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
such that
v-\phi
has a strict minimum at
x_{0}
,
v(x_{0})=\phi(p_{0})
and
\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}{\phi}(x_{0})\neq 0
, it holds

-\Delta^{N}_{\mathbb{H},p}\phi(x_{0})\geq 0.

Finally, a viscosity solution of (
6.1
) is a function which is both a viscosity subsolution and a viscosity supersolution of (
6.1
).

We now study the convergence of
u_{\varepsilon}
as
{\varepsilon}
goes to zero, where
u_{\varepsilon}
is the solution of (
1.11
) with boundary value
G\in C(\Gamma^{\mathbb{H}}_{1})
. For
x\in\Omega
define

(6.2)

\begin{split}\overline{u}(x):&=\limsup_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\varepsilon}\to 0^{+}\\
q\to x\end{subarray}}u_{\varepsilon}(q),\\
\underline{u}(x):&=\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\varepsilon}\to 0^{+}\\
q\to x\end{subarray}}u^{\varepsilon}(q)\end{split}

Note that
\overline{u}
is upper semicontinuous and
\underline{u}
is lower semicontinuous.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\overline{u}
and
\underline{u}
be as in (
6.2
). Then
\overline{u}
is a viscosity subsolution and
\underline{u}
is a viscosity supersolution of
-\Delta^{N}_{\mathbb{H},p}u=0
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

We give a proof for
\overline{u}
, the one for
\underline{u}
being analogous. Take
x_{0}\in\Omega
and a test function
\phi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
with
\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}\phi(x_{0})\neq 0
that touches
\overline{u}
at
x_{0}
from above, i.e.
\overline{u}(x_{0})=\phi(x_{0})
and
\phi-\overline{u}
has a strict minimum at
x_{0}
.
By definition of
\overline{u}
there exist
{\varepsilon}_{n}\to 0^{+}
and
x_{n}\to x_{0}
such that

(6.3)

u_{{\varepsilon}_{n}}(x_{n})\longrightarrow\overline{u}(x_{0}).

Fix
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{r}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
so that
\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}\phi\neq 0
on this set.
By definition of infimum, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
\overline{x}_{n}\in B^{\mathbb{H}}_{r}(x_{0})
such that

(6.4)

\phi(\overline{x}_{n})-u_{{\varepsilon}_{n}}(\overline{x}_{n})\leq\inf_{B^{\mathbb{H}}_{r}(x_{0})}(\phi-u_{{\varepsilon}_{n}})+{\varepsilon}_{n}^{3}.

By possibly passing to a subsequence, we can assume
\overline{x}_{n}\longrightarrow\overline{x}_{0}
for some
\overline{x}_{0}\in\overline{B^{\mathbb{H}}_{r}(x_{0})}
.
We get

\begin{split}\phi(\overline{x}_{0})-\overline{u}(\overline{x}_{0})&=\liminf_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\varepsilon}\to 0^{+}\\
q\to\overline{x}_{0}\end{subarray}}(\phi(q)-u_{\varepsilon}(q))\\
&\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\phi(\overline{x}_{n})-u_{{\varepsilon}_{n}}(\overline{x}_{n})\right)\\
&\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\inf_{B^{\mathbb{H}}_{r}(x_{0})}(\phi-u_{{\varepsilon}_{n}})+{\varepsilon}_{n}^{3}\right)\\
&\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left(\phi(x_{n})-u_{{\varepsilon}_{n}}(x_{n})+{\varepsilon}_{n}^{3}\right)\\
&=\phi(x_{0})-\overline{u}(x_{0}),\end{split}

where we used (
6.3
) and (
6.4
) respectively in the third and second lines.
As a consequence
\overline{x}_{0}=x_{0}
, because
\phi-\overline{u}
has a strict minimum at
x_{0}
.
Note that inequality (
6.4
) rewrites as

u_{{\varepsilon}_{n}}(x)-u_{{\varepsilon}_{n}}(\overline{x}_{n})\leq\phi(x)-\phi(\overline{x}_{n})+{\varepsilon}_{n}^{3}\quad\text{for all}\;\;x\in B^{\mathbb{H}}_{r}(x_{0}).

For
n
large enough, we have
{\varepsilon}_{n}<r/2
and
\overline{x}_{n}\in B^{\mathbb{H}}_{r/2}(x_{0})
so that

B^{\mathbb{H}}_{{\varepsilon}_{n}}(\overline{x}_{n})\subseteq B^{\mathbb{H}}_{r}(x_{0})
. Therefore

\begin{split}\mu_{p}(u_{{\varepsilon}_{n}},{\varepsilon}_{n})(\overline{x}_{n})-u_{{\varepsilon}_{n}}(\overline{x}_{n})&\leq\mu_{p}(\phi,{\varepsilon}_{n})(\overline{x}_{n})-\phi(\overline{x}_{n})+{\varepsilon}_{n}^{3}\\
&=c{\varepsilon}_{n}^{2}\Delta^{N}_{\mathbb{H},p}\phi(\overline{x}_{n})+o({\varepsilon}_{n}^{2}),\end{split}

where we used Lemma
1.6
. We remark that the proof of this Lemma shows that the error in the above expansion is uniform on the set where
\phi
has nonvanishing horizontal gradient. Since
\nabla_{\mathbb{H}}\phi\neq 0
on
B^{\mathbb{H}}_{r}(x_{0})
, such error is independent of
x_{n}
.
By definition of
u_{{\varepsilon}_{n}}
we get

0\leq\Delta^{N}_{\mathbb{H},p}\phi(\overline{x}_{n})+o(1),

which, after taking the limit as
n\to\infty
, concludes the proof for
\overline{u}
.
∎

We finalize now the proof of Theorem
1.12
.

Proof.

By Theorem
1.11
, for
y\in\partial\Omega
we have

\begin{split}\limsup_{\Omega\ni x\to y}\overline{u}(x)\leq G(y)\leq\liminf_{\Omega\ni x\to y}\underline{u}(x).\end{split}

Using the comparison principle for viscosity solutions of the
p
-Laplace equation in
\mathbb{H}
(see
[
Bie06
]
), we get
\overline{u}\leq\underline{u}
in
\Omega
.
Since trivially
\overline{u}\geq\underline{u}
, we have that
\overline{u}=\underline{u}:=u
is a viscosity solution of
-\Delta^{N}_{\mathbb{H},p}u=0
in
\Omega
. Moreover,
u
attains the appropriate boundary value and therefore, by a compactness argument, Theorem
1.12
follows.
∎

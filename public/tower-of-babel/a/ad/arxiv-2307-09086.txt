Strong solutions of fractional Brownian sheet driven SDEs with integrable drift.
Antoine-Marie Bogso, Olivier Menoukeu-Pamen, Frank Proske

Abstract
. We prove the existence of a unique Malliavin differentiable strong solution to a stochastic differential equation on the plane with merely integrable coefficients driven by the fractional Brownian sheet with Hurst parameters less than 1/2. The proof of this result relies on a compactness criterion for square integrable Wiener functionals from Malliavin calculus ([Da Prato, Malliavin and Nualart, 1992]), variational techniques developed in the case of fractional Brownian motion ([Baños, Nielssen, and Proske, 2020]) and the concept of sectorial local nondeterminism (introduced in [Khoshnevisan and Xiao, 2007]). The latter concept enable us to improve the bound of the Hurst parameter (compare with [Baños, Nielssen, and Proske, 2020]).



1.
Introduction

In this paper we aim at studying solutions
X^{x}=(X_{s,t}^{x},(s,t)\in[0,T]^{2})
of the stochastic differential equation (SDE) on the
plane

(1.1)

X_{s,t}=x+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}b(s_{1},t_{1},X_{s_{1},t_{1}})\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}+W_{s,t}^{H},\quad 0\leq s,t\leq T,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
W^{H}=(W_{s,t}^{H},(s,t)\in[0,T]^{2})
is a
d
-dimensional
fractional Brownian sheet with Hurst index
H=(H_{1},H_{2})\in(0,\frac{1}{2})^{2}
and where
b:\,[0,T]^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a Borel-measurable function satisfying some conditions that we will
specified later. We recall that
W^{H}
coincides with the Wiener sheet,
when
H=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})
.

The SDE
1.1
is a hyperbolic stochastic partial differential
equation (HPSDE), whose corresponding differential version takes the
following form for
X(s,t)=X_{s,t}^{x}
and
W^{H}(s,t)=W_{s,t}^{H}

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s\partial t}X(s,t)=b(s,t,X(s,t))+\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s\partial t}W^{H}(s,t),&(s,t)\in(0,T]^{2},\\
&\\
X(s,0)=X(0,t)=x,&s,t\in[0,T],\end{array}\right.

where
\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s\partial t}W^{H}(s,t)
is the white
noise of the fractional Brownian sheet. Interestingly, if
d=1
, the latter
SPDE can be transformed into the following stochastic wave equation with a
non-linear forcing term by using a formal
\frac{\pi}{2}
rotation:

(1.2)

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}X(t,x)-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}}X(t,x)=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t\partial x}B^{H}(t,x)+b(t,x,X(t,x)).

for another fractional Brownian sheet
B^{H}(t,x)
. See e.g.
[
47
]
and
[
24
]
for
further details in the case of a Brownian sheet.

The objective of this paper is to construct a unique global strong
solution to the HSPDE (
1.1
) in the case of a merely
integrable vector field

(1.3)

b\in L^{1}_{\infty}:=L^{\infty}([0,T]^{2};L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})),

when
H_{1}+H_{2}<\frac{1}{2(d+1)}
. A strong solution here refers to a solution that is a progressively measurable functional of the driving noise
W^{H}
. We point out that this result extends the recent findings of
[
13
]
from the case of a
Wiener sheet to the setting of non-Markov fields, when
W^{H}
is the
initial noise. Furthermore, it generalises the work in
[
8
]
, which dealt with additive noise driven by fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter
H<\frac{1}{2}
to the case of a fractional Brownian sheet.

Our motivation for analysing HSPDEs of the type (
1.1
), inspired by the relatively new field of stochastic regularization theory for (a priori ill-posed) ordinary and partial differential equations (see, e.g.,
[
26
]
and
[
25
]
for an overview), is twofold:

1. In
[
37
,
38
]
it was for the first time observed that
strong solutions to SDEs driven by a
L^{\infty}-
drift vector field and an
additive Wiener noise are (locally) Sobolev differentiable with respect to
the initial value and Malliavin differentiable (see also
[
11
]
). Later, the authors in
[
8
]

noted that additive noise given by a fractional Brownian motion with
a sufficiently small Hurst parameter yields even higher regularity in the
associated stochastic flow of the corresponding singular equation compare to

[
7
]
, in terms of higher order (local) Sobolev
differentiability. Further improvements in this direction was
achieved in
[
2
]
, where the authors obtained
C^{\infty}-
flows in the case of certain Gaussian driving noise with non-Hölder
continuous paths. As in
[
2
]
we may (more generally)
consider a two-parameter noise process
\mathbb{W}
in the HSPDE (
1.1
), defined as

(1.4)

\mathbb{W}_{s,t}=\sum_{i\geq 1}\lambda_{i}W_{s,t}^{H_{i}},\leq s,t\leq T\text{,}

where
W^{H_{i}},i\geq 1
is a sequence of a independent fractional Brownian
sheets with Hurst indices
H_{i}=(H_{1,i},H_{2,i})\in(0,\frac{1}{2})^{2}
and a sequence
(\lambda_{i})_{i\geq 1}\subset\mathbb{R}
, which e.g.
insures
L^{2}
-convergence of the sum in (
1.4
) and satisfies certain
conditions (compare
[
2
]
). Similarly to
[
2
]
, we may also expect in this case a regularization by
noise effect of singular hyperbolic PDEs, which gives rise to solutions that are smooth with respect to the initial condition. Hence, in
particular, if
d=1
, such a result would imply the existence (and
uniqueness) of a smooth solution to the stochastic wave equation (
1.2
), where
W^{H}
is replaced by
\mathbb{W}
.

2. Another motivation for this paper comes from the restoration of
well-posedness of (singular) hyperbolic PDEs of the type

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial s\partial t}u(s,t)=b(s,t,u(s,t))

in the context of
path-by-path
concept of solutions, originally introduced in Davie’s groundbreaking work
[
21
]
for Wiener noise-driven SDEs. Applied to HSPDE of the form (
1.1
), this concept means that there exists a measurable set
\Omega^{\ast}
(depending on the initial value
x
) of probability mass
1
such
that for all
\omega\in\Omega^{\ast}
, there exists a unique
deterministic
function
Y=Y(\omega)\in C([0,T]^{2};\mathbb{R}^{d})
that
satisfies (
1.1
). Based on a local-time-space decomposition and
techniques in
[
21
]
, this type of solutions was first studied in
[
12
]
and
[
14
]
in the case of
Brownian sheet and bounded variation drift vector fields. See also
Bechthold, Harang, Rana
[
10
]
for the case of fractional Brownian sheet and distributional vector fields in the framework
of Besov spaces
B_{p,q}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where solutions are described using equations in the nonlinear Young sense. In the this case, it is unclear whether solutions to (
1.1
) with respect to a classical Lebesgue integral and a discontinuous vector field can be interpreted as solutions of equations of the non-linear Young type (see e.g.
[
5
, Remark 4.5.(i)]
). Regarding the
1-
parameter version of (
1.1
), we also mention the work
[
3
]
, where the authors establish path-by-path uniqueness
of solutions in Davie’s sense for SDEs with fractional Brownian motion
additive noise, when
b
is essentially bounded and integrable and
H<\frac{1}{2}
. See also
[
17
]
for the case of continuous vector
fields, an approach later generalized by
[
10
]
to the
2-
parameter setting by applying techniques based on a multiparameter sewing
Lemma, the concept of local time and properties of an averaging operator. Compare also with
[
32
]
.
The result in
[
3
]
relies on techniques developed in
[
45
]
, combined with the aforementioned regularizing properties
of fractional Brownian motion (with a sufficiently small Hurst
parameter). The latter in connection with 1., suggests that such a
result should be also possible for the HSPDE (
1.1
).Therefore, the results obtained in this paper can be considered a first step towards
the goals formulated in 1. and 2., which we persue in forthcoming articles.

Finally, we would like to mention some related results in the literature concerning the analysis of solutions to (
1.1
). The
first results on strong existence and pathwise uniqueness of solutions to
SDEs on the plane with multiplicative Wiener noise date back to
[
16
]
and
[
51
]
, where the authors assume that the
driving vector fields are Lipschitz continuous and of linear growth. We also
refer to
[
53
]
for strong solutions in the case a given
deterministic boundary process, and
[
52
]
regarding the construction
of weak solutions under the conditions of a continuous drift vector field with
linear growth. Furthermore,
[
40
]
investigates the
regularity of solutions of Wiener sheet-driven SDEs in the sense of
Malliavin differentiability, requiring sufficient smoothness of the
coefficients.
See also
[
43
]
for the case of
a stochastic wave equation driven by fractional Brownian sheet.

As for SDEs on the plane with non-Lipschitz continuous vector fields,
results on existence and uniqueness in the Wiener sheet case can be found in Nualart, Tindel
[
41
]
. In that work, the authors use
a comparison theorem to prove these results, under growth and
monotonicity conditions with respect the (one-dimensional) drift coefficient.
We also highlight the work
[
23
]
, which extends the results of
[
41
]
to the case of a fractional Brownian motion sheet with Hurst
indices
H_{1},H_{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}
. In this context, for results on the path-by-path uniqueness in the sense of Davie with
respect to solutions to singular SDEs on the plane, we also recall the
previously mentioned work
[
12
]
in the Wiener sheet case (see also
[
14
]
).

In this paper, as mentioned earlier, we aim to prove the existence and uniqueness of Malliavin differentiable strong solutions to the HSPDE (
1.1
) for bounded and integrable vector fields
b
as defined in
1.3
. This result generalises the corresponding result in
[
13
]
for the
Wiener sheet to the case of
W^{H}
, where
H\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}
. The proof relies on a compactness criterion for square-integrable functionals of a
Wiener sheet from Malliavin calculus (see e.g.
[
13
]
or
[
8
]
,
[
37
]
in the one-parameter case, or
[
6
]
in
the Hilbert space setting), variational
techniques developed in
[
8
]
in the case of a
fractional Brownian motion and the concept of sectorial local
nondeterminism.

Let us stress that the sectorial local
nondeterminism was first introduced by
[
29
]
for the Brownian sheet and extended to fractional Brownian sheets in
[
48
]
. In
[
29
]
, the authors applied sectorial local nondeterminism to study distributional properties of the level sets and the continuity of the local times of the Brownian sheet. A continuation of this work by
[
30
]
focuses on the geometry of the Brownian sheet’s surface. In
[
48
]
, the sectorial local nondeterminism is used to describe the geometric and Fourier analytic properties of fractional Brownian sheets. It is well known that Brownian sheets are not locally nondeterministic, unlike fractional Brownian motions. As a result, we cannot obtain similar estimates as in
[
8
]
. However, sectorial local nondeterminism provides alternative estimates that also allow to prove the existence of a unique strong solution to (
1.1
) when
H_{1}+H_{2}<\frac{1}{2(d+1)}
. If, in addition,
b
is bounded, then (
1.1
) has a unique strong solution provided
\max\{H_{1},H_{2}\}<\frac{1}{2(d+1)}
.
We also note that the methods from related results mentioned above are not applicable to vector fields
b
in (
1.3
) for fractional Brownian sheet. Our strategy consists of four steps. First, we establish the weak existence and the joint uniqueness in law for (
1.1
). The next two steps demonstrate that every weak solution is a strong solution. Precisely, we show that for every
(s,t)\in[0,T]^{2}
,
X^{x}_{s,t}
is measurable with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}^{H}_{s,t}
generated by the random variables
\{W^{H}_{s_{1},t_{1}};s_{1}\leq s,t_{1}\leq t\}
. Since
X^{x}_{s,0}=x=X^{x}_{0,t}
is
\mathcal{F}_{0,0}
-measurable for every
s,\,t\in[0,T]
, it suffices to prove that
X^{x}_{s,t}
is
\mathcal{F}^{H}_{s,t}
-measurable for all
(s,t)\in(0,T]^{2}
. In the second step, we approximate the drift
b
by a sequence of smooth functions with compact support and we show that the sequence of the corresponding solutions converges weakly to a limit, which is
(\mathcal{F}^{H}_{s,t})
-adapted. In the third step, we apply a compactness criterion from Malliavin calculus to obtain a relative compactness property on the sequence constructed in the second step.
In the final step, we show that
(X^{x}_{s,t})
is a strong solution and apply a dual Yamada-Watanabe argument to deduce pathwise uniqueness.

Our paper is structured as follows: In Section
2
, we discuss the basic mathematical framework for this article. In Section
3
, we establish some key estimates based on the property of sectorial (strong) local nondeterminism of random fields (see, for example, Theorem
3
). Finally, in Section 4,we apply these estimates to prove our main result (Theorem
4
) on the strong uniqueness of solutions for (
1.1
).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Fractional calculus

Here are some basic definitions and results on two-parameter calculus that can be found in
[
23
]
and
[
46
]
. We refer to
[
44
]
and
[
35
]
for an exhaustive survey on classical one-parameter fractional calculus.

Let
f\in L^{p}([0,T]^{2})
with
T>0
and
p\geq 1
. For
\alpha>0
,
\beta>0
, the
(\alpha,\beta)
-order left-sided Riemann-Liouville fractional integral of
f
on
(0,T)^{2}
is defined by

(2.1)

\displaystyle I^{\alpha,\beta}f(x,y)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{x}\int_{0}^{y}(x-u)^{\alpha-1}(y-v)^{\beta-1}f(u,v)\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v,

for almost all
(x,y)\in[0,T]^{2}
, where
\Gamma
is the Gamma function. Observe that (
2.1
) rewrites

\displaystyle I^{\alpha,\beta}f(x,y)=I^{\alpha}(I^{\beta}f(\cdot,y))(x),

where
I^{\alpha}
(respectively
I^{\beta}
) is the
\alpha
-order (respectively
\beta
-order) left-sided Riemann-Liouville fractional integral on
(0,T)
. The integral
I^{\alpha,\beta}
can be iterated as it is shown by the following first composition formula.

\displaystyle I^{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}(I^{\alpha,\beta}f)=I^{\alpha^{\prime}+\alpha,\beta^{\prime}+\beta}f.

The fractional derivative can be defined as the reverse operation of the fractional integral. Assume in addition
\alpha<1
,
\beta<1
and denote by
I^{\alpha,\beta}(L^{p})
the image of
L^{p}([0,T]^{2})
by the operator
I^{\alpha,\beta}
. If
f\in I^{\alpha,\beta}(L^{p})
, then the function
\phi
such that
f=I^{\alpha,\beta}\phi
is unique in
L^{p}([0,T]^{2})
and is equal to the
(\alpha,\beta)
-order left-sided Riemann-Liouville derivative of
f
given by

\displaystyle D^{\alpha,\beta}f(x,y)=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}I^{1-\alpha,1-\beta}f}{\partial x\partial y}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\beta)}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x\partial y}\int_{0}^{x}\int_{0}^{y}\frac{f(u,v)}{(x-u)^{\alpha}(y-v)^{\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v.

If
\min\{\alpha p,\beta p\}>1
, then any function in
I^{\alpha,\beta}(L^{p})
is
(\alpha-1/p,\beta-1/p)
-Hölder continuous. Moreover, any Hölder continuous function of order
(\gamma,\delta)

with
\gamma>\alpha
and
\delta>\beta
has a fractional derivative of order
(\alpha,\beta)
. The left-sided derivative of
f
defined above has the following Weil representation

\displaystyle D^{\alpha,\beta}f(x,y)=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathbf{1}_{(0,T)^{2}}(x,y)}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)\Gamma(1-\beta)}\Big\{\frac{f(x,y)}{x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}}+\frac{\alpha}{y^{\beta}}\int_{0}^{x}\frac{f(x,y)-f(u,y)}{(x-u)^{\alpha+1}}\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\beta}{x^{\alpha}}\int_{0}^{y}\frac{f(x,y)-f(x,v)}{(y-v)^{\beta+1}}\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\qquad+\alpha\beta\int_{0}^{x}\int_{0}^{y}\frac{f(x,y)-f(u,y)-f(x,v)+f(u,v)}{(x-u)^{\alpha+1}(y-v)^{\beta+1}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v\Big\},

where the convergence of the integrals at singularity
x=u
or
y=v
holds in
L^{p}
sense.

By construction, we have

\displaystyle I^{\alpha,\beta}(D^{\alpha,\beta}f)=f,\,\forall\,f\in I^{\alpha,\beta}(L^{p})\,\text{ and }\,D^{\alpha,\beta}(I^{\alpha,\beta}f)=f,\,\forall\,f\in L^{1}([0,T]^{2}).

Finally, if
f\in I^{\alpha+\alpha^{\prime},\beta+\beta^{\prime}}(L^{1})
, with
\max\{\alpha+\beta,\alpha^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime}\}\leq 1
, we also have the composition formula

\displaystyle D^{\alpha,\beta}(D^{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}f)=D^{\alpha+\alpha^{\prime},\beta+\beta^{\prime}}f.

2.2.
Fractional Brownian sheet

Let
W^{H}=(W^{H}_{s,t},(s,t)\in[0,T]^{2})
be a
d
-dimensional fractional Brownian sheet with Hurst index
H=(H_{1},H_{2})\in(0,1/2)^{2}
defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. More precisely,
W^{H}
is a centered Gaussian process with covariance matrix

\displaystyle R_{H}(s_{1},t_{1},s_{2},t_{2})_{i,j}:=\mathbb{E}\left[W^{H,(i)}_{s_{1},t_{1}}W^{H,(j)}_{s_{2},t_{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\delta_{ij}(s_{2}^{2H_{1}}+s_{1}^{2H_{1}}-|s_{2}-s_{1}|^{2H_{1}})(t_{2}^{2H_{2}}+t_{1}^{2H_{2}}-|t_{2}-t_{1}|^{2H_{2}}),\,i,j=1,\ldots,d,

where
\delta_{ij}=1
if
i=j
and
0
if
i\neq j
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be the set of step functions on
[0,T]^{2}
and denote by
\mathcal{H}
the Hilbert space defined as the closure of
\mathcal{E}
with respect to the inner product

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{1}_{[0,s_{1}]\times[0,t_{1}]},\mathbf{1}_{[0,s_{2}]\times[0,t_{2}]}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=R_{H}(s_{1},t_{1},s_{2},t_{2}).

The mapping
\mathbf{1}_{[0,s]\times[0,t]}\mapsto W^{H}_{s,t}
can be extended to an isometry between
\mathcal{H}
and the Gaussian subspace of
L^{2}(\Omega)
associated with
W^{H}
. Denote by
\varphi\mapsto W^{H}(\varphi)
such isometry. The following representation of
R_{H}
holds for
H\in(0,1/2)^{2}
(see e.g.
[
23
]
and
[
39
, Proposition 5.1.3]
):

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
H=(H_{1},H_{2})\in(0,\frac{1}{2})^{2}
,
\alpha\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
and
F_{\alpha}:\,(0,\infty)^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be the function defined by

\displaystyle F_{\alpha}(r,s):=\Big(\frac{s}{r}\Big)^{\alpha-1/2}(s-r)^{\alpha-1/2}\mathbf{1}_{[0,s]}(r)\quad\text{for all }r,\,s.

Let
K_{H}
be the kernel

\displaystyle K_{H}(s_{1},t_{1},s_{2},t_{2})=K_{H_{1}}(s_{1},s_{2})K_{H_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2}),

where
0<s_{1}<s_{2}
,
0<t_{1}<t_{2}
,

\displaystyle K_{H_{1}}(s_{1},s_{2})=c_{H_{1}}\Big[F_{H_{1}}(s_{1},s_{2})+\Big(\frac{1}{2}-H_{1}\Big)\int_{s_{1}}^{s_{2}}\frac{F_{H_{1}}(s_{1},u)}{u}\,\mathrm{d}u\Big],

\displaystyle K_{H_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})=c_{H_{2}}\Big[F_{H_{2}}(t_{1},t_{2})+\Big(\frac{1}{2}-H_{2}\Big)\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{F_{H_{2}}(t_{1},u)}{u}\,\mathrm{d}u\Big]

and

\displaystyle c_{H_{i}}=\sqrt{\frac{2H_{i}}{(1-2H_{i})\beta(1-2H_{i},H_{i}+1/2)}},\quad i=1,2

with
\beta
, the Beta function. Then

\displaystyle R_{H}(s_{1},t_{1},s_{2},t_{2})=\int_{0}^{s_{1}\wedge s_{2}}\int_{0}^{t_{1}\wedge t_{2}}K_{H}(u,v,s_{1},t_{1})K_{H}(u,v,s_{2},t_{2})\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v.

Consider the linear operator
K^{\ast}_{H}:\,\mathcal{E}\to L^{2}([0,T]^{2})
given by

\displaystyle(K^{\ast}_{H}\varphi)(s,t)

\displaystyle=K_{H}(T,T,s,t)\varphi(s,t)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{s}^{T}\int_{t}^{T}(\varphi(u,v)-\varphi(s,t))\frac{\partial^{2}K_{H}}{\partial u\partial v}(u,v,s,t)\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v

for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{E}
. One can easily see that

(K^{\ast}_{H}\mathbf{1}_{[0,s]\times[0,t]})(s_{1},t_{1})=K_{H}(s,s_{1},t,t_{1})\mathbf{1}_{[0,s]\times[0,t]}(s_{1},t_{1}).

Hence, for any step functions
\varphi
,
\psi
in
\mathcal{E}
, we have from (
2.2
) and Proposition
2.1

\displaystyle\langle K^{\ast}_{H}\varphi,K^{\ast}_{H}\psi\rangle_{L^{2}([0,T]^{2})}=\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

As a consequence, the operator
K^{\ast}_{H}
is an isometry between
\mathcal{E}
and
L^{2}([0,T]^{2})
that can be extended to the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
. Then the
d
-dimensional process
W=(W_{s,t},(s,t)\in[0,T]^{2})
defined by

(2.3)

\displaystyle W_{s,t}=W^{H}(({K_{H}^{\ast}})^{-1}(\mathbf{1}_{[0,s]\times[0,t]}))

is a
d
-dimensional Brownian sheet, and the process
W^{H}
has the following representation

(2.4)

\displaystyle W^{H}_{s,t}=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}K_{H}(s,t,s_{1},t_{1})\,\mathrm{d}W_{s_{1},t_{1}}.

The proof of similar results in the one parameter case can be found in
[
1
]
.

For every
(s,t)\in[0,T]^{2}
, we denote by
\mathcal{F}_{s,t}
the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the random variables
\{W_{u,v};\,u\leq s,v\leq t\}
, where
W
is defined by (
2.3
). It follows from (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) that
\mathcal{F}_{s,t}
coincide with the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}^{H}_{s,t}
generated by the random variables
\{W^{H}_{u,v};\,u\leq s,v\leq t\}
.
We equip the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
with the filtration
(\mathcal{F}^{H}_{s,t},(s,t)\in[0,T]^{2})
augmented by all
\mathbb{P}
-null sets. Next, we give a multidimensional version of Girsanov’s theorem for fractional Brownian sheet provided by
[
23
, Theorem 3]
. First we need to introduce the isomorphism
K_{H}
from
L^{2}([0,T]^{2})
onto
I^{H_{1}+\frac{1}{2},H_{2}+\frac{1}{2}}(L^{2})
associated with the kernel
K_{H}(s,t,s_{1},t_{1})
and defined as

(2.5)

\displaystyle(K_{H}\varphi)(s,t)=I^{2H_{1},2H_{2}}s^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{1}}t^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{2}}I^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{1},\frac{1}{2}-H_{2}}s^{H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}t^{H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\varphi

for any
\varphi\in L^{2}([0,T]^{2})
.

Now, given a
d
-dimensional process
(\theta_{s,t},(s,t)\in[0,T]^{2})
with integrable trajectories, we consider the transformation

\displaystyle\widetilde{W}^{H}_{s,t}=W^{H}_{s,t}+\int_{0}^{s}\int_{0}^{t}\theta_{u,v}\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}v.

Observe that

almost surely if, and only if

for all

It follows from (

\displaystyle(K^{-1}_{H}\varphi)(s,t)=s^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{1}}t^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{2}}D^{1/2-H_{1},1/2-H_{2}}s^{H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}t^{H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}}D^{2H_{1},2H_{2}}\varphi

\displaystyle(K^{-1}_{H}\varphi)(s,t)=s^{H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}t^{H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}}I^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{1},\frac{1}{2}-H_{2}}s^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{1}}t^{\frac{1}{2}-H_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial s\partial t}.

\displaystyle\frac{(\text{det }Q)^{1/2}}{(2\pi)^{mp}}\frac{(2\pi)^{(2mp-1)/2}}{(\text{det }Q)^{1/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{v^{2}}{\widehat{\sigma}_{j}^{2}}\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}v^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}v=\frac{1}{\widehat{\sigma}^{2}_{j}},

where
\widehat{\sigma}_{j}^{2}:=\text{Var}[W^{(\ell),H}_{s_{j},t_{j}}|W^{(\ell),H}_{s_{i},t_{i}},\,i=1,\ldots,2mp,\,i\neq j]
.

Applying Theorem
2
and Young inequality, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\sigma}_{j}^{2}\geq

\displaystyle C_{\epsilon,H}\left(\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq 2mp,\,i\neq j}|s_{j}-s_{i}|^{2H_{1}}+\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq 2mp,\,i\neq j}|t_{j}-t_{i}|^{2H_{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2C_{\epsilon,H}\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq 2mp,\,i\neq j}|s_{j}-s_{i}|^{H_{1}}\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq 2mp,\,i\neq j}|t_{\sigma(j)}-t_{i}|^{H_{2}}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}(\widehat{\sigma}^{2}_{j})^{\alpha^{(\ell)}_{[j]}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(4C^{2}_{\epsilon,H})^{|\alpha^{(\ell)}|}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq 2mp,\,i\neq j}|s_{j}-s_{i}|^{H_{1}\alpha^{(\ell)}_{[j]}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq 2mp,\,i\neq j}|t_{j}-t_{i}|^{H_{2}\alpha^{(\ell)}_{[j]}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(4C^{2}_{\epsilon,H})^{|\alpha^{(\ell)}|}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq 2mp,\,i\neq\sigma^{-1}(j)}|s_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}-s_{i}|^{H_{1}\alpha^{(\ell)}_{[\sigma^{-1}(j)]}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq 2mp,\,i\neq\pi^{-1}(j)}|t_{\pi^{-1}(j)}-t_{i}|^{H_{2}\alpha^{(\ell)}_{[\pi^{-1}(j)]}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(4C^{2}_{\epsilon,H})^{|\alpha^{(\ell)}|}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}\min\{|s_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}-s_{\sigma^{-1}(j+1)}|,|s_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}-s_{\sigma^{-1}(j-1)}|\}^{H_{1}\alpha^{\sigma,(\ell)}_{[j]}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}\min\{|t_{\pi^{-1}(j)}-t_{\pi^{-1}(j+1)}|,|t_{\pi^{-1}(j)}-t_{\pi^{-1}(j-1)}|\}^{H_{2}\alpha^{\pi,(\ell)}_{[j]}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(4C^{2}_{\epsilon,H})^{|\alpha^{(\ell)}|}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}|s_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}-s_{\sigma^{-1}(j+1)}|^{2H_{1}\alpha^{\sigma,(\ell)}_{[j]}}|t_{\pi^{-1}(j)}-t_{\pi^{-1}(j+1)}|^{2H_{2}\alpha^{\pi,(\ell)}_{[j]}},

where
s_{\sigma^{-1}(2m+1)}=\bar{r}
,
t_{\pi^{-1}(2m+1)}=\bar{u}
,
\alpha^{\sigma,(\ell)}_{[j]}=\alpha^{(\ell)}_{[\sigma^{-1}(j)]}
and
\alpha^{\pi,(\ell)}_{[j]}=\alpha^{(\ell)}_{[\pi^{-1}(j)]}
.

As a consequence,

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[|\langle Q^{-1/2}G,e_{j}\rangle|^{2}\Big]\Big)^{\alpha^{(\ell)}_{[j]}}=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}\Big(\frac{1}{\widehat{\sigma}_{j}^{2}}\Big)^{\alpha^{(\ell)}_{[j]}}

(3.11)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(4C^{2}_{\epsilon,H})^{|\alpha^{(\ell)}|}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}\frac{1}{|s_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}-s_{\sigma^{-1}(j+1)}|^{2H_{1}\alpha^{\sigma,(\ell)}_{[j]}}|t_{\pi^{-1}(j)}-t_{\pi^{-1}(j+1)}|^{2H_{2}\alpha^{\pi,(\ell)}_{[j]}}}.

We deduce from (
3.10
) and (
3.11
) that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2mp}}\Big(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}|u_{j}^{(\ell)}|^{\alpha^{(\ell)}_{[j]}}\Big)\exp\Big\{-\frac{1}{2}\langle Qu^{(\ell)},u^{(\ell)}\rangle\Big\}\mathrm{d}u_{1}^{(\ell)}\ldots\mathrm{d}u_{2mp}^{(\ell)}

(3.12)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{mp}(C^{(1)}_{\epsilon,H})^{|\alpha^{(\ell)}|}\sqrt{(2|\alpha^{(\ell)}|)!}}{(\text{det }Q)^{1/2}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}|s_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}-s_{\sigma^{-1}(j+1)}|^{H_{1}\alpha^{\sigma,(\ell)}_{[j]}}|t_{\pi^{-1}(j)}-t_{\pi^{-1}(j+1)}|^{H_{2}\alpha^{\pi,(\ell)}_{[j]}}},

where
C^{(1)}_{\epsilon,H}=1/2C_{\epsilon,H}
. Using once again Theorem (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\text{det }Q=

\displaystyle\text{det Cov}\left(W^{(\ell),H}_{s_{i},t_{i}},\,i=1,\ldots,2mp\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\text{Var}[W^{(\ell),H}_{s_{1},t_{1}}]\prod\limits_{j=2}^{2mp}\text{Var}[W^{(\ell),H}_{s_{j},t_{j}}|W^{(\ell),H}_{s_{i},t_{i}},\,i=1,\,\ldots,\,j-1]

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon^{2(H_{1}+H_{2})}}{4}C_{\epsilon,H}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{2mp}\left(\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq j-1}|s_{j}-s_{i}|^{2H_{1}}+\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq j-1}|t_{j}-t_{i}|^{2H_{2}}\right)

(3.13)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\Big(\frac{\epsilon}{T}\Big)^{2(H_{1}+H_{2})}C_{\epsilon,H}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}|s_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}-s_{\sigma^{-1}(j+1)}|^{2H_{1}}|t_{\pi^{-1}(j)}-t_{\pi^{-1}(j+1)}|^{2H_{2}}.

Plugging (
3.13
) into (
3.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2mp}}\Big(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}|u_{j}^{(\ell)}|^{\alpha^{(\ell)}_{[j]}}\Big)\exp\Big\{-\frac{1}{2}\langle Qu^{(\ell)},u^{(\ell)}\rangle\Big\}\mathrm{d}u_{1}^{(\ell)}\ldots\mathrm{d}u_{2mp}^{(\ell)}

(3.14)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(C^{(2)}_{\epsilon,H,T})^{mp+|\alpha^{(\ell)}|}\sqrt{(2|\alpha^{(\ell)}|)!}}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}|s_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}-s_{\sigma^{-1}(j+1)}|^{H_{1}(\alpha^{\sigma,(\ell)}_{[j]}+1)}|t_{\pi^{-1}(j)}-t_{\pi^{-1}(j+1)}|^{H_{2}(\alpha^{\pi,(\ell)}_{[j]}+1)}}.

where

\displaystyle C^{(2)}_{\epsilon,H,T}=2\pi\Big(\frac{1}{\min\{1,2C^{2}_{\epsilon,H}\}}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\frac{T}{\min\{1,\epsilon\}}\Big)^{H_{1}+H_{2}}.

Using (
3.9
) and the change of variable
s^{\prime}_{k}=s_{\sigma^{-1}(k)},\,t^{\prime}_{k}=t_{\pi^{-1}(k)},\,k=1,\ldots,2mp
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[|\Lambda_{\alpha,p,m}^{f,g}(\bar{r},\bar{s},\bar{u},\bar{t},z)|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\epsilon,H,T}^{mpd+|\alpha|}\Big(\prod\limits_{\ell=1}^{d}\sqrt{(2|\alpha^{(\ell)}|)!}\Big)\sum\limits_{\sigma,\pi\in\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{m|2p}}\int_{\nabla^{2mp,\sigma}_{\bar{r},\bar{s}}}\int_{\nabla^{2mp,\pi}_{\bar{u},\bar{t}}}\widetilde{g}(s,t)|\widetilde{f}(s,t,z)|

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{\mathrm{d}t_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{2mp}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}s_{2mp}}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}|s_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}-s_{\sigma^{-1}(j+1)}|^{H_{1}(\alpha^{\sigma}_{[j]}|+d)}|t_{\pi^{-1}(j)}-t_{\pi^{-1}(j+1)}|^{H_{2}(|\alpha^{\pi}_{[j]}|+d)}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\epsilon,H,T}^{mpd+|\alpha|}\Big(\prod\limits_{\ell=1}^{d}\sqrt{(2|\alpha^{(\ell)}|)!}\Big)\sum\limits_{\sigma,\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{2mp}}\int_{\nabla^{2mp}_{\bar{r},\bar{s}}}\int_{\nabla^{2mp}_{\bar{u},\bar{t}}}\widetilde{g}_{\sigma,\pi}(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})|\widetilde{f}_{\sigma,\pi}(s^{\prime},t^{\prime},z)|

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}t^{\prime}_{2mp}\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}_{2mp}}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}|s^{\prime}_{j}-s^{\prime}_{j+1}|^{H_{1}(|\alpha^{\sigma}_{[j]}|+d)}|t^{\prime}_{j}-t^{\prime}_{j+1}|^{H_{2}(|\alpha^{\pi}_{[j]}|+d)}},

where
|\alpha|=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\alpha^{(\ell)}
,
|\alpha^{\sigma}_{[j]}|=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\alpha^{\sigma,(\ell)}_{[j]}
,
|\alpha^{\pi}_{[j]}|=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\alpha^{\pi,(\ell)}_{[j]}
,
C_{\epsilon,H,T}=4\pi C^{(2)}_{\epsilon,H,T}
,
\widetilde{g}_{\sigma,\pi}(s^{\prime},t^{\prime})=g(s^{\prime}_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,s^{\prime}_{\sigma(2mp)},t^{\prime}_{\pi(1)},\ldots,t^{\prime}_{\pi(2mp)})
and

\widetilde{f}_{\sigma,\pi}(s^{\prime},t^{\prime},z):=\widetilde{f}(s^{\prime}_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,s^{\prime}_{\sigma(2m)},t^{\prime}_{\pi(1)},\ldots,t^{\prime}_{\pi(2m)},z).

This completes the proof of (
3.6
).

We now prove inequality (
3.7
). Using (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{mp}}\Lambda_{\alpha}^{\kappa f}(\bar{r},\bar{u},\bar{s},\bar{t},z)\,\mathrm{d}z\Big]\Big|\leq\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{mp}}\Big(\mathbb{E}\Big[|\Lambda_{\alpha}^{\kappa f}(\bar{r},\bar{u},\bar{s},\bar{t},z)|^{2}\Big]\Big)^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C^{(mpd+|\alpha|)/2}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{mp}}\left(\Psi^{\kappa f}_{\alpha}(\bar{r},\bar{u},\bar{s},\bar{t},z)\right)^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\leq C^{(mpd+|\alpha|)/2}\Big(\prod\limits_{\ell=1}^{d}\sqrt{(2|\alpha^{(\ell)}|)!}\Big)^{1/2}\Big(\sup\limits_{0\leq r,u\leq T}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f_{[j]}(r,u,z_{j})|\mathrm{d}z_{j}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Big(\sum\limits_{\sigma,\pi\in\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{m|2p}}\int_{\nabla^{2mp}_{\bar{r},\bar{s}}}\int_{\nabla^{2mp}_{\bar{u},\bar{t}}}\widetilde{g}_{\sigma,\pi}(s,t)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2mp}\frac{\kappa_{[j]}(s_{\sigma(j)},t_{\pi(j)})}{|s_{j}-s_{j+1}|^{H_{1}(|\alpha^{\sigma}_{[j]}|+d)}|t_{j}-t_{j+1}|^{H_{2}(|\alpha^{\pi}_{[j]}|+d)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\mathrm{d}t_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{2mp}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}s_{2mp}\Big)^{1/2}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=C^{(mpd+|\alpha|)/2}\left(\Psi_{\alpha,p,m}^{\kappa,g}(\bar{r},\bar{u},\bar{s},\bar{t})\right)^{1/2}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{mp}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{1}_{\infty}}.

Finally, we show the integration by parts formula (
3.8
). For any
R>0
, define

\displaystyle\Lambda_{\alpha,p,m,R}^{f,g}(\bar{r},\bar{u},\bar{s},\bar{t},z):=

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{-dmp}\int_{B(R)}\int_{(\nabla^{m}_{\bar{r},\bar{s}})^{p}}\int_{(\nabla^{m}_{\bar{u},\bar{t}})^{p}}g(s,t)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{mp}f_{j}(s_{j},t_{j},z_{j})(-iv_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times\exp\{-i\langle v_{j},W^{H}_{s_{j},t_{j}}-z_{j}\rangle\}\,\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}v,

where
B(R):=\{z\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{m}:\,|z|<R\}
. This implies

(3.15)

\displaystyle|\Lambda_{\alpha,p,m,R}^{f,g}(\bar{r},\bar{u},\bar{s},\bar{t},z)|\leq C_{R}\int_{(\nabla^{m}_{\bar{r},\bar{s}})^{p}}\int_{(\nabla^{m}_{\bar{u},\bar{t}})^{p}}g(s,t)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{mp}|f_{j}(s_{j},t_{j},z_{j})|\,\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t

for a sufficiently large constant
C_{R}>0
. Since the right-hand side in (
3.15
) is integrable over
(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{m}
,
similar computations as above show that
\Lambda_{\alpha,p,m,R}^{f,g}(\bar{r},\bar{u},\bar{s},\bar{t},z)
converges to
\Lambda_{\alpha,p,m}^{f,g}(\bar{r},\bar{u},\bar{s},\bar{t},z)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
R
goes to
\infty
for all
\bar{r}
,
\bar{u}
,
\bar{s}
,
\bar{t}
and
z
.

Using the Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem and the fact that the Fourier transform is an automorphism on the Schwarz space, one has

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{mp}}\Lambda_{\alpha,p,m}^{f,g}(\bar{r},\bar{u},\bar{s},\bar{t},z)\,\mathrm{d}z=\lim\limits_{R\to\infty}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{mp}}\Lambda_{\alpha,p,m,R}^{f,g}(\bar{r},\bar{u},\bar{s},\bar{t},z)\,\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{R\to\infty}(2\pi)^{-dmp}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{mp}}\int_{B(R)}\int_{(\nabla^{m}_{\bar{r},\bar{s}})^{p}}\int_{(\nabla^{m}_{\bar{u},\bar{t}})^{p}}g(s,t)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{mp}f_{j}(s_{j},t_{j},z_{j})(-iv_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times e^{-i\langle v_{j},W^{H}_{s_{j},t_{j}}-z_{j}\rangle}\,\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{R\to\infty}\int_{(\nabla^{m}_{\bar{r},\bar{s}})^{p}}\int_{(\nabla^{m}_{\bar{u},\bar{t}})^{p}}g(s,t)\int_{B(R)}(2\pi)^{-dmp}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{mp}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{mp}f_{j}(s_{j},t_{j},z_{j})e^{i\langle v_{j},z_{j}\rangle}(-iv_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\times e^{-i\langle v_{j},W^{H}_{s_{j},t_{j}}\rangle}\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{R\to\infty}\int_{(\nabla^{m}_{\bar{r},\bar{s}})^{p}}\int_{(\nabla^{m}_{\bar{u},\bar{t}})^{p}}g(s,t)\int_{B(R)}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{mp}\widehat{f}_{j}(s_{j},t_{j},-v_{j})(-iv_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}e^{-i\langle v_{j},W^{H}_{s_{j},t_{j}}\rangle}\,\mathrm{d}v\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{(\nabla^{m}_{\bar{r},\bar{s}})^{p}}\int_{(\nabla^{m}_{\bar{u},\bar{t}})^{p}}g(s,t)D^{\alpha}f(s,t,W^{H}_{s,t})\,\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t,

where
\widehat{f}_{j}
denotes the Fourier transform of
f_{j}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the classical inner product in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This ends the proof.
∎

3.2.
Regularising estimates of the fractional Brownian sheet

The next estimate shows that the fractional Brownian sheet regularises (
1.1
).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(W_{r,u}^{H},(r,u)\in[0,T]^{2})
be a
d
-dimensional fractional Brownian sheet with Hurst index
H=(H_{1},H_{2})\in(0,\frac{1}{2})^{2}
under
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. Let
f
and
\kappa
be given as in (
3.1
) and (
3.2
) respectively. Let
p,\,m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha\in(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{m})^{d}
be a multi-index,
0<\epsilon\leq\bar{r}<r<\bar{s}\leq T
,
0<\epsilon\leq\bar{u}<u<\bar{t}\leq T
,

\displaystyle\kappa_{j}(s,t)=(K_{H}(s_{j},t_{j},r,u)-K_{H}(s_{j},t_{j},\bar{r},\bar{u}))^{\varepsilon_{j}}

for every
s=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{m})
,
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})
,
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and
(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m})\in\{0,1\}^{m}
. Suppose

(3.16)

\displaystyle\max\{H_{1},H_{2}\}<\frac{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}{d-1+|\alpha_{j}|}

for all
j=1,\ldots,m
, where
|\alpha_{j}|=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\alpha_{j}^{(\ell)}
and
\gamma\in(0,H)
is sufficiently small. Then there exists a universal constant
C=C_{\varepsilon,H,T,d}
(depending on
H
,
T
and
d
, but independent of
m
,
p
,
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1,\ldots,m}
and
\alpha
) such that

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\Big[\Big(\int_{\nabla^{m}_{r,\bar{s}}}\int_{\nabla^{m}_{u,\bar{t}}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}D^{\alpha_{j}}f_{j}(s_{j},t_{j},W^{H}_{s_{j},t_{j}})\kappa_{j}(s_{j},t_{j})\,\mathrm{d}t_{j}\mathrm{d}s_{j}\Big)^{p}\Big]\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{p(md+|\alpha|)}\left(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{d}(2p|{\alpha}^{(i)}|)!\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|^{p}_{L^{1}_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big[r^{H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\bar{u}^{H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\Big(\frac{|u-\bar{u}|^{\gamma}}{(u\bar{u})^{\gamma}}+\frac{|r-\bar{r}|^{\gamma}}{(r\bar{r})^{\gamma}}\Big)\Big]^{p\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{(\bar{s}-r)^{p(H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}-p(|{\alpha}|+md)H_{1}+pm}}{\Gamma\left(2p(H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}-2p(|{\alpha}|+md)H_{1}+2pm+1\right)^{1/2}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{(\bar{t}-u)^{p(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}-p(|{\alpha}|+md)H_{2}+pm}}{\Gamma\left(2p(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}-2p(|{\alpha}|+md)H_{2}+2pm+1\right)^{1/2}}.

Proof.

We start by noticing that

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\nabla^{m}_{r,\bar{s}}}\int_{\nabla^{m}_{u,\bar{t}}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}D^{\alpha_{j}}f_{j}(s_{j},t_{j},W^{H}_{s_{j},t_{j}})\kappa_{j}(s_{j},t_{j})\mathrm{d}t_{m}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{m}\ldots\mathrm{d}s_{1}\Big)^{p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{(\nabla_{r,\bar{s}}^{m})^{p}}\int_{(\nabla_{u,\bar{t}}^{m})^{p}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{pm}D^{\bar{\alpha}_{j}}\bar{f}_{j}(s_{j},t_{j},W^{H}_{s_{j},t_{j}})\bar{\kappa}_{j}(s_{j},t_{j})\mathrm{d}t_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{pm}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}s_{pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\bar{\kappa}\bar{f},g}_{\bar{\alpha},p,m}(r,\bar{s},u,\bar{t},z),

where
g=1
,
\bar{f}_{j}=f_{j-im}
,
\bar{\alpha}_{j}=\alpha_{j-im}
and

\displaystyle\bar{\kappa}_{j}(s,t)=(K_{H}(s_{j},t_{j},r,u)-K_{H}(s_{j},t_{j},\bar{r},\bar{u}))^{\bar{\varepsilon}_{j}}

with
\bar{\varepsilon}_{j}=\varepsilon_{j-im}
for every
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,p-1\}
,
j\in\{1+im,\ldots,(1+i)m\}
.

We deduce from Theorem
3
that

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}\left[\Lambda^{\bar{\kappa}\bar{f},g}_{\bar{\alpha},p,m}(r,\bar{s},u,\bar{t},z)\right]|\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\Lambda^{\bar{\kappa}\bar{f},g}_{\bar{\alpha},p,m}(r,\bar{s},u,\bar{t},z)^{2}\right]

(3.17)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}^{(pmd+|\widetilde{\alpha}|)/2}(\Psi_{\bar{\alpha},p,m}^{\bar{\kappa},g}(r,\bar{s},u,\bar{t}))^{1/2}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|^{p}_{L^{1}_{\infty}},

where

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bar{\alpha},p,m}^{\bar{\kappa},g}(r,\bar{s},u,\bar{t})=\prod\limits_{\ell=1}^{d}\sqrt{(2|\bar{\alpha}^{(\ell)}|)!}\sum\limits_{\sigma,\pi\in\widehat{\mathcal{P}}_{m|2p}}

\displaystyle\int_{\nabla^{2mp}_{r,\bar{s}}}\int_{\nabla^{2mp}_{u,\bar{t}}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2pm}\frac{|(K_{H}(s_{\sigma(j)},t_{\pi(j)},r,u)-K_{H}(s_{\sigma(j)},t_{\pi(j)},\bar{r},\bar{u}))^{\bar{\varepsilon}_{[j]}}|\mathrm{d}t_{j}\mathrm{d}s_{j}}{|s_{j}-s_{j+1}|^{H_{1}(|\alpha_{[\sigma^{-1}(j)]}|+d)}|t_{j}-t_{j+1}|^{H_{2}(|\alpha_{[\pi^{-1}(j)]}|+d)}}.

We deduce from (
3.16
) that

\displaystyle(H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\bar{\varepsilon}_{[j]}-(|\bar{\alpha}_{[j]}|+d)H_{1}>-1

and

\displaystyle(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\bar{\varepsilon}_{[j]}-(|\bar{\alpha}_{[j]}|+d)H_{2}>-1

for all
j=1,\ldots,2pm
. Then Lemma
18
yields

\displaystyle\Psi_{\bar{\alpha},p,m}^{\bar{\kappa},g}(r,\bar{s},u,\bar{t})\leq C_{1}^{pm}\prod\limits_{\ell=1}^{d}\sqrt{(2|\bar{\alpha}^{(\ell)}|)!}\,\Pi_{\gamma,pm}(H_{1})\Pi_{\gamma,pm}(H_{2})

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big[r^{H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\bar{u}^{H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\Big(\frac{|u-\bar{u}|^{\gamma}}{(u\bar{u})^{\gamma}}+\frac{|r-\bar{r}|^{\gamma}}{(r\bar{r})^{\gamma}}\Big)\Big]^{\sum_{j=1}^{2pm}\bar{\varepsilon}_{[j]}}

\displaystyle\quad\times(\bar{s}-r)^{(H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\sum_{j=1}^{2pm}\bar{\varepsilon}_{[j]}-2(|\bar{\alpha}|+pmd)H_{1}+2pm}

\displaystyle\quad\times(\bar{t}-u)^{(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\sum_{j=1}^{2pm}\bar{\varepsilon}_{[j]}-2(|\bar{\alpha}|+pmd)H_{2}+2pm},

where

\displaystyle\Pi_{\gamma,pm}(H_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\prod\limits_{j=2}^{2pm}\Gamma\left(1-(|\bar{\alpha}_{[j]}|+d)H_{1}\right)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2pm}\Gamma\left((H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\bar{\varepsilon}_{[j]}-(|\bar{\alpha}_{[j]}|+d)H_{1}+1\right)}{\Gamma\left((H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2pm}\bar{\varepsilon}_{[j]}-2(|\bar{\alpha}|+pmd)H_{1}+2pm+1\right)}

and

\displaystyle\Pi_{\gamma,pm}(H_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\prod\limits_{j=2}^{2pm}\Gamma\left(1-(|\bar{\alpha}_{[j]}|+d)H_{2}\right)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{2pm}\Gamma\left((H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\bar{\varepsilon}_{[j]}-(|\bar{\alpha}_{[j]}|+d)H_{2}+1\right)}{\Gamma\left((H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2pm}\bar{\varepsilon}_{[j]}-2(|\bar{\alpha}|+pmd)H_{2}+2pm+1\right)}.

Now observe that
\sum_{j=1}^{2pm}\bar{\varepsilon}_{[j]}=2p\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}
,
|\bar{\alpha}|=p|\alpha|
,
|\bar{\alpha}^{(\ell)}|=p|\alpha^{(\ell)}|
for every
\ell\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
and for a large enough positive constant
C_{2}
,

Theorem 8

\{b_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subset C^{\infty}_{0}([0,T]^{2}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{d}

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\int_{[\epsilon,s]^{2}\times[\epsilon,t]^{2}}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[\|D_{r,u}X^{x,n}_{s,t}-D_{\bar{r},\bar{u}}X^{x,n}_{s,t}\|^{2}\right]}{(|r-\bar{r}|+|u-\bar{u}|)^{2+2\beta}}\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\bar{u}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\bar{r}

\displaystyle D_{r,u}X^{x,n}_{s,t}=K_{H}(s,t,r,u)I_{d}+\int_{r}^{s}\int_{u}^{t}b_{n}^{\prime}(s_{1},t_{1},X^{x,n}_{s_{1},t_{1}})D_{r,u}X^{x,n}_{s_{1},t_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1},

\displaystyle D_{r,u}X^{x,n}_{s,t}-D_{\bar{r},\bar{u}}X^{x,n}_{s,t}

\displaystyle(K_{H}(s,t,r,u)I_{d}-K_{H}(s,t,\bar{r},\bar{u})I_{d})-(D_{\bar{r},\bar{u}}X^{x,n}_{s,u}-K_{H}(s,u,\bar{r},\bar{u})I_{d})

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(v_{1})\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}\text{Var}\Big[\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}v_{j}Z_{j}\Big]\Big)\,\mathrm{d}v_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}v_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{(n-1)/2}}{(\text{det }\text{Cov}(Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}))^{1/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}g\Big(\frac{v}{\sigma_{1}}\Big)\exp\Big(-\frac{1}{2}v^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}v,

where
\sigma_{1}^{2}:=\text{Var}[Z_{1}|Z_{2},\ldots,Z_{n}]
is the conditional variance of
Z_{1}
given
Z_{2},\,\ldots,\,Z_{n}
.

The following estimate is a special case of

Lemma 13

\displaystyle\geq\text{det Cov}\left(B^{H_{1}}_{s_{1}},\,\ldots,\,B^{H_{1}}_{s_{n}}\right)\times\text{det Cov}\left(B^{H_{2}}_{t_{1}},\,\ldots,\,B^{H_{2}}_{t_{n}}\right),

Here is a compactness criterion for subsets of
L^{2}(\Omega)
that has been proved in
[
20

.

Theorem 14
.

Let
\{(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P});H\}
be a Gaussian probability space, that is
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},\mathbb{P})
is a probability space and
H
a separable closed subspace of Gaussian random variables of
L^{2}(\Omega)
, which generate the
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{A}
. Denote by
\mathbf{D}
the derivative operator acting on elementary smooth random variables in the sense that

\displaystyle\mathbf{D}(f(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n}))=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\partial_{i}f(h_{1},\ldots,h_{n})h_{i},\quad h_{i}\in H,\,f\in C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Further let
\mathbb{D}^{1,2}
be the closure of the family of elementary smooth random variables with respect to the norm

\displaystyle\|F\|_{1,2}:=\|F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|\mathbf{D}F\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;H)}.

Assume that
C
is a self-adjoint compact operator on
H
with dense image. Then for any
c>0
the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}=\left\{G\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}:\,\|G\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|C^{-1}\mathbf{D}G\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;H)}\leq c\right\}

is relatively compact in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

In order to apply the above result, we consider the fractional Sobolev space:

\mathbb{G}^{2}_{\beta}(U;\mathbb{R}):=\Big\{g\in L^{2}(U,\mathbb{R}):\,\int_{U}\int_{U}\frac{|g(u)-g(u^{\prime})|^{2}}{|u-u^{\prime}|^{p+2\beta}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}<\infty\Big\},

where
U
is a domain of
\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
p\geq 1
and the norm is given by

\|g\|_{\mathbb{G}^{2}_{\beta}(U;\mathbb{R})}:=\|g\|_{L^{2}(U;\mathbb{R})}+\Big(\int_{U}\int_{U}\frac{|g(u)-g(u^{\prime})|^{2}}{|u-u^{\prime}|^{p+2\beta}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}\Big)^{1/2}.

We need the next compact embedding result from
[
42
, Lemma 10]
(see also
[
22
, Theorem 7.1]
).

Lemma 15
.

Let
p\geq 1
,
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{p}
be a Lipschitz bounded open set and
\mathcal{J}
be a bounded subset of
L^{2}(U;\mathbb{R})
. Suppose that

\sup\limits_{g\in\mathcal{J}}\int_{U}\int_{U}\frac{|g(u)-g(u^{\prime})|^{2}}{|u-u^{\prime}|^{p+2\beta}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}u^{\prime}<\infty

for some
\beta\in(0,1)
. Then
\mathcal{J}
is relatively compact in
L^{2}(U;\mathbb{R})
.

As a consequence of Theorem
14
and Lemma
15
, we have the following compactness criterion for subsets in the space

.

Corollary 16
.

Denote by
\mathcal{F}_{T}
the
\sigma
-algebra generated by the
d
-dimensional fractional Brownian sheet
W^{H}=(W^{H,(1)},\ldots,W^{H,(d)})
with Hurst index
H=(H_{1},H_{2})
. Let
(X^{(n)},n\in\mathbb{N})
be a sequence of
(\mathcal{F}_{T},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
-measurable random variables and let
D_{r,u}
be the Malliavin derivative associated with the random vector
W^{H}_{r,u}=(W_{r,u}^{H,(1)},\ldots,W_{r,u}^{H,(d)})
. Suppose

(C.1)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|X^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})}<\infty\,\text{ and }\,\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|D_{\cdot,\cdot}X^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega\times[\epsilon,s]\times[\epsilon,t],\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}<\infty

as well as

(C.2)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{[\epsilon,s]^{2}\times[\epsilon,t]^{2}}\frac{\mathbb{E}\left[|D_{r,u}X^{(n)}-D_{\bar{r},\bar{u}}X^{(n)}|^{2}\right]}{(|{}r-\bar{r}|{}+|{}u-\bar{u}|{})^{2+2\beta}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\bar{u}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\bar{r}<\infty

for some
(s,t)\in(0,T]^{2}
and
\epsilon\in(0,\min\{s,t\})
, where
\beta\in(0,1)
is independent of
\epsilon,\,s,\,t
and
|\cdot|
denotes any matrix norm. Then
(X^{(n)},n\in\mathbb{N})
is relatively compact in
L^{2}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

The proof is inspired from
[
27
, Section 5]
. We consider the symmetric form
\mathcal{L}
on
L^{2}([\epsilon,s]\times[\epsilon,t],\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f,g)=

\displaystyle\int_{[\epsilon,s]\times[\epsilon,t]}f(r,u)\cdot g(r,u)\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}u

\displaystyle+\int_{([\epsilon,s]\times[\epsilon,t])^{2}}\frac{(f(r,u)-f(\bar{r},\bar{u}))\cdot(g(r,u)-g(\bar{r},\bar{u}))}{(|r-\bar{r}|+|u-\bar{u}|)^{2+2\beta}}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\bar{r}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\bar{u}

for functions
f
,
g
in the dense domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})\subset L^{2}([\epsilon,s]\times[\epsilon,t],\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and a fixed
\beta\in(0,1)
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})=

\displaystyle\Big\{g:\,\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}([\epsilon,s]\times[\epsilon,t],\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{([\epsilon,s]\times[\epsilon,t])^{2}}\frac{|g(r,u)-g(\bar{r},\bar{u})|^{2}}{(|r-\bar{r}|+|u-\bar{u}|)^{2+2\beta}}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\bar{r}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\bar{u}<\infty\Big\}.

Then
\mathcal{L}
is a positive symmetric closed form and, by Kato’s first representation theorem (see e.g.
[
28
]
), one can find a positive self-adjoint operator
T_{\mathcal{L}}
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(f,g)=\langle f,T_{\mathcal{L}}g\rangle_{L^{2}([\epsilon,s]\times[\epsilon,t],\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}

for all
g\in\mathcal{D}(T_{\mathcal{L}})
and
f\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})
. Further, one may observe that the form
\mathcal{L}
is bounded from below by a positive number. Indeed,

(C.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(g,g)\geq\|g\|_{L^{2}([\epsilon,s]\times[\epsilon,t],\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})}

for all
g\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})
. Hence, we have that
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})=\mathcal{D}(T^{1/2}_{\mathcal{L}})
.
Now, define the operator
A
as
A=T^{1/2}_{\mathcal{L}}
. It follows from Lemma 1 in
[
33
, Section 1]
(see also
[
27
, Lemma 9]
) and Lemma
15

applied to
p=2
,
U=[\epsilon,s]\times[\epsilon,t]
that
A
has a discrete spectrum and a compact inverse
A^{-1}
. Then, using (C1)and (C2), the operator
A
and the sequence
(X^{(n)},n\in\mathbb{N})
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
14
.
.
∎

In order to apply the preceding compact criterion we will need the following estimates.

Lemma 17
.

Let
H=(H_{1},H_{2})\in(0,1/2)
and
(s,t)\in[0,T]^{2}
be fixed. Then, there exists
\beta\in(0,1/2)
such that

(C.4)

\displaystyle\int_{[0,s]^{2}}\int_{[0,t]^{2}}\frac{|K_{H}(s,t,r,u)-K_{H}(s,t,\bar{r},\bar{u})|^{2}}{(|r-\bar{r}|+|u-\bar{u}|)^{2+2\beta}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\bar{u}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\bar{r}<\infty.

Proof.

Let
H=(H_{1},H_{2})\in(0,1/2)
,
0\leq\bar{r},r\leq s\leq T
and
0\leq\bar{u},u\leq t\leq T
, we have

\displaystyle|K_{H}(s,t,r,u)-K_{H}(s,t,\bar{r},\bar{u})|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|K_{H_{1}}(s,r)K_{H_{2}}(t,u)-K_{H_{1}}(s,\bar{r})K_{H_{2}}(t,\bar{u})|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|K_{H_{1}}(s,r)(K_{H_{2}}(t,u)-K_{H_{2}}(t,\bar{u}))+(K_{H_{1}}(s,r)-K_{H_{1}}(s,\bar{r}))K_{H_{2}}(t,\bar{u})\right|^{2}

(C.5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left(|K_{H_{1}}(s,r)|^{2}|K_{H_{2}}(t,u)-K_{H_{2}}(t,\bar{u})|^{2}+|K_{H_{1}}(s,r)-K_{H_{1}}(s,\bar{r})|^{2}|K_{H_{2}}(t,\bar{u})|^{2}\right).

By Lemma A.4 in
[
8
]
, there exists
\beta_{1},\,\beta_{2}\in(0,1/2)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,s]^{2}}\frac{|K_{H_{1}}(s,r)-K_{H_{1}}(s,\bar{r})|^{2}}{|r-\bar{r}|^{1+2\beta_{1}}}\,\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\bar{r}<\infty

and

\displaystyle\int_{[0,t]^{2}}\frac{|K_{H_{2}}(t,u)-K_{H_{2}}(t,\bar{u})|^{2}}{|u-\bar{u}|^{1+2\beta_{2}}}\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\bar{u}<\infty.

Let
\beta\in(0,\min\{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\})
. Applying Young’s inequality
xy\leq\frac{1}{\eta}x^{\eta}+\frac{1}{\delta}x^{\delta}
with
\eta=\frac{2+2\beta}{1+2\beta_{1}}
,
\delta=\frac{\eta}{\eta-1}=\frac{2+2\beta}{1-2(\beta_{1}-\beta)}
,
x=|r-\bar{r}|^{1/\delta}
,
y=|u-\bar{u}|^{1/\eta}
, we have

(C.6)

\displaystyle\frac{|r-\bar{r}|+|u-\bar{u}|}{\min\{\eta,\delta\}}\geq\frac{|r-\bar{r}|}{\delta}+\frac{|u-\bar{u}|}{\eta}\geq|r-\bar{r}|^{1/\delta}|u-\bar{u}|^{1/\eta}.

We also have the following estimates (see e.g.
[
8
, Page 45]
)

(C.7)

\displaystyle|K_{H_{1}}(s,r)|\leq C_{H_{1},T}|s-r|^{H_{1}-1/2}r^{H_{1}-1/2}

and

(C.8)

\displaystyle|K_{H_{2}}(t,\bar{u})|\leq C_{H_{2},T}|t-\bar{u}|^{H_{1}-1/2}\bar{u}^{H_{1}-1/2}

for every
0<r<s<T
,
0<\bar{u}<t<T
and some positive constants
C_{H_{1},T},\,C_{H_{2},T}
.
We deduce from (
C.6
) and (
C.7
) that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,s]^{2}}\int_{[0,t]^{2}}\frac{|K_{H_{1}}(s,r)|^{2}|K_{H_{2}}(t,u)-K_{H_{2}}(t,\bar{u})|^{2}}{(|r-\bar{r}|+|u-\bar{u}|)^{2+2\beta}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\bar{u}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\bar{r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\eta,\delta}\Big(\int_{[0,s]^{2}}\frac{|K_{H_{1}}(s,r)|^{2}}{|r-\bar{r}|^{(2+2\beta)/\delta}}\,\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\bar{r}\Big)\Big(\int_{[0,t]^{2}}\frac{|K_{H_{2}}(t,u)-K_{H_{2}}(t,\bar{u})|^{2}}{|u-\bar{u}|^{(2+2\beta)/\eta}}\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\bar{u}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\eta,\delta}\Big(\int_{[0,s]^{2}}\frac{|K_{H_{1}}(s,r)|^{2}}{|r-\bar{r}|^{1-2(\beta_{1}-\beta)}}\,\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\bar{r}\Big)\Big(\int_{[0,t]^{2}}\frac{|K_{H_{2}}(t,u)-K_{H_{2}}(t,\bar{u})|^{2}}{|u-\bar{u}|^{1+2\beta_{1}}}\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\bar{u}\Big)

(C.9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\eta,\delta}C^{2}_{H_{1},T}\Big(\int_{[0,s]^{2}}|s-r|^{2H_{1}-1}r^{2H_{1}-1}|r-\bar{r}|^{2(\beta_{1}-\beta)-1}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{r}\mathrm{d}r\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\Big(\int_{[0,t]^{2}}\frac{|K_{H_{2}}(t,u)-K_{H_{2}}(t,\bar{u})|^{2}}{|u-\bar{u}|^{1+2\beta_{1}}}\,\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\bar{u}\Big)<\infty.

Similarly, using (
C.8
) we show that

(C.10)

\displaystyle\int_{[0,s]^{2}}\int_{[0,t]^{2}}\frac{|K_{H_{1}}(s,r)-K_{H_{1}}(s,\bar{r})|^{2}|K_{H_{2}}(t,\bar{u})|^{2}}{(|r-\bar{r}|+|u-\bar{u}|)^{2+2\beta}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\bar{u}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\bar{r}<\infty.

It follows from (
C
), (
C.9
) and (
C.10
) that

\displaystyle\int_{[0,s]^{2}}\int_{[0,t]^{2}}\frac{|K_{H}(s,t,r,u)-K_{H}(s,t,\bar{r},\bar{u})|^{2}}{(|r-\bar{r}|+|u-\bar{u}|)^{2+2\beta}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\bar{u}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\bar{r}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\Big\{\int_{[0,s]^{2}}\int_{[0,t]^{2}}\frac{|K_{H_{1}}(s,r)|^{2}|K_{H_{2}}(t,u)-K_{H_{2}}(t,\bar{u})|^{2}}{(|r-\bar{r}|+|u-\bar{u}|)^{2+2\beta}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\bar{u}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\bar{r}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{[0,s]^{2}}\int_{[0,t]^{2}}\frac{|K_{H_{1}}(s,r)-K_{H_{1}}(s,\bar{r})|^{2}|K_{H_{2}}(t,\bar{u})|^{2}}{(|r-\bar{r}|+|u-\bar{u}|)^{2+2\beta}}\mathrm{d}u\mathrm{d}\bar{u}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}\bar{r}\Big\}<\infty,

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 18
.

Let
\epsilon>0
,
H=(H_{1},H_{2})\in(0,1/2)^{2}
,
\epsilon\leq r,\bar{r}<s\leq T
,
\epsilon\leq u,\bar{u}<t\leq T

\sigma,\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{m}

for all

such that

\displaystyle\int_{\nabla^{m}_{r,s}}\int_{\nabla^{m}_{u,t}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}|K_{H}(s_{\sigma(j)},t_{\pi(j)},r,u)-K_{H}(s_{\sigma(j)},t_{\pi(j)},\bar{r},\bar{u})|^{\varepsilon_{j}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times|s_{j}-s_{j+1}|^{v_{j}}|t_{j}-t_{j+1}|^{w_{j}}\mathrm{d}t_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{m}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}s_{m}

\displaystyle\leq C^{m}\Pi_{\gamma,m}(v,H_{1})\Pi_{\gamma,m}(w,H_{2})

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big[r^{H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\bar{u}^{H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\Big(\frac{|u-\bar{u}|^{\gamma}}{(u\bar{u})^{\gamma}}+\frac{|r-\bar{r}|^{\gamma}}{(r\bar{r})^{\gamma}}\Big)\Big]^{\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}}

\displaystyle\quad\times(s-r)^{(H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}+m}(t-u)^{(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}+m},

where

\displaystyle\Pi_{\gamma,m}(v,H_{1}):=\frac{\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m}\Gamma\left(1+v_{j}\right)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma\left((H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\varepsilon_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}+v_{j}+1\right)}{\Gamma\Big((H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}+m+1\Big)}

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{(m)}_{\sigma,\pi}:=

\displaystyle\int_{\nabla^{m}_{r,s}}\int_{\nabla^{m}_{u,t}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}|K_{H}(s_{\sigma(j)},t_{\pi(j)},r,u)-K_{H}(s_{\sigma(j)},t_{\pi(j)},\bar{r},\bar{u})|^{\varepsilon_{j}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times|s_{j}-s_{j+1}|^{v_{j}}|t_{j}-t_{j+1}|^{w_{j}}\mathrm{d}t_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{m}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}s_{m}.

[
8

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{(m)}_{\sigma,\pi}\leq

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big(\int_{\nabla^{m}_{r,s}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}(s_{\sigma(j)}-r)^{(H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\varepsilon_{j}}|s_{j}-s_{j+1}|^{v_{j}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}s_{m}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(C^{(2)}_{T,H})^{m}\Big(r^{H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}u^{H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\frac{|u-\bar{u}|^{\gamma}}{(u\bar{u})^{\gamma}}+r^{H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma}\bar{u}^{H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{|r-\bar{r}|^{\gamma}}{(r\bar{r})^{\gamma}}\Big)^{\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big(\int_{\nabla^{m}_{r,s}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}(s_{j}-r)^{(H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{j}}|s_{j}-s_{j+1}|^{v_{j}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}s_{m}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big(\int_{\nabla^{m}_{u,t}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}(t_{j}-r)^{(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\varepsilon^{\pi}_{j}}|t_{j}-t_{j+1}|^{w_{j}}\,\mathrm{d}t_{1}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{m}\Big),

where

and

\displaystyle\Pi_{0,m}(w,H_{2}):=\frac{\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m}\Gamma\left(1+w_{j}\right)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}\Gamma\left((H_{2}-\frac{1}{2})\varepsilon_{\pi^{-1}(j)}+w_{j}+1\right)}{\Gamma\Big((H_{2}-\frac{1}{2})\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{j}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}+m+1\Big)}.

Proof.

Lemma 20
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
,
(H_{1},H_{2})\in(0,1/2)^{2}
,
\varepsilon\leq\bar{u}<u<t\leq T
,
\varepsilon\leq\bar{r}<r<s\leq T
,
\sigma,\,\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{m+\ell}
and
(\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{m})\in\{0,1\}^{m}
be fixed. Let
v_{j},\,w_{j}\leq 0
satisfying

v_{j}+(H_{1}-\frac{1}{2})\varepsilon_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}>-1
and
w_{j}+(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2})\varepsilon_{\pi^{-1}(j)}>-1
for all
j=1,\ldots,m
. Then there exists a finite constant
C=C_{\varepsilon,H,T}>0
such that

(C.26)

\displaystyle\int_{\nabla^{m+\ell}_{\bar{r},s}}\int_{\Delta^{m,\ell}_{\bar{u},u,t}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m+\ell}K_{H}(s_{\sigma(j)},t_{\pi(j)},\bar{r},\bar{u})(s_{j}-s_{j+1})^{v_{j}}(t_{j}-t_{j+1})^{w_{j}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathrm{d}t_{m+\ell}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{m+1}\mathrm{d}t_{m}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{m+\ell}\ldots\mathrm{d}s_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{m+\ell}_{H,T}\Pi_{0,m+\ell}(v,H_{1})\widetilde{\Pi}_{m,\ell}(w,H_{2})(\bar{r}^{H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}\bar{u}^{H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}})^{\sum_{j=1}^{m+\ell}\varepsilon_{j}}

\displaystyle\times(s-\bar{r})^{(H_{1}-\frac{1}{2})\sum_{j=1}^{m+\ell}\varepsilon_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{m+\ell}v_{j}+m+\ell}(t-u)^{(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2})\sum_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{\pi^{-1}(k)}+\sum_{k=1}^{m}w_{k}+m}

\displaystyle\times|u-\bar{u}|^{(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2})\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\varepsilon_{\pi^{-1}(m+j)}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}w_{m+j}+\ell},

where

\displaystyle\Pi_{0,m+\ell}(v,H_{1}):=\frac{\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m+\ell}\Gamma\left(1+v_{j}\right)\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m+\ell}\Gamma\left((H_{1}-\frac{1}{2})\varepsilon_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}+v_{j}+1\right)}{\Gamma\Big((H_{1}-\frac{1}{2})\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m+\ell}\varepsilon_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{m+\ell}v_{j}+m+\ell+1\Big)}

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Pi}_{m,\ell}(w,H_{2}):=

\displaystyle\frac{\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m+\ell}\Gamma\left(1+w_{j}\right)}{\Gamma\Big((H_{2}-\frac{1}{2})\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}\varepsilon_{\pi^{-1}(k)}+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}w_{k}+m+1\Big)}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m+\ell}\Gamma\left((H_{2}-\frac{1}{2})\varepsilon_{\pi^{-1}(j)}+w_{j}+1\right)}{\Gamma\Big((H_{2}-\frac{1}{2})\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\ell}\varepsilon_{\pi^{-1}(m+j)}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\ell}w_{m+j}+\ell+1\Big)}.

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{m,\ell}:=

\displaystyle\int_{\nabla^{m+\ell}_{\bar{r},s}}\int_{\Delta^{m,\ell}_{\bar{u},u,t}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m+\ell}K_{H}(s_{\sigma(j)},t_{\pi(j)},\bar{r},\bar{u})(s_{j}-s_{j+1})^{v_{j}}(t_{j}-t_{j+1})^{w_{j}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\mathrm{d}t_{m+\ell}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{m+1}\mathrm{d}t_{m}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{m+\ell}\ldots\mathrm{d}s_{1}.

Using (
C.11
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{m,\ell}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{m+\ell}(\bar{r}^{H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}}\bar{u}^{H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}})^{\sum_{j=1}^{m+\ell}\varepsilon_{j}}

\displaystyle\times\Big(\int_{\nabla^{m+\ell}_{\bar{r},s}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m+\ell}(s_{j}-\bar{r})^{(H_{1}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{j}}|s_{j}-s_{j+1}|^{v_{j}}\,\mathrm{d}s_{m+\ell}\ldots\mathrm{d}s_{m+1}\mathrm{d}s_{m}\ldots\mathrm{d}s_{1}\Big)

\displaystyle\times\Big(\int_{\Delta^{m,\ell}_{\bar{u},u,t}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m+\ell}(t_{j}-\bar{u})^{(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\varepsilon^{\pi}_{j}}|t_{j}-t_{j+1}|^{w_{j}}\,\mathrm{d}t_{m+\ell}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{m+1}\mathrm{d}t_{m}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{1}\Big),

where
s_{m+\ell+1}=\bar{r}
,
t_{m+\ell+1}=\bar{u}
,
\varepsilon^{\sigma}_{j}=\varepsilon_{\sigma^{-1}(j)}
,
\varepsilon^{\pi}_{j}=\varepsilon_{\pi^{-1}(j)}
for

.

Since
w_{j}\leq 0
,
H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma<0
,
t_{m}-t_{m+1}\geq t_{m}-u
and
t_{j}-\bar{u}\geq t_{j}-u
for all
j=1,\ldots,m
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Delta^{m,\ell}_{\bar{u},u,t}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m+\ell}(t_{j}-\bar{u})^{(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\varepsilon^{\pi}_{j}}|t_{j}-t_{j+1}|^{w_{j}}\,\mathrm{d}t_{m+\ell}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{m+1}\mathrm{d}t_{m}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\nabla^{m}_{u,t}}\int_{\nabla^{\ell}_{\bar{u},u}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m+\ell}(t_{j}-\bar{u})^{(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\varepsilon^{\pi}_{j}}|t_{j}-t_{j+1}|^{w_{j}}\,\mathrm{d}t_{m+\ell}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{m+1}\mathrm{d}t_{m}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\nabla^{m}_{u,t}}(t_{m}-\bar{u})^{(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\varepsilon^{\pi}_{m}}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}(t_{k}-\bar{u})^{(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\varepsilon^{\pi}_{k}}|t_{k}-t_{k+1}|^{w_{k}}

\displaystyle\times\Big(\int_{\nabla^{\ell}_{\bar{u},u}}(t_{m}-t_{m+1})^{w_{m}}\prod\limits_{j={m+1}}^{m+\ell}(t_{j}-\bar{u})^{(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\varepsilon^{\pi}_{j}}|t_{j}-t_{j+1}|^{w_{j}}

\displaystyle\times\mathrm{d}t_{m+\ell}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{m+1}\Big)\mathrm{d}t_{m}\ldots\mathrm{d}t_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\nabla^{m}_{u,t}}(t_{m}-u)^{(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\varepsilon^{\pi}_{m}}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m-1}(t_{k}-u)^{(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\varepsilon^{\pi}_{k}}|t_{k}-t_{k+1}|^{w_{k}}

\displaystyle\times\Big(\int_{\nabla^{\ell}_{\bar{u},u}}(t_{m}-u)^{w_{m}}\prod\limits_{j={m+1}}^{m+\ell}(t_{j}-\bar{u})^{(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\varepsilon^{\pi}_{j}}|t_{j}-t_{j+1}|^{w_{j}}

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\nabla^{m}_{u,t}}\prod\limits_{k=1}^{m}(\check{t}_{k}-u)^{(H_{2}-\frac{1}{2}-\gamma)\varepsilon_{\pi^{-1}(k)}}|\check{t}_{k}-\check{t}_{k+1}|^{w_{k}}\mathrm{d}\check{t}_{m}\ldots\mathrm{d}\check{t}_{1}\Big)

where
\check{t}_{k}=t_{k}
for

. Then the desired estimates follows from Claims 1 and 2 in the proof of Lemma 22. ∎

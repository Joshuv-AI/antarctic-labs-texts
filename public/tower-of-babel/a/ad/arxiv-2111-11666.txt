Applications of p p -harmonic transplantation for functional inequalities involving a Finsler norm
Sadaf Habibi 1, Futoshi Takahashi 2

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove several inequalities such as Sobolev, Poincaré, logarithmic Sobolev, which involve a general norm with accurate information of extremals, and are valid for some symmetric functions. We use Ioku’s transformation, which is a special case of p p -harmonic transplantation, between symmetric functions. Key words: Finsler norm, Functional inequalities, p p -harmonic transplantation. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification: 26D10, 46E35

1.
Introduction

The Sobolev inequality

(1.1)

S_{N,p}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p^{*}}dx\right)^{p/p^{*}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx

which holds for every
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where
N\geq 2
,
1\leq p<N
, and
p^{*}=\frac{Np}{N-p}
,
is one of the fundamental tools in analysis.
The best constant
S_{N,p}
is known as

(1.2)

\begin{cases}S_{N,p}=\pi^{p/2}N\left(\frac{N-p}{p-1}\right)^{p-1}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{N}{p})\Gamma(1+\tfrac{N}{p^{\prime}})}{\Gamma(N)\Gamma(1+\tfrac{N}{2})}\right)^{p/N}\quad 1<p<N,\\
S_{N,1}=\pi^{p/2}\frac{N}{\left(\Gamma(1+\tfrac{N}{2})\right)^{p/N}}\quad p=1,\end{cases}

where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
, see
[
19
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
34
]
.
It is well-known that the best constant for
1<p<N
is achieved by a family of functions of the form

(1.3)

U(x)=\left(a+b|x|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{-\frac{N-p}{p}},\quad a,b>0

and its translation
U(x-x_{0})
for
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
If we replace
\mathbb{R}^{N}
by a domain different from
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, then still Sobolev inequality with the same best constant holds true for functions in
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
,
however, the attainability of the constant is lost.

Recently, Ioku
[
23
]
obtained a new Sobolev type inequality for radially symmetric functions on the ball
B_{R}
with radius
R>0
, which admits extremals for the best constant in the inequality.

Theorem A (Ioku
[
23
]
)

Let
N\geq 2
,
1<p<N
and
p^{*}=\frac{Np}{N-p}
.
Then for any radially symmetric function
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{R})
, the inequality

S_{N,p}\left(\int_{B_{R}}\frac{|v(y)|^{p^{*}}}{\left(1-(\frac{|y|}{R})^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p(N-1)}{N-p}}}dy\right)^{p/p^{*}}\leq\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla v(y)|^{p}dy

holds true.
Here
S_{N,p}
on the left-hand side is the same constant in (
1.2
).
The equality occurs if and only if
v
is of the form

v(y)=\left(a+b\left(|y|^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}}-R^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-N}}\right)^{\frac{p-N}{p}}

for some
a,b>0
.

Ioku obtained the above theorem by exploiting a transformation between radially symmetric functions on
B_{R}
and on the whole
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Then the inequality in Theorem A is the ”disguised” version of (
1.1
) under the transformation.
His transformation is a special case of
p
-harmonic transplantation initiated by J. Hersh
[
22
]
, see also
[
4
]
.
This point of view is pursued in
[
33
]
.
Harmonic transplantation is used to prove the attainability of the supremum of Trudinger-Moser type functional, see
[
20
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
11
]
.
Other applications for functional inequalities, such as Hardy, or Hardy-Sobolev type, see also
[
32
]
,
[
31
]
,
[
30
]
, and the reference therein.

In this paper, we use the same transformation by Ioku,
but between radially symmetric functions on the whole space (resp. on the ball) and
Finsler radially symmetric
functions on the ball (resp. on the whole space).
The resulting inequalities include a general norm (Finsler norm) with the precise information on the extremizers of the best constant involved.
For other inequalities involving Finsler norms, we refer to
[
1
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
26
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
36
]
,
[
37
]
,
[
39
]
,
and the references therein..

2.
An integral formula for Finsler symmetric functions

Let
H:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,+\infty)
,
N\geq 2
be a function such that

H
is convex,
H(\xi)\geq 0
,
H(\xi)=0
if and only if
\xi=0
,
and satisfies

(2.1)

H(t\xi)=|t|H(\xi),\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,\forall t\in\mathbb{R}.

By (
2.1
),
H
must be even:
H(-\xi)=H(\xi)
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
We assume in this paper that
H\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\})
.
We call
H
a
Finsler norm
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Since all norms on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
are equivalent to each other, we see the existence of positive constants
\alpha
and
\beta
such that

\alpha|\xi|\leq H(\xi)\leq\beta|\xi|,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

The dual norm of
H
is the function
H^{0}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to[0,+\infty)
defined by

H^{0}(x)=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\xi\cdot x}{H(\xi)}.

It is well-known that
H^{0}
is also a norm on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and satisfies the inequality

\frac{1}{\beta}|x|\leq H^{0}(x)\leq\frac{1}{\alpha}|x|,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}.

The set

\mathcal{W}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\ |\ H^{0}(x)<1\}

is called the
Wulff ball
, or the
H^{0}
-unit ball, and we denote
\kappa_{N}=\mathcal{H}^{N}(\mathcal{W})
,
where
\mathcal{H}^{N}
denotes the
N
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
We also denote

\mathcal{W}_{r}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\ |\ H^{0}(x)<r\}

for any
r>0
.

Here we just recall some properties of
H
and
H^{0}
.
These will be proven by using the homogeneity property of
H
and
H^{0}
,
see
[
6
]
Lemma 2.1, and Lemma 2.2.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
H
be a Finsler norm on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Then the following properties hold true:

(1)

|\nabla_{\xi}H(\xi)|\leq C
for any
\xi\neq 0
.

(2)

\nabla_{\xi}H(\xi)\cdot\xi=H(\xi)
,
\nabla_{x}H(x)\cdot x=H(x)
for any
\xi\neq 0
,
x\neq 0
.

(3)

\left(\nabla_{\xi}H\right)(t\xi)=\frac{t}{|t|}\left(\nabla_{\xi}H\right)(\xi)
for any
\xi\neq 0
,
t\neq 0
.

(4)

H\left(\nabla_{x}H^{0}(x)\right)=1
.
H^{0}\left(\nabla_{\xi}H(\xi)\right)=1
.

(5)

H^{0}(x)\left(\nabla_{\xi}H\right)\left(\nabla_{x}H^{0}(x)\right)=x
.

For a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
and a Borel set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
the
anisotropic
H
-perimeter
of a set
E
with respect to
\Omega
is defined as

P_{H}(E;\Omega)=\sup\left\{\int_{E\cap\Omega}{\rm div}\sigma dx\ |\ \sigma\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),H^{0}(\sigma(x))\leq 1\right\}.

If
E
is Lipschitz, then it holds
P_{H}(E;\Omega)=\int_{\Omega\cap\partial^{*}E}H(\nu)d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}
,
where
\partial^{*}E
denotes the reduced boundary of the set
E
and
\nu(x)
is the measure theoretic outer unit normal of
\partial^{*}E
(see
[
18
]
).
For more explanation about the anisotropic perimeter, see
[
2
]
and
[
7
]
.

Now, we prove that
P_{H}(\mathcal{W};\mathbb{R}^{N})=N\kappa_{N}
.
If
H^{0}
is Lipschitz, then
\partial\mathcal{W}
is also Lipschitz and
\partial^{*}\mathcal{W}=\partial\mathcal{W}
holds.
In this case, the outer unit normal of
\mathcal{W}
is given by
\nu=\frac{\nabla H^{0}}{|\nabla H^{0}|}
.
Thus

\displaystyle P_{H}(\mathcal{W};\mathbb{R}^{N})

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\mathcal{W}}H(\nu(x))\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\mathcal{W}}H(\frac{\nabla H^{0}}{|\nabla H^{0}|})\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\mathcal{W}}\frac{1}{|\nabla H^{0}|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x).

Here we have used
H(\nabla H^{0}(x))=1
by Proposition
2.1
and the positive 1-homogeniety of
H
.
Similarly, we have

(2.2)

P_{H}(\mathcal{W}_{r};\mathbb{R}^{N})=\int_{\partial\mathcal{W}_{r}}\frac{1}{|\nabla H^{0}|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)

for any
r>0
.
On the other hand,
by the fact
H^{0}(x)\equiv 1
on
\partial\mathcal{W}
, the formula
x\cdot\nabla H^{0}(x)=H^{0}(x)
by Proposition
2.1
, and the divergence theorem,
we have

\displaystyle P_{H}(\mathcal{W};\mathbb{R}^{N})

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\mathcal{W}}\frac{1}{|\nabla H^{0}|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\mathcal{W}}\frac{H^{0}(x)}{|\nabla H^{0}(x)|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\mathcal{W}}\frac{x\cdot\nabla H^{0}(x)}{|\nabla H^{0}(x)|}\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\mathcal{W}}x\cdot\nu\,d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{W}}{\rm div}xdx=N\mathcal{H}^{N}(\mathcal{W}).

Thus we obtain

(2.3)

P_{H}(\mathcal{W};\mathbb{R}^{N})=N\mathcal{H}^{N}(\mathcal{W})=N\kappa_{N}.

Similarly, we have

(2.4)

P_{H}(\mathcal{W}_{r};\mathbb{R}^{N})=N\kappa_{N}r^{N-1}\quad(r>0).

The equality (
2.4
) is proved as follows:
Recall
\mathcal{W}_{r}=\{H^{0}(x)<r\}
for
r>0
.
Then
y=x/r\in\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{1}
for
x\in\mathcal{W}_{r}
by the homogeniety of
H^{0}
.
On the other hand, since
\nabla H^{0}
is
0
-homogeneous, we have
\nabla H^{0}(x)=\nabla H^{0}(y)
.
Thus by (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle P_{H}(\mathcal{W}_{r};\mathbb{R}^{N})

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial\mathcal{W}_{r}}\frac{d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)}{|\nabla H^{0}(x)|}=\int_{y\in\partial\mathcal{W}}\frac{d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(ry)}{|\nabla H^{0}(ry)|}

\displaystyle=r^{N-1}\int_{y\in\partial\mathcal{W}}\frac{d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(y)}{|\nabla H^{0}(y)|}=r^{N-1}P_{H}(\mathcal{W}).

In the following,
we call a function
g
of the form
g(x)=h(H^{0}(x))
for some
h:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
as
H^{0}
-symmetric , or
Finsler symmetric
function.

The following is a key fact in this paper.
Though the statement is widely known, we prove it here for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 2.2
.

(Polar formula)

Let
h:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that
h\circ H^{0}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Then it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{H^{0}(x)<t}h(H^{0}(x))dx

\displaystyle=P_{H}(\mathcal{W};\mathbb{R}^{N})\int_{0}^{t}h(s)s^{N-1}ds

(2.5)

\displaystyle=N\kappa_{N}\int_{0}^{t}h(s)s^{N-1}ds.

In particular, if
h\circ H^{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}h(H^{0}(x))dx=N\kappa_{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}h(s)s^{N-1}ds

holds.

For the proof of Proposition
2.2
, we use the coarea formula in the following form.

Theorem 2.3
.

(Coarea formula)

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
be Lipschitz and let
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Then it holds that

(2.6)

\int_{f(x)<t}g(x)dx=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{f(x)=s}\frac{g(x)}{|\nabla f(x)|}d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)ds.

See for example
[
18
]
§3.4.4, or
[
8
]
§13.4.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

For
h:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
as above, put
g(x)=h(H^{0}(x))
,
f(x)=H^{0}(x)
in Theorem
2.3
.
Then (
2.6
), (
2.2
), (
2.4
), and (
2.3
) yield that

\displaystyle\int_{H^{0}(x)<t}h(H^{0}(x))dx

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{coarea}}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{H^{0}(x)=s}\frac{h(H^{0}(x))}{|\nabla H^{0}(x)|}d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}h(s)\left(\int_{\partial\mathcal{W}_{s}}\frac{1}{|\nabla H^{0}(x)|}d\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(x)\right)ds

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{P_H1}}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{t}h(s)P_{H}(\mathcal{W}_{s};\mathbb{R}^{N})ds

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{P_H3}}}{{=}}\int_{0}^{t}h(s)P_{H}(\mathcal{W};\mathbb{R}^{N})s^{N-1}ds

\displaystyle=P_{H}(\mathcal{W};\mathbb{R}^{N})\int_{0}^{t}h(s)s^{N-1}ds

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{P_H2}}}{{=}}N\kappa_{N}\int_{0}^{t}h(s)s^{N-1}ds.

3.
A transformation between symmetric functions

Let
u=u(x)
be a radially symmetric function,
thus, there exists a function
U
defined on
[0,+\infty)
such that
u(x)=U(|x|)
.
Also let
v=v(y)
be a Finsler radially symmetric function on
\mathcal{W}_{R}
of the form
v(y)=V(H^{0}(y))
for some
V=V(s)
,
s\in[0,R)
,
where
R>0
be any number.
Let us assume that
u
and
v
are related with each other by the transformation

(3.1)

\begin{cases}&r=|x|,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
&s=H^{0}(y),y\in\mathcal{W}_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
&r^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}}=s^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}}-R^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}},\\
&u(x)=U(r)=V(s)=v(y).\end{cases}

Throughout of the paper,
\omega_{N-1}
denotes the surface measure of the unit sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{N-1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Under the transformation (
3.1
), we have the following equivalence.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
u,v
be as above. Let
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous.
Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx=\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N\kappa_{N}}\int_{\mathcal{W}_{R}}H(\nabla v)^{p}dy,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u(x))dx=\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N\kappa_{N}}\int_{\mathcal{W}_{R}}\frac{F(v(y))}{\left(1-\left(\frac{H^{0}(y)}{R}\right)^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p(N-1)}{N-p}}}dy,

Proof.

By (
3.1
), we see that if
r
runs from
0
to
\infty
, then
s
runs from
0
to
R
, and vice versa.
Also by differentiating the relation
r^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}}=s^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}}-R^{-\frac{N-p}{p-1}}
with respect to
s
,
we have

\left(\frac{p-N}{p-1}\right)r^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}-1}\left(\frac{dr}{ds}\right)=\left(\frac{p-N}{p-1}\right)s^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}-1},

which implies

\frac{dr}{ds}=(r(s))^{\frac{N-1}{p-1}}s^{\frac{1-N}{p-1}},\quad\frac{ds}{dr}=(s(r))^{\frac{N-1}{p-1}}r^{\frac{1-N}{p-1}}.

Since
U^{\prime}(r)=V^{\prime}(s)(\frac{ds}{dr})
, we compute

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|U^{\prime}(r)|^{p}r^{N-1}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}|V^{\prime}(s)|^{p}\left(\frac{ds}{dr}\right)^{p}r(s)^{N-1}\left(\frac{dr}{ds}\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}|V^{\prime}(s(r))|^{p}\left(\frac{ds}{dr}\right)^{p-1}r^{N-1}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}|V^{\prime}(s)|^{p}\left(\frac{ds}{dr}\right)^{p-1}r(s)^{N-1}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}|V^{\prime}(s)|^{p}\left(s^{\frac{N-1}{p-1}}r(s)^{\frac{1-N}{p-1}}\right)^{p-1}r(s)^{N-1}ds

(3.2)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}|V^{\prime}(s)|^{p}s^{N-1}ds.

Now, for
v(y)=V(H^{0}(y))
,
y\in\mathcal{W}_{R}
, we compute

\displaystyle\nabla v(y)=V^{\prime}(H^{0}(y))\nabla H^{0}(y),

(3.3)

\displaystyle H\left(\nabla v(y)\right)=H\left(V^{\prime}(H^{0}(y))\nabla H^{0}(y)\right)=|V^{\prime}(H^{0}(y)|H(\nabla H^{0}(y))=|V^{\prime}(H^{0}(y)|,

here we used Proposition
2.1

Recalling Proposition
2.2
(
2.2
) with
h(s)=|V^{\prime}(s)|^{p}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx

\displaystyle=\omega_{N-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}|U^{\prime}(r)|^{p}r^{N-1}dr

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{E1}}}{{=}}\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N\kappa_{N}}N\kappa_{N}\int_{0}^{R}|V^{\prime}(s)|^{p}s^{N-1}ds

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Polar}}}{{=}}\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N\kappa_{N}}\int_{\mathcal{W}_{R}}|V^{\prime}(H^{0}(y))|^{p}dy

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{E2}}}{{=}}\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N\kappa_{N}}\int_{\mathcal{W}_{R}}H(\nabla v)^{p}dy.

On the other hand, we compute

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}F(U(r))r^{N-1}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}F(V(s))r(s)^{N-1}\left(\frac{dr}{ds}\right)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}F(V(s))r(s)^{N-1}r(s)^{\frac{N-1}{p-1}}s^{\frac{1-N}{p-1}}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}F(V(s))r(s)^{(N-1)(1+\frac{1}{p-1})}s^{\frac{1-N}{p-1}+1-N}s^{N-1}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}F(V(s))\left(\left(s^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}}-R^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-N}}\right)^{(N-1)(\frac{p}{p-1})}s^{\frac{p(1-N)}{p-1}}s^{N-1}ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}\frac{F(V(s))}{s^{\frac{p(N-1)}{p-1}}\left(s^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}}-R^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p(N-1)}{N-p}}}s^{N-1}ds

(3.4)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}\frac{F(V(s))}{\left(1-\left(\frac{s}{R}\right)^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p(N-1)}{N-p}}}s^{N-1}ds.

Thus again Proposition
2.2
yields that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u(x))dx

\displaystyle=\omega_{N-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}F(U(r))r^{N-1}dr

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{E3}}}{{=}}\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N\kappa_{N}}N\kappa_{N}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{F(V(s))}{\left(1-\left(\frac{s}{R}\right)^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p(N-1)}{N-p}}}s^{N-1}ds

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{Polar}}}{{=}}\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N\kappa_{N}}\int_{\mathcal{W}_{R}}\frac{F(v(y))}{\left(1-\left(\frac{H^{0}(y)}{R}\right)^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p(N-1)}{N-p}}}dy.

Next, let us replace the roles of
u
and
v
in Proposition
3.1
.
That is, let
v=v(y)
be a radially symmetric function on
B_{R}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\ :\ |x|<R\}
, here
R>0
.
Thus, there exists a function
V
defined on
[0,R)
such that
v(y)=V(|y|)
.
Also let
u=u(x)
be a Finsler radially symmetric function on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
of the form
u(x)=U(H^{0}(x))
,
U=U(r)
,
r\in[0,+\infty)
.
Assume
u
and
v
are related by the transformation

(3.5)

\begin{cases}&r=H^{0}(x),x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
&s=|y|,y\in B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
&r^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}}=s^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}}-R^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}},\\
&u(x)=U(r)=V(s)=v(y).\end{cases}

Then as before, under the transformation (
3.5
), we have the following equivalence.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
u,v
be as above. Let
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous.
Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla v|^{p}dy=\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N\kappa_{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H(\nabla u)^{p}dx,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}F(v(y))dy=\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N\kappa_{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{F(u(x))}{\left(1+\left(\frac{H^{0}(x)}{R}\right)^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p(N-1)}{N-p}}}dx.

The proof is similar as above, and we omit it here.

4.
Functional inequalities for symmetric functions

In this section, we will prove several functional inequalities which hold for functions in the appropriate Sobolev space with some symmetry.
Though many similar inequalities can be derived by the same idea, we record here few of them.

Following inequalities are direct consequences of Proposition
3.1
, Proposition
3.2
,
and the known inequalities on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
or
B_{R}
, with the information of extremals.

4.1.
The sharp
L^{p}
-Sobolev inequality

First we treat the sharp
L^{p}
-Sobolev inequality.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
1<p<N
and
p^{*}=\frac{Np}{N-p}
.
Then for any Finsler radially symmetric function
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathcal{W}_{R})
, the inequality

\tilde{S}_{N,p}\left(\int_{\mathcal{W}_{R}}\frac{|v(y)|^{p^{*}}}{\left(1-(\frac{H^{0}(y)}{R})^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p(N-1)}{N-p}}}dy\right)^{p/p^{*}}\leq\int_{\mathcal{W}_{R}}H(\nabla v(y))^{p}dy

holds true.
Here

\tilde{S}_{N,p}=S_{N,p}\left(\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N\kappa_{N}}\right)^{p/p^{*}-1}

and
S_{N,p}
is defined in (
1.2
).
The equality occurs if and only if
v
is of the form

v(y)=\left(a+b\left((H^{0}(y))^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}}-R^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p}{p-N}}\right)^{\frac{p-N}{p}}

for some
a,b>0
.

Proof.

For any Finsler radially symmetric function
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\mathcal{W}_{R})
, define
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
u(x)=U(r)=V(s)=v(y)
, where

r=|x|
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
s=H^{0}(y)
,
y\in\mathcal{W}_{R}
.

U
and
V
are defining functions of
u,v
respectively.
Then the
L^{p}
-Sobolev inequality (
1.1
) for
u
(with the information of extremals) and Proposition
3.1
yield the result.

4.2.
Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequalities

The following optimal Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality was established by del Pino and Dolbeault
[
14
]
, which includes the sharp
L^{2}
-Sobolev inequality as a special case.
Let
q>1
if
N=2
and
1<q\leq\frac{N}{N-2}
if
N\geq 3
. Then for any function
u\in L^{q+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{2q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\nabla u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
the inequality

(4.1)

\|u\|_{L^{2q}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq A\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{q+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{1-\theta}

holds true where

\frac{1}{2q}=\theta\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{N}\right)+(1-\theta)\frac{1}{q+1},

i.e.,

(4.2)

\theta=\frac{N(q-1)}{q(N+2-(N-2)q)},

and

(4.3)

A=\left(\frac{(q-1)(q+1)}{2\pi N}\right)^{\theta/2}\left(\frac{2(q+1)-N(q-1)}{2(q+1)}\right)^{1/(2q)}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\frac{q+1}{q-1})}{\Gamma(\frac{q+1}{q-1}-\frac{N}{2})}\right)^{\theta/N}.

A
is optimal and the equality in (
4.1
) holds if and only if
u
is a constant multiple and translation of functions

(4.4)

\phi_{\sigma}(x)=\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}+|x|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}},\quad\sigma>0.

Note that the sharp
L^{2}
-Sobolev inequality is recovered when
q=\frac{N}{N-2}
.

By the same argument in proving Theorem
4.1
, but in this case we use the transformation

\begin{cases}&r=|x|,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
&s=H^{0}(y),y\in\mathcal{W}_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
&r^{2-N}=s^{2-N}-R^{2-N},\\
&u(x)=U(r)=V(s)=v(y),\end{cases}

we obtain the following:

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
N\geq 3
and
1<q\leq\frac{N}{N-2}
.
Then for any Finsler radially symmetric function
v\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathcal{W}_{R})
, the inequality

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathcal{W}_{R}}\frac{|v(y)|^{2q}}{\left(1-(\frac{H^{0}(y)}{R})^{N-2}\right)^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-2}}}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{2q}}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{A}\left(\int_{\mathcal{W}_{R}}H(\nabla v(y))^{2}dy\right)^{\frac{\theta}{2}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{\mathcal{W}_{R}}\frac{|v(y)|^{q+1}}{\left(1-(\frac{H^{0}(y)}{R})^{N-2}\right)^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-2}}}dy\right)^{\frac{1-\theta}{q+1}}

holds true.
Here
\tilde{A}=\left(\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N\kappa_{N}}\right)^{\frac{\theta}{N}}A
, where
\theta
is in (
4.2
) and
A
is the constant in (
4.3
).
The equality occurs if and only if
v
is of the form

v(y)=C\left(\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}+\left((H^{0}(y))^{2-N}-R^{2-N}\right)^{\frac{2}{2-N}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q-1}},\quad\sigma>0.

for some
\sigma>0
and
C\neq 0
.

Nash’s inequality

(4.5)

\|u\|^{1+2/N}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq B\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|u\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2/N}

which holds true where for any
u\in W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, is also a special case of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality.
Let

\lambda^{N}_{1}(B)=\inf\left\{\frac{\int_{B}|\nabla u|^{2}dx}{\int_{B}|u|^{2}dx}\ |\ u\in W^{1,2}(B),\int_{B}udx=0,\text{$u$ is radially symmetric}\right\}

denote the first non-zero Neumann eigenvalue of
-\Delta
for all radially symmetric functions
u\in W^{1,2}(B)
on the unit ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
with the average zero.
Carlen and Loss
[
9
]
proved that the best constant in the right-hand side is

(4.6)

B=2\left(1+\frac{N}{2}\right)^{1+\frac{2}{N}}N^{-1+\frac{2}{N}}\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{N}_{1}(B)\omega_{N-1}^{2/N}}\right)

and that the equality in (
4.5
) holds if and only if
u
is a constant multiple, scaling, and translation of the function

(4.7)

\Psi(|x|)=\begin{cases}U(|x|)-U(1),&\quad(|x|\leq 1)\\
0,&\quad(|x|\geq 1),\end{cases}

where

U(r)=r^{\frac{2-N}{2}}J_{\frac{N-2}{2}}(\mu r),

here
J_{\nu}
denotes the Bessel function of order
\nu
, and
\mu
is the first positive zero of
J_{\frac{N}{2}}
, which turns out to be the first positive zero of
U^{\prime}(r)
.
By this notation,
\lambda_{1}^{N}(B)
can be written as
\lambda_{1}^{N}(B)=\mu^{2}
.

Then as before, we have the next theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
N\geq 3
.
Then for any Finsler radially symmetric function
v\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\mathcal{W}_{R})
, the inequality

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathcal{W}_{R}}\frac{|v(y)|^{2}}{\left(1-(\frac{H^{0}(y)}{R})^{N-2}\right)^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-2}}}dy\right)^{1+\frac{2}{N}}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{B}\left(\int_{\mathcal{W}_{R}}H(\nabla v(y))^{2}dy\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{\mathcal{W}_{R}}\frac{|v(y)|}{\left(1-(\frac{H^{0}(y)}{R})^{N-2}\right)^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-2}}}dy\right)^{\frac{4}{N}}

holds true.
Here
\tilde{B}=\left(\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N\kappa_{N}}\right)^{\frac{2}{N}}B
, where
B
is the constant in (
4.6
).
The equality occurs if and only if
v
is of the form

v(y)=C\Psi\left(\lambda\left((H^{0}(y))^{2-N}-R^{2-N}\right)^{\frac{1}{2-N}}\right)

for some
\lambda>0
and
C\neq 0
.

4.3.
The Euclidean
L^{p}
-logarithmic Sobolev inequality

The Euclidean
L^{p}
-logarithmic Sobolev inequality states that

(4.1)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{p}\log|u|^{p}dx\leq\frac{N}{p}\log\left(\mathcal{L}_{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\right)

holds for any function
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|f|^{p}dx=1
.
This form of inequality was first proved by Weissler
[
38
]
for
p=2
, Ledoux
[
24
]
for
p=1
, del Pino and Dolbeault
[
15
]
for
1\leq p<N
,
and finally generalized by Gentil
[
21
]
for
1\leq p<\infty
.
Actually, Gentil extends the result in
[
15
]
not only for all
p\geq 1
, but also for any norm on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
other than usual Euclidean norm.
Here the sharp constant
\mathcal{L}_{p}
is given by

(4.2)

\begin{cases}&\mathcal{L}_{1}=\frac{1}{N}\pi^{-1/2}\left(\Gamma(N/2+1)\right)^{1/N},\quad p=1\\
&\mathcal{L}_{p}=\frac{p}{N}\left(\frac{p-1}{e}\right)^{p-1}\pi^{-p/2}\left(\frac{\Gamma(N/2+1)}{\Gamma(N/p^{\prime}+1)}\right)^{p/N},\quad p>1.\end{cases}

where
p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}
for
p>1
.
For
p=1
, Beckner
[
5
]
proved that the extremal functions for (
4.1
) are the characteristic functions of balls.
For
1<p<N
, it is proved in
[
15
]
that the extremal functions of (
4.1
) must be of the form

\displaystyle u(x)=U(|x|)=C(N,p)\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\sigma}|x|^{p^{\prime}}\right)

where
\sigma>0
and

(4.3)

C(N,p)=\left(\pi^{N/2}\left(\frac{\sigma}{p}\right)^{N/p^{\prime}}\frac{\Gamma(N/p^{\prime}+1)}{\Gamma(N/2+1)}\right)^{-1/p},

and its translation.

From this and the same argument as in Theorem
4.1
, we have

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
1\leq p<N
and
R>0
.
Then

\left(\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N\kappa_{N}}\right)\int_{\mathcal{W}_{R}}\frac{|v(y)|^{p}\log|v(y)|^{p}}{\left(1-\left(\frac{H^{0}(y)}{R}\right)^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p(N-1)}{N-p}}}dy\leq\frac{N}{p}\log\left(\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{p}\int_{\mathcal{W}_{R}}H(\nabla v)^{p}dy\right)

holds true for any Finsler radially symmetric function
v
satisfying

\left(\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N\kappa_{N}}\right)\int_{\mathcal{W}_{R}}\frac{|v(y)|^{p}}{\left(1-\left(\frac{H^{0}(y)}{R}\right)^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p(N-1)}{N-p}}}dy=1.

Here
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{p}=\left(\frac{\omega_{N-1}}{N\kappa_{N}}\right)\mathcal{L}_{p}
.
The equality holds if and only if
v
is of the form

\displaystyle v(y)=C(N,p)\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\sigma}\left((H^{0}(y))^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}}-R^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{-p}{N-p}}\right)

for
1<p<N
, where
C(N,p)
is defined in (
4.3
).
When
p=1
, then the extremals are the characteristic functions of Wulff balls.

4.4.
The Poincaré inequality on balls

The
L^{p}
-Poincaré inequality on balls states that

(4.1)

\lambda_{1}(B_{1})\int_{B_{R}}|v(y)|^{p}dy\leq R^{p}\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla v(y)|^{p}dy

holds for any function
v\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{R})
, where
B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a ball with radius
R>0
.
Here,
\lambda_{1}(B_{1})
is the first eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{p}
(
p
-Laplacian) with the Dirichlet boundary condition on the unit ball
B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
To the authors’ knowledge, the explicit expression is not known for
\lambda_{1}(B_{1})
unless
p=2
.
The equality in (
4.1
) holds if and only if
v
is a constant multiple of the first eigenfunction of
-\Delta_{p}
on
B_{R}
, which we denote
\phi_{R}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(B_{R})
.
Known regularity and symmetry results assure that the first eigenfunction of
-\Delta_{p}
is
C^{1,\alpha}
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
and radially symmetric.
Thus we can write
\phi_{R}(y)=\Phi_{R}(|y|)
,
y\in B_{R}
, for some
C^{1}
-function
\Phi_{R}
on
[0,R)
with
\Phi_{R}(R)=0
.

By these facts and Proposition
3.2
, we have the following.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
1\leq p<N
and
R>0
is arbitrarily given.
Then

\lambda_{1}(B_{1})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\left(1+\left(\frac{H^{0}(x)}{R}\right)^{\frac{N-p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p(N-1)}{N-p}}}dx\leq R^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H(\nabla u)^{p}dx

holds true for any Finsler radially symmetric function
u(x)=U(H^{0}(x))\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
u(\infty)=0
.
For fixed
R>0
, the equality holds if and only if
u
is the constant multiple of

\Phi_{R}\left(\left((H^{0}(x))^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}}+R^{\frac{p-N}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p-N}}\right)

where
\Phi_{R}\in C^{1}([0,R))
is such that
\phi_{R}(y)=\Phi_{R}(|y|)
,
y\in B_{R}
, is the first eigenfunction of
-\Delta_{p}
on
B_{R}
.

Proof.

The proof follows for a given Finsler radially symmetric function
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, define a radially symmetric function
v(y)=V(|y|)
as

v(y)=V(s)=U(r)=u(x)
where
s=|y|
,
y\in B_{R}
and
r=H^{0}(x)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
Then use Proposition
3.5
.

4.5.
The sharp
L^{p}
-Sobolev trace inequality

Let
N\geq 3
,
1<p<N
and put
p_{*}=\frac{(N-1)p}{N-p}
.
Let

\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}=\{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\}

denote the upper half space and identify
\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}=\{(x,0)\ |\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
.
The
L^{p}
-sharp Sobolev trace inequality

(4.1)

S_{T,N,p}\left(\int_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u(x,0)|^{p^{*}}dx\right)^{p/p_{*}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{p}dxdt

holds for every
u\in\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\{u\in L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\ |\ \nabla u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\}
, where
p^{*}=\frac{Np}{N-p}
.
The best constant
\tilde{S}_{N,p}
is

(4.2)

S_{T,N,p}=\pi^{(p-1)/2}\left(\frac{N-p}{p-1}\right)^{p-1}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{N-1}{2(p-1)})}{\Gamma(\tfrac{(N-1)p}{2(p-1)})}\right)^{\tfrac{p-1}{N-1}}

see Escobar
[
17
]
for
p=2
and Nazaret
[
28
]
for
p\in(1,N)
.
Also it is proven that the best constant
S_{T,N,p}
is achieved by a family of functions

(4.3)

\phi_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=\varepsilon^{-\frac{N-p}{p}}\phi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},\frac{t}{\varepsilon}\right)=\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{p}}}{(\varepsilon+t)^{2}+|x|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-p}{2(p-1)}}\quad\varepsilon>0

where

\phi(x,t)=\left(\frac{1}{(1+t)^{2}+|x|^{2}}\right)^{\frac{N-p}{2(p-1)}},

and its constant multiple and the translation
C\phi_{\varepsilon}(x-x_{0},t)
for
C\neq 0
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
.

Let
\mathcal{W}^{N-1}_{R}
denote the Wulff ball of radius
R>0
in
\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
:

\mathcal{W}^{N-1}_{R}=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\ |\ H^{0}(y)<R\},

and let
1<p<N-1
,
N\geq 3
.
We relate functions
u=u(x,t)
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{+}
of the form
u(x,t)=U(|x|,t)
, and functions on
v=v(y,t)
on
\mathcal{W}_{R}^{N-1}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}

of the form
v(y,t)=V(H^{0}(y),t)
for some
U
and
V
by the relation

(4.4)

\begin{cases}&r=|x|,x\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1},\\
&s=H^{0}(y),y\in\mathcal{W}^{N-1}_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
&r^{\frac{p-(N-1)}{p-1}}=s^{\frac{p-(N-1)}{p-1}}-R^{\frac{p-(N-1)}{p-1}},\\
&u(x,t)=U(r,t)=V(s,t)=v(y,t).\end{cases}

Under the transformation (
4.4
), we have the following equivalence.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
u,v
be as above.
Put

(4.5)

A_{R}(y)=\left\{1-\left(\frac{H^{0}(y)}{R}\right)^{\frac{N-1-p}{p-1}}\right\}^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N-1-p}},\quad y\in\mathcal{W}^{N-1}_{R}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N-1}}|\nabla_{x,t}u(x,t)|^{p}dxdt

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{N-2}}{(N-1)\kappa_{N-1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathcal{W}^{N-1}_{R}}\left(H(\nabla v)^{2}A_{R}(y)+\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}\right)^{2}\right)^{p/2}A_{R}(y)^{-p/2}dy.

By Proposition
4.6
and Proposition
3.1
(
N
replaced by
N-1
), we have the following Sobolev trace inequality involving the Finsler norm:

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
N\geq 3
,
1<p<N-1
and
p_{*}=\frac{(N-1)p}{N-p}
.
For all functions
v\in W^{1,p}(\mathcal{W}^{N-1}_{R}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
of the form
v(y,t)=V(H^{0}(y),t)
for a function
V=V(s,t)
,
(s,t)\in[0,R)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
the inequality

\displaystyle\tilde{S}_{T,N,p}\left(\int_{\mathcal{W}^{N-1}_{R}}|v(y,0)|^{p_{*}}A_{R}(y)^{-p/2}dy\right)^{p/p_{*}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathcal{W}^{N-1}_{R}}\left(H(\nabla v)^{2}A_{R}(y)+\left(\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}\right)^{2}\right)^{p/2}A_{R}(y)^{-p/2}dy

holds true, where
A_{r}(y)
is defined in (
4.5
) and

\tilde{S}_{T,N,p}=S_{T,N,p}\left(\frac{\omega_{N-2}}{(N-1)\kappa_{N-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-p_{*}}{p_{*}}},

here
S_{T,N,p}
is the same constant in (
4.2
).
The equality occurs if
v
is of the form

v(y,t)=C\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{p}}}{(\varepsilon+t)^{2}+\left((H^{0}(y))^{\frac{p-(N-1)}{p-1}}-R^{\frac{p-(N-1)}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{2(p-1)}{p-(N-1)}}}\right)^{\frac{N-p}{2(p-1)}}

for some
\varepsilon>0
and
C\neq 0
.

Remark 4.8
.

As in the classical case (
4.1
),
it is not known that the functions
v(y,t)
in Theorem
4.7
is the only extremizers or not for the best constant
\tilde{S}_{T,N,p}
,
except for
p=2
.

Proof.

Proof of the theorem is as before: For such a function
v(y,t)=V(H^{0}(y),t)
, define a new function
u(x,t)
by the relation (
4.4
),
and use the sharp
L^{p}
Sobolev trace inequality for
u
. The information of the extremals comes from the transformation and (
4.3
).

4.6.
Trudinger-Moser inequality

We can consider the same type of transformation between
u
and
v
on the
different dimension
of spaces.
For example, let
u=u(x)
be a Finsler radially symmetric function on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\geq 3
,
and let
v=v(y)
be a radially symmetric function on
B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
for some
R>0
.
Let us assume that
u
and
v
are related with each other by the transformation

(4.1)

\begin{cases}&r=H^{0}(x),x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
&s=|y|,y\in B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
&r^{2-N}=\log\frac{R}{s},\\
&u(x)=U(r)=V(s)=v(y).\end{cases}

Under the transformation (
4.1
), we have

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
u,v
be as above. Let
F:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be continuous.
Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla v(y)|^{2}dy=\frac{2\pi}{(N-2)N\kappa_{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H(\nabla u)^{2}dx,

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}F(v(y))dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u(x))W_{H,R}(x)dx,

where

(4.2)

W_{H,R}(x)=\left(\frac{2\pi(N-2)}{N\kappa_{N}}\right)\frac{R^{2}}{(H^{0}(x))^{2(N-1)}\exp\{2(H^{0}(x))^{2-N}\}}.

Proof.

By (
4.1
), we see that if
r
runs from
0
to
\infty
, then
s
runs from
0
to
R
, and vice versa.
Also by differentiating the relation,
we have

\frac{dr}{ds}=\frac{r^{N-1}}{N-2}\frac{1}{s}.

Since
V^{\prime}(s)=U^{\prime}(r)(\frac{dr}{ds})
, we compute

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|\nabla v(y)|^{2}dy

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{0}^{R}|V^{\prime}(s)|^{2}sds

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}|U^{\prime}(r)|^{2}\left(\frac{dr}{ds}\right)^{2}s(r)\left(\frac{ds}{dr}\right)dr

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}|U^{\prime}(r)|^{2}\frac{r^{N-1}}{s(r)(N-2)}s(r)dr

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{(N-2)N\kappa_{N}}N\kappa_{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}|U^{\prime}(r)|^{2}r^{N-1}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{(N-2)N\kappa_{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|U^{\prime}(H^{0}(x))|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi}{(N-2)N\kappa_{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}H(\nabla u)^{2}dx,

here we used

H\left(\nabla u(x)\right)=H\left(U^{\prime}(H^{0}(x))\nabla H^{0}(x)\right)=|U^{\prime}(H^{0}(x)|

in the last equality.
Also, we compute

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}F(v(y))dy

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{0}^{R}F(V(s))sds

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}F(U(r))s(r)\left(\frac{ds}{dr}\right)dr

\displaystyle=2\pi\int_{0}^{\infty}F(U(r))s(r)\frac{(N-2)s(r)}{r^{N-1}}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi(N-2)}{N\kappa_{N}}N\kappa_{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}F(U(r))\frac{s(r)^{2}}{r^{2(N-1)}}r^{N-1}dr

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi(N-2)}{N\kappa_{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u(x))\frac{R^{2}\exp\{-2(H^{0}(x))^{2-N}\}}{(H^{0}(x))^{2(N-1)}}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(u(x))W_{H,R}(x)dx.

Trudinger-Moser inequality
[
27
]
,
[
35
]
on

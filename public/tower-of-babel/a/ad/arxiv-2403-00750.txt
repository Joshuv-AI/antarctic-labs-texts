Edge open packing: complexity, algorithmic aspects, and bounds
Boštjan Brešar and Babak Samadi

Abstract
Given a graph G G , two edges e 1 , e 2 ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) e_{1},e_{2}\in E(G) are said to have a common edge e e if e e joins an endvertex of e 1 e_{1} to an endvertex of e 2 e_{2} . A subset B ⊆ E ⁡ ( G ) B\subseteq E(G) is an edge open packing set in G G if no two edges of B B have a common edge in G G , and the maximum cardinality of such a set in G G is called the edge open packing number, ρ e o ​ ( G ) \rho_{e}^{o}(G) , of G G . In this paper, we prove that the decision version of the edge open packing number is NP-complete even when restricted to graphs with universal vertices, Eulerian bipartite graphs, and planar graphs with maximum degree 4 4 , respectively. In contrast, we present a linear-time algorithm that computes the edge open packing number of a tree. We also resolve two problems posed in the seminal paper [Edge open packing sets in graphs, RAIRO-Oper. Res. 56 (2022) 3765–3776]. Notably, we characterize the graphs G G that attain the upper bound ρ e o ​ ( G ) ≤ | E ⁡ ( G ) | / δ ⁡ ( G ) \rho_{e}^{o}(G)\leq|E(G)|/\delta(G) , and provide lower and upper bounds for the edge-deleted subgraph of a graph and establish the corresponding realization result.

Introduction and preliminaries

Throughout this paper, we consider
G
as a finite simple graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. We use
[
11
]
as a reference for terminology and notation which are not explicitly defined here. The
(open)

neighborhood
of a vertex

is denoted by
N(v)
, and its

is

. The

and

of

are denoted by
\delta(G)
and

, respectively. Given the subsets

, by

we denote the set of edges with one endvertex in
A
and the other in
B
. If
X\subseteq V(G)
, then
G[X]
denotes the subgraph of
G
induced by the vertices of
X
. On the other hand, if
Y\subseteq E(G)
, then
G[Y]
denotes the subgraph of
G
induced by the endvertices of edges in
Y
.

An
induced matching

M
of
G
is a subset
M\subseteq E(G)
such that
M
is a matching in which no two edges are joined by an edge of
G
. The
induced matching number
is the maximum cardinality of an induced matching in
G
. The problem of finding a maximum induced matching was originally introduced by Stockmeyer and Vazirani
[
10
]
(under the name “risk-free marriage problem”) and extensively investigated in literature (see, for instance,
[
1
,
4
,
8
]
and references therein). Unlike for the standard matching problem which is solvable in polynomial time, it was proved in
[
10
]
that the decision version of the induced matching number is an NP-complete problem. In contrast, a linear-time algorithm for determining the induced matching number in trees was found in
[
12
]
.

Chelladurai et al.
[
3
]
introduced the concept of edge open packing in graphs. A subset
B\subseteq E(G)
is an
edge open packing set
(
EOP set
for short) if for each pair of edges
e_{1},e_{2}\in B
, there is no edge from
E(G)
joining an endvertex of
e_{1}
to an endvertex of
e_{2}
. In other words, for any two edges
e_{1},e_{2}\in B
, there is no edge
e\in E(G)
leading to the existence of a path
e_{1}ee_{2}
or a triangle
e_{1}ee_{2}e_{1}
in
G
. The
edge open packing number
(
EOP number
),
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)
, of
G
is the maximum cardinality of an EOP set in
G
. We observe that if
B
is an EOP set (resp. induced matching) of
G
, then
G[B]
is a disjoint union of induced stars (resp.
K_{1,1}
-stars). In this sense, the problem of induced matching can be considered as a variation of the EOP problem. Note that an EOP set is not necessarily a matching, let alone an induced matching.

An
injective
k
-edge coloring
of a graph
G
is an assignment of colors (integers) from
\{1,\ldots,k\}
to the edges of
G
such that any two edges
e_{1},e_{2}\in E(G)
having a common edge (that is an edge
e
with one endvertex common with
e_{1}
and the other endvertex common with
e_{2}
) receive different colors. The minimum number
k
of colors required for an injective edge coloring is called the
injective chromatic index
of
G
, denoted by
\chi^{\prime}_{i}(G)
. The study of this concept was initiated by Cardoso et al. in
[
2
]
and investigated in
[
5
,
9
]
among others. We observe that the color classes of an injective edge coloring of a graph
G
are the very EOP sets of
G
. In fact, the problem of injective edge coloring amounts to the problem of edge partitioning of a graph into EOP sets. Some real-world applications of these two concepts are given in
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
, respectively. In the only paper so far concerned with EOP sets and numbers
[
3
]
, the complexity and algorithmic issues have not been considered. In this paper, we fill this gap by proving three different NP-completeness results in specific families of graphs and constructing a linear-time algorithm resolving the problem in trees.

Section
2
of this paper pertains to the study of computational complexity of the EOP problem. We prove that the decision problem associated with
\rho_{e}^{o}
is NP-complete even when restricted to three special families of graphs, namely, graphs with universal vertices, Eulerian bipartite graphs, and planar graphs with maximum degree
4
. In Section
3
, we prove that the EOP number
\rho_{e}^{o}(T)
and an optimal EOP set for any tree
T
can be computed/constructed in linear time by exhibiting an efficient algorithm for EOP in trees. A version of dynamic programming is used, where four auxiliary parameters, versions of the EOP number, are being computed.
Finally, in Section
4
, we solve two problems posed in
[
3
]
. Firstly, we provide a structural characterization of all graphs
G
attaining the previously proved upper bound
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)\leq|E(G)|/\delta(G)
. Secondly, we analyze the effect of edge removal on the EOP number of a graph
G
, obtaining a lower and an upper bound for
\rho_{e}^{o}(G-e)
, where
e
is an edge of
G
. We also show that all possible values between the bounds can be realized by a graph and some of its edges.

By a
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)
-set and an
\alpha(G)
-set we represent an EOP set and an independent set of

of cardinality
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)
and
\alpha(G)
, respectively, where
\alpha(G)
stands for the independence number of
G

Theorem 2.1
.

Edge Open Packing Problem
is NP-complete even for graphs with universal vertices.

Proof.

The problem clearly belongs to NP because checking that a given subset of edges is indeed an EOP set of cardinality at least
k
can be done in polynomial time.

Suppose that
G
is a graph of order
n
and size
m
. We set
k=m+n+j
. Let
H
be obtained from
G
by adding the new vertices
v,u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m+n}
and joining
v
to all vertices in
V(G)\cup\{u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m+n}\}
. It is easy to observe that the construction of the graph
H
can be accomplished in polynomial time and that

has the universal vertex
v
. Let
I
be an
\alpha(G)
-set. Since
J=I\cup\{u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{m+n}\}
is an independent set in
H

B=\{vw\in E(H):\,w\in J\}
is an EOP in
H
. Therefore,
\rho_{e}^{o}(H)\geq|B|=m+n+\alpha(G)
.

Let
B^{\prime}
be a
\rho_{e}^{o}(H)
-set. Suppose first that
B^{\prime}\cap E(G)\neq\emptyset
and that
xy\in B^{\prime}\cap E(G)
. If there exists an edge
vu_{r}\in B^{\prime}
for some
1\leq r\leq m+n
, then we have the path
u_{r}vxy
in which
u_{r}v,xy\in B^{\prime}
, which is impossible. Therefore,
B^{\prime}\cap\{vu_{i}:\,1\leq i\leq m+n\}=\emptyset
. This, in particular, implies that
|B^{\prime}|\leq|E(H)\setminus\{vu_{i}:\,1\leq i\leq m+n\}|=m+n<m+n+\alpha(G)
, a contradiction. Hence,
B^{\prime}\cap E(G)=\emptyset
.

Suppose that
|B^{\prime}\cap\{vw\in E(H):\,w\in V(G)\}|>\alpha(G)
and
vw_{1},\dots,vw_{\alpha(G)+1}\in B^{\prime}\cap\{vw\in E(H):\,w\in V(G)\}
. Clearly, there exists an edge
w_{r}w_{s}\in E(G)
for some
1\leq r\neq s\leq\alpha(G)+1
. This, in turn, leads to the triangle
vw_{r}w_{s}v
. So, the edges
vw_{r}
and
vw_{s}
have the common edge

, which is a contradiction. This implies that
|B^{\prime}\cap\{vw\in E(H):\,w\in V(G)\}|\leq\alpha(G)
, and hence
\rho_{e}^{o}(H)=|B^{\prime}|\leq m+n+\alpha(G)
. This shows that
\rho_{e}^{o}(H)=m+n+\alpha(G)
.

Our reduction is now completed by taking into account the fact that
\rho_{e}^{o}(H)\geq k
if and only if
\alpha(G)\geq j
. Since
Independent Set Problem
is NP-complete, we have the same for
Edge Open Packing Problem
restricted to the family of graphs with universal vertices.
∎

In contrast to the family of graphs with maximum degree as large as possible (for which the decision problem (

Theorem 2.3
.

Edge Open Packing Problem
is NP-complete even when restricted to planar graphs of maximum degree at most
4
.

Proof.

We only need to prove the NP-hardness of the problem. Let
G
be a graph with
V(G)=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
. For every
1\leq i\leq n
, consider a path
P_{i}:x_{i}z_{i}y_{i}
. Let
G^{\prime}
be obtained from
G
by joining
z_{i}
to
v_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq n
. Note that
G^{\prime}
is planar if and only if
G
is planar. Suppose that
B^{\prime}
is a
\rho_{e}^{o}(G^{\prime})
-set. Let
H
be the subgraph of
G^{\prime}
induced by
B^{\prime}\cap E(G)
. If
H
is the null graph (that is, a graph whose vertex set and edge set are empty), then
B^{\prime}\subseteq\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}
, where
E_{i}=\{z_{i}x_{i},z_{i}y_{i},z_{i}v_{i}\}
. Set
A=\{v_{i}\in V(G):\,z_{i}v_{i}\in B^{\prime}\}
. Since
B^{\prime}
is an EOP set in
G^{\prime}
, it follows that
A
is an independent set in
G
. We then get

\rho_{e}^{o}(G^{\prime})=\big|B^{\prime}\cap\big(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}\big)\big|=\sum_{v_{i}\in A}|B^{\prime}\cap E_{i}|+\sum_{v_{i}\notin A}|B^{\prime}\cap E_{i}|\leq 3|A|+2(n-|A|)\leq 2n+\alpha(G).

Suppose now that
H
is not the null graph. By the definition of an EOP set,
H
is the disjoint union of some induced stars
K_{1,n_{1}},\ldots,K_{1,n_{r}}
with centers
v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{r}}
, respectively (if one of the stars is isomorphic to
K_{2}
, then we consider only one of its vertices as the center). Since
B^{\prime}
is a maximum EOP set in
G^{\prime}
, it necessarily follows that
E(K_{1,n_{t}})\cup\{v_{i_{t}}z_{i_{t}}\}\subseteq B^{\prime}
for all
1\leq t\leq r
. On the other hand, by the definition,
Q=\{v_{i}\in V(G):\,E_{i}\subseteq B^{\prime}\}
is an independent set in
G
. Moreover, there is no vertex in
Q
adjacent to a vertex in
\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}(V(K_{1,n_{i}})\setminus\{v_{n_{i}}\})
. This in particular implies that
|Q|+\sum_{t=1}^{r}n_{t}\leq\alpha(G)
. We now have

\rho_{e}^{o}(G^{\prime})=|B^{\prime}|\leq 3|Q|+\sum_{t=1}^{r}n_{t}+r+2(n-|Q|-\sum_{t=1}^{r}n_{t}-r)=|Q|+2n-\sum_{t=1}^{r}n_{t}-r<2n+\alpha(G).

In either case, we have proved that
\rho_{e}^{o}(G^{\prime})\leq 2n+\alpha(G)
.

On the other hand, let
I
be an
\alpha(G)
-set. We observe that
A^{\prime}=(\bigcup_{v_{i}\in I}E_{i})\cup(\bigcup_{v_{i}\notin I}\{x_{i}z_{i},y_{i}z_{i}\})
is an EOP set in
G^{\prime}
. Hence,
\rho_{e}^{o}(G^{\prime})\geq|A^{\prime}|=2n+\alpha(G)
. This leads to the desired equality
\rho_{e}^{o}(G^{\prime})=2n+\alpha(G)

Our reduction is completed by taking
\rho_{e}^{o}(G^{\prime})\geq k
if and only if
\alpha(G)\geq j
into account, where
k=j+2n
. Because
Independent Set Problem
is NP-complete for planar graphs of maximum degree
3
(see
[
7
]
),
\Delta(G^{\prime})=\Delta(G)+1
, and the fact that
G^{\prime}
is planar if
G
is planar, we have the NP-completeness of
Edge Open Packing Problem
for planar graphs with maximum degree
4
.
∎

An efficient algorithm for edge open packing in trees

We will use the following notation throughout this section.
Let

We need the following versions of optimal EOP sets in a (rooted) tree.

•

\rho_{e}^{o}(T_{v})=\max\{|S|:\,S\textrm{ is an EOP set in }T_{v}\}
;

(This is the standard EOP number of the subtree
T_{v}
. In particular,
\rho_{e}^{o}(T_{r})=\rho_{e}^{o}(T)
.)

•

\rho^{c}(T_{v})=\max\{|S|:\,S\textrm{ is an EOP set in }T_{v}\textrm{, where }v\textrm{ is the center of a star in }T_{v}[S]\}
;

•

\rho^{\ell}(T_{v})=\max\{|S|:\,S\textrm{ is an EOP set in }T_{v}\textrm{, where }v\textrm{ is a leaf of a star in }T_{v}[S]\}
;

•

\rho^{\prime}(T_{v})=\max\{|S|:\,S\textrm{ is an EOP set in }T_{v}\textrm{, where }v\textrm{ is not in }V(T_{v}[S])\}
;

(This is the maximum cardinality of an EOP set
S
in
T_{v}
in which
v
is not incident with an edge of
S
.)

•

\rho^{\prime\prime}(T_{v})=\max\{|S|:\,S\textrm{ is an EOP set in }T_{v}\textrm{, where }N[v]\cap V(T_{v}[S])=\emptyset\}

(This is the maximum cardinality of an EOP set
S
in
T_{v}
such that neither
v
nor any of its children is incident with an edge of
S
.).

Since every EOP set that appears in the definition of
\rho^{\prime\prime}(T_{v})
also satisfies the conditions from the definition of
\rho^{\prime}(T_{v})
, we immediately infer the following relation:

\rho^{\prime}(T_{v})\geq\rho^{\prime\prime}(T_{v})\ \mbox{for each}\ v\in V(T).

(6)

We next state a basic and important remark, which follows from the fact that in an arbitrary EOP set
S
of
T_{v}
one of the following possibilities occurs:
(i)

v
is the center of a star from
T_{v}[S]
,
(ii)

v
is a leaf of a star from
T_{v}[S]
, or
(iii)

v
is not incident with an edge from
S
. (If
v
belongs to a
K_{1,1}
-component of
T_{v}[S]
, then we may consider
v
either as the leaf or as the center of the corresponding star
K_{1,1}
.)

Observation 3.1
.

If
T
is a (rooted) tree and
v\in V(T)
, then

\rho_{e}^{o}(T_{v})=\max\{\rho^{c}(T_{v}),\rho^{\ell}(T_{v}),\rho^{\prime}(T_{v})\}.

From the above observation, one might think that the parameter
\rho^{\prime\prime}(T_{v})
is not needed, yet it turns out that the calculations are easier if we consider it as well. Note that if
v
is a leaf of the tree
T
, then
\rho_{e}^{o}(T_{v})=0
, and so all four parameters are also
0
. For a further simple example, consider the case when
C(v)
consists only of leaves (and so
T_{v}
is a star). Then,
\rho_{e}^{o}(T_{v})=\rho^{c}(T_{v})=|C(v)|
, while
\rho^{\ell}(T_{v})=1
and
\rho^{\prime}(T_{v})=\rho^{\prime\prime}(T_{v})=0
.

Now we will calculate
\rho^{c}(T_{v}),\rho^{\ell}(T_{v}),\rho^{\prime}(T_{v})
and
\rho^{\prime\prime}(T_{v})
by using the values of the parameters in vertices from
C(v)
.

For calculating
\rho^{c}(T_{v})
, we need to define two types of vertices

with respect to the values of
\rho^{\prime}
and
\rho^{\prime\prime}
of the corresponding subtrees
T_{u}
.
Let

. If

\rho^{\prime\prime}(T_{u})+1\geq\rho^{\prime}(T_{u}),

then

is of
Type 1
; otherwise
u
is of
Type 2
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For the rooted tree
T
and

\rho^{c}(T_{v})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in C(v),\\
u\textrm{ Type 1}\end{subarray}}{\hskip-10.0pt(\rho^{\prime\prime}(T_{u})+1)}\,\,+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in C(v),\\
u\textrm{ Type 2}\end{subarray}}{\hskip-8.0pt\rho^{\prime}(T_{u})}&\textrm{if there is some }u\in C(v)\textrm{ of Type 1},\\
\\
\sum\limits_{u\in C(v)}{\hskip-8.0pt\rho^{\prime}(T_{u})}+\min\limits_{u\in C(v)}\{\rho^{\prime\prime}(T_{u})+1-\rho^{\prime}(T_{u})\}&\textrm{if all }u\in C(v)\textrm{ are of Type 2}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Let
S
be an EOP set of
T_{v}
, which satisfies the conditions from the definition of
\rho^{c}(T_{v})
.
That is,

is the center of a star

. Therefore, at least one vertex in

is a leaf of the star

be the set of leaves from

C^{\prime\prime}=C(v)\setminus C^{\prime}
(it is possible that
C^{\prime\prime}=\emptyset
). For each

, let
S_{u}=S\cap E(T_{u})
. Note that

|S|=|C^{\prime}|+\sum_{u\in C(v)}{|S_{u}|}.

Let
u\in C^{\prime}
. Because

is the center of the star

in

and since

, neither
u
nor any of the children of

in

can be incident with an edge in

. We infer that

. On the other hand, if

, then

cannot be incident with an edge in

, yet its children may be. Therefore,

in this case.

First, assume that there exists a vertex in

of Type 1. Now, if

is of Type 2, then

\begin{array}[]{lll}|S|&=&|C^{\prime}|+\hskip-10.0pt\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in C^{\prime},\\

u\textrm{ Type 1}\end{subarray}}{\hskip-10.0pt|S_{u}|}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in C^{\prime\prime},\\
u\textrm{ Type 2}\end{subarray}}{\hskip-10.0pt|S_{u}|}\\

u\textrm{ Type 1}\end{subarray}}{\hskip-10.0pt\rho^{\prime\prime}(T_{u})}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in C^{\prime},\\

u\textrm{ Type 2}\end{subarray}}{\hskip-10.0pt(\rho^{\prime}(T_{u})-1)}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in C^{\prime\prime},\\
u\textrm{ Type 1}\end{subarray}}{\hskip-10.0pt(\rho^{\prime\prime}(T_{u})+1)}+\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in C^{\prime\prime},\\

and the above expression is in turn less than of equal to

Second, assume that all vertices in

\begin{array}[]{lll}|S|&\leq&|C^{\prime}|+\hskip-6.0pt\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in C^{\prime},\\

Lemma 3.3
.

For the rooted tree

\rho^{\ell}(T_{v})=\max\limits_{u\in C(v)}\Big\{\!\max\{\rho^{c}(T_{u}),\rho^{\prime\prime}(T_{u})\}+1+\hskip-8.0pt\sum\limits_{w\in C(v)\setminus\{u\}}\hskip-10.0pt{\rho^{\prime}(T_{w})}\,\Big\}.

Let

, which satisfies the conditions from the definition of

. On the other hand, since

Combining all findings from above, we get

On the other hand, the construction described in the previous paragraph leads to an EOP set

4
Two bounds and realization results

In this section, we first describe the structure of all graphs with given EOP number and minimum degree for which the number of edges is as small as possible. We then study how the EOP number changes under edge removal.

4.1
Extremal graphs for the bound
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)\leq|E(G)|/\delta(G)

Chelladurai et al.
[
3
]
showed that the EOP number of a graph
G
of size
m
can be bounded from above by
m/\delta(G)
. They also posed the open problem of characterizing all graphs for which the equality holds in the upper bound.

In this section, we give a complete solution to the above-mentioned problem. To do so, we define the family
\cal F
as follows. Let
G
be a bipartite graph of minimum degree
k\geq 2
with partite sets
A\cup C
and
B
such that
every vertex in
B
has
1
and
k-1
neighbors in
A
and
C
, respectively.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any graph
G
of size
m
,
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)=m/\delta(G)
if and only if either
G
is a disjoint union of stars or
G\in\cal F
.

Proof.

The equality evidently holds when
G
is isomorphic to a disjoint union of stars. So, let
G\in\cal F
. We observe that the subgraph of
G
induced by
A\cup B
is a disjoint union of induced stars. Therefore,
[A,B]
is an EOP set in
G
. By the structure of
G
, described in the definition
\cal F
, we observe that

\rho_{e}^{o}(G)\geq|[A,B]|=|B|=\dfrac{|B|+(k-1)|B|}{k}=\dfrac{m}{\delta(G)}.

This results in the equality
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)=m/\delta(G)
.

In order to prove the converse implication, we first give a proof of the upper bound
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)\leq m/\delta(G)
for any graph
G
of size
m
. Let
P
be a
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)
-set and let
xy
be an arbitrary edge in
P
. Since
P
is an EOP set of
G
, it follows that at least one of
x
and
y
, say
x
, is adjacent to
\deg(x)-1
vertices in
V(G)\setminus V(G[P])
. This shows that every edge in
P
is adjacent to at least
\delta(G)-1
edges not in
P
. Therefore,

m=|P|+|[V(G[P]),V(G)\setminus V(G[P])]|+|[V(G)\setminus V(G[P]),V(G)\setminus V(G[P])]|\geq|P|+(\delta(G)-1)|P|.

(7)

So,
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)=|P|\leq m/\delta(G)
.

Now let
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)=m/\delta(G)
for a graph
G
, of size
m
and order
n
, different from a disjoint union of stars. Therefore, we necessarily have equality in (
7
). With this in mind, there is no edge with both endvertices in
V(G)\setminus V(G[V(P)])
, for otherwise we end up with
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)<m/\delta(G)
. Note that
G[V(P)]
is a disjoint union of some induced stars
S_{1},\ldots,S_{t}
with centers
a_{1},\ldots,a_{t}
, respectively. Set
A^{\prime}=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{t}\}
,
B^{\prime}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{t}(V(S_{i})\setminus\{a_{i}\})
and
C^{\prime}=V(G)\setminus(A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime})=V(G)\setminus V(G[V(P)])
. Notice that
C^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
because
G
is different from a disjoint union of induced stars. Together the equality in (
7
) and the fact that
P
is an EOP set in
G
imply that every vertex in
B^{\prime}
is adjacent to precisely
\delta(G)-1
vertices in
C^{\prime}
and to exactly one vertex in
A^{\prime}
(in particular, this shows that
\delta(G)\geq 2
). Moreover, no vertex in
A^{\prime}
has a neighbor in
A^{\prime}\cup C^{\prime}
. In particular, the induced subgraph
G[A^{\prime}\cup C^{\prime}]
is edgeless. On the other hand, since
P
is an EOP set in
G
, there is no edge with endvertices in
B^{\prime}
. The above argument shows that
G[B^{\prime}\cup C^{\prime}]
and
G[A^{\prime}\cup B^{\prime}]
are bipartite graphs in which every vertex in
B^{\prime}
is adjacent to
\delta(G)-1
vertices in
C^{\prime}
and to one vertex in
A^{\prime}
. We now observe that
\delta(G)
,
A^{\prime}
,
B^{\prime}
and
C^{\prime}
have the same roles as
k
,
A
,
B
and
C
have in the description of the members of
\cal F
, respectively. Consequently,
G
belongs to
\cal F
.
∎

4.2
Effect of edge removal

Analysis of the effect of edge removal on the value of
\rho_{e}^{o}
was suggested in
[
3
]
, and here we give a comprehensive response. Note that characterizations of the graphs with
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)\in\{1,2\}
were also given in
[
3
]
. In fact, it is easy to see that
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)=1
if and only if
G\cong K_{n}
for
n\geq 2
. Moreover,
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)=2
if and only if
(i)

2\leq\mbox{diam}(G)\leq 4
,
(ii)

G
is claw-free and
(iii)
for any two nonadjacent edges
e_{1}=uv
and
e_{2}=xy
having no common edge, every vertex in
V(G)\setminus\{u,v,x,y\}
is adjacent to at least two vertices in
\{u,v,x,y\}
.

It is clear that
\rho_{e}^{o}(K_{n}-e)=2=\rho_{e}^{o}(K_{n})+1
for each edge
e
when
n\geq 3
. So, we consider the graphs
G
with
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)\geq 2
.

Theorem 4.2
.

For any graph
G
of order
n\geq 3
and
e\in E(G)
, the following statements hold.

(i)

If
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)=2
, then
1\leq\rho_{e}^{o}(G-e)\leq 3
.

(ii)

If
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)\geq 3
, then
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)-1\leq\rho_{e}^{o}(G-e)\leq 2(\rho_{e}^{o}(G)-1)
.

Proof.

First, let us consider the upper bound. Let
B
be a
\rho_{e}^{o}(G-e)
-set. Note that the subgraph of
G
induced by
B
is a disjoint union of induced stars
S_{1},\ldots,S_{k}
with centers
s_{1},\ldots,s_{k}
, respectively (if
S_{i}
has only one edge for some
1\leq i\leq k
, then we only consider one of its endvertices as the center). If
B
turns out to be an EOP set of
G
, then
\rho_{e}^{o}(G-e)\leq\rho_{e}^{o}(G)
, which is less than or equal to
2(\rho_{e}^{o}(G)-1)
. So, we may assume that
B
is not an EOP set of
G
. This implies that
e
joins a vertex
x\in V(S_{i})
to a vertex
y\in V(S_{j})
for some
1\leq i\leq j\leq k
. We deal with the following possibilities.

(a) Let
x=s_{i}
and
y
is a leaf of
S_{j}
for some
1\leq i<j\leq k
. Then,
B\setminus\{ys_{j}\}
is an EOP set of
G
of cardinality
\rho_{e}^{o}(G-e)-1
. So,
\rho_{e}^{o}(G-e)\leq\rho_{e}^{o}(G)+1
.

(b) Let
x
be a leaf of
S_{i}
and
y
be a leaf of
S_{j}
for some
1\leq i\leq j\leq k
(
i
may be equal to
j
). Then,
B\setminus\{xs_{i}\}
is an EOP set of
G
of cardinality
\rho_{e}^{o}(G-e)-1
. Hence,
\rho_{e}^{o}(G-e)\leq\rho_{e}^{o}(G)+1
.

(c) Let
x=s_{i}
and
y=s_{j}
for some
1\leq i<j\leq k
. We may assume, without loss of generality, that
|E(S_{i})|\leq|E(S_{j})|
. In such a case,
B^{\prime}=\big(B\setminus E(S_{i})\big)\cup\{e\}
is an EOP set in
G
. Thus,
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)\geq|B^{\prime}|\geq|B|/2+1=\rho_{e}^{o}(G-e)/2+1
.

Summing up, we have proved that
\rho_{e}^{o}(G-e)\leq\max\{2\rho_{e}^{o}(G)-2,\rho_{e}^{o}(G)+1\}
in each case, which proves the upper bounds in
(i)
and
(ii)
.

For the lower bounds, note that if
B
is a
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)
-set, then
B\setminus\{e\}
is an EOP set in
G-e
for each
e\in E(G)
. This leads to
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)-1\leq\rho_{e}^{o}(G-e)
.
∎

In the next remark we show that all integer values between the lower and upper bounds in Theorem
4.2
are realizable.

Observation 4.3
.

For any integers
b\geq 3
and
a
with
b-1\leq a\leq 2b-2
, there exists a connected graph
G
and
e\in E(G)
such that
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)=b
and
\rho_{e}^{o}(G-e)=a
. In addition, for any
a\in\{1,2,3\}
there exists a connected graph
G
and
e\in E(G)
such that
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)=2
and
\rho_{e}^{o}(G-e)=a
.

Proof.

Let
b\geq 3
. First, let
b+1\leq a\leq 2b-2
. Let
G
be obtained from
K_{1,b+1}
, with leaves
v_{1},\ldots,v_{b+1}
and central vertex
v
, by
(i)
attaching
\lfloor a/2\rfloor
new vertices to
v_{1}
as leaves and also
\lceil a/2\rceil
new vertices to
v_{2}
as leaves, and
(ii)
adding the edge
v_{1}v_{2}
. It is not difficult to check that
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)=b
and
\rho_{e}^{o}(G-v_{1}v_{2})=a
.
Second, let
a=b
. Consider the graph
G^{\prime}
obtained from the above-mentioned graph
G
by removing the vertices in
(i)
. We then have
\rho_{e}^{o}(G^{\prime})=\rho_{e}^{o}(G^{\prime}-vv_{1})=b
. Third, let
a=b-1
. Let
G^{\prime\prime}
be obtained from the paths
vv_{i}w_{i}
, for
1\leq i\leq b
, by adding edges in such a way that the set
\{w_{1},w_{2},\ldots,w_{b}\}
induces a clique. One can easily check that
\rho_{e}^{o}(G^{\prime\prime})=b
and
\rho_{e}^{o}(G^{\prime\prime}-vv_{b})=b-1
.

Suppose now that
b=2
. Let
G
be obtained from
K_{n}
, with
n\geq 3
, by joining a new vertex
v
to a vertex
u\in V(K_{n})
. It is then clear that
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)=2=\rho_{e}^{o}(G-uv)+1
. Moreover,
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)=2=\rho_{e}^{o}(G-xy)-1
for any edge
xy
of
K_{n}
with
x,y\neq u
. Finally,
\rho_{e}^{o}(G)=2=\rho_{e}^{o}(G-uw)
in which
u\neq w\in V(K_{n})
.
∎

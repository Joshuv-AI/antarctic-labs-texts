Hahn polynomials for hypergeometric distribution Thanks: The first author is partially supported by Simons Foundation Grant #635462.
Plamen Iliev, Yuan Xu

Abstract
. Orthogonal polynomials for the multivariate hypergeometric distribution are defined on lattices in polyhedral domains in ℝ d {\mathbb{R}}^{d} . Their structures are studied through a detailed analysis of classical Hahn polynomials with negative integer parameters. Factorization of the Hahn polynomials is explored and used to explain the relation between the index set of orthogonal polynomials and the lattice set in polyhedral domain. In the multivariate case, these constructions lead to nontrivial families of hypergeometric polynomials vanishing on lattice polyhedra. The generating functions and bispectral properties of the orthogonal polynomials are also discussed.

33C50, 33C70, 42C05

1.
Introduction

We study orthogonal polynomials with respect to the hypergeometric distribution in several variables. Let

N
be a positive integer and let
\ell_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq d+1
, be nonnegative integers, such that
\ell_{i}\leq N

and
\ell_{i}+\ell_{j}\geq N
for
i\neq j
. The hypergeometric
distribution in
d
variables is defined by

(1.1)

{\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N}(x):=\frac{N!}{(-|\ell|)_{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{(-\ell_{i})_{x_{i}}}{x_{i}!}\frac{(-\ell_{d+1})_{N-|x|}}{(N-|x|)!},

where
|\ell|=\ell_{1}+\cdots+\ell_{d+1}
,
|x|=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}
and
(a)_{k}=a(a+1)\ldots(a+k-1)
is the
Pochhammer symbol; it is a probability distribution on the set
V_{\ell,N}
of discrete polyhedral domain
defined by

(1.2)

V_{\ell,N}^{d}:=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}:0\leq x_{i}\leq\ell_{i},\,1\leq i\leq d,\,\hbox{and}\,N-\ell_{d+1}\leq|x|\leq N\right\}.

We studied orthogonal polynomials with respect to this distribution in
[
11
]
, which are called the
Hahn polynomials on polyhedra, since an orthogonal basis can be explicitly given in terms of the Hahn
polynomials. These polynomials are closely related to the classical Hahn polynomials of several variables

[
14
]
that are orthogonal with respect to the weight function

(1.3)

W_{{\kappa},N}(x)=\frac{N!}{(|{\kappa}|+d+1)_{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{({\kappa}_{i}+1)_{x_{i}}}{x_{i}!}\frac{({\kappa}_{d+1}+1)_{N-|x|}}{(N-|x|)!}

where
{\kappa}_{i}>-1
,
1\leq i\leq d+1
, defined on the lattice points within the simplex
V_{N}^{d}
defined by

V_{N}^{d}=\{x\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}:|x|\leq N\}
.

In
[
11
]
a family of Hahn polynomials on polyhedra, denoted by
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N)
, are explicitly
given with
\nu
belonging to an index set
H_{\ell,N}^{d}
, which is a subset of
V_{N}^{d}
, but different from
V_{\ell,N}^{d}
. The set

H_{\ell,N}^{d}
has a complicated structure and one of the main results of
[
11
]
is to show, with a strenuous
combinatorial proof, that
H_{\ell,N}^{d}
and
V_{\ell,N}^{d}
have the same cardinality, so that

\{{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N):\nu\in H_{\ell,N}^{d}\}
is an orthogonal basis. The definition of
H_{\ell,N}^{d}

comes from setting
{\kappa}_{i}
as negative integers
-\ell_{i}-1
in a basis of classical Hahn polynomials
and collecting those polynomials whose norms are finite and non-zero. Since the basis used in
[
11
]

is specifically normalized, one may ask if the set
H_{\ell,N}^{d}
is uniquely determined. Furthermore,
since the set
H_{\ell,N}^{d}
is a subset of
V_{N}^{d}
, one may ask if those classical Hahn polynomials
whose indices lie outside of
H_{\ell,N}^{d}
when setting
{\kappa}_{i}
to be
-\ell_{i}-1
are trivial or undefined.

The purpose of this paper is to answer these questions and to study the structures of the Hahn
polynomials for hypergeometric distribution. More specially, we want to understand the formal process
of setting
{\kappa}_{i}
as negative integers
-\ell_{i}-1
rigorously and, in particular, to determine if and how
the formal process leads to structural relations, such as generating functions, recurrence relations and difference equations,
for the Hahn polynomials on the polyhedra. The question has interesting implications even for the Hahn
polynomials of one variable, which we briefly describe to facilitate the discussion.

The classical Hahn polynomials
Q_{m}(x;a,b,N)
are orthogonal with respect to a discrete inner product
defined on the set
V_{N}=\{x\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:0\leq x\leq N\}
and
\{Q_{m}(x;a,b,N):0\leq m\leq N\}
is an orthogonal
basis. Assume, for example,
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\leq N
and
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\geq N
. Then, for
d=1
, a basis of
the orthogonal polynomials for the hypergeometric distribution is given by

\{Q_{m}(x;-\ell_{1}-1,-\ell_{2}-1,N):0\leq m\leq\ell_{1}\}
and the orthogonality is defined on the set

V_{\ell,N}=\{x\in{\mathbb{N}}:N-\ell_{2}\leq x\leq\ell_{1}\}
. We shall show that all polynomials

Q_{m}(x;-\ell_{1}-1,-\ell_{2}-1,N)
with
m>\ell_{1}
contain the factor

\prod_{j=N-\ell_{2}}^{\ell_{1}}(x-j)
. In particular, this shows that all polynomials with index outside

H_{\ell,N}=\{0,1,\ldots,\ell_{1}\}
vanishes on the set
V_{\ell,N}
.

The situation for
d>1
, however, is much more complicated. When we set
{\kappa}_{i}=-\ell_{i}-1
,
those classical Hahn polynomials with indices outside
H_{\ell,N}^{d}
still vanish on the polyhedral
lattice set
V_{\ell,N}^{d}
; however, they do not always contain linear factors that vanish trivially on

V_{\ell,N}^{d}
. In fact, they lead to non-trivial, likely irreducible, polynomials that vanish on large subsets
of lattice points. The complication for
d>1
requires a careful consideration when deriving structural
properties for the Hahn polynomials on polyhedra from those of classical Hahn polynomials.

The paper is organized as follows. In the next section we consider the case
d=1
and study Hahn
polynomials with negative integer parameters. The definition of the Hahn polynomials on the
polyhedra is discussed in Section 3, which ends with examples of nontrivial Hahn polynomials
that vanish on a large set of lattice points. The examples lead to the study of factorization of the Hahn
polynomials of two variables in Section 4. The generating functions of the Hahn polynomials on the
polyhedra are discussed in Section 5, whereas their bispectral properties are described in Section 6,
which contains, in particular, explicit recurrence relations and difference equations satisfied by these polynomials.

2.
Hahn polynomials with negative integer parameters

We start with a short review of the classical Hahn polynomials that depend on two real parameters

a,b>-1
. The main goal of this section is to study Hahn polynomials when
a
and
b
become
negative integers, for which some of the properties of the Hahn polynomials remain valid whereas
others become more subtle.

2.1.
Classical Hahn polynomials

For
a,b>-1
, the classical Hahn polynomials are
{}_{3}F_{2}
hypergeometric functions given by

(2.1)

Q_{n}(x;a,b,N):={}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-n,n+a+b+1,-x\\
a+1,-N\end{matrix};1\right),\qquad n=0,1,\ldots,N.

They are discrete orthogonal polynomials with respect to the weight function

w_{a,b}(x;N)=\frac{N!}{(a+b+2)_{N}}\frac{(a+1)_{x}(b+1)_{N-x}}{x!(N-x)!},\qquad x=0,1,\ldots,N,

over the set of integers
\{0,1,\ldots,N\}
, More precisely, they satisfy

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{x=0}^{N}Q_{n}(x;a,b,N)Q_{m}(x;a,b,N)w_{a,b}(x;N)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{(-1)^{n}n!(b+1)_{n}(a+b+N+2)_{n}(n+a+b+1)}{(a+1)_{n}(a+b+2)_{n}(-N)_{n}(2n+a+b+1)}\delta_{n,m},\quad n,m\leq N.

Furthermore, these polynomials can also be defined via a generating function

(2.3)

(1+t)^{N}\frac{P_{n}^{(a,b)}(\frac{1-t}{1+t})}{P_{n}^{(a,b)}(1)}=\sum_{x=0}^{N}\binom{N}{x}Q_{n}(x;a,b,N)t^{x},

where
P_{n}^{(a,b)}
is the Jacobi polynomial defined by

P_{n}^{(a,b)}(t)=\frac{(a+1)_{n}}{n!}{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}-n,n+a+b+1\\
a+1\end{matrix};\frac{1-t}{2}\right).

They also satisfy the relation

(2.4)

Q_{n}(x;a,b,N)=(-1)^{n}\frac{(b+1)_{n}}{(a+1)_{n}}Q_{n}(N-x,b,a,N).

2.2.
Hahn polynomials with negative integer parameters

Let
N
be a positive integer. Let
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
be two positive integers that satisfy

(2.5)

\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\geq N.

We consider the hypergeometric distribution or the weight function

(2.6)

{\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N}(x)=\frac{\binom{\ell_{1}}{x}\binom{\ell_{2}}{N-x}}{\binom{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}{N}}=\frac{N!}{(-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})_{N}}\frac{(-\ell_{1})_{x}(-\ell_{2})_{N-x}}{x!(N-x)!},\quad x\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

Throughout this paper, we shall adopt the following notation,

a\wedge b=\min\{a,b\}\quad\hbox{and}\quad a\vee b=\max\{a,b\},\qquad a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
N,\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
be positive integers satisfying (
2.5
). Then

(2.7)

{\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N}(x)=\frac{(\ell_{1}\vee N)!(\ell_{2}\vee N)!}{N!(-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})_{N}}\frac{(-\ell_{1}\wedge N)_{x}(-\ell_{2}\wedge N)_{N-x}}{(x-N+\ell_{2}\vee N)!(\ell_{1}\vee N-x)!}.

In particular, the function
{\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N}
is positive and supported on the set

V_{\ell,N}:=\big\{x\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:N-\ell_{2}\wedge N\leq x\leq\ell_{1}\wedge N\big\}.

Proof.

If
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\leq N
, then (
2.7
) and (
2.6
) coincide. In all other cases,
we can rewrite
{\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N}
given in (
2.6
) to the formula in (
2.7
) by
using the identity
(-m)_{n}=(-1)^{n}m!/(m-n)!
for
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
The claim on the support of
{\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N}
follows readily from
(-m)_{n}>0
if
n\leq m
and
(-m)_{n}=0
if
n>m
.
∎

We consider orthogonal polynomials with respect to the inner product

(2.8)

\displaystyle{\langle}f,g{\rangle}_{\ell,N}=\sum_{x=N-\ell_{2}\wedge N}^{\ell_{1}\wedge N}f(x)g(x){\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N}(x),

which satisfies
{\langle}1,1{\rangle}_{\ell,N}=1
. Applying the Gram-Schmidt process, we can identify a family of
orthogonal polynomials
\{{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}:0\leq n\leq\deg_{\ell,N}\}
, where

\deg_{\ell,N}:=\ell_{1}\wedge N+\ell_{2}\wedge N-N.

Evidently,
{\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N}(x)=w_{-\ell_{1}-1,-\ell_{2}-1}(x;N)
. We make the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
and
N
be positive integers such that
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\geq N
.
For
0\leq n\leq\ell_{1}\wedge N
, define Hahn polynomials with negative integer parameters by

(2.9)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)\,

\displaystyle=Q_{n}(x;-\ell_{1}-1,-\ell_{2}-1,N)

\displaystyle={}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-n,n-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}-1,-x\\
-\ell_{1},-N\end{matrix};1\right),

where
Q_{n}
is the classical Hahn polynomial (
2.1
).

For
0\leq n\leq\deg_{\ell,N}
, the orthogonality of the polynomials
{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(\cdot;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)

follows from that of classical Hahn polynomials. Indeed, the identity
(
2.2
) involves only rational functions in
a,b
, so that we can apply analytic continuation
to extend it to
a,b
being negative integers, while the support set of
{\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N}
shows that the inner
product becomes (
2.8
).

Theorem 2.3
.

For
0\leq m,n\leq\deg_{\ell,N}
,

\big\langle{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(\cdot;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N),{\mathsf{Q}}_{m}(\cdot;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)\big\rangle_{\ell,N}=\delta_{m,n}{\mathsf{B}}_{n}(\ell,N),

where

{\mathsf{B}}_{n}(\ell,N)=\frac{(-1)^{n}n!(-\ell_{2})_{n}(-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}+N)_{n}(-n+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+1)}{(-\ell_{1})_{n}(-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})_{n}(-N)_{n}(-2n+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+1)}.

From (
2.9
), the Hahn polynomials with negative integer parameters are well defined
if
0\leq n\leq\ell_{1}\wedge N
. If
\ell_{2}\geq N
, then
\deg_{\ell,N}=\ell_{1}\wedge N
. If
\ell_{2}<N
,
however,
\ell_{1}\wedge N>\deg_{\ell,N}
, we have more polynomials than needed. It turns out that the
extra polynomials are entirely zero when restricted on
V_{\ell,N}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume
\ell_{2}\leq N
. Let

n=\deg_{\ell,N}+m+1
for
m=0,1,\ldots,N-\ell_{2}-1
. Then

(2.10)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)=\,

\displaystyle\frac{(-N+\ell_{2}+1)_{m}}{(-\ell_{1}\wedge N)_{n}}\prod_{j=N-\ell_{2}}^{\ell_{1}\wedge N}(x-j)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times{\mathsf{Q}}_{m}(x,N-\ell_{2}-1,|N-\ell_{1}|-1,\ell_{1}\vee N).

In particular,
{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)
vanishes on
V_{\ell,N}
if
\deg_{\ell,N}<n\leq\ell_{1}\wedge N
.

Proof.

The assumption on
m
implies
n\leq\ell_{1}\wedge N
, which implies that the constant in front of
{\mathsf{Q}}_{m}

in the righthand side of (
2.10
) is nonzero.

First assume
\ell_{1}\leq N
. We need the following identity in
[
15
, Entry (7.4.4.83)]
,

{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-n,a,b\\
c,d\end{matrix};1\right)=\frac{(c+d-a-b)_{n}}{(c)_{n}}{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-n,d-a,d-b\\
d,c+d-a-b\end{matrix};1\right),

where
n
is a positive integer and the identity holds when both sides are finite. Choose

a=n-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}-1
,
b=-x
,
c=-\ell_{1}
and
d=-N
. Then for our choice of
n=\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N+m+1

we obtain

{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)=\frac{(-\ell_{1}-m+x)_{n}}{(-\ell_{1})_{n}}{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-n,-m,-N+x\\
-\ell_{1}-m+x,-N\end{matrix};1\right).

We write
(-\ell_{1}-m+x)_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N+m+1}=(-\ell_{1}-m+x)_{m}(-\ell_{1}+x)_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N+1}
. While
the second term is
(-\ell_{1}+x)_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N+1}=\prod_{j=N-\ell_{2}}^{\ell_{1}}(x-j)
, the first term
combining with the
{}_{3}F_{2}
function gives, using the identity
[
15
, Entry (7.4.4.86)]

\frac{(c)_{m}}{(c-a)_{m}}{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-m,a,b\\
c,d\end{matrix};1\right)={}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-m,a,d-b\\
d,a-c-m+1\end{matrix};1\right)

with
a=-N+x
,
b=-n
,
c=-\ell_{1}-m+x
, and
d=-N
, that

\frac{(-\ell_{1}-m+x)_{m}}{(N-\ell_{1}-m)_{m}}{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-n,-m,-N+x\\
-\ell_{1}-m+x,-N\end{matrix};1\right)={}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-m,-N+n,-N+x\\
-N+\ell_{1}+1,-N\end{matrix};1\right).

This last function can be identified with
{\mathsf{Q}}_{m}(N-x;N-\ell_{1}-1,N-\ell_{2}-1,N)
, which we further write, using
the identity (
2.4
), as

(-1)^{m}\frac{(-N+\ell_{2}+1)_{m}}{(-N+\ell_{1}+1)_{m}}{\mathsf{Q}}_{m}(x;N-\ell_{2}-1,N-\ell_{1}-1,N).

Putting these together proves the identity (
2.10
) when
\ell_{1}\leq N
.

Next we assume
\ell_{1}>N
. Exchanging the role of
\ell_{1}
and
N
in the
{}_{3}F_{2}
of (
2.9
),
we see that the Hahn polynomials satisfy

(2.11)

{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)={\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x;N,\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N,\ell_{1}).

With
\widehat{\ell}_{1}=N
,
\widehat{\ell}_{2}=\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N
and
\widehat{N}=\ell_{1}
, the polynomial in the righthand
side is
{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x;\widehat{\ell}_{1},\widehat{\ell}_{2},\widehat{N})
with
\widehat{\ell}_{1}\leq\widehat{N}
and
\widehat{\ell}_{1}+\widehat{\ell}_{2}\geq\widehat{N}
,
which is factorable by what we proved in the previous paragraph. Hence, (
2.10
) for
\ell_{1}>N

follows from that for
\ell_{1}\leq N
. The proof is complete.
∎

By symmetry, one may expect a factorization of
{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}
when
\ell_{2}>N\geq\ell_{1}
. Indeed, this
holds for the polynomial
{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(N-x;\ell_{2},\ell_{1},N)
, which is well defined for
n\leq\ell_{2}
and the factorization
holds for
n>\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N
. However, by (
2.4
),

{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)=(-1)^{n}\frac{(-\ell_{2})_{n}}{(-\ell_{1})_{n}}{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(N-x;\ell_{2},\ell_{1},N).

The constant in the right-hand side makes sense only if
n\leq\ell_{1}<\ell_{2}
. Thus, it may seem that applying
first (
2.4
) and then Theorem
2.4
can lead to interesting new factorizations,
but Theorem Theorem
2.4
cannot be applied.

The Hahn polynomial
{\mathsf{Q}}_{m}(x,N-\ell_{2}-1,|N-\ell_{1}|-1,\ell_{1}\vee N)
in the righthand side of
(
2.10
) still has negative integer parameters. Experiment with small
\ell_{i}
and
N
shows
that many such polynomials are irreducible. One may ask if these polynomials are irreducible or
irreducible after further factoring out the linear terms. The answer to both questions, however, is negative.

Example 2.5
.

If
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)=(6,8,12)
, then (
2.10
) becomes

{\mathsf{Q}}_{6}(x;6,8,12)=-\frac{1}{5!}(x-4)(x-5)(x-6){\mathsf{Q}}_{3}(x;3,5,12)

and the Hahn polynomial in the righthand side contains a further linear factor,

{\mathsf{Q}}_{3}(x;3,5,12)=-\frac{1}{132}(x-4)(2x^{2}-13x+33).

Furthermore, if
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)=(8,9,16)
, then (
2.10
) becomes

{\mathsf{Q}}_{7}(x;8,9,16)=\frac{1}{56}(x-7)(x-8){\mathsf{Q}}_{5}(x;6,7,16)

and the Hahn polynomial in the righthand side is not irreducible,

{\mathsf{Q}}_{5}(x;6,7,16)=-\frac{1}{24960}(52-14x+x^{2})(-480+159x-20x^{2}+x^{3}).

2.3.
Generating function

The generating function (
2.3
) can also be adopted for the Hahn polynomials
of negative integer parameters. When
a
and
b
are negative integers, the Jacobi polynomial

P_{n}^{(a,b)}
are known to have a degree reduction for some
n
. Indeed, if
n
is a positive
integer and
n+m+a+b=0
,
m
is an integer,
1\leq m\leq n
, then
[
16
, (4.22.3)]

\binom{n}{m-1}P_{n}^{(a,b)}(t)=\binom{n+a}{n-m+1}P_{m-1}^{(a,b)}(t).

However, we claim that this degree reduction will be irrelevant in our setting. Indeed, for
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}

satisfy (
2.5
), we are interested in
n
that satisfies
0\leq n\leq\deg_{\ell,N}
. With
a=-\ell_{1}-1
,

b=-\ell_{2}-1
, we have
m=-n-a-b=\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+2-n\geq\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+2-\deg_{\ell,N}
. If

N\leq\ell_{2}
, then
\deg_{\ell,N}=\ell_{1}\wedge N
, so tha
m\geq\ell_{1}+N+2-\ell_{1}\wedge N\geq N+2>n
,
whereas if
\ell_{2}\leq N
, then
\deg_{\ell,N}=\ell_{1}\wedge N-(N-\ell_{2})
, so that

m\geq\ell_{1}+N+2-\ell_{1}\wedge N\geq N+2>n
. This verifies the claim. To simplify notation,
we make the following definition.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
be positive integers. For
n=0,1,\ldots,\ell_{1}\wedge\ell_{2}
, we define

G_{n}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}(t)=\frac{P_{n}^{(-\ell_{1}-1,-\ell_{2}-1)}(t)}{P_{n}^{(-\ell_{1}-1,-\ell_{2}-1)}(1)}={}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}-n,n-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}-1\\
-\ell_{1}\end{matrix};\frac{1-t}{2}\right).

Since
P_{n}^{(-\ell_{1}-1,-\ell_{2}-1)}(1)=\frac{(-\ell_{1})_{n}}{n!}
and
P_{n}^{(a,b)}(-t)=(-1)^{n}P_{n}^{(b,a)}(t)
,
we have

(2.12)

G_{n}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}(t)=(-1)^{n}\frac{(-\ell_{2})_{n}}{(-\ell_{1})_{n}}G_{n}^{(\ell_{2},\ell_{1})}(-t).

By analytic continuation, the identity (
2.3
) remains valid when

a=-\ell_{1}-1
and
b=-\ell_{2}-1
, which gives

(2.13)

(1+t)^{N}G_{n}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}\left(\frac{1-t}{1+t}\right)=\sum_{x=0}^{N}\binom{N}{x}{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)t^{x}.

This works for all
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\geq N
. Its right-hand side, however, sums over all integers in
[0,N]

instead of over integers in
[N-\ell_{2}\wedge N,\ell_{1}\wedge N]
, on which the orthogonality of

{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)
is defined. A more general generating function is given below.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
{\alpha}=\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}\wedge N-\ell_{1}\wedge N
and
{\beta}=\ell_{2}+\ell_{1}\wedge N-\ell_{2}\wedge N
. Then
for
0\leq n\leq\deg_{\ell,N}
,

(2.14)

\displaystyle b_{\ell,N}t^{N-\ell_{2}\wedge N}

\displaystyle(1+t)^{\ell_{1}\wedge N+\ell_{2}\wedge N-N}G_{n}^{({\alpha},{\beta})}\left(\frac{1-t}{1+t}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x=N-\ell_{2}\wedge N}^{\ell_{1}\wedge N}\binom{\ell_{1}\wedge N+\ell_{2}\wedge N-N}{\ell_{1}\wedge N-x}{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)t^{x},

where
b_{\ell,N}
is the constant given by

b_{\ell,N}=\frac{(-\ell_{2})_{n}(-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}\wedge N+N)_{n}}{(-\ell_{1})_{n}(-\ell_{2}+\ell_{2}\wedge N-N)_{n}}.

In particular,
b_{\ell,N}=1
if
\ell_{2}\geq N
.

Proof.

The identity (
2.13
) gives (
2.14
) when
\ell_{1}\geq N
and
\ell_{2}\geq N
.
In all other cases, we need to modify the righthand side so that the summation is over the integers in the
interval
[N-\ell_{2}\wedge N,\ell_{1}\wedge N]
. Assume
\ell_{2}\geq N
.
The identity (
2.13
) for
{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}
in the right-hand side of (
2.11
) gives
(
2.14
) for
\ell_{1}<N
.

Next we assume
\ell_{2}\leq N
. By (
2.4
),

(2.15)

{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x,\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)=(-1)^{n}\frac{(-\ell_{2})_{n}}{(-\ell_{1})_{n}}{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(N-x,\ell_{2},\ell_{1},N).

Applying (
2.11
) on the Hahn polynomial in the right hand side, we deduce

{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x,\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)=(-1)^{n}\frac{(-\ell_{2})_{n}}{(-\ell_{1})_{n}}{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(N-x,N,\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N,\ell_{2}).

Using this identity, the right-hand side of (
2.14
) for
\ell_{1}\geq N
becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{x=N-\ell_{2}}^{N}

\displaystyle\binom{\ell_{2}}{N-x}{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)t^{x}=\sum_{x=0}^{\ell_{2}}\binom{\ell_{2}}{x}{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(N-x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)t^{N-x}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\frac{(-\ell_{2})_{n}}{(-\ell_{1})_{n}}\sum_{x=0}^{\ell_{2}}\binom{\ell_{2}}{x}{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x;N,\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N,\ell_{2})t^{N-x}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\frac{(-\ell_{2})_{n}}{(-\ell_{1})_{n}}t^{N}(1+\tfrac{1}{t})^{\ell_{2}}G_{n}^{(N,\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N)}\left(\frac{1-\frac{1}{t}}{1+\frac{1}{t}}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\frac{(-\ell_{2})_{n}}{(-\ell_{1})_{n}}t^{N-\ell_{2}}(1+t)^{\ell_{2}}G_{n}^{(N,\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N)}\left(-\frac{1-t}{1+t}\right),

where the third equality follows from (
2.13
). Finally, applying (
2.12
), we have
established (
2.14
) for
\ell_{1}\geq N
and
\ell_{2}\leq N
. In the last case, when

\ell_{1}\leq N
and
\ell_{2}\leq N
, we rewrite the Hahn polynomial, starting from (
2.11
),
using (
2.15
) and then (
2.11
) one more time, to obtain

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\frac{(N-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})_{n}}{(-N)_{n}}{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(\ell_{1}-x;\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N,N,\ell_{1})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\frac{(N-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})_{n}}{(-N)_{n}}{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(\ell_{1}-x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N).

With this identity, the righthand side of (
2.14
) for
\ell_{1}\leq N
becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{x=N-\ell_{2}}^{\ell_{1}}\binom{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N}{\ell_{1}-x}{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)t^{x}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{x=0}^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N}\binom{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N}{x}{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(\ell_{1}-x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)t^{\ell_{1}-x}

\displaystyle\quad=(-1)^{n}\frac{(N-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})_{n}}{(-N)_{n}}\sum_{x=0}^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N}\binom{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N}{x}{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N)t^{\ell_{1}-x},

from which the proof can be completed as in the case
\ell_{1}\geq N
and
\ell_{2}\leq N
.
This completes the proof.
∎

The usual orthogonality of the Jacobi polynomials, however, no longer holds when the parameters are
negative integers, since
(1-x)^{a}(1+x)^{b}
is not integrable on
[-1,1]
if
a
and/or
b
are negative
integers.
It is possible, however, to define a linear functional
{\mathcal{L}}
, so that the polynomials
G_{n}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}

are orthogonal in the sense that
{\mathcal{L}}\big(G_{m}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}G_{n}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}\big)=0
for
m\neq n
,
although this linear function is no longer positive definite.

Let
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
be positive integers. We define the linear functional
{\mathcal{L}}

on the space
\Pi_{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}
of polynomials of degree at most
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}

so that its moments are given by

{\mathcal{L}}(x^{k})={}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}-k,-\ell_{1}\\
-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}\end{matrix};2\right),\qquad 0\leq k\leq\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
be two positive integer. Then the polynomials
G_{k}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}
satisfy the orthogonal relation

{\mathcal{L}}\left(G_{m}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}G_{n}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}\right)=h_{n}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}\delta_{m,n},\qquad 0\leq m,n\leq\ell_{1}\wedge\ell_{2},

where the constants
h_{n}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}
are given by

h_{n}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}=\frac{n!(-\ell_{2})_{n}(1+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-n)}{(-\ell_{1})_{n}(-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})_{n}(1+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-2n)}.

Proof.

First we claim that the following relation holds

(2.16)

{\mathcal{L}}\left(\big(\tfrac{1-x}{2}\big)^{m}\right)=\frac{(-\ell_{1})_{m}}{(-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})_{m}},\qquad m=0,1,\ldots,\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}.

Indeed, by the binomial formula and the definition of the
{}_{2}F_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}\left(\big(\tfrac{1-x}{2}\big)^{m}\right)\,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m}{k}(-1)^{k}{\mathcal{L}}(x^{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\frac{(-m)_{k}}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{(-k)_{j}(-\ell_{1})_{j}}{j!(-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})_{j}}2^{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{m}}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{(-\ell_{1})_{j}2^{j}}{j!(-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})_{j}}\sum_{k=j}^{m}\frac{(-m)_{k}(-k)_{j}}{k!}.

Changing summation index and rewriting, it is easy to see that

\sum_{k=j}^{m}\frac{(-m)_{k}(-k)_{j}}{k!}=(-1)^{j}(-m)_{j}\sum_{k=0}^{m-j}\frac{(-m+j)_{k}}{k!}=m!\delta_{j,m},

from which the claimed formula (
2.16
) follows immediately.

Now let
n\leq\ell_{1}\wedge\ell_{2}
. For
0\leq m\leq n
, we use (
2.16
) and the identity

(a)_{m+k}=(a)_{m}(a+m)_{k}
to obtain

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}\big(G_{n}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}(x)\big(\tfrac{1-x}{2}\big)^{m}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-n)_{k}(n-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}-1)_{k}}{k!(-\ell_{1})_{k}}{\mathcal{L}}\big((\tfrac{1-x}{2})^{m+k}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-\ell_{1})_{m}}{(-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})_{m}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-n)_{k}(n-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}-1)_{k}(-\ell_{1}+m)_{k}}{k!(-\ell_{1})_{k}(-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}+m)_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-\ell_{1})_{m}}{(-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})_{m}}{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-n,n-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}-1,-\ell_{1}+m\\
-\ell_{1},-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}+m\end{matrix};1\right).

This hypergeometric function is a balanced terminating
{}_{3}F_{2}
and, by Saalschütz summation formula,
we conclude that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}\big(G_{n}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}(x)\big(\tfrac{1-x}{2}\big)^{m}\big)\,

\displaystyle=\frac{(-\ell_{1})_{m}}{(-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})_{m}}\frac{(1+\ell_{2}-n)_{n}(-m)_{n}}{(-\ell_{1})_{n}(1+\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-n-m)_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}n!(-\ell_{2})_{n}}{(-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})_{2n}}\delta_{m,n},

where we have used
(-m)_{n}=0
for
m<n
and
(-n)_{n}=(-1)^{n}n!
. This proves that
G_{n}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}
is orthogonal to
(1-x)^{m}
for
m<n
and hence, by linearity, to
G_{m}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}
. Furthermore, multiplying the above identity by
the coefficient
(-1)^{n}(n-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}-1)_{n}/(-\ell_{1})_{n}
of
\big(\tfrac{1-x}{2}\big)^{n}
in
G_{n}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}
verifies the
formula for
h_{n}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}
.
∎

Since
{\mathsf{h}}_{n}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}
has the sign
(-1)^{n}
, the moment functional
{\mathcal{L}}
is not positive definite.
This can also be seen in the coefficients of the three-term relations satisfied by
tG_{n}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}(t)
,
where the coefficients of
G_{n+1}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}
and
G_{n-1}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}
have opposite signs.

The generating function (
2.14
) of the Hahn polynomials of negative parameters requires

0\leq n\leq\deg_{\ell,N}=\ell_{1}\wedge N+\ell_{2}\wedge N-N
. Since
\deg_{\ell,N}\leq\ell_{1}\wedge\ell_{2}
, we see that all
G_{n}^{(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})}
in the
generating function (
2.14
) are orthogonal polynomials.

Finally, let us mention that the three-term relation satisfied by
{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(\cdot,\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)
can be deduced
from that of the classical Hahn polynomials, which shows

(2.17)

x\phi_{n}=-A_{n}\phi_{n+1}+(A_{n}+C_{n})\phi_{n}-C_{n}\phi_{n-1},\quad 0\leq n\leq\deg_{\ell,N}-1,

where
\phi_{n}={\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(\cdot,\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)
and the coefficients
A_{n}
and
C_{n}
are given by

\displaystyle A_{n}\,

\displaystyle=\frac{(n-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}-1)(n-\ell_{1})(N-n)}{(2n-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}-1)(2n-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2})},

\displaystyle C_{n}\,

\displaystyle=\frac{n(n+N-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}-1)(n-\ell_{2}-1)}{(2n-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}-1)(2n-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}-2)}.

In our statement of (
2.17
), we assume
n\leq\deg_{\ell,N}-1
. For
\ell_{2}\leq N
, the identity
also holds for
n=\deg_{\ell,N}
by Theorem
2.4
whenever
A_{n}
and
C_{n}
are finite.
Since
\deg_{\ell,N}\leq\frac{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}{2}
, the coefficients are well defined unless
n=\deg_{\ell,N}=\frac{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}}{2}
. This last equation is attained if
\ell_{1}=\ell_{2}=N
and
n=\deg_{\ell,N}=N
, which
leads to a pole in
A_{n}
so that (
2.17
) fails for
n=\deg_{\ell,N}
in this particular case.

3.
Hahn polynomials for hypergeometric distribution

Classical Hahn polynomials in several variables are those on lattice points inside a simplex. A brief
review of these polynomials will be given in the first subsection. When their parameters become negative
integers, these polynomials become orthogonal polynomials for hypergeometric distribution, which will
be discussed in the second subsection.

3.1.
Classical Hahn polynomials of several variables

Let
N
be a positive integer. Recall that
V_{N}^{d}
is the set of lattice points in a discrete simplex

(3.1)

V_{N}^{d}:=\{\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}:|\nu|\leq N\},

and, for
{\kappa}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
with
{\kappa}_{i}>-1
,
1\leq i\leq d+1
, the function
W_{{\kappa},N}
defined in
(
1.3
) is the normalized Hahn weigh function.
The Hahn polynomials are orthogonal with respect to the discrete inner product

{\langle}f,g{\rangle}_{W_{{\kappa},N}}=\sum_{x\in V_{N}^{d}}f(x)g(x)W_{{\kappa},N}(x).

For
0\leq n\leq N
, let
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{{\kappa},N})
denote the space of orthogonal polynomials of degree
n

with respect to this inner product. Then

\operatorname{dim}{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}\left(W_{{\kappa},N}\right)=\binom{n+d-1}{n},\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots.

An orthogonal basis of
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{{\kappa},N})
can be given in terms of
classical Hahn polynomials of one variable, for which we need the following notation:

For
y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and
1\leq j\leq d
, we define

(3.2)

{\mathbf{y}}_{j}:=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{j})\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\mathbf{y}}^{j}:=(y_{j},\ldots,y_{d}),

and also define
{\mathbf{y}}_{0}:=0
and
{\mathbf{y}}^{d+1}:=0
. It follows that
{\mathbf{y}}_{d}={\mathbf{y}}^{1}=y
,
and

|{\mathbf{y}}_{j}|=y_{1}+\cdots+y_{j},\quad|{\mathbf{y}}^{j}|=y_{j}+\cdots+y_{d},\quad\hbox{and}\quad|{\mathbf{y}}_{0}|=|{\mathbf{y}}^{d+1}|=0.

For
{\kappa}=({\kappa}_{1},\ldots,{\kappa}_{d+1})
, we have
{\boldsymbol{\large{\kappa}}}^{j}:=({\kappa}_{j},\ldots,{\kappa}_{d+1})
for
1\leq j\leq d+1
. Furthermore,
for
\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
and
{\kappa}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
, we define

(3.3)

a_{j}:=a_{j}(\kappa,\nu):=|{\boldsymbol{\large{\kappa}}}^{j+1}|+2|\nu^{j+1}|+d-j,\qquad 1\leq j\leq d.

Notice that
a_{d}={\kappa}_{d+1}
since
|\nu^{d+1}|=0
by definition.

Proposition 3.1
.

For
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{N}^{d+1}
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
,
|\nu|\leq N
, define

(3.4)

\displaystyle Q_{\nu}(x;\kappa,N)=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{d}(-N+|{\mathbf{x}}_{j-1}|+|\nu^{j+1}|)_{\nu_{j}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times Q_{\nu_{j}}(x_{j};\kappa_{j},a_{j},N-|{\mathbf{x}}_{j-1}|-|\nu^{j+1}|).

The polynomials in
\{Q_{\nu}(x;\kappa,N):|\nu|=n\}
form a mutually orthogonal basis of
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{{\kappa},N})

and
B_{\nu}:={\langle}Q_{\nu}(\cdot;\kappa,N),\ Q_{\nu}(\cdot;\kappa,N){\rangle}_{W_{{\kappa},N}}
is given by, setting

{\lambda}_{\kappa}:=|{\kappa}|+d+1
,

\displaystyle B_{\nu}({\kappa},N):=\frac{(-1)^{|\nu|}(-N)_{|\nu|}({\lambda}_{k})_{N+|\nu|}}{({\lambda}_{k})_{N}({\lambda}_{k})_{2|\nu|}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{(a_{j}+1)_{\nu_{j}}({\kappa}_{j}+a_{j}+1)_{2\nu_{j}}\nu_{j}!}{({\kappa}_{j}+1)_{\nu_{j}}(\kappa_{j}+a_{j}+1)_{\nu_{j}}}.

These Hahn polynomials of several variables were defined and studied in
[
14
]
through
a generating function that was later recognized as the Jacobi polynomial
P_{\nu}(x)
on the
simplex
T^{d}
defined by

T^{d}:=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:x_{1}\geq 0,\ldots,x_{d}\geq 0,|x|\leq 1\}.

Proposition 3.2
.

For
{\kappa}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
with
{\kappa}_{i}>-1
,
\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
and
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, define

(3.5)

P_{\nu}(x)=P_{\nu}^{\kappa}(x):=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1-|{\mathbf{x}}_{j-1}|\right)^{\nu_{j}}\frac{P_{\nu_{j}}^{(a_{j},\kappa_{j})}\left(\frac{2x_{j}}{1-|{\mathbf{x}}_{j-1}|}-1\right)}{P_{\nu_{j}}^{(a_{j},\kappa_{j})}(1)},

where
a_{j}=a_{j}({\kappa},\nu)
is defined in (
3.3
). Then the polynomials in
\{P_{\nu}:|\nu|=n\}
form an
orthogonal basis of
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}(W_{\kappa})
, where

(3.6)

W_{\kappa}(x):=x_{1}^{\kappa_{1}}\cdots x_{d}^{\kappa_{d}}(1-|x|)^{\kappa_{d+1}}.

These polynomials serve as generating functions of the Hahn polynomials, for which it is more convenient to
use a different normalization of the Hahn polynomials, denoted by
H_{\nu}(\cdot;{\kappa},N)
, given by

(3.7)

H_{\nu}({\alpha};{\kappa},N)=\frac{(-1)^{|\nu|}}{(-N)_{|\nu|}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{(\kappa_{j}+1)_{\nu_{j}}}{(a_{j}+1)_{\nu_{j}}}\,Q_{\nu}({\alpha}^{\prime};\kappa,N),

where we use homogeneous coordinates
{\alpha}=({\alpha}^{\prime},N-|{\alpha}|)\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d+1}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
{\kappa}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
with
\kappa_{i}>-1
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. For
\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
,
|\nu|\leq N
,
the Hahn polynomials
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}({\alpha};{\kappa},N)
satisfy

(3.8)

P_{\nu,N}(y)=|y|^{N}P_{\nu}\Big(\frac{y^{\prime}}{|y|}\Big)=\sum_{|\alpha|=N}\frac{N!}{{\alpha}!}H_{\nu}(\alpha;\kappa,N)y^{\alpha},

where
{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d+1}
,
{\alpha}!={\alpha}_{1}!\cdots{\alpha}_{d+1}!
and
y=(y^{\prime},y_{d+1})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
.

The generating function serves as a starting point and an essential tool in the study of
[
10
,
17
]
.

3.2.
Hahn polynomials with negative integer parameters

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
\ell_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
for
1\leq i\leq d+1
. We assume that they satisfy

(3.9)

\ell_{i}\leq N\quad\hbox{and}\quad\ell_{i}+\ell_{j}\geq N,\quad i\neq j,\quad 1\leq i,j\leq d+1.

Recall that
V_{\ell,N}^{d}
, defined in (
1.2
), denotes the discrete polyhedral domain, which
we restate below,

V_{\ell,N}^{d}:=\left\{x\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}:0\leq x_{i}\leq\ell_{i},\,1\leq i\leq d,\,\hbox{and}\,N-\ell_{d+1}\leq|x|\leq N\right\},

where
|x|=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{d}
. Evidently,
V_{\ell,N}^{d}
is the simplex
V_{N}^{d}
if all
\ell_{i}=N
. The polyhedral
is the simplex
V_{N}^{d}
with its corners sliced off. In particular,

|V_{\ell,N}^{d}|=\binom{N+d}{d}-\sum_{k=1}^{d+1}\binom{N-\ell_{k}+d-1}{d},

where we denote by
|E|
the cardinality of the discrete set
E
. For
\ell
and
N
satisfying
(
3.9
), the hypergeometric distribution in
d
variables is defined by
(
1.1
), which is restated below,

{\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N}(x)=\frac{1}{\binom{|\ell|}{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\binom{\ell_{i}}{x_{i}}\binom{\ell_{d+1}}{N-|x|}=\frac{N!}{(-|\ell|)_{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{d}\frac{(-\ell_{i})_{x_{i}}}{x_{i}!}\frac{(-\ell_{d+1})_{N-|x|}}{(N-|x|)!}.

This defines a probability measure on
V_{\ell,N}^{d}
. The first identity is used more commonly in the
probability theory; see, for example,
[
12
]
. The Hahn polynomials for this distribution are orthogonal
polynomials with respect to the inner product

(3.10)

{\langle}f,g{\rangle}_{\ell,N}=\sum_{x\in V_{\ell,N}^{d}}f(x)g(x){\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N}(x).

Let
\Pi_{N}^{d}
denote the space of polynomials of total degree at most
N
in
d
variables. Let
{\mathcal{I}}(V_{\ell,N}^{d})

denote the ideal of polynomials that vanish on
V_{\ell,N}^{d}
. It is known that the space of orthogonal polynomials,
denoted by
\Pi_{\ell,N}^{d}
, with respect to
{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}_{\ell,N}
satisfies

\Pi_{\ell,N}^{d}={\mathbb{R}}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]/{\mathcal{I}}(V_{\ell,N}^{d}).

Since
{\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N}(x)=W_{-\ell-{\mathbf{1}},N}(x)
where
{\mathbf{1}}=(1,1,\ldots,1)
and
{\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N}(x)=0
if

x\in V_{N}^{d}\setminus V_{\ell,N}^{d}
, orthogonal polynomials with respect to the inner product

{\langle}\cdot,\cdot{\rangle}_{\ell,N}
can be deduced from
Q_{\nu}(x;{\kappa},N)
in (
3.4
) by setting

{\kappa}=-\ell-{\mathbf{1}}
for
\nu
in an appropriate subset of
\{\nu:|\nu|\leq N\}
. This narrative was carried out
in
[
11
]
. Let us now rewrite the polynomials
Q_{\nu}(x;-\ell-{\mathbf{1}},N)
in terms of the Hahn polynomials
with negative integer parameters.

With the notation
{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{j}:=(\ell_{j},\ldots,\ell_{d+1})
for
1\leq j\leq d+1
, we define

(3.11)

\mathpzc{a}_{j}:=-a_{j}(-\ell-{\mathbf{1}},\nu)-1=|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{j+1}|-2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{j+1}|,\qquad 1\leq j\leq d.

Definition 3.4
.

Let
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d+1}
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Assume
\ell_{i}\leq N
and (
3.9
).
We define Hahn polynomials with negative integer parameters on the polyhedron
V_{\ell,N}^{d}
by

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N)\,

\displaystyle=Q_{\nu}(x;-\ell-{\mathbf{1}},N)

\displaystyle=\prod_{j=1}^{d}(-N+|{\mathbf{x}}_{j-1}|+|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{j+1}|)_{\nu_{j}}{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{j}}\left(x_{j};\ell_{j},\mathpzc{a}_{j},N-|{\mathbf{x}}_{j-1}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{j+1}|\right).

In
[
11
]
, we considered these polynomials for
\nu
in the index set
H_{\ell,N}^{d}
defined by

\displaystyle H_{\ell,N}^{d}:=\left\{\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}:|\nu|\leq N,\,|\nu|\leq|\ell|-N,\nu_{j}\leq\ell_{j},\,\nu_{j}\leq\mathpzc{a}_{j},\,1\leq j\leq d\right\}.

The main result of
[
11
]
states that they form an orthogonal basis of the space
\Pi_{\ell,N}^{d}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and let
\ell_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
satisfy (
3.9
). Then

(i)

The polynomials
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N)
are orthogonal and satisfy

\left\langle{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N),{\mathsf{Q}}_{\mu}(\cdot;\ell,N)\right\rangle_{\ell,N}={\mathsf{B}}_{\nu}(\ell,N)\delta_{\nu,\mu}

for all
\nu,\mu\in H_{\ell,N}^{d}
, where

(3.12)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{B}}_{\nu}(\ell,N):=\frac{(-1)^{|\nu|}(-N)_{|\nu|}(-|\ell|)_{N+|\nu|}}{(-|\ell|)_{N}(-|\ell|)_{2|\nu|}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\frac{(-\mathpzc{a}_{j})_{\nu_{j}}(-\ell_{j}-\mathpzc{a}_{j}-1)_{2\nu_{j}}\nu_{j}!}{(-\ell_{j})_{\nu_{j}}(-\ell_{j}-\mathpzc{a}_{j}-1)_{\nu_{j}}}.

(ii)

The set
\{{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N):\nu\in H_{\ell,N}^{d}\}
is a basis of
\Pi_{\ell,N}^{d}
. In particular,

|H_{\ell,N}^{d}|=|V_{\ell,N}^{d}|=\operatorname{dim}\Pi_{\ell,N}^{d}.

The part (i) follows from the classical Hahn polynomials on
V_{N}^{d}
as we indicated above. The proof of
part (ii) is highly non-trivial and requires a rather involved combinatorial proof. The norm
{\mathsf{B}}_{\nu}(\ell,N)
is
nonzero, in fact positive, for
\nu\in H_{\ell,N}^{d}
, which is how the set
H_{\ell,N}^{d}
is conceived
and defined.

The definition of the polynomials
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N)
shows that these polynomials are well defined
for
\nu
in a set
{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell,N}^{d}
that contains
H_{\ell,N}^{d}
as a subset. The next theorem is a complementary result of
Theorem
3.5
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\ell_{i}\in{\mathbb{N}}
satisfying (
3.9
). The polynomials
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N)

are well defined on
V_{\ell,N}^{d}
if
\nu\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell,N}^{d}
, where

(3.13)

{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell,N}^{d}=\{\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}:\nu_{i}\leq\ell_{i},\,1\leq i\leq d,\,|\nu|\leq N\}.

Furthermore, if
\nu\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell,N}^{d}\setminus H_{\ell,N}^{d}
, then the polynomials
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N)

vanishes on
V_{\ell,d}
.

Proof.

Let
\nu\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell,N}^{d}
. For
0\leq k\leq\nu_{j}
, expanding
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{j}}
in
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}
using its
{}_{3}F_{2}

definition, and using the identity

\frac{(-N+|{\mathbf{x}}_{j-1}|+|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{j+1}|)_{\nu_{j}}}{(-N+|{\mathbf{x}}_{j-1}|+|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{j+1}|)_{k}}=(-N+|{\mathbf{x}}_{j-1}|+|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{j+1}|+k)_{\nu_{j}-k},

it is easy to see that
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}
is well defined on
{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell,N}^{d}
. Since the orthogonal relation in
Theorem
3.5
is derived by setting
{\kappa}=-\ell-{\mathbf{1}}
, it holds for
\nu\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell,N}^{d}
.
In particular, the expression of
{\mathsf{B}}_{\nu}(\ell,N)
in (
3.12
) shows that the norm of

{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N)
is zero if
|\nu|>|\ell|-N
or if
\nu_{j}>\mathpzc{a}_{j}
for some
j
, so that the polynomial is
entirely zero on
V_{\ell,N}^{d}
if
\nu\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\ell,N}^{d}
but
\nu\notin H_{\ell,N}^{d}
.
∎

This theorem can be regarded as an extension of Theorem
2.4
in one variable. In that theorem,
the polynomial
{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(\cdot,\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)
in one variable is factored into two lower degree polynomials,
one of which vanishes on
V_{\ell,N}
so that the vanishing of
{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(\cdot,\ell_{1},\ell_{2},N)
becomes obvious
from the factorization. An analogous factorization, however, no longer holds for
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N)
in
higher dimension. In fact, what makes this theorem interesting lies in the existence of non-trivial polynomials that
vanish on the set
V_{\ell,N}^{d}
, as illustrated by the following example.

Example 3.7
.

Let
d=2
,
\ell_{1}=6
,
\ell_{2}=4
,
\ell_{3}=4
,
N=7
. Then
V_{\ell,N}^{2}
and
H_{\ell,N}^{2}
each
contains 23 points; see Figure 1.

.

. Left:

. Right:

The indices
\nu=(0,5)
and
\nu=(3,3)
are outside of

and their corresponding polynomials

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Q}}_{0,5}(x;\ell,N)=\,

\displaystyle(x_{1}-3)\big(840-638x_{1}-910x_{2}+179x_{1}^{2}+480x_{1}x_{2}+375x_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\,\qquad\quad-22x_{1}^{3}-85x_{1}^{2}x_{2}-120x_{1}x_{2}^{2}-70x_{2}^{3}

\displaystyle\,\qquad\quad+x_{1}^{4}+5x_{1}^{3}x_{2}+10x_{1}^{2}x_{2}^{2}+10x_{1}x_{2}^{3}+5x_{2}^{4}\big);

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Q}}_{3,3}(x;\ell,N)=\,

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{48}(x_{1}-4)(x_{1}-3)(x_{1}-2)(x_{1}+2x_{2}-7)

\displaystyle\times(60-22x_{1}-35x_{2}+2x_{1}^{2}+5x_{1}x_{2}+5x_{2}^{2}),

of degree 5 and 6, respectively, vanish on

; see Figure 2.

.

In the case of
\nu=(0,5)
, the polynomial
{\mathsf{Q}}_{0,5}(x;\ell,N)
contains an irreducible polynomial
of degree
4
, thus its vanishing on
V_{\ell,N}^{d}
is no longer obvious from the factorization, in
contrast to Theorem
2.4
in the one variable case. Observe that the polynomial of degree
4

in
{\mathsf{Q}}_{0,5}(x;\ell,N)
and the irreducible polynomial of degree
2
in
{\mathsf{Q}}_{3,3}(x;\ell,N)
have

It is possible to be more specific about the factors in
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N)
when
\nu
is outside
of
H_{\ell,N}^{d}
. Let
N_{j}:=N-|{\mathbf{x}}_{j-1}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{j+1}|
so that
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{j}}
can be written as

{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{j}}(x_{j};\ell_{j},\mathpzc{a}_{j},N_{j})
. From
\nu_{j}\leq\ell_{j}
,
1\leq j\leq d
, we
obtain
|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{j+1}|\leq|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{j+1}|-\ell_{d+1}
, which implies, by (
3.9
), that

\ell_{j}+\mathpzc{a}_{j}=\ell_{j}+|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{j+1}|-2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{j+1}|\geq\ell_{j}+\ell_{d+1}-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{j+1}|\geq N-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{j+1}|.

Hence, for
x\in V_{\ell,N}^{d}
,
\ell_{j}+\mathpzc{a}_{j}\geq N-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{j+1}|-|{\mathbf{x}}_{j-1}|=N_{j}
, which shows that the
parameters of
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{j}}
in
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}
satisfy (
2.5
). Thus, if
N_{j}
is a positive integer,
then
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{j}}
is a Hahn polynomial with negative integer parameters, so that Theorem
2.4

may apply if
\mathpzc{a}_{j}<N_{j}
.

4.
Factorization of Hahn polynomials of two variables with negative integer parameters

We examine the factorization of the Hahn polynomials of two variables more closely in this section.
For
d=2
, the polynomial
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N)
is given by

(4.1)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N)=(-N)_{\nu_{1}}

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}(x_{1};\ell_{1},\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}-2\nu_{2},N-\nu_{2})

\displaystyle\times(-N+x_{1})_{\nu_{2}}{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{2}}(x_{2};\ell_{2},\ell_{3},N-x_{1})

and the index set
\nu\in H_{\ell,N}^{2}
is given by

\displaystyle H_{\ell,N}^{2}=\,

\displaystyle\big\{(\nu_{1},\nu_{2}):\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}\leq N,\,\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}\leq|\ell|-N,

\displaystyle\quad\nu_{1}\leq\ell_{1},\,\nu_{2}\leq\ell_{2},\,\nu_{2}\leq\ell_{3},\,\nu_{1}+2\nu_{2}\leq\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}\big\}.

It is easy to see that both
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{i}}
,
i=1,2
, are Hahn polynomial with negative integer parameters.
To determine their factorization for
\nu
is outside of
H_{\ell,N}^{2}
, we need a precise description
of
H_{\ell,N}^{2}
using the height function
h_{\ell,N}
introduced in
[
11
]
.

The height function measures the number of integer points on the vertical line
x_{1}=\nu_{1}
in
H_{\ell,N}^{d}
.
For
d=2
, it is defined by

h_{\ell,N}(\nu_{1}):=\min\left(\ell_{2},\ell_{3},\left\lfloor\frac{\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}-\nu_{1}}{2}\right\rfloor,\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}-N-\nu_{1},N-\nu_{1}\right)+1.

It follows immediately that the index set
H_{\ell,N}^{2}
can be written as

\displaystyle H_{\ell,N}^{2}:=\{(\nu_{1},\nu_{2}):0\leq\nu_{1}\leq\ell_{1},0\leq\nu_{2}\leq h_{\ell,N}(\nu_{1})-1\}.

The value of the height function can be described more explicitly.

Lemma 4.1
.

The height function
h_{\ell,N}
satisfies

(i)

if
0\leq\nu_{1}\leq|\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}|
, then
h_{\ell,N}(\nu_{1})=\ell_{2}\wedge\ell_{3}+1
;

(ii)

if
|\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}|\leq\nu_{1}\leq\ell_{1}-\big|2N-|\ell|\big|
, then

h_{\ell,N}(\nu_{1})=\ell_{2}\wedge\ell_{3}-\left\lfloor\frac{\nu_{1}-|\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}|+1}{2}\right\rfloor+1;

(iii)

if
\ell_{1}-\big|2N-|\ell|\big|\leq\nu_{1}\leq\ell_{1}
, then

h_{\ell,N}(\nu_{1})=\begin{cases}|\ell|-N-\nu_{1}+1,&|\ell|\leq 2N,\\
N-\nu_{1}+1,&|\ell|>2N.\end{cases}

Proof.

We can assume
\ell_{3}\geq\ell_{2}
. The proof is a simple but tedious verification. The first two items have
already been used in the proof of Lemma 4.3 in
[
11
]
. We omit the details.
∎

To consider polynomials
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N)
with
\nu=(\nu_{1},\nu_{2})
outside the domain
H_{\ell,N}^{2}
,
we then assume
\nu_{2}\geq h_{\ell,N}(\nu_{1})
. Below we study
Q_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N)
with

\nu_{2}=h_{\ell,N}(\nu_{1})
, that is, with index just outside
H_{\ell,N}^{2}
. For convenience, we shall
write

(4.2)

{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N)=(-N)_{\nu_{1}}{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}(x_{1};\widehat{\ell},\widehat{N})\,{\mathsf{R}}_{\nu_{2}}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{3},N),

where
\widehat{\ell}=(\ell_{1},\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}-2\nu_{2})
,
\widehat{N}=N-\nu_{2}
, and

(4.3)

{\mathsf{R}}_{\nu_{2}}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{3},N):=(-N+x_{1})_{\nu_{2}}{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{2}}(x_{2},\ell_{2},\ell_{3},N-x_{1}).

We say that a polynomial of a variable
x
splits if it splits in
{\mathbb{Q}}
, i.e. if it can be written as

c\prod_{j}(x-a_{j})
,
a_{j}\in{\mathbb{Q}}
. We examine
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}(x_{1};\widehat{\ell},\widehat{N})
first. Applying
Theorem
2.4
, this polynomial has linear factors if
\widehat{\ell}_{2}\leq\widehat{N}
and

\deg_{\widehat{\ell},\widehat{N}}<\nu_{1}\leq\min\{\ell_{1},\widehat{N}\}
, or more specifically, if

(4.4)

\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}-\nu_{2}\leq N,\quad 0\leq\min\{\ell_{1},N-\nu_{2}\}-\nu_{1}<N-\ell_{2}-\ell_{3}+\nu_{2}.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume
\ell_{3}\geq\ell_{2}
. Let
\nu_{2}=h_{\ell,N}(\nu_{1})
and set

{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}(x_{1})={\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}(x_{1};\widehat{\ell},\widehat{N})
. Then

(1)

{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}
is undefined if
|\ell|>2N
and
\ell_{1}-(|\ell|-2N)+1\leq\nu_{1}\leq\ell_{1}
;

(2)

{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}
splits if
|\ell|\leq 2N
and
1\leq\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}-1\leq\nu_{1}\leq\ell_{1}
, or if

|\ell|>2N
and
1\leq\nu_{1}\leq\ell_{1}-(|\ell|-2N)
.

More precisely, for
j=-1,0,1,\ldots
and
\nu_{1}=\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}+j\leq\ell_{1}-\big|2N-|\ell|\big|
,

(4.5)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}(x_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(-N+\ell_{2}-i+1)_{\nu_{1}}}\prod_{k=N-\ell_{3}-i+1}^{N-\ell_{2}+i-1}(x_{1}-k),\quad j=2i-1,

(4.6)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}(x_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{-N+\ell_{3}+i}{(-N+\ell_{2}-i+1)_{\nu_{1}}}(1-a_{i}x_{1})\prod_{k=N-\ell_{3}-i+1}^{N-\ell_{2}+i-1}(x_{1}-k),\quad j=2i,

where
a_{i}=(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-\ell_{3}-2i)/((N-\ell_{3}-i)(N-\ell_{2}+i-1))
. Furthermore, if
|\ell|\leq 2N
, then for

\nu_{1}=\ell_{1}+|\ell|-2N+j
with
j=1,2,\ldots,2N-|\ell|
,

(4.7)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}(x_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(-\ell_{1})_{\nu_{1}}}\prod_{k=2N-|\ell|-j+1}^{\ell_{1}}(x_{1}-k).

Proof.

If
0\leq\nu_{1}\leq\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}
, then
h_{\ell,N}(\nu_{1})=\ell_{2}+1
. Hence, with
\nu_{2}=\ell_{2}+1
, it is
easy to see that
\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}-\nu_{2}=\ell_{3}-1\leq N
and
\min\{\ell_{1},N-\nu_{2}\}=N-\ell_{2}-1
and

\deg_{\widehat{\ell},\widehat{N}}=\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}-2
.
Thus, (
4.4
) holds if
\nu_{1}>\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}-2
. Furthermore, if
\nu_{1}=\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}-1
, then

\nu_{1}=\deg_{\widehat{\ell},\widehat{N}}+1
, so that we can apply Theorem
2.4
with
m=0
to factor

{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}
, which gives (
4.5
) for
i=0
, whereas if
\nu_{1}=\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}
, then we can
apply Theorem
2.4
with
m=1
to factor
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}
and the factorization contains also

{\mathsf{Q}}_{1}(x_{1},\widehat{N}-\widehat{\ell}_{2}-1,|N-\ell_{1}|-1,\widehat{N})
, a polynomial of degree 1 that gives the factor
1-a_{0}x_{1}
,
which gives (
4.6
) for
i=0

If
\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}<\nu_{1}\leq\ell_{1}-\big|2N-|\ell|\big|
, we write
\nu_{1}=\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}+j
. By
Lemma
4.1
,

h_{\ell,N}(\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}+j)=\ell_{2}-\Big\lfloor\frac{j+1}{2}\Big\rfloor+1,\quad 1\leq\Big\lfloor\frac{j+1}{2}\Big\rfloor\leq\min\{N-\ell_{3},\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}-N\}.

With
\nu_{2}=h_{\ell,N}(\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}+j)
, we have
\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}-\nu_{2}\leq\ell_{3}+\lfloor\frac{j+1}{2}\rfloor-1\leq N-1<N
. Moreover,
\min\{\ell_{1},N-\nu_{2}\}=N-\ell_{2}+\lfloor\frac{j+1}{2}\rfloor-1
, so that

\deg_{\widehat{\ell},\widehat{N}}=\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}+2\lfloor\frac{j+1}{2}\rfloor-2
. Hence, (
4.4
) holds for all

\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}<\nu_{1}\leq\ell_{1}-\big|2N-|\ell|\big|
. Furthermore, if
j=2i-1
, then
\nu_{1}=\deg_{\widehat{\ell},\widehat{N}}+1
, so that we can apply Theorem
2.4
with
m=0
to factor
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}
, which gives (
4.5
) for
i>0
, whereas if
j=2i
, then
\nu_{1}=\deg_{\widehat{\ell},\widehat{N}}+2
, we can then apply
Theorem
2.4
with
m=1
to factor
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}
and the factorization contains also

{\mathsf{Q}}_{1}(x_{1},\widehat{N}-\widehat{\ell}_{2}-1,|\widehat{N}-\ell_{1}|-1,\widehat{N})
, which gives (
4.6
) for
i>0
.

If
\ell_{1}-\big|2N-|\ell|\big|+1\leq\nu_{1}\leq\ell_{1}
, we write
\nu_{1}=\ell_{1}-\big|2N-|\ell|\big|+j
for

j=1,2,\ldots,\big|2N-|\ell|\big|
. Here we need to consider two cases. First, assume
|\ell|\leq 2N
. Then,
by Lemma
4.1
,

h_{\ell,N}(\ell_{1}-\big|2N-|\ell|\big|+j)=N-\ell_{1}-j+1,\quad 1\leq j\leq 2N-|\ell|.

With
\nu_{2}=h_{\ell,N}(\ell_{1}-\big|2N-|\ell|\big|+j)
, we have
\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}-\nu_{2}=|\ell|-N+j-1\leq N-1<N

and
\deg_{\widehat{\ell},\widehat{N}}=\ell_{1}-(2N-|\ell|)+j-1
, so that
\nu_{1}=\ell_{1}-\big|2N-|\ell|\big|+j=\deg_{\widehat{\ell},\widehat{N}}+1
for all
j
. Thus, we can apply Theorem
2.4
with
m=0
to factor

{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}
, which gives (
4.7
). Next we assume
|\ell|>2N
. Then, by
Lemma
4.1
,

h_{\ell,N}(\ell_{1}-\big|2N-|\ell|\big|+j)=\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}-N-j+1,\quad 1\leq j\leq 2N-|\ell|.

With
\nu_{2}=h_{\ell,N}(\ell_{1}-\big|2N-|\ell|\big|+j)
, a quick verification shows that

\nu_{1}+2\nu_{2}-|\ell|-1=-\ell_{1}-j+1
and
N-\nu_{2}=\nu_{1}-1
, which by (
2.9
) leads to

{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}(x_{1})={}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-\nu_{1},-\ell_{1}-j+1,-x\\
-\ell_{1},-\nu_{1}+1\end{matrix};1\right).

Since
(-\nu_{1}+1)_{\nu_{1}}=0
, this function is infinite for all
j\geq 1
.
This completes the proof.
∎

This proposition shows that the polynomials
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}
is either undefined or splits with only
possible exception when
2\leq\nu_{1}\leq|\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}|-2
, and
|\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}|\geq 4
, since the
factorizations are trivial for polynomials of degree
0
or
1
.

Proposition 4.3
.

For
\nu_{2}=h_{\ell,N}(\nu_{1})
, the polynomial
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N)
is not well-defined if and only if

(i)

\ell_{3}\geq\ell_{2}
and
\nu_{1}\leq\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}
;

(ii)

|\ell|>2N
and
2N+1-\ell_{2}-\ell_{3}\leq\nu_{1}
.

Proof.

We first assume
\ell_{3}\geq\ell_{2}
. If
0\leq\nu_{1}\leq\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}
,
then
\nu_{2}=\ell_{2}+1

according to the proof of Proposition
4.2
. For this
\nu_{2}
, it follows by (
2.9
) that

{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}+1}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{3},N)=(-N+x_{1})_{\ell_{2}+1}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-\ell_{2}-1,-\ell_{3},-x_{2}\\
-\ell_{2},-N+x_{1}\end{matrix};1\right),

which shows that
{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}+1}
is undefined, since
(-\ell_{2})_{\ell_{2}+1}=0
, if
\ell_{3}>\ell_{2}
. The
case
\nu_{1}>\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}
follows readily from Proposition
4.2
. Assume now
\ell_{3}<\ell_{2}

and
\nu_{1}\leq\ell_{2}-\ell_{3}
. Then
\nu_{2}=h_{\ell,N}(\nu_{1})=\ell_{3}+1
and

\displaystyle{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{3}+1}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{3},N)

\displaystyle=(-N+x_{1})_{\ell_{3}+1}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-\ell_{3}-1,-\ell_{2},-x_{2}\\
-\ell_{2},-N+x_{1}\end{matrix};1\right)

\displaystyle=(-N+x_{1})_{\ell_{3}+1}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}-\ell_{3}-1,-x_{2}\\
-N+x_{1}\end{matrix};1\right)=(-N+x_{1}+x_{2})_{\ell_{3}+1},

so that
{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{3}+1}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{3},N)
is well defined and splits. Thus, the statement follows from Proposition
4.2
.
∎

This shows that the non-trivial polynomials that vanishes on a larger set of lattice points, as seen in
Example
3.7
, are
{\mathsf{R}}_{\nu_{2}}(x;\ell,N)
. For
\nu_{2}=h_{\ell,N}(\nu_{1})
and
\nu_{1}
small,
it is possible to determine
{\mathsf{R}}_{\nu_{2}}
more explicitly.

Proposition 4.4
.

If
\ell_{2}=\ell_{3}
, then

{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}}(x,\ell_{2},\ell_{2},N)=\frac{(-N+x_{1}+x_{2})_{\ell_{2}+1}-(-1)^{\ell_{2}+1}(-x_{2})_{\ell_{2}+1}}{-N+\ell_{2}+x_{1}},

which contains a linear factor
N-x_{1}-2x_{2}
if
\ell_{2}
is odd. Furthermore,

{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}+1}(x,\ell_{2},\ell_{2},N)=(-N+\ell_{2}+x_{1}){\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}}(x,\ell_{2},\ell_{2},N).

Proof.

We consider the case
{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}+1}
first, which corresponds to
\nu_{1}=0
and
\nu_{2}=h_{\ell,N}(0)=\ell_{2}+1
.
Thus,

{\mathsf{R}}_{\nu_{2}}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{2},N)=(-N+x_{1})_{\ell_{2}+1}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-\ell_{2}-1,-\ell_{2},-x_{2}\\
-\ell_{2},-N+x_{1}\end{matrix};1\right).

Taking as a limiting case, the
{}_{3}F_{2}
function is given by

\displaystyle\lim_{\ell_{3}\to\ell_{2}}{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-\ell_{2}-1,-\ell_{3},-x_{2}\\
-\ell_{2},-N+x_{1}\end{matrix};1\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell_{2}}\frac{(-\ell_{2}-1)_{k}(-x_{2})_{k}}{k!(-N+x_{1})_{k}}

\displaystyle={}_{2}F_{1}\left(\begin{matrix}-\ell_{2}-1,-x_{2}\\
-N+x_{1}\end{matrix};1\right)-\frac{(-1)^{\ell_{2}+1}(-x_{2})_{\ell_{2}+1}}{(-N+x_{1})_{\ell_{2}+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-N+x_{1}+x_{2})_{\ell_{2}+1}}{(-N+x_{1})_{\ell_{2}+1}}-\frac{(-1)^{\ell_{2}+1}(-x_{2})_{\ell_{2}+1}}{(-N+x_{1})_{\ell_{2}+1}},

where the last step follows from Chu-Vandermonde identity. Consequently,

\displaystyle{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}+1}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{2},N)=(-N+x_{1}+x_{2})_{\ell_{2}+1}-(-1)^{\ell_{2}+1}(-x_{2})_{\ell_{2}+1}.

If
\ell_{2}
is odd, then the righthand side becomes zero if
x_{1}=N-2x_{2}
, which shows that this
polynomial contains a factor
N-x_{1}-2x_{2}
. Furthermore, rewriting the first Pochhammer symbol in the
righthand side, we obtain

{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}+1}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{2},N)=(-1)^{\ell_{2}+1}\big((-x_{2}-x_{1}+N-\ell_{2})_{\ell_{2}+1}-(-x_{2})_{\ell_{2}+1}\big),

which is zero when
x_{1}=N-\ell_{2}
, so that it also contains a factor
N-\ell_{2}-x_{1}
.

The case
{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}}
corresponds to
\nu_{1}=1
and
\nu_{2}=\ell_{2}
. We end up
with the same
{}_{3}F_{2}
function,
so that, using
(-N+x_{1})_{\ell_{2}+1}=(-N+\ell_{2}+x_{1})(-N+x_{1})_{\ell_{2}}
, we obtain

{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}+1}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{2},N)=(-N+\ell_{2}+x_{1}){\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{2},N).

Since
{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell+1}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{2},N)
contains a factor
-N+\ell_{2}+x_{1}
, this completes the proof.
∎

Since the first factor of
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N)
when
\nu_{1}=1
,
\nu_{2}=\ell_{2}
and
\ell_{3}=\ell_{2}

is

{\mathsf{Q}}_{1}(x_{1};\ell_{1},0,N-\ell_{2})=\frac{-N+\ell_{2}+x_{1}}{-N+\ell_{2}},

we conclude that
{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{2},N)
is a polynomial of degree
\ell_{2}
that
vanishes on
V_{\ell,N}^{2}\setminus\{(N-\ell_{2},j):0\leq j\leq\ell_{2}\}
. This is the polynomial of
degree 4 in
{\mathsf{Q}}_{0,5}(\cdot;\ell,N)
in Example
3.7
.

If
\ell_{3}>\ell_{2}
, then the smallest suitable
\nu_{1}
is
\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}+1
, for which
\nu_{2}=\ell_{2}
.
Consequently, we obtain

\displaystyle{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{3},N)\,

\displaystyle=(-N+x_{1})_{\ell_{2}}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\begin{matrix}-\ell_{2},-\ell_{3}-1,-x_{2}\\
-\ell_{2},-N+x_{1}\end{matrix};1\right)

\displaystyle=(-N+x_{1})_{\ell_{2}}\sum_{k=0}^{\ell_{2}}\frac{(-\ell_{3}-1)_{k}(-x_{2})_{k}}{k!(-N+x_{1})_{k}}.

If
\ell_{3}-\ell_{2}
is small, then the last sum can be made more explicit by writing it in terms of

{}_{2}F_{1}
, by adding and subtracting a few terms, and then using the Chu-Vandermonde identity,
which gives

\displaystyle\frac{{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{3},N)}{(-N+x_{1})_{\ell_{2}}}=\frac{(-N+x_{1}+x_{2})_{\ell_{3}+1}}{(-N+x_{1})_{\ell_{3}+1}}-\sum_{k=\ell_{2}+1}^{\ell_{3}+1}\frac{(-\ell_{3}-1)_{k}(-x_{2})_{k}}{k!(-N+x_{1})_{k}}.

By (
4.5
) and (
4.2
), the polynomial
{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}}
must
vanishes if
x_{1}>N-\ell_{2}
or
x_{1}<N-\ell_{3}
, which however is not obvious from the formula.

The simplest case is when
\ell_{3}=\ell_{2}+1
, for which
\nu_{1}=2
. We then obtain

\displaystyle{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{2}+1,N)\,

\displaystyle=\frac{(-N+x_{1}+x_{2})_{\ell_{2}+2}}{(-N+\ell_{2}+x_{1})_{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{(-1)^{\ell_{2}+1}(\ell_{2}+2)(-x_{2})_{\ell_{2}+1}}{-N+\ell_{2}+x_{1}}-\frac{(-1)^{\ell_{2}+2}(-x_{2})_{\ell_{2}+2}}{(-N+\ell_{2}+x_{1})_{2}}.

Since the first factor of
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N)
in this case is a quadratic polynomial

{\mathsf{Q}}_{2}(x_{1};\ell_{1},1,N-\ell_{2})=\frac{(N-\ell_{2}-x_{1})(N-\ell_{2}-1-x_{1})}{(N-\ell_{2})(N-\ell_{2}-1)}

that vanishes when
x_{1}=N-\ell_{2}
and
N-\ell_{2}-1
, it follows that the polynomial

{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{2}+1,N)
is a polynomial of degree
\ell_{2}
that vanishes on

V_{\ell,N}^{2}\setminus(\{(N-\ell_{2},j):0\leq j\leq\ell_{2}\}\cup\{(N-\ell_{2}-1,j):0\leq j\leq\ell_{2}\}).

This is not, however, obvious from the explicit formula of
{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{2}+1,N)
given above.

We also observe that
V_{\ell,N}^{2}
depends on
\ell_{1}
, whereas
{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{2}+1,N)

is independent of
\ell_{1}
. Consequently, the polynomial
{\mathsf{R}}_{\ell_{2}}(x;\ell_{2},\ell_{2}+1,N)
vanishes on a
large number of lattice points.

We end this subsection by making a conjecture on the irreducibility of the polynomial
{\mathsf{R}}_{\nu_{2}}
, which we
state more generally for the polynomial

\displaystyle R_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},y)

\displaystyle:=(-y)_{n}{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},y)

\displaystyle=(-y)_{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-n)_{k}(n-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}-1)_{k}(-x)_{k}}{(-\ell_{1})_{k}\,k!\,(-y)_{k}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-n)_{k}(n-\ell_{1}-\ell_{2}-1)_{k}(-x)_{k}(-y+k)_{n-k}}{(-\ell_{1})_{k}\,k!}.

This is a well-defined polynomial in
{\mathbb{Q}}[x,y]
when
n\leq\ell_{1}\wedge\ell_{2}
.

Conjecture 4.5
.

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\ell_{1},\ell_{2}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
n\leq\ell_{1}\wedge\ell_{2}
, the polynomial

R_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},y)
is irreducible unless
\ell_{1}=\ell_{2}
and
n
is odd, in which case

R_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},y)
is the product of
(y-2x)
and an irreducible polynomial of degree
n-1
.

For all
n\leq\ell_{1}\wedge\ell_{2}
, the identity

R_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},y)=(-1)^{n}\frac{(-\ell_{2})_{n}}{(-\ell_{1})_{n}}R_{n}(y-x;\ell_{2},\ell_{1},y)

holds, which shows that that
R_{n}(x;\ell_{1},\ell_{2},y)
has a factor
y-2x
if
\ell_{1}=\ell_{2}
and
n
is odd.
The conjecture states that the polynomial is irreducible apart from this trivial factor.

The conjecture can be naturally viewed as a two-dimensional analog of irreducibility results for classical orthogonal polynomials, which have a long history. Indeed, a famous result by Hilbert asserts that there exist irreducible polynomials of every degree
n
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
having the largest possible Galois group
S_{n}
. While Hilbert’s proof was nonconstructive, Schur provided a rather explicit example by proving that the
n
-th Laguerre polynomial is irreducible and has Galois group
S_{n}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. In the early 50’s, Grosswald conjectured the irreducibility of the Bessel polynomials, which was proved in
[
6
]
.

It is worth noting that, in general, irreducibility results for polynomials of two variables are easier to prove, since
{\mathbb{Q}}[x,y]={\mathbb{Q}}[x][y]
and we can try to use the Eisenstein criterion for the unique factorization domain
{\mathbb{Q}}[x]
. This is sometimes the “easy step” in the classical one-dimensional results, see for instance Proposition 3.1 in
[
2
]
. However, these arguments do not work in our case, since the expansion is in Pochhammer terms, and not in ordinary powers of
x
or
y
.

5.
Generating function

For Hahn polynomials on lattice points in the simplex, the generating function is a useful tool and plays
an essential role in
[
10
,
17
]
. In view of the generating function (
2.14
), one
would expect that there is a generating function for
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N)
given in terms of the Jacobi polynomial
on the simplex with negative integer parameters. Using the normalized Hahn polynomials

{\mathsf{H}}_{\nu}({\alpha};\ell,N)=H_{\nu}({\alpha};-\ell-{\mathbf{1}},N),\qquad{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d+1},

see (
3.7
), one such extension holds straightforwardly.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\ell_{i}
and
N
are positive integers, so that
\ell_{i}\leq N
and (
3.9
) holds.
For
\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
, define

G_{\nu}(x):=G_{\nu}^{\ell}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\left(1-|{\mathbf{x}}_{j-1}|\right)^{\nu_{j}}G_{\nu_{j}}^{(\mathpzc{a}_{j},\ell_{j})}\left(\frac{2x_{j}}{1-|{\mathbf{x}}_{j-1}|}-1\right),

where
\mathpzc{a}_{j}
is defined in (
3.11
). Then, for
\nu\in H_{\ell,N}^{d}
, the Hahn
polynomials
{\mathsf{H}}_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N)
satisfy

(5.1)

G_{\nu,N}(y)=|y|^{N}G_{\nu}\Big(\frac{y^{\prime}}{|y|}\Big)=\sum_{|\alpha|=N}\frac{N!}{\alpha!}{\mathsf{H}}_{\nu}(\alpha;\ell,N)y^{\alpha}.

This follows from the identity (
3.8
), which is a finite sum for
\nu\in H_{\ell,N}^{d}
, by
setting
{\kappa}_{i}=-\ell_{i}-1
. The sum in the right-hand side is over all lattice points in
V_{N}^{d}
, which
contains
V_{\ell,N}^{d}
as a subset. One may ask if this is a multidimensional analog of the generating
function in Proposition
2.7
so that the right-hand side of (
5.1
)
is summed over lattice points in
V_{\ell,N}^{d}
. However, this does not look to be possible, since for the factor

{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{j}}(x_{j};\ell_{j},\mathpzc{a}_{j},\widehat{N})
of
{\mathsf{H}}_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N)
, where
\widehat{N}=N-|{\mathbf{x}}_{j-1}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{j+1}|

and
j\geq 2
, both
\ell_{j}\wedge\widehat{N}
and
\mathpzc{a}_{j}\wedge\widehat{N}
depend on
|{\mathbf{x}}_{j-1}|
for

x\in V_{\ell,N}^{d}
.

The generating function (
3.8
) is used to derive properties of the Hahn polynomials on

V_{N}^{d}
in
[
17
]
. Some of those properties remain valid when the parameters are negative integers.
Of particular interests are those on the reproducing kernel of the Hahn polynomials of degree
n
, defined by

P_{n}(W_{{\kappa},N};x,y)=\sum_{|\nu|=n}\frac{Q_{\nu}(x;{\kappa},N)Q_{\nu}(y;{\kappa},N)}{B_{\nu}({\kappa},N)},\qquad 0\leq n\leq N.

For the Hahn polynomials with negative integer parameters, the corresponding kernel is defined by

{\mathsf{P}}_{n}({\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N};x,y)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\nu|=n\\
\nu\in H_{\ell,N}^{d}\end{subarray}}\frac{{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N){\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(y;\ell,N)}{{\mathsf{B}}_{\nu}(\ell,N)},\qquad 0\leq n\leq N.

The space
{\mathcal{V}}_{n}^{d}({\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N})
is of the dimension
\#\{\nu\in H_{\ell,N}^{d}:|\nu|=n\}
, which is less
than
\#\{\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}:|\nu|=n\}
for
n
large. However, let

\ell_{\rm min}:=\min\{\ell_{i}:1\leq i\leq d+1\}.

Lemma 5.2
.

The set
\{\nu\in H_{\ell,N}^{d}:|\nu|=n\}
coincides with
\{\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}:|\nu|=n\}
if and only if

0\leq n\leq\ell_{\rm min}
.

Proof.

If
0\leq n\leq\ell_{\rm min}
and
|\nu|=n
, then
\nu_{j}\leq n\leq\ell_{\rm min}
so that

\nu_{j}\leq\ell_{j}
follows trivially. Moreover,
|\nu^{j}|+|\nu^{j+1}|\leq 2n\leq|\ell^{j+1}|
holds if

j\leq d-1
, which implies
\nu_{j}\leq\mathpzc{a}_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq d-1
, whereas
\mathpzc{a}_{d}=\ell_{d+1}
, so that

\nu_{d}\leq\mathpzc{a}_{d}
holds as well. This shows that
\{\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}:|\nu|=n\}\subset\{\nu\in H_{\ell,N}^{d}:|\nu|=n\}
, while the other direction of inclusion is trivial. Hence, the two
sets are equal. If
n>\ell_{\rm min}
and assume, say,
\ell_{\rm min}=\ell_{1}
, then the element

\nu=(n,0,\ldots,0)
does not belong to
\{\nu\in H_{\ell,N}^{d}:|\nu|=n\}
as
\nu_{1}=n>\ell_{1}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

For
0\leq n\leq\ell_{\rm min}
, we can then write the sum of
{\mathsf{P}}_{n}({\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N};\cdot,\cdot)
as over

\{\nu:|\nu|=n\}
. In this way, it is easy to see that the kernel agrees with
P_{n}(W_{{\kappa},N};\cdot,\cdot)

when
{\kappa}_{j}=-\ell_{j}-1
. In particular, by
[
17
, Theorem 4.3]
, we can rewrite
{\mathsf{P}}_{n}({\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N})

in terms of elementary function

{\mathcal{E}}_{k}(x,y;\ell):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|{\gamma}|=k\\
{\gamma}_{i}\leq\ell_{i}\end{subarray}}\frac{(-X)_{{\gamma}}(-Y)_{\gamma}}{(-\ell)_{\gamma}{\gamma}!},\quad x,y\in V_{\ell,N}^{d},

where
X=(x,N-|x|)
,
Y=(y,N-|y|)
and
{\gamma}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d+1}
, and we have used the notation

(a)_{\gamma}=(a_{1})_{{\gamma}_{1}}\cdots(a_{d+1})_{{\gamma}_{d+1}}
for
a\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\ell_{i}
be nonnegative integers,
\ell_{i}\leq N
and
\ell_{i}+\ell_{j}\geq N
. Then, for
x,y\in V_{\ell,N}^{d}

and
0\leq n\leq\ell_{\rm min}
,

(5.2)

\displaystyle{\mathsf{P}}_{n}({\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N};x,y)=

\displaystyle\frac{(-N)_{n}(-|\ell|)_{N}(-|\ell|)_{n}(|\ell|+1-2n)}{n!(-|\ell|)_{N+n}(|\ell|+1-n)}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-n)_{k}(n-1-|\ell|)_{k}}{(-N)_{k}(-N)_{k}}{\mathcal{E}}_{k}(x,y;\ell).

If
n>\ell_{\rm min}
, then
\{\nu:|\nu|=n\}
contains
\nu
outside of
H_{\ell,N}^{d}
, so that some
of
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N)
with
|\nu|=n
vanishes on
V_{\ell,N}^{d}
by Theorem
3.6
. One
may ask if it is possible to include such polynomials in the summation of
{\mathsf{P}}_{n}({\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N};\cdot,\cdot)

so that some version of (
5.2
) can be deduced from that of
P_{n}(W_{{\kappa},N};\cdot,\cdot)
.
The answer, however, is negative since the norm of such polynomials,
B_{\nu}(\ell,N)
, is necessarily
zero and, as a result, no such terms can be included in the sum of
{\mathsf{P}}_{n}({\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N};\cdot,\cdot)
.

There is an exceptional case when
d=2
, for which Hahn polynomials of negative integer parameters of
degree
n
have full range for all suitable
n
. This is the case when
\ell_{1}=\ell_{2}=\ell_{3}=\ell
and

N=2\ell
, so that the set
V_{\ell,N}^{2}
consists of lattice points in a regular triangle, whereas the set

H_{\ell,N}^{2}
is given below.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
\ell
be a positive integer. If
d=2
,
\ell_{i}=\ell
for
1\leq i\leq 3
and
N=2\ell
, then

H_{\ell,N}^{2}=\{\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{2}:|\nu|\leq\ell\}.

Proof.

Since
N=2\ell
and
|\ell|=3\ell
, we also have
\mathpzc{a}_{1}=2\ell-2\nu_{2}
and
\mathpzc{a}_{2}=\ell
. Hence,

H_{\ell,N}^{2}=\big\{(\nu_{1},\nu_{2}):|\nu|\leq 2\ell,\,|\nu|\leq\ell,\,\nu_{1}\leq\ell,\,\nu_{2}\leq\ell,\nu_{1}+2\nu_{2}\leq 2\ell\big\}

by the definition of
H_{\ell,N}^{2}
. Clearly,
|\nu|\leq\ell
implies
\nu_{i}\leq\ell
and it also implies

\nu_{1}+2\nu_{2}=\nu_{1}+|\nu|\leq 2\ell
, which proves the statement.
∎

For the Hahn polynomials
Q_{\nu}(\cdot;{\kappa},N)
on
V_{N}^{d}
, the closed form expression of the reproducing
kernel is used to prove that the Poisson kernel

\Phi_{r}(W_{{\kappa},N};x,y):=\sum_{n=0}^{N}{\mathsf{P}}_{n}(W_{{\kappa},N};x,y)r^{n},\qquad 0\leq r\leq 1

is nonnegative for all
x,y\in V_{N}^{d}
and
0\leq r\leq 1
, see
[
17
]
. The proof relies on the fact that the
corresponding function

{\mathcal{E}}_{k}(x,y;{\kappa}):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|{\gamma}|=k\end{subarray}}\frac{(-X)_{{\gamma}}(-Y)_{\gamma}}{({\kappa}+1)_{\gamma}{\gamma}!},

for
{\kappa}_{i}>-1
is nonnegative for
x,y\in V_{N}^{d}
.

For the Hahn polynomials with negative integer parameters, we could define the Poisson kernel analogously
as

\Phi_{r}({\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N};x,y):=\sum_{n=0}^{M}{\mathsf{P}}_{n}({\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N};x,y)r^{n},\qquad 0\leq r\leq 1,

where
M\leq N
is the highest degree
M=\max\{|\nu|:\nu\in H_{\ell,N}^{d}\}
. This kernel, however,
is no longer nonnegative, as shown by examples of small
\ell
and
N
. One of the reasons that the
proof in
[
17
]
fails is that
{\mathcal{E}}_{k}(x,y;\ell)
is of the sign
(-1)^{k}
instead of nonnegative. Furthermore,
for
M>\ell_{\min}
, we could question if the definition
\Phi_{r}({\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N};\cdot,\cdot)
reflects the symmetry
of
{\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N}^{d}
. In this regard, we may examine the special case when
d=2
,
\ell_{i}=\ell
and
N=2\ell

in Proposition
5.4
closely.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
d=2
,
\ell_{i}=\ell
,
1\leq i\leq 3
, and
N=2\ell
. Then, for all
x_{2}
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{r}\big({\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N};\big((x_{1},x_{2}),(0,\ell)\big)\big)=\sum_{n=0}^{\ell}\frac{(-1)^{n}(-3\ell-1)_{n}(-3\ell)_{2n}}{n!(-3\ell-1)_{2n}}{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x_{1};\ell,2\ell,2\ell)r^{n}

Proof.

Using the explicit formula of
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(\cdot;\ell,N)
in (
4.1
), it is easy to verify that

{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}}((0,\ell);\ell,2\ell)=(-2\ell)_{\nu_{1}}\delta_{\nu_{2},0}

by the Chu-Vandermonde identity. For
\nu_{2}=0
, we have

{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1},0}(x;\ell,2\ell)=(-2\ell)_{\nu_{1}}{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1}}(x_{1};\ell,2\ell,2\ell).

Furthermore, by (
3.12
), the norm of
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1},0}
is equal to

B_{\nu_{1},0}(\ell,2\ell)=\frac{(-1)^{\nu_{1}}\nu_{1}!(-2\ell)_{\nu_{1}}^{2}(-3\ell-1)_{2\nu_{1}}}{(-3\ell-1)_{\nu_{1}}(-3\ell)_{2\nu_{1}}}.

Together, these identities lead to

\displaystyle{\mathsf{P}}_{n}\big({\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,2\ell};(\ell,x_{2}),(0,\ell)\big)\,

\displaystyle=\sum_{\nu_{1}=0}^{n}\frac{{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1},n-\nu_{1}}((0,\ell);\ell,2\ell){\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{1},n-\nu_{1}}(x;\ell,2\ell)}{B_{\nu_{1},n-\nu_{1}}(\ell,2\ell)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}(-3\ell-1)_{n}(-3\ell)_{2n}}{n!(-3\ell-1)_{2n}}{\mathsf{Q}}_{n}(x_{1};\ell,2\ell,2\ell).

from which the formula for
\Phi_{r}\big({\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,N};(\ell,x_{2}),(0,\ell)\big)
follows immediately.
∎

This gives an explicit expression for the Poisson kernel
\Phi_{r}({\mathsf{H}}_{\ell,2\ell};\cdot,\cdot)
, which is a polynomial
of degree
\ell
in the variable
r
, at the points
(x_{1},x_{2})
and
(0,\ell)
in
V_{\ell,2\ell}^{2}
, which depends
on
x_{1}
but not
x_{2}
. Numerical experiment shows that this polynomial changes sign once on
[0,1]

when
\ell
is even and
x=(\ell,x_{2})
and, in some cases, when
\ell
is odd and
x
is an even integer.

6.
Bispectral properties

The Hahn polynomials for the hypergeometric distribution can be characterized as common eigenfunctions of two families of commutative algebras of difference operators: one acting on the variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}
, and another one acting on the indices
\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{d}
. These operators can be linked to mutually commuting symmetries of a discrete extension of the generic quantum superintegrable system on the sphere
[
3
,
8
,
11
,
13
]
.
We discuss these families of operators in the next subsections.

In this section, let
\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N)
denote the Hahn polynomials normalized as follows

(6.1)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N)=\frac{1}{(-N)_{|\nu|}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}

\displaystyle(-N+|{\mathbf{x}}_{j-1}|+|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{j+1}|)_{\nu_{j}}

\displaystyle\times{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu_{j}}\left(x_{j};\ell_{j},\mathpzc{a}_{j},N-|{\mathbf{x}}_{j-1}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{j+1}|\right).

The polynomial
\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}
differs from
{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}
by an an extra factor
(-N)_{|\nu|}
in the denominator. This factor makes it possible to write the recurrence operators
{\mathcal{L}}_{d}^{\nu}
, defined in
(
6.4
), in its relatively simple form.

6.1.
Spectral equations in the variables.

We denote by
\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{d}\}
the standard basis for
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and by
E_{x_{i}}
and
E_{x_{i}}^{-1}
the shift operators acting on a function
f(x)
as follows

\displaystyle E_{x_{i}}f(x)=f(x+e_{i})\quad\hbox{and}\quad E_{x_{i}}^{-1}f(x)=f(x-e_{i}).

The operator

(6.2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}^{x}_{d}

\displaystyle:={\mathcal{L}}^{x}_{d}(x;\ell;N)=\sum_{1\leq i\neq j\leq d}x_{j}(x_{i}-\ell_{i})(E_{i}E_{j}^{-1}-1)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{d}(x_{i}-\ell_{i})(N-|x|)(E_{i}-1)+\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}(N-|x|-\ell_{d+1})(E_{i}^{-1}-1),

is self-adjoint with respect to the hypergeometric distribution and acts diagonally on the basis of polynomials in
(
6.1
) with eigenvalue
-|\nu|(|\nu|-|\ell|-1)
which depends only on the total degree
|\nu|
of the
polynomial
\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N)
, see
[
10
, Section 5]
. Fix now
k<d
and note now that, up to a factor
independent of
x_{1},\dots,x_{d-k}
, the product of the last
k
-terms in (
6.1
) can be regarded as
a Hahn polynomial in the variables
\tilde{x}={\mathbf{x}}^{d-k+1}=(x_{d-k+1},\dots,x_{d})
, with indices

\tilde{\nu}={\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{d-k+1}=(\nu_{d-k+1},\dots,\nu_{d})
, and parameters

\tilde{\ell}={\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{d-k+1}=(\ell_{d-k+1},\dots,\ell_{d+1})
,
\tilde{N}=N-|{\mathbf{x}}_{d-k}|
. Therefore, if we set

{\mathcal{L}}^{x}_{k}:={\mathcal{L}}^{x}_{k}({\mathbf{x}}^{d-k+1};{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{d-k+1};N-|{\mathbf{x}}_{d-k}|),\qquad\text{ for }k=1,\dots,d-1,

we see that polynomials in (
6.1
) will be eigenfunctions of the operators
{\mathcal{L}}^{x}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{L}}^{x}_{d}

and satisfy the spectral equations, for
k=1,\dots,d
,

(6.3)

{\mathcal{L}}^{x}_{k}\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N)=-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{d-k+1}|(|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{d-k+1}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{d-k+1}|-1)\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N).

From these equations it follows easily that the operators
{\mathcal{L}}^{x}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{L}}^{x}_{d}
commute with each other.
They generate a Gaudin subalgebra for a representation of the Kohno-Drinfeld algebra associated with the
hypergeometric distribution. The operators in the larger algebra can be regarded as symmetries, or integrals
of motion, for a discrete extension of the generic quantum superintegrable system on the sphere, see
[
11
]
.

6.2.
Spectral equations in the indices.

In this section, we will use the shift operators
E_{\nu_{i}}

and
E_{\nu_{i}}^{-1}
acting on a function
f_{\nu}
as follows

\displaystyle E_{\nu_{i}}f_{\nu}=f_{\nu+e_{i}}\quad\hbox{and}\quad E_{\nu_{i}}^{-1}f_{\nu}=f_{\nu-e_{i}}.

For
j,k\in\{0,\pm 1\}^{2}
and
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, we define
B_{i}^{j,k}

as follows

\displaystyle B_{i}^{0,0}=|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i}|(|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i}|-1)+|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i+1}|(|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i+1}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i+1}|-1)+\frac{|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i+1}|(|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i}|+2)}{2},

\displaystyle B_{i}^{0,1}=-\nu_{i}(\nu_{i}+2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i+1}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i}|-1),

\displaystyle B_{i}^{0,-1}=(\ell_{i}-\nu_{i})(\nu_{i}+2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i+1}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i+1}|-1),

\displaystyle B_{i}^{1,0}=(\ell_{i}-\nu_{i})(\nu_{i}+2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i+1}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i}|-1),

\displaystyle B_{i}^{-1,0}=-\nu_{i}(\nu_{i}+2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i+1}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i+1}|-1),

\displaystyle B_{i}^{1,1}=(\nu_{i}+2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i+1}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i}|-1)(\nu_{i}+2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i+1}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i}|),

\displaystyle B_{i}^{-1,1}=\nu_{i}(\nu_{i}-1),

\displaystyle B_{i}^{1,-1}=(\ell_{i}-\nu_{i})(\ell_{i}-\nu_{i}-1),

\displaystyle B_{i}^{-1,-1}=(\nu_{i}+2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i+1}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i+1}|-1)(\nu_{i}+2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i+1}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i+1}|-2).

We extend the formulas above and define
B_{0}^{0,k}
for
k\in\{0,\pm 1\}
as follows

B_{0}^{0,0}=-N+|\ell|/2,\quad B_{0}^{0,1}=N-|\nu|,\quad B_{0}^{0,-1}=|\nu|-|\ell|+N-1.

Next, for
i\in\{1,\dots,d\}
we define

\displaystyle b_{i}^{0}=\frac{(2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i}|)(2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i}|-2)}{2},

\displaystyle b_{i}^{1}=(2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i}|)(2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i}|-1),

\displaystyle b_{i}^{-1}=(2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i}|-2)(2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{i}|-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{i}|-1),

and for
\mu=(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{d})\in\{0,\pm 1\}^{d}
we set

C_{\mu}=\frac{\prod_{k=0}^{d}B_{k}^{\mu_{k},\mu_{k+1}}}{\prod_{k=1}^{d}b_{k}^{\mu_{k}}},

with the convention that
\mu_{0}=\mu_{d+1}=0
.
With the above notations, we define an operator acting on the indices as follows

(6.4)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}^{\nu}_{d}:={\mathcal{L}}^{\nu}_{d}(\nu;\ell;N)=\sum_{0\neq\mu\in\{-1,0,1\}^{d}}C_{\mu}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{d}E_{\nu_{k}}^{\mu_{k}-\mu_{k+1}}-1\right).

Note that this operator is significantly more complicated than the difference operator
{\mathcal{L}}^{x}_{d}

in (
6.2
). It can be obtained as a limit of the image of the
d
-dimensional Racah operator
under the bispectral involution associated with the Racah polynomials, see
[
7
, Section 5.2]
. In particular,
this shows that

(6.5)

{\mathcal{L}}^{\nu}_{d}(\nu;\ell;N)\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N)=|x|\,\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N).

Remark 6.1

(Boundary conditions)
.

Note that the operator
{\mathcal{L}}^{\nu}_{d}
contains backward shift operators, and the polynomials
\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N)

are defined for
\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
. However, one can easily see that the coefficients of the operator
{\mathcal{L}}^{\nu}_{d}

that multiply the polynomials containing negative indices are zero, so we can simply ignore these terms. Indeed,
the operator
{\mathcal{L}}^{\nu}_{d}
will contain a negative power of
E_{\nu_{k}}
in one of the following cases:

Case 1:

\mu_{k}-\mu_{k+1}=-1
, and the corresponding term in the sum in (
6.4
)
contains
E_{\nu_{k}}^{-1}
. Note that
\mu_{k}-\mu_{k+1}=-1
is possible for two sets of indices

–

when
(\mu_{k},\mu_{k+1})=(-1,0)
, in which case
C_{\mu}
contains the term

B_{k}^{-1,0}
, which is
0
when
\nu_{k}=0
,

–

or when
(\mu_{k},\mu_{k+1})=(0,1)
, in which case
C_{\mu}
contains the term
B_{k}^{0,1}
, which is

0
when
\nu_{k}=0
.

Case 2:

\mu_{k}-\mu_{k+1}=-2
, and the corresponding term in the sum in (
6.4
) contains
E_{\nu_{k}}^{-2}
. This happens only when
\mu_{k}=-1
and
\mu_{k+1}=1
, in which case
C_{\mu}
contains the term
B_{k}^{-1,1}
, which is
0
when
\nu_{k}=0
or
\nu_{k}=1
.

This shows that we can ignore all terms containing negative indices. With this convention, the results in
[
7
, Section 5.2]
imply that (
6.5
) holds for all
\nu\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
when
\ell_{j}
and
N
are generic (non-integer) parameters.
For parameters
\ell_{j}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, equation (
6.5
) holds when the indices on the left-hand side
belong to the set
H_{\ell,N}^{d}
. We will use the same convention for the other operators we construct in this section.

Similarly to the difference operators in
x
, we can construct a family of commuting difference operators acting on the indices
\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{d}
, which represent the multiplication by the variables
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}
in the basis (
6.1
).

Theorem 6.2
.

If we set

{\mathcal{L}}^{\nu}_{k}:={\mathcal{L}}^{\nu}_{k}({\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}_{k};\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{k},|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{k+1}|-2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{k+1}|;N-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{k+1}|),\qquad\text{ for }k=1,\dots,d-1,

the polynomials in (
6.1
) will satisfy also the following spectral equations

(6.6)

{\mathcal{L}}^{\nu}_{k}\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N)=|{\mathbf{x}}_{k}|\,\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N),\quad\text{for }k=1,\dots,d.

Proof.

If
k=d
, equation (
6.6
) follows from (
6.5
).
Fix now
k<d
and note that, for
j>k
the terms in the product on the right-hand side of (
6.1
) are independent of
{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}_{k}=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{k})
. From this, it follows that, up to a factor independent of

\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{k}
,
\widehat{\mathsf{Q}}_{\nu}(x;\ell,N)
coincides with the Hahn polynomial in the variables

\tilde{x}={\mathbf{x}}_{k}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
, with indices
\tilde{\nu}={\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}_{k}=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{k})
, and parameters

\tilde{\ell}=(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\dots,\ell_{k},|{\boldsymbol{\large{\ell}}}^{k+1}|-2|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{k+1}|)
,
\tilde{N}=N-|{\boldsymbol{\large{\nu}}}^{k+1}|
.
The proof now follows from the
k
-dimensional analog of (
6.5
).
∎

6.3.

In this subsection, we write the operators and the difference equations in Theorem

The operator in (

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}^{\nu}_{2}

\displaystyle=C_{1,0}E_{\nu_{1}}+C_{1,1}E_{\nu_{2}}+C_{1,-1}E_{\nu_{1}}^{2}E_{\nu_{2}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad+C_{0,1}E_{\nu_{1}}^{-1}E_{\nu_{2}}+C_{0,-1}E_{\nu_{1}}E_{\nu_{2}}^{-1}+\widehat{C}_{0,0}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\times(2\nu_{2}(\nu_{2}-\ell_{2}-\ell_{3}-1)+\ell_{3}(\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}+2))

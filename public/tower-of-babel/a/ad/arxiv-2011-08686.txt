Ensemble fluctuations matter for variances of macroscopic variables
G. George, L. Klochko, A.N. Semenov, J. Baschnagel, J.P. Wittmer

Abstract
Extending recent work on stress fluctuations in complex fluids and amorphous solids we describe in general terms the ensemble average v ⁡ ( Δ ​ t ) v(\Delta t) and the standard deviation δ ​ v ​ ( Δ ​ t ) \delta v(\Delta t) of the variance v ⁡ [ 𝐱 ] v[\mathbf{x}] of time series 𝐱 \mathbf{x} of a stochastic process x ⁡ ( t ) x(t) measured over a finite sampling time Δ ​ t \Delta t . Assuming a stationary, Gaussian and ergodic process, δ ​ v \delta v is given by a functional δ ​ v G ​ [ h ] \delta v_{\mathrm{G}}[h] of the autocorrelation function h ⁡ ( t ) h(t) . δ ​ v ​ ( Δ ​ t ) \delta v(\Delta t) is shown to become large and similar to v ⁡ ( Δ ​ t ) v(\Delta t) if Δ ​ t \Delta t corresponds to a fast relaxation process. Albeit δ ​ v = δ ​ v G ​ [ h ] \delta v=\delta v_{\mathrm{G}}[h] does not hold in general for non-ergodic systems, the deviations for common systems with many microstates are merely finite-size corrections. Various issues are illustrated for shear-stress fluctuations in simple coarse-grained model systems.

5
System-size effects

Due to the central limit theorem

the stochastic process of many systems
is to a good approximation Gaussian since the data entries
x_{i}
are averages over
N_{\mathrm{m}}\gg 1

microscopic (often unknown or inaccessible) contributions

.
Specifically, we shall consider below the instantaneous shear stress

\hat{\sigma}_{i}=\int\mathrm{d}\underline{r}\ \hat{\sigma}_{i,\underline{r}}/V

being the volume average over the local shear stress
\hat{\sigma}_{i,\underline{r}}
.
For such intensive field averages
N_{\mathrm{m}}
corresponds to the number of local volume elements

computed,
i.e.
N_{\mathrm{m}}\approx V/dV
.
Albeit these microscopic contributions

may be correlated, i.e. they may not all fluctuate independently,
the fluctuations of the
x_{i}
commonly decrease with increasing

.
Since

for uncorrelated variables

this reference in the definition of the data entries by rescaling

.
(This is done in Sec.

by rescaling the stress by
\sqrt{V}
.)
For perfectly uncorrelated microscopic variables
x_{im}
subject to a finite quenched random field this leads to

\displaystyle v\propto h\propto\delta v_{\mathrm{G}}

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle N_{\mathrm{m}}^{0}\mbox{ and }

(48)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathrm{ne}}\equiv\lim_{\Delta t\to\infty}\delta v(\Delta t)

\displaystyle\propto

\displaystyle N_{\mathrm{m}}^{-\gamma_{\mathrm{ext}}}\mbox{ with }\gamma_{\mathrm{ext}}=1/2.

-independence of

-independence of

To see that

-independent) ensemble averages
\left<\ldots\right>_{c}

for the different quenched meta-basins

Substituting

Using that also the variances

A smaller exponent

Details will be given elsewhere

The generally important point is here that

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the Lotka-Volterra prey-predator models consisting of two species on graphs under Neumann boundary condition and the condition that there is no boundary condition. Our results show that the existence of solutions and the global stability of the unique constant equilibrium solution of each parabolic system.



1.
Introduction

Let
G=G(V,E)
be a finite connected weighted graph, where
V
denotes the vertex set and
E
denotes the edge set.
For each edge
xy\in E
, We suppose that its weight
w_{xy}>0
and that
w_{xy}=w_{yx}
. Let
\mu:V\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be a finite measure. For any function
u:V\to\mathbb{R}
, the Laplacian of
u
is defined by

\Delta_{V}u(x)=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{yx}(u(y)-u(x)),

where
y\sim x
means
xy\in E
.

For a subgraph
\Omega
of a graph
G=G(V,E)
, the (vertex) boundary
\partial{\Omega}(\neq\emptyset)
of
\Omega
is the set of all vertices
z\in V
not in
\Omega
but adjacent to some vertex in
\Omega
, i.e.,

\partial{\Omega}:=\{z\in V\backslash\Omega|z\sim y~\text{for some}~y\in\Omega\}.

Denote
\bar{\Omega}
a graph whose vertices and edges are in
\Omega
and vertices in
\partial{\Omega}
. For any function
u:\bar{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
, the Laplacian of
u
on
\bar{\Omega}
is defined by

\Delta_{\Omega}u(x)=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in\bar{\Omega}:y\sim x}w_{yx}(u(y)-u(x)).

Let the (outward) normal derivative
\frac{\partial u}{\partial_{\Omega}n}
at
z\in\partial\Omega
by

(1.1)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial_{\Omega}n}(z):=\sum_{y\in{\Omega}}(u(z)-u(y))\frac{\omega_{zy}}{\mu(z)}.

For given functions
u_{0},v_{0}:V\to\mathbb{R}
and
\tilde{u}_{0},\tilde{v}_{0}:\bar{\Omega}\to\mathbb{R}
, we study the following Lotka-Volterra prey-predator systems

(1.2)

\begin{cases}u_{t}-d_{1}\Delta_{V}u=u\left(a_{1}-b_{1}u-c_{1}v\right),&x\in V,t>0,\\
v_{t}-d_{2}\Delta_{V}v=v\left(a_{2}+b_{2}u-c_{2}v\right),&x\in V,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\geq 0,~v(x,0)=v_{0}(x)\geq 0,&x\in V,\end{cases}

(1.3)

\begin{cases}u_{t}-d_{1}\Delta_{{\Omega}}u=u\left(a_{1}-b_{1}u-c_{1}v\right),&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
v_{t}-d_{2}\Delta_{{\Omega}}v=v\left(a_{2}+b_{2}u-c_{2}v\right),&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=\tilde{u}_{0}(x)\geq 0,~v(x,0)=\tilde{v}_{0}(x)\geq 0,&x\in\bar{\Omega},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial_{\Omega}n}=0,~\frac{\partial v}{\partial_{\Omega}n}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where the positive constants
d_{i}
and
a_{i}
(
i=1,2
) stand for the diffusion, growth rates of the populations
u
,
v
, respectively;
b_{1}>0
and
c_{2}>0
represent the rates of death of the species
u
and
v
, respectively;
c_{1}
and
b_{2}
are positive constants that measure the interaction of the populations
u
and
v
, respectively.

The system (
1.3
) can be regarded as the space-discretized version of the reaction diffusion system with Neumann boundary condition

(1.4)

\begin{cases}u_{t}-d_{1}\Delta u=u\left(a_{1}-b_{1}u-c_{1}v\right),&x\in Q,~t>0\\
v_{t}-d_{2}\Delta v=v\left(a_{2}+b_{2}u-c_{2}v\right),&x\in Q,~t>0,\\
u(x,0)=\tilde{u}_{0}(x),~v(x,0)=\tilde{v}_{0}(x),&x\in\bar{Q},\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial n}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}=0,&x\in\partial Q.\end{cases}

where
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain, and
\Delta
is the standard Laplacian operator, which describe the isotropic dispersal of species. The problem (
1.4
) has been studied by
[
11
]
with
a_{2}
replaced with a constant
e_{2}<0
. The authors in
[
11
]
showed that all positive solutions of (
1.4
), regardless of the initial value, converge to the constant equilibrium solution as
t\to\infty
. For more studies on diffusive Lotka-Volterra prey-predator systems of two species, one may refer to
[
3
,
4
,
13
,
17
,
18
]
and the references therein.

In recent decade years, increasing efforts have been devoted to the investigation of anisotropy diffusion on graphs
[
1
,
2
,
6
,
9
,
10
,
12
]
. For further related works, Gao et al.
[
6
]
use the method of establishing Lyapunov functions based on graph-theoretical approach to analyse the global stability for a predator-prey model among
n
patches.
Yokoi et al.
[
15
]
present two-patches system composed of prey and ambush predator. Nagatani
[
16
]
deal with a metapopulation model on various graphs for a prey–predator system with different migration rates.
Slavík
[
19
]
consider a model of two competing species of Lotka-Volterra type with migration, where the spatial domain is an arbitrary network. They study the existence of stationary states and the asymptotic behavior of solutions.

Here, one of our purpose of this paper is to discuss the boundeness of solutions to (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) by using upper and lower solutions method. Therefore, we have the following results:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite connected graph. Suppose that
(u_{0},v_{0})\geq(0,0)
. Then (
1.2
) admits a unique solution
(u_{1},u_{2})
that satisfies

(1.5)

(0,0)\leq(u_{1},u_{2})\leq\left(M_{1},M_{2}\right)~\text{for}~x\in V~\text{and}~t\geq 0,

where

M_{1}=\max\{\max\limits_{V}u_{0}(x),\frac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}\}\;\mbox{and}\;M_{2}=\max\left\{\frac{a_{2}+b_{2}M_{1}}{c_{2}},\max\limits_{V}v_{0}(x)\right\}.

Furthermore, if
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
and
v_{0}\not\equiv 0
on
V
, then
(u,v)
is positive for
x\in V
and
t>0
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\bar{\Omega}
be a finite connected graph. Assume that
(\tilde{u}_{0},\tilde{v}_{0})\geq(0,0)
. Then (
1.3
) admits a unique solution
(u_{3},u_{4})
that satisfies

(1.6)

(0,0)\leq(u_{3},u_{4})\leq\left(M_{3},M_{4}\right)~\text{for}~x\in\bar{\Omega}~\text{and}~t\geq 0,

where

M_{3}=\max\{\max\limits_{\overline{\Omega}}\tilde{u}_{0}(x),\frac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}\}\;\mbox{and}\;M_{4}=\max\left\{\frac{a_{2}+b_{2}M_{3}}{c_{2}},\max\limits_{\overline{\Omega}}\tilde{v}_{0}(x)\right\}.

Moreover, if
\tilde{u}_{0}\not\equiv 0
and
\tilde{v}_{0}\not\equiv 0
on
\bar{\Omega}
, then
(u,v)
is positive for
x\in\bar{\Omega}
and
t>0
.

The equilibrium problems corresponding to (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) are the following elliptic systems:

(1.7)

\begin{cases}-d_{1}\Delta_{V}u=u\left(a_{1}-b_{1}u-c_{1}v\right),&x\in V,\\
-d_{2}\Delta_{V}v=v\left(a_{2}+b_{2}u-c_{2}v\right),&x\in V,\end{cases}

and

(1.8)

\begin{cases}-d_{1}\Delta_{\Omega}u=u\left(a_{1}-b_{1}u-c_{1}v\right),&x\in\Omega,\\
-d_{2}\Delta_{\Omega}v=v\left(a_{2}+b_{2}u-c_{2}v\right),&x\in\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial_{\Omega}n}=0,~\frac{\partial v}{\partial_{\Omega}n}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

respectively.
The biologically interesting case occurs when

(1.9)

\frac{a_{1}}{c_{1}}>\frac{a_{2}}{c_{2}}.

Clearly, under the hypothese (
1.9
), both (
1.2
) and (
1.3
) admit a unique positive constant steady-state
(e,g)
, where

(1.10)

e=\frac{a_{1}c_{2}-c_{1}a_{2}}{b_{1}c_{2}+c_{1}b_{2}},\hskip 10.00002ptg=\frac{b_{1}a_{2}+a_{1}b_{2}}{b_{1}c_{2}+c_{1}b_{2}}.

The other goal of the present paper is to analyse the global stability of the equilibrium
(e,g)
by energy method. Now, we state the major results of the paper as following:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite connected graph and
(u_{1},u_{2})
be the solution to (
1.2
). If
\frac{a_{1}}{c_{1}}>\frac{a_{2}}{c_{2}}
, then

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}(u_{1},u_{2})=(e,g)\;\mbox{ uniformly for}\;x\in V

provided that
u_{0}\geq,\not\equiv 0
and
v_{0}\geq,\not\equiv 0
on
V
, where
(e,g)
is defined by (
1.10
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\bar{\Omega}
be a finite connected graph and
(u_{3},u_{4})
be the solution to (
1.3
). If
\frac{a_{1}}{c_{1}}>\frac{a_{2}}{c_{2}}
, then

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}(u_{3},u_{4})=(e,g)\;\mbox{uniformly for}\;x\in\bar{\Omega}

provided that
\tilde{u}_{0}\geq,\not\equiv 0
and
\tilde{v}_{0}\geq,\not\equiv 0
on
\overline{\Omega}
, where
(e,g)
is defined by (
1.10
).

) and (

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Suppose that
u_{0}\geq 0
and
v_{0}\geq 0
on
V
.
Let

M_{1}=\max\{\max\limits_{V}u_{0},\frac{a_{1}}{b_{1}}\}~\text{ and}~M_{2}=\max\left\{\frac{a_{2}+b_{2}M_{1}}{c_{2}},\max\limits_{V}v_{0}(x)\right\}.

It is easy to check that
\left(\bar{u}_{1},\bar{u}_{2}\right)\equiv\left(M_{1},M_{2}\right)
and
\left(\underline{u}_{1},\underline{u}_{2}\right)\equiv(0,0)
satisfy

\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} &\frac{\partial\bar{u}_{1}}{\partial t}-d_{1}\Delta_{V}\bar{u}_{1}\geq\bar{u}_{1}\left(a_{1}-b_{1}\bar{u}_{1}-c_{1}\underline{u}_{2}\right),x\in V,t>0,\\
&\frac{\partial\bar{u}_{2}}{\partial t}-d_{2}\Delta_{V}\bar{u}_{2}\geq\bar{u}_{2}\left(a_{2}+b_{2}\bar{u}_{1}-c_{2}\bar{u}_{2}\right),x\in V,t>0,\\
&\frac{\partial\underline{u}_{1}}{\partial t}-d_{1}\Delta_{V}\underline{u}_{1}\leq\underline{u}_{1}\left(a_{1}-b_{1}\underline{u}_{1}-c_{1}\bar{u}_{2}\right),x\in V,t>0,\\
&\frac{\partial\underline{u}_{2}}{\partial t}-d_{2}\Delta_{V}\underline{u}_{2}\leq\underline{u}_{2}\left(a_{2}+b_{2}\underline{u}_{1}-c_{2}\underline{u}_{2}\right),x\in V,t>0,\\
&\bar{u}_{1}(0)\geq u_{0}(x)\geq\underline{u}_{1}(0),\quad\bar{u}_{2}(0)\geq v_{0}(x)\geq\underline{u}_{2}(0),~x\in V.\end{aligned}\end{cases}

By
[
8
, Definition 3.4 ]
, we see that
(M_{1},M_{2})
and
(0,0)
is a pair of coupled upper and lower solutions of (
1.2
). By virtue of
[
8
, Theorem 3.4]
, (
1.2
) admits a unique solution
(u_{1},u_{2})
satisfying

(2.1)

(0,0)\leq(u_{1},u_{2})\leq\left(M_{1},M_{2}\right)~\text{for}~x\in V~\text{and}~t\geq 0.

Furthermore, if
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
and
v_{0}\not\equiv 0
on
V
, then
u_{1}>0
and
u_{2}>0
for
x\in V
and
t>0
, by
[
8
, Theorem 2.4]
.

1.2
.
Suppose that
\tilde{u}_{0}\geq 0
and
\tilde{v}_{0}\geq 0
on
\bar{\Omega}
.
Since
(\bar{u}_{3},\bar{u}_{4})\equiv(M_{3},M_{4})
and
(\underline{u}_{3},\underline{u}_{4})\equiv(0,0)

, we can conclude that

).

, (

) admits a unique solution

satisfying

3.
The proof of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4

In this section, we prove the global stability of the unique positive constant steady-state
(e,g)
. For this purpose, we introduce some notations on graphs. For any functions
u
and
v

:V\to\mathbb{R}
. Define the gradient form of
u
and
v
as

\Gamma(u,v)(x)=\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}(u(y)-u(x))(v(y)-v(x)).

Write
\Gamma(u)=\Gamma(u,u)
.
Denote the length of the gradient of
u
by

|\nabla u|(x)=\sqrt{\Gamma(u)(x)}=\left(\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}(u(y)-u(x))^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Define, for any function
u:V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, an integral of
u
on
V
by

\int\limits_{V}ud\mu=\sum\limits_{x\in V}\mu(x)u(x).

For any functions
u,v
on
V
, we use integration by parts to conclude that

(3.1)

\int_{V}u\Delta_{V}vd\mu=-\int_{V}\Gamma(u,v)d\mu.

Proof of theorem
1.3
.
For any solution
(u_{1},u_{2})
of system (
1.2
), we define

L(t):=\int\limits_{V}E(u_{1}(x,t),u_{2}(x,t))d\mu

where

E\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right):=b_{2}u_{1}-b_{2}e\ln\left(\frac{u_{1}}{b_{2}e}\right)+c_{1}u_{2}-c_{1}g\ln\left(\frac{u_{2}}{c_{1}g}\right).

Clearly,

(3.2)

E\left(u_{1},u_{2}\right)\geq E(e,g)~\text{for~all}~u_{1}>0~\text{and}~u_{2}>0.

Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{dL}{dt}=

\displaystyle\int_{V}\left[b_{2}\left(1-\frac{e}{u_{1}}\right)\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial t}+c_{1}\left(1-\frac{g}{u_{2}}\right)\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial t}\right]d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{V}\bigg\{b_{2}\left(1-\frac{e}{u_{1}}\right)\left[d_{1}\Delta_{V}u_{1}+u_{1}\left(a_{1}-b_{1}u_{1}-c_{1}u_{2}\right)\right]+

\displaystyle c_{1}\left(1-\frac{g}{u_{2}}\right)\left[d_{2}\Delta_{V}u_{2}+u_{2}\left(a_{2}+b_{2}u_{1}-c_{2}u_{2}\right)\right]\bigg\}d\mu.

A direct calculation shows that

(3.3)

\displaystyle b_{2}\left(1-\frac{e}{u_{1}}\right)u_{1}\left(a_{1}-b_{1}u_{1}-c_{1}u_{2}\right)+c_{1}\left(1-\frac{g}{u_{2}}\right)u_{2}\left(a_{2}+b_{2}u_{1}-c_{2}u_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{2}\left(u_{1}-e\right)\left[a_{1}-b_{1}\left(u_{1}-e\right)-c_{1}\left(u_{2}-g\right)-b_{1}e-c_{1}g\right]

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle c_{1}\left(u_{2}-g\right)\left[a_{2}+b_{2}\left(u_{1}-e\right)-c_{2}\left(u_{2}-g\right)+b_{2}e-c_{2}g\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-b_{1}b_{2}\left(u_{1}-e\right)^{2}-c_{1}c_{2}\left(u_{2}-g\right)^{2}.

From (
1.5
) and (
3.1
), we obtain that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{V}\left(1-\frac{e}{u_{1}}\right)\Delta_{V}u_{1}d\mu

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{V}\Gamma\left(\frac{e}{u_{1}},u_{1}\right)d\mu

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{V}\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}\left[\frac{e}{u_{1}(x)}-\frac{e}{u_{1}(y)}\right]w_{xy}\left[u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)\right]d\mu

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{V}\frac{-e}{2\mu(x)}\sum_{y_{\sim}x}w_{xy}\frac{\left[u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)\right]^{2}}{u_{1}(x)u_{1}(y)}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{V}-\frac{e}{M_{1}^{2}}\frac{1}{2\mu(x)}\sum_{y\sim x}w_{xy}\left[u_{1}(x)-u_{1}(y)\right]^{2}d\mu=-\frac{e}{M_{1}^{2}}\int\limits_{V}\left|\nabla u_{1}\right|^{2}d\mu.

Similarly, we have

\int\limits_{V}\left(1-\frac{g}{u_{2}}\right)\Delta_{V}u_{2}d\mu\leq-\frac{g}{M_{2}^{2}}\int\limits_{V}\left|\nabla u_{2}\right|^{2}d\mu\text{. }

Therefore, we deduce that

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{dL}{dt}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{b_{2}ed_{1}}{M_{1}^{2}}\int\limits_{V}\left|\nabla u_{1}\right|^{2}d\mu-\frac{c_{1}gd_{2}}{M_{2}^{2}}\int\limits_{V}\left|\nabla u_{2}\right|^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle-\int\limits_{V}b_{1}b_{2}\left(u_{1}-e\right)^{2}d\mu-\int\limits_{V}c_{1}c_{2}\left(u_{2}-g\right)^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle\leq-\int\limits_{V}b_{1}b_{2}\left(u_{1}-e\right)^{2}d\mu-\int\limits_{V}c_{1}c_{2}\left(u_{2}-g\right)^{2}d\mu.

Setting
F(t)=\int_{V}\left[u_{1}(x,t)-e\right]^{2}d\mu
. It follows from (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\frac{dF}{dt}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{V}2\left(u_{1}(x,t)-e\right)\left[d_{1}\Delta_{V}u_{1}+u_{1}\left(a_{1}-b_{1}u_{1}-c_{1}u_{2}\right)\right]d\mu

\displaystyle=2\int\limits_{V}\left[-d_{1}\Gamma\left(u_{1},u_{1}\right)+\left(u_{1}-e\right)u_{1}\left(a_{1}-b_{1}u_{1}-c_{1}u_{2}\right)\right]d\mu.

Thus, by (
1.5
), we can find a constant
B=B(M_{1},M_{2},e,V)>0
such that

(3.5)

\frac{dF}{dt}<B~\text{for}~t\geq 0.

Now, we claim that

(3.6)

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}F(t)=0.

Otherwise, we can find
\epsilon>0
and a sequence
\{s_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfying
s_{n}+1<s_{n+1}
and
s_{n}\to+\infty
so that

F(s_{n})>\epsilon~\text{for}~n=1,2,\cdots.

Let
s=\min\{1,\frac{\epsilon}{2B}\}
, we use (
3.5
) to observe that

(3.7)

\frac{\varepsilon}{2}<F\left(s_{n}\right)-Bs<F(t)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{ for all }t\in\left[s_{n}-s,s_{n}\right],n=1,2,\cdots

By virtue of (
3.4
) and (
3.7
), we conclude that

\frac{dL}{dt}\leq\int_{V}-b_{1}b_{2}\left(u_{1}-e\right)^{2}d\mu=-b_{1}b_{2}F(t)\leq-b_{1}b_{2}\frac{\varepsilon}{2}

for all
t\in\left[s_{n}-s,s_{n}\right],n=1,2,\cdots
.
This implies that
L\left(s_{n}\right)-L\left(s_{n}-s\right)\leqslant-b_{1}b_{2}\frac{\varepsilon}{2}s
, which contradicts with (
3.2
). Hence, (
3.6
) holds.

Next, we assert that

(3.8)

u_{1}(x,t)-e\rightarrow 0\text{ as }t\rightarrow+\infty\text{ for all }x\in V\text{. }

Otherwise, there exists
\epsilon>0
,
x_{0}\in V
and a sequence
\{t_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
such that
|u_{1}\left(x_{0},t_{n}\right)-e|>\varepsilon,n=1,2,\cdots
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\int_{V}\left[u_{1}\left(x,t_{n}\right)-e\right]^{2}d\mu

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in V}\left[u_{1}\left(x,t_{n}\right)-e\right]^{2}\mu(x)

\displaystyle\geq\left[u_{1}\left(x_{0},t_{n}\right)-e\right]^{2}\mu\left(x_{0}\right)>\varepsilon^{2}\mu\left(x_{0}\right)>0

which contradicts with (
3.6
) as
t\to\infty
.

By a similar discussion, we see that

\int_{V}\left[u_{2}(x,t)-g\right]^{2}d\mu\rightarrow 0\text{ as }t\rightarrow+\infty\text{. }

Then, we apply similar arguments to conclude that

u_{2}(x,t)-g\rightarrow 0\text{ as }t\rightarrow+\infty\text{ for all }x\in V\text{. }

We now complete the proof.

Proof of Theorem
1.4

Define

From this and (

Define

\tilde{F}(t):=\int\limits_{\bar{\Omega}}[u_{3}(\cdot,t)-e]^{2}d\mu.

Due to

2

\frac{d\tilde{F}}{dt}=2\int\limits_{\bar{\Omega}}\left[-d_{1}\Gamma\left(u_{3},u_{3}\right)+\left(u_{3}-e\right)u_{3}\left(a_{1}-b_{1}u_{3}-c_{1}u_{4}\right)\right]d\mu.

In view of (

\frac{d\tilde{F}}{dt}<\tilde{B},~t\geq 0

for some constant
\tilde{B}>0
. By a similar discussion as the proof of (

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\tilde{F}(t)=0

\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}u_{3}(x,t)=e\;\mbox{and}\;\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}u_{4}(x,t)=g\;\mbox{ for all}\;x\in\bar{\Omega}.

We now complete the proof.

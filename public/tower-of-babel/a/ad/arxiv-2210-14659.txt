Abstract
. In this article, we investigate the maximal bilinear Riesz means S ∗ α S^{\alpha}_{*} associated to the sublaplacian on the Heisenberg group. We prove that the operator S ∗ α S^{\alpha}_{*} is bounded from L p 1 × L p 2 L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}} into L p L^{p} for 2 ≤ p 1 , p 2 ≤ ∞ 2\leq p_{1},p_{2}\leq\infty and 1 / p = 1 / p 1 + 1 / p 2 1/p=1/p_{1}+1/p_{2} when α \alpha is large than a suitable smoothness index α ⁡ ( p 1 , p 2 ) \alpha(p_{1},p_{2}) . For obtaining a lower index α ⁡ ( p 1 , p 2 ) \alpha(p_{1},p_{2}) , we define two important auxiliary operators and investigate their L p L^{p} estimates,which play a key role in our proof.

1.
Introduction

A classical problem in Fourier analysis is to make precise the sense in
which the Fourier inversion formula

f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widehat{f}(\xi)e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}d\xi

holds when
f
is a function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. A natural way of formulating this identity is in term of a
summability method. For instance, one may consider the convergence of the
Bochner-Riesz means defined by

B_{R}^{\delta}f(x)=\int_{\left|\xi\right|<R}\widehat{f}(\xi)\left(1-\frac{\left|\xi\right|^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\delta}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}d\xi

as
R\rightarrow\infty
for some suitable
\delta
. The almost everywhere
convergence of the Bochner-Riesz is related to the maximal operator
B_{\ast}^{\delta}=\sup_{R>0}\left|B_{R}^{\delta}\right|
. When
n=1
,
Hunt showed that if
\delta=0
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
1<p<\infty
,
B_{R}^{\delta}f
converges to
f
almost everywhere as
R\rightarrow\infty
. When
n\geq 2
and
p\geq 2
, Carbery,
Rubio de Francia and Vega
[
1
]
showed that for all
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
2\leq p\leq 2n/(n-1-2\delta)
,
B_{R}^{\delta}f
converges to

f
almost everywhere as
R\rightarrow\infty
by using the weighted
L^{2}
-estimates of the the maximal operator
B_{\ast}^{\delta}=\sup_{R>0}\left|B_{R}^{\delta}\right|
. When
n=2
and
1<p<2
,
Tao
[
10
]
proved that if
\delta>\max\left\{\frac{3}{4p}-\frac{3}{8},\frac{7}{6p}-\frac{2}{3}\right\}
,
B_{\ast}^{\delta}
is bounded from
L^{p}
into
L^{p,\infty}
, i.e.
B_{R}^{\delta}f
converges to
f
almost
everywhere as
R\rightarrow\infty.

The bilinear Bochner-Riesz means is defined by

B_{R}^{\alpha}(f,g)(x)=\int_{\left|\xi\right|<R}\widehat{f}(\xi)\widehat{g}(\eta)\left(1-\frac{\left|\xi\right|^{2}+\left|\eta\right|^{2}}{R^{2}}\right)^{\alpha}e^{2\pi ix\cdot(\xi+\eta)}d\xi d\eta\text{.}

Its almost everywhere convergence is depended on the
L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}
boundedness of the maximal oprator
B_{\ast}^{\alpha}=\sup_{R>0}\left|B_{R}^{\alpha}\right|
. Grafakos, He
and Honzík
[
5
]
showed that
B_{\ast}^{\alpha}
is bounded from

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if
\alpha>\frac{2n+3}{4}
. Jeong and Lee
[
4
]
gave a
comprehensive study on this problem when
n\geq 2
and
2\leq p_{1},p_{2}\leq\infty
,
1/p=1/p_{1}+1/p_{2}
. Inspired by their work, we shall investigated the
L^{p_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}
boundedness of the maximal bilinear Riesz means
on the Heisenberg group.

Strichartz
[
8
,
9
]
developed the harmonic analysis on the
Heisenberg group as the spectral theory of the sublaplacian. One may define
the Riesz means in terms of the spectral decomposition of the sublaplacian.
Let

\mathcal{L}f=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda P_{\lambda}f\,d\mu(\lambda)

be the spectral decomposition of the sublaplacian
\mathcal{L}
. The Riesz
means associated to the sublaplacian
\mathcal{L}
is defined by

S_{r}^{\delta}f=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-r\lambda\right)_{+}^{\delta}P_{\lambda}fd\mu(\lambda).

Gorges and Müller
[
2
]
investigate the almost everywhere
convergence of the Riesz means and obtained a similar result to that in
[
1
]
. They showed that
S_{r}^{\delta}f\rightarrow f
as
r\rightarrow 0
for
\delta>0
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
provided that
\frac{Q-1}{Q}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\delta}{D-1}\right)<\frac{1}{p}\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

The bilinear Riesz means associated to the sublaplacian
\mathcal{L}
on the
Heisenberg is defined by

S_{r}^{\alpha}(f,g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-r(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\right)_{+}^{\alpha}P_{\lambda_{1}}fP_{\lambda_{2}}g\,d\mu(\lambda_{1})d\mu(\lambda_{2}).

The corresponding maximal operator is denoted by
S_{\ast}^{\alpha}=\sup_{r>0}\left|S_{r}^{\alpha}\right|
. As same as the
Euclidean case, we hope to obtain the smooth indices
\alpha(p_{1},p_{2})

as low as possible so that
S_{\ast}^{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\times L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
into
L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})

when
\alpha>\alpha(p_{1},p_{2})
.

2.
Preliminaries

First we recall some basic facts about the Heisenberg group. These facts are
familiar and easy to find in many references. Let
\mathbb{H}^{n}
denote
the Heisenberg group whose underlying manifold is
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
and the group law is given by

(z,t)(w,s)=(z+w,t+s+\frac{1}{2}\text{Im}(z\cdot\overline{w})).

The Haar measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
coincides with the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
. A homogeneous structure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}

is given by the non-isotropic dilations
\delta_{r}(z,t)=(rz,r^{2}t)
. We
define a homogeneous norm on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
by

\left|x\right|=\Big(\frac{1}{16}\left|z\right|^{4}+t^{2}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}},\quad x=(z,t)\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.

This norm satisfies the triangle inequality and leads to a left-invariant
distance
d(x,y)=\left|x^{-1}y\right|
. The ball of radius
r

centered at
x
is

B(x,r)=\{y\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:\left|x^{-1}y\right|<r\}.

The Haar measure
dx
satisfies
d\delta_{r}(x)=r^{Q}dx
where
Q=2n+2
is
the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. If
f
and
g
are functions
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, their convolution is defined by

(f\ast g)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}f\left(xy^{-1}\right)g(y)\,dy,\quad x,y\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.

For each
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{\ast}
and
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, the inverse Fourier transform of
f
in variable
t
is defined by

f^{\lambda}(z)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{i\lambda t}f(z,t)\,dt.

An easy calculation shows that

(f\ast g)^{\lambda}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f^{\lambda}(z-\omega)g^{\lambda}(\omega)e^{\frac{i}{2}\lambda\mathrm{{Im}(z\cdot\overline{\omega})}}\,d\omega,\quad z,\omega\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Thus, we are led to the convolution of the form

f\ast_{\lambda}g=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(z-\omega)g(\omega)e^{\frac{i}{2}\lambda\mathrm{{Im}(z\cdot\overline{\omega})}}\,d\omega,

which are called the
\lambda
-twisted convolution.

The sublaplacian
\mathcal{L}
is defined by

\mathcal{L}=-\sum_{j=1}^{n}(X_{j}^{2}+Y_{j}^{2})

where

\displaystyle X_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{1}{2}y_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\quad j=1,2,\cdots,n,

\displaystyle Y_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}-\frac{1}{2}x_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial t},\quad j=1,2,\cdots,n,

are left invariant vector fields on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Up to a constant
multiple,
\mathcal{L}
is the unique left invariant, rotation invariant
differential operator that is homogeneous of degree two. Therefore, it is
regarded as the counterpart of the Laplacian on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The
sublaplacian
\mathcal{L}
is a positive and essentially self-adjoint
operator. In the following, we state the spectral decomposition of
\mathcal{L}
(cf.
[
11
]
).

Let
\varphi_{k}
be the Laguerre functions on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
given by

\varphi_{k}(z)=L_{k}^{n-1}\big(\frac{1}{2}\left|z\right|^{2}\big)e^{-\frac{1}{4}\left|z\right|^{2}},

where
L_{k}^{n-1}
are the Laguerre polynomials of type
n-1
defined on
\mathbb{R}
by

L_{k}^{n-1}(t)e^{-t}t^{n-1}=\frac{1}{k!}\left(\frac{d}{dt}\right)^{k}(e^{-t}t^{k+n-1}).

Define functions

e_{k}^{\lambda}(z,t)=e^{-i\lambda t}\varphi_{k}^{\lambda}(z)=e^{-i\lambda t}\varphi_{k}(\sqrt{\left|\lambda\right|}z),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{\ast}.

For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, we have the expansion

(2.1)

f(z,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f\ast e_{k}^{\lambda}(z,t)\,d\mu(\lambda)

where
d\mu(\lambda)=(2\pi)^{-n-1}\left|\lambda\right|^{n}d\lambda
is the Plancherel measure for
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Each
f\ast e_{k}^{\lambda}
is the eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}
with eigenvalue
(2k+n)\left|\lambda\right|
. We also have the Plancherel formula

(2.2)

\left\|f\right\|_{2}^{2}=(2\pi)^{-2n-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|f^{\lambda}\ast_{\lambda}\varphi_{k}^{\lambda}(z)\right|^{2}\lambda^{2n}\,dzd\lambda.

Defining

\widetilde{e}_{k}^{\lambda}(z,t)=e_{k}^{\frac{\lambda}{2k+n}}(z,t),

we can rewrite the decomposition (
2.1
) as

f(z,t)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(2k+n)^{-n-1}f\ast\widetilde{e}_{k}^{\lambda}(z,t)\,d\mu(\lambda).

Let

P_{\lambda}f(z,t)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(2k+n)^{-n-1}f\ast(\widetilde{e}_{k}^{\lambda}+\widetilde{e}_{k}^{-\lambda})(z,t).

Then (
2.1
) can be written as

f(z,t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}P_{\lambda}f(z,t)\,d\mu(\lambda).

It is clear that
P_{\lambda}f
is an eigenfunction of the
\mathcal{L}

with eigenvalue
\lambda
and we have the spectral decomposition

\mathcal{L}f=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda P_{\lambda}f\,d\mu(\lambda).

Define the bilinear Riesz means associated to the sublaplacian
\mathcal{L}

for
f,g\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
by

S_{r}^{\alpha}(f,g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-r\left(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\right)\right)_{+}^{\alpha}P_{\lambda_{1}}fP_{\lambda_{2}}g\,d\mu(\lambda_{1})d\mu(\lambda_{2}).

The corresponding maximal operator is defined by

S_{\ast}^{\alpha}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{r>0}\left|S_{r}^{\alpha}(f,g)(x)\right|.

3.
L^{p}
-estimate for auxiliary multiplier operator

In this Section, we shall define two auxiliary operators and investigate
their
L^{p}(l^{\infty})
estimates, which play a key role in the proof of
our main Theorem
1
.

Let
I=[-1,1]
and consider a class of smooth function

C^{N}(I)=\left\{\varphi:\text{supp}\varphi\subset I\text{, }\left\|\varphi\right\|_{C^{N}(\mathbb{R})}=\max_{0\leq n\leq N}\left\|\frac{d^{n}}{dt^{n}}\varphi\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 1\right\}.

For
\varphi\in C^{N}(I)
and
\rho,\delta,r>0
, we define the multiplier
operator

(3.1)

F_{\rho,\delta,r}^{\varphi}f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi\left(\frac{\rho-r\lambda}{\delta}\right)P_{\lambda}fd\mu(\lambda)\text{, \ }f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{H}^{n}),

and define its corresponding operator for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
by

(3.2)

D_{\delta,k}^{\varphi}f(x)=\left(\sum_{\rho\in\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,2]}\int_{1}^{2}\left|F_{\rho,\delta,2^{k}r}^{\varphi}f(x)\right|^{2}dr\right)^{1/2}\text{, }\text{ }f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{H}^{n}).

Write
F_{\rho,\delta}^{\varphi}=F_{\rho,\delta,1}^{\varphi}
for
simplicity. Since that
P_{\lambda}
is a convolution operator, then

F_{\rho,\delta,r}^{\varphi}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}f(x\omega^{-1})K_{\rho,\delta,r}^{\varphi}(\omega)d\omega

where the kernel
K_{\rho,\delta,r}^{\varphi}
is given by

K_{\rho,\delta,r}^{\varphi}(\omega)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(2k+n)^{-n-1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi\left(\frac{\rho-r\left|\lambda\right|}{\delta}\right)\widetilde{e}_{k}^{\lambda}(\omega)d\mu(\lambda).

Notice that for any
t>0
,

K_{\rho,\delta,\frac{r}{t}}^{\varphi}(\omega)=t^{\frac{Q}{2}}K_{\rho,\delta,r}^{\varphi}\left(\delta_{\sqrt{t}}\omega\right).

It is easy to verify that

(3.3)

F_{t\rho,t\delta,r}^{\varphi}f(x)=F_{\rho,\delta,\frac{r}{t}}^{\varphi}f(x)=F_{\rho,\delta,r}^{\varphi}f_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}}\left(\delta_{\sqrt{t}}x\right).

where
f_{s}=f(\delta_{s}\cdot)
for any
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
s\neq 0
. Especially, if
r=1
and
t=1/r
, we have

(3.4)

K_{\rho,\delta,r}^{\varphi}(\omega)=\left(\frac{1}{r}\right)^{\frac{Q}{2}}K_{\rho,\delta}^{\varphi}\left(\delta_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}}\omega\right)\text{ and }F_{\rho,\delta,r}^{\varphi}f(x)=F_{\rho,\delta}^{\varphi}f_{\sqrt{r}}\left(\delta_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}}x\right)\,.

Proposition 1
.

Let
2\leq p\leq\infty
and
0<\delta\leq 1/4
.
Suppose that
b>\frac{1}{2}(D-1)
where
D=2n+1
is the topological
dimension of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Then, we have that

(3.5)

\left\|\left(\int_{1/2}^{1}\left|F_{\rho,\delta}^{\varphi}f\right|^{2}d\rho\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leq C\delta^{-\left(b-\frac{1}{2}\right)}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}.

It follows that for any
\varepsilon>0
,

(3.6)

\left\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|D_{\delta,k}^{\varphi}f\right|\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}=\left\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{\rho\in\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,2]}\int_{1}^{2}\left|F_{\rho,\delta,2^{k}r}^{\varphi}f\right|^{2}dr\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leq C\delta^{-\left(b-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\varepsilon}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}.

Proof.

It is easy to prove (
3.5
). By Corollary 2.6 in
[
6
]
, we know that the kernel
K_{\rho,\delta}^{\varphi}
of
multiplier operator
F_{\rho,\delta}^{\varphi}
satisfies

\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left|K_{\rho,\delta}^{\varphi}(\omega)\right|d\omega\leq\left\|\varphi\left(\frac{\rho-\cdot}{\delta}\right)\right\|_{L_{b+\frac{1}{2}}^{2}}\leq C\delta^{-b}

for any
b>\frac{1}{2}(D-1)
, where the Sobolev norm is defined by

\left\|f\right\|_{L_{\alpha}^{2}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\left|x\right|^{\alpha}\widehat{f}(x)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

By Young’s inequality, we get that for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

\left\|F_{\rho,\delta}^{\varphi}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leq\left\|K_{\rho,\delta}^{\varphi}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leq C\delta^{-b}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}.

Then, using Minkowski’s inequality for
p\geq 2
, it follows that

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}\left|F_{\rho,\delta}^{\varphi}f\right|^{2}d\rho\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\frac{1}{2}}^{1}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left|F_{\rho,\delta}^{\varphi}f\right|^{2\cdot\frac{p}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}d\rho\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|F_{\rho,\delta}^{\varphi}f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leq C\delta^{-(b-\frac{1}{2})}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\text{.}

To obtain (
3.6
), we decompose interval
[0,2]
into dyadic
subintervals as follows:

[0,2]=[0,4\delta]\cup[4\delta,2]\text{,}\quad[4\delta,2]=\bigcup_{j=-1}^{j_{0}}I_{j}=\bigcup_{j=-1}^{j_{0}}[4\delta,2]\cap[2^{-j-1},2^{-j}]

where
j_{0}
is the smallest integer satisfying
2^{-j_{0}-1}\leq 4\delta
. Then, by the triangle inequality, we have that

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|D_{\delta,k}^{\varphi}f(x)\right|\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{\rho\in\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,2]}\int_{1}^{2}\left|F_{\rho,\delta,2^{k}r}^{\varphi}f(x)\right|^{2}dr\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\rho\in\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,4\delta]}\int_{1}^{2}\left|F_{\rho,\delta,2^{k}r}^{\varphi}f(x)\right|^{2}dr+\sum_{j=-1}^{j_{0}}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\rho\in\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap I_{j}}\int_{1}^{2}\left|F_{\rho,\delta,2^{k}r}^{\varphi}f(x)\right|^{2}dr\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{1}^{2}\sum_{\rho\in\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,4\delta]}\left|F_{\rho,\delta,2^{k}r}^{\varphi}f(x)\right|^{2}dr\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=-1}^{j_{0}}\left\|\left(\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{1}^{2}\sum_{\rho\in\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap I_{j}}\left|F_{\rho,\delta,2^{k}r}^{\varphi}f(x)\right|^{2}dr\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}.

Setting

I_{j}=\left\|\left(\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\rho\in\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap I_{j}}\int_{1}^{2}\left|F_{\rho,\delta,2^{k}r}^{\varphi}f(x)\right|^{2}dr\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})},

for
-1\leq j\leq j_{0}
and

II=\left\|\left(\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\rho\in\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,4\delta]}\int_{1}^{2}\left|F_{\rho,\delta,2^{k}r}^{\varphi}f(x)\right|^{2}dr\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})},

it follows that

(3.7)

\left\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|D_{\delta,k}^{\varphi}f(x)\right|\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leq\sum_{j=-1}^{j_{0}}I_{j}+II\text{. }

By the first equality relation in (
3.3
), we notice that for any
-1\leq j\leq j_{0}
,
j\neq 0
,

\left\|\left(\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\rho\in\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap I_{j}}\int_{1}^{2}\left|F_{\rho,\delta,2^{k}r}^{\varphi}f(x)\right|^{2}dr\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}=\left\|\left(\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{2^{j}\rho\in 2^{j}\rho\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap I_{0}}\int_{1}^{2}\left|F_{2^{j}\rho,2^{j}\delta,2^{k-j}r}^{\varphi}f(x)\right|^{2}dr\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})},

and
2^{-j}\geq 2^{-j_{0}}>4\delta
such that
2^{j}\delta<1/4
for any
0<\delta\leq 1/4
. These imply that once
I_{0}\leq\delta^{-(b-\frac{1}{2})}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}
, then
I_{j}\leq\left(2^{j}\delta\right)^{-(b-\frac{1}{2})}
and

(3.8)

\sum_{j=-1}^{j_{0}}I_{j}\leq\sum_{j=-1}^{j_{0}}\left(2^{j}\delta\right)^{-(b-\frac{1}{2})}\leq C\delta^{-\left(b-\frac{1}{2}\right)-\varepsilon}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

for any
\varepsilon>0
since
j_{0}=O(\log(1/\delta))
. Thus, to obtain (
3.6
), it suffices to show that

\max\{I_{0},II\}\leq\delta^{-(b-\frac{1}{2})}\text{.}

To estimate
I_{0}
, we consider the Littlewood-Paley projection operator
P_{m}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, defined by

P_{m}f=\int_{0}^{\infty}\beta(2^{-m}\lambda)P_{\lambda}fd\mu(\lambda),

where
\beta\in C_{0}^{\infty}[\frac{1}{2},2]
satisfying
0\leq\beta\leq 1
and
\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\beta(2^{-m}t)=1
for each
t>0
. Then, we have that

f=\int_{0}^{\infty}P_{\lambda}fd\mu(\lambda)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\beta(2^{-m}\lambda)P_{\lambda}fd\mu(\lambda)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{m}f.

Since supp
\varphi\subset[-1,1]
,
\rho\in[1/2,1]
,
r\in[1,2]
and
0<\delta\leq 1/4
, we see that

\varphi\left(\frac{\rho-2^{k}r\lambda}{\delta}\right)\beta(2^{-m}\lambda)\equiv 0\ \text{expect}\ -3\leq k+m\leq 2\text{. }

Using this, we can get that for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(3.9)

\displaystyle F_{\rho,\delta,2^{k}r}^{\varphi}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle F_{\rho,\delta,2^{k}r}^{\varphi}\left(\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}P_{m}f\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi\left(\frac{\rho-2^{k}r\lambda}{\delta}\right)\beta(2^{-m}\lambda)P_{\lambda}fd\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{Z},\\
-3\leq k+m\leq 2\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi\left(\frac{\rho-2^{k}r\lambda}{\delta}\right)\beta(2^{-m}\lambda)P_{\lambda}fd\mu(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\in\mathbb{Z},\\
-3\leq k+m\leq 2\end{subarray}}F_{\rho,\delta,2^{k}r}^{\varphi}\left(P_{m}f\right).

Since that
p\geq 2
, applying (
3.9
), (
3.4
) and Mikowski’s
inequality, it follows that

(3.10)

\displaystyle I_{0}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left(\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\rho\in\delta\mathbb{Z}\cap I_{0}}\int_{1}^{2}\left|F_{\rho,\delta,2^{k}r}^{\varphi}f(x)\right|^{2}dr\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=

4.
Boundedness of the maximal operator
S_{\ast}^{\alpha}

Theorem 1
.

Let
2\leq p_{1},p_{2}\leq\infty
and
1/p=1/p_{1}+1/p_{2}
. If
\alpha>

D(1-\frac{1}{p})+\frac{3}{p}
, then
S_{\ast}^{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\times L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
into
L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
.

Proof.

Fix
\alpha>0
. Let us choose
\psi\in C_{0}^{\infty}([\frac{1}{2},2])

and
\psi_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}([-\frac{3}{4},\frac{3}{4}])
such that

(1-t)_{+}^{\alpha}=\sum_{\delta\in D}\delta^{\alpha}\psi\left(\frac{1-t}{\delta}\right)+\psi_{0}(t)\text{, \ \ }0\leq t\leq 1,

where
D=\{2^{k}:k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
k\leq-2\}
. Using this, we can decompose

S_{r}^{\alpha}=\sum_{\delta\in D}^{\infty}\delta^{\alpha}S_{r}^{\delta}+S_{r}^{0}

where

S_{r}^{\delta}(f,g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi\left(\frac{1-r\lambda_{1}-r\lambda_{2}}{\delta}\right)P_{\lambda_{1}}fP_{\lambda_{2}}g\,d\mu(\lambda_{1})d\mu(\lambda_{2}),

and

S_{r}^{0}(f,g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi_{0}\left(r\lambda_{1}+r\lambda_{2}\right)P_{\lambda_{1}}fP_{\lambda_{2}}g\,d\mu(\lambda_{1})d\mu(\lambda_{2}).

It follows that

(4.1)

S_{\ast}^{\alpha}(f,g)(x)\leq\sum_{\delta\in D}\delta^{\alpha}\sup_{r>0}\left|S_{r}^{\delta}(f,g)(x)\right|+\sup_{r>0}\left|S_{r}^{0}(f,g)(x)\right|.

Since that
\psi_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}([-\frac{3}{4},\frac{3}{4}])
, using
Holder’s inequality, it is easy to see that for any
2\leq p_{1},p_{2}\leq\infty
and
1/p=1/p_{1}+1/p_{2}
,

\left\|S_{\ast}^{0}(f,g)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}=\left\|\sup_{r>0}\left|S_{r}^{0}(f,g)\right|\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leq\left\|fg\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}.

Therefore, to obtain Theorem
1
, we have to focus on obtaining
estimates for the maximal operator

S_{\ast}^{\delta}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{r>0}\left|S_{r}^{\delta}(f,g)(x)\right|\quad\text{for}\quad 0<\delta\leq\frac{1}{4}.

By the fundamental theorem of calculus, we see that
\left|F(t)\right|\leq\left|F(s)\right|+\int_{1}^{2}\left|F^{\prime}(\tau)\right|d\tau
for any
t,s\in[1,2]
. This implies
that

S_{\ast}^{\delta}(f,g)(x)=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\sup_{1\leq r\leq 2}\left|S_{2^{k}r}^{\delta}(f,g)(x)\right|\leq\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{1}^{2}\left|S_{2^{k}r}^{\delta}(f,g)(x)\right|dr+\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{1}^{2}\left|\frac{\partial}{\partial r}S_{2^{k}r}^{\delta}(f,g)(x)\right|dr.

Since that

\frac{\partial}{\partial r}S_{2^{k}r}^{\delta}(f,g)=\frac{-2^{k}}{\delta}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})\psi^{\prime}\left(\frac{1-2^{k}r(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})}{\delta}\right)P_{\lambda_{1}}fP_{\lambda_{2}}g\,d\mu(\lambda_{1})d\mu(\lambda_{2}),

we can conclude that
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}S_{2^{k}r}^{\delta}(f,g)

satisfies the same quantitative properties as
\frac{1}{\delta}S_{2^{k}r}^{\delta}(f,g)
when
1\leq r\leq 2
. Hence, to estimate
S_{\ast}^{\delta}(f,g)
, it suffices to consider the operator

(f,g)\rightarrow\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{1}^{2}\left|S_{2^{k}r}^{\delta}(f,g)(x)\right|dr.

To eatimate this operator, we choose
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(I)

satisfying
\sum_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(t+l)=1
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Fix
0<\delta\leq 1/4
and set
\widetilde{\delta}=\delta^{1+\kappa}

with some
\kappa>0
. Then, for any
r>0
, we can write

\psi\left(\frac{1-r\lambda_{1}-r\lambda_{2}}{\delta}\right)=\sum_{\sigma\in\widetilde{\delta}\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\rho\in\widetilde{\delta}\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,2]}\varphi\left(\frac{\rho-r\lambda_{1}}{\widetilde{\delta}}\right)\varphi\left(\frac{\sigma-\rho-r\lambda_{2}}{\widetilde{\delta}}\right)\psi\left(\frac{1-r\lambda_{1}-r\lambda_{2}}{\delta}\right).

Since supp
\varphi\subset[-1,1]
and supp
\psi\subset[1/2,2]

\varphi\left(\frac{\rho-r\lambda_{1}}{\widetilde{\delta}}\right)\psi\left(\frac{1-r\lambda_{1}-r\lambda_{2}}{\delta}\right)=0\quad\text{except}\quad 1-3\delta\leq r\lambda_{2}+\rho\leq 1+\delta.

It follows that

\varphi\left(\frac{\rho-r\lambda_{1}}{\widetilde{\delta}}\right)\varphi\left(\frac{\sigma-\rho-r\lambda_{2}}{\widetilde{\delta}}\right)\psi\left(\frac{1-r\lambda_{1}-r\lambda_{2}}{\delta}\right)=0\text{ except }\sigma\in[1-4\delta,1+2\delta]).

Thus,

(4.2)

\displaystyle S_{r}^{\delta}(f,g)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma\in\widetilde{\delta}\mathbb{Z}\cap[1-4\delta,1+2\delta]}\sum_{\rho\in\widetilde{\delta}\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,2]}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi\left(\frac{\rho-r\lambda_{1}}{\widetilde{\delta}}\right)\varphi\left(\frac{\sigma-\rho-r\lambda_{2}}{\widetilde{\delta}}\right)

\displaystyle\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ }\times\psi\left(\frac{1-r\lambda_{1}-r\lambda_{2}}{\delta}\right)P_{\lambda_{1}}fP_{\lambda_{2}}g\,d\mu(\lambda_{1})d\mu(\lambda_{2}).

At the same time, by the Fourier inversion formula, we have

(4.3)

Applying Taylor’s theorem for

\rho\in\widetilde{\delta}\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,2]

(4.4)

\displaystyle e^{2\pi i\tau\left(\frac{\sigma-r\lambda_{1}-r\lambda_{2}}{\delta}\right)}

) and let

. Notice that

for any

. Then, we can obtain that

\displaystyle S_{r}^{\delta}(f,g)

\displaystyle=

,

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{\rho\in\widetilde{\delta}\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,2]}\int_{1}^{2}\left|F_{\rho,\widetilde{\delta},2^{k}r}^{\varphi_{a}}f\right|^{2}dr\right)^{1/2}=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|D_{\widetilde{\delta},k}^{\varphi_{a}}f\right|,\text{ }

and

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{\rho\in\widetilde{\delta}\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,2]}\int_{1}^{2}\left|F_{\sigma-\rho,\widetilde{\delta},2^{k}}^{\varphi_{b}}g\right|^{2}dr\right)^{1/2}\leq\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left(\sum_{\sigma-\rho\in\widetilde{\delta}\mathbb{Z}\cap[0,2]}\int_{1}^{2}\left|F_{\sigma-\rho,\widetilde{\delta},2^{k}}^{\varphi_{b}}g\right|^{2}dr\right)^{1/2}=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|D_{\widetilde{\delta},k}^{\varphi_{b}}g\right|.

Using (
4.5
) and Corollary
1
, we see that

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{1}^{2}\left|S_{2^{k}r}^{\delta}(f,g)\right|dr\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{0\leq a+b\leq N}c_{a,b}\delta^{\kappa(a+b)}\sum_{\sigma\in\widetilde{\delta}\mathbb{Z}k1-4\delta,1+2\delta]}\left|\psi^{(a+b)}\left(\frac{1-\sigma}{\delta}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\times\left\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|D_{\widetilde{\delta},k}^{\varphi_{a}}f\right|\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\left\|\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\left|D_{\widetilde{\delta},k}^{\varphi_{b}}g\right|\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\delta^{-\left[(b-\frac{1}{2})(1-\frac{2}{p_{1}})-\frac{1}{p_{1}}\right]-\left[(b-\frac{1}{2})(1-\frac{2}{p_{2}})-\frac{1}{p_{2}}\right]-\varepsilon}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{0\leq a+b\leq N}c_{a,b}\delta^{\kappa(a+b)}\sum_{\sigma\in\widetilde{\delta}\mathbb{Z}\cap[1-4\delta,1+2\delta]}\left|\psi^{(a+b)}\left(\frac{1-\sigma}{\delta}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\delta^{-(2b-1)(1-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{1}{p}-\varepsilon}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\delta^{-1-\kappa}\sum_{N=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{0\leq a+b\leq N}c_{a,b}\delta^{(\kappa-1)(a+b)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\delta^{-(2b-1)(1-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{1}{p}-2-\varepsilon}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

holds for

with any
\varepsilon>0
and
b>\frac{1}{2}(D-1)
with

is the topological dimension of

. It
follows that

\displaystyle\left\|S_{\ast}^{\delta}(f,g)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\delta^{-(2b-1)(1-\frac{1}{p})-\frac{1}{p}-2-\varepsilon}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}.

Therefore, whenever

, we can choose

\displaystyle\leq

Global minimizers of the two-phase Bernoulli problem with the p p -Laplace operator Thanks: M. Bayrami and M. Fotouhi was supported by Iran National Science Foundation (INSF) under project No. 4001885.
Masoud Bayrami, Morteza Fotouhi

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the classification of Lipschitz global solutions for a two-phase p p -Laplace Bernoulli problem, subject to a mild assumption. Specifically, we focus on the scenario where the interior two-phase points of the global solution are non-empty. Our results show that the expected C 1 , η C^{1,\eta} regularity holds in a suitable neighborhood of certain two-phase points referred to as regular two-phase points.

1.
Introduction and main result

We study the regularity of the free boundary, obtaining from the minimization of the following two-phase functional

(1)

J_{\mathrm{TP}}(v,D):=\int_{D}|\nabla v|^{p}+(p-1)\lambda_{+}^{p}\chi_{\{v>0\}}+(p-1)\lambda_{-}^{p}\chi_{\{v<0\}}\,dx,\qquad v\in{\mathcal{K}}.

Here,
D
is a bounded and smooth domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\chi_{A}
denotes the characteristic function of the set
A
, the positive constants
\lambda_{+},\lambda_{-}>0
are given, and
1<p<\infty
.
The class of admissible functions
{\mathcal{K}}
consists of all functions
v\in W^{1,p}(D)
with boundary data
v=g
on
\partial D
for a prescribed boundary datum
g\in W^{1,p}(D)
.

Before proceeding, we need to review some common terminologies and definitions:

•

A function
u:D\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be a
minimizer
of
J_{\mathrm{TP}}
in
D
if and only if

J_{\mathrm{TP}}(u,D)\leq J_{\mathrm{TP}}(v,D),

for all
v\in{\mathcal{K}}
.

•

The two sets of points
\Omega^{+}_{u}=\{x\in D\,:\,u(x)>0\}
and
\Omega^{-}_{u}=\{x\in D\,:\,u(x)<0\}
are respectively referred to as the positivity sets of the functions
u^{+}:=\max\{u,0\}
and
u^{-}:=\max\{-u,0\}
.

•

F(u):=\left(\partial\Omega^{+}_{u}\cup\partial\Omega^{-}_{u}\right)\cap D
represents the free boundary of the minimizer
u
.

•

The set
\Gamma_{\mathrm{TP}}(u):=\partial\Omega^{+}_{u}\cap\partial\Omega^{-}_{u}\cap D
is the set of two-phase points of the free boundary
F(u)
, and for the simplicity, we denote it by
\Gamma_{\mathrm{TP}}
.

•

The boundary of positive and negative phases, i.e.
\partial\Omega^{\pm}_{u}\cap D
can be decomposed as

\partial\Omega^{\pm}_{u}\cap D=\Gamma^{\pm}_{\mathrm{OP}}\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{TP}},

where
\Gamma^{+}_{\mathrm{OP}}:=\left(\partial\Omega^{+}_{u}\setminus\partial\Omega^{-}_{u}\right)\cap D
and
\Gamma^{-}_{\mathrm{OP}}:=\left(\partial\Omega^{-}_{u}\setminus\partial\Omega^{+}_{u}\right)\cap D
are the one-phase parts of
F(u)
.

•

We say that
x_{0}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{TP}}
is an
interior
two-phase point and denote it by
x_{0}\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{TP}}
, if

|B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\{u=0\}|=0,\qquad\text{for some}\quad r>0.

•

We say that
x_{0}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{TP}}
is a
branch
point, denoted by
x_{0}\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{br}}_{\mathrm{TP}}
, if

|B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\{u=0\}|>0,\qquad\text{for every}\quad r>0.

Any minimizer
u
satisfies, in a certain weak sense, the following problem

(2)

\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}u:=\mathrm{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)=0,\quad&\text{in}\quad\,\,\Omega_{u}^{+}\cup\Omega_{u}^{-},\\
|\nabla u^{+}|^{p}-|\nabla u^{-}|^{p}=\lambda_{+}^{p}-\lambda_{-}^{p},\quad|\nabla u^{+}|\geq\lambda_{+},\quad|\nabla u^{-}|\geq\lambda_{-},\quad&\text{on}\quad\Gamma_{\mathrm{TP}},\\
|\nabla u^{+}|=\lambda_{+},\quad&\text{on}\quad\Gamma^{+}_{\mathrm{OP}},\\
|\nabla u^{-}|=\lambda_{-},\quad&\text{on}\quad\Gamma^{-}_{\mathrm{OP}},\end{cases}

where
\Delta_{p}u=\mathrm{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
is the
p
-Laplace operator; see
[
8
, Lemma 3.1]
.
These types of problems are known as Bernoulli-type free boundary problems, which appear in various models of fluid mechanics or heat conduction; see e.g.
[
2
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
26
]
.

For the admissible functions in
{\mathcal{K}}^{+}:=\{v\in{\mathcal{K}}\,:\,v\geq 0\}
, the analogous one-phase functional and the corresponding overdetermined problem called the one-phase Bernoulli problem, was first studied in
[
1
]
for the case
p=2
. Also, the case of uniformly elliptic quasilinear equations has been treated in
[
3
]
.
For the earlier known results of the one-phase Bernoulli’s problem for the
p
-Laplace operator, see
[
15
,
16
,
25
,
30
]
.
See also
[
23
]
for the
p(x)
-Laplacian Bernoulli problem with the right hand side.
It is worth noting that, generally, in the one-phase Bernoulli problem, the
(n-1)
-Hausdorff dimension of the singular points of the free boundary is zero and that the free boundary is analytic in a suitable neighborhood of the regular points,
[
15
]
.

The two-phase problem, in the case
p=2
, was first studied in
[
6
]
, followed by a series of works by Luis Caffarelli,
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
; see also
[
14
]
.
A comprehensive study and the review of the earlier known results for
p=2
can be found in
[
17
,
31
]
.

The challenge with problem (
2
) is that the governing operator,
\Delta_{p}u
, is not uniformly elliptic.
However, the regularity of the free boundary implies non-degeneracy of
|\nabla u|
near the free boundary, which makes the
p
-Laplacian uniformly elliptic.
Without regularity, it is difficult to prove non-degeneracy up to the free boundary.

For the minimizers of the following functional instead of (
1
)

J(u,D):=\int_{D}|\nabla u|^{p}+\lambda_{+}^{p}\chi_{\{u>0\}}+\lambda_{-}^{p}\chi_{\{u\leq 0\}}\,dx=\int_{D}|\nabla u|^{p}+\left(\lambda_{+}^{p}-\lambda_{-}^{p}\right)\chi_{\{u>0\}}\,dx+\lambda_{-}^{p}\left|D\right|,

or with neglecting the constant term
\lambda_{-}^{p}\left|D\right|
, and denoting
\lambda_{+}^{p}-\lambda_{-}^{p}
with
\Lambda
, i.e. considering the functional

(3)

u\mapsto\int_{D}|\nabla u|^{p}+\Lambda\chi_{\{u>0\}}\,dx,

the best known results comes from the stratification argument in
[
21
]
.
The important fact about the minimizers of the functional (
3
) is that their the free boundary does not contain any branch points.
In
[
28
,
29
]
, the authors show that the Lipschitz free boundaries of the minimizers of (
3
) are
C^{1,\eta}
smooth for some
\eta\in(0,1)
.
Moreover, in
[
21
]
, it is also showed that the
(n-1)
-Hausdorff dimension of the singular points of the free boundary is zero.

For the free boundary of the minimizers of the functional (
1
), which appearance of the branch point is expected, there is a complete regularity result in the case
p=2
; see
[
17
]
.
In this work, the authors proved that a suitable neighbourhood of the two-phase points (i.e. both interior and branch points) is
C^{1,\eta}
regular.
The generalization of this result, for any
p\in(1,\infty)
, is highly nontrivial due to the nonlinear and degenerate nature of the
p
-Laplace operator, as well as the lack of the monotonicity formula, for
p\neq 2
.

In
[
8
]
, we extend the main result of
[
21
]
to functional (
1
), which covers the analysis of the behavior of the minimizers near the branch points as well.
In fact, we prove the local Lipschitz regularity of the minimizers using a dichotomy argument.
More precisely, we first establish
C^{1,\eta}
regularity of the flat part of the free boundary.
Then, we show that either the free boundary is flat or we have linear growth at the non-flat points of the free boundary.
In order to prove that flatness implies
C^{1,\eta}
regularity, the linearization technique of De Silva,
[
18
]
, is used.
It is vital to emphasize that in
[
8
]
, we use a linear improvement of flatness to obtain the compactness of the linearizing sequences.
However, in this paper we require a somewhat stronger, a quadratic improvement of flatness.

1.1.
Main results

Our goal in this paper is to continue our previous research,
[
8
]
, by classifying the Lipschitz global minimizers of (
1
) (which they are also Lipschitz solutions of (
2
) in
D=\mathbb{R}^{n}
). Our first main result is presented in the following classification theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Classification of Lipschitz global two-phase solutions)
.

Let
u
be a Lipschitz global viscosity solution to (
2
), i.e. with
D=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Assume that

\Gamma_{\mathrm{TP}}^{\mathrm{int}}(u)\neq\emptyset
(without loss of generality, assume that
0\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{TP}}(u)
), then
u
is a two-plane solution

u(x)=\alpha\left(x\cdot\nu\right)^{+}-\beta\left(x\cdot\nu\right)^{-},

for some
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, with

\alpha\geq\lambda_{+},\qquad\beta\geq\lambda_{-},\qquad\alpha^{p}-\beta^{p}=\lambda_{+}^{p}-\lambda_{-}^{p}.

In order to prove Theorem
1.1
, we will closely follow the work of De Silva and Savin,
[
20
]
.
As a result of this classification of Lipschitz global solutions to (
2
), the regularity of the free boundary
F(u)=\left(\partial\Omega^{+}_{u}\cup\partial\Omega^{-}_{u}\right)\cap D
, for any minimizers of
J_{\mathrm{TP}}
, around the
regular
two-phase points, will be deduced.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
x_{0}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{TP}}
is a regular two-phase point if

\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{|B_{r}(x_{0})\cap\{u=0\}|}{|B_{r}(x_{0})|}=0.

More precisely, we prove that, in a suitable neighborhood of the regular two-phase points, the sets
\Omega^{+}_{u}
and
\Omega^{-}_{u}
are two
C^{1,\eta}
regular domains touching along the closed set of two-phase points
\Gamma_{\mathrm{TP}}
.

Theorem 1.2

(Regularity of the regular part of the free boundary)
.

Let
u:D\to\mathbb{R}
be a minimizer of
J_{\mathrm{TP}}
in
D
. Then, for every regular two-phase point
x_{0}
, there exists a radius
r_{0}>0
(depending on
x_{0}
) such that
\partial\Omega^{\pm}_{u}\cap B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})
are
C^{1,\eta}
graphs, for any
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{3})
.

Finally, notice that once we have
C^{1,\eta}
regularity on two-phase points, it follows that
\Delta_{p}u=\mathrm{div}\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)
is uniformly elliptic locally near the two-phase points (recall the boundary condition in (
2
)) and we obtain the analyticity by
[
27
]
.

2.
Basic properties of minimizers

In this section, we present some fundamental properties of minimizers of
J_{\mathrm{TP}}
.
We start with the following existence result.

Theorem 2.1

(Existence)
.

If the admissible set
{\mathcal{K}}
is nonempty, then there exists a minimizer
u
of
J_{\mathrm{TP}}
over
{\mathcal{K}}
.
Moreover, every minimizer satisfies

\begin{cases}\Delta_{p}u=0,&\quad\mathrm{in}\quad\Omega^{+}_{u}\cup\Omega^{-}_{u},\\
\Delta_{p}u^{\pm}\geq 0,&\quad\mathrm{in}\quad D,\\
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}\leq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(D)}.\end{cases}

Proof.

See
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
.
∎

In the following proposition, we present a non-degeneracy property for the minimizers.
This property reveals the fact that each of the two phases
\Omega_{u}^{+}
and
\Omega_{u}^{-}
are optimal with respect to one-sided inwards perturbations; see
[
9
,
31
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(Non-degeneracy)
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set, and
u
be a minimizer of
J_{\mathrm{TP}}
. Then,

u
is non-degenerate; i.e. there exists a constant
C=C(n,\lambda_{\pm},p)>0
such that

\mathop{\,\hbox to0.0pt{$\diagup$\hss}\!\!\int}\nolimits_{B_{r}\left(x_{0}\right)}\left(u^{\pm}\right)^{p}\,dx\geq Cr^{p},\qquad\text{ for every }x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega_{u}^{\pm}}\cap D\text{ and every }0<r<\operatorname{dist}\left(x_{0},\partial D\right).

Proof.

See
[
8
, Proposition 2.2]
.
∎

In
[
8
]
, we have shown that the flat free boundary of the two-phase Bernoulli problem with the
p
-Laplace operator has
C^{1,\eta}
regularity, and using this, we have established the Lipschitz regularity of the minimizers by a dichotomy argument.

Theorem 2.3

(Lipschitz regularity)
.

Let
u:D\to\mathbb{R}
be a minimizer of
J_{\mathrm{TP}}
in
D
. Then
u
is locally Lipschitz continuous;
u\in C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{0,1}(D)
.

Proof.

See
[
8
, Theorem 1.2]
.
∎

By Theorem
2.3
in hand, we would review the concept of blow-up, which is significant prior to studying the regularity of the free boundary.
Let
u
be a minimizer of
J_{\mathrm{TP}}
in the open set
D
. For every
x_{0}\in F(u)
, and every
0<r<\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},\partial D)
, we define the function

u_{x_{0},r}(x):=\frac{u(x_{0}+rx)}{r},

which is well-defined for
|x|<\frac{1}{r}\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},\partial D)
, and vanishes at the origin.
When
x_{0}=0
, we denote
u_{0,r}
by
u_{r}
for the sake of convenience.
Note that, for every
R>0
, and
r\ll 1
, the functions
u_{x_{0},r}
are defined on the ball
B_{R}
and are uniformly Lipschitz in
B_{R}
because of the local Lipschitz continuity of minimizer
u
; Theorem
2.3
.
Given a sequence
r_{k}>0
such that
r_{k}\to 0
, we say that the sequence of functions
u_{x_{0},r_{k}}
is a
blow-up sequence
of
u
at
x_{0}
.
Hence, there is a Lipschitz continuous function
v:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
and a subsequence of
u_{x_{0},r_{k}}
converging to
v
uniformly on every ball
B_{R}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We say that
v
is a blow-up limit of
u
at
x_{0}
.
The blow-up limit
v
is a minimizer in any ball
B_{R}
(i.e. a global minimizer) and satisfies the following properties (see for instance
[
1
,
3
]
)

\displaystyle u_{x_{0},r_{k}}\to v

\displaystyle\text{in}\,C^{0,\alpha}_{\rm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})\,\text{for every}\,0<\alpha<1,

\displaystyle\nabla u_{x_{0},r_{k}}\to\nabla v

\displaystyle\text{a.e. in\,}{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

\displaystyle\partial\Omega_{u_{x_{0},r_{k}}}^{\pm}\to\partial\Omega_{v}^{\pm}

\displaystyle\text{locally in the Hausdorff distance,}

\displaystyle\chi_{\Omega_{u_{x_{0},r_{k}}}^{\pm}}\to\chi_{\Omega_{v}^{\pm}}

\displaystyle\text{in\,}L^{1}_{\rm{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

We remember that
u
is a weak solution (in the sense of distributions) to the
p
-Laplace equation
\Delta_{p}u=f
, in
\Omega
, if
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
, and for every
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, there holds that

-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f\varphi\,dx.

Also, by viscosity solution, we will refer to the usual
C
-viscosity definition of solutions of elliptic PDEs, as presented in e.g. the book
[
13
]
.
We emphasize on the equivalence of viscosity and weak solutions for the solutions to
\Delta_{p}u=f
,
[
22
, Theorem 2.2 and Remark 2.3]
.

Now, we define the optimality conditions (
2
), in a weak (viscosity) sense on the free boundary.

Definition 2.1
.

We say that a function
Q:D\to\mathbb{R}
, touches a function
w:D\to\mathbb{R}
from below (resp. from above) at a point
x_{0}\in D
, if
Q(x_{0})=w(x_{0})
, and

Q(x)-w(x)\leq 0\qquad(resp.\quad Q(x)-w(x)\geq 0),

for every
x
in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
. We will say that
Q
touches
w
strictly from below (resp. above) if the above inequalities are strict for
x\neq x_{0}
.

A function
Q
is an (admissible) comparison function in
D
, if

(a)

Q\in C^{1}(\overline{\{Q>0\}}\cap D)\cap C^{1}(\overline{\{Q<0\}}\cap D)
;

(b)

Q\in C^{2}(\{Q>0\}\cap D)\cap C^{2}(\{Q<0\}\cap D)
;

(c)

\partial\{Q>0\}
and
\partial\{Q<0\}
are smooth manifolds in
D
.

We should remark that if
\nabla Q\neq 0
on
\partial\{Q>0\}\cup\partial\{Q<0\}
, then condition
(c)
above holds.

Definition 2.2
.

The optimality conditions on the free boundary
F(u)
is as follows:

(A)

Suppose that
Q
is a comparison function that touches
u
from
below
at
x_{0}
.

(A.1)

If
x_{0}\in\Gamma^{+}_{\mathrm{OP}}
, then
|\nabla Q^{+}(x_{0})|\leq\lambda_{+}
;

(A.2)

if
x_{0}\in\Gamma^{-}_{\mathrm{OP}}
, then
Q^{+}\equiv 0
in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
, and
|\nabla Q^{-}(x_{0})|\geq\lambda_{-}
;

(A.3)

if
x_{0}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{TP}}
, then
|\nabla Q^{-}(x_{0})|\geq\lambda_{-}
, and

|\nabla Q^{+}(x_{0})|^{p}-|\nabla Q^{-}(x_{0})|^{p}\leq\lambda_{+}^{p}-\lambda_{-}^{p}.

(B)

Suppose that
Q
is a comparison function that touches
u
from
above
at
x_{0}
.

(B.1)

If
x_{0}\in\Gamma^{+}_{\mathrm{OP}}
, then
Q^{-}\equiv 0
in a neighborhood of
x_{0}
, and
|\nabla Q^{+}(x_{0})|\geq\lambda_{+}
;

(B.2)

if
x_{0}\in\Gamma^{-}_{\mathrm{OP}}
, then
|\nabla Q^{-}(x_{0})|\leq\lambda_{-}
;

(B.3)

if
x_{0}\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{TP}}
, then
|\nabla Q^{+}(x_{0})|\geq\lambda_{+}
, and

|\nabla Q^{+}(x_{0})|^{p}-|\nabla Q^{-}(x_{0})|^{p}\geq\lambda_{+}^{p}-\lambda_{-}^{p}.

Definition 2.3
.

If
u:D\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function such that the claim
(A)
in Definition
2.2
holds for every comparison function
Q
, then we say that
u
satisfies (
2
) on the free boundary in the viscosity supersolution sense.

Similarly, we say that
u
satisfies (
2
) on the free boundary in the viscosity subsolution sense if the claim
(B)
holds for every comparison function
Q
.

Also, if both
(A)
and
(B)
hold, then we say that
u
satisfies (
2
) on the free boundary in the viscosity sense.

Remark 2.4
.

Notice that Definition
2.3
together with the usual definition of viscosity supersolution to
\Delta_{p}u=0
means that
u
is a supersolution to (
2
).
Similarly, Definition
2.3
together with the definition of viscosity subsolution to
\Delta_{p}u=0
means that
u
is a subsolution to (
2
).
Also,
u
is a solution to (
2
) if it is both a subsolution and a supersolution.

.

3.
Quadratic improvement of flatness

In this section, we will provide a quadratic improvement of flatness.
However, before we proceed, we need to introduce some definitions and notations.

First, we define a two-plane solution to the equation (
2
) as

U(x)=U_{a^{+},\nu}(x):=a^{+}(x\cdot\nu)^{+}-a^{-}(x\cdot\nu)^{-},

for some
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and
a^{+},a^{-}
that satisfy

\left(a^{+}\right)^{p}-\left(a^{-}\right)^{p}=\lambda_{+}^{p}-\lambda_{-}^{p},\qquad a^{+}\geq\lambda_{+},\quad a^{-}\geq\lambda_{-}.

We denote by
\mathcal{U}_{\lambda_{+},\lambda_{-}}
the set of all two-plane solutions.

Next, we introduce the following approximate quadratic polynomials

P_{M,\nu}(x):=x\cdot\nu+\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Mx,

for some
\nu\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and a symmetric matrix
M=(M_{ij})\in\mathcal{S}^{n\times n}
. In addition, they satisfy the conditions

(4)

M\nu=0,\qquad\mathrm{tr}(M)=0.

We also denote by
V_{a^{+},a^{-},M,\nu}
, or for simplicity by
V_{a^{+},M,\nu}
, the two-phase quadratic polynomial of positive slopes
a^{+},a^{-}

(5)

3.1.

We need the following Harnack-type inequality for

Lemma 3.4

For every

,
\lambda_{\pm}>0
, and

\overline{\epsilon},\delta,c>0

Assume that

\|M\|\leq\delta\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad 0<\epsilon\leq\overline{\epsilon}.

Then, one can find new constants

Proof.

Suppose that
\lambda_{+}\geq\lambda_{-}
, which this is not a restrictive assumption. This assumption infer also that
a^{+}\geq a^{-}
.

Let
\overline{x}=\frac{1}{5}\textbf{e}_{n}
and
\sigma=\frac{(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})a^{+}}{2}

u(\overline{x})\geq V(\overline{x}+\sigma_{1}{\textbf{e}}_{n})+\sigma\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u(x)\geq V(x+\overline{\sigma}_{1}{\textbf{e}}_{n}),\qquad\text{in}\quad\overline{B_{\frac{1}{2}}},

for
\overline{\sigma}_{1}=\sigma_{1}+c\epsilon
, where

is a universal constant.
Similarly,

u(\overline{x})\leq V(\overline{x}+\sigma_{2}{\textbf{e}}_{n})-\sigma\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u(x)\leq V(x+\overline{\sigma}_{2}{\textbf{e}}_{n}),\qquad\text{in}\quad\overline{B_{\frac{1}{2}}},

for
\overline{\sigma}_{2}=\sigma_{2}-c\epsilon

We prove only the first implication. The second one can be obtained by the same arguments.

Step 1:

We first prove that

u(x)\geq V(x+\sigma_{1}{\textbf{e}}_{n})+c_{0}\epsilon,\qquad\text{for all}\quad x\in B_{2r_{0}}(x_{0}),

for some universal constants

, and
r_{0}
such that
B_{4r_{0}}(x_{0})\subset\{x_{n}>0\}
.
To do this, we distinguish two cases:

Case (i)
. Suppose
|\nabla u(\overline{x})|<\frac{1}{4}a^{+}
for
\overline{x}=\frac{1}{5}\textbf{e}_{n}
.
Then, there exists
r_{1}=r_{1}(n,p,L_{0})>0
such that
|\nabla u(x)|<\frac{1}{2}a^{+}
in
B_{4r_{1}}(\overline{x})
;
note that
u
is universally bounded and
p
-harmonic in
B_{\frac{1}{10}}(\overline{x})
.
Now, by invoking Lemma
A.1
in
B_{4r_{1}}(\overline{x})
for
h:=u-V
, one can guarantee that

u(x)-V(x+\sigma_{1}{\textbf{e}}_{n})\geq C^{-1}\left(u(\overline{x})-V(\overline{x}+\sigma_{1}{\textbf{e}}_{n})\right)-a^{+}r_{1},\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{r_{1}}(\overline{x}),

for an appropriate universal constant
C=C(n,p)>0
.
By the assumption (
10
), we obtain for all

\displaystyle C^{-1}\sigma-a^{+}r_{1}

\displaystyle\leq u(x)-V(x+\sigma_{1}{\textbf{e}}_{n})

\displaystyle\leq u(x-2r_{1}\textbf{e}_{n})-V(x+(\sigma_{1}-2r_{1})\textbf{e}_{n})

\displaystyle+\left(V(x+(\sigma_{1}-2r_{1})\textbf{e}_{n})-V(x+\sigma_{1}{\textbf{e}}_{n})\right)+2r_{1}\Arrowvert{\nabla u}\Arrowvert_{L^{\infty}(B_{4r_{1}}(\overline{x}))}

\displaystyle\leq u(x-2r_{1}\textbf{e}_{n})-V(x+(\sigma_{1}-2r_{1})\textbf{e}_{n})-2a^{+}r_{1}+2r_{1}\Arrowvert{\nabla u}\Arrowvert_{L^{\infty}(B_{4r_{1}}(\overline{x}))}

\displaystyle\leq u(x-2r_{1}\textbf{e}_{n})-V(x+(\sigma_{1}-2r_{1})\textbf{e}_{n})-a^{+}r_{1}.

Thus, with
x^{*}=\overline{x}-2r_{1}\textbf{e}_{n}
, we get

(12)

C^{-1}\sigma\leq u(x)-V(x+\sigma_{1}{\textbf{e}}_{n}),\qquad\text{for all}\quad x\in B_{r_{1}}(x^{*}).

Case (ii)
. Suppose
|\nabla u(\overline{x})|>\frac{1}{4}a^{+}
.
By the interior gradient estimate, we know that
|\nabla u|
is bounded in
B_{\frac{1}{10}}(\overline{x})
, and there exists a constant
0<r_{0}=r_{0}(n,p,L_{0},L_{1})
, with
8r_{0}\leq\frac{1}{10}

\frac{1}{8}a^{+}\leq|\nabla u(x)|\leq Ca^{+},\qquad\text{for all}\quad x\in B_{8r_{0}}(\overline{x}),

for an appropriate universal constant
C=C(n,p)>0
. Now,

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\theta_{ij}\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}u=0,\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{4r_{0}}(\overline{x}),

with
\theta_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+(p-2)|\nabla u|^{-2}\partial_{x_{i}}u\partial_{x_{j}}u
.
Also, in this way, we conclude that
h=u-V
satisfies the following uniformly elliptic equation

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\theta_{ij}\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}h=-a^{+}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\theta_{ij}M_{ij}

\displaystyle=-a^{+}\mathrm{tr}(M)-a^{+}(p-2)\frac{\left(\nabla u\right)^{T}M\nabla u}{|\nabla u|^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{a^{+}(p-2)}{|\nabla u|^{2}}(\nabla u+Mx)^{T}M\nabla h-a^{+}(p-2)\frac{x^{T}M^{3}x}{|\nabla u|^{2}}.

Then, recalling (

),
one can guarantee that

C^{-1}\sigma\leq u(x)-V(x+\sigma_{1}{\textbf{e}}_{n}),\qquad\text{for all}\quad x\in B_{r_{0}}(\overline{x}).

Thus, summarizing (

, or

, and the universal constant

Step 2:

In this step, we will construct a barrier.
For a given positive constant

in the closure of the annulus

. Also, the positive constant

is such that
\psi
satisfies the following boundary conditions:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\psi=1,&\text{on}\quad\partial B_{\frac{3}{4}}(x_{0}),\\
\psi=0,&\text{on}\quad\partial B_{r_{0}}(x_{0}).\end{array}\right.

Extend

to be equal to zero on

. We can choose

such that

\Delta_{p}(P-\epsilon\psi)\geq c(n,p)\epsilon>0,\qquad\text{in}\quad A,

for sufficiently small
\epsilon>0
, where

.
To see this, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{p}(P-\epsilon\psi)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}\left(|{\textbf{e}}_{n}+Mx-\epsilon\nabla\psi|^{p-2}({\textbf{e}}_{n}+Mx-\epsilon\nabla\psi)\right)

\displaystyle=|{\textbf{e}}_{n}+Mx-\epsilon\nabla\psi|^{p-4}\Big(-\epsilon|{\textbf{e}}_{n}+Mx-\epsilon\nabla\psi|^{2}\Delta\psi

\displaystyle\quad+(p-2)({\textbf{e}}_{n}+Mx-\epsilon\nabla\psi)^{T}(M-\epsilon D^{2}\psi)({\textbf{e}}_{n}+Mx-\epsilon\nabla\psi)\Big)

\displaystyle=-\epsilon\left(\Delta\psi+(p-2)\partial_{nn}\psi\right)+O(\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}).

On the other hand,

\Delta\psi+(p-2)\partial_{nn}\psi=-\frac{c\gamma}{|x-x_{0}|^{\gamma+4}}\left((\gamma-n-p+4)|x-x_{0}|^{2}+(p-2)(\gamma+2)|(x-x_{0})\cdot{\textbf{e}}_{n}|^{2}\right).

If we choose

, when

, and

, when

, then (

) holds for small

.

Step 3:

Now, define for

where

,

First, for

v_{0}(x)\leq(1+\epsilon\ell)a^{+}P(x)^{+}\leq V(x+\sigma_{1}{\textbf{e}}_{n})+a^{+}\tilde{c}\epsilon=V(x+\sigma_{1}{\textbf{e}}_{n})+c_{0}\epsilon\leq u(x).

When

\tilde{c}\psi(x)\geq\tilde{c}cr_{0}^{-\gamma}(1-2^{-\gamma})\geq\ell P(x).

Now, assume that

, hence

\begin{split}v_{0}(x)&=(1+\epsilon\ell)a^{+}\left(P(x)-\epsilon\tilde{c}(\psi(x)+k)\right)\\
&\leq V(x+\sigma_{1}{\textbf{e}}_{n})+\epsilon a^{+}\left(\ell P(x)-(1+\epsilon\ell)\tilde{c}\psi(x)\right)\\
&\leq V(x+\sigma_{1}{\textbf{e}}_{n})\leq u(x).\end{split}

Also, if

; because
v_{0}(x)\leq 0\leq a^{+}P(x)=V(x+\sigma_{1}{\textbf{e}}_{n})\leq u(x)
. If

v_{0}(x)=a_{-}(P(x)-\epsilon\tilde{c}(\psi(x)+k))\leq a_{-}P(x)=V(x+\sigma_{1}{\textbf{e}}_{n})\leq u(x).

Let
\overline{t}
be the largest

such that

We want to show that

Suppose this is true, then we will have

\begin{split}u(x)\geq v_{1}(x)&=(1+\epsilon\ell)a^{+}\left(P(x)+\epsilon\tilde{c}(1-\psi(x)-k)\right)^{+}-a^{-}\left(P(x)+\epsilon\tilde{c}(1-\psi(x)-k)\right)^{-}\\

where

Note that in the last inequality, we have used that

Suppose
\overline{t}<1

\begin{split}v_{t}(x)&=(1+\epsilon\ell)a^{+}\left(P(x)+\epsilon\tilde{c}(t-1-k)\right)^{+}-a^{-}\left(P(x)+\epsilon\tilde{c}(t-1-k)\right)^{-}<V(x).\end{split}

Also,

This contradicts (

\epsilon

Finally, if
\tilde{x}\in\{u=0\}

\displaystyle|\nabla v_{\overline{t}}^{+}(\tilde{x})|^{p}-|\nabla v_{\overline{t}}^{-}(\tilde{x})|^{p}

\displaystyle=\left(a_{+}^{p}(1+\epsilon\ell)^{p}-a_{-}^{p}\right)\left|{\textbf{e}}_{n}+M\tilde{x}-\epsilon\tilde{c}\nabla\psi(\tilde{x})\right|^{p}

\displaystyle=\left(\lambda_{+}^{p}-\lambda_{-}^{p}+pa_{+}^{p}\epsilon\ell+O(\epsilon^{2})\right)\left(1+\frac{p}{2}|M\tilde{x}|^{2}-p\epsilon\tilde{c}\partial_{n}\psi(\tilde{x}))+O(\epsilon^{\frac{3}{2}})\right).

One can verify that

for a universal constant

Therefore,
\tilde{x}\in\overline{B_{r_{0}}(x_{0})}

∎

Proof of Proposition

We divide the proof into three steps.

Step 1. Compactness.
Fix
r_{1}
universal to be made precise later in step 3.
Assume for a contradiction that we can find sequences
\epsilon_{k}\to 0
, and
\delta_{k}\to 0
, and also a sequence
u_{k}
of solutions to (
2
) in
D=B_{1}
such that

|u_{k}-V_{a_{k}^{+},M_{k},\textbf{e}_{n}}|\leq\epsilon_{k},\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{1},\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{TP}}(u_{k}),

with

\|M_{k}\|\leq\delta_{k}\epsilon_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad M_{k}\textbf{e}_{n}=0,\quad\left(a_{k}^{+}\right)^{p}-\left(a_{k}^{-}\right)^{p}=\lambda_{+}^{p}-\lambda_{-}^{p},\quad a_{k}^{+}\in\left[L_{0},L_{1}\right],

but

does not satisfy the conclusion (
8
).

Let
\tilde{u}_{k}:=\tilde{u}_{a_{k}^{+},M_{k},\textbf{e}_{n},\epsilon_{k}}
be defined as in (
19
). Then, (
20
) gives us

|\tilde{u}_{k}|\leq C,\qquad\text{for}\quad x\in B_{1}.

From Lemma
3.4
, we can easily obtain that the oscillation of
\tilde{u}
decreases by a factor

, as we restrict from
B_{1}
to
B_{\frac{1}{2}}
(see e.g.
[

).
This result can be iterated

\epsilon_{k}(2(1-c))^{m}\leq\overline{\epsilon}
. By the Arzelá-Ascoli theorem it follows that, as
\epsilon_{k}\to 0
,
\tilde{u}_{k}
(up to a subsequence) converges uniformly in

to a Hölder continuous function

.
Also, up to a subsequence,

and hence

a_{k}^{-}\to\overline{a}^{-}=\left((\overline{a}^{+})^{p}+\lambda_{-}^{p}-\lambda_{+}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Notice that
0\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{TP}}(u_{k})
implies that
\tilde{u}_{k}(0)=0
; hence
u^{*}(0)=0
.

Step 2. Limiting solution.
We now show that
u^{*}
solves

(21)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{p}(u)=0,&\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{\frac{1}{2}}\cap\{x_{n}\neq 0\},\\
a\partial_{n}^{+}u-b\partial_{n}^{-}u=0,&\qquad\text{on}\quad B_{\frac{1}{2}}\cap\{x_{n}=0\},\end{cases}

with
a=\left(\overline{a}^{+}\right)^{p}
and
b=\left(\overline{a}^{-}\right)^{p}
, where

(22)

\mathcal{L}_{p}(u):=\Delta u+(p-2)\partial_{nn}u.

First, we prove that
\mathcal{L}_{p}(u^{*})=0
. Let
P(x)
be a quadratic polynomial touching
u^{*}
at
\overline{x}\in B_{\frac{1}{2}}\cap\{x_{n}>0\}
strictly from below. We need to show that at this point

\mathcal{L}_{p}(P)=\Delta P+(p-2)\partial_{nn}P\leq 0.

Since
\tilde{u}_{k}\to u^{*}
, there exist points
x_{k}\in\Omega^{+}_{u_{k}}\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}
,
x_{k}\to\overline{x}
, and constants
c_{k}\to 0
such that

(23)

\tilde{u}_{k}(x_{k})=P(x_{k})+c_{k},

and

(24)

\tilde{u}_{k}\geq P+c_{k},\qquad\text{in a neighborhood of $x_{k}$.}

From the definition of
\tilde{u}_{k}
, (
23
) and (
24
) read

u_{k}(x_{k})=Q_{k}(x_{k}),

and

u_{k}(x)\geq Q_{k}(x),\qquad\text{in a neighborhood of $x_{k}$,}

respectively, where

Q_{k}(x)=\epsilon_{k}a_{k}^{+}(P(x)+c_{k})+a_{k}^{+}P_{M_{k},{\textbf{e}}_{n}}(x).

Note that

\nabla Q_{k}(x)=\epsilon_{k}a_{k}^{+}\nabla P(x)+a_{k}^{+}\left(\textbf{e}_{n}+M_{k}x\right),

thus by recalling
\|M_{k}\|=O(\epsilon_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}})
,

\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})\neq 0,\qquad\text{for $k$ large.}

Since
u_{k}
is
p
-harmonic, and
Q_{k}
touches
u_{k}
from below at
x_{k}
, and
\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})\neq 0
, by the equivalence of weak and viscosity solutions of
p
-harmonic functions, we get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geq\Delta_{p}Q_{k}(x_{k})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{div}\left(|\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})|^{p-2}\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})\right)

\displaystyle=\left|\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})\right|^{p-2}\left(\Delta Q_{k}(x_{k})+(p-2)\left(\frac{\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})}{|\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})|}\right)^{T}D^{2}Q_{k}(x_{k})\left(\frac{\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})}{|\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})|}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\left|\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})\right|^{p-2}\Bigg(\epsilon_{k}a_{k}^{+}\Delta P(x_{k})+a_{k}^{+}\mathrm{tr}(M_{k})

\displaystyle+\frac{p-2}{|\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})|^{2}}\left(\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})\right)^{T}\left(\epsilon_{k}a_{k}^{+}D^{2}P(x_{k})+a_{k}^{+}M_{k}\right)\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})\Bigg)

\displaystyle=a_{k}^{+}\left|\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})\right|^{p-2}\left(\epsilon_{k}\Delta P(x_{k})+\frac{p-2}{|\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})|^{2}}\left(\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})\right)^{T}\left(\epsilon_{k}D^{2}P(x_{k})+M_{k}\right)\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})\right)

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{k}a_{k}^{+}\left|\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})\right|^{p-2}\left(\Delta P(x_{k})+\frac{p-2}{|\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})|^{2}}\left(\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})\right)^{T}D^{2}P(x_{k})\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})+O(\epsilon_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}})\right).

Now, dividing both sides by
\epsilon_{k}
, and passing to the limit

, and recalling that

\nabla Q_{k}(x_{k})\to\overline{a}^{+}\textbf{e}_{n},

we conclude that

\Delta P(\overline{x})+(p-2)\partial_{nn}P(\overline{x})\leq 0.

Touching from above and reaching the opposite inequality is similar.
Also, the same reasoning can be applied to prove
\mathcal{L}_{p}(u^{*})=0
in
B_{\frac{1}{2}}\cap\{x_{n}<0\}
.

Now, we verify the boundary condition in the viscosity sense.
Let

w(x):=A+sx_{n}^{+}-tx_{n}^{-}+x^{T}Nx+\xi^{\prime}\cdot x^{\prime},

with

and

\mathcal{L}_{p}(w)>0
, and

as-bt>0.

We can always assume, via a change of coordinate in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}

satisfies (
16
).
Assume also that
w
touches
u^{*}
strictly from below at a point

. Set

\overline{a}_{k}^{+}=a_{k}^{+}(1+\epsilon_{k}s),\qquad\overline{a}_{k}^{-}=a_{k}^{-}(1+\epsilon_{k}t),

\overline{M}_{k}=M_{k}+2\epsilon_{k}N,\qquad Q_{k}:=P_{\overline{M}_{k},\textbf{e}_{n}}+\epsilon_{k}\xi^{\prime}\cdot x^{\prime}+A\epsilon_{k},

and

Let
\tilde{v}_{k}
be the
\epsilon

around the function
V_{a^{+},M_{k},\textbf{e}_{n}}
; then, it
converges uniformly to
w

B_{\frac{1}{2}}

Since

converges uniformly to

, and
w
touches
u^{*}
strictly from below at

, we conclude that for a sequence of constants

, and points

, the function

touches
u_{k}
from below at
x_{k}
.
Thus, Lemma
3.5

is a strict subsolution to our free boundary problem provided that we first choose

small enough, so as to guarantee that (
18

We reach a contradiction as we let

is a solution to the linearized problem (

Step 3. Contradiction.

Since
\tilde{u}_{k}
converges uniformly to
u^{*}
, and

enjoys the quadratic estimate of

|\tilde{u}_{k}-(x^{\prime}\cdot\nu^{\prime}+\tilde{s}x_{n}^{+}-\tilde{t}x_{n}^{-})|\leq Cr^{2},\qquad\text{for}\quad x\in B_{r},

with

a\tilde{s}-b\tilde{t}=0,\qquad|\nu^{\prime}|\leq C.

Define

\overline{a}_{k}^{+}=a_{k}^{+}\left(1+\epsilon_{k}\tilde{s}\,\right),\qquad\overline{a}_{k}^{-}=\left((\overline{a}^{+}_{k})^{p}+\lambda_{-}^{p}-\lambda_{+}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}=a_{k}^{-}\left(1+\epsilon_{k}\tilde{t}\,\right)+o(\epsilon_{k}),

and

\nu_{k}=\frac{\textbf{e}_{n}+\epsilon_{k}(\nu^{\prime},0)}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{k}^{2}|\nu^{\prime}|^{2}}}=\textbf{e}_{n}+\epsilon_{k}(\nu^{\prime},0)+\epsilon_{k}^{2}\tau,\qquad|\tau|\leq C.

We claim that we can decompose

(26)

M_{k}=\overline{M}_{k}+D_{k},

as

\overline{M}_{k}\nu_{k}=0,\qquad\mathrm{tr}(\overline{M}_{k})=0,\qquad\|D_{k}\|\leq C\|M_{k}\|\,|\nu_{k}-\textbf{e}_{n}|;

hence
\|D_{k}\|=O(\epsilon_{k}^{\frac{3}{2}})

To prove the claim, we first decompose

as

M_{k}=N_{k}+L_{k},

such that

, and

\|L_{k}\|\leq C\|M_{k}\|\,|\nu_{k}-\textbf{e}_{n}|.

Since
\mathrm{tr}(M_{k})=0
, we obtain that

\mathrm{tr}(N_{k})=O(\|L_{k}\|).

Then, we can decompose
N_{k}
further (here
\nu_{k}^{\perp}
is a unit vector perpendicular to
\nu_{k}

N_{k}=\overline{M}_{k}+z_{k}\left(\nu_{k}^{\perp}\otimes\nu_{k}^{\perp}\right),\qquad z_{k}=\mathrm{tr}(N_{k}),\qquad\overline{M}_{k}\nu_{k}=0,

so that
\mathrm{tr}(\overline{M}_{k})=0
,
and the claim (

Let us now show that for
r=r_{1}
universal (say,
\alpha_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
)

|u_{k}-V_{\overline{a}_{k}^{+},\overline{M}_{k},\nu_{k}}|\leq\epsilon_{k}r^{1+\alpha_{1}},

which contradicts the fact that
u_{k}
does not satisfy (
8
).
Using (
25
) and the definition of
\tilde{u}_{k}
, we get that in
\overline{\Omega_{u_{k}}^{+}\cap B_{r}}
(we can argue similarly in the negative part)

|u_{k}-a_{k}^{+}P_{M,\textbf{e}_{n}}-\epsilon_{k}a_{k}^{+}(x^{\prime}\cdot\nu^{\prime}+\tilde{s}x_{n}^{+}-\tilde{t}x_{n}^{-})|\leq 2\epsilon_{k}Cr^{2}.

Since in this set
x_{n}\geq-3\epsilon_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, we conclude that for all large

|u_{k}-\overline{a}_{k}^{+}P_{M,\textbf{e}_{n}}-\epsilon_{k}a_{k}^{+}(x^{\prime}\cdot\nu^{\prime}+\tilde{s}x_{n})|\leq 3\epsilon_{k}Cr^{2},

which gives

\left|u_{k}-\overline{a}_{k}^{+}\left(x\cdot\nu_{k}+\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Mx\right)\right|\leq 4\epsilon_{k}Cr^{2}.

Finally, from (

|u_{k}-V_{\overline{a}_{k}^{+},\overline{M}_{k},\nu_{k}}|\leq 5C\epsilon_{k}r^{2},

from which the desired bound follows for

small enough.
∎

4.
Nonlinear dichotomy

In this section, we prove the following proposition which provides a dichotomy result for the solutions of (
2
).
This result is crucial for the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 4.1

(Nonlinear dichotomy)
.

Let
u
be a solution to (
2
) with
|\nabla u|\leq 1
and assume that
0\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{TP}}(u)
.
Then, there exist small universal positive constants
\epsilon_{0}
,
\delta_{0}
,
r_{0}
,
c_{0}
,
\alpha_{0}
such that if

|u(x)-P_{M,\nu}(x)|\leq\epsilon,\qquad\text{for}\quad x\in B_{1},

with
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
, and

\|M\|\leq\delta_{0}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}},

then one of the following two alternatives holds:

(i)

|\nabla u^{+}(0)|\leq 1-c_{0}\epsilon
, or

(ii)

|u-P_{\overline{M},\overline{\nu}}|\leq\epsilon r_{0}^{2+\alpha_{0}}
in
B_{r_{0}}
for some
\overline{M}
,
\overline{\nu}
with
\|M-\overline{M}\|\leq C\epsilon
and
C
universal.

Remark 4.2
.

If
u
satisfies alternative
(ii)
of Proposition
4.1
, then we can rescale and iterate one more time. Indeed, the rescaling

u_{r}(x):=\frac{u(rx)}{r},\qquad r=r_{0},

will satisfy

|u-P_{r\overline{M},\overline{\nu}}|\leq\epsilon_{r}:=\epsilon r^{1+\alpha_{0}},\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{1},

with

\|r\overline{M}\|\leq\delta_{0}\epsilon_{r}^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\epsilon_{r}\leq\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0},

provided that
\epsilon_{0}
is chosen small enough; see Remark
3.2
.

The proof of Nonlinear dichotomy, i.e. Proposition
4.1
, is very similar to that of Proposition
3.1
.
However, before that, we need the following Linear dichotomy which is similar to the nonlinear one.

Proposition 4.3

(Linear dichotomy)
.

Let
v
solves

(27)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{p}(v)=0,&\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{1}\cap\{x_{n}\neq 0\},\\
\partial_{n}^{+}v-b\partial_{n}^{-}v=0,&\qquad\text{on}\quad B_{1}\cap\{x_{n}=0\},\end{cases}

with
0<b_{0}\leq b\leq b_{1}
and
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
. Moreover, suppose that

\partial_{n}v\leq 0.

Then, there exist universal constants
c_{0},r_{0}>0
such that either

(i)

\partial_{n}^{+}v(0)\leq-c_{0}
, or

(ii)

|v(x)-Q(x^{\prime})|\leq\frac{1}{4}r_{0}^{2}
in
B_{r_{0}}
,

where
Q(x^{\prime})
is a quadratic polynomial in the
x^{\prime}
-direction with
\|Q\|\leq C
universal and

\mathcal{L}_{p}(Q)=\Delta Q=0.

Proof.

Let
r_{0}
be given, to be specified later.
Assume by contradiction that we can find a sequence
c_{k}\to 0
, and the sequences of constants
b_{0}\leq b_{k}\leq b_{1}
, and bounded monotone solutions
v_{k}
to (
27
) that satisfy

\partial_{n}^{+}v_{k}(0)>-c_{k},

and for which condition
(ii)
does not hold.
Then, up to extracting a sequence,
v_{k}
converges uniformly on compacts and in the
C^{1,\alpha}
norm from either side of
\{x_{n}\neq 0\}
to a solution
\overline{v}
for a limiting problem

(28)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{p}(\overline{v})=0,&\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{\frac{3}{4}}\setminus\{x_{n}\neq 0\},\\
\partial_{n}^{+}\overline{v}-b\partial_{n}^{-}\overline{v}=0,&\qquad\text{on}\quad B_{\frac{3}{4}}\cap\{x_{n}=0\},\end{cases}

with
\|\overline{v}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
, and

(29)

\partial_{n}\overline{v}\leq 0,\qquad\partial_{n}^{+}\overline{v}(0)=0.

After subtracting a linear function in the
x^{\prime}
-variable, we can assume that (in view of the free boundary condition
\partial_{n}^{-}\overline{v}(0)=0
)

(30)

\overline{v}(0)=0,\qquad\nabla\overline{v}(0)=0.

Step 1.
We prove that

D_{x^{\prime}}^{2}\overline{v}\leq CI_{x^{\prime}},\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{\frac{1}{4}},

or, in other words that
\overline{v}
is uniformly semiconcave in the
x^{\prime}
-variable.

For this, it suffices to show that

(31)

\overline{v}_{\tau\tau}^{h}(x):=\frac{\overline{v}(x+h\tau)+\overline{v}(x-h\tau)-2\overline{v}(x)}{h^{2}}\leq C,\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{\frac{1}{4}},

independently of
h
, where
\tau
is a unit direction with
\tau\perp\textbf{e}_{n}
, and
h>0
. We claim that

(32)

\overline{v}_{\tau\tau}^{h}(x)\leq\frac{C}{x_{n}^{2}},\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{\frac{1}{2}}.

This estimate and the fact that
\overline{v}_{\tau\tau}^{h}
is a solution for the transmission problem gives (
31
); in fact it is enough to use a suitable version of

-Harnack inequality for small
p>0
(see e.g.
[
13
, Theorem 4.8 (2)]
). To prove (
32
), notice that by the

interior estimates in
B_{\frac{|x_{n}|}{2}}(x)

|D^{2}\overline{v}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{x_{n}^{2}}\|\overline{v}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{C}{x_{n}^{2}},\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{\frac{5}{8}}\setminus\{x_{n}=0\}.

Since we can write

\overline{v}_{\tau\tau}^{h}(x_{0})=\int_{-1}^{1}\overline{v}_{\tau\tau}(x_{0}+th\tau)(1-|t|)\,dt,

(
32
) follows.

Step 2.
In this step, we wish to show that

(33)

\|\overline{v}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r})}\leq Cr^{2},\qquad r\leq\frac{1}{4},

with

universal.

Let us consider

\tilde{v}(x)=\frac{\overline{v}(rx)}{r^{2}},\qquad x\in B_{1}.

Then,
\tilde{v}
satisfies (

) and (

), we also have

\tilde{v}(0)=0,\qquad\nabla\tilde{v}(0)=0.

Then, the conclusion of Step 1 implies that

(34)

\tilde{v}(x^{\prime},0)\leq C|x^{\prime}|^{2},\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{1}\cap\{x_{n}=0\}.

Hence, by the monotonicity of
\tilde{v}
,

(35)

\tilde{v}\leq C,\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{1}\cap\{x_{n}>0\}.

Next, we claim that

(36)

\tilde{v}\geq-MC,\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{\frac{1}{3}}\left(\frac{\textbf{e}_{n}}{2}\right),

for some large constant
M
to be specified later.
Suppose by contradiction that this does not hold.
Then, for any arbitrary point on
x_{0}\in\{x_{n}=d\}\cap\{|x^{\prime}|=\frac{1}{3}\}
for small

, choose a sequence of points

with

and

Note that

, i.e. the number of points, depends only on

and is less than

.
Then, by virtue of Harnack inequality for
C-\tilde{v}\geq 0
in

such that

C-\tilde{v}(x_{i})\geq c_{0}(C-\tilde{v}(x_{i+1})).

This implies that

C-\tilde{v}\geq(1+M)Cd^{K},\qquad\text{on}\quad x_{n}=d,\quad|x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{3},

with

universal. In particular, if

then

(37)

\tilde{v}\leq-C,\qquad\text{on}\quad x_{n}=d,\quad|x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{3}.

We now compare
\tilde{v}
with the explicit barrier

\phi(x):=10C|x^{\prime}|^{2}-Ax_{n}^{2}-x_{n},

where

is chosen so that
\mathcal{L}_{p}(\phi)\leq 0
.
Define

R:=\left\{0<x_{n}<d,\quad|x^{\prime}|<\frac{1}{3}\right\}.

We show that for
d
small enough and hence
M
large enough, we have

(38)

\tilde{v}\leq\phi,\qquad\text{on}\quad\partial R.

Thus, we conclude that the inequality holds in
R
and we reach a contradiction because

0=\partial_{n}\tilde{v}(0)\leq\partial_{n}\phi(0)=-1.

Now, we check (
38
). On
\{x_{n}=d\}
, this follows from (
37
) if

is chosen small depending on
C
and
A
. Similarly, on
\{x_{n}=0\}
, the desired bound follows immediately from (

). Finally, in the set

\left\{0<x_{n}<d,\quad|x^{\prime}|=\frac{1}{3}\right\},

we use (
35
) and again we obtain (
38
) for

sufficiently small. In conclusion, the claim (

) holds.

Since
\tilde{v}
is decreasing in the
\textbf{e}_{n}
-direction, we obtain

\tilde{v}\geq-C,\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{\frac{1}{3}},

for some
C
. Now, recalling that

, and applying Harnack inequality for

which after rescaling gives the desired claim (

).

Step 3.
We prove that if
\overline{v}
solves (
28
) and satisfies (

), (

), and (

), then there exists a universal

such that alternative
(ii)
holds for
\overline{v}
with right-hand side
\frac{1}{8}r_{0}^{2}
instead of
\frac{1}{4}r_{0}^{2}
.

This claim follows by compactness from the classification of the global solution of

, i.e. Lemma

.

Assume by contradiction that there exists a sequence of
\delta_{k}\to 0
, and solutions
\overline{v}_{k}
to a sequence of problems

(satisfying the same properties as

) for which the alternative
(ii)
fails in the ball of radius

. Denote the quadratic rescalings by

w_{k}(x)=\frac{\overline{v}_{k}(\delta_{k}x)}{\delta_{k}^{2}}.

Then, up to extracting a subsequence,
w_{k}
converges uniformly on compacts to a global solution

to the limiting transmission problem

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{p}(U)=0,&\qquad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}\cap\{x_{n}\neq 0\},\\
\partial_{n}^{+}U=g\partial_{n}^{-}U,&\qquad\text{on}\quad\{x_{n}=0\},\end{cases}

with

.
The convergence is in the

norm from either side of
\{x_{n}\neq 0\}
, up to
\{x_{n}=0\}
, and in the

norm in the interior.
Clearly the global solution

also satisfies (

), (

), and (

).

Then according to Lemma
B.1
, we conclude that
U=Q(x^{\prime})
with

being a pure quadratic polynomial in the
x^{\prime}
-direction and with
\mathcal{L}_{p}(Q)=\Delta Q=0
.
Therefore, for
k
large,
\overline{v}_{k}
satisfies the alternative
(ii)
in
B_{\delta_{k}}
. The contradiction establishes Step 3.

By Step 3,
\overline{v}
satisfies alternative
(ii)
with right-hand side
\frac{1}{8}r_{0}^{2}
for some quadratic polynomial
\overline{Q}(x^{\prime})
with
\mathcal{L}_{p}(\overline{Q})=\Delta\overline{Q}=0
.
As above, this means that the
v_{k}
’s satisfy the alternative
(ii)
for all
k
large, which is a contradiction.
∎

4.1
.

We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1. Compactness and the limiting solution.
Fix
r_{0}
universal to be made precise later.
Toward a contradiction, assume that there is sequences
\epsilon_{k}\to 0
,
\delta_{k}\to 0
and a sequence
u_{k}
of solutions to (
2
) in
D=B_{1}
such that

|u_{k}-P_{M_{k},\textbf{e}_{n}}|\leq\epsilon_{k},\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{1},\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{TP}}^{\mathrm{int}}(u_{k}),

with

\|M_{k}\|\leq\delta_{k}\epsilon_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad M_{k}\textbf{e}_{n}=0,\qquad\mathrm{tr}(M_{k})=0,

but
u_{k}
does not satisfy either alternative
(i)
(for some small constant
c_{0}^{\prime}
to be specified later) or alternative
(ii)
.

Let
\tilde{u}_{k}:=\tilde{u}_{1,M_{k},\textbf{e}_{n},\epsilon_{k}}
be defined as in (
19
).
Using the Step 1 and 2 of the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we know that as
\epsilon_{k}\to 0
,
\tilde{u}_{k}
converges uniformly (up to a subsequence) in

to a Hölder continuous function
u^{*}
with
u^{*}(0)=0
,
\|u^{*}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C
, and
u^{*}
solves the transmission problem

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{p}(u^{*})=0,&\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{\frac{1}{2}}\cap\{x_{n}\neq 0\},\\
\partial_{n}^{+}{u^{*}}-b\partial_{n}^{-}{u^{*}}=0,&\qquad\text{on}\quad B_{\frac{1}{2}}\cap\{x_{n}=0\},\end{cases}

with
b>0
bounded by a universal constant. (Indeed, in Proposition
3.1
we can choose
L_{0}=\frac{1}{2}
and
L_{1}=2
due to the condition
|\nabla u|\leq 1
.)

Next, we claim that
u^{*}
is monotone decreasing in the
\textbf{e}_{n}
-direction, i.e.

(39)

\partial_{n}{u^{*}}\leq 0.

To prove this claim, we consider the following nonlinear elliptic equation satisfied by
\tilde{u}_{k}

(40)

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\theta_{ij}\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}\tilde{u}_{k}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}\tilde{u}_{k}=\frac{2-p}{\epsilon_{k}|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}}x^{T}M_{k}^{2}\nabla u_{k},\qquad\text{in}\quad\Omega_{u_{k}}^{+}\cap\{|\nabla u_{k}|\neq 0\}.

where
\theta_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+(p-2)|\nabla u_{k}|^{-2}\partial_{x_{i}}u_{k}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{k}
and

\beta_{i}=(p-2)|\nabla u_{k}|^{-2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{M_{k}}_{{}_{ij}}\partial_{x_{j}}u_{k}
.

As
\Gamma_{\mathrm{TP}}^{\mathrm{int}}(u_{k})\subset\{|x_{n}|\leq\epsilon_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}\}
, if
\overline{x}\in\{x_{n}>0\}
, we have for
k
large enough

B:=B_{\frac{\overline{x}_{n}}{2}}(\overline{x})\subset\Omega_{u_{k}}^{+},

and we first show that

(41)

|\nabla\tilde{u}_{k}|\leq C\left(\overline{x}_{n}\right),\qquad\text{in}\quad B.

Inside
\{|\nabla u_{k}|\geq\frac{1}{4}\}\cap B
, we get
|\nabla\tilde{u}_{k}|\leq C
from (
40
) for a universal constant
C
.
Thus,

|\nabla u_{k}|\geq 1-\delta_{k}\epsilon_{k}^{\frac{1}{2}}-C\epsilon_{k}\geq\frac{1}{2},

for sufficiently large
k
.
This along with the continuity of
\nabla u
inside
\Omega_{u_{k}}^{+}
necessitates that
|\nabla u_{k}|\geq\frac{1}{2}
for sufficiently large
k
. This proves (
41
)

Hence,

\nabla u_{k}(\overline{x})=\nabla(P_{M_{k},\textbf{e}_{n}}+\epsilon_{k}\tilde{u}_{k})(\overline{x})=\textbf{e}_{n}+M_{k}\overline{x}+\epsilon_{k}\nabla\tilde{u}_{k}(\overline{x}).

Using that
|\nabla u_{k}(\overline{x})|^{2}\leq 1
and
M_{k}\textbf{e}_{n}=0
, we get

1\geq 1+|M_{k}\overline{x}|^{2}+2\epsilon_{k}\partial_{n}\tilde{u}_{k}(\overline{x})+O(\epsilon_{k}^{\frac{3}{2}}),

where the constant in
O(\epsilon_{k}^{\frac{3}{2}})
depends on
\overline{x}_{n}
.
By
C^{1,\alpha}
estimates of (
40
) in the ball
B
,
\tilde{u}_{k}
converges to
u^{*}
in
C^{1}
.
Passing to the limit, as

, we get that

\partial_{n}u^{*}(\overline{x})\leq 0.

By continuity, since
u^{*}
is
C^{1,\alpha}
up to
\{x_{n}=0\}
, we conclude that

\partial_{n}^{+}{u^{*}}\leq 0,\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{1}\cap\{x_{n}\geq 0\}.

We can argue similarly to show that
\partial_{n}^{-}{u^{*}}\leq 0
.

Step 2. Contradiction.

According to Proposition
4.3
, i.e. Linear dichotomy (since (
39
) holds and
\|u^{*}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
), there exist universal constants
c_{0}>0
and
r_{0}>0
such that either of the following conditions is satisfied:

(i)

\partial_{n}^{+}u^{*}(0)\leq-c_{0}
, or

(ii)

|u^{*}(x)-Q(x^{\prime})|\leq\frac{1}{4}r_{0}^{2}
in
B_{r_{0}}
, where
Q(x^{\prime})
is a quadratic polynomial in the
x^{\prime}
-direction with
\|Q\|\leq C
universal and
\mathcal{L}_{p}(Q)=\Delta Q=0
.

If
(i)
above is satisfied, by the quadratic estimate for
u^{*}
(see
[
19
, Theorem 3.2]
or
[
8
, Lemma 4.12]
), we obtain that

|u^{*}(x)-(\xi^{\prime}\cdot x^{\prime}+sx_{n}^{+}-tx_{n}^{-})|\leq Cr^{2},\qquad\text{for}\quad x\in B_{r},

with
|s|,|t|,|\xi^{\prime}|\leq C
, and

s-bt=0,\qquad s\leq-c_{0}.

Using the convergence of the
\tilde{u}_{k}
to
u^{*}
and the definition of
\tilde{u}_{k}
, we immediately get that for
k
large,

|u_{k}(x)-\left(P_{M_{k},\textbf{e}_{n}}(x)+\epsilon_{k}\left(\xi^{\prime}\cdot x^{\prime}+sx_{n}^{+}-tx_{n}^{-}\right)\right)|\leq C\epsilon_{k}r^{2},\qquad\text{for}\quad x\in B_{r}.

Arguing as in Step 3 of the proof of Proposition
3.1
, we conclude that for
r\leq r_{1}
,

|u_{k}-V_{\overline{a}_{k}^{+},\overline{M}_{k},\nu_{k}}|\leq\epsilon_{k}r^{1+\alpha_{1}},\qquad\text{with}\quad\overline{a}^{+}=1+\epsilon_{k}s.

Thus, after rescaling (see Remark
3.2
), Corollary
3.3
gives that

|\nabla u_{k}^{+}(0)|\leq 1+s\epsilon_{k}+C\epsilon_{k}r^{1+\alpha_{1}}\leq 1-\frac{1}{2}c_{0}\epsilon_{k},

provided that

is chosen small, universal. Thus,
u_{k}
satisfies alternative
(i)
in Proposition
4.1
for
c_{0}^{\prime}=\frac{c_{0}}{2}
and we arrive at a contradiction.

If condition
(ii)
above is satisfied,
since
u^{*}(0)=0
, we have

Q(x^{\prime})=\xi^{\prime}\cdot x^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}x^{T}Nx,\qquad N\textbf{e}_{n}=0,\qquad\mathrm{tr}(N)=0.

Now using the convergence of the
\tilde{u}_{k}
to
u^{*}
and the definition of
\tilde{u}_{k}
, we get

(42)

\left|u_{k}(x)-\left(x_{n}+\epsilon_{k}\xi^{\prime}\cdot x^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}x^{T}N_{k}^{*}x\right)\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{k}r_{0}^{2},

with

N_{k}^{*}=M_{k}+\epsilon_{k}N,\qquad N_{k}^{*}\textbf{e}_{n}=0,\qquad\mathrm{tr}(N_{k}^{*})=0,

where we have used

\mathrm{tr}(M_{k})=0
and
\mathrm{tr}(N)=0
.
Finally, as before, denote

\nu_{k}=\frac{\textbf{e}_{n}+\epsilon_{k}(\xi^{\prime},0)}{\sqrt{1+\epsilon_{k}^{2}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}}}=\textbf{e}_{n}+\epsilon_{k}(\xi^{\prime},0)+\epsilon_{k}^{2}\tau,\qquad|\tau|\leq C.

We argue as in Step 3 of Proposition
3.1
, and decompose

N_{k}^{*}=\overline{M}_{k}+D_{k},\qquad\overline{M}_{k}\nu_{k}=0,\qquad\|D_{k}\|=o(\epsilon_{k}),

with
\mathrm{tr}(\overline{M}_{k})=0
.
Thus, (

) yields for

large

|u_{k}-P_{\overline{M}_{k},\nu_{k}}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{k}r_{0}^{2}\leq\epsilon_{k}r_{0}^{2+\alpha_{0}},

for

∎

5.
Classification of Lipschitz global solutions

The objective of this section is to prove Theorem
1.1
, a Liouville type theorem for the Lipschitz global viscosity solutions of the two-phase Bernoulli problem (
2
).
Before that, we establish the following lemma which shows that a two-plane solution with maximum slopes at some scale (possibly small) can approximate the solution
u
well.
Then, by using Proposition
4.1
, we obtain a ”reversed” improvement of the flatness, and hence conclude that the solution
u
is well-approximated by the same two-plane solution at all large scales.

Let
u
be a Lipschitz viscosity solution to (
2
) in
D=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e. a global solution. Define

(43)

\alpha:=\sup_{\Omega_{u}^{+}}|\nabla u|,\qquad\beta:=\sup_{\Omega_{u}^{-}}|\nabla u|.

An initial flatness condition for
u
can be obtained by the following lemma, at any scale.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
u
,
\alpha
and
\beta
be as above.
Then,

\alpha^{p}-\beta^{p}=\lambda_{+}^{p}-\lambda_{-}^{p}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\alpha\geq\lambda_{+},\quad\beta\geq\lambda_{-}.

Moreover, if
\Gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{TP}}(u)\neq\emptyset
, then there exists a sequence
u_{k}(x):=\frac{u(x_{k}+d_{k}x)}{d_{k}}
of rescalings of
u
in
B_{R}
(for any fixed
R>1
) with

u_{k}\to\alpha x_{n}^{+}-\beta x_{n}^{-},

uniformly on
B_{R}
, as
k\to+\infty
.

Proof.

Given
\epsilon>0
, let
x_{\epsilon}\in\Omega_{u}^{+}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,u(x)>0\}
be such that

|\nabla u(x_{\epsilon})|\geq\alpha-\epsilon.

Let
d_{\epsilon}
be the distance from
x_{\epsilon}
to
\partial\Omega_{u}^{+}
and consider the rescaling

u_{\epsilon}(x):=\frac{u(x_{\epsilon}+d_{\epsilon}x)}{d_{\epsilon}}.

These rescalings will still satisfy (
2
), with
B_{1}\subset\Omega_{u_{\epsilon}}^{+}
being tangent to
\partial\Omega_{u_{\epsilon}}^{+}=\partial\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,u_{\epsilon}(x)>0\}
.
In fact, after a rotation, we can also assume that

\nabla u_{\epsilon}(0)=t_{\epsilon}\textbf{e}_{n},\qquad\alpha\geq t_{\epsilon}\geq\alpha-\epsilon.

Since
u_{\epsilon}
is uniformly Lipschitz, up to extracting a subsequence, we can conclude that

u_{\epsilon}\to\overline{u},

uniformly on compacts in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, as
\epsilon
goes to zero. In addition, the convergence is in
C^{1,\eta}(B_{\frac{1}{2}})
due to
\Delta_{p}u_{\epsilon}=0
in
B_{1}
.
Thus

\overline{u}
solves (
2
) in
D=B_{R}
, for any fixed
R>1
, and it satisfies

\partial_{n}\overline{u}(0)=\alpha,\qquad\text{and}\qquad|\nabla\overline{u}|\leq\alpha\qquad\text{in}\quad\Omega_{\overline{u}}^{+},

with
B_{1}\subset\Omega_{\overline{u}}^{+}
tangent to
\partial\Omega_{\overline{u}}^{+}
.
Hence, we have
\Delta_{p}\overline{u}=0
in
B_{1}
and
\overline{u}\geq 0
in
B_{1}
, but it is not identically zero because
\partial_{n}\overline{u}(0)=\alpha>0
. Therefore
\overline{u}>0
in
B_{1}
.
Since
\overline{u}
is
p
-harmonic, it is locally
C^{1,\eta}
and we can choose a small
\delta>0
such that
\partial_{n}\overline{u}>\frac{\alpha}{2}
in
B_{\delta}
.
Thus,
v=\partial_{n}\overline{u}
will satisfy the uniform elliptic equation

(44)

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\partial_{i}(a_{ij}\partial_{j}v)=0,\qquad a_{ij}=|\nabla\overline{u}|^{p-2}\left(\delta_{ij}+(p-2)\frac{\partial_{i}\overline{u}\partial_{j}\overline{u}}{|\nabla\overline{u}|^{2}}\right),

in
B_{\delta}
.
Hence by the strong maximum principle

\partial_{n}\overline{u}\equiv\alpha,\qquad\text{in}\quad\mathcal{O},

where
\mathcal{O}
is the connected component on
\Omega_{\overline{u}}^{+}
containing
B_{1}
.
Therefore,

\overline{u}(x)=\alpha x_{n}+\mathrm{const},\qquad\text{in}\quad\mathcal{O}.

Moreover,
\overline{u}=0
at the point, say
-e_{n}
, where the unit ball is tangent to
\partial\Omega_{\overline{u}}^{+}
; thus

\overline{u}(x)=\alpha(x_{n}+1),\qquad\text{in}\quad\{x_{n}>-1\}.

On the other hand,
\overline{u}
is also a viscosity solution to (
2
) and we must have

\alpha\geq\lambda_{+}.

Now recall (
43
). Then,
|\nabla\overline{u}|\leq\beta
in
\Omega_{\overline{u}}^{-}
shows that the function

H(x):=\alpha(x_{n}+1)^{+}-\beta(x_{n}+1)^{-},

touches
\overline{u}
from below on
\{x_{n}=-1\}
.
We distinguish here two cases:

Case
(i)
:

\{x_{n}=-1\}\cap\Gamma_{\mathrm{OP}}(\overline{u})\neq\emptyset
.

From the definition of viscosity solution, we obtain that

\alpha=|\nabla H^{+}|\leq\lambda_{+}.

So, we have
\alpha=\lambda_{+}
in this case.

Case
(ii)
:

\{x_{n}=-1\}\subseteq\Gamma_{\mathrm{TP}}(\overline{u})
.

From the definition of viscosity solution, we must have

\alpha^{p}-\beta^{p}=|\nabla H^{+}|^{p}-|\nabla H^{-}|^{p}\leq\lambda_{+}^{p}-\lambda_{-}^{p}.

Similarly, we can argue as above, starting with points
x_{\epsilon}\in\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,:\,u(x)<0\}
where
\nabla u(x_{\epsilon})>\beta-\epsilon
, to get
\beta\geq\lambda_{-}
, as well as one of the following cases hold:

\beta=\lambda_{-}\qquad\text{or}\qquad\alpha^{p}-\beta^{p}\geq\lambda_{+}^{p}-\lambda_{-}^{p}.

Thus, we have shown that

\alpha^{p}-\beta^{p}=\lambda_{+}^{p}-\lambda_{-}^{p}.

In order to prove the second part of the lemma, notice that if either
\alpha>\lambda_{+}
or
\beta>\lambda_{-}
, we are in situation Case
(ii)
, and hence
\Omega^{-}_{\overline{u}}=\{x_{n}<-1\}
.
Noting that
H(x)
touches
\overline{u}(x)
from below on
\{x_{n}=-1\}
, and invoking the Hopf boundary lemma, we infer that

\overline{u}(x)=\alpha(x_{n}+1)^{+}-\beta(x_{n}+1)^{-}.

Now, consider the case
\alpha=\lambda_{+}
and
\beta=\lambda_{-}
, and assume that
0\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{TP}}(u)
.
Let

\ell=\limsup_{\Omega_{u}^{+}\ni x\to 0}|\nabla u(x)|\leq\alpha=\lambda_{+},

and choose a maximizing sequence
y_{k}\to 0
such that
|\nabla u(y_{k})|\to\ell
and
u(y_{k})>0
.
Repeating the argument in the first part of the proof, we conclude that
\ell\geq\lambda_{+}
and
\frac{u(y_{k}+d_{k}x)}{d_{k}}\to\overline{u}(x)
, up to a subsequence. Also,
\overline{u}(x)=\lambda_{+}(x_{n}+1)
in
\{x_{n}>-1\}
.
Since
0\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{TP}}(u)
and
|B_{r}\cap\{u=0\}|=0
for some
r>0
, so
\{x_{n}=-1\}\subseteq\Gamma_{\mathrm{TP}}(\overline{u})
and

\overline{u}(x)=\lambda_{+}(x_{n}+1)^{+}-\lambda_{-}(x_{n}+1)^{-}.

∎

Remark 5.2
.

From now on, for the sake of convenience, we assume that

\alpha=\beta=1.

This is not a restrictive assumption as one can replace
u
by
\alpha^{-1}u^{+}-\beta^{-1}u^{-}
. In this case, we have
\lambda_{+}=\lambda_{-}\leq 1
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Assume
|\nabla u|\leq 1
,
0\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{TP}}(u)
and
t\textbf{e}_{n}\in\Omega_{u}^{+}
for all
t\in(0,1]
. If

\partial_{n}u(t\textbf{e}_{n})\geq 1-\eta,\qquad\forall t\in(0,1],

for some
\eta>0
, then

|u(x)-x_{n}|\leq\epsilon(\eta),\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{2},

with
\epsilon(\eta)\to 0
as
\eta\to 0
.

Proof.

This follows immediately by compactness, the same lines of arguments as the proof of Lemma
5.1
.
Assume by contradiction that there exists a sequence
\eta_{k}\to 0
, and a sequence of equi-Lipschitz solutions
u_{k}
such that

(45)

\partial_{n}u_{k}(t\textbf{e}_{n})\geq 1-\eta_{k},\qquad\forall t\in(0,1],\,t\textbf{e}_{n}\in\Omega_{u_{k}}^{+},

but

(46)

|u_{k}(x)-x_{n}|>\delta,\qquad\text{at some point $x\in B_{2}$ for some fixed $\delta>0$}.

From
u_{k}(0)=0
and (
45
), we obtain

u_{k}(\textbf{e}_{n})\geq 1-\eta_{k}.

Since
|\nabla u_{k}|\leq 1
, it follows that

u_{k}>0,\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{1-\eta_{k}}(\textbf{e}_{n}).

We can now argue as in the previous lemma and extract a subsequence that will converge uniformly on
\overline{B_{2}}
to
\overline{u}=x_{n}^{+}-x_{n}^{-}=x_{n}
and contradict (
46
).
∎

We are now ready to provide the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

According to Lemma
5.1
, after a translation, a rotation, and a rescaling, we can assume that (recall also the assumption
\alpha=\beta=1
)

0\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{TP}}(u),\qquad\text{and}\qquad|u-x_{n}|\leq\epsilon^{\prime}\quad\text{in}\quad B_{1},

for
\epsilon^{\prime}
small, to be made precise later. Now, let
\epsilon_{0}
be a sufficiently small universal constant so that the conclusions of Proposition
4.1
, Proposition
3.1
, and Corollary
3.3
hold for all
\epsilon\leq\epsilon_{0}
. If
\epsilon^{\prime}\leq\epsilon_{0}
, then Corollary
3.3
implies that
\Gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{TP}}(u)\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}
is locally
C^{1,\eta}
smooth and
u\in C^{1,\eta}\left(\overline{\Omega_{u}^{+}\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)
.

By denoting
h:=u-x_{n}\in C^{1,\eta}\left(\overline{\Omega_{u}^{+}\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}}\right)
, we easily achieve the following nonlinear uniformly elliptic equation

(47)

\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\theta_{ij}\partial_{x_{i}x_{j}}h=0,\qquad\text{in}\quad\{\nabla u\neq 0\},

with
C^{0,\eta}
coefficients
\theta_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+(p-2)|\nabla u|^{-2}\partial_{x_{i}}u\partial_{x_{j}}u
.
Now, choose the largest ball
B_{r}
(for
r\leq\frac{1}{2}
) such that
\nabla u\neq 0
inside
\Omega_{u}^{+}\cap B_{r}
; then, the flatness of the free boundary implies that
|u_{r}-x_{n}|\leq C\epsilon^{\prime}
for a universal constant
C
.
So,
h_{r}=u_{r}-x_{n}
satisfies (
47
) in
\Omega_{u_{r}}^{+}\cap B_{1}
, and the
C^{1,\eta}
estimate of
h_{r}
yields that

|\partial_{n}u-1|\leq C\epsilon^{\prime},\qquad\text{in}\quad\Omega_{u}^{+}\cap B_{r},

for a universal constant
C
.
If
\epsilon_{0}
is sufficiently small, we can conclude that
\nabla u\neq 0
in
\Omega_{u}^{+}\cap B_{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Using again
C^{1,\eta}
estimate of
h
in (
47
), we deduce that

(48)

\partial_{n}u(t\textbf{e}_{n})\geq 1-C\epsilon^{\prime},\qquad\forall t\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right],

and also

(49)

\partial_{n}u^{+}(0):=\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\frac{u(t\textbf{e}_{n})}{t}\geq 1-C\epsilon^{\prime}.

Now, let
\epsilon(\eta)
be as defined in Lemma
5.3
, and choose a universal constant
\overline{\eta}
small enough such that
\epsilon(\overline{\eta})\leq\epsilon_{0}
. We claim that the following inequality holds

(50)

\partial_{n}u(t\textbf{e}_{n})\geq 1-\overline{\eta},\qquad\text{for all}\quad t>0,

provided that
\epsilon^{\prime}
is chosen sufficiently small.
Suppose this is true, then according to Lemma
5.3
,

|u-x_{n}|\leq\epsilon(\overline{\eta})R\leq\epsilon_{0}R,\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{R},

for all
R
large. This, along with the improvement of flatness
[
8
, Theorem 4.1]
, implies that

|u-U_{{a}^{+},\overline{\nu}}|\leq\epsilon_{0}\rho^{1+\gamma}R,\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{\rho R},

for some
a^{+}=a^{+}(R)
,
\overline{\nu}=\overline{\nu}(R)
, and universal constants
\rho,\gamma>0
. Then, for all
R

{\rm dist}(u,\mathcal{U}_{\lambda_{+},\lambda_{-}})\leq\epsilon_{0}\rho^{\gamma}R,

where the distance is measured in
L^{\infty}(B_{R})
. Repeat this argument to find out
{\rm dist}(u,\mathcal{U}_{\lambda_{+},\lambda_{-}})\to 0
, for a fixed
R
.
So,
u
must be a two-plane solution which concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Now, we should prove the claim (
50
). For this denote
C\epsilon^{\prime}
from (
48
) by
\epsilon^{\prime\prime}:=C\epsilon^{\prime}
, and we will choose
\epsilon^{\prime\prime}
(and therefore
\epsilon^{\prime}
) later, so that
\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\ll\overline{\eta}
.
Let
\overline{t}
be the first

for which (
50
) fails, and assume without loss of generality after a rescaling that
\overline{t}=1
. In other words,

(51)

\partial_{n}u(\textbf{e}_{n})=1-\overline{\eta},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\partial_{n}u(t\textbf{e}_{n})\geq 1-\overline{\eta},\quad\forall t\in[0,1].

Notice that after rescaling, (
49
) is still satisfied, i.e.

\partial_{n}u^{+}(0)\geq 1-\epsilon^{\prime\prime}.

From Lemma
5.3
and (
51
), we conclude that

|u-x_{n}|\leq\epsilon(\overline{\eta})\leq\epsilon_{0},\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{2}.

Now, Corollary
3.3
implies that
u\in C^{1,\eta}\left(\overline{\Omega_{u}^{+}\cap B_{1}}\right)
and that
\Gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{TP}}(u)\cap B_{1}
is a
C^{1,\eta}
graph with small norm.

Since
1-\partial_{n}u
satisfies the uniform elliptic equation (
44
), we are able to apply the Harncak inequality for
1-\partial_{n}u\geq 0
in
\{x_{n}>\epsilon_{0}\}
, and noting that
(1-\partial_{n}u)(\textbf{e}_{n})=\overline{\eta}
, gives that

1-\partial_{n}u\geq c\overline{\eta},\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\frac{\textbf{e}_{n}}{2}\right)\subset\Omega_{u}^{+}\cap B_{1}.

In
\left(\Omega_{u}^{+}\cap B_{1}\right)\setminus B_{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\frac{\textbf{e}_{n}}{2}\right)
, we compare

with a suitable barrier function

, which is a solution to (

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}w=0,&\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega_{u}^{+}\cap B_{1},\\
w=c\overline{\eta},&\text{on}\quad\partial B_{\frac{1}{4}}\left(\frac{\textbf{e}_{n}}{2}\right),\\
w=0,&\text{on}\quad\partial B_{1}\cap\Omega_{u}^{+}.\end{array}\right.

Using the Hopf lemma in
C^{1,\eta}
domains, together with
C^{1,\eta}
estimates up to the boundary, we conclude that
w
grows linearly away from
\partial\Omega_{u}^{+}\cap B_{1}
; hence

\partial_{n}u(t\textbf{e}_{n})\leq 1-c\overline{\eta}t,\qquad\forall t\in\left[0,\frac{1}{4}\right],

with
c
independent of
u
. Integrating in the
\textbf{e}_{n}
-direction, and using that
u(0)=0
and
c,\overline{\eta}
are universal, we find

(52)

u(t\textbf{e}_{n})\leq t-c_{1}t^{2},\qquad\forall t\in\left[0,\frac{1}{4}\right],

for some small universal constant
c_{1}>0
.

We now apply Proposition
4.1
and conclude that either alternative
(i)
or
(ii)
is satisfied. However, if
\epsilon^{\prime\prime}
is small enough, then alternative
(i)
cannot hold; otherwise, by (
49
)

1-c_{0}\epsilon_{0}\geq|\nabla u^{+}(0)|\geq\partial_{n}u^{+}(0)\geq 1-\epsilon^{\prime\prime},

which leads to a contradiction.
Thus
(ii)
holds and we can rescale and iterate one more time; see Remark
4.2
.
Similarly, after applying the conclusion of Proposition
4.1
for
m
-times, we obtain that if
\epsilon^{\prime\prime}
is sufficiently small depending on
m
and
\epsilon_{0}
,
c_{0}
,
r_{0}
, then only alternative
(ii)
can hold for the
m
iterations and conclude that

(53)

|u-P_{M,\nu}|\leq\epsilon_{0}r^{2+\alpha_{0}},\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{r},\quad r=r_{0}^{m},

with

(54)

\left|\frac{\nabla u^{+}(0)}{|\nabla u^{+}(0)|}-\nu\right|\leq C\epsilon_{0}r^{1+\alpha_{0}},\qquad\text{and}\qquad M\nu=0.

Here we have also used Corollary
3.3
. Now, notice that

1-\epsilon^{\prime\prime}\leq\partial_{n}u^{+}(0)=\textbf{e}_{n}\cdot\nabla u^{+}(0)\leq|\nabla u^{+}(0)|\leq 1,

which implies

\left|\frac{\nabla u^{+}(0)}{|\nabla u^{+}(0)|}-\textbf{e}_{n}\right|\leq(2\epsilon^{\prime\prime})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Thus, if
\epsilon^{\prime\prime}
is small enough depending on
r
, the inequality above together with (
54
) gives that

|\nu-\textbf{e}_{n}|\leq 2C\epsilon_{0}r^{1+\alpha_{0}}.

Thus, since
M\nu=0
and
\|M\|\leq 1
,

\left|P_{M,\nu}\left(\frac{r}{2}\textbf{e}_{n}\right)-\frac{r}{2}\right|=\left|\frac{r}{2}\textbf{e}_{n}\cdot(\nu-\textbf{e}_{n})+\frac{r^{2}}{8}(\textbf{e}_{n}-\nu)^{T}M(\textbf{e}_{n}-\nu)\right|\leq C^{\prime}r^{2+\alpha_{0}},

which combined with (
53
) gives that

\left|u\left(\frac{r}{2}\textbf{e}_{n}\right)-\frac{r}{2}\right|\leq 2C^{\prime}r^{2+\alpha_{0}}.

This contradicts (
52
) for
t=\frac{r}{2}
, as long as
r
is small enough universal, i.e.
m
is large enough, and
\epsilon^{\prime\prime}
is small enough.
This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

The main consequence of Theorem
1.1
is the regularity of the free boundary at regular points, i.e. Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Consider a regular two-phase point of the free boundary.
Then, the Lipschitz regularity of the solution, i.e Theorem
2.3
, and the nondegeneracy property, i.e. Theorem
2.2
, proves that any blow-ups at this point will be a Lipschitz global solution with
0\in\Gamma^{\mathrm{int}}_{\mathrm{TP}}(u)
.
According to Theorem
1.1
, we know that this global solution is a two-plane solution, and we get that
the (initial) flatness assumption in
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
is satisfied.
Therefore, by
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
, the boundaries
\partial\Omega_{u}^{\pm}
are
C^{1,\eta}
smooth in a neighborhood of the regular two-phase point, for any
\eta\in(0,\frac{1}{3})
.
∎

Appendix A
A Harnack inequality

In the following lemma, for the convenience of the readers, we prove a Harnack inequality for an auxiliary problem of the
p
-Laplacian type.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
1<p<\infty
, and
\Omega
be a smooth bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose
x_{0}\in\Omega
, and
0<R\leq 1
such that
\overline{B_{4R}(x_{0})}\subset\Omega
.
Let
h\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
be a non-negative solution to

(55)

\mathrm{div}\left(\left|\nabla h(x)+Fx+E\right|^{p-2}\left(\nabla h(x)+Fx+E\right)\right)=0,\qquad\text{in}\quad\Omega,

with
F
being an
n\times n
symmetric matrix, and
E\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then, there exists
C
depending only on
n
,
p
,
F
,
E
, and
\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{4R}(x_{0}))}
such that

\sup_{B_{R}(x_{0})}h\leq C\left(\inf_{B_{R}(x_{0})}h+R\right).

Proof.

Let
A:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be defined as

A(x,\xi)=\left|\xi+Fx+E\right|^{p-2}\left(\xi+Fx+E\right).

Then, (
55
) can be written in the form

\mathrm{div\,}A(x,\nabla u)=0,\qquad\text{in}\quad\Omega.

We first observe that, for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\left|A(x,\xi)\right|=\left|\xi+Fx+E\right|^{p-1}\leq C_{1}\left(\left|\xi\right|^{p-1}+\max_{x\in\Omega}\left|Fx+E\right|^{p-1}\right),

where
C_{1}
depends only on
p
. On the other hand, for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

(56)

\displaystyle\xi^{T}A(x,\xi)

\displaystyle=\left|\xi+Fx+E\right|^{p-2}\xi^{T}\left(\xi+Fx+E\right)

\displaystyle=\left|\xi+Fx+E\right|^{p}-\left|\xi+Fx+E\right|^{p-2}\left(Fx+E\right)^{T}\left(\xi+Fx+E\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left|\xi+Fx+E\right|^{p}-\left(\max_{x\in\Omega}\left|Fx+E\right|\right)\left|\xi+Fx+E\right|^{p-1}.

Now, if
|\xi+Fx+E|\leq 2\max_{x\in\Omega}\left|Fx+E\right|
, we get from (
56
)

(57)

\displaystyle\xi^{T}A(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\geq\left|\xi+Fx+E\right|^{p}-2^{p-1}\left(\max_{x\in\Omega}\left|Fx+E\right|\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}\left|\xi\right|^{p}-C_{2}\max_{x\in\Omega}\left|Fx+E\right|^{p}-2^{p-1}\max_{x\in\Omega}\left|Fx+E\right|^{p},

where
C_{2}
depends only on
p
.

On the other hand, if
|\xi+Fx+E|>2\max_{x\in\Omega}\left|Fx+E\right|
, we obtain from (
56
)

(58)

\displaystyle\xi^{T}A(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\geq\left|\xi+Fx+E\right|^{p}-\left(\max_{x\in\Omega}\left|Fx+E\right|\right)\left|\xi+Fx+E\right|^{p-1}

\displaystyle=\left|\xi+Fx+E\right|^{p}\left(1-\left(\max_{x\in\Omega}\left|Fx+E\right|\right)|\xi+Fx+E|^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left|\xi+Fx+E\right|^{p}\geq C_{3}\left|\xi\right|^{p}-C_{3}\max_{x\in\Omega}\left|Fx+E\right|^{p},

where
C_{3}
depends only on
p
. Then, from (
57
) and (
58
), we deduce

\xi^{T}A(x,\xi)\geq C_{4}|\xi|^{p}-C_{5},

where
C_{4}
depends only on
p
, and
C_{5}
depends on
F
,
E
, and
p
. Now, the result follows from
[
32
, Theorem 1.1]
.
∎

Appendix B
A Liouville type result for the linearized operator

In the following lemma, we present a Liouville type result for the global solutions of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{p}
, introduced in (
22
).

Lemma B.1
.

Let
U
be a global solution to

(59)

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{p}(U)=0,&\qquad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{n}\cap\{x_{n}\neq 0\},\\
\partial_{n}^{+}U=g\partial_{n}^{-}U,&\qquad\text{on}\quad\{x_{n}=0\},\end{cases}

with
0<b_{0}\leq g\leq b_{1}
and

(60)

\partial_{n}U\leq 0,\qquad|U(x)|\leq C|x|^{2},

for some constant
C
. Then,
U=Q(x^{\prime})
where
Q(x^{\prime})
is a pure quadratic polynomial in the
x^{\prime}
-direction such that
\mathcal{L}_{p}(Q)=\Delta Q=0
.

Proof.

Let

U_{k}(x):=\frac{U(r_{k}x)}{r_{k}^{2}},\qquad\text{with}\quad r_{k}\to\infty,

be a sequence of blow-downs that converges uniformly on compact sets to another global solution
\overline{U}
to (
59
) with (
60
).

Let
\tau
be a unit tangential direction, i.e.
\tau\perp\textbf{e}_{n}
, and denote

\gamma=\gamma(\tau):=\sup_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{x_{n}=0\}}\partial_{\tau\tau}\overline{U}.

Notice that
\gamma\neq\infty
.
Indeed, by a similar argument of the Step 1 in the proof of Proposition
4.3
, we obtain that
\left(U_{k}\right)^{h}_{\tau\tau}
is bounded above in
B_{1}
independently of
h
, and this implies that
\gamma
is well-defined.

We claim that

(61)

\partial_{\tau\tau}\overline{U}\equiv\gamma,\qquad\text{on}\quad\{x_{n}\neq 0\}.

Let
x_{0}
be a point, say for simplicity in
B_{1}\setminus\{x_{n}=0\}
, and let us show that

\partial_{\tau\tau}\overline{U}(x_{0})=\gamma.

Since
U_{k}\to\overline{U}
in
C^{2,\alpha}
in a small ball
B\subset\{x_{n}\neq 0\}
around
x_{0}
, clearly,

\partial_{\tau\tau}\overline{U}(x_{0})\leq\gamma.

Assume by contradiction that the inequality above is strict. Then, for all
k
large,

\partial_{\tau\tau}(U_{k})(x_{0})\leq\gamma-\delta,

for some small
\delta>0
. By
C^{2,\alpha}
regularity,

\partial_{\tau\tau}(U_{k})\leq\gamma-\frac{\delta}{2},\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{c}(x_{0})\subset B.

Hence, for all
h>0
small,

(U^{h}_{k})_{\tau\tau}\leq\gamma-\frac{\delta}{2},\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{c^{\prime}}(x_{0}).

On the other hand, from the definition of
\gamma
, we have

(U^{h}_{k})_{\tau\tau}\leq\gamma,\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{\frac{3}{4}}.

Since
(U^{h}_{k})_{\tau\tau}
is a solution of (
59
), we can construct an explicit upper barrier in
B_{\frac{3}{4}}\setminus B_{c^{\prime}}(x_{0})
, and conclude that

(U^{h}_{k})_{\tau\tau}\leq\gamma-c(\delta),\qquad\text{in}\quad B_{\frac{1}{2}},

for all
k
large. This contradicts the definition of
\gamma
, and the claim (
61
) is proved.

Next, we show that
\overline{U}
is equal to a quadratic polynomial
Q_{+}
(resp.
Q_{-}
) in
\{x_{n}>0\}
(resp.
\{x_{n}<0\}
) and

(62)

Q_{+}=Q_{-}\qquad\text{on}\quad\{x_{n}=0\},\qquad Q_{\pm}(0)=0,\qquad\nabla Q_{\pm}(0)=0,\qquad\partial_{n}Q_{\pm}\leq 0.

We know that

\partial_{\tau\tau}\overline{U}=\mathrm{const},\qquad\forall\tau\perp\textbf{e}_{n};

hence

\overline{U}(x)=b(x_{n})+x^{\prime}\cdot a(x_{n})+Q(x^{\prime}),\qquad a(x_{n})=(a_{1}(x_{n}),\cdots,a_{n-1}(x_{n})),

for some quadratic polynomial
Q
and functions
b
and
a_{i}
depending on one variable.

Since
\overline{U}
satisfies (
59
), so does
\partial_{i}\overline{U}=a_{i}(x_{n})+\partial_{i}Q(x^{\prime})
for
i<n
. On the other hand,
\partial_{i}Q
is a linear function; hence
\mathcal{L}_{p}(a_{i})=0
.
This means that
a_{i}
is linear in the set
\{x_{n}>0\}
.
Now, we use
\mathcal{L}_{p}(\partial_{n}\overline{U})=0
, and we argue as above to find that
b^{\prime}
is linear in
\{x_{n}>0\}
, and our claim (
62
) is proved.

Invoking condition
\partial_{n}Q_{\pm}\leq 0
, and
Q_{+}=Q_{-}
on
\{x_{n}=0\}
, we have

\partial_{ni}Q_{\pm}=0,\qquad\text{for}\quad i\neq n,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\partial_{ij}Q_{+}=\partial_{ij}Q_{-},\qquad\text{for}\quad i,j\neq n.

Finally, since
Q_{\pm}
both solve
\mathcal{L}_{p}(Q_{\pm})=0
,
from either side of
\{x_{n}\neq 0\}
, we conclude that

\partial_{nn}Q_{+}=\partial_{nn}Q_{-},

and therefore
Q_{+}\equiv Q_{-}
.
Let us call this common polynomial
Q
. In particular, since
\partial_{n}Q\leq 0
, it follows that
\partial_{n}Q\equiv 0
. Thus,
Q
is a pure quadratic polynomial in the
x^{\prime}
-direction.

Finally, we need to show that
U\equiv Q
.
For all tangential directions
\tau
, we have

\sup\partial_{\tau\tau}U=\partial_{\tau\tau}Q.

Thus,
U-Q
is concave in the
x^{\prime}
-direction. Moreover

(63)

(U-Q)(0)=0,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nabla(U-Q)(0)=0.

Hence,

U(x^{\prime},0)\leq Q(x^{\prime}),

and by the monotonicity of
U
, we get

U(x^{\prime},x_{n})\leq Q(x^{\prime}),\qquad\text{on}\quad\{x_{n}\geq 0\}.

As both
U
and
Q
solve (
59
), we can use (
63
) and Hopf lemma to conclude that
U=Q
, in
\{x_{n}\geq 0\}
.
By the monotonicity of
U
, we have
U\geq Q
, in the set
\{x_{n}\leq 0\}
; hence
U=Q
, again by the Hopf lemma.
∎

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Göttsche’s Formula on the Orbifold
[A/G]

In one of the seminal results of modern algebraic geometry, Göttsche computed the Betti numbers of the Hilbert scheme of points
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(S)
where
S
is a smooth quasi-projective surface
[
Gö90
]
. When
S
is a
K3
surface, he found the remarkable formula

(1)

\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}e(\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(S))q^{d-1}=q^{-1}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})^{-24}.

The reciprocal of the righthand side equals the modular cusp form
\Delta(q)=\eta(q)^{24}
of weight
12
.

However, in the case of an Abelian surface
A
, Göttsche’s formula is trivial since
e(\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(A))=0
for
d>0
. One can instead consider Abelian surfaces together with an action by a finite group. By studying the invariant loci—or equivalently, working on the orbifold—we will find a family of results analogous to (
1
).

Consider a complex Abelian surface
A
, and a finite group
G
acting on
A
preserving the holomorphic symplectic form. We will call such an action
symplectic
. The natural generating function produced from this data is

(2)

Z_{A,G}(q)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}e(\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(A)^{G})q^{d}

where
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(A)^{G}
is the
G
-invariant Hilbert scheme, parameterizing finite
G
-invariant subschemes of length
d
. It is equivalently the fixed locus of the induced
G
action on
\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(A)
. The
G
-invariant Hilbert scheme is disconnected, though each component is a smooth projective holomorphic symplectic variety of
K3
-type.

By definition of the orbifold
[A/G]
, we have

\operatorname{Hilb}(A)^{G}=\operatorname{Hilb}([A/G]).

So we regard
Z_{A,G}(q)
as analogous to the lefthand side of (
1
) for
[A/G]
. Our following result can be understood as the analogue of Göttsche’s formula for the orbifold
[A/G]
(see Appendix
A

Theorem 1.1
.

The function
Z_{A,G}^{-1}(q)
is a modular form of weight
\frac{1}{2}e(A/G)
for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{0}(|G|)

is an explicit eta product (see Table
1

Our proof of Theorem

[

in the case of

1.2.
Refinement to
\chi_{y}
-genus

We can refine our formulas by replacing the Euler characteristic with a more elaborate index. For our purposes, we will focus on the (normalized)
\chi_{y}
-genus, which for a compact complex manifold
M
is defined in terms of the Hodge numbers as

(3)

\begin{split}\overline{\chi}_{y}(M)&=(-y)^{-\frac{1}{2}\dim(M)}\chi_{y}(M)\\
&=(-y)^{-\frac{1}{2}\dim(M)}\sum_{p,q}(-1)^{p}h^{p,q}(M)y^{q}.\end{split}

Notice that setting
y=-1
recovers the Euler characteristic,
\overline{\chi}_{-1}(M)=e(M)
.

Our formulas will involve the function

(4)

\phi_{-2,1}(q,y)=\big(y^{\frac{1}{2}}-y^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{2}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-yq^{n})^{2}(1-y^{-1}q^{n})^{2}}{(1-q^{n})^{4}}

which is the unique weak Jacobi form of weight
-2
and index
1

[
EZ85
, Thm. 9.3]
. We define the generating function

Z^{\overline{\chi}}_{A,G}(q,y)=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}\overline{\chi}_{y}(\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(A)^{G})q^{d}.

Proposition 1.4
.

For all non-trivial translation-free symplectic actions we have

Z^{\overline{\chi}}_{A,G}(q,y)=-\big(y^{\frac{1}{2}}+y^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{2}\frac{Z_{A,G}(q)}{\phi_{-2,1}(q^{|G|},-y)}.

Following
[
BG20
]
, one can give similar formulas for the elliptic genus, the motivic class, and more generally, the birationality class, but we will not need those here.

1.3.
Enumerative Geometry of the Orbifold Kummer Surface

The Katz-Klemm-Vafa (KKV) formula
[
KKV99
]
was predicted by string theorists to compute the BPS states of D-branes moving in a
K3

S
.

In its modern mathematical formulation, the Maulik-Toda proposal
[

\mathsf{n}_{\beta}^{K3}(g)

&=\frac{1}{q}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1-q^{n})^{20}(1+yq^{n})^{2}(1+y^{-1}q^{n})^{2}}.\end{split}

The coefficient of

and virtual counts of curves on a

the origin. The group of biholomorphisms from
X
to itself is denoted
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
, while the subgroup of linear maps (automorphisms of
X
as a complex Lie group) is denoted
\operatorname{Aut_{0}}(X)\subset\operatorname{Aut}(X)
. Given any
x\in X
, let
t_{x}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\to X
be the biholomorphism translating by
x
.

Given any holomorphic map
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X\to X^{\prime}
between complex tori, using that
f
is equivalent to a map between the corresponding universal covers, one can show that
h\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=t_{-f(0)}\circ f
is linear. Therefore, holomorphic maps between complex tori can be uniquely factored as a linear map composed with a translation

f=t_{f(0)}\circ h.

This factorization induces a canonical surjective group homomorphism

\sigma\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Aut}(X)\to\operatorname{Aut_{0}}(X)

mapping

to
h
, which restricts to the identity on
\operatorname{Aut_{0}}(X)\subset\operatorname{Aut}(X)
and whose kernel is the subgroup of all translations of

. This proves the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

The biholomorphism group of a complex torus
X
decomposes as
\operatorname{Aut}(X)=\operatorname{Aut_{0}}(X)\rtimes X
, where
X
is identified with the subgroup of translations.

Given a subgroup
G\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}(X)
, we get an action of
G
on
X
by biholomorphisms. We will consider actions up to the following equivalence condition.

Definition 2.2
.

Consider pairs
(X_{i},G_{i})
for
i=1,2
with
G_{i}\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}(X_{i})
. We say the two pairs are
equivalent
if there exists a biholomorphism
w\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}X_{1}\to X_{2}
such that
G_{2}=wG_{1}w^{-1}
in
\operatorname{Aut}(X_{2})
.

We specialize to the case where
X
is a 2-dimensional complex torus. A non-zero class
\alpha\in H^{2,0}(X)
is called a
holomorphic symplectic form
. Since
h^{2,0}(X)=1
, a holomorphic symplectic form is unique up to scale.

Definition 2.3
.

An automorphism
f\in\operatorname{Aut}(X)
is holomorphic symplectic, or just
symplectic
, if
f
preserves a holomorphic symplectic form
\alpha
. That is, if
f^{*}\alpha=\alpha
. An action by a group
G
on
X
is
symplectic
if each element of
G
defines a symplectic automorphism.

Lemma 2.4
.

A group
G
with a symplectic action on
X
can be written uniquely as the (possibly non-split) extension

(13)

0\to T\to G\to\sigma(G)\to 0

where
T\subseteq G
is the subgroup of all elements acting by translation, and the induced action by
\sigma(G)
is symplectic and linear.

Proof

The existence of the short exact sequence is clear from the definition of
\sigma
. Given
f\in G
we can write
f=t_{f(0)}\circ h
, and then
\sigma(f)=h
, which acts linearly. The kernel is precisely elements of
G
acting by translation. Since the symplectic form can be taken to be constant—induced from
dz_{1}dz_{2}
on the universal cover—it is clearly invariant under translations. Therefore,
h=t_{-f(0)}\circ f
is symplectic.
∎

This proof illustrates the obstruction to the splitting of the extension. We have the unique factorization
f=t_{f(0)}\circ h
, but notice
t_{f(0)}
might not be an element of
G
. The extension splits if and only if
t_{f(0)}\in G
for all
f\in G
.

When
T
is trivial, we say the action is
translation-free
. Note that a translation-free action is not necessarily linear: if
T
is trivial,
G
and
\sigma(G)
are abstractly isomorphic, but may act differently on
X
. We say that a translation-free action by
G
is
maximal
if there does not exist a translation-free group
H\subset\operatorname{Aut}(X)
with
G\subsetneq H
such that the
G
action is the restriction of the
H
action.

Proof of Proposition

\begin{split}Z_{A,G}(q)=\sum_{d=0}^{\infty}e(\operatorname{Hilb}^{d}(A)^{G})q^{d}&=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}e(\operatorname{Hilb}^{m|T|}(A)^{G})q^{m|T|}\\

∎

Lemma 4.8

Appendix A
Modular Forms and Eta Products

This appendix will be devoted to giving a brief overview of the modular objects relevant to our results. An excellent reference is Chapters 1 and 2 of
[
Kö11
]
. We are interested in modular forms of integral or half-integral weight with multiplier system for the congruence subgroup

\Gamma_{0}(N)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigg\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\,\bigg|\,c\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N)\bigg\}\subset\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

for an integer
N\geqslant 1
. A multiplier system on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
is a function
v\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\Gamma_{0}(N)\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
satisfying some consistency conditions. We will not need the details, so we refer the reader to
[
Iwa97

.

Let
\mathfrak{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\,|\,\operatorname{Im}(z)>0\}
be the upper-half plane in
\mathbb{C}
.

Definition A.1
.

A holomorphic function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathfrak{H}\to\mathbb{C}
is called a
modular form
of weight
k\in\mathbb{R}
and multiplier system
v
on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
if

is holomorphic at all cusps
\mathbb{Q}\cup\{\infty\}
, and if for all
L\in\Gamma_{0}(N)
,

transforms as

(28)

f(L\tau)=f\bigg(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\bigg)=v(L)(c\tau+d)^{k}f(\tau),\,\,\,\,\,\,\,L=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}.

We call
f
a

if additionally,
f
vanishes at all cusps.

We employ the change of variables
q=\exp(2\pi i\tau)
and with it, the abuse of notation writing
f(\tau)
and
f(q)
interchangeably. The fundamental building block of a large class of modular forms is the Dedekind eta function (or just eta function)

\eta(q)=q^{\frac{1}{24}}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n}),

which is a modular form of weight
\frac{1}{2}
and multiplier system
v_{\eta}
on
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
; see
[
Iwa97
, Sec. 2.8]
where

is given explicitly.
9
9

9

Equivalently,
\eta(q)
is a modular form of weight
\frac{1}{2}
on the metaplectic double cover

of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Definition A.2
.

An
eta product
of level
N\geqslant 1
is a function
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathfrak{H}\to\mathbb{C}
of the form

f(q)=\prod_{m|N}\eta(q^{m})^{a_{m}}

such that

(possibly negative, or zero) for all

, and where the product is over positive divisors of
N
.

From the modular properties of the Dedekind eta function, one can show that an eta product
f
of level
N
transforms as a modular form on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
of weight

k=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m|N}a_{m}\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z},

and with multiplier system

v_{f}(L)=\prod_{m|N}\bigg(v_{\eta}\begin{pmatrix}a&mb\\
c/m&d\end{pmatrix}\bigg)^{a_{m}},\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,L=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}

When we say “transforms as” we mean that
f
satisfies (

) for all

. An eta product

. This is because the form of
\eta(q)
indicates that any poles of

must occur at
q=0
or
|q|=1
. All that is left to consider is when an eta product is holomorphic at the cusps. The following proposition gives necessary and sufficient conditions.

Proposition A.3

(
[
Kö11
, Cor. 2.3]
)
.

An eta product
f
of level
N
is holomorphic at the cusps if and only if the following holds for all positive divisors

\sum_{m|N}\frac{(\gcd(c,m))^{2})}{m}a_{m}\geqslant 0.

Moreover,
f
vanishes at all cusps if and only if each inequality is strict. An eta product is therefore a modular form of weight
k
for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
if and only if each inequality is satisfied, and it is a cusp form if and only if each is strictly satisfied.

A new approach to topological singularities via a weak notion of Jacobian for functions of bounded variation
L. De Luca, R. Scala, N. Van Goethem

Abstract
. We introduce a weak notion of 2 × 2 2\times 2 -minors of gradients of a suitable subclass of B ​ V BV functions. In the case of maps in B ​ V ​ ( ℝ 2 , ℝ 2 ) BV(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2}) such a notion extends the standard definition of Jacobian determinant to non-Sobolev maps. We use this distributional Jacobian to prove a compactness and Γ \Gamma -convergence result for a new model describing the emergence of topological singularities in two dimensions, in the spirit of Ginzburg-Landau and core-radius approaches. Within our framework, the order parameter is an S ​ B ​ V SBV map u u taking values in 𝕊 1 \mathbb{S}^{1} and the energy is made by the sum of the squared L 2 L^{2} norm of ∇ u \nabla u and of the length of (the closure of) the jump set of u u multiplied by 1 ε \frac{1}{\varepsilon} . Here, ε \varepsilon is a length-scale parameter. We show that, in the | log ⁡ ε | |\log\varepsilon| regime, the Jacobian distributions converge, as ε → 0 + \varepsilon\to 0^{+} , to a finite sum μ \mu of Dirac deltas with weights multiple of π \pi , and that the corresponding effective energy is given by the total variation of μ \mu . Keywords : Functions of Bounded Variation; Strict Convergence; Jacobian determinant; Topological Singularities; Γ \Gamma -convergence; Ginzburg-Landau Model; Core-Radius Approach. Introduction

Topological singularities are ubiquitous in Physics and Materials Science: Vortices in superconductivity and superfluidity and (screw end edge) dislocations in single crystal plasticity are two paradigmatic examples
[
10
,
33
,
35
,
40
,
41
,
42
]
.
Loosely speaking, dislocations (resp., vortices) are identified as points around which the deformation gradient (resp., the order parameter) has non trivial circulation.
In view of their central role in the theory of phase transitions, topological singularities have been the object of an extensive and intensive study in the last decades (see e.g.
[
12
,
49
]
).
Such an analysis focused basically on two main models: The core-radius approach (CR) and the Ginzburg-Landau model (GL).
In the former, a finite distribution of topological singularities is identified by a sum of Dirac deltas (with integer weights) and the energy is assumed to be purely elastic “far enough” from such singularities. Hence, the
core parameter

\varepsilon
is introduced, and the energy takes the form

(0.1)

\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{CR}}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu)}|\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+|\mu|(\Omega)\,.

In (
0.1
),
\mu
represents the distribution of singularities,
\Omega
is the reference domain, and
\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu):=\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{\xi\in\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}\overline{B}_{\varepsilon}(\xi)
is the domain deprived of the cores; moreover,
u\in H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu);\mathbb{S}^{1})
represents the order parameter that should be “compatible” with the measure
\mu
, namely, it should satisfy
\deg(u,\partial A)=\mu(A)
for every smooth domain
A\subset\Omega
with
\partial A\subset\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu)
.
(CR) is mainly used to describe dislocations in semi-discrete models, i.e., models in which “far from the singularities” (in
\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu)
) the material is assumed to be continuous and elastic, whereas “close to the singularities” (in
\bigcup_{\xi\in\mathrm{supp}\,\mu}B_{\varepsilon}(\xi)
) the material resembles its “discrete” nature and the energy behaves like the cardinality of the atoms in the core. Here, (up to a scaling)
\varepsilon
represents an integer multiple of the lattice spacing

For screw dislocations in antiplane elasticity it is well known that the displacement is a multiple of the purely axial Burgers vector times a phase (see, e.g.
[
53
]
). Let us denote by
w
a suitable normalization of this vertical displacement and introduce the order parameter
u:=e^{2\pi\imath w}
. Then, the first addendum in the right-hand side of (
0.1
) is nothing but a suitable normalization of the stored elastic energy outside the cores.
As for the “plastic term”
|\mu|(\Omega)
, it does not play any role in the asymptotic analysis as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
and serves only to guarantee compactness; indeed, without such a term a distribution
\mu
with
\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu)=\varnothing
would pay zero energy.

We notice that the scalar field
w
cannot belong to
H^{1}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}(\mu))
, since its deformation gradient has a nontrivial circulation condition on a curve enclosing a singularity located at
x^{0}
; from a modeling point of view, the function
w
must be in
SBV^{2}(\Omega)
, hence showing a nonzero jump on some one-dimensional set
S_{w}
which has
x^{0}
as one of its endpoints . This jump set represents the (section of) a slip plane, while its amplitude
[w]
corresponds to the circulation of the absolutely continuous gradient
\nabla w
, which is quantized (i.e., it is integer valued), and represents the mismatch between the atomic lattices above and below
S_{w}
.

The most celebrated model used to describe topological singularities is, of course, (GL). In such a case, the energy depends only on the order parameter
u\in H^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
which is “penalized” (instead of “constrained”) to take values in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
and which is defined on the whole
\Omega
. The most basic form of the energy is given by

(0.2)

\mathcal{E}^{\mathrm{GL}}_{\varepsilon}(u):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\big(1-|u|^{2}\big)^{2}\,\;\mathrm{d}x\,,

where the parameter
\varepsilon
is called in this case
coherence length
.
Note that the role of the measure
\mu
in (CR) is actually played in the (GL) model
by
Ju=\det\nabla u
which is here a diffuse measure.

Let us focus on how the models (CR) and (GL) are able to detect the topological singularities.
Loosely speaking,
a prototypical topological singularity of degree
z\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
at a point
x^{0}\in\Omega
can be thought of as the
point singularity of a vectorial order parameter
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
which, in a ball
B_{\delta}(x^{0})
with
\varepsilon\ll\delta\ll 1
, winds around the center as
\big(\frac{x-x^{0}}{|x-x^{0}|}\big)^{z}
. The energy of
\bar{u}_{\varepsilon}
diverges at order
|\log\varepsilon|
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
. As a consequence, to detect the effective
energy cost of finitely many vortex singularities, one needs to study the (CR) and (GL) energy functionals
at a logarithmic scaling.
It has been proved (see e.g.
[
1
,
38
]
and references therin) that a sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
, along which these
energy functionals are equi-bounded, has Jacobians
Ju_{\varepsilon}
that, up to a subsequence,
converge in the flat sense (see Section
1
) to an atomic measure
\pi\mu=\pi\sum_{i=1}^{I}z^{i}\delta_{x^{i}}
(here the
x^{i}
represent the positions of the limiting vortices and
z^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
are their multiplicities).
The
\Gamma
-limit of
\frac{\mathcal{E}_{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{GL}}}{|\log\varepsilon|}

with respect to this convergence is then given by
\pi|\mu|
. The same analysis has been developed within the (CR) approach in
[
47
]
;
moreover, in
[
5
]
it is proven that the two models are
asymptotically equivalent (in terms of
\Gamma
-convergence)
at energy regime
|\log\varepsilon|^{p}
with
p\geq 1
.
Variants of the models (CR) and (GL) have been intensively studied; we recall, for instance, discrete linear models for screw dislocations and vortices
[
3
,
47
,
6
,
23
]
(see also
[
2

4

for the case of periodic inhomogeneous materials) and semidiscrete and discrete models for edge dislocations
[
46
,
29

24
,

]
.

In this paper, we are interested in a generalization of the models (CR) and (GL) from a different perspective; specifically, we aim at providing a meaningful energy functional which depends on order parameter
u
defined on the whole domain
\Omega
, taking values in
\mathbb{S}^{1}
and whose approximate gradient is square-integrable in
\Omega
.
From the point of view of screw dislocations, this means that the jump
[w]
of the scalar displacement
w
cannot

S_{w}

[w]
of the displacement should exhibit a transition between integers in a “small” subset
\Sigma_{w}
of
S_{w}
, close to the singularity.
In our model this transition has a length of the order
\varepsilon
and as we let
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
the displacement jump is increasingly “forced” to take integer values everywhere on
S_{w}
;
in other words the total length of
\Sigma_{w}
vanishes asymptotically as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, giving rise to isolated atomic singularities. These considerations lead us to consider the following energy

(0.3)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}(w):=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla w|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{S_{w}}W([w])\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\,,

where the first term is the standard antiplane elastic energy associated to the displacement
w
, whereas the second one is a multi-well potential which is null on
\mathbb{Z}
and positive elsewhere. As for instance, one can take the potential

W(t):=\textrm{dist}^{\alpha}(t,\mathbb{Z})\,,\qquad\qquad\textrm{ for some parameter }\alpha\geq 0\,,

and hence, passing to
u:=e^{2\pi\imath w}
, one arrives at

(0.4)

W([w]):=|[u]|^{\alpha}\,.

For fixed
\varepsilon
, minimizing (
0.3
) involves a competition between the stored-elastic energy (first term in (
0.3
)) which might be large (if for instance boundary conditions for
u
are given on
\partial\Omega
) and the creation of a integer displacement mismatch within the otherwise regular crystal lattice (which has an energetical cost given by the second term in (
0.3
)).

Notice that, although potentials as in (
0.4
) are largely used in the context of crack mechanics
[
14
]
, with the jump term meaning the energetic cost of crack creation or propagation,
the role of
W
in our case is a bit different.
Indeed, here,
W
penalizes the “wrong” mismatch of atomic lattices through the interface
S_{w}
. Therefore, passing to
u:=e^{2\pi\imath w}
,
the functional
\widehat{\mathcal{G}}_{\varepsilon}
in (
0.3
) takes the form

(0.5)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\int_{S_{u}}|[u]|^{\alpha}\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\,.

Clearly, in order to describe the presence of topological singularities, the functional
\mathcal{G}_{\varepsilon}
cannot be restricted to Sobolev functions, but should be defined on the set of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-valued
SBV^{2}
maps .

In this paper we focus on the case
\alpha=0
and define

(0.6)

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}|\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\overline{S}_{u})\,.

We point out that, at least under some geometric assumptions on
\Omega
and prescribing the set
S_{u}
accordingly

e.g. when
\Omega
is a square,
S_{u}
the horizontal axis of
\Omega
, and prescribing periodic boundary conditions of
u
.
, one can relate the elastic energy to the
H^{\frac{1}{2}}
-norm of the jump of
u
, whence leading to 1d models which have been already investigated (see
[
28
]
; related models for different lattice mismatches can be found in
[

and references therein). Related to this setting is
[

Therefore we aim at studying the asymptotic behavior of
\frac{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}}{|\log\varepsilon|}
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
To this purpose, we first need to introduce a suitable notion of Jacobian for
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-valued
SBV^{2}
maps. This task is accomplished in Section
2
, where we introduce an extension of the Jacobian determinant for maps in
BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
(see
[
17
,
18
,
13
]
and the references therein for fine properties of the Jacobian determinant in Sobolev spaces). For a map
u:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the distributional Jacobian determinant
\textrm{Det}(\nabla u)
, introduced in
[
43
]
, is defined as the distribution

(0.7)

\displaystyle\langle\textrm{Det}(\nabla u),\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}:=\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi\cdot\lambda_{u}\;\mathrm{d}x,

where

(0.8)

\displaystyle\lambda_{u}:=\frac{1}{2}\big(-u^{1}\frac{\partial u^{2}}{\partial x_{2}}+u^{2}\frac{\partial u^{1}}{\partial x_{2}},u^{1}\frac{\partial u^{2}}{\partial x_{1}}-u^{2}\frac{\partial u^{1}}{\partial x_{1}}\big);

it is well-defined under suitable summability assumptions on
u
ensuring that

\lambda_{u}\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Moreover, denoting by
j(u)
the
current
associated to
u
, i.e.,
j(u):=\frac{1}{2}(u^{1}\nabla u^{2}-u^{2}\nabla u^{1})
, one has
j(u)^{\perp}=\lambda_{u}
and it is easy to check that

\mathrm{Det}(\nabla u)=-\mathrm{Div}\lambda_{u}=\mathrm{curl}j(u)={\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{curl}(\nabla w)},

holds in the sense of distributions, and where
\nabla w
is the approximate gradient of
w
.
In general
\textrm{Det}(\nabla u)
does not coincide with the pointwise Jacobian determinant
\textrm{det}(\nabla u)
, but they coincide under suitable assumptions on
u
(see
[
45
]
). When
u\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
it is possible to extend definition (
0.7
) if one shows that
\lambda_{u}
is a Radon measure uniquely determined by
u
. Such a fact is trivial for smooth maps.
Hence, arguing by approximation, it is possible to show that if
\{v_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of smooth functions approaching
u
w.r.t. the strict topology of
BV
, then the distribution
\lambda_{u}
is uniquely determined as the limit of the distributions
\lambda_{v_{k}}
. This is a consequence of the results by Jerrard and Jung who showed the existence and uniqueness of a so-called
minimal lifting
of
\mathrm{D}u
, for all
u\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
(see Theorem
1.1
and
[
37
]
for details).

We emphasize that in this planar case with maps with values in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, using the aforementioned result of Jerrard and Jung, an extension of distributional determinant for maps of bounded variation has been recently obtained in
[
44
]
, under the additional assumption that a map
u
has finite relaxed graph area (where relaxation is done w.r.t. the strict topology of
BV
). This means that
u
is approximable strictly in
BV
by smooth maps
v_{k}
with uniformly bounded graphs area (see
[
11
,
44
]
for the relaxed area functional).
This implies that the Cartesian current in
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
obtained as the limit of the graphs of the approximating
v_{k}
is uniquely determined, which guarantees, in turn, that the Jacobian of
u
is always a Radon measure with finite total variation (see
[
44
]
for details).

However, requiring only that
u\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, the weak Jacobian determinant
Ju
of
u
(defined in (
2.2
)) is, in general, not a measure,
but a mere distribution with finite flat norm.
Moreover, although we will employ the notion of weak Jacobian only for
\mathbb{S}^{1}
- valued maps defined on a planar domain, we point out that the definition of weak Jacobian can be extended to more general (possibly unbounded) functions with bounded variation
u:U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
, also in dimension and codimension greater than two, allowing to give a notion of weak
2\times 2
-minors of
\mathrm{D}u
. This is stated and proved in Theorem
2.5
, which also shows that the notion of weak Jacobian extends the distributional determinant in the case of unbounded Sobolev maps.

Once introduced our weak notion of Jacobian determinant, our main result is Theorem
3.1
, where we state compactness and
\Gamma
-convergence for the functionals
\frac{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}}{|\log\varepsilon|}
. As in the classical (CR) and (GL) models, the compactness property shows that, if
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset SBV^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{1})
with

(0.9)

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})}{|\log\varepsilon|}<+\infty\,,

then, up to a subsequence, the weak Jacobians
Ju_{\varepsilon}
converge in the flat sense to a measure
\pi\mu:=\pi\sum_{n=1}^{N}z^{n}\delta_{x^{n}}
with
z^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
.
The starting point of the compactness analysis is that
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\overline{S}_{u_{\varepsilon}})\leq C\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|
, which guarantees that
\overline{S}_{u_{\varepsilon}}
can be covered by a finite (for fixed
\varepsilon
) family of balls such that the sum of its radii vanishes as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Starting from this family one can use
the
ball construction
technique introduced in
[
48
,
36
]
in order to provide lower bounds for the (CR) and (GL) models. However, such a tool is not enough in order to guarantee compactness in our case.
Indeed, in (GL) the corresponding bound as in (
0.9
), implies that
|Ju_{\varepsilon}|(\Omega)\leq C|\log\varepsilon|
, which in turns allows the authors of
[
8
]
to prove that the flat distance between the diffuse Jacobian of
u_{\varepsilon}
and the atomic measure provided by the ball construction tends to zero.
In order to bypass the control on the total variation of the Jacobians (which in our case are not even measures), we adopt the following strategy.
In order to avoid to estimate the flat distance of the singularities accumulating at the boundary,
in our main Theorem
3.1
we assume that the jump sets of the maps
u_{\varepsilon}
are all contained in a fixed set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\subset\Omega
. In Theorem
3.5
we use this setting to provide the same result with an applied displacement boundary condition on
\partial\Omega
but this time with possibly
S_{u}\cap\partial\Omega\neq\varnothing
.
Moreover, in order to deal with zero-average clusters contained in
\Omega
, we provide an iterative machinery that allows to modify the balls provided by the ball construction in order to get a “good bound” of
|\mathrm{D}u_{\varepsilon}|
on the boundary of small regions containing these clusters. From a physical standpoint these zero-average clusters are related to the so-called
statistically-stored
dislocations in single crystals, as opposed to the (
geometrically necessary
)
dislocations required to accommodate the incompatible deformations.

Some comments on our approach are in order.
A natural question is whether in (
0.6
) one can drop the closure on
S_{u}
, replacing
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\overline{S}_{u})
with
\mathcal{H}^{1}(S_{u})
. This creates some problems in the proof of compactness since, in such a case, the jump set, whose
\mathcal{H}^{1}
measure vanishes as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
, can be dense in
\Omega
.
In terms of our proof, this means that
S_{u_{\varepsilon}}
can be covered by a family of
countably many
, not necessarily
finitely many
,
balls whose sum of the radii vanishes as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
Since the ball construction
[
48
,
36
]
requires that the number of initial balls is finite, the problem above requires an extension of the ball construction procedure to the case of countably many initial balls.

Another issue concerns the case
\alpha>0
in (
0.5
). Also such a problem cannot be studied in full analogy with the one considered here, in view of the
lack of a-priori control of the
\mathcal{H}^{1}
-measure of the jump set in that case.
Furthermore, we do believe that our approach could be extended to higher energy regimes as well as
to the vectorial case of edge dislocations. This last issue is much more delicate, since it requires the extension of our approach to maps with vectorial lifting.
Finally, an extension of our approach to the 3d setting
[
21
]
deserves future investigations; in such a case, techniques coming from the theory of integral and Cartesian currents could be exploited, as successfully done in
[
34
,
20
,
50
,
51
,

,

.

1.
Preliminary results

In this section we collect some preliminary notion on the flat norm of measures and currents, as well as some properties of
BV
functions that will be used throughout the paper.

Multi-indices.

Let
m,n\geq 2
be two fixed integers.
Let
I\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
and
J\subset\{1,\dots,m\}
be multi-indices (in particular, they have a specific order). We denote by
\widehat{I}
the ordered set
\{1,\dots,n\}\setminus I
and
\widehat{J}=\{1,\dots,m\}\setminus J
. We also denote by
\sigma(I,\widehat{I})
and
\sigma(J,\widehat{J})
the signs of the permutations
(I,\widehat{I})\in S(n)
and
(J,\widehat{J})\in S(m)
, where
(I,\widehat{I})
and
(J,\widehat{J})
are seen as the sets
\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\{1,\dots,m\}
with a precise order. We will usually deal with the case in which
I=\{i,i^{\prime}\}
and
J=\{j,j^{\prime}\}
are multi-indices consisting of exactly to distinct elements.

Flat norm of Radon measures

Let
n\geq 1
be an integer and let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open and bounded set. We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{b}(U)
the space of Radon measures on
U
. If
\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(U)
, we denote by
|\mu|(U)
the total variation of
\mu
.
We also introduce the concept of flat norm of a measure
\mu
, noted by
\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{flat}}
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle{\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{flat}}:=\sup_{{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\ffi\in\Cc^{0,1}(U)}{\|\ffi\|_{C^{0,1}(U)}\le 1}}}\int_{U}\varphi\;\mathrm{d}\mu\,,}

Here and below, the Lipschitz norm
\|\varphi\|_{C^{0,1}(U)}
is defined by

\|\varphi\|_{C^{0,1}(U)}:=\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}+\sup_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{x,y\in U}{x\neq y}}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{|x-y|}\,.

By a density argument we easily see that the supremum in (
1.1
) can be equivalently computed among smooth and compactly supported (in
U
) functions
\varphi
with
\|\varphi\|_{C^{0,1}(U)}\leq 1
.

Flat norm of
k
-currents.

Let
n\geq 2
be an integer and let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set. For every
k\in\mathbb{N}
with
0\leq k\leq n
, we denote by
\mathcal{D}^{k}(U)
the topological vector space of smooth and compactly supported
k
-forms on
U
, and by
\mathcal{D}_{k}(U)
its dual, i.e., the space of
k
-currents on
U
. The canonical basis of 1-vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
, and its dual basis of 1-covectors by
\{\mathrm{d}x^{1},\dots,\mathrm{d}x^{n}\}
.

The mass
|T|
of a current
T\in\mathcal{D}_{k}(U)
is defined as

|T|=\sup\{\langle T,\omega\rangle:\;\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{k}(U),\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1\}\,.

As for measures, we define the
flat norm
of a current
T\in\mathcal{D}_{k}(U)
in
U
by

(1.2)

\|T\|_{\mathrm{flat},U}:=\sup_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{\omega\in\mathcal{D}^k(U)}{\|\omega\|_{F,U}\le 1}}\langle T,\omega\rangle,

where

\|\omega\|_{F,U}:=\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}+\|\mathrm{d}\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\,.

In the special case that
T
is a
0
-current and has finite mass, then it can be standardly identify with a measure, and the flat norm of
T
coincides with the flat norm of the measure
T
defined in (
1.1
).

Jacobian and
\mathbb{S}^{1}
-valued maps

Given a map
u\in W^{1,1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap L^{\infty}(U;\mathbb{R}^{2})
we recall that
Ju=\textrm{Det}(\nabla u)
is defined by (
0.7
).
Notice that
\textrm{Det}(\nabla u)
is well-defined also if
u\in W^{1,\frac{4}{3}}(U;\mathbb{R}^{2})
, since by Sobolev embedding theorem we have
\lambda_{u}\in L^{1}(U;\mathbb{R}^{2})
(see (
0.8
)).

In the sequel we will use the fact that a function
u\in H^{1}(U;\mathbb{S}^{1})
satisfies
\textrm{Det}(\nabla u)=0
in the sense of distributions.

Moreover, if
u\in H^{1}(U\setminus\overline{B};\mathbb{S}^{1})
, where
B\subset U
is a ball, then, integrating by parts,

\int_{U\setminus\overline{B}}\lambda_{u}\cdot\nabla\varphi\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\partial B}\lambda_{u}\cdot\nu\varphi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}=\int_{\partial B}j(u)\cdot\tau\varphi\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1},\qquad\forall\varphi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\Omega),

where
\nu
is the inner normal vector to
\partial B
,
\tau=-\nu^{\perp}
is the counter-clockwise tangent normal vector to
\partial B
, and
j(u)=\frac{1}{2}(u^{1}\nabla u^{2}-u^{2}\nabla u^{1})=-\lambda_{u}^{\perp}
. Notice that
j(u)\cdot\tau=\frac{1}{2}(u^{1}\frac{\partial u^{2}}{\partial\tau}-u^{2}\frac{\partial u^{1}}{\partial\tau})
on
\partial B
.

We recall that
\deg(u,\partial B)\in\mathbb{Z}
is defined as

(1.3)

\displaystyle\deg(u,\partial B):=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial B}j(u)\cdot\tau\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\partial B}\lambda_{u}\cdot\nu\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\,,

whenever
u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial B;\mathbb{S}^{1})
.

Vector-valued
BV
functions

Here we recall some basic facts on vector-valued
BV
-functions that will be used throughout the paper.
We refer to the monograph
[
9
]
for a complete analysis of
BV
-functions.

Let
m,n\geq 2
be two integers and let

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set. Let moreover
u\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Then, there is an
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-rectifiable set
S_{u}\subset\Omega
such that
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.
x\in\Omega\setminus S_{u}
is a Lebesgue point for
u
. For every such
x\in\Omega\setminus S_{u}
, we denote by
\overline{u}(x)\,
, the Lebesgue value of
u
at
x
, so that
\overline{u}
turns out to be well-defined
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-almost everywhere on
\Omega\setminus S_{u}
.
The set
S_{u}
has the following property: For
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.
x\in S_{u}
there exists a unit vector
\nu(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is orthogonal to the approximate tangent space to
S_{u}
at
x
, and there exist
u^{+}(x),u^{-}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
such that the maps

\nu,u^{+},u^{-}
are
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-measurable on
S_{u}
, and

u^{+}(x)=\textrm{aplim}_{y\rightarrow x,\;(y-x)\cdot\nu(x)>0}u(y),\qquad u^{-}(x)=\textrm{aplim}_{y\rightarrow x,\;(y-x)\cdot\nu(x)<0}u(y)\,,

for
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.
x\in S_{u}
.

The distributional gradient
\mathrm{D}u
of
u
reads as

\mathrm{D}u=\mathrm{D}^{D}u+\mathrm{D}^{S}u
, where
\mathrm{D}^{S}u
is the jump part of the gradient provided by

\mathrm{D}^{S}u=[u]\otimes\nu\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{n-1}\,,

with
[u]:=u^{+}-u^{-}
(
u^{\pm}
being the traces of
u
on the two sides of
S_{u}
), and
\mathrm{D}^{D}u=\mathrm{D}u\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits(\Omega\setminus S_{u})
is the diffuse part of the gradient, which satisfies
\mathrm{D}^{D}u(A)=0
if
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(A)<\infty
. In turn,

\mathrm{D}^{D}u=\mathrm{D}^{a}u+\mathrm{D}^{c}u
splits into two mutually singular measures,
\mathrm{D}^{a}u
which is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure
\mathcal{L}^{n}
, and the Cantor part
\mathrm{D}^{c}u
.

Eventually, we extend the definition of
\overline{u}
, which has been defined so far only
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e. on
\Omega\setminus S_{u}
, by setting

(1.4)

\overline{u}(x)=\frac{1}{2}(u^{+}(x)+u^{-}(x)),\qquad x\in S_{u}\,.

In this way,
\overline{u}
is well-defined
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e. on
S_{u}
and, in general,
\overline{u}
is well-defined
|\mathrm{D}u|
-a.e. in
\Omega
.
Furthermore, for every
\theta\in[0,1]
and
\mathcal{H}^{n-1}
-a.e.
x\in S_{u}
we define the function
\overline{u}^{\theta}
as

(1.5)

\overline{u}^{\theta}(x):=\theta u^{+}(x)+(1-\theta)u^{-}(x)\,,

so that
\overline{u}\equiv\overline{u}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Finally, we say that a sequence
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})

strictly converges
to
u
in
BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
(as
k\to+\infty
), and we write
u_{k}\overset{\mathrm{strict}}{\rightharpoonup}u
(as
k\to+\infty
), if
u_{k}\to u
(strongly) in
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
|\mathrm{D}u_{k}|(\Omega)\to|\mathrm{D}u|(\Omega)
(as
k\to+\infty
) .

Minimal lifting

Here we deal with maps
v:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded open set. We denote by
x=(x_{1};\dots;x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the coordinates in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
y=(y^{1};\dots;y^{m})
those in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.
Given
v=(v^{1};\ldots;v^{m})\in C^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, for every
i=1,\dots,m
and
j=1,\dots,n
, we define
(\mu_{v})^{i}_{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m})
as the only measure satisfying

(1.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\phi(x,y)\;\mathrm{d}(\mu_{v})^{i}_{j}=\int_{\Omega}\phi(x,v(x))\frac{\partial v^{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\;\mathrm{d}x\,,\qquad\textrm{for every }\phi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m})\,.

In
[
37
]
the following result is proved.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
u\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
. Then, for every
i=1,\dots,m
and
j=1,\dots,n
, there exists a measure
(\mu_{u})^{i}_{j}\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that the following holds: if
\{v_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset{C^{1}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
satisfies
v_{k}\overset{\mathrm{strict}}{\rightharpoonup}v
(as
k\to+\infty
) , then
(\mu_{v_{k}})^{i}_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}(\mu_{u})^{i}_{j}
and the
\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
-valued measure
\mu_{v_{k}}:=\big((\mu_{v_{k}})_{j}^{i}\big)_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{i=1,\ldots,m}{j=1,\ldots,n}}
converges tightly to
\mu_{u}:=\big((\mu_{u})_{j}^{i}\big)_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{i=1,\ldots,m}{j=1,\ldots,n}}
(as
k\to+\infty
). Furthermore,
for every
i=1,\dots,m
and
j=1,\dots,n
, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\phi(x,y)\;\mathrm{d}(\mu_{u})^{i}_{j}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus S_{u}}\phi(x,\overline{u}(x))\;\mathrm{d}(D^{D}_{j}u^{i})(x)

(1.7)

\displaystyle+\int_{S_{u}}\int_{0}^{1}\phi(x,\overline{u}^{\theta}(x))\;\mathrm{d}\theta\;\mathrm{d}(D^{S}_{j}u^{i})(x)\,,

for every
\phi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m})
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.1
, it turns out that the
\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
-valued measure
\mu_{u}
is uniquely determined by
u\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and is called
minimal lifting
of
\mathrm{D}u
. Moreover, it easily follows that
if
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
satisfies
u_{k}\overset{\mathrm{strict}}{\rightharpoonup}u
in
BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
(as
k\to+\infty
) , then
\mu_{u_{k}}\rightarrow\mu_{u}
tightly.

2.
Weak
2\times 2
-minors of gradients and jacobian determinants of
BV
functions

We now see how the notion of minimal lifting provided by Theorem
1.1
allows to define, in a weak sense, the
2\times 2
-minors of gradient of suitable
BV
-maps.

Let
m\geq 2
and
n\geq 2
be two integers and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open and bounded set.
Given
v\in C^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, testing (
1.6
) with
\phi(x,y)=\varphi(x)\psi(y)
, where
\varphi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)
and
\psi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(x)\psi(y)\;\mathrm{d}(\mu_{v})^{i}_{j}=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\psi(v(x))\frac{\partial v^{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\;\mathrm{d}x\,.

It follows that, if also
v\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, for every
h=1,\ldots,m
, we can take
\psi:=\psi^{h}
with

\psi^{h}\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
such that

\psi^{h}(y)=y^{h}
in
{\overline{B}_{\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}}
, thus obtaining

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(x)\psi^{h}(y)\;\mathrm{d}(\mu_{v})^{i}_{j}=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)v^{h}(x)\frac{\partial v^{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\;\mathrm{d}x\,.

Now, let
u\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and
\{v_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m}){\cap W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}
be such that
v_{k}\overset{\mathrm{strict}}{\rightharpoonup}u
as
k\to+\infty
, and
\|v_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C
for all
k\geq 1
.
Then, it is easy to see that for every
i,h=1,\ldots,m
and for every
j=1,\ldots,n
, it holds

(2.2)

v_{k}^{h}(x)\frac{\partial v_{k}^{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\;\mathrm{d}x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}(\nu_{u})^{i,h}_{j}\qquad\textrm{as }k\to+\infty\,,

where the measure
(\nu_{u})^{i,h}_{j}
is defined by the formula

(2.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\;\mathrm{d}(\nu_{u})^{i,h}_{j}=\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(x)y^{h}\;\mathrm{d}(\mu_{u})^{i}_{j}\qquad\forall\varphi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)\,,

for all
i,h\in\{1,\dots,m\}
,
j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.
More precisely, we have the following result.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
u\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, then there exists a unique measure
\nu_{u}\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m\times n\times m})
such that, whenever
\{v_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})

satisfies
\|v_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C<+\infty
for all
k\geq 1
and

v_{k}\overset{\mathrm{strict}}{\rightharpoonup}u
in
BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, then
v_{k}\otimes\nabla v_{k}\rightarrow\nu_{u}
, where
(\nu_{u})_{j}^{i,h}
is defined by (
2.3
) for every
i,h=1,\ldots,m
and
j=1,\ldots,n
.
Finally, if
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
with

(2.4)

\|u_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}\leq C

for some constant
C>0
, and
u_{k}\overset{\mathrm{strict}}{\rightharpoonup}u
in
BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, then

(2.5)

\nu_{u_{k}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\nu_{u}\qquad\textrm{in }\mathcal{M}_{b}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m\times n\times m})\,.

Proof.

The first part of the statement follows straightforwardly from the discussion above (see formulas (
2.1
), (
2.2
), (
2.3
)). Let us comment on the last claim, and assume
u_{k}\overset{\mathrm{strict}}{\rightharpoonup}u
with
\{u_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
.
Let
\varphi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)
,
i,h\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Let moreover
\psi^{h}\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
be such that
\psi^{h}=y^{h}
inside
B_{C}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where
C
is the constant in (
2.4
).
By Theorem
1.1
, we get

(2.6)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(x)\psi^{h}(y)\;\mathrm{d}(\mu_{u_{k}})^{i}_{j}=\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(x)\psi^{h}(y)\;\mathrm{d}(\mu_{u})^{i}_{j}\,.

On the other hand, in view of (
2.4
), by (
2.3
), we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(x)\psi^{h}(y)\;\mathrm{d}(\mu_{u_{k}})^{i}_{j}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\;\mathrm{d}(\nu_{u_{k}})^{i,h}_{j}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(x)\psi^{h}(y)\;\mathrm{d}(\mu_{u})^{i}_{j}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\;\mathrm{d}(\nu_{u})^{i,h}_{j},

which, together with (
2.6
), implies that

\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\;\mathrm{d}(\nu_{u_{k}})^{i,h}_{j}\overset{k\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\;\mathrm{d}(\nu_{u})^{i,h}_{j}\,.

By the arbitrariness of
\varphi
we get (
2.5
).
∎

In view of Corollary
2.1
, for every
u\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, we are allowed to adopt the following notation

\displaystyle[u^{h}\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i}]:=(\nu_{u})^{i,h}_{j},\;\;\;\;i,h\in\{1,\dots,m\},\;\;\;\;j\in\{1,\dots,n\}\,,

and

[u\otimes\mathrm{D}u]:=\nu_{u}:=\Big((\nu_{u})^{i,h}_{j}\Big)_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{i,h\in\{1,\ldots,m\}}{j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}}}\,.

Let
u\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})

and let us fix four indices
i,i^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,m\}
and
j,j^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. We set
I:=\{i,i^{\prime}\}
,
J:=\{j,j^{\prime}\}
, and consider the measures

\lambda^{I}_{j},\lambda^{I}_{j^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{M}_{b}(\Omega)
given by

(2.7)

\displaystyle\lambda^{I}_{j}:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big([u^{i}\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i^{\prime}}]-[u^{i^{\prime}}\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i}]\big)=\frac{1}{2}((\nu_{u})_{j}^{i^{\prime},i}-(\nu_{u})_{j}^{i,i^{\prime}})\,,

\displaystyle\lambda^{I}_{j^{\prime}}:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\big([u^{i}\mathrm{D}_{j^{\prime}}u^{i^{\prime}}]-[u^{i^{\prime}}\mathrm{D}_{j^{\prime}}u^{i}]\big)=\frac{1}{2}((\nu_{u})_{j^{\prime}}^{i^{\prime},i}-(\nu_{u})_{j^{\prime}}^{i,i^{\prime}})\,.

We define the current
(T_{u})_{I,J}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}(\Omega)
as

\displaystyle(T_{u})_{I,J}:=

\displaystyle\sigma^{I}_{J}(\lambda_{j}^{I}e_{j^{\prime}}-\lambda_{j^{\prime}}^{I}e_{j})

(2.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{J}^{I}\frac{1}{2}\big([u^{i}\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i^{\prime}}]-[u^{i^{\prime}}\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i}]\big)e_{j^{\prime}}-\sigma_{J}^{I}\frac{1}{2}\big([u^{i}\mathrm{D}_{j^{\prime}}u^{i^{\prime}}]-[u^{i^{\prime}}\mathrm{D}_{j^{\prime}}u^{i}]\big)e_{j},

where
\sigma_{J}^{I}:=\sigma(I,\widehat{I})\sigma(J,\widehat{J})\in\{-1,1\}
. The
1
-current
(T_{u})_{I,J}
acts on
1
-forms
\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{1}(\Omega)
with
\omega=\sum_{h}\omega_{h}\;\mathrm{d}x^{h}
,
\omega_{h}\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m})
, as

\displaystyle(T_{u})_{I,J}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\sigma_{J}^{I}\Big(\int_{\Omega}\omega_{j^{\prime}}\;\mathrm{d}\lambda_{j}^{I}-\int_{\Omega}\omega_{j}\;\mathrm{d}\lambda_{j^{\prime}}^{I}\Big)

(2.9)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{J}^{I}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\omega_{j^{\prime}}\;\mathrm{d}([u^{i}\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i^{\prime}}]-[u^{i^{\prime}}\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i}])-\frac{\sigma_{J}^{I}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\omega_{j}\;\mathrm{d}([u^{i}\mathrm{D}_{j^{\prime}}u^{i^{\prime}}]-[u_{i^{\prime}}\mathrm{D}_{j^{\prime}}u_{i}]).

A consequence of Corollary
2.1
is that
(T_{u})_{I,J}

is well-defined (for all
u\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
), it is a Radon measure on the space of compactly supported and continuous
1
-forms and is weakly continuous with respect to strict convergence of
u_{k}
to
u
in
BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, provided that
u_{k}
are uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
.
Therefore we arrive at the following result.

Theorem 2.2
.

For all
u\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and for all
I=\{i,i^{\prime}\}\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
,
J=\{j,j^{\prime}\}\subset\{1,\dots,m\}
, the current
\partial(T_{u})_{I,J}\in\mathcal{D}_{0}(\Omega)
is uniquely determined as the boundary of the current
(T_{u})_{I,J}
given by (
2
). Moreover,

(2.10)

\|\partial(T_{u})_{I,J}\|_{\mathrm{flat},\Omega}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})}|\mathrm{D}u|(\Omega)\,,

for some universal constant
C>0
(independent of
u
) .

Proof.

The first part of the statement is clear. Let us prove (
2.10
):
Let
\varphi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\Omega)
and consider the
0
-form
\omega=\varphi
, whose differential is
\mathrm{d}\omega=\sum_{h}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{h}}\;\mathrm{d}x^{h}
. Hence, by definition of boundary of a current

\displaystyle\partial(T_{u})_{I,J}(\omega)

\displaystyle=(T_{u})_{I,J}(\mathrm{d}\omega)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{j^{\prime}}}\;\mathrm{d}\lambda_{j}^{I}-\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{j}}\;\mathrm{d}\lambda_{j^{\prime}}^{I}

(2.11)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{J}^{I}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{j^{\prime}}}\;\mathrm{d}([u^{i}\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i^{\prime}}]-[u^{i^{\prime}}\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i}])-\frac{\sigma_{J}^{I}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{j}}\;\mathrm{d}([u^{i}\mathrm{D}_{j^{\prime}}u^{i^{\prime}}]-[u^{i^{\prime}}\mathrm{D}_{j^{\prime}}u^{i}])\,,

from which (
2.10
) follows by definition of flat norm.
∎

We can then introduce the definition of weak

:

.

Let

and let

\displaystyle\langle M^{J}_{I}(Du),\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}:

(2.12)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{J}^{I}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{j^{\prime}}}\;\mathrm{d}([u^{i}\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i^{\prime}}]-[u^{i^{\prime}}\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i}])-\frac{\sigma_{J}^{I}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{j}}\;\mathrm{d}([u^{i}\mathrm{D}_{j^{\prime}}u^{i^{\prime}}]-[u^{i^{\prime}}\mathrm{D}_{j^{\prime}}u^{i}])\,,

for all

,
where

and

Notice that, if

, by (

), and (

\displaystyle M^{J}_{I}(\nabla v)=

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{J}^{I}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{j^{\prime}}}\Big(v^{i}\frac{\partial v^{i^{\prime}}}{\partial x_{j}}-v_{i^{\prime}}\frac{\partial v^{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\Big)\;\mathrm{d}x-\frac{\sigma_{J}^{I}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{j}}\Big(v^{i}\frac{\partial v^{i^{\prime}}}{\partial x_{j^{\prime}}}-v^{i^{\prime}}\frac{\partial v^{i}}{\partial x_{j^{\prime}}}\Big)\;\mathrm{d}x

namely, the distribution

coincides with the

-subdeterminant of the matrix

and columns in

.

2.1.
An extension of the
2\times 2
-minors for more general
BV

In this section we refine the result in Theorem
2.2
weakening the hypothesis that
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
.
To this purpose, we preliminarily notice that, if
u\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
, we can take
\phi(x,y)=\varphi(x)y^{h}
in (
1.1
) , with
\varphi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}(\Omega)
and
h\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
.
From this expression, using (
2.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi(x)\;\mathrm{d}(\nu_{u})^{i,h}_{j}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(x)y^{h}\;\mathrm{d}(\mu_{u})^{i}_{j}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\setminus S_{u}}\varphi(x)\overline{u}^{h}(x)\;\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{D}^{D}_{j}u^{i})+\int_{S_{u}}\varphi(x)\int_{0}^{1}(\overline{u}^{\theta})^{h}(x)\;\mathrm{d}\theta\;\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{D}^{S}_{j}u^{i})

(2.13)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega\setminus S_{u}}\varphi(x)\overline{u}^{h}(x)\;\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i})+\int_{S_{u}}\varphi(x)\overline{u}^{h}(x)\;\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i})\,,

where
\overline{u}^{\theta}=((\overline{u}^{\theta})^{1};\ldots;(\overline{u}^{\theta})^{m})
is defined in (
1.5
).
Let now
u\in BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{m})
and assume that

(P)

\displaystyle\textrm{ For some $i,i^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,m\}$ with $i\neq i^{\prime}$ and $j,j^{\prime}\in\{1,\dots,n\}$ with $j\neq j^{\prime}$\,, it holds}

\displaystyle y^{i}\in L^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m},(\mu_{u})^{i^{\prime}}_{j})\cap L^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m},(\mu_{u})^{i^{\prime}}_{j^{\prime}})\,,

\displaystyle y^{i^{\prime}}\in L^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m},(\mu_{u})^{i}_{j})\cap L^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m},(\mu_{u})^{i}_{j^{\prime}})\,,

\displaystyle\textrm{{where $L^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m},\mu)$ denotes the space of $L^{1}$ functions with respect to the measure $\mu$\,.}}

We emphasize that if

) is readily satisfied, since

is bounded in

In Theorem

) as well as to guarantee that it and its boundary have flat norm.
To this purpose, we preliminarily notice that, in view of (

), for every

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(x)\psi(y)|y^{h}|\;\mathrm{d}(\mu_{u})^{i}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus S_{u}}\varphi(x)\psi(\overline{u}(x))|\overline{u}^{h}(x)|\;\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i})+\int_{S_{u}}\varphi(x)\int_{0}^{1}\psi(\overline{u}^{\theta}(x))|(\overline{u}^{h})^{\theta}(x)|\;\mathrm{d}\theta\;\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i})

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\setminus S_{u}}|\overline{u}^{h}(x)|\;\mathrm{d}|\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i}|+\int_{S_{u}}\int_{0}^{1}|(\overline{u}^{\theta})^{h}(x)|\;\mathrm{d}\theta\;\mathrm{d}|\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i}|.

and

(2.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}|y^{h}|\;\mathrm{d}|(\mu_{u})^{i}_{j}|\leq\int_{\Omega\setminus S_{u}}|\overline{u}^{h}(x)|\;\mathrm{d}|\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i}|+\int_{S_{u}}\int_{0}^{1}|(\overline{u}^{\theta})^{h}(x)|\;\mathrm{d}\theta\;\mathrm{d}|\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i}|.

In particular, if

satisfies (

Lemma 2.4

(2.17)

\displaystyle\overline{u}^{h}\in L^{1}(\Omega\setminus S_{u},|\mathrm{D}^{D}_{j}u^{i}|),\qquad\overline{u}^{h}\in L^{1}(S_{u},|\mathrm{D}^{S}_{j}u^{i}|)\,,

Proof.

Fix

\displaystyle\int_{U_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}\setminus S_{u}}\varphi_{{U^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}}}(x)\psi(\overline{u}(x))|\overline{u}^{h}(x)|\;\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i})+\int_{\Omega\setminus(U_{\delta}^{\varepsilon}\cup S_{u})}\varphi_{{U^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}}}^{c}(x)\psi(\overline{u}(x))|\overline{u}^{h}(x)|\;\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i})

\displaystyle+\int_{{{U^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}}}\cap S_{u}}\varphi_{{U^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}}}(x)\int_{0}^{1}\psi(\overline{u}^{\theta}(x))|(\overline{u}^{\theta})^{h}(x)|\;\mathrm{d}\theta\;\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i})

\displaystyle+\int_{(\Omega\setminus{{U^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}}})\cap S_{u}}\varphi_{{U^{\varepsilon}_{\delta}}}^{c}(x)\int_{0}^{1}\psi(\overline{u}^{\theta}(x))|(\overline{u}^{\theta})^{h}(x)|\;\mathrm{d}\theta\;\mathrm{d}(\mathrm{D}_{j}u^{i})

,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}|y^{h}|\;\mathrm{d}{|(\mu_{u})^{i}_{j}|}\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\phi(x,y)y^{i}\;\mathrm{d}(\mu_{u})^{i^{\prime}}_{j}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{m}}\varphi(x)y^{i}\;\mathrm{d}(\mu_{u})^{i^{\prime}}_{j}

Remark 2.8
.

In

the existence and well-posedness of

is obtained under the additional condition that the function

\displaystyle Ju_{k}\rightarrow Ju\qquad\text{ weakly star as measures.}

\{B_{r^{m,k}}(x^{m,k})\}_{k=1,\ldots,K^{m}}\subseteq\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{>}
be such that
\widetilde{D}^{m}:=\bigcup_{k=1}^{K^{m}}B_{R^{m,k}_{\varepsilon}}(x^{m,k})
, where
R^{m,k}_{\varepsilon}:=R(r^{m,k},\varepsilon)
is defined by (
3.38
) . Moreover, for every
m=1,\ldots,M_{\varepsilon}
, we set

\widehat{D}^{m}:=\bigcup_{k=1}^{K^{m}}B_{r^{m,k}}(x^{m,k})
and we define
the function
f^{m}:\widetilde{D}^{m}\to[0,1]
as

f^{m}(x):=\inf_{k=1,\ldots,K^{m}}\Big\{\frac{1}{(16\,\overline{C}\,|\log\varepsilon|-1)r^{m,k}}\textrm{dist}(x,B_{r^{m,k}}(x^{m,k}))\Big\}\,;

we notice that
f^{m}
is Lipschitz continuous,
f^{m}=0
on
\widehat{D}^{m}
,
f^{m}=1
on
\partial\widetilde{D}^{m}
, and

(3.40)

|\nabla f^{m}|\leq\frac{1}{(16\,\overline{C}\,|\log\varepsilon|-1)\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\,.

Finally, for every
m=1,\ldots,M_{\varepsilon}
, let us define the sets

\displaystyle T^{m}_{1}:=

\displaystyle\Big\{t\in[0,1]\,:\,\{f^{m}=t\}\cap\bigcup_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}B\neq\varnothing\Big\}\,,

\displaystyle T^{m}_{2}:=

\displaystyle\Big\{t\in[0,1]\,:\,\{f^{m}=t\}\cap\bigcup_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}B=\varnothing\Big\}\,,

and we show that

(3.41)

\mathcal{H}^{1}(T^{m}_{2})\geq\frac{1}{2}\,.

Indeed, in view of (
3.40
)) for every
B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{1}\Big(\left\{t\in[0,1]\,:\,\{f^{m}=t\}\cap B\neq\varnothing\right\}\Big)\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{osc}_{B}(f^{m})\leq 2r(B)|\nabla f^{m}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2r(B)\frac{1}{(16\,\overline{C}\,|\log\varepsilon|-1)\frac{\varepsilon}{2}}\leq\frac{r(B)}{2\overline{C}\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|}\,,

which, together with (
3.18
), implies

\mathcal{H}^{1}(T^{m}_{1})\leq\sum_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}\mathcal{H}^{1}\Big(\left\{t\in[0,1]\,:\,\{f^{m}=t\}\cap B\neq\varnothing\right\}\Big)\leq\frac{\mathcal{R}ad(\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<})}{2\overline{C}\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,,

whence (
3.41
) follows.
For every
k=1,\ldots,K^{m}
we set

E^{m}_{r^{m,k}}:=\Big\{x\in\widetilde{D}^{m}\,:\,|\nabla f^{m}(x)|=\frac{1}{(16\,\overline{C}\,|\log\varepsilon|-1)r^{m,k}}\Big\}\,;

it is easy to check that

E^{m}_{r^{m,k}}\subset B_{16\,\overline{C}\,|\log\varepsilon|\,r^{m,k}}(x^{m,k})\setminus\overline{B}_{r^{m,k}}(x^{m,k})\subset\widetilde{D}^{m}\setminus\widehat{D}^{m}\,.

Since
f^{m}
is Lipschitz continuous, by the coarea formula and by the mean value theorem, there exists
t^{m}\in T^{m}_{2}
such that

(3.42)

\displaystyle\int_{\{f^{m}(x)=t^{m}\}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\leq\frac{1}{\mathcal{H}^{1}(T^{m}_{2})}\int_{T^{m}_{2}}\;\mathrm{d}t\int_{\{f^{m}(x)=t\}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{(\widetilde{D}^{m}\setminus\widehat{D}^{m})\setminus\bigcup_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}B}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}||\nabla f^{m}|\;\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{k=1}^{K^{m}}\frac{1}{(16\,\overline{C}\,|\log\varepsilon|-1)r^{m,k}}\int_{E^{m}_{r^{m,k}}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K^{m}\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}\leq C|\log\varepsilon|^{\frac{3}{2}}\,,

where the last but one inequality follows by Hölder inequality and in last passage we have used (
3.37
) and (
3.3
) .
For every
m=1,\ldots,M_{\varepsilon}
, we set

D^{m}:=\{x\in\widetilde{D}^{m}\,:\,f^{m}(x)\leq t^{m}\}\,,

so that
\widehat{D}^{m}\subset D^{m}
;
by (
3.42
) we have that

(3.43)

\int_{\partial D^{m}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\leq C|\log\varepsilon|^{\frac{3}{2}}\,.

Moreover, we notice that, in view of (
3.39
) ,

(3.44)

\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}\mathrm{diam}(D^{m})\leq C\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{2}\,.

We remark that

(3.45)

D^{m}:=\bigcup_{k=1}^{K^{m}}B_{\alpha^{m,k}r^{m,k}}(x^{m,k})\,,\quad\textrm{for some }\alpha^{m,k}\in(1,16\overline{C}|\log\varepsilon|)\textrm{ (for all $k=1,\dots,K^{m}$)}\,;

by (
3.39
),

(3.46)

\sum_{k=1}^{K^{m}}\alpha^{m,k}r^{m,k}\leq C\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{2}\,.

Eventually, observe that, thanks to the choice of
t^{m}\in T^{m}_{2}
, the boundary
\partial D^{m}
does not intersect other balls in
\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}
. However,
D^{m}
might contain some other ball in
\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}
.

Step 5: Definition of
\widehat{\mu}_{\varepsilon}
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, we define
\widehat{\mu}_{\varepsilon}
as

\widehat{\mu}_{\varepsilon}:=\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{m})\delta_{\widehat{x}^{m}}\,,

where, for every
m=1,\ldots,M_{\varepsilon}
,
\widehat{x}^{m}
is the center of an arbitrarily chosen ball among the balls
\{B_{r^{m,k}}(x^{m,k})\}_{k=1,\ldots,K^{m}}
contained in
D^{m}
.
Notice that
\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{m})=\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{B\in\Ccal_\ep}{B\subset D^m}}\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial B)
.
As a consequence, it easily follows by the very definition of
\widetilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}
in (
3.14
), that

(3.47)

\displaystyle|\widehat{\mu}_{\varepsilon}|(\Omega)\leq|\widetilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}|(\Omega)\leq C|\log\varepsilon|\,.

By (
3.44
) and Theorem
3.4
(i), we get

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathrm{flat}}\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\|\varphi\|_{C_{\mathrm{c}}^{0,1}(\Omega)}\leq 1}\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}\Big|\int_{D^{m}}\varphi\;\mathrm{d}(\widetilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{\mu}_{\varepsilon})\Big|\leq 2\sup_{\|\varphi\|_{C_{\mathrm{c}}^{0,1}(\Omega)}\leq 1}\sum_{m=1}^{{M}_{\varepsilon}}|\widetilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}|(B)\mathrm{osc}_{D^{m}}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{3}\,,

and hence
\|\widetilde{\mu}_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathrm{flat}}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Thus, in order to get (
3.16
), we are left to prove that

(3.48)

\|Ju_{\varepsilon}-\pi\widehat{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathrm{flat},\Omega}\to 0\qquad\textrm{as }\varepsilon\to 0\,.

Step 6: Definition of
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
and
\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}
.

In order to prove (
3.48
) we introduce the measures
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
and
\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}
such that the following identities

(3.49)

-\mathrm{Div}\,\lambda_{\varepsilon}=Ju_{\varepsilon}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad-\mathrm{Div}\,\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}={\pi}\widehat{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\,,

hold in the sense of distributions in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
.
We set

\lambda^{1}_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{2}([u_{\varepsilon}^{1}\mathrm{D}_{1}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}]-[u_{\varepsilon}^{2}\mathrm{D}_{1}u_{\varepsilon}^{1}])\,,\qquad\lambda^{2}_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{2}([u_{\varepsilon}^{1}\mathrm{D}_{2}u_{\varepsilon}^{2}]-[u_{\varepsilon}^{2}\mathrm{D}_{2}u_{\varepsilon}^{1}])\,,\qquad\lambda_{\varepsilon}:=(-\lambda^{2}_{\varepsilon},\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{1})\,.

By definition of weak Jacobian determinant (see Definition
2.3
, and (
2.2
)),
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the first equation in (
3.49
).
Moreover, by (
3.36
) and (
3.43
),

(3.50)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B}|\lambda_{\varepsilon}|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\leq C|\log\varepsilon|^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\textrm{for every }B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}

\displaystyle\int_{\partial D^{m}}|\lambda_{\varepsilon}|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\leq C|\log\varepsilon|^{\frac{3}{2}}

\displaystyle\textrm{for every }m=1,\ldots,M_{\varepsilon}\,.

Furthermore, by Hölder inequality, (
3.3
), and (
3.9
) (for the estimate of
|\mathrm{D}^{s}u_{\varepsilon}|
), we get

(3.51)

\displaystyle|\lambda_{\varepsilon}|\bigg(\bigcup_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}B\cup\bigcup_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}D^{m}\bigg)\leq

\displaystyle|\mathrm{D}u_{\varepsilon}|\Big(\bigcup_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}B\cup\bigcup_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}D^{m}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}\bigg(\sum_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}|B|+\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}|D^{m}|\bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{{\frac{5}{2}}}\,,

where we have used also (
3.18
) and (
3.44
) to deduce that

(3.52)

\sum_{B\in{\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}}|B|\leq C\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{2}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}|D^{m}|\leq C\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|^{4}\,.

Now, for every
m=1,\dots,M_{\varepsilon}
, we define
v^{m}_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{1})

as

v^{m}_{\varepsilon}(x):=e^{\imath\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{m})\vartheta(x-\widehat{x}^{m})},

where
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is identified with
\mathbb{C}
and

\vartheta
is the angular polar coordinate defined by

(3.53)

\vartheta(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\arctan\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}&\textrm{if }x_{1}>0\\
\frac{\pi}{2}&\textrm{if }x_{1}=0\textrm{ and }x_{2}>0\\
\pi+\arctan\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}&\textrm{if }x_{1}<0\\
\frac{3}{2}\pi&\textrm{if }x_{1}=0\textrm{ and }x_{2}<0\,.\end{array}\right.

We set

v_{\varepsilon}(\cdot):=\Pi_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}v_{\varepsilon}^{m}(\cdot)\equiv e^{\imath\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{m})\vartheta(\cdot-\widehat{x}^{m})}

and we define

\widehat{\lambda}^{1}_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{2}(v_{\varepsilon}^{1}\nabla_{1}v_{\varepsilon}^{2}-v_{\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla_{1}v_{\varepsilon}^{1})\,,\qquad\widehat{\lambda}^{2}_{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{2}(v_{\varepsilon}^{1}\nabla_{2}v_{\varepsilon}^{2}-v_{\varepsilon}^{2}\nabla_{2}v_{\varepsilon}^{1})\,,\qquad\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}:=(-\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}^{2},\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}^{1})\,.

We notice that
v_{\varepsilon}\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{1})
for any
1\leq p<2
.
It is well-known that, for every
m=1,\ldots,M_{\varepsilon}
, the distributional determinant of
v^{m}_{\varepsilon}
coincides with
{\pi}\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{m})\delta_{\widehat{x}^{m}}={\pi}\widehat{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\mathop{\hbox{\vrule height=7.0pt,width=0.5pt,depth=0.0pt\vrule height=0.5pt,width=6.0pt,depth=0.0pt}}\nolimits D^{m}
, so that also the second equation in (
3.49
) holds true.
Finally, we show that
\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}
satisfies estimates similar to (
3.50
) and (
3.51
).

Let
m=1,\ldots,M_{\varepsilon}
. Recalling (
3.45
), we can assume without loss of generality that
\widehat{x}^{m}=x^{m,1}
.
Then, by (
3.45
), (
3.46
), (
3.44
) and (
3.47
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\partial D^{m}}|\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\partial D^{m}}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}|\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{j})|\int_{\partial D^{m}}|\nabla\vartheta(\cdot-\widehat{x}^{j})|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{m})|\int_{\partial D^{m}}|\nabla\vartheta(\cdot-\widehat{x}^{m})|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{j=1}{j\neq m}}^{M_{\varepsilon}}|\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{j})|\int_{\partial D^{m}}|\nabla\vartheta(\cdot-\widehat{x}^{j})|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{m})|\int_{\partial B_{\alpha^{m,1}r^{m,1}}(x^{m,1})}|\nabla\vartheta(\cdot-x^{m,1})|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}

\displaystyle+|\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{m})|\sum_{k=2}^{K^{m}}\int_{\partial B_{\alpha^{m,k}r^{m,k}}(x^{m,k})\setminus B_{\alpha^{m,1}r^{m,1}}(x^{m,1})}|\nabla\vartheta(\cdot-x^{m,1})|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{j=1}{j\neq m}}^{M_{\varepsilon}}|\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{j})|\frac{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial D^{j})}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\pi|\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{m})|+2\pi|\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{m})|\sum_{k=1}^{K^{m}}\frac{\alpha^{m,k}r^{m,k}}{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle+C\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{j=1}{j\neq m}}^{M_{\varepsilon}}|\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{j})|\frac{\mathrm{diam}(D^{j})}{\varepsilon}

(3.54)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|\widehat{\mu}_{\varepsilon}|(\Omega)+C|\widehat{\mu}_{\varepsilon}|(\Omega)|\log\varepsilon|^{2}\leq C|\log\varepsilon|^{3}\,.

Analogously, recalling that the balls in
\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}
are pairwise disjoint and that the balls in
\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{>}
have radius larger than
\frac{\varepsilon}{2}
, for every
B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}
we have

(3.55)

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B}|\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}|\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{j})|\int_{\partial B}|\nabla\vartheta(\cdot-\widehat{x}^{j})|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum_{j=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}|\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{j})|\frac{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial B)}{\varepsilon}\leq C|\log\varepsilon|\,,

where the last inequality follows by (
3.18
) and (
3.47
).

Finally, we prove that

(3.56)

|\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}|\bigg(\bigcup_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}B\cup\bigcup_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}D^{m}\bigg)\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}|\log\varepsilon|^{\frac{7}{3}}\,.

By definition of
v_{\varepsilon}
we easily get, for some fixed
p\in(1,2)
, and thanks to (
3.47
),

\displaystyle\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}=

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{m})\nabla\vartheta(\cdot-\widehat{x}^{m})\Big\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}|\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{m})|=C|\widehat{\mu}_{\varepsilon}|(\Omega)\leq C|\log\varepsilon|\,.

Hence,
by Hölder inequality and (
3.52
), we deduce

\displaystyle|\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}|\bigg(\bigcup_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}B\cup\bigcup_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}D^{m}\bigg)\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\bigcup_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}B\cup\bigcup_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}D^{m}}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}|\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nabla v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})}\bigg(\sum_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}|B|+\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}|D^{m}|\bigg)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{\frac{2p-2}{p}}|\log\varepsilon|^{\frac{5p-4}{p}}\,,

whence (
3.56
) follows by taking
p=\frac{3}{2}
.

Step 7: Final estimate.

Let
\varphi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{1}(\Omega)
with
\|\varphi\|_{C^{0,1}(\Omega)}\leq 1
.
By definition of
Ju_{\varepsilon}=\partial T_{u_{\varepsilon}}
we have

(3.57)

\displaystyle\langle Ju_{\varepsilon},\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi\cdot\;\mathrm{d}\lambda_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}\int_{B}\nabla\varphi\cdot\;\mathrm{d}\lambda_{\varepsilon}+\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}\int_{D^{m}\setminus\bigcup_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}B}\nabla\varphi\cdot\;\mathrm{d}\lambda_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega\setminus(\bigcup_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}D^{m}\cup\bigcup_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}B)}\nabla\varphi\cdot\;\mathrm{d}\lambda_{\varepsilon}

and, analogously, by integrating by parts

(3.58)

\displaystyle\langle{\pi}\widehat{\mu}_{\varepsilon},\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\varphi\cdot\;\mathrm{d}\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}\int_{B}\nabla\varphi\cdot\;\mathrm{d}\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}+\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}\int_{D^{m}\setminus\bigcup_{{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}}B}\nabla\varphi\cdot\;\mathrm{d}\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega\setminus(\bigcup_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}D^{m}\cup\bigcup_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}B)}\nabla\varphi\cdot\;\mathrm{d}\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}\,.

Using that
u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(\Omega\setminus(\bigcup_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}D^{m}\cup\bigcup_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}B);\mathbb{S}^{1})
, and hence
Ju_{\varepsilon}=Jv_{\varepsilon}=0
in
\Omega\setminus(\bigcup_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}D^{m}\cup\bigcup_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}B)
,
we can integrate by parts the last integrals in (
3.57
) and (
3.58
), thus obtaining

(3.59)

\displaystyle\Big|\langle Ju_{\varepsilon}-{\pi}\widehat{\mu}_{\varepsilon},\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}\Big|\leq

\displaystyle|\lambda_{\varepsilon}|\big(\bigcup_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}B\cup\bigcup_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}D^{m}\big)+|\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}|\big(\bigcup_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}B\cup\bigcup_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}D^{m}\big)

\displaystyle{\displaystyle+\Big|\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\partial D^{m}}\varphi\,(\lambda_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nu\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\Big|}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle+\Bigg|\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{B\in\iteuno}{B\cap\bigcup_{m=1}^{M_\ep}D^m=\varnothing}}\int_{\partial B}\varphi\,(\lambda_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nu\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\Bigg|}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{{\frac{5}{2}}}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}|\log\varepsilon|^{\frac{7}{3}}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle+\Big|\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\partial D^{m}}\varphi\,(\lambda_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nu\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\Big|}

\displaystyle{\displaystyle+\Bigg|\sum_{\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{1}{B\in\iteuno}{B\cap\bigcup_{m=1}^{M_\ep}D^m=\varnothing}}\int_{\partial B}\varphi\,(\lambda_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nu\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\Bigg|}\,,

where in the last inequality we have used (
3.51
) and (
3.56
).

We now estimate the remaining integrals on the right-hand side of (
3.59
).
By (
3.50
) and (
3
), using that
\deg(u_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{m})=\deg(v_{\varepsilon},\partial D^{m})
, for every
m=1,\ldots,M_{\varepsilon}
, we have that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\partial D^{m}}\varphi(\lambda_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nu\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\Big|\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{osc}_{D^{m}}(\varphi)\Big(\int_{\partial D^{m}}|\lambda_{\varepsilon}|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}+\int_{\partial D^{m}}|\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon}|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrm{diam}(D^{m})C|\log\varepsilon|^{3}\,,

which, summing over
m
, and using (
3.44
), yields

(3.60)

\sum_{m=1}^{M_{\varepsilon}}\Big|\int_{\partial D^{m}}\varphi(\lambda_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nu\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\Big|\leq C\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{5}\,.

Analogously, using (
3.50
) and (
3.55
) together with (
3.18
), we obtain

(3.61)

\sum_{B\in\mathscr{I}_{\varepsilon}^{<}}\Big|\int_{\partial B}\varphi(\lambda_{\varepsilon}-\widehat{\lambda}_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nu\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\Big|\leq C\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{3}\,.

Therefore, by (
3.59
), (
3.60
), and (
3.61
), for
\varepsilon
small enough, we get

\Big|\langle Ju_{\varepsilon}-{\pi}\widehat{\mu}_{\varepsilon},\varphi\rangle_{\Omega}\Big|\leq C\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{5}\,,

for some constant
C
independent of
\varphi
.
Taking the supremum on
\varphi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\|\varphi\|_{C^{0,1}}\leq 1
we infer that
\|Ju_{\varepsilon}-{\pi}\widehat{\mu}_{\varepsilon}\|_{\mathrm{flat},\Omega}\leq C\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|^{5}
, whence
(
3.48
) follows. This concludes the proof of (i).

Proof of (ii).
We can assume without loss of generality that
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
satisfies (
3.3
) for some
C>0
.
By (
3.12
) and (
3.10
), applying Theorem
3.4
(ii) , we get that

\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})}{|\log\varepsilon|}\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{F(\mathscr{B}_{\varepsilon},\mu_{\varepsilon},\Omega)}{|\log\mathcal{R}ad(\mathscr{B}_{\varepsilon})|}\geq\pi|\mu|(\Omega)\,,

i.e., the claim.

Proof of (iii).

We divide the proof into three steps. In the first one we construct the sequence
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}
and we show that it satisfies (
3.5
); whereas the second and third steps are devoted to show that
\|Ju_{\varepsilon}-\pi\mu\|_{\mathrm{flat},\Omega}\to 0
(as
\varepsilon\to 0
).

Step 1 (Definition of
u_{\varepsilon}
and energy estimates):

Let
\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{I}z^{i}\delta_{x^{i}}
with
z^{i}\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
and
x^{i}\in\Omega
for every
i=1,\ldots,I
. By standard density arguments in
\Gamma
-convergence, we can assume that
|z^{i}|=1
for every
i=1,\ldots,I
.

Let
R>0
be such that the balls
B_{2R}(x^{i})
are pairwise (essentially) disjoint and contained in
\Omega
. For every
\rho>0
we set
\Omega_{\rho}(\mu):=\Omega\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{I}\overline{B}_{\rho}(x^{i})
and we define
\Theta:\Omega_{R}(\mu)\to\mathbb{R}
as
\Theta(\cdot):=\sum_{i=1}^{I}z^{i}\vartheta(\cdot-x^{i})
,
where
\vartheta
is the function defined in (
3.53
).

For every
0<\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}
and for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
we set
A_{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}}(x):=B_{\rho_{2}}(x)\setminus\overline{B}_{\rho_{1}}(x)
.
Let moreover
\sigma_{R}\in C^{\infty}(A_{R,2R}(0);[0,1])
be such that
\sigma_{R}\equiv 1
in
A_{\frac{7}{4}R,2R}(0)
and
\sigma_{R}\equiv 0
in
A_{R,\frac{5}{4}R}(0)
. Analogously, for every
\varepsilon>0
, let
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(B_{\varepsilon}(0);[0,1])
be such that
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\equiv 0
in
B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{4}}(0)
and
\sigma_{\varepsilon}\equiv 1
in
A_{\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon,\varepsilon}(0)
. We can also assume that

(3.62)

|\nabla\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\quad\textrm{for every }x\in B_{\varepsilon}(0)\,,\qquad|\nabla\sigma_{R}(x)|\leq\frac{C}{R}\quad\textrm{for every }x\in A_{R,\frac{5}{4}R}(0)\,,

for some constant
C>0
independent of
\varepsilon
(and of
x
) .

For
\varepsilon<R
we define the function
\vartheta_{\varepsilon}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
as

\vartheta_{\varepsilon}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x-x^{i})z^{i}\vartheta(x-x^{i})&\textrm{if }x\in B_{\varepsilon}(x^{i})\setminus\{x^{i}\}\textrm{ for some }i\\
z^{i}\vartheta(x-x^{i})&\textrm{if }x\in A_{\varepsilon,R}(x^{i})\textrm{ for some }i\\
(1-\sigma_{R}(x-x^{i}))z^{i}\vartheta(x-x^{i})+\sigma_{R}(x-x^{i})\Theta(x)&\textrm{if }x\in A_{R,2R}(x^{i})\textrm{ for some }i\\
\Theta(x)&\textrm{if }x\in\Omega_{2R}(\mu)\end{array}\right.

and we define
u_{\varepsilon}:\Omega\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
as

(3.63)

u_{\varepsilon}(\cdot):=e^{\imath\vartheta_{\varepsilon}(\cdot)}\,.

Then,
S_{u_{\varepsilon}}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{I}S^{i}_{\varepsilon}
where

(3.64)

S_{\varepsilon}^{i}:=\Big\{(x^{i}_{1},x_{2})\,:\,x^{i}_{2}-\frac{3}{4}\varepsilon<x_{2}<x_{2}^{i}-\frac{\varepsilon}{4}\Big\}\qquad\textrm{ for every }i=1,\ldots,I\,,

and hence

(3.65)

\mathcal{H}^{1}(\overline{S}_{u_{\varepsilon}})=I\frac{\varepsilon}{2}=|\mu|(\Omega)\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\,.

In particular, the energy term

(3.66)

\frac{\mathcal{H}^{1}(\overline{S}_{u_{\varepsilon}})}{\varepsilon|\log\varepsilon|}\rightarrow 0\qquad\text{as }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}\,.

Let us analyse the stored elastic energy.
It is easy to see that

(3.67)

\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{2R}(\mu)}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x{\leq}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{2R}(\mu)}|\nabla\Theta|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq C(I)\log\frac{\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)}{2R}\,,

where
C(I)>0
is a constant depending only on
I
.
Similarly, by the second estimate in (
3.62
), we deduce

(3.68)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{A_{R,2R}(x^{i})}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq

\displaystyle\int_{A_{R,2R}(x^{i})}|\nabla\vartheta(x-x^{i})|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{A_{R,2R}(x^{i})}\bigg|\nabla\bigg(\sigma_{R}(x-x^{i})\Big(\sum_{{j\neq i}}z^{j}\vartheta(x-x^{j})\Big)\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(I,R)\,,

where we have estimated

\displaystyle\int_{A_{R,2R}(x^{i})}\bigg|\nabla\bigg(\sigma_{R}(x-x^{i})\Big(\sum_{{j\neq i}}z^{j}\vartheta(x-x^{j})\Big)\bigg)\bigg|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C(I)+\int_{A_{R,2R}(x^{i})}\big|\sum_{{j\neq i}}z^{j}\nabla\vartheta(x-x^{j})\big|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq C(I,R).

Furthermore, by the first estimate in (
3.62
), for every
i=1,\ldots,I
, and for every
x\in B_{R}(x^{i})
it holds

\displaystyle|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)|=|\nabla\vartheta_{\varepsilon}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq 2\pi\Big|\nabla\sigma_{\varepsilon}(x-x^{i})\chi_{B_{\varepsilon}(x^{i})}(x)\Big|+\Big|\nabla\vartheta(x-x^{i})\chi_{A_{\frac{\varepsilon}{4},R}(x^{i})}(x)\Big|

(3.69)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\chi_{B_{\varepsilon}(x^{i})}(x)+\frac{1}{|x-x^{i}|}\chi_{A_{\frac{\varepsilon}{4},R}(x^{i})}(x)\,,

for some constant
C
independent of
\varepsilon
(and of
x
).

It follows that, for every
i=1,\ldots,I
,

(3.70)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{\varepsilon}(x^{i})}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{A_{\varepsilon,R}(x^{i})}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\pi\log\frac{R}{\varepsilon}\,,

which, together with (
3.67
) and (
3.68
), implies that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{2|\log\varepsilon|}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x\leq\pi I=\pi|\mu|(\Omega)\,,

which, together with (
3.66
), yields (
3.5
).

Now, in order to conclude the proof of (iii) of Theorem
3.1
, it remains to prove that

(3.71)

\displaystyle\|Ju_{\varepsilon}-\pi\mu\|_{\textrm{flat},\Omega}\rightarrow 0\qquad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

To this purpose in view of Remark
2.9
, it is enough to prove the following two facts: There exists a constant
C>0
(independent of
\varepsilon
) such that

(3.72)

|Ju_{\varepsilon}|(\Omega)\leq C\,,

and

(3.73)

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}\overset{\mathrm{strict}}{\rightharpoonup}\bar{u}\qquad\text{ in }BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\,,

for some
\bar{u}\in W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{S}^{1})
with
\textrm{Det}(\nabla\bar{u})=\pi\mu
.

Step 2: Proof of (
3.72
).

In order to show (
3.72
), thanks to Remark
2.8
, it is sufficient to show that for all
\varepsilon
small enough there exists a sequence of maps
\{v_{\varepsilon,k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that

(3.74)

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}|Jv_{\varepsilon,k}|(\Omega)\leq C\,,

for some
C>0
independent of
k
and
\varepsilon
, and

(3.75)

v_{\varepsilon,k}\overset{\mathrm{strict}}{\rightharpoonup}u_{\varepsilon}\quad\textrm{in }BV(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\qquad\textrm{(as $k\to+\infty$)}\,.

Furthermore, by standard density arguments, it is enough to construct
\{v_{\varepsilon,k}\}_{k}\subset W^{1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Let
0<\varepsilon\ll R
be fixed.
For every
i=1,\ldots,I
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
t_{1}<t_{2}
we set
S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,t_{1},t_{2}}:=[x_{1}^{i}-\frac{\varepsilon}{k},x_{1}^{i}+\frac{\varepsilon}{k}]\times[x_{2}^{i}+t_{1},x_{2}^{i}+t_{2}]
.
We will modify
u_{\varepsilon}
in the set
S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon}{k},\frac{\varepsilon}{k}}
, that is a neighborhood of the jump set
S^{i}_{\varepsilon}
in (
3.64
). Precisely, for every
i=1,\ldots,I
, and for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, let
u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{int}}\in C^{\infty}(S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon}{k},\frac{\varepsilon}{k}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
be defined as

\displaystyle u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{int}}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{k}{2\varepsilon}(x_{1}-x_{1}^{i})\Big)u_{\varepsilon}\Big(x_{1}^{i}-\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{k}{2\varepsilon}(x_{1}-x_{1}^{i})\Big)u_{\varepsilon}\Big(x_{1}^{i}+\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big).

Moreover, for every
i=1,\ldots,I
, and for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
we denote by
u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{bottom}}\in C^{\infty}(S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon}{k},-\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{up}}(S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,0,\frac{\varepsilon}{k}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
the functions

\displaystyle u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{bottom}}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{\varepsilon}(x_{2}^{i}-\varepsilon-x_{2})u_{\varepsilon}(x)+\Big(1-\frac{k}{\varepsilon}(x_{2}^{i}-\varepsilon-x_{2})\Big)u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{int}}(x)\,,

\displaystyle u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{up}}(x)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{k}{\varepsilon}(x_{2}-x_{2}^{i})u_{\varepsilon}(x)+\Big(1-\frac{k}{\varepsilon}(x_{2}-x_{2}^{i})\Big)u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{int}}(x)\,.

Finally we define
v_{\varepsilon,k}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
as

v_{\varepsilon,k}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{int}}(x)&\textrm{if }x\in S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon,0}\textrm{ for some }i\\
u_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{up}}^{i}(x)&\textrm{if }x\in S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,0,\frac{\varepsilon}{k}}\textrm{ for some }i\\
u_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{bottom}}^{i}(x)&\textrm{if }x\in S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon}{k},-\varepsilon}\textrm{ for some }i\\
\ u_{\varepsilon}(x)&\textrm{elsewhere in }\Omega\,.\end{array}\right.

An easy check shows that the function
v_{\varepsilon,k}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is Lipschitz continuous in
\Omega
.
Indeed,
u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{int}}
is Lipschitz continuous in
S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,0,\frac{\varepsilon}{k}}
(and smooth in its interior), and
u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{bottom}}
and
u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{up}}
are Lipschitz as well in
S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon}{k},-\varepsilon}
and
S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,0,\frac{\varepsilon}{k}}
(respectively).

We now check that
\{v_{\varepsilon,k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies (
3.75
).
To this purpose it is enough to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon}{k},\frac{\varepsilon}{k}}}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon,k}|\;\mathrm{d}x=\int_{S^{i}_{\varepsilon}}|[u_{\varepsilon}]|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1},

for all
i=1,\dots,I
.
Furthermore, since
v_{\varepsilon,k}\to u_{\varepsilon}
pointwise a.e. in
\Omega
(as
k\to+\infty
), it is sufficient to prove that

(3.76)

\displaystyle\limsup_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon}{k},\frac{\varepsilon}{k}}}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon,k}|\;\mathrm{d}x\leq\int_{S^{i}_{\varepsilon}}|[u_{\varepsilon}]|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}\,,

for all
i=1,\dots,I
. We notice that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\textrm{int}}}{\partial x_{1}}(x)=\frac{k}{2\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\Big(x_{1}^{i}-\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big)-\frac{k}{2\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\Big(x_{1}^{i}+\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\textrm{int}}}{\partial x_{2}}(x)=\Big(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{k}{2\varepsilon}(x_{1}-x_{1}^{i})\Big)\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{2}}\Big(x_{1}^{i}-\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big)+\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{k}{2\varepsilon}(x_{1}-x_{1}^{i})\Big)\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{2}}\Big(x_{1}^{i}+\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big)\,,

whence, using that

(3.77)

|x_{1}-x_{1}^{i}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{k}\textrm{ in }S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon}{k},\frac{\varepsilon}{k}}\,,

we deduce

(3.78)

\displaystyle\int_{S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon,0}}|\nabla v_{\varepsilon,k}|\;\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\int_{S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon,0}}|\nabla u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\textrm{int}}|\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon,0}}\Big|\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\textrm{int}}}{\partial x_{1}}\Big|\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon,0}}\Big|\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\textrm{int}}}{\partial x_{2}}\Big|\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{-\varepsilon}^{0}\Big|u_{\varepsilon}\Big(x_{1}^{i}+\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big)-u_{\varepsilon}\Big(x_{1}^{i}-\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big)\Big|\;\mathrm{d}x_{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{-\varepsilon}^{0}\int_{-\frac{\varepsilon}{k}}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{k}}\Big|\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{2}}\Big(x_{1}^{i}-\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big)\Big|+\Big|\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{2}}\Big(x_{1}^{i}+\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big)\Big|\;\mathrm{d}x_{1}\;\mathrm{d}x_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{S^{i}_{\varepsilon}}|[u_{\varepsilon}]|\;\mathrm{d}\mathcal{H}^{1}+\mathrm{o}_{k}(1)\,,

where
\mathrm{o}_{k}(1)
tends to
0
as
k\rightarrow+\infty
.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{bottom}}}{\partial x_{1}}(x)=\frac{k}{\varepsilon}(x_{2}^{i}-\varepsilon-x_{2})\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{1}}(x)+\Big(1-\frac{k}{\varepsilon}(x_{2}^{i}-\varepsilon-x_{2})\Big)\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{int}}}{\partial x_{1}}(x)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{bottom}}}{\partial x_{2}}(x)=-\frac{k}{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}(x)+\frac{k}{\varepsilon}u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{int}}(x)+\frac{k}{\varepsilon}(x_{2}^{i}-\varepsilon-x_{2})\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{2}}(x)

\displaystyle\phantom{\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{bottom}}}{\partial x_{2}}(x)=}+\Big(1-\frac{k}{\varepsilon}(x_{2}^{i}-\varepsilon-x_{2})\Big)\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{int}}}{\partial x_{2}}(x)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{up}}}{\partial x_{1}}(x)=\frac{k}{\varepsilon}(x_{2}-x_{2}^{i})\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{1}}(x)+\Big(1-\frac{k}{\varepsilon}(x_{2}-x_{2}^{i})\Big)\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{int}}}{\partial x_{1}}(x)\,,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{up}}}{\partial x_{2}}(x)=\frac{k}{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}(x)-\frac{k}{\varepsilon}u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{int}}(x)+\frac{k}{\varepsilon}(x_{2}-x_{2}^{i})\frac{\partial u_{\varepsilon}}{\partial x_{2}}(x)+\Big(1-\frac{k}{\varepsilon}(x_{2}-x_{2}^{i})\Big)\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{int}}}{\partial x_{2}}(x)\,;

hence, using that
|u_{\varepsilon}|,|u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\textrm{int}}|\leq 1
,
(
3.77
) and the fact that

|\varepsilon+x_{2}-x_{2}^{i}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{k}\textrm{ in }S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon}{k},-\varepsilon}\,,\quad|x_{2}-x_{2}^{i}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{k}\textrm{ in }S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,0,\frac{\varepsilon}{k}},

we can estimate

(3.79)

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\textrm{int}}}{\partial x_{1}}(x)\Big|\leq\frac{k}{\varepsilon}\,,

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\textrm{int}}}{\partial x_{2}}(x)\Big|\leq\Big|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\Big(x^{i}_{1}-\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big)\Big|+\Big|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\Big(x^{i}_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big)\Big|\,,

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{bottom}}}{\partial x_{1}}(x)\Big|\leq|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)|+\frac{k}{\varepsilon}\,,

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{bottom}}}{\partial x_{2}}(x)\Big|\leq 2\frac{k}{\varepsilon}+|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)|+\Big|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\Big(x^{i}_{1}-\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big)\Big|+\Big|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\Big(x^{i}_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big)\Big|\,,

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{up}}}{\partial x_{1}}(x)\Big|\leq|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)|+\frac{k}{\varepsilon}\,,

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\mathrm{up}}}{\partial x_{2}}(x)\Big|\leq 2\frac{k}{\varepsilon}+|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(x)|+\Big|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\Big(x^{i}_{1}-\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big)\Big|+\Big|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\Big(x^{i}_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big)\Big|\,.

By (
3.79
) and (
3.70
) it follows, in particular, that the integrals of

on

and
S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,0,\frac{\varepsilon}{k}}
are both negligible as

. This fact, together with (
3.78
), implies (
3.76
) and, in turn, (
3.75
).

In order to prove (
3.74
), we notice that,
in view of (
3.79
) and of (
3
),
it holds

(3.80)

\displaystyle\int_{S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon,0}}|Jv_{\varepsilon,k}|\;\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\int_{S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon,0}}|Ju^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\textrm{int}}|\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon,0}}\Big|\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\textrm{int}}}{\partial x_{1}}(x)\Big|\Big|\frac{\partial u^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\textrm{int}}}{\partial x_{2}}(x)\Big|\;\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\int_{-\varepsilon}^{0}\Big|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\Big(x^{i}_{1}-\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big)\Big|+\Big|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\Big(x^{i}_{1}+\frac{\varepsilon}{k};x_{2}\Big)\Big|\;\mathrm{d}x_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\,,

for some constant
C
independent of
\varepsilon
and

.

Analogously, using again (

) and (

), one can prove that there exists a universal constant

(3.81)

\displaystyle\int_{S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon}{k},-\varepsilon}}|Jv_{\varepsilon,k}|\;\mathrm{d}x=

\displaystyle\int_{S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,-\varepsilon-\frac{\varepsilon}{k},-\varepsilon}}|Ju^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,\textrm{bottom}}|\;\mathrm{d}x\leq C\,,

\displaystyle\int_{S^{i}_{\varepsilon,k,0,\frac{\varepsilon}{k}}}|Jv_{\varepsilon,k}|\;\mathrm{d}x=

Step 3: Proof of (

).

It is easy to see that the functions

tend pointwise to

\bar{\vartheta}(x):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}z^{i}\vartheta(x-x^{i})&\textrm{if }x\in B_{R}(x^{i})\textrm{ for some }i\\

.
Notice moreover that, since

Dirichlet boundary conditions

We conclude this section dealing with prescribed boundary conditions on
\partial\Omega
.
To this purpose, let
\widetilde{\Omega},\widehat{\Omega}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be open bounded sets with
\widetilde{\Omega}\subset\subset\Omega\subset\subset\widehat{\Omega}
, and let
w\in H^{1}(\widehat{\Omega}\setminus\widetilde{\Omega};\mathbb{S}^{1})
. Admissible functions for the problem with boundary conditions will be given by

\mathcal{AD}^{w}(\Omega):=\{u\in SBV^{2}(\widehat{\Omega};\mathbb{S}^{1}):u=w\text{ on }\widehat{\Omega}\setminus\overline{\Omega}\}.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, let
\mathcal{F}^{w}_{\varepsilon}:L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\to[0,\infty]
be the functional defined by

\mathcal{F}^{w}_{\varepsilon}(u):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\overline{S}_{u})&\textrm{if }u\in\mathcal{AD}^{w}(\Omega)\\
\displaystyle+\infty&\textrm{elsewhere in }L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\,.\end{array}\right.

Notice that we can have
S_{u}\cap\partial\Omega\neq\varnothing
, and that we take the closure
\overline{S}_{u}
of
S_{u}
in
\widehat{\Omega}
(in particular, the functional
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
penalizes jumps of
u
also on
\partial\Omega
).

We show that, up to slight modifications in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we can obtain a compactness and
\Gamma
-convergence result for the functional
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{w}
.
Specifically, we will prove the following statement.

Theorem 3.5
.

The following
\Gamma
-convergence result holds true.

(i)

(Compactness) Let
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
be such that

(3.83)

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\frac{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})}{|\log\varepsilon|}\leq C,

for some
C>0
. Then there exists
\mu\in X(\overline{\Omega})
, such that , up to a subsequence,
\|Ju_{\varepsilon}-\pi\mu\|_{\mathrm{flat},U}\to 0
(as
\varepsilon\to 0
) , for any open set
U
with
\Omega\subset\subset U\subset\subset\widehat{\Omega}
.
Moreover, it holds

(3.84)

\mu(U)=\deg(w,\partial\Omega)\,,

for any open set
U
with
\Omega\subset\subset U\subset\subset\widehat{\Omega}
.

(ii)

(
\Gamma
-liminf inequality) For every
\mu\in X(\overline{\Omega})
and for every
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\|Ju_{\varepsilon}-\pi\mu\|_{\mathrm{flat},U}\to 0
(as
\varepsilon\to 0
) for any open set
U
with
\Omega\subset\subset U\subset\subset\widehat{\Omega}
, it holds

\pi|\mu|(\overline{\Omega})\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})}{|\log\varepsilon|}\,.

(iii)

(
\Gamma
-limsup inequality) For every
\mu\in X(\overline{\Omega})
satisfying (
3.84
), there exists
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon}\subset L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\|Ju_{\varepsilon}-\pi\mu\|_{\mathrm{flat},U}\to 0
(as
\varepsilon\to 0
) for any open set
U
with
\Omega\subset\subset U\subset\subset\widehat{\Omega}
, such that

\pi|\mu|(\overline{\Omega})\geq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\mathcal{F}^{w}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})}{|\log\varepsilon|}\,.

Proof.

We divide the proof into three steps corresponding to each of the items in the statement.

Step 1: Proof of (i).

Notice that, by (
3.83
), we can assume without loss of generality that
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{AD}(\widehat{\Omega},\Omega)
for all
\varepsilon>0
(see (
3.1
)).
Moreover, recalling the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
in (
3.2
), by (
3.83
) we have

(3.85)

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon};\widehat{\Omega})\leq\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{w}(u_{\varepsilon})+\|w\|^{2}_{H^{1}(\widehat{\Omega}\setminus\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C|\log\varepsilon|\,.

Hence, we can apply Theorem
3.1

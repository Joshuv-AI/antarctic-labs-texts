Lower bounds for moments of quadratic twisted self-dual GL ⁡ ( 3 ) \operatorname{GL}(3) central L L -values
Shenghao Hua and Bingrong Huang

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove the conjectured order lower bound for the k k -th moment of central values of quadratic twisted self-dual GL ⁡ ( 3 ) \operatorname{GL}(3) L L -functions for all k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , based on our recent work on the twisted first moment of central values in this family of L L -functions.



1.
Introduction

Estimating moments of central values in families of
L
-functions is an active topic of number theory.
There are now well-established conjectures for these moments,
thanks to the work of
Keating and Snaith
[
9
,
10
]
,
with subsequently works of
Diaconu, Goldfeld and Hoffstein
[
2
]
and Conrey, Farmer, Keating, Rubinstein and Snaith
[
1
]
.
In
[
17
,
18
]
, Rudnick and Soundararajan developed a method for establishing conjectured order lower bounds for moments of central values of families of
L
-functions, provided a little more than the first moment of this family of
L
-functions can be computed.
This method was extended by Radziwiłł and Soundararajan
[
14
]
and Heap and Soundararajan
[
7
]
to all positive real moments.
Recently, the authors
[
8
]
proved an asymptotic formula for the twisted first moment of central values of quadratic twisted
\operatorname{GL}(3)

L
-functions.
In this paper, we work out the conjectured order lower bound for the
k
-th moment of central values of this family of
L
-functions for all
k\geq 1
, based on
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
.

Let
\phi
be a self-dual Hecke–Maass cusp form of type
(\nu,\nu)
for
\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z})
with the normalized Fourier coefficients

A(m,n)
.
We have the conjugation relation
A(m,n)=\overline{A(n,m)}=A(n,m)
, see Goldfeld
[
3
, Theorem 9.3.11]
.
For an automorphic representation
\pi
of
\operatorname{GL}(3,\mathbb{A_{Q}})
,
the symmetric square lift
L
-function
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}\pi)
has a simple pole at
s=1
if and only if
\pi
is the Gelbart–Jacquet lift of an automorphic representation on
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{A_{Q}})
with trivial central character, see
[
5
]
. That is,
\pi
is a self-contragredient cuspidal automorphic representation, see
[
4
]
and
[
16
]
.
So
\phi
is self-dual if and only if
L(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}\phi)
has a simple pole at
s=1
. Note that when the
\operatorname{GL}(3)
cusp form is self-dual, its Fourier coefficients are all real.
We denote
\theta_{3}
be the least common upper bound of power of
p
for
|A(p,1)|
, i.e.,
|A(p,1)|\leq 3p^{\theta_{3}}
for all prime
p
.
The Generalized Ramanujan Conjecture implies that
\theta_{3}=0
, and from Kim–Sarnak
[
11
, Appendix 2]
we know
\theta_{3}\leq\frac{5}{14}
.

Any real primitive character must be of the form
\chi_{d}(n)=(\frac{d}{n})
where
d
is a fundamental discriminant
[
13
, Theorem 9.13]
, i.e., a product of pairwise coprime integers of the form
-4
,
\pm 8
,
(-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}p
where
p
is an odd prime.
There are two primitive characters to the modulus
q
if
8\parallel q
and only one otherwise.
For
\operatorname{Re}s
sufficiently large we define the twisted
L
-function
L(s,\phi\otimes\chi_{8d})
to be

L(s,\phi\otimes\chi_{8d})=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{A(n,1)\chi_{8d}(n)}{n^{s}}.

It has an analytic continuation to the entire complex plane and satisfies the functional equation (see
[
3
, Theorem 7.1.3]
)

\Lambda(s,\phi\otimes\chi_{8d}):=(8d)^{s/2}\pi^{-3s/2}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\Gamma\left(\frac{s-\gamma_{i}}{2}\right)L(s,\phi\otimes\chi_{8d})=\Lambda(1-s,\phi\otimes\chi_{8d})

when
d
is positive and
\phi
is self-dual, where
\gamma_{1}=1-3\nu
,
\gamma_{2}=0
, and
\gamma_{3}=-1+3\nu
are the Langlands parameters of
\phi
.

Recently, we
[
8
]
proved asymptotic formulas for the twisted first moment of quadratic twisted
\operatorname{GL}(3)
central
L
-values.
In this paper,
we only consider the self-dual case and prove the conjectured lower bound for the
k
-th moment for quadratic twisted self-dual
\operatorname{GL}(3)
central
L
-values for all
k\geq 1
, by using our twisted first moment result.
We will use the lower bounds principle of Heap and Soundararajan
[
7
]
, where
they achieved lower bounds of moments of Riemann zeta function on the critical line,
which is a case touch to unitary group.
Recently, Gao and Zhao
[
6
]
estiblished the analogous result on lower bounds for moments of central values of quadratic twisted modular form
L
-functions.
We extend their method to the
\operatorname{GL}(3)
case.
Our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\phi
be a self-dual Hecke–Maass cusp form of
\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z})
, and
\Phi
a smooth nonnegative Schwarz class function supported in interval
(1,2)
.
For any
k\geq 1
we have

(1.1)

\mathop{\sum\nolimits^{\flat}}_{2\nmid d}\left|L\left(\frac{1}{2},\phi\otimes\chi_{8d}\right)\right|^{k}\Phi\left(\frac{d}{X}\right)\gg_{k,\Phi}X(\log X)^{\frac{k(k+1)}{2}},

where
\sum_{2\nmid d}\nolimits^{\flat}
means summing over positive odd square-free
d
.

Our result shows that the family of quadratic twisted
L
-functions
L(s,\phi\otimes\chi_{8d})
with
\phi
self-dual is a symplectic family, which is the same as the family of quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions
L(s,\chi_{8d})
.
Note that the family of quadratic twists of a given
\operatorname{GL}(2)
newform
f
,
L(s,f\otimes\chi_{8d})
, is an orthogonal family as proved in
[
17
,
18
]
.
If
\phi
is a non self-dual Hecke–Maass cusp form of
\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z})
, then our result in
[
8
, Theorem 1.3]
implies that the family of quadratic twisted
L
-functions
L(s,\phi\otimes\chi_{8d})
is an orthogonal family as the
\operatorname{GL}(2)
case.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
N,M
be sufficiently large natural numbers depending on
k
only.
Let
\{\ell_{j}\}|_{j=1}^{R}
be a sequence of decreasing even integers, where
\ell_{1}=2\lceil N\log\log X\rceil
and
\ell_{j+1}=2\lceil N\log\ell_{j}\rceil
for
j\geq 1
, and
R
is the largest integer satisfying
\ell_{R}>10^{M}
.
Assume that
M
is large enough so that
\ell_{j-1}>\ell_{j}^{2}
for all
2\leq j\leq R
.
We will use the following two inequalities

R\ll\log\log\ell_{1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum^{R}_{j=1}\frac{1}{\ell_{j}}\leq\frac{2}{\ell_{R}}.

Let
{P}_{1}
be the set of primes in interval
[3,X^{1/\ell_{1}^{2}}]
and

{P_{j}}
be the set of primes in interval
(X^{1/\ell_{j-1}^{2}},X^{1/\ell_{j}^{2}}]
for
2\leq j\leq R
.
We define

{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(d)=\sum_{p\in P_{j}}\frac{A(p,1)}{\sqrt{p}}\chi_{8d}(p),

E_{\ell}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}\frac{x^{j}}{j!}
,

{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(d,\alpha)=E_{\ell_{j}}(\alpha{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(d))
,
and

\mathcal{N}(d,\alpha)=\prod_{j=1}^{R}{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(d,\alpha)=\prod_{j=1}^{R}E_{\ell_{j}}(\alpha{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(d)),\quad(\alpha\in\mathbb{R}).

Note that
{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(d)

are all real for self-dual form
\phi
, since
A(p,1)\in\mathbb{R}
.
For
l>0
even and
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
then we can choose
x_{0}\neq 0
which is a local minimum of
E_{l}
because
E_{l}(\pm\infty)=+\infty
and
E_{l}^{\prime}(0)=E_{l-1}(0)=1
,
thus
E_{l}^{\prime}(x_{0})=E_{l-1}(x_{0})=0
,
and we know
E_{l}(x_{0})=\frac{x_{0}^{l}}{l!}+E_{l-1}(x_{0})>0
.
Together with
E_{l}(0)=1
,
we have
E_{l}(x)
must be positive for any even integer
l\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
This is a part of
[
15
, Lemma 1]
.
Hence we know
{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(d,\alpha)
is always positive in our case.

The case
k=1
in Theorem
1.1
is a simple consequence of our Lemma
3.1
below with
l=1
which we proved in
[
8
]
. From now on, we assume
k>1
.
To prove Theorem
1.1
, we need the following propositions.
By the Hölder’s inequality, we know

\sum_{2\nmid d}\nolimits^{\flat}L(\frac{1}{2},\phi\otimes\chi_{8d}){\mathcal{N}}(d,k-1)\Phi(\frac{d}{X})\\
\leq(\sum_{2\nmid d}\nolimits^{\flat}|L(\frac{1}{2},\phi\otimes\chi_{8d})|^{k}\Phi(\frac{d}{X}))^{\frac{1}{k}}(\sum_{2\nmid d}\nolimits^{\flat}{\mathcal{N}}(d,k-1)^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\Phi(\frac{d}{X}))^{\frac{k-1}{k}}

when
k>1
. So by Propositions
2.1
and
2.2
we prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 2.1
.

When
k>1
, we have

\sum_{2\nmid d}\nolimits^{\flat}L(\frac{1}{2},\phi\otimes\chi_{8d}){\mathcal{N}}(d,k-1)\Phi(\frac{d}{X})\gg X(\log X)^{\frac{k^{2}+1}{2}}.

Proposition 2.2
.

When
k>1
, we have

\sum_{2\nmid d}\nolimits^{\flat}{\mathcal{N}}(d,k-1)^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\Phi(\frac{d}{X})\ll X(\log X)^{\frac{k^{2}}{2}}.

To prove the above two propositions we will use the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
12
]
)
.

For normalized self-dual Hecke–Maass cusp form
\phi
for
\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z})
with Fourier coefficients
A(m,n)
,
we have

\sum_{p\leq x}\frac{A(p,1)^{2}}{p}=\log\log x+O_{\phi}(1).

3.
Proof of Proposition
2.1

For convenience we write an integer
n=(n)_{1}(n)_{2}^{2}
with
(n)_{1}
is square-free.
We would prove Proposition
2.1
with the help of the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\phi
be a self-dual Hecke–Maass cusp form of
\operatorname{SL}(3,\mathbb{Z})
with normalized Fourier coefficients
A(m,n)
.
For sufficiently large
X>0
and odd
l\ll X^{\frac{1}{10}-\varepsilon}
with arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
, and any smooth nonnegative Schwarz class function
\Phi
supported in the interval
(1,2)
, we have

\sum_{2\nmid d}\nolimits^{\flat}L(\frac{1}{2},\phi\otimes\chi_{8d})\chi_{8d}(l)\Phi(\frac{d}{X})=c\check{\Phi}(0)\frac{\lim_{s\to 1}(s-1)L^{\{2\}}(s,\operatorname{sym}^{2}\phi)}{\sqrt{(l)_{1}}}\\
\times X(G(l)(\log\frac{X}{(l)_{1}^{\frac{2}{3}}}+c_{1})+H(l))+O_{\Phi}(l^{\frac{3}{4}+\varepsilon}X^{\frac{19}{20}+\varepsilon})

where
\hat{\Phi}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\Phi(t)e(-xt)dt
,
\Phi_{(3)}=\max_{0\leq j\leq 3}\int_{1}^{2}|\Phi^{(j)}(t)|dt
,

G(l)=\prod_{\textrm{odd prime }p}G_{p}(l)

with

G_{p}(p^{a}M)=G_{p}(p^{a})=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1+O(p_{2}^{-2+2\theta_{3}+\varepsilon}),\quad&2\mid a,\\
&A(p_{1},1)+O(p^{-1+2\theta_{3}+\varepsilon}),\quad&2\nmid a,\end{aligned}\right.

for
(M,p)=1
, with effective
O
-constant no more than 35.

H(l)=O\Big(\prod_{p|l_{1}}(|A(p_{1},1)|+p^{-1+2\theta_{3}+\varepsilon})+\sum_{p\mid l_{1}}p^{-1+2\theta_{3}}\log p\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p_{1}|l_{1}\\
p_{1}\neq p\end{subarray}}(|A(p_{1},1)|+p^{-1+2\theta_{3}+\varepsilon})\Big),

with effective
O
-constant.
And
c,c_{1}
are constants depending on
\phi
and
\Phi
.

Let
g(n)
be a multiplicative function with
g(p^{m})=m!
.
Let
\Omega(n)
be the total number of prime factors of
n
.
Let indicator function
b_{j}(n)=1
when all prime factors of
n
are in
P_{j}
and
\Omega(n)\leq\ell_{j}
, and
b_{j}(n)=0
otherwise.
Let
A(m)
be a completely multiplicative function with
A(p)=A(p,1)
when
p
is prime,
so
A(m)=A(m,1)
when
m
is square-free.
Thus

{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(d,k-1)=\sum_{n_{j}}\frac{A(n_{j})}{\sqrt{n_{j}}}\frac{(k-1)^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}b_{j}(n_{j})\chi_{8d}(n_{j})

for
1\leq j\leq R
.

From Lemma
3.1
we know

\sum_{2\nmid d}\nolimits^{\flat}L(\frac{1}{2},\phi\otimes\chi_{8d}){\mathcal{N}}(d,k-1)\Phi(\frac{d}{X})\\
\gg X\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{R}}\prod_{j=1}^{R}\frac{A(n_{j})b_{j}(n_{j})}{\sqrt{n_{j}(n_{j})_{1}}}\frac{(k-1)^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}\\
\times(G(\prod_{j=1}^{R}n_{j})(\log\frac{X}{((n_{1})_{1}\dots(n_{R})_{1})^{\frac{2}{3}}}+c_{1})+H(\prod_{j=1}^{R}n_{j})).

Here we only concentrate on the terms involving
G(\prod_{j=1}^{R}n_{j})\log\frac{X}{((n_{1})_{1}\dots(n_{R})_{1})^{\frac{2}{3}}}
.
By using the same argument in the following discussion,
the terms involving
G(\prod_{j=1}^{R}n_{j})c_{1},H(n_{1}\dots n_{R})
contribute as

O(X(\log X)^{\frac{k^{2}+1}{2}-1+\varepsilon})
,
then

\begin{split}&\sum_{2\nmid d}\nolimits^{\flat}L(\frac{1}{2},\phi\otimes\chi_{8d}){\mathcal{N}}(d,k-1)\Phi(\frac{d}{X})\\
&\hskip 30.0pt\gg X\log X\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{R}}G(\prod_{j=1}^{R}n_{j})\prod_{j=1}^{R}\frac{A(n_{j})b_{j}(n_{j})}{\sqrt{n_{j}(n_{j})_{1}}}\frac{(k-1)^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}\\
&\hskip 60.0pt-\frac{2}{3}X\sum_{n_{1},\dots,n_{R}}G(\prod_{j=1}^{R}n_{j})(\prod_{j=1}^{R}\frac{A(n_{j})b_{j}(n_{j})}{\sqrt{n_{j}(n_{j})_{1}}}\frac{(k-1)^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}\log(\prod_{j=1}^{R}(n_{j})_{1})\\
&\hskip 90.0pt+O(X(\log X)^{\frac{k^{2}+1}{2}-1+2\theta_{3}+\varepsilon})\\
&\hskip 30.0pt=S_{1}-\frac{2}{3}S_{2}+O(X(\log X)^{\frac{k^{2}+1}{2}-1+2\theta_{3}+\varepsilon}).\end{split}

Evidently all
n_{j}
in
S_{1}
satisfy that
\Omega(n_{j})\leq\ell_{j}
.
Removing the restriction for
\Omega(n_{j})
we get

\begin{split}S_{1}\geq X\log X\prod_{j=1}^{R}\Big(\prod_{p_{j}\in P_{j}}(&\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{A(p_{j})^{2i}G_{p_{j}}(p_{j}^{2i})}{p^{i}}\frac{(k-1)^{2i}}{(2i)!}+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{A(p_{j})^{2i+1}G_{p_{j}}(p_{j}^{2i+1})}{p^{i+1}}\frac{(k-1)^{2i+1}}{(2i+1)!})\\
&-\sum_{n_{j}}\frac{|A(n_{j})\prod_{p_{j}\in P_{j}}G_{p_{j}}(n_{j})|}{\sqrt{n_{j}(n_{j})_{1}}}\frac{(k-1)^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}2^{\Omega(n_{j})-\ell_{j}}\Big)\prod_{p>X^{\frac{1}{\ell_{R}^{2}}}}G_{p}(1).\end{split}

Note that
2^{\Omega(n_{j})-\ell_{j}}>1
when
\Omega(n_{j})>\ell_{j}
.
Hence we have

\begin{split}S_{1}\geq&\frac{X\log X}{\zeta(2-2\theta_{3}-0.01)^{35}}\prod_{j=1}^{R}\Bigg(\prod_{p_{j}\in P_{j}}\Big(1-(\frac{(k-1)^{2}}{2}+k-1)\frac{A(p_{j})^{2}}{p_{j}}\Big)^{-1}(1+O(\frac{|A(p_{j})|+1}{p_{j}^{2-2\theta_{3}}}))\\
&\hskip 30.0pt-2^{-\ell_{j}}\prod_{p_{j}\in P_{j}}\Big(1+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{A(p_{j})^{2i}G_{p_{j}}(p_{j}^{2i})}{p_{j}^{i}}\frac{(k-1)^{2i}2^{2i}}{(2i)!}\\
&\hskip 60.0pt+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{A(p_{j})^{2i+1}G_{p_{j}}(p_{j}^{2i+1})}{p_{j}^{i+1}}\frac{(k-1)^{2i+1}2^{2i+1}}{(2i+1)!}\Big)\Bigg)\\
&\geq\frac{X\log X}{\zeta(2-2\theta_{3}-0.01)^{35}}\prod_{j=1}^{R}\Bigg(\exp\Big(\frac{k^{2}-1}{2}\sum_{p_{j}\in P_{j}}(\frac{A(p_{j},1)^{2}}{p_{j}}+O(\frac{|A(p_{j},1)|}{p_{j}^{2-2\theta_{3}}}))\Big)\\
&\hskip 30.0pt-2^{-\ell_{j}}\exp\Big((2(k-1)^{2}+2(k-1))\sum_{p_{j}\in P_{j}}(\frac{A(p_{j},1)^{2}}{p_{j}}+O(\frac{|A(p_{j},1)|}{p_{j}^{2-2\theta_{3}}}))\Big)\Bigg),\end{split}

because
1+x\leq\exp(x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
In our setting
M
is large enough such that any
\ell_{j}
is large,
and
N
is large enough such that

(3.1)

\frac{\ell_{j}}{4N}\leq\sum_{p_{j}\in P_{j}}\frac{A(p_{j},1)^{2}}{p_{j}}\leq\frac{2\ell_{j}}{N}

for every
j
from Lemma
2.3
.
Thus

2^{-\ell_{j}}\exp\Big((2(k-1)^{2}+2(k-1))\sum_{p_{j}\in P_{j}}(\frac{A(p_{j},1)^{2}}{p_{j}}+O(\frac{|A(p_{j},1)|}{p_{j}^{2-2\theta_{3}}}))\Big)\\
\leq 2^{-\frac{\ell_{j}}{2}}\exp\Big(\frac{k^{2}-1}{2}\sum_{p_{j}\in P_{j}}(\frac{A(p_{j},1)^{2}}{p_{j}}+O(\frac{|A(p_{j},1)|}{p_{j}^{2-2\theta_{3}}}))\Big).

So we get

(3.2)

S_{1}\geq\frac{X\log X}{\zeta(2-2\theta_{3}-0.01)^{35}}\prod_{j=1}^{R}\Bigg((1-2^{-\frac{\ell_{j}}{2}})\exp\Big(\frac{k^{2}-1}{2}\sum_{p_{j}\in P_{j}}(\frac{A(p_{j},1)^{2}}{p_{j}}+O(\frac{|A(p_{j},1)|}{p_{j}^{2-2\theta_{3}}}))\Big)\Bigg).

Now we estimate
S_{2}
.
Consider the primes dividing
\prod_{i=1}^{R}(n_{i})_{1}
.
Let indicator function
\tilde{b}_{i,l}(n_{i})=b_{i}(n_{i}p^{l})
when
b_{i}(p)\neq 0
and
\tilde{b}_{i,l}(n_{i})=b_{i}(n_{i})
otherwise, we have

\begin{split}S_{2}&\leq X\sum_{p\in\cup P_{j}}(\sum_{l\geq 0}\frac{A(p)^{2l+1}G_{p}(p^{2l+1})\log p}{p^{l+1}}\frac{(k-1)^{2l+1}}{(2l+1)!})\\
&\hskip 30.0pt\times\prod_{i=1}^{R}(\sum_{(n_{i},p)=1}\frac{A(n_{i})\prod_{p\neq p_{i}\in R_{i}}G_{p_{i}}(n_{i})}{\sqrt{n_{i}(n_{i})_{1}}}\frac{(k-1)^{\Omega(n_{i})}\tilde{b}_{i,l}(n_{i})}{g(n_{i})})\prod_{p>X^{\frac{1}{\ell_{R}^{2}}}}G_{p}(1)\\
&=:X\prod_{p>X^{\frac{1}{\ell_{R}^{2}}}}G_{p}(1)\sum_{p\in\cup P_{j}}S_{p}.\end{split}

When
p\in P_{i_{0}}
, we know

\begin{split}S_{p}=&\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq i_{0}\end{subarray}}^{R}(\sum_{n_{i}}\frac{A(n_{i})b_{i}(n_{i})\prod_{p_{i}\in P_{i}}G_{p_{i}}(n_{i})}{\sqrt{n_{i}(n_{i})_{1}}}\frac{(k-1)^{\Omega(n_{i})}}{g(n_{i})})(\sum_{l\geq 0}\frac{A(p)^{2l+1}G(p^{2l+1})\log p}{p^{l+1}}\frac{(k-1)^{2l+1}}{(2l+1)!})\\
&\hskip 30.0pt\times(\sum_{(n_{i_{0}},p)=1}\frac{A(n_{i_{0}})\tilde{b}_{i_{0},l}(n_{i_{0}})\prod_{p\neq p_{i}\in P_{i_{0}}}G_{p_{i}}(n_{i_{0}})}{\sqrt{n_{i_{0}}(n_{i_{0}})_{1}}}\frac{(k-1)^{\Omega(n_{i_{0}})}}{g(n_{i_{0}})}).\end{split}

Removing the restriction for
\tilde{b}_{i,l}
on
\Omega(n_{i})
, just as our discussion above, we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{(n_{i},p)=1}\frac{A(n_{i})b_{i}(n_{i})\prod_{p_{i}\in P_{i}}G_{p_{i}}(n_{i})}{\sqrt{n_{i}(n_{i})_{1}}}\frac{(k-1)^{\Omega(n_{i})}}{g(n_{i})}\\
&\leq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\in P_{i}\\
(p,q)=1\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{A(q)^{2m}G_{q}(q^{2m})}{q^{m}}\frac{(k-1)^{2m}}{(2m)!}+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{A(q)^{2m+1}G_{q}(q^{2m+1})}{q^{m+1}}\frac{(k-1)^{2m+1}}{(2m+1)!}\Big)\\
&\leq\exp\Big(\frac{k^{2}-1}{2}\sum_{q\in P_{i}}(\frac{A(q,1)^{2}}{q}+O(\frac{|A(q,1)|}{q^{2-2\theta_{3}}}))\Big),\end{split}

and the error is no more than

\sum_{n_{j}}\frac{A(n_{i})\prod_{p\neq q\in P_{i}}G_{q}(n_{i})}{\sqrt{n_{i}(n_{i})_{1}}}\frac{(k-1)^{\Omega(n_{i})}2^{l\delta_{i=i_{0}}+\Omega(n_{i})-\ell_{i}}}{g(n_{i})}\\
\leq 2^{l\delta_{i=i_{0}}-\frac{\ell_{i}}{2}}\exp\Big(\frac{k^{2}-1}{2}\sum_{q\in P_{i}}(\frac{A(q,1)^{2}}{q}+O(\frac{|A(q,1)|}{q^{2-2\theta_{3}}}))\Big).

From
|A(p,1)|\leq 3p^{\theta_{3}}
it shows that

\begin{split}S_{p}&\leq\prod_{i=1}^{R}\exp\Big(\frac{k^{2}-1}{2}\sum_{q\in P_{i}}(\frac{A(q,1)^{2}}{q}+O(\frac{|A(q,1)|}{q^{2-2\theta_{3}}}))\Big)\\
&\hskip 30.0pt\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq i_{0}\end{subarray}}^{R}(1+2^{-\frac{\ell_{i}}{2}})(\sum_{l\geq 0}\frac{A(p)^{2l+1}G(p^{2l+1})\log p}{p^{l+1}}\frac{(k-1)^{2l+1}}{(2l+1)!}(1+2^{l-\frac{\ell_{i}}{2}}))\\
&\leq\prod_{i=1}^{R}\exp\Big(\frac{k^{2}-1}{2}\sum_{q\in P_{i}}(\frac{A(q,1)^{2}}{q}+O(\frac{|A(q,1)|}{q^{2-2\theta_{3}}}))\Big)\\
&\hskip 30.0pt\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\\
i\neq i_{0}\end{subarray}}^{R}(1+2^{-\frac{\ell_{i}}{2}})(\sum_{l\geq 0}\frac{A(p)^{2l+1}G_{p}(p^{2l+1})\log p}{p^{l+1}}\frac{(k-1)^{2l+1}}{(2l+1)!}(1+2^{l-\frac{\ell_{i}}{2}}))\\
&\leq B\frac{A(p)^{2}\log p}{p}\prod_{i=1}^{R}\exp\Big(\frac{k^{2}-1}{2}\sum_{q\in P_{i}}\frac{A(q,1)^{2}}{q}\Big),\end{split}

where
B
is a constant depending only on
k
.
Thus

(3.3)

\begin{split}S_{2}&\leq\zeta(2-2\theta_{3}-0.01)^{35}BX\prod_{i=1}^{R}\exp\Big(\frac{k^{2}-1}{2}\sum_{q\in P_{i}}\frac{A(q,1)^{2}}{q}\Big)\sum_{p\in\cup P_{j}}\frac{A(p,1)^{2}\log p}{p}\\
&\leq\zeta(2-2\theta_{3}-0.01)^{35}BX(\frac{\log X}{10^{2M}}+O(1))\exp\Big(\frac{k^{2}-1}{2}\sum_{q\in P_{i}}\frac{A(q,1)^{2}}{q}\Big).\end{split}

For
M
large enough, by (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we obtain

S_{1}-\frac{2}{3}S_{2}\gg X\log X\prod_{i=1}^{R}\exp\Big((\frac{k^{2}-1}{2})\sum_{q\in P_{i}}\frac{A(q,1)^{2}}{q}\Big)\gg X(\log X)^{\frac{k^{2}+1}{2}},

which proves Proposition
2.1
.

4.
Proof of Proposition
2.2

In this section we will prove Proposition
2.2
with the help of the following lemmas.

Lemma 4.1

(
[
19
]
)
.

For any odd
n>0
, we have

\sum_{2\nmid d}\nolimits^{\flat}\chi_{8d}(n)\Phi(\frac{d}{X})=\delta_{n=\square}{\hat{\Phi}}(0)\frac{2X}{3\zeta(2)}\prod_{p|n}(\frac{p}{p+1})+O(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}\sqrt{n}),

where
\delta_{n=\square}=1
if
n
is a square and
\delta_{n=\square}=0
otherwise.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
1\leq j\leq R
, we have

{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(d,k-1)^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\leq{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(d,k)\frac{(1+e^{-\ell_{j}})^{\frac{k}{k-1}}}{(1-e^{-\ell_{j}})}+(\frac{12k^{2}{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(d)}{\ell_{j}})^{2r_{k}\ell_{j}}.

where
r_{k}=\lceil\frac{k}{2k-2}\rceil
.

Proof.

We will prove this lemma analogous to
[
6
, Lemma 3.4]
and
[
7
, Lemma 1]
.
For
|x|\leq\frac{K}{10}
, we have

|\sum_{r=0}^{K}\frac{x^{r}}{r!}-e^{x}|\leq\frac{|x|^{K}}{K!}\leq(\frac{e}{10})^{K}\leq e^{-|x|-K},

then

\sum_{r=0}^{K}\frac{x^{r}}{r!}=e^{x}(1+O(e^{-K}))

and the
O
-constant could takes
1
.
For
x=\alpha{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(d)
and
K=\ell_{j}
, with
\alpha>0
and
|\alpha{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(d)|\leq\ell_{j}/10
, we have

{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(d,\alpha)=\exp(\alpha{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(d))(1+O(e^{-\ell_{j}})).

Thus when
|{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(d)|\leq\frac{\ell_{j}}{10k}
, we have

(4.1)

{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(d,k-1)^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\leq\exp(k{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(d))(1+e^{-\ell_{j}})^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\leq|{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(d,k)|(1+e^{-\ell_{j}})^{\frac{k}{k-1}}(1-e^{-\ell_{j}})^{-1}.

When
|{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(d)|\geq\frac{\ell_{j}}{10k}
, we know

(4.2)

|{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(d,k-1)|\leq\sum_{r=0}^{\ell_{j}}\frac{|(k-1){\mathcal{P}}_{j}(d))|^{r}}{r!}\leq|k{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(d))|^{\ell_{j}}\sum_{r=0}^{\ell_{j}}(\frac{10k}{\ell_{j}})^{\ell_{j}-r}\frac{1}{r!}\leq(\frac{12k^{2}|{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(d))|}{\ell_{j}})^{\ell_{j}}.

By (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), we complete the proof of the lemma.
∎

From
\prod_{j=1}^{R}\max(\frac{(1+e^{-\ell_{j}})^{\frac{k}{k-1}}}{(1-e^{-\ell_{j}})},1)\ll 1
, we have

(4.3)

\begin{split}\sum_{2\nmid d}\nolimits^{\flat}{\mathcal{N}}(d,k-1)^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\Phi(\frac{d}{X})&\ll\sum_{2\nmid d}\nolimits^{\flat}\prod_{j=1}^{R}({\mathcal{N}}_{j}(d,k)+(\frac{12k^{2}{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(d)}{\ell_{j}})^{2r_{k}\ell_{j}})\Phi(\frac{d}{X})\\
&=:\sum_{2\nmid d}\nolimits^{\flat}\prod_{j=1}^{R}(\sum_{n_{j}\leq X^{\frac{2r_{k}}{\ell_{j}}}}\frac{a_{n_{j}}b_{j}(n_{j})}{\sqrt{n_{j}}}\chi_{8d}(n_{j}))\Phi(\frac{d}{X}).\end{split}

Recall that

{\mathcal{N}}_{j}(d,k)=\sum_{n_{j}}\frac{A(n_{j})}{\sqrt{n_{j}}}\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}b_{j}(n_{j})\chi_{8d}(n_{j}),

and

{\mathcal{P}}_{j}(d)^{2r_{k}\ell_{j}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Omega(n_{j})=2r_{k}\ell_{j}\\
p\mid n_{j}\Rightarrow p\in P_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{A(n_{j})}{\sqrt{n_{j}}}\frac{(2r_{k}\ell_{j})!}{g(n_{j})}\chi_{8d}(n_{j}),

where
r_{k}=\lceil\frac{k}{2k-2}\rceil
.
From

(4.4)

(\frac{n}{e})^{n}\leq n!\leq n(\frac{n}{e})^{n},

we know

(\frac{12k^{2}}{\ell_{j}})^{2r_{k}\ell_{j}}(2r_{k}\ell_{j})!\leq 2r_{k}\ell_{j}(\frac{24k^{2}}{e})^{2r_{k}\ell_{j}}.

By setting
M
large enough so that
\ell_{R}>12r_{k}
,
we have
|a_{n_{j}}|\leq B_{k}^{\ell_{j}}n_{j}^{\theta_{3}}
with some constant
B_{k}
depending only on
k
.

Using Lemma
4.1
on the right side in (
4.3
), the contribution from error terms in Theorem
4.1
are

\ll X^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{\ell_{j}}B_{k}^{\ell_{j}}X^{(1+\theta_{3})\frac{2r_{k}}{\ell_{j}}}\ll B_{k}^{R\ell_{1}}X^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{4(1+\theta_{3})r_{k}}{\ell_{R}}+\varepsilon}\ll X^{1-\varepsilon}.

The main term of the right side in (
4.3
) is a scalar multiple of

X\times\sum_{\prod_{j=1}^{R}n_{j}=\square}(\prod_{j=1}^{R}\frac{a_{n_{j}}b_{j}(n_{j})}{\sqrt{n_{j}}}\prod_{p\mid n_{j}}\frac{p}{p+1})=X\times\prod_{j=1}^{R}\sum_{n_{j}=\square}\frac{a_{n_{j}}b_{j}(n_{j})}{\sqrt{n_{j}}}\prod_{p\mid n_{j}}\frac{p}{p+1}\\
=X\times\prod_{j=1}^{R}\Big(\sum_{n_{j}=\square}\frac{A(n_{j})}{\sqrt{n_{j}}}\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}b_{j}(n_{j})\prod_{p\mid n_{j}}\frac{p}{p+1}\\
+(\frac{12k^{2}}{\ell_{j}})^{2r_{k}\ell_{j}}(2r_{k}\ell_{j})!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j}=\square\\
\Omega(n_{j})=2r_{k}\ell_{j}\\
p\mid n_{j}\Rightarrow p\in P_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{A(n_{j})}{\sqrt{n_{j}}}\frac{1}{g(n_{j})}\prod_{p\mid n_{j}}\frac{p}{p+1}\Big).

From
|A(p,1)|\leq 3p^{\theta_{3}}
and the Taylor expansion of
\exp(x)
we can see

\begin{split}\sum_{n_{j}=\square}\frac{A(n_{j})}{\sqrt{n_{j}}}\frac{k^{\Omega(n_{j})}}{g(n_{j})}b_{j}(n_{j})\prod_{p\mid n_{j}}\frac{p}{p+1}&\leq\prod_{p\in P_{j}}\Big(1+\sum_{1\leq i\leq\lceil\frac{\ell_{i}}{2}\rceil}\frac{A(p,1)^{2i}k^{2i}}{p^{i}(2i)!}\frac{p}{p+1}\Big)\\
&\leq\prod_{p\in P_{j}}(1+\frac{A(p,1)^{2}k^{2}}{2p}\frac{p}{p+1}(1+\sum_{i\geq 1}\frac{(3k)^{2i}}{p^{(1-2\theta_{3})i}(2i)!}))\\
&\leq\exp\Big(\frac{k^{2}}{2}\sum_{p\in P_{j}}\frac{A(p,1)^{2}}{p}(1+\frac{e^{3k}}{p^{1-2\theta_{3}}})\Big)\\
&\ll_{k}\exp(\frac{k^{2}}{2}\sum_{p\in P_{j}}\frac{A(p,1)^{2}}{p}).\end{split}

From (
3.1
) and (
4.4
) we know

\begin{split}&(\frac{12k^{2}}{\ell_{j}})^{2r_{k}\ell_{j}}(2r_{k}\ell_{j})!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{j}=\square\\
\Omega(n_{j})=2r_{k}\ell_{j}\\
p\mid n_{j}\Rightarrow p\in P_{j}\end{subarray}}\frac{A(n_{j})}{\sqrt{n_{j}}}\frac{1}{g(n_{j})}\prod_{p\mid n_{j}}\frac{p}{p+1}\\
&\leq(\frac{12k^{2}}{\ell_{j}})^{2r_{k}\ell_{j}}\frac{(2r_{k}\ell_{j})!}{(r_{k}\ell_{j})!}(\sum_{p\in P_{j}}\frac{A(p,1)^{2}}{p})^{r_{k}\ell_{j}}\\
&\leq 2r_{k}\ell_{j}(\frac{576k^{4}r_{k}}{e\ell_{j}})^{r_{k}\ell_{j}}(\sum_{p\in P_{j}}\frac{A(p,1)^{2}}{p})^{r_{k}\ell_{j}}\\
&\leq 2r_{k}\ell_{j}(\frac{1152k^{4}r_{k}}{eN})^{r_{k}\ell_{j}}\\
&\ll e^{-\ell_{j}}\exp\Big(\frac{k^{2}}{2}\sum_{p\in P_{j}}\frac{A(p,1)^{2}}{p}\Big).\end{split}

Then we have

\begin{split}\sum_{2\nmid d}\nolimits^{\flat}{\mathcal{N}}(d,k-1)^{\frac{k}{k-1}}\Phi(\frac{d}{X})&\ll X\prod_{j=1}^{R}(1+e^{-\ell_{j}})\exp\Big(\frac{k^{2}}{2}\sum_{p\in P_{j}}\frac{A(p,1)^{2}}{p}\Big)\ll X(\log X)^{\frac{k^{2}}{2}}.\end{split}

This proves Proposition
2.2
.

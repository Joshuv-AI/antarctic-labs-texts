On Nonlinear Hybrid Fractional Differential Equations with Atangana-Baleanu-Caputo Derivative


Abstract
In this paper, we develop the theory of nonlinear hybrid fractional differential equations involving Atangana–Baleanu–Caputo (ABC) fractional derivative. We construct the equivalent fractional integral equation and establish the existence results through it. Further, we build up the theory of inequalities for ABC–hybrid fractional differential equations and use it to examine the uniqueness, existence of a maximal and minimal solution and the comparison results.

3
Existence result

In the following Theorem, we derive an equivalent fractional integral equation to ABC–hybrid FDEs (
1.1
)-(
1.2
).

Theorem 3.1

Let
g\in C(J\times\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R})
and assume that,
\omega\to\dfrac{\omega}{f(\tau,\omega)}
is increasing in
\mathbb{R}
a.e. for each
\tau\in J
. Then
\omega\in AC(J,\mathbb{R})
is a solution of
ABC–hybrid FDEs
(
1.1
)–(
1.2
) if and only if
\omega
is a solution of fractional integral equation

\omega(\tau)=f(\tau,\omega(\tau))\left[\dfrac{\omega_{0}}{f(0,\omega_{0})}+\dfrac{1-\alpha}{B(\alpha)}g(\tau,\omega(\tau))+\dfrac{\alpha}{B(\alpha)(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}g(\sigma,\omega(\sigma))d\sigma\right],\tau\in J.

(3.1)

Proof:

In the view of Lemma
2.2
, if
\omega
is a solution of ABC–hybrid FDEs (
1.1
)–(
1.2
), then
\omega
satisfies fractional integral equation

\small\dfrac{\omega(\tau)}{f(\tau,\omega(\tau))}=\dfrac{\omega_{0}}{f(0,\omega_{0})}+\dfrac{1-\alpha}{B(\alpha)}g(\tau,\omega(\tau))+\dfrac{\alpha}{B(\alpha)(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}g(\sigma,\omega(\sigma))d\sigma,\;\tau\in J,

(3.2)

which gives Eq.(
3.1
). Conversely, let
\omega
satisfies Eq.(
3.1
). Then it can be written in the form of Eq.(
3.2
).
Operating
{}^{ABR}{{}_{0}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{\tau}}
on both sides of Eq.(
3.2
), we obtain

{}^{ABR}{{}_{0}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{\tau}}\left(\dfrac{\omega(\tau)}{f(\tau,\omega(\tau))}\right)

\displaystyle=\;^{ABR}{{}_{0}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{\tau}}\left[\dfrac{\omega_{0}}{f(0,\omega_{0})}+^{AB}{{}_{0}}I_{\tau}^{\alpha}g(\tau,\omega(\tau))\right]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\omega_{0}}{f(0,\omega_{0})}^{ABR}{{}_{0}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{\tau}}(1)+g(\tau,\omega(\tau))

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\omega_{0}}{f(0,\omega_{0})}\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}\left[-\dfrac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\tau^{\alpha}\right]+g(\tau,\omega(\tau),\;\tau\in J.

This gives,

{}^{ABR}{{}_{0}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{\tau}}\left(\dfrac{\omega(\tau)}{f(\tau,\omega(\tau))}\right)-\dfrac{\omega_{0}}{f(0,\omega_{0})}\mathbb{E}_{\alpha}\left[-\dfrac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\tau^{\alpha}\right]=g(\tau,\omega(\tau)),\;\tau\in J.

Using relation between fractional differential operators
{}^{ABR}{{}_{0}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{\tau}}
and
{}^{ABC}{{}_{0}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{\tau}}
given in Theorem 1
[
11
]
, we obtain

{}^{ABC}{{}_{0}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha}_{\tau}}\left(\dfrac{\omega(\tau)}{f(\tau,\omega(\tau))}\right)=g(\tau,\omega(\tau)),\;\tau\in J.

Now putting
\tau=0
in Eq.(
3.2
) and using the fact
g(0,\omega(0))=0
, we obtain

\dfrac{\omega(0)}{f(0,\omega(0))}=\dfrac{\omega_{0}}{f(0,\omega_{0})}.

(3.3)

For each
\tau\in J
, consider the mapping
h_{\tau}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by,

h_{\tau}(\eta)=\dfrac{\eta}{f(\tau,\eta)},\;\eta\in\mathbb{R}.

By assumption
h_{\tau}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is increasing and hence it is injective. Using definition of
h_{\tau}
, Eq.(
3.3
)
can be written as

h_{0}(\omega(0))=h_{0}(\omega_{0}).

Since
h_{0}
is injective, we have
\omega(0)=\omega_{0}
.
This completes the proof of the Theorem.

\Box

To prove existence results for solution of ABC–hybrid FDEs (
1.1
)-(
1.2
), we need following assumptions on
f
and
g
.

(H1)

The function
f\in C\left(J\times\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}\backslash\left\{0\right\}\right)
is such
that,

(i)

|f(\tau,\omega)-f(\tau,\eta)|\leq L|\omega-\eta|,\;L>0
,

(ii)

the mapping
\omega\to\dfrac{\omega}{f(\tau,\omega)}
is increasing in
\mathbb{R}
a.e. for each
\tau\in J
.

(H2)

The function
g\in\mathcal{C}
is such that,

|g(\tau,\omega(\tau)|\leq h(\tau),a.e.\;\tau\in J,\;h\in C(J,\mathbb{R}^{+})
.

Theorem 3.2

Suppose the hypotheses
(H1)–(H2)
hold. Then
ABC–hybrid FDEs
(
1.1
)–(
1.2
) has a solution if

L\left(\left|\frac{\omega_{0}}{f(0,\omega_{0})}\right|+\left[1-\alpha+\frac{T^{\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\right]\frac{\|h\|}{B(\alpha)}\right)<1.

(3.4)

Proof:
Let
\Omega=\left(C(J,\mathbb{R}),\|\cdot\|\right)
,
where
\|\omega\|=\underset{\tau\in J}{\sup}|\omega(\tau)|.
Then
\Omega
is Banach algebra with multiplication defined by

(\omega\eta)\tau=\omega(\tau)\eta(\tau),\;\omega,\eta\in\Omega,\;\tau\in J.

Define,

R=\dfrac{M_{f}L\left(\left|\dfrac{\omega_{0}}{f(0,\omega_{0})}\right|+\left[1-\alpha+\frac{T^{\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\right]\dfrac{\|h\|}{B(\alpha)}\right)}{1-L\left(\left|\dfrac{\omega_{0}}{f(0,\omega_{0})}\right|+\left[1-\alpha+\frac{T^{\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\right]\dfrac{\|h\|}{B(\alpha)}\right)},

(3.5)

where
M_{f}=\underset{\tau\in J}{\sup}|f(\tau,0)|
. In the view condition (
3.4
),
R>0
.

Consider the set,

\mathcal{S}=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\;\|\omega\|\leq R\right\}.

One can verify that
\mathcal{S}
is closed, convex and bounded subset of Banach algebra
\Omega
. Consider the operators
\mathcal{F}_{1}:\Omega\to\Omega
and
\mathcal{F}_{1}:\mathcal{S}\to\Omega
defined by,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}_{1}\omega)(\tau)

\displaystyle=f(\tau,\omega(\tau)),\;\tau\in J,

(3.6)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}_{2}\omega)(\tau)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\omega_{0}}{f(0,\omega_{0})}+\dfrac{1-\alpha}{B(\alpha)}g(\tau,\omega(\tau))+\dfrac{\alpha}{B(\alpha)(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}g(\sigma,\omega(\sigma))d\sigma,\;\tau\in J,

(3.7)

The equivalent fraction integral Eq.(
3.1
) to the ABC–hybrid FDEs (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) can be written in operator equation form given by

\omega=\mathcal{F}_{1}\omega\mathcal{F}_{2}\omega,\;\omega\in\Omega.

We prove that the operators
\mathcal{F}_{1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{2}
satisfies conditions of Lemma
2.5
. The proof of the same have been given in following steps.

Step 1)
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is Lipschitz.

Using Lipschitz condition on
f
, for any
\omega,\eta\in\Omega
and
\tau\in J
we obtain,

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{F}_{1}\omega)(\tau)-(\mathcal{F}_{1}\eta)(\tau)|

\displaystyle=|f(\tau,\omega(\tau))-f(\tau,\eta(\tau))|\leq L|\omega(\tau)-\eta(\tau)|,

This gives,

\|\mathcal{F}_{1}\omega-\mathcal{F}_{1}\eta\|\leq L\|\omega-\eta\|,\;\omega,\eta\in\Omega.

Step 2)
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is completely continuous.

We show that
\mathcal{F}_{2}:\mathcal{S}\to\Omega
is a compact and continuous operator on
\mathcal{S}
into
\Omega
. First we show that
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is continuous on
\mathcal{S}
. Let
\left\{\omega_{n}\right\}

be a sequence in
\mathcal{S}
converging to a point
\omega\in S
. Then by the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem,

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}(\mathcal{F}_{2}\omega_{n})(\tau)

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\left[\dfrac{1-\alpha}{B(\alpha)}g(\tau,\omega_{n}(\tau))+\dfrac{\alpha}{B(\alpha)(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}g(\sigma,\omega_{n}(\sigma))d\sigma\right]

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1-\alpha}{B(\alpha)}\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}g(\tau,\omega_{n}(\tau))+\dfrac{\alpha}{B(\alpha)(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}\left\{\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}g(\sigma,\omega_{n}(\sigma))\right\}d\sigma

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1-\alpha}{B(\alpha)}g(\tau,\omega(\tau))+\dfrac{\alpha}{B(\alpha)(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}g(\sigma,\omega(\sigma))d\sigma

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{F}_{2}\omega)(\tau),

for all
\tau\in J
. This shows that
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is a continuous operator on
\mathcal{S}
.
Using hypothesis (H2), for any
\omega\in\mathcal{S}
and
\tau\in J
, we have

\displaystyle|(\mathcal{F}_{2}\omega)(\tau)|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\dfrac{\omega_{0}}{f(0,\omega_{0})}\right|+\dfrac{1-\alpha}{B(\alpha)}\left|g(\tau,\omega(\tau))\right|+\dfrac{\alpha}{B(\alpha)(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}\left|g(\sigma,\omega(\sigma))\right|d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\left|\dfrac{\omega_{0}}{f(0,\omega_{0})}\right|+\dfrac{1-\alpha}{B(\alpha)}|h(\tau)|+\dfrac{\alpha}{B(\alpha)(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}|h(\sigma)|d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\left|\dfrac{\omega_{0}}{f(0,\omega_{0})}\right|+\dfrac{1-\alpha}{B(\alpha)}\|h\|+\dfrac{\|h\|}{B(\alpha)(1-\alpha)}\tau^{\alpha},

This gives,

|(\mathcal{F}_{2}\omega)(\tau)|\leq\left|\frac{\omega_{0}}{f(0,\omega_{0})}\right|+\left(1-\alpha+\frac{T^{\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\right)\frac{\|h\|}{B(\alpha)},\;\omega\in\mathcal{S},\;\tau\in J,

(3.8)

which shows that
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is uniformly bounded on
J
. Next we prove that
\mathcal{F}_{2}(\mathcal{S})
is equicontinious set in
\Omega
. Let any
\omega\in\mathcal{S}
and
0\leq\tau_{1}<\tau_{2}\leq T
. Then we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}_{2}\omega(\tau_{1})-\mathcal{F}_{2}\omega(\tau_{2})|\leq\dfrac{1-\alpha}{B(\alpha)}\left|g(\tau_{1},\omega(\tau_{1}))-g(\tau_{2},\omega(\tau_{2}))\right|

\displaystyle+\dfrac{\alpha}{B(\alpha)(1-\alpha)}\left|\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}(\tau_{1}-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}g(\sigma,\omega(\sigma))d\sigma-\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}(\tau_{2}-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}g(\sigma,\omega(\sigma))d\sigma\right|

(3.9)

Since

is continuous on compact set

, it is uniformly continuous there and hence we have

\left|g(\tau_{1},\omega(\tau_{1}))-g(\tau_{2},\omega(\tau_{2}))\right|\to 0,\;\text{as}\;|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|\to 0,\;\text{for each}\;\omega\in\mathcal{S}.

(3.10)

Next using hypothesis (H2), we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}(\tau_{1}-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}g(\sigma,\omega(\sigma))d\sigma-\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}(\tau_{2}-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}g(\sigma,\omega(\sigma))d\sigma\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}\left\{(\tau_{1}-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}-(\tau_{2}-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}\right\}|g(\sigma,\omega(\sigma))|d\sigma+\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}(\tau_{2}-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}|g(\sigma,\omega(\sigma))|d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}\left\{(\tau_{1}-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}-(\tau_{2}-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}\right\}|h(\sigma)|d\sigma+\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}(\tau_{2}-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}|h(\sigma)|d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq\|h\|\left(\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}\left\{(\tau_{1}-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}-(\tau_{2}-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}\right\}d\sigma+\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}(\tau_{2}-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}d\sigma\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|h\|\left(\left\{\tau_{1}^{\alpha}+(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{\alpha}-\tau_{2}^{\alpha}\right\}+(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\|h\|(\tau_{2}-\tau_{1})^{\alpha}.

(3.11)

Therefore,

\left|\int_{0}^{\tau_{1}}(\tau_{1}-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}g(\sigma,\omega(\sigma))d\sigma-\int_{\tau_{1}}^{\tau_{2}}(\tau_{2}-\sigma)^{\alpha-1}g(\sigma,\omega(\sigma))d\sigma\right|\to 0,\;\text{as}\;|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|\to 0,\;\omega\in\mathcal{S}.

(3.12)

Therefore it follows from (
3
), (
3.10
) and (

) that

|(\mathcal{F}_{2}\omega)(\tau_{1})-(\mathcal{F}_{2}\omega)(\tau_{2})|\to 0,\;\text{as}\;|\tau_{1}-\tau_{2}|\to 0,\;\text{for each}\;\omega\in\mathcal{S}.

This proves
\mathcal{F}_{2}(\mathcal{S})
is equicontinious set in
\Omega
. Since
\mathcal{F}_{2}(\mathcal{S})
is uniformly bounded and equicontinious set in
\Omega
, by Ascoli-Arzela theorem
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is completely continuous.

\eta\in\mathcal{S}
. For

, consider the operator equation
\omega=\mathcal{F}_{2}\omega\mathcal{F}_{2}\eta
. Our aim is to prove that

.

Using hypothesis (H1) and condition (

\displaystyle|\omega(\tau)|

\displaystyle=|(\mathcal{F}_{1}\omega)(\tau)||(\mathcal{F}_{2}\eta)(\tau)|

\displaystyle\leq|f(\tau,\omega(\tau))||(\mathcal{F}_{2}\eta)(\tau))|

\displaystyle\leq\left\{|f(\tau,\omega(\tau))-f(\tau,0))|+|f(\tau,0)|\right\}\left(\left|\frac{\omega_{0}}{f(0,\omega_{0})}\right|+\left[1-\alpha+\frac{T^{\alpha}}{1-\alpha}\right]\frac{\|h\|}{B(\alpha)}\right)

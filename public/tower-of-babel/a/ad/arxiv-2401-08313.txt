Central extensions of restricted Lie superalgebras and classification of p p -nilpotent Lie superalgebras in dimension 4 4 Thanks: The authors were supported by the grant NYUAD-065.

Abstract
. The first main result of this paper is to build the first and second restricted cohomology groups for restricted Lie superalgebras in characteristic p ≥ 3 p\geq 3 , modifying a construction by Yuan, Chen and Cao. We will explain how these groups capture some algebraic structures, such as extensions and derivations. Further, we apply this construction to classify p p -nilpotent restricted Lie superalgebras up to dimension 4 4 over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p ≥ 3 p\geq 3 .

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Restricted Lie (super)algebras and their cohomology

Over a field of positive characteristic
p>0
, restricted Lie algebras are of prime interest, mainly due to their link to algebraic groups and their role in representation theory and classification (see
[
SF
]
). Their definition goes back to Jacobson (
[
J
]
), who derived it from the study of the space of derivations of a Lie algebra. A given Lie algebra
L
is called
restricted
if it is endowed with a so-called
p
-map
(\cdot)^{[p]}:L\rightarrow L
that behaves roughly like the
p^{\text{th}}
power in associative algebras, see Definition
2.1
. The cohomology associated with restricted Lie algebras is considerably more complicated than the ordinary Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology (see
[
CE
]
). In
[
H
,
H1
,
H2
]
, Hochschild defined the restricted cohomology of a restricted Lie algebra
L
with values in a module
M
by

H^{k}_{\rm{res}}(L;M):=\text{Ext}^{k}_{U_{p}(L)}(\K;M),~k\geq 0,

where
U_{p}(L)
denotes the restricted enveloping algebra of
L
. Although correct, this expression only allows explicit calculations for
k\in\{0,1\}
, in the context of certain extensions (see
[
H
]
). Evans and Fuchs then proposed an explicit construction (for
p\geq 3
) of a cochain complex in
[
E
,
EF
]
, which allows restricted cohomology groups to be computed up to order
2
in the general case. Our understanding of this restricted cohomology is still incomplete, although the work of Evans and Fuchs has led to good cohomological interpretations of certain algebraic phenomena, see
[
EF
]
. This cohomology was used to study central extensions of several classes of restricted Lie algebras; for instance, restricted Witt algebras (see
[
EFP
]
), restricted filiform algebras (see
[
EF2
]
) and restricted affine nilpotent algebras (see
[
EF3
]
). Moreover, formal deformations were investigated in
[
EM
]
. The superization of the notion of restricted Lie algebra is due to Mikhalev (
[
Mi
]
), who called them
p
-superalgebras. Several authors have since studied this notion in various contexts. For instance, identities on the restricted enveloping superalgebras were investigated in
[
P
,
U
]
, double extensions in
[
BBH
,
BEM
]
, descriptions of
p|2p
-maps on simple restricted Lie superalgebras for
p=3
in
[
BKLLS
]
, and representations of the restricted Witt superalgebras and
\text{sl}(n|1)
in
[
SZ
,
Z
]
, respectively.
A generalization of restricted cohomology to restricted Lie superalgebras was investigated in
[
YCC
]
. However, their construction only involves even elements, so it is not suitable to capture central extensions. Therefore, some results, like
[
YCC
, Theorem 3.7]
, have to be amended, see Theorem
3.5.3.  Theorem
. The purpose of this paper is to propose a new definition for restricted cohomology of restricted Lie superalgebras (see Section
3.2
) and investigate some classical interpretations (see mainly Section
3.5
). This paper does not consider any versions of restricted Lie superalgebras for
p=2
as well as the corresponding restricted cohomology.

1.2.
Classification of
p
-nilpotent restricted Lie (super)algebras

A classical question related to the structure theory of Lie algebras is the classification problem. The application of root systems to the classification of semi-simple Lie algebras has been known since Cartan and Killing. In case that the Lie algebra is nilpotent, then the possibility of obtaining an explicit classification (by explicitly giving the multiplication table based on the basis elements) is possible. The classification of nilpotent Lie algebras over any field has existed for a long time up to dimension 5. Over a field of characteristic
0
and dimension
6
, the classification was achieved by Morozov in 1958 (
[
M
]
). In 2012, a complete classification of nilpotent Lie algebras of dimension
6
over an arbitrary field was achieved in (
[
CGS
,
DG
]
). In the case where the Lie algebra is restricted, Schneider and Usefi have investigated
p
-nilpotent restricted Lie algebra of dimension up to
4
over a perfect field (including the case
p=2
) in
[
SU
]
, then Darijani and Usefi tackled the case of the dimension
5
(over a perfect field of characteristic other than
2
) in
[
DU
]
. Nevertheless, some of their results are incorrect, as explained in
[
MS
]
, and their classification lacks some Lie algebras. Maletesta and Siciliano solved the case of the dimension
5
(over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>3
) in
[
MS
]
by using a cohomological method that can be described as follows. As a first result, they showed that any
n
-dimensional
p
-nilpotent restricted Lie algebra can be obtained as a central extension by a restricted
2
-cocycle of a
(n-1)
-dimensional
p
-nilpotent restricted Lie algebra. Cohomologous cocycles lead to isomorphic Lie algebras. As a next step, they classified up to equivalence the restricted
2
-cocycles associated with the
p
-nilpotent restricted Lie algebras obtained in
[
SU
]
and elaborated their central extensions. As a final step, isomorphic algebras need to be removed from the list.
Our paper builds on their work and uses our restricted cohomology for restricted Lie superalgebras to classify
p
-nilpotent restricted Lie superalgebras of dimension
3
and
4
. Our main tool is the concept of central extension, extensively studied in literature; see for example
[
KL
,
N
,
OR
]
in the case of Lie algebras, see
[
BGLL
,
IK
,
Ma
,
YL
,
SZ2
]
in the case of Lie superalgebras, see
[
EF
,
EFP
,
EF2
,
EF3
]
in the case of restricted Lie algebras.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}s_{i}(x,y)

2.3.
p
-nilpotent restricted Lie (super)algebras

Let
L
be a Lie superalgebra. We define a
descending central sequence
by

C^{0}(L)=L,~\text{ and }~C^{k+1}(L)=\bigl[C^{k}(L),L\bigl].

The Lie superalgebra
L
is called

if there exists
k\geq 0
such that
C^{k}(L)=0
. The smallest
k\geq 0
satisfying
C^{k}(L)=0
is called the

of

and
L
is said to be nilpotent of order
k

Suppose that
L
is restricted. Then
L
is called
p
-nilpotent
if there exists

such that

where
x^{[p]^{n}}=(\cdots(x^{[p]})^{[p]}\cdots)^{[p]}
. Any
p
-nilpotent restricted Lie superalgebra is nilpotent, using Engel’s theorem.

3.
Restricted cohomology for restricted Lie superalgebras

Evans and Fuchs constructed the restricted cohomology of restricted Lie algebras up to order
2

[

EF
]

3.1.
Chevalley-Eilenberg cohomology for Lie superalgebras

Let

5.1.
The strategy

Here we present our method for classifying

-dimensional nilpotent Lie superalgebras by means of a suitable

Let

be a restricted Lie superalgebra. We denote by
\mathop{\mathrm{{A\hskip-0.86108ptu\hskip-0.43057ptt\/}}}\nolimits(L)
(resp.
\mathop{\mathrm{{A\hskip-0.86108ptu\hskip-0.43057ptt\/}}}\nolimits_{p}(L)
) the automorphism group (resp. the restricted automorphism group) of

. Let

be a restricted

-module. The restricted

-cocycles space

is endowed with an
\mathop{\mathrm{{A\hskip-0.86108ptu\hskip-0.43057ptt\/}}}\nolimits_{p}(L)
-structure in the following way. Let

and

\mathop{\mathrm{{A\hskip-0.86108ptu\hskip-0.43057ptt\/}}}\nolimits_{p}(L)

\begin{cases}(A\varphi)(x,y)&=\varphi\left(A(x),A(y)\right),~\forall x,y\in L;\\

One can show that the restricted

5.7.
Comparison with the purely cohomological methods

The authors in
[
MS
]
employed the restricted cohomology to obtain
5
-dimensional
p
-nilpotent Lie algebras as restricted central extensions. To build
p
-maps on the extensions, they used equivalence classes of
restricted

2
-cocycles. In the present work, we did not use the “restricted part” of the
2
-cocycles, since the possible
p|2p
structures are completely determined on the even part of the Lie superalgebra. The purpose of this section is to compare our method on an example with the “purely cohomological” method described in
[
MS
]
.

Consider
L={\bf L_{1|2}^{2}}=\left\langle e_{1}|e_{2},e_{3},~[e_{2},e_{3}]=e_{1},~e_{1}^{[p]}=0\right\rangle
. The non-equivalent non-zero (ordinary)
2
-cocycles are
\phi_{1}=\Delta_{2,2}
and
\phi_{2}=\Delta_{2,2}+\Delta_{3,3}
. For
j\in\{1,2\}
, a
p
-homogeneous map
\omega:L_{\bar{0}}\rightarrow\K
is
\phi_{j}
-compatible if

(42)

\omega(x+y)=\omega(x)+\omega(y)+\displaystyle\sum_{\underset{x_{1}=x,~x_{2}=y}{x_{i}\in\{x,y\}}}\frac{1}{\sharp(x)}\phi_{j}\biggl([\cdots[x_{1},x_{2}],x_{3}],\cdots,x_{p-1}],x_{p}\biggl),~\forall x,y\in L.

Suppose that
p>3
. It follows that Eq (
42
) reduces to
\omega(x+y)=\omega(x)+\omega(y)
. Therefore, any
p
-semilinear map
L_{\bar{0}}\rightarrow\K
is
\phi_{j}
-compatible and is of the form
\omega=\alpha e_{1}^{*},~\alpha\in\K
. The “restricted part” of the cocycle condition is given by

(43)

\bigl[e_{1}^{[p]},e_{1}\bigl]=\phi_{j}\Bigl(e_{1},[e_{1},\cdots,[e_{1},e_{1}]\cdots]\Bigl),

must always be satisfied.

5.7.1.
Case
\phi_{1}=\Delta_{2,2}

We compute the equivalences classes of restricted
2
-cocycles. Two restricted cocycles

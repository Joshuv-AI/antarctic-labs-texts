rational θ {\theta} -parallelogram envelopes via θ {\theta} -congruent elliptic curves
Sajad Salami and Arman Shamsi Zargar

Abstract
. We introduce a new generalization of θ {\theta} -congruent numbers by defining the notion of rational θ {\theta} -parallelogram envelope for a positive integer n n , where θ ∈ ( 0 , π ) {\theta}\in(0,\pi) is an angle with rational cosine. Then, we study more closely some problems related to the rational θ {\theta} -parallelogram envelopes, using the arithmetic of algebraic curves. Our results generalize the recent work of T. Ochiai, where only the case θ = π / 2 {\theta}=\pi/2 was considered. Moreover, we answer the open questions in his paper and their generalizations for any Pythagorean angle.

1.
Introduction

A positive integer
n
is called a
congruent number
if
it is equal to the area of a right triangle with rational sides.
Equivalently, if there exist positive rational numbers
a
,
b
, and

c
such that
a<b<c,
and

a^{2}+b^{2}=c^{2},\quad ab=2n.

(1.1)

Determining all the congruent numbers is an old problem in Number Theory.
There are various sorts of generalizations of the problem,
see for example
[
3
,
4
,
10
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
.

In
[
3
]
, M. Fujiwara introduced and studied an interesting generalization of congruent numbers, called
{\theta}
-congruent numbers. Afterwards, several authors (
[
1
,
4
,
6
,
9
,
11
,
12
]
) studied this new concept with different approaches using the arithmetic of elliptic curves
[
10
,
14
]
.
In order to describe this generalization, let
{\theta}\in(0,\pi)
be an angle with rational cosine, i.e., it satisfies
\cos({\theta})=s/r
with
r,s\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
0\leq|s|<r
and
\gcd(r,s)=1
.
Let
{\mathbb{N}}
be the set of natural numbers. An element
n
of
{\mathbb{N}}
is called a
{\theta}
-congruent number
if there exists a triangle with rational sides and area equal to
n\sqrt{r^{2}-s^{2}}
. Equivalently, if there are three positive rational numbers
a
,
b
, and
c
satisfying
a\leq b<c
, and

a^{2}+b^{2}-\frac{2s}{r}ab=c^{2},\quad ab=2rn.

We denote such a triangle by
(a,b,c)
and call it a
rational
{\theta}
-triangle for
n
.
It is clear that if a positive integer
n
is
{\theta}
-congruent with a
{\theta}
-triangle
(a,b,c)
, then

nm^{2}
is also a
{\theta}
-congruent number with the
{\theta}
-triangle
(ma,mb,mc)
.
Hence, one may concentrate on the square-free positive integers.
The problem of determining
{\theta}
-congruent numbers is
related to finding non-2-torsion points on the elliptic curve given by the following Weierstrass equation,

E_{\theta}^{n}:y^{2}=x(x+(r+s)n)(x-(r-s)n),

where
r
and
s
are as above. One can see
[
3
,
4
,
9
]
for more details.

In a recent work, T. Ochiai
[
12
]
generalized the congruent numbers in a novel way.
Indeed, he considered the set
{\mathcal{N}}
of all positive integers
n
such that there is an
envelope for
n
, i.e., a quintuple
(a,b,c,d,e)
of positive rational numbers satisfying

a^{2}+b^{2}=c^{2},\quad a^{2}+d^{2}=e^{2},\quad a(b+d)=n.

(1.2)

We note that any congruent number belongs to the set
{\mathcal{N}}
, because if we let
b=d
, then
Equations (
1.2
) are equivalent to (
1.1
) for
4n
and therefore for
n
. It is proved that
n\in{\mathcal{N}}
if either
n
or
2n
is a congruent number. Moreover, it is shown that

n\in{\mathcal{N}}
if and only if a certain set of algebraic equations
has some rational solutions, see Theorem 2 in
[
12
]
.
Given any positive rational number
m
, T. Ochiai also considered the set
{\mathcal{N}}(m)
consisting of all positive integers
n\in{\mathcal{N}}
such that there exists an envelope
(a,b,c,d,e)
for
n
with
d=mb
.
By studying the set of rational points on certain family of elliptic curves and a genus five algebraic curve,
he provided certain conditions which lead to deciding whether a given positive integer
n
belongs to
{\mathcal{N}}(m)
.
In the end of his paper
[
12
]
, T. Ochiai asked the following questions concerning the sets
{\mathcal{N}}
and
{\mathcal{N}}(m)
.

Question 1.1
.

Notation being as above, one may ask that:

(i)

Is
{\mathcal{N}}={\mathbb{N}}
?

(ii)

Given any
n\in{\mathcal{N}}
, are there infinitely many distinct envelopes for
n
?

(iii)

Given any
n\in{\mathcal{N}}
, are there infinitely many positive rational numbers
m
such that
n\in{\mathcal{N}}(m)
?

In this paper, by generalizing the notion of envelope in T. Ochai’s paper
[
12
]
,
we introduce naturally a real-life geometric object, namely, the rational
{\theta}
-parallelogram envelope for
n
.
This new notion can be viewed as a generalization of
{\theta}
-congruent numbers which involves the rational
{\theta}
-triangles.
We will study the set of
{\theta}
-parallelogram envelopes for positive numbers by investigating the set of rational points on a certainly defined algebraic curve.
Moreover, we shall respond affirmatively to Question
1.1
in a more general setting related to the
set of
{\theta}
-parallelogram envelopes which arises through this study.

2.
The rational
{\theta}
-parallelogram envelopes

Let
{\theta}\in(0,\pi)
be an angle with rational cosine, i.e.,
\cos({\theta})=s/r

with
r,s\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
0\leq|s|<r
and
\gcd(r,s)=1
.
We call
{\theta}
a
Pythagorean angle
if its sine is also rational, say

\sin({\theta})=t/r
for which
0\leq|t|=\sqrt{r^{2}-s^{2}}<r
and
\gcd(r,t)=1
.
Then, we start by the following definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\theta}\in(0,\pi)
be angle with rational cosine.
We define
{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}
to be the set of all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that there exist positive rational numbers
a,b,c,d,
and
e
satisfying

a^{2}+b^{2}-\frac{2s}{r}ab=c^{2},\quad a^{2}+d^{2}+\frac{2s}{r}ad=e^{2},\quad a(b+d)=rn.

(2.1)

For any
n\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}
, we denote such a quintuple of positive rational numbers satisfying Equations (
2.1
)
by
(a,b,c,d,e)_{\theta}
and call it a
rational
{\theta}
-parallelogram envelope for
n
.
This notion with
{\theta}=\pi/2
can be viewed as a generalization of the envelopes
defined by T. Ochiai in
[
12
]
. Figure 1 shows a
{\theta}
-parallelogram envelope for
n
.

Figure 1.
A rational
{\theta}
-parallelogram envelope for
n

Remark 2.2
.

The following facts can be easily deduced from Definition
2.1
.

(1)

A natural number
n
belongs to
{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}
if and only if
nm^{2}\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}
for any integer
m
.
Hence, the area of a rational
{\theta}
-parallelogram envelope for
n
is equal to
n\sqrt{r^{2}-s^{2}}\bmod({\mathbb{Q}}^{*})^{2}
.

(2)

A quintuple
(a,b,c,d,e)_{\theta}
is a rational
{\theta}
-parallelogram envelope for
n
if and only if the quintuple
(a,d,e,b,c)_{\theta}
is a rational
(\pi-{\theta})
-parallelogram envelope for
n
.

(3)

If
n
is both
{\theta}
- and

(\pi-{\theta})
-congruent number, then
n\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}
, but the converse is not true.
For instant,
n=7
is neither a
\pi/3
-congruent number nor a

2\pi/3
-congruent number, but there is a rational
\pi/3
-parallelogram envelope for
n=7
given by

(a,b,c,d,e)_{\frac{\pi}{3}}=\left(\frac{143}{42},\frac{20}{7},\frac{19}{6},\frac{1256}{1001},\frac{3583}{858}\right)_{\frac{\pi}{3}}.

By the above remarks, we restrict our study to the case
{\theta}\in(0,\pi/2]
and the square-free natural numbers
n
throughout this work. Furthermore, by a
{\theta}
-parallelogram envelope for an
n
we always mean the rational one.

In the next definition, we consider a subset of
{\mathcal{N}}_{{\theta}}
which involves the
{\theta}
-parallelogram envelope
(a,b,c,d,e)_{{\theta}}

having an angle
\tau
with rational cosine between the sides
c
and
e
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\tau\in(0,\pi)
be an angle with rational cosine. We denote by
{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}(\tau)
the set
of all
n\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}
such that there exist positive rational numbers
a,b,c,d,
and
e
satisfying
(
2.1
) and

\cos\tau=\frac{a^{2}-bd-sa(b-d)/r}{ce}.

For any
n\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}(\tau)
, we denote such a quintuple by
(a,b,c,d,e)_{\theta}^{\tau}
and
call it a
{\theta}
-parallelogram envelope with an angle
\tau
for
n
.

Figure 2 shows a
{\theta}
-parallelogram envelope with an angle
\tau
for an element
n
of
{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}(\tau)
.

Figure 2.
A
{\theta}
-parallelogram envelope with angle
\tau
for
n

The following theorem, proved in Section
7
, gives a relation between the set
{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}(\tau)
and the
\tau
-congruent numbers.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
{\theta}\in(0,\pi/2]
and
\tau\in(0,\pi)
be angles with rational cosines satisfying

\sin({\theta})/\sin(\tau)\equiv n^{\prime}\ \bmod\ ({\mathbb{Q}}^{*})^{2}
for some positive integer
n^{\prime}
.
Then,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
belongs to
{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}(\tau)
if and only if
2nn^{\prime}
is a
\tau
-congruent number. In particular,
n\in{\mathcal{N}}_{{\theta}}({\theta})
if and only if
2n
is a
{\theta}
-congruent number. Equivalently,

n
is a
{\theta}
-congruent number if and only if
2n\in{\mathcal{N}}_{{\theta}}({\theta})
.

3.
The
{\theta}
-congruent number elliptic curve and the set
{\mathcal{N}}_{{\theta}}

In this section, we provide an algebraically necessary and sufficient condition for
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
to be an element of
{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}
using the
{\theta}
-congruent number elliptic curves.

Theorem 3.1
.

A natural number
n
belongs to
{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}
if and only if the equations,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\hskip-2.84544ptE_{\theta}^{w}:y^{2}=x(x+(r+s)w)(x-(r-s)w),\\
\hskip-2.84544ptF_{\theta}^{N}:v^{2}=u(u-(r+s)N)(u+(r-s)N),\\
\hskip-2.84544ptC_{R}:xv=uy,\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

have a rational solution
(u,v,w,x,y)
where
N=2n-w
,
yv\not=0
and
0<w\leq n
.

Taking
w=n
in Theorem
3.1
leads to the fact that if a natural number
n
is simultaneously
{\theta}
- and (
\pi-{\theta}
)-congruent number,
then
n\in{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}
.
The existence of a rational solution
(u,v,w,x,y)
in Theorem
3.1
means that there exists a natural number
0<w\leq n

such that both the elliptic curves
E_{\theta}^{w}
and
F_{\theta}^{N}
have some non-
2
-torsion points
(x,y)
and
(u,v)
satisfying
x/y=u/v
, where the letter “R” in
C_{R}
refers to the word “Ratio”.
We note that the ratio
b/d
of a
{\theta}
-parallelogram envelope
(a,b,c,d,e)_{\theta}
for
n
depends on the parameter
w
.
For
0<w\leq n
, the point
P_{w}
moves from the point
A_{1}

to the middle point
P_{n}
of the side
A_{1}A_{2}
, see Figure 3 below.

in
{\theta}
-parallelogram envelope for

In order to reduce the number of variables in (

), we let

in

to obtain the following equivalence for Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

A natural number
n
belongs to
{\mathcal{N}}_{\theta}
if and only if the following equations,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\hskip-2.84544ptE_{\theta}^{w}:y^{2}=x(x+(r+s)w)(x-(r-s)w),\\

have a rational solution
(w,x,y,z)

5.
An elliptic curve related to
{\mathcal{N}}_{{\theta},m}

In order to investigate the set of rational points satisfying Equations (

the following two results on the quartic curve
G_{\theta}^{(m,n)}
in
Section

Theorem 5.1
.

The quartic curve
G_{\theta}^{(m,n)}
in the statement of Theorem
4.2
can be birationally transformed into the elliptic curve given by the following cubic equation,

{\mathcal{G}}_{\theta}^{m}:\ {\mathcal{Y}}^{2}={\mathcal{X}}^{3}+(r^{2}m^{2}+2s^{2}m+r^{2}){\mathcal{X}}^{2}+m^{2}(r^{2}-s^{2})^{2}{\mathcal{X}}.

Moreover, for
{\theta}\in(0,\pi/2]
with rational cosine, the rank
r_{\theta}(m)
of the Mordell-Weil group of rational points on

{\mathcal{G}}_{\theta}^{m}
is at least one for all but finitely many
m\in{\mathbb{Q}}
with an independent point given by

{\mathcal{P}}=(-(r^{2}-s^{2})m^{2},s(r^{2}-s^{2})m^{2}(m+1)).

Given
{\theta}\in(0,\pi/2]
with
\cos({\theta})=s/r\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}
, we
define the following quantities:

\begin{array}[]{l}M_{0}=m(r^{2}-s^{2}),\\
M_{1}=r(m+r)\left(r(m+1)+2\sqrt{M_{0}}\right),\\
M_{2}=r(m+r)\left(r(m+1)-2\sqrt{M_{0}}\right).\end{array}

(5.1)

We note that
M_{0}
,
M_{1}
, and
M_{2}
are all positive numbers.
Evidently
M_{0},
and
M_{1}>0
, since
0\leq|s|<r

rm+r-2\sqrt{M_{0}}>0\Longleftrightarrow r^{2}(m+1)^{2}>4m(r^{2}-s^{2})\Longleftrightarrow r^{2}(m-1)^{2}+4ms^{2}>0

In the next result, we determine all possibilities for

{\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{m}}(\mathbb{Q})_{\text{tors}}

\mathcal{G}_{\theta}^{m}

Theorem 5.2
.

Keeping the above notations, we have:

\hskip-2.84544pt\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{2\mathbb{Z}}\times\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{8\mathbb{Z}}&\text{if~}\sqrt{M_{0}},\sqrt{M_{1}},\text{and}~\sqrt{M_{2}}\in{\mathbb{Q}}^{*}\\
\hskip-2.84544pt\displaystyle\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{4\mathbb{Z}}~\text{or}~\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{2\mathbb{Z}}\times\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{4\mathbb{Z}}&\text{otherwise}.\end{array}\right.

The points

are of order
4
and points with the following

-coordinates

\begin{array}[]{l}{\mathcal{X}}_{1}=M_{0}+\left(r(m+1)+\sqrt{M_{1}}\right)\sqrt{M_{0}},\\
{\mathcal{X}}_{2}=M_{0}+\left(r(m+1)-\sqrt{M_{1}}\right)\sqrt{M_{0}},\\
{\mathcal{X}}_{3}=M_{0}-\left(r(m+1)+\sqrt{M_{2}}\right)\sqrt{M_{0}},\\

If

The set

Proof of Theorem

Let

In order to prove the part (i) of Theorem

, one needs to do cumbersome computations using a series of transformations which we only give a sketch and leave the detailed computations to the reader.

8

(x_{i},z_{i})
on
G_{\theta}^{(m,n)}
by (

), and finding

y_{i}
-coordinates on

using
x_{i}
’s, we obtain rational solutions
(x_{i},y_{i},z_{i})
of

4.1
). Calculating

from

and

) and then putting

of Equations (
3.2
). Now, using the correspondence (
8.1
), one may obtain a

{\theta}
-parallelograms envelope with ratio

.

\displaystyle\quad\times\left({\mathcal{X}}^{2}+(r+s)(ms-r){\mathcal{X}}+(r+s){\mathcal{Y}}\right).

If it is necessary, one can choose the sign of
{\mathcal{Y}}
in a way such that

is a positive rational number. To see this, we may assume

.

.

For

{\mathcal{X}}>(r-s)(ms-r)\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{Y}}<0

.

\displaystyle\varphi({\mathcal{X}},{\mathcal{Y}})

\hskip-2.84544pt0&\text{\rm if}\ q=0.\end{array}\right.

Because, if we assume that

-rational points as above.

[
8
]
on the set of rational points on algebraic curves with genus

Step 1:

{\theta}
-parallelogram envelope

\displaystyle a^{2}+b^{2}-c^{2}-\frac{2sab}{r}

Systematic incorporation of the ionic hard-core size into the Debye-Hückel theory via the cumulant expansion of the Schwinger-Dyson equations
Sahin Buyukdagli

Abstract
ABSTRACT: The Debye-Hückel (DH) formalism of bulk electrolytes equivalent to the gaussian-level closure of the electrostatic Schwinger-Dyson (SD) identities without the interionic hard-core (HC) coupling is extended via the cumulant treatment of these equations augmented by HC interactions. By confronting the monovalent ion activity and pressure predictions of our cumulant-corrected DH (CCDH) theory with hypernetted-chain (HNC) results and Monte-Carlo (MC) simulations from the literature, we show that this rectification extends the accuracy of the DH formalism from submolar into molar salt concentrations. In the case of internal energies or the general case of divalent electrolytes mainly governed by charge correlations, the improved accuracy of the CCDH theory is limited to submolar ion concentrations. Comparison with experimental data from the literature shows that via the adjustment of the hydrated ion radii, the CCDH formalism can equally reproduce the non-uniform effect of salt increment on the ionic activity coefficients up to molar concentrations. The inequality satisfied by these HC sizes coincides with the cationic branch of the Hofmeister series.

I
Introduction

The repulsive core interactions between the elementary constituents of matter play a central role in the setting of the stability conditions in the Universe. From the electrostatic forces governing ordinary matter at the molecular scale
Dyson
to the intense gravitational forces dominating astronomical objects
OppenAstro
, HC interactions counterbalancing attractive forces of various origins prevent packed matter from collapsing in on itself at high densities.

In nanoscale systems incorporating charged fluids, electrostatic forces attenuated by HC interactions drive various salt-regulated mechanisms essential to the persistance and evolution of life on Earth, such as the transport of ions and viral pathogens through nuclear pores
Bont
;
Holm
, and the affinity of DNA molecules to biological membranes as well as their stable wrapping around histone proteins
gn1
. The collective effect of electrostatic and HC interactions is also relevant to nanofluidic charge transport and energy conversion techniques
Boc1
;
Boc2
, the functioning of energy storage devices
rev2
, the manufacturing of synthetic fuels
sf
, liquid purification by flocculation
pur
, and water desalination by nanofiltration
Yar
;
Szymczyk
.

In solutions containing ions with typical hydrated sizes, HC interactions becoming substantial at submolar salt concentrations largely dominate the thermodynamics of the liquid in the molar concentration regime
Book
. This hierarchy limits the validity of the predictions by purely electrostatic theories such as the original DH formalism
DH
to dilute salt concentrations. Hence, the characterization of the aforementioned nanoscale systems often involving charged solutes at molar concentrations necessitates the incorporation of the HC and electrostatic interactions into electrolyte models on an equal footing.

In this regard, MC simulation techniques have been particularly reliable tools for accessing the molar concentration regime, thereby enabling the verification of the electrolyte models via direct confrontation with experimental data
Valleau
;
Svensson
;
NetzMC
;
NetzMC2
;
LevinMC
. In addition, classical density functional theories and integral equation formalisms with substantially lower computational cost have proved to be accurate alternatives to MC simulations
Hansen
.

The explicit solution of the Ornstein-Zernike (OZ) equation belonging to the second category requires a closure relation for the pair correlation function. The first technical issue encountered at this level is the lack of a systematic and controlled method for the choice of this approximate closure. Moreover, in the case of charged liquids governed by long-range interactions, closure relations beyond the weak-coupling (WC) random phase and mean-spherical approximations
Blum
;
Henderson
;
Boda
lead to involved solution schemes that can shadow the analytical transparency of the underlying physics
Hoye
;
Hansen
;
Roji
. This indicates the necessity of complementing the integral equation theories by simpler theoretical methods providing analytical insight into the macroscopic effects originating from the intermolecular interactions in the solution.

Along these lines, Attard
AttardPRE
;
AttardJCP
and Kjellander
Kj1
;
Kj2
;
KjJCP2016
;
Kj2020
have developed ingenious theoretical approaches enabling the analytically transparent characterization of the liquid thermodynamics at solute concentrations inaccessible by the DH formalism. Namely, by applying the global electroneutrality constraint and the second moment condition
secmom
to generalized DH-like charge distribution functions, they derived effective screening parameters, and evaluated the thermodynamic functions of bulk liquids in the large density regime governed by the close competition between electrostatic and HC correlations. The accuracy of these generalized DH formalisms has been confirmed via extensive comparison of their predictions with MC simulations and the quasi-exact numerical solution of the OZ equation with HNC closures. In particular, Kjellander’s modified DH theory has been shown to reproduce accurately the Kirkwood transition
Kirk1
;
Kirk2
from the exponentially decaying to the damped oscillatory regime of the charge distribution function
Kj2020
;
NetzMC
;
AttardPRE
;
AttardJCP
.

In the context of conventional statistical mechanics, the field theory approach to electrostatic interactions has emerged as an efficient and convenient alternative to the aforementioned calculation techniques
PodWKB
. Owing to its formulation as an explicit partition function, the technical advantages provided by the field theory framework are numerous. First of all, this approach allows the treatment of many-body interactions via systematic and controlled perturbation techniques
NetzLoop
;
NetzSC
;
NetzVir
;
Podgornik2010
as well as self-consistent computation schemes
netzvar
;
HatloVar
;
Buyuk2020
. Then, the field theory formalism enables the straightforward incorporation of the specific molecular details of electrolytes beyond the primitive model, such as structured solute charges
Demery
;
Buyuk2023
, explicit solvent molecules
DuncSolv
;
NL1
, structured membranes
dipmem
, and polyelectrolytes with conformational degrees of freedom
RudiPol
;
NetzPol
.

Due to the onset of the competition between the electrostatic and HC interactions at submolar salt concentrations
Book
, the validity of the field-theoretic models neglecting the
pairwise HC interactions
is limited to dilute salt solutions. In order to overcome this limitation, in this work, we formulate a field-theoretic calculation scheme that enables the evaluation of thermodynamic averages by treating the Coulombic and HC interactions between all ion species on an equal footing. The corresponding CCDH formalism is based on the
cumulant expansion
of the liquid partition function together with the SD identities
justin
. In our earlier works, the
virial expansion
of these identities

whose validity requires the dilute salt condition has been used to characterize correlation effects in nanofluidic ion transport
Heyden2005
;
JPCB2020
, polymer translocation
BuyukLang2022
, and salt-induced dielectric decrement in polar liquids
BuyukDielDec
. The cumulant expansion method adopted in the present work allows us to avoid precisely the dilute salt constraint accompanying the virial approximation used in the aforementioned studies.

By confronting the CCDH formalism with MC and HNC data from the literature
NetzMC
;
Svensson
;
Valleau
;
AttardPRE
, we show that the incorporation of the HC ion size extends the accuracy of the DHLLs for the osmotic pressure and the ionic activity coefficients of monovalent salt solutions from submolar into molar salt concentrations. This substantial upgrade is the main progress of our work. Throughout the article, we also identify systematically the limitations of our cumulant calculation scheme, and elaborate potential improvements and applications in Conclusions.

II
Theory

We introduce here our electrolyte model, and derive its partition function in the form of a functional integral over fluctuating electrostatic and HC potentials. Then, by exploiting the invariance of this partition function with respect to the variation of the electrostatic potential, we derive the electrostatic SD equations. By solving the resulting identities via a first order cumulant expansion, we obtain the electrostatic Green’s function, and calculate the two point distribution functions characterizing ion correlations. In Sec.
III.1
, these distribution functions will be used for the computation of the excess energy, the pressure, and the ionic activity coefficients.

II.1
Electrolyte model and liquid partition function

The electrolyte model is composed of
p
ion species located in an implicit solvent of uniform permittivity
\varepsilon_{\rm w}
. The ions of the species
i
with fugacity
\lambda_{i}
and concentration
n_{i}
are point charges of valency
q_{i}
placed at the center of a HC sphere with diameter
d
. Thus, the interactions between two ions separated by the distance
r
are set by the HC potential
v_{\rm h}(r)
defined for all species as

e^{-v_{\rm h}(\mathbf{r})}=\theta(r-d),

(1)

where
\theta(x)
is the Heaviside step function
math
, and the bulk Coulomb potential
v_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})=\ell_{\rm B}/r
characterized by the Bjerrum length
\ell_{\rm B}=e^{2}/(4\pi\varepsilon_{\rm w}k_{\rm B}T)
, where
e
stands for the electron charge,
k_{B}
is the Boltzmann constant, and
T
is the liquid temperature.

We derive now the partition function of this electrolyte in a functional integral form. The grand canonical (GC) partition function of the liquid is given by the trace of the Boltzmann distribution function over the particle positions and number fluctuations,

\displaystyle Z_{\rm G}=\prod_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{N_{i}=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{i}^{N_{i}}}{N_{i}!}\prod_{j=1}^{p}\prod_{k=1}^{N_{j}}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}_{jk}e^{-\beta(E_{\rm m}+E_{\rm n})}.

(2)

In Eq. (
2
), the pairwise ionic interaction energy is

\displaystyle\beta E_{\rm m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{i}}\sum_{l=1}^{N_{j}}\left[q_{i}q_{j}v_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r}_{ik}-\mathbf{r}_{jl})\right.

(3)

\displaystyle\hskip 75.39963pt\left.+v_{\rm h}(\mathbf{r}_{ik}-\mathbf{r}_{jl})\right].

Moreover, the single-body energy component reads

\beta E_{\rm n}=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{i}}w_{i}(\mathbf{r}_{ij})-\sum_{i=1}^{p}N_{i}\epsilon_{i},

(4)

where the external potential
w_{i}(\mathbf{r})
will enable us to derive the density distributions, and the self-energy component

\epsilon_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\left[q_{i}^{2}v_{\rm c}(0)+v_{\rm h}(0)\right]

(5)

has been subtracted from the Hamiltonian.

Using now the charge and number density operators

\hat{n}_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{i}}q_{i}\delta^{3}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{ij});\;\hat{n}_{\rm h}(\mathbf{r})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\sum_{j=1}^{N_{i}}\delta^{3}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}_{ij}),

(6)

the pairwise interaction energy (
3
) can be recast as

\displaystyle\beta E_{\rm m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d^{3}}\mathbf{r}\mathrm{d^{3}}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\left[\hat{n}_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})v_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\hat{n}_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\right.

(7)

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 56.9055pt+\hat{n}_{\rm h}(\mathbf{r})v_{\rm h}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\hat{n}_{\rm h}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\right].

Next, we switch from the particle density to the field representation via an Hubbard-Stratonovich (HS) transformation for each type of pairwise interaction,

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\hat{n}_{\gamma}(\mathbf{r})v_{\gamma}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\hat{n}_{\gamma}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}

(8)

\displaystyle=\int\mathcal{D}\psi_{\gamma}\;e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\psi_{\gamma}(\mathbf{r})v^{-1}_{\gamma}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\psi_{\gamma}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})}e^{i\int\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}c_{\gamma}(\mathbf{r})\psi_{\gamma}(\mathbf{r})},

where
\psi_{\gamma}(\mathbf{r})
stand for the fluctuating potentials associated with the Coulomb (
\gamma={\rm c}
) or HC coupling (
\gamma={\rm h}
). This transformation enables us to evaluate exactly the geometric sum in Eq. (
2
), and to recast the GC partition function as a double functional integral of the form

Z_{\rm G}=\int\mathcal{D}\mathbf{\Psi}\;e^{-\beta H[\mathbf{\Psi}]},

(9)

where we used the shorthand vector notations for the fluctuating potentials
\mathbf{\Psi}=(\psi_{\rm c},\psi_{\rm h})
and the functional integration measure
\mathcal{D}\mathbf{\Psi}=\mathcal{D}\psi_{\rm c}\mathcal{D}\psi_{\rm h}
.

In Eq. (
9
), the Hamiltonian functional is defined as

\displaystyle\beta H[\mathbf{\Psi}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\gamma={\rm c,h}}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\psi_{\gamma}(\mathbf{r})v^{-1}_{\gamma}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\psi_{\gamma}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})

(10)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{p}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}\;\hat{n}_{i}(\mathbf{r}).

The first term on the r.h.s. of Eq. (
10
) incorporates the energy of the quadratic potential fluctuations. Then, the second line of Eq. (
10
) includes the fluctuating ion density

\hat{n}_{i}(\mathbf{r})=\lambda_{i}e^{\epsilon_{i}-w_{i}(\mathbf{r})}e^{i\psi_{\rm h}(\mathbf{r})+iq_{i}\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})}.

(11)

Unless stated otherwise, in the remainder, we will set
w_{i}(\mathbf{r})=0
.

II.2
Derivation of the electrostatic SD identities

We review here the derivation of the SD equations
justin
introduced for charged systems in Ref.
Buyuk2020
. To this aim, we define first the functional integral

I=\int\mathcal{D}\mathbf{\Psi}\;e^{-\beta H[\mathbf{\Psi}]}F[\mathbf{\Psi}]

(12)

including the general functional
F[\mathbf{\Psi}]
. Next, we introduce an infinitesimal shift of the electrostatic potential,
\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})\to\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})+\delta\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})
, and linearize Eq. (
12
) in terms of the infinitesimally small function
\delta\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})
. The variation of the integral follows in the form

\displaystyle\delta I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}\delta\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})\int\mathcal{D}\mathbf{\Psi}e^{-\beta H[\mathbf{\Psi}]}

(13)

\displaystyle\hskip 65.44142pt\times\left\{\frac{\delta F[\mathbf{\Psi}]}{\delta\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})}-F[\mathbf{\Psi}]\frac{\delta H[\mathbf{\Psi}]}{\delta\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})}\right\}.

At this point, we exploit the invariance of the integral (
12
) under the potential shift
\delta\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})
. Thus, setting Eq. (
13
) to zero, i.e.
\delta I=0
, and dividing the result by the partition function (
9
), one obtains

\left\langle\frac{\delta F[\mathbf{\Psi}]}{\delta\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})}\right\rangle=\left\langle F[\mathbf{\Psi}]\frac{\delta H[\mathbf{\Psi}]}{\delta\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})}\right\rangle,

(14)

where the field average of the general functional
F[\mathbf{\Psi}]
is

\left\langle F[\mathbf{\Psi}]\right\rangle=\frac{1}{Z_{\rm G}}\int\mathcal{D}\mathbf{\Psi}\;e^{-\beta H[\mathbf{\Psi}]}F[\mathbf{\Psi}].

(15)

Finally, specifying the form of the functional in Eq. (
14
) as
F[\mathbf{\Psi}]=1
and
F[\mathbf{\Psi}]=\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
, the formally exact SD equations respectively follow as

\left\langle\frac{\delta\left(\beta H[\mathbf{\Psi}]\right)}{\delta\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})}\right\rangle=0;\hskip 14.22636pt\left\langle\frac{\delta\left(\beta H[\mathbf{\Psi}]\right)}{\delta\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})}\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\right\rangle=\delta^{3}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime}).

(16)

In order to simplify the notation, from now on, the argument of the functionals will be omitted.

II.3
Cumulant expansion scheme

Due to the non-linear form of the Hamiltonian functional (
10
), the statistical averages defined by Eq. (
15
) cannot be calculated analytically. Thus, we will evaluate these averages within a cumulant expansion scheme. This cumulant approximation is based on the exact splitting of the Hamiltonian into two components as

H=H_{0}+t\delta H,

(17)

with the gaussian component

\displaystyle\beta H_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}{2}\left[\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})G^{-1}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\right.

(18)

\displaystyle\left.\hskip 49.79231pt+\psi_{\rm h}(\mathbf{r})v^{-1}_{\rm h}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\psi_{\rm h}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\right],

and the non-linear part to be treated perturbatively,

\displaystyle\beta\delta H

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}{2}\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})\left[v_{\rm c}^{-1}-G^{-1}\right]_{\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r}^{\prime}}\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})

(19)

\displaystyle-\sum_{i=1}^{p}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}\;\hat{n}_{i}(\mathbf{r}).

In Eq. (
17
), the parameter
t
to be set to unity will allow to keep track of the cumulant expansion order. Moreover, the unknown electrostatic kernel
G(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})
will be determined from the solution of the SD identities in Eq. (
16

The main approximations underlying this cumulant approach are (i) the choice of the reference Hamiltonian (
18
) as a purely gaussian functional, which assumes the higher order non-linear fluctuations included in the perturbative correction (
19

v^{-1}_{\rm h}(\mathbf{r})
in Eq. (
18
). In the remainder, the consequences and the validity limits of these approximations will be identified via comparison with MC simulations and HNC results.

Following this cumulant expansion scheme, we substitute now the decomposition (
17
) into Eq. (
15

O(t)
. The statistical average of the general functional

reduces to

\left\langle F\right\rangle=\left\langle F\right\rangle_{0}-t\left[\left\langle\beta\delta HF\right\rangle_{0}-\left\langle\beta\delta H\right\rangle_{0}\left\langle F\right\rangle_{0}\right]+O\left(t^{2}\right),

(20)

where the gaussian-level average is

\left\langle F\right\rangle_{0}=\frac{1}{Z_{0}}\int\mathcal{D}\mathbf{\Psi}\;e^{-\beta H_{0}}F,

(21)

with the partition function
Z_{0}=\int\mathcal{D}\mathbf{\Psi}\;e^{-\beta H_{0}}
. Owing to the gaussian form of the Hamiltonian

included in Eq. (

\displaystyle\left\langle e^{i\int\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}\left[J_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})\psi_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})+J_{\rm h}(\mathbf{r})\psi_{\rm h}(\mathbf{r})\right]}\right\rangle_{0}

(22)

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{r}^{\prime}\left[J_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r})G(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})J_{\rm c}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})+J_{\rm h}(\mathbf{r})v_{\rm h}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}^{\prime})J_{\rm h}(\mathbf{r}^{\prime})\right]}

including the generating functions

To recapitulate, the generality of the CCDH scheme with respect to the DH theory is due to the incorporation of the HC interactions in the reference Hamiltonian (

The limitations of the calculation scheme underlying our formalism have been meticulously assessed. The first limitation of our approach originates from the cumulant treatment of the bare HC interactions equivalent to the second order virial expansion of the CS identities.

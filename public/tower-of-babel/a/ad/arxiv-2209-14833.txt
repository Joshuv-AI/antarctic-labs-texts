Dimensions of Higher Order Factor Analysis Models
Muhammad Ardiyansyah, Luca Sodomaco

Abstract
. The factor analysis model is a statistical model where a certain number of hidden random variables, called factors, affect linearly the behaviour of another set of observed random variables, with additional random noise. The main assumption of the model is that the factors and the noise are Gaussian random variables. This implies that the feasible set lies in the cone of positive semidefinite matrices. In this paper, we do not assume that the factors and the noise are Gaussian, hence the higher order moment and cumulant tensors of the observed variables are generally nonzero. This motivates the notion of k k th-order factor analysis model, that is the family of all random vectors in a factor analysis model where the factors and the noise have finite and possibly nonzero moment and cumulant tensors up to order k k . This subset may be described as the image of a polynomial map onto a Cartesian product of symmetric tensor spaces. Our goal is to compute its dimension and we provide conditions under which the image has positive codimension.

1.
Introduction

In statistics, factor analysis is a method for describing statistical models where the involved random variables can be arranged into two distinct groups. On one hand, one considers a vector
X\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
of
observed
random variables
X_{i}
. On the other hand, the variables
X_{i}
are conditionally independent given another vector
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
of
hidden
or
latent
random variables
Y_{j}
, usually called
factors
. In applications, it is common that the number of factors
m
is considerably smaller than the number of observed variables
n
. Factor analysis may be addressed as a dimension reduction technique where the number of dimensions is specified by the user, see
[
Spe04
,
Spe27
]
.
In those two papers, Charles Spearman introduced the concept of the factor analysis model.
Spearman noticed the huge variety of measures for cognitive study, including visuo-spatial skills, artistic abilities, and reasoning. Through the factor analysis model, he was curious if the underlying general intelligence variable, which is called the “g” factor, and specific abilities variable, which is called the “s” factor, could explain them all.
In applications, factor analysis is used in many fields such as behavioral and social sciences
[
Bra20
]
, medical sciences
[
SD89
]
, economics
[
BHD15
]
, and geography
[
CDJ74
]
as a result of the technological advancements of computers.

In general, statistical models rely on certain sets of assumptions.
In the case of factor analysis model, each variable
X_{i}
is a linear combination of the factors
Y_{j}
with some independent noise, namely

(1.1)

X=\Lambda Y+\varepsilon\,

for some unknown coefficient matrix
\Lambda=(\lambda_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m}
, whose entries are sometimes referred as
factor loadings
, and for some “noise” random vector
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
.
In particular, several observed variables
X_{i}
might be measures of the same factor
Y_{j}
.
The factor analysis model may be regarded as a special instance of a much more general graphical model, where the components of a certain random vector
Z
interact with each other, and their interaction is encoded by the edges of a directed acyclic graph with vertex set equal to the components of
Z

[
RS21
]
. In our setting, the random vector
Z
is the joint vector
(X,Y)
, and the interactions between
X
and
Y
are described by a directed bipartite graph where all edges are directed from elements of
Y
to elements of
X
, as in Figure
1
.

.

Frequently, the parametric representations of a statistical model are useful for statistical inferences. In factor analysis model, the vector
Y
and the noise vector
\varepsilon
are assumed to be Gaussian with mean zero. Therefore, all the information about
X
is encoded by its covariance matrix, which can be written as the sum of a positive diagonal matrix and a symmetric matrix of a certain rank.
These considerations produce the parametrization of the factor analysis model in
[
DSS07
, Proposition 1]
.

In this paper we drop the assumption of Gaussianity of the factors and of the noise vector. In particular, in order to understand all the statistical information about the observed variables
X_{i}
we need to consider the higher order moment and cumulant tensors of
X
.
We define the
k
th-order cumulant (moment) factor analysis model
to be the set of tuples of length
k-1
of symmetric tensors of orders
2,3,\ldots,k
that are the cumulant (moment) tensors of some random vector
X
of observed variables, see Definition
2.9
.
Since we are assuming that the factors
Y_{i}
are mutually independent, we have that all higher order cumulant tensors of
Y
are diagonal tensors. The same property does not hold for moments of order higher than 3. Therefore, similarly as in the more general linear non-Gaussian graphical model studied in
[
RS21
, Definition 4]
, the higher order cumulants of the vector of observed variables
X
have a nice polynomial parametrization as the sum of a diagonal tensor and a symmetric tensor obtained as the Tucker product between a diagonal tensor and a rectangular matrix. This parametrization is defined rigorously in (
3.2
).
Using the described parametrization, we are able to compute the dimension of the
k
th-order cumulant factor analysis model, which for
k=2
was computed in
[
DSS07
, Section 2]
.
What is more, using the relations in (
2.2
) and in (
2.3
), we can polynomially map the
k
th-order cumulant factor analysis model to the
k
th-order moment factor analysis model, and vice versa.
In particular, the dimensions of the two models coincide. For this reason, we may talk about the
dimension of the
k
th-order factor analysis model
.
The dimension clearly depends on the maximal order
k
of cumulant or moment tensors considered, on the number
p
of observed variables, and on the number
m
of factors. In particular, we provide conditions on these parameters under which the
k
th-order factor analysis model has positive codimension, namely when the previous parametrization is “non-filling”.

One of the most important tasks in statistical modeling is to perform model selection which is the set of rules to select the best model among a set of candidate models. In model selection, we assign a score to each model that depends on its log-likelihood and number of parameters and then we choose the model with the lowest score. The most popular model selection criteria include Akaike Information Criterion (AIC), Hannan-Quinn Information Criterion (HQIC), and Bayesian Information Criterion (BIC)
[
Aka98
,
BA04
,
Sug78
,
Sch78
]
. Therefore, it is crucial to know the dimension of a statistical model. Moreover, knowing the codimension of the model is more desirable since it can be used to measure the complexity of a statistical model.

The paper is organized as follows. After setting up notations, we recall some important properties of moment and cumulant tensors of random vectors, and we define the higher order factor analysis model.
In Section
3
, we compute the dimension of the
k
th-order factor analysis model in Theorem
3.1
. Secondly, we provide conditions on the parameters
k
,
p
, and
m
such that the
k
th-order factor analysis model has positive codimension in the space of tuples of length
k-1
of symmetric tensors of degrees
2,3,\ldots,k
. Finally, we provide a Macaulay2 code
[
GS97
]
to verify numerically the previous formulas.

2.
Preliminaries on higher order factor analysis models

First, we set up the main notations used throughout the paper.

Notation 2.1
.

For any integer
n\geq 1
, we denote by
[n]
the set
\{1,\ldots,n\}
. The ground field used throughout the paper is mainly the field of real numbers
\mathbb{R}
, although the preliminary definitions may be stated for an arbitrary field
\mathbb{K}
. By a
tensor of format
m_{1}\times\cdots\times m_{r}
with entries in
\mathbb{K}
we mean an
r
-dimensional array
\mathcal{T}=(t_{i_{1}\cdots i_{r}})
filled with entries in
\mathbb{K}
. The vector space of such tensors is usually denoted by
\mathbb{K}^{m_{1}\times\cdots\times m_{r}}
and is isomorphic to the tensor product
\bigotimes_{j=1}^{r}\mathbb{K}^{m_{j}}
. Assuming
m_{1}=\cdots=m_{r}=m
, we say that a tensor
\mathcal{T}\in(\mathbb{K}^{m})^{\otimes r}
is
symmetric
if its entries
t_{i_{1}\cdots i_{r}}
do not change after a permutation of the indices
i_{1},\ldots,i_{r}
. Symmetric tensors in
(\mathbb{K}^{m})^{\otimes r}
form a subspace denoted by
\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(\mathbb{K}^{m})
. Furthermore, we denote by
\Delta^{r}(\mathbb{K}^{m})
the subset of
\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(\mathbb{K}^{m})
of diagonal tensors. In particular, the subset
\Delta^{r}(\mathbb{K}^{m})
can be identified with
\mathbb{K}^{m}
.

Definition 2.2
.

The
r
th-order
moment
tensor
\mathcal{M}_{X}^{(r)}\in(\mathbb{R}^{p})^{\otimes r}
of a random vector
X\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is defined by the element-wise equation

(2.1)

(\mathcal{M}_{X}^{(r)})_{j_{1}\cdots j_{r}}=\mathrm{mom}(X_{j_{1}},\ldots,X_{j_{r}})=\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{1}}\cdots X_{j_{r}}]\,.

Definition 2.3
.

The
r
th-order
cumulant
tensor
\mathcal{C}_{X}^{(r)}\in(\mathbb{R}^{p})^{\otimes r}
of a random vector
X\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
is defined by the element-wise equation

(2.2)

(\mathcal{C}_{X}^{(r)})_{j_{1}\cdots j_{r}}=\mathrm{cum}(X_{j_{1}},\ldots,X_{j_{r}})=\sum_{(A_{1},\ldots,A_{L})}(-1)^{L-1}(L-1)!\prod_{i=1}^{L}\mathrm{mom}((X_{j})_{j\in A_{i}})\,,

where the sum in (
2.2
) is taken with respect to all partitions
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{L}\}
of the set
\{j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}\}
.

Remark 2.4
.

Suppose that all moment tensors
\mathcal{M}_{X}^{(r)}
of
X
are known up to order
k
. Then equation (
2.2
) allows us to compute all
r
th-order cumulant tensors
\mathcal{C}_{X}^{(r)}
of
X
, up to order
k
. What is more, equation (
2.2
) defines a polynomial map from the set of moments up to order
k
to the set of cumulants up to order
k
of
X
. This polynomial map has a polynomial inverse, which is defined by the element-wise equation (see
[
McC87
, Section 2.3.4]
for further details)

(2.3)

(\mathcal{M}_{X}^{(r)})_{j_{1}\cdots j_{r}}=\mathrm{mom}(X_{j_{1}},\ldots,X_{j_{r}})=\sum_{(A_{1},\ldots,A_{L})}\prod_{i=1}^{L}\mathrm{cum}((X_{j})_{j\in A_{i}})\,.

Given a real random vector
X\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
, the first three cumulant tensors of
X
are defined by the identities

(2.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\mathcal{C}_{X}^{(1)})_{j}&=\mathbb{E}[X_{j}]\,,\\
(\mathcal{C}_{X}^{(2)})_{j_{1}j_{2}}&=\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{1}}X_{j_{2}}]-\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{1}}]\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{2}}]\,,\\
(\mathcal{C}_{X}^{(3)})_{j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}}&=\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{1}}X_{j_{2}}X_{j_{3}}]-\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{1}}]\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{2}}X_{j_{3}}]-\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{2}}]\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{1}}X_{j_{3}}]\\
&\quad-\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{3}}]\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{1}}X_{j_{2}}]+2\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{1}}]\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{2}}]\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{3}}]\,.\\
\end{split}

If additionally we impose that
X
has mean zero, namely
\mathbb{E}[X_{i}]=0
for all
i\in[p]
, we see immediately from the previous identities that
\mathcal{C}_{X}^{(r)}=\mathcal{M}_{X}^{(r)}
for
r\in[3]
. Instead, the two tensors are in general different for
r\geq 4
, as we can see from the expression of the fourth cumulant tensor when
X
has mean zero:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\mathcal{C}_{X}^{(4)})_{j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}j_{4}}&=\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{1}}X_{j_{2}}X_{j_{3}}X_{j_{4}}]-\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{1}}X_{j_{2}}]\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{3}}X_{j_{4}}]\\
&\quad-\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{1}}X_{j_{3}}]\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{2}}X_{j_{4}}]-\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{1}}X_{j_{4}}]\mathbb{E}[X_{j_{2}}X_{j_{3}}]\,.\end{split}

It is almost immediate to check from their definitions that both moment tensors and cumulant tensors are symmetric tensors. In order to state another important property shared by moment and cumulant tensors, we need to recall a natural operation between tensors and matrices that generalizes the classical operation of matrix multiplication.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a tensor of format
m_{1}\times\cdots\times m_{r}
with entries in a field
\mathbb{K}
. For every
\ell\in[r]
consider a matrix
U_{\ell}=(u_{ij}^{(\ell)})\in\mathbb{K}^{m_{\ell}\times p_{\ell}}
. The
Tucker product
(or
multilinear multiplication
) of
\mathcal{T}
by
(U_{1},\ldots,U_{r})
is the tensor
\mathcal{T}\bullet(U_{1},\ldots,U_{r})
of format
p_{1}\times\cdots\times p_{r}
whose entry
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r})
is

(\mathcal{T}\bullet(U_{1},\ldots,U_{r}))_{i_{1}\cdots i_{r}}=\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{m_{1}}\cdots\sum_{j_{r}=1}^{m_{r}}t_{j_{1}\cdots j_{r}}u_{j_{1}i_{1}}^{(1)}\cdots u_{j_{r}i_{r}}^{(r)}\,.

In particular for
r=2
we have that
\mathcal{T}\bullet(U_{1},U_{2})=U_{1}^{T}\,\mathcal{T}\,U_{2}
. If
m_{1}=\cdots=m_{r}=m
and
p_{1}=\cdots=p_{r}=p
, we use the shorthand
\mathcal{T}\bullet^{r}U
to denote the Tucker product
\mathcal{T}\bullet(U,\ldots,U)
, where
U\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times p}
is repeated
r
times.

Example 2.6
.

In our paper, we will have
m_{1}=\cdots=m_{r}=m
,
p_{1}=\cdots=p_{r}=p
, and we will compute the Tucker product of a diagonal tensor
\mathcal{D}\in\Delta^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
by the transpose of the matrix
\Lambda=(\lambda_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m}
introduced in (
1.1
). If we denote by
\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{m}
the diagonal entries of
\mathcal{D}
, then

(2.6)

(\mathcal{D}\bullet^{r}\Lambda^{T})_{i_{1}\cdots i_{r}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{m}\delta_{\ell}\,\lambda_{i_{1},\ell}\cdots\lambda_{i_{r},\ell}\,.

The tensor
\mathcal{D}\bullet^{r}\Lambda^{T}
is symmetric, so it suffices to study the entries with multi-index
(i_{1},\ldots,i_{r})
such that
i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{r}
. Without loss of generality, we will assume that
\Lambda
is a lower-triangular matrix, that is
\lambda_{ij}=0
if
j>i
. Then

(2.7)

(\mathcal{D}\bullet^{r}\Lambda^{T})_{i_{1}\cdots i_{r}}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\min\{m,i_{1}\}}\delta_{\ell}\,\lambda_{i_{1},\ell}\cdots\lambda_{i_{r},\ell}\quad\forall\,i_{1}\leq\cdots\leq i_{r}\,.

In the following proposition we recall some important properties of cumulant tensors, which are discussed more in detail in
[
CJ10
, Chapter 5]
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
X,Z\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
,
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
be random vectors.

(a)

If
X=\Lambda Y
for some matrix
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m}
, then

(2.8)

\mathcal{C}_{X}^{(r)}=\mathcal{C}_{Y}^{(r)}\bullet^{r}\Lambda^{T}\quad\forall\,r\geq 1\,.

(b)

If
X,Z\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
are mutually independent, then

(2.9)

\mathcal{C}_{X+Z}^{(r)}=\mathcal{C}_{X}^{(r)}+\mathcal{C}_{Z}^{(r)}\quad\forall\,r\geq 1\,.

(c)

If the components
X_{i}
of
X
are mutually independent, then
\mathcal{C}_{X}^{(r)}
is a diagonal tensor for all
r\geq 1
, namely
(\mathcal{C}_{X}^{(r)})_{j_{1}\cdots j_{r}}\neq 0
only if
j_{1}=\cdots=j_{r}
.

It is worth mentioning that property
(a)
is valid also when
\mathcal{C}_{X}^{(r)}
and
\mathcal{C}_{Y}^{(r)}
are replaced by the moment tensors
\mathcal{M}_{X}^{(r)}
and
\mathcal{M}_{Y}^{(r)}
. Instead, properties
(b)
and
(c)
are not valid in general for moment tensors of order
r\geq 3
.

The statistical model we consider in this paper is the factor analysis model described via equation (
1.1
) in the introduction, where all correlations among the coordinates of a random vector
X\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
are due to another random vector
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
whose components are usually called
factors
. Correlations between
X
and
Y
are encoded by the coefficient matrix
\Lambda=(\lambda_{ij})\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m}
, and by the random vector
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
.

A fundamental assumption on the (classical) factor analysis model is that
Y
and
\varepsilon
are random
Gaussian
vectors. In this paper we want to drop this assumption, hence we allow distributions with nonzero moments or cumulants up to order
k\geq 2
. For this reason we consider the following modified model.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
k\geq 2
be an integer. A
k
th-order factor analysis model
is a family of random vectors
X
of observed variables that are correlated to another vector
Y
of hidden variables (called factors) via equation (
1.1
), where
\varepsilon
is a noise component. The model relies on the following assumptions:

(1)

All moment and cumulant tensors of
Y
and
\varepsilon
exist and are finite up to order
k
.

(2)

The vectors
Y
and
\varepsilon
are independent each other.

(3)

The components of
Y
are mutually independent, and similarly for
\varepsilon
.

(4)

The vectors
Y
and
\varepsilon
have mean equal to zero.

The last assumption in Definition
2.8
and the relations in (
2.4
) imply that
\mathcal{C}_{Y}^{(r)}=\mathcal{M}_{Y}^{(r)}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{(r)}=\mathcal{M}_{\varepsilon}^{(r)}
for all
r\in[3]
, therefore
\mathcal{C}_{X}^{(r)}=\mathcal{M}_{X}^{(r)}
for all
r\in[3]
.
This is no longer true for
k\geq 4
.

Definition
2.8
has a natural counterpart in terms of cumulant and moment tensors.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
p
,
m
and
k
be nonnegative integers with
k\geq 2
.

(a)

The
k
th-order cumulant factor analysis model
is the subset of tuples
(\mathcal{C}^{(2)},\ldots,\mathcal{C}^{(k)})
of symmetric tensors
\mathcal{C}^{(r)}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{p})
that are the cumulant tensors for some random vector
X\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
in the
k
th-order factor analysis model. We denote this subset by
\mathcal{C}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m}
.

(b)

The
k
th-order moment factor analysis model
is the subset of tuples
(\mathcal{M}^{(2)},\ldots,\mathcal{M}^{(k)})
of symmetric tensors
\mathcal{M}^{(r)}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{p})
that are the moment tensors for some random vector
X\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
in the
k
th-order factor analysis model. We denote this subset by
\mathcal{M}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m}
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
p
,
m
and
k
be nonnegative integers with
k\geq 2
. Consider an element
(\mathcal{C}^{(2)},\ldots,\mathcal{C}^{(k)})\in\mathcal{C}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m}
. Then for all
r\in\{2,\ldots,k\}

(2.10)

\mathcal{C}^{(r)}=\mathcal{D}^{(r)}\bullet^{r}\Lambda^{T}+\mathcal{E}^{(r)}

for some
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m}
,
\mathcal{D}^{(r)}\in\Delta^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{m})
, and
\mathcal{E}^{(r)}\in\Delta^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{p})
.
Furthermore, for
r=2
the diagonal matrices
\mathcal{D}^{(2)}
and
\mathcal{E}^{(2)}
are positive semidefinite.

Proof.

Suppose that, for some
r\in\{2,\ldots,k\}
, the tensor
\mathcal{C}^{(r)}
is the
r
th-order cumulant tensor
\mathcal{C}_{X}^{(r)}
of some random vector
X\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
in the
k
th-order factor analysis model.
The second assumption in Definition
2.8
, together with Propositions
2.7
(a)
and
2.7
(b)
, say that the linear relation (
1.1
) implies the following relation between
\mathcal{C}_{X}^{(r)}
and
\mathcal{C}_{Y}^{(r)}
:

(2.11)

\mathcal{C}_{X}^{(r)}=\mathcal{C}_{Y}^{(r)}\bullet^{r}\Lambda^{T}+\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{(r)}\,.

Furthermore, the third assumption in Definition
2.8
and Proposition
2.7
(c)
imply that both tensors
\mathcal{C}_{Y}^{(r)}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}^{(r)}
are diagonal for all
r\geq 1
.
The last property for
r=2
follows because the covariance matrix of a random vector is always positive semidefinite.
This completes the proof.
∎

The last proposition tells us that the family
\mathcal{C}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m}
has a natural description as the image of a certain polynomial map. Using this fact, our goal is to compute the dimension and the codimension of
\mathcal{C}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m}
in the space
\prod_{r=2}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{p})
. Furthermore, the fact that all tensors
\mathcal{E}^{(r)}
and
\mathcal{D}^{(r)}
in Proposition
2.10
are diagonal simplifies a lot the computation of the dimension of
\mathcal{C}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m}
.

Unfortunately, a similar statement does not hold for the
k
th-order moment factor analysis model
\mathcal{M}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m}
: in fact, in this case the assumptions given in Definition
2.8
do not imply that the moment tensors of
Y
and
\varepsilon
of order
k\geq 4
are diagonal. Nevertheless, as we discussed in Remark
2.4
, the polynomial relations in (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) allow us to conclude that the dimensions of the models
\mathcal{C}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m}
and
\mathcal{M}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m}
coincide.
Hence it makes sense to talk about the
dimension of the
k
th-order factor analysis model
, without specifying if we are dealing with cumulant or moment tensors.

Remark 2.11
.

One may also consider the Zariski closure of
\mathcal{C}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m}
, which is an algebraic variety. This leads to another fundamental question, that is studying the implicitization problem of the
k
th-order factor analysis model. There have been many attempts to study the polynomial relations describing a factor analysis model, and the problem is hard even when
k=2
. See
[
Har76
,
BdL87
,
DSS07
]
for more details. The best known polynomial invariants are the tetrads, which arise in one-factor analysis model. Additionally, the pentads, which are fifth degree polynomials, were found to be model invariants for two-factor analysis model. When the number of factors is greater than two, there is no significant progress in finding the model invariants due to some computational difficulties.
The implicitization problem has also been recently studied for more general linear non-Gaussian graphical models
[
RS21
,
ADG+21
]
.

3.
Main results

In this section, we prove the two main results of this paper. First, in Theorem
3.1
we compute the dimension of the
k
th-order factor analysis model for all
k\geq 2
and
p\geq m+1
. Secondly, in Theorem
3.4
we study when the
k
th-order factor analysis model has positive codimension.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume
k\geq 2
and
p\geq m+1
.
Let
d_{k}=\dim(\mathcal{M}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m})=\dim(\mathcal{C}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m})
be the dimension of the
k
th-order factor analysis model. Then

(3.1)

d_{k}=(k-1)p+(k-2)m+\min\left\{pm-\binom{m}{2},\binom{p+k-1}{k}-p\right\}\,.

Proof.

Consider the
k
th-order cumulant factor analysis model
\mathcal{C}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m}
. Our goal is to compute the dimension of
\mathcal{C}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m}
. Then, since there is a polynomial bijective correspondence between
\mathcal{C}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m}
and
\mathcal{M}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m}
, described by the equations (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), we conclude that
\dim(\mathcal{M}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m})=\dim(\mathcal{C}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m})
.
Up to an orthogonal transformation, we can assume that
\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m}
is lower-triangular, namely

\Lambda\in L_{p,m}=\{\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{p\times m}\mid\text{$\lambda_{ij}=0$ if $i<j$}\}\,.

Since we are assuming
p\geq m+1
, we have that
\dim(L_{p,m})=pm-\binom{m}{2}
.
Recall that we identify the subset
\Delta^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for all
r\geq 2
.
Consider the polynomial map

(3.2)

\begin{gathered}\varphi_{k}\colon(\mathbb{R}^{p})^{\times(k-1)}\times(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{\times(k-1)}\times L_{p,m}\longrightarrow\prod_{r=2}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(p)\\
\varphi_{k}(\mathcal{E}^{(2)},\ldots,\mathcal{E}^{(k)},\mathcal{D}^{(2)},\ldots,\mathcal{D}^{(k)},\Lambda)\coloneqq(\mathcal{T}^{(r)})_{r=2}^{k}\,,\end{gathered}

where

(3.3)

\mathcal{T}^{(r)}\coloneqq\mathcal{E}^{(r)}+\mathcal{D}^{(r)}\bullet^{r}\Lambda^{T}\quad\forall\,r\in\{2,\ldots,k\}\,.

The dimensions of the domain and of the image space of
\varphi_{k}
are respectively

(3.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}M&=\dim((\mathbb{R}^{p})^{\times(k-1)}\times(\mathbb{R}^{m})^{\times(k-1)}\times L_{p,m})=(k-1)p+(k-1)m+pm-\binom{m}{2}\\
N&=\dim\left(\prod_{r=2}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(p)\right)=\sum_{r=2}^{k}\binom{p+r-1}{r}\\
&=\sum_{r=0}^{k}\binom{p+r-1}{r}-p-1=\binom{p+k}{k}-p-1\,.\end{split}

The last equality in (
3.4
) follows because we applied for
a=k
and
b=p-1
the identity

\sum_{r=0}^{a}\binom{b+r}{r}=\binom{a+b+1}{a}\quad\forall\,a,b\geq 0\,.

The core of the proof is studying the dimension of the image of
\varphi_{k}
, which is equal to the rank of the Jacobian matrix
J(\varphi_{k})\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times M}
evaluated at a generic point in the domain of
\varphi_{k}
.
In fact, by Definition
2.9
and Proposition
2.10
, this is equal to computing the dimension of
\mathcal{C}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m}
.

In order to study the rank of
J(\varphi_{k})
, we choose coordinates for the domain and for the image space of
\varphi_{k}
. First, we identify the diagonal tensors
\mathcal{E}^{(r)}=(\varepsilon^{(r)}_{j\cdots j})
, and
\mathcal{D}^{(r)}=(\delta^{(r)}_{j\cdots j})
with the vectors

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{(r)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq(\varepsilon^{(r)}_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon^{(r)}_{p})\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\,,\ \varepsilon^{(r)}_{j}\coloneqq\varepsilon^{(r)}_{j\cdots j}\ \forall\,j\in[p]

\displaystyle\delta^{(r)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq(\delta^{(r)}_{1},\ldots,\delta^{(r)}_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\,,\ \delta^{(r)}_{j}\coloneqq\delta^{(r)}_{j\cdots j}\ \forall\,j\in[m]\,,

respectively. Secondly, we order the variables of the matrix
\Lambda
as in the vector

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\lambda&\coloneqq(\lambda_{\geq\,1}^{T}\mid\cdots\mid\lambda_{\geq\,m}^{T})\in\mathbb{R}^{pm-\binom{m}{2}}\\
\lambda_{\geq\,j}&\coloneqq(\lambda_{j,j},\lambda_{j+1,j},\cdots,\lambda_{p,j})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{p-j+1}\ \forall\,j\in[m]\,.\end{split}

Finally we denote by
t_{j_{1}\cdots j_{r}}^{(r)}
the entries of the
r
th component
\mathcal{T}^{(r)}
of
\varphi_{k}
, and we consider only multiindices
j_{1}\leq\cdots\leq j_{k}
due to the symmetry of
\mathcal{T}^{(r)}
.
Then, using equation (
2.7
) and the definition of
\varphi_{r}
in (
3.2
), we get

(3.6)

t^{(r)}_{j_{1}\cdots j_{r}}=\begin{cases}\varepsilon^{(r)}_{j}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{\min\{j,m\}}\delta^{(r)}_{\ell}\lambda_{j\ell}^{r}&\text{if $j_{1}=\cdots=j_{r}=j$}\\
\sum_{\ell=1}^{\min\{j_{1},m\}}\delta^{(r)}_{\ell}\lambda_{j_{1}\ell}\cdots\lambda_{j_{r}\ell}&\text{if $j_{1}\leq\cdots\leq j_{r}$ and $j_{u}\neq j_{v}$ for some $u,v$}\,.\end{cases}

Furthermore, we adopt also the following notations to simplify the writing of
J(\varphi_{k})
:

(3.7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}t_{\Delta}^{(r)}&\coloneqq(t^{(r)}_{1},\ldots,t^{(r)}_{p})^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}\,,\ t^{(r)}_{j}\coloneqq t^{(r)}_{j\cdots j}\ \forall\,j\in[p]\\
t_{<}^{(r)}&\coloneqq(t^{(r)}_{j_{1}\cdots j_{r}}\mid j_{u}\neq j_{v}\ \text{for some}\ u,v)^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{\binom{p+r-1}{r}-p}\,.\end{split}

The Jacobian matrix

can be written as

(3.8)

J(\varphi_{k})=\begin{cases}\begin{pNiceMatrix}&\text{if $k=2$}\\
&\\
\begin{pNiceMatrix}&\text{if $k\geq 3$}\end{cases}

where the white part is zero and for all
r

(3.9)

A^{(r)}=\frac{\partial\,t^{(r)}_{<}}{\partial\,\delta^{(r)}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\left(\binom{p+r-1}{r}-p\right)\times m}\,,\quad B^{(r)}=\frac{\partial\,t^{(r)}_{<}}{\partial\,\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}^{\left(\binom{p+r-1}{r}-p\right)\times\left(pm-\binom{m}{2}\right)}\,.

Thanks to the upper triangular block structure of

shown in (

), we conclude that

(3.10)

\mathrm{rank}(J(\varphi_{k}))\geq\begin{cases}p+\mathrm{rank}([A^{(2)}\mid B^{(2)}])&\text{if $k=2$}\\
(k-1)p+\sum_{r=2}^{k-1}\mathrm{rank}(A^{(r)})+\mathrm{rank}([A^{(k)}\mid B^{(k)}])&\text{if $k\geq 3$}\,.\end{cases}

Claim 1.
For all
r\in\{2,\ldots,k\}
, we have that

(3.11)

\mathrm{rank}([A^{(r)}\mid B^{(r)}])=\mathrm{rank}(B^{(r)})\,.

Given
r\in\{2,\ldots,k\}
and

, for every multi-index
(j_{1},\ldots,j_{r})
, we have that

(3.12)

\frac{\partial\,t_{j_{1}\cdots j_{r}}^{(r)}}{\partial\,\delta_{i}^{(r)}}=\lambda_{j_{1}i}\cdots\lambda_{j_{r}i}=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{s=1}^{p}\lambda_{si}\frac{\partial\,(\lambda_{j_{1}i}\cdots\lambda_{j_{r}i})}{\partial\,\lambda_{si}}=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{s=1}^{p}\frac{\lambda_{si}}{\delta_{i}^{(r)}}\frac{\partial\,t_{j_{1}\cdots j_{r}}^{(r)}}{\partial\,\lambda_{si}}\,,

where the second equality follows from Euler’s homogeneous function theorem and the last identity holds since, for every pair of indices
(u,v)
, by (
3.6
) we have that

(3.13)

\frac{\partial\,t_{j_{1}\cdots j_{r}}^{(r)}}{\partial\,\lambda_{uv}}=\frac{\partial\,(\delta_{v}^{(r)}\lambda_{j_{1}v}\cdots\lambda_{j_{r}v})}{\partial\,\lambda_{uv}}=\delta_{v}^{(r)}\alpha_{u}\lambda_{uv}^{\alpha_{u}-1}\prod_{\ell\mid j_{\ell}\neq u}\lambda_{j_{\ell}v}\,,

where
\alpha_{u}(j_{1},\ldots,j_{r})\coloneqq|\{\ell\mid j_{\ell}=u\}|

Equation (

) implies that every column
\partial\,t_{<}^{(r)}/\partial\,\delta_{i}^{(r)}
of

is a linear combination of the columns
\partial\,t_{<}^{(r)}/\partial\,\lambda_{si}
of

, proving Claim 1.

Claim 2.
For all
r\in\{2,\ldots,k\}
and for a generic choice of parameters in the domain of
\varphi_{k}
, we have

\mathrm{rank}(A^{(r)})=\min\left\{m,\binom{p+r-1}{r}-p\right\}=m\,.

By construction of
\Lambda
and by the first equality in (

t_{j,*}^{(r)}\coloneqq(t_{j_{1}\cdots j_{r}}^{(r)}\mid\text{$j_{1}=j$ and $j_{u}\neq j_{v}$ for some $u,v$})\quad\forall j\in[p-1]\,.

In particular, each vector

has
\binom{p-j+r-1}{r-1}-1
entries. Reordering the rows of

using the concatenation of the vectors

,…,

D^{(r)}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc|ccc}\frac{\partial\,t_{1,*}^{(r)}}{\partial\,\delta_{1}^{(r)}}&\frac{\partial\,t_{2,*}^{(r)}}{\partial\,\delta_{1}^{(r)}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial\,t_{m,*}^{(r)}}{\partial\,\delta_{1}^{(r)}}&\frac{\partial\,t_{m+1,*}^{(r)}}{\partial\,\delta_{1}^{(r)}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial\,t_{p-1,*}^{(r)}}{\partial\,\delta_{1}^{(r)}}\\
&\frac{\partial\,t_{2,*}^{(r)}}{\partial\,\delta_{2}^{(r)}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial\,t_{m,*}^{(r)}}{\partial\,\delta_{2}^{(r)}}&\frac{\partial\,t_{m+1,*}^{(r)}}{\partial\,\delta_{2}^{(r)}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial\,t_{p-1,*}^{(r)}}{\partial\,\delta_{2}^{(r)}}\\

&&&\frac{\partial\,t_{m,*}^{(r)}}{\partial\,\delta_{m}^{(r)}}&\frac{\partial\,t_{m+1,*}^{(r)}}{\partial\,\delta_{m}^{(r)}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial\,t_{p-1,*}^{(r)}}{\partial\,\delta_{m}^{(r)}}\end{array}\right)^{T}\,,

where the void blocks are identically zero, and the right block is not present if

.
Since each entry of the block column vectors

is a monomial in the variables

, the matrix

has full rank

if

for all

. The last identity in (
3.14
) follows by the assumption
p\geq m+1
.

Claim 3.
For all
k\geq 2
and for a generic choice of parameters in the domain of
\varphi_{k}
, we have

(3.17)

\mathrm{rank}(B^{(k)})=\min\left\{pm-\binom{m}{2},\binom{p+k-1}{k}-p\right\}\,.

The case

is covered in the proof of

, where the matrix

is called

and has full rank equal to

.
By construction of
\Lambda
and by the first equality in (

), for all

, the partial derivative

is identically zero if

. Using the notations introduced in (

) and (

), we can reorder the rows and the columns of

and obtain a matrix like

B^{(k)}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc|ccc}\frac{\partial\,t_{1,*}^{(k)}}{\partial\,\lambda_{\geq\,1}}&\frac{\partial\,t_{2,*}^{(k)}}{\partial\,\lambda_{\geq\,1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial\,t_{m,*}^{(k)}}{\partial\,\lambda_{\geq\,1}}&\frac{\partial\,t_{m+1,*}^{(k)}}{\partial\,\lambda_{\geq\,1}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial\,t_{p-1,*}^{(k)}}{\partial\,\lambda_{\geq\,1}}\\
&\frac{\partial\,t_{2,*}^{(k)}}{\partial\,\lambda_{\geq\,2}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial\,t_{m,*}^{(k)}}{\partial\,\lambda_{\geq\,2}}&\frac{\partial\,t_{m+1,*}^{(k)}}{\partial\,\lambda_{\geq\,2}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial\,t_{p-1,*}^{(k)}}{\partial\,\lambda_{\geq\,2}}\\

where the void blocks are identically zero, and the right block is not present if

. Using the lower triangular block structure of

, we conclude that
\mathrm{rank}(B^{(k)})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\mathrm{rank}(\partial\,t_{i,*}^{(k)}/\partial\,\lambda_{\geq\,i})
.

has full rank given by (

).

First, we assume
\delta_{s}^{(k)}\neq 0
for all

for all

for simplicity.
Every matrix

rows and

columns, see the following equation (

) for an explicit example when

,

,

, and

:

(3.19)

\frac{\partial\,t_{1,*}^{(4)}}{\partial\,\lambda_{\geq\,1}}=\scriptsize\begin{pNiceMatrix}\,.

Fix an index

. We want to find all non-decreasing tuples
(j_{2},\ldots,j_{k})
with

such that
\partial\,t_{i,j_{2},\ldots,j_{k}}^{(k)}/\partial\,\lambda_{ji}
is not identically zero if and only if

. The latter condition implies that all indices

are in

, and at least one is equal to

. This means that there are exactly

such choices of multiindices. In the example highlighted in (

, since

. Call

the submatrix of

just constructed. For example, from the matrix in (

R_{1}^{(4)}=\small\begin{pNiceMatrix}\,.

has full rank

for a generic choice of
\Lambda
.
Up to reordering the indices, we can assume that the first

rows of the vector

are
t_{i\cdots i,i+1}^{(k)},\ldots,t_{i\cdots i,p}^{(k)},t_{i\cdots i,i+1,i+1}^{(k)}
. Using (

), the top maximal minor of

is equal to

(3.21)

\begin{gathered}\left|\begin{array}[]{c|ccc}(k-1)\,\lambda_{ii}^{k-2}\lambda_{i+1,i}&\lambda_{ii}^{k-1}&&0\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\\

Remark 3.3
.

Consider the dimension formula (
3.1
). We show that

(3.24)

pm-\binom{m}{2}\leq\binom{p+k-1}{k}-p

for all
k\geq 3
and
p\geq m+1
. First, we observe that the sequence
\{\binom{p+k-1}{k}\}
is nondecreasing in
k
, therefore it is sufficient to show that
pm-\binom{m}{2}\leq\binom{p+2}{3}-p
for all
p\geq m+1
.
The previous inequality is equivalent to
f(p)\coloneqq p^{3}+3p^{2}-2(3m+2)p+3m(m-1)\geq 0
. We have
f(m+1)=m(m^{2}+3m-4)\geq 0
for all
m\geq 1
, and one verifies that the largest root of
f^{\prime}(p)
is
-1+\sqrt{2m+\frac{7}{3}}\leq m+1
, therefore
f(p)\geq 0
for all

. This implies that (

) holds for all
k\geq 3
and

, hence in this case the dimension formula (

) simplifies as

(3.25)

d_{k}=(m+k-1)p+(k-2)m-\binom{m}{2}\,.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
c_{k}
be the codimension
\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{M}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m})=\mathrm{codim}(\mathcal{C}^{(\leq k)}_{p,m})
of the
k
th-order factor analysis model as a subvariety in
\prod_{r=2}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(p)
.

(i)

If
k=2
, then
c_{k}>0
if and only if
p\geq\left\lfloor m+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{8m+1}+\frac{1}{2}\right\rfloor+1
.

(ii)

If
k\geq 3
and
p\geq m+1
, then
c_{k}=h_{m}^{(k)}(p)/k!
, where

(3.26)

h_{m}^{(k)}(p)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(p+i)-k!(k+m)p+\frac{k!}{2}[m^{2}+(3-2k)m-2]\,.

In particular

(a)

if
m\in[2k-3]
, then
h_{m}^{(k)}(p)
has a unique positive root
p^{(k)}
, therefore
c_{k}>0
if
p\geq\lfloor p^{(k)}\rfloor+1
.

(b)

for finitely many values of
m\geq 2k-2
, the polynomial
h_{m}^{(k)}(p)
has two positive roots, the largest denoted by
p^{(k)}
. Therefore
c_{k}>0
if
p\geq\lfloor p^{(k)}\rfloor+1
.

(c)

There exists an integer
m^{*}\geq 2k-2
such that
h_{m}^{(k)}(p)
has no positive roots for
m\geq m^{*}
, in particular
c_{k}>0
for all
m\geq m^{*}
and
p\geq m+1
.

Before starting the proof of Theorem
3.4
, we recall a property of real univariate polynomials related to a theorem of Pólya
[
P2́8
]
(see also
[
HLP52
, Theorem 56, p. 57]
). The following statement is taken from
[
Sta13
, Exercise 3(a), Chapter 3]
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
f\in\mathbb{R}[x]
be a nonzero polynomial with real coefficients. The following two conditions are equivalent.

(i)

There exists a nonzero polynomial
g\in\mathbb{R}[x]
with real coefficients such that all coefficients of
fg
are nonnegative.

(ii)

There does not exist a real number
a>0
such that
f(a)=0
.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

The case
k=2
is studied in
[
DSS07
, Theorem 2]
, so we assume
k\geq 3
.
The codimension
c_{k}
is the difference between the dimension
N=\binom{p+k}{k}-p-1
of the image space
\prod_{r=2}^{k}\mathrm{Sym}^{r}(p)
computed in (
3.4
) and the value
d_{k}
computed in (
3.25
). This yields the polynomial
h_{m}^{(k)}(p)
in (
3.26
) up to division by
k!
.
Given
m
and
k
, we want to find the minimum integer
p^{(k)}
such that
h_{m}^{(k)}(p)>0
for every
p\geq p^{(k)}
. In other words, we want to give sufficient conditions on
p
such that
c_{k}>0
.
For all
j\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
, let

(3.27)

e_{j}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\text{if $j=0$}\\
\sum_{1\leq\ell_{1}<\cdots<\ell_{j}\leq k}x_{\ell_{1}}\cdots x_{\ell_{j}}&\text{if $j\in[k]$}\end{cases}

be the
j
-th elementary symmetric polynomial on
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
.
We also denote by
e_{j}^{(k)}
the number
e_{j}(1,\ldots,k)
.
We can rewrite
h_{m}^{(k)}(p)
as

\displaystyle h_{m}^{(k)}(p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k}e_{j}^{(k)}p^{k-j}-k!(k+m)p+\frac{k!}{2}[m^{2}+(3-2k)m-2]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}e_{j}^{(k)}p^{k-j}+[e_{k-1}^{(k)}-k!(k+m)]p+\frac{k!}{2}m(m-2k+3)\,.

In particular the coefficients of
p^{\ell}
with
\ell\geq 2
are always positive. Regarding the linear coefficient

u(k,m)\coloneqq e_{k-1}^{(k)}-k!(k+m)=k!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{i}-k-m\right)\,,

it is not difficult to verify that
u(k,m)<0
for all
m\geq 1
.
This implies that there is always at least one change of sign in
h_{m}^{(k)}(p)
, but no more than two.
More precisely, the number of sign changes depends on the sign of the constant term

v(k,m)\coloneqq\frac{k!}{2}m(m-2k+3)

of
h_{m}^{(k)}(p)
. In particular
v(k,m)>0
for all
m\geq 2k-2
.
Therefore, applying Descartes’ rule of signs, the polynomial
h_{m}^{(k)}(p)
has exactly one positive root for
m\in[2k-3]
, and either two or zero real positive roots for
m\geq 2k-2
.
Since for
m\in[2k-3]
the polynomial
h_{m}^{(k)}(p)
has exactly one positive root, say
p^{(k)}
, then for
p\geq p^{(k)}
, the value of
h_{m}^{(k)}(p)
is positive. This proves part
(a)
of the statement.
To prove
(b)
and
(c)
, it remains to show that
h_{m}^{(k)}
has exactly two positive real roots for finitely many integers
m\geq 2k-2
and then for sufficiently large
m
, it has no positive real roots.

First, suppose that
k\in\{3,4,5\}
. In particular, we have

(3.28)

\displaystyle\begin{split}h_{m}^{(3)}(p)&=p^{3}+6p^{2}-(6m+7)p+3m(m-3)\\
h_{m}^{(4)}(p)&=p^{4}+10p^{3}+35p^{2}-2(12m+23)p+12m(m-5)\\

Now assume

. We need the following claim.

Claim 1:
For any
k\geq 6
, there exists an integer

such that for all

we have

(3.29)

Expanding the polynomials

and

and dividing by

, the inequality (

) is equivalent to

(3.30)

k!(k+m)^{2}-2(k+m)e_{k-1}^{(k)}-\frac{1}{2}m(m-2k+3)e_{k-2}^{(k)}+k!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{i}\right)^{2}\leq 0\,.

The left-hand side of (
3.30
) is the quadratic polynomial
H_{k}(m)=\alpha_{2}m^{2}+\alpha_{1}m+\alpha_{0}
, where

(3.31)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\alpha_{2}&=k!-\frac{1}{2}e_{k-2}^{(k)}\\
\alpha_{1}&=2k!k+\frac{2k-3}{2}e_{k-2}^{(k)}-2e_{k-1}^{(k)}\\
\alpha_{0}&=k^{2}k!-2k\,e_{k-1}^{(k)}+k!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{i}\right)^{2}\,.\end{split}

The polynomial
H_{k}(m)
has at most two roots, and an upper bound
m^{*}
for these roots can be obtained using the bounds provided by Lagrange or Cauchy
[

]
.
Since the leading coefficient
\alpha_{2}
is negative for all
k\geq 6
, we conclude that
H_{k}(m)\leq 0
for all

. This concludes the proof of Claim 1.

Now using Proposition
3.5
and Claim 1, we show that the polynomial
h^{(k)}_{m}(p)
has no positive root for sufficiently large
m
, thus verifying (3).
To simplify furthermore, let

s(p)\coloneqq\sum_{j=0}^{k-2}e_{j}^{(k)}p^{k-j}\,.

Consider the product depending on the parameter
b\in\mathbb{R}

h^{(k)}_{m}(p)(p+b)=s(p)\,p+\left(u(k,m)+b\,\frac{s(p)}{p^{2}}\right)p^{2}+\left[v(k,m)+b\,u(k,m)\right]p+b\,v(k,m)\,.

Note that all coefficients of
s(p)p
are always positive.
If we choose
b\geq 0
and
m\geq 2k-2
, then the constant term of
h^{(k)}_{m}(p)(p+b)
is nonnegative.
Moreover, the inequality (
3.29
) of Claim 1 and the fact that
u(k,m)<0
for all
m\geq 1
imply that there exists an integer
m^{*}\geq 1
and a real number
b_{0}\geq 0
such that

0\leq-\frac{u(k,m)}{e_{k-2}^{(k)}}\leq b_{0}\leq-\frac{v(k,m)}{u(k,m)}\quad\forall\,m\geq m^{*}\,.

This choice of

for all

with

implies that
h^{(k)}_{m}(p)
does not have any positive root for all

. Since

has either zero or two positive roots for

, then

has exactly two positive roots for all
2k-2\leq m\leq m^{*}-1
. This completes the proof.∎

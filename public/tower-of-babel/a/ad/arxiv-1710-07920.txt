Invariance principle for biased Boostrap Random Walk.
A. Collevecchio, K. Hamza, Yunxuan Liu

Abstract
Our main goal is to study a class of processes whose increments are generated via a cellular automata rule. Given the increments of a simple biased random walk, a new sequence of (dependent) Bernoulli random variables is produced. It is built, from the original sequence, according to a cellular automata rule. Equipped with these two sequences, we construct two more according to the same cellular automata rule. The construction is repeated a fixed number of times yielding an infinite array ( { − K , … , K } × ℕ \{-K,\ldots,K\}\times\mathbb{N} ) of (dependent) Bernoulli random variables. Taking partial sums of these sequences, we obtain a ( 2 ​ K + 1 ) (2K+1) -dimensional process whose increments belong to the state space { − 1 , 1 } 2 ​ K + 1 \{-1,1\}^{2K+1} . The aim of the paper is to study the long term behaviour of this process. In particular, we establish transience/recurrence properties and prove an invariance principle. The limiting behaviour of these processes depends strongly on the direction of the iteration, and exhibits few surprising features. This work is motivated by an earlier investigation (see [ 2 ] ), in which the starting sequence is symmetric, and by the related work [ 4 ] .

Our main goal is to study a class of processes whose increments are generated via cellular automata rule.
We consider a generalization of a model introduced in

k\in[\![1,K]\!]
and
\ell\in[\![-K,0]\!]
. Using representation (
3.4
), we see that
X_{n}(\ell)
is a function of
(X_{0}(-\ell),\ldots,X_{0}(0),X_{1}(0),\ldots,X_{n}(0))
. Therefore,
X_{0}(k)
and
X_{n}(\ell)
are independent.

The case
k\in[\![-K,0]\!]
and
\ell\in[\![1,K]\!]
is similar. First, we observe that
X_{n}(\ell)
is a function of
(X_{0}(1),\ldots,X_{0}(\ell),X_{1}(0),\ldots,X_{n}(0))
, and is therefore again independent of
X_{0}(k)
.

In both of these cases,
\mbox{cov}_{\pi}(X_{0}(k),X_{n}(\ell))=0
.

Next, we look at the case
k,\ell\in[\![1,K]\!]
. Using representation (
3.4
) and the independence of
X_{0},X_{1}(0),X_{2}(0),\ldots
, we write

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[X_{0}(k)X_{n}(\ell)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[X_{0}(k)\left(\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}X_{0}(\ell-j)^{\alpha_{j,n}}\right)\left(\prod_{m=1}^{n}X_{n-m+1}(0)^{\nu_{\ell,m}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\left[X_{0}(k)\left(\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}X_{0}(\ell-j)^{\alpha_{j,n}}\right)\right]\left(\prod_{m=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[X_{n-m+1}(0)^{\nu_{\ell,m}}]\right)

which, in the case
k<\ell
, yields to
\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[X_{0}(k)X_{n}(\ell)]=0
in view of the fact
\alpha_{0,n}=1
and, for
\ell\geq 1
,
X_{0}(\ell)
is symmetric. The case
k>\ell
is dealt with in exactly the same way yielding again
\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[X_{0}(k)X_{n}(\ell)]=0
. Finally, we look at the case
k=\ell\geq 1
:

\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[X_{0}(k)X_{n}(k)]=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[X_{0}(k-j)^{\alpha_{j,n}}]\right)\left(\prod_{m=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[X_{n-m+1}(0)^{\nu_{k,m}}]\right).

Since the
X_{0}(k-j)
are symmetric, the first product takes the value 1 if
\alpha_{j,n}=0
for any
j\in[\![1,k-1]\!]
, and takes the value 0 otherwise. Using Lemma
5.3
of the Appendix, we see that, letting
2^{\kappa}
be the largest power of 2 that divides
n
, if
k\leq 2^{\kappa}
, then the aforesaid product is 1, and if
k>2^{\kappa}
, then the product is 0. The second product equals
(2p-1)^{\omega_{k,n}}
.

The last case is
k,\ell\in[\![-K,0]\!]
. Here, our focus is the upward process
(\widehat{X}_{n})_{n}
, which reaches stationarity after
K+1
steps (see Proposition
3.1
):

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[X_{0}(k)X_{n}(\ell)]\ =\ \mathbb{E}_{\widehat{\pi}}[\widehat{X}_{0}(k)\widehat{X}_{n}(\ell)]\ =\ \mathbb{E}[\widehat{X}_{K+1}(k)\widehat{X}_{n+K+1}(\ell)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\prod_{j=0}^{-k}X_{K+1-j}(0)^{\beta_{-k,j}}\right)\left(\prod_{j=0}^{-\ell}X_{n+K+1-j}(0)^{\beta_{-\ell,j}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\prod_{m=K+1+k}^{K+1}X_{m}(0)^{\beta_{-k,K+1-m}}\right)\left(\prod_{m=n+K+1+\ell}^{n+K+1}X_{m}(0)^{\beta_{-\ell,n+K+1-m}}\right)\right].

Clearly, in the case
n+\ell\geq 1
, the two products are independent and we have once more
\mbox{cov}_{\pi}(X_{0}(k),X_{n}(\ell))=0
. If
n\leq-\ell
, then we can rearrange the above products in the 3 independent products the means of which are:
\prod_{j\in I_{1}\setminus I_{2}}(2p-1)^{\beta_{-k,j}}
,
\prod_{j\in I_{1}\cap I_{2}}(2p-1)^{\beta_{-k,j}+\beta_{-\ell,n+j}-2\beta_{-k,j}\beta_{-\ell,n+j}}
and
\prod_{j\in I_{2}\setminus I_{1}}(2p-1)^{\beta_{-\ell,n+j}}
,
where
I_{1}=[\![0,-k]\!]
and
I_{2}=[\![-n,-n-\ell]\!]
. However, for
-k\leq-n-\ell
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in I_{1}\setminus I_{2}}\beta_{-k,j}+\sum_{j\in I_{1}\cap I_{2}}\big(\beta_{-k,j}+\beta_{-\ell,n+j}-2\beta_{-k,j}\beta_{-\ell,n+j}\big)+\sum_{j\in I_{2}\setminus I_{1}}\beta_{-\ell,n+j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{-k}\beta_{-k,j}+\sum_{j=0}^{-k}\beta_{-\ell,n+j}-2\sum_{j=0}^{-k}\beta_{-k,j}\beta_{-\ell,n+j}+\sum_{j=-n}^{-1}\beta_{-\ell,n+j}+\sum_{j=-k+1}^{-n-\ell}\beta_{-\ell,n+j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{\theta(-k)}+2^{\theta(-\ell)}-2\sum_{j=0}^{-k}\beta_{-k,j}\beta_{-\ell,n+j}.

Similarly, if
-k>-n-\ell
,

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in I_{1}\setminus I_{2}}\beta_{-k,j}+\sum_{j\in I_{1}\cap I_{2}}\big(\beta_{-k,j}+\beta_{-\ell,n+j}-2\beta_{-k,j}\beta_{-\ell,n+j}\big)+\sum_{j\in I_{2}\setminus I_{1}}\beta_{-\ell,n+j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=-n-\ell+1}^{-k}\beta_{-k,j}+\sum_{j=0}^{-n-\ell}\beta_{-k,j}+\sum_{j=0}^{-n-\ell}\beta_{-\ell,n+j}-2\sum_{j=0}^{-n-\ell}\beta_{-k,j}\beta_{-\ell,n+j}+\sum_{j=-n}^{-1}\beta_{-\ell,n+j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{\theta(-k)}+2^{\theta(-\ell)}-2\sum_{j=0}^{-n-\ell}\beta_{-k,j}\beta_{-\ell,n+j}.

We immediately get that

\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[X_{0}(k)X_{n}(\ell)]=(2p-1)^{\mathcal{B}(-k,-\ell,n)}.

\sigma^{2}=\mbox{var}_{\pi}(f(X_{0}))+2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mbox{cov}_{\pi}(f(X_{0}),f(X_{n})).

Substituting the expressions from 1. and 2. leads to the required representation.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let, for
k=\sum_{i\geq 0}k_{i}2^{i}
,
\theta(k)=\sum_{i\geq 0}k_{i}
be the number of the base 2 digits of
k
that are equal to 1. Then,

\#\{j\in[\![0,k]\!]:\beta_{k,j}=1\}=2^{\theta(k)}.

Proof.

By Lucas Theorem,
\beta_{k,j}=1
if and only if, for every
i\geq 0
,
k_{i}\geq j_{i}
. This leads to
j_{i}=0
whenever
k_{i}=0
. On the other hand, if
k_{i}=1
, then
j_{i}
can be either 0 or 1. The result immediately follows.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let, for
k=\sum_{i\geq 0}k_{i}2^{i}
and
\ell=\sum_{i\geq 0}\ell_{i}2^{i}
,
\left<k,\ell\right>=\sum_{i\geq 0}k_{i}\ell_{i}2^{i}
. Then,

B(k,\ell)=2^{\theta(k)}+2^{\theta(\ell)}-2\sum_{j=0}^{k\wedge\ell}\beta_{k,j}\beta_{\ell,j}=2^{\theta(k)}+2^{\theta(\ell)}-2^{\theta(\left<k,\ell\right>)+1}.

\theta(\left<k,\ell\right>)
is the number of the base 2 digits of
k
and
\ell
that are concurrently equal to 1. In particular,
B(k,k)=0
.

Proof.

Since
\beta_{k,j}+\beta_{\ell,j}=1
if and only if
\beta_{k,j}+\beta_{\ell,j}-2\beta_{k,j}\beta_{\ell,j}=1
and the second expression is always in
\{0,1\}
,

\displaystyle B(k,\ell)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\#\{j\in[\![1,k\vee\ell]\!]:\beta_{k,j}+\beta_{\ell,j}=1\}\ =\ \sum_{j=1}^{k\vee\ell}\left(\beta_{k,j}+\beta_{\ell,j}-2\beta_{k,j}\beta_{\ell,j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\beta_{k,j}+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\beta_{\ell,j}-2\sum_{j=1}^{k\wedge\ell}\beta_{k,j}\beta_{\ell,j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(2^{\theta(k)}-1\big)+\big(2^{\theta(k)}-1\big)-2\big(2^{\theta(\left<k,\ell\right>)}-1\big).

In the last step, we use the fact that
\beta_{k,j}=\beta_{\ell,j}=1
if and only if
\beta_{\left<k,\ell\right>,j}=1
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

If
2^{\kappa}
is the largest power of 2 that divides
n
(i.e.
n=m2^{\kappa}
where
m
is odd), then
\min\{j\geq 1:\ \alpha_{j,n}=1\}=2^{\kappa}
.

Proof.

Let
n=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}n_{\ell}2^{\ell}
be the binary representation of
n
. If
n=m2^{\kappa}
, then
n_{\ell}=0
for
\ell<\kappa
and
n_{\kappa}=1
, and

n-1=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\kappa-1}2^{\ell}+\sum_{\ell\geq\kappa+1}n_{\ell}2^{\ell}.

Let
j\in[\![1,2^{\kappa}-1]\!]
,
j=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\kappa-1}j_{\ell}2^{\ell}
be its binary representation and
\ell^{\star}=\max\{\ell\in[\![0,\kappa-1]\!]:\ j_{\ell}=1\}
. Then the
\ell^{\star}
digit of
n+j-1
is 0 while the same digit of
j
is 1. By Lucas Theorem,
\alpha_{j,n}=0
. On the other hand, all digits of
n+2^{\kappa}-1
are at least as large as the same digits of
2^{\kappa}
. Again, by Lucas Theorem,
\alpha_{2^{\kappa},n}=1
.
∎

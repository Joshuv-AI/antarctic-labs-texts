Descent problems for derived Azumaya algebras
Federico Binda, Mauro Porta

Abstract
. This paper is dedicated to a further study of derived Azumaya algebras. The first result we obtain is a Beauville-Laszlo-style property for such objects (considered up to Morita equivalence), which is consequence of a more general Beauville-Laszlo kind of statement for quasi-coherent sheaves of categories. Next, we prove that given any (derived) scheme X X , proper over the spectrum of a quasi-excellent Henselian ring, the derived Brauer group of X X injects into the one of the Henselization of X X along the base, generalizing a classical result of Grothendieck and a more recent theorem of Geisser-Morin. As a separate application, we deduce that Grothendieck’s existence theorem holds for the stable ∞ \infty -categories of twisted sheaves even when the corresponding 𝔾 m \mathbb{G}_{m} -gerbe does not satisfy the resolution property, offering an improvement of a result of Alper, Rydh and Hall.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a scheme.
The Brauer-Grothendieck group of
X
, i.e. the second étale cohomology group
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{G}_{m}
, is an important arithmetico-geometric invariant of the scheme: for example, it can be used to measure obstructions to various Hasse principles for zero-cycles and rational points, thanks to the Brauer-Manin pairing; or it serves, in the form of the unramified Brauer group, as a useful birational invariant, as shown in the construction, due to Artin and Mumford, of first example of a variety that is unirational but not rational (a counterexample to Luroth’s problem in dimension 3, see
[
3
]
for a survey and
[
9
]
).

As it is often the case for étale cohomology groups, it is somewhat difficult to get a concrete grasp on the geometric meaning of classes in
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
.
Nevertheless, in the special case of the multiplicative group it is suggestive to consider the following table:

n

0

1

2

Geometric meaning of
\mathrm{H}^{n}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})

Invertible algebraic functions on
X

Invertible coherent sheaves on
X

?

A first attempt at filling this table has been done by Grothendieck: building on earlier works of Auslander and Goldman, he introduced In
[
15
, Exposé VI]
the notion of sheaf of Azumaya algebras on
X
: a vector bundle equipped with an associative multiplication satisfying a certain
invertibility
property, generalizing the notion of central simple algebra over a field.
When considered up to Morita equivalence, Azumaya algebras form a group
\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{Az}}(X)
, known as the
Brauer-Azumaya group
of
X
(following e.g.
[
9
]
).
Grothendieck provided a natural injective map

\mathrm{Br}_{\mathrm{Az}}(X)\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})

which, however, is far from being surjective in general.
Indeed, the classical Skolem-Noether theorem implies that it can only hit torsion classes, whereas D. Mumford constructed an example of a singular surface
S
whose
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(S,\mathbb{G}_{m})
contains a non-torsion classes (on the other hand O. Gabber showed that the above map is surjective on torsion classes, at least when
X
is quasi-projective).

Much later, the advent of homotopical techniques allowed to solve this problem in a completely satisfactory way.
In his groundbreaking work
[
35
]
, B. Toën introduced derived Morita theory.
In a nutshell, objects of derived Morita theory are (sheaves of)
\infty
-categories equipped with an action of the derived
\infty
-category
\mathrm{QCoh}(X)
.
However, these
\infty
-categories are not considered up to equivalence, but only up to
Morita equivalence
(two
\mathrm{QCoh}(X)
-linear stable
\infty
-categories are Morita equivalent if there is a
\mathrm{QCoh}(X)
-linear equivalence between their categories of
\mathrm{QCoh}(X)
-valued presheaves, generalizing the classical Morita equivalence between rings, defined by looking at equivalences between the corresponding category of modules).
In this paper we denote by
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{X}
the resulting
\infty
-category.
A key result of Toën (later revisited by J. Lurie) is that
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{X}
admits a symmetric monoidal structure.
Building on this theory, in
[
35
]
, B. Toën proved that every cohomology class in
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X;\mathbb{G}_{m})
can be represented by an invertible object in
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{X}
, thus completing the above table.
He actually went much further than that: replacing vector bundles by perfect complexes, he adapted the original definition of Grothendieck of sheaf of Azumaya algebras, following the general philosophy of derived geometry. He called the resulting class of objects
derived Azumaya algebras
and proved that, when considered up to Morita equivalence, they coincide exactly with the invertible objects of
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}
.
The resulting group is nowadays called the
derived Brauer group
and denoted
\mathrm{dBr}(X)
.
This group contains the classical Brauer group
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X;\mathbb{G}_{m})
of
X
as a summand, but it has better formal properties.

The goal of this work is to exploit once more Toën’s categorical framework in order to deduce new results about the Brauer-Grothendieck group even for classical schemes. We are interested in particular in problems related to
descent questions
for
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X;\mathbb{G}_{m})
with respect to certain naturally occurring colimits, that we will solve by considering the corresponding problem for “quasi-coherent” sheaves of
categories
.
We will consider two different kinds of situations:

(1)

the Beauville-Laszlo problem for
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X;\mathbb{G}_{m})
, and

(2)

the formal GAGA problem for
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X;\mathbb{G}_{m})
,

that we now describe in details.

1.2.
Formal GAGA

The second kind of colimit that we consider arises from the following geometric situation: let
S=\Spec(R)
be the spectrum of a commutative ring which is complete with respect to a finitely generated ideal
I\subseteq\pi_{0}(R)
, and let
p\colon X\to S
be a proper
S
-scheme.
For
i\geqslant 0
, set
S_{i}\coloneqq\Spec(R/I^{i+1})
and
X_{i}\coloneqq S_{i}\times_{S}^{\mathrm{d}}X
. Let
\mathfrak{X}
denote the formal completion of
X
along
S_{0}\times_{S}X
, i.e.
\mathfrak{X}=\colim_{i}X_{i}
.
For every positive integer
n
, we can consider the comparison map

(1.2)

\mathrm{H}^{n}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X;\mathbb{G}_{m})\longrightarrow\lim_{i}\mathrm{H}^{n}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{i};\mathbb{G}_{m})\ .

When
n=0
or
n=1
, this map is an equivalence.
This is the consequence of Grothendieck’s existence theorem and the equivalence
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X;\mathbb{G}_{m})\simeq\Pic(X)
.
However, already for
n=2
, the situation is more complex.

Indeed, in
[
15
, Exposé VI]
Grothendieck himself studied this problem.
He proved injectivity of (
1.2
) under the following two key assumptions:

•

X
is regular and the map
p\colon X\to S
is flat;

•

the vanishing
\lim^{1}_{i}\Pic(X_{i})\simeq 0
holds;

This result can be improved using
continuous cohomology

[
21
]
, which is the étale cohomology
\mathrm{H}^{n}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathfrak{X};\mathbb{G}_{m})
and which can be explicitly defined as

\mathrm{H}^{n}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathfrak{X},\mathbb{G}_{m})\simeq\mathrm{H}^{n}\Big(\lim_{i}\mathrm{R}\Gamma_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{i},\mathbb{G}_{m})\Big),

where the limit inside the parentheses is understood in the
\infty
-categorical way (i.e. it is a homotopy limit).
Then (
1.2
) can be factored as

\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X;\mathbb{G}_{m})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathfrak{X};\mathbb{G}_{m})\longrightarrow\lim_{i}\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X_{i};\mathbb{G}_{m})\ .

In
[
14
, Theorem 7.2]
it was shown that the first map is injective when
X
is regular and flat over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Toën’s framework of derived Morita theory allows to treat this problem in a much greater generality.
Representing classes in
\mathrm{dBr}(X)
as invertible objects in the Morita theory
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{X}
of
X
, one is naturally led to consider the natural comparison map

\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{X}\longrightarrow\lim_{i}\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{X_{i}}.

Full faithfulness of this functor would immediately imply the injectivity of
\mathrm{dBr}(X)\to\mathrm{dBr}(\mathfrak{X})
.
Although this is too much to hope for (just as one cannot expect Grothendieck’s existence theorem to hold for arbitrary quasi-coherent sheaves), we can restrict to compact objects in
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{X}
.
These can be understood as sheaves of
smooth and proper
stable
\infty
-categories with a coherent action of
\mathrm{QCoh}(X)
.
Denoting by
\mathsf{SmPr}^{\mathsf{cat}}(X)
(resp.
\mathsf{dAz}^{\mathrm{cat}}(X)
) the full subcategory of
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{X}
spanned by smooth and proper (resp. invertible) categories, we can now state our second main theorem:

Theorem 1.3

(See
4.4
and
4.6
)
.

Let
S=\Spec(R)
be the spectrum of a Noetherian ring which is complete with respect an ideal
I
.
Let
X\to S
be a proper
S
-scheme.
Then:

(1)

The natural symmetric monoidal functor

(1.4)

\mathsf{SmPr}^{\mathsf{cat}}(X)\longrightarrow\mathsf{SmPr}^{\mathsf{cat}}(\mathfrak{X})\coloneqq\lim_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathsf{SmPr}^{\mathsf{cat}}(X_{i}).

is fully faithful. In particular it induces a fully faithful functor on the subcategory of invertible objects

\mathsf{dAz}^{\mathrm{cat}}(X)\longrightarrow\mathsf{dAz}^{\mathrm{cat}}(\mathfrak{X})\coloneqq\lim_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\mathsf{dAz}^{\mathrm{cat}}(X_{i}).

(2)

(Formal injectivity)
The natural map

\mathrm{dBr}(X)\longrightarrow\mathrm{dBr}(\mathfrak{X})

is injective. In particular, the group homomorphism
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathfrak{X},\mathbb{G}_{m})
is injective.

(3)

Asssume that
R
is a noetherian complete local ring. Let
\ell
be a prime different from the residue characteristic.
Then there exists a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\Pic(X)/\ell\longrightarrow\lim_{i}(\Pic(X_{i})/\ell)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\longrightarrow}}\lim_{i}^{1}\Pic(X_{i})

such that

\mathrm{Im}(\rho)\simeq\ker\big(\mathrm{Br}(X)[\ell]\to\lim_{i}\mathrm{Br}(X_{i})\big)\ .

The conclusion of (2) holds if
S
is assumed to be the spectrum of a local Noetherian Henselian ring
R
such that the canonical map
R\to\lim_{n}R/\mathfrak{m}^{n}
has regular geometric fibers.

Note that if
p\colon X\to S
was assumed to be
smooth
, full faithfulness of the functor (
1.4
) would be a formal consequence of Lurie’s
[
26
, Theorems 11.1.4.1, 11.3.6.1 and 11.4.4.1]
.
In any case, the functor (
1.4
) is not essentially surjective, even if we restrict ourselves to the subcategory of invertible objects (see
4.7
).
It would be interesting to determine a specific class of
algebraizable
formal families of smooth and proper (or even invertible)
\infty
-categories, i.e. an explicit characterization of the essential image of the functor (
1.4
).

1.3.
Grothendieck’es existence theorem for
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes

We now offer another consequence of
1.3
-(1).
The elements of the Brauer-Grothendieck group have another, different, geometric interpretation.
Indeed, the work of Giraud
[
16
]
shows that a class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
can be represented by a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe.
Informally, this is the datum of a (derived) stack
\pi\colon\mathfrak{A}\to X
which étale locally looks like
\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X
.
One might wonder whether it is possible to prove the formal injectivity statement of
1.3
from this point of view.
It turns out that in order to do so, one would need to know Grothendieck’s existence theorem for arbitrary
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes (see the explanation at the beginning of
Section

5
).
However, to the best of our knowledge this is only known for
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes that have the resolution property
[
1
, Corollary 1.7]
.
On the other hand, Totaro showed in
[
37
, Theorem 1.1]
that this is equivalent to ask that the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe in question is a global quotient, and by
[
12
, Corollary 3.8 & Example 3.12]
this is not always the case (since a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe is a global quotient if and only if the class in
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
is representable by a
classical
Azumaya algebra).

Reversing the perspective
, we can use
1.3
to prove that Grothendieck’s existence theorem holds for
arbitrary

\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes:

(See
5.26
)
.

Let
S=\Spec(R)
be the spectrum of a Noetherian ring which is complete with respect an ideal
I
.
Let
X\to S
be a proper derived

-scheme.
Let

be a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe over
X
.
Then the canonical map

\mathrm{Perf}(\mathfrak{A})\longrightarrow\lim_{n}\mathrm{Perf}(\mathfrak{A}_{n})

is a symmetric monoidal equivalence of stable, symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories.

\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}
in
(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{R})^{\otimes}
belongs to
(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{R})^{\otimes}
if and only if
\mathcal{B}
does.

We can identify
\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{R})
with the category of maps of
\infty
-operads
\mathsf{Fin}_{*}\to(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{R})^{\otimes}
, and similarly for
\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{R})
.
Since the functor
j
is faithful, we see that a section of
s\colon\mathsf{Fin}_{*}\to(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{R})^{\otimes}
factors through
(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{R})^{\otimes}
if and only if it satisfies the following two conditions:

(1)

for every object
\langle n\rangle\in\mathsf{Fin}_{*}
,
s(\langle n\rangle)\in(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{R})^{\otimes}
;

(2)

for every morphism
f\colon\langle n\rangle\to\langle m\rangle
,
s(f)
is a morphism in
(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{R})^{\otimes}
.

Let
s\colon\mathsf{Fin}_{*}\to(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{R})^{\otimes}
be the section corresponding to the presentably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category
\mathcal{A}
.
We have to prove that rigidity of
\mathcal{A}
implies that
s
satisfies the above two conditions.
Since
s
is a map of
\infty
-operads, we have canonical equivalences

s(\langle n\rangle)_{i}\simeq\mathcal{A}.

Since
\mathcal{A}
is compactly generated by assumption, we conclude that condition (1) is automatically satisfied.
We now verify condition (2).
Since every morphism in
\mathsf{Fin}_{*}
can be written as the composition of an inert and an active morphism (see
[
25
, Remark 2.1.2.2]
), we see that it is enough to prove that condition (2) is satisfied separately by these two classes of morphisms.
To begin with, assume that
f
is inert.
Since
s
is a map of
\infty
-operads,
s(f)
is coCartesian.
Since
s(\langle n\rangle)
belongs to
(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{R})^{\otimes}_{\langle n\rangle}
, we deduce that
s(f)
belongs to
(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{R})^{\otimes}
as well.

Assume now that
f
is active.
In this case, we can factor
f
as a surjective morphism followed by an injective one, and it is therefore enough to prove that condition (2) holds separately in these two cases.
Assume first that
f
is active and surjective.
Then
f
can be (non-uniquely) factored as a composition

\langle n\rangle\xrightarrow{f_{1}}\langle n-1\rangle\xrightarrow{f_{2}}\cdots\xrightarrow{f_{m}}\langle m\rangle,

where each
f_{i}
is active and surjective.
It therefore enough to treat the case of a morphism
g\colon\langle n^{\prime}\rangle\to\langle n^{\prime}-1\rangle
which is active and surjective.
In this case, there exists an element
k\in\langle n^{\prime}-1\rangle^{\circ}
such that:

(1)

g^{-1}(k)=\{i_{1},i_{2}\}
consists of exactly two elements;

(2)

every other element of
\langle n^{\prime}-1\rangle
has exactly one preimage via
g
.

Let

s(\langle n^{\prime}\rangle)\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}

be a coCartesian lift for
g
starting at
s(\langle n^{\prime}\rangle)
.
Then
s(f)
belongs to
(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{R})^{\otimes}
if and only if the uniquely determined morphism
\gamma\colon\mathcal{B}\to s(\langle n^{\prime}-1\rangle)
does.
Unraveling the definitions, we have:

\mathcal{B}_{h}\simeq\begin{cases}s(\langle n^{\prime}\rangle)_{g^{-1}(h)}&\text{if }h\neq k\\
s(\langle n^{\prime}\rangle)_{i_{1}}\otimes s(\langle n^{\prime}\rangle)_{i_{2}}&\text{if }h=k.\end{cases}

In other words,
\mathcal{B}_{h}\simeq\mathcal{A}
if
h\neq k
and
\mathcal{B}_{k}\simeq\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}
.
With these conventions, we see that
\gamma_{h}
is (equivalent to) the identity of
\mathcal{A}
if
h\neq k
and it is the multiplication

\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}

when
h=k
.
Since this map belongs to
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{R}
by assumption, the conclusion follows in the active and surjective case.

We are left to deal with the case where
f\colon\langle n\rangle\to\langle m\rangle
is active and injective.
Let once again

s(\langle n\rangle)\longrightarrow\mathcal{B}

be the coCartesian lift of
f
starting at
s(\langle n\rangle)
.
Then
s(f)
belongs to
(\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{R})^{\otimes}
if and only if the uniquely determined morphism
\delta\colon\mathcal{B}\to s(\langle m\rangle)
does.
Unraveling the definitions, we see that

\mathcal{B}_{k}=\begin{cases}\mathcal{A}&\text{if }k\in\mathrm{Im}(f)\\
\mathrm{Mod}_{R}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

In these terms, the map
\delta_{k}
is the identity of
\mathcal{A}
if
k\in\mathrm{Im}(f)
, and it corresponds to the the map
\mathrm{Mod}_{R}\to\mathcal{A}
selecting the tensor unit of
\mathcal{A}
otherwise.
As the latter is in
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{R}
by assumption, the proof of point (1) follows.

As for statement (2), it follows from the fact that
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{R}\to\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{R}
is strong monoidal and commutes with small colimits together with
[
25
, Theorem 4.2.2.8]
.
∎

2.3.
Derived Azumaya algebras

Derived Azumaya algebras were introduced in

as derived counterpart of classical Azumaya algebras.
When

\mathcal{A}
of

In the derived setting, we give the following definition:

Definition 2.10
.

Let

be a derived scheme.
A

is an object
\mathcal{A}\in\mathrm{Alg}(\mathrm{Perf}(X))

(1)

(2)

\mathcal{A}

\mathrm{QCoh}(X)

Remark 2.11
.

In
[
35

The first condition can obviously be checked étale locally.
As for the second one,

3.
Categorified Beauville-Laszlo theorem

In this section we provide a categorification of the classical Beauville-Laszlo theorem.
Recall the basic setup: let
S=\Spec(A)
and
T=\Spec(B)
be two affine derived schemes.
Let
I\subseteq\pi_{0}(A)
be a finitely generated ideal.
Let

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S\ .}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

be a pullback diagram, where

denotes the open complementary of the closed subset determined by

For the rest of this section, we will be working under the following assumption:

Assumption 3.1
.

The map
f\colon T\to S
induces an equivalence between the

Then it is shown in

This is a far-reaching generalization of the classical Beauville-Laszlo theorem, originally formulated for vector bundles.
The main goal of this section is to prove the following
categorified

Theorem 3.2

The canonical restriction functor

(3.3)

\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{S}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{T}\times_{\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{V}}\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{U}

is an equivalence of symmetric monoidal
\infty
-categories.

Passing to dualizable (resp. invertible) objects in the above equivalence, we immediately obtain:

Corollary 3.4
.

The equivalence (

) restricts to equivalences

\mathsf{SmPr}^{\mathsf{cat}}_{S}\simeq\mathsf{SmPr}^{\mathsf{cat}}_{T}\times_{\mathsf{SmPr}^{\mathsf{cat}}_{V}}\mathsf{SmPr}^{\mathsf{cat}}_{U}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathsf{dAz}_{S}\simeq\mathsf{dAz}_{T}\times_{\mathsf{dAz}_{V}}\mathsf{dAz}_{U}\ .

In particular, we obtain a long exact sequence
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}(S)^{\times}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}(U)^{\times}\oplus\mathcal{O}(T)^{\times}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}(V)^{\times}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{dPic}(S)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{dPic}(U)\oplus\mathrm{dPic}(T)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{dPic}(V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{dBr}(S)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{dBr}(U)\oplus\mathrm{dBr}(T)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{dBr}(V)}
\delta_{0}
\delta_{1}

3.1.
General setup

Consider the commutative square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{S}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{T}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{U}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{V}\ .}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\ast}}

This induces a canonical functor

\Phi\colon\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{S}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{T}\times_{\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{V}}\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{U}\ ,

which admits a right adjoint
\Psi
that can be explicitly described as follows.
Let us represent an object in the fiber product
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{T}\times_{\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{V}}\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{U}
as a quintuple
\underline{\mathcal{C}}=(\mathcal{C}_{T},\mathcal{C}_{U},\mathcal{C}_{V},\alpha,\beta)
, where
\mathcal{C}_{T}\in\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{T}
,
\mathcal{C}_{U}\in\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{U}
,
\mathcal{C}_{V}\in\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{V}
and

\alpha\colon\mathcal{C}_{T}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{V}\qquad\text{(resp.\ }\beta\colon\mathcal{C}_{U}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{V}\text{)}

is a
\mathrm{QCoh}(T)
-linear (resp.
\mathrm{QCoh}(U)
-linear) functor inducing an equivalence
\mathcal{C}_{T}\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(T)}\mathrm{QCoh}(V)\simeq\mathcal{C}_{V}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}_{U}\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(U)}\mathrm{QCoh}(V)\simeq\mathcal{C}_{V}
).
Then
\Psi
sends such a datum to the fiber product

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi(\underline{\mathcal{C}})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{T})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{U})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{\ast}g_{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{V})\ .}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}

The functor
\Phi
restricts to the functor (
3.3
):

\Phi^{\omega}\colon\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{S}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{T}\times_{\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{V}}\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{U}\ .

Notice that a priori
\Psi
does not take
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{T}\times_{\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{V}}\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{U}
to
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{S}
.
Nevertheless, a mild variation of
[
35
, Theorem 0.2]
will show that this is true (see
3.11
).

3.2.
Full faithfulness

We start discussing the full faithfulness of
\Phi^{\omega}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{S}
is dualizable.
Then the unit transformation

\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\Psi(\Phi(\mathcal{C}))

is an equivalence.
In particular,
\Phi
restricts to a fully faithful functor

\Phi\colon\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{cg}}_{S}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{cg}}_{T}\times_{\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{cg}}_{V}}\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{cg}}_{U}\ .

Proof.

Unraveling the definitions, we have to check that for every
\mathcal{C}\in\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{cg}}_{S}
, the square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(S)}\mathrm{QCoh}(T)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(S)}\mathrm{QCoh}(U)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(S)}\mathrm{QCoh}(V)}

is a pullback square.
Thanks to
[
26
, Theorem 7.4.0.1]
, we know that the square

(3.6)

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{QCoh}(S)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{QCoh}(T)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{QCoh}(U)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{QCoh}(V)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j^{\ast}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\ast}}

is a pullback square.
Since
\mathcal{C}
is dualizable in
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{S}
, (
3.6
) remains a pullback after tensoring with
\mathcal{C}
, whence the first half.
As for the second statement, observe that
\mathrm{QCoh}(S)
is a rigid and compactly generated symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category.
Thus,
[
13
, Proposition D.5.4]
shows that if
\mathcal{C}
is compactly generated (hence dualizable in
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}
), it is dualizable in
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{S}
as well.
The conclusion follows.
∎

A little extra effort allows to pass from
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{cg}}
to
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}
:

Corollary 3.7
.

The functor

\Phi^{\omega}\colon\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{S}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{T}\times_{\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{V}}\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{U}

is fully faithful.

Proof.

The canonical inclusion
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{S}\to\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{cg}}_{S}
is faithful.
Thus,
3.5
immediately implies that the map

\Phi^{\omega}\colon\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{S}\longrightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{T}\times_{\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{V}}\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{U}

is faithful as well.
In order to check fullness, it is enough to check that given a functor

F\colon\mathcal{C}\longrightarrow\mathcal{D}

between compactly generated
\mathrm{QCoh}(S)
-linear
\infty
-categories, if
\Phi(F)
preserves compact objects, then so does
F
.
We know from the proof of
3.5
that the square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\ast}(\mathcal{D})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{\ast}(\mathcal{D})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g^{\ast}i^{\ast}(\mathcal{D})}

is a pullback.
Moreover, the map
i^{\ast}(\mathcal{D})\to g^{\ast}i^{\ast}(\mathcal{D})
is obtained by applying
\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(S)}-
to the map
g^{\ast}\mathrm{QCoh}(U)\to\mathrm{QCoh}(V)
.
Since the latter preserves compact objects and
\mathcal{D}
is compactly generated, the functoriality of the tensor product guarantees that
i^{\ast}(\mathcal{D})\to g^{\ast}i^{\ast}(\mathcal{D})
preserves compact objects.
A similar reasoning shows that
f^{\ast}(\mathcal{D})\to g^{\ast}i^{\ast}(\mathcal{D})
preserves compact objects as well.
Thus, the conclusion follows from
2.6
.
∎

3.3.
Essential surjectivity

We start with a very general statement:

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\underline{\mathcal{C}}=(\mathcal{C}_{T},\mathcal{C}_{U},\mathcal{C}_{V},\alpha,\beta)
be an element of
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{T}\times_{\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{V}}\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{U}
.
Then:

(1)

the natural transformation

i^{\ast}\Psi(\underline{\mathcal{C}})\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{U}

is an equivalence;

(2)

assume that
\mathcal{C}_{T}
is compactly generated.
Then the natural transformation

f^{\ast}\Psi(\underline{\mathcal{C}})\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{T}

is an equivalence.

Proof.

We start with statement (1).
Since
\mathrm{QCoh}(S)
is rigid and compactly generated, we see that
\mathrm{QCoh}(U)
Is a dualizable object in
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{cg}}_{S}
.
This implies that the square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{\ast}(\mathcal{C})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{\ast}f_{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{T})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{\ast}i_{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{U})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{\ast}i_{\ast}g_{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{V})}

is a pullback.
Moreover, since
U\to S
is a Zariski open immersion, the canonical map

\mathrm{QCoh}(U)\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(S)}\mathrm{QCoh}(U)\longrightarrow\mathrm{QCoh}(U)

is an equivalence.
This implies that for every
\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{U}
, the unit transformation
\mathcal{D}\to i^{\ast}i_{\ast}(\mathcal{D})
is an equivalence.
Besides, using the base change equivalence
i^{\ast}f_{\ast}\simeq g_{\ast}j^{\ast}
and the given identification
\mathcal{C}_{V}\simeq j^{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{T})
, we can rewrite the left square as

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i^{\ast}(\mathcal{C})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{\ast}j^{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{T})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{U}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g_{\ast}j^{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{T})\ .}

Since the right vertical map is an equivalence and the square is a pullback, the same goes for the left vertical map.

We now prove statement (2).
As above, we see that
\mathrm{QCoh}(T)
is a dualizable object in
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{cg}}_{S}
.
Thus, the square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\ast}(\mathcal{C})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\ast}f_{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{T})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\ast}i_{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{U})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\ast}f_{\ast}j_{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{V})}

is a pullback.
Using the base change equivalence
f^{\ast}i_{\ast}\simeq j_{\ast}g^{\ast}
, and the given identification
\mathcal{C}_{V}\simeq g^{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{U})
, we can rewrite it as

(3.9)

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\ast}(\mathcal{C})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\ast}f_{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{T})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j_{\ast}j^{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{T})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\ast}f_{\ast}j_{\ast}j^{\ast}(\mathcal{C}_{T})\ .}

On the other hand, since
\mathrm{QCoh}(T)
is dualizable as an object in
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{cg}}_{S}
, we see that the square (
3.6
) remains a pullback after tensoring with
\mathrm{QCoh}(T)
.
In other words, the diagram

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{QCoh}(T)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{QCoh}(T)\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(S)}\mathrm{QCoh}(T)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{QCoh}(U)\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(S)}\mathrm{QCoh}(T)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{QCoh}(V)\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(S)}\mathrm{QCoh}(T)}

is a pullback square.
Now,
\mathrm{QCoh}(T)
is a rigid and compactly generated symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category.
Since
\mathcal{C}_{T}
is compactly generated, we deduce that it is also a dualizable object in
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{T}
.
Thus, the square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{T}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{QCoh}(T)\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(S)}\mathrm{QCoh}(T)\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(T)}\mathcal{C}_{T}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{QCoh}(U)\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(S)}\mathrm{QCoh}(T)\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(T)}\mathcal{C}_{T}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{QCoh}(V)\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(S)}\mathrm{QCoh}(T)\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(T)}\mathcal{C}_{T}}

obtained from the previous one applying
-\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(T)}\mathcal{C}_{T}
, is a pullback.
Observe furthermore that we have a canonical identification

\mathrm{QCoh}(U)\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(S)}\mathrm{QCoh}(T)\simeq\mathrm{QCoh}(V)\ .

At this point, a simple diagram chase identifies the lower cospan of the above square with the one of (
3.9
).
Since both squares are pullback, we deduce that the canonical map

f^{\ast}(\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{T}

is an equivalence, whence the conclusion.
∎

To go any further, we need to study more in depth the functor
\Psi
.
More precisely, we need to know that it takes compactly generated gluing data to compactly generated
\infty
-categories.
For this, we need a couple of preliminaries.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
T=\Spec(R)
be an affine derived scheme and let
I\subseteq\pi_{0}(R)
be a finitely generated ideal.
Let
K
be the closed subset of
T
cut by
I
and let
U\coloneqq T\smallsetminus K
.
Let
\mathcal{D}\in\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{T}
and set

\mathcal{D}_{K}\coloneqq\mathrm{QCoh}_{K}(T)\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(T)}\mathcal{D}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{D}_{V}\coloneqq\mathrm{QCoh}(V)\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(T)}\mathcal{D}\ .

Then:

(1)

The functor
\mathcal{D}_{K}\to\mathcal{D}
is fully faithful and cocontinuous;

(2)

the functor
\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}_{V}
has a fully faithful right adjoint;

(3)

the pair
(\mathcal{D}_{K},\mathcal{D}_{V})
is a semiorthogonal decomposition of
\mathcal{D}
;

Assume additionally that
\mathcal{D}
is compactly generated.
Then:

(4)

\mathcal{D}_{K}
is compactly generated, and
\mathcal{D}_{K}^{\omega}=\mathcal{D}_{K}\cap\mathcal{D}^{\omega}
(equivalently, the inclusion
\mathcal{D}_{K}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}
preserves compact objects);

(5)

assume that
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a set of compact objects of
\mathcal{D}
that belong to
\mathcal{D}_{K}
and form a system of generators for
\mathcal{D}_{K}
, and that
\{E_{j}\}_{j\in J}
is a set of compact objects of
\mathcal{D}
whose restriction to
\mathcal{D}_{V}
provides a system of compact generators for
\mathcal{D}_{V}
.
Then
\{F_{i},E_{j}\}_{i\in I,j\in J}
is a system of compact generators for
\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

Combining
[
26
, Proposition 7.1.5.3 & Corollary 7.1.2.11]
we canonically identify the functor
\mathcal{D}_{K}\to\mathcal{D}
with the inclusion
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{Nil}(I)}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}
.
Moreover,
[
26
, Proposition 7.2.3.1]
guarantees that
(\mathrm{QCoh}_{K}(T),\mathrm{QCoh}(V))
is a semiorthogonal decomposition of
\mathrm{QCoh}(T)
.
Therefore, the square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{QCoh}_{K}(T)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{QCoh}(T)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{QCoh}(V)\ .}

is both a pullback and a pushout.
Applying
\mathcal{D}\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(T)}-
the resulting square

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}_{K}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}_{V}}

is again a pushout.
In particular,
\mathcal{D}_{V}
gets canonically identified with the localization of
\mathcal{D}
at
\mathcal{D}_{K}
, and therefore the canonical functor
\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}_{V}
admits a fully faithful right adjoint.
It immediately follows that
\mathcal{D}_{V}
is identified with
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{Loc}(I)}
, and therefore that
(\mathcal{D}_{K},\mathcal{D}_{V})
is a semiorthogonal decomposition of
\mathcal{D}
.
This proves statements (1), (2) and (3).

Statement (4) follows from
[
26
, Proopsition 7.1.1.12-(e)]
.
Finally, (5) is a formal consequence of the other points: indeed, let
M\in\mathcal{D}
be an object and assume that for all
i\in I
and
j\in J
, one has

\Map_{\mathcal{D}}(F_{i},M)\simeq\Map_{\mathcal{D}}(E_{j},M)\simeq 0\ .

Since the inclusion
\mathcal{D}_{K}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{D}
commutes with colimits and the
F_{i}
are compact generators for
\mathcal{D}_{K}
, it follows from the first set of vanishing that
\Map_{\mathcal{D}}(F,M)\simeq 0
for every
F\in\mathcal{D}_{K}
.
Thus,
M\in\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{Loc}(I)}
and at this point the second set of vanishing implies that
M\simeq 0
.
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\underline{\mathcal{C}}=(\mathcal{C}_{T},\mathcal{C}_{U},\mathcal{C}_{V},\alpha,\beta)
be an element of
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{T}\times_{\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{V}}\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{U}
.
If
\mathcal{C}_{T}
and
\mathcal{C}_{U}
are compactly generated, then so is
\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\mathcal{C}_{T}\times_{\mathcal{C}_{V}}\mathcal{C}_{U}
.

Proof.

Consider the commutative triangles

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{T}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{T}\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(T)}\mathrm{QCoh}(V)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{V}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}

and

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{U}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{U}\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(U)}\mathrm{QCoh}(V)}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{V}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}

By definition, the vertical maps are equivalences, hence in particular they preserve compact objects.
On the other hand, the functors

\mathrm{QCoh}(T)\longrightarrow\mathrm{QCoh}(V)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{QCoh}(U)\longrightarrow\mathrm{QCoh}(V)

preserve compact objects.
Since
\mathcal{C}_{T}
and
\mathcal{C}_{U}
are compactly generated, it follows that the horizontal maps in the above two triangles also preserve compact objects.
Thus,
\alpha
and
\beta
are automatically morphisms in
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}
.

Let
K
be the closed complementary of
V
inside
T
.
Then
3.10
guarantees that
(\mathcal{C}_{T})_{K}\coloneqq\mathrm{QCoh}_{K}(T)\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(T)}\mathcal{D}
is a compactly generated full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{T}
and that the inclusion
(\mathcal{C}_{T})_{K}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{T}
preserves compact objects.
Choose a system of compact generators
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
for
(\mathcal{C}_{T})_{K}
.
Their image in
\mathcal{C}_{V}
is zero, so every
F_{i}
automatically defines an object
\widetilde{F}_{i}=(F_{i},0)
in
\mathcal{C}
, which is compact in virtue of
2.6
.

Choose now a system of compact generators
\{G_{j}\}_{j\in J}
for
\mathcal{C}_{U}
.
Set
E_{j}\coloneqq G_{j}\oplus G_{j}[1]
.
Then
\{E_{j}\}_{j\in J}
is again a system of compact generators of
\mathcal{C}_{U}
.
It follows that
\{g^{\ast}(E_{j})\}_{j\in J}
is a system of compact generators for
\mathcal{C}_{V}
, and since

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathcal{C}_{T})_{K}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{T}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{V}}

is both a pullback and a pushout square, Thomason’s trick (
[
7
, Corollary 3.2.3]
) implies that each
g^{\ast}(E_{j})\simeq g^{\ast}(G_{j})\oplus g^{\ast}(G_{j})[1]
is of the form
j^{\ast}(E_{j}^{\prime})
for some
E_{j}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{T}^{\omega}
.
Therefore, the pair
(E_{j}^{\prime},E_{j})
defines an object
\widetilde{E}_{j}\in\mathcal{C}
, which is once again a compact object thanks to
2.6
.

Consider now the family
\{(\widetilde{F}_{i},\widetilde{E}_{j})\}_{i\in I,j\in J}
.
This is a system of compact objects of
\mathcal{C}
.
Its restriction to
\mathcal{C}_{U}
coincides with the family of compact generators
\{E_{j}\}_{j\in J}
, while its restriction to
\mathcal{C}_{T}
coincides with the system
\{F_{i},E^{\prime}_{j}\}_{i\in I,j\in J}
.
Applying
3.10
-(5), we see that this is a system of compact generators of
\mathcal{C}_{T}
, and therefore that
\{(\widetilde{F}_{i},\widetilde{E}_{j})\}_{i\in I,j\in J}
is a system of compact generators for
\mathcal{C}
.
Thus,
\mathcal{C}
is compactly generated itself.
∎

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
\underline{\mathcal{C}}=(\mathcal{C}_{T},\mathcal{C}_{U},\mathcal{C}_{V},\alpha,\beta)
be an element of
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{cg}}_{T}\times_{\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{cg}}_{V}}\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{cg}}_{U}
.
Then the fiber product

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{T}\lx@nobreakspace}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{U}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{C}_{V}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}

computed in
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{S}
, is equally a pullback in
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{cg}}_{S}
and in
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{S}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\mathrm{cg}}_{S}
is fully faithful inside
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}_{S}
, the statement in this case is directly a consequence of
3.11
.
For
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{S}
, observe that in this case
3.8
implies that the canonical maps

\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(S)}\mathrm{QCoh}(U)\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{U}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(S)}\mathrm{QCoh}(T)\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{T}

are equivalences.
Thus, we can identify the map
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}_{U}
(resp.
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}_{T}
) with the map obtained applying
\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(S)}-
to
\mathrm{QCoh}(S)\to\mathrm{QCoh}(U)
(resp.
\mathrm{QCoh}(S)\to\mathrm{QCoh}(T)
).
As the latter commute with compact objects, the same goes for the original morphisms.
Therefore the square in consideration is a square in
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{S}
.
Since its image in

Remark 4.9

The problem of the injectivity of the natural map

The following Lemma is a simple diagram chase.

Lemma 4.17
.

The kernel of the composite morphism

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Br}(X)[\ell]}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}(\mathfrak{X};\mathbb{G}_{m})[\ell]}

Recall now that by the classical Skolem-Noether Theorem

, there is a canonical injective homomorphism

In fact, if

can be realized as a (classical) Azumaya algebra on

For such

\ell
coprime to the residue characteristic of
S
, the map

This is not true for an arbitrary proper morphism

.
Notice that this is clearly the case if the transition maps

are surjective, for example when the relative Picard functor

is representable by a

However notice that the criterion provided by

is strictly finer than the vanishing of the

Example 4.18
.

Let

be a field and let

However, there are known examples where the Picard scheme

is not smooth, and in such cases we are not aware of any method to prove the vanishing of the whole

5.
Grothendieck existence theorem for
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes

Assume again that
S=\Spec(R)
is a noetherian ring, complete along an ideal
I
.
Let
p\colon X\to S
is a proper morphism.
Write
S_{n}\coloneqq\Spec(R/I^{n})
and set
X_{n}\coloneqq S_{n}\times_{S}X
.
As in the previous section we let

\mathfrak{X}\coloneqq\colim_{n}X_{n}\simeq\Spf(R,I)\times_{S}X

be the formal completion of
X
along the special fiber.
In
4.6
-(2) we proved that the natural map

\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X;\mathbb{G}_{m})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(\mathfrak{X};\mathbb{G}_{m})

is injective.
This was obtained by interpreting classes in
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X;\mathbb{G}_{m})
as derived Azumaya algebras and, ultimately, as smooth and proper
\infty
-categories linear over
X
.
On the other hand, every class in
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X;\mathbb{G}_{m})
can also be interpreted as a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe.
A
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe on the formal scheme
\mathfrak{X}
is exactly the given of a sequence
(\mathfrak{A}_{n},\phi_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, where each
\mathfrak{A}_{n}
is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe on
X_{n}
and
\phi_{n}
is an equivalence

\phi_{n}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{n}\simeq X_{n}\times_{X_{n+1}}\mathfrak{A}_{n+1}.

The formal injectivity therefore can be phrased as follows: if
\mathfrak{A}
is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe on
X
and we are given trivializations
\sigma_{n}

\sigma_{n}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{n}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X_{n}

as
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes over
X_{n}
,
together with
homotopies
h_{n}
making the diagrams

(5.1)

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{A}_{n}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{A}_{n+1}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X_{n}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X_{n+1},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{n}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sigma_{n+1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}\times i_{n}}

then there exists a global trivialization
\sigma\colon\mathfrak{A}\simeq\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X
as
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes over
X
.
The trivializations
\sigma_{n}
encode line bundles
\mathcal{L}_{n}\in\Pic(\mathfrak{A}_{n})
, and the homotopies
h_{n}
allow to promote these line bundles to a formal system

(\mathcal{L}_{n})_{n\geqslant 0}\in\lim_{n\geqslant 0}\Pic(\mathfrak{A}_{n})\ .

If we had at our disposal Grothendieck’s existence theorem for
\mathcal{A}
, this would allow to construct a global line bundle on
\mathfrak{A}
, which would be easily checked to be a trivialization.
However, Grothendieck’s existence theorem is only known for
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes that have global resolution property (see
[
1
, Corollary 1.7]
).
On the other hand, as proven by Totaro in
[
37
, Theorem 1.1]
, this is possible if and only if the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe is a quotient stack.
In turn, it was shown in
[
12
, Theorem 3.6]
that asking that a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe is a quotient stack is equivalent to ask that the associated class
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X;\mathbb{G}_{m})
is representable by a classical Azumaya algebra.

The goal of this section is to exploit our
4.4
to deduce that Grothendieck’s existence theorem holds for arbitrary
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes, see
5.26
below.
The key ingredient needed to deduce this result from
1.3
is the character decomposition for sheaves on a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe, originally obtained by Lieblich
[
23
]
and extended to the level of triangulated categories in
[

.
We briefly review this theory, taking the opportunity to recast the result of
[

]
in an
\infty

5.1.
Character decomposition for trivial
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes

The starting point is the trivial
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe over a base.
The simplest is to start working relative to the sphere spectrum
\mathbb{S}\in\Sp
, but the reader inexperienced with spectral algebraic geometry can safely replace
\mathbb{S}
by
\mathbb{Z}
: every construction will go through.
Let
S\coloneqq\Spec(\mathbb{S})\in\mathrm{dSt}_{\mathbb{S}}
be the associated spectral stack.
Denote by
\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
the classifying stack of the
flat
multiplicative group scheme.
Recall from
[
28
, Theorem 4.1]
that the following theorem holds true:

Theorem 5.2
.

There exists a symmetric monoidal equivalence

\Phi\colon\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m})\simeq\Fun(\mathbb{Z},\Sp)\ ,

where
\mathbb{Z}
denotes the
set
of integers, and where the tensor structure of the right hand side is given by Day’s convolution product.

It is easy to extend this result to the case where the base is no longer

, using the following standard fact:

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
F
and
G
be two derived stacks.
Assume that
\mathrm{QCoh}(G)
is compactly generated.
Then the canonical box product

\boxtimes\colon\mathrm{QCoh}(F)\otimes\mathrm{QCoh}(G)\longrightarrow\mathrm{QCoh}(F\times G)

is an equivalence.

Proof.

Observe that the box product is (contravariantly) functorial in both
F
and
G
.
Because
\mathrm{QCoh}(G)
is compactly generated, the functor
-\otimes\mathrm{QCoh}(G)
commutes with arbitrary limits in
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L}}
.
We can therefore reduce ourselves to the case where
F
is affine.
Say
F=\Spec(A)
.
Then
\mathrm{QCoh}(F)\simeq\mathrm{Mod}_{A}
is again compactly generated, so repeating the same argument reduces to the case where
G
is also affine, say
G\simeq\Spec(B)
.
In this case, we have to check that the box product

\boxtimes\colon\mathrm{Mod}_{A}\otimes\mathrm{Mod}_{B}\longrightarrow\mathrm{Mod}_{A\otimes B}

is an equivalence, and this is a particular case of
[
25
, Theorem 4.8.5.16-(4)]
.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
X
be a derived stack.
Then there are canonical equivalences

\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X)\simeq\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m})\otimes\mathrm{QCoh}(X)\simeq\Fun(\mathbb{Z},\mathrm{QCoh}(X))\ .

Let us fix some notation.

Notation 5.5
.

Let
X
be a derived stack and let
d\in\mathbb{Z}
be an integer.

•

We let
\mathcal{O}_{X}(d)\in\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X)
be the object corresponding to

concentrated in weight

\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X)\simeq\Fun(\mathbb{Z},\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m})
.

•

For
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X)
, we write
\mathcal{F}(d)\coloneqq\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(d)
.

•

Write
\pi_{X}\colon\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X\to X
for the canonical projection.
We let

\mathrm{T}_{X,d}\coloneqq\pi_{X}^{\ast}(-)(d)\colon\mathrm{QCoh}(X)\longrightarrow\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X)

be the functor sending
\mathcal{F}
to
\pi^{\ast}(\mathcal{F})(d)
.
Similarly, we let

\mathrm{wt}_{X,d}\coloneqq\pi_{\ast}((-)(-d))\colon\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m})\longrightarrow\Sp

\mathcal{G}\in\mathrm{QCoh}(X\times\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m})
to

Remark 5.6
.

Notice that for every integer

, we have canonical identifications

\mathrm{T}_{X,d}\simeq\mathrm{T}_{d}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{QCoh}(X)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{wt}_{X,d}\simeq\mathrm{wt}_{d}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{QCoh}(X)}\ ,

where

and

denote the same functors for the the base

.
In particular, for

\mathrm{T}_{X,d}(\mathcal{F})\simeq\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathrm{T}_{X,d}(\mathcal{O}_{X})\simeq\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(d)\ .

Committing a slight abuse of notation, we will often write
\mathcal{F}(d)
instead of
\mathrm{T}_{d}(\mathcal{F})
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}
be an integer and let
X
be a derived stack.
Then the functor
\mathrm{T}_{X,d}\colon\mathrm{QCoh}(X)\to\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X)
is fully faithful.
Furthermore, it is both left and right adjoint to the functor
\mathrm{wt}_{X,d}
.
Finally, both
\mathrm{T}_{X,d}

\mathrm{wt}_{X,d}

Proof.

Under the equivalence
\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X)\simeq\Fun(\mathbb{Z},\mathrm{QCoh}(X))
, the functor
\mathrm{T}_{X,d}
corresponds to the left Kan extension along

Since

coincides with right Kan extension.
On the other hand,
\mathrm{wt}_{X,d}
is canonically identified with restriction along

.
Thus, all statements follow.
∎

Remark 5.8

When

5.2.
Inertial actions and bandings

We need a brief digression concerning inertial actions, which are the fundamental ingredient to both define gerbes and to provide a generalization of
5.2
for a general
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe.

Let
f\colon Y\to X
be a morphism of derived stacks.
We let
\mathrm{I}_{Y}^{\bullet}X\coloneqq\check{\mathcal{C}}(f)
be the Čech nerve of
f
.
Notice that
[
24
, Proposition 6.1.2.11]
shows that this is a groupoid object in the sense of
[
24
, Definition 6.1.2.7]
.
We refer to it as the
self-intersection groupoid of
Y
inside
X
.
We let

\mathrm{B}_{Y}X\coloneqq|\mathrm{I}_{Y}^{\bullet}X|

be the geometric realization of the self-intersection groupoid.
It coincides with its classifying groupoid.
By construction, there is a canonical map

(5.9)

\mu\colon\mathrm{B}_{Y}X\longrightarrow X\ .

Notice that this map is sensitive to the infinitesimal geometry of
Y
inside
X
, and in particular does
not
factor as a projection to
Y
followed by the map to
X
.
Observe that the atlas map
u\colon Y\to\mathrm{B}_{Y}X
is by definition an epimorphism.
Since groupoids are effective in
\mathrm{dSt}
, we deduce that the Čech nerve
\check{\mathcal{C}}(u)
is canonically isomorphic to
\mathrm{I}_{Y}^{\bullet}X
.
Unraveling the definitions, we find more precisely:

Lemma 5.10
.

Then the map
\mu
induces a transformation
\mu_{\bullet}\colon\check{\mathcal{C}}(u)\to\check{\mathcal{C}}(f)
of simplicial objects which is homotopic to the identity.

Let
Y\to X
again be a morphism of derived stacks and consider the canonical diagonal map
\delta_{Y/X}\colon Y\to Y\times_{X}Y
.
We refer to the self-intersection groupoid of
\delta_{Y/X}
as the
inertia groupoid of
Y
relative to
X
.
We denote it by
\mathrm{I}^{\bullet}(Y/X)
and when
X
is clear out of the context we simply write
\mathrm{I}^{\bullet}Y
.
This is an object in
\Fun(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{dSt}_{/Y\times_{X}Y})
.
Notice that we have

\mathrm{I}^{0}(Y/X)\simeq Y,\qquad\mathrm{I}^{1}(Y/X)\simeq Y\times_{Y\times_{X}Y}Y.

In particular, this simplicial object has the usual inertia stack
\mathrm{I}(Y/X)
of
Y
relative to
X
as stack of morphisms, and
Y
as stack of objects.
Let
p_{1},p_{2}\colon Y\times_{X}Y\to Y
be the two projections.
Composing with
p_{1}
yields a groupoid object in
\Fun(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathrm{dSt}_{/Y})
, which is easily seen to be a group object.
We can therefore think of
\mathrm{I}^{\bullet}(Y/X)
as a group structure on
\mathrm{I}(Y/X)
.
Beware however that the choice of the projection is important, as using
p_{2}
instead of
p_{1}
yields the opposite group structure.
Notice further that the forgetful functor
\mathrm{dSt}_{/Y\times_{X}Y}\to\mathrm{dSt}_{/Y}
commutes with colimits.
In particular, computing the geometric realization in
\mathrm{dSt}_{/Y\times_{X}Y}
or in
\mathrm{dSt}_{/Y}
produces the same output, but with the difference that doing it in the second category allows us to identify
\mathrm{B}_{Y}(Y\times_{X}Y)
with the classifying stack of the group object
\mathrm{I}(Y/X)
.
For this reason, we simply denote this object by
\mathrm{B}_{Y}(\mathrm{I}(Y/X))
.
Finally, we define the map
\mathrm{act}\colon\mathrm{B}_{Y}(\mathrm{I}(Y/X))\to X
as the composition

\mathrm{act}\colon\mathrm{B}_{Y}(\mathrm{I}(Y/X))\simeq\mathrm{B}_{Y}(Y\times_{X}Y)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y\times_{X}Y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p_{2}}}{{\longrightarrow}}Y.

This map is not the projection, but the composite

Y\longrightarrow\mathrm{B}_{Y}(\mathrm{I}(Y/X))\xrightarrow{\mathrm{act}}Y

is canonically equivalent to the identity.
This implies that pulling back along
\mathrm{act}
we can endow objects (e.g. quasi-coherent sheaves) over
Y
with a canonical right action of the inertia stack
\mathrm{I}(Y/X)
.

We now turn to the notion of banding.
We start with the following easy construction:

Construction 5.11
.

Let
q\colon Y\to X
be a morphism of derived stacks.
The functor

q^{*}\colon\mathrm{dSt}_{/X}\longrightarrow\mathrm{dSt}_{/Y}

given by pullback along
q
admits a
right
adjoint
q_{*}\colon\mathrm{dSt}_{/Y}\to\mathrm{dSt}_{/X}
.
Since
q^{*}
is monoidal (with respect to the cartesian structures),
q_{*}
is lax-monoidal.
In particular, it gives rise to an adjunction

q^{*}\colon\mathrm{Mon}_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}^{\mathrm{gp}}(\mathrm{dSt}_{/X})\leftrightarrows\mathrm{Mon}_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}^{\mathrm{gp}}(\mathrm{dSt}_{/Y})\colon q_{*}.

Definition 5.12
.

Let
q\colon Y\to X
be a morphism of derived stacks and let
\mathrm{G}
be a derived group stack over
X
.
A
weak
\mathrm{G}
-banding on
Y
is a morphism

\alpha\colon\mathrm{G}\longrightarrow q_{*}(\mathrm{I}(Y/X))

in
\mathrm{Mon}_{\mathbb{E}_{1}}^{\mathrm{gp}}(\mathrm{dSt}_{/X})
, where
\mathrm{I}(Y/X)
is considered as a group via the structure introduced in
Section

5.2
.
A weak
\mathrm{G}
-banding
\alpha
is said to be a
\mathrm{G}
-banding
if
\alpha
is an equivalence.

Let
q\colon Y\to X
be a morphism of derived stacks and let
\alpha
be a weak
\mathrm{G}
-banding on
Y
.
By adjunction, it corresponds to a morphism of group stacks over
Y

q^{*}\mathrm{G}\longrightarrow\mathrm{I}(Y/X).

Applying the deloop functor, we obtain a canonical map

\varpi_{\alpha}\colon q^{*}(\mathrm{B}_{X}(\mathrm{G}))\simeq\mathrm{B}_{Y}(q^{*}\mathrm{G})\longrightarrow\mathrm{B}_{Y}(\mathrm{I}(Y/X)).

Composing with
\mathrm{act}\colon\mathrm{B}_{Y}(\mathrm{I}(Y/X))\to Y
we obtain a map

(5.13)

\mathrm{act}_{\alpha}\coloneqq\mathrm{act}\circ\varpi_{\alpha}\colon q^{*}(\mathrm{B}_{X}(\mathrm{G}))\longrightarrow Y.

Observe that the composition of
\mathrm{act}_{\alpha}
with the atlas
u\colon Y\to q^{*}(\mathrm{B}_{X}(\mathrm{G}))
is canonically equivalent to the identity of
Y
.
In particular, we have:

Lemma 5.14
.

Assume that
\mathrm{G}
is flat group stack over
X
.
Then the functor

\mathrm{act}_{\alpha}^{*}\colon\mathrm{QCoh}(Y)\longrightarrow\mathrm{QCoh}(q^{*}(\mathrm{B}_{X}(\mathrm{G})))

is flat.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{G}
is flat over
X
, the induced map
Y\to q^{*}(\mathrm{B}_{X}(\mathrm{G}))
is flat as well.
Therefore the conclusion simply follows from the fact that
\mathrm{act}_{\alpha}\circ u
is the identity of
Y
.
∎

Example 5.15
.

We unravel the construction of
\mathrm{act}_{\alpha}
in the special case where
X=\Spec(\mathbb{S})
and
Y=\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
We have a canonical identification

\mathrm{I}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m})\simeq\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}\ ,

where the group structure relative to
\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
is simply the one induced by the absolute group structure of
\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
We have

q_{\ast}([\mathbb{G}_{m}/\mathbb{G}_{m}])\simeq\mathbb{G}_{m}\ ,

and the banding
\alpha
in this case is the identity.
As a consequence, the map
q^{\ast}(\mathbb{G}_{m})\to\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}
is the identity as well.
Consider now the commutative triangle

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{G}_{m})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\ ,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu}

where
u
is the atlas map and
\Delta
the diagonal.
Notice that there is a natural identification
\mathrm{B}_{\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{G}_{m})\simeq\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
Under the equivalence
\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m})\simeq\Fun(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z},\Sp)
, let us write for
\mathcal{O}(p,q)
for the line bundle on
\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
concentrated in the bigrade
(p,q)
.
Then a map
f\colon X\to\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
is completely determined by the pair of line bundles
(f^{\ast}(\mathcal{O}(1,0)),f^{\ast}(\mathcal{O}(0,1)))
.
In these terms, the map
\Delta
is classified by the pair
(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}}(1),\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}}(1))
, and the map
u
by the pair
(\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}}(1),\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}})
.
By construction we have

\mu^{\ast}(\mathcal{O}(1,0))\simeq\mathcal{O}(1,0)\ .

On the other hand, if
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
is such that
\mu^{\ast}(\mathcal{O}(0,1))\simeq\mathcal{O}(p,q)
, then the commutativity of the above diagram forces

\mathcal{O}(p)\simeq u^{\ast}(\mathcal{O}(p,0))\otimes u^{\ast}(\mathcal{O}(0,q))\simeq u^{\ast}(\mathcal{O}(p,q))\simeq u^{\ast}\mu^{\ast}(\mathcal{O}(0,1))\simeq\Delta^{\ast}(\mathcal{O}(0,1))\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}}(1)\ ,

and therefore
p=1
.
On the other hand, write
\mu_{\bullet}\colon\check{\mathcal{C}}(u)\to\check{\mathcal{C}}(\Delta)
for the map of simplicial objects induced by
\mu
.

5.10
guarantees that the map

\mu_{1}\colon\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}\longrightarrow\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}

is homotopic to the identity.
On the other hand, unraveling the definitions we see that if
\mu^{\ast}(\mathcal{O}(0,1))\simeq\mathcal{O}(1,q)
, then
\mu_{1}
is induced by the homomorphism of groups
(-)^{q}\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
Thus,
q=1
as well.

In conclusion,
\mu^{\ast}(\mathcal{O}(0,1))\simeq\mathcal{O}(1,1)
.
It follows that
\mu
actually coincides with the multiplication map
m\colon\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
.

GAGA theorem for twisted sheaves

We start with a brief review of the notion of

-gerbes and twisted sheaves (see

,

,
5

Definition 5.16
.

Let
X
be a derived stack.
A
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe on
X
is a pair
(\mathfrak{A},\alpha)
, where:

(1)

\pi\colon\mathfrak{A}\to X
is a derived stack over
X
such that both the structural map
\pi
and the diagonal
\delta_{\pi}\colon\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{A}\times_{X}\mathfrak{A}
are epimorphism;

(2)

a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-banding
\alpha\colon X\times\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\pi_{*}\mathrm{I}(\mathfrak{A}/X)
(see
5.12
) .

Construction 5.17
.

Let
(\mathfrak{A},\alpha)
be a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe over
X
and fix an integer
d\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Let

p\colon\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathfrak{A}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{A}\qquad\text{and}\qquad u\colon\mathfrak{A}\longrightarrow\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathfrak{A}

for the canonical projection and the atlas map, respectively.
We define the functor

\delta_{d}\colon\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathfrak{A})\longrightarrow\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathfrak{A})

by setting

\delta_{d}(\mathcal{F})\coloneqq u^{\ast}\big(p^{\ast}\big(p_{\ast}(\mathrm{act}_{\alpha}^{\ast}(\mathcal{F})(-d)\big)(d)\big)\ ,

where
\mathrm{act}_{\alpha}
is the map (
5.13
).
Since
\mathrm{act}_{\alpha}\circ u\simeq\mathrm{id}_{\mathfrak{A}}
, the unit of the adjunction
p^{\ast}\dashv p_{\ast}
induces a canonical natural transformation

j_{d}\colon\delta_{d}\longrightarrow\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathfrak{A})}\ .

Definition 5.18
.

Let
X
be a derived stack and let
(\mathfrak{A},\alpha)
be a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe on
X
.
Let
d\in\mathbb{Z}
be an integer.
We say that a quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathfrak{A})
is
d
-homogeneous
if the canonical map
j_{d,\mathcal{F}}\colon\delta_{d}(\mathcal{F})\to\mathcal{F}
is an equivalence.
We let
\mathrm{QCoh}_{d}(\mathfrak{A})
denote the full subcategory of
\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathfrak{A})
spanned by
d
-homogeneous sheaves.

Notation 5.19
.

For a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe
(\mathfrak{A},\alpha)
on a derived stack
X
and an integer
d\in\mathbb{Z}
, we set
\mathrm{Perf}_{d}(\mathfrak{A})\coloneqq\mathrm{Perf}(\mathfrak{A})\cap\mathrm{QCoh}_{d}(\mathfrak{A})
.

Lemma 5.20
.

Let
(\mathfrak{A},\alpha)
be a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe over a derived stack
X
.
Fix an integer
d\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then:

(1)

The functor
\delta_{d}
is
t
-exact.
In particular a quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathfrak{A})
is
d
-homogeneous if and only if
\pi_{i}(\mathcal{F})
is
d
-homogeneous for every
i\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

If

X\simeq\colim_{i\in I}X_{i}

inside
\mathrm{dSt}
, then

\mathrm{QCoh}_{d}(\mathfrak{A})\simeq\lim_{i\in I^{\mathrm{op}}}\mathrm{QCoh}_{d}(X_{i}\times_{X}\mathfrak{A})\ .

(3)

If
(\mathfrak{A},\alpha)
is the trivial
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe
\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X
, then a quasi-coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X)
is
d
-homogeneous if and only if it is of the form
\mathcal{G}(d)
for some
\mathcal{G}\in\mathrm{QCoh}(X)
.
In particular, the functor
\mathrm{T}_{X,d}\colon\mathrm{QCoh}(X)\to\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X)
restricts to an equivalence

\mathrm{QCoh}(X)\simeq\mathrm{QCoh}_{d}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times X)\ .

Proof.

For point (1), it is enough to observe that

\delta_{d}(\mathcal{F})\simeq p_{\ast}(\mathrm{act}_{\alpha}^{\ast}(\mathcal{F})(-d))\ .

Now,
\mathrm{act}_{\alpha}^{\ast}
is
t
-exact thanks to
5.14
, while
p_{\ast}\colon\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathfrak{A})
is
t
-exact because it is identified with evaluation at
0\in\mathbb{Z}
under the equivalence
\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathfrak{A})\simeq\Fun(\mathbb{Z},\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathfrak{A}))
(see
5.7
).

For point (2), observe first that since
\mathrm{dSt}
is an
\infty
-topos the canonical map

\colim_{i\in I}X_{i}\times_{X}\mathfrak{A}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{A}

is an equivalence.
The conclusion now follows from the observation that if
f_{i}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{i}\coloneqq X_{i}\times_{X}\mathfrak{A}\to\mathfrak{A}
is the canonical map, then the induced natural transformation

f_{i}^{\ast}\circ\delta_{d,\mathfrak{A}}\longrightarrow\delta_{d,\mathfrak{A}_{i}}\circ f_{i}^{\ast}

is an equivalence.

We are left to check point (3).
Since
\delta_{d}
is
\mathrm{QCoh}(X)
-linear, we can simply assume
X=\Spec(\mathbb{S})
.
In virtue of
5.15
, we see that
\mathrm{act}_{\alpha}
coincides in this case with the multiplication map

m\colon\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\longrightarrow\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\ .

Fix
\mathcal{F}\in\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m})
and for
d\in\mathbb{Z}
write

\mathcal{G}_{d}\coloneqq\mathrm{wt}_{d}(\mathcal{F})\in\Sp\ ,

so that the canonical map

\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{G}_{k}(k)\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}

is an equivalence.
It follows that

m^{\ast}(\mathcal{F})\simeq\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{G}_{k}(k,k)\ .

Therefore,

\delta_{d}(\mathcal{F})\simeq\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}p_{\ast}(\mathcal{G}_{d}(k,k)(-d,0))\simeq\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathcal{G}_{k}\otimes p_{\ast}(\mathcal{O}(k-d,k))\ ,

where
p\colon\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathrm{B}\mathbb{G}_{m}
is the second projection.
Now,
p_{\ast}(\mathcal{O}(k-d,k))
is non-zero if and only if
k=d
.
Besides, in this case we have
\mathcal{O}(0,d)\simeq p^{\ast}(\mathcal{O}(d))
, so the full faithfulness of
p^{\ast}
(guaranteed by
5.7
) immediately yields

p_{\ast}(\mathcal{O}(0,d))\simeq\mathcal{O}(d)\ .

Thus,

\delta_{d}(F)\simeq\mathcal{G}_{d}(d)\simeq\mathrm{T}_{d}(\mathrm{wt}_{d}(\mathcal{F}))\ .

The conclusion follows.
∎

.

-(3).

As a consequence of

]
.

Definition 5.23
.

Let
X
be a derived stack and let
\alpha\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}(X;\mathbb{G}_{m})

The following result has been obtained by M. Lieblich at the level of abelian categories
[

, and it has recently been extended to the derived categories by D. Bergh and O. Schnürer
[
5
]
for underived schemes.
We are now in position to generalize to the much vaster class of geometric derived stacks in the sense of Simpson

Remark 5.24
.

Without repeating the inductive definition of geometric derived stack here, let us recall to the reader that this class includes: derived schemes, derived algebraic spaces, derived (higher) Deligne-Mumford stacks, derived (higher) Artin stacks. The key difference between geometric derived stacks and Artin stacks lies in the very weak conditions on the diagonal that are imposed in the former; for instance, we allow derived stacks

admitting a smooth cover

, where each

is a derived affine such that each fiber product

are (not necessarily separated) derived Artin stacks (i.e. each

has itself a smooth atlas and its diagonal is representable by – not necessarily separated – algebraic spaces).

Theorem 5.25

(Lieblich, Bergh-Schnürer)
.

Let
X
be a geometric derived stack and let
\mathfrak{A}
be a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe on

Then:

(1)

Let

and let

be the associated
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe on

Then pullback along the structural map

induces an equivalence

(2)

The

introduced in

induce an equivalence

\mathrm{D}\colon\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathfrak{A})\longrightarrow\prod_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{QCoh}_{d}(\mathfrak{A}).

(3)

The decomposition functor
\mathrm{D}
of the previous point induces a fully faithful functor

\mathrm{Perf}(\mathfrak{A})\longrightarrow\prod_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Perf}_{d}(\mathfrak{A})\ ,

whose essential image consists of those elements
(\mathcal{F}_{d})_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}
such that for every map

from an affine derived scheme

f^{\ast}(F_{d})=0
for all but a finite number of integers.

Proof.

When
X
is an underived scheme, statement (1) follows from
[
5

, and statement (2) follows from Theorem 5.4 in loc. cit.
We sketch the proof in the general case.
Thanks to
5.20
-(2), the three statements are smooth-local relative to

.
We can therefore assume that

is a derived affine scheme and that the gerbe is trivial.
Since the functors

and

are
\mathrm{QCoh}(X)
-linear, we can further reduce to the case where

.
In this case, (1) follows from

, and (2) is an immediate consequence of

.
We are left checking statement (3).
Notice that every perfect complex

on

is compact in

Using point (2), we see that the canonical map

Corollary 5.26
.

Let
S
be as at the beginning of
Section

5
and let

be a morphism in
\mathrm{dSt}
, where

is a quasi-compact geometric derived stack.
Let

be a

-gerbe.
Assume that

Then
\mathfrak{A}
is categorically proper as well, i.e. the canonical functor

\mathrm{Perf}(\mathfrak{A})\longrightarrow\lim_{n}\mathrm{Perf}(\mathfrak{A}_{n})

is also a symmetric monoidal equivalence of stable symmetric monoidal

Proof.

Consider the natural map

\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathfrak{A})\longrightarrow\lim_{n}\mathrm{QCoh}(\mathfrak{A}_{n}).

Using the decomposition provided by
5.25
, we can rewrite it as

\prod_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{QCoh}_{d}(\mathfrak{A})\longrightarrow\lim_{n}\prod_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{QCoh}_{d}(\mathfrak{A}_{n}).

Consider the diagrams

\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Perf}(\mathfrak{A})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lim_{n}\mathrm{Perf}(\mathfrak{A}_{n})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\prod_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Perf}_{d}(\mathfrak{A})}
\displaystyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lim_{n}\prod_{d\in\mathbb{Z}}\mathrm{Perf}_{d}(\mathfrak{A}_{n})\ .}

As a consequence of
5.25
-(2), we see that the vertical arrows are fully faithful.
On the other hand,
5.21
implies that
\mathrm{QCoh}_{\chi}(\mathfrak{A})
is invertible in
\mathcal{P}\mathrm{r}^{\mathrm{L},\omega}_{X}
.
In particular, it is smooth and proper and hence

We are therefore left to prove essential surjectivity.
Fix an element

Since

is quasi-compact,

has only finitely many non-zero weights by

for every

Thus, it follows that

5.4.
Derived Azumaya algebra vs.
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes: a dictionary

Derived Azumaya algebras and
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes are connected in a rather indirect way: both objects can be used to represent classes of
\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\acute{e}t}}(X;\mathbb{G}_{m})
.
It is nevertheless possible to provide a direct dictionary between the two.
Since the appearance of the first version of this paper, G. Nocera and M. Pernice made this dictionary explicit in
[
29
]
.
Their method converts a derived Azumaya algebra into a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe by looking at the stack of its “positive trivializations”, and Proposition 2.9 in
loc. cit.
settles the following statement, that appeared as a conjecture in the first version of this paper (motivated by
[
5
, Corollary 5.9]
):

Conjecture 5.27
.

Let
X
be a quasi-compact and quasi-separated scheme.
Let
(\mathfrak{A},\alpha)
and
(\mathfrak{A}^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime})
be two
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbes on
X
.
Let
d,d^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
be two integers.
Then there exists a canonical equivalence

\mathrm{QCoh}_{d}(\mathfrak{A})\otimes_{\mathrm{QCoh}(X)}\mathrm{QCoh}_{d^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{A}^{\prime})\simeq\mathrm{QCoh}_{(d,d^{\prime})}(\mathfrak{A}\times_{X}\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}),

where
\mathfrak{A}\times_{X}\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
is considered as
\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe.
Furthermore let
\mathfrak{A}\star\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}
be
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-gerbe on
X
classifying the product
\alpha\alpha^{\prime}\in\mathrm{H}^{2}_{\mathrm{\'{e}t}}(X;\mathbb{G}_{m})
.
Then pulling back along the canonical projection map of
[
5
, Construction 3.8]

\rho_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}\colon\mathfrak{A}\times_{X}\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{A}\star\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}

yields an equivalence

\rho_{\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}}^{*}\colon\mathrm{QCoh}_{1}(\mathfrak{A}\star\mathfrak{A}^{\prime})\simeq\mathrm{QCoh}_{(1,1)}(\mathfrak{A}\times_{X}\mathfrak{A}^{\prime}).

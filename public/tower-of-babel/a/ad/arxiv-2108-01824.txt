Asymptotic stability of the superposition of viscous contact wave with rarefaction waves for the compressible Navier-Stokes-Maxwell equations
Huancheng Yao, Changjiang Zhu

Abstract
We study the large-time asymptotic behavior of solutions toward the combination of a viscous contact wave with two rarefaction waves for the compressible non-isentropic Navier-Stokes equations coupling with the Maxwell equations through the Lorentz force (called the Navier-Stokes-Maxwell equations). It includes the electrodynamic effects into the dissipative structure of the hyperbolic-parabolic system and turns out to be more complicated than that in the simpler compressible Navier-Stokes equations. Based on a new observation of the specific structure of the Maxwell equations in the Lagrangian coordinates, we prove that this typical composite wave pattern is time-asymptotically stable for the Navier-Stokes-Maxwell equations under some smallness conditions on the initial perturbations and wave strength, and also under the assumption that the dielectric constant is bounded. The main result is proved by using elementary energy methods. This is the first result about the nonlinear stability of the combination of two different wave patterns for the compressible Navier-Stokes-Maxwell equations. 2021 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35Q30, 76N06, 76N30, 35Q61. Keywords: compressible non-isentropic Navier-Stokes-Maxwell equations, composite wave, asymptotic behavior, electromagnetic field, dielectric constant.

Introduction and main results

1.1
The problem

Plasma dynamics is a field of studying flow problems of electrically conducting fluids.
The research objects of plasma dynamics are very broad.
A complete analysis in this field consists of the study of the gasdynamic field, the electromagnetic field and the radiation field simultaneously (see
[
36
]
).
In the present paper, we consider the flow of an electrically conducting fluid under the influence of the electromagnetic field.
At the macroscopic level, the motion of this electrically conducting fluid such as the movement in the electromagnetic field generated by itself is described by hydrodynamics equations,
for example the compressible Navier-Stokes equations.
Because the dynamic motion of the fluid and the electromagnetic field interact tightly on each other,
the governing system in the non-isentropic case is derived from fluid mechanics with appropriate modifications to account for the electromagnetic effects,
which is composed of the laws of conservation of mass, momentum and energy, Maxwell’s law, and the law of conservation of electric charge (see

Here, we briefly give some remarks on our problem and review some key analytical techniques.
To state our ideas clearly, we first consider the stability of only a viscous contact discontinuity wave.

Comparing with the stability of wave patterns for the Navier-Stokes equations, the main difficulties in the present paper to prove the nonlinear stability of wave patterns lie in the additional terms produced by the electrodynamic effects.
The extra terms about electromagnetic field
E
and
b
in the fluid equations part of (
1.7
) are
-v(E+ub)b
and

.
They both contain no derivatives of the solution,
which is disadvantageous to the derivation of the zero-order energy estimates.
The first bad term we suffered is
-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}v(E+\psi b+\bar{u}b)\psi b\,{\rm{d}}x{\rm{d}}\tau
.
However, the lack of damping decay mechanism of the magnetic field

hinders us from obtaining the time-space integrable good term
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}b^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}\tau
.
It brings a lot of difficulties when deriving the zero-order estimates.
To overcome this obstacle, we try to use the structure of the Maxwell equations and package extra terms together with the bad term

\lambda_{1}=-\sqrt{\frac{\gamma p}{v}}<0,\qquad\lambda_{2}=0,\qquad\lambda_{3}=\sqrt{\frac{\gamma p}{v}}>0,

The basic theory of hyperbolic systems of conservation laws (for example, see Ref.

(v_{-},u_{-},\theta_{-})
with

,

such that for any

,
the Riemann problem
(
1.11
)
and
(
1.12
)
admits a solution consisting of the basic wave patterns.

In this paper, we will conclude the asymptotic stability of the linear combination of a viscous contact wave and two rarefaction waves for the Cauchy problem of the compressible non-isentropic
Navier-Stokes-Maxwell equations
(
1.7
)
,
(
1.8
)
,
(
1.10

We first consider the case of only a viscous contact discontinuity wave.
When omitting the effect of the electromagnetic field, the system (
1.7
) reduces to the classical Navier-Stokes equations.
Recalling for the Riemann problem (
1.11
)–(
1.12
), the contact discontinuity wave
(v^{c},u^{c},\theta^{c})(x,t)
takes the form

(v^{c},u^{c},\theta^{c})(x,t)=\left\{\begin{aligned} \left(v_{-},u_{-},\theta_{-}\right),\quad&x<0,\;t>0,\\[5.69054pt]
\left(v_{+},u_{+},\theta_{+}\right),\quad&x>0,\;t>0,\end{aligned}\right.

provided that

u_{-}=u_{+},\qquad p_{-}:=\frac{R\theta_{-}}{v_{-}}=\frac{R\theta_{+}}{v_{+}}=:p_{+}.

(1.13)

In the setting of the compressible Navier-Stokes equations, the corresponding wave
(\bar{v},\bar{u},\bar{\theta})
to the contact discontinuity
(v^{c},u^{c},\theta^{c})
behaves as a diffusion wave due to the dissipation effect, which called a viscous contact wave (see
[
13
]
,
[
8
]
).
Now we construct the viscous contact wave
(\bar{v},\bar{u},\bar{\theta})
as follows.
Since the pressure for the profile
(\bar{v},\bar{u},\bar{\theta})
is hoped to be almost constant (see
[
11
]
), we take

\bar{p}:=\frac{R\bar{\theta}}{\bar{v}}=p_{+},

which indicates the leading part of the temperature equation is

\frac{R}{\gamma-1}\bar{\theta}_{t}+p_{+}\bar{u}_{x}=\kappa\left(\frac{\bar{\theta}_{x}}{\bar{v}}\right)_{x}.

(1.14)

The equations (
1.14
) and
\bar{v}_{t}-\bar{u}_{x}=0
can deduce a nonlinear diffusion equation:

\bar{\theta}_{t}=a\left(\frac{\bar{\theta}_{x}}{\bar{\theta}}\right)_{x},\quad\bar{\theta}(\pm\infty,t)=\theta_{\pm},\quad a=\frac{\kappa p_{+}(\gamma-1)}{\gamma R^{2}}>0.

(1.15)

Then we have the following lemma (cf.
[
7
]
,
[
39
]

Lemma 1.1
.

Set the strength of wave
\delta:=\left|\theta_{+}-\theta_{-}\right|
.
Then the problem (
1.15

\bar{\theta}(\xi)
,
\xi=\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+t}}
satisfying

\mathrm{(i)}

\bar{\theta}(\xi)
is a monotone function, increasing if
\theta_{-}<\theta_{+}
and decreasing if
\theta_{-}>\theta_{+};

\mathrm{(ii)}

There exists a positive constant
\bar{\delta}

\delta\leq\bar{\delta}

\bar{\theta}(\frac{x}{\sqrt{1+t}})
satisfies

(1+t)^{\frac{k}{2}}\left|\partial_{x}^{k}\bar{\theta}\right|+\left|\bar{\theta}-\theta_{\pm}\right|\leq c_{1}\delta\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\hat{c}x^{2}}{1+t}},\quad\text{ as }|x|\rightarrow\infty,\;\;k\geq 1,

(1.16)

where

\theta_{-}
and
\bar{\delta}

Once

(\bar{v},\bar{u},\bar{\theta})(x,t)
can be defined as follows:

\bar{v}(x,t)=\frac{R}{p_{+}}\bar{\theta},\qquad\bar{u}(x,t)=u_{-}+\frac{\kappa(\gamma-1)}{\gamma R}\frac{\bar{\theta}_{x}}{\bar{\theta}},\qquad\bar{\theta}(x,t)=\bar{\theta}.

(1.17)

Importantly,
the contact wave
(\bar{v},\bar{u},\bar{\theta})(x,t)
solves the compressible Navier-Stokes equations time asymptotically, i.e.,

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\bar{v}_{t}-\bar{u}_{x}=0,\\[5.69054pt]
&\bar{u}_{t}+\left(\frac{R\bar{\theta}}{\bar{v}}\right)_{x}=\mu\left(\frac{\bar{u}_{x}}{\bar{v}}\right)_{x}+R_{1},\\[5.69054pt]
&\frac{R}{\gamma-1}\bar{\theta}_{t}+{p}_{+}\bar{u}_{x}=\kappa\left(\frac{\bar{\theta}_{x}}{\bar{v}}\right)_{x}+\mu\frac{\bar{u}^{2}_{x}}{\bar{v}}+R_{2},\\[5.69054pt]
\end{aligned}\right.

where

\displaystyle R_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa(\gamma-1)}{\gamma R}\left((\ln\bar{\theta})_{xt}-\mu\left(\frac{p_{+}}{R\bar{\theta}}(\ln\bar{\theta})_{xx}\right)_{x}\right)

\displaystyle=O(\delta)(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\hat{c}x^{2}}{1+t}},\qquad\text{ as }|x|\rightarrow\infty,

\displaystyle R_{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\kappa(\gamma-1)}{\gamma R}\right)^{2}\left((\ln\bar{\theta})_{x}(\ln\bar{\theta})_{xt}-\mu\left(\frac{p_{+}}{R\bar{\theta}}(\ln\bar{\theta})_{x}(\ln\bar{\theta})_{xx}\right)_{x}\right)

\displaystyle=O(\delta)(1+t)^{-2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\hat{c}x^{2}}{1+t}},\qquad\text{ as }|x|\rightarrow\infty.

Define the perturbation of the solution as

(\phi,\psi,\zeta)(x,t)=(v-\bar{v},u-\bar{u},\theta-\bar{\theta})(x,t).

For interval

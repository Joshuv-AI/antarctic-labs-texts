Abstract
Let G G be a graph with vertex set V V and of order n = | V | n=|V| , and let δ ⁡ ( G ) \delta(G) and Δ ⁡ ( G ) \Delta(G) be the minimum and maximum degree of G G , respectively. Two disjoint sets V 1 , V 2 ⊆ V V_{1},V_{2}\subseteq V form a coalition in G G if none of them is a dominating set of G G but their union V 1 ∪ V 2 V_{1}\cup V_{2} is. A vertex partition Ψ = { V 1 , … , V k } \Psi=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}\} of V V is a coalition partition of G G if every set V i ∈ Ψ V_{i}\in\Psi is either a dominating set of G G with the cardinality | V i | = 1 |V_{i}|=1 , or is not a dominating set but for some V j ∈ Ψ V_{j}\in\Psi , V i V_{i} and V j V_{j} form a coalition. The maximum cardinality of a coalition partition of G G is the coalition number 𝒞 ⁡ ( G ) \mathcal{C}(G) of G G . Given a coalition partition Ψ = { V 1 , … , V k } \Psi=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}\} of G G , a coalition graph CG ⁡ ( G , Ψ ) {\rm CG}(G,\Psi) is associated on Ψ \Psi such that there is a one-to-one correspondence between its vertices and the members of Ψ \Psi , where two vertices of CG ⁡ ( G , Ψ ) {\rm CG}(G,\Psi) are adjacent if and only if the corresponding sets form a coalition in G G . In this paper, we partially solve one of the open problems posed in Haynes et al. [ 1 ] and we solve two open problems posed by Haynes et al. [ 2 ] . We characterize all graphs G G with δ ⁡ ( G ) ≤ 1 \delta(G)\leq 1 and 𝒞 ⁡ ( G ) = n \mathcal{C}(G)=n , and we characterize all trees T T with 𝒞 ⁡ ( T ) = n − 1 \mathcal{C}(T)=n-1 . We determine the number of coalition graphs that can be defined by all coalition partitions of a given path. Furthermore, we show that there is no universal coalition path, a path whose coalition partitions defines all possible coalition graphs.

Let

and

3

Given a coalition partition

A

Trees with

In this section, we characterize trees
T
of order
n
satisfying
C(T)=n
. First we characterize graphs
G
of order
n
and
\delta(G)=0
such that
\mathcal{C}(G)=n
.

Theorem 4

If
G
is a graph of order
n
with
\delta(G)=0
, then
\mathcal{C}(G)=n
if and only if
G\cong K_{1}\cup K_{n-1}
.

Proof.

Clearly,
\mathcal{C}(K_{1}\cup K_{n-1})=n
. Conversely, suppose that
G
is a graph of order
n
with
\delta(G)=0
and
\mathcal{C}(G)=n
. Thus,
\{\{v\}\colon v\in V(G)\}
is a
\mathcal{C}
-partition of
G
. Let
x
be an isolated vertex of
G
. If
v
and
v^{\prime}
are two distinct vertices in
V(G)\backslash\{x\}
, then since the set
\{v,v^{\prime}\}
does not dominate the graph
G
, the pair
\{v\}
and
\{v^{\prime}\}
do not form a coalition. Therefore,
\{v\}
and
\{x\}
form a coalition for each
v\in V(G)\backslash\{x\}
, implying that
V(G)\backslash\{x\}
is a clique in
G
of cardinality
n-1
. Thus,
G\cong K_{1}\cup K_{n-1}
.
\Box

We next define a family
\mathcal{F}_{1}
of graphs.

Definition 1

(The family
\mathcal{F}_{1}
)

Let
G
be an isolate-free graph constructed as follows. Let
V(G)=\{x,y,w\}\cup P\cup Q
, where
P\cap Q\cap\{x,y,w\}=\emptyset
and
|P\cup Q|\geq 1
. Further, if
Q\neq\emptyset
, then
|Q|\geq 2
. Let the edge set
E(G)
be defined as follows. Let
N_{G}(x)=\{y\}
and let
N_{G}(w)=P\cup Q
. Join each vertex
p\in P
to every vertex in
(P\cup Q)\setminus\{p\}
. If
Q\neq\emptyset
, then add all edges from the vertex
y
to every vertex in
Q
, and so
Q\cup\{x\}\subseteq N_{G}(y)
. Further if
Q\neq\emptyset
, then add edges between vertices in
Q
, including the possibility of adding no edge (in which case
Q
is an independent set), in such a way that
G[Q]
does not contain a full vertex. Thus, every vertex
q\in Q
is not adjacent in
G
to at least one vertex in
Q\setminus\{q\}
. Finally, add any number of edges between the vertex
y
and vertices in
P
, including the possibility of adding no edge between
y
and vertices in
P
.

We note that if
G\in\mathcal{F}_{1}
is a disconnected graph of order
n
, then
G\cong K_{2}\cup K_{n-2}
. We are now in a position to characterize graphs
G
of order
n
with
\delta(G)=1
and with no full vertex that satisfy
\mathcal{C}(G)=n
.

Theorem 5

If
G
is a graph of order
n
with
\delta(G)=1
and with no full vertex, then
\mathcal{C}(G)=n
if and only if
G\in{\cal F}_{1}
.

Proof.

Suppose firstly that
G\in{\cal F}_{1}
. Adopting our notation in Definition
1
, we have that if
p\in P\cup\{w\}
, then
\{x,p\}
is a dominating set of
G
, and if
q\in Q\cup\{w\}
, then
\{y,q\}
is a dominating set of
G
, implying that every subset
\{v\}
, where
v\in N[w]
, forms a coalition with
\{x\}
or
\{y\}
. Therefore,
\mathcal{C}(G)=n
.

Conversely, let
G
be a graph of order
n
with
\delta(G)=1
and with no full vertex, and suppose that
\mathcal{C}(G)=n
. Let
x
be a leaf of
G
and let
y
be the unique neighbor of
x
in
G
. Let
\Psi
be a
\mathcal{C}
-partition of
G
, and so
|\Psi|=n
and
\Psi
is a

-partition of
G
. Thus, each member of
\Psi
is a singleton set (of cardinality

), that is, if
v\in V(G)
, then
\{v\}\in\Psi
. We show that
G\in{\cal F}_{1}
. We know that
\{x\}\in\Psi
and
\{y\}\in\Psi
. Suppose that
\{x\}
and
\{y\}
form a coalition in
G
. Since

is a leaf and

, all members of

must be adjacent to
y
. Thus, the degree of

is
n-1
, and so

is a full vertex of

, a contradiction. Hence,
\{x\}
and
\{y\}
do not form a coalition. Let
w
be a vertex not dominated by
y
, and so
w\neq y
and
w
is not adjacent to
y
. Let
N(w)=\{w_{1},\dots,w_{k}\}
.

We show that
V=\{x,y\}\cup N[w]
. Suppose, to the contrary, that there exists a vertex
v\in V\backslash(\{x,y\}\cup N[w])
. If
z
is an arbitrary vertex distinct from
x
and
y
, then the pair
\{v\}
and
\{z\}
do not form a coalition since the vertex
x
is not dominated by the set
\{v,z\}
. Moreover,
\{v\}
does not form a coalition with
\{x\}
or
\{y\}
since the vertex
w
is not dominated by the set
\{v,x,y\}
. Hence,
\{v\}
has no coalition partner, a contradiction. Therefore,
V=\{x,y\}\cup N[w]
. Recall that
w\neq y
and
w
is not adjacent to
y
. Since
G
has no full vertices, every set of
\Psi
must be a coalition partner of some other set of
\Psi
. Let

{\cal W}=\{S\in\Psi\colon N[w]\cap S\neq\emptyset\}.

Since
\mathcal{C}(G)=n
, we note that
{\cal W}=\left\{\{w\},\{w_{1}\},\ldots,\{w_{k}\}\right\}
. Necessarily, each member of
\cal W
forms a coalition with
\{x\}
or
\{y\}
, and there is no coalition between the members of
\cal W
. In particular,
\{w\}
is a coalition partner of each of
\{x\}
and
\{y\}
. Let
{\cal W}_{P}\subseteq{\cal W}\setminus\left\{\{w\}\right\}
be the collection of all sets of

that form a coalition with

, and let
P=\{p\in N(w)\colon\{p\}\in{\cal W}_{P}\}
. Moreover, let
{\cal W}_{Q}=\left({\cal W}\setminus\left\{\{w\}\right\}\right)\setminus{\cal W}_{P}
and let

. We note that
\{P,Q\}
is a weak partition of

, that is,

, where

and where in a weak partition we allow some of the sets in the partition to be empty (in our case, possibly

or

). No set in
{\cal W}_{Q}
forms a coalition with
\{x\}
, and therefore every member of
{\cal W}_{Q}
forms a coalition with

.

Let

. Thus,
\{p\}\in{\cal W}_{P}
forms a coalition with
\{x\}
, and so the set
\{x,p\}
is a dominating set of
G
, implying that the vertex

dominates the set

, and so

is adjacent to every vertex in

. Let

. Thus,
\{q\}\in{\cal W}_{Q}
and the set

is not a dominating set of

, implying that the vertex

is not adjacent to at least one vertex in

. As observed earlier, every vertex in

is adjacent to every vertex in

, implying that all non-neighbors of

in

belong to the set

. Further, since

is a dominating set of
G
, the vertex

is adjacent to every vertex in

. In particular, if

is a vertex in

that is not adjacent to

, then since
\{y,q^{\prime}\}
is a dominating set of
G
, the vertex

is adjacent to the vertex

. Therefore, the vertex

. According to the definition of family

, we infer that

.

Next we characterize graphs

of order

with
\delta(G)=1

.

Theorem 6

If
G
is a graph of order
n\geq 3
with
\delta(G)=1
and with exactly one full vertex, then
\mathcal{C}(G)=n
if and only if
G
is obtained from the graph
K_{1}\cup K_{n-1}
by adding an edge joining the isolated vertex to an arbitrary vertex of the complete graph

Proof.

For

, let
G
be obtained from

by adding an edge

x
is the vertex in the copy of
K_{1}
and
y
is a vertex in the complete graph
K_{n-1}
. In the resulting graph

, the vertex

has degree

and the vertex

has degree

. Thus,

is a full vertex. If

, then
\{w,x\}
is a dominating set of
G
, and so
\{w\}
forms a coalition with the set
\{x\}
, implying that
\mathcal{C}(G)=n
. This proves the sufficiency.

To prove the necessity, let
G
be a graph of order
n\geq 3
with
\delta(G)=1
and with exactly one full vertex and suppose that
\mathcal{C}(G)=n
. Let
\Psi
be a
\mathcal{C}
-partition of
G
. Let

and

be vertices of

where
\deg_{G}(u)=1
and
\deg_{G}(v)=n-1
, and let
w
be an arbitrary vertex in

. Since

is a full vertex, the set
\{v\}
does not form a coalition with any vertex of
G
. Hence since the set
\{w\}
is not a dominating set of

, the only coalition partner of the set
\{w\}
in
\Psi
is the set
\{u\}
. This implies that the vertex
w
is adjacent to every vertex in

except for the vertex

. This is true for all vertices

, implying that the graph

. Rebuilding the graph

from

to the

, the desired result follows.
\Box

We are now in a position to characterize all trees

\mathcal{C}(T)=n

Theorem 7

If
T
is a tree of order

, then
\mathcal{C}(T)=n

is a path of order at most

.

Proof.

By Theorem

(a), if

where

, then

. Conversely, let

be a tree of order

satisfying
\mathcal{C}(T)=n
. The result is immediate if

or

, and so we may assume that

. If there is a full vertex in

, as desired. Hence we may assume that

does not have a full vertex, implying that

. In this case by Theorem

, the tree

belongs to the family
{\cal F}_{1}
. Adopting our earlier notation in Definition

, if

, and the graph

contains a

\Box

3

In this section, we characterize all trees

Theorem 8

Claim 1

4
Coalition graphs

In this section, we solve Problem
2
and Problem
3
. In particular, we determine the number of the coalition graphs that can be defined by all coalition partitions of the path
P_{k}
for a given positive integer

(see Theorem

A coalition graph corresponding to a coalition partition of a path is called a CP-

in
[

]
. We say a path

defines a
{\rm CP}
-graph

{\rm CG}(P_{k},\Psi)
is isomorphic to

[

]
, Haynes et al. proved the following theorem.

Theorem 9

(
[
2
]
)

A graph
G
is a
{\rm CP}
-graph if and only if
G\in{\cal F}
.

According to Theorem
9
, all 18 graphs of
{\cal F}
are
{\rm CP}
-graph. For
k\geq 2
, let the ordered list
s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{k}
be the vertices of the path
P_{k}
whose edges are
\{s_{i},s_{i+1}\}
where

. Now, we prove the following proposition.

Proposition 3

The following properties hold.

(a)

The path
P_{1}
defines the
{\rm CP}
-graph
K_{1}
.

(b)

The path
P_{2}
defines the
{\rm CP}
-graph
\overline{K_{2}}
.

(c)

The path
P_{3}
defines the
{\rm CP}
-graph
K_{1}\cup K_{2}
.

(d)

For any integer
k\geq 4
, the path
P_{k}
does not define the
{\rm CP}
-graphs
\overline{K_{2}}
and
K_{1}\cup K_{2}
.

Proof.
It is immediate that the only path that defines the
{\rm CP}
-graph
K_{1}
is
P_{1}
. This establishes (a). For the path
P_{2}
, the set
\left\{\{s_{1}\},\{s_{2}\}\right\}
is a coalition partition and the corresponding coalition graph is

. This establishes (b). For the path
P_{3}
, the set
\left\{\{s_{1}\},\{s_{2}\},\{s_{3}\}\right\}

and
\{s_{3}\}
is a dominating set but they form a coalition. Moreover, the set
\{s_{2}\}
is a singleton dominating set. Hence, the coalition graph corresponding to

is

. This establishes (c). Since the graphs

and

have at least one isolated vertex, their corresponding coalition partition must contain at least one singleton dominating set. We know that for any

is at least two. Therefore, every coalition partition of

with

does not contain any singleton dominating set. This proves part (d).
\Box

By Proposition
1
and Theorem

Corollary 4

, the path

-graphs

,
2K_{2}
,
P_{4},C_{4},F_{1},K_{4}-e,P_{2}\cup P_{3},F_{2},B_{1},P_{5},S(2,1),S(2,2)

Conclusion

In this paper, we characterized all graphs
G
of order
n
with
\delta(G)\leq 1
and
\mathcal{C}(G)=n
. Furthermore, we characterized all trees
T
of order
n
with
\mathcal{C}(T)=n
and all trees
T
of order
n
with
\mathcal{C}(T)=n-1
. On the other hand, we theoretically and empirically determined the number of coalition graphs that can be defined by all coalition partitions of a given path
P_{k}
. Furthermore, we showed that there is no universal coalition path. It remains an open problem to characterize all trees
T
of order
n
with
\mathcal{C}(T)=n-k
for all
k
where
2\leq k\leq n-2
. It would also be interesting to determine whether there is a linear-time algorithm to compute the coalition number of a given tree.

Constraining vector dark matter and dark photon with degenerate mass in a hidden local S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) model
Takaaki Nomura, Xinran Xu

Abstract
We discuss degenerate vector dark matter and dark photon that are induced from hidden S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) H SU(2)_{H} gauge sector where it is spontaneously broken by vacuum expectation value of S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) H SU(2)_{H} doublet. Kinetic mixing between S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) H SU(2)_{H} and U ​ ( 1 ) Y U(1)_{Y} gauge fields can be generated by introducing dimension six operator realizing dark photon interactions. In estimating relic density we focus on the process in which dark matter annihilate into dark photons, and search for the region of dark matter mass and gauge coupling realizing observed relic density. We then discuss constraints from dark photon physics, thermalization of dark sector and direct detection of dark matter. It is then found that constraints from direct detection experiments give us the strongest upper limits on the dark photon interactions.

Introduction

Understanding of dark matter (DM) nature is one of the biggest challenge in physics.
The existence of DM definitely requires physics beyond the standard model (SM) where we call new physics sector containing DM as dark sector.
In fact various candidates of DM are considered from new physics models.
One promising candidate of DM is a massive stable particle that weakly interacts with SM sector as its relic density can be explained via thermal production.
Such a DM has been searched for by direct detection, indirect detection and collider experiments.
However we do not have any clear evidence of DM yet and nature of dark sector is an open question.

One attractive scenario is to consider dark sector as hidden gauge sector as the SM is also described by gauge symmetry.

II
A model

We consider a model with hidden sector described by hidden
SU(2)_{H}
gauge symmetry where we introduce
SU(2)_{H}
doublet scalar field
\Phi
to break the symmetry spontaneously.
The new scalar field
\Phi
will develop a vacuum expectation value(VEV)
v_{\Phi}
and without the loss of generality it can be written by

\Phi=\begin{pmatrix}G^{+}_{H}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(v_{\Phi}+\phi+iG_{H})\end{pmatrix},

(1)

where
G^{\pm}_{H}
and
G_{H}
correspond to massless Nambu-Goldstone(NG) boson that are absorbed by
SU(2)_{H}
gauge bosons.
In scalar sector we also have the SM Higgs field
H
written by

H=\begin{pmatrix}w^{+}\\
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(v+\tilde{h}+iG_{Z})\end{pmatrix},

(2)

where
v
is the VEV of
H
and
\{w^{\pm},G_{Z}\}
correspond to NG boson absorbed by the SM massive gauge bosons
\{W^{\pm},Z\}
.
Fermion sector is considered to be the same as the SM and we do not focus on fermions in this work.

The renormalizable Lagrangian in the model is

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm SM}+(D_{\mu}\Phi)^{\dagger}(D^{\mu}\Phi)-\frac{1}{4}X^{a}_{\mu\nu}X^{a\mu\nu}-\mathcal{V}

(3)

where
X^{a}_{\mu\nu}(a=1,2,3)
is the gauge field strength for
SU(2)_{H}
,
\mathcal{L}_{SM}
is the Lagrangian of the SM without scalar potential, and
\mathcal{V}
is the scalar potential including the SM Higgs field
H
.
Explicit form of the scalar potential is given by

\mathcal{V}=\mu_{H}^{2}(H^{\dagger}H)+\mu_{\Phi}^{2}(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)+\lambda_{H}(H^{\dagger}H)^{2}+\lambda_{\Phi}(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi)^{2}+\lambda_{H\Phi}(H^{\dagger}H)(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi).

(4)

The covariant derivative of
\Phi
is written by

D_{\mu}\Phi=\left(\partial_{\mu}+ig_{H}\frac{\sigma^{a}}{2}X^{a}_{\mu}\right)\Phi

(5)

where
g_{H}
is gauge coupling associated with
SU(2)_{H}
and
\sigma^{a}
is the Pauli matrix acting on representation space of
SU(2)_{H}
.
In addition to the renormalizable Lagrangian we introduce a gauge invariant effective interaction of

\mathcal{L}_{\rm eff}=\frac{1}{\Lambda^{2}}(\Phi^{\dagger}\sigma^{a}\Phi)X^{a}_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}

(6)

where
B_{\mu\nu}
is the gauge field strength of
U(1)_{Y}
and
\Lambda
represents a high energy scale
3
3

3

Dimension 8 operator of
(\Phi^{\dagger}\sigma^{a}\Phi)X^{a}_{\mu\nu}(H^{\dagger}\sigma^{b}H)W^{b}_{\mu\nu}
is also possible that induces mixing between
SU(2)_{H}
and
SU(2)_{L}

[
30
]
. Here we do not consider the operator considering it is more suppressed by
1/\Lambda^{4}
factor.

After
\Phi
developing its VEV the effective Lagrangian provides kinetic mixing term such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm eff}\supset-\frac{v_{\Phi}^{2}}{4\Lambda^{2}}X^{3}_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\equiv-\frac{\epsilon}{2}X^{3}_{\mu\nu}B^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon}{2}(\partial_{\mu}X^{3}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}X^{3}_{\mu}+g_{H}(X^{1}_{\mu}X^{2}_{\nu}-X^{1}_{\nu}X^{2}_{\mu}))B^{\mu\nu},

(7)

indicating mixing between third component of
SU(2)_{H}
gauge field
X^{3}_{\mu}
and
U(1)_{Y}
gauge field
B_{\mu}
.
Kinetic terms of
X^{3}_{\mu}
and
B_{\mu}
can be diagonalized by transformation

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}X^{3}_{\mu}\\
B_{\mu}\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}r&0\\
-\epsilon r&1\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\tilde{Z}^{\prime}_{\mu}\\
\tilde{B}_{\mu}\\
\end{array}\right),

(8)

where
r=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\epsilon^{2}}}
.
We then obtain Lagrangian for gauge sector as

\mathcal{L}_{G}=-\frac{1}{4}X^{i}_{\mu\nu}X^{i\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{Z}^{\prime\mu\nu}\tilde{Z}^{\prime}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}W^{\alpha\mu\nu}W^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{4}\tilde{B}^{\mu\nu}\tilde{B}_{\mu\nu},

(9)

where
i=1,2
and
W^{\alpha}_{\mu\nu}
is the gauge field strength of
SU(2)_{L}
gauge field
W^{\alpha}_{\mu}(\alpha=1-3)
.

II.1
Scalar sector

Here we consider scalar sector in the model and derive mass eigenvalues with corresponding eigenstates.
The VEVs
v
and
v_{\Phi}
can be obtained from the scalar potential Eq. (
4
) requiring stationary conditions
\partial V/\partial v=\partial V/\partial v_{\Phi}=0

that provide

\displaystyle\mu_{H}^{2}+\lambda_{H}v^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{H\Phi}v_{\Phi}^{2}=0,

(10)

\displaystyle\mu_{\Phi}^{2}+\lambda_{\Phi}v_{\Phi}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\lambda_{H\Phi}v^{2}=0.

(11)

Here we require
\mu_{H}^{2},\mu_{\Phi}^{2}<0
to make square of VEVs positive definite.

After spontaneous symmetry breaking, we obtain mass matrix for CP-even neutral scalar as

\mathcal{L}\supset\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{h}\\
\phi\end{pmatrix}^{T}\begin{pmatrix}2\lambda_{H}v^{2}&\lambda_{H\Phi}vv_{\varphi}\\
\lambda_{H\Phi}vv_{\Phi}&2\lambda_{\Phi}v_{\Phi}^{2}\end{pmatrix}.\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{h}\\
\phi\end{pmatrix}

(12)

We obtain mass eigenvalues by diagonalizing the mass matrix such that

\displaystyle m_{h,H}^{2}=\lambda_{H}v^{2}+\lambda_{\Phi}v_{\Phi}^{2}\pm\sqrt{(\lambda_{H}v^{2}-\lambda_{\Phi}v_{\varphi}^{2})^{2}+\lambda_{H\Phi}^{2}v^{2}v_{\Phi}^{2}},

(13)

where we identify
m_{h}=125
GeV is the mass of SM Higgs boson.
The mass eigenstates and relevant mixing angle are obtained as

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{h}\\
\phi\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\beta&-\sin\beta\\
\sin\beta&\cos\beta\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}h\\
H\end{pmatrix},

\displaystyle\tan 2\beta=\frac{vv_{\Phi}\lambda_{H\Phi}}{v^{2}\lambda_{H}-v^{2}_{\Phi}\lambda_{\Phi}},

(14)

where
h
is identified as the SM Higgs boson.
In the analysis of this paper we assume scalar mixing is negligibly small choosing tiny
\lambda_{H\Phi}
and focus on the effect of gauge interactions.

II.2
Gauge boson masses and interactions

After spontaneous breaking of electroweak and hidden gauge symmetries, we obtain mass terms of electrically neutral gauge fields including the SM ones as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{M}=m_{X}^{2}X^{+\mu}X^{-}_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}_{\tilde{Z}^{\prime}}\tilde{Z}^{\prime\mu}\tilde{Z}^{\prime}_{\mu}+\frac{1}{2}m^{2}_{Z_{\rm SM}}\tilde{Z}^{\mu}\tilde{Z}_{\mu}+\Delta M^{2}\tilde{Z}^{\mu}\tilde{Z}^{\prime}_{\mu},

(15)

where
X_{\mu}^{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(X^{1}_{\mu}\mp iX^{2}_{\mu})
and
\tilde{Z}_{\mu}=\cos\theta_{W}W^{3}_{\mu}-\sin\theta_{W}\tilde{B}_{\mu}
with
\theta_{W}
being the Weinberg angle.
Squared mass parameters are given by

\displaystyle m^{2}_{Z_{\rm SM}}=\frac{g_{Z}^{2}v^{2}}{4},\quad m_{X}^{2}=\frac{g_{H}^{2}v_{\Phi}^{2}}{4},\quad m_{\tilde{Z}^{\prime}}^{2}=r^{2}(m_{X}^{2}+\epsilon^{2}m^{2}_{Z_{\rm SM}}\sin^{2}\theta_{W}),

\displaystyle\Delta M^{2}=\epsilon r\sin\theta_{W}m^{2}_{Z_{\rm SM}}.

(16)

Thus
\tilde{Z}_{\mu}
and
\tilde{Z}^{\prime}_{\mu}
fields mix as a consequence of kinetic mixing effect while
X^{\pm}_{\mu}
is mass eigenstate with mass
m_{X}
.
We obtain mass eigenvalues by diagonalizing mass matrix for
\tilde{Z}
and
\tilde{Z}^{\prime}
as

m^{2}_{Z,Z^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}(m^{2}_{Z_{\rm SM}}+m_{\tilde{Z}^{\prime}}^{2})\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{(m^{2}_{Z_{\rm SM}}-m_{\tilde{Z}^{\prime}}^{2})^{2}+4\Delta M^{4}}.

(17)

The mass eigenstates are given by

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}Z\\
Z^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\chi&\sin\chi\\
-\sin\chi&\cos\chi\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{Z}\\
\tilde{Z}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},

(18)

\displaystyle\tan 2\chi=\frac{2\epsilon\sin\theta_{W}m^{2}_{Z_{\rm SM}}}{m^{2}_{Z_{\rm SM}}-m^{2}_{\tilde{Z}}}.

(19)

In our analysis we consider
\chi
to be tiny by choosing
\epsilon
sufficiently small as the
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing is strongly constrained by the electroweak precision tests.
We thus take
\tan\chi\simeq\sin\chi\simeq\chi
henceforth.
In addition, gauge boson masses can be approximated as
m_{Z}\simeq m_{Z_{\rm SM}}
and
m_{Z^{\prime}}\simeq m_{X}
for small
\epsilon
.

The gauge interactions among
X^{\pm}
and
Z^{\prime}
can be obtained from Lagrangian Eq. (
9
) as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{X^{\pm}Z^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle\ ig_{H}\Bigl[(\partial_{\mu}X^{+}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}X^{+}_{\mu})X^{-\mu}Z^{\prime\nu}-(\partial_{\mu}X^{-}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}X^{-}_{\mu})X^{+\mu}Z^{\prime\nu}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}Z^{\prime}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}Z^{\prime}_{\mu})(X^{+\mu}X^{-\nu}-X^{-\mu}X^{+\nu})\Bigr]

\displaystyle-g_{H}^{2}[X^{+}_{\mu}X^{-\mu}Z^{\prime}_{\nu}Z^{\prime\nu}-X^{+}_{\mu}X^{-}_{\nu}Z^{\prime\mu}Z^{\prime\nu}],

(20)

where we adopted approximation of
r\simeq 1
and
\cos\chi\simeq 1
.
In addition
Z^{\prime}
can interact with the SM particles through
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing.
The interactions between
Z^{\prime}
and SM fermions
f
can be written by

\mathcal{L}_{Z^{\prime}ff}=\frac{g}{\cos\theta_{W}}Z^{\prime}_{\mu}\bar{f}\gamma^{\mu}[-\sin\chi(T_{3}-Q_{f}\sin^{2}\theta_{W})-\cos\chi\epsilon Y_{f}\sin\theta_{W}]f,

(21)

where
T_{3}
is diagonal generator of
SU(2)_{L}
,
Q_{f}
is the electric charge and
Y
is hypercharge.
In the model
Z^{\prime}
decays into SM particles while
X^{\pm}
is stable and can be DM candidate.
In addition, we have DM-photon and DM-Z interactions from effective term Eq. (
6
) such that

\mathcal{L}_{\rm eff}\supset-ig_{H}\epsilon\cos\theta_{W}(\partial^{\mu}A^{\nu}-\partial^{\nu}A^{\mu})X^{+}_{\mu}X^{-}_{\nu}-ig_{H}\epsilon\sin\theta_{W}(\partial^{\mu}Z^{\nu}-\partial^{\nu}Z^{\mu})X^{+}_{\mu}X^{-}_{\nu},

(22)

where
A_{\mu}
is photon field and we ignore
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing effect since it is negligibly small.
We notice that coupling
g_{H}
appears instead of electromagnetic coupling
e
for DM-photon interaction.

Dark matter phenomenology

III.1
Relic density

Relic density of DM is estimated by solving the Boltzmann equation for number density
n_{X}
of
X^{\pm}
,

\dot{n}_{X}+3Hn_{X}=\langle\sigma v\rangle(n_{X_{\rm eq}}^{2}-n_{X}^{2}),

(23)

where
\langle\sigma\rangle
is thermal average of DM annihilation cross section,
H
is Hubble parameter, and
n_{X_{eq}}
is number density of
X^{\pm}
in thermal equilibrium.
Here we adopt
micrOMEGAs
code
[
35
]
to estimate relic density by implementing relevant interactions in the model.
In estimating relic density, the relevant parameters in the model are DM(Z’) mass
m_{X}
, new gauge coupling
g_{H}
and scalar mass
m_{\phi}
.
In this work we consider
\phi
is heaver than
X^{\pm}
and
Z^{\prime}
so that a DM annihilation mode including
\phi
in final states is forbidden kinematically.
Thus the DM annihilation process in our scenario is solely

X^{+}X^{-}\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}.

(24)

Here we fix
m_{\phi}=1.5m_{X}
for simplicity and search for parameter region on
\{m_{X},g_{H}\}
plane that can provide observed DM relic density.
Here we consider two mass range of DM (dark photon) as follows

\displaystyle{\rm(i)}\ m_{X}=[1.0,60]\ {\rm GeV}

(25)

\displaystyle{\rm(ii)}\ m_{X}=[150,3000]\ {\rm GeV}.

(26)

Here the range (i) is light WIMP like DM case where
Z^{\prime}
is also lighter than
Z
and more dark photon like.
The range (ii) corresponds to heavy WIMP DM case and
Z^{\prime}
is heavier than
Z
.
We do not consider the region
m_{X}\sim m_{Z}
since
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing tends to be large.

In Fig.
1
, we show the region providing
\Omega h^{2}<0.12
,
\Omega h^{2}\simeq 0.12
and
\Omega h^{2}>0.12
on
\{m_{X},g_{H}\}
plane.
The red dashed curve indicate the parameters explaining observed relic density.
The shaded region is excluded since relic density is over abundant while the white region provides smaller relic density.
We find that observed relic density can be obtained by gauge coupling
g_{H}\in[0.01,1]
for DM mass of 1 GeV to 3000 GeV.

III.2
Constraints on kinetic mixing

Here we summarize constraints on kinetic mixing parameter
\epsilon
from dark photon searches, thermalization condition of DM and mili-charged DM interaction.

Constraints from dark photon search
:

In the mass range (i) of our interest
\epsilon
is constrained by BaBar
[
36
,
37
]
and LHCb
[
38
]
experiments that search for dark photon.
The BarBar experiment search for the process
e^{+}e^{-}\to\gamma Z^{\prime}
where
Z^{\prime}
is considered to decay into visible modes
\{e^{+}e^{-},\mu^{+}\mu^{-},\text{light mesons}\}
or
invisible mode such as neutrinos and DM states.
The LHCb experiment search for the dark photon which is produced in proton-proton collision and decays into
\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
pair.
We apply constraint from these experiments for the allowed range of
\epsilon
where we use the
DARKCAST
code to extract the limit
[
39
]
.

DM thermalization condition
:

We require particles in dark sector, DM and dark photon, are in thermal equilibrium before freeze-out of DM to guarantee our relic density calculation.
In addition, dark photon lifetime should be smaller than
\mathcal{O}(1)
s to avoid inconsistency with Big Bang Nucleosynthesis.
In the model, dark sector is thermalized in the early Universe via dark photon decays into electrons.
Then we impose the condition where the decay rate is larger than the Hubble rate at freeze-out.
This condition can be written by
[
40
,
41
]

|\epsilon|\gtrsim 2.0\times 10^{-8}\sqrt{\frac{10\ {\rm GeV}}{m_{Z^{\prime}}}}\left(\frac{m_{X}}{10\ {\rm GeV}}\right).

(27)

We adopt the condition as a lower bound of
\epsilon
in the analysis below.

Constraint from mili-charged DM interaction
:

Unlike to the Abelian hidden
U(1)
case we have DM-photon interaction Eq. (
22
) which arise from the effective interaction Eq. (
6
).
We thus comment on constraint from DM-Baryon scattering for milli-charged DM
[
42
]
through the interaction.
In this case the cross section is
\sigma_{X}=\sigma_{X0}v^{2}
where
v
is DM-Baryon relative velocity.
It is suppressed by
v^{2}
factor compared to milli-charged fermion DM case
(\sigma_{F}=\sigma_{F0}v^{4})
that is due to
momentum factor appearing from derivative in the interaction Eq. (
6
).
Therefore the constraint is not severe and DM direct detection experiment provide stronger constraint that is estimated below.

III.3
Direct detection

In the model DM
X^{\pm}
interacts with nucleon via
Z^{\prime}
exchanging process through interactions in Eqs. (
20
) and (
21
).
In addition
X^{\pm}
have interaction with the SM
Z
boson via
Z
-
Z^{\prime}
mixing where relevant interaction for DM-nucleon scattering is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{ZX^{+}X^{-}}=

\displaystyle\ ig_{H}\sin\chi\Bigl[(\partial_{\mu}X^{+}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}X^{+}_{\mu})X^{-\mu}Z^{\nu}-(\partial_{\mu}X^{-}_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}X^{-}_{\mu})X^{+\mu}Z^{\nu}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\epsilon}{\sin\chi}\right)(\partial_{\mu}Z_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}Z_{\mu})(X^{+\mu}X^{-\nu}-X^{-\mu}X^{+\nu})\Bigr],

(28)

where we included Eq. (
22

Thus we need to take into account both
Z^{\prime}
and
Z
boson exchanging diagrams to obtain DM-nucleon scattering cross section.
Here vector type interaction among
Z(Z^{\prime})
and nucleon can be obtained as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{N}=\sum_{N=p,n}\frac{g}{\cos\theta_{W}}\bar{N}(C_{ZNN}^{V}Z_{\mu}+C_{Z^{\prime}NN}^{V}Z^{\prime}_{\mu})\gamma^{\mu}N,

(29)

\displaystyle C_{Zpp}^{V}=\frac{1}{4}-\sin^{2}\theta_{W},\quad C_{Znn}^{V}=-\frac{1}{4},

(30)

\displaystyle C_{Z^{\prime}pp}^{V}=-\sin\chi\left(\frac{1}{4}-\sin^{2}\theta_{W}\right)-\frac{1}{3}\epsilon\sin\theta_{W},\quad C_{Z^{\prime}nn}^{V}=\frac{1}{4}\sin\chi-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon\sin\theta_{W},

where

and

respectively indicate proton and neutron, and we approximate
\cos\chi\simeq 1

We then calculate diagrams and obtain DM-nucleon scattering cross section in non-relativistic limit such that

\sigma_{NX}=\frac{2g_{H}^{2}g^{2}}{\pi\cos^{2}\theta_{W}}\left(\frac{m_{X}m_{N}}{m_{X}+m_{N}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{\sin\chi C^{V}_{ZNN}}{m_{Z}^{2}}+\frac{\cos\chi C^{V}_{Z^{\prime}NN}}{m_{Z^{\prime}}^{2}}\right)^{2},

(32)

where
m_{N}
denote nucleon mass
(N=p,n)
.
Here we consider average of DM-nucleon scattering cross section
(\sigma_{nX}+\sigma_{pX})/2
and compare it with experimental constraints
to estimate upper limit of
\epsilon
.
In estimating the cross section we adopt
g_{H}
value explaining relic density
\Omega h^{2}\simeq 0.12
in Fig.
1
for each DM (dark photon) mass.

Summary

We have discussed DM and dark photon from a hidden
SU(2)_{H}
model with minimal new field content which is one
SU(2)_{H}
doublet scalar fields,
introducing dimension 6 effective operator among
SU(2)_{H}
and
U(1)_{Y}
gauge fields and the doublet.
The effective operator induces kinetic mixing between these gauge fields after spontaneous gauge symmetry breaking and one component of
SU(2)_{H}
gauge field becomes
dark photon
Z^{\prime}
while the other two components become vector DM
X^{\pm}
.
Note that
X^{\pm}
and
Z^{\prime}
have almost degenerate mass in the model.

Then we have considered the scenario in which relic density of DM is explained by
X^{+}X^{-}\to Z^{\prime}Z^{\prime}
process since it is determined by gauge interaction only and more constrained due to fixed mass relation between
X^{\pm}
and
Z^{\prime}
.
Numerically analyzing the Boltzmann equation, we searched for parameter region that satisfy observed relic density.
It has been found that
\mathcal{O}(0.01)
to
\mathcal{O}(1)
gauge coupling of
SU(2)_{H}
can realize the relic density in the DM (dark photon) mass range of
\sim
1 GeV to
\sim 3
TeV.
We have also discussed relevant constraints from DM and dark photon physics to constrain the kinetic mixing parameter.
In addition, cross section of DM-nucleon scattering via dark photon exchange is estimated to explore constraints from DM direct detection searches.
As a result, we have found the direct detection searches can provide the strongest constraint on kinetic mixing parameter and
further parameter space will be tested in future unless the cross section is above neutrino floor.

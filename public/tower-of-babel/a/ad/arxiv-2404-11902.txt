On the spectrum of a differential operator on a Hilbert-Pólya space
Xian-Jin Li

Abstract
In this paper we study the spectrum of a fundamental differential operator on a Hilbert-Pólya space. A number is an eigenvalue of this differential operator if and only if it is a nontrivial zero of the Riemann zeta function. An explicit formula is given for the eigenfunction associated with each nontrivial zero of the zeta function. Every eigenfunction is characterized via the Poisson summation formula by a sequence of mysterious functions whose explicit formulas are given.

Introduction

Let
S(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times})
be the set of all infinitely
differentiable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}

with
\sup_{x\in(0,\infty)}|(\log x)^{a}f^{(b)}(x)|<\infty
for
a,b=0,1,2,\cdots
.
The fundamental differential operator on
S(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times})
is

Df(x)=-xf^{\prime}(x).

We denote

Zf(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f(nx)
,

\mathcal{H}_{-}=\{f\mid\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}\text{ such that }x^{\alpha}f(x)\in S(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times})\text{ for every real number $\alpha$}\},

and
\mathcal{H}_{\cap}
is the set of all even functions

f\in S(\mathbb{R})
such that
f(0)=0
and
\mathfrak{F}f(0)=0

where the Fourier transform of
f
is

\mathfrak{F}f(y)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(x)e^{2\pi ixy}dx.

The the quotient space

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{-}/Z\mathcal{H}_{\cap}

is called a Hilbert-Pólya space.
Let
D_{-}
be the operator induced by
D
on
\mathcal{H}
.

If
\rho
is a nontrivial zero of
\zeta(s)
, the author
proved in
[
3
]
that

F_{\rho}(x)=\int_{1}^{\infty}Z\eta(tx)t^{\rho-1}dt

is an eigenfunction of
D_{-}

in
\mathcal{H}
associated with the eigenvalue

\rho
, where
\eta(x)=8\pi x^{2}\left(\pi x^{2}-\frac{3}{2}\right)e^{-\pi x^{2}}
.
That is,

DF_{\rho}(x)=\rho F_{\rho}(x)+Z\eta(x),\quad\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\cap}.

(1)

It is also proved in
[
4
]
that a complex number
\rho
is an eigenvalue of
D_{-}
on
\mathcal{H}
if and only if
\rho
is
a nontrivial zero of the zeta function.

In his research on global trace formula
[
2
]
, the author noticed a mysterious
function associated with each function in
\mathcal{H}
that
prevents the Poisson summation formula to hold for that function. In
[
3
]

he was able to identify this mysterious function and obtained the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
) Let

\delta_{l,F}(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}Z^{-1}F(xt)\,\frac{\sin(2l+1)\pi t}{\pi t}dt
. Then

\lim_{l\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{x}\,\sum_{m=1}^{l}\mathfrak{F}Z^{-1}F\left(\frac{m}{x}\right)-\delta_{l,F}(x)\right)=F(x)

for every
F\in\mathcal{H}_{-}
.

Because of the appearance of the unbounded operator
Z^{-1}

inside
\delta_{l,F}(x)
, it is difficult to know what the function

\delta_{l,F}(x)
is exactly. In this paper, author was able to get an
exact simple formula for
\delta_{l,F_{\rho}}(x)
for every
nontrivial zero
\rho
of the Riemann zeta function.
More precisely, the author obtained the following formula.

Theorem 1.2

For every nontrivial zero
\rho
of the
Riemann zeta function, we have

\displaystyle\delta_{l,F_{\rho}}(x)

\displaystyle={\widehat{\eta}(1-\rho)(l+{1\over 2})^{\rho}\over\rho x^{\rho}}+4\pi({l+{1\over 2}\over x})^{\rho}\int_{l+{1\over 2}\over x}^{\infty}e^{-\pi u^{2}}u^{2-\rho}du

\displaystyle=-4\pi({l+{1\over 2}\over x})^{3}\int_{0}^{1}u^{2-\rho}e^{-\pi({l+{1\over 2}\over x}u)^{2}}du.

When
\Re\rho<1/2
, the following improvement of Theorem
1.1

is also obtained in Section 3.

Theorem 1.3

If
\Re\rho<1/2
, then

\|{1\over x}\sum_{m=1}^{l}\mathfrak{F}Z^{-1}F_{\rho}({m\over x})-F_{\rho}(x)-\delta_{l,F_{\rho}}(x)\|_{L^{2}(0,\infty)}\ll{1\over 2l+1}

for each positive integer
l
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2

Lemma 2.1

(
[
3
, Lemma 3.2]
)
For every element
F\in\mathcal{H}_{-}
we have

\delta_{l,F}(x)=\int_{0}^{l+{1\over 2}\over x}\mathfrak{F}Z^{-1}F(t)dt.

Lemma 2.2

We can write

\delta_{l,F_{\rho}}(x)={\widehat{\eta}(1-\rho)(l+{1\over 2})^{\rho}\over\rho x^{\rho}}-\int_{0}^{l+{1\over 2}\over x}\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\eta(tu)u^{-\rho}du\right)dt.

Proof
. Since
\eta\in\mathcal{H}_{\cap}
, for any fixed
x>0
and
for all
t\geq 1
we have
|\eta(tx)|\leq c_{x}(tx)^{-2}

for a constant
c_{x}
depending on
x
. Thus,

\int_{1}^{\infty}\left|\sum_{n\geq X+1}\eta(ntx)\right|t^{\Re\rho-1}dt\leq\frac{c_{x}}{x^{2}X(2-\Re\rho)}\to 0

as
X\to\infty
. This implies that we can change the order of summation
and integration to write

F_{\rho}(x)=Z\int_{1}^{\infty}\eta(tx)t^{\rho-1}dt.

By Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle\delta_{l,F_{\rho}}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}Z^{-1}F_{\rho}(t){\sin{l+{1\over 2}\over x}2\pi t\over\pi t}dt

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\int_{0}^{l+{1\over 2}\over x}\cos 2\pi vtdv\int_{1}^{\infty}\eta(ut)u^{\rho-1}du

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{l+{1\over 2}\over x}dv\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{1}^{\infty}\eta(ut)u^{\rho-1}du\right)\cos 2\pi vtdt

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{l+{1\over 2}\over x}dv\int_{1}^{\infty}u^{\rho-1}du\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(ut)\cos 2\pi vtdt

where changes of order of integration are permissible because the
triple integral is absolute integrable.
Since
\mathfrak{F}\eta=\eta
, we have

2\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta(ut)\cos 2\pi vtdt=\mathfrak{F}_{t}\{\eta(ut)\}(v)={1\over u}\eta({v\over u})

Thus

\displaystyle\delta_{l,F_{\rho}}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{l+{1\over 2}\over x}dv\int_{1}^{\infty}\eta({v\over u})u^{\rho-2}du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{l+{1\over 2}\over x}v^{\rho-1}dv\int_{0}^{\infty}\eta({1\over u})u^{\rho-2}du-\int_{0}^{l+{1\over 2}\over x}dv\int_{0}^{1}\eta({v\over u})u^{\rho-2}du

\displaystyle={\widehat{\eta}(1-\rho)(l+{1\over 2})^{\rho}\over\rho x^{\rho}}-\int_{0}^{l+{1\over 2}\over x}dt\int_{1}^{\infty}\eta(tu)u^{-\rho}du.

This completes the proof of the lemma.
\hfill\Box

Proof of Theorem
1.2
. Since

{d\over dt}\left(-4\pi t^{3}e^{-\pi t^{2}}\right)=-4\pi(3t^{2}-2\pi t^{4})e^{-\pi t^{2}}=8\pi t^{2}(\pi t^{2}-{3\over 2})e^{-\pi t^{2}}=\eta(t),

Lemma 3.2

Let
f(t)=Z^{-1}F_{\rho}(t)

\Re\rho<1/2

Then

\int_{0}^{\infty}|\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{-{1\over 2}}^{1\over 2}\left(f(tx+mx)-f(mx)\right){\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\sin\pi t}dt|^{2}dx\ll{1\over(2l+1)^{4}}

f(t)={1\over 2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}{\widehat{F}_{\rho}(s)\over\zeta(s)}t^{-s}ds

for
c\geq 1
. We write

(tx+mx)^{-s}-(mx)^{-s}=-s\int_{mx}^{tx+mx}{du\over u^{s+1}}={-s\over(mx)^{s}}\int_{1}^{1+{t\over m}}{du\over u^{s+1}}

Since

\displaystyle|\left(f(tx+mx)-f(mx)\right){\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\sin\pi t}|

\displaystyle\leq|\left({1\over 2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}{-s\widehat{F}_{\rho}(s)\over\zeta(s)}[\int_{mx}^{tx+mx}u^{-s-1}du]ds\right){\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\sin\pi t}|

\displaystyle\leq{(2l+1)\pi t\over 2\pi\cdot 2t}{t\over m^{1+c}x^{c}}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}|{-s\widehat{F}_{\rho}(s)\over\zeta(s)}||ds|\ll{1\over m^{1+c}}

for
|t|\leq 1/2

depending only on
x
and
l
not on
t
, the series

\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\left(f(tx+mx)-f(mx)\right){\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\sin\pi t}

is uniformly convergent for
|t|\leq 1/2
.
Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{-{1\over 2}}^{1\over 2}\left(f(tx+mx)-f(mx)\right){\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\sin\pi t}dt

\displaystyle={1\over 2\pi i}\int_{c-i\infty}^{c+i\infty}{-s\widehat{F}_{\rho}(s)\over x^{s}\zeta(s)}\{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}{1\over m^{s}}\int_{-{1\over 2}}^{1\over 2}{\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\sin\pi t}dt\int_{1}^{1+{t\over m}}{du\over u^{s+1}}\}ds

where the change of order of integration is permissible as the triple
integral is absolute integrable.

By (
1
),
\widehat{F}_{\rho}(s)=\xi(s)/(s-\rho)

for all complex

. Hence,

{\widehat{F}_{\rho}(s)\over\zeta(s)}={s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\over s-\rho}

An extension of Stirling’s

|t|\to\infty

Thus,

{\widehat{F}_{\rho}(s)\over\zeta(s)}\ll|s|^{-k}

for any positive integer

when

and
|s|\to\infty
.
It follows that we can move the

\Re s=c\geq 1
to
\Re s=\Re\rho+\epsilon<1/2

and derive

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{-{1\over 2}}^{1\over 2}\left(f(tx+mx)-f(mx)\right){\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\sin\pi t}dt

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}{1\over 2\pi i}\int_{\Re s=\Re\rho+\epsilon}{-s\widehat{F}_{\rho}(s)\over x^{s}\zeta(s)}\{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}{1\over m^{s}}\int_{-{1\over 2}}^{1\over 2}{\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\sin\pi t}dt\int_{1}^{1+{t\over m}}{du\over u^{s+1}}\}ds&\text{if $0<x<1$}\\
{1\over 2\pi i}\int_{\Re s=1}{-s\widehat{F}_{\rho}(s)\over x^{s}\zeta(s)}\{\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}{1\over m^{s}}\int_{-{1\over 2}}^{1\over 2}{\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\sin\pi t}dt\int_{1}^{1+{t\over m}}{du\over u^{s+1}}\}ds&\text{if $x\geq 1$}.\end{cases}

We write

\displaystyle\int_{-{1\over 2}}^{1\over 2}{\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\sin\pi t}dt\int_{1}^{1+{t\over m}}{du\over u^{s+1}}=\int_{-{1\over 2}}^{1\over 2}{\cos(2l+1)\pi t\over s(2l+1)\pi}{d\over dt}\{{{1-(1+{t\over m})^{-s}\over t}\over{\sin\pi t\over t}}\}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-{1\over 2}}^{1\over 2}{\cos(2l+1)\pi t\over(2l+1)\pi}\{{{t\over m}(1+{t\over m})^{-s-1}-{1-(1+{t\over m})^{-s}\over s}\over t\sin\pi t}+{d\over dt}({t\over\sin\pi t}){1\over t}\int_{1}^{1+{t\over m}}{du\over u^{s+1}}\}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-{1\over 2}}^{1\over 2}{\cos(2l+1)\pi t\over(2l+1)\pi}\{{{(1+{t\over m})^{-s-1}\over t}\int_{1}^{1+{t\over m}}{1-({1+{t\over m}\over u})^{s+1}\over t}du\over{\sin\pi t\over t}}+{d\over dt}({t\over\sin\pi t}){1\over t}\int_{1}^{1+{t\over m}}{du\over u^{s+1}}\}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-{1\over 2}}^{1\over 2}{\cos(2l+1)\pi t\over(2l+1)\pi}\{{{s+1\over t^{2}(1+{t\over m})^{s+1}}\int_{1}^{1+{t\over m}}du\int_{1}^{1+{t\over m}\over u}{dv\over v^{s+2}}\over{\sin\pi t\over t}}+{d\over dt}({t\over\sin\pi t}){1\over t}\int_{1}^{1+{t\over m}}{du\over u^{s+1}}\}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{-{1\over 2}}^{1\over 2}{\cos(2l+1)\pi t\over(2l+1)\pi}\{{{s+1\over m^{2}(1+{t\over m})^{s+1}}\int_{0}^{1}du\int_{0}^{1-u\over 1+{t\over m}u}{dv\over(1+{t\over m}v)^{s+2}}\over{\sin\pi t\over t}}+{d\over dt}({t\over\sin\pi t}){1\over m}\int_{0}^{1}{du\over(1+{t\over m}u)^{s+1}}\}dt

Since
\sin t\geq 2t/\pi
for

, for
|t|\leq 1/2

we have
|\sin\pi t/t|\geq 2
. Applying partial integration to the last term
of the above identity we obtain that

\int_{-{1\over 2}}^{1\over 2}{\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\sin\pi t}dt\int_{1}^{1+{t\over m}}{du\over u^{s+1}}\ll{(|s|+1)^{2}\over m(2l+1)^{2}}.

It follows that

|\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{-{1\over 2}}^{1\over 2}\left(f(tx+mx)-f(mx)\right){\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\sin\pi t}dt|\ll\begin{cases}{x^{-\Re\rho-\epsilon}\over(2l+1)^{2}}&\text{if $0<x<1$}\\
{x^{-1}\over(2l+1)^{2}}&\text{if $x\geq 1$}.\end{cases}

Therefore

\int_{0}^{\infty}|\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\int_{-{1\over 2}}^{1\over 2}\left(f(tx+mx)-f(mx)\right){\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\sin\pi t}dt|^{2}dx\ll{1\over(2l+1)^{4}}.

Lemma 3.3

Let
f(t)=Z^{-1}F_{\rho}(t)
. Then

\int_{0}^{\infty}|\int_{0}^{1\over 2}f(xt)({1\over\pi t}-{1\over\sin\pi t})\sin(2l+1)\pi tdt|^{2}dx\ll{1\over(2l+1)^{2}}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.

Lemma 3.4

Let
f(t)=Z^{-1}F_{\rho}(t)
with

\Re\rho<1/2

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{1\over 2}^{\infty}f(xt){\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\pi t}dt\right|^{2}dx\ll{1\over(2l+1)^{4}}

Proof
. As

f(t)={1\over 2\pi i}\int_{\Re s=c}{\widehat{F}_{\rho}(s)\over\zeta(s)}t^{-s}ds

c\geq 1
, we can write

\int_{1\over 2}^{\infty}f(xt){\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\pi t}dt={1\over 2\pi i}\int_{\Re s=c}{\widehat{F}_{\rho}(s)\over\zeta(s)}\{\int_{1\over 2}^{\infty}{\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\pi t^{1+s}}dt\}{ds\over x^{s}}

where change of order of integration is permissible as the double integral is
absolute integrable.

By partial integration,

\int_{1\over 2}^{\infty}f(xt){\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\pi t}dt=\begin{cases}{-1\over 2\pi i}\int_{\Re s=1}{(1+s)\widehat{F}_{\rho}(s)\over\zeta(s)}\{\int_{1\over 2}^{\infty}{\cos(2l+1)\pi t\over(2l+1)\pi^{2}t^{2+s}}dt\}{ds\over x^{s}}&\text{if $x\geq 1$}\\
{-1\over 2\pi i}\int_{\Re s=\Re\rho+\epsilon}{(1+s)\widehat{F}_{\rho}(s)\over\zeta(s)}\{\int_{1\over 2}^{\infty}{\cos(2l+1)\pi t\over(2l+1)\pi^{2}t^{2+s}}dt\}{ds\over x^{s}}&\text{if $x<1$}\end{cases}

where
\epsilon
is a sufficiently small positive satisfying

\Re\rho+\epsilon<{1\over 2}
. Also,

\int_{1\over 2}^{\infty}{\cos(2l+1)\pi t\over(2l+1)t^{2+s}}dt=-{2^{2+s}\sin(l+{1\over 2})\pi\over(2l+1)^{2}\pi}+{2+s\over\pi(2l+1)^{2}}\int_{1\over 2}^{\infty}{\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over t^{3+s}}dt\ll{|s|+2\over(2l+1)^{2}}

for
0<\Re s<1
. It follows that

|\int_{1\over 2}^{\infty}f(xt){\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\pi t}dt|\ll\begin{cases}{x^{-\Re\rho-\epsilon}\over(2l+1)^{2}}&\text{if $0<x\leq 1$}\\
{x^{-1}\over(2l+1)^{2}}&\text{if $x>1$}.\end{cases}

This inequality implies that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\int_{1\over 2}^{\infty}f(xt){\sin(2l+1)\pi t\over\pi t}dt\right|^{2}dx\ll{1\over(2l+1)^{4}}.

This completes the proof of the lemma.

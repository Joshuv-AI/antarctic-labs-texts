Extensions and crossed modules of n n -Lie Rinehart algebras
A. Ben Hassine, T. Chtioui, M. Elhamdadi, S. Mabrouk

Abstract
We introduce a notion of n n -Lie Rinehart algebras as a generalization of Lie Rinehart algebras to n n -ary case. This notion is also an algebraic analogue of n n -Lie algebroids. We develop representation theory and describe a cohomology complex of n n -Lie Rinehart algebras. Furthermore, we investigate extension theory of n n -Lie Rinehart algebras by means of 2 2 -cocycles. Finally, we introduce crossed modules of n n -Lie Rinehart algebras to gain a better understanding of their third dimensional cohomology groups.



Keywords:

n
-Lie Rinehart algebras,
n
-Lie algebroids, representations, cohomology, Extensions, crossed modules.

Definition 2.1
.

An
n
-Lie algebra is a vector space
L
equipped with a bracket operation
[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot]:\wedge^{n}L\longrightarrow L
such that for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}
,
y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\in L
, we have :

\displaystyle[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}]]

\displaystyle=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}[y_{1},\cdots,y_{i-1},[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}],y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n}].

(2.1)

The elements in
\wedge^{n-1}L
are called fundamental elements.
On
\wedge^{n-1}L
, one can define a new bracket operation
[\cdot,\cdot]_{F}
by

[X,Y]_{F}=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n-1}y_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge y_{i-1}\wedge[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}]_{L}\wedge y_{i+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge y_{n-1},

(2.2)

for all
X=x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{n-1}
and
Y=y_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge y_{n-1}
.
It is proved in
[
58
]
that
(\wedge^{n-1}L,[\cdot,\cdot]_{F})
is a Leibniz algebra.

Definition 2.2
.

A morphism of
n
-Lie algebras
f:(L,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot]_{L})\longrightarrow(L^{\prime},[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot]_{L^{\prime}})
is a linear map
f:L\longrightarrow L^{\prime}
such that

\displaystyle f[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[f(x_{1}),\cdots,f(x_{n})]_{L^{\prime}},\hskip 8.53581pt\forall x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in L.

(2.3)

Definition 2.3
.

A representation of an
n
-Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot])
on a vector space

is a linear map
\rho:\wedge^{n-1}L\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(M)

such that for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}\in L
, we have

\displaystyle\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})\circ\rho(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1})-\rho(y_{1},\cdots,y_{n-1})\circ\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n-1}\rho(y_{1},y_{2},\cdots,[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}],y_{i+1},\cdots,y_{n-1});

(2.4)

\displaystyle\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-2},[y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\rho(y_{1},\cdots,\widehat{y_{i}},\cdots,y_{n})\circ\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-2},y_{i}).

(2.5)

We denote such a representation by
(M,\rho)
.

Example 2.4
.

Define
\mathsf{ad}:\wedge^{n-1}L\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(L)
by

\mathsf{ad}(X)y=[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y],\quad\forall X=x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{n-1}\in\wedge^{n-1}L,~y\in L.

(2.6)

Then

-Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot])

Proposition 2.5
.

Let

be a representation of an
n
-Lie algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot])
.
Define a bracket operation
[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot]_{\rho}:\wedge^{n}(L\oplus M)\longrightarrow L\oplus M

[x_{1}+m_{1},\ldots,x_{n}+m_{n}]_{\rho}=[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{n-i}\rho(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{i},\ldots,x_{n})m_{i},\,\,\,\,\forall x_{i}\in L,m_{i}\in M.

-Lie algebra

(L,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot])

An

In this section, we introduce a notion of

Definition 3.1
.

A
n
-Lie Rinehart algebra over
A
is a tuple
(L,A,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot],\rho)
,
where
A
is an associative commutative algebra,
L
is an
A-
module,
[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot]:L\times\cdots\times L\to L

a skew-symmetric

\rho:\wedge^{n-1}L\to Der(A)

such that the following conditions hold:

1.

(L,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot])
is an
n
-Lie algebra.

2.

The map
\rho
is a representation of
(L,[\cdot,\cdots,\cdot])
on
A
.

3.

For all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}\in L,a\in A,

\rho(ax_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})=a\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1}).

(3.1)

4.

The compatibility condition:

[x_{1},\cdots,ax_{n}]=a[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]+\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})ax_{n},

(3.2)

for all
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}\in L,a\in A
.

Example 3.2
.

Let
(A,[~,\ldots,~],\rho)
be an

-Lie algebroid over a smooth manifold

. This

-Lie algebroid provides an

-Lie Rinehart algebra

Definition 3.4
.

A Leibniz-Rinehart algebra over

is a tuple
(L,A,[\cdot,\cdot],\rho)
, where
A
is an

L
is an
A
-module,

\rho([x,y])=\rho(x)\rho(y)-\rho(y)\rho(x)
.

3.

\rho(ax)=a\rho(x)
.

4.

Compatibility condition

\displaystyle[x,ay]

\displaystyle=a[x,y]+\rho(x)(a)y.

(3.3)

for all

.

be a
n
-Lie Rinehart algebra and denote

aX=x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge ax_{i}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{n-1},\ \text{for~any}~X=x_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge x_{n-1},\ i=1,...,n-1.

Proposition 3.5
.

With the above notations,

is a Leibniz-Rinehart algebra, where the bracket

For all

\widehat{\rho}([X,Y]_{F})=\widehat{\rho}(X)\widehat{\rho}(Y)-\widehat{\rho}(Y)\widehat{\rho}(X),\forall X,Y\in\mathcal{L}.

Furthermore, we have

\widehat{\rho}(aX)=\rho(ax_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})=a\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})=a\widehat{\rho}(X).

Thanks to Eqs.(
2.2
) and (

\displaystyle[X,aY]_{F}=

\displaystyle[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},ay_{1}]\wedge y_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge y_{n-1}+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=2}^{n-1}ay_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge y_{i-1}\wedge[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}]\wedge y_{i+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{1}]\wedge y_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge y_{n-1}+\rho(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1})ay_{1}\wedge y_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge y_{n-1}

\displaystyle+a\sumop\displaylimits_{i=2}^{n-1}y_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge y_{i-1}\wedge[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n-1},y_{i}]\wedge y_{i+1}\wedge\cdots\wedge y_{n-1}

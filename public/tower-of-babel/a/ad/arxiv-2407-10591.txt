Courant algebroid without constraints on fluxes on its Dirac structures
I. Ivanišević, B. Sazdović

Abstract
We examine the standard Courant bracket and its extensions, defined by twists with different O ⁡ ( D , D ) O(D,D) transformations relevant to string theory. We analyze Dirac structures on these Courant algebroids and derive the constraints they impose on fluxes. In the end, we show that the Courant algebroid simultaneously twisted by both B B and θ \theta is characterized by Dirac structures with no restrictions on fluxes.

In the first chapter, we deal with the mathematical preliminaries. We procede by considering the so called standard Courant algebroid, defined with the Courant bracket,
O(D,D)
invariant inner product, and the projection to the tangent bundle as its anchor. We show that symplectic and Poisson manifolds are its Dirac structures. Though these results are already well known

In the third chapter, we show how every element of the

O(D,D)
element. The appropriate transformation of the anchor ensures the manifest satisfaction of all Courant algebroid compatibility conditions. Though Courant algebroids related with
O(D,D)
are isomorphic, the particular realisations of algebroids and their respective Dirac structures are different. This can affect different physical properties, such as fluxes, interpretations of dualities and symmetries, and (non)-geometric nature of the background.

In the proceeding two chapters, we illustrate the characteristics of some well known Courant algebroids and their associated Dirac structures, including the case of
B

\theta

B

\theta

Lastly, we consider the Courant bracket twisted at the same time by
B
and
\theta
and its respective Courant algebroid. On its associated Dirac structures, we demonstrate that all fluxes can exist. This way, we obtain the most important result that has motivated our paper.

2.1
Lie derivative

The Lie derivative determines the change of any tensor field along the parametrized flow defined by a vector field. Specifically, the Lie derivative of the tensor field T of type (p, q) with respect to the vector field
\xi
is another tensor field
{\cal L}_{\xi}T
of type (p, q) with components

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\xi}T^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}...\mu_{p}}_{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}...\nu_{q}}=\xi^{\rho}\partial_{\rho}T^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}...\mu_{p}}_{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}...\nu_{q}}-\sum_{i=0}^{p}T^{\mu_{1}...\rho\hat{\mu}_{i}...\mu_{p}}_{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}...\nu_{q}}\partial_{\rho}\xi^{\mu_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{q}T^{\mu_{1}...\mu_{p}}_{\nu_{1}...\rho\hat{\nu}_{i}...\nu_{q}}\partial_{\nu_{i}}\xi^{\rho}\,,

(2.15)

where
\hat{\nu}_{i}
denotes omission of such index, e.g.
T^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}...\mu_{p}}_{\nu_{1}...\rho\hat{\nu}_{q}}\partial_{\nu_{q}}\xi^{\rho}=T^{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}...\mu_{p}}_{\nu_{1}\nu_{2}...\rho}\partial_{\nu_{q}}\xi^{\rho}
.
In the local coordinate basis, the action of Lie derivative on vectors and functions is given by

({\cal L}_{\xi}\eta)^{\mu}=\xi^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}\eta^{\mu}-\eta^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}\xi^{\mu}\,,\quad{\cal L}_{\xi}f=\xi^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}f\,,

(2.16)

where
\eta
is a smooth vector field, and
f
is a smooth function. The action of Lie bracket between two vector fields
\xi_{1}
and
\xi_{2}
on a smooth function is defined through the relation

[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]_{L}f=({\cal L}_{\xi_{1}}\xi_{2})f\,.

(2.17)

It is a well known fact that for each smooth function
f
on a manifold
{\cal M}
the Lie bracket satisfies the Leibniz rule

[\xi_{1},f\xi_{2}]_{L}=f[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]_{L}+({\cal L}_{\xi_{1}}f)\ \xi_{2}\,,

(2.18)

as well as the Jacobi identity

[\xi_{1},[\xi_{2},\xi_{3}]_{L}]_{L}+[\xi_{2},[\xi_{3},\xi_{1}]_{L}]_{L}+[\xi_{3},[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]_{L}]_{L}=0\,.

(2.19)

It is exactly the Lie bracket that governs the Poisson bracket algebra of a string general coordinate transformations

\{{\cal G}_{\xi_{1}},{\cal G}_{\xi_{2}}\}=-{\cal G}_{[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]_{L}}\,.

(2.20)

Apart from vectors and functions, the Lie derivative can also act on differential
p
-forms
\lambda
by

{\cal L}_{\xi}\lambda=di_{\xi}\lambda+i_{\xi}d\lambda\,,

(2.21)

where
i_{\xi}
is the interior derivative, and
d
is the exterior derivative. The interior derivative is a degree -1 derivation of the algebra of differential forms, given by

(i_{\xi}\lambda)(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},...\xi_{p-1})=\lambda(\xi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2},...\xi_{p-1})\,,

(2.22)

where
\xi_{1}
, … ,
\xi_{p}
are vectors. In case of 1-form
\lambda
, we have

i_{\xi}\lambda=\xi^{\mu}\lambda_{\mu}\,.

(2.23)

The exterior derivative is a degree 1 derivation of the algebra of differential forms. On function it acts as a differential operator

df(\xi)={\cal L}_{\xi}f\Leftrightarrow(df)_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}f\,.

(2.24)

In case of an arbitrary
p
-form
\lambda
, the exterior derivative is defined by

\displaystyle d\lambda(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}(-1)^{i+1}\xi_{i}\Big(\lambda(\xi_{1},...,\hat{\xi}_{i},...,\xi_{p})\Big)

(2.25)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\lambda([\xi_{i},\xi_{j}]_{L},\xi_{1},...,\hat{\xi}_{i},...,\hat{\xi}_{j},...,\xi_{p})\,,

where
\hat{\xi}_{i}
denotes the emission of
\xi_{i}
, and
\xi(\lambda)=\xi^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\lambda
. In case of a 1-form
\lambda
, the above expression reduces to

(d\lambda)_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}\lambda_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}\lambda_{\mu}\,.

(2.26)

2.2
Lie algebroid

Let us now consider a more general vector bundle
V

{\cal M}
with a bracket on its space of smooth sections, together with a vector bundle map
\rho:V\to T{\cal M}

\rho[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]=[\rho(\xi_{1}),\rho(\xi_{2})]_{L}\,,

[\xi_{1},f\xi_{2}]=f[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]+({\cal L}_{\rho(\xi_{1})}f)\ \xi_{2}\,.

(2.28)

In a similar manner, one can define the exterior derivative for any Lie algebroid by
[
39

\displaystyle d\lambda(\xi_{1},...,\xi_{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{p}(-1)^{i+1}\rho(\xi_{i})\Big(\lambda(\xi_{1},...,\hat{\xi}_{i},...,\xi_{p})\Big)

(2.29)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i<j}(-1)^{i+j}\lambda([\xi_{i},\xi_{j}],\xi_{1},...,\hat{\xi}_{i},...,\hat{\xi}_{j},...,\xi_{p})\,,

where
\lambda
are elements of the smooth sections of the
n

V^{\star}
, i.e.
n

n

2.3
Generalized tangent bundle

The generalized tangent bundle
[
1
]
is the direct sum of a tangent and cotangent bundle over a manifold. Its elements are generalized vectors decomposable into

\Lambda^{M}=\begin{pmatrix}\xi^{\mu}\\
\lambda_{\mu}\\

\langle\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\rangle=(\Lambda_{1}^{T})^{M}\eta_{MN}\Lambda_{2}^{N}\,,

\eta_{MN}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\

(2.40)

The inner product can also be expressed in terms of the components as

(2.41)

These mathematical structures are convenient to describe string symmetries. The generator of both general coordinate and local gauge transformation is given by

X^{M}=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa x^{\prime\mu}\\
\pi_{\mu}\\
\end{pmatrix}\,.

(2.44)

Assuming the standard canonical coordinate-momentum relations, it was shown that parameters of the symmetry generators in the Poisson bracket algebra of their respective generators

(2.47)

The Courant algebroid is the structure on the generalized tangent bundle analogous to the Lie algebroid. It was firstly introduced in
[

]
as the Drinfel’d double
[
35
,
36
]
of a Lie bialgebroid - a structure that contains two Lie algebroid structures on two dual vector bundles satisfying certain compatiblity conditions between its brackets (see

]

To define the Courant algebroid, one introduces a vector bundle

\langle,\rangle
and a skew-symmetric bracket
[,]
on a smooth section of a vector bundle
E
, with a smooth bundle map
\rho:E\to TM
as an anchor
2
2

2

[
11
]
.
. Then, it is possible to define the differential operator on

\langle{\cal D}f,\Lambda\rangle={\cal L}_{\rho(\Lambda)}f\,.

(2.51)

The structure
\Big(E,\langle,\rangle,[,],\rho\Big)
is called the Courant algebroid if it satisfies the following compatibility relations between its elements

\displaystyle\rho[\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}]=[\rho(\Lambda_{1}),\rho(\Lambda_{2})]_{L}\,,

(2.52)

\displaystyle[\Lambda_{1},f\Lambda_{2}]=f[\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}]+({\cal L}_{\rho(\Lambda_{1})}f)\Lambda_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\langle\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\rangle{\cal D}f\,,

(2.53)

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{\rho(\Lambda_{1})}\langle\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3}\rangle=\langle[\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}]+\frac{1}{2}{\cal D}\langle\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\rangle,\Lambda_{3}\rangle+\langle\Lambda_{2},[\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{3}]+\frac{1}{2}{\cal D}\langle\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{3}\rangle\rangle\,,

(2.54)

\displaystyle\langle{\cal D}f,{\cal D}g\rangle=0\,,

(2.55)

\displaystyle\text{Jac}(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3})={\cal D}\text{Nij}(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3})\,,

(2.56)

for all
\Lambda_{1}
,
\Lambda_{2}
,
\Lambda_{3}

\text{Jac}(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3})=[[\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}]\,,\Lambda_{3}]+[[\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3}]\,,\Lambda_{1}]+[[\Lambda_{3},\Lambda_{1}]\,,\Lambda_{2}]\,,

(2.57)

and
Nij
is the Nijenhuis operator, given by

\text{Nij}(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3})=\frac{1}{6}\Big(\langle[\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}],\Lambda_{3}\rangle+\langle[\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3}],\Lambda_{1}\rangle+\langle[\Lambda_{3},\Lambda_{1}],\Lambda_{2}\rangle\Big)\,.

(2.58)

The most well known example of the Courant algebroid, what we will denote as the standard Courant algebroid, consists of the generalized tangent bundle
T{\cal M}\oplus T^{\star}{\cal M}
with the Courant bracket, and the natural projection
\pi
as its anchor, given by

\pi(\Lambda)=\pi(\xi\oplus\lambda)=\xi\,,

(2.59)

which in the matrix notation can be expressed as

\pi=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\,.

(2.60)

The differential operator is just the exterior derivative
d
. It is well known in literature (see for example
[
1
]
for proof) that the compatibility conditions (
2.52
) - (
2.56
) are satisfied. Throughout the paper other examples of Courant algebroids will be presented.

2.5
Dirac structures

Both of the Leibniz and Jacobi identity are violated with the term in the form of the inner product of two generalized vectors. It is clear that the sub-bundles so that the inner product of vectors in its section is zero will satisfy both the Jacobi identity and the Leibniz rule.
The sub-bundles on which

\langle\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\rangle=0\,,

(2.61)

for each generalized vectors
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
are called isotropic sub-bundles. The isotropic spaces with the maximal dimension that are closed under the skew-symmetric bracket are called Dirac structures
[
22
]
.

For any generalized vector, there are two natural projections on isotropic spaces. For a 2-form
B
, one can define

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{B}(\Lambda)=\xi^{\mu}\oplus 2B_{\mu\nu}\xi^{\nu}\,,\qquad B_{\mu\nu}=-B_{\nu\mu}\,,

(2.62)

while for a bi-vector
\theta
, one can define

\displaystyle{\cal I}_{\theta}(\Lambda)=\kappa\theta^{\mu\nu}\lambda_{\nu}\oplus\lambda_{\mu}\,,\qquad\theta^{\mu\nu}=-\theta^{\nu\mu}\,.

(2.63)

The sub-bundle in the form (
2.62
) we will refer to as the graph of
B
over the tangent bundle, while the sub-bundle in the form (
2.63
) we will refer to as the graph of
\theta
over the cotangent bundle. In
[
1
]
(Proposition 2.6, page 7), it has been demonstrated that all maximally isotropic suspaces are in the form of graphs (
2.62
) and (
2.63
). In case when isotropic subspaces are in addition closed under the appropriate algebroid bracket, they are Dirac structures.

Dirac structures can define various different geometries, which can be illustrated on the example of standard Courant algebroid. Let us firstly consider the condition for the isotropic space in the image of projection
{\cal I}_{B}
to be a Dirac structures. Substituting the relations between the vector and 1-form components (
2.62
) in (
2.47
) and (
2.48
), one obtains

[\xi_{1}^{\mu}\oplus 2B_{\mu\rho}\xi_{1}^{\rho},\ \xi_{2}^{\nu}\oplus 2B_{\nu\sigma}\xi_{2}^{\sigma}]_{\cal C}=\xi^{\mu}\oplus\lambda_{\mu}\,,

(2.64)

where

\xi^{\mu}=\xi_{1}^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}\xi_{2}^{\mu}-\xi_{2}^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}\xi_{1}^{\mu}\,,

and

\lambda_{\mu}=2B_{\mu\rho}(\xi^{\nu}_{1}\partial_{\nu}\xi^{\rho}_{2}-\xi^{\nu}_{2}\partial_{\nu}\xi^{\rho}_{1})-2B_{\mu\nu\rho}\xi^{\nu}_{1}\xi_{2}^{\rho}\,,

(2.65)

where
B_{\mu\nu\rho}
is the Kalb-Ramond field strength (or
H
-flux), given by

B_{\mu\nu\rho}=\partial_{\mu}B_{\nu\rho}+\partial_{\nu}B_{\rho\mu}+\partial_{\rho}B_{\mu\nu}\,.

(2.66)

Due to this term,
{\cal I}_{B}(\Lambda)
is a Dirac structure only in a special case of a closed form 2-form

[{\cal I}_{B}(\Lambda_{1}),{\cal I}_{B}(\Lambda_{2})]_{\cal C}={\cal I}_{B}\Big([\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C}\Big)\,,\ dB=0\,.

(2.67)

We can identify this as a symplectic structure for case of a non-degenerate
B
, or presymplectic structure in a more general case.

In the case of
{\cal I}_{\theta}
(
2.63
), we have

[\theta^{\mu\rho}\lambda_{1\rho}\oplus\lambda_{1\mu},\ \theta^{\nu\sigma}\lambda_{2\sigma}\oplus\lambda_{2\nu}]_{\cal C}=\xi^{\mu}\oplus\lambda_{\mu}\,,

(2.68)

where

\xi^{\mu}=\theta^{\mu\sigma}\theta^{\nu\rho}(\lambda_{1\rho}\partial_{\nu}\lambda_{2\sigma}-\lambda_{2\rho}\partial_{\nu}\lambda_{1\sigma})+(\theta^{\nu\rho}\partial_{\nu}\theta^{\mu\sigma}-\theta^{\nu\sigma}\partial_{\nu}\theta^{\mu\rho})\lambda_{1\rho}\lambda_{2\sigma}=\theta^{\mu\sigma}\lambda_{\sigma}+R^{\mu\rho\sigma}\lambda_{1\rho}\lambda_{2\sigma}\,,

(2.69)

and

\lambda_{\mu}=\theta^{\nu\rho}(\lambda_{1\rho}\partial_{\nu}\lambda_{2\mu}-\lambda_{2\rho}\partial_{\nu}\lambda_{1\mu})+\partial_{\mu}\theta^{\nu\rho}\lambda_{1\rho}\lambda_{2\nu}\,,

(2.70)

where
R
is the non-geometric flux, given by

R^{\mu\nu\rho}=\theta^{\mu\sigma}\partial_{\sigma}\theta^{\nu\rho}+\theta^{\nu\sigma}\partial_{\sigma}\theta^{\rho\mu}+\theta^{\rho\sigma}\partial_{\sigma}\theta^{\mu\nu}\,.

(2.71)

The
R
flux can be written as Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket
2R=[\theta,\theta]_{S}
(
2.31
), so we see that the subspace
{\cal I}_{\theta}
with
\theta
being the Poisson bi-vector represents a Dirac structure

[{\cal I}_{\theta}(\Lambda_{1}),{\cal I}_{\theta}(\Lambda_{2})]_{\cal C}={\cal I}_{\theta}\Big([\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C}\Big)\,,\quad[\theta,\theta]_{S}=0\,.

(2.72)

Both Dirac structures impose restriction on string fluxes. In order to construct Courant algebroid so that we can lift some of these restrictions on Dirac structures, we introduce twisted Courant brackets.

3
Twisted Courant bracket

In this paper, we adopt an approach that allows for twisting of the Courant bracket by an arbitrary
O(D,D)
transformation, i.e. the transformation that preserves the inner product
(\ref{eq:skalproizvod})

\langle{\cal O}\Lambda_{1},{\cal O}\Lambda_{2}\rangle=\langle\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\rangle\Leftrightarrow({\cal O}\Lambda_{1})^{T}\ \eta\ ({\cal O}\Lambda_{2})=\Lambda^{T}_{1}\ \eta\ \Lambda_{2}\,,

(3.1)

which is satisfied for the condition

{\cal O}^{T}\ \eta\ {\cal O}=\eta\,.

(3.2)

The solutions for the above equation are in the form
{\cal O}=e^{T}
, where
[
1
]

T=\begin{pmatrix}A&\theta\\
B&-A^{T}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(3.3)

with
\theta:T^{\star}{\cal M}\to T{\cal M}
and
B:T{\cal M}\to T^{\star}{\cal M}
being antisymmetric, and
A:T{\cal M}\to T{\cal M}
, being the endomorphisms on the space of vectors. In general case,
B
and
\theta
can be independent for
{\cal O}
to satisfy condition (
3.2
). If
{\cal O}=e^{T}
, then it is the element of the
O(D,D)
group
[
1
]
.

In order to see how twisted Courant brackets appear in the algebra of symmetry generators, we note that the relation (
2.45
) can be written as

\int d\sigma_{1}d\sigma_{2}\Big\{\langle\Lambda_{1},X\rangle(\sigma_{1}),\langle\Lambda_{2},X\rangle(\sigma_{2})\Big\}=-\int d\sigma\langle[\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C},X\rangle(\sigma)\,.

(3.4)

Consider now the action of some element of
O(D,D)
on the double coordinate
X
(
2.44
) and the double gauge parameter
\Lambda
(
2.38
)

\hat{X}^{M}={\cal O}^{M}_{\ N}\ X^{N}\,,\ \hat{\Lambda}^{M}={\cal O}^{M}_{\ N}\ \Lambda^{N}\,,

(3.5)

Using (
3.1
) and (
3.5
), the relation (
3.4
) can be written as

\displaystyle\int d\sigma_{1}d\sigma_{2}\Big\{\langle\hat{\Lambda}_{1},\hat{X}\rangle(\sigma_{1}),\langle\hat{\Lambda}_{2},\hat{X}\rangle(\sigma_{2})\Big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int d\sigma\langle[\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C},X\rangle(\sigma)

(3.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int d\sigma\langle[\hat{\Lambda}_{1},\hat{\Lambda}_{2}]_{{\cal C}_{T}},\hat{X}\rangle(\sigma)\,,

where we expressed the right hand side in terms of some new bracket
[\hat{\Lambda}_{1},\hat{\Lambda}_{2}]_{{\cal C}_{T}}
.
The left hand side is the Poisson bracket of two inner product of two generalized vectors. It can be recognized as the Poisson bracket algebra of two generators of the form

{\cal\hat{G}}_{\hat{\Lambda}}=\int d\sigma\langle\hat{\Lambda},\hat{X}\rangle\,.

(3.7)

Moreover, using (
3.5
) and (
3.1
), the right hand side of (
3.4
) can be written as

\langle[\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C},X\rangle=\langle[{\cal O}^{-1}\hat{\Lambda}_{1},{\cal O}^{-1}\hat{\Lambda}_{2}]_{\cal C},{\cal O}^{-1}\hat{X}\rangle=\langle{\cal O}[{\cal O}^{-1}\hat{\Lambda}_{1},{\cal O}^{-1}\hat{\Lambda}_{2}]_{\cal C},\hat{X}\rangle\,.

(3.8)

Using (
3.4
), (
3.6
) and (
3.8
), one obtains

[\hat{\Lambda}_{1},\hat{\Lambda}_{2}]_{{\cal C}_{T}}={\cal O}[{\cal O}^{-1}\hat{\Lambda}_{1},{\cal O}^{-1}\hat{\Lambda}_{2}]_{\cal C}=e^{T}[e^{-T}\hat{\Lambda}_{1},e^{-T}\hat{\Lambda}_{2}]_{\cal C}\,.

(3.9)

This is a definition of
T
-twisted Courant bracket. We see that it can be easily obtained from the Poisson bracket algebra of two generators written in a suitable basis, as in (
3.7
).

Throughout this paper, we use the notation where the
[,]_{\cal C}
is the Courant bracket, while when
{\cal C}
is accompanied with an index, it represents the twist of the Courant bracket by the indexed field, e.g.
[,]_{{\cal C}_{B}}
is the Courant bracket twisted by
B
.

3.1
Courant algebroid

The relation (
3.9
) tells us that an element of the
O(D,D)
group defines an isomorphism between different brackets

e^{T}[\hat{\Lambda}_{1},\hat{\Lambda}_{2}]_{{\cal C}}=[e^{T}\hat{\Lambda}_{1},e^{T}\hat{\Lambda}_{2}]_{{\cal C}_{T}}\,,

(3.10)

while keeping the inner product invariant. We want to see if this isomorphism between the brackets translate into the isomorphism between the Courant algebroids as well. In order to complete the construction of the Courant algebroids that can be defined with the twisted Courant brackets we are looking for an anchor that satisfies

\rho\Big([\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C_{T}}\Big)=[\rho(\Lambda_{1}),\rho(\Lambda_{2})]_{L}\,.

(3.11)

Using (
3.9
), we rewrite the previous relation as

\rho\Big(e^{T}[e^{-T}\Lambda_{1},e^{-T}\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C}\Big)=[\rho(\Lambda_{1}),\rho(\Lambda_{2})]_{L}=[\rho\Big(e^{T}(e^{-T}\Lambda_{1})\Big),\rho\Big(e^{T}(e^{-T}\Lambda_{2})\Big)]_{L}\,.

(3.12)

Now we can use the fact that the natural projection is the anchor for the standard Courant algebroid, with Courant bracket as its anchor bracket. The anchor for the twisted Courant bracket is then given by

\rho(\Lambda)=\pi(e^{-T}\Lambda)\,.

(3.13)

Next, the differential operator can be obtained from

\langle{\cal D}f,\Lambda\rangle={\cal L}_{\rho(\Lambda)}\ f={\cal L}_{\pi(e^{-T}\Lambda)}f=\langle df,e^{-T}\Lambda\rangle=\langle e^{T}df,\Lambda\rangle\,,

(3.14)

where in the second step the definition of
\rho
is used, in the next step we the Courant algebroid property for the natural projection was used, and in the last step the fact that
e^{-T}
is orthogonal. The differential operator is given by

{\cal D}f=e^{T}df\,.

(3.15)

The anchor
\rho
(
3.13
) was defined so that it satisfies the first Courant algebroid compatibility condition (
2.52
). Now we verify the remaining conditions.
We have

\displaystyle[\Lambda_{1},f\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C_{T}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{T}[e^{-T}\Lambda_{1},fe^{-T}\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C}

(3.16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{T}\Big(f[e^{-T}\Lambda_{1},e^{-T}\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C}+({\cal L}_{\pi(e^{-T}\Lambda_{1})}f)(e^{-T}\Lambda_{2})-\frac{1}{2}\langle e^{-T}\Lambda_{1},e^{-T}\Lambda_{2}\rangle df\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f[\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C_{T}}+({\cal L}_{\rho(\Lambda_{1})}f)\Lambda_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\langle\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\rangle{\cal D}f\,,

where we firstly used definition of the twisted Courant bracket (
3.9
), afterwards we applied the second compatibility condition (
2.53
) for the Courant bracket, and in the end used the expressions for the anchor
\rho
(
3.13
) and the differential operator
{\cal D}
(
3.15
), as well as the fact that
O(D,D)
transformations keep the inner product invariant (
3.1
).

For the following condition (
2.54
), we firstly write

\displaystyle\langle[\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C_{T}}+\frac{1}{2}{\cal D}\langle\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\rangle,\Lambda_{3}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle e^{T}[e^{-T}\Lambda_{1},e^{-T}\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C}+\frac{1}{2}e^{T}d\langle\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\rangle,\Lambda_{3}\rangle

(3.17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle[e^{-T}\Lambda_{1},e^{-T}\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C}+\frac{1}{2}d\langle e^{-T}\Lambda_{1},e^{-T}\Lambda_{2}\rangle,e^{-T}\Lambda_{3}\rangle\,,

and

{\cal L}_{\rho(\Lambda_{1})}\langle\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3}\rangle={\cal L}_{\pi(e^{-T}\Lambda_{1})}\langle e^{-T}\Lambda_{2},e^{-T}\Lambda_{3}\rangle\,.

(3.18)

From the previous two relations it is obvious that the third compatibility condition is equivalent to the respective condition for the standard Courant algebroid for generalized vectors
e^{-T}\Lambda_{i}\,,\ i\in 1,\ 2,\ 3
.

The fourth condition (
2.55
) is as easily obtained from the orthogonality of
e^{T}

\langle{\cal D}f,{\cal D}g\rangle=\langle e^{T}df,e^{T}dg\rangle=\langle df,dg\rangle=0\,.

(3.19)

Lastly, we note that

[[\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C_{T}},\Lambda_{3}]_{\cal C_{T}}=e^{T}[[e^{-T}\Lambda_{1},e^{-T}\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C},e^{-T}\Lambda_{3}]_{\cal C}\,,

(3.20)

from which we write the Jacobiator for the twisted Courant bracket

\text{Jac}_{{\cal C}_{T}}(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3})=e^{T}\text{Jac}_{\cal C}(e^{-T}\Lambda_{1},e^{-T}\Lambda_{2},e^{-T}\Lambda_{3})\,.

(3.21)

Similarly, we note that

\langle[\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C_{T}},\Lambda_{3}\rangle=\langle e^{T}[e^{-T}\Lambda_{1},e^{-T}\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C},\Lambda_{3}\rangle=\langle[e^{-T}\Lambda_{1},e^{-T}\Lambda_{2}]_{\cal C},e^{-T}\Lambda_{3}\rangle\,,

(3.22)

from which one easily obtains the Nijenhuis operator

\text{Nij}_{{\cal C}_{T}}(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{3})=\text{Nij}_{\cal C}(e^{-T}\Lambda_{1},e^{-T}\Lambda_{2},e^{-T}\Lambda_{3})\,.

(3.23)

Using (
3.15
), (
3.21
) and (
3.23
), we obtain the last compatibility condition (
2.56
).

We have proven that each
O(D,D)
transformation defines a Courant algebroid
\Big(T{\cal M}\oplus T^{\star}{\cal M},\langle,\rangle,[,]_{{\cal C}_{T}},\rho\Big)
with the appropriate twisted Courant bracket and the natural inner product (
2.39
). As we consider all
O(D,D)
transformations, including those that are not automorphisms of the Courant bracket, we can derive various twisted Courant brackets along with their corresponding Courant algebroids and Dirac structures, as will be discussed in the following chapters.

We can make some sense of it if we consider the twisted Schouten-Nijenhuis bracket, as the generalization of the twisted Lie bracket on the space of multi-vectors

\displaystyle[f,g]_{\hat{S}}=0\,,\quad[\xi,f]_{\hat{S}}={\cal\hat{L}}_{\xi}(f)\,,\quad[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]_{\hat{S}}=[\xi_{1},\xi_{2}]_{\hat{L}}\,,

(7.23)

with other brackets defined using the appropriate graded identities. Then, it has been shown
[
31
]
that

\breve{R}=\frac{1}{2}[\breve{\theta},\breve{\theta}]_{\hat{S}}\,.

(7.24)

We see that in case of the simultaneous twisting, all generalized fluxes exist. They necessitate some other twisted structures to be defined and all of them depend on both fields
B
and
\theta
.

7.1
Courant algebroid

The Courant bracket twisted at the same time by
B
and
\theta
defines the Courant algebroid with

\rho_{\breve{B}}\Lambda=\pi e^{-\breve{B}}\Lambda=\begin{pmatrix}{\cal C}\xi-\kappa({\cal S}\theta)\lambda\\
0\end{pmatrix}\,,

(7.25)

as its anchor, and

{\cal D}_{\breve{B}}f=e^{\breve{B}}df=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa{\cal S}\theta\ df\\
{\cal C}^{T}df\end{pmatrix}\,

(7.26)

as its differential operator.

7.2
Dirac structures

Consider following fields

\breve{B}=B{\cal S}{\cal C}^{-1}\,,\quad\breve{\theta}={\cal S}{\cal C}^{-1}\theta\,.

(7.27)

We can define the graph over tangent bundle as

{\cal I}_{\breve{B}}(\Lambda)=\xi^{\mu}\oplus 2\breve{B}_{\mu\nu}\xi^{\nu}\,,

(7.28)

and over cotangent bundle as

{\cal I}_{\breve{\theta}}(\Lambda)=\breve{\theta}^{\mu\nu}\lambda_{\nu}\oplus\lambda_{\mu}\,.

(7.29)

In the former case, the generator
\breve{G}
becomes

\displaystyle\breve{\cal{G}}_{{\cal I}_{\breve{B}}(\breve{\Lambda})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\sigma\Big(\breve{\xi}^{\mu}(\pi_{\nu}{\cal C}^{\nu}_{\ \mu}+2\breve{B}_{\mu\rho}{\cal C}^{\rho}_{\ \nu}\kappa x^{\prime\nu})+2\breve{B}_{\mu\nu}\breve{\xi}^{\nu}({\cal C}^{\mu}_{\ \rho}\kappa x^{\prime\rho}+\kappa{\cal C}^{\mu}_{\ \rho}\breve{\theta}^{\rho\nu}\pi_{\nu})\Big)

(7.30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\sigma\pi_{\mu}({\cal C}^{-1})^{\mu}_{\ \nu}\breve{\xi}^{\nu}\,,

where we have used the well known identity between hyperbolic functions

1=\cosh^{2}\sqrt{\alpha}-\sinh^{2}\sqrt{\alpha}={\cal C}^{2}-2\kappa\breve{\theta}\breve{B}{\cal C}^{2}\,.

(7.31)

The generator (
7.30
) is just the generator of the general coordinate transformations. Since such a generator gives rise to the Lie bracket, the sub-bundle
{\cal I}_{\breve{B}}
(
7.28
) is Dirac structure.

In the latter case, the generator
\breve{{\cal G}}
becomes

\displaystyle\breve{\cal{G}}_{{\cal I}_{\breve{\theta}}(\breve{\Lambda})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\sigma\Big(\breve{\theta}^{\mu\nu}\breve{\lambda}_{\nu}(\pi_{\rho}{\cal C}^{\rho}_{\ \mu}+2\breve{B}_{\mu\rho}{\cal C}^{\rho}_{\ \sigma}\kappa x^{\prime\sigma})+2\breve{\lambda}_{\mu}({\cal C}^{\mu}_{\ \rho}\kappa x^{\prime\rho}+\kappa{\cal C}^{\mu}_{\ \rho}\breve{\theta}^{\rho\nu}\pi_{\nu})\Big)

(7.32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\sigma({\cal C}^{-1})^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\prime\nu}\breve{\lambda}_{\mu}\,.

However, since there is no dependence on momenta, the Poisson bracket algebra of this generator is zero. Hence, we obtain

[{\cal I}_{\breve{\theta}}(\Lambda_{1}),{\cal I}_{\breve{\theta}}(\Lambda_{2})]_{{\cal C}_{\theta B}}=0\,.

(7.33)

Again, we see this is a Dirac structure for all
\breve{\theta}
. This is a very interesting result - we were able to construct the Courant algebroid invariant under T-duality, that contains all generalized fluxes, such that they can exist on its Dirac structures without any constraints put on them.

8
Conclusion

The idea of this paper was to research and describe the family of Courant algebroids that are generated by the elements of the
O(D,D)
group, with the focus on Dirac structures for these algebroids. Every
O(D,D)
transformation acts as an isomorphism between the standard Courant bracket and the appropriate twisted Courant bracket. We showed how the twisted bracket, together with the
O(D,D)
invariant inner product and the anchor obtained from the composition of the inverse of the corresponding
O(D,D)
transformation and the projection on the tangent bundle defines the Courant algebroid, where all compatibility conditions are a priori satisfied.

The first twisted Courant algebroid considered was the one defined with the Courant bracket twisted by a 2-form
B
. This is the most well known example of a deformation of the Courant bracket, that results in additional term related to the
H
-flux. We demonstrated that the sub-bundle
{\cal I}_{B}
(
2.62
) is the Dirac structure regardless of whether the 2-form
B
is closed. On the other hand, the sub-bundle
{\cal I}_{\theta}
(
2.63
) is a Dirac structure only if the generalized
R
flux is zero.

The second example considered was the Courant algebroid with the
\theta
-twisted Courant bracket as its defining bracket. Again, we investigated the Dirac structures in the form
{\cal I}_{B}
and
{\cal I}_{\theta}
. In the former case we obtained that additional constraint on fluxes needs to be imposed in order for it to be Dirac structure. We showed that the latter case is the Dirac structure regardless whether the bi-vector
\theta
is Poisson. Moreover, we showed that the bracket on such Dirac structure reduces to the Courant bracket of two pure 1-forms and is therefore zero.

The subsequent example was the case of Courant algebroid defined with the Courant bracket twisted by
B
and then by
\theta
, also known as the Roytenberg bracket. Even though we saw that the twisting by
B
allowed for existence of arbitrary
H
-flux on Dirac structures, when that bracket is further twisted by
\theta
, another constraints on
H
flux on its Dirac structures appear.

The last example is the one of twisting the Courant bracket simultaneously by
B
and
\theta
. We showed that such a bracket features all generalized fluxes
{\cal\breve{H}},\ {\cal\breve{F}},\ {\cal\breve{Q}},\ {\cal\breve{R}}
, that are themselves functions of some effective fields
\breve{B}
and
\breve{\theta}
, which are both functions of both
B
and
\theta
. We were then looking for Dirac structures in the form
{\cal I}_{\breve{B}}
and
{\cal I}_{\breve{\theta}}
and showed that no conditions on the effective background fields is necessary for such sub-bundles to be Dirac structures.

There are a few differences in our approach, that we would like explicitly to outline.

Firstly, we use a different definition for the twisted Courant bracket. There are few terms that are widely circulating and having ambiguous meaning in physics as the term ”twisted”. The authors in
[
27
]
define a

R

B

R

3.9
) - the
\theta

-flux.

Secondly, the brackets that we obtained are by construction the brackets that define the Courant algebroids, as proven in the Chapter 3 of the paper. This is not the case for all brackets in
[
27

O(D,D)

\theta

The importance of the Dirac structures for physicist among others reflects in their interpretation as the D-branes in string theory. The D-branes represent the class of extended dynamical objects upon which open strings end with Dirichlet boundary conditions being imposed on its ends. They can be interpreted as leaves in a foliation, where the foliation deformations are interpreted as the scalar and vector field fluctuations of the D-branes. The transformations governed by these fluctuations is closed in the space of the Dirac structures
[
26
]
. As such, the results of this paper have a potential to further understand the fluxes on D-branes.

Abstract




ABSTRACT

In this paper, we first discuss cohomology and a one-parameter formal deformation theory of Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Next, we study finite group actions on Lie-Yamaguti algebras and introduce equivariant cohomology for Lie-Yamaguti algebras equipped with group actions. Finally, we study an equivariant one-parameter formal deformation theory and show that our equivariant cohomology is the suitable deformation cohomology.

Key words
: Group action; Lie-Yamaguti algebra; equivariant cohomology; formal deformation; rigidity.

Introduction

Lie triple systems arose initially in Cartan’s study of Riemannian geometry.
Jacobson
[
7
]
first introduced Lie triple systems and Jordan triple systems in connection with problems from Jordan theory
and quantum mechanics, viewing Lie triple systems as subspaces of Lie algebras that are closed relative to the ternary product.
Lie-Yamaguti algebras were introduced by Yamaguti in
[
13
]
to give an algebraic interpretation of the characteristic properties of the torsion and curvature of homogeneous spaces with canonical connection studied in
[
12
]
. He called them generalized Lie triple systems
at first, which were later called “Lie triple algebras”. Recently, they were renamed as “Lie-Yamaguti algebras”. The notion of Lie-Yamaguti algebra is a generalization of both Lie algebra and the Lie triple system. A Lie-Yamaguti algebra is a vector space together with a binary and ternary operation satisfying some compatibility conditions. In
[
15
]
, the authors studied
(2,3)
-cohomology groups and related deformation theory of Lie-Yamaguti algebras.

In this paper, we first introduce cohomology groups of all orders for Lie-Yamaguti algebra, and using this cohomology we study a one-parameter formal deformation theory. Our main aim of the present paper is to study Lie-Yamaguti algebras equipped with finite group actions. We define group actions on Lie-Yamaguti algebras following Bredon’s
[
1
]
idea for the topological
G
-spaces. We provide some examples of equivariant Lie-Yamaguti algebras. Next, we define equivariant cohomology for Lie-Yamaguti algebras. We also develop an equivariant formal deformation theory for Lie-Yamaguti algebras equipped with group actions and show that this equivariant deformation is controlled by our equivariant cohomology. We also obtain some classical deformation-related results following Gerstenhaber
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
deformation theory for associative algebras.

Theorem 2.10

[a,b]:=a\cdot b-b\cdot a,~~~\{a,b,c\}:=-(a\cdot b)\cdot c,~\text{for all}~a,b,c\in L.

Example 3.3
.

Consider a
2
-dimensional Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with a basis
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
defined by
[e_{1},e_{2}]=e_{1}
,
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{2}\}=e_{1}
and for a finite group
G=\{e,g\}
of order
2
, we define
\psi:G\times\mathfrak{g}\mapsto\mathfrak{g}
by
\psi(e,a)=a
for all
a\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\psi(g,a)=-a
for all
a\in\mathfrak{g}.
Here
G
acts on the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.

We use the notation
(G,L)
to denote a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
L
equipped with an action of
G
. Let
(G,L)
and
(G,L^{{}^{\prime}})
be two Lie-Yamaguti algebra equipped with action of
G
. A morphism between
(G,L)
and
(G,L^{{}^{\prime}})
is a morphism of Lie-Yamaguti algebra
\varphi:L\mapsto L^{{}^{\prime}}
which satisfies
\varphi(ga)=g\varphi(a)
for all
g\in G
and

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
L
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra. Then
G
acts on
L
if and only if there exists a group homomorphism

\phi:G\rightarrow Iso_{LYA}(L,L),g\mapsto\phi(g)

from the group
G
to the group of Lie-Yamaguti algebra isomorphisms from
L
to
L.

Proof.

For a given action
(L,G)
, define a map

\phi:G\rightarrow Iso_{LYA}(L,L),g\mapsto\phi(g)=\psi_{g}

Then clearly
\phi
is a group homomorphism. Conversely, for a given group homomorphism

\phi:G\rightarrow Iso_{LYA}(L,L),g\mapsto\phi(g)

define a map
G\times L\rightarrow L
by
(g,a)\mapsto\psi(g,a)=\phi(g)a.
Clearly, this is an action of
G
on the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
L.

∎

Let
L
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra equipped with an action of
G
and
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Then
H
-fixed point set is denoted by
L^{H}
and defined by

L^{H}=\{a\in L~|~ha=a,~\text{for all}~h\in H\}.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra equipped with an action of
G
. For every subgroup
H
of
G
,
L^{H}
is a Lie-Yamaguti sub-algebra of
L
.

Proof.

To prove
L^{H}
is a Lie-Yamaguti sub-algebra , it is enough to show that
L^{H}
is closed under
[\cdot,\cdot]~and~\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}
. Let
a,b,c\in L^{H}
and
h\in H
. Then

h[a,b]=[ha,hb]=[a,b]

and

h\{a,b,c\}=\{ha,hb,hc\}=\{a,b,c\}.

Therefore
[a,b]
and
\{a,b,c\}\in L^{H}
.
Thus,
L^{H}
is a Lie-Yamaguti sub-algebra.
∎

4
Equivariant cohomology of Lie-Yamaguti algebras

In this section, we introduce an equivariant cohomology of Lie-Yamaguti algebras equipped with finite group
actions.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a fixed finite group acting on a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
and
(V,\rho,D,\theta)
be a representation of
L
. Then the equivariant action of
G
on
V
is defined by a map
G\times V\mapsto V
satisfying the following conditions

1.

(e,v)\mapsto v
, for all
v\in V
, where
e
is the identity element of the group
G.

2.

g_{1}(g_{2}v)=(g_{1}g_{2})v
for all
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
and
v\in V.

3.

g(v_{1}+cv_{2})=gv_{1}+c(gv_{2})
for any
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
and for any scaler
c.

4.

\rho(gx)(v)=g(\rho(x)(g^{-1}v))

5.

D(gx,gy)(v)=g(D(x,y)(g^{-1}v))

6.

\theta(gx,gy)(v)=g(\theta(x,y)(g^{-1}v))

where
x,y,z\in L
and
v\in V.

In this case, we say that the Lie-Yamaguti algebra
L
is equipped with an equivariant action of a finite group
G
compatible with the representation of
L
by a vector space
V
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
L
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
G
be a group action on
L
. Consider the natural adjoint representation on itself where
\rho,D,\theta
are given by

\displaystyle\rho(x)(y):=[x,y],~~D(x,y)z:=\{x,y,z\},~~\theta(x,y)z:=\{z,x,y\},~for~~x,y,z\in L.

Now we can see that

\displaystyle\rho(gx)(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[gx,v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g[x,g^{-1}v]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\rho(x)(g^{-1}(v)).

Similarly,
D(gx,gy)(v)=g(D(x,y)(g^{-1}v))
and
\theta(gx,gy)(v)=g(\theta(x,y)(g^{-1}v))
holds for any
v\in L.
Thus,
L
is equipped with the action of
G
compatible with the adjoint representation.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra and
L
is equipped with an equivariant action of a finite group
G
compatible with a representation of
L
by a vector space
V
. We define

\displaystyle C_{G}^{n}(L,V)=\{f\in C^{n}(L,V):f(gx_{1},\ldots,gx_{i},\ldots,gx_{n})=gf(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i},\ldots,x_{n})\}

Clearly
C_{G}^{n}(L,V)
forms a subspace of
C^{n}(L,V)
. The elements of this space is called an equivariant
n
-cochain of
L
, for
n\geq 1
.

Lemma 4.4
.

For any
(f,h)\in C_{G}^{2n}(L,V)\times C_{G}^{2n+1}(L,V)
, the coboundary operator
\delta(f,h)=(\delta_{I}f,\delta_{II}h)\in C_{G}^{2n+2}(L,V)\times C_{G}^{2n+3}(L,V)
.

Proof.

Let
g
be an element of the group
G.
Now,

\displaystyle(\delta_{I}f)(gx_{1},gx_{2},\ldots,gx_{2n+2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho(gx_{2n+1})h(gx_{1},gx_{2},\ldots,gx_{2n},gx_{2n+2})-\rho(gx_{2n+2})h(gx_{1},gx_{2},\ldots,gx_{2n},gx_{2n+1})

\displaystyle-h(gx_{1},gx_{2},\ldots,gx_{2n},[gx_{2n+1},gx_{2n+2}])

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{n+k+1}D(gx_{2k-1},gx_{2k})f(gx_{1},\ldots,\widehat{gx}_{2k-1},\widehat{gx}_{2k},\ldots,gx_{2n+2})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=2k+1}^{2n+2}(-1)^{n+k}f(gx_{1},\ldots,\widehat{gx}_{2k-1},\widehat{gx}_{2k},\ldots,\{gx_{2k-1},gx_{2k},gx_{j}\},\ldots,gx_{2n+2}),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g[\rho(x_{2n+1})h(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{2n},x_{2n+2})]-g[\rho(x_{2n+2})h(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{2n},x_{2n+1})]

\displaystyle-g[h(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{2n},[x_{2n+1},x_{2n+2}])]

\displaystyle+g[\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{n+k+1}D(x_{2k-1},x_{2k})f(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{2k-1},\widehat{x}_{2k},\ldots,x_{2n+2})]

\displaystyle+g[\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sum_{j=2k+1}^{2n+2}(-1)^{n+k}f(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{2k-1},\widehat{x}_{2k},\ldots,\{x_{2k-1},x_{2k},x_{j}\},\ldots,x_{2n+2})],

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\delta_{I}f)(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{2n+2}).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(\delta_{II}h)(gx_{1},gx_{2},\ldots,gx_{2n+3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta(gx_{2n+2},gx_{2n+3})h(gx_{1},\ldots,gx_{2n+1})-\theta(gx_{2n+1},gx_{2n+3})h(gx_{1},\ldots,gx_{2n},gx_{2n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{n+k+1}D(gx_{2k-1},gx_{2k})h(gx_{1},\ldots,\widehat{gx}_{2k-1},\widehat{gx}_{2k},\ldots,gx_{2n+3})

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\sum_{j=2k+1}^{2n+3}(-1)^{n+k}h(gx_{1},\ldots,\widehat{gx}_{2k-1},\widehat{gx}_{2k},\ldots,\{gx_{2k-1},gx_{2k},gx_{j}\},\ldots,gx_{2n+3}).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g[\theta(x_{2n+2},x_{2n+3})h(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n+1})]-g[\theta(x_{2n+1},x_{2n+3})h(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n},x_{2n+1})]

\displaystyle+g[\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{n+k+1}D(x_{2k-1},x_{2k})h(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{2k-1},\widehat{x}_{2k},\ldots,x_{2n+3})]

\displaystyle+g[\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}\sum_{j=2k+1}^{2n+3}(-1)^{n+k}h(x_{1},\ldots,\widehat{x}_{2k-1},\widehat{x}_{2k},\ldots,\{x_{2k-1},x_{2k},x_{j}\},\ldots,x_{2n+3})].

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\delta_{II}h)(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{2n+3}).

Therefore, the coboundary operator
\delta(f,h)=(\delta_{I}f,\delta_{II}h)\in C_{G}^{2n+2}(L,V)\times C_{G}^{2n+3}(L,V)
.

∎

Let
Z_{G}^{2n}(L,V)\times Z_{G}^{2n+1}(L,V)
be the subspace of
C_{G}^{2n}(L,V)\times C_{G}^{2n+1}(L,V)
spanned by
(f,h)

such that
\delta(f,h)=0
which is called the space of equivariant cocycles and
B_{G}^{2n}(L,V)\times B_{G}^{2n+1}(L,V)=\delta(C_{G}^{2n-2}(L,V)\times C_{G}^{2n-1}(L,V))
which is called the space of equivariant coboundaries.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
G
be a fixed finite group, for the case
n\geq 2
, the
(2n,2n+1)
- equivariant cohomology group of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
with
coefficients in
V
is defined to be the quotient space:

\displaystyle H_{G}^{2n}(L,V)\times H_{G}^{2n+1}(L,V):=(Z_{G}^{2n}(L,V)\times Z^{2n+1}(L,V))/(B_{G}^{2n}(L,V)\times B_{G}^{2n+1}(L,V)).

Thus we have a sub-cochain complex of the cochain complex defined in Definition 2.5 whose cohomology groups are defined to be equivariant cohomology group.

5
Equivariant one-parameter formal deformations of Lie-Yamaguti algebras

In this section, we define an equivariant one-parameter formal deformations of Lie-Yamaguti algebras equipped with
an action of a finite group.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra equipped with an action of a finite group
G
compatible with a representation of
L
by a vector space
V
. Then,
a equivariant 1-parameter formal deformations of
L
is a pair of formal power series

(f_{t},g_{t})
of the form

\displaystyle f_{t}=[\cdot,\cdot]+\sum_{i\geq 1}f_{i}t^{i},~~~~g_{t}=\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}+\sum_{i\geq 1}g_{i}t^{i},

where each
f_{i}
is a
\mathbb{K}
-bilinear map
f_{i}:L\times L\rightarrow L
(extended to be
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-bilinear) and each
g_{i}
is a
\mathbb{K}
-trilinear map
g_{i}:L\times L\times L\rightarrow L
(extended to be
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-trilinear) such that

1.

(L[[t]],f_{t},g_{t})
is a Lie-Yamaguti algebra over
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
.

2.

f_{i}(ga,gb)=g(f_{i}(a,b))
and
g_{i}(ga,gb,gc)=g(g_{i}(a,b,c))
for all
a,b,c\in L
,
g\in G
and
i\geq 1
.

Note that in the above definition if we write
f_{0}=[\cdot,\cdot]
and
g_{0}=\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}
, then
f_{t}
and
g_{t}
can be written as
f_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}f_{i}t^{i}
and
g_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}g_{i}t^{i}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
(f_{t},h_{t})
be an equivariant 1-parameter formal deformation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
. Then
(f,h)\in C_{G}^{2}(L,L)\times C_{G}^{3}(L,L)
.

Proof.

From the deformation equations of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra we have
(f,g)\in C^{2}(L,L)\times C^{3}(L,L)
.
Again, from the definition of equivariant deformation of Lie-Yamaguti algebra we have

\displaystyle f_{n}(ga,gb)=g(f_{n}(a,b))

\displaystyle h_{n}(ga,gb,gc)=g(h_{n}(a,b,c)),

for all
a,b,c\in L
and for all
g\in G
.
Therefore,
(f,h)\in C_{G}^{2}(L,L)\times C_{G}^{3}(L,L)
.
∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(f_{t},h_{t})
be an equivariant 1-parameter formal deformation of a Lie-Yamaguti algebra
(L,[\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
. Then
(f_{1},h_{1})\in Z_{G}^{2}(L,L)\times Z_{G}^{3}(L,L)
. This
(f_{1},g_{1})
is called the infinitesimal equivariant deformation of
(f_{t},h_{t}).

Proof.

From Remarks 2.7 we have

\displaystyle(f_{1},g_{1})\in Z^{2}(L,L)\times Z^{3}(L,L).

Again, from the definition of equivariant deformation of Lie-Yamaguti algebra we have

\displaystyle f_{1}(ga,gb)=g(f_{1}(a,b))

\displaystyle h_{1}(ga,gb,gc)=g(h_{1}(a,b,c)),

for all
a,b,c\in L.

Therefore,
(f_{1},h_{1})\in Z_{G}^{2}(L,L)\times Z_{G}^{3}(L,L)
.

∎

Definition 5.4
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra.
Two equivariant 1-parameter formal deformations
(f_{t},g_{t})
and
(f^{\prime}_{t},g^{\prime}_{t})
of
L
are said to be equivalent, denoted by
(f_{t},g_{t})\sim(f^{\prime}_{t},g^{\prime}_{t})
,
if there exists a formal isomorphism of
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-modules

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(x)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\phi_{i}(x)t^{i}:(L[[t]],f_{t},g_{t})\rightarrow(L[[t]],f^{\prime}_{t},g^{\prime}(t)),

where
\phi_{i}:L\rightarrow L
is a
\mathbb{K}
-linear map (extended to be
\mathbb{K}[[t]]
-linear) such that

\displaystyle\phi_{0}=id_{L},\phi_{t}\circ f_{t}(x,y)=f^{\prime}_{t}(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y)),

\displaystyle\phi_{t}\circ g_{t}(x,y,z)=g^{\prime}_{t}(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y),\phi_{t}(z)),

\displaystyle\phi_{i}(gx)=g\phi_{i}(x)~~~\mbox{for all}~~~i\geq 0~~~\mbox{for all}~~g\in G.

In particular, if
(f_{1},g_{1})=(f_{2},g_{2})=\cdots=(0,0),
then
(f_{t},g_{t})=(f_{0},g_{0})
is called the null equivariant deformation.
If
(f_{t},g_{t})\sim(f_{0},g_{0})
, then
(f_{t},g_{t})
is called the trivial equivariant deformation.
If every equivariant 1-parameter formal deformation
(f_{t},g_{t})
is trivial, then
L
is called an analytically rigid equivariant Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
(f_{t},g_{t})
and
(f^{\prime}_{t},g^{\prime}_{t})
be
two equivalent equivariant 1-parameter formal deformations of
L
.
Then the infinitesimal deformations
(f_{1},g_{1})
and
(f^{\prime}_{1},g^{\prime}_{1})

belong to the same cohomology class in
H_{G}^{2}(L,L)\times H_{G}^{3}(L,L).

Proof.

From Theorem 2.9 we have
(f_{1}-f^{\prime}_{1},g_{1}-g^{\prime}_{1})=(\delta^{1}_{I},\delta^{1}_{II})(\phi_{1},\phi_{1})\in B^{2}(L,L)\times B^{3}(L,L),

and for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in L,g\in G

\displaystyle(f_{1}-f_{1}^{{}^{\prime}})(gx_{1},gx_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{1}([gx_{1},gx_{2}])-[\phi_{1}(gx_{1}),gx_{2}]-[gx_{1},\phi_{1}(gx_{2})],

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(g[x_{1},x_{2}])-[g\phi_{1}(x_{1}),gx_{2}]-[gx_{1},g\phi_{1}(x_{2})],

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\phi_{1}([x_{1},x_{2}])-[\phi_{1}(x_{1}),x_{2}]-[x_{1},\phi_{1}(x_{2})]),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(f_{1}-f_{1}^{{}^{\prime}})(x_{1},x_{2}).

Again from the proof of Theorem 2.9 for any
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in L,g\in G

\displaystyle(g_{1}-g_{1}^{\prime})(gx_{1},gx_{2},gx_{3})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(\{gx_{1},gx_{2},gx_{3}\})-\{\phi_{1}(gx_{1}),gx_{2},gx_{3}\}-\{gx_{1},\phi_{1}(gx_{2}),gx_{3}\}-\{gx_{1},gx_{2},\phi_{1}(gx_{3})\}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(g\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\})-\{g\phi_{1}(x_{1}),gx_{2},gx_{3}\}-\{gx_{1},g\phi_{1}(x_{2}),gx_{3}\}-\{gx_{1},gx_{2},g\phi_{1}(x_{3})\}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\phi_{1}(\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\})-\{\phi_{1}(x_{1}),x_{2},x_{3}\}-\{x_{1},\phi_{1}(x_{2}),x_{3}\}-\{x_{1},x_{2},\phi_{1}(x_{3})\}).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(g_{1}-g_{1}^{\prime})(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}).

Therefore
(f_{1}-f^{\prime}_{1},g_{1}-g^{\prime}_{1})\in B_{G}^{2}(L,L)\times B_{G}^{3}(L,L)
. This proves the theorem.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
(L,[\cdot,\cdot],\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\})
be a Lie-Yamaguti algebra with
H_{G}^{2}(L,L)\times H_{G}^{3}(L,L)=0
, then
L
is equivariantly rigid Lie-Yamaguti algebra.

Proof.

Let
(f_{t},g_{t})
be an equivariant 1-parameter formal deformation of
L
. Suppose
f_{t}=f_{0}+\sum_{i\geq r}f_{i}t^{i}
,
g_{t}=g_{0}+\sum_{i\geq r}g_{i}t^{i}
.
Then from the proof of Theorem
2.10
, we have

\displaystyle(f_{r},g_{r})\in Z^{2}(L,L)\times Z^{3}(L,L)=B^{2}(L,L)\times B^{3}(L,L).

Again, since
f_{r}(gx_{1},gx_{2})=g(f_{r}(x_{1},x_{2}))
and
(g_{r}(gx_{1},gx_{2},gx_{3}))=g(g_{r}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}))
,
for any
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in L
and
g\in G.
Hence

\displaystyle(f_{r},g_{r})\in Z_{G}^{2}(L,L)\times Z_{G}^{3}(L,L)=B_{G}^{2}(L,L)\times B_{G}^{3}(L,L).

Then there exists
h_{r}\in C_{G}^{1}(L,L)
such that
(f_{r},g_{r})=(\delta^{1}_{I}h_{r},\delta^{1}_{II}h_{r})
.

Consider
\phi_{t}=id_{L}-h_{r}t^{r},
then
\phi_{t}:L\rightarrow L
is a linear isomorphism
and for any
g\in G
and
x\in L
,

\displaystyle\phi_{t}(gx)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(id_{L}-h_{r}t^{r})(gx)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle gx-h_{r}(gx)t^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(id_{L}(x))-gh_{r}(x)t^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(\phi_{t}(x)).

Thus, we can define another equivariant 1-parameter formal deformation by
\phi_{t}^{-1}
in the form of

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{t}(x,y)=\phi_{t}^{-1}f_{t}(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y)),g^{\prime}_{t}(x,y,z)=\phi_{t}^{-1}g_{t}(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y),\phi_{t}(z)).

Set
f^{\prime}_{t}=\sum_{i\geq 0}f^{\prime}_{i}t^{i}
and use the fact
\phi_{t}f^{\prime}_{t}(x,y)=f_{t}(\phi_{t}(x),\phi_{t}(y))
, then we have

\displaystyle(id_{L}-h_{r}t^{r})\sum_{i\geq 0}f^{\prime}_{i}(x,y)t^{i}=(f_{0}+\sum_{i\geq 0}f_{i}t^{i})(x-h_{r}(x)t^{r},y-h_{r}(y)t^{r}),

that is,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 0}f^{\prime}_{i}(x,y)t^{i}-\sum_{i\geq 0}h_{r}\circ f^{\prime}_{i}(x,y)t^{i+r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{0}(x,y)-f_{0}(h_{r}(x),y)t^{r}-f_{0}(x,h_{r}(y))t^{r}+f_{0}(h_{r}(x),h_{r}(y))t^{2r}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i\geq r}f_{i}(x,y)t^{i}-\sum_{i\geq r}\{f_{i}(h_{r}(x),y)-f_{i}(x,h_{r}(y))\}t^{i+r}+\sum_{i\geq r}f_{i}(h_{r}(x),h_{r}(y))t^{i+2r}.

By the above equation, it follows that

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{0}(x,y)=f_{0}(x,y)=[x,y],

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{1}(x,y)=f^{\prime}_{1}(x,y)=\cdots=f^{\prime}_{r-1}(x,y)=0,

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{r}(x,y)-h_{r}([x,y])=f_{r}(x,y)-[h_{r}(x),y]-[x,h_{r}(y)].

Therefore, we deduce

\displaystyle f^{\prime}_{r}(x,y)=f_{r}(x,y)-\delta^{1}_{I}h_{r}(x,y)=0.

It follows that
f^{\prime}_{t}=[\cdot,\cdot]+\sum_{i\geq r+1}f^{\prime}_{i}t^{i}
. Similarly, we have
g^{\prime}_{t}=\{\cdot,\cdot,\cdot\}+\sum_{i\geq r+1}g^{\prime}_{i}t^{i}

By induction, we have
(f_{t},g_{t})\sim(f_{0},g_{0})
, that is,
L
is equivariantly rigid Lie-Yamaguti algebra.
∎

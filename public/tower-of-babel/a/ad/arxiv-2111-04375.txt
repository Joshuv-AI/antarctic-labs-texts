Abstract
. We show that spin systems with generic (ferro- or paramagnetic, or random) interactions are ”completely integrable”. The approach is worked out, by way of example, for the Sherrington-Kirkpatrick model: we derive an exact formula for the quenched free energy in finite volume which involves an integral over a Gaussian field with correlation structure given by the interaction matrix (with a twist).



The Sherrington-Kirkpatrick (SK) model
[
12
]
for mean field spin glasses is constructed as follows: for
N\in\mathbb{N}
, consider centered Gaussians
(g_{ij})_{1\leq i<j\leq N}
issued on some probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. These Gaussians,
the disorder
, are assumed to be all independent and with variance
1/N
. The Ising configuration space
\Sigma_{N}\equiv\{\pm 1\}^{N}
is endowed with coin tossing measure,
P_{o}(\sigma)\equiv 2^{-N}
for
\sigma\in\Sigma_{N}
. We denote by
E_{o}
expectation under
P_{o}
.

The SK-Hamiltonian is

H_{N}(\sigma)\equiv\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq N}g_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}g_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\,,

(1)

with symmetrised random interaction matrix
{\bf G}\equiv(g_{ij})
, where
g_{ij}=g_{ji}
, and
g_{ii}=0
.

The quenched SK-free energy to inverse temperature
\beta>0
and external field
h\in\mathbb{R}
is

Nf_{N}(\beta,h)\equiv\log E_{o}\exp\left(\beta H_{N}(\sigma)+h\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sigma_{i}\right)\,.

(2)

Theorem.

Let
{\sf X}=({\sf X}_{i})_{i=1}^{N}
be a centered Gaussian random field with covariance

{\sf{E}}{\sf X}_{i}{\sf X}_{j}=\begin{cases}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left|g_{ik}\right|\,,&i=j,\\
g_{ij}\,,&i\neq j.\end{cases}

(3)

Then the exact formula for the quenched, finite volume SK-free energy holds:

Nf_{N}(\beta,h)=\log{\sf{E}}\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\cosh\left(h+\sqrt{\beta}{\sf X}_{i}\right)\right)-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}|g_{ij}|\,.

(4)

A minor yet key modification of the diagonal therefore turns the Gaussian disorder matrix
\bf G
into the covariance of yet another Gaussian field.

Before giving the elementary proof of the theorem, some further comments are in order.

First we stress that the treatment also yields exact formulas for spin magnetizations, (higher order) correlations etc. under the quenched Gibbs measure.

We furthermore note that, albeit quenched, formula (
4
) is reminiscent of expressions obtained through replica computations before breaking of the symmetries
[
9
]
. What is perhaps even more surprising, the approach we implement here relies on a positivity principle combined with a Gaussian integral to decouple the two-body interaction in order to perform the trace over the Ising spins: this is particularly intriguing since the mathematically elusive ”replica trick”
[
8
,
12
]
relies on the very same steps,
but executed in different order
, to achieve the very same goal.

We emphasize that neither the Ising nature of the spins, nor the Gaussian character of the disorder play any structural role in the derivation. The method allows,
mutatis mutandis
, to derive exact formulas for the Hopfield

or, incidentally, also classical systems such as the Ising model on
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
for
d\geq 1

The case of the EA-model is particularly interesting, and relevant. The model is to these days deemed intractable, also from the standpoint of theoretical physics: indeed, even numerical simulations have been frighteningly inconclusive so far. Now, a minor variation of formula (

It thus follows that all physically relevant quantities can be efficiently simulated via polynomially many Gaussian random variables only. There is reason to believe that this dramatic reduction of the complexity will help to settle the long-standing debate between the droplet picture of Fisher and Huse
[

The decoupling is straightforward also in models where the underlying measure (
P_{o}

[

for magnetism (
P_{o}\to

[

for weakly self avoiding walks (
P_{o}\to

Given the amount of similarity among the ”Babylonian formulas” for the models listed above, it is somewhat puzzling how different choices of the covariance matrix in the underlying Gaussian fields lead to drastically different types of phase transitions. This, if anything, suggests that a rigorous asymptotic evaluation of the Gaussian integrals may not be a simple matter, in general. One may also expect that different types of covariance structure require radically different tools: when looked through the lenses of the Gaussian representations, classical models (such as Ising) appear as a branch of algebraic spectral theory (the covariance is given by adjacency matrices modified on the diagonal); mean field models (such as SK) are strongly tied to correlated Wigner matrices, whereas realistic spin glasses (such as EA) somehow link with
random
algebraic geometry.
These concerns seem however premature: after all, even basic features of the (random) matrices as in (
3
) have not yet been fully addressed, let alone properties of the associated Gaussian fields.

Finally, the Babylonian trick stems from an effort to deal directly, and decidedly, with the issue of frustration, ever so substantial yet unwieldy in disordered systems.

Proof of the theorem.

Some conventions: since the parameter
N
is fixed throughout, we will mostly omit it from our notations. In particular we write
\sum_{i}a_{i}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{N}a_{i}
and
\sum_{ij}a_{ij}\equiv\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a_{ij}
for double sums. We shorten

g_{ij}^{+}\equiv\max\left\{g_{ij},0\right\},\qquad g_{ij}^{-}\equiv\max\left\{-g_{ij},0\right\}\,,

(5)

in which case

i)

both
g^{+}
and
g^{-}
are positive:
g_{ij}^{+}\geq 0
and
g_{ij}^{-}\geq 0
;

ii)

the decomposition holds true:
g_{ij}=g_{ij}^{+}-g_{ij}^{-}
;

iii)

similarly, it holds:
\left|g_{ij}\right|=g_{ij}^{+}+g_{ij}^{-}
.

We first split the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{ii)}}}{{=}}\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}^{+}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}+\sum_{i,j}\left(-g_{ij}^{-}\right)\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}

(6)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}^{+}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}+\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}^{-}\left(-\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\right)\,.

The
Babylonian trick
amounts to writing

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{i}+\sigma_{j})^{2}-1,\qquad-\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\frac{1}{2}(\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{j})^{2}-1,

(7)

(also using the simplification
\sigma_{i}^{2}=1
, valid for Ising spins). This implies

\eqref{split}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}^{+}(\sigma_{i}+\sigma_{j})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}^{-}(\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{j})^{2}+\boldsymbol{g}\,,

(8)

where the
\sigma
-independent term reads

{\boldsymbol{g}}\equiv-\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}^{+}-\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}^{-}=-\sum_{i,j}\left(g_{ij}^{+}+g_{ij}^{-}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{iii)}}}{{=}}-\sum_{i,j}|g_{ij}|\,.

(9)

The quenched free energy is thus

\displaystyle

\displaystyle Nf_{N}(\beta,h)=\log E_{o}\exp\left(\frac{\beta}{4}\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}^{+}(\sigma_{i}+\sigma_{j})^{2}+\frac{\beta}{4}\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}^{-}(\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{j})^{2}+h\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}\right)+\frac{\beta}{2}{\boldsymbol{g}}\,.

(10)

We henceforth focus on the quenched partition function

Z_{N}(\beta,h)\equiv E_{o}\exp\left(\frac{\beta}{4}\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}^{+}(\sigma_{i}+\sigma_{j})^{2}+\frac{\beta}{4}\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}^{-}(\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{j})^{2}+h\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}\right)\,.

(11)

The double sums in the exponential consist of
positive
terms: we can thus apply the Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation
[
7
]
. To do so we introduce
(\tilde{X}_{ij}),(\tilde{Y}_{ij})
standard Gaussians, all independent, issued on some probability space
(\Omega^{\prime},\mathcal{F}^{\prime},{\sf P})
. We denote by
{\sf{E}}
their joint expectation, and shorten

{\sf X}_{ij}\equiv\tilde{X}_{ij}\sqrt{g_{ij}^{+}},\qquad{\sf Y}_{ij}\equiv\tilde{Y}_{ij}\sqrt{g_{ij}^{-}}\,.

(12)

We stress that the disorder matrix
{\bf G}
is quenched. In particular,
g_{ij}
,
g_{ij}^{+}
and
g_{ij}^{-}
are all constant. Furthermore, the matrices
{\bf G}^{+}\equiv\left(g_{ij}^{+}\right),{\bf G}^{-}\equiv\left(g_{ij}^{-}\right)
are by construction symmetric, but the
{\sf X}\equiv({\sf X}_{ij}),{\sf Y}\equiv({\sf Y}_{ij})
are
not
. Performing a Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation, and interchanging the order of integration yields

Z_{N}(\beta,h)\equiv{\sf{E}}E_{o}\exp\left(\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{2}}\sum_{i,j}{\sf X}_{ij}(\sigma_{i}+\sigma_{j})+\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{2}}\sum_{i,j}{\sf Y}_{ij}(\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{j})+h\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}\right)\,.

(13)

We write the double sums in (
13
) as

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}{\sf X}_{ij}(\sigma_{i}+\sigma_{j})+\sum_{i,j}{\sf Y}_{ij}(\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{j})=\sum_{i}\left({\sf X}_{i\bullet}+{\sf X}_{\bullet i}+{\sf Y}_{i\bullet}-{\sf Y}_{\bullet i}\right)\sigma_{i}\,,

(14)

with the shorthand notation
A_{i\bullet}\equiv\sum_{j}a_{ij}
and
A_{\bullet j}\equiv\sum_{i}a_{ij}
, which we use for any
N\times N
matrix
A=(a_{ij})
. Using (
14
) in (
13
), and integrating out the Ising spins yields

\displaystyle Z_{N}(\beta,h)={\sf{E}}\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\cosh\left(h+\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{2}}\left({\sf X}_{i\bullet}+{\sf X}_{\bullet i}+{\sf Y}_{i\bullet}-{\sf Y}_{\bullet i}\right)\right)\right)\,.

(15)

This is, in essence, the claim of the theorem, the rest being only a (co)variance check.

The involved random variables are by definition centered:

{\sf{E}}\left[{\sf X}_{i\bullet}+{\sf X}_{\bullet i}\right]=0\,,\,\qquad{\sf{E}}\left[{\sf Y}_{i\bullet}-{\sf Y}_{\bullet i}\right]=0\,.

(16)

Furthermore,
{\sf X}
and
{\sf Y}
are independent, hence

{\sf var}\left[\left({\sf X}_{i\bullet}+{\sf X}_{\bullet i}\right)+\left({\sf Y}_{i\bullet}-{\sf Y}_{\bullet i}\right)\right]={\sf var}\left[{\sf X}_{i\bullet}+{\sf X}_{\bullet i}\right]+{\sf var}\left[{\sf Y}_{i\bullet}-{\sf Y}_{\bullet i}\right]\,,

(17)

By (
16
), the variance equals the second moment: writing out the
\sf X
-contribution we get

\displaystyle{\sf var}\left[{\sf X}_{i\bullet}+{\sf X}_{\bullet i}\right]=\sum_{j,k}\left({\sf{E}}{\sf X}_{ij}{\sf X}_{ik}+{\sf{E}}{\sf X}_{ij}{\sf X}_{ki}+{\sf{E}}{\sf X}_{ji}{\sf X}_{ik}+{\sf{E}}{\sf X}_{ji}{\sf X}_{ki}\right)\,.

(18)

The middle terms vanish (recall also that
g_{ii}=0
, hence
{\sf X}_{ii}=0
), whereas first and last are
\neq 0
only if
k=j
. Using furthermore that
{\sf{E}}{\sf X}_{ij}^{2}=g_{ij}^{+}=g_{ji}^{+}={\sf{E}}{\sf X}_{ji}^{2}
(symmetry of
{\bf G}^{+}
), we get

{\sf var}\left[{\sf X}_{i\bullet}+{\sf X}_{\bullet i}\right]=2\sum_{j}{\sf{E}}\left[{\sf X}_{ij}^{2}\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{sho}}}{{=}}2\sum_{j}{\sf{E}}\left[\left(\sqrt{g_{ij}^{+}}\tilde{X}_{ij}\right)^{2}\right]=2\sum_{j}g_{ij}^{+}=2g_{i\bullet}^{+}\,.

(19)

The computation of the second term on the r.h.s. of (
17
) is just as straightforward: also using, this time, that
{\sf{E}}{\sf Y}_{ij}^{2}=g_{ij}^{-}=g_{ji}^{-}={\sf{E}}{\sf Y}_{ji}^{2}
(symmetry of
{\bf G}^{-}
), one steadily checks that

{\sf var}\left[{\sf Y}_{i\bullet}-{\sf Y}_{\bullet i}\right]={\sf{E}}\left[\left({\sf Y}_{i\bullet}-{\sf Y}_{\bullet i}\right)^{2}\right]=2\sum_{j}g_{ij}^{-}=2g_{i\bullet}^{-}\,.

(20)

Plugging (
19
) and (
20
) in (
17
) therefore yields

{\sf var}\left[\left({\sf X}_{i\bullet}+{\sf X}_{\bullet i}\right)+\left({\sf Y}_{i\bullet}-{\sf Y}_{\bullet i}\right)\right]=2\left(g_{i\bullet}^{+}+g_{i\bullet}^{-}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{iii)}}}{{=}}2\sum_{k}\left|g_{ik}\right|\,.

(21)

We next compute, for
i\neq j
, the covariance:

\displaystyle{\sf C}_{ij}

\displaystyle\equiv{\sf{E}}\left\{\left({\sf X}_{i\bullet}+{\sf X}_{\bullet i}\right)+\left({\sf Y}_{i\bullet}-{\sf Y}_{\bullet i}\right)\right\}\left\{\left({\sf X}_{j\bullet}+{\sf X}_{\bullet j}\right)+\left({\sf Y}_{j\bullet}-{\sf Y}_{\bullet j}\right)\right\}

(22)

\displaystyle={{\sf{E}}\left[\left({\sf X}_{i\bullet}+{\sf X}_{\bullet i}\right)\left({\sf X}_{j\bullet}+{\sf X}_{\bullet j}\right)\right]}+{{\sf{E}}\left[\left({\sf Y}_{i\bullet}-{\sf Y}_{\bullet i}\right)\left({\sf Y}_{j\bullet}-{\sf Y}_{\bullet j}\right)\right]}\,,

again since
\sf X
and
\sf Y
are independent (and centered). The
\sf X
-contribution reads

\displaystyle

\displaystyle{\sf{\displaystyle E}}\left[\left({\sf X}_{i\bullet}+{\sf X}_{\bullet i}\right)\left({\sf X}_{j\bullet}+{\sf X}_{\bullet j}\right)\right]={\sf{E}}\left[{\sf X}_{i\bullet}{\sf X}_{j\bullet}\right]+{\sf{E}}\left[{\sf X}_{i\bullet}{\sf X}_{\bullet j}\right]+{\sf{E}}\left[{\sf X}_{\bullet i}{\sf X}_{j\bullet}\right]+{\sf{E}}\left[{\sf X}_{\bullet i}{\sf X}_{\bullet j}\right].

(23)

First and last term on the r.h.s. of (
23
) vanish since for
i\neq j
the involved random variables are independent and centered, whereas second and third terms read, respectively,

\displaystyle

\displaystyle{\sf{\displaystyle E}}\left[{\sf X}_{i\bullet}{\sf X}_{\bullet j}\right]=\sum_{k,l}{\sf{E}}\left[{\sf X}_{ik}{\sf X}_{lj}\right]=\sum_{k,l}1_{k=j,l=i}{\sf{E}}\left[{\sf X}_{ik}{\sf X}_{lj}\right]={\sf{E}}\left[{\sf X}_{ij}^{2}\right]=g_{ij}^{+},

(24)

\displaystyle{\sf{\displaystyle E}}\left[{\sf X}_{\bullet i}{\sf X}_{j\bullet}\right]=\sum_{k,l}{\sf{E}}\left[{\sf X}_{ki}{\sf X}_{jl}\right]=\sum_{k,l}1_{k=j,l=i}{\sf{E}}\left[{\sf X}_{ki}{\sf X}_{jl}\right]=g_{ji}^{+}=g_{ij}^{+},

again by independence, and symmetry in the last step. Using all this in (
23
) yields

{\sf{E}}\left[\left({\sf X}_{i\bullet}+{\sf X}_{\bullet i}\right)\left({\sf X}_{j\bullet}+{\sf X}_{\bullet j}\right)\right]=2g_{ij}^{+}\,.

(25)

The computation of the
{\sf Y}
-contribution in (
22
) is fully analogous: the upshot reads

{\sf{E}}\left[\left({\sf Y}_{i\bullet}-{\sf Y}_{\bullet i}\right)\left({\sf Y}_{j\bullet}-{\sf Y}_{\bullet j}\right)\right]=-2g_{ij}^{-}\,.

(26)

Combining (
25
) and (
26
) yields, for
i\neq j
,

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\sf{\displaystyle C}_{ij}=2g_{ij}^{+}-2g_{ij}^{-}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{ii)}}}{{=}}2g_{ij}.

(27)

Using (
21
) and (
27
) in (
15
), together with (
10
), settles the claim of the theorem.

∎

Remark.

The treatment works also for generic
p
-spin interactions. Consider e.g. the mean field 3-spins (Ising) Hamiltonian

H_{N,3}(\sigma)\equiv\sum_{1\leq i<j<k\leq N}g_{ijk}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k},

(28)

with, say, random couplings: one uses the splitting
ii)
and the Babylonian trick

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sigma_{i}+\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}\right)^{2}-1,\qquad-\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sigma_{i}-\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}\right)^{2}-1\,.

(29)

followed by Hubbard-Stratonovich transformation. This leads to a non-interacting Hamiltonian (”
\sigma_{i}
”), and a two body interaction (”
\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}
”), but one simply repeats the procedure on the latter, iterating until full decoupling is reached.

As a matter of fact, exact expressions for nonlinear models such as the perceptron are also possible: one Taylor expands the activation function, and applies the above procedure to the Taylor terms, one by one.

points out that in case of two body interactions such as the SK-model, the Gaussian representation (

) is not in the least unique. The rationale is extremely charming, and best laid out by ”reversed engineering”: consider a centered Gaussian field
\{{\sf Z}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
with
{\sf{E}}{\sf Z}_{i}{\sf Z}_{j}=g_{ij},i\neq j
; the diagonal will be specified
a posteriori
. Undoing the
\log\cosh
, interchanging the order of integration, and by Gaussian integration we get

\displaystyle{\sf{E}}\exp\sum_{i}\log\cosh\left(h+\sqrt{\beta}{\sf Z}_{i}\right)

\displaystyle={\sf{E}}E_{o}\exp\left(h\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}+\sqrt{\beta}\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}{\sf Z}_{i}\right)

(30)

\displaystyle=E_{o}\exp\left(h\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}+\frac{\beta}{2}\text{\sf var}\Big[\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}{\sf Z}_{i}\Big]\right)\,.

By definition of the
\sf Z
-field, and using that
\sigma_{i}^{2}=1
, it holds

\text{\sf var}\left[\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}{\sf Z}_{i}\right]={\sf E}\left[\sum_{i,j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}{\sf Z}_{i}{\sf Z}_{j}\right]=\sum_{i\neq j}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}g_{ij}+\sum_{i}{\sf{E}}{\sf Z}_{i}^{2}\,.

(31)

Plugging this in (
30
) yields

\displaystyle{\sf{\displaystyle E}}\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\cosh\left(h+\sqrt{\beta}{\sf Z}_{i}\right)\right)=

(32)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{i}{\sf{E}}{\sf Z}_{i}^{2}\right)\times E_{o}\exp\left(h\sum_{i}\sigma_{i}+\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{i\neq j}g_{ij}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\right)\,.

The rightmost term above is the quenched partition function. Rearranging, this leads to the following representation of the quenched free energy for the SK-model:

Nf_{N}(\beta,h)=\log{\sf{E}}\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\log\cosh\left(h+\sqrt{\beta}{\sf Z}_{i}\right)\right)-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\sf{E}}{\sf Z}_{i}^{2}\,.

(33)

There is thus has a great deal of freedom for the specification of the
{\sf Z}
-field: the only requirement is that the covariance matrix be positive semi-definite. Under this light, a most natural choice is a covariance given by
{\bf G}-\lambda_{1}\mathbf{1}
, with
\lambda_{1}
the smallest eigenvalue of the (Wigner) matrix
\bf G
. Consequences of this point of view will be worked out elsewhere.

Diffeological Principal Bundles and Principal Infinity Bundles
Emilio Minichiello

Abstract
. In this paper, we study diffeological spaces as certain kinds of discrete simplicial presheaves on the site of cartesian spaces with the coverage of good open covers. The Čech model structure on simplicial presheaves provides us with a notion of ∞ \infty -stack cohomology of a diffeological space with values in a diffeological abelian group A A . We compare ∞ \infty -stack cohomology of diffeological spaces with two existing notions of Čech cohomology for diffeological spaces in the literature [ KWW21 ] [ Igl20 ] . Finally, we prove that for a diffeological group G G , that the nerve of the category of diffeological principal G G -bundles is weak homotopy equivalent to the nerve of the category of G G -principal ∞ \infty -bundles on X X , bridging the bundle theory of diffeology and higher topos theory.



1.
Introduction

Principal
G
-bundles and Čech cohomology are important tools in the study of smooth manifolds. However, in recent years, the desire to expand the typical objects of study in differential geometry has led to various frameworks in which one can define a “generalized smooth space.” In this paper, we construct a bridge between two such frameworks. One of them is diffeology, as popularized in the textbook
[
Igl13
]
. A diffeological space consists of a set
X
, and a set
\mathscr{D}_{X}
of functions
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to X
, for varying
n\geq 0
, satisfying three simple conditions. While the definition of a diffeological space is simple, a large number of interesting spaces outside the purview of classical differential geometry can be given a diffeology. Every finite dimensional smooth manifold inherits a canonical diffeology, as does the set
C^{\infty}(X,Y)
of smooth maps between any two diffeological spaces. In fact the category of diffeological spaces is complete, cocomplete and cartesian closed. More precisely it is a quasi-topos
[
BH11
]
. This is of course not the case for the category of finite dimensional smooth manifolds, and thus provides a “better” category in which to work. Various important constructions in classical differential geometry have been defined for diffeological spaces, like differential forms, deRham cohomology, fiber bundles, tangent spaces
[
CW16
]
, and recently Čech cohomology
[
Igl20
]

[
KWW21
]

[
Ahm23
]
.

The second framework is higher topos theory. Here the objects of interest are
\infty
-stacks over the site
\mathsf{Cart}
of cartesian spaces, or more colloquially known as smooth
\infty
-groupoids. Many constructions of classical differential geometry can be extended to smooth
\infty
-groupoids, with interesting applications. One such extension is the notion of a principal
\infty
-bundle, as defined in
[
NSS14
]
and
[
NSS14a
]
. Classical principal bundles and non-abelian bundle gerbes are particular examples of principal
\infty
-bundles. Principal
\infty
-bundles allow for a robust framework wherein one can study twisted, equivariant or differential refinements of generalized cohomology theories. For more on this theory we recommend the texts
[
FSS+12
]
,
[
ADH21
]
,
[
Sch13
]
,
[
BNV16
]
. In this paper, we will use the presentation of this theory by simplicial presheaves. Thus, the reader does not need to be comfortable with the language of
\infty
-categories in order to read this paper.

The following theorem is the main result of this paper, which shows that when
G
is a diffeological group, then homotopically speaking, the notion of diffeological principal
G
-bundle over a diffeological space
X
and the notion of
G
-principal
\infty
-bundle over
X
coincide.

Theorem
6.8
.

Given a diffeological space
X
and a diffeological group
G
, there is a weak homotopy equivalence,

N\mathsf{Prin}_{G}^{\infty}(X)\simeq N\mathsf{DiffPrin}_{G}(X).

where
N\mathsf{DiffPrin}_{G}(X)
is the nerve of the category of diffeological principal
G
-bundles on
X
and
N\mathsf{Prin}_{G}^{\infty}(X)
is the nerve of the category of
G
-principal
\infty
-bundles on
X
.

Some explanations are in order. Baez-Hoffnung proved in
[
BH11
]
that the category of diffeological spaces is equivalent to the category
\mathsf{ConSh}(\mathsf{Open})
of concrete sheaves on
\mathsf{Open}
, the category of open subsets of cartesian spaces and smooth maps, equipped with the coverage of open covers. This means that diffeological spaces can be thought of as certain kinds of sheaves. This is a very powerful point of view, especially with respect to studying the homotopy theory of diffeological spaces
[
Pav22a
]
. In particular, the category of concrete sheaves on
\mathsf{Cart}
embeds fully faithfully into the category
\mathsf{sPre}(\mathsf{Cart})
of simplicial presheaves on
\mathsf{Cart}
, whose objects are functors
X:\mathsf{Cart}^{op}\to\mathsf{sSet}
. Just as some presheaves of sets are sheaves, some simplicial presheaves are
\infty
-stacks. Higher topos theory can also be called homotopical sheaf theory, and
\infty
-stacks are the main objects of study. Sheaves of sets are discrete
\infty
-stacks, and stacks of groupoids are
1
-truncated
\infty
-stacks, see Section
5
for a more detailed discussion.

Embedding the category of diffeological spaces into the category of simplicial presheaves puts diffeological spaces into a homotopical framework that is both easy to work with and connects powerfully with the underlying homotopy theory of simplicial sets. For instance, using this embedding, we can immediately define a notion of cohomology on diffeological spaces, which we call
\infty
-stack cohomology. If
X
is a diffeological space, and
A
is an
\infty
-stack, whose
k
-fold delooping
\overline{W}^{k}A
exists (notions we will explain in Section
5
), then the
\infty
-stack cohomology of
X
with coefficients in
A
is given by the connected components of the derived mapping space

\check{H}^{k}_{\infty}(X,A)=\pi_{0}\mathbb{R}\text{Hom}(X,\overline{W}^{k}A)=\pi_{0}\underline{\mathsf{sPre}(\mathsf{Cart})}(QX,\overline{W}^{k}A),

where
Q
is a cofibrant replacement functor for the Čech model structure. This functor
Q
has a simple and explicit description in terms of the plots of
X
. In fact, it is the nerve of a diffeological category, see Proposition
5.20
. In Corollary
5.36
we construct an explicit cochain complex whose cohomology gives the
\infty
-stack cohomology of
X
with values in
A
when
A
is a diffeological abelian group.

There are two other examples of diffeological categories of diffeological spaces that act as “resolutions” that already exist in the literature, namely the Čech groupoid
\check{C}(X)
of
[
KWW21
]
and the gauge monoid
B//M
of
[
Igl20
]
. These resolutions are used to define different notions of Čech cohomology for diffeological spaces, the Krepski-Watts-Wollbert diffeological Čech cohomology
\check{H}^{k}_{KWW}
and the PIZ diffeological Čech cohomology
\check{H}^{k}_{PIZ}
respectively. In Section
5
we compare these various notions of diffeological Čech cohomologies and find that

\check{H}^{0}_{\infty}(X,A)\cong\check{H}^{0}_{KWW}(X,A)\cong\check{H}^{0}_{PIZ}(X,A),\qquad\check{H}^{1}_{\infty}(X,A)\cong\check{H}^{1}_{KWW}(X,A).

A full summary of known results about these cohomology theories is given after (
46
). It is currently unknown if all three of these cohomology theories are isomorphic in all degrees.

Now if
G
is a diffeological group, there exists an
\infty
-stack called
\mathbf{B}G
, see Example
5.13
, and
\infty
-stack cohomology of a diffeological space
X
with values in
\mathbf{B}G
is denoted
\check{H}^{1}_{\infty}(X,G)
. An immediate consequence of Theorem
6.8
is the following result.

Corollary
6.9
.

Given a diffeological space
X
and a diffeological group
G
, there is an isomorphism of pointed sets

\check{H}^{1}_{\infty}(X,G)\cong\pi_{0}\mathsf{DiffPrin}_{G}(X),

where
\pi_{0}\mathsf{DiffPrin}_{G}(X)
denotes the set of isomorphism classes of diffeological principal
G
-bundles on
X
, pointed at the isomorphism class of trivial bundles.

Thus
\infty
-stack cohomology of diffeological spaces also encompasses nonabelian cohomology. In the course of proving Theorem
6.8
we will prove a bundle construction-type theorem, Theorem
3.15
, which gives an equivalence between a category of plotwise defined cocycles on
X
and the category of diffeological principal
G
-bundles over
X
. This result may be of independent interest, and thus we prove it in Section
3
, before we need to introduce any abstract machinery.

It is our view that by applying tools from higher topos theory can be beneficial to the still young subject of diffeological spaces. In particular we believe that while the machinery of
\infty
-stack cohomology may come from an abstract framework, it can ultimately output important and down-to-earth results. Furthermore, higher topos theory already has definitions for higher principal bundles (called bundle gerbes) and connections on such objects inherently built into it. Pulling these definitions over to diffeological spaces and analyzing the results are the subject of future work.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we will give some background information about diffeological spaces. In Section
3
we turn to diffeological principal
G
-bundles. We define
G
-cocycles and prove a bundle construction-type theorem, Theorem

. In Section

. In Appendix

, we compare several categories of concrete sheaves on various sites, and show that they are all equivalent, proving that the category of diffeological spaces as given in Definition

is equivalent to the usual category of diffeological spaces considered in the literature. In Section

, we will review the Čech model structure on simplicial presheaves over cartesian spaces. Proposition

provides a cofibrant replacement of a diffeological space as the nerve of a diffeological category. We compare this diffeological category to two other diffeological categories
\check{C}(X)
and
B//M
, which have been introduced in
[
KWW21
]
and
[
Igl20
]
, respectively. From these three diffeological categories, we obtain three separate notions of Čech cohomology for diffeological spaces, and compare them in Section
5.3
. In Section

, we turn to the main result of this paper, that if
G
is a diffeological group and
X
is a diffeological space, then the nerve of the category of principal

-bundles on

is weak homotopy equivalent to the nerve of the category of
G
-principal
\infty
-bundles over

.

Remark 2.3

In what follows, when we say that

Definition 3.1

A

Remark 3.9

In what follows we will not use the notation

\displaystyle=[x_{p_{0}},g^{\prime}_{f_{0}}(x_{p_{1}})h_{p_{1}}(x_{p_{1}})k_{1}]

\displaystyle=[x_{p_{1}},h_{p_{1}}(x_{p_{1}})k_{1}]

\displaystyle=\widetilde{h}([x_{p_{1}},k_{1}]),

where the third equality follows from (
7
). Thus if
(x_{p_{1}},k_{1})
and
(x_{p_{0}},k_{0})
are connected by an arbitrary finite zig-zag, then using the above argument on every morphism in the zig-zag shows that
\widetilde{h}([x_{p_{1}},k_{1}])=\widetilde{h}([x_{p_{0}},k_{0}])
. Thus
\widetilde{h}
is well-defined, and clearly smooth. It is also
G
-equivariant, as

(14)

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}([x_{p_{0}},k_{0}]\cdot g)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{h}([x_{p_{0}},k_{0}g])

\displaystyle=[x_{p_{0}},h_{p_{0}}(x_{p_{0}})k_{0}g]

\displaystyle=[x_{p_{0}},h_{p_{0}}(x_{p_{0}})k_{0}]\cdot g

\displaystyle=\widetilde{h}([x_{p_{0}},k_{0}])\cdot g.

Thus
\widetilde{h}
is a morphism of diffeological principal
G
-bundles over
X
.

Now given a plot
p_{0}:U_{p_{0}}\to X
, we obtain the commutative diagram (
12
) by pulling back
\widetilde{h}
along
p_{0}
and the plotwise trivialization
\varphi
. We need only show that
\widetilde{h}_{p_{0}}(x_{p_{0}},k_{0})=(x_{p_{0}},h_{p_{0}}(x_{p_{0}})\cdot k_{0})
. Since
\widetilde{h}_{p_{0}}=(\varphi^{\prime}_{p_{0}})^{-1}\widehat{h}_{p_{0}}\varphi_{p_{0}}
we have

(15)

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}_{p_{0}}(x_{p_{0}},k_{0})

\displaystyle=(\varphi^{\prime}_{p_{0}})^{-1}\widehat{h}_{p_{0}}\varphi_{p_{0}}(x_{p_{0}},k_{0})

\displaystyle=(\varphi^{\prime}_{p_{0}})^{-1}\widehat{h}_{p_{0}}(x_{p_{0}},[x_{p_{0}},k_{0}])

\displaystyle=(\varphi^{\prime}_{p_{0}})^{-1}(x_{p_{0}},[x_{p_{0}},h_{p_{0}}(x_{p_{0}})\cdot k_{0}])

\displaystyle=(x_{p_{0}},h_{p_{0}}(x_{p_{0}})\cdot k_{0}),

where we have used the plotwise trivialization from the proof of Proposition
3.12
and the convention of Remark
3.13
.
∎

The content of Propositions

and
3.14
can be summarized by saying that we have a functor
\mathsf{Cons}:\mathsf{Coc}(X,G)\to\mathsf{DiffPrin}_{G}(X)
. Our goal now is to show that this functor is an equivalence, namely that it is fully faithful and essentially surjective.

Let us first show essential surjectivity. Suppose that
\pi:P\to X

, where

Any choice of plotwise trivialization

Remark 3.17
.

4.
Diffeological Spaces as Concrete Sheaves

A major development in the theory of diffeological spaces was made in
[
BH11
]
, which showed that the category of diffeological spaces is equivalent to the category of concrete sheaves on the site of open subsets of cartesian spaces. Here we introduce the theory necessary to understand this result, and to prepare the ground for Section
5
. In Appendix
A
, we will show that the category of concrete sheaves on several various sites of interest are equivalent, giving a justification for Definition
2.2
. Nothing in this section is new, but it may be helpful to those less familiar with topos theory.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a category, and
U\in\mathscr{C}
. A
family of morphisms
over
U
is a set of morphisms
r=\{r_{i}:U_{i}\to U\}_{i\in I}
in
\mathscr{C}
with codomain
U
.

A
refinement
of a family of morphisms
t=\{t_{j}:V_{j}\to U\}_{j\in J}
over
U
consists of a family of morphisms
r=\{r_{i}:U_{i}\to U\}_{i\in I}
, a function
\alpha:I\to J
and for each
i\in I
a map
f_{i}:U_{i}\to V_{\alpha(i)}
making the following diagram commute:

(22)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{U_{i}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{V_{\alpha(i)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{\alpha(i)}}

If
r
is a refinement of
t
, with maps
f_{i}:U_{i}\to V_{\alpha(i)}
, then we write
f:r\to t
.

We wish to consider added structure to a category that generalizes the notion of a topology. We will use families of morphisms as a generalized notion of ”open cover.”

Definition 4.2
.

A
collection of families

j
on a category
\mathscr{C}
consists of a set
j(U)
for each
U\in\mathscr{C}
, whose elements
\{r_{i}:U_{i}\to U\}\in j(U)
are families of morphisms over
U
.

We call a collection of families
j
on
\mathscr{C}
a
coverage
if it satisfies the following property: for every
\{r_{i}:U_{i}\to U\}\in j(U)
, and every map
g:V\to U
in
\mathscr{C}
, then there exists a family
\{t_{j}:V_{j}\to V\}\in j(V)
such that
gt_{j}
factors through some
r_{i}
. Namely for every
t_{j}
there exists some
i
and some map
s_{j}:V_{j}\to U_{i}
making the following diagram commute:

(23)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{V_{j}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{U_{i}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{j}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s_{j}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

The families
\{r_{i}:U_{i}\to U\}\in j(U)
are called
covering families
over

. If a map

belongs to a covering family

, then we say that

is a

.

If
\mathscr{C}
is a category, and

is a coverage on
\mathscr{C}
, then we call the pair

a

.

Example 4.3
.

Let
X
be a topological space and let
\mathcal{O}(X)
denote the partially ordered set of open subsets of
X
. Let
j_{X}
denote the collection of familes on
\mathcal{O}(X)
such that
j_{X}(U)
is the set of all open covers of
U
, namely
\{U_{i}\subseteq U\}\in j_{X}(U)
if
\bigcupop\displaylimits_{i}U_{i}=U
.

This collection of families is a coverage, for suppose we have fixed an open cover
\{U_{i}\subseteq U\}
and an open subset
V\subseteq U
. Then
\{V\cap U_{i}\subseteq V\}
is an open cover of
V
, and
V\cap U_{i}\subseteq U_{i}
. We call
j_{X}
the
open cover coverage
of
X
.

Example 4.4
.

Define a collection of families
j_{\text{open}}
on
\mathsf{Man}
as follows: For
M\in\mathsf{Man}
, let
j_{\text{open}}(M)
denote the collection of open covers of
M
. Then
j_{\text{open}}
is a coverage. Indeed if
\{U_{i}\subseteq M\}
is an open cover and

is a smooth map, then
\{f^{-1}(U_{i})\subseteq N\}
is an open cover of

satisfying (

).

Now consider the following full subcategories

\mathsf{Cart}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{Open}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{Man}.

Where
\mathsf{Cart}
is the full subcategory whose objects are cartesian spaces and
\mathsf{Open}
is the full subcategory whose objects are diffeomorphic to open subsets of a cartesian space. The collection of families
j_{\text{open}}
can be restricted to
\mathsf{Open}
and is clearly a coverage there as well.

Notice however that if we restrict
j_{\text{open}}
to
\mathsf{Cart}
, and
U
is a cartesian space, then an open cover

is a covering family for

if and only if it is a cartesian open cover, otherwise it could not be a collection of morphisms in
\mathsf{Cart}
. For
\mathsf{Man}
and
\mathsf{Open}
any open cover will do. However if
\{U_{i}\subseteq U\}
is a cartesian open cover and

is a smooth map, there is no reason that

will be a cartesian open cover. However as we will see in Example

, every open cover can be refined by a cartesian open cover, and thus

is indeed a coverage on
\mathsf{Cart}
.

.

Define a collection of families

on

as follows: For

, let

of

. Let us show that the good covers form a coverage. If

, and thus the following diagram commutes:

. Similarly it defines a coverage on

and

.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\mathsf{Smooth}
denote a site of the form
(\mathscr{C},j)
with
\mathscr{C}\in\{\mathsf{Cart},\mathsf{Open},\mathsf{Man}\}
and

. We will call any such site a

.

Example 4.7
.

We note here that the collection of families

of subductions on the category

of diffeological spaces is a coverage, because the pullback of a subduction is a subduction. We will not use this observation in this section, but it will come up in Section

when we talk about diffeological categories.

Coverages are those collections of families with the least amount of structure with which we can define sheaves on
\mathscr{C}
.

Definition 4.8
.

A
presheaf
on a category
\mathscr{C}
is a functor
F:\mathscr{C}^{op}\to\mathsf{Set}
. An element
x\in F(U)
for an object

is called a
section
over
U
. If
f:U\to V
is a map in
\mathscr{C}
, and
x\in F(V)
, then we sometimes denote
F(f)(x)
by
x|_{U}
. If
\{r_{i}:U_{i}\to U\}_{i\in I}
is a covering family, then a
matching family
is a collection
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in I}
,
x_{i}\in F(U_{i})
, such that given a diagram in
\mathscr{C}
of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{U_{j}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{U_{i}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces U}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces r_{j}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

for all

. An

x
for a matching family

is a section

.

Definition 4.9
.

Given a family of morphisms

. Let

denote the full subcategory of

whose objects are sheaves on

.

Remark 4.10
.

If

is a site that has pullbacks, then we can equivalently express the condition for

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\prodop\displaylimits_{i}F(U_{i})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\prodop\displaylimits_{i,j}F(U_{i}\times_{U}U_{j})}}

is an equalizer. This is how the sheaf condition is often presented in the literature.

Example 4.11
.

Given a smooth manifold

.

and as above is a sheaf. We call a site
(\mathscr{C},j)
where every representable presheaf is a sheaf
subcanonical
. It is not hard to see that
\mathsf{Smooth}
is a subcanonical site.

We wish to single out those sheaves that are in some sense a set with extra structure.

Definition 4.12
.

A site
(\mathscr{C},j)
is
concrete
if:

(1)

it is subcanonical,

(2)

it has a terminal object
*
,

(3)

the functor
\mathscr{C}(*,-):\mathscr{C}\to\mathsf{Set}
is faithful, and

(4)

for every covering family
\{U_{i}\to U\}
, the family of maps
\mathscr{C}(*,U_{i})\to\mathscr{C}(*,U)
is
jointly surjective
, namely the map
\coprodop\displaylimits_{i}\mathscr{C}(*,U_{i})\to\mathscr{C}(*,U)
is surjective.

It is not hard to show that all of the smooth sites are concrete.

Definition 4.13
.

If
(\mathscr{C},j)
is a concrete site and
F
is a presheaf, then we call
F(*)
its
underlying set
, and for any
U\in\mathscr{C}
there always exists a map

\phi_{U}:F(U)\to\mathsf{Set}(\mathscr{C}(*,U),F(*))

defined by
\phi_{U}(x)=\left(u\mapsto F(u)(x)\right)
. We say a sheaf
F
is
concrete
if for every object
U\in\mathscr{C}
, the function
\phi_{U}
is injective. Let
\mathsf{ConSh}(\mathscr{C},j)
denote the full subcategory of concrete sheaves on a concrete site
(\mathscr{C},j)
.

Example 4.14
.

For any smooth manifold
M
, the sheaf
\underline{M}
on
\mathsf{Smooth}
is concrete. This is equivalent to saying that for every
U\in\mathsf{Smooth}
the function

\phi_{U}:C^{\infty}(U,M)\to\mathsf{Set}(U(*),M(*))

is injective, which is the same thing as saying that the set of smooth maps from
U
to
M
is a subset of all set functions from
U
to
M
.

Note that the sheaf
k
is not concrete on
\mathsf{Smooth}
. Indeed if
U\in\mathsf{Smooth}
, then
\phi_{U}
takes the form

\phi_{U}:{}^{k}(U)\to\mathsf{Set}(U(*),{}^{k}(*))

but
{}^{k}(*)=\{0\}
is the zero vector space, thus
\mathsf{Set}(U(*),\{0\})=*
is the singleton set. Since in general
{}^{k}(U)
is nontrivial, this shows that
k
is not concrete.

Theorem 4.15

(
[
BH11
, Proposition 24]
)
.

The category
\mathsf{Diff}^{\prime}
of classical diffeological spaces is equivalent to the category of concrete sheaves on
\mathsf{Open}
with the open cover coverage,

\mathsf{Diff^{\prime}}\simeq\mathsf{ConSh}(\mathsf{Open},j_{\text{open}}).

However, the proof of
[
BH11
, Proposition 24]
can be applied nearly word for word to prove the following.

Theorem 4.16
.

The category
\mathsf{Diff}
of diffeological spaces as defined in Definition
2.2
is equivalent to the category of concrete sheaves on
\mathsf{Cart}
with the good open cover coverage

\mathsf{Diff}\simeq\mathsf{ConSh}(\mathsf{Cart},j_{\text{good}}).

Remark 4.17
.

In Appendix
A
we will prove
\mathsf{Diff}\simeq\mathsf{Diff}^{\prime}
by showing that
\mathsf{ConSh}(\mathsf{Cart},j_{\text{good}})\simeq\mathsf{ConSh}(\mathsf{Open},j_{\text{open}})
.

Theorem
4.16
allows us to make a perspective shift. Constructions made in
\mathsf{Diff}
can be compared with already defined notions of sheaves. For example a differential
k
-form
\omega
on a diffeological space
X

[
Igl13
, Article 6.28]
, is precisely a map

X\xrightarrow{\omega}{}^{k}

of sheaves on
\mathsf{Cart}
. This viewpoint on diffeological spaces, namely as concrete sheaves on
\mathsf{Cart}
, will also be the starting point for Section
5
, where we consider the fully faithful embedding of presheaves into simplicial presheaves. Since concrete sheaves are in particular presheaves, this means that there is a fully faithful embedding of diffeological spaces into simplicial presheaves, where we have a powerful homotopy theory to leverage.

\underline{\mathsf{sPre}(\mathsf{Cart})}(X,Y)_{n}=\mathsf{sPre}(\mathsf{Cart})(X\times\Delta^{n}_{c},Y).

X
and
Y
are simplicial sets, then let
\underline{\mathsf{sSet}}(X,Y)
denote the simplicial set defined degreewise by
\underline{\mathsf{sSet}}(X,Y)_{n}=\mathsf{sSet}(X\times\Delta^{n},Y).

(X^{K})(U)=\underline{\mathsf{sSet}}(K,X(U))

\mathsf{sPre}(\mathsf{Cart})
is tensored and cotensored over
\mathsf{sSet}
in the sense that for simplicial presheaves
X
and
Y
and simplicial set
K

\underline{\mathsf{sPre}(\mathsf{Cart})}(X\times K_{c},Y)\cong\underline{\mathsf{sPre}(\mathsf{Cart})}(X,Y^{K}).

The category
\mathsf{sPre}(\mathsf{Cart})
admits many model structures. Here we will discuss two of them. Say a map
f:X\to Y
of simplicial presheaves is an
objectwise weak equivalence
if
f:X(U)\to Y(U)
is a weak equivalence of simplicial sets for every
U\in\mathsf{Cart}
. Similarly a
objectwise fibration
is a map
f:X\to Y
of simplicial presheaves such that
f:X(U)\to Y(U)
is a Kan fibration of simplicial sets for every
U\in\mathsf{Cart}
.

Theorem 5.2

(
[
BK72
, Page 314]
)
.

There is a cofibrantly generated, simplicial model structure, which we call the
projective model structure
or
Bousfield-Kan model structure
on
\mathsf{sPre}(\mathsf{Cart})
, whose weak equivalences are the objectwise weak equivalences, and whose fibrations are the objectwise fibrations.

Remark 5.3
.

In fact, the projective model structure makes
\mathsf{sPre}(\mathsf{Cart})
a combinatorial model category, see
[
Lur09
, Section A.2.6]
.

Remark 5.4
.

There is a Quillen equivalent model structure on simplicial presheaves where the cofibrations and weak equivalences are objectwise, which is called the injective or Heller model structure. See
[
Bla01
]
for an overview of the different model structures on simplicial presheaves.

As is often the case with model structures, while the descriptions of weak equivalences and fibrations in the projective model structure are convenient, the cofibrations of the projective model structure are less simple to describe. However, the following result gives a sufficient condition for a simplicial presheaf to be cofibrant.

Theorem 5.5

(
[
Dug01
, Corollary 9.4]
)
.

A simplicial presheaf
X
is cofibrant in the projective model structure on simplicial presheaves if

(1)

X
is degreewise a coproduct of representables, i.e.
X_{n}=\coprodop\displaylimits_{i\in I}yU_{i}
for every
n\geq 0
,

(2)

X
is split, in the sense that as a functor
X:\mathscr{C}^{op}\to\mathsf{sSet}
it factors through the category
\mathsf{sSet}_{nd}
whose objects are simplicial sets and whose morphisms are those maps of simplicial sets that map non-degenerate simplices to non-degenerate simplices.

We say that
X
is a
projective cofibrant
simplicial presheaf.

Corollary 5.6
.

If
U\in\mathsf{Cart}
, then
{}^{c}yU
is a projective cofibrant simplicial presheaf on
\mathsf{Cart}
.

Example 5.7
.

If
M
is a finite dimensional smooth manifold, and
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a good open cover, then consider the simplicial presheaf
\check{C}(\mathcal{U})
defined in degree
n
by

\check{C}(\mathcal{U})_{n}=\prodop\displaylimits_{i_{0},\dots,i_{n}}y\left(U_{i_{0}}\cap\dots\cap U_{i_{n}}\right),

with face and degeneracy maps given by inclusions of open sets. Since
\mathcal{U}
is a good open cover,
\check{C}(\mathcal{U})
is a projective cofibrant simplicial presheaf. We call it the
Čech nerve
on
\mathcal{U}
. There is a canonical map

\check{C}(\mathcal{U})\xrightarrow{\pi}{}^{c}M,

of simplicial presheaves on
\mathsf{Cart}
. However this map is not an objectwise weak equivalence in general.

If
U\in\mathsf{Cart}
, and
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is a good open cover of
U
, then we can consider the canonical map

\check{C}(\mathcal{U})\xrightarrow{\pi}{}^{c}yU.

Let
\check{C}
denote the class of such maps as
U
varies over
\mathsf{Cart}
and
\mathcal{U}
varies over good open covers of
U
.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
\mathsf{s}\check{\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{re}(\mathsf{Cart})
denote the left Bousfield localization of the projective model structure on
\mathsf{sPre}(\mathsf{Cart})
at the class of maps
\check{C}
. We call this the
Čech model structure
on
\mathsf{sPre}(\mathsf{Cart})
.

The Čech model structure is described in greater detail in
[
DHI04
, Appendix A]
. Since
\mathsf{s}\check{\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{re}(\mathsf{Cart})
is a left Bousfield localization of the projective model structure, it inherits the same cofibrations, and therefore cofibrant objects. We call its weak equivalences the Čech weak equivalences. Note that any objectwise weak equivalence of simplicial presheaves will be a Čech weak equivalence.

We call the fibrant objects of this model structure
\infty

on
\mathsf{Cart}
. They can be characterized as follows.

Proposition 5.9
.

A simplicial presheaf
X

\mathsf{Cart}
is an

Proof.

This follows from the definition of left Bousfield localization.
∎

The main example of (

Example 5.13
.

Suppose
G
is a group object in
\mathsf{Sh}(\mathsf{Cart})
, which we will call a sheaf of groups. Consider the presheaf of groupoids on
\mathsf{Cart}
that sends a

to the groupoid

\mathbb{B}G(U)\coloneqq[G(U)\rightrightarrows*],

where both source and target maps are the unique map to the singleton set. Thus there is a single object in this groupoid, and for every
s\in G(U)
, there is an isomorphism from the unique object to itself. We visualize morphisms in
\mathbf{B}G(U)
with diagrams like the following

*\xrightarrow{g}*

We define composition in this groupoid using the
opposite
of multiplication in
G

*\xrightarrow{g}*\xrightarrow{h}*\qquad=\qquad*\xrightarrow{hg}*

This convention might seem strange, but we have chosen it to agree with
[
Sch13
, Section 1.2.5.1]
, which was consulted often in the formulation of this paper. Note that in other conventions
[
FSS+12
]
we must simply swap
G
with
G^{op}
, the opposite of the presheaf of groupoids, or the presheaf of groupoids with one object and morphisms the sheaf of groups with multiplication defined by
g\cdot^{op}h=hg
. It is important to note that the key equations (
6
) and (
7
) of Section
3

Now if

is in particular a Lie group, then we can consider it as a sheaf of groups on
\mathsf{Cart}
. Then

will be the groupoid
[C^{\infty}(U,G)\rightrightarrows*]
. Now since all principal
G

[C^{\infty}(U,G)\rightrightarrows*]\simeq\text{Prin}_{G}(U),

where
\text{Prin}_{G}(U)
denotes the groupoid of principal
G
-bundles on
U
. Indeed, every object of the right hand groupoid is isomorphic to the trivial bundle
U\times G\to U
, and the automorphisms of a trivial bundle are in bijection with maps
U\to G
. In
[

it is proven that

\mathsf{Cart}

is also a stack (in the classical sense) on
\mathsf{Cart}
. However
\mathbb{B}G
is not a stack on
\mathsf{Man}
. If we take the nerves of these presheaves of groupoids

and

, then since they are nerves of classical stacks on
\mathsf{Cart}

\infty

\mathsf{Cart}
, and they are objectwise weak equivalent as simplicial presheaves. See
[
FSS+12
, Section 3.2]
for more details.

We often drop the nerve
N
from the notation of our
\infty

\mathbb{B}G

of

Remark 5.14
.

Note that in the above example, strictly speaking
\mathsf{Prin}_{G}
is a not a presheaf of groupoids, because if

is a pair of composable morphisms in
\mathsf{Cart}
, then we obtain functors
\mathsf{Prin}_{G}(W)\xrightarrow{g^{*}}\mathsf{Prin}_{G}(V)\xrightarrow{f^{*}}\mathsf{Prin}_{G}(U)
given by pulling back the principal bundles. However if
P\in\mathsf{Prin}_{G}(W)
, then
(gf)^{*}P\neq f^{*}g^{*}P
, but there is an isomorphism
(gf)^{*}P\cong f^{*}g^{*}P
. Thus
\mathsf{Prin}_{G}
is called a pseudofunctor
\mathsf{Prin}_{G}:\mathsf{Cart}^{op}\to\mathsf{Gpd}
, where
\mathsf{Gpd}
is the
2
-category of groupoids. There is an elegant theory
[
JY20
, Chapter 10]
relating pseudofunctors with categories fibered in groupoids, both of which can be used to develop the theory of stacks of groupoids
[
Vis07
]
. However, the homotopy theory of presheaves of groupoids, pseudofunctors, and categories fibered in groupoids are all equivalent in the sense of
[
Hol08
, Corollary 4.3]
. Furthermore, the notion of being a
1
-stack is independent across the three models. Thus in what follows we elect to use presheaves of groupoids, as they are the simplest to connect with the theory of simplicial presheaves by applying the nerve functor objectwise.

One of the most useful aspects of simplicial model categories is being able to define homotopically invariant mapping spaces.

Definition 5.15
.

If
X
and
A
are simplicial presheaves, then define

(30)

\mathbb{R}\text{Hom}(X,A)\coloneqq\underline{\mathsf{sPre}(\mathsf{Cart})}(QX,RA)

where
QX
is a cofibrant replacement for
X
and
RA
is a fibrant replacement of
A
in the Čech model structure. We call
\mathbb{R}\text{Hom}(X,A)
the
derived mapping space
of
X
and
A
.

A key property of derived mapping spaces is their invariance under weak equivalence. Indeed suppose there is a Čech weak equivalence
f:X\to X^{\prime}
, then the canonical map

\mathbb{R}\text{Hom}(f,A):\mathbb{R}\text{Hom}(X^{\prime},A)\to\mathbb{R}\text{Hom}(X,A)

is a weak equivalence of simplicial sets, similarly for a Čech weak equivalence
g:A\to A^{\prime}
, see
[
Hir09
, Chapter 17]
.

Proposition 5.16

(
[
FSS+12
, Page 23]
)
.

Given a Lie group
G
, and a finite dimensional smooth manifold
M
, there is a weak equivalence of simplicial sets

(31)

N\text{Prin}_{G}(M)\simeq\mathbb{R}\text{Hom}(M,\mathbb{B}G),

where
N\text{Prin}_{G}(M)
denotes nerve of the groupoid of principal
G
-bundles on
M
.

Now if
G
is a diffeological group, and
X
is a diffeological space, we can consider them both as simplicial presheaves on
\mathsf{Cart}
, and compute
\mathbb{R}\text{Hom}(X,\mathbb{B}G)
. It would be hoped that this would in some way reproduce diffeological principal
G
-bundles, in analogy to Proposition
5.16
. One main goal of this paper is to prove that this is indeed the case. But first we must investigate
\mathbb{R}\text{Hom}(X,\mathbb{B}G)

X
was cofibrant, and
\mathbb{B}G
were fibrant in the Čech model structure, then
\mathbb{R}\text{Hom}(X,\mathbb{B}G)

However, it is indeed the case that

is fibrant, thanks to the following wonderful theorem.

Theorem 5.17

(
[
SS21

,
[
Pav22
, Proposition 4.13]
)
.

Let

be a sheaf of groups on
\mathsf{Cart}
. Then
\mathbb{B}G
is an
\infty

\mathsf{Cart}
.

Thus if

5.2.
Resolutions of Diffeological Spaces

Here we discuss three ways of ”resolving” a diffeological space into a diffeological category. One of which,
QX

and

Let us start by describing the resolution
QX

\mathsf{s}\check{\mathsf{P}}\mathsf{re}(\mathsf{Cart})

Lemma 5.18

(
[
Dug01

)
.

Given a diffeological space

QX=\intop\nolimits^{[n]\in\mathsf{\Delta}}\Delta^{n}_{c}\times\left(\coprodop\displaylimits_{U_{p_{n}}\to\dots\to U_{p_{0}}\to X}yU_{p_{n}}\right)

Let us examine this coend formula in more detail. In degree

(QX)_{k}\cong\coprodop\displaylimits_{(f_{k-1},\dots,f_{0}):U_{p_{k}}\to\dots\to U_{p_{0}}}yU_{p_{k}}

where the coproduct is taken over the set

, namely the set of

N_{k}\coloneqq(N\mathsf{Plot}(X))_{k}

The face maps are given as follows:

(34)

d_{i}(x_{p_{k}},f_{k-1},f_{k-2},\dots,f_{0})=\begin{cases}(f_{k-1}(x_{p_{k}}),f_{k-2},\dots,f_{0})&i=0\\

(x_{p_{k}},f_{k-1},\dots,f_{1})&i=k.\end{cases}

Degeneracies insert identity maps.

For convenience, we will denote the coproduct over all plots as

B\coloneqq(QX)_{0}=\coprodop\displaylimits_{p_{0}\in\mathsf{Plot}(X)}U_{p_{0}}.

Notice that there is a canonical map

B\xrightarrow{\pi}X

given by
\pi(p_{0},x_{p_{0}})=p_{0}(x_{p_{0}})

This map induces a map
\pi:QX\to{}^{c}X

Lemma 5.19

If

(35)

\pi_{0}QX\cong X,

\pi_{0}:\mathsf{sPre}(\mathsf{Cart})\to\mathsf{Pre}(\mathsf{Cart})

Proof.

This follows from the fact that every presheaf is a colimit of representables, see the discussion above
[

∎

In low degrees, this simplicial presheaf/simplicial diffeological space looks like:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{B=\coprodop\displaylimits_{p_{0}\in\mathsf{Plot}(X)}U_{p_{0}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\coprodop\displaylimits_{f_{0}:U_{p_{1}}\to U_{p_{0}}}U_{p_{1}}}}

For a diffeological space

If

Appendix A
Comparison of Site Structures

Here we will prove that the definition of diffeological spaces as given in Definition

We will do this by exploiting Theorem

\overline{r^{\prime}}
is a covering sieve of
\tau(j)
. Thus if
F
is a sheaf on
j
, then by Lemma
A.11
, it will be a sheaf on
\tau(j)
, so it will then be a sheaf on
\overline{r^{\prime}}
, and thus by Lemma
A.2
it will be a sheaf on
r^{\prime}
. Since
r^{\prime}
was arbitrary,
F
is therefore a sheaf on all of
j^{\prime}
. Thus if
F
is a sheaf on
(\mathscr{C},j)
, then it will be a sheaf on
(\mathscr{C},j^{\prime})
. Conversely
j\leq j^{\prime}
proves that
\mathsf{Sh}(\mathscr{C},j)=\mathsf{Sh}(\mathscr{C},j^{\prime})
.
∎

Proposition A.13
.

Let
j_{\text{good}}
denote the good cover coverage on
\mathsf{Man}
defined in Example
4.5
, and
j_{\text{open}}
denote the open cover coverage on
\mathsf{Man}
defined in Example
4.4
. Then
\mathsf{Sh}(\mathsf{Man},j_{\text{good}})=\mathsf{Sh}(\mathsf{Man},j_{\text{open}})
. This similarly holds for
\mathsf{Cart}
and
\mathsf{Open}
.

Proof.

By
[
BT+82
, Corollary 5.2]
, we have that
j_{\text{open}}\leq j_{\text{good}}
. Now
j_{\text{good}}\leq j_{\text{open}}
, since every good open cover is in particular an open cover.
∎

Corollary A.14
.

The categories of concrete sheaves on
\mathsf{Man}
with the open and good open coverages agree:

\mathsf{ConSh}(\mathsf{Man},j_{\text{good}})=\mathsf{ConSh}(\mathsf{Man},j_{\text{open}}).

This result remains true if we replace
\mathsf{Man}
with
\mathsf{Open}
or
\mathsf{Cart}
.

Now we wish to compare sites whose underlying categories differ. Let
\mathscr{C}
be a category and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{C}
a full subcategory. Then a sieve
R\hookrightarrow yU
on
\mathscr{C}
is said to be a
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
-sieve if it is generated by a family of morphisms all of whose domains are objects in
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
.

Definition A.15
.

Let
(\mathscr{C},J)
be a category with a Grothendieck coverage, and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{C}
a full subcategory. We say that
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
is
J
-dense
in
\mathscr{C}
if every object
U\in\mathscr{C}
has a covering sieve
R\in J(U)
that is a
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
-sieve.

If
(\mathscr{C},j)
is a site where
j
is not necessarily a Grothendieck coverage, then we say that a full subcategory
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{C}
is
j
-dense if it is
\tau(j)
-dense in
(\mathscr{C},\tau(j))
.

By
[
BT+82
, Theorem 5.1]
, every finite dimensional smooth manifold has a good open cover. Thus if
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{i}\subseteq M\}
denotes a good open cover of
M
, then
\overline{\mathcal{U}}
is a covering sieve of
(\mathsf{Man},\tau(j_{\text{open}}))
and it is a
\mathsf{Cart}
-sieve. Since this is true for any manifold
M
, it follows that
\mathsf{Cart}
is
j_{\text{open}}
-dense in
(\mathsf{Man},j_{\text{open}})
. By the same argument
\mathsf{Cart}
is also dense in
(\mathsf{Man},j_{\text{good}})
. This also implies that
\mathsf{Open}
is dense in
(\mathsf{Man},j)
for
j\in\{j_{\text{open}},j_{\text{good}}\}
.

Now suppose
(\mathscr{C},J)
is a site with a Grothendieck coverage. If
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{C}
is a full subcategory, define a collection of families
J^{\prime}
on
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
by defining
J^{\prime}(U)
to be the collection of those covering sieves
R\in J(U)
that are also
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
-sieves. It is not hard to show that this is also a Grothendieck coverage, called the
induced coverage
on
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
, and denoted
J|_{\mathscr{C}^{\prime}}
.

The following result is well-known in the literature as the
Comparison Lemma
.

Theorem A.16

(
[
Joh02
, Theorem 2.2.3]
)
.

Let
(\mathscr{C},J)
be a site with a Grothendieck coverage and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{C}
a
J
-dense full subcategory. Then the restriction functor
\text{res}:\mathsf{Pre}(\mathscr{C})\to\mathsf{Pre}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime})
itself restricts to a functor
\text{res}:\mathsf{Sh}(\mathscr{C},J)\to\mathsf{Sh}(\mathscr{C}^{\prime},J|_{\mathscr{C}^{\prime}})
, and this functor is an equivalence of categories.

Note that
\tau(j^{\mathsf{Cart}}_{\text{open}})=\tau(j^{\mathsf{Man}}_{\text{open}})|_{\mathsf{Cart}}
. This can be seen by simply noting that every sieve in
\tau(j^{\mathsf{Man}}_{\text{open}})|_{\mathsf{Cart}}
is generated by a open cover by cartesian spaces, and contains every such sieve. A similar argument proves the same for
j^{\mathsf{Cart}}_{\text{good}}
and
j^{\mathsf{Open}}_{\text{good}},j^{\mathsf{Open}}_{\text{open}}
.

Corollary A.17
.

All categories of the form
\mathsf{ConSh}(\mathscr{C},j)
for
\mathscr{C}\in\{\mathsf{Cart},\mathsf{Open},\mathsf{Man}\}
and
j\in\{j_{\text{open}},j_{\text{good}}\}
are equivalent.

Proof.

Theorem
A.16
implies that the categories
\mathsf{Sh}(\mathscr{C},j)
for
\mathscr{C}\in\{\mathsf{Cart},\mathsf{Open},\mathsf{Man}\}
and
j\in\{j_{\text{open}},j_{\text{good}}\}
are all equivalent. Further, using the same argument as in the proof of
[
WW14
, Lemma 2.9]
, the above equivalences restrict to equivalences of all the full subcategories
\mathsf{ConSh}(\mathscr{C},j)
of concrete sheaves.
∎

Thus by Theorem
4.15
, we have that
\mathsf{Diff}^{\prime}\simeq\mathsf{ConSh}(\mathsf{Open},j_{\text{open}})
, and by Corollary
A.17
, we have that
\mathsf{ConSh}(\mathsf{Open},j_{\text{open}})\simeq\mathsf{ConSh}(\mathsf{Cart},j_{\text{good}})\simeq\mathsf{Diff}
. Thus we have proved the main proposition of this section.

Proposition A.18
.

The category of classical diffeological spaces
\mathsf{Diff}^{\prime}

introduced in Definition

.

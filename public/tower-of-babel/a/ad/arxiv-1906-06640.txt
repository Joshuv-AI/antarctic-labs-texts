Hamilton’s Dynamics in Complex Phase Space
M. A. Shahzad

Abstract
We present the basic formulation of Hamilton dynamics in complex phase space. We extend the Hamilton’s function by including the imaginary part and find out the corresponding Hamilton’s canonical equation of motion. Example of simple harmonic motion are considered and the corresponding trajectory are plotted on real and complex phase space.

These first-order differential equations are called as Hamilton’s canonical equation of motion. By considering a complex Hamilton function of the form
H(q,p,t)=U(q,p,t)+jV(q,p,t)
, we obtained the Hamilton’s canonical equation of motion for the Hamilton’s function
H(q,p,t)
defined on complex phase space.
In order to understand the Hamilton dynamics in complex phase-space, we consider an example of simple harmonic motion defined by the following Hamilton’s function
H(q,p,t)
with imaginary part equal to zero,

H(q,p,t)=U(q,p,t)+jV(q,p,t)={P^{2}\over 2m}+{k\over 2}q^{2}

From equation (7,8,9), we have

\displaystyle\dot{q}

\displaystyle={\partial H\over\partial p}={p\over m}\qquad\dot{p}=-{\partial H\over\partial q}=-kq

\displaystyle{\partial H\over\partial t}=0

Consider an example of simple harmonic motion defined by the following complex Hamilton’s function
H(q,p,t)
with non-zero imaginary part,

H(q,p,t)=U(q,p,t)+jV(q,p,t)={P^{2}\over 2m}+j{k\over 2}q^{2}

The corresponding equation of motion can be written as

\displaystyle\dot{q}

\displaystyle={\partial H\over\partial p}={p\over m}\qquad\dot{p}=-{\partial H\over\partial q}=-jkq

\displaystyle{\partial H\over\partial t}=0

Let
F(q,p)
and
H(q,p)
, with
H(q,p)=U(q,p)+jV(q,p)
, be two functions on phase space. Then the Poisson bracket is given by

[F,H]=[F,U]+j[F,V]

(10)

In particular,
[q_{i},q_{i}+jp_{k}]=[q_{i},q_{i}]+j[q_{i},p_{k}]=j\delta_{ik}
and
[q_{i},jp_{k}]=[q,0]+j[q,p]=j\delta_{ik}
.
With the help of Hamilton’s equations (7,8,9), we have

{dF\over dt}=[F,H]+{\partial F\over\partial t}=[F,U]+j[F,V]+{\partial F\over\partial t}

Consider a bivariate Hamilton function
H^{\prime}(q,p)
associated to the univariate Hamilton function
H(z)

via
H^{\prime}(x,y)=U(x,y)+jV(x,y)=H(z)|_{z=x+jy}
. The total differential is defined as
P

dH^{\prime}={\partial H^{\prime}(q,p)\over\partial q}dq+{\partial H^{\prime}(q,p)\over\partial p}dp

Using
H^{\prime}(q,p)=U(q,p)+jV(q,p)
, we obtain

\displaystyle dH^{\prime}={\partial U(q,p)\over\partial q}dq+j{\partial V(q,p)\over\partial q}dq

\displaystyle+{\partial U(q,p)\over\partial p}dp+j{\partial U(q,p)\over\partial p}dp

Since,
z=q+jp
and
z^{*}=q-jp
, we have

\displaystyle dz=dq+jdp

\displaystyle dz^{*}=dq-jdp

and

\displaystyle dq={1\over 2}(dz+dz^{*})

\displaystyle dp={1\over 2j}(dz-dz^{*})

The total differential
dH
of a complex valued Hamilton function
H(z)
can be expressed as

dH={\partial H(z)\over\partial z}dz+{\partial H(z)\over\partial z^{*}}dz^{*}

(11)

where the Wirtinger derivatives
W
are defined by

\displaystyle{\partial\over\partial z}={1\over 2}\Big[{\partial\over\partial q}-j{\partial\over\partial p}\Big]

(12)

\displaystyle{\partial\over\partial z^{*}}={1\over 2}\Big[{\partial\over\partial q}+j{\partial\over\partial p}\Big]

(13)

Using
H=p\dot{q}-L
, we have

\displaystyle dH=pd\dot{q}+\dot{q}dp-{\partial L\over\partial z^{1}}dz^{1}-{\partial L\over\partial z^{1*}}dz^{1*}

(14)

where

\displaystyle z^{1}

\displaystyle=(q+j\dot{q})

\displaystyle z^{1*}

\displaystyle=(q-j\dot{q})

\displaystyle dq

\displaystyle={1\over 2}(dz^{1}+dz^{1*})

\displaystyle d\dot{q}

\displaystyle={1\over 2j}(dz^{1}-dz^{1*})

Equation (14) becomes

\displaystyle dH=pd\dot{q}+\dot{q}dp-{1\over 2}\Big({\partial\over\partial q}-j{\partial\over\partial\dot{q}}\Big)L\big(dq+jd\dot{q}\big)

\displaystyle-{1\over 2}\Big({\partial\over\partial q}+j{\partial\over\partial\dot{q}}\Big)L\big(dq-jd\dot{q}\big)

(15)

Using equation (4) and (5), equation (15) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle dH=\dot{q}dp-\dot{p}dq

(16)

Using
dq=(dz+dz^{*})/2
, and
dp=(dz-dz^{*})/2j
, we have

\displaystyle dH

\displaystyle={\dot{q}\over 2j}(dz-dz^{*})-{\dot{p}\over 2}(dz+dz^{*})

(17)

or

\displaystyle dH=\Big({\dot{q}\over 2j}-{\dot{p}\over 2}\Big)dz-\Big({\dot{q}\over 2j}+{\dot{p}\over 2}\Big)dz^{*}

(18)

Comparing the coefficient of
dz
and
dz^{*}
in equation (18) with equation (11), we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{q}=j\Big({\partial\over\partial z}-{\partial\over\partial z^{*}}\Big)H

(19)

\displaystyle\dot{p}=-\Big({\partial\over\partial z}+{\partial\over\partial z^{*}}\Big)H

(20)

Equation (19-20) are Hamilton equation of motion in complex phase space. Substituting back the Wirtinger derivatives (equation (12,13)) in equation (19-20), we obtain Hamilton equation of motion in real phase space;

\displaystyle\dot{q}={\partial H\over\partial p}

(21)

\displaystyle\dot{p}=-{\partial H\over\partial q}

(22)

Equation (19-20) are basic Hamilton’s canonical equation of motion which can be used to understand the dynamics of particles in complex phase space.

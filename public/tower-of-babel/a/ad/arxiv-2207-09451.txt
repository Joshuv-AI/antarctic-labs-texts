Balancing polynomials, Fibonacci numbers and some new series for π \pi


Abstract
We evaluate some types of infinite series with balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials in closed form. These evaluations will lead to some new curious series for π \pi involving Fibonacci and Lucas numbers. Our findings complement those of Castellanos from 1986 and 1989.

1
Motivation and Preliminaries

Castellanos
[
1
,
2
]
has found, among other things, the following curious series for
\pi
:

\frac{\pi}{4}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{F_{(2n+1)(2m+1)}}{5^{n}}\Big(\frac{2}{F_{2m+1}+\sqrt{F_{2m+1}^{2}+4/5}}\Big)^{2n+1},\quad m\geq 0.

(1)

When
m=0
the expression simplifies to (
[
1
, Eq. (46)]
and
[
2
, Eq. (3.4)]
)

\frac{\pi}{4}=\sqrt{5}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}F_{2n+1}\Big(\frac{2}{3+\sqrt{5}}\Big)^{2n+1},

which has the equivalent form

\frac{\pi}{4}=\sqrt{5}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{F_{2n+1}}{\alpha^{4n+2}}.

(2)

Another series involving the squares of
F_{2n+1}
is (
[
2
, Eqs. (3.2) and (3.3)]
)

\frac{\pi}{20}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{F_{(2n+1)(2m+1)}^{2}}{(t+\sqrt{t^{2}+1})^{2n+1}},\quad m\geq 0,

(3)

with

t=\frac{5F_{2m+1}^{2}}{4}\Big(1+\sqrt{1+\Big(\frac{16}{25}\Big(\frac{5}{2}F_{2m+1}^{2}-1\Big)/F_{2m+1}^{4}\Big)},

which, for
m=0
, simplifies to

\frac{\pi}{20}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{F_{2n+1}^{2}}{(3+\sqrt{10})^{2n+1}}.

(4)

In the identities above,
F_{n}
(respectively
L_{n}
) are the famous Fibonacci (Lucas) numbers,
defined for
n\geq 0
by the recursion
F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_{n}
(
L_{n+2}=L_{n+1}+L_{n}
)
with initial conditions
F_{0}=0,F_{1}=1
(
L_{0}=2
and
L_{1}=1
), and
\alpha=\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
is the golden ratio.
Castellanos’ series pose the question, whether more series of these types exist?
Here, we give a positive answer to this question.
Working with balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials we derive more series for
\pi

involving Fibonacci and Lucas numbers and exhibiting such a structure.

Recall that, for any integer
n\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{C}
, balancing polynomials
\big(B_{n}(x)\big)_{n\geq 0}
and
Lucas-balancing polynomials
\big(C_{n}(x)\big)_{n\geq 0}
are defined by the second-order homogeneous linear recurrence
[
4
]

u_{n}(x)=6xu_{n-1}(x)-u_{n-2}(x),

(5)

but with different initial terms. Balancing polynomials start with
B_{0}(x)=0
and
B_{1}(x)=1
, while for Lucas-balancing polynomials
we set
C_{0}(x)=1
,
C_{1}(x)=3x
. These polynomials have been introduced as a natural extension of the popular balancing and Lucas-balancing numbers
B_{n}
and
C_{n}
, respectively, and must be seen as a special member of the Horadam sequence.
Obviously,
B_{n}=B_{n}(1)
and
C_{n}=C_{n}(1)
. The first few polynomials are

\displaystyle B_{0}(x)=0,\quad\,\,B_{1}(x)=1,\quad\,\,B_{2}(x)=6x,\quad\,\,B_{3}(x)=36x^{2}-1,

\displaystyle B_{4}(x)=216x^{3}-12x,\,\,\quad B_{5}(x)=1296x^{4}-108x^{2}+1,

and

\displaystyle C_{0}(x)=1,\,\,\quad C_{1}(x)=3x,\,\,\quad C_{2}(x)=18x^{2}-1,\,\,\quad C_{3}(x)=108x^{3}-9x,

\displaystyle C_{4}(x)=648x^{4}-72x^{2}+1,\,\,\quad C_{5}(x)=3888x^{5}-540x^{3}+15x.

The closed forms, known as Binet’s formulas, for balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials are given by

B_{n}(x)=\frac{\lambda_{1}^{n}(x)-\lambda_{2}^{n}(x)}{\lambda_{1}(x)-\lambda_{2}(x)},\qquad C_{n}(x)=\frac{\lambda_{1}^{n}(x)+\lambda_{2}^{n}(x)}{2},

(6)

where
\lambda_{1}(x)=3x+\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}
and
\lambda_{2}(x)=3x-\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}
. We note the following properties:

\lambda_{1}(x)\cdot\lambda_{2}(x)=1,\lambda_{1}(x)-\lambda_{2}(x)=2\sqrt{9x^{2}-1},\lambda_{1}(x)+\lambda_{2}(x)=6x

and
0<\lambda_{2}(x)<\lambda_{1}(x)
for
x>1/3
. The polynomials possess a simple connection to Chebyshev polynomials
[
4
]
via

B_{n}(x)=U_{n-1}(3x)\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad C_{n}(x)=T_{n}(3x),

where
U_{n}(x)
and
T_{n}(x)
are Chebyshev polynomials of the first and second kind, respectively.
Some other interesting properties have been discovered in the articles
[
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
]
.
Balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials are also linked in various ways to Fibonacci and Lucas numbers.
Two such connections, which will be exploited in the text, are

B_{n}\Big(\frac{L_{2m}}{6}\Big)=\frac{F_{2mn}}{F_{2m}}\,\,,\qquad C_{n}\Big(\frac{L_{2m}}{6}\Big)=\frac{L_{2mn}}{2},

(7)

and

B_{2n+1}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{5}}{6}F_{2m+1}\Big)=\frac{L_{(2m+1)(2n+1)}}{L_{2m+1}}\,\,,\qquad C_{2n+1}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{5}}{6}F_{2m+1}\Big)=\frac{\sqrt{5}}{2}F_{(2m+1)(2n+1)}.

(8)

These relations can be deduced from the corresponding relations between Fibonacci numbers and Chebyshev polynomials
(see
[
2
]
and
[
4
]
).

In the present article, we study special infinite series involving
B_{n}(x)
and
C_{n}(x)
, respectively.
We evaluate these series in closed form. Based on these results, new infinite series for
\pi
with Fibonacci and
Lucas numbers will be stated as an immediate consequence. These new and curious series must be seen as complements
of Castellanos’ results from 1986 and 1989.

2
Complementing the work of Castellanos (Part 1)

The next theorem is our starting point.

Theorem 1
.

For each real
x
with
x>1/3
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|>|\lambda_{1}(x)|
, we have

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{B_{2n+1}(x)}{z^{2n+1}}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\arctan\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z}{z^{2}+1}\Big)

(9)

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{C_{2n+1}(x)}{z^{2n+1}}=\frac{1}{2}\arctan\Big(\frac{6xz}{z^{2}-1}\Big).

(10)

Proof.

Recall the Taylor series for the arctangent function

\arctan(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}z^{2n+1},\quad|z|\leq 1.

(11)

Hence, for all
x
and
z
with
|z|>|\lambda_{1}(x)|
,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{B_{2n+1}(x)}{z^{2n+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}(x)-\lambda_{2}(x)}\Big(\arctan\Big(\frac{\lambda_{1}(x)}{z}\Big)-\arctan\Big(\frac{\lambda_{2}(x)}{z}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\arctan\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z}{z^{2}+1}\Big),

where in the last step we applied the identity

\arctan(a)\pm\arctan(b)=\arctan\Big(\frac{a\pm b}{1\mp ab}\Big).

(12)

This proves the first identity. The proof of the second identity is very similar and omitted.
∎

Corollary 2
.

The following series representation for
\pi
holds

\frac{\pi}{8}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{C_{2n+1}}{(3+\sqrt{10})^{2n+1}}.

(13)

Remark 3
.

Observe the similarity of equation (
13
) to Castellanos’ series (
4
).
To be particular, the two series provide an interesting example for series whose members’ denominators
and the sum are the same but the numerators are different. Namely,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{F_{2n+1}^{2}}{(3+\sqrt{10})^{2n+1}}=\frac{\pi}{20}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{\frac{2}{5}C_{2n+1}}{(3+\sqrt{10})^{2n+1}}.

Such series seem to be rare. Another example given by Mező
[
10
]
(and involving Lucas numbers)
are the series

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{L_{n}}{n2^{n}}=2\cdot\ln(2)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{2}{n2^{n}}.

2.1
Series with even Fibonacci (Lucas) coefficients

Setting
x=L_{2m}/6,m\geq 0
, we first note that

\lambda_{1}\Big(\frac{L_{2m}}{6}\Big)=\frac{L_{2m}+\sqrt{L_{2m}^{2}-4}}{2}=\alpha^{2m},

with
\alpha=(1+\sqrt{5})/2
and where we have used that
L_{n}^{2}=5F_{n}^{2}+(-1)^{n}4
.
We can now use (
7
) to obtain the relations, valid for all
|z|>\alpha^{2m}
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{F_{2m(2n+1)}}{z^{2n+1}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\arctan\Big(\sqrt{5}\frac{F_{2m}z}{z^{2}+1}\Big),

(14)

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{L_{2m(2n+1)}}{z^{2n+1}}=\arctan\Big(\frac{L_{2m}z}{z^{2}-1}\Big).

(15)

Especially, for
z=L_{2m}
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{F_{2m(2n+1)}}{L_{2m}^{2n+1}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\arctan\Big(\sqrt{5}\frac{F_{4m}}{L_{2m}^{2}+1}\Big),

(16)

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{L_{2m(2n+1)}}{L_{2m}^{2n+1}}=\arctan\Big(\frac{L_{2m}^{2}}{L_{2m}^{2}-1}\Big).

(17)

Next, from
\alpha^{n}=\alpha F_{n}+F_{n-1},n\geq 1,
we see that

\alpha^{2m}<2F_{2m}+F_{2m-1}=F_{2m+2}.

Hence, inserting
z=F_{2m+2}
, we also get the relations

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{F_{2m(2n+1)}}{F_{2m+2}^{2n+1}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\arctan\Big(\sqrt{5}\frac{F_{2m}F_{2m+2}}{F_{2m+1}F_{2m+3}}\Big),

(18)

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{L_{2m(2n+1)}}{F_{2m+2}^{2n+1}}=\arctan\Big(\frac{L_{2m}F_{2m+2}}{F_{2m}F_{2m+4}}\Big),

(19)

where we have used the Catalan identities

F_{2m+2}^{2}+1=F_{2m+3}F_{2m+1}\quad\mbox{and}\quad F_{2m+2}^{2}-1=F_{2m+4}F_{2m}.

To derive Castellanos-like expressions for
\pi
with Fibonacci (Lucas) coefficients,
we use (
14
) and (
15
), and relate them to special arctan arguments.
We start with

\frac{\sqrt{5}F_{2m}z}{z^{2}+1}=1\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\frac{L_{2m}z}{z^{2}-1}=1.

Solving
z^{2}-\sqrt{5}F_{2m}z+1=0,m\geq 1,
gives

z_{1/2}=\frac{\sqrt{5}F_{2m}\pm\sqrt{5F_{2m}^{2}-4}}{2}.

But it can be checked easily that for all
m\geq 1

0<z_{2}=\frac{\sqrt{5}F_{2m}-\sqrt{5F_{2m}^{2}-4}}{2}<z_{1}=\frac{\sqrt{5}F_{2m}+\sqrt{5F_{2m}^{2}-4}}{2}<\alpha^{2m}

with
\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}z_{2}=0
and
z_{1}\sim\alpha^{2m}
as
m\rightarrow\infty
.
Similarly, solving the equation
z^{2}-L_{2m}z-1=0,m\geq 0
gives

z_{1/2}=\frac{L_{2m}\pm\sqrt{L_{2m}^{2}+4}}{2}

and

z_{2}=\frac{L_{2m}-\sqrt{L_{2m}^{2}+4}}{2}<0<\alpha^{2m}<z_{1}=\frac{L_{2m}+\sqrt{L_{2m}^{2}+4}}{2}.

Note also that
\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}z_{2}=0
while
z_{1}\sim\alpha^{2m}
as
m\rightarrow\infty
.
Hence, inserting
z_{1}
in (
15
) and simplifying proves the next result.

Theorem 4
.

For each integer
m\geq 0
the following expression for
\pi
is valid

\frac{\pi}{4}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}L_{2m(2n+1)}\Big(\frac{2}{L_{2m}+\sqrt{L_{2m}^{2}+4}}\Big)^{2n+1}.

(20)

Equation (
20
) is the first Lucas number counterpart of (
1
).
It is worth to note, that the case
m=0
yields

\frac{\pi}{8}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\Big(\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{2}}\Big)^{2n+1},

which can be obtained directly from (
11
) with
z=1/(1+\sqrt{2})
and using the arctan identity

2\arctan(x)=\arctan\Big(\frac{2x}{1-x^{2}}\Big),\qquad x^{2}<1.

When
m=1
, then

\frac{\pi}{4}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}L_{4n+2}\Big(\frac{2}{3+\sqrt{13}}\Big)^{2n+1}.

In a similar manner, working with other arguments of the arctan function,
we can derive the following presumably new series for
\pi
involving even Lucas numbers.

Theorem 5
.

For each integer
m\geq 0
the following expressions for
\pi
are valid

\frac{\pi}{6}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}L_{2m(2n+1)}\Big(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}L_{2m}+\sqrt{3L_{2m}^{2}+4}}\Big)^{2n+1},

(21)

\frac{\pi}{12}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}L_{2m(2n+1)}\Big(\frac{2(2-\sqrt{3})}{L_{2m}+\sqrt{L_{2m}^{2}+4(2-\sqrt{3})^{2}}}\Big)^{2n+1},

(22)

and

\frac{\pi}{5}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}L_{2m(2n+1)}\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{5-2\sqrt{5}}}{L_{2m}+\sqrt{L_{2m}^{2}+4(5-2\sqrt{5})}}\Big)^{2n+1}.

(23)

2.2
Series with odd Fibonacci (Lucas) coefficients

Setting
x=\sqrt{5}F_{2m+1}/6,m\geq 0
, we observe that

\lambda_{1}\Big(\frac{\sqrt{5}F_{2m+1}}{6}\Big)=\frac{\sqrt{5}F_{2m+1}+L_{2m+1}}{2}=\alpha^{2m+1},

where again we have used
L_{n}^{2}=5F_{n}^{2}+(-1)^{n}4
.
Proceeding as before, inserting the value of
x
into (
9
) and (
10
) we obtain the following
expressions via (
8
), valid for all
|z|>\alpha^{2m+1}
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{F_{(2m+1)(2n+1)}}{z^{2n+1}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\arctan\Big(\sqrt{5}F_{2m+1}\frac{z}{z^{2}-1}\Big),

(24)

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{L_{(2m+1)(2n+1)}}{z^{2n+1}}=\arctan\Big(L_{2m+1}\frac{z}{z^{2}+1}\Big).

(25)

Especially, for
z=L_{2m+1}
,

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{F_{(2m+1)(2n+1)}}{L_{2m+1}^{2n+1}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{5}}\arctan\Big(\sqrt{5}\frac{F_{4m+2}}{L_{2m+1}^{2}+1}\Big),

(26)

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{L_{(2m+1)(2n+1)}}{L_{2m+1}^{2n+1}}=\arctan\Big(\frac{L_{2m+1}^{2}}{L_{2m+1}^{2}-1}\Big).

(27)

Once more, deriving Castellanos-like expressions for
\pi
is fairly simple.
We start with

\frac{\sqrt{5}F_{2m+1}z}{z^{2}-1}=1\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\frac{L_{2m+1}z}{z^{2}+1}=1.

Solving
z^{2}-\sqrt{5}F_{2m+1}z-1=0,m\geq 1,
gives

z_{1/2}=\frac{\sqrt{5}F_{2m+1}\pm\sqrt{5F_{2m+1}^{2}+4}}{2}.

But it can be checked easily that
z_{2}<\alpha^{2m+1}<z_{1}
for all
m\geq 0
.
Inserting the value
z_{1}
in (
24
) gives Castellanos’ series (
1
).
Similarly, solving the equation
z^{2}-L_{2m+1}z+1=0,m\geq 0,
gives

z_{1/2}=\frac{L_{2m+1}\pm\sqrt{L_{2m+1}^{2}-4}}{2}

We note that, for
m=0
the zeros
z_{1/2}
are complex numbers with
|z_{1}|=|z_{2}|=1<\alpha
.
For
m\geq 1
, the zeros are real and
z_{2}<z_{1}<\alpha^{2m+1}
. Hence, no such series for
\pi
will exist.

In the same manner other arctan arguments can be analyzed.
Interestingly, the careful analysis shows that no series involving odd Lucas numbers will come to appearance.

\pi
that we found.

Theorem 6
.

For each integer
m\geq 0
the following expressions for
\pi
are valid

\frac{\pi}{12}=\sqrt{5}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}F_{(2m+1)(2n+1)}\Big(\frac{2(2-\sqrt{3})}{\sqrt{5}F_{2m+1}+\sqrt{5F_{2m+1}^{2}+4(2-\sqrt{3})^{2}}}\Big)^{2n+1},

(29)

and

\frac{\pi}{5}=\sqrt{5}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}F_{(2m+1)(2n+1)}\Big(\frac{2(\sqrt{5-2\sqrt{5}})}{\sqrt{5}F_{2m+1}+\sqrt{5F_{2m+1}^{2}+4(5-2\sqrt{5})}}\Big)^{2n+1}.

(30)

The special case of (
28
) when
m=0
appears in one of Castellanos’ papers
[
1
, Eq. (48)]
.
The generalization and the other two series seem to be new.

Complementing the work of Castellanos (Part 2)

Replacing
z
in (
9
) by
z/\lambda_{1}(x)
and
z/\lambda_{2}(x)
, respectively, and combining the terms according to the Binet form
gives

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{B_{2n+1}^{2}(x)}{z^{2n+1}}=\frac{1}{4(9x^{2}-1)}\Big(\arctan\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\lambda_{1}(x)z}{z^{2}+\lambda_{1}^{2}(x)}\Big)-\arctan\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}\lambda_{2}(x)z}{z^{2}+\lambda_{2}^{2}(x)}\Big)\Big)

Now, we can apply (
12
) and simplify to end with the following identity involving squares of balancing polynomials

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{B_{2n+1}^{2}(x)}{z^{2n+1}}=\frac{1}{4(9x^{2}-1)}\arctan\Big(\frac{4(9x^{2}-1)z(z^{2}-1)}{z^{4}+6(12x^{2}-1)z^{2}+1}\Big),\quad z>\lambda_{1}^{2}(x).

(31)

Similarly, the same replacement in (

) gives

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{C_{2n+1}^{2}(x)}{z^{2n+1}}=\frac{1}{4}\arctan\Big(\frac{36x^{2}z(z^{2}-1)}{z^{4}-2(36x^{2}-1)z^{2}+1}\Big),\quad z>\lambda_{1}^{2}(x).

(32)

To get a series for, say,
\pi/128
we set
x=1
in (
31
) and are left with the quadric

z^{4}-32z^{3}+66z^{2}+32z+1=0.

(33)

The roots are

\displaystyle 8-\sqrt{47}-4\sqrt{7-\sqrt{47}},\qquad 8-\sqrt{47}+4\sqrt{7-\sqrt{47}},

B_{2n+1}^{2}

Setting

\frac{\pi}{16}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{C_{2n+1}^{2}}{z^{*2n+1}},

is a positive root of the polynomial equation

z^{4}-36z^{3}-70z^{2}+36z+1=0.

(34)

Here, also all four roots of equation (

) are real

\displaystyle 9-7\sqrt{2}-3\sqrt{20-14\sqrt{2}},\qquad 9-7\sqrt{2}+3\sqrt{20-14\sqrt{2}},

\displaystyle 9+7\sqrt{2}-3\sqrt{20+14\sqrt{2}},\qquad 9+7\sqrt{2}+3\sqrt{20+14\sqrt{2}}

and the only (biggest) root that satisfies the condition
z>\lambda_{1}^{2}(1)
is

z^{*}=9+7\sqrt{2}+3\sqrt{20+14\sqrt{2}}=37,8254\ldots.

Hence,

\frac{\pi}{16}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{C_{2n+1}^{2}}{(9+7\sqrt{2}+3\sqrt{20+14\sqrt{2}})^{2n+1}}.

(35)

From (
31
) and (

), with

, we arrive at

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{F_{2m(2n+1)}^{2}}{z^{2n+1}}=\frac{1}{5}\arctan\Big(\frac{5F_{2m}^{2}z(z^{2}-1)}{z^{4}+2(5F_{2m}^{2}+1)z^{2}+1}\Big)

(36)

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{L_{2m(2n+1)}^{2}}{z^{2n+1}}=\arctan\Big(\frac{L_{2m}^{2}z(z^{2}-1)}{z^{4}-2(L_{2m}^{2}-1)z^{2}+1}\Big).

(37)

To get a new Castellanos’ type series for
\pi
it is necessary to study the equations

z^{4}-L_{2m}^{2}z^{3}-2(L_{2m}^{2}-1)z^{2}+L_{2m}^{2}z+1=0

(38)

and

z^{4}-5F_{2m}^{2}z^{3}+2(5F_{2m}^{2}+1)z^{2}+5F_{2m}^{2}z+1=0.

(39)

Note that, if in (

) we set
a=-L_{2m}^{2}
then the quartic takes the form

z^{4}+az^{3}+2(a+1)z^{2}-az+1=0.

(40)

The four roots are given by

z_{1/2}=-\frac{a}{4}-\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{a^{2}-8a-16}\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{a^{2}-4a+a\sqrt{a^{2}-8a-16}}{2}}

(41)

and

z_{3/4}=-\frac{a}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{a^{2}-8a-16}\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{a^{2}-4a-a\sqrt{a^{2}-8a-16}}{2}}.

(42)

Finally, if we set
\tilde{t}=-(a+\sqrt{a^{2}-8a-16})/4
and
t=-(a-\sqrt{a^{2}-8a-16})/4
then

z_{1/2}=\tilde{t}\pm\sqrt{\tilde{t}^{2}+1}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad z_{3/4}=t\pm\sqrt{t^{2}+1}.

Among the four roots we choose the biggest root that satisfies the condition for convergence and arrive at the main theorem.

Theorem 7
.

For each
m\geq 0
we have the following series for
\pi
involving squared even-indexed Lucas numbers

\frac{\pi}{4}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{L_{2m(2n+1)}^{2}}{(t+\sqrt{t^{2}+1})^{2n+1}}

(43)

with

t=t(m)=\frac{L_{2m}^{2}+\sqrt{L_{2m}^{4}+8L_{2m}^{2}-16}}{4}.

(44)

Theorem
7
is a wonderful Lucas analogue of Castellanos’ equation (
3
).
For
m=0
,
t(0)=1+\sqrt{2}
and

\frac{\pi}{16}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{1}{(1+\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{4+2\sqrt{2}})^{2n+1}}.

(45)

For
m=1
,
t(1)=(9+\sqrt{137})/4
and

\frac{\pi}{16}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2n+1}\frac{L_{4n+2}^{2}4^{2n}}{(9+\sqrt{137}+3\sqrt{26+2\sqrt{137}})^{2n+1}}.

(46)

The analysis of the quartic (
39
) is very similar. With
b=-5F_{2m}^{2}
the relevant equation becomes

z^{4}+bz^{3}+2(1-b)z^{2}-bz+1=0.

(47)

The four roots are given by

z_{1/2}=-\frac{b}{4}-\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{b^{2}+8b-16}\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{b^{2}+4b+b\sqrt{b^{2}+8b-16}}{2}}

and

z_{3/4}=-\frac{b}{4}+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{b^{2}+8b-16}\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{b^{2}+4b-b\sqrt{b^{2}+8b-16}}{2}}.

These roots can be expressed as

z_{1/2}=\tilde{s}\pm\sqrt{\tilde{s}^{2}+1}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad z_{3/4}=s\pm\sqrt{s^{2}+1}

with
\tilde{s}=\tilde{s}(m)=-(b+\sqrt{b^{2}+8b-16})/4
and
s=s(m)=-(b-\sqrt{b^{2}+8b-16})/4
.
For
m=0
and
m=1
we have only complex roots:
\tilde{s}(0)=-i,s(0)=i
and
\tilde{s}(1)=(5-\sqrt{31}i)/4,s(1)=(5+\sqrt{31}i)/4

with
i=\sqrt{-1}
. For
m\geq 2
, we get the real roots but no root satisfies the condition
z>\lambda_{1}^{2}(L_{2m}/6)=\alpha^{4m}
.

In a similar manner other arctan arguments can be treated, yielding (if they exist) other series for
\pi

with squares of Fibonacci and Lucas numbers.

4
Similar series via subseries

Before closing the investigation, we present some additional results that are closely related to those from Section 2.
The next theorem will be useful to prove some subseries analogues of these results.

Theorem 8
.

For each real
x
with
x>1/3
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|>|\lambda_{1}(x)|
, we have the following identity

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4n+3}\frac{B_{4n+3}(x)}{z^{4n+3}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\ln\Big(\frac{z^{2}+2\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z-1}{z^{2}-2\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z-1}\Big)

(48)

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\frac{1}{4\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}}\arctan\Big(\frac{2\sqrt{9x^{2}-1}z}{z^{2}+1}\Big)

and

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4n+3}\frac{C_{4n+3}(x)}{z^{4n+3}}=\frac{1}{8}\ln\Big(\frac{z^{2}+6xz+1}{z^{2}-6xz+1}\Big)-\frac{1}{4}\arctan\Big(\frac{6xz}{z^{2}-1}\Big).

(49)

Proof.

From Chen’s paper
[
3
]
we have the relation

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{4n+3}}{4n+3}=\frac{1}{4}\ln\Big(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\Big)-\frac{1}{2}\arctan(z),\quad|z|<1.

(50)

The remainder of the proof is the same as in the proof of Theorem
1
.
∎

In the applications we can proceed as in the previous sections.
First, from (
48
) and (
49
) with
x=1
it is evident that
we can obtain some series for
\pi
with balancing and Lucas-balancing coefficients by
appropriately choosing the argument of the arctan function on the right.
The structure of the equations also shows that all series will start with a constant logarithmic term.
These arguments yield to the following exotic representations:

\frac{\pi}{3}=\ln\Big(\frac{46+4\sqrt{138}+8\sqrt{12}+4\sqrt{46}}{46+4\sqrt{138}-8\sqrt{12}-4\sqrt{46}}\Big)-16\sqrt{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4n+3}\frac{B_{4n+3}}{(2\sqrt{6}+\sqrt{23})^{4n+3}},

(51)

\frac{\pi}{3}=\ln\Big(\frac{56+12\sqrt{21}+18\sqrt{3}+12\sqrt{7}}{56+12\sqrt{21}-18\sqrt{3}-12\sqrt{7}}\Big)-8\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4n+3}\frac{C_{4n+3}}{(3\sqrt{3}+2\sqrt{7})^{4n+3}},

(52)

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{6}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln\Big(\frac{2+8\sqrt{3}+4\sqrt{2+8\sqrt{3}}+4(2-\sqrt{3})(4+\sqrt{2+8\sqrt{3}})}{2+8\sqrt{3}+4\sqrt{2+8\sqrt{3}}-4(2-\sqrt{3})(4+\sqrt{2+8\sqrt{3}})}\Big)

(53)

\displaystyle\qquad-16\sqrt{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4n+3}B_{4n+3}\Big(\frac{2-\sqrt{3}}{2\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{1+4\sqrt{3}}}\Big)^{4n+3},

and

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{6}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln\Big(\frac{32-8\sqrt{3}+12\sqrt{4-\sqrt{3}}+6(2-\sqrt{3})(3+2\sqrt{4-\sqrt{3}})}{32-8\sqrt{3}+12\sqrt{4-\sqrt{3}}-6(2-\sqrt{3})(3+2\sqrt{4-\sqrt{3}})}\Big)

(54)

\displaystyle\qquad-8\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4n+3}C_{4n+3}\Big(\frac{2-\sqrt{3}}{3+2\sqrt{4-\sqrt{3}}}\Big)^{4n+3}.

Working with Fibonacci and Lucas numbers we can derive the next series,
where each series is stated as a separate theorem without proof.

Theorem 9
.

For each integer
m\geq 0
the following series identity holds

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big(5F_{2m+1}^{2}+\sqrt{5}F_{2m+1}\sqrt{5F_{2m+1}^{2}+4}+2\Big)\Big)

(55)

\displaystyle\qquad-4\cdot\sqrt{5}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4n+3}F_{(2m+1)(4n+3)}\Big(\frac{2}{\sqrt{5}F_{2m+1}+\sqrt{5F_{2m+1}^{2}+4}}\Big)^{4n+3}.

The series in Theorem
9
is the Fibonacci subseries analogue of Castellanos’ series (
1
).

When
m=0
the expression reads as

\frac{\pi}{2}=\ln\Big(\frac{7+3\sqrt{5}}{2}\Big)-4\sqrt{5}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4n+3}F_{4n+3}\Big(\frac{2}{3+\sqrt{5}}\Big)^{4n+3},

which has the equivalent form

\frac{\pi}{8}=\ln(\alpha)-\sqrt{5}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4n+3}\frac{F_{4n+3}}{\alpha^{8n+6}}.

(56)

We conclude with three other examples exhibiting this structure and leave the derivations to the interested reader.

Theorem 10
.

For each integer
m\geq 0
the following expression for
\pi
is valid

\frac{\pi}{2}=\ln\Big(\frac{1}{2}\Big(L_{2m}^{2}+L_{2m}\sqrt{L_{2m}^{2}+4}+2\Big)\Big)-4\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4n+3}L_{2m(4n+3)}\Big(\frac{2}{L_{2m}+\sqrt{L_{2m}^{2}+4}}\Big)^{4n+3}.

(57)

Theorem 11
.

For each integer
m\geq 0
, the following identity for
\pi
is valid

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{6}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{(9+5\sqrt{3})L_{2m}^{2}+(3+\sqrt{3})L_{2m}\sqrt{(2+\sqrt{3})^{2}L_{2m}^{2}+4}+4}{(5+3\sqrt{3})L_{2m}^{2}+(1+\sqrt{3})L_{2m}\sqrt{(2+\sqrt{3})^{2}L_{2m}^{2}+4}+4}\right)

(58)

\displaystyle-4\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4n+3}L_{2m(4n+3)}\Big(\frac{2}{(2+\sqrt{3})L_{2m}+\sqrt{(2+\sqrt{3})^{2}L_{2m}^{2}+4}}\Big)^{4n+3}.

Theorem 12
.

For each integer
m\geq 0
, we have the following expression for
\pi

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{6}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\ln\left(\frac{(9+5\sqrt{3})\sqrt{5}\Big(\sqrt{5}F_{2m+1}^{2}+F_{2m+1}\sqrt{5F_{2m+1}^{2}+4(2-\sqrt{3})^{2}}\Big)+4}{(5+3\sqrt{3})\sqrt{5}\Big(\sqrt{5}F_{2m+1}^{2}+F_{2m+1}\sqrt{5F_{2m+1}^{2}+4(2-\sqrt{3})^{2}}\Big)+4}\right)

(59)

\displaystyle-4\sqrt{5}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4n+3}F_{(2m+1)(4n+3)}\Big(\frac{2(2-\sqrt{3})}{\sqrt{5}F_{2m+1}+\sqrt{5F_{2m+1}^{2}+4(2-\sqrt{3})^{2}}}\Big)^{4n+3}.

5
Conclusion

Castellanos has derived curious series for
\pi
involving odd Fibonacci and odd squared Fibonacci numbers.
The goal of the paper was to provide complements of his work. This was achieved by studying some infinite series involving
balancing and Lucas-balancing polynomials. We have also indicated how more series representations of this nature can be
established.

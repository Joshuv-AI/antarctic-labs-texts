Port-Hamiltonian Discontinuous Galerkin Finite Element Methods
N. Kumar, J.J.W. van der Vegt, H.J. Zwart

Abstract
A port-Hamiltonian (pH) system formulation is a geometrical notion used to formulate conservation laws for various physical systems. The distributed parameter port-Hamiltonian formulation models infinite dimensional Hamiltonian dynamical systems that have a non-zero energy flow through the boundaries. In this paper we propose a novel framework for discontinuous Galerkin (DG) discretizations of pH-systems. Linking DG methods with pH-systems gives rise to compatible structure preserving finite element discretizations along with flexibility in terms of geometry and function spaces of the variables involved. Moreover, the port-Hamiltonian formulation makes boundary ports explicit, which makes the choice of structure and power preserving numerical fluxes easier. We state the Discontinuous Finite Element Stokes-Dirac structure with a power preserving coupling between elements, which provides the mathematical framework for a large class of pH discontinuous Galerkin discretizations. We also provide an a priori error analysis for the port-Hamiltonian discontinuous Galerkin Finite Element Method (pH-DGFEM). The port-Hamiltonian discontinuous Galerkin finite element method is demonstrated for the scalar wave equation showing optimal rates of convergence.

Introduction

This paper defines a novel elementwise discontinuous finite element Stokes-Dirac structure, which provides the mathematical framework for elementwise discontinuous Galerkin (DG) discretizations of port-Hamiltonian (pH) systems. The pH-system formulation,
[
18
,
25
,
27
,
33

[
18
,
33
,
32
]
. These distributed parameter pH-systems comprise of an interconnection structure called Stokes-Dirac structure and an energy functional called Hamiltonian. The Stokes-Dirac structure gives us an effective way to express the behaviour of a system through pairs of input-output variables (whose product gives the power) at the boundary. This structural property of pH-systems makes them very suitable for the modeling and analysis of interconnected multi-physics systems. Due to the varied complexities of these multi-physics systems (inter-domain coupling, non-linearities, etc.), the interest in numerical discretization techniques for pH-systems is increasing. In particular, numerical techniques that can preserve the mathematical structure (power balance) of the system upon discretization are important.

Various approaches have been followed to obtain structure preserving discretizations of port-Hamiltonian systems. Finite difference and model order reduction methods have been proposed by Lopezlena et al.,
[
23
]
, and Trenchant et al.,
[
31
]
. Golo et al.
[
15
,
30
]
, proposed a method which is effective in preserving the structure of a pH-system on discretization, but this method has restrictive compatibility conditions and becomes complicated for higher dimensions.

Based on Discrete Exterior Calculus,
[
17
]
, Seslija et al.
[
29
]
, proposed a discrete formulation of pH conservation laws, along with a consistent approximation of the closure equations, which results in a compatible discretization. In
[
6
]
, Cardoso-Ribeiro et al., have proposed the partitioned Finite Element Method (pFEM) for pH-systems with 2 conservation laws, e.g., shallow water equations, acoustic wave equations. The pFEM for structure preserving discretizations of pH-systems solves one conservation law in weak form and the other one in strong form. Serhani et al.,
[
28
]
, have extended this pFEM to damped pH-systems. Kotyczka et al.,
[
19
,
20
]
proposed a mixed finite element discretization based on a general pH weak formulation. In this approach, the weak form of the Dirac structure on each element is defined as the basis for a mixed-Galerkin pH formulation. Using a power-preserving interconnection, a structure preserving discretization of the entire system is obtained.

The consistent approximation of the closure equation in the Discrete Exterior Calculus approach and the weak form in the partitioned-FEM and mixed-FEM methods provide more flexibility in terms of consistency and stability. However, these methods are restricted in terms of mesh geometry and the flexibility in the choice of function spaces chosen for the variables in the conservation laws. In this paper, using the concept of an elementwise Dirac structure and power preserving interconnection, we move a step forward by proposing a discontinuous Galerkin (DG) method for structure preserving discretizations of pH-systems.

The flexibility in terms of mesh geometry (e.g., hanging nodes) and function spaces allowing hp-adaptive methods, which DG methods
[
10
,
11
,
16
,
26
]
provide, makes them a rich environment for the discretization of various physical systems. DG methods use the basic idea of solving the PDE elementwise and then through appropriate numerical fluxes at the shared boundaries of the elements a stable discretization is ensured for the whole system. However, most DG methods are unable to preserve the mathematical or Hamiltonian structure of the PDEs (if the PDEs possesses one). Hence, linking DG methods with the concept of pH-systems has a two fold advantage. On one hand the pH formulation makes the boundary port-variables explicit, so choosing a numerical flux that preserves the mathematical structure for DG discretizations becomes easier. On the other hand DG discretizations provide a flexibile (in terms of mesh geometry and parallel computing) structure preserving discretization of pH-systems.

Since the power preserving interconnection of two Dirac structures again gives a Dirac structure,
[
7
,
18
]
, the elementwise DG formulation is extended to the whole manifold using a power preserving interconnection structure. We also provide an a priori error analysis of the port-Hamiltonian DG discretization.

We state the power preserving coupling between finite elements in Section

3.1
Dirac structure

The concept of a Dirac structure, as introduced in
[
8
,
9
]
, is the geometrical notion formalizing general power-conserving interconnections, thereby allowing the Hamiltonian formulation of various kinds of combined systems.
The definition of a Dirac structure is as follows. Let
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{E}
be linear spaces, with a bilinear operation :
\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. The bilinear product is denoted as
\langle e|f\rangle,f\in\mathcal{F},e\in\mathcal{E}
. We call this bilinear pairing a non-degenerate duality pairing if
\langle e|f\rangle=0
for all
f\in\mathcal{F}
implies
e=0
and if
\langle e|f\rangle=0
for all
e\in\mathcal{E}
implies
f=0
. By symmetrizing the pairing we get a symmetric bilinear pairing
\langle\langle\ ,\ \rangle\rangle
on
\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{E}
, with values in
\mathbb{R}
given by

\displaystyle\langle\langle(f_{1},e_{1}),(f_{2},e_{2})\rangle\rangle=\langle e_{1}|f_{2}\rangle+\langle e_{2}|f_{1}\rangle,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ (f_{i},e_{i})\in\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{E}.

(14)

A Dirac structure is a linear subspace
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{F}\times\mathcal{E}
such that
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}^{\perp}
with
{\perp}
denoting the orthogonal complement with respect to the bilinear pairing
\langle\langle\ ,\ \rangle\rangle
,
[
32
]
.

From the above definition it follows that for any
(f,e)
in the Dirac structure
\mathcal{D}
we have,

\displaystyle 0=\langle\langle(f,e),(f,e)\rangle\rangle=2\langle e|f\rangle.

(15)

Thus, if
(f,e)
is a pair of power variables, where
\langle e|f\rangle
equals the power, then the condition
(f,e)\in\mathcal{D}
implies power conservation
\langle e|f\rangle=0
.

K_{L}\neq K_{R}

\Gamma_{i}=\bar{K}_{L}\cap\bar{K}_{R}\neq\emptyset
. Likewise, we denote the set of exterior faces in the tessellation with
\mathcal{F}_{o}
. Thus,
\Gamma_{o}\in\mathcal{F}_{o}
if and only if

such that
\Gamma_{0}=\bar{K}\cap\partial\Omega\neq\emptyset

\mathcal{F}_{h}=\mathcal{F}_{i}\cup\mathcal{F}_{o}
. In the tessellation we have
\mathcal{\dim}(K)=\mathcal{\dim}(\Omega)
, which means

is an
n
-cell for an
n
-dimensional oriented manifold
\Omega
. Let
\Delta(K)
be the set of all subelements of element
K
, e.g. the element, its faces, edges and vertices. By the definition of an orientable manifold it follows that each element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
inherits the orientation of the manifold
\Omega
such that all elements have a positive Jacobian. Following this orientation all internal faces connect to a right and a left element, whose orientation is in the opposite direction when viewed locally from the face. As we focus on simplices in this paper, we will refer to the elements
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
as simplices and
\Delta(K)
to be the set of all subsimplices of the simplex
K
. Note that the set of all subsimplices of simplex
K
,
\Delta(K)
, also includes the simplex
K

For these polynomial differential form spaces we define the broken-polynomial differential form spaces as

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{k}\mathcal{T}_{h})

with

4.3
Duality product on element
K
for discrete dual port-variable pairs

The port-variables have a well-defined non-degenerate duality product (
1
). We extend the duality product now to the discrete dual port-variable pairs. On each subsimplex

, we have a duality product between

and
\mathrm{tr}_{K,f}f^{p}_{h}\in\mathrm{tr}_{K,f}F_{p}(K)
. Summing over all
f\in\Delta(K)
and using the fact that
\{E_{p}(f),\mathrm{tr}_{K,f}F_{p}(K)\}
are dual pairs we have

\displaystyle\langle e^{p}_{h}\mid f^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}=(-1)^{p(n-p)}\langle f^{p}_{h}\mid e^{p}_{h}\rangle=(-1)^{p(n-p)}\underset{f\in\Delta(K)}{\sum}\int_{f}\mathrm{tr}_{K,f}f^{p}_{h}\wedge e^{p}_{h,f},

where

, as explained in (

).

Using Theorem 4.8 in
[
4
]
, we can prove that if

\displaystyle\langle e^{p}_{h}\mid f^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}=(-1)^{p(n-p)}\underset{f\in\Delta(K)}{\sum}\int_{f}\mathrm{tr}_{K,f}f^{p}_{h}\wedge e^{p}_{h,f}=0\quad\forall e^{p}_{h,f}\in E_{p}(f),

(53)

then
f^{p}_{h}=0
.

\displaystyle\langle e^{q}_{h}\mid f^{q}_{h}\rangle_{K}=\underset{f\in\Delta(K)}{\sum}\int_{f}\mathrm{tr}_{K,f}e^{q}_{h}\wedge f^{q}_{h,f}=0\qquad\forall f^{q}_{h,f}\in F_{q}(f),

4.4
Duality product on subelements of element
K
for discrete dual port-variable pairs

The trace operator
\mathrm{tr}_{f,\partial f}
restricts the spaces defined on the subsimplices
f\in\Delta(K)
to the boundaries
\partial f
. For example: A
(k-1)
-form,
\lambda_{h,f}
defined on a subsimplex
f\in\Delta(K)
with
k\leq\dim(f)\leq n
, is restricted on the boundary
\partial f
as

\displaystyle\lambda^{k}_{h,f}(\partial f)=\mathrm{tr}_{f,\partial f}\lambda^{k}_{h,f}.

(55)

We stated the duality product over the element
K
as the sum of duality products over the subsimplices
f\in\Delta(K)
. Similarly, we state the duality product over the boundaries of
K
as the sum of duality products over the boundaries
\partial f
of
f\in\Delta(K)
, which are denoted as
\partial\Delta(K)
.

Considering the theory of port-Hamiltonian systems, at the boundaries
\partial\Omega
of the oriented manifold
\Omega
we have using the Dirac structure (
20
) duality between the trace of efforts at the boundaries. Hence, at the boundaries
\partial f
of each
f\in\Delta(K)
we have to express the duality between
E_{p}(\partial\Delta(K))
and
E_{q}(\partial\Delta(K))
. Since the spaces
E_{p}(\partial\Delta(K))
and
E_{q}(\partial\Delta(K))
will in general not have the same dimension we need to do this in two steps. First, we define a bilinear product between
E_{p}(\partial\Delta(K))
and
E_{q}(\partial\Delta(K))
. Next, we will introduce a conversion operator
Q
that accounts for the differences in the dimension of
E_{p}(\partial\Delta(K))
and
E_{q}(\partial\Delta(K))
.

Using (
55
) and Theorems 4.15 and 4.21 in
[
4
]
, these two spaces at the boundary
\partial f
of

are,

\displaystyle E_{p}(\partial\Delta(K))

\displaystyle=\underset{f\in\Delta(K),\mathrm{\dim}(f)\in[p,r+p-1]}{\oplus}\mathrm{tr}_{f,\partial f}E_{p}(f)

(56)

\displaystyle E_{q}(\partial\Delta(K))

\displaystyle=\underset{f\in\Delta(K),\mathrm{\dim}(f)\in[p,r+p-1]}{\oplus}\mathrm{tr}_{K,\partial f}E_{q}(K)

Using the relation
p+q=n+1
, these spaces for Case 1 and Case 2 then become

Case 1:

\displaystyle E_{p}(\partial\Delta(K))

\displaystyle=\underset{f\in\Delta(K),\mathrm{\dim}(f)\in[p,r+p-1]}{\oplus}\mathrm{tr}_{f,\partial f}\mathcal{P}^{-}_{r+p-\mathrm{\dim}(f)}\Lambda^{\mathrm{\dim}(f)-p}(f),

(57)

\displaystyle E_{q}(\partial\Delta(K))

\displaystyle=\underset{f\in\Delta(K),\mathrm{\dim}(f)\in[p,r+p-1]}{\oplus}\mathrm{tr}_{K,\partial f}\mathcal{P}_{r+1}\Lambda^{n-q}(K).

Case 2:

\displaystyle E_{p}(\partial\Delta(K))

\displaystyle=\underset{f\in\Delta(K),\mathrm{\dim}(f)\in[p,r+p-1]}{\oplus}\mathrm{tr}_{f,\partial f}\mathcal{P}_{r+p-\mathrm{\dim}(f)-1}\Lambda^{\mathrm{\dim}(f)-p}(f),

(58)

\displaystyle E_{q}(\partial\Delta(K))

\displaystyle=\underset{f\in\Delta(K),\mathrm{\dim}(f)\in[p,r+p-1]}{\oplus}\mathrm{tr}_{K,\partial f}\mathcal{P}^{-}_{r}\Lambda^{n-q}(K).

The bilinear product between

is stated as

\displaystyle\langle u^{q}_{h}\mid u^{p}_{h}\rangle_{\partial K}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathrm{tr}e^{q}_{h}\mid\mathrm{tr}e^{p}_{h}\rangle_{\partial K}

(59)

\displaystyle=\underset{f\in\Delta(K),\mathrm{\dim}(f)\in[p,r+p-1]}{\sum}\int_{\partial f}\mathrm{tr}_{K,\partial f}e^{q}_{h}\wedge\mathrm{tr}_{f,\partial f}e^{p}_{h,f},

with

and
e^{p}_{h}
can be represented as

f_{1},\cdots,f_{m}\in\Delta(K)
as explained in (
39
).

In general the function spaces
E_{p}(\partial\Delta(K))
and
E_{q}(\partial\Delta(K))
do not have the same dimensions. This poses the crucial problem for the bilinear product (
59
) to be
non-degenerate. Hence, in general (
59
) is a bilinear product, but not a duality product. To convert this bilinear product into a duality product we will use a linear (surjective) operator
Q
. We will discuss the general construction of the operator
Q
in the next subsection. Note that from now on with an abuse of notation we will write
\partial\Delta(K)
as
\partial K
.

4.4.1
The conversion operator Q

Let us take two finite-dimensional linear spaces
X(\partial K)
and
Y(\partial K)
such that
\mathrm{\dim}(X(\partial K))\geq\mathrm{\dim}(Y(\partial K))
. Suppose that, as in (
59
), we have the bilinear product between
X(\partial K)
and
Y(\partial K)
,
\langle x\mid y\rangle_{\partial K}
,
x\in X(\partial K)
and
y\in Y(\partial K)
.

For fixed
x\in X(\partial K)
,
q_{x}(y)=\langle x\mid y\rangle_{\partial K}
, is a linear map from
Y(\partial K)
to
\mathbb{R}
. Thus, there exists a unique
{y}^{\ast}\in Y^{\ast}(\partial K)
, (
Y^{\ast}(\partial K)
is the dual function space to
Y(\partial K)
) such that
q_{x}(y)=\langle y^{\ast}\mid y\rangle_{{Y(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times Y(\partial K)}
.

So, to
x\in X(\partial K)
we have uniquely assigned a
{y}^{\ast}\in Y^{\ast}(\partial K)
. This we can do for all
x\in X(\partial K)
, and we define the operator
Q
,

\displaystyle Q:X(\partial K)\rightarrow Y(\partial K)^{\ast},

(60)

such that
Q(x)=y^{\ast}
, and thus

\displaystyle q_{x}(y)=\langle x\mid y\rangle_{\partial K}=\langle Q(x)\mid y\rangle_{{Y(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times Y(\partial K)}.

(61)

Since,
{y}^{\ast}
is unique the operator
Q
is well-defined. Also, for all
y\in Y(\partial K)

\displaystyle q_{\alpha x+\beta\tilde{x}}(y)

\displaystyle=\langle\alpha x+\beta\tilde{x}\mid y\rangle_{\partial K}

(62)

\displaystyle=\alpha\langle x\mid y\rangle_{\partial K}+\beta\langle\tilde{x}\mid y\rangle_{\partial K}

\displaystyle=\alpha\langle Q(x)\mid y\rangle_{{Y(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times Y(\partial K)}+\beta\langle Q(\tilde{x})\mid y\rangle_{{Y(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times Y(\partial K)}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle q_{\alpha x+\beta\tilde{x}}(y)

\displaystyle=\langle Q(\alpha x+\beta\tilde{x})\mid y\rangle_{{Y(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times Y(\partial K)}.

(63)

Thus,
Q
is a linear operator.

Definition 4.4.1
.

The bilinear product
\langle x\mid y\rangle_{\partial K}
is defined to be half-degenerate if for any
y\in Y(\partial K)
the assertion:
\langle x\mid y\rangle_{\partial K}=0\ \forall x\in X(\partial K)
implies that
y=0
.

Lemma 4.4.1
.

The operator
Q
of (
61
) is surjective if and only if the bilinear product
\langle x\mid y\rangle_{\partial K}
is half-degenerate.

Proof.

Assume that
Q
is surjective and let
y_{0}\in Y(\partial K)
be such that
\langle x\mid y_{0}\rangle_{\partial K}=0,\quad\forall x\in X(\partial K)
. This gives that
\langle y^{\ast}\mid y_{0}\rangle_{{Y(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times Y(\partial K)}=0,\ \forall y^{\ast}\in{Y(\partial K)}^{\ast}
, which implies
y_{0}=0
.

Now we prove the other implication. Assume that
Q
is not surjective. Since the range of
Q
is a linear subspace of
{Y(\partial K)}^{\ast}
and
Y(\partial K)
has the dual
{Y(\partial K)}^{\ast}
, there exists a non-zero
y_{0}\in Y(\partial K)
such that
\langle Q(x)\mid y_{0}\rangle_{{Y(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times Y(\partial K)}=0,\forall x\in X(\partial K)
. By (
61
) we see that
\langle x\mid y_{0}\rangle_{\partial K}=0,\forall x\in X(\partial K)
for a nonzero
y_{0}
, providing the contradiction.
∎

As the operator
Q
converts the bilinear product on
X(\partial K)\times Y(\partial K)
into a duality product on
{Y(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times Y(\partial K)
, we can define dual operators with respect to the bilinear form
\langle\cdot\mid\cdot\rangle_{\partial K}
. Let
S
be a linear operator, that maps the space
X(K)
to the space
X(\partial K)
, i.e.,
S:X(K)\rightarrow X(\partial K)
. So for
x=S(x_{h})
, with
x_{h}\in X(K)
, and
y\in Y(\partial K)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle x\mid y\rangle_{\partial K}=\langle S(x_{h})\mid y\rangle_{\partial K}.

(64)

Using the operator
Q
, (
61
),

\displaystyle\langle S(x_{h})\mid y\rangle_{\partial K}

\displaystyle=\langle QS(x_{h})\mid y\rangle_{{Y(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times Y(\partial K)}

(65)

\displaystyle=\langle x_{h}\mid(QS)^{\ast}(y)\rangle_{X(K)\times{X(K)}^{\ast}}.

Here,
(QS)^{\ast}:Y(\partial K)\rightarrow{X(K)}^{\ast}
, denotes the (standard) dual of
QS
.

Similarly, let
T
be an operator that maps the space
Y(K)
to the space
Y(\partial K)
, i.e.,

T:Y(K)\rightarrow Y(\partial K)
. So for
y=T(y_{h})
, with
y_{h}\in Y(K)
, and
x\in X(\partial K)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle x\mid y\rangle_{\partial K}=\langle x\mid T(y_{h})\rangle_{\partial K}.

(66)

Using the operator
Q
, (
61
),

\displaystyle\langle x\mid T(y_{h})\rangle_{\partial K}

\displaystyle=\langle Q(x)\mid T(y_{h})\rangle_{{Y(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times Y(\partial K)}

(67)

\displaystyle=\langle T^{\ast}Q(x)\mid y_{h}\rangle_{{Y(K)}^{\ast}\times Y(K)}.

Here,
(T^{\ast}Q):X(\partial K)\rightarrow{Y(K)}^{\ast}
.

4.4.2
Duality on the boundaries of
K

We assume that
\mathrm{\dim}(E_{q}(\partial K))\geq\mathrm{\dim}(E_{p}(\partial K))

\mathrm{\dim}(E_{q}(\partial K))\leq\mathrm{\dim}(E_{p}(\partial K))
, but the analysis is similar for this case.

On
E_{q}(\partial K)\times E_{p}(\partial K)
we have the bilinear product (
59
). Let
Q:E_{q}(\partial K)\rightarrow{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}
be the mapping associated to this product, see (
60
) and (
61

The trace operator
\mathrm{tr}_{p}
is the map
\mathrm{tr}_{p}:E_{p}(K)\rightarrow E_{p}(\partial K)
and the trace operator
\mathrm{tr}_{q}
is the map
\mathrm{tr}_{q}:E_{q}(K)\rightarrow E_{q}(\partial K)

e^{p}_{h}\in E_{p}(K)
and

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{tr}_{q}e^{q}_{h}\mid\mathrm{tr}_{p}e^{p}_{h}\rangle_{\partial K}

\displaystyle=\langle Q(\mathrm{tr}_{q}e^{q}_{h})\mid\mathrm{tr}_{p}e^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathrm{tr}^{\ast}_{p}(Q(\mathrm{tr}_{q}e^{q}_{h}))\mid e^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(K)}.

Here,
(\mathrm{tr}^{\ast}_{p}\circ Q):E_{q}(\partial K)\rightarrow E_{p}(K)^{\ast}
.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\langle\mathrm{tr}_{q}e^{q}_{h}\mid\mathrm{tr}_{p}e^{p}_{h}\rangle_{\partial K}

\displaystyle=\langle Q(\mathrm{tr}_{q}e^{q}_{h})\mid\mathrm{tr}_{p}e^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}

\displaystyle=\langle e^{q}_{h}\mid(Q\mathrm{tr}_{q})^{\ast}(\mathrm{tr}_{p}e^{p}_{h})\rangle_{E_{q}(K)\times{E_{q}(K)}^{\ast}}.

Here,
(Q\circ\mathrm{tr}_{q})^{\ast}:E_{p}(\partial K)\rightarrow E_{q}(K)^{\ast}
.

For
\mathrm{tr}_{p}e^{p}_{h}\in E_{p}(\partial K)
and
\mathrm{tr}_{q}e^{q}_{h}\in E_{q}(\partial K)
with
\mathrm{\dim}(E_{q}(\partial K))\geq\mathrm{\dim}(E_{p}(\partial K))
the bilinear product
\langle\mathrm{tr}_{q}e^{q}_{h}\mid\mathrm{tr}_{p}e^{p}_{h}\rangle_{\partial K}
is half-degenerate. So, using Lemma
4.4.1
the operator

is surjective.

Lemma 4.4.2
.

For
e^{p}_{h},\nu^{p}_{h}\in E_{p}(K),\nu^{p}_{h,f}\in E_{p}(f)
and
e^{q}_{h},\nu^{q}_{h}\in E_{q}(K)
with
p+q=n+1
, the integration by parts rule (
13
) for an element
K
gives

\langle d_{pq}e^{q}_{h}\mid\nu^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}=(-1)^{p}\langle e^{q}_{h}\mid d_{qp}\nu^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle\mathrm{tr}_{q}e^{q}_{h}\mid\mathrm{tr}_{p}\nu^{p}_{h}\rangle_{\partial K}.

(70)

Proof.

For
e^{q}_{h}\in E_{q}(K),\nu^{p}_{h}\in E_{p}(K)
, using the exterior derivative operator
d_{pq}(e^{q}_{h}(K))\in F_{p}(K)
as the exterior derivative operator operator
d_{pq}
is defined as the map
d_{pq}:E_{q}(K)\rightarrow F_{p}(K)
. Using the duality product between
E_{p}(K)
and
F_{p}(K)
, (
40
) or (

), we write

\displaystyle\langle d_{pq}e^{q}_{h}\mid\nu^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}

\displaystyle=\underset{f\in\Delta(K),p\leq\mathrm{\dim}(f)\leq r+p-1}{\sum}\int_{f}\mathrm{tr}_{K,f}(de^{q}_{h})\wedge\nu^{p}_{h,f},

(71)

where
\nu^{p}_{h}
can be represented as
\nu^{p}_{h}=(\nu^{p}_{h,f_{1}},\cdots,\nu^{p}_{h,f_{m}}),f_{1},\cdots,f_{m}\in\Delta(K)
, as explained in (
39

As the exterior derivative operator
d
and the trace operator
\mathrm{tr}
are commutative, we can simplify the above as

\displaystyle\langle d_{pq}e^{q}_{h}\mid\nu^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}

\displaystyle=\underset{f\in\Delta(K),p\leq\mathrm{\dim}(f)\leq r+p-1}{\sum}\int_{f}d(\mathrm{tr}_{K,f}e^{q}_{h})\wedge\nu^{p}_{h,f}.

(72)

Using (

) and
E_{q}(K)\subset\Lambda^{n-k}(K)
in the first step and then (

) we obtain

\displaystyle\langle d_{pq}e^{q}_{h}\mid\nu^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}

\displaystyle=\underset{f\in\Delta(K),p\leq\mathrm{\dim}(f)\leq r+p-1}{\sum}\Bigg((-1)^{p}\Big(\int_{f}\mathrm{tr}_{K,f}e^{q}_{h}\wedge d\nu^{p}_{h,f}\Big)

(73)

\displaystyle+\int_{\partial f}\mathrm{tr}_{K,\partial f}e^{q}_{h}\wedge\mathrm{tr}_{f,\partial f}\nu^{p}_{h,f}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p}\langle e^{q}_{h}\mid d_{qp}\nu^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle\mathrm{tr}_{q}e^{q}_{h}\mid\mathrm{tr}_{p}\nu^{p}_{h}\rangle_{\partial K}.

∎

Definition 4.4.2

(Extended bilinear form)
.

Let

be an element of

\displaystyle\langle\langle(f^{p}_{h},f^{q}_{h},e^{p}_{h},e^{q}_{h}),(f^{\prime p}_{h},f^{\prime q}_{h},e^{\prime p}_{h},e^{\prime q}_{h})\rangle\rangle_{h}

(74)

\displaystyle=\langle e^{\prime p}_{h}\mid f^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle e^{p}_{h}\mid f^{\prime p}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle e^{\prime q}_{h}\mid f^{q}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle e^{q}_{h}\mid f^{\prime q}_{h}\rangle_{K}

4.5
Discontinuous Finite Element Dirac structure

In the discontinuous finite element discretization we consider elements to be independent from each other. The interaction occurs only through common boundaries. We define now a Dirac structure, called generalized Stokes-Dirac structure that is suitable for a discontinuous finite element discretization. We also state the interconnection between adjacent elements in the spatial domain using a Dirac structure at the element boundaries, called interconnection Dirac structure. This will provide the mathematical framework for defining port-Hamiltonian discontinuous Galerkin discretizations.

4.5.1
The generalized Stokes-Dirac structure

The polynomial differential form spaces on which the port-variables are projected are denoted as

\displaystyle F_{p,q}

\displaystyle:=F_{p}(K)\times F_{q}(K)\times E_{p}(\partial K)\times E_{q}(\partial K),

(75)

\displaystyle E_{p,q}

\displaystyle:=E_{p}(K)\times E_{q}(K)\times E_{q}(\partial K)\times E_{p}(\partial K).

For port variables
e^{p}_{h}\in E_{p}(K),e^{q}_{h}\in E_{q}(K),f^{p}_{h}\in F_{p}(K),f^{q}_{h}\in F_{q}(K)
and the input and output port boundary pairs
\{y^{p}_{h},u^{p}_{h}\}\in E_{p}(\partial K),\{y^{q}_{h},u^{q}_{h}\}\in E_{q}(\partial K)
, we have the following bilinear product

\displaystyle\langle e^{p}_{h}\mid f^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle e^{q}_{h}\mid f^{q}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle u^{q}_{h}\mid y^{p}_{h}\rangle_{\partial K}+\langle y^{q}_{h}\mid u^{p}_{h}\rangle_{\partial K}.

(76)

However since,
\mathrm{\dim}(E_{p}(\partial K))\neq\mathrm{\dim}(E_{q}(\partial K))
, the bilinear product (
76
) is degenerated. As explained in subsection 4.3.4 we use the linear (surjective) operator
Q
, (
61
) to transform this bilinear product into a non-degenerate duality product. Recall that we assume
\mathrm{\dim}(E_{q}(\partial K))\geq\mathrm{\dim}(E_{p}(\partial K))
. Using the abstract operator
Q
, (
61
), we can map the boundary port variable space
E_{q}(\partial K)
to the dual of
E_{p}(\partial K)
, while maintaining the bilinear product. That is, for each
u^{q}_{h}\in E_{q}(\partial K)
, we have an element
{u^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\in{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}
, such that
Q(u^{q}_{h})={u^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}
and
\langle u^{q}_{h}\mid y^{p}_{h}\rangle_{\partial K}=\langle Q(u^{q}_{h})\mid y^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}
. Using this we can rewrite the bilinear product (
76
) as

\displaystyle\langle e^{p}_{h}\mid f^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle e^{q}_{h}\mid f^{q}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle u^{q}_{h}\mid y^{p}_{h}\rangle_{\partial K}+\langle y^{q}_{h}\mid u^{p}_{h}\rangle_{\partial K}

(77)

\displaystyle=\langle e^{p}_{h}\mid f^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle e^{q}_{h}\mid f^{q}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle Q(u^{q}_{h})\mid y^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}

\displaystyle+\langle Q(y^{q}_{h})\mid u^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}

\displaystyle=\langle e^{p}_{h}\mid f^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle e^{q}_{h}\mid f^{q}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle{u^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\mid y^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}

\displaystyle+\langle{y^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\mid u^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}.

We replace the polynomial differential form spaces, (
75
), with the (abstract) spaces

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{p,q}

\displaystyle:=F_{p}(K)\times F_{q}(K)\times E_{p}(\partial K)\times{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast},

(78)

\displaystyle E^{\prime}_{p,q}

\displaystyle:=E_{p}(K)\times E_{q}(K)\times{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K).

Definition 4.5.1
.

For the port variables
e^{p}_{h}\in E_{p}(K),e^{q}_{h}\in E_{q}(K),f^{p}_{h}\in F_{p}(K),f^{q}_{h}\in F_{q}(K)
, the input and output port boundary pairs
\{y^{p}_{h},u^{p}_{h}\}\in E_{p}(\partial K),\ \{{y^{p}_{h}}^{\ast},{u^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\}\in{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}
, we have the following duality product on
F^{\prime}_{p,q}\times E^{\prime}_{p,q}

\displaystyle\langle e^{p}_{h}\mid f^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle e^{q}_{h}\mid f^{q}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle{y^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\mid u^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}+\langle{u^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\mid y^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}.

(79)

Using the duality product (
79
) as the power we can now define the generalized Stokes-Dirac structure.

Definition 4.5.2

(Generalized Stokes-Dirac Structure)
.

For
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we define the subspace
D_{h}
of
F^{\prime}_{p,q}\times E^{\prime}_{p,q}
as

\displaystyle D_{h}=\Bigg\{(f^{p}_{h},f^{q}_{h},y^{p}_{h},{y^{p}_{h}}^{\ast},e^{p}_{h},e^{q}_{h},{u^{p}_{h}}^{\ast},u^{p}_{h})\in F^{\prime}_{p,q}\times E^{\prime}_{p,q}\mid

(80)

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{c}f^{p}_{h}\\

where

,

and

as defined in (

) and (

We now prove that the generalized Stokes-Dirac structure given in the definition above is indeed a Dirac structure. For that we use the following lemma. Although the statement in the following lemma is well known, it is difficult to find the proof, so we state the proof as well.

Lemma 4.5.1
.

Consider the finite dimensional linear spaces
F_{h}
and
E_{h}
, having a non-degenerate bilinear product defined on
F_{h}\times E_{h}
. A subspace
D_{h}\in F_{h}\times E_{h}
is a Dirac structure if and only if the power is conserved and
\mathrm{\dim}(D_{h})=\mathrm{\dim}(F_{h})
.

Proof.

Given,
\langle e\mid f\rangle=0,\forall(f,e)\in D_{h}
and
\mathrm{\dim}(D_{h})=\mathrm{\dim}(F_{h})
, we have to prove that
D_{h}
is a Dirac structure, i.e.,
D_{h}=(D_{h})^{\perp}
. And, conversely if
D_{h}
is a Dirac structure, i.e.,
D_{h}={D_{h}}^{\perp}
, we have to prove that
\mathrm{\dim}(D_{h})=\mathrm{\dim}(F_{h})
.

Suppose
\forall(f,e)\in D_{h}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle\langle(f,e),(f,e)\rangle\rangle_{h}=\langle e\mid f\rangle+\langle e\mid f\rangle=2\langle e\mid f\rangle=0,

(81)

then using (
15
)
D_{h}\subseteq(D_{h})^{\perp}
. Let
n
be the dimension of
F_{h}
and let
\{[f_{1},e_{1}]^{T},...,

[f_{n},e_{n}]^{T}\}
be a basis of
D_{h}
. For
[\tilde{f},\tilde{e}]^{T}\in D^{\perp}_{h}
it implies

\displaystyle\langle\langle(f,e),(\tilde{f},\tilde{e})\rangle\rangle_{h}=\langle e_{k}\mid\tilde{f}\rangle+\langle\tilde{e}\mid f_{k}\rangle=0,\qquad k=1,\cdots,n.

(82)

This gives
n
independent linear equations to be solved in a
2n
dimensional space. This implies that the solution set is of
2n-n=n
dimensions. So,
\mathrm{\dim}(D^{\perp}_{h})=n
.
Since,
\mathrm{\dim}(D_{h})=\mathrm{\dim}(E_{h})=\mathrm{\dim}(F_{h})=n
and
D_{h}\subseteq(D_{h})^{\perp}
, we have
D_{h}=(D_{h})^{\perp}
.

Conversely, if
D_{h}
is a Dirac structure, then
D_{h}=(D_{h})^{\perp}
, and (
15
) implies in particular that

\displaystyle\langle e\mid f\rangle=0,\quad\forall(f,e)\in D_{h}.

(83)

Let
\{[f_{1},e_{1}]^{T},...,[f_{m},e_{m}]^{T}\}
be a basis of
D_{h}
, for
[\tilde{f},\tilde{e}]^{T}\in(D_{h})^{\perp}
it implies

\displaystyle\langle e_{k}\mid\tilde{f}\rangle+\langle\tilde{e}\mid f_{k}\rangle=0,\qquad k=1,\cdots,m.

(84)

This gives
m
independent linear equations to be solved in a
2n
dimensional space. This implies that the solution set is of
2n-m
dimensions. So,
\mathrm{\dim}(D^{\perp})=2n-m
. Since,
D_{h}=(D_{h})^{\perp}
, we have
2n-m=n
, or
m=n
. This proves that
\mathrm{\dim}(D_{h})^{\perp}=\mathrm{\dim}(D_{h})=\mathrm{\dim}(F_{h})=n
.
∎

Lemma
4.5.1
states the conditions for a linear subspace to be a Dirac structure. Using this lemma we prove now that the subspace (

, we state two more lemmas, which will be used to prove that the structure (

Lemma 4.5.2
.

For port variables

, input and output port boundary pairs
\{y^{p}_{h},u^{p}_{h}\}\in E_{p}(\partial K),\ \{{y^{p}_{h}}^{\ast},{u^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\}\in{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}

\displaystyle f^{p}_{h}

\displaystyle=((-1)^{r_{1}}d_{pq}+(-1)^{r_{1}+q}\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}_{p}^{\ast}Q(\mathrm{tr_{q}}))e^{q}_{h}+(-1)^{r_{1}+q}\mathrm{tr}_{p}^{\ast}{u^{p}_{h}}^{\ast},

(85)

\displaystyle f^{q}_{h}

\displaystyle=(d_{qp}+(-1)^{p}\frac{1}{2}(Q\mathrm{tr}_{q})^{\ast}\mathrm{tr}_{p})e^{p}_{h}+(-1)^{p}(Q\mathrm{tr}_{q})^{\ast}u^{p}_{h},

then for
\nu^{p}_{h}\in E_{p}(K)
,
\nu^{q}_{h}\in E_{q}(K)
,

\displaystyle\langle\nu^{p}_{h}\mid f^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}

\displaystyle=-\langle e^{q}_{h}\mid d_{qp}\nu^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}+(-1)^{p+1}\frac{1}{2}\langle Q(\mathrm{tr}_{q}e^{q}_{h})\mid\mathrm{tr}_{p}\nu^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}

(86)

We extend the

where

Theorem 7.2.1

(Energy Conservation)
.

Given an
n
-dimensional polyhedral oriented manifold
\Omega
with Lipschitz continuous boundary
\partial\Omega
, discretized into a set of discontinuous elements
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Let
\beta^{p}_{h}(t)\in E_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\ \beta^{q}_{h}(t)\in E_{q}(\mathcal{T}_{h})

101

115

be given by (

\displaystyle=2\underset{F\in\mathcal{F}_{o}}{\sum}\Big(\langle y^{q}_{h}\mid u^{p}_{h}\rangle_{F}+\langle u^{q}_{h}\mid y^{p}_{h}\rangle_{F}\Big).

(139)

Proof.

The energy on each element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
of the discretised system represented by the Stokes-Dirac structure (
124
) is given by (
137
).
The change in discrete energy on each element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
is

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{K}

\displaystyle=\langle\beta^{p}_{h}\mid\star_{p}\dot{\beta}^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle\dot{\beta}^{p}_{h}\mid\star_{p}\beta^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle\dot{\beta}^{q}_{h}\mid\star_{q}\beta^{q}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle\beta^{q}_{h}\mid\star_{q}\dot{\beta}^{q}_{h}\rangle_{K},

(140)

which using the symmetry of
g_{wp}
and
g_{wq}
, given by (

), results in

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{K}

\displaystyle=2\Big(\langle\beta^{p}_{h}\mid\star_{p}\dot{\beta}^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle\beta^{q}_{h}\mid\star_{q}\dot{\beta}^{q}_{h}\rangle_{K}\Big).

(141)

Using (
125
) and (
135
), the duality product (
79
) can be written in terms of state space variables as

\displaystyle\langle e^{p}_{h}\mid f^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle e^{q}_{h}\mid f^{q}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle{y^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\mid u^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}+\langle{u^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\mid y^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}

(142)

\displaystyle=-\langle\beta^{p}_{h}\mid\dot{\alpha}^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}-\langle\beta^{q}_{h}\mid\dot{\alpha}^{q}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle{y^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\mid u^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}

\displaystyle+\langle{u^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\mid y^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}

\displaystyle=-\langle\beta^{p}_{h}\mid\star_{p}\dot{\beta}^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}-\langle\beta^{q}_{h}\mid\star_{q}\dot{\beta}^{q}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle{y^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\mid u^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}

\displaystyle+\langle{u^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\mid y^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}.

\displaystyle-\langle\beta^{p}_{h}\mid\star_{p}\dot{\beta}^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}-\langle\beta^{q}_{h}\mid\star_{q}\dot{\beta}^{q}_{h}\rangle_{K}+\langle{y^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\mid u^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}

\displaystyle+\langle{u^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\mid y^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}=0.

143

141

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{K}

\displaystyle=2\Big(\langle{y^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\mid u^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}+\langle{u^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\mid y^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(\partial K)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(\partial K)}\Big).

115

\displaystyle\dot{E}_{h}

\displaystyle=2\underset{F\in\mathcal{F}_{o}}{\sum}\Big(\langle{y^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\mid u^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(F)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(F)}+\langle{u^{p}_{h}}^{\ast}\mid y^{p}_{h}\rangle_{{E_{p}(F)}^{\ast}\times E_{p}(F)}\Big).

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\Big(||\pi^{n-p}_{h-}er^{p}||^{2}_{wL^{2}\Lambda^{n-p}(\Omega)}+||\pi^{n-q}_{h}er^{q}||^{2}_{wL^{2}\Lambda^{n-q}(\Omega)}\Big)

(175)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{2(r+1)}\Big((||\dot{\beta}^{p}(t)||^{2}_{H^{r+1}\Lambda^{n-p}(\Omega)}+||\dot{\beta}^{q}(t)||^{2}_{H^{r+1}\Lambda^{n-q}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle+\epsilon(||\pi^{n-p}_{h-}er^{p}||^{2}_{wL^{2}\Lambda^{n-p}(\Omega)}+||\pi^{n-q}_{h}er^{q}||^{2}_{wL^{2}\Lambda^{n-q}(\Omega)})\Big).

Using Gronwall’s inequality in differential form we obtain

\displaystyle||\pi^{n-p}_{h-}er^{p}||^{2}_{wL^{2}\Lambda^{n-p}(\Omega)}+||\pi^{n-q}_{h}er^{q}||^{2}_{wL^{2}\Lambda^{n-q}(\Omega)}\leq e^{2\epsilon T}(||\pi^{n-p}_{h-}er^{p}(0)||^{2}_{wL^{2}\Lambda^{n-p}(\Omega)}

(176)

\displaystyle+||\pi^{n-q}_{h}er^{q}(0)||^{2}_{wL^{2}\Lambda^{n-q}(\Omega)})+Ch^{2(r+1)}\int_{0}^{T}e^{2\epsilon(T-t)}(||\dot{\beta}^{p}(t)||^{2}_{H^{r+1}\Lambda^{n-p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+||\dot{\beta}^{q}(t)||^{2}_{H^{r+1}\Lambda^{n-q}(\Omega)})dt.

Let
\beta^{p}_{h}(x,0)=\pi^{n-p}_{h-}\beta^{p}(x,0)
and
\beta^{q}_{h}(x,0)=\pi^{n-q}_{h}\beta^{q}(x,0)
for all

\pi^{n-p}_{h,-}

Results

To support our theory we apply the formulation for the DG discretization of linear port-Hamiltonian systems, presented in Section 6, to the scalar wave equation.

For an
n
-dimensional oriented manifold
\Omega
the scalar wave equation is given in vector notation by

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}u}{dt^{2}}=\frac{1}{\mu}\nabla\cdot(Y\nabla u)\quad\mathrm{in}\ \Omega,

(177)

along with appropriate boundary conditions at the boundary
\partial\Omega
. Here
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the displacement,
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
the mass density and
Y\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
the modulus of rigidity,
\nabla
the nabla operator and
t
time. In a port-Hamiltonian formulation (
177
) is written as, see
[
30
,
32
,
33
,
15
]
,

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\dot{\rho}\\
\dot{\epsilon}\\
\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}0&(-1)^{n+2}d\\
-d&0\\
\end{bmatrix}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}V\\
\sigma\\
\end{array}\right]\ ,

(178)

\displaystyle\left[\begin{array}[]{c}V^{b}\\
\sigma^{b}\\
\end{array}\right]

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}-1&0\\
0&(-1)^{n-1}\\
\end{bmatrix}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}\mathrm{tr}(V)\\
\mathrm{tr}(\sigma)\\
\end{array}\right].\

Here, the kinetic momentum
\rho=\mu\frac{du}{dt}\in L^{2}\Lambda^{n}(\Omega)
and elastic strain
\epsilon=du\in L^{2}\Lambda^{1}(\Omega)
are the energy variables. The velocity
V=\frac{du}{dt}\in H^{1}\Lambda^{0}(\Omega)
and elastic stress
\sigma=(Y\ast du)\in H\Lambda^{n-1}(\Omega)
are the co-energy variables. Note,
d
is the exterior derivative for differential forms and
\ast
the Hodge star operator.

The Hamiltonian energy for the wave equation is given by

\displaystyle H=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(\epsilon\wedge\sigma+\rho\wedge V).

(179)

In
2D
the energy variables
\rho
is a
2
-form and
\epsilon
a
1
-form, whereas the co-energy variable
V
is a
0
-form and
\sigma
a
1
-form. So, we take
p=2,q=1,n=2
and consider the domain
\Omega=[-1,1]\times[-1,1]
.

We choose the discrete energy variables as
V_{h}\in E_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\sigma_{h}\in E_{q}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\rho_{h}\in F_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
and
\epsilon\in F_{q}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
. Following the procedure discussed in Section 6, see also (
136
), we obtain the following port-Hamiltonian discontinuous Galerkin (pHDG) formulation of the wave equation: Find
V_{h}\in E_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\sigma_{h}\in E_{q}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
such that forall
\nu^{p}\in E_{p}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\nu^{q}\in E_{q}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
,

\displaystyle\underset{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\sum}\langle\nu^{p}\mid\star_{p}\dot{V}_{h}\rangle_{K}

\displaystyle=\underset{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\sum}\langle\sigma_{h}\mid d_{qp}\nu^{p}_{h}\rangle_{K}+(-1)^{p}\underset{F\in\mathcal{F}_{o}}{\sum}\langle\hat{\sigma}_{h}\mid\mathrm{tr}_{p}\nu^{p}_{h}\rangle_{F}

(180)

\displaystyle+(-1)^{p}\underset{F\in\mathcal{F}_{i}}{\sum}\langle\hat{\sigma}_{h}\mid(\mathrm{tr}_{p}\nu^{p}_{h}|_{L}+\mathrm{tr}_{p}\nu^{p}_{h}|_{R})\rangle_{F},

\displaystyle\underset{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\sum}\langle\nu^{q}\mid\star_{q}\dot{\sigma}_{h}\rangle_{K}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p+1}\underset{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}{\sum}\langle d_{pq}\nu^{q}_{h}\mid V_{h}\rangle_{K}+(-1)^{p}\underset{F\in\mathcal{F}_{o}}{\sum}\langle\mathrm{tr}_{q}\nu^{q}_{h}\mid\hat{V}_{h}\rangle_{F}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{p}\underset{F\in\mathcal{F}_{i}}{\sum}\langle(\mathrm{tr}_{q}\nu^{q}_{h}|_{L}+\mathrm{tr}_{q}\nu^{q}_{h}|_{R})\mid\hat{V}_{h}\rangle_{F},

where for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{i}

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{h}

\displaystyle=(1-\theta)\mathrm{tr}_{p}V_{h}|_{L}+\theta\mathrm{tr}_{p}V_{h}|_{R},

(181)

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{h}

\displaystyle=\theta\mathrm{tr}_{q}\sigma_{h}|_{L}+(1-\theta)\mathrm{tr}_{q}\sigma_{h}|_{R},

with
\theta\in[0,1]
and for
F\in\mathcal{F}_{o}
,

\displaystyle\hat{V}_{h}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}_{p}V_{h},

\displaystyle\qquad\hat{\sigma}_{h}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}_{q}\sigma_{h}.

(182)

As model problem we use the exact solution for the displacement
u
, velocity
V
and elastic stress
\sigma
given by

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sin(2\pi t)(\sin(2\pi x)+\sin(2\pi y)),

(183)

\displaystyle V

\displaystyle=\cos(2\pi t)(\sin(2\pi x)+\sin(2\pi y)),

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=\sin(2\pi t)(\cos(2\pi x)dx+\cos(2\pi y)dy),

with Dirichlet’s boundary conditions for
V
and
\sigma
obtained by restricting
(x,y)
in (
183
) to
\partial\Omega
. The mass density and modulus of rigidity are taken as 1.

p=2,q=1

E_{p}

E_{q}

On congruent isomorphisms for tori
Anne-Marie Aubert, Sandeep Varma

Abstract
. Let F F and F ′ F^{\prime} be two l l -close nonarchimedean local fields, where l l is a positive integer, and let T \mathrm{T} and T ′ \mathrm{T}^{\prime} be two tori over F F and F ′ F^{\prime} , respectively, such that their cocharacter lattices can be identified as modules over the “at most l l -ramified” absolute Galois group Γ F / I F l ≅ Γ F ′ / I F ′ l \Gamma_{F}/I_{F}^{l}\cong\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{l} . In the spirit of the work of Kazhdan and Ganapathy, for every positive integer m m relative to which l l is large, we construct a congruent isomorphism T ⁡ ( F ) / T ​ ( F ) m ≅ T ′ ​ ( F ′ ) / T ′ ​ ( F ′ ) m \mathrm{T}(F)/\mathrm{T}(F)_{m}\cong\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})_{m} , where T ​ ( F ) m \mathrm{T}(F)_{m} and T ​ ( F ′ ) m \mathrm{T}(F^{\prime})_{m} are the minimal congruent filtration subgroups of T ⁡ ( F ) \mathrm{T}(F) and T ⁡ ( F ′ ) \mathrm{T}(F^{\prime}) , respectively, defined by J.-K. Yu. We prove that this isomorphism is functorial and compatible with both the isomorphism constructed by Chai and Yu and the Kottwitz homomorphism for tori. We show that, when l l is even larger relative to m m , it moreover respects the local Langlands correspondence for tori.

1.3.1.
Deligne’s triples.

We first digress to remark on some fine print.
For all these considerations, choosing isomorphisms is important to ensure

\mathfrak{p}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{l+1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{l}

F^{\times}/(1+\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{l})\rightarrow{F^{\prime}}^{\times}/(1+\mathfrak{p}_{F^{\prime}}^{l})

constructed by Deligne from the realization

F\leftrightarrow_{l}F^{\prime}
, in a canonical manner starting from the
truncated data, in
[
Del84
, Section 1.2]
.
A more concrete but slightly less obviously canonical description
for (
3
) requires it to

•

restrict to
(\mathfrak{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{l})^{\times}\cong(\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{F^{\prime}}^{l})^{\times}

on
\mathfrak{O}_{F}^{\times}/(1+\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{l})=(\mathfrak{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{l})^{\times}
, and

•

send the image of a uniformizer
\varpi_{F}
to that of
a uniformizer
\varpi_{F^{\prime}}
whenever
\varpi_{F}
and
\varpi_{F^{\prime}}

are compatible under
\mathfrak{p}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{l+1}\rightarrow\mathfrak{p}_{F^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{F^{\prime}}^{l+1}
.

1.3.3.
General split tori

When
\mathrm{T}
and
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}
are split but
otherwise general, the datum relating
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}
to
\mathrm{T}

amounts to just an isomorphism
X^{*}(\mathrm{T})\rightarrow X^{*}(\mathrm{T}^{\prime})

of abelian groups, say
\chi\mapsto\chi^{\prime}
. Then our
isomorphism
\mathrm{T}(F)/\mathrm{T}(F)_{m}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})_{m}

is defined so as to match the images of
t\in\mathrm{T}(F)
and
t^{\prime}\in\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})

precisely when for each
\chi\in X^{*}(\mathrm{T})
identifying with

\chi^{\prime}\in X^{*}(\mathrm{T}^{\prime})
, the images of
\chi(t)
and
\chi^{\prime}(t^{\prime})
correspond
under (
3
).

For general tori, in the spirit of the above discussion,
we find it convenient to specify the isomorphism

\mathrm{T}(F)/\mathrm{T}(F)_{m}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})_{m}
by
forcing compatibilities that characterize it.

1.4.
Standard and congruent isomorphisms

In addition to the congruent filtration subgroups
\mathrm{T}(F)_{m}
,
we will need the “naive” filtration subgroups
\mathrm{T}(F)^{\naive}_{r}
(
r\geq 0
):

(4)

\mathrm{T}(F)^{\naive}_{r}=\{t\in\mathrm{T}(F)_{b}\mid\val_{F}(\chi(t)-1)\geq r,\;\forall\,\chi\in X^{*}(\mathrm{T})\},

for a suitable extension
\val_{F}
of the normalized discrete valuation on
F
.

When
(F,\mathrm{T})\leftrightarrow_{l}(F^{\prime},\mathrm{T}^{\prime})
, one defines:

(i)

for
0<r\mathrel{\underline{\ll}}_{\mathrm{T}}l
,
a standard isomorphism
\mathrm{T}(F)/\mathrm{T}(F)^{\naive}_{r}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}
to be one that matches the images of
t\in\mathrm{T}(F)

and
t^{\prime}\in\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})
whenever
\chi(t)
and
\chi^{\prime}(t^{\prime})
have images
that match under a suitable extension of (
3
) (see Definition
3.1.3
(
i
) for more details).

(ii)

a congruent isomorphism
\mathrm{T}(F)/\mathrm{T}(F)_{m}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})_{m}
to be an isomorphism induced by a standard isomorphism
after passage to maximal unramified extensions
(see Definition
3.1.3
(
ii
)
for more details).

Standard isomorphisms are unique when they exist,
and have good functoriality properties, compatibility
with the Kottwitz homomorphism, and
(in the case of complete fields with finite residue field)
compatibility with the local Langlands correspondence.
Congruent isomorphisms inherit
the first three of these four properties, and under stronger assumptions
the fourth too.

Like with
[
Gan22
]
, we too make use of an argument following
the construction of the Kottwitz homomorphism
for tori (see
[
KP23
, Proposition 11.1.1]
):
the following simple yet not entirely obvious fact gets us started
(Proposition
3.3.1
).

Proposition 1.4.1
.

If
(F,\mathrm{T})\leftrightarrow_{l}(F^{\prime},\mathrm{T}^{\prime})
,
F
is strictly Henselian, and

0<r\mathrel{\underline{\ll}}_{\mathrm{T}}l
, then a standard isomorphism

\mathrm{T}(F)/\mathrm{T}(F)^{\naive}_{r}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}
exists.

The difficulty is that we cannot see an obvious way to descend this
to the non-strictly-Henselian case, without going through
congruent isomorphisms. This difficulty is what
motivates congruent isomorphisms for us, notwithstanding
their unpleasantness: indeed, if

\tilde{F}/F
is a maximal unramified extension and

\tilde{F}\leftrightarrow_{l}\tilde{F}^{\prime}
“lies over”
F\leftrightarrow_{l}F^{\prime}
,
then a “Galois invariant” isomorphism
\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})/\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})_{m}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})_{m}
, on taking Galois invariants, gives
us an isomorphism
\mathrm{T}(F)/\mathrm{T}(F)_{m}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})_{m}
:

\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})_{m}
has trivial
\Gal(\tilde{F}/F)
-cohomology, being
sort of pro-unipotent over the (perfect) residue field of
\mathfrak{O}_{F}

(
[
KP23
, Proposition 13.8.1]
).

1.5.
Using the work of Chai and Yu

Thus, the main question now becomes: when can a standard
isomorphism
\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})/\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})^{\naive}_{r}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}
induce an
isomorphism

\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})/\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})_{m}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})_{m}
?
This seems to require a much deeper ingredient:
the spectacular work of Chai and Yu (
[
CY01
]
).

Under the assumption
m+3h(F,\mathrm{T})\mathrel{\underline{\ll}}_{\mathrm{T}}l

of Theorem
1.2.1
,
Chai and Yu construct a canonical isomorphism

\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F}}\mathfrak{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}}\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{F^{\prime}}^{m}
,
where
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{ft}}
is the finite type Néron model of
\mathrm{T}
,
and
{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ft}}
that of
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}
. The properties that characterize
their isomorphism are implicit in their construction.
A careful examination of their construction, together with
a few arguments that are tedious but not difficult
(see Proposition
3.5.1
),
tells us that their isomorphism

\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F}}\mathfrak{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}}\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{F^{\prime}}^{m}

is characterized by the fact that, upon evaluating
on
\mathfrak{O}_{\tilde{F}}/\mathfrak{p}_{\tilde{F}}^{m}=\mathfrak{O}_{\tilde{F}^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{\tilde{F}^{\prime}}^{m}
,
it is induced by a restriction of a standard isomorphism

\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})/\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})^{\naive}_{m+h(F,\mathrm{T})}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})^{\naive}_{m+h(F,\mathrm{T})}
. This is the
key observation that lets us construct congruent (and hence also
standard) isomorphisms outside the strictly Henselian
and “weakly induced” cases.

Thus, more generally, we define a Chai-Yu isomorphism to be an
isomorphism
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F}}\mathfrak{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}}\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{F^{\prime}}^{m}

that, when evaluated at
\mathfrak{O}_{\tilde{F}}/\mathfrak{p}_{\tilde{F}}^{m}=\mathfrak{O}_{\tilde{F}^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{\tilde{F}^{\prime}}^{m}
,
is induced by a standard isomorphism

\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})/\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})^{\naive}_{r}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}

for some
0<r\mathrel{\underline{\ll}}_{\mathrm{T}}l
such that
\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})^{\naive}_{r}\subset\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})_{m}
and
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}\subset\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})_{m}
. The existence of a Chai-Yu isomorphism easily
gives the existence of a congruent isomorphism that is
tautologically compatible with the Chai-Yu isomorphism.

1.6.
The organization of the paper

In Section
2
, we define notation and recall
some material, as well as provide some simple arguments such
as an explanation as to why various results of
[
Del84
]
, though
stated for “local fields” (quotient
fields of complete discrete valuation rings with perfect residue
field) automatically extend to the case of Henselian discretely valued fields
with perfect residue fields. This
extension is convenient because our arguments involve passage
to maximal unramified extensions, which preserves Henselian-ness
but not completeness. We also review the result of Chai and Yu
of interest to us, stating
their isomorphism and articulating the characterization implicit
in their work, in Theorem
2.5.3
.

In Section
3
, we study standard
isomorphisms, congruent isomorphisms and Chai-Yu isomorphisms.
Much of the content of this section has been summarized above.

In Section
4
, we restrict to the
case of tori that are weakly induced in the sense of
[
KP23
]
.
These tori are much simpler than general tori. In this
case, one can construct a Chai-Yu isomorphism under the milder,
natural, assumption
0<m\mathrel{\underline{\ll}}_{\mathrm{T}}l

(Proposition
4.2.2
),
which is what lets us replace
h(F,\mathrm{T})
by
0
in the statement
of Theorem
1.2.1

for weakly induced tori.

Finally, in Section
5
,
we put things together and prove
Theorem
1.2.1
.

2.1.

This is because we will need to pass to maximal

of fields

(see
[

valued Henselian fields are the quasi-complete fields

[

doing so preserves only Henselian-ness, not completeness.

2.1.1.
Objects associated to a DVHF

For a DVHF

\mathfrak{p}_{F}\subset\mathfrak{O}_{F}

\mathfrak{O}_{F}

\kappa_{F}=\mathfrak{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}

any algebraic extension of
F
. Given a
field

has been chosen. For a DVHF

, we will write
\Gamma_{F}
and

respectively for the absolute Galois group

\Gal(F^{\mathrm{sep}}/F)
and the inertia group of
F
,
and
\Gamma_{E/F}
for the Galois group
of any Galois extension
E/F
. Let
\Gamma_{\kappa_{F}}=\Gamma_{F}/I_{F}
; it
is isomorphic to the absolute Galois group of
\kappa_{F}
.
In case
\kappa_{F}
is finite,
\Gamma_{\kappa_{F}}

is topologically generated by a Frobenius element, and we will write

W_{F}\subset\Gamma_{F}
for the Weil group, namely the inverse image
in
\Gamma_{F}
of the subgroup of
\Gamma_{\kappa_{F}}
abstractly generated the
Frobenius element. Given a DVHF
F
with perfect residue field,
we will often be interested in separable finitely ramified extensions

,
which may not be finite or Galois. In such a situation, we will use without
further comment that

is also a DVHF with perfect residue field
(thus,

is algebraically closed if

contains a maximal unramified

the associated ramification degree.

2.1.2.
Passage to completion

Write
\hat{F}
for the completion of any DVHF

; it is a complete DVHF.
Let
F
be a DVHF. For the following, we refer to

[
KP23
, Proposition 2.1.6]
and
[
Ber93
, Proposition 2.4.1]
.
If
E/F
is a finite separable extension, noting that
E
is
also a DVHF, the obvious map

of rings is an isomorphism.
We have an equivalence of categories between the category

\ext F
of finite separable extensions
of
F
and the analogous category
\ext\hat{F}
,
given by

, so that choosing an embedding of

into
{\hat{F}}^{\mathrm{sep}}:=(\hat{F})^{\mathrm{sep}}

.

2.1.3.
Ramification theory

We assume the setting and notation from Subsubsection

2.1.2
above, but assume also that
\kappa_{F}

is perfect. Let
E/F
be a finitely ramified separable extension.
For each
r\in[-1,\infty)
,
we have the “lower ramification (equivalence) relation”
\Xi_{r}
on

\Hom_{F-\mathrm{alg}}(E,F^{\mathrm{sep}})
as in
[
Del84
, (A.3.3)]
(whose

R_{u}
is our
\Xi_{r}
), under which
\sigma
and
\tau
are equivalent
if and only if for some (or equivalently, any) finitely ramified
Galois extension
M/F
contained in
F^{\mathrm{sep}}
and containing
a normal closure of
E
,
we have
\val_{M}(\sigma(x)-\tau(x))\geq e(M/E)(r+1)
for all

x\in\mathfrak{O}_{E}
. When
E=M
is Galois, the lower ramification
subgroup
\Gal(E/F)_{r}\subset\Gal(E/F)
is the equivalence class
of the identity element under
\Xi_{r}
, which is then just “lies in the
same
\Gal(E/F)_{r}
-coset”.

Choosing any
M
as above,
\Gal(M/F)
has a well-defined
transitive action on the set of
\Xi_{r}
-equivalence classes, so that
they all have the same cardinality. This cardinality
is easily seen to be bounded above
by
e(E/F)
for
r>-1
: if
\sigma,\tau\in\Hom_{F-\mathrm{alg}}(E,F^{\mathrm{sep}})

belong to the same class under
\Xi_{r}
with
r>-1
, it is an easy exercise
to see that they agree on the maximal
unramified subextension of
E/F
.
For
r>-1
, let
1\leq g_{r}\leq e(E/F)
be the
cardinality of each
\Xi_{r}
-equivalence class (our
g_{r}

is the

of
[
Del84
]
). This also lets us define the Herbrand function
associated to each finitely ramified separable extension

E/F
by the familiar integral as in
[
Del84
, (A.4.3)]
,
for

:

(5)

e(E/F)^{-1}r\leq\varphi_{E/F}(r)=\int_{0}^{r}dt/(g_{0}/g_{t})\leq r.

\varphi_{E/F}
is a piecewise linear
self-homeomorphism of
[0,\infty)
. In fact, one can show that this also
defines a self-homeomorphism of
[-1,\infty)
, but we will only
be interested in its values on
[0,\infty)
.
Let its inverse be
\psi_{E/F}
, another self-homeomorphism of
[0,\infty)
.

The following allow us to reduce the study of

\varphi_{E/F}
and
\psi_{E/F}
to the case
where
E/F
is finite and
F
is complete:

•

For any subextension
E_{\circ}/F
of
E/F
with

e(E_{\circ}/F)=e(E/F)
— note that
there exist finite such

—
we have
\varphi_{E/F}=\varphi_{E_{\circ}/F}

and
\psi_{E/F}=\psi_{E_{\circ}/F}
.
To see this, make use of the same argument that was
used above to prove that
g_{r}\leq e(E/F)
for

, to see that
the value of

associated to
E/F
equals
that associated to
E_{\circ}/F
.

•

If
E/F
is finite, it is easy to see that the identification

\Hom_{F-\mathrm{alg}}(E,F^{\mathrm{sep}})\rightarrow\Hom_{\hat{F}-\mathrm{alg}}(\hat{E},(\hat{F})^{\mathrm{sep}})

respects each of the equivalence
relations
\Xi_{r}
, so that
\varphi_{E/F}=\varphi_{\hat{E}/\hat{F}}

and
\psi_{E/F}=\psi_{\hat{E}/\hat{F}}

as functions

.

We claim that for finitely ramified separable extensions

, we have

\varphi_{E_{2}/F}=\varphi_{E_{1}/F}\circ\varphi_{E_{2}/E_{1}}
,
or equivalently
\psi_{E_{2}/F}=\psi_{E_{2}/E_{1}}\circ\psi_{E_{1}/F}
.
To see this, reduce using the arguments above
to the case where
E_{2}/F
is finite, and then to the case where

and
E_{2}
are complete, then to the case where

E_{2}/F
is Galois, and use
[

, (A.4.1) and Proposition A.4.2]
.

For
E/F
finitely ramified separable, define

.
If
E/F
is Galois, this is the

.

When
E/F
is finite, it is easy to check that

(using

Let

Remark 2.1.1
.

Here are some properties of a finitely ramified

to the category
(\ext\Tr_{l}F)^{l}
(see Subsubsection
2.2.1
),
which is in fact an equivalence of categories.
However, even when
F
is not complete, using the equivalence of categories

(\ext F)^{l}\rightarrow(\ext\hat{F})^{l}

(Subsubsection
2.1.4
)
and the tautological isomorphism
\Tr_{l}F\rightarrow\Tr_{l}\hat{F}
,
we formally get an equivalence of categories
(\ext F)^{l}\rightarrow(\ext\Tr_{l}F)^{l}
. Moreover,
it is immediate that this equivalence of categories has an intrinsic description
independent of
\hat{F}
, exactly as the one used for

\hat{F}
in
[
Del84
, Theorem 2.8]
. In particular, any isomorphism

\Tr_{l}F\rightarrow\Tr_{l}F^{\prime}
determines an equivalence
of categories
(\ext F)^{l}\rightarrow(\ext F^{\prime})^{l}
; this takes
each
E
in
(\ext F)^{l}
to some
E^{\prime}
in
(\ext F^{\prime})^{l}

such that we have an isomorphism

\Tr_{el}E=\Tr_{el}E^{\prime}
in the category

(\ext\Tr_{l}F)^{l}=(\ext\Tr_{l}F^{\prime})^{l}
, where
e=e(E/F)=e(E^{\prime}/F^{\prime})
.

2.2.3.
Close local fields

The notation
F\leftrightarrow_{l}F^{\prime}
will mean not only that

F
and
F^{\prime}
are DVHFs with perfect residue fields that are

l
-close in the sense that
we have an isomorphism
\mathfrak{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{l}\cong\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{F^{\prime}}^{l}
of rings,
but also that the following additional data have been chosen:

•

An identification
\Tr_{l}F=\Tr_{l}F^{\prime}
has been chosen in
\mathfrak{T}
,
as also an equivalence of categories

U\colon(\ext F)^{l}\rightarrow(\ext F^{\prime})^{l}
as in
Subsubsection
2.2.2
above,
which then as in
[
Del84
, Section 3.5]
,
determines the inner class of an isomorphism

\Gamma_{F}/I_{F}^{l}\cong\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{l}
.

•

An identification
\Gamma_{F}/I_{F}^{l}=\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{l}

from the inner class mentioned above has been
chosen by means of a choice of a fixed
isomorphism
U((F^{\mathrm{sep}})^{I_{F}^{l}})\rightarrow({F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}})^{I_{F^{\prime}}^{l}}

over
F^{\prime}
, as follows:

\Gamma_{F}/I_{F}^{l}=\Aut_{F-\mathrm{alg}}(({F}^{\mathrm{sep}})^{I_{F}^{l}})\overset{U}{\rightarrow}\Aut_{F^{\prime}-\mathrm{alg}}(U((F^{\mathrm{sep}})^{I_{F}^{l}}))=\Aut_{F^{\prime}-\mathrm{alg}}(({F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}})^{I_{F^{\prime}}^{l}})=\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{l},

where
U\colon(\ext F)^{l}\cong(\ext F^{\prime})^{l}
is now
extended to the level of the ind-objects.

We refer to
[
Del84
, Section 3.5, especially Section 3.5(c)]

for some of the details, which do not need the assumption that
F
is
complete.

This involves choices of
F^{\mathrm{sep}}
and
{F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}}

among other things, changing which will change associated objects
in an appropriate sense, e.g., up to an inner automorphism for Galois groups.

2.2.4.
A variation

Given
F\leftrightarrow_{l}F^{\prime}
, it will be helpful to consider the
following variant of
U
. Let
(\ext F)^{l,+}
be the category of
embeddings
E\hookrightarrow F^{\mathrm{sep}}
, where
E/F
is
finitely ramified and at most
l
-ramified. Similarly, we have

(\ext F^{\prime})^{l,+}
. These categories have the following advantage which
is important for us: between any two objects in them is either
a unique morphism, or none at all, depending on whether the stabilizer
of the former in
\Gamma_{F}
or
\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}

contains that of the latter. Clearly,
U
, considered at the
level of ind-objects, defines an equivalence of categories

U^{+}\colon(\ext F)^{l,+}\rightarrow(\ext F^{\prime})^{l,+}
; it sends

E\hookrightarrow F^{\mathrm{sep}}
to

U(E)\hookrightarrow U((F^{\mathrm{sep}})^{I_{F}^{l}})\rightarrow({F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}})^{I_{F^{\prime}}^{l}}\hookrightarrow{F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}}
, where

U((F^{\mathrm{sep}})^{I_{F}^{l}})\rightarrow({F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}})^{I_{F^{\prime}}^{l}}
is part
of the datum
F\leftrightarrow_{l}F^{\prime}
.
Moreover, it is an easy exercise to describe when

E^{\prime}\hookrightarrow{F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}}
is isomorphic in

(\ext F^{\prime})^{l,+}
to
U^{+}(E\hookrightarrow F^{\mathrm{sep}})
:
this is so if and only if
E\hookrightarrow F^{\mathrm{sep}}
and

E^{\prime}\hookrightarrow{F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}}
have the same stabilizer
in
\Gamma_{F}/I_{F}^{l}=\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{l}
.

Upshot: the datum of an extension
E^{\prime}/F
and an isomorphism

U(E)\rightarrow E^{\prime}
, is the same as that of an embedding

E^{\prime}\hookrightarrow{F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}}
with the same stabilizer
as
E\hookrightarrow F^{\mathrm{sep}}
in
\Gamma_{F}/I_{F}^{l}=\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{l}
.

2.2.5.
Close extensions of close local fields

Given
F\leftrightarrow_{l}F^{\prime}
, we will typically need to
work with realizations
E\leftrightarrow_{l(1)}E^{\prime}
involving
finitely ramified at most
l
-ramified extensions

E
and
E^{\prime}
of
F
and
F^{\prime}
with
U(E)\cong E^{\prime}
,
where
l(1)=\psi_{E/F}(l)
is an integer.
We will use without further mention that, for any such
E^{\prime}
, we have

\psi_{E/F}(l)=\psi_{E^{\prime}/F^{\prime}}(l)
:
use the discussion in
[
Del84
, Section 1.5.3]

(expressing
\psi_{E/F}(l)
in terms of truncated data).

Let us describe how a choice of an isomorphism
U(E)\rightarrow E^{\prime}

gives a realization
E\leftrightarrow_{l(1)}E^{\prime}
.
As in
[
Del84
, Construction 3.4.1]

(and using a direct limit argument to reduce to the case of finite extensions),
this choice determines an isomorphism

\Tr_{l(1)}E\rightarrow\Tr_{l(1)}E^{\prime}
;
by Subsubsection
2.2.2

above this does not need the assumption that
F
is complete.
Moreover, we have obvious choices of the additional data
needed to upgrade this to a realization

E\rightarrow_{l(1)}E^{\prime}
, as follows. An analogue
U_{E}

of
U
for this isomorphism can be obtained by
restricting

to extensions of

and thinking of

U(E)\rightarrow E^{\prime}
as an identification, which we can also
use to choose
F^{\mathrm{sep}}
and
{F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}}
as
algebraic closures of
E
and
E^{\prime}
, and get an
identification
U_{E}((F^{\mathrm{sep}})^{I_{E}^{l(1)}})=U((F^{\mathrm{sep}})^{I_{F}^{l}})\rightarrow({F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}})^{I_{F^{\prime}}^{l}}=({F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}})^{I_{E^{\prime}}^{l(1)}}
.

Remark 2.2.1
.

The above construction of
E\leftrightarrow_{l(1)}E^{\prime}
has the
following properties, which will be used without further mention
in what follows:

(i)

\Gamma_{E}
and
\Gamma_{E^{\prime}}
, as realized in

E\leftrightarrow_{l(1)}E^{\prime}
, identify with the stabilizers of

E\hookrightarrow F^{\mathrm{sep}}
in
\Gamma_{F}
and
E^{\prime}\hookrightarrow{F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}}

in
\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}
, and the resulting isomorphism

\Gamma_{E}/I_{E}^{l(1)}\rightarrow\Gamma_{E^{\prime}}/I_{E^{\prime}}^{l(1)}
is simply
the restriction of
\Gamma_{F}/I_{F}^{l}\rightarrow\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{l}
.

(ii)

We get a bijection

(6)

\Gamma_{E/F}\rightarrow(\Gamma_{F}/I_{F}^{l})/(\Gamma_{E}/I_{E}^{l(1)})\rightarrow(\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{l})/(\Gamma_{E^{\prime}}/I_{E^{\prime}}^{l(1)})\rightarrow\Gamma_{E^{\prime}/F^{\prime}},

which is an isomorphism of groups if
E/F
is Galois,
in which case these groups act compatibly on
\Tr_{l(1)}E=\Tr_{l(1)}E^{\prime}

over
\Tr_{l}F=\Tr_{l}F^{\prime}
.

(iii)

Suppose
L/F
is a finitely ramified at most
l
-ramified
extension ‘containing’
E/F
. Then any extension
L^{\prime}/F^{\prime}
together with
an isomorphism
U(L)\rightarrow L^{\prime}
determines an extension
L^{\prime}/E^{\prime}

(via
E^{\prime}\rightarrow U(E)\rightarrow U(L)\rightarrow L^{\prime}
),
together with an isomorphism
U_{E}(L)=U(L)\rightarrow L^{\prime}
, and vice versa.
Given any such
L^{\prime}
and
U(L)\rightarrow L^{\prime}
,
it is easily verified that the realization
L\leftrightarrow_{\psi_{L/F}(l)}L^{\prime}
, obtained using the above construction starting from

F\leftrightarrow_{l}F^{\prime}
and
U(L)\rightarrow L^{\prime}
,
is the same as the realization

L\leftrightarrow_{\psi_{L/E}(\psi_{E/F}(l))}L^{\prime}
, obtained using
the above construction starting from
E\leftrightarrow_{\psi_{E/F}(l)}E^{\prime}

and
U_{E}(L)=U(L)\rightarrow L^{\prime}
.

However, it seems inconvenient to keep track of fixed isomorphisms

U(E)\rightarrow E^{\prime}
, or even to keep referring to
U
.
This is why we had Subsubsection

2.2.4
: the discussion
there shows that, given
E\hookrightarrow F^{\mathrm{sep}}
in

(\ext F)^{l,+}
, any
E^{\prime}\hookrightarrow{F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}}
with
the same stabilizer as it in
\Gamma_{F}/I_{F}^{l}=\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{l}

determines a unique isomorphism

, and
hence a realization
E\leftrightarrow_{l(1)}E^{\prime}
.

Notation 2.2.2
.

If a realization

is understood, and
we talk of compatible embeddings

and

and

-ramified

Remark 2.3.2

Let

, where

and
\Gamma_{E}=\Gal(F^{\mathrm{sep}}/E)

We have a “universal”, surjective, homomorphism

which, at the level of

the map

to the multiplication map

of

over

Lemma 2.4.1
.

Suppose

.
Let

L^{\prime}/F^{\prime}

\Res_{L/F}

homomorphism of
\Gamma_{F}
-modules that, when viewed as a homomorphism
of
\Gamma_{L}
-modules and pre-composed with the
“universal”
\Gamma_{L}
-module homomorphism

yields the identity. In the previous sentence, we can replace

\Gamma_{F}
by
\Gamma_{F}/I_{F}^{l}=\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{l}

and
\Gamma_{E}
by
\Gamma_{E}/I_{E}^{l(1)}=\Gamma_{E^{\prime}}/I_{E^{\prime}}^{l(1)}
,
where

. Since an analogous assertion applies
for
(F^{\prime},\mathrm{T}^{\prime})

(i.e., of Proposition

Lemma 2.5.5
.

Let
\mathrm{T}

Remark 2.5.6
.

In the situation of the above lemma, we claim that whenever

(

).

Proof of Lemma
2.5.5
.

It suffices to show that
\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})^{\naive}_{m+h}\subset\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})_{m}
,

\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})^{\std}_{m+h}\subset\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})^{\naive}_{m+h}=\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})^{\naive}_{m+h}\cap\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})_{m}\subset\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})^{\naive}_{m+h}\cap\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})_{0}=\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})^{\std}_{m+h}.

Let
\mathrm{R}=\Res_{L/F}\mathrm{T}
.
Since the connected Néron model

\mathcal{T}

3.
Standard, congruent and Chai-Yu isomorphisms

3.1.
The definition of standard and congruent isomorphisms

Notation 3.1.1
.

Let
(F,\mathrm{T})\leftrightarrow_{l}(F^{\prime},\mathrm{T}^{\prime})
, and let

r>0
. Elements
t\mathrm{T}(F)_{r}^{\naive}\in\mathrm{T}(F)/\mathrm{T}(F)^{\naive}_{r}
and

t^{\prime}\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})_{r}^{\naive}\in\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}

(or by abuse of notation,
t\in\mathrm{T}(F)
and
t^{\prime}\in\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})
)
are said to be standard correspondents of each other if
for some (and hence by Lemma
3.2.1

below, any) compatible embeddings
L\hookrightarrow F^{\mathrm{sep}}
and

L^{\prime}\hookrightarrow{F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}}
, such that
L
splits
\mathrm{T}
and

r\mathrel{\underline{\ll}}_{L}l
(see Notation
2.2.4
), the following
holds: for every
\chi=\chi^{\prime}\in X^{*}(\mathrm{T}^{\prime})=X^{*}(\mathrm{T})
,

\chi(t)
and
\chi^{\prime}(t^{\prime})
have images that match under
the isomorphism
L^{\times}/(1+\mathfrak{p}_{L}^{\lceil e(L/F)r\rceil})\rightarrow{L^{\prime}}^{\times}/(1+\mathfrak{p}_{L^{\prime}}^{\lceil e(L/F)r\rceil})

(described below Notation
2.2.4
).

Remark 3.1.2
.

(i)

Note that the condition in Notation
3.1.1

does not change if
t
or
t^{\prime}
is replaced by another element
of
t\mathrm{T}(F)^{\naive}_{r}
or
t^{\prime}\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}
(this follows from
the definition of the naive filtration subgroups
\mathrm{T}(F)^{\naive}_{r}
and

\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}
).

(ii)

We will see in Lemma
3.2.1
below
that, in the setting of Notation
3.1.1
,
every element of
\mathrm{T}(F)/\mathrm{T}(F)^{\naive}_{r}
has either a unique
standard correspondent in
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}
,
or none at all (and vice versa).
This will sometimes be used without further comment in what follows.

Definition 3.1.3
.

Let
(F,\mathrm{T})\leftrightarrow_{l}(F^{\prime},\mathrm{T}^{\prime})
.

(i)

Let
r>0
be a positive real number.
An isomorphism

\mathrm{T}(F)/\mathrm{T}(F)^{\naive}_{r}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}

of abelian groups is said to be a standard isomorphism if
it maps every element of its source to a standard correspondent of
it.

(ii)

Let
m
be a positive integer.
An isomorphism
\mathrm{T}(F)/\mathrm{T}(F)_{m}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})_{m}
is
said to be a congruent isomorphism if for some
(or equivalently by Lemma
3.4.1
(
i
) below, any)
compatible embeddings
\tilde{F}\hookrightarrow F^{\mathrm{sep}}
and

\tilde{F}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow{F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}}
, where
\tilde{F}/F
is
a maximal unramified extension, and some
r>0
such that

\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})^{\naive}_{r}\subset\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})_{m}
and

\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}\subset\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})_{m}
,
the given isomorphism is a restriction of an isomorphism

\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})/\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})_{m}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})_{m}
,
which in turn is induced by a standard isomorphism

\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})/\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})^{\naive}_{r}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}
associated to
(\tilde{F},\mathrm{T}_{\tilde{F}})\leftrightarrow_{l}(\tilde{F}^{\prime},\mathrm{T}^{\prime}_{\tilde{F}^{\prime}})
.

(iii)

Let
m
be a positive integer.
An isomorphism
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F}}\mathfrak{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}}\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{F^{\prime}}^{m}
is
said to be a Chai-Yu isomorphism if for some
(or equivalently by
Lemma
3.4.1
(
i
) below, any) compatible embeddings

\tilde{F}\hookrightarrow F^{\mathrm{sep}}
and

\tilde{F}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow{F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}}
, where
\tilde{F}/F
is a
maximal unramified extension,
and some
r>0
such that
\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})^{\naive}_{r}\subset\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})_{m}
and

\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}\subset\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})_{m}
,
the isomorphism

\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})_{b}/\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})_{m}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})_{b}/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})_{m}

obtained by evaluating the given isomorphism at

\mathfrak{O}_{\tilde{F}}/\mathfrak{p}_{\tilde{F}}^{m}=\mathfrak{O}_{\tilde{F}^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{\tilde{F}^{\prime}}^{m}

is induced
by a “restricted standard isomorphism”

\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})_{b}/\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})^{\naive}_{r}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})_{b}/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}
, namely, one
that maps each element of its source to a standard correspondent of it,
for the realization
(\tilde{F},\mathrm{T}_{\tilde{F}})\leftrightarrow_{l}(\tilde{F}^{\prime},\mathrm{T}^{\prime}_{\tilde{F}^{\prime}})
.

The following remark helps make sense of the above definition.

Remark 3.1.4
.

(i)

Note that, by definition, a standard isomorphism

\mathrm{T}(F)/\mathrm{T}(F)^{\naive}_{r}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}

does not exist unless

\mathrm{T}

a congruent isomorphism

or a Chai-Yu isomorphism

(ii)

(iii)

To relate the definition of a Chai-Yu isomorphism to

Lemma 3.2.1
.

Let

\mathrm{T}

if and only if for all

3.3.
Further properties of standard isomorphisms

In this subsection, we prove less obvious properties of standard
isomorphisms: their existence when
F
is strictly Henselian,
and compatibility with Kottwitz homomorphisms and the
local Langlands correspondence.

Proposition 3.3.1
.

Let
(F,\mathrm{T})\leftrightarrow_{l}(F^{\prime},\mathrm{T}^{\prime})
, and let

0<r\mathrel{\underline{\ll}}_{\mathrm{T}}l
. If further
F
is strictly Henselian
(and hence so is
F^{\prime}
), a standard isomorphism

\mathrm{T}(F)/\mathrm{T}(F)^{\naive}_{r}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}

Proof.

Choose a finite separable extension

\mathrm{T}
,
such that
r\mathrel{\underline{\ll}}_{L}l
. Without loss of generality,
L/F
is minimal such
(making

\psi_{L/F}(l)/e(L/F)

and hence Galois. Let
L\hookrightarrow F^{\mathrm{sep}}

and
L^{\prime}\hookrightarrow{F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}}
be compatible embeddings, so

L\leftrightarrow_{\psi_{L/F}(l)}L^{\prime}
. Abbreviate
e:=e(L/F)
.

We have a standard isomorphism

\mathrm{T}(L)/\mathrm{T}(L)^{\naive}_{er}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(L^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(L^{\prime})^{\naive}_{er}
,
since
L
splits
\mathrm{T}
and

It suffices to show that this isomorphism restricts to an isomorphism

\mathrm{T}(F)/\mathrm{T}(F)^{\naive}_{r}\to\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}
.

The isomorphism

\mathrm{T}(L)/\mathrm{T}(L)^{\naive}_{er}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(L^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(L^{\prime})^{\naive}_{er}

is equivariant for
\Gamma_{L/F}=\Gamma_{L^{\prime}/F^{\prime}}

by Corollary
3.2.7
, and hence
for the action of the product

of the elements of
\Gamma_{L/F}=\Gamma_{L^{\prime}/F^{\prime}}

it suffices to show that

or equivalently, that the analogous maps

and
\Res_{L^{\prime}/F^{\prime}}\mathrm{T}^{\prime}_{L^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}

are surjective respectively at the levels of

-rational points and

F^{\prime}
-rational points. This is well-known:
the kernel
\mathrm{T}_{0}
of

is connected, and

by

[

is
strictly Henselian and
\kappa_{F}
is perfect.
∎

4.
The case of weakly induced tori

In this section, we will restrict to a class
of tori that includes all induced tori, namely,
the class of tori satisfying the beautiful condition (T)
identified in
[
Yu15
]
, which, following
[
KP23
]
,
we will refer to as the class of weakly induced tori.
For these tori, the standard and minimal congruent filtrations coincide
(
[
KP23
, Corollary B.10.13]
).
We will show that for weakly induced tori,
standard, congruent and Chai-Yu isomorphisms exist in the
“best possible” generality. It will follow that for these
tori, congruent isomorphisms are a special case of standard isomorphisms.

4.1.
Weakly induced tori

Notation 4.1.1
.

A torus
\mathrm{T}
over a DVHF
F
is said to be weakly
induced if it becomes an induced torus over some finite tamely ramified
extension of
F
. It is easy to see (
[
KP23
, Remark B.6.3]
)
that
\mathrm{T}
is weakly induced if and only if
X^{*}(\mathrm{T})
has a basis
that is permuted by the wild inertia group

I_{F}^{>0}:=\bigcup_{r>0}I_{F}^{r}\subset I_{F}\subset\Gamma_{F}
.

The following lemma is one reason why weakly induced tori are easy to work with.

Lemma 4.1.2
.

Let
\mathrm{T}
be a weakly induced torus over a DVHF
F
with perfect
residue field.
For any
r\geq 0
, we have

\mathrm{T}(F)_{r}=\mathrm{T}(F)^{\std}_{r}
, and for any
r>0
, we have

\mathrm{T}(F)^{\std}_{r}=\mathrm{T}(F)^{\naive}_{r}
.
Consequently, using Remark
2.3.3
,

\mathrm{T}(L_{1})_{r}=(\mathrm{T}(L_{2})_{e(L_{2}/L_{1})r})^{\Gal(L_{2}/L_{1})}

and
\mathrm{T}_{1}(F)_{r}=\mathrm{T}_{1}(F)\cap\mathrm{T}_{2}(F)_{r}
whenever

r>0
,
L_{2}/L_{1}/F
is a chain of finitely ramified
separable field extensions with
L_{2}/L_{1}
Galois,
and
\mathrm{T}_{1}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{T}_{2}
is an injective homomorphism
of weakly induced tori over
F
.

Proof.

For the equality
\mathrm{T}(F)_{r}=\mathrm{T}(F)^{\std}_{r}
, use

[
KP23
, Corollary B.10.13]
(and intersect with
\mathrm{T}(F)
).
For the equality
\mathrm{T}(F)^{\std}_{r}=\mathrm{T}(F)^{\naive}_{r}
when
r>0
,
see
[
KP23
, Proposition B.6.4(3)]
.
∎

4.2.
Standard, congruent and Chai-Yu
isomorphisms for weakly induced tori

Proposition 4.2.1
.

Let
(F,\mathrm{T})\leftrightarrow_{l}(F^{\prime},\mathrm{T}^{\prime})
,
with
\mathrm{T}
assumed to be weakly induced.
Suppose
0<r\mathrel{\underline{\ll}}_{\mathrm{T}}l
. Then there is a standard isomorphism

\mathrm{T}(F)/\mathrm{T}(F)^{\naive}_{r}=\mathrm{T}(F)/\mathrm{T}(F)_{r}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})_{r}=\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(F^{\prime})^{\naive}_{r}
. If
r=m

is an integer, then this is also a congruent isomorphism.

Proof.

Since
\mathrm{T}
is weakly induced over
F
, so is

\mathrm{T}^{\prime}
:
I_{F}^{>0}
, acting through
I_{F}^{>0}/I_{F}^{l}=I_{F^{\prime}}^{>0}/I_{F^{\prime}}^{l}
,
permutes a basis of
X^{*}(\mathrm{T})=X^{*}(\mathrm{T}^{\prime})
.
Choose compatible embeddings
\tilde{F}\hookrightarrow F^{\mathrm{sep}}

and
\tilde{F}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow{F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}}
, where

\tilde{F}/F
is a maximal unramified extension.
Consider the standard isomorphism
\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})/\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})_{r}\rightarrow\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})/\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})_{r}

associated to
(\tilde{F},\mathrm{T}_{\tilde{F}})\leftrightarrow_{l}(\tilde{F}^{\prime},\mathrm{T}^{\prime}_{\tilde{F}^{\prime}})

(Proposition
3.3.1
).
It is equivariant for
\Gamma_{\tilde{F}/F}=\Gamma_{\tilde{F}^{\prime}/F^{\prime}}

(Corollary
3.2.7
). Thus,
as in the proof of
Lemma
3.4.1
(
iii
),
the first assertion follows if we show
that
H^{1}(\Gamma_{\tilde{F}/F},\mathrm{T}(\tilde{F})_{r})=0=H^{1}(\Gamma_{\tilde{F}^{\prime}/F^{\prime}},\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})_{r})
.
This is a special case of
[
KP23
, Proposition 13.8.1]
.
The second assertion is immediate.
∎

Proposition 4.2.2
.

Let
(F,\mathrm{T})\leftrightarrow_{l}(F^{\prime},\mathrm{T}^{\prime})
,
with
\mathrm{T}
a weakly induced torus over
F
.
Let
m
be a positive integer, with

m\mathrel{\underline{\ll}}_{\mathrm{T}}l
. Then there is a unique Chai-Yu isomorphism

\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F}}\mathfrak{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{m}\cong\mathcal{T^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}}\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{F^{\prime}}^{m}
.

Proof.

Let
m\mathrel{\underline{\ll}}_{L}l
for some finite Galois extension
L/F
splitting
\mathrm{T}
, and let

L\hookrightarrow F^{\mathrm{sep}}
and
L^{\prime}\hookrightarrow{F^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{sep}}

be compatible embeddings. Form
(F,\mathrm{R}:=\Res_{L/F}\mathrm{T}_{L})\leftrightarrow_{l}(F^{\prime},\mathrm{R}^{\prime}:=\Res_{L^{\prime}/F^{\prime}}\mathrm{T}_{L^{\prime}})
.

By Lemma
3.5.2
, there exists
a Chai-Yu isomorphism
\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F}}\mathfrak{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}}\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{F^{\prime}}^{m}
.
On the other hand, we also know that

\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{ft}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ft}}
and

{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ft}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ft}}
are
closed immersions, as a special case of

[
KP23
, Lemma B.7.11]
(this nontrivially uses the fact
that
\mathrm{T}
and
\mathrm{T}^{\prime}
are weakly induced). This allows us to make
sense of the following claim: that the Chai-Yu isomorphism

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F}}\mathfrak{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}}\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{F^{\prime}}^{m}

restricts to an isomorphism

\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F}}\mathfrak{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}}\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{F^{\prime}}^{m}

Since the image of

takes the image of

{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ft}}(\mathfrak{O}_{\tilde{F}^{\prime}})=\mathrm{T}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})_{b}

In other words, if we show that

\mathrm{R}(\tilde{F})_{b}/\mathrm{R}(\tilde{F})_{m}\rightarrow\mathrm{R}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})_{b}/\mathrm{R}^{\prime}(\tilde{F}^{\prime})_{m}

\mathcal{R}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F}}\mathfrak{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}_{F}^{m}\rightarrow{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{ft}}\times_{\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}}\mathfrak{O}_{F^{\prime}}/\mathfrak{p}_{F^{\prime}}^{m}

This not only proves that

3.4.2
.

Uniform Envelopes
Eike Neumann

Abstract
. In the author’s PhD thesis (2019) universal envelopes were introduced as a tool for studying the continuously obtainable information on discontinuous functions with the aim of extending the scope of traditional computable analysis. To any function f : X → Y f\colon X\to Y between qcb 0 \operatorname{qcb}_{0} -spaces one can assign a so-called universal envelope which, in a well-defined sense, encodes all continuously obtainable information on the function. A universal envelope consists of two continuous functions F : X → L F\colon X\to L and ξ L : Y → L \xi_{L}\colon Y\to L with values in a Σ \Sigma -split injective space L L . Any continuous function with values in a Σ \Sigma -split injective space whose composition with the original function is again continuous factors through the universal envelope. This leaves open the question how to obtain such a factorisation for a given function. By an observation which essentially goes back to Scott (1972) any continuous function with values in a Σ \Sigma -split injective space admits a greatest continuous “right extension” along ξ L \xi_{L} , which yields an explicit solution. However, this extension is not in general computable from the function. It follows from a result of Escardó (1998) that this extension depends continuously on the function if and only if ξ L \xi_{L} is a proper map in the sense of Hofmann and Lawson (1984). In order to solve this problem in greater generality we propose the notion of uniform envelopes. A uniform envelope is additionally endowed with a map u L : L → 𝒪 2 ​ ( Y ) u_{L}\colon L\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y) that is compatible with the multiplication of the double powerspace monad 𝒪 2 \mathcal{O}^{2} in a certain sense. This yields for every continuous map with values in a Σ \Sigma -split injective space a choice of uniformly computable extension. Under a suitable condition which we call uniform universality, this extension yields a uniformly computable solution for the above factorisation problem. Uniform envelopes can be endowed with a composition operation. We establish criteria that ensure that the composition of two uniformly universal envelopes is again uniformly universal. These criteria admit a partial converse and we provide evidence that they cannot be easily improved in general. We show that a qcb 0 \operatorname{qcb}_{0} -space Y Y has the property that all functions with values in Y Y admit a uniformly universal envelope if and only if Y Y is Noetherian. Dually, a space X X has the property that all functions with domain X X admit a uniformly universal envelope if and only if X X contains no infinite compact sets. Not every function admits a uniformly universal uniform envelope. We can however assign to every function a canonical envelope that is in some sense as close as possible to a uniform envelope. We obtain a composition theorem similar to the uniform case.

Introduction, Background, and Motivation

Computable analysis is the extension of the theory of computation from discrete to continuous data.
As such, it offers itself as a rigorous foundation for numerical computation.
The main theorem of computable analysis asserts that all computable functions are continuous.
Yet, discontinuous functions play an important role in numerical analysis,
both as solution operators of important computational problems,
such as algebraic or differential equations,
and as building blocks for stable algorithms,
such as testing for inequality or matrix diagonalisation.
In
[
Neu19
]

continuous envelopes
were introduced as a way of systematically studying the amount of continuously available information on a discontinuous function with the two-fold purpose of enabling the computation of partial information on problems with discontinuous solution operators and of facilitating the use of discontinuous operations within rigorous numerical software.
An envelope of a function

f\colon X\to Y

between effective
T_{0}
spaces is a pair of continuous maps

F\colon X\to L

and

\xi_{L}\colon Y\to L

with values in an effective injective space
L

such that

\xi_{L}\circ f\geq F

with respect to the specialisation order.

Before we proceed, let us review these notions.
We assume familiarity with the basic definitions and results of computable analysis, as for instance presented in
[

]

We will also make heavy use of the definitions and results of
[

]
.
A detailed introduction that covers everything we need is given in

We denote Sierpinski space by

For a represented space

We let

We call

All effective

The class of effective injective spaces is in a sense much larger than the class of effective continuous domains.

Effective and computable injective spaces have excellent closure properties: they are closed under finite products, exponentiation, and retracts

Propositions

1

and

1

are somewhat unsatisfactory.
They assert the existence of functions with certain properties but do not
yield an effective method that allows one to find such functions.

The issue stems from the non-constructivity of tightening relation:
an envelope
F
tightens an envelope
G
if there exists a map

\Phi\colon L\to M

satisfying

\Phi\circ\xi_{L}\leq\xi_{M}

and

\Phi\circ F\geq G
.
But the definition does not tell us how to find such a map.
By an observation of Escardó
[
Esc98
]
which essentially goes back to Scott
[
Sco72
]
there always exists
a greatest continuous “right extension”

(\xi_{M}/\xi_{L})\colon L\to M

of
\xi_{M}
along
\xi_{L}
,

i.e.
,
the map
(\xi_{M}/\xi_{L})
satisfies

(\xi_{M}/\xi_{L})\circ\xi_{L}\leq\xi_{M}
,
and if

is a continuous map which satisfies

then

h\leq(\xi_{M}/\xi_{L})
.
If any map witnesses the tightening relation, then so does
(\xi_{M}/\xi_{L})
.
But the map
R_{L,M}\colon M^{Y}\to M^{L}
which sends

to

is discontinuous in general.
We can therefore in general not expect
\widetilde{\nu_{Z}\circ\varphi}

in Proposition
1

to depend continuously on
i
and
\varphi
.
Computability of

may fail even point-wise.
The example
[

, Example 4.32]
shows that there exist computable

and

such that all continuous functions
\widetilde{i\circ\varphi}

as in Proposition
1

are uncomputable.

On the other hand, if

is such that

is continuous for all effective injective spaces

and

.
Escardó
[

]
has shown that

is continuous if and only if the upper adjoint of

is a proper map in the sense of Hofmann and Lawson

]

This constitutes arguably too much of a restriction.
For instance, it precludes the choice of the natural approximation space

But the existence of a continuous upper adjoint is not necessary for uniform envelopability.

Theorem

is nothing more than a simple observation.
Yet it has remarkable consequences:
firstly, it reduces the task of finding an envelope

and maps

as in Definition

to the rather more tractable task of checking a fairly concrete property of the

\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)
-envelope.
Secondly, we make hardly any assumptions on the maps

in Definition

By contrast,

is a monad on the category of effective

by applying the functor

In this paper, we introduce
uniform envelopes
, which constitute mild generalisations of principal

-envelopes.
A uniform envelope

of

is equipped with a map

that satisfies certain axioms which connect it to the monad structure of

.
As above, this yields a family of maps

for all injective spaces

.
The uniform envelope

tightens an (arbitrary) envelope

of
f
if it tightens
G
via the map
E_{L,M}(\xi_{M})
.
The uniform envelope

is uniformly universal if it uniformly tightens all envelopes
G
of
f
.
It follows essentially immediately from the definition that if

F
is uniformly universal
and

\varphi\colon Y\to Z

is any continuous map then

E_{L,\mathcal{O}^{2}(Z)}(\nu_{Z}\circ\varphi)\circ F

is a uniformly universal envelope of
\varphi\circ f
.

More generally, a uniform envelope
F\colon X\to L
of
f\colon X\to Y

and a uniform envelope

of

can be composed
to yield an envelope
G\bullet F
of

.
If
g
is not continuous, then

may fail to be uniformly universal.
We can explain this failure and identify situations in which it does not occur by studying how open sets of the
co-domain can be transported back along a function and its universal envelope.
An arbitrary function

transports open sets of
Y
to open sets of
X
via the – not necessarily continuous – function

f^{\circ}\colon\mathcal{O}(Y)\to\mathcal{O}(X),\;f^{\circ}(V)=f^{-1}(V)^{\circ}.

A continuous function

transports open sets of
Y
to open sets of
X
via the continuous function

F^{\star}\colon\mathcal{O}(Y)\to\mathcal{O}(X),\;F^{\star}(V)=\left\{x\in X\mid F(x)\ni V\right\}.

We show that
F
is uniformly universal if and only if
F^{\star}=f^{\circ}
.
We further show that
(G\bullet F)^{\star}=F^{\star}\circ G^{\star}

for all continuous maps
F\colon X\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)
and
G\colon Y\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(Z)
.
Thus,
G\bullet F
is a uniformly universal envelope of

if and only if
(g\circ f)^{\circ}=f^{\circ}\circ g^{\circ}
.
A sufficient condition for the latter is that
f
is open or that

is continuous.
We establish a partial converse to this.
We also show that in general the problem of proving that

(g\circ f)^{\circ}=f^{\circ}\circ g^{\circ}

can be extremely hard by reducing the hyperbolicity conjecture from complex dynamics to a specific instance of this problem.

It is natural to seek for a-priori criteria that guarantee uniform envelopability.
We show that a space
Y
has the property that every function
f\colon X\to Y
is uniformly envelopable if and only if it is Noetherian.
Dually, we show that a space
X

we have
E_{L,M}(\xi_{M}\circ g)\circ F\geq G
.
Now,

\displaystyle E_{L,M}(\xi_{M}\circ g)

\displaystyle=\rho_{M}\circ(\xi_{M}\circ g)^{**}\circ u_{L}

\displaystyle=\rho_{M}\circ\xi_{M}^{**}\circ g^{**}\circ u_{L}

\displaystyle=\rho_{M}\circ\xi_{M}^{**}\circ u_{\mathcal{O}^{2}(Z)}\circ g^{**}\circ u_{L}

\displaystyle=E_{\mathcal{O}^{2}(Z),M}(\xi_{M})\circ g^{**}\circ u_{L}.

It follows that
g^{**}\circ u_{L}\circ F
uniformly tightens
G
.

Proposition
9
is an obvious consequence of the definition of uniform universality.
If
\mathcal{C}
is the class of all envelopes then it says that post-composing a continuous map with a uniformly universal envelope
will again yield a uniformly universal envelope.

We immediately obtain effective versions of Propositions

1

and

1
.
We only state an effective version of the latter, as it is less straightforward:

Corollary 10
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a function between computable
T_{0}
spaces.
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a class of approximation spaces.
Let
F\colon X\to L
be a uniformly
\mathcal{C}
-universal envelope.
Let
\widetilde{X}
and

be a effective
T_{0}
spaces.
Let

be an effective continuous lattice.
Let

be a continuous map.
Let

be defined by

.
Assume that

belongs to
\mathcal{C}
.
Let

(1)

Every point

is a point of continuity of the function

If the right hand side of a composition is allowed to be the universal envelope of a discontinuous function then the composition may no longer be automatically uniformly universal.
As mentioned in the introduction, this leads to the study of how functions and their envelopes transport open sets of the co-domain to open sets of the domain.

For an arbitrary function
f\colon X\to Y
, let

f^{\circ}\colon\mathcal{O}(Y)\to\mathcal{O}(X),\;f^{\circ}(V)=f^{-1}(V)^{\circ}.

For a continuous function
F\colon X\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)
, let

F^{\star}\colon\mathcal{O}(Y)\to\mathcal{O}(X),\;F^{\star}(V)=\left\{x\in X\mid F(x)\ni V\right\}.

If
F
is an envelope of
f
then we clearly have
F^{\star}\subseteq f^{\circ}
.

Lemma 11
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a function between effective
T_{0}
spaces.
Let
F\colon X\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)
be an envelope of
f
.
Then
F
is uniformly universal if and only if

F^{\star}(V)=f^{\circ}(V)

for all open sets

V\in\mathcal{O}(Y)
.

Proof 2.7
.

Assume that
F
is uniformly universal.
Let
V\in\mathcal{O}(Y)
.
Then

F^{\star}(V)\subseteq f^{-1}(V)^{\circ}

since
F
is an envelope.
Let
x\in f^{-1}(V)^{\circ}
.
Then
V
is a robust property of
f(x)
.
It follows that
F(x)\ni V
.
Thus,
F^{\star}(V)=f^{-1}(V)^{\circ}
.

Conversely, assume that

F^{\star}(V)=f^{\circ}(V)

for all
V\in\mathcal{O}(Y)
.
If
V
is a robust property of
f(x)

then
x\in f^{-1}(V)^{\circ}
.
It follows that
V\in F(x)
.
It follows from Theorem
1

that
F
is uniformly universal.

Lemma 12
.

Let
X
,
Y
, and
Z
be effective
T_{0}
spaces.
Let
F\colon X\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)

and
G\colon Y\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(Z)

be continuous maps.
We have

(G\bullet F)^{\star}=F^{\star}\circ G^{\star}
.

Proof 2.8
.

One easily verifies that

F^{\star}=F^{*}\circ\nu_{\mathcal{O}(Y)}
.
Using this, we obtain

(G\bullet F)^{\star}=(\nu_{\mathcal{O}(Z)}^{*}\circ G^{**}\circ F)^{*}\circ\nu_{\mathcal{O}(Z)}=F^{*}\circ G^{***}\circ\nu_{\mathcal{O}(Z)}^{**}\circ\nu_{\mathcal{O}(Z)}.

Now, by naturality of the unit
\nu_{\mathcal{O}(Z)}
we have

\nu_{\mathcal{O}(Z)}^{**}\circ\nu_{\mathcal{O}(Z)}=\nu_{\mathcal{O}^{3}(Z)}\circ\nu_{\mathcal{O}(Z)}
,
so that

(G\bullet F)^{\star}=F^{*}\circ G^{***}\circ\nu_{\mathcal{O}^{3}(Z)}\circ\nu_{\mathcal{O}(Z)}=F^{*}\circ\nu_{\mathcal{O}(Y)}\circ G^{*}\circ\nu_{\mathcal{O}(Z)}=F^{\star}\circ G^{\star}.

The second-to-last equality is again using the naturality of
\nu
.

We conclude:

Theorem 13
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
and
g\colon Y\to Z
be functions between computable
T_{0}
spaces.
Let
F\colon X\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)
and
G\colon X\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(Z)
be the principal
\mathcal{O}^{2}
-envelopes of
f
and
g
respectively.
Assume that
F
and
G
are uniformly universal.
Then
G\bullet F
is a uniformly universal envelope of
g\circ f
if and only if

(g\circ f)^{\circ}=f^{\circ}\circ g^{\circ}
.

Theorem
13
leads to the question
for which pairs of functions
f\colon X\to Y
and
g\colon Y\to Z
we have the equality

(g\circ f)^{\circ}=f^{\circ}\circ g^{\circ}
.
We obtain a result that is sharp in a certain sense:

Theorem 14
.

(1)

Let
f\colon X\to Y
and
g\colon Y\to Z
be functions between effective
T_{0}
spaces.
Then we have
f^{\circ}\circ g^{\circ}\subseteq(g\circ f)^{\circ}
.

(2)

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a function between effective
T_{0}
spaces.
Let
Z
be an effective
T_{0}
space with at least two points.
We have

f^{\circ}\circ g^{\circ}=(g\circ f)^{\circ}

for all

g\colon Y\to Z

if and only if

f

sends open sets to open sets.

(3)

Let
g\colon Y\to Z
be a function between effective
T_{0}
spaces.
Let
X
be an effective
T_{0}
space.
We have

f^{\circ}\circ g^{\circ}=(g\circ f)^{\circ}

for all

f\colon X\to Y

if and only if

g

is continuous.

Proof 2.9
.

(1)

Obvious.

(2)

Assume that
f
sends open sets to open sets.
Let
V\in\mathcal{O}(Z)
.
Since we always have

f^{\circ}\circ g^{\circ}(V)\subseteq(g\circ f)^{\circ}(V)

it remains to prove the converse inclusion.
Let
x\in(g\circ f)^{\circ}(V)
.
Since
f
maps open sets to open sets,
the set

f((g\circ f)^{\circ}(V))

is contained in

g^{\circ}(V)
.
By applying
f^{-1}
to both sides of this containment we obtain

f^{-1}(g^{\circ}(V))\supseteq(g\circ f)^{\circ}(V)^{\circ}\ni x.

Thus,
x
is contained in an open set which is contained in

f^{-1}(g^{\circ}(V))
.
It follows that
x\in f^{-1}(g^{\circ}(V))^{\circ}=f^{\circ}\circ g^{\circ}(V)
.

This establishes the “if” part of the claim.
Let us now establish the “only if” part.
Fix
t,b\in Z
with
t\neq b
and
b\not\geq t
in the specialisation order of
Z
.
Let
U\in\mathcal{O}(X)
.
Our aim is to show that
f(U)
is an open subset of
Y
.
Consider the function

g\colon Y\to Z,\;g(y)=\begin{cases}t&\text{if }y\in f(U)\\
b&\text{if }y\notin f(U).\end{cases}

By assumption on
t
and
b
there exists an open set
V\in\mathcal{O}(Z)
such that

t\in V
and
b\notin V
.
We have by assumption on
f
:

U\subseteq f^{-1}(f(U))^{\circ}=(g\circ f)^{\circ}(V)=f^{\circ}\circ g^{\circ}(V)=f^{-1}(f(U)^{\circ})^{\circ}\subseteq f^{-1}(f(U)^{\circ}).

Applying
f
to both sides of the containment we find that

f(U)\subseteq f(U)^{\circ}
,
hence

f(U)=f(U)^{\circ}
.

(3)

If
g
is continuous, then
g^{\circ}=g^{-1}
, so that

(g\circ f)^{\circ}=f^{\circ}\circ g^{\circ}
follows immediately.

Conversely, assume that

g

satisfies

(g\circ f)^{\circ}=f^{\circ}\circ g^{\circ}

for all functions
f\colon X\to Y
.
Let
V\in\mathcal{O}(Z)
.
We claim that
g^{-1}(V)
is open.
If
g^{-1}(V)
is empty then the claim is trivial.
Thus, assume that
g^{-1}(V)
is non-empty.
Let
y\in g^{-1}(V)
.
Let
f\colon X\to Y
be the constant map with value
y
.
Then, by assumption,

X=f^{-1}(g^{-1}(V))^{\circ}=f^{-1}(g^{-1}(V)^{\circ})^{\circ}\subseteq f^{-1}(g^{-1}(V)^{\circ})

It follows that

f^{-1}(g^{-1}(V)^{\circ})=X
,
so that

y\in g^{-1}(V)^{\circ}
.
Thus,
g^{-1}(V)=g^{-1}(V)^{\circ}
,
so that
g^{-1}(V)
is open.

As an obvious corollary to Theorems
13
and
14

we obtain that the composition of two uniformly universal envelopes
G
of
g
and
F
of

is uniformly universal if the function
f
sends open sets to open sets.
Conversely, observing that the functions
g
used in the proof of Theorem

.2 are uniformly envelopable, we obtain that if

G\bullet F

is uniformly universal for all uniformly universal envelopes

,
where

is a fixed space containing at least two points,
then
f
sends open sets to open sets.

In this sense our result is sharp:
openness of
f
is sufficient and necessary in order for
all
compositions

G\bullet F

to be uniformly envelopable again.

Of course, the function
f
need not be open in order for the equation

(g\circ f)^{\circ}=f^{\circ}\circ g^{\circ}

to hold for
some

g
.
A stronger result would be obtained if one could characterise for a given function

f\colon X\to Y

and a given space

Z

precisely those functions

g\colon Y\to Z

that satisfy

(g\circ f)^{\circ}=f^{\circ}\circ g^{\circ}
.

A result of this generality seems to be completely out of reach, however.
Already for the – continuous – diagonal embedding

f\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}

we can find a single function

g\colon\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}\to\{0,1\}

such that the question whether

(g\circ f)^{\circ}=f^{\circ}\circ g^{\circ}

is equivalent to a long-standing open problem in complex dynamics.

The following argument will make heavy use of definitions and results from complex dynamics that due to space restrictions we can only briefly summarize.
The results we use are well known, but not easy to prove.
See
e.g.

[
CG93
,
Mil06
]
for good introductions to complex dynamics and
[
Bra89
]
for a good introduction to the Mandelbrot set.
See also
[
BY08
,
Her05
]
for results on the computability of Julia sets and the Mandelbrot set.

Let
X
be a set.
The orbit of a point
x\in X
under a function
f\colon X\to X

is the set
\left\{f^{(n)}(x)\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
.

The Mandelbrot set

M\subseteq\mathbb{C}

is the set of points

c\in\mathbb{C}

such that the orbit of

0\in\mathbb{C}

under the polynomial

z\mapsto z^{2}+c

is bounded.

The filled-in Julia set
K_{c}
of a point
c\in\mathbb{C}
is the set of all points
z\in\mathbb{C}

such that the orbit of
z
under the polynomial
z^{2}+c
is bounded.
The Julia set
J_{c}
of a point
c\in\mathbb{C}
is the boundary
\partial K_{c}
of the filled-in Julia set.

From these definitions we immediately obtain that
c\in M
if and only if
c\in K_{c}
.
Letting
\chi_{M}\colon M\to\{0,1\}
denote the characteristic function of the Mandelbrot set,
we obtain the commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{0,1\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x\mapsto(x,x)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\chi_{M}}

where

u\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}

is the diagonal embedding and

\Phi(z,c)=1

if and only if

z\in K_{c}
.
It is easy to see that all functions with finite co-domain are uniformly envelopable.
See also Theorem
15
below for a sharper result.
It follows that
\Phi
,
\chi_{M}
, and
\Phi\circ u
are uniformly envelopable.
Let
\widetilde{\chi_{M}}
denote the principal
\mathcal{O}^{2}(\{0,1\}))
-envelope of
\chi_{M}
.
Let

\widetilde{\Phi}\colon\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(\{0,1\})

denote the principal
\mathcal{O}^{2}(\{0,1\}))
-envelope of
\Phi
.
The function
u
is continuous and hence uniformly envelopable with
uniformly universal envelope

\nu_{\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}}\circ u\colon\mathbb{C}\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C})
.
Clearly,

\widetilde{\Phi}\bullet(\nu_{\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}}\circ u)=\widetilde{\Phi}\circ u
.
We will show that

\widetilde{\Phi}\circ u
is the principal

\mathcal{O}^{2}(\{0,1\})
-envelope of
\Phi\circ u
if and only if the so-called hyperbolicity conjecture holds true.
A parameter
c\in\mathbb{C}
is called
hyperbolic
if the polynomial
p_{c}(z)=z^{2}+c
has an attracting
cycle, see
e.g.

[
CG93
, pp. 127–128]
,
[
Bra89
, p. 82 ff.]
.
The set of hyperbolic parameters is an open subset of
M
.
The hyperbolicity conjecture
[
Bra89
, p. 83]
states that the set of hyperbolic parameters is equal to the interior of
M
.
It is considered to be one of the major unsolved open problems in complex dynamics.

We clearly have:

\widetilde{\chi_{M}}(c)=\begin{cases}\nu_{\{0,1\}}(1)&\text{if }c\in M^{\circ},\\
\nu_{\{0,1\}}(0)&\text{if }c\in M^{C},\\
\emptyset&\text{if }c\in\partial M.\end{cases}

By
[
Dou94
]
the sets

K_{c}

and

J_{c}

both depend continuously on
c
in the Hausdorff metric when
c
ranges over the set

\mathbb{C}\setminus\partial M
.
It follows that given
(z,c)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
we can test (relative to some oracle) if
c\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\partial M

and then semi-decide relative to some oracle if
c\in K_{c}\setminus J_{c}
.
Again by
[
Dou94
]
the predicate
z\notin K_{c}
is open for all
(z,c)\in\mathbb{C}
.
There are two cases not covered by these (relative) semi-decision procedures:
the case where
z\in\partial K_{c}
and the case where
c\in\partial M
.
But in both cases the value of
\Phi(z,c)
changes under arbitrarily small perturbations.

In summary, we have:

\widetilde{\Phi}(z,c)=\begin{cases}\nu_{\{0,1\}}(0)&\text{if }z\notin K_{c},\\
\nu_{\{0,1\}}(1)&\text{if }c\in M^{\circ}\text{ and }z\in K_{c}^{\circ},\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Now,

\widetilde{\Phi}(c,c)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }c\notin M,\\
1&\text{if }c\in M^{\circ}\text{ and }z\in K_{c}^{\circ},\\
\bot&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

It is shown in
[
DH84
, Proposition 5]
that if
c
belongs to a hyperbolic component of the interior of
M
, then
c
belongs to the interior of
K_{c}
.
Conversely, assume that
c
belongs to a non-hyperbolic component of the interior of
M
.
Then it follows from the Fatou-Sullivan classification

[
CG93
, Theorem 1.3, Theorem 2.1]

that
K_{c}
has empty interior, so that
c\in\partial K_{c}
.

It follows that
\widetilde{\Phi}(c,c)
is equal to
\widetilde{\chi_{M}}(c)
if and only if the interior of
M
is the union of the hyperbolic components.

3.
A connection with Noetherian spaces

Since uniform universality is a very desirable property it seems natural to ask if there exist simple criteria on a function

f\colon X\to Y

that a-priori guarantee uniform universality.
Perhaps the simplest conceivable criteria are those that put constraints on the domain or co-domain and let
f
be arbitrary otherwise.

Recall that a topological space is Noetherian if all descending sequences of closed sets stabilise after finitely many steps.
Noetherian spaces admit the following, straightforwardly verified, equivalent characterisation:
a space
Y
is Noetherian if and only if all open subsets of
Y
are compact, cf.
[
GL13
,
dBP17
]
.

Theorem 15
.

The following are equivalent for an effective
T_{0}
space
Y
:

(1)

For all effective
T_{0}
spaces
X
, all functions
f\colon X\to Y
are uniformly envelopable.

(2)

All functions
f\colon 2^{\mathbb{N}}\to Y
are uniformly envelopable.

(3)

Y
is Noetherian.

Proof 3.1
.

Let us first show the implication
(3)\Rightarrow(1)
.
Assume that
Y
is Noetherian.
Then every open subset of
Y
is compact.
Let
f\colon X\to Y
be any function.
Let
x\in X
.
Let
U\in\mathcal{O}(Y)
be a robust property of
f(x)
.
Then the set

\left\uparrow{U}\right.=\left\{V\in\mathcal{O}(Y)\mid V\supseteq U\right\}

is open.
Let
W\in\mathcal{O}(X)
be such that
x\in W\subseteq f^{-1}(U)
.
Consider the
\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)
-envelope

G(z)=\begin{cases}\left\uparrow{U}\right.&\text{if }z\in W,\\
\emptyset&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

This is an envelope of
f
by construction.
It satisfies
U\in G(x)
.
It follows that the principal
\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)
-envelope
F
satisfies
U\in F(x)
.
Since
U
was an arbitrary robust property it follows from Theorem
1
that
F
is uniformly universal.

The implication
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
is trivial.
It thus remains to show the implication
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
.
Assume that all functions
f\colon 2^{\mathbb{N}}\to Y
are uniformly envelopable.
Let
U\in\mathcal{O}(Y)
be an open subset of
Y
.
We will show that
U
is compact.
We may assume that
U
contains at least two points, for otherwise the claim is trivial.
Let
0\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
denote the constant zero sequence.
It suffices to show that there exists a function

f\colon 2^{\mathbb{N}}\to Y

such that the set of robust properties of

f(0)

is

\left\{V\in\mathcal{O}(Y)\mid V\supseteq U\right\}
.
Indeed, since the principal
\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)
-envelope
F
of
f
is assumed to be uniformly universal
we must have that
F(0)
is the filter of robust properties of
f(0)
.
It then follows that
\left\{V\in\mathcal{O}(Y)\mid V\supseteq U\right\}
is open, i.e.,
U
is compact.

To prove the claim, fix
y_{0}\in U
.
Note that
U\setminus\{y_{0}\}
is non-empty by assumption on
U
.
Observe that there exist continuum-many disjoint sequences in
2^{\mathbb{N}}
that converge to
0\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}
.
Since
U\setminus\{y_{0}\}
contains at most continuum-many points, we can find a set of distinct points

p_{y,n}\in 2^{\mathbb{N}}

where
y
ranges over
U\setminus\{y_{0}\}

and
n
ranges over
\mathbb{N}
,
such that for all
y\setminus\{y_{0}\}
we have that
p_{y,n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Thus, we obtain a well-defined partial function
f
on
2^{\mathbb{N}}
by letting

f(p_{y,n})=y
.
Let
f
map all remaining points of
2^{\mathbb{N}}
to
y_{0}
.

Then
f^{-1}(U)=2^{\mathbb{N}}
, so that
U
is a robust property of
f(0)
.
Now, let
V\in\mathcal{O}(Y)
be an open set
not containing
U

with
y_{0}\in V
.
Then there exists
y\in U
such that
y\notin V
.
It follows that

f^{-1}(V)\subseteq 2^{\mathbb{N}}\setminus\left\{p_{y,n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

which implies that
f^{-1}(V)
cannot be a neighbourhood of
0
.
It follows that
V
is not a robust property of
f(0)
.
This finishes the proof.

While being Noetherian is a rather strong restriction, there are some interesting non-trivial examples of Noetherian effective
T_{0}
spaces.
Notably the spectra of Noetherian commutative rings with the Zariski topology, provided that their topology is countably based,
and well-quasi orders on
\mathbb{N}
with the Alexandroff topology
[
dBP17
,
GL13
]
.

Let us now turn to the analogous question for
X
.
We first require a technical lemma:

Lemma 16
.

Let
X
be an effective
T_{0}
space that does not contain an infinite compact set.
Then
X
is countable, countably based, and for all
x\in X
the set
\left\uparrow{x}\right.
is open and finite.

Proof 3.2
.

Let
x\in X
.
Let
(x_{n})_{n}
be a sequence that converges to
x
.
Then the set
\left\{x_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\cup\{x\}
is compact, and hence finite.
It follows that
x_{n}\in\left\uparrow{x}\right.
for all sufficiently large
n
.
Since the topology on
X
is sequential, the set
\left\uparrow{x}\right.
is open.
Since
\left\uparrow{x}\right.
is compact, it is finite.

It remains to show that
X
is countable.
Then
X
is countably based, for the sets
\left\uparrow{x}\right.
, where
x\in X
, form a basis of the topology.
Since
X
is an effective
T_{0}
space, it admits a countable pseudobase
(B_{n})_{n}

[
Sch02
, Theorem 13]
.
Let
y\in\left\uparrow{x}\right.
. Then, by definition (
[
Sch02
, Section 3.1]
), there exists
j\in\mathbb{N}
with

y\in B_{j}\subseteq\left\uparrow{x}\right.
.
Since
\left\uparrow{x}\right.
is finite, there exist finitely many numbers
j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\left\uparrow{x}\right.=B_{j_{1}}\cup\dots\cup B_{j_{s}}
.
Fix any map that sends
x\in X
to
\langle j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\rangle\in\mathbb{N}^{*}

with
\left\uparrow{x}\right.=B_{j_{1}}\cup\dots\cup B_{j_{s}}
.
Since
X
is
T_{0}
, this map is injective.
It follows that
X
is countable.
Thus, everything is shown.

Theorem 17
.

Let
X
be an effective
T_{0}
space.
Then all functions
f\colon X\to Y
are uniformly envelopable if and only if
X
contains no infinite compact sets.

Proof 3.3
.

Assume that
X
contains an infinite compact set.
Then
X
contains an infinite sequence
(x_{n})_{n}
of distinct points that
converges to a limit
x_{\infty}
with
x_{\infty}\neq x_{n}
for all
n
.
Let
f\colon X\to\mathbb{R}
be defined as follows:

f(x_{n})=1/n

and
f(x)=1

for all
x\in X\setminus\left\{x_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
.
Let
F\colon X\to\mathcal{K}_{\bot}(\mathbb{R})
be the principal
\mathcal{K}_{\bot}(\mathbb{R})
-envelope of
f
.
Then
F(x_{\infty})\ni 0=\lim_{n\to\infty}f(x_{n})
.
But
f^{-1}((0,+\infty))=X
, so that the interval
(0,+\infty)
is a robust property of
f(x_{\infty})

wich is not witnessed by
F
.
It follows that
F
is not uniformly universal.

Assume that
X
contains no infinite compact subsets.
Let
f\colon X\to Y
be any function.
By Lemma
16

the space
X
contains at most countably many points
and
for all
x\in X
the set
\left\uparrow{x}\right.
is a finite open set.
It follows in particular that the set
f(\left\uparrow{x}\right.)
is a finite and hence compact subset of
Y
.

There hence exist continuous functions

\omega\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathcal{O}(X)

and

\varphi\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathcal{K}(Y),

such that
\omega
maps
\mathbb{N}
onto the set of all sets of the form
\left\uparrow{x}\right.
for
x\in X
,
and
\varphi(n)=f(\omega(n))
.

Let

F(x)=\left\{V\in\mathcal{O}(Y)\mid V\supseteq f(\left\uparrow{x}\right.)\right\}.

Then
F
is continuous, for it is computable relative to the oracles
\omega
and
\varphi
above.
Indeed, consider the following algorithm:
given

x\in X

and

V\in\mathcal{O}(Y)

search for a number
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\omega(n)\supseteq\left\uparrow{x}\right.

and

\varphi(n)\subseteq V
.
Halt if and only if such a number exists.
On the one hand, if this algorithm halts then there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

f(\left\uparrow{x}\right.)\subseteq f(\omega(n))=\varphi(n)\subseteq V.

On the other hand, if
f(\left\uparrow{x}\right.)\subseteq V
then the algorithm will halt,
for there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
with
\omega(n)=\left\uparrow{x}\right.
.

Now,
F(x)\subseteq\nu_{Y}\circ f(x)
by definition.
The function
F
is hence an envelope of
f
.
If
V\in\mathcal{O}(Y)
is a robust property of
f(x)
then clearly
V\supseteq f(\left\uparrow{x}\right.)
.
It follows that
F
is uniformly universal.

4.
Regular envelopes

{defi}

An approximation space
\xi_{L}\colon Y\to L
is called
separated

if the map

\xi_{L}^{-1}(\left\uparrow{\cdot}\right.)\colon L\to\mathcal{A}(Y),\;\xi_{L}^{-1}(\left\uparrow{x}\right.)=\left\{y\in Y\mid\xi_{L}(y)\geq x\right\}

is well-defined and continuous.
It is called
computably separated
if
\xi_{L}^{-1}(\left\uparrow{\cdot}\right.)
is computable.

A separated approximation space

\xi_{L}\colon Y\to L

is called
regular

if for all

x\in L
and all
V\in\mathcal{O}(L)
with
x\in V

there exists
U\in\mathcal{O}(L)
with
x\in U
and

\operatorname{cl}{\xi_{L}^{-1}(U)}\subseteq\xi_{L}^{-1}(V)
.

Note that the definition of separated approximation space given here is strictly more general than the one
given in
[
Neu19
]
.
The name “separated” is motivated by the following observation:

Proposition 18
.

Let
X
be an effective
T_{0}
space.
Then
X
is (computably) Hausdorff if and only if the approximation space

\nu_{X}\colon X\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(X)

is (computably) separated.

The name “regular” is motivated by the following observation:

Proposition 19
.

Let
X
be a an effective
T_{0}
space.
If the approximation space

\nu_{X}\colon X\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(X)

is regular, then
X
is a regular topological space.

For countably based spaces the converse of Proposition
19
holds true as well:

Proposition 20
.

Let
Y
be a complete effective metric space.
Then the approximation spaces

(\kappa_{\bot})_{Y}\colon Y\to\mathcal{K}_{\bot}(Y)

and

\nu_{Y}\colon Y\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)

are regular.

Proof 4.1
.

The space
\mathcal{K}_{\bot}(Y)
is an approximation space since
\mathcal{K}_{\bot}(Y)
is injective by
[
Neu19
, Proposition 3.24]
.
It is easy to see that
\mathcal{K}_{\bot}(Y)
is separated.
Let us now verify that the approximation space is regular.
The element
\bot
is contained in a single open set, namely
\mathcal{K}_{\bot}(Y)
.
We have

(\kappa_{\bot})_{Y}^{-1}(\left\uparrow{\bot}\right.)=Y=\operatorname{cl}{Y}=(\kappa_{\bot})_{Y}^{-1}(\mathcal{K}_{\bot}(Y)).

Now, let
K\in\mathcal{K}(Y)
and let
V\in\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{K}_{\bot}(Y))
with
K\in V
.
The sets of the form

[U]=\left\{K\in\mathcal{K}(Y)\mid K\subseteq U\right\},

where
U\in\mathcal{O}(Y)
,
form a basis for the topology of
\mathcal{K}(Y)

[
Neu19
, Proposition 2.40]
.
There hence exists
U\in\mathcal{O}(Y)
with
K\in[U]\subseteq V
.
We have
(\kappa_{\bot})_{Y}^{-1}(\left\uparrow{K}\right.)=K

and
(\kappa_{\bot})_{Y}^{-1}([U])=U
.
Since
K
is compact and
Y
is regular Hausdorff there exists a set

W\in\mathcal{O}(Y)
such that

K\subseteq W\subseteq\operatorname{cl}{W}\subseteq U\subseteq(\kappa_{\bot})_{Y}^{-1}(V).

We have
W=(\kappa_{\bot})_{Y}^{-1}([W])
with
K\in[W]
and

(\kappa_{\bot})_{Y}^{-1}(\left\uparrow{K}\right.)\subseteq(\kappa_{\bot})_{Y}^{-1}([W])\subseteq\operatorname{cl}{(\kappa_{\bot})_{Y}^{-1}([W])}\subseteq(\kappa_{\bot})_{Y}^{-1}(V).

This proves the claim.

Now consider the approximation space
\nu_{Y}\colon Y\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)
.
By
[
dBK19
, Theorem 6.11]
a basis for the open sets of
\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)
is given by sets the of the form

[U_{1},\dots,U_{n}]=\left\{\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)\mid U_{1}\in\mathcal{U}\land\dots\land U_{n}\in\mathcal{U}\right\}.

with
U_{1},\dots,U_{n}\in\mathcal{O}(Y)
.
Thus,
let

\mathcal{U}\in\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)

and assume that

\mathcal{U}\in[U_{1},\dots,U_{n}]
.
Since Polish spaces are consonant (see, e.g.,
[
dBK19
, Section 6]
or
[
Neu19
, Section 2.6]
) there exists a family of compact sets

(K_{i})_{i\in I}

such that

U\in\mathcal{U}

if and only if

U\supseteq K_{i}

for some
i\in I
.
It follows that there exist
i_{1},\dots,i_{n}
such that

K_{i_{j}}\subseteq U_{j}

for
j=1,\dots,n
.
Again using that
Y
is a regular topological space, we can find for each
j=1,\dots,n
an open set
V_{j}\in\mathcal{O}(Y)

with

K_{i_{j}}\subseteq V_{j}\subseteq\operatorname{cl}{V_{j}}\subseteq U_{j}
.
Then

\mathcal{U}\in[V_{1},\dots,V_{n}]

and

\displaystyle\nu_{Y}(\left\uparrow{\mathcal{U}}\right.)

\displaystyle\subseteq\nu_{Y}([V_{1},\dots,V_{n}])=V_{1}\cap\dots\cap V_{n}

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{cl}{V_{1}\cap\dots\cap V_{n}}\subseteq U_{1}\cap\dots\cap U_{n}=\nu_{Y}^{-1}([U_{1}\cap\dots\cap U_{n}]).

This proves the claim.

A straightforward compactness argument shows that the interpolation property of regular approximation spaces
extends to all compact sets:

Lemma 21
.

A separated approximation space
\xi_{L}\colon Y\to L
is regular
if and only if
for all

K\in\mathcal{K}(L)
and all
V\in\mathcal{O}(L)
with
K\subseteq V

there exists
U\in\mathcal{O}(L)
with
K\subseteq U
and

\operatorname{cl}{\xi_{L}^{-1}(U)}\subseteq\xi_{L}^{-1}(V)
.

Proposition 22
.

Let

\xi_{L}\colon Y\to L

be a regular approximation space.
Let
Z
be an effective
T_{0}
space.
Assume that
L^{Z}
carries the compact-open topology.
Then

(\xi_{L})_{*}\colon Y^{Z}\to L^{Z},\;(\xi_{L})_{*}(h)(z)=\xi_{L}(h(z))

is again a regular approximation space.

Proof 4.2
.

Let us first show that

(\xi_{L})_{*}\colon Y^{Z}\to L^{Z}

is separated.
For all

\varphi\colon Z\to L

we have

(\xi_{L})_{*}^{-1}(\left\uparrow{\varphi}\right.)=\left\{h\colon Z\to Y\mid\forall z\in Z.h(z)\in\xi_{L}^{-1}(\left\uparrow{\varphi(z)}\right.)\right\}.

By assumption, the map

\xi_{L}^{-1}(\left\uparrow{\cdot}\right.)\colon L\to\mathcal{A}(Y)

is well-defined and continuous.
It follows that the predicate

\exists z\in Z.h(z)\notin\xi_{L}^{-1}(\left\uparrow{\varphi(z)}\right.)

is relatively semi-decidable in
\varphi
and
h
.
This proves the claim.

Now let us show that the approximation space is regular.
Let
\varphi\colon Z\to L
.
Let
\varphi\in V\in\mathcal{O}(L^{Z})
.
By assumption
L^{Z}
carries the compact-open topology,
so that we may assume without loss of generality that

V=\left\{\psi\colon Z\to L\mid\psi(K)\subseteq W\right\}

for some
K\in\mathcal{K}(Z)
and
W\in\mathcal{O}(L)
.
Thus,

\varphi(K)\subseteq W
.
By Lemma
21

there exists a set
U\in\mathcal{O}(L)
with

\varphi(K)\subseteq U

and

\operatorname{cl}{\xi_{L}^{-1}(U)}\subseteq\xi_{L}^{-1}(W)
.
We thus have

\varphi\in\left\{\psi\colon Z\to L\mid\psi(K)\subseteq U\right\}

and

\displaystyle(\xi_{L})_{*}^{-1}\left(\left\{\psi\colon Z\to L\mid\psi(K)\subseteq U\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=\left\{h\colon Z\to Y\mid h(K)\subseteq\xi_{L}^{-1}(U)\right\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\operatorname{cl}{\left\{h\colon Z\to Y\mid h(K)\subseteq\xi_{L}^{-1}(U)\right\}}

\displaystyle\subseteq\left\{h\colon Z\to Y\mid h(K)\subseteq\xi_{L}^{-1}(W)\right\}

\displaystyle=(\xi_{L})_{*}^{-1}(V).

This proves the claim.

Proposition 23
.

Let

and

be countably based effective

If

or

Proof 4.3
.

It follows from Theorem 7 and Section 4.4 in

that the space

carries the sequentialisation of the compact-open topology.
If

is locally compact and

then the compact-open topology on

is countably based.

This establishes the first part of the claim.

To prove the second part, observe that the topology on

(cf.
[

It hence suffices to show that the Scott topology on

coincides with the compact-open topology.
This can be proved by a rather straightforward adaptation of the proof of Theorem 4.1 in

, which establishes the result in the special case
where

.
For the sake of completeness, the proof is included in Appendix
A
.

Propositions

and

show that the definition of “separated approximation space” chosen here

Proposition 24
.

Let

and

be effective

Let

.

Proof 4.4
.

Since
g
is continuous,
the map

is well-defined and continuous.
For all

(\xi_{M}\circ g)^{-1}(\left\uparrow{x}\right.)=g^{-1}(\xi_{M}^{-1}(\left\uparrow{x}\right.))
.
Separatedness follows.

Let
x\in M
and

Then by regularity of

such that

Let
\mathcal{R}
denote the class of all regular approximation spaces.
Accordingly an envelope is called an
\mathcal{R}
-envelope if it takes values in a regular approximation space.
It is called (uniformly)
\mathcal{R}
-universal if it (uniformly) tightens all
\mathcal{R}
-envelopes.

\mathcal{R}
-universal envelopes witness all robust properties “up to a small perturbation”
in the following sense:

.

By Urysohn’s lemma there exists a continuous function

\displaystyle\xi_{L}^{-1}\circ\Phi^{-1}\left(\left\{K\in\mathcal{K}_{\bot}(\mathbb{R})\mid K\subseteq\left(-\infty,\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\right\}\right)

5.
Advice bundles and co-envelopes

We can assign to any function
f\colon X\to Y
a canonical envelope that generalises the principal
\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)
-envelope of uniformly envelopable functions.
On these envelopes we can define a – in general non-associative – composition operation which generalises composition of uniform envelopes.
In order to achieve this we need to first pass to a dual notion.
The point of departure is the observation that we can identify a uniform envelope
F\colon X\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)
with the map

F^{\star}\colon\mathcal{O}(Y)\to\mathcal{O}(X)
which approximates the function
f^{\circ}\colon\mathcal{O}(Y)\to\mathcal{O}(X)
.

Let
Y
be an effective
T_{0}
space.
An
advice bundle

(A,\pi_{A})

over
Y
is an injective space
A

together with a continuous map

\pi_{A}\colon A\to\mathcal{O}(Y)

which preserves finite meets and arbitrary joins,
and which admits a continuous section

s\colon\mathcal{O}(Y)\to A
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a function.
A
co-envelope
of
f
is an advice bundle
A
over
\mathcal{O}(Y)
together with a continuous map

F^{\star}\colon A\to\mathcal{O}(X)

satisfying

F^{\star}\leq f^{\circ}\circ\pi_{A}
.
As in the case of envelopes, for all advice bundles
A
over
\mathcal{O}(Y)
there exists a greatest continuous
map
F^{\star}\colon A\to\mathcal{O}(X)
satisfying
F^{\star}\leq f^{\circ}\circ\pi_{A}
.
We call this the
principal
A
-co-envelope
.

If

F^{\star}\colon A\to\mathcal{O}(X)

and

G^{\star}\colon B\to\mathcal{O}(X)

are co-envelopes of
f
then

F^{\star}

tightens

G^{\star}

if there exists a continuous map

\Phi\colon B\to A

such that

\pi_{A}\circ\Phi=\pi_{B}

and

F^{\star}\circ\Phi\geq G^{\star}
.
A co-envelope of
f
is called
universal
if it tightens all co-envelopes of
f
.

The name “advice bundle” is motivated as follows:
Since the map
\pi_{A}
preserves joins and meets, the fibre

\pi_{A}^{*}(V)

of an open set
V\in\mathcal{O}(Y)
under
\pi_{A}
is a lattice.
By virtue of a section
s\colon\mathcal{O}(Y)\to A

the set
V
can be identified with the element
s(V)\in\pi_{A}^{*}(V)
.
The elements of
\pi_{A}^{*}(V)
above
s(V)
can be interpreted as
“
V
enriched with additional information”.

Envelopes and co-envelopes are connected as follows:
For a function

f\colon X\to Y

let

\mathcal{E}(f)

denote the category whose objects are envelopes

(F,\xi_{L})
of
f

such that
\xi_{L}^{*}
has a continuous section

s\colon\mathcal{O}(Y)\to\mathcal{O}(L)
,
and whose morphisms

\Phi\colon(F,\xi_{L})\to(G,\xi_{M})

are continuous maps

\Phi\colon L\to M

satisfying

\Phi\circ\xi_{L}=\xi_{M}

and

\Phi\circ F\geq G
.

It is easy to see that the pre-order one obtains from
\mathcal{E}(f)
by collapsing all morphisms into a single arrow
is isomorphic to the pre-order of envelopes of
f
with the tightening relation:
An envelope

F\colon X\to L

with inclusion map

\xi_{L}\colon Y\to L

can be made into the envelope

H\times F\colon X\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)\times L
,
where
H
is the constant function with value
\emptyset
,
with inclusion map

\nu_{Y}\times\xi_{L}
.
If
F
tightens
G

then
H\times F
tightens
H\times G

via the greatest continuous extension

(\nu_{Y}\times\xi_{M})/(\nu_{Y}\times\xi_{L})

of
(\nu_{Y}\times\xi_{M})
along
(\nu_{Y}\times\xi_{L})
,
which is easily seen to satisfy

(\nu_{Y}\times\xi_{M})/(\nu_{Y}\times\xi_{L})\circ(\nu_{Y}\circ\xi_{L})=(\nu_{Y}\times\xi_{M})
.
Thus, for our purpose, the additional restrictions put on envelopes and the tightening relation are inessential.

Let

\mathcal{A}(f)

denote the category whose objects are co-envelopes

(F^{\star},\pi_{A})
of
f

and whose morphisms

\Phi\colon(F^{\star},\pi_{A})\to(G^{\star},\pi_{B})

are continuous maps

\Phi\colon B\to A

that witness the tightening relation.
Then we have a functor

\mathcal{F}\colon\mathcal{A}(f)\to\mathcal{E}(f)

that sends objects

(F^{\star},\pi_{A})

to

((F^{\star})^{*}\circ\nu_{X},\pi_{A}^{*}\circ\nu_{Y})

and morphisms

\Phi\colon(F^{\star},\pi_{A})\to(G^{\star},\pi_{B})

to

\Phi^{*}\colon\mathcal{O}(A)\to\mathcal{O}(B)
.
In the other direction we have a functor

\mathcal{G}\colon\mathcal{E}(f)\to\mathcal{A}(f)

that sends objects

(F,\xi_{L})

to

(F^{*},\xi_{L}^{*})

and morphisms

\Phi\colon L\to M

to

\Phi^{*}\colon\mathcal{O}(M)\to\mathcal{O}(L)
.
One easily verifies that
\mathcal{F}
is a left adjoint of
\mathcal{G}
.
Any co-envelope

(F^{\star},\pi_{A})

is naturally tightened by

\mathcal{G}\circ\mathcal{F}(F^{\star},\pi_{A})

and any envelope

(F,\xi_{L})

naturally tightens

\mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{G}(F,\xi_{L})
.
Conversely,
\mathcal{F}\circ\mathcal{G}(F,\xi_{L})
tightens
(F,\xi_{L})
,
and
\mathcal{G}\circ\mathcal{F}(F^{\star},\pi_{A})
tightens
(F^{\star},\pi_{A})
,
but this tightening is not natural.
Nonetheless, if we forget about the specific choice of map for the tightening and thus make

\mathcal{E}(f)
and
\mathcal{A}(f)
into pre-orders,
then the functors
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
induce an equivalence.

In particular every function has a universal co-envelope, which corresponds to a universal envelope.

We have the following straightforward dual to Scott’s observation
[
Sco72
]
.

.

Let

A,B,C

be effective complete lattices.

An advice bundle

If

Theorem 37

Let

be a function between effective
T_{0}
spaces.
Then
f
has a least advice bundle

\mathfrak{A}_{f}
.

Proof 5.1
.

Let

L_{f}=\left\{(U,V)\in\mathcal{O}(X)\times\mathcal{O}(Y)\mid U\subseteq f^{-1}(V)\right\}.

Let
\pi_{X}\colon L_{f}\to\mathcal{O}(X)
be the map that sends
(U,V)
to
U
.
Let
\pi_{Y}\colon L_{f}\to\mathcal{O}(Y)
be the map that sends
(U,V)
to
V
.
Observe that the space
L_{f}
is an effective complete lattice:

\mathcal{O}(X)
and
\mathcal{O}(Y)
are effective complete lattices, so that
the product
\mathcal{O}(X)\times\mathcal{O}(Y)
is an effective complete lattice by

[
Neu19
, Proposition 3.5]
.
Further, by the same proposition, overt joins and compact meets in
\mathcal{O}(X)\times\mathcal{O}(Y)
are given
by component-wise union and intersection respectively.
Since the map
f^{-1}\colon 2^{\mathcal{O}(Y)}\to 2^{\mathcal{O}(X)}
preserves arbitrary unions and intersections,
it follows that
L_{f}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(X)\times\mathcal{O}(Y)
is closed under overt joins and compact meets.
Now,
[
Neu19
, Proposition 3.4]
yields that
L_{f}
is an effective complete lattice, as claimed.
By Proposition
36
there exists a greatest continuous map

P_{f}\colon L_{f}\to L_{f}

with

\pi_{Y}\circ P_{f}=\pi_{Y}
.
Since

\pi_{Y}\circ\operatorname{id}_{L_{f}}=\pi_{Y}

and

\pi_{Y}\circ P_{f}^{2}=\pi_{Y}

we have

P_{f}^{2}=P_{f}
.
It follows that
P_{f}
is a projection onto its range
\mathfrak{A}_{f}
.
It follows from
[
Neu19
, Proposition 3.7]
that
\mathfrak{A}_{f}
is an effective complete lattice.
The map

\pi_{Y}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{f}\to\mathcal{O}(Y)

preserves arbitrary joins and finite meets.
It has the continuous section

\sigma\colon\mathcal{O}(Y)\to\mathfrak{A}_{f},\;\sigma(V)=P_{f}(\emptyset,V).

We claim that

\mathfrak{A}_{f}

is a least advice bundle.
We will defer the proof that
\mathfrak{A}_{f}
is injective until the very end.
Nonetheless we will apply the terminology we have introduced above for advice bundles
to
\mathfrak{A}_{f}
already.

We claim that the principal
\mathfrak{A}_{f}
-co-envelope of
f^{\circ}
is given by
\pi_{X}
.
Let

F^{\star}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{f}\to\mathcal{O}(X)

be a co-envelope of

f^{\circ}
.
Then

F^{\star}(U,V)\subseteq f^{\circ}(V)
.
It follows that the map

L_{f}\to L_{f},\;(U,V)\mapsto(F^{\star}\circ P_{f}(U,V),V)

is well-defined.
It is hence below
P_{f}
.
But this means

F^{\star}(U,V)\subseteq U

for all

(U,V)\in\mathfrak{A}_{f}
.
Thus,
\pi_{X}
is indeed the principal co-envelope.

Let

\pi_{E}\colon E\to\mathcal{O}(Y)

be an advice bundle such that the principal co-envelope

G^{\star}\colon E\to\mathcal{O}(X)

is co-universal.

Consider the map

\Phi\colon E\to\mathfrak{A}_{f},\;\Phi(x)=P_{f}(G^{\star}(x),\pi_{E}(x)).

This map is well-defined, for

G^{\star}\subseteq f^{\circ}\circ\pi_{E}

since

G^{\star}

is a co-envelope.
It obviously satisfies
\pi_{Y}\circ\Phi=\pi_{E}
.
Now,

\pi_{X}\circ\Phi

with projection

\pi_{Y}\circ\Phi=\pi_{E}

is an
E
-co-envelope of
f
.
Since
G^{\star}
is assumed to be the principal
E
-co-envelope
it follows that
\pi_{X}\circ\Phi=G^{\star}
.
From this it easily follows that
\Phi
is the only map that witnesses
the tightening of
G^{\star}
by
F^{\star}
.

Since
G^{\star}
is assumed to be universal there exists a map

\Psi\colon\mathfrak{A}_{f}\to E

such that

\pi_{E}\circ\Psi=\pi_{Y}

and

G^{\star}\circ\Psi=\pi_{X}
.

Let
(U,V)\in\mathfrak{A}_{f}
.
Then

\Phi\circ\Psi((U,V))=P_{f}(G^{\star}\circ\Psi(U,V),\pi_{E}\circ\Psi(U,V))=P_{f}(U,V)=(U,V).

It follows that

\mathfrak{A}_{f}

is a retract of

E

as claimed.

To make

\mathfrak{A}_{f}

into a least advice bundle it remains to show that

\mathfrak{A}_{f}

is an injective space.
But using the above proof and the functors
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{G}
defined above,
we obtain that

\mathcal{O}^{2}(\mathfrak{A}_{f})

is a universal advice bundle,
so that
\mathfrak{A}_{f}

is a retract of
\mathcal{O}^{2}(\mathfrak{A}_{f})
in a unique way.
This shows that
\mathfrak{A}_{f}
is indeed injective.

Theorem
37
yields an explicit construction
of the least advice bundle
\mathfrak{A}_{f}
of a given function
f\colon X\to Y
.
However, the specific representation of
\mathfrak{A}_{f}
obtained in this construction is
not very useful from a computational point of view,
since the principal co-envelope is simply a projection.
The computational content of this envelope seems to be, so to speak, hidden in the complexity of the
identification of
\mathfrak{A}_{f}
with a subspace of
\mathcal{O}(X)\times\mathcal{O}(Y)
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a function between effective
T_{0}
spaces.
Let
\mathfrak{A}_{f}
be its least advice bundle.
We claim that the fibres
\pi_{Y}^{*}(V)
are injective spaces.
For every

V\in\mathcal{O}(Y)

the set

\pi_{Y}^{*}(V)

is a retract of the lattice

L_{f}(V)=\left\{U\in\mathcal{O}(X)\mid U\subseteq f^{-1}(V)\right\}
.
The lattice
L_{f}(V)
itself is a retract of
\mathcal{O}(X)
under the continuous map

U\mapsto U\cap f^{-1}(V)^{\circ}
.
Since injective spaces are closed under retracts, it follows that

\pi_{Y}^{*}(V)

is injective.
We will also write
\mathfrak{A}_{f}(V)
for
\pi_{Y}^{*}(V)
.

It is easy to see that the map

\sigma\colon\mathcal{O}(Y)\to\mathfrak{A}_{f}

which sends

V\in\mathcal{O}(Y)

to

P_{f}(\emptyset,V)\in\mathfrak{A}_{f}

is a lower adjoint of

\pi_{Y}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{f}\to\mathcal{O}(Y)
.

The

primary co-envelope

of

f\colon X\to Y

is the principal co-envelope

\mathfrak{E}_{f}^{\star}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{f}\to\mathcal{O}(X).

The

primary envelope

of

f

is its dual

\mathfrak{E}_{f}\colon X\to\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{A}_{f}).

Let
f\colon X\to Y
and
g\colon Y\to Z
be functions.
Let

\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{g}\colon\mathcal{O}(Y)

and

\mathfrak{E}_{f}^{\star}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{f}\to\mathcal{O}(X)

be their respective primary co-envelopes.
Then the

composition

\mathfrak{E}_{f}^{\star}\bullet\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}

is defined as:

\mathfrak{E}_{f}^{\star}\bullet\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}=\mathfrak{E}_{f}^{\star}\circ\left(\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{$\pi_{Y}$}\hskip-2.5pt{\big\backslash}\hskip-1.99997pt\raisebox{2.5pt}{$\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}$}\right).

This generalises the composition of principal
\mathcal{O}^{2}(Y)
-envelopes.
Due to the presence of the greatest continuous lift, the composition is in general no longer associative.

We obtain an analogous result to
Theorem
13
:

Theorem 38
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
,

g\colon Y\to Z
.
Let

\mathfrak{E}_{f}^{\star}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{f}\to\mathcal{O}(X)

and

\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{g}\to\mathcal{O}(Y)

be the primary co-envelopes of
f
and
g
respectively.
Assume that either of the following two conditions is satisfied:

(1)

f
is open

(2)

g
is uniformly envelopable and
(g\circ f)^{\circ}=f^{\circ}\circ g^{\circ}
.

Then

\mathfrak{E}_{f}^{\star}\bullet\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}

is the principal

\mathfrak{A}_{g}
-co-envelope
of
g\circ f
.

Proof 5.2
.

Let

H^{\star}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{g}\to\mathcal{O}(X)

be a
\mathfrak{A}_{g}
-co-envelope of

g\circ f
.
We use the representation of
\mathfrak{A}_{g}
from Theorem
37
.
Let

(U,V)\in\mathfrak{A}_{g}
.
Then, under either of the above assumptions we have by Theorem
14
:

H^{\star}(U,V)\subseteq(g\circ f)^{\circ}(V)=f^{\circ}\circ g^{\circ}(V).

(5)

Assume that
f
is open.
Then,
since the spaces
\mathcal{O}(X)
and
\mathcal{O}(Y)
carry the Scott topology,
the map
f_{*}\colon\mathcal{O}(X)\to\mathcal{O}(Y)

which sends
U\in\mathcal{O}(X)
to
f(U)\in\mathcal{O}(Y)

is well-defined and continuous.
Now (
5
)
implies

f_{*}(H^{\star}(U,V))\subseteq g^{\circ}(V)
,
for all
(U,V)\in\mathfrak{A}_{g}
,
so that the map

L_{g}\to L_{g},\;(U,V)\mapsto(f_{*}\circ H^{\star}\circ P_{g}(U,V),V)

is well-defined and continuous.
Here,
L_{g}
and
P_{g}
are defined as in the proof of Theorem
37
.
For
(U,V)\in\mathfrak{A}_{g}
it follows from the definition of
P_{g}
that

f_{*}\circ H^{\star}\circ P_{g}(U,V)\leq\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}\circ P_{g}(U,V)=\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}(U,V)=\pi_{Y}(U,V)=U.

This in turn implies that the map

\Phi\colon\mathfrak{A}_{g}\to\mathfrak{A}_{f},\;(U,V)\mapsto P_{f}(H^{\star}(U,V),U)

is well-defined and continuous.
By definition of

\left(\raisebox{-2.04439pt}{$\pi_{Y}$}\hskip-2.5pt{\big\backslash}\hskip-1.99997pt\raisebox{2.55554pt}{$\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}$}\right)

we have

\left(\raisebox{-2.04439pt}{$\pi_{Y}$}\hskip-2.5pt{\big\backslash}\hskip-1.99997pt\raisebox{2.55554pt}{$\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}$}\right)\geq\Phi
.
Hence

\mathfrak{E}_{f}^{\star}\circ\left(\raisebox{-2.04439pt}{$\pi_{Y}$}\hskip-2.5pt{\big\backslash}\hskip-1.99997pt\raisebox{2.55554pt}{$\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}$}\right)(U,V)\geq\pi_{X}\circ\Phi(U,V)\geq H^{\star}(U,V).

This shows that

\mathfrak{E}_{f}^{\star}\circ\left(\raisebox{-2.04439pt}{$\pi_{Y}$}\hskip-2.5pt{\big\backslash}\hskip-1.99997pt\raisebox{2.55554pt}{$\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}$}\right)

is greater than or equal to every co-envelope of
g\circ f

with values in
\mathfrak{A}_{g}
.
It is therefore the principal
\mathfrak{A}_{g}
-co-envelope.

Now assume that
g
is uniformly envelopable.
Then

\mathfrak{A}_{g}\simeq\mathcal{O}(Z)

and

\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}=g^{\circ}\colon\mathcal{O}(Z)\to\mathcal{O}(Y)
.
Under this identification the map
H^{\star}
becomes a map of type

H^{\star}\colon\mathcal{O}(Z)\to\mathcal{O}(X)

with

H^{\star}(V)\subseteq f^{\circ}(g^{\circ}(V))
.
We obtain a well-defined continuous map

\mathcal{O}(Z)\to\mathfrak{A}_{f},\;V\mapsto P_{f}(H^{\star}(V),g^{\circ}(V)).

By definition this map is smaller than or equal to
\left(\raisebox{-2.04439pt}{$\pi_{Y}$}\hskip-2.5pt{\big\backslash}\hskip-1.99997pt\raisebox{2.55554pt}{$g^{\circ}$}\right)
.
It follows that
H^{\star}(V)\subseteq\pi_{X}\circ\left(\raisebox{-2.04439pt}{$\pi_{Y}$}\hskip-2.5pt{\big\backslash}\hskip-1.99997pt\raisebox{2.55554pt}{$g^{\circ}$}\right)(V)
.
This proves the claim.

Compared with
Theorem
38
,
Theorem
13
has a stronger conclusion.
The composition is even asserted to be uniformly universal,
rather than just being the principal
\mathcal{O}^{2}(Z)
-envelope.
The next proposition constitutes somewhat of a converse to this.

Proposition 39
.

(1)

Let
f\colon X\to Y
.
Let
\mathfrak{E}_{f}^{\star}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{f}\to\mathcal{O}(X)
be its primary co-envelope.
If for all effective
T_{0}
spaces and all continuous functions

g\colon Y\to Z

the composition

\mathfrak{E}_{f}^{\star}\bullet g^{*}

is uniformly universal then
f
is uniformly envelopable.

(2)

Let
g\colon Y\to Z
.
Let
\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{g}\to\mathcal{O}(X)
be its primary co-envelope.
If for all effective
T_{0}
spaces and all continuous functions

f\colon X\to Y

the composition

f^{*}\bullet\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}

is uniformly universal then
g
is uniformly envelopable.

Proof 5.3
.

For the first claim, take
g=\operatorname{id}_{Y}\colon Y\to Y
.
Then
\mathfrak{E}_{f}^{\star}\bullet g^{*}=\mathfrak{E}_{f}^{\star}
,
so that
\mathfrak{A}_{f}\simeq\mathcal{O}(Y)

and
f
is uniformly envelopable.
For the second claim, take
f=\operatorname{id}_{Y}\colon Y\to Y
.
Then

f^{*}\bullet\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}=f^{*}\circ\left(\raisebox{-2.04439pt}{$\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{O}(Y)}$}\hskip-2.5pt{\big\backslash}\hskip-1.99997pt\raisebox{2.55554pt}{$\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}$}\right)=\mathfrak{E}_{g}^{\star}
.
It follows that
\mathfrak{A}_{g}\simeq\mathcal{O}(Z)

and that
g
is uniformly envelopable.

6.
Examples

To conclude we collect a few toy examples for the purpose of building some basic intuition about the main definitions and
of observing some of the basic phenomena that may occur.

Consider the functions

f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\;f(x)=\begin{cases}-x&\text{if }x\leq 0,\\
1&\text{if }x>0,\end{cases}

and

g\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},\;g(x)=\begin{cases}-x&\text{if }x<0,\\
1&\text{if }x\geq 0.\end{cases}

Then both
f\circ g
and
g\circ f
are equal to the constant function with value
1
.
Let
\mathcal{K}_{\bot}(\mathbb{R})
denote the lattice of compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}
, ordered by reverse inclusion, with a bottom element added.
The best continuous approximation of
f
with values in
\mathcal{K}_{\bot}(\mathbb{R})
is

F\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{K}_{\bot}(\mathbb{R}),\;F(x)=\begin{cases}\{-x\}&\text{if }x<0,\\
\{0,1\}&\text{if }x=0,\\
\{1\}&\text{if }x>0.\end{cases}

The best continuous approximation
G\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{K}_{\bot}(\mathbb{R})
of
g
with values in
\mathcal{K}_{\bot}(\mathbb{R})
is the same function:
G(x)=F(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

The envelope
F
is uniformly universal.
The envelope
G
is uniformly
\mathcal{R}
-universal.
It is however not uniformly universal.
The open set
(0,+\infty)
is a robust property of
g(0)
that is not witnessed by
G
.

We have
G\bullet F(x)=\{1\}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}

and
F\bullet G(x)=\{1\}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
with
x\neq 0
,
but
F\bullet G(0)=\{0,1\}
.
Hence, the envelope
G\bullet F
is again uniformly universal,
but

F\bullet G
already fails to be the best approximation of
f\circ g

in the lattice
\mathcal{K}_{\bot}(\mathbb{R})
.

The least advice bundle
\mathfrak{A}_{g}
of
g
assigns to every open set
V\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R})

a fibre
\mathfrak{A}_{g}(V)
which is an effective injective space.
The fibres can be up to isomorphism described as follows:

\mathfrak{A}_{g}(V)\simeq\begin{cases}\Sigma&\text{if }1\in V,0\notin V,\text{ and }(0,\varepsilon)\subseteq V\text{ for some }\varepsilon>0,\\
\{\bot\}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let
\mathbb{R}_{<}
denote the space of real numbers with the Scott topology for the usual ordering.
Let
\mathbb{R}_{>}
denote the space of real numbers with the Scott topology for the opposite ordering.
The space
\mathbb{R}_{<}
is also called the space of
lower reals
.
The space
\mathbb{R}_{>}
is also called the space of
upper reals
.
A reasonable computability structure on
\mathfrak{A}_{g}
is obtained by identifying it with a quotient of the space

\left\{(V,\varepsilon)\in\mathcal{O}(Y)\times\mathbb{R}_{<}\mid(0,\varepsilon)\subseteq V\right\}.

We identify pairs
(V,\varepsilon)
and
(V,\delta)
as follows:
If

1\in V

and

0\notin V
,
then

(V,\varepsilon)\sim(V,\delta)

if and only if the predicates

\varepsilon>0

and

\delta>0

have the same truth value.
For all other
V
we identify all
(V,\varepsilon)
and
(V,\delta)
.
The resulting quotient space is easily seen to be homeomorphic to
\mathfrak{A}_{g}
.

Let
\pi_{\mathfrak{A}_{g}}\colon\mathfrak{A}_{g}\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R})
denote the projection of the advice bundle
\mathfrak{A}_{g}
onto
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R})
.
We can compute the greatest continuous lift

\left(\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{$\pi_{\mathfrak{A}_{g}}$}\hskip-2.5pt{\big\backslash}\hskip-1.99997pt\raisebox{2.5pt}{$F^{\star}$}\right)\colon\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathfrak{A}_{g}

of
F^{\star}
along
\pi_{\mathfrak{A}_{g}}

explicitly:
Given
V\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R})
, output the class

[(W,\varepsilon)]\in\mathfrak{A}_{g}
, where

W=F^{\star}(V)
,

\varepsilon=1

if

1\in V
,
and

\varepsilon=0

if
1\notin V
.
Hence, the extra information that is needed to verify that

(0,+\infty)

is a robust property of

g(0)

can be provided by

\left(\raisebox{-1.99997pt}{$\pi_{\mathfrak{A}_{g}}$}\hskip-2.5pt{\big\backslash}\hskip-1.99997pt\raisebox{2.5pt}{$F^{\star}$}\right)
.
We obtain that

G^{\star}\bullet F^{\star}\colon\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R})

is equal to
(f\circ g)^{*}=(f\circ g)^{\circ}
.

It is worth noting that
\mathfrak{A}_{g}
is not isomorphic to a space of the form
\mathcal{O}(Z)
.
It is not even distributive.
If we call an advice bundle
A

spatial
if
A
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}(Z)
for some
Z
,
then we observe that there always exists a spatial advice bundle whose associated principal co-envelope is universal.
However, one can show that for the function
g
there exists no least spatial advice bundle,

i.e.
, there exists no spatial advice bundle which is the least advice bundle among the spatial ones.

As a second example we consider various types of limit operators on sequences of real numbers.
Let

\operatorname{lim}_{[0,1]}\colon\subseteq[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}\to[0,1]

be the function which sends a convergent sequence in
[0,1]
to its limit.
Let

{\lim}_{\mathbb{R}}\colon\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{R}

be the function which sends a convergent sequence in
\mathbb{R}
to its limit.
Let

\operatorname{Mlim}_{(-\infty,1]}\colon\subseteq(-\infty,1]^{\mathbb{N}}\to(-\infty,1]

be the function which sends a bounded monotonically increasing sequence in
(-\infty,1]
to its limit.
Let

\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}\colon\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathbb{R}

be the function which sends a bounded monotonically increasing sequence in
\mathbb{R}
to its limit.

All four functions are Weihrauch equivalent
[
BGP21
, Theorem 8.9]
,
so that they have the same “computational power”.
However, the amount of continuously obtainable information,
as encoded by the primary envelope,
is quite different for each function.

The functions

{\lim}_{[0,1]}

and

\operatorname{Mlim}_{(-\infty,1]}

are uniformly envelopable.
The primary envelope of

{\lim}_{[0,1]}

is the constant map

[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathcal{K}([0,1])

which sends every point to
[0,1]
.
The primary envelope of

\operatorname{Mlim}_{(-\infty,1]}

sends

(x_{n})_{n}
to

[\lim x_{n},1]\in\mathcal{K}_{\bot}((-\infty,1])
.

Neither

{\lim}_{\mathbb{R}}

nor

\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}

are uniformly envelopable.
The least advice bundle

\mathfrak{A}_{{\lim}_{\mathbb{R}}}

for
{\lim}_{\mathbb{R}}
can be described as follows:

\mathfrak{A}_{{\lim}_{\mathbb{R}}}(U)\simeq\begin{cases}\{\bot\}&\text{if }U\neq\mathbb{R}\\
\Sigma&\text{if }U=\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

This bundle is spatial,

\mathfrak{A}_{{\lim}_{\mathbb{R}}}\simeq\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R}_{\bot})
.
The primary envelope can be represented spatially as

F\colon\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathcal{O}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{\bot}),\;F((x_{n})_{n})=\nu_{\mathbb{R}_{\bot}}(\bot).

We carry out the computation of the least advice bundle of
\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}
in some detail,
following the construction in the proof of Theorem
37
.
An open set
V\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R})
is a robust property of

\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}((x_{n})_{n})

if and only if it contains the interval
[\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n},+\infty)
.
It follows that the principal

\mathcal{O}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
-envelope
of

\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}

is the map which sends every point to
\emptyset\in\mathcal{O}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
Picking up the notation of the proof of
Theorem
37
,
let

L_{\operatorname{Mlim}}=\left\{(U,V)\in\mathcal{O}(\operatorname{dom}(\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}))\times\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R})\mid U\subseteq\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}^{-1}(V)\right\}.

Let

P_{\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}}\colon L_{\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}}\to L_{\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}}

be the greatest continuous function such that

P_{\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}}(U,V)=(U^{\prime},V)
.
This function is explicitly computable.
Using that the eventually constant monotone sequences are dense in
\operatorname{dom}(\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}})
,
it is not difficult to see that given an open set

U
in
\mathcal{O}(\operatorname{dom}(\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}))

one can compute the number

I(U)=\inf\left\{\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}\mid(x_{n})_{n}\in U\right\}\in[-\infty,+\infty]_{>}

as an upper real.
It follows that we have

P_{\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}}(U,V)=\left(\left\{(x_{n})_{n}\in\operatorname{dom}(\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}})\mid\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}>I(U)\right\},V\right).

For an open set
V\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R})
,
let

r(V)=\inf\left\{s\in\mathbb{R}\mid(s,+\infty)\subseteq V\right\}
.
Then for all
V\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R})
we have by the above:

\mathfrak{A}_{\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}}(V)\simeq\left\{x\in[-\infty,+\infty]_{>}\mid x>r(V)\right\}.

We can hence identify

\mathfrak{A}_{\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}}

with a subspace of

\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{R})\times[-\infty,+\infty]_{>}
.
Under this identification, the primary envelope of
\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}
is:

F\colon\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}\to\mathcal{O}(\mathfrak{A}_{\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}}),\;F((x_{n})_{n})=\left\{(V,s)\in\mathfrak{A}_{\operatorname{Mlim}_{\mathbb{R}}}\mid\lim_{n\to\infty}x_{n}>s\right\}.

, it suffices to establish the following result:

Proposition 40
.

Let
L
be an
\omega
-continuous lattice.
Let
Z
be a Polish space.
Then the Scott topology on
L^{Z}
coincides with the compact-open topology.

Proof A.1
.

We closely follow the proof of
[
DGL95
, Theorem 4.1]
, which establishes the result in the special case where
L=\Sigma
.
The Scott topology is finer than the compact-open topology, so that it suffices to show that every Scott-open set is open in the compact-open topology.

Thus, let
\mathcal{U}\subseteq L^{Z}
be Scott-open.
Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{U}
.
Our goal is to show that
\varphi\in N\subseteq\mathcal{U}
,
where
N
is an open set in the compact-open topology.

Since
L
is
\omega
-continuous, it has a countable basis
(b_{n})_{n}
.
Choose a countable basis
(U_{n})_{n}
of the topology on
Z
.
Since
L
is a complete lattice,
L^{Z}
is again a complete lattice.
Suprema in
L^{Z}
are given pointwise.

For
(j,k)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, let

\chi_{j,k}\colon Z\to L,\;\chi_{j,k}(z)=\begin{cases}b_{k}&\text{if }z\in U_{j}\\
\bot&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Let

S=\left\{\chi_{j,k}\mid j\in\mathbb{N},\varphi(z)\gg b_{k}\;\text{ for all }z\in U_{j}\right\}.

Then

\varphi=\sup S
.
Since
\mathcal{U}
is Scott-open and contains
\varphi
,
it contains the supremum over a finite subset of
S
.

It follows that
there exist non-empty open sets

U_{0,1},\dots,U_{0,s}\in\mathcal{O}(Z)

and lattice elements

\ell_{1}\dots,\ell_{s}\in L

such that

\varphi(z)\gg\ell_{j}

for all
z\in U_{0,j}

and

\mathcal{U}
contains all
\psi\colon L\to Z
satisfying

\psi(z)\geq\ell_{j}

for all
z\in U_{0,j}
.

Fix a complete metric that generates the topology on
Z
.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{B}_{n}
denote the set of all balls of radius
2^{-n}
with respect to that metric.
For an open set
W\in\mathcal{O}(Z)
and
j\in\{1,\dots,s\}
, let

\psi_{W,j}\colon Z\to L

be the function which sends all elements of
W
to
\ell_{j}
and all elements of
Z\setminus W
to
\bot\in L
.

Each set
U_{0,j}
is the union of all open sets
W\in\mathcal{O}(Z)
satisfying

\operatorname{cl}{W}\subseteq U_{0,j}\cap B
,
where
B\in\mathcal{B}_{1}
.

Let

A_{0}=\left\{\psi_{W,j}\mid j\in\{1,\dots,s\},W\in\mathcal{O}(Z),\exists B\in\mathcal{B}_{1}.\left(\operatorname{cl}{W}\subseteq U_{0,j}\cap B\right)\right\}.

Then, by construction, the supremum of
A_{0}
is contained in
U
.
Since
U
is Scott-open, there exists a finite subset of
A_{0}
whose supremum is contained in
U
.

Thus, we obtain finitely many non-empty open sets

U_{1,1},\dots,U_{1,s}\in\mathcal{O}(Z)

such that

\operatorname{cl}{U_{1,j}}\subseteq U_{0,j}
,
the union

\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}U_{1,j}

is covered by finitely many elements of
\mathcal{B}_{1}
,
and

U

contains all
\psi\colon Z\to L
such that for all

j=1,\dots,s

and all

z\in U_{1,j}

we have

\psi(z)\geq\ell_{j}
.

By an analogous argument we obtain for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
a collection of finitely many non-empty open sets

U_{n,1},\dots,U_{n,s}\in\mathcal{O}(Z)
,
such that

\operatorname{cl}{U_{n+1,j}}\subseteq U_{n,j}
,
the union

\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}U_{n,j}

is covered by finitely many elements of
\mathcal{B}_{n}
,
and

U

contains all
\psi\colon Z\to L
such that for all

j=1,\dots,s

and all

z\in U_{n,j}

we have

\psi(z)\geq\ell_{j}
.

For
j=1,\dots,s
, consider the set

K_{j}=\bigcap_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{cl}{U_{n,j}}.

By Kőnig’s lemma and completeness of
Z
, the set
K_{j}
is non-empty.
It is closed and totally bounded by construction, and hence compact.
For
j=1,\dots,s
, let

V_{j}=\left\{x\in L\mid x\gg\ell_{j}\right\}
.

Let

N=\left\{\psi\colon Z\to L\mid\forall j\in\{1,\dots,s\}.\psi(K_{j})\subseteq V_{j}\right\}.

Then
N
is an open neighbourhood of
\varphi
in the compact-open topology by construction.
If
\psi\in N
, then for all
j=1,\dots,s
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\psi
sends
U_{n,j}
to
V_{j}
,
so that
\psi\in\mathcal{U}
.
This proves the claim.

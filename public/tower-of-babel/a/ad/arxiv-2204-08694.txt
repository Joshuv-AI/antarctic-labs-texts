Linear-Quadratic Optimal Controls for Stochastic Volterra Integral Equations: Causal State Feedback and Path-Dependent Riccati Equations
Hanxiao Wang Jiongmin Yong Chao Zhou

Abstract




Abstract.
A linear-quadratic optimal control problem for a forward stochastic Volterra integral equation (FSVIE, for short) is considered. Under the usual convexity conditions, open-loop optimal control exists, which can be characterized by the optimality system, a coupled system of an FSVIE and a Type-II backward SVIE (BSVIE, for short). To obtain a causal state feedback representation for the open-loop optimal control, a path-dependent Riccati equation for an operator-valued function is introduced, via which the optimality system can be decoupled. In the process of decoupling, a Type-III BSVIE is introduced whose adapted solution can be used to represent the adapted M-solution of the corresponding Type-II BSVIE. Under certain conditions, it is proved that the path-dependent Riccati equation admits a unique solution, which means that the decoupling field for the optimality system is found. Therefore a causal state feedback representation of the open-loop optimal control is constructed. An additional interesting finding is that when the control only appears in the diffusion term, not in the drift term of the state system, the causal state feedback reduces to a Markovian state feedback.

Keywords.
Linear-quadratic optimal control,
stochastic Volterra integral equation, optimality system,
decoupling field, path-dependent Riccati equation, causal state feedback representation.

Next, we introduce

\displaystyle\Lambda=\big\{(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))~|~t\in[0,T),\,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{t}\big\}.

For any given

\displaystyle X(s)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\mathscr{X}[t,T]=\Big\{X:[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\bigm|X(\cdot)\hbox{~is $\mathbb{F}^{t}$-progressively measurable on $[t,T]$, continuous},\\

V(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\triangleq\inf_{u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[t,T]}J(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot);u(\cdot)),\qquad\forall(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\in\Lambda,

is called the

By a variational method and duality principle, we can obtain the following
optimality system
associated with Problem (LQ-FSVIE) (see
Theorem 3.3
): Let
(\bar{X}(\cdot),\bar{u}(\cdot))
be an open-loop optimal pair. Then

R(s)\bar{u}(s)+Y^{0}(s)=0,\quad s\in[t,T],

(1.7)

with

\left\{\begin{aligned} X(s)&=\varphi(t,s)+\int_{t}^{s}\big[A(s,r)X(r)+B(s,r)\bar{u}(r)\big]dr\\
&\quad+\int_{t}^{s}\big[C(s,r)X(r)+D(s,r)\bar{u}(r)\big]dW(r),\\
Y(s)&=Q(s)X(s)+A(T,s)^{\top}GX(T)+C(T,s)^{\top}\zeta(s)\\
&\quad+\int_{s}^{T}\big[A(r,s)^{\top}Y(r)+C(r,s)^{\top}Z(r,s)\big]dr-\int_{s}^{T}Z(s,r)dW(r),\\
Y^{0}(s)&=B(T,s)^{\top}GX(T)+D(T,s)^{\top}\zeta(s)\\
&\quad+\int_{s}^{T}\big[B(r,s)^{\top}Y(r)+D(r,s)^{\top}Z(r,s)\big]dr-\int_{s}^{T}Z^{0}(s,r)dW(r),\\
\eta(s)&=GX(T)-\int_{s}^{T}\zeta(r)dW(r).\end{aligned}\right.

(1.8)

Basically, the above is the MP for Problem (LQ-FSVIE). Note that in the above (
1.8
), the second equation is a BSDE with unknown
(\eta(\cdot),\zeta(\cdot))
; the third is called a Type-II BSVIE with unknown
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))
, whose main feature is that both
Z(s,r)
and
Z(r,s)
appear; the last is a Type-I BSVIE with unknown
(Y^{0}(\cdot),Z^{0}(\cdot\,,\cdot))
, whose free term and drift are known processes from the first three equations. It is seen that the system (
1.8
) is a fully coupled FBSVIE, with the coupling being through (
1.7
).

It is not hard to see that the representation of the optimal control obtained from (
1.7
) (assuming
R(s)
to be invertible for all
s\in[0,T]
) is not practically feasible. The reason is that in determining
Y^{0}(s)
, future information
\bar{X}(T)
of the optimal state process
\bar{X}(\cdot)
is involved. In the classical LQ theory (either for ODEs or SDEs), under proper conditions, the optimality system (which is a two-point boundary value problem for ODEs, or an FBSDE for SDEs) can be decoupled by the solution to a proper Riccati equation. The state feedback representation of the open-loop optimal control can be obtained as a by-product. See
[
36
]
for the standard LQ problems of ODEs and SDEs. The main tool used in the decoupling procedure for SDEs is the (classical) Itô’s formula (and the chain rule for ODEs). However, for FBSVIEs, the classical Itô’s formula is not applicable. By looking at the problem deeper, one realizes that
the decoupling technique is essentially relying on the flow property of the equations, or some kind of semigroup property of the dynamic system. Unfortunately, the controlled FSVIE in the optimality system does not satisfy the flow property in the standard sense. In fact, for
t\leqslant r\leqslant s\leqslant T
,

X(s)\neq X(r)+\int_{r}^{s}\big[A(s,\tau)X(\tau)+B(s,\tau)\bar{u}(\tau)\big]d\tau+\int_{r}^{s}\big[C(s,\tau)X(\tau)+D(s,\tau)\bar{u}(\tau)\big]dW(\tau),

(1.9)

due to which, the decoupling method for SDEs (see Ma–Protter-Yong
[
19
]
, called the four-step-scheme) cannot be applied here, and the corresponding Riccati type equation is completely unclear from this path. Indeed, this problem has been open for more than ten years (see
[
7
,
35
]
for some early suggestions on this topic).

Recently, Viens–Zhang
[
27
]
and Wang–Yong–Zhang
[
29
]
have developed a theory established some relations between BSVIEs and path-dependent PDEs, which are natural and significant extensions of the four-step-scheme (for FBSDEs decoupling
[
19
,
21
]
) to the FBSVIEs. These results provide some hopes for our decoupling the optimality system of Problem (LQ-FSVIE), so that getting a practically feasible representation for optimal control becomes possible.

We now briefly describe the main clue of this paper. Let
(\bar{X}(\cdot),\bar{u}(\cdot))
be an open-loop optimal pair. First of all, motivated by
[
27
]
(see also
[
29
]
), we introduce the following auxiliary process
\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,\cdot)
with two time variables:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\bar{\cal X}(s,r)={\bf x}_{t}(s)+\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{t}^{r}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\big[A(s,\tau)\bar{X}(\tau)+B(s,\tau)\bar{u}(\tau)\big]d\tau+\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{t}^{r}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\big[C(s,\tau)\bar{X}(\tau)+D(s,\tau)\bar{u}(\tau)\big]dW(\tau),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad t\leqslant r\leqslant s\leqslant T.\end{array}

(1.10)

Then the flow property holds for the state process
\bar{X}(\cdot)
in the following sense:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\bar{X}(s)=\bar{\cal X}(s,r)+\int_{r}^{s}\big[A(s,\tau)\bar{X}(\tau)+B(s,\tau)\bar{u}(\tau)\big]d\tau+\int_{r}^{s}\big[C(s,\tau)\bar{X}(\tau)+D(s,\tau)\bar{u}(\tau)\big]dW(\tau)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad t\leqslant r\leqslant s\leqslant T.\end{array}

(1.11)

It is worthy of pointing out that if
r
is the current time, then for
s\in[r,T]
,
s\mapsto\bar{\cal X}(s,r)
only depends on the history
\{(\bar{X}(\tau),\bar{u}(\tau))\bigm|\tau\in[t,r]\}
of the optimal pair
(\bar{X}(\cdot),\bar{u}(\cdot))
and no future information of the state and control is involved. Therefore, we say that
s\mapsto\bar{\cal X}(s,r)
is
causal
. Next, we are trying to express the optimal control in the following manner:

\bar{u}(r)=\Theta(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r),\qquad r\in[t,T],

(1.12)

where
\Theta(r):\mathscr{X}_{t}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a linear bounded operator which can be determined by the coefficients of the state equation and the weighting matrix functions in the cost functional. As
s\mapsto\bar{\cal X}(s,r)
is causal, the above representation implies that the value
\bar{u}(r)
of the optimal control
\bar{u}(\cdot)
at current time
r
does not involve future information of the corresponding state process
\bar{X}(\cdot)
. Thus, we call the above a
casual state feedback representation
of optimal control
\bar{u}(\cdot)
(see
Theorem 5.1
). Such a path is basically the analog of Steps (ii) and (iii) for solving classical LQ problems of ODEs and SDEs. The idea is pretty natural. But to achieve the goal, namely to determine the operator
\Theta(\cdot)
, is by no means trivial.

\bullet
Derived the (path-dependent) Riccati equation (
4.9
) for the bilinear operator valued function

\bullet
Introduced a Type-III BSVIE whose adapted solution can be used to represent the adapted M-solution of the Type-II BSVIEs in the adjoint equation. (see

), which will play a crucial role in decoupling the optimality system via the solution of the Riccati equation (

). By a Type-III BSVIE, we mean a BSVIE that contains the diagonal value

the first time, and has been widely used in

). From this, one sees that the decoupling method for the optimality system of Problem (LQ-FSVIE) is significantly different from that for Problem (LQ-SDE).

\bullet
Proved the existence and uniqueness of the

to the path-dependent Riccati equation (

) under the following standard condition:

where

is a given constant (see

). It follows that the path-dependent HJB equation admits a unique classical solution

2.1
Basic results for FSVIEs and BSVIEs

Throughout this paper, let
\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
be the Euclidean space consisting of
n\times m
real matrices,
and
\mathbb{S}^{n}
be the subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
consisting of symmetric matrices.
Let
T>0
be the time horizon. Denote

\Delta_{*}[t,T]=\big\{(s,r)\bigm|t\leqslant r\leqslant s\leqslant T\big\},\qquad\Delta^{*}[t,T]=\big\{(s,r)\bigm|t\leqslant s\leqslant r\leqslant T\big\}.

They are the lower and the upper triangle domains in
[t,T]^{2}
, respectively. For any Euclidean space
\mathbb{H}
, we introduce the following spaces: For any
t\in[0,T)
, (with
{\cal B}([t,T])
being the Borel
\sigma
-field of
[t,T]
)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle L^{\infty}(t,T;\mathbb{H})=\big\{\varphi:[t,T]\to\mathbb{H}~|~\varphi~\hbox{is essentially bounded}\big\},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle L_{{\cal F}_{T}^{t}}^{2}(t,T;\mathbb{H})=\Big\{\varphi:[t,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\bigm|\varphi(\cdot)~\hbox{is ${\cal B}([t,T])\otimes{\cal F}^{t}_{T}$-measruable},~\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|\varphi(\tau)|^{2}d\tau<\infty\Big\},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle L_{\mathbb{F}^{t}}^{2}(t,T;\mathbb{H})=\Big\{\varphi(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{{\cal F}_{T}^{t}}(t,T;\mathbb{H})\bigm|\varphi(\cdot)~\hbox{is $\mathbb{F}^{t}$-progressively measurable on $[t,T]$}\Big\},\\
\displaystyle L_{\mathbb{F}^{t}}^{2}(\Omega;C([t,T];\mathbb{H}))=\Big\{\varphi(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}^{t}}^{2}(t,T;\mathbb{H})\bigm|\varphi(\cdot)~\hbox{has continuous paths,}~\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|\varphi(s)|^{2}\big]<\infty\Big\},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle L_{\mathbb{F}^{t}}^{2}([t,T]^{2};\mathbb{H})=\Big\{\varphi:[t,T]^{2}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{H}\bigm|\varphi(s,\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}^{t}}^{2}(t,T;\mathbb{H}),\,\hbox{a.e.}~s\in[t,T],~\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{t}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|\varphi(s,\tau)|^{2}d\tau ds<\infty\Big\},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle L_{\mathbb{F}^{t}}^{2}(\Delta^{*}[t,T];\mathbb{H})=\Big\{\varphi:\Delta^{*}[t,T]\negthinspace\times\negthinspace\Omega\negthinspace\to\negthinspace\mathbb{H}\bigm|\varphi(s,\cdot)\negthinspace\in\negthinspace L_{\mathbb{F}^{s}}^{2}(s,T;\mathbb{H}),\,\hbox{a.e.}~s\negthinspace\in\negthinspace[t,T],~\mathop{\rm esssup}_{s\in[t,T]}\mathbb{E}\negthinspace\negthinspace\int_{s}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace|\varphi(s,\tau)|^{2}d\tau\negthinspace<\negthinspace\infty\Big\}.\end{array}

For the state equation (
1.2
) and the cost functional (
1.3
), we impose the following assumptions.

(H1)
.

The coefficients of the state equation
A,C:\Delta_{*}[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
B,D:\Delta_{*}[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times m}
are (deterministic) bounded and differentiable, with bounded derivatives.

(H2)
.

The weighting coefficients in the cost functional (
1.3
) satisfy

Q\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{S}^{n}),\quad R\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{S}^{m}),\quad G\in\mathbb{S}^{n}.

Applying the results of Ruan
[
22
]
, we have the following result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(H1)
hold. Then for any
(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\in\Lambda
and
u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[t,T]
, state equation (
1.2
) admits a unique solution
X(\cdot)\equiv X(\cdot\,;t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot),u(\cdot))\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}^{t}}(\Omega;C([t,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. Moreover, there exists a constant
K>0
, independent of
(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\in\Lambda
and
u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[t,T]
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|X(s)|^{2}\Big]\leqslant K\Big[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|{\bf x}_{t}(s)|^{2}+\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|u(s)|^{2}ds\Big].

(2.1)

From
2.1
, we see that Problem (LQ-FSVIE) is well-formulated under the assumptions
(H1)
and
(H2)
. We remark that
2.1
still holds true under some weaker assumptions than
(H1)
.
Since we mainly focus on the form of the associated Riccati equation (or the decoupling field of the optimality system) in this paper, we prefer not to pursue the weakest possible conditions to simplify our presentation.

We now recall some fundamental results of the following Type-II linear BSVIEs:

Y(s)=\psi(s)+\int_{s}^{T}\big[{\cal A}(s,\tau)Y(\tau)+{\cal C}(s,\tau)Z(\tau,s)\big]d\tau-\int_{s}^{T}Z(s,\tau)dW(\tau),\quad s\in[t,T],

(2.2)

which can be found in Yong
[
35
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

A pair of processes
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}^{t}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}^{t}}([t,T]^{2};\mathbb{R}^{n})

is called an
adapted M-solution
to BSVIE (
2.2
)
if (
2.2
) is satisfied in the usual Itô sense for the Lebesgue measure almost every
t\leqslant\tau\leqslant s\leqslant T
and, in addition, the following holds:

Y(s)=\mathbb{E}_{r}[Y(s)]+\int_{r}^{s}Z(s,\tau)dW(\tau),\qquad\hbox{a.e.}~t\leqslant r\leqslant s\leqslant T.

(2.3)

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
{\cal A}(\cdot\,,\cdot),{\cal C}(\cdot\,,\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(\Delta^{*}[t,T];\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
. Then for any
\psi(\cdot)\in L^{2}_{{\cal F}^{t}_{T}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})
, BSVIE (
2.2
) admits a unique adapted M-solution
(Y(\cdot),Z(\cdot\,,\cdot))\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}^{t}}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})\times L^{2}_{\mathbb{F}^{t}}([t,T]^{2};\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Let
{\cal L}(\mathscr{X}_{t};\mathbb{R}^{m})
be the set of all bounded
\mathbb{R}^{m}
-valued linear functionals on
\mathscr{X}_{t}
, with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal L}}
defined by

\|L\|_{{\cal L}}\triangleq\sup_{\|{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\|\leqslant 1}|L{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)|,\qquad\forall L\in{\cal L}(\mathscr{X}_{t};\mathbb{R}^{m}).

Let
L^{\infty}(t,T;{\cal L}(\mathscr{X}_{t};\mathbb{R}^{m}))
be the set of all
{\cal L}(\mathscr{X}_{t};\mathbb{R}^{m})
-valued functions defined on
[t,T]
.
In other words, for any
F(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(t,T;{\cal L}(\mathscr{X}_{t};\mathbb{R}^{m}))
,

F(t)\in{\cal L}(\mathscr{X}_{t};\mathbb{R}^{m}),\quad\hbox{a.e.}~t\in[0,T],\quad\hbox{and}\quad\mathop{\rm esssup}_{t\in[0,T]}\|F(t)\|_{\cal L}<\infty.

(2.4)

Definition 2.4
.

Any
\bar{\Theta}(\cdot)\in L^{\infty}(t,T;{\cal L}(\mathscr{X}_{t};\mathbb{R}^{m}))
is called an
optimal causal feedback operator
of Problem (LQ-FSVIE) on
[t,T]
if

J(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot);\bar{\Theta}(\cdot)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,\cdot))\leqslant J(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot);u(\cdot)),\qquad\forall u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[t,T],

(2.5)

where
(s,r)\mapsto\bar{\cal X}(s,r)\equiv\bar{\cal X}_{r}(s)
is the unique solution to the closed-loop auxiliary system

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\bar{\cal X}(s,r)={\bf x}_{t}(s)+\int_{t}^{r}\big[A(s,\tau)\bar{X}(\tau)+B(s,\tau)\bar{\Theta}(\tau)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,\tau)\big]d\tau\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{t}^{r}\big[C(s,\tau)\bar{X}(\tau)+D(s,\tau)\bar{\Theta}(\tau)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,\tau)\big]dW(\tau),\qquad(s,r)\in\Delta_{*}[t,T],\end{array}

(2.6)

and
\bar{X}(\cdot)
is the unique solution to the closed-loop system

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\bar{X}(s)={\bf x}_{t}(s)+\int_{t}^{s}\big[A(s,\tau)\bar{X}(\tau)+B(s,\tau)\bar{\Theta}(\tau)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,\tau)\big]d\tau\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\int_{t}^{s}\big[C(s,\tau)\bar{X}(\tau)+D(s,\tau)\bar{\Theta}(\tau)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,\tau)\big]dW(\tau),\quad s\in[t,T].\end{array}

(2.7)

Remark 2.5
.

It is clear that the outcome
\tau\mapsto\bar{u}(\tau)=\bar{\Theta}(\tau)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,\tau)
of the optimal causal state feedback operator
\bar{\Theta}(\cdot)
is an open-loop optimal control. Note that the auxiliary process
\bar{\cal X}_{r}(\cdot)=\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)
is uniquely determined by the portion
\bar{X}(\cdot)|_{[t,r]}
of the state process
\bar{X}(\cdot)
. Thus,
\bar{u}(\cdot)
has a causal state feedback representation. Namely, the value
\bar{u}(r)
of the optimal control
\bar{u}(\cdot)
at any time
r
does not involve future information of the corresponding state process
\bar{X}(\cdot)
.

2.2
Bilinear operators

For any Banach space
\mathbb{X}
, let
{\cal L}^{2}(\mathbb{X})
be the set of all bounded bilinear functionals on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{X}
,
with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal L}}
defined by

\|P\|_{{\cal L}^{2}}\triangleq\sup_{\|{\bf x}\|,\|{\bf y}\|\leqslant 1}|P({\bf x},{\bf y})|,\qquad\forall P\in{\cal L}^{2}(\mathbb{X}).

Suggestively, we usually denote

P({\bf x},{\bf y})=\langle P{\bf x},{\bf y}\rangle={\bf y}^{\top}P{\bf x},\qquad{\bf x},{\bf y}\in\mathbb{X},

where,
P{\bf x}\in\mathbb{X}^{*}
, the dual of
\mathbb{X}
, and
\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle
is the duality pairing between
\mathbb{X}^{*}
and
\mathbb{X}
.
A bilinear functional
P\in{\cal L}^{2}(\mathbb{X})
is said to be
symmetric
if it satisfies

{\bf y}^{\top}P{\bf x}=P({\bf x},{\bf y})=P({\bf y},{\bf x})={\bf x}^{\top}P{\bf y},\qquad\forall{\bf x},{\bf y}\in\mathbb{X}.

Let
{\cal S}(\mathbb{X})
be the set of all symmetric bilinear functionals on
\mathbb{X}
, and
{\cal S}_{+}(\mathbb{X})
be the subset of
{\cal S}(\mathbb{X})
consisting of all non-negative bilinear functionals, that is
P\in{\cal S}_{+}(\mathbb{X})
, if and only if
P\in{\cal S}(\mathbb{X})
and

P({\bf x},{\bf x})\geqslant 0,\qquad\forall{\bf x}\in\mathbb{X}.

For any
P\in{\cal S}(\mathbb{X})
, define

\|P\|_{\cal S}\triangleq\sup_{\|{\bf x}\|\leqslant 1}|P({\bf x},{\bf x})|.

The following result shows that
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal S}}
is an equivalent norm of
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal L}^{2}}
on
{\cal S}(\mathbb{X})
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
P\in{\cal S}(\mathbb{X})
, it holds

\|P\|_{\cal S}\leqslant\|P\|_{{\cal L}^{2}}\leqslant 2\|P\|_{\cal S}.

(2.8)

Proof.

On one hand, by the definition, it is clear to see that
\|P\|_{\cal S}\leqslant\|P\|_{{\cal L}^{2}}
.
On the other hand, since the bilinear operator
P
satisfies
P({\bf x},{\bf y})=P({\bf y},{\bf x})
, we have

P({\bf x}+{\bf y},{\bf x}+{\bf y})-P({\bf x}-{\bf y},{\bf x}-{\bf y})=2P({\bf x},{\bf y})+2P({\bf y},{\bf x})=4P({\bf x},{\bf y}),\quad\forall{\bf x},{\bf y}\in\mathbb{X}.

Thus

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle|P({\bf x},{\bf y})|={|P({\bf x}+{\bf y},{\bf x}+{\bf y})-P({\bf x}-{\bf y},{\bf x}-{\bf y})|\over 4}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\leqslant\|P\|_{\cal S}{\|{\bf x}+{\bf y}\|^{2}+\|{\bf x}-{\bf y}\|^{2}\over 4}\leqslant\|P\|_{\cal S}[\|{\bf x}\|^{2}+\|{\bf y}\|^{2}],\qquad\forall{\bf x},{\bf y}\in\mathbb{X},\end{array}

which implies

\|P\|_{{\cal L}^{2}}=\sup_{\|{\bf x}\|,\|{\bf y}\|\leqslant 1}|P({\bf x},{\bf y})|\leqslant\|P\|_{\cal S}\sup_{\|{\bf x}\|,\|{\bf y}\|\leqslant 1}[\|{\bf x}|^{2}+|{\bf y}\|^{2}]=2\|P\|_{\cal S},

proving our conclusion. ∎

Remark 2.7
.

If
\mathbb{X}
is a Hilbert space, by the standard results in functional analysis we have
\|\cdot\|_{\cal S}=\|\cdot\|_{{\cal L}^{2}}
on
{\cal S}(\mathbb{X})
.
When
\mathbb{X}
is merely a Banach space, though
\|\cdot\|_{\cal S}=\|\cdot\|_{{\cal L}^{2}}
might not hold in general,
we still have the equivalence between
\|\cdot\|_{\cal S}
and
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal L}^{2}}
on
{\cal S}(\mathbb{X})
.

We often simply write
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal S}}
(or
\|\cdot\|_{{\cal L}^{2}}
) as
\|\cdot\|
when there is no confusion. If the values
P({\bf x},{\bf x})
are determined for all
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{X}
, then we can extend
L
to the whole space
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{X}
by

P({\bf x},{\bf y})={P({\bf x}+{\bf y},{\bf x}+{\bf y})-P({\bf x}-{\bf y},{\bf x}-{\bf y})\over 4},\qquad\forall{\bf x},{\bf y}\in\mathbb{X}.

Clearly, the extension
P\in{\cal S}(\mathbb{X})
; that is
P
is a symmetric bilinear operator on
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{X}
. By
2.6
, we know that such an extension is unique in the space
{\cal S}(\mathbb{X})
.
This extension will play a crucial role in establishing the well-posedness of the Riccati equation
associated with Problem (LQ-FSVIE).

2.3
Functional Itô’s formula

Recall
\Lambda
from (
1.1
). As in Viens–Zhang
[
27
]
, we introduce the following metric:

{\bf d}\big((t,{\bf x}_{t}),(t^{\prime},{\bf x}_{t^{\prime}}^{\prime})\big)\triangleq|t-t^{\prime}|+\sup_{s\in[0,T]}|{\bf x}_{t}(s\vee t)-{\bf x}^{\prime}_{t^{\prime}}(s\vee t^{\prime})|,\qquad\forall(t,{\bf x}_{t}),(t^{\prime},{\bf x}^{\prime}_{t^{\prime}})\in\Lambda.

It can be shown that
\Lambda
is a complete metric space under
{\bf d}
.
For any
t\in[0,T)
, let
\mathscr{X}_{t}^{+}
be the subspace of
\mathscr{X}_{t}
consists of all
{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{t}
such that
{\bf x}_{t}(s)
is right-differentiable at each
s\in[t,T]
. Further, we denote
\mathscr{X}^{0}_{t}
to be the subspace of
\mathscr{X}_{t}
consisting of all constant functions. Correspondingly, we denote
\Lambda^{+}
and
\Lambda^{0}
as follows:

\Lambda^{+}=\{(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\in\mathscr{X}_{t}\bigm|{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{t}^{+}\},\qquad\Lambda^{0}=\{(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\in\mathscr{X}_{t}\bigm|{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{t}^{0}\}.

Therefore,

\mathscr{X}_{t}^{0}\varsubsetneq\mathscr{X}_{t}^{+}\varsubsetneq\mathscr{X}_{t},\qquad\Lambda^{0}\varsubsetneq\Lambda^{+}\varsubsetneq\Lambda.

Let
C^{0}(\Lambda)
denote the set of all functions
v:\Lambda\to\mathbb{R}
which are continuous under
{\bf d}
. For any
v\in C^{0}(\Lambda)
and given
(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\in\Lambda
,
v(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))
takes real values. We denote
v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))
to be the Fréchet derivative of
v(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))
with respect to
{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)
. Namely
v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)):\mathscr{X}_{t}\to\mathbb{R}
is the linear functional satisfying the following:

v(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)+\eta_{t}(\cdot))-v(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))=v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))(\eta_{t}(\cdot))+o(\|\eta_{t}(\cdot)\|),\qquad\forall\eta_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{t}.

(2.9)

It is clear that the above Fréchet derivative can be calculated in the following way, which defines the Gâteaux derivative:

v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))(\eta_{t}(\cdot))=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{v(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)+\varepsilon\eta_{t}(\cdot))-v(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\over\varepsilon},\qquad\forall\eta_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{t}.

(2.10)

Similarly, we define the second order derivative
v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))
as a bilinear functional on
\mathscr{X}_{t}\times\mathscr{X}_{t}
:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)+\eta_{t}(\cdot))(\eta^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot))-v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))(\eta^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot))\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle=v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))(\eta_{t}(\cdot),\eta^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot))+o(\|\eta_{t}\|+\|\eta^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot)\|),\qquad\forall\eta_{t}(\cdot),\eta^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{t}.\end{array}

(2.11)

To define the right
t
-partial derivative
v_{t}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))
of
v(\cdot\,,\cdot)
at
(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))
, we need to “fix”
{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)
and define
v(t+\delta,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))
. Naturally, we define

v(t+\delta,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))=v(t+\delta,[{\bf x}_{t}]_{t+\delta}(\cdot)),\qquad{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{t},

(2.12)

where

[{\bf x}_{t}]_{t+\delta}(s)={\bf x}_{t}(s){\bf 1}_{[t+\delta,T]}(s),\qquad s\in[t+\delta,T],\quad\forall{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{t}.

(2.13)

According to the above, we see that

[{\bf x}_{t}]_{t+\delta}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{t+\delta},\qquad\forall{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{t},

which can be regarded as the natural “projection” from
\mathscr{X}_{t}
to
\mathscr{X}_{t+\delta}
.
Thus, (
2.12
) makes sense. Having such a natural restriction, we can define the right
t
-partial derivative
v_{t}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))
as follows:

v_{t}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}{v(t+\delta,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))-v(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\over\delta},

(2.14)

provided the limit exists.

To get some feeling about
v_{t}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))
, let us present a simple example.

Example 2.8
.

Let

v(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))=\int_{t}^{T}{\bf x}_{t}(s)^{\top}F(t,s){\bf x}_{t}(s)ds,\qquad(t,{\bf x}(\cdot))\in\Lambda,

for some nice
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
-valued function
F(\cdot\,,\cdot)
, not necessarily symmetric. Then, by (
2.12
), one has

v(t+\delta,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))=v(t+\delta,[{\bf x}_{t}]_{t+\delta}(\cdot))=\int_{t+\delta}^{T}{\bf x}_{t}(s)^{\top}F(t+\delta,s){\bf x}_{t}(s)ds

Hence, according to (
2.14
), we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle v_{t}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}{1\over\delta}\Big[\int_{t+\varepsilon}^{\top}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace{\bf x}_{t}(s)^{\top}F(t+\varepsilon,s){\bf x}_{t}(s)ds-\int_{t}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace{\bf x}_{t}(s)^{\top}F(t,s){\bf x}_{t}(s)ds\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad=\int_{t}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace{\bf x}_{t}(s)^{\top}F_{t}(t,s){\bf x}_{t}(s)ds-{\bf x}_{t}(t)^{\top}F(t,t){\bf x}_{t}(t),\qquad\forall{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{t}.\end{array}

However, if our
v(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))
is given by

v(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))={\bf x}_{t}(t)^{\top}F_{0}(t){\bf x}_{t}(t)+\int_{t}^{T}{\bf x}_{t}(s)^{\top}F(t,s){\bf x}_{t}(s)ds,\qquad(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\in\Lambda,

(2.15)

for some nice functions
F_{0}(\cdot)
and
F(\cdot\,,\cdot)
, then

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle v(t+\delta,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))=v(t+\delta,[{\bf x}_{t}]_{t+\delta}(\cdot))\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad={\bf x}_{t}(t+\delta)F_{0}(t+\delta){\bf x}_{t}(t+\delta)+\int_{t+\delta}^{T}{\bf x}_{t}(s)F(t+\delta,s){\bf x}_{t}(s)ds.\end{array}

Thus, for any
(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\in\Lambda^{+}
, one has

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle v_{t}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}{1\over\delta}\Big[{\bf x}_{t}(t+\delta)^{\top}F_{0}(t+\delta){\bf x}_{t}(t+\delta)-{\bf x}(t)^{\top}F_{0}(t){\bf x}(t)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{t+\varepsilon}^{\top}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace{\bf x}_{t}(s)^{\top}F(t+\varepsilon,s){\bf x}_{t}(s)ds-\int_{t}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace{\bf x}_{t}(s)^{\top}F(t,s){\bf x}_{t}(s)ds\Big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad~={\bf x}_{t}(t)^{\top}\dot{F}_{0}(t){\bf x}_{t}(t)+2\dot{\bf x}_{t}(t)^{\top}F_{0}(t){\bf x}_{t}(t)+\int_{t}^{T}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace{\bf x}_{t}(s)^{\top}F_{t}(t,s){\bf x}_{t}(s)ds-{\bf x}_{t}(t)^{\top}F(t,t){\bf x}_{t}(t),\end{array}

where
\dot{{\bf x}}_{t}(t)
stands for the right-derivative of
{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)
at
t
. In particular,

v_{t}(t,{\bf x})={\bf x}^{\top}\Big[\dot{F}_{0}(t)+\int_{t}^{T}F_{t}(t,s)ds-F(t,t)\Big]{\bf x},\qquad\forall(t,{\bf x})\in\Lambda^{0}.

From the above, we see that the function
v(\cdot\,,\cdot)
given by (
2.15
) does not have right
t
-partial derivative at
(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\in\Lambda\setminus\Lambda^{+}
.

Let us introduce the following.

Definition 2.9
.

(i) Let
C^{1,2}(\Lambda)
be the set of all
v\in C^{0}(\Lambda)
such that
v_{t},v_{\bf x},v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}
exist on
\Lambda
.

(ii) Let
C^{1,2}_{+}(\Lambda)
denote the set of all
v\in C^{1,2}(\Lambda)
such that the following are satisfied:

(a)

There exist constants
\kappa,K>0
such that, for any
(t,{\bf x})\in\Lambda
,

|v_{t}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))|+\sup_{{\eta_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{t}}\atop\|\eta_{t}(\cdot)\|\leqslant 1}|v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))(\eta_{t}(\cdot))|+\sup_{{\eta_{t}(\cdot),\eta^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{t}}\atop{\|\eta_{t}(\cdot)\|,\|\eta^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot)\|\leqslant 1}}|v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))(\eta_{t}(\cdot),\eta^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot))|\leqslant K[1+\|{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\|^{\kappa}].

(b)

For any
\eta_{t}(\cdot),\eta^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{0}
,
v_{t}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)),v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))(\eta_{t}(\cdot)),v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))(\eta_{t}(\cdot),\eta^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot))
are continuous in
(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))
, where the continuity in
t
always means right-continuity.

(c)

There exist
\kappa>0
and a modulus of continuity
\rho
such that:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\big|[v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))-v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x}^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot))](\eta_{t}(\cdot),\eta_{t}(\cdot))\big|\leqslant\big[1+\|{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\|^{\kappa}+\|{\bf x}^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot)\|^{\kappa}\big]\|\eta_{t}(\cdot)\|^{2}\rho(\|{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)-{\bf x}^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot)\|),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\forall(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\in\Lambda,~\eta_{t}(\cdot),\eta^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{t}.\end{array}

Note that for

3
Solvability of Problem (LQ-FSVIE) and Optimality System

In this section, we shall study the functional (
1.3
) as a quadratic functional of the controls
u(\cdot)
on the Hilbert space
\mathscr{U}[t,T]
. A necessary condition and a sufficient condition for the existence of an open-loop optimal control will be derived by a standard variational method. This will be expressed by an optimality system which is an FBSVIE.

For any
u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[t,T]
, consider the following FSVIE:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle X^{0,u}(s)=\int_{t}^{s}\big[A(s,r)X^{0,u}(r)+B(s,r)u(r)\big]dr+\int_{t}^{s}\big[C(s,r)X^{0,u}(r)+D(s,r)u(r)\big]dW(r),\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad s\in[t,T].\end{array}

(3.1)

By
2.1
, the above FSVIE admits a unique solution
X^{0,u}(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{F}^{t}}^{2}(\Omega;C([t,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))

satisfying

\mathbb{E}\Big[\sup_{s\in[t,T]}|X^{0,u}(s)|^{2}\Big]\leqslant K\mathbb{E}\int_{t}^{T}|u(s)|^{2}ds,

(3.2)

where the constant
K>0
is independent of
u(\cdot)
.
Thus we can define two bounded linear operators
\Gamma:\mathscr{U}[t,T]\to L_{\mathbb{F}^{t}}^{2}(\Omega;C([t,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))\subseteq L_{\mathbb{F}^{t}}^{2}(t,T;\mathbb{R}^{n})

and
\widehat{\Gamma}:\mathscr{U}[t,T]\to L_{{\cal F}^{t}_{T}}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
as follows:

[\Gamma u(\cdot)](\cdot)=X^{0,u}(\cdot),\quad[\widehat{\Gamma}u(\cdot)]=X^{0,u}(T),\qquad\forall u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[t,T].

(3.3)

For any
{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\in C([t,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})
, let
X^{{\bf x}_{t},0}(\cdot)
be the unique solution to the following linear un-controlled FSVIE:

X^{{\bf x}_{t},0}(s)={\bf x}_{t}(s)+\int_{t}^{s}A(s,r)X^{{\bf x}_{t},0}(r)dr+\int_{t}^{s}C(s,r)X^{{\bf x}_{t},0}(r)dW(r),\quad s\in[t,T].

(3.4)

Then the following linear operators
\varXi:C([t,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L_{\mathbb{F}^{t}}^{2}(\Omega;C([t,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and
\widehat{\varXi}:C([t,T];\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L_{{\cal F}^{t}_{T}}^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{n})
can also be well-defined:

[\varXi{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)](\cdot)=X^{{\bf x}_{t},0}(\cdot),\quad[\widehat{\varXi}{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)]=X^{{\bf x}_{t},0}(T),\qquad\forall{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\in C([t,T];\mathbb{R}^{n}).

(3.5)

With (
3.3
) and (
3.5
), the unique solution
X(\cdot)\equiv X(\cdot\,;t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot),u(\cdot))
of state equation (
1.2
) corresponding to

and
u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[t,T]
can be represented by

X(\cdot)=[\Gamma u(\cdot)](\cdot)+[\varXi{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)](\cdot),\quad X(T)=[\widehat{\Gamma}u(\cdot)]+[\widehat{\varXi}{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)].

(3.6)

By substituting the above into (

), we obtain the following representation of the functional (

J(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot);u(\cdot))=\langle{\cal M}_{2}u,u\rangle+2\langle{\cal M}_{1}{\bf x}_{t},u\rangle+\langle{\cal M}_{0}{\bf x}_{t},{\bf x}_{t}\rangle,

(3.7)

where

{\cal M}_{2}\triangleq{\Gamma^{*}Q\Gamma+\widehat{\Gamma}^{*}G\widehat{\Gamma}+R\over 2}\qquad{\cal M}_{1}\triangleq{\Gamma^{*}Q\varXi+\widehat{\Gamma}^{*}G\widehat{\varXi}\over 2},\qquad{\cal M}_{0}={\varXi^{*}Q\varXi+\widehat{\varXi}^{*}G\widehat{\varXi}\over 2}.

(3.8)

Using the representation (
3.7
),
we get the following abstract characterization for the open-loop optimal controls of Problem (LQ-FSVIE).

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\in\Lambda
be any given free pair and
\bar{u}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[t,T]
. Then
\bar{u}(\cdot)
is an open-loop optimal control of
Problem (LQ-FSVIE)
for
(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))
if and only if

{\cal M}_{2}\geqslant 0,\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad{\cal M}_{2}\bar{u}+{\cal M}_{1}{\bf x}_{t}=0.

(3.9)

Proof.

It is clear to see that
\bar{u}(\cdot)
is an optimal control of Problem (LQ-FSVIE) if and only if

J(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot);\bar{u}(\cdot)+\lambda u(\cdot))-J(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot);\bar{u}(\cdot))\geqslant 0,\qquad\forall u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[t,T],~\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.10)

For any
u(\cdot)\in\mathscr{U}[t,T]
and

, by (
3.7
), we have

J(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot);\bar{u}(\cdot)+\lambda u(\cdot))-J(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot);\bar{u}(\cdot))=\lambda^{2}\langle{\cal M}_{2}u,u\rangle+2\lambda\langle{\cal M}_{2}\bar{u}+{\cal M}_{1}{\bf x}_{t},u\rangle.

(3.11)

Thus (
3.10
) holds if and only if (
3.9
) holds. The proof is thus complete. ∎

\displaystyle Y(s)=\psi(s)+\int_{s}^{T}\big[A(\tau,s)^{\top}Y(\tau)+C(\tau,s)^{\top}Z(\tau,s)\big]d\tau-\int_{s}^{T}Z(s,\tau)dW(\tau),\\
\displaystyle Y^{0}(s)=\psi^{0}(s)+\int_{s}^{T}\big[B(\tau,s)^{\top}Y(\tau)+D(\tau,s)^{\top}Z(\tau,s)\big]d\tau-\int_{s}^{T}Z^{0}(s,\tau)dW(\tau).\end{array}\right.

(3.18)

Note that (
3.18
) is a coupled system of FBSVIEs. To our best knowledge, there is no general result on the solvability of coupled FBSVIEs on an arbitrary time horizon. In the rest of the paper, we are going to develop a decoupling method, which is the most important contribution of our paper. The key point is to find the so-called
decoupling field
for FBSVIE (
3.18
). As a preparation, we next provide a new representation for the optimality condition of Problem (LQ-FSVIE).

We introduce the following system of BSVIEs on
[t,T]
:

\left\{\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle Y^{A}(s)=\int_{s}^{T}A(\tau,s)^{\top}\Big[\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[\psi(\tau)]+Y^{A}(\tau)+Z^{C}(\tau,\tau)\Big]d\tau-\int_{s}^{T}Z^{A}(s,\tau)dW(\tau),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle Y^{B}(s)=\int_{s}^{T}B(\tau,s)^{\top}\Big[\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[\psi(\tau)]+Y^{A}(\tau)+Z^{C}(\tau,\tau)\Big]dr-\int_{s}^{T}Z^{B}(s,\tau)dW(r),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle Y^{C}(s)=\int_{s}^{T}C(\tau,s)^{\top}\Big[\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[\psi(\tau)]+Y^{A}(\tau)+Z^{C}(\tau,\tau)\Big]d\tau-\int_{s}^{T}Z^{C}(s,\tau)dW(\tau),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle Y^{D}(s)=\int_{s}^{T}D(\tau,s)^{\top}\Big[\mathbb{E}_{\tau}[\psi(\tau)]+Y^{A}(\tau)+Z^{C}(\tau,\tau)\Big]d\tau-\int_{s}^{T}Z^{D}(s,\tau)dW(\tau).\end{array}\right.

(3.19)

Note that in the above, the diagonal value
Z^{C}(\tau,\tau)
of the process
Z^{C}(\cdot\,,\cdot)
appears, which makes such BSVIEs essentially different from Type-I and Type-II BSVIEs. Thus, we call the above Type-III BSVIEs to distinguish them from the others. BSVIEs with the diagonal values
Z(\tau,\tau)
presented were introduced by Wang and Yong
[
28
]
the first time while studying the time-inconsistent optimal control problems for FSVIEs. The well-posedness of such type of BSVIEs with general generators was established by Hernández and Possamai
[
14
]
. Here, it is the first time that we use the name “Type-III” for these types of BSVIEs. In
[
28
,
14
]
, the diagonal value
Z(\tau,\tau)
was due to the equilibrium HJB equations. Here, we will use this type of BSVIEs to represent the solutions of adjoint equations in the optimality system. This is surprising.

4
Derivation of the Path-dependent Riccati Equation

In this section, we will find the path-dependent Riccait equation for our Problem (LQ-FSVIE) via the HJB equation for the value function. The procedure is formal. However, the arguments over verification is rigorous. Thus, once the well-posedness of the Riccati equation is established, our Problem (LQ-FSVIE) is solved. This also decouples the optimality system (
3.18
).

By
[
27
, Subsection 4.3]
, the path-dependent HJB equation associated with Problem (LQ-FSVIE) reads

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle v_{t}(t,{\bf x})+\inf_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}{\cal H}(t,{\bf x},u,v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x}),v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x}))=0,\qquad(t,{\bf x})\in\Lambda,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle v(T,{\bf x})={1\over 2}\langle G{\bf x}(T),{\bf x}(T)\rangle,\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

where the Hamiltonian
{\cal H}
is defined by

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle{\cal H}(t,{\bf x},u,v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x}),v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x}))\triangleq{1\over 2}\big[C(\cdot\,,t){\bf x}(t)+D(\cdot\,,t)u\big]^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})\big[C(\cdot\,,t){\bf x}(t)+D(\cdot\,,t)u\big)\big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})\big[A(\cdot\,,t){\bf x}(t)\negthinspace+\negthinspace B(\cdot\,,t)u\big]\negthinspace+\negthinspace{1\over 2}{\bf x}(t)^{\top}Q(t){\bf x}(t)\negthinspace+\negthinspace{1\over 2}u^{\top}R(t)u.\end{array}

(4.2)

To understand each term in the above, let us denote

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle A(s,t)=(A_{1}(s,t),A_{2}(s,t),\cdots,A_{n}(s,t)),~B(s,t)=(B_{1}(s,t),B_{2}(s,t),\cdots,B_{m}(s,t)),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle C(s,t)=(C_{1}(s,t),C_{2}(s,t),\cdots,C_{n}(s,t)),~D(s,t)=(D_{1}(s,t),D_{2}(s,t),\cdots,D_{m}(s,t)),\end{array}\quad(s,t)\in\Delta_{*}[0,T],

where all
A_{i}(\cdot\,,\cdot),B_{i}(\cdot\,,\cdot),C_{i}(\cdot\,,\cdot),D_{i}(\cdot\,,\cdot)
are
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued functions. Then, for any
v(\cdot\,,\cdot)\in C^{1,2}_{+}(\Lambda)
, we have the following:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\big[C(\cdot\,,t){\bf x}(t)+D(\cdot\,,t)u\big]^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})\big[C(\cdot\,,t){\bf x}(t)+D(\cdot\,,t)u\big)\big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle={\bf x}(t)^{\top}\big[C(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})C(\cdot\,,t)\big]{\bf x}(t)+{\bf x}(t)^{\top}\big[C(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})D(\cdot\,,t)\big]u\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad+u^{\top}\big[D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})C(\cdot\,,t)\big]{\bf x}(t)+u^{\top}\big[D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})D(\cdot\,,t)\big]u,\end{array}

with (for all
t\in[0,T]
)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle C(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})C(\cdot\,,t)\equiv\Big(C_{i}(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})C_{j}(\cdot\,,t)\Big)\in\mathbb{S}^{n},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})D(\cdot\,,t)\equiv\Big(D_{i}(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})D_{j}(\cdot\,,t)\Big)\in\mathbb{S}^{m},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})C(\cdot\,,t)\equiv\Big(D_{i}(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})C_{j}(\cdot\,,t)\Big)\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle C(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})D(\cdot\,,t)\equiv\Big(C_{i}(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})D_{j}(\cdot\,,t)\Big)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times m},\end{array}

and

v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})\big[A(\cdot\,,t){\bf x}(t)\negthinspace+\negthinspace B(\cdot\,,t)u\big]=\big[v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})A(\cdot\,,t)\big]{\bf x}(t)+\big[v_{\bf x}(t,x)B(\cdot\,,t)\big]u,

with (for all
t\in[0,T]
)

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})A(\cdot\,,t)=\big(v_{x}(t,{\bf x})A_{1}(\cdot\,,t),\cdots,v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})A_{n}(\cdot\,,t)\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times n},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})B(\cdot\,,t)=\big(v_{x}(t,{\bf x})B_{1}(\cdot\,,t),\cdots,v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})B_{m}(\cdot\,,t)\big)\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times m}.\end{array}

From the above, we see that the following condition makes sense:

{\bm{R}}(t,{\bf x})\equiv D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})D(\cdot\,,t)+R(t)\geqslant\lambda I_{m},\quad\hbox{ for some }\lambda>0,

(4.3)

If such a condition holds, then by denoting

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle{\bm{S}}(t,{\bf x})=[D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})C(\cdot\,,t)]{\bf x}(t)+[v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})B(\cdot\,,t)]^{\top},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle{\bm{Q}}(t,{\bf x})=[v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})A(\cdot\,,t)]{\bf x}(t)+{\bf x}(t)^{\top}[v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})A(\cdot\,,t)]^{\top}\negthinspace\negthinspace\negthinspace+{\bf x}(t)^{\top}\big[C(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})C(\cdot\,,t)+Q(t)\big]{\bf x}(t),\end{array}

we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle 2{\cal H}(t,{\bf x},u,v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x}),v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x}))=\big[C(\cdot\,,t){\bf x}(t)+D(\cdot\,,t)u\big]^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})\big[C(\cdot\,,t){\bf x}(t)+D(\cdot\,,t)u\big)\big]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+2v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})\big[A(\cdot\,,t){\bf x}(t)\negthinspace+\negthinspace B(\cdot\,,t)u\big]\negthinspace+\negthinspace{\bf x}(t)^{\top}Q(t){\bf x}(t)\negthinspace+\negthinspace u^{\top}R(t)u\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle=u^{\top}\Big(\big[D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})D(\cdot\,,t)\big]+R(t)\Big)u\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad+u^{\top}\Big(\big[D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})C(\cdot\,,t)\big]{\bf x}(t)+[v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})B(\cdot\,,t)]^{\top}\Big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad+\Big({\bf x}(t)^{\top}\big[C(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})D(\cdot\,,t)\big]+[v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})B(\cdot\,,t)]\Big)u\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad+2[v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})A(\cdot\,,t)]{\bf x}(t)+{\bf x}(t)^{\top}\big[C(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})C(\cdot\,,t)+Q(t)\big]{\bf x}(t)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle=u^{\top}{\bm{R}}(t,{\bf x})u+u^{\top}{\bm{S}}(t,{\bf x})+{\bm{S}}(t,{\bf x})^{\top}u+{\bm{Q}}(t,{\bf x})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle=\Big|{\bm{R}}(t,{\bf x})^{1\over 2}\Big[u+{\bm{R}}(t,{\bf x})^{-1}{\bm{S}}(t,{\bf x})\Big]\Big|^{2}+{\bm{Q}}(t,{\bf x})-{\bm{S}}(t,{\bf x}){\bm{R}}(t,{\bf x})^{-1}{\bm{S}}(t,{\bf x})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\geqslant{\bm{Q}}(t,{\bf x})-{\bm{S}}(t,{\bf x}){\bm{R}}(t,{\bf x})^{-1}{\bm{S}}(t,{\bf x})=\inf_{u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}2{\cal H}(t,{\bf x},u,v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x}),v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x}))\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\equiv 2{\cal H}(t,{\bf x},\bar{\Gamma}(t,{\bf x}),v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x}),v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})),\end{array}

where

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\bar{\Gamma}(t,{\bf x})=-{\bm{R}}(t,{\bf x})^{-1}{\bm{S}}(t,{\bf x})\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle=-\big[D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})D(\cdot\,,t)+R(t)\big]^{-1}\big\{[D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})C(\cdot\,,t)]{\bf x}(t)+[v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})B(\cdot\,,t)]^{\top}\big\}.\end{array}

(4.4)

Substituting the above into (
4.1
), by some straightforward calculations, we get

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle v_{t}(t,{\bf x})+{1\over 2}{\bf x}(t)^{\top}[C(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})C(\cdot\,,t)]{\bf x}(t)+{1\over 2}{\bf x}(t)^{\top}Q(t){\bf x}(t)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\quad+[v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})A(\cdot\,,t)]{\bf x}(t)-{1\over 2}\Big(\big[D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})C(\cdot\,,t){\bf x}(t)+[v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})B(\cdot\,,t)]^{\top}\Big)^{\top}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\quad\times\big[D(\cdot,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})D(\cdot,t)\negthinspace+\negthinspace R(t)\big]^{-1}\Big(\big[D(\cdot,t)^{\top}v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x})C(\cdot,t){\bf x}(t)+[v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x})B(\cdot\,,t)]^{\top}\Big)=0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad(t,{\bf x})\in\mathscr{D},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle v(T,{\bf x})={1\over 2}{\bf x}(T)^{\top}G{\bf x}(T).\end{array}\right.

(4.5)

In the rest of the paper, we shall prove that (
4.5
) really admits a classical solution by introducing a new type of Riccati equations. With the solution of this Riccati equation, the form of the optimal strategy
\bar{\Gamma}(\cdot\,,\cdot)
, defined by (
4.4
), can be simplified and some interesting phenomenons will be found. More importantly, we will show that the solution of the derived Riccati equation is exactly the decoupling filed for the optimality system (
3.18
).

Recall that
{\cal S}(\mathbb{X})
is the set of all symmetric bilinear functionals on the Banach space
\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{X}
. We let

{\cal S}^{n}_{t}\triangleq{\cal S}(\mathscr{X}_{t}),\qquad\forall t\in[0,T],

and let
C([0,T];{\cal S}^{n})
be the set of all
P(\cdot)
having
property that

P(t)\in{\cal S}^{n}_{t},\quad\forall t\in[0,T],

and the map
t\mapsto P(t)
is continuous with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\cal S}
.
From the definition (
1.5
) of the value function of Problem (LQ-FSVIE),
we can see that

v(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))={P(t)\big({\bf x}_{t}(\cdot),{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\big)\over 2},

(4.6)

for some bilinear operator-valued function
P(\cdot)\in C([0,T];{\cal S}^{n})
. We define

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\dot{P}(t)({\bf x}(\cdot),{\bf x}(\cdot))\triangleq\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}{P(t+\delta)\big([{\bf x}]_{t+\delta},[{\bf x}]_{t+\delta}\big)-P(t)\big({\bf x}(\cdot),{\bf x}(\cdot)\big)\over\delta}=v_{t}(t,{\bf x}),\quad\forall(t,{\bf x})\in\Lambda,\end{array}

(4.7)

provided the limit exists. It should be a quadratic form of
{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)
again. Moreover, by (
2.10
)–(
2.11
) and (
4.6
), we get

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))(\eta_{t}(\cdot))=P(t)\big({\bf x}_{t}(\cdot),\eta_{t}(\cdot)\big),\qquad\forall(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\in\Lambda,\,\eta_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{t},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))(\eta_{t}(\cdot),\eta^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot))=P(t)(\eta_{t}(\cdot),\eta^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot)),\qquad\forall(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\in\Lambda,\,\eta_{t}(\cdot),\eta^{\prime}_{t}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{X}_{t}.\end{array}

(4.8)

With the representation (
4.6
) and (
4.8
), from (
4.5
), we identify that
P(\cdot)
should satisfy the following Riccati equation:

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\dot{P}(t)+P(t)A(\cdot\,,t)\delta_{t}+\delta_{t}^{\top}A(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}P(t)+\delta_{t}^{\top}C(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}P(t)C(\cdot\,,t)\delta_{t}+\delta_{t}^{\top}Q(t)\delta_{t}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad-\big[B(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}P(t)+D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}P(t)C(\cdot\,,t)\delta_{t}\big]^{\top}\big[R(t)+D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}P(t)D(\cdot\,,t)\big]^{-1}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\times\big[B(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}P(t)+D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}P(t)C(\cdot\,,t)\delta_{t}\big]=0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle P(T)=G.\end{array}\right.

(4.9)

The above is understood as an equation for a symmetric bilinear form valued function defined on
\Lambda
. More precisely, for any
(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\in\Lambda
, the term
{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)^{\top}\dot{P}(t){\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\equiv\dot{P}(t)\big({\bf x}_{t}(\cdot),{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\big)
is understood as in (
4.7
), and other terms are as follows:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)^{\top}[P(t)A(\cdot\,,t)\delta_{t}]{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)=P(t)\big({\bf x}_{t}(\cdot),A(\cdot\,,t){\bf x}_{t}(t)\big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad=P(t)\big(A(\cdot\,,t){\bf x}_{t}(t),{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\big)={\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)^{\top}[\delta_{t}^{\top}A(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}P(t)]{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)^{\top}[\delta_{t}^{\top}\negthinspace C(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}\negthinspace P(t)C(\cdot\,,t)\delta_{t}]{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\negthinspace=\negthinspace P(t)\big(C(\cdot\,,t){\bf x}_{t}(t),C(\cdot\,,t){\bf x}_{t}(t)\big)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad=\negthinspace{\bf x}_{t}(t)^{\top}\negthinspace\Big(P(t)\big(C_{i}(\cdot\,,t),C_{j}(\cdot\,,t)\big)\Big){\bf x}_{t}(t),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)^{\top}[\delta_{t}^{\top}Q(t)\delta_{t}]{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)={\bf x}_{t}(t)^{\top}Q(t){\bf x}_{t}(t),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}P(t)D(\cdot,t)=\Big(P(t)\big(D_{i}(\cdot\,,t),D_{j}(\cdot\,,t)\big)\Big)\in\mathbb{S}^{m},\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\big[B(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}\negthinspace P(t)\negthinspace+\negthinspace D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}\negthinspace P(t)C(\cdot\,,t)\delta_{t}\big]{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)=\Big(P(t)\big(B_{j}(\cdot\,,t),{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\big)\negthinspace+\negthinspace P(t)\big(D_{j}(\cdot\,,t),C(\cdot\,,t){\bf x}_{t}(t)\big)\Big)\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.\end{array}

Definition 4.1
.

We call
P(\cdot)\in C([0,T];{\cal S}^{n})
a
solution
of Riccati equation (
4.9
) if it satisfies (
4.9
) for any
t\in[0,T]
.
Further, it is called a
strongly regular solution
,
if, in addition, the following holds:

R(t)+D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}P(t)D(\cdot\,,t)\geqslant\lambda I_{m};\quad t\in[0,T],\quad\hbox{ for some }\lambda>0.

(4.10)

We emphasize that for any
t\in[0,T]
, the domain of
P(t)
is
\mathscr{X}_{t}\times\mathscr{X}_{t}
, which is merely a Banach space rather than a Hilbert space, and as
t\in[0,T]
varies, it changes. Thus, (
4.9
) is significantly different from the so-called
operator-valued Riccati equation
derived from the LQ control problem for (stochastic) evolution equations (see Li–Yong
[
17
]
and Lü
[
18
]
, for example). To emphasize the new features, we would like to call (
4.9
) a
path-dependent Riccati equation
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that the path-dependent Riccati equation (
4.9
) admits a strongly regular solution
P(\cdot)\in C([0,T];{\cal S}^{n})
.
Then the function
v(\cdot\,,\cdot)
, defined by

v(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))={P(t)\big({\bf x}_{t}(\cdot),{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\big)\over 2},\qquad\forall(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\in\Lambda,

(4.11)

is a classical of the path-dependent HJB equation (
4.5
). Moreover, the minimizer
\bar{\Gamma}(\cdot,\cdot)
of the Hamiltonian
{\cal H}
can be represented as

\bar{\Gamma}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))=-\big[D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}P(t)D(\cdot\,,t)+R(t)\big]^{-1}\big[D(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}P(t)C(\cdot\,,t){\bf x}_{t}(t)+B(\cdot\,,t)^{\top}P(t){\bf x}_{t}(\cdot)\big].

(4.12)

Proof.

By (
4.9
), with (
4.7
)–(
4.8
), we can get that the function
v(\cdot,\cdot)
defined by (
4.11
)
satisfies (
4.5
) immediately.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

From
Theorem 4.2
, we have obtained the Riccati equation associated with Problem (LQ-FSVIE). The key of our approach is that
v(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))
, as well as
v_{t}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))
,
v_{\bf x}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))
and
v_{{\bf x}{\bf x}}(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))
are well-defined on the space
\mathscr{X}_{t}
. Then, from the HJB equation, we correctly identify
P(\cdot)
.

Remark 4.4
.

If
\alpha(s,r)\equiv\alpha(r)
for
\alpha(\cdot\,,\cdot)=A(\cdot\,,\cdot),B(\cdot\,,\cdot),C(\cdot\,,\cdot),D(\cdot\,,\cdot)
,
and
\varphi(t,s)\equiv x;s\in[t,T]
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
then state equation (
1.2
) becomes an SDE and Problem (LQ-FSVIE) reduces to a classical stochastic LQ optimal control problem. The corresponding Riccati equation reads (see
[
36
, Chapter 6]
):

\left\{\negthinspace\negthinspace\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\dot{\Sigma}(t)+\Sigma(t)A(t)+A(t)^{\top}\Sigma(t)+C(t)^{\top}\Sigma(t)C(t)+Q(t)-[\Sigma(t)B(t)+C(t)^{\top}\Sigma(t)D(t)]\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\quad\times[R(t)+D(t)^{\top}\Sigma(t)D(t)]^{-1}[B(t)^{\top}\Sigma(t)+D(t)^{\top}\Sigma(t)C(t)]=0,\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad t\in[0,T],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\Sigma(T)=G.\end{array}\right.

(4.13)

By comparing the above with (
4.9
), we have

P(t)({\bf x}_{t}(\cdot),{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))={\bf x}_{t}(t)^{\top}\Sigma(t){\bf x}_{t}(t),\qquad\forall(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\in\Lambda^{0}.

The corresponding value function is given by

V(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))={1\over 2}P(t)({\bf x}_{t}(\cdot),{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))={1\over 2}{\bf x}_{t}(t)^{\top}\Sigma(t){\bf x}_{t}(t),\qquad\forall(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))\in\Lambda^{0}.

Then, all the derivatives of
V(t,{\bf x}_{t}(\cdot))
are taken for all points in
\Lambda^{0}
which is idetified with
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.. In the above sense, our result recovers the classical one for Problem (LQ-SDE).

5
Decoupling the Optimality System

In this section, we shall show that the solution
P(\cdot)
to Riccati equation (
4.9
) is exactly the so-called
decoupling field
for the optimality system (
3.18
),
which is a coupled FBSVIE. Note that the solution of the path-dependent HJB equation associated with Problem (LQ-FSVIE)
can be represented by (
4.6
). Thus, the connection between the Hamiltonian system (
3.18
) and the HJB equation (
4.5
) associated with Problem (LQ-FSVIE) is also established.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(H1)
–
(H3)
hold. Suppose that Riccati equation (
4.9
) admits a strongly regular solution
P(\cdot)\in C([0,T];{\cal S}^{n})
. Then the solutions of the optimality system (
3.18
) admit the following representation:

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\bar{X}(s)=\bar{\cal X}(s,s),\qquad Y^{0}(s)=-R(s)\Theta(s)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot,s),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle Y(s)=\big[C(\cdot\,,s)^{\top}\negthinspace P(s)C(\cdot\,,s)+Q(s)\big]\bar{X}(s)+\big[A(\cdot\,,s)^{\top}\negthinspace P(s)+C(\cdot\,,s)^{\top}\negthinspace P(s)D(\cdot\,,s)\Theta(s)\big]\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,s),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad s\in[t,T],\end{array}

(5.1)

where

\Theta(s)\triangleq-[R(s)+D(\cdot\,,s)^{\top}P(s)D(\cdot\,,s)]^{-1}[B(\cdot\,,s)^{\top}P(s)+D(\cdot\,,s)^{\top}P(s)C(\cdot\,,s)\delta_{s}],\quad s\in[t,T],

(5.2)

with
\bar{X}(\cdot)
being the unique solution to the closed-loop system

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\bar{X}(s)={\bf x}_{t}(s)+\int_{t}^{s}\big[A(s,\tau)\bar{X}(\tau)+B(s,\tau)\Theta(\tau)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,\tau)\big]d\tau\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{t}^{s}\big[C(s,\tau)\bar{X}(\tau)+D(s,\tau)\Theta(\tau)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot,\tau)\big]dW(\tau),\qquad s\in[t,T],\end{array}

(5.3)

and
\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,\cdot)
being the unique solution to the closed-loop auxiliary system

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\bar{\cal X}(s,r)={\bf x}_{t}(s)+\int_{t}^{r}\big[A(s,\tau)\bar{\cal X}(\tau,\tau)+B(s,\tau)\Theta(\tau)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot,\tau)\big]d\tau\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\int_{t}^{r}\big[C(s,\tau)\bar{\cal X}(\tau,\tau)+D(s,\tau)\Theta(\tau)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot,\tau)\big]dW(\tau),\qquad(s,r)\in\Delta_{*}[t,T].\end{array}

(5.4)

Moreover, the optimal control
\bar{u}(\cdot)
admits the following causal state feedback representation:

\bar{u}(s)=\Theta(s)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,s),\quad s\in[t,T].

(5.5)

Remark 5.2
.

By
Theorem 5.1
, the optimality system (
3.18
) is decoupled, and the optimal control
\bar{u}(\cdot)
is represented as a causal state feedback (see (
5.5
)). Consequently, in principle, the optimal control is practically realizable.

Remark 5.3
.

Note that when
B(\cdot\,,\cdot)\equiv 0
, the optimal control
\bar{u}(\cdot)
can be represented by

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\bar{u}(s)=\Theta(s)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,s)=-[R(s)+D(\cdot\,,s)^{\top}P(s)D(\cdot\,,s)]^{-1}D(\cdot\,,s)^{\top}P(s)C(\cdot\,,s)\bar{X}(s)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad=:\Theta^{*}(s)\bar{X}(s),\qquad s\in[t,T],\end{array}

(5.6)

with

\bar{X}(s)={\bf x}_{t}(s)+\int_{t}^{s}A(s,r)\bar{X}(r)dr+\int_{t}^{s}\big[C(s,r)\bar{X}(r)+D(s,r)\Theta^{*}(r)\bar{X}(r)\big]dW(r),\quad s\in[t,T].

(5.7)

It is particularly worthy of pointing out that though the state equation, (
5.7
) is a non-Markovian system,
the optimal control
\bar{u}(\cdot)
, defined by (
5.6
), can be uniquely determined by the current value of the state.
Thus, when the drift term of (
1.2
) dose not contain controls,
the causal feedback representation of the optimal control, obtained by
Theorem 5.1
,
reduces to a
state feedback
(or called a
Markovian feedback
), which is interesting.

Proof of
Theorem 5.1
.

From the uniqueness of the solution to (
5.3
), we have
\bar{X}(s)=\bar{\cal X}(s,s),s\in[t,T]
. Let

\eta(s)=G\bar{\cal X}(T,T)-\int_{s}^{T}\zeta(r)dW(r),\qquad s\in[t,T],

(5.8)

and denote

\displaystyle\psi(s)=Q(s)\bar{\cal X}(s,s)+A(T,s)^{\top}G\bar{\cal X}(T,T)+C(T,s)^{\top}\zeta(s),

\displaystyle\psi^{0}(s)=B(T,s)^{\top}G\bar{\cal X}(T,T)+D(T,s)^{\top}\zeta(s).

(5.9)

Then for any
{\bf x}_{s}\in\mathscr{X}_{s}
, by the functional Itô’s formula (see
2.11
), we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle d\big\{[{\bf x}_{s}]_{r}(\cdot)^{\top}P(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)\big\}\equiv d\big\{P(r)\big([{\bf x}_{s}]_{r}(\cdot),\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)\big)\big\}\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\quad=\big\{[{\bf x}_{s}]_{r}(\cdot)^{\top}\dot{P}(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)+[{\bf x}_{s}]_{r}(\cdot)^{\top}P(r)[A(\cdot\,,r)\bar{\cal X}(r,r)+B(\cdot\,,r)\Theta(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)]\big\}dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\qquad+[{\bf x}_{s}]_{r}(\cdot)^{\top}P(r)\big[C(\cdot\,,r)\bar{\cal X}(r,r)+D(\cdot\,,r)\Theta(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)\big]dW(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\quad=\big\{P(r)\big([{\bf x}_{s}]_{r}(\cdot),A(\cdot\,,r)\bar{\cal X}(r,r)+B(\cdot\,,r)\Theta(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)\big)-{\bf x}_{s}(r)^{\top}C(\cdot,r)^{\top}P(r)C(\cdot,r)\bar{\cal X}(r,r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\quad\qquad-{\bf x}_{s}(r)^{\top}Q\bar{\cal X}(r,r)-\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)^{\top}P(r)A(\cdot\,,r){\bf x}_{s}(r)-[{\bf x}_{s}]_{r}(\cdot)^{\top}P(r)A(\cdot,r)\bar{\cal X}(r,r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\quad\qquad-{\bf x}_{s}(r)^{\top}C(\cdot\,,r)^{\top}P(r)D(\cdot\,,r)\Theta(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot,r)-{\bf x}_{s}(r)^{\top}P(r)B(\cdot\,,r)\Theta(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot,r)\big\}dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\quad\qquad+[{\bf x}_{s}]_{r}(\cdot)^{\top}P(r)\big[C(\cdot\,,r)\bar{\cal X}(r,r)+D(\cdot\,,r)\Theta(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)]dW(r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\quad=-\big\{{\bf x}_{s}(r)^{\top}A(\cdot\,,r)^{\top}P(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)+{\bf x}_{s}(r)^{\top}C(\cdot\,,r)^{\top}P(r)C(\cdot\,,r)\bar{\cal X}(r,r)\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\quad\qquad+{\bf x}_{s}(r)^{\top}Q(r)\bar{\cal X}(r,r)+{\bf x}_{s}(r)^{\top}C(\cdot\,,r)^{\top}P(r)D(\cdot\,,r)\Theta(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)\big\}dr\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle\quad\qquad+[{\bf x}_{s}]_{r}(\cdot)^{\top}P(r)[C(\cdot\,,r)\bar{\cal X}(r,r)+D(\cdot\,,r)\Theta(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)]dW(r),\quad r\in[s,T].\end{array}

Combining the above with (
5.8
), we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle[{\bf x}_{s}]_{r}(\cdot)^{\top}P(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)-{\bf x}_{s}(T)^{\top}\mathbb{E}_{r}[G\bar{\cal X}(T,T)]\\
\displaystyle\quad=\int_{r}^{T}\Big\{{\bf x}_{s}(\tau)^{\top}A(\cdot\,,\tau)^{\top}P(\tau)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,\tau)+{\bf x}_{s}(\tau)^{\top}C(\cdot\,,\tau)^{\top}P(\tau)C(\cdot\,,\tau)\bar{\cal X}(\tau,\tau)\\
\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+{\bf x}_{s}(\tau)^{\top}Q(\tau)\bar{\cal X}(\tau,\tau)+{\bf x}_{s}(\tau)^{\top}C(\cdot\,,\tau)^{\top}P(\tau)D(\cdot\,,\tau)\Theta(\tau)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,\tau)\Big\}d\tau\\
\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{r}^{T}\Big\{[{\bf x}_{s}]_{\tau}(\cdot)^{\top}P(\tau)[C(\cdot\,,\tau)\bar{\cal X}(\tau,\tau)+D(\cdot\,,\tau)\Theta(\tau)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,\tau)]-{\bf x}_{s}(T)^{\top}\zeta(\tau)\Big\}dW(\tau).\end{array}

(5.10)

Denote

\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle Y^{A}(s)=A(\cdot\,,s)^{\top}P(s)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,s)-A(T,s)^{\top}\mathbb{E}_{s}[G\bar{\cal X}(T,T)],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle Z^{A}(s,r)=[A(\cdot\,,s)]_{r}P(r)\big[C(\cdot\,,r)\bar{\cal X}(r,r)+D(\cdot\,,r)\Theta(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)\big]-A(T,s)^{\top}\zeta(r),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle Y^{B}(s)=B(\cdot\,,r)^{\top}P(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)-B(T,s)^{\top}\mathbb{E}_{s}[G\bar{\cal X}(T,T)],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle Z^{B}(s,r)=[B(\cdot\,,s)]_{r}^{\top}P(r)\big[C(\cdot\,,r)\bar{\cal X}(r,r)+D(\cdot\,,r)\Theta(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)\big]-B(T,s)^{\top}\zeta(r),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle Y^{C}(s)=C(\cdot\,,r)^{\top}P(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)-C(T,s)^{\top}\mathbb{E}_{s}[G\bar{\cal X}(T,T)],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle Z^{C}(s,r)=[C(\cdot\,,s)]_{r}^{\top}P(r)\big[C(\cdot\,,r)\bar{\cal X}(r,r)+D(\cdot\,,r)\Theta(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)]-C(T,s)^{\top}\zeta(r),\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle Y^{D}(s)=D(\cdot\,,r)^{\top}P(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)-D(T,s)^{\top}\mathbb{E}_{s}[G\bar{\cal X}(T,T)],\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\displaystyle Z^{D}(s,r)=[D(\cdot\,,s)]_{r}^{\top}P(r)\big[C(\cdot\,,r)\bar{\cal X}(r,r)+D(\cdot\,,r)\Theta(r)\bar{\cal X}(\cdot\,,r)]-D(T,s)^{\top}\zeta(r).\end{array}

(5.11)

Taking
{\bf x}_{s}(\cdot)=A(\cdot\,,s)
and

7
Conclusion

The main contribution of this paper is that we develop a decoupling method for
the optimality system associated with Problem (LQ-FSVIE)
and then represent the optimal control as a causal feedback of the state process.
The key technique is establishing a link between the type-I and type-II BSVIEs, deriving
the associated path-dependent Riccati equation, and proving the solvability of this new type of Riccati equations.
We believe that this paper can open the door to the Riccati-equation approach for the LQ problem of SVIEs.
Applying the mollification method, the main results obtained in the paper
still hold true for the case with some nonsmooth (or singular) coefficients;
Using the method developed in the paper, the framework can be further extended to the indefinite LQ optimal control problems for SVIEs,
the LQ game problems for SVIEs, the LQ control/game problems for mean-field SVIEs, etc.
A still challenging problem is the LQ optimal control problem for SVIEs with random coefficients.
Moreover, we hope that our approach of decoupling the optimality systems and establishing the
well-posedness of the path-dependent Riccati (or HJB) equation can help us to solve some nonlinear problems.
We will report the related results in our future publications.

A metric uniformization model for the Quasi-Fuchsian space Thanks: Christian El Emam (christian.elemam@uni.lu): University of Luxembourg, Department of Mathematics, 6 avenue de la Fonte, L-4364 Esch-Sur-Alzette, Luxembourg


Abstract
. We introduce and study a novel metric uniformization model for the quasi-Fuchsian space QF ⁡ ( S ) \mathrm{QF}(S) , defined through a class of ℂ {\mathbb{C}} -valued bilinear forms on S S , called Bers metrics, which coincide with hyperbolic Riemannian metrics along the Fuchsian locus. By employing this approach, we present a new model of the holomorphic tangent bundle of QF ⁡ ( S ) \mathrm{QF}(S) that extends the metric model for 𝒯 ⁡ ( S ) \mathcal{T}(S) defined by Berger and Ebin, and give an integral representation of the Goldman symplectic form and of the holomorphic extension of the Weil-Petersson metric to QF ⁡ ( S ) \mathrm{QF}(S) , with a new proof of its existence and non-degeneracy. We also determine new bounds for the Schwarzian of Bers projective structures extending Kraus estimate. Lastly, we use this formalism to give alternative proofs to several classic results in quasi-Fuchsian theory.



1.
Introduction

Let
S
be a closed oriented surface of genus
\mathrm{g}\geq 2
. The quasi-Fuchsian space
\mathrm{QF}(S)
is a distinctive neighborhood of the space of Fuchsian representations inside the
(\pi_{1}(S),\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}}))
-character variety. Quasi-Fuchsian hyperbolic manifolds and the space
\mathrm{QF}(S)
have been widely studied in the last century. One of the most powerful theorems in this area is Bers Simultaneous Uniformization Theorem (see Theorem
1.1
), showing that
\mathrm{QF}(S)
is biholomorphic to
\mathcal{T}(S)\times T(\overline{S})
, with
\mathcal{T}(S)
being the Teichmüller space of
S
.

In this paper, we approach a new
metric
perspective in the study of the geometry of quasi-Fuchsian space. In fact, Bers Theorem suggests the definition of a family of
{\mathbb{C}}
-valued bilinear forms on
S
, that we call
Bers metrics
(see Sections
1.1
and
3.1
), and a way to associate to each of them a quasi-Fuchsian holonomy, in a way that extends the holonomy assignment of hyperbolic Riemannian metrics to Fuchsian representations. One useful aspect of this approach is that there is a natural way to deform Bers metrics into new Bers metrics by adding holomorphic quadratic differentials, inducing a natural way to deform quasi-Fuchsian representations (see Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
).
This approach comes with several consequences:

•

We give a new metric model for the holomorphic tangent bundle of
\mathrm{QF}(S)
, which extends the metric model for the tangent bundle of the space of hyperbolic metrics up to isotopy as in
[
BE69
]
and
[
FT84
]
(see Remark
4.5
).

•

We give an integral description for the Goldman symplectic form on
\mathrm{QF}(S)
and of the holomorphic extension of the Weil-Petersson metric to
\mathrm{QF}(S)
(which coincides with the one showed in
[
LS17
]
), and provide a new proof of its holomorphicity and non-degeneracy.

•

We present a new bound for the Schwarzian of Bers projective structures. More precisely, we give a lower bound for the distance from the boundary of any point inside the open bounded subset
\mathcal{S}_{[c]}^{+}\subset\mathrm{HQD}([c])
of the Schwarzian derivatives of Bers projective structures for
[c]\in\mathcal{T}(S)
.

•

We give new proofs and interpretations of a few well-known results, about the differential of the Schwarzian map, the description of the affine structures on
\mathcal{T}(S)
from Bers embeddings, and McMullen’s quasi-Fuchsian reciprocity Theorem.

1.1.
The metric uniformizing approach for quasi-Fuchsian space

The complex Lie group
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
acts on
\mathbb{H}^{3}
by isometries and by Möbius maps on its visual boundary
\mathbb{CP}^{1}\cong\partial{\mathbb{H}^{3}}
.

We say that a discrete and faithful representation
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
is
quasi-Fuchsian
if its limit set
\Lambda_{\rho}\subset\mathbb{CP}^{1}
is a Jordan curve on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, hence its complementary is the disjoint union of two topological disks. The reader can find a short introduction to this topic in Section
2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}(S)
denote the space of complex structures on the oriented surface
S
. The most important theorem about quasi-Fuchsian representations is Bers Simultaneous Uniformization Theorem (
[
Ber60
]
), that we recall in the following version.

Theorem 1.1

(Bers Simultaneous Uniformization Theorem)
.

For all
(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})\in\mathcal{C}(S)\times\mathcal{C}(\overline{S})
, there exist:

•

a unique quasi-Fuchsian representation
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
up to conjugacy,

•

a unique
\rho
-equivariant holomorphic diffeomorphism

f_{1}=f_{1}(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})\colon(\widetilde{S},c_{1})\to\Omega_{\rho}^{+}\ ,

•

a unique
\rho
-equivariant antiholomorphic diffeomorphism

\overline{f_{2}}=\overline{f_{2}}(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})\colon(\widetilde{S},\overline{c_{2}})\to\Omega_{\rho}^{-}\ .

where
\Omega_{\rho}^{+}\sqcup\Omega_{\rho}^{-}=\mathbb{CP}^{1}\setminus\Lambda_{\rho}
.
This correspondence defines a biholomorphism

\mathfrak{B}\colon\mathcal{T}(S)\times\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{QF}(S).

In the paper, we will mostly see
\mathfrak{B}
as an identification.

Now, for each
c\in\mathcal{C}(S)
, the uniformization theorem provides a
(\pi_{1}(S),\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}}))
-equivariant biholomorphism
f\colon(\widetilde{S},c)\to H\subset{\mathbb{C}}
from its universal cover to the upper half plane, and the hyperbolic metric in the conformal class of
c
is the pull-back through
f
of the Riemannian metric on the hyperbolic space in its half-plane model, namely

f^{*}\left(\frac{1}{Im(z)^{2}}dzd\overline{z}\right)=-\frac{4}{(f-\overline{f})^{2}}dfd\overline{f}\

which, being
\pi_{1}(S)
-invariant, defines a hyperbolic metric on
S
.

Bers Theorem suggests a way to extend this construction, assigning to each element of
\mathcal{C}(S)\times\mathcal{C}(\overline{S})
, a symmetric 2-tensor on
S
in the following way:

(1)

\begin{split}g\colon\mathcal{C}(S)\times\mathcal{C}(\overline{S})&\to\Gamma(Sym_{2}(TS,\mathbb{C}))\\
(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})&\mapsto g(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}}):=-\frac{4}{(f_{1}-\overline{f_{2}})^{2}}df_{1}\cdot d\overline{f_{2}}\end{split}

where
f_{1}=f_{1}(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})
and
\overline{f_{2}}=\overline{f_{2}}(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})
are as in the statement of Bers Theorem
1.1
, and
\Gamma(Sym_{2}(TS,\mathbb{C}))
denotes the space of smooth sections of
\mathbb{C}
-valued symmetric 2-forms on
TS
(in other words,
Sym_{2}(TS,\mathbb{C})=Sym_{2}(TS,\mathbb{R})\oplus iSym_{2}(TS,\mathbb{R})
). One can easily show that the tensors defined this way are well-posed on
\widetilde{S}
, namely they depend neither on the choice of the affine chart of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
(containing the images of
f_{1}
and
\overline{f_{2}}
), nor on the conjugacy class of the quasi-Fuchsian representation in the construction. In addition, it is clearly
\pi_{1}(S)
-invariant, defining a symmetric
{\mathbb{C}}
-valued tensor on
S
. Finally, if
c_{1}=c_{2}=:c
, then
g(c,\overline{c})
is just the hyperbolic metric in the conformal class of
c
.

We call
Bers metrics
the bilinear forms in the form
g(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})
as in (
1
).

In fact, Bers metrics admit an intrinsic characterization: they correspond to the connected component of positive complex metrics of constant curvature
-1
that contains hyperbolic Riemannian metrics (see Section
3.1
below).

In a sense, Bers metrics allow to
uniformize

\mathcal{C}(S)\times\mathcal{C}(\overline{S})
in a way that extends the classic uniformization of complex structures with hyperbolic Riemannian metrics.

We define the
holonomy
of
g=g(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})
as
[g]=([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])\in\mathrm{QF}(S)
.

A useful aspect of this approach is that (pointwise) holomorphic deformations of Bers metrics induce holomorphic deformations of the holonomy in
\mathrm{QF}(S)
(see Lemma
4.10
). In fact, the first part of the paper discusses an interesting type of holomorphic deformation, which is the following.
Let
\mathrm{HQD}(c)
denote the space of holomorphic quadratic differentials for the Riemann surface
(S,c)
. Then, if
g=g(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})
is a Bers metric, for all
q_{1}\in\mathrm{HQD}(c_{1})
and
\overline{q_{2}}\in\mathrm{HQD}(\overline{c_{2}})
small enough,

g+q_{1}\qquad\qquad\text{and}\qquad\qquad g+\overline{q_{2}}

are Bers metrics as well. This comes with several interesting consequences, as explained in the following theorems (in the text, they follow from Propositon
3.8
, Proposition
3.13
, Theorem
4.1
)

Theorem A
.

Let
(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})\in\mathcal{C}(S)\times\mathcal{C}(\overline{S})
and consider the Bers metric
g=g(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})
.

There exists an open subset
U_{g}\subset\mathrm{HQD}(c_{1})
,
0\in U_{g}
, such that, for all
q_{1}\in U_{g}
,
g+q_{1}
is a Bers metric.

The map

\displaystyle U_{g}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{T}(S)\times\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})

\displaystyle q_{1}

\displaystyle\mapsto[g+q_{1}]

gives a holomorphic local parametrization of the Bers slice
\{[c_{1}]\}\times\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})
, which is essentially independent from the choice of
c_{1}
and
\overline{c_{2}}
in their isotopy classes
[c_{1}]\in\mathcal{T}(S)
and
[\overline{c_{2}}]\in\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})
.

Theorem B
.

Let
(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})\in\mathcal{C}(S)\times\mathcal{C}(\overline{S})
and consider the Bers metric
g=g(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})
.

There exists an open subset
V_{g}\subset\mathrm{HQD}(\overline{c_{2}})
,
0\in V_{g}
, such that, for all
\overline{q_{2}}\in V_{g}
,
g+\overline{q_{2}}
is a Bers metric.

The map

\displaystyle V_{g}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{T}(S)\times\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})

\displaystyle\overline{q_{2}}

\displaystyle\mapsto[g+\overline{q_{2}}]

gives a holomorphic local parametrization of the Bers slice
\mathcal{T}(S)\times\{[\overline{c_{2}}]\}
, which is essentially independent from the choice of
c_{1}
and
\overline{c_{2}}
in their isotopy classes
[c_{1}]\in\mathcal{T}(S)
and
[\overline{c_{2}}]\in\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})
.

As a result, we get a
uniformizing metric
model of the holomorphic tangent bundle to quasi-Fuchsian space given by

(2)

T_{([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])}\mathcal{T}(S)\times\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})=\{q_{1}+\overline{q_{2}}\ |\ q_{1}\in\mathrm{HQD}(c_{1}),\ \overline{q_{2}}\in\mathrm{HQD}(\overline{c_{2}})\},

which is a vector subspace of the space of smooth sections of
Sym_{2}(TS,\mathbb{C})
and which extends the metric model for the tangent bundle of
\mathcal{T}(S)
on the Fuchsian locus (see Remark
4.5
).

1.2.
Metric deformation and Schwarzian deformation

Bers Theorem associates to each pair
(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})\in\mathcal{C}(S)\times\mathcal{C}(\overline{S})
two projective structures
(f_{1}(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}}),\rho)
and
(\overline{f_{2}}(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}}),\rho)
, for the complex structures
c_{1}
and
\overline{c_{2}}
respectively.

Consider the maps

(3)

\begin{split}\mathbf{Schw}_{+}\colon\{\text{Bers metrics}\}&\to\mathbf{HQD}(S)\\
g(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})&\mapsto Schw(f_{1}(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}}))\end{split}

and

(4)

\begin{split}\mathbf{Schw}_{-}\colon\{\text{Bers metrics}\}&\to\mathbf{HQD}(\overline{S})\\
g(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})&\mapsto Schw(\overline{f_{2}}(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}}))\end{split}

where
Schw
denotes the Schwarzian derivative (see Section
2.4
).

The following theorem (Theorem
4.1
in the paper) shows the relation between the metric deformation of
g
with
g+q_{1}
and
g+\overline{q_{2}}
and the deformation of the Schwarzian of the corresponding Bers’ projective structures.

Theorem C
.

Let
g=g(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})
be a Bers metric.

Then,

\mathbf{Schw}_{+}(g+q_{1})=\mathbf{Schw}_{+}(g)-\frac{1}{2}q_{1}

for all
q_{1}\in\mathrm{HQD}(c_{1})
such that
g+q_{1}
is a Bers metric, and

\mathbf{Schw}_{-}(g+\overline{q_{2}})=\mathbf{Schw}_{-}(g)-\frac{1}{2}\overline{q_{2}}

for all
\overline{q_{2}}\in\mathrm{HQD}(\overline{c_{2}})
such that
g+\overline{q_{2}}
is a Bers metric.

1.3.
The holomorphic extension of Weil-Petersson metric

A few extensions of the Weil-Petersson metric to
\mathrm{QF}(S)
have been studied in literature.
We mention, for instance, that in
[
Bri10
]
and
[
Bri+15
]
, the authors define and study a Riemannian extension.

In
[
LS17
]
, Loustau and Sanders show that the Weil-Petersson metric extends to a holomorphic Riemannian metric (see Section
2.7
for the definition) on the quasi-Fuchsian space, whose real part is therefore a pseudo-Riemannian metric on
\mathrm{QF}(S)
. We show that the metric formalism allows to give an alternative proof of the existence and uniqueness of the holomorphic extension of the Weil-Petersson metric, providing a new explicit description of it.

Let us define it. Let
(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})\in\mathcal{C}(S)\times\mathcal{C}(\overline{S})
and consider the corresponding Bers metric
g=g(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})
.
The metric
g
induces a
{\mathbb{C}}
-bilinear form on the bundle
Sym_{2}(TS)\oplus iSym_{2}(TS)
given by

<\alpha,\beta>_{g}=\sum_{i,j,k,\ell=1}^{2}\alpha(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})\beta(\partial_{k},\partial_{\ell})g^{ik}g^{j\ell}\ .

where
\{\partial_{1},\partial_{2}\}
is any local basis for
TS
and
{g^{i}j}
is the inverse matrix of the representative matrix
g_{ij}
.

Bers metrics come with a natural notion of area form compatible with the orientation (see Remark
3.7
), namely

dA_{g}=-\frac{2i}{(f_{1}-\overline{f_{2}})^{2}}df_{1}\wedge d\overline{f_{2}}\ .

One can therefore define a
{\mathbb{C}}
-bilinear form on
T_{([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])}\mathrm{QF}(S)=\{q_{1}+\overline{q_{2}}\ |\ q_{1}\in\mathrm{HQD}(c_{1}),\overline{q_{2}}\in\mathrm{HQD}(\overline{c_{2}})\}
by considering

(5)

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{g}=\frac{1}{8}\int_{S}<\alpha,\beta>_{g}dA_{g}

The following theorem follows from Proposition
5.1
, Theorem
5.4
, Corollary
5.7
, and Corollary
5.8
.

Theorem D
.

The bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{g}
only depends on
g
through its holonomy, and it defines a well-posed holomorphic Riemannian metric on
\mathrm{QF}(S)
which coincides with the Weil-Petersson metric on the Fuchsian locus and which is invariant under the action of the mapping class group.

By uniqueness of the holomorphic extension (see Corollary
5.7
), this coincides with the holomorphic extension introduced in
[
LS17
]
.

Explicitly,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{g}
can be seen as follows:

(1)

\langle q_{1},q^{\prime}_{1}\rangle_{g}=0
, for all
q_{1},q^{\prime}_{1}\in\mathrm{HQD}(c_{1})
.

(2)

\langle\overline{q_{2}},\overline{q^{\prime}_{2}}\rangle_{g}=0
for all
\overline{q_{2}},\overline{q_{2}^{\prime}}\in\mathrm{HQD}(\overline{c_{2}})
.

(3)

Let
g=\rho dzd\overline{w}
,
q_{1}=\varphi dz^{2}\in\mathrm{HQD}(c_{1})
,
\overline{q_{2}}=\overline{\psi}d\overline{w}^{2}\in\mathrm{HQD}(\overline{c_{2}})
, then

\langle q_{1},\overline{q_{2}}\rangle_{g}=\frac{1}{4}i\int_{S}\frac{\varphi\cdot\overline{\psi}}{\rho}dz\wedge d\overline{w}

As a consequence we get an integral description of the Goldman symplectic form
\omega_{G}
on
\mathrm{QF}(S)
given by

\omega_{G}(q_{1},\overline{q_{2}})_{|([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])}=2i\int_{S}\frac{\varphi\cdot\overline{\psi}}{\rho}dz\wedge d\overline{w}

with Bers slices being Lagrangian (see Proposition
5.9
).

1.4.
Bounds for the Schwarzian of Bers projective structures

The maps
\mathbf{Schw}_{+}
and
\mathbf{Schw}_{-}
defined above in (
3
) and (
4
) descend to maps from
\mathrm{QF}(S)
, namely

\begin{split}Schw_{+}\colon\mathrm{QF}(S)&\to\mathrm{HQD}(S)\\
([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])&\mapsto[Schw(f_{1}(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}}))]\end{split}\qquad\qquad\begin{split}Schw_{-}\colon\mathrm{QF}(S)&\to\mathrm{HQD}(\overline{S})\\
([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])&\mapsto[Schw(\overline{f_{2}}(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}}))]\end{split}\ ,

where
[\mathbf{Schw}_{+}(g(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}}))]=Schw_{+}([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])
and
[\mathbf{Schw}_{-}(g(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}}))]=Schw_{-}([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])

Both maps are known to be biholomorphisms onto their images (see for instance
[
Dum09
]
).
In particular, for all given
([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])\in\mathcal{T}(S)\times\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{+}_{[c_{1}]}=Schw_{+}\Big(\{[c_{1}]\}\times\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})\Big)\subset\mathrm{HQD}([c_{1}])

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{-}_{[\overline{c_{2}}]}:=Schw_{-}\Big(\mathcal{T}(S)\times\{[\overline{c_{2}}]\}\Big)\subset\mathrm{HQD}([\overline{c_{2}}])

are open subsets.

The shape of these open subsets is not clear in general. By the classic Kraus-Nehari theorem (
[
Kra32
]
,
[
Neh49
]
), we know that
\mathcal{S}^{+}_{[c_{1}]}
(resp.
\mathcal{S}^{-}_{[\overline{c_{2}}]}
) contains the ball of radius
\frac{1}{2}
and is contained in the ball of radius
\frac{3}{2}
centered in zero with respect to the norm-infinity ball for the hyperbolic metric on
\mathrm{HQD}([c_{1}])
(resp.
\mathrm{HQD}([\overline{c_{2}}]
) (see Equation (
36
)). By the works of Lempert
[
Lem81
]
and Markovic
[
Mar18
]
,
\mathcal{T}(S)
cannot be biholomorphic to a convex domain in
{\mathbb{C}}^{6\mathrm{g}-6}
, hence
\mathcal{S}^{+}_{[c_{1}]}
and
\mathcal{S}^{-}_{[\overline{c_{2}}]}
are not convex.

The metric formalism for
T\mathrm{QF}(S)
allows to give explicit lower bounds on the radius of balls inside
\mathcal{S}^{+}_{[c_{1}]}
and
\mathcal{S}^{-}_{[\overline{c_{2}}]}
, as shown in the following Theorem (Corollary
6.4
in the text, with finer estimates in Lemma
6.1
and Theorem
6.2
).

Theorem E
.

Let
(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})\in\mathcal{C}(S)\times\mathcal{C}(\overline{S})
, let
g=g(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})
denote the corresponding Bers metric, and let
g_{0}=g(c_{1},\overline{c_{1}})
be the Riemannian hyperbolic metric in the conformal class of
c_{1}
.
Let

(6)

0<R:=\frac{1}{2}\min_{S}\ \left(\Big(1-\left|\frac{\partial_{\overline{z}}w}{\partial_{z}w}\right|\Big)\left|\frac{dA_{g}}{dA_{g_{0}}}\right|\ \right)

where
z
and
\overline{w}
are any local coordinates for
c_{1}
and
\overline{c_{2}}
respectively and
dA_{g}
and
dA_{g_{0}}
are the area forms of
g
and
g_{0}
.
Then,

B_{\infty}\Big(Schw_{+}([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}]),R\Big)\subset\mathcal{S}^{+}_{[c_{1}]}\,

where
B_{\infty}
denotes the norm-infinity ball (with respect to the hyperbolic metric) on
\mathrm{HQD}([c_{1}])
.

For the case
c_{1}={c_{2}}
we get
R=\frac{1}{2}
, which coincides with Kraus’s Theorem in
[
Kra32
]
.

Observe that the definition of
R
in
(
6
)
depends on the choice of
c_{1}
and
\overline{c_{2}}
in their isotopy classes
[c_{1}]\in\mathcal{T}(S)
,
[\overline{c_{2}}]\in\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})
: in fact it would be interesting to determine for each
c_{1}\in\mathcal{C}(S)
what choice of
\overline{c_{2}}
in its isotopy class
[\overline{c_{2}}]
maximizes the estimate
R
(also see Remark
6.6
).

2.
Essential background and notation

Throughout the whole paper,
S
denotes a closed oriented surface of genus
\mathrm{g}\geq 2
. We will use
\overline{S}
to denote the surface with opposite orientation.

Notation

Geometric structures (such as complex structures, Bers metrics, quadratic differentials, and tensors in general) on
S
will often be identified with their
\pi_{1}(S)
-invariant lifts on the universal covering space
\widetilde{S}
.

2.1.
The Teichmüller space

Let
\mathcal{C}(S)
denote the
space of complex structures
on
S
that are compatible with its orientation.

Let Diff
{}_{+}(S)
denote the group of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms of
S
, let Diff
{}_{0}(S)
denote the subgroup of Diff
{}_{+}(S)
consisting of diffeomorphisms of
S
that are isotopic to the identity, and define the
mapping class group
of
S
as
MCG(S):=\nicefrac{{\text{Diff}_{+}(S)}}{{\text{{Diff}}_{0}(S)}}
. Elements in Diff
{}_{+}(S)
will be often identified with their
\pi_{1}(S)
-invariant lifts to the universal cover
\widetilde{S}
.

The
Teichmüller space

\mathcal{T}(S)=\nicefrac{{\mathcal{C}(S)}}{{\text{Diff}_{0}(S)}}
is the space of complex structures on
S
up to isotopy. We denote the equivalence class of
c
with
[c]
. The mapping class group
MCG(S)
acts naturally on
\mathcal{T}(S)
.

We say that a representation
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
is (orientation-preserving and)
Fuchsian
if it is discrete and faithful and its extension to the boundary
\partial\pi_{1}(S)\cong\partial\widetilde{S}\to\partial{\mathbb{H}^{2}}
is an orientation-preserving homeomorphism. The
Fuchsian space

\mathrm{Fuch}(S)
is the space of conjugacy classes of Fuchsian representations by elements in
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
.

The Uniformization Theorem provides a correspondence between
\mathcal{T}(S)
and the
Fuchsian space

\mathrm{Fuch}(S)
, obtained as follows.
H\subset{\mathbb{C}}
denoting the upper half-plane, for all
c\in\mathcal{C}(S)
there exists a biholomorphism
(\widetilde{S},c)\to H
which is unique up to conjugation with elements of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
, and which is equivariant for a Fuchsian
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
: the map sending
c
to the conjugacy class of
\rho
descends to a bijection between
\mathcal{T}(S)
and
\mathrm{Fuch}(S)
called
holonomy map
which assigns by pull-back a smooth structure on
\mathcal{T}(S)
.

By pulling-back the hyperbolic metric of
\mathbb{H}^{2}
in the upper-half plane model through the uniformizing map, we get a bijection between
\mathcal{T}(S)
and the space
Met_{-1}(S)
of hyperbolic metrics on
S
up to isotopy, whose inverse is just the map sending a Riemannian metric to its conformal class.

2.2.
Beltrami coefficients

The content of Sections
2.2
and
2.3
are well-known to experts. There are several surveys on these topics, such as
[
Ota07
]
,
[
Ahl06
]
,
[
FM10
]
,
[
Hub16
]
,
[
Pet19
]
,
[
Mor38
]
, and many more.
We recall here some essential aspects of Beltrami equations and Beltrami differentials leading to the description of the tangent bundle to Teichmüller space.

Fix
c_{0}\in\mathcal{C}(S)
, let
\rho_{0}
be its Fuchsian holonomy, and denote
\Gamma=\rho_{0}(\pi_{1}(S))\subset\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
.

We define the subspace
{L^{\infty}}(\Gamma)\subset L^{\infty}(H,{\mathbb{C}})
given by Lebesgue-integrable functions
\mu\colon H\to\mathbb{C}
such that
\mu(\gamma(z))=\frac{\overline{\gamma^{\prime}(z)}}{\gamma^{\prime}(z)}\mu(z)
for all
\gamma\in\Gamma
: in other words, the section
\mu\frac{d\overline{z}}{dz}
of
\mathbb{C}T^{*}S\otimes\mathbb{C}T^{**}S
(or equivalently
\mu\ {d\overline{z}}\otimes\partial_{z}
in the identification
\mathbb{C}T^{**}S\cong\mathbb{C}TS
) is
\Gamma
-invariant on
\widetilde{S}
. Also denote
L^{\infty}_{1}(\Gamma)
as the intersection of
{L^{\infty}}(\Gamma)
with the unit ball of
L^{\infty}(H,{\mathbb{C}})
.
Observe that
L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
can be seen as the tangent space in zero of
L^{\infty}_{1}(\Gamma)
: we call
\Gamma
-
Beltrami coefficients
(or just Beltrami coefficients) elements in
L^{\infty}_{1}(\Gamma)
and denote them with
\mu
, while we call
\Gamma
-
Beltrami differentials
(or just Beltrami differentials) elements in
L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
and denote them with
\beta=\mu\frac{d\overline{z}}{dz}
.

By the classic theory of Beltrami equations (e.g. see
[
Mor38
]
,
[
Ota07
]
), for all
\mu
such that
\|\mu\|_{\infty}<1
, there exists a unique quasi-conformal map
f^{\mu}\colon{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
such that:

•

\frac{\partial f^{\mu}}{\partial\overline{z}}=\mu\frac{\partial f^{\mu}}{\partial z}
almost everywhere on
H
,

•

the restriction of
f^{\mu}
to
(-H)
is holomorphic,

•

f^{\mu}(0)=0
and
f^{\mu}(1)=1

Moreover,
f^{\mu}
is equivariant for a discrete and faithful representation
\rho\circ\rho_{0}^{-1}\colon\Gamma\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
, with
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
acting freely and properly discontinuously on
f^{\mu}(H)
. Hence
f^{\mu}
induces a quasi-conformal map between the Riemann surfaces
f^{\mu}\colon\nicefrac{{H}}{{\Gamma}}\to\nicefrac{{f^{\mu}(H)}}{{(\rho\circ\rho_{0}^{-1})(\Gamma)}}
.

Define
\mathrm{Belt}_{1}(\rho_{0})
as the quotient of
L^{\infty}_{1}(\Gamma)
defined by the relation
\mu\sim\mu^{\prime}
equivalently if (with reference to the construction above):

•

f^{\mu}
and
f^{\mu^{\prime}}
are equivariant for the same representation
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
;

•

there exists an element
\varphi\in
Diff
{}_{0}(S)
such that
f^{\mu^{\prime}}=f^{\mu}\circ\varphi
;

•

the pull-back complex structures on
S
defined by
f^{\mu}
and
f^{\mu^{\prime}}
are isotopic.

The last identification provides a map
\mathrm{Belt}_{1}(\rho_{0})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{T}(S)
which is in fact a diffeomorphism.

Under this identification, the tangent space
T_{[c_{0}]}\mathcal{T}(S)
can be seen as the quotient of
L^{\infty}(\Gamma)
with the kernel of the differential map of the projection
L^{\infty}_{1}(\Gamma)\to\mathrm{Belt}_{1}(\rho_{0})
, as we describe in the next section.

2.3.
Tangent and cotangent bundle to Teichmüller space

Let
c\in\mathcal{C}(S)
. A
holomorphic quadratic differential
on
(S,c)
is a holomorphic section of the square of the holomorphic cotangent bundle. We will denote the complex vector space of holomorphic quadratic differentials for
c
with
\mathrm{HQD}(c)
. One can see (the lift to the universal cover of) a holomorphic quadratic differential on
S
as a
\pi_{1}(S)
-invariant tensor
\varphi dz^{2}
on
\widetilde{S}
, where
z
is a global holomorphic coordinate for (the lift of)
c
on
\widetilde{S}
, and
\varphi\colon\widetilde{S}\to\mathbb{C}
is
c
-holomorphic.
We also denote
\mathrm{HQD}([c])={\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\bigcup_{c\in[c]}\mathrm{HQD}(c)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\text{Diff}_{0}(S)}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\bigcup_{c\in[c]}\mathrm{HQD}(c)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\text{Diff}_{0}(S)}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{\bigcup_{c\in[c]}\mathrm{HQD}(c)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{\text{Diff}_{0}(S)}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\bigcup_{c\in[c]}\mathrm{HQD}(c)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\text{Diff}_{0}(S)}$}}}
, which is naturally a complex vector space with the natural identification
\mathrm{HQD}(c)\cong\mathrm{HQD}([c])
. By Riemann-Roch Theorem,
\mathrm{HQD}([c])
has complex dimension
3\mathrm{g}-3
.

Recall the notation of the previous paragraph. The identification
\mathrm{Belt}_{1}(\rho_{0})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{T}(S)
defined above actually allows to give a description of the tangent and cotangent space to
\mathcal{T}(S)
in
[c_{0}]
as follows.

Let
\rho_{0}
be the Fuchsian representation corresponding to
c_{0}\in\mathcal{C}(S)
, and regard
{L^{\infty}}(\rho_{0}(\pi_{1}(S)))
as the tangent space to
L^{\infty}_{1}(\rho_{0}(\pi_{1}(S)))
in zero.

For all
q=\varphi dz^{2}\in\mathrm{HQD}(c_{0})
,
\beta=\mu\frac{d\overline{z}}{dz}\in{L^{\infty}}(\rho_{0}(\pi_{1}(S)))
, the 2-form
\varphi\mu dz\wedge d\overline{z}
on
\widetilde{S}
is
\pi_{1}(S)
-invariant, so one can consider the pairing defined by

(7)

q(\beta):=\int_{S}\varphi\cdot\mu\ \frac{i}{2}dz\wedge d\overline{z}\ .

The vector subspace

\mathcal{N}=\left\{\beta\in{L^{\infty}}(\rho_{0}(\pi_{1}(S)))\ |\ q(\beta)=0\text{ for all }q\in\mathrm{HQD}(c_{0})\right\}.

is precisely the kernel of the differential of the quotient map
{L^{\infty}_{1}}(\rho_{0}(\pi_{1}(S)))\to\mathrm{Belt}_{1}(\rho_{0})
in zero.

As a result, the tangent space in
[c_{0}]
to Teichmüller space, canonically identified with
T_{[0]}\mathrm{Belt}_{1}(\rho_{0})
, can be seen as the finite vector space

T_{[c_{0}]}\mathcal{T}(S)\cong\mathrm{Belt}([c_{0}]):={\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{{L^{\infty}}(\rho_{0}(\pi_{1}(S)))}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{{L^{\infty}}(\rho_{0}(\pi_{1}(S)))}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{N}}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{{L^{\infty}}(\rho_{0}(\pi_{1}(S)))}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{N}}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{{L^{\infty}}(\rho_{0}(\pi_{1}(S)))}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{N}}$}}}\ .

Finally, denote with
h_{0}
the hyperbolic metric in the conformal class of
c_{0}
. The map

\displaystyle\mathrm{HQD}(\overline{c_{0}})

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{Belt}([c_{0}])

\displaystyle\overline{q}

\displaystyle\mapsto\left[\frac{\overline{q}}{h_{0}}\right]=\left[\frac{\overline{\varphi}d\overline{z}}{\alpha dz}\right]

where
h_{0}=\alpha dzd\overline{z}
, is a linear isomorphism
[
Ota07
]
, and it gives a nice set of representatives for the elements of
\mathrm{Belt}([c_{0}])
. The Beltrami differentials of the form
\frac{\overline{q}}{h_{0}}
as above are called
harmonic Beltrami differentials
.

Since
q(\frac{\overline{q}}{h_{0}})>0
for all
q\in\mathrm{HQD}(c_{0})
, we get that the pairing on
\mathrm{HQD}([c_{0}])\times\mathrm{Belt}([c_{0}])
given by
q([\beta]):=q(\beta)
as in Equation (
7
) is well-posed and non-degenerate, thus we get the identification
T^{*}_{[c_{0}]}\mathcal{T}(S)\cong\mathrm{HQD}([c_{0}])
.

Both
\mathrm{HQD}([c_{0}])
and
\mathrm{Belt}([c_{0}])
are naturally complex vector spaces, providing a quasi-complex structures on
T\mathcal{T}(S)
and consistently on
T^{*}\mathcal{T}(S)
. As we will see in Section
2.5
, this quasi-complex structure is integrable, and
\mathcal{T}(S)
has a natural structure of complex manifold.

We will denote with
\textbf{HQD}(S)
the space of quadratic differentials which are holomorphic with respect to some complex structure on
S
compatible with the orientation (this is an infinite-dimensional Banach manifold) and with
\mathrm{HQD}(S)={\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\textbf{HQD}(S)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\text{Diff}_{0}(S)}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\textbf{HQD}(S)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\text{Diff}_{0}(S)}$}}{\raisebox{2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{\textbf{HQD}(S)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{\text{Diff}_{0}(S)}$}}{\raisebox{1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\textbf{HQD}(S)}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\text{Diff}_{0}(S)}$}}}
the vector bundle of rank
6\mathrm{g}-6
on
\mathcal{T}(S)
which is isomorphic to its cotangent bundle.

2.4.
Complex projective structures

The complex Lie group
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
acts by isometries on the
3
-dimensional hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
and on its visual boundary at infinity
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
by Möbius maps.

A
(complex) projective structure
on
S
is a
(\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}}),\mathbb{CP}^{1})
-structure, namely a maximal atlas of charts to open subsets of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
with changes of coordinates being restrictions of Möbius maps. By the classic theory of
(G,X)
-structures, this is equivalent to giving a
developing map

dev\colon\widetilde{S}\to\mathbb{CP}^{1}
that is equivariant for some representation
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
, called the
holonomy
of the projective structure. A nice survey on complex projective structures is
[
Dum09
]
.

The space of projective structures up to isotopy, that we will denote with
\mathcal{P}(S)
, is a complex manifold, and the forgetful map
\mathcal{P}(S)\to\mathcal{T}(S)
that maps a projective structure to the complex structure induced by the atlas is smooth (in fact holomorphic as we see in the next section).

Let
\mathcal{X}(\pi_{1}(S),\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}}))
denote the
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
-
character variety
, which is the GIT quotient (or, from the topological viewpoint, the greatest Hausdorff quotient) of the space of conjugacy classes of representations in
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
, namely
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\{\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})\}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\{\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})\}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\scriptstyle{\{\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})\}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\{\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})\}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})}$}}}
. The character variety
\mathcal{X}(\pi_{1}(S),\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}}))
is naturally a complex algebraic variety, and the holonomy map

(8)

\begin{split}hol\colon\mathcal{P}(S)&\to\mathcal{X}(\pi_{1}(S),\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}}))\\
[(dev,\rho)]&\mapsto[\rho]\end{split}

is a local biholomorphism.

A projective structure on
S
can be encoded in a tensor on
S
by taking the Schwarzian derivative of the developing map, let us define it. Given an open subset
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{C}}
and a holomorphic map
F\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we denote its
Schwarzian derivative
as the holomorphic quadratic differential

Schw(f):=\left(\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(z)}{f^{\prime}(z)}\right)^{2}\right)dz^{2}\ .

The key properties of the Schwarzian derivative are that
Schw(f)=0
if and only if
f
is Möbius, and the chain rule
Schw(f\circ g)=g^{*}(Schw(f))+Schw(g)
. Given a projective structure
(f,\rho)
on
S
, denoting with
c
its complex structure, the properties of the Schwarzian derivative allow to say that
Schw(f)
is a
\pi_{1}(S)
-invariant holomorphic quadratic differential on (
\widetilde{S}
,c), hence an element of
\mathrm{HQD}(c)
.
Denoting with
\mathcal{P}([c])
the isotopy classes of projective structures with complex structure in
[c]
, the map

(9)

\begin{split}\mathcal{P}([c])&\to\mathrm{HQD}([c])\\
[(f,\rho)]&\mapsto[Schw(f)]\end{split}\

is a biholomorphism.

2.5.
Quasi-Fuchsian representations and Bers Simultaneous Uniformization

Let
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
be a representation. We define its
limit set

\Lambda_{\rho}
as the topological boundary of the orbit of any point in
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
.

We say that a discrete and faithful representation
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
is
quasi-Fuchsian
if its limit set is a Jordan curve on
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. The
quasi-Fuchsian space
is the space of conjugacy classes of quasi-Fuchsian representations, namely

\mathrm{QF}(S)={\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\{\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})\text{ quasi-Fuchsian representation}\}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\{\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})\text{ quasi-Fuchsian representation}\}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.75pt}{$\textstyle{\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\scriptstyle{\{\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})\text{ quasi-Fuchsian representation}\}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.625pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\{\rho\colon\pi_{1}(S)\to\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})\text{ quasi-Fuchsian representation}\}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.875pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})}$}}}

which is an open smooth subset of the character variety
\mathcal{X}(\pi_{1}(S),\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}}))
, hence a smooth complex manifold.

Let
\rho
be a quasi-Fuchsian representation. The orientation on
S
induces an orientation on
\partial\pi_{1}(S)
and hence on
\Lambda_{\rho}
, which in turn gives a natural orientation to the connected components of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}\setminus\Lambda_{\rho}
: one of the two discs, that we name
\Omega_{\rho}^{+}
, is therefore identified with the complex disk
\mathbb{D}
, while the other one, that we name
\Omega_{\rho}^{-}
, is identified with
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, the complex disk with opposite orientation. The representation
\rho
defines a free and properly discontinuous action of
\pi_{1}(S)
on both
\Omega_{\rho}^{+}
and
\Omega_{\rho}^{-}
, defining two Riemann surfaces on
S
and
\overline{S}
respectively.

Bers Simultaneous Uniformization Theorem, as stated in Theorem
1.1
, allows to build a diffeomorphism

(10)

\mathfrak{B}\colon\mathcal{T}(S)\times\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{QF}(S),

(that in the rest of the paper will be often seen as an identification) associating to every pair
([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])
a unique
[\rho]\in\mathrm{QF}(S)
such that there exists a (essentially unique) projective structure
[(f_{1},\rho)]\in\mathcal{P}(S)
with
f_{1}(\widetilde{S})=\Omega_{\rho}^{+}
and induced complex structure
[c_{1}]
, and a (essentially unique) projective structure
[(\overline{f_{2}},\rho)]\in\mathcal{P}(\overline{S})
with
\overline{f_{2}}(\widetilde{S})=\Omega_{\rho}^{-}
and induced complex structure
[\overline{c_{2}}]
.
The diffeomorphism
\mathfrak{B}
in Equation (
10
) is in fact a biholomorphism in the following sense. The map
\mathfrak{B}
maps each
\mathcal{T}(S)\times\{[\overline{c_{2}}]\}
(resp. each
\{[c_{1}]\}\times\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})
) to a complex submanifold. The complex structure on
\mathcal{T}(S)
(resp.
\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})
) defined by pull-back by
\mathfrak{B}
is independent from the choice of
[\overline{c_{2}}]\in\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})
(resp.
[c_{1}]\in\mathcal{T}(S)
), and it is consistent with the almost complex structure on
\mathcal{T}(S)
mentioned in the end of Section
2.3
.

We call
Bers slices
both the subsets of the form

\displaystyle\mathrm{QF}([c_{1}],\bullet):=\{[c_{1}]\}\times\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})\ ,

\displaystyle\mathrm{QF}(\bullet,[\overline{c_{2}}]):=\mathcal{T}(S)\times\{[\overline{c_{2}}]\}\ ,

and their images through
\mathfrak{B}
.

The projective structures
[(f_{1},\rho)]
and
[(\overline{f_{2}},\rho)]
defined from
\mathfrak{B}^{-1}([\rho])
are called
Bers projective structures
.

Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}(S)
is biholomorphic to an open subset of
\mathbb{C}^{3\mathrm{g}-3}
. With respect to this complex structure, the projections
\mathrm{Belt(S)}\to\mathcal{T}(S)
(with fibers
\mathrm{Belt([c])}
) and
\mathrm{HQD}(S)\to\mathcal{T}(S)
have a natural structure of holomorphic vector bundles and are isomorphic to the holomorphic tangent and cotangent bundles respectively. The forgetful map
\mathcal{P}(S)\to\mathcal{T}(S)
is holomorphic as well, and the action of the mapping class group
MCG(S)
on
\mathcal{T}(S)
is by biholomorphisms.

2.6.
The Weil-Petersson metric

The description through harmonic Beltrami differentials allows to define a Riemannian metric on Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}(S)
, called the
Weil-Petersson metric
, as follows. Let
z
be a global holomorphic coordinate for
c
on
\widetilde{S}
,
q_{1}=\varphi_{1}dz^{2}
and
q_{2}=\varphi_{2}dz^{2}
denote elements in
\mathrm{HQD}(c)
and
h_{0}=\alpha_{0}dzd\overline{z}
be the hyperbolic metric in the conformal class
c
. Then, for all
\left[\frac{\overline{q_{1}}}{h_{0}}\right],\left[\frac{\overline{q_{2}}}{h_{0}}\right]\in\mathrm{Belt}([c])
the Weil-Petersson Riemannian metric is defined as

\langle{\left[\frac{\overline{q_{1}}}{h_{0}}\right],\left[\frac{\overline{q_{2}}}{h_{0}}\right]}\rangle_{WP}:=q_{1}\left(\frac{\overline{q_{2}}}{h_{0}}\right)=Re\left(\int_{S}\frac{\varphi_{1}\cdot\overline{\varphi_{2}}}{\alpha_{0}}\frac{i}{2}dz\wedge d\overline{z}\right)\ .

The Weil-Petersson metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{WP}
and the complex structure determine on
\mathcal{T}(S)
a
MCG(S)
-invariant Kähler manifold structure, together with the symplectic form

\omega\left(\left[\frac{\overline{q_{1}}}{h_{0}}\right],\left[\frac{\overline{q_{2}}}{h_{0}}\right]\right):=\langle\left[i\frac{\overline{q_{1}}}{h_{0}}\right],\left[\frac{\overline{q_{2}}}{h_{0}}\right]\rangle_{WP}=Im\left(\int_{S}\frac{\varphi_{1}\cdot\overline{\varphi_{2}}}{\rho_{0}}\frac{i}{2}dz\wedge d\overline{z}\right)\ .

In the identification of
\mathcal{T}(S)
with
\mathrm{Fuch}(S)
, the symplectic form
\omega
coincides up to a multiplicative factor 8 with the
Goldman symplectic form

\omega_{G}=8\omega
, a natural symplectic form arising in the character variety of a wide class of real and complex Lie groups
G
(see
[
Gol84
]
). When
G
is a complex Lie group, the Goldman symplectic form is
{\mathbb{C}}
-bilinear and holomorphic with respect to the natural complex structure on the character variety. This class of Lie groups includes
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
, and the restriction of its Goldman symplectic form to
\mathrm{Fuch}(S)
coincides with the Goldman symplectic form for
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{R}})
.

2.7.
Holomorphic Riemannian metrics

We now recall some essential aspects of holomorphic Riemannian metrics, you can find a more detailed treatment in
[
BE22
]
.

Let
\mathbb{M}
be a complex manifold.
Holomorphic Riemannian metrics
on complex manifolds can be seen as a natural
{\mathbb{C}}
-bilinear analog of pseudo-Riemannian metrics: in fact, a holomorphic Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{M}
is a nowhere-degenerate holomorphic section of the space of symmetric
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear forms on the holomorphic tangent bundle.

A trivial example is
\mathbb{C}^{N}
with the holomorphic Riemannian metric on
T\mathbb{C}^{N}\cong\mathbb{C}^{N}\times\mathbb{C}^{N}
defined by

\langle\underline{v},\underline{w}\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{N}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}v_{k}w_{k}.

To see a couple of less trivial examples, observe that the metric above descends to the holomorphic Riemannian metric on the compact manifold
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.5714pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.5714pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathbb{Z}^{n}+i\mathbb{Z}^{n}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathbb{C}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathbb{Z}^{n}+i\mathbb{Z}^{n}}$}}{\raisebox{2.58528pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathbb{C}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.58528pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathbb{Z}^{n}+i\mathbb{Z}^{n}}$}}{\raisebox{1.7986pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{C}^{n}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.7986pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{Z}^{n}+i\mathbb{Z}^{n}}$}}}
, or consider the complex Killing form on a complex semisimple Lie group (see for instance
[
BE22
]
for
\mathrm{SL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
). Another example that we will mention in the paper is given by the space of oriented geodesics of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
, which admits a unique
\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
-invariant holomorphic Riemannian metric of constant curvature
-1
(see Section
3.1
and
[
BE22
]
).

The real and the imaginary part of a holomorphic Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{M}=\mathbb{M}^{n}
are pseudo-Riemannian metrics of signature
(n,n)
. To see this, observe that if
(e_{1},\dots e_{n})
is an orthonormal basis for a holomorphic Riemannian metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
T_{p}\mathbb{M}
, then the bases
(e_{1},\dots,e_{n},ie_{1},\dots,ie_{n})
and
(\sqrt{i}e_{1},\dots,\sqrt{i}e_{n},i\sqrt{i}e_{1},\dots,i\sqrt{i}e_{n})
are orthonormal for
Re\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
Im\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
respectively.

Holomorphic Riemannian metrics come with a notion of
Levi-Civita connection
: namely there exists a unique affine connection,

\displaystyle D\colon\Gamma(T\mathbb{M})

\displaystyle\to\Gamma(End_{\mathbb{R}}(T\mathbb{M}))

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\mapsto DX

being torsion free and compatible with the metric, namely

. The affine connection

coincides with the Levi-Civita connection of both the real and the imaginary part of the metric.

The metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and its Levi-Civita connection determine a
Riemann curvature tensor

\mathrm{R}(X,Y,Z,W)=\langle D_{X}D_{Y}Z-D_{Y}D_{X}Z-D_{[X,Y]}Z,W\rangle
which is
{\mathbb{C}}
-multilinear.

Finally, let

be a complex vector space with
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}V=2
and such that the restriction of

to

is non-degenerate, namely there exist two
{\mathbb{C}}
-linearly independent vectors

such that
\langle X,Y\rangle\neq 0
. Then, we can define the
sectional curvature

\mathrm{K}(V)
as
\mathrm{K}(V):=\frac{R(X,Y,Y,X)}{\langle X,X\rangle\langle Y,Y\rangle-\langle X,Y\rangle^{2}}
, whose definition is independent from the choice of the linearly independent vectors
X,Y
in
V
.

Similarly as for pseudo-Riemannian metrics, for all

and

there exists a unique

(
[
BE22
]
).

3.
Bers metrics

3.1.
Hyperbolic complex metrics and Bers Theorem

As we already mentioned, throughout the paper, we will assume that
S
is a closed connected oriented surface of genus greater than or equal to
2
. We denote with
\overline{S}
the surface with opposite orientation.

We need to recall some essential technical tools in order to state Theorem
3.3
, which will be used in some of the proofs of this paper. Most of the content of this subsection follows from
[
BE22
]
, with a few changes in the notation.

A
complex metric
on
S
is a smooth section
g
of
Sym_{2}(TS)+iSym_{2}(TS)
such that
g
is non-degenerate, namely for all
p\in S
the
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear extension of
g_{p}
to the complexified tangent space
g_{p}\colon\mathbb{C}T_{p}S\times\mathbb{C}T_{p}S\to\mathbb{C}
is a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form. This notion includes Riemannian metrics on
S
.
We equip the set of complex metrics on
S
with the
C^{\infty}
topology.

With a simple linear algebra argument, one has that for each
p\in S
the set
\{X\in\mathbb{C}T_{p}S\ |\ g_{p}(X,X)=0\}
is the union of two complex lines in
\mathbb{C}T_{p}S
, corresponding to two points of

\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}T_{p}S)
. We will call these two points the
isotropic
directions of
g
, and we call isotropic vectors the vectors
X\in{\mathbb{C}}TS
such that
g(X,X)=0
.

Observe that, for each
p\in S
,
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}T_{p}S)\setminus\mathbb{P}(T_{p}S)
is homeomorphic to a 2-sphere from which we remove an equator, so it has two connected components homeomorphic to disks.

A
positive complex metric
is a complex metric
g
such that:

•

there are no non-zero isotropic vectors on
TS
, namely
g(v,v)=0
with
v\in TS
if and only if
v=0
;

•

the two isotropic directions of
g
in
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}T_{p}S)
are points in
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}T_{p}S)\setminus\mathbb{P}(T_{p}S)
that lie in different connected components.

One can see that a Riemannian metric
g
is a positive complex metric: denoting with
z
a complex coordinate around
p\in S
for
g
, its isotropic directions are
Span_{\mathbb{C}}(\partial_{z})
and
Span_{\mathbb{C}}(\partial_{\overline{z}})
, which lie in different connected components of
\mathbb{P}(\mathbb{C}T_{p}S)\setminus\mathbb{P}(T_{p}S)
.

Every complex metric
g
has a unique
Levi-Civita connection
, namely a torsion free affine connection
\nabla\colon\Gamma(\mathbb{C}TS)\to\Gamma(End(\mathbb{C}TS))
such that

(11)

d_{X}(g(Y,Z))=g(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)+g(Y,\nabla_{X}Z)

for all
X,Y,Z\in\Gamma(\mathbb{C}TS)
. This induces the definition of a
\mathbb{C}
-multilinear curvature tensor
\mathrm{R}_{g}
and a curvature
\mathrm{K}_{g}\colon S\to\mathbb{C}
.

Complex metrics of constant curvature
\mathrm{K}_{g}=-1
have a particularly interesting geometric meaning in terms of immersions inside
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{CP}^{1}\times\mathbb{CP}^{1}\setminus\Delta
, which can be interpreted as the space of (maximal, oriented, unparametrized) geodesics of
\mathbb{H}^{3}
.

The complex manifold
\mathbb{G}
can be equipped with a holomorphic Riemannian metric (see Section
2.7
) that can be defined as follows: let
(U,z)
be an affine chart for
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, then in the chart
(U\times U\setminus\Delta,z\times z=(z_{1},z_{2}))
the metric can be written as

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathbb{G}}=-\frac{4}{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}}dz_{1}\cdot dz_{2}\ .

This description is independent from the affine chart
(U,z)
. This holomorphic Riemannian metric is invariant under the diagonal action of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
on
\mathbb{G}
and has constant sectional curvature
-1
. The isometry group
\mathrm{Isom}(\mathbb{G})
of
\mathbb{G}
is generated by the diagonal action of
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
and by the diagonal swap
(z_{1},z_{2})\mapsto(z_{2},z_{1})

It is simple to construct the continuous inverse of

4.2.
Bers metrics and Epstein metrics at infinity

In order to prove Theorem
4.1

\mathbb{H}^{3}
and their data at infinity: in fact, this will help us in the proof of Theorem

to show that the set of Bers metrics

Epstein (

]

[

gave a notion of second fundamental forms at infinity, with the pair of first and second fundamental forms at infinity determining uniquely the immersion in
\mathbb{H}^{3}

Assume as usual that
S
is closed, oriented, with genus greater than or equal to 2.
Let
\sigma\colon\widetilde{S}\to\mathbb{H}^{3}

(\pi_{1}(S),\mathrm{PSL}(2,{\mathbb{C}}))

be a normal vector field along

compatible with the orientation on

\mathrm{I}
of
\sigma
is the pull-back metric, the

is the
\mathrm{I}
-self-adjoint endomorphism

, while the

\mathrm{I}\hskip-1.13791pt\mathrm{I}
is the symmetric bilinear form on

\mathrm{I}\hskip-1.13791pt\mathrm{I}=\mathrm{I}(B\cdot,\cdot)

\mathrm{I}
,

and
\mathrm{I}\hskip-1.13791pt\mathrm{I}

\pi_{1}(S)

\sigma

(\mathrm{I},\mathrm{I}\hskip-1.13791pt\mathrm{I})
(or equivalently by
(\mathrm{I},B)

∎

5.3.
Uniqueness of holomorphic extensions

We prove that there is a unique extension of the Weil-Petersson metric to the quasi-Fuchsian space. This is a simple consequence of the complex-analiticity of the metric, but we show it explicitly.

Let

(32)

\Lambda\colon\mathrm{QF}(S)\to\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{6\mathrm{g}-6}

be a biholomorphism such that the image of the Fuchsian locus is
\Omega\cap\mathbb{R}^{6\mathrm{g}-6}
. There are several ways to choose
\Lambda
: for instance, fixing a geodesic lamination
\lambda
for
S
,
QF(S)
can be parametrized with an open subset of the vector space of
{\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathbb{C}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\displaystyle{2\pi i\mathbb{Z}}$}}{\raisebox{3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{\mathbb{C}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.41666pt}{$\textstyle{2\pi i\mathbb{Z}}$}}{\raisebox{2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathbb{C}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.39166pt}{$\scriptstyle{2\pi i\mathbb{Z}}$}}{\raisebox{1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathbb{C}}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.6994pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{2\pi i\mathbb{Z}}$}}}
-valued cocycles along

through the so-called shear-bend coordinates, with

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
be an open connected subset intersecting
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
If

is a holomorphic function such that

, then
f\equiv 0
.

Proof.

This is a simple consequence of the fact that

is complex-analytic.
For all
x^{0}\in\Omega\cap\mathbb{R}^{N}
,

can be locally written as
f(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_{k,n}(z_{k}-x^{0}_{k})^{n}
, and its restriction to
\mathbb{R}
being analytic implies that all the coefficients

are zero. So
f\equiv 0
in a neighborhood of
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
, implies that
f\equiv 0
on
\Omega
because
f
is holomorphic and
\Omega
is connected.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

A
(p,q)
-tensor on the Fuchsian locus
\mathrm{Fuch}(S)

{\mathbb{C}}
-bilinear holomorphic
(p,q)
-tensor on
\mathrm{QF}(S)

Proof.

Denote with

defined by

. This metric induces a nondegenerate holomorphic symmetric bilinear form (that we will still denote with
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{{\mathbb{C}}^{n}}

Assume there exist two holomorphic extensions of a
(p,q)
-tensor on
\mathrm{Fuch}(S)
. By pushing them forward through
\Lambda
, we get two holomorphic
(p,q)
-tensors on

Corollary 5.7
.

There exists a unique holomorphic Riemannian metric on
\mathrm{QF}(S)
which extends the Weil-Petersson metric on the Fuchsian locus. Hence
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
coincides with the holomorphic Riemannian metric defined in

.

Corollary 5.8
.

The holomorphic Riemannian metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
\mathrm{QF}(S)
is invariant under the diagonal action of the mapping class group on
\mathrm{QF}(S)\cong\mathcal{T}(S)\times\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})

\displaystyle 4\omega_{G}(q,q^{\prime})=

\displaystyle\frac{|\rho|}{2}\big(|\partial_{z}w|-|\partial_{\overline{z}}w|\big)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|g(\partial_{z},\partial_{\overline{z}})|-\frac{1}{2}|g(\partial_{z},\partial_{z})|=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(1-\left|\frac{\partial_{\overline{z}}w}{\partial_{z}w}\right|\Big)|g(\partial_{z},\partial_{\overline{z}})|\ .

Proof of Theorem
6.2
.

We prove the part of the statement regarding the image of
Schw_{+}
.

Denote
q_{1}=\varphi dz^{2}\in\mathrm{HQD}(c_{1})
. From Lemma
6.1
, we know that
g-2q_{1}
is Bers if and only if
|\rho\partial_{z}\overline{w}-2\varphi|<|\rho\partial_{\overline{z}}\overline{w}|
.

If
|\varphi|<\frac{|\rho|}{2}\big(|\partial_{z}w|-|\partial_{z}\overline{w}|\big)
, then

|\rho\partial_{z}\overline{w}-2\varphi|\leq|\rho\partial_{z}\overline{w}|+2|\varphi|<|\rho\partial_{z}\overline{w}|+|\rho\partial_{z}w|-|\rho\partial_{z}\overline{w}|=|\rho\partial_{z}w|\ ,

so
g-2q_{1}
is a Bers metric.

By Theorem
4.1

[\mathbf{Schw}_{+}(g-2q_{1})]=[\mathbf{Schw}_{+}(g)+q_{1}]=Schw_{+}([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])+[q_{1}]\

is therefore an element of
\mathcal{S}^{+}_{[c_{1}]}
.

We can now prove the part of the statement regarding
Schw_{-}
by conjugating the metrics. Let
\overline{q_{2}}=\overline{\psi}d\overline{w}^{2}
. By Remark
3.5
,
\overline{g}=g(c_{2},\overline{c_{1}})=\overline{\rho}dwd\overline{z}
, and
\overline{g}+q_{2}
is a complex metric of constant curvature
-1
. As in the proof of the first part of the theorem, if

|\overline{g}(\partial_{\overline{w}},\partial_{\overline{w}})|=|\overline{\psi}|=|\psi|=|g(\partial_{w},\partial_{w})|<\frac{|\overline{\rho}|}{2}(|\partial_{w}z|-|\partial_{w}\overline{z}|)=\frac{|\rho|}{2}(|\partial_{w}z|-|\partial_{w}\overline{z}|)\ ,

then
\overline{g}-2{q_{2}}
is a Bers metric. By Remark
3.5
and by Theorem
4.1
,
g-2\overline{q_{2}}
is a Bers metric and
[\mathbf{Schw}_{-}(g-2\overline{q_{2}})]=[\mathbf{Schw}_{-}(g)+\overline{q_{2}}]=Schw([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])+[\overline{q_{2}}]
lies in
\mathcal{S}^{-}_{[\overline{c_{2}}]}
.
∎

A key remark is that the left-hand sides in the Inequalities (
34
)-(
35
) are strictly positive terms as a consequence of the fact that
z
and
w
induce the same orientation. This actually allows us to estimate the radius of a ball with center in
Schw_{+}([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])\in\mathrm{HQD}([c_{1}])
which is contained in
\mathcal{S}^{+}_{[c_{1}]}
. Let us put this precise.

The space
\mathrm{HQD}(c_{1})
has a natural
L^{\infty}
-norm defined as follows: let
g_{0}=g(c_{1},\overline{c_{1}})
be the hyperbolic metric on
S
corresponding to
g
, then for
q_{1}\in\mathrm{HQD}(c_{1})
we define

(36)

\|q_{1}\|_{\infty}:=\frac{1}{2}\big\|\|Re(q_{1})\|_{g_{0}}\big\|_{\infty}=\left\|\frac{\varphi}{\rho_{0}}\right\|_{\infty}=\left\|\frac{q_{1}(\partial_{z},\partial_{z})}{2g_{0}(\partial_{z},\partial_{\overline{z}})}\right\|_{\infty}

where
g_{0}=\rho_{0}dzd\overline{z}
and
q_{1}=\varphi dzd\overline{z}
. This norm on
\mathrm{HQD}(c_{1})
clearly determines a well-posed norm on
\mathrm{HQD}([c_{1}])
which is independent from the representative of the isotopy class.

Let
(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})\in\mathcal{C}(S)\times\mathcal{C}(\overline{S})
.
Let
g=g(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})
be the corresponding Bers metric, and let
g_{0}=g(c_{1},\overline{c_{1}})
be the Riemannian hyperbolic metric corresponding to
c_{1}
.

Corollary 6.4
.

Let
(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})\in\mathcal{C}(S)\times\mathcal{C}(\overline{S})
,
g=g(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})
, let
g_{0}=g(c_{1},\overline{c_{1}})
be the hyperbolic metric uniformizing
c_{1}
, and denote with
dA_{g}
and
dA_{g_{0}}
respectively their area forms.

Let

(37)

R:=\frac{1}{2}\min_{S}\ \left(\Big(1-\left|\frac{\partial_{\overline{z}}w}{\partial_{z}w}\right|\Big)\left|\frac{dA_{g}}{dA_{g_{0}}}\right|\ \right)

where
z
and
\overline{w}
are any local coordinates for
c_{1}
and
\overline{c_{2}}
respectively.

Then,

B_{\infty}\Big(Schw_{+}([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}]),R\Big)\subset\mathcal{S}^{+}_{[c_{1}]}\,

where
B_{\infty}
denotes the ball with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
on
\mathrm{HQD}([c_{1}])
.

Proof.

Let
q_{1}=\varphi dz^{2}\in\mathrm{HQD}(c_{1})
with
\|q_{1}\|_{\infty}<R
.
Observe that, denoting
g=\rho dzd\overline{w}
, one has

(38)

dA_{g}=\frac{i}{2}\rho dz\wedge d\overline{w}=\frac{i}{2}\rho\partial_{\overline{z}}\overline{w}dz\wedge d\overline{z}=ig(\partial_{z},\partial_{\overline{z}})dz\wedge d\overline{z}\ ,

and similarly
dA_{g_{0}}=ig_{0}(\partial_{z},\partial_{\overline{z}})dz\wedge d\overline{z}
.
Hence
\frac{dA_{g_{0}}}{dA_{g}}=\frac{g(\partial_{z},\partial_{\overline{z}})}{g_{0}(\partial_{z},\partial_{\overline{z}})}
, and in every point
p\in S

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\varphi}{2g_{0}(\partial_{z},\partial_{\overline{z}})}\right|_{|p}\leq\|q_{1}\|_{\infty}<

\displaystyle\min_{S}\ \left(\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-\left|\frac{\partial_{\overline{z}}w}{\partial_{z}w}\right|\Big)\left|\frac{g(\partial_{z},\partial_{\overline{z}})}{g_{0}(\partial_{z},\partial_{\overline{z}})}\right|\ \right)\leq

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\ \frac{1}{2}\Big(1-\left|\frac{\partial_{\overline{z}}w}{\partial_{z}w}\right|\Big)\left|\frac{g(\partial_{z},\partial_{\overline{z}})}{g_{0}(\partial_{z},\partial_{\overline{z}})}\right|_{|p}\

hence

|\varphi|<\Big(1-\left|\frac{\partial_{\overline{z}}w}{\partial_{z}w}\right|\Big)|g(\partial_{z},\partial_{\overline{z}})|\

everywhere on
S
.

By Theorem
6.2
and Remark

,
Schw_{+}([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])+[q_{1}]\in\mathcal{S}^{+}_{[c_{1}]}
.
∎

7.
Revisiting classic results with the metric formalism

7.1.
The Schwarzian map is affine

Proposition 7.1
.

The Schwarzian maps

Schw_{+}\colon\mathrm{QF}([c_{1}],\bullet)\to\mathrm{HQD}([c_{1}])\qquad\quad\text{and}\qquad\quad Schw_{-}\colon\mathrm{QF}(\bullet,[\overline{c_{2}}])\to\mathrm{HQD}([\overline{c_{2}}])

are affine diffeomorphisms onto their images.

Proof.

The Schwarzian map
Schw_{+}\colon\mathrm{QF}([c_{1}],\bullet)\to\mathrm{HQD}([c_{1}])
can be locally seen around each point
([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])\in\mathcal{T}(S)\times\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})
as the composition of the inverse of the local parametrization
u\colon U_{g}\to\mathrm{QF}([c_{1}],\bullet)
as in Proposition
5.11
and the immersion

\displaystyle U_{g}

\displaystyle\to\mathrm{HQD}([c_{1}])

\displaystyle q_{1}

\displaystyle\mapsto[\mathbf{Schw}_{+}(g+q_{1})]

which is affine by Theorem
4.1
.
The thesis follows in the same fashion for
Schw_{-}
.
∎

7.2.
Quasi-Fuchsian reciprocity and the differential of the Schwarzian map

Let
([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])\in\mathcal{T}(S)\times\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})
.

Recall the Schwarzian maps
Schw_{+}([c_{1}],\bullet)\colon\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})\to\mathrm{HQD}([{c_{1}}])
and
Schw_{-}(\bullet,[\overline{c_{2}}])\colon\mathcal{T}(S)\to\mathrm{HQD}([\overline{c_{2}}])
. Since the targets are vector spaces, their differentials are mapped in each point to
\mathrm{HQD}([c_{1}])
and
\mathrm{HQD}([\overline{c_{2}}])
too

A classic result in quasi-Fuchsian geometry is the following.

Theorem

(McMullen’s quasi-Fuchsian reciprocity
[
McM00
]
)
.

Let
(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})\in\mathcal{C}(S)\times\mathcal{C}(\overline{S})
,
X\in T_{[c_{1}]}\mathcal{T}(S)
,
\overline{Y}\in T_{[\overline{c_{2}}]}\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})
. Then,

\big(\partial_{\overline{Y}}\ Schw_{+}([c_{1}],\bullet)\big)(X)=\big(\partial_{X}\ Schw_{-}(\bullet,[\overline{c_{2}}])\big)(\overline{Y})\

where we used the identifications
\mathrm{HQD}([c_{1}])\cong T^{*}_{[c_{1}]}\mathcal{T}(S)
and
\mathrm{HQD}([\overline{c_{2}}])\cong T^{*}_{[\overline{c_{2}}]}\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})
.

McMullen’s reciprocity can actually be seen using the holomorphic extension of the Weil-Petersson metric on
\mathrm{QF}(S)
, providing an alternative description of the derivative of the Schwarzian maps and of the holomorphic Riemannian metric itself.

For all
X\in T_{[c_{1}]}\mathcal{T}(S)
and
\overline{Y}\in T_{[\overline{c_{2}}]}\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})
, denote

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}=(X,0)\in T_{([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])}\Big(\mathcal{T}(S)\times\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})\Big)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\overline{Y}}=(0,\overline{Y})\in T_{([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])}\Big(\mathcal{T}(S)\times\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})\Big)

\displaystyle\ .

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
g=g(c_{1},\overline{c_{2}})

\big(\partial_{\overline{Y}}\ Schw_{+}([c_{1}],\bullet)\big)(X)=-\langle\mathbf{X},\mathbf{\overline{Y}}\rangle_{[g]}=\big(\partial_{X}\ Schw_{-}(\bullet,[\overline{c_{2}}])\big)({\overline{Y}})\ ,

in particular, one gets McMullen’s quasi-Fuchsian reciprocity.

We remark that a similar result is shown inside a proof of Theorem 5.13 in
[
LS17
]
.

Proof.

With respect to the metric model for
T\mathrm{QF}(S)
in (
22
),
\mathbf{X}
corresponds to

{q_{X}=:\overline{\psi}d\overline{w}^{2}\in\mathrm{HQD}(\overline{c_{2}})<T_{([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])}\mathrm{QF}(S)}
, and
\mathbf{Y}
corresponds to
q_{Y}=:\varphi dz^{2}\in\mathrm{HQD}(c_{1})<T_{([c_{1}],[\overline{c_{2}}])}\mathrm{QF}(S)
.
By Remark
4.3
,
X
and
\overline{Y}
correspond to the Beltrami differentials

\beta_{X}=\frac{\overline{\psi}\partial_{\overline{z}}\overline{w}}{\rho}\frac{d\overline{z}}{dz}
and
\beta_{\overline{Y}}=\frac{\varphi\partial_{w}z}{\rho}\frac{dw}{d\overline{w}}
, respectively.

Recalling Equations (
20
) and (
21
).

\displaystyle\partial_{\overline{Y}}\big(\mathbf{Schw}_{+}(g)\big)(\beta_{X})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{d}{dt}_{|0}\mathbf{Schw}_{+}(g+tq_{\overline{Y}})\right)(\beta_{X})=-\frac{1}{2}q_{\overline{Y}}(\beta_{X})=

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{S}\varphi\cdot\frac{\overline{\psi}\partial_{\overline{z}}\overline{w}}{\rho}\frac{i}{2}dz\wedge d\overline{z}=-\frac{i}{4}\int_{S}\frac{\varphi\overline{\psi}}{\rho}dz\wedge d\overline{w}=-\langle\mathbf{X},\mathbf{\overline{Y}}\rangle_{g}\ .

In the same fashion,

\displaystyle\partial_{X}\big(\mathbf{Schw}_{-}(g)\big)(\beta_{\overline{Y}})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{d}{dt}_{|0}\mathbf{Schw}_{-}(g+tq_{{X}})\right)(\beta_{\overline{Y}})=-\frac{1}{2}q_{X}(\beta_{\overline{Y}})=

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{S}\overline{\psi}\cdot\frac{\varphi\partial_{w}z}{\rho}\frac{i}{2}dw\wedge d{\overline{w}}=-\frac{i}{4}\int_{S}\frac{\varphi\overline{\psi}}{\rho}dz\wedge d\overline{w}=-\langle\mathbf{X},\mathbf{\overline{Y}}\rangle_{g}\ .

∎

As a consequence, we get an alternative description of the imaginary part of
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
in terms of the Hessian of the renormalized volume
\mathcal{V}_{Ren}:\mathrm{QF}(S)\to{\mathbb{R}}
(see
[
KS08
]
).

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
\mathbf{X}\in T_{[c_{1}]}\Big(\mathcal{T}(S)\times\{[\overline{c_{2}}]\}\Big)
and
\overline{\mathbf{Y}}\in T_{[\overline{c_{2}}]}\Big(\{[c_{1}]\}\times\mathcal{T}(\overline{S})\Big)
. Then,

Re(\langle\mathbf{X},{\mathbf{\overline{Y}}}\rangle_{[g]})=-4\partial_{\mathbf{X}}\partial_{\mathbf{\overline{Y}}}(\mathcal{V}_{Ren})

In other words,

\langle\mathbf{X},{\mathbf{\overline{Y}}}\rangle_{[g]}=-4\partial_{\mathbf{X}}\partial_{\mathbf{\overline{Y}}}(\mathcal{V}_{Ren})+4i\partial_{i\mathbf{X}}\partial_{\mathbf{\overline{Y}}}(\mathcal{V}_{Ren})

Proof.

Let use the notation in the proof of Proposition
7.2
. By the work of Krasnov and Schlenker (
[
KS08
]
),
Re\left(Schw_{+}([g])(\beta_{X})\right)=4\partial_{X}\mathcal{V}_{Ren}
. As a consequence

Re\left(\partial_{X}\big(Schw_{+}([g])\big)(\beta_{\overline{Y}})\right)=Re\left(\partial_{\overline{Y}}\big(Schw_{-}([g])\big)(\beta_{X})\right)=4\partial_{\textbf{X}}\partial_{\overline{\textbf{Y}}}(\mathcal{V}_{Ren}),

and the result follows by Proposition
7.2
.
∎

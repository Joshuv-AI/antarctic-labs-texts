An ideal conformally covariant characterization of the Kerr conformal structure
A. García-Parrado

Abstract
We present an ideal, conformally covariant characterization of the family of four dimensional Lorentzian spacetimes that are conformally related to the Kerr vacuum solution.



Keywords:
Conformal structure, Kerr solution, Ideal characterization.

1
Introduction

Conformal geometry has played a very important role in general relativity (see
[
12
]
for a complete review of its applications).
The main reason for this is that a
conformal structure
encompasses naturally a family of spacetimes which share the same

causal structure
. A conformal structure is a pair formed by a smooth manifold and an equivalence class formed by Lorentzian metrics
that are conformally related to each other (see
[
1
]
for precise definitions). The simplest conformal structure is the set of
Lorentzian metrics conformally related to the flat Minkowski metric (conformally flat spacetimes). As is well-known, a Lorentzian metric is
conformally flat if and only if its Weyl tensor vanishes (in this paper we assume spacetimes of dimension 4).
The Weyl tensor is defined only in terms
of
concomitants
of the metric tensor (a concomitant is a quantity defined in terms of the metric tensor, its inverse and the Levi-Civita connection of the metric tensor). Such a characterization will be termed as an
ideal characterization
. The advantage of ideal characterizations
is that they can be tested
algorithmically
for any given Lorentzian metric. For example, if we wish to know whether a Lorentzian metric is
conformally flat we only need to compute its Weyl tensor and check wether it vanishes or not.

One can seek ideal characterizations for other conformal structures similar to the one described above for the
Minkowski’s conformal structure. In general relativity the vacuum solutions play a fundamental role and they are mathematically characterized as

Einstein spaces
. This is a characterization that is ideal in the sense described in the previous paragraph
but unfortunately it is not
conformally covariant
. This means that the set of vacuum solutions in general relativity
does not define a conformal structure. However, if we are given a Lorentzian metric we can ask ourselves whether such a metric
is conformal to a vacuum solution. Ideal characterizations that answer the previous question have been studied in

[
11
,
13
,
2
,
8
]
.

The aim of this paper is to present an ideal characterization of the family of Lorentzian metrics that are conformally
related to the Kerr solution
[
10
]
. This characterization is presented as a set of metric concomitants in
Theorem
48
which is the main result of this work. The relevance of this ideal characterization
is that it does not require the explicit computation of the conformal factor that relates the Lorentzian metric we are testing
with the Kerr metric. Moreover, we prove that our ideal characterization of the Kerr conformal family is also
conformally covariant
.

This paper is organized as follows: in section
2
we summarize the notions of
conformal geometry that are needed. Section
3
presents an ideal, conformally covariant characterization of
spacetimes that are conformal to vacuum solutions of the Einstein field equations (Theorem
1
taken
from
[
8
]
). In section
4
we review the ideal characterization of the Kerr solution
that was found in
[
6
]
. Finally section
5
is devoted to our main result given in Theorem

48
.

All the tensor computations in this paper have been carried out with the system
xAct

[
21
]
,
a
Wolfram Language
suite for doing tensor analysis (see also
[
22
]
).

2
Elements of conformal geometry

Let
(\tilde{\mathcal{M}},{\tilde{g}}_{ab})
be a 4-dimensional Lorentzian manifold (referred to as the physical space-time) and

(\mathcal{M},g_{ab})
a second Lorentzian manifold (called the unphysical spacetime) which is
conformally related
to the
first in the following fashion (the signature convention for both metrics is
(-,+,+,+)
)

g_{ab}=\Theta^{2}\tilde{g}_{ab}.

(1)

The previous relation can be used to define a conformal map (conformal embedding)

\Phi:\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
and the
conformal factor
\Theta
is assumed to be a smooth function which does not vanish
on
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
(unless otherwise stated, all structures are assumed to be smooth).
Since
\Phi
is an embedding, there exists an inverse map

\Phi^{-1}:\Phi(\tilde{\mathcal{M}})\subset\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
.
The conformal embedding just defined is used to construct the
conformal boundary

of
(\tilde{\mathcal{M}},\tilde{g}_{ab})

[
23
]
and this is the reason for the
adopted terminology. It can also be used to set up an equivalence relation between diffeomorphic
spacetime manifolds under a conformal map. The resulting equivalence classes are called

conformal structures
. In this way, if (
1
) holds
then
g_{ab}
and
\tilde{g}_{ab}
define the same conformal structure.

We shall use small Latin letters to denote abstract indices
of tensors in
\mathcal{M}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
.
Indices are always raised and lowered with respect to the unphysical metric
g_{ab}

with the exception of
\tilde{g}^{ab}
, where we follow
the traditional convention that it represents the inverse
of
\tilde{g}_{ab}

\tilde{g}^{ac}\tilde{g}_{cb}=\delta_{b}{}^{a}.

(2)

From (
1
), the explicit relation between
the physical and the unphysical contravariant metric tensors becomes

g^{ab}=\frac{\tilde{g}^{ab}}{\Theta^{2}}.

(3)

Each of the metric tensors
g_{ab}
,
\tilde{g}_{ab}
has its own volume element,
denoted respectively by
\eta_{abcd}
and
\tilde{\eta}_{abcd}
.
Using again (
1
) we deduce the relation

\tilde{\eta}_{abcd}=\frac{\eta_{abcd}}{\Theta^{4}}.

(4)

Also each metric tensor has its own Levi-Civita connection denoted respectively
by
\nabla_{a}
,
\tilde{\nabla}_{a}
which are used to define the connection coefficients and the curvature
tensors in the standard fashion. Our conventions for the (unphysical) Riemann, Ricci and Weyl tensors
are

\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}\omega_{c}-\nabla_{b}\nabla_{a}\omega_{c}=R_{abc}{}^{d}\omega_{d}\;,

(5)

R_{ac}\equiv R_{abc}{}^{b}\;,

(6)

C_{abc}{}^{d}\equiv R_{abc}{}^{d}-2L^{d}{}_{[b}g_{a]c}-2\delta^{d}{}_{[b}L_{a]c}\;,

(7)

where the unphysical Schouten tensor is defined by

L_{ab}\equiv\tfrac{1}{2}(R_{ab}-\tfrac{1}{6}Rg_{ab}).

(8)

Tensors defined in terms of the physical metric
\tilde{g}_{ab}
will be denoted with a tilde over
the symbol employed for an unphysical spacetime tensor.
The difference between the Levi-Civita connection
coefficients of the physical and the unphysical metrics
is the tensor
\Gamma[\nabla,\tilde{\nabla}]^{e}{}_{ac}
given by

\Gamma[\nabla,\tilde{\nabla}]^{e}{}_{ac}=\delta_{c}{}^{e}\ \Upsilon_{a}-g_{ca}\Upsilon^{e}+\delta_{a}{}^{e}\ \Upsilon_{c},\quad\Upsilon_{a}\equiv\frac{\nabla_{a}\Theta}{\Theta}.

(9)

Using the previous equation, standard computations enable us to find the relations between the curvature
tensors of
\nabla
and
\tilde{\nabla}
.
In this work, the relation between the unphysical
Weyl tensor and the physical one will be specially important

C_{abc}{}^{d}=\tilde{C}_{abc}{}^{d},\quad

(10)

Here,
\tilde{C}_{abc}{}^{d}
is the physical Weyl tensor as defined by (
7
)
in terms of
\tilde{L}_{ab}
,
\tilde{g}_{de}
and
\tilde{R}_{abc}{}^{d}
.
The star
*
is used to denote both the Hodge dual (with respect to the physical or unphysical metric)
and the complex conjugation and we leave to the context the distinction between these.

The notions of
metric concomitant
and
conformally covariant
metric concomitant
are of fundamental relevance for this work.

Definition 1

(Metric concomitant)
.

A
metric concomitant
is a quantity defined in terms of the metric tensor,
its inverse and the Levi-Civita connection of the metric tensor.

Definition 2

(Conformally covariant metric concomitant)
.

A
metric concomitant

T(g,\nabla)
is said to be

conformally covariant
if it fulfills the following
relation under the transformation
g_{ab}=\Theta^{2}\tilde{g}_{ab}

{\tilde{T}}({\tilde{g}}_{ab},\tilde{\nabla})=\Theta^{p}T(g_{ab},\nabla),\quad p\in\mathbb{R},

(11)

where the real number
p
is called the
conformal weight
of
T

and
{\tilde{g}}_{ab}
,
g_{ab}
are related by (
1
)

For example the
Weyl tensor with the index configuration
as described by eq. (
39a
)
is conformally covariant with weight zero
(aka conformally invariant).
Any metric dependent quantity satisfying (
11
)
can be regarded as a section of a suitable vector bundle of

conformal densities
(see
[
1
]

for further details).

3
An ideal conformally invariant characterization of Einstein spacetimes

Recall that a physical spacetime is Einstein iff

\tilde{R}_{ab}=\lambda\tilde{g}_{ab},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

(12)

A basic consequence of the Einstein field equations is that a vacuum solution is represented by an Einstein spacetime. The question now is whether for a given unphysical spacetime

(\mathcal{M},g_{ab})
we can find a
local
,

ideal
and
conformally covariant
set of conditions that guarantee that
g_{ab}
is conformal to an Einstein physical spacetime.
By local we mean that the conditions should hold in the neighbourhood of a point of
\mathcal{M}
and by ideal conformally covariant
we mean that the conditions only involve the metric
g_{ab}
, its
covariant concomitants
(tensors constructed out of the metric
and its Levi-Civita compatible covariant derivative) and also each of the conditions should
fulfill (
11
) for some
p\in\mathbb{R}
.
This relevant question has been studied in
[
11
,
13
,
2
,
8
]

The following Theorem is one of the results presented in
[
8
]

although we choose to present it in a form adapted to our purposes.

Theorem 1

(Gover & Nurowski 2006)
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g_{ab})
be a 4-dimensional spacetime and define the following concomitants of
g_{ab}
.
Define the unphysical metric concomitants

\Lambda^{d}\equiv\left(\frac{8}{C\cdot C}\right)C^{damp}\nabla_{[m}L_{p]a},\quad C\cdot C\equiv C_{abcd}C^{abcd}.

Then, assuming that
C\cdot C\neq 0
,
(\mathcal{M},g_{ab})
is locally conformal to an Einstein spacetime if and only if the following
covariant concomitant of
g_{ab}
vanishes

E_{ab}(g_{ab},\nabla)\equiv\mbox{\rm TF}_{g}\big[L_{ab}+\nabla_{a}\Lambda_{b}+\Lambda_{a}\Lambda_{b}\big],

(13)

where
{\rm TF}_{g}
stands for “trace-free part” with respect to
g_{ab}
.
Moreover
E_{ab}
is
conformally invariant
according to

\tilde{E}_{ab}(\tilde{g}_{ab},\tilde{\nabla})=E_{ab}(g_{ab},\nabla).

Proof.

The relation between the
unphysical and physical Schouten tensors can be always written in the form (see e.g.
[
12
]
)

L_{ab}=\tilde{L}_{ab}-\Upsilon_{a}\Upsilon_{b}+\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}\Upsilon_{c}\Upsilon^{c}-\nabla_{b}\Upsilon_{a}.

(14)

Computing the unphysical covariant derivative of the previous equation and using (
5
) with
\omega_{d}
replaced by

\Upsilon_{d}
we get the following integrability condition

2\tilde{L}_{b[p}g_{m]a}\Upsilon^{b}+2\tilde{L}_{a[m}\Upsilon_{p]}+\Upsilon^{b}C_{abmp}+2\nabla_{[p}\tilde{L}_{m]a}+2\nabla_{[m}L_{p]a}=0.

(15)

Assume now that we are under the hypotheses of
Theorem
1
and the physical spacetime is Einstein. Thus, according to
(
12
) we have
\tilde{L}_{ab}=\lambda\tilde{g}_{ab}/6
. Replacing this into
(
15
) we obtain, after some manipulations involving
(
1
) and the definition of
\Upsilon_{a}

\Upsilon^{b}C_{abmp}+2\nabla_{[m}L_{p]a}=0.

(16)

If we multiply both sides of this equation by
C^{damp}
and use the identity valid only
in dimension four,

C_{abmp}C^{dbmp}=\tfrac{1}{4}\delta_{a}{}^{d}C_{cbmp}C^{cbmp}

(see e.g.
[
3
]
),
it yields

\Upsilon^{d}=\left(\frac{8}{C\cdot C}\right)C^{damp}\nabla_{[m}L_{p]a},

(17)

where we assumed
C\cdot C\neq 0
. Replacing
\Upsilon^{d}

back in (
14
)
we get
E_{ab}=0
. Conversely, assume that
E_{ab}=0
. Then from (
13
)
we get

\nabla_{[a}\Lambda_{b]}=0.

This means that locally
\Lambda_{a}=\nabla_{a}\log\psi
for some function
\psi>0
.
Define now the physical metric
{\tilde{g}}_{ab}=\tfrac{g_{ab}}{\psi^{2}}
.
Then eq. (
14
) becomes

L_{ab}=\tilde{L}_{ab}-\Lambda_{a}\Lambda_{b}+\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}\Lambda_{c}\Lambda^{c}-\nabla_{b}\Lambda_{a}

(18)

Replacing this value of
L_{ab}
into (
13
) we
conclude that
{\rm TF}_{g}(\tilde{L}_{ab})=0
so
\tilde{L}_{ab}
is proportional
to
{\tilde{g}}_{ab}
and thus
\tilde{g}_{ab}
is necessarily Einstein.
Finally, to prove the conformal invariance of
E_{ab}
let us
assume the relation (
1
)
but this time we do not impose that the physical
\tilde{g}_{ab}

is Einstein. Then we have the relations
(see eqs. (
44
), (
14
) and (
9
))

\displaystyle\tilde{g}_{de}\tilde{\Lambda}^{e}=\Lambda_{d}-\Upsilon_{d},

(19)

\displaystyle\tilde{L}_{ab}=L_{ab}+\Upsilon_{a}\Upsilon_{b}-\tfrac{1}{2}\ g_{ab}\Upsilon_{c}\Upsilon^{c}+\nabla_{b}\Upsilon_{a},

(20)

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{b}(\tilde{g}_{ae}\tilde{\Lambda}^{e})=\Lambda_{b}\Upsilon_{a}+\ \Lambda_{a}\Upsilon_{b}-2\Upsilon_{a}\Upsilon_{b}+g_{ab}(-\Lambda^{c}\Upsilon_{c}+\Upsilon_{c}\Upsilon^{c})+\nabla_{b}\Lambda_{a}-\nabla_{b}\Upsilon_{a}.

(21)

Putting all together we get

\mbox{\rm TF}_{\tilde{g}}\big[\tilde{L}_{ab}+\tilde{\nabla}_{a}(\tilde{g}_{be}\Lambda^{e})+\tilde{\Lambda}_{a}\tilde{\Lambda}_{b}\big]=\mbox{\rm TF}_{g}\big[L_{ab}+\nabla_{a}\Lambda_{b}+\Lambda_{a}\Lambda_{b}\big],

(22)

which is the sought conformal invariance.
∎

Eq. (
17
) is an useful relation that enables us to write the differential of
\Theta
in terms of concomitants
of the metric
g_{ab}
whenever
\Theta
defines a conformal relation between an unphysical metric and
a physical Einstein space in dimension 4. See
[
2
]
to find out how the previous computation can be generalized to any dimension.

If the unphysical spacetime does not fulfill the hypotheses of Theorem
1
then (
17
)
does not necessarily hold. However, we can still define a concomitant of the unphysical metric
g_{ab}
using the right hand side
of this equation

\Lambda^{d}\equiv\left(\frac{8}{C\cdot C}\right)C^{damp}\nabla_{[m}L_{p]a}.

(23)

The concomitant
\Lambda^{d}
will be needed in our main result, Theorem
48
. Clearly
if
\tilde{g}_{ab}
is Einstein then
\Lambda_{a}=\Upsilon_{a}
.

An ideal characterization of the physical Kerr solution

A set of
local
characterizations of the Kerr solution
were presented in
[
15
,
14
]
. However,
these results are not ideal in the sense that they necessitate the
existence of a Killing vector field (in
[
15
]
also
the asymptotic flatness is required). In
[
6
]
a local ideal characterization of
the Kerr solution was produced. This characterization of the Kerr solution
will be taken as our starting point for the ideal characterization of
the Kerr conformal family, so it will be next reviewed.
Let us introduce the required geometric elements:
from the physical metric
\tilde{g}_{ab}
and
its inverse
\tilde{g}^{ab}

\tilde{\eta}_{abcd}
,
the physical Weyl tensor
\tilde{W}_{abcd}\equiv\tilde{g}_{ah}\tilde{C}_{bcd}{}^{h}

the physical Weyl tensor right dual
\tilde{W}^{*}_{abcd}

\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{abcd}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{W}_{abcd}-{\rm i}\tilde{W}^{*}{}_{abcd}),\quad\tilde{W}^{*}{}_{abcd}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\tilde{\eta}_{cdpq}\tilde{g}^{pr}\tilde{g}^{qs}\tilde{W}_{abrs}.

(24)

Next we introduce the following physical Weyl scalars

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathit{a}}\equiv\tilde{g}^{ac}\tilde{g}^{be}\tilde{g}^{fq}\tilde{g}^{pd}\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{abpf}\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{cedq}\;,

(25)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathit{b}}\equiv\tilde{g}^{ai}\tilde{g}^{bj}\tilde{g}^{ce}\tilde{g}^{dg}\tilde{g}^{pf}\tilde{g}^{qh}\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{abcd}\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{egpq}\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{fhij}\;,

(26)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathit{w}}\equiv-\frac{\tilde{\mathit{b}}}{2\tilde{\mathit{a}}}\;,\quad\tilde{a}\neq 0

(27)

and the tensors

\tilde{G}_{abmh}\equiv\tilde{g}_{am}\tilde{g}_{bh}-\tilde{g}_{ah}\tilde{g}_{bm}\;,\quad\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{abcd}\equiv\tfrac{1}{2}(-{\rm i}\tilde{\eta}_{abcd}+\tilde{G}_{abcd}).

(28)

The Kerr solution belongs to the class of vacuum
type D
solutions. An
ideal characterization of the latter family of algebraically special solutions was
presented in
[
4
,
5
]
. We reproduce here this
ideal characterization, adopting the formulation of
[

]

Theorem 2

The physical spacetime
(\tilde{\mathcal{M}},\tilde{g}_{ab})

\tilde{a}\neq 0\;,\quad\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{abcd}=0\;,

\tilde{\mathcal{D}}_{abhe}\equiv\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{abcd}\tilde{g}^{cp}\tilde{g}^{dq}\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{pqhe}-\frac{\tilde{\mathit{a}}}{6}\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{abhe}-\frac{\tilde{\mathit{b}}}{\tilde{\mathit{a}}}\tilde{\mathcal{W}}_{abhe}.

physical Kerr family

6
Conclusions

In the proof of Theorem
48
the unphysical spacetime
(\mathcal{M},g_{ab})

is conformal to a physical spacetime
(\tilde{\mathcal{M}},\tilde{g}_{ab})
that is Einstein, since
Theorem
1
is assumed to hold.
This means that a conformal embedding
\Phi:\tilde{\mathcal{M}}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
exists and hence
Theorem
48
only applies to an open subset
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{M}
such that
\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal{U})\subset\tilde{\mathcal{M}}
.
However, if all concomitants displayed in (
48a
)-(
48
) are smooth in

\mathcal{M}
and the conditions of Theorem
1
hold only in an open subset
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{M}
,
then it is plausible to think that the unphysical spacetime
(\mathcal{M},g_{ab})
is a
conformal extension
of the Kerr physical spacetime
(\tilde{\mathcal{M}},\tilde{g}_{ab})
.
In this sense the result presented in this article would be the first step towards an ideal characterization of
a smooth conformal extension of the Kerr solution which in turn could provide a construction of the
Kerr conformal boundary
.
It is worth noting that studies about initial data on the conformal boundary of the Kerr solution
are already available in the literature when the cosmological
constant does not vanish and the conformal boundary is spacelike
(namely, the Kerr de Sitter solution, see
[
19
,
16
,
17
,
20
,
18
]
). Our result could be used for a similar purpose
when the cosmological constant vanishes if
one finds suitable
hyperbolic reductions
and (characteristic) initial
data for the conditions found in Theorem
48
.

As already pointed out in Remark
4
,
looking at the covariant concomitants of the unphysical metric given by
Theorem
48
we realize that all of them are
conformally covariant
.
This renders our characterization of the Kerr conformal family as conformally covariant,
a property already shared by family of 4-dimensional metrics that are conformal
to the vacuum solutions of the Einstein field equations (see Theorem
1
).
To the best of our knowledge this is
a new result about the Kerr (conformal) family whose consequences are still to be investigated.

Deviation of nonradial geodesics in a static spherically symmetric space-time
V.P. Vandeev, A.N. Semenova

Abstract
The article generalizes the description of tidal forces to the case of geodesics with non-zero angular momentum in the metric of static spherically symmetric black holes. We show that the geodesic deviation equation can be diagonalized even with non-radial free motion of a test body in the gravitational field. We present expressions for the spatial components of the tidal force in a spherically symmetric metric. We also solve geodesic deviation equation in the Schwarzschild metric and demonstrate how the presence of angular momentum and its magnitude affect the solution.

2
Geodesics in static spherically symmetric spacetime

We consider spacetime with line element of static spherically symmetric black hole given by

ds^{2}=g_{\mu\nu}dx^{\mu}dx^{\nu}=f(r)dt^{2}-\frac{dr^{2}}{f(r)}-r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\right).

(1)

At this stage we do not specify the form of the function
f(r)
.
In this metric there are four meaningful geodesic equations
[
23
]
for all coordinates:

u^{0}=\frac{dt}{d\tau}=\frac{E}{f(r)},

(2)

\left(u^{1}\right)^{2}=\left(\frac{dr}{d\tau}\right)^{2}=E^{2}-f(r)\left(1+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}\right),

(3)

u^{2}=\frac{d\theta}{d\tau}=0,

(4)

u^{3}=\frac{d\phi}{d\tau}=\frac{L}{r^{2}},

(5)

where
\tau
is the proper time,
E
and
L
are energy and angular momentum of a freely moving test body respectively. And it should be noted that dynamics of the azimuthal variable is trivial due to the
spherical symmetry of spacetime, and the dynamics of the polar variable is nontrivial because there is angular momentum
L
. The set of expressions (
2
)-(
5
) forms a unit covariant
4-velocity vector
u^{\mu}
tangent to the geodesic. Also it should be noted that work considers only timelike geodesics.

3
Geodesic deviation equation

Below we consider geodesic deviation equation. As it is well known
[
20
]
, the equation for the spacelike geodesic deviation vector
\tilde{\xi}^{\mu}
is given by

\frac{D^{2}\tilde{\xi}^{\mu}}{d\tau^{2}}=R^{\mu}_{.\nu\alpha\beta}u^{\nu}u^{\alpha}\tilde{\xi}^{\beta},

(6)

where
\frac{D^{2}}{d\tau^{2}}
is covariant derivative along the geodesic,
R^{\mu}_{.\nu\alpha\beta}
is Riemann curvature tensor and
u^{\nu}
is the unit vector of 4-velocity tangent to the geodesic.

\tilde{\xi}^{\mu}
describes the distance between two infinitesimally close free moving particles.

The nonzero components of the Riemann tensor are calculated by the metric (
1
) and have the form

R^{0}_{.101}=-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{2f},\\

(7a)

R^{0}_{.202}=R^{1}_{.212}=-\frac{f^{\prime}r}{2},\\

(7b)

R^{0}_{.303}=R^{1}_{.313}=-\frac{f^{\prime}r\sin^{2}{\theta}}{2},\\

(7c)

R^{2}_{.323}=\left(1-f\right)\sin^{2}{\theta}.

(7d)

It is seen that on the right-hand side of the Eq. (
6
) there is a matrix
P^{\mu}_{.\beta}\equiv R^{\mu}_{.\nu\alpha\beta}u^{\nu}u^{\alpha}
which is

\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\dot{r}^{2}f^{\prime\prime}}{2f}+\frac{\chi L^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{r^{2}}&-\frac{E\dot{r}f^{\prime\prime}}{2f^{2}}&0&-\frac{\chi EL\sin^{2}\theta}{f}\\
\frac{E\dot{r}f^{\prime\prime}}{2}&\frac{\chi L^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{r^{2}}-\frac{E^{2}f^{\prime\prime}}{2f}&0&-\chi L\dot{r}\sin^{2}\theta\\
0&0&\frac{(f-1)L^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{r^{4}}-\chi\omega&0\\
\frac{\chi EL}{r^{2}}&-\frac{\chi L\dot{r}}{fr^{2}}&0&-\chi\omega\end{pmatrix},

(8)

where

\chi=\frac{f^{\prime}}{2r},

(9a)

\omega=1+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}},

(9b)

\dot{r}=u^{1}=\frac{dr}{d\tau}=\sqrt{E^{2}-f\left(1+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)}.

(9c)

Matrix (
8
) has spectrum

\lambda_{t}=0,

(10a)

\lambda_{r}=\frac{\chi L^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{r^{2}}-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}\omega}{2},

(10b)

\lambda_{\theta}=\frac{(f-1)L^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{r^{4}}-\chi\omega,

(10c)

\lambda_{\phi}=\frac{\chi L^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{r^{2}}-\chi\omega.

(10d)

Therefore, a tetrad basis for the free fall frame of reference can be constructed. It has form

e_{t}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\frac{L^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{r^{2}}}}\left(\frac{E}{f},\dot{r},0,\frac{L}{r^{2}}\right),

(11a)

e_{r}^{\mu}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}}}\left(-\frac{\dot{r}}{f},-E,0,0\right),

(11b)

e_{\theta}^{\mu}=\left(0,0,\frac{1}{r},0\right),

(11c)

e_{\phi}^{\mu}=\frac{L\sin\theta}{r\sqrt{1+\frac{L^{2}\cos^{2}\theta}{r^{2}}}\sqrt{1+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}}}\left(\frac{E}{f},\dot{r},0,\frac{1+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}}{L\sin^{2}{\theta}}\right).\\

(11d)

This tetrad set
e^{\mu}_{\alpha}
satisfy normalization condition
{e^{\mu}_{\alpha}\>e^{\nu}_{\beta}\>g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\alpha\beta}}
with Minkowski metric
\eta_{\alpha\beta}=\text{diag}\left(1,-1,-1,-1\right)
.
The geodesic deviation vector
\tilde{\xi}^{\mu}
can be substituted as

\tilde{\xi}^{\mu}=e^{\mu}_{\nu}\>\xi^{\nu}.

(12)

Thus, the meaningful components of Eq. (
6
) in the new coordinate system are

\ddot{\xi}^{r}=\left[-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{2}\left(1+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)+\frac{f^{\prime}}{2r}\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}\right]\xi^{r},

(13)

\ddot{\xi}^{\theta}=\left[-\frac{f^{\prime}}{2r}\left(1+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}\right)+\frac{f-1}{r^{2}}\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}\right]\xi^{\theta},

(14)

\ddot{\xi}^{\phi}=-\frac{f^{\prime}}{2r}\xi^{\phi}.

(15)

These equations are the diagonal form of Eq. (
6
). It is worth noting that the dependence on the
\theta
disappeared from Eqs. (
13
)–(
15
) because there is no dynamics along the
direction of the azimuthal angle
\theta
according to Eq. (
4
) and geodesics lie in the equatorial plane
\theta=\frac{\pi}{2}
.

The diagonal form of Eq. (
6
) for a static spherically symmetric spacetime for non-radial geodesics was obtained in
[
8
]
but a slightly more complex mathematical apparatus was used there,
although in fact it is sufficient to use the methods of linear algebra. For
L=0
Eqs. (
13
)–(
15
) coincide with Eqs. (14) and (15) of Ref.
[
12
]
.

To illustrate the result obtained, we can use superposition of charged black hole and (Anti)-de Sitter spacetime, its metric is

ds^{2}=f(r)dt^{2}-\frac{dr^{2}}{f(r)}-r^{2}\left(d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\phi^{2}\right),

(16)

where

f(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{q^{2}}{r^{2}}-\frac{\Lambda}{3}r^{2}.

(17)

Using (
17
) in Eqs. (
13
)–(
15
) we get

\ddot{\xi}^{r}=\left[\frac{2M}{r^{3}}-\frac{3q^{2}}{r^{4}}+\frac{\Lambda}{3}+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}\left(\frac{3M}{r^{3}}-\frac{4q^{2}}{r^{4}}\right)\right]\xi^{r},

(18a)

\ddot{\xi}^{\theta}=\left[-\frac{M}{r^{3}}+\frac{q^{2}}{r^{4}}+\frac{\Lambda}{3}+\frac{L^{2}}{r^{2}}\left(-\frac{3M}{r^{3}}+\frac{2q^{2}}{r^{4}}\right)\right]\xi^{\theta},

(18b)

\ddot{\xi}^{\phi}=\left[-\frac{M}{r^{3}}+\frac{q^{2}}{r^{4}}+\frac{\Lambda}{3}\right]\xi^{\phi}.

(18c)

4.1
Solutions of the geodesic deviation equations

Using Eq. (
3

(26b)

P_{\theta}(\rho)=\frac{\rho^{2}+3l^{2}}{(E^{2}-1)\rho^{5}+2\rho^{4}-l^{2}\rho^{3}+2l^{2}\rho^{2}},

(26c)

P_{\phi}(\rho)=\frac{1}{(E^{2}-1)\rho^{3}+2\rho^{2}-l^{2}\rho+2l^{2}}.

(26d)

(27)

(28)

where

(29)

and

(31c)

5
Conclusion

In this article we consider tidal properties of spherical symmetric spacetimes. In Sec.
3
it was possible to show an alternative way to diagonalize the geodesic deviation equation in the case of geodesics with
non-zero angular momentum. We perform specific calculations in the Schwarzschild metric, which is determined only by the black hole mass. Sec.
4
demonstrates that the presence of angular momentum
does not lead to the appearance of extremum points for all the tidal force components and leaves them constant in sign.

Further, in Sec.
4.1
we investigate the solutions of geodesic deviation equations in Schwarzschild spacetime. First we present the previously known
[
8

and (

25

that for all Eqs. (

)–(

\rho=0
is special, which is expected because it is physical singularity. Thus, in Sec.
4.3
we consider solutions near
\rho=0
,
\rho=\infty

In a vicinity of zero and infinity we found solutions in the form of generalized power series using the Frobenius method because Equations (
23
)–(

From a comparison of (
30a

40a

\rho=0

Therefore, regardless of fall trajectory on the Schwarzschild black hole, the test body will experience stretching, which is described by the leading order of radial geodesic deviation vector component

The study of tidal effects for non-radial geodesics in general relativity can evolve towards considering more general metrics, for example, Reissner–Nordström or Kottler metric. Also quite
interesting is the question of geodesic deviation when both a test free falling body and a black hole have angular momentum. In addition, it is interesting to investigate the geodesic deviation equation
in multidimensional spacetimes and, in particular, in the metrics of black rings. As we can see, despite the fact that the classical effects in the general relativity have been studied for a very long time,
this issue still contains many unsolved problems.

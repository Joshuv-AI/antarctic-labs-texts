Abstract
. In the recent paper A. Dimca proves that when one adds to or deletes a line from a free curve the resulting curve is either free or plus-one generated. We prove the converse statements, we give an additional insight into the original deletion result, and derive a characterisation of free curves in terms of behaviour to addition/deletion of lines. We describe the possible splitting types of the bundle of logarithmic vector fields associated to a plus-one generated curve.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a reduced curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}
defined by the equation
f_{\mathcal{C}}=0
, where
f_{\mathcal{C}}\in S\coloneqq\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]
.
The logarithmic derivation module associated to the curve
\mathcal{C}
is the submodule of
{\rm Der}(S)
defined by

D(\mathcal{C})\coloneqq\{\theta\in{\rm Der}(S)\;|\;\theta(f_{\mathcal{C}})\in Sf_{\mathcal{C}}\}.

The Euler derivation
\theta_{E}=x\partial x+y\partial y+z\partial z
is an element of
D(\mathcal{C})
for any curve
\mathcal{C}
. Furthermore, the module
D(\mathcal{C})
admits a decomposition as the direct sum

D(\mathcal{C})=D_{0}(\mathcal{C})\oplus S\theta_{E},

where

D_{0}(\mathcal{C})\coloneqq\{\theta\in{\rm Der}(S)\;|\;\theta(f_{\mathcal{C}})=0\}.

The curve
\mathcal{C}
is called
free
if
D_{0}(\mathcal{C})
is free as an S-module. In this case,
D_{0}(\mathcal{C})
is a rank
2
graded module and we call
exponents
of
\mathcal{C}
the pair of degrees of the elements in a basis of homogeneous derivations of
D_{0}(\mathcal{C})
. In other words, if
\{\theta_{2},\theta_{3}\}
is a basis of homogeneous derivations of
D_{0}(\mathcal{C})
, then
\exp(\mathcal{C})\coloneqq(\deg(\theta_{2}),\deg(\theta_{3}))
.

We recall the definition of a recent notion introduced by Abe in
[
1
]
for hyperplane arrangements and extended to curves by Dimca and Sticlaru in
[
6
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A reduced curve
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is called
plus-one generated
if its associated module of derivations
D(\mathcal{C})
admits a minimal set of homogeneous generators
\{\theta_{E},\theta_{2},\theta_{3},\psi\}
that satisfy a unique relation of the form

(1)

f_{1}\theta_{E}+f_{2}\theta_{2}+f_{3}\theta_{3}+\alpha\psi=0,

where
f_{i}\in S
for
i=1,2,3
and
\alpha\in S
with
\deg(\alpha)=1
. The
exponents
of
\mathcal{C}
are, by definition, the pair
(\deg(\theta_{2}),\deg(\theta_{3}))
and
\deg(\psi)
is called the
level
of
\mathcal{C}
.

In the recent paper
[
3
]
, Dimca proves the following addition result for free curves (see Section
2
for the additional notions and notations).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a free reduced curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
of exponents
(d_{2},d_{3})
and
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a line that is not an irreducible component of
\mathcal{C}
. Then
\mathcal{C}\cup L
is either free with the exponents
(d_{2},d_{3}+1)
and
d_{2}+1=|\mathcal{C}\cap L|+\epsilon(\mathcal{C},L)

or plus-one generated with the exponents
(d_{2}+1,d_{3}+1)
and level
d=|\mathcal{C}\cap L|+\epsilon(\mathcal{C},L)-1
.

In Section
2
we recall some necessary notions and results from the literature and we prove some new results related to Chern numbers and splitting types for the vector bundle associated to a plus-one generated curve, defined in
2
. In general, evaluating splitting types onto projective lines for vector bundles is a difficult endeavor.
We describe in Theorem
2.8
the set of possible values for the splitting types of the vector bundle associated to a plus-one generated curve, generalizing similar results on plus-one generated line arrangements, respectively on nearly free curves, from
[
4
]
, respectively from
[
2
]
.

In Section
3
we present our main results.
We prove in Theorem
3.1
a converse of the above result. In the same paper Dimca proved a deletion result for free reduced curves, see
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
. It states that for a free curve
\mathcal{C}
with a line
L
being an irreducible component, the curve
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
obtained by removing from
\mathcal{C}
line
L
is either free or plus-one generated. The author asks whether some relation between the exponents of
\mathcal{C}
and the exponents (and level) of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
can be given. We answer this question in Theorem
3.2
.
We prove in Theorem
3.3
a converse of the deletion result
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem
3.4
generalizes a similar result on conic-line arrangements of Schenck and Tohaneanu, see
[
9
, Theorem 2.5]
, describing the situation when the addition or the deletion of a projective line from a free curve results in a free curve.

Finally, summing up Theorems
3.1
,
3.2
,
3.3
,
3.4
and
[
3
, Theorems 1.1, 1.2]
, we give in Theorems
3.6
and
3.5
a freeness criterion for curves in terms of behaviour to addition or to deletion of projective lines.

Preliminaries

Unless otherwise stated,
\mathcal{C}
is a reduced curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
is a line that is an irreducible component of
\mathcal{C}
. Let
f_{\mathcal{C}}\in S
be a homogeneous polynomial such that
f_{\mathcal{C}}=0
defines
\mathcal{C}
.
We denote by
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime})

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is the curve obtained by removing from

.
Without loss of generality, we may take
\alpha_{L}=z
as the defining linear form of the line

f_{\mathcal{C}}=zf_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}

Denote by
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}}
the locally free sheaf (i.e. vector bundle) obtained by the sheafification of the graded module

.
Recall that
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}}=\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}(-1)
, where
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{C}}
is Saito’s sheaf of logarithmic vector ﬁelds along

Remark 2.1
.

By
[
3
, Theorem 2.3]
(see also
[
8
, Theorem 1.6, Remark 1.8]
), we have an exact sequence of sheaves (actually vector bundles) for the triple
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime})
defined above, namely

(2)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(-1)\overset{\alpha_{L}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(1-|\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}|-\epsilon(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},L))\rightarrow 0,

where
\epsilon(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},L)\in\mathbb{Z}
is a constant defined by Dimca in
[
3
, page 2]
. This constant measures the defect from the property of being a quasi-homogeneous triple, namely if all singularities of the triple
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime})
are quasi-homogeneous,
then the above exact sequence has becomes

(3)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(-1)\overset{\alpha_{L}}{\longrightarrow}\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(1-|\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}|)\rightarrow 0.

Note also that by
[
3
, Remark 2.4]
we only need to assume that the singularities of
\mathcal{C}
on
L
are quasi-homogeneous in order for the exact sequence (
3
) to hold.

Denote by
c(\mathcal{E})
the Chern polynomial of a complex vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
. It is well-known that, for an exact sequence of vector bundles

0\rightarrow\mathcal{E}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}\rightarrow 0,

we have the relation

(4)

c(\mathcal{F})=c(\mathcal{E})c(\mathcal{G}).

In particular, if
c_{i}(\mathcal{E})
is the
i
-th Chern number of
\mathcal{E}
, then we have the equality

(5)

c_{2}(\mathcal{F})=c_{2}(\mathcal{E})+c_{1}(\mathcal{E})c_{1}(\mathcal{G})+c_{2}(\mathcal{G}).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a rank
2
vector bundle and
c(\mathcal{E})=1+c_{1}(\mathcal{E})t+c_{2}(\mathcal{E})t^{2}
its Chern polynomial. Then the Chern polynomial of
\,\mathcal{E}(-1)
has the following form

c(\mathcal{E}(-1))=1+(-2+c_{1}(\mathcal{E}))t+(c_{2}(\mathcal{E})-c_{1}(\mathcal{E})+1)t^{2}.

Proof.

Immediate follows from the equality
\mathcal{E}(-1)=\mathcal{E}\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-1)
and the formula for the Chern classes of a tensor product of a rank
2
vector bundle and a line bundle, taking into account that
c(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(-1))=1-t
.
∎

Let us recall a result due to Yoshinaga devoted to the splitting type along a line of rank
2
vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, which we will apply to sheaves of logarithmic vector fields along a curve, see Remark
2.4
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
10
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a rank
2
vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
,
L
a line in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and
\mathcal{E}|_{L}={\pazocal O}_{L}(-a)\oplus{\pazocal O}_{L}(-b)
. Then

c_{2}(\mathcal{E})-ab={\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}Coker(\Gamma_{*}(\mathcal{E})\overset{\pi_{L}}{\longrightarrow}(\Gamma_{*}(\mathcal{E}|_{L})),

where
c_{2}(\mathcal{E})
is the second Chern number of
\mathcal{E}
and
\pi_{L}
is the morphism of graded modules of sections induced by the restriction to
L
of the vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{E}
is splitting if and only if
\pi_{L}
is surjective.

The pair
(a,b)
is called the
splitting type
onto
L
of the bundle
\mathcal{E}
.

Remark 2.4
.

For an arbitrary reduced curve
\mathcal{C}
we have an isomorphism
\Gamma_{*}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}})\cong D_{0}(\mathcal{C})
, see
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
for details. Then
\mathcal{C}
is free if and only if
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}
is splitting.

Another important instrument to test the freeness property for hyperplane arrangements is Saito’s criterion. A version of this result holds for reduced hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{n}_{\mathbb{C}}
, in particular for reduced curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, see
[
9
]
.

Theorem 2.5

(Saito’s criterion for curves)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a reduced curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
defined by
f_{\mathcal{C}}=0
with
f_{\mathcal{C}}\in S
. Given a set of homogeneous derivations
\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}\}\subset D(\mathcal{C})
, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\{\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}\}
is a basis in
D(\mathcal{C})
.

(2)

The determinant of the matrix
M
is equal to
f_{\mathcal{C}}
(modulo a non-zero constant), where
M
is the
3\times 3
-matrix with lines
[\theta_{i}(x)\;\theta_{i}(y)\;\theta_{i}(z)],\;i=1,2,3
.

We will denote by
mdr(\mathcal{C})=mdr(f_{\mathcal{C}})
the minimal degree of relations for
f_{\mathcal{C}}
, which is by definition the smallest degree of a non-zero derivation from
D_{0}(\mathcal{C})
. It is known that for free or for plus-one generated curves
\mathcal{C}
one has
mdr(\mathcal{C})={\rm min}\exp(\mathcal{C})={\rm min}\{d_{2},d_{3}\}
.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a plus-one generated curve with the exponents
(d_{2},d_{3})
and level
d
. Then the second Chern number of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}}
is given by the formula:

(6)

c_{2}({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}})=d_{2}(d_{3}-1)+d-d_{3}+1

Proof.

Assume without loss of generality that
d_{2}\leq d_{3}
. Since
d_{2}+d_{3}=\deg(\mathcal{C})
, then
d_{2}=mdr(\mathcal{C})\leq\deg(\mathcal{C})/2
. Let
L_{0}
be a generic line in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
and denote by
(d_{2}^{L_{0}},d_{3}^{L_{0}}),\;d_{2}^{L_{0}}\leq d_{3}^{L_{0}},
the splitting type onto
L_{0}
of the vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}}
. Recall that
d_{2}^{L_{0}}+d_{3}^{L_{0}}=\deg(\mathcal{C})-1
. From
[
2
]
, we know that

(7)

c_{2}({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}})=d_{2}^{L_{0}}d_{3}^{L_{0}}+\nu(\mathcal{C}),

where
\nu(\mathcal{C})
is the defect of the curve
\mathcal{C}
, which is equal to
(d-d_{3}+1)
by
[
6
, Corollary 3.10]
. In order to finish our proof, it remains to connect the generic splitting type
(d_{2}^{L_{0}},d_{3}^{L_{0}})
with the exponents of
\mathcal{C}
.
By
[
2
, Proposition 3.2 (1)]
,
d_{2}^{L_{0}}\leq mdr(\mathcal{C})
, i.e.,
d_{2}^{L_{0}}\leq d_{2}
. There are, according to
[
2
, Proposition 3.2 (2)-(4)]
, three cases to consider, namely:

Case 1:
d_{2}^{L_{0}}<(\deg(\mathcal{C})-2)/2
, and then
d_{2}^{L_{0}}=mdr(\mathcal{C})
.

Case 2:
d_{2}^{L_{0}}\geq(\deg(\mathcal{C})-2)/2
and
\deg(\mathcal{C})=2m
. Then
d_{2}^{L_{0}}=m-1
, which implies that
mdr(\mathcal{C})\in\{m-1,m\}
.

Case 3:
d_{2}^{L_{0}}\geq(\deg(\mathcal{C})-2)/2
and
\deg(\mathcal{C})=2m+1
. Then
d_{2}^{L_{0}}=m
, so
m\leq mdr(\mathcal{C})\leq(2m+1)/2
, i.e.,
mdr(\mathcal{C})=m
.

In all the above cases we get a formula for
d_{2}^{L_{0}}
in terms of
mdr(\mathcal{C})
. In Case 1 and Case 3 we get
d_{2}^{L_{0}}=mdr(\mathcal{C})
. In Case 2 we get either
d_{2}^{L_{0}}=mdr(\mathcal{C})=m-1
or
d_{2}^{L_{0}}=mdr(\mathcal{C})-1=m-1
.
Since
mdr(\mathcal{C})=d_{2},\;d_{2}+d_{3}=\deg(\mathcal{C})
, and
d_{2}^{L_{0}}+d_{3}^{L_{0}}=\deg(\mathcal{C})-1
, in all the above cases formula (
6
) follows from (
7
).
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a plus-one generated curve with exponents
(d_{2},d_{3}),\;d_{2}\leq d_{3},
and level
d
. Let
(d_{2}^{L_{0}},d_{3}^{L_{0}}),d_{2}^{L_{0}}\leq d_{3}^{L_{0}},
be the generic splitting type of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}}
. Then
d^{L_{0}}_{2}=d_{2}
, except possibly when
\deg(\mathcal{C})=2m
, when
d_{2}^{L_{0}}\in\{d_{2}-1,d_{2}\}
.

Proof.

Immediate from the proof of Proposition
2.6
.
∎

In fact, we are able to describe all possible splitting types of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}}
onto an arbitrary line
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
, if
\mathcal{C}
is a plus-one generated curve.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a plus-one generated curve with exponents
(d_{2},d_{3})
and level
d
. Then the splitting type of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}}
onto an arbitrary line
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
is one of the pairs
\{(d_{2},d_{3}-1),(d_{2}-1,d_{3}),(d_{2}-(d-d_{3}+1),d)\}
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as the one of
[
4
, Theorem 4.4]
, which proves a similar result for plus-one generated projective line arrangements. We reproduce the arguments here for completion.

Assume without loss of generality that
d_{2}\leq d_{3}
.
Denote by
(d^{L}_{2},d^{L}_{3}),\;d^{L}_{2}\leq d^{L}_{3}
the the splitting type of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}}
onto the line
L
. Then
d^{L}_{2}+d^{L}_{3}=\deg(\mathcal{C})-1
, see for instance
[
2
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Since
\nu(\mathcal{C})=d-d_{3}+1
(
[
6
, Proposition 3.7]
), by

[
2
, Corollary 3.4]
we have that

(d^{L}_{2},d^{L}_{3})\in\{(d_{2},d_{3}-1),(d_{2}-1,d_{3}),\dots,d_{2}-(d-d_{3}+1,d)\}.

If
d^{L}_{2}\in\{d_{2},d_{2}-1\}
we are done, since
d_{2}+d_{3}=\deg(\mathcal{C})
.
It remains to consider the case
d^{L}_{2}<d_{2}-1
. By Remark
2.4
, the domain of the map
\pi_{L}
from Theorem
2.3
can be identified to the module of derivations
D_{0}(\mathcal{C})
. One can choose the minimal system of generators
\{\theta_{E},\theta_{2},\theta_{3},\psi\}
of
D(\mathcal{C})
from Definition
1.1
such that
\{\theta_{2},\theta_{3},\psi\}
is a minimal set of generators for
D_{0}(\mathcal{C})
, with
\deg(\theta_{2})=d_{2},\;\deg(\theta_{3})=d_{3},\;\deg(\psi)=d
.

Let
\{\theta_{2}^{L},\theta_{3}^{L}\}
be a homogeneous basis of the codomain of
\pi_{L}
,
\Gamma_{*}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}|_{L})
, which is a free graded module of rank
2
over
\overline{S}\coloneqq S/(\alpha_{L})
, with
\deg(\theta_{2}^{L})=d^{L}_{2}
and
\deg(\theta_{3}^{L})=d^{L}_{3}
.
Since
d^{L}_{2}<d_{2}-1
, we get
d^{L}_{3}>d_{3}
, hence

\pi_{L}(\theta_{2}),\pi_{L}(\theta_{3})
are in the
\overline{S}
, a submodule of
\Gamma_{*}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}|_{L})
generated by
\theta_{2}^{L}
.

Let
\pi_{L}(\psi)=f\theta^{L}_{2}+g\theta^{L}_{3}
, for some
f,g\in\overline{S}
. Then necessarily
g\neq 0
, otherwise we would have
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\Coker(\pi_{L})=\infty
, a contradiction with Theorem
2.3
. Moreover, we must have
\deg(g)=0
, otherwise we can construct an infinite series of elements from
\Gamma_{*}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}|_{L})
which are not in the image of
\pi_{L}
, for instance elements of type
m\theta^{L}_{2}+\theta^{L}_{3}
, with
m\in\overline{S}
arbitrary monomial, again a contradiction to the finite dimensionality over
\mathbb{C}
of
\Coker(\pi_{L})
, stated in Theorem
2.3
.

In conclusion,
g\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
, i.e.,
\deg\theta^{L}_{3}=d
.
∎

Remark 2.9
.

If
\mathcal{C}
is a reduced projective curve, then the first Chern number of
{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}}
is given by the formula
c_{1}({\mathcal{E}}_{\mathcal{C}})=1-\deg(\mathcal{C})

.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be reduced curves in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cup\{L\}
, where
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
is a line not included in
\mathcal{C^{\prime}}
having the equation
\alpha_{L}=0
. Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cap L
.
Then we have the exact sequence of derivation modules, where
\rho
is defined by taking modulo
\alpha_{L}
:

(8)

0\rightarrow D(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\overset{\cdot\alpha_{L}}{\rightarrow}D(\mathcal{C})\overset{\rho}{\rightarrow}D(\mathcal{C}")

Proof.

Let
f_{\mathcal{C}}\in\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]
be the defining polynomial of
\mathcal{C}
. We may assume that
\alpha_{L}=z
. Then
f_{\mathcal{C}}=zf
with
f\in\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]
, where
f
is the defining polynomial for
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. It is easy to check that if
\theta^{\prime}\in D(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
, then
z\theta^{\prime}\in D(\mathcal{C})
. Also, notice that the multiplication map by
z
is obviously injective.

Let
\theta\in D(\mathcal{C})
. Then
f_{\mathcal{C}}
divides
\theta(f_{\mathcal{C}})
. This implies, since
{\rm gcd}(z,f)=1
, that
z
divides
\theta(z)
and
f
divides
\theta(f)
. Let

\theta=f_{1}\partial_{x}+f_{2}\partial_{y}+f_{3}\partial_{z}\in\Ker(\rho).

Since
z
divides
\theta(z)
, we get that
z\,|\,f_{3}
. Now from

\rho(\theta)=f_{1}(x,y,0)\partial_{x}+f_{2}(x,y,0)\partial_{y}=0

it follows that
z\,|f_{1}
and
z\,|f_{2}
. Then
\theta=z\theta^{\prime}
. Since
f
divides
\theta(f)=z\theta^{\prime}(f)
and
{\rm gcd}(z,f)=1
, it follows that
f
divides
\theta^{\prime}(f)
, so
\theta^{\prime}\in D(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
. In conclusion, we obtain
\im(\cdot z)=\im(\cdot\alpha_{L})\supset\Ker(\rho)
. The converse inclusion is obvious, so we have
\im(\cdot\alpha_{L})=\Ker(\rho)
.

Let us check that the map
\rho
is well-defined, i.e., for
\theta\in D(\mathcal{C})
we have
\rho(\theta)\in D(\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime})
.
For
X\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}
having a defining equation
\gamma_{X}=0,\;\gamma_{X}\in\mathbb{C}[x,y]
, we have that
\gamma_{X}
divides
f(x,y,0)
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
be maximal such that

f(x,y,0)=\gamma_{X}^{k}h,\;h\in\mathbb{C}[x,y],\;{\rm gcd}(\gamma_{X},h)=1.

Evaluating at
z=0
the expression
f\,|\,\theta(f)
one gets
f(x,y,0)\,|\,\rho(\theta)(f(x,y,0))
.
Denote furthermore
\overline{\theta}:=\rho(\theta)
. We have the divisibility

\gamma_{X}^{k}h\,|\,\overline{\theta}(\gamma_{X}^{k}h)

and we need to show that

\gamma_{X}\,|\,\overline{\theta}(\gamma_{X}).

Observe that
\overline{\theta}(\gamma_{X}^{k}h)=\gamma_{X}^{k}\overline{\theta}(h)+h\overline{\theta}(\gamma_{X}^{k})
and since
{\rm gcd}(\gamma_{X},h)=1
, one has
\gamma_{X}^{k}\,|\,\overline{\theta}(\gamma_{X}^{k})
. If
k=1
, then we are done. Otherwise, if
k>1
, we compute
\overline{\theta}(\gamma_{X}^{k})=k\gamma_{X}^{k-1}\overline{\theta}(\gamma_{X})
to conclude that
\gamma_{X}\,|\,\overline{\theta}(\gamma_{X})
. Since this holds for an arbitrary
X\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}
, it follows
\overline{\theta}\in D(\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime})
.
∎

3.
Main results

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a plus-one generated reduced curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with the exponents
(d_{2},d_{3})
and level
d
such that there exists a line
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
that is an irreducible component of
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cup\{L\}
,
L
is not included in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
, and
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}|+\epsilon(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},L)=d+1
, where
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cap L
. Then
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is free with the exponents
(d_{2}-1,d_{3}-1)
.

Proof.

A computation of the second Chern number of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
, using (
5
)
and (
2
) and taking into account that
d+1=|\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}|+\epsilon(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},L)
, shows that

c_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}})=(d_{2}-1)(d_{3}-1)

To prove that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is free, it would be enough to show that there is a line
K
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that the splitting type of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
onto
K
is equal to
(d_{2}-1,d_{3}-1)
. We will show that
(d_{2}-1,d_{3}-1)
is the generic splitting type of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}
onto a line. Let
K
be a generic line, with corresponding splitting type
(d_{2}^{K},d_{3}^{K}),\;d_{2}^{K}\leq d_{3}^{K}
.

We may assume without loss of generality that
d_{2}\leq d_{3}
.
Since
d_{2}^{K}+d_{3}^{K}=d_{2}+d_{3}-2
and
(d_{2}-1)(d_{3}-1)\geq d_{2}^{K}d_{3}^{K}
by Theorem
2.3
, it follows that

d_{2}^{K}\leq d_{2}-1

From
[
2
, Proposition 3.2 (2)-(4)]
, we know that
d_{2}^{K}=mdr(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
except possibly when
\deg(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=2m
and
d_{2}^{K}=m-1
or
\deg(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=2m+1
and
d_{2}^{K}=m
. We will treat separately these cases.

First assume
d_{2}^{K}=mdr(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
. By
[
5
, Proposition 3.1]
,
mdr(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})\in\{d_{2}-1,d_{2}\}
. If
mdr(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=d_{2}-1
then
d_{2}^{K}=d_{2}-1
, so
(d_{2}^{K},d_{3}^{K})=(d_{2}-1,d_{3}-1)
, which proves the claim.

If
mdr(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=d_{2}
, then
d_{2}^{K}=d_{2}
, but this contradicts the inequality
d_{2}^{K}\leq d_{2}-1
, so the case
mdr(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=d_{2}
cannot happen.

Case
\deg(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=2m,\;d_{2}^{K}=m-1
. Then
m\leq d_{2}
, but
\deg(\mathcal{C})=2m+1
, so
d_{2}=m
. It follows
(d_{2}^{K},d_{3}^{K})=(d_{2}-1,d_{3}-1)
, which proves the claim.

Case
\deg(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=2m+1,\;d_{2}^{K}=m
. Then
m+1\leq d_{2}
, but
\deg(\mathcal{C})=2m+2
, so
d_{2}=m+1
. Again it follows that
(d_{2}^{K},d_{3}^{K})=(d_{2}-1,d_{3}-1)
, which completes our proof.

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a reduced free curve with the exponents
(d_{2},d_{3})
, not necessarily ordered, such that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cup L
for some line
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2},\;L\not\subset\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cap L
.

1.

If
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is free, then
\exp(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=(d_{2},d_{3}-1)
and
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}|+\epsilon(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},L)=d_{2}+1
.

2.

If
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is plus-one generated, then
\exp(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=(d_{2},d_{3})
and the level
d=\deg(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})-|\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}|-\epsilon(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},L)
.

Proof.

Assume first
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is free with the exponents
(d^{\prime}_{2},d^{\prime}_{3}),\,d^{\prime}_{2}\leq d^{\prime}_{3}
. Notice that
d_{2}+d_{3}=d^{\prime}_{2}+d^{\prime}_{3}+1

and recall that by
[
5
, Proposition 3.1]
,
mdr(\mathcal{C})=\min\{d_{2},d_{3}\}\in\{d^{\prime}_{2},d^{\prime}_{2}+1\}
. It follows that

(d^{\prime}_{2},d^{\prime}_{3})\in\{(d_{2}-1,d_{3}),\;(d_{2},d_{3}-1)\}.

Assume without loss of generality that
(d^{\prime}_{2},d^{\prime}_{3})=(d_{2},d_{3}-1)
, since
(d_{2},d_{3})
are unordered. Then the Chern polynomial has the following form

c(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}})=1+(-d^{\prime}_{2}-d^{\prime}_{3})t+(d^{\prime}_{2}d^{\prime}_{3})t^{2}=1+(-d_{2}+1-d_{3})t+d_{2}(d_{3}-1)t^{2}

and by Lemma
2.2
,

c(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(-1))=1+(-1-d_{2}-d_{3})t+(d_{2}d_{3}+d_{3})t^{2}.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is free with the exponents
(d_{2},d_{3}),\;c(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}})=1+(-d_{2}-d_{3})t+d_{2}d_{3}t^{2}
.
Compute (
5
)
for the exact sequence (
2
) to conclude
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}|+\epsilon(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},L)=d_{2}+1
.

Assume next that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is plus-one generated with
\exp(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=(d^{\prime}_{2},d^{\prime}_{3}),\,d^{\prime}_{2}\leq d^{\prime}_{3},
and level
d
. Choose an order among the exponents of
\mathcal{C}
, say
d_{2}\leq d_{3}
.

We have
d_{2}+d_{3}=d^{\prime}_{2}+d^{\prime}_{3}
and, by
[
5
, Proposition 3.1]
,
mdr(\mathcal{C})=d_{2}\in\{d^{\prime}_{2},d^{\prime}_{2}+1\}
. Assume that
d_{2}=d^{\prime}_{2}+1
. Since
f_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}\;|\;f_{\mathcal{C}}
, we have an inclusion between modules of derivations:

D(\mathcal{C})\subset D(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})

Since
\mathcal{C}
is free with the exponents
(d_{2},d_{3})
, the module
D(\mathcal{C})
is spanned by
\theta_{E}
and two other homogeneous derivations
\theta_{2},\theta_{3}
of degrees
d_{2}=d^{\prime}_{2}+1
, respectively
d_{3}=d^{\prime}_{3}-1
. The module of derivations
D(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
of the plus-one generated curve
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is spanned by
\theta_{E}
and three other homogeneous derivations
\theta^{\prime}_{2},\theta^{\prime}_{3},\psi
of degrees
d^{\prime}_{2},\;d^{\prime}_{3}
, respectively
d
. Due to degree constraints, both
\theta_{2},\theta_{3}
are in the submodule of
D(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})
spanned by
\theta_{E}
and
\theta^{\prime}_{2}
, but this implies that
\theta_{2},\theta_{3},\theta_{E}
are not S-independent, contradiction.
Then necessarily
d_{2}=d^{\prime}_{2}
, so

\exp(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=(d_{2},d_{3})

It remains to prove the formula for the level
d
.

By Proposition
2.6
,

c(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}})=1+(1-d_{2}-d_{3})t+(d_{2}(d_{3}-1)+d-d_{3}+1)t^{2}.

By Lemma
2.2
,

c(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(-1))=1+(-1-d_{2}-d_{3})t+(d_{2}d_{3}+d+1)t^{2}.

Compute again (
5
)
for the exact sequence (
2
), and the equality
d=\deg(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})-|\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}|-\epsilon(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},L)
follows.

∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be a plus-one generated curve with the exponents
(d_{2},d_{3})
and level
d=\deg(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})-|\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}|-\epsilon(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},L),\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cap L
for some line
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that
L
is not included in
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. Then
\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cup L
is free with the exponents
(d_{2},d_{3})
.

Proof.

Using Proposition
2.6
and Lemma
2.2
, we get that

c(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}^{\prime}}(-1))=1+(-1-d_{2}-d_{3})t+(d_{2}d_{3}+d+1)t^{2}.

A computation of the second Chern number of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}
, using (
5
)
and (
2
) and taking into account that
d=\deg(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})-|\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}|-\epsilon(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},L)
, shows that

c_{2}(\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}})=d_{2}d_{3}

By Theorem
2.3
, to prove that
\mathcal{C}
is free with the exponents
(d_{2},d_{3})
, it would be enough to show that the generic splitting type of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}
onto a projective line is
(d_{2},d_{3})
.

Assume without loss of generality
d_{2}\leq d_{3}
. Let
(d_{2}^{K},d_{3}^{K}),\,d_{2}^{K}\leq d_{3}^{K},
be the splitting type of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}
onto some generic line
K\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
. In any case, from Theorem
2.3
it follows that

d_{2}d_{3}\geq d_{2}^{K}d_{3}^{K}

Since
d_{2}+d_{3}=d_{2}^{K}+d_{3}^{K}
, it follows that
d_{2}^{K}\leq d_{2}
. From
[
2
, Proposition 3.2 (2)-(4)]
(see the proof of Proposition
2.6
for details) we know that
d_{2}^{K}=mdr(\mathcal{C})
except possibly when
\deg(\mathcal{C})=2m
and
d_{2}^{K}=m-1
or
\deg(\mathcal{C})=2m+1
and
d_{2}^{K}=m
. We will treat separately these cases.

For now, assume
d_{2}^{K}=mdr(\mathcal{C})
.
Since
mdr(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=d_{2}
, then
mdr(\mathcal{C})\in\{d_{2},d_{2}+1\}
. But
mdr(\mathcal{C})=d_{2}+1
is not possible, since
mdr(\mathcal{C})=d_{2}^{K}\leq d_{2}
. So
mdr(\mathcal{C})=d_{2}=d_{2}^{K}
. But this means that the generic splitting type of
\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}
onto a line is
(d_{2},d_{3})
, and we are done.

Case
\deg(\mathcal{C})=2m,\;d_{2}^{K}=m-1
. Notice that
d_{2}+d_{3}=2m-1
, so
d_{2}\leq m-1
. But
d_{2}\geq d_{2}^{K}=m-1
, so
d_{2}=m-1
. It follows
(d_{2}^{K},d_{3}^{K})=(d_{2},d_{3})
, and we are done.

Case
\deg(\mathcal{C})=2m+1,\;d_{2}^{K}=m
. Notice that
d_{2}+d_{3}=2m
, so
d_{2}\leq m
. In fact,
d_{2}=m
since
d_{2}\geq d_{2}^{K}
. Then
(d_{2}^{K},d_{3}^{K})=(d_{2},d_{3})
, and this concludes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C}^{\prime},\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime})
be a triple with respect to a line
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
, such that
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}|+\epsilon(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},L)=d_{2}+1
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is free of exponents
(d_{2},d_{3})
.

(2)

\mathcal{C}
is free of exponents
(d_{2},d_{3}+1)
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
Assume
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is free with the exponents
(d_{2},d_{3})
. We already know that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cup L
must be either free or plus-one generated. If it is free, then, since
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}|+\epsilon(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},L)=d_{2}+1
, its exponents are
(d_{2},d_{3}+1)
by
[
3
, Theorem 1.3]
.

If
\mathcal{C}
would be plus-one generated, then its exponents would be
(d_{2}+1,d_{3}+1)
and level
d
such that
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}|+\epsilon(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},L)=d+1
. But then
d=d_{2}
, so
d<d_{2}+1
, i.e., the level is strictly smaller than one of the exponents of the plus-one generated
\mathcal{C}
, a contradiction.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
Assume
\mathcal{C}
is free of exponents
(d_{2},d_{3}+1)
. We already know that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
must be either free or plus-one generated. If it is free, then, since
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}|+\epsilon(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},L)=d_{2}+1
, by Theorem
3.2
, it must be free with the
exponents
(d_{2},d_{3})
.

If
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
would be plus-one generated plus-one generated, then, again by Theorem
3.2
, its exponents would be
(d_{2},d_{3})
and its level
d=\deg(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})-|\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}|-\epsilon(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},L)
. Then
d=d_{3}-1<d_{3}
, contradiction.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a reduced curve such that
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cup L
for some line
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2},\;L\not\subset\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.The following are equivalent:

1.

\mathcal{C}
is free with exponents
(d_{2},d_{3})
.

2.

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is either

(i)

free with
\exp(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=(d_{2},d_{3}-1)
and
|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cap L|+\epsilon(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},L)=d_{2}+1
or

(ii)

plus-one generated with
\exp(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=(d_{2},d_{3})
and level
d=\deg(\mathcal{C})-1-|\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\cap L|-\epsilon(\mathcal{C}^{\prime},L)
.

Proof.

1.\Rightarrow 2.
By
[
3
, Theorem 1.1]
and Theorem
3.2
.

2.\Rightarrow 1.
If (i) holds, then the claim follows from Theorem
3.4
. If (ii) holds, then the claim follows from Theorem
3.3
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a reduced curve. The following are equivalent:

1.

\mathcal{C}
is free with exponents
(d_{2},d_{3})
.

2.

For some line
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
that is not an irreducible component of
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{C}\cup L
is either

(i)

free with exponents
(d_{2},d_{3}+1)
and
|\mathcal{C}\cap L|+\epsilon(\mathcal{C},L)=d_{2}+1
or

(ii)

plus-one generated with exponents
(d_{2}+1,d_{3}+1)
and level
d=\epsilon(\mathcal{C},L)+|\mathcal{C}\cap L|-1
.

3.

For any line
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
that is not an irreducible component of
\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{C}\cup L
is either

(i)

free with exponents free with exponents
(d_{2},d_{3}+1)
and
|\mathcal{C}\cap L|+\epsilon(\mathcal{C},L)=d_{2}+1
or

(ii)

plus-one generated with exponents
(d_{2}+1,d_{3}+1)
and level
d=\epsilon(\mathcal{C},L)+|\mathcal{C}\cap L|-1
.

Proof.

1.\Rightarrow 3.
follows from Theorem
1.2
.
3.\Rightarrow 2.
is obvious. Let us prove
2.\Rightarrow 1.
If (i) holds, then the claim follows from Theorem
3.4
. If (ii) holds, then the claim follows from Theorem
3.1
.
∎

Shockwave-Enhanced Floquet Engineering in Relativistic Quasiparticles
Takashi Oka

Abstract
We investigate Floquet engineering of three-dimensional Dirac fermions driven by propagating waves, identifying distinct quantum states and phase transitions in the time-like, light-like, and space-like regimes. Notably, we uncover a novel regime where Floquet Weyl bands emerge and transition into Type-II Weyl states as the wave speed nears the Fermi velocity. Using Floquet-Bloch theory, we demonstrate that Lorentz contraction strongly amplifies Floquet band modulation effects, leading to a shockwave-like state synchronized with the wave motion. These findings extend beyond electrons to quasiparticles with relativistic dispersions, opening new avenues for dynamic band engineering in quantum materials.

Floquet-Bloch theorem for electrons in propagating waves.
—
We consider a non-interacting electron system described by a one-body wave function
\ket{\Psi(\bm{x},t)}
satisfying
the Schrödinger equation
i\partial_{t}\ket{\Psi}=(H_{0}(\bm{x})+V(\bm{x},t))\ket{\Psi}
.
Here,
H_{0}
determines the unperturbed dispersion, expanded in Fourier space as

H_{0}(\bm{x})=\int d\bm{k}H_{0}(\bm{k})e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}}
.
We study a propagating wave perturbation

\displaystyle V(x)=V_{1}\cos(Qx-\omega t)+V_{2}\sin(Qx-\omega t)

(2)

with wavenumber
Q
, frequency
\omega

and speed
v=\omega/Q
, defining a
space-time reciprocal vector
given by
q^{\mu}=(\omega,Q,0,0)
.
Here on, we use the four-vector notation with
the space-time and momentum-energy variables

x^{\mu}=(t,\bm{x})
and
k^{\mu}=(\varepsilon,\bm{k})
,
and product
k\cdot x=k^{\mu}x_{\mu}=\varepsilon t-\bm{k}\cdot\bm{x}
.
We introduce a Floquet-Bloch decomposition of the wave function as

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi(\bm{x},t)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e^{-ik\cdot x}\ket{\Phi(q\cdot x)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e^{-ik\cdot x}\sum_{n=\infty}^{\infty}\ket{\phi^{n}}e^{-inq\cdot x},

(3)

where the Floquet-Bloch state
\ket{\Phi(q\cdot x)}
has a periodicity of

q\cdot x\sim q\cdot x+2\pi
(
N
is a normalization factor).
We employ Sambe’s space-time picture denoting

\ket{\Phi_{\bm{k}}}=^{t}(\ldots,\ket{\phi^{n-1}_{\bm{k}}},\ket{\phi^{n}_{\bm{k}}},\ket{\phi^{n+1}_{\bm{k}}},\ldots)

as an infinite dimensional vector with Fourier modes as components.
Then, the time dependent Schrödinger equation is recasted to the
Floquet-Bloch equation (see also Ref.
Yang et al. 2024
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\bm{k}}\ket{\Phi_{\alpha\bm{k}}}=\varepsilon_{\alpha\bm{k}}\ket{\Phi_{\alpha\bm{k}}},

(4)

with the Floquet-Bloch Hamiltonian (
V=(V_{1}-iV_{2})/2
)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{\bm{k}}^{nn^{\prime}}=(H_{\bm{k}+n\bm{q}}-nq_{0})\delta_{nn^{\prime}}+V^{\dagger}\delta_{n-1n^{\prime}}+V\delta_{n+1n^{\prime}}.

(5)

This result shows that Floquet replica bands emerge along the space-time reciprocal vector direction (see Fig.
1
(a)).
The computed Floquet-Bloch spectrum for a 1D Dirac electron under propagating wave driving (
H(k_{x})=\sigma_{x}k_{x}
,
V(x)=V_{0}\sigma_{y}\cos(Qx-\omega t)
) is shown in Fig.
1
(b),
demonstrating band hybridization and gap opening.
The resulting Floquet states exhibit modified dispersion under propagating waves.

Geometric aspect of the Floquet-Bloch states in propagating waves.
—
The formulation of the geometric properties of the Floquet-Bloch states follows those of the Bloch states
Xiao et al. 2010
.
We can define the Berry connection four vector

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha\bm{k}}^{\mu}=(\mathcal{A}_{\alpha\bm{k}}^{0},\mathcal{A}_{\alpha\bm{k}}^{i})

of the
\alpha
-th state,
whose components are defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\alpha\bm{k}}^{\mu}(q\cdot x)=\bra{\Phi_{\alpha\bm{k}}(q\cdot x)}\eta^{\mu\nu}(i\partial_{k^{\nu}})\ket{\Phi_{\alpha\bm{k}}(q\cdot x)}.

(6)

The Berry curvature of the Floquet-Bloch state can be defined by
Xiao et al. 2010

\displaystyle\Omega^{\mu\nu}_{\alpha\bm{k}}(q\cdot x)=\partial_{k_{\mu}}\mathcal{A}_{\alpha\bm{k}}^{\nu}(q\cdot x)-\partial_{k_{\nu}}\mathcal{A}_{\alpha\bm{k}}^{\mu}(q\cdot x),

(7)

which is gauge invariant.
We can formulate the
Floquet TKNN formula
Oka and Aoki 2009
as

\displaystyle\sigma_{xy}=e^{2}\int\frac{d\bm{k}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}(\bm{k})\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{ds}{2\pi}\Omega^{xy}_{\alpha\bm{k}}(s)

(8)

using the distribution function
f_{\alpha}(\bm{k})

Schüler et al. 2020
;
Oka and Aoki 2009
;
Dehghani et al. 2014
;
Dehghani et al. 2015
;
Genske and Rosch 2015
;
Seetharam et al. 2019
,
with
s=q\cdot x
and
d
is the spatial dimension.
This result extends the Chern number formulation to propagating-wave-driven systems.
Floquet-Bloch states also exhibit non-adiabatic polarization current
Resta 1992
;
King-Smith and Vanderbilt 1993
and geometric phase effects
Berry 1984
.
Integrating the Berry connection Eqn. (
8
) along the wave vector
Q
, we define:

\displaystyle P^{x}_{\alpha\bm{k}_{\perp}}(q\cdot x)=\int_{0}^{Q}dk_{x}\bra{\Phi_{\alpha\bm{k}}(q\cdot x)}(-i\partial_{k^{x}})\ket{\Phi_{\alpha\bm{k}}(q\cdot x)},

(9)

\displaystyle\gamma_{\alpha\bm{k}}=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{ds}{2\pi}\bra{\Phi_{\alpha\bm{k}}(s)}(i\partial_{s})\ket{\Phi_{\alpha\bm{k}}(s)},

(10)

whose gauge invariance follows from the periodicity of the Floquet-Bloch state.
Here,
\bm{k}_{\perp}=(0,k_{y},k_{z})
is the crystal momentum
perpendicular to the wave propagation direction
\bm{Q}
.
The geometric phase
\gamma_{\alpha\bm{k}}
is well studied in Floquet systems
and are related to topological pumping (see for
e.g.
, Ref.
Nakagawa et al. 2020
).
Using Eq.(
9
),
we can define the total polarization by

\displaystyle P^{x}_{\rm tot}(Qx-\omega t)=e\int\frac{d\bm{k}_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\sum_{\alpha}f_{\alpha}(\bm{k})P^{x}_{\alpha\bm{k}_{\perp}}(Qx-\omega t),

(11)

whose derivative gives the longitudinal polarization current
J^{x}_{\rm tot}(Qx-\omega t)=\frac{d}{dt}P^{x}_{\rm tot}(Qx-\omega t)
flowing parallel to the propagating wave.

Application to relativistic fermions.
—
We now apply the Floquet-Bloch formalism to relativistic quasiparticles.
Let us consider a 3D Dirac electrons described by a Hamiltonian (see
e.g.,
Ref.
Bjorken and Drell 1964
)

\displaystyle H_{0}(\bm{x})=v_{F}\sum_{i}\Gamma^{i}(\hat{p}_{i}+eA_{i}+eA_{5i}\gamma^{5})+m\gamma^{0}+eA_{0}I+eA_{0}^{5}\gamma^{5},

(12)

with gamma matrices
\gamma^{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&I_{2}\\
I_{2}&0\end{array}\right),\;\Gamma^{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\sigma_{i}&0\\
0&\sigma_{i}\end{array}\right)

and
\gamma^{5}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-I_{2}&0\\
0&I_{2}\end{array}\right)
.
Here,
m
is the mass parameter,
A_{\mu}
denotes external electromagnetic fields, and

A_{5\mu}
denotes the chiral gauge fields that induces Weyl particles
Yan and Felser 2017
.
Let us consider a propagating wave Eq. (
2
) defined by

\displaystyle V=A/2\left(\Gamma^{y}-i\Gamma^{z}\right),

(13)

representing a circularly polarized wave propagating in the
x
-direction with speed
v=\omega/Q
, physically realizable by laser fields
Yoshikawa et al. 2022
or coherent chiral phonons.
Its field strength is given by
A=E/\omega
with
E
being the electric field strength.
The Dirac Hamiltonian Eq. (
12
) is Lorentz covariant where the Fermi velocity plays the role of the speed of light.
We classify the waves as:

•

Time-like (
v>v_{F}
): Wave moves faster than the Fermi velocity.

•

Light-like (
v=v_{F}
): Wave propagates at the Fermi velocity.

•

Space-like (
v<v_{F}
): Wave moves slower than the Fermi velocity.

To simplify the analysis, we transform into a “rest frame”, where the wave becomes static (space-like) or homogeneous (time-like) (Fig.
2
).
The Lorentz boost is:

x^{\prime\mu}=a^{\mu}_{\ \nu}x^{\nu}
,
with
a^{\mu}_{\ \nu}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\gamma&-v\gamma/v_{F}^{2}\\
-v\gamma/v_{F}^{2}&\gamma\end{array}\right)

with
y^{\prime}=y,\;z^{\prime}=z
.
Choosing the boost parameter
\gamma=1/\sqrt{1-v_{F}^{2}/v^{2}}
(
\gamma=1/\sqrt{1-v^{2}/v_{F}^{2}}
) for time (space)-like waves, we obtain the rest-frame expressions:

\displaystyle q^{\prime\mu}=(\omega/\gamma,0,0,0)\quad\mbox{(time-like)}

(14)

\displaystyle q^{\prime\mu}=(0,Q/\gamma,0,0)\qquad\mbox{(space-like)}.

(15)

The physical picture of the boost is Lorentz contraction: Moving object’s periodicity is measured
\gamma
-times longer in its rest frame.
The Floquet-Bloch solution Eq. (
3
) in the lab and rest frames are related by

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_{\alpha\bm{k}}(x)}=D(a^{-1})\ket{\Psi^{\prime}_{\alpha\bm{k}^{\prime}}(ax)}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e^{-i(a^{-1}k^{\prime})\cdot x}D(a^{-1})\ket{\Phi^{\prime}_{\alpha\bm{k}^{\prime}}(q\cdot x)},

(16)

where
D(a)
denotes the Lorentz boost for a Dirac spinor.
Note that
\ket{\Psi^{\prime}(x^{\prime})}
is to be determined by solving the Floquet-Bloch equation Eqn. (
4
)
using fields in the rest frame

\displaystyle A^{\prime}{}^{\mu}=a^{\mu}{}_{\nu}A^{\nu},\;A_{5}^{\prime}{}^{\mu}=a^{\mu}{}_{\nu}A_{5}{}^{\nu},\;F^{\prime\mu\nu}=a^{\mu}{}_{\rho}a^{\nu}{}_{\sigma}F^{\rho\sigma}.

(17)

While generically the matrix
V
transforms according to its representation, our choice Eq. (
13
) is invariant by the
x
-boost.
From Eqn. (
16
), we
find that the quasi-momentum-energy vector
and the Floquet-Bloch state
in the two frames are related by

k=a^{-1}k^{\prime}

and

\ket{\Phi(q\cdot x)}=D(a^{-1})\ket{\Phi^{\prime}(q\cdot x)}
.
The former relation implies that the spectra
in the two frames are related by Lorentz boost
(See Fig.
3
below for an example).

Massive Dirac electron and phase transitions
.—
We now examine Floquet-induced phase transitions in massive Dirac electrons.
The mass term
m
in the Dirac equation competes with the effective chiral gauge field leading to a topological phase transition
as we approach the shockwave limit (Fig.
4
).
In the time-like case, the low-energy Floquet-Bloch spectrum in the rest frame is given by

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\alpha}(\bm{k}^{\prime})=\pm v_{F}\sqrt{(\sqrt{(k_{x}^{\prime})^{2}+m^{2}}\pm A^{\prime}_{5{\rm eff}}{}^{x})^{2}+k_{y}^{\prime 2}+k_{z}^{\prime 2}}.

(21)

A topological phase transition occurs when the Floquet-induced chiral gauge field compensates the mass term, i.e.,
A^{\prime 5}_{\rm{eff},x}=m
.
At this critical point, the Dirac gap closes, and a pair of Weyl nodes emerges at
k_{x}^{\prime}=\pm\sqrt{(A^{\prime}_{5{\rm eff}}{}^{x})-m^{2}}
,
signaling a transition into a Floquet Weyl semimetal
Wang et al. 2014
;
Ebihara et al. 2016
;
Chan et al. 2016
;
Hübener et al. 2017
;
Yoshikawa et al. 2022
;
Bucciantini et al. 2017
;
Hirai et al. 2024
.
In the lab frame, the Weyl nodes are initially type-II
Soluyanov et al. 2015
on the transition line
and eventually tilts back to type-I: This can be understood by the
rest-frame energy slope
\frac{d\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\alpha}(\bm{k}^{\prime})}{dk^{\prime}}=\sqrt{1-(m/A^{\prime}_{5{\rm eff}}{}^{x})^{2}}
that vanishes at gap closing.
When we are close to the shockwave limit, bands become intensively folded
and near adiabatic dynamics

in a slowly varying gapped Hamiltonian is realized.
In the adiabatic limit, the rest-frame state is
\ket{\Psi^{\prime}_{n\bm{k}}(t^{\prime})}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}e^{-i\varepsilon^{\prime}_{n}t^{\prime}}\ket{n(\bm{k}+e\bm{A}(t^{\prime}))}

with the adiabatic basis satisfying
h(\bm{k})\ket{n(\bm{k})}=E_{n}(\bm{k})\ket{n(\bm{k})}
with
h(\bm{k})=v_{F}\bm{\Gamma}\cdot\bm{k}+m\gamma^{0}

and
n=1,\ldots 4
.
The quasi-energy is given as

\varepsilon_{\bm{k}}=\frac{1}{T^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{T^{\prime}}E_{n}(\bm{k}+e\bm{A}(t^{\prime}))dt^{\prime}+\gamma_{n}/T^{\prime}
,
with the Berry phase given by
\gamma_{n}/T^{\prime}=\pm(\omega/\gamma)|A|^{2}/(2(|A|^{2}+m^{2}))
for
\bm{k}=0
.
This expression do not diverge in the shockwave limit and smoothly connects to the Volkov solution at
v=v_{F}

Volkov 1935
.
If the field becomes stronger or frequency larger, inter band tunneling
Higuchi et al. 2017
takes place.
When circularly polarized wave is used, the tunneling is described by the twisted Landau-Zener formula
leading to non-uniform excitation and current generation
Takayoshi et al. 2021
.
On the other hand, in the space like case, the gap is always open at
\bm{k}=0
, while the
bands are tilted and shows a chiral flow (Fig.
4
(iv)) similarly to the 1D case
Yang et al. 2024
.
In this regime, Weyl points that splits in the energy direction can exist if
A_{5{\rm eff}}^{\prime 0}>m
is satisfied in the rest frame.

Discussion.
—
We have demonstrated that Floquet engineering of relativistic fermions in propagating waves leads to a novel class of dynamically tunable band modifications.
By leveraging Lorentz covariance, we classified these waves as time-like, light-like, and space-like, each producing distinct electronic states.
In the shockwave limit, where the Lorentz contraction factor
\gamma
diverges, Floquet effects are dramatically amplified,
resulting in emergent Weyl points and chiral gauge fields.
These results pave the way for dynamically tuning non-equilibrium topological phases, presenting new opportunities for experimental realization in driven quantum materials.
Although we have formulated our theory with 3D Dirac electrons as an example, potential experimental platforms include:
(i) Acoustic wave modulation of relativistic spin excitations and,
(ii) Floquet engineering by chiral phonons.
These findings establish propagating waves as a novel platform for non-equilibrium quantum engineering. Future research on interactions, disorder, and nonperturbative effects will further expand the frontier of Floquet-driven topological matter

2.2.
Parabolic induction.

Let us recall some results on parabolic induction. Let
\Delta(\mathfrak{g})
be the root set of
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to
\mathfrak{h}
, that is
\Delta(\mathfrak{g})\sqcup\{0\}
is the support of the adjoint module
\mathfrak{g}

Recall that a subdivision
\Delta(\mathfrak{g})=\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{+}\sqcup\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{-}
is called a

if and only if
\langle-\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{+}\cup\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{-}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\cap\langle-\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{-}\cup\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{+}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}=\{0\}
. And a

(or

) of

is a subdivision
\Delta(\mathfrak{g})=\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{+}\sqcup\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{0}\sqcup\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{-}

p(\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{+})\cap p(\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{-})=\emptyset,0\notin p(\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{\pm})
, and
p(\Delta(\mathfrak{g})\setminus\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{0})=p(\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{+})\sqcup p(\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{-})
is a triangular decomposition of
p(\Delta(\mathfrak{g})\setminus\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{0}),
where

is the natural projection
\langle\Delta(\mathfrak{g})\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}\to\langle\Delta(\mathfrak{g})\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}/\langle\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{0}\rangle_{\mathbb{R}}
.

If
\Delta(\mathfrak{g})=\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{+}\sqcup\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{0}\sqcup\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{-}
is a parabolic decompostion, set

\mathfrak{g}^{0}:=\mathfrak{h}\oplus(\oplus_{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{0}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}),\,\mathfrak{g}^{\pm}:=\oplus_{\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{g})^{\pm}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

A subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}
of

with

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in\Delta^{\prime}\,|\,\alpha,-\alpha\notin\Gamma\}.

Fix

\lambda\in\mathrm{Supp}(M)

For any

[

(1)

(2)

(2) Since

Lemma 3.3
.

[
22
, Lemma 6]
Let
\lambda\in\mathrm{Supp}(M)
be extremal, and
\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta\in\Delta^{\prime}
, then

(1)

If
\alpha,\beta\in K_{\lambda}
, then
\alpha+\beta\in K_{\lambda}
;

(2)

If
\alpha,\beta\in\bar{K}_{\lambda}
, then
\alpha+\beta\in\bar{K}_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

(1) Suppose to the contrary that
\alpha+\beta\not\in K_{\lambda}
. First, assume that at least one of the two roots
\alpha,\beta
(say
\alpha
) belongs to
\Delta^{\prime\prime}
. Then
\mu=\lambda+\alpha+\beta\in\mathrm{Supp}(M)
,
\alpha\in K_{\lambda},-\alpha\in K_{\mu}
. From Lemma
3.2
,
(\lambda,\alpha)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
and
(\mu,\alpha)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{-}
. Since
(\mu-\lambda,\alpha)=(\alpha+\beta,\alpha)=1
, it is impossible.

Let now
\alpha,\beta\not\in\Delta^{\prime\prime}
. Then without loss of generality, we may assume that
\alpha=-\epsilon_{i}
and
\beta=\epsilon_{j}
. Since
\alpha+\beta=\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i}\not\in K_{\lambda}
, we have
\mu=\lambda+\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i}\in\mathrm{Supp}(M)
. Then
-\epsilon_{i},\epsilon_{j}\in K_{\lambda}
,
\epsilon_{i},-\epsilon_{j}\in K_{\mu}
, which from Lemma
3.2
is impossible.

(2) Suppose to the contrary that
\alpha+\beta\in K_{\lambda}
. Then
\lambda+\alpha+\beta\not\in\mathrm{Supp}(M),\lambda+\alpha,\lambda+\beta\in\mathrm{Supp}(M)
. As in the proof of (a), we consider two cases. First, let
\alpha\in\Delta^{\prime\prime}
. If
-\alpha\in\Gamma
, then
\beta\in K_{\lambda+\alpha}=K_{\lambda}
, which is a contradiction. On the other hand
\alpha\not\in\Gamma
since
\alpha\in K_{\lambda+\beta}
. Therefore from Lemma
3.2

(\lambda+\beta,\alpha)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
. Since
(\beta,-\alpha)=1
, we have
(\lambda,\alpha)\geq 1
. Furthermore,
\beta\in K_{\lambda+\alpha}\setminus K_{\lambda}
. Since
\lambda
is extremal, one can find
\delta\in K_{\lambda}\setminus K_{\lambda+\alpha}
. Then
\lambda+\alpha+\delta\in\mathrm{Supp}(M)
and
\lambda+\delta\not\in\mathrm{Supp}(M)
, which implies
(\lambda+\alpha+\delta,\alpha)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{-}
. But
(\delta,\alpha)\geq-1
and therefore
(\lambda,\alpha)\in-((\alpha,\alpha)+(\delta,\alpha))+{\mathbb{Z}}_{-}\leq 0
which contradicts to
(\lambda,\alpha)\geq 1
.

Now let
\alpha,\beta\not\in\Delta^{\prime\prime}
, for example,
\alpha=-\epsilon_{i},\beta=\epsilon_{j}

\lambda-\epsilon_{i},\lambda+\epsilon_{j}\in\mathrm{Supp}(M)

\lambda+\epsilon_{j}-\epsilon_{i}\not\in\mathrm{Supp}(M)

-\epsilon_{j}\not\in\Gamma

3.2
(a).∎

Lemma 3.5

[
22
, Lemma 8]

(1)

\Delta^{{}^{\prime}-}\subseteq\Gamma

(2)

(3)

(\alpha,\beta)=0

The first statement is clear. For the second statement, from Lemma

Theorem 3.10

gives a Lie superalgebra homomorphism from

to
\mathcal{K}_{q,n}\otimes U(\mathcal{T})
, which induces an associative superalgebra homomorphism from
U(W_{q,n}\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes A_{q,n}\oplus A_{q,n}))
to
\mathcal{K}_{q,n}\otimes U(\mathcal{T})
with
\mathcal{J}
contained in the kernel. Hence, we have the induced associative superalgebra homomorphism from
\overline{U}
to
\mathcal{K}_{q,n}\otimes U(\mathcal{T})
, which is the inverse map of
\iota
.
∎

Lemma 4.8
.

(1)

\big(\mathfrak{m}\nabla\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes A_{q,n})\big)/\big(\mathfrak{m}^{2}\nabla\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes\mathfrak{m})\big)\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{q,n}\oplus\mathfrak{k}
.

(2)

Any simple module
V
in
\mathscr{B}_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\mathfrak{m}\nabla\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes A_{q,n}))
is annihilated by
\mathfrak{m}^{2}\nabla\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes\mathfrak{m})
.

Proof.

The first statement follows from
\mathfrak{m}\nabla/\mathfrak{m}^{2}\nabla\cong\mathfrak{gl}_{q,n}
, which holds by
[
16
, Lemma 3.7]
.

For the second statement, since
V\in\mathscr{B}_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\mathfrak{m}\nabla\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes A_{q,n}))
, there exists a nonzero weight vector in
V
such that
dv=cv
for some
c\in{\mathbb{C}}
, where
d:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{q}d_{i}+\sum\limits_{i=m+1}^{m+n}d_{i}
. The simplicity of
V
tells us that
V=U(\mathfrak{m}\nabla\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes A_{q,n}))v
. Because the adjoint action of
d
on
\mathfrak{m}\nabla\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes A_{q,n})
is diagonalizable, we know
d
acts diagonalizbly on
V
with eigenvalues in
c+{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
.
(\mathfrak{m}^{2}\nabla\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes\mathfrak{m}))V
is a submodule of
V
with eigenvalues in
c+{\mathbb{N}}
, since
\mathfrak{m}^{2}\nabla\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes\mathfrak{m})
is an ideal. Thus,
(\mathfrak{m}^{2}\nabla\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes\mathfrak{m}))V\neq V
, and is therefore
0
.
∎

We now have the associative superalgebra homomorphism

\pi:\overline{U}\to\mathcal{K}_{q,n}\otimes U(\mathcal{T})\to\mathcal{K}_{q,n}\otimes U(\mathfrak{m}\nabla\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes A_{q,n}))\\
\to\mathcal{K}_{q,n}\otimes U(\big(\mathfrak{m}\nabla\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes A_{q,n})\big)/\big(\mathfrak{m}^{2}\nabla\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes\mathfrak{m})\big))\to\mathcal{K}_{q,n}\otimes U(\mathfrak{gl}_{q,n})\otimes U(\mathfrak{k})

with

\begin{split}\pi(t^{\alpha}\xi_{I})=&t^{\alpha}\xi_{I}\otimes 1\otimes 1,\\
\pi(t^{\alpha}\xi_{I}\partial_{i})=&t^{\alpha}\xi_{I}\partial_{i}\otimes 1\otimes 1+\sum_{s=1}^{q}\partial_{s}(t^{\alpha}\xi_{I})\otimes E_{s,i}\otimes 1+(-1)^{|I|-1}\sum_{l=1}^{n}\partial_{m+l}(t^{\alpha}\xi_{I})\otimes E_{q+l,i}\otimes 1,\\
\pi(x\otimes t^{\alpha}\xi_{I})&=t^{\alpha}\xi_{I}\otimes 1\otimes x,\end{split}

where
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{q},I\subseteq\overline{1,n},i\in\overline{1,q}\cup\overline{m+1,m+n},x\in\mathfrak{k}
. Let
P
be an
\mathcal{K}_{q,n}
module,
M
be a
\mathfrak{gl}_{q,n}
module and
S
be a
\mathfrak{k}
module. Then we have the tensor module
\mathcal{F}(F(P,M),S):=(P\otimes M\otimes S)^{\pi}
over
\hat{\mathfrak{k}}
, which is an
A_{q,n}\hat{\mathfrak{k}}
module, with the action given by

x\cdot(u\otimes v\otimes s):=\pi(x)(u\otimes v\otimes s),\,x\in\overline{U},u\in P,v\in M,s\in S.

Note that as
A_{q,n}W_{q,n}
module,
(P\otimes M\otimes s)^{\pi}\cong F(P,M)
for any
s\in S
. Since

\displaystyle\pi(d_{i})

\displaystyle=d_{i}\otimes 1\otimes 1+1\otimes E_{i,i}\otimes 1,d_{i}\in W_{q,n},

\displaystyle\pi(h)

\displaystyle=1\otimes 1\otimes h,h\in\mathfrak{h}^{\prime},

we know that
\mathcal{F}(F(P,M),S)
is a weight module if
P,M,S
are weight modules over
\mathcal{K}_{q,n},\mathfrak{gl}_{q,n}
and
\mathfrak{k}
, respectively. Moreover,
\mathcal{F}(F(P,M),S)\in\mathscr{B}_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\mathfrak{k})
if and only if
F(P,M)\in\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{h}_{q}}(W_{q,n})
and
S\in\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{k})
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
P,M,S
be simple module over
\mathcal{K}_{q,n},\mathfrak{gl}_{q,n}
and
\mathfrak{k}
, respectively. Then
\mathcal{F}(F(P,M),S)
is a simple
\hat{\mathfrak{k}}
module if and only if one of the following hold

(1)

S
is nontrivial;

(2)

S
is trivial and
F(P,M)
is simple as
W_{q,n}
module.

Proof.

When
S
is trivial the statement is clear. So it suffices to show
\mathcal{F}(F(P,M),S)
is simple over
\hat{\mathfrak{k}}
when
S
is nontrivial, which is suffices to show any
\hat{\mathfrak{k}}
submodule
V
of
\mathcal{F}(F(P,M),S)
is
A_{q,n}
-invariant since
\mathcal{F}(F(P,M),S)
is a simple
A_{q,n}\hat{\mathfrak{k}}
module. For any nonzero
v\in V
, we may write
v=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}v_{i}\otimes s_{i}
with
v_{i}\in P\otimes M,s_{i}\in S
and
s_{i}
’s being linearly independent. From the Theorem of Density, there exists
x\in U(\mathfrak{k})
such that
xs_{i}=\delta_{i,1}s_{i}
. Therefore,
(1\otimes 1\otimes x)v=v_{1}\otimes s_{1}\in V
. From the simplicity of
S
, we get
v_{1}\otimes S\subseteq V
. Then
(\mathfrak{k}\otimes A_{q,n})\cdot(v_{1}\otimes S)=A_{q,n}v_{1}\otimes S=A_{q,n}\cdot(v_{1}\otimes S)\subseteq V
. Similarly, we have
A_{q,n}\cdot(v_{i}\otimes S)\subseteq V
for all
i
, which means that
V
is
A_{q,n}
-invariant.
∎

The following theorem classify all simple bounded
A_{q,n}\hat{\mathfrak{k}}
modules.

Theorem 4.10
.

Any simple bounded
A_{q,n}\hat{\mathfrak{k}}
module is isomorphic to some
\mathcal{F}(F(P,M),S)
for some simple weight
\mathcal{K}_{q,n}
module
P
, some finite dimensional simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{q,n}
module
M
and some simple bounded
\mathfrak{k}
module
S
.

Proof.

D=\mathrm{span}\{t_{j}\cdot\partial_{j}\,|\,j\in\overline{1,q}\cup\overline{m+1,m+n}\}+\mathfrak{H}_{q,n}+\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}
is an abelian Lie super-subalgebra of
\overline{U}
that preserves weight spaces of simple bounded
\overline{U}
module
V
. So there is a homogeneous weight vector
v\in V
that is a common eigenvector of
D
. Then from
\overline{U}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{K}_{q,n}\otimes U(\mathfrak{m}\nabla\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes A_{q,n}))
, we may regard
V
as a module in
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{H}_{q,n}\otimes 1+1\otimes(\mathfrak{H}_{q,n}+\mathfrak{h}^{\prime})}(\mathcal{K}_{q,n}\otimes U(\mathfrak{m}\nabla\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes A_{q,n})))
module, and we only need to show
V
is isomorphic to some
P\otimes M\otimes S
. For any nonzero weight vector
v\in V
, the weight module
\mathcal{K}_{q,n}v
has a simple submodule
P
, which is strictly simple by Lemma
2.4
. Hence,
V\cong P\otimes M^{\prime}
for some simple bounded
\mathfrak{m}\nabla\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes A_{q,n})
module
M^{\prime}
. Since
M^{\prime}
is bounded, it can be viewed as a simple module over
\mathfrak{gl}_{q,n}\oplus\mathfrak{k}
by Lemma
4.8
. Moreover,
M^{\prime}
contains a simple submodule
S
over
\mathfrak{k}
because
M^{\prime}
is bounded. Therefore,
M^{\prime}\cong M\otimes S
for some simple module
M
over
\mathfrak{gl}_{q,n}
. Thus,
V\cong P\otimes M\otimes S
. ∎

Thus, from Lemma
2.6
, Lemma
4.1
, Theorem
4.4
and Theorem
4.10
, we have

Theorem 4.11
.

Any simple module in
\mathscr{B}_{\hat{\mathfrak{h}}}(\hat{\mathfrak{k}})
is isomorphic to one of the following:

(1)

\mathcal{F}(F(P,M),S)
for some nontrivial simple
S\in\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{k})
, some simple
P\in\mathscr{W}_{\mathfrak{H}_{q,n}}(K_{q,n})
and some simple finite dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{q,n}
module
M
;

(2)

a simple module with
\mathfrak{k}\otimes A_{q,n}
acting trivially, hence a simple module in
\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{h}_{q}}(W_{q,n})
.

5.
Main result

In this section, we will give the classification of simple strong Harish-Chandra modules over
W
. First, we have the following property on tensor modules.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
P_{1}
be simple weight
\mathcal{K}_{m}
module,
V_{1}\in\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m})
, and
V_{2}
be a finite-dimensional simple
\mathfrak{gl}_{n}
module. Then
F(P_{1}\otimes\Lambda(n),L(V_{1}\otimes V_{2}))\in\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{h}}(W)
if and only if
F(P_{1},V_{1})\in\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{h}}(W_{m})
if and only if
({\Delta^{\prime\prime}}^{I}_{P_{1}}\sqcup{\Delta^{\prime\prime}}^{-}_{P_{1}})\subset({\Delta^{\prime\prime}}^{F}_{V_{1}}\sqcup{\Delta^{\prime\prime}}^{-}_{V_{1}})
. Here
\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}I}_{P_{1}}=\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\cap\Delta_{P_{1}}^{{}^{\prime}I},\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}F}_{V_{1}}=\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\cap\Delta_{V_{1}}^{{}^{\prime}F},\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}-}_{P_{1}}=\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\cap\Delta^{{}^{\prime}-}_{P_{1}},\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}-}_{V_{1}}=\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\cap\Delta^{{}^{\prime}-}_{V_{1}}
.

Proof.

Lemma follows from
F(P_{1}\otimes\Lambda(n),L(V_{1}\otimes V_{2}))_{\gamma}=\bigoplus\limits_{\alpha+\beta=\gamma}(P_{1})_{\alpha}\otimes\Lambda(n)\otimes L(V_{1}\otimes V_{2})_{\beta},F(P_{1},V_{1})_{\gamma}=\bigoplus\limits_{\alpha+\beta=\gamma}(P_{1})_{\alpha}\otimes(V_{1})_{\beta}
and

\dim(V_{1})_{\beta}\dim V_{2}\leqslant\dim L(V_{1}\otimes V_{2})_{\beta}\leqslant\dim\Lambda(\mathfrak{gl}^{-1})\dim(V_{1})_{\beta}\dim V_{2}.

The last statement follows from
[
13
, Theorem 3.5]
.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Any simple module in
\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{h}}(W)
is isomorphic to the unique simple submodule of a tensor module
F(P,M)
that is in
\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{h}}(W)
, where
P
is a simple weight
\mathcal{K}
module, and
M
is a simple weight
\mathfrak{gl}
module.

Proof.

Let
V\in\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{h}}(W)
be simple. Without loss of generality, we may assume
\epsilon_{i}\in\Delta_{V}^{{}^{\prime}I},i\in\overline{1,q},\epsilon_{i}\in\Delta_{V}^{{}^{\prime}+},i\in\overline{q+1,p+q},\epsilon_{i}\in\Delta_{V}^{{}^{\prime}-},i\in\overline{p+q+1,m}
.

From Theorem
3.9
,
V
is isomorphic to the unique simple quotient of
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{W}N
, where
\mathfrak{p}
is a parabolic subalgebra whose Levi subalgebra is isomorphic to
\hat{\mathfrak{k}}=W_{q,n}\ltimes(\mathfrak{k}\otimes A_{q,n})
with
\mathfrak{k}
a Levi subalgebra of
\mathfrak{gl}_{p}\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_{m-p-q}
(see Theorem
3.10
), and
N
is a simple weight
\hat{\mathfrak{k}}
module satisfying

(1)
\mathrm{Supp}(N)=\lambda+\Delta(\hat{\mathfrak{k}})
for any
\lambda\in\mathrm{Supp}(N)
;

(2)
\dim N_{\lambda}=\dim N_{\mu},\forall\lambda,\mu\in\mathrm{Supp}(N)
.

If
q=m
, then the statement follows from
[
16
, Theorem 4.4]
. Now suppose
q<m
. Let
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{p}\cap\mathfrak{gl}
. The Levi subalgebra of
\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{gl}_{q,n}
. We need to show the unique simple quotient of
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{W}N
is isomorphic to the unique simple submodule of a tensor module, where from Theorem
4.11
,
N
is isomorphic to the unique nonzero simple submodule of
\mathcal{F}(F(P,M),S)
with simple
P\in\mathscr{W}_{\mathfrak{H}_{q,n}}(\mathcal{K}_{q,n}),S\in\mathscr{B}_{\mathfrak{h}\cap\mathfrak{k}}(\mathfrak{k})
and
M
a simple finite dimensional
\mathfrak{gl}_{q,n}
module.

Let
\mathfrak{n}
be the nilradical of
\mathfrak{p}
. Let
U
be the
1
-dimensional
\mathfrak{k}
module of weight
\sum\limits_{i=q+1}^{p+q}\epsilon_{i}
, and
\hat{S}
be the unique simple quotient of
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}}^{\mathfrak{gl}}(M\otimes(S\otimes U))
. Let
\tilde{P}
be the
\mathcal{K}
module defined by
P\otimes A_{p,0}^{\sigma}\otimes A_{m-p-q,0}
. We claim that
F(\tilde{P},\hat{S})^{\mathfrak{p}-top}\cong\mathcal{F}(F(P,M),S)
, where
F(\tilde{P},\hat{S})^{\mathfrak{p}-top}:=\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathrm{Supp}(F(\tilde{P},\hat{S}))\\
\lambda+\alpha\notin\mathrm{Supp}(F(\tilde{P},\hat{S})),\forall\alpha\in\Delta(\mathfrak{n})\end{subarray}}F(\tilde{P},\hat{S})_{\lambda}
is the
\mathfrak{p}
-top of
F(\tilde{P},\hat{S})
. Indeed, from

(5.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Supp}(\tilde{P})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Supp}(P)-\sum\limits_{i=q+1}^{p+q}\epsilon_{i}-\sum\limits_{i=q+1}^{p+q}{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}\epsilon_{i}+\sum\limits_{i=p+q+1}^{m}{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}\epsilon_{i},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Supp}(\hat{S})

\displaystyle\subset\mathrm{Supp}(S)+\sum\limits_{i=q+1}^{p+q}\epsilon_{i}+\mathrm{Supp}(M)-\mathrm{Supp}(U(\mathfrak{n}\cap\mathfrak{gl})),

we get

\mathrm{Supp}(\mathcal{F}(F(P,M),S))\subset\mathrm{Supp}(F(\tilde{P},\hat{S}))\subset\mathrm{Supp}(\mathcal{F}(F(P,M),S))-\mathrm{Supp}(U(\mathfrak{n}\cap\mathfrak{gl})).

For any
\mu\in\mathrm{Supp}(F(\tilde{P},\hat{S}))
, write
\mu=\alpha+\beta
with
\alpha\in\mathrm{Supp}(\hat{S}),\beta\in\mathrm{Supp}(\tilde{P})
. Note that
\hat{S}
is simple, if
\alpha\notin\mathrm{Supp}(M\otimes(S\otimes U))
, then there exists
\alpha_{1}\in\mathrm{Supp}(\mathfrak{n}\cap\mathfrak{gl})
such that
\alpha+\alpha_{1}\in\mathrm{Supp}(\hat{S})
. Also, from (
5.1
), if
\beta\notin\mathrm{Supp}(P)-\sum\limits_{i=q+1}^{p+q}\epsilon_{k}
, then there exists
\beta_{1}\in\{\epsilon_{j},-\epsilon_{k}\,|\,j\in\overline{q+1,p+q},k\in\overline{p+q+1,m}\}\subseteq\mathrm{Supp}(\mathfrak{n}\cap\mathfrak{gl})
, such that
\beta+\beta_{1}\in\mathrm{Supp}(\tilde{P})
.
Thus,
\mu\in\mathrm{Supp}(F(\tilde{P},\hat{S})^{\mathfrak{p}-top})
if and only if
\mu\in\mathrm{Supp}(\mathcal{F}(F(P,M),S))
. Then it is straightforward to verify

F(\tilde{P},\hat{S})=(P\otimes 1\otimes 1)\otimes(M\otimes(S\otimes U))\cong F(P,M)\otimes S.

So, we have a monomorphism
\varphi
from
N
to
F(\tilde{P},\hat{S})^{\mathfrak{p}-top}
. Hence, we have the induced homomorphism
\tilde{\varphi}
of
W
modules from
\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{W}N
to
F(\tilde{P},\hat{S})
. Note that from (
5.1
),
\mathfrak{n}
acts locally nilpotently on
F(\tilde{P},\hat{S})
, hence
R\cap F(\tilde{P},\hat{S})^{\mathfrak{p}-top}\neq 0
, where
R
is the unique simple submodule of
F(\tilde{P},\hat{S})
. On the other hand, if
\mathcal{F}(F(P,M),S)
is not simple, its unique simple submodule is contained in any nonzero submodule. So, the nonzero submodule
R\cap F(\tilde{P},\hat{S})^{\mathfrak{p}-top}
of
F(\tilde{P},\hat{S})^{\mathfrak{p}-top}
contains the unique simple submodule
\varphi(N)
of
F(\tilde{P},\hat{S})^{\mathfrak{p}-top}
. Therefore,
\tilde{\varphi}(\mathrm{Ind}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{W}N)\subseteq R
. Thus, from the simplicity of
R
,
V\cong R
.
∎

Now we can state our main result, which follows from Lemma
2.6
, Lemma
5.1
and Theorem
5.2
.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
V\in\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{h}}(W_{m,n})
be simple. Then
V
is the unique simple submodule of some tensor module
F(P,M)
that is in
\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{h}}(W_{m,n})
. More precisely, one of the following holds.

(i)

V
is isomorphic to
F(P,M)
for a simple weight
\mathcal{K}_{m,n}
-module
P
and a simple
M\in\mathscr{F}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\mathfrak{gl})
, such that
({\Delta^{\prime\prime}}^{I}_{P}\sqcup{\Delta^{\prime\prime}}^{-}_{P})\subset({\Delta^{\prime\prime}}^{F}_{M}\sqcup{\Delta^{\prime\prime}}^{-}_{M})
and such that
V
is not isomorphic to a finite-dimensional fundamental representation. Here
\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}I}_{P}=\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\cap\Delta_{P}^{{}^{\prime}I},\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}F}_{M}=\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\cap\Delta_{M}^{{}^{\prime}F},\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}-}_{P}=\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\cap\Delta^{{}^{\prime}-}_{P},\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}-}_{M}=\Delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\cap\Delta^{{}^{\prime}-}_{M}
.

(ii)

V
is isomorphic to
{\rm diff}(F(P,M))
for a simple weight
\mathcal{K}_{m,n}
-module
P
and a finite-dimensional fundamental representation
M
that is not isomorphic to
{\rm Str}_{m,n}
or
\Pi({\rm Str}_{m,n})
.

(iii)

V
is trivial, which is the unique simple submodule of
F(A,A[0])
or
F(A,\Pi(A[0]))
.

On the N N -waves hierarchy with constant boundary conditions. Spectral properties


Abstract
The paper is devoted to N N -wave equations with constant boundary conditions related to symplectic Lie algebras. We study the spectral properties of a class of Lax operators L L , whose potentials Q ⁡ ( x , t ) Q(x,t) tend to constants Q ± Q_{\pm} for x → ± ∞ x\to\pm\infty . For special choices of Q ± Q_{\pm} we outline the spectral properties of L L , the direct scattering transform and construct its fundamental analytic solutions. We generalise Wronskian relations for the case of CBC – this allows us to analyse the mapping between the scattering data and the x x -derivative of the potential Q x Q_{x} . Next, using the Wronskian relations we derive the dispersion laws for the N N -wave hierarchy and describe the NLEE related to the given Lax operator.

Introduction

The three-wave resonant interactions equations are one of the most important nonlinear models that appeared
at the early stages of development of the inverse scattering method (ISM). They have numerous applications in physics and have attracted the attention of the
scientific community over the last few decades. Such models describe a special class of wave-wave interactions in nonlinear media, where the nonlinear
dynamics is regarded as a perturbation of linear wave propagation. This explains why they find numerous applications in physics, from plasma physics (radio frequency heating, laser-plasma interactions, plasma instabilities), fluid dynamics
(water waves’ interaction), acoustics (light-acoustic interactions), nonlinear optics (frequency conversion, parametric amplification,
and stimulated Raman scattering) to quantum field theory (the Lee model of weak interactions) and solid state physics (Brillouin scattering).

In the case of CBC, the first important constraint which ensures that the problem is solvable consists in the requirement that the asymptotic
operators:

\begin{split}L_{\pm}&\equiv i\frac{\partial\psi_{\pm}}{\partial x}+U_{\pm}(\lambda)\psi_{\pm}=0,\qquad U_{\pm}(\lambda)=Q_{\pm}-\lambda J\end{split}

(18)

have the same spectra. Here
Q_{\pm}=\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}Q(x,t)
. In particular this condition means that
U_{\pm}(\lambda)

must have the same eigenvalues, i.e. have the same characteristic polynomials.

The next important step is to choose the asymptotic values of
Q_{\pm}=\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}Q(x,t)
so that
the above constraint is satisfied. Without being exhaustive we will consider a few examples.

We start with the generic case, when

\begin{split}U_{1,\pm}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}(q_{\alpha,\pm}E_{\alpha}+s_{\alpha,\pm}E_{-\alpha})-\lambda J.\end{split}

(19)

The spectrum of the asymptotic operators
L_{\pm}
is determined by the eigenvalues of the corresponding
U_{1,\pm}(\lambda)
.
So we need the characteristic polynomial
C_{1}(z,\lambda)=\det(z\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.3pt\normalsize 1}-U_{1,\pm}(\lambda))
of
U_{1,\pm}(\lambda)
which comes out to be rather
complicated. One can relate to each such polynomial an algebraic curve, which in this case has genus 3.

Next we consider less complicated case with

\begin{split}U_{2,\pm}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}-\lambda a_{1}&q_{4,\pm}&q_{3,\pm}&0\\
s_{4,\pm}&-\lambda a_{2}&0&-q_{3,\pm}\\
s_{3,\pm}&0&\lambda a_{2}&q_{4,\pm}\\
0&-s_{3,\pm}&s_{4,\pm}&\lambda a_{1}\end{array}\right).\end{split}

(20)

Now the characteristic polynomial is much simpler:

\begin{split}C_{2}(z,\lambda)=z^{4}-((a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2})\lambda^{2}-2(\rho_{3}^{2}+\rho_{4}^{2}))z^{2}+a_{1}^{2}a_{2}^{2}\lambda^{4}-2a_{1}a_{2}(\rho_{3}^{2}-\rho_{4}^{2})\lambda^{2}+2\rho_{3}^{2}\rho_{4}^{2}.\end{split}

(21)

Here and below
\rho_{k}^{2}=-|q_{k,\pm}|^{2}
.
Now the corresponding curve has genus 1, i.e. the eigenvalues of
L_{2,\pm}
will be expressed in terms of Jacobi elliptic functions.

The third case that we will consider is

\begin{split}U_{\pm}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}-\lambda a_{1}&0&0&q_{1,\pm}\\
0&-\lambda a_{2}&q_{2,\pm}&0\\
0&s_{2,\pm}&\lambda a_{2}&0\\
s_{1,\pm}&0&0&\lambda a_{1}\end{array}\right).\end{split}

(22)

The characteristic polynomial now can be factorized into:

\begin{split}C(z,\lambda)=(z^{2}-\lambda^{2}a_{1}^{2}+\rho_{1}^{2})(z^{2}-\lambda^{2}a_{2}^{2}+\rho_{2}^{2}).\end{split}

(23)

The corresponding curve now is a direct product of two genus 0 curves. This is the simplest nontrivial case which we
will focus on below. Each of this curves is related to a Riemannian surface determined by
\sqrt{a_{1}^{2}\lambda^{2}-\rho_{1}^{2}}

and
\sqrt{a_{2}^{2}\lambda^{2}-\rho_{2}^{2}}
.

Indeed, the eigenvalues of the asymptotic operators are
\pm z_{k}(\lambda)
where

\begin{split}z_{k}(\lambda)=\sqrt{a_{k}^{2}\lambda^{2}-\rho_{k}^{2}}.\end{split}

(24)

The corresponding eigenvectors of
U_{\pm}
can be combined into the matrix

\begin{split}\phi_{0,\pm}&=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}-q_{1,\pm}&0&0&\frac{1}{2z_{1}}\\
0&-q_{2,\pm}&\frac{1}{2z_{2}}&0\\

-\lambda a_{1}+z_{1}&0&0&\frac{\lambda a_{1}+z_{1}}{2q_{1,\pm}z_{1}}\end{array}\right),\\
\phi^{-1}_{0,\pm}&=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}-\frac{\lambda a_{1}+z_{1}}{2q_{1,\pm}z_{1}}&0&0&\frac{1}{2z_{1}}\\
0&-\frac{\lambda a_{2}+z_{2}}{2q_{2,\pm}z_{2}}&\frac{1}{2z_{2}}&0\\
0&-\lambda a_{2}+z_{2}&q_{2,\pm}&0\\
-\lambda a_{1}+z_{1}&0&0&q_{1,\pm}\end{array}\right).\end{split}

2.3
Jost solutions and FAS of
L

The starting point in developing the direct scattering transform for (
1
) are the eigenfunctions (the so-called Jost solutions) of L (
2
),
determined uniquely by their asymptotics for
x\to\pm\infty
:

\begin{split}\lim_{x\to\infty}\psi(x,t,\lambda)e^{i\mathcal{J}(\lambda)x}\hat{\phi}_{0,+}=\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.3pt\normalsize 1},\qquad\lim_{x\to-\infty}\phi(x,t,\lambda)e^{i\mathcal{J}(\lambda)x}\hat{\phi}_{0,-}=\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.3pt\normalsize 1}.\end{split}

(27)

The scattering matrix
T(t,\lambda)
is introduced by:

\begin{split}\phi(x,t,\lambda)=\psi(x,t,\lambda)T(t,\lambda).\end{split}

(28)

If we now introduce:

\begin{split}X_{+}(x,t,\lambda)=\psi(x,t,\lambda)e^{i\mathcal{J}(\lambda)x}\hat{\phi}_{0,+},\qquad X_{-}(x,t,\lambda)=\phi(x,t,\lambda)e^{i\mathcal{J}(\lambda)x}\hat{\phi}_{0,-},\end{split}

(29)

then it is not difficult to check that
X_{\pm}
satisfy the equation:

\begin{split}i\frac{\partial X_{\pm}}{\partial x}+(Q(x,t)-Q_{\pm})X_{\pm}(x,t,\lambda)+\left[Q_{\pm}-\lambda J,X_{\pm}(x,t,\lambda)\right]=0.\end{split}

(30)

Multiplying eq. (
30
) by
\hat{\phi}_{0,\pm}
on the left and by
\phi_{0,\pm}
on the
right we obtain:

\begin{split}i\frac{\partial\tilde{X}_{\pm}}{\partial x}+\tilde{U}_{\pm}(x,t,\lambda)\tilde{X}_{\pm}(x,t,\lambda)-\left[\mathcal{J}(\lambda),\tilde{X}_{\pm}(x,t,\lambda)\right]=0,\end{split}

(31)

where
\tilde{X}_{\pm}\equiv\hat{\phi}_{0,\pm}X_{\pm}\phi_{0,\pm}
and

\begin{split}\tilde{U}_{\pm}(x,t,\lambda)=\hat{\phi}_{0,\pm}(Q(x,t)-Q_{\pm})\phi_{0,\pm}.\end{split}

(32)

From eqs. (
31
) one can derive the following integral equations for the Jost solutions:

\begin{split}\tilde{X}_{\pm}=\hbox{\small 1\kern-3.3pt\normalsize 1}+i\int_{\pm\infty}^{x}dy\;e^{-i\mathcal{J}(\lambda)(x-y)}\tilde{U}_{\pm}(y,t,\lambda)\tilde{X}_{\pm}(y,t,\lambda)e^{i\mathcal{J}(\lambda)(x-y)},\end{split}

(33)

or in components we have:

\begin{split}(\tilde{X}_{\pm})_{jk}=\delta_{jk}+i\int_{\pm\infty}^{x}dy\;e^{-i(\mathcal{J}_{j}-\mathcal{J}_{k})(x-y)}\left(\tilde{U}_{\pm}(y,t,\lambda)\tilde{X}_{\pm}(y,t,\lambda)\right)_{jk}.\end{split}

(34)

It is natural to assume that the potential
Q(x,t)
is smooth enough and that
Q(x,t)-Q_{\pm}
tend to 0
for
x\to\pm\infty
faster than any polynomial of
x
. Therefore eqs. (
34
) have solutions for all

\lambda
, for which
{\mbox{Im}}(\mathcal{J}_{j}-\mathcal{J}_{k})=0
. Then the exponential factors in (
33
) are
bounded and the integrals in the right hand side of (
33
) converge due to the above condition on
Q(x,t)
.
Thus the Jost solutions of
L
are well defined on the lines in the complex
\lambda
-plane for
which
{\mbox{Im}}(\mathcal{J}_{j}-\mathcal{J}_{k})=0
, which constitute the continuous spectrum of
L
.

Let us now outline the possibility to extend analytically in
\lambda
some of the columns of the Jost solutions.
Such extension for the
j
-th column of
\tilde{X}_{\pm}
in the region
\lambda\in\mathcal{A}_{\pm}
will be possible if one can ensure that all exponential factors

\mathcal{E}_{jk}(\lambda)=\exp(-i(\mathcal{J}_{j}-\mathcal{J}_{k})(x-y))
fall off for
x-y\to\pm\infty
and
\lambda\in\mathcal{A}_{\pm}
. We will define
\mathcal{A}_{\pm}
as follows:

\begin{split}\mathcal{A}_{+}\equiv\{{\mbox{Im}}(\mathcal{J}_{j}-\mathcal{J}_{k})>0,\quad j<k\},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{-}\equiv\{{\mbox{Im}}(\mathcal{J}_{j}-\mathcal{J}_{k})<0,\quad j<k\}.\end{split}

(35)

It is easy to check that the first columns of
\psi(x,t,\lambda)
and
\phi(x,t,\lambda)
allow analytic extensions for
\lambda\in\mathcal{A}_{-}
and

\lambda\in\mathcal{A}_{+}
respectively; analogously, the last columns of
\psi(x,t,\lambda)
and
\phi(x,t,\lambda)
allow analytic extensions for
\lambda\in\mathcal{A}_{+}
and
\lambda\in\mathcal{A}_{-}
respectively. As to the rest of the columns of the Jost solutions we find, that some of their exponential factors
fall off, while the others blow up for
x-y\to\pm\infty
. Thus they do not allow analytic extensions in
\lambda
.

The construction of FAS however is possible using Shabat’s method
[
50
,
51
]
, see also
[
54
,
20
]
. It consists in modifying the integral equations (
34
) into:

\displaystyle(\tilde{Y}_{+})_{jk}

\displaystyle=i\int_{\infty}^{x}dy\;e^{-i(\mathcal{J}_{j}-\mathcal{J}_{k})(x-y)}\left(\tilde{U}_{+}(y,t,\lambda)\tilde{Y}_{+}(y,t,\lambda)\right)_{jk},

\displaystyle\quad j

\displaystyle<k;

(36)

\displaystyle(\tilde{Y}_{+})_{jk}

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}+i\int_{-\infty}^{x}dy\;e^{-i(\mathcal{J}_{j}-\mathcal{J}_{k})(x-y)}\left(\tilde{U}_{-}(y,t,\lambda)\tilde{Y}_{+}(y,t,\lambda)\right)_{jk},

\displaystyle\quad j

\displaystyle\geq k;

and

\displaystyle(\tilde{Y}_{-})_{jk}

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}+i\int_{-\infty}^{x}dy\;e^{-i(\mathcal{J}_{j}-\mathcal{J}_{k})(x-y)}\left(\tilde{U}_{-}(y,t,\lambda)\tilde{Y}_{-}(y,t,\lambda)\right)_{jk},

\displaystyle\quad j

\displaystyle\geq k;

(37)

\displaystyle(\tilde{Y}_{-})_{jk}

\displaystyle=i\int_{\infty}^{x}dy\;e^{-i(\mathcal{J}_{j}-\mathcal{J}_{k})(x-y)}\left(\tilde{U}_{+}(y,t,\lambda)\tilde{Y}_{-}(y,t,\lambda)\right)_{jk},

\displaystyle\quad j

\displaystyle<k.

The integral equations (
36
) and (
37
) give rise to two FAS
\chi^{+}(x,t,\lambda)
and
\chi^{-}(x,t,\lambda)
.
It is well known that any two fundamental solutions of the same operator
L
are linearly related. That means that we can
find the relations between
\chi^{\pm}(x,t,\lambda)
and the Jost solutions of
L
. This can be done by comparing their asymptotics
for
x\to\pm\infty
with the asymptotics of the Jost solutions. Skipping the details we obtain:

\begin{split}\chi^{+}(x,t,\lambda)=\phi(x,t,\lambda)S^{+}(t,\lambda)=\psi(x,t,\lambda)T^{-}(t,\lambda)D^{+}(\lambda),\\
\chi^{-}(x,t,\lambda)=\phi(x,t,\lambda)S^{-}(t,\lambda)=\psi(x,t,\lambda)T^{+}(t,\lambda)D^{-}(\lambda),\end{split}

(38)

where the factors
S^{\pm}(t,\lambda)
,
T^{\pm}(t,\lambda)
and
D^{\pm}(\lambda)
are related to the scattering matrix
T(t,\lambda)
by:

\begin{split}T(t,\lambda)=T^{-}(t,\lambda)D^{+}(\lambda)\hat{S}^{+}(t,\lambda)=T^{+}(t,\lambda)D^{-}(\lambda)\hat{S}^{-}(t,\lambda).\end{split}

(39)

Let us note that from the integral equations (
36
) and (
37
) it follows that the factors
S^{+}(t,\lambda)
and

T^{+}(t,\lambda)
are upper-triangular with units on the diagonal; likewise,
S^{-}(t,\lambda)
and
T^{-}(t,\lambda)
are lower-triangular
with units on the diagonal. The matrices
D^{\pm}(\lambda)
are diagonal. As we will find below,
D^{\pm}(\lambda)
are time independent;
they are generating functions of the integrals of motion of the corresponding NLEE. Keeping in mind that the Jost solutions, as well
as the FAS of
L
must belong to the group
SP(2r)
we find that the factors
S^{\pm}(t,\lambda)
,
T^{\pm}(t,\lambda)
and
D^{\pm}(\lambda)

must have the form:

\displaystyle S^{\pm}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}\boldsymbol{\tau}^{\pm}_{\alpha}E_{\pm\alpha}\right),

\displaystyle\quad T^{\pm}(t,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{+}}\boldsymbol{\rho}^{\mp}_{\alpha}E_{\pm\alpha}\right),

(40)

\displaystyle D^{\pm}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\sum_{j=1}^{r}d_{j}^{\pm}(\lambda)H_{j}\right).

The problem of splitting the scattering matrix into triangular and diagonal factors (
39
) is known also as Gauss decomposition.
It has unique solution for all simple Lie algebras, see e.g. the appendix of
[
18
]
. As a results all the coefficients
\tau^{\pm}(t,\lambda)
,

\rho^{\pm}(t,\lambda)
and
d_{j}^{\pm}(\lambda)
cab be expressed explicitly through the matrix elements of the scattering matrix
T(t,\lambda)
.

We can derive two more FAS slightly modifying the integral equations (
36
) and (
37
)

\displaystyle(\tilde{Y}^{\prime}_{+})_{jk}

\displaystyle=i\int_{\infty}^{x}dy\;e^{-i(\mathcal{J}_{j}-\mathcal{J}_{k})(x-y)}\left(\tilde{U}_{+}(y,t,\lambda)\tilde{Y}^{\prime}_{+}(y,t,\lambda)\right)_{jk},

\displaystyle\quad j

\displaystyle\leq k;

(41)

\displaystyle(\tilde{Y}_{+}^{\prime})_{jk}

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}+i\int_{-\infty}^{x}dy\;e^{-i(\mathcal{J}_{j}-\mathcal{J}_{k})(x-y)}\left(\tilde{U}_{-}(y,t,\lambda)\tilde{Y}^{\prime}_{+}(y,t,\lambda)\right)_{jk},

\displaystyle\quad j

\displaystyle>k;

and

\displaystyle(\tilde{Y}^{\prime}_{-})_{jk}

\displaystyle=\delta_{jk}+i\int_{-\infty}^{x}dy\;e^{-i(\mathcal{J}_{j}-\mathcal{J}_{k})(x-y)}\left(\tilde{U}_{-}(y,t,\lambda)\tilde{Y}^{\prime}_{-}(y,t,\lambda)\right)_{jk},

\displaystyle\quad j

\displaystyle>k;

(42)

\displaystyle(\tilde{Y}^{\prime}_{-})_{jk}

\displaystyle=i\int_{\infty}^{x}dy\;e^{-i(\mathcal{J}_{j}-\mathcal{J}_{k})(x-y)}\left(\tilde{U}_{+}(y,t,\lambda)\tilde{Y}^{\prime}_{-}(y,t,\lambda)\right)_{jk},

\displaystyle\quad j

\displaystyle\leq k.

The relations of these FAS to the Jost solutions are slightly different from (
38
)

\begin{split}\chi^{+,\prime}(x,t,\lambda)=\phi(x,t,\lambda)S^{+}(t,\lambda)\hat{D}^{+}(\lambda)=\psi(x,t,\lambda)T^{-}(t,\lambda),\\
\chi^{+,\prime}(x,t,\lambda)=\phi(x,t,\lambda)S^{-}(t,\lambda)\hat{D}^{-}(\lambda)=\psi(x,t,\lambda)T^{+}(t,\lambda).\end{split}

(43)

Note that the diagonal factors
D^{\pm}(\lambda)
are analytic functions of
\lambda
for
\lambda\in\mathcal{A}_{\pm}
.

3
Wronskian relations for Lax operators with CBC

There is substantial difference between the NLEEs with vanishing boundary conditions and the NLEE with CBC.
The potentials
Q(x,t)
of the Lax operators in both cases may be considered as local coordinates of the relevant phase space.
For vanishing boundary conditions the corresponding phase space
\Phi_{\rm VBC}
is linear, while for the CBC case the space

\Phi_{\rm CBC}
is
nonlinear
. As we shall see below, this fact substantially changes the dispersion law of the
NLEE.

Another problem that arises from it, is the interpretation of the ISM as a generalised Fourier transform. This idea was proposed in the seminal AKNS paper

[
2
]
; in the next two decades it was shown to be valid to a large class of Lax operators, see
[
18
,
19
,
23
]
. In the case of vanishing boundary conditions,
one was able to derive expansions for the potential
Q(x,t)
over the “squared solutions” of
L
. Of course, such a generalised Fourier transform hold true only
for linear spaces. In fact, the completeness of the ‘squared solutions‘ was derived also for the Zakharov-Shabat system with CBC

[
43
]
, but no expansion of
Q(x,t)
and description of the class of NLEE s could be presented.

Our idea is to use generalized Wronskian relations, which will allow us to study the mapping between
x
-derivative of the potential

Q_{x}(x,t)
and the scattering data. Note, that
Q_{x}(x,t)
is an element from a linear space. Therefore its expansion over the ‘squared
solutions‘ of
L
is legitimate and can be used for the description of the
N
-wave hierarchy.

3.1
Wronskian relations for
Q_{x}

The importance of the Wronskian relations for the analysis of the mappings
\mathcal{F}:Q(x,t)\to\mathcal{T}
between
the potential of
L
and the scattering data has been noted long ago by Calogero and Degasperis
[
4
,
5
,
8
,
9
]
.
They were based on the basic relations

\begin{split}\left.i\left(\hat{\psi}J\psi\right)\right|_{x=-\infty}^{\infty}&=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{\psi}[J,Q(x,t)]\psi\;dx,\\
\left.i\left(\hat{\psi}J\delta\psi\right)\right|_{x=-\infty}^{\infty}&=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{\psi}\delta Q(x,t)\psi\;dx.\end{split}

(44)

Calogero and Degasperis
[
4
,
5
]
also generalized these relations which allowed them to describe
the classes of Bäcklund transformations for the relevant NLEE.

However their approach can not be applied directly for the equations with CBC.
The difficulties come from the fact that the asymptotics of the Jost solutions (
27
)
are more complicated than the ones for VBC. Here we propose generalizations of
Calogero and Degasperis approach which allows one to treat also CBC.
One of the important facts is that we are using the FAS instead of the Jost solutions.
The second issue is that we use a modification of (
44
), namely:

\begin{split}\left.i\left(\hat{\chi}(Q-\lambda J)\chi\right)\right|_{x=-\infty}^{\infty}&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\hat{\chi}iQ_{x}\chi\;dx,\\
\mbox{tr}\,\left.i\left(\hat{\chi}(Q-\lambda J)\chi\right)E_{\alpha}\right|_{x=-\infty}^{\infty}&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mbox{tr}\,iQ_{x}\chi E_{\alpha}\hat{\chi}\;dx=-i\big[\hskip-1.99168pt\big[\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}Q_{x},e_{\alpha}(x,\lambda)\big]\hskip-1.99168pt\big],\end{split}

(45)

where

\begin{split}e_{\alpha}^{\pm}&=\pi_{j}\chi^{\pm}E_{\alpha}\hat{\chi}^{\pm}(x,\lambda),\qquad\pi_{j}=\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}\mathrm{ad\,}_{J},\\
\big[\hskip-1.99168pt\big[X,Y\big]\hskip-1.99168pt\big]&=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\mbox{tr}\,(X,[J,Y]).\end{split}

(46)

The limits in the l.h.side must be expressed by the scattering data,
and now the limits
Q_{\pm}
do not come up
.
Indeed, the l.h.side of (
45
) is expressed using (
38
) as follows:

\begin{split}&\lim_{x\to\infty}i\left(\hat{\chi}(Q-\lambda J)\chi\right)=-i\hat{D}^{\pm}\hat{T}^{\mp}\mathcal{J}(\lambda)T^{\mp}(t,\lambda)D^{\pm}(\lambda),\end{split}

(47)

and

\begin{split}&\lim_{x\to-\infty}i\left(\hat{\chi}(Q-\lambda J)\chi\right)=-i\hat{S}^{\mp}\mathcal{J}(\lambda)S^{\pm}(t,\lambda).\end{split}

(48)

In a similar manner, for the second pair of FAS we have:

\begin{split}&\lim_{x\to\infty}i\left(\hat{\chi^{\prime}}(Q-\lambda J)\chi^{\prime}\right)=-i\hat{T}^{\mp}\mathcal{J}(\lambda)T^{\mp}(t,\lambda),\end{split}

(49)

and

\begin{split}&\lim_{x\to-\infty}i\left(\hat{\chi}(Q-\lambda J)\chi\right)=-i\hat{D}^{\pm}\hat{S}^{\pm}\mathcal{J}(\lambda)S^{\pm}(t,\lambda)D^{\pm}.\end{split}

(50)

Let us now multiply both sides of (
45
) by
E_{\alpha}
and take a Killing form with
E_{\mp\alpha}
.
The left hand sides becomes:

\begin{split}\left.i\left\langle\left(\hat{\chi}^{\pm}(Q-\lambda J)\chi^{\pm},E_{\mp\beta}\right\rangle\right)\right|_{x=-\infty}^{\infty}&=i\left\langle\hat{S}^{\pm}\mathcal{J}(\lambda))S^{\pm}(\lambda)E_{\mp\beta}\right\rangle\\
&=i\tau_{\beta}^{\pm}(\lambda,t),\\
\left.i\left\langle\left(\hat{\chi}^{\pm}(Q-\lambda J)\chi^{\pm},E_{\pm\beta}\right\rangle\right)\right|_{x=-\infty}^{\infty}&=-i\left\langle\hat{D}^{\pm}\hat{T}^{\mp}\mathcal{J}(\lambda))T^{\mp}(\lambda)D^{\pm}E_{\pm\beta}\right\rangle\\
&=-i\rho_{\beta}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)\exp(\pm\vec{d}^{\pm}(\lambda),\beta).\end{split}

(51)

Similarly, using
\chi^{\pm,\prime}
we have:

\begin{split}\left.i\left\langle\left(\hat{\chi}^{\pm,\prime}(Q-\lambda J)\chi^{\pm,\prime},E_{\mp\beta}\right\rangle\right)\right|_{x=-\infty}^{\infty}&=i\left\langle D^{\pm}\hat{S}^{\pm}\mathcal{J}(\lambda))S^{\pm}(\lambda)\hat{D}^{\pm}(\lambda)E_{\mp\beta}\right\rangle\\
&=i\tau_{\beta}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)\exp(\pm\vec{d}^{\pm}(\lambda),\beta),\\
\left.i\left\langle\left(\hat{\chi}^{\pm,\prime}(Q-\lambda J)\chi^{\pm,\prime},E_{\pm\beta}\right\rangle\right)\right|_{x=-\infty}^{\infty}&=-i\left\langle\hat{T}^{\mp}\mathcal{J}(\lambda))T^{\mp}(\lambda)E_{\pm\beta}\right\rangle\\
&=-i\rho_{\beta}^{\pm}(\lambda,t).\end{split}

(52)

The r.h.side of (
45
) provides skew-scalar product between
\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}
and the corresponding ‘squared solutions‘ of
L
:

\begin{split}e_{\pm\beta}^{\pm}=\pi_{J}\chi^{\pm}E_{\pm\beta}\hat{\chi}^{\pm},\qquad e_{\pm\beta}^{\pm,\prime}=\pi_{J}\chi^{\pm,\prime}E_{\pm\beta}\hat{\chi}^{\pm,\prime}.\end{split}

(53)

Indeed, from eqs. (
51
) we get the following relations:

\displaystyle i\rho_{\alpha}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)e^{\pm(\vec{d}^{\pm},\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\big[\hskip-1.99168pt\big[\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}Q_{x},e^{\pm}_{\pm\alpha}(x,\lambda)\big]\hskip-1.99168pt\big],

\displaystyle\quad\rho_{\alpha}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathcal{J}(\lambda),T^{\mp}E_{\pm\alpha}\hat{T}^{\mp}(\lambda)\right\rangle,

(54)

\displaystyle i\tau_{\alpha}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=-\big[\hskip-1.99168pt\big[\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}Q_{x},e_{\mp\alpha}^{\pm}\big]\hskip-1.99168pt\big],

\displaystyle\quad\tau_{\alpha}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathcal{J}(\lambda),S^{\pm}E_{\mp\alpha}\hat{S}^{\pm}(\lambda)\right\rangle,

and

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\big[\hskip-1.99168pt\big[\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}Q_{x},e^{\pm,\prime}_{\pm\alpha}(x,\lambda)\big]\hskip-1.99168pt\big],

\displaystyle\quad\rho_{\alpha}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathcal{J}(\lambda),T^{\mp}E_{\pm\alpha}\hat{T}^{\mp}(\lambda)\right\rangle,

(55)

\displaystyle\tau_{\alpha}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)e^{\mp(\vec{d}^{\pm},\alpha)}

\displaystyle=-\big[\hskip-1.99168pt\big[\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}Q_{x},e_{\mp\alpha}^{\pm,\prime}\big]\hskip-1.99168pt\big],

\displaystyle\quad\tau_{\alpha}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\mathcal{J}(\lambda),S^{\pm}E_{\mp\alpha}\hat{S}^{\pm}(\lambda)\right\rangle.

3.2
Wronskian relations for
\delta Q

We put restrictions on
\delta Q
so that they preserve the characteristic polynomial of
U_{\pm}
, i.e.

\begin{split}Q_{\pm}+\delta Q_{\pm}-\lambda J=-\widehat{\tilde{u}}_{0,\pm}\mathcal{J}(\lambda)\tilde{u}_{0,\pm}.\end{split}

(56)

In fact we need
\delta Q\to 0
for
x\to\pm\infty
.

For the Wronskian relations we need also the equation for
\delta\chi
:

\begin{split}i\frac{\partial\delta\chi}{\partial x}+(Q-\lambda J)\delta\chi(x,t,\lambda)+\delta Q\chi(x,t,\lambda)=0.\end{split}

(57)

Then we consider

\begin{split}\left.i\hat{\chi}\delta\chi(x,t,\lambda)\right|_{-\infty}^{\infty}&=i\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\;\left(\hat{\chi}_{x}\delta\chi+\hat{\chi}(\delta\chi)_{x}\right)\\
&=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\;\hat{\chi}\delta Q\chi(x,t,\lambda).\end{split}

(58)

Multiplying eq. (
58
) on the right by
E_{\alpha}
and taking the Killing form we find:

\begin{split}\left.\mbox{tr}\,\left(i\hat{\chi}\delta\chi(x,t,\lambda)E_{\alpha}\right)\right|_{-\infty}^{\infty}&=-\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\;\mbox{tr}\,\left(\hat{\chi}\delta Q\chi(x,t,\lambda)E_{\alpha}\right)\\
&=\big[\hskip-1.99168pt\big[\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}\delta Q,e_{\alpha}^{\pm}(x,\lambda)\big]\hskip-1.99168pt\big].\end{split}

(59)

Thus from eqs. (
59
) we get the following relations:

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{\alpha}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)e^{\pm(\vec{d}^{\pm},\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\big[\hskip-1.99168pt\big[\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}\delta Q,e^{\pm}_{\pm\alpha}(x,\lambda)\big]\hskip-1.99168pt\big],

\displaystyle\quad\delta\rho_{\alpha}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\hat{T}^{\mp}(\lambda)\delta T^{\mp},E_{\pm\alpha}\right\rangle,

(60)

\displaystyle\delta\tau_{\alpha}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=-\big[\hskip-1.99168pt\big[\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}\delta Q,e_{\mp\alpha}^{\pm}(x,\lambda)\big]\hskip-1.99168pt\big],

\displaystyle\quad\delta\tau_{\alpha}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\hat{S}^{\pm}(\lambda)\delta S^{\pm},E_{\mp\alpha}\right\rangle,

and

\displaystyle\delta\rho_{\alpha}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\big[\hskip-1.99168pt\big[\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}\delta Q,e^{\pm,\prime}_{\pm\alpha}(x,\lambda)\big]\hskip-1.99168pt\big],

\displaystyle\quad\delta\rho_{\alpha}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\hat{T}^{\mp}(\lambda)\delta T^{\mp},E_{\pm\alpha}\right\rangle,

(61)

\displaystyle\delta\tau_{\alpha}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)e^{\pm(\vec{d}^{\pm},\alpha)}

\displaystyle=-\big[\hskip-1.99168pt\big[\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}\delta Q,e_{\mp\alpha}^{\pm,\prime}(x,\lambda)\big]\hskip-1.99168pt\big],

\displaystyle\quad\delta\tau_{\alpha}^{\pm}(\lambda,t)

\displaystyle=\left\langle\hat{S}^{\pm}(\lambda)\delta S^{\pm},E_{\mp\alpha}\right\rangle.

3.3
The
t
-dependence of the scattering matrix

Here we need the second operator of the Lax pair:

\begin{split}M\phi&\equiv\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+\left(\sum_{k=0}^{N-2}V_{k}(x,t)\lambda^{k}+\lambda^{N-1}(Q-\lambda J)\right)\phi(x,t,\lambda)=0.\end{split}

(62)

We will also need the limits for
x\to\pm\infty
, namely:

\begin{split}\lim_{x\to\pm\infty}V_{k}(x,t)&=0,\qquad k=1,2,\dots,N-2;\\
M_{\rm\pm}\phi_{\rm\pm}&=i\frac{\partial\phi_{\rm\pm}}{\partial t}+\lambda^{N-1}(Q_{\pm}-\lambda J)\phi_{\rm\pm};\\
\phi_{-}&=\hat{u}_{0,-}e^{-i\mathcal{J}x},\qquad\phi_{+}=\hat{u}_{0,+}e^{-i\mathcal{J}x}T(\lambda,t).\end{split}

(63)

In fact the compatibility condition requires thet

\begin{split}M\phi(x,t,\lambda)=\phi(x,t,\lambda)C(\lambda).\end{split}

(64)

Consider first the limit of (
64
) for
x\to-\infty
. We get:

\begin{split}M_{-}\phi_{-}=\left(i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\lambda^{N-1}(Q_{-}-\lambda J)\right)\hat{u}_{0,-}e^{-i\mathcal{J}x}=\hat{u}_{0,-}e^{-i\mathcal{J}x}C(\lambda),\end{split}

(65)

i.e.

C(\lambda)=-\lambda^{N-1}\mathcal{J}(\lambda)
.
Next consider the limit of (
64
) for
x\to\infty
. We have:

\begin{split}M_{+}\phi_{+}&=\left(i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\lambda^{N-1}(Q_{-}-\lambda J)\right)\hat{u}_{0,+}e^{-i\mathcal{J}x}T(\lambda,t)=\hat{u}_{0,+}e^{-i\mathcal{J}x}T(\lambda,t)C(\lambda).\end{split}

(66)

Thus:

\begin{split}\hat{u}_{0,+}e^{-i\mathcal{J}x}\left(i\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}-\lambda^{N-1}\mathcal{J}T(\lambda,t)\right)=-\hat{u}_{0,+}e^{-i\mathcal{J}x}T(\lambda,t)\lambda^{N-1}\mathcal{J}.\end{split}

(67)

The final result is:

\begin{split}i\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}=\lambda^{N-1}[\mathcal{J},T(\lambda,t)].\end{split}

(68)

It is easy to find also the
t
dependence of the Gauss factors of
T(\lambda,t)
:

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial S^{\pm}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{N-1}[\mathcal{J},S^{\pm}(\lambda,t)],\qquad i\frac{\partial T^{\pm}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{N-1}[\mathcal{J},T^{\pm}(\lambda,t)],\qquad i\frac{\partial D^{\pm}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=0.

(69)

Now we assume that the variations of the scattering data in eqs. (
60
) and (
61
) is due to the
evolution of the scattering matrix along the NLEE defined by the
M
operator (
62
). Using (
69
) we get:

\begin{split}\delta\tau^{\pm}_{\alpha}&\rightarrow\left\langle\hat{S}^{\pm}\frac{\partial S^{\pm}}{\partial t},E_{\mp\alpha}\right\rangle\delta t=\lambda^{N-1}\delta t\left\langle\hat{S}^{\pm}[\mathcal{J},S^{\pm}],E_{\mp\alpha}\right\rangle=\lambda^{N-1}\delta t\tau^{\pm}_{\alpha}(\lambda),\\
\delta\rho^{\pm}_{\alpha}&\rightarrow\left\langle\hat{T}^{\mp}\frac{\partial T^{\mp}}{\partial t},E_{\pm\alpha}\right\rangle\delta t=\lambda^{N-1}\delta t\left\langle\hat{T}^{\mp}[\mathcal{J},T^{\mp}],E_{\pm\alpha}\right\rangle=\lambda^{N-1}\delta t\tau^{\pm}_{\alpha}(\lambda).\end{split}

(70)

Thus for the expansion coefficients of
Q_{t}
we obtain:

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial\rho_{\alpha}^{\pm}}{\partial t}e^{\pm(\vec{d}^{\pm},\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\big[\hskip-1.99168pt\big[\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}Q_{t},e^{\pm}_{\pm\alpha}(x,\lambda)\big]\hskip-1.99168pt\big],

\displaystyle\quad i\frac{\partial\tau_{\alpha}^{\pm}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=-\big[\hskip-1.99168pt\big[\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}Q_{t},e_{\mp\alpha}^{\pm}\big]\hskip-1.99168pt\big],

(71)

and

\displaystyle i\frac{\partial\rho_{\alpha}^{\pm}}{\partial t}

\displaystyle=\big[\hskip-1.99168pt\big[\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}Q_{t},e^{\pm,\prime}_{\pm\alpha}(x,\lambda)\big]\hskip-1.99168pt\big],

\displaystyle\quad i\frac{\partial\tau_{\alpha}^{\pm}}{\partial t}e^{\pm(\vec{d}^{\pm},\alpha)}

\displaystyle=-\big[\hskip-1.99168pt\big[\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}Q_{t},e_{\mp\alpha}^{\pm,\prime}\big]\hskip-1.99168pt\big].

(72)

4
The
N
-wave hierarchy

The dispersion laws of the
N
-wave and MNLS hierarchy may contain several
\sqrt{\lambda^{2}-\rho_{k}^{2}}
.

As we mentioned above, the
x
-derivatives
Q_{x}
of the potentials with CBC
vanish
for
x\to\pm\infty
. In other
words they are elements of linear spaces, and the ‘squared solutions‘ are proper tools for the GFT.

In addition we need the recursion operators which have the ‘squared solutions‘ as eigenfunctions.
Note that the potential
Q(x,t)
and its derivative
Q_{x}
are restricted to the image of the operator
\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}
. Thus the
recursion operator also must be restricted to the image of the operator
\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}
. This means that we have to split the
‘squared solutions‘ as follows:

\begin{split}e^{\pm}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)=e^{\pm,\rm f}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)+e^{\pm,\rm d}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda),\end{split}

(73)

where
e^{\pm,\rm d}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)\in\mathfrak{h}
, in other words is diagonal matrix.

Then the derivation follows the well known steps. First ‘squared solutions obviously satisfied the equation

\begin{split}i\frac{\partial e^{\pm}_{\alpha}}{\partial x}+[Q(x,t)-\lambda J,e^{\pm}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)]=0.\end{split}

(74)

Insert the splitting (
73
) into (
74
) and treat separately the diagonal and the off-diagonal
parts of
e^{\pm}_{\alpha}
. For the diagonal part we have:

\begin{split}i\frac{\partial e^{\pm,\rm d}_{\alpha}}{\partial x}+[Q(x,t),e^{\pm,\rm f}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)]^{\rm d}=0,\end{split}

(75)

or

\begin{split}i\frac{\partial e^{\pm,\rm d}_{\alpha}}{\partial x}+\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left\langle[Q(x,t)e^{\pm,\rm f}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)]^{\rm d},H_{k}\right\rangle H_{k}=0,\end{split}

(76)

where the Cartan generators
H_{k}
are normalized by
\langle H_{j},H_{k}\rangle=\delta_{jk}
.

Integrating once we find:

\begin{split}e^{\pm,\rm d}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)=i\int_{\epsilon\infty}^{x}\sum_{k=1}^{r}\left\langle[Q(x,t),e^{\pm,\rm f}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)],H_{k}\right\rangle H_{k}+\lim_{x\to\epsilon\infty}e^{\pm,\rm d}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda),\end{split}

(77)

where
\epsilon=\pm 1
. For the off-diagonal part we obtain:

\begin{split}i\frac{\partial e^{\pm,\rm f}_{\alpha}}{\partial x}+[Q(x,t),e^{\pm,\rm f}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)]^{\rm f}+[Q(x,t),e^{\pm,\rm d}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)]=\lambda[J,e^{\pm,\rm f}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)].\end{split}

(78)

Now we apply
\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}
and obtain the formal expression for recursion operator
\Lambda_{\epsilon}
:

\begin{split}\Lambda_{\epsilon}X&=\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}\left(i\frac{\partial X}{\partial x}+[Q(x,t),X]^{\rm f}+i\left[Q(x,t),\int_{\epsilon\infty}^{x}\sum_{k=1}^{r}H_{k}\left\langle[Q(x,t),X],H_{k}\right\rangle\right]\right),\end{split}

(79)

where
X\equiv X^{\rm f}
. Acting with it on the ‘squared solutions‘ and making use of (
77
) we find:

\begin{split}\Lambda_{\epsilon}e^{\pm,\rm f}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)=\lambda e^{\pm,\rm f}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)+\left[Q(x,t),\lim_{x\to\epsilon\infty}e^{\pm,\rm d}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)\right].\end{split}

(80)

Therefore the ‘squared solutions‘
e^{\pm,\rm f}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)
will be eigenfunctions of
\Lambda_{\epsilon}
if and
only if

\lim_{x\to\epsilon\infty}e^{\pm,\rm d}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)=0.

Some
e^{\pm,\rm f}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)
can not be an eigenfunction of
\Lambda_{\epsilon}
, but convenient linear combination of
them are eigenfunctions.

4.1
Higher
N
-wave equations

Here we will use an equivalent, but slightly different form of the Lax operator:

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}\psi\equiv i\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}+(Q(x,t)-\lambda J)\psi(x,t,\lambda)=0,\qquad Q\in\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}.\end{split}

(81)

Indeed we may use a hierarchy of
M
operators which are polynomials of
\lambda
of any order
p
:

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}^{(N)}&\equiv i\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}+(V(x,t,\lambda)-\lambda^{N}J)\psi(x,t,\lambda)=0,\\
V(x,t,\lambda)&=\sum_{k=1}^{N}V_{k}(x,t)\lambda^{N-k}.\end{split}

(82)

Again we request that the compatibility condition:

\begin{split}[\mathcal{L},\mathcal{M}^{(N)}]=0\end{split}

(83)

holds identically with respect to
\lambda
. The leading term in
\lambda
in (
83
) is
\lambda^{N+1}[J,J]=0
.
The next order terms are:

\displaystyle\lambda^{p}

\displaystyle\colon

\displaystyle-[Q,J]-[J,V_{1}]=0,

\displaystyle V_{1}=Q,

(84)

\displaystyle\lambda^{N-1}

\displaystyle\colon

\displaystyle[Q,V_{1}]+iV_{1,x}-[J,V_{2}]=0,

\displaystyle V_{2}=i\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}Q_{x},

\displaystyle\lambda^{N-k}

\displaystyle\colon

\displaystyle iV_{k,x}+[Q,V_{k}]-[J,V_{k+1}]=0,

\displaystyle k=2,3,\dots,N-1;

\displaystyle\lambda^{0}

\displaystyle\colon

\displaystyle-iQ_{t}+iV_{N,x}+[Q,V_{N}]=0.

The first two relations in (
84
) allowed us to determine easily the first two coefficients

V_{1}
and
V_{2}
in
\mathcal{M}
. The rest of these equations we may interprete as recurrent relations,
which will allow us to express all
V_{k}
in terms of
Q
and its
x
-derivatives. In order to demonstrate this we
need to split each
V_{k}
into diagonal and off-diagonal parts:

\begin{split}V_{k}(x,t)=V_{k}^{\rm d}(x,t)+V_{k}^{\rm f}(x,t),\qquad V_{k}^{\rm d}(x,t)=\sum_{j=1}^{r}w_{k,j}(x,t)H_{j},\end{split}

(85)

where
w_{k,j}=\langle V_{k}(x,t),H_{j}\rangle
. The Cartan generators
H_{j}
are normalized by
\langle H_{j},H_{k}\rangle=\delta_{jk}
and
V_{k}^{\rm f}(x,t)\in\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{h}
.
Then the diagonal part of the
p-k
equation in (
84
) gives:

\begin{split}iV_{k,x}^{\rm d}+[Q,V_{k}^{\rm f}]^{\rm d}=0.\end{split}

(86)

Integrating on
x
we find:

\begin{split}w_{k,j}(x,t)=i\int_{x}^{\epsilon\infty}\langle H_{j},[Q,V_{k}^{\rm f}]\rangle+\lim_{x\to\epsilon}V_{k}^{\rm d}.\end{split}

(87)

The off-diagonal part of the same equation gives:

\begin{split}&iV^{\rm f}_{k,x}+[Q,V_{k}]^{\rm f}+[Q,V_{k}^{\rm d}=[J,V^{\rm f}_{k+1}],\qquad\mbox{i.e.}\qquad V_{k+1}^{\rm f}=\Lambda_{\epsilon}V_{k}^{\rm f},\end{split}

(88)

where
\Lambda_{\epsilon}
is the recursion operator (
79
).
Thus we have outlined the generalization of AKNS ideas
[
2
,
17
]
for homogeneous spaces. The MNLS equation (
90
)
is a consequence from the above consideration for
p=2
. It is well known
[
14
,
18
]
that
(\Lambda_{\epsilon})^{k}Q

is local, ie. it is expressed in terms of
Q
and its
x
-derivatives.

Finally, the corresponding NLEE, i.e. the last equation of (
84
) becomes:

\begin{split}i\frac{\partial Q}{\partial t}+\Lambda_{\epsilon}^{N-1}\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}=0.\end{split}

(89)

Comparing these results with the ones in Subsection 3.3 we conclude that if
Q(x,t)
satisfies the above NLEE then the
scattering data will satisfy eq. (
69
). In particular, for
N=2
eq. (
89
) becomes the NLS eq.

5
Conclusions

Here we analyzed the spectral properties of the
N
-wave hierarchy with CBC. We paid most attention to
the higher
N
-wave equations whose
M
-operators are polynomial of order
\lambda^{N}
with
N\geq 2
. For these equations we
determined the dispersion laws which have rather nontrivial character:
\lambda^{N-1}\mathcal{J}(\lambda)
which contain
at least two
\pm\sqrt{\lambda^{2}a_{k}^{2}-\rho_{k}^{2}}
,
k=1,2
. It will be important to physical phenomena that are compatible
with such dispersion laws. Similar analysis can be done for
N
-wave resonant interaction models with more general boundary conditions.

The next natural step would be to demonstrate that the Inverse scattering problems applied to this class of Lax
operators also can be understood as generalized Fourier transform, see
[
31
,
35
,
32
,
35
,
34
,
18
]
. Such results
will be based on the completeness relations for the ‘squared solutions‘
e^{\pm}_{\alpha}(x,t,\lambda)
.

It is also an open problem to derive the soliton solutions of (
1
) in the case of constant boundary conditions (the so-called ”dark solitons”), various types of dark-dark and dark-bright soliton solutions
[
49
]
by modifying the dressing Zakharov-Shabat method
[
54
]
, or by using the Darboux transformation method
[
11
]
.

Another open problem is to study the behavior of the scattering data at the end-points of the continuous spectrum in the complex
\lambda
-plane;
this requires generalization of the the method developed in
[
13
]
.

If we choose the
M
-operator to be a quadratic polynomial in
\lambda

then the AKNS scheme will allow us to determine recursively all its coefficients in terns of
Q

. This will give the second member of the integrable hierarchy associated to

\begin{split}i\frac{\partial Q}{\partial t}+\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}\frac{\partial^{2}Q}{\partial x^{2}}+\left[\mathrm{ad\,}_{J}^{-1}Q_{x},Q\right]+[w(x,t),Q(x,t)]=0.\end{split}

(90)

This is a multicomponent nonlinear Schrödinger system related to Homogeneous spaces
[
14
]
. The construction and the structure of its Lax pair are compatible with the structure of the homogeneous spaces, see
[
39
]
.

As an example, if we take the potential (
15
), then we can get the second member of the
4
-wave hierarchy related to
sp(4,{\mathbb{C}})
:

\mathrm{ad\,}_{j}^{-1}Q(x,t)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&\displaystyle{\frac{q_{4}}{a_{1}-a_{2}}}&\frac{q_{3}}{a_{1}+a_{2}}&\frac{q_{1}}{2a_{1}}\\

(91)

where

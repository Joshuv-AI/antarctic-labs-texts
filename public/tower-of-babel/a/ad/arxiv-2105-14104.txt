Large, moderate deviations principle and α \alpha -limit for the 2D Stochastic LANS- α \alpha
Z.I. Ali, P.A. Razafimandimby, T.A. Tegegn

Abstract
. In this paper we consider the Lagrangian Averaged Navier-Stokes Equations, also known as, LANS- α \alpha Navier-Stokes model on the two dimensional torus. We assume that the noise is a cylindrical Wiener process and its coefficient is multiplied by α \sqrt{\alpha} . We then study through the lenses of the large and moderate deviations principle the behaviour of the trajectories of the solutions of the stochastic system as α \alpha goes to 0. Instead of giving two separate proofs of the two deviations principles we present a unifying approach to the proof of the LDP and MDP and express the rate function in term of the unique solution of the Navier-Stokes equations. Our proof is based on the weak convergence approach to large deviations principle. As a by-product of our analysis we also prove that the solutions of the stochastic LANS- α \alpha model converge in probability to the solutions of the deterministic Navier-Stokes equations.



Keywords:
LANS-
\alpha
model, Camassa–Holm equations, large deviations principle, moderate deviations principle, Stochastic Navier-Stokes Equations

3.
Assumptions on the noise coefficient and a well-posdeness result

This section is devoted to the formulation of the standing assumption on the noise and the presentation of a well-posedness result.

3.1.
Formulation of the assumptions on the noise

Throughout we fix a complete filtered probability space
\mathscr{U}:=(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
where the filtration

we infer that

be a cylindrical Wiener process evolving on
\mathrm{K}

It is well-known, see
[

has the following series representation

where

is a sequence of mutually independent and identically distributed standard Brownian motions,

\mathrm{K}

Q

[
15

[

is an
\mathrm{K}_{1}

Now, we recall few basic facts about stochastic integrals with respect to a cylindrical Wiener process evolving on
\mathrm{K}
. For this purpose, let
\mathscr{H}
be a separable Banach space,

\mathrm{K}
, and

\Psi\in\mathscr{L}(\mathrm{K},\mathscr{H})

From the theory of stochastic integration on infinite dimensional Hilbert space, see
[

\mathbb{E}\biggl(\biggl\|\int_{0}^{t}\Psi(r)dW(r)\biggr\|^{2}_{\mathscr{H}}\biggr)=\mathbb{E}\biggl(\int_{0}^{t}\|\Psi(r)\|^{2}_{\mathscr{L}_{2}(\mathrm{K},\mathscr{H})}dr\biggr),\quad\forall t\in[0,T],

c_{3}>0
such that with probability
1

(5.17)

e^{\int_{0}^{T}C_{0}\mathrm{Y}_{n}(s)ds}\leq e^{(T+c_{3})\left(\lambda_{\delta}^{2}(\alpha)+\lvert\lambda_{\delta}(\alpha)-(1-\delta)\rvert+\lambda_{\delta}^{\frac{4}{3}}(\alpha)\right)+\delta^{2}c_{3}(1+c_{3})M+\alpha^{2}\lambda_{\delta}(\alpha)T.}

In a similar way, we can show that there exists a deterministic constant
C_{3}>0
, which may depend on
M
,
N
and
T
, such that with probability
1

(5.18)

\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{n}}\mathrm{R}_{n}(s)ds\leq C_{3}\Sigma_{n},

where the sequence
\Sigma_{n},\;n\in\mathbb{N}
is defined by

(5.19)

\begin{split}\Sigma_{n}=&\lvert\lambda_{\delta}(\alpha_{n})-(1-\delta)\rvert+\alpha_{n}^{2}[\lambda_{\delta}^{2}(\alpha_{n})+(1-\delta)]+\delta^{2}\alpha_{n}^{2}+\alpha_{n}^{2}\lambda_{\delta}^{-1}(\alpha_{n})+\alpha_{n}\lambda_{\delta}^{-2}(\alpha_{n})+\alpha_{n}^{2}+\alpha_{n}\end{split}

Next, since
\lambda_{\delta}(\alpha_{n})\leq 1
,
\lvert\lambda_{\delta}(\alpha_{n})-(1-\delta)\rvert\to 0
, and
\alpha_{n}^{2}\lambda_{\delta}(\alpha_{n})\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, we deduce that there exists a deterministic constant
C_{4}>0
such that with probability
1

(5.20)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}e^{\int_{0}^{T}C_{0}\mathrm{Y}_{n}(s)ds}\leq C_{4}.

Thus, by choosing
\theta>0
so that
2\theta C_{4}\leq 1
and applying the version of Gronwall’s lemma given in
[
13
, Lemma A.1]

we obtain that for all
t\in[0,T]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\|\mathbf{w}_{n}(t\wedge\tau_{N})\|_{\alpha}^{2}+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{N}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\mathrm{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{w}_{n}(s)\right\rvert^{2}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\left\lvert\mathrm{A}\mathbf{w}_{n}(s)\right\rvert^{2}\right]ds\leq C_{4}C_{3}\Sigma_{n},

which implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\lvert\mathbf{w}_{n}(t\wedge\tau_{N})\rvert^{2}+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{N}}\frac{1}{2}\left\lvert\mathrm{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{w}_{n}(s)\right\rvert^{2}ds\leq C_{4}C_{3}\Sigma_{n}.

Now, since
\lambda_{\delta}(\alpha_{n})\leq 1
,
\lvert\lambda_{\delta}(\alpha_{n})-(1-\delta)\rvert\to 0
and
\alpha_{n}\lambda_{\delta}^{-\ell}(\alpha_{n})\to 0
,
\ell\in\{1,2\}
, as
n\to\infty
we infer that

(5.21)

\mathbb{E}\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lvert\mathbf{w}_{n}(r\wedge\tau_{N})\rvert^{2}+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau_{N}}\lvert\mathrm{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{w}_{n}(s)\rvert^{2}\,ds\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

Next,
let
\gamma>0
and
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary numbers. Let us set

X_{n}(T)=\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lvert\mathbf{w}_{n}(r)\rvert^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\lvert\mathrm{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{w}_{n}(s)\rvert^{2}\,ds.

Then, it is not difficult to check that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(X_{n}(T)\geq\varepsilon\right)\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lvert X_{n}(T),\tau_{N}=T)+\mathbb{P}(\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lvert\mathbf{y}_{n}(r)\rvert^{2}\geq N)

(5.22)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\mathbb{E}X_{n}(T\wedge\tau_{N})+\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\sup_{r\in[0,T]}(\lvert\mathbf{y}_{n}(r)\rvert).

Owing to estimate (
4.4
) one can find
N_{0}>0
such that if
N\geq N_{0}
then

\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{E}\sup_{r\in[0,T]}(\lvert\mathbf{y}_{n}(r)\rvert^{2})<\frac{\gamma}{2}.

Thus, thanks to (
5.21
) and (
5.22
) we infer that there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}

\mathbb{P}\left(\left[\sup_{r\in[0,T]}\lvert\mathbf{w}_{n}(r)\rvert^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\lvert\mathrm{A}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{w}_{n}(s)\rvert^{2}\,ds\right]\geq\varepsilon\right)<\gamma,

which completes the proof of Proposition
5.6
.
∎

On Theta Ranks of Irreducible Characters of a Finite Classical Group
Shu-Yen Pan

Abstract
. In this paper, we interpret the theta rank of an irreducible character of a finite classical group in terms of the data from the Lusztig classification. Then we prove the following two results: (1) the agreement of the U U -rank and the theta rank for any irreducible character of low Theta rank; (2) the agreement of the parabolic asymptotic rank and the theta rank for any irreducible character.

1.
Introduction

1.1.

Let
\text{\bf G}=\text{\bf G}_{n}
be a finite symplectic group
{\rm Sp}_{2n}
,
a finite orthogonal group
{\rm O}^{+}_{2n}
,
{\rm O}^{-}_{2n+2}
,
{\rm O}_{2n+1}
,
or a finite unitary group
{\rm U}_{2n}
,
{\rm U}_{2n+1}
defined over a finite field

\text{\bf f}_{q}
where
q
is a power of an odd prime.
Let
G
denote the finite group of rational points of
G
.
For an irreducible character
\rho
of
G_{n}
,
several notions of “rank” attached to
\rho
are defined in
[
GH20
]
,
namely the
U
-rank
(denoted by
U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
),
the
asymptotic rank
(denoted by
A\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
),
and the
tensor rank
(denoted by
\otimes\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
).
It is known that
0\leq U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)\leq n
.
An irreducible character
\rho
of
G_{n}
is said to be
of low
U
-rank
if

U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)<n
(resp.
U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)<n-1
)
if
G_{n}
is a symplectic group or a unitary group (resp. an orthogonal group).

Because the tensor rank of
\rho
can be interpreted as the first occurrence in the theta correspondence
for some dual pair involving
G
, in this article the tensor rank will be called the

\Theta
-rank
and denoted by
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
(
cf
. Subsection
3.1
).
Note that
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
is always even if
G
is an orthogonal group.
A similar notion of the
\Theta
-rank which is called
level
and denoted by

{\mathfrak{l}}(\rho)
is also defined in
[
GLT20
]
and
[
GLT19
]
.
Due to different normalizations, we can see that

{\mathfrak{l}}(\rho)=\begin{cases}\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho),&\text{if $G$ is a symplectic group or a unitary group;}\\
\frac{1}{2}\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho),&\text{if $G$ is an orthogonal group.}\end{cases}

Moreover, we will modify the definition of the asymptotic ranks and define
the
parabolic asymptotic rank
(denoted by
\overline{A}\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
,
cf
. Subsection
4.1
).

Of course, it is expected these ranks are closely related.
In fact, one can see that

U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)\leq A\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)\leq\overline{A}\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)\leq\otimes\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)

(
cf
. (
4.10
)) for any
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)
.
Moreover, Gurevich-Howe make the following conjecture in
[
GH20
]
:

Conjecture 1.1

(Gurevich-Howe)
.

Let
\rho
be an irreducible character of a finite classical group
G
.
Then

(i)

A\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
;

(ii)

if
\rho
is of low
U
-rank,
then
U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
.

As indicated in
[
GH20
]
, if the conjecture is true,
the theta correspondence provides a method to construct an irreducible character of a
given
U
-rank or
A
-rank.
When
G
is a general linear group, (i) of the conjecture is already proved in
[
GH20
]
theorem 11.4.
One of the purposes of the article is to provide similar results for other classical groups.

1.2.

Let
{\mathcal{E}}(G)
denote the set of irreducible characters of
G
.
It is known that the set
{\mathcal{E}}(G)
partitions into subsets
{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{s}
, called
Lusztig series
,
indexed by the set of conjugacy classes of semisimple elements
s
in the connected component
of the dual group
G^{*}
of
G
(
cf
.
[
Lus84
]
).
Elements in
{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{1}
are called
unipotent
.
It is known by Lusztig that unipotent characters
\rho=\rho_{\Lambda}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{1}

are parametrized by the equivalence classes of
symbols

\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}}

satisfying certain conditions where
\{a_{i}\}
,
\{b_{j}\}
are finite sets of non-negative integers written
in decreasing order, i.e,
a_{1}>a_{2}>\cdots>a_{m_{1}}
and
b_{1}>b_{2}>\cdots>b_{m_{2}}
(
cf
.
[
Lus77
]
).
Note that by replacing
\Lambda
by another symbol in its equivalence class if necessary,
we can always assume that both rows
\{a_{i}\},\{b_{j}\}
of
\Lambda
are non-empty.
Our first result (
cf
. Proposition
3.4
) is to interpret the
\Theta
-rank of
a unipotent character
\rho_{\Lambda}
in terms of the invariants of symbols:

(1.2)

\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{\Lambda})=\begin{cases}\min\{2\,{\rm rk}(\Lambda^{\rm t}\smallsetminus\textstyle\binom{-}{a_{1}}),2\,{\rm rk}(\Lambda^{\rm t}\smallsetminus\textstyle\binom{b_{1}}{-})\},&\text{if $\text{\bf G}$ is ${\rm Sp}$ or ${\rm O}$};\\
\min\{{\rm rk}_{\rm U}(\Lambda^{\rm t}\smallsetminus\textstyle\binom{-}{a_{1}}),{\rm rk}_{\rm U}(\Lambda^{\rm t}\smallsetminus\textstyle\binom{b_{1}}{-})\}&\text{if $\text{\bf G}$ is ${\rm U}$}\end{cases}

where

\displaystyle\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}^{\rm t}

\displaystyle=\binom{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}

\displaystyle{\rm rk}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}a_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m_{2}}b_{j}-\left\lfloor\left(\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}-1}{2}\right)^{2}\right\rfloor,

\displaystyle{\rm rk}_{\rm U}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}2a_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m_{2}}2b_{j}+\frac{|m_{1}-m_{2}|}{2}-\frac{(m_{1}+m_{2})(m_{1}+m_{2}-2)}{2}.

From (
1.2
), we see that
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{\Lambda})
is always even if
G
is a symplectic
group or an orthogonal group.

1.3.

Next we want to investigate the
\Theta
-rank of a general irreducible character.
First suppose
G
is a symplectic group or an orthogonal group.
For
s\in(G^{*})^{0}
, we can define
\text{\bf G}^{(0)},\text{\bf G}^{(-)},\text{\bf G}^{(+)}
(
cf
. Subsection
2.4
) where

\text{\bf G}^{(0)}
is a product of unitary groups or general linear groups,
and
\text{\bf G}^{(-)},\text{\bf G}^{(+)}
are given as follows:

•

if
\text{\bf G}={\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{2n}
,
then
\text{\bf G}^{(-)}={\rm O}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}_{2n^{(-)}}
, and
\text{\bf G}^{(+)}={\rm O}^{\epsilon^{\prime}\epsilon}_{2n^{(+)}}
;

•

if
\text{\bf G}={\rm Sp}_{2n}
,
then
\text{\bf G}^{(-)}={\rm O}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}_{2n^{(-)}}
, and
\text{\bf G}^{(+)}={\rm Sp}_{2n^{(+)}}
;

•

if
\text{\bf G}={\rm O}_{2n+1}
,
then
\text{\bf G}^{(-)}={\rm Sp}_{2n^{(-)}}
, and
\text{\bf G}^{(+)}={\rm Sp}_{2n^{(+)}}
,

for some
\epsilon^{\prime}=\pm
, and
n^{(-)},n^{(+)}
depending on
s
.
Then we have a (modified)
Lusztig correspondence

\Xi_{s}\colon{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{s}\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}(G^{(0)}\times G^{(-)}\times G^{(+)})_{1}.

The mapping
\Xi_{s}
is two-to-one:
\Xi_{s}(\rho)=\Xi_{s}(\rho\cdot{\rm sgn})
when
G
is an odd-orthogonal group;
and
\Xi_{s}
is bijective otherwise.
Then we can write

\Xi_{s}(\rho)=\rho^{(0)}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda^{(-)}}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda^{(+)}}

for
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{s}
where
\rho^{(0)}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G^{(0)})_{1}
,
\Lambda^{(-)}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}^{(-)}}
,
and
\Lambda^{(+)}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}^{(+)}}
.

Next suppose
G
is a unitary group.
As described in Subsection
3.4
,
for the Lusztig correspondence
{\mathfrak{L}}_{s}\colon{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{1}\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}(C_{G^{*}}(s))_{1}

we can write

(1.3)

{\mathfrak{L}}_{s}(\rho)=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\bigotimes_{j=1}^{t_{i}}\rho^{(ij)}.

Note that
t_{1}=q+1
and
\rho^{(1,j)}
is a unipotent character of
{\rm U}_{n_{1,j}}(q)

for
j=1,\ldots,q+1
.
Now we have the following formula (
cf
. Proposition
3.12
and Proposition
3.17
)
for the
\Theta
-rank of a general irreducible character in terms of data from
the Lusztig classification:

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)
.

(i)

If
\text{\bf G}={\rm Sp}_{2n}
,
then

\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=\min\{2n-2n^{(+)}+\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{\Lambda^{(+)}}),2n-2n^{(-)}+\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{\Lambda^{(-)}})+1\}.

(ii)

If
\text{\bf G}={\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{2n}
or
{\rm O}_{2n+1}
,
then

\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=\min\{2n-2n^{(+)}+\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{\Lambda^{(+)}}),2n-2n^{(-)}+\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{\Lambda^{(-)}})\}.

(iii)

If
\text{\bf G}={\rm U}_{n}
,
then

\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=\min\{n-n_{1,j}+\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho^{(1,j)})\mid j=1,\ldots,q+1\}.

1.4.

After we analyze several properties of the
\Theta
-ranks,
the following result can be derived easily from the result on
\eta
-correspondence for a reductive dual
pair in stable range by Gurevich-Howe in
[
GH20
]
:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{n})
where
\text{\bf G}_{n}
is
{\rm Sp}_{2n}
,
{\rm O}^{+}_{2n}
,
{\rm O}^{-}_{2n+2}
,
{\rm O}_{2n+1}
,

{\rm U}_{2n}
, or
{\rm U}_{2n+1}
.
If
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)\leq n
,
then

U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=A\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=\overline{A}\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho).

Finally, by using the same arguments in
[
GH20
]
section 11,
we can show the agreement of the parabolic asymptotic rank and the theta rank:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)
where
G
is a symplectic group, an orthogonal group or a unitary group.
Then

\overline{A}\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho).

Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
provide a partial answer for Conjecture
1.1
.

2.
Theta Correspondence and Lusztig Correspondence

2.1.
Basic notations

Let
\text{\bf f}_{q}
be a finite field of
q
elements where
q
is a power of an odd prime
p
,
and let
\overline{\text{\bf f}}_{q}
denote a fixed algebraic closure of
\text{\bf f}_{q}
.

Let
G
be a symplectic group, an orthogonal group or a unitary group defined over
\text{\bf f}_{q}
,
and let
G
denote the finite group of rational points.
The character of an irreducible representation of
G
is called an
irreducible character

of
G
, and the set of all irreducible characters of
G
is denoted by
{\mathcal{E}}(G)
.
The character of a one-dimensional representation is also called a
linear character
.
It is proved by Lusztig that the set
{\mathcal{E}}(G)
admits a partition

{\mathcal{E}}(G)=\bigcup_{(s)\subset(G^{*})^{0}}{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{s}

indexed by conjugacy classes
(s)
of semisimple simple elements
s
in the
connected component
(G^{*})^{0}
of the dual group
G^{*}
of
G
.
Each
{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{s}
is called a
Lusztig series
,
and the elements in
{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{1}
are called
unipotent
.
The trivial character of
G
is denoted by
\bf 1
.

2.2.
Unipotent characters of a symplectic group or an orthogonal group

The set of
partitions
of
n
is denoted by
{\mathcal{P}}(n)
.
If
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(n)
, we also write
|\mu|=n
.
An ordered pair
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu}{\nu}
of two partitions
\mu,\nu
,
is called a
bi-partition of
n
if
\left|\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu}{\nu}\right|:=|\mu|+|\nu|=n
.
The set of bi-partitions of
n
is denoted by
{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(n)
.

Now we recall the notations of symbols from
[
Lus77
]
.
Note that for our convenience, the definition here is slightly different from the original one.
A
\beta
-set

A=\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}\}
is a finite subset (possibly empty) of non-negative integers
written in strictly decreasing order, i.e.,
a_{1}>a_{2}>\cdots>a_{m}
.
A
symbol

\Lambda=\binom{A}{B}=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}

is an ordered pair of two
\beta
-sets
A,B
.
On the set of symbols we define an equivalence relation “
\sim
” generated by

\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}\sim\binom{a_{1}+1,a_{2}+1\ldots,a_{m_{1}}+1,0}{b_{1}+1,b_{2}+1,\ldots,b_{m_{2}}+1,0}.

The set of equivalence classes of symbols is denoted by
{\mathcal{S}}
.

For a symbol
\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}
,
we define its
defect
and
rank
by

\displaystyle{\rm def}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=m_{1}-m_{2},

\displaystyle{\rm rk}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}a_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m_{2}}b_{j}-\left\lfloor\left(\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}-1}{2}\right)^{2}\right\rfloor.

It is not difficult to see that two equivalent symbols have the same defect and the same rank.
For a symbol
\Lambda=\binom{A}{B}
we define its
transpose

\Lambda^{\rm t}=\binom{B}{A}
.
It is obvious that
{\rm rk}(\Lambda^{\rm t})={\rm rk}(\Lambda)
and

{\rm def}(\Lambda^{\rm t})=-{\rm def}(\Lambda)
.
We define a mapping
\Upsilon
from symbols to bi-partitions by

(2.1)

\Upsilon\colon\binom{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}\mapsto\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{a_1-(m_1-1),a_2-(m_1-2),\ldots,a_{m_1-1}-1,a_{m_1}}{b_1-(m_2-1),b_2-(m_2-2),\ldots,b_{m_2-1}-1,b_{m_2}}.

The image
\Upsilon(\Lambda)
in fact depends only on the equivalence class of
\Lambda
.
Moreover, it is easy to check that

{\rm rk}(\Lambda)=\begin{cases}|\Upsilon(\Lambda)|+\tfrac{1}{4}({\rm def}(\Lambda)-1)({\rm def}(\Lambda)+1),&\text{if ${\rm def}(\Lambda)$ is odd};\\
|\Upsilon(\Lambda)|+\tfrac{1}{4}({\rm def}(\Lambda))^{2},&\text{if ${\rm def}(\Lambda)$ is even}.\end{cases}

For a symplectic group or an orthogonal group
G
,
we define the following sets of (equivalence classes of) symbols:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm O}^{+}_{2n}}

\displaystyle=\{\,\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}\mid{\rm rk}(\Lambda)=n,\ {\rm def}(\Lambda)\equiv 0\pmod{4}\,\};

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm Sp}_{2n}}

\displaystyle=\{\,\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}\mid{\rm rk}(\Lambda)=n,\ {\rm def}(\Lambda)\equiv 1\pmod{4}\,\};

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm O}^{-}_{2n}}

\displaystyle=\{\,\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}\mid{\rm rk}(\Lambda)=n,\ {\rm def}(\Lambda)\equiv 2\pmod{4}\,\};

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm SO}_{2n+1}}={\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm O}_{2n+1}}

\displaystyle=\{\,\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}\mid{\rm rk}(\Lambda)=n,\ {\rm def}(\Lambda)\equiv 3\pmod{4}\,\}.

The following are modified from the results proved by Lusztig (
cf
.
[
Lus77
]
):

(1)

Suppose that
\text{\bf G}={\rm Sp}_{2n},{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{2n},{\rm SO}_{2n+1}
where
\epsilon=+
or
-
.
There is a parametrization
{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{1}
by
{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}}
,
so a unipotent character
\rho
of
G
is written as
\rho=\rho_{\Lambda}
for
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}}
.
If
\text{\bf G}={\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{2n}

.

(2)

Suppose that
\text{\bf G}={\rm O}_{2n+1}
.
Then a unipotent character
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{1}
can be written as
\rho=\rho_{\Lambda}
or
\rho=\rho_{\Lambda}\cdot{\rm sgn}

for
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm SO}_{2n+1}}

2.3.
Unipotent characters of a unitary group

In this subsection, suppose that
G

For a symbol

(2.2)

{\rm rk}_{\rm U}\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}2a_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m_{2}}2b_{j}+\frac{|m_{1}-m_{2}|}{2}-\frac{(m_{1}+m_{2})(m_{1}+m_{2}-2)}{2}.

2.4.
Lusztig correspondence

The following result is proved by Lusztig (
cf
.
[
DM91
]
theorem 13.23, remark 13.24):

Proposition 2.4

(Lusztig)
.

There is a bijection

{\mathfrak{L}}_{s}\colon{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{s}\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}(C_{G^{*}}(s))_{1}

such that
R^{\text{\bf G}}_{\text{\bf T}^{*},s}\mapsto\varepsilon_{\text{\bf G}}\varepsilon_{C_{\text{\bf G}^{*}}(s)}R^{C_{\text{\bf G}^{*}}(s)}_{\text{\bf T}^{*},1}

where
C_{G^{*}}(s)
denotes the centralizer in
G^{*}
of
s
,

\varepsilon_{\text{\bf G}}=(-1)^{\kappa}
and
\kappa
is the relative rank of
G
.

The bijection
{\mathfrak{L}}_{s}
called a
Lusztig correspondence
.

Remark 2.5
.

Clearly,
{\rm def}(\Lambda)\equiv 1\pmod{4}
if and only if
{\rm def}(\Lambda^{\rm t})\equiv 3\pmod{4}
.
The the Lusztig correspondence
{\mathfrak{L}}_{1}\colon{\mathcal{E}}({\rm Sp}_{2n}(q))_{1}\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}({\rm SO}_{2n+1}(q))_{1}
is a
bijection given by
\rho_{\Lambda}\mapsto\rho_{\Lambda^{\rm t}}
.

For
s\in(G^{*})^{0}
, we define

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}G^{(0)}=G^{(0)}(s)&=\prod_{\langle\lambda\rangle\subset\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\},\ \lambda\neq\pm 1}G_{[\lambda]}(s);\\
G^{(-)}=G^{(-)}(s)&=G_{[-1]}(s);\\
G^{(+)}=G^{(+)}(s)&=\begin{cases}(G_{[1]}(s))^{*},&\text{if $G$ is symplectic};\\
G_{[1]}(s),&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}\end{split}

where
G_{[\lambda]}(s)
is given in
[
AMR96
]
subsection 1.B
(see also
[
Pan19b
]
subsection 2.2).
Hence we have a bijection

{\mathcal{E}}(C_{G^{*}}(s))_{1}\simeq\begin{cases}{\mathcal{E}}(G^{(0)}\times G^{(-)}\times G^{(+)})_{1}\times\{\pm 1\},&\text{if $G$ is odd-orthogonal};\\
{\mathcal{E}}(G^{(0)}\times G^{(-)}\times G^{(+)})_{1},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It is known that
\text{\bf G}^{(0)}
is a product of unitary groups or general linear groups,
and
\text{\bf G}^{(-)},\text{\bf G}^{(+)}
are given as follows:

•

if
\text{\bf G}={\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{2n}
,
then

\text{\bf G}^{(-)}={\rm O}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}_{2n^{(-)}}
, and
\text{\bf G}^{(+)}={\rm O}^{\epsilon^{\prime}\epsilon}_{2n^{(+)}}

for some
\epsilon^{\prime}=+
or
-
;

•

if
\text{\bf G}={\rm Sp}_{2n}
,
then
\text{\bf G}^{(-)}={\rm O}^{\epsilon^{\prime}}_{2n^{(-)}}
, and
\text{\bf G}^{(+)}={\rm Sp}_{2n^{(+)}}

for some
\epsilon^{\prime}=+
or
-
;

•

if
\text{\bf G}={\rm O}_{2n+1}
,
then
\text{\bf G}^{(-)}={\rm Sp}_{2n^{(-)}}
, and
\text{\bf G}^{(+)}={\rm Sp}_{2n^{(+)}}
;

•

if
\text{\bf G}={\rm U}_{n}
,
then
\text{\bf G}^{(-)}={\rm U}_{n^{(-)}}
, and
\text{\bf G}^{(+)}={\rm U}_{n^{(+)}}
,

where
n^{(+)}=n^{(+)}(s)
(resp.
n^{(-)}=n^{(-)}(s)
) denotes the multiplicity
of
+1
(resp.
-1
) in the set
\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}
of “eigenvalues”
of
s
.

So now a Lusztig correspondence
{\mathfrak{L}}_{s}
induces a mapping

\Xi_{s}\colon{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{s}\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}(G^{(0)}(s)\times G^{(-)}(s)\times G^{(+)}(s))_{1}

called the
modified Lusztig correspondence
.
The mapping
\Xi_{s}
is two-to-one and
\Xi_{s}(\rho)=\Xi_{s}(\rho\cdot{\rm sgn})
when
G
is an odd-orthogonal group;
and
\Xi_{s}
is a bijection otherwise.
We can write
\Xi_{s}(\rho)=\rho^{(0)}\otimes\rho^{(-)}\otimes\rho^{(+)}
.
Because now
\rho^{(\varepsilon)}
where
\varepsilon=+
or
-
is a unipotent character of a symplectic group,
an even-orthogonal group, or a unitary group,
we can write
\rho^{(\varepsilon)}=\rho_{\Lambda^{(\varepsilon)}}
for some symbol

Let
\text{\bf G}_{n}
denote the group of split rank

\text{\bf G}_{n}
is one of
{\rm Sp}_{2n}
,
{\rm O}^{+}_{2n}

{\rm O}^{-}_{2n+2}

{\rm O}_{2n+1}
,

{\rm U}_{2n}
, or
{\rm U}_{2n+1}

For

For
m<n

\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{m})

\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}_{G_{m}}^{G_{n}}(\rho^{\prime})

.

,

2.6.
Finite Howe correspondences on unipotent characters

First we recall the concept of finite reductive dual pairs from
[
How
]
.
Let
(\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G})
be a finite reductive dual pair of one of the following types:

(I)

({\rm U}_{n^{\prime}},{\rm U}_{n})

(II)

({\rm O}_{2n^{\prime}}^{\epsilon},{\rm Sp}_{2n})
or
({\rm Sp}_{2n^{\prime}},{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{2n})

\epsilon=+
or
-

(III)

({\rm O}_{2n^{\prime}+1},{\rm Sp}_{2n})
or

are non-negative integers.
By restricting the Weil character with respect to a nontrivial additive character
\psi
of
\text{\bf f}_{q}

to the dual pair
(\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G})
, we obtain a decomposition

\omega_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G}}^{\psi}=\sum_{\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime}),\ \rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)}m_{\rho^{\prime},\rho}\rho^{\prime}\otimes\rho

where

.
Then we have a relation between
{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})
and
{\mathcal{E}}(G)
:

\Theta_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G}}=\{\,(\rho^{\prime},\rho)\in{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})\times{\mathcal{E}}(G)\mid m_{\rho^{\prime},\rho}\neq 0\,\},

called the
\Theta
-correspondence
for the dual pair
(\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G})
.
For
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})
, we write

\Theta(\rho^{\prime})=\Theta_{\text{\bf G}}(\rho^{\prime})=\{\,\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)\mid(\rho^{\prime},\rho)\in\Theta_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G}}\,\}.

It is well known that for cases (I) and (II) above, the unipotent characters
are preserved by
\Theta
-correspondence, i.e., when
(\rho^{\prime},\rho)\in\Theta_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G}}
,
then
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})_{1}
if and only if
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{1}
(
cf
.
[
AM93
]
).
For these two cases,
the unipotent part
\omega_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G},1}
of the Weil character
\omega_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G}}^{\psi}
has the
decomposition

\omega_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G},1}=\sum_{\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})_{1},\ \rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{1}}m_{\rho^{\prime},\rho}\rho^{\prime}\otimes\rho=\sum_{(\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda)\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G}}}\rho_{\Lambda^{\prime}}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda}

where the relation
{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G}}
are given in
[
Pan19a
]

and
[
Pan19b
]
.

theorem 6.9 and theorem 7.9:

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
(\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G})
be a finite reductive dual pair, and let

,

\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{s}
for some

Write

Suppose that

•

\text{\bf G}^{\prime(0)}\simeq\text{\bf G}^{(0)}

•

\begin{CD}\rho^{\prime}@>{\Theta_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G}}}>{}>\rho\\

(ii)

occurs in

\text{\bf G}^{\prime(0)}\simeq\text{\bf G}^{(0)}

\text{\bf G}^{\prime(-)}\simeq\text{\bf G}^{(+)}

\rho_{\Lambda^{\prime(-)}}=\rho_{\Lambda^{(+)}}

i.e., the diagram

\begin{CD}\rho^{\prime}@>{\Theta_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G}}}>{}>\rho\\

commutes where

.

Recall that an irreducible character
\rho
of

3.1.

Let

(1)

({\rm Sp}_{2k},\text{\bf G})

So the
\Theta
-rank of an irreducible character of an orthogonal group is always even.
Note that for a non-trivial orthogonal group,

\bf 1

{\rm sgn}

\chi_{\text{\bf G}}

(3)

\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=\min\{\,k\mid\rho\chi\in\Theta(\rho^{\prime})\text{ for some }\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}({\rm U}_{k}(q))\\
\text{and linear character }\chi\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)\,\}.

From the above definition, we see that

.

3.2.
\Theta
-ranks of unipotent or pseudo-unipotent characters

Let

where

is a symplectic group, an orthogonal group, or a unitary group.

(3.3)

\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\Lambda)=\begin{cases}\min\{2{\rm rk}(\Lambda^{\rm t}\smallsetminus\textstyle\binom{-}{a_{1}}),2{\rm rk}(\Lambda^{\rm t}\smallsetminus\textstyle\binom{b_{1}}{-})\},&\text{if $\text{\bf G}$ is ${\rm Sp}$ or ${\rm O}$};\\

Proposition 3.4

.

(b)

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{-}{\frac{n-k-1}{2},1,1,\ldots,1},&\text{if $n$ is odd.}\end{cases}

(i)

Suppose that
n
is even.
Now
\binom{\frac{k-1}{2},\frac{k-3}{2},\ldots,1}{\frac{k-1}{2},\frac{k-3}{2},\ldots,0}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}

\binom{\frac{n}{2},\frac{k-1}{2},\frac{k-3}{2},\ldots,1}{\frac{k-1}{2},\frac{k-3}{2},\ldots,0}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{n}}
.
Hence
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{\Lambda_{k}})=\min\{k,n\}=k

(ii)

Suppose that

Now
\binom{\frac{k}{2},\frac{k}{2}-1,\ldots,1}{\frac{k}{2}-1,\frac{k}{2}-2,\ldots,0}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}

.
Hence
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{\Lambda_{k}})=\min\{k,n+2\}=k
.

(2)

Suppose that

(a)

If

(b)

3.5.
\Theta
-ranks and parabolic induction

Let
\text{\bf G}_{n}
be one of
{\rm Sp}_{2n}
,
{\rm O}^{+}_{2n}
,
{\rm O}^{-}_{2n+2}
,
{\rm O}_{2n+1}
,

{\rm U}_{2n}
, or
{\rm U}_{2n+1}
.
For
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{n})
and an integer
m\geq 0
,
recall that a subset
{\mathcal{E}}_{G_{n}}^{G_{m}}(\rho)\subset{\mathcal{E}}(G_{m})
is defined in Subsection
2.5
.

Lemma 3.21
.

Let
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{n})_{1}
and
m\geq n
.
Then

\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=\min\{\,\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho^{\prime})\mid\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{G_{n}}^{G_{m}}(\rho)\,\}

Proof.

Let
\rho
be a unipotent character of
G_{n}
.
It is clear that we need only to show that

\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho^{\prime})\geq\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
for any
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{G_{n}}^{G_{n+1}}(\rho)
,
and there exists
\rho_{0}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{G_{n}}^{G_{n+1}}(\rho)
such that

\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{0})=\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
.

Now we can write
\rho=\rho_{\Lambda}
(
\rho=\rho_{\Lambda}
or
\rho=\rho_{\Lambda}\cdot{\rm sgn}
if
\text{\bf G}={\rm O}_{2n+1}
)
for some symbol
\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}_{n}}
.
Consider the following reductive dual pairs
(\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}},\text{\bf G}_{n})
:

(a)

({\rm O}^{+}_{2n^{\prime}},{\rm Sp}_{2n})
,
({\rm Sp}_{2n^{\prime}},{\rm O}^{+}_{2n})
,

({\rm U}_{2n^{\prime}},{\rm U}_{2n})
,
({\rm U}_{2n^{\prime}+1},{\rm U}_{2n+1})
;

(b)

({\rm O}^{-}_{2n^{\prime}},{\rm Sp}_{2n})
,
({\rm Sp}_{2n^{\prime}},{\rm O}^{-}_{2n+2})
,

({\rm U}_{2n^{\prime}+1},{\rm U}_{2n})
,
({\rm U}_{2n^{\prime}},{\rm U}_{2n+1})
.

Let
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{G_{n}}^{G_{n+1}}(\rho)
.
Then
\rho^{\prime}
is also unipotent and we write
\rho^{\prime}=\rho_{\Lambda^{\prime}}

(
\rho^{\prime}=\rho_{\Lambda^{\prime}}
or
\rho^{\prime}=\rho_{\Lambda^{\prime}}\cdot{\rm sgn}
if
\text{\bf G}_{n}={\rm O}_{2n+1}
) where

\Lambda^{\prime}
is equal to one of the following:

\textstyle\binom{a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},a_{i}+1,a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}},\binom{a_{1}+1,a_{2}+1,\ldots,a_{m_{1}}+1,1}{b_{1}+1,b_{2}+1,\ldots,b_{m_{2}}+1,0},\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},\ldots,b_{i-1},b_{i}+1,b_{i+1},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}},\binom{a_{1}+1,a_{2}+1,\ldots,a_{m_{1}}+1,0}{b_{1}+1,b_{2}+1,\ldots,b_{m_{2}}+1,1}

for some index
i
.
By Proposition
3.4
, it is clear that

\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{\Lambda^{\prime}})=\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\Lambda^{\prime})\geq\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\Lambda)=\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{\Lambda})
.
Now define
\Lambda_{0}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}_{n+1}}
by

\Lambda_{0}=\begin{cases}\binom{a_{1}+1,a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}},&\text{if $(\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}},\text{\bf G}_{n})$ is in case (a)};\\
\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1}+1,b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}},&\text{if $(\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}},\text{\bf G}_{n})$ is in case (b)}.\end{cases}

Then
\rho_{0}:=\rho_{\Lambda_{0}}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{G_{n}}^{G_{n+1}}(\rho)
and by Proposition
3.4
, we know that

\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{\Lambda_{0}})=\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{\Lambda})
.
∎

From the proof of the lemma, we have the following result:

Corollary 3.22
.

Let
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{n})
and
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{G_{n}}^{G_{n+1}}(\rho)
.
Then either
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho^{\prime})=\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)

or
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho^{\prime})=\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)+2
.

Then we generalize Lemma
3.21
to any irreducible characters.

Lemma 3.23
.

Let
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{n})
and
m\geq n
.
Then

\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=\min\{\,\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho^{\prime})\mid\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{G_{n}}^{G_{m}}(\rho\chi),\ \chi\text{ a linear character of }G_{n}\,\}

Proof.

As in the proof of Lemma
3.21
, we need only to show that

\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho^{\prime})\geq\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
for any
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{G_{n}}^{G_{n+1}}(\rho\chi)

for any linear character
\chi
,
and there exists
\rho_{0}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{G_{n}}^{G_{n+1}}(\rho\chi)
for some linear character
\chi
such that

\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{0})=\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
.

Now we suppose that
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{n})_{s}
for some
s
.
When
\text{\bf G}_{n}
is a symplectic group or an orthogonal group,
we write
\Xi_{s}(\rho)=\rho^{(0)}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda^{(-)}}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda^{(+)}}

for some
\Lambda^{(\varepsilon)}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}^{(\varepsilon)}}
where
\varepsilon=\pm
.

(1)

Suppose that
\text{\bf G}_{n}={\rm Sp}_{2n}
and
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
is even.
By Proposition
3.12
, we see that
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=2n-2n^{(+)}+\Theta(\rho_{\Lambda^{(+)}})
.
Let
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{G_{n}}^{G_{n+1}}(\rho)
.
Then we know that
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{n+1})_{s_{n+1}}
where

s_{n+1}=(s,1)\in(G_{n}^{*})^{0}\times T_{1}\subset(G^{*}_{n+1})^{0}
.
Now
\Xi_{s_{n+1}}(\rho^{\prime})=\rho^{(0)}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda^{(-)}}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda^{\prime(+)}}
where

\rho_{\Lambda^{\prime(+)}}\in{\mathcal{E}}^{G^{(+)}_{n^{(+)}+1}}_{G^{(+)}_{n^{(+)}}}(\rho_{\Lambda^{(+)}})
by Lemma
2.10
.
Then by Lemma
3.21
,

\displaystyle\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho^{\prime})=2(n+1)-2(n^{(+)}+1)+\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{\Lambda^{\prime(+)}})

\displaystyle\geq 2n-2n^{(+)}+\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{\Lambda^{(+)}})

\displaystyle=\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho).

Next let
\Lambda^{(+)}_{0}
be defined as
\Lambda_{0}
in the proof of Lemma
3.21
,
and let

\rho_{0}=\Xi^{-1}_{s_{n+1}}(\rho^{(0)}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda^{(-)}}\otimes\rho_{\Lambda^{(+)}_{0}}).

Then we have
\rho_{0}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{G_{n}}^{G_{n+1}}(\rho)
and
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{0})

(2)

Suppose that
\text{\bf G}_{n}={\rm Sp}_{2n}
and
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
is odd.
Then by Proposition
3.12
, we have

\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=2n-2n^{(-)}+\Theta(\rho_{\Lambda^{(-)}})+1
.
The remaining proof is similar to case (1) with only “
+
” replaced by “
-
”.

(3)

Suppose that
\text{\bf G}_{n}
is an orthogonal group.
By twisting a linear character
\chi
of
\text{\bf G}_{n}
,
by Lemma
2.9
and Proposition
3.12
, we may assume
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=2n-2n^{(+)}+\Theta(\rho_{\Lambda^{(+)}})
.
Then the remaining proof is similar to case (1).

Now suppose that
\text{\bf G}_{n}
is a unitary group.
Write
{\mathfrak{L}}_{s}(\rho)=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\bigotimes_{j=1}^{t_{i}}\rho^{(ij)}
as in (
3.16
).
By Proposition
3.17
,
after twisting a linear character
\chi
of
\text{\bf G}_{n}
if necessary,
we may assume that
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=n-n_{1,1}+\Theta(\rho^{(1,1)})

where the notations are as in Subsection
3.4
.
Then the remaining proof is similar to case (1), again.
∎

4.
\Theta
-ranks and Other Ranks

4.1.
U
-rank,
A
-rank,
\overline{A}
-rank, and
\otimes
-rank

First we recall the definition of the
U
-rank and the asymptotic rank of an irreducible character
\rho

of a classical group
G=G(V)
given in
[
GH17
]
and
[
GH20
]

where
V
is the standard space with an associated form over
\text{\bf f}_{q}

(if
G
is a symplectic group or an orthogonal group) or
\text{\bf f}_{q^{2}}
(if
G
is a unitary group)
on which
G
acts.
Suppose that we have a Witt decomposition

V=X\oplus V_{0}\oplus Y

where
X,Y
are totally isotropic and dual to each other, and
V_{0}
is anisotropic.
The group

U=\{\,g\in G(V)\mid(g-1)(X\oplus V_{0})=0,\ (g-1)(Y)\subset X\,\}

is an abelian subgroup of
G
.
Then we have a decomposition

\rho|_{U}=\sum_{\phi\in{\mathcal{E}}(U)}m_{\phi}\phi

for
m_{\phi}\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
.
We say that
\rho|_{U}

contains

\phi
if
m_{\phi}\neq 0
.
Now each character
\phi
of
U
is naturally assigned a rank.

The irreducible character
\rho
is said to be of
U
-rank

k
, denoted by
U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=k
,
if the restriction
\rho|_{U}
contains a character
\phi
of rank
k
,
but contains no character of higher rank.
From the definition, we see that
0\leq U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)\leq n
where
n=\dim(X)=\dim(Y)
.
Moreover, it is clear that
U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho\chi)

for any linear character
\chi
of
G
because
\chi|_{U}
is always trivial.
An irreducible character
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{n})
is said to be of
low
U
-rank
if

U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)<\begin{cases}n,&\text{if $\text{\bf G}_{n}={\rm Sp}_{2n}$, ${\rm U}_{2n}$ or ${\rm U}_{2n+1}$};\\
n-1,&\text{if $\text{\bf G}_{n}={\rm O}^{+}_{2n}$, ${\rm O}^{-}_{2n+2}$, or ${\rm O}_{2n+1}$}.\end{cases}

An irreducible character
\rho
of
G_{n}
is said to have
asymptotic rank
less than or equal to
k
,
if for all sufficiently large
N
, there is a character
\rho_{N}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{N})

such that
U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{N})\leq k<N
and
\rho
occurs in
\rho_{N}|_{G_{n}}
.
The asymptotic rank of
\rho
is denoted by
A\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
.
As indicated in
[
GH20
]
, it is known that

(4.1)

U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)\leq A\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)

for any
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)
.

To study the representation theory of reductive groups,
parabolic induction is usually more convenient than usual induction,
so we modified the definition of asymptotic rank to define the parabolic asymptotic rank as follows.
An irreducible character
\rho
of
G_{n}
is said to have
parabolic asymptotic rank
less than or equal to
k
,
if for all sufficiently large
N
, there is a character
\rho_{N}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{G_{n}}^{G_{N}}(\rho)

such that
U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{N})\leq k<N
.
The parabolic asymptotic rank of
\rho
is denoted by
\overline{A}\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
.
It is clear from the definition that

(4.2)

A\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)\leq\overline{A}\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)

for any
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\rho
be an irreducible character of
G_{n}
and
m\geq n
.
Then

\overline{A}\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=\min\{\,\overline{A}\text{\rm-rk}(\rho^{\prime})\mid\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{G_{n}}^{G_{m}}(\rho)\,\}.

Proof.

Let
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{G_{n}}^{G_{m}}(\rho)
and suppose that
\overline{A}\text{\rm-rk}(\rho^{\prime})=k_{1}
.
This means for all sufficiently large
N
, there exists
\rho_{N}\in{\mathcal{E}}^{G_{N}}_{G_{m}}(\rho^{\prime})

such that
U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{N})=k_{1}<N
.
Clearly,
\rho_{N}
is in
{\mathcal{E}}_{G_{n}}^{G_{N}}(\rho)
,
so we have
\overline{A}\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)\leq k_{1}
.

On the other hand, suppose that
\overline{A}\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=k_{2}
.
Then for all sufficiently large
N
, there exists
\rho_{N}\in{\mathcal{E}}^{G_{N}}_{G_{n}}(\rho)

such that
U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho_{N})=k_{2}<N
.
For
m\geq n
, we know that

{\mathcal{E}}^{G_{N}}_{G_{n}}(\rho)=\bigcup_{\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}^{G_{m}}_{G_{n}}(\rho)}{\mathcal{E}}^{G_{N}}_{G_{m}}(\rho^{\prime}),

i.e.,
\rho_{N}\in{\mathcal{E}}^{G_{N}}_{G_{m}}(\rho^{\prime})
for some
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}^{G_{m}}_{G_{n}}(\rho)
.
Then we have
\overline{A}\text{\rm-rk}(\rho^{\prime})\leq k_{2}
for some
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}^{G_{m}}_{G_{n}}(\rho)
.
Thus the lemma is proved.
∎

According to
[

]
definition 6.4.1,
the
tensor rank
of an irreducible character

, denoted by
\otimes\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)
,
is defined as follows:

(1)

Suppose that
G

An irreducible character
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)

\rho

(\omega_{{\rm O}^{\epsilon_{1}}_{1},\text{\bf G}}\otimes\omega_{{\rm O}^{\epsilon_{2}}_{1},\text{\bf G}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\omega_{{\rm O}^{\epsilon_{k}}_{1},\text{\bf G}})|_{G}

for some
\epsilon_{i}=+
or
-
and
i=1,\ldots,k
.

(2)

Suppose that
G
is an orthogonal group.
An irreducible character
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)
has tensor rank less than or equal to
2k
if

\rho\chi
occurs in
(\omega_{{\rm Sp}_{2},\text{\bf G}})^{\otimes k}|_{G}
for some linear character
\chi
of
G
.

(3)

Suppose that
G
is a unitary group.
An irreducible character
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)
has tensor rank less than or equal to
k
if

\rho\chi
occurs in
(\omega_{{\rm U}_{1},\text{\bf G}})^{\otimes k}|_{G}
for some linear character
\chi
of
G
.

Here
\omega^{\otimes k}
means the tensor product
\omega\otimes\cdots\otimes\omega

of
k
copies of
\omega
.
Because

\displaystyle(\omega_{{\rm O}^{\epsilon_{1}}_{1},\text{\bf G}}\otimes\omega_{{\rm O}^{\epsilon_{2}}_{1},\text{\bf G}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\omega_{{\rm O}^{\epsilon_{k}}_{1},\text{\bf G}})|_{G}

\displaystyle=(\omega_{{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{k},\text{\bf G}})|_{G}

\displaystyle(\omega_{{\rm Sp}_{2},\text{\bf G}})^{\otimes k}|_{G}

\displaystyle=(\omega_{{\rm Sp}_{2k},\text{\bf G}})|_{G}

\displaystyle(\omega_{{\rm U}_{1},\text{\bf G}})^{\otimes k}|_{G}

\displaystyle=(\omega_{{\rm U}_{k},\text{\bf G}})|_{G}

for some
\epsilon=+
or
-
,
we see that

(4.4)

\otimes\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)

for any
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)
.

4.2.
\eta
- and
\underline{\theta}
-correspondence

The following fundamental result is from
[
GH20
]
:

Proposition 4.5

(Gurevich-Howe)
.

Let
(\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G})
be a dual pair in stable range, i.e., it is of one of the following types:

(i)

({\rm Sp}_{k},{\rm O}^{+}_{2n})
,
({\rm Sp}_{k},{\rm O}^{-}_{2n+2})
or
({\rm Sp}_{k},{\rm O}_{2n+1})
with
k
even and
k\leq n
;

(ii)

({\rm O}_{k}^{\epsilon},{\rm Sp}_{2n})
with
k\leq n
;

(iii)

({\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{2n})
or
({\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{2n+1})
with
k\leq n
.

Then
for
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})
there is a unique irreducible character

\eta(\rho^{\prime})\in\Theta_{\text{\bf G}}(\rho^{\prime})
of
U
-rank
k
, and
all other elements in
\Theta_{\text{\bf G}}(\rho^{\prime})
have
U
-rank less than
k
.
Furthermore, the mapping
\eta\colon{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}(G)
is injective.

The mapping
\eta\colon{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}(G)

is called the
\eta
-correspondence
for the dual pair
(\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G})

in stable range.
In
[
Pan20a
]
and
[
Pan20b
]
, a sub-relation of
\Theta
,
called the
\underline{\theta}
-correspondence
, is defined as follows.
Let
(\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G})
be one of the following types of dual pairs:

(I)

({\rm O}^{+}_{2n^{\prime}},{\rm Sp}_{2n})
or
({\rm Sp}_{2n^{\prime}},{\rm O}^{+}_{2n})

(II)

({\rm O}^{-}_{2n^{\prime}},{\rm Sp}_{2n})
or
({\rm Sp}_{2n^{\prime}},{\rm O}^{-}_{2n})

(III)

({\rm U}_{2n^{\prime}},{\rm U}_{2n})
or
({\rm U}_{2n^{\prime}+1},{\rm U}_{2n+1})

(IV)

({\rm U}_{2n^{\prime}},{\rm U}_{2n+1})
or
({\rm U}_{2n^{\prime}+1},{\rm U}_{2n})

Let
\Lambda^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime}}
and
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}}

such that
(\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda)\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime},\text{\bf G}}
.
Then we know that
|\Upsilon(\Lambda)|=|\Upsilon(\Lambda^{\prime})|+\tau

where
\tau
is given by

\tau=\begin{cases}n-n^{\prime}+\left\lfloor\frac{{\rm def}(\Lambda)}{2}\right\rfloor,&\text{for case (I);}\\
n-n^{\prime}-\left\lfloor\frac{{\rm def}(\Lambda)}{2}\right\rfloor,&\text{for case (II);}\\
n-n^{\prime}+|{\rm def}(\Lambda)|,&\text{for case (III);}\\
n-n^{\prime}-|{\rm def}(\Lambda)|-1,&\text{for case (IV).}\\
\end{cases}

For
\Lambda^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime}}
such that
\tau\geq 0
,
we define
\underline{\theta}(\Upsilon(\Lambda^{\prime}))

Agreement of

-ranks and

It is proved by Howe-Gurevich in
[

]

that
A\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=\otimes\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)

for any
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}({\rm GL}_{n}(q))
.
Now we want to apply their method to other classical groups.

Let
G=G_{n}=G(V)
and
V=X_{n}\oplus V_{0}\oplus Y_{n}
be a complete polarization of
V
where
V_{0}
is anisotropic.
Let
\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}
be a basis of
X_{n}
, and let
\{y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n}\}
be the
corresponding dual basis of
Y_{n}
, i.e.,
\langle x_{i},y_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta symbol.
For
1\leq k\leq n
, let
X_{k}
(resp.
Y_{k}
) denote the space spanned by
\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k}\}

(resp.
\{y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{k}\}
).
So we have the decomposition
V=X_{k}\oplus V_{n-k}\oplus Y_{k}
where
V_{n-k}
is a space of Witt index
n-k
.
Define

\displaystyle H_{k}

\displaystyle=\{\,g\in G(V)\mid(g-1)(X_{k})=0,\ (g-1)(Y_{k})=0\,\}.

Then
H_{k}
is a subgroup of
G(V)
,
H_{k}\subset H_{k-1}
for each

, and
H_{0}=G_{n}
.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{n})
be of low
U
-rank

U\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=k<\begin{cases}n,&\text{if\/ $\text{\bf G}_{n}$ is ${\rm Sp}_{2n}$, ${\rm U}_{2n}$ or ${\rm U}_{2n+1}$};\\
n-1,&\text{if\/ $\text{\bf G}_{n}$ is ${\rm O}^{+}_{2n}$, ${\rm O}^{-}_{2n+2}$ or ${\rm O}_{2n+1}$}.\end{cases}

Then
\rho|_{H_{k}}
contains a linear character.

Proof.

Suppose that
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{n})
is of low
U

k
.
We define the following unipotent subgroups of

\displaystyle U_{1}

\displaystyle=\{\,g\in G(V)\mid(g-1)(X_{k})=0,\ (g-1)(V_{n-k}\oplus Y_{k})\subset X_{k}\,\},

\displaystyle U_{2}

\displaystyle=\{\,g\in G(V)\mid(g-1)(X_{k}\oplus V_{n-k})=0,\ (g-1)(Y_{k})\subset X_{k}\,\},

\displaystyle U_{3}

\displaystyle=U_{1}\cap H_{k}.

It is not difficult to see that
U_{1}\simeq U_{2}\times U_{3}
,
and
U_{1}
is normalized by
H_{k}
.
Define the subgroup
U_{4}
of
G(V)
as follows:

•

If

k<n
,
and let

–

(g-1)y_{k+1}=ax_{k+1}

(g-1)(X_{k+1}\oplus V_{n-k-1}\oplus Y_{k})=0

If

k<n-1
,

, and

(g-1)(X_{k+2}\oplus V_{n-k-2}\oplus Y_{k})=0

•

If
G(V)
is a unitary group,
then
k<n
,
and let
U_{4}
be the subgroup of elements

such that

–

(g-1)y_{k+1}=ax_{k+1}

–

(g-1)(X_{k+1}\oplus V_{n-k-1}\oplus Y_{k})=0

\text{\bf f}_{q^{2}}
over

Finally we define the subgroup
U_{5}
of

•

If
G(V)

•

If
G(V)
is an orthogonal group,

–

(g-1)(X_{k+2}\oplus V_{n-k-2})=0

(g-1)(Y_{k+2})\subset X_{k+2}

There exists a linear character

of rank

and trivial on

,

still has rank

It is easy to see that
h(u_{2},u_{3})h^{-1}=(u_{2},u_{3}^{\prime})
for

,

and

Because now

.
Because now

Because

Let

Now we claim that the representation

The Clifford Algebra Bundle on Loop Space
Matthias LUDEWIG

Abstract
We construct a Clifford algebra bundle formed from the tangent bundle of the smooth loop space of a Riemannian manifold, which is a bundle of super von Neumann algebras on the loop space. We show that this bundle is in general non-trivial, more precisely that its triviality is obstructed by the transgressions of the second Stiefel–Whitney class and the first (fractional) Pontrjagin class of the manifold.

Introduction

The bundle of Clifford algebras
\mathrm{Cl}(X)
constructed from the tangent bundle over a Riemannian manifold
X
is fundamental to spin geometry.
In particular,
X
has a spin
c
structure if and only if it is oriented and the Dixmier–Douady class of
\mathrm{Cl}(X)
vanishes.
In particular, the spin
c
condition is related to (partial) triviality of
\mathrm{Cl}(X)
.
The purpose of this paper is to obtain similar results for the analogous bundle on the loop space
\mathrm{L}X
of an
oriented
Riemannian manifold, which is fundamental to string geometry (i.e., to spin geometry on the loop space).

The Riemannian metric on
X
(which we assume to be oriented throughout) induces a natural metric on the smooth loop space.
Forming the (infinite-dimensional) algebraic Clifford algebra on each tangent space is unproblematic, but in order to make the setting amenable to analysis, we must complete these algebras in some way.

It is a fact that the infinite-dimensional Clifford algebra has a unique
C^{*}
norm, and completing the fibers with respect to this norm yields a bundle of
C^{*}
-algebras.
However, it turns out that this bundle is always trivial, hence does not encode any information on whether the loop space satisfies a spin condition.

Instead, we consider a fiberwise completion in a suitable weak topology, which leads to a continuous bundle
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{L}X}
of von Neumann algebras.
The canonical grading of the Clifford algebra carries over to the von Neumann completion, and in fact, the fibers are
super factors of type I
, meaning that the fibers are type I von Neumann algebras with trivial graded center.

Super factors of type I come in two stable isomorphism classes:
Those of
even kind
, which are stably isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}
, and those of
odd kind
, which are stably isomorphic to
\mathrm{Cl}_{1}
.
It turns out that if the dimension of
X
is even, then the fibers of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{L}X}
are of even kind, while otherwise, they are of odd kind.

The classifying space of the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(A)
of a non-trivially graded, properly infinite super factor of type I turns out to be a product of Eilenberg–MacLane spaces,
B\mathrm{Aut}(A)\simeq K(\mathbb{Z},3)\times K(\mathbb{Z}_{2},1)
.
Hence bundles
\mathcal{A}\to X
with typical fiber
A
are classified by two characteristic classes

\mathrm{DD}(\mathcal{A})\in H^{3}(X,\mathbb{Z})
,
\mathfrak{or}(\mathcal{A})\in H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
,
which we call the
Dixmier–Douady class
and the
orientation class
.
The first is an analog of the class first defined in
[
10
]
.
The second class comes from the fact that we work with bundles of
super
algebras, and that all automorphisms considered are required to respect the grading.

Our main result is the calculations of these characteristic classes for the loop space Clifford algebra bundle
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{L}X}
, which in particular shows that it is non-trivial in many cases.
Explicitly, we find:

Main Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be an oriented Riemannian manifold of dimension
d\geq 5
.
Then

2\cdot\mathrm{DD}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{L}X})=\tau(p_{1}(X)),\qquad\mathfrak{or}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{L}X})=\tau(w_{2}(X)),

where
\tau
denotes transgression, and where
p_{1}(X)
and
w_{2}(X)
are the first Pontrjagin class, respectively the second Stiefel–Whitney class.
Moreover, if
X
is spin, then
\mathrm{DD}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{L}X})
equals the transgression of the fractional Pontrjagin class
\frac{1}{2}p_{1}(X)
.

In fact, we have a more refined version of the above theorem (see Theorem
4.16
):
There is a canonical characteristic class
\mathfrak{S}(X)\in H^{3}(\mathrm{L}X,\mathbb{Z})
on the loop space such that
2\cdot\mathfrak{S}(X)=\tau(p_{1}(X))
, which we call
loop spin class
(see Definition
4.12
),
and
\mathrm{DD}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{L}X})
is expressed in terms of this class.
The interesting point is that while the fractional Pontrjagin class
\frac{1}{2}p_{1}(X)
only exists when
X
is spin, the corresponding class
\mathfrak{S}(X)
on the loop space always exists.

The typical fiber of the bundle
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{L}X}
is a suitable completion
A_{d}
of the algebraic Clifford algebra on
H^{d}=L^{2}\big(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{d}\big)
.
The loop group
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d)
acts naturally on the von Neumann completion
A_{d}
by Bogoliubov automorphisms, and it turns out that
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{L}X}
can be written as an associated bundle to the looped frame bundle
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(X)
of
X
.
Our proof of Theorem
1.1
is then based on the fact that the map
\Omega\mathrm{SO}(d)\to\mathrm{Aut}(A_{d})
induces an isomorphism on
\pi_{k}
for
k\leq 2
; in other words,
\mathrm{Aut}(A_{d})
is the Postnikov truncation of
\Omega\mathrm{SO}(d)
.

The relation of our Clifford von Neumann algebra bundle to other objects from loop space spin geometry, such as the transgression of the Chern–Simons gerbe
[
34
]
and the loop space spinor bundle
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
is best understood using the language of 2-vector bundles
[
15
]
.
This point of view is discussed in
[
19
, Section 1]
.

Recall that a spin manifold
X
is called a
string manifold
if the fractional Pontrjagin class
\frac{1}{2}p_{1}(X)
vanishes.
Our theorem therefore implies in particular that the loop space Clifford algebra bundle
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{L}X}
of a string manifold
X
is trivializable.
Hence
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{L}X}
admits a bundle of irreducible modules, the
loop space spinor bundle
, so that
\mathrm{L}X
is spin.

The converse of the above statement is false, as the transgression
\tau\big(\frac{1}{2}p_{1}(X)\big)
may vanish without
\frac{1}{2}p_{1}(X)
being zero.
It is a general fact that such converses require the extra condition of
fusion

[
31
,
32
,
33
]
.
In the present context, it turns out that the bundle
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{L}X}
is the transgression of a certain bundle of
free fermion conformal nets

[
12
]
, which (on a spin manifold) is classified by
\frac{1}{2}p_{1}(X)
.
This will be discussed in future work.

2
Bundles of super von Neumann algebras

In this section, we define bundles of von Neumann algebras with fiber a super factor of type I, a notion that will be explained in the next subsection.
Throughout the paper, all Hilbert spaces are assumed to be separable and all von Neumann algebras are
\sigma
-finite.

2.1
Super factors of type I and their classification

A
super von Neumann algebra
is a von Neumann algebra
A
together with a normal (i.e., ultraweakly continuous) involutive
*
-automorphism
\gamma
.
Such an automorphism gives a direct sum decomposition
A=A^{0}\oplus A^{1}
, such that
A_{i}\cdot A_{j}\subset A_{i+j}
.
The graded center of
A
is defined as

Z(A)=\{a\in A\mid\forall b\in A\colon[a,b]=0\},

where the graded commutator is defined by
[a,b]=ab-(-1)^{|a||b|}ba
on homogeneous elements and extends to all of
A
by bilinearity.

Definition 2.1

(super factor)
.

We say that a von Neumann algebra
A
is a
super factor of type I
if it is type I (as an ungraded von Neumann algebra) and its graded center is equal to
\mathbb{C}
.
We say that
A
is
properly infinite
if its even part
A^{0}
is properly infinite in the usual sense.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
A
be a super factor.
Then the ungraded center
Z^{\mathrm{un}}(A)
is a graded subalgebra, which is isomorphic to either
\mathbb{C}

(
trivially graded
)
or to
\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}

(
with the grading operator given by swapping the two summands
)
.

Proof.

Let
a=a_{0}+a_{1}\in Z^{\mathrm{un}}(A)
.
Then comparing the odd and even components of
ab
and
ba
, for
b
homogeneous, shows that both
a_{0},a_{1}\in Z^{\mathrm{un}}(A)
.
Hence
Z^{\mathrm{un}}(A)
is a graded subalgebra.

The subalgebra
Z^{\mathrm{un}}(A)
is an abelian von Neumann algebra, hence isomorphic to
L^{\infty}(X)
for some measure space
X
.
As it is a graded subalgebra, we can write
Z^{\mathrm{un}}(A)=Z^{0}\oplus Z^{1}
for its graded components.
That
A
is a super factor implies that
Z^{0}=\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathbf{1}
, as any even element in the ungraded center is also an element of the graded center, which is trivial by assumption.
Hence
Z^{\mathrm{un}}(A)=\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathbf{1}\oplus Z^{1}
, where
Z^{1}
satisfies
Z^{1}\cdot Z^{1}\subseteq\mathbb{C}\cdot\mathbf{1}
.
Suppose that
Z^{\mathrm{un}}(A)\neq\mathbb{C}
.
Then there exists a projection
p\neq 0,1
in
Z^{\mathrm{un}}(A)
.
Write
p=\lambda\mathbf{1}+p^{1}
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
,
p^{1}\in Z^{1}(A)
.
Then
\lambda\neq 0
, as projections cannot be purely odd.
Now, for any other non-zero projection
q=\mu\mathbf{1}+q^{1}
with
pq=0
, we have

0=pq=\lambda\mu\mathbf{1}+p^{1}q^{1}+\lambda q^{1}+\mu p^{1}.

Considering the odd part, we obtain
\lambda q^{1}+\mu p^{1}=0
.
Hence (as both
\lambda,\mu\neq 0
)
q^{1}
is a multiple of
p^{1}
, so that
q
lies in the span of
1
and
p
.
As von Neumann algebras are generated by their projections, this implies that
Z^{\mathrm{un}}(A)
is two-dimensional.
An isomorphism
\mathbb{Z}^{\mathrm{un}}(A)\cong\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}
of super von Neumann algebras is then obtained by mapping
\lambda\mathbf{1}+\mu p^{1}
to
(\lambda+\mu,\lambda-\mu)\in\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}
, where
p^{1}\in Z^{1}(A)
is a self-adjoint element of norm one.
∎

Definition 2.3

(even/odd kind)
.

We say that a super factor
A
of type I is
of even kind
if its ungraded center
Z^{\mathrm{un}}(A)
is trivial, and
of odd kind
otherwise.

Example 2.4
.

The complex Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}_{d}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a finite-dimensional super factor of type I.
It is of even kind when
d
is even and of odd kind when
d
is odd.

Example 2.5
.

If
H
is a graded Hilbert space with grading operator
\Gamma
, then
\mathrm{B}(H)
is a super factor of type I with grading automorphism given by conjugation with
\Gamma
.

\mathrm{B}(H)
is always of even kind, and it is non-trivially graded if and only if
\Gamma
is non-trivial and properly infinite if and only if both
H^{0}
and
H^{1}
are infinite-dimensional.

Example 2.6
.

A type I super factor of odd kind is obtained by taking the super tensor product
{\mathrm{B}(H)\otimes\mathrm{Cl}_{1}}
, where
\mathrm{Cl}_{1}
is the complex Clifford algebra of degree one.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
A
be a super factor of type I.
If
A
is of even kind, then it is isomorphic to
\mathrm{B}(H)
, with the grading operator given by conjugation with some unitary involution
\Gamma
of
H
.
If
A
is of odd kind, then it is isomorphic to
\mathrm{B}(H)\oplus\mathrm{B}(H)
, with grading operator given by exchanging the two summands.

Proof.

We distinguish by the two cases of Lemma
2.2
.

(i) If
Z^{\mathrm{un}}(A)=\mathbb{C}
, then
A
is an ordinary type I factor, hence isomorphic to
\mathrm{B}(H)
.
As any automorphism of
\mathrm{B}(H)
is inner, the grading automorphism
\gamma
is given by conjugation with a unitary
\Gamma
, which must satisfy
\Gamma^{2}=z\cdot\mathbf{1}
for some
z\in\mathrm{U}(1)
as
\gamma
is an involution.
If
w
is some square root of
z
, then
\tilde{\Gamma}=\overline{w}\Gamma
also implements
\gamma
and satisfies
\tilde{\Gamma}^{2}=\mathbf{1}
.

(ii) If
Z^{\mathrm{un}}(A)=\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathbb{C}
, then (as
A
is type I), we have
A=\mathrm{B}(H)\oplus\mathrm{B}(K)
for Hilbert spaces
H
and
K
.
The grading operator
\gamma
of
A
is then given by conjugation with a unitary

\Gamma=\begin{pmatrix}u&z\\
x&w\end{pmatrix}

on
H\oplus K
.
Since the restriction of
\gamma
to
Z^{\mathrm{un}}(A)
swaps the two factors, we have

\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&\mathbf{1}_{K}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}u&z\\
x&w\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{1}_{H}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u^{*}&x^{*}\\
z^{*}&w^{*}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}uu^{*}&ux^{*}\\
xu^{*}&xx^{*}\end{pmatrix}

and

\begin{pmatrix}\mathbf{1}_{H}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}u&z\\
x&w\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&\mathbf{1}_{K}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u^{*}&x^{*}\\
z^{*}&w^{*}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}zz^{*}&zw^{*}\\
wz^{*}&ww^{*}\end{pmatrix}.

This implies that
u
and
w
are zero, hence
x
and
z
are unitary.
After modifying
\Gamma
by an element of
\mathrm{U}(1)
as above, we may assume that
\Gamma^{2}=\mathbf{1}
.
Then
x
and
z
are inverses to each other, and identifying
K
with
H
using
x
gives an isomorphism of
A
to a super factor of type I of the form claimed.
∎

Remark 2.8
.

A reformulation of the previous theorem is that each super factor is isomorphic to either
\mathrm{B}(H)
for some super Hilbert space
H
or to
\mathrm{B}(H)\,\bar{\otimes}\,\mathrm{Cl}_{1}
for some
ungraded
Hilbert space
H
.

Remark 2.9
.

By the isomorphism
\mathrm{Cl}_{1}\,\bar{\otimes}\,\mathrm{Cl}_{1}\cong\mathrm{B}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2}\big)
, the previous remark implies that if
A
and
B
are two super factors of type I, then their spatial super tensor product
A\,\bar{\otimes}\,B
is again a super factor of type I. Here the product in
A\,\bar{\otimes}\,B
is on homogeneous elements defined by

a_{1}\otimes b_{1}\cdot a_{2}\otimes b_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}(-1)^{|a_{2}||b_{1}|}a_{1}a_{2}\otimes b_{1}b_{2}.

Remark 2.10
.

The isomorphism classification of super factors of type I is complicated by the fact that some Hilbert spaces involved may be finite-dimensional.
In particular, in the case that
A=\mathrm{B}(H)
for some super Hilbert space
H
, there is a variety of possibilities, as the even and the odd part of
H
may have different dimensions.

Calling type I super factors
A
,
B

stably isomorphic
when
A\,\bar{\otimes}\,\mathrm{B}(H)\cong B\,\bar{\otimes}\,\mathrm{B}(H)
for some super Hilbert space
H
, the set of equivalence classes forms a group (isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and generated by
\mathrm{Cl}_{1}
), which is an infinite-dimensional version of the
graded Brauer group
1
1

1

This is the group of equivalence classes of finite-dimensional central simple algebras.
We remark that over
\mathbb{C}
, such algebras are the same thing as a finite-dimensional von Neumann algebras.
considered in
[

]
.

2.2
The automorphism group of super factors of type I

We will now calculate the automorphism group (i.e., the group of grading-preserving
*
-automorphisms) of a non-trivially graded, properly infinite super factor
A
of type I.
2
2

2

We remark that
*
-automorphisms of a von Neumann algebra are automatically normal, i.e., ultraweakly continuous.

To this end, let
H
be a Hilbert space and define involutions
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ev}}
and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{odd}}
on
H\oplus H
by

\Gamma_{\mathrm{ev}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\begin{pmatrix}1&\hphantom{-}0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad\Gamma_{\mathrm{odd}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(2.1)

Set moreover

A_{\mathrm{ev}}=\mathrm{B}(H\oplus H),\qquad A_{\mathrm{odd}}=\mathrm{B}(H)\oplus\mathrm{B}(H),

with grading automorphisms given by conjugation with
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ev}}
, respectively
\Gamma_{\mathrm{odd}}
.
Then
A_{\mathrm{ev}}
is of even kind, while
A_{\mathrm{odd}}
is of odd kind.
One can also show that any non-trivially graded, properly infinite super factor of type I is isomorphic to either
A_{\mathrm{ev}}
or
A_{\mathrm{odd}}
, assuming that the Hilbert space
H
is infinite-dimensional.

Proposition 2.11
.

The automorphism groups of
A_{\mathrm{ev}}
and
A_{\mathrm{odd}}
are given as follows.

(i)

\mathrm{Aut}(A_{\mathrm{ev}})\cong\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{U}(H)\times\mathrm{U}(H))\rtimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
acts by permuting the factors.

(ii)

\mathrm{Aut}(A_{\mathrm{odd}})\cong\mathrm{PU}(H)\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Here the letter
\mathrm{P}
denotes the corresponding projective group, i.e., the quotient by
\mathrm{U}(1)
(where
\mathrm{U}(1)
acts diagonally on
\mathrm{U}(H)\times\mathrm{U}(H)
).

Proof.

(i) It is well known that the group of (not necessarily grading-preserving) normal
*
-automorphisms of
A_{\mathrm{ev}}
is
\mathrm{PU}(H\oplus H)
, the projective unitary group of
H\oplus H
, which acts on
A_{\mathrm{ev}}
by conjugation.
That the conjugation with a unitary
U\in\mathrm{U}(H\oplus H)
is grading preserving is equivalent to the requirement

\Gamma_{\mathrm{ev}}UaU^{*}\Gamma_{\mathrm{ev}}=U\Gamma_{\mathrm{ev}}a\Gamma_{\mathrm{ev}}U^{*},\qquad\forall a\in A_{\mathrm{ev}}.

Hence
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ev}}U^{*}\Gamma_{\mathrm{ev}}U
is in the (ungraded) center of
A_{\mathrm{ev}}
, which consists only of multiples of the identity.
Consequently, there exists
\lambda\in\mathrm{U}(1)
such that
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ev}}U\Gamma_{\mathrm{ev}}=\lambda U
.
Writing

U=\begin{pmatrix}u&z\\
x&v\end{pmatrix},

we get

\begin{pmatrix}u&z\\
x&v\end{pmatrix}=\lambda\begin{pmatrix}u&-z\\
-x&v\end{pmatrix},

which implies that
\lambda\in\{\pm 1\}
and, moreover, this implies that either
x=z=0
(when
\lambda=1
) or
u=v=0
(when
\lambda=-1
).
Hence the non-zero entries must be unitary, and we obtain

\mathrm{Aut}(A_{\mathrm{ev}})=G_{\mathrm{ev}}/\mathrm{U}(1),

where

G_{\mathrm{ev}}=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}u&0\\
0&v\end{pmatrix}\right|u,v\in\mathrm{U}(H)\right\}\cup\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}0&z\\
x&0\end{pmatrix}\right|x,z\in\mathrm{U}(H)\right\}.

(2.2)

There is an obvious short exact sequence

\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{U}(H)\times\mathrm{U}(H))\to\mathrm{Aut}(A_{\mathrm{ev}})\to\mathbb{Z}_{2},

which is split by sending the generator of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
to the operator
\Gamma_{\mathrm{odd}}
defined in (
2.1
).
This realizes
\mathrm{Aut}(A_{\mathrm{ev}})
as a semidirect product of
\mathrm{P}(\mathrm{U}(H)\times\mathrm{U}(H))
with
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

(ii) It is straightforward to see that the group of not necessarily grading-preserving automorphisms of
A_{\mathrm{odd}}
is precisely
\mathrm{Aut}(A_{\mathrm{ev}})
.
The additional requirement that such an automorphism preserves the grading operator means that
U\Gamma_{\mathrm{odd}}a\Gamma_{\mathrm{odd}}U^{*}=\Gamma_{\mathrm{odd}}UaU^{*}\Gamma_{\mathrm{odd}}
for all
a\in A_{\mathrm{odd}}
, where
U
is a representing unitary.
This is the case if and only if and only if
\Gamma_{\mathrm{odd}}U\Gamma_{\mathrm{odd}}=\lambda U
for some
\lambda
in the (ungraded) center of
A_{\mathrm{odd}}
, which in this case is generated by the identity operator and
\Gamma_{\mathrm{ev}}
.
As
U
is either diagonal or off-diagonal, this means that

\begin{pmatrix}u&0\\
0&v\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda v&0\\
0&\mu u\end{pmatrix},\qquad\text{respectively}\qquad\begin{pmatrix}0&z\\
x&0\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\lambda x\\
\mu z&0\end{pmatrix},

for some
\lambda,\mu\in\mathbb{C}
.
So
u=\lambda v
, respectively
z=\lambda x
.
Hence

\mathrm{Aut}(A_{\mathrm{odd}})=G_{\mathrm{odd}}/\mathrm{U}(1),

where

G_{\mathrm{odd}}=\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}u&0\\
0&u\end{pmatrix}\right|u\in\mathrm{U}(H)\right\}\cup\left\{\left.\begin{pmatrix}0&u\\
u&0\end{pmatrix}\right|u\in\mathrm{U}(H)\right\}.

(2.3)

But clearly,
G_{\mathrm{odd}}\cong\mathrm{U}(H)\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, hence
\mathrm{Aut}(A_{\mathrm{odd}})\cong\mathrm{PU}(H)\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
∎

The automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(A)
of a von Neumann algebra
A
will always be endowed with Haagerup’s u-topology (see
[
14
, Section 3]
).
Under the identification of Proposition
2.11
, this coincides with the (quotient of the) strong topology on
G_{\mathrm{ev}}/\mathrm{U}(1)
, respectively
G_{\mathrm{odd}}/\mathrm{U}(1)

[
14
, Corollary 3.8]
.

Remark 2.12
.

The subgroups
G_{\mathrm{ev}}
and
G_{\mathrm{odd}}
of
\mathrm{U}(H\oplus H)
defined in (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) both have two connected components.
It is clear that the continuous group homomorphism
i
from
G_{\mathrm{ev}/\mathrm{odd}}
to
\mathrm{Aut}(A)
(sending a unitary
U
to the automorphism given by conjugation with
U
) induces an isomorphism on
\pi_{0}
.

Remark 2.13
.

Observe that
G_{\mathrm{ev}}
equals the set of homogeneous unitaries inside
A_{\mathrm{ev}}
.
It follows that all automorphisms of
A_{\mathrm{ev}}
are inner.
As
A_{\mathrm{odd}}\cap G_{\mathrm{odd}}=(G_{\mathrm{odd}})_{0}
, the identity component of
G_{\mathrm{odd}}
, only the automorphisms in the identity component
\mathrm{Aut}(A_{\mathrm{odd}})_{0}
are inner.

Observe that the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(A_{\mathrm{odd}})
is a subgroup of the automorphism group
\mathrm{Aut}(A_{\mathrm{ev}})
.
We will need the following lemma.

Lemma 2.14
.

If
H
is infinite-dimensional, then the inclusion
\mathrm{Aut}(A_{\mathrm{odd}})\to\mathrm{Aut}(A_{\mathrm{ev}})
is a weak homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

Consider the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{U}(1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{\mathrm{odd}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{\mathrm{odd}}/\mathrm{U}(1)=\mathrm{Aut}(A_{\mathrm{odd}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{U}(1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{\mathrm{ev}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G_{\mathrm{ev}}/\mathrm{U}(1)=\mathrm{Aut}(A_{\mathrm{ev}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

with exact rows.
It is clear that the inclusion
G_{\mathrm{odd}}\to G_{\mathrm{ev}}
is a weak homotopy equivalence, as it is on
\pi_{0}
by inspection, and the connected components of
G_{\mathrm{ev}}
and
G_{\mathrm{odd}}
are contractible.
We obtain that the first two vertical maps induce isomorphisms on
\pi_{k}
for all

, hence so must the third.
∎

Corollary 2.15
.

The classifying space of the automorphism group of a non-trivially graded, properly infinite super factor

B\mathrm{Aut}(A)\simeq K(\mathbb{Z},3)\times K(\mathbb{Z}_{2},1),

and the map induced by the inclusion

of the identity component is trivial in the second component.

Proof.

If

{\mathrm{Aut}(A)\cong\mathrm{PU}(H)\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}}
(Proposition

\mathrm{PU}(H)

If

In particular, we obtain that the homotopy groups of

\pi_{k}(\mathrm{Aut}(A))=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{2},&k=0,\\
\mathbb{Z},&k=2,\\

2.3
Bundles of graded type I factors

Let
S
be a topological space and let
\mathcal{A}_{s}
,
s\in S
be a collection of super von Neumann algebras.
For a subset
U\subset S
, we write
\mathcal{A}|_{U}
for the disjoint union of all
\mathcal{A}_{s}
,
s\in U
.
By a
local trivialization
, we mean a map
\varphi\colon\mathcal{A}|_{U}\to U\times A
,
A
a super von Neumann algebra, that restricts to grading preserving
*
-homomorphisms
\mathcal{A}_{s}
to
\{s\}\times A
for each
s\in U
.
Two local trivializations are called
compatible
if the corresponding transition function is continuous as a map
U\cap V\to\mathrm{Aut}(A)
(endowed with Haagerup’s u-topology).

Definition 2.16
.

A collection
\mathcal{A}
as above together with a maximal compatible collection of transition functions is called a (continuous)
von Neumann algebra bundle with typical fiber

A
.

If
P
is a principal
\mathrm{Aut}(A)
-bundle over
S
, then the associated bundle

\mathcal{A}=P\times_{\mathrm{Aut}(A)}A

is a von Neumann algebra bundle with typical fiber
A
in the sense of Definition
2.16
.
It follows that isomorphism classes of super von Neumann algebra bundles over
S
correspond bijectively to isomorphism classes of principal
\mathrm{Aut}(A)
-bundles (see, e.g.,
[
13
, Section 4]
).
As (for sufficiently nice spaces
S
) such bundles are in turn classified by maps to the classifying space
B\mathrm{Aut}(A)
, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 2.17
.

Isomorphism classes of von Neumann algebra bundles with typical fiber
A
over a paracompact Hausdorff space
S
are in bijection with homotopy classes of maps
S\to B\mathrm{Aut}(A)
.

Cohomology classes over
B\mathrm{Aut}(A)
provide characteristic classes for bundles with typical fiber
A
via pullback.
If
A
is a non-trivially graded, properly infinite super factor of type I,
Corollary
2.15
implies that

H^{3}(B\mathrm{Aut}(A),\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z},\qquad H^{1}(B\mathrm{Aut}(A),\mathbb{Z}_{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Denote by
\mathfrak{or}
the generator of
H^{1}(B\mathrm{Aut}(A),\mathbb{Z}_{2})
.
There is also a preferred generator
\mathrm{DD}
of
H^{3}(B\mathrm{Aut}(A),\mathbb{Z})
, defined as the transgression of the first Chern class of the canonical
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle over
\mathrm{Aut}(A)
(which over the identity component is
\mathrm{U}\big(A^{0}\big)\to\mathrm{Aut}(A)_{0}
);
see Appendix
A
for more details.

Definition 2.18

(characteristic classes)
.

Let
A
be a non-trivially graded, properly infinite super factor of type I and let
\mathcal{A}
be a von Neumann algebra bundle with typical fiber
A
and classifying map
f\colon S\to B\mathrm{Aut}(A)
.
The characteristic classes

\mathrm{DD}(\mathcal{A})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}f^{*}\mathrm{DD}\in H^{3}(S,\mathbb{Z}),\qquad\mathfrak{or}(\mathcal{A})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}f^{*}\mathfrak{or}\in H^{1}(S,\mathbb{Z}_{2})

will be called the
Dixmier–Douady class
, respectively the
orientation class
of
\mathcal{A}
.

The terminology for
\mathrm{DD}(\mathcal{A})
follows that for the analogous class for bundles with typical fiber the algebra of compact operators, first defined by Dixmier and Douady
[
10
]
.

By Corollary
2.15
, for a non-trivially graded, properly infinite super factor of type I,
B\mathrm{Aut}(A)
is a product of Eilenberg–MacLane spaces.
As these are classifying spaces for cohomology, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 2.19
.

Suppose that
S
has the homotopy type of a CW complex and let
\mathcal{A}
be a von Neumann algebra bundle with typical fiber
A
over
S
, where
A
is a super factor of type I.
Then
\mathcal{A}
is trivializable if and only if the characteristic classes
\mathrm{DD}(\mathcal{A})
and
\mathfrak{or}(\mathcal{A})
are zero.

Remark 2.20
.

For CW-complexes
S
, the characteristic classes of Definition
2.18
can be conveniently described using Čech cohomology, as follows.
Over a suitable open cover
\{O_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in I}
, we can choose super Hilbert spaces
H_{\alpha}
and grading-preserving
*
-isomorphisms
\phi_{\alpha}\colon\mathcal{A}|_{O_{\alpha}}\to\mathrm{B}(H_{\alpha})
.
Over two-fold intersections, we can choose families
U_{\alpha\beta}\colon(O_{\alpha}\cap O_{\beta})\times H_{\alpha}\to H_{\beta}
of homogeneous unitaries such that
\phi_{\alpha}\circ\phi_{\beta}^{-1}
is given by conjugation with
U_{\alpha\beta}
.
A
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued Čech 1-cocycle is obtained by defining
\varepsilon_{\alpha\beta}=\{\pm 1\}
, depending on whether
U_{\alpha\beta}
is grading preserving or grading reversing.
Over
O_{\alpha}\cap O_{\beta}\cap O_{\gamma}
, we have

U_{\gamma\alpha}U_{\beta\gamma}U_{\alpha\beta}=\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\cdot\mathrm{id}_{\alpha}

for some function
\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\colon O_{\alpha}\cap O_{\beta}\cap O_{\gamma}\to\mathrm{U}(1)
, so we obtain a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-valued Čech cochain
\{\lambda_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\}_{\alpha\beta\gamma\in I}
.
One checks that this cochain is closed with respect to the Čech coboundary, and that a cochain obtained from another choice of unitaries
\{\tilde{U}_{\alpha\beta}\}_{\alpha\beta\in I}
differs from this cochain by a coboundary.
Hence we obtain a well-defined element in
\check{H}^{2}(S,\mathrm{U}(1))
.
Under the Bockstein homomorphism for the sequence
\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}\to\mathrm{U}(1)
, this element corresponds to the Dixmier–Douady class.

The Dixmier–Douady class can also be defined for bundles
\mathcal{A}
with typical fiber a
finite-dimensional
non-trivially graded super factor
A
of type I.
One way to do this is through the Čech picture from Remark
2.20
.
A second, equivalent, way is via stabilization: We replace
\mathcal{A}
by the bundle
\mathcal{A}\,\bar{\otimes}\,\mathrm{B}(H)
for some infinite-dimensional super Hilbert space
H
such that both
H^{0}
and
H^{1}
are infinite-dimensional and take the characteristic classes in the sense of Definition
2.18
of this bundle.

Remark 2.21
.

Let
A=A_{\mathrm{ev}}
or
A_{\mathrm{odd}}
be one of the super factors from Section
2.1
, constructed in terms of a
finite-dimensional
Hilbert
H
.
In this case, it turns out that

H^{1}(B\mathrm{Aut}(A),\mathbb{Z}_{2})=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\qquad and\qquad H^{3}(B\mathrm{Aut}(A),\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}_{n},

where
n=\dim(H)
.
We get a group homomorphism
\mathrm{Aut}(A)\to\mathrm{Aut}(A\bar{\otimes}\mathrm{B}(H^{\prime}))
(where
H^{\prime}
is an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space as above) by sending
\varphi\to\varphi\otimes\mathrm{id}
, and one can show that pullback along this homomorphism induces an isomorphism on
H^{1}
and is reduction mod
n
on
H^{3}
.
If now
\mathcal{A}
is a bundle over
S
with typical fiber
A
, the classifying map for
\mathcal{A}\bar{\otimes}\mathrm{B}(H^{\prime})
factors through
B\mathrm{Aut}(A)
, which shows that the Dixmier–Douady class of such a bundle is
n
-torsion.

Remark 2.22
.

A bundle
\mathcal{A}
with typical fiber a properly infinite super factor of type I is trivial if and only if
\mathcal{A}=\mathrm{B}(\mathcal{H})
for some bundle
\mathcal{H}
of super Hilbert spaces over
S
.
For the class
\mathrm{DD}(\mathcal{A})
to be zero it suffices that
\mathcal{H}
exists as a bundle of Hilbert spaces which is not necessarily globally graded (i.e.,
\mathcal{H}
does split locally into two subbundles but not necessarily globally).
The class
\mathfrak{or}(\mathcal{A})
is zero if
\mathcal{H}
splits into the direct sum of two subbundles, but may only be a projective bundle.
In the ungraded setting, projective bundles of Hilbert spaces were discussed in
[
3
]
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
be two von Neumann algebra bundles over a space
S
with typical fibers type I super factors
A
and
B
.
Then the fiberwise spatial super tensor product
\mathcal{A}\,\bar{\otimes}\,\mathcal{B}
has a canonical structure of a von Neumann algebra bundle with typical fiber
A\,\bar{\otimes}\,B
, which is again a type I super factor (see Remark
2.9
).
The proof of the following result is analogous to that of Lemma 9 (respectively, Lemma 4) in
[
11
]
.

Proposition 2.23
.

We have

\mathfrak{or}(\mathcal{A}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B})=\mathfrak{or}(\mathcal{A})+\mathfrak{or}(\mathcal{B}),\qquad\mathrm{DD}(\mathcal{A}\bar{\otimes}\mathcal{B})=\mathrm{DD}(\mathcal{A})+\mathrm{DD}(\mathcal{B})+\beta(\mathfrak{or}(\mathcal{A})\smile\mathfrak{or}(\mathcal{B})),

where
\beta\colon H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Z}_{2})\to H^{3}(S,\mathbb{Z})
is the Bockstein homomorphism.

3
Clifford von Neumann algebras

In this section, we explain the construction of the von Neumann algebra completion of the algebraic Clifford algebra, given the choice of an equivalence class of (sub-)Lagrangians, and we recall the action by restricted orthogonal transformations on this algebra.
General references for the theory of Clifford (and, closely related, CAR) algebras and Fock representations are, e.g.,
[
2
,
24
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
29
,
36
]
.
The completion of the Clifford algebra to a hyperfinite factor of type II
1
is considered in
[
24
, Section 1.3]
, but the subsequent discussion of a completion to a factor of type I
∞
seems to be new.

3.1
Clifford algebras and Fock spaces

Let
H
be a real Hilbert space and denote its complexification by
H_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Let
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H)
be the algebraic Clifford algebra, generated by elements of
H_{\mathbb{C}}
, subject to the relation

v\cdot w+w\cdot v=-2\langle\overline{v},w\rangle.

In order to make the situation accessible to analysis, we have to complete
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H)
to
C^{*}
-algebra, using the
*
-operation given by

(v_{1}\cdots v_{n})^{*}=\overline{v}_{n}\cdots\overline{v}_{1},\qquad v,w\in H_{\mathbb{C}}.

In fact, any
*
-representation of
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H)
induces the same
C^{*}
-norm on
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H)

[
28
, Proposition 1]
, and it follows that the Clifford algebra has a unique norm-completion
\mathrm{Cl}(H)
to a
C^{*}
-algebra, which turns out to be isomorphic to the infinite tensor product
M_{2}(\mathbb{C})^{\otimes\infty}

[
2
]
.
It is moreover a Real
C^{*}
-algebra, as the complex conjugation of
H
extends to an anti-linear
*
-automorphism of
\mathrm{Cl}(H)
.

The situation is quite different when we ask for completions of
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H)
to a von Neumann algebra.
Such a completion can be obtained by the choice of a
Lagrangian
, which is a complex subspace
L\subset H_{\mathbb{C}}
such that
L^{\perp}=\overline{L}
.
The Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H)
then has a natural representation
\pi_{L}
on the
Fock space

\mathcal{F}_{L}=\Lambda L
(the Hilbert space exterior power of
L
) where elements
v\in L\subset H_{\mathbb{C}}
act by exterior multiplication and elements
v\in\overline{L}\subset H_{\mathbb{C}}
act by contraction. We can therefore take the von Neumann completion

\mathrm{Cl}_{L}(H):=\pi_{L}(\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H))^{\prime\prime}

in the space
\mathrm{B}(\mathcal{F}_{L})
of bounded operators on
\mathcal{F}_{L}
.
The Fock space is a super Hilbert space with its even/odd grading and the Fock representation is a graded representation, hence
\mathrm{Cl}_{L}(H)
is naturally a super von Neumann algebra.
However, the real structure on
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H)
does
not
extend to a real structure on
\mathrm{Cl}_{L}(H)
if
H
is infinite-dimensional. It follows from irreducibility of the Fock representation
\mathcal{F}_{L}

[
24
, Theorem 2.4.2]
that any bounded operator on
\mathcal{F}_{L}
commuting with the Clifford action is scalar, hence

\mathrm{Cl}_{L}(H)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\pi_{L}(\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H))^{\prime\prime}=\mathrm{B}(\mathcal{F}_{L}),

(3.1)

is a super factor of type I, of even kind.

The choice of Lagrangian can be partially eliminated as follows:
Two Lagrangians
L_{1}
,
L_{2}\subset H
are
equivalent
if the difference
P_{L_{1}}-P_{L_{2}}
is a Hilbert–Schmidt operator, where
P_{L_{i}}
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
L_{i}
.
By the
Segal–Shale equivalence criterion
, two Fock representation
\pi_{L_{1}}
and
\pi_{L_{2}}
are unitarily equivalent if and only if
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are equivalent
[
24
, Theorem 3.4.1]
.
Moreover, the unitary implementing the equivalence is grading-preserving if and only if
\dim\big(\overline{L}_{1}\cap L_{2}\big)
is even (see
[
25
, Theorem 1.22]
and
[
24
, Theorem 3.5.1]
).
We denote the equivalence class of a Lagrangian
L
by
[L]
.

For any
L^{\prime}\in[L]
,
\mathrm{Cl}_{L^{\prime}}(H)
is a completion of
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H)
with respect to the pullback via
\pi_{L^{\prime}}
of the weak operator topology on
\mathrm{B}(\mathcal{F}_{L^{\prime}})
.
However, as all representations
\pi_{L^{\prime}}
,
L^{\prime}\in[L]
, are equivalent, all these topologies coincide. As any two completions of a topological vector space are canonically isomorphic, we obtain a universal Clifford algebra associated to an equivalence class of Lagrangians.

Remark 3.1
.

An explicit description of this von Neumann algebra is as the set of equivalence classes
(a_{L^{\prime}})_{L^{\prime}\in[L]}
with
a_{L^{\prime}}\in\mathrm{Cl}_{L^{\prime}}(H)
, which are related by
\varphi_{L^{\prime},L^{\prime\prime}}(a_{L^{\prime}})=a_{L^{\prime\prime}}
, with
\varphi_{L^{\prime},L^{\prime\prime}}
the unique normal
*
-homomorphism
\mathrm{B}(\mathcal{F}_{L^{\prime}})\to\mathrm{B}(\mathcal{F}_{L^{\prime\prime}})
sending
\pi_{L^{\prime}}(v)
to
\pi_{L^{\prime\prime}}(v)
for every
v\in H
.
Another approach is to take the abstract completion of
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H)
with respect to the ultraweak topology induced by any
\pi_{L}
, defined in terms of equivalence classes of Cauchy nets.

A
sub-Lagrangian
is a closed subspace
L\subset H_{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\overline{L}\subseteq L^{\perp}
and such that
L+\overline{L}
has finite codimension in
H_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Again, two sub-Lagrangians
L_{1}
,
L_{2}
are called
equivalent
if
P_{L_{1}}-P_{L_{2}}
is a Hilbert–Schmidt operator.
Associated to an equivalence class of sub-Lagrangians, we still have a canonical completion of
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H)
, constructed as follows.
First we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
L_{1}
,
L_{2}
are two equivalent sub-Lagrangians, then
\dim\big(L_{1}\oplus\overline{L}_{1}\big)^{\perp}
and
\dim\big(L_{2}\oplus\overline{L}_{2}\big)^{\perp}
have the same parity.

Proof.

Consider the operators
J_{i}^{\mathbb{C}}=i(P_{L_{i}}-P_{\overline{L}_{i}})
on
H_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Since
\overline{P}_{L_{i}}=P_{\overline{L}_{i}}
, the operators
J_{i}^{\mathbb{C}}
commute with complex conjugation, hence are the complex linear extension of operators on
H
denoted by
J_{i}
.
Observe that these operators are skew-adjoint, hence by
[
4
]
, they have a well-defined index
\operatorname{ind}(J_{i})=\dim\ker(J_{i})\mod 2\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
Observe that
\ker(J_{i})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}=\ker\big(J_{i}^{\mathbb{C}}\big)=\big(L_{i}\oplus\overline{L}_{i}\big)^{\perp}
, hence
\operatorname{ind}(J_{i})=\dim\big(L_{i}\oplus\overline{L}_{i}\big)^{\perp}\mod 2
.
However, by the assumption that
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
are equivalent, the difference
J_{1}-J_{2}
is a Hilbert–Schmidt operator, in particular compact.
This implies that
\operatorname{ind}(J_{1})=\operatorname{ind}(J_{2})
, so the lemma follows.
∎

For a sub-Lagrangian
L
, consider the complex subspace
K=\big(L\oplus\overline{L}\big)^{\perp}
of
H_{\mathbb{C}}
.
The construction of the desired completion of
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H)
depends on the dimension of
K
.

(i)

If
K
is even-dimensional, we can find a Lagrangian
F\subset K
, and
L+F\in[L]
is a Lagrangian in
H_{\mathbb{C}}
.
This yields the completion
\mathrm{Cl}_{L+F}(H)
of
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H)
.
If
L^{\prime}
is a sub-Lagrangian equivalent to
L
, then by Lemma
3.2
,
K^{\prime}=\big(L^{\prime}\oplus\overline{L}^{\prime}\big)^{\perp}
is still even-dimensional, and for any Lagrangian
F^{\prime}\subset K^{\prime}
,
L^{\prime}+F^{\prime}
is equivalent to
L+F
.
Hence
\mathrm{Cl}_{L^{\prime}+F^{\prime}}(H)
is canonically isomorphic to
\mathrm{Cl}_{L+F}(H)
.

(ii)

If
K
is odd-dimensional, then
K\oplus\mathbb{C}
is even-dimensional and admits a Lagrangian
F\subset K\oplus\mathbb{C}
.
Then
L+F
is a Lagrangian in
H_{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\mathbb{C}
, equivalent to the sub-Lagrangian
L\oplus\{0\}
.
Hence we obtain the completion
\mathrm{Cl}_{L+F}(H)
of
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H\oplus\mathbb{R})\cong\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H)\otimes\mathrm{Cl}_{1}
.
In particular, we get a completion of
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H)
, as a closed subalgebra of
\mathrm{Cl}_{L+F}(H)
.
If
L^{\prime}\subset H_{\mathbb{C}}
is a sub-Lagrangian equivalent to
L
,
K^{\prime}=\big(L^{\prime}\oplus\overline{L}^{\prime}\big)^{\perp}
is still odd-dimensional, by Lemma
3.2
, and for any Lagrangian
F^{\prime}\subset K^{\prime}\oplus\mathbb{C}
,
L^{\prime}+F^{\prime}
is equivalent to
L+F
.
Hence
\mathrm{Cl}_{L^{\prime}+F^{\prime}}(H)
is canonically isomorphic to
\mathrm{Cl}_{L+F}(H)
, and the isomorphism induces an isomorphism between the corresponding completions of
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H)
.

We denote by
\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H)
the canonical von Neumann completion of
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H)
, determined by the equivalence class
[L]
of sub-Lagrangians, as constructed above.

Observe that in the case that
K
is even-dimensional, (
3.1
) implies that
\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H)\cong\mathrm{B}(\mathcal{F}_{L^{\prime}})
, for any Lagrangian
L^{\prime}\in[L]
.
Hence
\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H)
is of even kind.
If
K
is odd-dimensional, then
\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H)\otimes\mathrm{Cl}_{1}\cong\mathrm{B}(\mathcal{F}_{L})
for some Lagrangian
L
in
H_{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\mathbb{C}
equivalent to
L\oplus\{0\}
.
Hence
\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H)
is of odd kind.

3.2
The restricted orthogonal group

The algebraic Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(H)
has an action of the orthogonal group
\mathrm{O}(H)
by Bogoliubov automorphisms, by its universal property.
This action extends to an action on the
C^{*}
-Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}(H)
.
In contrast, by the Segal–Shale equivalence criterion, the Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H)
does no longer have an action of the entire orthogonal group
\mathrm{O}(H)
.

Definition 3.3

(restricted orthogonal group)
.

The
restricted orthogonal group
of
H
with respect to an equivalence class
[L]
of sub-Lagrangians, denoted by
\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H,[L])
, consists of those orthogonal transformations
g
of
H
such that the commutator
[g,P_{L}]
with the orthogonal projection
P_{L}
onto
L
is a Hilbert–Schmidt operator.
If the equivalence class
[L]
is clear from the context, we write just
\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H)
.

That
\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H,[L])
acts on
\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H)
is well known in the case that
L
is equivalent to a Lagrangian (see, e.g.,
[
2
, Section 6]
).
To get the same statement in the odd case, embed

\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H,[L])\longrightarrow\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H\oplus\mathbb{R},[L\oplus 0])

with the upper left corner embedding.
Then
L\oplus 0
is equivalent to a Lagrangian and the latter group now acts on the Clifford von Neumann algebra
\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H\oplus\mathbb{R})\cong\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H)\otimes\mathrm{Cl}_{1}
of even kind.
Then
\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H,[L])
preserves the subalgebra

\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H)\cong\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H)\otimes\mathbb{C}\subset\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H)\otimes\mathrm{Cl}_{1}.

We always consider
\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H,[L])
with the coarsest topology finer than the norm topology induced from
\mathrm{O}(H)
that makes the group homomorphism

\theta\colon\ \mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H)\to\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H))

(3.2)

continuous, which sends an orthogonal transformation to its Bogoliubov automorphism.
In fact,
\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H,[L])
is a Banach Lie group with this topology
[
26
, Sections 6.2 and 2.4]
.

Theorem 3.4
.

The map
\theta
from (
3.2
) induces an isomorphism on
\pi_{k}
for
k\leq 5
.

The proof uses the bundle of implementers, defined as follows.
Depending on whether the equivalence class
[L]
contains a Lagrangian or not, we may choose a Lagrangian
L
either in
H_{\mathbb{C}}
or in
H_{\mathbb{C}}\oplus\mathbb{C}
and let
\mathcal{F}_{L}
be the corresponding Fock space.
For
g\in\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H,[L])
, define

\mathrm{Imp}_{g}=\{U\in\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{F}_{L})\mid\forall v\in H\colon\pi_{L}(gv)=U\pi_{L}(v)U^{*}\}.

By irreducibility of the Fock representation,
\mathrm{Imp}_{g}
is a
\mathrm{U}(1)
-torsor.
It follows from the proof of Proposition
2.11
that
U
is either even or odd.
Let
\mathrm{Imp}
be the union of all
\mathrm{Imp}_{g}
, a subgroup of
\mathrm{U}(\mathcal{F}_{L})
.
Then
\mathrm{Imp}
can be equipped with the structure of a Banach Lie group such that the map
\mathrm{Imp}\to\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H)
is a central extension of Banach Lie groups (where the fiber over
g
is
\mathrm{Imp}_{g}
), see
[
18
, Section 3.5]
.

Proof.

First suppose that
\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H)
is of even kind.
In this case, we may assume that
L
is a Lagrangian, so that
\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H)\cong\mathrm{B}(\mathcal{F}_{L})
.
Now, the group
\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H)
is well known to have the homotopy type of the based loop space of the infinite orthogonal group
[
26
, Proposition 12.4.2]
.
In particular, the first few homotopy groups are

\pi_{k}(\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H))=\begin{cases}\mathbb{Z}_{2},&k=0,\\
0,&k=1,\\
\mathbb{Z},&k=2,\\
0,&k=3,4,5.\end{cases}

(3.3)

Comparing with (
2.4
), we observe that it has the same homotopy groups as
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H))
for
k\leq 5
.
For
k\notin\{0,2\}
, the statement of the theorem is therefore automatic, and it remains to consider the cases
k=0
and
k=2
.

For
k=0
, we use that when
g
does not lie in the identity component of
\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H)
, then
\dim\big(g\overline{L}\cap L\big)
is odd
[
24
, Theorem 3.5.1]
.
Hence the (projectively unique) unitary
U\colon\mathcal{F}_{gL}\to\mathcal{F}_{L}
with
\pi_{gL}(a)=U\pi_{L}(a)U^{*}
(which exists as
gL
and
L
are equivalent) is parity reversing.
Let
\Lambda_{g}\colon\mathcal{F}_{L}\to\mathcal{F}_{gL}
be the unitary map given by taking the exterior power of
g
.
As
\Lambda_{g}
is parity preserving, the unitary
U\Lambda_{g}
on
\mathcal{F}_{L}
is still parity reversing.
By Remark
2.12
, this implies that the
*
-automorphism of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H))\cong\mathrm{B}(\mathcal{F}_{L})
given by conjugation with
U\Lambda_{g}
lies in the non-identity component of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H))
. But
U\Lambda_{g}
implements
\theta(g)
, hence
[\theta(g)]
is the non-trivial element in
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H)))
.

We now consider
k=2
.
Here we use the fact that
\mathrm{Imp}
is a generator for the group of line bundles over the identity component
\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H)_{0}
, i.e., the first Chern class of
\mathrm{Imp}
is a generator for
H^{2}(\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H)_{0},\mathbb{Z})\cong\pi_{2}(\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H))\cong\mathbb{Z}

[
29
, Proposition 1.2]
.

On the other hand,
\mathrm{Imp}
is (by definition) the pullback of the canonical line bundle over
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H))

(
given over the identity component by
\mathrm{U}\big(A^{0}\big)
)
, the first Chern class of which is a generator for
H^{2}(\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H))_{0},\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
.

But this implies that
\theta
is an isomorphism on
H^{2}
, hence also on
\pi_{2}
(applying the Hurewicz isomorphism to the identity component).

This finishes the proof in the even case, so we now discuss the odd case.
Then there exists a sub-Lagrangian
L
in the fixed equivalence class such that
K=\big(L\oplus\overline{L}\big)^{\perp}
is one-dimensional.
The Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H^{\prime})
, is then of even kind, where
H^{\prime}\subset H
is the real subspace of
K^{\perp}
.
We now have the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H^{\prime})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H^{\prime}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H)),}

where the right vertical map is
\theta\mapsto\theta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{\mathrm{Cl}_{1}}
(using the isomorphism
\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H)\cong\mathrm{Cl}_{[L]}(H^{\prime})\otimes\mathrm{Cl}_{1}
) and the left vertical map is the upper corner embedding
\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H^{\prime})\to\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H)
.
The latter is a homotopy equivalence, as the quotient
\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H^{\prime})/\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H)
is homeomorphic to the unit sphere in

, which is contractible.
The long exact sequence for homotopy groups therefore implies that the map
\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H^{\prime})\to\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H)
is a weak homotopy equivalence.
By the first part of the proof, the bottom horizontal map induces an isomorphism on
\pi_{k}
for
k\leq 5
.
The same statement for the top horizontal map now follows from commutativity of the diagram and the fact that all groups involved are either
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.
∎

4
The loop space Clifford algebra bundle

In this section, we define the loop space Clifford algebra bundle and calculate its characteristic classes.
We also discuss transgression and define the loop spin class.

4.1
Definition of the bundle

Let
X
be an oriented Riemannian manifold of dimension
d
and let
\mathrm{L}X=C^{\infty}\big(S^{1},X\big)
be its smooth loop space.

\mathrm{L}X
is an infinite-dimensional manifold, modeled on the nuclear Fréchet space
C^{\infty}\big(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{d}\big)
.
Its tangent space
T_{\gamma}\mathrm{L}X
at a loop
\gamma\in\mathrm{L}X
can be identified with the space
C^{\infty}\big(S^{1},\gamma^{*}TX\big)
of vector fields along
\gamma
.
It has a natural inner product coming from the standard parametrization of
S^{1}
and the Riemannian metric of
X
, turning it into a pre-Hilbert space.
As we will form completed Clifford algebras (which are insensitive to whether the underlying pre-Hilbert space is complete or not
[
24
]
), it is natural to consider the completion

\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}L^{2}\big(S^{1},\gamma^{*}TX\big),

of the tangent space.
These Hilbert spaces fit together to a bundle
\mathcal{H}
of Hilbert spaces over
\mathrm{L}X
.
To describe the bundle structure of
\mathcal{H}
, let
\mathrm{SO}(X)
be the oriented frame bundle of
X
and
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(X)
its loop space.

\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(X)
is a principal
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d)
-bundle as
X
is orientable.
3
3

3

Here one only needs to show that any loop
\gamma
has a lift to
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(X)
. Such a lift exists precisely if
\gamma^{*}TX
is trivializable.
Now, a vector bundle
E
over
S^{1}
is trivializable if and only if
w_{1}(E)=0
. For
E=\gamma^{*}TX
, we have
w_{1}(\gamma^{*}TX)=\gamma^{*}w_{1}(TX)
, which is zero for all loops
\gamma
if and only if
X
is orientable.

Now,
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d)
acts on the Hilbert space

H^{d}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}L^{2}\big(S^{1},\mathbb{R}^{d}\big)

(4.1)

by pointwise multiplication, and we have the canonical identification

\mathcal{H}=\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(X)\times_{\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d)}H^{d}.

Here we interpret elements
q\in\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(X)
as orthogonal transformations
H^{d}\to\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}
.
As the group
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d)
acts smoothly on
H^{d}
, this bundle is a smooth bundle of Hilbert spaces.
4
4

4

We remark that often, Hilbert spaces bundles are only continuous (namely when they have the structure group
\mathrm{U}(H)
with its strong topology, which is not a Lie group).
But in this case, the map
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d)\to\mathrm{O}\big(H^{d}\big)
is smooth when the latter group carries its Banach Lie group structure.

For each
\gamma\in\mathrm{L}X
, we can form the algebraic Clifford algebra
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{H}_{\gamma})
and its canonical
C^{*}
-completion
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{H}_{\gamma})
.
These algebras fit together to a continuous bundle of
C^{*}
-algebras, which, as above, can be identified with the associated bundle
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(X)\times_{\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d)}\mathrm{Cl}\big(H^{d}\big)
, where
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d)
acts on
\mathrm{Cl}\big(H^{d}\big)
through
\mathrm{O}\big(H^{d}\big)
, by Bogoliubov automorphisms.
Here it is important that the homomorphism
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d)\to\mathrm{O}\big(H^{d}\big)\to\mathrm{Aut}\big(\mathrm{Cl}\big(H^{d}\big)\big)
is continuous when
\mathrm{O}\big(H^{d}\big)
carries the norm topology
[
1
, Proposition 4.35]
; this implies that
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{H})
has the structure of a continuous bundle of
C^{*}
-algebras.
However, this bundle seems rather uninteresting for loop space spin geometry, by the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

The bundle
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{H})
is trivial.

Proof.

The action of
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d)
extends to an action of the orthogonal group
\mathrm{O}\big(H^{d}\big)
of
H^{d}
, which is contractible by Kuiper’s theorem.
So
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{H})
is an associated bundle for the principal
\mathrm{O}\big(H^{d}\big)
-bundle
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(X)\times_{\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d)}\mathrm{O}\big(H^{d}\big)
, which must be trivial by contractibility of
\mathrm{O}\big(H^{d}\big)
and its classifying space
B\mathrm{O}\big(H^{d}\big)
.
Hence
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{H})
is trivial as well.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

As an infinite tensor product algebra,
\mathrm{Cl}\big(H^{d}\big)
is an example of a so-called
strongly self-absorbing

C^{*}
-algebra, which has a contractible automorphism group
[
8
,
9
]
.
Hence
any
bundle with typical fiber
\mathrm{Cl}\big(H^{d}\big)
must in fact be contractible.
However, this argument does not take into account the grading or the real structure, while the above argument also shows that
\mathrm{Cl}(\mathcal{H})
is trivial as a bundle of graded, real
C^{*}
-algebras.

In order to obtain a non-trivial bundle, we now construct a suitable completion of
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{H})
to a bundle of von Neumann algebras.
To this end, we observe that the model space
H^{d}_{\mathbb{C}}
admits a canonical sub-Lagrangian

L^{d}=\mathrm{span}\big\{\xi\otimes{\rm e}^{\rm int}\mid n\in\mathbb{N},\,\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\big\}.

(4.2)

The space
\big(L^{d}+\overline{L}^{d}\big)^{\perp}
is just the space of constant functions on the circle, which has dimension
d
.
By the discussion in Section
3.1
, we therefore obtain a canonical von Neumann completion

A_{d}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\mathrm{Cl}_{[L^{d}]}\big(H^{d}\big),

(4.3)

which is of even kind when
d
is even and of odd kind when
d
is odd.

To obtain a von Neumann completion
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}
of
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{H}_{\gamma})
for
\gamma\in\mathrm{L}X
, we observe that any lift
{q\in\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(X)}
gives a Lagrangian
qL^{d}\subset\mathcal{H}_{\gamma}
.
It is now crucial that the multiplication action of
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d)
on
H^{d}
is in fact by elements of the restricted orthogonal group
\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}\big(H^{d}\big)=\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}\big(H^{d},\big[L^{d}\big]\big)
and that we get a continuous group homomorphism

\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d)\longrightarrow\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}\big(H^{d}\big),

(4.4)

see
[
26
, Proposition 6.3.1]
,
[
18
, Proposition 3.23]
.
Hence for any two lifts

of
\gamma

qL^{d}
and
q^{\prime}L^{d}
are equivalent.
We therefore obtain a well-defined von Neumann completion
\mathcal{A}_{\gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\mathrm{Cl}_{[qL^{d}]}(\mathcal{H}_{\gamma})
, independent of the choice of
q
.
These algebras can be canonically identified with the fibers of the bundle

\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{L}X}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(X)\times_{\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d)}A_{d}.

(4.5)

This is a continuous bundle of von Neumann algebras as the homomorphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\theta}\colon\ \mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}\big(H^{d}\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Aut}(A_{d})}

(4.6)

obtained by composing (
4.4
) with the Bogoliubov action (
3.2
) is continuous.

Remark 4.3
.

An alternative construction of the von Neumann completion of
\mathrm{Cl}_{\mathrm{alg}}(\mathcal{H}_{\gamma})
is the following.
Let
D_{\gamma}=\mathrm{i}\frac{\nabla}{{\rm d}t}
be the operator acting on the bundle
\gamma^{*}TX\otimes\mathbb{C}

Let

be the Hilbert space direct sum of eigenspaces to negative eigenvalues of

.
This is a sub-Lagrangian in

for any

.
For details, see
[

.

Remark 4.4
.

A closely connected bundle of Clifford algebras on

has recently been considered in the somewhat different context of

[

For a Lie group
G

For

.

If

\pi_{k}
for
k\leq 2
.

Proof.

For
d

[
26

, for any

, the map
\Omega\mathrm{SO}(2m)\to\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}(H^{2m})
is
(2m-3)
-connected.
This shows the claim in even dimensions
d=2m\geq 6
.
For
d\geq 5
odd, we consider the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega\mathrm{SO}(d)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}\big(H^{d}\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega\mathrm{SO}(d+1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{O}_{\mathrm{res}}\big(H^{d+1}\big),}

(4.7)

the bottom map of which is an isomorphism on

for

by the discussion for the even case.
The right vertical map in (

) is the restriction of the map

The left vertical map of the diagram is induced by the canonical embedding

for
k\leq 3
. By

into the

for

.

By the above considerations and the commutativity of (

As seen in (

4.2
Transgression and the loop spin class

For a manifold
Y
and a coefficient group
R
,
transgression
is the composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau\colon\ H^{k}(Y,R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{k}\big(\mathrm{L}Y\times S^{1},R\big)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{k-1}(\mathrm{L}Y,R),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{ev}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\int_{S^{1}}}

where the left map is pullback with the evaluation map
\mathrm{ev}\colon\mathrm{L}Y\times S^{1}\to Y
and the right map is fiber integration over the
S^{1}
factor.
Transgression is natural, in the sense that for a smooth map
f\colon Y\to Y^{\prime}
, the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{k}(Y^{\prime},R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{k-1}(\mathrm{L}Y^{\prime},R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{k}(Y,R)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{k-1}(\mathrm{L}Y,R)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{L}f^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau}

commutes. Let
G=\mathrm{SO}(d)
or
\mathrm{Spin}(d)
.
The classifying spaces
BG
and the universal bundle
EG
admit an infinite dimensional manifold model, so that their smooth loop space
\mathrm{L}BG
is well-defined.
As
\mathrm{L}EG
is again contractible and
\mathrm{L}G
acts freely on it, the quotient
\mathrm{L}BG
is a model for the classifying space
B\mathrm{L}G
.
We therefore have transgression homomorphisms

\tau\colon\ H^{k}(BG,R)\longrightarrow H^{k-1}(B\mathrm{L}G,R).

Remark 4.7
.

Of course, transgression is also defined for general topological spaces, using the continuous loop space instead of the smooth version.
However, as we work with the smooth loop space throughout, we presented the construction in this case.
We recall that the smooth loop space of a manifold is homotopy equivalent to the continuous loop space, and the same is true for based loop spaces
[
6
, Proposition 7.1]
.

The base loop group
\Omega G
is the kernel of the evaluation-at-zero map
\mathrm{ev}_{0}\colon\mathrm{L}G\to G

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{L}G}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces G,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}

(4.8)

which is split via the inclusion

as constant loops.
This induces a fibration of the corresponding classifying spaces, and an exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{3}(B\mathrm{SO}(d),\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{3}(B\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d),\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{3}(B\Omega\mathrm{SO}(d),\mathbb{Z})}

(4.9)

on the corresponding cohomology groups.
The first group here is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, generated by the third universal integral Stiefel–Whitney class
W_{3}
(i.e., the Bockstein image of the second universal Stiefel–Whitney class
w_{2}
).
As
B\Omega\mathrm{SO}(d)\simeq\mathrm{SO}(d)
, the right group equals
H^{3}(\mathrm{SO}(d),\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}
.

Lemma 4.8
.

If
d\geq 5
, the sequence (
4.9
) is split exact, hence we have a canonical isomorphism

H^{3}(B\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d),\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Proof.

As (
4.8
) is split exact, with
G=\mathrm{SO}(d)
including into
\mathrm{L}G
as constant loops, the corresponding fibration of classifying spaces admits a section
B\iota
.
This induces a left split of (
4.9
).
That the sequence (
4.9
) is exact in the middle follows from the Serre spectral sequence for the classifying space fibration of (
4.8
).
The right map in (
4.9
) is surjective by the following argument.
By Corollary
4.6
, the map
B\tilde{\theta}\colon B\Omega\mathrm{SO}(d)\to B\mathrm{Aut}(A_{d})
induces an isomorphism on
\pi_{k}
for
k\leq 3
.
It is moreover trivially surjective on
\pi_{4}
as
\pi_{4}(B\mathrm{Aut}(A_{d}))=0
.
Hence pullback induces an isomorphism

B\tilde{\theta}^{*}\colon\ H^{3}(B\mathrm{Aut}(A_{d},\mathbb{Z}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}H^{3}(B\Omega\mathrm{SO}(d),\mathbb{Z}).

On the other hand, the group homomorphism
\tilde{\theta}\colon\Omega\mathrm{SO}(d)\to\mathrm{Aut}(A_{d})
extends to all of
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d)
, hence the above isomorphism factors through
H^{3}(B\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d),\mathbb{Z})
.
This shows that the right map in (

∎

We are now interested in the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{3}(B\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d),\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{3}(B\mathrm{L}\mathrm{Spin}(d),\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{4}(B\mathrm{SO}(d),\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{4}(B\mathrm{Spin}(d),\mathbb{Z}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cdot 2}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

(4.10)

where

.
The bottom left group is
\mathbb{Z}
, generated by the universal Pontrjagin class
p_{1}
.
The bottom right group is also
\mathbb{Z}
, generated by the fractional Pontrjagin class
\frac{1}{2}p_{1}
, and the bottom horizontal map has been shown to be multiplication by two on generators by McLaughlin
[

, more specifically it sends

to the class

.
The right transgression map is an isomorphism as

is 2-connected
[
21
, p. 149]
.

Proposition 4.9
.

If
d\geq 5
, the top horizontal map in (

For the proof, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.10
.

The map
H^{3}(B\Omega\mathrm{SO}(d),\mathbb{Z})\to H^{3}(B\Omega\mathrm{Spin}(d),\mathbb{Z})
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Observe that
\Omega\mathrm{Spin}(d)
is canonically identified with the identity component of

.
We therefore obtain the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega\mathrm{Spin}(d)=\Omega\mathrm{SO}(d)_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Aut}(A_{d})_{0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega\mathrm{SO}(d)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Aut}(A_{d}).}

The horizontal maps of this diagram induce isomorphisms on

for

, by Corollary

.
It follows that in the corresponding diagram on classifying spaces, the horizontal maps induce isomorphisms on

for

They are moreover trivially surjective on

(since this group is trivial for the right-hand side).
By Whitehead’s theorem

From the universal coefficient theorem and the five lemma, we obtain that they also induce isomorphisms on

[
7
]
, which we recall now.
Let
\omega
be a connection 1-form on a principal
G
-bundle
E
over
B
with curvature
\Omega
.
Let
P\in\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(\mathfrak{g}^{*})^{G}
be an invariant polynomial on
\mathfrak{g}
.
Then the corresponding Chern–Simons form is
\mathrm{T}P(\omega)\in H^{3}(E,\mathbb{R})
defined by

\mathrm{T}P(A)=P(\omega\wedge\Omega)-\frac{1}{6}P(\omega\wedge[\omega,\omega]),

see
[
7
, formula (3.5)]
.
Denoting by
\iota\colon G\to E
the inclusion of a fiber (for some fixed base point
e\in E
), one uses that the curvature form
\Omega
is horizontal, so that
\iota^{*}\Omega=0
, while
\iota^{*}\omega=\omega_{G}
, the Maurer–Cartan form of
G
.
Hence

\iota^{*}\mathrm{T}P(\omega)=-\frac{1}{6}P(\omega_{G}\wedge[\omega_{G},\omega_{G}]),

which differs from the formula (3.11) in
[
7
]
by a factor of 2.
Going through the conventions used in
[
7
]
for the wedge product and commutator of
\mathfrak{g}
-valued differential forms (see
[
7
, p. 50]
), one obtains that
\iota^{*}\mathrm{T}P(\omega)
is the left-invariant form corresponding to the Lie algebra cocycle

\alpha(x,y,z)=-\frac{1}{3}P(x\otimes[y,z]+y\otimes[z,x]+z\otimes[x,y]).

(A.3)

Proof.

We take
G=\mathrm{SO}(d)
and
E=E\mathrm{SO}(d)
,
B=B\mathrm{SO}(d)
, the universal bundles.
Choosing models for these that are infinite-dimensional Fréchet manifolds (for example, the infinite Grassmannian and Stiefel manifold), we may choose a connection 1-form
\omega\in H^{1}(E\mathrm{SO}(d),\mathfrak{so}(d))
.
Setting
P(X\otimes Y)=-\mathrm{tr}(XY)/8\pi^{2}
,
[
7
, Proposition 3.2]
states that

d\mathrm{T}P(\omega)=P(\Omega\otimes\Omega)=-\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\mathrm{tr}\big(\Omega^{2}\big).

By the usual Chern–Weil formulas for Pontrjagin classes, we see that this equals the pullback
\pi^{*}p_{1}
of the de Rham representative of the universal first Pontrjagin class along the bundle projection
\pi\colon E\mathrm{SO}(d)\to B\mathrm{SO}(d)
.

Using the description (
A.2
) of the transgression homomorphism, we see that
\tilde{\tau}(p_{1})=\iota^{*}\mathrm{T}P(\omega)
.
As discussed above, the pullback
\iota^{*}\mathrm{T}P(\omega)
is the left invariant differential form that corresponds to the Lie algebra cocycle
\alpha
given by (
A.3
).
For our particular choice of
P
, we get

\alpha(x,y,z)=\left(-\frac{1}{3}\right)\cdot\left(-\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\right)\mathrm{tr}(x[y,z]+y[z,x]+z[x,y])=\sigma(x,y,z).

Lemma A.3
.

We have
\tau(\overline{\sigma})=-\overline{\omega}/2\pi
, where
\overline{\omega}
is the left-invariant
2
-form on
L\mathrm{SO}(d)
corresponding to the Lie algebra cocycle

\omega(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{S^{1}}\mathrm{tr}(X(t)Y^{\prime}(t)),\qquad X,Y\in\mathrm{L}\mathfrak{so}(d).

This result can be found as
[
26
, Proposition 4.4.4]
, but with incorrect prefactors and an incomplete proof, which is why we repeat the proof below.

Proof.

Tangent vectors
X
,
Y
at
\gamma\in L\mathrm{SO}(d)
can be identified with those elements of
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{Mat}_{d\times d}
such that
\tilde{X}\in L\mathfrak{so}(d)
, where
\tilde{X}(t)=\gamma(t)^{-1}X(t)
. We now calculate

\displaystyle\tau(\overline{\sigma})_{\gamma}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{1}}\overline{\sigma}(\gamma^{\prime}(t),X(t),Y(t)){\rm d}t=\int_{S^{1}}\sigma\big(\gamma(t)^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}(t),\gamma(t)^{-1}X(t),\gamma(t)^{-1}Y(t)\big){\rm d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\int_{S^{1}}\mathrm{tr}\big(\gamma(t)^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}(t)\big[\tilde{X}(t),\tilde{Y}(t)\big]\big){\rm d}t.

Let
\beta\in\Omega^{1}(\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d))
be given by

\beta_{\gamma}(X)=\int_{S^{1}}\mathrm{tr}\big(\gamma(t)^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}(t)\tilde{X}(t)\big){\rm d}t.

For the exterior derivative of
\beta
, we find

\displaystyle d\beta_{\gamma}(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\partial_{X}\{\beta(Y)\}_{\gamma}-\partial_{Y}\{\beta(X)\}_{\gamma}-\beta_{\gamma}([X,Y])

\displaystyle=\int_{S^{1}}\big(\mathrm{tr}\big(\tilde{X}^{\prime}(t)\tilde{Y}(t)\big)-\mathrm{tr}\big(\tilde{Y}^{\prime}(t)\tilde{X}(t)\big)-\mathrm{tr}\big(\gamma(t)^{-1}\gamma^{\prime}(t)[\tilde{X}(t),\tilde{Y}(t)]\big)\big){\rm d}t

for suitable extensions of
X
and
Y
to vector fields on
\mathrm{L}\mathrm{SO}(d)
.
Integrating by parts, we see that
{\rm d}\beta=-2\cdot 2\pi\cdot\overline{\omega}-8\pi^{2}\cdot\tau(\overline{\sigma})
, hence
2\pi\cdot\tau(\overline{\sigma})
and
-\overline{\omega}
are cohomologous.
∎

Let
\tilde{G}\to G
be a central

Choosing a linear section of the Lie algebra homomorphism

With respect to this choice, the Lie bracket of

[(X,\lambda),(Y,\mu)]=([X,Y],\Omega(X,Y))

for some continuous Lie algebra cocycle 2-cocycle
\Omega
, which represents a class in

The first Chern class of the principal
\mathrm{U}(1)
-bundle

is then given by

c_{1}(\tilde{G})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\overline{\Omega},

(A.4)

where
\overline{\Omega}
denotes the associated left-invariant 2-form, see for example
[
26
, Proposition 4.5.6]
.

In the case that

\tilde{G}=\mathrm{Imp}_{L}

where

{L=L^{d}+L_{0}}

Lemma A.4
.

We have
2\cdot j^{*}c_{1}(\mathrm{Imp})=-\overline{\omega}/2\pi
.

A similar computation, for the Lagrangian (
4.15
) instead of
L
, can be found in
[
18
]
; unfortunately, the result is off by a factor of
\pm{\rm i}
.
Proposition 6.7.1 in
[
26
]
is an analogous result for the basic central extension of restricted unitary group
\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{res}}(H)
of a polarized complex Hilbert space
H
.

Proof.

We will establish the cocycle identity

2\cdot j^{*}\Omega=-\omega,

(A.6)

which gives the result by (
A.4
).
By continuity and bilinearity, it suffices to verify that both Lie algebra cocycles evaluate identically on the specific Lie algebra elements
X,Y\in L\mathfrak{so}(d)
of the form

X(t)=a{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}kt},\qquad Y(t)=b{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}\ell t},\qquad a,b\in\mathfrak{so}(d),\qquad k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}.

On these elements, the right-hand side of (
A.6
) is given by

\displaystyle\omega(X,Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{tr}(X(t)Y^{\prime}(t)){\rm d}t

\displaystyle=-\frac{{\rm i}\ell}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\mathrm{tr}(ab){\rm e}^{-{\rm i}(k+\ell)t}{\rm d}t=\begin{cases}-{\rm i}\ell\cdot\mathrm{tr}(ab)&k+\ell=0,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(A.7)

To calculate the left-hand side of (
A.6
), we write

j_{*}X=\begin{pmatrix}x^{\prime}&\overline{x}\\
x&\overline{x}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix},\qquad j_{*}Y=\begin{pmatrix}y^{\prime}&\overline{y}\\
y&\overline{y}^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}

with respect to the decomposition
H_{\mathbb{C}}=L\oplus\overline{L}
.
In other words,
x^{\prime}=P_{L}XP_{L}
and
x=P_{\overline{L}}XP_{L}
and similarly for
y^{\prime}
and
y
, while
\overline{x}
denotes the conjugation of
x
by the real structure of
H_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Then since
j_{*}X
and
j_{*}Y
are restricted, the off-diagonal entries
x
and
y
are Hilbert–Schmidt operators and a straightforward calculation gives

\Omega(j_{*}X,j_{*}Y)=\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\mathrm{tr}(\overline{x}y-\overline{y}x).

(A.8)

Write
V_{n}=\big\{\xi\otimes{\rm e}^{\rm int}\mid\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{d}\big\}\subset H^{d}_{\mathbb{C}}
.
We observe that
j_{*}X
sends
V_{n}
to
V_{n+k}
and
j_{*}Xj_{*}Y
sends
V_{n}
to
V_{n-k-\ell}
.
On the other hand,
P_{L}
and
P_{\overline{L}}
preserve the subspaces
V_{n}
for
n\neq 0
and send
V_{0}
to
K
(where
K=V_{0}
if
d
is even, while
K=V_{0}\oplus\mathbb{C}
if
d
is odd).
We conclude that both
\overline{x}y
and
x\overline{y}
send
V_{n}
to
V_{n-k-\ell}
, respectively
V_{n-k-\ell}\oplus\mathbb{C}
if
d
is odd.
Choosing an orthonormal basis adapted to the decomposition
H_{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{n}
, respectively
H_{\mathbb{C}}=(\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}V_{n})\oplus\mathbb{C}
to calculate the trace on the right-hand side of (
A.8
), we see that the result can be non-zero only if
k+\ell=0
.

So suppose now that
k=-\ell
and let
\xi\otimes{\rm e}^{{\rm int}}\in V_{n}^{\mathbb{C}}
,
n\geq 1
. Then we have

\displaystyle\overline{x}y(\xi\otimes\chi_{n})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}ab\xi\otimes\chi_{n}&1\leq n\leq\ell-1,\\
a\overline{P}_{0}b\xi\otimes\chi_{n}&n=\ell,\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\overline{y}x(\xi\otimes\chi_{n})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}ba\xi\otimes\chi_{n},&1\leq n-1\leq-\ell-1,\\
b\overline{P}_{0}a\xi\otimes\chi_{n},&n=-\ell,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
P_{0}
is the orthogonal projection onto
L_{0}
in
K
.
For
\xi\otimes 1\in V_{0}
, we find

\displaystyle\overline{x}y(\xi\otimes 1)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}P_{0}ab\xi\otimes 1,&\ell>0,\\
P_{0}a\overline{P}_{0}b\xi\otimes 1,&\ell=0,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\overline{y}x(\xi\otimes 1)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}P_{0}ba\xi\otimes 1,&\ell<0,\\
P_{0}b\overline{P}_{0}a\xi\otimes 1,&\ell=0,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Concluding, if
\ell>0
, we obtain

\Omega(j_{*}X,j_{*}Y)=\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\mathrm{tr}(\overline{x}y)=\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\ell-1}\mathrm{tr}(ab)+\mathrm{tr}\big(a\overline{P}_{0}b\big)+\mathrm{tr}(P_{0}ab)\right)=\frac{{\rm i}\ell}{2}\mathrm{tr}(ab).

Here we used that

\mathrm{tr}\big(a\overline{P}_{0}b\big)+\mathrm{tr}(P_{0}ab)=\mathrm{tr}(ab),

which follows from the following calculation.
First, because
a
and
b
are real and skew-adjoint and
P_{0}
is self-adjoint, we get

\mathrm{tr}(aP_{0}b)=\mathrm{tr}(baP_{0})=\overline{\mathrm{tr}((baP_{0})^{*})}=\overline{\mathrm{tr}(P_{0}(-a)(-b))}=\mathrm{tr}\big(\overline{P}_{0}ab\big),

hence

\mathrm{tr}\big(a\overline{P}_{0}b\big)+\mathrm{tr}(P_{0}ab)=\mathrm{tr}\big(\overline{P}_{0}ab\big)+\mathrm{tr}(P_{0}ab)=\mathrm{tr}(ab),

using
\overline{P}_{0}+P_{0}=\mathrm{id}
.
If
\ell<0
, we obtain in a similar fashion

\Omega(j_{*}X,j_{*}Y)=-\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\mathrm{tr}(\overline{y}x)=-\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{|\ell|-1}\mathrm{tr}(ba)+\mathrm{tr}\big(b\overline{P}_{0}a\big)+\mathrm{tr}(P_{0}ba)\right)=-\frac{{\rm i}|\ell|}{2}\mathrm{tr}(ab).

Finally, if
\ell=0
, then

\displaystyle\Omega(j_{*}X,j_{*}Y)=\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\mathrm{tr}(\overline{x}y-\overline{y}x)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\big(\mathrm{tr}\big(P_{0}a\overline{P}_{0}b\big)-\mathrm{tr}\big(P_{0}b\overline{P}_{0}a\big)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\big(\mathrm{tr}(P_{0}ab)-\mathrm{tr}(P_{0}a{P}_{0}b)-\mathrm{tr}(P_{0}ba)+\mathrm{tr}(P_{0}bP_{0}a)\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm i}}{2}\big(\mathrm{tr}(P_{0}ab)-\mathrm{tr}(ba)+\mathrm{tr}\big(\overline{P}_{0}ba\big)\big)

\displaystyle=0.

Comparing with (
A.7
), this establishes (
A.6
).
∎

1
Introduction and main results

A pseudo-differential operator (PDO for short) is given by

T_{a}f(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}a(x,\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi,

where
\widehat{f}
is the Fourier transform of
f
and
a
is the amplitude. We always omit the constant
(2\pi)^{-n}
throughout this note.

In its basic form, a Fourier integral operator (FIO for short) is defined by

\displaystyle T_{\phi,a}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\phi(x,\xi)}a(x,\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi,

where
\phi
is the phase. In this note, we always assume that
f
belongs to the Schwartz class
S(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

A FIO
T_{\phi,a}
is simply a pseudo-differential operator if
\phi(x,\xi)=x\cdot\xi
. When
\phi(x,\xi)=x\cdot\xi+|\xi|
,
T_{\phi,a}
is closely related to the wave equation and Fourier transform on the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see
[
32
, p. 395]
).

FIOs have been widely used in the theory of partial differential equations and micro-local analysis. For instance, the solution to an initial value problem for a hyperbolic equation with variable coefficients can be effectively approximated using an FIO of the initial value (see
[
32
, p. 425]
). Therefore, the boundedness of related FIOs provides a priori estimate for the solution. A systematic study of
these operators was initiated by Hörmander
[
19
]
.

At first, we recall some simplest and most useful definitions on amplitudes and phases.

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set
\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
. A function
a
belongs to the Hörmander class
S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}

(m\in\mathbb{R},0\leq\rho,\delta\leq 1)
if it satisfies

\sup_{x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|\xi|)^{-m+\rho N-\delta M}|\nabla^{N}_{\xi}\nabla^{M}_{x}a(x,\xi)|=A_{N,M}<+\infty

(1.1)

for any
N,M\in\mathbb{N}
. Immediately, one have

S^{m_{1}}_{\rho,\delta}\subset S^{m_{2}}_{\rho,\delta},S^{m}_{\rho_{2},\delta}\subset S^{m}_{\rho_{1},\delta},S^{m}_{\rho,\delta_{1}}\subset S^{m}_{\rho,\delta_{2}},

if
m_{1}<m_{2},\rho_{1}<\rho_{2},\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}.

A real-valued function
\phi
belongs to the class
\Phi^{2}
if
\phi
is positively homogeneous of order 1 in the frequency variable
\xi
and satisfies

\sup_{(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})}|\xi|^{-1+N}|\nabla^{N}_{\xi}\nabla^{M}_{x}\phi(x,\xi)|=B_{N,M}<+\infty

(1.2)

for all
N,M\in\mathbb{N}
with
N+M\geq 2
.

A real-valued function
\phi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}))
satisfies the strong non-degeneracy condition (SND for short), if there exists a constant
\lambda>0
such that

\textrm{det}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\phi}{\partial x_{j}\partial\xi_{k}}(x,\xi)\right)\geq\lambda

(1.3)

for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})
.

For PDOs and FIOs, the most important problem is whether they are bounded on Lebesgue spaces and Hardy spaces.
This problem has been extensively studied and there are numerous results. Here we always assume that
a\in S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}
and
\phi\in\Phi^{2}
satisfies the SND condition (
1.3

Before the next theorem, we recall the definitions of Hardy spaces and local Hardy spaces. Let
\Phi
be a function in the Schwartz space
S(\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Phi(x)dx=1
.
Set
\Phi_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{t^{n}}\Phi(\frac{x}{t})
. Following Stein
[
32
, p. 91]
, we can define the Hardy space
H^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})(0<p<+\infty)
as the space of all tempered distributions
f
satisfying

\|f\|_{H^{p}}=\|\sup\limits_{t>0}|f\ast\Phi_{t}(x)|\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty.

The local Hardy space (see
[
15
]
)
h^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})(0<p<+\infty)
is defined as the space of all tempered distributions
f
satisfying

\|f\|_{h^{p}}=\|\sup\limits_{0<t<1}|f\ast\Phi_{t}(x)|\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty.

It is well known that
H^{p}=h^{p}=L^{p}
for equivalent norms when
1<p<+\infty
and
H^{p}\subset h^{p}\subset L^{p}
when
0<p\leq 1
.

In
[
22
]
, Israelsson-Mattsson-Staubach extend Theorem A to
p\leq 1
implicity.

Theorem C
.

(
[
22
]
) Let
n\geq 1,0\leq\rho\leq 1,0\leq\delta<1
,

m=(\rho-n)|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|+\frac{n}{2}\min\{0,\rho-\delta\}

and
a\in S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}
. If
\phi\in\Phi^{2}
satisfies the SND condition (
1.3
), then
T_{\phi,a}
is bounded from the local Hardy space
h^{p}
to
L^{p}
when
\frac{n}{n+1}<p\leq 1
.
Furthermore, if
a
has compact support in the spatial variable
x
, then
T_{\phi,a}
is bounded from
h^{p}
to
L^{p}
for any
0<p\leq 1
.

Theorem C can be considered as a corollary of
[

, Proposition 5.7]
,
[

and
[

due to the facts

Set
\lambda_{\rho}=1
when

and
\lambda_{\rho}=\frac{1}{2\rho}

and

(1.4)

It is easy to see that

\frac{1}{2}\leq\lambda_{\rho}\leq 1,\rho_{0}=\lambda_{\rho}\rho.

Now we state our main result in this note.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
0\leq\rho\leq 1,n\geq 2
or
0\leq\rho<1,n=1
,
a\in S^{m_{p}}_{\rho,1}
where
m_{p}
is given by (
1.4
) and
\phi\in\Phi^{2}
satisfies the SND condition (
1.3
). Then for any

\frac{n}{n+1}<p\leq 1
, we have

\|T_{\phi,a}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|f\|_{h^{p}}.

Furthermore, if
a
has compact support in variable
x
, then for any
0<p\leq 1
we have

\|T_{\phi,a}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|f\|_{h^{p}}.

The constants here depend only on
n,\rho,\lambda,p
and finitely many semi-norms
A_{N,M},B_{N,M}
.

As the most important special case, we generalize the well-known Seeger-Sogge-Stein theorem to the forbidden Hörmander class
S^{(1-n)/2}_{1,1}
when
n\geq 2
.

Corollary 1.2
.

If
n\geq 2
,
a\in S^{(1-n)/2}_{1,1}
and
\phi\in\Phi^{2}
satisfies the SND condition (
1.3
), then we have

\|T_{\phi,a}f\|_{L^{1}}\leq C\|f\|_{h^{1}}.

In fact, we establish a more comprehensive theorem in this paper, which generalizes Theorem 1.1. Prior to presenting our theorem, we introduce the rough Hörmander class
L^{\infty}S^{m}_{\rho}
,
which is defined by Kenig-Staubach
[
24
]
. Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
0\leq\rho\leq 1
. A function
a
that is smooth in the frequency variable
\xi
and bounded measurable in the spatial variable

belongs to the rough Hörmander class

, if it satisfies

\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}(1+|\xi|)^{-m+\rho N}\left\|\nabla^{N}_{\xi}a(\cdot,\xi)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=A_{N}<\infty

(1.5)

for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
. It is easy to see that

.

In this note, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
0\leq\rho\leq 1,n\geq 2
or
0\leq\rho<1,n=1
,
a\in L^{\infty}S^{m_{p}}_{\rho}
where

is given by (

satisfies the SND condition (
1.3
).
Then for any
\frac{n}{n+1}<p\leq 1
, we have

\|T_{\phi,a}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|f\|_{h^{p}}.

Furthermore, if
a
has compact support in variable
x
, then for any
0<p\leq 1
we have

\|T_{\phi,a}f\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\|f\|_{h^{p}}.

The constants here depend only on
n,\rho,\lambda,p
and finitely many semi-norms
A_{N},B_{N,M}
.

Obviously, Theorem 1.3 can be considered as a supplement of Theorem C to

Remark 4.
More interestingly, one can check that

Let

be the ball in

centered at

At first we introduce the theory of atom decomposition of

with

It is also well known that a distribution

has an atomic decomposition

with
\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|^{p}<+\infty

Moreover, there holds

\|f\|_{h^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\inf\{(\sum_{j}|\lambda_{j}|^{p})^{\frac{1}{p}}:f=\sum\limits_{j}\lambda_{j}b_{j}\}.

Secondly, thank to the SND condition (

Thirdly, we introduce the Littlewood-Paley dyadic decomposition and the second dyadic decomposition.

Take a nonnegative function

For every

For

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\nu}_{j}=\{\xi:|\frac{\xi}{|\xi|}-\xi^{\nu}_{j}|\leq 2^{2-j\rho_{0}}\}\textrm{ and }A^{\nu}_{j}=\Gamma^{\nu}_{j}\cap\{\xi:2^{j-1}<|\xi|<2^{j+1}\}.

Lemma 2.2

Lemma 2.3

If

\phi\in\Phi^{2}

Proof.

By Lemma 2.2 and the product rule, for any
k,l\geq 0,j>0
we can get that

\displaystyle|\partial^{k}_{\xi^{\nu}_{j}}\nabla^{l}_{\xi}e^{ih^{\nu}_{j}}|\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum^{k+l}_{t=1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+\cdots+k_{t}=k,l_{1}+\cdots+l_{t}=l\\
k_{1}+l_{1},\ldots,k_{t}+l_{t}>0\end{subarray}}|\partial^{k_{1}}_{\xi^{\nu}_{j}}\nabla^{l_{1}}_{\xi}h^{\nu}_{j}\cdots\partial^{k_{1}}_{\xi^{\nu}_{j}}\nabla^{l_{1}}_{\xi}h^{\nu}_{j}|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum^{k+l}_{t=1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k_{1}+\cdots+k_{t}=k,l_{1}+\cdots+l_{t}=l\\
k_{1}+l_{1},\ldots,k_{t}+l_{t}>0\end{subarray}}2^{-j(k_{1}\rho+l_{1}\rho_{0})}\cdots 2^{-j(k_{t}\rho+l_{t}\rho_{0})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{-j(k\rho+l\rho_{0})}.

Similarly, thank to the facts

and
\rho_{0}\leq\rho
, by Lemma 2.2 we get that

\displaystyle|\partial^{k}_{\xi^{\nu}_{j}}\nabla^{l}_{\xi}(a_{j}\psi^{\nu}_{j})|\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k}\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}=l}|\nabla^{k_{1}+l_{1}}_{\xi}a_{j}||\partial^{k_{2}}_{\xi^{\nu}_{j}}\nabla^{l_{2}}_{\xi}\psi^{\nu}_{j}|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}=k}\sum_{l_{1}+l_{2}=l}2^{j(m-(k_{1}+l_{1})\rho)}2^{-j(k_{2}\rho+l_{2}\rho_{0})}\lesssim 2^{j(m-k\rho-l\rho_{0})}.

Therefore, by the chain rule, we show that

\displaystyle|\partial^{N}_{\xi^{\nu}_{j}}\nabla^{M}_{\xi}(e^{ih^{\nu}_{j}}a_{j}\psi^{\nu}_{j})|\lesssim\sum_{N_{1}+N_{2}=N}\sum_{M_{1}+M_{2}=M}2^{-j(N_{1}\rho+M_{1}\rho_{0})}2^{j(m-N_{2}\rho-M_{2}\rho_{0})}=2^{j(m-N\rho-M\rho_{0})}.

This finishes the proof.

Thank to

When

\partial_{r}
is the radial derivative. Indeed, by using the polar coordinates
\xi=r\theta
with
r=|\xi|
, we get

\displaystyle 0\leq|\xi|-\xi^{\nu}_{j}\cdot\xi=\frac{|\xi-(\xi^{\nu}_{j}\cdot\xi)\xi^{\nu}_{j}|^{2}}{|\xi|+\xi^{\nu}_{j}\cdot\xi}\lesssim 2^{j(1-2\rho_{0})},

\displaystyle\partial_{r}=O(1)\cdot\nabla_{\xi},\qquad\partial_{\theta}=O(2^{j})\cdot\nabla_{\xi},

and

|\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\xi^{\nu}_{j}}|=\frac{\left||\xi|\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial\xi^{\nu}_{j}}-\frac{\partial|\xi|}{\partial\xi^{\nu}_{j}}\xi\right|}{|\xi|^{2}}\leq\frac{|\xi-(\xi^{\nu}_{j}\cdot\xi)\xi^{\nu}_{j}|^{2}+|\xi^{\nu}_{j}\cdot\xi||\xi-(\xi^{\nu}_{j}\cdot\xi)\xi^{\nu}_{j}|}{|\xi|^{3}}\lesssim 2^{-j(1+\rho_{0})}.

Thus when
\xi\in A^{\nu}_{j}
with
j>0
, we can write

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi^{\nu}_{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial r}{\partial\xi^{\nu}_{j}}\partial_{r}+\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\xi^{\nu}_{j}}\partial_{\theta}=\frac{\xi^{\nu}_{j}\cdot\xi}{|\xi|}\partial_{r}+O\left(2^{-j(1+\rho_{0})}\right)O(2^{j})\cdot\nabla_{\xi}

\displaystyle=\partial_{r}+\frac{\xi^{\nu}_{j}\cdot\xi-|\xi|}{|\xi|}\partial_{r}+O(2^{-j\rho_{0}})\cdot\nabla_{\xi}=\partial_{r}+O(2^{-j\rho_{0}})\cdot\nabla_{\xi}.

So the claim is true.

Since
\psi^{\nu}_{j}
is homogeneous of degree 0, we have
\partial^{k}_{r}\psi^{\nu}_{j}=0
for any
k>0
.
Now (
2.2
) and (
6.2
) yield that

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{N}_{\xi^{\nu}_{j}}\nabla^{M}_{\xi}\psi^{\nu}_{j}(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left(\partial_{r}+O\left(2^{-j\rho_{0}}\right)\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\right)^{N}\nabla^{M}_{\xi}\psi^{\nu}_{j}(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-jN\rho_{0}}\left|\nabla^{N+M}_{\xi}\psi^{\nu}_{j}(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-jN\rho_{0}}2^{-j(N+M)}2^{j(N+M)\rho_{0}}

\displaystyle=2^{-j(N+(1-\rho_{0})M)}\leq 2^{-j(N+\rho_{0}M)}

as desired.

7
Appendix C: proof of Lemma 2.5

When
j=0
, one can see
[
12
, Theorem 1.18]
.

When
j>0
, set

S_{\phi,a_{j}}g(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\phi(x,\xi)}a_{j}(x,\xi)g(\xi)d\xi.

It is easy to see that
S_{\phi,a_{j}}S^{*}_{\phi,a_{j}}
can be given by

S_{\phi,a_{j}}S^{*}_{\phi,a_{j}}f(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}K_{j}(x,y)f(y)dy,

where

K_{j}(x,y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i(\phi(x,\xi)-\phi(y,\xi))}a_{j}(x,\xi)\overline{a_{j}(y,\xi)}d\xi.

Set
G(x,y,\xi)=\phi(x,\xi)-\phi(y,\xi)
. Define the operator
L
as

L=\frac{\nabla_{\xi}G\cdot\nabla_{\xi}}{|\nabla_{\xi}G|^{2}}

and let
L^{*}
be the dual of
L
.

As
\phi\in\Phi^{2}
, for any
k\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k}_{\xi}G(x,y,\xi)|\leq|x-y|\sup_{(z,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})}|\nabla_{z}\nabla^{k}_{\xi}\phi(z,\xi)|\lesssim|x-y||\xi|^{1-k}.

(7.1)

On the other hand, as
\phi\in\Phi^{2}
satisfies the SND condition (
1.3
), by using
[
12
, Proposition 1.11]
, one can get that

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\xi}G(x,y,\xi)|=|\nabla_{\xi}\phi(x,\xi)-\nabla_{\xi}\phi(y,\xi)|\geq c|x-y|,

(7.2)

where
c
depends only on
\lambda
.

One can easily see that

L(e^{i(\phi(x,\xi)-\phi(y,\xi))})=\frac{\nabla_{\xi}G\cdot\nabla_{\xi}}{|\nabla_{\xi}G|^{2}}e^{iG}=ie^{iG}=ie^{i(\phi(x,\xi)-\phi(y,\xi))}.

So, for any
M\in\mathbb{N}
, applying Lemma 3.2 in
[
27
]
, from (
7.1
), (
7.2
) and the fact
a\in L^{\infty}S^{m}_{\rho}
, we have

\displaystyle\left|K_{j}(x,y)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i(\phi(x,\xi)-\phi(y,\xi))}a_{j}(x,\xi)\overline{a_{j}(y,\xi)}d\xi\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(-i)^{M}L^{M}(e^{i(\phi(x,\xi)-\phi(y,\xi))})a_{j}(x,\xi)\overline{a_{j}(y,\xi)}d\xi\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i(\phi(x,\xi)-\phi(y,\xi))}(L^{*})^{M}(a_{j}(x,\xi)\overline{a_{j}(y,\xi)})d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla_{\xi}G|^{-4M}\sum_{k_{0}+k_{1}+\cdots+k_{3M}=M,k_{s}\geq 0}\left|\nabla_{\xi}^{k_{0}}(a_{j}(x,\xi)\overline{a_{j}(y,\xi)})\right|\prod^{3M}_{s=1}\left|\nabla_{\xi}^{k_{s}+1}G\right|d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2^{j+1}}\setminus B_{2^{j-1}}}|x-y|^{-4M}\sum_{k_{0}+k_{1}+\cdots+k_{3M}=M,k_{s}\geq 0}|\xi|^{2m-k_{0}\rho}\prod^{3M}_{s=1}\left(|x-y||\xi|^{-k_{s}}\right)d\xi

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{jn}|x-y|^{-4M}\sum^{M}_{k_{0}=0}2^{j(2m-k_{0}\rho)}|x-y|^{3M}2^{j(k_{0}-M)}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{j(n+2m)}(2^{j\rho}|x-y|)^{-M}.

(7.3)

For any
x
, letting
M=0
or
n+1
in (
7.3
), one can obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|K_{j}(x,y)|dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|y-x|<2^{-j\rho}}|K_{j}(x,y)|dy+\int_{|y-x|\geq 2^{-j\rho}}|K_{j}(x,y)|dy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{|y-x|<2^{-j\rho}}2^{j(n+2m)}dy+\int_{|y-x|\geq 2^{-j\rho}}2^{j(n+2m)}(2^{j\rho}|x-y|)^{-n-1}dy

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2^{j(2m-n(\rho-1))}.

(7.4)

Similarly, for any
y
, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|K_{j}(x,y)|dx\lesssim 2^{j(2m-n(\rho-1))}.

Therefore,

\|S_{\phi,a_{j}}S^{*}_{\phi,a_{j}}\|_{L^{1}-L^{1}}\lesssim 2^{j(2m-n(\rho-1))},\|S_{\phi,a_{j}}S^{*}_{\phi,a_{j}}\|_{L^{\infty}-L^{\infty}}\lesssim 2^{j(2m-n(\rho-1))},

which implies that

\|S_{\phi,a_{j}}S^{*}_{\phi,a_{j}}\|_{L^{2}-L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{j(2m-n(\rho-1))}.

By a standard dual argument, we have

\|S_{\phi,a_{j}}\|_{L^{2}-L^{2}}=\|S_{\phi,a_{j}}S^{*}_{\phi,a_{j}}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}-L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{j(m-\frac{n}{2}(\rho-1))}.

It follows from the Plancherel theorem that

\displaystyle\|T_{\phi,a_{j}}f\|_{2}=\|S_{\phi,a_{j}}\widehat{f}\|_{2}\lesssim 2^{j(m-\frac{n}{2}(\rho-1))}\|\widehat{f}\|_{2}=2^{j(m-\frac{n}{2}(\rho-1))}\|f\|_{2}.

This finishes the proof.

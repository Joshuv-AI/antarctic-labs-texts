Abstract
. In this paper, we are concerned with the one-dimensional initial boundary value problem for isentropic gas dynamics. Through the contribution of great researchers such as Lax, P. D., Glimm, J., DiPerna, R. J. and Liu, T. P., the decay theory of solutions was established. They treated with the Cauchy problem and the corresponding initial data have the small total variation. On the other hand, the decay for initial data with large oscillation has been open for half a century. In addition, due to the reflection of shock waves at the boundaries, little is known for the decay of the boundary value problem on a bounded interval. Our goal is to prove the existence of a global attractor, which yields a decay of solutions for large data. To construct approximate solutions, we introduce a modified Godunov scheme.

Introduction

The present paper is concerned with isentropic gas
dynamics in a bounded interval
I=[0,1]
.

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\rho_{t}+m_{x}=0,}\\
\displaystyle{m_{t}+\left(\frac{m^{2}}{\rho}+p(\rho)\right)_{x}=0,}\end{cases}x\in(0,1),\quad t\in(0,\infty),

(1.1)

where
\rho
,
m
and
p
are the density, the momentum and the
pressure of the gas, respectively. If
\rho>0
,

v=m/\rho
represents the velocity of the gas. For a barotropic gas,

p(\rho)=\rho^{\gamma}/\gamma
, where
\gamma\in(1,5/3]
is the
adiabatic exponent for usual gases.

We consider the initial boundary value problem (
1.1
)
with the initial and boundary data

\displaystyle(\rho,m)|_{t=0}=(\rho_{0}(x),m_{0}(x))\quad m|_{x=0}=m|_{x=1}=0.

(1.2)

The above problem (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) can be written in the following form

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}u_{t}+f(u)_{x}=0,\quad{x}\in(0,1),\quad t\in(0,\infty),\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}(x),\\
m|_{x=0}=m|_{x=1}=0\end{array}\right.

by using
u={}^{t}(\rho,m)
,
\displaystyle f(u)={}^{t}\!\left(m,\frac{m^{2}}{\rho}+p(\rho)\right)
.

We survey the known results related to the above problem. The existence of solutions to conservation laws including (
1.1
) was first established by Glimm
[
8
]
. Glimm studied the Cauchy problem
with initial data having small total variation. DiPerna
[
5
]

proved the global existence of solutions to
(
1.1
) by the vanishing viscosity method and a compensated compactness argument. We notice that this result can treat with the arbitrary
L^{\infty}
data.

The theory of decay for genuinely nonlinear 2
\times
2 systems of conservation laws was established by Glimm-Lax
[
9
]
. Glimm-Lax showed that if initial data are constant outside a finite interval and have locally bounded total variation and small oscillation, then the tonal
variation of the solution of
[
8
]
decays to zero.
The Glimm-Lax theory had been further developed by DiPerna and Liu:
general conservation laws with a convex entropy function
[
4
]
,
general conservation laws with small initial data in total variation
[
10
]
. However, the decay for initial data with large oscillation has been open for half a century. Recently, Tsuge
[
20
]

obtained a decay estimate for large
L^{\infty}
data.
On the other hand, little is known for the decay of the
boundary value problem such as (
1
), because it is difficult
to treat with the reflection of shock waves at the both boundaries.

Our goal in the present paper is to investigate the decay structure of the boundary value problem (
1
). We first introduce a modified Godunov scheme developed in
[
11
]
–
[
20
]

to construct approximate solutions. To deduce the convergence,
we employ the compensated compactness developed by DiPerna. We next prove the existence of a global attractor, which
yields decay estimates of solutions for initial data with large oscillation.

To state our main theorem, we define the Riemann invariants
w,z
, which play important roles
in this paper, as

Definition 1.1
.

\displaystyle w:=\frac{m}{\rho}+\frac{\rho^{\theta}}{\theta}=v+\frac{\rho^{\theta}}{\theta},\quad{z}:=\frac{m}{\rho}-\frac{\rho^{\theta}}{\theta}=v-\frac{\rho^{\theta}}{\theta}\quad\left(\theta=\frac{\gamma-1}{2}\right).

These Riemann invariants satisfy the following.

Remark 1.1
.

\displaystyle|w|\geq|z|,\;w\geq 0,\;\mbox{\rm when}\;v\geq 0.\quad|w|\leq|z|,\;z\leq 0,\;\mbox{\rm when}\;v\leq 0.

\displaystyle v=\frac{w+z}{2},\;\rho=\left(\frac{\theta(w-z)}{2}\right)^{1/\theta},\;m=\rho v.

From the above, the lower bound of
z
and the upper bound of
w
yield the bound of
\rho
and
|v|
.

Moreover, we define the entropy weak solution.

Definition 1.2
.

A measurable function

is called an entropy weak solution of the initial boundary value problem (
1
) if

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}\int^{\infty}_{0}\rho(\varphi_{1})_{t}+m(\varphi_{1})_{x}dxdt+\int^{1}_{0}\rho_{0}(x)\varphi_{1}(x,0)dx=0,

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}\int^{\infty}_{0}m(\varphi_{2})_{t}+\left(\frac{m^{2}}{\rho}+p(\rho)\right)(\varphi_{2})_{x}dxdt+\int^{1}_{0}m_{0}(x)\varphi_{2}(x,0)dx=0

hold for any test function
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in C^{1}_{0}([0,1]\times[0,\infty))

satisfying
\varphi_{2}(0,t)=\varphi_{2}(1,t)=0
and

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}\int^{\infty}_{0}\hskip-4.30554pt\eta(u)\psi_{t}+q(u)\psi_{x}dxdt\geq 0

(1.6)

holds for any non-negative test function
\psi\in C^{1}_{0}((0,1)\times(0,\infty))
, where

(\eta,q)
is a pair of convex entropy–entropy flux of (
1.1
).

We set

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}=\int^{1}_{0}\rho_{0}(x)dx

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}=0

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}>0

We choose a positive constant

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\bar{\eta}=\int^{1}_{0}\eta(u_{0}(x))dx+\mu,\quad\nu=\dfrac{3\gamma-1}{\gamma+1}\dfrac{\bar{\eta}}{\bar{\rho}},\quad K=\bar{\rho}\nu-\bar{\eta},\\
&M_{\infty}=\dfrac{4}{3\gamma-1}\left(\dfrac{2\gamma^{2}(\gamma-1)}{3\gamma-1}\right)^{\frac{\gamma+1}{2(\gamma-1)}}\dfrac{\nu^{\frac{3\gamma-1}{2(\gamma-1)}}}{\bar{\rho}\nu-\bar{\eta}}+\dfrac{2\left(\nu\bar{\rho}+\bar{\eta}+K\right)}{\gamma-1}.\end{aligned}

\displaystyle\int^{x}_{0}\zeta(u(y,t))dy\leq

\displaystyle\int^{x}_{0}\left\{\eta_{\ast}(u(y,t))+\nu\rho(y,t)+K\right\}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}\left\{\eta_{\ast}(u(x,t))+\nu\rho(x,t)+K\right\}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int^{1}_{0}\left\{\eta_{\ast}(u_{0}(x))+\nu\rho_{0}(x)+K\right\}dy.

Theorem 1.1
.

We assume that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho_{0}(x)\geq 0\quad{a.e.\ }x\in{I},\quad\rho_{0}\in L^{\infty}(I),\quad\dfrac{m_{0}}{\rho_{0}}\in L^{\infty}(I).\end{split}

(1.11)

where

For simplicity, we set

semiclassical analysis on compact nil-manifolds
Véronique Fischer

Abstract
. In this paper, we define and study semi-classical analysis and semi-classical limits on compact nil-manifolds. As an application, we obtain properties of quantum limits for sub-Laplacians in this context, and more generally for positive Rockland operators.

1.
Introduction

The analysis of hypoelliptic operators has made fundamental progress
over the last twenty years,
see e.g.
[
1
,
33
,
38
]
and references therein.
The underlying methods and ideas are so comprehensive
that they extend beyond the class of sub-Riemannian manifolds to the setting of Hörmander’s family of vector fields.
In fact, a crucial tool for these results has turned out to be the generalisation of Connes’ tangent groupoid
[
1
,
39
,
11
,
12
]
, in some sense reuniting the lifting theory of Stein and his collaborators (see, for example,
[
34
]
or
[
36
]
) with more geometric concepts, such as tangent groups and nilpotentization
[
4
,
32
]
.
However, these approaches are seldom symbolic in a way that would make them appropriate for general questions in spectral geometry and micro-local and semi-classical analysis, such as quantum ergodicity.

The primary objective of this paper is to understand quantum limits
for a large class of hypoelliptic operators in an accessible setting.
This class encompasses intrinsic sub-Laplacians on compact nil-manifolds, without imposing any additional assumptions beyond the existence of a Carnot structure on the underlying group.
The methods and results presented herein align with the symbolic approach of semi-classical analysis in sub-Riemannian and subelliptic settings and utilising the representation theory of nilpotent Lie groups, see
[
23
]
and references therein.
Following the group case studied in
[
17
,
16
,
18
]
,
the next natural context to test this approach
is to explore the context of compact nil-manifolds

[
21
,
24
]
.
These manifolds serve as the analogues of the torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
(the quotient of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by a lattice) in spectral Euclidean geometry.
Already the (flat and commutative) tori provide a rich framework for the conventional (i.e., Euclidean and commutative) semi-classical or micro-local analysis. For instance, references such as
[
3
,
29
]
offer valuable insights into this topic.
Moreover, reduced quantum ergodicity for the canonical Laplacian on the flat torus can be obtained with elementary means involving semi-classical analysis in this context, see
[
2
, Section 3.1]
and Theorem
5.8
in this paper for a precise statement.
The starting point and initial motivation behind this paper was to understand whether any form of quantum ergodicity could be obtained on nil-manifolds in the same way.
It seems the non-commutativity makes the situation much more complicated (see Remark
5.3
, Sections
5.3.3
and
7.5
).

The pseudo-differential theory presented in this paper relies on the Kohn-Nirenberg quantization, which is always available for groups satisfying Dixmier’s Plancherel theorem (see e.g.
[
5
]
).
This leads to replacing the conventional phase space in commutative settings with
G\times{\widehat{G}}
, where
{\widehat{G}}
is the unitary dual of
G
.
In such a highly non-commutative setting, scalar-valued functions on the cotangent space are no longer suitable symbols. Instead,
we consider symbols that are fields of operators (or equivalently measurable sections) on
G\times{\widehat{G}}
or
M\times{\widehat{G}}
.
Graded nilpotent Lie groups naturally possess dilations, which provide a semi-classical scaling on the group and its unitary dual, and therefore for the Kohn-Nirenberg quantization
{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}
.
The case of symbol classes of Hörmander type was studied in
[
24
]
with applications to semiclassical Weyl laws. In this paper, we focus on the semiclassical analysis based on smoothing symbols, especially the study of further asymptotics and semiclassical measures.

The paper aims to develop the theory supporting semi-classical measures in this context, particularly focusing on quantum limits for sub-Laplacians and more generally positive Rockland operators.
In the Euclidean or Riemannian case,
the existence of semi-classical or micro-local defect measures is traditionally proven using
Gårding inequalities in pseudo-differential theories
(for example, see
[
41
, Chapter 5]
and
[
27
]
respectively).
However, an alternative proof of the existence of Euclidean micro-local defect measures (or H-measures) relies solely on multiplication operators and Fourier multipliers (see
[
37
]
).
A potentially deeper and unifying argument, essentially due to Vladimir Georgescu, has emerged in recent years. This argument relies on the properties of states of
C^{*}
-algebras, particularly the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by the semi-classical pseudo-differential calculus.
The justification for the positivity comes naturally from the symbolic calculus, the positivity of a state, and the fact that the limit of a sequence of states is itself a state.
The arguments and comments in the Euclidean case and in the context of nilpotent Lie groups can be found in the expository article
[
23
]
.
In non-commutative settings, such as those provided by sub-Riemannian contexts or nilpotent Lie groups, we cannot expect any adapted notion of micro-local or semi-classical measures to be scalar-valued. Instead, they should be operator-valued, like the symbols.
Such a notion was introduced on nilpotent Lie groups in
[
16
,
17
]
, and further developed in
[
18
]
using the approach via states of
C^{*}
-algebras.
In this paper, we demonstrate that the same arguments extend to the nil-manifold setting:

Theorem 1.1
.

We consider
\Gamma
a discrete co-compact subgroup of
a graded nilpotent Lie group
G
, and denote by
M:=\Gamma\backslash G

the corresponding compact nil-manifold.
Let
(\phi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
be a bounded family in
L^{2}(M)
.
There exists a sequence
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
going to 0 as
k\to\infty
and a positive operator-valued measure
\Gamma d\gamma\in{\mathcal{M}}^{+}_{ov}(M\times{\widehat{G}})

such that we have the convergence for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
:

\left({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)\phi_{\varepsilon},\phi_{\varepsilon}\right)_{L^{2}(M)}\longrightarrow_{\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{k},k\to\infty}\iint_{M\times{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}\left(\sigma(x,\pi)\Gamma(x,\pi)\right)d\gamma(x,\pi).

Moreover, we may assume that the limit

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\phi_{\varepsilon_{k}}\|_{L^{2}(M)}
exists
and in this case,

\iint_{M\times{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}\left(\Gamma(x,\pi)\right)d\gamma(x,\pi)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\phi_{\varepsilon_{k}}\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}.

We call

\Gamma d\gamma
the semi-classical measure of the family
(\phi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}

for the sequence
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.
The class of smoothing symbols
\mathcal{A}_{0}
is defined in Section
4.1
, and the concept of vector-valued measures is introduced in Section
6.3
.
Theorem
1.1
follows from the arguments outlined above (see Section
6
).
This notion of semi-classical measure provides a way to describe the obstruction to
L^{2}
-strong convergence of the family of functions
\phi_{\varepsilon}
on the ‘phase-space’
M\times{\widehat{G}}
.
We further explore the subject by decomposing the semi-classical measures into a sum of a scalar-valued measure and an operator-valued measure, corresponding to the 1-dimensional and infinite-dimensional parts of the unitary dual, respectively, see (
6.5
) in Section
6.4
.
Additionally, we delve into the properties of
quantum limits
, which, in this context, refer to semi-classical limits associated with a sequence of eigenfunctions of a sub-Laplacian or, more generally, a positive Rockland operator on
M
.
This concept is further explored in Section
7
.
In particular, we demonstrate that the operator-valued measure possesses properties of localisation and invariance.
This work raises several intriguing questions. For instance, the analogous properties in more commutative settings enable the description of all quantum limits on e.g. tori and spheres.
It would be fascinating to determine all quantum limits in the context of nil-manifolds of Heisenberg types and for quotients of the Engel group.

In this paper, we consider not only
intrinsic sub-Laplacians on Carnot groups but more generally positive Rockland operators on graded Lie groups.
This broader approach offers several advantages.
Firstly, by considering this larger class of hypoelliptic operators, we gain properties shared with elliptic differential operators in Euclidean and Riemannian settings. Specifically, a positive Rockland operator
\mathcal{R}
is not necessarily of degree 2, and any power
\mathcal{R}^{N}
, where
N\in\mathbb{N}
, is also a positive Rockland operator. In addition,
the setting of Rockland operators also lends itself to considering vector-valued operators, such as the Rumin complex and the Rumin-Seshadri operators.
Secondly, while the product of two stratified Lie groups may possess a stratified structure, it may be more advantageous to equip the resulting product with a graded structure (that is, with a Lie algebra equipped with a gradation but not necessarily generated by the first stratum). For instance, when studying a heat partial differential equation
\partial_{t}+\mathcal{L}_{0}
on a stratified group
G_{0}
, where
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is a sub-Laplacian on
G_{0}
, the corresponding differential operator on the product group
G=\mathbb{R}\times G_{0}
will not be homogeneous if
G
is equipped with the stratified structure. However, it will be if
G
is equipped with the graded structure derived from considering
\mathbb{R}
with weight 2.
Thirdly, the heat semi-groups associated with the sub-Laplacians on stratified groups are Markov, which implies, in particular, the positivity of the heat kernels. On a Lie group, Hunt’s theorem states that the left-invariant semi-group generated by a left-invariant differential operator of degree greater than 2 will not be Markov. However, it turns out that most arguments involving heat kernels in our field of study do not rely on Markov theory but rather on hypoellipticity as a more fundamental premise.
Lastly, our choice of setting with Rockland operators on graded groups serves as a means to emphasise the significance of hypoellipticity.
This is the setting of the Helffer-Nourrigat theorem, which characterises hypoelliptic left-invariant operators on nilpotent Lie groups. Recently, it has been extended to manifolds
[
1
]
that are not necessarily subRiemannian or operators that are not necessarily sub-Laplacians.
Finally, we note that many results and techniques presented in this paper may generalise to positive Rockland operators perturbed by lower-order terms, such as a potential (see, for example,
[
24
]
).

The paper is organised as follows.
After presenting the setting in detail in Sections
2
and
3
, we develop the semi-classical calculus based on representation theory in Section
4
. This provides us with the tools to determine asymptotics in Section
5
.
In Section
6
, we define the notion of semi-classical limit of a sequence of functions in
L^{2}(M)
in our context.
In Section
7
, we consider the semi-classical limit of a sequence of eigenfunctions of a positive Rockland operator and demonstrate some properties of localisation and invariance.

2.
Preliminaries on nilpotent Lie groups and compact nil-manifold

In this section, we set our notation for nilpotent Lie groups and nil-manifolds.
We also recall some elements of harmonic analysis in this setting.

2.1.
About nilpotent Lie groups

In this paper, a nilpotent Lie group
G
is always assumed connected and simply connected unless otherwise stated.
It is a smooth manifold which is identified with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
via the exponential mapping and a choice of coordinate system.
This leads to a corresponding Lebesgue measure on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
and the Haar measure
dx
on the group
G
,
hence
L^{p}(G)\cong L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
This also allows us
[
10
, p.16]

to define the spaces

\mathcal{D}(G)\cong\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{S}(G)\cong\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

of test functions which are smooth and compactly supported or Schwartz,
and the corresponding spaces of distributions

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G)\cong\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)\cong\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Note that this identification with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
does not usually extend to the convolution: the group convolution, i.e. the operation between two functions on
G
defined formally via

(f_{1}*f_{2})(x):=\int_{G}f_{1}(y)f_{2}(y^{-1}x)dy,

is not commutative in general whereas it is a commutative operation for functions on the abelian group
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

2.1.1.
Representations of
G
and
L^{1}(G)

In this paper, we always assume that the representations of the group
G

are strongly continuous and acting on separable Hilbert spaces.
Unless otherwise stated, the representations of
G
will also be assumed unitary.
For a representation
\pi
of
G
,
we denote by
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
its Hilbert space, and
we keep the same notation for the corresponding infinitesimal representation
which acts on the universal enveloping algebra
\mathfrak{U}(\mathfrak{g})
of the Lie algebra of the group.
It is characterised by its action on
\mathfrak{g}
:

(2.1)

\pi(X)=\partial_{t=0}\pi(e^{tX}),\quad X\in\mathfrak{g}.

The infinitesimal action acts on the space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}

of smooth vectors, that is, the space of vectors
v\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
such that
the mapping
G\ni x\mapsto\pi(x)v\in\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
is smooth.

We will use the following equivalent notations for the group Fourier transform of a function
f\in L^{1}(G)

at
\pi

\pi(f)\equiv\widehat{f}(\pi)\equiv\mathcal{F}_{G}(f)(\pi)=\int_{G}f(x)\pi(x)^{*}dx.

2.1.2.
The Plancherel formula

We denote by
{\widehat{G}}
the unitary dual of
G
,
that is, the unitary irreducible representations of
G
modulo equivalence and identify a unitary irreducible representation
with its class in
{\widehat{G}}
. The set
{\widehat{G}}
is naturally equipped with a structure of standard Borel space.
The Plancherel measure is the unique positive Borel measure
\mu

on
{\widehat{G}}
such that
for any
f\in C_{c}(G)
, we have:

(2.2)

\int_{G}|f(x)|^{2}dx=\int_{{\widehat{G}}}\|\mathcal{F}_{G}(f)(\pi)\|_{HS(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}^{2}d\mu(\pi).

Here
\|\cdot\|_{HS(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}
denotes the Hilbert-Schmidt norm on
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}
.
This implies that the group Fourier transform extends unitarily from

L^{1}(G)\cap L^{2}(G)
to
L^{2}(G)
onto the Hilbert space

L^{2}({\widehat{G}}):=\int_{{\widehat{G}}}\mathcal{H}_{\pi}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{*}d\mu(\pi),

which we identify with the space of
\mu
-square integrable fields
\sigma
on
{\widehat{G}}
with Hilbert norm

\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}({\widehat{G}})}=\sqrt{\int_{{\widehat{G}}}\|\sigma(\pi)\|_{HS(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}d\mu(\pi)}.

Consequently (
2.2
) holds for any
f\in L^{2}(G)
and may be restated as

\|f\|_{L^{2}(G)}=\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}({\widehat{G}})},

this formula is called the Plancherel formula.
It is possible to give an expression for the Plancherel measure
\mu
, see
[
10
, Section 4.3]
, although we will not need this in this paper.
We deduce the inversion formula: for any
\kappa\in\mathcal{S}(G)
,

(2.3)

\forall x\in G\ \int_{{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}(\pi(x)\mathcal{F}_{G}\kappa(\pi))d\mu(\pi)=\kappa(x).

2.1.3.
The von Neumann algebra and
C^{*}
-algebra of
G

The von Neumann algebra of the group
G
may be realised as the von Neumann algebra
\mathscr{L}(L^{2}(G))^{G}
of
L^{2}(G)
-bounded operators commuting with the left-translations on
G
.
As our group is nilpotent, the
C^{*}
-algebra of the group is then the closure of the space of right-convolution operators with convolution kernels in the Schwartz space.

Dixmier’s full Plancherel theorem
[
15
, Ch. 18]
states that the von Neumann algebra of
G
can also be realised as
the space

L^{\infty}({\widehat{G}})

of measurable fields of operators that are bounded, that is,
of measurable fields of operators

\sigma=\{\sigma(\pi)\in\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi}):\pi\in{\widehat{G}}\}
such that

\exists C>0\qquad\|\sigma(\pi)\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}\leq C\ \mbox{for}\ d\mu(\pi)\mbox{-almost all}\ \pi\in{\widehat{G}}.

The smallest of such constant
C>0
is the norm
\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}({\widehat{G}})}
of
\sigma

in
L^{\infty}({\widehat{G}})
.
Similarly, the
C^{*}
-algebra of the group
C^{*}(G)
is then
the closure of
\mathcal{F}_{G}\mathcal{S}(G)
for the
L^{\infty}({\widehat{G}})
-norm,
and

L^{\infty}({\widehat{G}})
is the von Neumann algebra generated by the
C^{*}
-algebra of the group.

The isomorphism between
the von Neumann algebras
L^{\infty}({\widehat{G}})
and
\mathscr{L}(L^{2}(G))^{G}
are described as follows.
Clearly, the Fourier multiplier
f\mapsto\mathcal{F}_{G}^{-1}\sigma\widehat{f}
of a symbol
\sigma\in L^{\infty}({\widehat{G}})
is in
\mathscr{L}(L^{2}(G))^{G}
.
The converse is given by
[
15
, Ch. 18]
:
if
T\in\mathscr{L}(L^{2}(G))^{G}
, then
there exists a unique field
\widehat{T}\in L^{\infty}({\widehat{G}})
such that

T
and
f\mapsto\mathcal{F}_{G}^{-1}\widehat{T}\widehat{f}
coincide; moreover,
\|\widehat{T}\|_{L^{\infty}({\widehat{G}})}=\|T\|_{\mathscr{L}(L^{2}(G))}
.
By the Schwartz kernel theorem,
the operator
T
admits a distributional convolution kernel
\kappa\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)
.
We may also write
\widehat{\kappa}=\widehat{T}
and call this field the group Fourier transform of
\kappa
or of
T
.
It extends the previous definition of the group Fourier transform on
L^{1}(G)
and
L^{2}(G)
.

2.2.
Compact nil-manifolds

A compact nil-manifold is the quotient
M=\Gamma\backslash G
of
a nilpotent Lie group
G
by a discrete co-compact subgroup
\Gamma
of
G
.
A concrete example of discrete co-compact subgroup is the natural discrete subgroup of the Heisenberg group, as described in

[
10
, Example 5.4.1]
.
Abstract characterisations are discussed in
[
10
, Section 5.1]
.

An element of
M
is a class

\dot{x}:=\Gamma x

of an element
x
in
G
. If the context allows it, we may identify this class with its representative
x
.

The quotient
M
is naturally equipped with the structure of a compact smooth manifold.
Furthermore, fixing a Haar measure on the unimodular group
G
,

M
inherits a measure
d\dot{x}
which is invariant under the translations for each
g\in G
given by

\dot{x}\longmapsto\dot{x}g=\Gamma xg,\qquad M\longrightarrow M.

Recall that the Haar measure
dx
on
G
is unique up to a constant and, once it is fixed,
d\dot{x}
is the only
G
-invariant measure on
M
satisfying
for any function
f:G\to\mathbb{C}
, for instance continuous with compact support,

(2.4)

\int_{G}f(x)dx=\int_{M}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}f(\gamma x)\ d\dot{x}.

We denote by
{\rm vol}(M)=\int_{M}1d\dot{x}
the volume of
M
.

2.3.
\Gamma
-periodic functions on
G
and functions on
M

Let
\Gamma
be a discrete co-compact subgroup of a nilpotent Lie group
G
.

We say that a function
f:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is
\Gamma
-left-periodic or just
\Gamma
-periodic when we have

\forall x\in G,\;\;\forall\gamma\in\Gamma,\;\;f(\gamma x)=f(x).

This definition extends readily to measurable functions and to distributions.

There is a natural one-to-one correspondence between the functions on
G
which are
\Gamma
-periodic and the functions on
M
.
Indeed, for any map
F
on
M
,
the corresponding periodic function on
G
is
F_{G}
defined via

F_{G}(x):=F(\dot{x}),\quad x\in G,

while if
f
is a
\Gamma
-periodic function on
G
,
it defines a function
f_{M}
on
M
via

f_{M}(\dot{x})=f(x),\qquad x\in G.

Naturally,
(F_{G})_{M}=F
and
(f_{M})_{G}=f
.

We also define, at least formally, the periodisation
\phi^{\Gamma}
of a function
\phi(x)
of the variable
x\in G
by:

\phi^{\Gamma}(x)=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\phi(\gamma x),\qquad x\in G.

If
E
is a space of functions or of distributions on
G
, then we denote by
E^{\Gamma}
the space of elements in
E
which are
\Gamma
-periodic.
Although

\mathcal{D}(G)^{\Gamma}=\{0\}=\mathcal{S}(G)^{\Gamma},

many other periodised functions or functional spaces have interesting descriptions on
M
(see
[
22
]
for comments and proofs):

Proposition 2.1
.

(1)

The periodisation of a Schwartz function
\phi\in\mathcal{S}(G)
is a well-defined function
\phi^{\Gamma}
in
C^{\infty}(G)^{\Gamma}
.
Furthermore, the map
\phi\mapsto\phi^{\Gamma}
yields a surjective morphism of topological vector spaces from

\mathcal{S}(G)
onto
C^{\infty}(G)^{\Gamma}

and from

\mathcal{D}(G)
onto
C^{\infty}(G)^{\Gamma}
.

(2)

For every
F\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)
, the tempered distribution
F_{G}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)
is defined by

\forall\phi\in\mathcal{S}(G)\qquad\langle F_{G},\phi\rangle=\langle F,(\phi^{\Gamma})_{M}\rangle.

The map
F\mapsto F_{G}
yields an isomorphism of topological vector spaces
from
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)
onto
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)^{\Gamma}=\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G)^{\Gamma}
.

(3)

For every
p\in[1,\infty]
,
the map
F\mapsto F_{G}
is a topological vector space isomorphism of (in fact, an isomorphism between Banach spaces) from
L^{p}(M)
onto
L^{p}_{loc}(G)^{\Gamma}
with inverse
f\mapsto f_{M}
.

(4)

Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)^{\Gamma}
and
\kappa\in\mathcal{S}(G)
.
Then
(\dot{x},\dot{y})\mapsto\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\kappa(y^{-1}\gamma x)

is a smooth function on
M\times M
and
f*\kappa\in C^{\infty}(G)^{\Gamma}
.
Viewed as a function on
M
,

(f*\kappa)_{M}(\dot{x})=\int_{M}f_{M}(\dot{y})\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\kappa(y^{-1}\gamma x)\ d\dot{y}.

(5)

If
F\in L^{p}(M)
with
p\in[1,+\infty]
then

\|(F_{G}*\kappa)_{M}\|_{L^{p}(M)}\leq\|\kappa\|_{L^{1}(G)}\|F\|_{L^{p}(M)}.

A more precise norm for the
L^{2}
-boundedness follows
from general considerations in harmonic analysis on homogeneous domains (see e.g.
[
24
, Lemma 2.5]
):

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
\kappa\in L^{1}(G)
and any
F_{G}\in L^{2}(M)
,

\|(F_{G}*\kappa)_{M}\|_{L^{2}(M)}\leq\sup_{\pi\in\widehat{\pi}}\|\pi(\kappa)\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}\|F\|_{L^{2}(M)}.

2.4.
Operators on
M
and
G

A mapping
T:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)

or
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G)\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G)
is invariant under an element
g\in G
when

\forall f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)\ (\mbox{resp.}\,\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G)),\qquad T(f(g\,\cdot))=(Tf)(g\,\cdot).

It is invariant under a subset of
G
if it is invariant under every element of the subset.

Consider a linear continuous mapping
T:\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)

or
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G)\to\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G)
respectively
which is invariant under
\Gamma
. Then it naturally induces
a linear continuous mapping

T_{M}
on
M
given via

T_{M}F=(TF_{G})_{M},\qquad F\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M).

Consequently,
if
T
coincides with a smooth differential operator on
G
that is invariant under
\Gamma
, then
T_{M}
is a smooth differential operator on
M
.
For convolution operators
T
, we obtain

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\kappa\in\mathcal{S}(G)
be a given convolution kernel, and let us denote by
T
the associated convolution operator:

T(\phi)=\phi*\kappa,\qquad\phi\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G).

The operator
T
is a linear continuous mapping
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)\to\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)
.
The corresponding operator
T_{M}
maps
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)
to
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)
continuously and linearly. Its integral kernel is

K\in C^{\infty}(M\times M),\qquad K(\dot{x},\dot{y})=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\kappa(y^{-1}\gamma x).

Consequently, the operator
T_{M}
is Hilbert-Schmidt on
L^{2}(M)
with Hilbert-Schmidt norm

\|T_{M}\|_{HS}=\|K\|_{L^{2}(M\times M)}.

Proof.

This follows from
the results in Proposition
2.1
.

3.
Preliminaries on Rockland operators on
G
and
M

In this section, we recall the definition and known properties of Rockland operators.
Our convention here is that they are homogeneous left-invariant operators on a nilpotent Lie group
G
whose group Fourier transform is injective (see Section
3.2.1
). This implies that the group
G
is graded. We therefore start this section with describing the setting of graded groups.

3.1.
Graded nilpotent Lie group

In the rest of the paper,
we will be concerned with graded Lie groups.
References on this subject includes
[
26
]
and

[
25
]
.

3.1.1.
Definition

A graded Lie group
G
is a connected and simply connected
Lie group
whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}

admits an
\mathbb{N}
-grading

\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\mathfrak{g}_{\ell}

where the
\mathfrak{g}_{\ell}
,
\ell=1,2,\ldots

3.1.2.

For any

Then the Lie algebra

The weights of the dilations are the integers

We denote by

\mathfrak{g}_{\ell}\neq\{0\}
.

We construct a basis

of

by choosing a basis

\{X_{n_{1}+1},\ldots,X_{n_{1}+n_{2}}\}
a basis of

The associated group dilations are defined by

In a canonical way, this leads to the notions of homogeneity for functions, distributions and operators and we now give a few important examples.

The Haar measure is

-homogeneous where

is called the homogeneous dimension of

.

Identifying the element of
\mathfrak{g}
with left invariant vector fields,
each

is a
\upsilon_{j}
-homogeneous differential operator. More generally, the differential operator

The unitary dual

{\widehat{G}}
obtained from

through dilations by

r\cdot\pi(x)=\pi(r\cdot x)
,

and
x\in G

3.1.3.
Homogeneous quasi-norms

An important class of homogeneous maps are the homogeneous quasi-norms,
that is, a
1

which is symmetric and definite in the sense that

In fact, all the homogeneous quasi-norms are equivalent in the sense that if

Examples may be constructed easily, such as

with the convention above and the weights

introduced in Section

.
In the stratified case, it is also possible to construct a homogeneous quasi-norm which is also a norm.

3.2.
Positive Rockland operators on
G

Let us briefly review the definition and main properties of positive Rockland operators.
References on this subject includes
[

and

[

.

3.2.1.
Definitions

A
Rockland operator

\mathcal{R}_{G}
on
G
is
a left-invariant differential operator
which is homogeneous of positive degree and satisfies the Rockland condition, that is,
for each unitary irreducible representation
\pi
on
G
,
except for the trivial representation,
the operator
\pi(\mathcal{R}_{G})
is injective on the space
\mathcal{H}_{\pi}^{\infty}
of smooth vectors of the infinitesimal representation.

Remark 3.2
.

Some authors may not assume the homogeneity of the Rockland operator, but as a convention here, we assume that Rockland operators are homogeneous.
This convention does not change the analysis since authors who do not assume homogeneity study the principal part of the operators, that is, what we call Rockland operator.

Recall
that Rockland operators
\mathcal{R}_{G}
are hypoelliptic.
In fact, they are equivalently characterised as the left-invariant homogeneous differential operators that are hypoelliptic.
If this is the case, then
\mathcal{R}_{G}+\sum_{[\alpha]<\nu}c_{\alpha}X^{\alpha}
, where
c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\nu
is the homogeneous degree of
\mathcal{R}
, is hypoelliptic.

A Rockland operator is
positive
when

\forall f\in\mathcal{S}(G),\qquad\int_{G}\mathcal{R}_{G}f(x)\ \overline{f(x)}dx\geq 0.

Any sub-Laplacian with the sign convention
-(X_{1}^{2}+\ldots+X_{n_{1}}^{2})
of a stratified Lie group is a positive Rockland operator; here
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n_{1}}
form a basis of the first stratum
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
.
The reader familiar with the Carnot group setting may
view positive Rockland operators as generalisations of the natural sub-Laplacians.
Positive Rockland operators are easily constructed on any graded Lie group, see
[
25
, Corollary 4.1.10]
.

A positive Rockland operator is essentially self-adjoint on
L^{2}(G)
and we keep the same notation for their self-adjoint extension.
Its spectrum is
{\rm sp}(\mathcal{R}_{G})
included in
[0,+\infty)
and the point 0 may be neglected in its spectrum
[
17
, Lemma 2.12]
.

For each unitary irreducible representation
\pi
of
G
,
the operator

\pi(\mathcal{R}_{G})
is essentially self-adjoint on
\mathcal{H}^{\infty}_{\pi}
,
and we keep the same notation for this self-adjoint extension.
Its spectrum
{\rm sp}(\pi(\mathcal{R}_{G}))
is a discrete subset of
(0,\infty)
if
\pi\not=1_{{\widehat{G}}}
is not trivial, while
\pi(\mathcal{R}_{G})=0
if
\pi=1_{{\widehat{G}}}
is the trivial representation, see e.g.
[
22
]
.

Let us denote by

the spectral measures

\mbox{of}\ \mathcal{R}_{G}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda dE_{\lambda},\quad\mbox{and of}\ \pi(\mathcal{R}_{G})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda dE_{\pi}(\lambda),\quad\pi\in{\widehat{G}}.

Then
\widehat{E}(\pi)=E_{\pi}
in the sense that
for any interval

,
the group Fourier transform

\widehat{E(I)}
of the projection
E(I)\in\mathscr{L}(L^{2}(G))^{G}
coincides with the field of operator

\widehat{E}(I):=\{\pi(E(I)),\pi\in{\widehat{G}}\}

which is in

.
It satisfies:

\widehat{E}(I)^{2}=\widehat{E}(I)\quad\mbox{and when not trivial}\quad\|\widehat{E}(I)\|_{L^{\infty}({\widehat{G}})}=1.

We will use the notation for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

(3.1)

E_{\lambda}=E(\{\lambda\})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\widehat{E}(\{\lambda\})=\widehat{E}_{\lambda}.

3.2.2.
Spectral multipliers in
\mathcal{R}_{G}
and in
\widehat{\mathcal{R}_{G}}

If

is a measurable function,
the spectral multiplier
\psi(\mathcal{R}_{G})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(\lambda)dE_{\lambda}
is well defined as a possibly unbounded operator on

.
If the domain of
\psi(\mathcal{R}_{G})
contains

and defines a continuous map

it is invariant under right-translation and, by the Schwartz kernel theorem, admits a right-convolution kernel

(3.2)

\psi(r^{\nu}\mathcal{R}_{G})\delta_{0}(x)=r^{-Q}\psi(\mathcal{R}_{G})\delta_{0}(r^{-1}\cdot x),\quad x\in G.

Furthermore, for each unitary irreducible representation
\pi
of

,
the domain of the operator

\psi(\pi(\mathcal{R}_{G}))=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi(\lambda)dE_{\pi}(\lambda)

\widehat{\psi(\mathcal{R}_{G})}(\pi)=\psi(\pi(\mathcal{R}_{G})).

The following statement is the famous result due to Hulanicki

Theorem 3.3

(Hulanicki’s theorem)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{G}

For instance, the heat kernels

This was mainly obtained by Christ for sub-Laplacians on stratified groups

Theorem 3.4

[Christ, Hulancki]
Let
\mathcal{R}_{G}
be a positive Rockland operator of homogeneous degree

on

If the measurable function

By plugging the function

of

See
[

This section is devoted to the general properties of positive Rockland operators on nil-manifolds.

Here

In this section, we discuss the semi-classical calculus on graded compact nil-manifolds and for smoothing symbols.

4.1.
Semi-classical pseudodifferential operators

The semi-classical pseudodifferential calculus
in the context of groups of Heisenberg type was presented in
[
17
,
18
]
, but in fact extends readily to any graded group
G
.
Here, we show how to define it on the quotient manifold
M
.

We denote by
\mathcal{A}_{0}={\mathcal{A}}_{0}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
the class of symbols, that is of fields of operators defined on
M\times{\widehat{G}}

\sigma(\dot{x},\pi)\in\mathscr{L}({\mathcal{H}}_{\pi}),\;\;(\dot{x},\pi)\in M\times{\widehat{G}},

that are of the form

\sigma(\dot{x},\pi)=\mathcal{F}_{G}\kappa_{\dot{x}}(\pi),

where
\dot{x}\mapsto\kappa_{\dot{x}}
is a smooth map from
M
to
\mathcal{S}(G)
.
The group Fourier transform yields a bijection
(\dot{x}\mapsto\kappa_{\dot{x}})\mapsto(\dot{x}\mapsto\sigma(\dot{x},\cdot)=\mathcal{F}_{G}(\kappa_{\dot{x}}))
from
C^{\infty}(M;\mathcal{S}(G))
onto
\mathcal{A}_{0}
. We equip
\mathcal{A}_{0}
with the Fréchet topology so that this mapping is an isomorphism of topological vector spaces.

We observe that
\mathcal{A}_{0}
is an algebra for the usual composition of symbols. Furthermore, it is also equipped with the involution
\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{*}
, where
\sigma^{*}=\{\sigma(\dot{x},\pi)^{*},(\dot{x},\pi)\in M\times{\widehat{G}}\}
.

Note that by the Fourier inversion formula (
2.3
), we have

\kappa_{\dot{x}}(z)=\int_{{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}(\pi(z)\,\sigma(\dot{x},\pi))d\mu(\pi)=\int_{{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}(\pi(z)\,\sigma_{G}(x,\pi))d\mu(\pi).

For any
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
,
we define the operator
{\rm Op}_{G}(\sigma)
at
F\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)
via

{\rm Op}_{G}(\sigma)F(x):=F*\kappa_{\dot{x}}(x),\quad x\in G.

This makes sense since, for each
x\in G
, the convolution of the tempered distribution
F
with the Schwartz function
\kappa_{\dot{x}}

yields a smooth function
F*\kappa_{\dot{x}}
on
G
.
Because of the Fourier inversion formula (
2.3
), it may be written formally as

{\rm Op}_{G}(\sigma)F(x)=\int_{G\times{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}(\pi(y^{-1}x)\sigma_{G}(x,\pi))F(y)dyd\mu(\pi).

If
F
is periodic, then
{\rm Op}_{G}(\sigma)F
is also periodic with

{\rm Op}_{G}(\sigma)F\in C^{\infty}(G)^{\Gamma}

and
we can view
F
and
{\rm Op}_{G}(\sigma)F
as functions on
M
,
see Section
2.3
.
In other words,
we set for any
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)
and
\dot{x}\in M
:

{\rm Op}(\sigma)f(\dot{x}):={\rm Op}_{G}(\sigma)f_{G}(x)=(f_{G}*\kappa_{\dot{x}})_{M}(\dot{x})=\int_{M}f(\dot{y})\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\kappa_{\dot{x}}(y^{-1}\gamma x)\,d\dot{y},

and this defines the function
{\rm Op}(\sigma)f\in\mathcal{D}(M)
.
We say that
\kappa_{\dot{x}}
is the
kernel associated
with the symbol
\sigma
or
{\rm Op}(\sigma)
. It satisfies the following properties:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
and let
\kappa_{\dot{x}}
be its associated kernel.
Then
{\rm Op}(\sigma)
maps
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M)
to
\mathcal{D}(M)
continuously, and its Schwartz integral is the smooth function
K
on
M\times M
given by

K(\dot{x},\dot{y})=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\kappa_{\dot{x}}(y^{-1}\gamma x).

Consequently, the operator
{\rm Op}(\sigma)
is Hilbert-Schmidt on
L^{2}(M)
with Hilbert-Schmidt norm

\|{\rm Op}(\sigma)\|_{HS}=\|K\|_{L^{2}(M\times M)}.

Proof.

The results follow from the properties in Sections
2.3
and
2.4
.
∎

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
be a small parameter.
For every symbol
\sigma\in{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
, we consider the symbol

(4.1)

\sigma^{(\varepsilon)}:=\{\sigma(\dot{x},\varepsilon\cdot\pi):(\dot{x},\pi)\in M\times{\widehat{G}}\},

whose associated kernel is then

(4.2)

\kappa^{(\varepsilon)}_{\dot{x}}(z):=\varepsilon^{-Q}\kappa_{\dot{x}}(\varepsilon^{-1}\cdot z),\quad z\in G,

if
\kappa_{\dot{x}}=\kappa^{(1)}_{\dot{x}}
is the kernel associated with the symbol
\sigma=\sigma^{(1)}
.
The semi-classical pseudo-differential calculus is then defined via

{\rm Op}^{\varepsilon}(\sigma):={\rm Op}(\sigma^{(\varepsilon)})\quad\mbox{and}\quad{\rm Op}_{G}^{\varepsilon}(\sigma):={\rm Op}_{G}(\sigma^{(\varepsilon)}).

An interesting example is given by the spectral multiplier in a positive Rockland operator
\mathcal{R}
on
G
.
For any
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
,
the operator
\psi(\mathcal{R}_{M})
defined spectrally as a bounded spectral multiplier on
L^{2}(M)
coincides with the operator
{\rm Op}(\sigma)
on
C^{\infty}(M)

with symbol
\sigma(\pi):=\psi(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}(\pi))
in
\mathcal{A}_{0}
independent of
\dot{x}\in M
:

\psi(\mathcal{R}_{M})={\rm Op}(\psi(\widehat{\mathcal{R}})).

The associated kernel is
\kappa:=\psi(\mathcal{R}(\pi))\delta_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(G)
. The integral kernel of
\psi(\mathcal{R}_{M})
is a smooth function on
M\times M
given by

K(\dot{x},\dot{y})=\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\kappa(y^{-1}\gamma x).

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, we have
\psi(\varepsilon^{\nu}\mathcal{R}_{M})={\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)
on
C^{\infty}(M)
,
where
\nu
is the degree of homogeneity of
\mathcal{R}
.

In the rest of this section, we give the general properties of the semi-classical calculus, starting with the boundedness on
L^{2}

4.2.
Boundedness in
L^{2}(M)

First, let us introduce the following norm on
\mathcal{A}_{0}
:

\|\sigma\|_{\mathcal{A}_{0}}:=\int_{G}\sup_{\dot{x}\in M}|\kappa_{\dot{x}}(y)|dy,

where
\kappa_{\dot{x}}
is the kernel associated with
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}

This is a continuous seminorm on
\mathcal{A}_{0}

Later on, we will use another continuous seminorm on
\mathcal{A}_{0}
, which is given by

(4.3)

\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})}:=\sup_{(\dot{x},\pi)\in M\times{\widehat{G}}}\|\sigma(\dot{x},\pi)\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}.

Here the supremum is the essential supremum with respect to the Plancherel measure.
Note that since
\|\pi(f)\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{1}(G)}
for any
f\in L^{1}(G)
, we have

(4.4)

\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})}\leq\sup_{\dot{x}\in M}\|\kappa_{\dot{x}}\|_{L^{1}(G)}\leq\|\sigma\|_{\mathcal{A}_{0}}.

The main property of the semi-classical calculus regarding
L^{2}
-boundedness is the following:

Proposition 4.2
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
,

\|{\rm Op}^{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))}\leq\|\sigma^{(\varepsilon)}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{0}}=\|\sigma\|_{\mathcal{A}_{0}}

where
\sigma^{(\varepsilon)}
is given in (
4.1
).

Proof.

The equality in the statement follows from
a simple change of variable
y=\varepsilon^{-1}\cdot z
in

\|\sigma^{(\varepsilon)}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{0}}=\|\sup_{\dot{x}_{1}\in M}|\kappa^{(\varepsilon)}_{\dot{x}_{1}}|\|_{L^{1}(G)}=\int_{G}\sup_{\dot{x}_{1}\in M}|\kappa_{\dot{x}_{1}}|(\varepsilon^{-1}\cdot z)\ \varepsilon^{-Q}dz=\int_{G}\sup_{\dot{x}_{1}\in M}|\kappa_{\dot{x}_{1}}(y)|dy=\|\sigma\|_{\mathcal{A}_{0}}.

Hence it suffices to show the case of
\varepsilon=1
. We observe that
we have for any
f\in\mathcal{D}(M)
,

|{\rm Op}(\sigma)f(\dot{x})|=\big|\int_{M}f(\dot{y})\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\kappa_{\dot{x}}(y^{-1}\gamma x)\,d\dot{y}\big|\leq\int_{M}|f(\dot{y})|\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sup_{\dot{x}_{1}\in M}|\kappa_{\dot{x}_{1}}(y^{-1}\gamma x)|\ d\dot{y},

consequently using (
2.4
)

\displaystyle\|{\rm Op}(\sigma)f\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\int_{M}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\sup_{\dot{x}_{1}\in M}|\kappa_{\dot{x}_{1}}(y^{-1}\gamma x)|\ d\dot{y}=\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\int_{G}\sup_{\dot{x}\in M}|\kappa_{\dot{x}}(y)|dy,

\displaystyle\|{\rm Op}(\sigma)f\|_{L^{1}(M)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}|f(\dot{y})|\int_{z\in G}\sup_{\dot{x}_{1}\in M}|\kappa_{\dot{x}_{1}}(y^{-1}z)|dz\ d\dot{y}=\|f\|_{L^{1}(M)}\int_{G}\sup_{\dot{x}\in M}|\kappa_{\dot{x}}(z^{\prime})|dz^{\prime}.

In other words, the linear map
{\rm Op}(\sigma)
extends continuously as an operator
L^{p}(M)\to L^{p}(M)
for
p=1,\infty
with norm
\leq\|\sigma\|_{\mathcal{A}_{0}}
.
By interpolation (Riesz-Thorin theorem), we obtain the first inequality in the statement.
∎

4.3.
Singularity of the operators as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

The following lemma is similar to Proposition 3.4 in
[
16
]

and shows that the singularities of the integral kernels of the operators
{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)
concentrate on the diagonal as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
It may also justify for many semi-classical properties that
the kernel associated with a symbol may be assumed to be compactly supported in the variable of the group:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\eta\in\mathcal{D}(G)
be identically equal to
1
close to
0
.
Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
and let
\kappa_{\dot{x}}(z)
denote its associated kernel.
For every
\varepsilon>0
, the symbol
\sigma_{\varepsilon}
defined via

\sigma_{\varepsilon}(\dot{x},\pi)=\mathcal{F}_{G}\left(\kappa_{\dot{x}}\eta(\varepsilon\,\cdot)\right),

that is, the symbol with associated kernel
\kappa_{\dot{x}}(z)\eta(\varepsilon\cdot z)
, is in
\mathcal{A}_{0}
.
Furthermore,
for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a constant
C=C_{N,\sigma,\eta}>0
such that

\forall\varepsilon\in(0,1]\qquad\left\|\sigma_{\varepsilon}-\sigma\right\|_{\mathcal{A}_{0}}\leq C{\varepsilon}^{N}.

Proof.

As the function
\eta
is identically
1
close to
z=0
, for all
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a bounded continuous function
\theta
, identically 0 near 0,
such that

\forall y\in G,\;\;1-\eta(y)=\theta(y)\|y\|^{N},

where
\|\cdot\|
is a fixed homogeneous quasi-norm on
G
(see Section
3.1.2
).
This notation implies

\kappa_{\dot{x}}(z)-\kappa_{\dot{x}}(z)\eta(\varepsilon\cdot z)=\kappa_{\dot{x}}(z)\theta(\varepsilon\cdot z)\|\varepsilon\cdot z\|^{N}.

As
\|\varepsilon\cdot z\|=\varepsilon\|z\|
, we obtain

\left\|\sigma_{\varepsilon}-\sigma\right\|_{\mathcal{A}_{0}}=\int_{G}\sup_{\dot{x}}|\kappa_{\dot{x}}(z)-\kappa_{\dot{x}}(z)\eta(\varepsilon\cdot z)|dz\leq\varepsilon^{N}\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{G}\sup_{\dot{x}\in M}|\kappa_{\dot{x}}(z)|\|z\|^{N}dz.

This last integral is finite and this concludes the proof.
∎

4.4.
Symbolic calculus

In order to present
the symbolic properties of the semi-classical calculus,
we first need to introduce the notions of difference operators.
They aim at replacing the derivatives with respect to the Fourier variable in the Euclidean case.

If
q
is a smooth function on
G
with polynomial growth,
we define the associated difference operator
\Delta_{q}
via

\Delta_{q}\widehat{f}(\pi)=\mathcal{F}_{G}(qf)(\pi),\quad\pi\in{\widehat{G}},

for any function
f\in\mathcal{S}(G)
and even any tempered distribution
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(G)
.
On the commutative group
(\mathbb{R}^{n},+)
if
q=x_{j}
then
\Delta_{q}
is the derivative
\partial_{\xi_{j}}
on
j
th component of the Fourier variable (up to a constant depending on the convention about the Euclidean Fourier transform).
In particular, considering the basis
(X_{j})
constructed in Section
3.1.2
,
we consider its dual
[
25
, Proposition 5.2.3]
formed by the polynomials
q_{\alpha}
such that
X^{\beta}q_{\alpha}=\delta_{\alpha,\beta}
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}
.
We then define the difference operator associated with
q_{\alpha}(\cdot^{-1}):y\mapsto q_{\alpha}(y^{-1})
:

\Delta^{\alpha}:=\Delta_{q_{\alpha}(\cdot^{-1})}.

We obtain readily the following symbolic properties of the calculus:

Proposition 4.4
.

(1)

If
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
,
then

{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma_{1}){\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma_{2})=\sum_{[\alpha]\leq N}\varepsilon^{[\alpha]}{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\Delta^{\alpha}\sigma_{1}\,X^{\alpha}_{M}\sigma_{2})\ +\ O(\varepsilon)^{N+1},

for any
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
in the
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))
sense, that is,

\forall\varepsilon\in(0,1]\qquad\|{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma_{1}){\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma_{2})-\sum_{[\alpha]\leq N}\varepsilon^{[\alpha]}{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\Delta^{\alpha}\sigma_{1}\,X^{\alpha}_{M}\sigma_{2})\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))}\lesssim_{N,\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}}\varepsilon^{N+1}.

(2)

If
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
,
then

{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)^{*}=\sum_{[\alpha]\leq N}\varepsilon^{[\alpha]}{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\Delta^{\alpha}X^{\alpha}\sigma^{*})\ +\ O(\varepsilon)^{N+1},

for any
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
in the
\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))
sense, that is,

\forall\varepsilon\in(0,1]\qquad\|{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)^{*}-\sum_{[\alpha]\leq N}\varepsilon^{[\alpha]}{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\Delta^{\alpha}X^{\alpha}\sigma^{*})\|_{\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(M))}\lesssim_{N,\sigma}\varepsilon^{N+1}.

The proof of Proposition
4.4
is a straightforward adaptation of similar arguments
on calculi on
G
and
M
with symbol classes, see e.g.
[
24
]
or
[
25
, Section 5.5]
(see also
[
16
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
):

Proof.

We follow closely
[
24
]
, more precisely the proof of Theorem 5.1 therein given in its Appendix A.
We observe that
\mathcal{A}_{0}
coincides with the smoothing symbols
S^{-\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
by
[
24
, Corollary 4.19]
.
Let
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
.
By
[
24
, Theorem 4.11]
,

{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma_{1}){\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma_{2})={\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)

with the symbol
\sigma

depending on
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
given by
its convolution kernel

\kappa_{\dot{x}}(y)=\varepsilon^{Q}\int_{G}\kappa^{(\varepsilon)}_{\sigma_{2},\Gamma xz^{-1}}((\varepsilon\cdot y)z^{-1})\ \kappa^{(\varepsilon)}_{1,x}(z)dz=\int_{G}\kappa_{\sigma_{2},\Gamma x(\varepsilon\cdot z)^{-1}}(yz^{-1})\ \kappa_{\sigma_{1},\dot{x}}(z)dz.

By the Taylor estimates due to Folland
and Stein
[
26
]
(also stated in
[
24
, Appendix A.1]
), having fixed a quasi-norm
\|\cdot\|
, we have for any
N\in\mathbb{N}

|\kappa_{\sigma_{2},\Gamma x(\varepsilon\cdot z)^{-1}}(w)-\sum_{[\alpha]\leq N}q_{\alpha}((\varepsilon\cdot z)^{-1})X^{\alpha}_{x}\kappa_{\sigma_{2},\dot{x}}(w)|\leq C_{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha^{\prime}|\leq\lceil N\rfloor\\
[\alpha^{\prime}]>N\end{subarray}}(\varepsilon\|z\|)^{[\alpha^{\prime}]}\sup_{z^{\prime}\in G}|X_{z^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\kappa_{\sigma_{2},\Gamma z^{\prime}}(w)|.

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\sigma-\sum_{[\alpha]\leq N}\varepsilon^{[\alpha]}\Delta^{\alpha}\sigma_{1}\,X^{\alpha}_{M}\sigma_{2}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{0}}=\int_{G}\sup_{\dot{x}\in M}|\kappa_{\dot{x}}(y)-\sum_{[\alpha]\leq N}\varepsilon^{[\alpha]}\kappa_{X^{\alpha_{2}}_{M}\sigma_{2}}*(q_{\alpha}(\cdot^{-1})\kappa_{\sigma_{1},\dot{x}})|dy

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{G}\sup_{\dot{x}\in M}|\int_{G}(\kappa_{\sigma_{2},\Gamma x(\varepsilon\cdot z)^{-1}}(yz^{-1})-\sum_{[\alpha]\leq N}q_{\alpha}((\varepsilon\cdot z)^{-1})X^{\alpha}_{x}\kappa_{\sigma_{2},\dot{x}}(yz^{-1}))\ \kappa_{\sigma_{1},\dot{x}}(z)dz|dy

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{G}\sup_{\dot{x}\in M}\int_{G}C_{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha^{\prime}|\leq\lceil N\rfloor\\
[\alpha^{\prime}]>N\end{subarray}}(\varepsilon\|z\|)^{[\alpha^{\prime}]}\sup_{\dot{z}^{\prime}\in M}|X_{z^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\kappa_{\sigma_{2},\dot{z}^{\prime}}(yz^{-1})||\kappa_{\sigma_{1},\dot{x}}(z)|dzdy

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C_{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha^{\prime}|\leq\lceil N\rfloor\\
[\alpha^{\prime}]>N\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{[\alpha^{\prime}]}\int_{G}\int_{G}\sup_{\dot{z}^{\prime}\in M}|X_{z^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\kappa_{\sigma_{2},\dot{z}^{\prime}}(yz^{-1})|\ \|z\|^{[\alpha^{\prime}]}\sup_{\dot{x}\in M}|\kappa_{\sigma_{1},\dot{x}}(z)|dzdy

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=C_{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha^{\prime}|\leq\lceil N\rfloor\\
[\alpha^{\prime}]>N\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{[\alpha^{\prime}]}\|\sup_{\dot{z}^{\prime}\in M}|X_{z^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\kappa_{\sigma_{2},\dot{z}^{\prime}}\|_{L^{1}(G)}\|\|\cdot\|^{[\alpha^{\prime}]}\sup_{\dot{x}\in M}|\kappa_{\sigma_{1},\dot{x}}\|_{L^{1}(G)}.

This estimate together with Proposition
4.2
shows Part (1).

We now prove
Part (2) with the same arguments.
Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
.
By
[
24
, Theorem 4.11]
,

{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)^{*}={\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma^{\varepsilon,*})

with the symbol
\sigma^{\varepsilon,*}

depending on
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
given by
its convolution kernel

\kappa_{\sigma^{\varepsilon,*},\cdot x}(y)=\bar{\kappa}_{\sigma,\Gamma x\varepsilon\cdot y^{-1}}(y^{-1}).

By the Taylor estimates due to Folland and Stein, we have

|\kappa_{\sigma,\Gamma x\varepsilon\cdot y^{-1}}(w)-\sum_{[\alpha]\leq N}q_{\alpha}(\varepsilon\cdot y^{-1})X^{\alpha}_{x}\kappa_{\sigma,x}(w)|\leq C^{\prime}_{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha^{\prime}|\leq\lceil N\rfloor\\
[\alpha^{\prime}]>N\end{subarray}}(\varepsilon\|y\|)^{[\alpha^{\prime}]}\sup_{z^{\prime}\in G}|X^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{z^{\prime}}\kappa_{\sigma,\Gamma z^{\prime}}(w)|.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\|\sigma^{\varepsilon,*}-\sum_{[\alpha]\leq N}\varepsilon^{[\alpha]}{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\Delta^{\alpha}X^{\alpha}\sigma^{*})\|_{\mathcal{A}_{0}}

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{G}\sup_{\dot{x}\in M}|\bar{\kappa}_{\sigma,\Gamma x\varepsilon\cdot y^{-1}}(y^{-1})-\sum_{[\alpha]\leq N}q_{\alpha}(\varepsilon\cdot y^{-1})X^{\alpha}_{x}\bar{\kappa}_{\sigma,x}(y^{-1})|dy

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\int_{G}\sup_{\dot{x}\in M}C^{\prime}_{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha^{\prime}|\leq\lceil N\rfloor\\
[\alpha^{\prime}]>N\end{subarray}}(\varepsilon\|y\|)^{[\alpha^{\prime}]}\sup_{z^{\prime}\in G}|X^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{z^{\prime}}\bar{\kappa}_{\sigma,\Gamma z^{\prime}}(y^{-1})|dy

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=C^{\prime}_{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha^{\prime}|\leq\lceil N\rfloor\\
[\alpha^{\prime}]>N\end{subarray}}\varepsilon^{[\alpha^{\prime}]}\int_{G}\sup_{\dot{z}^{\prime}\in M}|X^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{z^{\prime}}\bar{\kappa}_{\sigma,\dot{z}^{\prime}}(y^{-1})|\|y\|^{[\alpha^{\prime}]}dy.

This estimate together with Proposition
4.2
shows Part (2).
∎

The analysis shows that if
\sigma_{2}
and
\sigma
do not depend on
x
then

{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma_{1}){\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma_{2})={\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2})\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)^{*}={\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma^{*}).

Moreover, if
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma
are the symbols of differential operators, then the sums over
\alpha
in
Proposition
4.4

are finite and the expansions are exact for
N
large enough.

5.
Asymptotics

The semi-classical approach lends itself to understand asymptotics as the small parameter
\varepsilon
goes to 0.

5.1.
Estimates for kernels, Hilbert-Schmidt norms and traces

The integral kernels enjoy the following estimates:

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
with associated kernel
\kappa_{\dot{x}}(z)
.

(1)

The integral kernel
K^{(\varepsilon)}
of
{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)
is smooth on
M\times M
and satisfies for
\varepsilon
small:

\forall\dot{x}\in M\qquad K^{(\varepsilon)}(\dot{x},\dot{x})=\varepsilon^{-Q}\kappa_{\dot{x}}(0)+O(\varepsilon^{\infty}).

(2)

For
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
,
{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)
is a Hilbert-Schmidt and trace-class operator on
M
satisfying

{\rm Tr}\left({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)\right)=\varepsilon^{-Q}\int_{M}\kappa_{\dot{x}}(0)d\dot{x}\ +\ O(\varepsilon^{\infty}).

Proof.

When the symbol
\sigma
and the associated kernel
\kappa
do not depend on
\dot{x}
, the statement boils down to the ones in
[
22
, Section 4.1]
.
The case of a general
\sigma
extends readily for Part (1), which then readily implies the trace property in Part (2) since

{\rm Tr}\left({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)\right)=\int_{M}K^{(\varepsilon)}(\dot{x},\dot{x})d\dot{x}.

∎

Note that the Fourier inversion formula (
2.3
) yields with the notation of Proposition
5.1
:

\kappa_{\dot{x}}(0)=\int_{{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}\left(\sigma(\dot{x},\pi)\right),\quad\mbox{so}\quad\int_{M}\kappa_{\dot{x}}(0)d\dot{x}=\iint_{M\times{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}\left(\sigma(\dot{x},\pi)\right)d\dot{x}d\mu(\pi).

We open a brief parenthesis devoted to
the tensor product of the Hilbert spaces
L^{2}(M)
and
L^{2}({\widehat{G}})
defined in
Section
2.1
:

L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}}):=\overline{L^{2}(M)\otimes L^{2}({\widehat{G}})}.

We may identify
L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
with the space of measurable fields of Hilbert-Schmidt operators
\sigma=\{\sigma(\dot{x},\pi)\ :\ (\dot{x},\pi)\in M\times{\widehat{G}}\}
such that

\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})}^{2}:=\iint_{M\times{\widehat{G}}}\|\sigma(\dot{x},\pi)\|_{HS(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}^{2}d\dot{x}d\mu(\pi)<\infty.

Here
\mu
is the Plancherel measure on
{\widehat{G}}
, see Section
2.1
.
The group Fourier transform yields an isomorphism between the Hilbert spaces
L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
and
L^{2}(M\times G)
,
and
\mathcal{F}_{G}^{-1}\sigma(\dot{x},\cdot)=\kappa_{\dot{x}}
will still be called the associated kernel of
\sigma
.
The Plancherel formula yields:

\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})}=\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}(M\times G)}.

Naturally
\mathcal{A}_{0}\subset L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
. Proposition
5.1
and the properties of the semi-classical calculus imply:

Corollary 5.2
.

For
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
,
{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)
is a Hilbert-Schmidt on
M
satisfying

\|{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)\|_{HS(L^{2}(M))}^{2}=\varepsilon^{-Q}\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(G\times M)}^{2}\ +\ O(\varepsilon).

Proof.

The properties of the semi-classical calculus (Section
4.4
) imply

\displaystyle\|{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)\|_{HS(L^{2}(M))}^{2}

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\left({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)\ {\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)^{*}\right)

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}\left({\rm Op}^{\varepsilon}\left(\sigma\sigma^{*}\right)\right)+O(\varepsilon).

By Proposition
5.1
,

{\rm Tr}\left({\rm Op}^{\varepsilon}\left(\sigma\sigma^{*}\right)\right)=\varepsilon^{-Q}\int_{M\times{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}\left(\sigma\sigma^{*}\right)d\dot{x}d\mu+O(\varepsilon^{\infty}).

We conclude with
\int_{M\times{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}\left(\sigma\sigma^{*}\right)d\dot{x}d\mu=\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(G\times M)}^{2}
.
∎

5.2.
Non-centred quantum variance

In this section, we consider an orthonormal basis

(\varphi_{j})
of
\mathcal{R}_{M}
-eigenfunctions for the Hilbert space
L^{2}(M)
and the corresponding spectral counting function
N(\Lambda)
as in Corollary
3.7
, and we set

V_{\varepsilon}(\sigma):=\frac{1}{N(\varepsilon^{-\nu})}\sum_{j:\varepsilon^{\nu}\mu_{j}\in[0,1]}\left|({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j})_{L^{2}(M)}\right|^{2},\quad\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0},

and in its lim-sup:

V_{0}(\sigma):=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}V_{\varepsilon}(\sigma).

We call
V_{0}(\sigma)
the (non-centred quantum) variance associated with
(\varphi_{j})
for a symbol
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
.

Remark 5.3
.

The motivation for considering this is that the properties of
V_{0}
imply reduced quantum ergodicity in the case of the torus, see
[
2
, Section 3.1]
and Section
5.3
below.
The starting point and original motivation of this work was to understand whether the ideas could be applied in the context of nil-manifolds.
At least in the case of nil-manifolds of Heisentberg type, our analysis shows an obstruction, see Section
5.3.3

below.

5.2.1.
First consequences of the semi-classical calculus

From Proposition
4.2
,
we have for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1]

V_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\leq\|{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)\|_{\mathscr{L}(L^{2}(M))}\leq\|\sigma\|_{\mathcal{A}_{0}},

Hence
V_{0}(\sigma)\in[0,\|\sigma\|_{\mathcal{A}_{0}}]
is finite.

The properties of the semi-classical calculus, see Section
4.4
,
imply
for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}

\displaystyle[\varepsilon^{\nu}\mathcal{R},{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)]

\displaystyle={\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}){\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)-{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma\widehat{\mathcal{R}})

(5.1)

\displaystyle={\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}([\widehat{\mathcal{R}},\sigma])+\varepsilon^{\upsilon_{1}}\sum_{[\alpha]=\upsilon_{1}}{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\Delta^{\alpha}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}\ X_{\dot{x}}^{\alpha}\sigma)\ +\ O(\varepsilon^{\upsilon_{1}+1}),

where
\upsilon_{1}
is the smallest weight of the dilations, see Section
3.1.2
.
Therefore,
for any
\mathcal{R}_{M}
-eigenfunction
\varphi
, we have:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=([\varepsilon^{\nu}\mathcal{R},{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)]\varphi,\varphi)

(5.2)

\displaystyle=({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}([\widehat{\mathcal{R}},\sigma])\varphi,\varphi)+\varepsilon^{\upsilon_{1}}\sum_{[\alpha]=\upsilon_{1}}({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\Delta^{\alpha}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}\ X_{\dot{x}}^{\alpha}\sigma)\varphi,\varphi)\ +\ O(\varepsilon^{\upsilon_{1}+1})\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}.

The second term on the right-hand sides of (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) leads us to define the operation

\mathscr{E}:=\sum_{[\alpha]=\upsilon_{1}}\Delta^{\alpha}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}X_{\dot{x}}^{\alpha},

on the symbols.
For example, we will compute the operation
\mathscr{E}
explicitely for the intrinsic sub-Laplacians in the stratified case in Lemma
7.8
.
We have obtained:

(5.3)

({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}([\widehat{\mathcal{R}},\sigma])\varphi,\varphi)_{L^{2}(M)}=-\varepsilon^{\upsilon_{1}}({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\mathscr{E}\sigma)\varphi,\varphi)_{L^{2}(M)}+O(\varepsilon^{\upsilon_{1}+1})\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}.

5.2.2.
Symbols annihilating
V_{0}

The equalities in (
5.1
) and (
5.2
)
allow us to identify some symbols annihilating
V_{0}
:

Lemma 5.4
.

If
\sigma={\rm ad}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}})(\tau)=[\widehat{\mathcal{R}},\tau]
for some
\tau\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
then

V_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)=O(\varepsilon^{\upsilon_{1}})\qquad\mbox{so}\qquad V_{0}(\sigma)=0.

Proof.

From (
5.2
) applied to
\tau
, we see

\displaystyle({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j})_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle=({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}([\widehat{\mathcal{R}},\tau])\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j})_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle=([\varepsilon^{\nu}\mathcal{R}_{M},{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\tau)]\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j})_{L^{2}(M)}+O(\varepsilon^{\upsilon_{1}}).

The first term of the last right-hand side vanishes,
and the statement follows.
∎

Proceeding as in the proof of Lemma
5.4
, we obtain:

Lemma 5.5
.

If
\sigma=\mathscr{E}\tau
with
\tau\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
commuting with
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}
then

V_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)=O(\varepsilon)\qquad\mbox{so}\qquad V_{0}(\sigma)=0.

Proof.

By (
5.3
),
we have

\displaystyle({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j})_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle=({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\mathscr{E}\tau)\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j})_{L^{2}(M)}

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon^{-\upsilon_{1}}({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}([\widehat{\mathcal{R}},\tau])\varphi_{j},\varphi_{j})_{L^{2}(M)}+O(\varepsilon),

By hypotheses,
[\widehat{\mathcal{R}},\tau]=0
. The statement follows.
∎

5.2.3.
Further consequences of the semi-classical calculus

The properties of the semi-classical calculus already imply also the following properties:

Proposition 5.6
.

(1)

We have for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}

V_{0}(\sigma)=V_{0}(\sigma^{*})\quad\mbox{and}\qquad V_{0}(\sigma)\leq\frac{1}{c}\|\sigma\|^{2}_{L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})},

with
c
the constant from the Weyl laws (see Corollaries
3.7
).

(2)

The map
V_{0}:\mathcal{A}_{0}\to[0,\infty)
is a quadratic form that
admits a unique continuous extension to the Hilbert space
L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
.
It satisfies the inequalities of Part (1) on
L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
, as well as

(5.4)

\forall\sigma\in L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})\qquad V_{0}(\sigma)=V_{0}(\widehat{E}[0,1]\sigma)=V_{0}(\widehat{E}[0,1]\sigma)=V_{0}(\widehat{E}[0,1]\sigma\widehat{E}[0,1]).

Denoting by
V_{0}:L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})\times L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})\to\mathbb{C}
the corresponding skew-hermitian map, its kernel
\ker V_{0}:=\{\rho\in L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}}):V_{0}(\rho,\cdot)=0\}
is a closed subspace of
L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
containing

Proof of Proposition

The properties of the semiclassical calculus implies

V_{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\leq\frac{\|{\rm Op}^{\varepsilon}(\sigma)\|_{HS(L^{2}(M))}^{2}}{N(\varepsilon^{-\nu})}=\frac{\varepsilon^{-Q}\|\sigma\|_{L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})}^{2}}{c\varepsilon^{-Q}}+O(\varepsilon),

Part (1) follows by passing to the lim-sup.

We observe that

\displaystyle V_{0}(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2})

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})

Remark 5.7
.

Proposition
5.6
and its proof imply that any operator

satisfying

leads to a centred quantum variance
given via

Examples of such

onto a closed subspace of

or of

5.3.
Case of the torus

Here, we show that
Proposition
5.6
implies easily the reduced quantum regodicity in the case of the flat torus:

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
(\varphi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n})
for the canonical Laplacian
\Delta=-\partial_{1}^{2}-\ldots-\partial_{n}^{2}

\Delta\varphi_{j}=\mu_{j}\varphi_{j},\quad 0=\mu_{0}<\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\ldots.

Then there exists a subsequence
(j_{k})\subset\mathbb{N}
of density 1 such that

\forall f\in C(\mathbb{T}^{n})\qquad\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{M}f(\dot{x})|\varphi_{j_{k}}(\dot{x})|^{2}d\dot{x}=\int_{M}f(\dot{x})d\dot{x}.

This is a sophisticated version of the proof given in
[
2
, Section 3.1]
given by more elementary means.

5.3.1.
Determining
(\mathscr{E}\mathcal{A}_{0})^{\perp}
in the torus case

First, we apply what precedes to the case where
\mathcal{R}=\Delta
is the canonical Laplacian of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.
The
\mathcal{A}_{0}
-symbols are the functions
\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\ni(\dot{x},\xi)\mapsto\sigma(\dot{x},\xi)
in
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
.
We compute easily

\mathscr{E}=-2\sum_{j}\partial_{\dot{x}_{j}}\partial_{\xi_{j}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad{\rm ad}(\widehat{\mathcal{L}})\mathcal{A}_{0}=\{0\},\qquad\mathscr{E}\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\widehat{\mathcal{L}}}=\mathscr{E}\mathcal{A}_{0},

since
all the symbols commute with each other in this setting.
Moreover,
(\mathscr{E}\mathcal{A}_{0})^{\perp}

is the space of symbols
\sigma\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n};\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
viewed as functions on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and satisfying
\mathscr{E}\sigma=0
in the sense of distributions on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We can determine the projection onto
(\mathscr{E}\mathcal{A}_{0})^{\perp}

using the well-known properties of the Fourier series and of the Euclidean Fourier transforms:

Lemma 5.9
.

With the setting of Theorem
5.8
,
the orthogonal projection onto
(\mathscr{E}\mathcal{A}_{0})^{\perp}\subset L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
is given by

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\sigma,\qquad\mbox{where}\quad\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\sigma\right)(\dot{x},\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\sigma(\dot{x}^{\prime},\xi)d\dot{x}^{\prime},\quad\sigma\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Proof of Lemma
5.9
.

If
\sigma\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies
\mathscr{E}\sigma=0
in the sense of distributions, then
applying the Fourier transform on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}

to
\sigma
and denoting the resulting function as
\widehat{\sigma}(\ell,y)
,
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},

y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we see that

\forall\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\qquad(\sum_{j}\ell_{j}y_{j})\widehat{\sigma}(\ell,y)=0,

so
\widehat{\sigma}(\ell,\cdot)\equiv 0
for any
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
.
This shows that
(\mathscr{E}\mathcal{A}_{0})^{\perp}
is the space of functions
\sigma\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
which are constant in
\dot{x}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
. The conclusion follows.
∎

5.3.2.
Proof of Theorem
5.8

We can now deduce the reduced QE from Proposition
5.6
:

By Lemma
5.9
, for any
\sigma\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})

\sigma-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\sigma\in\overline{\mathscr{E}\mathcal{A}_{0}}\subset\ker V_{0}

by Proposition

(2),
so
V_{0}(\sigma-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\sigma)=0
.
We apply this to
\sigma(\dot{x},\xi)=f(\dot{x})\psi(|\xi|^{2})

for any

, having fixed a function
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})
with
\psi\equiv 1
on

. We have

V_{0}\left(\sigma-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\sigma\right)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{N(\varepsilon^{-\nu})}\sum_{j:\varepsilon^{\nu}\lambda_{j}\in[0,1]}\left|\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}f(\dot{x})|\varphi_{j}(\dot{x})|^{2}d\dot{x}-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}f(\dot{x})d\dot{x}\right|^{2}=0

The end of the proof is now a classical argument relying on the separability of the space

of continuous functions on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Indeed,
the null limit above together with a well-known result on Cesaro means of positive sequences, see e.g.

(or equivalently with a direct proof ‘by hand’

We apply the above result to each continuous function

,

, of a dense countable family

We then extract diagonally a new subsequence

6.3.1.
Operator-valued measures

Let us recall the notion of operator-valued measure as defined in

Definition 6.5
.

Let

be a complete separable metric space,
and let

Denote by

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{ov}(Z,({\mathcal{H}}_{\xi})_{\xi\in Z})

the set of pairs

where

is a positive Radon measure on
Z

and
\Gamma=\{\Gamma(\xi)\in{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{H}}_{\xi}):\xi\in Z\}
is a measurable field of trace-class operators
such that

(6.4)

\int_{Z}{\rm Tr}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\xi}}|\Gamma(\xi)|d\gamma(\xi)<\infty.

Two pairs
(\gamma,\Gamma)
and
(\gamma^{\prime},\Gamma^{\prime})

in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{ov}(Z,({\mathcal{H}}_{\xi})_{\xi\in Z})

are

when there exists a measurable function

such that

for
\gamma
-almost every
\xi\in Z
.
The equivalence class of
(\gamma,\Gamma)
is denoted by
\Gamma d\gamma
,
and the resulting quotient set is
denoted by
{\mathcal{M}}_{ov}(Z,({\mathcal{H}}_{\xi})_{\xi\in Z})
.

A pair
(\gamma,\Gamma)

in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{ov}(Z,({\mathcal{H}}_{\xi})_{\xi\in Z})

is

when

\Gamma(\xi)\geq 0
for
\gamma
-almost all
\xi\in Z
.
In this case, we may write
(\gamma,\Gamma)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{ov}^{+}(Z,({\mathcal{H}}_{\xi})_{\xi\in Z})
,
and
\Gamma d\gamma\geq 0
for
\Gamma d\gamma\in{\mathcal{M}}_{ov}^{+}(Z,({\mathcal{H}}_{\xi})_{\xi\in Z})
.

The quantity in (
6.4
) is constant on the equivalence class of
(\gamma,\Gamma)

and is denoted by

\|\Gamma d\gamma\|_{\mathcal{M}}.

It is a norm on
{\mathcal{M}}_{ov}(Z,({\mathcal{H}}_{\xi})_{\xi\in Z})
, which is then a Banach space.

6.3.2.
The dual of

Proceeding as in
[

[

, Section 3.1]

Following

\mathcal{R}
, while if

\exists s>0,\qquad\sup_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}\|(\varepsilon^{\nu}\mathcal{R}_{M})^{s}\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(M)}+\|(\varepsilon^{\nu}\mathcal{R}_{M})^{-s}\phi_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(M)}<\infty.

then it is uniformly strictly
\varepsilon
-oscillating.

Proceeding as in
[
18
, Proposition 4.1]
, we obtain:

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
(\phi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon\in(0,1]}
be a bounded family in
L^{2}(M)
that is uniformly
\varepsilon
-oscillating for
\mathcal{R}
.
Let
\Gamma d\gamma
be a semi-classical measure, and denote by
(\varepsilon_{k})
the corresponding subsequence.
Then for any
f\in\mathcal{D}(M)
, we have

(f\phi_{\varepsilon_{k}},\phi_{\varepsilon_{k}})_{L^{2}(M)}\longrightarrow_{k\to\infty}\iint_{M\times{\widehat{G}}}f(\dot{x}){\rm Tr}\left(\Gamma(\dot{x},\pi)\right)d\gamma(\dot{x},\pi).

If
(\phi_{\varepsilon})
is in addition uniformly strictly
\varepsilon
-oscillating, then the semi-classical measure does not charge the trivial representation
1_{{\widehat{G}}}
in the sense that

\gamma(M\times\{1_{{\widehat{G}}}\})=0.

The proof is omitted due to its similarity to the one of
[
18
, Proposition 4.1]
: the only difference is that the symbols depend on
x\in G
in
[
18
]
instead of
\dot{x}\in M
here, while the difficulty is the analysis on
{\widehat{G}}
.
Consequently, with the notation of the statement,
the weak-star limit of
|\phi_{\varepsilon_{k}}(\dot{x})|^{2}d\dot{x}
is equal to
\int_{{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}\left(\Gamma(x,\pi)\right)d\gamma(\dot{x},\pi)
.
It admits a further decomposition, see Remark
6.10
.

6.4.
The decomposition
{\widehat{G}}={\widehat{G}}_{\infty}\sqcup{\widehat{G}}_{1}

The unitary dual of any nilpotent Lie group may be described as the disjoint union

{\widehat{G}}={\widehat{G}}_{\infty}\sqcup{\widehat{G}}_{1},

of the class of the infinite dimensional representations parametrised
{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}

with the class of the finite dimensional representations
{\widehat{G}}_{1}
.
By the orbit method, the finite dimensional representations are of dimension one
and may be identified with a closed subset of
{\widehat{G}}
given by characters:

{\widehat{G}}_{1}:=\{\pi^{\omega}\ \mbox{given by}\ \chi_{\omega}(v)=e^{i\omega(v)}\ :\ \omega\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\perp}\},

with
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\perp}
denoting the subspace of linear forms
\omega\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
such that
\omega=0
on
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
.
To fix the idea, we identify

{\widehat{G}}_{1}\sim[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]^{\perp}\sim\mathfrak{v}^{*},

with the dual
\mathfrak{v}^{*}
of a subspace
\mathfrak{v}
of
\mathfrak{g}
; as we have already fixed a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
, we can choose
\mathfrak{v}
to be the orthogonal complement for the corresponding scalar on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Any linear functional
\ell=\ell_{\Gamma d\gamma}\in(C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{*}
admits a unique extension to a weakly continuous linear functional on the von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
enveloping
C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
.
The decomposition
{\widehat{G}}={\widehat{G}}_{\infty}\sqcup{\widehat{G}}_{1}
will turn out to be useful to decompose these extended linear functionals.
Indeed, as the representation in
{\widehat{G}}_{1}
are of dimension one,
1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}}\Gamma d\gamma
is a scalar valued measure.
Hence, any operator valued measure
\Gamma d\gamma\in\mathcal{M}_{ov}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
may be decomposed into the sum of one scalar valued measure and one operator-valued measure:

(6.5)

\Gamma d\gamma=1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}}\Gamma d\gamma+1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}\Gamma d\gamma.

Let us understand this decomposition on linear functionals
\ell
.
First, we will need the following observation:

Example 6.8
.

The symbols
1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}}
and
1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}
are in
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
.
Therefore, for any
\sigma\in C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
, the symbols
\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}}
and
\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}
are in

L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
.

This example together with the natural extension of any functional
\ell=\ell_{\Gamma d\gamma}\in(C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{*}
to
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
shows that the following decomposition makes sense:

\forall\sigma\in C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})\qquad\ell(\sigma)=\ell(\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}})+\ell(\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}).

The operator-valued measure
1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}}\Gamma d\gamma
is a scalar measure on
M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}\sim M\times\mathfrak{v}^{*}
.
We may assume that
\Gamma=1
on

M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}
, and by convention we do.
The following lemma shows that a symbol
\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}}
,
\sigma\in C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
, may be identified with an element of
C_{0}(M\times\mathfrak{v}^{*})
;
this provides a simpler description of the scalar measure
1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}}\gamma
that will be useful in Proposition
7.6
(2):

Lemma 6.9
.

(1)

Any
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
,
\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}}
, identified with the restriction of
\sigma
to
M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}
, coincides with the element in
C^{\infty}(M;\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{v}^{*}))

\dot{x}\longmapsto(\omega\mapsto\sigma(\dot{x},\pi^{\omega})).

(2)

Moreover,
the closure for
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
of the algebra of symbols
\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}}
,

\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
, is equal to
\{\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}},\sigma\in C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})\}
and is
naturally isomorphic to the
C^{*}
-algebra
C_{0}(M\times\mathfrak{v}^{*})
of continuous functions on
M\times\mathfrak{v}^{*}
vanishing at infinity.

Proof.

Before starting the proof, we observe that we can choose the canonical basis
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
adapted to the gradation of
\mathfrak{g}
and to the decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{v}\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
.
This allows us to define the Lebesgue measures
dV
and
dZ
on
\mathfrak{v}
and
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
such that
dVdZ
is the exponential pull back of the Haar measure:

\forall f\in L^{1}(G)\qquad\int_{G}f(x)dx=\iint_{\mathfrak{v}\times[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}f(\exp(V+Z))dVdZ.

The derived group
[G,G]
, whose Lie algebra is
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, is therefore equipped with a compatible Haar measure:

\int_{[G,G]}f(z)dz=\int_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}f(\exp Z)dZ.

Let
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
, and denote its kernel by
\kappa_{\dot{x}}(y)
.
Then

(6.6)

\sigma(\dot{x},\pi^{\omega})=\int_{\mathfrak{v}}\kappa_{\dot{x}}(\exp(V+Z))\overline{\chi}_{\omega}(V)dVdZ=\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{v}}(\int_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}\kappa_{\dot{x}})(\omega),\qquad\dot{x}\in M,\ \omega\in\mathfrak{v}^{*},

where
\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{v}}
denotes the Euclidean Fourier transform on
\mathfrak{v}
,
and
\int_{[G,G]}
the integration on
[G,G]
.
Since
\kappa\in C^{\infty}(M;\mathcal{S}(G))
, Part (1) follows.

The computation (
6.6
) also implies that
given any
t\in C^{\infty}(M;\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{v}^{*}))
, we can find a symbol
\tau\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
such that
\tau 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}}
coincides with
t
.
Indeed, fixing a function
\chi\in\mathcal{D}([\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}])
satisfying
\int_{[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]}\chi(Z)dZ=1
,
the kernel

\kappa
defined via

\kappa_{\dot{x}}(\exp(V+Z))=\chi(Z)\mathcal{F}_{\mathfrak{v}}^{-1}t(\dot{x},\cdot),\qquad V\in\mathfrak{v},\ Z\in[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}],

is in
C^{\infty}(M;\mathcal{S}(G))
,
and by (
6.6
),
its symbol
\sigma
satisfies
\sigma(x,\pi^{\omega})=t(\dot{x},\omega)
for all

(\dot{x},\omega)\in M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}
.
Consequently,
the
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
-closure of
\{\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}},\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}\}
, identifies naturally
with the closure of
C^{\infty}(M;\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{v}^{*}))
for the supremum norm on
M\times\mathfrak{v}^{*}
,
and therefore identifies naturally with
C_{0}(M\times\mathfrak{v}^{*})
.

Part (1) implies that the
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
-closure of
\{\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}},\sigma\in C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})\}
also identifies with a
C^{*}
-sub-algebra of
C_{0}(M\times\mathfrak{v}^{*})
.
It is straightforward to check that

\forall\sigma\in C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})\qquad\|\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}}\|_{L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})}\leq\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})}.

So the
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
-closure of
\{\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}},\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}\}
, is a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\{\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}},\sigma\in C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})\}
.
As the former identifies with the whole of
C_{0}(M\times\mathfrak{v}^{*})
, they are both equal to
C_{0}(M\times\mathfrak{v}^{*})
.
∎

Remark 6.10
.

Let us consider the setting of Proposition
6.7
:
let
\Gamma d\gamma
be the semi-classical limit of a bounded family
(\phi_{\varepsilon})
in
L^{2}(M)
that is
\varepsilon
-oscillating for a positive Rockland operator
\mathcal{R}_{M}
and a subsequence
\varepsilon_{j}
.
Then we have obtained a further decomposition of the weak-star limit

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}|\phi_{\varepsilon_{k}}(\dot{x})|^{2}d\dot{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}(\Gamma(\dot{x},\pi))d\gamma(\dot{x},\pi)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\widehat{G}}_{1}}d\gamma(\dot{x},\pi)+\int_{{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}{\rm Tr}(\Gamma(\dot{x},\pi))d\gamma(\dot{x},\pi).

With different means (in fact, with the Euclidean micro-local analysis), this observation was already obtained in particular cases that essentially boil down to Heisenberg nilmanidolds, see
[
9
,
30
]
.

7.
Quantum limits for
\mathcal{R}_{M}

In this section, we study the semi-classical limit in our theory associated with a sequence of eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{R}_{M}
, where
\mathcal{R}
is a (fixed) positive Rockland operator.
We obtain properties of localisation and invariance in Proposition
7.1

and Corollary
7.2

below, described more explicitly in the case of sub-Laplacians in Section
7.4
.
The proof requires to analyse symbols and operator-valued measures commuting with
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}
in Section
7.3
.

7.1.
Family of
\mathcal{R}_{M}
-eigenfunctions

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a positive Rockland operator on
G
and denote by
\mathcal{R}_{M}
the corresponding operator on
M
, as well as its self-adjoint extension to
L^{2}(M)
. Recall that
the spectral decompositions of
\mathcal{R}
and
\pi(\mathcal{R})
for
\pi\in{\widehat{G}}
are denoted by

E
and
\pi(E)
, see Section
3.2
.
Moreover,
\pi^{\omega}(\mathcal{R})
is the (scalar) eigenvalue of
\mathcal{R}
for
\chi_{\omega}
:
\mathcal{R}\chi_{\omega}=\pi^{\omega}(\mathcal{R})\chi_{\omega}
.

We can already obtain properties of localisation of the semi-classical measures of a sequence of
\mathcal{R}_{M}
-eigenfunctions with eigenfunctions going to infinity:

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
(\phi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of eigenfunctions for
\mathcal{R}_{M}
:

\mathcal{R}_{M}\phi_{j}=\mu_{j}\phi_{j},\quad j=0,1,2,\ldots.

Assume
\mu_{j}\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
.
Consider a semi-classical measure
\Gamma d\gamma
of
(\phi_{j})
at scale
\mu_{j}^{-1/\nu}
for the subsequence
(j_{k})
where
\nu
is the degree of homogeneity of
\mathcal{R}
.
We have for
\gamma
-almost all
(\dot{x},\pi)\in M\times{\widehat{G}}

\Gamma(\dot{x},\pi)=\pi(E_{1})\Gamma(\dot{x},\pi)\pi(E_{1}).

The decomposition (
6.5
) of
\Gamma d\gamma
according to
{\widehat{G}}={\widehat{G}}_{1}\sqcup{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}

satisfies the following properties:

(1)

The scalar valued measure

1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}}\gamma
on
M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}
is supported in

M\times\{\pi^{\omega}\in{\widehat{G}}_{1}:\pi^{\omega}(\mathcal{R})=1\}\sim M\times\{\omega\in\mathfrak{v}^{*}:\mathcal{R}\chi_{\omega}=\chi_{\omega}\}.

(2)

For
\gamma
-almost all
(\dot{x},\pi)\in M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}
, the operator

\Gamma(\dot{x},\pi)
maps the finite dimensional 1-eigenspace for
\pi(\mathcal{R})
onto itself and is trivial anywhere else.

Proof.

The properties of the functional and semi-classical calculi imply for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}

({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma\widehat{\mathcal{R}})\phi_{j},\phi_{j})=({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)\varepsilon^{\nu}\mathcal{R}_{M}\phi_{j},\phi_{j})=\varepsilon^{\nu}\mu_{j}({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)\phi_{j},\phi_{j}).

Similarly, since
\mathcal{R}
is self-adjoint, we have:

\displaystyle({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}\sigma)\phi_{j},\phi_{j})

\displaystyle=(\varepsilon^{\nu}\mathcal{R}{\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)\phi_{j},\phi_{j})+O(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon^{\nu}({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)\phi_{j},\mathcal{R}\phi_{j})+O(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{\nu}\mu_{j}({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\sigma)\phi_{j},\phi_{j})+O(\varepsilon).

We take
\varepsilon_{j}=\mu_{j}^{-1/\nu}
and
j=j_{k}
, and then the limit as
k\to\infty
to obtain for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
:

(7.1)

\int_{M\times{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}(\sigma\widehat{\mathcal{R}}\Gamma)d\gamma=\int_{M\times{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}(\sigma\Gamma)d\gamma=\int_{M\times{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}\sigma\Gamma)d\gamma.

As explained in Section
6.4
,
any linear function
\ell=\ell_{\Gamma d\gamma}
admits a natural unique extension to a continuous linear function on the von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
.
Hence, (
7.1
) is also valid for any symbol
\sigma
in
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
,
in particular for
\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}}
and also
\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}\widehat{E}_{\lambda}
or
\widehat{E}_{\lambda}\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}
below.
Consequently, (
7.1
) also holds for
\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}}
,
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
, and
we obtain

\forall f\in C_{0}(M\times\mathfrak{v}^{*})\qquad\qquad\int_{M\times\mathfrak{v}^{*}}f(\dot{x},\omega)\pi^{\omega}(\mathcal{R})d\gamma(\dot{x},\pi^{\omega})=\int_{M\times\mathfrak{v}^{*}}\sigma(\dot{x},\pi^{\omega})d\gamma(\dot{x},\pi^{\omega}).

Part (1) follows.
Equality (
7.1
)
also holds for any
\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}\widehat{E}_{\lambda}
with
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
fixed, see (
3.1
).
Since we have
\widehat{E}_{\lambda}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}=\widehat{E}_{\lambda}\lambda
, we obtain for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
:

\int_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}{\rm Tr}(\sigma\widehat{E}_{\lambda}\lambda\Gamma)d\gamma=\int_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}{\rm Tr}(\sigma\widehat{E}_{\lambda}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}\Gamma)d\gamma=\int_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}{\rm Tr}(\sigma\widehat{E}_{\lambda}\Gamma)d\gamma.

By uniqueness, we have

\lambda\widehat{E}_{\lambda}\Gamma=\widehat{E}_{\lambda}\Gamma
, which means that the projection of the image of
\Gamma(x,\pi)

onto the (finite dimensional)
\lambda
-eigenspaces of
\pi(\mathcal{R})

is zero except perhaps for
\lambda=1
.
Similarly, Equality (
7.1
)
also holds for any
\widehat{E}_{\lambda}\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}
so

\lambda\Gamma\widehat{E}_{\lambda}=\Gamma\widehat{E}_{\lambda}
, which means that
\Gamma(x,\pi)=0
on all the (finite dimensional)
\lambda
-eigenspaces of
\pi(\mathcal{R})
except perhaps for
\lambda=1
.
This concludes the proof of Part (2).
∎

7.2.
Invariance properties

The properties of the semi-classical calculus can give us further information on the semi-classical measures, in particular properties of invariance.
Indeed, the property (
5.3
) implies for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
commuting with
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}
,

\forall\varepsilon\in(0,1],\ j\in\mathbb{N}\qquad({\rm Op}^{(\varepsilon)}(\mathscr{E}\sigma)\phi_{j},\phi_{j})=O(\varepsilon),

where
\mathscr{E}:=\sum_{[\alpha]=\upsilon_{1}}\Delta^{\alpha}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}X_{\dot{x}}^{\alpha}.

Taking
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{j}
and
j\to\infty
, Proposition
7.1
implies the following property of invariance:

\iint_{M\times{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}\left(\mathscr{E}\sigma\ \Gamma\right)d\gamma=0.

The rest of this section is devoted to showing the following lemma stating
that the equality just above also holds on
M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}
and on
M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}
separately:

Corollary 7.2
.

We continue with the setting of Proposition
7.1
.
We have

\forall f\in\mathcal{D}(M\times\mathfrak{v}^{*})\qquad\iint_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}}\sum_{[\alpha]=\upsilon_{1}}\Delta^{\alpha}\widehat{\mathcal{R}}(\chi_{\omega})\ X_{M}^{\alpha}f(\dot{x},\omega)\ d\gamma(\dot{x},\pi^{\omega})=0,

and

\forall\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}\qquad\iint_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}{\rm Tr}\left(\mathscr{E}\sigma\ \Gamma\right)d\gamma=0.

The proof of Corollary
3.7
will be given in Section
7.3.3
. It will rely on the properties of symbols commuting with
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}
we now present.

7.3.
Symbols and operator-valued measures commuting with
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}

7.3.1.
Spaces of symbols commuting with (the spectral decomposition of)
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}

Here, we study the spaces of symbols commuting with the spectral decompositions
\widehat{E}
of
\mathcal{R}
, see Section
3.2
:

Definition 7.3
.

We denote by

\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}},\ (C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}},

the subsets of elements respectively in
\mathcal{A}_{0}
,
C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
and
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
that commute with
\widehat{E}(I)
for every interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
.

As may be expected, these spaces enjoy the following properties:

Proposition 7.4
.

(1)

A symbol
\sigma
is in
\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
if and only if it commutes with
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}
.
The space
\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
is a subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
that contains all the symbols given by
a(\dot{x})\psi(\pi(\mathcal{R}))
, where
a\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
.
In particular, it contains the approximate identity of
C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
given by
\psi_{j}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}})
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
,
where
\psi_{j}\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})
is valued in
[0,1]
and satisfies
\psi_{j}(\lambda)=1
on
\{|\lambda|\geq j\}
.

(2)

The space
(C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
is a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
.
It is the closure of
\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
for the
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
-norm.

(3)

The space
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
is a von Neumann subalgebra of
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
. It is the von Neumann algebra envoloping
(C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
.

Proof.

If
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
, then
\sigma(\dot{x},\pi)
commutes with
\pi(\mathcal{R})=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lambda d\pi(E_{\lambda})
for all
(\dot{x},\pi)\in M\times{\widehat{G}}
.
The converse holds for any
\pi\in{\widehat{G}}\setminus\{1_{{\widehat{G}}}\}
since the spectrum of
\pi(\mathcal{R})
is a discrete unbounded subset of
(0,\infty)
and its eigenspaces are finite dimensional, see Section
3.2
.
The rest of Part (1) follows from routine checks and Hulanicki’s theorem (Theorem
3.3
).

As
\mathcal{A}_{0}
is dense in
C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
,

(C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
is the closure of

\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
and it is a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
. This proves Part (2).
As the
C^{*}
-algebra
C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
generates the von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
(for instance, in the sense of taking the closure for the Strong Operator Topology),
the
C^{*}
-algebra
(C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
generates the von Neumann algebra
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
which is a von Neumann subalgebra of
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
. This proves Part (3).
∎

7.3.2.
Space of operator valued measures commuting with
\widehat{E}

We say that the pair
(\gamma,\Gamma)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{ov}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
commutes with
\widehat{E}
when
for any interval

, we have

\Gamma(\dot{x},\pi)\pi(E(I))=\pi(E(I))\Gamma(\dot{x},\pi)
for
\gamma
-almost all
(\dot{x},\pi)\in M\times{\widehat{G}}
.
We check readily that if
(\gamma,\Gamma)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{ov}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
commutes with
\widehat{E}
, then so does any pair equivalent to it.
We then say that the operator valued measure
\Gamma d\gamma
commutes with
\widehat{E}
.

Definition 7.5
.

We denote the space of
\Gamma d\gamma
commuting with
\widehat{E}
by

\mathcal{M}_{ov}(M\times{\widehat{G}})^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}.

As may be expected, this space may identified with the dual of
(C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
amongst other properties:

Proposition 7.6
.

(1)

The space
\mathcal{M}_{ov}(M\times{\widehat{G}})^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{M}_{ov}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
.
Its image under
\Gamma d\gamma\mapsto\ell_{\Gamma d\gamma}
is the dual of
(C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
.

(2)

We have (with the closure for the

-norm)

\displaystyle\overline{\{\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}},\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}\}}

\displaystyle=\{\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}},\sigma\in(C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}\}

\displaystyle=\{\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}},\sigma\in C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})\}\sim C_{0}(M\times\mathfrak{v}^{*}).

The rest of this paragraph is devoted to the proof of Proposition

.
In order to prove Part 1, we will need the following result of functional analysis:

Lemma 7.7
.

Let
R
be a self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
We assume that its spectrum
{\rm sp}(R)
is discrete with accumulation point at infinity and that every eigenspace is
finite dimensional.
Denoting by
F_{\zeta}
the projection onto the
\zeta
-eigenspace, given any operator
A\in\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H})
, we may define

A^{R}=\sum_{\zeta\in{\rm sp}(R)}F_{\zeta}AF_{\zeta}.

Then
A^{R}
is a bounded operator on
\mathcal{H}
that commutes with any
F_{\zeta}
,
\zeta\in{\rm sp}(R)
. Moreover,
\|A^{R}\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H})}\leq\|A\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H})}
and if
A
is in addition trace-class then so is
A^{R}
with
{\rm Tr}|A^{R}|\leq{\rm Tr}|A|
.

Proof of Lemma
7.7
.

Let
A\in\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H})
. Then

\|A^{R}u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\sum_{\zeta\in{\rm sp}(R)}(F_{\zeta}AF_{\zeta}u,AF_{\zeta}u)_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\|A\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}^{2}\sum_{\zeta\in{\rm sp}(R)}\|F_{\zeta}u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\|A\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}^{2}\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},

since the
F_{\zeta}
’s are orthogonal projections satisfying
\sum_{\zeta\in{\rm sp}(R)}F_{\zeta}=\text{\rm I}_{\mathcal{H}}
.
This proves
\|A^{R}\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H})}\leq\|A\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H})}
.
If
A
is trace class then for any
B\in\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H})
,

|{\rm Tr}(A^{R}B)|=|{\rm Tr}(AB^{R})|\leq{\rm Tr}|A|\|B^{R}\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}\leq{\rm Tr}|A|\|B\|_{\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\pi})}

showing
{\rm Tr}|A^{R}|\leq{\rm Tr}|A|
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
7.6
(1).

With the spectral properties of
\pi(\mathcal{R})
recalled in Section
3.2
, given
(\Gamma,\gamma)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{ov}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
, we can define
the measurable field
\Gamma^{(\widehat{\mathcal{R}})}
as in Lemma
7.7
via

\Gamma^{(\widehat{\mathcal{R}})}(\dot{x},\pi):=\sum_{\zeta\in{\rm sp}(\pi(\mathcal{R}))}\pi(E_{\zeta})\Gamma(\dot{x},\pi)\pi(E_{\zeta}),\qquad(\dot{x},\pi)\in M\times{\widehat{G}};

Clearly,
\Gamma^{(\widehat{\mathcal{R}})}
is trace-class with

{\rm Tr}|\Gamma^{(\widehat{\mathcal{R}})}(\dot{x},\pi)|\leq{\rm Tr}|\Gamma(\dot{x},\pi)|

and commutes with
\pi(E_{\zeta})
,
\zeta\in{\rm sp}(\pi(\mathcal{R}))
.
This implies that
(\Gamma^{(\widehat{\mathcal{R}})},\gamma)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{ov}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
commutes with
\widehat{E}
.
This construction passes through the quotient, defining a map
\Gamma d\gamma\mapsto\Gamma^{(\widehat{\mathcal{R}})}d\gamma
on

\mathcal{M}_{ov}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
.
We check readily that this map is linear, 1-Lipschitz for
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{M}}
and that its image is
\mathcal{M}_{ov}(M\times{\widehat{G}})^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
where it is the identity; moreover, this is a continuous projection onto
\mathcal{M}_{ov}(M\times{\widehat{G}})^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
.
Hence,
\mathcal{M}_{ov}(M\times{\widehat{G}})^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{M}_{ov}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
.

Let
\ell:(C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}\to\mathbb{C}
be a continuous linear functional.
By the Hahn-Banach theorem,
\ell
extends continuously to an element
\widetilde{\ell}
of
(C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{*}
. Let
\Gamma d\gamma\in\mathcal{M}_{ov}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
be such that
\widetilde{\ell}=\ell_{\Gamma d\gamma}
.
If
\sigma\in(C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
then using the resolution
\sum_{\zeta\in{\rm sp}(\pi(\mathcal{R}))}\pi(E_{\zeta})=\text{\rm I}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}
of the identity operator
\text{\rm I}_{\mathcal{H}_{\pi}}

and
\pi(E_{\zeta})^{2}=\pi(E_{\zeta})
, we have:

\displaystyle\ell(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{\ell}(\sigma)=\iint_{M\times{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}\left(\sigma(x,\pi)\Gamma(x,\pi)\right)d\gamma(x,\pi)

\displaystyle=\iint_{M\times{\widehat{G}}}\sum_{\zeta\in{\rm sp}(\pi(\mathcal{R}))}{\rm Tr}\left(\pi(E_{\zeta})\sigma(x,\pi)\pi(E_{\zeta})\Gamma(x,\pi)\right)d\gamma(x,\pi)

\displaystyle=\iint_{M\times{\widehat{G}}}{\rm Tr}\left(\sigma(x,\pi)\Gamma^{(\widehat{\mathcal{R}})}(x,\pi)\right)d\gamma(x,\pi).

In other words,
\widetilde{\ell}=\ell_{\Gamma^{(\widehat{\mathcal{R}})}d\gamma}
on
(C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
.
Consequently
\Gamma d\gamma\mapsto\ell_{\Gamma d\gamma}\big|_{(C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}}
maps

\mathcal{M}_{ov}(M\times{\widehat{G}})^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
into
((C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}})^{*}
.
This map is continuous and linear. It remains to show that it is injective.

Let
\Gamma d\gamma\in\mathcal{M}_{ov}(M\times{\widehat{G}})^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
such that
\ell_{\Gamma d\gamma}=0
on
(C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
.
The natural extension of
\ell_{\Gamma d\gamma}
to
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
(for which we keep the same notation) will vanish on
L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
.
Note that if
\sigma\in C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
and
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
then
\widehat{E}_{\zeta}\sigma\widehat{E}_{\zeta}\in L^{\infty}(M\times{\widehat{G}})^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}
(see (
3.1
)),
so

\ell(\widehat{E}_{\zeta}\sigma\widehat{E}_{\zeta})=0
.
With a similar computation as above, this shows that
\widehat{E}_{\zeta}\Gamma\widehat{E}_{\zeta}d\gamma=0
.
But this implies
\Gamma^{(\widehat{\mathcal{R}})}d\gamma=0=\Gamma d\gamma
.
By linearity, this shows the injectivity.
∎

Proof of Proposition
7.6
(2).

By Lemma

and its proof, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\{\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}},\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}\}}

\displaystyle=\{\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}},\sigma\in(C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}}))^{\widehat{\mathcal{R}}}\}

Let
\ell:\{\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}},\sigma\in C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})\}\to\mathbb{C}
be a continuous linear functional. It may be identified with a Radon measure
\gamma
on

via

It is naturally extended into the operator-valued measure

with

and
1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}\Gamma d\gamma=0
.
In other words,

\Gamma(\dot{x},\pi)d\gamma(\dot{x},\pi)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{if}\ \pi\in{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}\\

As

are scalar, they commute; this shows that

commutes with

If

.

.
By the Hahn-Banach theorem, the closure of

is equal to
\{\sigma 1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}},\sigma\in C^{*}(M\times{\widehat{G}})\}
.
∎

7.4.
The case of sub-Laplacians

In this section, we consider a stratified Lie group
G
,
and fix a basis
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n_{1}}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
(see Section
3.1
).
We denote by
\mathcal{L}=-X_{1}^{2}-\ldots-X_{n_{1}}^{2}
the associated sub-Laplacian on
G
.
This is a positive Rockland operator,
and we can choose the first stratum
\mathfrak{g}_{1}=\mathbb{R}X_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathbb{R}X_{n_{1}}
as the complement
\mathfrak{v}

of the derived algebra
[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\oplus_{i>1}\mathfrak{g}_{i}
.

In order to give a more concrete description of the objects in the previous sections in this particular case,
we will need the following computations:

Lemma 7.8
.

(1)

Decomposing an element
\omega=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}\omega_{j}X_{j}^{*}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{*}
with respect to the basis dual to
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n_{1}}
, we have

\chi_{\omega}(\exp(\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}X_{j}))=\exp(i\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}x_{j}\omega_{j}).

Equipping
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
with the scalar product that makes
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n_{1}}
orthonormal, the
\mathcal{L}
-eigenvalue corresponding to
\chi_{\omega}
is
|\omega|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}\omega_{j}^{2}
:

\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\chi_{\omega}=|\omega|^{2}\chi_{\omega},\qquad\pi^{\omega}(\mathcal{L})=|\omega|^{2}.

(2)

The operator
\mathscr{E}
defined in Section
5.2
is given here via:

\mathscr{E}=\sum_{[\alpha]=1}\Delta^{\alpha}\widehat{\mathcal{L}}\ X_{\dot{x}}^{\alpha}=-2\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}\widehat{X}_{j}X_{j,M},

where
\widehat{X}_{j}=\{\pi(X_{j}):\pi\in{\widehat{G}}\}
.
In particular,
\mathscr{E}
acts on
M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}
as

\sum_{[\alpha]=1}\Delta^{\alpha}\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(\pi^{\omega})\ X_{\dot{x}}^{\alpha}=-2i\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}\omega_{j}X_{j,M}.

Proof.

Part (1) is straightforward.
For Part (2), we can identify
G
with
\mathbb{R}^{n}
via the exponential map and the choice of basis
X_{j}
, i.e.
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\sim\exp_{G}\sum_{j}x_{j}X_{j}
.
The coordinates
q_{j}
are then
q_{j}(x)=x_{j}
.
We compute easily for
j=1,\ldots,n_{1}
:

X_{j_{1},y=0}(q_{j}(y))=\delta_{j=j_{1}},\qquad\mbox{and when}\ k\neq 1\quad X_{j_{1},y=0}^{k}(q_{j}(y))=0,

so

\displaystyle\Delta_{q_{j}}\widehat{X}_{j_{1}}^{2}(\pi)

\displaystyle=X_{j_{1},y=0}^{2}\left(q_{j}(y)\pi(y)\right)

\displaystyle=\left(X_{j_{1},y=0}^{2}q_{j}(y)\right)\pi(0)+2\left(X_{j_{1},y=0}q_{j}(y)\right)\left(X_{j_{1},y=0}\pi(y)\right)+q_{j}(0)\left(X_{j_{1},y=0}^{2}\pi(y)\right)

\displaystyle=\delta_{j=j_{1}}2\widehat{X}_{j}(\pi),

therefore

\Delta_{q_{j}}\widehat{\mathcal{L}}(\pi)=-\sum_{j_{1}=1}^{n_{1}}\Delta_{q_{j}}\widehat{X}_{j_{1}}^{2}(\pi)=-2\pi(X_{j})

The statement follows. ∎

The advantage of considering sub-Laplacians is that the operation
\mathscr{E}
will lead to invariance under flows of vector fields as in the commutative case:

Example 7.9
.

We consider as in Section
5.3
the case of the torus
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Then, the operator
\mathscr{E}
boils down to the vector field
-2\sum_{j}\partial_{\dot{x}_{j}}\partial_{\xi_{j}}
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proposition
7.1
, Corollary
7.2
and Lemma
7.8
yield in the case of
\mathcal{R}_{M}=\mathcal{L}_{M}
the following properties of localisation and invariance for the semi-classical measures:

Corollary 7.10
.

Let
(\phi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of eigenfunctions for
\mathcal{L}_{M}
with

\mathcal{L}_{M}\phi_{j}=\mu_{j}\phi_{j},\quad\mu_{0}<\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\ldots\leq\mu_{j}\longrightarrow_{j\to\infty}\infty.

Consider a semi-classical measure
\Gamma d\gamma
of
(\phi_{j})
at scale
\mu_{j}^{-1/2}
for the subsequence
(j_{k})
.
We have for
\gamma
-almost all
(\dot{x},\pi)\in M\times{\widehat{G}}

\Gamma(\dot{x},\pi)=\pi(E_{1})\Gamma(\dot{x},\pi)\pi(E_{1}).

The decomposition (
6.5
) of
\Gamma d\gamma
according to
{\widehat{G}}={\widehat{G}}_{1}\sqcup{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}

satisfies the following properties:

(1)

The scalar valued measure

1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}}\gamma
on
M\times{\widehat{G}}_{1}
is supported in
M\times\{\omega\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{*}:|\omega|=1\}

and satisfies

\forall f\in\mathcal{D}(M\times\mathfrak{v}^{*})\qquad\iint_{M\times\mathfrak{g}_{1}}\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}\omega_{j}\ X_{j,M}f(\dot{x},\omega)\ d\gamma(\dot{x},\pi^{\omega})=0.

Consequently, it is invariant under the flow

(\dot{x},\omega)\longmapsto(\exp(s\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}\omega_{j}X_{j,M})\dot{x},\omega).

(2)

For
\gamma
-almost all
(\dot{x},\pi)\in M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}
, the operator

\Gamma(\dot{x},\pi)
maps the finite dimensional 1-eigenspace for
\pi(\mathcal{L})
onto itself and is trivial anywhere else.
Moreover, we have

\iint_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}{\rm Tr}\left(\widehat{E}_{1}\mathscr{E}\widehat{E}_{1}\sigma\ \Gamma\right)d\gamma=\iint_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}{\rm Tr}\left(\mathscr{E}\sigma\ \Gamma\right)d\gamma=0,

for any
\sigma\in\mathcal{A}_{0}^{\widehat{\mathcal{L}}}
,
where

\mathscr{E}=-2\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}\widehat{X}_{j}X_{j,M}
.

7.5.
Comments

7.5.1.
Case of nil-manifolds of Heisenberg types

Let us consider the particular case of Heisenberg nil-manifolds, or more generally of nil-manifolds of Heisenberg types, that is, nil-manifolds
M=\Gamma\backslash G
with
G
a group of Heisenberg type.

(1)

On the groups of Heisenberg type (see e.g.
[
18
, Appendix B]
),
we have

\widehat{E}_{1}\widehat{X}_{j}\widehat{E}_{1}=0
for
j=1,\ldots,n_{1}
.
This implies readily on nil-manifolds of Heisenberg type,

\widehat{E}_{1}\mathscr{E}\widehat{E}_{1}=-2\sum_{j=1}^{n_{1}}\widehat{E}_{1}\widehat{X}_{j}\widehat{E}_{1}\ X_{j,M}=0,

Corollary
7.10
(2) above does not give any information in this case.

(2)

However, we can prove further invariances adapting the semi-classical analysis on groups of Heisenberg type,
especially
[
18
, Lemma 4.1]
.
This has led in
[
20
]
to further scalar invariances than the general ones described in Corollary
7.10
.

In particular, by
[
20
, Theorem 2.4 (ii) (2b)]
,

1_{M\times{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}\Gamma d\gamma

is invariant under
(x,\pi^{\lambda})\mapsto(\exp sZ^{\lambda}x,\pi^{\lambda})
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
, where
\lambda

is a non-zero linear functional on the centre of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
,
\pi^{\lambda}
the corresponding representation via the orbit method, and
Z^{\lambda}
the element of
\mathfrak{g}
(viewed as a left-invariant vector field) corresponding to
\lambda
by duality.
Consequently,
\int_{{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}}\Gamma d\gamma
is a measure on
M
invariant under central translation on
M
.

(3)

In the case of the canonical sub-Laplacian on the Heisenberg nil-manifolds of dimension 3, a study of quantum limits in the traditional Euclidean micro-local sense have been attempted in
[
30
]
, see also
[
9
]
.
Extensions beyond products of Heisenberg nil-manifolds of dimension 3
to slightly less simple case (e.g. nil-manifolds of Heisenberg types) seem unlikely because of the increasing non-commutativity and the related increase in multiplicities of the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}_{M}
.

7.5.2.
More general cases

(1)

The localisation and invariance given for
{\widehat{G}}_{1}
was obtained in
[
20
, Theorem 2.4]
, but only for step-two nil-manifolds. Here, Corollary
7.10
(1) is proved for any canonical sub-Laplacian on a nil-manifold.

(2)

The localisation and invariance given for
{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}
obtained in
[
20
, Theorem 2.4]
for step-two nil-manifolds is more refined than our result in Corollary
7.10
.
Indeed, the analysis in
[
20
]
suggests to decompose further
{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}
in
{\widehat{G}}={\widehat{G}}_{1}\sqcup{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}
with the orbit method.
Indeed, via the orbit method,
{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}
may be written as the disjoint union of closed subsets
\Omega_{d}
comprised of co-adjoint orbits corresponding to a given dimension
d
of the kernel of the associated skewsymmetric bilinear form.
The analysis in
[
20
]
shows that
each of these sets
\Omega_{d}
will yield a different invariance on
M\times\Omega_{d}
.

In the case of a group of Heisenberg type, and more generally a Metivier group,
{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}
identifies with one such set, namely
\Omega_{0}
. However, for other two-step nilpotent Lie groups,
{\widehat{G}}_{\infty}
will decompose into various
\Omega_{d}
.

(3)

It is not difficult to see that in the case when
\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{L}
is a sub-Laplacian,
the operator
\mathscr{E}
is self-adjoint on
L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
.
Hence the one-parameter group
e^{it\mathscr{E}}
is unitary on
L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
.
It is not difficult to prove that it also acts on
\mathcal{A}_{0}
, with furthermore
t\mapsto e^{it\mathscr{E}}
being a continuous map from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathscr{L}(\mathcal{A}_{0})
.

In the commutative case,
M
is a torus and it was easy to determine the kernel of
\mathscr{E}
or equivalently the subspace of
L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
invariant under the action of the one-parameter group
e^{it\mathscr{E}}
, see Lemma
5.9
and its proof: it is the subspace of
\sigma\in L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
such that
\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\sigma(\dot{x},\pi)d\dot{x}=0
for every
\pi\in{\widehat{G}}
.
In particular,
e^{it\mathscr{E}}\sigma\longrightarrow_{t\infty}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{n}}\sigma
.

In the non-commutative case, determining
\ker\mathscr{E}
or equivalently the
e^{it\mathscr{E}}
-invariant subspaces of
L^{2}(M\times{\widehat{G}})
is an open question as already discussed in the case of the nil-manifolds of Heisenberg type in Section
5.3.3
.

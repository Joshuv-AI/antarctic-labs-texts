Primary Decomposition with Differential Operators
Yairon Cid-Ruiz, Bernd Sturmfels

Abstract
. We introduce differential primary decompositions for ideals in a commutative ring. Ideal membership is characterized by differential conditions. The minimal number of conditions needed is the arithmetic multiplicity. Minimal differential primary decompositions are unique up to change of bases. Our results generalize the construction of Noetherian operators for primary ideals in the analytic theory of Ehrenpreis-Palamodov, and they offer a concise method for representing affine schemes. The case of modules is also addressed. We implemented an algorithm in Macaulay2 that computes the minimal decomposition for an ideal in a polynomial ring.

Introduction

Macaulay’s theory of inverse systems
[
13
]
employs differential operators
to characterize membership in an ideal that is primary to the maximal ideal in a power series ring or to the maximal irrelevant ideal in a polynomial ring.
The number of operators needed is the multiplicity of the ideal.
Building on

4
]

this description was extended
to primary ideals in our previous papers
[

]
.
The present article develops a minimal such representation
for arbitrary ideals in a commutative
\mathbb{K}
-algebra that is essentially of finite type over a perfect field
\mathbb{K}
.
We introduce
differential primary decompositions
.
These are differential conditions that characterize ideal membership.

Example 1.1
.

We describe an ideal
I
in
R=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z]
.
A polynomial

lies in

if and only if

(a)

both

and

vanish on the

-axis,

(b)

both

and
y\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}
vanish on the
y
-axis,

(c)

both

and
z\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}
vanish on the
z

(d)

vanishes at the origin
(0,0,0)

More familiar formats would be a list of ideal generators or a minimal primary decomposition:

\begin{matrix}\quad I&=&\langle\,xyz^{2},\,xy^{2}z,\,x^{2}yz,\,y^{2}z^{2},\,2xyz-xz^{2}+yz^{3},2xyz-x^{2}y+x^{3}z,2xyz-y^{2}z+xy^{3}\rangle\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\

&&\,\,\qquad\cap\,\,\langle\,x^{3},\,y^{3},\,z^{3},\,xy^{2},\,yz^{2},\,zx^{2},\,2xyz-x^{2}y,\,2xyz-y^{2}z,\,2xyz-z^{2}x\,\rangle.\end{matrix}

We notice four associated primes:

{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}=\langle y,z\rangle

{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}=\langle x,z\rangle

{\mathfrak{p}}_{3}=\langle x,y\rangle
and
{\mathfrak{p}}_{4}=\langle x,y,z\rangle

In (a)-(d) we characterized membership in

\mathfrak{A}_{1}=\{1,\,x\partial_{y}{+}\partial_{z}\},\,\mathfrak{A}_{2}=\{1,\,y\partial_{z}{+}\partial_{x}\,\},\,\mathfrak{A}_{3}=\{1,\,z\partial_{x}{+}\partial_{y}\},\mathfrak{A}_{4}=\{\partial_{x}\partial_{y}\partial_{z}{+}\partial_{x}^{2}\partial_{y}{+}\partial_{y}^{2}\partial_{z}{+}\partial_{z}^{2}\partial_{x}\}.

The primary ideal

[

We now discuss the issues that are addressed in this paper.
Consider an ideal
I
in an essentially of finite type
\mathbb{K}
-algebra

Suppose that its set of associated primes is

\text{Ass}(R/I)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\}\subset{\text{Spec}}(R)

We wish to characterize ideal membership in

The natural place to look for these is the

{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{K}}(R,R)

\mathbb{K}
-linear differential operators on

.

As in

, we hope to find

\mathfrak{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{A}_{k}\subset{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{K}}(R,R)
such that

(1)

I\,\,=\,\,\big\{f\in R\mid\delta(f)\in{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\text{ for all }\delta\in\mathfrak{A}_{i}\text{ and }i=1,2,\ldots,k\big\}.

Such differential primary decompositions exist for ideals
I

in a polynomial ring
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
An instance with
n=3
,
k=4
,
|\mathfrak{A}_{1}|=|\mathfrak{A}_{2}|=|\mathfrak{A}_{3}|=2
and

.
This existence result is the Fundamental Principle of Palamodov-Ehrenpreis,
which is important in analysis
[

,
10
,
17
,

]
.
The elements

are known as Noetherian operators. They are the

The computation of Noetherian operators was addressed in

[

,
5
,
6
,
8
,
7
,
21
]

For recent work along these lines see
[

]

If each

is a rational maximal ideal then we are dealing with inverse systems
[

[

An algorithm for the case when

[

where

and punctual Hilbert schemes play a major role.

In this article, we present answers to the following two questions:

(a)

What is the analog of the representation
(1)
in
\mathbb{K}
-algebras other than polynomial rings?

(b)

If a representation
(1)
exists for an ideal

To motivate

(1)
fails for
\mathbb{K}
-algebras

that are not regular.
The noncommutative ring

It is usually not Noetherian. A classical example is the cubic cone that was studied by

Problem (b)
is motivated by a foundational question in computational algebraic geometry:

Our answer is drawn from

We define the

If

(\delta,{\mathfrak{p}}_{i})

{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
is a monomial prime and we can

\delta

\prod_{x_{j}\in{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}^{u_{j}}

The set
\mathfrak{A}_{i}
of such operators describes the contribution of the coordinate

(1)

Example 1.2
.

Let
R=\mathbb{Q}[x,y,z]
and

Our contribution is a general theory that resolves both problems

Definition 2.2

Let

be
R
-modules.
The
m
-th order

-linear differential operators
, denoted

\,{\text{Diff}}_{R/A}^{m}(M,N)\subseteq\text{Hom}_{A}(M,N)

form an

-module that is defined inductively by

(i)

{\text{Diff}}_{R/A}^{0}(M,N)\,:=\,\text{Hom}_{R}(M,N)
.

(ii)

{\text{Diff}}_{R/A}^{m}(M,N)\,:=\,\big\{\delta\in\text{Hom}_{A}(M,N)\,\mid\,[\delta,r]\in{\text{Diff}}_{R/A}^{m-1}(M,N)\,\text{ for all }\,r\in R\big\}

The set of all
A
-linear differential operators from

to
N

is the
(R\otimes_{A}R)
-module

{\text{Diff}}_{R/A}(M,N)\,\,:=\,\,\bigcup_{m=0}^{\infty}{\text{Diff}}_{R/A}^{m}(M,N).

Subsets
\mathcal{E}\subseteq{\text{Diff}}_{R/A}(M,N)
are viewed
as differential equations. Their solution spaces are

(4)

{\rm Sol}(\mathcal{E})\,\,:=\,\,\big\{w\in M\,\mid\,\delta(w)=0\text{ for all }\delta\in\mathcal{E}\big\}\,\,=\,\,\bigcap_{\delta\in\mathcal{E}}{\rm Ker}(\delta).

Remark 2.7

Given

Setup 3.1

Let

-algebra essentially of finite type over

Our definition rests on localizing along associated prime ideals

Definition 3.2
.

Let
I\subset R
be an ideal with
\text{Ass}(R/I)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\}\subset{\text{Spec}}(R)
.
A

of

({\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\mathfrak{A}_{1}),\ldots,({\mathfrak{p}}_{k},\mathfrak{A}_{k})

where
\mathfrak{A}_{i}\subset{\text{Diff}}_{R/\mathbb{K}}(R,R/{\mathfrak{p}}_{i})

such that the following equation holds for each

(5)

The next lemma records fundamental properties of differential primary decompositions.

Lemma 3.3
.

For a differential primary decomposition as in

, the following holds:

(i)

The ideal
I
satisfies
(1)
, i.e.

I=\big\{f\in R\mid\delta(f)=0\text{ for all }\,\delta\in\mathfrak{A}_{i}\text{ and }\,1\leq i\leq k\big\}
.

(ii)

For each index

, the set
\,\mathfrak{A}_{i}
of differential operators is non-empty.

Proof.

Fix a minimal primary decomposition
I=Q_{1}\cap\cdots\cap Q_{k}
where

is a
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
-primary ideal.

(i) By applying

, we get

(6)

\bigcap_{i:{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}}Q_{i}\;=\;IR_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap R\quad=\;\bigcap_{i:{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}}\big\{f\in R\mid\delta(f)=0\text{ for all }\delta\in\mathfrak{A}_{i}\big\}.

The equality on the left holds because

Q_{i}
is a
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
-primary ideal (see, e.g.,
[
19
, Theorem 4.1]
).
By intersecting the equation above over all
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Ass}(R/I)
, we obtain the
desired identity
(1)
.

(ii) Suppose that
\mathfrak{A}_{i}=\emptyset
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
, and set
\mathfrak{q}={\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
.
By intersecting
(6)
for all
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Ass}(R/I)
with
{\mathfrak{p}}\subsetneq\mathfrak{q}
, we obtain

\,\bigcap_{j:{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}\subsetneq\mathfrak{q}}Q_{j}\;=\;\bigcap_{j:{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}\subsetneq\mathfrak{q}}\big\{f\in R\mid\delta(f)=0\text{ for all }\delta\in\mathfrak{A}_{j}\big\}

Here each

is strictly contained in

. Since

we conclude

∎

Remark 3.4
.

Differential primary decompositions always exist.
Let
I=Q_{1}\cap\cdots\cap Q_{k}
where each
Q_{i}
is a
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
-primary ideal.
By
[
8
, Theorem A]
, there is a finite set
\mathfrak{B}_{i}^{\prime}\subset{\text{Diff}}_{R_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}/\mathbb{K}}(R_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}},k({\mathfrak{p}}_{i}))
of Noetherian operators such that
Q_{i}R_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}=\{f\in R_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}\mid\delta(f)=0\text{ for all }\delta\in\mathfrak{B}_{i}^{\prime}\}
.
By lifting
\mathfrak{B}_{i}^{\prime}
to a set of operators
\mathfrak{B}_{i}\subset{\text{Diff}}_{R/\mathbb{K}}(R,R/{\mathfrak{p}}_{i})
, it follows that

({\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\mathfrak{B}_{1}),\ldots,({\mathfrak{p}}_{k},\mathfrak{B}_{k})
is a differential primary decomposition of
I
.
Hence the requirement in
Section 3
can always be achieved.

What we are looking for here is more ambitious.
We wish to represent any ideal
I
by a
minimal differential primary decomposition
.
This should reflect the intrinsic complexity of the affine scheme defined by
I
.
This brings us back to the notion from
[
23
]
we saw in the Introduction.

Definition 3.5
.

For an ideal
I\subset R
, its
arithmetic multiplicity
is the positive integer

{\text{amult}}(I)\,\,\,:=\sum_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Ass}(R/I)}\!\!\!\text{length}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}^{0}\left(R_{\mathfrak{p}}/IR_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\right)\quad=\sum_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Ass}(R/I)}\!\!\!\!\text{length}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\frac{\left(IR_{\mathfrak{p}}:_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}{({\mathfrak{p}}R_{\mathfrak{p}})}^{\infty}\right)}{IR_{\mathfrak{p}}}\right).

In our Introduction and in
[
23
]
,
the length inside the sum was denoted
{\rm mult}_{I}({\mathfrak{p}})
and called the
multiplicity of
I
along
{\mathfrak{p}}
. It is the length of the largest ideal of finite length in the ring

R_{{\mathfrak{p}}}/IR_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
.

The next theorem is our main result in this section.
An ideal
I
always has a differential primary decomposition whose total number of
operators is equal to the arithmetic multiplicity.
Moreover,
{\rm amult}(I)
is a lower bound on the size of any
differential primary decomposition.

Theorem 3.6
.

Assume
Section 3
with
\mathbb{K}
perfect.
Fix an ideal
I\subset R
with
\text{Ass}(R/I)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\}\subset{\text{Spec}}(R)
.
The size of a differential primary decomposition is at least
{\rm amult}(I)
, and this
upper bound is tight.
More precisely:

(i)

I
has a differential primary decomposition
({\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\mathfrak{A}_{1})
,
\ldots
,
({\mathfrak{p}}_{k},\mathfrak{A}_{k})
such that

|\mathfrak{A}_{i}|={\rm mult}_{I}({\mathfrak{p}}_{i})
.

(ii)

If
({\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\mathfrak{A}_{1})
,
\ldots
,
({\mathfrak{p}}_{k},\mathfrak{A}_{k})
is a differential primary decomposition for
I
, then

|\mathfrak{A}_{i}|\,\geq\,{\rm mult}_{I}({\mathfrak{p}}_{i})
.

The proof of
Theorem 3.6
appears further below.
We start with a proposition that transfers the approximation technique
used in
[
8
,
7
]

to ideals that are not necessarily primary.

Proposition 3.7

Fix an ideal

Let

(ii)

\,\gamma_{{\mathfrak{p}}}:R_{\mathfrak{p}}\;\rightarrow\;\mathbb{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}R_{\mathfrak{p}}

(iii)

be the
\,(R_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}R_{\mathfrak{p}})
-submodule of
{\text{Diff}}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{K}}^{m-1}\left(R_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathbb{F}\right)

\mathcal{M}
-primary ideal

\,\mathfrak{a}_{m}

(iii).

is recovered as follows:

I_{\mathfrak{p}}\,=\,\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}\text{Sol}(\mathcal{E}_{m}).

Proof.

(i) We have a canonical surjection
{J_{\mathfrak{p}}}\twoheadrightarrow\frac{J_{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathfrak{p}}^{m}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}{I_{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathfrak{p}}^{m}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}
.
From this we obtain the isomorphism

\frac{J_{\mathfrak{p}}}{J_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap(I_{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathfrak{p}}^{m}R_{\mathfrak{p}})}\;\xrightarrow{\cong}\;\frac{J_{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathfrak{p}}^{m}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}{I_{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathfrak{p}}^{m}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}.

We must show that

for
m\gg 0
.
The left hand side contains the right hand side for any

.
On the other hand, since

\,J_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap(I_{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathfrak{p}}^{m}R_{\mathfrak{p}})=I_{\mathfrak{p}}+J_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap{\mathfrak{p}}^{m}R_{\mathfrak{p}}

By localizing we get

.

(ii) From

Hence the result is obtained by combining these isomorphisms with that in part (i).

(iii)
Section 2

give the equality

\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}\!\text{Sol}(\mathcal{E}_{m})=\bigcap_{m=1}^{\infty}\!\left(I_{\mathfrak{p}}+{\mathfrak{p}}^{m}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)

Finally, by Krull’s Intersection Theorem
[

. ∎

Example 4.4
.

Primary decompositions for submodules have been studied in

Working with their differential operators is unfamiliar, and the development of

Let us consider

We verified this example with the homological methods described in

Proposition 4.5
.

Fix an

(iii)

Let

-primary submodule

5.
Polynomial Rings

In this section, we fix a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero and
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
Here,
Section 4
holds. Differential operators
live in the Weyl algebra

{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{K}}(R,R)\,=\,\mathbb{K}\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n}\rangle
.
For a free module
M=R^{p}
we have

{\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{K}}(M,R)={\rm Diff}_{R/\mathbb{K}}(R,R)^{p}=\mathbb{K}\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},\partial_{1},\ldots,\partial_{n}\rangle^{p}
.
In words, a differential operator on
M=R^{p}
is a
p
-tuple of elements in the Weyl algebra.
In what follows we focus on explicit descriptions and computations. We will employ a framework similar to that
in
[
7
]
. One goal is to present
an algorithm which we implemented in
Macaulay2

[
12
]
.

Remark 5.1
.

We begin with a convenient formula for the multiplicity of an associated prime:

\quad{\rm mult}_{\tt I}({\tt P})\,=\,{\tt degree(saturate(I,P)/I)/degree(P)}

This means that the arithmetic multiplicity
{\rm amult}({\tt I})
can be computed
in
Macaulay2
as follows:
sum(apply( ass(I), P -> degree(saturate(I,P)/I)/degree(P) ))

As an illustration, we list the four associated primes in
Section 1
with their multiplicities:

R = QQ[x,y,z]; I = ideal(x^2*y, x^2*z, x*y^2, x*y*z^2);
apply( ass(I), P -> {P,degree(saturate(I,P)/I)/degree(P)})

The output shows that the primes
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},{\mathfrak{p}}_{2},{\mathfrak{p}}_{3},{\mathfrak{p}}_{4}
have multiplicities
1,1,1,2

in this affine scheme.

We now turn to differential primary decompositions.
Let
\mathcal{S}=\{x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{\ell}}\}
be a subset of the variables in
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
Consider the polynomial subring
\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}]:=\mathbb{K}[x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{\ell}}]\subseteq R
and
the field of rational functions
\mathbb{K}(\mathcal{S}):=\mathbb{K}(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{\ell}})
.
As in
[
7
]
, we work with the
relative Weyl algebra
,
which is defined as the ring of
\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}]
-linear differential operators on
R
:

D_{n}(\mathcal{S})\,:=\,{\text{Diff}}_{R/\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}]}(R,R)\,=\,R\big<\partial_{x_{i}}\mid x_{i}\not\in\mathcal{S}\big>\,\subseteq\,R\langle\partial_{x_{1}},\ldots,\partial_{x_{n}}\rangle.

Every differential operator
\delta\in D_{n}(\mathcal{S})
is a unique
\mathbb{K}
-linear combination of standard monomials
\,\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}\partial_{\mathbf{x}}^{\beta}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\left(\prod_{i\not\in\mathcal{S}}\partial_{x_{i}}^{\beta_{i}}\right)
, where
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\beta_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
.
Differential operators
\delta\in D_{n}(\mathcal{S})
act on
polynomials
f\in R
in the familiar way, which is given by

\,x_{i}(f)=x_{i}\cdot f\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\partial_{x_{i}}(f)=\partial f/\partial x_{i}
.

Let
{\mathfrak{p}}\in{\text{Spec}}(R)
be a prime ideal of dimension
\dim(R/{\mathfrak{p}})=d
.
We say that the set of variables
\mathcal{S}
is a
basis
modulo
{\mathfrak{p}}
if

|\mathcal{S}|=d
and
\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}]\cap{\mathfrak{p}}=\{0\}
.
This specifies the bases of the
algebraic matroid
of the prime
{\mathfrak{p}}
.
In the notation of
[
20
, Example 13.2]
, this is the algebraic
matroid associated with the generators
E=\{\overline{x}_{1},\ldots,\overline{x}_{n}\}

of the field extension
K=k({\mathfrak{p}})
over
F=\mathbb{K}
.

We propose the following more refined notion of strong differential primary decomposition.
This following definition for polynomial rings differs from Definitions
3

and
4
in that
every component is now a triplet, with the new entry being a choice of subset
of
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
I
be an ideal in the polynomial ring
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
,
with associated primes
\text{Ass}(R/I)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\}
.
A
differential primary decomposition
of
I
is a list of triplets

({\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathfrak{A}_{1}),\;\;({\mathfrak{p}}_{2},\mathcal{S}_{2},\mathfrak{A}_{2}),\;\;\ldots\,,\;\;({\mathfrak{p}}_{k},\mathcal{S}_{k},\mathfrak{A}_{k}),

where
\mathcal{S}_{i}
is a basis modulo
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
and

\mathfrak{A}_{i}
is a finite set of differential operators in
D_{n}(\mathcal{S}_{i})
such that

\qquad I_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap R\,\,\,=\;\bigcap_{i:{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}}\!\!\big\{f\in R\mid\delta(f)\in{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\text{ for all }\delta\in\mathfrak{A}_{i}\big\}\qquad\text{for each ${\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Ass}(R/I)$.}

This condition implies
(1)
, so we get the desired
test of membership in
I
by differential operators.

What follows is our main result on
differential primary decompositions over polynomial rings.

Theorem 5.3
.

Fix a polynomial ideal
I\subset R
with
\text{Ass}(R/I)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\}\subset{\text{Spec}}(R)
.
The size of a differential primary decomposition satisfies
k\geq{\rm amult}(I)
, and
this is tight. More precisely,

(i)

I
has a differential primary decomposition
\{({\mathfrak{p}}_{i},\mathcal{S}_{i},\mathfrak{A}_{i})\}_{i=1,\ldots,k}
such that

|\mathfrak{A}_{i}|={\rm mult}_{I}({\mathfrak{p}}_{i})
.

(ii)

If
\{({\mathfrak{p}}_{i},\mathcal{S}_{i},\mathfrak{A}_{i})\}_{i=1,\ldots,k}
is any differential primary decomposition for
I
, then

|\mathfrak{A}_{i}|\,\geq\,{\rm mult}_{I}({\mathfrak{p}}_{i})
.

Proof.

As before, we fix a primary decomposition
I=Q_{1}\cap\cdots\cap Q_{k}

where
{\rm rad}(Q_{i})={\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,k
. We assume that
{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}\subsetneq{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
implies
j<i
.
Let
\mathcal{S}_{i}\subset\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
be a basis modulo
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
.

(i) We proceed by induction. By
Section 2
, we may consider differential operators in
{\text{Diff}}_{R/\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}_{i}]}(R,R/{\mathfrak{p}}_{i})
.
Fix
i\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
and assume that the following induction hypotheses hold:

(a)

There exist
\mathfrak{A}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{A}_{i-1}
with
\mathfrak{A}_{j}\subset D_{n}(\mathcal{S}_{j})
and

|\mathfrak{A}_{j}|={\rm mult}_{I}({\mathfrak{p}}_{i})

for all
1\leq j\leq i-1
.

(b)

The identity
(7)
holds for
1\leq j\leq i-1
,
with
\mathfrak{A}_{\ell}
identified with its image in
{\text{Diff}}_{R/\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}_{i}]}(R,R/{\mathfrak{p}}_{\ell})
.

Set
{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
,
\mathbb{F}=k({\mathfrak{p}})
and
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{i}
. We invoke the same steps as in the proof of
Theorem 3.6
(i) and arrive again at the conclusions of
(10)
.
We now work over the field
\mathbb{K}(\mathcal{S})
instead of
\mathbb{K}
, and we construct a subset

\mathfrak{B}=\{\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{s}\}\subset{\text{Diff}}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{K}(\mathcal{S})}(R_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathbb{F})
with
s={\rm mult}_{I}({\mathfrak{p}})
such that

I_{\mathfrak{p}}\,\,\,=\bigcap_{1\leq\ell\leq i-1\atop{\mathfrak{p}}_{\ell}\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}}\!\big\{f\in R_{\mathfrak{p}}\mid\mathfrak{L}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\delta)(f)=0\text{ for all }\delta\in\mathfrak{A}_{\ell}\big\}\,\,\bigcap\,\,\text{Sol}(\mathfrak{B}).

Since
\mathcal{S}
is a basis modulo
{\mathfrak{p}}
, we have

R/{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}]}\mathbb{K}(\mathcal{S})=\mathbb{F}
, and we obtain canonical isomorphisms

(18)

{\text{Diff}}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathbb{K}(\mathcal{S})}(R_{\mathfrak{p}},\mathbb{F})\cong{\text{Diff}}_{\left(R\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}]}\mathbb{K}(\mathcal{S})\right)/\mathbb{K}(\mathcal{S})}(R\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}]}\mathbb{K}(\mathcal{S}),\mathbb{F})\cong\mathbb{K}(\mathcal{S})\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}]}{\text{Diff}}_{R/\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}]}(R,R/{\mathfrak{p}}).

See
[
8
, Lemma 2.7 (iii)]
for the isomorphism on the left.
For each
1\leq h\leq s
, we now write
\xi_{h}
as
\frac{\delta_{h}}{r_{h}}
,
where
\delta_{h}\in{\text{Diff}}_{R/\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}]}(R,R/{\mathfrak{p}})
and
r_{h}\in\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}]\backslash\{0\}
.
After lifting
\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{s}\}
into
\mathfrak{A}_{i}\subset D_{n}(\mathcal{S}_{i})
, we obtain
the desired decomposition for
I_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

(ii) Suppose that
({\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathfrak{A}_{1})
,
\ldots
,
({\mathfrak{p}}_{k},\mathcal{S}_{k},\mathfrak{A}_{k})
is a differential primary decomposition for
I
.
Fix
{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
with
1\leq i\leq k
, and set
\mathbb{F}=k({\mathfrak{p}})
,
\mathfrak{A}=\mathfrak{A}_{i}
,
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}_{i}
,
W=\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}]\setminus\{0\}
,
S=W^{-1}R
,
\mathcal{I}=I_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap R
and
J=(\mathcal{I}:_{R}{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}^{\infty})
.
From
Section 5
and by analogy with
(12)
, we obtain

\mathcal{I}\,\,=\,\,\big\{f\in J\mid\widetilde{\delta}(f)=0\text{ for all }\widetilde{\delta}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}\big\}.

Here
\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}
denotes the image of
\mathfrak{A}
under the canonical map

D_{n}(\mathcal{S})={\text{Diff}}_{R/\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}]}(R,R)\rightarrow{\text{Diff}}_{R/\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}]}(J,R/{\mathfrak{p}}),\;\;\delta\mapsto\pi\circ\delta\circ\iota

determined by
\iota:J\hookrightarrow R
and
\pi:R\twoheadrightarrow R/{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Since the operators in

\mathfrak{A}\subset D_{n}(\mathcal{S})
are
\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}]
-linear,

\mathcal{I}S\;=\;\big\{f\in JS\mid\mathfrak{L}_{W}(\widetilde{\delta})(f)=0\text{ for all }\widetilde{\delta}\in\widetilde{\mathfrak{A}}\big\}.

Using the isomorphism in
(18)
, we can proceed as in

Theorem 3.6
(ii) to infer
|\mathfrak{A}|\geq{\rm mult}_{I}({\mathfrak{p}})
.
∎

Finally, we present our algorithm for computing a minimal differential primary decomposition.
The algorithm is correct because it
realizes the steps in the proof of
Theorem 5.3
(i).
We use the representation in
[
7
, Theorem 2.1]
and the method for Noetherian operators in
[
7
, Algorithm 8.1]
.

Algorithm 5.4

(Differential primary decomposition for an ideal in a polynomial ring)
.

Input:
An ideal
I
in
R=\mathbb{K}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
{\rm char}(\mathbb{K})=0
.

Output:
A differential primary decomposition for
I
of minimal size
{\rm amult}(I)
.

(1)

Compute the set of associated prime ideals,
\text{Ass}(R/I)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\}
.

(2)

For
i
from
1
to
k
do:

(2.1)

Compute a basis
\mathcal{S}_{i}
modulo
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
, and let
\mathbb{F}_{i}=k({\mathfrak{p}}_{i})
be the residue field of
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
.

(2.2)

Compute the ideal
\mathcal{I}=I_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}\cap R
– this is the intersection of
all primary components of
I
whose radical is contained in
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
.

(2.3)

Compute the ideal
J=\mathcal{I}:_{R}{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}^{\infty}
– this is the intersection of
all primary components of
I
whose radical is strictly contained in
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
.

(2.4)

Find
m>0
giving the isomorphism in
Section 3
(i):

J/\mathcal{I}\xrightarrow{\cong}(J+{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{m})/(\mathcal{I}+{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{m})
.

(2.5)

By using
[
7
, Theorem 2.1, Algorithm 8.1]
, compute the
\mathbb{F}_{i}
-vector subspaces
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{H}
of the Weyl-Noether module

\mathbb{F}_{i}\otimes_{R}D_{n}(\mathcal{S}_{i})\,\cong\,\mathbb{F}_{i}\big\langle\partial_{x_{j}}\mid x_{j}\not\in\mathcal{S}_{i}\big\rangle
.
These are
(R\otimes_{\mathbb{K}[\mathcal{S}_{i}]}R)
-modules that correspond to the
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
-primary ideals
\mathcal{I}+{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}^{m}
and
J+{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}^{m}
respectively.

(2.6)

Compute an
\mathbb{F}_{i}
-vector subspace
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{E}
complementary to
\mathcal{H}
in
\mathcal{E}
, i.e., we have the direct sum
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{G}
.
Compute an
\mathbb{F}_{i}
-basis
\overline{\mathfrak{A}_{i}}
of
\mathcal{G}
.

(2.7)

Lift the basis
\overline{\mathfrak{A}_{i}}
to a subset
\mathfrak{A}_{i}\subset D_{n}(\mathcal{S}_{i})
in the corresponding relative Weyl algebra.

(3)

Output the triples
({\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathfrak{A}_{1})
,
\ldots
,
({\mathfrak{p}}_{k},\mathcal{S}_{k},\mathfrak{A}_{k})
.

We implemented this in
Macaulay2

[
12
]
.
Our code is made available at
mathrepo.mis.mpg.de
.
This augments the package for primary ideals that is described in
[
5
]
,
and which rests on
[
6
,

]
.

The two commands in our implementation are called

solvePDE
and
getPDE
. The command
solvePDE

takes as its input an ideal
I
in a polynomial ring and it
creates a list of pairs
({\mathfrak{p}}_{i},\mathfrak{A}_{i})
such that
(1)
holds and

|\mathfrak{A}_{i}|={\rm mult}_{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}(I)
for all
i
. The command
getPDE
reverses that process.
It starts from a list of Noetherian operators and computes ideal generators.
That reverse process does not check whether the given differential operators
satisfy the conditions stipulated in
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
.
Thus, running
getPDE
after
solvePDE
always returns the
ideal one starts with, but running
solvePDE
after
getPDE
might
lead to larger sets
\mathfrak{A}_{i}
than those one starts with.

Example 5.5
.

We run our two
Macaulay2
commands on the ideal given in
Section 1
:

load "noetherianOperatorsCode.m2"
R = QQ[x,y,z]; I = ideal(x^2*y,x^2*z,x*y^2,x*y*z^2)
solvePDE(I)
getPDE(oo)

The output of the command
solvePDE
(I) is the list of four pairs
({\mathfrak{p}}_{i},\mathfrak{A}_{i})

that realizes
(1)
:

{{ideal x,{1}}, {ideal(y,z),{1}}, {ideal(x,y),{dx}}, {ideal(x,y,z),{dx*dy,dx*dy*dz}}}

Our choice of the name
solvePDE
is a reference to
the dual interpretation of the ideal
I
, namely as
a system of linear partial differential equations with
constant coefficients. The Noetherian operators in

\mathfrak{A}_{i}
can be interpreted as polynomials
in
2n
variables, called
Noetherian multipliers
,
as in
[
7
, eqn (20)]
.
With this reinterpretation, our theory describes a
minimal integral representation for
all solutions to the given PDE. The command
solvePDE

computes all solutions to the PDE in the sense of the
Ehrenpreis-Palamodov Fundamental Principle

[
7
, Theorem 3.3]
.
We illustrate this for the binomial ideal in
[
9
, Example 5.1]
,
which served in a statistics application.

Example 5.6
.

Consider the following system of linear PDE for an unknown function
f:\mathbb{R}^{4}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
:

(19)

\frac{\partial^{5}f}{\partial x_{1}^{3}\partial x_{3}^{2}}=\frac{\partial^{5}f}{\partial x_{2}^{5}}\,,\,\,\,\frac{\partial^{5}f}{\partial x_{2}^{2}\partial x_{4}^{3}}=\frac{\partial^{5}f}{\partial x_{3}^{5}}\,,\,\,\,\frac{\partial^{7}f}{\partial x_{1}^{5}\partial x_{4}^{2}}=\frac{\partial^{7}f}{\partial x_{2}^{7}}\,,\,\,\,\frac{\partial^{7}f}{\partial x_{1}^{2}\partial x_{4}^{5}}=\frac{\partial^{7}f}{\partial x_{3}^{7}}.

We wish to describe all sufficiently differentiable functions
f
that satisfy these four PDE.
The system
(19)
corresponds to an ideal
I
in
R=\mathbb{Q}[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}]
.
We enter this into
Macaulay2
:

R = QQ[x1,x2,x3,x4];
I = ideal( x1^3*x3^2-x2^5, x2^2*x4^3-x3^5, x1^5*x4^2-x2^7, x1^2*x4^5-x3^7 );

This has four associated primes. The one minimal prime is the toric ideal

{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}={\tt I}:\langle x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}\rangle^{\infty}
. The three embedded primes are

{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}=\langle x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\rangle
,

{\mathfrak{p}}_{3}=\langle x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\rangle
,

{\mathfrak{p}}_{4}=\langle x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4}\rangle
.
Using
primaryDecomposition(I)
, we obtain a primary decomposition,
where the primary components have multiplicities

1,67,60,916
. The methods in
[
5
,
6
,
7
]

would compute
1044
Noetherian operators to describe
I
.
However,
Section 5
reveals that

{\rm amult}({\tt I})=1+18+18+170
suffice.
Our command
solvePDE(I)
computes
a minimal list of
170
Noetherian operators in under ten minutes.
For instance, the last of the
18
Noetherian operators
\delta
displayed for the prime
{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}
is

x4^5*dx1*dx2*dx3^8 + 1120*x4^2*dx1*dx2^3*dx3^3 + 6720*dx1^3*dx2*dx3

This translates into the following integral representation for certain special solutions to
(19)
:

f(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4})\,\,=\,\,\int\bigl(x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}^{8}\,t^{5}+1120x_{1}x_{2}^{3}x_{3}^{3}\,t^{2}+6720x_{1}^{3}x_{2}x_{3}\bigr)d\mu(t).

Here the notation is as in
[
7
, Theorem 3.3]
.
Our
Macaulay2
output furnishes
170
such formulas.
The (differential) primary decomposition
gives insight into connectivity of random walks in
[

]

The command
solvePDE

Abstract
In 1979 1979 , using the theory of ultrafilters, N. Hindman proved that for every finite coloring of ℕ \mathbb{N} , there exists a color that contains both additive and multiplicative I ​ P IP sets. Later, in 1993 1993 , V. Bergelson and N. Hindman found an elementary proof. In this article, we prove that the partial product of these two I ​ P IP sets also lies in the same color. As an immediate consequence, we have the equation a + b = c ⋅ d a+b=c\cdot d partition regular. This was a conjecture of P. Csikvári, K. Gyarmati, and A. Sárközy, which was solved by V. Bergelson and N. Hindman independently. In this article, we separately give a very short proof of this conjecture. Then we extend our techniques to deduce systems of nonlinear equations that are partition regular. We prove that a relatively weak version of the nonlinear Rado systems is partition regular.

1   Introduction

Arithmetic Ramsey theory deals with the monochromatic patterns found in any given finite coloring of the
integers or of the natural numbers
\mathbb{N}
. Here “coloring” means disjoint partition and a set is called “monochromatic” if it is included in one piece of the partition. A collection
\mathcal{F}
of subsets of
\mathbb{N}
is called partition regular if, for every finite coloring of
\mathbb{N}
, there exists a monochromatic element
F\in\mathcal{F}
. Similarly an equation
F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=0
over
\mathbb{N}
is called partition regular if, for every finite partition of
\mathbb{N}
, there exists a monochromatic solution of the equation
F.
Classification of partition regular equations in Ramsey theory is a long-standing and one of the most challenging open problem in Ramsey theory.

One of the earliest results in Ramsey theory was due to I. Schur
[
25
]
, where he proved the pattern
\{a,b,a+b\}
is partition regular. Passing to an exponent one can immediately show that
\{c,d,c\cdot d\}
is partition regular. Using a little bit of ultrafilter theory even one can show that the pattern
\{a,b,a+b,c,d,c\cdot d\}
is partition regular. Then an immediate question appears whether
a+b=c\cdot d
is partition regular or not. Inspired by some previous works of Gyarmati, and Sárközy
[
15
,
23
]
, in
[
8
]
, P. Csikvári, K. Gyarmati, and A. Sárközy conjectured that the equation
a+b=c\cdot d
is partition regular. In
[
5
,
19
]
, V. Bergelson and N. Hindman independently answered this conjecture. They used the Algebra in the Stone-Čech compactification of discrete semigroups to prove their result. This article first gives a very short proof of this result. Then extending this result, we find several nonlinear systems of equations that are partition regular.

The notions of
IP
set is the fundamental building block of today’s Ramsey theory.
IP
sets can be thought of an infinitary extensions of Schur patterns. This was a long-standing open problem if these patterns are partition regular which was settled by N. Hindman in
1974.

Definition 1.1

(
IP Set
)
.

1.

For any set
X
, let
\mathcal{P}_{f}\left(X\right)
be the collection of all nonempty finite subsets of
X.

2.

Let
(S,+)
be a commutative semigroup. A set
A\subseteq S
is said to be an
IP
set if there exists an injective sequence
\langle x_{n}\rangle_{n}
in
S
such that

A=\operatorname{FS}\left(\langle x_{n}\rangle_{n}\right)=\left\{\sum_{n\in\alpha}x_{n}:\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{f}\left(\mathbb{N}\right)\right\}.

Abbreviately for
\alpha\in\mathcal{P}_{f}\left(\mathbb{N}\right),
we write
x_{\alpha}=\sum_{n\in\alpha}x_{n}
.

The following theorem is a cornerstone theorem in Ramsey theory, known
as the Hindman theorem
[
16
]
. For a short proof, see
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(
Hindman Theorem
)
.

If
(S,+)
is a commutative semigroup, then for every finite coloring of
S
, there exists a monochromatic IP set.

Algebra of the Stone-Čech compactification of discrete semigroups

Ultrafilters are set-theoretic objects which are intimately related to the Ramsey theory. In this section, we give a brief introduction to this theory. For details, we refer to the book
[
21
]
to the readers.

•

A
filter

\mathcal{F}
over any nonempty set
X
is a collection of subsets of
X
such that

1.

\emptyset\notin\mathcal{F}
, and
X\in\mathcal{F}
,

2.

A\in\mathcal{F}
, and
A\subseteq B
implies
B\in\mathcal{F},

3.

A,B\in\mathcal{F}
implies
A\cap B\in\mathcal{F}.

Using Zorn’s lemma we can guarantee the existence of maximal filters which are called ultrafilters. Any ultrafilter
p
has the following property:

•

if
X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}
is any finite partition of
X
, then there exists
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
such that
A_{i}\in p.

Due to this finite decomposability nature, to prove the partition regularity of an equation, it is sufficient to prove the existence of ultrafilters witnessing the solutions of equations.

Space of ultrafilters

Let
(S,\cdot)
be a discrete semigroup. Denote by
\beta S
the collection of all ultrafilters over
S.
For any
(\emptyset\neq)A\subseteq S,
let
\overline{A}=\{p\in\beta S:A\in p\}.
The collection of sets
\{\overline{A}:(\emptyset\neq)A\subseteq S\}
forms a basis for a topology. One can show with this topology
\beta S
is the Stone-Čech compactification
of
S.
For any
A\subseteq S,
and
x\in S
denote
x^{-1}A=\{y:x\cdot y\in A\}.
Now one can naturally extend the operation ‘
\cdot
’ over the set
\beta S:
for any
p,q\in\beta S
, let us define
p\cdot q
by

A\in p\cdot q\iff\{x:x^{-1}A\in q\}\in p.

One can show with this definition
(\beta S,\cdot)
is a semigroup. In fact one can prove that

(\beta S,\cdot)
is a compact right topological semigroup. Here right topological means right action is continuous: for each
q\in\beta S,
the map
\rho_{q}:\beta S\rightarrow\beta S
defined by
\rho_{q}(p)=p\cdot q
is continuous. However, the left action is not always continuous but: for
x\in S,
the map
\lambda_{x}:\beta S\rightarrow\beta S
defined by
\lambda_{x}(p)=x\cdot p
is continuous.
Now from Ellis
[
10
]
Theorem, we can say that any closed subsemigroup of
(\beta S,\cdot)
contains idempotents. However, with some little modification, we can define the notions of
IP
sets over any arbitrary semigroup, but we are not going for that. One can see that

•

If
(S,\cdot)
is a semigroup, then a set
A\subseteq S
contains an
IP
set if and only if there exists an element
p\in E(\beta S,\cdot)
such that
A\in p.

In our first proof, we use the finitary version of Hindman’s theorem, known as
IP_{r}
sets.

Definition 1.3

(
IP_{r}
set
)
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
, and
(S,+)
be a commutative semigroup.

1.

A set
A\subseteq S
is said to be an
IP_{r}
set if there exists a sequence
\langle x_{n}\rangle_{n=1}^{r}
in
S
such that

A=\operatorname{FS}\left(\langle x_{n}\rangle_{n=1}^{r}\right)=\left\{\sum_{n\in\alpha}x_{n}:\alpha(\neq\emptyset)\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,r\}\right\}.

2.

Define
\mathcal{IP}=\{p\in\beta\mathbb{N}:\forall A\in p,A\text{ contains an additive $IP$ set }\}.

3.

Define
\mathcal{IP}_{0}=\{p\in\beta\mathbb{N}:\forall A\in p,\text{ and }r\in\mathbb{N},A\text{ contains an additive $IP_{r}$ set }\}.

It is a routine exercise to check that
\overline{\mathcal{IP}}
, and
\overline{\mathcal{IP}_{0}}
both are left ideals of
(\beta\mathbb{N},\cdot).
Infact
\overline{\mathcal{IP}_{0}}
is a two sided ideal of
(\beta\mathbb{N},\cdot)
and hence
\overline{\mathcal{IP}_{0}}\subseteq K(\beta\mathbb{N},\cdot)
. Hence from the Zorns lemma we have
\overline{\mathcal{IP}}\cap E\left(\beta\mathbb{N},\cdot\right)\neq\emptyset
, and
\overline{\mathcal{IP}_{0}}\cap E\left(\beta\mathbb{N},\cdot\right)\neq\emptyset
(note that
\overline{\mathcal{IP}_{0}}\subseteq E\left(K(\beta\mathbb{N},\cdot)\right)
).

Notions of large sets

In the study of ultrafilter theory, one can show some members of
\beta S
are combinatorial rich. In fact one can characterize combinatorially the members of such ultrafilters. During our study we will apply both combinatorial and algebraic characterizations of those ultrafilters. Here we summarize some of the notions of large sets intimately related to our study.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
(S,\cdot)
be a semigroup, let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
A\subseteq S
. We say that

1.

for any subset
T\subseteq(\beta S,\cdot)
, we let
E(T)
be the collection of all idempotents of
T;

2.

A
is a
thick set
if for any finite subset
F\subset S
, there exists an element
x\in S
such that
Fx=\{fx:f\in F\}\subset A
;

3.

A
is a
syndetic set
if there exists a finite set
F\subset S
such that
S=\bigcup_{x\in F}x^{-1}A
, where
x^{-1}A=\{y:xy\in A\}
;

4.

A
is
piecewise syndetic set
if there exists a finite set
F\subset S
such that
\bigcup_{x\in F}x^{-1}A
is a thick set. It is well known that
A
is piecewise syndetic if and only if there exists
p\in K(\beta S,\cdot)
such that
A\in p.

A set
L\subseteq\beta S
is said to be a left ideal if
\beta S\cdot L=\{q\cdot p:p\in L,q\in\beta S\}\subseteq L.
Similarly
R\subseteq\beta S
is said to a left ideal if
R\cdot\beta S=\{p\cdot q:p\in R,q\in\beta S\}\subseteq R.
Again using Zorn’s lemma one can show that minimal left/ right ideals (w.r.t. inclusion) exist. It can be proved that minimal left ideals are closed.
One can show that if
A\subseteq S
is a piecewise syndetic set, then there exists a minimal left ideal
L
of
(\beta S,\cdot)
, and
p\in L
such that
A\in p.
Denote by
K(\beta S,\cdot)
, the union of all minimal left ideals of
(\beta S,\cdot)
. It can be proved that
K(\beta S,\cdot)
is also the union of all minimal right ideals of
(\beta S,\cdot)
. That is

\displaystyle K(\beta S)

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{L:L\text{ is a minimal left ideal of }\beta S\}

\displaystyle=\bigcup\{R:R\text{ is a minimal right ideal of }\beta S\}.

As each minimal left ideal is the image of
\beta S
right action map, it is closed, and hence it contains idempotents. Hence,
E\left(K(\beta S,\cdot)\right)\neq\emptyset
.

Central sets play a vital role in Ramsey theory.
The notion of central sets was first introduced by H. Furstenberg
[
14
]
, using the methods of topological dynamics. Later in
[
4
]
, V. Bergelson and N. Hindman first proved an equivalent version of central sets in
\mathbb{N}
, using ultrafilter theory, and then in
[
24
]
, H. Shi and H. Yang extended this result for arbitrary semigroups. For a detailed introduction to Central sets we refer to the article
[
20
]
.
Here we recall the algebraic definition.

Definition 1.5

(
Central set
)
.

A set
A\subseteq S
is said to be central if there exists
p\in E\left(K(\beta S,\cdot)\right)
such that
A\in p.

Thick sets and syndetic sets can also be characterized in terms of ultrafilters.
It can be proved that a set
A\subseteq S
is thick if and only if there exists a left ideal
L
such that
L\subseteq\overline{A}
. And a set
A\subseteq S
is syndetic if and only if for every left ideal
L
,
L\cap\overline{A}\neq\emptyset.

Throughout our article, we follow the following convention:

•

A set
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is additively (resp. multiplicatively) “large” if this set is large in
(\mathbb{N},+)
(resp. in
(\mathbb{N},\cdot)
).

The following theorem is a more general version of Csikvári, Gyarmati, and Sárközy’s conjecture, proved in
[
19
]
.

Theorem 1.6

(
Sum equal product theorem,

[
19
]
)
.

For every
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, the equation
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}=y_{1}\cdots y_{m}
is partition regular.

Our first task is to give a very simple proof of this theorem. Then we improve our arguments to extend the main result of
[
17
,
6
]
by proving an additive and a multiplicative
IP
set, and their partial products lie in the same color. This result can be realized as an infinitary extension of Theorem
1.6
. We prove the following.

Theorem 1.7
.

For any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\mathbb{N}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}
, there exist two sequences
\langle a_{n}\rangle_{n}
and
\langle b_{n}\rangle_{n}
, and
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
such that

1.

FS\left(\langle a_{n}\rangle_{n}\right)\bigcup FP\left(\langle b_{n}\rangle_{n}\right)\subset A_{i},
and

2.

for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\bigcup_{m=1}^{N}FS\left(\langle a_{n}\rangle_{n=1}^{m}\right)\cdot FP\left(\langle b_{n}\rangle_{n=m}^{N}\right)\subset A_{i}.

•

As
(a_{1}+a_{2})\cdot b_{2}=a_{1}\cdot b_{2}+a_{2}\cdot b_{2},
from the above theorem we have
a+b=c\cdot d
is partition regular.

•

Again note that

(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})\cdot b_{n+1}b_{n+2}\cdots b_{n+(m-1)}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}b_{n+1}b_{n+2}\cdots b_{n+(m-1)}.

Hence from Theorem
1.7
, we conclude Theorem
1.6
.

Polynomial extensions of classical Ramsey theorems are difficult to prove. One of the classical results in Ramsey theory is due to van der Waerden
[
26
]
, where he proved that for every finite coloring of
\mathbb{N}
, there exist arithmetic progressions of arbitrary length.
One of the first advances in the direction of polynomialization was made by V. Bergelson and A. Liebman in
[
7
]
, where they proved the nonlinear version of van der Waerden’s theorem, known as the polynomial van der Waerden’s theorem. In
[
18
]
, N. Hindman found an algebraic proof. Let
\mathbb{P}
be the set of all polynomials from
\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathbb{Z}
having no constant term, that is for every
P\in\mathbb{P}
,
P(0)=0.

Theorem 1.8

(
Polynomial van der Waerden theorem,

[
18
]
)
.

Let
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
be any piecewise syndetic set, then for every finite
F\subset\mathbb{P}
, and
p\in E(\beta\mathbb{N},+)
,

\{d:\text{ there exists }a\in\mathbb{N}\text{ such that }\{a,a+P(d):P\in F\}\subset A\}\in p.

In other words, if
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is any central set, then there exists
a,d
such that
\{a,d,a+P(d):P\in F\}\subset A\}.

Nonlinear Rado system

In
1933,
R. Rado classified those finite matrices
A
which have monochromatic solution of the equation
A\overrightarrow{X}=\overrightarrow{0}.
This property is known as kernel partition regularity. He found a condition over the matrices known as the column condition such that if any matrix satisfies this condition then the matrix is kernel partition regular. The van der Waerden theorem and Schur theorem can be derived from Rado’s result. For detailed literature, we refer to the book
[
21
]
.

Definition 1.9

(
Column condition
)
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
A
be a matrix with integer coefficients. Let
(C_{1},\cdots,C_{n})
be the set of all columns of
A.
The matrix
A
is said to satisfy the column condition if there is a partition of
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
as
I_{1}\cup\ldots\cup I_{v}
such that

•

\sum_{j\in I_{1}}C_{j}=\overrightarrow{0}
; and

•

for each
t>1,

\sum_{j\in I_{t}}C_{j}
can be expressed as the rational combination of
\{C_{j}:j\in I_{1}\cup\ldots\cup I_{t-1}\}.

In attempting to prove the polynomial extension of Rado’s theorem, in
[
9
]
, authors introduced a new class of polynomials:
Rado polynomials
.

Definition 1.10

(
Rado polynomial
)
.

A polynomial with integer coefficients is called a
Rado polynomial
if it can be written in the form

c_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}x_{n}+P(y_{1},\ldots,y_{k})

where
n\geq 2,

P
has no constant term, and there exists a nonempty subset
J\subseteq\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
such that
\sum_{j\in J}c_{j}=0.

In
[
9
, Theorem 2.9.]
, authors proved the Rado polynomials are partition regular. This extends Rado theorem for single equations. Later in
[
1
]
, authors studied the partition regularity of a single equation. In the second part of the article, we extend the partition regularity of Rado polynomials to the partition regularity of the system of Rado polynomials.

Definition 1.11

(
Nonlinear Rado system
)
.

Let
A
be a
m\times n(n\geq 2)
matrix with integer coefficients satisfying the column condition, and
P_{1},\cdots,P_{m}
be the collection of polynomials from
\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathbb{Z}
having no constant term. We define the following system of equations

\displaystyle a_{1,1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{1,n-1}x_{n-1}+a_{1,n}\cdot x_{n}+P_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle a_{m,1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{m,n-1}x_{n-1}+a_{m,n}\cdot x_{n}+P_{m}(z)

\displaystyle=0

is
nonlinear Rado system
.

We don’t know whether the nonlinear Rado system is partition regular, but we prove a little weak version is partition regular.
Where we can not guarantee the existence of a single
x_{n},
but we prove the existence of different
y_{1},\cdots,y_{n}
such that if for each
i=1,\ldots,n,
if we replace
x_{n}^{\prime}s
by
y_{i}^{\prime}s
then this system is partition regular.

Definition 1.12

(
Weak nonlinear Rado system
)
.

Let
A
be a
m\times n(n\geq 2)
matrix with integer coefficients satisfying the column condition, and
P_{1},\cdots,P_{m}
be the collection of polynomials from
\mathbb{Z}
to
\mathbb{Z}
having no constant term. We define the following system of equations

\displaystyle a_{1,1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{1,n-1}x_{n-1}+a_{1,n}\cdot y_{1}+P_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle a_{m,1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{m,n-1}x_{n-1}+a_{m,n}\cdot y_{m}+P_{m}(z)

\displaystyle=0

is
Weak nonlinear Rado system
.

In Theorem
3.5
we prove weak nonlinear Rado systems are partition regular.

2   Sum equal product theorems

The primary objective of this section is to prove Theorem
1.6
in a much simpler way, and then to find several nonlinear extensions of this Theorem. In our proofs, using variable rearrangements arguments we explore the beauty of the intersection property of ultrafilters. First let us start with the proof of Theorem
1.6
.
We first prove Theorem
1.6
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

For
m=1,
this follows from the Hindman theorem
1.2
. So we assume
m\geq 2.

Let
p\in\overline{\mathcal{IP}_{0}}\cap E\left(\beta\mathbb{N},\cdot\right),
and
A\in p=p\cdot p.
Hence we have
A^{\star}=\{x\in A:x^{-1}A\in p\}\in p.
Then by
[
21
, Lemma 4.14]
,
x\in A^{\star}
implies
x^{-1}A^{\star}\in p.
Now choose a sequence
\langle a_{i}\rangle_{i=1}^{n}
such that
FS\left(\langle a_{i}\rangle_{i=1}^{n}\right)\subset A^{\star}.
As each member of
p
contains multiplicatively
IP
sets, we can choose a sequence
\langle b_{i}\rangle_{i=1}^{m-1}
such that

FP\left(\langle b_{i}\rangle_{i=1}^{m-1}\right)\subset A^{\star}\cap\bigcap_{y\in FS\left(\langle a_{i}\rangle_{i=1}^{n}\right)}y^{-1}A^{\star}\in p.

Let us define

1.

for every
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,m-1\},
let
y_{i}=b_{i}\in A^{\star},

2.

y_{m}=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}\in A^{\star},
and

3.

for every
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\},
let
x_{i}=a_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}y_{j}\in A^{\star}.

Then we have

\prod_{j=1}^{m}y_{j}=(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}y_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}.

This completes the proof.

∎

Note in the above proof, first we can choose
a_{1}<\cdots<a_{n}
, then we can choose
b_{1}
arbitrarily large so that
b_{1}<\cdots<b_{m-1}.
Hence the full injectivity of the solution in the above Theorem is guaranteed.

The following theorem is another version of Theorem
1.6
where we deal with geometric progressions instead of multiplicative
IP_{n}
sets. Here we prove the partition regularity of certain systems of nonlinear equations.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
m,n\in\mathbb{N},
then the following system of equations is partition regular with full injectivity.

\displaystyle x_{1,1}+\cdots+x_{1,n}

\displaystyle=yz

\displaystyle x_{m,1}+\cdots+x_{m,n}

\displaystyle=yz^{n}.

Proof.

Let
p\in E\left(K\left(\beta\mathbb{N},\cdot\right)\right),
and
A\in p=p\cdot p.
Then as in the above proof,
A^{\star}=\{x\in A:x^{-1}A\in p\}\in p.
Now choose a sequence
\langle a_{i}\rangle_{i=1}^{n}
such that
FS\left(\langle a_{i}\rangle_{i=1}^{n}\right)\subset A^{\star}.
Hence
B=A^{\star}\cap\bigcap_{y\in FS\left(\langle a_{i}\rangle_{i=1}^{n}\right)}y^{-1}A^{\star}\in p.
Then
B
is a multiplicatively central set. Hence from
[
3
, Corollary 2.7]
, there exists
a,d\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\{a,d,ad,\ldots,ad^{m}\}\subset B.

Define

1.

for each
1\leq i\leq m,
and
1\leq j\leq n,

x_{i,j}=a_{j}\cdot ad^{i};

2.

y=(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})\cdot a;
and

3.

d=z.

Then for each
1\leq i\leq m,
we have

(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n})\cdot ad^{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{i,j}=yz^{i}.

Note in the proof we can choose arbitrarily large
a,d
so that for any given (already chosen)
a_{j_{1}},a_{j_{2}},
and
i_{1}\neq i_{2},
we have
a_{j_{1}}\cdot ad^{i_{1}}\neq a_{j_{2}}\cdot ad^{i_{2}}.
In other words each
x_{i,j}
is different. Similarly
y,z
are different from these
x_{i,j}^{\prime}s
. This completes the proof.

This completes the proof.

∎

Now we adapt the arguments of the proof of Theorem
1.6
to extend in a way so that the product of some additive components is also equal to the product of single variables. Note that the proof can be simplified if we use

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
m,r\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
\{n_{1},\ldots,n_{m}\}\subset\mathbb{N}.
Then the following equation is partition regular

(x_{1,1}+\cdots+x_{1,n_{1}})\cdots(x_{m,1}+\cdots+x_{m,n_{m}})=y_{1}\cdots\cdot y_{r}=z_{1}+\cdots+z_{n_{m}}.

Proof.

First assume
r\geq 2.

Let
p\in\overline{\mathcal{IP}_{0}}\cap E\left(\beta\mathbb{N},\cdot\right),
and
A\in p=p\cdot p.
Choose the sequence
\langle x_{1,j}\rangle_{j=1}^{n_{1}}
such that
\operatorname{FS}\left(\langle x_{1,j}\rangle_{j=1}^{n_{1}}\right)\subset A^{\star}
. As
A^{\star}\in p,
we have
B_{1}=A^{\star}\cap\bigcap\limits_{x\in\operatorname{FS}\left(\langle x_{1,j}\rangle_{j=1}^{n_{1}}\right)}x^{-1}A^{\star}\in p.

Inductively for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\},
we can construct sequences

Abstract
Many practical optimization problems lack strong convexity. Fortunately, recent studies have revealed that first-order algorithms also enjoy linear convergences under various weaker regularity conditions. While the relationship among different conditions for convex and smooth functions is well-understood, it is not the case for the nonsmooth setting. In this paper, we go beyond convexity and smoothness, and clarify the connections among common regularity conditions in the class of weakly convex functions, including strong convexity , restricted secant inequality , subdifferential error bound , Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality , and quadratic growth . In addition, using these regularity conditions, we present a simple and modular proof for the linear convergence of proximal point method (PPM) for convex and weakly convex optimization problems. The linear convergence also holds when the subproblems of PPM are solved inexactly with a proper control of inexactness.

Motivation and preliminaries

In this section, we first motivate the study of equivalent regularity conditions by revisiting the standard gradient descent algorithm, and then review some techniques in nonsmooth optimization.

2.1
Motivation: Linear convergence of gradient descent algorithms

To motivate our discussion, consider a smooth convex optimization problem
\min_{x}\,f(x)
,
where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a convex and
L
-smooth function, i.e., its gradient is
L
-Lipschitz satisfying

\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|\leq L\|x-y\|,\;\forall x,

y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Let
S:=\argmin_{x}\,f(x)
be the set of optimal solutions. Assume
S\neq\emptyset
and denote
f^{\star}=\min_{x}f(x)
. The standard
gradient descent
(GD) follows the update

x_{k+1}=x_{k}-t_{k}\nabla f(x_{k}),

(1)

where
t_{k}>0
is the step size. A textbook result says that when choosing a constant step size
t_{k}=\frac{1}{L}
, the GD iterates converge to
f^{\star}
with a
sublinear
rate (precisely,
f(x_{k})-f^{\star}\leq L\mathrm{dist}^{2}(x_{0},S)/(2k)
); see e.g.,
[
4
, Corollary 2.1.2]
. If the function
f
is strongly convex, GD achieves a global linear convergence
[
4
, Theorem 2.1.15]
.

However, the assumption of strong convexity is often not satisfied in practice. It is known that some alternative weaker assumptions are sufficient for linear convergences. We here introduce two notions:
restricted secant inequality
(RSI)
[
7
]
, and
Polyak-Łojasiewicz
(PL) inequality
[
6
]
. A differentiable function
f
satisfies RSI if there exists
\mu_{\mathrm{r}}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle\nabla f(x),x-{x}^{\star}}}\right\rangle\geq\mu_{\mathrm{r}}\|x-{x}^{\star}\|^{2}=\mu_{\mathrm{r}}\cdot\mathrm{dist}^{2}(x,S),\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},{x}^{\star}\in\Pi_{S}(x),

(2)

and it satisfies the PL inequality if there exists
\mu_{\mathrm{p}}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}\geq 2\mu_{\mathrm{p}}(f(x)-f^{\star}),\;\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(3)

Note that both RSI
eq.

2
and PL
eq.

3
imply that any stationary point of
f
is a global minimum. However, they do not imply the uniqueness of stationary points or the convexity of the function. One can think that RSI
eq.

2
(resp. PL
eq.

3
) requires that the gradient
\nabla f(x)
grows faster than that of quadratic functions when moving away from the solution set
S
(resp. the optimal value
f^{\star}
). Linear convergence
of GD under the PL inequality was first proved in
[
6
]
, and linear convergence under RSI was discussed in
[
34
, Proposition 1]
and
[
35
, Proposition 1]
. We summarize a simple version below.

Theorem 2.1

(Linear convergence of GD)
.

Consider the optimization problem
\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x),
where
f
is an
L
-smooth function. Suppose its solution set
S
is nonempty. If RSI
eq.

2
holds with
\mu_{\mathrm{r}}>0
and PL inequality
eq.

3
holds with
\mu_{\mathrm{p}}>0
, then the GD algorithm
eq.

1
with a constant stepsize
t_{k}=\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{r}}}{L^{2}}
has global linear convergence for iterates and function values, i.e.,

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(x_{k+1},S)

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{1}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(x_{k},S),

(4a)

\displaystyle f(x_{k+1})-f^{\star}

\displaystyle\leq\omega_{2}\cdot(f(x_{k})-f^{\star}),

(4b)

with
\omega_{1}=\sqrt{1-\mu_{\mathrm{r}}^{2}/L^{2}}\in(0,1)
and
\omega_{2}=(L^{3}-2\mu_{\mathrm{r}}L\mu_{\mathrm{p}}+\mu_{\mathrm{r}}^{2}\mu_{\mathrm{p}})/L^{3}\in(0,1)
.

Thanks to RSI
eq.

2
and PL inequality
eq.

3
, the proof of
theorem

2.1
is very elegant, taking only a few lines. For self-completeness, we provide a simple proof and some additional discussions in
section

B.1
. In particular, the RSI
eq.

2
leads to a quick proof of
eq.

4a
, and the PL
eq.

3
allows for a simple proof of
eq.

4b
. It is known that for
L
-smooth convex functions, the two conditions RSI
eq.

2
and PL
eq.

3
are equivalent (cf.
[
10
, Theorem 2]
). Some recent studies, such as
[
18
,
9
,
36
]
, have explored the relationships among different regularity conditions for linear convergences. A nice summary appeared in
[
10
, Theorem 2]
, but it only works in the context of
L
-smooth functions.

In this paper, we aim to characterize the relationships among different regularity conditions for
nonsmooth and nonconvex
functions (
Section

3
), and apply them to derive

proofs for linear convergences of (inexact) proximal point methods for convex and weakly convex (possibly nonsmooth) optimization (

4

2.2
The notion of slope and Ekeland’s variational principle

In this subsection, we review three important techniques in nonsmooth optimization, 1) the notion of slope, 2) a key distance estimation result, and 3) Ekeland’s variational principle, which will play an important role in our proofs later.

We first introduce the Fréchet subdifferential. Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper closed function. We define the Fréchet subdifferential (see e.g.,
[
37
, Page 27]
):

\hat{\partial}f(x):=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{s\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\liminf_{y\to x}\frac{f(y)-f(x)-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle s,y-x}}\right\rangle}{\|y-x\|}\geq 0}}\right\}.

If
f
is convex, the Fréchet subdifferential
\hat{\partial}f
is the same as the usual convex subdifferential, i.e.,

\hat{\partial}f(x)=\partial f(x):=\{s\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid f(y)\geq f(x)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle s,y-x}}\right\rangle,\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\},\;\;\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

and if
f
is differentiable, Fréchet subdifferential reduces to the usual gradient, i.e.,
\hat{\partial}f(x)=\{\nabla f(x)\},\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Definition 2.1

(Slope
[
21
, Section 2]
)
.

Consider a closed function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and a point
\bar{x}
with
f(\bar{x})
finite. The slope of
f
at
\bar{x}
is its maximal instantaneous rate of decrease:

\displaystyle|\nabla f|(\bar{x}):=\limsup_{x\to\bar{x}}\frac{(f(\bar{x})-f(x))^{+}}{\|x-\bar{x}\|},

where we use the notation
r^{+}=\max\{0,r\}
.

If
f
is smooth, the slope
|\nabla f|(\bar{x})
is simply the norm of its gradient
\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|
. If
f(x)
is convex, the slope
|\nabla f|(\bar{x})
is equivalent to the length of the minimal norm element in the subdifferential, i.e.,
|\nabla f|(\bar{x})=\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial f(\bar{x}))
. If it is
\rho
-weakly convex (i.e.,
f+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
is convex), the slope
|\nabla f|(\bar{x})
is the same as the length of the minimal norm element in the Fréchet subdifferential, i.e.,
|\nabla f|(\bar{x})=\mathrm{dist}(0,\hat{\partial}f(\bar{x}))
(see

[
38
,
39
]
for more details).
We summarize these useful properties into a lemma below; a brief proof is presented in
Section

B.2
for self-completeness:

Lemma 2.1
.

Consider a closed function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
and a point
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
f(\bar{x})
finite. The following statements for the slope hold.

1.

If
f
is differentiable, then
|\nabla f|(\bar{x})=\|\nabla f(\bar{x})\|
;

2.

If
f
is convex, then
|\nabla f|(\bar{x})=\mathrm{dist}(0,\partial f(\bar{x}))
;

3.

If
f
is
\rho
-weakly convex, then
|\nabla f|(\bar{x})=\mathrm{dist}(0,\hat{\partial}f(\bar{x}))
.

We then introduce a key result from
[
40
, Lemma 2.5]
that will serve as a crucial step to estimate the distance to the optimal solution set.

Lemma 2.2

(Lemma 2.5
[
40
]
)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper closed function. Suppose for some point
x\in\text{dom}(f)
, there are constants
\alpha<f(x)
and
r>0,K>0
such that
f(x)-\alpha<Kr
and

\displaystyle|\nabla f|(u)\geq r,\qquad\forall u\in[\alpha<f\leq f(x)],\|u-x\|\leq K.

Then the sublevel set
[f\leq\alpha]
is nonempty and
\mathrm{dist}(x,[f\leq\alpha])\leq(f(x)-\alpha)/r.

Note that if
\alpha=f^{\star}=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)
in
lemma

2.2
, we can upper bound the distance to the solution set
\mathrm{dist}(x,[f=f^{\star}])
by
(f(x)-f^{\star})/r
.

Finally, we review Ekeland’s variational principle
[
41
]
.

Theorem 2.2

(
Ekeland’s variational principle

[
41
]
)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper closed function. Suppose
\epsilon>0
and
z\in[f\leq\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)+\epsilon]
.
Then for any
\rho>0
there exists
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\|z-y\|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon/\rho,

(5a)

\displaystyle f(y)

\displaystyle\leq f(z),

(5b)

\displaystyle f(x)+\rho\|x-y\|

\displaystyle>f(y),\;\;\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}/\{y\}.

(5c)

Note that
eq.

5a
means that the distance between
y
and
z
is bounded,
eq.

5b
tells us that
y
is also in the sublevel set, and
eq.

5c
shows that

uniquely minimizes the function

From the definition of slope (

\displaystyle|\nabla f|(y)=\limsup_{x\to y}\frac{(f(y)-f(x))^{+}}{\|x-y\|}\leq\limsup_{x\to y}\frac{\rho\cdot\mathrm{dist}(x,y)}{\|x-y\|}=\rho.

(6)

Lemmas

and

will play an important role in our analysis of different regularity conditions in

Relationships between regularity conditions

In this section, we aim to show the equivalence between different regularity conditions in the class of
weakly convex (potentially nonsmooth)
functions. A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is called
\rho
-weakly convex if the function
f+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
is convex. The class of weakly convex functions is very rich: it includes all convex functions,
L
-smooth functions, certain compositions of convex functions with smooth functions, and many cost functions in modern machine learning applications; we refer interested readers to
[
27
,
28
]
for more details.

3.1
Regularity conditions and their relationship

Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper, closed,
\rho
-weakly convex function. Let
S
be the optimal solution set of
f
, i.e.,
S=\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)
, and we assume
S\neq\emptyset
. Let
f^{\star}=\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)
and
\nu>0
. We consider the following five regularity conditions:

1.

Local Strong Convexity (SC)
: there exists a positive constant
\mu_{\mathrm{s}}>0
such that

\displaystyle f(x)+\langle g,y-x\rangle+\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{s}}}{2}\|y-x\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq f(y),

(SC)

\displaystyle\forall x,y\in[f\leq f^{\star}+\nu],g\in\hat{\partial}f(x).

2.

Restricted Secant Inequality (RSI)
: there exists a positive constant
\mu_{\mathrm{r}}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mu_{\mathrm{r}}\cdot\mathrm{dist}^{2}(x,S)

\displaystyle\leq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle g,x-\hat{x}}}\right\rangle,

(RSI)

\displaystyle\forall x\in[f\leq f^{\star}+\nu],g\in\hat{\partial}f(x),\hat{x}\in\Pi_{S}(x).

3.

Error bound (EB)
1
1

1

Error bound is closely related to the notion of
metric subregularity
at an optimal point
x^{\star}
for
0\in\hat{\partial}f(\mathbb{R}^{n})

[
17
, Definition 2.3]
: there exist a constant
\alpha>0
and a set
\mathcal{U}
containing
x^{\star}
such that
\mathrm{dist}(x,(\hat{\partial}f)^{-1}(0))\leq\alpha\cdot\mathrm{dist}(0,\hat{\partial}f(x)),\forall x\in\mathcal{U}
.
:
there exists a constant
\mu_{\mathrm{e}}>0
such that

\mathrm{dist}(x,S)\leq\mu_{\mathrm{e}}\cdot\mathrm{dist}(0,\hat{\partial}f(x)),\quad\forall x\in[f\leq f^{\star}+\nu].

(EB)

4.

Polyak-Łojasiewicz (PL) inequality
2
2

2

To be consistent with the smooth case in
[
10
]
, we call the property
eq.

PL
Polyak-Łojasiewicz, which is usually used for smooth functions. The property
eq.

is actually a special case of the

\varphi^{\prime}(f(x)-f^{\star})\mathrm{dist}(0,\hat{\partial}f(x))\geq 1

\varphi(s)=cs^{1/2}
and

.
: there exists a constant
\mu_{\mathrm{p}}>0
such that

2\mu_{\mathrm{p}}\cdot(f(x)-f^{\star})\leq\mathrm{dist}^{2}(0,\hat{\partial}f(x)),\quad\forall x\in[f\leq f^{\star}+\nu].

(PL)

5.

Quadratic Growth (QG)
: there exists a constant
\mu_{\mathrm{q}}>0
such that

\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{q}}}{2}\cdot\mathrm{dist}^{2}(x,S)\leq f(x)-f^{\star},\quad\forall x\in[f\leq f^{\star}+\nu].

(QG)

All the regularity conditions above are defined over a sublevel set
[f\leq f^{\star}+\nu]
. If
v=+\infty
, then they are global. In particular,
eq.

SC
imposes a quadratic lower bound for every point in the sublevel set.
On the other hand,
eq.

RSI
,
eq.

EB
and
eq.

PL
all require a certain growth of the minimal norm element in
\hat{\partial}f(x)
when moving away from its solution set
S
or optimal value
f^{\star}
. It is easy to see that
eq.

RSI
,
eq.

EB
and
eq.

PL
all imply that every stationary point
0\in\hat{\partial}f(x)
in the sublevel set
[f\leq f^{\star}+\nu]
is a global minimum (but they do not imply the uniqueness of stationary points). Finally,
eq.

QG
shows that
f(x)
grows at least quadratically when moving away from the solution set
S
. Unlike
eq.

RSI
,
eq.

EB
and
eq.

PL
, this

alone allows suboptimal stationary points.

4
Proximal point method for convex optimization

In this section, we will utilize the regularity conditions in
Section

3
to derive fast linear convergence guarantees of the classical proximal point method (PPM)
[
43
]
for convex (potentially nonsmooth) optimization and generalize it to weakly convex settings. PPM is a conceptually simple algorithm, which has been historically used for guiding algorithm design and analysis, such as proximal bundle methods
[
44
]
and augmented Lagrangian methods
[
43
]
. It has recently found increasing applications in modern machine learning; see
[
32
]
.

4.1
Proximal point method

Consider the optimization problem

f^{\star}=\min_{x}\;f(x),\vskip-2.84526pt

(10)

where
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is a proper closed convex function. Note that
eq.

10
is also an abstract model for constrained optimization, since given a closed convex set
X
, we can define
\bar{f}(x)=f(x)
if
x\in X
, and
\bar{f}(x)=+\infty
otherwise. Let
S=\argmin_{x}\,f(x)
.
We define the
proximal mapping
as

\text{prox}_{\alpha f}(x_{k}):=\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\;f(x)+\frac{1}{2\alpha}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\|x-x_{k}}}\right\|^{2},

(11)

where
\alpha>0
.
Starting with any initial point
x_{0}
, the PPM generates a sequence of points as follows

x_{k+1}=\mathrm{prox}_{c_{k}f}(x_{k}),\quad k=0,1,2,\ldots

(12)

where
\{c_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
is a sequence of positive real numbers. The quadratic term in
eq.

11
makes the objective function strongly convex and always admits a unique solution. The iterates
eq.

12
are thus well-defined.

The convergence of PPM
eq.

12
for (nonsmooth) convex optimization has been studied since the 1970s
[
24
]
. The sublinear convergence is relatively easy to establish, and many different assumptions exist for linear convergences of
eq.

12
; see
[
24
,
33
,
16
,
25
,
8
]
. However, as we will highlight later, some assumptions are restrictive and the corresponding proofs are sophisticated and nontransparent. We aim to provide simple proofs under the general regularity conditions in
Section

3
.

4.2
(Sub)linear convergences of PPM

Under a very general setup, the PPM
eq.

12
converges at a sublinear rate for cost value gaps, and the iterates converge asymptotically, as summarized in
theorem

4.1
. This result is classical
[
45
, Theorem 2.1]
, and a new bound with an improved constant 4 is also available in
[
46
, Theorem 4.1]
using the performance estimation technique.

Theorem 4.1

(Sublinear convergence
[
45
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
f\!:\!\mathbb{R}^{n}\!\to\!\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a proper closed convex function with

S=\argmin_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)
and
S\neq\emptyset
. Then, the iterates
eq.

12
with a positive sequence
\{c_{k}\}_{k\geq 0}
satisfy

f(x_{k})-f^{\star}\leq\mathrm{dist}^{2}(x_{0},S)/(2\textstyle\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}c_{t}).

(13)

If we further have
\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}c_{t}=\infty
, then the iterates converge to an optimal solution
\bar{x}
asymptotically, i.e.,
\lim_{k\to\infty}x_{k}=\bar{x}
, where
\bar{x}\in S
.

The proof of
eq.

13
is immediate from a telescope sum via the following one-step improvement:

2c_{k}(f(x_{k+1})-f(x^{\star}))\leq\|x_{k}-x^{\star}\|^{2}-\|x_{k+1}-x^{\star}\|^{2},\quad\forall c_{k}>0,x^{\star}\in S.

(14)

This fact is not difficult to establish. Note that choosing any constant step size
c_{k}=c>0
in
eq.

13
directly implies the common sublinear rate
\mathcal{O}(1/k)
. In
theorem

4.1
,
f
does not need to be
L
-smooth, and it can also be non-differentiable. Thus, the guarantees in
theorem

4.1
are much stronger than those by (sub)gradient methods. This is because the proximal mapping
eq.

11
is a stronger oracle than simple (sub)gradient updates.

Similar to GD in
section

2
,
when the function
f
satisfies additional regularity conditions, the PPM enjoys linear convergence. Our next main technical result establishes linear convergences of the PPM under the common regularity conditions in
theorem

3.1
.

Theorem 4.2

(Linear convergence)
.

Let

Inexact proximal point method (iPPM) and its convergence

In

5.1
iPPM and stopping criteria

We replace the exact update
eq.

12
with an inexact update

\displaystyle x_{k+1}\approx\text{prox}_{c_{k}f}(x_{k}).

(20)

Two classical criteria suggested in Rockafellar’s seminal work
[
24
]

are

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-\text{prox}_{c_{k}f}(x_{k})\|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon_{k},\quad\textstyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\epsilon_{k}<\infty,

(A)

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-\text{prox}_{c_{k}f}(x_{k})\|

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{k}\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|,\quad\textstyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k}<\infty.

(B)

The inexact update
eq.

20
with
eq.

A
or
eq.

B
is called iPPM. The two criteria are not directly implementable as the value of
\text{prox}_{c_{k}f}(x_{k})
is unknown. As discussed in
[
24
, Proposition 3]
, two implementable alternatives that imply
eqs.

A
and
B
, respectively, are

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(0,H_{k}(x_{k+1}))

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon_{k}/c_{k},\quad\textstyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\epsilon_{k}<\infty,

(
\text{A}^{\prime}
)

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}(0,H_{k}(x_{k+1}))

\displaystyle\leq(\delta_{k}/c_{k})\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|,\quad\textstyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\delta_{k}<\infty,

(
\text{B}^{\prime}
)

where
H_{k}(x)=\partial f(x)+(x-x_{k})/c_{k}
is the subdifferential of
f+\|\cdot-x_{k}\|^{2}/(2c_{k})
at
x
(since
f
is convex by assumption).
Note that
eqs.

A
and
B
only require the inexact update
x_{k+1}
to stay close enough to
\text{prox}_{c_{k}f}(x_{k})
with respect to the Euclidean distance, but they do not require the inexact update
x_{k+1}
to be within the domain of
f
, i.e.,
f(x_{k+1})
might be infinity.
However, the stopping criteria
(

A
′

) and
(

B
′

) require that
x_{k+1}
is in the domain of

.

5.2

The seminal work by Rockafellar

Theorem 5.1

(Asymptotic convergence of iterates
[
24

.

Consider a proper closed convex function

20
under

converges to an optimal point

We next establish a sublinear convergence of iPPM for cost value gaps. Our simple proof is based on the boundedness of the iterates from

Theorem 5.2

(

)
.

Let

be a proper closed convex function with

20
under

′

\displaystyle\textstyle\min_{j=0,\ldots,k}f(x_{j})-f^{\star}\leq(\mathrm{dist}^{2}(x_{0},S)+2D\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\epsilon_{j})/(2\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}c_{j}),

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we have established the relationship between different regularity conditions under the class of
\rho
-weakly convex functions (see
theorem

3.1
). The result shows that, in the class of
\rho
-weakly convex functions, strong convexity implies restricted secant inequality, which in turn implies error bound. Meanwhile, error bound and Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality are equivalent, and any of them implies quadratic growth. If a
\rho
-weakly convex function satisfies quadratic growth and the quadratic growth constant satisfies
\mu_{\mathrm{q}}>\rho
, then all these four properties: restricted secant inequality, error bound, Polyak-Łojasiewicz inequality, and quadratic growth, become equivalent. Unlike previous results in the literature, our result goes beyond smoothness and convexity, benefiting the analysis of various iterative algorithms.

We have also presented simple and modular proofs for the exact PPM for both convex and weakly convex functions which makes the analysis of the exact PPM more accessible (see
theorems

4.2
and
4.3
). In the setting of inexact PPM, we first clarified the subtly in terms of inexactness and infeasibility, and then established the linear convergence of inexact PPM when the inexactness is controlled properly (see
theorem

5.3
).
The numerical experiments also validate our theoretical findings. We believe these results on (inexact) PPM will facilitate algorithm development in nonsmooth optimization, and we are particularly interested in further applications in large-scale conic optimization
[
54
,
55
]
.

Appendix A
Subdifferential characterization of weakly convex functions

For self-completeness, we review a useful subdifferential characterization of weakly convex functions. Recall that
\overline{\mathbb{R}}
denotes the extended real line, i.e.,
\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
.

We say that a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is proper if
f(x)<\infty
for at least one
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
-\infty<f(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
We say that a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is closed if the epigraph
\text{epi}f:\{(x,\alpha)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\mid f(x)\leq\alpha\}
is closed.

Definition A.1
.

For a closed function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
, we define the Fréchet subdifferential as

\hat{\partial}f(x)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{s\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid\liminf_{y\to x}\frac{f(y)-f(x)-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle s,y-x}}\right\rangle}{\|y-x\|}\geq 0}}\right\}.

Definition A.2
.

A function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is called
\rho
-weakly convex if
f+\frac{\rho}{2}\|\cdot\|^{2}
is convex.

For this class of
\rho
-weakly convex functions, the Fréchet subdifferential always exists for any point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This is because the Fréchet subdifferential is the same as the Clarke subdifferential for
\rho
-weakly convex functions and the Clarke subdifferential always exists
[
37
, Section 3 and fact 5]
.
The class of
\rho
-weakly convex functions also has the following nice characterizations. These characterizations offer favorable properties for
\rho
-weakly convex functions, which can be viewed as extensions from strongly convex functions.

Lemma A.1

(Subdifferential characterization
[
26
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

The following statements are equivalent for any closed function
f
.

•

The function
f
is
\rho
-weakly convex.

•

The approximate secant inequality holds:

\displaystyle f(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\leq\lambda f(x)+(1-\lambda)f(y)+

\displaystyle\frac{\rho\lambda(1-\lambda)}{2}\|x-y\|^{2},

\displaystyle\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\lambda\in[0,1].

•

The subgradient inequality holds:

f(y)\geq f(x)+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle v,y-x}}\right\rangle-\frac{\rho}{2}\|y-x\|^{2},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},v\in\hat{\partial}f(x).

(26)

•

The subdifferential map is hypomonotone:

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\langle v-w,x-y}}\right\rangle\geq-\rho\|x-y\|^{2},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},v\in\hat{\partial}f(x),\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ w\in\hat{\partial}f(y).

If
f
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth, then the four properties above are all equivalent to

\nabla^{2}f(x)\succeq-\rho I,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The subgradient inequality
eq.

26
is particularly useful in our proof for
theorem

3.1
. The proof repeatedly applies
eq.

26
to establish the connection between subdifferential and cost value gap.

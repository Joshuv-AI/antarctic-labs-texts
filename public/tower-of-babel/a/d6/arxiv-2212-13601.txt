Abstract
. We introduce new generalized q q -deformed coherent states ( q q -CS) by replacing the q q -factorial of [ n ] q ! [n]_{q}! in the series expansion of the classical q q -CS by the generalized factorial x n q , α ! x_{n}^{q,\alpha}! where x n q , α = ( 1 + α ​ q n − 1 ) ​ [ n ] q x_{n}^{q,\alpha}=(1+\alpha q^{n-1})[n]_{q} . We use the shifted operators method based on the sequence x n q , α x_{n}^{q,\alpha} to obtain a realization in terms of Al-Salam-Chihara polynomials for the basis vectors of the Fock space carrying the constructed q q -CS. These new states interpolate between the q q -CS of Arik-Coon type ( α = 0 \alpha=0 , 0 < q < 1 0<q<1 ) and a set of coherent states of Barut–Girardello type for the Meixner-Pollaczek oscillator ( α ≠ 0 \alpha\neq 0 , q → 1 q\to 1 ). We also discus their associated Bargmann type transforms.

1.
Introduction

Coherent states (CS) have been introduced by Schrödinger as states which
behave in many respects like classical states
[
1
]
. The
canonical
CS of the harmonic oscillator denoted
\vartheta_{z}
and labeled by points
z\in\mathbb{C}
, may be defined in four ways: (i) as eigenstates of the
annihilation operator
a
, (ii) by applying the displacement operator
e^{za^{\ast}-\bar{z}a}
on the vacuum state
|0\rangle
such that
a|0\rangle=0
, where
a^{\ast}
is the Hermitian conjugate of
a
satisfying the relation
[a,a^{*}]=1
, (iii)
by finding states that minimize the Heisenberg uncertainty principle and
(iv) as a specific superposition of eigenstates
\varphi_{n}
of the harmonic
oscillator (HO) number operator
a^{\ast}a
as

(1.1)

\vartheta_{z}:=\left(e^{z\bar{z}}\right)^{-1/2}\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\bar{z}^{n}}{\sqrt{n!}}\varphi_{n},

where the
\varphi_{n}
’s span a Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}
usually called Fock space.

The generalized CS (GCS) may be constructed by extending one of the four
aforementionned ways defining the canonical ones or by superposing CS themselves
[
2
]
. The terminology of GCS was first appeared and studied in
[
2
,
3
]
in connection with states discussed in
[
4
]
. GCS belong to a larger class of states named
nonclassical
which

are involved in quantum optics and in other fields
ranging from solid states to cosmology. These states exhibit some purely
quantum-mechanical properties, such as squeezing and antibunching
[
5
]
.

GCS are usually associated with potential algebras other than the
oscillator one
[
6
,
7
]
. An important example is provided by the
q
-deformed CS (
q
-CS for brevity) related to deformations of boson
operators
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
.
q
-CS are usually constructed in a way that
they reduce to their standard counterparts as
q\rightarrow 1
. Among
q
-CS, there are those associated with the relation
a_{q}a_{q}^{\ast}-qa_{q}^{\ast}a_{q}=1
with
0<q<1
, where
a_{q}
are often termed maths-type
q
-bosons operators
[
11
,
12
]
. These
q
-CS may be defined
[
8
]
through a
q
-analog of the number states expansion (
1.1
) as

(1.2)

\vartheta_{z}^{q}:=\left(e_{q}(z\bar{z})\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\bar{z}^{n}}{\sqrt{[n]_{q}!}}\varphi_{n}^{(q)},

for
z\in\mathbb{C}_{q}:=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C},\>(1-q)\zeta\bar{\zeta}<1\}
where

(1.3)

e_{q}(\xi):=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\frac{\xi^{n}}{[n]_{q}!}=\frac{1}{(\xi(1-q);q)_{\infty}}

being a
q
-exponential function and
[n]_{q}!=\displaystyle\prod_{k=1}^{n}[k]_{q}
where

(1.4)

[k]_{q}=\frac{1-q^{k}}{1-q}\to k\quad\mathrm{as}\quad q\to 1.

Details on the basic notations of
q
-calculus can be found in
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
. Here, the
\varphi_{n}^{(q)}
’s span a Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}_{q}
which stands for a
q
-analog of the Fock space
\mathscr{H}
.

In this paper, we construct a new class of generalized
q
-CS by replacing the
q
-factorial
[n]_{q}!
in the denominator following the summation sign in (
1.2
) by the generalized factorial

(1.5)

x_{n}^{q,\alpha}!:=(-\alpha;q)_{n}[n]_{q}!,\quad x_{0}^{q,\alpha}=1,\quad 0<q<1,\>-1<\alpha<q.

We discuss the resolution of the identity operator on
\mathscr{H}_{q}
satisfied by these states in connection with a moment problem associated with the sequence of numbers
x_{n}^{q,\alpha}=(1+\alpha q^{n-1})[n]_{q}
,
n=0,1,2,\cdots
. We also give a realization in terms of the Al-Salam-Chihara polynomials of the basis
\varphi_{n}^{(q)}
by using the shift operators method
[
16
]
based on the sequence
x_{n}^{q,\alpha}
. The constructed
q
-CS interpolate between the
q
-CS of Arik-Coon type
[
17
]
, which correspond to
\alpha=0
and
0<q<1
and a set of coherent states of Barut-Girardello type for the Meixner-Pollaczek oscillator
[
18
]
, which we recover at the limit
q\to 1
with
\alpha\neq 0
. We also discuss the associated coherent states transforms and some particular cases of parameters
q
and
\alpha
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we introduce a new class of generalized

Proposition 1
.

For
(q,\,\alpha)
such that

and

Proof.

Let us assume that the measure takes the form
\mu_{\alpha,q}(d^{2}z)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\mathcal{N}_{q,\alpha}(r^{2})\rho_{q,\alpha}(dr)\lambda_{[0,2\pi)}(d\theta)

,
\theta\in[0,2\pi),

where
\rho_{q,\alpha}
is a positive radial measure to be determined. Using the expression of the states (

), the operator

\mathcal{O}=\int_{\mathbb{C}}|\vartheta_{z}^{q,\alpha}\rangle\langle\vartheta_{z}^{q,\alpha}|\mu_{q,\alpha}(d^{2}z)

(2.8)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n,m=0}^{+\infty}|\varphi_{n}^{(q)}\rangle\langle\varphi_{m}^{(q)}|\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}\frac{r^{n+m}}{\sqrt{(-\alpha;q)_{n}[n]_{q}!(-\alpha;q)_{m}[m]_{q}!}}\rho_{q,\alpha}(dr)\>\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{i(n-m)\theta}\lambda_{[0,2\pi)}(d\theta)\right)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=0}^{+\infty}|\varphi_{n}^{(q)}\rangle\langle\varphi_{n}^{(q)}|\frac{1}{(-\alpha;q)_{n}[n]_{q}!}\left(\int_{0}^{+\infty}r^{2n}\rho_{q,\alpha}(dr)\right).

One now has to find

\int_{0}^{+\infty}r^{2n}\rho_{q,\alpha}(dr)=(-\alpha;q)_{n}[n]_{q}!,\quad n=0,1,2,\cdots.

By using the radial measure in (

∎

3.
A polynomials realization of the basis
\{\varphi_{n}^{(q)}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

In this section we use the
shift operator method
as discussed by Ali and Ismail
[
16
]
to obtain a polynomial realization of the basis
\{\varphi_{n}^{(q)}\}
. For this we may define the operators
a
and
a^{*}
on the space
\mathcal{H}
by

(3.1)

a\varphi_{n}^{(q)}=\sqrt{2x_{n}^{q,\alpha}}\varphi_{n-1}^{(q)},\;\;a\varphi_{0}^{(q)}=0,\qquad a^{*}\varphi_{n}^{(q)}=\sqrt{2x_{n+1}^{q,\alpha}}\varphi_{n+1}^{(q)}.

Using
a
and
a^{*}
, it is possible to identify the basis vectors
\{\varphi_{n}^{(q)}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
with another family of real orthogonal polynomials. We now define the operators,

(3.2)

Q=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\;[a+a^{*}]\;,\qquad P=\frac{1}{i\sqrt{2}}\;[a-a^{*}]\;,

to play the roles of the standard position and momentum operators. The operator
Q
acts on the basis vectors
\varphi_{n}
as

(3.3)

Q\varphi_{n}^{(q)}=\sqrt{x_{n}^{q,\alpha}}\;\varphi_{n-1}^{(q)}+\sqrt{x_{n+1}^{q,\alpha}}\;\varphi_{n+1}^{(q)}\;.

Here, the sum
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{x_{n}^{q,\alpha}}}
diverges, therefore, the operator
Q
is essentially self-adjoint and hence has a unique self-adjoint extension, which we again denote by
Q
. Let
E_{x},\;x\in\mathbb{R}
, be the spectral family of
Q
, so that,

Q=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x\;dE_{x}\;.

Thus there exists a measure
\omega(dx)
on
\mathbb{R}
such that on the Hilbert space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\omega(dx))
,

Q
is just the operator of multiplication by
x
. Consequently, on this space, the relation (
3.3
)
takes the form

(3.4)

x\varphi_{n}^{(q)}=\sqrt{x_{n}^{q,\alpha}}\varphi_{n-1}^{(q)}+\sqrt{x_{n+1}^{q,\alpha}}\varphi_{n+1}^{(q)}\;

which is a two-term recursion relation, familiar from the theory of orthogonal polynomials. It follows that

\omega(dx)=d\langle\varphi_{0}^{(q)}|E_{x}|\varphi_{0}^{(q)}\rangle
, and the
\varphi_{n}^{(q)}
may be realized as the polynomials obtained
by orthonormalizing the sequence of monomials
1,x,x^{2},x^{3},\ldots\;,
with respect to this measure
(using a Gram-Schmidt procedure). Let us use the notation
p_{n}(x)
to write the vectors
\varphi_{n}^{(q)}
, when they are
so realized, as orthogonal polynomials in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\omega(dx))
. Then,

(3.5)

\langle\varphi_{k}^{(q)},\varphi_{n}^{(q)}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}p_{k}(x)p_{n}(x)\;\omega(dx)=\delta_{k,n}\;.

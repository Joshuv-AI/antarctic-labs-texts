Automorphic tensor products and cuspidal cohomology of the GL 4 {\rm GL}_{4}
Chandrasheel Bhagwat & Sudipa Mondal

Abstract
. In this article, we establish an asymptotic lower bound estimate on the contribution of cuspidal automorphic representations of GL 4 ​ ( 𝔸 ℚ ) {\rm GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}) to cuspidal cohomology of the GL 4 {\rm GL}_{4} which are obtained from automorphic tensor product of two automorphic representations of GL 2 ​ ( 𝔸 ℚ ) {\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}}) of given weights and with varying level structure. In the end, we also prove that the symmetric cube of a representation of GL 2 {\rm GL}_{2} and the automorphic tensor product of two representations of GL 2 {\rm GL}_{2} can not be equal (up to a twist by a character of GL 1 {\rm GL}_{1} ) to each other, under the suitable assumptions on the representations being cuspidal and cohomological.

11F41, 11F75

1.
Introduction

For a connected reductive algebraic group over a number field
{\mathbb{F}}
, one studies the notion of cohomological representations of
G({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{F}})
in terms of the sheaves on the associated adélic symmetric spaces, defined by the highest weight representations of
G
.

It is interesting to ask how much of the cohomology of
G
is captured by the automorphic representations of
G({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{F}})
. In the case of
{\rm GL}_{n}
, such representations are often obtained by functorial transfer from products of lower rank
{\rm GL}_{n^{\prime}}
subgroups. In
[
4
]
, authors of this manuscript proved an estimate of the contribution of symmetric cube transfer from
{\rm GL}_{2}
to the cohomology of
{\rm GL}_{4}
.

The other possible functorial transfers for
{\rm GL}_{4}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
are automorphic tensor product from
{\rm GL}_{2}\times{\rm GL}_{2}
to
{\rm GL}_{4}
and Asai transfer from
{{\rm GL}_{2}}|_{{\mathbb{K}}}
for some imaginary quadratic extension
{\mathbb{K}}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. In this manuscript, we prove the following estimate on the contribution of automorphic tensor products from
{\rm GL}_{2}\times{\rm GL}_{2}
to cohomology of the
{\rm GL}_{4}
.

Let
k_{1}>k_{2}\geq 2
be two positive integers. Suppose they are of different parity. Without loss of generality, assume
k_{1}
to be odd and
k_{2}
even. Define
\mathcal{T}_{k_{1},k_{2}}(N)
to be the set of all cuspidal automorphic representations of
{\rm GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})
corresponding to the level structure
K_{f}^{4}(N)
that are obtained by automorphic tensor product of two cuspidal automorphic representations of
{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})
with highest weights
\left(\dfrac{k_{1}-1}{2},~\dfrac{1-k_{1}}{2}\right)
and
\left(\dfrac{k_{2}}{2}-1,~1-\dfrac{k_{2}}{2}\right)
, respectively. (If
k_{1},~k_{2}
are of same parity, we consider the half-integral twist of the automorphic tensor product of two representations
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
of
{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})
with suitable highest weight depending on
k_{1},k_{2}
to be even or odd). See section
5
for the notations.

Theorem 1.0.1
.

Let
k_{1}>k_{2}\geq 2
be two integers. Then for any integer
N>24
,

|\mathcal{T}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(p^{N})|\gg_{k_{1},~k_{2}}~p^{2\lfloor N/4\rfloor}\text{ as the prime }p\longrightarrow\infty.

We briefly describe the structure of this manuscript. After setting up preliminaries about cusp forms in Sec.
2
, we discuss the automorphic tensor product representations in Sec
3
including a calculation on the conductor of these representations. In Sec.
4
, we discuss briefly about the cohomological representations of
{\rm GL}_{4}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
and examples of cohomological automorphic tensor product representations. In Sec.
5
, we give the proof of Thm.
1.0.1
using the construction and criteria for cuspidality of automorphic tensor product for
{\rm GL}_{2}\times{\rm GL}_{2}
proved by D. Ramakrishnan in
[
13
]
and
[
14
]
.
In Sec.
6
, we give a proof of the fact that the symmetric cube of a representation of
{\rm GL}_{2}
and the automorphic tensor product of two representations of
{\rm GL}_{2}
can not be equal (up to a twist by a character of
{\rm GL}_{1}
) to each other, under the suitable assumptions on the representations being cuspidal and cohomological. This result may be possibly known to the experts and may be unsurprising. However, we add the details of the calculations and proofs for the purpose of completion.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Dimension formulae for spaces of cusp forms

We discuss some well-known and relevant results in this section. Let
N
be an integer. Consider the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{1}(N)
of
\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
defined by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}(N)

\displaystyle=\left\{A\in\text{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}):A\equiv\begin{bmatrix}1&*\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}(\text{mod }N)\right\}.

For an integer
k
, let
S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
and
S_{k}^{\text{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(N))
denote the spaces of cusp forms of weight
k
and newforms of weight
k
, respectively for the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{1}(N)
. The following well-known results will be important in the proof of the main theorem (see
[
15
]
for the details and
[
1
]
for the formulation of these results as given below).

Lemma 2.1.1
.

An estimate for the dimension of the space of cusp forms:

\frac{\rm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(N)))}{N^{2}}=\frac{k-1}{4\pi^{2}}+o(1)\text{ as }N\longrightarrow\infty.

Lemma 2.1.2
.

A dimension formula for newforms for
\Gamma_{1}(N)
:
For
n\geq 2
and a prime
p\geq 2
, we have

\frac{{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}~(S_{k}^{\text{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(p^{n})))}{p^{2n}}=\left(\frac{k-1}{4\pi^{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{2}}-\frac{2}{p^{4}}\right)+o(1)\quad\text{ as }~p\longrightarrow\infty.

Proof.

Let
M\in\mathbb{N}
. For a prime
p
, let
v_{p}(M)
denote the highest power of
p
that divides
M
. For
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
, let
a_{M}
be the the number of prime factors of
M
such that
v_{p}(M)=2
. We define a function
\bar{\mu}
on
{\mathbb{N}}
by,

\bar{\mu}(M)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }v_{p}(M)\geq 3\text{ for some prime }p\\
(-2)^{a_{M}}&\text{ otherwise }\\
\end{cases}.

From (
[
15
, Prop. 6.6]
), we know that

{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(S_{k}^{\text{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(M)))=\sum_{d|M}\bar{\mu}(M/d)\cdot{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(d))).

For
M=p^{n},n\geq 2
, there are at most three terms such that
\bar{\mu}(M/d)\neq 0
in the above sum. Adding these three terms gives

\displaystyle{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(S_{k}^{\text{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(M)))={\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(p^{n})))+{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(p^{n-1})))-2\text{ dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(S_{k}(\Gamma_{1}(p^{n-2}))).

Using Lem.
2.1.1
and fixing
n
while varying
p
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}(S_{k}^{\text{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(M)))}{p^{2n}}=\frac{k-1}{4\pi^{2}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p^{2}}-\frac{2}{p^{4}}\right)+o(1)\text{ as }p\longrightarrow\infty.

∎

2.2.
An upper bound for normalised eigenforms

Let
C_{k}(N)
denote the set of normalised cusp eigenforms of Hecke operators of
\Gamma_{1}(N)
of weight
k
obtained by automorphic induction of größencharacters of all possible imaginary quadratic extensions of
{\mathbb{Q}}
. From the work of Ambi
[
1
]
, we have an upper bound on the cardinality of this set as described below.

Notation: Let
f
and
g
be two non-negative real valued functions on
\mathbb{N}
. We write
f\ll g
(or equivalently
g\gg f
) if there exists a constant
C
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

f(n)\leq Cg(n)\quad\forall~n\geq n_{0}.

We write
f\sim g
if both
f\ll g
and
g\ll f
hold.

Theorem 2.2.1
.

(
[
1
]
)
For
k,N\geq 1
and
\epsilon\in(0,1),
let
N^{\prime}:=\prod\limits_{p~\text{prime},~p|N}p
. Then

|C_{k}(N)|~\ll_{k,\epsilon}N\cdot{N^{\prime}}^{1+\epsilon}\text{ as }N\longrightarrow\infty

If
N=p^{n},n\in\mathbb{N}
, then more is true.

Theorem 2.2.2
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. We have,

|C_{k}(p^{n})|\ll_{\epsilon}p^{n+\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\text{ as }\epsilon\longrightarrow 0,~p\longrightarrow\infty.

The following auxiliary results are discussed in
[
1
]
and
[
4
]
respectively. We mention them here for a reference.

Lemma 2.2.3
.

Suppose
p
is an odd prime.

(1)

If
p\equiv 1\mod 4
, then

|C_{k}(p^{n})|=0\quad\forall~\text{ }k\geq 2\text{ and }\forall\text{ }n\geq 1.

(2)

If
p\equiv 3\mod 4
,
\mathbb{E}={\mathbb{Q}}(\sqrt{-p})
and
\mathfrak{f}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{E}}
such that
N_{\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{F}}(\mathfrak{f})\cdot\allowbreak|D_{\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{Q}}~||~p^{n}
for some
n\geq 1
, then
\mathfrak{f}~|~p^{m}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{E}}
where
m=\lfloor\dfrac{n}{2}\rfloor
.

We now prove Thm.
2.2.2
. We need to only consider the case
p\equiv 3\mod 4
case in view of Lem.
2.2.3
.

Proof.

Let, for an ideal
\mathfrak{J}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{E}}
,
h_{\mathbb{E}}^{0}(\mathfrak{J})
and
h_{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{J})
denote the number of Hecke characters with conductor exactly
\mathfrak{J}
and the number of characters of the narrow ray class group modulo
\mathfrak{J}
respectively. Hence
\sum\limits_{\mathfrak{f}~|~\mathfrak{J}}h_{\mathbb{E}}^{0}(\mathfrak{f})=h_{\mathbb{E}}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{J})
. Using (Theorem 12.5,
[
8
]
) we have,

|C_{k}(p^{n})|\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p}),\\
N_{\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{f})\cdot|D_{\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{Q}}~||~p^{n}\end{subarray}}h_{\mathbb{E}}^{0}(\mathfrak{f}).

By part (2) of Lem.
2.2.3
,

|C_{k}(p^{n})|\leq\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{f}~|~p^{m}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{E}}\\
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-p})\end{subarray}}h_{\mathbb{E}}^{0}(\mathfrak{f})=h^{\prime}_{\mathbb{E}}(p^{m}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{E}})\leq h_{\mathbb{E}}\Phi_{\mathbb{E}}(p^{m}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{E}}),

where
h_{\mathbb{E}}=
the class number of
\mathbb{E}
and
\Phi_{\mathbb{E}}(\mathcal{I})=N_{\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathcal{I})\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}|\mathcal{I},\\
\mathfrak{p}\text{ prime }\end{subarray}}(1-N_{\mathbb{E}/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})^{-1})
with
\Phi_{\mathbb{E}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{E}})=1
.

The last inequality follows from
[
11
, Theorem 3.25(i)]
. Hence,

|C_{k}(p^{n})|\leq h_{\mathbb{E}}\cdot p^{2m}.

We use the following asymptotic formula for the class number as in
[
11
, Corollary 1 of Theorem 8.14]
.

\begin{split}&\quad{\log}~h_{{\mathbb{Q}}({\sqrt{-d}})}=\left(\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\right)\log|d|\quad\text{ as }~d\longrightarrow\infty\\
\implies&\quad h_{{\mathbb{Q}}({\sqrt{-d}})}\ll_{\epsilon}d^{{\frac{1}{2}}+\epsilon}\quad\text{ as }\epsilon\longrightarrow 0,~d\longrightarrow\infty\\
\implies&\quad|C_{k}(p^{n})|\ll_{\epsilon}p^{2m+\frac{1}{2}+1}\text{ as }\epsilon\longrightarrow 0,~p\longrightarrow\infty\\
\implies&\quad|C_{k}(p^{n})|\ll_{\epsilon}p^{n+\frac{1}{2}+\epsilon}\text{ as }\epsilon\longrightarrow 0,~p\longrightarrow\infty.\end{split}

∎

2.3.
The Level Structure :

Consider the algebraic group
{\rm GL}_{m}
. We very briefly recall the definition of a level structure for
{\rm GL}_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. For a finite prime
p
of
{\mathbb{Q}}
, let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
be the ring of
p
-adic integers. For each integer
n\geq 0
, define

K_{p}^{m}(n):=\{x=(x_{i,j})_{m\times m}\in{\rm GL}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}):x_{m,k}\in p^{n}\mathbb{Z}_{p},~\text{ }1\leq k<m,~x_{m,m}-1\in p^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\}.

Let
\mathbb{A}_{f}
denote the finite part of adèles over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. Let
N
be a positive integer with prime factorisation
N=\prod\limits_{1\leq i\leq r,p_{i}|N}p_{i}^{n_{i}}
. Define a compact open subgroup
K_{f}^{m}(N)=\prod\limits_{p}K_{p}
of
{\rm GL}_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
where

K_{p}=\begin{cases}&K_{p_{i}}^{m}{(n_{i})}\quad\text{if}~p\mid N~\text{i.e.},~p=p_{i}\\
&{\rm GL}_{m}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})\quad\text{if}~p\nmid N\end{cases}.

For each
N\geq 1
, the compact open subgroup
K_{f}^{m}(N)\subseteq{\rm GL}_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
is called the level structure corresponding to
N
.

3.
Automorphic Tensor product

3.1.
Definition

We recall the definition of automorphic tensor product transfer from
\rm{GL}_{2}\times\rm{GL}_{2}
to
\rm{GL}_{4}
over a number field
{\mathbb{F}}
. Let
\pi_{1}=\bigotimes\limits_{v}\pi_{1,v}
and
\pi_{2}=\bigotimes\limits_{v}\pi_{2,v}
be two cuspidal automorphic representations of
\rm{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{F}})
. By local Langlands correspondence for each place
v
, the local constituents
\pi_{1,v}
and
\pi_{2,v}
correspond to two dimensional representations of the local Weil-Deligne group.

D. Ramakrishnan in
[
13
, Thm. M]
proved the existence of an automorphic representation (automorphic tensor product)
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
of
\rm{GL}_{4}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{F}})
that satisfies:

•

Equality of local
L
-functions:
L(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})_{v})=L(s,\pi_{1,v}\times\pi_{2,v})
for all places
v
,

•

Equality of local
\epsilon
-factors:
\varepsilon(s,(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})_{v})=\varepsilon(s,\pi_{1,v}\times\pi_{2,v})
for all finite places
v
.

3.2.
The cuspidality criterion for automorphic tensor product

We describe the cuspidality criterion for the automorphic tensor product (proved by D. Ramakrishnan in
[
14
]
) (we need it only for the case of base field =
{\mathbb{Q}}
).

Theorem 3.2.1
.

Let
\pi_{1},~\pi_{2}
be two cuspidal automorphic representations of
{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})
. Then
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
is an isobaric automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})
. Further more
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
is cuspidal under the following conditions.

(1)

If exactly one of them is dihedral (i.e. automorphically induced from imaginary quadratic extensions of
{\mathbb{Q}}
), then
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
is cuspidal.

(2)

If both of them are non-dihedral, then
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
is cuspidal if and only if
\pi_{2}
is not of the form
\pi_{1}\otimes\chi
for any idéle class character
\chi
of
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

(3)

Suppose
\pi_{1},~\pi_{2}
are both dihedral, say

\pi_{i}=I^{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{{\mathbb{K}}_{i}}
for
i=1,~2
, where
{\mathbb{K}}_{i}
’s are quadratic extensions of
{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\mu_{1}
(resp.,
\mu_{2}
) is an idéle class character of
{\mathbb{K}}_{1}
(resp.,
\mathbb{K}_{2}
). Then
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
is cuspidal if and only if
{\mathbb{K}}_{1}\neq{\mathbb{K}}_{2}
.

3.3.
Conductors for automorphic tensor products

Conductor of an automorphic representation: We briefly recall the notion of the conductor of an automorphic representation here.

Let
(\rho,\mathcal{H})=(\bigotimes\limits_{p\leq\infty}\rho_{p},\bigotimes\limits_{p\leq\infty}\mathcal{H}_{p})
be an irreducible automorphic representation of
{\rm GL}_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}})
.

Definition 3.3.1
.

For each finite prime
p
, the conductor of
\rho_{p}
is defined to be the smallest integer
c(\rho_{p})\geq 0
such that the space
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{K_{p}^{m}({{c(\rho_{p})}})}
consisting of all
K_{p}^{m}({{c(\rho_{p})}})
-fixed vectors of
\mathcal{H}_{p}
is non-zero. Thus
c(\rho_{p})=0
for almost all primes. The conductor of
\rho
is defined as

N_{\rho}=\prod_{p<\infty}p^{c(\rho_{p})}.

Conductor of a local character:
Let
\mathcal{K}
be a non-archimedean local field of characteristic zero. Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{K}}
,
\mathfrak{p}
,
\kappa
be its ring of integers, the maximal ideal and the residue field respectively.

Definition 3.3.2
.

The conductor
c(\zeta)
of a character
\zeta
of
\mathcal{K}^{\times}
is the smallest positive integer
n
such that
{\zeta}_{|_{1+\mathfrak{p}^{n}}}=1
.

A relation between level and conductor:
For a supercuspidal representation
\pi
of
{\rm GL}_{n}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
, if
c(\pi)
is the conductor of
\pi
, we have
c(\pi)=n\left(1+\dfrac{m}{e}\right)
, where
m
is the level of
\pi
and
e
is the ramification index (see
[
2
, p.149]
). We will consider only the case when
n=2
. Note that,
c(\pi)\geq 2
.

Since,
1\leq e\leq 2
, we have

(1)

\begin{split}&\quad\dfrac{m}{2}\leq\dfrac{m}{e}\leq m\\
\implies&\quad\frac{m}{2}\leq\dfrac{c(\pi)}{2}-1\leq m\\
\implies&\quad m+2\leq c(\pi)\leq 2m+2.\end{split}

Lemma 3.3.3
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
\pi_{1},~\pi_{2}
be two supercuspidal representations of
\rm{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})
. Let
c(\pi_{1}),~c(\pi_{2})\text{ and }c(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})
be the conductors of
\pi_{1},~\pi_{2}\text{ and }\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}
respectively. Then we have,

2\leq c(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})\leq 4c(\pi_{1})+4c(\pi_{2})-12.

Proof.

Using
[
5
, Thm. 6.5 ]
and the calculation before the Lem.
3.3.3
, we get the desired inequality.

Take
e={\rm l.c.m.}(e_{1},e_{2})
, and
m_{1}
,
m_{2}
be the levels of
\pi_{1}
and
\pi_{2}
, respectively. Let
m
be defined by

m=\max\left\{\frac{em_{1}}{e_{1}},\frac{em_{2}}{e_{2}}\right\}.

As in the notation in
[
5
]
, we have

n_{1}=n_{2}=2,\quad 1\leq e_{1},e_{2}\leq 2,

1\leq d\leq 2,\quad 0\leq c(\beta),c(\gamma),\quad l\leq m.

Let
\check{\pi_{1}}
denote the contragredient representation of
\pi
. Using
[
5
, Thm. 6.5 ]
, the inequality (
1
), and the fact
c(\pi)=c(\check{\pi})
(see
[
6
, Prop., p.23]
), we conclude that

2\leq c(\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2})\leq 4c(\pi_{1})+4c(\pi_{2})-12.

∎

4.
Cohomology of the
{\rm GL}_{m}

4.1.
Cohomological representations of
{{\rm GL}}_{m}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})

Let
\pi
be a cuspidal automorphic representation of
{{\rm GL}}_{m}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
with finite and infinite parts denoted by
\pi_{f}
and
\pi_{\infty}
respectively. Let
\mu
be a dominant integral weight corresponding to the standard Borel subgroup of
{{\rm GL}}_{m}
and
\mathcal{M}_{\mu,\mathbb{C}}
be the underlying vector space of the finite dimensional irreducible highest weight representation corresponding to
\mu
. For a given level structure
K_{f}^{m}\subset{\rm GL}_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{f})
, let
\pi^{K^{m}_{f}}_{f}
denote the space of
K_{f}^{m}
-fixed vectors of
\pi_{f}
.

Define
{\rm Coh}({{\rm GL}}_{m},\mu,K_{f}^{m})
= the set of all automorphic representations
\pi
such that its relative Lie algebra cohomology with coefficients in
\mathcal{M}_{\mu,\mathbb{C}}
is non zero in some degree i.e.,

H^{*}(\mathfrak{gl}_{m},\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}\cdot{\rm SO}_{m}(\mathbb{R}),\pi_{\infty}\bigotimes\mathcal{M}_{\mu,\mathbb{C}})\bigotimes\pi_{f}^{K_{f}^{m}}\neq 0.

We say that
\pi
is cohomological if
\pi\in{\rm Coh}({{\rm GL}}_{m},\mu,K_{f}^{m})
for some highest weight
\mu
and level structure
K_{f}^{m}
. Now we state a necessary condition for
\pi
to be cohomological. Let
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{+}({\rm GL}_{m})
be the set of all pairs
(w,\ell)
with
\ell=(\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\dots,\ell_{m})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
such that
\ell_{1}>\ell_{2}>\dots>\ell_{m}>0
and
\ell_{i}=-\ell_{m-i+1}
and
w\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that for all
i
,

w+\ell_{i}\equiv\begin{cases}1\text{ if }m\text{ is even }\\
0\text{ if }m\text{ is odd }\end{cases}.

For
(w,\ell)\in\mathcal{L}_{0}^{+}({\rm GL}_{m})
, let
D_{\ell_{i}}
be the discrete series representation of
\rm{GL}_{2}({\mathbb{R}})
of lowest weight
\ell_{i}+1
,
1\leq i\leq m
. Define,

J(w,\ell)=\begin{cases}{\rm Ind}_{P_{2,2,\dots,2}}^{{\rm GL}_{m}}\left((D_{\ell_{1}}\otimes|.|^{w/2}_{{\mathbb{R}}})\otimes\dots\otimes(D_{\ell_{m/2}}\otimes|.|^{w/2}_{{\mathbb{R}}})\right)&\text{ if }m\text{ is even}\\
{\rm Ind}_{P_{2,2,\dots,2,1}}^{{\rm GL}_{m}}\left((D_{\ell_{1}}\otimes|.|^{w/2}_{{\mathbb{R}}})\otimes\dots\otimes(D_{\ell_{(m-1)/2}}\otimes|.|^{w/2}_{{\mathbb{R}}})\otimes|.|^{w/2}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\right)&\text{ if }m\text{ is odd}\end{cases}.

If
\pi
is cuspidal cohomological representation of
{\rm GL}_{m}(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})
, then the representation
\pi_{\infty}
must be equal to
J(w,\ell)
for some
(w,\ell)\in\mathcal{L}_{0}^{+}({\rm GL}_{m})
. See (
[
12
]
, section 5) for more details.

4.2.
Examples

We are interested in studying the contribution to the
{\rm GL}_{4}
-cohomology of the representations that are obtained via automorphic tensor products of two cusp forms on
{\rm GL}_{2}
. For that, consider the following examples.

Example 4.2.1
.

(see
[
12
]
) Let
k_{1}>k_{2}\geq 2
be positive integers. Let
\phi_{1}\text{ and }\phi_{2}
be two cusp forms of weight
k_{1},k_{2}
respectively and
\pi_{\phi_{1}}
and
\pi_{\phi_{2}}
be the corresponding automorphic cuspidal representations of
{\rm GL}_{2}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
. Let
\pi:=\pi_{\phi_{1}}\boxtimes\pi_{\phi_{2}}
denote their automorphic tensor product.
\pi_{\infty}
= the representation at infinity as a representation of
{\rm GL}_{4}({\mathbb{R}})
, can be computed using a well-known recipe for Langlands parameters as

\pi_{\infty}={\rm{Ind}}_{P_{2,2}}^{{\rm GL}_{4}}(D_{k_{1}+k_{2}-2}\otimes D_{k_{1}-k_{2}}).

We have to consider two cases based on the parity of
k_{1}+k_{2}
.

Case (1):
Suppose
k_{1}+k_{2}
is odd. In this case we see that
\pi_{\infty}=J(0,\ell),
where:

\ell=(k_{1}+k_{2}-2,~k_{1}-k_{2},~k_{2}-k_{1},~-k_{1}-k_{2}+2).

Hence
\pi
is cohomological with respect to the dominant integral weight

\mu_{\pi}=\nu_{k_{1},k_{2}}=\left(\frac{k_{1}+k_{2}-5}{2},~\frac{k_{1}-k_{2}-1}{2},~\frac{k_{2}-k_{1}+1}{2},~\frac{-k_{1}-k_{2}+5}{2}\right).

Case (2):
Suppose
k_{1}+k_{2}
is even. In this case we see that
\pi
is not cohomological but its half-integral Tate-twist
\pi\otimes|\cdot|^{1/2}
is cohomological. Moreover,

(\pi\otimes|\cdot|^{1/2})_{\infty}=J(1,\ell),
where

\ell=(k_{1}+k_{2}-2,~k_{1}-k_{2},~k_{2}-k_{1},~-k_{1}-k_{2}+2).

Hence
\pi
is cohomological with respect to the dominant integral weight

\mu_{\pi}=\left(\frac{k_{1}+k_{2}-4}{2},~\frac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{2},~\frac{k_{2}-k_{1}+2}{2},~\frac{-(k_{1}+k_{2})+6}{2}\right).

Example 4.2.2
.

The following example from (
[
12
]
, section 5) shows that we must consider two cusp forms of different weight for their automorphic tensor product to be cohomological (up to a half-integral Tate-twist).

Let
\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
are two cusp forms of weight
k\in{\mathbb{Z}},k
even such that
\pi=\pi_{\phi_{1}}\boxtimes\pi_{\phi_{2}}
is cuspidal. Then the similar computation involving the Langlands parameters gives that the infinite part of
\pi
is given by

\pi_{\infty}={\rm Ind}_{P_{2,1,1}}^{{\rm GL}_{4}}(D_{2(k-1)}\otimes sgn\otimes 1).

From the shape of
\pi_{\infty}
, it follows that neither
\pi_{\infty}
nor
(\pi\otimes|\cdot|^{1/2})_{\infty}
are of the type
J(w,\ell)
for any
(w,\ell)\in\mathcal{L}_{0}^{+}({\rm GL}_{m})
and hence neither
\pi
nor
\pi\otimes|\cdot|^{1/2}
are cohomological.

5.
An estimate of the contribution of automorphic tensor products to cohomology of the
{\rm GL}_{4}

5.1.
Notations

We develop a few required notations for the statement of the main theorem in this subsection.

•

Let
k_{1}>k_{2}\geq 2
be two positive integers. Suppose they are of different parity. Without loss of generality, assume that
k_{1}
is odd and
k_{2}
is even. Define
\mathcal{T}_{k_{1},k_{2}}(N)
to be the set of all cuspidal automorphic representations of
{\rm GL}_{4}(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})
corresponding to the level structure
K_{f}^{4}(N)
that are obtained by automorphic tensor product of two cuspidal automorphic representations of
{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})
with highest weights
\left(\dfrac{k_{1}-1}{2},~\dfrac{1-k_{1}}{2}\right)
and
\left(\dfrac{k_{2}}{2}-1,~1-\dfrac{k_{2}}{2}\right)
, respectively. (If
k_{1},~k_{2}
are of same parity, we consider the half-integral twist of the automorphic tensor product of two representations
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
of
{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})
with suitable highest weight depending on
k_{1},k_{2}
are even or odd).

•

Let
C^{0}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(N_{1},N_{2})
denote the set of ordered pairs
(f_{1},f_{2})
of all normalized cusp eigenforms
f_{1},f_{2}
of Hecke operators of
\Gamma_{1}(N_{1})
of weight
k_{1}
and
\Gamma_{1}(N_{2})
of weight
k_{2}
respectively such that both
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are dihedral, and are obtained by automorphic induction from Hecke characters of (same) imaginary quadratic extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Note that,
C^{0}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(N_{1},N_{2})\subseteq C_{k_{1}}(N_{1})\times C_{k_{2}}(N_{2})
(see the Sec.
2.2
for notations). Hence, if
N_{1}=p^{n_{1}}\text{ and }N_{2}=p^{n_{2}}
, using Thm.
2.2.2
we have,

|C^{0}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(p^{n_{1}},p^{n_{2}})|\ll_{\epsilon}p^{n_{1}+n_{2}+2+2\epsilon}\text{ as }p\longrightarrow\infty.

•

Let
C^{\prime}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(N_{1},N_{2})
denote the set of ordered pairs
(g_{1},g_{2})
of normalized cusp eigenforms
g_{1},~g_{2}
of Hecke operators of
\Gamma_{1}(N_{1})
of weight
k_{1}
and
\Gamma_{1}(N_{2})
of weight
k_{2}
respectively such that
g_{1},~g_{2}
are both non-dihedral and one of them is the twist of the other by some idéle class character of
{\mathbb{Q}}
.

Note that under the assumption that
k_{1}>k_{2},
the set
C^{\prime}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(N_{1},N_{2})
is indeed an empty set.

5.2.
The main theorem

Theorem 5.2.1
.

Let
k_{1}>k_{2}\geq 2
be two integers. Then for any integer
N>24
,

|\mathcal{T}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(p^{N})|\gg_{k_{1},~k_{2}}~p^{2\lfloor N/4\rfloor}\text{ as the prime }p\longrightarrow\infty.

Proof.

We first consider the
different parity
case where
k_{1}+k_{2}
is odd,
k_{1}
is odd and
k_{2}
is even. Let
p
be an odd prime. Suppose
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
are cuspidal automorphic representations of
{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})
corresponding to the level structure
K_{f}^{2}(p^{n_{1}})
and
K_{f}^{2}(p^{n_{2}})
, respectively, with
n_{1},n_{2}\geq 1
and of weights
\left(\dfrac{k_{1}-1}{2},\dfrac{1-k_{1}}{2}\right)
and
\left(\dfrac{k_{2}}{2}-1,~1-\dfrac{k_{2}}{2}\right)
, respectively.

Using Thm.
3.2.1
, we conclude that exactly one of the following holds:

•

(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\in C^{\prime}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(p^{n_{1}},p^{n_{2}})

•

(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\in C^{0}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(p^{n_{1}},p^{n_{2}})

•

\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}\in\bigcup\limits_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{T}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(p^{n})
.

Let
\pi_{1}=\bigotimes_{q\leq\infty}\pi_{1,q}
and
\pi_{2}=\bigotimes_{q\leq\infty}\pi_{2,q}
. We know that
\pi_{1,p}
(resp
\pi_{2,p}
) corresponds to a unique newform in
S_{k_{1}}^{\rm{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(p^{s_{1}}))
(resp.
S_{k_{2}}^{\rm{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(p^{s_{2}}))
) with
1\leq s_{1}\leq n_{1}
(resp
1\leq s_{2}\leq n_{2}
). Note that,
c(\pi_{1,p})=s_{1}
and
c(\pi_{2,p})=s_{2}
.

We want to count the contributions of supercuspidal representations
\pi_{i,p},i=1,2
to
\bigcup\limits_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{T}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(p^{n})
. Using Lem.
3.3.3
, we can conclude that two newforms in
S_{k_{1}}^{\rm{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(p^{s_{1}}))
and
S_{k_{2}}^{\rm{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(p^{s_{2}}))
that are obtained from the supercuspidal representations
\pi_{1,p}
and
\pi_{2,p}
respectively, can contribute to
\mathcal{T}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(p^{n})
only if

2\leq n\leq 4c(\pi_{1,p})+4c(\pi_{2,p})-12.

Since
1\leq s_{i}\leq n_{i},i=1,2
, we have,

2\leq n\leq 4n_{1}+4n_{2}-12.

Note that,
n_{i}\geq 2
since
s_{i}\geq 2
as
\pi_{i,p}
is supercuspidal,
i=1,2
. Hence, we conclude that, automorphic tensor product of any pair of supercuspidal representations in the set

\bigoplus\limits_{1\leq i\leq n_{1}}S_{k_{1}}^{\text{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(p^{i}))\times\bigoplus\limits_{1\leq i\leq n_{2}}S_{k_{2}}^{\text{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(p^{i}))~\setminus~\{C^{0}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(p^{n_{1}},p^{n_{2}})\cup C^{\prime}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(p^{n_{1}},p^{n_{2}})\}

is also in the set
\mathcal{T}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(p^{4n_{1}+4n_{2}-12})
.

Since we have the following bound

\sum\limits_{1\leq i\leq n_{1}}{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{C}}S_{k}^{\text{new}}(\Gamma_{1}(p^{i}))\gg_{k}p^{2n_{1}}

and we also have

|C^{0}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(p^{n_{1}},p^{n_{2}})\cup C^{\prime}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(p^{n_{1}},p^{n_{2}})|\ll_{k_{1},~k_{2}}p^{n_{1}+n_{2}+4},

which is negligible compared to
p^{2n_{1}+2n_{2}}
for
p\longrightarrow\infty
and
n_{1}+n_{2}>4
, we conclude that

p^{2n_{1}+2n_{2}}\ll_{k_{1},~k_{2}}|\mathcal{T}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(p^{4n_{1}+4n_{2}-12})|\text{ as }p\longrightarrow\infty\text{ and }n_{1}+n_{2}>4.

In particular, taking
N=4(n_{1}+n_{2})
with
N>24
, we have,

|\mathcal{T}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(p^{N})|\gg_{k_{1},~k_{2}}p^{2\lfloor N/4\rfloor}\text{ as }p\longrightarrow\infty.

The
same parity
case where
k_{1}+k_{2}
is even can be handled in an exactly same manner by considering
(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})\otimes|\cdot|^{1/2}
instead of
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
.
∎

Using Lem.
2.1.1
we have an upper bound as stated below.

Corollary 5.2.2
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

|\mathcal{T}_{k_{1},~k_{2}}(p^{N})|\ll_{k_{1},~k_{2}}~p^{2N}\text{ as }p\longrightarrow\infty.

6.
Possible Overlap between symmetric cube and automorphic tensor product representations of
{\rm GL}_{4}

It is natural to investigate the overlap between the two types of contributions to the cohomology of
{\rm GL}_{4}
, the symmetric cube of a representation of
{\rm GL}_{2}
and the automorphic tensor product of two representations of
{\rm GL}_{2}
.

6.1.
Comparison of representations at infinity

We first describe the representations at infinity. Now suppose
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}
are integers
\geq 2
,
k_{1}>k_{2}
and they are weights of cusp forms corresponding to three automorphic representations
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}
of
{\rm GL}_{2}
, respectively.

From the discussion in Sec.
4.2
, we conclude that for a half-integer
s
,

(2)

((\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})\otimes|\cdot|^{s})_{\infty}=\begin{cases}J(0,\ell)\otimes|\cdot|_{\infty}^{s}\quad&\text{if}~k_{1}+k_{2}~\text{is odd}\\
J(1,\ell)\otimes|\cdot|_{\infty}^{s-1/2}&\text{if}~k_{1}+k_{2}~\text{is even}\end{cases}

where in both cases,

\ell=(k_{1}+k_{2}-2,~k_{1}-k_{2},~k_{2}-k_{1},~-k_{1}-k_{2}+2).

On the other hand,
({\rm Sym^{3}}(\pi_{3})\otimes|\cdot|^{s})_{\infty}
is given by
[
12
, Thm 5.5]

(3)

({\rm Sym^{3}}(\pi_{3})\otimes|\cdot|^{s})_{\infty}=\begin{cases}J(1,\ell^{\prime})\otimes|\cdot|_{\infty}^{s-1/2}\quad&\text{if}~k_{3}~\text{is odd}\\
J(0,\ell^{\prime})\otimes|\cdot|_{\infty}^{s}&\text{if}~k_{3}~\text{is even}\end{cases}

where in both cases,

\ell^{\prime}=(3k_{3}-3,~k_{3}-1,~1-k_{3},~3-3k_{3}).

Refer to Sec.
4.1
for the discussion of cohomological representations
J(w,\ell)
. It follows that for a half-integer
s
(i.e.
2s\in{\mathbb{Z}}
), the twisted representation
J(w,\ell)\otimes|\cdot|^{s}_{\infty}
is cohomological iff
s\in{\mathbb{Z}}
(i.e.
2s
is even) and in that case,

(4)

J(w,\ell)\otimes|\cdot|^{s}_{\infty}=J(w+2s,\ell)

We analyze all possible scenarios as follows.

(a)

Suppose
k_{1}+k_{2}
and
k_{3}
are even. Let
s
be a half-integer (i.e.
2s\in{\mathbb{Z}}
). Then from (
2
) and (
4
), we conclude that
(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})\otimes|\cdot|^{s}
is cohomoloigcal if and only if
2s
is odd, and in that case it equals
J(2s,\ell)
.
Since
{\rm Sym^{3}}(\pi_{3})_{\infty}=J(0,\ell^{\prime})
, we conclude that
{\rm Sym^{3}}(\pi_{3})\otimes|\cdot|^{s}\neq\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
for any half integer
s
.

(b)

Suppose
k_{1}+k_{2}
and
k_{3}
are odd. Let
s
be a half-integer (i.e.
2s\in{\mathbb{Z}}
). Then from (
3
) and (
4
) we conclude that

{\rm Sym^{3}}(\pi_{3})\otimes|\cdot|^{s}
is cohomoloigcal if and only if
2s
is odd, and in that case it equals
J(2s,\ell^{\prime})
. Since

(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})_{\infty}=J(0,\ell)
, we conclude that
{\rm Sym^{3}}(\pi_{3})\otimes|\cdot|^{s}\neq\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
for any half integer
s
.

(c)

Suppose
k_{1}+k_{2}
and
k_{3}
are of different parity and
k_{1}
is even. Thus
k_{2},k_{3}
have different parity. From (
2
) and (
3
), we conclude that:

((\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})\otimes|\cdot|^{s})_{\infty}={\rm Sym^{3}}(\pi_{3})_{\infty}
for some half integer
s
(i.e.
2s\in{\mathbb{Z}}
) if and only if
s=0
and
\ell=\ell^{\prime}
.

If
\ell=\ell^{\prime}
, then we have

\frac{k_{1}-1}{2}=k_{2}=k_{3}

This is impossible since we assumed that
k_{1}
is even and
k_{2},k_{3}
are of different parity. So we conclude that
{\rm Sym^{3}}(\pi_{3})\otimes|\cdot|^{s}\neq\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
for any half integer
s
.

(d)

Suppose
k_{1}+k_{2}
and
k_{3}
are of different parity and
k_{1}
is odd. Thus
k_{2},k_{3}
are of same parity. Similar to the previous case, we have the following necessary conditions for
{\rm Sym^{3}}(\pi_{3})\otimes|\cdot|^{s}=\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
for some half integer
s
(i.e.
2s\in{\mathbb{Z}}
):

•

\ell=\ell^{\prime}
, i.e.
\dfrac{k_{1}-1}{2}=k_{2}=k_{3}

•

s=0

This case is inconclusive and we can not rule out the possibility of an overlap using the method of comparing the representations at infinity.

6.2.
Proof of ‘lack of overlap’

Now we prove that the overlap is in fact impossible up to any twist by a Hecke character of
{\rm GL}_{1}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
under suitable conditions on the representations being cuspidal and cohomological.

Proposition 6.2.1
.

Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\pi_{3}
be three cuspidal automorphic representations of
{{\rm GL}}_{2}{(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})}
such that they are given by cusp forms of weights
k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\geq 2
and
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}
and
{{\rm Sym}}^{3}{(\pi_{3})}
are cuspidal automorphic representations of
{{\rm GL}}_{4}{(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathbb{Q}}})}
.

Then there is no Hecke character of
{\rm GL}_{1}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
such that

\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}={\rm Sym}^{3}{(\pi_{3})}\otimes\chi
.

Proof.

Suppose
\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2}={\rm Sym}^{3}{(\pi_{3})}\otimes\chi
for some Hecke character
\chi
. This gives

(5)

\Lambda^{2}(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})=\Lambda^{2}({\rm Sym}^{3}{(\pi_{3})}\otimes\chi)

Let
\omega_{\pi_{i}}
denote the central character of
\pi_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
. From (
[
3
]
, Proposition 3.1),
[
10
]
, we have the following isobaric decompositions of the exterior square of representations.

(6)

\begin{split}\Lambda^{2}(\pi_{1}\boxtimes\pi_{2})&~=~({\rm Sym}^{2}{(\pi_{1})}\otimes\omega_{\pi_{2}})\text{ }\boxplus\text{ }({\rm Sym}^{2}{(\pi_{2})}\otimes\omega_{\pi_{1}})\\
\Lambda^{2}({\rm Sym}^{3}{(\pi_{3})}\otimes\chi)&~=~({\rm Sym}^{4}{(\pi_{3})}\otimes\chi^{2}\omega_{\pi_{3}})\text{ }\boxplus\text{ }\chi^{2}\omega_{\pi_{3}}^{3}\end{split}

Combining the two equations (
5
) and (
6
), we get

(7)

~{\rm Sym}^{2}{(\pi_{1})}\otimes\omega_{\pi_{2}}\otimes(\chi^{2}\omega_{\pi_{3}}^{3})^{-1}\text{ }\boxplus\text{ }{\rm Sym}^{2}{(\pi_{2})}\otimes\omega_{\pi_{1}}\otimes(\chi^{2}\omega_{\pi_{3}}^{3})^{-1}~=~{\rm Sym}^{4}{(\pi_{3})}\otimes\omega_{\pi_{3}}^{-2}\text{ }\boxplus\text{ }1

We assumed that
{\rm Sym}^{3}(\pi_{3})
is cuspidal. If
{\rm Sym}^{4}(\pi_{3})
is not cuspidal, then using Langlands-Tunnel theorem (see
[
7
, Thm.1.3, Gelbart’s article]
), we conclude that
\pi_{3}
must have come from a cusp form of weight one and this already contradicts the assumption on the weight for
\pi_{3}
i.e.,
k_{3}\geq 2
. Thus
{\rm Sym}^{4}(\pi_{3})
is cuspidal.

Thus the first term
{\rm Sym}^{4}{(\pi_{3})}\otimes\omega_{\pi_{3}}^{-2}
on RHS in (
7
) is a representation of
{\rm GL}_{5}({\mathbb{A}}_{\mathbb{Q}})
and it will not split further into an isobaric sum with respect to lower dimensional
{\rm GL}_{n^{\prime}}
. We get a contradiction as there is no
{\rm GL}_{5}
-isobaric term on the LHS of Eq. (
7
).

∎

Remark 6.2.2
.

The authors of this manuscript established lower bound estimates on the contribution of symmetric cube transfer representations to the cohomology of the
{\rm GL}_{4}
in
[
4
]
. Prop.
6.2.1
establishes ‘lack of overlap between symmetric cube and automorphic tensor product’ and hence the estimates obtained in this manuscript improve the lower bound on the total contribution of cuspidal automorphic representations to the cohomology of the
{\rm GL}_{4}
. The case of Asai transfer from
{{\rm GL}_{2}}|_{{\mathbb{K}}}
for some imaginary quadratic extension
{\mathbb{K}}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
is remaining to be explored. One can ask these questions for the
{\rm GL}_{4}
over a general number field and it will be interesting to know whether the similar estimates hold in that case also.

Small deviations of Gaussian multiplicative chaos and the free energy of the two-dimensional massless Sinh–Gordon model
Nikolay Barashkov, Joona Oikarinen, Mo Dick Wong

Abstract
We prove a global decomposition result for log \log -correlated Gaussian fields on the d d -dimensional torus and use this to derive new small deviations bounds for a class of Gaussian multiplicative chaos measures obtained from Gaussian fields with zero spatial mean on the d d -dimensional torus. The upper bound is obtained by a modification of the method that was used in [ LRV22 ] , and the lower bound is obtained by applying the Donsker–Varadhan variational formula. We also give the probabilistic path integral formulation of the massless Sinh–Gordon model on a torus of side length R R , and study its partition function as R R tends to infinity. We apply the small deviation bounds for Gaussian multiplicative chaos to obtain lower and upper bounds for the logarithm of the partition function, leading to the existence of a non-zero and finite subsequential infinite volume limit for the free energy.



1
Introduction

1.1
Decomposition of
\log
-correlated Gaussian fields

Let
Z
be a log-correlated centred Gaussian process on an open domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with covariance kernel

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z(z)Z(w)]

\displaystyle=\log\frac{1}{|z-w|}+h(z,w)\,,

where
h
is a continuous function. Often one would prefer to work with a field with good scaling properties. To this end, there exists several works on decompositions of
\log
-correlated Gaussian fields of the form

\displaystyle Z=X+H

where
X
is an almost
\star
-scale invariant field and
H
is Hölder continuous. In
[
JSW19
]
the authors show that a non-degenerate
\log
-correlated Gaussian field with some regularity assumptions can locally be decomposed in this way. They also prove a global decomposition result without independence of the two fields
X
and
H
. In
[
AJJ22
]
the decomposition in the case when
Z
and
H
are independent was generalised to be almost global in the sense that the decomposition holds on any compact subset of
D
. In this article we obtain a global decomposition of this type for
\log
-correlated fields defined on the
d
-dimensional torus. As an application of the decomposition, we prove small deviations estimates for Gaussian multiplicative chaos measures.

1.2
Small deviations of Gaussian multiplicative chaos measures

The Gaussian multiplicative chaos (GMC) measure corresponding to the
\log
-correlated Gaussian field
Z
with parameter
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
is formally defined as the exponential of
X

\displaystyle M_{Z,\gamma}(dz)

\displaystyle=e^{\gamma Z(z)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[Z(z)^{2}]}\,dz\,.

Such measures were initially studied by Kahane
[
Kah85
]
, and resurfaced again in
[
RoVa10
,
DuSh11
]
. Later the theory was heavily applied in the path integral construction of Liouville conformal field theory
[
DKRV16
]
. Some basic properties of GMC measures were already studied by Kahane, including universality and the existence of all negative and some positive moments of the total mass of the measure. Later, large deviations of GMC measures was understood in the works
[
RoVa19
,
Won20
,
Won19
]
.

The small deviations behaviour of GMC measures is not yet fully understood. The previous works
[
Nik13
,
TaWi24
]
concern a case where the GMC measure is built out of a Gaussian field with non-zero spatial average. From the point of view of small deviations this setting is simpler, as fluctuations of the spatial average of the underlying Gaussian field dominate the small deviations behaviour. Some results exist also in the case of fields with zero spatial average, including
[
LRV22
]
, although the upper bound derived there is not quite optimal. In the case of the one-dimensional GMC measure constructed from the trace of the two-dimensional Gaussian free field, small deviations results follow from the results in
[
Rem20
,
ReZh20
]
, where an explicit formula for the probability distribution of the GMC mass was obtained via CFT methods. Negative moments of GMC measures built out of the Gaussian free field on fractal sets were considered in
[
GHSS18
]
.

In this article we prove new upper and lower bounds for a class of GMC measures obtained from Gaussian fields with zero spatial average. We apply these estimates to the Sinh–Gordon model to obtain bounds for the free energy.

1.3
Exponentially interacting quantum field theories and Gaussian multiplicative chaos

In recent years there has been tremendous progress in applying the theory of GMC measures to path integral constructions of exponentially interacting quantum field theories. Most notable progress has happened in Liouville theory, see the recent surveys
[
GKR24
,
ChWi24
]
, but GMC methods have also been applied to (non-affine) Toda field theories
[
CRV23
]
, imaginary Liouville theory
[
GKR23
,
Cha25
,
UGRS25
]
and the
\mathbb{H}^{3}
-Wess–Zumino–Witten model
[
GKR25
]
.

The exponentially interacting two-dimensional quantum field theories include several interesting models: the Liouville theory is a relatively simple CFT with a continuous spectrum, the compactified imaginary Liouville theory is an example of a logarithmic CFT, the Sine–Gordon, Sinh–Gordon and Bullough–Dodd models are simple examples of integrable quantum field theories. Due to their physical relevance, these models have been studied extensively in the physics and mathematics literature.

In the physics literature, the form factors of the Sinh–Gordon model were studied in
[
KoMu93
,
FMS93
]
, and exact formulas were obtained in
[
Zam95
,
FLZZ98
,
Zam06
,
Tes08
]
. Recently, the nature of the self-duality of the Sinh–Gordon model has been investigated in
[
KLM21
,
BLC22
,
Til22
]
. Rigorous results on the infinite volume limit of the Sinh–Gordon model with a mass term have been obtained in
[
FrPa77
,
AH74
,
BV21
]
. In
[
GGV24
]
the model was constructed without a mass term on the infinite cylinder by studying the spectral theory of the Hamiltonian of the model. See also
[
Koz21
,
Koz23
]
for rigorous results in the (
1+1
)-dimensional setting.

In the present article we focus on the Sinh–Gordon model, which is a two-dimensional exponentially interacting theory obtained by perturbing the Liouville model. The results obtained would also hold for the Bullough–Dodd model, but we do not explicitly include it to keep the notation simpler. The Sinh–Gordon and Bullough–Dodd models are the only integrable perturbations of Liouville theory, see
[
Mus
,
Dor98
]
. We start by constructing the path integral in finite volume, and then study the behaviour of the partition function in the infinite volume limit. By using the small deviations bounds for GMC measures, we obtain upper and lower bounds for (the logarithm of) the partition function, from which we obtain the existence of non-zero and finite subsequential limit for the free energy of the model in the infinite volume limit.

1.4
Main results

Our first result is a decomposition result into a sum of an almost
\star
-scale invariant field and a regular field for non-degenerate
\log
-correlated Gaussian fields with suitable Sobolev regularity.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
Z:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-degenerate Gaussian field on the
d
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
with the covariance kernel

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z(z)Z(w)]

\displaystyle=-\log d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z,w)+h(z,w)\,,

with
h\in H^{d+s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
for some
s>0
and
d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
being the distance function on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
. Then there exists
0\;\leqslant\;t<\infty
and Gaussian fields
X^{t}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
and
H:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, independent of each other, such that

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=X^{t}+H

and the fields
X^{t}
has the covariance kernel (see Section
2.1.2
for definitions)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X^{t}(z)X^{t}(w)]

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{\infty}\rho(e^{u}d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z,w))(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du\,,

and
H
is Hölder continuous almost surely.

Remark 1.2
.

In Proposition
2.5
we prove an analogous decomposition result, where the almost
\star
-scale invariant field is replaced with a Gaussian field with covariance operator
(-\Delta^{-\frac{d}{2}}+\Delta^{-\frac{d}{2}-\xi}){P_{>N}}
, where
\Delta^{-1}
is the inverse of the zero-mean Laplace operator on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
and
{P_{>N}}
is the projection onto functions with Fourier transform supported outside of
B(0,N)\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Our second result concerns the probability for the total mass of a GMC measure to be very small.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
Z
be as in Theorem
1.1
and denote

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}(z)

\displaystyle:=Z(z)-\frac{1}{v_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}Z(z)\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\,.

Then for all
\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2d})
there exists
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
the GMC measure
M_{\widetilde{Z},\gamma}
corresponding to
\widetilde{Z}
satisfies

\displaystyle\exp\big(-c_{1}(d,\gamma)\varepsilon^{-\frac{2d}{\gamma^{2}}}\big)\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{P}(M_{\widetilde{Z},\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{d})<\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\exp\big(-c_{2}(d,\gamma)\varepsilon^{-\frac{2d}{\gamma^{2}}}\big)\,.

for some
c_{1}(d,\gamma),c_{2}(d,\gamma)>0
.

Remark 1.4
.

Removing the average of the field is crucial for obtaining the upper bound, as otherwise fluctuations of the average of the field would make the probability of the GMC being small much larger. As a result, the lower bound would be trivial without removing the average.

The third result concerns the large volume behaviour of the Sinh–Gordon partition function, formally given on the two-dimensional
R
-torus
{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}
by the path integral

\displaystyle Z_{R}

\displaystyle=\int\exp\big(-\int_{{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}}(\tfrac{1}{2}|d\varphi|_{R}^{2}+2\mu\cosh(\gamma\varphi))dv_{R}\big)\,D\varphi\,.

The rigorous definition of this object will be given in Section
4
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For any
\gamma\in(0,2)
, there exists positive constants
f_{\gamma}
and
\widetilde{f}_{\gamma}
such that for any
R
large enough we have that

\displaystyle f_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\frac{-\log Z_{R}}{\mu^{\frac{2}{\gamma Q}}R^{2}}\;\leqslant\;\widetilde{f}_{\gamma}\,,

where
Q=\frac{2}{\gamma}+\frac{\gamma}{2}
.

Remark 1.6
.

In the case of the zero-mean hierarchical free field (or branching random walk) Hofstetter and Zeitouni
[
HZ24
, Theorem 1.5]
independently obtained a stronger version of our results.
They are able to show that, in the notation of Theorem
1.3
,
\widetilde{c}_{\gamma}=c_{\gamma}(1+\mathchoice{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{O}}}{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{O}}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{O}}}{\scalebox{.7}{$\scriptscriptstyle\mathcal{O}$}}(1))
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. This also translates into a statement that the limit

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\log Z_{R}}{R^{2}}
exists, see below for a discussion of this problem. Furthermore in
[
HZ24
]
the authors study correlation function of the zero-mean Liouville model, and obtain decay of correlations, leveraging knowledge about how small deviations of the GMC are achieved.
Such results would also be of interest the setting of the zero-mean Gaussian free field.

1.5
Outlook

We have demonstrated the possibility to study properties of the infinite volume limit of the massless Sinh–Gordon model via GMC methods. It is then natural to try to expand these methods to study the Sinh–Gordon model further. Given the bounds we derive for the logarithm of the partition function, it is then expected that the limit

\displaystyle F_{\gamma}

\displaystyle:=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{-\log Z_{R}}{R^{2}}

exists. This quantity is called the
free energy
in physics literature, and an explicit formula for it has been conjectured, see for example
[
FLZZ98
,
Til22
]
and references therein. As the partition function
Z_{R}
will behave essentially like a Laplace transform

\displaystyle Z_{R}

\displaystyle\sim\mathbb{E}[e^{-R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\sqrt{M_{\widetilde{X}_{1},\gamma}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})M_{\widetilde{X}_{1},-\gamma}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})}}]\,,

an application of De Bruijn’s Tauberian theorem then leads to the conjecture that the limit

\displaystyle C_{\gamma}

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}}\log\mathbb{P}\big(\sqrt{M_{\widetilde{X}_{1},\gamma}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})M_{\widetilde{X}_{1},-\gamma}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})}\less\varepsilon\big)

exists and is non-zero. The Tauberian theorem also gives an explicit relation between
F_{\gamma}
and
C_{\gamma}
, and thus the conjectural formula for
F_{\gamma}
also yields a conjectural explicit formula for the small deviations constant
C_{\gamma}
. In this way the integrability of the Sinh–Gordon model are related to integrability properties of Gaussian multiplicative chaos.

Beyond the partition function, the integrability of the Sinh–Gordon model is also supposed to lead to an explicit formula for the expected value of the vertex operator

\displaystyle\langle e^{\alpha\varphi(0)}\rangle

\displaystyle:=\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{\int e^{\alpha\varphi(0)}\exp\big(-\int_{{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}}(\tfrac{1}{2}|d\varphi|_{R}^{2}+2\mu\cosh(\gamma\varphi))dv_{R}\big)\,D\varphi}{Z_{R}}\,.

Thus, showing the existence of the above limit is another natural open question. Beyond this, major open problems include the existence of a mass-gap in the infinite volume limit and understanding the integrability of the model in a rigorous way. We also mention that it would be interesting to study the model at the critical value
\gamma=2
. In
[
BLC22
,
Til22
]
the self-dual nature of the Sinh–Gordon model has been discussed, and the authors are not fully certain if the model behaves the same in the
\gamma\in(0,\sqrt{2})
and
\gamma\in[\sqrt{2},2)
ranges. From the point of view of GMC theory, and our paper, there does not seem to be a big difference between these two ranges. Our analysis does not include the critical case
\gamma=2
. It is speculated in
[
BLC22
]
that the infinite volume limit might be massless at the critical value.

Decomposition of logarithmically correlated Gaussian fields

2.1
Preliminaries

2.1.1
Notations

Let
\mathbb{T}^{d}:=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be the
d
-dimensional unit torus. It inherits the flat Riemannian metric from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and we denote the corresponding volume form by
v_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
. The distance function
d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, is given by
d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z,w)=|(z-w)\mod 1|
, with the convention that for
z\in\mathbb{T}^{d}

\displaystyle z\mod 1:=(z_{1}\mod 1,\ldots,z_{d}\mod 1)\in(-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}]^{d}\,.

(2.1)

The
L^{2}
-Sobolev spaces are defined for
s>0
as

\displaystyle H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

\displaystyle:=\Big\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d}):\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(1+|k|^{2})^{s}|{\hat{f}}(k)|^{2}<\infty\Big\}\,,

where
{\hat{f}}
denotes the Fourier transform of
f
. For
s>0
we define the
\|\cdot\|_{H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}
-norm of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\frac{|\hat{f}(k)|^{2}}{(1+|k|^{2})^{s}}\,,

which is always finite for
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
-functions.

Let
{P_{>N}}:L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
denote the projections on functions with Fourier support outside a ball of radius
N
, that is,

\displaystyle{P_{>N}}f(x)

\displaystyle:=\underset{|n|>N}{\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}}\hat{f}(n)e^{2\pi in\cdot x}\,.

We will denote
{P_{<N}}:=I-{P_{>N}}
, where
I
is the identity operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

The space of continuous functions on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is denoted by
C(\mathbb{T}^{d})
, the Fourier transform of a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by
\mathcal{F}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}[f]
and the measure of a set
D\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
by
|D|:=\int_{D}dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
.

Remark 2.1

(Translation invariant kernels)
.

Let
k\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
and define
K:L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
by

\displaystyle Kf(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}k(x-y)f(y)\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(y)\,.

Then we have that

\displaystyle(\widehat{Kf})(n)

\displaystyle={\hat{k}}(n){\hat{f}}(n)\,,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\,,

that is,
K
acts diagonally in Fourier space. This implies that
K
commutes with
{P_{<N}}
and
{P_{>N}}
.

2.1.2
Logarithmically correlated Gaussian fields

Let
C:\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a symmetric positive definite function satisfying

\displaystyle C(z,w)

\displaystyle=-\log d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z,w)+h(z,w)\,,\quad(z,w)\in\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\,,

(2.2)

where
h\in C(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Then it holds that

1.

There exists a Gaussian field
X
on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
with the covariance kernel
C
.

2.

X\in H^{-s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
almost surely for any
s>0
.

3.

The integral operator corresponding to the kernel
C
is a Hilbert–Schmidt operator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

For proofs of these facts, see Section 2 in
[
JSW20
]
. The Gaussian field
X
is called a
logarithmically correlated Gaussian field
, or
log-correlated field
for short, as Gaussianity will always be assumed. We will often use the abuse of notation

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X(z)X(w)]

\displaystyle:=C(z,w)\,,

even though the field
X
is not defined pointwise.

Important examples of logarithmically correlated Gaussian fields include different variants of the Gaussian free field in two dimensions, and the so-called
\star
-scale invariant fields. The latter are usually defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
by the covariance kernel

\displaystyle C_{\infty}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho(e^{u}(z-w))\,du\,,\quad(z,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\,,

(2.3)

where
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
is a positive definite function satisfying the following properties

1.

\rho(0)=1
,

2.

\rho
is rotationally symmetric
\rho(x)=\rho((|x|,0,\ldots,0))
,

3.

\rho
is supported in the ball
B(0,\frac{1}{2})\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

4.

\rho\in H^{d+s}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
for some
s>0
.

We call
\rho
the seed covariance function. We also introduce the almost
\star
-scale invariant Gaussian fields with parameter
\xi\in(0,\infty)
, defined by the covariance kernel

\displaystyle C_{\xi}(z,w)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho(e^{u}(z-w))(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du\,,\quad(z,w)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

(2.4)

For
t>0
and
\xi\in(0,\infty]
wee will use the following notations

\displaystyle C_{\xi}^{t}(z,w)

\displaystyle:=\int_{t}^{\infty}\rho(e^{u}(z-w))(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du\,,

(2.5)

\displaystyle C_{\xi,t}(z,w)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\rho(e^{u}(z-w))(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du\,.

(2.6)

For
\xi\in(0,\infty)
we also define
R_{\xi}=C_{\infty}-C_{\xi}
with
R_{\xi,t}
and
R_{\xi}^{t}
defined as above. More details on
\star
-scale invariant fields can be found in
[
JSW19
,
AJJ22
]
.

Remark 2.2

(Almost
\star
-scale invariant fields on the torus)
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
be a seed covariance function. We can define the corresponding almost
\star
-scale invariant Gaussian field on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}=\mathbb{R}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by defining the covariance
C_{\xi}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle C_{\xi}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho\big(e^{u}(d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(x,y),0,\ldots,0)\big)(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du\,,

We define the kernels
C_{\xi}^{t}
and
C_{\xi,t}
similarly to (
2.5
). We will often slightly abuse the notation by writing
\rho(e^{u}(x-y))
instead of
\rho\big(e^{u}(d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(x,y),0,\ldots,0)
for
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Definition 2.3
.

We say the field
X
defined in (
2.2
) is
non-degenerate
if its covariance kernel is a positive definite function, meaning that for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}C(z,w)f(z)f(w)\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z)\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(w)>0\,.

(2.7)

As we assumed that
\rho
is a positive definite function, it follows that
C_{\infty}
,
C_{\xi}
and
R_{\xi}
for all
\xi\in(0,\infty)
are covariance kernels of a non-degenerate Gaussian fields, as well as the
t
-cutoff versions of these kernels.

2.1.3
Gaussian multiplicative chaos

For a logarithmically correlated Gaussian field
X:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, we define its Gaussian multiplicative chaos (GMC) measure on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
as

\displaystyle M_{X,\gamma}(dz)

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}e^{\gamma X_{\varepsilon}(z)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[X_{\varepsilon}(z)^{2}]}\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z)\,,

(2.8)

where
X_{\varepsilon}
is a mollification of
X
in scale
\varepsilon
and the limit exists weakly in probability for all
\gamma\in(-\sqrt{2d},\sqrt{2d})
. In the sequel we will sometimes denote GMC measures formally by

\displaystyle M_{X,\gamma}(dz)=e^{\gamma X(z)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[X(z)^{2}]}\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z)

without explicitly referring to mollifications and limits. For more details on GMC measures, see for example
[
BePo
,
RoVa14
]
.

2.2
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section we prove the decomposition result formulated in Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
Z:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be a non-degenerate logarithmically correlated Gaussian field with the covariance kernel

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z(z)Z(w)]

\displaystyle=-\log d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z,w)+h(z,w)\,,

with
h\in H^{d+s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
for some
s>0
. Let
\xi>0
. Then there exists
t>0
and a Gaussian field
H
, such that
H
is Hölder continuous almost surely, and

\displaystyle Z\overset{d}{=}X^{t}+H\,,

where
X^{t}
has covariance

\displaystyle C_{\xi}^{t}(z,w)=\int_{t}^{\infty}\rho\big(e^{u}(z-w)\big)(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du\,,

and the fields
X^{t}
and
H
are independent of each other.

Proof.

The covariance kernel of the
\star
-scale invariant field with seed covariance
\rho
can be written as

\displaystyle C_{\infty}(z,w)

\displaystyle=-\log d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z,w)+\int_{d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z,w)}^{1}\big(\rho(t)-1\big)\tfrac{dt}{t}=-\log d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z,w)+h_{\rho}(z,w)

where
h_{\rho}\in H^{d+s_{1}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
for some
s_{1}>0
(see Proposition 4.1. (vi) in
[
JSW19
]
). This implies that
C_{\infty}
satisfies the assumptions made in (
2.2
). We define
G:L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
by

\displaystyle Gf(x)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(h(z,w)-h_{\rho}(z,w))f(w)\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z)\,.

By the assumption we have
h\in H^{d+s_{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
for some
s_{2}>0
, so the integral kernel of
G
belongs to
H^{d+s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
where
s:=\min\{s_{1},s_{2}\}>0
. We denote the covariance kernel of
Z
by
C
. Thus, as integral operators, we have that

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=C_{\infty}+G\,.

Let

\displaystyle G^{<N}:={P_{<N}}G{P_{<N}}\qquad G^{>N}:={P_{>N}}G{P_{<N}}+{P_{<N}}G{P_{>N}}+{P_{>N}}G{P_{>N}}\,.

Now the covariance operator
C
decomposes as

\displaystyle C=C_{\infty}+G

\displaystyle=C_{\infty}^{t}+C_{\infty,t}+G

\displaystyle=C_{\infty}^{t}+C_{\infty,t}{P_{<N}}+C_{\infty,t}{P_{>N}}+G^{<N}+G^{>N}

\displaystyle=C_{\infty}^{t}+C_{\infty}{P_{<N}}-C_{\infty}^{t}{P_{<N}}+C_{\infty,t}{P_{>N}}+G^{<N}+G^{>N}\,.

(2.9)

As
C_{\infty}
is translation invariant, it commutes with
{P_{<N}}
and
{P_{>N}}
, so we can rewrite

\displaystyle C_{\infty}{P_{<N}}+G^{<N}

\displaystyle={P_{<N}}(C_{\infty}+G){P_{<N}}-\varepsilon R_{\xi}{P_{<N}}+\varepsilon R_{\xi}{P_{<N}}

\displaystyle={P_{<N}}C{P_{<N}}-\varepsilon R_{\xi}{P_{<N}}+\varepsilon R_{\xi}{P_{<N}}\,,

where
\varepsilon>0
,
\xi>0
and
R_{\xi}
is the integral operator with the kernel

\displaystyle R_{\xi}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho(e^{u}(z-w))e^{-\xi u}\,du\,.

We decompose
C^{t}_{\infty}
as

\displaystyle C_{\infty}^{t}

\displaystyle=C_{\xi}^{t}+R^{t}_{\xi}\,,

where
R_{\xi}^{t}
is the integral operator with the kernel

\displaystyle R_{\xi}^{t}(z,w)

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{\infty}\rho(e^{u}(z-w\textasciidieresis))e^{-\xi u}\,du\,.

Thus the covariance is decomposed as

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=C_{\xi}^{t}+{P_{<N}}(C-\varepsilon R_{\xi}){P_{<N}}+R_{\xi}^{t}+G^{>N}+(\varepsilon R_{\xi}-C_{\infty}^{t}){P_{<N}}+(C_{\xi,t}+R_{\xi,t}){P_{>N}}\,,

(2.10)

where in the second term we used translation invariance of the integral kernel of
R_{\xi}
to commute it with
{P_{<N}}
. We will show that
{P_{<N}}(C-\varepsilon R_{\xi}){P_{<N}}
is the covariance operator of a smooth Gaussian field and that
R_{\xi}^{t}+G^{>N}+(\varepsilon R_{\xi}-C_{\infty}^{t}){P_{<N}}+(C_{\xi,t}+R_{\xi,t}){P_{>N}}
is the covariance operator of a Gaussian field that almost surely belongs to
H^{\frac{d}{2}+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
for some
\alpha>0
once
N
,
t
and
\xi
are suitably chosen.

By Lemma
2.9
, there exists
\delta_{1}>0
such that for any

we have

\displaystyle\langle f,Cf\rangle>\delta_{1}\|f\|_{H^{-\frac{d}{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\,.

By Lemma
2.7
(ii) for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
we have

\displaystyle\langle f,R_{\xi}f\rangle

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\delta_{2}\|f\|_{H^{-d/2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\,.

Now for all

we get

\displaystyle\langle f,{P_{<N}}(C-\varepsilon R_{\xi}){P_{<N}}f\rangle

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;(\delta_{1}-\varepsilon\delta_{2})\|{P_{<N}}f\|_{H^{-d/2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\,.

This shows that for
\varepsilon<\frac{\delta_{1}}{\delta_{2}}
the operator
{P_{<N}}(C-\varepsilon R_{\xi}){P_{<N}}
is the covariance of a Gaussian field that almost surely belongs to
{P_{<N}}L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})\subset C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
.

The integral kernel of
R_{\xi}^{t}+G^{>N}+(\varepsilon R_{\xi}-C_{\infty}^{t}){P_{<N}}+(C_{\xi,t}+R_{\xi,t}){P_{>N}}
is Hölder continuous, as the integral kernel of

belongs to
H^{d+s}(\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d})
for some

and the covariance kernel of

is Hölder continuous by Proposition 4.1. (ii) in
[
JSW19
]
. Next we prove the positivity.

First, we use
C_{\xi,t}\;\geqslant\;0
,
R_{\xi}^{t}\;\geqslant\;0
and
\varepsilon<1

\displaystyle R_{\xi}^{t}+\varepsilon R_{\xi}{P_{<N}}+(C_{\xi,t}+R_{\xi,t}){P_{>N}}

\displaystyle=R_{\xi}^{t}+\varepsilon R_{\xi,t}{P_{<N}}+\varepsilon R_{\xi}^{t}{P_{<N}}+(C_{\xi,t}+R_{\xi,t}){P_{>N}}

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;R_{\xi}^{t}+\varepsilon R_{\xi,t}{P_{<N}}+R_{\xi,t}{P_{>N}}

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;\varepsilon R_{\xi}^{t}+\varepsilon R_{\xi,t}{P_{<N}}+\varepsilon R_{\xi,t}{P_{>N}}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon R_{\xi}\,.

Thus, it remains to show that the operator

is positive. By Lemma

(i), there exists a

such that

for all

. Lemmas
2.6
and
2.8
give upper bounds for

\displaystyle>\varepsilon\delta\|f\|_{H^{-d/2-\xi/2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}-ce^{-dt}N^{d+\xi}\|f\|_{H^{-d/2-\xi/2}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}-cN^{-2\alpha}\|f\|_{H^{-d/2-s/2+\alpha}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}^{2}\,,

where

. This leads to

for all

is a Hölder continuous and positive kernel, so it is the covariance kernel of a Hölder continuous Gaussian field.

3
Small deviations of Gaussian multiplicative chaos

Let
Z:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
a logarithmically correlated Gaussian field. Let
V\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
be a Borel set with positive Lebesgue measure and denote

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}(z)

\displaystyle:=Z(z)-\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}Z(z)\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z)\,.

(3.1)

In this section we use a slightly different convention than in (
2.8
), and we denote

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{Z,\gamma}(dz)

\displaystyle=e^{\gamma\widetilde{Z}(z)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[Z(z)^{2}]}\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z)\,,

(3.2)

that is, the normalization is with respect to
Z
instead of
\widetilde{Z}
. This does not make a big difference, but will be convenient. In this section we will derive upper and lower bounds for the probability
\mathbb{P}(\widetilde{M}_{Z,\gamma}(V)<\varepsilon)
.

3.1
Upper bound

Our starting point is the following lemma concerning functions with zero mean over some fixed set
D\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\alpha>0
and
D\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
be a Borel set with positive Lebesgue measure. Assume that
Z\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{D}Z\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle=0\,.

Fix
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that
8\frac{(2/e)^{\kappa}}{1-2/e}\less 1
.
Then one of the following holds

1.

There exists
\beta>0
(depending only on
\kappa
and not on
\alpha
) and a Borel set
B\subset D
with
|B|\;\geqslant\;\beta|D|
such that
Z\;\geqslant\;-\alpha
on
B
.

2.

There exists
n\;\geqslant\;\kappa
and a Borel set
A_{n}\subset D
with
|A_{n}|\;\geqslant\;e^{-n}|D|
such that
Z\;\geqslant\;4\alpha 2^{n}
on
A_{n}
.

Proof.

Assume that (1) does not hold. Then there must exist a set
B^{c}
such that
|B^{c}|\;\geqslant\;(1-\beta)|D|
and
Z\less-\alpha
on
B^{c}
. Let
B
be the complement of
B^{c}
. Then, by the zero-mean property,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|D|}\int_{B}Z\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{|D|}\int_{B^{c}}Z\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\;\geqslant\;(1-\beta)\alpha\,.

Now, if also (2) does not hold, then for all
n\;\geqslant\;\kappa
we have
|\{x\in D:Z\;\geqslant\;4\alpha 2^{n}\}|\less e^{-n}|D|
and thus

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|D|}\int_{B}Z\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\frac{4\alpha 2^{\kappa}}{|D|}|\{0\;\leqslant\;Z\;\leqslant\;4\alpha 2^{\kappa}\}|+\frac{1}{|D|}\sum_{n=\kappa}^{\infty}4\alpha 2^{n+1}|\{4\alpha 2^{n}\;\leqslant\;Z\;\leqslant\;4\alpha 2^{n+1}\}|

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;4\alpha 2^{\kappa}\beta+8\alpha\sum_{n=\kappa}^{\infty}2^{n}e^{-n}

\displaystyle=\alpha\left(4\beta 2^{\kappa}+8(\tfrac{2}{e})^{\kappa}\tfrac{1}{1-\frac{2}{e}}\right)\,.

Thus

\displaystyle(1-\beta)\alpha

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\alpha\left(4\beta 2^{\kappa}+8(\tfrac{2}{e})^{\kappa}\tfrac{1}{1-\frac{2}{e}}\right)\,.

As
\beta\to 0
, the left-hand side goes towards
\alpha
and the right-hand side towards
8\frac{(2/e)^{\kappa}}{1-2/e}\alpha
. Thus for
\kappa
suitably large such that
8\frac{(2/e)^{\kappa}}{1-2/e}\less 1
, we get a contradiction.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The small deviations estimate in
[
LRV22
]
uses a similar lemma. The main difference is that in our setting, the parameters
\beta
and
\kappa
will not depend on the parameter
\varepsilon
, which simplifies some parts of the argument. In
[
LRV22
]
this lemma is applied twice, after which a crude Gaussian estimate is used to terminate the calculation. Because our choice of
\beta
and
\kappa
will be independent of
\varepsilon
, we have to continue this iteration many more times (depending on how small
\varepsilon
is), after which we also terminate by a crude Gaussian estimate. This is one of the differences between the two approaches.

Next we give a proof of Theorem
1.3
assuming certain technical estimates, which we will prove afterwards.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
Z:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
a logarithmically correlated Gaussian field satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
2.4
and
V\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
a Borel set with positive measure. Let
\widetilde{Z}=Z-\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}Z\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
and

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{Z,\gamma}(V)

\displaystyle=\int_{V}e^{\widetilde{Z}(z)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[Z(z)^{2}]}\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z)\,.

Then there exists a constant
c_{\gamma}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\widetilde{M}_{Z,\gamma}(V)<\varepsilon\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\exp\big(-c_{\gamma}|V|\varepsilon^{-\frac{2d}{\gamma^{2}}}\big)\,.

In the case
V=\mathbb{T}^{d}
we also get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(M_{\widetilde{Z},\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{d})<\varepsilon\big)\;\leqslant\;\exp\big(-\widetilde{c}_{\gamma}e^{-\frac{2d}{\gamma^{2}}}\big)

for some
\widetilde{c}_{\gamma}>0
.

Proof.

We give the proof in the two-dimensional case
d=2
. The generalization to an arbitrary dimension is straightforward.

By Theorem (
2.4
), there exists
\mathbf{t}>0
and
\xi>0
such that we can decompose
Z
as a sum of two independent Gaussian fields

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=X^{\mathbf{t}}+H\,,

(3.3)

where
X^{\mathbf{t}}:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a
\log
-correlated Gaussian field with the covariance kernel

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X^{\mathbf{t}}(z)X^{\mathbf{t}}(w)]

\displaystyle=C^{\mathbf{t}}_{\xi}(z,w)=\int_{\mathbf{t}}^{\infty}\rho\big(e^{u}(z-w)\big)(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du

and
H:\mathbb{T}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is Hölder continuous almost surely. Note that
X^{\mathbf{t}}
has the property that
X^{\mathbf{t}}(z)
is independent of
X^{\mathbf{t}}(w)
if
d_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z,w)>e^{-\mathbf{t}}
.

For
t>\mathbf{t}
we will define the field
X_{t}
with covariance

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X_{t}(z)X_{t}(w)]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbf{t}}^{t}\rho\big(e^{u}(z-w)\big)(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du\,.

We denote

\displaystyle\widetilde{X}^{t}

\displaystyle:=X^{t}-\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}X^{t}\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\,,\quad\widetilde{X}_{t}:=X_{t}-\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}X_{t}\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\,.

Now we can decompose
\widetilde{X}^{\mathbf{t}}=\widetilde{X}_{t}+\widetilde{X}^{t}
for some
t>\mathbf{t}
, where
\widetilde{X}_{t}
is independent of
\widetilde{X}^{t}
. The independence implies that the GMC measure splits as

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{X^{\mathbf{t}},\gamma}(dz)

\displaystyle=e^{\gamma\widetilde{X}^{\mathbf{t}}(z)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[X^{\mathbf{t}}(z)^{2}]}\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z)=e^{\gamma\widetilde{X}_{t}(z)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[X_{t}(z)^{2}]}\widetilde{M}_{X^{t},\gamma}(dz)\,.

From now on we will denote

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}^{(t)}_{\gamma}(dz)

\displaystyle:=\widetilde{M}_{X^{t},\gamma}(dz)\,.

By using the decomposition of
Z
(
3.3
), we write the measure
\widetilde{M}_{Z,\gamma}
as

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{Z,\gamma}(V)

\displaystyle=\int_{V}e^{\gamma(\widetilde{X}_{t}(z)+\widetilde{H}(z))-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}(\mathbb{E}[X_{t}(z)^{2}]+\mathbb{E}[H(z)^{2}])}\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t)}(dz)\,.

By
\mathbb{E}[X_{t}(z)^{2}]=\int_{0}^{t}(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du\;\leqslant\;t
we get that

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{Z,\gamma}(V)

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;c_{H}e^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}t}\int_{V}e^{\gamma(\widetilde{X}_{t}(z)+\widetilde{H}(z))}\widetilde{M}^{(t)}_{\gamma}(dz)\,.

(3.4)

where
c_{H}=e^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\sup_{V}\mathbb{E}[H^{2}]}
. As the covariance kernel of
H
is Hölder continuous on
\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}
(see proof of Theorem
2.4
), we have
c_{H}>0
.

Next we introduce some notation to set up things for applying Lemma
3.1
. For a tuple of natural numbers
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{j})\in\mathbb{N}^{j}
we denote

\displaystyle\bar{n}_{j}

\displaystyle:=(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{j})\,,

\displaystyle|\bar{n}_{j}|

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i=1}^{j}n_{i}\,.

We will also use the convention
\bar{n}_{0}:=0
. We also denote

\displaystyle t_{j}

\displaystyle:=t_{0}+|\bar{n}_{j}|\,,

where
t_{0}>\mathbf{t}
is a scale parameter depending on
\varepsilon
and
\gamma
to be fixed later. For
j\in\mathbb{N}
we define the events

\displaystyle E_{\bar{n}_{j},t_{0}}(A)

\displaystyle:=\{\exists\;B_{\bar{n}_{j}}\subset A:\;|B_{\bar{n}_{j}}|\;\geqslant\;\beta|A|\,,\;\bar{X}_{j}\;\geqslant\;-\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}\text{ on }B_{\bar{n}_{j}}\}\,,

\displaystyle F_{\bar{n}_{j+1},t_{0}}(A)

\displaystyle:=\{\exists\;A_{\bar{n}_{j+1}}\subset A:\;|A_{\bar{n}_{j+1}}|\;\geqslant\;e^{-n_{j+1}}|A|\,,\;\bar{X}_{j}\;\geqslant\;4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j+1}|}\text{ on }A_{\bar{n}_{j+1}}\}\,,

where
\beta>0
is arbitrary,
\alpha>0
will be fixed later,
\bar{X}_{0}=\widetilde{X}_{t_{0}}
and for
j\;\geqslant\;1

\displaystyle Y_{j}(A)

\displaystyle:=\tfrac{1}{|A|}\int_{A}(\widetilde{X}_{t_{j}}-\widetilde{X}_{t_{j-1}})\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{X}_{j}

\displaystyle:=(\widetilde{X}_{t_{j}}-\widetilde{X}_{t_{j-1}})-Y_{j}(A)\,.

Denote
S_{\gamma,\varepsilon}=\{\widetilde{M}_{Z,\gamma}(V)\;\leqslant\;\varepsilon\}
. We apply Lemma
3.1
to the function
\widetilde{X}_{t}+\widetilde{H}
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(S_{\gamma,\varepsilon}\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{P}(S_{\gamma,\varepsilon}\cap E_{0,t}(V))+\mathbb{P}(S_{\gamma,\varepsilon}\cap F_{\bar{n}_{1},t}(V))\,.

(3.5)

We choose
t=t_{0}
such that
e^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}t_{0}}\varepsilon=c_{H}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}
, where
\alpha,\beta>0
are the constants appearing in Lemma
3.1
, and
\varepsilon
small enough so that
t_{0}>\mathbf{t}
still holds. Note that
\beta
is fixed, but
\alpha
is arbitrary for now. By (
3.4
), the first term in (
3.5
) is bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(S_{\gamma,\varepsilon}\cap E_{0,t_{0}}(V))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\big(\big\{c_{H}e^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}t_{0}}\int_{V}e^{\gamma(\widetilde{X}_{t_{0}}(z)+\widetilde{H}(z))}\widetilde{M}^{(t_{0})}_{\gamma}(dz)\;\leqslant\;\varepsilon\big\}\cap E_{0,t_{0}}(V)\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{P}\big(\big\{c_{H}e^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}t_{0}}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{0})}(B_{0})\;\leqslant\;\varepsilon\big\}\cap E_{0,t_{0}}(V)\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\underset{|B_{0}|\;\geqslant\;\beta|V|}{\sup_{B_{0}\in\mathcal{B}(V)}}\mathbb{P}\big(\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{0})}(B_{0})\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}\big)\,,

where
\mathcal{B}(V)
is the set of Borel subsets of
V
. We have

\displaystyle\underset{|B_{0}|\;\geqslant\;\beta|V|}{\sup_{B_{0}\in\mathcal{B}(V)}}\mathbb{P}\big(\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{0})}(B_{0})\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\underset{|B_{0}|\;\geqslant\;\beta|V|}{\sup_{B_{0}\in\mathcal{B}(V)}}\mathbb{P}\big(\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{0})}(B_{0})\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{|B_{0}|}{2}\big)\,.

As we have
|B_{0}|\;\geqslant\;\beta|V|\;\geqslant\;e^{-2t_{0}}
for
t_{0}
large (i.e.
\varepsilon
small), we can bound this by the concentration inequality from Lemma
3.9
to obtain

\displaystyle\underset{|B_{0}|\;\geqslant\;\beta|V|}{\sup_{B_{0}\in\mathcal{B}(V)}}\mathbb{P}\big(\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{0})}(B_{0})\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{|B_{0}|}{2}\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\underset{|B_{0}|\;\geqslant\;\beta|V|}{\sup_{B_{0}\in\mathcal{B}(V)}}\exp\big(-c|B_{0}|e^{2t_{0}}\big)\;\leqslant\;\exp\big(-c\beta|V|e^{2t_{0}}\big)

for some
c>0
. To bound the second term in (
3.5
), we start by writing

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(S_{\gamma,\varepsilon}\cap F_{\bar{n}_{1},t_{0}}(V))

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{P}\big(\big\{e^{\gamma 4\alpha 2^{n_{1}}}\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{0})}(A_{1})\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\big\}\cap F_{\bar{n}_{1},t_{0}}(V)\big)\,.

By applying Lemma
3.7
we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\big\{e^{\gamma 4\alpha 2^{n_{1}}}\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{0})}(A_{1})\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\big\}\cap F_{\bar{n}_{1},t_{0}}(V)\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{P}\big(\big\{e^{\gamma 4\alpha 2^{n_{1}}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}n_{1}}e^{\gamma Y_{1}}\int_{A_{1}}e^{\gamma\bar{X}_{1}}d\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{1})}\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\big\}\cap F_{\bar{n}_{1},t_{0}}(V)\big)\,.

Now by, Lemma
3.4
there exists
A_{2}
and
n_{2}
such that

\displaystyle S_{\gamma,\varepsilon}\cap F_{\bar{n}_{1},t_{0}}(V)\subset

\displaystyle H(V,t_{0})\cup\{e^{\gamma 4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{2}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|\bar{n}_{2}|}e^{\gamma Y_{2}(A_{2})}\int_{A_{2}}e^{\gamma\bar{X}_{2}}d\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{2})}\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\}\,,

where
H(V,t_{0})
is an event satisfying

\mathbb{P}(H(V,t_{0}))\;\leqslant\;C_{1}\exp(-C_{2}\alpha^{2}|V|(\tfrac{4}{e})^{|\bar{n}_{1}|}e^{2t_{0}})+C_{1}\exp\big(-C_{2}|V|e^{2t_{0}+|\bar{n}_{1}|}\big)\,.

Now, by iterating the application of Lemma
3.4
, we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(S_{\gamma,\varepsilon}\cap F_{\bar{n}_{1},t_{0}}(V))

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C\sum_{j=1}^{J-1}\Big(\exp\big(-c|V|e^{2t_{0}+|\bar{n}_{j}|}\big)+\exp\big(-c|V|(\tfrac{4}{e})^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}e^{2(t_{0}+|\bar{n}_{j-1}|)}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\underset{|A_{J}|\;\geqslant\;e^{-|\bar{n}_{J}|}|V|}{\sup_{A_{J}\in\mathcal{B}(V)}}\mathbb{P}\big(e^{\gamma 4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{J}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|\bar{n}_{J}|}e^{\gamma Y_{J}}\int_{A_{J}}e^{\gamma\bar{X}_{J}}d\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j})}\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C\Big(\exp\big(-c|V|e^{2t_{0}}\big)+\exp\big(-c|V|e^{2t_{0}}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\underset{|A_{J}|\;\geqslant\;e^{-|\bar{n}_{J}|}|V|}{\sup_{A_{J}\in\mathcal{B}(V)}}\mathbb{P}\big(e^{\gamma 4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{J}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|\bar{n}_{J}|}e^{\gamma Y_{J}}\int_{A_{J}}e^{\gamma\bar{X}_{J}}d\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j})}\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\big)\,.

Now, by applying Lemma
3.1
we see that

\displaystyle\{e^{\gamma 4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{J}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|\bar{n}_{J}|}e^{\gamma Y_{J}}\int_{A_{J}}e^{\gamma\bar{X}_{J}}d\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j})}\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\}

\displaystyle\subseteq\big(E_{\bar{n}_{J}}(A_{J})\cap\{e^{\gamma 4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{J}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|\bar{n}_{J}|}e^{\gamma Y_{J}}\int_{A_{J}}e^{\gamma\bar{X}_{J}}d\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j})}\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\}\big)\cup F_{n_{J+1}}(A_{n_{J}})\,.

It is shown in the proof of Lemma
3.4
below that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(E_{\bar{n}_{J}}(A_{J})\cap\{e^{\gamma 4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{J}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|\bar{n}_{J}|}e^{\gamma Y_{J}}\int_{A_{J}}e^{\gamma\bar{X}_{J}}d\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j})}\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\}\big)\;\leqslant\;\frac{c_{3}}{\alpha|V|}\exp\big(-c\alpha^{2}|V|(\tfrac{4}{e})^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}e^{2t_{j}}\big)\,,

and by Lemma
3.5
that

\displaystyle F_{n_{J+1}}(A_{n_{J}})

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\exp\big(-c(1+\delta)^{|\bar{n}_{J}|+\kappa}\big)\,,

for some
\delta>0
. As
n_{j}\;\geqslant\;\kappa
for each
j
, this can be made arbitrarily small by continuing the iteration until a large enough number of steps
J
.

Finally, we show that the bound for
\widetilde{M}_{Z,\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
implies the bound for
M_{\widetilde{Z},\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle M_{\widetilde{Z},\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{d})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}e^{\gamma\widetilde{Z}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Z}^{2}]}\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}e^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[Z^{2}-\widetilde{Z}^{2}]}\,d\widetilde{M}_{Z,\gamma}(z)\,.

Where by
\mathbb{E}[Z^{2}-\widetilde{Z}^{2}]
we denote the function

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z(x)^{2}-\widetilde{Z}(x)^{2}]

\displaystyle:=2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbb{E}[Z(x)Z(z)]\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z)-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}\times\mathbb{T}^{d}}\mathbb{E}[Z(z)Z(w)]\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(z)dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(w)\,.

By the assumptions on the field
Z
, this is a bounded function on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
and the bound for
\mathbb{P}(M_{\widetilde{Z},\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{2})<\varepsilon)
follows.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\bar{n}_{j}\in\mathbb{N}^{j}
and
A_{j}\subset V
be a Borel set with
|A_{j}|\;\geqslant\;e^{-|\bar{n}_{j}|}|V|
. Let
\gamma\in(0,2)
and
\alpha>0
be large enough so that
\gamma\alpha+\gamma 2\alpha 2^{n}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}n\;\geqslant\;n
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there exists a natural number
n_{j+1}\;\geqslant\;\kappa
, a Borel set
A_{j+1}\subset A_{j}
with
|A_{j+1}|\;\geqslant\;e^{-|\bar{n}_{j+1}|}|V|
, and an event
H(A_{j},t_{j})
such that

\mathbb{P}(H(A_{j},t_{j}))\;\leqslant\;C_{1}\exp(-C_{2}\alpha^{2}|V|(\tfrac{4}{e})^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}e^{2t_{j-1}})+C_{1}\exp\big(-C_{2}|V|e^{2t_{0}+|\bar{n}_{j}|}\big)\,,

and

\displaystyle\{e^{\gamma 4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|\bar{n}_{j}|}e^{\gamma Y_{j}(A_{j})}\int_{A_{j}}e^{\gamma\bar{X}_{j}}d\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j})}\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\}

\displaystyle\quad\subseteq H(A_{j},t_{j})\cup\{e^{\gamma 4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j+1}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|\bar{n}_{j+1}|}e^{\gamma Y_{j+1}(A_{j+1})}\int_{A_{j+1}}e^{\gamma\bar{X}_{j+1}}d\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j+1})}\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\}\,.

The constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
do not depend on the set
A_{j}
.

Proof.

We split into the events
G_{j}
and
G_{j}^{c}
, where

\displaystyle G_{j}

\displaystyle=\{Y_{j}(A_{j})\;\leqslant\;-\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}\}\,.

By Gaussian estimate, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(G_{j})

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;c_{1}\frac{\mathbb{E}[Y_{j}(A_{j})^{2}]}{\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}}\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha^{2}2^{2|\bar{n}_{j}|}}{2\mathbb{E}[Y_{j}(A_{j})^{2}]}\right)\,.

By Lemma
3.6
, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y_{j}(A_{j})^{2}]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;c_{2}\frac{e^{-2t_{j-1}}}{|A_{j}|}\,,

leading to

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(G_{j})

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\frac{c_{3}}{\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}e^{2t_{j-1}}|A_{j}|}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}2^{2|\bar{n}_{j}|}e^{2t_{j-1}}|A_{j}|)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\frac{c_{3}}{\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}e^{2t_{j-1}}e^{-|\bar{n}_{j}|}|V|}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}2^{2|\bar{n}_{j}|}e^{2t_{j-1}}e^{-|\bar{n}_{j}|}|V|)

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{3}}{\alpha|V|(\frac{2}{e})^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}e^{2t_{j-1}}}\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\alpha^{2}|V|(\tfrac{4}{e})^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}e^{2t_{j-1}})

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\frac{c_{3}}{\alpha|V|}\exp\big(-c\alpha^{2}|V|(\tfrac{4}{e})^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}e^{2t_{j-1}}\big)\,.

On the other hand, in
G_{j}^{c}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(G_{j}^{c}\cap\{e^{\gamma 4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|\bar{n}_{j}|}e^{\gamma Y_{j}(A_{j})}\int_{A_{j}}e^{\gamma\bar{X}_{j}}d\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j})}\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\}\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{P}\big(e^{\gamma 3\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|\bar{n}_{j}|}\int_{A_{j}}e^{\gamma\bar{X}_{j}}d\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j})}\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\big)\,,

and then we apply Lemma
3.1
to
\bar{X}_{j}:A_{j}\to\mathbb{R}
. We get the events
E_{\bar{n}_{j},t_{0}}(A_{j})
and
F_{\bar{n}_{j+1},t_{0}}(A_{j})
. The first event can be bounded by

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(E_{\bar{n}_{j},t_{0}}(A_{j})\cap\{e^{\gamma 3\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|\bar{n}_{j}|}\int_{A_{j}}e^{\gamma\bar{X}_{j}}dM^{(t_{j})}\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\}\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\underset{|B_{\bar{n}_{j}}|\;\geqslant\;e^{-|\bar{n}_{j}|}\beta|V|}{\sup_{B_{\bar{n}_{j}}\in\mathcal{B}(V)}}\mathbb{P}\big(e^{\gamma 2\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|\bar{n}_{j}|}\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j})}(B_{\bar{n}_{j}})\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\big)\,.

Now, by the assumption on
\alpha
, for any
B_{\bar{n}_{j}}
with
|B_{\bar{n}_{j}}|\;\geqslant\;e^{-|\bar{n}_{j}|}\beta|V|
we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(e^{\gamma 2\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|\bar{n}_{j}|}\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j})}(B_{\bar{n}_{j}})\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{P}\big(\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j})}(B_{\bar{n}_{j}})\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-|\bar{n}_{j}|}\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{P}\big(\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j})}(B_{\bar{n}_{j}})\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{1}{2}|B_{\bar{n}_{j}}|\big)\,.

Now, since
|B_{\bar{n}_{j}}|\;\geqslant\;e^{-|\bar{n}_{j}|}\beta|V|\;\geqslant\;e^{-2t_{j}}
, we may apply the concentration inequality given by Lemma
3.9
to obtain the bound

\displaystyle\underset{|B_{\bar{n}_{j}}|\;\geqslant\;e^{-|\bar{n}_{j}|}\beta|V|}{\sup_{B_{\bar{n}_{j}}\in\mathcal{B}(V)}}\mathbb{P}\big(\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j})}(B_{\bar{n}_{j}})\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{1}{2}|B_{\bar{n}_{j}}|\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\underset{|B_{\bar{n}_{j}}|\;\geqslant\;e^{-|\bar{n}_{j}|}\beta|V|}{\sup_{B_{\bar{n}_{j}}\in\mathcal{B}(V)}}\exp\big(-c|B_{\bar{n}_{j}}|e^{2t_{j}}\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\exp\big(-c\beta|V|e^{-|\bar{n}_{j}|}e^{2(t_{0}+|\bar{n}_{j}|)}\big)

\displaystyle=\exp\big(-c\beta|V|e^{2t_{0}+|\bar{n}_{j}|}\big)\,.

For the other event, by using the definition of
F_{\bar{n}_{j+1},t_{0}}(A_{j})
and Lemma
3.7
, we get that there exists a set
A_{j+1}
with
|A_{j+1}|\;\geqslant\;e^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}|V|
so that the following inclusions hold

\displaystyle F_{\bar{n}_{j+1},t_{0}}(A_{j})\cap\{e^{\gamma 3\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|\bar{n}_{j}|}\int_{A_{j}}e^{\gamma\bar{X}_{j}}d\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j})}\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\}

\displaystyle\subset\big\{e^{\gamma 3\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|\bar{n}_{j}|+\gamma 4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j+1}|}}\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j+1})}(A_{j+1})\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\big\}

\displaystyle\subset\big\{e^{\gamma 3\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|\bar{n}_{j}|+\gamma 4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j+1}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}n_{j+1}}e^{\gamma Y_{j+1}(A_{j+1})}\int_{A_{j+1}}e^{\gamma\bar{X}_{j+1}}d\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j+1})}\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\big\}

\displaystyle\subset\big\{e^{\gamma 4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j+1}|}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}|\bar{n}_{j+1}|}e^{\gamma Y_{j+1}(A_{j+1})}\int_{A_{j+1}}e^{\gamma\bar{X}_{j+1}}d\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t_{j+1})}\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{\beta|V|}{2}e^{-\gamma\alpha}\big\}\,.

Thus we obtain the desired result.

∎

Lemma 3.5
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(F_{\bar{n}_{J+1},t_{0}}(A_{\bar{n}_{J}})\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\exp\big(-c(1+\delta)^{|\bar{n}_{J}|+\kappa}\big)

for some
\delta>0
. The constant
c
is independent of the set
A_{\bar{n}_{j}}
.

Proof.

Recall the definition

\displaystyle F_{\bar{n}_{j+1},t_{0}}(A_{\bar{n}_{j}})=\{\exists\;n_{j+1}\;\geqslant\;\kappa\,,\;A_{\bar{n}_{j+1}}\subset A_{\bar{n}_{j}}:\;|A_{\bar{n}_{j+1}}|\;\geqslant\;e^{-n_{j+1}}|A_{\bar{n}_{j}}|\,,\;\bar{X}_{j}\;\geqslant\;4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{j+1}|}\text{ on }A_{\bar{n}_{j+1}}\}\,.

We use a crude Gaussian estimate. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\{x\in A_{\bar{n}_{J}}:\bar{X}_{J}\;\geqslant\;4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{J+1}|}\}|]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\big[\int_{A_{\bar{n}_{J}}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\bar{X}_{J}(x)\;\geqslant\;4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{J+1}|}\}}\,dx\big]

\displaystyle=\int_{A_{\bar{n}_{J}}}\mathbb{P}\big(\bar{X}_{J}(x)\;\geqslant\;4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{J+1}|}\big)\,dx

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\int_{A_{\bar{n}_{J}}}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{J}(x)^{2}]}{4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{J+1}|}}\exp\big(-c_{2}\alpha^{2}2^{2|\bar{n}_{J+1}|}/\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{J}(x)^{2}]\big)\,dx\,.

By Lemma
3.8
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}_{J}(x)^{2}]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;n_{J}+\tfrac{c}{|A_{J}|}e^{-2t_{J-1}}\;\leqslant\;n_{J}+\tfrac{c}{|V|}e^{|\bar{n}_{J}|-2t_{J-1}}=n_{J}+\tfrac{c}{|V|}e^{-2t_{0}-|\bar{n}_{J-1}|+n_{J}}\,.

Thus we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\{x\in A_{\bar{n}_{J}}:\bar{X}_{J}\;\geqslant\;4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{J+1}|}\}|]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;|A_{\bar{n}_{J}}|\frac{n_{J}+\frac{c}{|V|}e^{n_{J}}}{4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{J+1}|}}\exp\Big(-c_{2}\alpha^{2}\frac{2^{2|\bar{n}_{J+1}|}}{n_{J}+\tfrac{c}{|V|}e^{-2t_{0}-|\bar{n}_{J-1}|+n_{J}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C_{1}|A_{\bar{n}_{J}}|\exp\big(-C_{2}(\tfrac{4}{e})^{|\bar{n}_{J+1}|}\big)\,.

Now, by Markov’s inequality we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(F_{\bar{n}_{J+1},t_{0}}(A_{\bar{n}_{J}})\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\sum_{n_{J+1}\;\geqslant\;\kappa}\mathbb{P}\big(|\{x\in A_{\bar{n}_{J}}:\bar{X}_{j}\;\geqslant\;4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{J+1}|}\}|\;\geqslant\;e^{-n_{J+1}}|A_{\bar{n}_{J}}|\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\sum_{n_{J+1}\;\geqslant\;\kappa}e^{n_{J+1}}|A_{\bar{n}_{J}}|^{-1}\mathbb{E}[|\{x\in A_{\bar{n}_{J}}:\bar{X}_{j}\;\geqslant\;4\alpha 2^{|\bar{n}_{J+1}|}\}|]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\sum_{n_{J+1}\;\geqslant\;\kappa}C_{1}\exp\big(-C_{2}(\tfrac{4}{e})^{|\bar{n}_{J+1}|}\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C_{1}^{\prime}\exp\big(-C_{2}^{\prime}(\tfrac{4}{e})^{|\bar{n}_{J}|+\kappa}\big)

and the claim follows.
∎

3.1.1
Estimates for almost
\star
-scale invariant fields

In this section we will often denote
dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(x)
by
dx
.

Lemma 3.6
.

For any
A\subset\mathbb{T}^{2}
with positive Lebesgue measure and
t,s>0
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{A}\int_{t}^{t+s}\rho(e^{u}(x-y))(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du\,dy

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;ce^{-2t}\big(1-e^{-2s}\big)\,.

In particular, for
Y:=\frac{1}{|A|}\int_{A}\big(\widetilde{X}_{t+s}(x)-\widetilde{X}_{t}(x)\big)\,dx
we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y^{2}]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;c\frac{e^{-2t}}{|A|}\,.

Proof.

First, by
1-e^{-\xi u}\;\leqslant\;1
, it suffices to focus on the case of exactly
\star
-scale invariant fields. By a change of variable in
u
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t+s}\rho(e^{u}(x-y))\,du

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}\rho(e^{u}e^{t}(x-y))\,du\,.

By using the facts that
\rho
is bounded and supported in the unit ball, we get the upper bound

\displaystyle\int_{A}\int_{0}^{s}\rho(e^{u}e^{t}(x-y))\,du\,dy

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;c_{1}\int_{A}\int_{0}^{s}\,\mathbf{1}_{|x-y|<e^{-u-t}}\,du\,dy

\displaystyle=c_{1}\int_{0}^{s}\int_{y\in A\cap B(x,e^{-u-t})}dy\,du

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;c_{1}\int_{0}^{s}|B(x,e^{-u-t})|\,du

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;c_{2}e^{-2t}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-2u}\,du

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}c_{2}e^{-2t}\big(1-e^{-2s}\big)\,.

By definition

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{X}_{s_{1}}(x)\widetilde{X}_{s_{2}}(y)]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s_{1}\land s_{2}}\widetilde{Q}_{u}(x,y)\,du\,,

where
\widetilde{Q}_{u}
is the covariance kernel obtained from

\displaystyle Q_{u}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=\rho(e^{u}(x-y))(1-e^{-\xi u})\,,

by forcing it to have zero mean over
V
, that is,

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}_{u}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=Q_{u}(x,y)-\frac{1}{|V|}\big(\int_{V}Q_{u}(x^{\prime},y)\,dx^{\prime}+\int_{V}Q_{u}(x,y^{\prime})\,dy^{\prime}\big)+\frac{1}{|V|^{2}}\int_{V^{2}}Q_{u}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}\,dy^{\prime}\,.

(3.6)

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y^{2}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|A|^{2}}\int_{A^{2}}\mathbb{E}[\big(\widetilde{X}_{t+s}(x)-\widetilde{X}_{t}(x)\big)\big(\widetilde{X}_{t+s}(y)-\widetilde{X}_{t}(y)\big)]\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|A|^{2}}\int_{A^{2}}\big(\int_{0}^{t+s}\widetilde{Q}_{u}(x,y)\,du-2\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Q}_{u}(x,y)du+\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Q}_{u}(x,y)du\big)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|A|^{2}}\int_{A^{2}}\int_{t}^{t+s}\widetilde{Q}_{u}(x,y)\,dx\,dy\,du\,.

Now, we plug in (
3.6
), use positivity of
Q_{u}
and the previous computation, leading to the upper bound

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y^{2}]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\frac{1}{|A|^{2}}\int_{A^{2}}\int_{t}^{t+s}\rho(e^{u}(x-y))(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du\,d^{2}x\,d^{2}y

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{|V|^{2}}\int_{V^{2}}\int_{t}^{t+s}\rho(e^{u}(x-y))(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du\,d^{2}x\,d^{2}y

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\frac{c}{|A|}e^{-2t}(1-e^{-2s})\,.

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
t,s>0
and
A\subset V
be a Borel set with positive Lebesgue measure. We have

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}^{(t)}_{\gamma}(A)

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;e^{\gamma Y-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}s}\int_{A}e^{\gamma\bar{X}}d\widetilde{M}^{(t+s)}_{\gamma}\,,

where

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{|A|}\int_{A}(\widetilde{X}_{t+s}-\widetilde{X}_{t})\,d^{2}x\,,

\displaystyle\bar{X}

\displaystyle:=\widetilde{X}_{t+s}-\widetilde{X}_{t}-Y\,.

Proof.

Recall that

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}^{(t)}_{\gamma}(dz)

\displaystyle:=e^{\gamma\widetilde{X}^{(t)}(z)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[X^{(t)}(z)^{2}]}\,dz\,.

We use
X^{(t)}\overset{d}{=}(X_{t+s}-X_{t})+X^{(t+s)}
. Because
\rho
is a positive definite function, the two terms on the right-hand side are independent, so we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t)}(A)

\displaystyle=\int_{A}e^{\gamma(\widetilde{X}_{t+s}-\widetilde{X}_{t})-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}(X_{t+s}-X_{t})^{2}}d\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t+s)}\,.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(X_{t+s}-X_{t})^{2}]

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{t+s}\rho(0)(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du\;\leqslant\;s\,,

(3.7)

and the result follows.

∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\bar{X}=\widetilde{X}_{t+s}-\widetilde{X}_{t}-\tfrac{1}{|A|}\int_{A}(\widetilde{X}_{t+s}-\widetilde{X}_{t})\,dx
. Then for all
x\in A
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}^{2}(x)]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;s+\tfrac{c}{|A|}e^{-2t}\,.

Proof.

We can replace
\widetilde{X}_{t}
,
\widetilde{X}_{t+s}
by
X_{t}
,
X_{t+s}
due to cancellations:

\displaystyle\bar{X}

\displaystyle=X_{t+s}-X_{t}-\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}(X_{t+s}-X_{t})\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{|A|}\int_{A}\big(X_{t+s}-X_{t}-\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}\big(X_{t+s}-X_{t}\big)\,dy\big)\,dx

\displaystyle=X_{t+s}-X_{t}-\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}(X_{t+s}-X_{t})\,dx

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{|A|}\int(X_{t+s}-X_{t})\,dx+\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}(X_{t+s}-X_{t})\,dy

\displaystyle=X_{t+s}-X_{t}-\frac{1}{|A|}\int(X_{t+s}-X_{t})\,dx\,.

By (
3.7
)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[(X_{t+s}-X_{t})^{2}]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;s\,,

and by the calculations done in Lemma
3.6
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\big(\tfrac{1}{|A|}\int_{A}(X_{t+s}-X_{t})\,dx\big)^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\frac{c}{|A|}e^{-2t}(1-e^{-2s})\,.

Thus, since
\rho
is non-negative, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\bar{X}(x)^{2}]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;s+\frac{c}{|A|}e^{-2t}(1-e^{-2s})-\frac{2}{|A|}\int_{A}\mathbb{E}[(X_{t+s}(x)-X_{t}(x))(X_{t+s}(y)-X_{t}(y)]\,dy

\displaystyle=s+\frac{c}{|A|}e^{-2t}(1-e^{-2s})-\frac{2}{|A|}\int_{A}\int_{t}^{t+s}\rho(e^{u}(x-y))(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du\,dy

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;s+\frac{c}{|A|}e^{-2t}\,.

∎

3.1.2
Concentration inequality for zero mean almost
\star
-scale invariant GMC

In this section we recall parts of the proof of the concentration inequality of
[
LRV22
]
(Proposition 6.2.) for GMC built out of a zero-mean almost
\star
-scale invariant field.

Let
X
be an almost
\star
-scale invariant field with parameter
\xi
and
V\subset\mathbb{T}^{2}
a Borel set with positive Lebesgue measure. We denote

\displaystyle\widetilde{X}(z)

\displaystyle:=X(z)-\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}X(z)\,dz\,,

where we again use the notation
dx=dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}(x)
.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
t
be large enough so that
|V|\;\geqslant\;e^{-2t}
. Then for any Borel set
D\subset V
with
|D|\;\geqslant\;e^{-2t}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t)}(D)\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{|D|}{2}\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\exp\big(-c|D|e^{2t}\big)\,.

Proof.

We will use Kahane’s convexity inequality (see for example Corollary 6.2. in
[
RoVa10
]
). We start by estimating the covariance kernel of
\widetilde{X}^{t}
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{X}^{t}(z)\widetilde{X}^{t}(w)]

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{\infty}\rho(e^{u}(z-w))(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du-\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}\int_{t}^{\infty}\rho(e^{u}(z^{\prime}-w))(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du\,dz^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{|V|}\int_{V}\int_{t}^{\infty}\rho(e^{u}(z-w^{\prime}))(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du\,dw^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{|V|^{2}}\int_{V^{2}}\int_{t}^{\infty}\rho(e^{u}(z^{\prime}-w^{\prime}))(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du\,dz^{\prime}\,dw^{\prime}

Recall that
\rho
is non-negative. By Lemma
3.6
we get the inequalities

\displaystyle-c_{1}e^{-2t}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{X}^{(t)}(z)\widetilde{X}^{(t)}(w)]-\int_{t}^{\infty}\rho(e^{u}(z-w))(1-e^{-\xi u})\,du\;\leqslant\;c_{2}e^{-2t}\,.

(3.8)

By using the left-hand side of the above inequality, we get that

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t)}(D)

\displaystyle=\int_{D}e^{\gamma\widetilde{X}^{t}(z)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[X^{t}(z)^{2}]}\,dz

\displaystyle=\int_{D}e^{\gamma\widetilde{X}^{t}(z)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{X}^{t}(z)^{2}]}e^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{X}^{(t)}(z)^{2}-X^{(t)}(z)^{2}]}\,dz

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;e^{-c_{1}e^{-2t}}M_{\widetilde{X}^{(t)},\gamma}(D)

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;\frac{1}{1+\eta}M_{\widetilde{X}^{(t)},\gamma}(D)

(3.9)

for any
\eta>0
once
t
is large enough. Now we are ready to use Kahane’s inequality. Let
X_{\infty}^{t}
be the rough part of an exactly
\star
-scale invariant field, i.e. a Gaussian field with the covariance kernel

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[X_{\infty}^{t}(z)X_{\infty}^{t}(w)]

\displaystyle=\int_{t}^{\infty}\rho(e^{u}(z-w))\,du\,.

We denote the corresponding GMC measure by

\displaystyle M_{X_{\infty}^{t},\gamma}(dz)

\displaystyle:=e^{\gamma X_{\infty}^{t}(z)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[X_{\infty}^{t}(z)^{2}]}\,dz\,.

Let
N\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
be an independent standard Gaussian. We first apply (
3.9
), and then Kahane’s convexity inequality (which is valid by (
3.8
)) to get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(-(1+\eta)r\widetilde{M}^{(t)}_{\gamma}(D)\big)\big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(-rM_{\widetilde{X}^{(t)},\gamma}(D)\big)\big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(-re^{\gamma\sqrt{c_{2}e^{-2t}}N-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}c_{2}e^{-2t}}M_{X_{\infty}^{t},\gamma}(D)\big)\big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(-re^{-\gamma c-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}c_{2}e^{-2t}}M_{X_{\infty}^{t},\gamma}(D)\big)+\mathbb{P}(|N|\;\geqslant\;c(c_{2}e^{-2t})^{-1/2})\big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(-re^{-\gamma c-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}M_{X_{\infty}^{t},\gamma}(D)\big)+\mathbb{P}(|N|\;\geqslant\;c(c_{2}e^{-2t})^{-1/2})\big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(-\tfrac{9r}{10}M_{X_{\infty}^{t},\gamma}(D)\big)+\mathbb{P}(|N|\;\geqslant\;c(c_{2}e^{-2t})^{-1/2})\big]

for a suitable
c
. The second term is bounded by
C_{1}e^{-C_{2}e^{2t}}
for some
C_{1},C_{2}
. By Appendix C in
[
LRV22
]
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(-r\big(M_{X_{\infty}^{t},\gamma}(D)-|D|\big)\big)\big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\exp\big(c_{p}r^{p}e^{2t(1-p)}|D|\big)

for all
p\in(1,4/\gamma^{2})
and
r\;\leqslant\;e^{2t}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(-(1+\eta)r\big(\widetilde{M}^{(t)}_{\gamma}(D)-|D|\big)\big)\big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(-\tfrac{9r}{10}M_{X_{\infty}^{t},\gamma}(D)\big)+C_{1}e^{-C_{2}e^{2t}}\big]e^{(1+\eta)r|D|}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;e^{\widetilde{c}_{p}r^{p}e^{2t(1-p)}|D|+(\frac{1}{10}+\eta)r|D|}+C_{1}e^{(1+\eta)r|D|-C_{2}e^{2t}}\,,

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t)}(D)\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{|D|}{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\big(-r\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t)}(D)\;\geqslant\;-r\tfrac{|D|}{2}\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;e^{r\frac{|D|}{2}}\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(-r\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t)}(D)\big)\big]

\displaystyle=e^{-r\frac{|D|}{2}}\mathbb{E}\big[\exp\big(-r(\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t)}(D)-|D|)\big)\big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;e^{-r\frac{|D|}{2}}e^{\widetilde{c}_{p}r^{p}e^{2t(1-p)}|D|+\frac{\frac{1}{10}+\eta}{1+\eta}r|D|}+C_{1}e^{(1+\eta)r|D|-C_{2}e^{2t}}\,.

Now, for
r=\delta e^{2t}
for small enough
\delta
we get that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\widetilde{M}_{\gamma}^{(t)}(D)\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{|D|}{2}\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\exp\big((-(\tfrac{1}{2}-\tfrac{\frac{1}{10}+\eta}{1+\eta})\delta|D|+\widetilde{c}_{p}\delta^{p}|D|)e^{2t}\big)+C_{1}\exp\big(((1+\eta)\delta|D|-\tfrac{C_{2}}{|V|}|D|)e^{2t}\big)\,,

which is of the wanted form for small enough
\delta
and
\eta
.

∎

3.2
Lower bound

In this section we will derive a lower bound for
\mathbb{P}(M_{\widetilde{Z},\gamma}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})<\varepsilon)
for a rather general class of
\log
-correlated fields
Z
on
{\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}}
. We will apply the Donsker–Varadhan theorem (see e.g. Proposition 2.3. in
[
BuDu
]
).

Definition 3.10
.

Let
\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{P}
be probability measures such that
\mathbb{Q}
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mathbb{P}
(denoted by
\mathbb{Q}\ll\mathbb{P}
). We define the relative entropy between
\mathbb{Q}
and
\mathbb{P}
by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ent}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\log\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}]\,,

where
\frac{d\mathbb{Q}}{d\mathbb{P}}
is the Radon–Nikodym derivative.

Theorem 3.11

(Donsker–Varadhan)
.

Let
\mathbb{P}
be a probability measure on a Polish space
E
and
k:E\to\mathbb{R}
a random variable that is bounded from below. Then

\displaystyle-\log\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[e^{-k}]

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mathbb{Q}\ll\mathbb{P}}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[k]+\operatorname{Ent}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{P})\big)\,.

Remark 3.12
.

Let
N_{1}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{1}^{2})
and
N_{2}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{2}^{2})
be two Gaussian random variables. The relative entropy between them is given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ent}(N_{1},N_{2})

\displaystyle=\log\frac{\sigma_{2}}{\sigma_{1}}+\frac{1}{2}\big(\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}}{\sigma_{2}^{2}}-1\big)\,.

(3.10)

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
Z
be a logarithmically correlated Gaussian field satisfying the conditions of Theorem
1.1
. Denote
\widetilde{Z}=Z-\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}Z\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}
. Then we have that

\displaystyle-\log\mathbb{E}[e^{-R^{d+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}M_{\widetilde{Z},\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{d})}]\;\leqslant\;cR^{d}

(3.11)

Proof.

We give the proof for
d=2
. The generalisation to
d\;\geqslant\;2
is straightforward.

We have
Z=X+H
, where
X
has the covariance operator
{P_{>N}}(-\Delta^{-1}+\Delta^{-1-\xi})
and
H
is an independent Gaussian field with
H\in H^{1+s}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
almost surely for some
s>0
. Let
\mu
be the law of
X
. Now

\displaystyle-\log\mathbb{E}[e^{-R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}M_{\widetilde{Z},\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}]

\displaystyle=-\log\mathbb{E}[\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[e^{-R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}M_{\widetilde{X}+\widetilde{H},\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}]]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{E}[-\log\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[e^{-R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}M_{\widetilde{X}+\widetilde{H},\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}]]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[\inf_{\nu\ll\mu}\big(R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\nu}[M_{\widetilde{X}+\widetilde{H},\gamma}(\mathbb{T}^{2})]+\operatorname{Ent}(\nu,\mu)\big)]\,.

(3.12)

We will choose a suitable
\nu\ll\mu
to obtain an upper bound.

The Fourier transform of
X
is given by

\displaystyle\hat{X}(k)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{k}}{\sqrt{2\pi}|k|}\big(1-\tfrac{1}{|k|^{2\xi}}\big)^{1/2}\mathbf{1}_{|k|>N}\,,\quad k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\,,

where
(\alpha_{k})_{k}
is a sequence of i.i.d. standard Gaussians. We will choose
\nu
to be the law of the Gaussian field
Y:\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by

\displaystyle\hat{Y}(k)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\frac{|k|}{R}\hat{X}(k)\,,\quad 0\;\leqslant\;|k|\;\leqslant\;R\,,\\
\hat{X}(k)\,,\quad|k|>R\,.\end{cases}

(3.13)

and we assume that
R>N
. Note that only finitely many Fourier modes of
\mu
and
\nu
differ, so
\nu
is absolutely continuous with respect to
\mu
. Now it suffices to bound the quantity

\displaystyle R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}e^{\gamma(\widetilde{Y}+\widetilde{H})-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Z}^{2}]}\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\Big]+\operatorname{Ent}(\nu,\mu)\,,

where
Y
and
H
are independent. This has been done in Lemmas
3.15
and
3.17
and the claim follows.
∎

Corollary 3.14
.

There exists
c_{\gamma}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(M_{\widetilde{Z},\gamma}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})<\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;\tfrac{1}{2}\exp\big(-c_{\gamma}\varepsilon^{-\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}}\big)\,.

Proof.

Let
a>0
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[e^{-R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}M_{\widetilde{Z},\gamma}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[e^{-R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}M_{\widetilde{Z},\gamma}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})}(\mathbf{1}_{M_{\widetilde{Z},\gamma}<aR^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}}+\mathbf{1}_{M_{\widetilde{Z},\gamma}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})\;\geqslant\;aR^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}})]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{P}(M_{\widetilde{Z},\gamma}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})<aR^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}})+\exp\big(-aR^{2}\big)\,.

By Lemma
3.13
we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(M_{\widetilde{Z},\gamma}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})<aR^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}})

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;\exp\big(-cR^{2}\big)-\exp\big(-aR^{2}\big)\,.

For
a>c
and
\varepsilon=aR^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}
this implies that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(M_{\widetilde{Z},\gamma}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})<\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;\exp\big(-ca^{\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}}\big)-\exp\big(-a^{\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}+1}\varepsilon^{-\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}}\big)\;\geqslant\;\tfrac{1}{2}\exp\big(-ca^{\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}}\big)

for
\varepsilon
small enough.
∎

Lemma 3.15
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ent}(\nu,\mu)\;\leqslant\;CR^{2}\,.

Proof.

By independence of the Fourier modes, we can write

\displaystyle\nu=\bigotimes_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\nu_{k}\qquad\mu=\bigotimes_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mu_{k}

where
\nu_{k}
and
\mu_{k}
are the marginals on the
k
th Fourier mode. Then the relative entropy is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ent}(\nu,\mu)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mathrm{Ent}(\nu_{k},\mu_{k}).

This can be computed explicitly, since
\mu_{k},\nu_{k}
are one-dimensional Gaussians. Since
\mu_{k},\nu_{k}
coincide for
|k|>R
, only the terms with
|k|\;\leqslant\;R
are non-zero. We get

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ent}(\nu,\mu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},0<|k|\;\leqslant\;R}\Big(\log\frac{\mathbb{E}[\hat{X}(k)^{2}]^{1/2}}{\mathbb{E}[\hat{Y}(k)^{2}]^{1/2}}+\frac{1}{2}\big(\frac{\mathbb{E}[\hat{Y}(k)^{2}]}{\mathbb{E}[\hat{X}(k)]^{2}}-1\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{0<|k|\;\leqslant\;R}\big(\log\tfrac{R}{|k|}+\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\tfrac{|k|^{2}}{R^{2}}-1\big)\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\sum_{0<|k|\;\leqslant\;R}\log\tfrac{R}{|k|}\,.

By a relabelling we get

\sum_{0\less|k|\;\leqslant\;R}\log\tfrac{R}{|k|}=\sum_{k\in\frac{1}{R}\mathbb{Z}^{2},0\less|k|\;\leqslant\;1}\log(\tfrac{1}{|k|}).

Dividing this by
R^{2}
we end up with

\sum_{k\in\frac{1}{R}\mathbb{Z}^{2},0\less|k|\;\leqslant\;1}\log(\tfrac{1}{|k|})\frac{1}{R^{2}}\,,

which converges to

\int_{|x|\;\leqslant\;1}-\log|x|\,d^{2}x<\infty
as
R\to\infty
.
∎

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
{P_{<R}}
be the projections to Fourier modes with
|k|\;\leqslant\;R
. Then

(i)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[({P_{<R}}Y(0)^{2}]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C\,.

(ii)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[({P_{<R}}X(0))^{2}]

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;\log\tfrac{R}{N}\,.

Proof.

(i)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[({P_{<R}}Y(0))^{2}]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},0<|k|\;\leqslant\;R}\tfrac{|k|^{2}}{R^{2}}\Big(\big(\tfrac{1}{|k|^{2}}-\tfrac{1}{|k|^{2+2\xi}}\big)\mathbf{1}_{|k|>N}\Big)\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{0<|k|\;\leqslant\;R}\tfrac{1}{R^{2}}\;\leqslant\;C\,.

(ii)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[({P_{<R}}X(0))^{2}]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},|k|\;\leqslant\;R}\Big(\big(\tfrac{1}{|k|^{2}}-\tfrac{1}{|k|^{2+2\xi}}\big)\mathbf{1}_{|k|>N}\Big)\;\geqslant\;\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{N<|x|<R}\tfrac{1}{|x|^{2}}\,d^{2}x=\log\tfrac{R}{N}\,.

∎

Lemma 3.17
.

Let
Y
be the Gaussian field defined in (
3.13
). Then we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}e^{\gamma(\widetilde{Y}+\widetilde{H})-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Z}^{2}]}\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\Big]\;\leqslant\;CR^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\,.

Proof.

We can write (note that
\widetilde{Y}=Y
)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}e^{\gamma(\widetilde{Y}+\widetilde{H})-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Z}^{2}]}\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}=e^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[({P_{<R}}X(0))^{2}]}\int e^{\gamma({P_{<R}}Y+\widetilde{H})-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{H}^{2}]}e^{\gamma{P_{>R}}Y-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[({P_{>R}}X)^{2}]}\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}

where
{P_{>R}}
is the projection on Fourier-modes with
|k|>R
. Now by independence of
{P_{<R}}Y
and
{P_{>R}}Y
we have (note that the dependency on
H
cancels out)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}e^{\gamma(\widetilde{Y}+\widetilde{H})-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{Z}^{2}]}\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\Big]

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[({P_{<R}}X(0))^{2}]}\int\mathbb{E}[\exp(\gamma{P_{<R}}Y)]\mathbb{E}e^{\gamma{P_{>R}}Y-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[({P_{>R}}X)^{2}}\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[({P_{<R}}X)^{2}]}\int e^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[({P_{<R}}Y)^{2}]+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[({P_{>R}}Y)^{2}]-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[({P_{>R}}X)^{2}]}\,dv_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[({P_{<R}}X(0))^{2}]}\int e^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[({P_{<R}}Y)^{2}]}\,dx

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;e^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\log R+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}C}

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}C}R^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\,,

where we used Lemma
3.16
to obtain the upper bound.
∎

4
Massless Sinh–Gordon model on a torus

As an application of our small deviations bounds for GMC, we derive upper and lower bounds for the free energy of the massless Sinh–Gordon model on the two-dimensional torus.

4.1
Gaussian Free Field on the two-dimensional torus

Let
R>0
and
{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}
be the
R
-torus

\displaystyle{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{R}^{2}/(R\mathbb{Z})^{2}\,.

We denote by
g_{R}
the flat metric on
{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}
. Let
\psi_{R}:{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}\to{\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}}
be given by
\psi_{R}(z)=\frac{z}{R}
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle g_{R}

\displaystyle=R^{2}\psi_{R}^{*}g_{1}\,,

(4.1)

where
\psi_{R}^{*}
denotes the pullback. Let
\widetilde{X}_{R}:{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}\to\mathbb{R}
be the zero-mean Gaussian free field on
{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}
. Its covariance kernel is given by the zero-mean Green function

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{X}_{R}(z)\widetilde{X}_{R}(w)]

\displaystyle=G_{R}(z,w)=-\log d_{{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}}(z,w)+h_{R}(z,w)\,,

(4.2)

where
d_{R}
is the distance function corresponding to
g_{R}
and
h_{R}
is a smooth function given by

\displaystyle h_{R}(z,w)

\displaystyle=m_{R}(\log d_{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}(z,\cdot))+m_{R}(\log d_{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}(\cdot,w))-m_{R}(\log d_{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}(\cdot,\cdot))\,,

where
m_{R}
denotes taking the average over
{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}
.

The scaling property (
4.1
) implies that

\displaystyle\widetilde{X}_{R}(z)\overset{d}{=}\widetilde{X}_{1}(\tfrac{z}{R})\,,

(4.3)

which is equivalent to the scaling property of the covariance kernel

\displaystyle G_{R}(z,w)=G_{1}(\tfrac{z}{R},\tfrac{w}{R})\,.

We denote the corresponding GMC by

\displaystyle M_{\gamma,R}(dz)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}e^{\gamma\widetilde{X}_{R,\varepsilon}(z)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}s_{R,\varepsilon}(z)}d^{2}z\,,

(4.4)

where

\displaystyle\widetilde{X}_{R,\varepsilon}(z)

\displaystyle:=\tfrac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\widetilde{X}_{R}(z+\varepsilon e^{i\theta})\,d\theta\,,

\displaystyle s_{R,\varepsilon}(z)

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}[\widetilde{X}_{R,\varepsilon}(z)\widetilde{X}_{R,\varepsilon}(z)]-h_{R}(z,z)\,.

The limit (
4.4
) exists weakly in probability, see
[
Ber17
]
. By using (
4.3
) we get the GMC scaling relation

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}}M_{\gamma,R}(dz)

\displaystyle=R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}}}M_{\gamma,1}(dz)\,,

(4.5)

see for example Proposition 2.2 in
[
KuOi20
]
for a detailed proof.

4.2
Path integral formulation of the massless Sinh–Gordon model

The classical action functional of the model on
({\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}},g_{R})
given by

\displaystyle S(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}}\big(\tfrac{1}{2}|d\varphi|_{R}^{2}+\tfrac{Q}{4\pi}K_{R}\varphi+\mu e^{\gamma\varphi}+\mu e^{-\gamma\varphi}\big)dv_{R}\,,

where
v_{R}
is the volume form,
K_{R}
the scalar curvature and
|\cdot|_{R}
the norm induced by the Riemannian metric
g_{R}
. As
g_{R}
is flat, we have
K_{R}\equiv 0
, and we get the coordinate expression

\displaystyle S(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,R]^{2}}\big(\tfrac{1}{2}|\nabla\varphi(z)|^{2}+\mu e^{\gamma\varphi(z)}+\mu e^{-\gamma\varphi(z)}\big)\,dz\,.

(4.6)

Remark 4.1
.

For what follows we could just as well use the more general action

\displaystyle\int_{[0,R]^{2}}\big(\tfrac{1}{2}|\nabla\varphi(z)|^{2}+\mu_{1}e^{\gamma_{1}\varphi(z)}+\mu_{2}e^{-\gamma_{2}\varphi(z)}\big)\,dz\,,

\mu_{i}>0
,
\gamma_{i}\in(0,2)
, but we will work with (
4.6
) to keep the notation simple.

The massless free field on
{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}
is given by
\varphi(z)=c+\widetilde{X}_{R}(z)
, where
\widetilde{X}_{R}
is the zero mean GFF on
{\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
is distributed according to the Lebesgue measure. Let us now consider the partition function of the massless Sinh–Gordon model, which we obtain by tilting the law of the massless free field by the GMC terms

\displaystyle Z_{R}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{R}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\mu e^{\gamma c}M_{\gamma,R}({\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}})-\mu e^{-\gamma c}M_{-\gamma,R}({\mathbb{T}_{R}^{2}})}\,dc\Big]\,,

where
\mathbb{E}_{R}
is the expectation with respect to
\widetilde{X}_{R}
. By the GMC scaling (
4.5
), we get that

\displaystyle Z_{R}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{1}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\mu R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}(e^{\gamma c}M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})+e^{-\gamma c}M_{-\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}}))}\,dc\Big]\,.

By a change of variables
e^{\gamma c}\sqrt{M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})}\to\sqrt{M_{-\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})}c^{\prime}
we get that

\displaystyle Z_{R}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{1}\Big[\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\mu R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\sqrt{M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})M_{-\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})}(c+c^{-1})}\tfrac{dc}{c}\Big]\,.

We want to derive upper and lower bounds for (the logarithm of) this quantity.

Remark 4.2
.

We also have the identity

\displaystyle Z_{R}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{\gamma}\mathbb{E}_{1}\Big[K_{0}\big(2\mu R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\sqrt{M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})M_{-\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})}\big)\Big]\,,

(4.7)

where
K_{0}
is the modified Bessel function of the second kind with index
0
.

4.3
Free energy bounds: proof of Theorem
1.5

In this section we give a proof of Theorem
1.5
.

4.3.1
Lower bound

Proposition 4.3
.

For large enough
R
we have the bound

\displaystyle-\log Z_{R}

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;f_{\gamma}\mu^{\frac{2}{\gamma Q}}R^{2}

(4.8)

for some
f_{\gamma}>0
.

Proof.

We write the proof for
\mu=1
, as the result for general
\mu>0
follows by replacing
R
by
\mu^{\frac{1}{\gamma Q}}R
.

We denote
\mathcal{M}:=\sqrt{M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})M_{-\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})}
. First we split the expectation into two parts using the events
\{\mathcal{M}\;\leqslant\;R^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\}
and
\{\mathcal{M}>R^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\}
. We estimate the two terms separately. First, for the latter we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}\Big[\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathcal{M}>R^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}(c+c^{-1})\mathcal{M}}\tfrac{1}{c}\,dc\Big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{E}_{1}\Big[\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathcal{M}>R^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-R^{2}(c+c^{-1})}\tfrac{1}{c}\,dc\Big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-R^{2}(c+c^{-1})}\tfrac{1}{c}\,dc\,.

By Laplace’s method, this integral behaves as
CR^{-1}e^{-2R^{2}}
when
R
is large, so for sufficiently large
R
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-R^{2}(c+c^{-1})}\tfrac{1}{c}\,dc

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;e^{-2R^{2}}\,.

Next, we deal with the event
\{\mathcal{M}\;\leqslant\;R^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\}
. We split this further with the event
\{\mathcal{M}>R^{-2-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}+\delta}\}
where
\delta\in(0,\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2})
. We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}\Big[\mathbf{1}_{\{R^{-2-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}+\delta}<\mathcal{M}\;\leqslant\;R^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}(c+c^{-1})\mathcal{M}}\tfrac{dc}{c}\Big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{M}\;\leqslant\;R^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\big)\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-R^{\delta}(c+c^{-1})}\tfrac{dc}{c}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{M}\;\leqslant\;R^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\big)e^{-2R^{\delta}}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{M}\;\leqslant\;R^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\big)\,,

where the second inequality follows by applying Laplace’s method.

For the last remaining term, we estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}\Big[\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathcal{M}\;\leqslant\;R^{-2-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}+\delta}\}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}(c+c^{-1})\mathcal{M}}\tfrac{1}{c}\,dc\Big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{E}_{1}\Big[\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathcal{M}\;\leqslant\;R^{-2-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}+\delta}\}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-(c+c^{-1})\mathcal{M}}\tfrac{1}{c}\,dc\Big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{1}\Big[\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathcal{M}\;\leqslant\;R^{-2-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}+\delta}\}}K_{0}(2\mathcal{M})\Big]\,,

where in the equality we used the identity (
4.7
). By using the fact that
K_{0}
diverges logarithmically near the origin, we can estimate

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{1}\Big[\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathcal{M}\;\leqslant\;R^{-2-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}+\delta}\}}K_{0}(2\mathcal{M})\Big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C\mathbb{E}_{1}\Big[\mathbf{1}_{\{\mathcal{M}\;\leqslant\;R^{-2-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}+\delta}\}}|\log\big(2\mathcal{M}\big)|\Big]

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;C\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}\;\leqslant\;R^{-2-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}+\delta})^{\frac{1}{p}}\mathbb{E}_{1}[(\log|2\mathcal{M}|)^{q}]^{\frac{1}{q}}\,,

where
p,q>1
satisfy
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}=1
. The expected value in the above expression is finite by simple arguments using the facts that
M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})
and
M_{-\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})
have finite negative moments and some finite positive moments. Now, we have shown that

\displaystyle Z_{R}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;e^{-2R^{2}}+\mathbb{P}\big(\mathcal{M}\;\leqslant\;R^{-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\big)+C\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{M}\;\leqslant\;R^{-2-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}+\delta})^{\frac{1}{p}}\,.

(4.9)

Note that we have the union bound and
\gamma\to-\gamma
symmetry

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(\sqrt{M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})M_{-\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})}\less\varepsilon\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\mathbb{P}\big(\{M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})\less\varepsilon\}\cup\{M_{-\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})\less\varepsilon\}\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;2\mathbb{P}\big(M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})\less\varepsilon\big)\,.

(4.10)

Now by Lemma
4.4
we get that

\displaystyle Z_{R}

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;e^{-2R^{2}}+2e^{-c_{\gamma}R^{2}}+e^{-cR^{k}}

(4.11)

for some
k>2
. Now the claim follows.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})\less\varepsilon\big)

\displaystyle\;\leqslant\;\exp\big(-c\varepsilon^{-\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}}\big)

for some
c>0
and
\varepsilon
small enough.

Proof.

We check that
\widetilde{X}_{1}
satisfies the conditions of Theorem
1.3
. As the constant functions are the only harmonic functions on
{\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}}
, it follows that for any non-constant
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})
we have

\displaystyle\int_{({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})^{2}}G_{1}(x,y)f(x)f(y)\,dv_{1}(x)\,dv_{1}(x)>0\,,

where
G_{1}
is the zero-mean Green function. Thus, if
N
is a Gaussian random variable independent of
\widetilde{X}_{1}
, then
Y=\widetilde{X}_{1}+N
is a non-degenerate
\log
-correlated Gaussian field and it holds that
\widetilde{Y}=\widetilde{X}_{1}
where

\displaystyle\widetilde{Y}

\displaystyle:=Y-\tfrac{1}{v_{\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})}\int_{\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}}Y\,dv_{\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}}\,.

Now we have

\displaystyle M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}}}e^{\gamma\widetilde{X}_{1}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}s_{1}}\,d^{2}z=\int_{{\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}}}e^{\gamma\widetilde{X}_{1}(z)-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[(\widetilde{X}_{1}(z))^{2}]+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}h_{1}(z,z)}\,d^{2}z\,,

where
h_{1}
is a smooth function. Thus
M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})\;\geqslant\;cM_{\widetilde{X}_{1},\gamma}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})
. Now note that

\displaystyle M_{\widetilde{X}_{1},\gamma}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}}e^{\gamma\widetilde{Y}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[Y^{2}]}e^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[Y^{2}-(\widetilde{X}_{1})^{2}]}\,d^{2}z=e^{\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}\mathbb{E}[N^{2}]}\widetilde{M}_{Y,\gamma}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})\,.

Now the bound given by Theorem
1.3
then implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})<\varepsilon\big)\;\leqslant\;\exp\big(-c_{\gamma}c^{\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}}\varepsilon^{-\frac{4}{\gamma^{2}}}\big)

for some
c>0
.

∎

4.3.2
Upper bound

Lemma 4.5
.

We have

\displaystyle-\log Z_{R}\;\leqslant\;\widetilde{f}_{\gamma}\mu^{\frac{2}{\gamma Q}}R^{2}\,,

(4.12)

for some
\widetilde{f}_{\gamma}>0
.

Proof.

We set
\mu=1
, as replacing
R
by
\mu^{\frac{1}{\gamma Q}}R
will give the result for general
\mu>0
.

To begin, we truncate the zero-mode integral

\displaystyle Z_{R}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}[\exp(-R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}(e^{\gamma c}M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})+e^{-\gamma c}M_{-\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})))]\,dc

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;\int_{-\frac{1}{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{E}[\exp(-R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}(e^{\gamma c}M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})+e^{-\gamma c}M_{-\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})))]\,dc

\displaystyle\;\geqslant\;\mathbb{E}[\exp(-e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}(M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})+M_{-\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})))]\,.

By the Donsker–Varadhan theorem
3.11
, we have

\displaystyle-\log\mathbb{E}[\exp(-e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}(M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})+M_{-\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})))]

\displaystyle=\inf_{\mathbb{Q}\ll\mathbb{P}}\Big(e^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}R^{2+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})+M_{-\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})]+\operatorname{Ent}(\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{P})\Big)\,,

where
\mathbb{P}
is the probability law of
\widetilde{X}_{1}
. Now we can follow the argument given in the proof of Lemma
3.13
and the symmetry of
M_{\gamma,1}({\mathbb{T}_{1}^{2}})
under
\gamma\to-\gamma
when
\mathbb{Q}
is a law of a Gaussian field.
∎

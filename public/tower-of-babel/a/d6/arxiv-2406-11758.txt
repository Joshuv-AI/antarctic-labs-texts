Minkowski Inequalities and non-isolated hypersurface singularities
David B. Massey

Abstract
. We derive a number of inequalities involving Lê numbers of non-isolated hypersurface singularities. In particular, we derive Lê-Iomdine formulas with inequalities and use these, together with Teissier’s Minkowski inequalities for sectional Milnor numbers of isolated hypersurface singularities, to arrive at “Minkowski inequalities” for Lê numbers.

Throughout this paper, we use the following notation:
{\mathcal{U}}

Since we introduced the Lê numbers

Our main tool from obtaining inequalities when

Using the Lê-Iomdine inequalities, we prove in Proposition

:

Proposition
:
Suppose that the Lê numbers of
f
at the origin with respect to coordinates
{\mathbf{z}}
are defined. Then,

\sum_{j=0}^{s}(-1+{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}f)^{j}\lambda^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\geq(-1+{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}f)^{n+1},

with equality holding if

To generalize Teissier’s Minkowski inequalities to Lê numbers and non-isolated singularities, we need to use restrictions to
generic
linear subspaces and then use generic coordinates for calculating the Lê numbers. This is unusual for us, as we normally like to use coordinates which are “generic” in some sense which is easy to check in examples, such as just being generic enough that the Lê numbers are defined (as above). It also means that, in this paper, we will use two notations for the Lê numbers (at
{\mathbf{0}}
):
\lambda^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
when we are going to specify how generic the coordinates
{\mathbf{z}}
need to be, and
\lambda^{j}_{f}
when the coordinates are arbitrarily generic (i.e., so generic that all
\lambda^{j}_{f}
attain their generic values at a given point).

We also need to alter Teissier’s notation for the sectional Milnor numbers
\mu^{(k)}(f)
. We let
f^{[k]}
denote the restriction of
f
to a generic linear subspace of dimension
k
. With this notation, rather than writing
\mu^{(k)}(f)
for a sectional Milnor number, we will write
\mu(f^{[k]})
. The point is that we can now refer to
\lambda^{j}_{f^{[k]}}
, where we will always use generic coordinates to accompany the generic linear subspace restriction when
k\neq n+1
. Following Teissier, we define
\lambda^{0}_{f^{[0]}}=\mu^{(0)}(f)=1
.

The inequalities that we obtain when
s=1
are sharper than what we obtain we
s\geq 2
. So we separate the cases where
s=1
and where
s\geq 2
into different sections of this paper.

When
s=1
, we prove in Theorem
4.1
:

Theorem
: Suppose that
n\geq 1
,
s=1
, and the Lê numbers of
f
at the origin are defined. Then we have the following inequality:

\frac{\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+\big(\mu(f^{[n]})-\mu(f^{[n-1]})+1\big)\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}}{\mu(f^{[n]})}\ \geq\ \frac{\mu(f^{[n]})}{\mu(f^{[n-1]})}.

Note that if
s=0
, then
\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=0
and
\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=\mu(f)
, so that the inequality reduces to Teissier’s inequality.

The case when
s\geq 2
is complex enough that we will not state the result in the introduction. Also, there are a number of other interesting, relatively easy, inequalities with Lê numbers (and relative polar numbers) which we prove along the way.

Preliminary definitions and results

If

Corollary 2.2

Suppose that

\mu(f^{[k+1]})\geq[({\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}f)-1]\mu(f^{[k]})\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and so }\hskip 14.45377pt\mu(f^{[k+1]})\geq[({\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}f)-1]^{k+1}.

Another bit of notation: if we have an effective cycle

We should also remind the reader that we have the following:

Proposition 2.3

For generic

Remark 2.4
.

The reader should realize that Proposition
2.3
is far from obvious. It is certainly true that for a fixed cycle
C
, its multiplicity at the origin can be calculated as the intersection number in
{\mathcal{U}}
with a generic linear subspace of complementary dimension (see
[
1
]
, Example 12.4.5). However,
\Gamma^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
and
\Lambda^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
move as
{\mathbf{z}}
changes, and so the proof is highly non-trivial.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose that the Lê numbers of
f
at the origin with respect to coordinates
{\mathbf{z}}
are defined. Consider the relative polar curve as a cycle:
\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=\sum_{C}p_{C}\,C
, where the
C
are the irreducible curves germs at
{\mathbf{0}}
. Suppose that
\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\neq 0
or, equivalently, that
\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\neq 0
.

Then,

\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\geq\big({\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\big)\left({\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}\right)\right)\geq\big({\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\big)(-1+{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}f).

In particular, if
\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\neq 0
, then
\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\geq-1+{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}f
.

Proof.

First note that if
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}
were identically zero, then the relative polar curve
\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
, as a set, would be empty; hence, as a cycle, would be zero. We have excluded this case.

Now suppose that
\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}
is not identically zero. Then,

\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=\left(\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\cdot V\Big(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}\Big)\right)_{\mathbf{0}}=\left(\big(\sum_{C}p_{C}\,C\big)\cdot V\Big(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}\Big)\right)_{\mathbf{0}}.

But it is well-known (and easy for the intersections of curves and hypersurfaces) that

\left(C\cdot V\Big(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}\Big)\right)_{\mathbf{0}}\geq\big({\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}C\big)\left({\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}\right)\right);

see Corollary 12.4 of
[
1
]
for the general statement. This proves the first inequality.

The second inequality is also easy. If the coordinates are generic, then we would have

{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}=-1+{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}f.

However, since we are assuming only that the Lê numbers are defined, we know only that

{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}\geq-1+{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}f.

The second inequality follows.
∎

If we are willing to assume that the relative polar numbers of
f
at the origin with respect to
{\mathbf{z}}
are defined, then we can generalize Lemma
2.5
. :

Proposition 2.6
.

Suppose that the Lê and relative polar numbers at the origin are defined. Then,

\lambda^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+\gamma^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\geq(-1+{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}f)\big({\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}\Gamma^{j+1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\big).

Thus, with generic coordinates, we have

\lambda^{j}_{f}+\gamma^{j}_{f}\geq(-1+{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}f)\gamma^{j+1}_{f}.

Furthermore, if
f
is a homogeneous polynomial, then equality holds in the above inequality for generic coordinates.

Proof.

This is completely straightforward.

By definition,

\Lambda^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+\Gamma^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=\Gamma^{j+1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\cdot V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{j}}\right).

Thus,

\lambda^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+\gamma^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=\Big(\Gamma^{j+1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\cdot V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{j}}\right)\cdot V(z_{0},\dots,z_{j-1})\Big)_{\mathbf{0}}.

But then by Corollary 12.4 of
[
1
]
,

\lambda^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+\gamma^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\geq\left({\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}V\Big(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{j}}\Big)\right)\big({\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}\Gamma^{j+1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\big),

which is greater than or equal to
(-1+{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}f)\big({\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}\Gamma^{j+1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\big)
.

The claim involving equality for homogeneous polynomials follows from Theorem 1.72 of
[
8
]
.
∎

3.
Lê-Iomdine Formulas with Inequalities

In this section, we will alter our statement of Theorem 4.5 of
[
7
]
, the Lê-Iomdine formulas, to include an inequality statement; this essentially follows immediately form the proof in
[
7
]
, with just a slight modification. However, we also improve the needed bound on the exponent.

We need to discuss briefly our use of not-so-generic for the Lê numbers in some results; in the next theorem, we let

{\mathbf{z}}=(z_{0},\dots,z_{n})
be a linear choice of coordinates such that the Lê numbers of
f
at the origin are defined. This is a fairly weak condition on
{\mathbf{z}}
which just means that all intersections which are involved are proper. It is very convenient in examples to
not
require more genericity of
{\mathbf{z}}
.

In addition, the Lê numbers at the origin being defined implies that each of the relative polar varieties/cycles
\Gamma^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
has the correct dimension, i.e., is purely
j

\Gamma^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
has no irreducible components, that is, is empty/zero.

However, it is important to realize that the Lê numbers being defined does
not
imply that the relative polar numbers are defined. Thus, in Item 5 of the following theorem, we have the added hypothesis that
\gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
is defined, i.e., that
\dim_{\mathbf{0}}\big(\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\cap V(z_{0}))\leq 0
(where
<0
would mean
{\mathbf{0}}
is not in
\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
). It is worth noting
\gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}({\mathbf{0}})
being defined implies that, near
{\mathbf{0}}\in\Sigma f
,
\Sigma\big(f_{|_{V(z_{0})}}\big)=\Sigma f\cap V(z_{0})
; see Remark 1.10 in
[
7
]
.

Now we need a definition of

; we follow
[

]
and

]
(see also

]
, Definition 4.1).

Definition 3.1

Lemma 3.2

Suppose that the Lê numbers

\left(C\cdot V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}\right)\right)_{{}_{\mathbf{0}}}\geq\big({\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}C\big)\left({\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}\right)\right)=\big((C\cdot V(z_{0}))_{\mathbf{0}}\big)\left({\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}\right)\right),

\frac{(C\cdot V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}\right))_{{}_{\mathbf{0}}}}{(C\cdot V(z_{0}))_{{}_{\mathbf{0}}}}+1\geq\left({\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}\right)\right)+1\geq{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}f.

We need the following trivial lemma. Even though the proof is via elementary school algebra, the inequality is important for us and is not obvious, so we give the short argument.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose that
a
and
b
are real numbers, that
a\geq 1
and
b\geq 1
. Then,

a\geq b\hskip 7.22743pt\textnormal{ if and only if }\hskip 7.22743pta-b+1\geq\frac{a}{b}.

Proof.

If
b=1
, then the conclusion is that
a\geq 1
if and only if
a\geq a
. As we are assuming that
a\geq 1
, this statement is true.

Now suppose that
b>1

a\geq b\enskip\Leftrightarrow\enskip a(b-1)\geq b(b-1)\enskip\Leftrightarrow\enskip ab-a\geq b^{2}-b\enskip\Leftrightarrow\enskip ab-b^{2}+b\geq a\enskip\Leftrightarrow\enskip a-b+1\geq\frac{a}{b}.

∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose that the Lê numbers
\lambda^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
are defined.

(1)

We have the inequality

\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+1\geq\operatorname{mpr}\{f,{\mathbf{z}}\}.

(2)

Suppose also that
{\mathbf{z}}

\gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}

\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}-\lambda^{0}_{f_{|_{V(z_{0})}},\check{{\mathbf{z}}}}+2=\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}-\gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+2\geq\operatorname{mpr}\{f,{\mathbf{z}}\}.

We work in a neighborhood of the origin.

Item 1 is trivial given the definitions of
\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
and the maximum polar multiplicity.

Proof of Item 2:

First, note that
\lambda^{0}_{f_{|_{V(z_{0})}},\check{{\mathbf{z}}}}=\gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
by Proposition 1.21 of
[

Now if

Suppose then that
\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\neq 0

\gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}={\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}

Let
C

for which the polar ratio is maximal. As

\left(C\cdot V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}\right)\right)_{\mathbf{0}}\geq\left(C\cdot V(z_{0})\right)_{\mathbf{0}}\geq 1

Thus,

,
\lambda^{0}_{f}-\gamma^{1}_{f}+2\geq\operatorname{mpr}\{f,{\mathbf{z}}\}
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let

\check{{\mathbf{z}}}:=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})

Then, for all but a finite number of complex

(1)

\Sigma(f+az_{0}^{m})=\Sigma f\cap V(z_{0})
as germs of sets at
{\mathbf{0}}
,

(2)

If

{\operatorname{dim}}_{\mathbf{0}}\Sigma(f+az_{0}^{m})=s-1

(3)

The Lê numbers of

(4)

For

\lambda^{j}_{f+az_{0}^{m},\tilde{{\mathbf{z}}}}=(m-1)\lambda^{j+1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}},

\lambda^{0}_{f+az_{0}^{m},\tilde{{\mathbf{z}}}}\leq\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+(m-1)\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}};

(5)

•

\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\neq 0

m\geq 1+\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}

•

\gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}

(6)

If
\gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
is defined, then we have also
\lambda^{0}_{f+az_{0}^{m},\tilde{{\mathbf{z}}}}\leq(m-1)\lambda^{0}_{f_{|_{V(z_{0})}},\check{{\mathbf{z}}}}

Proof.

The proof of this is identical to that of Theorem 4.5 of

hold and that Item (ii) of that lemma holds with the equality replaced by inequality

Proof of Lemma 4.3, Item (i) from
[
7
]
for
m\geq 2

In fact, when writing Lemma 4.3, we omitted the trivial case where
\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=0

\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=0

So assume that
\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=\sum p_{C}[C]\neq 0

1

, for all but a finite number of
a

\dim_{\mathbf{0}}\left(C\cap V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}+maz_{0}^{m-1}\right)\right)=0

is defined. Therefore, for each

Proof of Items (4), (5), and
(6)
for

The equalities in Items (4) and (5) are proved exactly as in the proof of Theorem 4.5 of

The proof of Theorem 4.5 of

\lambda^{0}_{f+az_{0}^{m},\tilde{{\mathbf{z}}}}=\left(\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\cdot V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{0}}+maz_{0}^{m-1}\right)\right)_{\mathbf{0}}+(m-1)\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}.

Remark 3.6
.

It will be important to us in the next section that Theorem
3.5
applies to the case where
\dim_{0}\Sigma f=0
. In this case, for
j\geq 1
,
\lambda^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=0
, and
\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=\mu(f)
the Milnor number of
f
at the origin; if we let

, then we have that, for all but a finite number of
a
,
f+az_{0}^{m}
has an isolated critical point at
{\mathbf{0}}
and
\mu(f+az_{0}^{m})=\mu(f)
.

Before we get to versions of the Minkowski inequalities in the next section, we have the following easy to obtain bound:

Proposition 3.7
.

Suppose that the Lê numbers of
f
at the origin with respect to coordinates
{\mathbf{z}}
are defined. Then,

\sum_{j=0}^{s}(-1+{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}f)^{j}\lambda^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\geq(-1+{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}f)^{n+1},

with equality holding if
f
is a homogeneous polynomial.

Proof.

Let
m:={\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}f
. Then, for all but a finite number of
a_{j}
,

g:=f+a_{0}z_{0}^{m}+a_{1}z_{1}^{m}+\cdots+a_{s-1}z_{s-1}^{m}

has multiplicity
m
, has a
0
-dimensional critical locus, and Theorem
3.5
may be applied inductively to conclude that

\sum_{j=0}^{s}(-1+m)^{j}\lambda^{j}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\geq\mu(g)\geq(-1+m)^{n+1}.

The equality for homogeneous polynomials is Corollary 4.7 of
[
7
]
.
∎

Example 3.8
.

Consider
f(x,y,z)=(x^{2}-z^{2}+y^{2})(x-z)
, and use
{\mathbf{z}}:=(x,y,z)
for our coordinate system. Then
\Sigma f=V(x-z,y)
, and we find:

\Gamma^{2}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial z}\right)=V\big(2z(x-z)+(x^{2}-z^{2}+y^{2})\big),

\Gamma^{2}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\cdot V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right)=V\big(2z(x-z)+(x^{2}-z^{2}+y^{2})\big)\cdot V(y(x-z))=

V\big(y,2z(x-z)+(x^{2}-z^{2})\big)+2V(x-z,y)=V(y,3z+x)+3V(x-z,y)=\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+\Lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}},

and

\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\cdot V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)=V(y,3z+x)\cdot V(2x(x-z)+x^{2}-z^{2}+y^{2})=

V(y,3z+x,2x(x-z)+x^{2}-z^{2})=V(y,3z+x,2x+x+z)+V(y,3z+x,x-z)=2[\{{\mathbf{0}}\}].

Thus,
\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=3
and
\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=2
.

Therefore, the equality guaranteed by Proposition
3.7
reads

2+(-1+3)\cdot 3=(-1+3)^{3}.

Example 3.9
.

Consider
f(t,x,y)=y^{3}-x^{4}-t^{2}x^{2}
, where we use the coordinates
{\mathbf{z}}:=(t,x,y)
. One easily finds that
\Sigma f=V(x,y)
.

Now, we calculate, as cycles:

\Gamma^{2}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\right)=V(3y^{2})=2[V(y)],

\Gamma^{2}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\cdot V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}\right)=2V(y,-4x^{3}-2t^{2}x)=2V(2x^{2}+t^{2},y)+2V(x,y)=\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+\Lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}},

and

\Gamma^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\cdot V\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\right)=2V(2x^{2}+t^{2},y)\cdot V(tx^{2})=2V(2x^{2}+t^{2},y)\cdot V(t)+2V(2x^{2}+t^{2},y)\cdot V(x^{2})=12[\{{\mathbf{0}}\}].

Thus,
\lambda^{1}_{f}=2
and
\lambda^{0}_{f}=12
.

The inequality in Proposition
3.7
tells us that

16=12+(-1+3)\cdot 2\geq(-1+3)^{3}=8.

4.
Minkowski Inequalities: the
1
-dimensional case

We continue with the notation for the introduction:
{\mathcal{U}}
is an open neighborhood of the origin in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
,
f:({\mathcal{U}},{\mathbf{0}})\rightarrow({\mathbb{C}},0)
is a complex analytic function which is nowhere locally constant. We assume that
{\mathbf{0}}\in\Sigma f
.

Before we state and prove a Minkowski inequality, we remind the reader of the notation from the introduction:
f^{[k]}
denotes the restriction of
f
to a generic linear subspace of dimension
k
.

We will also need to use that
(f+az_{0}^{m})^{[k]}=f^{[k]}+az_{0}^{m}
. Perhaps this seems obvious to the reader, but, if not, we wish to give a bit of an intuitive explanation which may help in thinking about it. Consider the case where
k=n
, i.e., where we are restricting to a generic hyperplane
H
through the origin. As we are assuming that
H
is generic, we may take

H=V(z_{1}-b_{2}z_{2}-b_{3}z_{3}-\dots-b_{n}z_{n}-b_{0}z_{0})

for a generic choice of the complex numbers
b_{k}
. This means that, in the functions
f
and
f+az_{0}^{m}
, we replace
z_{1}
with
b_{2}z_{2}+b_{3}z_{3}+\dots+b_{n}z_{n}+b_{0}z_{0}
; clearly then
(f+az_{0}^{m})_{|_{H}}=f_{|_{H}}+az_{0}^{m}
. One continues in this fashion with each successive hyperplane slice, next replacing
z_{2}
with a generic linear combination of
z_{3},\dots,z_{n},z_{0}
and so on.

With this discussion out of the way, we may now use the Minkowski inequalities for isolated singularities, combined with the Lê-Iomdine formulas (with inequalities), to prove that a Minkowski-type inequality holds when
\dim_{\mathbf{0}}\Sigma f=1
. First, however, we need to address the case where
n=1
, i.e., where
f
defines a non-reduced plane curve. In order to interpret the theorem below and its proof in this case, we remind the reader that, by definition,
\mu^{(0)}(f)=\mu(f^{[0]})=1
; we also need to point out that
\gamma^{1}_{f^{[1]}}=1=\mu(f^{[0]})
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
n\geq 1
,
s=1
, and the Lê numbers of
f
at the origin are defined. Then the following inequality holds:

\frac{\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+\big(\mu(f^{[n]})-\mu(f^{[n-1]})+1\big)\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}}{\mu(f^{[n]})}\ \geq\ \frac{\mu(f^{[n]})}{\mu(f^{[n-1]})}.

Proof.

Let
\tilde{{\mathbf{z}}}=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n},z_{0})
and let
a\neq 0
be such that Items (1)-(4) of Theorem
3.5
hold for
f
, for
f^{[n]}
, and for
f^{[n-1]}
.

Then we have

\frac{\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+(m-1)\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}}{\mu(f^{[n]}+az_{0}^{m})}\geq\frac{\mu(f+az_{0}^{m})}{\mu(f^{[n]}+az_{0}^{m})}\geq\frac{\mu(f^{[n]}+az_{0}^{m})}{\mu(f^{[n-1]}+az_{0}^{m})}\geq\frac{\mu(f^{[n]}+az_{0}^{m})}{\mu(f^{[n-1]})}

where the middle inequality follows from Teissier’s Theorem
2.1
, and the other two inequalities follow from the inequality in Item (4) of Theorem
3.5
.

As we use generic coordinates for
f^{[n]}
,
\gamma^{1}_{f^{[n]}}
is defined and
\gamma^{1}_{f^{[n]}}={\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}\Gamma^{1}_{f^{[n]}}
. Now, applying Item (5) of Theorem
3.5
, the inequality follows by letting
m=\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n]}}+\lambda^{1}_{f^{[n]}}-\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n-1]}}+2
and noting that, since
f^{[n]}
has an isolated critical point at
{\mathbf{0}}
, we have that
\lambda^{1}_{f^{[n]}}=0
,
\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n]}}=\mu(f^{[n]})
, and
\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n-1]}}=\mu(f^{[n-1]})
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The reader should note that the inequality that is concluded in Theorem
4.1
collapses to Teissier’s inequality if
\dim_{\mathbf{0}}\Sigma f=0
, since then
\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=0
. Thus Theorem
4.1
holds for
\dim_{\mathbf{0}}\Sigma f\leq 1
.

One might wonder, in analogy to the isolated critical point case, if we always have
\lambda^{0}_{f}\geq\mu(f^{[n]})
. The answer is “no”. Counterexamples to this “hope” are not hard to find; in fact, we looked at one earlier:

Example 4.3
.

Let us return to Example
3.8
, where
f(x,y,z)=(x^{2}-z^{2}+y^{2})(x-z)
and
{\mathbf{z}}=(x,y,z)
. There we found
\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=3
and
\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=2
.

Now, as
f
is homogeneous degree 3 with a 1-dimensional critical locus,
f^{[2]}
and
f^{[1]}
have isolated critical points and are also homogeneous of degree 3; thus
\mu(f^{[2]})=(3-1)^{2}=4
and
\mu(f^{[1]})=(3-1)=2
. Hence, in this example
\mu(f^{[2]})>\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
.

For this example, the inequality in Theorem
4.1
gives us:

\frac{11}{4}=\frac{2+(4-2+1)\cdot 3}{4}=\frac{\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+\big(\mu(f^{[2]})-\mu(f^{[1]})+1\big)\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}}{\mu(f^{[2]})}\ \ \geq\ \frac{\mu(f^{[2]})}{\mu(f^{[1]})}=\frac{4}{2}=2.

The reader may wonder if the fact that
\mu(f^{[2]})>\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
in this example is due to the fact that our coordinates for calculating the Lê numbers were not generic enough, since we required only that the Lê numbers were defined. Is it possible in this example that, had we chosen our coordinates
{\mathbf{z}}
really generic, that we would have
\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\geq\mu(f^{[2]})
?

In fact,
\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=3
and
\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=2

are
the generic values. To see this, note that
f_{|_{V(x)}}
and
f_{|_{V(x,y)}}
have isolated critical points at the origin; thus, the coordinates are
prepolar
as defined in
[
7
]
. Therefore, by Theorem 3.3 of
[
7
]
,
b_{2}(f)-b_{1}(f)=\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}-\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
, where
b_{2}(f)
and
b_{1}(f)
are the Betti numbers in degrees 2 and 1, respectively, of the Milnor fiber of
f
at the origin. Thus, if
\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
has its generic value, then so does
\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
. But for
\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
to obtain its generic value, it is enough for the Lê numbers to be defined and for each irreducible component
C
of
\Sigma f
at
{\mathbf{0}}
to have the intersection number
(C\cdot V(x))_{\mathbf{0}}
equal to
\operatorname{mult}_{\mathbf{0}}C
; this common value is 1 in the present example.

Example 4.4
.

Let us return to Example
3.9
where we considered
f(t,x,y)=y^{3}-x^{4}-t^{2}x^{2}
and used coordinates
{\mathbf{z}}:=(t,x,y)
. We found that
\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=2
and
\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=12
.

Now,
\mu(f^{[1]})=-1+{\operatorname{mult}}_{\mathbf{0}}f=-1+3=2
. One either checks by hand or by computer-algebra system (CAS) that
\mu(f^{[2]})=6
.

Now the inequality in Theorem
4.1
gives us:

\frac{11}{3}=\frac{12+(6-2+1)\cdot 2}{6}=\frac{\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+\big(\mu(f^{[2]})-\mu(f^{[1]})+1\big)\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}}{\mu(f^{[2]})}\ \ \geq\ \frac{\mu(f^{[2]})}{\mu(f^{[1]})}=\frac{6}{2}=3.

5.
Minkowski Inequalities: higher dimensions

The relative polar numbers do
not
have nice Lê-Iomdine equalities we would need to inductively use the expression
\mu(f^{[n]})-\gamma^{1}_{f^{[n]}}+2
for an upper-bound on the maximum polar multiplicity of
f^{[n]}
. Consequently, we will use the other upper-bound from Theorem
3.5
: if
\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n]}}\neq 0
and
1+\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n]}}\geq\operatorname{mpr}\{f^{[n]},\hat{{\mathbf{z}}}\}
in our induction (where
\hat{{\mathbf{z}}}
is a set of generic coordinates for
f^{[n]}
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
n\geq 1
and the Lê numbers of
f
at the origin with respect to
{\mathbf{z}}
are defined. Further, suppose that
\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n]}}\neq 0
. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+k_{1}\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+k_{1}k_{2}\lambda^{2}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+\cdots+k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{s}\lambda^{s}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}}{\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n]}}+k_{1}\lambda^{1}_{f^{[n]}}+k_{1}k_{2}\lambda^{2}_{f^{[n]}}+\cdots+k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{s-1}\lambda^{s-1}_{f^{[n]}}}\geq\hfill

\displaystyle\hfill\frac{\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n]}}+k_{1}\lambda^{1}_{f^{[n]}}+k_{1}k_{2}\lambda^{2}_{f^{[n]}}+\cdots+k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{s-1}\lambda^{s-1}_{f^{[n]}}}{\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n-1]}}+k_{1}\lambda^{1}_{f^{[n-1]}}+k_{1}k_{2}\lambda^{2}_{f^{[n-1]}}+\cdots+k_{1}k_{2}\dots k_{s-2}\lambda^{s-2}_{f^{[n-1]}}}

that is,

\frac{\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+\sum_{i=1}^{s}\big[\lambda^{i}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\big(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}k_{\ell}\big)\big]}{\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n]}}+\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}\big[\lambda^{i}_{f^{[n]}}\big(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}k_{\ell}\big)\big]}\geq\frac{\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n]}}+\sum_{i=1}^{s-1}\big[\lambda^{i}_{f^{[n]}}\big(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}k_{\ell}\big)\big]}{\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n-1]}}+\sum_{i=1}^{s-2}\big[\lambda^{i}_{f^{[n-1]}}\big(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}k_{\ell}\big)\big]},

where

•

k_{1}=\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n]}}
,

•

k_{2}=\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n]}}+k_{1}\lambda^{1}_{f^{[n]}}
,

•

k_{3}=\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n]}}+k_{1}\lambda^{1}_{f^{[n]}}+k_{1}k_{2}\lambda^{2}_{f^{[n]}}
, and generally

•

k_{p}=\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n]}}+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}\big[\lambda^{i}_{f^{[n]}}\big(\prod_{\ell=1}^{i}k_{\ell}\big)\big]
, for
1\leq p\leq s
.

Proof.

This immediate from an inductive application of Theorem
3.5
using
m=1+\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n]},{\mathbf{z}}}
, exactly as in Corollary 4.6 of
[
7
]
(except there, we did not select the
j_{k}
’s to be as small as possible).
∎

Remark 5.2
.

We have several comments to make about Theorem
5.1
.

First, note that the denominator on the left, which equals the numerator on the right, is equal to
k_{s}
.

Second, note that
\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n]}}=\gamma^{1}_{f}+\lambda^{1}_{f}
,
\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n-1]}}=\gamma^{2}_{f}+\lambda^{2}_{f}
and, for
i\geq 1
,
\lambda^{i}_{f^{[n]}}=\lambda^{i+1}_{f}
and
\lambda^{i}_{f^{[n-1]}}=\lambda^{i+2}_{f}
.

Third, downward induction on
n
yields the further descending inequalities as in Teissier’s sequence of inequalities.

Finally, suppose that
\lambda^{0}_{f^{[n]}}=0
; by Proposition 1.21 of
[
7
]
, this is equivalent to
\lambda^{0}_{f}=\lambda^{1}_{f}=0
(note the generic coordinates). Let
\omega
be the largest dimension such that
\lambda^{0}_{f^{[\omega]}}\neq 0
. As
{\mathbf{0}}\in\Sigma f
and
\lambda^{0}_{f^{[1]}}=-1+{\operatorname{mult}}f
, we know that
\omega\geq 1
. Then one can replace
f
in Theorem
5.1
by
f^{[1+\omega]}
and replace
n
by
\omega
. Then one can apply Proposition 1.21 of
[
7
]
to obtain inequalities for the generic Lê numbers of the original
f
: for
0\leq j\leq n-\omega
,
\lambda^{j}_{f}
would be zero and, for
j\geq n-\omega+1
,
\lambda^{j}_{f}=\lambda^{j-n+\omega}_{f^{[1+\omega]}}
.

Of course, examples get more complicated for higher-dimensional critical locus.

Example 5.3
.

Let
f(w,x,y,z)=z^{2}+(w^{4}+x^{3}+y^{2})^{2}
and use
{\mathbf{z}}:=(w,x,y,z)
for coordinates. Then, one easily finds that
\Sigma f=V(z,w^{6}+x^{4}+y^{3})
, and so
s=2
. By hand or by (CAS), one finds
\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=14
,
\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=3
, and
\lambda^{2}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=2
.

Now,
f^{[3]}(w,x,y)=(aw+bx+cy)^{2}+(w^{4}+x^{3}+y^{2})^{2}
for generic
(a,b,c)\in{\mathbb{C}}^{3}
and
\Sigma(f^{[3]})=V(aw+bx+cy,w^{4}+x^{3}+y^{2})
. One calculates
\lambda^{0}_{f^{[3]}}=5
and
\lambda^{1}_{f^{[3]}}=2
.

Finally, one calculates
\lambda^{0}_{f^{[2]}}=\mu(f^{[2]})=3
.

Theorem
5.1
tells us that

\frac{\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+k_{1}\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+k_{1}k_{2}\lambda^{2}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}}{\lambda^{0}_{f^{[3]}}+k_{1}\lambda^{1}_{f^{[3]}}}\geq\frac{\lambda^{0}_{f^{[3]}}+k_{1}\lambda^{1}_{f^{[3]}}}{\lambda^{0}_{f^{[2]}}},

where
k_{1}=\lambda^{0}_{f^{[3]}}=5
and
k_{2}=\lambda^{0}_{f^{[3]}}+k_{1}\lambda^{1}_{f^{[3]}}=5+5\cdot 2=15
.

Thus, our “Minkowski inequality” becomes

\frac{179}{15}=\frac{14+5\cdot 3+5\cdot 15\cdot 2}{5+5\cdot 2}\geq\frac{5+5\cdot 2}{3}=5.

6.
Questions and remarks

When we first began writing this paper, in the case where
s=1
, we used our classic Lê-Iomdine formulas with
\lambda^{0}_{f+az_{0}^{j},\tilde{{\mathbf{z}}}}({\mathbf{0}})=\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}({\mathbf{0}})+(j-1)\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}({\mathbf{0}})
, if
j\geq 1+\lambda^{0}_{f,\mathbf{z}}({\mathbf{0}})
. This gave us two cases.

Case 1
: Suppose that
\mu(f^{[n]})\geq\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
. Then we obtained an inequality that follows trivially from Theorem
4.1
, namely that

\frac{\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}}{\mu(f^{[n]})}+\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\ \geq\ \frac{\mu(f^{[n]})}{\mu(f^{[n-1]})}.

Case 2
: Suppose that
\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\geq\mu(f^{[n]})
. Then we obtained the inequality

[formula]

\frac{\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}}{\mu(f^{[n]})}\big(1+\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\big)\ \geq\ \frac{\mu(f^{[n]})}{\mu(f^{[n-1]})}.

Of course, if
\lambda^{0}_{,{\mathbf{z}}}>\mu(f^{[n]})
, then the inequality in Case 1 is a stronger inequality, i.e., closer to being an equality, and implies the inequality in (
\dagger
).

On the other hand, if
\mu(f^{[n]})>\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
, then the inequality in (
\dagger
) is stronger than the other inequality, but
does the inequality (
\dagger
) always hold

The answer is: certainly not. By taking a cross-product family (or a
\mu

f(x,y,z)=y^{2}+z^{2}
, we have a example where
\mu(f^{[n]})>\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=0
, and so (
\dagger
) cannot hold.

The interesting question is
:

Question 6.1
.

Suppose that
s=1
. Is there an example where

\mu(f^{[n]})>\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}>0\hskip 14.45377pt\textnormal{ and }\hskip 14.45377pt\frac{\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}}{\mu(f^{[n]})}\big(1+\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\big)\ <\ \frac{\mu(f^{[n]})}{\mu(f^{[n-1]})}?

Thus far, we have found no such example, but we are not ready to conjecture that (
\dagger
) always holds.

One might search for homogeneous examples that give an affirmative answer to the above question, but the following proposition tells us there are no such examples.

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose that
s=1
, the Lê numbers exist,
\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\neq 0
, and
f
is a homogeneous polynomial. Then,

\frac{\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}}{\mu(f^{[n]})}\big(1+\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\big)\ \geq\ \frac{\mu(f^{[n]})}{\mu(f^{[n-1]})}.

Proof.

Suppose to the contrary that

[formula]

\frac{\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}}{\mu(f^{[n]})}\big(1+\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\big)\ <\ \frac{\mu(f^{[n]})}{\mu(f^{[n-1]})}.

We shall derive a contradiction.

Let
d\geq 2
equal the degree of
f
, where
d
is necessarily at least 2. We know that
\mu(f^{[n]})=(d-1)^{n}
and
\mu(f^{[n-1}])=(d-1)^{n-1}
. Furthermore, Corollary 4.7 of
[
7
]
tells us that
\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}=(d-1)^{n+1}-(d-1)\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
. Thus
\lambda^{0}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\neq 0
implies that
(d-1)^{n+1}-(d-1)\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}>0
, i.e., that
\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}<(d-1)^{n}
.

The inequality (
*
) becomes

[formula]

\frac{(d-1)^{n+1}-(d-1)\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}}{(d-1)^{n}}\big(1+\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\big)\ <\ d-1.

Therefore,

\big[(d-1)^{n+1}-(d-1)\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\big]\big(1+\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\big)<(d-1)^{n+1},

and so

(d-1)^{n+1}-(d-1)\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}+\big[(d-1)^{n+1}-(d-1)\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}\big]\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}<(d-1)^{n+1}.

Subtracting
(d-1)^{n+1}
from each side, and using that
\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}>0
, we conclude that

-(d-1)+(d-1)^{n+1}-(d-1)\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}<0,

i.e., that
(d-1)^{n}-1<\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
.

But now, we have
(d-1)^{n}-1<\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}<(d-1)^{n}
, which is a contradiction (since
\lambda^{1}_{f,{\mathbf{z}}}
is an integer).
∎

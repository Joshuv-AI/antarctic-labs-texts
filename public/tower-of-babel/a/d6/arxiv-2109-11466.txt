Explosive growth for a constrained Hastings–Levitov aggregation model
Nathanaël Berestycki University of Vienna nathanael.berestycki@univie.ac.at Vittoria Silvestri University of Rome La Sapienza silvestri@mat.uniroma1.it

Abstract
We consider a constrained version of the HL ( 0 ) (0) Hastings–Levitov model of aggregation in the complex plane, in which particles can only attach to the part of the cluster that has already been grown. Although one might expect that this gives rise to a non-trivial limiting shape, we prove that the cluster grows explosively: in the upper half plane, the aggregate accumulates infinite diameter as soon as it reaches positive capacity. More precisely, we show that after n ​ t nt particles of (half-plane) capacity 1 / ( 2 ​ n ) 1/(2n) have attached, the diameter of the shape is highly concentrated around t ​ log ⁡ n \sqrt{t\log n} , uniformly in t ∈ [ 0 , T ] t\in[0,T] . This illustrates a new instability phenomenon for the growth of single trees/fjords in unconstrained HL ( 0 ) (0) .

1.2
Geometric consequences and conjectures

We use Theorem
1.1
to deduce geometric information
about

\mathcal{K}_{nt}

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\diam(\mathcal{K}_{nt})=\sup\{|x-y|:x,y\in\mathcal{K}_{nt}\}

\frac{\diam(\mathcal{K}_{nt})}{\sqrt{t\log n}}\to 1

in probability. Furthermore,

\max_{z\in\mathcal{K}_{nt}}\{\Im(z)\}\leq\sqrt{2t},

almost surely.

The above theorem says that the shape of the cluster is very elongated, being of order

It is tempting to conjecture that the cluster shape (viewed from the outside) is more precisely described by the following Loewner–Kufarev evolution: namely, denote by

\begin{cases}\dot{f}_{t}(z)=\partial_{z}f_{t}(z)\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{x-z}\mu_{t}(dx)\\

1.3
Aggregation outside the unit disc

We have so far focused for convenience and ease on the situation in the upper-half plane, but it is in fact more standard to consider HL
(0)
as an aggregation model outside the unit disc (the recent paper
[
3
]
by Berger, Procaccia and Turner being the exception). The model is defined similarly by replacing the upper-half plane
\mathbb{H}
with the unit disc
\mathbb{D}
, and the elementary maps describing the slits are obtained from
F
by conjugating with respect to a conformal map from
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{D}
to
\mathbb{H}
. See e.g.
[
14
]
for details.

In order to keep the paper short and simple, we decided not to include results related to this case,
although it should not be hard to see that our techniques apply to this setting as well
, at least as long as the two arms of the cluster remain macroscopically far apart (i.e., they do not shield the unit disc almost entirely).
In particular, we believe that the following result holds
: for any
0<\alpha<1
, let
\tau_{\alpha}
denote the first
k\geq 1
such that the
k
th particle attaches at a position whose argument is in
[\pi(1-\alpha),\pi(1+\alpha)]
. Then, for any
0<\alpha<1
, as
n\to\infty
,

{\tau_{\alpha}}\sim{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{2\pi^{2}n(1-\alpha)^{2}}{\log n}}}

(1.7)

in probability, where
X_{n}\sim Y_{n}
in probability as
n\to\infty
means
X_{n}/Y_{n}\to 1
in probability. Observe that the surprising phenomenon here is that
\tau_{\alpha}=o(n)
.

1.4
Heuristic relation to instability in unconstrained Hastings–Levitov

Compared to the unconstrained model (especially the disc one discussed in Section
1.3
), the only difference with our constrained model is that particles can only attach to the tree generated by the first particle. In other words, the constrained model describes the growth of a
single
tree. From this perspective, the explosive growth demonstrated in Theorem
1.1
shows a fundamental instability property which seems not to have been noticed before: left to its own devices, a single tree or fjord grows explosively. The existence of a shape theorem for regular (i.e., unconstrained) HL
(0)
appears from this point of view nothing short of miraculous: essentially, this suggests that multiple fjords and subtrees are “canceling each other out” from the point of view of conformal maps.

We believe that this effect becomes less pronounced when particle sizes are rescaled according to the derivative of the conformal map at the location where particles attach – this is for instance the situation of the HL
(\alpha)
, for
\alpha>0
(see e.g.
[
9
]
,
[
12
]
and
[
13
]
for a definition and some fine properties of the model).

It has been proposed (and indeed proved for
\alpha<1
in
[
12
]
for the unconstrained model)
that compensating the size of the particles by a power of the derivative of the conformal map has an overall identical effect as keeping the size fixed but reweighing the law of the attachment by a power of the harmonic measure. In the constrained model however we expect that these two models behave differently for every
\alpha>0
, and the constrained model might well remain explosive even for values of
\alpha
above
\alpha=1
.

The main argument for proving Theorem

\frac{d\alpha}{dt}\approx\frac{1}{8\alpha(t)}.

(1.8)

This comes from a dyadic decomposition where we consider the contribution to the position of the front
R_{nt}
of particles arriving at time
k\in[nt,n(t+h)]
in a dyadic interval

2^{j}
at distance
2^{j}
from the right front

. Here one should think that
2^{j}
is much smaller than
n
, but at least
1/\sqrt{n}
; with the minimal distance
1/\sqrt{n}
determined by the behaviour of the conformal map which uniformises a single particle. We show that the contribution to the push of the front of scale
j
during this interval is, roughly,

\Delta_{j}\approx nh\times\frac{|I_{j}|}{2\alpha\sqrt{\log n}}\times\frac{\log 2}{n2^{j+1}}\approx\frac{h\log 2}{4\alpha\sqrt{\log n}}

where the first term

is the total number of particles arriving during the interval, the second term corresponds to the probability for a particle to fall in
I_{j}
, and the last term gives the average push caused by such a particle. Since the right hand side does not depend on

, we see that all scales contribute equally. This is the reason beyond the explosive growth.

Summed over all scales, this gives

R_{(t+h)n}-R_{tn}\approx\sum_{j}\Delta_{j}\approx\frac{h\sqrt{\log n}}{8\alpha(t)}

where the constant

from the tip of the interval. Dividing by
\sqrt{\log n}
to express the left hand side in terms of

satisfies the differential equation (
1.8
).

Equivalently, starting from very small

so that, for arbitrary

For the lower bound, we proceed similarly and note that

\displaystyle\geq\int^{R_{i}-2^{j}}_{R_{i}-2^{j+1}}\frac{1}{2(R_{i}-x)}\frac{dx}{n2^{j}}-\int^{R_{i}-2^{j}}_{R_{i}-2^{j+1}}\frac{C}{(R_{i}-x)^{3}}\frac{dx}{n^{2}2^{j}}

as desired.
Finally,

\displaystyle\var(R_{i+1}-R_{i}|x_{i+1}\in I_{i,j})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}((R_{i+1}-R_{i})^{2}|x_{i+1}\in I_{i,j})

\displaystyle\leq\int_{2^{j}}^{2^{j+1}}\frac{1}{n^{2}x^{2}}\frac{dx}{2^{j+1}}\leq\frac{C}{n^{2}2^{2j}},

as desired.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

For
k\geq 2
,
\delta>0

\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{j=j_{\min}}^{j_{\max}}\Delta_{j}<\frac{\delta}{2}\Big(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{8}\Big)\lambda_{T_{k-1}}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{T_{k-1}}\right)\geq 1-e^{-c(\log n)^{2}}

\delta

Proof.

For
j_{\min}\leq j\leq j_{\max}

\displaystyle D_{j}

\displaystyle=\Delta_{j}-\left(\frac{\delta}{1+\varepsilon}\right)\frac{\lambda_{T_{k-1}}}{\log_{2}n}.

In words,

\delta

\varepsilon
.
This bound can be summed over all scales, to get that

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\Big(\exists j\in[j_{min},j_{max}]:&\Delta_{j}\geq\frac{\delta\lambda_{T_{k-1}}}{(\log_{2}n)(1+\varepsilon/2)}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{T_{k-1}}\Big)\\

for

).
It follows that, except on an event of probability at most

\displaystyle\sum_{j=j_{\min}}^{j_{\max}}\Delta_{j}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=j_{\min}}^{j_{\max}}\frac{\delta\lambda_{T_{k-1}}}{(\log_{2}n)(1+\varepsilon/2)}\leq\frac{\delta}{2}\Big(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{8}\Big)\lambda_{T_{k-1}}

∎

It remains to bound the displacement caused by particles arriving closer than

Lemma 2.5
.

For
k\geq 2
and any

\mathbb{P}\left(\Delta_{\min}<\left(\frac{6\delta}{1+\varepsilon}\right)\frac{\lambda_{T_{k-1}}}{\log n}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{T_{k-1}}\right)\geq 1-e^{-c(\log n)^{2}}

\delta

Proof.

This can be bounded crudely: indeed, note that if

\Delta_{\min}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}N_{\min},\text{ where }N_{\min}=\#\{T_{k-1}+1\leq i\leq T_{k-1}+m_{k}:x_{i}\in I_{i,\min}\}.

Note that

since

We conclude the proof of the lemma with the elementary fact on binomial random variables (which can also be deduced from Friedman’s inequality) that for some

\mathbb{P}(N_{\min}\geq(3/2)m_{k}p_{\min})\leq\exp(-cm_{k}p_{\min}),

which implies that

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(\Delta_{min}\geq\frac{3}{2}\frac{m_{k}p_{min}}{\sqrt{n}}\Big|\mathcal{F}_{T_{k-1}}\right)&\leq\mathbb{P}(N_{min}\geq(3/2)m_{k}p_{\min})\\
&\leq\exp(-cm_{k}p_{\min})\\
&\leq\exp(-c(\log n)^{2})\end{split}

as

∎

To conclude the proof of Proposition

\{T_{k}-T_{k-1}\geq m_{k}\mbox{ for all }2\leq k\leq K(T)\}
, we have

T_{K(t)}\geq\sum_{k=2}^{K(t)}(T_{k}-T_{k-1})\geq\sum_{k=2}^{K(t)}m_{k}\geq\frac{nt}{(1+\varepsilon)^{5}}.

It follows that

R_{\frac{nt}{(1+\varepsilon)^{5}}}\leq R_{T_{K(t)}}=R_{T_{1}}+\sum_{k=2}^{K(t)}(R_{T_{k}}-R_{T_{k-1}})

for all
t\in(t_{0}(n),T]
.
Restricting further to the event
\{T_{k}-T_{k-1}\leq n_{k}\mbox{ for all }2\leq k\leq\max\{K(T),\tilde{K}(T)\}\}
we have that
R_{T_{k}}-R_{T_{k-1}}\leq R_{T_{k-1}+n_{k}}-R_{T_{k-1}}
. It can be shown, using exactly the same argument as the one of Proposition
2.1
, that

\mathbb{P}\left(R_{T_{k-1}+n_{k}}-R_{T_{k-1}}\leq\frac{\delta}{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\lambda_{T_{k-1}}\mbox{ for all }2\leq k\leq K(T)\right)\geq 1-e^{-c(\log n)^{2}}

for
n
large enough (
n\geq n_{0}(\varepsilon,T)
). Thus, with probability exceeding
1-3e^{-c(\log n)^{2}}
, we have that

R_{\frac{nt}{(1+\varepsilon)^{5}}}\leq\lambda_{T_{1}}+\frac{\delta}{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\sum_{k=2}^{K(t)}\lambda_{T_{k-1}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\leq\frac{(\log n)^{3}+2}{\sqrt{n}}+\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{2}}{2}\sqrt{t\log n}}}\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{3}}{2}\sqrt{t\log n}

for all
t\in(t_{0}(n),T]
,
where in the second inequality we have used the definition of
K(t)
. Rearranging, this gives

\mathbb{P}\left(R_{nt}\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{6}}{2}\sqrt{t\log n}\quad\forall t\in(t_{0}(n),T]\right)\geq 1-2e^{-c(\log n)^{2}}.

Similarly one shows that

\mathbb{P}\left(L_{nt}\leq\frac{(1+\varepsilon)^{6}}{2}\sqrt{t\log n}\quad\forall t\in(t_{0}(n),T]\right)\geq 1-2e^{-c(\log n)^{2}}.

Combining these with the lower bound in (
2.9
) we also deduce that, with probability exceeding
1-5e^{-c(\log n)^{2}}
,

R_{nt}=\lambda_{nt}-L_{nt}\geq\sqrt{t\log n}\left(\frac{1}{(1+\varepsilon)^{3}}-\frac{1}{2}(1+\varepsilon)^{6}\right)\geq\frac{1}{2(1+\varepsilon)^{6}}\sqrt{t\log n}

for all
t\in[(\log n)^{9}/n,T]
,
where the last inequality holds as long as
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
for some absolute constant
\varepsilon_{0}>0
. The same holds for
L_{nt}
, which concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

3
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We first prove that the height of the cluster is deterministically bounded. This comes from the simple observation that the half-plane capacity of
\mathcal{K}_{nt}
is deterministically given by
t
and the following simple lemma: if
K
is a compact
\mathbb{H}
-hull such that
x+iy\in K
for some
x\in\mathbb{R},y>0
then

\hcap(K)\geq y^{2}/2.

This can be proved using a simple reflection argument about the line
\{\Re(z)=x\}
and monotonicity of the half-plane capacity; we leave the details to the reader (this is implicit in
[
11
]
, and explicitly proved in the note
[
10
]
, see also Proposition 6.1 of
[
2
]
for a closely related statement).

As a consequence, since
\hcap(\mathcal{K}_{nt})=t
then we deduce that

\max_{z\in\mathcal{K}_{nt}}\{\Im(z)\}\leq\sqrt{2t},

as desired.

Now let us turn to the diameter. The argument is based on estimating the harmonic measure of
\mathcal{K}_{nt}
viewed from infinity (which is conformally invariant) and relating this to the diameter, given that the cluster is necessarily very flat by the above. We start with the lower bound on the diameter which is slightly easier.

Fix
t>0
. Let
L=(1-\varepsilon)\sqrt{t\log n}
and denote by
K=\mathcal{K}_{nt}
for ease of notations. Let
z=iy
with
y>0
very large. Suppose for contradiction that
\diam(K)\leq L
, so
K
could fit in a rectangle
\mathcal{R}
of height
\sqrt{2t}
and width
L
, in the upper half plane and with the bottom side on the real line, and which we can assume to be centered without loss of generality. See Figure
6
. Then applying the conformal map
g_{K}
, the hull
K
is by definition mapped to the interval
[-L_{t},R_{t}]
. Let
\tilde{\mathcal{R}}=g_{K}(\mathcal{R}\setminus K)
. If we then apply the conformal map
g_{\tilde{\mathcal{R}}}
, then by monotonicity, we have

R_{t}\leq r,L_{t}\leq\ell

where
-\ell
and
r
respectively are
the extreme points of
g_{\mathcal{R}}(\partial\mathcal{R}\cap\mathbb{H})
. (See Figure
6
). In fact by symmetry note that
\ell=r
. Furthermore we claim that

\ell\leq L/2+C,

(3.1)

where
C
is a constant depending only on
t
. From here the contradiction (and thus the lower bound) follows, since we know that
\min(R_{t},L_{t})\geq(1-\varepsilon/2)(1/2)\sqrt{t\log n}
with probability converging to
1
as
n\to\infty
.

Figure 6:
Composition of conformal maps
g_{\mathcal{R}}=g_{\tilde{\mathcal{R}}}\circ g_{K}
.

This can be seen directly because the conformal map
g_{\mathcal{R}}
is known explicitly (it is a Schwarz–Christoffel map, since
D=\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathcal{R}
is a polygon), but it can be seen more robustly as follows.
Fix
z=iy
with
y>0
very large. Let
\tau_{D}
denote the first time at which a Brownian motion
B
leaves the domain
D
.
Let
\tau
denote the first time that the imaginary part of
B
reaches
\sqrt{2t}
. Let
E
be the event that
|\Re(B_{\tau})|\leq L/2
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{iy}(B_{\tau_{D}}\in\mathcal{R})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}^{iy}(E)+\mathbb{P}^{z}(B_{\tau_{D}}\in\mathcal{R};E^{c}).

Now observe that
\mathbb{P}^{z}(E)
may be computed through the integral over the interval
[-L/2,L/2]
of a Cauchy distribution with scale parameter
y^{\prime}=y-\sqrt{2t}
: hence after a change of variables,

\mathbb{P}^{iy}(E)=\int_{-L/(2y^{\prime})}^{L/(2y^{\prime})}\frac{1}{\pi(x^{2}+1)}dx\sim\frac{L}{\pi y}

as
y\to\infty
(and
t>0
fixed), where
a_{n}\sim b_{n}
means that the ratio
a_{n}/b_{n}\to 1
. Now let us turn to
E^{c}
, and observe that if
B_{\tau_{D}}\in\mathcal{R}
and
E^{c}
both hold, then necessarily Brownian motion hits one of the vertical sides of the rectangle
\mathcal{R}
before the real line. In words, if
I_{1},I_{2}
are the vertical sides, and
D^{i}=\mathbb{H}\setminus I_{i}
, then

(\{B_{\tau_{D}}\in\mathcal{R}\}\cap E^{c})\subset\left\{B_{\tau_{D^{1}}}\in I_{1}\right\}\cup\left\{B_{\tau_{D^{2}}}\in I_{2}\right\}

Hence by symmetry between
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
,

\mathbb{P}^{iy}(B_{\tau_{D}}\in\mathcal{R};E^{c})\leq 2\mathbb{P}^{iy}(B_{\tau_{D^{1}}}\in I_{1})\sim 2{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\frac{\text{cap}(I_{1})}{\pi y}}},

where
\text{cap}(I)
denotes the capacity of
I
from infinity in
\mathbb{H}
(see
[
2
]
, Section 4.1) which, we remark, differs from the half–plane capacity
\text{hcap}(I)
.

Altogether, since
\text{cap}(I_{1})=2\sqrt{2t}
(see
[
2
]
, Section 6.1)
, we see that

\lim_{y\to\infty}y\mathbb{P}^{iy}(B_{\tau_{D}}\in\mathcal{R})\leq L/\pi+4\sqrt{2t}/\pi.

(3.2)

To get the desired inequality on
\ell
we simply argue by conformal invariance:

\mathbb{P}^{iy}(B_{\tau_{D}}\in\mathcal{R})=\mathbb{P}^{g_{\mathcal{R}}(iy)}(B_{\tau_{\mathbb{H}}}\in[-\ell,\ell])\sim\frac{2\ell}{\pi y}

(3.3)

by a similar integration of the Cauchy distribution and the fact that
g_{\mathcal{R}}(iy)\sim iy
. Multiplying by
y
and letting
y\to\infty
, and comparing (
3.3
) with (
3.2
), we get

\frac{2\ell}{\pi}\leq\frac{L}{\pi}+4\sqrt{2t}/\pi,

which proves (
3.1
) with
C=\sqrt{32t}
. This proves the desired lower bound on
\diam(\mathcal{K}_{nt})
.

Now let us turn to the corresponding upper bound. We again argue by contradiction. Let
L=(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{t\log n}
. Suppose
\diam(K)\geq L
. Then there exists
a,b\in K
with
|a-b|\geq L
. Since
K
is connected there is a curve in
K
connecting
a
to
b
(and so its maximal height is at most
\sqrt{2t}
). We argue similarly as above. Let
I_{1},I_{2}
be the vertical segments connecting
a
and
b
respectively to the real line; call
x_{1}
and
x_{2}
the respective base points on
\mathbb{R}
of
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
. By translation we can assume
x_{1}=-x_{2}
and by the triangle inequality
|x_{1}-x_{2}|\geq L-O(1)
. Let
E=\{\tau_{H}\leq\tau_{\mathbb{H}}\}=\{B_{\tau_{H}}\in K\}
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}^{iy}(B_{\tau_{\mathbb{H}}}\in[x_{1},x_{2}])

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}^{iy}(B_{\tau_{\mathbb{H}}}\in[x_{1},x_{2}];E)+\mathbb{P}(B_{\tau_{\mathbb{H}}}\in[x_{1},x_{2}];E^{c}).

For the same topological reasons as above, if
B_{\tau_{\mathbb{H}}}\in[x_{1},x_{2}]
and
E^{c}
both hold, it must be the case that the Brownian motion
B
touched either
I_{1}
or
I_{2}
before the real line (indeed, to enter the interval
[x_{1},x_{2}]
the Brownian curve cannot avoid
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
without touching the curve connecting
a
and
b
, but then
E
holds). Therefore,

\mathbb{P}^{iy}(B_{\tau_{\mathbb{H}}}\in[x_{1},x_{2}])\leq\mathbb{P}^{iy}(E)+\mathbb{P}^{iy}(B_{\tau_{D_{1}}}\in I_{1})+\mathbb{P}^{iy}(B_{\tau_{D_{2}}}\in I_{2}).

Now we multiply by
y
and let
y\to\infty
. Using the same argument as above, we see that the left hand side converges to
|x_{1}-x_{2}|/\pi=L/\pi-O(1).
The right hand side has three terms. The first, by conformal invariance of Brownian motion, converges to
|L_{t}-R_{t}|/\pi
. The second and third respectively converge to
\text{cap}(I_{1})
and
\text{cap}(I_{2})
, both of which are
O(1)
.
Consequently, we obtain

L\leq|L_{t}-R_{t}|+O(1).

By Theorem
1.1
, the probability of the above tends to zero.

The curvature-induced gauge potential and the geometric momentum for a particle on a hypersphere
Z. Li, L. Q. Lai, Y. Zhong, Q. H. Liu

Abstract
A particle that is constrained to freely move on a hyperspherical surface in an N ( ≥ 2 ) N\left(\geq 2\right) dimensional flat space experiences a curvature-induced gauge potential, whose form was given long ago (J. Math. Phys. 34 (1993)2827). We demonstrate that the momentum for the particle on the hypersphere is the geometric one including the gauge potential and its components obey the commutation relations [ p i , p j ] = − i ℏ J i ​ j / r 2 \left[p_{i},p_{j}\right]=-i\hbar J_{ij}/r^{2} , in which ℏ \hbar is the Planck’s constant, and p i p_{i} ( i , j = 1 , 2 , 3 , … ​ N i,j=1,2,3,...N ) denotes the i − i- th component of the geometric momentum, and J i ​ j J_{ij} specifies the i ​ j − ij- th component of the generalized angular momentum containing both the orbital part and the coupling of the generators of continuous rotational symmetry group S ​ O ​ ( N − 1 ) SO(N-1) and curvature, and r r denotes the radius of the N − 1 N-1 dimensional hypersphere.

[p_{\lambda},J_{\alpha\beta}]=[\Pi_{\lambda}-A_{\lambda},L_{\alpha\beta}+S_{\alpha\beta}]=[\Pi_{\lambda},L_{\alpha\beta}]+[\Pi_{\lambda},S_{\alpha\beta}]-[A_{\lambda},L_{\alpha\beta}]-[A_{\lambda},S_{\alpha\beta}].

(37)

The first part of the commutators in the right-hand-side was already treated

liu13
and the results are given by,

[\Pi_{\lambda},L_{\alpha\beta}]=i\hbar(\Pi_{\alpha}\delta_{\beta\lambda}-\Pi_{\beta}\delta_{\alpha\lambda}).

(38)

The second part vanishes because of,

[\Pi_{\lambda},S_{\alpha\beta}]=0.

(39)

The third part
[A_{\lambda},L_{\alpha\beta}]
is with
L_{\alpha\beta}\equiv x_{\alpha}\Pi_{\beta}-x_{\beta}\Pi_{\alpha},

\displaystyle[A_{\lambda},L_{\alpha\beta}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\frac{S_{\lambda\tau}n_{\tau}}{r+x_{N}},x_{\alpha}\Pi_{\beta}-x_{\beta}\Pi_{\alpha}]

(40a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x_{\alpha}[\frac{S_{\lambda\tau}n_{\tau}}{r+x_{N}},\Pi_{\beta}]-x_{\beta}[\frac{S_{\lambda\tau}n_{\tau}}{r+x_{N}},\Pi_{\alpha}]

(40b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\hbar(\frac{S_{\lambda\beta}n_{\alpha}}{r+x_{N}}-\frac{S_{\lambda\alpha}n_{\beta}}{r+x_{N}}),

(40c)

where we used results (
32
). The fourth part
[A_{\lambda},S_{\alpha\beta}]
in (
37
) can be easily carried out with use of the algebraic
relations (
3
),

[A_{\lambda},S_{\alpha\beta}]=\frac{n_{\tau}}{r+x_{N}}[S_{\lambda\tau},S_{\alpha\beta}]=-i\hbar(\delta_{\alpha\lambda}A_{\beta}-\delta_{\beta\lambda}A_{\alpha}+\frac{n_{\alpha}S_{\lambda\beta}}{r+x_{N}}-\frac{n_{\beta}S_{\lambda\alpha}}{r+x_{N}}).

(41)

Combination of results for the third and fourth part (
40c
) and (
41
) yields a simple result,

\displaystyle-[A_{\lambda},L_{\alpha\beta}]-[A_{\lambda},S_{\alpha\beta}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\hbar(\frac{S_{\lambda\beta}n_{\alpha}}{r+x_{N}}-\frac{S_{\lambda\alpha}n_{\beta}}{r+x_{N}})+i\hbar(\delta_{\alpha\lambda}A_{\beta}-\delta_{\beta\lambda}A_{\alpha}+\frac{n_{\alpha}S_{\lambda\beta}}{r+x_{N}}-\frac{n_{\beta}S_{\lambda\alpha}}{r+x_{N}})

(42a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\hbar(\delta_{\alpha\lambda}A_{\beta}-\delta_{\beta\lambda}A_{\alpha}).

(42b)

Thus, the commutators
[p_{\lambda},J_{\alpha\beta}]
give,

\displaystyle[p_{\lambda},J_{\alpha\beta}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\hbar(\Pi_{\alpha}\delta_{\beta\lambda}-\Pi_{\beta}\delta_{\alpha\lambda})+i\hbar(\delta_{\alpha\lambda}A_{\beta}-\delta_{\beta\lambda}A_{\alpha})

(43a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\hbar(p_{\alpha}\delta_{\beta\lambda}-p_{\beta}\delta_{\alpha\lambda}).

(43b)

Similarly, we can prove following two relations,

[p_{\lambda},J_{\alpha N}]=-i\hbar p_{N}\delta_{\alpha\lambda},

(44)

and

\left[p_{N},J_{ij}\right]=i\hbar\left(p_{i}\delta_{Nj}-p_{j}\delta_{Ni}\right).

(45)

In final, from results (
43b
)-(
45
), we see that the proof of
the fundamental quantum conditions (
20
) is complete.
Q.E.D.

VI
Conclusions and discussions

For a particle that is constrained on an
\left(N-1\right)
-dimensional
hypersphere, the classical bracket is
\left[p_{i},p_{j}\right]_{D}=-L_{ij}/r^{2}
. In contrast to the usual understanding of the
corresponding quantum condition remains
\left[p_{i},p_{j}\right]=-i\hbar L_{ij}/r^{2}
in which
L_{ij}
represents orbital angular momentum, we
argue that it must be replaced by
\left[p_{i},p_{j}\right]=-i\hbar J_{ij}/r^{2}
in which
J_{ij}
includes two parts, and one of them is the
usual orbital angular momentum
L_{ij}
and another comes from the gauge
potential
f_{ij}
. In other words, we adapt the definition of momentum such
that
\left[p_{i},p_{j}\right]=-i\hbar J_{ij}/r^{2}
holds true. What is
more, we see that another set of the fundamental quantum conditions
\left[p_{k},J_{ij}\right]=i\hbar\left(p_{i}\delta_{kj}-p_{j}\delta_{ki}\right)
comes true as well. The momentum is the geometric one which
came into our sight recently.

The present study gives us an insight into the noncommutativity between
different components of momentum. Our general conjecture is: where there is
such a noncommutativity, there is a generalized angular momentum which is
possibly associated with both the gauge field angular momentum and the
orbital one. The most familiar system is a charged particle in
E^{3}
under
influence of uniform magnetic field
\mathbf{B}=B_{0}\mathbf{e}_{z}
, where
we have
[p_{x},p_{y}]=i\hbar qB_{0}
in which
p_{x}
and
p_{y}
are
x
-
and
y
-component of the kinetic momentum
\mathbf{p}=-i\hbar\nabla-q\mathbf{A}
, and
q
is the charge. The magnetic field is a gauge field,
but where is the angular momentum? A usually overlooked fact is that the
z
-component of the field angular momentum is
prl
,
L_{z}^{field}=qB_{0}\rho^{2}/2
with
\rho=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}}
, we
immediately find that
[p_{x},p_{y}]=2i\hbar L_{z}^{field}/\rho^{2}
. Thus,
the conjecture is true for the well-known system. With this understanding at
hand, we can boldly guess that the angular momentum part
f_{ij}
in (
4
) to be closely related to the angular momentum of the gauge field, which is
still under investigations.

1
Introduction

1.1
Special types of non-Kähler Hermitian
metrics

Let
(M,I)
be a connected, complex manifold of
complex dimension
n\geqslant 2
. For a Hermitian metric
g
,
we shall denote
\omega(\cdot,\cdot):=g(I\cdot,\cdot)
its
fundamental two-form. We shall denote by
d^{c}
the complex
differential
d^{c}:=IdI
, where the complex structure acts
on differential forms of degree
k
by

I(\eta)(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})=(-1)^{k}\eta(Ix_{1},\ldots,Ix_{k})
. Sometimes we write
\eta^{c}
for
I(\eta)
.

Usually, we work on compact manifolds.

Further on, we will say “non-Kähler”
referring to a complex manifold not admitting any Kähler
structure. A complex manifold admitting a Kähler
structure is called “Kähler-type manifold” if
we do not want to specify the particular Kähler metric.

In the realm of non-Kähler geometry, the most studied
class of Hermitian metrics is the locally conformally
Kähler one. We recall the definition.

Definition 1.1:

A Hermitian manifold

(M,I,g,\omega)
is called

locally conformally Kähler
(LCK) if
there exists a closed 1-form
\theta
such that
d\omega=\theta\wedge\omega
.
The 1-form
\theta
is called the
Lee form

and its cohomology class
the Lee class
.

Remark 1.2:
This definition is equivalent to
the existence of a Kähler cover
(\tilde{M},\tilde{\omega}){\>\longrightarrow\>}M
such that the deck group
\Gamma

acts on
(\tilde{M},\tilde{\omega})

by holomorphic homotheties. Indeed, suppose that

\theta
is exact,
df=\theta
. Then
e^{-f}\omega

is a Kähler form. Let
\tilde{M}

be a covering such that the pullback
\tilde{\theta}
of
\theta
is exact,

df=\tilde{\theta}
.
Then the pullback of
\tilde{\omega}
is conformal to the Kähler form

e^{-f}\tilde{\omega}
.

An up-to-date account of this kind of geometry appears in
[
OV6
]
.

However, there exist several other interesting types of non-Kähler Hermitian metrics. Below, we recall the definitions of two of these classes, probably the most frequently studied:

Definition 1.3:
A Hermitian metric is called
pluriclosed
or
SKT
(shortcut for
strongly Kähler with torsion
) if its fundamental two-form satisfies
dd^{c}\omega=0
(
[
Bi
]
).

Remark 1.4:
An Hermitian form
\omega
is called
Gauduchon
if
dd^{c}(\omega^{n-1})=0
, where
n=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M
.
In complex dimension 2, the SKT condition is
equivalent to the Gauduchon condition, and
hence SKT metrics exist in any conformal class of metrics
on a compact complex surface. In higher dimension, a
general existence result is not known, but many examples
are found on certain nilmanifolds (see, for example, the
papers of Anna Fino and collaborators). Moreover SKT
metrics are connected with certain theories in theoretical
phyisics and are essential in the literature about
generalized complex geometry and generalized Kähler
structures (see the works of Cavalcanti, Gualtieri,
Hitchin etc.).

Remark 1.5:
Recall that a two-form
\omega
is called
taming
or

symplectic-Hermitian
if it is
the (1,1)-part of a symplectic form.
Clearly, a symplectic-Hermitian form is pluriclosed.
The converse is false. Indeed, there are no examples of
symplectic-Hermitian form on non-Kähler compact
complex manifolds; it is conjectured they don’t exist (
[
ST
]
).

Definition 1.6:
A Hermitian metric is called
balanced
or
semi-Kähler
if its fundamental two-form satisfies
d\omega^{n-1}=0
,
n=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M
. Equivalently, the metric is balanced if its two-form is coclosed (
[
M
]
).

Remark 1.7:
In complex dimension 2, balanced and Kähler
metrics are equivalent. However, in any complex dimension
greater than 3 there exist compact complex manifolds
carrying balanced metrics, but no Kähler
metric. Balanced metrics exist on twistor spaces of
4-dimensional ASD Riemannian manifolds,

[
M
]
, while only the twistor spaces of
{\mathbb{C}}P^{2}
and
S^{4}
are Kähler (
[
H
]
). Moreover,
all Moishezon (and, more generally, Fujiki class C)
manifolds are balanced, because the existence of
balanced metrics is preserved by bimeromorphisms
(
[
AB
]
).

Denote by
\mathcal{B}
, resp.
\mathcal{S\!K\!T}
, resp.
\mathcal{L\!C\!K}
the class of balanced, resp. SKT, resp. LCK manifolds. A natural question arises:

Question 1.8:
Is it possible that
a compact complex non-Kähler manifold
(M,I)
admit metrics in
two of the above classes? We recall that “non-Kähler manifold”
means “manifold not admitting a Kähler metric”.

In general, the answer is negative, at least for a smaller
class of LCK metrics, namely the Vaisman ones (see
below), for example
[
AO
]
. More precisely, one proves that the simultaneous
existence of metrics in two different classes forces the
manifold to be of Kähler type. Most computations are carried out
on nilmanifolds, see
e.g.
[
FV1
,
FV2
]

(for metrics in
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{S\!K\!T}
),

[
OOS
]
(for metrics in
\mathcal{B}
and

\mathcal{L\!C\!K}
), or
[
AN
]
for the general existence of invariant SKT metrics on
k
-step nilmanifolds. We dare to conjecture:

Conjecture 1.9:
Let
M
be a compact complex manifold which admits
Hermitian forms
\omega_{1}
and
\omega_{2}
which belong to
two classes in the set
\{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L\!C\!K},\mathcal{S\!K\!T}\}
.
Then
M
admits a Kähler metric.

The aim of this paper is to give a partial negative answer to
1.1
. Namely, we shall prove that balanced metrics cannot exist on any of the known examples of compact LCK manifolds (
5
).

1.2
Positive currents in LCK geometry

For an introduction to positive currents
we refer to
[
De
]
.

The notion of “Kähler rank” was introduced first
in
[
HL
]
, and further developed
in
[
Br2
,
CT
]
.
This notion is a powerful tool used to classify
non-Kähler complex surfaces. A non-Kähler complex
surface
M
is said to be of Kähler rank 1
if it admits a smooth, positive form (which will
a posteriori be semi-positive of rank 1).
Otherwise, it is said that
M
has Kähler rank 0.

The Bott–Chern cohomology group
H^{1,1}_{BC}(M)

of a complex manifold is the space of closed
(1,1)-forms (or currents) up to
dd^{c}(C^{\infty}M)
.
It is well known (see e.g.
[
VVO
]
)
that the kernel of the tautological map
\tau:\;H^{1,1}_{BC}(M){\>\longrightarrow\>}H^{2}(M,{\mathbb{R}})

is 1-dimensional for any non-Kähler surface.
If
M
is a non-Kähler complex surface,
the kernel of
\tau
can be represented
by a positive closed (1,1)-current
(
[
L
, Théorème 6.1]
).
This current can be chosen as a smooth positive (1,1)-form
for surfaces of Kähler rank 1, and it
is always singular for surfaces of Kähler rank 0
(
[
Br2
,
CT
]
).

In
[
VVO
]
, it was shown that
all non-Kähler surfaces admit an LCK structure,
with the exception of one of the three Inoue surfaces and
possible non-Kato class VII surfaces
(which conjecturally don’t exist).
The proof is based on Lamari’s Theorem 6.1;
the positive current constructed by Lamari
is Bott–Chern homologous to
d^{c}\theta
, where
\theta

is the Lee form of the LCK structure on
the surface.

On Vaisman and OT-manifolds, equipped with the
most natural LCK metric, the form
d^{c}\theta

is actually semi-positive (
[
Va2
,
OV2
]
;
see also equation (
3.1
)).

This observation leads us to

4.2

which claims essentially that
d^{c}\theta

is Bott–Chern homologous to a positive current
on any LCK manifold.

We verify this conjecture for all known
examples of LCK manifolds
(
4.2
).

This conjecture easily implies that a strict LCK manifold
does not admit a balanced metric
(
5
).

2
A metric which belongs to two of
the classes
\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{L\!C\!K}
,
\mathcal{S\!K\!T}
is Kähler

By using only the very definitions of balanced, LCK and SKT metrics, one can prove that on a compact complex manifold, a Hermitian metric cannot belong to the intersections
\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{L\!C\!K}
,
\mathcal{B}\cap\mathcal{S\!K\!T}
, or
\mathcal{L\!C\!K}\cap\mathcal{S\!K\!T}
unless it is Kähler.

The following
2
is
partly known, but we provide a unified proof. For other
proofs showing that a Hermitian metric which is both
balanced and SKT on a compact complex manifold is
Kähler, see
[
IP
, Theorem 1.3]
,

[
DP
, Proposition 2.6]
. See also

[
CRS
]
for the relation
between SKT and balanced metrics.
Also, in
[
AI
, Remark 1]
it
was shown that a non-Kähler metric on a compact complex
manifold of dimension
>2
cannot
be both SKT and LCK.

Theorem 2.1:

Let
(M,I,\omega)
be a compact Hermitian
n
-manifold,
n\geqslant 3
.
Assume that
\omega
belongs to two of the classes
\{\mathcal{B},\mathcal{L\!C\!K},\mathcal{S\!K\!T}\}
.
Then
\omega
is Kähler.

Proof. Step 1: Assume
(M,I,\omega)
is SKT and LCK.

Let
\theta\in\Lambda^{1}(M)
be the Lee form of
\omega
as an LCK metric:
d\omega=\theta\wedge\omega
.
Let
\theta^{c}:=I(\theta)
.
Then
d^{c}\omega=I^{-1}dI(\omega)=I^{-1}(\theta\wedge\omega)=-\theta^{c}\wedge\omega
. This gives

0=d^{c}d\omega=d^{c}(\theta\wedge\omega)=d^{c}\theta\wedge\omega-\theta\wedge d^{c}\omega=d^{c}\theta\wedge\omega+\theta\wedge\theta^{c}\wedge\omega

(2.1)

Since
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M>2
, the multiplication map

\eta\mapsto\eta\wedge\omega
is injective, hence
(
2.1
) implies that
d^{c}\theta=-\theta\wedge\theta^{c}
.
Then

\begin{split}dd^{c}\omega^{n-1}&=(n-1)d^{c}\theta\wedge\omega^{n-1}-(n-1)^{2}\theta\wedge\theta^{c}\wedge\omega^{n-1}\\
&=-(n-1)(n-2)\theta\wedge\theta^{c}\wedge\omega^{n-1}.\end{split}

However,
\theta\wedge\theta^{c}\wedge\omega^{n-1}=2n|\theta|^{2}\omega^{n}
.
This brings a contradiction:

\begin{split}0&=\int_{M}dd^{c}\omega^{n-1}=\int_{M}-(n-1)(n-2)\theta\wedge\theta^{c}\wedge\omega^{n-1}\\
&=-\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{2}n\int_{M}|\theta|^{2}\wedge\omega^{n}\end{split}

The last integral vanishes if and only if
\theta=0
, hence

\omega
is closed.

Step 2:
Assume
(M,I,\omega)
is balanced and LCK.

Then
0=d\omega^{n-1}=(n-1)\theta\wedge\omega^{n-1}
.
However, the multiplication map

\eta\mapsto\eta\wedge\omega^{n-1}
is an isomorphism for all
\eta

and any Hermitian
\omega
, hence again

\theta=0
(see also
[
OV6
, Exercise 4.16]
).

Step 3:
Assume
(M,I,\omega)
is balanced and SKT.
Then

d(\omega^{n-1})=(n-1)d\omega\wedge\omega^{n-2}=0
. Moreover,

dd^{c}\omega=0
, hence
d\omega
and
d^{c}\omega

are
d
and
d^{c}
-closed. The equation

d\omega\wedge\omega^{n-2}=0
implies that
d\omega
is

primitive
, that is, it satisfies
\Lambda_{\omega}(d\omega)=0
,
where
\Lambda_{\omega}=L_{\omega}^{*}
, and
L_{\omega}(\eta):=\omega\wedge\eta
.
This form is of Hodge type
(1,2)+(2,1)
because
\omega
is of type (1,1),
and the de Rham differential shifts the Hodge grading at most by 1.

Recall that by the Hodge-Riemann relations,
any primitive
(1,2)+(2,1)
real form
\alpha

satisfies
\alpha\wedge I(\alpha)\wedge\omega^{n-3}=-C|\alpha|^{2}\omega^{n}
,
where
C
is a positive rational constant.

Let
\alpha:=d\omega
.
Since
\omega
is SKT, we have

\begin{split}0&=dd^{c}(\omega^{n-1})=(n-1)(n-2)d\omega\wedge d^{c}\omega\wedge\omega^{n-3}\\
&=-(n-1)(n-2)C|\alpha|^{2}\omega^{n}.\end{split}

(2.2)

This follows by the previous remark, since
\alpha
is a
primitive
(1,2)+(2,1)
-form (see also
[
OV6
, Exercise 4.24]
). It follows again that
d\omega=0
.

3
LCK manifolds

Clearly, the LCK condition (
1.1
)
is conformally invariant, and hence conformally equivalent
LCK metrics give rise to cohomologous Lee forms. We are
interested in compact LCK manifolds with non-exact Lee
form, called
strict LCK
. Usually, this condition
is tacitly assumed.
The following result states the dichotomy between these manifolds and the Kähler ones:

Theorem 3.1:
(Vaisman,
[
Va1
]
)
A compact LCK manifold
(M,I,\theta)
with non-exact Lee form does not
admit any Kähler structure.

Proof:
Suppose that
(M,I)
admits a Kähler structure.
By
dd^{c}
-lemma,
d^{c}\theta=dd^{c}f
for some function
f
.
This is impossible, as the following lemma implies.

Lemma 3.2:

Let
(M,\omega,\theta)
be a compact LCK manifold.
Suppose that
d^{c}\theta
is
dd^{c}
-exact. Then
\theta
is exact.

Proof:
Suppose that
d^{c}\theta=d^{c}df
.
Replacing
\omega
by
\omega_{1}:=e^{-f}\omega
gives an LCK structure
with LCK form
\theta_{1}=\theta-df
. Then
\theta_{1}
is closed
and
d^{c}
-closed, giving

dd^{c}(\omega_{1}^{n-1})=(n-1)^{2}\theta_{1}\wedge\theta_{1}^{c}\wedge\omega_{1}^{n-1}

The latter form is a volume form, strictly positive at all points
where
\theta_{1}\neq 0
, hence it cannot be exact unless

\theta_{1}=0
.

Next, we present the main constructions used
to produce LCK manifolds.

3.1
Blow-up of an LCK manifold

We owe to Tricerri and Vuletescu the following fundamental result:

Theorem 3.3:
(
[
T
]
,
[
Vu
]
) The blow-up at points preserves the LCK class.

Remark 3.4:
However, the blow-up along subvarieties does not preserve the LCK class unless the Lee form is exact along the submanifold,
[
OVV
]
.

3.2
LCK manifolds with potential

Definition 3.5:
An LCK manifold
M
is called
LCK manifold with LCK potential

if the Kähler form on the universal cover
\tilde{\omega}
of

\tilde{M}
has a positive Kähler potential

\varphi:\tilde{M}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{>0}
such that the action of

\pi_{1}(M)
multiplies this function by a positive constant.

Remark 3.6:
A small deformation of an LCK manifold
might be non-LCK,
[
Be
]
. However, a small
deformation of LCK with potential is LCK with potential,
[
OV1
]
.

Example 3.7:
All Hopf manifolds, linear and non-linear, admit an LCK structure
with LCK potential,
[
OV1
,
OV4
]
.

Theorem 3.8:
(
[
OV6
, Theorem 13.22]
)
A compact complex manifold
M
,
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M>2

admits an LCK potential if and only if

M
admits a holomorphic embedding
to a Hopf manifold.

Remark 3.9:
This property
can be used instead of the definition.

Remark 3.10:
In complex dimension 2,
3.2
is still true if we assume the GSS conjecture,
[
OV6
, Theorem 25.43]
.

3.3
Vaisman manifolds

In this subsection we recite a few classical results about
Vaisman manifolds. For the reference see

[
OV6
]
.

The best understood (and known) subclass of LCK manifolds with potential is formed by those having the Lee form parallel with respect to the Levi-Civita connection of the LCK metric. Indeed, if
\pi:\ (\tilde{M},\tilde{\omega})\to(M,\theta,\omega)
is the universal cover of such an LCK manifold, then one easily verifies that the
\tilde{\omega}
-squared norm of
\pi^{*}\theta
is an LCK potential for
\tilde{\omega}
.

Example 3.11:
Almost all non-Kähler compact complex surfaces are LCK,
see e.g.
[
VVO
]
. Diagonal Hopf surfaces and
Kodaira surfaces are Vaisman, but Kato surfaces and Inoue
surfaces, as well as OT manifolds (see below) are not Vaisman. In any dimension, all diagonal
Hopf manifolds are Vaisman, see
[
OV3
]
, but non-diagonal Hopf manifolds are not Vaisman. Also, all compact complex submanifolds of a Vaisman manifold are Vaisman (
[
OV6
, Corollary 7.35]
).

On a Vaisman manifold, the Lee form can be assumed of norm
1. Then one can prove the following fundamental identity
(
[
Va2
, Formula (2.8)]
,
[
OV6
, Proposition 8.3]
):

\omega=d^{c}\theta+\theta\wedge\theta^{c}.

(3.1)

Remark 3.12:
Since a non-diagonal Hopf manifold is a small deformation of a diagonal Hopf manifold, which is Vaisman, it follows that small deformations do not preserve the Vaisman property. However, due to
3.2
, a small deformation of a Vaisman manifold is still LCK (with potential).

3.4
Oeljeklaus–Toma manifolds

The construction of Oeljeklaus–Toma manifolds,
sometimes abbreviated as OT manifolds, is related to number
theory. Their construction generalizes the
construction of the Inoue surfaces
S^{0}
in class VII. We
refer to the monograph
[
N
]
for the necessary
background in number theory.

Let
K
be a number field which has
2t

complex embeddings denoted
\tau_{i},\overline{\tau}_{i}
and

s
real ones denoted
\sigma_{i}
,
s>0
,
t>0
.

Let
{\cal O}_{K}^{*,+}:={\cal O}_{K}^{*}\cap\bigcap_{i}\sigma^{-1}_{i}({\mathbb{R}}^{>0})
.
Choose in
{\cal O}_{K}^{*,+}

a free abelian subgroup
{\cal O}_{K}^{*,U}
of rank

s
such that the quotient

{\mathbb{R}}^{s}/{\cal O}_{K}^{*,U}
is compact, where

{\cal O}_{K}^{*,U}
is mapped to
{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
as

\xi{\>\longrightarrow\>}\big(\log(\sigma_{1}(\xi)),...,\log(\sigma_{s}(\xi))\big).
The existence of such a subgroup is proven in
[
OT
]
.
Let
\Gamma:={\cal O}^{+}_{K}\rtimes{\cal O}_{K}^{*,U}
, and
H^{s}
be the upper half-plane
\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s})\ ;\ \operatorname{Im}(z_{1})>0,\ldots,\operatorname{Im}(z_{s})>0\}
.

Definition 3.13:
(
[
OT
]
)
An
Oeljeklaus–Toma (OT) manifold
is a quotient

{\mathbb{C}}^{t}\times H^{s}/\Gamma
, where

{\cal O}^{+}_{K}
acts on
{\mathbb{C}}^{t}\times H^{s}

as

\zeta(x_{1},...,x_{t},y_{1},...,y_{s})=\bigg(x_{1}+\tau_{1}(\zeta),...,x_{t}+\tau_{t}(\zeta),y_{1}+\sigma_{1}(\zeta),...,y_{s}+\sigma_{s}(\zeta)\bigg),

and
{\cal O}_{K}^{*,U}
as

\xi(x_{1},...,x_{t},y_{1},...,y_{s})=\bigg(x_{1},...,x_{t},\sigma_{1}(\xi)y_{1},...,\sigma_{s}(\xi)y_{s}\bigg)

Theorem 3.14:
(
[
OT
]
)
The OT-manifold
M:={\mathbb{C}}^{t}\times H^{s}/\Gamma

is a non-Kähler compact complex manifold, without any non-constant
meromorphic functions.

Theorem 3.15:
An Oeljeklaus–Toma manifold admits an LCK metric if and only if
t=1
(
[
OT
]
for the sufficiency of the condition,
[
DV
]
for the necessity).

3.5
Kato manifolds

Definition 3.16:
Let
B
be an open ball in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
,
n>1
, and

\tilde{B}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{{\>\longrightarrow\>}}}B
be a bimeromorphic,
holomorphic map, which is an isomorphism outside
of a compact subset. Remove a small ball in
\tilde{B}

and glue it to the boundary of
\tilde{B}
, extending
the complex structure smoothly (and holomorphically)
on the resulting manifold, denoted by
M
.
Then
M
is called
a Kato manifold.

Theorem 3.17:
(
[
Br1
]
)
Suppose that
M
is a Kato manifold obtained from

\tilde{B}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{{\>\longrightarrow\>}}}B
with
\tilde{B}
Kähler.
Then
M
is LCK.

Also, Kato manifolds can be obtained as
limits of modifications of Hopf manifolds:

Theorem 3.18:
(
[
K
]
,
[
Dl
]
)
Let
M
be a Kato manifold. Then there exists
a family
M_{t}
of complex manifolds over a punctured disk
such that
M=M_{0}
and all other
M_{t}
are bimeromorphic to
a Hopf manifold.

Definition 3.19:
Let
M
be a complex manifold, and
\Gamma\subset M
be an open
subset which is isomorphic as a complex manifold to a small
neighbourhood of a sphere
S^{2n-1}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
. The set
\Gamma

is called
a global spherical shell
if the complement

M\backslash\Gamma
is connected.

Theorem 3.20:
(
[
K
]
)
Let
M
be a compact complex manifold. Then

M
is a Kato manifold if and only if it contains a
global spherical shell.

More recently, the LCK structure of Kato manifolds was
investigated in
[
IOP
,
IOPR
]
.

4
The Lee cone versus the Gauduchon cone

4.1
The Gauduchon cone

Definition 4.1:
A Hermitian metric
\omega
on a complex
n
-manifold is
called a
Gauduchon metric
if its fundamental form satisfies
dd^{c}(\omega^{n-1})=0
.

One of the very few statements
which is valid (and useful) to all compact
complex manifolds is the following theorem of Gauduchon:

Theorem 4.2:
(
[
G
]
) In any conformal class of Hermitian metrics on a compact complex manifold there exists a Gauduchon metric, unique up to a constant multiplier.

Definition 4.3:
Let
M
be a complex manifold, and
\omega
a Hermitian form of a Gauduchon metric.

The Gauduchon form
of
M
is
\omega^{n-1}
.

Claim 4.4:

Fix a positive volume form
\operatorname{Vol}
on
M
.
A form
\eta\in\Lambda^{n-1,n-1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})

defines a Hermitian form on
\Lambda^{1}(M)

taking
x,y\in T^{*}M
to
\frac{\eta\wedge x\wedge y}{\operatorname{Vol}}
.
Then this Hermitian form on
\Lambda^{1}M
is positive definite if
and only if
\eta=\alpha^{n-1}
, where
\alpha
is a
Hermitian form on
TM
.

Remark 4.5:
From this claim it follows that the set of all Gauduchon forms
is a convex cone in
\Lambda^{n-1,n-1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
.
Indeed, a sum of closed, strictly positive
(n-1,n-1)
-forms is the Gauduchon
form of a metric, as
4.1
implies.

Recall that the Aeppli cohomology group
H^{p,q}_{AE}(M)

is the space of
dd^{c}
-closed
(p,q)
-forms modulo

\partial(\Lambda^{p-1,q}M)+\overline{\partial}(\Lambda^{p,q-1}M)
.
The Aeppli cohomology is finite-dimensional for
M
compact. Moreover,
the natural pairing

H^{p,q}_{BC}(M)\times H^{n-p,n-q}_{AE}(M){\>\longrightarrow\>}H^{2n}(M)={\mathbb{C}}
, taking
x,y
to
\int_{M}x\wedge y

is non-degenerate and identifies
H^{p,q}_{BC}(M)

with the dual
H^{n-p,n-q}_{AE}(M)^{*}
(
[
A
]
). Now we can give:

Definition 4.6: The Gauduchon cone
of a compact complex

n
-manifold is the set of all classes

\omega^{n-1}\in H^{n-1,n-1}_{AE}(M)
of all Gauduchon
forms.

Definition 4.7:
The
pseudo-effective cone

P\subset H^{1,1}_{BC}(M)

is the set of Bott–Chern classes of all
positive, closed (1,1)-currents.

Theorem 4.8:

On a compact complex manifold, the Gauduchon cone is dual to the pseudo-effective cone.

Proof:
[
L
, Lemme 3.3]
; see also
[
PU
]
.

4.2
The Lee cone of an LCK manifold

Definition 4.9:
Let
M
be a compact complex manifold
admitting an LCK structure.
The Lee cone
of
M

is the set of all classes
[\theta]\in H^{1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})

such that
\theta
is the Lee form for an LCK structure on
M
.

Remark 4.10:
The “Lee cone” might be a bad term, because generally speaking the Lee cone is not a cone.
This set can be even discrete, for example on Inoue surfaces
S^{0}
(
[
O
]
).
However, for Vaisman manifolds and, more generally,
LCK manifolds with potential, the Lee cone is half-space, and
ipso facto
a cone
(
[
OV5
]
).

On compact LCK manifolds with potential, one can characterize the Lee cone using the
degree
of a cohomology class in
H^{1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
with respect to a fixed Gauduchon form as follows.

Let
M
be a compact complex manifold, and
\omega
a
Gauduchon form. Consider the natural map

H^{1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})\ni[\alpha]{\>\longrightarrow\>}H^{1,1}_{BC}(M)

which takes the class of a closed real 1-form
\alpha
to the Bott–Chern
class of
d^{c}\alpha
. This makes sense because locally,
\alpha=df
, hence

d^{c}\alpha
is a (1,1)-form.

Definition 4.11:
The degree of
[\alpha]
is
\deg_{\omega}\alpha:=\int_{M}\omega^{n-1}\wedge d^{c}\alpha
.

Remark 4.12:
Recall that
dd^{c}=-d^{c}d
. Then
\deg\alpha=\deg(\alpha+dh)
and hence
\deg_{\omega}
is a well defined functional on the first cohomology group
H^{1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
. Moreover, observe that
d^{c}\alpha\in\Lambda^{2}(M)
is always exact.

Theorem 4.13:
[
OV5
, Theorem 8.4]

Let
M
be a compact LCK manifold with potential,
and
u
a Gauduchon form. Then its Lee cone
is the set of all
\alpha\in H^{1}(M,{\mathbb{R}})
such that

\deg_{u}\alpha>0
.

We conjecture that half of this characterization is true on all LCK manifolds:

Conjecture 4.14:

Let
\theta
be a Lee class on a compact LCK manifold,
and
u
a Gauduchon metric. Then
\deg_{u}\theta>0
.

Remark 4.15:
This conjecture is non-trivial, because the choice of the Gauduchon
metric
u
is arbitrary. When
\omega
is an LCK form such that
d\omega=\omega\wedge\theta
,
it is easy to check that
\deg_{\omega}\theta>0
. Indeed,

0=dd^{c}(\omega^{n-1})=(n-1)d(\omega^{n-1}\wedge\theta^{c})=(n-1)^{2}\omega^{n-1}\wedge\theta\wedge\theta^{c}+(n-1)\omega^{n-1}d\theta^{c}.

On the other hand,
d\theta^{c}=-d^{c}\theta
because
\theta=df
locally,
and
dd^{c}=-d^{c}d
. Then
(n-1)\deg_{\omega}\theta=(n-1)^{2}\int\omega^{n-1}\wedge\theta\wedge\theta^{c}
,
and
\int\omega^{n-1}\wedge\theta\wedge\theta^{c}
is the
L^{2}
-norm of
\theta
.

Remark 4.16:
Recall that an element of
H^{1,1}_{BC}(M)
is
pseudo-effective
if it is the class of a positive closed (1,1)-current. By
4.1
,
the pseudo-effective cone is dual to the Gauduchon cone, that is, a Bott–Chern class
is pseudo-effective if and only if it evaluates positively on all Gauduchon forms.
Then
4.2
is equivalent to the pseudo-effectivity of the
Bott–Chern class of
d^{c}\theta
.

We still don’t know how to prove
4.2
. However, we can prove a weaker version:

Theorem 4.17:

Let
(M,\omega,\theta)
be a compact, non-Kähler LCK-manifold of complex dimension greater than 3, which
is bimeromorphic to either LCK with potential, Oeljeklaus–Toma or Kato
manifold, and
u
a Gauduchon metric. Then

\deg_{u}\theta>0
.

Proof. Step 1:
The statement is true for free for OT and Vaisman manifolds,
because
d^{c}\theta
is a positive, exact (1,1)-form.
For Vaisman manifolds
d^{c}\theta
is positive as shown
in
[
Ve
]
. For OT manifolds
this form is positive as shown in
[
OV2
]
.
It is also true for all manifolds which are bimeromorphic
to Vaisman and OT manifolds, because these manifolds are always
obtained as blow-ups of Vaisman and OT manifolds,
[
OV7
]
, and
the pullback of a positive form is positive.
This implies that
\deg_{u}\theta>0

in all these situations.

Step 2:

Suppose we have a smooth family
(M_{t},\omega_{t},\theta_{t})
of LCK manifolds
such that the statement of the theorem is true for all

t\neq 0
. Fix a Gauduchon metric
u_{0}
on the central
fiber
M_{0}
. We can extend it
u_{0}
to a smooth family
of Gauduchon metrics using the Gauduchon theorem. Indeed,
an Hermitian form
u_{0}
in the central fiber
M_{0}
can be extended to
a family
u_{t}^{\prime}
of Hermitian forms on the fibers in the
neighbourhood of
M_{0}
. Applying Gauduchon theorem, we find a
Gauduchon form
u_{t}
conformal to
u^{\prime}_{t}
.
Since the solution of the Gauduchon equation
depends smoothly on the data, the form
u_{t}

smoothly depends on
t
.

On all fibers except the central, we have

\deg_{u_{t}}\theta_{t}\geqslant 0
, hence the same is true on
the central fiber.

Step 3:
Let
M_{0}
be
an LCK manifold with potential. By
[
OV6
, Theorem 16.21]
,

M_{t}
has a deformation
(M_{t},\omega_{t},\theta_{t})

such that
(M_{t},\omega_{t},\theta_{t})
is Vaisman
for all
t\neq 0
. The Kato manifold has a deformation

(M_{t},\omega_{t},\theta_{t})

with all the fibers except the central one biholomorphic to
a blown-up Hopf (
[
K
]
). However, a blown-up Hopf
manifold is bimeromorphic to an LCK manifold with potential,
hence it also satisfies the statement of the theorem.
Applying Step 2, we obtain that
\deg_{u}\theta\geqslant 0

for all Gauduchon metrics
u
.

Step 4:
It remains to prove that the inequality

\deg_{u}\theta\geqslant 0
is strict.
4.1
,
together with Step 3, implies that
d^{c}\theta
is pseudo-effective,
that is, can be represented by a positive current
\Theta
.
If the integral
\int_{M}\Theta\wedge u^{n-1}
vanishes
for some Hermitian form
u
, this implies that
\Theta=0
,
and hence the Bott–Chern class of
d^{c}\theta
vanishes,
Then
d^{c}\theta=dd^{c}f
, for some
f\in C^{\infty}M
.
This is impossible by
3
.

5
Non-existence of balanced metrics on LCK manifolds

Theorem 5.1:

Let
(M,\omega,\theta)
be a compact strict LCK manifold,

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}M=n
.
Assume that
4.2
holds for
M
,
that is, that the Bott–Chern class of
d^{c}\theta
is
pseudo-effective. Then
M
does not admit a balanced metric.

Proof:
Let
\omega_{1}
be a balanced Hermitian form on
M
.
Then
\omega^{n-1}_{1}
is closed. Therefore,
4.2

implies that
\int_{M}\omega^{n-1}_{1}\wedge d^{c}\theta>0
.
This is impossible, because
d^{c}\theta=-d\theta^{c}
is exact.

Together with
4.2
,
this result immediately implies:

Corollary 5.2:

Let
(M,\omega,\theta)
be a compact non-Kähler LCK-manifold which
is bimeromorphic to either an LCK manifold with potential,
an Oeljeklaus–Toma manifold or a Kato
manifold. Then
(M,\omega,\theta)
does not admit a balanced metric.

Proof:
4.2
is true for these three classes
of LCK manifolds by
4.2
.

Remark 5.3:
All known examples of LCK manifolds fall in one of
the three classes described in Sections 3.2, 3.3, 3.4 (LCK
with potential, Oeljeklaus–Toma, and Kato) or are
blow-ups of manifolds in these classes. Consequently,

5

is at the moment the best result in this area.

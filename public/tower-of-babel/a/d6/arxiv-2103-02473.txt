A series of integral formulas for a foliated sub-Riemannian manifold
Vladimir Rovenski

Abstract
In this article, we prove a series of integral formulae for a codimension-one foliated sub-Riemannian manifold, which is a new geometric object denoting a Riemannian manifold ( M , g ) (M,g) equipped with a distribution 𝒟 = T ​ ℱ ⊕ span ⁡ ( N ) {\mathcal{D}}=T{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\,{\rm span}(N) , where ℱ {\mathcal{F}} is a foliation of M M and N N a unit vector field g g -orthogonal to ℱ {\mathcal{F}} . Our integral formulas involve r r th mean curvatures of ℱ {\mathcal{F}} , Newton transformations of the shape operator of ℱ {\mathcal{F}} with respect to N N and the curvature tensor of induced connection on 𝒟 {\mathcal{D}} and generalize some known integral formulas (due to Brito-Langevin-Rosenberg, Andrzejewski-Walczak and the author) for codimension-one foliations. We apply our formulas to sub-Riemannian manifolds with restrictions on the curvature and extrinsic geometry of a foliation. Keywords : Distribution, sub-Riemannian manifold, codimension-one foliation, Newton transformation, shape operator Mathematics Subject Classifications (2010) 53C12, 53C17



Introduction

Integral formulas are useful for solving many problems in Riemannian geometry, both manifolds (for example, the Gauss-Bonnet formula for closed surfaces) and foliations, see surveys
[
1
,
10
]
:

\bullet
characterizing of foliations, whose leaves have a given geometric property;

\bullet
prescribing the higher mean curvatures of the leaves of a foliation;

\bullet
minimizing functionals like volume defined for tensor fields on a foliation.

The first known integral formula for a codimension-one foliation of a closed Riemannian manifold belongs to G. Reeb, see
[
8
]
,

\int_{M}H\,d\operatorname{vol}_{g}=0;

(1)

thus,
either the mean curvature of the leaves
H\equiv 0
or
H(x)\,H(x^{\prime})<0
for some points
x\neq x^{\prime}
on
M
.
Recall that any compact manifold with Euler number equal to zero admits codimension-one foliations.
There is a series of integral formulas for codimension-one foliations,
starting with (
1
), with consequences for foliated space forms, see surveys in
[
1
,
4
,
10
]
.
These integral formulas were obtained by applying the Divergence Theorem to suitable vector fields.
The second formula in the series of total
r
th mean curvatures
\sigma_{r}
’s
(symmetric functions of the principal curvatures of the leaves) of a codimension-one foliation with a unit normal vector field
N

[
7
]

\int_{M}(2\,\sigma_{2}-\operatorname{Ric}_{N,N})\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}=0.

(2)

By (

and there are no harmonic codimension-one foliations of a closed manifold of positive Ricci curvature.

In
[
2
]
, the Newton transformations

(of the
Weingarten operator

A

\int_{M}\big((r+2)\sigma_{r+2}-\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}(T_{r}(A)\,{\mathcal{R}}_{N})-\sum\nolimits_{1\leq j\leq r}(-1)^{j-1}\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}(T_{r-j}(A)\,{\mathcal{R}}_{A^{j-1}\nabla_{N}N}\big)\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}=0.

(3)

Here, the linear operator

for

is given by

{\mathcal{R}}_{\,X}:Y\to R(Y,X)N
.

The natural question

In studying this question, we consider a foliation

1
Preliminaries

Here, we use the induced linear connection to define the shape operator (with its Newton transformations) and the curvature tensor related to a codimension-one foliated sub-Riemannian manifold, then we prove three auxiliary lemmas.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be an
(n+1)
-dimensional distribution on a smooth
m
-dimensional manifold
M
, i.e.,
a subbundle of
TM
of rank
n+1
(where
0<n<m
).
In other words, to each point
x\in M
we assign an
(n+1)
-dimensional subspace
{\mathcal{D}}_{x}
of the tangent space
T_{x}M
smoothly depending on
x
.
An integrable distribution determines a foliation; in this case the Lie bracket of any two vector fields from
{\mathcal{D}}
also belongs to
{\mathcal{D}}
.
A pair
(M,{\mathcal{D}})
, where
M
is a manifold and
{\mathcal{D}}
is a non-integrable distribution on
M
, is called a
non-holonomic manifold
, see
[
3
]
.
The notion of non-holonomic manifold was introduced for a geometric interpretation of constrained systems in classical mechanics.
A
sub-Riemannian manifold
is a non-holonomic manifold
(M,{\mathcal{D}})
, equipped with a sub-Riemannian metric
g=\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle
, i.e.,
the scalar product
g:{\mathcal{D}}_{x}\times{\mathcal{D}}_{x}\to\mathbb{R}
for all
x\in M
, see
[
3
]
.
Usually, they assume that the sub-Riemannian metric on
{\mathcal{D}}
(the horizontal bundle)
is extended to a Riemannian metric on the whole space
M
, also denoted by
g
.

The orthoprojector
P:TM\to{\mathcal{D}}
onto the distribution
{\mathcal{D}}
is characterized by the properties

P=P^{*}\ (\textrm{self-adjoint}),\quad P^{2}=P,

e.g.,
[
5
]
,
and similarly for the (1,1)-tensor
P^{\bot}=\operatorname{id}_{\,TM}-{P}
– the orthoprojector onto the orthogonal distribution
{\mathcal{D}}^{\bot}
(the vertical subbundle).
A sub-Riemannian manifold
(M,{\mathcal{D}},g)
equipped with a foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
such that the tangent bundle
T{\mathcal{F}}
is a subbundle of
{\mathcal{D}}
will be called a
foliated sub-Riemannian manifold
.

In this article, we assume that
\dim{\mathcal{F}}=n
(that is
{\mathcal{F}}
is a codimension-one foliation relative to
{\mathcal{D}}
),
and there exists a unit vector field
N
orthogonal to
{\mathcal{F}}
and tangent to
{\mathcal{D}}
. This means that the Euler characteristic of
{\mathcal{D}}
is zero.
The following orthogonal decomposition is valid:

{\mathcal{D}}=T{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\,{\rm span}(N).

The Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
on
(M,g)
induces a linear connection
\nabla^{P}
on
{\mathcal{D}}=P(TM)
:

\nabla^{P}_{X}PY=P\nabla_{X}PY,\quad X,Y\in\Gamma(TM),

which is compatible with the metric:
X\langle U,V\rangle=\langle\nabla^{P}_{X}U,\,V\rangle+\langle U,\,\nabla^{P}_{X}V\rangle
.

Define the horizontal vector field

Z=\nabla^{P}_{N}\,N,

and note that
\nabla_{N}\,N
is the curvature vector of
N
-curves in
(M,g)
.

The
shape operator

A_{N}:T{\mathcal{F}}\to T{\mathcal{F}}
of the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
with respect to
N
is defined by

A_{N}(X)=-\nabla^{P}_{X}\,N,\quad X\in T{\mathcal{F}}.

(4)

The
elementary symmetric functions

\sigma_{j}(A_{N})
of
A_{N}

(
r
th mean curvatures of
{\mathcal{F}}
in
{\mathcal{D}}
)
are given by the equality

\sum\nolimits_{\,r=0}^{\,n}\sigma_{r}(A_{N})\,t^{r}=\det(\,\operatorname{id}_{\,T{\mathcal{F}}}+\,t\,A_{N}),\quad t\in\mathbb{R}.

Note that
\sigma_{r}(A_{N})=\sum\nolimits_{\,i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}}\lambda_{i_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{i_{r}}
,
where
\lambda_{1}\leq\ldots\leq\lambda_{n}
are the eigenvalues of
A_{N}
.
Let
\tau_{j}(A_{N})=\operatorname{tr}\,(A_{N})^{j}
for
j\in\mathbb{N}
be the
power sums
symmetric functions of
A_{N}
.
For example,
\sigma_{0}(A_{N})=1
,
\sigma_{1}(A_{N})=\tau_{1}(A_{N})=\operatorname{tr}A_{N}
,
\sigma_{n}(A_{N})=\det A_{N}
, and

2\,\sigma_{2}(A_{N})=\tau_{1}^{2}(A_{N})-\tau_{2}(A_{N}).

(5)

For short, we set

A=A_{N},\quad\sigma_{r}=\sigma_{r}(A_{N}),\quad\tau_{r}=\tau_{r}(A_{N}).

Next, we introduce the curvature tensor
R^{P}:TM\times TM\rightarrow{\rm End}({\mathcal{D}})
of the connection
\nabla^{P}
:

{R}^{P}(X,Y)=\nabla^{P}_{X}\nabla^{P}_{Y}-\nabla^{P}_{Y}\nabla^{P}_{X}-\nabla^{P}_{[X,\,Y]};

(6)

Set
{R}^{P}(X,Y,V,U)=\langle{R}^{P}(X,Y)V,\,U\rangle
for
U,V\in{\mathcal{D}}
.
Obviously,
{R}^{P}(Y,X)V=-{R}^{P}(X,Y)V
(for any linear connection). Since
\nabla^{P}
is compatible with the metric, then

{R}^{P}(X,Y,V,U)=-{R}^{P}(X,Y,U,V).

(7)

Recall the Codazzi’s equation for a codimension-one foliation (or a submanifold) of
(M,g)
:

(\nabla_{X}\,h)(Y,U)-(\nabla_{Y}\,h)(X,U)=(R(X,Y)Z)^{\bot},

(8)

where

R:TM\times TM\rightarrow{\rm End}(TM)
is the Riemann curvature tensor of the Levi-Civita connection,

{R}(X,Y)=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}-\nabla_{[X,\,Y]},

⊥
denotes the projection onto the vector bundle orthogonal to
{\mathcal{F}}
,
and
h:T{\mathcal{F}}\times T{\mathcal{F}}\to(T{\mathcal{F}})^{\bot}
is the second fundamental form of
{\mathcal{F}}
in
(M,g)
defined by

h(X,Y)=(\nabla_{X}Y)^{\bot}.

Lemma 1
.

For
{\mathcal{D}}=T{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\,{\rm span}(N)
on
(M,g)
, the following Codazzi type equation is valid:

(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}_{X}\,A)Y-(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}\,A)X=-R^{P}(X,Y)N,\quad X,Y\in T{\mathcal{F}}.

(9)

Proof.

From (
8
), for all vectors
X,Y,U\in T{\mathcal{F}}
we get

\langle(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}_{X}\,A)Y-(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}_{Y}\,A)X,\ U\rangle+\langle h(X,U),\nabla_{Y}N\rangle-\langle h(Y,U),\nabla_{X}N\rangle=-\langle R(X,Y)N,U\rangle.

(10)

Applying the orthoprojector
on the vector bundle orthogonal to
{\mathcal{F}}
,
we find

\langle R(X,Y)N,U\rangle=\langle R^{P}(X,Y)N,U\rangle+\langle\nabla_{X}((\nabla_{Y}\,N)^{\bot})-\nabla_{Y}((\nabla_{X}\,N)^{\bot})-\nabla_{[X,\ Y]^{\bot}}\,N,U\rangle.

(11)

Using the equalities
[X,\ Y]^{\bot}=0
(since
T{\mathcal{F}}
is integrable), (
11
) and

\displaystyle\langle h(X,U),\,\nabla_{Y}\,N\rangle=\langle\nabla_{Y}N,\,(\nabla_{X}\,U)^{\bot}\rangle=-\langle\nabla_{X}((\nabla_{Y}\,N)^{\bot}),\,U\rangle,

\displaystyle\langle h(Y,U),\,\nabla_{X}\,N\rangle=\langle\nabla_{X}N,\,(\nabla_{Y}\,U)^{\bot}\rangle=-\langle\nabla_{Y}((\nabla_{X}\,N)^{\bot}),\,U\rangle,

in (
10
) completes the proof.
∎

T_{r}(A)Z
as follows:

\operatorname{div}_{\mathcal{F}}T_{r}(A)Z=\sum\nolimits_{\,1\leq i\leq n}\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}(T_{r}(A)Z),e_{i}\rangle=\langle\operatorname{div}_{\mathcal{F}}T_{r}(A),\,Z\rangle+\sum\nolimits_{\,1\leq i\leq n}\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}{Z},\,T_{r}(A)e_{i}\rangle.

Using (
12
) of Lemma
2
, we compute
\sum\nolimits_{\,1\leq i\leq n}\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}{Z},\,T_{r}(A)e_{i}\rangle
as

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{\,1\leq i\leq n}\big(\langle A^{2}e_{i}+{R}^{P}(e_{i},N)N-(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}_{N}A)e_{i},\,T_{r}(A)e_{i}\rangle+\langle Z,e_{i}\rangle\,\langle Z,\,T_{r}(A)e_{i}\rangle\big)

\displaystyle\qquad=-\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}\big(T_{r}(A)(\nabla^{\mathcal{F}}_{N}A-A^{2}-{\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{N})\big)+\langle T_{r}(A)Z,\,Z\rangle.

By Lemma
3
, we can write

\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}\big(T_{r}(A)(\nabla_{N}A-A^{2}-{\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{N})\big)=N(\sigma_{r+1})-\sigma_{1}\sigma_{r+1}+(r+2)\sigma_{r+2}-\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}(T_{r}(A)\,{\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{N}).

Finally, we have

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{\,1\leq i\leq n}\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}{Z},\,T_{r}(A)e_{i}\rangle=-N(\sigma_{r+1})+\sigma_{1}\,\sigma_{r+1}-(r+2)\sigma_{r+2}

\displaystyle+\,\langle T_{r}(A)Z,\,Z\rangle+\sum\nolimits_{\,1\leq i\leq r}(-1)^{j-1}\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}(T_{r-j}(A)\,{\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{A^{j-1}Z}).\quad\qed

From the above, applying the Divergence Theorem to any compact leaf, we get

Theorem 1
.

For any compact leaf
L
of
{\mathcal{F}}
and
Z=P\,\nabla_{N}N
we have

\displaystyle\int_{L}\big((r+2)\,\sigma_{r+2}+N(\sigma_{r+1})-\sigma_{1}\sigma_{r+1}-\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}(T_{r}(A){\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{N})

\displaystyle\ -\,\langle T_{r}(A)Z,Z\rangle-\sum\nolimits_{\,1\leq i\leq r}(-1)^{j-1}\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}(T_{r-j}(A)\,{\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{A^{j-1}Z})\big)\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{L}=0.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\tau_{1}={\rm const}
and
\operatorname{Ric}^{P}_{N,N}>0
. Then
{\mathcal{F}}
has no compact leaves.

Proof.

For
r=0
and
Z=P\nabla_{N}N
, by Proposition
2
,

\operatorname{div}_{\mathcal{F}}{Z}=\tau_{2}-N(\tau_{1})+\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}{\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{N}+\langle{Z},{Z}\rangle.

By conditions,
N(\tau_{1})=0
and
\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}{\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{N}=\operatorname{Ric}^{P}_{N,N}>0
, hence
\operatorname{div}_{\mathcal{F}}{Z}>0
. Applying the Divergence Theorem to a compact leaf yields a contradiction.
∎

For any vector field
X
in
{\mathcal{D}}
, we have

\operatorname{div}X=\operatorname{div}_{\mathcal{F}}X-\langle X,\,Z\rangle-\langle X,\,H^{\bot}\rangle,

(20)

where
H^{\bot}
is the mean curvature vector field of the distribution
{\mathcal{D}}^{\bot}
in
(M,g)
.
Recall that a distribution on a Riemannian manifold is called
harmonic
if its mean curvature vector field vanishes.
There are topological restrictions for the existence of a Riemannian metric on closed manifold,
for which a given distribution becomes harmonic, see
[
11
]
.

The following statement generalizes
(
1
).

Theorem 2
.

For a closed sub-Riemannian manifold
(M,{\mathcal{D}},g)
with
{\mathcal{D}}=T{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\,{\rm span}(N)

and a harmonic orthogonal distribution
{\mathcal{D}}^{\bot}
, the following integral formula is valid:

\int_{M}\sigma_{1}\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}=0.

(21)

Proof.

Recall that
\sigma_{1}=\operatorname{tr}A
, see (
4
), and observe that

\operatorname{div}_{\mathcal{F}}N=\sum\nolimits_{\,1\leq i\leq n}\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}\,N,\,e_{i}\rangle=-\sigma_{1}.

Thus,

\operatorname{div}N=\operatorname{div}_{\mathcal{F}}N-\langle N,\,H^{\bot}\rangle=-\sigma_{1}-\langle N,\,H^{\bot}\rangle.

(22)

Applying the Divergence Theorem and using the assumption
H^{\bot}=0
, yields (
21
).
∎

The following integral formula generalizes (
3
).

Theorem 3
.

For a closed sub-Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
with
{\mathcal{D}}=T{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\,{\rm span}(N)

and a harmonic orthogonal distribution
{\mathcal{D}}^{\bot}
, the following integral formula is valid:

\displaystyle\int_{M}\Big((r+2)\,\sigma_{r+2}-\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}(T_{r}(A){\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{N})

\displaystyle-\sum\nolimits_{\,1\leq j\leq r}(-1)^{j-1}\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}(T_{r-j}(A){\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{A^{j-1}\nabla^{P}_{N}N})\Big)\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}=0.

(23)

Proof.

Using (
22
), we calculate the divergence of
\sigma_{r+1}\cdot N
as

\operatorname{div}(\sigma_{r+1}\cdot N)=\sigma_{r+1}\operatorname{div}N+N(\sigma_{r+1})=N(\sigma_{r+1})-\sigma_{1}\sigma_{r+1}.

Then, by (
20
) with
X=T_{r}(A)Z
,
(since
{\mathcal{D}}^{\bot}
is a harmonic distribution), we get

\operatorname{div}\big(T_{r}(A)Z+\sigma_{r+1}\cdot N\big)=\langle\operatorname{div}_{\mathcal{F}}T_{r}(A),\,Z\rangle-(r+2)\,\sigma_{r+2}+\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}(T_{r}(A){\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{N}).

Thus, by Proposition
2
, we get (
3
).
∎

Corollary 2
.

For
r=0
, (
3
) gives us the following generalization of (
2
):

\int_{M}(2\,\sigma_{2}-\operatorname{Ric}^{P}_{N,N})\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}=0.

(24)

For
n=1
, we get
\sigma_{2}=0
, thus, (
24
) gives the zero integral of “Gaussian
P
-curvature” of
{\mathcal{D}}
.

Example 1
.

(a) For
r=1,2
, (
3
) reduces to

\int_{M}\Big(3\,\sigma_{3}-\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}(T_{1}(A)\,{\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{N}+{\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{Z})\Big)\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}=0,

\int_{{\mathcal{N}}_{1}{\mathcal{F}}}\Big(4\,\sigma_{4}+\langle T_{2}(A)Z,{H}^{\bot}\rangle-\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}\big(T_{2}(A){\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{\,N}+T_{1}(A){\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{\,Z}-{\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{\,AZ}\big)\Big)\,{\rm d}\,\omega^{\perp}=0.

(25)

(b) If
N
is a
P
-
geodesic vector field
, i.e.,
Z=0
, then (
3
) shortens to the formula

\int_{{\mathcal{N}}_{1}{\mathcal{F}}}\big((r+2)\,\sigma_{r+2}-\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}(T_{r}(A){\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{N})\big)\,{\rm d}\,\omega^{\bot}=0.

3
Some consequences

Here, we apply our formulas to sub-Riemannian manifolds with restrictions on the shape operator
A
or the induced curvature tensor
R^{P}
.

Let
(M,{\mathcal{D}},{\mathcal{F}},g)
be a foliated sub-Riemannian manifold with
{\mathcal{D}}=T{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\,{\rm span}(N)
, then

\bullet

{\mathcal{F}}
will be called
P
-
harmonic
, if

\sigma_{1}=0
,

\bullet

{\mathcal{F}}
will be called
P
-
totally umbilical
, if

A=(\sigma_{1}/n)\operatorname{id}_{\,T{\mathcal{F}}}
.

Obviously,

P
-harmonic and
P
-totally umbilical distributions are
harmonic and totally umbilical, respectively, but the opposite is not true.
By (
24
), we obtain the following.

Corollary 3
.

Let
(M,{\mathcal{D}},g)
be a closed sub-Riemannian manifold with
a harmonic orthogonal distribution
{\mathcal{D}}^{\bot}
.

(i)
If
\,\operatorname{Ric}^{P}>0
, then there are no
P
-harmonic codimension-one foliations in
{\mathcal{D}}
.

(ii)
If
\,\operatorname{Ric}^{P}<0
, then there are no
P
-totally umbilical codimension-one foliations in
{\mathcal{D}}
.

Proof.

(i) If
{\mathcal{F}}
is a
P
-harmonic codimension-one foliation in
{\mathcal{D}}
, then
2\,\sigma_{2}=-\tau_{2}\leq 0
, see (
5
).
(ii) If
{\mathcal{F}}
is a
P
-totally umbilical codimension-one foliation in
{\mathcal{D}}
, then
2\,\sigma_{2}=\frac{n-1}{n}\,(\sigma_{1})^{2}\geq 0
.

In both cases, (i) and (ii), we get a contradiction to integral formula (
24
).
∎

The
total
r
-th mean curvature
of
{\mathcal{F}}
are defined by

\sigma_{r}({\mathcal{F}})=\int_{M}\sigma_{r}\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}.

The following corollary of Theorem
3
generalizes
[
4
, Theorem 1.1]

(see also
[
6
, Section 4.1]
).

Corollary 4
.

Let
(M,{\mathcal{D}},g)
be a closed sub-Riemannian manifold with
{\mathcal{D}}=T{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\,{\rm span}(N)

and a harmonic orthogonal distribution
{\mathcal{D}}^{\bot}
, satisfying condition (
19
).
Then
\sigma_{r}({\mathcal{F}})
depends on
r,n,c
and the volume of
(M,g)
only, i.e., the following integral formula is valid:

\sigma_{r}({\mathcal{F}})=\bigg\{\begin{array}[]{cc}c^{r/2}\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}n/2\\
r/2\end{smallmatrix}\Big){\rm Vol}(M,g),&n,r\ {\rm even},\\
0,&n\ {\rm or}\ r\ {\rm odd}.\end{array}

Proof.

Using Lemma
3
, we get

\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}(T_{r}(A){\mathcal{R}}^{P}_{N})=\sum\nolimits_{\,1\leq i\leq n}\langle R^{P}(e_{i},N)N,\,T_{r}(A)e_{i}\rangle=c\,\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}T_{r}(A)=c(n-r)\,\sigma_{r}.

From (
3
) we obtain the equality

(r+2)\,\sigma_{r+2}({\mathcal{F}})=c(n-r)\,\sigma_{r}({\mathcal{F}}).

(26)

Since
\sigma_{1}({\mathcal{F}})=0
, see (
21
), by induction we obtain
\sigma_{r}({\mathcal{F}})=0
for any odd
r
.
For even
r=2s
and
n=2l
, using (
26
) and induvtion implies

\sigma_{2s}({\mathcal{F}})=c^{s}\big(\begin{smallmatrix}l\\
s\end{smallmatrix}\big){\rm Vol}(M,g)
.
∎

We can get similar formulas when
(M,{\mathcal{D}},g)
has the property

\operatorname{Ric}^{P}_{X,N}=C\,\langle X,\,N\rangle,\quad X\in{\mathcal{D}},

(27)

for some
C\in\mathbb{R}
and
{\mathcal{F}}
(with
\dim{\mathcal{F}}>1
) is
P
-totally umbilical.
Note that for
{\mathcal{D}}=TM
, condition (
27
) is satisfied for Einstein manifolds, see
[
6
]
.

The following corollary of Theorem
3
generalizes result in
[
6
, Section 4.2]
.

Corollary 5
.

Let
(M,{\mathcal{D}},g)
be a closed sub-Riemannian manifold with

{\mathcal{D}}=T{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\,{\rm span}(N)
,
P
-totally umbilical foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
, a harmonic orthogonal distribution
{\mathcal{D}}^{\bot}
,
and satisfying (
27
).
Then
\sigma_{r}({\mathcal{F}})
depends on
r,n,C
and the volume of
(M,g)
only, i.e., the following integral formula holds:

\sigma_{r}({\mathcal{F}})=\bigg\{\begin{array}[]{cc}(C/n)^{n/2}\Big(\begin{smallmatrix}n/2\\
r/2\end{smallmatrix}\Big){\rm Vol}(M,g),&n,r\ {\rm even},\\
0,&r\ {\rm odd}.\end{array}

(28)

Proof.

In this case,
T_{r}(A)
has the form

T_{r}(A)=a_{r}\,(\sigma_{1}/n)^{r}\operatorname{id}_{\,T{\mathcal{F}}},\quad{\rm where}\quad a_{r}=\sum\nolimits_{\,0\leq i\leq r}(-1)^{r-i}\Big(\begin{matrix}n\\
i\end{matrix}\Big).

Since
\operatorname{tr}_{\,{\mathcal{F}}}T_{r}(A)=(n-r)\sigma_{r}=(n-r)\big(\begin{smallmatrix}n\\
r\end{smallmatrix}\big)(\sigma_{1}/n)^{r}
, then

a_{r}=\frac{n-r}{n}\Big(\,\begin{matrix}n\\
r\end{matrix}\,\Big),\quad T_{r}(A)=\frac{n-r}{n}\,\sigma_{r}\operatorname{id}_{\,T{\mathcal{F}}}.

By our assumption (
27
),

\operatorname{Ric}^{P}_{Z,N}=0,\quad\operatorname{Ric}^{P}_{N,N}=C.

Thus, for a closed manifold with a
P
-
totally umbilical
foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
and condition (
27
), formula (
3
) becomes

\sigma_{r+2}({\mathcal{F}})=\frac{C(n-r)}{n(r+2)}\,\sigma_{r}({\mathcal{F}}).

Using induction similarly to Corollary
4
, we obtain (
28
).
∎

Remark 2
.

Our integral formulas provide more conditions for the mean curvature
H=\sigma_{1}/n
of
{\mathcal{F}}
.
In the case of a
P
-totally umbilical codimension-one foliation in
{\mathcal{D}}
with
A=H\operatorname{id}_{\,T{\mathcal{F}}}
, such conditions can be easily derived from (
3
) using
\sigma_{r}=\big(\begin{smallmatrix}n\\
r\end{smallmatrix}\big)H^{r}
:

\displaystyle\int_{M}H^{r-1}\Big({H}^{3}(n-1)(n-r-1)\,n

\displaystyle-{H}(n-1)(r+1)\operatorname{Ric}^{P}_{\,N,N}-r(r+1)\operatorname{Ric}^{P}_{\,Z,N}\Big)\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}=0.

(29)

Here we used the following identity with binomial coefficients:

\sum\nolimits_{\,1\leq j\leq r}\,(-1)^{j-1}\,\frac{n-r+j}{n}\Big(\,\begin{matrix}n\\
r-j\end{matrix}\Big)=\Big(\,\begin{matrix}n-2\\
r-1\end{matrix}\Big).

These integrals contain polynomials depending on
H
, and one can get obstructions for existence of
P
-totally umbilical foliations.
For example, if
r=n-1
, then (
2
) reads as

\int_{M}H^{n-2}(H\operatorname{Ric}^{P}_{\,N,N}+\operatorname{Ric}^{P}_{\,Z,N})\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}=0
.

Example 2
.

Here is an amazing consequence of (
21
) and (
24
).
Let a compact sub-Riemannian manifold
(M,g;{\mathcal{D}})
with a harmonic orthogonal distribution
{\mathcal{D}}^{\bot}
and the condition

\operatorname{Ric}^{P}\geq 2\,c>0

for some real
c
, be equipped with a codimension-one foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
, i.e.,
{\mathcal{D}}=T{\mathcal{F}}\oplus\,{\rm span}(N)
.
Then
the image of the function
\sigma_{2}:M\to\mathbb{R}
contains interval
[0,c+\varepsilon]
for some
\varepsilon>0
.
Indeed, by Reeb type formula (
21
),
\sigma_{1}(x)=0
at some
x\in M
. Then
\sigma_{2}(x)=-\tau_{2}(x)\leq 0
, see (
5
).
By (
24
),
\sigma_{2}({\mathcal{F}})\geq c\,{\rm Vol}(M,g)
. Hence, there exists
y\in M
such that
\sigma_{2}(y)>c

(otherwise,
\sigma_{2}\leq c
on
M
, therefore,
\sigma_{2}\equiv c>0
on
M
– a contradiction to
\sigma_{2}(x)\leq 0
).

4
Conclusion

In the article we generalized integral formula (
3
) (and its consequences) for sub-Riemannian setting.
We delegate the following for further study.

1. Our integral formulas can be easily extended for foliations and distributions defined outside of a “singularity set”
\Sigma

(a finite union of pairwise disjoint closed submanifolds of codimension at least
k
of a closed manifold
M
)
under additional assumption of convergence of certain integrals.
Then, instead of the Divergence theorem, we apply the following, see
[
6
,
13
]
:
if
(k-1)(q-1)\geq 1
and
X
is a vector field on
M\setminus\Sigma
such that
\int_{M}\|X\|^{q}\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}<\infty
, then

\int_{M}\operatorname{div}X\,{\rm d}\operatorname{vol}_{g}=0
.

2. One can extend our integral formulas for holomorphic foliations of
complex sub-Riemannian manifolds, see
[
12
]
for the case of Riemannian manifolds, i.e.,
{\mathcal{D}}=TM
.

3. Our integral formulas can be extended to foliations of arbitrary codimension of a sub-Riemannian manifold,
see
[
9
]
for the case of Riemannian manifolds, i.e.,
{\mathcal{D}}=TM
.

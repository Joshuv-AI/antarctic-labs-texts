Functional Correlation Bounds and Optimal Iterated Moment Bounds for Slowly-mixing Nonuniformly Hyperbolic Maps
Nicholas Fleming-Vázquez

Abstract
Consider a nonuniformly hyperbolic map T : M → M T\colon M\rightarrow M modelled by a Young tower with tails of the form O ⁡ ( n − β ) O(n^{-\beta}) , β > 2 \beta>2 . We prove optimal moment bounds for Birkhoff sums ∑ i = 0 n − 1 v ∘ T i \sum_{i=0}^{n-1}v\circ T^{i} and iterated sums ∑ 0 ≤ i < j < n v ∘ T i ​ w ∘ T j \sum_{0\leq i<j<n}v\circ T^{i}\,w\circ T^{j} , where v , w : M → ℝ v,w\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{R} are (dynamically) Hölder observables. Previously iterated moment bounds were only known for β > 5 \beta>5 . Our method of proof is as follows; (i) prove that T T satisfies an abstract functional correlation bound, (ii) use a weak dependence argument to show that the functional correlation bound implies moment estimates. Such iterated moment bounds arise when using rough path theory to prove deterministic homogenisation results. Indeed, by a recent result of Chevyrev, Friz, Korepanov, Melbourne & Zhang we have convergence to an Itô diffusion for fast-slow systems of the form x k + 1 ( n ) = x k ( n ) + n − 1 a ( x k ( n ) , y k ) + n − 1 / 2 b ( x k ( n ) , y k ) , y k + 1 = T y k x^{(n)}_{k+1}=x_{k}^{(n)}+n^{-1}a(x_{k}^{(n)},y_{k})+n^{-1/2}b(x_{k}^{(n)},y_{k}),\quad y_{k+1}=Ty_{k} in the optimal range β > 2 . \beta>2.



1
Introduction

Let
T\colon M\rightarrow M
be an ergodic, measure-preserving transformation defined on a bounded metric space
(M,d)
with Borel probability measure
\mu
.
Consider a fast-slow system on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times M
of the form

x_{k+1}^{(n)}=x_{k}^{(n)}+n^{-1}a(x_{k}^{(n)},y_{k})+n^{-1/2}b(x_{k}^{(n)},y_{k}),\quad y_{k+1}=Ty_{k}

(1.1)

where the initial condition
x^{(n)}_{0}\equiv\xi
is fixed and
y_{0}
is picked randomly from
(M,\mu).
When the fast dynamics
T\colon M\rightarrow M
is chaotic enough, it is expected that the stochastic process
X_{n}
defined by
X_{n}(t)=x^{(n)}_{[nt]}
will weakly converge to the solution of a stochastic differential equation driven by Brownian motion. This is referred to as
deterministic homogenisation
and has been of great interest recently
[
Dol04
,
MS11
,
GM13
,
KM16
,
DSL16
,
DSL18
,
CFK+19a
,
KKM20
]
. See
[
CFK+19b
]
for a survey of the topic.

In
[
KM16
]
, Kelly and Melbourne considered the special case where
a(x,y)\equiv a(x)
and
b(x,y)=h(x)v(y)
. By using rough path theory, they showed that deterministic homogenisation reduces to proving two statistical properties for
T\colon M\rightarrow M
. In
[
CFK+19a
]
this result was extended to general
a,b
satisfying mild regularity assumptions.

One of the assumed statistical properties is an “iterated weak invariance principle”. In
[
KM16
,
MV16
]
it was shown that this property is satisfied by nonuniformly expanding/hyperbolic maps modelled by Young towers, provided that the tails of the return time decay at rate
O(n^{-\beta})
for some
\beta>2
(which is the optimal range for such results).

The second assumed statistical property is control of “iterated moments”, which gives tightness in the rough path topology used for proving convergence. This condition has proved much more problematic. Advances in rough path theory
[
CFK+19b
,
CFK+19a
]
significantly weakened the moment requirements from
[
KM16
]
and these weakened moment requirements were eventually proved for nonuniformly
expanding
maps in the optimal range (i.e.
\beta>2
) in
[
KKM20
]
.

However, for nonuniformly hyperbolic maps modelled by Young towers previously it was only possible to show iterated moment bounds for
\beta>5

[
DMN20
]
. In this article, we extend iterated moment bounds to the optimal range
\beta>2.

1.1
Illustrative examples

Many examples of invertible dynamical systems are modelled by Young towers
[
You98
,
You99
]
. For example, Axiom A (uniformly hyperbolic) diffeomorphisms, Henon attractors and the finite-horizon Sinai billiard are modelled by Young towers with exponential tails, so for such systems deterministic homogenisation results follow from
[
KM16
,
KM17
]
. We now give some examples of slowly-mixing nonuniformly hyperbolic dynamical systems for which it was not previously possible to show deterministic homogenisation, due to a lack of control of iterated moments. We start with an example which is easy to write down:

•

Intermittent Baker’s maps.
Let
\alpha\in(0,1).
Define
g\colon[0,1/2]\rightarrow[0,1]
by
g(x)=x(1+2^{\alpha}x^{\alpha})
.
The Liverani-Saussol-Vaienti map
\bar{T}\colon[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]
,

\bar{T}x=\begin{cases}$~$g(x),&x\leq 1/2,\\
2x-1,&x>1/2\end{cases}

is a prototypical example of a slowly-mixing nonuniformly expanding map
[
LSV99
]
. As in
[
MV16
, Exa. 4.1]
, consider an intermittent Baker’s map
T\colon M\rightarrow M
,
M=[0,1]^{2}
defined by

T(x_{1},x_{2})=\begin{cases}(\bar{T}x_{1},g^{-1}(x_{2})),&x_{1}\in[0,\frac{1}{2}],\ x_{2}\in[0,1],\\
(\bar{T}x_{1},(x_{2}+1)/2),&x_{1}\in(\frac{1}{2},1],\ x_{2}\in[0,1].\\
\end{cases}

There is a unique absolutely continuous invariant probability measure
\mu
. The map
T
is nonuniformly hyperbolic and has a neutral fixed point at
(0,0)
whose influence increases with
\alpha
. In particular,
T
is modelled by a two-sided Young tower with tails of the form
\sim n^{-\beta}
where
\beta=1/\alpha
.

For
\beta>2
the central limit theorem (CLT) holds for all Hölder observables. For
\beta\leq 2
the CLT fails for typical Hölder observables
[
Gou04a
]
, so it is natural to restrict to
\beta>2
when considering deterministic homogenisation. By
[
DMN20
]
it is possible to show iterated moment bounds for
\beta>5
. Our results yield iterated moment bounds and hence deterministic homogenisation in the full range
\beta>2.

Dispersing billiards provide many examples of slowly-mixing nonuniformly hyperbolic maps. Markarian
[
Mar04
]
, Chernov and Zhang
[
CZ05a
]
showed how to model many examples of dispersing billiards by Young towers with polynomial tails.

We give two classes of dispersing billiards for which it is now possible to show deterministic homogenisation:

•

Bunimovich flowers
[
Bun73
]
.
By
[
CZ05a
]
the billiard map is modelled by a Young tower with tails of the form
O(n^{-3}(\log n)^{3}).

•

Dispersing billiards with vanishing curvature.
In
[
CZ05b
]
Chernov and Zhang introduced a class of billiards modelled by Young towers with tails of the form
O((\log n)^{\beta}n^{-\beta})
to any prescribed value of
\beta\in(2,\infty)
.

Notation
We endow
\mathbb{R}^{k}
with the norm
|y|=\sum_{i=1}^{k}|y_{i}|
.

Let
\eta\in(0,1]
. We say that an observable
v\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
on a metric space
(M,d)
is
\eta
-Hölder
, and write
v\in\mathcal{C}^{\eta}(M)
, if
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\eta}=\left|v\right|_{\infty}+[v]_{\eta}<\infty,
where
\left|v\right|_{\infty}=\sup_{M}|v|
and
[v]_{\eta}=\sup_{x\neq y}|v(x)-v(y)|/d(x,y)^{\eta}.

If
\eta=1
we call
v
Lipschitz and write
\mathrm{Lip}(v)=[v]_{1}.
For
1\leq p\leq\infty
we use
|\cdot|_{p}
to denote the
L^{p}
norm.

The rest of this article is structured as follows. In Section
2
we state our main results. Our first main result, Theorem
2.3
, is that mixing nonuniformly hyperbolic maps modelled by Young towers with polynomial tails satisfy a functional correlation bound. Our second main result, Theorem
2.4
, is that this functional correlation bound implies control of iterated moments.

In Section
3
we recall background material on Young towers and prove Theorem
2.3
. In Section
4
we prove that our functional correlation bound implies an elementary weak dependence condition. Finally in Section
5
we use this condition to prove Theorem
2.4
.

2
Main results

Let
T\colon M\rightarrow M
be a nonuniformly hyperbolic map modelled by a Young tower. We state our results for the class of dynamically Hölder observables, noting that this includes Hölder observables.
We delay the definitions of Young tower and dynamically Hölder until Section
3.1
. Let
\mathcal{H}(M)
denote the class of dynamically Hölder observables on
M
and let
[\cdot]_{\mathcal{H}}
denote the dynamically Hölder seminorm.

Definition 2.1
.

Fix an integer
q\geq 1.
Given a function
G\colon M^{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
0\leq i<q
we denote

[G]_{\mathcal{H},i}=\sup_{x_{0},\dots,x_{q-1}\in M}[G(x_{0},\dots,x_{i-1},\cdot,x_{i+1},\dots,x_{q-1})]_{\mathcal{H}}.

We call
G
separately dynamically Hölder, and write
G\in\mathcal{SH}_{q}(M)
, if
\left|G\right|_{\infty}+\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}[G]_{\mathcal{H},i}<\infty.

Fix
\gamma>0
. We consider dynamical systems which satisfy the following property:

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose that there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all integers
0\leq p<q,\ 0\leq n_{0}\leq\cdots\leq n_{q-1}
,

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{M}G(T^{n_{0}}x,\dots,T^{n_{q-1}}x)d\mu(x)-\int_{M^{2}}G(T^{n_{0}}x_{0},\dots,T^{n_{p-1}}x_{0},T^{n_{p}}x_{1},\dots,T^{n_{q-1}}x_{1})d\mu(x_{0})d\mu(x_{1})\bigg|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C(n_{p}-n_{p-1})^{-\gamma}\biggl(\left|G\right|_{\infty}+\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}[G]_{\mathcal{H},i}\biggr)

(2.1)

for all
G\in\mathcal{SH}_{q}(M)
. Then we say that
T
satisfies the Functional Correlation Bound with rate
n^{-\gamma}
.

A similar condition was introduced by Leppänen in
[
Lep17
]
and further studied by Leppänen and Stenlund in
[
LS17
,
LS20
]
. In particular,
[
Lep17
]
showed that functional correlation decay implies a multi-dimensional CLT with bounds on the rate of decay.
We are now ready to state the main results which we prove in this paper.

The rate of decay of correlations of a dynamical system modelled by a Young tower is determined by the tails of the
return time to the base of the tower. Indeed, let
T
be a mixing transformation modelled by a two-sided Young tower with tails of the form
O(n^{-\beta})

for some
\beta>1
. In
[
MT14
]
by using ideas from
[
CG12
,
Gou
]
, it was shown that there exists
C>0
such that

\bigg|\int_{M}v\ w\circ T^{n}d\mu-\int_{M}vd\mu\int_{M}wd\mu\bigg|\leq Cn^{-(\beta-1)}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}

for all
n\geq 1,v,w\in\mathcal{H}(M)
.
Our first main result is that the Functional Correlation Bound holds with the same rate:

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\beta>1
. Let
T
be a mixing transformation modelled by a two-sided Young tower whose return time has tails of the form
O(n^{-\beta})
. Then
T
satisfies the Functional Correlation Bound with rate
n^{-(\beta-1)}
.

Given
v,w\in\mathcal{H}(M)
mean zero define

S_{v}(n)=\sum_{0\leq i<n}v\circ T^{i},\quad\mathbb{S}_{v,w}(n)=\sum_{0\leq i<j<n}v\circ T^{i}\ w\circ T^{j}.

Our second main result is that the Functional Correlation Bound implies moment estimates for
S_{v}(n)
and
\mathbb{S}_{v,w}(n)
. Let
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}=\left|\cdot\right|_{\infty}+[\cdot]_{\mathcal{H}}
denote the dynamically Hölder norm.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\gamma>1
. Suppose that
T
satisfies the Functional Correlation Bound with rate
n^{-\gamma}
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
n\geq 1
, for any mean zero
v,w\in\mathcal{H}(M)
,

(a)

\left|S_{v}(n)\right|_{2\gamma}\leq Cn^{1/2}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}
.

(b)

\left|\mathbb{S}_{v,w}(n)\right|_{\gamma}\leq Cn\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Remark 2.5
.

As mentioned above, by
[
CFK+19a
, Theorem 2.10]
to obtain deterministic homogenisation results it suffices to prove the iterated WIP and iterated moment bounds. Let
T
be a mixing transformation modelled by a two-sided Young tower with tails of the form
O(n^{-\beta})
for some
\beta>2
. By
[
MV16
]
, the Iterated WIP holds for all Hölder observables. Together Theorem
2.3
and Theorem
2.4
imply that for all
\eta\in(0,1]
there exists
C>0
such that

(a)

\left|S_{v}(n)\right|_{2(\beta-1)}\leq Cn^{1/2}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\eta}
.

(b)

\left|\mathbb{S}_{v,w}(n)\right|_{\beta-1}\leq Cn\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\eta}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\eta}
.

for all mean zero
v,w\in\mathcal{C}^{\eta}(M)
, giving the required control of iterated moments.

3
Young towers

3.1
Prerequisites

Young towers were first introduced by L.-S. Young in
[
You98
,
You99
]
, as a broad framework to prove decay of correlations for nonuniformly hyperbolic maps. Our presentation follows
[
BMT21
]
. In particular, this framework does not assume uniform contraction along stable manifolds and hence covers examples such as billiards.

Gibbs-Markov maps:
Let
(\bar{Y},\bar{\mu}_{Y})
be a probability space and let
\bar{F}\colon\bar{Y}\rightarrow\bar{Y}
be ergodic and measure-preserving. Let
\alpha
be an at most countable, measurable partition of
\bar{Y}
. We assume that there exist constants
K>0,\ \theta\in(0,1)
such that for all elements
a\in\alpha
:

•

(Full-branch condition) The map
\bar{F}|_{a}\colon a\rightarrow\bar{Y}
is a measurable bijection.

•

For all distinct
y,y^{\prime}\in\bar{Y}
the separation time

s(y,y^{\prime})=\inf\{n\geq 0:\bar{F}^{n}y,\,\bar{F}^{n}y^{\prime}\text{ lie in distinct elements of }\alpha\}<\infty.

•

Define
\zeta\colon a\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{+}
by
\zeta=d\bar{\mu}_{Y}/(d\,(F|_{a}^{-1})_{*}\bar{\mu}_{Y})
. We have
|\log\zeta(y)-\log\zeta(y^{\prime})|\leq K\theta^{s(y,y^{\prime})}
for all
y,y^{\prime}\in a
.

Then we call
\bar{F}\colon\bar{Y}\rightarrow\bar{Y}
a full-branch Gibbs-Markov map.

Two-sided Gibbs-Markov maps
Let
(Y,d)
be a bounded metric space with Borel probability measure
\mu_{Y}
and let
F\colon Y\rightarrow Y
be ergodic and measure-preserving. Let
\bar{F}\colon\bar{Y}\rightarrow\bar{Y}
be a full-branch Gibbs-Markov map with associated measure
\bar{\mu}_{Y}
.

We suppose that there exists a measure-preserving semi-conjugacy
\bar{\pi}\colon Y\rightarrow\bar{Y}
, so
\bar{\pi}\circ F=\bar{F}\circ\bar{\pi}
and
\bar{\pi}_{*}\mu_{Y}=\bar{\mu}_{Y}.
The separation time
s(\cdot,\cdot)
on
\bar{Y}
lifts to a separation time on
Y
given by
s(y,y^{\prime})=s(\bar{\pi}y,\bar{\pi}y^{\prime})
. Suppose that there exist constants
K>0,\theta\in(0,1)
such that

d(F^{n}y,F^{n}y^{\prime})\leq K(\theta^{n}+\theta^{s(y,y^{\prime})-n})\text{ for all }y,y^{\prime}\in Y,n\geq 0.

(3.1)

Then we call
F\colon Y\rightarrow Y
a
two-sided Gibbs-Markov map.

One-sided Young towers:
Let
\bar{\phi}\colon\bar{Y}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{+}
be integrable and constant on partition elements of
\alpha
. We define the one-sided Young tower
\bar{\Delta}=\bar{Y}^{\bar{\phi}}
and tower map
\bar{f}\colon\bar{\Delta}\rightarrow\bar{\Delta}
by

\bar{\Delta}=\{(\bar{y},\ell)\in\bar{Y}\times\mathbb{Z}:0\leq\ell<\bar{\phi}(y)\},\quad\bar{f}(\bar{y},\ell)=\begin{cases}(\bar{y},\ell+1),&\ell<\bar{\phi}(y)-1,\\
(\bar{F}\bar{y},0),&\ell=\bar{\phi}(y)-1.\end{cases}

(3.2)

We extend the separation time
s(\cdot,\cdot)
to
\bar{\Delta}
by defining

s((\bar{y},\ell),(\bar{y}^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}))=\begin{cases}s(\bar{y},\bar{y}^{\prime}),&\ell=\ell^{\prime},\\
0,&\ell\neq\ell^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Note that for
\theta\in(0,1)
we can define a metric by
d_{\theta}(\bar{p},\bar{q})=\theta^{s(\bar{p},\bar{q})}
.

Now,
\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}=(\bar{\mu}_{Y}\times\text{counting})/\int_{\bar{Y}}\bar{\phi}d\bar{\mu}_{Y}
is an ergodic
\bar{f}
-invariant probability measure on
\bar{\Delta}
.

Two-sided Young towers
Let
F\colon Y\rightarrow Y
be a two-sided Gibbs-Markov map and let
\phi\colon Y\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{+}
be an integrable function that is constant on
\bar{\pi}^{-1}a
for each
a\in\alpha
. In particular,
\phi
projects to a function
\bar{\phi}\colon\bar{Y}\rightarrow M
that is constant on partition elements of
\alpha
.

Define the one-sided Young tower
\bar{\Delta}=\bar{Y}^{\bar{\phi}}
as in (
3.2
). Using
\phi
in place of
\bar{\phi}
and
F\colon Y\rightarrow Y
in place of
\bar{F}\colon\bar{Y}\rightarrow\bar{Y}
, we define the
two-sided Young tower

\Delta=Y^{\phi}
and tower map
f\colon\Delta\rightarrow\Delta
in the same way. Likewise, we define an ergodic
f
-invariant probability measure on
\Delta
by
\mu_{\Delta}=(\mu_{Y}\times\text{counting})/\int_{Y}\phi\,d\mu_{Y}
.

We extend
\bar{\pi}\colon Y\rightarrow\bar{Y}
to a map
\bar{\pi}\colon\Delta\rightarrow\bar{\Delta}
by setting
\bar{\pi}(y,\ell)=(\bar{\pi}y,\ell)
for all
(y,\ell)\in\Delta
. Note that
\bar{\pi}
is a measure-preserving semi-conjugacy;
\bar{\pi}\circ f=\bar{f}\circ\bar{\pi}
and
\bar{\pi}_{*}\mu_{\Delta}=\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}
. The separation time
s
on
\bar{\Delta}
lifts to
\Delta
by defining
s(y,y)=s(\bar{\pi}y,\bar{\pi}y^{\prime}).

We are now finally ready to say what it means for a map to be modelled by a Young tower:

Let
T\colon M\rightarrow M
be a measure-preserving transformation on a probability space
(M,\mu)
. Suppose that there exists
Y\subset M
measurable with
\mu(Y)>0
such that:

•

F=T^{\phi}\colon Y\rightarrow Y
is a two-sided Gibbs-Markov map with respect to some probability measure
\mu_{Y}
.

•

\phi
is constant on partition elements of
\bar{\pi}^{-1}\alpha
, so we can define Young towers
\Delta=Y^{\phi}
and
\bar{\Delta}=\bar{Y}^{\bar{\phi}}
.

•

The map
\pi_{M}\colon\Delta\rightarrow M
,
\pi_{M}(y,\ell)=T^{\ell}y
is a measure-preserving semiconjugacy.

Then we say that
T\colon M\rightarrow M
is
modelled by a (two-sided) Young tower.

From now on we fix
\beta>1
and suppose that
T\colon M\rightarrow M
is a mixing transformation modelled by a Young tower
\Delta
with tails of the form
\mu_{Y}(\phi\geq n)=O(n^{-\beta}).

Remark 3.1
.

Here we have not assumed that the tower map
f\colon\Delta\rightarrow\Delta
is mixing. However, as in
[
Che99
, Theorem 2.1, Proposition 10.1]
and
[
BMT21
]
the a priori knowledge that
\mu
is mixing ensures that this is irrelevant.

Let
\psi_{n}(x)=\#\{j=1,\dots,n\colon f^{j}x\in\Delta_{0}\}
denote the number of returns to
\Delta_{0}=\{(y,\ell)\in\Delta:\ell=0\}
by time
n
. The following bound is standard, see for example
[
KKM19
, Lemma 5.5]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1)
. Then there exists a constant
D_{1}>0
such that

\int_{\Delta}\theta^{\psi_{n}}d\mu_{\Delta}\leq D_{1}n^{-(\beta-1)}\text{ for }n\geq 1.\qed

The transfer operator
L
corresponding to
\bar{f}\colon\bar{\Delta}\rightarrow\bar{\Delta}
and
\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}
is given pointwise by

(Lv)(x)=\sum_{\bar{f}z=x}g(z)v(z),\text{ where }g(y,\ell)=\begin{cases}\zeta(y),&\ell=\phi(y)-1,\\
1,&\ell<\phi(y)-1\end{cases}.

It follows that for
n\geq 1
, the operator
L^{n}
is of the form
(L^{n}v)(x)=\sum_{\bar{f}^{n}z=x}g_{n}(z)v(z)
, where
g_{n}=\prod_{i=0}^{n-1}g\circ\bar{f}^{i}.

We say that
z,z^{\prime}\in\bar{\Delta}
are in the same cylinder set of length
n
if
\bar{f}^{k}z
and
\bar{f}^{k}z^{\prime}
lie in the same partition element of
\bar{\Delta}
for
0\leq k\leq n-1
. We use the following distortion bound (see e.g.
[
KKM19
, Proposition 5.2]
):

Proposition 3.3
.

There exists a constant
K_{1}>0
such that for all
n\geq 1
, for all points
z,z^{\prime}\in\bar{\Delta}
which belong to the same cylinder set of length
n
,

|g_{n}(z)-g_{n}(z^{\prime})|\leq Cg_{n}(z)d_{\theta}(\bar{f}^{n}z,\bar{f}^{n}z^{\prime}).\qed

Let
\theta\in(0,1).
We say that
v\colon\bar{\Delta}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is
d_{\theta}
-Lipschitz if
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\theta}=\left|v\right|_{\infty}+\sup_{x\neq y}|v(x)-v(y)|/d_{\theta}(x,y)<\infty
. If
f\colon\Delta\rightarrow\Delta
is mixing then by
[
You99
]
,

\left|L^{n}v-\int v\,d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}\right|_{1}=O(n^{-(\beta-1)}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\theta}).

The same bound holds pointwise on
\bar{\Delta}_{0}
:

Lemma 3.4
.

Suppose that
f\colon\Delta\rightarrow\Delta
is mixing. Then there exists
D_{2}>0
such that for all
d_{\theta}
-Lipschitz
v\colon\bar{\Delta}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, for any
n\geq 1,

\left|\mathbbm{1}_{\bar{\Delta}_{0}}L^{n}v-\int_{\bar{\Delta}}v\,d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}\right|_{\infty}\leq D_{2}n^{-(\beta-1)}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\theta}.

This is a straightforward application of operator renewal theory developed by Sarig
[
Sar02
]
and Gouëzel
[
Gou04b
,
Gou04c
]
. However, we could not find a reference to this result in the literature so we provide a proof.

Proof.

Define partial transfer operators
T_{n}
and
B_{n}
as in
[
Gou05
, Section 4]
. Then

\mathbbm{1}_{\bar{\Delta}_{0}}L^{n}v=\sum_{k+b=n}T_{k}B_{b}v.

Define an operator
\Pi
by
\Pi v=\int_{\bar{\Delta}_{0}}v\,d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}
. Then as in the proof of
[
Gou05
, Theorem 4.6]
we can write
T_{k}=\Pi+E_{k}
where
\left\lVert E_{k}\right\rVert=O(k^{-(\beta-1)}).
Moreover, by
[
Gou05
, Theorem 4.6]
,
\left\lVert B_{b}\right\rVert=O(b^{-\beta})
and

\sum_{b=0}^{\infty}\int_{\bar{\Delta}_{0}}B_{b}v\,d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}=\int_{\bar{\Delta}}v\ d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{\bar{\Delta}_{0}}L^{n}v

\displaystyle=\sum_{k+b=n}\Pi B_{b}v+\sum_{k+b=n}E_{k}B_{b}v

\displaystyle=\sum_{b=0}^{n}\int_{\bar{\Delta}_{0}}B_{b}v\,d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}+\sum_{k+b=n}E_{k}B_{b}v

\displaystyle=\int_{\bar{\Delta}}v\,d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}-\sum_{b=n+1}^{\infty}\int_{\bar{\Delta}_{0}}B_{b}v\,d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}+\sum_{k+b=n}E_{k}B_{b}v.

The conclusion of the lemma follows by noting that the expressions
\sum^{\infty}_{b=n+1}b^{-\beta}
and

\sum_{k+b=n}(k+1)^{-(\beta-1)}(b+1)^{-\beta}

are both
O(n^{-(\beta-1)})
.
∎

Finally we recall the class of observables on
M
that are of interest to us:

Dynamically Hölder observables
Fix
\theta\in(0,1)
. For
v\colon M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, define

\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}=\left|v\right|_{\infty}+[v]_{\mathcal{H}},\quad[v]_{\mathcal{H}}=\sup_{y,y^{\prime}\in Y,y\neq y^{\prime}}\sup_{0\leq\ell<\phi(y)}\frac{|v(T^{\ell}y)-v(T^{\ell}y^{\prime})|}{d(y,y^{\prime})+\theta^{s(y,y^{\prime})}}.

We say that
v
is dynamically Hölder if
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}<\infty
and denote by
\mathcal{H}(M)
the space of all such observables.

It is standard (see e.g.
[
BMT21
, Proposition 7.3]
) that Hölder observables are also dynamically Hölder for the classes of dynamical systems that we are interested in:

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\eta\in(0,1]
and let
d_{0}
be a bounded metric on
M
. Let
\mathcal{C}^{\eta}(M)
be the space of observables that are
\eta
-Hölder with respect to
d_{0}
. Suppose that there exists
K>0
,
\gamma_{0}\in(0,1)
such that
d_{0}(T^{\ell}y,T^{\ell}y^{\prime})\leq K(d_{0}(y,y^{\prime})+\gamma_{0}^{s(y,y^{\prime})})
for all
y,y^{\prime}\in Y,0\leq\ell<\phi(y).

Then
\mathcal{C}^{\eta}(M)
is continuously embedded in
\mathcal{H}(M)
where we may choose any
\theta\in[\gamma_{0}^{\eta},1)
and
d=d_{0}^{\eta^{\prime}}
for any
\eta^{\prime}\in(0,\eta].

3.2
Reduction to the case of a mixing Young tower

In proofs involving Young towers it is often useful to assume that the Young tower is mixing, i.e.
gcd\{\phi(y):y\in Y\}=1.
Hence in subsequent subsections we focus on proving the Functional Correlation Bound under this assumption:

Lemma 3.6
.

Suppose that
T
is modelled by a mixing two-sided Young tower whose return time has tails of the form
O(n^{-\beta})
. Then
T
satisfies the Functional Correlation Bound with rate
n^{-(\beta-1)}
.

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

Let
d=\gcd\{\phi(y):y\in Y\}
. Set
T^{\prime}=T^{d}
and
\phi^{\prime}=\phi/d.
Construct a mixing two-sided Young tower
\Delta^{\prime}=Y^{\phi^{\prime}}
, with tower measure
\mu^{\prime}_{\Delta}.
Define
\pi^{\prime}_{M}\colon\Delta^{\prime}\rightarrow M
by
\pi^{\prime}_{M}(y,\ell)=(T^{\prime})^{\ell}y.
Then
T^{\prime}
is modelled by
\Delta^{\prime}
with ergodic,
T^{\prime}
-invariant measure
(\pi^{\prime}_{M})_{*}\mu^{\prime}_{\Delta}.

Now by assumption the measure
\mu
is mixing so by the same argument as in
[
BMT21
, Section 4.1]
we must have
\mu=(\pi^{\prime}_{M})_{*}\mu^{\prime}_{\Delta}
.

Let
G\in\mathcal{SH}_{q}(M)
and fix integers
0\leq n_{0}\leq\cdots\leq n_{q-1}
. Define
n^{\prime}_{i}=[n_{i}/d],r_{i}=n_{i}\!\mod d
. We need to bound

\displaystyle\nabla G

\displaystyle=\int_{M}G(T^{n_{0}}x,\dots,T^{n_{q-1}}x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{M^{2}}G(T^{n_{0}}x_{0},\dots,T^{n_{p-1}}x_{0},T^{n_{p}}x_{1},\dots,T^{n_{q-1}}x_{1})d\mu(x_{0})d\mu(x_{1}).

Define
G^{\prime}\colon M^{q}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by
G^{\prime}(x_{0},\dots,x_{q-1})=G(T^{r_{0}}x_{0},\dots,T^{r_{q-1}}x_{q-1}).
Then

\displaystyle\nabla G

\displaystyle=\int_{M}G^{\prime}((T^{\prime})^{n^{\prime}_{0}}x,\dots,(T^{\prime})^{n^{\prime}_{q-1}}x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{M^{2}}G^{\prime}({(T^{\prime})}^{n^{\prime}_{0}}x_{0},\dots,(T^{\prime})^{n^{\prime}_{p-1}}x_{0},(T^{\prime})^{n^{\prime}_{p}}x_{1},\dots,(T^{\prime})^{n^{\prime}_{q-1}}x_{1})d\mu(x_{0})d\mu(x_{1}).

Let
[\cdot]_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}
denote the dynamically Hölder seminorm as defined with
T^{\prime},\phi^{\prime}
in place of
T,\phi
. Then by Lemma
3.6
,

\displaystyle|\nabla G|

\displaystyle\leq C(n^{\prime}_{p}-n^{\prime}_{p-1})^{-\gamma}\biggl(\left|G^{\prime}\right|_{\infty}+\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}[G^{\prime}]_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime},i}\biggr)

\displaystyle\leq Cd^{\gamma}(n_{p}-n_{p-1}-d)^{-\gamma}\biggl(\left|G\right|_{\infty}+\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}[G^{\prime}]_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime},i}\biggr)

Now fix
0\leq i<q.
Let
x_{0},\dots,x_{q-1}\in M
and write

\displaystyle v^{\prime}(y)

\displaystyle=G^{\prime}(x_{0},\dots,x_{i-1},y,x_{i+1},\dots,x_{q-1})

\displaystyle=G(T^{r_{0}}x_{0},\dots,T^{r_{i-1}}x_{i-1},T^{r_{i}}y,T^{r_{i+1}}x_{i+1},\dots,T^{r_{q-1}}x_{q-1})=v(T^{r_{i}}y).

Let
y,y^{\prime}\in Y
and
0\leq\phi^{\prime}(y)<\ell.
Then

|v^{\prime}((T^{\prime})^{\ell}y)-v^{\prime}((T^{\prime})^{\ell}y^{\prime})|=|v(T^{d\ell+r_{i}}y)-v(T^{d\ell+r_{i}}y^{\prime})|\leq[G]_{\mathcal{H},i}(d(y,y^{\prime})+\theta^{s(y,y^{\prime})}),

so
[G^{\prime}]_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime},i}\leq[G]_{\mathcal{H},i}.

∎

3.3
Approximation by one-sided functions

Let
0\leq p<q
and
0\leq n_{0}\leq\cdots\leq n_{q-1}
be integers and consider a function
G\in\mathcal{SH}_{q}(M)
. We wish to bound

\displaystyle\nabla G

\displaystyle=\int_{M}G(T^{n_{0}}x,\dots,T^{n_{q-1}}x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{M^{2}}G(T^{n_{0}}x_{0},\dots,T^{n_{p-1}}x_{0},T^{n_{p}}x_{1},\dots,T^{n_{q-1}}x_{1})d\mu^{2}(x_{0},x_{1}).

Now since
\pi_{M}\colon\Delta\rightarrow M
is a measure-preserving semiconjugacy

\nabla G=\int_{\Delta}\widetilde{H}(x,f^{n_{p}}x)d\mu_{\Delta}(x)-\int_{\Delta^{2}}\widetilde{H}(x_{0},x_{1})d\mu^{2}_{\Delta}(x_{0},x_{1})=\nabla\widetilde{H}

(3.3)

where
\widetilde{H}\colon\Delta^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by

\widetilde{H}(x,y)=\widetilde{G}(f^{n_{0}}x,f^{n_{1}}x,\dots,f^{n_{p-1}}x,f^{k_{p}}y,f^{k_{p+2}}y,\dots,f^{k_{q-1}}y),

where
\vphantom{G}\smash[t]{\widetilde{G}}=G\circ\pi_{M}
and
k_{i}=n_{i}-n_{p}
.

Let
R\geq 1
. We approximate
\widetilde{H}(f^{R}\cdot,f^{R}\cdot)
by a function
\widetilde{H}_{R}
that projects down onto
\bar{\Delta}
. Our approach is based on ideas from Appendix B of
[
MT14
]
.

Recall that
\psi_{R}(x)=\#\{j=1,\dots,R:f^{j}x\in\Delta_{0}\}
denotes the number of returns to
\Delta_{0}=\{(y,\ell)\in\Delta:\ell=0\}
by time
R
. Let
\mathcal{Q}_{R}
denote the at most countable, measurable partition of
\Delta
with elements of the form
\{x^{\prime}\in\Delta:s(x,x^{\prime})>2\psi_{R}(x)\}
,
x\in\Delta
. Choose a reference point in each partition element of
\mathcal{Q}_{R}
. For
x\in\Delta
let
\hat{x}
denote the reference point of the element that
x
belongs to. Define
\widetilde{H}_{R}\colon\Delta^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\widetilde{H}_{R}(x,y)=\widetilde{G}(f^{R}\widehat{f^{n_{0}}x},\dots,f^{R}\widehat{f^{n_{p-1}}x},f^{R}\widehat{f^{k_{p}}y},\dots,f^{R}\widehat{f^{k_{q-1}}y}).

Proposition 3.7
.

The function
\widetilde{H}_{R}
lies in
L^{\infty}(\Delta^{2})
and projects down to a function
\bar{H}_{R}\in L^{\infty}(\bar{\Delta}^{2})
. Moreover, there exists a constant
K_{2}>0
depending only on
T\colon M\rightarrow M
such that,

(i)

\left|\bar{H}_{R}\right|_{\infty}=\left|\vphantom{H}\smash[t]{\widetilde{H}}_{R}\right|_{\infty}\leq\left|G\right|_{\infty}.

(ii)

For all
x,y\in\Delta
,

|\vphantom{H}\smash[t]{\widetilde{H}}(f^{R}x,f^{R}y)-\vphantom{H}\smash[t]{\widetilde{H}}_{R}(x,y)|\leq K_{2}\biggl(\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}[G]_{\mathcal{H},i}\,\theta^{\psi_{R}(f^{n_{i}}x)}+\sum_{i=p}^{q-1}[G]_{\mathcal{H},i}\,\theta^{\psi_{R}(f^{k_{i}}y)}\biggr).

(iii)

For all
\bar{y}\in\bar{\Delta},

\left\lVert L^{R+n_{p-1}}\bar{H}_{R}(\cdot,\bar{y})\right\rVert_{\theta}\leq K_{2}\biggl(\left|G\right|_{\infty}+\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}[G]_{\mathcal{H},i}\biggr).

Here we recall that
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\theta}
denotes the
d_{\theta}
-Lipschitz norm, which is given by
\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\theta}=\left|v\right|_{\infty}+\sup_{x\neq y}|v(x)-v(y)|/d_{\theta}(x,y)
for
v\colon\bar{\Delta}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

We follow the proof of Proposition 7.9 in
[
BMT21
]
.

By definition
\widetilde{H}_{R}
is piecewise constant on a measurable partition of
\Delta^{2}
. Moreover, this partition projects down to a measurable partition on
\bar{\Delta}
, since it is defined in terms of
s
and
\psi_{R}
which both project down to
\bar{\Delta}
. It follows that
\bar{H}_{R}
is well-defined and measurable. Part
(i)
is immediate.

Let
x,y\in\Delta
. Write
\vphantom{H}\smash[t]{\widetilde{H}}(f^{R}x,f^{R}y)-\vphantom{H}\smash[t]{\widetilde{H}}_{R}(x,y)=I_{1}+I_{2}
where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{G}(f^{R}f^{n_{0}}x,\dots,f^{R}f^{n_{p-1}}x,f^{R}f^{k_{p}}y,\dots,f^{R}f^{k_{q-1}}y)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\widetilde{G}(f^{R}\widehat{f^{n_{0}}x},\dots,f^{R}\widehat{f^{n_{p-1}}x},f^{R}f^{k_{p}}y,\dots,f^{R}f^{k_{q-1}}y),

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{G}(f^{R}\widehat{f^{n_{0}}x},\dots,f^{R}\widehat{f^{n_{p-1}}x},f^{R}f^{k_{p}}y,\dots,f^{R}f^{k_{q-1}}y)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\widetilde{G}(f^{R}\widehat{f^{n_{0}}x},\dots,f^{R}\widehat{f^{n_{p-1}}x},f^{R}\widehat{f^{k_{p}}y},\dots,f^{R}\widehat{f^{k_{q-1}}y}).

Let
a_{i}=f^{n_{i}}x
and
b_{i}=f^{R}f^{k_{i}}y
. By successively substituting
a_{i}
by
\hat{a}_{i}
,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\vphantom{G}\smash[t]{\widetilde{G}}(f^{R}a_{0},\dots,f^{R}a_{p-1},b_{p},\dots,b_{q-1})-\vphantom{G}\smash[t]{\widetilde{G}}(f^{R}\hat{a}_{0},\dots,f^{R}\hat{a}_{p-1},b_{p},\dots,b_{q-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\bigl(\widetilde{G}(f^{R}a_{0},\dots,f^{R}a_{i-1},f^{R}a_{i},f^{R}\hat{a}_{i+1},f^{R}\hat{a}_{p-1},b_{p},\dots,b_{q-1})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\widetilde{G}(f^{R}a_{0},\dots,f^{R}a_{i-1},f^{R}\hat{a}_{i},f^{R}\hat{a}_{i+1},f^{R}\hat{a}_{p-1},b_{p},\dots,b_{q-1})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}\bigl(\tilde{v}_{i}(f^{R}a_{i})-\tilde{v}_{i}(f^{R}\hat{a}_{i})\bigr)

(3.4)

where
\tilde{v}_{i}(x)=\tilde{G}(f^{R}a_{0},\dots,f^{R}a_{i-1},x,f^{R}\hat{a}_{i+1},\dots,f^{R}\hat{a}_{p-1},b_{p},\dots,b_{q-1}).

Fix
0\leq i<p
. Since
a_{i}
and
\hat{a}_{i}
are in the same partition element,
s(a_{i},\hat{a}_{i})>2\psi_{R}(a_{i})
. Write
a_{i}=(y,\ell),\hat{a}_{i}=(\hat{y},\ell).
Then
f^{R}a_{i}=(F^{\psi_{R}(a_{i})}y,\ell_{1})
and similarly
f^{R}\hat{a}_{i}=(F^{\psi_{R}(a_{i})}\hat{y},\ell_{1})
, where
\ell_{1}=\ell+R-\Phi_{\psi_{R}(a_{i})}(y)
. (Here,
\Phi_{k}=\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\phi\circ F^{k}
.) Now by the definition of
[G]_{\mathcal{H},i}
and (
3.1
),

\displaystyle|\tilde{v}_{i}(f^{R}a_{i})-\tilde{v}_{i}(f^{R}\hat{a}_{i})|

\displaystyle=|\tilde{v}_{i}(F^{\psi_{R}(a_{i})}y,\ell_{1})-\tilde{v}_{i}(F^{\psi_{R}(a_{i})}\hat{y},\ell_{1})|

\displaystyle\leq[G]_{\mathcal{H},i}(d(F^{\psi_{R}(a_{i})}y,F^{\psi_{R}(a_{i})}y^{\prime})+\theta^{s(F^{\psi_{R}(a_{i})}y,F^{\psi_{R}(a_{i})}y^{\prime})})

\displaystyle\leq(K+1)[G]_{\mathcal{H},i}(\theta^{\psi_{R}(a_{i})}+\theta^{s(a_{i},a_{i}^{\prime})-\psi_{R}(a_{i})})

\displaystyle\leq 2(K+1)[G]_{\mathcal{H},i}\theta^{\psi_{R}(a_{i})}.

Thus

|I_{1}|\leq 2(K+1)\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}[G]_{\mathcal{H},i}\theta^{\psi_{R}(f^{n_{i}}x)}.

By a similar argument,

|I_{2}|\leq 2(K+1)\sum_{i=p}^{q-1}[G]_{\mathcal{H},i}\theta^{\psi_{R}(f^{k_{i}}y)},

completing the proof of
(ii)
.

Let
\bar{x},\bar{x}^{\prime},\bar{y}\in\bar{\Delta}.
Recall that

L^{R+n_{p-1}}\bar{H}_{R}(\cdot,\bar{y})(\bar{x})=\sum_{\bar{f}^{R+n_{p-1}}\bar{z}=\bar{x}}g_{R+n_{p-1}}(\bar{z})\bar{H}_{R}(\bar{z},\bar{y}).

It follows that
\left|L^{R+n_{p-1}}\bar{H}_{R}(\cdot,\bar{y})\right|_{\infty}\leq\left|\bar{H}_{R}\right|_{\infty}\leq\left|G\right|_{\infty}.
If
d_{\theta}(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{\prime})=1
, then

|L^{R+n_{p-1}}\bar{H}_{R}(\cdot,\bar{y})(\bar{x})-L^{R+n_{p-1}}\bar{H}_{R}(\cdot,\bar{y})(\bar{x}^{\prime})|\leq 2\left|G\right|_{\infty}=2\left|G\right|_{\infty}d_{\theta}(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{\prime}).

Otherwise, we can write
L^{n_{p-1}+R}\bar{H}_{R}(\cdot,\bar{y})(\bar{x})-L^{n_{p-1}+R}\bar{H}_{R}(\cdot,\bar{y})(\bar{x}^{\prime})=J_{1}+J_{2}
where

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{f}^{n_{p-1}+R}\bar{z}=\bar{x}}\bigl(g_{n_{p-1}+R}(\bar{z})-g_{n_{p-1}+R}(\bar{z}^{\prime})\bigr)\bar{H}_{R}(\bar{z},\bar{y}),

\displaystyle J_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bar{f}^{n_{p-1}+R}\bar{z}^{\prime}=\bar{x}^{\prime}}g_{n_{p-1}+R}(\bar{z}^{\prime})\bigl(\bar{H}_{R}(\bar{z},\bar{y})-\bar{H}_{R}(\bar{z}^{\prime},\bar{y})\bigr).

Here, as usual we have paired preimages
\bar{z},\bar{z}^{\prime}
that lie in the same cylinder set of length
n_{p-1}+R
. By bounded distortion (Proposition
3.3
),
|J_{1}|\leq C\left|G\right|_{\infty}d_{\theta}(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{\prime}).
We claim that
|\bar{H}_{R}(\bar{z},\bar{y})-\bar{H}_{R}(\bar{z}^{\prime},\bar{y})|\leq K_{2}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}[G]_{\mathcal{H},i}d_{\theta}(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{\prime})
. It follows that
|J_{2}|\leq K_{2}\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}[G]_{\mathcal{H},i}d_{\theta}(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{\prime}).

It remains to prove the claim.
Choose points
z,z^{\prime},y\in\Delta
that project to
\bar{z},\bar{z}^{\prime},\bar{y}
. Let
a_{i}=f^{n_{i}}z,a_{i}^{\prime}=f^{n_{i}}z^{\prime},b_{i}=f^{R+n_{i}}y.
As in part
(ii)
,

\displaystyle\bar{H}_{R}(\bar{z},\bar{y})-\bar{H}_{R}(\bar{z}^{\prime},\bar{y})=\vphantom{H}\smash[t]{\widetilde{H}}_{R}(z,y)-\vphantom{H}\smash[t]{\widetilde{H}}_{R}(z^{\prime},y)=\sum_{i=0}^{p-1}(\tilde{w}_{i}(f^{R}\hat{a}_{i})-\tilde{w}_{i}(f^{R}\hat{a}^{\prime}_{i}))

where
\tilde{w}_{i}(x)=\tilde{G}(f^{R}\hat{a}_{0},\dots,\hat{a}_{i-1},x,f^{R}\hat{a}^{\prime}_{i+1},\dots,\hat{a}^{\prime}_{p-1},\hat{b}_{p},\dots,\hat{b}_{q-1})
.

Let
0\leq i<p
. We bound
E_{i}=\tilde{w}_{i}(f^{R}\hat{a}_{i})-\tilde{w}_{i}(f^{R}\hat{a}^{\prime}_{i})
. Without loss suppose that

\psi_{R}(\hat{a}^{\prime}_{i})\geq s(\hat{a}_{i},\hat{a}^{\prime}_{i})-\psi_{R}(\hat{a}_{i}),

for otherwise
\hat{a}_{i}
and
\hat{a}^{\prime}_{i}
are reference points of the same partition element so
\hat{a}_{i}=\hat{a}^{\prime}_{i}
and
E_{i}=0
. Now as in part
(ii)
,

E_{i}\leq(K+1)(\theta^{\psi_{R}(\hat{a}_{i})}+\theta^{s(\hat{a}_{i},\hat{a}^{\prime}_{i})-\psi_{R}(\hat{a}_{i})}).

Note that

\displaystyle s(\hat{a}_{i},\hat{a}^{\prime}_{i})-\psi_{R}(\hat{a}_{i})

\displaystyle\geq\min\{s(\hat{a}_{i},a_{i}),s(a_{i},a^{\prime}_{i}),s(a^{\prime}_{i},\hat{a}^{\prime}_{i})\}-\psi_{R}(\hat{a}_{i}).

Since
\bar{z},\bar{z}^{\prime}
lie in the same cylinder set of length
R+n_{p-1}
, we have
\psi_{R}(a_{i})=\psi_{R}(a^{\prime}_{i})
and

\displaystyle s(a_{i},a^{\prime}_{i})=s(\bar{f}^{n_{i}}\bar{z},\bar{f}^{n_{i}}\bar{z}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=s(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{\prime})+\psi_{R+n_{p-1}-n_{i}}(\bar{f}^{n_{i}}\bar{z})

\displaystyle\geq s(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{\prime})+\psi_{R}(a_{i}).

Now
a_{i}
and
\hat{a}_{i}
are contained in the same partition element so
s(\hat{a}_{i},a_{i})-\psi_{R}(\hat{a}_{i})\geq\psi_{R}(\hat{a}_{i})
and

\psi_{R}(\hat{a}_{i})=\psi_{R}(a_{i})=\psi_{R}(a_{i}^{\prime})=\psi_{R}(\hat{a}^{\prime}_{i}).

Hence
s(\hat{a}_{i},\hat{a}_{i}^{\prime})-\psi_{R}(\hat{a}_{i})\geq\min\{s(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{\prime}),\psi_{R}(a_{i})\}
. It follows that
E_{i}\leq 2(K+1)\theta^{s(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{\prime})}
, completing the proof of the claim.
∎

3.4
Proof of Lemma
3.6

We continue to assume that
\beta>1
and that
\mu_{Y}(\phi\geq n)=O(n^{-\beta}).
We also assume that
\gcd\{\phi(y):y\in Y\}=1
so that
f\colon\Delta\rightarrow\Delta
is mixing.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\theta\in(0,1).
There exists
D_{3}>0
such that for any
V\in L^{\infty}(\bar{\Delta}^{2})
,

\biggl|\int_{\bar{\Delta}}V(x,\bar{f}^{n}x)d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}(x)-\int_{\bar{\Delta}^{2}}V(x_{0},x_{1})d\bar{\mu}^{2}_{\Delta}(x_{0},x_{1})\biggr|\leq D_{3}n^{-(\beta-1)}\sup_{y\in\bar{\Delta}}\left\lVert V(\cdot,y)\right\rVert_{\theta}

for all
n\geq 1.

Remark 3.9
.

Let
V(x,y)=v(x)w(y)
where
v
is
d_{\theta}
-Lipschitz and
w\in L^{\infty}(\bar{\Delta})
. Then we obtain that

\left|\int_{\bar{\Delta}}v\,w\circ\bar{f}^{n}d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}-\int_{\bar{\Delta}}v\,d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}\int_{\bar{\Delta}}w\,d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}\right|\leq D_{3}n^{-(\beta-1)}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\theta}\left|w\right|_{\infty},

so Lemma
3.8
can be seen as a generalisation of the usual upper bound on decay of correlations for observables on the one-sided tower
\bar{\Delta}
.

Remark 3.10
.

Our proof of Lemma
3.8
is based on ideas from
[
CG12
, Section 4]
. However, we have chosen to present the proof in full because (i) our assumptions are weaker, in particular we only require
\beta>1
instead of
\beta>2
and
V
need not be separately
d_{\theta}
-Lipschitz and (ii) we avoid introducing Markov chains.

Proof of Lemma
3.8
.

Write
v(x)=V(x,\bar{f}^{n}x)
so

\displaystyle\int_{\bar{\Delta}}V(x,f^{n}x)\,d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\bar{\Delta}}v\,d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}=\int_{\bar{\Delta}}L^{n}v\,d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}

\displaystyle=\int_{\bar{\Delta}}\sum_{\bar{f}^{n}z=x}g_{n}(z)V(z,\bar{f}^{n}z)d\bar{\mu}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\bar{\Delta}}\sum_{\bar{f}^{n}z=x}g_{n}(z)V(z,x)d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}(x)=\int_{\bar{\Delta}}(L^{n}u_{x})(x)\,d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}(x).

where
u_{x}(z)=V(z,x)
.
Let
\bar{\Delta}_{\ell}=\{(y,j)\in\bar{\Delta}:j=\ell\}
denote the
\ell
-th level of
\bar{\Delta}
. It follows that we can decompose

\int_{\bar{\Delta}}V(x,\bar{f}^{n}x)d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}(x)-\int_{\bar{\Delta}^{2}}V(x_{0},x_{1})d\bar{\mu}^{2}_{\Delta}(x_{0},x_{1})=\sum_{\ell\geq 0}A_{\ell}

where

A_{\ell}=\int_{\bar{\Delta}_{\ell}}\bigg((L^{n}u_{x})(x)-\int_{\bar{\Delta}}V(z,x)d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}(z)\bigg)d\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}(x).

For all
\ell\geq 0
,

|A_{\ell}|\leq 2\left|V\right|_{\infty}\bar{\mu}_{\Delta}(\bar{\Delta}_{\ell})=2\left|V\right|_{\infty}\frac{\bar{\mu}_{Y}(\phi>\ell)}{\int\phi d\bar{\mu}_{Y}}=O(\left|V\right|_{\infty}(\ell+1)^{-\beta}).

Hence,

\sum_{\ell\geq n/2}|A_{\ell}|=O\big(\!\left|V\right|_{\infty}n^{-(\beta-1)}\big).

Let
x\in\bar{\Delta}_{\ell}
,
\ell\leq n

4
An abstract weak dependence condition

The Functional Correlation Bound can be seen as a weak dependence condition. Let
k\geq 1
and consider
k
disjoint blocks of integers
\{\ell_{i},\ell_{i}+1,\dots,u_{i}\}
,
0\leq i<k
with
\ell_{i}\leq u_{i}<\ell_{i+1}.
Consider random variables
X_{i}
on
(M,\mu)
of the form

X_{i}(x)=\varPhi_{i}(T^{\ell_{i}}x,\dots,T^{u_{i}}x)

where
\varPhi_{i}\in\mathcal{SH}_{u_{i}-\ell_{i}+1}(M)
,
0\leq i<k.

When the gaps
\ell_{i+1}-u_{i}
between blocks are large, the random variables
X_{0},\dots,X_{k-1}
are weakly dependent. Let
\widehat{X}_{0},\dots,\widehat{X}_{k-1}
be independent random variables with
\widehat{X}_{i}{=_{d}X_{i}}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
T
satisfies the Functional Correlation Bound with rate
n^{-\gamma}
for some
\gamma>0
. Let
R=\max_{i}\left|\varPhi_{i}\right|_{\infty}
. Then for all Lipschitz
F\colon[-R,R]^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

\big|\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[F(X_{0},\dots,X_{k-1})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[F(\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{0},\dots,\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{k-1})\right]\big|\\
\leq C\sum_{r=0}^{k-2}(\ell_{r+1}-u_{r})^{-\gamma}\biggl(\left|F\right|_{\infty}+\mathrm{Lip}(F)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sum_{j=0}^{u_{i}-\ell_{i}}[\varPhi_{i}]_{\mathcal{H},j}\biggr),

where
C>0
only depends on
T\colon M\rightarrow M
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
k
. For
k=1
the inequality is trivial. Assume that this lemma holds for
k\geq 1
.

Consider an enriched probability space which contains independent copies of
\{X_{i}\}
and
\{\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{i}\}
. Write

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[F(X_{0},\dots,X_{k})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[F(\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{0},\dots,\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{k})\right]=I_{1}+I_{2}

where

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[F(X_{0},\dots,X_{k-1},\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{k})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[F(\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{0},\dots,\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{k})\right],

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[F(X_{0},\dots,X_{k})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[F(X_{0},\dots,X_{k-1},\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{k})\right].

Since
\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{k}=_{d}X_{k}
and
\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{k}
is independent of
X_{0},\dots,X_{k-1}
and
\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{0},\dots,\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{k-1}
,

I_{1}=\int_{M}\big(\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[F\big(X_{0},\dots,X_{k-1},X_{k}(y)\big)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[F\big(\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{0},\dots,\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{k-1},X_{k}(y)\big)\right]\big)d\mu(y).

Let
y\in M.
The function
F_{y}=F(\cdot,\dots,\cdot,X_{k}(y))\colon M^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies
\mathrm{Lip}(F_{y})\leq\mathrm{Lip}(F)
. Hence by the inductive hypothesis,

\begin{split}|I_{1}|&\leq\int\big|\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[F_{y}(X_{0},\dots,X_{k-1})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[F_{y}(\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{0},\dots,\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{k-1})\right]\big|d\mu(y)\\
&\leq\int C\sum_{r=0}^{k-2}(\ell_{r+1}-u_{r})^{-\gamma}\biggl(\left|F_{y}\right|_{\infty}+\mathrm{Lip}(F_{y})\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sum_{j=0}^{u_{i}-\ell_{i}}[\varPhi_{i}]_{\mathcal{H},j}\,\biggr)d\mu(y)\\
&\leq C\sum_{r=0}^{k-2}(\ell_{r+1}-u_{r})^{-\gamma}\biggl(\left|F\right|_{\infty}+\mathrm{Lip}(F)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sum_{j=0}^{u_{i}-\ell_{i}}[\varPhi_{i}]_{\mathcal{H},j}\biggr).\end{split}

Now

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[F(X_{0},\dots,X_{k})\right]-\int_{M}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[F\big(X_{0},\dots,X_{k-1},X_{k}(y)\big)\right]d\mu(y)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}F\big(X_{0}(x),\dots,X_{k}(x)\big)d\mu(x)-\int_{M^{2}}F\big(X_{0}(x),\dots,X_{k-1}(x),X_{k}(y)\big)d\mu^{2}(x,y).

Write

\displaystyle F(X_{0}

\displaystyle(x),\dots,X_{k}(x))

\displaystyle=F(\varPhi_{0}(T^{\ell_{0}}x,\dots,T^{u_{0}}x);\varPhi_{1}(T^{\ell_{1}}x,\dots,T^{u_{1}}x);\dots;\varPhi_{k}(T^{\ell_{k}}x,\dots,T^{u_{k}}x))

\displaystyle=G(T^{\ell_{0}}x,\dots,T^{u_{0}}x;T^{\ell_{1}}x,\dots,T^{u_{1}}x;\dots;T^{\ell_{k}}x,\dots,T^{u_{k}}x).

and

\displaystyle F(X_{0}

\displaystyle(x),\dots,X_{k-1}(x),X_{k}(y))

\displaystyle=G(T^{\ell_{0}}x,\dots,T^{u_{0}}x;T^{\ell_{1}}x,\dots,T^{u_{1}}x;\dots;T^{\ell_{k-1}}x,\dots,T^{u_{k-1}}x;T^{\ell_{k}}y,\dots,T^{u_{k}}y)

where
G\colon M^{s}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
s=\sum_{i=0}^{k}(u_{i}-\ell_{i}+1)
. By a straightforward calculation,
G\in\mathcal{SH}_{s}(M)
and

\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}[G]_{\mathcal{H},i}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{u_{i}-\ell_{i}}\mathrm{Lip}(F)[\varPhi_{i}]_{\mathcal{H},j}.

Hence by the Functional Correlation Bound,

\displaystyle|I_{2}|

\displaystyle=\bigg|\int_{M}G(T^{\ell_{0}}x,\dots,T^{u_{0}}x;\dots;T^{\ell_{k}}x,\dots,T^{u_{k}}x)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{M^{2}}G(T^{\ell_{0}}x,\dots,T^{u_{0}}x;\dots;T^{\ell_{k-1}}x,\dots,T^{u_{k-1}}x;T^{\ell_{k}}y,\dots,T^{u_{k}}y)d\mu^{2}(x,y)\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C(\ell_{k}-u_{k-1})^{-\gamma}\biggl(\left|F\right|_{\infty}+\sum_{i=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{u_{i}-\ell_{i}}\mathrm{Lip}(F)[\varPhi_{i}]_{\mathcal{H},j}\biggr).

This completes the proof.
∎

In this section we prove Theorem

In both parts of Theorem

[

Lemma 5.1
.

Fix

Let

be mean zero. For

we denote

Note that

and

Proposition 5.2

and

\displaystyle S_{v}(a_{\ell})=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}S_{v}(a_{i},a_{i+1}).

(ii)

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{v,w}(a_{\ell})=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell-1}\mathbb{S}_{v,w}(a_{i},a_{i+1})+\sum_{0\leq i<j<\ell}S_{v}(a_{i},a_{i+1})S_{w}(a_{j},a_{j+1}).
∎

We also need the following elementary proposition:

Proposition 5.3
.

Fix
R>0
,
p\geq 1
and an integer

. Define

Note that
\left|F\right|_{\infty}\leq(kR)^{p}

\displaystyle|F(y_{0},\dots,y_{k-1})-F(y^{\prime}_{0},\dots,y^{\prime}_{k-1})|

\displaystyle\leq p\max\{a^{p-1},b^{p-1}\}|a-b|

\displaystyle\leq p(kR)^{p-1}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}|y_{i}-y^{\prime}_{i}|=p(kR)^{p-1}|y-y^{\prime}|,

Let

(5.1)

(5.7)

We now bound
\mathbb{E}\left[|\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{i}|^{2\gamma}\right]
by using Lemma
5.1
and the inductive hypothesis.

Fix
0\leq i<k
. By stationarity,
X_{i}=S_{v}(a_{2i},a_{2i+1})=_{d}S_{v}(a_{2i+1}-a_{2i}).

Thus by the inductive hypothesis (
5.6
),
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[|X_{i}|^{2\gamma}\right]\leq D^{2\gamma}(a_{2i+1}-a_{2i})^{\gamma}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2\gamma}
.
Hence by (
5.1
),

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}\left[|\vphantom{X}\smash[t]{\widehat{X}}_{i}|^{2\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}D^{2\gamma}(a_{2i+1}-a_{2i})^{\gamma}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}D^{2\gamma}(n/k)^{\gamma}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2\gamma}=D^{2\gamma}k^{1-\gamma}n^{\gamma}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2\gamma}.

Now by the Functional Correlation Bound,
|\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[v\,v\circ T^{n}\right]|\leq Cn^{-\gamma}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
. By a standard calculation, it follows that
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[S_{v}(n)^{2}\right]\leq Cn\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}
. Thus

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{X}_{i}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[S_{v}(a_{2i+1}-a_{2i})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}C(a_{2i+1}-a_{2i})\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\leq C(a_{2k-1}-a_{0})\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq Cn\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

By Lemma
5.1
(ii)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\biggl|\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\widehat{X}_{i}\biggr|^{2\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\big((Cn\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2})^{\gamma}+D^{2\gamma}k^{1-\gamma}n^{\gamma}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2\gamma}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+D^{2\gamma}k^{1-\gamma})n^{\gamma}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2\gamma}.

Hence by (
5.7
), overall

\left|I_{1}\right|_{2\gamma}^{2\gamma}\leq C(k^{1+\gamma}+D^{2\gamma}k^{1-\gamma})n^{\gamma}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2\gamma}.

Exactly the same argument applies to
|I_{2}|_{2\gamma}^{2\gamma}.
The conclusion of the claim follows by noting that

\left|S_{v}(n)\right|_{2\gamma}^{2\gamma}=\left|I_{1}+I_{2}\right|_{2\gamma}^{2\gamma}\leq 2^{2\gamma}(\left|I_{1}\right|_{2\gamma}+\left|I_{2}\right|_{2\gamma}).\qed

We now prove Theorem
2.4
(b)
. Our proof follows the same lines as that of part
(a)
.

Let
n,k\geq 1.
Recall that
a_{i}=\left[\tfrac{in}{2k}\right]
. For
0\leq i<k
define mean zero random variables
X_{i}
on
(M,\mu)
by

X_{i}=\mathbb{S}_{v,w}(a_{2i},a_{2i+1})-\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[\mathbb{S}_{v,w}(a_{2i},a_{2i+1})\right].

Let
\widehat{X}_{0},\dots,\widehat{X}_{k-1}
be independent random variables with
\widehat{X}_{i}=_{d}X_{i}
.

The following lemma plays the same role that Lemma
5.4
played in the proof of Theorem
2.4
(a)
.

Lemma 5.5
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for any
v,w\in\mathcal{H}(M)
,

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[\bigg|\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}X_{i}\bigg|^{\gamma}\right]\leq Ckn^{\gamma}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{\gamma}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{\gamma}+\mathbb{E}\left[\bigg|\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\widehat{X}_{i}\bigg|^{\gamma}\right]

for all
n\geq 2k,k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Note that

\displaystyle X_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a_{2i}\leq q<r\leq a_{2i+1}-1}v(T^{q}x)w(T^{r}x)-\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[\mathbb{S}_{v,w}(a_{2i},a_{2i+1})\right]

\displaystyle=\varPhi_{i}(T^{\ell_{i}}x,\dots,T^{u_{i}}x),

where
\ell_{i}=a_{2i},u_{i}=a_{2i+1}-1
and

\varPhi_{i}(x_{0},\dots,x_{u_{i}-\ell_{i}})=\sum_{0\leq q<r\leq u_{i}-\ell_{i}}v(x_{q})w(x_{r})-\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[\mathbb{S}_{v,w}(a_{2i},a_{2i+1})\right].

Let
R=\max_{i}\left|\varPhi_{i}\right|_{\infty}
. Observe that

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[\bigg|\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}X_{i}\bigg|^{\gamma}\right]=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[F(X_{0},\dots,X_{k-1})\right],

where
F\colon[-R,R]^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by
F(y_{0},\dots,y_{k-1})=|y_{0}+\dots+y_{k-1}|^{\gamma}.
Hence by Lemma
4.1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[\bigg|\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}X_{i}\bigg|^{\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle\leq A+\mathbb{E}\left[\bigg|\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\widehat{X}_{i}\bigg|^{2\gamma}\right]

where

|A|\leq C\sum_{r=0}^{k-2}(\ell_{r+1}-u_{r})^{-\gamma}\biggl(\left|F\right|_{\infty}+\mathrm{Lip}(F)\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sum_{j=0}^{u_{i}-\ell_{i}}[\varPhi_{i}]_{\mathcal{H},j}\biggr).

(5.8)

It remains to bound
A
. The first step is to bound the expressions
[\varPhi_{i}]_{\mathcal{H},j}
. Fix
0\leq i<k,0\leq j\leq u_{i}-\ell_{i}.
Let
x_{0},\dots,x_{k-1},x_{j}^{\prime}\in M.
Note that

\varPhi_{i}(x_{0},\dots,x_{u_{i}-\ell_{i}})-\varPhi_{i}(x_{0},\dots,x_{j-1},x^{\prime}_{j},x_{j+1}\dots,x_{u_{i}-\ell_{i}})=J_{1}+J_{2},

where

\displaystyle J_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j<r\leq u_{i}-\ell_{i}}(v(x_{j})w(x_{r})-v(x^{\prime}_{j})w(x_{r})),\qquad J_{2}=\sum_{0\leq q<j}(v(x_{q})w(x_{j})-v(x_{q})w(x^{\prime}_{j})).

Now,

|J_{1}|\leq\sum_{j<r\leq u_{i}-\ell_{i}}|v(x_{j})-v(x^{\prime}_{j})||w(x_{r})|\leq\left|w\right|_{\infty}\sum_{j<r\leq u_{i}-\ell_{i}}|v(x_{j})-v(x^{\prime}_{j})|

and similarly
|J_{2}|\leq\left|v\right|_{\infty}\sum_{0\leq q<j}|w(x_{j})-w(x^{\prime}_{j})|
, so

[\varPhi_{i}]_{\mathcal{H},j}\leq(u_{i}-\ell_{i})\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}.

Now recall from (
5.1
) that
u_{i}-\ell_{i}+1=a_{2i+1}-a_{2i}\leq n/k
so

\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\sum_{j=0}^{u_{i}-\ell_{i}}[\varPhi_{i}]_{\mathcal{H},j}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(u_{i}-\ell_{i}+1)^{2}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\leq\tfrac{n^{2}}{k}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}.

(5.9)

Next note that

\displaystyle\left|\varPhi_{i}\right|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{0\leq q<r\leq u_{i}-\ell_{i}}\left|v\right|_{\infty}\left|w\right|_{\infty}+\left|\mathbb{S}_{v,w}(a_{2i},a_{2i+1})\right|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 2(n/k)^{2}\left|v\right|_{\infty}\left|w\right|_{\infty}

so by Proposition
5.3
,

\left|F\right|_{\infty}\leq\bigl(\tfrac{2n^{2}}{k}\left|v\right|_{\infty}\left|w\right|_{\infty}\bigr)^{\gamma}

and

\mathrm{Lip}(F)\leq\gamma\big(\tfrac{2n^{2}}{k}\left|v\right|_{\infty}\left|w\right|_{\infty}\big)^{\gamma-1}.
Combining these bounds with (
5.5
), (
5.8
) and (
5.9
) yields that

\displaystyle|A|

\displaystyle\leq C2^{\gamma}k^{1+\gamma}n^{-\gamma}\bigl((\tfrac{2n^{2}}{k}\left|v\right|_{\infty}\left|w\right|_{\infty})^{\gamma}+\gamma(\tfrac{2n^{2}}{k}\left|v\right|_{\infty}\left|w\right|_{\infty}\big)^{\gamma-1}\tfrac{n^{2}}{k}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}\bigr)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2\gamma}(1+\gamma/2)Ckn^{\gamma}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{\gamma}\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{\gamma},

as required.
∎

Bose-Hubbard models with on-site and nearest-neighbor interactions: Exactly solvable case
Saidakhmat Lakaev, Shokhrukh Kholmatov, Shakhobiddin Khamidov

Abstract
. We study the discrete spectrum of the two-particle Schrödinger operator H ^ μ ​ λ ​ ( K ) , \widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(K), K ∈ 𝕋 2 , K\in\mathbb{T}^{2}, associated to the Bose-Hubbard Hamiltonian ℍ ^ μ ​ λ \widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu\lambda} of a system of two identical bosons interacting on site and nearest-neighbor sites in the two dimensional lattice ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2} with interaction magnitudes μ ∈ ℝ \mu\in\mathbb{R} and λ ∈ ℝ , \lambda\in\mathbb{R}, respectively. We completely describe the spectrum of H ^ μ ​ λ ​ ( 0 ) \widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(0) and establish the optimal lower bound for the number of eigenvalues of H ^ μ ​ λ ​ ( K ) \widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(K) outside its essential spectrum for all values of K ∈ 𝕋 2 . K\in\mathbb{T}^{2}. Namely, we partition the ( μ , λ ) (\mu,\lambda) -plane such that in each connected component of the partition the number of bound states of H ^ μ ​ λ ​ ( K ) \widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(K) below or above its essential spectrum cannot be less than the corresponding number of bound states of H ^ μ ​ λ ​ ( 0 ) \widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(0) below or above its essential spectrum.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we consider the family
\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(K)
Schrödinger operators, associated to the Bose-Hubbard Hamiltonian
of a system of two identical bosons on the two dimensional lattice

\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with on-site interaction
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
and nearest-neighbor
interaction
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

Lattice Bose-Hubbard models have become popular in recent years
since they represent a minimal, natural Hamiltonian in ultracold
atoms in optical lattices, systems with highly controllable
parameters such as lattice geometry and dimensionality, particle
masses, tunneling, two-body potentials, temperature etc. (see e.g.,

[
2
,
6
,
7
,
17
]

and references therein). Unlike the traditional condensed matter
systems, where stable composite objects are usually formed by
attractive forces and repulsive forces separate particles in free
space, the controllability of collision properties of ultracold
atoms has enabled to experimentally observe a stable

repulsive
bound pair of ultracold atoms in the optical
lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, see e.g.,

[
4
,
20
,
23
,
24
]
. In all these
observations Bose-Hubbard Hamiltonians became a link between the
theoretical basis and experimental results.

The main difficulty in solving Bose-Hubbard Hamiltonian even with
the on-site interaction is due to the tunneling, i.e., the kinetic
energy necessary for a boson to hop from site to site, since it is
highly nonlocal. Moreover, unlike its continuous counterpart, the
lattice Hamiltonian, corresponding to short-range interacting sytems
of particle pairs, is not rotationally invariant, hence, the
separation of lattice Hamiltonian related to the center of
mass-motion is not possible. However, the translation-invariance of
the Hamiltonian (in
d
-dimensional lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
) allows to use
the Floquet-Bloch decomposition: the underlying Hilbert space

\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{2}
and the total two-particle Hamiltonian
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}
are represented as the von Neumann direct integral

\ell^{2}[(\mathbb{Z}^{d})^{2}]\simeq\int\limits_{K\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}\oplus\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\mathrm{d}K,\qquad\widehat{\mathbb{H}}\simeq\int\limits_{K\in\mathbb{T}^{d}}\oplus\widehat{H}(K)\mathrm{d}K,

associated to the representation of the discrete group
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
by the
shift operators, here
\mathbb{T}^{d}
is the
d
-dimensional torus, a
Pontryagin dual group to
\mathbb{Z}^{d}.
The fiber Hamiltonians
\widehat{H}_{\mu}(K)
nontrivially depend on the so-called
quasi-momentum

K\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
(see e.g.,

[
1
,
3
,
18
,
19
]
).

In what follows any nonzero solution
\widehat{\psi}_{K}
to the Schrödinger
equation

\widehat{H}(K)\widehat{\psi}_{K}=e_{K}\widehat{\psi}_{K},\qquad\widehat{\psi}_{K}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}),

will be called bound state with energy
e_{K}
.

In the current paper, we study the family

\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(K):=\widehat{H}_{0}(K)+\widehat{V}_{\mu\lambda},\qquad K\in\mathbb{T}^{2},

discrete Schrödinger operators associated to the Hamiltonian

\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu\lambda}
of a system of two identical bosons on the two
dimensional lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with zero-range on-site interaction

\mu\neq 0
and nearest-neighbor interaction
\lambda\neq 0
(see (
2.5
) in
Subsection
2.3
). To the best of our knowledge,
this is a new, exactly solvable model, for which the exact number of
eigenvalues and their locations as well as exact lower and upper
bounds for all values of the pair interactions
\mu,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

can be found.

First we observe that the essential spectrum of
\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(K)
consists of a segment

[\mathcal{E}_{\min}(K),\allowbreak\mathcal{E}_{\max}(K)],
where

\mathcal{E}_{\min}(K):=2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}\Big(1-\cos\tfrac{K_{i}}{2}\Big),\qquad\mathcal{E}_{\max}(K):=2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}\Big(1+\cos\tfrac{K_{i}}{2}\Big)

(see Subsection
2.4
).

Now we study the discrete spectrum of
\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(K).
The
eigenvalue problem for
\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(K)
with general
K
is
not an easy problem; note that
\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(K)
becomes a
small perturbation of
\widehat{V}_{\mu\lambda}
if
K
is close to
\vec{\pi}:=(-\pi,\pi),
and
\widehat{V}_{\mu\lambda}
is a small
pertubation of
\widehat{H}_{0}
provided that
\mu,\lambda
is small.
However, it turns out that for any
\mu,\lambda\in\mathbb{R},
if

e_{n}(K;\mu,\lambda)
and
E_{n}(K;\mu,\lambda)
are the
n
-th
eigenvalues of
\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(K)
below and above the
essential spectrum, respectively, then the functions
\mathcal{E}_{\min}(K)-e_{n}(K;\mu,\lambda)
and
E_{n}(K;\mu,\lambda)-\mathcal{E}_{\max}(K)
as a
function of
K
will have a minimum at
K=0
(see Lemma

4.9
). Subsequently, the number of
eigenvalues
\mathrm{n}_{+}(\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(K))
(resp.

\mathrm{n}_{-}(\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(K))
) of
\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(K)

above (resp. below) the essential spectrum is not less than that of

\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(0)
(Theorem
3.1
). This result is a generalization of
[
1
, Theorems 1 and 2]
which contain the assertion related only to the ground state of
\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(K).

To study the discrete spectrum of
\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(0)
we introduce the Fredholm determinants
\Delta_{\mu\lambda}^{(s)}(z)
and
\Delta_{\lambda}^{(a)}(z),
associated to the restriction of
\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(0)
onto symmetric and antisymmetric functions in
\ell^{2,e}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})=\ell^{2,e}(\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z})
w.r.t. coordinate permutations

of one-particle.

This fact is tightly connected to

2.
Discrete Schrödinger operators on lattices

2.1.
The two-particle Hamiltonian: the position-space representation

Let
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
be the two dimensional lattice and let

\ell^{2,s}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2})
be the Hilbert space of square-summable
symmetric functions on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

In the position-space representation the two particle
Hamiltonian
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu\lambda},
associated to a system of two
bosons interacting via zero-range and nearest-neighbor potential

\widehat{v}_{\mu\lambda}
is a bounded self-adjoint operator
acting in
\ell^{2,s}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2})
as

\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu\lambda}=\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{0}+\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{\mu\lambda},\qquad\mu,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

Here the free Hamiltonian
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{0}
of a system of two identical particles (bosons) is a bounded self–adjoint operator acting in
\ell^{2,s}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2})
as

\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{0}\widehat{f}(x,y)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\widehat{\varepsilon}(x-n)\widehat{f}(n,y)+\sum\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\widehat{\varepsilon}(y-n)\widehat{f}(x,n),

where

(2.1)

\widehat{\varepsilon}(s)=\begin{cases}2&\text{if $|s|=0,$}\\
-\frac{1}{2}&\text{if $|s|=1,$}\\
0&\text{if $|s|>1,$}\end{cases}

and
|s|=|s_{1}|+|s_{2}|
for
s=(s_{1},s_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

The interaction
\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{\mu\lambda}
is the multiplication
operator

\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{\mu\lambda}\widehat{f}(x,y)=\widehat{v}_{\mu\lambda}(x-y)\widehat{f}(x,y),

given by the function

(2.2)

\widehat{v}_{\mu\lambda}(s)=\begin{cases}\mu&\text{if $|s|=0,$}\\
\frac{\lambda}{2}&\text{if $|s|=1,$}\\
0&\text{if $|s|>1.$}\end{cases}

2.2.
The two-particle Hamiltonian: the momentum-space representation

Let
\mathbb{T}^{2}=\allowbreak(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{2}\equiv[-\pi,\pi)^{2}
be the two
dimensional torus, the Pontryagin dual group of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, equipped
with the Haar measure
\mathrm{d}p,
and let
L^{2,s}(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{2})
be
the Hilbert space of square-integrable symmetric functions on

\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}.
Let
\mathcal{F}:\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
be the
standard Fourier transform

\mathcal{F}\widehat{f}(p)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\widehat{f}(x)e^{ip\cdot x},

where
p\cdot x:=p_{1}x_{1}+p_{2}x_{2}
for
p=(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
and

x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

In the momentum-space via
\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{F}
the two-particle
Hamiltonian is represented in
L^{2,s}(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{2})
as

\mathbb{H}_{\mu\lambda}:=(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{F})\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu\lambda}(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{F})^{*}:=\mathbb{H}_{0}+\mathbb{V}_{\mu\lambda}.

Here the free Hamiltonian
\mathbb{H}_{0}=(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{F})\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{0}(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{F})^{*}
is the multiplication operator:

\mathbb{H}_{0}f(p,q)=[\varepsilon(p)+\varepsilon(q)]f(p,q),

where

\varepsilon(p):=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}\big(1-\cos p_{i}),\quad p=(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbb{T}^{2},

is the
dispersion relation
of a single boson.
The interaction
\mathbb{V}_{\mu\lambda}=(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{F})\widehat{\mathbb{V}}_{\mu\lambda}(\mathcal{F}\otimes\mathcal{F})^{*}
is the (partial) integral operator

\mathbb{V}_{\mu\lambda}f(p,q)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}v_{\mu\lambda}(p-u)f(u,p+q-u)\mathrm{d}u,

where

v_{\mu\lambda}(p)=\mu+\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{2}\cos p_{i},\quad p=(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbb{T}^{2}.

2.3.
The Floquet-Bloch decomposition of
\mathbb{H}_{\mu\lambda}
and discrete Schrödinger operators

Since
\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}
commutes with the representation of the
discrete group
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by shift operators on the lattice, we can
decompose the space
L^{2,s}(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{2})
and
\mathbb{H}_{\mu\lambda}

into the von Neumann direct integral as

(2.3)

L^{2,s}(\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\mathbb{T}^{2})\simeq\int\limits_{K\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\oplus L^{2,e}(\mathbb{T}^{2})\,\mathrm{d}K

and

(2.4)

\mathbb{H}_{\mu\lambda}\simeq\int\limits_{K\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\oplus H_{\mu\lambda}(K)\,\mathrm{d}K,

where
L^{2,e}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
is the Hilbert space of square-integrable even
functions on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
(see, e.g.,
[
1
]
).

The fiber operator
H_{\mu\lambda}(K),

K\in\mathbb{T}^{2},
is a self-adjoint operator defined in
L^{2,e}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
as

H_{\mu\lambda}(K):=H_{0}(K)+V_{\mu\lambda},

where the unperturbed operator
H_{0}(K)
is the multiplication operator by the function

\mathcal{E}_{K}(p):=2\sum_{i=1}^{2}\Big(1-\cos\tfrac{K_{i}}{2}\,\cos p_{i}\Big),

and the perturbation
V_{\mu\lambda}
is defined as

V_{\mu\lambda}f(p)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}\Big(\mu+\lambda\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}\cos p_{i}\cos q_{i}\Big)f(q)\mathrm{d}q.

In the literature the parameter
K\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
is called the

two-particle quasi-momentum
and the fiber
H_{\mu\lambda}(K)

is called the
discrete Schrödinger operator
associated to
the two-particle Hamiltonian
\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu\lambda}.

Using the Fourier transform (
2.3
) and (
2.4
)
can be represented as

\ell^{2,s}(\mathbb{Z}^{2}\times\mathbb{Z}^{2})\simeq\int\limits_{K\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\oplus\ell^{2,e}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})\,\mathrm{d}K

and

\widehat{\mathbb{H}}_{\mu\lambda}\simeq\int\limits_{K\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\oplus\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(K)\,\mathrm{d}K,

where
\ell^{2,e}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
is the Hilbert space of square-summable
even functions on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and

\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(K):=\mathcal{F}^{*}H_{\mu\lambda}(K)\mathcal{F},

where

(2.5)

\widehat{H}_{\mu\lambda}(K)=\widehat{H}_{0}(K)+\widehat{V}_{\mu\lambda}(K),

and

\widehat{H}_{0}(K)f(x)\ =\ \sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{K}(x-s)\widehat{f}(s),\qquad f\in\ell^{2,e}\mathbb{Z}^{2}

with

\widehat{\mathcal{E}}_{K}(x)=2\widehat{\varepsilon}(x)\,\cos\frac{K\cdot x}{2}

and the operator
\widehat{V}_{\mu\lambda}
acts in
\ell^{2,e}(\mathbb{Z}^{2})
as

\widehat{V}_{\mu\lambda}f(x)=\widehat{v}_{\mu\lambda}(x)f(x),

and the functions
\widehat{\varepsilon}
and
\widehat{v}_{\mu\lambda}
are given by (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), respectively.

2.4.
The essential spectrum of discrete Schrödinger operators

Since
V_{\mu\lambda}
has rank at most three, by Weyl’s Theorem for
any
K\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
the essential spectrum

\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H_{\mu\lambda}(K))
coincides with the spectrum of

H_{0}(K),
i.e.,

(2.6)

\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(H_{\mu\lambda}(K))=\sigma(H_{0}(K))=[\mathcal{E}_{\min}(K),\mathcal{E}_{\max}(K)],

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\min}(K):=

\displaystyle\min_{p\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\,\mathcal{E}_{K}(p)=2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}\Big(1-\cos\tfrac{K_{i}}{2}\Big)\geq 0=\mathcal{E}_{\min}(0),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\max}(K):=

\displaystyle\max_{p\in\mathbb{T}^{2}}\,\mathcal{E}_{K}(p)=2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{2}\Big(1+\cos\tfrac{K_{i}}{2}\Big)\leq 8=\mathcal{E}_{\max}(0).

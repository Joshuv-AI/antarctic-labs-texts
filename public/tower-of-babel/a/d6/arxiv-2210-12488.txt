Symmetry breaking and weighted Euclidean logarithmic Sobolev inequalities
Jean Dolbeault and Andres Zuniga

Abstract
Abstract. On the Euclidean space, we establish some Weighted Logarithmic Sobolev (WLS) inequalities. We characterize a symmetry range in which optimal functions are radially symmetric, and a symmetry breaking range. (WLS) inequalities are a limit case for a family of subcritical Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg (CKN) inequalities with similar symmetry properties. A generalized carré du champ method applies not only to the optimal solution of the nonlinear elliptic Euler-Lagrange equation and proves a rigidity result as for (CKN) inequalities, but also to entropy type estimates, with the full strength of the carré du champ method in a parabolic setting. This is a significant improvement on known results for (CKN). Finally, we briefly sketch some consequences of our results for the weighted diffusion flow. Keywords. Logarithmic Sobolev inequality; Hardy-Sobolev inequality; Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality; symmetry breaking; symmetry; concentration-compactness; optimal functions; optimal constant; carré du champ method MSC 2020. Introduction and main results

Logarithmic Sobolev inequalities
are well known cases of functional inequalities with many applications in various areas of mathematics ranging from information theory to probability theory, functional analysis, differential geometry and mathematical physics. In partial differential equations, these inequalities now appear as fundamental tools for the understanding of rates of convergence, not only for diffusion equations but also, for instance, in kinetic theory. Various settings have been considered depending on the geometry, the presence of a drift or a potential, or the choice of a reference measure. Sharp inequalities, with optimal constants, and equality cases are trickier issues, as the problem is usually difficult to reduce to spectral estimates. Among the few known examples, we can quote the case of the sphere and the characterization
[
15
]
by E. Carlen of the set of optimal functions in the Euclidean logarithmic Sobolev inequalities. See
[
40
]
for general weights.

In this article, we mainly focus on the case of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with homogeneous (power-law) weights because of the
symmetry versus symmetry breaking
issue. This is a well-known question for Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities. Although the weights are invariant under rotations, optimal functions are not necessarily spherically symmetric. V. Felli and M. Schneider gave in
[
44
]
a condition for symmetry breaking based on the linear instability of the radial solutions of the Euler-Lagrange equations. Symmetry is a global property. Proving symmetry is therefore a delicate issue and standard methods like moving planes or symmetrization techniques are not sufficient to cover all cases. The problem has recently been fully solved in
[
35
,
38
]
for some special sub-families of the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities using a nonlinear version of the
carré du champ
method introduced by D. Bakry and M. Emery in
[
3
]
, applied to the Euler-Lagrange equation solved by the optimal functions. The underlying framework is based on entropy methods for nonlinear diffusion equations, but the approach is so far formal by lack of regularity estimates to justify all computations: see
[
34
]
for partial results. In the case of (WLS) inequalities, we can use the whole parabolic approach of entropy methods as there is a dense set of (Hermite) polynomials and integrations by parts can be justified. As far as we know, this is the first application of the parabolic
carré du champ
method to the
symmetry versus symmetry breaking
issue for weighted inequalities on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.

Let
\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

\left\|{f}\right\|_{q,\gamma}:=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|f|^{q}\,|x|^{-\gamma}}\,dx\right)^{1/q}

Euclidean logarithmic Sobolev inequalities with Hardy-type correction terms
. We denote by

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\left(|\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pth|^{2}+V_{\alpha,\nu,\sigma}\,|h|^{2}-\sigma\,|h|^{2}\,\log\left(\tfrac{|h|^{2}}{\left\|{h}\right\|_{2}^{2}}\right)\right)}\,dx\geq\sigma\left(\tfrac{n}{2}\,\log\left(\tfrac{2\,e}{n\,\sigma}\right)-\mathscr{K}_{n,\alpha}\right)\left\|{h}\right\|_{2}^{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,h\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

V_{\alpha,\nu,\sigma}(x):=-\frac{1}{4}\,\alpha^{2}\,\nu\,\big(2\,(d-2)-\nu\big)\,\frac{1}{|x|^{2}}-\sigma\,\nu\,\log|x|\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\,.

Inequality (
12

\alpha=1
and we omit the potential, a Schwarz symmetrization shows that the minimum of the Schrödinger energy is achieved by a radially symmetric function

in the set of the minima of

which, as a curved surface is not compatible with radial symmetry centred at

\alpha

The

\sigma=1

Corollary 1.3

Under the assumptions of Theorem

In the limit case

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=|x|^{\gamma}\,\nabla\cdot\left(|x|^{-\beta}\,\nabla u\right)\,,

(15)

The results on sharp functional inequalities in
[

]
are inspired, on the one hand, by the rigidity results for nonlinear elliptic equations studied in

carré du champ

See
[
10

Applied to nonlinear flows on the Euclidean space, new difficulties arise as, for instance, integration by parts require precise decay bounds which are not easy to justify. Progress in the absence of singular weights has been achieved in
[

,

. In presence of weights, the method formally applies but only partial results have been rigorously justified in
[

,

,

For the optimizers of functional inequalities involving singular weights, the difficulty can be bypassed by proving the existence of minimizers and testing directly the solutions of the Euler-Lagrange, which amounts to testing such critical points in the direction corresponding to the flow. This is the simplest interpretation of the method of B. Gidas and J. Spruck in
[
47

. The issue is then reduced to a rigidity issue for solutions of elliptic equations which, as such, have good regularity and decay properties. So far, all sharp results of symmetry in Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities have been obtained using such an approach. In the case of logarithmic Sobolev inequalities, we are able to perform the whole parabolic method as there is a dense set of Hermite functions, in the appropriate version of the inequality, and integrations by parts can be justified. To our knowledge, this is the first result of symmetry
versus
symmetry breaking to be proved with the parabolic version of the
carré du champ
method.

2
Optimal functions, symmetry and symmetry breaking in (
WLS
) inequalities

2.1
The weighted logarithmic Sobolev inequality

We start by proving that Inequality (
WLS
) is well-defined.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
. Assume that
(\beta,\gamma)\neq(0,0)
satisfies (
2
). Then the inequality

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{|f|^{2}}{\left\|{f}\right\|_{2,\gamma}^{2}}\,\log\left(\frac{|f|^{2}}{\left\|{f}\right\|_{2,\gamma}^{2}}\right)\,|x|^{-\gamma}}\,dx\leq\mathscr{C}_{\beta,\gamma}^{\star}+\frac{n}{2}\,\log\left(\frac{\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{2,\beta}^{2}}{\left\|{f}\right\|_{2,\gamma}^{2}}\right)\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,f\in\mathrm{H}^{1,1}_{\beta,\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

holds with
\mathscr{C}_{\beta,\gamma}^{\star}
defined by (
5
).

In other words, we prove here that Inequality (
WLS
) holds for some constant
\mathscr{C}_{\beta,\gamma}\leq\mathscr{C}_{\beta,\gamma}^{\star}
. Since (
WLS
) is subcritical, it is a standard strategy to establish the inequality using an Hölder interpolation and a critical inequality.

Proof 2.2
.

Let
p:=2\,\frac{d-\gamma}{d-2-\beta}\in(2,2^{*})
with
2^{*}=+\infty
if
d=1
,
2
and
2^{*}=2\,d/(d-2)
if
d\geq 3
. Let us consider the critical Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|\nabla u|^{2}\,|x|^{-\beta}}\,dx\geq\mathcal{C}_{\rm{CKN}}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|u|^{p}\,|x|^{-\gamma}}\,dx\right)^{2/p}

(16)

which has been widely studied, see for instance
[
51
,
14
,
17
,
35
]
. Here
\beta
and
\gamma
satisfy (
2
) and
n
given by (
1
) is such that

\frac{p}{p-2}=\frac{n}{2}\,.

Hölder’s inequality

\left\|{u}\right\|_{q,\gamma}\leq\left\|{u}\right\|_{2}^{\eta}\,\left\|{u}\right\|_{p,\gamma}^{1-\eta}\,,

written with
\eta=2\,\frac{p-q}{q\,(p-2)}
for any
q\in(2,p)
, degenenerates into an equality as
q\to 2_{+}
. By differentiating this inequality with respect to
q
at
q=2
, we obtain the
logarithmic Hölder inequality

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|u|^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{|u|^{2}}{\left\|{u}\right\|_{2,\gamma}^{2}}\right)|x|^{-\gamma}}\,dx\leq\frac{p}{p-2}\,\left\|{u}\right\|_{2,\gamma}^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{\left\|{u}\right\|_{p,\gamma}^{2}}{\left\|{u}\right\|_{2,\gamma}^{2}}\right)

(17)

for any
p>2
. Combined with (
16
) in the case
p=2\,\frac{d-\gamma}{d-2-\beta}
, this establishes the
weighted logarithmic Sobolev inequality

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|u|^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{|u|^{2}}{\left\|{u}\right\|_{2,\gamma}^{2}}\right)|x|^{-\gamma}}\,dx\leq\mathcal{A}\,\left\|{u}\right\|_{2,\gamma}^{2}\,\log\left(\frac{\left\|{\nabla u}\right\|_{2,\beta}^{2}}{\left\|{u}\right\|_{2,\gamma}^{2}}\right)+\mathcal{B}\,\left\|{u}\right\|_{2,\gamma}^{2}

(18)

with
\mathcal{A}=n/2
,
n
given by (
1
) and
\mathcal{B}=n\,\log\mathcal{C}_{\rm{CKN}}
. The value of
\mathcal{A}
cannot be improved, as shown by the scaling

\lambda\mapsto u_{\lambda}:=\lambda^{n/2}\,u(\lambda\cdot)\,.

Testing (
18
) by
f_{\star}(x)=c_{n,d}\,\sqrt{\alpha}\,e^{-\,\frac{1}{4}\,|x|^{2\alpha}}
shows that
\mathcal{B}\geq\mathscr{C}_{\beta,\gamma}^{\star}
. The optimal value of
\mathcal{B}
is therefore the minimal value for which (
18
) holds for any
u\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\beta,\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

2.2
Existence of optimal functions

The existence of an optimal function for (
WLS
) is proved in
[
13
]
by concentration-compactness methods when
n=d
and
\alpha=1
. A similar proof can be found in
[
30
]
, which itself relies on an extension of the concentration-compactness method of
[
54
]
. The proof in the case
(n,\alpha)\neq(d,1)
can also be done by the same method.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
d\geq 1
and asume that
(\beta,\gamma)
satisfies (
2
). Equality in (
WLS
) is achieved by a function
u\in\mathrm{H}^{1}_{\beta,\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
if
\mathscr{C}_{\beta,\gamma}
is taken to its optimal value.

Proof 2.4
.

We work with the inequality written in the form (
12
) with
V_{\alpha,\nu,\sigma}
defined by (
13
) and rely on direct variational methods. Since
V_{\alpha,\nu,\sigma}
is bounded from below, there is no significant difficulty compared to the proof of the existence of a minimizer for logarithmic Sobolev inequalities without weights or potentials (see for instance
[
13
,
30
]
for similar results). For completeness, let us give a sketch of a proof.

Using the homogeneity, let us consider a minimizing sequence
(h_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of functions in
\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
\left\|{h_{n}}\right\|_{2}=1
for any
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\left(|\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pth_{n}|^{2}+V_{\alpha,\nu,\sigma}\,|h_{n}|^{2}-\sigma\,|h_{n}|^{2}\,\log|h_{n}|^{2}\right)}\,dx=\sigma\left(\mathscr{K}_{n,\alpha}+\frac{n}{2}\,\log\left(\frac{2\,e}{n\,\sigma}\right)\right)\,.

An optimization under scalings shows that we can choose
\left\|{\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pth_{n}}\right\|_{2}=\sigma\,n/2
with no loss of generality. Using
\left\|{\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pth_{n}}\right\|_{2}\geq\min\{1,\alpha\}\,\left\|{\nabla h_{n}}\right\|_{2}
and the standard Euclidean logarithmic Sobolev inequality, we have that
\big(|h_{n}|^{2}\,\log|h_{n}|^{2}\big)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
and
\big((V_{\alpha,\nu,\sigma})_{+}^{1/2}\,h_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
are bounded in
\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. For any
R>1
large enough, since

\int_{|x|>R}|h_{n}|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{\log R}\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{V_{\alpha,\nu,\sigma}\,|h_{n}|^{2}}\,dx

for some positive constant
C
and since concentration is forbidden away from origin by standard Gagliardo-Nirenberg embedding inequalities and (
17
) while concentration at
x=0
would provide us with an infinite contribution to the potential energy term, the sequence
(h_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is relatively compact in
\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Up to the extraction of a subsequence,
(h_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
strongly converges in
\mathrm{L}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to some limit
h
such that
\left\|{h}\right\|_{2}=1
. According to
[
12
, Theorem 2]
, we have

\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|h_{n}|^{2}\,\log|h_{n}|^{2}}\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|h|^{2}\,\log|h|^{2}}\,dx+\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|h-h_{n}|^{2}\,\log|h-h_{n}|^{2}}\,dx\,.

By (
12
) applied to
(h-h_{n})
and a convexity argument as in
[
30
]
,
\lim_{n\to+\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|h-h_{n}|^{2}\,\log|h-h_{n}|^{2}}\,dx=0
and we conclude that
h
realizes the equality case in (
12
). This completes the proof.

2.3
Linear instability and a symmetry breaking range

With
\mathsf{f}(\mathsf{x},\mathsf{y}):=\mathsf{x}^{1-\frac{2}{n}}\,e^{\frac{2}{n}\,\frac{\mathsf{y}}{\mathsf{x}}}
, Inequality (
WLSn
) becomes

\mathcal{F}[g]:=\left\|{\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5ptg}\right\|_{2,\nu}^{2}-e^{-\frac{2}{n}\,\mathscr{K}_{n,\alpha}}\,\mathsf{f}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|g|^{2}\,|x|^{-\nu}}\,dx,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|g|^{2}\,\log\left(|g|^{2}\right)\,|x|^{-\nu}}\,dx\right)\geq 0\,.

We Taylor expand
\mathcal{F}[g]
around
g_{*}
by computing
\mathsf{F}[\phi]:=\frac{1}{2}\,\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{-2}\,\mathcal{F}[g_{*}+\varepsilon\,\phi]
and find that

\mathsf{F}[\phi]=\left\|{\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\phi}\right\|_{2,\nu}^{2}-e^{-\frac{2}{n}\,\mathscr{K}_{n,\alpha}}\,\Bigg(\partial_{\mathsf{x}}\mathsf{f}(1,\mathsf{y}_{*})\,\left\|{\phi}\right\|_{2,\nu}^{2}+\partial_{\mathsf{y}}\mathsf{f}(1,\mathsf{y}_{*})\left(3\,\left\|{\phi}\right\|_{2,\nu}^{2}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\log\left(|g_{*}|^{2}\right)\,|\phi|^{2}\,|x|^{-\nu}}\,dx\right)\Bigg)

for any
\phi
such that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\left(1,|x|^{2}\right)g_{*}\,\phi\,|x|^{-\nu}}\,dx=(0,0)
, where

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|g_{*}|^{2}\,|x|^{-\nu}}\,dx=1\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathsf{y}_{*}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|g_{*}|^{2}\,\log\left(|g_{*}|^{2}\right)\,|x|^{-\nu}}\,dx=2\,\log c_{n,d}-\frac{n}{2}\,.

In the symmetry range, we have
\mathscr{K}_{n,\alpha}=\mathscr{K}_{n,\alpha}^{\star}
and

e^{-\frac{2}{n}\,\mathscr{K}_{n,\alpha}}\,\mathsf{f}(1,\mathsf{y}_{*})=e^{-\frac{2}{n}\,\mathscr{K}_{n,\alpha}}\,e^{\frac{2}{n}\,\mathsf{y}_{*}}=\left\|{\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5ptg_{*}}\right\|_{2,\nu}^{2}=\frac{n}{4}\,\alpha^{2}\,.

Since
\partial_{\mathsf{x}}\mathsf{f}(1,\mathsf{y}_{*})=\left(1-\frac{2}{n}-\frac{2}{n}\,\mathsf{y}_{*}\right)\mathsf{f}(1,\mathsf{y}_{*})
and
\partial_{\mathsf{y}}\mathsf{f}(1,\mathsf{y}_{*})=\frac{2}{n}\,\mathsf{f}(1,\mathsf{y}_{*})
, in that case we obtain

\mathsf{F}[\phi]=\left\|{\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\phi}\right\|_{2,\nu}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\,\alpha^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|\phi|^{2}\,|x|^{2+n-d}}\,dx-\frac{n}{4}\,\alpha^{2}\left(1-\frac{2}{n}-\frac{2}{n}\,\mathsf{y}_{*}+\frac{6}{n}+\frac{4}{n}\,\log c_{n,d}\right)\,,

so that, under the condition
\mathscr{K}_{n,\alpha}=\mathscr{K}_{n,\alpha}^{\star}
, we have

\mathsf{F}[\phi]=\left\|{\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\phi}\right\|_{2,\nu}^{2}-\alpha^{2}\left(1+\frac{n}{2}\right)\left\|{\phi}\right\|_{2,\nu}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\,\alpha^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{|\phi|^{2}\,|x|^{2+n-d}}\,dx\,.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
n>1
and
\alpha>0
be two real numbers and consider any integer
d\geq 2
. If
\mathscr{K}_{n,\alpha}=\mathscr{K}_{n,\alpha}^{\star}
, the lowest nonradial eigenmode associated with the quadratic form
\phi\mapsto\mathsf{F}[\phi]
is

\lambda_{1}(\alpha)=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\sqrt{4\,(d-1)+\alpha^{2}\,(n-2)^{2}}-\alpha\,n\right)\,.

(19)

Proof 2.6
.

We use a decomposition into spherical harmonics. Since the lowest eigenvalue of the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
{\mathbb{S}}^{d-1}
is
(d-1)
, we have to solve the eigenvalue problem

-\,\alpha^{2}\left(\varphi^{\prime\prime}+\frac{n-1}{r}\,\varphi^{\prime}\right)+\frac{d-1}{r^{2}}\,\phi+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{4}\,r^{2}\,\varphi=\lambda_{1}\,\varphi

for some positive radial function
r\mapsto\varphi(r)
,
r\in(0,+\infty)
. Elementary computations show that
\varphi(r)=r^{1+\delta}\,e^{-r^{2}/4}
solves the equation with
\delta=\lambda_{1}(\alpha)/\alpha^{2}
and
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
given by (
19
).

On
{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
, it is an elementary computation to check that
\alpha\mapsto\lambda_{1}(\alpha)
takes negative values if and only if

\alpha>\alpha_{\mathrm{FS}}=\sqrt{\frac{d-1}{n-1}}\,.

(20)

Here we find exactly the
Felli & Schneider condition
for symmetry breaking as
in
[
44
,
38
]
.

Proposition 2.7
.

If (
20
) holds, then
\mathscr{K}_{n,\alpha}<\mathscr{K}_{n,\alpha}^{\star}
.

Proof 2.8
.

We argue by contradiction. If
\mathscr{K}_{n,\alpha}=\mathscr{K}_{n,\alpha}^{\star}
, then
g_{\star}
is an optimal function. However, a perturgation of
g_{\star}
by an eigenfunction associated with the eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(\alpha)<0
given by (
19
) proves that
\mathcal{F}
takes negative values, a contradiction with the definition of
\mathscr{K}_{n,\alpha}
.

We learn from Propositions
2.3
and
2.7
that Inequality (
WLS
) admits only non-radial optimal functions if (
20
) holds. The next step is to prove that all optimal functions for (
WLS
) are radially symmetric if
\alpha\leq\alpha_{\mathrm{FS}}
and given up to a multiplication by a constant and a scaling by
f_{\star}
.

2.4
A symmetry result by the
carré du champ
method

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, Part (ii), corresponding to the symmetry case, which is the difficult range. The method relies on the
carré du champ
method and it is inspired from
[
34
, Section 3]
. However, the presence of the logarithmic nonlinearity imposes various non-trivial changes that are detailed below. Altogether, this is a striking application of the nonlinear
carré du champ
method and we give a complete and self-contained proof. Computations which have already appeared in the context of (
CKN
) inequalities are clearly indicated. We consider here the
weighted logarithmic Sobolev inequality
written in the form of (
10b
) with
\sigma=1/2
. If
\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 1.0pt
is the adjoint operator of
\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt
acting on vector-valued functions
{\mathsf{F}}
, with respect to the measure

d\mu_{n}:=r^{n-1}\,dr\,d\omega\,,

then we have

\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 1.0pt{\mathsf{F}}=-\,|x|^{d-n}\,\nabla\cdot(|x|^{n-d}\,{\mathsf{F}})-(\alpha-1)\,r^{1-n}\,\omega\cdot\partial_{r}(r^{n-1}\,{\mathsf{F}})\,.

Moreover we have the useful identity

\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 1.0pt(u\,{\mathsf{F}})=-\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5ptu\cdot{\mathsf{F}}+u\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 1.0pt{\mathsf{F}}

(21)

if
u
and
{\mathsf{F}}
are respectively scalar- and vector-valued functions. Let us define the operator
\mathsf{L}_{\alpha}
by

\mathsf{L}_{\alpha}=-\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 1.0pt\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt=\alpha^{2}\left(\partial^{2}_{r}+\frac{n-1}{r}\,\partial_{r}\right)+\frac{\Delta_{\omega}}{r^{2}}\,,

where
\Delta_{\omega}
denotes the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}
, and consider the Fokker-Planck equation

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=-\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 1.0pt(u\,{\mathsf{F}})

(22)

where the flux
{\mathsf{F}}
and the relative pressure variable
\mathsf{p}
are defined by

{\mathsf{F}}(t,x):=\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\log u+x=\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\left(\log u+\frac{|x|^{2}}{2\,\alpha^{2}}\right)=\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\mathsf{p}\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\mathsf{p}:=\log u-2\,\log g_{\star}^{\alpha,\frac{1}{2}}

and
g_{\star}^{\alpha,1/2}
is the normalized optimal function for (
10b
) with
\sigma=1/2
. Hermite functions are dense and stable under the action of the flow (
22
) so that we can always work on a finite dimensional space generated by some Hermite functions and argue by density. Since there is no difficulty in integrating by parts, we will do it without further justification. This is the first major difference with
[
34
]
where a nonlinear flow is considered and one has to do an approximation procedure on larger and larger balls. To simplify the proof, we divide it in several simple statements. Our main goal is to prove the exponential decay of the
Fisher information
, which goes as follows.

Lemma 2.9
.

Assume that
d\geq 2
,
n>d
and
\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{\rm{FS}}]
. If
u
solves (
22
) with
{\mathsf{F}}=\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\mathsf{p}
, then

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}}\,d\mu_{n}\leq-\,2\,\alpha\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}}\,d\mu_{n}\,.

It is straightforward to check that

\frac{\partial{\mathsf{F}}}{\partial t}+\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\,\Big(u^{-1}\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 1.0pt\left(u\,{\mathsf{F}}\right)\Big)=0

and, as a consequence,

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}}\,d\mu_{n}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}\,|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}}\,d\mu_{n}+2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,{\mathsf{F}}\cdot\frac{\partial{\mathsf{F}}}{\partial t}}\,d\mu_{n}\\
=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 1.0pt(u\,{\mathsf{F}})\,|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}}\,d\mu_{n}-2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,{\mathsf{F}}\cdot\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\,\Big(u^{-1}\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 1.0pt\left(u\,{\mathsf{F}}\right)\Big)}\,d\mu_{n}\,.

A first integration by parts shows that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 1.0pt(u\,{\mathsf{F}})\,|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}}\,d\mu_{n}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,{\mathsf{F}}\,\cdot\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\left(|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}\right)}\,d\mu_{n}\,.

Using (
21
) and
\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\log u={\mathsf{F}}-x
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}}\,d\mu_{n}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,{\mathsf{F}}\,\cdot\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\left(|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}\right)}\,d\mu_{n}-2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,{\mathsf{F}}\cdot\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\left(\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 1.0pt{\mathsf{F}}-{\mathsf{F}}\cdot\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\log u\right)}\,d\mu_{n}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,{\mathsf{F}}\,\cdot\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\left(|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}\right)}\,d\mu_{n}-2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,{\mathsf{F}}\cdot\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\left(\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 1.0pt{\mathsf{F}}-|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}+{\mathsf{F}}\cdot x\right)}\,d\mu_{n}

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt=

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,{\mathsf{F}}\,\cdot\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\left(|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}\right)}\,d\mu_{n}-2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,{\mathsf{F}}\cdot\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt({\mathsf{F}}\cdot x)}\,d\mu_{n}-2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,{\mathsf{F}}\cdot\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\left(\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 1.0pt{\mathsf{F}}\right)}\,d\mu_{n}\,.

Using
u\,{\mathsf{F}}=\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5ptu+x\,u
and integrating by parts, we obtain

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,{\mathsf{F}}\,\cdot\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\left(|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}\right)}\,d\mu_{n}=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,\mathsf{L}_{\alpha}\left(|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}\right)}\,d\mu_{n}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,x\,\cdot\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\left(|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}\right)}\,d\mu_{n}\,.

Hence

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}}\,d\mu_{n}=-\,2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,\mathsf{K}[\mathsf{F}]}\,d\mu_{n}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,x\,\cdot\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\left(|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}\right)}\,d\mu_{n}-2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,{\mathsf{F}}\cdot\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt({\mathsf{F}}\cdot x)}\,d\mu_{n}-\frac{2}{n}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u\,(\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 1.0pt{\mathsf{F}})^{2}}\,d\mu_{n}

with

\mathsf{K}[\mathsf{F}]:=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathsf{L}_{\alpha}\left(|{\mathsf{F}}|^{2}\right)+{\mathsf{F}}\cdot\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\left(\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 1.0pt{\mathsf{F}}\right)-\frac{1}{n}\,(\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}^{*}\kern 1.0pt{\mathsf{F}})^{2}\,.

(23)

We recall that
{\mathsf{F}}=\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\mathsf{p}
so that

\mathsf{K}[\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\mathsf{p}]=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathsf{L}_{\alpha}\,|\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\mathsf{p}|^{2}-\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\mathsf{p}\cdot\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\mathsf{L}_{\alpha}\mathsf{p}-\frac{1}{n}\,(\mathsf{L}_{\alpha}\mathsf{p})^{2}\,.

Let us state a result inspired by
[
35
, Lemma 5.1]
,
[
38
, Lemma 4.2 and Lemma 4.3]
.

Lemma 2.10
.

With the above notations, we have the two following estimates:

(i)

Pointwise estimate:

\mathsf{K}[\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\mathsf{p}]=\alpha^{4}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\left|\mathsf{p}^{\prime\prime}-\frac{\mathsf{p}^{\prime}}{r}-\frac{\Delta_{\omega}\,\mathsf{p}}{\alpha^{2}\,(n-1)\,r^{2}}\right|^{2}+\frac{2\,\alpha^{2}}{r^{2}}\left|\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}^{\prime}-\frac{\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}}{r}\right|^{2}+\frac{\mathsf{k}[\mathsf{p}]}{r^{4}}

Proof 2.11
.

[

, Lemma 5.1]
and

. See Lemma

in
Appendix A.

3.1
A brief summary of the symmetry properties of some (
CKN
) inequalities

On the space
\mathrm{H}^{1,p}_{\beta,\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
of the functions
f\in\mathrm{L}^{p+1}_{\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, such that
\nabla f\in\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\beta}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

\left\|{f}\right\|_{2p,\gamma}\leq\mathsf{C}_{\beta,\gamma,p}\,\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|_{2,\beta}^{\vartheta}\,\left\|{f}\right\|_{p+1,\gamma}^{1-\vartheta}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,f\in\mathrm{H}^{1,p}_{\beta,\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

(CKN)

with optimal constant
\mathsf{C}_{\beta,\gamma,p}
, and parameters
\beta
,
\gamma
and
p
such that

\gamma-2<\beta<\frac{d-2}{d}\,\gamma\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\gamma\in(-\infty,d)\,,\hskip 10.00002ptp\in\left(1,p_{\star}\right)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002ptp_{\star}:=\frac{d-\gamma}{d-2-\beta}\,.

(28)

The exponent

\vartheta=\frac{(d-\gamma)\,(p-1)}{p\,\big((d+2+\beta-2\,\gamma)-p\,(d-2-\beta)\big)}

is determined by the invariance under scalings. The limitation
p\leq p_{\star}
in (
28
) amounts, for a given
p>1
to a restriction to the admissible set of parameters
(\beta,\gamma)
, namely

\beta\geq d-2-\frac{d-\gamma}{p}\,.

(29)

On the other hand, if

p_{\star}<d/(d-2)
is equivalent to

\beta<\frac{d-2}{d}\,\gamma\,.

The range of admissible parameters
(\beta,\gamma)
is limited by (
28
) to a cone in the quadrant
\beta<d-2
and
\gamma<d
with the additional condition (
29

[

The

versus

issue is central in Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities (

\mathsf{g}(x)=\big(1+|x|^{2+\beta-\gamma}\big)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\,.

(30)

According to
[
44
]
,
[
8
, Theorem 2]
and in
[
38

occurs if and only if
(\beta,\gamma)

\mathsf{C}_{\beta,\gamma,p}=\mathsf{C}_{\beta,\gamma,p}^{\star}

where

where

\displaystyle\zeta:=\frac{\vartheta}{2}+\frac{1-\vartheta}{p+1}-\frac{1}{2\,p}=\frac{(2+\beta-\gamma)\,(p-1)}{2\,p\,\big(d+2+\beta-2\,\gamma\,-p\,(d-2-\beta)\big)}\,,

) vanish in the limit as
p\to 1_{+}
, so that the inequality degenerates into an equality. Let us divide both sides of (
33
) by
(p-1)
and consider the limit. Using the identity

\frac{d}{dq}\log\left\|{f}\right\|_{q,\gamma}=\frac{1}{q^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{|f|^{q}}{\left\|{f}\right\|_{q,\gamma}^{q}}\,\log\left(\frac{|f|^{q}}{\left\|{f}\right\|_{q,\gamma}^{q}}\right)\,|x|^{-\gamma}}\,dx\,,

\lim_{p\to 1_{+}}\vartheta(p)/(p-1)=n/4
where
n
is given by (
1
) and

\limsup_{p\to 1_{+}}\frac{1}{p-1}\,\log\left(\frac{\left\|{f}\right\|_{2p,\gamma}}{\left\|{f}\right\|_{p+1,\gamma}}\right)=\frac{1}{4}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{|f|^{2}}{\left\|{f}\right\|_{2,\gamma}^{2}}\,\log\left(\frac{|f|^{2}}{\left\|{f}\right\|_{2,\gamma}^{2}}\right)\,|x|^{-\gamma}}\,dx\,,

we can pass to the limit as
p\to 1_{+}
. The overall picture is consistent with logarithmic Sobolev inequalities (
WLS
). In the limit as
p\to 1_{+}
, it is straightforward to see that the conditions that define the symmetry range (
7
) in (
CKN
) provide us with the conditions that define the symmetry range in (
WLS
) as stated in Theorem
1.1
. This is also true at the level of the optimal constants in the symmetry range. In fact, these observations provide us with an alternative strategy of proof of Theorem
1.1
based on
[
38
, Theorem 1.1]
using
\Gamma
-convergence methods in the spirit of
[
39
,
41
]
. We do not expand on this as we already have a direct proof but for consistency, we state the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
and assume that
(\beta,\gamma)\neq(0,0)
satisfies (
2
). Then we have

\mathscr{C}_{\beta,\gamma}\leq\mathscr{C}_{\beta,\gamma}^{\star}:=4\,\limsup_{p\to 1_{+}}\frac{\mathsf{C}_{\beta,\gamma,p}^{\star}-1}{p-1}\,.

Proof 3.2
.

In view of (
31
), in the symmetry range for the parameters (
7
), we can directly differentiate the formula

\log\,\mathsf{C}_{\beta,\gamma,p}^{\star}=\zeta(p)\,\log\,\alpha+\log\mathsf{K}^{\star}_{\alpha,n,p}

(34)

where
\vartheta=n\,\zeta(p)
and

\frac{1}{\mathsf{K}^{\star}_{\alpha,n,p}}=\alpha^{n\,\zeta(p)}\left(\frac{4\,n}{b(p)}\,\frac{1}{p-1}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}\,\zeta(p)}\left(\frac{2\,(p+1)}{b(p)}\right)^{\frac{n}{p+1}\,\zeta(p)}\left(\frac{1}{2}\,\sigma_{d}\,\Gamma\big(\tfrac{n}{2}\big)\,\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{2\,p}{p-1}-\frac{n}{2}\big)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{2\,p}{p-1}\big)}\right)^{\zeta(p)}

and
b(p):=n+2-p\,(n-2)
. In particular, note that
b(1)=4
and
b^{\prime}(1)=-\,(n-2)
. By taking the logarithm, we obtain the identity

-\,\log\mathsf{K}^{\star}_{\alpha,n,p}=\zeta(p)\left(n\log\alpha+\frac{n}{p+1}\log\left(\frac{2\,(p+1)}{b(p)}\right)+\log\left(\frac{1}{2}\,\sigma_{d}\,\Gamma\big(\tfrac{n}{2}\big)\right)+f(p)\right)

(35)

where

f(p):=\frac{n}{2}\,\log\left(\frac{4\,n}{b(p)}\,\frac{1}{p-1}\right)+\log\left(\frac{\Gamma\big(\frac{2\,p}{p-1}-\frac{n}{2}\big)}{\Gamma\big(\frac{2\,p}{p-1}\big)}\right).

By using the asymptotic expansion for the
Gamma
function

\lim_{x\to+\infty}\frac{\Gamma(x+\alpha)}{x^{\alpha}\,\Gamma(x)}=1

for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
, one can compute the limits

\lim_{p\to 1_{+}}f(p)=-\,\frac{3}{2}\,n\,\log 2\,.

As a result, we can take the derivative with respect to
p
and evaluate the limit as
p\to 1_{+}
in (
35
) by

-\,\lim_{p\to 1_{+}}\frac{\log\mathsf{K}^{\star}_{\alpha,n,p}}{p-1}=\frac{1}{4}\,\log\left(\frac{1}{2}\,\sigma_{d}\,\alpha^{n}\,\left(\tfrac{n\,e}{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\Gamma\left(\tfrac{n}{2}\right)\right)

using the fact that
\zeta(p)=\alpha\,\frac{p-1}{p\,b(p)}
is such that
\zeta(1)=\lim_{p\to 1_{+}}\zeta(p)=0
and
\zeta^{\prime}(1)=1/4
. With
n
and
\alpha
given in terms of
\beta
and
\gamma
respectively by (
1
) and (
4
), we deduce from (
34
) that
\mathscr{C}_{\beta,\gamma}^{\star}=-\,\log\big(\frac{1}{2}\,\sigma_{d}\,\alpha^{n-1}\,\left(\tfrac{n\,e}{2}\right)^{n/2}\,\Gamma\big(\tfrac{n}{2}\big)\big)
.

4
Some consequences for weighted diffusion flows

4.1
Self-similar solutions, intermediate asymptotics and entropy decay rates

Let us consider the self-similar change of variables

u(t,x)=R(t)^{\gamma-d}\,v\left(\log R(t),\frac{x}{R(t)}\right)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{with}\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{dR}{dt}=R^{\gamma-\beta-1}=R^{1-2\,\alpha}

which transforms (
15
) into the
weighted Fokker-Planck equation

\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}=|x|^{\gamma}\,\nabla\cdot\left(|x|^{-\beta}\,\nabla v+x\,|x|^{-\gamma}\,v\right)\,.

(36)

A simple stationary solution is given by
v_{\star}(x)=c_{n,d}\,(2/\alpha)^{n/4}\,\exp\big(-\frac{1}{2\,\alpha}\,|x|^{2\,\alpha}\big)=g_{\star}^{\alpha,\alpha}(x)
with the notations of Section
1
. With
R(0)=R_{0}\geq 0
, we find that

R(t)=\left(R_{0}^{2\alpha}+2\,\alpha\,t\right)^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\geq 0\,,

which shows that
u_{\star}(t,x)=R(t)^{\gamma-d}\,f_{\star}\big(\log R(t),x/R(t)\big)
is simply the Green function associated to (
15
) if we choose
R_{0}=0
. Another interesting choice of
R_{0}
is
R_{0}=1
so that the initial datum for (
36
) is the same as for (
15
). If
v
solves (
36
), then the function
w=v/v_{\star}
solves the
weighted Ornstein-Uhlenbeck equation

\frac{\partial w}{\partial t}=\frac{|x|^{\gamma}}{v_{\star}(x)}\,\nabla\cdot\left(|x|^{-\beta}\,v_{\star}\,\nabla w\right)\,.

(37)

Proposition 4.1
.

In the symmetry range, with
d\mu_{\alpha}=|x|\,^{-\gamma}\,v_{\star}(x)\,dx
defined as in Section
1
, any solution of (
37
) with nonnegative initial datum
w_{0}
such that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}w_{0}\,d\mu_{\alpha}=1
decays according to

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}w(t,\cdot)\,\log w(t,\cdot)\,d\mu_{\alpha}\leq\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}w_{0}\,\log w_{0}\,d\mu_{\alpha}\right)e^{-\,4\,\alpha\,t}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\geq 0\,.

Proof 4.2
.

We compute
\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}w(t,\cdot)\,\log w(t,\cdot)\,d\mu_{\alpha}
and apply (
11b
) to
u=\sqrt{w}
.

By the Csiszár-Kullback-Pinsker inequality

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|w-1|\,d\mu_{\alpha}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\sqrt{\textstyle{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}w\,\log w\,d\mu_{\alpha}}}

for any nonnegative function
w
such that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}w\,d\mu_{\alpha}=1
. By undoing the above changes of variables with
R_{0}=1
, we can write the following
intermediate asymptotics
result.

Corollary 4.3
.

In the symmetry range, any solution of (
15
) with nonnegative initial datum
u_{0}
such that
\left\|{u_{0}}\right\|_{1,\gamma}=1
obeys to

\left\|{u(t,\cdot)-u_{\star}(t,\cdot)}\right\|_{1,\gamma}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,\sqrt{\textstyle{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{u_{0}\,\log(u_{0}/v_{\star})\,|x|^{-\gamma}}\,dx}}\,\left(1+2\,\alpha\,t\right)^{-1}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\geq 0\,.

The above results are consistent with the flow

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=|x|^{\gamma}\,\nabla\cdot\left(|x|^{-\beta}\,\nabla u^{m}\right)

with
m<1
, which is adapted to (
CKN
) inequalities with
p=1/(2\,m-1)
. Notice however that parabolic computations as in Section
2.4
are, so far, only formal if
m<1
: see
[
8
,
35
,
38
,
34
]
for details.

4.2
Hyper-contractivity estimates

Let us measure the gain of regularity by the weighted heat flow (
15
). the following result generalizes
[
55
,
43
,
48
]
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
r>q>1
and assume that
\beta
and
\gamma
satisfy (
2
). If
u
is a solution of (
15
) with initial datum
u_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\gamma}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, then

\left\|{u(t,\cdot)}\right\|_{r,\gamma}\leq\mathscr{H}^{\,q,r}_{\beta,\gamma}\,\left\|{u_{0}}\right\|_{q,\gamma}\,t^{-\frac{n}{2}\,\frac{r-q}{q\,r}}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\geq 0

(38)

where
\mathscr{H}^{\,q,r}_{\beta,\gamma}:=t_{\star}^{\frac{n}{2}\,\frac{r-q}{q\,r}}
and
t_{\star}:=\tfrac{n}{8}\,e^{\frac{2}{n}\,\mathscr{C}_{\beta,\gamma}-1}\log\big(\tfrac{r-1}{q-1}\big)
.

Proof 4.5
.

For some exponent
p
depending smoothly on
s
with
p^{\prime}(s)>0
, let us consider the function

h(s):=\left\|{u(s,\cdot)}\right\|_{p(s),\gamma}\,.

By a standard computation which goes back to
[
48
]
we have

\frac{h^{\prime}}{h}=\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\frac{|u|^{p}}{h^{p}}\,\log\left(\frac{|u|^{p}}{h^{p}}\right)|x|^{-\gamma}}\,dx-\frac{1}{h^{p}}\,\frac{4\,(p-1)}{p^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\left|\nabla|u|^{p/2}\right|^{2}\,|x|^{-\beta}}\,dx\leq\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{2}}\left(\mathscr{C}_{\beta,\gamma}-\frac{n}{2}\,\log\left(\frac{2\,e}{n\,\sigma}\right)\right)

(39)

where the inequality holds as a consequence of (
10a
) applied to
|u|^{p/2}
with

p^{\prime}=4\,\sigma\,(p-1)\,.

(40)

With the choice
\sigma=\sigma_{\beta,\gamma}
where
\sigma_{\beta,\gamma}:=\frac{2}{n}\,e^{1-\frac{2}{n}\,\mathscr{C}_{\beta,\gamma}}
, so that
h^{\prime}\leq 0
, and
p(0)=q
, (
39
) is solved by

p(s)=1+(q-1)\,e^{4\,\sigma_{\beta,\gamma}\,s}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,s\geq 0\,.

(41)

The condition
p(t_{\star})=r
determines

t_{\star}=\frac{1}{4\,\sigma_{\beta,\gamma}}\,\log\left(\frac{r-1}{q-1}\right)

such that

\left\|{u(t_{\star},\cdot)}\right\|_{r,\gamma}=\left\|{u_{0}}\right\|_{q,\gamma}\,.

If
t\neq t_{\star}
, we use (
39
) again for
\sigma>0
such that
r=1+(q-1)\,e^{4\,\sigma\,t}
,
i.e.
,

t=\frac{1}{4\,\sigma}\,\log\left(\frac{r-1}{q-1}\right)\,,

(42)

and obtain

\frac{h^{\prime}}{h}\leq\frac{p^{\prime}}{p^{2}}\,\frac{n}{2}\,\log\left(\frac{\sigma}{\sigma_{\beta,\gamma}}\right)\,,

that is, after integration with respect to
s\in[0,t]
,

\left\|{u(t,\cdot)}\right\|_{r,\gamma}=\left\|{u_{0}}\right\|_{q,\gamma}\,\big(t_{\star}^{-1}\,t\big)^{-\frac{n}{2}\,\frac{r-q}{r\,q}}\hskip 10.00002pt\forall\,t\geq 0\,.

Notice that the choice of
t_{\star}
in (
38
) is optimal because (
38
) with
\sigma=\sigma_{\beta,\gamma}
means that
h(s)\leq h(0)
for any
s>0
, hence
h^{\prime}(0)\leq 0
so that the optimal value of
\sigma_{\beta,\gamma}
in (
41
) determines the optimal constant in (
10a
). Slightly more subtle is the fact that
\mathscr{H}^{\,q,r}_{\beta,\gamma}
is also the optimal constant. Using (
39
) with the condition
p(t)=r
, we can write that

\left\|{u(t,\cdot)}\right\|_{r,\gamma}=h(t)\leq h(0)\,\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\frac{p^{\prime}(s)}{p^{2}(s)}\left(\mathscr{C}_{\beta,\gamma}-\frac{n}{2}\,\log\left(\frac{2\,e}{n\,\sigma}\right)\right)ds\right)

where
h(0)=\left\|{u_{0}}\right\|_{q,\gamma}
and
\sigma
can be taken
s
-dependent. With the change of variables
s\mapsto z
,
z=Z(s)=1/p(s)
, we can compute

\int_{0}^{t}\frac{p^{\prime}(s)}{p^{2}(s)}\left(\mathscr{C}_{\beta,\gamma}-\frac{n}{2}\,\log\left(\frac{2\,e}{n\,\sigma}\right)\right)ds=\left(\mathscr{C}_{\beta,\gamma}-\frac{n}{2}\,\log\left(\frac{2\,e}{n}\right)\right)\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{r}\right)-\frac{n}{2}\int_{1/r}^{1/q}\log\sigma(z)\,dz

where, up to a slight abuse of notations, we consider
\sigma
as a function of
z
and deduce from (
40
) that

\sigma(z)=4\,\frac{p^{2}}{p^{\prime}}\,\frac{p-1}{p^{2}}=4\,\frac{z\,(z-1)}{(Z^{\prime}\circ Z^{-1})(z)}\,,

although we do not make use of this identity. Indeed, an infinitesimal variation of
\int_{1/r}^{1/q}\log\sigma(z)\,dz
directly shows that the optimal case is achieved by a constant function
z\mapsto\sigma(z)
corresponding to the choice (
42
). Hence
\mathscr{H}^{\,q,r}_{\beta,\gamma}
as defined in Proposition
4.4
is optimal.

Appendix A.

A purely algebraic computation

For completeness, let us give a proof of Lemma
2.10
, (i). We recall that

\mathsf{K}[\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\mathsf{p}]=\frac{1}{2}\,\mathsf{L}_{\alpha}\,|\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\mathsf{p}|^{2}-\,\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\mathsf{p}\cdot\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\mathsf{L}_{\alpha}\mathsf{p}-\frac{1}{n}\,(\mathsf{L}_{\alpha}\mathsf{p})^{2}

and

\mathsf{k}[\mathsf{p}]=\frac{1}{2}\,\Delta_{\omega}\,|\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}|^{2}-\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}\cdot\nabla_{\omega}\Delta_{\omega}\,\mathsf{p}-\frac{1}{n-1}\,(\Delta_{\omega}\,\mathsf{p})^{2}-(n-2)\,\alpha^{2}\,|\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}|^{2}\,.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
d\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\in{\mathbb{R}}
such that
n>d\geq 2
, and consider a function
\mathsf{p}\in C^{3}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}\setminus\{0\})
. Then,

\mathsf{K}[\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\mathsf{p}]=\alpha^{4}\left(1-\frac{1}{n}\right)\left|\mathsf{p}^{\prime\prime}-\frac{\mathsf{p}^{\prime}}{\mathsf{r}}-\frac{\Delta_{\omega}\,\mathsf{p}}{\alpha^{2}\,(n-1)\,\mathsf{r}^{2}}\right|^{2}+\frac{2\,\alpha^{2}}{\mathsf{r}^{2}}\left|\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}^{\prime}-\frac{\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}}{\mathsf{r}}\right|^{2}+\frac{\mathsf{k}[\mathsf{p}]}{\mathsf{r}^{4}}\,.

This result is a purely algebraic computation which involves no integration by parts and in which, for (
CKN
), neither
p
nor
m
plays any role, so that it perfectly makes sense to consider the limit case
m=1
and
p=1
. We recall that the original result of
[
35
, Lemma 5.1]
was given in the framework of
m<1
.

Proof A.2
.

By definition of
\mathsf{K}[\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\mathsf{p}]
, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}[\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\mathsf{p}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\left[\alpha^{2}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime 2}+\frac{|\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}|^{2}}{\mathsf{r}^{2}}\right)^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\frac{(n-1)}{\mathsf{r}}\left[\alpha^{2}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime 2}+\frac{|\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}|^{2}}{\mathsf{r}^{2}}\right)^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2\,\mathsf{r}^{2}}\,\Delta_{\omega}\left[\alpha^{2}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime 2}+\frac{|\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}|^{2}}{\mathsf{r}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle-\,\alpha^{2}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime}\left(\alpha^{2}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime\prime}+\alpha^{2}\,\frac{(n-1)}{\mathsf{r}}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime}+\frac{\Delta_{\omega}\,\mathsf{p}}{\mathsf{r}^{2}}\right)^{\prime}-\frac{1}{\mathsf{r}^{2}}\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}\cdot\nabla_{\omega}\left(\alpha^{2}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime\prime}+\alpha^{2}\,\frac{(n-1)}{\mathsf{r}}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime}+\frac{\Delta_{\omega}\,\mathsf{p}}{\mathsf{r}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle-\,\frac{1}{n}\left(\alpha^{2}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime\prime}+\alpha^{2}\,\frac{(n-1)}{\mathsf{r}}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime}+\frac{\Delta_{\omega}\,\mathsf{p}}{\mathsf{r}^{2}}\right)^{2}\,,

which can be expanded as

\displaystyle\mathsf{K}[\mathsf{D}_{\alpha}\kern 0.5pt\mathsf{p}]\kern-6.0pt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\kern-6.0pt\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2}\left[2\,\alpha^{2}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime\prime 2}+2\,\alpha^{2}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime\prime\prime}+2\,\frac{|\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}^{\prime}|^{2}+\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}\cdot\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}^{\prime\prime}}{\mathsf{r}^{2}}-8\,\frac{\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}\cdot\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}^{\prime}}{\mathsf{r}^{3}}+6\,\frac{|\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}|^{2}}{\mathsf{r}^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\kern-6.0pt+\,\alpha^{2}\,\frac{(n-1)}{\mathsf{r}}\left[\alpha^{2}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}\cdot\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}^{\prime}}{\mathsf{r}^{2}}-\frac{|\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}|^{2}}{\mathsf{r}^{3}}\right)+\frac{1}{\mathsf{r}^{2}}\left[\alpha^{2}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime}\Delta_{\omega}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime}+\alpha^{2}\,|\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{\Delta_{\omega}\,|\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}|^{2}}{2\,\mathsf{r}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle-\,\alpha^{2}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime}\left(\alpha^{2}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime\prime\prime}+\alpha^{2}\,\frac{(n-1)}{\mathsf{r}}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime\prime}-\,\alpha^{2}\,\frac{(n-1)}{\mathsf{r}^{2}}\mathsf{p}^{\prime}-2\,\frac{\Delta_{\omega}\,\mathsf{p}}{\mathsf{r}^{3}}+\frac{\Delta_{\omega}\,\mathsf{p}^{\prime}}{\mathsf{r}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\kern-6.0pt\hskip 56.9055pt-\frac{1}{\mathsf{r}^{2}}\left(\alpha^{2}\,\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}\cdot\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}^{\prime\prime}+\alpha^{2}\,\frac{(n-1)}{\mathsf{r}}\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}\cdot\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}^{\prime}+\frac{\nabla_{\omega}\mathsf{p}\cdot\nabla_{\omega}\Delta_{\omega}\,\mathsf{p}}{\mathsf{r}^{2}}\right)

First Order System Least Squares Neural Networks
Joost A. A. Opschoor, Philipp C. Petersen, Christoph Schwab

Abstract
We introduce a conceptual framework for numerically solving linear elliptic, parabolic, and hyperbolic PDEs on bounded, polytopal domains in euclidean spaces by deep neural networks. The PDEs are recast as minimization of a least-squares (LSQ for short) residual of an equivalent, well-posed first-order system, over parametric families of deep neural networks. The associated LSQ residual is a) equal or proportional to a weak residual of the PDE, b) additive in terms of contributions from localized subnetworks, indicating locally “out-of-equilibrium” of neural networks with respect to the PDE residual, c) serves as numerical loss function for neural network training, and d) constitutes, even with incomplete training, a computable, (quasi-)optimal numerical error estimator in the context of adaptive LSQ finite element methods. In addition, an adaptive neural network growth strategy is proposed which, assuming exact numerical minimization of the LSQ loss functional, yields sequences of neural networks with realizations that converge rate-optimally to the exact solution of the first order system LSQ formulation.

Neural Networks, FoSLS, Adaptivity, De Rham Complex, Finite Elements

Introduction

Numerous recently proposed algorithms for deep neural network (NN) training
in connection with partial differential equations (PDEs) in physical domains

{\mathrm{D}}

are derived from energies in variational principles
(e.g.
[
19
,
1
,
2
]
)
even for linear, deterministic and well posed PDEs.
Loss functions can be based on classical- (i.e. pointwise),
weak- and variational-formulations of the PDE and corresponding residuals,
see e.g. the overview in
[
36
, Section 2.3]

Importantly, adopting “convenient” and “intuitive” loss functions
may entail implicit unrealistic assumptions on solution regularity, whereas
mathematically correct loss functions may entail the necessity
of numerical evaluation of “inconvenient”, nonlocal Sobolev norms of
NN residuals in the PDE of interest.
Here, we indicate advantages of NN approximations
based on a first order system least squares (FoSLS) reformulation
of the underlying PDE.
As we show, FoSLS variational formulations
of PDEs lead to
L^{2}({\mathrm{D}})
-NN residuals which, for NN emulations
of spaces in the DeRham complex (
1.1
), (
1.2
) below,
are easily numerically evaluated
exactly (up to unavoidable data oscillation errors):
based on
[
31
]
,
we present compatible, structure preserving NN emulations
of FoSLS methods for a wide range of boundary- and initial-boundary
value problems of PDEs.

1.1.3
First Order System Least Squares for NN Approximation

Early results exploring a first order least squares approach
for neural network approximation include

[
11
,
32
]
,
where in numerical experiments a discrete loss function was minimized.
This loss function was obtained from the continuous loss function
by quadrature and Monte Carlo sampling,
respectively.
Recently, in
[
7
, Section 4]
it was shown that for
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
considering a discretization of the integrals in the loss function
by Monte Carlo sampling in
N
points,
it suffices to approximately minimize the discrete loss function
up to a tolerance that decreases to
0
for
N\to\infty
.
It is then shown that under these conditions,
the approximate solutions of the discrete problems
converge to the true solution of the PDE.

1.2
Contributions

We propose FoSLS NNs as NN architectures for the numerical
approximation of linear elliptic, parabolic and hyperbolic PDEs.
They are based on the variational least squares (LSQ) formulation of the
PDE of interest, which is
variationally correct
under minimal
regularity of the physical fields.
I.e., it is quadratic, strictly convex in the unknown fields, and
admits unique solutions which are known to coincide with the
physically correct solution of the PDE of interest.
To this end, we propose a design of
FoSLS NNs which are
structure-preserving
,
i.e.,
their realizations as functions on a contractible spatial domain
{\mathrm{D}}

or on a spatiotemporal domain

{\mathrm{D}}=(0,T)\times\mathrm{G}

must be de Rham compatible.
These properties result, usually,
from an
equivalent reformulation of variational principles
in terms of LSQ functionals for first order systems of PDEs.

Adopting De Rham compatible Finite Element spaces
such as those in (
1.2
),
[
31
]

as feature spaces in FoSLS NNs
results in the
transfer principle
, which allows to leverage
known mathematical results on
LSQ FE methods
for the analysis of FoSLS NN approximations.
In particular,

•

existing LSQ-FE approximation rate bounds
transfer

to expression rate bounds for the proposed FoSLS-based NNs.

•

homogeneous essential boundary conditions

can be realized exactly,
which is a severe issue in other approaches,
see
[
11
,
32
,
7
]

and the references there.

•

physically correct, computable loss functions
:
as is well-known and as we show,
physically correct and numerically accessible
LSQ functionals are available
for a wide range of PDE boundary- and initial-boundary value problems.
I.e.,
there exist unique minimizers in function spaces of physical relevance
of the LSQ functionals,
resulting in
computable numerical loss functions
which are based on
L^{2}({\mathrm{D}})
-norms of the FoSLS NN residuals
.

•

deterministic, high-order numerical quadratures
:
being essentially emulations of piecewise polynomial functions on
regular partitions of the physical domain
{\mathrm{D}}
,
the
L^{2}({\mathrm{D}})
-based LSQ residual is numerically accessible via
(high-order) standard numerical integration.

•

computable expression errors in physically relevant norms
:
computable
L^{2}({\mathrm{D}})
-based loss functions based on FoSLS formulations
are known to be equivalent to
NN expression errors in physically relevant norms,
thereby allowing reliable and efficient numerical control of NN approximation errors
(subject to the assumption of polynomial source terms and boundary data, to
discard data-oscillation error terms).

•

localized loss functions
:
additivity of the
Lebesgue integral over triangulations
{\cal T}
of
{\mathrm{D}}
,
combined with the locality of differential operators
implies

loss functions composed additively of contributions from localized subnetworks
,
corresponding to subdomains of the physical domain
{\mathrm{D}}
.

•

convergence
: standard (bisection) refinements of the partitions
{\cal T}

produces sequences of NNs of increasing width
which are dense in the function spaces underlying
the LSQ form of the PDEs of interest.

•

provably rate-optimal, adaptive NN growth strategies
:
the use of loss functions based on the LSQ functional
as computable error estimator, with local contributions
driving adaptive mesh-refining strategies is well established

[
15
,
16
,
14
,
10
]

in the adaptive LSQ finite element method (A-LSQFEM for short).
Combined with the FoSLS NN emulations developed in Section
4
,
this is used in Section
5
to infer

practical algorithms for adaptive NN growth which produce
sequences of convergent NN approximations of minimizers of LSQ functionals

[
23
]
.

It follows that ALSQFEM-derived, adaptive NN growth strategies
can result in rate-optimally convergent sequences of NNs,
with a posteriori (i.e. upon completion of NN training)
numerically verifiable guarantees on error reduction.

1.3
Further Comparison with “Physics-Informed” DL Approaches

We discuss in more detail
some benefits of the considered
FoSLS-based approach for learning PDE solutions as compared to other
formulations, notably the so-called ‘PiNN’ methodology.
See, e.g.,
[
36
]
and references there.

Each FoSLS NN is of feedforward-type and
realizes a de Rham-compatible finite element function
which is specified by the weights and biases of the NN.
We may consider some of these fixed,
optimizing only the remaining weights and biases.
As usual, these are collected
in the vector
\theta
of trainable NN parameters.
In the FoSLS NNs based on
[
31
]
,
which exactly emulate de Rham compatible finite elements
from Section
1.1.1
,
weights and biases in the hidden layers encode
the mesh connectivity and the node positions.
The finite element shape functions are a basis of the FoSLS NN feature space,
and the NN weights in the output layer
correspond to the finite element degrees of freedom.
By
\Theta
we denote the set of all admissible values of
\theta
,
which give FoSLS NNs on regular,
shape regular,
simplicial triangulations of the polytope
{\mathrm{D}}
.
By
U_{\theta}
we will denote
the function realized by the FoSLS NN with weights and biases
\theta\in\Theta
.

We partition

\theta=(\theta_{\mathrm{hid}},\theta_{\mathrm{out}})\in\Theta_{\mathrm{hid}}\times\Theta_{\mathrm{out}}=\Theta
,
where

\theta_{\mathrm{out}}
denotes the trainable weights in the output layer, and

\theta_{\mathrm{hid}}
the remaining trainable weights associated with the hidden layers
(recall that we consider only feedforward NNs without skip connections).
The standard FoSLS finite element methodology corresponds to
only optimizing the output layer for given,
fixed choices of admissible
\theta_{\mathrm{hid}}\in\Theta_{\mathrm{hid}}
.
Then,
\Theta_{\mathrm{out}}=\mathbb{R}^{N}
with

N
denoting the dimension of the feature space, i.e.,
the number of weights in the output layer.
The linear dependence of the FoSLS residual on
\theta_{\mathrm{out}}

means that

at fixed feature space parameters
\theta_{\mathrm{hid}}
,
an optimizer
\theta^{*}_{\mathrm{out}}\in\Theta_{\mathrm{out}}

can be found by numerical solution of a linear LSQ problem
,
and
nonlinear optimization is not necessary
to determine

at given, fixed
\theta_{\mathrm{hid}}
.

Let
{\mathrm{D}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\in\mathbb{N}

be a bounded, contractible, polytopal Lipschitz domain.
We explain the FoSLS NN methodology
with the (textbook) example of the Poisson equation
(in the main body of this paper, we consider a much more general abstract setting,

In terms of the Hilbertian Sobolev space

,
it can be written as:

\displaystyle{\bm{L}}U:={\bm{L}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u\\
{\bm{\sigma}}\end{array}\right):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-{\rm div}{\bm{\sigma}}\\
\nabla u-{\bm{\sigma}}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}f\\
{\bm{0}}\end{array}\right)=:F\in L^{2}({\mathrm{D}})^{d+1}=:\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{D}})\;.

The unique solution

We use

The error of the FoSLS NN approximation

By (

.

For any given

,

The FOSLS-loss additionally satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{FOSLS}}(\theta)=\|F-{\bm{L}}U_{\theta}\|_{\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{D}})}\simeq\|U-U_{\theta}\|_{\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}})}=\mathcal{E}(\theta),

.

.

As usual, the incurred error can be decomposed into

We define the generalization error in terms of the FoSLS loss, i.e.

.
Given a set of interpolation points

,
we denote by

.
Let
\theta^{*}\in\Theta
be such that
\mathcal{E}_{T}(\theta^{*},S)
is small.
We recall the error decomposition from e.g.
[
36
, Equation (3.11)]
.
For all
\hat{\theta}\in\Theta
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{G}(\theta^{*})\leq\mathcal{E}_{G}(\hat{\theta})+2\sup_{\theta\in\Theta}|\mathcal{E}_{G}(\theta)-\mathcal{E}_{T}(\theta,S)|+|\mathcal{E}_{T}(\theta^{*},S)-\mathcal{E}_{T}(\hat{\theta},S)|.

The first term on the right-hand side is an upper bound for

\inf_{\theta\in\Theta}\mathcal{E}_{G}(\theta)
, which is the
approximation error
.
For the NNs in the present note, it can be bound with standard Finite-Element based
error bounds.
The second term is called
generalization gap

and measures to which extent smallness of the discrete loss implies
smallness of the continuous loss.
The third term is the
approximation error
,
i.e. the quality of the algorithm by which
the approximate minimizer
\theta^{*}
of
\mathcal{E}_{T}(\cdot,S)

has been determined.

In Section
4.3
,
we show that FoSLS NNs exactly emulate finite element spaces
appearing in the discrete exact sequence (
1.2
).
Therefore, the generalization error can be estimated using existing finite element theory.

The functions emulated by the FoSLS NNs introduced in Section
4.3

exactly realize piecewise polynomial functions
on a regular, simplicial partition
{\cal T}
of the physical domain
{\mathrm{D}}
.
Note that these piecewise polynomial functions need not be continuous.
Considering the case that

F
is also a piecewise polynomial function on
{\cal T}
,
on each element
K\in{\cal T}
,
the residual
{\bm{L}}u_{\theta}-F
restricted to
K

is a polynomial of finite, known degree.
We can define quadrature points that integrate it exactly.
Collecting these quadrature points in a set
S
,
we obtain
\mathcal{E}_{T}(\theta,S)=\mathcal{E}_{G}(\theta)
for all
\theta\in\Theta
.
I.e., for
collocation points based on mesh-adapted quadratures,
there is no generalization gap
provided
F
is a piecewise polynomial function
on
{\cal T}
.

Concerning the approximation error,
if
\theta_{\mathrm{hid}}\in\Theta_{\mathrm{hid}}
is fixed,
then the squared loss function
\mathcal{E}_{T}(\theta,S)^{2}

depends quadratically on

\theta_{\mathrm{out}}(\theta_{\mathrm{hid}})\in\Theta_{\mathrm{out}}=\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

The optimal
\theta^{*}_{\mathrm{out}}(\theta_{\mathrm{hid}})

can be computed via a linear LSQ problem with
standard numerical linear algebra such as PCCG or thin QR decompositions.
The LSQ solution realizes a
\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}})
best approximation of the solution
U

in the NN feature space corresponding to
\theta_{\mathrm{hid}}
.

In Section

, we present an abstract setting of

of

.
FoSLS formulations are valid
under low regularity assumptions on the domain

which

polytopal with Lipschitz boundary

.
All FoSLS-formulations involve an

-norm
of the residual of the PDE with respect to arguments
in suitable function spaces,
forming, for second order elliptic systems on contractible
{\mathrm{D}}
,

In Section
2.2
,
we illustrate this by (textbook) examples of LSQ formulations

,

Optimal control problems also admit LSQ formulations,
as we will see in Section

.
Other examples covered by the present, unified approach
include (but are not limited to)

[

, Section 3.5]

and also
[

, Section 3.2]

2.1
Abstract Setting

We assume throughout a physical
domain
{\mathrm{D}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
d\geq 2

which satisfies

(D)

Domain assumptions:

{\mathrm{D}}
is a bounded, contractible, polytopal Lipschitz
2
2

2

Cf. e.g.
[
21
, Definition 3.2]
.

domain.

For

F\in\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{D}}):=L^{2}({\mathrm{D}}_{1})\times\cdots\times L^{2}({\mathrm{D}}_{n})

for some
n\geq 1
and polytopal domains
{\mathrm{D}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathrm{D}}_{n}
satisfying (D),
consider a linear PDE in the generic form

{\bm{L}}U=F\quad\mbox{in}\;\;\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{D}})\;.

(2.1)

Here,

{\bm{L}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}}),\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{D}}))

is an operator defined on some Hilbertian Sobolev space

\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}})

(carrying homogeneous essential boundary conditions for (
2.1
))
satisfying the following assumptions.

(A1)

Well-posedness

{\bm{L}}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}}),\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{D}}))

is boundedly invertible:
there are constants
0<c_{{\bm{L}}}\leq C_{{\bm{L}}}<\infty

such that

\forall v\in\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}}):\quad c_{\bm{L}}\|v\|_{\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}})}\leq\|{\bm{L}}v\|_{{\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{D}})}}\leq C_{\bm{L}}\|v\|_{\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}})}\;.

The inner product corresponding to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{D}})}

will be denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{D}})}
.
Thus, the least squares bilinear form

\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}\to\mathbb{R}:(v,w)\mapsto({\bm{L}}v,{\bm{L}}w)_{\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{D}})}

is assumed coercive with coercivity constant
c_{\bm{L}}^{2}
.

(A2)

Existence
of solutions:
the data satisfies
F\in{\rm range}({\bm{L}})\subset\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{D}})
.

From (A2), for given
F
, there exists a solution
U\in\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}})
of (
2.1
).
With (A1), we find the
error-residual relation
:
for every
v\in\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}})
, there holds

c_{\bm{L}}\|U-v\|_{\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}})}\leq\|F-{\bm{L}}v\|_{\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{D}})}\leq C_{\bm{L}}\|U-v\|_{\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}})}\;.

(2.2)

This immediately implies uniqueness of the solution
U\in\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}})
.
This solution is, in particular, the unique minimizer of the

least squares functional

{\mathrm{L}\mathrm{S}}(\cdot;F)

associated to (
2.1
):

U={\rm arg}\min_{v\in\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}})}{\mathrm{L}\mathrm{S}}(v;F),\quad\mbox{with}\quad{\mathrm{L}\mathrm{S}}(v;F):=\|F-{\bm{L}}v\|_{\mathbb{L}({\mathrm{D}})}^{2}\;.

(2.3)

We spell out some further structural hypotheses for
\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}})
,
{\bm{L}}

and the norm
\|\circ\|_{\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}})}
.
For many PDEs of interest,
{\bm{L}}
is an integer order differential operator,
i.e. in particular a local operator, and
\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}})
is an integer order
Sobolev space in
{\mathrm{D}}
.
The corresponding (Hilbertian) norm
\|\circ\|_{\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}})}

then satisfies

(A3)

\|\circ\|_{\mathrm{D}}
Norm additivity
:
for any disjoint, measurable subsets

\omega_{1},\omega_{2}\subset{\mathrm{D}}

it holds that

\forall v\in\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}}):\quad\|v\|_{\mathbb{V}(\omega_{1}\cup\omega_{2})}^{2}=\|v\|^{2}_{\mathbb{V}(\omega_{1})}+\|v\|^{2}_{\mathbb{V}(\omega_{2})}\;\quad\|{\bm{L}}v\|_{\mathbb{L}(\omega_{1}\cup\omega_{2})}^{2}=\|{\bm{L}}v\|_{\mathbb{L}(\omega_{1})}^{2}+\|{\bm{L}}v\|_{\mathbb{L}(\omega_{2})}^{2}\;.

(A4)

Norm continuity
:
for measurable subdomains
\omega\subset{\mathrm{D}}

holds

\forall v\in\mathbb{V}({\mathrm{D}}):\quad\|v\|_{\mathbb{V}(\omega)}\to 0\;\;\mbox{as}\;\;|\omega|\to 0\;.

Here,
|\omega|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of the domain
\omega\subset{\mathrm{D}}
.

In the LSQ formulation (
2.3
),
for given data
F
,
the quadratic (for a linear
{\bm{L}}
) functional
v\mapsto{\mathrm{L}\mathrm{S}}(v;F)

defined in (
2.3
) takes the role of loss-functional in NN training.
Due to the additivity (A3) and the locality of
{\bm{L}}
, the numerical
evaluation of
{\mathrm{L}\mathrm{S}}(v;F)
amounts to a sum of local integrals
over a regular, simplicial triangulation
{\cal T}
of
{\mathrm{D}}
.

In (

with the unknown

,

)

\displaystyle{\bm{L}}U:={\bm{L}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u\\
{\bm{\sigma}}\end{array}\right):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}-{\rm div}{\bm{\sigma}}-k^{2}u\\

2.2.4
Space-Time LSQ for Advection-Reaction-Diffusion

In a bounded polytopal domain

\mathrm{G}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

satisfying condition (D),
with boundary

\Gamma=\partial\mathrm{G}

where homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions are imposed,
and
a finite time interval
I=(0,T)
,
we consider the
parabolic initial boundary value problem
:
given data

f,{\bm{a}},{\bm{b}},c,u_{0}
,
find

u:I\times\mathrm{G}\to\mathbb{R}

such that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\partial_{t}u-\divv_{x}({\bm{a}}\nabla_{x}u)+{\bm{b}}\cdot\nabla_{x}u+cu&=&f\quad\mbox{in}\quad I\times\mathrm{G},\\
u&=&0\quad\mbox{on}\quad I\times\Gamma,\\
u(0,\cdot)&=&u_{0}\quad\mbox{in}\quad\mathrm{G}.\end{array}

(2.15)

Here,
we have
{\mathrm{D}}:=I\times\mathrm{G}
,

\divv_{x}
and
\nabla_{x}
denote differential operators on
\Omega

and the diffusion coefficient

{\bm{a}}={\bm{a}}^{\top}\in L^{\infty}({\mathrm{D}})^{d\times d}

is a given, symmetric matrix function which is assumed
to be uniformly positive definite,

0\leq c\in L^{\infty}({\mathrm{D}})
is a reaction coefficient,
and

{\bm{b}}\in L^{\infty}({\mathrm{D}})^{d}
an advection field
such that for all
u,v\in H^{1}_{0}(\mathrm{G})

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathrm{G}}{\bm{a}}(t,\cdot)\nabla_{x}u\cdot\nabla_{x}v+v{\bm{b}}(t,\cdot)\cdot\nabla_{x}u+c(t,\cdot)uv\right|\leq

\displaystyle\,C\|u\|_{H^{1}(\mathrm{G})}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\mathrm{G})},

(2.16a)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathrm{G}}{\bm{a}}(t,\cdot)\nabla_{x}v\cdot\nabla_{x}v+v{\bm{b}}(t,\cdot)\cdot\nabla_{x}v+c(t,\cdot)v^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\,C^{\prime}\|v\|_{H^{1}(\mathrm{G})}^{2}-C^{\prime\prime}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathrm{G})}^{2},

(2.16b)

for all
t\in I
, with some
constants
C,C^{\prime}>0
,
C^{\prime\prime}\geq 0
independent of
t
.
In addition, we consider a right-hand side
f\in L^{2}({\mathrm{D}})

and an initial value
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathrm{G})
.

We introduce the Hilbert-space for the FoSLS space-time formulation:
with

U=(u_{1},{\bm{u}}_{2}),\quad\mbox{where}\quad u_{1}=u,\;{\bm{u}}_{2}=-{\bm{a}}\nabla_{x}u\;,

we obtain from (

) the first order system

{\bm{L}}U:={\bm{L}}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{1}\\
{\bm{u}}_{2}\end{array}\right):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\bm{u}}_{2}+{\bm{a}}\nabla_{x}u_{1}\\
\partial_{t}u_{1}+\divv_{x}{\bm{u}}_{2}+{\bm{b}}\cdot\nabla_{x}u_{1}+cu_{1}\\
u_{1}(0,\cdot)\end{array}\right)=F:=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}{\bm{0}}\\
f\\
u_{0}\end{array}\right)

(2.17)

where

\displaystyle\,+\|u_{1}(0,\cdot)|_{\partial_{0}K}\|_{L^{2}(\partial_{0}K)}^{2}+\|u_{1}(T,\cdot)|_{\partial_{T}K}\|_{L^{2}(\partial_{T}K)}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\|Y\|_{\mathbb{V}_{Y}(K)}^{2},

where we denoted

Y=(u_{1},{\bm{u}}_{2})
.
Similarly,
it holds that

\|\bm{I}_{P}P\|_{\widehat{\mathbb{L}}(K)}\leq\|P\|_{\mathbb{V}_{P}(K)}
.
Thus,
{\bm{A}},{\bm{A}}^{*},{\bm{B}},{\bm{B}}^{*},{\bm{C}}^{-1},\Pi_{\mathrm{ad}},\bm{I}_{Y},\bm{I}_{P}

are bounded linear operators on the function spaces on

and

{\bm{A}}^{*}{\bm{A}}\bm{I}_{Y}
.
This completes the proof of (L) for
{\bm{L}}
.

6
Conclusions and Perspectives

The proposed FoSLS formulations yield, for a wide range of elliptic
and parabolic PDEs in bounded, polyhedral domains,

variationally correct, numerically computable loss functions

for NN training.
The numerically computable FoSLS loss
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{FOSLS}}(\theta)

of a NN realization
U_{\theta}

occurring at an admissible
\theta\in\Theta
during
training is a
computable upper bound of the
approximation accuracy of
U_{\theta}
with respect to the FoSLS solution
U
,
in the physically relevant norm
.
We indicated the connection of adaptive least squares FE algorithms
based on FoSLS formulations with
L^{2}({\mathrm{D}})
loss functions, with

Neural Network Growth
strategies.
Adaptive FoSLS based FEM, as emulated in the present paper
are known to be rate-optimal in most of the problems considered
in Sec.
2
.
See, e.g.,
[
15
,
14
]
.
Proven mathematical statements of rate optimality on the sequence of
approximate solutions produced by adaptive least squares FE methods
transfer to corresponding adaptive NN growth strategies based on the
proposed LSQ formulations.
Within the proposed, adaptive LSQ formulations,
the ESTIMATE-MARK loop corresponds to
local equilibrium indicators for out-of-equilibrium of certain subnetworks.
Furthermore, algorithmic steering of mesh-refinement patterns
could also be delegated to NNs
[
9
]
.

We related the LSQ functional of FoSLS PDE formulations,
which is known to allow reliable and efficient error control,
to corresponding loss functions in NN training.
Similar strategies are conceivable for other variational formulations,
such as e.g. the “deep Ritz” approach of
[
19
]
.
Rather than training approximating NNs by minimizing the quadratic potential energy,
NN training could be based
on reliable and efficient residual a posteriori error estimators.

The proposed deep FoSLS LSQ formulation
extends to high-dimensional, parametric PDEs,
as arise in Uncertainty Quantification.
There, a LSQ regression formulation
in the product space
L^{2}({\mathrm{D}})\otimes L^{2}_{\mathbb{P}}(P)

for a suitable probability space
(P,{\cal A},\mathbb{P})
on the parameter domain
P

naturally implies variance optimal, maximum likelihood estimator
properties of the corresponding optimal NN approximations.
The corresponding parametric solution families are
minimizers of suitable FoSLS formulations over
NNs resolving parametric solutions families.
Relevant issues,
such as the parsimonious emulation of polynomial chaos
expansions of parametric solution families,
and
extension to nonlinear PDEs shall be discussed elsewhere.

A mollifier approach to regularize a Cauchy problem for the inhomogeneous Helmholtz equation
Pierre MARÉCHAL, Walter Cedric SIMO TAO LEE, Faouzi TRIKI

Abstract
The Cauchy problem for the inhomogeneous Helmholtz equation with non-uniform refraction index is considered. The ill-posedness of this problem is tackled by means of the variational form of mollification. This approach is proved to be consistent, and the proposed numerical simulations are quite promising.



1
Introduction

Let
V
be a
C^{3,1}
bounded domain of
\mathds{R}^{3}
with boundary
\partial V
. For
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\partial V
, we denote by

\nu(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
the unit normal vector to
\partial V
pointing outward
V
.
Let
\Gamma
be a nonempty open subset of
\partial V
.
We consider the Cauchy problem for the inhomogeneous Helmholtz equation

\displaystyle\Delta u(\mathbf{x})+k^{2}\eta(\mathbf{x})u(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=S(\mathbf{x}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}\in V,

(1)

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}u(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=f(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\Gamma,

(2)

\displaystyle u(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=g(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\Gamma.

(3)

Here,
u=u(\mathbf{x})
is the unknown amplitude of
the incident field,

\eta\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
is the refraction index,
k
is a positive wave number,

S\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is the source function,
and
f\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
and
g\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
are empirically known boundary conditions.

The Helmholtz equation arises in a large range of applications related to
the propagation of acoustic and electromagnetic waves in the time-harmonic
regime. In this paper, we consider the inverse problem
of reconstructing an acoustic or electromagnetic field from partial data given
on an open part
of the boundary of a given domain. This problem called the
Cauchy problem for the Helmholtz equation is known to be ill-posed if
\Gamma
does not occupy the
whole boundary
\partial V

[
13
,
9
,
18
,
1
]
. In
[
17
]
, the above system was considered in the particular case where the refraction index is constant. However, in practice, the emitted wave travels through an environment in which the refraction index fails to be constant, and we need to investigate the corresponding problem.

We are facing a linear inverse problem. Our aim is to derive a
stable approximation method for this problem,
which yields stable and amenable computational scheme.
Our main focus will be on
mollification
, in the variational
sense of the term, which turns out to be both
flexible and numerically efficient.

Mollifiers were introduced in partial differential equations by
K.O. Friedrichs
[
27
,
11
]
.
The term
mollification
has been used in the field
of inverse problems since the eighties.
In the original works on the subject, mollifiers were
used to smooth the data prior to inversion.
In his book, D.A. Murio
[
23
]

provides an overview of this
approach and its application to some classical inverse problems.
Let us also mention the paper by
D.N. Hao
[
14
]
, which
provides a wide framework for the mollification
approach in this initial meaning.
In
[
21
]
, A.K. Louis and P. Maass
proposed another approach, based on

inner product duality
. This approach
has been subsequently referred to as
the method
of approximate inverses

[
24
]
.
The approximate inverses are particularly well
adapted to problems in which
the adjoint equation has explicit solutions. A third
approach, based on a variational formulation,
also appeared in the same period of time.
In
[
20
]
, A. Lannes
et al.

gave such a formulation while studying the problems
of Fourier extrapolation and deconvolution.
This variational formulation was not studied further until the
papers by N. Alibaud
et al.

[
2
]

and by X. Bonnefond and
P. Maréchal
[
7
]
,
where convergence properties of the variational
formulation was considered.

A definite advantage of the variational approach to
mollification lies in the fact that it offers a quite
flexible framework, just like the Tikhonov regularization,
while being more respectful of the initial model equation
than the latter.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
,
we introduce the linear operator associated to the Cauchy problem
for the inhomogeneous Helmholtz equation.
We propose a regularized variational formulation of the
ill-posed problem based on mollification. Under an additional smoothness assumption
on the targeted solution we show in Theorem
7
that
the unique minimizer converges strongly to the minimum-norm least square solution.
Section
3
is devoted to numerical experiments.
We consider two numerical examples in order to illustrate the
efficiency of our regularization approach.

2
Functional setting and regularization

We shall work in a functional space which enables us to interpret
the ideal (exact) data as the image of
u
by a bounded linear
operator. We observe that:

(I)

the Laplacian
\Delta
is a bounded operator from
H^{2}(V)
to
L^{2}(V)
,
so that, since
\eta\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, the operator
T_{1}u=(\Delta+k^{2}\eta)u
is also
bounded from
H^{2}(V)
to
L^{2}(V)

[
12
]
;

(II)

\nabla u\in\left(H^{1}(V)\right)^{3}
, so that

u\mapsto\partial_{\nu}u\in H^{1/2}(\Gamma)

is a continuous linear operator, which implies in turn that the
operator
T_{2}u=\partial_{\nu}u_{|\Gamma}
is compact
from
H^{2}(V)
to
L^{2}(\Gamma)
;

(III)

the trace operator
u\mapsto u_{|\partial V}
maps
H^{2}(V)

to
H^{3/2}(\Gamma)
continuously, so that the
operator
T_{3}u=u_{|\Gamma}

is compact from
H^{2}(V)
to
L^{2}(\Gamma)
.

Therefore, a natural choice for our workspace is
H^{2}(V)
.
We can then write our system in the form

Tu=v,

(4)

in which

\begin{array}[]{lcll}\displaystyle T\colon&\displaystyle H^{2}(V)&\longrightarrow&\displaystyle L^{2}(V)\times L^{2}(\Gamma)\times L^{2}(\Gamma)\\[4.30554pt]
&\displaystyle u&\longmapsto&\displaystyle\big(\Delta u+k^{2}\eta u,\partial_{\nu}u,u\big)\end{array}

and

v=(S,f,g)\in G:=L^{2}(V)\times L^{2}(\Gamma)\times L^{2}(\Gamma).

We first show that
T
is injective.

Proposition 1
.

The linear bounded map
T
defined by (
4
) is injective.

Proof.

For a proof of this classical result, we refer to the Fritz John’s
book
[
19
]
. More recent proofs
based on Carleman estimates can be found
in
[
18
,
9
,
1
,
26
]
.
The principal idea is to show that a part of
\Gamma
is non-characteristic
with respect to the Helmholtz operator, which in turns leads to the existence
of a small neighborhood of that part of the boundary in
V
where the solution
is identically zero. Since
\eta\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, the Helmholtz operator
T_{1}

possesses the unique continuation property in
V
, and hence the solution
is identically zero in the whole domain
V
which completes the proof.
∎

We are now going to set up our approach to the regularization
of the problem. We consider the mollifier

\varphi_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{\alpha^{3}}\varphi\left(\frac{\mathbf{x}}{\alpha}\right),\quad\alpha\in(0,1],\quad\mathbf{x}\in\mathds{R}^{3},

in which
\varphi
is an integrable function such that

(1)

\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}{\varphi}\subset B_{r}:=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathds{R}^{3}\left|\vphantom{\mathbf{x}\in\mathds{R}^{3}\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|\leq r}\right.\left\|\mathbf{x}\right\|\leq r\right\}

for some positive
r
,

(2)

\int\varphi(\mathbf{x})\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}=1
.

Desirable additional properties of
\varphi
are, as usual,
nonnegativity, isotropy, smoothness, radial decrease.

We denote by
C_{\alpha}
the convolution operator by
\varphi_{\alpha}
:
for every
u\in L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})
,

C_{\alpha}u:=\varphi_{\alpha}\ast u
.
Our regularization principle will control smoothness by means
of
C_{\alpha}
, and
\alpha
will play the role of the
regularization parameter.
One difficulty lies in the fact that convolving
u
by
\varphi_{\alpha}

entails extrapolating
u
from
V
to the larger
set
V+\alpha B_{r}
. Zero padding is obviously forbidden here since
we wish to preserve
H^{2}
regularity. It is then necessary to introduce an extension operator that preserves the properties
of the solution on
V
.

For
s\in[0,1)
,
we denote by
H^{2+s}_{0}(\mathds{R}^{3})
the set of functions in
H^{2+s}(\mathds{R}^{3})

having a compact support. Our objective is to derive an extension
operator
E\colon H^{2+s}(V)\to H^{2+s}_{0}(\mathds{R}^{3})

that in addition of satisfying
Eu|_{V}=u
for all
u\in H^{2+s}(V)
,
is bounded and invertible. Note that there are many extension operators
satisfying these properties. We next provide a complete characterization
of a useful extension operator suited for
C^{3,1}
smooth bounded domains.
For other extension operators on Sobolev spaces under weaker regularity
assumptions on the domain
V,
see, for example,

[
10
,
22
,
8
,
25
]
.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
small enough define the tubular domains

\displaystyle V_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}:=\left\{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\pm t\nu(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}):\;(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},t)\in\partial V\times(0,\varepsilon)\right\},\textrm{ and }V_{\varepsilon}:=V\cup\partial V\cup V_{\varepsilon}^{+}.

(5)

We first notice that
V_{\varepsilon}^{-}\subset V\subset V_{\varepsilon}

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Due to the regularity of
\partial V
,
the function defined by

\displaystyle\phi(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},t)=\mathbf{x}^{\prime}-t\nu(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}),

(6)

is a
C^{2,1}
-diffeomorphism from
\partial V\times(0,\varepsilon)

onto
V_{\varepsilon}^{-}
for
\varepsilon
small enough.

Let
u
be fixed
in
H^{2}(V)
. The first step is to construct an extension
u_{\varepsilon}

of
u
to
H^{2}(V_{\varepsilon/2})
. For
\alpha,\beta\in\mathds{R}
, set

\displaystyle\tilde{u}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}+t\nu(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))=\alpha u(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}-t\nu(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))+\beta u(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}-2t\nu(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})),\textrm{ for }(\mathbf{x}^{\prime},t)\in\partial V\times(0,\varepsilon/2).

(7)

Since
\nu
is a
C^{2}
vector field,
\tilde{u}
lies in
H^{2}(V_{\varepsilon}^{+})
, and verifies

\displaystyle\tilde{u}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=(\alpha+\beta)u(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}),\textrm{ and }\;\partial_{\nu}\tilde{u}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})=(-\alpha-2\beta)\partial_{\nu}u(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}),\;\;\mathbf{x}^{\prime}\in\partial V.

(8)

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be defined by

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(\mathbf{x})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{llcc}\tilde{u}(\mathbf{x}),&\mathbf{x}\in V_{\varepsilon/2}^{+},\\
u(\mathbf{x}),&\mathbf{x}\in V.\end{array}\right.

By construction, we have
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{2}(V)\cup H^{2}(V_{\varepsilon}^{+})
.
Considering the traces (
8
) and taking
\alpha=3
and
\beta=-2
,
implies that
u_{\varepsilon}
and its first derivatives have no jumps
across
\partial V
, and thus
u_{\varepsilon}\in H^{2}(V_{\varepsilon/2})
.

Let
\chi_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathds{R}^{3})
be a cut off function satisfying

\displaystyle\chi_{\varepsilon}=1,\textrm{ on }V_{\varepsilon/8},\textrm{ and }\chi_{\varepsilon}=0,\textrm{ on }\mathds{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{V_{3\varepsilon/8}}.

(12)

Now, we are ready to introduce the operator
E
. For
u\in H^{2}(V)
, define

\displaystyle Eu=\chi_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon},

where
u_{\varepsilon}
and
\chi_{\varepsilon}
are respectively given
in (
2
) and (
12
).

Proposition 2
.

Let
s\in[0,1)
be fixed.
The extension operator
E\colon H^{2+s}(V)\to H^{2+s}_{0}(\mathds{R}^{3})
is bounded,
invertible, and satisfies

\displaystyle\left\|u\right\|_{H^{2+s}(V)}\leq\left\|Eu\right\|_{H^{2+s}(\mathds{R}^{3})}\leq C_{s}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{2+s}(V)},

(13)

where
C_{s}>1
is a constant that only depends on
s
,

\varepsilon
and
V
. In addition,
\textrm{Supp}(Eu)\subset V_{\varepsilon}
.

Proof.

The left side inequality is straightforward.
The functions
\psi_{j}:V_{\varepsilon/j}^{+}\to V_{\varepsilon/j}^{-},j=1,2
, defined by

\displaystyle\psi_{j}(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}+t\nu(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}))=\mathbf{x}^{\prime}-jt\nu(\mathbf{x}^{\prime}),\quad(x^{\prime},t)\in\partial V\times(0,\varepsilon),\;j=1,2,

(14)

are
C^{2,1}
-diffeomorphisms.

Forward calculations give

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}^{3}\setminus\overline{V}}|Eu|^{2}+|\nabla Eu|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}Eu|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\leq\|\chi_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{2,1}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\int_{V_{\varepsilon/2}^{+}}|\tilde{u}|^{2}+|\nabla\tilde{u}|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}\tilde{u}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\|\chi_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{2,1}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left(\|\psi_{j}\|_{C^{2,1}(V^{+}_{\varepsilon/j})}^{2}+1\right)\|D\psi^{-1}_{j}\|_{C^{1}(V^{-}_{\varepsilon/j})}^{2}\int_{V_{\varepsilon}^{-}}|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla^{2}u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mathbf{x},

with
D\psi^{-1}_{j}
is the gradient of the vector
field
\psi^{-1}_{j}
, and
\kappa>0
is a universal constant.
Therefore

\displaystyle\left\|Eu\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}\leq C\left\|u\right\|_{H^{2}(V)},

where

\displaystyle C^{2}=\kappa\|\chi_{\varepsilon}\|_{C^{2,1}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2}\left(\|\psi_{j}\|_{C^{2,1}(V^{+}_{\varepsilon/j})}^{2}+1\right)\|D\psi^{-1}_{j}\|_{C^{1}(V^{-}_{\varepsilon/j})}^{2}.

Similarly, tedious calculations lead to the following estimate of the seminorm

\displaystyle|D_{\beta}Eu|_{s,\mathds{R}^{3}}^{2}=\int_{\mathds{R}^{3}}\int_{\mathds{R}^{3}}\frac{\left|D_{\beta}Eu(x)-D_{\beta}Eu(y)\right|^{2}}{|x-y|^{3+2s}}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\widetilde{C}_{s}^{2}(|D_{\beta}u|_{s,V}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{H^{2}(V)}^{2}),

for all
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{3}
, satisfying
|\beta|=2
, where
\widetilde{C}_{s}>0
, depends on

s,\varepsilon
and
V
. By taking
C_{s}=\max(1,C,\widetilde{C}_{s})
, we complete the proof
of the proposition.
∎

The defined extension operator
E
opens the way to
the following variational formulation of mollification:

(\mathscr{P})\quad\left|\begin{array}[]{rl}\hbox{Minimize}&J_{\alpha}(u,v,T):=\left\|v-Tu\right\|_{G}^{2}+\left\|(I-C_{\alpha})Eu\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}\\
\hbox{subject to}&u\in H^{2}(V).\end{array}\right.

Our aim is now to prove

1.

the well-posedness of the above variational
problem, that is, that the solution
u_{\alpha}

depends continuously on the data
v
;

2.

the consistency of the regularization, that is, that

u_{\alpha}
converges to
T^{\dagger}u
in some sense
as
\alpha\downarrow 0
, where
T^{\dagger}
is the
pseudo-inverse of
T
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
K,s>0
.
Let
\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathds{R}^{3})
be such that

\hat{\varphi}(0)=1\quad\hbox{and}\quad 1-\hat{\varphi}(\xi)\sim K\left\|\xi\right\|^{s}\quad\hbox{as}\quad\xi\to 0.

Assume in addition that
\hat{\varphi}(\xi)\not=1
for every
\xi\in\mathds{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
.
Define

m_{\alpha}=\min_{\left\|\xi\right\|=1}|1-\hat{\varphi}(\alpha\xi)|^{2}\quad\hbox{and}\quad M_{\alpha}=\max_{\left\|\xi\right\|=1}|1-\hat{\varphi}(\alpha\xi)|^{2}.

Then,

(i)

for every
\alpha>0
,
0<m_{\alpha}\leq M_{\alpha}\leq(1+\left\|\varphi\right\|_{1})^{2}
;

(ii)

\sup_{\alpha>0}M_{\alpha}/m_{\alpha}<\infty
and

M_{\alpha}\to 0
as
\alpha\downarrow 0
;

(iii)

there exists
\nu_{\circ},A_{\circ}>0
such that, for every
\alpha\in(0,1]
,
for every
\xi\in\mathds{R}^{3}\setminus\{0\}
,

\nu_{\circ}\left(\left\|\xi\right\|^{2s}\mathds{1}_{B_{1/\alpha}}(\xi)+\frac{1}{M_{\alpha}}\mathds{1}_{B_{1/\alpha}^{c}}(\xi)\right)\leq\frac{|1-\hat{\varphi}(\alpha\xi)|^{2}}{|1-\hat{\varphi}(\alpha\xi/\left\|\xi\right\|)|^{2}}\leq\mu_{\circ}\left\|\xi\right\|^{2s}.

Proof
.
See
[
2
, Lemma 12]
.

Corollary 4
.

Let
\varphi
,
m_{\alpha}
,
M_{\alpha}
,
\nu_{\circ}
,
\mu_{\circ}
be
as in Lemma
3
, and let

C_{\alpha}
be the operator of convolution with
\varphi_{\alpha}
,
For every
u\in L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})
,

\left\|(I-C_{\alpha})u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}\geq\nu_{\circ}m_{\alpha}\int_{\mathds{R}^{3}}\left(\left\|\xi\right\|^{2s}\mathds{1}_{B_{1/\alpha}}(\xi)+\frac{1}{M_{\alpha}}\mathds{1}_{B_{1/\alpha}^{c}}(\xi)\right)|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

(15)

Proof
.
From Lemma
3
, we have:

\displaystyle\left\|(I-C_{\alpha})u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}^{3}}|1-\hat{\varphi}(\alpha\xi)|^{2}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}^{3}}|1-\hat{\varphi}(\alpha\xi/\left\|\xi\right\|)|^{2}\frac{|1-\hat{\varphi}(\alpha\xi)|^{2}}{|1-\hat{\varphi}(\alpha\xi/\left\|\xi\right\|)|^{2}}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle m_{\alpha}\nu_{\circ}\int_{\mathds{R}^{3}}\left(\left\|\xi\right\|^{2s}\mathds{1}_{B_{1/\alpha}}(\xi)+\frac{1}{M_{\alpha}}\mathds{1}_{B_{1/\alpha}^{c}}(\xi)\right)|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Lemma 5
.

Let
C_{\alpha}
be the operator of convolution with
\varphi_{\alpha}
,
where
\varphi
is as in Lemma
3
.
There exists a positive constant
A_{\circ}
, depending on
V
only, such that
for every
s\geq 0
and every
u\in H^{s}(V)
,

A_{\circ}m_{\alpha}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}\leq\left\|(I-C_{\alpha})u\right\|_{H^{s}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}.

(16)

Proof
.
We start with the case
s=0
.
From (
15
), we have:

\displaystyle\left\|(I-C_{\alpha})u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle m_{\alpha}\nu_{\circ}\int_{\mathds{R}^{3}}\left(\left\|\xi\right\|^{2s}\mathds{1}_{B_{1/\alpha}\setminus B_{1}}(\xi)+\frac{1}{M_{\alpha}}\mathds{1}_{B_{1/\alpha}^{c}}(\xi)\right)|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle m_{\alpha}\nu_{\circ}(1+\left\|\varphi\right\|_{1})^{-2}\int_{\mathds{R}^{3}}\mathds{1}_{B_{1}^{c}}(\xi)|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle m_{\alpha}\nu_{\circ}(1+\left\|\varphi\right\|_{1})^{-2}\left\|T_{B_{1}^{c}}^{-1}\right\|^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2},

in which
T_{B_{1}^{c}}:=\mathds{1}_{B_{1}^{c}}F
, the operator of Fourier truncation to
B_{1}^{c}
.
Thus

A_{\circ}=\nu_{\circ}(1+\left\|\varphi\right\|_{1})^{-2}\left\|T_{B_{1}^{c}}^{-1}\right\|^{2}

is suitable. Notice that, for
s=0
, Parseval’s identity
enables to rewrite (
16
) in the form

A_{\circ}m_{\alpha}\left\|\hat{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}\leq\left\|(1-\hat{\varphi}(\alpha\cdot)\hat{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2},

(17)

in which
F
denotes the Fourier-Plancherel operator.
Now, let
s>0
and assume that
u\in H^{s}(V)
.
We readily see that

F^{-1}\big((1+\left\|\xi\right\|^{2})^{s/2}\hat{u}(\xi)\big)
belongs
to
L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})
. Applying (
17
)
to the latter function yields

A_{\circ}m_{\alpha}\left\|(1+\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2})^{s/2}\hat{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}\leq\left\|(1-\hat{\varphi}(\alpha\cdot))(1+\left\|\cdot\right\|^{2})^{s/2}\hat{u}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2},

and (
16
) follows.

Lemma 6
.

Let
C_{\alpha}
be as in the previous lemma,
s>0
, and
s_{\circ}\geq 0
.
If
u\in H^{s_{\circ}+s}(\mathds{R}^{3})
, then

\left\|(I-C_{\alpha})u\right\|_{H^{s_{\circ}}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}\leq\mu_{\circ}M_{\alpha}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s_{\circ}+s}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2},

with
\mu_{\circ}
is the positive constant provided
by Lemma
3
.

Proof
.
We have:

\displaystyle\left\|(I-C_{\alpha})u\right\|_{H^{s_{\circ}}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathds{R}^{3}}|1-\hat{\varphi}(\alpha\xi/\left\|\xi\right\|)|^{2}\frac{|1-\hat{\varphi}(\alpha\xi)|^{2}}{|1-\hat{\varphi}(\alpha\xi/\left\|\xi\right\|)|^{2}}(1+\left\|\xi\right\|^{2})^{s_{\circ}}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu_{\circ}M_{\alpha}\int_{\mathds{R}^{3}}\left\|\xi\right\|^{2s}(1+\left\|\xi\right\|^{2})^{s_{\circ}}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu_{\circ}M_{\alpha}\int_{\mathds{R}^{3}}(1+\left\|\xi\right\|^{2})^{s_{\circ}+s}|\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{\circ}M_{\alpha}\left\|u\right\|_{H^{s_{\circ}+s}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2},

in which the first inequality stems from Lemma
3
.

Theorem 7
.

Assume
u^{\dagger}\in H^{2+s}(\mathds{R}^{3})
, with
s\in(0,1)
, and
v=Tu^{\dagger}_{|V}
, so that
u^{\dagger}_{|V}=T^{\dagger}v
.
Let
u_{\alpha}
be the solution to Problem
(\mathscr{P})
.
Then
u_{\alpha}\to T^{\dagger}v
in
H^{2}(V)
as
\alpha\downarrow 0
.

Proof
.
We walk in the steps of the proof of Theorem 11 in
[
2
]
,
which we adapt to the present context. The main differences lie
in that the regularization term uses a Sobolev norm and in that we make use of the
extension operator
E
in order to cope with boundary constraints.
In Step 1, we show that the family
(u_{\alpha})
is bounded in
H^{2}(V)
,
thus weekly compact; in Step 2, we establish the weak convergence of
u_{\alpha}

to
T^{\dagger}v
, and finally in Step 3, we use a compactness argument to show
that the convergence is, in fact, strong.

Step 1.

By construction, we have:

\left\|(I-C_{\alpha})Eu_{\alpha}\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}\leq J_{\alpha}(u_{\alpha},v,T)\leq J_{\alpha}(u^{\dagger}_{|V},v,T)\leq\left\|(I-C_{\alpha})Eu^{\dagger}_{|V}\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}.

(18)

Using Lemma
5
and Lemma
6
, we obtain:

\left\|u_{\alpha}\right\|_{H^{2}(V)}^{2}\leq\left\|Eu_{\alpha}\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}\leq\frac{\mu_{\circ}}{A_{\circ}}\frac{M_{\alpha}}{m_{\alpha}}\left\|Eu^{\dagger}_{|V}\right\|_{H^{2+s}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2},

and Lemma
3
(ii) then shows that
the set
(u_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in(0,1]}
is bounded
in
H^{2}(V)
, therefore is weakly compact.

Step 2.
Denote
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{G}
the natural norm
in the Hilbert space
G
.
Now, let
(\alpha_{n})_{n}
be a sequence converging to
0
. There then exists
a subsequence
(u_{\alpha_{n_{k}}})_{k}
which converges weakly in
H^{2}(V)
.
Let
\tilde{u}
be the weak limit of this subsequence. We then have:

\displaystyle\left\|v-Tu_{\alpha_{n_{k}}}\right\|_{G}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle J_{\alpha_{n_{k}}}(u_{\alpha_{n_{k}}},v,T)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle J_{\alpha_{n_{k}}}(u^{\dagger}_{|V},v,T)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|(I-C_{\alpha_{n_{k}}})u^{\dagger}_{|V}\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mu_{\circ}M_{\alpha_{n_{k}}}\left\|u^{\dagger}_{|V}\right\|_{H^{2+s}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}.

Since
M_{\alpha_{n_{k}}}
goes to zero as
k\to\infty
,
so does
\left\|v-Tu_{\alpha_{n_{k}}}\right\|_{G}^{2}
.
By weak lower semicontinuity of the norm on the Hilbert space
G
,
we see that the weak limit
\tilde{u}
satisfies:

\left\|v-T\tilde{u}\right\|_{G}^{2}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\left\|v-Tu_{\alpha_{n_{k}}}\right\|_{G}^{2}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|v-Tu_{\alpha_{n_{k}}}\right\|_{G}^{2}=0.

Therefore,
T\tilde{u}=v
and the injectivity of
T
(Proposition
1
)
implies that
\tilde{u}=u^{\dagger}
.

Step 3.

We will show that, for every multi-index
\beta\in\mathds{N}^{3}

such that
|\beta|\leq 2
,

D_{\beta}Eu_{\alpha}\to D_{\beta}Eu^{\dagger}_{|V}\hbox{ in }L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})\hbox{ as }\alpha\downarrow 0,

(19)

which will imply the announced strong convergence.
Observe first that, by the previous step and the continuity
of
E
,

Eu_{\alpha}\rightharpoonup Eu^{\dagger}_{|V}\hbox{ in }H^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})\hbox{ as }\alpha\downarrow 0,

so that, for every multi-index
\beta\in\mathds{N}^{3}

such that
|\beta|\leq 2
,

D_{\beta}Eu_{\alpha}\rightharpoonup D_{\beta}Eu^{\dagger}_{|V}\hbox{ in }L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})\hbox{ as }\alpha\downarrow 0,

(20)

Fix
\beta\in\left\{\beta^{\prime}\in\mathds{N}^{3}\left|\vphantom{\beta^{\prime}\in\mathds{N}^{3}|\beta^{\prime}|\leq 2}\right.|\beta^{\prime}|\leq 2\right\}
and
let
(\alpha_{n})_{n}
be a sequence converging to
0
,
as in the previous step. For convenience,
let
u_{n}:=u_{\alpha_{n}}
,
C_{n}:=C_{\alpha_{n}}
,

M_{n}:=M_{\alpha_{n}}
and
m_{n}:=m_{\alpha_{n}}
.
Since
Eu_{n}
has compact support, it is obvious that

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{n}\int_{\left\|x\right\|\geq R}|D_{\beta}Eu_{n}(x)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

(21)

Now, for every
h\in\mathds{R}^{3}
and every function
u
,
let
\mathscr{T}_{h}u
denote the translated function
x\mapsto u(x-h)
.
We proceed to show that

\sup_{n}\left\|\mathscr{T}_{h}D_{\beta}Eu_{n}-D_{\beta}Eu_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}\to 0\hbox{ as }\left\|h\right\|\to 0.

(22)

Together with (
20
) and (
21
),
this will establish (
19
) via
the Fréchet-Kolmogorov Theorem
(see
e.g.

[
16
, Theorem 3.8 page 175]
).
We have:

\displaystyle\left\|\mathscr{T}_{h}D_{\beta}Eu_{n}-D_{\beta}Eu_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|F(\mathscr{T}_{h}D_{\beta}Eu_{n}-D_{\beta}Eu_{n})\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int|e^{-2i\pi\left\langle h,\xi\right\rangle}-1|^{2}|FD_{\beta}Eu_{n}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2},

in which

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{\left\|\xi\right\|\leq 1/\alpha_{n}}|e^{-2i\pi\left\langle h,\xi\right\rangle}-1|^{2}|FD_{\beta}Eu_{n}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\int_{\left\|\xi\right\|>1/\alpha_{n}}|e^{-2i\pi\left\langle h,\xi\right\rangle}-1|^{2}|FD_{\beta}Eu_{n}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

We now bound
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
.
On the one hand,

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0<\left\|\xi\right\|\leq 1/\alpha_{n}}\frac{|e^{-2i\pi\left\langle h,\xi\right\rangle}-1|^{2}}{\left\|\xi\right\|^{2s}}\left\|\xi\right\|^{2s}|FD_{\beta}Eu_{n}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{\xi\not=0}\frac{|e^{-2i\pi\left\langle h,\xi\right\rangle}-1|^{2}}{\left\|\xi\right\|^{2s}}\int_{\left\|\xi\right\|\leq 1/\alpha_{n}}\left\|\xi\right\|^{2s}|FD_{\beta}Eu_{n}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

Since
|e^{-2i\pi\left\langle h,\xi\right\rangle}-1|=\mathscr{O}(\left\|\xi\right\|)
and
s\leq 1
,
the above supremum is finite. Let
\gamma_{\circ}
denote its value.
Therefore

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma_{\circ}\left\|h\right\|^{2s}\left(\int_{\left\|\xi\right\|\leq 1/\alpha_{n}}|FD_{\beta}Eu_{n}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi+\int_{\left\|\xi\right\|\leq 1/\alpha_{n}}\left\|\xi\right\|^{2s}|FD_{\beta}Eu_{n}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\gamma_{\circ}\left\|h\right\|^{2s}\left(\left\|FD_{\beta}Eu_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}+\int_{\left\|\xi\right\|\leq 1/\alpha_{n}}\left\|\xi\right\|^{2s}|FD_{\beta}Eu_{n}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right).

Since
(u_{n})
is bounded in

independently
of

, so is

(\left\|FD_{\beta}Eu_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2})
. Moreover,
by using Corollary
4

with
D_{\beta}Eu_{n}
in place of

, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\left\|\xi\right\|\leq 1/\alpha_{n}}\left\|\xi\right\|^{2s}|FD_{\beta}Lu_{n}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu_{\circ}m_{n}}\left\|(I-C_{n})D_{\beta}Eu_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu_{\circ}m_{n}}\left\|D_{\beta}(I-C_{n})Eu_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu_{\circ}m_{n}}\left\|(I-C_{n})Eu_{n}\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\nu_{\circ}m_{n}}\left\|(I-C_{n})Eu_{|V}^{\dagger}\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{\circ}}{\nu_{\circ}}\frac{M_{n}}{m_{n}}\left\|Eu_{|V}^{\dagger}\right\|_{H^{2+s}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2},

in which the last two inequalities are respectively due to
the inequality (
18
) and
Lemma
6
(with
s_{\circ}=2
).
It follows that
I_{1}=\mathscr{O}(\left\|h\right\|^{2s})
.
On the other hand, using again Corollary
4

with
D_{\beta}Eu_{n}
in place of
u
, we have:

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\int_{\left\|\xi\right\|>1/\alpha_{n}}|FD_{\beta}Eu_{n}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\nu_{\circ}}\frac{M_{n}}{m_{n}}\left\|(I-C_{n})D_{\beta}Eu_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\nu_{\circ}}\frac{M_{n}}{m_{n}}\left\|D_{\beta}(I-C_{n})Eu_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\nu_{\circ}}\frac{M_{n}}{m_{n}}\left\|(I-C_{n})Eu_{n}\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4}{\nu_{\circ}}\frac{M_{n}}{m_{n}}\left\|(I-C_{n})Eu_{|V}^{\dagger}\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\frac{\mu_{\circ}}{\nu_{\circ}}\frac{M_{n}}{m_{n}}M_{n}\left\|Eu_{|V}^{\dagger}\right\|_{H^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2+s},

in which the last two inequalities are respectively due to
the inequality (
18
) and
Lemma
6
(with
s_{\circ}=2
).
It follows that
I_{2}=\mathscr{O}(M_{n})
.
Gathering the obtained bounds on

and
I_{2}
,
we see that there exists a positive constant
K
such that

\left\|\mathscr{T}_{h}D_{\beta}Eu_{n}-D_{\beta}Eu_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}\leq K\big(\left\|h\right\|^{2s}+M_{n}\big).

(23)

Now, fix
\varepsilon>0
. There exists
n_{\circ}\in\mathds{N}^{*}
such that
for every
n\geq n_{\circ}
,
M_{n}\leq\varepsilon
.
From (
23
), we see that

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\left\|\mathscr{T}_{h}D_{\beta}Eu_{n}-D_{\beta}Eu_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\big\{\max_{1\leq n\leq n_{\circ}}\left\|\mathscr{T}_{h}D_{\beta}Eu_{n}-D_{\beta}Eu_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2},K\big(\left\|h\right\|^{2s}+\varepsilon\big).\big\}

By the
L^{2}
-continuity of translation, we have:

\forall n\in\mathds{N}^{*},\quad\left\|\mathscr{T}_{h}D_{\beta}Eu_{n}-D_{\beta}Eu_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}\to 0\quad\hbox{as}\quad\left\|h\right\|\to 0.

Consequently,

\max_{1\leq n\leq n_{\circ}}\left\|\mathscr{T}_{h}D_{\beta}Eu_{n}-D_{\beta}Eu_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}\to 0\quad\hbox{as}\quad\left\|h\right\|\to 0,

so that

\limsup_{h\to 0}\sup_{n}\left\|\mathscr{T}_{h}D_{\beta}Eu_{n}-D_{\beta}Eu_{n}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathds{R}^{3})}^{2}\leq K\varepsilon.

Since
\varepsilon>0
was arbitrary, (
22
)
is established, which achieves the proof.

Remark 8

Notice that the real

into

\partial_{xx},\partial_{yy},\partial_{xy}
,
C_{\alpha}
is the matrix approximating the convolution with the function
\varphi_{\alpha}
defined in (
27
) and
E
is the matrix modeling the extension operator. From (
46
), we compute
U_{\alpha}^{\epsilon}
as the solution of the matrix equation

\left[A^{\top}A+E^{\top}(I_{n_{x}n_{y}}-C_{\alpha})^{\top}D(I_{n_{x}n_{y}}-C_{\alpha})E\right]U_{\alpha}^{\epsilon}=A^{\top}B_{\epsilon},

(47)

where
\alpha
is the regularization parameter,
I_{n_{x}n_{y}}
is the square identity matrix of size
n_{x}n_{y}
and
D
is the matrix defined by

D=I_{n_{x}n_{y}}+D_{x}^{\top}D_{x}+D_{y}^{\top}D_{y}+D_{xx}^{\top}D_{xx}+D_{yy}^{\top}D_{yy}+2D_{xy}^{\top}D_{xy}.

Selection of the regularization parameter

The choice of the regularization parameter
\alpha
is a crucial step of the regularization. Indeed, the reconstruction error
U-U_{\alpha}^{\epsilon}
has two components: the regularization error
U-U_{\alpha}
(corresponding to exact data) and the data error propagation
U_{\alpha}-U_{\alpha}^{\epsilon}
. The former error is generally monotonically increasing with respect to
\alpha
and attains its minimum at
\alpha=0
while the latter error blows up as
\alpha
goes to
0
and decreases when
\alpha
gets larger. Consequently, the reconstruction error norm
||U-U_{\alpha}^{\epsilon}||
is minimal in some located region (depending on the noise level
\epsilon
in the data) where both error terms have approximately the same magnitude. Outside that region, the reconstruction error is dominated by one of the two error terms which leads to an undesirable approximate solution
U_{\alpha}^{\epsilon}
.

In the following, we consider the heuristic selection
rule (
48
)-(
49
)
which has a similitude with the discrete quasi-optimality rule

[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
except for
the denominator which in our case is not equal to one.

Let
(\alpha_{n})_{n}
be a sample of the regularization parameter
\alpha
on a discrete grid defined as

\alpha_{n}:=\alpha_{0}q^{n},\quad\alpha_{0}\in(0,\left\|T\right\|^{2}],\quad 0<q<1,\quad n=1,...,N_{0},

(48)

we consider the parameter
\alpha(\epsilon)^{*}
defined by

\alpha(\epsilon)^{*}=\alpha_{n_{*}},\quad\text{with}\quad n_{*}=\argmin_{n\in\{1,...,N_{0}\}}\frac{\left\|U_{\alpha_{n}}^{\epsilon}-U_{\alpha_{n+1}}^{\epsilon}\right\|}{\alpha_{n}-\alpha_{n+1}}.

(49)

The heuristic behind the rule (
49
) is the following:

Indeed, we aim at approximating the best regularization parameter
\alpha(\epsilon)
(over the chosen grid) which minimizes the reconstruction error norm
||U-U_{\alpha}^{\epsilon}||
over the grid
(\alpha_{n})_{n}
, i.e.

\alpha(\epsilon)=\alpha_{n(opt)}\quad\text{with}\quad n(opt)=\argmin_{n}\,\,K(\alpha_{n}):=\left\|U-U_{\alpha_{n}}^{\epsilon}\right\|.

Given that minimizers of a differentiable function are critical points of that function, provided the function
K
is differentiable,
\alpha_{n(opt)}
can be characterized as a minimizer of the absolute value of the derivative of function
K
, that is

n(opt)\approx\argmin_{n}\,\,\left\lvert K^{\prime}(\alpha_{n})\right\rvert.

By approximating the derivative
K^{\prime}(\alpha_{n})
of the function
K
at
\alpha_{n}
by its growth rates over the grid
(\alpha_{n})_{n}
, we get that

n(opt)\approx\argmin_{n}\,\,\,\,\,\left\lvert\frac{K(\alpha_{n+1})-K(\alpha_{n})}{\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}}\right\rvert.

However, since the exact solution
u
is unknown, we cannot evaluate the function
K
.
In such a setting, we search a tight upper bound of the function
K
and aim at
minimizing that upper bound. Using the triangle inequality, we have

\left\lvert K(\alpha_{n+1})-K(\alpha_{n})\right\rvert=\left\lvert\left\|U-U_{\alpha_{n+1}}^{\epsilon}\right\|-\left\|U-U_{\alpha_{n}}^{\epsilon}\right\|\right\rvert\leq\left\|U_{\alpha_{n+1}}^{\epsilon}-U_{\alpha_{n}}^{\epsilon}\right\|

(50)

Hence from (
50
), we get an upper bound of the unknown term
\left\lvert K(\alpha_{n+1})-K(\alpha_{n+1})\right\rvert
which is actually computable.

By approximating
\left\lvert K(\alpha_{n+1})-K(\alpha_{n+1})\right\rvert
by its upper bound in (
50
), we get

n(opt)\approx\argmin_{n}\,\,\,\,\,\left\lvert\frac{\left\|U_{\alpha_{n+1}}^{\epsilon}-U_{\alpha_{n}}^{\epsilon}\right\|}{\alpha_{n+1}-\alpha_{n}}\right\rvert=\frac{\left\|U_{\alpha_{n+1}}^{\epsilon}-U_{\alpha_{n}}^{\epsilon}\right\|}{\alpha_{n}-\alpha_{n+1}}.

which is precisely the definition of our heuristic selection rule (
49
).

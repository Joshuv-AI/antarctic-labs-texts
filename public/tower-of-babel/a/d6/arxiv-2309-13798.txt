Matching-Logic-Based Understanding of Polynomial Functors and their Initial/Final Models
Dorel Lucanu

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate how the initial models and the final models for the polynomial functors can be uniformly specified in matching logic.



1
Introduction

It is known that many data types used in programming are defined as initial algebra or final coalgebra for an appropriate functor
F:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
, where
\mathbb{C}
is a category of data types. In this paper we assume that
\mathbb{C}
is the category of sets (see, e.g.,
[
3
]
).
If
F
is bicontinous, i.e., it preserves the colimits of
\omega
-sequences and and the limits of
\omega^{\mathit{op}}
-sequences, then the initial algebra (model) is obtained via colimit of the
\omega
-sequence

\mathbf{0}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{2.0pt}{\hbox{!`}}}F\;\mathbf{0}\xrightarrow{F\;\raisebox{2.0pt}{\hbox{!`}}}F\;F\;\mathbf{0}=F^{2}\;\mathbf{0}\xrightarrow{F^{2}\;\raisebox{2.0pt}{\hbox{!`}}}F^{3}\;\mathbf{0}\xrightarrow{F^{3}\;\raisebox{2.0pt}{\hbox{!`}}}\cdots

where
\mathbf{0}
is the initial object in
\mathbb{C}
, and
\mathbf{0}\xrightarrow{\raisebox{2.0pt}{\hbox{!`}}}X
is the unique arrow from the initial object, and the final coalgebra (model) is the limit of the
\omega^{\mathit{op}}
-sequence

\mathbf{1}\xleftarrow{\hbox{!}}F\;\mathbf{1}\xleftarrow{F\;\hbox{!}}F\;F\;\mathbf{1}=F^{2}\;\mathbf{1}\xleftarrow{F^{2}\;\hbox{!}}F^{3}\;\mathbf{1}\xleftarrow{F^{3}\;\hbox{!}}\cdots

where
\mathbf{1}
is the final object in
\mathbb{C}
, and
\mathbf{1}\xleftarrow{\hbox{!}}X
is the unique arrow to the final object
[
3
]
.

This is a nice abstract framework, but, as we know, the evil is hidden in details. How the elements of the initial and final models look like for various concrete functors? How could they be handled in practice?

A possible answer can be obtained by capturing these objects in Matching Logic (ML), the logical foundation of the K Framework, where the program languages and the properties of their programs can be specified in a uniform way (see, e.g.,
[
13
,
9
,
14
,
8
,
12
]
). First steps are done in
[
7
]
, where the initial algebra semantics is captured in ML, and in
[
8
]
, where it is shown how examples of inductive/coinductive data types are fully specified in ML.
We say that ML captures a (inductive/coinductive) data type
\mathit{DT}
if there is an ML theory
\mathsf{Th}^{\sf ML}(\mathit{DT})
such that:

•

from each
\mathsf{Th}^{\sf ML}(\mathit{DT})
-model
M
we may extract a structure
\alpha(M)
that is isomorphic to
\mathit{DT}
, and

•

each deduction principle for
\mathit{DT}
(e.g., induction or coinduction) can be expressed as a theorem within ML using its proof system.

In this paper, we investigate how data types specified as initial
F
-algebras or as a final
F
-coalgebras, where
F
is a polynomial functor, can be captured in ML.
The polynomial functors can be defined in two ways:

1.

Using ”classical” inductive definition of polynomials (see, e.g.,
[
15
]
): the polynomial functors is the smallest class including the constant and the identity functors, and it is closed to sum, product, and constant-exponent functors.

2.

Using unary container functors (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
): a polynomial functor is of the form
X\mapsto\sum_{a:A}X^{B[a]}
, where
a:A\vdash B[a]
is an
A
-indexed family.

The constant and the identity functors, together with their initial and final models, can be easily captured in ML. Moreover, if we exclude the exponent functor, then the initial algebra can be captured using the approach similar to that from
[
7
]
. The exponent functor complicates the things.
A possible approach for the classical definition is as follows: supposing that we have captured
F_{1}\;X
and
F_{2}\;X
by the ML theories (specifications)
\mathrm{SPEC}(F_{1}\;X)
and resp.
\mathrm{SPEC}(F_{2}\;X)
, then use these specifications to build
\mathrm{SPEC}(F_{1}\;X~\mathit{op}~F_{2}\;X)
, i,.e., to obtain something like

\mathrm{SPEC}(F_{1}\;X~\mathit{op}~F_{2}\;X)=\mathrm{SPEC}(F_{1}\;X)~\overline{\mathit{op}}~\mathrm{SPEC}(F_{2}\;X)

where
\overline{\mathit{op}}
reflects
\mathit{op}
to the level of ML specifications, and it follows to be defined.
In order to accomplish that, we need a ”uniform standard” definition for the specifications
\mathrm{SPEC}(F\;X)
.

The container functors already have a uniform standard definition, and therefore they are more tempting for our investigation.
This approach is a work-in-progress, and the results obtained up now show that:

•

it is possible to specify unary container functors, together with their initial algebras and final coalgebras, as ML theories;

•

it is possible to derive the induction principle and the coinduction principle as theorems of the corresponding theories;

•

the ML reasoning can be used to understand better the intimate structure of the initial algebra and the final coalgebra.

The approach is instantiated on the lists example, in order to see the relationship with the classical approach of these data types, and on that of Moore machines, in order to see the (constant) exponential functor at work.

The paper is structured as follows. Section
2
recalls the definitions for polynomial functors and for unary container functors, and the the relationship between them.
Section
3
briefly recalls the main elements of matching logic, together with the theories of the equality and of the sorts.
Section
4
includes the main contribution, showing that how the unary container functors and their related concepts can be specified in matching logic. The instantiation of the general approach on the examples of lists and Moore machines are included in Section
5
and Section
6
, respectively. The paper ends with some concluding remarks.

2.1
Polynomial Functors as Container Functors

Here we recall the relationship between polynomial functors and container functors (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
).

Constant Functor

The main idea is to identify the constant value with the shapes
A
.
Consider
B
as being
a{\kern 0.86108pt:\kern 0.86108pt}A\vdash\mathbf{0}
(no positions of shape
a
), where
\mathbf{0}
denotes the initial object of
\mathbb{C}
.
We get
\sum_{a{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}A}X^{B[a]}\approxeq\sum_{a{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}A}X^{\mathbf{0}}\approxeq A
.

Remark
.

The elements of
\sum_{a{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}A}X^{\mathbf{0}}
are pairs
\left\langle a,f:\mathbf{0}\to X\right\rangle
, where
a\in A
. Since
f:\mathbf{0}\to X
is unique, we obtain
\left\langle a,f:\mathbf{0}\to X\right\rangle\approxeq a
.

Identity Functor

Consider
A
as being
\mathbf{1}
(just one shape) and
B
as being
\star{\kern 0.86108pt:\kern 0.86108pt}\mathbf{1}\vdash\mathbf{1}
(just one position), where
\star
is the unique element in
\mathbf{1}
. It follows that

\sum_{a{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}A}X^{B[a]}\approxeq\sum_{\star}X^{\mathbf{1}}\approxeq X
.

Remark
.

The elements of
\sum_{\star}X^{\mathbf{1}}
are pairs
\left\langle\star,f:\mathbf{1}\to X\right\rangle
. Since
f
selects just one element
x
in
X
, it follows that
\left\langle\star,f:\mathbf{1}\to X\right\rangle\approxeq x
.

Sum

Assume that
F\;X=\sum_{a{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}A}X^{B[a]}
and
F^{\prime}\;X=\sum_{a^{\prime}{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}A^{\prime}}X^{B^{\prime}[a]^{\prime}}
. Then
(F+F^{\prime})\;X
is
\sum_{a^{\prime\prime}{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}A+A^{\prime}}X^{[B,B^{\prime}][a^{\prime\prime}]}
, where
[B,B^{\prime}][a^{\prime\prime}]=B[a]
if
a^{\prime\prime}=\iota_{1}\;a
, and
[B,B^{\prime}][a^{\prime\prime}]=B^{\prime}[a^{\prime}]
if
a^{\prime\prime}=\iota_{2}\;a^{\prime}
.

Remark
.

The following commutative diagram may help to understand the definition of
a^{\prime\prime}{\kern 0.86108pt:\kern 0.86108pt}A+A^{\prime}\vdash[B,B^{\prime}][a^{\prime\prime}]
:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A+A^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
B+B^{\prime}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{2}}

The arrow
B+B^{\prime}\to A+A^{\prime}
is equivalently written as the
A+A^{\prime}
-indexed set
a^{\prime\prime}{\kern 0.86108pt:\kern 0.86108pt}A+A^{\prime}\vdash[B,B^{\prime}][a^{\prime\prime}]
.
Set theoretically,
\sum_{a^{\prime\prime}{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}A+A^{\prime}}X^{[B,B^{\prime}][a^{\prime\prime}]}
is the set of pairs
\left\langle a^{\prime\prime},[f,f^{\prime}]:[B,B^{\prime\prime}]\;a^{\prime\prime}\to X\right\rangle
, where

•

either
a^{\prime\prime}=\iota_{1}[a]
,
\left\langle a,f:B[a]\to X\right\rangle
in
F\;X
, and
\forall x{\kern 0.86108pt:\kern 0.86108pt}B[a]\mathord{.\;}[f,f^{\prime}]\;x=f\;x
, or

•

a^{\prime\prime}=\iota_{2}[a]^{\prime}
,
\left\langle a^{\prime},f^{\prime}:B^{\prime}[a]^{\prime}\to X\right\rangle
in
F^{\prime}\;X
, and
\forall x^{\prime}{\kern 0.86108pt:\kern 0.86108pt}B[a^{\prime}]\mathord{.\;}[f,f^{\prime}]\;x^{\prime}=f^{\prime}\;x^{\prime}
.

In other words, the shape of the sum is the sum of the component shapes, and a labelling function for the sum is the sum of two corresponding component labelling functions.

Product

We have
(F+F^{\prime})\;X=\sum_{\left\langle a,a^{\prime}\right\rangle{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}A\times A^{\prime}}X^{\langlebar B,B^{\prime}\ranglebar[\left\langle a,a^{\prime}\right\rangle]}
, where
F\;X
and
F^{\prime}\;X
are similar to those from the sum, and
\langlebar B,B^{\prime}\ranglebar[\left\langle a,a^{\prime}\right\rangle]=B[a]+B^{\prime}[a^{\prime}]
(the positions of the product is the disjoint union of the component positions).

Remark
.

We prefer to write
\langlebar B,B^{\prime}\ranglebar[a,a^{\prime}]
for
\langlebar B,B^{\prime}\ranglebar[\left\langle a,a^{\prime}\right\rangle]
.
Set theoretically,
\sum_{\left\langle a.a^{\prime}\right\rangle{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}A\times A^{\prime}}X^{\langlebar B,B^{\prime}\ranglebar[a,a^{\prime}]}
is the set of pairs
\left\langle\left\langle a,a^{\prime}\right\rangle,[f,f^{\prime}]:B[a]+B^{\prime}[a^{\prime}]\to X\right\rangle
, where
f:B[a]\to A
and
f^{\prime}:B^{\prime}[a^{\prime}]\to A^{\prime}
. In other words, the shape of the product is the product of the component shapes and a labelling function for the product is the sum of two corresponding component labelling functions.

Exponentiation

Assuming
F\;X=\sum_{a{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}A}X^{B[a]}

is

\sum_{g:C\to A}X^{\sum_{c{\kern 0.43054pt:\kern 0.43054pt}C}B[g\;c]}

Remark
.

An element of

is a pair
\left\langle g,f\right\rangle
consisting of a function

C
and a
C
-indexed function
c{\kern 0.86108pt:\kern 0.86108pt}C\vdash f_{c}:B[g\;c]\to X

for each

in

.

Matching Logic (ML)

Matching logic
[
13
,
9
,
8
]
provides a unifying framework for defining semantics of programming languages.
A programming language is defined in
matching logic as a
logical theory
, i.e., a set of axioms.
The key concept in matching logic is that of
patterns
,
which can be
matched
by certain elements.
By building complex patterns, we can match elements that have
complex structures or certain properties, or both.
The presentation of matching logic in this review section
follows
[
8
]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let us fix two sets
\mathit{EV}
and
\mathit{SV}
.
The set
\mathit{EV}
includes
element variables

x,y,\dots
.
The set
\mathit{SV}
includes

.
A

is a set of
(constant) symbols
, denoted
\sigma,\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots
.
Let us fix a signature
\Sigma
.
The set of
(
\Sigma
-)patterns
is inductively defined as follows:

\displaystyle\varphi::=

\displaystyle\ x\mid X\mid\sigma\mid\varphi_{1}\ \varphi_{2}\mid\bot\mid\varphi_{1}\rightarrow\varphi_{2}\mid\exists x\mathord{.\;}\varphi\mid\mu X\mathord{.\;}\varphi

where in
\mu X\mathord{.\;}\varphi
, called a least-fixpoint pattern,
we require that
\varphi
is positive in
X
,
i.e.,
X
does not occur in an odd number
of times of the left-hand sides of implications
\varphi_{1}\rightarrow\varphi_{2}
.

Definition 3.2
.

A
(matching logic)

-model

consists of

1.

a nonempty

, which we also denote
M
;

2.

an

\_{\mathbin{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}}\_\colon M\times M\to\mathbb{P}(M)
, where

a

\sigma_{M}\subseteq M
as a subset
for
\sigma\in\Sigma
.

Definition 3.3
.

Given

\rho\colon(\mathit{EV}\cup\mathit{SV})\to M\cup\mathbb{P}(M)

,
we inductively define

\left|\varphi\right|_{M,\rho}

\left|x\right|_{M,\rho}=\{\rho(x)\}
for

2.

for
\sigma\in\Sigma

4.

\left|\varphi_{1}\,\varphi_{2}\right|_{M,\rho}=\bigcup_{a_{i}\in\left|\varphi_{i}\right|_{M,\rho},i\in\{1,2\}}a_{1}\mathbin{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\bullet$}}}}}a_{2}

.

5.

7.

\left|\exists x\mathord{.\;}\varphi\right|_{M,\rho}=\bigcup_{a\in M}\left|\varphi\right|_{M,\rho[a/x]}

8.

\left|\mu X\mathord{.\;}\varphi\right|_{M,\rho}=\mathbf{lfp}(A\mapsto\left|\varphi\right|_{M,\rho[A/X]})

where

\rho[a/x]
(resp.

with

(resp.
\rho^{\prime}(X)=A

agreeing with
\rho

in
\mathit{EV}\cup\mathit{SV}\setminus\{x\}
(resp.
\mathit{EV}\cup\mathit{SV}\setminus\{X\}
).
We use

to denote the

We abbreviate

Case Study: Moore Machines

Here we consider an example of (constant) exponential functor, whose ML specification is more tricky.
Moore machines (automata) have the signature given by the functor
M\;X=O\times X^{I}
, where
O
is for outputs and
I
for inputs.

Capturing Final M-Coalgebra in ML Using Container Functors

We first express
M
as a unary container functor:

\displaystyle M\;X

\displaystyle=O\times X^{I}

\displaystyle\approxeq\sum_{o{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}O}X^{\mathbf{0}}\times(\sum_{\star{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}\mathbf{1}}X^{\mathbf{1}})^{\sum_{i{\kern 0.43054pt:\kern 0.43054pt}I}X^{\mathbf{0}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{o{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}O}X^{\mathbf{0}}\times\sum_{g{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}I\to\mathbf{1}}X^{\sum_{i{\kern 0.43054pt:\kern 0.43054pt}I}\mathbf{1}[g\;i]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\left\langle o,g\right\rangle{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}(O\times(I\to\mathbf{1}))}X^{\mathbf{0}[o]+\sum_{i{\kern 0.43054pt:\kern 0.43054pt}I}\mathbf{1}[g\;i]}

\displaystyle\approxeq\sum_{o{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}O}X^{\sum_{i{\kern 0.43054pt:\kern 0.43054pt}I}\mathbf{1}}

\displaystyle\approxeq\sum_{o{\kern 0.60275pt:\kern 0.60275pt}O}X^{I}

\displaystyle=M^{c}\;X

The instantiation of the ML specification for the final coalgebra is as follows:

•

the constructor
\mathit{cons}\in\Sigma
is specified by the following axioms:

\begin{aligned} &\forall o{\kern 0.86108pt:\kern 0.86108pt}O\mathord{.\;}\forall f\mathord{.\;}f\in X^{I}\rightarrow\exists x\mathord{.\;}x\in X\land\mathit{cons}\;\left\langle a,f\right\rangle=x&&\textnormal{({Functional})}\\

•

the destructors
\mathit{out},\mathit{nxt}\in\Sigma
are specified by the axioms:

~~~~\begin{aligned} &\forall o{\kern 0.86108pt:\kern 0.86108pt}O\mathord{.\;}\forall f\mathord{.\;}f\in X^{I}\rightarrow(\mathit{cons}\;\left\langle a,f\right\rangle).\mathit{out}=a&&\textnormal{({No Coconfusion.1)})}\\
&\forall a{\kern 0.86108pt:\kern 0.86108pt}A\mathord{.\;}\forall f\mathord{.\;}f\mathord{.\;}f\in X^{I}\rightarrow(\mathit{cons}\;\left\langle a,f\right\rangle).\mathit{nxt}=f&&\textnormal{({No Coconfusion.2)})}\\

the sort
\nu M^{c}
with final semantics:

~~~~\begin{aligned} \top_{\!\!\nu M^{c}}=\nu X\mathord{.\;}\exists o{\kern 0.86108pt:\kern 0.86108pt}O\mathord{.\;}\mathit{cons}\;\left\langle o,X^{I}\right\rangle&&\textnormal{({No Cojunk})}\end{aligned}

We should have

\top_{\!\!\nu M^{c}}\approxeq\nu X\mathord{.\;}M^{C}\;X

Computing
\top\land\varphi_{M}(\top)\land\varphi^{2}_{M}(\top)\land\varphi^{3}_{M}(\top)\land\cdots
, where

7
Conclusion

The technical experiments reported in this paper show that both the initial models and the final models for polynomial functors can be fully captured in matching logic (ML) using their representation as unary container functors. The ML specification of the polynomial functors is possible due to the fact the sum, product, and function sorts can be specified in ML
[
8
]
, and these specifications are a part of capturing the category of sets
\mathbb{C}
in ML.

A functor represented as a ”classical” polynomial can be translated into a container functor shape using the fact the later are closed under sum, product, and exponential. However, the result could be cumbersome and not easy to handle in matching logic because the construction starts from constant and identity functors. Therefore it is preferable to simplify it using the isomorphisms in the category of sets.

This result can help in defining in ML programming languages using both inductive data-types and coinductive data-types.
Another advantage is given by a better understanding of the abstract constructions from the category theory.
A possibly use is as follows:

•

define in front-end a suitable syntax for data types intended to be defined as initial algebra or final coalgebra;

•

extract the canonical form of the functor underlying the front-end definition;

•

generate the corresponding ML theory;

•

derive the proof principles needed to soundly handle the defined data type.

Future work will focus on the following aspects of the proposed approach:

•

a more formal presentation of the approach;

•

how to capture in ML the iteration principle and the primitive recursive principle;

•

extending the approach to larger classes of functors admitting initial algebras and final coalgebras, e.g., indexed containers
[
4
]
, the bounded natural functors (BNFs) underlying Isabelle/HOL’s datatypes
[
17
]
, or the quotients of polynomial functors, experimentally implemented in Lean
[
6
]
.

Matrix factorizations of generic polynomials
Daniel Erman

Abstract
. We prove that the Buchweitz-Greuel-Schreyer Conjecture on the minimal rank of a matrix factorization holds for a generic polynomial of given degree and strength. The proof introduces a notion of the secondary strength of a polynomial, and uses a variant of the ultraproduct technique of Erman, Sam, and Snowden.

We define the

to be the rank of the free module

\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{coker}\phi+\operatorname{rank}\operatorname{coker}\psi=\operatorname{rank}F

\operatorname{MCM-rank}(f):=\min\left\{\begin{matrix}r\text{ where $(\phi,\psi)$ is a reduced, homogeneous matrix}\\
\text{factorization of $f$ with }\det(\phi)\text{ a scalar multiple of $f^{r}$}\end{matrix}\right\}.

The main idea behind the proof is a spin on the
Ananyan–Hochster Principle
which was implicitly at the heart of
[

[

for an expository discussion of the principle, which we briefly review. The
collective strength
of homogeneous polynomials

is the minimal strength of a

f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
. The Ananyan–Hochster Principle says:
homogenous polynomials
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
of sufficiently high collective strength will behave increasingly like independent variables

x_{1},\dots,x_{r}
. We use a variant of this principle based on the following definition:

Definition 1.3
.

If
f
is a homogeneous polynomial of strength
s
, then the
secondary strength of
f
is the maximal collective strength of
g_{0},g_{1},\dots,g_{s},h_{0},h_{1},\dots,h_{s}
where
f=\sum_{i=0}^{s}g_{i}h_{i}
.

For instance, in characteristic
0
, the power sum
g_{d,n}:=\sum_{i=0}^{n}z_{i}^{d}
has strength
\geq\frac{n-1}{2}
.

We do not know the precise strength of

Thus

Following the logic of the Ananyan–Hochster Principle: as the

will behave increasingly like independent variables, and so

\sum_{i=0}^{s}x_{i}y_{i}

(1)

\begin{matrix}\text{{A polynomial $f$ of strength $s$ will behave increasingly like}}\\
\text{{ a quadratic form of rank $2s+2$ as the secondary strength $\to\infty$.}}\end{matrix}

This is the central idea behind Theorem
1.2
. If

\operatorname{MF-rank}(q)=2^{s}

Theorem 1.4
.

Fix
d\geq 2
and
s\geq 1
. There exists

where, for any homogeneous polynomial
f
of degree
d
, strength
s
, and secondary strength
\geq N

\operatorname{MF-rank}(f)=2^{s}
and
\operatorname{MCM-rank}(f)=2^{s-1}

By noting that

The upper bound

\begin{pmatrix}g_{0}&-g_{1}\\
h_{1}&h_{0}\end{pmatrix}\text{ and }\begin{pmatrix}h_{0}&g_{1}\\
-h_{1}&g_{0}\end{pmatrix}

is such a matrix factorization of

\operatorname{MF-rank}(f)\leq 2^{s}

To prove these, we apply ultraproduct machinery from

Remark 1.5

Invariants like

2.
Notation

We let
k
be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic. Let
S=k[z_{0},z_{1},\dots]
with the standard grading and let
\mathfrak{m}=\langle z_{0},z_{1},\dots\rangle
be the homogeneous maximal ideal. We summarize some basic facts about matrix factorizations; details and background can be found in
[
28
]
. A
matrix factorization
of
f
is a pair of graded morphisms
(\phi,\psi)=(\phi\colon F\to G,\psi\colon G\to F)
where
F
and
G
are finite rank free graded
S
-modules, and where
\phi\circ\psi=f\cdot\operatorname{id}_{G}
and
\psi\circ\phi=f\cdot\operatorname{id}_{F}
. We say that
(\phi,\psi)
is
reduced
if
\operatorname{im}\phi\subseteq\mathfrak{m}G
and
\operatorname{im}\psi\subseteq\mathfrak{m}F
. Throughout the remainder of this paper, we focus entirely on reduced matrix factorizations. We set the
rank of a matrix factorization
to be the rank of
F
, which equals the rank of
G
.
The map
(\phi,\psi)\mapsto\operatorname{coker}\phi
yields a bijection between reduced matrix factorizations of
f
and maximal Cohen-Macaulay modules of
S/f
, up to isomorphism.

The rank of
\operatorname{coker}\phi
can be determined from properties of the matrix factorization
(\phi,\psi)
. If
f=gh
is reducible then
(g)
and
(h)
form a rank
1
matrix factorization, and we will ignore this case from here on. If
f
is irreducible then
\det(\phi)
is a scalar multiple of
f^{r}
, where
r
is the rank of
\operatorname{coker}\phi
over
S/f
; see
[
18
, Remark 2.5(3)]
. Since we are over an algebraically closed field, we can rescale
\phi
so that
\det\phi=f^{r}
on the nose, and we will assume this throughout.

Our main proof uses the graded ultraproduct ring as defined in
[
30
, §4.1]
, and we refer the reader to
[
30
]
for a more detailed discussion or to
[
31
]
for an expository treatment. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
. Following conventions
[
30
]
, we refer to subsets of
\mathcal{F}
as
neighborhoods of
\ast
. Let
\mathbf{R}
denote the graded ultraproduct of
\{k[z_{0},z_{1},,\dots]\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, where each polynomial ring is given the standard grading.

Remark 2.1
.

The key points about ultrafilters and the ring
\mathbf{R}
are the following:

(1)

Every degree
d
element
g\in\mathbf{R}
can be represented by a sequence
(g_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of degree
d
elements
g_{i}\in S
. This is a many-to-one relation, i.e. there are many sequences which correspond to the same element. The equivalence relation is determined by the ultrafilter. Specifically, if we represent
g
as
(g_{i})
then we have that
g=0
if and only if
g_{i}=0
for all

in some neighborhood of
\ast
.

(2)

For any decomposition of
\mathbb{N}
into a finite number of disjoint subsets
\mathbb{N}=\sqcup_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{N}_{i}
, one of the
\mathcal{N}_{i}
is a neighborhood of
\ast
.

For example, say we have a sequence of elements
(a_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and each

is homogeneous of degree
\leq D
. We can decompose
\mathbb{N}=\sqcup_{d=0}^{D}\mathcal{N}_{d}
where
\mathcal{N}_{d}
is the set of indices

such that

. Then one of the
\mathcal{N}_{d}
is a neighborhood of
\ast
. Thus, for some

for all

in some neighborhood. We will use this argument several times.

Remark 3.1

We choose to the space
\mathbf{S}_{d}
of degree

, which is a direct limit of the

Polynomials of degree

\mu=(\mu_{0},\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{s})
be an integer vector satisfying

. Let

be a homogeneous polynomial of degree

; a

where
\deg(g_{i})=\mu_{i}
. For instance, the polynomial

\mathbf{S}_{d,\mu}

\mathbf{S}_{d,\mu}

\pi_{\mu}\colon\mathbf{S}_{\mu_{0}}\times\mathbf{S}_{\mu_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbf{S}_{\mu_{s}}\times\mathbf{S}_{d-\mu_{0}}\times\mathbf{S}_{d-\mu_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbf{S}_{d-\mu_{s}}\to\mathbf{S}_{d}

. In particular,

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix

and
s\geq 0
. Any irreducible component of
\mathbf{S}_{d,s}
has the form
\mathbf{S}_{d,\mu}
for some
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{s})

.

Proof.

Given
d
and
s
, there are only finitely many possibilities for
\mu
, and the union of these

(each of which is irreducible) equals the space of
\mathbf{S}_{d,s}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
d_{1},\dots,d_{r}
be any positive integers. Let
W_{<N}
be the sublocus of
\mathbf{S}_{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbf{S}_{d_{r}}
of tuples
(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})
of collective strength
<N
.
Then
W_{<N}
is contained in a proper Zariski closed subset of
\mathbf{S}_{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbf{S}_{d_{r}}
.

Proof.

Write
W^{(n)}_{<N}
for the locus of tuples
(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})
of degrees
d_{1},\dots,d_{r}
in
k[z_{0},\dots,z_{n}]
of collective strength
<N
. Since collective strength is unchanged under adjoining an extra variable, we have
W_{<N}=\cup_{n}W^{(n)}_{<N}
.
Write
\mathbf{S}_{d_{i}}^{(n)}
for the space of polynomials of degree
d_{i}
in
k[z_{0},\dots,z_{n}]
.
It suffices to show that
W^{(n)}_{<N}
belongs to a proper, Zariski closed subset of
\mathbf{S}_{d_{1}}^{(n)}\times\cdots\times\mathbf{S}_{d_{r}}^{(n)}
for all
n\gg 0
.

Consider the
r\times(n+1)
Jacobian matrix with
(i,j)
entry
\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial z_{j}}
and let
J
be the ideal of
r\times r
minors of this matrix. This ideal is invariant under replacing
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
by elements which generate the same ideal. So if
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
has collective strength
<N
, then some
k

has strength
<N
; and so we may assume that
f_{1}
has strength
<N
without altering
J
. Write
f_{1}=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}g_{i}h_{i}
. Each partial derivative of
f_{1}
belongs to the ideal generated by the

and
h_{i}
(by the product rule) and thus

also belongs to that ideal. Thus

has codimension
\leq 2N
in

, because it belongs to an ideal with
\leq 2N
generators.

We let
Z\subseteq\mathbf{S}_{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbf{S}_{d_{r}}
be the locus of tuples in

where this ideal of Jacobian minors

has codimension
\leq 2N
; and we let

be the locus of tuples in
k[z_{0},\dots,z_{n}]
where this ideal

has codimension
\leq 2N
. Since the codimension of the ideal

is unchanged under adjoining an extra variable, we have

.

It thus suffices to prove that

is Zariski closed and nontrivial for

. We consider the universal family
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathbf{S}_{d_{1}}^{(n)}\times\cdots\times\mathbf{S}_{d_{r}}^{(n)}\times\mathbb{P}^{n}
consisting of tuples
(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})
of degrees

in
k[z_{0},\dots,z_{n}]
and points

such that

. We have a projection

and
Z^{(n)}
is the locus in the base where the fiber has codimension
\leq 2N
. Since

is proper,

is closed by upper semicontinuity of fiber dimensions, e.g.
[

, 05F6]
. For any

which are smooth in codimension

∎

Finally we turn to the corollary that we will use to connect Theorems

and

.

Corollary 3.4
.

For any
N,d
and
\mu
, the subset of

having secondary strength
\geq N
contains a dense open subset of
\mathbf{S}_{d,\mu}
.

Proof.

Let
U\subseteq\mathbf{S}_{d,\mu}
be the locus of
f
of secondary strength
\geq N
. Let

be the dense open subset of
\mathbf{S}_{d_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbf{S}_{d_{r}}
constructed in Lemma
3.3
which misses the locus

of collective strength

. Then

is, by definition, the image of

under the map
\pi_{\mu}
from (
2
). So it suffices to check that
\pi_{\mu}(U^{\prime})
contains a dense open subset. As in the previous argument, we can check this after restricting to a polynomial ring involving a finite number of variables.

Let
\mathbf{S}_{d}^{(n)}
as defined in the proof of Lemma
3.3
and let
\mathbf{S}^{(n)}_{d,\mu}
be the intersection of
\mathbf{S}_{d,\mu}
and
\mathbf{S}^{(n)}_{d}
. In other words,
\mathbf{S}_{d,\mu}^{(n)}
is the set of degree

. Let

4.
Proof of Main Results

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f\in k[z_{0},\dots,z_{n}]
. Neither
e(f)
nor
\operatorname{MF-rank}(f)
nor
\operatorname{MCM-rank}(f)
changes if we view
f\in S=k[z_{0},z_{1},\dots]
.

Proof.

The extension
k[z_{0},\dots,z_{n}]\subseteq S=k[z_{0},z_{1},\dots]
preserves the codimension of ideals; a detailed proof of this fact is in
[
30
, Proposition 3.3]
. Since
e(f)
is defined in terms of the codimension of the Jacobian ideal
J=\langle\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{i}}|i=0,1,\dots,n\rangle
it follows that
e(f)
is invariant upon passage to
S
. Turning now to
\operatorname{MF-rank}(f)
: if we have a reduced matrix factorization
(\phi,\psi)
of
f
over
k[z_{0},\dots,z_{n}]
then the same matrices yield a reduced matrix factorization of
f
over
S
. Conversely, consider a reduced matrix factorization
(\phi,\psi)
of
f
over
S
. Let
(\overline{\phi},\overline{\psi})
be the images of those matrices under the quotient map
S\to k[z_{0},\dots,z_{n}]
where
z_{i}=0
for
i>n
. Then
(\overline{\phi},\overline{\psi})
is a reduced matrix factorization of
f
over
k[z_{0},\dots,z_{n}]
. It follows
\operatorname{MF-rank}(f)
is the same over
k[z_{0},\dots,z_{n}]
or
S
. Since
\det(\phi)=f^{r}
in
S
if and only if
\det(\overline{\phi})=f^{r}
in
k[z_{0},\dots,z_{n}]
, we obtain the desired invariance of
\operatorname{MCM-rank}(f)
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

In the introduction, we noted that we have
\operatorname{MF-rank}(f)\leq 2^{s}
and
\operatorname{MCM-rank}(f)\leq 2^{s-1}
; while the essential idea is that we can simply specialize the matrix factorization of the quadric
x_{0}y_{0}+\cdots+x_{s}y_{s}
, we provide details here for completeness. For any
t
, we choose any homogeneous polynomials
g_{0},\dots,g_{t}
and
h_{0},\dots,h_{t}
where
\deg(g_{i})+\deg(h_{i})=d
for all
i=0,1,\dots,t
; we will build a reduced, homogeneous matrix factorization of
g_{0}h_{0}+\dots+g_{t}h_{t}
. For
t=0
we set
F_{0}=S
and
B_{0}=S(-\deg g_{0})
and we define
\alpha_{0}=(g_{0})
and
\beta_{0}=(h_{0})
. We have that
F_{0}(-d)\overset{\beta_{0}}{\to}G_{0}\overset{\alpha_{0}}{\to}F_{0}
is then a homogeneous, reduced matrix factorization of
g_{0}h_{0}
. For
t>1
, we inductively define
F_{t}=F_{t-1}\oplus G_{t-1}(\deg h_{t})
and
G_{t}=G_{t-1}\oplus F_{t-1}(-\deg g_{t})
, and we define
\alpha_{t}
and
\beta_{t}
as

\bordermatrix{&G_{t-1}&F_{t-1}(-\deg g_{t})\cr F_{t-1}&\alpha_{t-1}&g_{t}\cdot\operatorname{id}\cr G_{t-1}(\deg h_{t})&h_{t}\cdot\operatorname{id}&-\beta_{t-1}\cr}\ \ \text{ and }\ \ \bordermatrix{&F_{t-1}&G_{t-1}(-\deg g_{t})\cr G_{t-1}&\beta_{t-1}&g_{t}\cdot\operatorname{id}\cr F_{t-1}(\deg h_{t})&h_{t}\cdot\operatorname{id}&-\alpha_{t-1}\cr}.

By induction, one verifies that: the product of
\alpha_{t}
and
\beta_{t}
is
g_{0}h_{0}+\dots+g_{t}h_{t}
times the identity matrix; the above matrices induce homogeneous maps; and the ranks of
F_{t}
and
G_{t}
are
2^{t}
. Since each nonzero entry of
\alpha_{t}
or
\beta_{t}
is one of the
g
’s or
h
’s, this matrix factorization is also reduced, and we have thus produced a homogeneous, reduced matrix factorization of
g_{0}h_{0}+\dots+g_{t}h_{t}
of rank
2^{t}
for all
t
. This shows that if
f
has strength
s
, then
\operatorname{MF-rank}(f)
is at most
2^{s}
. Moreover, since
\det(\alpha_{t})=\det(\beta_{t})
and
\det(\alpha_{t}\beta_{t})=f^{\operatorname{rank}F_{t}}=f^{2^{t}}
we conclude that
\det(\alpha_{t})=f^{2^{t-1}}
. It follows that if
f
has strength
s
then
\operatorname{MCM-rank}(f)
is at most
2^{s-1}
.

We now turn to showing that there is some
N
where the following holds: if the collective strength of
g_{0},\dots,g_{s},h_{0},\dots,h_{s}
is at least
N
, then
\operatorname{MF-rank}(f)\geq 2^{s}
. Assume for contradiction that this is not the case. Then we can choose a sequence of collections of polynomials
\mathcal{P}_{j}:=(g_{0,j},\dots g_{s,j},h_{0,j},\dots h_{s,j})
with
j\in\mathbb{N}
where:

•

The collective strength of
\mathcal{P}_{j}
goes to infinity as
j\to\infty
and

•

f_{j}:=\sum_{i=0}^{s}g_{i,j}h_{i,j}
has
\operatorname{MF-rank}(f_{j})<2^{s}
.

We let
F,G_{0},\dots,G_{s},H_{0},\dots,H_{s}
denote the elements in the ultraproduct ring
\mathbf{R}
corresponding to the sequences
(f_{j}),(g_{0,j}),\dots(g_{s,j}),(h_{0,j}),\dots,(h_{s,j})
. Since there are only finitely many positive integers
m<2^{s}
, by Remark
2.1
(2), after passing to a neighborhood of
\ast
, we can assume that
\operatorname{MF-rank}(f_{j})=m<2^{s}
for some fixed
m
and all
j
in the chosen neighborhood of
\ast
. For each
j
in this neighborhood, we choose any reduced matrix factorization
(\phi_{j},\psi_{j})
of
f_{j}
of rank
m
.
Each of the entries of
\phi_{j}
and
\psi_{j}
must be homogeneous of degree at least
1
and most
d-1
(because we started with reduced matrix factorizations); thus by again applying Remark
2.1
(2), after passing to a neighborhood of
\ast
, we can assume that the degree of each entry of
\phi_{j}
and
\psi_{j}
is indepdendent of
j
.

Now we get to the key point: we can take an ultraproduct of these matrix factorizations
(\phi_{j},\psi_{j})
of
f_{j}
to obtain a matrix factorization of
F
. Specifically, we let
\Phi=(\phi_{j})
and
\Psi=(\psi_{j})
be the
m\times m
matrices whose entries are the ultraproducts of the entries of the
\phi_{j}
and
\psi_{j}
matrices. Since
\phi_{j}\cdot\psi_{j}=f_{j}\cdot\operatorname{id}_{S^{m}}=\psi_{j}\cdot\phi_{j}
for all
j
in our neighborhood, these equations pass to the ultraproduct and we have
\Phi\cdot\Psi=F\cdot\operatorname{id}_{\mathbf{R}^{m}}=\Psi\cdot\Phi.
In particular,
(\Phi,\Psi)
is a homogeneous, reduced matrix factorization of
F
of rank
m<2^{s}
.

We now produce the contradiction. The ultrapower ring
\mathbf{R}
is a polynomial ring by
[
30
, Theorem 1.3]
. Moreoever, since the collective strength of
\mathcal{P}_{j}
went to infinity, the elements
G_{0},\dots,G_{s},H_{0},\dots,H_{s}
have infinite collective strength. These elements are thus abstract variables in the polynomial ring
\mathbf{R}
by
[
30
, Theorem 1.3]
, and we have
F=G_{0}H_{0}+\cdots+G_{s}H_{s}
. The degree of
G_{i}
is
\mu_{i}
and the degree of
H_{i}
is

. But the classification of matrix factorizations of quadrics from
[
45
]
does not make use of the grading, and so Knörrer’s classification (when the characteristic of
k
is not
2
) implies that
\operatorname{MF-rank}(F)\geq 2^{s}
. When the characteristic of
k
is
2
, one can instead apply
[
19
, Proposition 3.2]
to obtain
\operatorname{MF-rank}(F)\geq 2^{s}
. We have thus contradicted the conclusion of the previous paragraph.

For the statement about
\operatorname{MCM-rank}(f)
, the argument will be quite similar. We must show that there is some
N
such that if the collective strength of
g_{0},\dots,g_{s},h_{0},\dots,h_{s}
is at least
N
, then
\operatorname{MCM-rank}(f)\geq 2^{s-1}
. Assume that this is not the case. Then we can choose a sequence of collections of polynomials
\mathcal{P}_{j}:=(g_{0,j},\dots g_{s,j},h_{0,j},\dots h_{s,j})
with
j\in\mathbb{N}
where:

•

The collective strength of
\mathcal{P}_{j}
goes to infinity as
j\to\infty
and

•

f_{j}:=\sum_{i=0}^{s}g_{i,j}h_{i,j}
has
\operatorname{MCM-rank}(f_{j})<2^{s-1}
.

The latter condition implies that, for each
j
, there is a matrix factorization
(\phi_{j},\psi_{j})
of
f_{j}
where
\det(\phi_{j})=f_{j}^{r_{j}}
and

. From here, the argument is nearly identical to the one above: by passing to a neighborhood of
\ast
, we can produce homogeneous matrices
(\Phi,\Psi)
over
\mathbf{R}
where
\det(\Phi)=F^{r}
for some

. But

is a quadric in the polynomial ring
\mathbf{R}
, and this would contradict the known results for quadrics
[
45
,
19
]
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By Lemma
3.2
it suffices to prove the claim for an open subset of
\mathbf{S}_{d,\mu}
for each
\mu=(\mu_{0},\dots,\mu_{s})
satisfying
1\leq\mu_{0}\leq\mu_{1}\leq\cdots\mu_{s}\leq\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor
. In other words, we may assume that
f
has a decomposition

with
\deg(g_{i})=\mu_{i}
and prove that the claim holds for a generic such

.

By a simple application of the product rule, the Jacobian ideal
\langle\frac{\partial f}{\partial z_{i}}|i=0,1,\dots\rangle
belongs to the ideal
\langle g_{0},\dots,g_{s},h_{0},\dots,h_{s}\rangle
; this ideal has codimension
\leq 2s+2
in
S
by the Principal Ideal Theorem, and thus it has codimenion
\leq 2s+1
in
S/f
. It follows that the codimension of the singular locus of
S/f
in
S/f
is at most
2s+1
and thus that

. It therefore suffices to prove
\operatorname{MF-rank}(f)\geq 2^{s}
.

Theorem
1.4
implies that there is some
N
such that:
\operatorname{MF-rank}(f)\geq 2^{s}
and
\operatorname{MCM-rank}(f)\geq 2^{s-1}
for any
f\in\mathbf{S}_{d,\mu}
where the collective strength of
g_{0},\dots,g_{s},h_{0},\dots,h_{s}
is at least
N
, and Corollary
3.4
implies the locus of secondary strength
\geq N
in
\mathbf{S}_{d,\mu}
contains a dense open subset. Since

, this implies the desired result.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Theorem
1.4
shows that strength determines
\operatorname{MF-rank}(f)
and
\operatorname{MCM-rank}(f)
for generic
f
, but
Faenzi and Pons–Llopis have pointed out that this cannot hold for abitrary
f
. For instance, they noted the Pfaffian of a general skew-symmetric

matrix of linear forms, in
\geq 6
variables, will have strength
\geq 3
; but the skew-symmetric matrix is part of a rank
6
matrix factorization, and
6<2^{3}
. Similarly, the cokernel of the skew-symmetric matrix is a rank
2<2^{3-1}
MCM module (an Ulrich module, in fact) on the hypersurface.

This provides a counterexample to a question from a prior draft of this paper and raises a question: if
f\in R=k[x_{1,1},\dots,x_{n,n}]
is the determinant of a generic
n\times n
matrix of linear forms, then
f
has a matrix factorization of rank

, namely, the matrix
(x_{i,j})
and its adjoint. By Laplace expansion along the top row, one can see that the strength of
f
is at most
n-1
. Is the strength equal to
n
? If so, this would show that
\operatorname{MF-rank}(f)
and
\operatorname{MCM-rank}(f)
can be arbitrarily far away from

. A similar question can be asked about Pfaffians.

The proof technique raises some new geometric and arithmetic directions to consider. For instance, if the strength of a polynomial

is sufficiently large, then it is known that

is unirational; this follows from the main result of
[
37
]
and various results relating strength with the codimension of the singular locus c.f.
[
1
,
43
]
. But what happens if the strength is small–so that
V(f)
is highly singular–but
f
has large secondary strength?

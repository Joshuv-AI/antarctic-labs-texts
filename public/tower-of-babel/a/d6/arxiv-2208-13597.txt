On the reconstruction of functions from values at subsampled quadrature points
Felix Bartel, Lutz Kämmerer, Daniel Potts, Tino Ullrich

Abstract
. This paper is concerned with function reconstruction from samples. The sampling points used in several approaches are (1) structured points connected with fast algorithms or (2) unstructured points coming from, e.g., an initial random draw to achieve an improved information complexity. We connect both approaches and propose a subsampling of structured points in an offline step. In particular, we start with structured quadrature points (QMC), which provide stable L 2 L_{2} reconstruction properties. The subsampling procedure consists of a computationally inexpensive random step followed by a deterministic procedure to further reduce the number of points while keeping its information. In these points functions (belonging to a RKHS of bounded functions) will be sampled and reconstructed from whilst achieving state of the art error decay. Our method is dimension-independent and is applicable as soon as we know some initial quadrature points. We apply our general findings on the d d -dimensional torus to subsample rank-1 lattices, where it is known that full rank-1 lattices lose half the optimal order of convergence (expressed in terms of the size of the lattice). In contrast to that, our subsampled version regains the optimal rate since many of the lattice points are not needed. Moreover, we utilize fast and memory efficient Fourier algorithms in order to compute the approximation. Numerical experiments in several dimensions support our findings.

2.
Exact quadrature,
L_{2}
-MZ inequalities, and least squares

The problem in this section is the recovery of functions
f\colon D\to\mathds{C}
defined on some domain
D\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
.
For that we have given samples
\bm{f}=(f(\bm{x}^{1}),\dots,f(\bm{x}^{M}))^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathds{C}^{M}
in points
\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}=\{\bm{x}^{1},\dots,\bm{x}^{M}\}\subset D
.
For now, we assume
f
to be an element of a finite-dimensional function space
V\subset L_{2}
with dimension smaller than
M
(oversampling).
Our method of choice to recover the function from its samples is least squares approximation and, as we will see,
L_{2}
-MZ inequalities come into play in a natural way.

At first, for a
\sigma
-finite measure
\nu:D\to\mathds{R}
, we define the space of square-integrable functions
L_{2}=L_{2}(D,\nu)
with

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{L_{2}}\coloneqq\int_{D}f(\bm{x})\overline{g(\bm{x})}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\bm{x})\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{L_{2}}\coloneqq\Big(\int_{D}|f(\bm{x})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\bm{x})\Big)^{1/2}.

Having an inner product we calculate an orthonormal basis
\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}
of
V
and, with that, have an expansion for functions
f\in V
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle f(\bm{x})=\sum_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}a_{k}\eta_{k}(\bm{x})\quad\text{with}\quad\bm{a}=(a_{k})_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}=(\langle f,\eta_{k}\rangle_{L_{2}})_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}.

Now we seek a way to recover the coefficients
\bm{a}
from the samples
\bm{f}
.
The following system of linear equations establishes this connection

(2.2)

\displaystyle\bm{L}\bm{a}=\bm{f}\quad\text{with}\quad\bm{L}\coloneqq\bm{L}_{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}=\begin{pmatrix}\eta_{1}(\bm{x}^{1})&\cdots&\eta_{|I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}|}(\bm{x}^{1})\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\eta_{1}(\bm{x}^{M})&\cdots&\eta_{|I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}|}(\bm{x}^{M})\end{pmatrix}.

Assuming
\bm{L}
has full rank, a stable way to solve this overdetermined system of equations is to minimize the residual
\|\bm{L}\bm{a}-\bm{f}\|_{2}^{2}
.
The solution is obtained by solving the normal equation and the approximation
S_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f
takes the form

\displaystyle(S_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f)(\bm{x})\coloneqq\sum_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}a_{k}\eta_{k}(\bm{x})\quad\text{with}\quad\bm{a}=(\bm{L}^{\ast}\bm{L})^{-1}\bm{L}^{\ast}\bm{f}.

Further, we consider weighted versions of the least squares algorithm.
For a diagonal matrix
\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}=\diag(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{M})\in[0,\infty)^{M\times M}
the weighted least squares approximation
S_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f=S_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}(\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}})f
takes the form

(2.3)

\displaystyle(S_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f)(\bm{x})=\sum_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}a_{k}\eta_{k}(\bm{x})\quad\text{with}\quad\bm{a}=(\bm{L}^{\ast}\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\bm{L})^{-1}\bm{L}^{\ast}\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\bm{f}

where
\bm{a}
is the minimizer of
\bm{a}\mapsto\|\bm{L}\bm{a}-\bm{f}\|_{\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{2}=\|\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}^{1/2}(\bm{L}\bm{a}-\bm{f})\|_{2}^{2}
.
So it is obtained from the unweighted least squares version by a change of measure using the discrete weights
\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}
.

If the singular values of
\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}^{1/2}\bm{L}
are non-zero, we immediately obtain that the least squares algorithm
S_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}
reconstructs all functions
f\in V
.
The stability of the reconstruction as well as the number of iterations
of a conjugate gradient method used to solve the system of equations heavily depends on the condition number of the matrix

[

Lemma 2.1
.

[

\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}^{1/2}\bm{L}
as follows

\displaystyle A\|\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}=\!A\|\overline{\bm{a}}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}^{1/2}\bm{L}\overline{\bm{a}}\|_{2}^{2}=\!\sum_{i=1}^{M}|\langle\bm{a},\sqrt{\omega_{i}}(\eta_{k}(\bm{x}^{i}))_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}\rangle_{2}|^{2}\leq\!B\|\overline{\bm{a}}\|_{2}^{2}=B\|\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}\,.

This immediately shows the equivalence of the two stated conditions.

Step 2.
Now we show the equivalence of (ii) and (iii).
Using the series expansion (
2.1
) of
f
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}|\langle\overline{\bm{a}},\sqrt{\omega_{i}}(\eta_{k}(\bm{x}^{i}))_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}\rangle_{2}|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\omega_{i}\Big|\sum_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}a_{k}\eta_{k}(\bm{x}^{i})\Big|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\omega_{i}|f(\bm{x}^{i})|^{2}

and further using Parseval’s identity, we obtain

\displaystyle\|\overline{\bm{a}}\|_{2}^{2}=\|\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}=\Big\|\sum_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}a_{k}\eta_{k}(\bm{x}^{i})\Big\|_{L_{2}}^{2}=\|f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}\,.

Plugging these two formulas into the frame condition, we obtain a reformulation in terms of functions, which is an
L_{2}
-MZ inequality for the function space
V
with bounds
A
and
B
.
∎

The
L_{2}
-MZ characterization supports the availability of points with well-behaved least squares matrices.
With Lemma
2.2
, using a set of points
\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}
coming from an
L_{2}
-MZ inequality with well-behaved constants, we automatically have a good point set to use in least squares approximation, and vice versa.
Point sets fulfilling
L_{2}
-MZ inequalities are widely available and well-studied, cf.
[
35
,
27
,
14
,
7
,
8
,
44
]
.
Furthermore, the next theorem shows an equivalence to an exact integration condition when the constants in the
L_{2}
-MZ inequality coincide.
This widens the applicability even further, cf.
[
9
,
48
,
23
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

The points
\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}=\{\bm{x}^{1},\dots,\bm{x}^{M}\}\subset D
and weights
\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}=\diag(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{M})\in[0,\infty)^{M\times M}
obey an
L_{2}
-MZ inequality (
2.4
) on the function space
V=\spn\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}
with constants
A=B
if and only if we have exact quadrature on
V\cdot\overline{V}\coloneqq\{f=\overline{g}\cdot h:g,h\in V\}
, i.e., we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{M}\omega_{i}g(\bm{x}^{i})\overline{h(\bm{x}^{i})}=A\int_{D}g(\bm{x})\overline{h(\bm{x})}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\bm{x})\quad\text{for all}\quad g,h\in V\,.

Proof.

Starting with the
L_{2}
-MZ inequality, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{M}\omega_{i}|f(\bm{x}^{i})|^{2}=A\int_{D}|f(\bm{x})|^{2}\;\mathrm{d}\nu(\bm{x})\quad\text{for all}\quad f\in V\,.

By the parallelogram law the corresponding inner products also coincide, which is on direction of the assertion.
The reverse is achieved by using
g=h
.
∎

3.
Subsampling of
L_{2}
-MZ inequalities

In this section we utilize recent random and deterministic frame subsampling techniques from

to select subsets of

-MZ point sets keeping their good approximation properties following the illustration in Figure

The connection from

The best possible start are tight frames, i.e., with frame bounds

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

4.
Sampling recovery in reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces

In this section we tackle the same problem as in Section
2
, namely function recovery from samples, but now for infinite-dimensional function spaces.
We consider functions from separable reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces
H(K)\subset\mathds{C}^{D}
with Hermitian positive definite kernels
K(\cdot,\cdot)
on
D\times D
, where
D\subset\mathds{R}^{d}
is some domain.
Fundamental is the reproducing property

\displaystyle f(\bm{x})=\langle f,K(\cdot,\bm{x})\rangle_{H(K)}

for all
\bm{x}\in D
, which implies that point evaluations are continuous functionals on
H(K)
.
Introductory information about these spaces can be found in
[
3
, Chapter 4]
or
[
6
, Chapter 1]
.
In the framework of this paper, we assume the kernels to be bounded, i.e.,

(4.1)

\|K\|_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}\coloneqq\sqrt{\sup_{\bm{x}\in D}K(\bm{x},\bm{x})}<\infty\,.

With
\|\cdot\|_{H(K)}^{2}=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H_{K}}
, this condition implies
\|f\|_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}\leq\|K\|_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}\cdot\|f\|_{H(K)}
, which, in other words, is the continuous embedding of
H(K)
into the space of essentially bounded functions
\ell_{\infty}(D)
.
Furthermore, we assume the finite trace property

(4.2)

\displaystyle\|K\|_{2}^{2}\coloneqq\int_{D}K(\bm{x},\bm{x})\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\bm{x})<\infty\,,

which, for finite measures, follows from (
4.1
).
This integrability condition ensures the compactness of the embedding operator

\mathrm{Id}\colon H(K)\hookrightarrow L_{2}

which is Hilbert-Schmidt.
Now we consider the compact and self-adjoint linear operator
W=\mathrm{Id}^{\ast}\circ\mathrm{Id}\colon H(K)\to H(K)
.
Applying the spectral theorem, we obtain the representation

\displaystyle Wf=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{k}\langle f,e_{k}\rangle_{H(K)}e_{k}\,,

where
(\lambda_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
is the non-increasing rearrangement of the eigenvalues of
W
and
(e_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset H(K)
the system of eigenfunctions of
W
which form an orthonormal system in
H(K)
.
Using the reproducing property, we obtain a representation of the kernel in terms of the eigenfunctions

(4.3)

\displaystyle K(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\sum_{k\in\mathds{N}}\langle K(\bm{x},\cdot),e_{k}\rangle_{H(K)}e_{k}(\bm{y})=\sum_{k\in\mathds{N}}\overline{e_{k}(\bm{x})}e_{k}(\bm{y})\,.

Further, we have

\displaystyle\langle e_{k},e_{l}\rangle_{L_{2}}=\langle\mathrm{Id}(e_{k}),\mathrm{Id}(e_{l})\rangle_{L_{2}}=\langle W(e_{k}),e_{l}\rangle_{H(K)}=\lambda_{k}\langle e_{k},e_{l}\rangle_{H(K)}=\lambda_{k}\delta_{k,l}\,.

Therefore, with
\eta_{k}(\bm{x})\coloneqq\sigma_{k}^{-1}e_{k}(\bm{x})
where
\sigma_{k}=\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}
are the singular numbers of
\mathrm{Id}
, we obtain an orthonomal system in
L_{2}
.

Remark 4.1
.

For
V=\spn\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\in I}
,
\eta_{k}

L_{2}
-orthonormal, we have for the projection
P_{I}f
defined in (
1.4
)

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{H(K)}\leq 1}\|f-P_{I}f\|_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\bm{x}\in D}\sup_{\|f\|_{H(K)}\leq 1}\Big|\sum_{k\notin I}\langle f,e_{k}\rangle_{H(K)}e_{k}(\bm{x})\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\bm{x}\in D}\sup_{\|\bm{a}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 1}\Big|\sum_{k\notin I}a_{k}e_{k}(\bm{x})\Big|^{2}\,.

Interpreting this as a functional on
\ell_{2}(\mathds{N}\setminus I)
applied to
\bm{a}=(a_{k})_{k\notin I}
, we obtain that the above equals the norm of the Riesz representer
(e_{k}(\bm{x}))_{k\notin I}
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{H(K)}\leq 1}\|f-P_{I}f\|_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}^{2}=\sup_{\bm{x}\in D}\sum_{k\notin I}|e_{k}(\bm{x})|^{2}\,.

4.1.
Sampling recovery with
L_{2}
-MZ points

A reconstruction model using finitely many samples cannot reconstruct every function in an infinite-dimensional function space.
Thus, we make an error.
In particular, for least squares we reconstruct functions
f\in V=\spn\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}
but none in
H(K)\setminus V
.
In this section we quantify the worst-case error of the least-squares method using samples in
L_{2}
-MZ points.
Similar results can be found in
[
16
]
.

We consider the following set of assumptions on the initial points
\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}
and weights
\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}
for the theorems of this section.

Assumption 4.2
.

Let
H(K)
be a separable reproducing kernel Hilbert space with a finite trace (
4.2
) and bounded (
4.1
) kernel.
Further, let
\lambda_{k}
and
\eta_{k}
denote the eigenvalues and
L_{2}
-orthonormal eigenfunctions of the operator
W=\mathrm{Id}^{\ast}\circ\mathrm{Id}
with
\mathrm{Id}\colon H(K)\hookrightarrow L_{2}
the embedding.

For
V=\spn\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}\subset H(K)
a finite-dimensional function space, we assume the points
\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\subset D
and weights
\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\in[0,\infty)^{M\times M}
fulfill an
L_{2}
-MZ inequality for
V
with constants
A
and
B
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let Assumptions
4.2
hold.
Then the error of the reconstruction operator
S_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}=S_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}(\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}})
is zero for functions in
V=\spn\{\eta_{k}\}_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}
and otherwise bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{H(K)}\leq 1}\Big\|f-S_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f\Big\|_{L_{2}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{k\notin I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}\lambda_{k}+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{M}\omega_{i}}{A}\sup_{\|f\|_{H(K)}\leq 1}\|f-P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f\|_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}^{2}\,.

Proof.

The reconstructing property for functions in
V
is immediate.
For functions
f\in H(K)
we use orthogonality of the projection (
1.4
) to obtain

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|f-S_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}=\|f-P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}+\|P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f-S_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}

of which we now estimate each summand individually.

Step 1.

We bound the first summand of (
4.4
) by

\displaystyle\|f-P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{k\notin I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}\langle f,e_{k}\rangle_{H(K)}e_{k}\Big\|_{L_{2}}^{2}=\sum_{k\notin I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}\lambda_{k}|\langle f,e_{k}\rangle_{H(K)}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{H(K)}^{2}\sup_{k\notin I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}\lambda_{k}\,.

Step 2.

For the second summand of (
4.4
) we use the reconstructing property of
S_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}
for functions in
V

\displaystyle\|P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f-S_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}=\|S_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}(P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f-f)\|_{L_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\,\leq\Big\|((\bm{L}_{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}})^{\ast}\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\bm{L}_{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}})^{-1}(\bm{L}_{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}})^{\ast}\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}^{1/2}\Big\|_{2\to 2}^{2}\Big\|\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}^{1/2}\Big((P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f-f)(\bm{x}^{i})\Big)_{i=1}^{M}\Big\|_{2}^{2}\,.

By Lemmata
2.1
and
2.2
we have for the first factor

\displaystyle\Big\|((\bm{L}_{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}})^{\ast}\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\bm{L}_{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}})^{-1}(\bm{L}_{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}})^{\ast}\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}^{1/2}\Big\|_{2\to 2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{A}\,.

Finally, we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{H(K)}\leq 1}\|P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f-S_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{A}\sup_{\|f\|_{H(K)}\leq 1}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\omega_{i}|(P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f-f)(\bm{x}^{i})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{M}\omega_{i}}{A}\sup_{\|f\|_{H(K)}\leq 1}\|P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f-f\|_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}^{2}\,.\qed

The above are rather basic bounds which hold in a general setting.
In special cases like the exponential functions, Chebyshev polynomials, and the half-period cosine functions this topic is examined in detail in
[
32
]
for rank-1 lattices.
In particular for function spaces with mixed smoothness there exist almost tight error bounds in
[
4
]
which improve on Theorem
4.3
.

4.2.
Sampling recovery with subsampled
\mathrm{L}_{2}
-MZ points

The next step is to subsample the
L_{2}
-MZ points like in Section
3
whilst paying attention to the approximation error.
With that we obtain better error bounds and will relate the error using the subsampled points to the error of the initial point set.
In contrast to Section
2
, we subsample two frames and one Bessel sequence simultaneously from the points
\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}
.
In order to control the subsampling we use a change of measure via a convex combination of the respective probability densities.
Each point
\bm{x}^{i}
from
\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}
will be drawn with the following probability density:

(4.5)

\displaystyle\varrho_{i}=\frac{\displaystyle\omega_{i}\sum_{k\in I}|\eta_{k}(\bm{x}^{i})|^{2}}{\displaystyle 3\sum_{j=1}^{M}\omega_{j}\sum_{k\in I}|\eta_{k}(\bm{x}^{j})|^{2}}+\frac{\displaystyle\omega_{i}\sum_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}|e_{k}(\bm{x}^{i})|^{2}}{\displaystyle 3\sum_{j=1}^{M}\omega_{j}\sum_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}|e_{k}(\bm{x}^{j})|^{2}}+\frac{\omega_{i}}{3}\,.

Since
\varrho_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{M}\varrho_{i}=1
, these are proper density weights.
This is based on an idea first used in
[
28
]
.

For the later analysis we need some preparations, starting with a concentration inequality for random infinite matrices from
[
36
]
.

Proposition 4.4

(
[
36
, Proposition 3.8]
)
.

Let
\bm{u}^{1},\dots,\bm{u}^{n}
be i.i.d. random sequences from
\ell_{2}(\mathds{N})
.
Let further
n\geq 3
,
t>0
,
M>0
such that
\|\bm{u}^{i}\|_{2}\leq M
almost surely and
\mathds{E}(\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{u}^{i})=\bm{\Lambda}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
.
Then

\displaystyle\Big\|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{u}^{i}\Big\|_{2\to 2}\leq\frac{21(\log(n)+t)}{n}M^{2}+2\|\bm{\Lambda}\|_{2\to 2}

with probability exceeding
1-2^{3/4}\exp(-t)
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let Assumptions
4.2
hold and let
\bm{X}=\{\bm{x}^{i}\}_{i\in J}
,
|J|=n
, be points drawn i.i.d. from
\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}
with respect to the discrete density weights
\varrho_{i}
, defined in (
4.5
).
Further, for
I\subset I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}
, we define the matrices

\displaystyle\bm{W}=\diag(\omega_{i}/\varrho_{i})_{i\in J}\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{\Phi}^{I^{\prime}}_{\bm{X}}=(e_{k}(\bm{x}^{i}))_{i\in J,k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\,.

Then we have with probability exceeding
1-2^{3/4}\exp(-t)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\Big\|\bm{W}^{1/2}\bm{\Phi}_{\bm{X}}^{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\Big\|_{2\to 2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{63(\log(n)+t)}{n}B\sum_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\lambda_{k}+2B\sup_{k\notin I}\lambda_{k}\,.

Proof.

Since points with probability
\varrho_{i}=0
will not be drawn, we assume
\varrho_{i}>0
.
Let

\displaystyle\bm{L}_{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}=\Big(\eta_{k}(\bm{x}^{i})\Big)_{\bm{x}^{i}\in\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}},k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}=\bm{\Phi}_{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\diag(\sigma_{k}^{-1})_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}.

We want to apply Proposition
4.4
.
To this end we define
\bm{u}^{i}=\sqrt{\omega_{i}/\varrho_{i}}(e_{k}(\bm{x}^{i}))_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}
.
Note,

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in J}\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{u}^{i}=\Big(\bm{\Phi}_{\bm{X}}^{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\Big)^{\ast}\bm{W}\bm{\Phi}_{\bm{X}}^{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\,.

Now we estimate
\|\bm{u}^{i}\|_{2}^{2}
and
\|\mathds{E}\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{u}^{i}\|_{2\to 2}
.

We bound
\|\bm{u}^{i}\|_{2}
by using the
L_{2}
-MZ inequality and
\|e_{k}\|_{L_{2}}^{2}=\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle\|\bm{u}^{i}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 3\sum_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\omega_{j}|e_{k}(\bm{x}^{j})|^{2}\leq 3B\sum_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\|e_{k}\|_{L_{2}}^{2}=3B\sum_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\lambda_{k}\,.

We further have by the compatibility of the spectral norm and Lemma
2.2

\displaystyle\Big\|\mathds{E}\bm{u}^{i}\otimes\bm{u}^{i}\Big\|_{2\to 2}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}^{1/2}\bm{\Phi}_{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\Big\|_{2\to 2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\bm{W}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}^{1/2}\bm{L}_{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}^{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\Big\|_{2\to 2}^{2}\Big\|\diag(\sigma_{k})_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\Big\|_{2\to 2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq B\sup_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\lambda_{k}\,,

which gives the assertion after applying Proposition
4.4
.
∎

We now formulate a central result of the paper, which bounds the worst-case reconstruction error for the least squares method where the points are drawn randomly from a discrete set of points fulfilling an
L_{2}
-MZ inequality, cf. middle of Figure
1.1
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let Assumption
4.2
hold and
I\subset I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\subset\mathbb{N}
,
|I|\geq 3
, be an index set.
For
n\in\mathds{N}
,
t>0
, and
r\geq 1
such that

\displaystyle n\coloneqq\Big\lceil\frac{36B}{A}|I|(\log|I|+t)\Big\rceil\leq|I|^{r},

let
\bm{X}=(\bm{x}^{i})_{i\in J}
,
|J|=n
, be points drawn i.i.d. from
\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}
with respect to the discrete density weights
\varrho_{i}
, defined in (
4.5
).
Then we have with probability exceeding
1-4\exp(-t)

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{H(K)}\leq 1}\!\!\!\|f-S_{I}^{\bm{X}}f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{9B}{A}\sup_{k\notin I}\lambda_{k}+\frac{7r}{|I|}\!\sum_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{12}{A}\sup_{\|f\|_{H(K)}\leq 1}\|f-P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f\|_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}^{2}\,,

where
\bm{W}=\diag(\omega_{i}/\varrho_{i})_{i\in J}
and
S_{I}^{\bm{X}}=S_{I}^{\bm{X}}(\bm{W})
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality let
\varrho_{i}>0
.
We begin by defining two events.
The first one bounds the spectral norm of the least squares matrix

\displaystyle S_{1}=\Big\{\bm{X}\subset\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}:\Big\|\Big(\bm{L}^{\ast}\bm{W}\bm{L}\Big)^{-1}\bm{L}^{\ast}\bm{W}^{1/2}\Big\|_{2\to 2}^{2}\leq\frac{2}{An}\Big\}.

Using Lemma
2.1
, we have, that this is equivalent to a lower bound of the singular values of
\bm{W}^{1/2}\bm{L}
.
Via Lemma
2.2
we use the
L_{2}
-MZ characterization and the assumption on
n
in order to apply Theorem
3.1
with
C=1/3
and obtain that the above holds with probability
1-2\exp(-t)
.

For
\bm{Y}\in\{\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}},\bm{X}\}
we define the matrix

\displaystyle\bm{\Phi}^{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}_{\bm{Y}}=\Big(e_{k}(\bm{x}^{i})\Big)_{\bm{x}^{i}\in\bm{Y},k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\,.

Event
S_{2}
is the inequalities of Lemma
4.5
which holds with probability at least
1-2^{3/4}\exp(-t)
.
Together we obtain the desired probability

\displaystyle\mathds{P}(S_{1}\cap S_{2})\geq 1-\mathds{P}S_{1}^{\complement}-\mathds{P}S_{2}^{\complement}\geq 1-4\exp(-t)\,.

It remains to show the bound based on the events
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
.
On that account we decompose the recovery error using triangle inequality

\displaystyle\|f-S_{I}^{\bm{X}}f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-P_{I}f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}+\|P_{I}f-S_{I}^{\bm{X}}f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq\|f-P_{I}f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}+2\|P_{I}f-S_{I}^{\bm{X}}P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}+2\|S_{I}^{\bm{X}}P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f-S_{I}^{\bm{X}}f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}\,.

In the following we estimate each of the three summands individually.

Step 1.

We estimate the first summand of (
4.2
) in the same manner as in the first step of the proof of Theorem
4.3
:

\displaystyle\|f-P_{I}f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{H(K)}^{2}\sup_{k\notin\mathcal{I}}\lambda_{k}\,.

Step 2.
Using the invariance of
S_{I}^{\bm{X}}
on
P_{I}f
, we estimate the second summand of (
4.2
) by

\displaystyle\Big\|P_{I}f-S_{I}^{\bm{X}}P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f\Big\|_{L_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big\|S_{I}^{\bm{X}}(P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f-P_{I}f)\Big\|_{L_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big\|(\bm{L}^{\ast}\bm{W}\bm{L})^{-1}\bm{L}^{\ast}\bm{W}((P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f-P_{I}f)(\bm{x}^{i}))_{i\in J}\Big\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|(\bm{L}^{\ast}\bm{W}\bm{L})^{-1}\bm{L}^{\ast}\bm{W}^{1/2}\Big\|_{2\to 2}\sum_{i\in J}\frac{\omega_{i}}{\varrho_{i}}|(P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f-P_{I}f)(\bm{x}^{i})|^{2}\,.

We estimate the first factor by the means of event
S_{1}
and write the latter in terms of its coefficients in order to apply event
S_{2}

\displaystyle\Big\|S_{I}^{\bm{X}}P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f-S_{I}^{\bm{X}}f\Big\|_{L_{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{2}{An}\Big\|\bm{W}^{1/2}\bm{\Phi}_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}^{\bm{X}}\Big(\langle f,e_{k}\rangle_{H(K)}\Big)_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\Big\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{2}{An}\Big\|\bm{W}^{1/2}\bm{\Phi}_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}^{\bm{X}}\Big\|_{2\to 2}^{2}\sum_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}|\langle f,e_{k}\rangle_{H(K)}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\Big(\frac{126B(\log(n)+t)}{An}\sum_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\lambda_{k}+\frac{4B}{A}\sup_{k\notin I}\lambda_{k}\Big)\|f\|_{H(K)}^{2}\,.

Step 3.
We start estimating the third summand of (
4.2
) analogously to Step 2

\displaystyle\|P_{I}f-S_{I}^{\bm{X}}P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{An}\sum_{i\in J}\frac{\omega_{i}}{\varrho_{i}}|(f-P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f)(\bm{x}^{i})|^{2}\,.

Now we use the third part of
\varrho_{i}
, to obtain

\displaystyle\|P_{I}f-S_{I}^{\bm{X}}P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6}{An}\sum_{i\in J}|(f-P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f)(\bm{x}^{i})|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{6}{A}\|f-P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f\|_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}^{2}\,.

Overall
, applying the estimates 1. – 3. in (
4.2
) we obtain

\displaystyle\sup_{\|f\|_{H(K)}\leq 1}\|f-S_{I}^{\bm{X}}f\|_{L_{2}}^{2}\leq\sup_{k\notin I}\lambda_{k}+\frac{252B(\log(n)+t)}{An}\sum_{k\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\setminus I}\lambda_{k}+\frac{8B}{A}\sup_{k\notin I}\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{12}{A}\sup_{\|f\|_{H(K)}\leq 1}\|f-P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f\|_{\ell_{\infty}(D)}^{2}\,.

By the assumption on
|I|
we have

\displaystyle\frac{252B(\log(n)+t)}{An}\leq\frac{7(\log(n)+t)}{|I|(\log(|I|)+t)}\leq\frac{7r(\log(|I|)+t)}{|I|(\log(|I|)+t)}=\frac{7r}{|I|}\leq 3\,,

and obtain the assertion.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

(i)

Given a bounded orthonormal system, i.e.,

for all

we can alter events

and

such that we won’t need to sample with respect to the density weights

and sample with respect to the weights

coming from the

(ii)

Whenever the Christoffel-function

is constant, independent of the underlying index set

in (

(iii)

Up to the quotient of the constants for the

-MZ inequality, the first two summands of Theorem

are the same as in
[

]
where points were drawn with respect to a continuous probability measure.
The difference is in the latter summand, which only depends on the initial point set.

-MZ inequality for large enough

In particular smaller than the first two terms, which, therefore, determine the error decay behaviour.

5.
Application: Sampling recovery on
\mathds{T}^{d}
with rank-1 lattices

In this section we apply our general theory from Section
4.2
to the
d
-dimensional torus
\mathds{T}^{d}\simeq(\mathds{R}/\mathds{Z})^{d}
.
First we deal with function spaces on
\mathds{T}^{d}
.
To begin with, we consider a weight function
w(\mathbf{k})\colon\mathds{Z}^{d}\to(0,\infty)
for
\mathbf{k}\in\mathds{Z}^{d}
and define the kernel

(5.1)

\displaystyle K_{w}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}):=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathds{Z}^{d}}\frac{\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}\langle\mathbf{k},(\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{x})\rangle)}{w(\bm{k})^{2}}\quad\text{for}\quad\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}\in{\mathds{T}}^{d}\,,

which is a reproducing kernel for
H^{w}:=H(K_{w})
.
The kernel defined in (
5.1
) is associated to the inner product

(5.2)

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{H^{w}}:=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathds{Z}^{d}}\hat{f}_{\bm{k}}\,\overline{\hat{g}_{\bm{k}}}\,w(\bm{k})^{2}

and the corresponding norm.
These definitions make sense if
\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathds{Z^{d}}}w(\bm{k})^{-2}<\infty
.

An interesting case are Sobolev spaces with mixed smoothness.
For
s\in\mathds{N}
we define the space
H^{s}_{\textnormal{mix}}=H^{s}_{\textnormal{mix}}(\mathds{T}^{d})
as the Hilbert space with the inner product

(5.3)

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle_{H^{s}_{\textnormal{mix}}}:=\sum_{\bm{j}\in\{0,s\}^{d}}\langle D^{(\bm{j})}f,D^{(\bm{j})}g\rangle_{L_{2}}\,,\quad\text{with}\quad D^{(\bm{j})}=\frac{\partial^{\|j\|_{1}}}{\partial x_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{d}^{j_{d}}}\,.

In the univariate case the kernel equals

\displaystyle K^{1}_{s}(x,y):=\sum_{k\in\mathds{Z}}\frac{\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}k(y-x))}{w_{s}(k)^{2}}\quad\text{with}\quad w_{s}(k)=\Big(1+(2\pi|k|)^{2s}\Big)^{1/2}

for
k\in\mathds{Z}
and
x,y\in\mathds{T}
.
This directly leads to the
d
-dimensional kernel

\displaystyle K^{d}_{s}(\bm{x},\bm{y}):=K^{1}_{s}(x_{1},y_{1})\otimes\cdots\otimes K^{1}_{s}(x_{d},y_{d})\quad\text{for}\quad\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathds{T}^{d}\,.

This construction of the kernel extends to
s\in\mathds{R}
,
s>1/2
naturally.
These function spaces are suitable for high-dimensional approximation.
By collecting the most important frequencies with respect to the singular values we end up with a special structure for the frequency index set, namely a hyperbolic cross.
Detailed information on approximation in these spaces can be found in
[
43
,
10
]
.

Next, we deal with
L_{2}
-MZ inequalities on the torus.
In particular we are interested in those coming from exact quadrature rules, cf. Lemma
2.3
.
The canonical example would be equispaced points

\displaystyle\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}=\Big\{\frac{1}{\sqrt[d]{M}}\bm{i}:\bm{i}\in\{1,\dots,\sqrt[d]{M}\}^{d}\Big\}

(assuming
\sqrt[d]{M}
is integer) which fulfill an
L_{2}
-MZ inequality with equal weights
\omega_{i}=1/M
and
A=B
for the frequency index set

I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}=\{-\sqrt[d]{M}/2,\dots,\sqrt[d]{M}/2-1\}^{d}
.
However, these points are unfeasible in high-dimensional approximation if we do not subsample.
Another candidate for quadrature rules suitable for Sobolev spaces with mixed smoothness are rank-1 lattices

\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}\coloneqq\Big\{\frac{1}{M}(i\bm{z}\!\!\!\!\mod M\mathds{1})\in\mathds{T}^{d}:i=0,\dots,M-1\Big\}

which were studied in
[
42
,
21
,
23
,
39
,
11
]
.
We say a rank-1 lattices
\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}=\{\bm{x}^{1},\dots,\bm{x}^{M}\}
has a reconstructing property for a frequency index set
I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}
when

(5.4)

\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\exp(2\pi\mathrm{i}\langle\bm{k}-\bm{l},\bm{x}^{i}\rangle)=\delta_{\bm{l},\bm{k}}\,,

for all
\bm{k},\bm{l}\in I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}
, which is equivalent to an
L_{2}
-MZ inequality (
2.4
) for
A=B
with equal weights
\omega_{i}
.
There exist algorithms which, given a frequency index set
I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}
and
M
, compute a generating vector
\bm{z}
such that the rank-1 lattice
\bm{X}_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}
has this reconstructing property, cf.
[
21
,
32
]
.
In particular, the probabilistic approach recently presented in
[
22
]
seems to be particularly suitable for downstream subsampling. On the one hand the component-by-component (CBC) rank-1 lattice construction is extremely efficient with computational costs that are linear in
|I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}|
up to a few logarithmic factors. On the other hand, the sizes
M
of the resulting rank-1 lattices might be slightly (but only up to a factor of two with very high probability) larger than those resulting from the deterministic approaches. In summary, the CBC construction costs are not a major concern, and random subsampling the rank-1 lattice reduces the number of samples actually used to a number that does not depend on the slightly too large
M
.

Next, we consider asymptotic approximation results for rank-1 lattices in Sobolev spaces with dominating mixed smoothness from
[
4
]
.
For simplicity we omit constants in the following.
For two sequences
(a_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
and
(b_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathds{R}
we write

if there exists a constant

such that
a_{n}\leq cb_{n}
for all
n
.
We will write
a_{n}\asymp b_{n}
if
a_{n}\lesssim b_{n}
and
b_{n}\lesssim a_{n}
.

.
For random points one has the rate
M^{-2s}
and additional logarithmic terms, however.
So, using full rank-1 lattices we lose half the rate of convergence in the main order.
But we recover the order of convergence by subsampling as stated in Corollary
1.2
.

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Since we are dealing with a reconstructing lattice, we have a tight frame
A=B
.
We need to apply Theorem
4.8
and Remark
4.7
and use the following two inequalities

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{k}\notin I}\lambda_{\bm{k}}=\sum_{\bm{k}\notin I}\prod_{j=1}^{d}w_{s}(k_{j})^{-2}\lesssim|I|^{-2s+1}(\log|I|)^{2(d-1)s}

from
[
10
, (2.3.2)]
.
Using Hölder’s inequality and the above inequality again, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f-P_{I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}f\|_{\ell_{\infty}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\sum_{k\notin I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}\lambda_{\bm{k}}^{-1}\lambda_{\bm{k}}|\hat{f}_{\bm{k}}|\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\notin I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}\lambda_{\bm{k}}^{-2}\sum_{k\notin I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}}\lambda_{\bm{k}}^{2}|\hat{f}_{\bm{k}}|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim|I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}|^{-2s+1}(\log|I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}|)^{2(d-1)s}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{\textnormal{mix}}}^{2}\,.\qed

Remark 5.2

(Optimality for rank-1 lattices)
.

With the initial hyperbolic cross
I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}
in Corollary
1.2
slightly bigger than
I
we recover the optimal error bound
n^{-s}(\log n)^{(d-1)s}
, cf.
[
10
, (2.3.2)]
, up to a logarithmic factor.
Thus, the phrase “This main rate (without logarithmic factors) is half the optimal main rate […] and turns out to be the best possible among all algorithms taking samples on lattices” from
[
4
]
has to be stated more precisely to “…samples on
full
lattices”.
We conjecture that the non-constructive approach in
[
13
]
(based on Kadison-Singer and Weaver subsampling
[
37
]
) may lead to a bound without additional logarithm.

Here, we use hyperbolic crosses for the frequency index sets with size

, cf. Theorem

(iii).
Using the Fast Fourier Transform this yields the following complexity for the matrix-vector product with the full rank-1 lattice and, subsequently, the subsampled one:

\mathcal{O}(M\log M)=\mathcal{O}(|I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}|^{2}(\log|I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}|)^{1-d})

In contrast, if we naively set up the matrix with the

If we further use

\mathcal{O}(|I_{\mathrm{\tiny{MZ}}}|^{2})
, which is still slower than using the Fast Fourier Transform.
In general, whenever we start with a rank-1 lattice with fewer than quadratic points, we gain computation speed.

Moduli space of simple polygons
Ahtziri González, Manuel Sedano-Mendoza

Abstract
In this work we study the space S ⁡ ( n ) S(n) of positively oriented, simple n n -gons with labeled vertices up to oriented similarity and ℳ ⁡ ( n ) {\mathcal{M}}(n) the moduli space of simple n n -gons as a quotient of S ⁡ ( n ) S(n) . We give a local description of S ⁡ ( n ) S(n) and ℳ ⁡ ( n ) {\mathcal{M}}(n) around the regular n n -gon and describe completely the two spaces when n = 4 n=4 using a topological Morse function. We also provide an asymptotic description of the compactifications S ⁡ ( 4 ) ¯ \overline{S(4)} and ℳ ⁡ ( 4 ) ¯ \overline{{\mathcal{M}}(4)} up to a complicated set at their boundaries.



Keywords:
Polygons; Similarity; Quadrilaterals; Moduli space; Compactification.

, the existence or non-existence of global regularizing flows in differential geometry

,
[

]
,
[

]
, geometric constructions on spaces of polyhedrons
[

]
and
[
17
]
, compactifications of other Moduli spaces
[

]
,
[
20
]
. In addition to this, the study of spaces of polygons have consecuences on physics and engineering, with applications such as robotic motion planing and pattern recognition
[
25
]
,
[
26
]
. The study of the geometry and topology of spaces of polygons with fixed properties is interesting by itself. Examples of this are the study of spaces of polygons with fixed side lengths
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
, fixed angles
[
23
]
and star shaped polygons
[
24

.

In this work we study the space of simple
n
-gons, with labeled vertices, modulo oriented similarity. More precisely, the set of simple
n
-gons is naturally embedded in the complex projective space
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-2}
, having two homeomorphic connected components, corresponding to positively and negatively oriented
n
-gons respectively, see Section
2
. The connected component of positively oriented polygons is denoted here as
S(n)
and as an open subset of
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-2}
,
S(n)
has a structure of an analytic manifold. We propose the following conjecture

Conjecture 1.1
.

S(n)
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{2n-4}
.

There is a parallelism between the space
S(n)
and the concept of Teichmüller space of Riemann surfaces, and between its symmetry group
D_{n}
(isomorphic to the dihedral group) and the modular group of the corresponding surface, see Remark
2.6
. Motivated by this, we refer to the quotient
{\mathcal{M}}(n)=S(n)/D_{n}
as the moduli space of simple polygons. If
C(X)=\big(X\times(0,1]\big)/(X\times\{1\})
denotes the open cone over a topological space
X
, then by linearizing the action of the dihedral group around the regular polygon (i.e. the polygon whose sides and angles are all equal), we can recover the local result

Theorem 1.2
.

There is a neighborhood
U\subset{\mathcal{M}}(n)
around the regular
n
-gon such that
U
is homeomorphic to
C(\mathbb{S}^{2n-5}/\langle\Sigma,T\rangle)
, where

(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n-2})\overset{\Sigma}{\longrightarrow}(e^{4\pi i/n}z_{1},e^{6\pi i/n}z_{2},\dots,e^{2\pi i(n-1)/n}z_{n-2}),\quad\text{and}

(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n-2})\overset{T}{\longrightarrow}-(e^{-4\pi i/n}\overline{z}_{1},e^{-6\pi i/n}\overline{z}_{2},\dots,e^{-2\pi i(n-1)/n}\overline{z}_{n-2}).

We conjecture that such result holds globally.

Conjecture 1.3
.

{\mathcal{M}}(n)
is homeomorphic to
C(\mathbb{S}^{2n-5}/\langle\Sigma,T\rangle)
.

General strategy.
Conjectures
1.1
and
1.3
can be proven independently, but a strategy to prove both simultaneously is to find a differentiable Morse function in
S(n)
with a unique singular point at the regular polygon and whose level sets are invariant under the action of the symmetry group
D_{n}
. In fact, we can observe that proving the cone structure on
S(n)
and constructing a Morse function with a unique singular point are equivalent problems in the differentiable category. Moreover, the existence of such Morse function implies the existence of a regularizing flow on
S(n)
(its gradient flow), that is, a flow taking each polygon onto the regular one withouth loosing the properties of being simple and withouth collapsing vertices. To the knowledge of the authors, the existence of such regularizing flow remains open besides numerous attempts.

In this work, we propose a height function
h:\overline{S(4)}\subset\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{2}\rightarrow[0,1]
, which is a Morse function in the topological sense (see
[
11
]
,
[
12
]
and
[
9
]
), which has a unique maximum
h=1
at the regular quadrilateral (square) and whose level sets are homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{3}
and are invariant under the action of the dihedral group, leading to the following

Theorem 1.4
.

There is a homeomorphism between
S(4)
and the open cone
C(\mathbb{S}^{3})\cong\mathbb{R}^{4}
, such that the height function
h
corresponds to the function
C(\mathbb{S}^{3})\rightarrow(0,1]
, induced from the projection
\mathbb{S}^{3}\times(0,1]\rightarrow(0,1]
.

In
[
3
]
it was already proven that
S(4)
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{4}
and a description of the boundary
\partial S(4)\subset\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{2}
was given by gluing a polyhedron, making evident that the closure
\overline{S(4)}
is complicated, in particular it is not a manifold. The advantage of the approach given here, is that the cone structure on
S(4)
is invariant under the action of the dihedral group, leading to the following result which was out of reach with the techniques of
[
3
]
.

Corolary 1.5
.

Conjectures
1.1
and
1.3
are true for
n=4
.

Difficulties.
Here, we are calling
h
a “Morse function”, however, it is not easy to prove this statement. In fact, it seems to the authors that it is equally difficult to prove that the height function
h
is a Morse function than to prove Theorem
1.4
directly and then conclude that
h
is a Morse function from there. The most problematic regions are the neighborhoods of the square, where there are a lot of quadrilaterals with different symmetries and where the height function loses differentiability (a phenomenon present in other constructions were symmetries cause problems, such as in
[
1
]
). To prove Theorem
1.4
, we may attempt to give a family of homeomorphisms
f_{s}:h^{-1}(s)\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{3}
which vary continuously on the parameter
s
, and then construct the homeomorphism
S(4)\rightarrow C(\mathbb{S}^{3})
as
Z\mapsto[f_{h(Z)}(Z),h(Z)]
. This strategy is hard to carry out and it seems to the authors that the construction of such a continuous family of homeomorphisms equals the difficulty of the construction of a regularizing flow, which theoretically exists precisely by Theorem
1.4
. We overcome this difficulty by splitting
S(4)
into subsets where the height function
h
behaves well (see Remark
5.6
for instance) and where we are able to construct the continuous family of homeomorphisms, thus obtaining a cone structure on every such set. With the previous partition, the problem translates into understanding the combinatorics of the intersections of the sets, which we give explicitly. The last main difficulty we have to face is on the compactifications
\overline{S(4)}
and
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}(4)}
, where there are some bad convergence phenomena. We face this difficulty by identifying the problematic region which gives bad behaviour at the boundary and prove that we have a very good asymptotic behaviour outside of it

Theorem 1.6
.

There exists a subspace
{\mathcal{D}}\subset\overline{S(4)}
such that
\overline{S(4)}\smallsetminus{\mathcal{D}}\cong\mathbb{H}^{4}\sqcup\mathbb{H}^{4}
, where
\mathbb{H}^{4}
denotes the fourth-dimensional, closed upper half-space. The space
{\mathcal{D}}
is an open cone over the two sphere
{\mathcal{D}}\cap S(4)\cong C(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, compactified with a space
{\mathcal{D}}\cap\partial S(4)
, which is obtained as a union of a wedge of spheres
\mathbb{S}^{2}\vee\mathbb{S}^{2}
and a closed disc
\mathbb{D}^{2}
.

As these constructions are invariant under the action of the dihedral group, we obtain the asymptotic description of the Moduli space

Theorem 1.7
.

There is a subspace
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\subset\overline{{\mathcal{M}}(4)}=\overline{S(4)}/D_{4}
, that is homeomorphic to an open cone over the
2
-disc
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\cap{\mathcal{M}}(4)\cong C(\mathbb{D}^{2})
, compactified with a topological disc
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\cap\partial{\mathcal{M}}(4)\cong\mathbb{D}^{2}
, so that the complement
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}(4)}\smallsetminus{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{H}^{4}
.

As the subset
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}
in Theorem
1.7
is capturing the bad asymptotic behaviour, the compactification of the open cone
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\cap{\mathcal{M}}(4)
with the topological disc
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}\cap\partial{\mathcal{M}}(4)
is complicated, see Remark
7.19
.

It is worth noting that there have been previous uses of Morse functions on spaces of polygons, by considering parameters such as side lengths
[
18
]
or diagonals
[
19
]
. Those functions don’t work here, because they don’t capture the asymptotic behaviour to the boundary
\partial S(n)
. There is however a Morse function on Teichmüller spaces of Riemann surfaces whose behaviour resembles much more to the one presented here, which is the systole
[
10
]
.

2
Preliminaries

A polygon with
n
sides (or simply an
n
-gon) in the complex plane with consecutive vertices at
z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
, is completely determined by the point
(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. In this way, the set of
n
-gons in
\mathbb{C}
with labeled vertices is equipped with the topology of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For all
Z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
\mathfrak{c}(Z)
denotes the polygonal curve
\overline{z_{1}z_{2}}\cup\overline{z_{2}z_{3}}\cup\cdots\cup\overline{z_{n-1}z_{n}}\cup\overline{z_{n}z_{1}}
, where
\overline{ab}\subset\mathbb{C}
is the segment from
a
to
b
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
Z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a
n
-gon.

(a)

We say that
Z
is simple if its vertices are different and
\mathfrak{c}(Z)
is a Jordan’s curve.
\mathbb{S}(n)\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
denotes the set of simple
n
-gons.

(b)

If
Z
is simple, then we say that
Z
is positively (negatively) oriented if the increasing order in the vertices of
\mathfrak{c}(Z)
is in counter-clockwise (clockwise).

In
[
2
]
it is proved that
\mathbb{S}(n)
is an open subset and has two homeomorphic connected components, corresponding to positively and negatively oriented
n
-gons. The group of complex affine transformations on
\mathbb{C}
, denoted here as
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}
, acts diagonally on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
as

(az+b,(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}))\mapsto(az_{1}+b,\dots,az_{n}+b),

preserving angles, labeled vertices and proportional side lengths in the
n
-gons. The quotient
P(n):=\left(\mathbb{C}^{n}\smallsetminus\{(z,z,\dots,z)\}\right)/{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}
is the
space of shapes
of
n
-gons with labeled vertices up to oriented similarity as considered in
[
8
]
. Notice that two equivalent
n
-gons in the linear subspace
V_{n}=\{(0,z_{2},\dots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\}
differ by just a complex factor, so the space
P(n)
is a complex projective space

P(n)\cong\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}V_{n}\cong\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-2}.

The action of
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}
preserves orientation and the property of being simple in
n
-gons, so if
\eta\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\smallsetminus\{(z,z,\dots,z)\}\to P(n)
denotes the natural projection, then
\eta\left(\mathbb{S}(n)\right)\subset P(n)
is open and has two connected components, corresponding to positively and negatively oriented
n
-gons. Denote by
S(n)\subset\eta(\mathbb{S}(n))
the connected component of positively oriented shapes of
n
-gons and denote by
\eta(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n})=[z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n}]\in S(n)
the corresponding class of an
n
-gon.

The linear isomorphism
\widehat{\sigma}(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n})=(z_{2},\dots,z_{n},z_{1})
, re-enumerates the vertices of an
n
-gon in a cyclic way. Notice that two
n
-gons
Z,W\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
have the same shape if and only if
\widehat{\sigma}(Z)
and
\widehat{\sigma}(W)
have the same shape, so there exists a function
\sigma\colon P(n)\to P(n)
such that
\sigma\circ\eta=\eta\circ\widehat{\sigma}
.

Remark 2.2
.

We may observe that, as the diagonal
\{(z,z,\dots,z)\}
is not considered in
P(n)
, at least two vertices of a polygon in
P(n)
must be different. So if we consider the set
U_{12}=\{[z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n}]\in P(n)\colon z_{1}\neq z_{2}\}
and the coordinate chart
\phi_{12}\colon U_{12}\to\mathbb{C}^{n-2}
, given by

\phi_{12}\left([z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n}]\right)=\left(\frac{z_{3}-z_{1}}{z_{2}-z_{1}},\dots,\frac{z_{n}-z_{1}}{z_{2}-z_{1}}\right),

then for every
Z\in P(n)
,
\sigma^{k}(Z)\in U_{12}
, for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. That is, up to re-enumeration of the vertices,
P(n)
is contained in
U_{12}
. Through the paper, we will mostly work with representatives
[0,1,z_{3},\dots,z_{n}]\in U_{12}
.

Example 2.3

(Triangles)
.

The space
P(3)
is biholomorphic to the Riemann sphere. There are three options for a triangle
[0,1,x+iy]\in U_{12}
:

(a)~~y>0,\qquad\quad(b)~~y<0,\qquad\text{~~and~~}\qquad~(c)~~y=0.

In the plane
\phi_{12}(U_{12})
, the case
(a)
is the upper half plane and corresponds to the set
S(3)
of positively oriented triangles; the case
(b)
is the lower half plane and corresponds to the set of negatively oriented triangles; and the case
(c)
, which corresponds to triangles degenerated to a segment, compactifies to a circle in
P(3)
with the degenerated triangle
[0,0,1]
. Thus, the set of degenerated triangles is a circle that splits the sphere
P(3)
into two discs, corresponding to positively and negatively oriented triangles.

The involution
\tau\colon U_{12}\to U_{12}
, which reflects the last
(n-2)
-vertices trough the line
\textrm{Re}(z)=\frac{1}{2}
, changes the order in which the interior angles are presented but without changing the orientation and can be written as

\tau([0,1,z_{3},\dots,z_{n}])=[0,1,1-\bar{z}_{n},\dots,1-\bar{z}_{3}].

Since
U_{12}\subset P(n)
is dense (Remark
2.2
), then
\tau
can be continuously extended to an involution of
P(n)
.

Proposition 2.4
.

The functions
\sigma,\tau\colon P(n)\to P(n)
are diffeomorphisms and the group generated by them
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
is isomorphic to the dihedral group of order
2n
.

Proof.

As the funciton
\sigma
is defined by the property
\sigma\circ\eta=\eta\circ\widehat{\sigma}
, with
\widehat{\sigma}(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n})=(z_{2},\dots,z_{n},z_{1})
and
\eta\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\smallsetminus\{(z,z,\dots,z)\}\to P(n)
the natural projection, the differentiability of
\sigma
follows from the differentiability of
\widehat{\sigma}
. Analogously, if we define
\widehat{\tau}\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
as
\widehat{\tau}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})=-(\overline{z_{2}},\overline{z_{1}},\overline{z_{n}},\dots,\overline{z_{3}})
, then
\sigma\circ\tau=\eta\circ\widehat{\tau}
and the differentiability of
\tau
follows from the differentiability of
\widehat{\tau}
. Finally, it is straightforward to check that
\sigma^{n}=\tau^{2}=(\tau\sigma)^{2}=1
. Then
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
is isomorphic to the dihedral group of order
2n
.
∎

Just as the action of the group
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
eliminates translations, rotations and homotheties in
n
-gons, the action of the dihedral group
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
in
P(n)
, removes the labels at the vertices and identifies
n
-gons equivalent under reflections.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\overline{S(n)}\subset P(n)
denote the closure of
S(n)
. Then

(a)

the quotient
{\mathcal{M}}(n)=S(n)/\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
is the Moduli Space of
n
-gons.

(b)

the quotient
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}(n)}=\overline{S(n)}/\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
is a compactification of
{\mathcal{M}}(n)
.

Remark 2.6
.

A different description of the space
S(n)
, is to consider it as the set of graph isomorphisms
\mathfrak{R}_{n}\rightarrow Z
(where
\mathfrak{R}_{n}
is the regular
n
-gon and
Z
is an arbitrary simple polygon), with the action of the dihedral group acting via precomposition. This approach creates a parallelism between the concept of Teichmüller space and
S(n)
, and between the modular group of a surface and the dihedral group as a symmetry group of
S(n)
(see
[
31
]
for this and a similar construction for the group of outer automorphisms
Out(F_{n})
). Moreover, by construction, the action of the dihedral group extends to the closure
\overline{S(n)}\subset\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{n-2}
, which is a well behaved property for this parallelism and a good justification to consider
\overline{S(n)}/\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
as the compactification of
{\mathcal{M}}(n)
, see
[
30
]
.

Local description of
{\mathcal{M}}(n)

In this section we describe the topology of
{\mathcal{M}}(n)
around the regular
n
-gon and explain in detail the construction for triangles and quadrilaterals.

Let
\mathfrak{R}_{n}=[1,e^{2i\pi/n},\dots,e^{2i\pi(n-1)/n}]\in S(n)
be the shape of the regular
n
-gon. It is straightforward to check that
\mathfrak{R}_{n}=\tau(\mathfrak{R}_{n})=\sigma(\mathfrak{R}_{n})
, and in fact,
\mathfrak{R}_{n}
is the only element in
S(n)
that is fixed by
\sigma
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
D_{\mathfrak{R}_{n}}\sigma
and
D_{\mathfrak{R}_{n}}\tau
be the differentials of
\sigma
and
\tau
at
\mathfrak{R}_{n}
. Then there is a basis
\{\zeta_{2},\dots,\zeta_{n-1}\}
of the complex vector space
T_{\mathfrak{R}_{n}}S(n)
such that

D_{\mathfrak{R}_{n}}\sigma\Big(\!\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}s_{k}\zeta_{k}\!\Big)\!=\!\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}e^{2\pi ik/n}s_{k}\zeta_{k},~\text{and}~~D_{\mathfrak{R}_{n}}\tau\Big(\!\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}s_{k}\zeta_{k}\!\Big)\!=\!\sum_{k=2}^{n-1}-e^{-2\pi ik/n}\overline{s_{k}}\zeta_{k}\,.

Proof.

Let
B(Z,W)=\sum_{j}z_{j}\overline{w}_{j}
denotes the standard Hermitian product in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Consider the basis
\beta=\big\{\widehat{\zeta}_{k}=(1,e^{2\pi ik/n},(e^{2\pi ik/n})^{2},\dots,(e^{2\pi ik/n})^{n-1})\big\}
with
k=0,1,\dots,n-1
, of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. The products between these vectors are

B(\widehat{\zeta}_{k},\widehat{\zeta}_{l})=\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\left(e^{\frac{2\pi i(k-l)}{n}}\right)^{m}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcr}n&\text{if}&k=l\\
0&\text{if}&k\neq l\end{array}\right.

and therefore,
\beta
is an orthogonal basis. Let
V=\{(z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\colon z_{1}+z_{2}+\cdots+z_{n}=0\}
be the complex vector subspace of
n
-gons that have center of mass at the origin. Note that
V=\langle\widehat{\zeta}_{1},\widehat{\zeta}_{2},\dots,\widehat{\zeta}_{n-1}\rangle
,
\mathbb{C}^{n}=\langle\widehat{\zeta}_{0}\rangle\oplus V
,
\eta(V)=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{C}}V=P(n)
and
\eta(\widehat{\zeta}_{1})=\mathfrak{R}_{n}
. Since
\langle\widehat{\zeta}_{1}\rangle
is the kernel of the differential
D_{\widehat{\zeta}_{1}}\eta\colon V\to T_{\mathfrak{R}_{n}}P(n)
, then the restriction

D_{\widehat{\zeta}_{1}}\eta\colon\langle\widehat{\zeta_{2}},\cdots,\widehat{\zeta}_{n-1}\rangle\to T_{\mathfrak{R}_{n}}S(n)

is a linear isomorphism.

Let
\widehat{\tau}\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
be the function
(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\mapsto-(\overline{z_{2}},\overline{z_{1}},\overline{z_{n}},\dots,\overline{z_{3}})
. Since
\widehat{\sigma}(\widehat{\zeta}_{k})=e^{2ik\pi/n}\widehat{\zeta}_{k}
and
\widehat{\tau}(\widehat{\zeta}_{k})=-e^{-2ik\pi/n}\widehat{\zeta}_{k}

V
and
\langle\widehat{\zeta_{2}},\dots,\widehat{\zeta}_{n-1}\rangle
are invariant under
\widehat{\sigma}
and
\widehat{\tau}
, moreover,
\widehat{\sigma}
and
\widehat{\tau}
satisfy
\eta\circ\widehat{\sigma}=\sigma\circ\eta
and
\eta\circ\widehat{\tau}=\tau\circ\eta
. Differentiating in
\widehat{\zeta}_{1}
, we obtain

D_{\widehat{\zeta}_{1}}\eta\circ\widehat{\sigma}=D_{\mathfrak{R}_{n}}\sigma\circ D_{\widehat{\zeta}_{1}}\eta\,,\quad\text{and}\quad D_{\widehat{\zeta}_{1}}\eta\circ\widehat{\tau}=D_{\mathfrak{R}_{n}}\tau\circ D_{\widehat{\zeta}_{1}}\eta\,.

The proof is finished by taking
\zeta_{k}=D_{\widehat{\zeta}_{1}}\eta(\widehat{\zeta_{k}})
, for

∎

Let

Theorem 3.2

Local neighbourhoods of

Proof.

Since the maps

4
Height function in quadrilaterals

In this section, we define the height function in the space of simple quadrilaterals, which will be used to describe its topology, and give some of its properties. We also describe the analogous function in the case of triangles and use it to describe its topology.

Definition 4.1
.

For every
Z=(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4})\in\mathbb{C}^{4}
,

(a)

the longest side function is
\ell\colon\mathbb{C}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}
,
\ell(Z)=\max_{1\leq j\leq 4}|z_{j}-z_{j+1}|
, and

(b)

the minimum distance function is
r\colon\mathbb{C}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}
,
r(Z)=\min_{1\leq j\leq 4}\{r_{j}(Z)\}
, where
r_{j}(Z):=d(z_{j},{\mathcal{L}}_{j})
with
{\mathcal{L}}_{j}:=\overline{z_{j+1}z_{j+2}}\cup\overline{z_{j+2}z_{j+3}}
.

Notice that the functions
\ell
and
r
are continuous and non-negative. Moreover,
r(Z)\leq\ell(Z)
for all
Z\in\mathbb{C}^{4}
, and
\ell(Z)=0
if and only if
Z=(z,z,z,z)
, for some
z\in\mathbb{C}
. Thus, the function
h\colon\mathbb{C}^{4}\smallsetminus\{(z,z,z,z)\}\to\mathbb{R}
defined as
h(Z)=\frac{r(Z)}{\ell(Z)}
is continuous and
0\leq h(Z)\leq 1
. For all
Z\in\mathbb{C}^{4}\smallsetminus\{(z,z,z,z)\}
and
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
with
a\neq 0
, the properties

r(aZ+b)=|a|r(Z)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\ell(aZ+b)=|a|\ell(Z)

hold, so that
h
is invariant under the action of the complex affine group
{\mathcal{A}}_{\mathbb{C}}
on
\mathbb{C}^{4}
. We conclude that there is a well defined and continuous function
h\colon P(4)\to\mathbb{R}
in the space of shapes of quadrilaterals.

Definition 4.2
.

The height function in the space of shapes of simple and positively oriented quadrilaterals is the restriction
h\colon S(4)\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by

h[z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4}]=\frac{r(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4})}{\ell(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4})}.

Proposition 4.3
.

The height function
h\colon S(4)\to\mathbb{R}
is positive and has a global maximum in the square
\mathfrak{R}_{4}=[0,1,1+i,i]
, where
h
equals
1
.

Proof.

It is straightforward to check from Definition
4.1
that
h(\mathfrak{R}_{4})=1
and
h(Z)>0
, for every
Z\in S(4)
. If
Z
has one side strictly smaller than the others, say
|z_{j}-z_{j+1}|
for some
j\in\{1,2,3,4\}
, then
r(Z)\leq r_{j}(Z)\leq|z_{j}-z_{j+1}|<\ell(Z)
, and therefore,
h(Z)<1
. If all of the sides of
Z\in S(4)
have equal length (we may choose a representative whose sides are equal to 1), then it is a rhombus. If
Z\neq\mathfrak{R}_{4}
, then there exists
j\in\{1,2,3,4\}
, such that the interior angle at the vertex
z_{j}
satisfies
0<\theta_{j}<\frac{\pi}{2}
which implies that
h(Z)=r(Z)=r_{j+1}(Z)=\sin(\theta_{j})<1
.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

The height function
h\colon S(4)\to\mathbb{R}
is continuously extended to the closure
\overline{S(4)}
such that
h(Z)=0
if and only if
Z\in\partial S(4)

Proof.

For every
Z=[z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4}]\in\overline{S(4)}
, we have that
h(Z)=0
if and only if there is an index
j\in\{1,2,3,4\}
, such that
r_{j}(Z)=d(z_{j},{\mathcal{L}}_{j})=0
, with
{\mathcal{L}}_{j}=\overline{z_{j+1}z_{j+2}}\cup\overline{z_{j+2}z_{j+3}}
the opposite polygonal to the vertex
z_{j}
. Thus
h(Z)=0
if and only if
z_{j}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{j}
, for some
j\in\{1,2,3,4\}
, and this is true if and only if
Z\in\partial S(4)
. We may observe that the property
\ell(Z)>0
holds for every
Z\in\overline{S(4)}
.
∎

Level sets of the height function

In this section we prove that for all

1.

Introduce the sets in

and

are uniquely reached, this is Definition

there are three such sets, completely determined by

2.

Show that for all

, the level set

, intersected with the closure of each of the sets in the previous step, is homeomorphic to the closed ball
\mathbb{D}^{3}

. In the case of

3.

Describe the decomposition of

. The corresponding behaviour in

is the fact that
h^{-1}(s)
, as a union of closed segments, is a topological triangle, and the non-trivial intersection of the segments are described by the dual polygon which is again a triangle.

4.

Conclude that
h^{-1}(s)\cong\mathbb{S}^{3}
by constructing a decomposition of
\mathbb{S}^{3}
into closed balls which is equivalent to the one given for

. This is given in Theorem

.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
Z=(z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4})\in\mathbb{S}(4)
. Then

(a)

we say that

is unique if there exists

and for

.

(b)

we say that

and

, such that
r(Z)=|z_{j}-w|
and
r(Z)<|z_{k}-w^{\prime}|

and

such that

.

Notice that in a quadrilateral,
r
cannot be realized as the distance between two opposite vertices, because if two non-adjacent vertices are close, then

is reached between one of them and a side non-adjacent to it. However,

Proposition 5.2
.

If
Z\in S(4)

Remark 5.8
.

Three consequences of the proof of Lemma
5.7
.

I
.

For
1\leq j\leq 4
, the sets
\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{j}),\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{V}}_{j}),\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{W}}_{j})
are
I
-bundles with basis homeomorphic to the disc
\mathbb{D}^{2}
and with collapsed fibers at some closed regions on their boundaries.

II
.

The boundaries of the balls
\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{j}),\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{V}}_{j}),\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{W}}_{j})
correspond to the loss of uniqueness of the values
\ell
or
r
(see Definitions
5.1
and
5.3
). Then for
A\in\{{\mathcal{U}}_{j},{\mathcal{W}}_{j},{\mathcal{V}}_{j}:1\leq j\leq 4\}
, it is satisfies that

\partial\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap A)=\bigcup_{B\neq A}\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap B)\cap\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap A),

where the union is over the set
\{{\mathcal{U}}_{j},{\mathcal{W}}_{j},{\mathcal{V}}_{j}:1\leq j\leq 4\}
. The decompositions of the boundaries of the balls given by these intersections are permuted by the action of the dihedral group
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
.

III
.

The points
q_{1}=e^{i\arcsin(s)}
and
q_{2}=-e^{-i\arcsin(s)}
(see Figures
5
and
7
) are special because the quadrilaterals
Q_{1}=[0,1,q_{1}+1,q_{1}]
and
Q_{2}=[0,1,q_{2}+1,q_{2}]
satisfy

\{Q_{1},Q_{2}\}=h^{-1}(s)\cap\bigcap_{j=1}^{4}\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}}_{j})\cap\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{V}}_{j})\cap\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{W}}_{j}).

Moreover, the action of the group
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
in this points reduces to
\sigma(Q_{1})=\tau(Q_{1})=Q_{2}
,
\sigma(Q_{2})=\tau(Q_{2})=Q_{1}
and the fibers of the
I
-bundle structures on each sets collapse on these quadrilaterals.

In order to give a homeomorphism between
h^{-1}(s)
and
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, we describe the intersection pattern of the twelve balls of Lemma
5.7
through their boundaries. These intersections are codified in a combinatorial structure that we encounter naturally in
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, in the remainder of the section we describe this combinatorial pattern.

Definition 5.9
.

A bigon is a topological closed disc with two marked points on the boundary. The marked points are the vertices of the bigon and the two arcs in the boundary bounded by the vertices are the sides of the bigon. A decomposition of a surface into bigons is a covering of topological bigons such that the intersection of two such bigons is on the vertices or in one of its sides.

Proof.

1.

There is exactly one ball

\mathcal{G}_{0}

2.

The intersection

Lemma 6.1
.

If
1\leq j\leq 4
, then the sets
\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}}_{j}),\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{V}}_{j}),\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{W}}_{j})
are homeomorphic to the open cone over the closed ball
C(\mathbb{D}^{3})
. Moreover, under such homeomorphism, the height function
h
corresponds to the function on
C(\mathbb{D}^{3})\rightarrow(0,1]
induced from the projection

\mathbb{D}^{3}\times(0,1]\rightarrow(0,1].

Proof.

In order to prove the result for a set
\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}})\in\{\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}}_{j}),\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{V}}_{j}),\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{W}}_{j})\}
, we construct a family of homeomorphisms
f_{s}\colon h^{-1}(s)\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}})\rightarrow h^{-1}(1/2)\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}})
that vary continuously on the parameter
s
and a curve
\mu\colon(0,1)\rightarrow\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}})
such that
h(\mu(t))
is strictly decreasing (in fact, after a reparametrization of
\mu
, we can make
h(\mu(t))=t
). As the curve
\mu
is transversal to the level sets of the height function
h
, we can conclude that
\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}})\smallsetminus\{\mathfrak{R}_{4}\}
has a structure of a fiber bundle with base
(0,1)
and fiber
h^{-1}(1/2)\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}})
, homeomorphic to
\mathbb{D}^{3}
by Lemma
5.7
; this fiber bundle is trivial because it has a contractible base space, see
[
6
, Theorem 9.9]
). We can write down the trivialization as

h^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\cap\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}})\times(0,1)\rightarrow\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}})\smallsetminus\{\mathfrak{R}_{4}\},\qquad(x,t)\mapsto f^{-1}_{h(\mu(t))}(x)=f^{-1}_{t}(x),

with continuous inverse
Z\mapsto(f_{h(Z)}(Z),h(Z))
. As the fibers
h^{-1}(s)\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}})
collapse to
\{\mathfrak{R}_{4}\}
, when
s\rightarrow 1
, the product structure on
\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}})\smallsetminus\{\mathfrak{R}_{4}\}
induces the structure of an open cone on
\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}})\subset S(4)
, where the height function
h
corresponds to the projection onto the second factor.

Recall from Lemma
5.7
that for all
s\in(0,1)
, the level set
h^{-1}(s)\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}})
have the structure of a fiber bundle over a topological disc with some collapsed fibers at the boundary. Using this structure, we can construct the function
f_{s}:h^{-1}(s)\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}})\rightarrow h^{-1}(1/2)\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}})
by giving first the homeomorphism between the base spaces and then extending such homeomorphism to the fibers as linear maps on the arc-length parameter defining them. If the homeomorphisms on the base spaces depend continuously on the parameter
s
, then the extensions on the fibers depend continuously on the parameter
s
as well.

If
{\mathcal{U}}
is either
{\mathcal{U}}_{1}
or
{\mathcal{W}}_{1}

, and every

. Using this decomposition of

, we can give the homeomorphism

as

, where

x^{\prime}(x,s)=\left(\frac{\cos(\arcsin(\frac{1}{2}))}{\cos(\arcsin(s))}\right)x,

and
y\mapsto y^{\prime}(x,y,s)
is the linear map between the intervals

and
I_{x^{\prime}}\subset\Omega_{1/2}
. The expression for the imaginary part can be made explicit for

\mu_{2}\colon[0,1)\to\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{W}}_{1})

\mu_{1}(t)=[0,1,1-\frac{t}{2}+i(1-t),i(1-\frac{t}{2})]~~\text{and}~~~\mu_{2}(t)=[0,1,(1-\frac{t}{2})(1+i),\frac{t}{2}+i(1-\frac{t}{2})],

satisfy that

,

, and they are transversal to the level sets of the height function

. By the previous discussion, these curves give us the missing part for the construction of the homeomorphisms

and

.

Finally, from Remark

, it follows that for all

, the homeomorphisms sought can be constructed of the form

,

and
g\circ\sigma^{-j}\colon\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{W}}_{j})\to C(\mathbb{D}^{3})
.
∎

The strategy in the proof of Lemma

, can be used to prove the cone structure of

over

. For this, we should be able to construct a family of functions

Theorem 6.2
.

There is a homeomorphism

, such that the height function

, induced from the projection

Proof.

where for every

Lemma 6.3
.

For every
s\in(0,1)
,
h^{-1}(s)/\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{3}
.

Proof.

A fundamental domain in the level set
h^{-1}(s)
for the action of the subgroup
\langle\sigma\rangle<\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
is the set

\big[\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}}_{1})\cup\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{V}}_{1})\cup\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{W}}_{1})\big]\cap h^{-1}(s).

The only reflection that leaves setwise fixed this subset is
\tau
, the action of which permutes
\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{1})
with
\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{V}}_{1})
and leaves setwise fixed
\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{W}}_{1})
. Thus, the set

B_{s}=\big\{[0,1,z_{3},z_{4}]\in\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{W}}_{1}):\textrm{Im}(z_{3})\leq\textrm{Im}(z_{4})\big\},

is a closed ball (Lemma
5.7
), and the union
B_{s}\cup\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{1})
is a fundamental domain for the action of the dihedral group
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
.

If we take the identifications on the sphere
\partial\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{1})
determined by the actions of elements of the dihedral group
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
, we obtain a closed ball with boundary sphere splitted into two discs obtained from the intersections
\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{1})\cap\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{W}}_{1})
and
\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{1})\cap\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{W}}_{4})
. If we do the analogous identifications on
\partial B_{s}
, we obtain a closed ball with boundary sphere splitted into two discs obtained from the intersections
\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{W}}_{1})\cap\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}}_{1})
and
\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{W}}_{1})\cap\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{V}}_{4})
. As
\sigma^{3}\tau(\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{W}}_{1})\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{V}}_{4}))=\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{W}}_{4})\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}}_{1})
, we conclude that after performing the identifications by elements of the dihedral group
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
in the fundamental domain, we obtain two closed balls glued together through their boundary spheres. The space obtained in this way is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, finishing the proof.

As the statements on the identifications on
\partial\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{1})
and
\partial B_{s}
by elements of
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
are not obvious, we make a detailed account of them separately.

Identifications on
\partial\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{1})
. Recall from Lemma
5.10
that
\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{1})
is homeomorphic to a closed ball with boundary sphere divided the bigons,

1.

\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{1})\cap\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{V}}_{1})
, consisting of trapezoids with height
s
.

2.

\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{1})\cap\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{W}}_{4})
, whose elements have two adjacent sides of maximal length.

3.

\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{1})\cap\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{V}}_{3})
, whose elements have two oposite sides of maximal length

4.

\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{1})\cap\mathrm{cl}({\mathcal{W}}_{1})
, whose elements satisfy that
z_{3}
lies in the bisector of the angle
\measuredangle z_{4}z_{1}z_{2}
and it is at distance
s
of both sides
\overline{z_{1}z_{2}}
and
\overline{z_{1}z_{4}}
.

As the geometric properties of the quadrilaterals at each intersection are mutually exclusive, we see that there are no identifications among different bigons under the action of the dihedral group
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
. We have two maps leaving fixed setwise two faces:

\tau(\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}}_{1})\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{V}}_{1}))=\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{V}}_{1})\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}}_{1})\quad\textrm{and}\quad\sigma^{2}\tau(\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}}_{1})\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{V}}_{3}))=\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{V}}_{3})\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}}_{1}).

The map
\tau
folds the face
\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{1})\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{V}}_{1})
in half along the curve of symmetric trapezoids

\big\{[0,1,1/2+t+is,1/2-t+is]\colon s\leq 2t\leq 1\big\}.

Analogously, the map
\sigma^{2}\tau
is a reflection in
\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{1})\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{V}}_{3})
that swaps the angles
\measuredangle z_{3}z_{4}0
with
\measuredangle z_{4}01
and
\measuredangle z_{4}z_{3}1
with
\measuredangle 01z_{3}
, so it folds this face along the one-dimensional curve

corresponding to quadrilaterals that are truncated isosceles triangles. The two curves, along which the maps
\tau
and

fold the corresponding faces, are connected in the rectangle

, so that, the two identifications can be combined to be seen as folding in half the topological disc

-ball, whose boundary sphere is decomposed as the union of two topological discs, coming from the intersections

and
\mathrm{cl}(h^{-1}(s)\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{1})\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{W}}_{4})

Remark 7.1
.

A quadrilateral

in the sets of non-convex 4-segments
{\mathcal{N}}
and wedges
{\mathcal{K}}
.

Non-convex 4-segments.
Since
{\mathcal{N}}=\bigcup_{j=0}^{3}\sigma^{j}T
with
T=\{[0,1,t,x]\colon 0\leq t\leq x\leq 1\}
(see Proposition
7.3

T

\langle\sigma\rangle

\tau

T

. Then the quotient
{\mathcal{N}}/\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
is identified with the triangle

\{[0,1,t,x]\colon 0\leq t\leq x\leq 1-t\},

whose sides are

\{[0,1,0,t]\colon 0\leq t\leq 1\}\cup\left\{[0,1,t,t]\colon 0\leq t\leq\frac{1}{2}\right\}\cup\left\{[0,1,t,1-t]\colon\frac{1}{2}\leq t\leq 1\right\}.

Wedges.
The action of
\langle\sigma\rangle
in an element
Z=[0,1,0,z_{4}]\in{\mathcal{K}}
is as follows:

\sigma(Z)=[0,1,1-z_{4},1],~~\sigma^{2}(Z)=\left[0,1,0,\frac{1}{z_{4}}\right],~~\sigma^{3}(Z)=\left[0,1,\frac{z_{4}-1}{z_{4}},1\right].

Notice that
\sigma
and
\sigma^{3}

{\mathcal{K}}\cong\mathbb{S}^{2}\vee\mathbb{S}^{2}
, and
\sigma^{2}
acts as the function
z\mapsto\frac{1}{z}
in the sphere
\{[0,1,0,z_{4}]\colon z_{4}\in\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}\}
(Proposition
7.3
). Moreover, the map
\tau\sigma[0,1,0,z_{4}]=[0,1,0,\overline{z_{4}}]
is the complex conjugate in the same sphere. Then, we can identify the quotient
{\mathcal{K}}/\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
with the half disc
\{[0,1,0,z]\colon z\in\mathbb{D},~Im(z)\geq 0\}
. We conclude that the quotients
{\mathcal{N}}/\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
and
{\mathcal{K}}/\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
are identified through the segment
\{[0,1,0,t]\colon 0\leq t\leq 1\}
at their boundaries, resulting in a space homeomorphic to
\mathbb{D}^{2}
, see the bottom in Figure
18

.
∎

Lemma 7.20
.

For every
s\in[0,1)
, the quotient
\big(h^{-1}(s)\smallsetminus{\mathcal{D}}_{s}\big)/\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}

Proof.

\langle\sigma,\tau,\rangle

If we take the identifications on the sphere
\partial\big(\overline{{\mathcal{U}}_{1}}\smallsetminus{\mathcal{D}}\big)\cap h^{-1}(s)\cong\mathbb{H}^{2}

\big(\overline{{\mathcal{U}}_{1}}\smallsetminus{\mathcal{D}}\big)\cap\overline{{\mathcal{W}}_{4}}\cap h^{-1}(s)\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}

\partial B_{s}

\mathbb{H}^{3}

\big(\overline{{\mathcal{W}}_{1}}\smallsetminus{\mathcal{D}}\big)\cap\overline{{\mathcal{V}}_{4}}\cap h^{-1}(s)\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}
. As
\sigma^{3}\tau(\overline{{\mathcal{W}}_{1}}\cap\overline{{\mathcal{V}}_{4}})=\overline{{\mathcal{W}}_{4}}\cap\overline{{\mathcal{U}}_{1}}

\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
in the fundamental domain, we obtain two copies of

.

The space

8
Conclusions and open questions

In this section we make some general conclusions about the technical steps we had to face on the paper and include some questions which we encountered along the way.

1.

The definition of the height function
h
, given here, generalizes immediately for every
n\geq 3
. We can see however, that when
n\geq 5
, this function will have multiple points were it attains its maximum and thus, it doesn’t behave as well as when
n\leq 4
. The authors believe that the following modification to the height function will work:

h^{\prime}([z_{1},z_{2},z_{3},z_{4}])=\frac{\min_{1\leq j\leq 4}\{r_{j}(Z)\}}{\max_{1\leq j,k\leq 4}|z_{j}-z_{k}|},

where
r_{j}(Z):=d(z_{j},{\mathcal{L}}_{j})
with
{\mathcal{L}}_{j}:=\overline{z_{j+1}z_{j+2}}\cup\overline{z_{j+2}z_{j+3}}\cup\cdots\cup\overline{z_{j-2}z_{j-1}}
, compare with Definition
4.1
. We note that, as we are adding the length of the diagonals
|z_{j}-z_{j+k}|
in the denominator of
h^{\prime}
, the amount of subsets on which we must decompose
S(n)
so that the function
h
behaves well, increases considerably, compare with Definition
5.3
.

2.

As explained in Lemma
5.10
, the decomosition of
S(4)
into the subsets
\{\overline{{\mathcal{U}}_{j}},\overline{{\mathcal{V}}_{j}},\overline{{\mathcal{W}}_{j}}\}
and the combinatorics of their intersections, are codified into a graph as a flag structure, dual to the graph. A combinatorically equivalent flag structure is constructed using cones, dual to the regular euclidean cuboctahedron in Theorem
5.12
. Is there an intrinsic relation of this combinatorial structure to quadrilaterals? or the appearance of such a structure is a mere coincidence.

3.

The height function
h
is differentiable on an open, dense subset of
S(4)
and we have a well define gradient flow there, however, this flow arrives to the singular points in finite time. Is it possible to smooth the function, so that it is a differentiable Morse function with a unique maximum at the regular square? such function would induce a smooth regularizing flow on
S(4)
. It is worth noting that Theorem
6.2
gives a continuous regularizing flow on
S(4)
, however in a non-constructive way. The question is thus, to obtain the results given in this paper, but in the differentiable category.

4.

As an open subspace of
\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{2}
,
S(4)
inherits the Fubini-Study metric which is no longer complete. Moreover, as both
S(4)
and
{\mathcal{M}}(4)
are homeomorphic to cones over
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, we can give the spherical metric to each level set of the height function and the corresponding cone metrics on
S(4)
and
{\mathcal{M}}(4)
. Is there a more natural metric on those spaces whose geodesics capture better the deformations, such as the Weil-Petersson metric on Teichmüller spaces? This is a geometric version of the suavization problem of
h
in the previous point.

5.

Is it true that a topological space
X
, with boundary homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and interior homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, must be homeomorphic to
\mathbb{H}^{n}
? This assertion would have made the proof of Lemma
7.16
shorter. What if we add the condition on
X
to be a manifold with boundary?

6.

As exemplified in Example
7.14
and explained in Remark
7.15
, the
I
-bundle structures described in the cones
\{\overline{{\mathcal{U}}_{j}},\overline{{\mathcal{V}}_{j}},\overline{{\mathcal{W}}_{j}}\}
on each level set
h^{-1}(s)
don’t converge good to the
I
-bundle structure described in the cones at the boundary
h^{-1}(0)
. We overcame this difficulty in the proof of Lemma
7.16
by restricting the
I
-bundle structures to subsets where we had good convergence and then gluing the structures with Theorem
A.1
. The conclusion of Lemma
7.16
, tells us indirectly that there exist
I
-bundle structures which do have good convergence phenomena. How can we describe such
I
-bundle structures?

7.

If we consider the normalized side lengths of a quadrilateral

\ell_{i}:S(4)\rightarrow\mathbb{R},\qquad\ell_{i}(Z)=\frac{2|z_{i}-z_{i+1}|}{\sum_{j=1}^{4}|z_{j}-z_{j+1}|},

then the function
f:S(4)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{4}
,
f(Z)=(\ell_{1}(Z),\ell_{2}(Z),\ell_{3}(Z),\ell_{4}(Z))
was considered in
[
19
]
and they proved that the fibers
f^{-1}(p)
are one dimensional curves. It is possible to see that the level sets of the height function
h^{-1}(s)
are transversal to those curves, and thus, the fibers of
f
define a singular foliation of curves on
S(4)
. As an example of this, the curve
f^{-1}(1/2,1/2,1/2,1/2)
is the fiber which contains the regular square and is defined by the intersection (see Remark
5.8
.III)

\bigcap_{j=1}^{4}\big(\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{U}}_{j})\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{V}}_{j})\cap\textrm{cl}({\mathcal{W}}_{j})\big).

8.

The cone structure
S(4)
give us the homeomorphisms
h^{-1}(0,1]\cong h^{-1}(\varepsilon,1]\cong S(4)
, for some
\varepsilon>0
and thus, it allows us to consider the compactification
\overline{S(4)}^{eucl}\cong h^{-1}[\varepsilon,1]
, which is homeomorphic to a closed cone over
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. This compactification is invariant under the action of the dihedral group, so it gives a compactification of the moduli space
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}(4)}^{eucl}
as a closed cone over
\mathbb{S}^{3}
as well and in particular
\overline{{\mathcal{M}}(4)}^{eucl}\cong\mathbb{D}^{4}
. A reason to prefer the compactifications of
S(4)
and
{\mathcal{M}}(4)
considered in this work is because it is compatible with the spaces considered in
[
19
]
, but there is still the question on what properties capture each compactification and why should we prefer one or the other.

9.

How much can we weaken the hypotheses of Proposition
B.1
? As a more concrete example of this question, we can ask the following: If
G=N\rtimes H
is a group acting by homeomorphisms on a topological space
X
, such that the action of
N
descends to a well defined action on
(X/H)
, is it true that
(X/H)/N
is homeomorphic to
X/G
? See Remark
B.3
and Example
B.2
. We should emphasize that we do not have a reference for Remark
B.3
as it is considered a folklore result.

Appendix A
Gluing closed balls and closed half-spaces

We prove two topological results that are used many times along the paper. The ideas of the proofs of the two results in this appendix are extracted from
[
16
]
. Recall the notations of the closed ball
\mathbb{D}^{n}=\{X\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon|X|\leq 1\}
and the upper half-space
\mathbb{H}^{n}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon x_{n}\geq 0\}
.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
H_{i}^{n}\cong\mathbb{H}^{n}
be two copies of the upper half
n
-space, and
H_{i}^{n-1}\subset\partial H_{i}^{n}
, such that
H_{i}^{n-1}\cong\mathbb{H}^{n-1}
and
\overline{\partial H_{i}^{n}\smallsetminus H_{i}^{n-1}}\cong\mathbb{H}^{n-1}
. For every homeomorphism
\varphi\colon H_{1}^{n-1}\to H_{2}^{n-1}
, the quotient
(H_{1}^{n}\cup H_{2}^{n})\big/\varphi
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

Proof.

We will use that
\mathbb{H}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times[0,\infty)
and the embedding
\mathbb{H}^{n-1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{H}^{n}
given by
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})\mapsto(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},0)
. The key property of the proof is that every homeomorphism
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
can be extended to a homeomorphism
G\colon\mathbb{H}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
as follows

G(p,t)=(g(p),t)\qquad\forall~~p\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}=\partial\mathbb{H}^{n},~~\text{and}~~t\in[0,\infty).

By applying the key property twice to a homeomorphism
f\colon H_{1}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{H}^{n-1}
, first extending to
\partial H_{1}^{n}
and then to the whole
H_{1}^{n}
, we obtain a homeomorphism
F\colon H_{1}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}
whose restriction to
H_{1}^{n-1}
is
f
. The homeomorphism
F\sqcup F\colon H_{1}^{n}\sqcup H_{1}^{n}\to\mathbb{H}^{n}\sqcup\mathbb{H}^{n}
, induces a homeomorphism

\frac{H_{1}^{n}\sqcup H_{1}^{n}}{id_{H_{1}^{n-1}}}\cong\frac{\mathbb{H}^{n}\sqcup\mathbb{H}^{n}}{id_{\mathbb{H}^{n-1}}}\cong\mathbb{H}^{n}.

By applying now, the key property twice to
\varphi\colon H_{1}^{n-1}\to H_{2}^{n-1}
, we obtain a homeomorphism
\Phi\colon H_{1}^{n}\to H_{2}^{n}
that extends
\varphi
. The homeomorphism
Id\sqcup\Phi\colon H_{1}^{n}\sqcup H_{1}^{n}\to H_{1}^{n}\sqcup H_{2}^{n}
, induces a homeomorphism from
(H_{1}^{n}\sqcup H_{1}^{n})\big/id_{H_{1}^{n-1}}
to
(H_{1}^{n}\sqcup H_{2}^{n})\big/\varphi
and the result follows.
∎

Theorem A.2
.

Let
D_{i}^{n}\cong\mathbb{D}^{n}
be two copies of the closed
n
-ball, and
D_{i}^{n-1}\subset\partial D_{i}^{n}
such that
D_{i}^{n-1}\cong\mathbb{D}^{n-1}
and
\overline{\partial D_{i}^{n}\smallsetminus D_{i}^{n-1}}\cong\mathbb{D}^{n-1}
. For every homeomorphism
\varphi\colon D_{1}^{n-1}\to D_{2}^{n-1}
, the quotient
(D_{1}^{n}\cup D_{2}^{n})\big/\varphi
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{D}^{n}
.

Proof.

The proof is analogous to the proof of Theorem
A.1
. We just have to use the cone structure
\mathbb{D}^{n}\cong C(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
and the embedding
\mathbb{D}^{n-1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{D}^{n}
given by

(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1})\mapsto\left(x_{1},\dots,x_{n-1},\sqrt{1-x_{1}^{2}-\cdots-x_{n-1}^{2}~}\right),

to get the property that every homeomorphism
g\colon\partial\mathbb{D}^{n}\rightarrow\partial\mathbb{D}^{n}
extends to a homeomorphism of
\mathbb{D}^{n}
as

G\colon\mathbb{D}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}^{n},\quad G(tp)=tg(p),\quad\forall~~p\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}=\partial\mathbb{D}^{n}.

∎

Remark A.3
.

One way to obtain the hypothesis in Theorem
A.2
that the complement of
D_{i}^{n-1}
in
\partial D_{i}^{n}
is again a ball is by adding PL-structures to the balls, as it is done for example in
[
16
, Corollary 3.13]
. Another way to check the hypothesis is to ask for the balls
D_{i}^{n-1}
to be bi-collared in
\partial D_{i}^{n}
, so that the complement ball hypothesis follows from the solution of the Schoenflies conjecture by Brown in
[
14
]
.

Appendix B
Intermediate quotients by normal subgroups

In this appendix, we give a proof of the following result which the authors consider is a well known result, but couldn’t find a reference for it.

Proposition B.1
.

If
G
is a group acting on the right by homeomorphisms on a topological space
X
and
N\leq G
is normal subgroup, then there is a homeomorphism between the double quotient
(X/N)/(G/N)
and
X/G
.

Proof.

Observe first that the projection map
\pi:X\rightarrow X/G
descends to the quotient map
\widehat{\pi}:X/N\rightarrow X/G
. As
N
is a normal subgroup of
G
, the
G
-action on
X
induces a
G
-action on
X/N
with
N
acting trivially, so that it induces a
G/N
-action on
X/N
. As
\widehat{\pi}
is
G/N
-invariant, it induces a continuous and bijective map
\widehat{\widehat{\pi}}:(X/N)/(G/N)\rightarrow X/G
. If
\widehat{\widehat{A}}\subset(X/N)/(G/N)
is any subset, then there is a subset
A\subset X
such that
\widehat{\widehat{A}}=\{(xN)(G/N):x\in A\}
, which means that
\widehat{\widehat{A}}=\{\widehat{x}(G/N):\widehat{x}\in\widehat{A}\}
, where
\widehat{A}=\{yN:y\in A\}
. By definition,
\widehat{\widehat{A}}
is open in
(X/N)/(G/N)
if and only if its inverse image under the successive projection maps is open, that is, if the set
\{(xg):x\in A,g\in G\}=AG
is open. As the projection
\pi
is an open map,
\pi(AG)=\pi(A)=\widehat{\widehat{\pi}}(\widehat{\widehat{A}})
is open in
X/G
and
\widehat{\widehat{\pi}}
is an homeomorphism.
∎

Example B.2

(non-example)
.

Consider the dihedral group
D_{4}=\langle\sigma\rangle\rtimes\langle\tau\rangle
acting in the circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}\subset\mathbb{C}
, as
\sigma(z)=e^{\pi i/2}z
and
\tau(z)=\overline{z}
. The quotient
\mathbb{S}^{1}/\langle\tau\rangle
is homeomorphic to
\{z\in\mathbb{S}^{1}:\textrm{Im}(z)\geq 0\}
and the homeomorphism
\sigma
descends to a continuous map on
\mathbb{S}^{1}/\langle\tau\rangle
, defined under this identification by

\tilde{\sigma}(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcr}e^{\pi i/2}z,&&\textrm{Re}(z)\geq 0,\\
e^{3\pi i/2}\overline{z},&&\textrm{Re}(z)\leq 0.\end{array}\right.

Under the equivalence class
z\sim\tilde{\sigma}(z)
, we have an homeomorphism

(\mathbb{S}^{1}/\langle\tau\rangle)/\tilde{\sigma}=(\mathbb{S}^{1}/\langle\tau\rangle)/\sim\cong\mathbb{S}^{1}/D_{4}.

In this case however, the map
\tilde{\sigma}^{k}
is non-injective, and thus, the action of the cyclic group
\langle\sigma\rangle
on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
doen’t descend to a well defined action on
\mathbb{S}^{1}/\langle\tau\rangle
.

Remark B.3
.

As we saw in Example
B.2
, there are cases where the condition of the subgroup on Proposition
B.1
to be normal is no longer true and nevertheless the result holds. This is not always the case as there examples of two non-commuting homeomorphisms
f_{1},f_{2}:X\rightarrow X
, such that for
X_{j}=X/\langle f_{j}\rangle
the quotient
X_{1}/\langle f_{2}\rangle
is not homeomorphic to
X_{2}/\langle f_{1}\rangle
and both spaces are not homeomorphic to
X/\langle f_{1},f_{2}\rangle
.

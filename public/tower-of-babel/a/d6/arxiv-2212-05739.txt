Abstract
Let F k F_{k} be the (friendship) graph obtained from k k triangles by sharing a common vertex. The F k F_{k} -free graphs of order n n which attain the maximal spectral radius was firstly characterized by Cioabă, Feng, Tait and Zhang [Electron. J. Combin. 27 (4) (2020)], and later uniquely determined by Zhai, Liu and Xue [Electron. J. Combin. 29 (3) (2022)] under the condition that n n is sufficiently large. In this paper, we get rid of the condition on n n being sufficiently large if k = 2 k=2 . The graph F 2 F_{2} is also known as the bowtie. We show that the unique n n -vertex F 2 F_{2} -free spectral extremal graph is the balanced complete bipartite graph adding an edge in the vertex part with smaller size if n ≥ 7 n\geq 7 , and the condition n ≥ 7 n\geq 7 is tight. Our result is a spectral generalization of a theorem of Erdős, Füredi, Gould and Gunderson [J. Combin. Theory Ser. B 64 (1995)], which states that ex ⁡ ( n , F 2 ) = ⌊ n 2 / 4 ⌋ + 1 \mathrm{ex}(n,F_{2})=\left\lfloor{n^{2}}/{4}\right\rfloor+1 . Moreover, we study the spectral extremal problem for F k F_{k} -free graphs with given number of edges. In particular, we show that the unique m m -edge F 2 F_{2} -free spectral extremal graph is the join of K 2 K_{2} with an independent set of m − 1 2 \frac{m-1}{2} vertices if m ≥ 8 m\geq 8 , and the condition m ≥ 8 m\geq 8 is tight.

.
We usually write

Let

be the set of neighbors of
v
,
and
d(v)
or

be the degree of a vertex
v
in
G
.
For a subset
A\subseteq V(G)
, we write
e(A)
for the
number of edges with two endpoints in
A
,
and
N(A)
for the union of neighborhoods of vertices of
A
.
For two disjoint sets
A,B
, we write
e(A,B)
for the number of edges between
A
and
B
.
Let
K_{n}
be the complete graph on
n
vertices, and

K_{s,t}
be the complete bipartite graph with parts of sizes

s
and
t
.
We write
C_{n}
and

for the cycle and

n

Let

be the join graph consisting of
G
and
H
in which each vertex of
G

is adjacent to each vertex of
H
.
To illustrate the background and history,
we shall partition the introduction into the following three subsections.

1.1
The classical extremal graph problems

A graph
G
is called
F
-free if it does not contain
an isomorphic copy of
F
as a subgraph.
Apparently, every bipartite graph is
C_{2k+1}
-free
for every integer
k\geq 1
.
The Mantel theorem
[
28
]
asserts that
the balanced complete bipartite graph
K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}

attains the maximum number of edges among all
n
-vertex triangle-free graphs.

Theorem 1.1

(Mantel
[
28
]
, 1907)
.

If
G
is a triangle-free graph on
n
vertices, then

e(G)\leq\lfloor{n^{2}}/{4}\rfloor,

(1)

equality holds if and only if
G
is the balanced complete bipartite graph
K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}
.

Mantel’s theorem has many interesting applications and generalizations in the literature; see, e.g.,

[
3
, pp. 294–301]
for standard proofs.
The
Turán number
of a graph
F
is the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
F
-free graph, and
it is usually denoted by
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
. That is,

\mathrm{ex}(n,F):=\max\bigl\{e(G):|G|=n~\text{and}~F\nsubseteq G\bigr\}.

A graph on
n
vertices with no subgraph
F
and with
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)
edges is called an
extremal graph
for
F
.
We write
\mathrm{Ex}(n,F)
for the set of all
n
-vertex
F
-free graphs
with maximum number of edges.
A well-known theorem of Turán (see
[
3
]
)
extended Theorem
1.1
and
proved that for every integer
r\geq 2
,

\mathrm{Ex}(n,K_{r+1})=\{T_{r}(n)\},

(2)

where
T_{r}(n)
is an
n
-vertex complete
r
-partite graph
whose parts have sizes as equal as possible.
In particular, we have
T_{2}(n)=K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}
.
The graph
T_{r}(n)
is now known as Turán’s graph.
Inspired by Turan’s result (
2
),
the extremal graph theory was widely studied and achieved a rapid development in the past 100 years; see, e.g.,
[
13
,
41
]
for recent comprehensive surveys.

Let
F_{k}
denote the
k
-fan graph, which is the graph
consisting of
k
triangles
that intersect in exactly one common vertex.
This graph is known as the friendship graph
because it is the only extremal graph in the
well-known Friendship Theorem
[
1
, Chapter 43]
.
Since the chromatic number
\chi(F_{k})=3
,
a celebrated result due to Erdős, Stone and Simonovits (see
[
3
, p. 339]
)
gives an asymptotic value of the Turán number of
F_{k}
, which states that

\mathrm{ex}(n,F_{k})=n^{2}/4+o(n^{2}).

A natural question is to determine the error term

o(n^{2})
accurately.
In 1995, Erdős, Füredi,
Gould and Gunderson
[
11
]
proved the following
result.

Theorem 1.2

(Erdős–Füredi–Gould–Gunderson
[
11
]
, 1995)
.

For every
k\geq 1
and
n\geq 50k^{2}
,

\mathrm{ex}(n,F_{k})=\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}k^{2}-k,\quad~~\mbox{if $k$ is odd,}\\
k^{2}-\frac{3}{2}k,\quad\mbox{if $k$ is even}.\end{array}\right.

The extremal graphs in
\mathrm{Ex}(n,F_{k})
are also determined in
[
11
]
as follows.

(a)

For odd
k
,
the extremal graphs are constructed from
K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}

by embedding two vertex-disjoint copies of the complete graph
K_{k}
in one side.

(b)

For even
k
, the extremal graphs
are obtained by taking
K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}
and embedding
a graph with
2k-1
vertices,
k^{2}-\frac{3}{2}k
edges and maximum degree
k-1
in one side.

We remark here that for even
k
, the embedded subgraph is a nearly
(k-1)
-regular graph
of order
2k-1
with degree
sequence
(k-1,\ldots,k-1,k-2)
. Such a graph does exist for every even
k\geq 2
.
Moreover, for odd
k
and even
n
,
the extremal graph is unique, it is obtained from

K_{\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}}
by embedding two vertex-disjoint copies of
K_{k}
in one part.
However, for other cases,
the extremal graph for
F_{k}
is clearly not unique.
Additionally, it was conjectured by Erdős, Füredi,
Gould and Gunderson
in the second paragraph after
[
11
, Theorem 2.1]
that
Theorem
1.2
gives
\mathrm{ex}(n,F_{k})

for all
n\geq 4k
, rather than
n\geq 50k^{2}
.
This conjecture remains open.

1.2
The spectral extremal graph problems

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices with
m
edges.
Let
A(G)
be the adjacency matrix of
G
.
Let
\lambda(G)
be the spectral radius of
G
,
which is defined as the maximum of
modulus of eigenvalues of the adjacency matrix
A(G)
.
The classical extremal graph problems
usually study the maximum or minimum
number of edges that the extremal graphs can have.
Correspondingly,
we can study the spectral extremal problem.
We denote by
\mathrm{ex}_{\lambda}(n,F)

the largest eigenvalue of the adjacency matrix
in an
n
-vertex graph that contains no copy of
F
.

In 2007, Nikiforov
[
32
]
published the spectral Turán theorem, which asserts that
the
r
-partite Turán graph
T_{r}(n)
is the unique
K_{r+1}
-free graph
attaining the maximum spectral radius.
In particular, the important triangle case provided a spectral version of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.3

(Nikiforov
[
32
]
, 2007)
.

Let
G
be a triangle-free graph on
n
vertices. Then

\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}),

(3)

equality holds if and only if
G

is a balanced complete bipartite graph

K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}
.

Comparing the spectral result with the classical
result in terms of edges,
one can see that Theorem
1.3

is stronger than Theorem
1.1
,
since
(
3
) can deduce (
1
). Indeed, using (
3
) and
Rayleigh’s formula, we get
{2e(G)}/{n}\leq\lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor}
and then
e(G)\leq\bigl\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\sqrt{\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor}\bigr\rfloor=\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor
.
Such a deduction enriches the study of spectral extremal graph theory.
In the past few years,
Theorem
1.3
stimulated the developments of the spectral extremal graph theory.
It is natural to consider the spectral extremal problems
for graphs with given number of vertices.
In view of this perspective,
various extensions and generalizations on
inequality (
3
) have been obtained in the literature; see, e.g.,

[
46
,
32
,
17
]
for extensions
on
K_{r+1}
-free graphs;
see
[
4
]
for relations
between cliques and spectral radius,

[
42
,
25
]
for outerplanar and planar graphs,

[
43
]
for the Colin de Verdière parameter,

[
26
,
24
]
for non-bipartite triangle-free graphs,

[
27
,
21
]
for non-bipartite graphs without short odd cycles,
and
[
35
]
for a comprehensive survey.

Recall that
F_{k}
is the graph obtained from
k
triangles
by intersecting a common vertex,
and
\mathrm{Ex}(n,F_{k})
is the set of
n
-vertex
F_{k}
-free graphs
with the maximum number of edges.
For fixed
k\geq 2
and sufficiently large order
n
,
the spectral extremal problem
for
F_{k}
was recently
characterized by Cioabă, Feng,
Tait and Zhang
[
9
]
.

Theorem 1.4

(Cioabă–Feng–Tait–Zhang
[
9
]
, 2020)
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
G
be an
F_{k}
-free graph on
n
vertices.
For sufficiently large
n
, if
G
has the maximal spectral radius, then

G\in\mathrm{Ex}(n,F_{k}).

In 2022, Zhai, Liu and Xue
[
50
]
provided a
further
characterization
of
G
and
determined the
unique
spectral extremal graph,
which is obtained from
K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}
by embedding a graph
H
in the part of smaller size

\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
, where
H=K_{k}\cup K_{k}
for odd
k
, and
H
is described as below for even
k
.
In this case,
H
is a graph with vertex set
V(H)=\{w_{0}\}\cup A\cup B

such that
N(w_{0})=A
and
|B|=|A|+2=k
.
Then we partition
A
into
A_{1}\cup A_{2}
,
and
B
into
\{u_{0}\}\cup B_{1}\cup B_{2}

such that
|A_{1}|=|A_{2}|=|B_{2}|=\frac{k-2}{2}
and
|B_{1}|=\frac{k}{2}
.
Finally, we join
k-1
edges from
u_{0}
to
A_{1}\cup B_{1}
,

\frac{k-2}{2}
independent edges between
B_{2}
and
A_{2}
,
and some additional edges such that both
A
and
B_{1}\cup B_{2}

are cliques.
In fact, it is challenging and difficult to
characterize the embedded small graph
H
in the even case.
We refer the readers to
[
50
]
for more details, and

[
6
,
22
,
10
]
for related problems,
and
[
45
]
for a stronger result.

Theorem 1.5

(Wang–Kang–Xue
[
45
]
, 2023)
.

Let
r\geq 2
be an integer, and
F
be a graph with
\mathrm{ex}(n,F)=e(T_{r}(n))+O(1)
. For sufficiently large
n
, if
G
has the maximal spectral radius
over all
n
-vertex
F
-free graphs, then

G\in\mathrm{Ex}(n,F).

Note that the above results
(Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
) hold under the condition that
n
is sufficiently large.
Nevertheless, the lower bound on
n
is still unknown.
For example, given a specific integer
n
, say
n=10^{8}
,
we still do not know whether the conclusion is true.
In extremal combinatorics,
it is also an important work to determine the extremal configurations for all values of
n
,
instead of
n
being large enough. For instance,
the following are three such works.

\heartsuit

In 2005, Füredi and Simonovits
[
12
]
,
Keevash and Sudakov
[
19
]
independently
provided the Turán number of Fano plane for sufficiently large

In 2019, Bellmann and Reiher
[

.
This confirmed a conjectura of Sós in a strong sense.

\heartsuit

Moreover, Tait and Tobin
[

In 2021, Lin and Ning
[

proved further that

This gives a complete solution to the Cvetković–Rowlinson conjecture.

\heartsuit

In addition, Nikiforov
[

]

proved that for every color-critical graph

with

\chi(F)=r+1
, there exists

such that if

,

-free graphs.
The bound

.
In 2022, Zhai and Lin
[
51

provided that
linear functions on

are valid for two classes of graphs, namely,

To some extent, it is meaningful to find an appropriate or smallest bound
n_{0}(k)

such that for every
n\geq n_{0}(k)
, the spectral extremal graphs
are contained in
\mathrm{Ex}(n,F_{k})
.

In this paper, we investigate such problems for
the bowtie
F_{2}
, which consists of two copies of
K_{3}

merged at a vertex. We refer to this vertex as the
central vertex of the bowtie.
Despite the simple nature of the bowtie, bowtie-free graphs have been crucial in several areas of graph theory,
such as in Ramsey problem
[
15
]
,
supersaturation problem
[
16
]

and chromatic number
[
7
]
, etc.
In fact, the Turán number of the bowtie
was also considered by
Erdős, Füredi, Gould and Gunderson
[
11
]
,
in which they presented the exact value of
\mathrm{ex}(n,F_{2})
for every integer
n\geq 5
.

Theorem 1.6

(Erdős et al.
[
11
]
, 1995)
.

For
n\geq 5
, one has

\mathrm{ex}(n,F_{2})=\left\lfloor{n^{2}}/{4}\right\rfloor+1.

Motivated by these results above, we will
determine
the unique
F_{2}
-free extremal graph attaining the maximal spectral radius
for all integers
n\geq 7
.
In what follows, we will establish a spectral extension on Theorem
1.6
.
Denote by
K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}^{+}
the graph obtained from
the balanced complete bipartite graph
K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}

by embedding an edge in the smaller part;
see Figure

.

The unique extremal graph in Theorem

One of the main results of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.7
.

If
n\geq 7
and
G
is an
F_{2}
-free graph on
n
vertices, then

\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}^{+}),

G=K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}^{+}
.

Remark.

The bound
n\geq 7
in Theorem
1.7

is the best possible, since for the case
n=6
,
there exists an
F_{2}
-free graph
with larger spectral radius. Namely,

K_{2}\vee I_{4}
.
Upon computations, we have

\lambda(K_{2}\vee I_{4})\approx 3.722
,
while
\lambda(K_{3,3}^{+})\approx 3.504
.

Observation.

Using the fundamental inequality
{2e(G)}/{n}\leq\lambda(G)
,
it is easy to verify that our result (Theorem
1.7
)
implies Theorem
1.6
.
A tedious calculation gives
\lambda(K_{\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}}^{+})<\frac{n}{2}+\frac{2}{n}+\frac{8}{n^{2}}
for even

,
and
\lambda(K_{\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n+1}{2}}^{+})<\frac{n}{2}+\frac{3}{2n}+\frac{1}{n}

for odd

. Hence, we get

.

We mention here that the proof of Theorem
1.7
is completely different from that
of Theorem
1.4
, since the triangle removal lemma was used in the proof
of Theorem
1.4
and it requires the order

of the graph

1.3
Spectral problems for graphs with given number of edges

In 1970, Nosal
[
40
]

determined the largest spectral radius of a triangle-free graph
in terms of the number of edges, which states that
if
G
is a triangle-free graph, then
\lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{m}
.
Furthermore, Nikiforov
[
30
,
31
]
extended Nosal’s theorem
and showed that if
G
is triangle-free, then
\lambda(G)<\sqrt{m}
unless
G
is a complete bipartite graph (possibly with some isolated vertices).
In the sequel, when we consider the result on a graph with respect to
the given number of edges,
we shall ignore the possible isolated vertices if there are no confusions.

Theorem 1.8

(Nosal–Nikiforov,
[
30
,
31
]
)
.

If
G
is a triangle-free graph with
m
edges, then

\lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{m},

(4)

equality holds if and only if

G
is a complete bipartite graph.

Theorem
1.8
implies that if
G
is a bipartite graph
with
m
edges,
then
\lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{m}
,
equality holds if and only if

G
is a complete bipartite graph.
On the one hand, inequality (
4
) can imply
(
1
) in Theorem
1.1
.
Indeed, applying the Rayleigh inequality, we have

\frac{2m}{n}\leq\lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{m}
,
which yields
m\leq\lfloor{n^{2}}/{4}\rfloor
.
On the other hand, combining (
4
) with Mantel’s theorem, we obtain

\lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{m}\leq\sqrt{\lfloor{n^{2}}/{4}\rfloor}=\lambda(K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil})
.
Henceforth, inequality (
4
) can also imply (
3
) in Theorem
1.3
.

During the past few years, the inequality (
4
) in Theorem
1.8

boosted the great interests of studying the maximum spectral radius of graphs
in terms of the number of edges, rather than the given number of vertices;
see
[
30
]
for an extension on
K_{r+1}
-free graphs,

[
33
]
for
C_{4}
-free graphs,

[
48
]
for
K_{2,r+1}
-free graphs,
and similar results of
C_{5}
-free and
C_{6}
-free graphs
[
29
]
as well,

[
38
]
for an extension on book-free graphs,
and
[
26
,
49
,
23
]
for non-bipartite triangle-free graphs. The spectral extremal problem for graphs with given size
is interesting in its own right, and it
is increasingly becoming an important and popular topic
in recent researches on spectral graph theory.

In this section, we will focus the attention mainly on the extremal spectral problems
for
F_{2}
-free graphs with given number of edges.
Recall that
K_{2}\vee I_{\frac{m-1}{2}}

denotes the join graph obtained from an edge
K_{2}
and an independent set

I_{\frac{m-1}{2}}
such that each vertex of the edge is adjacent to each vertex of the

.

If

\lambda(G)\leq\frac{1+\sqrt{4m-3}}{2},

The bound

.
An easy calculation gives

,
but
\lambda(K_{2}\vee I_{3})=\frac{1+\sqrt{4m-3}}{2}=3

-free graphs.

Proof of Theorem
1.7

To begin with,
we shall present two lemmas for simplicity.
The first lemma gives the characterization
of the spectral radius of
K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}^{+}
, and it yields some lower/upper bounds for our purpose.
The second lemma states that if
one adds an edge within one part of a bipartite graph,
then the maximum spectral radius is attained
by adding this edge into the smaller vertex part
of a balanced bipartite graph.

Lemma 2.1
.

(a) If
n
is even, then
\lambda(K_{\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}}^{+})

is the largest root of

f(x)=x^{3}-x^{2}-\frac{n^{2}}{4}x+\frac{n^{2}}{4}-n.

(b) if
n
is odd, then
\lambda(K_{\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n+1}{2}}^{+})

is the largest root of

g(x)=x^{3}-x^{2}+\frac{1-n^{2}}{4}x+\frac{n^{2}}{4}-n-\frac{5}{4}.

Consequently, we have

\lambda^{2}(K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}^{+})>\left\lfloor\frac{n^{2}}{4}\right\rfloor+2.

(5)

Proof.

(a) Let
\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})^{T}
be a Perron eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda(K_{\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}}^{+})
.
We partition the vertex set of
K_{\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}}^{+}
as
\Pi:V(\lambda(K_{\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}}^{+}))=X_{1}\cup X_{2}\cup Y
, where
X_{1}=\{u,v\}
forms an edge,

X_{1}\cup X_{2}
and
Y
are partite sets of
K_{\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}}
.
By comparing the neighborhoods, we can see that
x_{u}=x_{v}
,
all coordinates of the vector
\bm{x}

corresponding to vertices of
X_{2}
are equal.
(the coordinates of vertices of
Y
are equal)
For notational convenience, we may assume that

x_{u}=x_{v}=x
,
x_{s}=y
for all
s\in X_{2}
and
x_{t}=z

for all
t\in Y
. Then

\begin{cases}\lambda x=x+\frac{n}{2}z,\\
\lambda y=\frac{n}{2}z,\\
\lambda z=2x+(\frac{n}{2}-2)y.\end{cases}

Thus
\lambda(K_{\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}}^{+})
is the largest eigenvalue of

B_{\Pi}=\begin{matrix}X_{1}\\
X_{2}\\
Y\end{matrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\frac{n}{2}\\
0&0&\frac{n}{2}\\
2&\frac{n}{2}-2&0\end{bmatrix}.

By calculation, we know that
\lambda(K_{\frac{n}{2},\frac{n}{2}}^{+})
is the largest root of

f(x)=\det(xI_{3}-B_{\Pi})=x^{3}-x^{2}-\frac{n^{2}}{4}x+\frac{n^{2}}{4}-n.

(b) For odd
n
, the proof is similar with the previous case.
We partition the vertex set of
K_{\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n+1}{2}}^{+}

as
\Pi^{\prime}:V(K_{\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n+1}{2}}^{+})=X_{1}\cup X_{2}\cup Y
,
where
X_{1}=\{u,v\}
forms an edge,
X_{1}\cup X_{2}
and
Y

are partite sets of
K_{\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n+1}{2}}

satisfying
|X_{1}|+|X_{2}|=\frac{n-1}{2}

and
|Y|=\frac{n+1}{2}
. Then
a similar argument yields that

\lambda(K_{\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n+1}{2}}^{+})
is the largest eigenvalue of

B_{\Pi^{\prime}}=\begin{matrix}X_{1}\\
X_{2}\\
Y\end{matrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0&\frac{n+1}{2}\\
0&0&\frac{n+1}{2}\\
2&\frac{n-1}{2}-2&0\end{bmatrix}.

Thus,
\lambda(K_{\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n+1}{2}}^{+})

is the largest root of

g(x)=\det(xI_{3}-B_{\Pi^{\prime}})=x^{3}-x^{2}+\frac{1-n^{2}}{4}x+\frac{n^{2}}{4}-n-\frac{5}{4}.

Finally, we are ready to prove (
5
).
By calculation, we can verify that

f(\sqrt{{n^{2}}/{4}+2})=\sqrt{n^{2}+8}-n-2<0,

and

g(\sqrt{{(n^{2}-1)}/{4}+2})=\sqrt{n^{2}+7}-n-3<0.

This completes the proof.
∎

In fact, Rayleigh’s formula also gives a lower bound
on the spectral radius.

\lambda(K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}^{+})>\frac{2e(K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}^{+})}{n}=\begin{cases}\frac{n}{2}+\frac{2}{n},&\text{if $n$ is even;}\\
\frac{n}{2}+\frac{3}{2n},&\text{if $n$ is odd.}\end{cases}

(6)

Therefore, for even
n
, we get from (
6
)
that
\lambda^{2}>(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{2}{n})^{2}>\frac{n^{2}}{4}+2
.
On the other hand, for odd
n
, we construct a unit vector

\bm{x}:=(\frac{1}{\sqrt{n-1}},\ldots,\frac{1}{\sqrt{n-1}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{n+1}},\ldots,\frac{1}{\sqrt{n+1}})^{T}
, where

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n-1}}
corresponds to vertices of
X
, and

\frac{1}{\sqrt{n+1}}
corresponds to vertices of
Y
, respectively
(see Figure
2
). Then Rayleigh’s formula implies

\lambda(K_{\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n+1}{2}}^{+})\geq\bm{x}^{T}A(K_{\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n+1}{2}}^{+})\bm{x}=\frac{\sqrt{n^{2}-1}}{2}+\frac{2}{n-1}
, which yields
\lambda^{2}(K_{\frac{n-1}{2},\frac{n+1}{2}}^{+})\geq(\frac{\sqrt{n^{2}-1}}{2}+\frac{2}{n-1})^{2}>\frac{n^{2}-1}{4}+2
.
The above argument provides an alternative proof of (
5
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
a,b\geq 2
be integers with
a+b=n
,
and
K_{a,b}^{+}
be the graph obtained from
the complete bipartite graph
K_{a,b}

by adding an edge in the part of size

. Then

\lambda(K_{a,b}^{+})\leq\lambda(K_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor,\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}^{+}),

equality holds if and only if
a=\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor

and
b=\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
.

Proof.

Similar with the proof of Lemma
2.1
, we know that

\lambda(K_{a,b}^{+})
is the largest root of

f_{a,b}(x)=x^{3}-x^{2}-abx+ab-2b.

It is sufficient to prove the following two cases.

Case 1.

If
a\geq b+1
, then
our goal is to prove that

\lambda(K_{a,b}^{+})<\lambda(K_{a-1,b+1}^{+})
.
By computation, we obtain

f_{a,b}(x)-f_{a-1,b+1}(x)=x(a-b-1)-a+b+3.

If
a=b+1
, then
f_{a,b}(x)=f_{a-1,b+1}(x)+2
,
and
f_{a-1,b+1}(x)<f_{a,b}(x)
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}
;
if
a\geq b+2
,
then we get
f_{a-1,b+1}(x)<f_{a,b}(x)
for every
x>\frac{a-b-3}{a-b-1}
.
Note that
\lambda(K_{a,b}^{+})\geq\lambda(K_{3})=2
.
Therefore, we obtain
f_{a-1,b+1}(\lambda(K_{a,b}^{+}))<f_{a,b}(\lambda(K_{a,b}^{+}))=0
.
Recall that
\lambda(K_{a-1,b+1}^{+})

is the largest root of
f_{a-1,b+1}(x)
. Thus
we get
\lambda(K_{a,b}^{+})<\lambda(K_{a-1,b+1}^{+})
.

Case 2.

If
a+2\leq b
, then we need to prove
\lambda(K_{a,b}^{+})<\lambda(K_{a+1,b-1}^{+})
.
Similarly, we have

f_{a,b}(x)-f_{a+1,b-1}(x)=x(b-a-1)+a-b-1.

In view of
b\geq a+2
, for every
x>\frac{b-a+1}{b-a-1}
,
we get
f_{a+1,b-1}(x)<f_{a,b}(x)
.
Since
a\geq 2
and
b\geq 4
, we can see that

K_{2}\vee I_{4}
is a subgraph of
K_{a,b}^{+}
.
Then
\lambda(K_{a,b}^{+})\geq\lambda(K_{2}\vee I_{4})\approx 3.372
. Since
\frac{b-a+1}{b-a-1}=1+\frac{2}{b-a-1}\leq 3
,
we obtain
f_{a+1,b-1}(\lambda(K_{a,b}^{+}))<f_{a,b}(\lambda(K_{a,b}^{+}))=0
, which leads to

\lambda(K_{a,b}^{+})<\lambda(K_{a+1,b-1}^{+})
, as desired.
∎

Clearly, one can see that

Let

of

.

Claim 2.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be an
m
-edge graph obtained from a bipartite graph

B_{s,t}
by adding two new vertices
u
and
v_{0}
,
an edge
uv_{0}
and all edges between
\{u,v_{0}\}
and
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{t}\}
. Then

\lambda(G)<\frac{1+\sqrt{4m-3}}{2}.

For convenience of readers, we draw the graph
G
in Figure
5
.

Proof.

Since the structure of
B_{s,t}
is stochastic,
it seems impossible to get the required bound by a straightforward calculation.
The key idea of the proof depends on a trick analysis
of the Perron eigenvector of
\lambda(G)
.
Since
u
and
v_{0}
has the same neighborhood in
G
,
we have
x_{u}=x_{v_{0}}
.
In what follows, we will establish
an upper bound on
\lambda^{2}x_{u}
and
then show that
\lambda(G)<\frac{1+\sqrt{4m-3}}{2}
. Since
\lambda x_{u}=x_{v_{0}}+\sum_{i=1}^{t}x_{v_{i}}
, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{t}x_{v_{i}}=(\lambda-1)x_{u}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}x_{u}

\displaystyle=\lambda x_{v_{0}}+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\lambda x_{v_{i}}=x_{u}+\sum_{i=1}^{t}x_{v_{i}}+\sum_{i=1}^{t}\left(x_{u}+x_{v_{0}}+\sum_{w\in N(v_{i})\cap B}x_{w}\right)

\displaystyle=(2t+1)x_{u}+\sum_{i=1}^{t}x_{v_{i}}+\sum_{w\in B}d_{A}(w)x_{w}.

Observe that
\lambda x_{w}=\sum_{v\in N(w)}x_{v}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{t}x_{v_{i}}
,
we get
x_{w}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{i=1}^{t}x_{v_{i}}
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}x_{u}

\displaystyle\leq(2t+1)x_{u}+\left(1+\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{w\in B}d_{A}(w)\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{t}x_{v_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=(2t+1)x_{u}+\left(1+\frac{m-2t-1}{\lambda}\right)(\lambda-1)x_{u}

\displaystyle<mx_{u}+(\lambda-1)x_{u}.

Thus, we get
\lambda^{2}-\lambda-(m-1)<0
,
which yields
\lambda<\frac{1+\sqrt{4m-3}}{2}
.
∎

In this section, we will conclude more spectral extremal problems involving

[

In 2023, Ning and Zhai

In this paper, we have determined
the largest spectral extremal graphs of the bowtie
for graphs with given order and size, respectively.
A natural question one may ask is that how many copies of
the bowtie must have in a graph with the spectral radius larger than
that of the extremal graph in Theorems
1.7
or

.
In fact, the edge supersaturation for the bowtie was studied by Kang, Makai and

[

.

for the bowtie.

K_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}^{2K_{2}}

K_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil,\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}

into the part of size

Problem 4.2
.

If
G

n

The definition of the spectral radius was recently extended
to the
p
-spectral radius; see
[
20
,
17
,
36
]
and references therein.
We denote the
p
-norm of
\bm{x}
by

\lVert\bm{x}\rVert_{p}=(\sum_{i=1}^{n}|x_{i}|^{p})^{1/p}
.
For every real number
p\geq 1
,
the
p
-spectral radius
of
G
is defined as

\lambda^{(p)}(G):=2\max_{\lVert\bm{x}\rVert_{p}=1}\sum_{\{i,j\}\in E(G)}x_{i}x_{j}.

We remark that
\lambda^{(p)}(G)
is a versatile parameter.
Indeed,
\lambda^{(1)}(G)
is known as the Lagrangian function of
G
,

\lambda^{(2)}(G)
is the spectral radius of its adjacency matrix,
and

\lim_{p\to+\infty}\lambda^{(p)}(G)=2e(G).

(15)

The extremal function for
p
-spectral radius is given as

\mathrm{ex}_{\lambda}^{(p)}(n,{F}):=\max\{\lambda^{(p)}(G):|G|=n~\text{and $G$ is ${F}$-free}\}.

To some extent, the proof of results on the
p
-spectral radius shares some similarities with the usual spectral radius when
p>1
;
see
[
18
]
for more extremal problems.
In 2014, Kang and Nikiforov
[
17
]
extended the Turán theorem
to the
p
-spectral version for
p>1
.
They proved that

\mathrm{ex}_{\lambda}^{(p)}(n,K_{r+1})=\lambda^{(p)}(T_{r}(n))
,
and the unique extremal graph is the
r
-partite Turán graph
T_{r}(n)
.
This result unifies the classical Turán theorem and Nikiforov’s theorem by the fact (
15
).

Recall that
F_{k}
is the graph consisting of
k
triangles
by intersecting a common vertex.

Sharp embedding between Wiener amalgam and some classical spaces
Yufeng Lu

Abstract
We establish the sharp conditions for the embedding between Wiener amalgam spaces W p , q s W_{p,q}^{s} and some classical spaces, including Sobolev spaces L s , r L^{s,r} , local Hardy spaces h r h_{r} , Besov spaces B p , q s B_{p,q}^{s} , which partially improve and extend the main result obtained by Guo et al. in Guo2017Characterization . In addition, we give the full characterization of inclusion between Wiener amalgam spaces W p , q W_{p,q} and α \alpha -modulation spaces M p , q s , α M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha} . Especially, at the case of α = 0 \alpha=0 with M p , q s , α = M p , q s M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}=M_{p,q}^{s} , we give the sharp conditions of the most general case of these embedding. When 0 < p ⩽ 1 0<p\leqslant 1 , we also establish the sharp embedding between Wiener amalgam spaces and Triebel spaces F p , r s F_{p,r}^{s} .

Introduction

The amalgam spaces decouple the connection between local and global properties. They are first introduced by Norbert Wiener in
Wiener1926representation
;
Wiener1932Tauberian
;
Wiener1959Fourier
. The first systematic study has been undertaken by Holland in
Holland1975Harmonic
.
In the 1980s, H.G. Feichtinger in
Feichtinger1983Banach
;
Feichtinger1990Generalized
, described a far-reaching generalization of the Wiener amalgam spaces, where he used
W(B,C)
to denote the Wiener amalgam spaces with the local component in some Banach spaces
B
and the global component in some Banach spaces
C
. Feichtinger studied the basic properties of these spaces, including inclusions, duality, complex interpolation, pointwise multiplications, and convolution.
The Wiener amalgam spaces
W_{p,q}^{s}
we talk about here are a class of these spaces, which can be re-expressed as
W({\mathscr{F}^{-1}}L^{q}_{s},L^{p})
.

From another point of view, the Wiener amalgam spaces could be regarded as the Triebel-type space corresponding to the modulation space
M_{p,q}^{s}
. The modulation spaces
M_{p,q}^{s}
are one of the function spaces introduced by Feichtinger
Feichtinger2003Modulation
in the 1980s using the short-time Fourier transform to measure the decay and the regularity of the function differently from the usual
L^{p}
Sobolev spaces or Besov-Triebel spaces. By the frequency-uniform localization technique (
Wang2007Frequency
;
Cunanan2015Wiener
), Wiener amalgam spaces and modulation spaces could be defined by the uniform decomposition of frequency spaces in contrast with the dyadic decomposition in the definition of Besov-Triebel spaces. Therefore, Wiener amalgam spaces have many properties different from the Besov-Triebel spaces, but similar to modulation spaces. For instance, the Fourier multiplier
e^{i\lvert D\rvert^{\alpha}}(0<\alpha\leqslant 1)
is unbounded on any classical Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}
or Besov spaces
B_{p,q}
with
p\neq 2
, but bounded on all Wiener spaces
W_{p,q}^{s}
and modulation spaces
M_{p,q}^{s}
. One can see
Miyachi1981some
;
Guo2020Sharp
for more details. Even so, Wiener amalgam spaces have some distinctive properties from modulation spaces.. For example, the Fourier multiplier
e^{i\lvert D\rvert^{\alpha}}(1<\alpha\leqslant 2)
is unbounded on any modulation spaces
W_{p,q}^{s}
with
p\neq q
, but bounded on all modulation spaces
M_{p,q}^{s}
. One can refer
Benyi2007Unimodular
;
Miyachi2009Estimates
;
Tomita2010Unimodular
;
Chen2012Asymptotic
. These Fourier multipliers play a significant role in nonlinear dispersive equations such as nonlinear Schrödinger and wave equations. As a result, it is natural to solve these nonlinear equations in Wiener amalgams and modulation spaces. There are numerous papers about these questions. One can see
Wang2007global
;
Cordero2008Strichartz
;
Cordero2009Remarks
;
Benyi2009Local
;
Chaichenets2017existence
;
Chen2020dissipative
;
Oh2020Global
;
Bhimani2020Norm
.

One basic but important consideration is what these spaces are like embedded in each other, which can tell us how different they are. As for modulation spaces, Wang-Huang in
Wang2007Frequency
gave the full characterization of the embedding between modulation spaces and Besov spaces. Actually, we can define the
\alpha
-modulation spaces (
Borup2006Banach
;
Han2014$$
), which contain modulation spaces with
\alpha=0
and Besov spaces with
\alpha=1
. Guo et al. in
Guo2018Full
gave the sharp conditions between the
\alpha
-modulation spaces. Kobayashi and Sugimoto in
Kobayashi2011inclusion
proved the sharp embedding between Sobolev spaces and modulation spaces. As for Wiener amalgam spaces, Cunanan et al. in
Cunanan2015Inclusion
gave some necessary and sufficiency conditions for the inclusion relation between
W_{p,q}^{s}
and
L^{p}
. Later their results were completely extended by Guo et al. in
Guo2017Characterization
. Guo et al. characterized the embedding between
W_{p,q}^{s}
and
X
, where
X\in\left\{B_{p,q},L^{p},h_{p}\right\}
by a mild characterization of the embedding between Triebel and Wiener amalgam spaces.

In this paper, we consider the more general embeddings between
W_{p,q}^{s}
and
X
, where
X\in\left\{B_{p_{0},q_{0}},L^{r},h_{r},F_{p,q_{0}},M_{p_{0},q_{0}},M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}\right\}
. Here
(p_{0},q_{0},r)
could not be equal to
(p,q,p).

, denote

Preliminaries

2.1
Notation

We write
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to denote the Schwartz space of all complex-valued rapidly decreasing infinity differentiable functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and
\mathscr{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
to denote the dual space of
\mathscr{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, all called the space of all tempered distributions. For simplification, we omit
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
without causing ambiguity. The Fourier transform is defined by
{\mathscr{F}}f(\xi)=\hat{f}(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x)e^{-ix\xi}d\xi
, and the inverse Fourier transform by
{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}f(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(\xi)e^{ix\xi}d\xi
.

We use the notation
I\lesssim J
if there is an independently constant C such that
I\leqslant CJ
. Also we denote
I\approx J
if
I\lesssim J
and
J\lesssim I
. For
1\leqslant p\leqslant\infty
, we denote the dual index
p^{\prime}
with
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
, for
0<p<1
, denote
p^{\prime}=\infty
. For
0<p,q\leqslant\infty,d\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we also denote

\displaystyle a(p,q)

\displaystyle:=d(1/p+1/q-1);

\displaystyle\tau(p,q)

\displaystyle:=d\left(0\vee(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})\vee(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}-1)\right);

\displaystyle\sigma(p,q)

\displaystyle:=d\left(0\wedge(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p})\wedge(\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{p}-1)\right).

These indexes play a great role in the embedding between modulation spaces and Besov spaces (
Wang2007Frequency
).

2.2
Sobolev and local Hardy spaces

For
0<p<\infty
, we define the
L^{p}
norm:

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{p}=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\lvert f(x)\rvert^{p}dx\right)^{1/p}

and
\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}=\esssup_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\lvert f(x)\rvert
.
We also define the
L^{p}
Sobolev norm :

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{L^{s,p}}=\left\|(I-\triangle)^{s/2}f\right\|_{p},

where
(I-\triangle)^{s/2}={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}(1+\lvert\xi\rvert^{2})^{s/2}{\mathscr{F}}
is the Bessel potential. Recall that the Sobolev spaces is defined by
L^{s,p}=\left\{f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}:\left\|f\right\|_{L^{s,p}}<\infty\right\}
. For more details, One can see
Grafakos2009Modern
.

Next, we turn to introduce the local Hardy space of Goldberg
Goldberg1979local
. Let
\psi\in\mathscr{S}
with
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\psi(x)dx\neq 0
. Denote
\psi_{t}(x)=t^{-d}\psi(t^{-1}x)
. Let
0<p<\infty
, the local Hardy spaces is defined by

\displaystyle h_{p}:=\left\{f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}:\left\|f\right\|_{h_{p}}=\left\|\sup_{0<t<1}\lvert\psi_{t}*f\rvert\right\|_{p}<\infty\right\}.

Similarly, we can define
h_{p}^{s}:=\left\{f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}:\left\|(I-\triangle)^{s/2}f\right\|_{h_{p}}<\infty\right\}
. We note that the definition of the local Hardy spaces is independent of the choice of
\psi\in\mathscr{S}
. The local Hardy spaces could also be defined by
h_{p}
-atom. One can refer
Triebel1992Theory
.

2.3
Modulation and Wiener amalgam spaces

Let
0<p,q\leqslant\infty,s\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the short time Fourier transform (STFT) of
f
respect to a window function
g\in\mathscr{S}
is defined as (see
Feichtinger2003Modulation
;
Groechenig2001Foundations
):

\displaystyle V_{g}f(x,\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(t)\overline{g(t-x)}e^{-it\xi}dt.

We denote

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}

\displaystyle=\left\|V_{g}f(x,\xi)\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{s}\right\|_{L_{\xi}^{q}L_{x}^{p}},

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{W_{p,q}^{s}}

\displaystyle=\left\|V_{g}f(x,\xi)\left\langle\xi\right\rangle^{s}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}L_{\xi}^{q}},

where
\left\langle\xi\right\rangle=(1+\lvert\xi\rvert^{2})^{1/2}
.

Modulation space
M_{p,q}^{s}
are defined as the space of all tempered distribution
f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
for which
\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}
is finite. Wiener space
W_{p,q}^{s}
are defined as the space of all tempered distribution
f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
for which
\left\|f\right\|_{W_{p,q}^{s}}
is finite.

Also, we know another equivalent definition of modulation spaces and Wiener spaces by uniform decomposition of frequency space (see
Groechenig2001Foundations
;
Cunanan2015Wiener
).

Let
\sigma
be a smooth cut-off function adapted to the unit cube
[-1/2,1/2]^{d}
and
\sigma=0
outside the cube
[-3/4,3/4]^{d}
, we write
\sigma_{k}=\sigma(\cdot-k)
, and assume that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\sigma_{k}(\xi)\equiv 1,\ \forall\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

Denote
\sigma_{k}(\xi)=\sigma(\xi-k)
, and
\Box_{k}={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\sigma_{k}{\mathscr{F}}
, then we have the following equivalent norm of modulation space and Wiener spaces:

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}\left\|\Box_{k}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\right\|_{\ell_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{q}},

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{W_{p,q}^{s}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left\|\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s}\Box_{k}f\right\|_{\ell_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}^{q}}\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}.

For simplicity, we denote
X_{p,q}^{s}
to represent
M_{p,q}^{s}
or
W_{p,q}^{s}
below. We simply write
X_{p,q}
instead of
X_{p,q}^{0}
. One can prove the
X_{p,q}^{s}
norm is independent of the choice of cut-off function
\sigma
. Also
X_{p,q}^{s}
is a quasi Banach space and when
1\leqslant p,q\leqslant\infty
,
X_{p,q}^{s}
is a Banach space. When
p,q<\infty
, then
\mathscr{S}
is dense in
X_{p,q}^{s}
. Also,
X_{p,q}^{s}
has some basic properties, we list them in the following lemma (see
Wang2007Frequency
;
Wang2011Harmonic
;
Cunanan2015Wiener
;
Groechenig2001Foundations
).

Lemma 15
.

Let
s,s_{0},s_{1},\in{\mathbb{R}},0<p,p_{0},p_{1},q,q_{0},q_{1}\leqslant\infty
.

(1)

If
s_{0}\leqslant s_{1},p_{1}\leqslant p_{0},q_{1}\leqslant q_{0}
, we have
X_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{s_{1}}\hookrightarrow X_{p_{0},q_{0}}^{s_{0}}
.

(2)

When
p,q<\infty
, the dual space of
X_{p,q}^{s}
is
X_{p^{\prime},q^{\prime}}^{-s}
.

(3)

The interpolation spaces theorem is true for
X_{p,q}^{s}
, i.e. for
0<\theta<1

when

s=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1},\quad\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}},\quad\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}},

we have
\left(X_{p_{0},q_{0}}^{s_{0}},X_{p_{1},q_{1}}^{s_{1}}\right)_{\theta}=X_{p,q}^{s}
.

(4)

When
q_{1}<q,s+d/q>s_{1}+d/q_{1}
, then we have
X_{p,q}^{s}\hookrightarrow X_{p,q_{1}}^{s_{1}}
.

(5)

When
p\geqslant q,M_{p,q}^{s}\hookrightarrow W_{p,q}^{s}
. When
p\leqslant q,W_{p,q}^{s}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}^{s}
.

Lemma 16

(
Ruzhansky2011Changes
)
.

Let
0<p,q\leqslant\infty
, and
f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}
with support in
B(0,1)
. Then
f\in M_{p,q}
is equivalent to
f\in{\mathscr{F}}L^{q}
, is also equivalent to
f\in W_{p,q}
. Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}}\approx\left\|f\right\|_{W_{p,q}}\approx\left\|f\right\|_{{\mathscr{F}}L^{q}}.

2.4
Besov-Triebel spaces

Let
0<p,q\leqslant\infty,s\in{\mathbb{R}},
choose
\psi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\rightarrow[0,1]
be a smooth radial bump function adapted to the ball
B(0,2)
:
\psi(\xi)=1
as
\lvert\xi\rvert\leqslant 1
and
\psi(\xi)=0
as
\lvert\xi\rvert\geqslant 2
. We denote
\varphi(\xi)=\psi(\xi)-\psi(2\xi)
, and
\varphi_{j}(\xi)=\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)
for
1\leqslant j,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\varphi_{0}(\xi)=1-\sum_{j\geqslant 1}\varphi_{j}(\xi)
. Denote
\triangle_{j}={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\varphi_{j}{\mathscr{F}}
.
We say that
\left\{\triangle_{j}\right\}_{j\geqslant 0}
are the dyadic decomposition operators. The Besov spaces
B_{p,q}^{s}
and the Triebel spaces
F_{p,q}^{s}
are defined in the following way :

\displaystyle B_{p,q}^{s}=\left\{f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):\left\|f\right\|_{B_{p,q}^{s}}=\left\|2^{js}\left\|\triangle_{j}f\right\|_{L_{x}^{p}}\right\|_{\ell_{j\geqslant 0}^{q}}<\infty\right\},

2.5
\alpha
-modulation spaces

Definition 1

(
\alpha
-covering)
.

A countable set
\left\{Q_{i}\right\}_{i}
, where
Q_{i}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, is called a
\alpha
-covering of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
if:

(i)

{\mathbb{R}}^{d}=\cup_{i}Q_{i}
,

(ii)

\#\left\{Q^{\prime}\in Q_{i}:Q^{\prime}\cap Q\neq\emptyset\right\}\leqslant c(d),
uniformly for
Q\in Q_{i}
,

(iii)

\left\langle x\right\rangle^{\alpha d}\approx\lvert Q_{i}\rvert
uniformly for
x\in Q_{i}
.

Definition 2

(
\alpha
-Modulation spaces,
Han2014$$
)
.

Let
\alpha<1
, denote
\alpha=\alpha/(1-\alpha)
, suppose that
C>c>0
are two appropriate constants such that
\left\{B_{k}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
is a
\alpha
-covering of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, where
B_{k}=B(\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\alpha}k,\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\alpha})
. We can choose a Schwartz function sequence
\left\{\eta_{k}^{\alpha}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\lvert\eta_{k}^{\alpha}(\xi)\rvert\gtrsim 1,\ \mbox{ if }\lvert\xi-\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}k\rvert<c\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}};\\
{\mbox{supp}\ }\eta_{k}^{\alpha}\subseteq\left\{\xi:\lvert\xi-\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}k\rvert<C\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\right\};\\
\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\eta_{k}^{\alpha}(\xi)\equiv 1,\ \forall\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d};\\
\lvert\partial^{\gamma}\eta_{k}^{\alpha}(\xi)\rvert\leqslant C_{\alpha}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{-\frac{\alpha\lvert\gamma\rvert}{1-\alpha}},\ \forall\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d},\end{cases}

where
C_{\alpha}
is a positive constant depending only on
d
and
\alpha
. We usually call these
\left\{\eta_{k}^{\alpha}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
the bounded admission partition of unity corresponding (
\alpha-
BAPU) to the
\alpha
-covering
\left\{B_{k}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
. The frequency decomposition operators can be defined by

\displaystyle\Box_{k}^{\alpha}:={\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\eta_{k}^{\alpha}{\mathscr{F}}.

Let
1\leqslant p,q\leqslant\infty,s\in{\mathbb{R}},\alpha\in[0,1)
, the
\alpha
-modulation space is defined by

\displaystyle M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}=\left\{f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}:\left\|f\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}}=\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{sq/(1-\alpha)}\left\|\Box_{k}^{\alpha}f\right\|_{p}^{q}\right)^{1/q}<\infty\right\},

with the usual modification when
q=\infty
.

When
\alpha=0
, we usually denote
M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}
by
M_{p,q}^{s}
.
M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}
have some basic properties as follows. One can find their proofs in
Han2014$$
.

Lemma 18
.

Let
0<p\leqslant\infty,0<q,q_{1}\leqslant\infty,s,s_{1}\in{\mathbb{R}},\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then we have

(1)

if
s\geqslant 0
,
q_{1}\geqslant q
, then
M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q_{1}}^{0,\alpha}
;

(2)

if
q>q_{1},s>d(1-\alpha)(1/q_{1}-1/q)
, then
M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q_{1}}^{0,\alpha}.

The sharp embeddings between
M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}
have been proved before. One can refer
Han2014$$
and
Guo2018Full
.

Lemma 19
.

Let
0<p,q\leqslant\infty,s\in{\mathbb{R}},\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then

(1)

M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}
if and only if
s\geqslant\alpha\tau(p,q)
.

(2)

M_{p,q}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}
if and only if

(3)

B_{p,q}^{s}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}^{0,\alpha}

(4)

\displaystyle=\left\|a_{k}\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s/(1-\alpha)}\right\|_{\ell_{k}^{q}}=\left\|a_{k}\right\|_{\ell_{q}^{s/(1-\alpha),0}}.

\displaystyle\left\|f^{N}\right\|_{W_{p,q}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\|\Box_{k}f^{N}\|_{\ell_{k}^{q}}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left\|\|a_{k}T_{Nk}\left({\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\sigma(\xi-\xi_{k})\right)\|_{\ell_{k}^{q}}\right\|_{p}=\left\|\|a_{k}T_{Nk}\left({\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\sigma\right)\|_{\ell_{k}^{q}}\right\|_{p}.

Take
N\rightarrow\infty
, use the almost orthogonality of

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left\|f^{N}\right\|_{W_{p,q}}=\left\|\|a_{k}{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\sigma\|_{\ell_{k}^{q}}\right\|_{p}=\left\|a_{k}\right\|_{\ell_{p}^{0,0}}.

Take the estimates of
f^{N}
into (

Proposition 11
.

Let
0<q\leqslant\infty,0<p<\infty,s\in{\mathbb{R}},\alpha\in(0,1)
. Then

(1)

M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}\hookrightarrow W_{p,q}\Longrightarrow s\geqslant\alpha d(1/q-1/2);

(2)

W_{p,q}\hookrightarrow M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}\Longrightarrow s\leqslant\alpha d(1/q-1/2).

Proof.

We only give the proof of the assertion (1). For any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
, denote
\wedge_{k}=\left\{\ell\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}:\Box_{\ell}\Box_{k}^{\alpha}=\Box_{\ell}\right\}
. One can easily see that
\#\wedge_{k}\approx\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\alpha d/(1-\alpha)}.
Let
\overrightarrow{\omega}=\left\{\omega_{k}\right\}_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}
be a sequence of independent random variables (for instance, one can choose the Rademacher functions). Denote
f^{\overrightarrow{\omega}}=\sum_{\ell\in\wedge_{k}}\omega_{k}{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}(\sigma(\xi-\ell))
. Then by orthogonality, we have

\displaystyle\left\|f^{\overrightarrow{\omega}}\right\|_{W_{p,q}}

\displaystyle=\left\|\|\omega_{\ell}{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}(\sigma(\xi-\ell)\|_{\ell_{\ell\in\wedge_{k}}^{q}}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\left(\#\wedge_{k}\right)^{1/q}=\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\frac{\alpha d}{q(1-\alpha)}};

\displaystyle\left\|f^{\overrightarrow{\omega}}\right\|_{M_{p,q}^{s,\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle k\right\rangle^{s/(1-\alpha)}\left\|f^{\overrightarrow{\omega}}\right\|_{p}.

Note that
0<p<\infty
, then by Khinchin’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathbb{E}\left\|f^{\overrightarrow{\omega}}\right\|_{p}^{p}\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\approx\left\|\left(\sum_{\ell\in\wedge_{k}}\lvert{\mathscr{F}^{-1}}\left(\sigma(\xi-\ell)\right)\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\approx\left(\#\wedge_{k}\right)^{1/2}=\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\frac{\alpha d}{2(1-\alpha)}}.

Take these estimates of
f^{\overrightarrow{\omega}}
into (
11
), we have

\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\frac{\alpha d}{q(1-\alpha)}}\lesssim\left\langle k\right\rangle^{\frac{s}{1-\alpha}+\frac{\alpha d}{2(1-\alpha)}}.

Take

s\geqslant\alpha d(1/q-1/2).

∎

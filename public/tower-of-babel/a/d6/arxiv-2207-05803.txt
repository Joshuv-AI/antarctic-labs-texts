The linearized Calderón problem for polyharmonic operators
Suman Kumar Sahoo and Mikko Salo

Abstract
. In this article we consider a linearized Calderón problem for polyharmonic operators of order 2 ​ m ​ ( m ≥ 2 ) 2m\ (m\geq 2) in the spirit of Calderón’s original work [ 7 ] . We give a uniqueness result for determining coefficients of order ≤ 2 ​ m − 1 \leq 2m-1 up to gauge, based on inverting momentum ray transforms.

1.
introduction

Let
n\geq 3
and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. For
m\geq 2
, we consider the following polyharmonic operator
\mathcal{L}
with lower order anisotropic perturbations up to order
2m-1
:

(1.1)

\mathcal{L}(x,D)=(-\Delta)^{m}+Q(x,D),

where

(1.2)

Q(x,D)=\sum\limits_{l=0}^{2m-1}a^{l}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}(x)\,D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}

is a differential operator of order
2m-1
with
1\leq i_{1},\cdots,i_{l}\leq n
and
a^{l}
is a smooth symmetric tensor field of order
l
in
\overline{\Omega}
. Einstein summation convention is assumed for repeated indices throughout the article.

The boundary measurements corresponding to the equation
\mathcal{L}(x,D)u=0
in
\Omega

may be encoded in terms of the
Cauchy data set
(see e.g.
[
27
]
for the general case)

\displaystyle C_{\mathcal{L}}=\{(u|_{\partial\Omega},\partial_{\nu}u|_{\partial\Omega},\cdots,\partial^{2m-1}_{\nu}u|_{\partial\Omega})\,:\,u\in H^{2m}(\Omega),\ \mathcal{L}u=0\}.

The inverse problem of interest is to determine some information on the coefficients of the operator
\mathcal{L}
, up to suitable gauge transformations, from the knowledge of the Cauchy data set
\mathcal{L}
.

For various special choices of boundary conditions, one could define a Dirichlet-to-Neumann type operator for solutions of
\mathcal{L}u=0
in
\Omega
. This requires that the boundary conditions lead to an elliptic boundary value problem (Lopatinskii-Shapiro condition) and that
0
is not an eigenvalue for this problem. For example, one could consider solutions with the clamped boundary conditions

u|_{\partial\Omega}=f_{0},\ \partial_{\nu}u|_{\partial\Omega}=f_{1},\ \ldots\ ,\ \partial_{\nu}^{m-1}u|_{\partial\Omega}=f_{m-1}

and consider the boundary map

\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}^{C}:(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1})\mapsto(\partial_{\nu}^{m}u|_{\partial\Omega},\ldots,\partial_{\nu}^{2m-1}u|_{\partial\Omega}).

Alternatively, one could consider Navier boundary conditions

u|_{\partial\Omega}=f_{0},\ (-\Delta)u|_{\partial\Omega}=f_{1},\ \ldots\ ,\ (-\Delta)^{m-1}u|_{\partial\Omega}=f_{m-1}

and consider the boundary map

\Lambda_{\mathcal{L}}^{N}:(f_{0},\ldots,f_{m-1})\mapsto(\partial_{\nu}u|_{\partial\Omega},\ \partial_{\nu}(-\Delta)u|_{\partial\Omega},\ \ldots,\partial_{\nu}(-\Delta)^{m-1}u|_{\partial\Omega}).

If
0
is not an eigenvalue of the corresponding elliptic boundary value problem, then knowing the Cauchy data set is equivalent to knowing the boundary map. One can view the Cauchy data set as a generalization of such boundary maps that is independent of the choice of (elliptic) boundary conditions.

The most classical case is
m=1
, so that
\mathcal{L}
is a second order elliptic operator. The prototypical inverse problem for such operators is the inverse conductivity problem posed by Calderón
[
7
]
. The original problem was stated for the equation
\mathrm{div}(\gamma\nabla u)=0
, but if the conductivity function
\gamma
is
C^{2}
and positive one can reduce matters to the Schrödinger equation
(\Delta+q)u=0
for some potential
q
. One can further consider equations of the form
\Delta u+A(x)\cdot\nabla u+qu=0
for some vector field
A
and potential
q
. This includes the magnetic Schrödinger equation and equations with convection terms, which are clearly of the form (
1.1
). Various results are known for related inverse problems for determining the vector field
A(x)
, sometimes up to a gauge of the form
A\mapsto A+\nabla\phi
, and the potential
q
from boundary measurements. We refer the reader to the survey
[
34
,
35
]
for more information and references on the second order case.

In this paper we consider a Calderón type problem for polyharmonic operators of order
2m
with a lower order perturbation up to order
2m-1
. There are many previous results on inverse problems for polyharmonic operators, including
[
20
,
21
,
15
,
14
,
2
,
3
,
5
]
. In all of these results one only determines lower order coefficients up to order
\leq m
from boundary measurements. The reason for this restriction is that the method of complex geometrical optics solutions used in these inverse problems requires certain
L^{2}
Carleman estimates, which can become highly delicate for higher order operators. In the previous works the required Carleman estimate for the polyharmonic operator has been obtained by using a well known Carleman estimate for second order equations several times in a row. Since there is a loss of half derivative in the original Carleman estimate (it involves a limiting Carleman weight), iterating it many times leads to a loss of several derivatives and thus restricts the method to lower order terms of degree
\leq m
.

In this article we will consider a general higher order elliptic operator given by (
1.1
) and recover several lower order coefficients up to order
2m-1
. However, because of the restriction mentioned above, we are not able to consider the full nonlinear inverse problem. Rather, we will only consider the linearization of this inverse problem. In the linearized problem it is sufficient to use solutions of the “free” equation
(-\Delta)^{m}v=0
in the recovery of the coefficients, and this avoids the need to use Carleman estimates. However, even in the linearized problem the recovery of higher order terms becomes intricate. We will follow the ideas of
[
4
]
where it was observed that momentum ray transforms (MRT) appear naturally in solving inverse problem for polyharmonic operators. The inversion of various MRT is crucial for recovering the coefficients. In particular, to handle the coefficient of order
2m-1
, we study the kernel of a partial MRT. To do so, we demonstrate a new trace free Helmholtz type decomposition result for symmetric tensor fields.

The rest of the article is organised as follows. In Section
2
we derive the linearized inverse problem and state our main results. Section
3
is devoted to the unique recovery of tensor fields up to order
2m-2
. Then Section
4
deals with the recovery of coefficients up to order
2m-1
under certain assumptions. Section
5
deals with gauge transformations and recovery of coefficients up to natural obstructions; see Theorem
2.3
. Finally, in Section
6
we describe the kernel of MRT which is the key tool for proving our main results. The required Helmholtz type decomposition result for tensor fields is then proved in Section
7
. In Appendix
A
we review some known results and give the construction of special solutions (known as CGO solutions) of
(-\Delta)^{m}u=0
. The Navier to Neumann map is then linearized in Appendix
B
by computing the Fréchet derivative.

2.
Linearization and main results

We now derive the formal linearization of the polyharmonic inverse problem. The forward operator is formally given by the map
\mathcal{L}\mapsto C_{\mathcal{L}}
. Since
C_{\mathcal{L}}
is a set and not necessarily an element in a Banach space, we cannot directly compute the Fréchet derivative of the forward operator. We instead assume that

(2.1)

\displaystyle C_{\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}}=C_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}\text{ for all }\epsilon\in(-a,a),\quad a>0,

where
\mathcal{L}_{0}=(-\Delta)^{m}
,
\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}=(-\Delta)^{m}+Q(x,\epsilon,D)
and
Q(x,\epsilon,D)=\sum\limits_{l=0}^{2m-1}a^{l}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}(x,\epsilon)\,D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}
. We assume that
a^{l}
depend smoothly on
x
and
\epsilon
and that
a^{l}(x,0)=0
for all
0\leq l\leq 2m-1
, i.e. we are computing the linearization at the case of zero lower order coefficients.
Using that
C_{\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}}=C_{\mathcal{L}_{0}}
, from
[
27
, Lemma 2.8]
we have the integral identity

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle(\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}-\mathcal{L}_{0})u,v\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=0,

for any
u,v\in H^{2m}(\Omega)
satisfying

\mathcal{L}_{\epsilon}u=0
and
\mathcal{L}_{0}^{*}\,v=0
in
\Omega
. This can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{2m-1}a^{l}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}(x,\epsilon)\,D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}u(x,\epsilon),v(x)\right\rangle=0\text{ for all }\epsilon\in(-a,a).

Differentiating this with respect to
\epsilon
we obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{2m-1}\partial_{\epsilon}(a^{l}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}(x,\epsilon))\,D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}u(x,\epsilon),v(x)\right\rangle+\left\langle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{2m-1}a^{l}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}(x,\epsilon)\,D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}\partial_{\epsilon}u(x,\epsilon),v(x)\right\rangle=0.

Writing
w=u|_{\epsilon=0}
and setting
\epsilon=0
, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left\langle\sum\limits_{l=0}^{2m-1}\partial_{\epsilon}a^{l}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}(x,0)\,D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}w(x),v(x)\right\rangle=0.

Setting
\epsilon=0
in the equation
\mathcal{L}u=0
and using
a^{l}(x,0)=0
for all
0\leq l\leq 2m-1
, we see that
w
solves
(-\Delta)^{m}w=0
. It follows that the identity (
B.2
) holds for any
w
and
v
solving
(-\Delta)^{m}w=(-\Delta)^{m}v=0
in
\Omega
.

We can now formulate (with slightly different notation) the main uniqueness question for the linearized inverse problem considered in this article.

Question 2.1
.

If
a^{l}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}
for
0\leq l\leq 2m-1
are smooth tensor fields in
\overline{\Omega}
and if

\int_{\Omega}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{2m-1}a^{l}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}(D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}u)v\,dx=0

for all
u,v\in H^{2m}(\Omega)
satisfying
(-\Delta)^{m}u=(-\Delta)^{m}v=0
, is it true that the coefficients
a^{l}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}
vanish possibly up to suitable gauge transformations?

We mention that if the Cauchy data set is the graph of suitable Dirichlet-to-Neumann type map, one can make the above formal derivation rigorous and the linearized problem is still given by Question
2.1
. See Appendix
B
where this is done for the Navier boundary conditions.

We now show that
it is not in general possible to recover all the coefficients in Question
2.1
due to the presence of a gauge. One possible gauge is obtained by replacing
\mathcal{L}
by
e^{-\phi}\mathcal{L}e^{\phi}
for a suitable function
\phi
. Note that if

(2.4)

\phi\in C^{2m}(\overline{\Omega})\text{ and $\partial_{\nu}^{j}\phi|_{\partial\Omega}=0$ for $0\leq j\leq 2m-1$},

then
u
and
e^{\phi}u
will have the same Cauchy data up to order
2m-1
. Thus for such functions
\phi
one has

C_{\mathcal{L}}=C_{e^{-\phi}\mathcal{L}e^{\phi}}.

It is easy to compute the highest order terms for the conjugated operator:

\displaystyle e^{-\phi}\mathcal{L}e^{\phi}u

\displaystyle=e^{-\phi}((-\Delta)^{m}+a^{2m-1}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{2m-1}}D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{2m-1}}+\ldots)e^{\phi}u

\displaystyle=(e^{-\phi}(-\Delta)e^{\phi})^{m}\,u+e^{-\phi}(a^{2m-1}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{2m-1}}D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{2m-1}}+\ldots)(e^{\phi}u)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}(\Delta\phi+|\nabla\phi|^{2}+2\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla+\Delta)^{m}\,u+e^{-\phi}(a^{2m-1}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{2m-1}}D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{2m-1}}+\ldots)(e^{\phi}u)

\displaystyle=(-\Delta)^{m}u+(a^{2m-1}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{2m-1}}D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{2m-1}}+(-1)^{m}2m\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla(\Delta)^{m-1})u+\ldots.

Above
\ldots
denotes lower order terms. Thus there is always a gauge invariance, where one can add terms of the form
(-1)^{m}2m\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla(\Delta)^{m-1}
to the term of order
2m-1
. There will be corresponding changes in the lower order terms as well.

However, if we only consider operators
\mathcal{L}
for which
a^{2m-1}=0
, then this type of gauge does not arise: if the term
(-1)^{m}2m\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla(\Delta)^{m-1}
vanishes identically, then necessarily
\nabla\phi=0
and hence
\phi=0
using the condition
\phi|_{\partial\Omega}=0
.

Our first main result states that for operators with
a^{2m-1}=0
, one can recover all the lower order terms completely in the linearized inverse problem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\sum_{l=0}^{2m-2}a^{l}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}u\,v=0\quad\mbox{whenever}\quad\Delta^{m}u=\Delta^{m}v=0.

Then
a^{l}=0
in
\Omega
for
0\leq l\leq 2m-2
.

As an immediate corollary of Theorem
2.1
one obtains the following density result on certain spaces of symmetric tensor fields. For density results involving harmonic functions and tensor fields see e.g.
[
8
]
. One may also expect other related density results as a consequence of the method of proof of Theorem
2.1
. See Remark
3.3
.

Corollary 2.2
.

For each even integer
M\geq 0
consider the set

\mathcal{A}_{M}\coloneqq\mbox{span}\left\{v\,\oplus_{0\leq|\alpha|\leq M}D^{\alpha}u:\Delta^{\left[\frac{M+2}{2}\right]}u=\Delta^{\left[\frac{M+2}{2}\right]}v=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\right\}.

Then
\mathcal{A}_{M}
is dense in
\mathbf{S}^{M}\left(C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})\right)=\oplus_{p=0}^{M}S^{p}\left(C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})\right)
, where
S^{p}\left(C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})\right)
stands for the space of smooth symmetric
p
tensor fields in
\overline{\Omega}
and
\oplus
is the direct sum.

Note that for
m=1
, Theorem
2.1
reduces to the statement that if
f\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}f\,u\,v=0\quad\mbox{whenever}\quad\Delta u=\Delta v=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,

then
f
vanishes identically in
\Omega
. This is just the linearized Calderón problem for the Schrödinger equation (linearized at the zero potential), which has been studied in various settings including partial data
[
11
,
32
]
and Riemannian manifolds
[
16
,
22
]
. Results for such linearized problems have recently become important in the context of inverse problems for nonlinear PDEs, see e.g.
[
23
,
25
]
.

Now we state our second main result. It also includes terms of order
2m-1
and gives a complete answer (modulo an assumption for
a^{2m-1}
on
\partial\Omega
) for the linearized Calderón problem for polyharmonic operators when
m=2,3
, showing that one can determine the coefficients uniquely up to the gauge transform
\mathcal{L}\to e^{-\phi}\mathcal{L}e^{\phi}
. In particular, there are no other gauge invariances in this problem. We also obtain a partial answer when
m\geq 4
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be as in (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) and suppose
\partial^{r}_{\nu}a^{2m-1}=0
on
\partial\Omega
for all
0\leq r\leq 2m-1
. Assume that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\sum_{l=0}^{2m-1}a^{l}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}u\,v=0\quad\mbox{whenever}\quad\Delta^{m}u=\Delta^{m}v=0.

1.

For
m=2
or
m=3
, one has

\mathcal{L}=e^{-\phi}(-\Delta)^{m}e^{\phi}

for some
\phi
satisfying (
2.4
). In particular,

\displaystyle a^{2m-1}

\displaystyle=i^{m-1}_{\delta}(\nabla\phi)

where
i_{\delta}
is the symmetrization with Kronecker delta tensor; see (
3.1
) for the definition.

2.

For
m\geq 4
, if we additionally assume that
a^{2m-1}=i^{m-1}_{\delta}A^{1}
for some vector field
A^{1}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
, then
\mathcal{L}=e^{-\phi}(-\Delta)^{m}e^{\phi}
for some
\phi
satisfying (
2.4
). In particular,

\displaystyle A^{1}=\nabla\phi.

Remark 2.4
.

In Theorem
2.3
, the boundary assumption on the coefficient
a^{2m-1}
can be removed through a boundary determination result.

In the next section we present the proof of Theorem
2.1
. Then, in Section
4
, we prove few results (mainly Proposition
4.3
), which we then employ in Section
5
to demonstrate Theorem
2.3
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
2.1

In this section we demonstrate the proof of Theorem
2.1
. The proof will be based on the trace free decomposition on
S^{m}
, the space of symmetric
m
tensor fields over
\overline{\Omega}
. To this end, we define two operators
i_{\delta}:S^{m}\rightarrow S^{m+2}
and
j_{\delta}:S^{m}\rightarrow S^{m-2}
as follows:

(3.1)

\displaystyle(i_{\delta}f)_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m+2}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sigma(f_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}\otimes\delta_{i_{m+1}i_{m+2}})

(3.2)

\displaystyle(j_{\delta}f)_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m-2}}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}f_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m-2}kk},

where
\sigma
denotes the symmetrization of a tensor field. Here
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta tensor which is equal to
1
for
i=j
and
0
otherwise. We also define
j_{\delta}f=0
for
f\in S^{0}
or
f\in S^{1}
. Note that the operators
i_{\delta}
and
j_{\delta}
are dual to each other with respect to the
L^{2}
inner product. Based on this observation we write the trace free decomposition of tensor fields from
[
9
,
28
]
as follows: for
l\geq 2
one has

(3.3)

\displaystyle a^{l}=\sum\limits_{k_{l}=0}^{[\frac{l}{2}]}i^{k_{l}}_{\delta}b^{l,k_{l}}

where
b^{l,k_{l}}
is a symmetric tensor field of order
l-2k_{l}
with
j_{\delta}b^{l,k_{l}}=0
. Any
f\in S^{m}
satisfies
j_{\delta}f=0
is known as trace free tensor field. For
l=0,1
any tensor field
a^{l}
is trace free.

Note that (
3.3
) is an orthogonal decomposition on the space of square integrable symmetric tensor fields over
\overline{\Omega}
, denoted by
L^{2}(S^{m})
. This implies that
a^{l}=0
if and only if
b^{l,k_{l}}=0
for all
k_{l}
with
0\leq k_{l}\leq[\frac{l}{2}]
; see
[
31
, Equation 6.4.2]
. Thus in the remainder of this section we show that under the conditions in Theorem
2.1
, if
a^{l}
has the decomposition (
3.3
) for
0\leq l\leq 2m-2
, then
b^{l,k_{l}}=0
for all
k_{l}
with
0\leq k_{l}\leq[\frac{l}{2}]
. This completes the proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We insert (
3.3
) in the integral identity (
2.5
) and obtain

(3.4)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{2m-2}\sum\limits_{k_{l}=0}^{[\frac{l}{2}]}(i_{\delta}^{k_{l}}b^{l,k_{l}})_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}u\,v=\sum_{l=0}^{2m-2}\sum\limits_{k_{l}=0}^{[\frac{l}{2}]}b^{l,k_{l}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l-2k_{l}}}D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{l-2k_{l}}}(-\Delta)^{k_{l}}u\,v

whenever
\Delta^{m}u=\Delta^{m}v=0
. We now recall suitable complex geometrical optics (CGO) solutions to the polyharmonic equation given in (
A.5
),

\displaystyle u(x;h)=

\displaystyle e^{\frac{1}{h}(e_{1}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})\cdot x}\left(a_{0}(x)+ha_{1}(x)+\dots+h^{m-1}a_{m-1}(x)+r(x;h)\right)=e^{\frac{1}{h}(e_{1}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})\cdot x}\tilde{A},

\displaystyle v(x;h)=

\displaystyle e^{-\frac{1}{h}(e_{1}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})\cdot x}\left(b_{0}(x)+hb_{1}(x)+\dots+h^{m-1}b_{m-1}(x)+\widetilde{r}(x;h)\right)=e^{-\frac{1}{h}(e_{1}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})\cdot x}\tilde{B},

where the error terms
r(x;h)
and
\widetilde{r}(x;h)
satisfy the estimate
\lVert r(x,h)\rVert_{H_{scl}^{2m}}\leq ch^{m}
and
\lVert\widetilde{r}(x,h)\rVert_{H_{scl}^{2m}}\leq ch^{m}
. Moreover, we also have that
a_{j}
and
b_{j}
are smooth functions in
\overline{\Omega}
for all
j
with
0\leq j\leq m-1
.
Substituting the expressions for
u
and
v
given above in the identity (
3.4
) and using (
A.2
) and (
A.3
) we obtain

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{2m-2}\sum\limits_{k_{l}=0}^{[\frac{l}{2}]}b^{l,k_{l}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l-2k_{l}}}D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{l-2k_{l}}}\left[e^{\frac{1}{h}(e_{1}+i\eta_{2})\cdot x}(-\frac{1}{h}T-\Delta)^{k_{l}}\tilde{A}\right]e^{-\frac{1}{h}(e_{1}+i\eta_{2})\cdot x}\tilde{B}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{2m-2}\sum\limits_{k_{l}=0}^{[\frac{l}{2}]}b^{l,k_{l}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l-2k_{l}}}(D_{i_{1}}+\frac{1}{h}(e_{1}+i\eta_{2})_{i_{1}})\cdots(D_{i_{l-2k_{l}}}+\frac{1}{h}(e_{1}+i\eta_{2})_{i_{l-2k_{l}}})\left[(-\frac{1}{h}T-\Delta)^{k_{l}}\tilde{A}\right]\tilde{B}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{2m-2}\sum\limits_{k_{l}=0}^{[\frac{l}{2}]}\sum_{j=0}^{l-2k_{l}}\,\int\limits_{\Omega}\binom{l-2k_{l}}{j}\,\frac{b^{l,k_{l}}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l-2k_{l}}}}{h^{l-2k_{l}-j}}(e_{1}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})_{i_{1}}\cdots(e_{1}+\mathrm{i}\eta_{2})_{i_{l-2k_{l}-j}}

(3.5)

\displaystyle\qquad\times D^{i_{l-2k_{l}-j+1}}\cdots D^{i_{l-2k_{l}}}((-\frac{1}{h}T-\Delta)^{k_{l}}\tilde{A})\,\tilde{B}.

Here
\eta_{2}
can be any unit vector with
e_{1}\cdot\eta_{2}=0
.
From now on we write
\eta=\eta_{2}
.
We recover the coefficients one by one by multiplying (

) with suitable powers of

, by letting

and by inverting momentum ray transforms (MRT, see Lemma

). This will be done in several steps.

Step 1.

We first show that

\displaystyle b^{2m-2,0}=0.

We start by multiplying (

.
Next utilizing the estimates given in Lemma
A.4
,
\lVert r(x,h)\rVert_{H_{scl}^{2m}}\leq ch^{m}
,
\lVert\tilde{r}(x,h)\rVert_{H_{scl}^{2m}}\leq ch^{m}
and letting

, we see that only

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}b^{2m-2,0}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{2m-2}}(e_{1}+\mathrm{i}\eta)_{i_{1}}\cdots(e_{1}+\mathrm{i}\eta)_{i_{2m-2}}a_{0}\,b_{0}=0.

Adapting ideas from
[

\displaystyle b^{2m-2,0}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{2m-2}}(e_{1}+\mathrm{i}\eta)_{i_{1}}\cdots(e_{1}+\mathrm{i}\eta)_{i_{2m-2}}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{p=0}^{2m-2}(\mathrm{i})^{p}\,\binom{2m-2}{p}\,b_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}1\cdots 1}^{2m-2,0}\eta_{i_{1}}\cdots\eta_{i_{p}}

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{p=0}^{2m-2}b_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}}^{2m-2,0,p}\,\eta_{i_{1}}\cdots\eta_{i_{p}},\quad 2\leq i_{1},\cdots,i_{p}\leq n\quad\mbox{for each}\quad 0\leq p\leq 2m-2,

where

for each

. We end this section with the following remarks.

Remark 3.2
.

This remark suggests that there is a another way of avoiding the gauge appearing in the Theorem

Instead of assuming

, one can assume that

for

Remark 3.3
.

Corollary
2.2
gave a density result for even integers. For odd integers
K\geq 0
, Theorem
2.1
implies the following (non-optimal) result: the set

\mathcal{A}_{K}=\mbox{span}\left\{v\,\oplus_{|\alpha|\leq K}D^{\alpha}u:\Delta^{\left[\frac{K+2}{2}\right]+1}u=\Delta^{\left[\frac{K+2}{2}\right]+1}v=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\right\}

is dense in the space
\mathbf{S}^{K}\left(C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})\right)=\oplus_{p=0}^{K}S^{p}\left(C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})\right)
.

One could also ask if the linear span of the set

\mathcal{A}_{p_{1}p_{2}}\coloneqq\left\{D^{\alpha}v\otimes D^{\beta}u:\Delta^{p_{1}+1}u=\Delta^{p_{2}+1}v=0\,\,\mbox{in}\,\,\Omega,\,\,\mbox{for all multi-indices $\alpha$, $\beta$ such that}\,\,\lvert\alpha\rvert=p_{1},\lvert\beta\rvert=p_{2}\right\},

is dense in the space smooth symmetric tensor fields defined in
\overline{\Omega}
of order
p_{1}+p_{2}
. Here
\otimes
denotes the symmetric tensor product.

In particular when
p_{1}=p_{2}=1
, this is asking if the linear span of tensor products of gradients of any two biharmonic functions is dense in the space of smooth symmetric two tensor fields. However, a similar density result is not true if one replace biharmonic functions by harmonic functions. The latter density question arises in the linearized version of the
boundary rigidity problem
; see
[
31
, Chapter 1]
and
[
28
, Chapter 11]
. This question also appears while linearizing certain anisotropic elliptic PDE from the Cauchy data set; see
[
8
, Section 6]
.

4.
Recovery of coefficients under an additional assumption

In this section we present the proof of Proposition
4.3
which will be the main ingredient to prove Theorem
2.3
in the following section.
To proceed further, we define two differential operators on
C^{\infty}(S^{m})
, the space of smooth symmetric tensor fields of order
m
.

Definition 4.1
.

[
31
, Chapter 2]

The symmetric covariant derivative
\mathrm{d}:C^{\infty}(S^{m})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(S^{m+1})
is given by

\displaystyle(\mathrm{d}f)_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m+1}}=\sigma(i_{1}\cdots i_{m+1})\frac{\partial f_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}}{\partial x_{i_{m+1}}},\quad 1\leq i_{1}\cdots,i_{m+1}\leq n.

Definition 4.2
.

[
31
, Chapter 2]

The divergence operator
\delta:C^{\infty}(S^{m+1})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(S^{m})
is given by

\displaystyle(\delta f)_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}k}}{\partial x_{k}},\quad 1\leq i_{1}\cdots,i_{m}\leq n.

The operators
-\mathrm{d}
and
\delta
are dual to each other with respect to
L^{2}
inner product. We also define
\mathrm{d}^{k}:C^{\infty}(S^{m})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(S^{m+k})
by taking composition of
\mathrm{d}
with itself
k
times. Similarly we define
\delta^{k}:C^{\infty}(S^{m})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(S^{m-k})
. In particular,

\displaystyle\delta^{m}f

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i_{1},\cdots,i_{m}=1}^{n}\frac{\partial^{m}f_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}}{\partial x_{i_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{i_{m}}},\quad\mbox{for any}\quad f\in C^{\infty}(S^{m}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{m}\phi

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{m}\phi}{\partial x_{i_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{i_{m}}},\quad\qquad\qquad\mbox{for any}\quad\phi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega}).

Our next result is as follows.

This together with (

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{a}^{2m-1,2m-1},\widehat{a}^{2m-1,2m-1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\widehat{a}^{2m-1,2m-1},-\sum\limits_{p=0}^{m-2}i^{\prime m-1-p}_{\delta}\widehat{a}^{2m-1,2p+1}+d_{x^{\prime}}^{2m-1}\phi\right\rangle

5.1.
Gauge transformation

To this end we denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{a^{3},a^{2},a^{1},a^{0}}

\displaystyle=(-\Delta)^{2}+a_{ijk}^{3}D^{ijk}+a_{ij}^{2}D^{ij}+a_{i}^{1}D^{i}+a^{0},

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{L}_{0}

\displaystyle=(-\Delta)^{2}.

Note that Cauchy data of
v
and
e^{\phi}v
up to order
3
are the same if
\phi\in C^{4}(\overline{\Omega})
satisfies
\partial_{\nu}^{j}\phi|_{\partial\Omega}=0
for
0\leq j\leq 3
. Now we compute

\displaystyle e^{-\phi}\mathcal{L}_{0}e^{\phi}v

\displaystyle=e^{-\phi}(-\Delta)^{2}e^{\phi}v

\displaystyle=(e^{-\phi}(-\Delta)e^{\phi})^{2}v

\displaystyle=(\Delta\phi+|\nabla\phi|^{2}+2\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla+\Delta)(\Delta\phi+|\nabla\phi|^{2}+2\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla+\Delta)v

\displaystyle=(\Delta)^{2}v+\Delta(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla v)+\Delta(f(\phi)v)+2\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla(\Delta)v

\displaystyle\qquad+4\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla(\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla v)+2\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla(f(\phi)v)+f^{2}(\phi)v+2f(\phi)\,\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla v+f(\phi)\Delta v

\displaystyle=(\Delta)^{2}v+\underbrace{4\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\Delta v}_{\mbox{\tiny{third order term}}}+4\nabla^{2}\phi\cdot\nabla^{2}+4\nabla\phi\otimes\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla^{2}+2f(\phi)\,\Delta

\displaystyle\qquad+2\nabla\Delta\phi\cdot\nabla v+4\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla^{2}\phi\cdot\nabla v+f(\phi)\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla v+2\nabla f(\phi)\cdot\nabla v+4f(\phi)\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla v

\displaystyle\qquad+v\Delta(f(\phi))+2\,v\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla f(\phi)+v\,f^{2}(\phi),

where
f(\phi)=\Delta\phi+|\nabla\phi|^{2}
.

The above calculation shows that if

is as above, then

C_{a^{3},a^{2},a^{1},a^{0}}=C_{0},

where

, and

can be read off from the computation above. Such calculations can also be done for

Substituting this into (

\displaystyle a^{3}=\mathrm{d}^{3}\psi_{1}+i_{\delta}a^{3,1},\quad a^{4,1}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}\mathrm{d}^{2}\psi_{1}+i_{\delta}a^{4,2},\quad a^{5,2}=-\frac{3}{4}\mathrm{d}\psi_{1}.

Again inserting above findings into (

\Omega

This entails

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\,(\cdot)=e^{-\psi_{1}}(-\Delta)^{3}e^{\psi_{1}}(\cdot)\quad\mbox{where $\psi_{1}$ satisfies \eqref{phi_conditions} for $m=3$.}

Step 3.
The case of

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}A_{i}^{1}D^{i}(-\Delta)^{m-1}u\,v+\int\limits_{\Omega}\sum_{l=0}^{2m-2}a^{l}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}u\,v=0\quad\mbox{where}\quad\Delta^{m}u=\Delta^{m}v=0.

h^{m}

. We start by multiplying (

.
Inserting these into (

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}A_{i}^{1}(e_{1}+\mathrm{i}\eta)_{i}\,T^{m-1}a_{0}\,b_{0}+\sum\limits_{l=2}^{m}\int\limits_{\Omega}b_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}^{2m-l,m-l}\,(e_{1}+\mathrm{i}\eta)_{i_{1}}\cdots(e_{1}+\mathrm{i}\eta)_{i_{l}}\,T^{m-l}a_{0}\,b_{0}=0.

\mathbb{R}^{n}

Now combination of these, (

6.
Momentum ray transforms

Let

S^{m}=S^{m}(C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
be the space of smooth compactly supported symmetric
m
-tensor fields in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For
f\in S^{m}
and for each non-negative integer
k
, the momentum ray transform (MRT) is denoted by
\mathcal{J}^{k}
and given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{k}f(x,\xi)\coloneqq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}t^{k}\,f_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}(x+t\xi)\,\xi^{i_{1}}\,\cdots\xi^{i_{m}}\,\mathrm{d}t\quad\mbox{for all}\quad(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}).

These transforms were first introduced by Sharafutdinov
[
30
,
31
]
. For
k=0
,
\mathcal{J}^{0}f=\mathcal{J}f
is the classical ray transform/ X-ray transform, which is well studied due to its potential application in different branches of science such as medical imaging, seismology, and inverse problems related to partial differential equations. For
m=0
, MRT appears when studying the inversion of the cone transform and conical Radon transform, where the latter transforms have promising applications in Compton cameras; see
[
24
]
.
We also need to define the MRT of
F\in\mathbf{S}^{m}=S^{0}\oplus S^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus S^{m}
. Any such
F
can be written uniquely as

(6.1)

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{p=0}^{m}f^{(p)}=f^{(0)}_{i_{0}}+f^{(1)}_{i_{1}}\,\mathrm{d}x^{i_{1}}+\cdots+f^{(p)}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}}\mathrm{d}x^{i_{1}}\cdots\mathrm{d}x^{i_{p}}+\cdots+f^{(m)}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}\mathrm{d}x^{i_{1}}\cdots\mathrm{d}x^{i_{m}}

\displaystyle=\left(f^{(0)}_{i_{0}},f^{(1)}_{i_{1}},\cdots,f^{(m)}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}\right)

which can be viewed as sum of a function, a vector field, and symmetric tensor fields with
f^{(p)}\in S^{p}
for each
0\leq p\leq m
. For any smooth and compactly supported
F\in\mathbf{S}^{m}
and for all
(x,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and for every integer
k\geq 0
the momentum ray transform
[
4
]
is given by

(6.2)

\displaystyle J^{k}F(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sum_{p=0}^{m}\,J^{k}\!f^{(p)}(x,\xi)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}t^{k}\left(f^{(0)}_{i_{0}}(x+t\xi)+f^{(1)}_{i_{1}}(x+t\xi)\,\xi^{i_{1}}+\cdots+f^{(m)}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{m}}(x+t\xi)\,\xi^{i_{1}}\,\cdots\xi^{i_{m}}\right)\mathrm{d}t.

We now introduce the notion of MRT on the set
\mathbf{S}^{m}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
consisting of compactly supported distributions in
\mathbf{S}^{m}
. This will be done by using adjoints. The momentum ray transforms
J^{k}:\mathbf{S}^{m}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\})

[
4
, Definition 4.4]
are defined as:

\displaystyle\langle J^{k}F,\Psi\rangle\negthinspace=\negthinspace\langle F,(J^{k})^{*}\Psi\rangle=\sum\limits_{p=0}^{m}\langle f^{(p)},(J^{k})^{*}_{p}\Psi\rangle\quad\mbox{where}\quad\Psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}),

and
(J^{k})^{*}\Psi
is given by

\displaystyle((J^{k})^{*}\Psi)(x)=\Bigg(

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{k}\,\,\Psi(x-t\xi,\xi)\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\cdots,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{k}\,\,\xi^{i_{1}}\cdots\xi^{i_{m}}\,\Psi(x-t\xi,\xi)\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\xi\Bigg),

which is an element of
\oplus_{p=0}^{m}C^{\infty}(S^{p})
and

(J^{k})^{*}_{p}\Psi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{k}\,\,\xi^{i_{1}}\cdots\xi^{i_{p}}\,\Psi(x-t\xi,\xi)\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\xi.

6.1.
Injectivity of MRT

We denote
I^{k}F\coloneqq J^{k}F|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
for every integers
k\geq 0
.
In this section we recall certain injectivity results of the operator
F\longmapsto J^{m}F
and
F\longmapsto I^{m}F
from
[
4
]
without their proofs.

Lemma 6.1
.

[
4
, Theorem 4.5]

Let
F\in\mathbf{S}^{m}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
and
m\geq 0
. Then

\displaystyle J^{k}F=0\quad\mbox{holds for all integers $k$ with $0\leq k\leq m$}\quad\implies\quad F=0.

Lemma 6.2
.

[
4
, Lemma 4.8]

Let
F\in\mathbf{S}^{m}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
, then the operator
J^{k}F
satisfies the following relation

\displaystyle\langle\xi,\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\rangle^{p}J^{k}F=\begin{cases}(-1)^{p}\,\binom{k}{p}\,p!\,J^{k-p}F\quad\mbox{if}\quad p\leq k\\
0\hskip 93.89418pt\mbox{if}\quad p>k.\end{cases}

After taking restrictions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
this gives

\displaystyle\langle\xi,\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\rangle^{p}I^{k}F=\begin{cases}(-1)^{p}\,\binom{k}{p}\,p!\,I^{k-p}F\quad\mbox{if}\quad p\leq k\\
0\hskip 93.89418pt\mbox{if}\quad p>k.\end{cases}

This lemma entails that highest order MRT
(J^{m}F)
uniquely determines all the lower order MRT
(J^{k}F)\,0\leq k\leq m
. Analogous result holds if one replace
J^{k}F
by
I^{k}F
. Consequently, one can obtain next results.

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose
F\in\mathbf{S}^{m}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
and
m\geq 0
. Then

J^{m}F=0\implies F=0.

Lemma 6.4
.

[
4
, Theorem 4.18]

Let
m\geq 2
and
F=\sum\limits_{l=0}^{m}f^{(l)}\in\mathbf{S}^{m}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
. Then

I^{m}F=J^{m}F|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}=0

if and only if

\displaystyle f^{\left(2\left[\frac{m}{2}\right]\right)}=-i_{\delta}\left(\sum\limits_{l=1}^{\left[\frac{m}{2}\right]}i^{\left[\frac{m}{2}\right]-l}_{\delta}f^{(2l-2)}\right)\quad\mbox{and}\quad f^{\left(2\left[\frac{m-1}{2}\right]+1\right)}

\displaystyle=-i_{\delta}\left(\sum\limits_{l=1}^{\left[\frac{m-1}{2}\right]}i^{\left[\frac{m}{2}\right]-l}_{\delta}f^{(2l-1)}\right).

Moreover,
I^{m}F=0
if and only if
F=0
for
m=0,1
.

Lemma 6.5
.

The following equality

\displaystyle I^{k}(i^{p}_{\delta}f^{(l)})(x,\xi)=I^{k}f^{(l)}(x,\xi)

holds for any
f^{(l)}\in S^{l}
and for all integers
p
with
p\geq 1
. For
p=0
this holds trivially because
i^{0}_{\delta}
is the identity operator.

Remark 6.6
.

We know that any symmetric
m
-tensor field in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
has
\binom{m+n-1}{m}
distinct components. Suppose
F
is the same as in (
6.1
). Then
F
has a total of
\sum\limits_{l=0}^{m}\binom{l+n-1}{l}=\binom{m+n}{m}
distinct components. As a consequence, recovering
F
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is equivalent to recovering a symmetric
m
-tensor field in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. However to recover a symmetric
m
-tensor field in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, MRT information is required on the tangent bundle of unit sphere which is a
2(n+1)-2=2n
dimensional data set. As a result, the kernel of
J^{m}F
is trivial (see
Lemma

6.3
), whereas the kernel of
I^{m}F
is nontrivial when
m\geq 2
(see
Lemma

6.4
) because
I^{m}F
is specified on the unit sphere bundle.

Next we prove the following result, which was used to segregate MRT of different order tensor fields in the previous sections.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
F_{m}\in\mathbf{S}^{m}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
and
m\geq 0
.

1.

Suppose

\displaystyle c_{m}I^{k+m}F_{m}+\cdots+c_{1}I^{k+1}F_{1}+c_{0}I^{k}F_{0}=0

holds for certain non-zero constants
c_{j}
where
0\leq j\leq m
and for all
0\leq k\leq m
. Then

(6.3)

\displaystyle I^{p}F_{p}=0\quad\mbox{for}\quad p=0,1,\cdots,m.

2.

Additionally, if we assume

\displaystyle d_{m}I^{k+m-1}F_{m}+\cdots+d_{1}I^{k}F_{1}=0

holds for certain non-zero constants
d_{j}
where
0\leq j\leq m-1
and for all
0\leq k\leq m-1
. Then

(6.4)

\displaystyle I^{p}F_{p+1}=0\quad\mbox{for}\quad p=0,1,\cdots,m-1.

Proof.

We only give the proof of (
6.3
) and that of (
6.4
) follows similarly.
We have that

\displaystyle c_{m}I^{k+m}F_{m}+\cdots+c_{1}I^{k+1}F_{1}+c_{0}I^{k}F_{0}=0.

Applying
\langle\xi,\partial_{x}\rangle^{k}\,
to the above equation and then using Lemma
6.2
we obtain

\displaystyle c_{m}\binom{k+m}{k}\,I^{m}F_{m}+c_{m-1}\,\binom{k+m-1}{k}\,I^{m-1}F_{m-1}+\cdots+c_{1}\,\binom{k+1}{k}\,I^{1}F_{1}+c_{0}\,\binom{k}{k}\,I^{0}F_{0}=0,

for all
k
with
0\leq k\leq m
.
The above relation can be written as the matrix equation
AX=0
where

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=(I^{m}F_{m},\cdots,I^{0}F_{0})^{t},

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}c_{m}&c_{m-1}&\cdots&c_{1}&c_{0}\\
c_{m}\binom{m+1}{1}&c_{m-1}\binom{m}{1}&\cdots&c_{1}\binom{2}{1}&c_{0}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
c_{m}\binom{m+2}{2}&c_{m-1}\binom{m+1}{2}&\cdots&c_{1}\binom{3}{2}&c_{0}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
c_{m}\binom{2m}{m}&c_{m-1}\binom{2m-1}{m}&\cdots&c_{1}\binom{m+1}{m}&c_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

We now complete the proof by showing
\det A\neq 0
. This follows from Lemma
6.8
. This implies
X=0
, i.e.

I^{p}F_{p}=0\quad\mbox{for}\quad p=0,1,\cdots,m.

∎

Lemma 6.8
.

\det A=(-1)^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}}\,c_{0}\cdots c_{m}
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\det A=c_{0}\cdots c_{m}\det A_{m+1}

where

\displaystyle A_{m+1}

\displaystyle=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&\cdots&1&1\\
\binom{m+1}{1}&\binom{m}{1}&\cdots&\binom{2}{1}&1\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
\binom{m+2}{2}&\binom{m+1}{2}&\cdots&\binom{3}{2}\,&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\binom{2m}{m}&\binom{2m-1}{m}&\cdots&\binom{m+1}{m}&1\end{pmatrix}.

We now subtract the
(m-k)
-th column from the
(m-k-1)
-th column for
1\leq k\leq m-2
to obtain

\displaystyle A_{m+1}\longrightarrow\begin{pmatrix}1&0&\cdots&0\\
\binom{m+1}{1}&-1&\cdots&-1\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\\
\binom{m+2}{2}&-\binom{m+1}{1}&\cdots&-\binom{2}{1}\,\\
\vdots&\vdots&\cdots&\vdots\\
\binom{2m}{m}&-\binom{2m-1}{m-1}&\cdots&-\binom{m}{m-1}\end{pmatrix}.

This implies

\det A_{m+1}=(-1)^{m}\det A_{m},

where
A_{m}
is obtained from
A_{m+1}
by considering first
m
rows and
m
columns respectively. Proceeding in this way after finally many steps we obtain

\det A_{m+1}=(-1)^{m+(m-1)+\cdots+2+1}.

This implies

\det A=(-1)^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}}\,c_{0}\cdots c_{m}.

∎

7.
A new decomposition of symmetric tensor fields

We now prove a suitable trace free Helmholtz type decomposition of symmetric tensor fields, which we used in the previous section to deal with partial data MRT.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
f
be a smooth symmetric
m
tensor field in
\overline{\Omega}
. Then the following decomposition holds:

\displaystyle f=\tilde{f}+i_{\delta}v+\mathrm{d}^{m}\phi,\quad\mbox{with}\quad j_{\delta}\tilde{f}=\delta^{m}\tilde{f}=0

and
\partial_{\nu}^{l}\phi|_{\partial\Omega}=0,l=0,1,\cdots,m-1
, where
\tilde{f}\in S^{m}
,
v\in S^{m-2}
and
\phi\in S^{0}
are smooth symmetric tensor fields in
\overline{\Omega}
.

This is a generalization of the decomposition shown in
[
9
]
. In
[
26
]
, they proved certain decomposition of symmetric tensors.

Remark 7.2
.

For
m=1
, this is the well known Helmholtz (or solenoidal) decomposition.

Proof.

We closely follow the arguments used in
[
9
]
. We first assume that
f
can be written in the given form as

(7.1)

\displaystyle f=\tilde{f}+i_{\delta}v+\mathrm{d}^{m}\phi,

and we derive an equation for
\phi
. Applying
j_{\delta}
to (
7.1
) and using
j_{\delta}\tilde{f}=0
we get

j_{\delta}f=j_{\delta}i_{\delta}v+j_{\delta}\mathrm{d}^{m}\phi.

Since
j_{\delta}i_{\delta}
is invertible by
[
9
, Lemma 2.3]
, we obtain

(7.2)

\displaystyle v=(j_{\delta}i_{\delta})^{-1}(j_{\delta}f-j_{\delta}\mathrm{d}^{m}\phi).

This together with (
7.1
) implies

(7.3)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=\tilde{f}+i_{\delta}((j_{\delta}i_{\delta})^{-1}(j_{\delta}f-j_{\delta}\mathrm{d}^{m}\phi))+\mathrm{d}^{m}\phi.

Denote

and

. From

we have that

is the projection to the trace free component of a symmetric tensor and that

for any

. Thus from (

) we obtain

\displaystyle pf

\displaystyle=\tilde{f}+p\mathrm{d}^{m}\phi.

Applying
\delta^{m}
to the above identity and using

we obtain

\displaystyle\delta^{m}p\mathrm{d}^{m}\phi=\delta^{m}pf.

Hence,
\phi
solves the following boundary value problem.

(7.4)

\displaystyle(-1)^{m}\delta^{m}p\mathrm{d}^{m}\phi

\displaystyle=(-1)^{m}\delta^{m}pf\quad\mbox{in }\quad\Omega

\displaystyle\partial^{l}_{\nu}\phi

\displaystyle=0\quad\hskip 51.21504pt\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega\quad\mbox{for}\quad l=0,1,\cdots,m-1.

We next show that
\delta^{m}p\mathrm{d}^{m}
acting on scalar functions in

is a strongly elliptic operator in the sense of
[

, Formula 11.79, Section 5.11]
. Then by
[

, Exercise 3, Section 5.11]
the map

(7.5)

\displaystyle H^{2m}(\Omega)\cap H_{0}^{m}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\delta^{m}p\mathrm{d}^{m}\phi

will be Fredholm operator of index zero. To prove the ellipticity of

we follow
[
9
, Section 5]
and introduce the following.
For

, we define the symmetric multiplication operators

by

(i_{x}f)_{i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{m+1}}=\sigma(i_{1},\dots,i_{m},i_{m+1})(x_{i_{m+1}}f_{i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{m}}).

We also define the dual of the operator
i_{x}
, the contraction operator
j_{x}:S^{m}\rightarrow S^{m-1}
by

(j_{x}f)_{i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{m-1}}=f_{i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{m}}x^{i_{m}}.

Similarly,
i_{x^{\otimes k}}
and

are defined by taking the composition of

and

with itself

The principal symbol of

at

is given by

and
\mathrm{d}^{m}
respectively. Thus the principal symbol of

, which is real valued and non-negative since for any
\phi

\displaystyle\langle j_{\xi^{\otimes m}}pi_{\xi^{\otimes m}}\phi,\phi\rangle=\langle pi_{\xi^{\otimes m}}\phi,i_{\xi^{\otimes m}}\phi\rangle=\langle pi_{\xi^{\otimes m}}\phi,pi_{\xi^{\otimes m}}\phi\rangle.

. Thus we have shown the ellipticity of the boundary value problem (

). By elliptic regularity any element in the kernel of (

\Omega

\displaystyle\langle(-1)^{m}\delta^{m}p\mathrm{d}^{m}\phi,\phi\rangle=\langle p\mathrm{d}^{m}\phi,p\mathrm{d}^{m}\phi\rangle=0\quad\mbox{using integration by parts}.

This implies

pd^{m}\phi=0
. From
[
9
, Lemma 3.4, Equation 3.8]
we have that

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}pf=p\mathrm{d}f+\frac{m}{n+2m-2}i\delta pf\quad\mbox{for}\quad f\in S^{m}.

Replacing

by
\mathrm{d}^{m-1}\phi
entails

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}p\mathrm{d}^{m-1}\phi=\frac{m-1}{n+2m-4}i\delta p\mathrm{d}^{m-1}\phi.

Denote

and

. Note that, trace of

. This gives

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}u=iv.

Hence

is a
trace free conformal Killing tensor field
. Now using the boundary conditions
\partial^{l}_{\nu}\phi|_{\partial\Omega}=0

. By

, Theorem 1.3]
we have

. Repeating this process finitely many times we will obtain

. Since

, this implies that

in
\Omega
. Thus the boundary value problem has (
7.4
) has zero kernel. Since it has Fredholm index zero, this says (
7.4
) also has zero co-kernel. This shows that the mapping (
7.5
) is an isomorphism.

Finally, given

, the fact that (

) is an isomorphism together with ellipticity shows that there exists a unique
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
solving (
7.4
). We then define
v
by (
7.2
), which implies that
i_{\delta}v=qf-qd^{m}\phi
. We also define

. It follows that

\tilde{f}=f-(qf-qd^{m}\phi)-d^{m}\phi=pf-pd^{m}\phi.

Then
j_{\delta}\tilde{f}=0
and
\delta^{m}\tilde{f}=\delta^{m}pf-\delta^{m}pd^{m}\phi=0
. This proves the required decomposition.
∎

Lemma 7.3

Appendix A
construction of special solutions

In this section, we describe the construction of special solutions known as
complex geometric optic (CGO)
solutions of polyharmonic operators that we have already utilized in the earlier sections.

A.1.
Carleman estimate

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary. In this section, we construct Complex Geometric Optics (CGO) solutions for (
1.1
) following a Carleman estimate approach from
[
6
,
10
]
.

We first introduce semiclassical Sobolev spaces.
For
h>0
be a small parameter, we define the semiclassical Sobolev space
H^{s}_{\mathrm{scl}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
as the space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
endowed with the semiclassical norm

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{H^{s}_{\mathrm{scl}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\lVert\langle hD\rangle^{s}\,u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad\langle\xi\rangle=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For open sets
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for non-negative integers
m
, the semiclassical Sobolev space
H^{m}_{\mathrm{scl}}(\Omega)
is the space
H^{m}(\Omega)
endowed with the following semiclassical norm

\displaystyle\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{H^{m}_{\mathrm{scl}}(\Omega)}=\sum\limits_{|\alpha|\leq m}\lVert(hD)^{\alpha}\,u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

These two norms are equivalent when
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and for every integers
m\geq 0
.

Let
\widetilde{\Omega}
be an open subset containing
\Omega
in its interior and let
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega})
with
\nabla\varphi\neq 0
in
\overline{\Omega}
. We consider the semi-classical conjugated Laplacian
P_{0,\varphi}=e^{\frac{\varphi}{h}}(-h^{2}\Delta)e^{\frac{-\varphi}{h}}
, where
0<h\ll 1
. The semi-classical principal symbol of this operator
P_{0,\varphi}
is given by
p_{0,\varphi}(x,\xi)=|\xi|^{2}-|\nabla_{x}\varphi|^{2}+2\mathrm{i}\xi\cdot\nabla_{x}\varphi
.

Definition A.1

(
[
17
]
)
.

We say that
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Omega})
is a limiting Carleman weight for
P_{0,\varphi}

in
\Omega
if

\nabla\varphi\neq 0
in
\overline{\Omega}
and
\mathrm{\mathrm{Re}}(p_{0,\varphi})
,
\mathrm{Im}(p_{0,\varphi})
satisfies

\Big\{\mathrm{Re}(p_{0,\varphi}),\mathrm{Im}(p_{0,\varphi})\Big\}(x,\xi)=0\mbox{ whenever }p_{0,\varphi}(x,\xi)=0\mbox{ for }(x,\xi)\in\widetilde{\Omega}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}),

where
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
denotes the Poisson bracket.

Examples of such
\varphi
are linear weights

\varphi(x)=\alpha\cdot x
, where
0\neq\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
or logarithmic weights
\varphi(x)=\log|x-x_{0}|
with
x_{0}\notin\overline{\widetilde{\Omega}}
.

As mentioned already, we consider the limiting Carleman weight in this paper to be
\varphi(x)=x_{1}
.

Proposition A.2

(Interior Carleman estimate)
.

Let
\varphi(x)
be a limiting Carleman weight for the conjugated semiclassical Laplacian. Then there exists a constant
C=C_{\Omega,A^{j},q}
such that for
0<h\ll 1
, we have

h^{m}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\lVert h^{2m}e^{\frac{\varphi}{h}}(-\Delta)^{m}e^{-\frac{\varphi}{h}}u\rVert_{H^{-2m}_{\mathrm{scl}}},\quad\mbox{ for all }u\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega).

This follows by iterating a Carleman estimate for the semiclassical Laplacian with a gain of two derivatives proved in
[
29
]
. We omit the proof here. We refer the reader to
[
21
,
20
,
14
]
.

A.2.
Construction of CGO solutions

Next we use Proposition
A.2
to construct CGO solutions for the equation

. To this end, we state an existence result whose proof is standard; see
[
10
,

]

Let
\varphi

to construct a solution for

\displaystyle u(x;h)

\displaystyle=e^{\frac{\varphi+\mathrm{i}\psi}{h}}\left(a_{0}(x)+ha_{1}(x)+\dots+h^{m-1}a_{m-1}(x)+r(x;h)\right),

\displaystyle((-\Delta)^{m}+Q)G_{Q}F=F\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega,\quad\quad\gamma G_{Q}F=0\quad\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega.

Then the linearized (or Fréchet derivative of) Navier to Neumann map

B_{Q}=(D\mathcal{N})_{Q}:L^{\infty}(\Omega)\longrightarrow B\left(\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}H^{2m-2k-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega),\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}H^{2m-2k-\frac{3}{2}}(\partial\Omega)\right)

is given by

(B.4)

\displaystyle(B_{Q}H)(f)=\partial_{\nu}\gamma G_{Q}(-HP_{Q}f)|_{\partial\Omega}.

Proof.

Suppose
\lVert H\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
is small so that
\mathcal{N}_{Q+H}
is well defined, where
H
is same as
Q
with different set of smooth tensor fields. Given
f=(f_{0},f_{1},\dots,f_{m-1})\in\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}H^{2m-2k-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{Q+H}f-\mathcal{N}_{Q}f=\partial_{\nu}(\gamma P_{Q+H}f-\gamma P_{Q}f)|_{\partial\Omega}.

The function
w\coloneqq P_{Q+H}f-P_{Q}f
satisfies the following partial differential equation.

\displaystyle\left((-\Delta)^{m}+Q(x,D)\right)w

\displaystyle=-H\,w-H\,P_{Q}f\quad\hskip 76.82234pt\mbox{in}\quad\Omega

\displaystyle\gamma w

\displaystyle=(w,(-\Delta)w,\cdots,(-\Delta)^{m-1}w)=0\quad\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega.

We can write
w=G_{Q}(-H\,w)+G_{Q}(-HP_{Q}f)
and
w\in\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{L})
.
Utilizing the continuity of the Green operator
G_{Q}(H\,w)
we obtain

\displaystyle\lVert G_{Q}(H\,w)\rVert_{H^{2m}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq c\lVert H\,w\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert w\rVert_{H^{2m}(\Omega)}\quad\mbox{if}\quad\lVert H\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\quad\mbox{is small}.

We have
\lVert w\rVert_{H^{2m}(\Omega)}=\lVert G_{Q}(-H\,w)+G_{Q}(-HP_{Q}f)\rVert_{H^{2m}(\Omega)}
. The combination of this with triangle inequality and last displayed relation implies
\lVert w\rVert_{H^{2m}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert H\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\lVert f\rVert_{\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}H^{2m-2k-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)}.
Next we observe that,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{N}_{Q+H}-\mathcal{N}_{Q}-D\mathcal{N}_{Q}(H))(f)=\partial_{\nu}\left(\gamma w-\gamma G_{Q}HP_{Q}f\right)|_{\partial\Omega}=\partial_{\nu}(\gamma G_{Q}(-Hw))|_{\partial\Omega}.

By trace theorem, continuity of
G_{Q}
and
\lVert w\rVert_{H^{2m}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert H\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\lVert f\rVert_{\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}H^{2m-2k-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)}
we obtain from above

\displaystyle\lVert\partial_{\nu}(\gamma G_{Q}(-Hw))\rVert_{\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}H^{2m-2k-\frac{3}{2}}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\lVert G_{Q}(H\,w)\rVert_{H^{2m}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert H\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\lVert w\rVert_{H^{2m}(\Omega)}\leq\lVert H\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\lVert f\rVert_{\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}H^{2m-2k-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)}.

This proves that the Fréchet derivative of
Q\mapsto\mathcal{N}_{Q}
at
Q
is
B_{Q}
.
∎

Our next result gives the required integral identity involving unknown coefficients under the assumption that
B_{Q}=0
at
Q=0
. Compare next result with (
B.2
) where a formal computation is given.

Lemma B.2
.

Assume that the linearized (or Fréchet derivative of) Navier to Neumann map vanishes at
Q=0

i.e.,

(B_{0}H)f=0
. Then the following integral identity

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{2m-1}\,\tilde{a}^{l}_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}(x)\,D^{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}u\,v=0\quad\mbox{such that}\quad\Delta^{m}u=\Delta^{m}v=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega.

holds. Where
H
is same as
Q
represented by
\tilde{a}^{l}
, for certain smooth symmetric tensor fields of order
l
for each
0\leq l\leq 2m-1
.

Proof.

Let
P_{0}g\in H^{2m}(\Omega)
be an arbitrary function satisfying

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{m}P_{0}g=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\quad\mbox{and}\quad\gamma P_{0}g=g\quad\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega,

where
g=(g_{0},\cdots,g_{m-1})\in\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}H^{2m-2k-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega).

Next consider the following:

\displaystyle\langle(B_{0}H)f,g\rangle_{\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}H^{2m-2k-\frac{3}{2}}(\partial\Omega),\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}H^{2m-2k-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)}=\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{\nu}\gamma G_{0}(-HP_{0}f)\,g

where
G_{0}(-HP_{0}f)
solves
(-\Delta)^{m}G_{0}(-HP_{0}h)=-H\,P_{0}f
in
\Omega
and
\gamma G_{0}(-HP_{0}f)|_{\partial\Omega}=0
. Multiplying
P_{0}g
to the equation
(-\Delta)^{m}G_{0}(-HP_{0}h)=-H\,P_{0}f
we get

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}(-\Delta)^{m}G_{0}(-HP_{0}h)\,P_{0}g

\displaystyle=-\int\limits_{\Omega}P_{0}g\,H(P_{0}f).

We next make use of integration parts,
(-\Delta)^{m}P_{0}g=0
in
\Omega
and
\gamma G_{0}(-HP_{0}f)=0
on
\partial\Omega
to derive

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}(-H\,P_{0}f)\,P_{0}g

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m-1}\int\limits_{\partial\Omega}\partial_{\nu}(-\Delta)^{m-k-1}G_{0}(-HP_{0}f)\,(-\Delta)^{k}P_{0}g

\displaystyle=\langle B_{0}(f),g\rangle_{\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}H^{2m-2k-\frac{3}{2}}(\partial\Omega),\prod_{k=0}^{m-1}H^{2m-2k-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\Omega)}.

This along with
(B_{0}H)f=0
implies

\int\limits_{\Omega}(-H\,P_{0}f)\,P_{0}g=0
,
where
P_{0}f
and
P_{0}g
solve
(-\Delta)^{m}\,(\cdot)=0
. Recall that
H
is same as
Q
represented by
\tilde{a}^{l}
. This finishes the proof.
∎

On the distribution of subset sums of certain sets in ℤ p 2 \mathbb{Z}^{2}_{p}
Norbert Hegyvári

Abstract
. A given subset A A of natural numbers is said to be complete if every element of ℕ \mathbb{N} is the sum of distinct terms taken from A A . This topic is strongly connected to the knapsack problem which is known to be NP complete. Interestingly if A A and B B are complete sequences then A × B A\times B is not necessarily complete in ℕ 2 \mathbb{N}^{2} . In this paper we consider a modular version of this problem, motivated by the communication complexity problem of [2]. 1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{S}
be any additive semigroup, For sets
A,B\subset\mathbb{S}
the sum is defined by
A\pm B\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{a\pm b\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}a\in A;\ b\in B\}
,
For any
X\subseteq\mathbb{S}
let

FS(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{i}x_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\ x_{i}\in X,\ \varepsilon_{i}\in\{0,1\},\ \sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{i}<\infty\},

and one can extend the notion of subset sums
FS(X)
for higher dimension, i.e. when
X\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{k};k\geq 1
.
A set
X\subseteq\mathbb{S}
is said to be complete respect to
\mathbb{S}
if every element of
\mathbb{S}
is the sum of distinct terms taken from
X
. If
\mathbb{S}=\mathbb{N}
, there is a wide literature on the subject (A good survey can be found e.g. in [6], [10] and [12]).

This type of problems are highly connected to the knapsack or subset-sum problem. The problem is to determine, given positive integers
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n}
and an integer
m
, whether there is a subset of the set
\{a_{j}\}
that sums up to
m
. It is well known as an NP-complete problem. This topic is connected to some communication complexity problem too (see e.g. [2]) which motivates the present work.

Completeness in higher dimensions is very different from the one-dimensional case. For example, a classic result is that if a series contains more than
c\sqrt{n}
elements up to
n>n_{0}
, then it is complete (see [12]). While in the two-dimensional case, one can give a dense set
A
for which
FS(A)
does not even contain an arithmetic progression (see [8] and [9]). Further results can be found in [1] and [4].

Let
F_{n}
be the
n^{th}
Fibonacci number, i.e. let
F_{0}=0;\ F_{1}=1
and for
n\geq 0

F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_{n}
. For negative integers let
F_{-n}=(-1)^{n-1}F_{n}
when the recursive formula also holds. In section 2 we will investigate the distribution of the subset sums of three sets in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{p}
.

More precisely let
A_{1}=\{2^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\times\{F_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
,
A_{2}=\{2^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\times\{2^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
A_{3}=\{F_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\times\{F_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
. We will show that for almost all prim
p
,
FS(A_{i})\pmod{p}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{p}
(
i=1,2,3
). For
X\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{2}

X\pmod{p}=\{(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{p}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\exists(x_{1},x_{2})\in X;\ v_{i}\equiv x_{i}\pmod{p};\ i=1,2\}
.

Furthermore we extend the analysis to sets of types
A_{4}=\{b^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\times\{F_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
A_{5}=\{b^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\times\{2^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, where
b>2
integer. Although the set
\{b^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is incomplete for
b>2
, note that there can be an unbounded number of times
b^{n}
as one of the coordinates in the sets
A_{4}
and
A_{5}
. Thus, since all positive integers can be represented in the
b
-ary expansion, this question fits the previous ones.

In [2] we also consider the above questions in
\mathbb{N}^{2}
. Interestingly, the distribution of the above sets is different than in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{p}
. For example, for
i=1,2
and
3
, we describe sets
E_{i}
for which
\mathbb{N}^{2}\setminus E_{i}\subseteq FS(A_{i})
. Furthermore, we show that
E_{i}
contains arbitrary ”large” squares which contain no elements from
FS(A_{i})
, and squares of size
k\times k
which contain
k\log k
many elements from
FS(A_{i})
.

2.
Modular case

In this section we will prove the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1
.

There exists a
\delta>0
such that for all but
O(x/(\log x)^{1+\delta})
primes
p\leq x
,
|FS(A_{4})\pmod{p}|\geq\frac{p^{2}}{b}.

When
b=2
, the previous theorem says that
|FS(A_{1})\pmod{p}|\geq\frac{p^{2}}{2}.
But in this case we get more:

Theorem 2.2
.

There exists a
\delta>0
such that for all but
O(x/(\log x)^{1+\delta})
primes
p\leq x
,
FS(A_{1})\pmod{p}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{p}
.

The following theorem can be proved in a similar way as Theorem
2.2

Theorem 2.3
.

There exists a
\delta>0
such that for all but
O(x/(\log x)^{1+\delta})
primes
p\leq x
,
FS(A_{i})\pmod{p}=\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{p}
(
i=2,3
).

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{1}
be the sets of all primes up to
x
for which
|\pmod{p}\{2^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}|>2\sqrt{p}
and let
\mathcal{P}_{2}
be the sets of all primes up to
x
for which
|\pmod{p}\{F_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}|>2\sqrt{p}
. Denote by
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}_{1}\cap\mathcal{P}_{2}
. We will check that for all but
O(x/(\log x)^{1+\delta})
primes
p\leq x
belong to
\mathcal{P}
.

We need the following lemmas

Lemma 2.4
.

There exists a
\delta>0
such that for all but
O(\frac{x}{(\log x)^{1+\delta}})
primes
p\leq x
we have

(1)

|\{F_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\pmod{p}|>3\sqrt{p},

where
\delta>0.086072
is admissible if
x
is large enough.

It is a slight modification of [7, Lemma 3.1].

Lemma 2.5
.

There exist a
\delta>0
such that

(2)

|\{b^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\pmod{p}|>2\sqrt{p}

for all but
O(x/(\log x)^{1+\delta})
primes
p\leq x
.
One can choose
\delta
as in the previous lemma.

This lemma is a simple consequence of Theorem 3 in [5].

Using these lemmas we get that for all but
O(x/(\log x)^{1+\delta})
primes
p\leq x
both (
2
) and (
1
) hold. Denote the set of these primes by
\mathcal{P}
and we get that for every
p\in\mathcal{P}
both
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=|\pmod{p}\{b^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}|>2\sqrt{p}
and
R\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=|\pmod{p}\{F_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}|>2\sqrt{p}
hold. (We only need the factor
3
of the Lemma
2.4
when we assume that
b>2
, see section 2.1). The third ingredient of our proof is the following

Lemma 2.6

(Olson)
.

Let
A=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\}
be distinct non-zero residue classes modulo the prime
p
. If
k>2\sqrt{p}
then
FS(A)=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

See the proof in [11].
From now on in this section we assume that
b=2
.
Write
\pmod{p}\{F_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}=\{F_{j_{1}},F_{j_{2}},\dots,F_{j_{R}}\}
and
\pmod{p}\{2^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}=\{2^{i_{1}},2^{i_{2}},\dots,2^{i_{T}}\}
.
Let
(v_{1},v_{2})
any point in
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{p}
. By Lemma
2.6
we can write
v_{1}
in the form
v_{1}=\sum^{T}_{m=1}\varepsilon_{m}2^{i_{m}};\ \varepsilon_{m}\in\{0,1\}
, and
v_{2}
in the form
v_{2}=\sum^{R}_{k=1}\eta_{k}F_{j_{k}};\ \eta_{k}\in\{0,1\}
.

Assume first that
\sum^{T}_{m=1}\varepsilon_{m}>\sum^{R}_{k=1}\eta_{k}
. We are going to find a subset
\{F^{\prime}_{j_{n}}\}
for which
\sum^{V}_{n=1}\zeta_{n}F^{\prime}_{j_{n}}=v_{2}
,
\zeta_{n}>0
and
\sum_{n}\zeta_{n}=T
. Note that we allow
\zeta_{n}
to be bigger than one, i.e. we allow some
F^{\prime}_{j}
to be repeated. Then the elements
(2^{i_{1}},F^{\prime}_{j_{1}}),(2^{i_{2}},F^{\prime}_{j_{2}}),\dots,(2^{i_{T}},F^{\prime}_{j_{T}})
are pairwise different (because their first coordinates are pairwise different) and
\sum^{T}_{t=1}(2^{i_{t}},F^{\prime}_{j_{t}})=(v_{1},v_{2})
, as we wanted.

So take
F_{i_{1}}
and find an element
F_{r}
, where
r>\sum^{T}_{m=1}\varepsilon_{m}
and
F_{r}\equiv F_{i_{1}}\pmod{p}
. Since the sequence
{F_{n}}
is periodic
\pmod{p}
, there exists such an
r
. Now change
F_{r}
to
F_{r-1}+F_{r-2}
. Repeat this changing process altogether

\sum^{T}_{m=1}\varepsilon_{m}-\sum^{R}_{k=1}\eta_{k}
many times (i.e. change
F_{r-1}
to
F_{r-2}+F_{r-3}
, etc.). Since
r>\sum^{T}_{m=1}\varepsilon_{m}
this process is terminated. Then we can matching the terms of powers of two to the (possible repeated) elements of the appropriate Fibonacci elements.

When
\sum^{T}_{m=1}\varepsilon_{m}<\sum^{R}_{k=1}\eta_{k}
the process is the same; just note that we replace a term
2^{m}
by
2^{m-1}+2^{m-1}
.
∎

2.1.
The case when
b>2

In the general case we give a bound for the cardinality of
FS(A_{4})\pmod{p}
, where
A_{4}=\{b^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}\times\{F_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
p\in\mathcal{P}
. Split
\pmod{p}\{F_{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}=\{F_{j_{1}},F_{j_{2}},\dots,\}
into two disjoint parts
G_{1}\cup G_{2}
where
|G_{2}|=b-1
. Write
\pmod{p}\{b^{n}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}=\{b^{i_{1}},b^{i_{2}},\dots,b^{i_{T}}\}
,
G_{1}=\{F{i_{1}},F_{i_{2}},\dots,F_{i_{V}}\}
and
G_{2}=\{F_{s_{1}},F_{s_{2}},\dots,F_{s_{b-1}}\}
. By the assumption and Lemma
2.5
we get that
V=|G_{1}|>2\sqrt{p}
. By Lemma
2.6
for any
(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{p}

v_{1}
can be written as
\sum^{T}_{k=1}\varepsilon_{k}b^{i_{k}};\ \varepsilon_{k}\in\{0,1\}
, and
v_{2}
in the form
v_{2}=\sum^{V}_{k=1}\eta_{k}F_{j_{k}};\ \eta_{k}\in\{0,1\}
.

Let us assume that

\sum^{T}_{m=1}\varepsilon_{m}>\sum^{V}_{k=1}\eta_{k}
. Then follow the previous process.

So let us assume that
\sum^{T}_{m=1}\varepsilon_{m}<\sum^{V}_{k=1}\eta_{k}
. Then take an
m
large enough for which
b^{m}\equiv b^{i_{1}}\pmod{p}
, and change
b^{m}=b^{m-1}+b^{m-1}+\dots+b^{m-1}
(
m
times). Repeat this process increasing the number of terms in each step by
b
elements. If one step the number of terms is exactly
\sum^{V}_{k=1}\eta_{k}
, then we are done. Otherwise one repetition we overstep
\sum^{V}_{k=1}\eta_{k}
by
r
many elements and clearly
\min r\leq b-1
. Then completing the sum
\sum^{V}_{k=1}\eta_{k}F_{j_{k}}
by the terms
F_{s_{1}},F_{s_{2}},\dots,F_{s_{r}}
we can represent
(v_{1},v_{2}+\sum^{r}_{t=1}F_{s_{t}})
in
FS(A_{4})\pmod{p}
.

Consider a coloring of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{p}
according the values of
\sum^{r}_{t=1}F_{s_{t}};(r=0,1,\dots b-1)
, i.e. let
(v_{1},v_{2})
be color
r
, if
(v_{1},v_{2}+\sum^{r}_{t=1}F_{s_{t}})
in
FS(A_{4})\pmod{p}
. Note that the coloring is not unique;
(v_{1},v_{2})
and
(v^{\prime}_{1},v^{\prime}_{2})
can have the same color. For clarity, choose the color
r
to be minimal. Denote by
L_{r}
, the set of points of color
r
. Now choose the
L_{r_{0}}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{p}
for which
|L_{r_{0}}|
is maximal. We have
|L_{r_{0}}|\geq\frac{p^{2}}{b}
. Then
L_{r_{0}}+\sum^{r_{0}}_{t=1}F_{s_{t}}\subseteq FS(A_{4})\pmod{p}
, and
|L_{r_{0}}+\sum^{r_{0}}_{t=1}F_{s_{t}}|=|L_{r_{0}}||\geq\frac{p^{2}}{b}
.

∎

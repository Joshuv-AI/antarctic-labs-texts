Framed Combinatorial Topology with Labels in ∞ \infty -Categories
Lukas Heidemann

Abstract
Framed combinatorial topology is a recent approach to tame geometry which expresses higher-dimensional stratified spaces via tractable combinatorial data. The resulting theory of spaces is well-behaved and computable. In this paper we extend FCT by allowing labelling systems of meshes and trusses in ∞ \infty -categories, and build an alternative model of FCT by constructing ∞ \infty -categories that classify meshes and trusses. This will serve as the foundation for future work on models of higher categories based on generalised string diagrams and the study of generalised tangles.

1
Introduction

Framed combinatorial topology
[
DD21
]
is a recent approach to tame geometry.
Well-behaved stratified spaces are equipped with a canonical ordering of coordinate dimensions, called a framing,
leading to a very tractable theory.
Meshes represent a stratified space as a sequence of
1
-dimensional bundles, each adding one additional coordinate in the order of the framing;
trusses are purely combinatorial representations of framed isomorphism classes of meshes which faithfully capture all the geometric structure of meshes in a form that is amenable to computer implementation.
In this paper we extend FCT with labels in
\infty
-categories.

Figure 1
:
A string diagram expressed both geometrically as a mesh and combinatorially as a truss.
The two representations are equivalent up to a contractible choice of diagram layout.

Motivation.

A central application of FCT is in models of higher-dimensional categories
based on a generalisation of string diagrams to arbitrary dimensions
[
DDV22
,
RV19
,
HRV22
]
.
Meshes detect critical points of braidings and higher-dimensional changes in relative positioning (see Figure
2
),
making them useful in the formulation of a semi-strict theory of
(\infty,n)
-categories with weak interchange laws arising from geometry.
FCT helps translate between geometric representations of diagrams and a combinatorial representation based on trusses (see Figure
1
), which has been implemented in the proof assistant
homotopy.io
.

Figure 2
:
Framed combinatorial topology detects the critical point in the braid in which the two wires move across each other.
This generalises to combinatorially capture the weak structure of higher dimensional categories, such as the Yang-Baxterator, the Zamolodchikov tetrahedron, etc.

However, semi-strict approaches to higher category theory based on string diagrams have not yet been proven sound or complete beyond dimension three
[
BMS12
]
.
Via trusses and meshes labelled in
\infty
-categorical labels and a unified treatment of truss morphisms and bordisms, the formulation of FCT in this paper is designed to ease comparison with accepted homotopy theoretic models of higher categories.
In particular, we will explore in future work how an inclusion functor from the
n
-fold product
\Delta^{\times n}
into
n
-trusses enables a comparison with
n
-fold Segal spaces
[
Rez01
,
Lur08
,
Lur09
,
BSP11
]
.

FCT also has promising applications to geometry and singularity theory,
beginning with the study of manifold diagrams and tame tangles laid out in
[
DD22
]
.
Through
\infty
-categorical labels, we can express local structures imposed on the normal and tangent bundles of manifolds realised as meshes.
Together with the interpretation of meshes as higher-dimensional string diagrams, we believe that this is a natural context to study generalised tangles.

Since this paper is a foundational paper, we will not discuss applications in detail.
For a more detailed discussion of applications, see
[
DD21
,
DDV22
,
HRV22
]
and future work of the author.

Results.

Trusses and meshes as defined in
[
DD21
]
can be equipped with labels in a
1
-category.
In this paper we demonstrate how this can be generalised to labels in an
(\infty,1)
-category,
which allows for labels arising from geometric data.
For every
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
we characterise an
\infty
-category
\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{C})
such that functors
\mathcal{B}\to\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{C})
are equivalent to truss bundles on
\mathcal{B}
labelled in
\mathcal{C}
.
Further we construct an
\infty
-category
\mathbb{M}(\mathcal{C})
such that for any (well-behaved)
stratified space
B
there is an equivalence between functors
\textup{Exit}(B)\to\mathbb{M}(\mathcal{C})
and mesh bundles on
B
labelled in
\mathcal{C}
.
We prove that there is a natural equivalence
\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathbb{M}(\mathcal{C})
and so the theories of trusses and meshes arising from our constructions are equivalent.
Since
\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{C})
and
\mathbb{M}(\mathcal{C})
are
\infty
-categories they can serve as labels themselves, allowing us to obtain
n
-trusses and
n
-meshes
\mathbb{T}^{n}(\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathbb{M}^{n}(\mathcal{C})
.

Related Work.

The framed combinatorial topology project
[
DD21
]
first defined trusses and meshes.
The results in this paper were originally developed as an evolution of the zigzag construction for the project set out in
[
RV19
,
HRV22
]
which aims to study the theoretical and algorithmic underpinnings of the proof assistant homotopy.io and were arrived at independently.
To avoid a splintering of terminology we have decided to adopt the terms trusses and meshes from the latter, although some care must be taken since our definitions are not exactly equivalent.
We believe that the present paper still constitutes a valuable contribution over the discussion in
[
DD21
]
due to its considerably different approach and the capacity to define labels in
\infty
-categories.

Our characterisation of the
\infty
-category of meshes as a sheaf on stratified spaces is
inspired by the striation sheaves and constructible bundles from
[
AFR18
]
.
Since the notion of stratified space employed in this paper is more general, we have to rebuild some part of the theory.

Notation.

Lemma 2.6

The universal truss bundle

i.e. the base change functor

We then need to show that the fibre of the restriction functor

(6)

The theory of trusses in
[
DD21
]
contained maps of (iterated) truss bundles.
These maps are subsumed in the truss bordisms in our formulation of the theory.
While this choice makes a duality theory or simultaneous treatment of open and closed trusses as in
[
DD21
]
harder due to the presence of outer face maps,
it allows to treat truss maps and bordisms in a unified manner.
In particular, it allows for the following comparison functor with
\Delta

Construction 2.13

For any

\mathcal{C}

\Delta\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{C})

which sends
([k],c)
to the truss bundle over
[k]
which is constantly labelled in

.
By induction on
n\geq 0
, this induces a fully faithful functor
\Delta^{\times n}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathbb{T}^{n}(\mathcal{C})

from the

-fold product of
\Delta
with
\mathcal{C}
into
n
-fold truss bundles labelled in
\mathcal{C}
.

In particular for
\mathcal{C}=*
we obtain an inclusion
\Delta^{\times n}\to\mathbb{T}^{n}(*)

In future work we will use this inclusion
in order to compare a theory of

-categories built on trusses with

-fold Segal spaces.

3
Stratified Spaces

Framed combinatorial topology studies stratified spaces which are expressible via meshes.
General poset-stratified spaces, conical stratified spaces or homotopically stratified spaces are too general for our purposes, while the conically smooth stratified spaces of
[
AFR18
]
are too rigid.
In this section we aim to strike a balance by outlining a notion of stratified spaces that is appropriate for our theory.

In particular, we define an
\infty
-category
\mathcal{S}\textup{trat}
of well-behaved stratified spaces represented by a Kan enriched category
Strat
,
together with a fully faithful functor
\textup{Exit}:\mathcal{S}\textup{trat}\to\mathcal{C}\textup{at}_{\infty}
which sends a stratified space to the
\infty
-category of exit paths.
This will allow us to characterise
\mathcal{C}\textup{at}_{\infty}
as a full subcategory of
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{S}\textup{trat})
.
The concepts in this section such as poset-stratified spaces and exit path
\infty
-categories are standard; we only claim originality of how we pull the parts together.

Definition 3.1
.

The Alexandroff topology on a poset
P
is the topology on the underlying set
of
P
in which a subset
U\subseteq P
is open if it is upwards closed, i.e. if for every
x\in U
and
x\leq y
also
y\in U
.

For a convenient category of topological spaces, we use the category
Top
of numerically generated topological spaces
[
Dug
,
FR08
]
.
Equipping posets with the Alexandroff topology induces a fully faithful functor

\textup{Pos}\hookrightarrow\textup{Top}
.
This is not true for the more common choice of weakly Hausdorff spaces, as only discrete posets are weakly Hausdorff with when equipped with the Alexandroff topology.
Moreover numerically generated topological spaces form a locally presentable category, which will be convenient for some arguments.

Definition 3.2
.

The category of
poset-stratified spaces
is the comma category
\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}
of the
inclusion
\textup{Pos}\to\textup{Top}
. In particular, the objects are maps
X\to P
from
a numerically generated topological space into a poset equipped with the Alexandroff
topology.
The preimage of an element
p\in P
is called a
stratum
of
X
.
The maps of
\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}
are commutative squares of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q}

By sending a space
X
to the unique map
X\to[0]
we obtain a fully faithful functor
\textup{Top}\to\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}
which we will use to consider unstratified spaces as trivially stratified spaces.
In particular, we obtain trivially stratified spaces for the topological standard simplices
{|\Delta[n]|}
,
which we use to enrich
\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}
in simplicial sets.

Definition 3.3
.

The category
\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}
of poset-stratified spaces can be simplically enriched:
For poset-stratified spaces

X\to P
and
Y\to Q
the simplicial set of maps is

\textup{Map}_{\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}}(X\to P,Y\to Q):[n]\mapsto(\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos})(X\times{|\Delta[n]|},Y).

The composition of maps

f:X\times{|\Delta[n]|}\to Y
and
g:Y\times{|\Delta[n]|}\to Z
is defined
as the map
X\times{|\Delta[n]|}\to Z
which sends
(x,s)
to

g(f(x,s),s)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}
is tensored and cotensored as a simplicially enriched category.

Proof.

\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}
is tensored by the definition of the simplicial set of maps.

Let
X\to P
be an object of
\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}
and
A
a simplicial set. Since
the tensoring
A\otimes-:\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}\to\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}
preserves colimits
and
\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}
is locally presentable, it has a right adjoint

-^{A}:\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}\to\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}
. Since we have a natural isomorphism

(\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos})(X\times|A|\times{|\Delta[n]|},Y)\cong(\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos})(X\times{|\Delta[n]|},Y^{A})

this functor defines a cotensoring.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}
is enriched in Kan complexes.

Proof.

The Kan fillers can be constructed via any choice of retraction of the topological horn inclusions
{|\Lambda_{k}[n]|}\hookrightarrow{|\Delta[n]|}
for
0\leq k\leq n
.
∎

An important example of stratified spaces are simplicial complexes equipped with their canonical stratification
[
Lur17
, Section A.6]
.

Definition 3.6
.

An abstract simplicial complex
(V,S)
is a pair of a finite set
V
of vertices and a
set
S
of simplices, where a simplex is a finite subset of
V
.
Whenever
\sigma\subseteq V
is a simplex in

a simplex in

An abstract simplicial complex

can be geometrically realised as a topological space

.
For convenience we define
|(V,S)|
to be the geometric realisation of the nerve of
S
regarded as a poset; this is homeomorphic to the usual notion of geometric realisation.
Then we obtain the canonical stratification

by sending

to

when

is contained in the interior of a simplex

where

.

Definition 3.7
.

A poset-stratified space
X\to P
is
triangulable
if there exists a simplicial complex
(S,V)

together with a diagram in

of the form

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{|(V,S)|}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

In this definition we do not require

to be an isomorphism;
in particular we allow the stratification of

to be coarser than the stratification of

by sending different elements of

to the same element in

.

Definition 3.8
.

Let

. The stratified
n
-simplex
\textup{st}[n]
is the stratification of the
topological
n
-simplex
{|\Delta[n]|}
by the map
{|\Delta[n]|}\to[n]
which sends a point

This induces a cosimplicial object

in poset-stratified spaces.

Definition 3.9
.

For any poset-stratified space
X\to P
there is a simplicial set
\textup{Exit}(X)
whose
n
-simplices are
stratified maps
\textup{st}[n]\to X
.
This defines a functor
\textup{Exit}:\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}\to\textup{sSet}
.

Definition 3.10
.

A poset-stratified space
X\to P
is
fibrant
when
\textup{Exit}(X)
is a quasicategory.

Definition 3.11
.

We write
Strat
for the full subcategory of
\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}
consisting of poset-stratified spaces
X\to P
that are fibrant, triangulable and have non-empty path-connected strata.
Then we let
\mathcal{S}\textup{trat}
denote the
\infty
-category arising from

by Dwyer-Kan localisation at the stratified homotopy equivalences.

Example 3.12
.

Every poset-stratified space that is conical in the sense of
[
Lur17
, Definition A.5.5.]

is fibrant by
[
Lur17
, Theorem A.6.4.]
.
Moreover the canonical stratification of any simplicial complex is fibrant by
[
Lur17
, Corollary A.6.9.]
.
However, not every triangulable poset-stratified space is fibrant:
for instance the stratification of the horn
{|\Lambda_{1}[2]|}
as a subspace of
\textup{st}[2]
is not fibrant.

The universal property of the Dwyer-Kan localisation states that for every
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
the space of functors
\mathcal{S}\textup{trat}\to\mathcal{C}

is equivalent to the space of functors
\textup{Strat}\to\mathcal{C}
which send stratified homotopy equivalences to equivalences in
\mathcal{C}
.
In particular, we see that
\textup{Exit}:\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}\to\textup{sSet}
descends to a functor of
\infty
-categories
\textup{Exit}:\mathcal{S}\textup{trat}\to\mathcal{C}\textup{at}_{\infty}
.

We will show that
\textup{Exit}:\mathcal{S}\textup{trat}\to\mathcal{C}\textup{at}_{\infty}
is fully faithful.
For this it is convenient to observe that
by
[
Lur17
, Example 1.3.4.8.]
the
\infty
-category
\mathcal{S}\textup{trat}
is equivalent to the
\infty
-category represented by

as a Kan enriched category via the enrichment inherited from
\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}
.
The mapping spaces of the
\infty
-category
\mathcal{S}\textup{trat}
therefore are represented by the mapping spaces in the Kan enrichment of

.

Definition 3.13
.

There is an adjunction between the comma categories

\|-\|:\textup{sSet}\downarrow\textup{Pos}\rightleftarrows\textup{Top}\downarrow\textup{Pos}:\mathbb{S}\textup{ing}

where

is the geometric realisation

via the

, and
\mathbb{S}\textup{ing}(X\to P)
is the canonical map of simplicial sets

.
For any fixed poset

Lemma 3.14
.

The adjunctions
\|-\|\vdash\mathbb{S}\textup{ing}
and
\|-\|_{P}\vdash\mathbb{S}\textup{ing}_{P}

for every poset

are simplicial.

Proof.

For any simplicial set
A
and object

in
\textup{sSet}/\textup{Pos}
we have that

\|A\otimes B\|\cong A\otimes\|B\|
since ordinary geometric realisation

|-|:\textup{sSet}\to\textup{Top}
preserves products. Hence
\|-\|
defines a simplicial
functor which acts on

-simplices via

\textup{Hom}_{\textup{sSet}/\textup{Pos}}(\Delta[n]\otimes A,B)\to\textup{Hom}_{\textup{Top}/\textup{Pos}}(\|\Delta[n]\otimes A\|,B)\to\textup{Hom}_{\textup{Top}/\textup{Pos}}(\Delta[n]\otimes\|A\|,B)

. This simplicial adjunction
then restricts to a simplicial adjunction

for

.
∎

Lemma 3.15
.

Let

and

is

Because

Then

is the

-truncation of the quasicategory

and for every

and map

Let

Proposition 3.17
.

The functor
\textup{Exit}:\mathcal{S}\textup{trat}\to\mathcal{C}\textup{at}_{\infty}

Proof.

Let

be a stratified space. We pick a triangulation

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{|(V,S)|}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces P}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}

where

is an abstract simplicial complex.
Via the map

we can see

itself as a

Then by

and

the unit

of the adjunction

When

4
Meshes

Figure 3
:
Mesh bundles over
\textup{st}[1]
which represent the inner face map
(0\mapsto 0,1\mapsto 1,2\mapsto 3):[2]\to[3]
,
the outer face map
(0\mapsto 2,1\mapsto 3):[1]\to[4]
and the degeneracy map
(0\mapsto 0,1\mapsto 1,2\mapsto 1,3\mapsto 2):[3]\to[2]
.
The vertical line is the fibre over the

-stratum of

The top and two bottom lines for the outer face map approach the boundary of the open interval

as they approach the left side of the diagram.

Meshes are a geometric version of trusses that augment truss bundles with a choice of coordinates.
Based on meshes, we can build a theory of tame stratified spaces which is equivalent to the purely combinatorial theory of trusses.

Definition 4.1
.

A
1
-mesh
is a finite stratification of the open interval
(-1,1)
such
that every stratum is either an isolated point or an open subinterval.
A point in a

1
-mesh is
singular
if it is an isolated point and
regular

otherwise.

A
1
-mesh should be seen as representing the finite ordinal
[n]
in which every element
i\in[n]
is a regular stratum of the mesh.
The exit path
\infty
-category of a
1
-mesh is a
1
-truss, with regular and singular strata corresponding to the regular and singular elements of the truss.

Definition 4.2
.

A
mesh bundle
over a stratified space
B
is a map of poset-stratified spaces
p:E\to B
which satisfies the following properties:

1.

The underlying map of spaces of
p
is the projection
B\times(-1,1)\to B
.

2.

p
restricts to a trivial bundle
S\times(-1,1)\to S
of spaces for each stratum
S\subseteq B
.

3.

For each
b\in B
the fibre
p^{-1}(b)
is a
1
-mesh.

4.

The regular points of all fibres form an open subset of
B\times(-1,1)
.

Mesh bundles over the stratified
1
-simplex
\textup{st}[1]
correspond to order-preserving maps between the ordinals corresponding to the
1
-meshes over the endpoints.
This is illustrated in Figure
3
.

Remark 4.3
.

Replacing the open interval
(-1,1)
by any other open interval in
\mathbb{R}
would yield an equivalent theory of meshes.
When meshes are applied to characterise manifolds or stratified spaces embedded into
\mathbb{R}^{n}
it is most natural to use
\mathbb{R}
itself.
For the purposes of this paper we have chosen an interval of finite length to simplify arguments based on linear interpolation.

We note that we defined mesh bundles
p:E\to B
over stratified spaces
B
but merely required
E
to be a poset-stratified space.
Lemma
4.9
below shows that
E
has to be triangulable,
and via Proposition
5.5
we will see that
E
is in fact a stratified space.

Lemma 4.4
.

Mesh bundles are closed under pullback.

Proof.

Let
p:E\to B
be a mesh bundle and
f:B^{\prime}\to B
a map of stratified spaces.
The first three properties of Definition
4.2
are preserved by pullback by the pasting lemma and since the forgetful functor
\textup{Strat}\to\textup{Top}
preserves pullbacks.
A point in
E\times_{B}B^{\prime}
over
b^{\prime}\in B
is regular if and only if it is regular in the fibre
(E\times_{B}B^{\prime})\times_{B^{\prime}}\{b^{\prime}\}\cong E\times_{B}\{f(b^{\prime})\}
.
It follows that the set of regular points in
E\times_{B}B^{\prime}
is the preimage of the regular points in
E
via the induced continuous map
E\times_{B}B^{\prime}\to E
and thus it is open.
∎

As mentioned above, the fibre of a mesh bundle
p:E\to B
over any given point is determined up to a contractible choice of coordinates within the interval
(-1,1)
by the number of its regular strata.
We show that this data fits together coherently in the form of a functor
\textup{reg}_{p}:\textup{Exit}(B)\to\Delta
.
Similarly, we can obtain a functor
\textup{sing}_{p}:\textup{Exit}(B)^{\textup{op}}\to\nabla
characterised by the singular strata of the fibres.
Moreover, any such functor gives rise to a mesh bundle which is determined up to a contractible choice.
In this sense, any mesh bundle is determined by combinatorial data.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
p:E\to B
be a mesh bundle. Then there exists a unique well-defined functor

\textup{reg}_{p}:\textup{Exit}(B)\to\Delta

which satisfies the following properties:

1.

When
b\in B
is a point in the base space then
\textup{reg}_{p}(b)

is the set of regular strata in the fibre

with a total
order induced by the canonical order on
(-1,1)
.

2.

When
\gamma:\textup{st}[1]\to B
is an exit path in the base
space, then
\textup{reg}_{p}(\gamma)
sends a regular stratum
R_{0}
over
\gamma(0)

to a regular stratum
R_{1}
over
\gamma(1)
when there exists a lift

\hat{\gamma}:\textup{st}[1]\to E
of
\gamma
which starts in

R_{0}
and ends in
R_{1}
.

Proof.

To show that
\textup{reg}_{p}
is well-defined as a map of sets we first show that the induced functor
\textup{Exit}(E)\to\textup{Exit}(B)
restricts to a discrete fibration on the regular objects in

The lower horizontal map is an equivalence by Proposition
4.11

and so it suffices to check that the upper horizontal map induces an equivalence on homotopy fibres.
Let
\pi:E\to B
be a mesh bundle over
B
.
By Lemma
5.7
the map
\mathbb{M}(B,\mathcal{C})\to\textup{BM}(B)
of Kan complexes
is a Kan fibration and so the homotopy fibre over
\pi

But then the map
\mathbb{M}(B,\mathcal{C})\to\mathbb{T}(\textup{Exit}(B),\mathcal{C})
restricts to the identity on

\mathcal{C}\textup{at}_{\infty}(\textup{Exit}(E),\mathcal{C})
in the fibres.
∎

We can now iterate this construction as we did for trusses to obtain
\infty
-categories

.

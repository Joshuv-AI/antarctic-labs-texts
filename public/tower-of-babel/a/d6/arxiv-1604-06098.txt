Generating Ray Class Fields of Real Quadratic Fields via Complex Equiangular Lines
Marcus Appleby, Steven Flammia, Gary McConnell, Jon Yard

Abstract
. For certain real quadratic fields K K with sufficiently small discriminant we produce explicit unit generators for specific ray class fields of K K using a numerical method that arose in the study of complete sets of equiangular lines in ℂ d \mathbb{C}^{d} (known in quantum information as symmetric informationally complete measurements or SICs). The construction in low dimensions suggests a general recipe for producing unit generators in infinite towers of ray class fields above arbitrary real quadratic K K , and we summarise this in a conjecture. There are indications [ 20 , 21 ] that the logarithms of these canonical units are related to the values of L L -functions associated to the extensions, following the programme laid out in the Stark Conjectures.

4.
Properties of the ray class fields

4.1.
Definition of ray class fields

By the Kronecker-Weber theorem
[
10
]
, any abelian extension of
\mathbb{Q}
is a subfield of some cyclotomic extension
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})
, where
\zeta_{m}
is a primitive
m
-th root of unity for some minimal positive integer
m
.
By the fundamental theorem of Galois theory, therefore, its Galois group is isomorphic under the Artin map to a quotient of
\left(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}\right)^{\times}
, the multiplicative group of the quotient ring
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}
.
The ideal
m\mathbb{Z}
is called the
conductor
of the extension: and indeed
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})
is the
narrow ray class field of
\mathbb{Q}
modulo
m\mathbb{Z}
.
The unique infinite prime of
\mathbb{Q}
is allowed to
ramify
here and we obtain a totally complex field.
If on the other hand we were to restrict ramification just to the finite places dividing
m
then the ray class field modulo the same conductor
m\mathbb{Z}
would be the maximal totally real subfield
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m}+\zeta_{m}^{-1})
of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m})
.

The classical ideal-theoretic version of Artin’s global reciprocity law for finite abelian extensions of any finite extension
K
of
\mathbb{Q}
then leads naturally to the more general notion of the ray class fields of
K
modulo any conductor.
Indeed, let
F
be any finite abelian extension of
K
.
Define
S
as the set of finite and infinite primes of
K
which ramify in
F
; let
I_{S}
be the free abelian group on the finite primes of
K
which do not lie in
S
.
Then the classical Artin map is the homomorphism
\phi_{F/K}:I_{S}\to{\mathrm{Gal}({F/K})}
which sends an unramified finite prime
v
of
K
to the Frobenius automorphism of
v
in
{\mathrm{Gal}({F/K})}
.
Artin’s global reciprocity law asserts that
\phi_{F/K}
is surjective; and moreover that there exists an integral ideal
\mathfrak{f}
of
K
divisible precisely by the finite primes in
S
, such that the kernel of
\phi_{F/K}
always contains the group
P_{\mathfrak{f}}
of principal ideals generated by elements
\alpha
of
K
satisfying:

•

\mathrm{ord}_{v}({\alpha-1})\geq\mathrm{ord}_{v}({\mathfrak{f}})
for all
v
dividing
\mathfrak{f}
; and

•

\alpha
is totally positive at the real primes of
K
which ramify in
F
.

The smallest such
\mathfrak{f}
is called the conductor of
F/K
.
The quotient group
I_{S}/P_{\mathfrak{f}}
is called the
ray class group of
K
modulo
\mathfrak{f}
, and thus Artin’s law asserts that the ray class group modulo
\mathfrak{f}
is mapped onto
{\mathrm{Gal}({F/K})}
by the Artin map.
Moreover, for every such ideal
\mathfrak{f}
, the existence theorem of global class field theory shows that there exists a finite abelian extension
F_{\mathfrak{f}}/K
, unramified outside
S
, such that the kernel of the Artin map for this extension is precisely
P_{\mathfrak{f}}
.
This field, unique up to isomorphism, is called the
ray class field of
K
modulo
\mathfrak{f}
.

Recurrence Structures, Finite State Decomposition, and Statistical Bias in Collatz Path Sequences
Sawon Pratiher

Abstract
We investigate the structure of Collatz path sequences { F k ​ ( n ) } k = 0 ∞ \{F^{k}(n)\}_{k=0}^{\infty} for positive integers n n , where F F denotes the standard Collatz map. By classifying natural numbers into residue classes modulo 4, we establish that the Collatz conjecture reduces to verifying convergence for integers congruent to 3 ( mod 4 ) 3\pmod{4} . For this class, we identify six recurrent forms—residue classes modulo 9—through which the path sequence elements cycle, and we prove that these forms are complete in the sense that every power of 2 belongs to exactly one of them. We construct a deterministic finite state machine (FSM) whose states correspond to these six forms and whose transitions encode the Collatz dynamics, yielding a system of coupled functional equations involving linear congruences. We prove closed-form characterizations of the power-of-2 elements within three of the six recurrent classes and establish an equivalence between the FSM dynamics and the Syracuse acceleration of the Collatz map. Numerical experiments on the first 10 8 10^{8} natural numbers reveal a pronounced statistical bias in the distribution of terminating recurrent forms, with form 9 ​ n + 8 9n+8 accounting for approximately 97.6 % 97.6\% of all terminations, and we formulate precise conjectures regarding the asymptotic frequencies. These results provide a structured decomposition of the Collatz problem into a finite system of interlocking recurrences and highlight the non-random character of the Collatz dynamics.

Introduction

The Collatz conjecture, also known as the
3n+1
problem, concerns the iteration of the map
\mathcal{C}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
defined by

\mathcal{C}(n)=\begin{cases}n/2&\text{if }n\equiv 0\pmod{2},\\
3n+1&\text{if }n\equiv 1\pmod{2}.\end{cases}

(1)

The conjecture asserts that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that
\mathcal{C}^{k}(n)=1
, or equivalently, that the trajectory eventually reaches a power of 2. Despite its elementary statement, the problem has resisted proof since its formulation by Lothar Collatz in 1937 and has attracted contributions from numerous mathematicians; see the surveys by Lagarias
[
5
,
7
]
and Chamberland
[
2
]
.

The conjecture has been computationally verified for all
n<2^{68}

[
1
]
. Terras
[
14
]
and Everett
[
3
]
established stopping-time results, while Steiner
[
12
]
and Simons
[
11
]
proved the non-existence of short cycles. Most recently, Tao
[
13
]
showed that the Collatz conjecture holds for
almost all
positive integers in the sense of logarithmic density.

In this paper, we take a complementary approach. Rather than attacking the conjecture directly, we investigate the
structural
properties of Collatz path sequences. Our main contributions are:

(i)

A rigorous reduction of the conjecture to the class of integers
n\equiv 3\pmod{4}
(
Theorem

3.2
).

(ii)

The identification and proof of completeness of six recurrent forms (residues modulo 9) through which the path sequence elements cycle (
Theorem

4.3
).

(iii)

A finite state machine model with explicit transition functions and an equivalent system of coupled functional equations (
Theorem

5.1
,
Theorem

6.1
).

(iv)

Closed-form characterizations of the power-of-2 elements within three recurrent classes (
Theorem

8.1
).

(v)

An equivalence between the FSM dynamics and the Syracuse map (
Theorem

7.1
).

(vi)

A reformulation of the Collatz conjecture as a covering condition on the congruence system (
Theorem

9.2
).

(vii)

Numerical evidence and precise conjectures regarding the asymptotic frequency distribution of terminating forms (
Conjecture

10.4
).

The

The

is defined by

\mathcal{S}(n)=\frac{3n+1}{2^{\nu_{2}(3n+1)}},

where
\nu_{2}(m)
denotes the 2-adic valuation of

(the largest

). This map accelerates the Collatz iteration by collapsing each odd
\to
even
\to\cdots\to

Definition 2.4

(Modulo-4 classification)
.

We partition
\mathbb{N}
into four residue classes modulo 4:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n\equiv 1\pmod{4}\}=\{4k+1:k\geq 0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n\equiv 2\pmod{4}\}=\{4k+2:k\geq 0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n\equiv 3\pmod{4}\}=\{4k+3:k\geq 0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n\equiv 0\pmod{4}\}=\{4k+4:k\geq 1\}.

3
Reduction to Class
\mathcal{C}

We first establish that the Collatz conjecture reduces to a statement about integers congruent to
3\pmod{4}
.

Lemma 3.1

(Descent for classes
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
, and
\mathcal{D}
)
.

For each class
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{D}
, there exists a fixed finite sequence of Collatz operations that maps any element

(i)

If
n=4k+1\in\mathcal{A}
, then
E(E(O(n)))=3k+1<4k+1

.

(ii)

If
n=4k+2\in\mathcal{B}
, then
E(n)=2k+1<4k+2
for all
k\geq 0
.

(iii)

If
n=4k+4\in\mathcal{D}
, then
E(n)=2k+2<4k+4
for all

.

Proof.

Each part follows by direct computation.

(i)

Let
n=4k+1
with
k\geq 1
. Since
n
is odd,

. Since
12k+4
is even,
E(12k+4)=6k+2

E(6k+2)=3k+1

3k+1<4k+1
if and only if

, which holds for
k\geq 1
. (For

, which is the fixed point of the

(ii)

Let
n=4k+2
. Since
n
is even,

. Clearly
2k+1<4k+2
for all
k\geq 0
.

(iii)

Let
n=4k+4
. Since
n
is even,

. Clearly
2k+2<4k+4
for all
k\geq 0
. ∎

Theorem 3.2

(Reduction to class
\mathcal{C}
)
.

The following are equivalent:

(a)

The Collatz conjecture holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

(b)

For every
n\in\mathcal{C}
, the trajectory of
n
reaches a power of 2.

Proof.

The implication (a)

(b) is trivial. For (b)

(a), we proceed by strong induction. The base case

holds trivially (
1=2^{0}
). Suppose the conjecture holds for all positive integers less than
n
. If
n\in\mathcal{A}\cup\mathcal{B}\cup\mathcal{D}
, then by

3.1
, a fixed sequence of Collatz operations maps
n
to some
m<n
, and the conjecture holds for
m
by the inductive hypothesis. If
n\in\mathcal{C}
, the conjecture holds by assumption (b).
∎

Lemma 3.3

(Non-descent for class
\mathcal{C}
)
.

For
n=4k+3\in\mathcal{C}
, the canonical Collatz path yields:

4k+3\xrightarrow{O}12k+10\xrightarrow{E}6k+5\xrightarrow{O}18k+16\xrightarrow{E}9k+8,

(2)

and
9k+8>4k+3
for all
k\geq 0
. In particular, class
\mathcal{C}
numbers do not necessarily descend under the Collatz map.

Proof.

Direct computation. We have
9k+8>4k+3\iff 5k>-5
, which holds for all
k\geq 0
.
∎

4
The Six Recurrent Forms

Lemma

3.3
shows that every class
\mathcal{C}
number
4k+3
reaches the form
9k+8
after four Collatz operations. This motivates the following definition.

Definition 4.1

(Recurrent forms)
.

We define six residue classes modulo 9, called
recurrent forms
:

\mathfrak{a}:9n+8,\quad\mathfrak{b}:9n+4,\quad\mathfrak{c}:9n+2,\quad\mathfrak{d}:9n+1,\quad\mathfrak{e}:9n+5,\quad\mathfrak{f}:9n+7,

where
n\geq 0
is a non-negative integer parameter.

Remark 4.2
.

The six recurrent forms correspond to residues
\{8,4,2,1,5,7\}\pmod{9}
, which is the set
\{1,2,4,5,7,8\}\pmod{9}
—precisely the residues coprime to 9 that are not divisible by 3. The missing residues
\{0,3,6\}
correspond to multiples of 3, which cannot be powers of 2 (since
\gcd(2^{m},3)=1
for all
m
).

Theorem 4.3

(Completeness of recurrent forms for powers of 2)
.

Every power of 2 belongs to exactly one of the six recurrent forms. More precisely, the powers of 2 cycle through the forms with period 6:

2^{i}\bmod 9\in\{1,2,4,8,7,5\}\quad\text{for }i\equiv\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}\pmod{6}.

(3)

Equivalently:

Residue

\bmod\,9

Powers of 2

\mathfrak{d}

\mathfrak{c}

2^{6j+1}

\mathfrak{b}

8

\mathfrak{f}

where

.

Proof.

Since
\gcd(2,9)=1
, the multiplicative order of
2
modulo
9
is
\ord_{9}(2)=6
, as
2^{6}=64\equiv 1\pmod{9}
and no smaller positive power of 2 is congruent to 1. Hence the sequence
2^{i}\bmod 9
is periodic with period 6:

2^{0}\equiv 1,\;2^{1}\equiv 2,\;2^{2}\equiv 4,\;2^{3}\equiv 8,\;2^{4}\equiv 7,\;2^{5}\equiv 5\pmod{9}.

These six residues
\{1,2,4,5,7,8\}
are precisely the residues of the six recurrent forms. Since the period is exactly 6 and each residue appears exactly once per period, every power of 2 belongs to exactly one form.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

The six recurrent forms partition the set of integers coprime to 3 and not divisible by 9 into six classes. Every power of 2 is coprime to both 3 and 9, confirming that the recurrent forms provide a natural modular framework for the Collatz problem.

For three of the six recurrent forms, we can completely characterize the power-of-2 elements.

Theorem 8.1

(Closed forms for

,
\mathfrak{d}
,
\mathfrak{f}
)
.

Define the

for form

(i)

.

(ii)

S_{\mathfrak{b}}\cap\{2^{m}:m\geq 0\}=\{2^{6j+2}:j\geq 0\}=\{4,256,16384,\ldots\}

(iii)

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of

\mathfrak{d}
(residue 1 mod 9) are those with exponent
\equiv 0\pmod{6}
, and similarly for the other forms, following the period-6 cycle of

Proposition 8.2

(Scaling invariance)
.

Let
x
be a positive integer belonging to recurrent form
r\in\{\mathfrak{a},\ldots,\mathfrak{f}\}
. Then for all
i\geq 0
, the integer
x\cdot 2^{i}
also belongs to form
r
.

Proof.

If
x\equiv s\pmod{9}
for
s\in\{1,2,4,5,7,8\}
, then
x\cdot 2^{i}\equiv s\cdot 2^{i}\pmod{9}
. Since
2^{6}\equiv 1\pmod{9}
, we have
x\cdot 2^{6j}\equiv s\pmod{9}
for all
j
. However, this only shows invariance under multiplication by
2^{6j}
.

For the stronger claim: the Collatz trajectory of
x\cdot 2^{i}
begins with
i
even operations, producing
x
. Hence the stopping power of
x
and
x\cdot 2^{i}
differ only in the exponent: if
x
reaches
2^{m}
, then
x\cdot 2^{i}
also reaches
2^{m}
(after
i

.

The following statements are equivalent:

11
Implications and Open Questions

Remark 11.1

(Relation to Tao’s almost-all result)
.

Tao
[
13
]
proved that the Collatz trajectory of almost every positive integer (in the sense of logarithmic density) reaches any prescribed bound. His proof exploits the ergodic properties of the sequence of 2-adic valuations along the trajectory. The FSM framework provides a complementary perspective: Tao’s result implies that
\Freq_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)+\cdots+\Freq_{\mathfrak{f}}(N)\to 1
as
N\to\infty
, and our numerical data (
Observation

10.2
) provides the refined decomposition of this density among the six forms.

Remark 11.2

(Cycle obstruction)
.

The FSM structure constrains the possible cycle lengths. A non-trivial cycle of the Collatz map would correspond to a periodic orbit of the FSM with the additional arithmetic constraint that the parameter returns to its initial value. By
Proposition

7.3
, the expansion ratios along any cycle must multiply to 1. Since the contractive transitions have ratio
\leq 3/4
and the expansive ones have ratio
\leq 3/2
, the number of expansive steps in a cycle of length
L
is bounded, providing constraints that become increasingly restrictive as
L
grows.

We formulate several open problems motivated by this work:

Q1.

Covering property.
Does the congruence system (
16
)–(
21
) have the property that for every initial condition, the parameter sequence eventually reaches a value making the current form a power of 2? An affirmative answer would prove the Collatz conjecture by
Theorem

9.2
.

Q2.

Constructive formulation for forms
\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathfrak{c}
.
The sets
S_{\mathfrak{a}}
and
S_{\mathfrak{c}}
do not appear to have closed-form descriptions analogous to those for
S_{\mathfrak{b}}
,
S_{\mathfrak{d}}
,
S_{\mathfrak{f}}
. Can these sets be characterized in terms of known number-theoretic functions?

Q3.

Asymptotic constant.
Does
\lim_{N\to\infty}\Freq_{\mathfrak{a}}(N)
exist, and if so, can the constant
\alpha\approx 0.9762
be expressed in closed form (e.g., as a ratio involving
\log 2
and
\log 3
)?

Q4.

Lyapunov function.
Does there exist a function
\Phi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that
\Phi
is strictly decreasing along every FSM trajectory that does not terminate at a power of 2? Such a function would immediately prove the conjecture.

Q5.

Cycle exclusion via FSM.
Can the FSM transition constraints, combined with the expansion ratio analysis of
Proposition

7.3
, be used to prove the non-existence of cycles of arbitrary length?

12
Conclusion

We have presented a rigorous finite state machine decomposition of the Collatz problem, establishing that the dynamics of Class
\mathcal{C}
numbers (which constitute the non-trivial core of the conjecture) are governed by six recurrent forms modulo 9. The completeness of these forms for powers of 2 (
Theorem

4.3
), the explicit FSM transition rules (
Theorem

5.1
), and the resulting congruence system (
Theorem

6.1
) provide a structured framework in which the Collatz conjecture is equivalent to a covering property (
Theorem

9.2
).

The pronounced statistical bias—with form
\mathfrak{a}
dominating at approximately 97.6%—reveals deep non-random structure in the Collatz dynamics and motivates
Conjecture

10.4
. The closed-form characterizations of three termination classes (
Theorem

8.1
) and the equivalence with the Syracuse map (
Theorem

7.1
) connect this framework to the broader landscape of Collatz-related research. We believe the recurrence quantification presented here opens new avenues for investigation, particularly through the coupled functional equations and the cycle-exclusion constraints imposed by the FSM geometry.

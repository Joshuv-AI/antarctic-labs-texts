1.
Introduction

A
(simple) graph

\Gamma=(\Omega,E)
consists of a set
\Omega
of vertices, and a set
E
of unordered pairs of vertices, which we call edges. The graphs we consider are all connected and have automorphism group
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
transitive on
arcs
, that is ordered pairs of adjacent vertices. A
map

\mathcal{M}=(\Omega,E,F)
is a
2
-cell embedding of a simple graph
\Gamma=(\Omega,E)
in a closed surface with face set
F
; and the automorphism group
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})
of
\mathcal{M}
is the subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
consisting of those graph automorphisms which extend to self-homeomorphisms of the supporting surface (see
[
21
, Section 6]
). Avoiding degenerate situations we assume that each edge of
\Gamma
is adjacent with exactly two faces, in which case the only element of
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})
fixing a
flag
(a mutually incident vertex-edge-face triple) is the identity. A consequence is that, if
G\leq{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})
and
G
is arc-transitive on
\Gamma
, then a vertex-stabiliser
G_{\alpha}
is cyclic or dihedral, and
G_{\alpha\beta}\leq C_{2}
for an arc
(\alpha,\beta)
. More details are given in Section
2
.

A group theoretic approach to studying graph embeddings was pioneered by Tony Gardiner, to whom this paper is dedicated. In 1990 Tony and his colleagues
[
8
]
showed that the problem of embedding a graph
\Gamma
as a
flag-regular map

\mathcal{M}
(that is, where
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})
is regular on flags) could be posed as an equivalent problem concerning subgroups of
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
. We address here a more general problem for the case where
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})
is assumed to be transitive on arcs.
Such embeddings have been studied extensively for some particular families of graphs. For example, edge-transitive embeddings of the complete graphs
K_{n}
have been classified completely by Jones
[
22
, Theorem 1.8]
building on constructions and classifications in
[
1
,
17
,
18
]
. In particular,
n
must be either 6 or a prime-power, and in both cases arc-transitive embeddings exist. Similarly flag-regular embeddings of complete bipartite graphs
K_{n,n}
,
n
-dimensional cubes
Q_{n}
, and merged Johnson graphs have also been classified (see
[
5
,
19
,
20
]
and references therein), and graphs from several of these families arise in our work.

Our focus in this paper is on embeddings of connected simple graphs
\Gamma
that have an arc-transitive
almost simple
group of automorphisms
X
, with simple socle
X_{0}
. There are many such graphs of interest, and of course the concept is very general: it just amounts to saying that
\Gamma
is a connected self-paired orbital graph of
X
.
Let
\mathcal{M}
be an embedding of
\Gamma
, and assume that
G\leq{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})\cap X
is also arc-transitive. Then either
G
contains the socle
X_{0}
or it does not. In the first case there is a great deal of information about the structure and action of
G
and
X
(see for example Proposition
2.1
). We shall focus on the second case, in which
G
does not contain the socle
X_{0}
. We prove that apart from the case where the socles of
X
and
G
are
A_{n}
and
A_{n-1}
respectively, the possibilities for the graph
\Gamma
are very restricted: namely,
\Gamma
must be a complete graph
K_{n}
with
n
a prime power, a Johnson graph
J(n,2)
with
n-1
a prime power, or one of 14 further graphs. In the exceptional case
(A_{n},A_{n-1})
, we construct infinitely many graph embeddings.

The strategy of our proof is based on the theory of group factorisations. In Proposition
2.1
, we show that the above situation pertains (namely
X\leq{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
,
G\leq{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})\cap X
both arc-transitive), precisely when the following conditions hold for
\Gamma
and the groups
X
and
G
, where
(\alpha,\beta)
is an arc:

(i)

X=GX_{\alpha}
and
X_{\alpha}=G_{\alpha}X_{\alpha\beta}
;

(ii)

G_{\alpha}
is cyclic or dihedral, and
G_{\alpha\beta}=1
or
C_{2}
;

(iii)

there is an involution
g\in N_{G}(X_{\alpha\beta})
such that
\langle X_{\alpha},g\rangle=X
.

Writing
A=G
,
B=X_{\alpha}
, parts (i) and (ii) lead to a factorisation
X=AB
with
A\cap B
cyclic or dihedral; we call such an expression a
cyclic/dihedral
factorisation of
X
. Motivated by this application (and potentially others), in this paper we classify all such factorisations of almost simple groups
X
. Using this classification, we then find all examples satisfying properties (i)-(iii), and hence classify all the corresponding arc-transitive embeddings. When
X_{0}
is an alternating group
A_{n}
, there are infinitely many arc-transitive graphs with such embeddings (see Theorem
1.7
), but when
X_{0}
is a simple group of Lie type, there are precisely five graphs, and when
X_{0}
is a sporadic simple group there are precisely four more (see Theorems
1.9
and
1.10
).

We now state our main results: first the cyclic/dihedral factorisations, and then the consequences for map embeddings. The factorisation results refer to tables, all of which can be found in Section
10
at the end of the paper.

if, for any vertices
\alpha,\beta
, there exists a finite vertex sequence
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{r}
such that
\alpha_{1}=\alpha
,
\alpha_{r}=\beta
, and each
\{\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i+1}\}
is an edge. The
automorphism group

{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
of a graph
\Gamma=(\Omega,E)
consists of all permutations of the vertex set
\Omega
which leave the edge set
E
invariant. An
arc
of
\Gamma
is an ordered pair
(\alpha,\beta)
of vertices such that
\{\alpha,\beta\}\in E
, and for
G\leq{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
,
\Gamma
is said to be
G
-arc-transitive
if
G
is transitive on the set of arcs. Note that if
\Gamma
is
G
-arc-transitive, then
G
is also vertex-transitive and edge-transitive on
\Gamma
; in particular,
\Gamma
is
regular
in the sense that the set
\Gamma(\alpha)
of vertices adjacent to
\alpha
has the same cardinality for each vertex
\alpha
, called the
valency
of
\Gamma
.

Maps.
A
map

\mathcal{M}=(\Omega,E,F)
is a
2
-cell embedding of a simple graph
\Gamma=(\Omega,E)
in a closed surface with face set
F
; and the automorphism group
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})
of
\mathcal{M}
is the subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
consisting of those graph automorphisms which extend to self-homeomorphisms of the supporting surface (see
[
21
, Section 6]
), sometimes also defined as the subgroup of automorphisms of
\Gamma
that preserve the set of
flags
(mutually incident vertex-edge-face triples)
[
34
, Section 2]
. We assume that
\Gamma
is connected, that
\Gamma
is not a simple cycle (equivalently
\Gamma
has valency at least
3
), and that each edge is incident with exactly two faces.
The only map automorphism that fixes a flag is the identity, that is to say,
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})
is semiregular on the set of flags, and we call
\mathcal{M}

flag-regular
if
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})
is transitive (hence regular) on flags. Also we call
\mathcal{M}

arc-transitive
if
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})
is transitive on the arcs of
\Gamma
. Since the stabiliser in
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})
of an arc
(\alpha,\beta)
must preserve the two faces of
\mathcal{M}
incident with the edge
\{\alpha,\beta\}
, the arc-stabiliser
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})_{\alpha,\beta}
has order at most two, and it has order
2
if and only if
\mathcal{M}
is flag-regular. In fact, for a flag-regular map
\mathcal{M}
, the stabiliser
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})_{\alpha}
of a vertex
\alpha
cyclically permutes the
k:=|\Gamma(\alpha)|
edges incident with
\alpha
and hence induces a circular ordering on
\Gamma(\alpha)
; the stabiliser of an edge
\{\alpha,\beta\}
in this action is the arc-stabiliser
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})_{\alpha,\beta}
, and this interchanges the two faces incident with
\{\alpha,\beta\}
, and hence interchanges the vertices before and after
\beta
in the circular ordering induced on
\Gamma(\alpha)
. Thus for a flag-regular map,
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})_{\alpha}
acts faithfully on
\Gamma(\alpha)
as the dihedral group
D_{2k}
of order
2k
, and the edge-stabiliser
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})_{\{\alpha,\beta\}}\cong C_{2}\times C_{2}
. An arc-transitive map
\mathcal{M}
which is not flag-regular acts regularly on the arc set, and the stabiliser
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})_{\alpha}
is regular on
\Gamma(\alpha)
; it is either a cyclic group
C_{k}
, or
k
is even and it is a dihedral group
D_{k}
(see
[
11
]
,
[
35
, Table I]
or the summary in
[
27
, Table before Theorem 1.3]
). If we wish to emphasise the arc-transitive subgroup
G
of
{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})
, then we refer to the embedding as a
G
-arc-transitive embedding.

We use the following necessary and sufficient conditions for connected graphs other than cycles to have an arc-transitive embedding. Proof of sufficiency relies on constructions in
[
26
,
27
]
which are effective for not necessarily simple arc-transitive graphs. An analogue of this result for flag-regular embeddings was proved in
[
8
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\Gamma=(\Omega,E)
be a finite connected
X
-arc-transitive graph of valency at least
3
, where
X\leq{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
, and let
(\alpha,\beta)
be an arc of
\Gamma
and
G\leq X
. Then
\Gamma
has a
G
-arc-transitive embedding
\mathcal{M}
if and only if the following three conditions hold:

(i)

X=GX_{\alpha}
and
X_{\alpha}=G_{\alpha}X_{\alpha\beta}
;

(ii)

G_{\alpha}
is cyclic or dihedral and
G_{\alpha\beta}=1
or
C_{2}
;

(iii)

there is an involution
g\in N_{G}(X_{\alpha\beta})
such that
\langle X_{\alpha},g\rangle=X
.

Furthermore, if these conditions hold and
X_{\alpha\beta}
contains a nontrivial normal subgroup
N
of
X_{\alpha}
, then
N^{g}\neq N
and
N^{g}\unlhd X_{\alpha\beta}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is a
G
-arc-transitive embedding of
\Gamma
, so
G\leq{\rm Aut}(\mathcal{M})
. Then both
G
and
X
are arc-transitive on
\Gamma
, and this yields part (i). Part (ii) follows from the discussion before the proposition. For part (iii), let
g\in G
be an element that maps the arc
(\alpha,\beta)
to the reverse arc
(\beta,\alpha)
(which exists since
G
is arc-transitive). Then
g\in G_{\{\alpha,\beta\}}
, and hence
g^{2}=1
since

G_{\{\alpha,\beta\}}\leq C_{2}\times C_{2}
, again by the discussion above. In particular

normalises
X_{\alpha\beta}
, and since
\Gamma
is connected,
\langle X_{\alpha},g\rangle=X
(see, for example,
[
26
, Theorems 2.2(a) and 2.11]
).

Conversely, suppose that conditions (i)–(iii) all hold for an arc-transitive subgroup
X\leq{\rm Aut}(\Gamma)
and a subgroup
G\leq X
.
Then condition (i) implies that
G
is arc-transitive on
\Gamma
. The equality
\langle X_{\alpha},g\rangle=X
in condition (iii) implies that
g\not\in X_{\alpha}
so
\beta=\alpha^{g}\neq\alpha
, and the inclusion
g\in N_{G}(X_{\alpha\beta})
in condition (iii) implies that
g\in N_{G}(G_{\alpha\beta})
, and hence
\langle G_{\alpha},g\rangle=G
as
\Gamma
is connected. Let
k:=|\Gamma(\alpha)|
, the valency of
\Gamma
, so
k\geq 3
. Then condition (ii) implies that, for the arc-transitive group
G
, the vertex-stabiliser
G_{\alpha}
is one of
D_{2k}
or
C_{k}
, or
k
is even and
G_{\alpha}=D_{k}
. Suppose first that
G_{\alpha}=D_{2k}
. Then by conditions (ii) and (iii),
G_{\alpha\beta}=\langle x\rangle\cong C_{2}
,
G_{\{\alpha,\beta\}}
(of order
4
) is equal to
\langle x,g\rangle\cong C_{2}\times C_{2}
, and there is a third involution

such that
G_{\alpha}=\langle x,y\rangle
. Then the involution triple
(x,y,g)
is a flag-regular triple as in
[
26
, Definition 4.8]
, and by
[
26
, Proposition 4.11]
, the construction
[
26
, Construction 4.9]
produces a flag-regular map with automorphism group
G
(or see
[
8
, Section 3]
), embedding a graph
\Sigma
with vertex set
V\Gamma
. Since

acts arc-transitively on
\Sigma
and
(\alpha,\beta)
is an arc of
\Sigma
in this construction, it follows that
\Sigma=\Gamma
, and we have a flag-regular embedding of
\Gamma
. Next suppose that
G_{\alpha}=\langle a\rangle=C_{k}
. Then the pair
(a,g)
is a rotary pair as in
[
26
, Definition 1.6]
, and by
[
26
, Propositions 4.2 and 4.5]
, the constructions
[
26
, Constructions 4.2 and 4.4]
produce arc-transitive maps with vertex set

and automorphism group containing

as an arc-transitive subgroup. The same argument as in the flag-regular case shows that the embedded graph is
\Gamma
. Moreover by
[
26

, these constructions provide the only arc-transitive embeddings of
\Gamma
up to map isomorphism.
Now let
G_{\alpha}=D_{k}
with

even. Then
G_{\alpha}=\langle x,y\rangle
for some involutions
x,y
, and by condition (iii),
G=\langle x,y,g\rangle
,
so
(x,y,z)
is an arc-transitive triple as in
[
27
, Definition 1.1]
. Hence by
[

, Propositions 3.4 and 3.7]
, the

[

produce maps with vertex set

and with automorphism groups containing the arc-regular subgroup

, these constructions are the only arc-transitive embeddings of

Finally suppose that conditions (i)–(iii) hold and that

contains a nontrivial normal subgroup

. Then by part (iii),

2.1.
The map theorem: strategy and set-up

Our main application of the factorisation results for almost simple groups in Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.4
, relates to arc-transitive embeddings of graphs. We are concerned with a graph
\Gamma
admitting an arc-transitive, almost simple subgroup
X
of automorphisms, such that
\Gamma
has a
G
-arc-transitive embedding for some subgroup
G
of
X
. We analyse the case where
G
does not contain
{\rm soc}(X)
, and obtain an almost complete classification.

Given the graph
\Gamma
and groups
X
and
G
, existence of a
G
-arc-transitive embedding is equivalent to conditions (i)–(iii) of Proposition
2.1
. Moreover,
as explained in the proof of Proposition
2.1
, for permutation groups
G<X
such that conditions (i)–(iii) of Proposition
2.1
hold, these three conditions may be used to construct both the
X
-arc-transitive graph and the embedding. This observation underpins our strategy.

Conditions (i)–(iii) of Proposition
2.1
together with the condition that
G
does not contain
{\rm soc}(X)
are captured in Hypothesis
1.6
(on taking
\beta=\alpha^{g}
in Hypothesis
1.6
). Using this hypothesis we examine each of the cyclic/dihedral factorisations identified in
Theorems
1.1
–
1.4
; we determine whether or not there exists an involution
g\in G
with the properties required by Hypothesis
1.6
, and if found then we construct the graph and note the embedding. We obtain explicit identifications of the graphs with the exception of the cases of Theorem
1.7
(ii).

To help with our analysis, we note that the last sentence of Proposition
2.1
provides additional information about the subgroup structure which follows from conditions (i)–(iii), and this extra condition proves very useful. Thus we make the following hypothesis which (by Proposition
2.1
) is equivalent to Hypothesis
1.6
.

Hypothesis 2.2
.

X
is an almost simple group with socle
X_{0}
, acting faithfully and transitively on a set
\Omega
,
G
is a core-free subgroup of
X
, and
g\in G
is an involution such that the following hold for
\alpha\in\Omega
and
\beta=\alpha^{g}
:

(a)

X=G\,X_{\alpha}
,
X_{\alpha}=G_{\alpha}\,X_{\alpha\beta}
,
G_{\alpha}
is cyclic or dihedral, and
|G_{\alpha\beta}|\leq 2
;

(b)

g\in N_{G}(X_{\alpha\beta})\setminus N_{G}(X_{\alpha})
, and
\langle X_{\alpha},g\rangle=X
;

(c)

if
1\neq N\unlhd X_{\alpha}
and
N\leq X_{\alpha\beta}
, then
N^{g}\neq N
and
N^{g}\unlhd X_{\alpha\beta}
.

Note that these conditions imply that
\Gamma=(\Omega,\{\alpha,\beta\}^{X})
is a connected simple graph of valency at least
3
with arc-transitive automorphism subgroups
X,G
, and that
g
reverses the arc
(\alpha,\beta)
.

We shall analyse the cyclic/dihedral factorisation for the alternating groups, Lie type groups, and sporadic groups in Sections
7
,
8
and
9
, respectively, to determine which of them satisfy Hypothesis

and hence correspond to arc-transitive embeddings, thus proving Theorems
1.7
,
1.9
and
1.10
.

2.2.
Our use of computation

In our proofs of the factorisation theorems

,

3.3.
Other general constructions

Several of our examples of arc-transitive graphs arise as orbital graphs of a transitive permutation group. Given a transitive permutation group

4.
A new infinite family of arc-transitive maps from alternating groups

In Construction
4.3
we define an infinite family of graphs which have arc-transitive embeddings: each of these graphs has prime valency
p
and admits an arc-transitive action of an alternating group
A_{n}
and also an arc-transitive action of one of its subgroups
A_{n-1}
, for certain primes
p
, where
n=(p-1)!/2
. We use the following set of primes.

Definition 4.1
.

Define
\Pi
to be the set of all primes
p
such that

(a)

p\equiv 3\pmod{4}
and
p>3
; and

(b)

the only insoluble transitive permutation groups of degree
p
are
A_{p}
and
S_{p}
.

Remark 4.2
.

It is well-known (see for example
[
12
, Theorem 1]
) that
\Pi
contains all primes satisfying part (a) except
11,23
, and those of the form
\frac{q^{d}-1}{q-1}
, where
q
is a prime-power. We claim that for a large positive integer
x
,

(4.1)

|\{p\in\Pi:p\leq x\}|\sim\frac{x}{2\log x},

(so in particular,
\Pi
is infinite). To see this, write
X=\mathbb{N}\cap[1,x]
, and note that the number of integers in
X
of the form
\frac{q^{d}-1}{q-1}
for some
d\geq 3
is at most
x^{1/2}+x^{1/3}+\cdots+x^{1/(d-1)}
. Also the number of primes in
X
of the form
q+1
, with
q
a prime power, is at most
\log_{2}x
. It follows that

|\Pi\cap X|=\pi_{1}(x)+O(x^{1/2}),

where
\pi_{1}(x)
is the number of primes in
X
congruent to 3 modulo 4. By Dirichlet’s theorem
\pi_{1}(x)\sim\frac{x}{2\log x}
, and (
4.1
) follows.

Construction 4.3
.

Let
p\in\Pi
and
n:=(p-1)!/2
.

(i)

Let
H=A_{p}
with subgroups
C=C_{p}
and
K=A_{p-1}
so that

, and
H
has a transitive coset action on
I:=[H:C]=\{Cx\mid x\in K\}
, with
|I|=n
.

(ii)

Let
X:=\operatorname{Alt}(I)
and
\nu:=C\in I
, so
G:=X_{\nu}=A_{n-1}
. Then
X
has a transitive coset action on
\Omega:=[X:H]=\{Hx\mid x\in X\}
and, for
\alpha:=H\in\Omega
,
X_{\alpha}=H
.

(iii)

Let
g_{0}\in{\rm Aut}(K)
correspond to a transposition in
{\rm Aut}(K)\cong S_{p-1}
, and define an associated map on
I=[H:C]
as follows:

g:Cx\mapsto Cx^{g_{0}}\;\hbox{ for }x\in K.

We show in Claim 1 below that
g\in G
, that
|g|=2
, and that
\beta:=\alpha^{g}\in\Omega\setminus\{\alpha\}
.

(iv)

Define
\Gamma(p)
to be the orbital graph on
\Omega
with edge set
\{\alpha,\beta\}^{X}
. So we have

X=A_{n},\,X_{\alpha}=A_{p},\,X_{\alpha\beta}=A_{p-1},\,G=A_{n-1},\,G_{\alpha}=C_{p},\,G_{\alpha\beta}=1.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
p\in\Pi
(as in Definition
4.1
), and let

and
\Gamma=\Gamma(p),X=A_{n},G=A_{n-1},

(i)

\Gamma
is a connected, non-bipartite,

-arc-transitive graph of valency

with

(ii)

the vertex-stabiliser

, and arc-stabiliser

We note that the map

Proof.

Adopt the notation of Construction

First we make some obervations about the

Next we establish the claims made in Construction

In this section we prove Theorem

For convenience, we restate Theorem

Theorem 7.1

2.2

We first prove two lemmas about

-transitive groups which are relevant for the proof of Theorem

Lemma 7.2

-transitive of affine type such that

We identify

is dihedral, so

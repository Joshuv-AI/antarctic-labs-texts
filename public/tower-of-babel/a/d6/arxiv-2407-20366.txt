Stackelberg-Nash null controllability for stochastic parabolic equations
Omar Oukdach 1 , Said Boulite 2 , Abdellatif Elgrou 3 , and Lahcen Maniar 3,4

Abstract
. We study a hierarchical control problem for stochastic parabolic equations involving gradient terms. We employ the Stackelberg-Nash strategy with two leaders and two followers. The leaders are responsible for selecting the policy targeting null controllability, while the followers solve a bi-objective optimal control problem which consists of maintaining the solution process close to prefixed targets. Once the Nash equilibrium is determined, the problem reduces to achieving null controllability of a coupled forward-backward stochastic system. To solve this problem, via Carleman estimates, we establish a suitable observability inequality. Subsequently, we achieve the desired controllability result.

1.
Introduction and the main result

In this paper, we study a hierarchical bi-objective control problem for linear stochastic heat equations in a bounded domain. Let
T>0
,
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N\geq 1
) be an open bounded domain with a smooth boundary
\Gamma
and let the sets
\mathcal{O}
,
\mathcal{O}_{1}
,
\mathcal{O}_{2}
,
\mathcal{O}_{1,d}
and
\mathcal{O}_{2,d}
be nonempty open subsets of
G
. We denote by
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{E}}
the characteristic function of a subset
\mathcal{E}\subset G
. Throughout this paper, we use
C
for a positive constant that may vary from one place to another. The norm and inner product of a given Hilbert space
H
will be respectively denoted by
|\cdot|_{H}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{H}
. Set

Q=(0,T)\times G,\,\,\quad\Sigma=(0,T)\times\Gamma\,\,\quad\text{and}\,\,\quad Q_{0}=(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]},\mathbb{P})
be a fixed complete filtered probability space on which a one-dimensional standard Brownian motion
W(\cdot)
is defined such that
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
is the natural filtration generated by
W(\cdot)
and augmented by all the
\mathbb{P}
-null sets in
\mathcal{F}
. For a Banach space
\mathcal{X}
, we denote by
C([0,T];\mathcal{X})
the Banach space of all
\mathcal{X}
-valued continuous functions defined on
[0,T]
; and
L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{t}}(\Omega;\mathcal{X})
denotes the Banach space of all
\mathcal{X}
-valued
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable random variables
X
such that
\mathbb{E}\big(|X|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}\big)<\infty
, with the canonical norm; and
L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;\mathcal{X})
indicates the Banach space consisting of all
\mathcal{X}
-valued
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
-adapted processes
X(\cdot)
such that
\mathbb{E}\big(|X(\cdot)|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,T;\mathcal{X})}\big)<\infty
, with the canonical norm; and
L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;\mathcal{X})
is the Banach space consisting of all
\mathcal{X}
-valued
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
-adapted essentially bounded processes, with its norm denoted simply by
|\cdot|_{\infty}
; and
L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T];\mathcal{X}))
defines the Banach space consisting of all
\mathcal{X}
-valued
\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,T]}
-adapted continuous processes
X(\cdot)
such that
\mathbb{E}\left[\displaystyle\max_{t\in[0,T]}|X(t)|^{2}_{\mathcal{X}}\right]<\infty
, with the canonical norm. In what follows, we adopt the following notations

\mathcal{H}_{i}=L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{i})),\,\quad\mathcal{H}_{i,d}=L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathcal{O}_{i,d}))\quad\,\textnormal{for}\;\;\;i=1,2,

and

\mathcal{U}=L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathcal{O}))\times L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G)),\,\quad\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}_{2}.

We consider the following stochastic parabolic equation

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{lll}dy-\Delta y\,dt\;\,=&\left[a_{1}y+B_{1}\cdot\nabla y+u_{1}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}}+v_{1}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}+v_{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}\right]\,dt\\
&+\left[a_{2}y+B_{2}\cdot\nabla y+u_{2}\right]\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in}\,\,Q,\\
y=0&&\textnormal{on}\,\,\Sigma,\\
y(0)=y_{0}&&\textnormal{in}\,\,G,\end{array}\end{cases}

where
a_{1},a_{2}\in L_{\mathcal{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(G))
,
B_{1},B_{2}\in L_{\mathcal{F}}^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}(G;\mathbb{R}^{N}))
,
y_{0}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))
is the initial state. The leaders and followers are constituted respectively of

(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{U}
and
(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathcal{H}
. In equation (
1.1
), the set
\mathcal{O}
represents the primary control domain, while
\mathcal{O}_{1}
and
\mathcal{O}_{2}
denote secondary control domains, all of which are assumed to be small. For simplicity, we use only four controls (two leaders and two followers), but very similar considerations hold with more than two followers. The case of a single leader is of great interest; however, this task is far from resolved. See Section
5
for more explanations.

From
[
15
]
, it is well-known that (
1.1
) is well-posed i.e., for any

y_{0}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))
,
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{U}
and
(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathcal{H}
, there exists a unique weak solution

y\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T];L^{2}(G)))\bigcap L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(G)).

Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
so that

\displaystyle|y|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega;C([0,T];L^{2}(G)))}+|y|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;H^{1}_{0}(G))}\leq C\,\left(|y_{0}|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))}+|(u_{1},u_{2})|_{\mathcal{U}}+|(v_{1},v_{2})|_{\mathcal{H}}\right).

System (
1.1
), without controls, describes various diffusion phenomena, such as thermal processes. These systems, subject to stochastic disturbances, also incorporate small independent changes during the heat process. For further details, see, for instance,
[
18
, Chapter 5]
and the references therein.

Remark 1.1
.

(1)

Only for the simplicity of the presentation, we have worked with the Laplacian operator in (
1.1
). As in
[
5
]
, it may be replaced by the following general self-adjoint second-order operator

\displaystyle L(t)y=\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(a_{ij}(t,x)\frac{\partial y}{\partial x_{j}}\right),

where
a_{ij}:\Omega\times Q\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfy the following assumptions:

(a)

a_{ij}\in L^{\infty}_{\mathcal{F}}(\Omega;C^{1}([0,T];W^{2,\infty}(G)))
and
a_{ij}=a_{ji}
, for any
1\leq i,j\leq N
.

(b)

There exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that

\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}a_{ij}\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\geq c_{0}|\xi|^{2}\qquad\textnormal{for any}\quad(\omega,t,x,\xi)\in\Omega\times Q\times\mathbb{R}^{N}.

It would be also interesting to consider only
W^{1,\infty}
-space regularity of coefficients
a_{ij}
as in the deterministic case.

(2)

As we mentioned above, using only one leader
u_{1}
is an open problem. It also seems that considering the diffusion term
“
\left[a_{2}y+B_{2}\cdot\nabla y+\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}u_{2}\right]\,dW(t)
” where
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is another nonempty open subset of
G
, is also unresolved problem; we refer to
[
5
,
24
]
for more explanations.

This paper deals with Stackelberg-Nash controllability for linear stochastic parabolic equations. To the best of our knowledge, in contrast to the deterministic setting, the present paper is the first to consider hierarchical control problems for stochastic parabolic equations with Dirichlet boundary conditions. Let us now formulate the problem under consideration: For fixed
y_{i,d}\in\mathcal{H}_{i,d}
(
i=1,2
) two target functions, we consider the following secondary functionals

J_{i}(u_{1},u_{2};v_{1},v_{2})=\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{i}}{2}\mathbb{E}\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}_{i,d}}|y-y_{i,d}|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{i}}{2}\mathbb{E}\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}_{i}}|v_{i}|^{2}\,dx\,dt,\quad i=1,2,

and the main cost functional

J(u_{1},u_{2})=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\left(|\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}}\,u_{1}|^{2}+|u_{2}|^{2}\right)\,dx\,dt,

where
\alpha_{i}
,
\beta_{i}
are positive constants and
y=y(y_{0},u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2})
is the solution of (
1.1
). For a fixed
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{U}
, the pair
(v^{\star}_{1},v^{\star}_{2})\in\mathcal{H}
is called a Nash equilibrium for
(J_{1},J_{2})
associated to
(u_{1},u_{2})
if

J_{1}(u_{1},u_{2};v^{\star}_{1},v^{\star}_{2})=\min\limits_{v\in\mathcal{H}_{1}}J_{1}(u_{1},u_{2};v,v^{\star}_{2})\,\,\;\;\textnormal{and}\;\;\,\,\,J_{2}(u_{1},u_{2};v^{\star}_{1},v^{\star}_{2})=\min\limits_{v\in\mathcal{H}_{2}}J_{2}(u_{1},u_{2};v^{\star}_{1},v).

Since the functionals
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
are differentiable and convex, then the pair
(v^{\star}_{1},v^{\star}_{2})\in\mathcal{H}
is a Nash equilibrium for
(J_{1},J_{2})
if and only if

(1.2)

J_{1}^{\prime}(u_{1},u_{2};v^{\star}_{1},v^{\star}_{2})(v,0)=0\qquad\textnormal{for any}\quad v\in\mathcal{H}_{1},

and

(1.3)

J_{2}^{\prime}(u_{1},u_{2};v^{\star}_{1},v^{\star}_{2})(0,v)=0\qquad\textnormal{for any}\quad v\in\mathcal{H}_{2}.

The objective is to prove that for any initial state
y_{0}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))
, there exist a couple of controls
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{U}
minimizing the functional
J
and an associated Nash equilibrium
(v^{\star}_{1},v^{\star}_{2})=(v^{\star}_{1}(u_{1},u_{2}),v^{\star}_{2}(u_{1},u_{2}))\in\mathcal{H}
such that the associated solution of equation (
1.1
) satisfies the null controllability

(1.4)

y(T,\cdot)=0\;\;\textnormal{in}\;\;G,\quad\mathbb{P}\textnormal{-a.s.}

To this end, we follow the Stackelberg-Nash strategy: For each choice of the leaders
(u_{1},u_{2})
, we look for a Nash equilibrium pair for the functionals
J_{i}
(
i=1,2
), meaning finding the controls
v^{\star}_{1}(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
v^{\star}_{2}(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{H}_{2}
, depending on
(u_{1},u_{2})
and satisfying (
1.2
)-(
1.3
). Once the Nash equilibrium has been identified and fixed for each
(u_{1},u_{2})
, we proceed to determine controls
(\widehat{u}_{1},\widehat{u}_{2})\in\mathcal{U}
such that

J(\widehat{u}_{1},\widehat{u}_{2})=\min_{(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{U}}J(u_{1},u_{2}),

and the associated solution of (
1.1
) satisfies the controllability property (
1.4
). In the subsequent sections, the entire problem can be reduced to the controllability of a coupled forward-backward stochastic system. This is demonstrated by employing some appropriate Carleman estimates for forward and backward stochastic parabolic equations.

Let us assume the following assumption

(1.5)

\mathcal{O}_{d}=\mathcal{O}_{1,d}=\mathcal{O}_{2,d}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{O}_{d}\cap\mathcal{O}\neq\emptyset.

The main result of this paper is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let us assume that the assumption (
1.5
) holds and
\beta_{i}>0
,
i=1,2
, are sufficiently large. Then, there exists a positive weight function
\rho=\rho(t)
blowing up at
t=T
such that for every target functions
y_{i,d}\in\mathcal{H}_{i,d}
satisfying

(1.6)

\mathbb{E}\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}_{i,d}}\rho^{2}|y_{i,d}|^{2}\,dx\,dt<\infty,\qquad i=1,2,

and for every initial condition
y_{0}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))
, there exist controls
(\widehat{u}_{1},\widehat{u}_{2})\in\mathcal{U}
minimizing
J
and an associated Nash equilibrium
(v^{\star}_{1},v^{\star}_{2})\in\mathcal{H}
such that the associated solution
\widehat{y}
of (
1.1
) satisfies

\widehat{y}(T,\cdot)=0\;\;\textnormal{in}\;\;G,\quad\mathbb{P}\textnormal{-a.s.}

Moreover, the controls
(\widehat{u}_{1},\widehat{u}_{2})
can be chosen so that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |(\widehat{u}_{1},\widehat{u}_{2})|^{2}_{\mathcal{U}}\leq C\left[\mathbb{E}|y_{0}|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\alpha^{2}_{i}\mathbb{E}\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}_{i,d}}\rho^{2}y^{2}_{i,d}\,dx\,dt\right],\end{aligned}

where the positive constant
C
depending on
G
,
\mathcal{O}_{i}
,
\mathcal{O}_{i,d}
,
T
,
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
.

2.
Nash-equilibrium

This section is devoted to demonstrating the existence and uniqueness, as well as characterizing the Nash equilibrium in the sense
of (
1.2
)-(
1.3
) for any
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{U}
.

2.1.
Existence and uniqueness

The following result shows the existence and uniqueness of the Nah equilibrium for the functionals
(J_{1},J_{2})
.

Proposition 2.1
.

There exists a large
\overline{\beta}>0
such that, if
\beta_{i}\geq\overline{\beta}
for
i=1,2
, then for each
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{U}
, there exists a unique Nash-equilibrium
(v^{\star}_{1},v^{\star}_{2})=(v^{\star}_{1}(u_{1},u_{2}),v^{\star}_{2}(u_{1},u_{2}))\in\mathcal{H}
for
(J_{1},J_{2})
associated to
(u_{1},u_{2})
. Furthermore, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(2.1)

|(v^{\star}_{1},v^{\star}_{2})|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq C\big(1+|(u_{1},u_{2})|_{\mathcal{U}}\big),

where the constant
C
depends on
G
,
\mathcal{O}
,
T
,
\mathcal{O}_{i}
,
\mathcal{O}_{i,d}
,
\alpha_{i}
,
\beta_{i}
,
y_{0}
,
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
.

Proof.

Let us consider the following bounded operators
L_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{i};L_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(G)))
defined by

\displaystyle L_{i}(v_{i})=y^{i},\qquad i=1,2,

where
y^{i}
is the solution of

(2.2)

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dy^{i}-\Delta y^{i}\,dt=[a_{1}y^{i}+B_{1}\cdot\nabla y^{i}+v_{i}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}]\,dt+[a_{2}y^{i}+B_{2}\cdot\nabla y^{i}]\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in}\,\,Q,\\
y^{i}=0&\textnormal{on}\,\,\Sigma,\\
y^{i}(0)=0&\textnormal{in}\,\,G.\end{array}\end{cases}

Note that the solution
y
of (
1.1
) can be written as follows

y=L_{1}(v_{1})+L_{2}(v_{2})+q,

where
q=q(y_{0},u_{1},u_{2})
is the solution of

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dq-\Delta q\,dt=[a_{1}q+B_{1}\cdot\nabla q+u_{1}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}}]\,dt+[a_{2}q+B_{2}\cdot\nabla q+u_{2}]\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in}\,\,Q,\\
q=0&\textnormal{on}\,\,\Sigma,\\
q(0)=y_{0}&\textnormal{in}\,\,G.\end{array}\end{cases}

For fixed
(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{U}
, we have that for all
v_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{i}

\displaystyle J_{i}^{\prime}(u_{1},u_{2};v^{\star}_{1},v^{\star}_{2})(v_{i})=

\displaystyle\,\,\alpha_{i}\big\langle L_{1}(v^{\star}_{1})+L_{2}(v^{\star}_{2})+q-y_{i,d},L_{i}(v_{i})\big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{i,d}}+\beta_{i}\big\langle v^{\star}_{i},v_{i}\big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{i}},\qquad i=1,2.

Then
(v^{\star}_{1},v^{\star}_{2})
is a Nash equilibrium for
(J_{1},J_{2})
if and only if

(2.3)

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}\big\langle L_{i}^{*}\big[L_{1}(v^{\star}_{1})+L_{2}(v^{\star}_{2})-(y_{i,d}-q)\big],v_{i}\big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{i,d}}+\beta_{i}\big\langle v^{\star}_{i},v_{i}\big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}=0,\quad\forall v_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{i},\qquad i=1,2,

which is equivalent to

\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{*}\left[(L_{1}(v^{\star}_{1})+L_{2}(v^{\star}_{2}))\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{i,d}}\right]+\beta_{i}v^{\star}_{i}=\alpha_{i}L_{i}^{*}((y_{i,d}-q)\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{i,d}})\quad\textnormal{in}\;\;\mathcal{H}_{i},\quad i=1,2,

where
L_{i}^{*}\in\mathcal{L}(L_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(G));\mathcal{H}_{i})
is the adjoint operator of
L_{i}
. Now, the objective is to show that there exists a unique
(v_{1}^{*},v_{2}^{*})\in\mathcal{H}
such that

\textbf{M}(v_{1}^{*},v_{2}^{*})=\Phi,

where
\textbf{M}:\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}
is the bounded operator defined by

\textbf{M}(v_{1},v_{2})=\Big(\alpha_{1}L_{1}^{*}\left[(L_{1}(v_{1})+L_{2}(v_{2}))\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{1,d}}\right]+\beta_{1}v_{1}\,,\,\alpha_{2}L_{2}^{*}[(L_{1}(v_{1})+L_{2}(v_{2}))\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{2,d}}]+\beta_{2}v_{2}\Big),

and

\Phi=(\alpha_{1}L_{1}^{*}((y_{1,d}-q)\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{1,d}}),\alpha_{2}L_{2}^{*}((y_{2,d}-q)\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{2,d}})).

On the other hand, it is easy to see that for any
(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathcal{H}
, one has

\displaystyle\langle\textbf{M}(v_{1},v_{2}),(v_{1},v_{2})\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\beta_{i}|v_{i}|_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{2}\alpha_{i}\langle L_{j}(v_{j}),L_{i}(v_{i})\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{i,d}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{2}(\beta_{i}-C)|v_{i}|_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}^{2}.

Then for a large
\overline{\beta}>0
so that
\beta_{i}\geq\overline{\beta}
,
i=1,2
, we conclude that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\langle\textbf{M}(v_{1},v_{2}),(v_{1},v_{2})\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\geq C|(v_{1},v_{2})|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

We now introduce the bi-linear functional
\textbf{a}:\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
as follows

\textbf{a}((v_{1},v_{2}),(\widetilde{v}_{1},\widetilde{v}_{2}))=\langle\textbf{M}(v_{1},v_{2}),(\widetilde{v}_{1},\widetilde{v}_{2})\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\qquad\textnormal{for any}\quad\big((v_{1},v_{2}),(\widetilde{v}_{1},\widetilde{v}_{2})\big)\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H},

and also define the following linear continuous functional
\Psi:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\Psi(v_{1},v_{2})=\big\langle(v_{1},v_{2}),(\alpha_{1}L_{1}^{*}((y_{1,d}-q)\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{1,d}}),\alpha_{2}L_{2}^{*}((y_{2,d}-q)\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{2,d}}))\big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\qquad\textnormal{for any}\quad(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathcal{H}.

Notice that
a
is continuous, and from (
2.4
), it is coercive. Therefore, by Lax-Milgram theorem, there exists a unique
(v^{*}_{1},v^{*}_{2})\in\mathcal{H}
so that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\textbf{a}((v^{*}_{1},v^{*}_{2}),(v_{1},v_{2}))=\Psi(v_{1},v_{2})\qquad\textnormal{for any}\quad(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathcal{H}.

This shows the existence and uniqueness of the Nash equilibrium
(v^{*}_{1},v^{*}_{2})
for
(J_{1},J_{2})
associated to
(u_{1},u_{2})
. Finally, from (
2.5
), we have that

|(v^{*}_{1},v^{*}_{2})|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq C|(\alpha_{1}L_{1}^{*}((y_{1,d}-q)\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{1,d}}),\alpha_{2}L_{2}^{*}((y_{2,d}-q)\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{2,d}}))|_{\mathcal{H}},

which easily implies the inequality (
2.1
).
∎

2.2.
Characterization of the Nash-equilibrium

Let us now characterize the Nash equilibrium by the following adjoint systems,
i=1,2
,

(2.6)

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dz^{i}+\Delta z^{i}\,dt=\big[-a_{1}z^{i}-a_{2}Z^{i}+\nabla\cdot(z^{i}B_{1}+Z^{i}B_{2})-\alpha_{i}(y-y_{i,d})\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{i,d}}\big]\,dt+Z^{i}\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in}\,\,Q,\\
z^{i}=0&\textnormal{on}\,\,\Sigma,\\
z^{i}(T)=0&\textnormal{in}\,\,G.\end{array}\end{cases}

By applying Itô formula for solutions of (
1.1
) and (
2.6
) and integration by parts, one has

\alpha_{i}\big\langle y(u_{1},u_{2};v_{1},v_{2})-y_{i,d},L_{i}(v_{i})\big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{i,d}}=\langle z^{i},v_{i}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{i}},\qquad i=1,2,

which with (
2.3
) yield that
(v^{\star}_{1},v^{\star}_{2})
is a Nash-equilibrium if and only if

\langle z^{i},v_{i}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}+\beta_{i}\langle v^{\star}_{i},v_{i}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}=0\quad\textnormal{for any}\quad v_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{i},\qquad i=1,2.

Hence,

v^{\star}_{i}=-\frac{1}{\beta_{i}}z^{i}|_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}_{i}},\qquad i=1,2.

Therefore, the problem is then reduced to show the null controllability for solutions of the following coupled forward-backward system, called the optimality system

(2.7)

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dy-\Delta y\,dt=\left[a_{1}y+B_{1}\cdot\nabla y+u_{1}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}}-\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{1}{\beta_{i}}z^{i}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}\right]\,dt+\left[a_{2}y+B_{2}\cdot\nabla y+u_{2}\right]\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in}\,\,Q,\\
dz^{i}+\Delta z^{i}\,dt=\left[-a_{1}z^{i}-a_{2}Z^{i}+\nabla\cdot(z^{i}B_{1}+Z^{i}B_{2})-\alpha_{i}(y-y_{i,d})\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{i,d}}\right]\,dt+Z^{i}\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in}\,\,Q,\\
y=0,\,\,z^{i}=0&\textnormal{on}\,\,\Sigma,\\
y(0)=y_{0}&\textnormal{in}\,\,G,\\
z^{i}(T)=0,\qquad i=1,2,&\textnormal{in}\,\,G.\end{array}\end{cases}

By the classical duality argument, the null controllability of (
2.7
) is equivalent to show an appropriate observability inequality for the following adjoint backward-forward system

(2.8)

\begin{cases}d\phi+\Delta\phi\,dt=\left[-a_{1}\phi-a_{2}\Phi+\nabla\cdot(\phi B_{1}+\Phi B_{2})+\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\alpha_{i}\psi^{i}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{i,d}}\right]\,dt+\Phi\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in}\,\,Q,\\
d\psi^{i}-\Delta\psi^{i}\,dt=\left[a_{1}\psi^{i}+B_{1}\cdot\nabla\psi^{i}+\frac{1}{\beta_{i}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(x)\phi\right]\,dt+\left[a_{2}\psi^{i}+B_{2}\cdot\nabla\psi^{i}\right]\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in}\,\,Q,\\
\phi=0,\,\,\psi^{i}=0&\textnormal{on}\,\,\Sigma,\\
\phi(T)=\phi_{T}&\textnormal{in}\,\,G,\\
\psi^{i}(0)=0,\qquad i=1,2,&\textnormal{in}\,\,G.\end{cases}

3.
Carleman estimates for the coupled system (
2.8
)

In this section, we prove some Carleman estimates for the coupled system (
2.8
). Let us first define some weight functions based on the following result, which is proven in
[
12
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For any nonempty open subset
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\Subset G
, there exists a function
\eta\in C^{4}(\overline{G})
such that

\eta>0\;\,\,\textnormal{in}\,\,G\,;\qquad\eta=0\;\,\,\,\textnormal{on}\,\,\Gamma;\qquad|\nabla\eta|>0\;\,\,\,\,\textnormal{in}\,\,\overline{G\setminus\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}.

For large parameters
\lambda>1
and
\mu>1
, we choose the weight functions

\displaystyle\,\alpha=\alpha(t,x)=\frac{e^{\mu\eta(x)}-e^{2\mu|\eta|_{\infty}}}{t(T-t)},\qquad\varphi=\varphi(t,x)=\frac{e^{\mu\eta(x)}}{t(T-t)},\qquad\theta=e^{\lambda\alpha}.

It is easy to check that there exists a constant
C=C(G,T)>0
so that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,\varphi\geq C,\quad\qquad|\varphi_{t}|\leq C\varphi^{2},\quad\qquad|\varphi_{tt}|\leq C\varphi^{3},\\
&|\alpha_{t}|\leq Ce^{2\mu|\eta|_{\infty}}\varphi^{2},\quad\qquad|\alpha_{tt}|\leq Ce^{2\mu|\eta|_{\infty}}\varphi^{3}.\end{aligned}

Let us first introduce the following forward stochastic parabolic equation

(3.2)

\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dz-\Delta z\,dt=(F_{1}+\nabla\cdot F)\,dt+F_{2}\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in}\,\,Q,\\

and

. We now recall the following Carleman estimate for solutions of equation (
3.2
). The proof is given in
[
5
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exist a large
\mu_{1}>1
such that for all
\mu\geq\mu_{1}
, one can find constants

and

depending only on

,

,

and

\mu_{2}>1

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

be the solution of (

) and assume that (

) holds. Then, there exist a large
\mu_{3}>1
such that for

and
\lambda_{3}>1

,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\

Proof.

Let us choose the set

where

\displaystyle 0\leq\zeta\leq 1,\,\quad\zeta=1\,\,\text{in}\,\,\mathcal{O}^{\prime},\quad\,\text{supp}(\zeta)\subset\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{1},

(3.7b)

\displaystyle\quad\frac{\Delta\zeta}{\zeta^{1/2}}\in L^{\infty}(G),\quad\frac{\nabla\zeta}{\zeta^{1/2}}\in L^{\infty}(G;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Such function
\zeta
exists. In fact, by standard arguments, one can take
\zeta_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(G)
satisfying (
3.7a
) and then choose
\zeta=\zeta_{0}^{4}
, see, e.g.,
[
22
]
.
Firstly, by applying the Carleman estimate (
3.3
) for solutions of the system satisfied by
h
, we conclude that there exist a large
\mu_{1}>1
such that for
\mu\geq\mu_{1}
, one can find a constant

and a large enough

so that for all

, we obtain

\displaystyle\,\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi|\nabla h|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg[\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h^{2}\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\Big|\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}(x)+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{\beta_{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}(x)\Big|^{2}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt\Bigg],

which gives that

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi|\nabla h|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq C\Bigg[\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h^{2}\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt\Bigg].\end{aligned}

Secondly, using the Carleman estimate (
3.5
), we deduce that there exists
\mu_{2}>1
such that for
\mu\geq\mu_{2}
, one can find a constant
C>0
and a sufficiently large
\lambda_{2}>1
such that for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{2}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq C\Bigg[\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}h^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{2}\Phi^{2}\,dx\,dt\Bigg].\end{aligned}

Combining (

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&+\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi|\nabla h|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq C\Bigg[\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\qquad\;+\lambda^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{2}\Phi^{2}\,dx\,dt\Bigg].\end{aligned}

Following this, we shall provide an estimate for the second term on the right-hand side of (
3.10
). To this end, using Itô’s formula, we compute
d(\lambda^{3}\zeta\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h\phi)
, integrate the resulting identity over
Q
, and take the mathematical expectation on both sides. This leads us to

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0&=\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}d(\lambda^{3}\zeta\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h\phi)\,dx\\
&=\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\lambda^{3}\zeta\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\Bigg\{\phi\Big[\Delta h+a_{1}h+B_{1}\cdot\nabla h+\Big(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}(x)+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{\beta_{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}(x)\Big)\phi\Big]+a_{2}h\Phi\\
&\quad\quad\;+\Phi B_{2}\cdot\nabla h+h\big[-\Delta\phi-a_{1}\phi-a_{2}\Phi+\nabla\cdot(\phi B_{1}+\Phi B_{2})+h\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{d}}(x)\big]\Bigg\}\,dx\,dt\\

&=\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\lambda^{3}\zeta\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\Bigg\{\phi\Big[\Delta h+B_{1}\cdot\nabla h+\Big(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}(x)+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{\beta_{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}(x)\Big)\phi\Big]+\Phi B_{2}\cdot\nabla h\\

Therefore, it follows that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h^{2}\,dx\,dt&\leq\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{1}}\zeta\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&=-\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\zeta\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\Big(\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}(x)+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{\beta_{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}(x)\Big)\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\quad\;-\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\zeta(\theta^{2}\varphi^{3})_{t}h\phi\,dx\,dt-\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\zeta\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\phi B_{1}\cdot\nabla h\,dx\,dt\\
&\quad\;-\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\zeta\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\Phi B_{2}\cdot\nabla h\,dx\,dt-\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\zeta\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h\nabla\cdot(\phi B_{1}+\Phi B_{2})\,dx\,dt\\
&\quad\;-\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\zeta\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\phi\Delta h\,dx\,dt+\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\zeta\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h\Delta\phi\,dx\,dt.\end{aligned}

Integrating by parts the fifth term on the right-hand side of (
3.11
), we find that

(3.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{1}}\zeta\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h^{2}\,dx\,dt&=-\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\zeta\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\left[\frac{\alpha_{1}}{\beta_{1}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}(x)+\frac{\alpha_{2}}{\beta_{2}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}(x)\right]\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt\\

&\quad\;+\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\Phi hB_{2}\cdot\nabla(\zeta\theta^{2}\varphi^{3})\,dx\,dt-\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\zeta\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\phi\Delta h\,dx\,dt\\
&\quad\;+\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\zeta\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h\Delta\phi\,dx\,dt\\
&:=\sum_{i=1}^{6}\textbf{I}_{i}.\end{aligned}

In the rest of the proof, fixing
\varepsilon>0
, it is straightforward to see that

\displaystyle\textbf{I}_{1}\leq C\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q_{0}}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt.

Using (

(3.14)

For

Combining (

, we obtain that

(3.24)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&+\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi|\nabla h|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq C\Bigg[\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q_{0}}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda^{7}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q_{0}}\theta^{2}\varphi^{7}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\quad\;\;\quad+\lambda^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{2}\Phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda^{5}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{5}\Phi^{2}\,dx\,dt\Bigg].\end{aligned}

Finally, by choosing a sufficiently large
\lambda
in (
3.24
), we deduce the desired Carleman estimate (
3.6
). This concludes the proof of Lemma
3.4
.
∎

To incorporate the influence of source terms
y_{1,d}
and
y_{2,d}
in the optimality system (
2.7
), we show an improved Carleman inequality. For this purpose, we first introduce the following modified weight functions

(3.25)

\displaystyle\,\overline{\alpha}=\overline{\alpha}(t,x)=\frac{e^{\mu\eta(x)}-e^{2\mu|\eta|_{\infty}}}{\ell(t)},\qquad\overline{\varphi}=\overline{\varphi}(t,x)=\frac{e^{\mu\eta(x)}}{\ell(t)},\qquad\overline{\theta}=e^{\lambda\overline{\alpha}},

where

\ell(t)=\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}T^{2}/4&\textnormal{for}\;\;0\leq t\leq T/2,\\
t(T-t)&\textnormal{for}\;\;T/2\leq t\leq T,\end{array}\end{cases}

and the functions

(3.26)

\overline{\alpha}^{\star}(t)=\min_{x\in\overline{G}}\overline{\alpha}(t,x)\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\varphi}^{\star}(t)=\max_{x\in\overline{G}}\overline{\varphi}(t,x).

Let us now prove the following main improved Carleman estimate for the coupled system (
2.8
).

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
((\phi,\Phi),\psi^{1},\psi^{2})
be the solution of (
2.8
) and assume that (
1.5
) holds. Then, for
\mu=\mu_{3}
given in Lemma
3.4
, there exist constants
C>0
and
\lambda_{4}>1
depending only on
G
,
\mathcal{O}
,
\mu_{3}
and
T
such that for all
\lambda\geq\lambda_{4}

(3.27)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,\mathbb{E}|\phi(0)|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\overline{\theta}^{2}\overline{\varphi}^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\overline{\theta}^{2}\overline{\varphi}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h^{2}\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi|\nabla h|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq C\Bigg[\lambda^{7}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q_{0}}\theta^{2}\varphi^{7}\phi^{2}\,\,dx\,dt+\lambda^{5}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{5}\Phi^{2}\,dx\,dt\Bigg],\end{aligned}

where
h=\alpha_{1}\psi^{1}+\alpha_{2}\psi^{2}
.

Proof.

Let us consider the function
\kappa\in C^{1}([0,T])
such that

(3.28)

\kappa=1\quad\text{in}\,\;[0,T/2],\quad\ \,\,\kappa=0\quad\text{in}\,\,\;[3T/4,T]\quad\text{and}\,\,\;\;\kappa_{t}\leq C/T^{2}.

Set
(p,P)=\kappa(\phi,\Phi)
, then
(p,P)
is the solution of the system

(3.29)

\begin{cases}dp+\Delta p\,dt=\left[-a_{1}p-a_{2}P+\nabla\cdot(pB_{1}+PB_{2})+\kappa h\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{d}}(x)+\kappa_{t}\phi\right]\,dt+P\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in }Q,\\
p=0&\textnormal{on }\Sigma,\\
p(T)=0&\textnormal{in }G.\end{cases}

From (
3.28
) and (
3.29
), there exists a constant
C>0
so that

(3.30)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,\mathbb{E}|\phi(0)|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}+|\phi|^{2}_{L_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}\left(0,T/2;L^{2}(G)\right)}+|\nabla\phi|^{2}_{L_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}\left(0,T/2;L^{2}(G;\mathbb{R}^{N})\right)}\\
&\leq C\left[\frac{1}{T^{2}}|\phi|^{2}_{L_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(T/2,3T/4;L^{2}(G))}+|h|^{2}_{L_{\mathcal{F}}^{2}(0,3T/4;L^{2}(G))}\right].\end{aligned}

Since the functions
\overline{\theta}
and
\overline{\varphi}
(resp.,
\theta
and
\varphi
) are bounded in
(0,T/2)\times\overline{G}
(resp.,
(T/2,3T/4)\times\overline{G}
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,\mathbb{E}|\phi(0)|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T/2}\int_{G}\overline{\theta}^{2}\overline{\varphi}^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T/2}\int_{G}\overline{\theta}^{2}\overline{\varphi}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq C\left[\mathbb{E}\int_{T/2}^{3T/4}\int_{G}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\phi^{2}dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{3T/4}\int_{G}h^{2}\,dx\,dt\right].\end{aligned}

Then, it follows that

(3.31)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,\mathbb{E}|\phi(0)|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T/2}\int_{G}\overline{\theta}^{2}\overline{\varphi}^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T/2}\int_{G}\overline{\theta}^{2}\overline{\varphi}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq C\left[\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{3T/4}\int_{G}h^{2}\,dx\,dt\right].\end{aligned}

Note that
\theta=\overline{\theta}
and
\varphi=\overline{\varphi}
on
(T/2,T)
. Therefore, we have that

(3.32)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,\mathbb{E}\int_{T/2}^{T}\int_{G}\overline{\theta}^{2}\overline{\varphi}^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{T/2}^{T}\int_{G}\overline{\theta}^{2}\overline{\varphi}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq C\left[\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt\right].\end{aligned}

From (
3.31
) and (
3.32
), we get

(3.33)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,\mathbb{E}|\phi(0)|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\overline{\theta}^{2}\overline{\varphi}^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\overline{\theta}^{2}\overline{\varphi}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq C\left[\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{3T/4}\int_{G}h^{2}\,dx\,dt\right].\end{aligned}

Using the system satisfied by
h
, we can easily deduce that

(3.34)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{3T/4}\int_{G}h^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\left(\frac{\alpha_{1}^{2}}{\beta_{1}^{2}}+\frac{\alpha_{2}^{2}}{\beta_{2}^{2}}\right)\,\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\overline{\theta}^{2}\overline{\varphi}^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt.

Combining (
3.33
) and (
3.34
) and choosing a large enough
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
, we end up with

\displaystyle\,\mathbb{E}|\phi(0)|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\overline{\theta}^{2}\overline{\varphi}^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\overline{\theta}^{2}\overline{\varphi}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt\right].

It follows that

(3.35)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,\mathbb{E}|\phi(0)|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\overline{\theta}^{2}\overline{\varphi}^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\overline{\theta}^{2}\overline{\varphi}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h^{2}\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi|\nabla h|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\leq C\bigg[\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi|\nabla\phi|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\qquad\;+\lambda^{3}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi^{3}h^{2}\,dx\,dt+\lambda\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\theta^{2}\varphi|\nabla h|^{2}\,dx\,dt\bigg].\end{aligned}

Finally, this with (
3.6
) provide the desired Carleman estimate, and this completes the proof.
∎

4.
Null controllability result

This section is devoted to proving the main null controllability result given in Theorem
1.1
. To achieve this, we shall first establish the following observability inequality.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assuming that (
1.5
) holds and
\beta_{i}>0
,
i=1,2
, are sufficiently large, then there exist a constant
C>0
and a positive weight function
\rho=\rho(t)
blowing up at
t=T
, such that, for any
\phi_{T}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))
, the solution
((\phi,\Phi),\psi^{1},\psi^{2})
of (
2.8
) satisfies

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,\mathbb{E}|\phi(0)|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\rho^{-2}|\psi^{i}|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\left[\mathbb{E}\int_{Q_{0}}\phi^{2}\,\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\Phi^{2}\,dx\,dt\right].\end{aligned}

Proof.

For fixed
\mu=\mu_{3}
and
\lambda=\lambda_{4}
(with
\mu_{3}
and
\lambda_{4}
given in Theorem
3.1
), we choose the weight function
\rho=\rho(t)=e^{-\lambda\overline{\alpha}^{\star}(t)}
.
Notice that

(4.2)

d(\rho^{-2}(\psi^{i})^{2})=-2\rho_{t}\rho^{-3}(\psi^{i})^{2}dt+\rho^{-2}\left[2\psi^{i}d\psi^{i}+(d\psi^{i})^{2}\right],\qquad i=1,2.

From (
4.2
), we observe that the solution
((\phi,\Phi),\psi^{1},\psi^{2})
of (
2.8
) satisfies for any
t\in(0,T)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}d(\rho^{-2}(\psi^{i})^{2})\,dx&\leq-2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}\rho_{t}\rho^{-3}(\psi^{i})^{2}\,dx\,ds-2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}\rho^{-2}|\nabla\psi^{i}|^{2}\,dx\,ds\\
&\quad\,+2\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}\rho^{-2}\left[a_{1}(\psi^{i})^{2}+\psi^{i}B_{1}\cdot\nabla\psi^{i}+\frac{1}{\beta_{i}}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(x)\psi^{i}\phi\right]\,dx\,ds\\
&\quad\,+\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}\rho^{-2}(a_{2}\psi^{i}+B_{2}\cdot\nabla\psi^{i})^{2}\,dx\,ds,\qquad i=1,2.\end{aligned}

By Young’s inequality, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}d(\rho^{-2}(\psi^{i})^{2})\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg[\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{G}\rho^{-2}(\psi^{i})^{2}\,dx\,ds+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\rho^{-2}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt\Bigg],\qquad i=1,2.

Using that
\psi^{i}(0)=0
and the Gronwall inequality, we obtain

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\rho^{-2}|\psi^{i}|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\,\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\rho^{-2}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt,\qquad i=1,2.

From (
4.3
), (
3.25
) and (
3.26
), we get that

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\rho^{-2}|\psi^{i}|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\,\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\overline{\theta}^{2}\overline{\varphi}^{3}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt.

By Carleman estimate (
3.27
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|\phi(0)|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\rho^{-2}|\psi^{i}|^{2}\,dx\,dt\leq C\left[\mathbb{E}\int_{Q_{0}}\phi^{2}\,\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\Phi^{2}\,dx\,dt\right].

This concludes the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Let us now establish our null controllability result for the optimality system (
2.7
) and also deduce the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\rho=\rho(t)
be the weight function given in Proposition
4.1
. Then, for any target functions
y_{i,d}\in\mathcal{H}_{i,d}
,
i=1,2,
satisfying (
1.6
) and
y_{0}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{0}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))
, there exists controls

(\widehat{u}_{1},\widehat{u}_{2})\in\mathcal{U},

with minimal norm such that the corresponding solution of (
2.7
) satisfies that

\widehat{y}(T,\cdot)=0\;\;\textnormal{in}\;\;G,\quad\mathbb{P}\textnormal{-a.s.}

Furthermore, the controls
(\widehat{u}_{1},\widehat{u}_{2})
fulfill that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,|\widehat{u}_{1}|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathcal{O}))}+|\widehat{u}_{2}|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}\\
&\leq C\left[\mathbb{E}|y_{0}|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\alpha^{2}_{i}\mathbb{E}\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}_{i,d}}\rho^{2}y^{2}_{i,d}\,dx\,dt\right],\end{aligned}

where the positive constant
C
depending on
G
,
\mathcal{O}_{i}

\mathcal{O}_{i,d}
,
T
,
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
.

Proof.

Multiplying the solution
(y,\mathcal{Z}^{1},\mathcal{Z}^{2})
of (
2.7
) by the solution
((\phi,\Phi),\psi^{1},\psi^{2})
of (
2.8
), where
\mathcal{Z}^{i}=(z^{i},Z^{i})
,
i=1,2
, and then taking the expectation and integrating by parts, we obtain that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,\mathbb{E}\left\langle y(T),\phi_{T}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}-\mathbb{E}\left\langle y_{0},\phi(0)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}\\
&=\mathbb{E}\int_{Q_{0}}u_{1}\phi\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}u_{2}\Phi\,dx\,dt+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\alpha_{i}\mathbb{E}\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}_{i,d}}y_{i,d}\psi^{i}dx\,dt.\end{aligned}

Notice that the null controllability property is equivalent to finding a control
(u_{1},u_{2})
for each
y_{0}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}0}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))
, such that for any
\phi_{T}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))
, the following holds

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}\left\langle y_{0},\phi(0)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q_{0}}u_{1}\phi\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}u_{2}\Phi\,dx\,dt+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\alpha_{i}\mathbb{E}\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}_{i,d}}y_{i,d}\psi^{i}dx\,dt=0.\end{aligned}

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\phi_{T}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))
, and consider the following functional

\displaystyle J_{\varepsilon}(\phi_{T})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q_{0}}\phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\Phi^{2}\,dx\,dt+\varepsilon\mathbb{E}|\phi_{T}|_{L^{2}(G)}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left\langle y_{0},\phi(0)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\alpha_{i}\mathbb{E}\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}_{i,d}}y_{i,d}\psi^{i}\,dx\,dt.

It is not difficult to see that
J_{\varepsilon}:L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
is continuous and strictly convex. On the other hand, we have that for any
\delta>0

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left\langle y_{0},\phi(0)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{1}{2\delta}\mathbb{E}|y_{0}|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}-\frac{\delta}{2}\mathbb{E}|\phi(0)|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}.

By the observability inequality (
4.1
), we get that

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathbb{E}\left\langle y_{0},\phi(0)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}&\geq-\frac{\delta}{2}C\left[\mathbb{E}\int_{Q_{0}}\phi^{2}\,\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\Phi^{2}\,dx\,dt\right]\\
&\quad\,+\frac{\delta}{2}\,\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\rho^{-2}|\psi^{i}|^{2}\,dx\,dt-\frac{1}{2\delta}\mathbb{E}|y_{0}|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}.\end{aligned}

We also have that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \sum_{i=1}^{2}\alpha_{i}\mathbb{E}\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}_{i,d}}y_{i,d}\psi^{i}\,dx\,dt&\geq-\frac{1}{2\delta}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\alpha_{i}^{2}\mathbb{E}\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}_{i,d}}\rho^{2}|y_{i,d}|^{2}\,dx\,dt\\
&\quad\,-\frac{\delta}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\rho^{-2}|\psi^{i}|^{2}\,dx\,dt.\end{aligned}

By combining (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), and taking
\delta=\frac{1}{2C}
(where
C
is the same constant as in (
4.6
)), we obtain

\displaystyle J_{\varepsilon}(\phi_{T})\geq

\displaystyle\,\frac{1}{4}\left[\mathbb{E}\int_{Q_{0}}\phi^{2}\,\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\Phi^{2}\,dx\,dt\right]+\varepsilon\mathbb{E}|\phi_{T}|_{L^{2}(G)}

\displaystyle-C\left[\mathbb{E}|y_{0}|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\alpha^{2}_{i}\mathbb{E}\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}_{i,d}}\rho^{2}|y_{i,d}|^{2}dx\,dt\right].

Therefore,
J_{\varepsilon}
is coercive, and thus
J_{\varepsilon}
admits a unique minimum
\phi^{\varepsilon}_{T}
. If
\phi^{\varepsilon}_{T}\neq 0
, then we conclude that

\langle J_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\phi^{\varepsilon}_{T}),\phi_{T}\rangle_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))}=0\quad\,\,\textnormal{for all }\quad\phi_{T}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G)).

It follows that for all
\phi_{T}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))

(4.8)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,\mathbb{E}\int_{Q_{0}}\phi^{\varepsilon}\,\phi\,dx\,dt+\mathbb{E}\int_{Q}\Phi^{\varepsilon}\,\Phi\,dx\,dt+\varepsilon\frac{\mathbb{E}\left\langle\phi^{\varepsilon}_{T},\phi_{T}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}}{|\phi^{\varepsilon}_{T}|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))}}\\
&+\mathbb{E}\left\langle y_{0},\phi(0)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\alpha_{i}\mathbb{E}\iint_{{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}_{i,d}}}y_{i,d}\psi^{i}dx\,dt=0.\end{aligned}

Taking controls
(u^{\varepsilon}_{1},u^{\varepsilon}_{2})=(\phi_{\varepsilon},\Phi_{\varepsilon})
in (
4.5
), and combining the resulting equality with (
4.8
), we find that for all
\phi_{T}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))

\varepsilon\frac{\mathbb{E}\left\langle\phi^{\varepsilon}_{T},\phi_{T}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}}{|\phi^{\varepsilon}_{T}|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))}}+\mathbb{E}\left\langle y_{\varepsilon}(T),\phi_{T}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(G)}=0.

Hence, we deduce that

(4.9)

\left|y_{\varepsilon}(T)\right|_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G))}\leq\varepsilon.

Choosing
(\phi,\Phi)=(\phi_{\varepsilon},\Phi_{\varepsilon})
in (
4.8
) and applying the observability inequality (
4.1
) along with Young’s inequality, we deduce that

(4.10)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\,|u^{\varepsilon}_{1}|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathcal{O}))}+|u^{\varepsilon}_{2}|^{2}_{L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}}(0,T;L^{2}(G))}\\
&\leq C\left[\mathbb{E}|y_{0}|^{2}_{L^{2}(G)}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\alpha^{2}_{i}\mathbb{E}\iint_{(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}_{i,d}}\rho^{2}y^{2}_{i,d}\,dx\,dt\right].\end{aligned}

If
\phi^{\varepsilon}_{T}=0
, we get that

(4.11)

\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{J_{\varepsilon}(t\,\phi_{T})}{t}\geq 0\quad\,\,\textnormal{for all }\quad\phi_{T}\in L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G)).

Using (
4.11
) and setting
(u^{\varepsilon}_{1},u^{\varepsilon}_{2})=(0,0)
, we find that the inequalities (
4.9
) and (
4.10
) are satisfied.

According to (
4.10
), there exists a subsequence (also denoted by
(u^{\varepsilon}_{1},u^{\varepsilon}_{2})
) such that as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0

(4.12)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u^{\varepsilon}_{1}\longrightarrow\widehat{u}_{1}\quad\text{weakly in}\,\,\;L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega;L^{2}(\mathcal{O}));&\\
u^{\varepsilon}_{2}\longrightarrow\widehat{u}_{2}\quad\text{weakly in}\,\,\;L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega;L^{2}(G)).\end{aligned}

By (
4.12
), it is easy to see that

(4.13)

y_{\varepsilon}(T)\longrightarrow\widehat{y}(T)\quad\text{weakly in}\;\,\,L^{2}_{\mathcal{F}_{T}}(\Omega;L^{2}(G)),\quad\textnormal{as}\;\varepsilon\rightarrow 0,

where
\widehat{y}
is the solution of (
2.7
) associated to the controls
\widehat{u}_{1}
and
\widehat{u}_{2}
. Combining (
4.9
) and (
4.13
), we finally conclude that

\widehat{y}(T,\cdot)=0\;\;\textnormal{in}\;\;G,\quad\mathbb{P}\textnormal{-a.s.}

From (
4.10
) and (
4.12
), we easily obtain the desired estimate (
4.4
). This completes the proof of Proposition
4.2
and establishes our null controllability result as stated in Theorem
1.1
.
∎

5.
Additional comments and some open problems

This paper is devoted to the application of Stackelberg and Nash strategies to stochastic heat equations with Dirichlet boundary conditions in a bounded domain. The problem is solved in two steps: first, we characterize the Nash equilibrium by means of a backward equation. Then, the whole problem is reduced to the question of null controllability for a forward-backward coupled stochastic system, which is solved using the Carleman estimation approach.

Now, we discuss some open problems:

•

As it is mentioned in
[
19
]
, the introduction of two leaders represents a technical constraint. This is a classical issue when dealing with the controllability of forward stochastic parabolic equations. See
[
5
,
24
]
for more details. Therefore, it is challenging to extend the result of the present paper to a system without using the extra control
u_{2}
.

In contrast to forward equations, we can handle the backward ones with only one leader
u
and two followers
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
. More precisely, using the same ideas as presented in this paper, we can treat Stackelberg-Nash null controllability of the following system

{\quad\quad\qquad\quad\begin{cases}\begin{array}[]{ll}dy+\Delta y\,dt=\left[a_{1}y+B\cdot\nabla y+a_{2}Y+u\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}}+v_{1}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}+v_{2}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}\right]\,dt+Y\,dW(t)&\textnormal{in}\,\,Q,\\
y=0&\textnormal{on}\,\,\Sigma,\\
y(T)=y_{T}&\textnormal{in}\,\,G.\end{array}\end{cases}}

•

It would be interesting to study Stackelberg-Nash controllability for some coupled stochastic parabolic equations. For this, we consider the following coupled backward stochastic parabolic system with one leader
u
and two followers
v_{1}
and
v_{2}

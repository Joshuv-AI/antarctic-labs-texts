Chong Gao, Taolue Chen, Zhilin Wu

Abstract
We identify difference-bound set constraints (DBS), an analogy of difference-bound arithmetic constraints for sets. DBS can express not only set constraints but also arithmetic constraints over set elements. We integrate DBS into separation logic with linearly compositional inductive predicates, obtaining a logic thereof where set data constraints of linear data structures can be specified. We show that the satisfiability of this logic is decidable. A crucial step of the decision procedure is to compute the transitive closure of DBS-definable set relations, to capture which we propose an extension of quantified set constraints with Presburger Arithmetic (RQSPA). The satisfiability of RQSPA is then shown to be decidable by harnessing advanced automata-theoretic techniques.

Introduction

Separation Logic (SL) is a well-established approach for deductive verification of programs that manipulate dynamic data structures
[
25
,
28
]
. Typically, SL is used in combination with inductive definitions (SLID), which provides a natural and convenient means to specify dynamic data structures.
To reason about the property (e.g. sortedness) of data values stored in data structures, it is also necessary to incorporate data constraints into the inductive definitions.

One of the most fundamental questions for a logical theory is whether its satisfiability is decidable. SLID with data constraints is no exception. This problem becomes more challenging than one would probably expect, partially due to the inherent intricacy brought up by inductive definitions and data constraints. It is somewhat surprising that only disproportional research has addressed this question (cf.
Related work
).
In practice, most available tools based on SLID only support heuristics without giving completeness guarantees, especially when data constraints are involved.
Complete
decision procedures for satisfiability, however, have been found important in software engineering tasks such as symbolic execution, specification debugging, counterexample generation, etc., let along the theoretical insights they usually shed on the logic system.

The dearth of complete decision procedures for SLID with data constraints has prompted us to launch a research program as of 2015, aiming to identify decidable
and
sufficiently expressive instances. We have made encouraging progress insofar. In
[
15
]
, we set up a general framework, but could only tackle linear data structures with data constraints in difference-bound arithmetic. In
[
34
]
, we were able to tackle tree data structures by exploiting machineries such as order graphs and counter machines, though the data constraints therein remained to be in difference-bound arithmetic.

An important class of data constraints that is currently elusive in our investigations is set constraints. They are mandatory for reasoning about, e.g., invariants of data collections stored in data structures. For instance, when specifying the correctness of a sorting algorithm on input lists, whilst the sortedness of the list can be described by difference-bound arithmetic constraints, the property that the sorting algorithm does not change the set of data values on the list requires inductive definitions with
set
data constraints. Indeed, reviewers of the papers
[
15
,
34
]
constantly raised the challenge of set constraints, which compelled us to write the current paper.

Satisfiability of
{\sf SLID^{S}_{LC}}[P]

The satisfiability problem is to decide whether there is a state (an assignment-heap pair) satisfying
\phi
for a given
{\sf SLID^{S}_{LC}}[P]
formula
\phi
. We shall follow the approach adopted in
[
12
,
15
]
, i.e., to construct
{\sf Abs}(\phi)
, an abstraction of
\phi
that is equisatisfiable to
\phi
. The key ingredient of the construction is to compute the transitive closure of the data constraints extracted from the inductive rule of
P
.

Let
\phi=\Pi\wedge\Delta\wedge\Sigma
be an
{\sf SLID^{S}_{LC}}[P]
formula. Suppose
\Sigma=a_{1}\ast\cdots\ast a_{n}
, where each
a_{i}
is either a points-to atom or a predicate atom. For predicate atom
a_{i}=P(Z_{1},\vec{\mu};Z_{2},\vec{\nu};\vec{\chi})
we assume that the inductive rule for
P
is

R_{1}:P(E,\vec{\alpha};F,\vec{\beta};\vec{\xi})::=\exists\vec{X}\exists\vec{S}.\ \varphi\wedge E\mapsto(\rho)\ast P(Y,\vec{\gamma};F,\vec{\beta};\vec{\xi}).\hskip 0.0pt(*)

We extract the data constraint
\varphi_{P}({\sf dt}(\vec{\alpha}),{\sf dt}(\vec{\beta}))
out of
R_{1}
. Formally, we define
\varphi_{P}({\sf dt}(\vec{\alpha}),{\sf dt}(\vec{\beta}))
as
\varphi[{\sf dt}(\vec{\beta})/{\sf dt}(\vec{\gamma})]
, where
{\sf dt}(\vec{\alpha})
(resp.
{\sf dt}(\vec{\gamma})
,
{\sf dt}(\vec{\beta})
) is the projection of
\vec{\alpha}
(resp.
\vec{\gamma}
,
\vec{\beta}
) to data variables. For instance,
\varphi_{\mathit{ldllseg}}(S,S^{\prime}):=\left(S=S^{\prime\prime}\cup\{\max(S)\}\wedge\max(S^{\prime\prime})=\max(S)-1\right)[S^{\prime}/S^{\prime\prime}]=S=S^{\prime}\cup\{\max(S)\}\wedge\max(S^{\prime})=\max(S)-1
.

We can construct
{\sf Abs}(\phi)
with necessary adaptations from
[
15
]
. For each spatial atom
a_{i}
,
{\sf Abs}(\phi)
introduces a Boolean variable to denote whether
a_{i}
corresponds to a nonempty heap or not. With these Boolean variables, the semantics of separating conjunction are encoded in
{\sf Abs}(\phi)
. Moreover, for each predicate atom
a_{i}
,
{\sf Abs}(\phi)
contains an abstraction of
a_{i}
, where the formulae
{\sf Ufld}_{1}(a_{i})
and
{\sf Ufld}_{\geq 2}(a_{i})
are used. Intuitively,
{\sf Ufld}_{1}(a_{i})
and
{\sf Ufld}_{\geq 2}(a_{i})
correspond to the separation logic formulae obtained by unfolding the rule
R_{1}

once
and
at least twice
respectively. We include the construction here so one can see the role of the transitive closure in
{\sf Abs}(\phi)
.
The details of
{\sf Abs}(\phi)
can be found in Appendix
0.B
.

Let
a_{i}=P(Z_{1},\vec{\mu};Z_{2},\vec{\nu};\vec{\chi})
and
R_{1}
be the inductive rule in Eqn. (
*
). If
E
occurs in
\vec{\gamma}
in the body of
R_{1}
, we use
{\sf idx}_{(P,\vec{\gamma},E)}
to denote the unique index
j
such that
\gamma_{j}=E
. (The uniqueness follows from
C4
.)

Definition 3
(
{\sf Ufld}_{1}(a_{i})
and
{\sf Ufld}_{\geq 2}(a_{i})
)

{\sf Ufld}_{1}(a_{i})
and
{\sf Ufld}_{\geq 2}(a_{i})
are defined by distinguishing the following two cases:

•

If
E
occurs in
\vec{\gamma}
in the body of
R_{1}
, then

{\sf Ufld}_{1}(a_{i}):=(E=\beta_{{\sf idx}_{(P,\vec{\gamma},E)}}\wedge\varphi_{P}({\sf dt}(\vec{\alpha}),{\sf dt}(\vec{\beta})))[Z_{1}/E,\vec{\mu}/\vec{\alpha},Z_{2}/F,\vec{\nu}/\vec{\beta},\vec{\chi}/\vec{\xi}]

and
{\sf Ufld}_{\geq 2}(a_{i}):=

\begin{array}[]{l c l }\left(\begin{array}[]{l}E\neq\beta_{{\sf idx}_{(P,\vec{\gamma},E)}}\wedge E\neq\gamma_{2,{\sf idx}_{(P,\vec{\gamma},E)}}\ \wedge\\
\varphi_{P}[{\sf dt}(\vec{\gamma_{1}})/{\sf dt}(\vec{\beta})]\wedge\varphi_{P}[{\sf dt}(\vec{\gamma_{1}})/{\sf dt}(\vec{\alpha}),{\sf dt}(\vec{\gamma_{2}})/{\sf dt}(\vec{\beta})]\ \wedge\\
(\textsf{TC}[\varphi_{P}])[{\sf dt}(\vec{\gamma_{2}})/{\sf dt}(\vec{\alpha})]\end{array}\!\!\!\right)[Z_{1}/E,\vec{\mu}/\vec{\alpha},Z_{2}/F,\vec{\nu}/\vec{\beta},\vec{\chi}/\vec{\xi}],\end{array}

where
\vec{\gamma_{1}}
and
\vec{\gamma_{2}}
are fresh variables.

•

Otherwise, let

{\sf Ufld}_{1}(a_{i}):=\varphi_{P}[Z_{1}/E,\vec{\mu}/\vec{\alpha},Z_{2}/F,\vec{\nu}/\vec{\beta},\vec{\chi}/\vec{\xi}]

and

{\sf Ufld}_{\geq 2}(a_{i}):=\begin{array}[]{l c l }\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\varphi_{P}[{\sf dt}(\vec{\gamma_{1}})/{\sf dt}(\vec{\beta})]\ \wedge\\
\varphi_{P}[{\sf dt}(\vec{\gamma_{1}})/{\sf dt}(\vec{\alpha}),{\sf dt}(\vec{\gamma_{2}})/{\sf dt}(\vec{\beta})]\ \wedge\\
(\textsf{TC}[\varphi_{P}])[{\sf dt}(\vec{\gamma_{2}})/{\sf dt}(\vec{\alpha})]\end{array}\!\!\!\right)[Z_{1}/E,\vec{\mu}/\vec{\alpha},Z_{2}/F,\vec{\nu}/\vec{\beta},\vec{\chi}/\vec{\xi}],\end{array}

where
\vec{\gamma_{1}}
and
\vec{\gamma_{2}}
are fresh variables.

Here,
\textsf{TC}[\varphi_{P}]({\sf dt}(\vec{\alpha}),{\sf dt}(\vec{\beta}))
denotes the transitive closure of
\varphi_{P}
. In Section
5
, it will be shown that
\textsf{TC}[\varphi_{P}]({\sf dt}(\vec{\alpha}),{\sf dt}(\vec{\beta}))
can be written as an
\mathcal{RQSPA}
formula. As a result, since we are only concerned with satisfiability and can treat the location data type
{\mathbb{L}}
simply as integers
\mathbb{Z}
,
{\sf Abs}(\phi)
can also be read as an
\mathcal{RQSPA}
formula. In Section
6
, we shall show that the satisfiability of
\mathcal{RQSPA}
is decidable. Following this chain of reasoning, we conclude that
the satisfiability of
{\sf SLID^{S}_{LC}}[P]
formulae is decidable
.

5
Transitive closure of difference-bound set relations

In this section, we show how to compute the transitive closure of the difference-bound set relation
R
given by a
\mathcal{DBS}
formula
\varphi_{R}(\vec{S},\vec{S}^{\prime})
. Our approach is, in a nutshell, to encode
TC[\varphi_{R}](\vec{S},\vec{S^{\prime}})
into
\mathcal{RQSPA}
.
We shall only sketch part of a simple case, i.e., in
\varphi_{R}(S,S^{\prime})
only one source and destination set parameter are present. The details are however given in Appendix
0.C.3
.

Recall that, owing to the simplicity of
\mathcal{DBS}
, the integer terms
T_{i}
in
\varphi_{R}(S,S^{\prime})
can only be
\min(S)
,
\max(S)
,
\min(S^{\prime})
or
\max(S^{\prime})
, whereas the set terms
T_{s}
are
\emptyset
,
\{\min(S)\}
,
\{\min(S^{\prime})\}
,
\{\max(S)\}
,
\{\max(S^{\prime})\}
, or their union. For reference, we write
\varphi_{R}(S,S^{\prime})=\varphi_{R,1}\wedge\varphi_{R,2}
, where
\varphi_{R,1}
is an equality of set terms (i.e., they are of the form
S=S^{\prime}\cup T_{s}
or
S^{\prime}=S\cup T_{s}
), and
\varphi_{R,2}
is a conjunction of constraints over integer terms (i.e., a conjunction of formulae
T_{i}\ \leq\ T_{i}+c
).
\varphi_{R,1}
and
\varphi_{R,2}
will be referred to as the
set
and
integer subformula
of
\varphi_{R}(S,S^{\prime})
respectively.
We shall focus on the case
\varphi_{R,1}:=S=S^{\prime}\cup T_{s}
. The symmetrical case
\varphi_{R,1}:=S^{\prime}=S\cup T_{s}
can be adapted easily.

The integer subformula
\varphi_{R,2}
can be represented by an
edge-weighted directed graph

\mathcal{G}(\varphi_{R,2})
, where the vertices are all integer terms appearing in
\varphi_{R,2}
, and there is an edge from
T_{1}
to
T_{2}
with weight
c
iff
T_{1}=T_{2}+c
(equivalent to
T_{2}=T_{1}-c
), or
T_{1}\leq T_{2}+c
, or
T_{2}+c\geq T_{1}
appears in
\varphi_{R,2}
.
The weight of a path in
\mathcal{G}(\varphi_{R,2})
is the sum of the weights of the edges along the path. A
negative cycle
in
\mathcal{G}(\varphi_{R,2})
is a cycle with negative weight. It is known that
\varphi_{R,2}
is satisfiable iff
\mathcal{G}(\varphi_{R,2})
contains no negative cycles
[
24
]
.
Suppose
\varphi_{R,2}
is satisfiable. We define the
normal form
of
\varphi_{R,2}
, denoted by
\mathsf{Norm}(\varphi_{R,2})
, as the conjunction of the formulae
T_{1}\leq T_{2}+c
such that
T_{1}\neq T_{2}
,
T_{2}
is reachable from
T_{1}
in
\mathcal{G}(\varphi_{R,2})
, and
c
is
path from
T_{1}
to
T_{2}
with the minimal weight in
\mathcal{G}(\varphi_{R,2})
.

S
(resp.
S^{\prime}
) is said to be
surely nonempty
in
\varphi_{R}
if
\min(S)
or
\max(S)
(resp.
\min(S^{\prime})
or
\max(S^{\prime})
) occurs in
\varphi_{R}
; otherwise,
S
(resp.
S^{\prime}
) is
possibly empty
in
\varphi_{R}
. Recall that, according to the semantics, an occurrence of
\min(S)
or
\max(S)
(resp.
\min(S^{\prime})
or
\max(S^{\prime})
) in
\varphi_{R}
implies that
S
(resp.
S^{\prime}
) is interpreted as a nonempty set in every satisfiable assignment. Provided that
S^{\prime}
is nonempty, we know that
\min(S^{\prime})
and
\max(S^{\prime})
belong to
S^{\prime}
. Therefore, for simplicity, here we assume that in
S=S^{\prime}\cup T_{s}
,
T_{s}
contains neither
\min(S^{\prime})
nor
\max(S^{\prime})
.
The situation that
T_{s}
contains
\min(S^{\prime})
and
\max(S^{\prime})
can be dealt with in a similar way.

Saturation.
For technical convenience, we introduce a concept of saturation. The main purpose of saturation is to regularise
T_{s}
and
\varphi_{R,2}
, which would make the transitive closure construction more “syntactic”.

Definition 4

Let
\varphi_{R}(S,S^{\prime}):=S=S^{\prime}\cup T_{s}\wedge\varphi_{R,2}
be a
\mathcal{DBS}
formula. Then
\varphi_{R}(S,S^{\prime})
is saturated if
\varphi_{R}(S,S^{\prime})
satisfies the following conditions

•

\varphi_{R,2}
is satisfiable and in normal forms,

•

T_{s}\subseteq\{\max(S),\min(S)\}
,

•

if
S
(resp.
S^{\prime}
) is surely nonempty in
\varphi_{R}
, then
\varphi_{R,2}
contains a conjunct
\min(S)\leq\max(S)-c
for some
c\geq 0
(resp.
\min(S^{\prime})\leq\max(S^{\prime})-c^{\prime}
for some
c^{\prime}\geq 0
),

•

if both
S
and
S^{\prime}
are surely nonempty in
\varphi_{R}
, then

–

\varphi_{R,2}
contains two conjuncts
\min(S)\leq\min(S^{\prime})-c
and
\max(S^{\prime})\leq\max(S)-c^{\prime}
for some
c,c^{\prime}\geq 0
,

–

\min(S)\not\in T_{s}
iff
\varphi_{R,2}
contains the conjuncts
\min(S)\leq\min(S^{\prime})
and
\min(S^{\prime})\leq\min(S)
,

–

\max(S)\not\in T_{s}
iff
\varphi_{R,2}
contains the conjuncts
\max(S^{\prime})\leq\max(S)
and
\max(S)\leq\max(S^{\prime})
,

•

if
\varphi_{R,2}
contains the conjuncts
\min(S)\leq\max(S)
and
\max(S)\leq\min(S)
, then
\max(S)\not\in T_{s}
(possibly
\min(S)\in T_{s}
).

For a formula
\varphi_{R}(S,S^{\prime}):=S=S^{\prime}\cup T_{s}\wedge\varphi_{R,2}
, one can easily saturate
\varphi_{R}
, yielding a saturated formula
\mathsf{Strt}(\varphi_{R}(S,S^{\prime}))
. (It is possible, however, to arrive at an unsatisfiable formula, then we are done.)

Proposition 1

Let
\varphi_{R}(S,S^{\prime}):=\varphi_{R,1}\wedge\varphi_{R,2}
be a
\mathcal{DBS}
formula such that
\varphi_{R,1}:=S=S^{\prime}\cup T_{s}
and
\varphi_{R,2}
is satisfiable. Then
\varphi_{R}
can be transformed, in polynomial time, to an equisatisfiable formula
\mathsf{Strt}(\varphi_{R}(S,S^{\prime}))
, and if the integer subformula of
\mathsf{Strt}(\varphi_{R}(S,S^{\prime}))
is satisfiable, then
\mathsf{Strt}(\varphi_{R}(S,S^{\prime}))
is saturated.

In the sequel, we assume that
\varphi_{R}(S,S^{\prime}):=\varphi_{R,1}\wedge\varphi_{R,2}
is satisfiable and
saturated
. For notational convenience, for
A\subseteq\{\min(S),\max(S),\min(S^{\prime}),\max(S^{\prime})\}
with

|A|=2
, let
\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{A}
denote the conjunction of atomic formulae in
\varphi_{R,2}
where
all
the elements of
A
occur.

Evidently,
\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{A}
gives a partition of atomic formulae of
\varphi_{R,2}
. Namely,

\varphi_{R,2}=\bigwedge_{A\subseteq\{\min(S),\max(S),\min(S^{\prime}),\max(S^{\prime})\},|A|=2}\ \lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{A}
.

We proceed by a case-by-case analysis of
\varphi_{R,1}
. There are four cases: (I)
\varphi_{R,1}:=S=S^{\prime}
, (II)
\varphi_{R,1}:=S=S^{\prime}\cup\{\min(S)\}
, (III)
\varphi_{R,1}=S=S^{\prime}\cup\{\max(S)\}
and
(IV)
\varphi_{R,1}=S=S^{\prime}\cup\{\min(S),\max(S)\}
. Case (I) is trivial, and Case (III) is symmetrical to (II). However, both (II) and (IV) are technically involved.
We shall only give a “sample” treatment of these cases, i.e., part of arguments for Case (II); the full account of Case (II) and (IV) are given in Appendix
0.C.3
.

To start with, Case (II) can be illustrated schematically as
\underbrace{|-\overbrace{|------|}^{S^{\prime}}}_{S}
.
We observe that
S
is surely nonempty in
\varphi_{R}
.
We then distinguish two subcases depending on whether
S^{\prime}
is possibly empty or surely nonempty in
\varphi_{R}
. Here we give the details of the latter subcase because it is more interesting. In this case, both
S
and
S^{\prime}
are surely nonempty in
\varphi_{R}
. By Definition
4
(4–5),

\varphi_{R,2}
contains a conjunct
\min(S)\leq\min(S^{\prime})-c
for some
c\geq 0
, as well as
\max(S^{\prime})\leq\max(S)
and
\max(S)\leq\max(S^{\prime})
(i.e.,
\max(S^{\prime})=\max(S)
).
Therefore, we can assume

\begin{array}[]{l c l}\varphi_{R,2}&=&\max(S^{\prime})\leq\max(S)\wedge\max(S)\leq\max(S^{\prime})\wedge\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\min(S^{\prime})}\ \wedge\\
&&\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\max(S)}\wedge\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S^{\prime}),\max(S^{\prime})}.\end{array}

Note that in
\varphi_{R,2}
above, the redundant subformulae

\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\max(S^{\prime})}
and
\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S^{\prime}),\max(S)}
have been omitted.

The formula
\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\min(S^{\prime})}
is said to be
strict
if it contains a conjunct
\min(S)\leq\min(S^{\prime})-c
for some
c>0
. Otherwise, it is said to be
non-strict
. Intuitively, if
\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\min(S^{\prime})}
is strict, then for
n,n^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}
, the validity of
(\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\min(S^{\prime})})[n/\min(S),n^{\prime}/\min(S^{\prime})]
implies that
n<n^{\prime}
.
For the sketch we only present
the case that
\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\min(S^{\prime})}
is strict
; the other cases are similar and can be found in Appendix
0.C.3
.

Evidently,
\textsf{TC}[\varphi_{R}](S,S^{\prime})
can be written as
(S=S^{\prime})\vee\bigvee\limits_{n\geq 1}\varphi^{(n)}_{R}
,
where
\varphi^{(n)}_{R}
is obtained by unfolding
\varphi_{R}
for
n
times, that is,

\varphi^{(n)}_{R}=\exists S_{1},\cdots,S_{n+1}.\left(\begin{array}[]{l}S_{1}=S\wedge S_{n+1}=S^{\prime}\ \wedge\\
\bigwedge\limits_{i\in[n]}(S_{i}=S_{i+1}\cup\{\min(S_{i})\}\wedge\varphi_{R,2}[S_{i}/S,S_{i+1}/S^{\prime}])\end{array}\right),

where
\varphi_{R,2}[S_{i}/S,S_{i+1}/S^{\prime}]
is obtained from
\varphi_{R,2}
by replacing
S
(resp.
S^{\prime}
) with
S_{i}
(resp.
S_{i+1}
).

Clearly,
\varphi^{(1)}_{R}=\varphi_{R}
, and

\varphi^{(2)}_{R}=\exists S_{2}.\ (S=S_{2}\cup\{\min(S)\}\wedge S_{2}=S^{\prime}\cup\{\min(S_{2})\}\wedge\varphi_{R,2}[S_{2}/S^{\prime}]\wedge\varphi_{R,2}[S_{2}/S]).

For
\varphi^{(n)}_{R}
where
n\geq 3
, we first simplify
\varphi^{(n)}_{R}
to construct a finite formula for
\textsf{TC}[\varphi_{R}](S,S^{\prime})
. The subformula
\bigwedge\limits_{i\in[n]}(S_{i}=S_{i+1}\cup\{\min(S_{i})\}\wedge\varphi_{R,2}[S_{i}/S,S_{i+1}/S^{\prime}])
can be rewritten as

\bigwedge\limits_{i\in[n]}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}S_{i}=S_{i+1}\cup\{\min(S_{i})\}\wedge\max(S_{i})=\max(S_{i+1})\ \wedge\\
(\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\min(S^{\prime})}[S_{i}/S,S_{i+1}/S^{\prime}])\wedge(\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\max(S)}[S_{i}/S])\ \wedge\\
(\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S^{\prime}),\max(S^{\prime})}[S_{i+1}/S^{\prime}])\end{array}\right).

Because
S_{i}=S_{i+1}\cup\{\min(S_{i})\}
for each
i\in[n]
, we have
\max(S_{1})=\cdots=\max(S_{n})
and
\min(S_{1})\leq\cdots\leq\min(S_{n})
. Since
\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\max(S)}
is a conjunction of difference-bound constraints involving
\min(S)
and
\max(S)
only, we have
\bigwedge\limits_{i\in[n]}\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\max(S)}[S_{i}/S]
is equivalent to
\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\max(S)}[S_{1}/S]\wedge\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\max(S)}[S_{n}/S]
. To see this, assume, for instance,

\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\max(S)}\equiv c\leq\max(S)-\min(S)\leq c^{\prime}

for some constants
c,c^{\prime}\geq 0
with
c\leq c^{\prime}
. Then
\max(S_{1})-\min(S_{1})\leq c^{\prime}
implies
\max(S_{i})-\min(S_{i})\leq c^{\prime}
for each
i\in[n]
, and
c\leq\max(S_{n})-\min(S_{n})
implies
c\leq\max(S_{i})-\min(S_{i})
for each
i\in[n]
. Therefore,
\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\max(S)}[S_{1}/S]\wedge\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\max(S)}[S_{n}/S]\equiv c\leq\max(S_{1})-\min(S_{1})\leq c^{\prime}\wedge c\leq\max(S_{n})-\min(S_{n})\leq c^{\prime}
implies that

\bigwedge\limits_{i\in[n]}\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\max(S)}[S_{i}/S]
, thus they are equivalent. (The other direction is trivial.) Likewise, one has
\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S^{\prime}),\max(S^{\prime})}[S_{2}/S^{\prime}]\wedge\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S^{\prime}),\max(S^{\prime})}[S_{n+1}/S^{\prime}]
implies
\bigwedge\limits_{i\in[n]}\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S^{\prime}),\max(S^{\prime})}[S_{i+1}/S^{\prime}]
, thus they are equivalent.
Therefore,
\varphi^{(n)}_{R}
can be transformed into

\exists S_{2},S_{n}.\left(\begin{array}[]{l}\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\max(S)}\wedge(\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\max(S)}[S_{n}/S])\ \wedge\\
(\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S^{\prime}),\max(S^{\prime})}[S_{2}/S^{\prime}])\wedge\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S^{\prime}),\max(S^{\prime})}\wedge S=S_{2}\cup\{\min(S)\}\ \wedge\\
S_{n}=S^{\prime}\cup\{\min(S_{n})\}\wedge\max(S)=\max(S_{2})\wedge\max(S_{n})=\max(S^{\prime})\ \wedge\\
(\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\min(S^{\prime})}[S_{2}/S^{\prime}])\wedge(\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\min(S^{\prime})}[S_{n}/S])\wedge\\
\exists S_{3},\cdots,S_{n-1}.\bigwedge\limits_{2\leq i\leq n-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}S_{i}=S_{i+1}\cup\{\min(S_{i})\}\wedge\max(S_{i})=\max(S_{i+1})\ \wedge\\
(\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\min(S^{\prime})}[S_{i}/S,S_{i+1}/S^{\prime}])\end{array}\right)\end{array}\right).

Claim
.

Suppose
n\geq 3
and
\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\min(S^{\prime})}
is strict. Then

\exists S_{3},\cdots,S_{n-1}.\bigwedge\limits_{2\leq i\leq n-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{l}S_{i}=S_{i+1}\cup\{\min(S_{i})\}\wedge\max(S_{i})=\max(S_{i+1})\ \wedge\\
(\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\min(S^{\prime})}[S_{i}/S,S_{i+1}/S^{\prime}])\end{array}\right)

is equivalent to

\small\begin{array}[]{l}S_{n}\neq\emptyset\wedge S_{2}\setminus S_{n}\neq\emptyset\wedge S_{n}\subseteq S_{2}\wedge|S_{2}\setminus S_{n}|=n-2\wedge\max(S_{2}\setminus S_{n})<\min(S_{n})\ \wedge\\
\forall y,z.\ \textsf{succ}((S_{2}\setminus S_{n})\cup\{\min(S_{n})\},y,z)\rightarrow(\lfloor\varphi_{R,2}\rfloor_{\min(S),\min(S^{\prime})}[y/\min(S),z/\min(S^{\prime})]),\end{array}

where
\textsf{succ}(S,x,y)
specifies intuitively that
y
is the successor of
x
in
S
, that is,

\textsf{succ}(S,x,y)=x\in S\wedge y\in S\wedge x<y\wedge\forall z\in S.\ (z\leq x\vee y\leq z).

Note that
|\cdot|
denotes the set cardinality which can be easily encoded into
\mathcal{RQSPA}
. (Appendix
0.C.3
gives the proof of the claim.)
It follows that
\textsf{TC}[\varphi_{R}](S,S^{\prime})=

\small\begin{array}[]{l}(S=S^{\prime})\vee\varphi_{R}(S,S^{\prime})\vee\varphi^{(2)}_{R}(S,S^{\prime})\ \vee\\

6
Satisfiability of
\mathcal{RQSPA}

In this section, we focus on the second ingredient of the procedure for deciding satisfiability of
{\sf SLID^{S}_{LC}}[P]
,
i.e., the satisfiability of
\mathcal{RQSPA}
. We first note that
\mathcal{RQSPA}
is defined over
\mathbb{Z}
. To show the decidability, it turns to be much easier to work on
\mathbb{N}
. We shall write
\mathcal{RQSPA}_{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\mathcal{RQSPA}_{\mathbb{N}}
to differentiate them when necessary. Moreover, for technical reasons, we also introduce
\mathcal{RQSPA}^{-}
, the fragment of
\mathcal{RQSPA}
excluding formulae of the form
T_{m}\bowtie 0
.

The decision procedure for the satisfiability of
\mathcal{RQSPA}
proceeds with the following three steps:

Step I.

Translate
\mathcal{RQSPA}_{\mathbb{Z}}
to
\mathcal{RQSPA}_{\mathbb{N}}
,

Step II.

Normalize an
\mathcal{RQSPA}_{\mathbb{N}}
formula
\Phi(\vec{x},\vec{S})
into
\bigvee\limits_{i}(\Phi^{(i)}_{\mathrm{core}}\wedge\Phi^{(i)}_{\mathrm{count}})
, where
\Phi^{(i)}_{\mathrm{core}}
is an
\mathcal{RQSPA}^{-}_{\mathbb{N}}
formula, and
\Phi^{(i)}_{\mathrm{count}}
is a conjunction of formulae of the form
T_{m}\bowtie 0
which contain only variables from
\vec{x}\cup\vec{S}
,

Step III.

For each disjunct
\Phi^{(i)}_{\mathrm{core}}\wedge\Phi^{(i)}_{\mathrm{count}}
, construct a Presburger automaton (PA)
\mathcal{A}^{(i)}_{\Phi}
which captures the models of
\Phi^{(i)}_{\mathrm{core}}\wedge\Phi^{(i)}_{\mathrm{count}}
. Satisfiability is thus reducible to the nonemptiness of PA, which is decidable
[
29
]
.

These steps are technically involved. In particular, the third step requires exploiting Presburger automata
[
29
]
. The details can be found in Appendix
0.D
.

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we have defined
{\sf SLID^{S}_{LC}}
, SL with linearly compositional inductive predicates and set data constraints. The main feature is to identify
\mathcal{DBS}
as a special class of set data constraints in the inductive definitions. We encoded the transitive closure of
\mathcal{DBS}
into
\mathcal{RQSPA}
, which was shown to be decidable. These together yield a complete decision procedure for the satisfiability of
{\sf SLID^{S}_{LC}}
.

The precise complexity of the decision procedure—
Nonelementary
is the best upper-bound we have now—is left open for further studies. Furthermore, the entailment problem of
{\sf SLID^{S}_{LC}}
is immediate future work.

Definition 5
(Presburger automata)

A Presburger automaton (PA)
\mathcal{A}
is a tuple
(Q,\Sigma,\delta,q_{0},F,\Psi)
, where
(Q,\Sigma,\delta,q_{0},F)
is an NFA with
Q=\{q_{0},q_{1},\ldots,q_{m}\}
, and
\Psi(x_{q_{0}},\cdots,x_{q_{m}})
is a
quantifier-free Presburger arithmetic
formula over the set of variables
\{x_{q_{i}}\mid i\in\{0\}\cup[m]\}
.

A word
w=w_{0}\cdots w_{n-1}\in(2^{AP})^{*}
is accepted by
\mathcal{A}
if there is a run
R=q_{0}\xrightarrow{w_{0}}q_{1}\cdots q_{n-1}\xrightarrow{w_{n-1}}q_{n}
such that
q_{n}\in F
and

\Psi(|R|_{q_{0}}/x_{q_{0}},\cdots,|R|_{q_{m}}/x_{q_{m}})
holds,
where
the vector
(|R|_{q})_{q\in Q}
is the Parikh image of the sequence
q_{0},\cdots,q_{n}
, that is,
|R|_{q}
is the number of occurrences of
q
in
R
. We use
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A})
to denote the set of words accepted by
\mathcal{A}
.

(

29

Nonemptiness of Presburger automata is decidable.

Given
\Phi_{\mathrm{core}}\wedge\Phi_{\mathrm{count}}

It is a simple observation that an

\Phi::=x+1=y\mid x<y\mid S(x)\mid\Phi\wedge\Phi\mid\neg\Phi\mid\forall x.\ \Phi\mid\forall S.\ \Phi,

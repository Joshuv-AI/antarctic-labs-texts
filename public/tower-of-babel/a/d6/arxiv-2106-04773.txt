Introduction

The aim of this paper is to discuss Schur
Q
-functions in connection with a representation of the Virasoro algebra.
Schur
Q
-functions are labelled by strict partitions and are defined as the Pfaffian of an alternating matrix.
Let
A=(a_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq 2m}
be an alternating
2m\times 2m
matrix.
The Pfaffian of
A
is

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pf}(A):=\sum_{\sigma\in F_{2m}}({\rm{sgn}}\,\sigma)a_{\sigma(1)\sigma(2)}a_{\sigma(3)\sigma(4)}\cdots a_{\sigma(2m-1)\sigma(2m)},

where

\displaystyle F_{2m}:=\{\sigma\in S_{2m};\sigma(1)<\sigma(3)<\cdots<\sigma(2m-1),\sigma(i)<\sigma(i+1)\,(i=1,3,\dots,2m-1)\}.

We see that
|F_{2m}|=(2m-1)!!
. The Laplace expansion of
\operatorname{Pf}(A)
is as follows.
For
1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{2\ell}\leq 2m
, let
A_{i_{1}i_{2}\dots i_{2\ell}}
be the
2\ell\times 2\ell

alternating matrix consisting of
i_{1}
th row,
i_{2}
th row, …, and
i_{1}
th column,
i_{2}
th column, ….
Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{Pf}(A)=\sum_{i=2}^{2m}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{Pf}(A_{1i})\operatorname{Pf}(A_{2\dots\widehat{i}\dots 2m}).

Here
\widehat{i}
means the omission of
i
. We will utilize this quadratic relation to derive
formulas for
Q
-functions.

Our previous paper
[
1
]
gives a formula of
L_{k}Q_{\lambda}
for
k\geq 1
,
where
L_{k}
denotes the Virasoro operator.
As a continuation of
[
1
]
we give in the present paper a formula for
L_{-k}Q_{\lambda}
.
Section
2
is a review of
Q
-functions containing some identities which do not seem to be obviously
derived from Pfaffian identities.
In Section
3
we first recall the reduced Fock representation of the Virasoro algebra on the space of the
Q
-functions.
Then the main result is given. Proofs consist of direct, simple calculations.

The Virasoro representations of this paper may be applied to, for example, the Kontsevich matrix models by certain rescaling. However we will not discuss here any relationship. Our motivation is to clarify the representation theoretical nature of the Hirota equations for certain soliton type hierarchies. In the final section we will give a conjecture on the Hirota equations for the KdV hierarchy.

2
Schur’s
\boldsymbol{Q}
-functions

A partition is an integer sequence

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{\ell})
,
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\dots\geq\lambda_{\ell}>0
, whose size is
|\lambda|=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\dots+\lambda_{\ell}
. The number of nonzero parts is the length of
\lambda
, denoted by
\ell(\lambda)
. Let
{\mathcal{SP}}(n)
be the set of partitions of
n
into distinct parts. We call a
\lambda\in{\mathcal{SP}(n)}
strict partition of
n
.
Let
V={\mathbb{C}}[t_{j};j\geq 1,{\rm odd}]
. This is decomposed as
V=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}V(n)
, where
V(n)
is the space of homogeneous polynomials of degree
n
, according to the counting
\deg t_{j}=j
. An inner product of
V
is defined by
\langle F,G\rangle=F\big(2\widetilde{\partial}\big)\overline{G}(t)|_{t=0}
, where
2\widetilde{\partial}=\big(2\partial_{1},\frac{2}{3}\partial_{3},\frac{2}{5}\partial_{5},\dots\big)
with
\partial_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t_{j}}
.

Schur’s
Q
-functions are defined in our context as follows. Put
\xi(t,u)=\sum_{j\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}t_{j}u^{j}
and define
q_{n}(t)\in V(n)
by

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_{n}(t)u^{n}.

For integers
a
,
b
with
a>b>0
, define

\displaystyle Q_{ab}(t):=q_{a}(t)q_{b}(t)+2\sum_{i=1}^{b}(-1)^{i}q_{a+i}(t)q_{b-i}(t),

\displaystyle Q_{ba}(t):=-Q_{ab}(t).

Finally, the
Q
-function labelled by the strict partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{2m})

(\lambda_{1}>\lambda_{2}>\dots>\lambda_{2m}\geq 0)
is defined by

\displaystyle Q_{\lambda}(t)=Q_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\dots\lambda_{2m}}(t)=\operatorname{Pf}{(Q_{\lambda_{i}\lambda_{j}})}_{1\leq i,j\leq 2m}.

The
Q
-function
Q_{\lambda}(t)
is homogeneous of degree
|\lambda|
. It is known that
\{Q_{\lambda}(t);\,|\lambda|=n\}
forms an orthogonal basis for
V(n)
, with respect to the above inner product. As Pfaffians, they satisfy the quadratic relations (cf.
[
2
]
):

If
\ell(\lambda)
is odd,

\displaystyle Q_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\dots\lambda_{\ell}}(t)=\sum^{\ell}_{i=1}(-1)^{i+1}q_{\lambda_{i}}(t)Q_{\lambda_{1}\dots\widehat{\lambda_{i}}\dots\lambda_{\ell}}(t).

If
\ell(\lambda)
is even,

\displaystyle Q_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\dots\lambda_{\ell}}(t)=\sum^{\ell}_{i=2}(-1)^{i}Q_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{i}}(t)Q_{\lambda_{2}\dots\widehat{\lambda_{i}}\dots\lambda_{\ell}}(t).

It is convenient to define
Q
-function
Q_{\alpha}(t)
for any non-negative integer sequence
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{\ell})
. We adopt the following rule for permutations of indices:

1.

If
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{\ell}
are all distinct, then
\sigma(\alpha)
is a strict partition for some permutation
\sigma\in S_{\ell}
, and

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha}(t)=({\rm sgn}\,\sigma)Q_{\sigma(\alpha)}(t).

2.

If
\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{j}>0
for some
i\neq j
, then
Q_{\alpha}(t)=0
.

3.

Using permutations,
0
’s should be moved in the tail of
\alpha
, keeping
0
’s order. After such permutation, all
0
’s should be deleted.

For example, we have
Q_{0,2,3,0,1}(t)=-Q_{3,2,1}(t)
.
Detailed arguments are found in
[
2
, Theorem 9.2]
.
Note that the above quadratic relations hold for
Q_{\alpha}(t)
with non-negative integer sequence
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{\ell})
. We also agree that, for
a>0
,
Q_{a,-a}(t)={(-1)}^{a-1}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\dots,\alpha_{\ell})
be a non-negative integer sequence, and
x
,
y
be non-negative integers.

(1)

If
\ell(\alpha)
is odd,

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha x}=-q_{x}Q_{\alpha}-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}{(-1)}^{i}q_{\alpha_{i}}Q_{\alpha_{1}\dots\widehat{\alpha_{i}}\dots\alpha_{\ell}x}-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}{(-1)}^{i}Q_{\alpha_{i}x}Q_{\alpha_{1}\dots\widehat{\alpha_{i}}\dots\alpha_{\ell}},

(2)

If
\ell(\alpha)
is even,

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha x}=-q_{x}Q_{\alpha}+\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}{(-1)}^{i}Q_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{i}}Q_{\alpha_{2}\dots\widehat{\alpha_{i}}\dots\alpha_{\ell}x}+\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}{(-1)}^{i}Q_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{i}x}Q_{\alpha_{2}\dots\widehat{\alpha_{i}}\dots\alpha_{\ell}},

(3)

If
\ell(\alpha)
is odd,

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha xy}=-Q_{xy}Q_{\alpha}-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}{(-1)}^{i}q_{\alpha_{i}}Q_{\alpha_{1}\dots\widehat{\alpha_{i}}\dots\alpha_{\ell}xy}-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}{(-1)}^{i}Q_{\alpha_{i}xy}Q_{\alpha_{1}\dots\widehat{\alpha_{i}}\dots\alpha_{\ell}},

(4)

If
\ell(\alpha)
is even,

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha xy}=-Q_{xy}Q_{\alpha}+\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}{(-1)}^{i}Q_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{i}}Q_{\alpha_{2}\dots\widehat{\alpha_{i}}\dots\alpha_{\ell}xy}+\sum_{i=2}^{\ell}{(-1)}^{i}Q_{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{i}xy}Q_{\alpha_{2}\dots\widehat{\alpha_{i}}\dots\alpha_{\ell}}.

Proof.

Let
\ell(\alpha,x,y)
be an even number. From the Pfaffian identity for
Q_{\alpha xy}
, the case (4) follows easily.
The cases (3) and (2) follow from (4) by setting
\alpha_{1}=0
and
y=0
, respectively. Finally case (1) is obtained from case (3) by setting
y=0
.
∎

Next, we recall the boson-fermion correspondence for neutral free fermions
\phi_{i}\ (i\in\mathbb{Z})
(cf.
[
3
]
). The Clifford algebra
\mathfrak{B}
is generated by free fermions
\phi_{i}\ (i\in\mathbb{Z})
satisfying the anti-commutation relation:

\displaystyle[\phi_{i},\phi_{j}]_{+}={(-1)}^{i}\delta_{i,-j}.

The vector space
F_{B}
has a basis consisting of
\phi_{i_{1}}\phi_{i_{2}}\cdots\phi_{i_{s}}|0\rangle
,
i_{1}>i_{2}>\dots>i_{s}\geq 0
, where
|0\rangle
is the vacuum vector.
The Clifford algebra
\mathfrak{B}
acts on
F_{B}
by
\phi_{i}|0\rangle=0
,
i<0
. For odd number
n
, we define the Hamiltonian by

\displaystyle H^{B}_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}{(-1)}^{i-1}\phi_{i}\phi_{-n-i}.

The operators
H_{n}^{B}

(n\in{\rm\mathbb{Z}_{odd}})
generate a Heisenberg algebra
\mathfrak{H}^{B}
with
\big[H_{n}^{B},H_{m}^{B}\big]=\frac{n}{2}\delta_{n,-m}
.
It is known that
F_{B}
is isomorphic to
V
:

\displaystyle\sigma_{B}(|0\rangle)=1,\qquad\sigma_{B}\big(H_{n}^{B}|0\rangle\big):=\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{n}},\qquad\sigma_{B}\big(H_{-n}^{B}|0\rangle\big):=np_{n},\qquad n\geq 1,\ {\rm odd}.

The map
\sigma_{B}\colon\mathfrak{H}^{B}\longrightarrow V
is called the boson-fermion correspondence of type
B
. Moreover, for the basis of
F_{B}

\sigma_{B}\left(\phi_{\lambda_{1}}\phi_{\lambda_{2}}\cdots\phi_{\lambda_{\ell}}|0\rangle\right)=\begin{cases}2^{-\frac{\ell}{2}}Q_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\dots\lambda_{\ell}}&\text{if $\ell$ is even},\\
2^{-\frac{\ell+1}{2}}Q_{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\dots\lambda_{\ell}}&\text{if $\ell$ is odd}.\end{cases}

In what follows, we denote
\sigma_{B}\left(\phi_{\lambda_{1}}\phi_{\lambda_{2}}\cdots\phi_{\lambda_{\ell}}|0\rangle\right)
by
\phi_{\lambda_{1}}\phi_{\lambda_{2}}\cdots\phi_{\lambda_{\ell}}|0\rangle
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
n=2m
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}(2i+1)t_{2i+1}(n-(2i+1))t_{n-(2i+1)}=2\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}{(-1)}^{i}(m-i)Q_{n-i,i}.

Proof.

First we rewrite the left-hand side of this equation by using power sum symmetric functions.

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}(2i+1)t_{2i+1}(n-(2i+1))t_{n-(2i+1)}=4\sum_{i\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}^{n}p_{i}p_{n-i}.

For an odd number
i
, the operator
H_{i}^{B}
acts on
{F_{B}}
. By the boson-fermion correspondence, the right hand side equals

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}^{n}\sum_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}}{(-1)}^{j+k}\phi_{j}\phi_{-j+i}\phi_{k}\phi_{-k+(n-i)}|0\rangle.

Since free fermions
\phi_{i}

(i<0)
act on vacuum vector
|0\rangle
as
0
, the above summation becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}^{n}\sum_{-n+i\leq j\leq n\atop 0\leq k\leq n-i}{(-1)}^{j+k}\phi_{j}\phi_{-j+i}\phi_{k}\phi_{-k+(n-i)}|0\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{i\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}^{n}\sum_{-n+i\leq j<0\atop 0\leq k\leq n-i}{(-1)}^{j+k}\phi_{j}\phi_{-j+i}\phi_{k}\phi_{-k+(n-i)}|0\rangle

(2.1)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+\sum_{i\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}^{n}\sum_{0\leq j\leq i\atop 0\leq k\leq n-i}{(-1)}^{j+k}\phi_{j}\phi_{-j+i}\phi_{k}\phi_{-k+(n-i)}|0\rangle

(2.2)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+\sum_{i\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}^{n}\sum_{i<j\leq n\atop 0\leq k\leq n-i}{(-1)}^{j+k}\phi_{j}\phi_{-j+i}\phi_{k}\phi_{-k+(n-i)}|0\rangle.

(2.3)

Here it is verified that the part (
2.2
) equals
0
. Next, we consider the parts (
2.1
) and (
2.3
). For the term
{(-1)}^{j+k}\phi_{j}\phi_{-j+i}\phi_{k}\phi_{-k+(n-i)}
, we only need to consider the cases that

j
or
-j+i
belongs to
\{-k,-(n-i)+k\}
. That is,
{(-1)}^{j+k}\phi_{j}\phi_{-j+i}\phi_{k}\phi_{-k+(n-i)}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\phi_{-k}\phi_{i+k}\phi_{k}\phi_{-k+(n-i)}&\text{if $j=-k$},\\
-\phi_{i+k}\phi_{-k}\phi_{k}\phi_{-k+(n-i)}&\text{if $j=i+k$},\\
-\phi_{-(n-i)+k}\phi_{-j+i}\phi_{k}\phi_{-k+(n-i)}&\text{if $j=-(n-i)+k$},\\
\phi_{n-k}\phi_{-j+i}\phi_{k}\phi_{-k+(n-i)}&\text{if $j=n-k$},\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}{(-1)}^{k+1}\phi_{i+k}\phi_{-k+(n-i)}&\text{if $j=-k$ or $j=i+k$},\\
{(-1)}^{k+1}\phi_{k}\phi_{-k+n}&\text{if $j=-(n-i)+k$ or $j=n-k$}.\end{cases}

Hence we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}^{n}\sum_{-n+i\leq j\leq n\atop 0\leq k\leq n-i}{(-1)}^{j+k}\phi_{j}\phi_{-j+i}\phi_{k}\phi_{-k+(n-i)}|0\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sum_{i\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}^{n}\sum_{0\leq k\leq n-i}{(-1)}^{k+1}\left(2\phi_{i+k}\phi_{-k+(n-i)}+2\phi_{k}\phi_{-k+n}\right)|0\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad{}=2\sum_{i\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}^{n}\sum_{0\leq k\leq n-i}{(-1)}^{k+1}\left(\phi_{i+k}\phi_{-k+(n-i)}+\phi_{k}\phi_{-k+n}\right)|0\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad{}=2\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}{(-1)}^{i}(n-2i)\phi_{n-i}\phi_{i}|0\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad{}=2\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}{(-1)}^{i}(m-i)Q_{n-i,i}.

∎

3
Reduced Fock representation of the Virasoro algebra

For a positive odd integer
j
, put
a_{j}=\sqrt{2}\partial_{j}
and
a_{-j}=\frac{j}{\sqrt{2}}t_{j}
so that they satisfy the Heisenberg relation as operators on
V
:

\displaystyle[a_{j},a_{i}]=j\delta_{j+i,0}.

For an integer
k
, put

\displaystyle L_{k}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\in\textrm{$\mathbb{Z}$}_{\rm{odd}}}{:}a_{-j}a_{j+2k}{:}+\frac{1}{8}\delta_{k,0},

where

\displaystyle{:}a_{j}a_{i}{:}=\begin{cases}a_{j}a_{i}&\text{if $j\leq i$},\\
a_{i}a_{j}&\text{if $j>i$}\end{cases}

is the normal ordering. For example,

\displaystyle L_{2}=2\partial_{1}\partial_{3}+\sum_{j\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}jt_{j}\partial_{j+4},\qquad L_{1}=\partial_{1}^{2}+\sum_{j\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}jt_{j}\partial_{j+2},

\displaystyle L_{0}=\sum_{j\geq 1}jt_{j}\partial_{j}+\frac{1}{8}\,{\rm id},

\displaystyle L_{-1}=\frac{1}{4}t_{1}^{2}+\sum_{j\geq 3,\,{\rm odd}}jt_{j}\partial_{j-2},\qquad\text{and}\qquad L_{-2}=\frac{3}{2}t_{1}t_{3}+\sum_{j\geq 5,\,{\rm odd}}jt_{j}\partial_{j-4}.

More generally, it is verified, by Proposition
2.2
, that

\displaystyle L_{-k}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}(2i+1)t_{2i+1}(2k-(2i+1))t_{2k-(2i+1)}+\sum_{i\geq 2k+1,\,{\rm odd}}it_{i}\partial_{i-2k}

\displaystyle\hphantom{L_{-k}}{}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}{(-1)}^{j}(k-j)Q_{2k-j,j}+\sum_{j\geq 2k+1,\,{\rm odd}}jt_{j}\partial_{j-2k}.

It is known that the operators
L_{k}
on
V
satisfy the Virasoro relation:

\displaystyle[L_{k},L_{\ell}]=2(k-\ell)L_{k+\ell}+\frac{k^{3}-k}{3}\delta_{k+\ell,0},\qquad k,\ell\in\mathbb{Z}.

A representation of the Virasoro algebra
{\mathcal{L}}=\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\mathbb{C}\ell_{k}}\oplus{\mathbb{C}z}
with central charge
1
is given by

\ell_{k}\mapsto\frac{1}{2}L_{k}
,
z\mapsto 1
, which we recall the reduced Fock representation. We have
L_{k}\cdot v\in V(n-2k)
for
v\in V(n)
. The inner product
\langle\ ,\,\rangle
defined in Section
2
is contravariant:

\displaystyle\langle L_{k}v,w\rangle=\langle v,L_{-k}w\rangle,\qquad v,w\in V.

Therefore the reduced Fock representation is infinitesimally unitary. The singular vectors are discussed in
[
6
]
. For the non-reduced Fock representation of the Virasoro algebra, see
for example
[
5
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

\displaystyle L_{-1}q_{n}=(n+1)q_{n+2}+\frac{1}{2}Q_{n2},\qquad n\geq 0.

Proof.

It is verified that

\displaystyle L_{-1}{\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}=\left(\frac{t_{1}^{2}}{4}+3t_{3}\partial_{1}+5t_{5}\partial_{3}+\cdots\right){\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}

\displaystyle\hphantom{L_{-1}{\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}}{}=\left(\frac{t_{1}^{2}}{4}+3t_{3}u+5t_{5}u^{3}+\cdots\right){\rm e}^{\sum_{j\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}t_{j}u^{j}}

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u}{\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}=\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\Big({\rm e}^{\sum_{j\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}t_{j}u^{j}}\Big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\frac{\partial}{\partial u}{\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}}{}=\big(t_{1}+3t_{3}u^{2}+5t_{5}u^{4}+7t_{7}u^{6}+\cdots\big){\rm e}^{\sum_{j\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}t_{j}u^{j}}.

By the relations
t_{1}=q_{1}
and
{t_{1}}^{2}=2q_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle uL_{-1}{\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}=\left(\frac{1}{2}uq_{2}-q_{1}\right){\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}+\frac{\partial}{\partial u}{\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}.

Here

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}uq_{2}-q_{1}\right)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_{n}u^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}q_{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_{n}u^{n+1}-q_{1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_{n}u^{n}

\displaystyle=u\left(\frac{1}{2}q_{2}\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}q_{n}u^{n}-q_{1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_{n+1}u^{n}\right)-q_{1},

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_{n}u^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nq_{n}u^{n-1}=u\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+2)q_{n+2}u^{n}+q_{1}.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle L_{-1}q_{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}q_{2}q_{n}-q_{1}q_{n+1}+(n+2)q_{n+2}

\displaystyle=(n+1)q_{n+2}+\frac{1}{2}Q_{n2}.

∎

Similarly, we have

Proposition 3.2
.

\displaystyle L_{-2}q_{n}=(n+2)q_{n+4}+Q_{n4}-\frac{1}{2}Q_{n3,1},\qquad n\geq 0.

Proof.

It is verified that

\displaystyle L_{-2}{\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3}{2}t_{1}t_{3}+5t_{5}\partial_{1}+7t_{7}\partial_{3}+\cdots\right){\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3}{2}t_{1}t_{3}+5t_{5}u+7t_{7}u^{3}+\cdots\right){\rm e}^{\sum_{j\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}t_{j}u^{j}}

and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u}{\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\Big({\rm e}^{\sum_{j\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}t_{j}u^{j}}\Big)

\displaystyle=\big(t_{1}+3t_{3}u^{2}+5t_{5}u^{4}+7t_{7}u^{6}+\cdots\big){\rm e}^{\sum_{j\geq 1,\,{\rm odd}}t_{j}u^{j}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle u^{3}L_{-2}{\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}=\left(-t_{1}-3t_{3}u^{2}+\frac{3}{2}t_{1}t_{3}u^{3}\right){\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}+\frac{\partial}{\partial u}{\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}.

We have
t_{3}=\frac{1}{3}(q_{3}-Q_{2,1})
and
t_{1}t_{3}=\frac{1}{3}(2q_{4}-Q_{3,1})
.
Therefore

\displaystyle u^{3}L_{-2}{\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}=\left(-q_{1}-u^{2}(q_{3}-Q_{2,1})+\frac{1}{2}u^{3}(2q_{4}-Q_{3,1})\right){\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}+\frac{\partial}{\partial u}{\rm e}^{\xi(t,u)}.

Here the first term equals

\displaystyle-q_{1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_{n}u^{n}-(q_{3}-Q_{2,1})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_{n}u^{n+2}+\frac{1}{2}(2q_{4}-Q_{3,1})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_{n}u^{n+3}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=u^{3}\left(-q_{1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_{n+3}u^{n}-(q_{3}-Q_{2,1})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_{n+1}u^{n}+\frac{1}{2}(2q_{4}-Q_{3,1})\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q_{n}u^{n}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}+u^{3}\big({-}q_{1}\big(u^{-3}+q_{1}u^{-2}+q_{2}u^{-1}\big)-(q_{3}-Q_{2,1})u^{-1}\big)

and the second term equals

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}nq_{n}u^{n-1}=u^{3}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n+4)q_{n+4}u^{n}+u^{3}\big(q_{1}u^{-3}+2q_{2}u^{-2}+3q_{3}u^{-1}\big).

Also it is easy to check that

\displaystyle u^{3}\big({-}q_{1}\big(u^{-3}+q_{1}u^{-2}+q_{2}u^{-1}\big)-(q_{3}-Q_{2,1})u^{-1}\big)+u^{3}\big(q_{1}u^{-3}+2q_{2}u^{-2}+3q_{3}u^{-1}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=u^{2}(2q_{3}-q_{1}q_{2}+Q_{2,1})=0.

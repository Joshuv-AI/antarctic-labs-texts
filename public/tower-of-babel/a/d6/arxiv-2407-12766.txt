Vanishing viscosity limit for hyperbolic system of Temple class in 1-d with nonlinear viscosity
Boris Haspot, Animesh Jana

Abstract
We consider hyperbolic system with nonlinear viscosity such that the viscosity matrix B ⁡ ( u ) \displaystyle B(u) is commutating with A ⁡ ( u ) \displaystyle A(u) the matrix associated to the convective term. The drift matrix is assumed to be Temple class. First we prove the global existence of smooth solutions for initial data with small total variation. We show that the solution to the parabolic equation converges to a semi-group solution of the hyperbolic system as viscosity goes to zero. Furthermore, we prove that the zero diffusion limit coincides with the one obtained in [Bianchini and Bressan, Indiana Univ. Math. J. 2000].

Lemma 3.1

\displaystyle n\geq 1,p\geq 1

\displaystyle u\in C^{3}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}^{n})

\displaystyle\mu_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow[c_{0},\infty),1\leq i\leq n

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\int\limits_{0}^{\tau}\kappa_{B}\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|G_{x}(\tau-s)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{1}}\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|h(s)\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{1}}\left|\hskip-0.56905pt\left|u_{x}\right|\hskip-0.56905pt\right|_{L^{\infty}}\,ds

Lemma 4.1

u_{+}&\mbox{ if }x>n\delta.\end{array}\right.

(8.12)

Due to finite speed of propagation, up to a small time
\displaystyle t_{0}
, the waves do not interact with each other and the solution to (
1.3
) can be written as

u^{\delta}(t,x)=\mathcal{R}_{i}(z_{i}(t,x-i\delta);w_{i-1})\mbox{ for }x\in[i\delta+\hat{\lambda}t,(i+1)\delta-\hat{\lambda}t]\mbox{ when }t\in[0,t_{0}].

(8.13)

Let
\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon,\delta}
be the solution to (
2.1
) corresponding to the initial data
\displaystyle\bar{u}^{\delta}
. By our previous analysis and
\displaystyle L^{1}_{loc}
-dependence (
8.9
), we obtain that
\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon,\delta}
converges to
\displaystyle u^{\delta}
. Due to strict hyperbolicity, we note that the waves of
\displaystyle u^{\delta}
has disjoint support of length at least
\displaystyle ct_{0}
. By repeat the previous argument, we can obtain that
\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon,\delta}
converges to
\displaystyle u^{\delta}
for all
\displaystyle t>0
. Now, by sending
\displaystyle\delta\rightarrow 0
, we can obtain that the viscosity solution
\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}
converges to solution of (
1.3
) for Riemann data. Since a Lipschitz continuous semigroup is determined by the local in time behavior for piecewise constant data, this characterizes the limit function
\displaystyle u
. Moreover, it also says that for any subsequence
\displaystyle\varepsilon_{k}\rightarrow 0
,
\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon_{k}}
converges to the same limit. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a number field and
S
a finite set of places containing all archimedean places of
K
. Let
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a rational map defined over
K
of degree
d\geq 2
and let
\beta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
be a non-exceptional point, i.e.
|\varphi^{-n}(\beta)|\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
. For a point
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
, Silverman
[
Sil93
]
has shown that only finitely many points in its forward orbit
\{\varphi^{n}(\alpha)\}
can be
S
-integral relative to
\beta
. Here, we say
x,y\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
are
S
-integral relative to each other if for all
v\not\in S
, the reductions
\overline{x},\overline{y}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(k_{v})
are distinct, where
k_{v}
denotes the residue field of
O_{K}
at the place
v
.

In this paper, we study the number of
S
-integral points that can exist in the forward orbit of
\alpha
. In
[
HS11
]
, Hsia and Silverman proved a bound of
c\,4^{|S|}
for some constant
c>0
using Roth’s theorem. More recently in
[
KLS+15
]
, Krieger et al also proved an exponential bound
c^{|S|}
on the number of
S
-integral points using Siegel’s theorem. Our first theorem is an improved upper bound on the number of
S
-integral points relative to
\beta
among
\{\varphi^{n}(\alpha)\}_{n\geq 0}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
K
be a number field and
\varphi
a rational map defined over
K
with degree
d\geq 2
. Then there exists a constant
c_{1}=c_{1}(K,\varphi)>0
such that for any non-preperiodic
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
, non-exceptional
\beta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
and any finite set of places
S
of
K
containing all archimedean places, we have

\left|\{n\geq 0\mid\varphi^{n}(\alpha)\text{ is S-integral relative to }\beta\}\right|\leq c_{1}|S|(\log|S|+1)^{6}.

This gives us a superlinear bound on the number of
S
-integral points. When
\varphi(z)
is a power map or a Lattès map (see Section
2
for the definition of a Lattès map), we are able to obtain a linear bound in
|S|
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
K
be a number field and
\varphi
a power or Lattès map defined over
K
. Then there exist constants
c_{2}
and
c_{3}=c_{3}(\varphi)
, such that for any non-preperiodic
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
, non-exceptional
\beta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
and any finite set of places
S
containing all archimedean places, we have

\left|\{n\geq 0\mid\varphi^{n}(\alpha)\text{ is S-integral relative to }\beta\}\right|\leq c_{2}|S|+c_{3}(\log|S|+1).

If
\varphi(z)=z^{d}
, we can take
c_{2}=3
and for Lattès maps, we can take
c_{2}=5
. If
c_{3}
is allowed to depend not just on
\varphi
but also on
\beta
, then we can take
c_{2}=2
and
4
respectively.

For additional results in the case of power and Lattès maps when
S
is the set of all archimedean places, see
[
Sil95
]
for the power map case and
[
Ing09
]
for the Lattès map case.

We also give explicit bounds for the case of
\varphi(z)=z^{2}
; see Theorem
8.3
. As an example, we show that

\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\left|\{(n,i,j)\in\mathbb{N}^{3}:y\cdot 2^{2^{n}}-x=3^{i}5^{j}\}\right|\leq 17.

This gives an explicit answer to a question raised by Hsia–Silverman; see
[
HS11
, Remark 14]
).

In the case of a power map
\varphi(z)=z^{2}
and a given
\beta
, Theorem
1.2
provides an upper bound of the form
2|S|+o(|S|)
. We can ask how tight is this upper bound. Taking
\beta=-1
, if there were infinitely many primes in the sequence
\{2^{2^{n}}+1\}
, also known as Fermat primes, we will be able to choose a finite set of places
S
, with
|S|
arbitrary large, such that the number of
S
-integral points (relative to
\beta)
in
\{\varphi^{n}(2)\}_{n\geq 1}
would be at least
|S|-1
. Hence proving an upper bound of
|S|-2
would disprove the existence of infinitely many Fermat primes, which is currently an open problem. Our upper bound is off from this by a factor of two, and it would be interesting to know if one could obtain an upper bound of
|S|+o(|S|)
using similar ideas.

Our approach follows that of
[
Sil93
]
and
[
HS11
]
. In
[
HS11
]
, Hsia and Silverman showed the existence of a constant
\gamma=\gamma(d,[K:\mathbb{Q}])
for which the number of points
S
-integral relative to
\beta
in
\{\varphi^{n}(\alpha)\}
is less than

\gamma 4^{|S|}+\log_{d}^{+}\left(\frac{h(\varphi)+\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\beta)}{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)}\right)

where
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}
is the canonical height for the rational map
\varphi
(see Section
2
).
Similarly we also obtain an upper bound of this form, upon which Theorem
1.1
follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Keep the notations of Theorem
1.1
. Then there exists a constant
c_{4}=c_{4}(d)
such that the following is true:

|\{n\geq 0\mid\varphi^{n}(\alpha)\text{ is S-integral relative to }\beta\}|\leq c_{4}|S|(\log|S|+1)^{6}+\log_{d}^{+}\left(\frac{h(\varphi)+1}{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)}\right).

If
\beta
is not in the forward orbit of a critical point, we may take
c_{4}=c_{5}d
for some constant
c_{5}>0
.

Theorem
1.1
follows easily from Theorem
1.3
since if
K
and
\varphi
are fixed, then

\inf_{\alpha\in K,\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)\not=0}\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)>0

due to the Northcott property for heights. Compared to Hsia–Silverman’s bound, other than the improvement as a function of
|S|
, we have also removed the dependency on
\beta
and have a superlinear bound in terms of
d
when
\beta
is not postcritical.

It has already been observed in
[
KLS+15
]
that it is impossible to obtain bounds for
S
-integral points that are independent of
\varphi
. We show in Section
9
that for
S
-integral points, the dependency of Theorem
1.3
in
\frac{h(\varphi)}{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)}
cannot be removed. In many cases, one knows the existence of a uniform constant
c>0
such that
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)>ch(\varphi)
which would lead to uniformity results. For example,
[
KLS+15
]
asks if a uniform bound for points in an orbit that are
S
-units can be achieved, and proves a uniform bound for monic polynomials
\varphi(z)\in O_{S}[z]
. Using results of Looper
[
Loo19
]
, we generalize
[
KLS+15
, Theorem 1.7]
to polynomials with at most
s
places of bad reduction.

Theorem 1.4
.

Keep the notation of Theorem
1.1
. Assume that
\varphi(z)\not=cz^{d}
. Then there exists a constant
c_{6}=c_{6}([K:\mathbb{Q}],d,s,|S|)>0
such that if
\varphi(z)\in K[z]
is a polynomial of degree
d
that has
s
places of bad reduction, then

\left|\{n\geq 0\mid\varphi^{n}(\alpha)\text{ is an S-unit }\}\right|\leq c_{6}.

We also study the special case where
\alpha=\beta
is a non-preperiodic critical point of
\varphi
. This situation has been studied before in the context of Zsigmondy sets by Krieger
[
Kri13
]
and Ren
[
Ren21
]
. Using the dynamical properties of a critical point, we are able to obtain a linear
|S|+o(|S|)
bound.

Theorem 1.5
.

There exists a constant
c_{7}>0
such that the following holds: let
K
be a number field,
S
a finite set of places containing the archimedean places and
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
a rational map defined over
K
of degree
d\geq 2
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
be a critical point of
\varphi
that is not preperiodic. Then

\left|\{n\geq 0\mid\varphi^{n}(\alpha)\text{ is S-integral relative to }\alpha\}\right|\leq|S|+c_{7}\log(|S|+1)+\log^{+}_{d}\left(\frac{h(\varphi)+1}{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)}\right).

1.1.
Proof sketch

We now give a sketch of our arguments. Let

\lambda_{v}([x_{0}:x_{1}],[y_{0}:y_{1}])=-\log\frac{|x_{0}y_{1}-x_{1}y_{0}|_{v}}{\max\{|x_{0}|_{v},|x_{1}|_{v}\}\max\{|y_{0}|_{v},|y_{1}|_{v}\}}

be the logarithmic chordal distance. Then if
\alpha
is
S
-integral relative to
\beta
, we have
\lambda_{v}(\alpha,\beta)=0

for all
v\not\in S
. Fix some positive
\delta>0
. To obtain finiteness, Hsia and Silverman show that if
\varphi^{n}(\alpha)
is
S
-integral relative to
\beta
and
n
is sufficiently large, then

\sum_{v\in S}N_{v}\lambda_{v}(\varphi^{n-m}(\alpha),\beta_{v,m})\geq(2+\delta)h(\varphi^{n-m}(\alpha))

where
m
is some fixed natural number depending only on the degree
d
and
\beta_{v,m}
are elements of
\varphi^{-m}(\beta)
. Applying a quantitative version of Roth’s theorem
[
HS11
, Theorem 10]
that allows summing over a finite set of places
S
, they obtain an upper bound on the number of
S
-integral points of the form
\gamma 4^{|S|}
.

In our proof, for each
n
where
\varphi^{n}(\alpha)
is
S
-integral relative to
\beta
, we instead show there exists a place
v_{n}\in S
such that the inequality

N_{v_{n}}\log^{+}|\varphi^{n-m^{\prime}}(\alpha)-\beta_{v_{n},m^{\prime}}|^{-1}_{v_{n}}\geq(2+\delta)h(\varphi^{n-m^{\prime}}(\alpha)),

where
m^{\prime}
now depends not only on
d
but also on
|S|
. This requires a finer control on some estimates which we prove in Proposition
4.6
. The advantage is that we can now apply a quantitative Roth’s theorem (Theorem
5.1
) that only deals with a single place
v
, but in return provides us with a much better upper bound on the number of exceptions, as we will now explain.

For simplicity, let’s first assume that
|S|
is much larger than
d
and that
\beta
is not in the forward orbit of a critical point; i.e.
\varphi^{n}(x)=\beta
has
d^{n}
distinct solutions for all
n
. We let
N
be the smallest positive integer such that
d^{N}>(2+\delta)|S|
. Then since

\sum_{v\in M_{K}}\log^{+}|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|^{-1}_{v}=h(\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta)\approx h(\varphi^{n}(\alpha)),

if
\varphi^{n}(\alpha)
is
S
-integral relative to
\beta
, we get

\sum_{v\in S}\log^{+}|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|^{-1}_{v}\approx h(\varphi^{n}(\alpha)).

Hence there exists a place
v
such that

N_{v}\log^{+}|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|_{v}^{-1}\geq\frac{1}{|S|}h(\varphi^{n}(\alpha)).

(1.1)

This implies that
\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)
is equally close, with some negligible error when
n
is sufficiently large, to a root of
\varphi^{N}(x)=\beta
, say
\beta_{n}
. Then

N_{v}\log^{+}|\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)-\beta_{n}|_{v}^{-1}\geq\frac{1}{|S|}h(\varphi^{n}(\alpha))\geq\frac{d^{N}}{|S|}h(\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha))\geq(2+\delta)h(\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)),

allowing us to apply Roth’s theorem for a single place
v
. For a fixed
\beta^{\prime}\in\varphi^{-N}(\beta)
and
v\in S
, the number of exceptions to the inequality of Roth’s theorem is at most
c\log([K(\beta^{\prime}):K])^{3}\leq c(d\log|S|)^{3}
, and each
n
where
\varphi^{n}(\alpha)
is
S
-integral relative to
\beta
gives us such an exception. Then since there are at most
(2+\delta)d|S|
elements in
\varphi^{-N}(\beta)
and
|S|
places, a pigeonhole argument implies that we can have at most
c|S|^{2}(d\log|S|)^{3}
values of
n
for which
\varphi^{n}(\alpha)
is
S
-integral relative to
\beta
, giving us a super-quadratic bound in
|S|
. Bringing it down to a superlinear bound requires some optimization and analyzing for how many distinct places
v
can we obtain (
1.1
) for a given
n
.

To obtain a linear bound in
|S|
for certain cases, we have to cut down the number of exceptions in Roth’s theorem. This is possible if we use theorems of Thue–Siegel type, where we have an inequality of the form

\log^{+}|\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)-\beta_{n}|^{-1}_{v}\geq c[K(\beta_{n}):K]^{\kappa}h(\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha))

with
c,\kappa>0
. However to obtain such a bound, we require
d^{N}>|S|^{1+\kappa}
and in particular, there will be at least
|S|^{1+\kappa}
many elements in
\varphi^{-N}(\beta)
and since we allow at least one exception for each
\beta^{\prime}\in\varphi^{-N}(\beta)
, give us at the very least an upper bound of
|S|^{1+\kappa}
.

We get around this issue by using a version of Roth’s theorem that allows
\beta
to vary in an extension
L
; i.e. bound the number of pairs
(\alpha_{i},\beta_{i})
’s such that

|\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}|_{v}>c[K(\beta_{i}):K]^{\kappa}h(\alpha_{i})

with
\alpha_{i}\in K
and
\beta_{i}\in L
. Such a version was already proven in
[
BvdP88
]
but we instead adapt the proof of Roth’s in
[
HS00
]
to prove the theorem that we need due to some technicalities. In general, the degree
[K(\varphi^{-N}(\beta)):K]
grows as fast as
d^{d^{N}}
which is too large for this argument to give a better bound. However for power and Lattès maps, the degree grows like
d^{kN}
for some fixed natural number
k>0
, allowing this method to work and give us a linear bound in
|S|
.

1.2.
Big O notation

Throughout the paper, we will have many bounds involving different constants. For many of them, we will omit the constant and instead use the big
O
notation. For us in any Proposition/Lemma/Theorem, a big
O
represents a constant that is independent of the parameters in the theorem. If it depends on some of the parameters in the theorem, we will indicate as such by adding the parameters as a subscript to the big
O
.

2.
Background and Notations

Let
K
be a number field and
M_{K}
the set of places of
K
. We define the Weil height
h
for
x\in K
by

h(x)=\sum_{v\in M_{K}}N_{v}\log^{+}|x|

where
N_{v}=\frac{[K_{v}:\mathbb{Q}_{p}]}{[K:\mathbb{Q}]}
with
p
being the prime that
v
lies above. For
x\in\overline{\mathbb{Q}}
, this is independent of the choice of
K
for which
x
lies in so we may omit the field
K
. This definition extends in general to points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}(K)
by

h([x_{0}:\cdots:x_{n}])=\sum_{v\in M_{K}}N_{v}\log\max\{|x_{0}|_{v},\ldots,|x_{n}|_{v}\}.

The product formula ensures that this definition is independent of the choice of homogeneous coordinates.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a rational map of degree
d\geq 2
defined over
K
. Then the canonical height
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}
is given by

\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{h(\varphi^{n}(x))}{d^{n}}.

It is the unique function
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}:\mathbb{P}^{1}(\overline{K})\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\varphi(x))=d\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)
and
|\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)-h(x)|
is uniformly bounded over all
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\overline{K})
.

For a rational map
\varphi
defined over
K
, we may write it as
f(z)/g(z)
with
f(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}z^{i}
and
g(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{d}b_{i}z^{i}
for some choice of
f,g\in K[z]
. We then define the height of
\varphi
as

h(\varphi)=h([a_{0}:\cdots:a_{d}:b_{0}:\cdots:b_{d}]).

This is independent of the choice of
f
and
g
as our presentation for
\varphi
.

A Lattès map
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a rational map such that we have the following commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}^{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}

where
E
is an elliptic curve,
\psi:E\to E
is a self map (not necessarily a group homomorphism) with degree
d\geq 2
and
\pi:E\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
a non-constant map. It is easy to see that the preperiodic points of
\varphi
are exactly the projections of the torsion points of
E
.

In section
4
, it is necessary for us to use potential theory not only over
\mathbb{C}
, but also over the non-archimedean places. For a non-archimedean place
v
of
K
, we let
\mathbb{C}_{v}
denote the completion of
\overline{K}_{v}
. We will then let
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\operatorname{Berk},v}
denote the Berkovich projective line of
\mathbb{C}_{v}
. It contains
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{v})
as a dense subset, along with other extra points. For example, each disc
\zeta(a,r)
centered at
a
and of radius
r>0
gives rise to an extra point, known as either a Type II point if
r
is rational, and a Type III point otherwise. Readers may consult with
[
BR10
]
or
[
Ben19
]
for more details.

3.
Quantitative Bounds involving Heights

Let
K
be a number field,
v\in M_{K}
a place and
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
a rational map of degree
d\geq 2
defined over
K
. In this section, we prove some quantitative bounds that involve heights. We first give an effective bound on the difference between the canonical height
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)
and the Weil height
h(x)
in terms of the degree
d
and
h(\varphi)
. We then study how
|\varphi(z)-\varphi(\beta)|_{v}
compares with
|z-\beta|_{v}
, under the assumption that
|z-\beta|_{v}
is sufficiently small.

We first state some basic upper bounds involving height that we will be using repeatedly.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
K
be a number field and let
x,y\in K
. Then

(1)

h(x+y)\leq h(x)+h(y)+\log 2,

(2)

h(x-y)\geq|h(x)-h(y)|-\log 2,

(3)

h(xy)\leq h(x)+h(y).

Proof.

The first inequality follows from Proposition 5(b) of
[
HS11
]
as we may view the height of the points
x,y
as the height of the linear polynomials
z+x
and
z+y
. The second follows from the first by rearranging it to

h(x)\geq h(x+y)-h(y)-\log 2
and the third inequality follows from
\log^{+}|xy|_{v}\leq\log^{+}|x|_{v}+\log^{+}|y|_{v}
.
∎

We now prove an effective bound on the difference
|\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)-h(x)|
in terms of the degree
d
and the height of our rational map
h(\varphi)
. Such bounds have already been given by
[
Hut18
]
and
[
Ing22
]
in the more general context of rational maps
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{n}\to\mathbb{P}^{n}
. However, their bounds are weaker than what can be obtained for
n=1
due to the generality of their results. We strengthen their bound in terms of the degree
d
for
n=1
for the dependency on
d
in Theorem
1.3
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a rational map defined over a number field
K
of degree
d\geq 2
. Then

|\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)-h(x)|\leq O(dh(\varphi)+d\log d).

(3.1)

for all
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\overline{K})
.

Proof.

We follow the proof of Theorem 3.11 in
[
Sil07
]
. We first establish a bound on
|\frac{1}{d}h(\varphi(x))-h(x)|.
In
[
Sil07
]
, the following upper bound

\frac{1}{d}h(\varphi(x))\leq h(x)+h(\varphi)+\log\binom{d+2}{2}=h(x)+h(\varphi)+O(\log d)

was proven. For a lower bound, we let
f_{0},f_{1}
be a homogeneous presentation of
\varphi
with
f_{0},f_{1}\in K[x,y]
with at least one of its coefficients being equal to
1
. Then we have to find homogeneous polynomials
g_{ij}
such that
g_{i0}f_{0}+g_{i1}f_{1}=x_{i}^{e}
for some
e
. Let

f_{0}(x_{0},x_{1})=a_{0}x_{0}^{d}+a_{1}x_{0}^{d-1}x_{1}+\cdots+a_{d}x_{1}^{d}\text{ and }f_{1}(x_{0},x_{1})=b_{0}x_{0}^{d}+b_{1}x_{0}^{d-1}x_{1}+\cdots+b_{d}x_{1}^{d}.

Then if we let

g_{00}(x_{0},x_{1})=u_{0}x_{0}^{e-d}+u_{1}x_{0}^{e-d-1}x_{1}+\cdots+u_{d}x_{1}^{e-d}\text{ and }g_{01}(x_{0},x_{1})=v_{0}x_{0}^{e-d}+v_{1}x_{0}^{e-d-1}x_{1}+\cdots+v_{d}x_{1}^{e-d},

and we set
e=2d
, we have to solve the system of equations

\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}&&&\cdots&b_{0}&&&\cdots\\
a_{1}&a_{0}&&\cdots&b_{1}&b_{0}&&\cdots\\
a_{2}&a_{1}&a_{0}&\cdots&b_{2}&b_{1}&b_{0}&\cdots\\
\vdots&&&&\vdots&&&\cdots\\
\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}u_{0}\\
\vdots\\
u_{d}\\
v_{0}\\
\vdots\\
v_{d}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\\
\vdots\\
0\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

Let’s denote the first matrix by
A
. Then The determinant of
A
is the homogeneous resultant of
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
, and is non-zero since
f_{0}
and
f_{1}
share no common zeroes. In particular, we can solve for the coefficients
\{u_{i},v_{j}\}
by Cramer’s rule. We will bound the height of the determinant with the next proposition.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
D=\det(A)
, i.e.
\operatorname{Res}(f_{0},f_{1})
. Then

h(\det(D))\leq(2d+2)h(\varphi)+O(d\log d).

Proof.

We can write the determinant as a sum of
(2d+2)!
many terms, each term a product of
2d+2
many coefficients
a_{i},b_{j}
. Applying the triangle inequality, we see that

\log^{+}|D|_{v}\leq\log((2d+2)!)+\log^{+}\max\{|a_{i}|_{v},|b_{j}|_{v}\}^{2d+2}\text{ if }v\text{ is archimedean,}

\log^{+}|D|_{v}\leq\log^{+}\max\{|a_{i}|_{v},|b_{j}|_{v}\}^{2d+2}\text{ if }v\text{ is non-archimedean.}

Summing up, we get

\sum_{v\in M_{K}}N_{v}\log^{+}\max\{|a_{i}|_{v},|b_{j}|_{v}\}^{2d+2}=(2d+2)h(\varphi)

and

\sum_{v\text{ arch. }}N_{v}\log((2d+2)!)=\log((2d+2)!),

giving us

h(D)\leq\log((2d+2)!)+(2d+2)h(\varphi)=(2d+2)h(\varphi)+O(d\log d)

as desired.
∎

Returning to the proof of Proposition
3.2
, by Cramer’s rule each
u_{i}
can be given by
\det(A_{i})/\det(A)
where one replaces the
i^{th}
column with the column vector
(1,0,\ldots,0)^{T}
and
A
is the matrix of coefficients. The same argument in Lemma
3.3
applies to
\det(A_{i})
too and in particular

h(u_{i})\leq h(\det(A_{i}))+h(\det(A))\leq(4d+2)h(\varphi)+O(d\log d).

Similarly, the same holds for
v_{j}
. Going back to the proof in
[
Sil07
]
, as
M=N=1
and
e=2d
, for each place
v\in M_{K}
and
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\overline{K})
, we have

|x|_{v}^{2d}\leq 2(d+1)\max\{|u_{i}|_{v},|v_{j}|_{v}\}|x|_{v}^{d}|\varphi(x)|_{v}\text{ for v archimedean }

|x|_{v}^{2d}\leq\max\{|u_{i}|_{v},|v_{j}|_{v}\}|x|_{v}^{d}|\varphi(x)|_{v}\text{ for v non-archimedean }.

Since we have
2d+2
many
u_{i},v_{j}
’s, we have

\sum_{v\in M_{K}}N_{v}\max\{|u_{i}|_{v},|v_{j}|_{v}\}\leq(2d+2)((4d+2)h(\varphi)+O(d\log d))=O(d^{2}h(\varphi)+d^{2}\log d).

Summing up, we get

h(\varphi(x))\geq dh(x)-O(d^{2}h(\varphi)+d^{2}\log d)

and so

\left|\frac{1}{d}h(\varphi(x))-h(x)\right|\leq O(dh(\varphi)+d\log d).

We then perform Tate’s telescoping argument to get

|\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)-h(x)|\leq\left(1+\frac{1}{d}+\cdots\right)O(dh(\varphi)+d\log d)=O(dh(\varphi)+d\log d)

as desired.
∎

Now fix a place
v\in M_{K}
. We want to bound
|\varphi(z)-\varphi(\beta)|_{v}
in terms of
|z-\beta|_{v}
, under the assumption that
|z-\beta|_{v}
is small. Let

\rho_{v}([x_{0}:x_{1}],[y_{0}:y_{1}])=\frac{|x_{0}y_{1}-x_{1}y_{0}|_{v}}{\|(x_{0},x_{1})\|_{v}\|(y_{0},y_{1})\|_{v}}

be the chordal distance on
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{v})
, where
\|\cdot\|_{v}
is the maximum norm on
\mathbb{C}_{v}^{2}
if
v
is non-archimedean and is the Euclidean norm if
v
is archimedean. For a rational function
\varphi
, we will set
C_{\varphi,v}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{P}^{1}}\varphi^{\#}(z)
for archimedean
v
, where
\varphi^{\#}(z)
is the chordal derivative, and
1/|\operatorname{Res}\varphi|_{v}
for non-archimedean
v
.

Proposition 3.4
.

We can take
C_{\varphi,v}
as a Lipschitz constant for the rational map
\varphi
under the metric
\rho_{v}
. Furthermore, we have the bound

\log C_{\varphi}=[K:\mathbb{Q}]O(dh(\varphi)+d\log d).

Proof.

By section 2.3 of
[
GOV20
]
, if
v
is archimedean we may take
C_{\varphi}=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{P}^{1}}\varphi^{\#}(z)
where
\varphi^{\#}(z)
is the chordal derivative of
\varphi(z)
. Then Proposition 2.5 of
[
GOV20
]
tells us that

\log(\varphi^{\#}(z))\leq-\log|d|-2\log|\operatorname{Res}F|_{v}+4d\log|F|_{v}+A_{2}(d)

=[K:\mathbb{Q}]O(dh(\varphi)+d\log d)

by Proposition
3.2
. If
v
is non-archimedean, then
[
RW15
, Theorem 0.1]
tells us we may take
C_{\varphi}=1/|\operatorname{Res}\varphi|_{v}
which again is bounded by the same expression by Lemma
3.3
.
∎

We convert this to a bound in terms of the Euclidean distance, instead of the chordal distance.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\varphi(z)=\frac{f(z)}{g(z)}
be a rational function defined over a number field
K
of degree
d\geq 2
and let
v
be a place of
K
. Let
\beta\in K
. If
\varphi(\beta)\not=\infty
, then

\log|z-\beta|_{v}^{-1}-\log|\varphi(z)-\varphi(\beta)|_{v}^{-1}\leq d[K:\mathbb{Q}]O(h(\varphi)+h(\beta)+\log d)

for all
z\in\mathbb{C}_{v}
satisfying

\log|z-\beta|_{v}\leq-d[K:\mathbb{Q}]O(h(\varphi)+h(\beta)+\log d).

Proof.

Let
C
be a Lipschitz constant for
\rho_{v}
. Then

\rho_{v}(\varphi(z),\varphi(\beta))\leq C\rho_{v}(z,\beta)\leq C|z-\beta|_{v}.

Thus if
|z-\beta|_{v}<(2\|1,\varphi(\beta)\|_{v}C)^{-1}
, we must have

\frac{|\varphi(z)-\varphi(\beta)|_{v}}{\|(\varphi(z),1)\|_{v}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\implies|\varphi(z)|_{v}\leq 2\|\varphi(\beta),1\|_{v}.

Now we have

\frac{|\varphi(z)-\varphi(\beta)|_{v}}{\|(\varphi(z),1)\|_{v}\|(\varphi(\beta),1)\|_{v}}\leq C\frac{|z-\beta|_{v}}{\|(z,1)\|_{v}\|(\beta,1)\|_{v}}

\implies|\varphi(z)-\varphi(\beta)|_{v}\leq C|z-\beta|_{v}\|(\varphi(z),1)\|_{v}\|(\varphi(\beta),1)\|_{v}\leq C|z-\beta|_{v}2\|(\varphi(\beta),1)\|_{v}^{2}.

Taking logarithms, we have

\log|\varphi(z)-\varphi(\beta)|_{v}^{-1}\geq\log|z-\beta|_{v}^{-1}-\log C-\log 2\|(\varphi(\beta),1)\|_{v}.

By Proposition
3.4
, we may take
C=C_{\varphi,v}=[K:\mathbb{Q}]O(dh(\varphi)+d\log d)
and using the inequality
\log\|(\varphi(\beta),1)\|_{v}\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]h(\varphi(\beta))
, we get

\log|\varphi(z)-\varphi(\beta)|_{v}^{-1}\leq\log|z-\beta|^{-1}_{v}-d[K:\mathbb{Q}]O(h(\varphi)+h(\beta)+\log d)

as desired.

∎

In some situations, we will require a finer control on
|\varphi(z)-\varphi(\beta)|_{v}
than simply an upper bound. If the order of vanishing of
\varphi(z)-\varphi(\beta)
at
z=\beta
is
k
, then we expect
|\varphi(z)-\varphi(\beta)|_{v}\approx|z-\beta|_{v}^{k}
. We give an upper bound on the error of
\log|\varphi(z)-\varphi(\beta)|^{-1}_{v}-k\log|z-\beta|_{v}^{-1}
, again under the assumption that
|z-\beta|_{v}
is small enough. However, we lose control on the dependency of the constants in terms of the degree
d
.

First by Leibniz’s rule, we have

(fg)^{(n)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}f^{(k)}g^{(n-k)}

for any two functions
f,g
. Writing
\varphi(z)=f(z)/g(z)
and applying it to
f(z)
and
1/g(z)
, we obtain

\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)^{(n)}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}f^{(k)}\left(\frac{1}{g}\right)^{(n-k)}.

(3.2)

By Faa di Bruno’s formula
[
Joh02
]
, we know that

\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}f(g(x))=\sum\frac{n!}{m_{1}!1^{m_{1}}m_{2}!2^{m_{2}}\cdots m_{n}!(n!)^{m_{n}}}\cdot f^{(m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n})}(g(x))\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(g^{(j)}(x)\right)^{m_{j}}

where the sum is over all
n
-tuples
(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})
satisfying
\sum_{i=1}^{n}im_{i}=n
. Now using
f=\frac{1}{x}
, we obtain

\left(\frac{1}{g}\right)^{(n-k)}=\sum_{m_{1}+\cdots+(n-k)m_{n-k}=n-k}\frac{(-1)^{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n-k}}(n-k)!(m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n-k})!}{m_{1}!1!^{m_{1}}\cdots m_{n-k}!(n-k)!^{m_{n-k}}g^{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n-k}}}\prod_{j}\left(g^{(j)}\right)^{m_{j}}

(3.3)

We now give an upper bound on the
n^{th}
derivative of
\varphi(\alpha)
in terms of
h(\varphi)
and
h(\alpha)
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
K
be a number field and
\varphi(z)=\frac{f(z)}{g(z)}
a rational map defined over
K
of degree
d\geq 2
. Then for any place
v\in M_{K}
and
\alpha\in K
with
g(\alpha)\not=0
, we have

\log\left|\frac{1}{n!}\varphi^{(n)}(\alpha)\right|_{v}\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]nO_{d}(h(\varphi)+h(\alpha)+1).

Proof.

Applying the triangle inequality, we have

|f^{(k)}(\alpha)|_{v}\leq d\cdot\text{(max coeff. of }f^{(k)})\cdot\max\{1,|\alpha|\}^{d}

\implies\log|f^{(k)}(\alpha)|_{v}\leq(k+1)\log d+\log|f|_{v}+d\log^{+}|\alpha|_{v}.

(3.4)

Similarly we obtain such a bound for
|g^{(k)}(\alpha)|_{v}
. Next, we may bound

\frac{1}{|g(\alpha)|^{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n-k}}}\leq(m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n-k})[K:\mathbb{Q}]h(g(\alpha))\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]dnO(h(g)+h(z)+1)

where the last inequality follows from repeatedly applying Lemma
3.1
and that
m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n-k}\leq n
. Since
g^{(j)}=0
for
j>d
, we can bound

\log\left|\prod_{j}\left(g^{(j)}(\alpha)\right)^{m_{j}}\right|_{v}\leq n\left((d+1)\log d+\log|g|_{v}+d\log^{+}|\alpha|_{v}\right)

using (
3.4
). Now let’s assume that
v
is archimedean. We first note that

\sum_{m_{1}+\cdots+(n-k)m_{n-k}=n-k}\left|\frac{(n-k)!}{m_{1}!1!^{m_{1}}\cdots(n-k)!^{m_{n-k}}}\right|_{v}\leq p(n-k)\leq O(e^{\pi\frac{\sqrt{2n}}{3}})

where
p(x)
is the partition function, as this binomial sum is at most the number of ways to partition
n
into a sum natural numbers
[
Joh02
]
. Thus using (
3.3
) we get

\log\left|\left(\frac{1}{g}\right)^{(n-k)}(\alpha)\right|_{v}\leq\log|n!|_{v}+O\left(\pi\frac{\sqrt{2n}}{3}\right)+[K:\mathbb{Q}]nO_{d}(h(g)+h(z)+1).

Putting it together with (
3.3
) we obtain

\log\left|\frac{1}{n!}\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)^{(n)(\alpha)}\right|_{v}\leq\log\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\right|_{v}+\max_{k\leq n}\log\left|f^{(j)}(\alpha)\right|_{v}+\max_{k\leq n}\left|\left(\frac{1}{g}\right)^{(k)}(\alpha)\right|_{v}

\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]nO_{d}(h(\varphi)+h(\alpha)+1)

as desired. Now let
v
be non-archimedean. Then as

\frac{(n-k)!}{m_{1}!1!^{m_{1}}\cdots m_{n-k}!(n-k)!^{m_{n-k}}}

is an integer, we have

\log\left|\left(\frac{1}{g}\right)^{(n-k)}(\alpha)\right|_{v}\leq\log\left|\frac{1}{|g(\alpha)|^{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n-k}}}\right|_{v}+\sum_{j}\log\left|g^{(j)}(\alpha)\right|_{v}

\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]nO_{d}(h(g)+h(z)+1).

Then since
\log|n!|_{v}\leq\frac{n\log p}{p-1}
, it follows again that

\log\left|\frac{1}{n!}\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)^{(n)}(\alpha)\right|_{v}\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]nO_{d}(h(\varphi)+h(\alpha)+1)

as desired.
∎

We now show that if
k
is the smallest positive integer such that
\varphi^{(k)}(\alpha)
is non-zero, then it cannot be too small.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
K
be a number field and
\varphi(z)=\frac{f(z)}{g(z)}
a rational map defined over
K
of degree
d\geq 2
. Then for any place
v\in M_{K}
and
\alpha\in K
, if
k>0
is the smallest index for which
\varphi^{(k)}(\alpha)\not=0
, then

-\log\left|\frac{1}{k!}\varphi^{(k)}(\alpha)\right|_{v}\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]O_{d}(h(\varphi)+h(\alpha)+1).

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is a rational map of degree
d
, we have
k\leq d
. Next, we see from (
3.3
) that for
n\leq 2d-2
,
(1/g)^{(n)}(\alpha)
is a sum of at most
(2d-2)^{2d-2}
terms, with each term being a rational number with height at most
2h((2d-2)!)
multiplied by

\frac{1}{g(\alpha)^{m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n}}}\prod_{j}\left(g^{(j)}(\alpha)\right)^{m_{j}}.

Since
m_{1}+\cdots+m_{n}\leq 2d-2
, the height of each term is thus bounded by some constant
O_{d}(h(\varphi)+h(z)+1)
. Then using (
3.3
), we see that
\varphi^{(k)}(\alpha)
is a sum of
k\leq 2d-2
terms, each again with height
\leq O_{d}(h(\varphi)+h(\alpha)+1)
. Now since
\varphi^{(k)}(\alpha)\not=0
, this implies that

-\log\left|\frac{1}{k!}\varphi^{(k)}(\alpha)\right|_{v}\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]O_{d}(h(\varphi)+h(z)+1)

as desired.
∎

Using the two propositions, we prove that
|\varphi(z)-\varphi(\beta)|_{v}\approx|z-\beta|_{v}^{k}
when
|z-\beta|_{v}
is sufficiently small.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
K
be a number field and
\varphi
a rational map defined over
K
of degree
d\geq 2
. Then for any place
v\in M_{K}
, there exists constants
C_{2}=C_{2}(d)>0,C_{3}=C_{3}(d)>0
, both depending only on the degree
d
, such that if
z\in\mathbb{C}_{v}
satisfies

\log|z-\beta|_{v}\leq-C_{2}[K:\mathbb{Q}](h(\varphi)+h(\beta)+1),

then

\left|\log|\varphi(z)-\varphi(\beta)|_{v}-k\log|z-\beta|_{v}\right|\leq C_{3}[K:\mathbb{Q}](h(\varphi)+h(\beta)+1)

where
k
is the order of vanishing of
\beta
for the equation
\varphi(z)=\varphi(\beta)
.

Proof.

Let
B=[K:\mathbb{Q}](h(\varphi)+h(\beta)+1)
for convenience. We expand
\varphi(z)
at
z=\beta
to get

\varphi(z)=\varphi(\beta)+\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{(n)}(\beta)}{n!}(z-\beta)^{n}.

Now let
\log|z-\beta|_{v}=-CB
and let
A_{1},A_{2}
be constants for the
O_{d}(\cdot)
in Lemmas
3.6
and
3.7
respectively. Then we have

\left|\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi^{(n)}(\beta)}{n!}(z-\beta)^{n}\right|\leq\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}e^{-n(C-A_{1})B}\leq 2e^{-(k+1)(C-A_{1})B}

if
C-A_{1}>2
. On the other hand by Proposition
3.7
we get

e^{-(kC-A_{2})B}\geq\left|\frac{\varphi^{(k)}(\beta)}{k!}(z-\beta)^{k}\right|\geq e^{-(kC+A_{2})B}.

Thus if
C
is sufficiently large such that
4+kC+A_{2}<(k+1)(C-A_{1})
, we get

\frac{1}{2}e^{-(kC-A_{2})B}\leq|\varphi(z)-\varphi(\beta)|_{v}\leq 2e^{-(kC+A_{2})B}

\implies\left|\log|\varphi(z)-\varphi(\beta)|_{v}-k\log|z-\beta|_{v}\right|\leq A_{2}B+\log 2,

giving us the constants
C_{2},C_{3}
as desired.
∎

4.
A Distribution Formula

In this short section, we will prove an error bound for the difference

\log^{+}|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|^{-1}_{v}-\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-n}(\beta)}\log^{+}|\alpha-\beta_{i}|^{-1}_{v}.

Intuitively, if
\varphi^{n}(\alpha)
is close to
\beta
, then
\alpha
should be close to a root of
\varphi^{n}(z)=\beta
. Our error bound will be in terms of the degree
d
,
n,h(\varphi)
and
h(\beta)
and most importantly with no dependency on
\alpha
.

In
[
HS11
]
, Hsia–Silverman use a quantitative inverse function theorem to bound this error whereas we will use a distribution formula coming from
[
BR10
]
and
[
Oku13
]
, which allows us to control the dependency on the degree
d
too. We will be working over the Berkovich projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\operatorname{Berk},v}
. For background, one can refer to
[
BR10
]
or
[
Ben19
]
. Fix a number field
K
, a rational map
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
defined over
K
of degree
d\geq 2
along with a place
v\in M_{K}
. We will denote the equilibrium measure of
\varphi
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\operatorname{Berk},v}
by
\mu_{\varphi,v}
. It is a probability measure on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\operatorname{Berk},v}
satisfying

\varphi^{*}\mu_{\varphi,v}=d\cdot\mu_{\varphi,v}.

For a disc
\zeta=\zeta(a,r)
centered at
a
with radius
r
, we let

\langle x,y\rangle_{\zeta}=-\log\frac{|x-y|}{\max(r,|x-a|)\cdot\max(r,|y-a|)}.

This is a function on
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\operatorname{Berk},v}
satisfying

\triangle_{x}\langle x,y\rangle_{\zeta}=\delta_{\zeta}-\delta_{y},

where we understand
\delta_{\zeta}
as the delta mass on the Type II point for non-archimedean
v
, and the uniform probability measure on the boundary circle for archimedean
v
. Fix a disc
\zeta(a,r)
and now let
G_{\varphi}(x,y)=-\langle x,y\rangle_{\zeta(a,r)}-g(x)-g(y)
be an Arakelov-Green function where
g
is a continuous function satisfying
\triangle g=\mu_{\varphi,v}-\delta_{\zeta(a,r)}
. We normalize
g
such that
\iint G_{\varphi}(x,y)d\mu_{\varphi}(x)d\mu_{\varphi}(y)=0
. The following lemma appears in
[
BR10
]
and
[
Oku13
]
but we reproduce it here for convenience.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
g
is normalized. Then for any
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\operatorname{Berk},v}
, we have

\int G_{\varphi}(x,y)d\mu_{\varphi,v}(y)=0

Proof.

Let
U(x)=\int G_{\varphi}(x,y)d\mu_{\varphi,v}(y)
. Then

U(x)=\int G_{\varphi}(x,y)\triangle g-\int G_{\varphi}(x,y)\delta_{\zeta(a,r)}.

By self-adjointedness, we have

\int G_{\varphi}(x,y)\triangle g=\int g\triangle_{y}G_{\varphi}(x,y)=g(x)-\int g(y)d\mu_{\varphi,v}(y).

For the second term, expanding out
G_{\varphi}(x,y)
as
-\langle x,y\rangle_{\zeta(a,r)}-g(x)-g(y)
, we see that

\int(-g(x)-g(y))\delta_{\zeta(a,r)}(y)=-g(x)-\int g(y)\delta_{\zeta(a,r)}(y)

and so adding with
-\int G_{\varphi}(x,y)\triangle g
, we get a constant independent of
x
. We now verify that

\int\langle x,y\rangle_{\zeta(a,r)}\delta_{\zeta(a,r)}(y)

is independent of
x
. When
v
is non-archimedean this is just zero. When
v
is archimedean, this is equals to

\int_{C(a,r)}(\log|x-y|-\log\max(r,|x-a|)-\log\max(r,|y-a|))dy

where
C(a,r)
is the boundary of
D(a,r)
and we simply note that
\int_{C(a,r)}\log|x-y|dy=\log\max(r,|x-a|)
, causing the first two terms cancel out and leaving us a value independent of
x
. Thus
U(x)
is a constant, and since
\int U(x)d\mu_{\varphi,v}(x)=0
, this constant must be zero as desired.
∎

Proposition 4.2

(Theorem 10.18
[
BR10
]
, Lemma 3.1
[
Oku13
]
)
.

We have

G_{\varphi}(\varphi(x),y)=\sum_{y_{i}\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}G_{\varphi}(x,y_{i})

where
y_{i}
’s are counted with multiplicity.

Proof.

Taking Laplacians with respect to
y
, we obtain

\triangle G_{\varphi}(\varphi(x),y)=\varphi^{*}(\mu_{\varphi,v}-\delta_{y})=d\cdot\mu_{\varphi,v}-\sum_{y_{i}\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\delta_{y_{i}}

and

\triangle\sum_{y_{i}\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}G_{\varphi}(x,y_{i})=d\cdot\mu_{\varphi,v}-\sum_{y_{i}\in\varphi^{-1}(y)}\delta_{y_{i}}.

Thus both sides have the same Laplacians and they differ by a constant. To determine this constant, we integrate both sides with respect to
d\mu_{\varphi,v}(x)
. Since
\varphi_{*}d\mu_{\varphi,v}(x)=\mu_{\varphi,v}(x)
, we have

\int G_{\varphi}(\varphi(x),y)d\mu_{\varphi,v}(x)=\int G_{\varphi}(x,y)d\mu_{\varphi,v}(x)=0

by Lemma
4.1
and similarly
\int G_{\varphi}(x,y_{i})d\mu_{\varphi,v}(x)=0,
implying that they are equal.
∎

We apply our formula with
x=\alpha,y=\beta
and
\zeta=\zeta(\beta,1)
. This gives us

G_{\varphi}(\varphi^{n}(\alpha),\beta)=\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-n}(\beta)}G_{\varphi}(\alpha,\beta_{i})

\implies\langle\varphi^{n}(\alpha),\beta\rangle_{\zeta}-g(\varphi^{n}(\alpha))-g(\beta)=\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-n}(\beta)}(\langle\alpha,\beta_{i}\rangle_{\zeta}-g(\alpha)-g(\beta_{i})).

We next relate
\langle x,y\rangle_{\zeta}
with
\log|x-y|^{-1}_{v}
and bound
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}}g(x)
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For
\zeta=\zeta(\beta,1)
, we have

\langle\varphi^{n}(\alpha),\beta\rangle_{\zeta}=\log^{+}|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|^{-1}_{v},

\langle\alpha,\beta_{i}\rangle_{\zeta}=\log^{+}|\alpha-\beta_{i}|^{-1}_{v}+O(\log^{+}|\beta_{i}|_{v}+\log^{+}|\beta|_{v}+1).

Proof.

By definition, we have

\langle\varphi^{n}(\alpha),\beta\rangle_{\zeta}=-\log\frac{|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|_{v}}{\max(1,|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|_{v})}

which is
0
if
|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|_{v}>1
, and is
\log|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|_{v}^{-1}
otherwise, giving us
\log^{+}|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|_{v}
. Now for the second, we have

\langle\alpha,\beta_{i}\rangle_{\zeta}=-\log|\alpha-\beta_{i}|_{v}+\log^{+}|\alpha-\beta|+\log^{+}|\beta_{i}-\beta|_{v}.

If
|\alpha-\beta_{i}|_{v}<1
, then
|\alpha-\beta|_{v},|\beta_{i}-\beta|_{v}<|\beta_{i}|_{v}+|\beta|_{v}+1
, and so we are bounded away from
\log^{+}|\alpha-\beta_{i}|^{-1}_{v}
by
O(\log^{+}|\beta_{i}|_{v}+\log^{+}|\beta|_{v}+1)
. On the other hand, if
|\alpha-\beta_{i}|_{v}\geq 1
then
\log^{+}|\alpha-\beta_{i}|_{v}=0
. Since
||\alpha-\beta_{i}|_{v}-|\alpha-\beta|_{v}|\leq|\beta_{i}-\beta|_{v}
, it is clear that we have
|\langle\alpha,\beta_{i}\rangle_{\zeta}|\leq O(\log^{+}|\beta|_{v}+\log^{+}|\beta_{i}|_{v}+1)
as desired.
∎

We thus have an expression of our error in terms of the dynamical Green’s function
g(z)
. We now bound
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{v})}g(z)
using a result of Ingram
[
Ing22
]
. Let
F
be a homogeneous lift of
f
and let

G_{F}(P)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\log\|F^{k}(P)\|}{d^{k}}

be the homogeneous escape rate function for
F
where
P\in\mathbb{C}_{v}^{2}
.

Proposition 4.4

(Lemma 5,
[
Ing22
]
)
.

We have

\left|G_{F}(P)-\log\|P\|-\frac{1}{d-1}\log\|F\|\right|

=\frac{1}{d-1}\left(\log|\operatorname{Res}(F)|_{v}+d\log\|F\|+O(d\log d)\right)

for all
P\in\mathbb{C}_{v}^{2}
.

The function
G_{F}(P)-\log\|P\|
defines for us a dynamical green’s function
g
such that
\triangle g=\mu_{\varphi,v}-\delta_{\zeta(0,1)}
. To shift it to one whose Laplacian is
\mu_{\varphi,v}-\delta_{\zeta(\beta,1)}
, we subtract off
\log^{+}|x|-\log^{+}|x-\beta|_{v}
, which gives an error of at most
O(\log^{+}|\beta|_{v}+1)
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
g
be a dynamical Green’s function for
\zeta=\zeta(\beta,1)
which is normalized such that
\iint G_{\varphi}(x,y)d\mu_{\varphi}(x)d\mu_{\varphi}(y)=0
. Then

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{v})}|g(z)|\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]O(\log^{+}|\beta|_{v}+dh(\varphi)+d\log d).

Proof.

By Proposition
4.4
and Lemma
3.3
, we may choose a dynamical Green’s function
\tilde{g}
such that
\triangle\tilde{g}=\mu_{\varphi,v}-\delta_{\zeta(0,1)}
such that
\sup|\tilde{g}(z)|\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]O(dh(\varphi)+d\log d)
. We subtract off
\log^{+}|x|_{v}-\log^{+}|x-\beta|_{v}
to get
\tilde{g}_{\beta}
with
\triangle\tilde{g}_{\beta}=\mu_{\varphi,v}-\delta_{\zeta(\beta,1)}
. This changes our bound of the
\sup
by
O(\log^{+}|\beta|_{v})
. To normalize it, we have to subtract off

\left|\iint\langle x,y\rangle_{\zeta}-g^{\prime}(x)-g^{\prime}(y)d\mu_{\varphi}(x)d\mu_{\varphi}(y)\right|

which may be bounded by

\left|\iint\langle x,y\rangle_{\zeta}d\mu_{\varphi}(x)d\mu_{\varphi}(y)\right|+2\sup_{z\in\mathbb{P}^{1}}|\tilde{g}(z)|.

For any fixed
y
, let
\tilde{g}_{y}
be a dynamical Green’s function with
\triangle\tilde{g}_{y}=\mu_{\varphi,v}-\delta_{\zeta(y,1)}
. Then

\left|\int\langle x,y\rangle_{\zeta}(d\mu_{\varphi}(x)-\delta_{\zeta(y,1)})\right|=\left|\int\tilde{g}_{y}\triangle_{x}\langle x,y\rangle_{\zeta}\right|=|\tilde{g}_{y}(y)-\tilde{g}_{y}(\zeta(y,1))|\leq 2\sup_{z\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{v})}|\tilde{g}_{y}(z)|

\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]O(dh(\varphi)+d\log d+\log^{+}|y|_{v}).

Since

\int\langle x,y\rangle_{\zeta}\delta_{\zeta(y,1)}=\int\log^{+}|x-y|_{v}\delta_{\zeta(y,1)}=0,

we get

\left|\iint\langle x,y\rangle_{\zeta}d\mu_{\varphi}(x)d\mu_{\varphi}(y)\right|\leq[K:\mathbb{Q}]O\left(\int(dh(\varphi)+d\log d+\log^{+}|y|_{v})d\mu_{\varphi,v}(y)\right).

Finally, we have

\left|\int\log^{+}|y|_{v}(d\mu_{\varphi,v}(y)-\delta_{\zeta(0,1)})\right|=\left|\int\tilde{g}(\delta_{\zeta(0,1)}-\delta_{\infty})\right|\leq 2\sup_{z\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{v})}|\tilde{g}(z)|.

Putting everything together, if
g
is our normalized dynamical Green’s function for
\zeta(\beta,1)
, we obtain

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C}_{v})}|g(z)|=[K:\mathbb{Q}]O(\log^{+}|\beta|_{v}+dh(\varphi)+d\log d)

as desired.

∎

We will now prove the following error bound which will be crucial in our proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
K
be a number field and
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
a rational function of degree
d\geq 2
defined over
K
. Then there exists
C_{1}>0
such that for any place
v\in M_{K}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C}_{v}
, we have

\log^{+}|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|^{-1}_{v}-\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-n}(\beta)}\log^{+}|\alpha-\beta_{i}|^{-1}_{v}

\leq C_{1}d^{n+1}[K:\mathbb{Q}]\left(h(\varphi)+\log^{+}|\beta|_{v}+1\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-n}(\beta)}\log^{+}|\beta_{i}|_{v}\right).

Proof.

By Proposition
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
, we know that

\log^{+}|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|^{-1}_{v}-\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-n}(\beta)}\log^{+}|\alpha-\beta_{i}|^{-1}_{v}

=g(\varphi^{n}(\alpha))+g(\beta)-\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-n}(\beta)}\left(g(\alpha)+g(\beta_{i})+O(\log^{+}|\beta_{i}|_{v}+\log^{+}|\beta|_{v}+1)\right).

Now using Proposition
4.5
as an upper for
|g(z)|
, we obtain

\log^{+}|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|^{-1}_{v}-\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-n}(\beta)}\log^{+}|\alpha-\beta_{i}|^{-1}_{v}

=d^{n+1}[K:\mathbb{Q}]O\left(h(\varphi)+\log^{+}|\beta|_{v}+1+\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-n}(\beta)}\log^{+}|\beta_{i}|_{v}\right)

as desired.
∎

We remark that this can be viewed as a version of
[
HS11
, Proposition 7]
that has better control over the dependency on the degree
d
. Another difference is that our proposition works for all points of
\mathbb{C}_{v}
, instead of just those defined over
\overline{K}
.

5.
Diophantine Approximation Results

We will prove the Diophantine approximation theorems needed in this section. The first is a quantitative version of Roth’s theorem given by Gross in
[
Gro90
]
. We will state it only for the case of a single place
v
, instead of the more general version that deals with a sum over a finite set of places
S
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
K
be a number field and
v
a place of
K
. Let
\beta\in\overline{K}
be an element of degree
r>1
over
K
. Then there are most
4c_{1}
elements
x\in K
satisfying both

N_{v}\log|x-\beta|^{-1}_{v}\geq\frac{5}{2}h(x)\text{ and }h(x)\geq c_{2}\max\{h(\beta),1\}

where one can take

c_{1}=(2304\log r)^{3}\text{ and }c_{2}=28(4608\log r+1)!

Proof.

This is
[
Gro90
, Corollary]
with the same
c_{2}
, but a different
c_{1}
. Let
c_{1}
be as in
[
Gro90
, Corollary]
. Using the fact that
\log 5rn\leq n-1
where
n=\lfloor 2304\log r\rfloor+1
as defined in
[
Gro90
]
, we have the following inequality

c_{1}\leq 2304\log r+8.5(2304\log r)\left(\log(2304\log r)+\log((4608\log r)!)\right)

\leq 9(2304\log r)\left((4608\log r)\log(4608\log r)\right)\leq(2304\log r)^{3}

and so we may replace
c_{1}
with
(2304\log r)^{3}
.
∎

For the case of power and Lattès maps, we will prove a Thue–Siegel type result for our purposes. The first thing one needs is to be able to handle approximation of different values
\beta_{i}\in L
where
L
is a finite extension of
K
. Secondly, we will want to have only a bounded number of exceptions, independent on the degree
[L:K]
, and it will be necessary to let the approximation exponent depend on
[L:K]
, much like Thue–Siegel. We will mostly follow the proof given in
[
HS00
]
and modify it accordingly.

Fix the base number field
K
and an extension
L
of degree
r
, along with a place
v\in K
and an extension to
L
. We fix a positive integer
m
and let
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}
be elements of
L
which are not necessarily distinct. As in the proof of Roth’s theorem, for a polynomial
P\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, a tuple of elements
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})
and a tuple of positive integers
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})
, we define the index of
P
with respect to
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m},r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})
to be the smallest value of

\frac{i_{1}}{r_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{i_{m}}{r_{m}}

for which
\partial_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}P(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})\not=0,
where

\partial_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})=\frac{\partial^{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{m}}}{\partial x_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{m}^{i_{m}}}P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}).

We also let

A(t)=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}):\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}\leq t\text{ and }0\leq x_{j}\leq 1\}

and define
V(t)
to be the volume of
A(t)
. We now state a lemma from Bombieri–van-Poorten.

Lemma 5.2

(Lemma 1,
[
BvdP88
]
)
.

Suppose
t>0
and
rV(t)<1
. There exists a constant
C^{\prime}>0
, such that for all sufficiently large positive integers
r_{1},\ldots,r_{m}
, there is a polynomial
P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
of multi-degree at most
D=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})
and not identically zero such that the index of
P
with respect to

(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m},r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})
is larger than
t
and

h(P)\leq C^{\prime}\left(\frac{rV(t)}{1-rV(t)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(h(\beta_{i})+\log 2)r_{i}\right).

(5.1)

Although Bombieri–van-Poorten do not give an explicit constant
C^{\prime}
, we can deduce one from their proof. For
x\in\overline{K}
, we let
H(x)=e^{h(x)}
be the (non-logarithmic) Weil height.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}
and fix an
\epsilon>0
. Then if each
r_{i}
is sufficiently large, we may take
P
in Lemma
5.2
to satisfy

h(P)\leq(1+\epsilon)\frac{rV(t)}{1-rV(t)}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(h(\beta_{i})+\log 2)r_{i}.

Proof.

Fix an
\epsilon>0
. Following the proof of Lemma 1 in
[
BvdP88
]
, we are to give a lower bound on the size of integer solutions to a system of equations with
N=(r_{1}+1)\cdots(r_{m}+1)
unknowns and
M
equations with

(1-\epsilon)rV(t)r_{1}\cdots r_{m}\leq M\leq(1+\epsilon)rV(t)r_{1}\cdots r_{m},

if
r_{i}
’s are sufficiently large. The matrix of coefficients
A
has height

H(A)\leq\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}(r_{i}+1)(2H(\beta_{i}))^{r_{i}}\right)^{M}

and applying Theorem 1 of
[
BV83
]
, we obtain

H(P)\leq\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}(r_{i}+1)(2H(\beta_{i}))^{r_{i}}\right)^{M/(N-M)}

\implies h(P)\leq\frac{M}{N-M}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(h(\beta_{i})+\log 2)r_{i}+\log(r_{i}+1)\right)

\leq(1+\epsilon)\frac{rV(t)}{1-rV(t)}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(h(\beta_{i})+\log 2)r_{i}\right)

if
r_{i}
’s are all sufficiently large.
∎

We are interested in the regime where
m
is fixed and
r
goes to
\infty
. We set
V(t)=\frac{1}{2r}
which gives us
t

\undertilde{>}

r^{-1/m}
by Lemma 5 (ii) of
[
BvdP88
]
. We next state Roth’s lemma which helps us bound the index of
P
at a point
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})
.

Lemma 5.4

(Proposition D.6.2
[
HS00
]
)
.

Let
m
be a positive integer and
P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
of multi-degree at most
(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})
. Let
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})
be an
m
-tuple of algebraic numbers. Fix a real number
\eta>0
such that

\frac{r_{h+1}}{r_{h}}\leq\eta^{2^{m-1}}\text{ for all }1\leq h\leq m-1

and

\eta^{2^{m-1}}\min_{1\leq h\leq m}\{r_{h}h(\alpha_{h})\}\geq h(P)+2mr_{1}.

Then the index of
P
with respect to
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m},r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})
is less than
2m\eta
.

Now let
C>0
be a constant such that
t\geq\frac{1}{Cr^{1/m}}
for all
r
and
t
satisfying
V(t)=\frac{1}{2r}
. We will prove a version of Lemma D.5.3 in
[
HS00
]
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Given
(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m},r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})
, let
P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})
be a polynomial satisfying the conditions of Lemma
5.2
and
C^{\prime}
a constant for (
5.1
). Suppose
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}
satisfy

N_{v}\log|\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}|^{-1}_{v}\geq 4Cr^{1/m}h(\alpha_{i})\text{ for all }1\leq i\leq m

and

\frac{3}{4}\max\{r_{i}h(\alpha_{i})\}\leq\min\{r_{i}h(\alpha_{i})\},

h(\alpha_{i})\geq 2C^{\prime}(h(\beta_{i})+\log 2)+\log 4.

Then the index of
P
with respect to
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m},r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})
is at least
\frac{t}{2}
.

Proof.

Let
j=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})
be a
m
-tuple and let

\partial_{j}P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m})=\frac{1}{j_{1}!\cdots j_{m}!}\frac{\partial^{j_{1}+\cdots+j_{m}}}{\partial x_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots\partial x_{m}^{j_{m}}}P(x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}).

Then Lemma 5.D.2 of
[
HS00
]
tells us that

h(\partial_{j}P(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}))\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}\log 4+h(P)+\sum_{h=1}^{m}r_{h}h(\alpha_{h}).

Now let
T=\partial_{j}P
and let
(j_{1},\ldots,j_{m})
satisfy

\sum_{i=h}^{m}\frac{j_{h}}{r_{h}}\leq\frac{t}{2}.

Following Lemma 5.D.3 of
[
HS00
]
, we may expand using a Taylor series at
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m})
to obtain

N_{v}\log|T(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m})|_{v}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}\log 4+N_{v}\log|P|_{v}-4Cr^{1/m}\min_{\sum\frac{i_{h}}{r_{h}}\geq\frac{t}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}i_{h}h(\alpha_{h}).

On the other hand, since
N_{v}\log|T(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m})|_{v}\geq-h(T(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}))=-h(\partial_{j}(P(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}))),
we get

2\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}\log 4+2h(P)+\sum_{h=1}^{m}r_{h}h(\alpha_{h})\geq 2Cr^{1/m}\min_{\sum\frac{i_{h}}{r_{h}}\geq\frac{t}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}i_{h}h(\alpha_{h}).

Now Lemma
5.2
tells us that

h(P)\leq C^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(h(\beta_{i})+\log 2)r_{i}

and so we may bound

\min_{\sum\frac{i_{h}}{r_{h}}\geq\frac{t}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}i_{h}h(\alpha_{h})\geq\frac{t}{2}m\min\{r_{h}h(\alpha_{h})\}\geq\frac{3t}{8}\sum_{h=1}^{m}r_{h}h(\alpha_{h}),

we get

2\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}\log 4+C^{\prime}\sum_{i=1}^{m}(h(\beta_{i})+\log 2)r_{i}+\sum_{h=1}^{m}r_{h}h(\alpha_{h})

\geq 4Cr^{1/m}\frac{3}{8Cr^{1/m}}\sum_{h=1}^{m}r_{h}h(\alpha_{h})=\frac{3}{2}\sum_{h=1}^{m}r_{h}h(\alpha_{h}).

So

\sum_{i=1}^{m}r_{i}(4\log 4+2C^{\prime}(\log 2+h(\beta_{i}))-h(\alpha_{i}))\geq 0

which is a contradiction if
h(\alpha_{i})>2C^{\prime}(h(\beta_{i})+\log 2)+\log 4.

∎

We now put the above propositions together to obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
K
be a number field and
v\in M_{K}
a place of
K
along with an extension to
\overline{K}
. Fix
m>0
and let
\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}\in L
where
L
is an extension of
K
of degree
r
. Let
C
be a constant such that if
V(t)=\frac{1}{2r}
then
t\geq\frac{1}{Cr^{1/m}}
and
C^{\prime}
be a constant for the upper bound of (
5.1
). Then there do not exist
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\in K
satisfying

(1)

N_{v}\log|\alpha_{h}-\beta_{h}|^{-1}_{v}\geq 4Cr^{1/m}h(\alpha_{h})
for all
1\leq h\leq m
;

(2)

\frac{h(\alpha_{h+1})}{h(\alpha_{h})}\geq 2(4mCr^{1/m})^{2^{m-1}}
for all
1\leq h\leq m-1
;

(3)

h(\alpha_{1})\geq C^{\prime}(4mCr^{1/m})^{2^{m-1}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(h(\beta_{i})+1)+2m\right)
and

h(\alpha_{1})\geq 2C^{\prime}\left(\max\{h(\beta_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}+\log 2\right)+\log 4.

Proof.

We proceed as in Theorem D.7.1. of
[
HS00
]
. Assume for a contradiction that there exists
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\in K
satisfying all three conditions. We first choose
r_{1}
such that
r_{1}h(\alpha_{1})>3h(\alpha_{m})
and set

r_{h}=\left\lceil\frac{r_{1}h(\alpha_{1})}{h(\alpha_{h})}\right\rceil

so that
r_{1}h(\alpha_{1})
is the minimum among
r_{i}h(\alpha_{i})
. Then

r_{h}h(\alpha_{h})\leq r_{1}h(\alpha_{1})+h(\alpha_{h})\leq\frac{4}{3}r_{1}h(\alpha_{1}).

We similarly verify that

\frac{r_{h+1}}{r_{h}}\leq\frac{h(\alpha_{h})}{h(\alpha_{h+1})}+\frac{h(\alpha_{h})}{r_{1}h(\alpha_{1})}\leq\frac{2}{2(4mCr^{1/m})^{2^{m-1}}}=\frac{1}{(4mCr^{1/m})^{2^{m-1}}}.

We now want to apply Lemma
5.4
with
\eta=\frac{1}{4mCr^{1/m}}
. We need to ensure that

\frac{1}{(4mCr^{1/m})^{2^{m-1}}}r_{1}h(\alpha_{1})\geq C^{\prime}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}(h(\beta_{i})+\log 2)r_{i}\right)+2mr_{1}

which holds by condition (3). Hence if
P
is the polynomial from Lemma
5.2
, then Lemma
5.4
implies that the index of
P
with respect to
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m},r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})
is less than
2m\frac{1}{4mCr^{1/m}}=\frac{1}{2Cr^{1/m}}
. We can however apply Lemma
5.5
to conclude that the index is at least
\frac{1}{2Cr^{1/m}}
, which is a contradiction.
∎

6.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

We now begin the proof of Theorem
1.3
. Let
K
be a number field,
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
a rational map defined over
K
with degree
d\geq 2
. Let
S
be a finite set of places of
K
, including all archimedean places. We will assume
|S|>1
for convenience so that
\log|S|>0
. Recall that we want to show the existence of a constant
c_{4}=c_{4}(d)
, depending only on the degree
d
, such that for any non-preperiodic
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
and non-exceptional
\beta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
, we have

|\{n\geq 0\mid\varphi^{n}(\alpha)\text{ is S-integral relative to }\beta\}|\leq c_{4}|S|(\log|S|)^{6}+\log^{+}_{d}\left(\frac{h(\varphi)+1}{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)}\right).

Due to the use of
\log^{+}|x-\beta|^{-1}_{v}
as our local height function instead of the chordal metric
\lambda_{v}(x,\beta)
, we have to avoid the case where any of the points we will consider are
\infty
. If
\varphi^{n}(\alpha)=\infty
for some
n
, we simply ignore it and this will increase our number of
S
-integral points by one as
\alpha
is not periodic. Let
N
be the positive integer such that
d^{N-1}\leq 56\cdot 2^{2d-2}|S|<d^{N}
. We may also assume that
\infty\not\in\varphi^{-i}(\beta)
for all
1\leq i\leq N
as follows. Let
M
be a positive integer that is not in
\varphi^{-i}(\beta)
for any
i\leq N
. We then conjugate
\varphi
by
\psi=\frac{1}{x-M}
, repacing
\varphi
with
\psi^{-1}\circ\varphi\circ\psi
and
\alpha,\beta
with
\psi^{-1}(\alpha),\psi^{-1}(\beta)
.

Since the map
\frac{1}{x-M}
has good reduction for all primes
p
,
\varphi^{n}(\alpha)
being
S
-integral relative to
\beta
is equivalent to
\psi(\varphi^{n}(\psi^{-1}(\alpha)))
being
S
-integral relative to
\psi(\varphi^{n}(\psi^{-1}(\beta)))
. We may choose
M
such that

M\leq 56\cdot\sum_{i=0}^{N}d^{i}\leq 2^{2d-2}d^{2}|S|

and by Proposition 5(c) of
[
HS11
]
, this changes the height of
\varphi
by
O(d+\log_{d}|S|)
which only increases our upper bound on the number of
S
-integral points by
O(d)
as the canonical height of
\alpha
and
\beta
do not change. Henceforth, we may assume that
\infty\not\in\varphi^{-i}(\beta)
for all
1\leq i\leq N
.

We now make some preliminary reductions. For convenience, we will denote by
N_{S}
the set of
n
’s where
\varphi^{n}(\alpha)
is
S
-integral relative to
\beta
.

Lemma 6.1
.

It suffices to prove there exists a constant
c_{4}=c_{4}(d)
, such that

|N_{S}|\leq c_{4}|S|(\log|S|)^{6}

for all
\alpha
satisfying
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)\geq h(\varphi)+1
. Furthermore, we may assume that
h(\varphi^{n}(\alpha))\geq\frac{d^{n}}{4}h(\alpha)
.

Proof.

By replacing
\alpha
with an iterate
\varphi^{i}(\alpha)
, we may assume that our starting point
\alpha
satisfies
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)\geq h(\varphi)+1
. Note that it suffices to take
i\geq\log^{+}_{d}((h(\varphi)+1)/\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha))
. Let
C
be a constant from Proposition
3.2
such that

|\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(x)-h(x)|\leq C(dh(\varphi)+d\log d)\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\overline{K}).

Iterating
\alpha
by an additional
\log_{d}C+2
times, we may assume that
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)>2C(dh(\varphi)+d\log d)
and so
2\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)\geq h(\alpha)\geq\frac{1}{2}\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)
. The same inequality is also true for further iterates
\varphi^{n}(\alpha)
and so we have

h(\varphi^{n}(\alpha))\geq\frac{d^{n}}{2}\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)\implies h(\varphi^{n}(\alpha))\geq\frac{d^{n}}{4}h(\alpha)

as desired since
d\geq 2
.

∎

We now make a further reduction, which allows us to assume that the height of
\alpha
is at least that of
\beta
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Assume that
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)\geq h(\varphi)+1
and
h(\varphi^{n}(\alpha))\geq\frac{d^{n}}{4}h(\alpha)
. Let
m

and by Lemma

S

We now start the proof of Theorem

. We first handle the case where

Theorem 6.3
.

Assume that
\beta
is not post-critical. Then there exists a constant

N_{S}\leq c_{5}d|S|(\log|S|)^{6}+\log^{+}_{d}\left(\frac{h(\varphi)+1}{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)}\right).

Proof.

By Lemmas
6.1
and
6.2
, we may assume

at the cost of foregoing the
(h(\varphi)+1)/\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)
term. Let
N
be the unique positive integer such that
d^{N-1}\leq 56|S|<d^{N}
. If
\varphi^{n}(\alpha)

\sum_{v\in S}N_{v}\log^{+}|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|_{v}=h(\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta).

Following Hsia–Silverman, we now replace
\log^{+}|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|^{-1}_{v}
with
\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-N}(\beta)}\log^{+}|\alpha-\beta_{i}|^{-1}_{v}
to obtain the following bound.

Lemma 6.4
.

Assume that
\varphi^{n}(\alpha)
is
S
-integral relative to
\beta
. There exists a constant
n_{0}>0
such that if
n\geq n_{0}\log|S|
, we have the lower bound

\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-N}(\beta)}N_{v}\log^{+}|\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)-\beta_{i}|_{v}^{-1}\geq\frac{d^{n}}{6}h(\alpha).

We apply Proposition
4.6

\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-N}(\beta)}\log^{+}|\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)-\beta_{i}|_{v}^{-1}

\geq\log^{+}|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|_{v}^{-1}-C_{1}d^{N+1}[K:\mathbb{Q}]\left(h(\varphi)+\log^{+}|\beta|_{v}+1+\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-N}(\beta)}\log^{+}|\beta_{i}|_{v}\right).

Multiplying by
N_{v}
and summing up over

\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-n}(\beta)}N_{v}\log^{+}|\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)-\beta_{i}|_{v}^{-1}

\geq\sum_{v\in S}N_{v}\log^{+}|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|_{v}^{-1}

-C_{1}d^{N+1}[K:\mathbb{Q}]\left(|S|h(\varphi)+|S|+\sum_{v\in S}N_{v}\log^{+}|\beta|_{v}+\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-N}(\beta)}N_{v}\log^{+}|\beta_{i}|_{v}\right)

\geq h(\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta)-C_{1}d^{N+1}[K:\mathbb{Q}]\left(|S|h(\varphi)+|S|+h(\beta)+\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-N}(\beta)}h(\beta_{i})\right).

6.1
and
6.2

h(\varphi^{n}(\alpha))\geq\frac{d^{n}}{4}h(\alpha)

h(\alpha)\geq h(\varphi)+1,h(\beta)

is large enough, we have by Lemma
3.1

h(\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta)\geq h(\varphi^{n}(\alpha))-h(\beta)-\log 2\geq\frac{d^{n}}{5}h(\alpha).

By Proposition
3.2
, since
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\beta_{i})\leq\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\beta)

h(\beta_{i})\leq\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\beta_{i})+O(dh(\varphi)+d\log d)\leq h(\beta)+O(dh(\varphi)+d\log d).

(6.1)

As

, for a suitable constant

C_{1}d^{N+1}[K:\mathbb{Q}]\left(|S|h(\varphi)+|S|+h(\beta)+\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-N}(\beta)}h(\beta_{i})\right)

Since

and
n_{0}
is large enough, independent of
d
, we get
\frac{d^{n}}{100}h(\alpha)
is larger than our expression above and so

\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-N}(\beta)}N_{v}\log^{+}|\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)-\beta_{i}|^{-1}_{v}\geq\frac{d^{n}}{5}h(\alpha)-\frac{d^{n}}{100}h(\alpha)\geq\frac{d^{n}}{6}h(\alpha),

proving our lemma.
∎

For each
v\in S
, we let
\beta_{v,n}
be the element of
\varphi^{-N}(\beta)
such that the quantity
\log^{+}|\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)-\beta_{i}|^{-1}_{v}

Lemma 6.5
.

Assume that
\varphi^{n}(\alpha)
is
S
-integral relative to
\beta
. There exists a constant
n_{0}>0
such that if

, we have

\sum_{v\in S}N_{v}\log^{+}|\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)-\beta_{v,n}|_{v}^{-1}\geq\frac{d^{n}}{7}h(\alpha).

For any
\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-N}(\beta)

\beta_{v,n}
, we have

|\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)-\beta_{i}|_{v}+|\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)-\beta_{v,n}|_{v}\geq|\beta_{i}-\beta_{v,n}|_{v}\implies|\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)-\beta_{i}|_{v}\geq\frac{1}{2}|\beta_{i}-\beta_{v,n}|_{v}

\beta_{v,n}
. Thus

\log^{+}|\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)-\beta_{i}|^{-1}_{v}\leq\log 2+\log^{+}|\beta_{i}-\beta_{v,n}|_{v}^{-1}.

We now sum over to get

\sum_{v\in S}N_{v}\log^{+}|\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)-\beta_{i}|_{v}^{-1}\leq|S|\log 2+\sum_{v\in S}N_{v}\log^{+}|\beta_{i}-\beta_{v,n}|_{v}^{-1}.

Now each element of
\varphi^{-N}(\beta)

over

and so
\beta_{i}-\beta_{v,n}

Thus we obtain

\sum_{v\in S}N_{v}\log^{+}|\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)-\beta_{i}|^{-1}\leq|S|\log 2+(56d|S|)^{2}h(\beta-\beta_{v,n})\leq Cd^{2}|S|^{2}(h(\beta)+h(\varphi)+1)

3.2

). Hence by Lemma

\sum_{v\in S}N_{v}\log^{+}|\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)-\beta_{v,n}|_{v}^{-1}\geq\frac{d^{n}}{6}h(\alpha)-\sum_{v\in S}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\beta_{i}\in\varphi^{-N}(\beta)\\
\beta_{i}\not=\beta_{v,n}\end{subarray}}N_{v}\log^{+}|\varphi^{n-N}(\alpha)-\beta_{i}|_{v}^{-1}

\geq\frac{d^{n}}{6}h(\alpha)-C(56d|S|)(d^{2}|S|^{2})(h(\beta)+h(\varphi)+1)\geq\frac{d^{n}}{7}h(\alpha)

for

if we take

sufficiently large enough independent of

. This finishes the proof of our lemma.

.

. Fix a constant

We move onto the case where

Theorem 6.8
.

Assume that
\beta
is a superattracting periodic point that is non-exceptional. Then there exists a constant
c_{4}=c_{4}(d)>0
, depending only on the degree

We first make the following observation. If the cycle length of
\beta
is larger than
N
where

d^{N-1}\leq 56\cdot 2^{2d-2}|S|<d^{N},

then we may repeat the argument of
6.7
as the multiplicity of its

preimage is still
\leq 2^{2d-2}
. Hence we may assume that the cycle length of
\beta
is
\leq\log_{d}|S|+O(d)

\infty

As in Theorem
6.3
, we will let
N_{S}
denote the set of such
n

6.1
and
6.2
, it suffices to prove

be the constants from Proposition
3.8
and let

be the multiplicity of
\beta_{i}
in
\varphi(z)=\varphi(\beta_{i-1})

\beta_{0}=\beta
. We claim that if

\log|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|_{v}^{-1}\geq 3m\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}k_{i}\right)\max\{C_{2},C_{3}\}B,

then

\log|\varphi^{n+m}(\alpha)-\beta|_{v}^{-1}\geq\log|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta_{i}|_{v}^{-1}.

(6.3)

Assume first that
\log|\varphi^{n+i}(\alpha)-\varphi^{i}(\beta)|_{v}\leq-C_{2}B

for all

3.8
a total of

\left|\log|\varphi^{n+j}(\alpha)-\varphi^{j}(\beta)|_{v}-\left(\prod_{i=1}^{j}k_{i}\right)\log|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|_{v}\right|

This lets us verify that

\log|\varphi^{n+j}(\alpha)-\varphi^{j}(\beta)|_{v}\leq-C_{2}B

3.8
to

Thus if (

) holds, we get

\frac{1}{3}\log|\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta|_{v}^{-1}\geq mC_{3}B

for convenience, if for each

. As

, we have the inequalities

((d-1)^{m}+\frac{1}{d})^{(n^{\prime}-n)/m}\leq(d-\frac{1}{2})^{n^{\prime}-n}\leq d^{n^{\prime}-n}\left(1+\frac{1}{d-\frac{1}{2}}\right)^{-(n^{\prime}-n)}

\leq d^{n^{\prime}-n}\cdot\frac{d-\frac{1}{2}}{n^{\prime}-n}\leq\frac{d^{n^{\prime}-n}}{5}.

Hence we must have

3mde|S|\max\{C_{2},C_{3}\}B\geq\frac{d^{n^{\prime}-n}}{20}h(\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta).

But we have
m\leq\log_{d}|S|+O(d)
, and
n^{\prime}-n\geq 5d
, and so for
n\geq n_{0}\log|S|
and
n_{0}
sufficiently large, this gives a contradiction, proving our proposition.
∎

We now return to the proof of our theorem. Finally if we had more than
5dm|S|=O_{d}(|S|\log|S|)
many elements of
N_{S}
that are all
\geq n_{0}\log|S|
for
n_{0}
sufficiently large, there would be at least
|S|+1
of them which are all the same mod
m
and differ from each other by at least
5d
. Let them be
n_{1},\ldots,n_{|S|+1}
. Then

|Y_{n_{|S|+1}}|>|Y_{n_{|S|}}|>\cdots>|Y_{n_{1}}|\geq 1\implies|Y_{n_{|S|}+1}|>|S|

which is impossible since
Y_{i}\subseteq S
. This concludes the proof of our theorem.

∎

7.
The case of Power and Lattès maps

We will prove Theorem
1.2
in this section. In fact, we will prove what seems to be a more general statement.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
K
be a number field,
S
a finite set of places of
K
and
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
a rational map defined over
K
of degree
d\geq 2
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
be non-preperiodic and
\beta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
be non-exceptional. Assume that

\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)\geq h(\varphi)+1,h(\alpha)\geq h(\beta)\text{ and }h(\varphi^{n}(\alpha))\geq\frac{d^{n}}{4}h(\alpha).

Assume further that there exists positive integers
A,k>0
depending on
\varphi
such that
[K(\varphi^{-n}(\beta)):K]\leq Ad^{kn}
for all
n>0
. Then there exists
c_{3}=c_{3}(\varphi)>0
depending on
\varphi
such that

|\{n\mid\varphi^{n}(\alpha)\text{ is S-integral relative to }\beta\}|\leq k|S|+c_{3}(\log|S|+1).

Before we prove Theorem
7.1
, we will show how to deduce Theorem
1.2
from it.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
assuming Theorem
7.1
.

We first want to apply the reductions given by Lemmas
6.1
and
6.2
. Lemma
6.1
requires us to replace
\alpha
with an iterate
\varphi^{i}(\alpha)
where
i\geq\log^{+}_{d}((h(\varphi)+1)/\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha))
times. When
\varphi(z)=z^{d}
is a power map, the canonical height
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}
is exactly the Weil height
h
and a result of Dobrowolski
[
Dob79
]
implies that there exists a constant
c>0
, independent of the number field K, such that

\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)\geq\frac{c}{D(\log D)^{3}}\text{ for all non-preperiodic }\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(\overline{K})

where
D=[K:\mathbb{Q}]
. If
\varphi
is a Lattès map associated to an elliptic curve
E
with a projection
\pi:E\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, then
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\pi(x))=\frac{1}{2}\widehat{h}_{E}(x)
where
\widehat{h}_{E}
is the Neron-Tate height of the elliptic curve. We can then use a result of Masser
[
Mas89
]
which says that

\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)\geq\frac{c}{D^{3}(\log D)^{2}}\text{ for all non-preperiodic }\alpha.

Since
2|S|\geq D
as
S
contains all archimedean places, we have

\log^{+}_{d}\left(\frac{h(\varphi)+1}{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)}\right)=\log_{d}(|S|^{3}(\log|S|)^{2})+O_{\varphi}(1)=O_{\varphi}(\log_{d}|S|).

Thus by increasing
c_{3}
by a constant that depends on
\varphi
, we may apply Lemma
6.1
and assume that
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)\geq h(\varphi)+1
and
h(\varphi^{n}(\alpha))\geq\frac{d^{n}}{4}h(\alpha)
.

Now if
\varphi(z)=z^{2}
, then we know that
K(\varphi^{-n}(\beta))=K(\beta_{n},\omega_{d^{n}})
where
\beta_{n}
is any root of
\varphi^{n}(z)=\beta
and
\omega_{d^{n}}
is a primitive
d^{n}
th root of unity. This has degree at most
d^{2n}
and so we can apply Theorem
7.1
to conclude that

|\{n\mid\varphi^{n}(\alpha)\text{ is S-integral relative to}\beta\}|\leq 2|S|+c_{3}(\log|S|+1).

This proves Theorem
1.2
for power maps when
c_{3}
is allowed to depend on
\beta
as we can simply replace
\alpha
with
\varphi^{m}(\alpha)
where
h(\varphi^{m}(\alpha))\geq\beta
. Otherwise, by Proposition
6.2
we may replace
\alpha
with an iterate
\varphi^{m}(\alpha)
such that
h(\varphi^{m}(\alpha))\geq h(\beta)
and add an additional
|S|+2\log_{d}|S|+O(1)
number of points to our count of
S
-integers. This then gives us

|\{n\mid\varphi^{n}(\alpha)\text{ is S-integral relative to}\beta\}|\leq 3|S|+c_{3}(\log|S|+1),

which gives us the case where
c_{3}
is not allowed to depend on
\beta
. The case for Lattès maps is similar since any Galois action on
\varphi^{-n}(\beta)
maps injectively into
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/d^{n}\mathbb{Z})
which has size
O(d^{4n})
and so we may take
k=4
when applying Theorem
7.1
. This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.2

∎

We remark that although Theorem
7.1
appears to cover a larger family of rational maps for which the field extension

containing all elements of
\varphi^{-n}(\beta)
has at most degree

over

Proof of Theorem

.

. For each

Hence we are in a position to apply Theorem

since the third condition is satisfied if

for large enough

, depending on
A
and
k
. We now wish to show that we may find a place

Explicit Upper Bounds for

We now make our upper bounds in Section

\varphi(z)=z^{2}

which answers
[

. We first prove a simple version of Proposition
4.6
in our setting.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let

and
\varphi(z)=z^{2}
. Let

be a place for

\max_{y_{i}\in\varphi^{-n}(y)}\log|x-y_{i}|^{-1}_{v}\geq\log|\varphi^{n}(x)-y|^{-1}_{v}-h(y)-(2^{n}+n)\log 2.

Proof.

Fix a primitive
2^{n}
th root of unity

and let

be the elements of
\varphi^{-n}(y)
where

\log|x-y_{k}|^{-1}_{v}\geq\log|\varphi^{n}(x)-y|^{-1}_{v}-h(y)-(2^{n}+n)\log 2

We also state a version of Proposition

Lemma 8.2

Let

\left(\frac{2^{m_{i}}}{4|S|}-2\right)h(\alpha)\geq 2^{38}|S|^{8}-2\geq 2^{11}|S|^{3}.

Hence condition (1) is always satisfied.
∎

We now check when condition (2) holds.

Lemma 8.5
.

Assume that
m_{i}\geq 9\log_{2}|S|+40
. Then condition (2) holds for
\alpha_{i}=\varphi^{m_{i}-N}(\alpha)
.

Proof.

Let
m_{2}>m_{1}
. Then we have to verify that

\frac{h(\varphi^{m_{2}-N}(\alpha))}{h(\varphi^{m_{1}-N}(\alpha))}\geq 2(12\cdot 2^{2N/3})^{4}

and we check that

2(12\cdot 2^{2N/3})^{4}\leq 2\cdot 12^{4}|S|^{8}\cdot 2^{25}.

Hence we have to check that

m_{2}-m_{1}\geq 8\log_{2}|S|+41.

Assume otherwise. Then by Lemma
8.2
,

\log^{+}|\varphi^{m_{1}}(\alpha)-\beta|_{v}^{-1}\geq\left(\frac{2^{m_{1}}}{|S|}-2\right)h(\alpha)

\implies\log^{+}|\varphi^{m_{2}}(\alpha)-\varphi^{m_{2}-m_{1}}(\beta)|_{v}\geq\left(\frac{2^{m_{1}}}{|S|}-2\right)h(\alpha)-2^{m_{2}-m_{1}+1}(h(\beta)+2\log 2)

and so

\log^{+}|\varphi^{m_{2}-m_{1}}(\beta)-\beta|^{-1}_{v}\geq\left(\frac{2^{m_{1}}}{|S|}-2\right)h(\alpha)-2^{m_{2}-m_{1}+1}(h(\beta)+2\log 2)+\log 2.

But
h(\varphi^{m_{2}-m_{1}}(\beta)-\beta|^{-1}_{v}\leq(2^{m_{2}-m_{1}}+1)h(\beta)+\log 2
and so

(2^{m_{2}-m_{1}+2}+1)(h(\beta)+2\log 2)\geq\left(\frac{2^{m_{1}}}{|S|}-2\right)h(\alpha).

Since
m_{2}-m_{1}<8\log_{2}|S|+41
, if
m_{1}\geq 9\log_{2}|S|+44
we obtain

(2^{8\log_{2}|S|+43}+2)(h(\beta)+\log 2)+2\log 2\geq\left(\frac{2^{m_{1}}}{|S|}-2\right)h(\alpha)\geq(2^{8\log_{2}|S|+44}-2)h(\alpha).

This is a contradiction as
h(\alpha)\geq\max\{4h(\beta),8\}
.
∎

Finally we check condition (3).

Lemma 8.6
.

Assume that
m_{1}\geq 11\log_{2}|S|+45
. Then condition (3) holds for
\alpha_{1}=\varphi^{m_{1}-N}(\alpha)
.

Proof.

We need

h(\varphi^{m_{1}-N}(\alpha))\geq(12\sqrt[3]{3}2^{2N/3})^{4}(9+\sum_{i=1}^{3}h(\beta_{i})).

and

h(\varphi^{m_{1}-N}(\alpha))\geq 2(\max\{h(\beta_{i})\}_{i=1}^{3}+\log 2)+\log 4.

We bound from below
m_{1}-N\geq 8\log_{2}|S|+30
and
h(\beta_{i})\leq h(\beta)
and so using
2^{N}\leq 2^{10}|S|^{3}
and
90000\geq(12\sqrt[3]{3})^{4}
, the first inequality becomes

2^{8\log_{2}|S|+45}h(\alpha)\geq 2^{8\log_{2}|S|+80/3}(90000)(3)(h(\beta)+3)

which is true as
2^{45-80/3}\geq 270000
and
h(\alpha)\geq h(\beta)+3
. The second inequality is easily verified.
∎

Putting all three Lemmas together, we see that if we had

\varphi^{n}(\alpha)
is

-integral relative to
\beta

. Thus we can have at most

possible such

we get an upper bound of

as desired.

∎

When
|S|
is small, we can further optimize this bound. We first state a lemma from elementary number theory that will be useful.

Lemma 8.7
.

Let
p
be an odd prime and
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
p\mid a-b
. Then if
v
is the
p
-adic absolute value, we have

|a^{n}-b^{n}|_{v}=|a-b|_{v}\cdot|n|_{v}.

Proof.

This is a folklore result known as “Lifting the Exponent Lemma”. We provide a proof here for convenience. First, by factoring out
\gcd(a,b
), we may assume that
a,b
are coprime. If
n=p
, then if one lets
b=a+kp^{j}
where
j
is the highest power of
p
that divides
b-a
and
p\nmid k
, we have

b^{p}-a^{p}=(a+kp^{j})^{p}-a^{p}=kp^{j+1}\pmod{p^{j+2}}

and so
|b^{p}-a^{p}|_{v}=|b-a|_{v}\cdot|p|_{v}
. Now if
n=mp^{a}
where
p\nmid m
, by induction we have

|b^{n}-a^{n}|_{v}=|b^{m}-a^{m}|_{v}\cdot|p^{a}|_{v}.

It now suffices to show that
|b^{m}-a^{m}|_{v}=|b-a|_{v}
but

\frac{b^{m}-a^{m}}{b-a}=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}a^{i}b^{m-1-i}\equiv ma^{m-1}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}

since
p\nmid a,m
as desired.
∎

We now answer the following question of Hsia–Silverman
[

Theorem 8.8
.

We have

\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}}\left|\{(n,i,j)\in\mathbb{N}^{3}:y\cdot 2^{2^{n}}-x=3^{i}5^{j}\}\right|\leq 17.

\varphi^{n}(2)

\beta

Now assume

9.
Uniformity Statements

We will prove Theorem
1.4
in this section. We first show that the dependency of the upper bound in Theorem
1.3
on
h(\varphi)/\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)
is necessary.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
S=\{\infty\}
where
\infty
is the archimedean place of
\mathbb{Q}
. Then there exists a sequence of rational maps
(\varphi_{m})
of fixed degree
d\geq 2
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, with potentially good reduction at all finite places
v
, and sequence of points
(\alpha_{m}),(\beta_{m})
of
\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{Q})
such that

(1)

h(\varphi_{m})/h_{\varphi_{m}}(\alpha_{m})\to\infty,

(2)

There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\{n\geq 0\mid\varphi_{m}^{n}(\alpha_{m})\text{ is S-integral relative to }\beta_{m}\}\geq\log^{+}_{d}\left(\frac{h(\varphi_{m})+1}{h_{\varphi_{m}}(\alpha_{m})}\right)-C.

Proof.

Let
\varphi(z)=z^{2}+1
. Then
0
is not preperiodic. We will consider affine transformations
\psi_{m}=a_{m}z+1
with
a_{m}\in K
and set

\varphi_{m}=\psi_{m}\circ\varphi\circ\psi_{m}^{-1},\quad\alpha_{m}=\beta_{m}=\psi_{m}(0)=1.

Then
h_{\varphi_{m}}(\alpha_{m})=\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(0)
remains constant and
\varphi_{m}^{n}(\alpha_{m})=\psi_{m}(\varphi^{n}(0))
. Now consider the first
m
points on the orbit
\varphi(0),\varphi^{2}(0),\ldots,\varphi^{m}(0)
and choose
a_{m}
such that
|a_{m}\varphi^{i}(0)|_{v}\geq 1
for all
1\leq i\leq m
and finite places
v
. We claim that
\varphi_{m}^{i}(\alpha_{m})
is
S
-integral relative to
\beta_{m}
for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Indeed, since
\beta_{m}=1
, we have that
\varphi_{m}^{i}(\alpha_{m})
is not
S
-integral relative to
\beta_{m}
only if

|\varphi_{m}^{i}(\alpha_{m})-1|_{v}<1\implies|\psi_{m}(\varphi^{i}(0))-1|_{v}<1\implies|a_{m}\varphi^{i}(0)|_{v}<1

for some finite place
v
, which is not the case due to our choice of
a_{m}
. It suffices to estimate how large
h(a_{m})
is. We let
a_{m}
be the product of the numerators of each
\varphi^{i}(0)
for
1\leq i\leq m
. This gives us

h(a_{m})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}h(\varphi^{i}(0)).

Since there exists a constant
c>0
such that

h(\varphi^{i}(0))\leq 2^{i}\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(0)+c\text{ for all }i\in\mathbb{N},

this gives us

h(a_{m})\leq 2^{m+1}\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(0)+mc

and since Proposition 5(c) of
[
HS11
]
implies that

h(\varphi_{m})\leq h(\varphi)+4(2^{m+1}\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(0)+\log 8+mc)

and so taking
d=2
, we get

\log_{d}^{+}\left(\frac{h(\varphi_{m})+1}{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(0)}\right)\leq\log_{2}^{+}\left(\frac{4(2^{m+1}\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(0)+\log 8+mc)+1}{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(0)}\right)\leq m+C

for some constant
C>0
as desired.

∎

The fact that we cannot have a bound on the number of
S
-integral points in orbits that is uniform over all rational maps of degree
\leq d
has already been noted in
[
KLS+15
]
. They instead consider the question of
S
-units in an orbit, and conjecture that there a uniform bound should exist for all rational maps of degree
\leq d
. We now prove the existence of a uniform bound for all polynomials with bounded number of places of bad reduction.

We first state a theorem of Looper. For
\mathbf{c}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{d-1})
, set

f_{\bf{c}}=\frac{1}{d}z^{d}-\frac{1}{d-1}(c_{1}+\cdots+c_{d-1})z^{d-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{d-1}c_{1}c_{2}\cdots c_{d-1}z.

Theorem 9.2
.

[
Loo19
, Theorem 1.2]

Suppose
K
is a number field and
\mathbf{c}\in K^{d-1}
with
f_{\bf{c}}
having at most
s
places of bad reduction. Then there exists
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}>0
, depending only on
d,s,[K:\mathbb{Q}]
such that

h_{f_{\bf{c}}}(\alpha)\geq\kappa_{1}h(f_{\bf{c}})+\kappa_{2}

for all
\alpha\in K
that is not preperiodic.

Since every polynomial
f\in K[z]
is conjugate to
f_{\bf{c}}
for some
\bf{c}
, with the same number of places of bad reduction, we can conjugate
f
to
f_{\bf{c}}
and apply Theorem
1.3
to it. However, conjugating might change the
S
-integrality of our points. To overcome this, we use Hsia–Silverman’s notion of quasi-
(S,\epsilon)
-integrality.

Fix an
\epsilon
with
0\leq\epsilon\leq 1
. We say that
x\in K
is quasi-
(S,\epsilon)
-integral if

\sum_{v\in S}N_{v}\log^{+}|x|_{v}\geq\epsilon h(x).

For a rational map
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree
d\geq 2
defined over
K
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
, we set

\Gamma_{\varphi,S}(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon)=\{n\geq 0\mid(\varphi^{n}(\alpha)-\beta)^{-1}\text{ is quasi-}(S,\epsilon)\text{-integral}.\}

The following theorem is proven in
[
HS11
]
, with one modification to remove the dependency on
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\beta)
.

Theorem 9.3
.

Fix an
\epsilon
with
0\leq\epsilon\leq 1
. Assume that
\alpha
is not preperiodic and
\beta
is non-exceptional. Then there exists
\gamma=\gamma([K:\mathbb{Q}],d,\epsilon)
such that

|\Gamma_{\varphi,S}(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon)|\leq\gamma 4^{|S|}+\log^{+}_{d}\left(\frac{h(\varphi)+1}{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)}\right).

Proof.

Theorem 11(b) of
[
HS11
]
tells us that

|\Gamma_{\varphi,S}(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon)|\leq\gamma 4^{|S|}+\log^{+}_{d}\left(\frac{h(\varphi)+\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\beta)}{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)}\right).

However, applying the analogue of Lemmas
6.1
and
6.2
for quasi-
(S,\epsilon)
-integral points, we may assume that
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)\geq\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\beta)
and thus get rid of the term
\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\beta)
in the numerator, giving us

|\Gamma_{\varphi,S}(\alpha,\beta,\epsilon)|\leq\gamma 4^{|S|}+\log^{+}_{d}\left(\frac{h(\varphi)+1}{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)}\right)

as desired.
∎

We now prove our uniform bound of the number of

-units in our orbit for polynomials with at most

.

Let

. It thus suffices to bound the number of points that change from being not quasi-
(S,1/10)

-units after applying
\psi

\psi^{-1}(0)

Theorem 10.2
.

There exists a constant
c_{7}>0
such that the following holds: let
K
be a number field,
S
a finite set of places containing the archimedean places and
\varphi:\mathbb{P}^{1}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
a rational map defined over
K
of degree
d\geq 2
. Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{P}^{1}(K)
is a critical point of
\varphi(z)
that is not preperiodic. Then

\left|\{n\geq 0\mid\varphi^{n}(\alpha)\text{ is S-integral relative to }\alpha\}\right|\leq|S|+c_{7}\log|S|+\log^{+}d\left(\frac{h(\varphi)+1}{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)}\right).

Proof.

We first analyze the possible behavior of the recurrence of
\varphi^{n}(\alpha)
to
\alpha
. We know that
C_{\varphi,v}
serves as a Lipschitz constant for
\rho_{v}
. Let
n
be the smallest positive integer, if it exists, such that

\rho_{v}(\varphi^{n}(\alpha),\alpha)=\epsilon\leq(8C_{\varphi,v})^{-(n+1)}.

Then by Lemma
10.1
, we have

\rho_{v}(\varphi^{2n}(\alpha),\varphi^{n}(\alpha))\leq\frac{1}{2}C_{\varphi,v}^{n+1}\epsilon^{2}\implies\rho_{v}(\varphi^{2n}(\alpha),\alpha)\leq\epsilon+\frac{1}{2}C_{\varphi,v}^{n+1}\epsilon^{2}.

Let
C=\frac{1}{2}C_{\varphi,v}^{n+1}
. We claim that

\epsilon<\frac{1}{4C}\implies\rho_{v}(\varphi^{mn}(\alpha),\alpha)\leq\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\epsilon C}}{2C}

for all
m\geq 1
. Indeed, by induction, we have

\rho_{v}(\varphi^{(m+1)n}(\alpha),\varphi^{n}(\alpha))\leq C\left(\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\epsilon C}}{2C}\right)^{2}=\frac{2-4\epsilon C-2\sqrt{1-4\epsilon C}}{4C}

\implies\rho_{v}(\varphi^{(m+1)n}(\alpha),\alpha)\leq\epsilon+\frac{2-4\epsilon C-2\sqrt{1-4\epsilon C}}{4C}=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4\epsilon C}}{2C},

completing the induction. Then

\rho_{v}(\varphi^{mn}(\alpha),\varphi^{n}(\alpha))\leq\frac{2-4\epsilon C-2\sqrt{1-4\epsilon C}}{4C}\implies\rho_{v}(\varphi^{mn_{1}}(\alpha),\alpha)\geq\epsilon-\frac{2-4\epsilon C-2\sqrt{1-4\epsilon C}}{4C}.

We have the inequality
\sqrt{1-4\epsilon C}>1-3\epsilon C
for
\epsilon<\frac{1}{2C}
, and so

\epsilon-\frac{2-4\epsilon C-2\sqrt{1-4\epsilon C}}{4C}\geq\epsilon-\frac{2\epsilon C}{4C}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

In particular, we get

\rho_{v}(\varphi^{kn}(\alpha),\alpha)\geq\frac{1}{2}\rho_{v}(\varphi^{n}(\alpha),\alpha)

(10.1)

for all
k\geq 1
. We now study the distance between
\varphi^{kn+i}(\alpha)
and
\alpha
for
1\leq i<n
. Let
\rho_{v}(\varphi^{kn+i}(\alpha),\alpha)=\epsilon^{\prime}
for some
i
with
0<i<n
. Then

\rho_{v}(\varphi^{(k+1)n}(\alpha),\varphi^{n-i}(\alpha))\leq C_{\varphi,v}^{n-i}\epsilon^{\prime}

\implies\rho_{v}(\varphi^{n-i}(\alpha),\alpha)\leq\rho_{v}(\varphi^{(k+1)n}(\alpha),\alpha)+C_{\varphi,v}^{n-1}\epsilon^{\prime}\leq\frac{3}{2}\epsilon+C_{\varphi,v}^{n-1}\epsilon^{\prime}.

By minimality of
n
, we have
\rho_{v}(\varphi^{n-i}(\alpha),\alpha)\geq(8C_{\varphi,v})^{-n+i+1}
and so

C_{\varphi,v}^{n-1}\epsilon^{\prime}\geq(8C_{\varphi,v})^{-n+i+1}-\frac{3}{2}(8C_{\varphi,v})^{-(n+1)}\implies\epsilon^{\prime}\geq(9C_{\varphi,v})^{-2n+i}.

(10.2)

Now let
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
be the set of places
v
for which there exists
n\geq 0
such that
\rho_{v}(\varphi^{n}(\alpha),\alpha)\leq(8C_{\varphi,v})^{-(n+1)}
. For each
v\in S^{\prime}
, we let
n_{v}
be the minimal such
n
. Let
c>0
be a constant and let
M=c\log|S|+\log^{+}_{d}\left(\frac{h(\varphi)+1}{\widehat{h}_{\varphi}(\alpha)}\right)
. Then among
|S|+M+1
values of
n
for which
\varphi^{n}(\alpha)
is
S
-integral relative to
\alpha
, we can find one, say
m
, such that
m\not=n_{v}
for all
v\in S^{\prime}
and there are at least
M
other values of
n<m
that are not
=n_{v}
. Then

\sum_{v\in M_{K}}N_{v}\lambda_{v}(\varphi^{m}(\alpha),\alpha)=\sum_{v\in S}N_{v}\lambda_{v}(\varphi^{m}(\alpha),\alpha)

\leq\sum_{v\in S^{\prime}}N_{v}\lambda_{v}(\varphi^{m}(\alpha),\alpha)+\sum_{v\in S\setminus S^{\prime}}N_{v}\lambda_{v}(\varphi^{m}(\alpha),\alpha).

For

\lambda_{v}(\varphi^{m}(\alpha),\alpha)\leq\log(8C_{\varphi,v})^{m+1}=(m+1)\log(9C_{\varphi,v}).

For

\max\{\lambda_{v}(\varphi^{n_{v}}(\alpha),\alpha)+\log 2,\log(9C_{\varphi,v}^{3m})\}.

Hence

\sum_{v\in S^{\prime}}N_{v}\lambda_{v}(\varphi^{m}(\alpha),\alpha)\leq\sum_{v\in S^{\prime}}\left(N_{v}\lambda_{v}(\varphi^{n_{v}}(\alpha),\alpha)+\log 2+3m\log C_{\varphi,v}\right)

\leq 3m|S|C_{\varphi,v}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in S^{\prime}\\
n_{v}\text{ distinct }\end{subarray}}\left(h(\varphi^{n_{v}}(\alpha))+h(\alpha)\right)\leq 3m|S|C_{\varphi,v}+|S|h(\alpha)+\sum_{i=1}^{\min\{m,|S|\}-M}h(\varphi^{n_{v}-i}(\alpha)).

Using Proposition

\sum_{i=1}^{\min\{m,|S|\}-M}h(\varphi^{m-i}(\alpha))\leq+|S|O(dh(\varphi)+d\log d)+\sum_{i=1}^{\min\{m,|S|\}-M}d^{m-i}h(\alpha).

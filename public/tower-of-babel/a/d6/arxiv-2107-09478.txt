Abstract
. We extend the theory of Mackey 2-functors [ BD20 ] by defining the appropriate notion of rings, namely Green 2-functors . After providing the first results of our theory and abundant examples, we show how all classical Green functors familiar from representation theory and topology arise by decategorification, in various ways, of some Green 2-functor occurring in Nature.

1.
Introduction

Mackey 2-functors, introduced in
[
BD20
]

[
BD21b
]
, provide an axiomatic framework for the induction and restriction functors which are omnipresent in equivariant mathematics, at least in the case when the groups are
finite
and the categories
additive
.
Thus Mackey 2-functors categorify the Mackey (1-) functors commonly used in representation theory and topology, whose values are abelian groups.
A ring (
i.e.
monoid) in the category of Mackey functors is called a
Green functor
.
Examples abound, and certainly provide the classical theory of Mackey functors with much of its power; see
[
Web00
]

[
Lew80
]

[
Bou97
]
.

We would similarly expect the notion of
Green 2-functor
,
i.e.
of a Mackey 2-functor equipped with a compatible multiplicative structure, to be quite useful.
The goal of this paper is to pin down a precise and workable definition of Green 2-functor, to develop its basic theory, and to provide many examples.

Recall that, formally, a Mackey 2-functor is a contravariant 2-functor

\mathcal{M}\colon\mathsf{gpd}^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{ADD}

from the 2-category of finite groupoids to the 2-category of additive categories, satisfying a few reasonable axioms.
(Variations in the source and target of
\mathcal{M}
are possible,
e.g.
in order to consider Mackey 2-functors for a fixed group rather than global ones.)
In particular, we ask the “restriction” functor
i^{*}=\mathcal{M}(i)\colon\mathcal{M}(G)\to\mathcal{M}(H)
along every
faithful
morphism
i\colon H\to G
of groupoids (
e.g.
the inclusion of a subgroup
H\leq G
) to admit an “induction” functor
i_{*}\colon\mathcal{M}(H)\to\mathcal{M}(G)
which is both left and right adjoint to
i^{*}
. Both adjunctions are required to satisfy the base change formula, called
Mackey formula
in this context. By the Rectification Theorem, one can always choose the adjunctions
i_{*}\dashv i^{*}\dashv i_{*}
so that the two base-change maps are mutual inverses (
2.6
); this “strict” Mackey formula will be of crucial importance for the present paper.

We now define a
Green 2-functor
to be a Mackey 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
taking values in additive monoidal categories
\mathcal{M}(G)
and strong monoidal additive functors
u^{*}\colon\mathcal{M}(G)\to\mathcal{M}(H)
, which we require to “preserve inductions”.
In order to make the latter condition precise, we first express the tensor product as an external pairing
\mathcal{M}(G_{1})\otimes\mathcal{M}(G_{2})\to\mathcal{M}(G_{1}\times G_{2})
; then we require the latter to satisfy base change for all faithful functors (as in the Mackey formula) with respect to
both
variables
G_{1},G_{2}
; see
5.4
for details.
This is different from the analogous condition on the internal pairing
\mathcal{M}(G)\otimes\mathcal{M}(G)\to\mathcal{M}(G)
, and is similar to how
monoidal derivators
are defined (see
[
GPS14
]
). Compared to monoidal derivators, though, we must deal with the extra difficulty of working with tensor products of additive categories rather than just Cartesian products. This complicates definitions and has the consequence that the (term-wise) tensor product of two Mackey 2-functors is
not
a Mackey 2-functor (
4.7
). The latter fact turns out to be rather inconsequential, and the most basic theory of Green 2-functors closely parallels that of monoidal additive derivators.
Similarly, we will also easily define braided and symmetric Green 2-functors and modules over them.

Examples of Green 2-functors in our sense are plentiful throughout equivariant mathematics, as we will try to impress upon the reader in
Section

10
.
For instance: abelian and derived categories of group algebras in representation theory, equivariant stable homotopy categories in topology, categories of equivariant sheaves in geometry, and equivariant Kasparov categories in noncommutative geometry, all provide symmetric Green 2-functors.

*\;*\;*

Armed with the above definition we can now formulate our first results.

To begin with, Green 2-functors, unlike monoidal derivators (
cf.

6.10
), always satisfy projection formulas:

1.1 Theorem

(Projection formulas;
6.4
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Green 2-functor, and write
\otimes_{G}
for the internal tensor product of the category
\mathcal{M}(G)
.
For any faithful morphism
i\colon H\to G
, there are in
\mathcal{M}(G)
canonical natural isomorphisms

i_{*}(Y\otimes_{H}i^{*}X)\cong i_{*}(Y)\otimes_{G}X\quad\textrm{ and }\quad i_{*}(i^{*}X\otimes_{H}Y)\cong X\otimes_{G}i_{*}(Y)

for all
X\in\mathcal{M}(G)
and
Y\in\mathcal{M}(H)
.

More generally, this holds for any
bimorphism

(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})\to\mathcal{L}
between three a priori distinct Mackey functors,
i.e.
for any external pairing which preserves inductions in both variables (
5.3
).
For example, it also holds for the action on left and right modules over a Green 2-functor.
In fact we will show that a pairing between Mackey 2-functors is a bimorphism
if and only if
it satisfies the projection formulas (see
6.11
). This provides an alternative definition of a Green 2-functor: It is a Mackey 2-functor whose underlying 2-functor
(-)^{*}
takes values in monoidal additive categories and satisfies the projection formulas (
6.13
).

Let us stress that the projection formulas of
1.1
are extremely nice, because the maps are given by mate transformations of the monoidal structure with respect to
one
of the adjunctions, while their inverses are obtained by the corresponding mates for the
other
adjunction. This is analogous to, and a consequence of, the above-mentioned strict Mackey formula for the underlying Mackey 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
, and is an important ingredient for proving our next result.

For any Green 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
and faithful
i\colon H\to G
, we may consider the object

A(i):=i_{*}(\mathbb{1}_{H})\in\mathcal{M}(G)

induced from the unit
\mathbb{1}_{H}
of the tensor category
\mathcal{M}(H)
.
As restriction
i^{*}
is strong monoidal, its left-and-right adjoint
i_{*}
is both a lax and a colax monoidal functor, hence
A(i)
becomes a monoid and a comonoid. Even better:

1.2 Theorem

(Frobenius structure; Theorems
7.9
and
8.3
)
.

The object
A(i)
inherits a canonical structure of special Frobenius algebra in the tensor category
\mathcal{M}(G)
, which lifts to a commutative Frobenius algebra in the monoidal center of
\mathcal{M}(G)
.

In fact, even forgetting the tensor structure on
\mathcal{M}
, the ambijunction
i_{*}\dashv i^{*}\dashv i_{*}
always induces a Frobenius monad
\mathbb{M}(i):=i_{*}i^{*}
on
\mathcal{M}(G)
.
The point here is that the projection formula yields an isomorphism
\mathbb{M}(i)(X)\cong A(i)\otimes_{G}X
of (co)monads so well-behaved that it allows us to transfer all this structure onto the object
A(i)
; one can say the Frobenius monad
\mathbb{M}(i)
is
canonically represented
by the Frobenius algebra
A(i)
.
The second part of the theorem, mostly relevant if
\mathcal{M}
is not braided, says more precisely that the composite
A(i)\otimes_{G}X\cong i_{*}i^{*}X\cong X\otimes_{G}A(i)
of the two projection isomorphisms provides a
half-braiding
on
A(i)
which is compatible with all the structure on
A(i)
and also with any other half-braiding on any other object of
\mathcal{M}(G)
.
In short,
A(i)
is intrinsically commutative in the best possible way.

Morally, the latter results belong to the same school as the study of
relative dualising objects
of
[
BDS16
]
: Both theories study a certain class of adjunctions through the properties of a canonically associated object. (The two are also roughly complementary because, in the shared examples, we get a non-trivial Frobenius object
A(i)
precisely when the relative dualizing object
\omega_{i}
is trivial.
It is an interesting challenge to develop a framework encompassing both theories;
cf.

9.4
.)
Still in the same vein, the next “tensor monadicity” is a consequence of
1.2
and the fact that special Frobenius algebras are in particular separable (co)algebras:

1.3 Theorem

(Tensor monadicity;
9.2
)
.

Suppose the Green 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
takes values in additive monoidal categories which are idempotent complete.
Then for every faithful
i\colon H\to G
there are canonical equivalences between
\mathcal{M}(H)
and the Eilenberg–Moore categories of
A(i)
-modules and of
A(i)
-comodules in
\mathcal{M}(G)
:

A(i)\,\text{-}\Comod_{\mathcal{M}(G)}\overset{\sim}{\longleftarrow}\mathcal{M}(H)\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}A(i)\,\text{-}\Mod_{\mathcal{M}(G)}

If the Green 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
is braided, both are equivalences of braided monoidal categories, where (co)modules are endowed with the usual tensor product over
A(i)
.

This theorem refines the general separable monadicity of the underlying Mackey 2-functor (see
[
BD20
, §2.4]
).
It also recovers and improves the disparate results of
[
BDS15
]
under a unified and more conceptual proof.

Finally, let us explain how our Green 2-functors relate to ordinary Green 1-functors.
(Here we understand
ordinary
Mackey and Green functors in a very flexible way, covering all the usual variants in use for finite groups, including local and global ones, inflation functors, biset functors, etc.; see Definitions
11.1
and
11.2
.)
There are at least two ways in which we can “decategorify” a Mackey 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
in order to obtain an ordinary Mackey functor
M
.
Firstly, and going back to
[
Dre73
]
, we have the ordinary
K-decategorification
, where we postcompose
\mathcal{M}
with (some version of) the Grothendieck group
\mathrm{K}_{0}
in order to get
G\mapsto M(G)=\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathcal{M}(G))
; see
12.1
.
Secondly, we have the
Hom-decategorification
introduced in
[
BD21a
]
, where we choose pairs of objects
X_{G},Y_{G}\in\mathcal{M}(G)
for all
G
in a coherent way in order to get
G\mapsto M(G)=\Hom_{\mathcal{M}(G)}(X_{G},Y_{G})
; see
12.4
.

1.4 Theorem

(Decategorification; Theorems
12.5
,
12.6
and
12.7
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be any Mackey 2-functor. Then:

(a)

Every Green 2-functor structure on
\mathcal{M}
yields through K-decategorification an ordinary Green functor structure on
M\mapsto\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathcal{M}(G))
. (For this, assume also that each value category
\mathcal{M}(G)
is essentially small.)

(b)

Every coherent choice of objects with
X_{G}=Y_{G}
yields through Hom-decategorification an ordinary Green functor
G\mapsto\End_{\mathcal{M}(G)}(X_{G})
. In particular, if
\mathcal{M}
is a Green 2-functor we may take each
X_{G}=Y_{G}
to be the unit
\mathbb{1}_{G}\in\mathcal{M}(G)
.

(c)

Suppose
\mathcal{M}
is a Green 2-functor. Every coherent choice of objects
X_{G},Y_{G}
in the tensor category
\mathcal{M}(G)
together with a comonoid structure on
X_{G}
and a monoid structure on
Y_{G}
yields, through Hom-decategorification, a Green functor
M
where
M(G)=\Hom(X_{G},Y_{G})
has the induced convolution product.

Similar results hold for modules.
Other variants are possible, such as using higher algebraic K-theory if
\mathcal{M}
takes values in exact categories, or using graded Hom-groups if
\mathcal{M}
takes values in categories with a grading (
e.g.
because they are triangulated). See
Section

12
for details.

As listed at the end of the article, all classical examples of Green functors we can think of arise either as a K-decategorification or as a Hom-decategorification (most often at the tensor units) of some Green 2-functor occurring in Nature. Thus as it turns out, all these useful Green functors are mere shadows projected—at various angles—by richer, higher-dimensional structures.
Interestingly, some examples such as the Burnside ring and the complex representation ring are
both
a K- and a Hom-decategorification of (different) Green 2-functors.

2.
Mackey 2-functors

We use the language of 2-categories in a standard way, as recalled for instance in
[
BD20
, App. A]
.
In particular, “2-categories” and “2-functors” will always be understood to be strict, in contrast to “bicategories” and “pseudofunctors” (
i.e.
Bénabou’s homomorphisms).
Further recollections will be provided along the way.

2.1 Hypothesis
.

In this article,
(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{J})
will denote a
spannable pair
in the sense of
[
Del22
, Def. 2.31]
.
This means the following (see
loc. cit.
for more details):

(1)

\mathbb{G}
is an
essentially small (2,1)-category
,
i.e.
a 2-category whose Hom categories are small groupoids and which, up to equivalence, only has a small set of objects.

(2)

\mathbb{G}
is an
extensive
2-category: it admits arbitrary finite coproducts, and for any pair of objects
G,H
the induced pseudofunctor between comma 2-categories

\mathbb{G}/G\times\mathbb{G}/H\longrightarrow\mathbb{G}/(G\sqcup H),\quad(X\overset{u}{\to}G,Y\overset{v}{\to}H)\mapsto(X\sqcup Y\overset{u\sqcup v}{\longrightarrow}G\sqcup H)

is a biequivalence (
i.e.
an equivalence of bicategories).

(3)

\mathbb{J}
is a 2-full 2-subcategory of
\mathbb{G}
containing all equivalences (in particular, all identity 1-cells) and closed under taking isomorphic 1-cells.

(4)

Pseudopullbacks of 1-cells of
\mathbb{J}
exist and are again in
\mathbb{J}
. That is, if
i\colon H\to G
and
u\colon K\to G
are 1-cells with
i\in\mathbb{J}
then there exists a pseudopullback square

(2.2)

\textstyle{P\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{p}
\scriptstyle{q}
\scriptstyle{\underset{\scriptstyle\gamma}{\overset{\sim}{\rotatebox[origin]{90.0}{$\Downarrow$}}}}
\textstyle{H\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{i}
\textstyle{K\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{u}
\textstyle{G}

in
\mathbb{G}
(which we usually call a
Mackey square
;
cf.

[
BD20
, §2.1-2]
), and
q\in\mathbb{J}
.

(5)

For every finite set
\{u_{\ell}\colon H_{\ell}\to G\}_{\ell}
of 1-cells with common target, the induced 1-cell
(u_{\ell})_{\ell}\colon\sqcup_{\ell}H_{\ell}\to G
on the coproduct is in
\mathbb{J}
iff every
u_{\ell}
is.

These axioms guarantee that
2.4
below makes sense, and also that we may use
(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{J})
in

to form a suitable (bi)category of spans and define an associated variant notion of (ordinary) Mackey and Green functors.

2.3 Examples
.

The reader should keep the following examples in mind; in fact, in this article we will not look for applications beyond these. In all four cases, arbitrary Mackey squares exist and are provided by iso-comma squares of groupoids:

(a)

\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{J}=\mathsf{gpd}
is the 2-category of all finite groupoids, functors and natural transformations between them.

(b)

\mathbb{G}=\mathsf{gpd}
and
\mathbb{J}=\mathsf{gpd}{}^{\mathsf{f}}
is the 2-full 2-subcategory of finite groupoids and faithful functors.

(c)

\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{J}=\mathsf{gpd}{}^{\mathsf{f}}
.

(d)

\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{J}={\mathsf{gpd}{}^{\mathsf{f}}_{\!{}^{\scriptscriptstyle/}\!G}}
is the comma 2-category of finite groupoids and faithful functors over a fixed finite groupoid
G
. Note that there is a biequivalence
{\mathsf{gpd}{}^{\mathsf{f}}_{\!{}^{\scriptscriptstyle/}\!G}}\simeq G\textrm{-}\mathsf{set}
with the (1-)category of finite left
G
-sets, under which Mackey squares correspond to usual pullbacks of
G
-sets (see
[
BD20
, App. B]
).

We will always identify a group
G
with the one-object groupoid having
G
as automorphism group, and a group morphism with the corresponding functor.

2.4 Definition

(Mackey 2-functor
[
BD20
]
)
.

Suppose
\mathbb{A}
is some
additive 2-category
as in
[
BD20
, App. A.7]
; that is,
\mathbb{A}
is a (possibly very large
1
1

1

What we mean by
very large
is that the Hom categories of
\mathbb{A}
may not be essentially small,
i.e.
may have a proper class of objects, even when taken up to equivalence.
) 2-category admitting finite direct sums (
i.e.
biproducts) of objects, whose Homs are additive categories and whose horizontal compositions are additive functors of both variables.
Then an
\mathbb{A}
-valued Mackey 2-functor for
(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{J})
, or just

, is a 2-functor

\mathcal{M}\colon\mathbb{G}^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}

satisfying the following axioms:

(1)

Additivity:

\mathcal{M}
sends finite coproducts to direct sums,
\mathcal{M}(\sqcup_{\ell}G_{\ell})\overset{\sim}{\to}\oplus_{\ell}\mathcal{M}(G_{\ell})
.

(2)

Induction:
if
(i\colon H\to G)\in\mathbb{J}
, the 1-cell

admits in
\mathbb{A}
a left adjoint
i_{!}
and a right adjoint
i_{*}
.

(3)

Mackey formulas:
for every Mackey square (
2.2
) with
i,q\in\mathbb{J}
, the left mate
\gamma_{!}\colon q_{!}p^{*}\Rightarrow u^{*}i_{!}
and right mate
(\gamma^{-1})_{*}\colon u^{*}i_{*}\Rightarrow q_{*}p^{*}
are invertible 2-cells of
\mathbb{A}
.

(4)

Ambidexterity:
for every
i\in\mathbb{J}
, there is an isomorphism
i_{!}\simeq i_{*}
.

Whenever convenient, we will suppose the choices of
(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{J})
and
\mathbb{A}
to be understood and will omit them from our terminology and notations.

.

By the Rectification Theorem

3.
Linear categories

We briefly recall some generalities on linear categories.
Fix a commutative ring
\Bbbk
.

3.1 Construction
.

If
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
are two
\Bbbk
-linear categories (
i.e.
categories enriched in
\Bbbk
-modules), their
tensor product

\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{B}
is the
\Bbbk
-linear category with object-set
\Obj\mathcal{A}\times\Obj\mathcal{B}
, Hom spaces

(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{B})((A,B),(A^{\prime},B^{\prime})):=\mathcal{A}(A,A^{\prime})\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{B}(B,B^{\prime})
,
and composition
(f\otimes g)(f^{\prime}\otimes g^{\prime})=(ff^{\prime})\otimes(gg^{\prime})
.
The (plain) functor
\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{B}
which is the identity on objects and
(f,g)\mapsto f\otimes g
on maps is the
universal
\Bbbk
-bilinear
functor: It is
\Bbbk
-linear in both variables separately, and for every
\Bbbk
-linear category
\mathcal{C}
it induces an equivalence (in fact an isomorphism)

(3.2)

\Fun_{\Bbbk}(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\Fun_{\Bbbk\textrm{-bilin}}(\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C})

between the category of
\Bbbk
-linear functors on
\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{B}
and that of
\Bbbk
-bilinear functors on
\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}
(with morphisms on both sides provided by natural transformations).

If the
\Bbbk
-linear categories
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
are additive (
i.e.
admit all finite direct sums), there is no reason for their tensor product to be additive, and similarly with the property of being idempotent complete (
[
BD20
, Def. A.6.9]
).
(Note in particular that the coordinate-wise constructions do not suffice: In

Luckily, simple universal constructions let us always adjoin finite direct sums and split subobjects.
Denote by
\mathsf{CAT}_{\Bbbk}
the (very large) 2-category of all (non-necessarily small)
\Bbbk
-linear categories.

3.3 Construction

(Additive hull)
.

The fully faithful inclusion
\mathsf{ADD}_{\Bbbk}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{CAT}_{\Bbbk}
of the 2-subcategory of additive
\Bbbk
-linear categories is reflexive: There is an

as well as a family of
\Bbbk

\Fun_{\Bbbk}(\mathcal{C}^{\oplus},\mathcal{D})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\Fun_{\Bbbk}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})

for all additive

-linear categories

Concretely, the additive hull
\mathcal{C}^{\oplus}
has finite lists of objects of
\mathcal{C}
for its objects and the evident matrix spaces for its Hom
\Bbbk
-modules (see

[

, §2]
for details).

3.4 Construction

(Idempotent completion)
.

The inclusion
\mathsf{ADD}^{\mathsf{ic}}_{\Bbbk}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{ADD}_{\Bbbk}
of the 2-subcategory of idempotent complete categories is reflexive:
There is an
idempotent completion

(-)^{\natural}\colon\mathsf{ADD}_{\Bbbk}\to\mathsf{ADD}^{\mathsf{ic}}_{\Bbbk}
, as well as
\Bbbk
-linear functors
\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{D}^{\natural}
, pseudonatural in
\mathcal{D}\in\mathsf{ADD}_{\Bbbk}
, which induce equivalences

\Fun_{\Bbbk}(\mathcal{D}^{\natural},\mathcal{E})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\Fun_{\Bbbk}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})

for all idempotent complete additive
\Bbbk
-linear

Concretely, the objects of
\mathcal{D}^{\natural}
are objects of
\mathcal{D}
endowed with an idempotent

which absorb the idempotents on source and target (see

[

).

3.5 Remark
.

Constructions
3.1
-
3.4
preserve the smallness of categories, hence restrict as operations on the respective full 2-subcategories
\mathsf{Cat}_{\Bbbk}\subset\mathsf{CAT}_{\Bbbk}
,
\mathsf{Add}_{\Bbbk}\subset\mathsf{ADD}_{\Bbbk}
and
\mathsf{Add}^{\mathsf{ic}}_{\Bbbk}\subset\mathsf{ADD}^{\mathsf{ic}}_{\Bbbk}
of those objects which happen to be small categories.

3.6 Remark
.

Note that the idempotent completion
(-)^{\natural}
also makes sense for
\Bbbk
-linear categories which are not additive, and that it preserves the property of being additive.
Thus
(\mathcal{C}^{\oplus})^{\natural}
is always additive and idempotent complete; but this is not true of
(\mathcal{C}^{\natural})^{\oplus}
(just consider the algebra
\mathcal{C}=\Bbbk
).
Usually, we only consider the property of being idempotent complete for categories which are already additive.

3.7 Definition
.

We define the
tensor product
of two additive
\Bbbk
-linear categories
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
to be the additive hull
(\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{B})^{\oplus}\in\mathsf{ADD}_{\Bbbk}
, as in
3.3
, of the tensor product of
3.1
.
Similarly, we define the
tensor product
of two idempotent complete (additive)
\Bbbk
-linear categories
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
to be the idempotent completion

((\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\mathcal{B})^{\oplus})^{\natural}
as in
3.4
of the additive hull; it belongs to
\mathsf{ADD}^{\mathsf{ic}}_{\Bbbk}
by
3.6
.
In the following, we will simply write
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}
for all three tensor products, counting on the context to clarify which one is meant.

3.8 Remark
.

By combining the universal property (
3.2
) with that of the 2-reflections
(-)^{\oplus}
and
(-)^{\natural}
, we obtain analogous universal properties for the tensor product
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}
of additive, resp. idempotent complete additive,
\Bbbk
-linear categories. Namely, the canonical (plain) composite functor

\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}

is pseudonatural in

and induces an equivalence

\Fun_{\Bbbk}(\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\Fun_{\Bbbk\textrm{-bilin}}(\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C})

for all
\mathcal{C}\in\mathsf{ADD}_{\Bbbk}
, resp. all
\mathcal{C}\in\mathsf{ADD}^{\mathsf{ic}}_{\Bbbk}
.

3.10 Example
.

By specializing
3.1
and
3.7
to the case of the integers
\Bbbk=\mathbb{Z}

i.e.

\mathbb{Z}
-linear), additive, and idempotent complete additive categories.

The next proposition says that each of the above three tensor products defines a symmetric tensor structure on the corresponding 2-category.
More precisely, recall that a
symmetric monoidal bicategory
is by definition a one-object tricategory in the sense of
[
GPS95

equipped with a symmetry (see also
[

]

[

3.11 Proposition
.

Each of the (very large) 2-categories
\mathsf{CAT}_{\Bbbk}
,
\mathsf{ADD}_{\Bbbk}
and
\mathsf{ADD}_{\Bbbk}^{\mathsf{ic}}
becomes a symmetric monoidal 2-category, when equipped with the tensor product of

or

\mathsf{Cat}_{\Bbbk}
,
\mathsf{Add}_{\Bbbk}
and
\mathsf{Add}_{\Bbbk}^{\mathsf{ic}}

Proof.

This must be well-known to the experts, but let us sketch a proof as details are scarce in the literature.
Like any other bicategory with finite products, the (very large) 2-category

admits the Cartesian symmetric monoidal structure; see
[

, Thm. 2.15]
.
(In fact,

This means that we have two pseudofunctors

and

Then for

to

along (

) or (

), respectively, all the aforementioned structure.
It also follows from the uniqueness of the extended 1- and 2-cells that they still satisfy the numerous necessary coherence relations.
We leave the lengthy verifications to the reader.
To do this, it is wise to first upgrade the equivalences (

) to adjoint equivalences and to exploit that (

) is an isomorphism.
It also helps that the underlying bicategory

is strict.
∎

3.12 Remark
.

In the tensor 2-category

3.13 Remark
.

The tensor 2-categories

4.

\mathbb{1}\oplus\mathbb{1}\not\simeq\mathbb{1}
.
The underlying problem is that the tensor structure on
\mathbb{A}
we are using here is not the Cartesian one.

4.8 Definition
.

Suppose now that the spannable pair
(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{J})
is Cartesian (Hypotheses
2.1
and
2.10
).
An
external pairing
from
(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
to
\mathcal{L}
is a pseudonatural transformation
\boxdot\colon\mathcal{M}\boxtimes\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{L}(-\times-)
of 2-functors
\mathbb{G}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathbb{G}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbb{A}
. Together with modifications as morphisms, they form a category
\PsNat(\mathcal{M}\boxtimes\mathcal{N},\mathcal{L}(-\times-))
.

4.9 Remark
.

Concretely, an external pairing
\boxdot\colon(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})\to\mathcal{L}
consists of a family

\boxdot=\boxdot_{G_{1},G_{2}}\colon\mathcal{M}(G_{1})\otimes\mathcal{N}(G_{2})\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}(G_{1}\times G_{2})

of 1-morphisms of
\mathbb{A}
for all pairs of objects
G_{1},G_{2}\in\mathbb{G}
, together with a family
\boxdot=\boxdot_{u_{1},u_{2}}
of invertible 2-morphisms of
\mathbb{A}

\textstyle{\mathcal{M}(G_{1})\otimes\mathcal{N}(G_{2})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{u_{1}^{*}\otimes u_{2}^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\boxdot_{G_{1},G_{2}}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{L}(G_{1}\times G_{2})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{(u_{1}\times u_{2})^{*}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{M}(H_{1})\otimes\mathcal{N}(H_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\boxdot_{H_{1},H_{2}}}
\scriptstyle{{}^{\simeq}\rotatebox[origin]{-45.0}{$\Downarrow$}\;\boxdot_{u_{1},u_{2}}}
\textstyle{\mathcal{L}(H_{1}\times H_{2})}

for all pairs of 1-morphisms
u_{1}\colon H_{1}\to G_{1}
and
u_{2}\colon H_{2}\to G_{2}

This data is subject to the usual functoriality and naturality axioms (see
e.g.

[
BD20
, App. A]
).

4.10 Proposition
.

Let
(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{J})
be a Cartesian pair.
For any triple of 2-functors
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N},\mathcal{L}\colon\mathbb{G}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathbb{A}
, there is a canonical adjoint equivalence of categories

\PsNat(\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{N},\mathcal{L})\simeq\PsNat(\mathcal{M}\boxtimes\mathcal{N},\mathcal{L}(-\times-))\,.

4.11 Terminology
.

The pseudonatural transformation

corresponding to an external pairing
\boxdot\colon(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})\to\mathcal{L}
will be called
internal pairing
and will be typically denoted by
\odot
; and vice-versa. We will simply say
pairing
for either
\odot
or the corresponding
\boxdot
, as context will clarify.

Proof.

The proof is virtually identical to that of
[

, Thm. 7.7]
.
As in

The unit and counit are given (in

for clarity) by the diagonals

The claimed equivalence is between transformations of the form

5.3 Definition

(Bimorphism)
.

We will say that a pairing
\boxdot\colon(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})\to\mathcal{L}
of Mackey 2-functors is a

.

5.4 Definition

(Green 2-functor)
.

We define a
Green 2-functor
for
(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{J})
to be a pseudomonoid object
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{M},\odot^{\mathcal{M}},\mathbb{1}^{\mathcal{M}})
(
3.14
– here we mostly suppress the associators and unitors from notations) in the symmetric monoidal 2-category
\2Fun(\mathbb{G}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{ADD}_{\Bbbk}{}^{\!\!(\mathsf{ic})})
(
4.3
), satisfying two properties:

(1)

The underlying 2-functor
\mathcal{M}\colon\mathbb{G}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{ADD}_{\Bbbk}{}^{\!\!(\mathsf{ic})}
is a Mackey 2-functor for
(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{J})
.

(2)

The external pairing
\boxdot^{\mathcal{M}}\colon\mathcal{M}\boxtimes\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}(-\times-)
associated with the (internal) multiplication
\odot^{\mathcal{M}}\colon\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
, as in
4.10
, is a
\mathbb{J}
-bimorphism in the sense of
5.3
.

Thus in plain English, a Green 2-functor is a Mackey 2-functor which is also a pseudomonoid in 2-functors such that its multiplication preserves inductions in both variables.
We will speak of a
braided
or
symmetric
Green 2-functor if it is braided or symmetric as a pseudomonoid.

5.5 Remark
.

By
4.4
and
4.6
, the data of a Green 2-functor amounts to that of a 2-functor
\mathcal{M}\colon\mathbb{G}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\PsMon(\mathsf{ADD}_{\Bbbk}{}^{\!\!(\mathsf{ic})})
with values in monoidal (idempotent complete) additive
\Bbbk
-linear categories, strong monoidal
\Bbbk
-linear functors and monoidal transformations; after forgetting to
\mathsf{ADD}_{\Bbbk}{}^{\!\!(\mathsf{ic})}
, each
i^{*}=\mathcal{M}(i)
(
i\in\mathbb{J}
) admits a left-and-right adjoint
i_{!}=i_{*}
satisfying various nice properties.
Similarly, a braided (or symmetric) Green 2-functor corresponds to a 2-functor with values in braided (symmetric)
\Bbbk
-linear monoidal categories, braided strong monoidal
\Bbbk
-linear functors and braided monoidal transformations.

5.6 Remark
.

In the definition of a Green 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
, the use of the external pairing
\boxdot^{\mathcal{M}}
arises quite naturally because it is needed for the notion of bimorphism of Mackey 2-functors, which is a clear analog of the (one variable) morphisms used in
[
BD20
]
.
It is
a priori
not obvious how to translate the bimorphism condition in terms of the
internal
pairing,
i.e.
of the lifting to monoidal categories of
4.4
.
In the next section we will see that this translation is precisely (a specific form of) the left and right projection formulas. The resulting reformulation of Green 2-functors (
6.13
) also make sense for non-Cartesian spannable pairs.

9.4 Remark
.

In
[
BDS15
]
we considered families of categories
\mathcal{M}(G)
for quite general topological groups
G
, with precise hypotheses depending on the various examples; the tensor monadicity result, however, was (and can be) proved only for subgroups
H\leq G
with finite discrete quotient
G/H
.
Our formalism of Mackey and Green 2-functors is only meant to treat finite groups, but can easily handle 2-categories of topological groupoids in order to capture the full generality of these examples, as long as one finds suitable spannable pairs
(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{J})
; we leave the latter task to interested readers.
However, we feel that a better generalization to topological groupoids shouldn’t be such an “obvious” one but rather one which also usefully describes (by new axioms) the observed behavior of subgroups with
non-finite
quotient (
e.g.
phenomena such as the Wirthmüller isomorphisms of
[
BDS16
]
for the cases when
\omega_{f}\not\simeq\mathbb{1}
). This is an interesting problem that remains to be solved.

10.
Examples of Green 2-functors

Many of the examples of Mackey 2-functors treated in
[
BD20
, Ch. 4]
are in fact symmetric Green 2-functors. Let us stress that, in all cases, the multiplicative structure is well-known, as indeed was the case for the underlying structure of Mackey 2-functor:
Our present contribution consists in providing easily verifiable yet useful axioms for it. We now turn in this section to the “easily verifiable” part.
For simplicitly, we take
\Bbbk=\mathbb{Z}
and leave to the reader the straightforward adjustments needed to work over other rings
\Bbbk
, when applicable.

A first vast family of examples comes from derivators.
By an
additive monoidal derivator
we mean a monoidal derivator (
[
GPS14
, §7]
) whose underlying derivator is additive (
i.e.
it lands in
\mathsf{ADD}
as a 2-functor on
\mathsf{Cat}^{\mathrm{op}}
) and such that each (internal or external) component of the tensor product is an additive functor of both variables.

10.1 Theorem

(Additive monoidal derivators)
.

If
\mathcal{D}\colon\mathsf{Cat}^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathsf{ADD}
is an additive monoidal derivator, its restriction
\mathcal{M}:=\mathcal{D}|_{\mathsf{gpd}}
is a Green 2-functor defined on
(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{J})=(\mathsf{gpd};\mathsf{gpd}{}^{\mathsf{f}})
.
If
\mathcal{D}
is braided or symmetric then so is the Green 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
.
Similarly, if
\mathcal{E}
is an additive left or right
\mathcal{D}
-module, its restriction
\mathcal{N}:=\mathcal{E}|_{\mathsf{gpd}}
is a left or right
\mathcal{M}
-module.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{D}
is in particular an additive derivator, by
[
BD20
, Theorem 4.1.1]
its restriction
\mathcal{M}
to finite groupoids is a Mackey 2-functor for
(\mathsf{gpd};\mathsf{gpd}{}^{\mathsf{f}})
.
By definition, the multiplicative structure on the derivator
\mathcal{D}
consists of a pseudomonoid structure on
\mathcal{D}
seen as an object of the Cartesian 2-category
\2Fun(\mathsf{Cat}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{ADD})
of additive prederivators, such that its multiplication morphism is cocontinuous in both variables (
[
GPS14
, Def. 7.15]
).
Restricting along the inclusion
\mathsf{gpd}\hookrightarrow\mathsf{Cat}
,
\mathcal{M}
is a pseudomonoid in the 2-category
\2Fun(\mathsf{gpd}^{\mathrm{op}},\mathsf{ADD})
equipped with the
Cartesian
tensor structure.
However, being biadditive, the multiplication
\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
extends along the (termwise) universal biadditive
\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}\otimes\mathcal{M}
, where
\otimes
is the diagonal tensor product of additive categories (
3.10
and
4.3
).
The cocontinuity condition on
\mathcal{D}
specializes to the condition in
5.2
(
2
), so that we indeed obtain a Green 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
.
The discussion of braidings, symmetries and modules is similarly straightforward and left to the reader.
∎

10.2 Example

(Model categories)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a monoidal model category in the sense of
[
Hov99
]
.
Suppose the homotopy category
\Ho(\mathcal{C})
is additive, for instance because the model structure is stable.
Then there is an additive monoidal derivator
\mathcal{D}
whose value
\mathcal{D}(J)
is the homotopy category of
J
-shaped diagrams for the levelwise weak equivalences (
[
GPS14
, Ex. 7.19]
).
Hence
10.1
yields a Green 2-functor
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{D}|_{\mathsf{gpd}}
for
(\mathsf{gpd};\mathsf{gpd}{}^{\mathsf{f}})
.
If the monoidal model category
\mathcal{C}
is braided or symmetric then so is the Green 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
.

Among the many concrete examples of Green 2-functors that can be derived this way, we should at least mention the following ones, for which there are well-known symmetric monoidal stable Quillen model structures getting the job done.

10.3 Example

(Linear representations)
.

The usual Mackey 2-functors of linear representations over a field (or commutative ring)
\Bbbk
are symmetric Green 2-functors thanks to the tensor product over
\Bbbk
equipped with diagonal
G
-action.
Here
\mathcal{M}(G)
could be
e.g.
the abelian category
\Bbbk G\,\text{-}\Mod
of all representations (
[
BD20
, Ex. 4.1.4]
), or its derived category
\mathrm{D}(\Bbbk G)
(
[
BD20
, Ex. 4.1.5]
).
We can obtain more examples of Green 2-functors by restricting attention to full subcategories of the previous ones, as long as they are stable under restrictions, inductions and tensor products (which may depend on
\Bbbk
). For exemple: the category
\fgmod(\Bbbk G)
of finitely generated representations, or
\mathrm{perm}_{\Bbbk}(G)
of finitely generated permutation representations, or
\mathrm{proj}(\Bbbk G)
of finite projective modules, or the idempotent completion

If
\Bbbk
is not a field, we may want to consider the subcategory
\mathcal{M}(G)=\latt_{\Bbbk}(G)
of lattices,

those
\Bbbk G
-modules which are finitely generated projective over

.

10.4 Example

(Group ring spectra)
.

Let
\mathrm{Mod}_{R}
be a symmetric monoidal stable model category of modules over some commutative ring spectrum

(for any of the highly structured meanings of “commutative ring spectrum”).
By specializing

, we get a Green 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
such that
\mathcal{M}(G)=\Ho((\mathrm{Mod}_{R})^{G})=\Ho(\mathrm{Mod}_{RG})

When

i.e.

G
-diagrams of spectra.

We now turn to the many Green 2-functors not arising this way from derivators.
In some situations, we can take quotients of Green 2-functors:

10.5 Proposition
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Green 2-functor on any spannable pair

(

Let
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{M}

A levelwise additive quotient by a Mackey sub-2-functor inherits a unique structure of Mackey 2-functor by

.
Similarly, since each

∎

10.6 Example

(Stable module categories)
.

Consider the archetypical symmetric Green 2-functor
\mathcal{M}\colon G\mapsto\Bbbk G\,\text{-}\Mod

(

Now note that projective modules form a tensor ideal

(This example cannot arise directly from

10.7 Theorem

(Presheaf Green 2-functors)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a cocomplete abelian category.
Let
\mathcal{S}
be a Green 2-functor for
(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{J})
whose values
\mathcal{S}(G)
are essentially small additive categories.
Then there is a Green 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
for
(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{J})
such that
\mathcal{M}(G)=\Fun_{\mathrm{add}}(\mathcal{S}(G)^{\mathrm{op}},\mathcal{A})

\mathcal{S}

Proof.

The underlying Mackey 2-functor of

and the formula for induction

are provided by
[

and its proof (which are only formulated for

The tensor structure

\mathcal{M}(G)
by the (additive) Day convolution product; the strong monoidal structure on each restriction functor

by the (2-)universal property of the coend which defines Day convolution.
The corresponding external product

W must define the pairing and show that it preserves inductions.
We only sketch the construction, using the notations of

The tensor product
(X,\varphi)\otimes(Y,\psi)
of two objects

,
(Y,\psi)
of

has object-components
(X\otimes Y)_{p}:=X_{p}\otimes Y_{p}

\textstyle{(\varphi\otimes\psi)_{g}\colon X_{p}\otimes Y_{p}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{g}\otimes\psi_{g}}
\textstyle{g^{*}X_{q}\otimes g^{*}Y_{q}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{g^{*}(X_{q}\otimes Y_{q})}

for all

The tensor product of morphisms is similarly diagonal:
(\xi\otimes\zeta)_{p}=\xi_{p}\otimes\zeta_{p}

This defines a monoidal structure on the category
\Gamma((H,i_{H});\mathcal{S})

For every 1-morphism

Similarly, the natural isomorphism induced by a 2-cell

We thus obtain a 2-functor
\Gamma(-;\mathcal{S})\colon({\mathsf{gpd}{}^{\mathsf{f}}_{\!{}^{\scriptscriptstyle/}\!G}})^{{\mathrm{op}}}\to\PsMon(\mathsf{ADD})

By

We leave this lengthy but straightforward verification to the reader, who may compute using the explicit Kan formulas for the adjunctions (

M:=\Kadd\circ\tau_{1}(\widehat{\mathcal{M}})\colon\tau_{1}\Spanname(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{J})\to\tau_{1}\mathsf{Add}\to\Ab

is additive,

is an ordinary Mackey functor for
(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{J})
as in

.

12.2 Remark

(Variant K-decategorifications)
.

If the value-categories of
\mathcal{M}
are equipped with further structure, we may prefer to use a more pertinent version of
\mathrm{K}_{0}
-groups. For instance, if
\mathcal{M}
lands in exact (
e.g.
abelian) categories and exact functors, or in triangulated categories and triangulated functors, we could use
\Kex
or
\Ktr
respectively, the
\mathrm{K}_{0}
-group of exact or triangulated categories.

12.3 Remark

(Higher K-theory)
.

If
\mathcal{M}
takes values in exact categories, we may consider Quillen’s higher algebraic K-theory of exact categories
\mathcal{E}\mapsto\Kexhigher(\mathcal{E})=\pi_{*}\Omega BQ\mathcal{E}
.
Since the
Q
-construction
Q\mathcal{E}
is functorial for exact functors, and since isomorphic functors induce the same map on K-theory groups, precisely the same argument as in

yields a Mackey functor

.

12.4 Construction

(Hom-decategorification; see
[
BD21a
, §3]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Mackey 2-functor for
(\mathbb{G};\mathbb{J})
, and suppose
\{X_{G},Y_{G},\lambda_{u},\rho_{u}\}_{G,u}
is a given
coherent family of pairs of objects in

.
It consists of objects
X_{G},Y_{G}\in\mathcal{M}(G)
for

and identifications

Then there exists an ordinary Mackey functor

with value

at

12.9 Remark
.

If
\mathcal{M}
takes values in
\mathbb{Z}
-graded categories and grading-preserving functors, we have graded versions of all the Green functors and modules in Theorems
12.6
and
12.7
, with values given instead by the graded Homs:
G\mapsto M(G)=\Hom^{*}_{\mathcal{M}(G)}(X_{G},Y_{G})
.
For Green 2-functors with values in tensor triangulated categories one typically uses the grading induced by the suspension functor.

12.10 Example
.

In the local case
\mathbb{G}=\mathbb{J}={\mathsf{gpd}{}^{\mathsf{f}}_{\!{}^{\scriptscriptstyle/}\!G}}
for a fixed
G
, any pair of objects
X,Y\in\mathcal{M}(G)
in the “top” category gives rise to a coherent family of pairs simply by restriction:
X_{H}=u^{*}X_{G}
and
Y_{H}=u^{*}X_{H}
for all
u\colon H\to G
, with
\lambda=\rho=\mathrm{id}
.

12.11 Example
.

For any Green 2-functor
\mathcal{M}
, the tensor unit objects
X_{G}=Y_{G}=\mathbb{1}
form a coherent family of pairs thanks to the unit isomorphisms
u^{*}X_{G}\cong X_{H}
of the strong monoidal functors
u^{*}
.
The resulting Green functor
G\mapsto\End_{\mathcal{M}(G)}(\mathbb{1})
is commutative by the Eckmann–Hilton argument.
In case the categories carry tensor-compatible gradings as in
[
SA04
]
(
e.g.
when
\mathcal{M}
takes values in tensor triangulated categories), we obtain a
graded
commutative version
G\mapsto\End_{\mathcal{M}(G)}^{*}(\mathbb{1})
.

), we similarly obtain the Tate cohomology Green functor
G\mapsto\widehat{\mathrm{H}}^{*}(G;\Bbbk)
and Tate cohomology with any twisted coefficients as a (
G
-local) module over it.

12.15 Example

(The
\mathrm{G}_{0}
-ring)
.

If
\Bbbk
is a field (so that
-\otimes_{\Bbbk}-
is exact), consider the Green 2-functor
G\mapsto\mathrm{mod}(\Bbbk G)
of finite dimensional modules (
10.3
).
By K-decategorification via
\Kex
(see
12.2
), we get the Green 2-functor whose values are
\mathrm{G}_{0}(\Bbbk G)
, the G-theory group of the group algebras.

12.16 Example

(The trivial source ring)
.

The trivial source ring Green functor is the
\Kadd
-decategorification of the Green 2-functor
G\mapsto\mathrm{perm}_{\Bbbk}(G)^{\natural}
of trivial source modules (
10.3
). This is usually defined over a field
\Bbbk
of positive characteristic
p>0
, in which case such modules are known as
p
-permutation modules.

12.17 Example

(The representation ring)
.

For a general
\Bbbk
, the representation ring (or Green ring) Green functor
G\mapsto\mathrm{R}_{\Bbbk}(G)

is the
\Kadd
-decategorification of the Green 2-functor
G\mapsto\latt_{\Bbbk}(G)
of
\Bbbk G
-lattices (
10.3
).

12.18 Example

(Algebraic K-theory)
.

For a field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero, consider the Green 2-functor
G\mapsto\mathrm{proj}(\Bbbk G)=\mathrm{mod}(\Bbbk G)
of finitely generated (necessarily projective) modules (
10.3
; note that
-\otimes_{\Bbbk}-
is exact on
\Bbbk G
-projectives even if
\Bbbk
is not a field, but we also want a tensor unit!).
Via K-decategorification as in
12.3
, we obtain from it a (graded) Green functor
G\mapsto\mathrm{K}_{*}^{\mathrm{alg}}(\Bbbk G)
whose values are the algebraic K-theory groups of the group algebra, with multiplication induced by the Hopf algebra structure of
\Bbbk G
.

12.19 Example

(The Burnside ring)
.

The Burnside ring Green functor
G\mapsto\mathrm{B}(G)=\mathrm{K}_{0}(G\textrm{-}\mathsf{set},\sqcup,\times)
(denoted
A(G)
by topologists) arises in two ways. Firstly as the
\Kadd
-decategorification of the Green 2-functor of “representable” ordinary Mackey functors,
i.e.
the additive category of spans of finite
G
-sets
G\mapsto\mathbb{Z}\tau_{1}\Spanname(G\textrm{-}\mathsf{set})
(see
10.8
), since its K-ring is the same as for
G
-sets. Secondly as the Hom-decategorification at the tensor units of the stable homotopy Green 2-functor
G\mapsto\SH(G)
(
10.11
).
For any genuine
G
-spectrum
X\in\SH(G)
, the homotopy groups
H\mapsto\pi_{*}^{H}(X)=\SH(H)_{*}(\mathbb{1},X|_{H})
yield a graded Mackey functor for
G
,
i.e.
a graded module over the Burnside ring (for
G
).
The latter can all be realized this way
by the existence of equivariant Eilenberg–Mac Lane spectra.

12.20 Example

(The complex representation ring)
.

Similarly, the complex representation ring Green functor
G\mapsto\mathrm{R}_{\mathbb{C}}(G)
arises both as the K-decategorification of the Green 2-functor
G\mapsto\fgmod(\mathbb{C}G)
(as in Examples
12.15
or
12.17
with
\Bbbk=\mathbb{C}
), and as the Hom-decategorification at the tensor units of the Kasparov theory Green 2-functor
G\mapsto\mathrm{KK}(G)
(
10.12
). For any separable
G
-C*-algebra
A\in\mathrm{KK}(G)
, the equivariant K-theory groups
H\mapsto\mathrm{K}_{*}^{H}(A)=\mathrm{KK}(H)_{*}(\mathbb{1},A|_{H})
yield a graded Mackey functor for
G
which is a module over the representation ring. This recovers results of
[
Del14
, §4]
.

Nonsimple closed geodesics with given intersection number on hyperbolic surfaces
Wujie Shen

Abstract
The minimal length of closed geodesics among all finite-type hyperbolic surfaces with self-intersection number k k is 2 ​ cosh − 1 ⁡ ( 1 + 2 ​ k ) 2\cosh^{-1}(1+2k) for k k sufficiently large, i.e. k > 10 13350 k>10^{13350} by [ 11 ] . In the present paper we will give an another proof independent of [ 11 ] , and improve the lower bound k > 10 13350 k>10^{13350} to k > 1750 k>1750 .

Introduction

The study of nonsimple closed geodesics on hyperbolic surfaces plays an essential role on 2-dimensional hyperbolic geometry, spectral theory and Teichmüller theory. One interesting question is, a non-simple closed geodesic on a hyperbolic surface with self-intersection number
k
(called a
k
-geodesic
for brevity), when
k
goes to infinity, does the minimal length of
k
-geodesics goes to infinity, and what is the growth rate.

There has been lots of works on the problem till now. Hempel showed in
[
7
]
that a nonsimple closed geodesic has a universal lower bound
2\log(1+\sqrt{2})
and Yamada showed in
[
9
]
that
2\cosh^{-1}(3)=4\log(1+\sqrt{2})
is the best possible lower bound and is attained on a pair of pants with ideal punctures. Basmajian showed in
[
3
]
that a nonsimple closed geodesic has a similar
stable neighborhood
, and the length of a closed geodesic gets arbitrarily large as its self-intersection number gets large (
[
3
, Corollary 1.2]
). In
[
2
]
, Baribaud computed the minimal length of geodesics with given self-intersection number on pairs of pants.

It is interesting to study the minimal length of non-simple closed geodesics with respect to its self-intersection number
k
. Let
\omega
be a closed geodesic or a geodesic segment on a hyperbolic surface. Denote its length by
\ell(\omega)
and its self-intersection number by
\left|\omega\cap\omega\right|
.
\left|\omega\cap\omega\right|
counts the intersection points of
\omega
with multiplicity that an intersection point with
n
preimages contribute
\binom{n}{2}
to
\left|\omega\cap\omega\right|
.

On a fixed hyperbolic surface, Basmajian showed in
[
4
]
that a
k
-geodesic on a closed hyperbolic surface has length no less than
C\sqrt{k}
, where
C
is a constant depending only on the hyperbolic surface. When
k
sufficiently large(depending on the surface) Hanh Vo computed the minimal length of
k
-geodesics in a fixed hyperbolic surface with at least one cusp in
[
12
]
.

Define
I(k,X)
is the self-intersection number of the shortest geodesic with at least
k
self-intersections on a hyperbolic surface
X
, by definition
I(k,X)\geqslant k
. Erlandsson and Parlier showed in
[
5
]
that
I(k,X)
is bounded by a function depending only on
k
that grows linearly as
k
gets large. To the best of the author’s knowledge, there is not a single surface
X
for which the equality
I(k,X)=k
is known to hold for all
k
.

Let
M_{k}
be the infimum of lengths of geodesics of self-intersection number at least
k
among all finite-type hyperbolic surfaces, i.e. metric complete hyperbolic surfaces without boundary, and have finite number of genuses and cusps. Basmajian showed (
[
4
, Corollary 1.4]
) that

\tfrac{1}{2}\log\frac{k}{2}\leqslant M_{k}\leqslant 2\cosh^{-1}(2k+1)\asymp 2\log k

(1)

The notation
f(k)\asymp g(k)
means that
{f(k)}/{g(k)}
is bounded from above and below by positive constants.
Basmajian also showed that
M_{k}
is realized by a
k
-geodesic on some hyperbolic surface.

Conjecture 1.1
.

When
k\geqslant 1
,

M_{k}=2\cosh^{-1}(1+2k)=2\log(1+2k+2\sqrt{k^{2}+k})

(2)

and the equality holds when
\Gamma
is a corkscrew geodesic(See definition below) on a thrice-punctured sphere.

In
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
Shen-Wang improved the lower bound of
M_{k}
, that
M_{k}
has explicit growth rate
2\log{k}
, and for a closed geodesic of length
L
, the self intersection number is no more than
9L^{2}e^{\frac{L}{2}}
. The exact value for
M_{k}
for sufficiently large
k
is computed in
[
11
, Theorem 1.1]
:

Theorem 1.2
.

Conjecture 1.1 holds when
k>10^{13350}
.

In
[
11
]
, the authors first noticed that when the length of a
k
-geodesic is smallest, it must lies on a cusped hyperbolic surface. And by direct use of main result of
[
12
]
to finish the proof.

In the present paper we give a different proof and a better result:

Theorem 1.3
.

Conjecture 1.1 holds when
k>1750
.

We first give a thick-thin decomposition of the surface, and similar as
[
12
, Lemma 2.5]
we have a precise calculation of self-intersection number in the thin part. Since the injective radius on the thick part is bounded below, we can calculate the self-intersection in the thick part.

We give a quite better result since
1750<<10^{13350}

using computer programming, and prove

for all

Theorem

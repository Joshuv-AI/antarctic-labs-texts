Introduction

Given two nonempty subsets
A
and
B
of an additive group
G
,
A+B
is the Minkowski sum of
A
and
B
defined by

A+B=\{a+b,\,{with}\,a\in A,\,b\in B\}.

We will study the size of the sumset
A+B
in two cases: when the ambient group
G
is
{\mathbb{Z}}
in which case the size of a finite set
A\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
will be its cardinality and denoted by
|A|
and when
G={\mathbb{R}}
in which case the size of a bounded set
A
will be its inner Lebesgue measure
\lambda(A)
.

In additive combinatorics, studying the structure of
A
,
B
and
A+B
when
A+B
is small is an important topic.
Using that
\min(A)+B
and
\max(B)+A
are both included in
A+B
if
A
and
B
are finite sets of integers or closed and bounded sets of real numbers, we easily get that
|A+B|\geq|A|+|B|-1
when we deal with subsets of
{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\lambda(A+B)\geq\lambda(A)+\lambda(B)
when
A
and
B
are subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}
. The sum
A+B
being small means that its size is close to this lower bound.

When the ambient group is the set of integers
{\mathbb{Z}}
, Freiman’s theorem (see for example
[
TV06
]
) gives a description of finite sets
A
such that
|A+A|\leq C|A|
where
C
is a fixed constant. It asserts that
A
has to be ”efficiently” included in a generalized arithmetic progression of dimension bounded by a function depending on
C
. Many works are devoted to the search of the better bounds for both the dimension and the size of this generalized arithmetic progression in terms of
C
(see for example
[
Sch11
]
).

When
C
is very small (smaller than
3
), the so called Freiman
3k-4
theorem, proved by Freiman in the symmetric case
A=B

[
Fre59
]
,
[
Fre09a
]
and generalized to the sum of two distinct sets by Freiman
[
Fre62
]
, Lev and Smeliansky
[
LS95
]
, Stanchescu
[
Sta96
]
and Bardaji and Grynkiewicz
[
BG10
]
gives a sharp statement describing the structures of the sets
A
,
B
and
A+B
.
We state below a simplified version of this theorem.

Theorem 1

(Freiman
3k-4
)
.

Let
A
and
B
be finite nonempty subsets of
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Assume
|B|\leq|A|
and
|A+B|\leq|A|+2|B|-3-\delta
with

\delta=\begin{cases}1&\mbox{ if $A$ is a translate of $B$},\\
0&\mbox{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then there exist arithmetic progressions
P_{A}
,
P_{B}
and
P_{A+B}
of common difference such that

•

A\subset P_{A}
,
B\subset P_{B}
,
P_{A+B}\subset A+B
and

•

|P_{A}|\leq|A+B|-|B|+1
,
|P_{B}|\leq|A+B|-|A|+1
,
|P_{A+B}|\geq|A|+|B|-1
.

Theorem
1
implies that any nonempty finite subset
A
of
{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfying
|A+A|\leq 3|A|-4
(hence the denomination
3k-4
theorem, if
k
is the size of the set) is included in a genuine arithmetic progression
P_{A}
of size at most
|A+A|-|A|+1
. Freiman gave in
[
Fre09b
]
a structural description of critical sets
A
such that the smallest arithmetic progression
P_{A}
containing
A
has size exactly
|A+A|-|A|+1\leq 2|A|-3
. This description involves the notion of additively left-stable sets.

A bounded subset
X
of
{\mathbb{R}}
or
{\mathbb{Z}}
will be
additively left-stable
if
\inf(X)=0
and
(X+X)\cap[0,\sup(X)]=X
.

Freiman states that critical sets
A\subset{\mathbb{Z}}
such that the smallest arithmetic progression
P_{A}
containing
A
has size exactly
|A+A|-|A|+1\leq 2|A|-3
are a partition of three sets of the form
A=(\min(A)+A_{1})\cup P\cup(\max(A)-A_{2})
where
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
are additively left-stable sets and
P
is an arithmetic progression. In this paper we shall give the maximum density of left stable sets of integers on intervals
[0,x]
.

To this purpose, we use a theorem by Grynkiewicz which improves on Theorem
1
when we have some information on the ratio between the size of
A
and the size of
B
. From the weaker hypothesis
|A+B|\leq|A|+s|B|-2s
, with some
s
depending on the relative sizes of
A
and
B
, Grynkiewicz gets the conclusion
B\subset P_{B}
with
P_{B}
an arithmetic progression of size
|P_{B}|\leq|A+B|-|A|+1
.

Grynkiewicz’s theorem is actually a discrete version of a result stated by Ruzsa in 1991
[
Ruz91
]
.

Theorem 2

(Ruzsa)
.

Let
A
and
B
be two bounded subsets of
{\mathbb{R}}
of positive inner Lebesgue measure.
If

is the unique integer such that

\frac{K(K-1)}{2}\leq\frac{\lambda(A)}{\lambda(B)}<\frac{K(K+1)}{2},

then either

(1)

\lambda(A+B)\geq\lambda(A)+{\rm diam}(B)\,\mbox{
where }\,{\rm diam}(B)=\sup(B)-\inf(B)

or

(2)

\lambda(A+B)\geq(K+1)\left(\frac{\lambda(A)}{K}+\frac{\lambda(B)}{2}\right).

Remark 1
.

The inequality (

) implies
\lambda(A+B)\geq\lambda(A)+K\lambda(B)
.

In
[
dR18
]
, the second author deduced from this theorem a continuous analog of the

theorem (Theorem

), completely characterized the critical sets

and

such that equality holds in (

).
In the symmetric case

these last critical sets can be partitioned into three parts as follows

A=\min(A)+\left(A_{1}\cup I\cup({\rm diam}(A)-A_{2})\right)

where

is an interval of measure

and

and

are, up to a set of measure

, additively left-stable sets such that

The property of being additively left-stable forces the density of the set near

to be small. This statement will be made more precise in Theorem

where we find a function

such that if

, then for any

. We also prove that this upper bound is sharp and describe for any fixed

, a set

The section 2 of this paper will be devoted to the study of additively left stable subsets of real numbers.
Our results in the continuous setting will be extended to the discrete setting in section 3.

If

2.
Sumsets in
{\mathbb{R}}
, additively left stable sets

In this section, we shall write
\lambda
for the inner Lebesgue measure. The inner Lebesgue measure
\lambda(A)
of a set
A
is defined as the supremum of the Lebesgue measure of closed sets included in
A
. We use the inner measure to avoid any problem of measurability.

In Theorem 3 of
[
dR18
]
, the second author gave a description of critical sets for Ruzsa’s inequality (
1
). In the symmetric case, her result can be stated this way.

Theorem 3
.

Let
A
be a bounded closed set of real numbers of positive inner Lebesgue measure.

Assume that
\lambda(A+A)={\rm diam}(A)+\lambda(A)<3\lambda(A)
.

Then
A
can be partitioned into three parts as follows

A=\min(A)+\left(A_{1}\cup I\cup({\rm diam}(A)-A_{2})\right)

where

•

I
is an interval of measure
\lambda(I)=2\lambda(A)-{\rm diam}(A)
,

•

for
i=1,2
,
\min(A_{i})=0
,
\lambda(A_{i})=\frac{1}{2}{\rm diam}(A_{i})
and
(A_{i}+A_{i})\cap[0,{\rm diam}(A_{i})]=A_{i}
up to a set of measure
0
.

We shall say that a nonempty bounded subset
X
of
{\mathbb{R}}
is
additively left stable
if
\inf(X)=0
and
(X+X)\cap[0,{\rm diam}(X)]=X
. We say that
X

has density
1/2
if
\lambda(X)=\frac{1}{2}{\rm diam}(X)
.

Note that if
X
is an additively left-stable set of diameter
d
, then for any positive integer
k
, we have
kX\cap[0,d]=X
. If
\lambda(X)\not=d
(i.e. if
X
is not of full measure in an interval), this forces the density of the set near
0
to be small.
One of the goals of this section is to make this statement more precise.

Lemma 1
.

Let
A
be an additively left-stable bounded subset of real numbers.

We write
d={\rm diam}(A)
and for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
A_{x}=A\cap[0,x]
.

Then for any
x\in A
, for any integer
k\geq 2
such that
\lambda(A\cap[(k-1)x,d])<\lambda([(k-1)x,d])
, we have

\lambda(A_{x})\leq\begin{cases}\frac{2}{k+1}\left(x-\frac{d-\lambda(A)}{k}\right)&\mbox{ if }kx>d,\\
\frac{2}{k(k+1)}\lambda(A_{kx})&\mbox{ if }kx\leq d.\end{cases}.

Remark 2
.

The hypothesis
\lambda(A\cap[(k-1)x,d])<\lambda([(k-1)x,d])
implies
(k-1)x<d
.

Proof.

We can assume without loss of generality that

\begin{cases}\forall y\geq 0,&\lambda\left((A+A)_{y}\right)\leq\lambda(A^{\prime}_{y})\\
\forall y\in[0,d],&\lambda\left(A_{y}\right)=\lambda(A^{\prime}_{y}).\end{cases}

Let

For

Let

The inequality

Since

\displaystyle\lambda(A^{\prime}\cap[(\ell-1)x,(\ell+1)x])

\displaystyle=\lambda((A^{\prime}+A^{\prime})\cap[(\ell-1)x,(\ell+1)x])

\displaystyle\geq\lambda(0+(A^{\prime}\cap[(\ell-1)x,\ell x]))+\lambda(x+(A^{\prime}\cap[(\ell-1)x,\ell x]))=2x

We shall prove by induction on

This is obviously true for

Let

We proved that we must have

If

Otherwise,

∎

3.

{\mathbb{Z}}

In this section, we shall call

a set of consecutive integers and if

we shall write

. We shall say that a bounded set

and

). In the general case, arithmetic progressions replace intervals.

The symmetric version of Freiman’s
3k-4
theorem for sets
A
in normal form states that given a nonempty finite subset
A
of
{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
\min(A)=0
and

, the inequality
|A+A|\leq 3|A|-4
implies that

has diameter at most
|A+A|-|A|
and that

contains an interval of size at least

.

Freiman’s result can be reformulated as follows: given a nonempty finite subset
A
of
{\mathbb{Z}}
in normal form, we have

(5)

|A+A|\geq\min(3|A|-3,|A|+{\rm diam}(A)).

Freiman characterized in
[
Fre73
]
the sets

for which equality
|A+A|=3|A|-3
holds (see also
[
Jin15
]
for a corrected and more precise statement). He also gave in
[
Fre09b
]
a general description of sets

such that

. As in the continuous setting, this equality implies that
A
can be partitioned into three parts, more precisely that

A=A_{1}\cup I\cup({\rm diam}(A)-A_{2})

with

and
A_{1}
and

additively left-stable sets in normal form where an

is a subset

{\mathbb{Z}}

As in the continuous setting, we shall give an upper bound for

for additively left stable sets

Given

We will actually use the following weaker result.

Proposition 1

(Grynkiewicz)
.

Let

and

Proof.

Assume that
{\rm diam}(B)\geq|A+B|-|A|+1

Let
s
be defined by (
6

\displaystyle\geq|A|+s\left({|B|}-2\right)+1\geq|A|+s^{\prime}\left({|B|}-2\right)+1.

Using properties of the Fourier transform we prove that if a Hartree-Fock molecular spatial orbital is in L 1 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) L_{1}(\text{${\mathbb{R}}$}^{3}) , then it decays to zero as its argument diverges to infinity. The proof is rigorous, elementary and short. Our result implies that occupied orbitals with positive eigenvalues will decay to zero provided they are in L 1 L_{1} .

Introduction

We begin with a statement of the Hartree–Fock (or HF) equations, their importance, and the relevance of our result in Quantum Chemistry. These topics are studied in many introductory texts, such as

The HF equations are nonlinear eigenvalue equations of the form

(1)

\left[-\mbox{$\frac{1}{2}$}\nabla^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{Z_{j}}{|r-\xi_{j}|}\right]\psi_{a}(r)+2\sum_{c=1}^{n}\int|\psi_{c}(s)|^{2}\frac{1}{|r-s|}\,ds\,\psi_{a}(r)\\
-\sum_{c=1}^{n}\int\psi_{c}^{\ast}(s)\psi_{a}(s)\frac{1}{|r-s|}\,ds\,\psi_{c}(r)=\varepsilon_{a}\psi_{a}(r),\qquad a=1,\dots,n,

where
Z_{j}
is the atomic number of nucleus
N_{j}
, which is located at point
\xi_{j}\in\text{${\mathbb{R}}$}^{3}
and
\nabla^{2}
is the Laplace operator.
These equations apply only to systems with an even number
N
of electrons, and
n=N/2
.
Their solutions are called HF approximations; they are approximate solutions of the electronic Schrödinger equation and provide a gateway to more accurate approximations. The solution of (
1
) yields a sequence of functions
\psi_{a}
–called spin
orbitals – with orbital energies
\varepsilon_{a}
; they are continuous on
\text{${\mathbb{R}}$}^{3}
and
||\psi_{a}||_{\infty}\leq 1
, and are usually assumed to form an orthonormal set in
L_{2}
. The
N
spin orbitals with lowest energy are called
occupied
spin orbitals

The HF equations may also be used on ions. If an HF calculation is performed on a multiply charged anion, even some of the occupied orbitals
\psi_{a}
will have positive eigenvalues. This amounts to a prediction, using a theorem of Koopmans (1934), that the multiply charged anion will spontaneously lose electrons. Our result implies that the occupied orbitals with positive eigenvalues will still decay to zero provided they are in
L_{1}
.

Lemma 1

If

Theorem 1
.

Let
\psi_{a}\in L_{1}
; then
\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\psi_{a}(r)=0
.

Proof.

Equation (
1
) may be written in the equivalent form

(2)

\mathfrak{[}\nabla^{2}\psi_{a}](r)=g_{a}(r),

where

g_{a}(r)=-2\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{Z_{j}}{|r-\xi_{j}|}\psi_{a}(r)+4A\psi_{a}(r)-2\sum_{c=1}^{n}B_{a,c}\psi_{c}(r)-2\varepsilon_{a}\psi_{a}(r)

with

A=\sum_{c=1}^{n}\int|\psi_{c}(s)|^{2}\frac{1}{|r-s|}\,ds

and

B_{a,c}=\int\psi_{c}^{\ast}(s)\psi_{a}(s)\frac{1}{|r-s|}\,ds.

On the other hand, let
K
be arbitrary but fixed and such that
|\xi_{j}|\leq K,j=1,\dots,n
; then

\int|r-\xi_{j}|^{-2}|\psi_{a}(r)|^{2}\,dr=\\
\int_{|r|\leq K+1}|r-\xi_{j}|^{-2}|\psi_{a}(r)|^{2}\,dr+\int_{|r|>K+1}|r-\xi_{j}|^{-2}|\psi_{a}(r)|^{2}\,dr=J_{1}+J_{2}.

We have:

J_{1}\leq\int_{|r|\leq K+1}|r-\xi_{j}|^{-2}\,dr\leq\int_{|r|\leq K+1+|\xi_{j}|}|r|^{-2}\,dr=4\pi(K+1+|\xi_{j}|)

and, since
|r|>K+1
implies that
|r-\xi_{j}|\geq 1
,

J_{2}\leq\int_{|r|>K+1}|\psi_{a}(r)|^{2}\,dr\leq||\psi_{a}||^{2}_{2},

which implies that
|r-\xi_{j}|^{-1}\psi_{a}(r)
is in
L_{2}
for all
1\leq j\leq n
, which in turn implies that
g(r)\in L_{2}
.
But
\psi_{a}\in L_{1}
by hypothesis; thus

\mathfrak{[}\nabla^{2}\psi_{a}](r)=-4\pi^{2}|r|^{2}\widehat{\psi}_{a}(r).

Since
\psi_{a}
is bounded, it is in
L_{2}
as well (and therefore so is
\widehat{\psi}_{a}

2

(1+|r|^{2})\widehat{\psi}_{a}\in L_{2}

\widehat{\psi}_{a}(r)=\frac{1}{1+|r|^{2}}(1+|r|^{2})\widehat{\psi}_{a}(r)

\frac{1}{1+|r|^{2}}\in L_{2}

\widehat{\psi}_{a}\in L_{1}

\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}\mathfrak{F}[\widehat{\psi}_{a}](r)=0.

Since
\psi_{a}

\text{${\mathbb{R}}$}^{3}

\psi_{a}(r)=\mathfrak{F}[\widehat{\psi}_{a}](-r)

On extensions of D ⁡ ( 4 ) D(4) -triples by adjoining smaller elements
Marija Bliznac Trebješanin and Pavao Radić

Abstract
This paper examines the problem of obtaining a D ⁡ ( 4 ) D(4) -quadruple by adding a smaller element to a D ⁡ ( 4 ) D(4) -triple. We prove some relations between elements of observed hypothetical D ⁡ ( 4 ) D(4) -quadruples under which conjecture of the uniqueness of such an extension holds. Also, it is shown that for any D ⁡ ( 4 ) D(4) -triple there are at most two extensions with a smaller element.

m
-tuples, Pellian equations, Hypergeometric method.

Introduction

Let
n\neq 0
be an integer. We call a set of
m
distinct positive integers a
D(n)
-
m
-tuple, or
m
-tuple with the property
D(n)
, if the product of any two of its distinct elements increased by
n
is a perfect square.

One of the most frequently studied questions is how large these sets can be. In the classical case
n=1
, first studied by Diophantus, Dujella proved in
[
7
]
that a
D(1)
sextuple doesn’t exist and that there are at most finitely many quintuples. Over the years, many authors have improved the upper bound on the number of
D(1)
-quintuples. Finally, in
[
13
]
, He, Togbé and Ziegler proved the nonexistence of
D(1)
-quintuples.

Similar conjectures and observations can be made for the case
n=4
as for the case
n=1
.
In view of this observation, Filipin and the first author proved in
[
4
]
that a
D(4)
-quintuple also doesn’t exist.

In both cases,
n=1
and
n=4
, conjectures about the uniqueness of an extension of a triple to a quadruple with a larger element are still open.

If we observe a
D(4)
-triple
\{a,b,c\}
, then it can be extended with an element
d
greater than
\max\{a,b,c\}
, namely, we take

d=d_{+}(a,b,c)=a+b+c+\frac{1}{2}(abc+\sqrt{(ab+4)(ac+4)(bc+4)}).

A
D(4)
-quadruple
\{a,b,c,d_{+}(a,b,c)\}
is called a regular quadruple. If
\{a,b,c,d\}
is a
D(4)
-quadruple such that
d>\max\{a,b,c\}
and
d\neq d_{+}(a,b,c)
then it is called an irregular quadruple.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Any
D(4)
-triple
\{a,b,c\}
has a unique extension to a Diophantine quadruple
\{a,b,c,d\}
by an element
d>\max\{a,b,c\}
.

Results which support this conjecture can be found, for example, in
[
1
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
.

Besides extending a
D(4)
-triple
\{a,b,c\}
,
a<b<c
, to a
D(4)
-quadruple with an element
d_{+}
, we can define number

d=d_{-}(a,b,c)=a+b+c+\frac{1}{2}(abc-\sqrt{(ab+4)(ac+4)(bc+4)}),

which will give us another extension.
If
c\neq a+b+2r
then
0<d_{-}<c
(but
d_{-}
is not smaller than
a
). Notice that
c=d_{+}(a,b,d_{-}(a,b,c))
.

In the case of
D(1)
-triples, Cipu, Dujella and Fujita in
[
5

studied extensions of the triples by adjoining an element smaller than all elements of the given triple. Similarly, the first author in
[

initiated a study of the analogous problem in the case of

-triples.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Suppose that
\{a_{1},b,c,d\}
is a
D(4)
-quadruple with
a_{1}<b<c<d
. Then,
\{a_{2},b,c,d\}

-quadruple for any
integer

Its validity is checked in
[
2

and is also true for

as a consequence of
[
3
, Theorem 1.6]
.

The main result of this paper is to give some conditions under which hypothetical counterexamples of Conjecture

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
\{a_{1},b,c,d\}
and
\{a_{2},b,c,d\}
are
D(4)
-quadruples with
a_{1}<a_{2}<b<c<d

.

.

-triple

-quadruple with an element smaller than

Corollary 1.4
.

If
\{a_{1},b,c,d\}

\{a_{2},b,c,d\}

-quadruples,
a_{1}<a_{2}<b<c<d

-quadruples.

Also, it follows that there are at most two extensions of a

-triple

Corollary 1.5

Let

be a

-triple. Then, there exist at most two positive integers

-quadruple.

By combining Theorem

[

]
and

Corollary 1.6
.

There are only finitely many quintuples

Remark
.

For further research it would be interesting to establish some better bounds on elements, similar to those presented in
[
6
]
. The first step would be to prove a lower bound for
b
in the form of
b>ka_{2}
, where
k>1
. The key concept, observed in
[
6
]
, is to exclude solutions
c=c_{\nu}^{\pm}
when
b\leq ka_{2}
. The sequence
\{c_{\nu}^{\pm}\}_{\nu}
refers to a specific sequence of solutions to a Pellian equation that emerges in the problem of extending a
D(4)
-pair
\{a,b\}
to a
D(4)
-triple
\{a,b,c\}
. An explicit formula for this sequence can be found, for example, in
[
3
]
. However, in the case of
D(4)
-
m
-tuples, a similar approach cannot be employed since there exist multiple
c
values of this form which fall within the bounds established in this paper. Consequently, alternative methods need to be devised to either eliminate the possibility of such
c
values or demonstrate that
b>ka_{2}
for some
k>1
.

Angle-Domain Intelligent Reflecting Surface Systems: Design and Analysis
Xiaoling Hu, Caijun Zhong, and Zhaoyang Zhang

Abstract
This paper considers an angle-domain intelligent reflecting surface (IRS) system. We derive maximum likelihood (ML) estimators for the effective angles from the base station (BS) to the user and the effective angles of propagation from the IRS to the user. It is demonstrated that the accuracy of the estimated angles improves with the number of BS antennas. Also, deploying the IRS closer to the BS increases the accuracy of the estimated angle from the IRS to the user. Then, based on the estimated angles, we propose a joint optimization of BS beamforming and IRS beamforming, which achieves similar performance to two benchmark algorithms based on full CSI and the multiple signal classification (MUSIC) method respectively. Simulation results show that the optimized BS beam becomes more focused towards the IRS direction as the number of reflecting elements increases. Furthermore, we derive a closed-form approximation, upper bound and lower bound for the achievable rate. The analytical findings indicate that the achievable rate can be improved by increasing the number of BS antennas or reflecting elements. Specifically, the BS-user link and the BS-IRS-user link can obtain power gains of order N N and N ​ M 2 NM^{2} , respectively, where N N is the antenna number and M M is the number of reflecting elements.

All the prior works on IRS beamforming assumed that perfect channel state information (CSI) is available at both the BS and the IRS. How to obtain this CSI, however, is still a challenging issue for an IRS system due to the typically large number of reflecting elements and the passive architecture of the IRS.
The typical channel estimation scheme is to estimate the cascaded IRS-aided channel by using various reflection patterns to train all the reflecting elements

However, the channel training overhead of such a scheme would become prohibitively high as the number of reflecting elements increases.
Besides, since the CSI is estimated only at the transmitter and receiver, the IRS has no access to the CSI needed to perform beamforming.
To tackle this problem, the works

However, the realization of a receiving mode would lead to higher hardware complexity as well as more power consumption, and diminishes the main strengths of the IRS, namely its low complexity and cost.
To reduce the hardware cost, a semi-passive IRS architecture has been proposed

Motivated by these observations, in this paper, we propose an angle-domain IRS-aided system model, where effective angles are estimated by using a low-complexity method, and then exploited to design the BS beam and IRS phase shifts.

Like the work

As such, the channel estimation and the corresponding beamforming design are more efficient compared to those of the conventional IRS-assisted communication. However, for non-sparse channels where there are a large number of scatters in the radio frequency environment, the proposed angle-domain design framework is no longer suitable, since the angular information detection may also be complicated and time-consuming.

Although the angle-domain signal processing technique has been well studied in the existing works (e.g.,

The main advantages of this model are listed below:

•

Only a very limited number of angles, instead of a large-scale channel matrix corresponding to the IRS, are needed, thereby avoiding massive channel training overhead and high computational complexity.

•

Due to the above, only a very small amount of CSI needs to be communicated from the BS to the IRS. This allows the passive IRS to obtain CSI without adding too much hardware complexity, by linking it to the BS via a low-capacity hardware line. As such, the BS and the IRS can exchange information (CSI and phase shifts), and thus the joint optimization of BS beamforming and IRS beamforming can be realized.

The contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

•

An angle-based channel estimation scheme with extremely low complexity has been proposed. In particular, the maximum likelihood (ML) estimators for effective angles from the BS to the user are presented, based on which the effective angles from the IRS to the user are derived.

•

With a Gaussian model for the estimation error, we propose a joint optimization algorithm for BS beamforming and IRS beamforming, which achieves similar performance to two benchmark algorithms based on full CSI and the multiple signal classification (MUSIC) method respectively. Furthermore, we have proved mathematically that the average received signal power is non-decreasing after each iteration of the proposed algorithm.

•

The expression for the achievable rate is presented, based on which a closed-form approximation, an upper bound for the achievable rate are derived. The analytical findings show that the BS-user link can obtain a power gain proportional to the number of antennas
N

IV
Joint optimization of BS beamforming and IRS beamforming

Using the estimated angles, in this section we aim to maximize the average received signal power:

\displaystyle P_{\text{r}}=\mathbb{E}\left\{{\left|\left({\bf h}_{\text{B2U}}^{T}+{\bf h}_{\text{I2U}}^{T}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}}{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}\right){\bf w}\right|}^{2}\right\},

(37)

by jointly optimizing BS beamforming and IRS beamforming.

We first give the expression of the average received signal power.

Proposition 2

The average received signal power is given by

\displaystyle P_{\text{r}}={\bf w}^{H}{\bf T}{\bf w},

(38)

with

\displaystyle{\bf T}\triangleq{\beta}_{\text{B2I2U}}{\left({\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}}\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}\right)}^{H}{\bf B}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}}\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}+\sqrt{{\beta}_{\text{B2I2U}}{\beta}_{\text{B2U}}}\left({\left({\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}}\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}\right)}^{H}{\bf C}+{\bf C}^{H}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}}\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}\right)

(39)

\displaystyle+{\beta}_{\text{B2U}}{\bf A}+\sigma_{\text{NLOS}}^{2}{\bf I}_{N},

where

\displaystyle\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}\triangleq{\bf b}\left(\bar{\theta}_{\text{x-B2Ia}},\bar{\theta}_{\text{y-B2Ia}}\right){\bf a}^{T}\left(\bar{\theta}_{\text{x-B2I}},\bar{\theta}_{\text{y-B2I}}\right)

\displaystyle{\beta}_{\text{B2I2U}}\triangleq\frac{\alpha_{\text{I2U}}\alpha_{\text{B2I}}v_{\text{B2I}}v_{\text{I2U}}}{\left(v_{\text{B2I}}+1\right)\left(v_{\text{I2U}}+1\right)},

(41)

\displaystyle{\beta}_{\text{B2U}}\triangleq\frac{v_{\text{B2U}}\alpha_{\text{B2U}}}{v_{\text{B2U}}+1},

(42)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\text{NLOS}}^{2}\triangleq M\frac{\alpha_{\text{I2U}}\alpha_{\text{B2I}}}{v_{\text{B2I}}+1}\left(1+\frac{v_{\text{B2I}}}{v_{\text{I2U}}+1}\right)+\frac{\alpha_{\text{B2U}}}{v_{\text{B2U}}+1}.

(43)

The elements of
{\bf A}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}

{\bf B}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times M}

{\bf C}\in\mathbb{C}^{M\times N}

\displaystyle{\left[{\bf B}\right]}_{mn}={\left[{\bf\hat{B}}\right]}_{mn}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sigma_{\text{est}}^{2}\left\{{\left(i_{M,mn}\varphi_{1}+j_{M,mn}\varphi_{2}\right)}^{2}+{\left(j_{M,mn}\varphi_{2}+j_{M,mn}\varphi_{3}\right)}^{2}\right\}\right),

where

j_{M,mn}\triangleq\left(j_{M,n}-j_{M,m}\right)

{\bf\hat{C}}\triangleq{\bf b}^{*}\left(\hat{\bar{\theta}}_{\text{x-I2U}},\hat{\bar{\theta}}_{\text{y-I2U}}\right){\bf a}^{T}\left(\hat{\bar{\theta}}_{\text{x-B2U}},\hat{\bar{\theta}}_{\text{y-B2U}}\right)
.

Then the optimization problem can be formulated as:

\displaystyle{}\begin{array}[]{ll}\max\limits_{\left\{\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$},{\bf w}\right\}}&P_{r}={\bf w}^{H}{\bf T}{\bf w},\\
\operatorname{s.t.}&\begin{array}[t]{lll}{\left\|\bf w\right\|}^{2}\leq P_{BS},\\
\left|{\left[\bf\Theta\right]}_{ii}\right|=1,i=1,...,M,\end{array}\end{array}

where
P_{\text{BS}}
is the maximum transmit power at the BS.

Since the phase shift matrix
\Theta
and the BS beam
{\bf w}
are coupled, the above optimization problem (
IV
) is difficult to
solve. Responding to this, we will first optimize
{\bf w}
by fixing

\Theta
and then update
\Theta
by fixing
{\bf w}
respectively. As such, we can
obtain sub-optimal solutions for both
{\bf w}
and
\Theta
by performing
this successive refinement process iteratively.

IV-A

BS beamforming

By fixing the phase shift matrix
\Theta
, we can simplify (
IV
) to

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\max\limits_{\left\{{\bf w}\right\}}&P\left({\bf w}\right)={\bf w}^{H}{\bf T}{\bf w},\\
\operatorname{s.t.}&\begin{array}[t]{lll}{\left\|\bf w\right\|}^{2}\leq P_{\text{BS}}\end{array}.\end{array}

The above problem is a convex problem equivalent to

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\min\limits_{\left\{{\bf w}\right\}}&L\left({\bf w},\mu\right)=-{\bf w}^{H}{\bf T}{\bf w}+\mu\left({\left|\bf w\right|}^{2}-P_{\text{BS}}\right),\\
\operatorname{s.t.}&\begin{array}[t]{lll}\mu>0,\end{array}\end{array}

where
\mu>0
is the Lagrangian multiplier.

The Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) conditions are

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\bf w}}L\left({\bf w},\mu\right)=-2{\bf Tw}+2\mu{\bf w}=0,

(54)

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\mu}}L\left({\bf w},\mu\right)={\left|\bf w\right|}^{2}-P_{\text{BS}}=0,

(55)

based on which, we have

\displaystyle{\bf w}=\sqrt{P_{\text{BS}}}{\bf t}_{\text{max}},

(56)

where
{\bf t}_{\text{max}}
is the eigenvector of
{\bf T}
corresponding to the largest eigenvalue.

IV-B

IRS beamforming

For a given
{\bf w}
, the optimization problem (
IV
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\max\limits_{\left\{\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}\right\}}P{\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}\right)}&\triangleq\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}{\bf w}^{H}\sqrt{\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\beta_{\text{B2U}}}{\left({\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}}\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}\right)}^{H}{\bf B}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}}\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}{\bf w}\\
&+\sqrt{\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\beta_{\text{B2U}}}\left({\bf w}^{H}{\left({\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}}\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}\right)}^{H}{\bf C}{\bf w}+{\bf w}^{H}{\bf C}^{H}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}}\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}{\bf w}\right)\\
&+{\beta}_{\text{B2U}}{\bf w}^{H}{\bf A}{\bf w}+\sigma_{\text{NLOS}}^{2}{\bf w}^{H}{\bf w}\\
\operatorname{s.t.}&\begin{array}[t]{lll}\left|{\left[\bf\Theta\right]}_{ii}\right|=1,i=1,...,M.\end{array}\end{array}

According to the rule that
{\bf E}^{H}{\bf X}^{H}{\bf F}={\bf x}^{H}\left({\bf E}^{*}\odot{\bf F}\right)
with
{\bf X}={\text{diag}}\left({\bf x}\right)
, the objective function can be rewritten as

\displaystyle P{\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\Theta$}\right)}

\displaystyle=\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}{\text{tr}}\left({\bf\Theta}^{H}{\bf B}{\bf\Theta}\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}{\bf w}{\bf w}^{H}\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{H}\right)

(61)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\beta_{\text{B2U}}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{H}\left(\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{*}{\bf w}^{*}\odot{\bf Cw}\right)+\sqrt{\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\beta_{\text{B2U}}}\left(\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{T}{\bf w}^{T}\odot{\bf w}^{H}{\bf C}^{H}\right){\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}

\displaystyle+{\beta}_{\text{B2U}}{\bf w}^{H}{\bf A}{\bf w}+\sigma_{\text{NLOS}}^{2}{\bf w}^{H}{\bf w}.

Using the property that
{\bf X}^{H}{\bf E}{\bf Y}{\bf F}={\bf x}^{H}\left({\bf E}\odot{\bf F}^{T}\right){\bf y}
with
{\bf X}={\text{diag}}\left({\bf x}\right)
and
{\bf Y}={\text{diag}}\left({\bf y}\right)
, the above equation can be further expressed as

\displaystyle P{\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right)}

\displaystyle=\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{H}\left({\bf B}\odot{\left(\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}{\bf w}{\bf w}^{H}\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{H}\right)}^{T}\right){\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}

(62)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\beta_{\text{B2U}}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{H}\left(\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{*}{\bf w}^{*}\odot{\bf Cw}\right)+\sqrt{\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\beta_{\text{B2U}}}\left(\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{T}{\bf w}^{T}\odot{\bf w}^{H}{\bf C}^{H}\right){\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}

\displaystyle+{\beta}_{\text{B2U}}{\bf w}^{H}{\bf A}{\bf w}+\sigma_{\text{NLOS}}^{2}{\bf w}^{H}{\bf w},

based on which, the optimization problem can be reformulated as

\displaystyle{}\begin{array}[]{ll}\max\limits_{\left\{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right\}}P{\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right)}&=\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{H}\left({\bf B}\odot{\left(\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}{\bf w}{\bf w}^{H}\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{H}\right)}^{T}\right){\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}\\
&+\sqrt{\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\beta_{\text{B2U}}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{H}\left(\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{*}{\bf w}^{*}\odot{\bf Cw}\right)+\sqrt{\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\beta_{\text{B2U}}}\left(\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{T}{\bf w}^{T}\odot{\bf w}^{H}{\bf C}^{H}\right){\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}\\
&+{\beta}_{\text{B2U}}{\bf w}^{H}{\bf A}{\bf w}+\sigma_{\text{NLOS}}^{2}{\bf w}^{H}{\bf w}\\
\operatorname{s.t.}&\begin{array}[t]{lll}\left|{\xi}_{i}\right|=1,i=1,...,M.\end{array}\end{array}

Since
\left|{\xi}_{i}\right|=1
, we have

{\text{tr}}\left({\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{H}\right)=M
. In order
to deal with the non-convex constraint of
\left|{\xi}_{i}\right|=1
, we relax
the problem (
IV-B
) into the following optimization problem with a
convex
\ell_{\infty}
constraint:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lll}\max\limits_{\left\{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right\}}&P{\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right)}\\
\operatorname{s.t.}&\begin{array}[t]{lll}{\text{tr}}\left({\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{H}\right)=M,\\
{\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right\|}_{\infty}\leq 1.\end{array}\end{array}

Since the
\ell_{\infty}
constraint is non-differentiable, we instead use
the
\ell_{p}
with a large
p
to approximate
\ell_{\infty}
,

\underset{p\to\infty}{\lim}\ell_{p}=\ell_{\infty}
. To solve
(
IV-B
), we incorporate the second constraint
by exploiting the barrier method with the logarithmic barrier function

F\left(x\right)
to approximate the penalty of violating the
\ell_{p}
constraint,

F(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} -\frac{1}{\kappa}\ln\left(x\right),x>0\\
\infty,x\leq 0\end{aligned}\right.,

where
\kappa
is used to scale the barrier function penalty. As such,
the optimization problem can be rewritten as:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{ll}\min\limits_{\left\{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right\}}G{\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right)}&=F\left(1-{\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right\|}_{p}\right)-\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{H}\left({\bf B}\odot{\left(\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}{\bf w}{\bf w}^{H}\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{H}\right)}^{T}\right){\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}\\
&-\sqrt{\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\beta_{\text{B2U}}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{H}\left(\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{*}{\bf w}^{*}\odot{\bf Cw}\right)-\sqrt{\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\beta_{\text{B2U}}}\left(\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{T}{\bf w}^{T}\odot{\bf w}^{H}{\bf C}^{H}\right){\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}\\
\operatorname{s.t.}&\begin{array}[t]{lll}{\text{tr}}\left({\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{H}\right)=M.\end{array}\end{array}

Due to the non-convex constraint
\text{tr}({\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{H})=M
, the optimization problem (
IV-B
) is non-convex. Next, we try to solve the above optimization problem by exploiting a gradient method. As such, sub-optimal solutions can be obtained.
The gradient of the objective function
G{\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right)}
can be calculated as

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}}G{\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right)}=\frac{{\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right\|}_{p}^{1-p}{\mbox{\boldmath$\zeta$}}}{2\kappa\left(1-{\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right\|}_{p}\right)}-\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}}P{\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right)},

(73)

where

\displaystyle{\mbox{\boldmath$\zeta$}}={\left[\xi_{1}{\left|\xi_{1}\right|}^{p-2},\xi_{2}{\left|\xi_{2}\right|}^{p-2},...,\xi_{M}{\left|\xi_{M}\right|}^{p-2}\right]}^{T}.

(74)

And the gradient
\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}}P{\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right)}
can be computed as

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}}P{\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right)}\!=\!2\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\left({\bf B}\odot{\left(\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}{\bf w}{\bf w}^{H}\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{H}\right)}^{T}\right){\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}\!+\!2\sqrt{\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\beta_{\text{B2U}}}{\text{Re}}\left(\left(\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{*}{\bf w}^{*}\odot{\bf Cw}\right){\bf 1}_{N}\right),

(75)

where
{\bf 1}_{N}
is a
N\times 1
vector whose elements all equal to 1.

Thus,
\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}}G{\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right)}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}}G{\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right)}

\displaystyle=-2\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\left({\bf B}\odot{\left(\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}{\bf w}{\bf w}^{H}\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{H}\right)}^{T}\right){\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}

(76)

\displaystyle-2\sqrt{\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\beta_{\text{B2U}}}{\text{Re}}\left(\left(\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{*}{\bf w}^{*}\odot{\bf Cw}\right){\bf 1}_{N}\right)+\frac{{\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right\|}_{p}^{1-p}{\mbox{\boldmath$\zeta$}}}{2\kappa\left(1-{\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right\|}_{p}\right)}.

Due to the constraint
\text{tr}\left({\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{H}\right)=M
, we project
{\bf g}_{\text{gd}}=-\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}}G{\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right)}
into the tangent plane of
\text{tr}\left({\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{H}\right)=M
:

\displaystyle{\bf g}_{\text{p}}={\bf g}_{\text{gd}}-\frac{{\bf g}_{\text{gd}}^{T}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{*}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}}{{\left\|{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}\right\|}^{2}}.

(77)

Then we use
{\bf g}_{\text{p}}
as the search direction.

Proposition 4

The joint optimization algorithm always guarantees

Proof:

1) Proof of
P\left({\bf w}_{i+1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i+1}\right)\geq P\left({\bf w}_{i},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i+1}\right)

By fixing
\xi
, the optimization of
{\bf w}
is a convex problem. And,
{\bf w}_{i+1}
is the optimal solution corresponding to the phase shift beam
{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i+1}
.
Thus, we have

\displaystyle P\left({\bf w}_{i+1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i+1}\right)\geq P\left({\bf w}_{i},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i+1}\right).

(80)

2) Proof of
P\left({\bf w}_{i+1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i+1}\right)\geq P\left({\bf w}_{i+1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}\right)

Fixing
{\bf w}_{i+1}
, the Taylor expansion of
P\left({\bf w}_{i+1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i+1}\right)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle P\left({\bf w}_{i+1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i+1}\right)

\displaystyle=P\left({\bf w}_{i+1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}\right)+{\left(\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}}P\right)}^{H}\left\{P\left({\bf w}_{i+1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i+1}\right)-P\left({\bf w}_{i+1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}\right)\right\}

(81)

\displaystyle+o\left\{{\left(P\left({\bf w}_{i+1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i+1}\right)-P\left({\bf w}_{i+1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}\right)\right)}^{2}\right\}.

Based on (
79
) in Algorithm
1
, for any
\varpi\to 0
, the above equation can be written as

\displaystyle P\left({\bf w}_{i+1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i+1}\right)

\displaystyle=P\left({\bf w}_{i+1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}\right)+{\left(\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}}P\right)}^{H}\sqrt{M}\frac{{\bf g}_{\text{p}}}{{\left\|{\bf g}_{\text{p}}\right\|}^{2}}\varpi^{\star}+o\left\{{\varpi^{\star}}^{2}\right\}

(82)

\displaystyle\approx P\left({\bf w}_{i+1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}\right)+{\left(\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}}P\right)}^{H}{\bf g}_{\text{p}}\frac{\sqrt{M}}{{\left\|{\bf g}_{\text{p}}\right\|}^{2}}\varpi^{\star}.

Next, we focus on the calculation of
{\left(\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}}P\right)}^{H}{\bf g}_{\text{p}}
.

\displaystyle{\left(\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}}P\right)}^{H}{\bf g}_{\text{p}}={\left({\bf g}_{\text{gd}}+\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}}F\left(1-{\left\|{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}\right\|}^{2}\right)\right)}^{H}{\bf g}_{\text{p}}.

(83)

Recall that

\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}}F\left(1-{\left\|{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}\right\|}^{2}\right)=\frac{{\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{i}\right\|}_{p}^{1-p}{\mbox{\boldmath$\Omega$}\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{i}}}{2\kappa\left(1-{\left\|\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{i}\right\|}_{p}\right)}

with

{\mbox{\boldmath$\Omega$}}=\text{diag}\left({\left|\xi_{1}\right|}^{p-2},{\left|\xi_{2}\right|}^{p-2},...,{\left|\xi_{M}\right|}^{p-2}\right)
, and
{\bf g}_{\text{p}}={\bf g}_{\text{gd}}-\frac{{\bf g}_{\text{gd}}^{T}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{i}}^{*}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{i}}}{{\left\|{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}_{i}}\right\|}^{2}}
. We have
{\left(\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}}F\left(1-{\left\|{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}\right\|}^{2}\right)\right)}^{H}{\bf g}_{\text{p}}=0
, and thus the above equation can be calculated as

\displaystyle{\left(\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}}P\right)}^{H}{\bf g}_{\text{p}}={\bf g}_{\text{gd}}^{H}{\bf g}_{\text{p}}={\left\|{\bf g}_{\text{gd}}\right\|}^{2}\left(1-{\left(\cos\varrho\right)}^{2}\right)\geq 0,

where

is the angle between vectors
{\bf g}_{\text{gd}}
and
{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}
.

Substituting (

\displaystyle P\left({\bf w}_{i+1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i+1}\right)-P\left({\bf w}_{i+1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}\right)={\left(\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}}P\right)}^{H}{\bf g}_{\text{p}}\frac{\sqrt{M}}{{\left\|{\bf g}_{\text{p}}\right\|}^{2}}\varpi^{\star}\geq 0.

(85)

To this end, combining 1) and 2), we have

\displaystyle P\left({\bf w}_{i+1},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i+1}\right)\geq P\left({\bf w}_{i},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i+1}\right)\geq P\left({\bf w}_{i},{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{i}\right).

(86)

Remark 4

Our method of iteratively optimizing the transmit beam and the phase shift beam can provide an efficient way to gradually increase the average received signal power, although only a
sub-optimal solution can be ensured due to the non-convexity of the problem.

Remark 5

For the BS beamforming scheme, the complexity is consumed by the eigenvector calculation. Therefore, the complexity order of BS beamforming is
\mathcal{O}\left(N^{3}\right)
.

For the IRS beamforming algorithm , the computational complexity is mainly determined by the calculation of the gradient (
76

Theorem 3

The achievable rate is given by

\displaystyle R=\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{\tilde{\bf w}^{H}{\bf T}\tilde{\bf w}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}\right),

(87)

where
\tilde{\bf w}\triangleq\sqrt{P_{\text{BS}}}{\bf t}_{\text{max}}

Proof:

Starting from Proposition

Theorem

presents an expression for the achievable rate which quantifies the impact
of key system parameters, such as the number of BS antennas and reflecting elements, as well as the impact of estimation error on the achievable rate. For instance, it can be seen that the SNR is related to

From the expression of
\bf T
which is defined in Proposition 2, we can conclude that
the SNR drops nearly exponentially with the variance of the angle estimation error. This is because inaccurate estimated angles makes it difficult to generate highly directional beams, thereby causing severe power loss.

Corollary 2

Assuming a large Rician K-factor and that the IRS and the user are in the similar direction, the achievable rate can be approximated as

\displaystyle R_{\text{approx}}=\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{P_{\text{BS}}\Omega}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}\right),

where

\displaystyle\Omega

\displaystyle\triangleq N\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{M}{\xi}_{m}^{*}{\xi}_{n}{\left[\bf B\right]}_{mn}b_{\text{B2Ia},m}^{*}b_{\text{B2Ia},n}

Proof:

See Appendix
F
.
∎

Corollary
2
implies that increasing the number of BS antennas can greatly improve the achievable rate. In particular, both the BS-user link and BS-IRS-user link SNRs grow linearly with
N
.
To get more insights, we derive an upper bound for the achievable rate.

Proposition 5

An upper bound for the achievable rate is given by

\displaystyle R_{\text{upper}}=\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{P_{\text{BS}}N\left(\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}M^{2}+2\sqrt{\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\beta_{\text{B2U}}}M+\beta_{\text{B2U}}+\sigma^{2}_{\text{NLOS}}\right)}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}\right).

\left|{\left[\bf C\right]}_{mi}\right|\leq 1

∎

Proposition
5
indicates that with fixed transmit power, the achievable rate is mainly determined by the numbers of reflecting elements and BS antennas. Specifically, the effective SNR is proportional to the number of BS antennas. Moreover, there is a gain
M^{2}
corresponding to the BS-IRS-user link. This is reasonable because the IRS not only achieves the phase shift beamforming gain of order
M
in the IRS-user link, but also captures an inherent aperture gain of order
M
by collecting more signal power in the BS-IRS link. It is worth noting that this
M^{2}
gain only holds when
\sqrt{MA_{p}}\ll d_{\text{B2I}}
with
A_{p}
being the effective aperture/area of each reflecting element, due to the law of energy conservation
[
41
]
.

Remark 6

This paper mainly focuses on a single user scenario. However, the proposed angle domain design framework can be easily extended to the multi-user case. For instance, with orthogonal pilot sequences, the proposed angle-domain estimation method can be directly applied to separately estimate the effective angles of each user. Also, the alternating optimization method can be similarly applied to reduce the complexity of beamforming algorithms.
The key difference is that, in a multi-user scenario, the co-channel interference should be taken into account. In addition, since all users share the same BS and IRS beamforming vectors, the resultant optimization problem becomes much more complicated.

VII
conclusion

This paper considers an IRS-aided system from an angle-domain aspect. The ML estimators for the effective angles from the BS to the user are provided, based on which the effective angles from the IRS to the user are calculated.
It has been shown that increasing the number of BS antennas can significantly reduce the estimation error. Also, placing the IRS closer to the BS would lead to a smaller estimation error of the estimated angles from the IRS to the user. Then, exploiting the estimated angles, a joint optimization algorithm of BS beamforming and IRS beamforming has been proposed, which achieves similar performance to two benchmark algorithms based on full CSI and the MUSIC method respectively. Beam patterns
of the optimized BS beam indicate that as the number of reflecting elements becomes larger, the beam becomes more focused towards the IRS direction, as should be expected.
Analysis of the achievable rate quantifies the benefit of deploying a large number of BS antennas or reflecting elements. In particular,
the BS-user link and the BS-IRS-user link can obtain power gains of order
N
and
NM^{2}
, respectively.

Appendix E
Proof of Proposition
3

The constant-modulus constraint
|\xi_{i}|=1,i=1,\dots,M
is equivalent to the following two constraints:

\displaystyle\text{tr}\left({\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}\right)=M,

(133)

\displaystyle\|{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{\infty}\|\leq 1.

(134)

Since we project
{\bf g}_{\text{gd}}=-\nabla_{{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}}G{\left(\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}\right)}
into the tangent plane of
\text{tr}\left({\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{H}\right)=M
:

{\bf g}_{\text{p}}={\bf g}_{\text{gd}}-\frac{{\bf g}_{\text{gd}}^{T}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}^{*}{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}}{{\left\|{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}\right\|}^{2}},

and use
{\bf g}_{\text{p}}
as the search direction,
the first constraint
\text{tr}\left({\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}\right)=M
holds.
Noticing that
\underset{p\to\infty}{\lim}\ell_{p}=\ell_{\infty}
, the constraint

is approximately equivalent to
\|{\mbox{\boldmath$\xi$}}_{p}\|\leq 1
with a large

. Then a barrier method is exploited to make

To this end, we complete our proof.

Appendix F
Proof of Corollary
2

Recall that the optimal
{\bf w}
is
\sqrt{P_{\text{BS}}}{\bf t}_{\text{max}}
with
{\bf t}_{\text{max}}
being the eigenvector of
{\bf T}
corresponding to the largest eigenvalue
\lambda_{\text{max}}
.
Thus, the average received signal power is given by

P_{\text{r}}=P_{\text{BS}}\lambda_{\text{max}}.

Due to the fact that the trace of a matrix is the sum of all eigenvalues, the following equation holds

\text{tr}\left({\bf T}\right)=\sum\limits_{n=1}^{N}\lambda_{T,n}.

Since the channel is sparse, the largest eigenvalue
\lambda_{\text{max}}
dominates the trace
\text{tr}\left({\bf T}\right)
. As such, we have
\lambda_{\text{max}}\approx\text{tr}\left({\bf T}\right)
.

Thus, the achievable rate can be approximated as

\displaystyle R_{\text{approx}}=\log_{2}\left(1+\frac{P_{\text{BS}}\text{tr}\left({\bf T}\right)}{\sigma_{0}^{2}}\right).

(135)

Starting from Theorem
3
,
\text{tr}\left({\bf T}\right)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\text{tr}\left({\bf T}\right)

\displaystyle=\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}\sum\limits_{n=1}^{M}{\xi}_{m}^{*}{\xi}_{n}{\left[\bf B\right]}_{mn}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{\left[\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{*}\right]}_{mi}{\left[\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}\right]}_{ni}\right)

(136)

\displaystyle+2\sqrt{\beta_{\text{B2I2U}}\beta_{\text{B2U}}}\text{Re}\left\{\sum\limits_{m=1}^{M}{\xi}_{m}^{*}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}{\left[\bf C\right]}_{mi}{\left[\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}^{*}\right]}_{mi}\right)\right\}+N\beta_{\text{B2U}}+N\sigma_{\text{NLOS}}^{2}.

Recall that
\bar{\bf H}_{\text{B2I}}={\bf b}\left(\bar{\theta}_{\text{x-B2Ia}},\bar{\theta}_{\text{y-B2Ia}}\right){\bf a}^{T}\left(\bar{\theta}_{\text{x-B2I}},\bar{\theta}_{\text{y-B2I}}\right)
. We can obtain the desired result.

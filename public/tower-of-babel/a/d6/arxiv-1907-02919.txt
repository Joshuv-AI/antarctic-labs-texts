Petr A. Golovach, Giannos Stamoulis, Dimitrios M. Thilikos

Abstract
For a finite collection of graphs ℱ {\cal F} , the ℱ {\cal F} -TM-Deletion problem has as input an n n -vertex graph G G and an integer k k and asks whether there exists a set S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G) with | S | ≤ k |S|\leq k such that G ∖ S G\setminus S does not contain any of the graphs in ℱ {\cal F} as a topological minor. We prove that for every such ℱ {\cal F} , ℱ {\cal F} -TM-Deletion is fixed parameter tractable on planar graphs. Our algorithm runs in a 2 𝒪 ⁡ ( k 2 ) ⋅ n 2 2^{\mathcal{O}(k^{2})}\cdot n^{2} time or, alternatively in 2 𝒪 ⁡ ( k ) ⋅ n 4 2^{\mathcal{O}(k)}\cdot n^{4} time. Our techniques can easily be extended to graphs that are embeddable on any fixed surface.

1.3
High level description of our algorithm

Our main approach towards proving
Theorem 1.1
is the application of the so-called
irrelevant vertex technique
.
This technique was introduced for the first time by Roberston and Seymour in
[
46
]

for the design of an
FPT
-algorithm for the
Disjoint Paths
problem, parameterized by the number of terminals. Subsequently,
it was applied, in diverse ways, for the design of
FPT
-algorithms
for several graph-theoretical problems and is now considered
as a powerful technique of parameterized algorithm
design
[
3
,
18
,
29
,
32
,
33
,
35
,

,

,

.
We also refer to
[
17

for a high-level
overview of the irrelevant vertex technique.
The general algorithmic paradigm of the irrelevant vertex technique
takes advantage of some structural characteristic of the input graph
in order to detect, in
FPT
-time,
some vertex, called
irrelevant
, whose
removal from
G
generates an equivalent instance of the problem.
By recursing on the produced equivalent instance we end up with
a graph where the structural parameter is bounded (by some function of
k
).
This fact permits the resolution of the problem with other techniques – typically by dynamic programming.
Most of the times, this structural parameter
is treewidth (see
Section 2

for the formal definition) and this is the one that we use in this paper.
Towards proving
Theorem 1.1
, the application of the irrelevant vertex technique is based on
Theorem 1.2
that we present below.

Let

be a graph,

be a subset of

, and

We say that

we also make progress by reducing

.

There exists a function
f_{1}:{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}

2.3

In the rest of this section we show how to reroute topological minor models inside railed annuli.

According to the classic meta-algorithmic results of

6
Discussion

In this paper we prove that
{\cal F}
-TM-Deletion
is Fixed Parameter Tractable on planar graphs.

6.1
Running time dependency on
h

The parametric dependency of our
FPT
-algorithm
is
2^{\mathcal{O}_{h}(k^{2})}
and it can be dropped to
2^{\mathcal{O}_{h}(k)}

if we admit a cubic polynomial dependency on
n
.
However both these parametric dependencies hide huge dependency on
h
. To estimate this,
one may observe that the complexity of the dynamic programming algorithm of
Lemma 3.4
dominates the overall running time of the
algorithm of
Theorem 1.2
, in terms of the contribution of
h
. This permits us to
estimate that the algorithm of
Theorem 1.1

runs in
2^{k^{2}\cdot 2^{2^{2^{2^{\mathcal{O}(h)}}}}}\cdot n^{2}
time, or, alternatively, in

\mathcal{O}(k\cdot n^{4})+2^{2^{2^{2^{2^{\mathcal{O}(h)}}}}}\cdot n^{4}+2^{k\cdot 2^{2^{2^{2^{\mathcal{O}(h)}}}}}\cdot n^{2}
.

6.3
Recent advances on the general problem

The remaining question is whether
the same result can be derived for
all
graphs. Recently an
\mathcal{O}_{h,k}(n^{4})
algorithm for the general
{\cal F}
-TM-Deletion
problem was proposed by Fomin et al.
[
25
]
. Using the words of
[
25
]
, the parametric
dependency of this algorithm, on
k
and
h
, is humongous. However, in the same paper, it was proven
that better parametric dependancies can be achieved when restricting the problem to graphs of bounded Euler genus. According to the results of
[
25
]
,
{\cal F}
-TM-Deletion
on graphs of Euler genus at most
\textgamma
can be solved by an algorithm running in
2^{2^{\mathcal{O}_{h,\textgamma}(k)}}\cdot n^{2}
time. The algorithms claimed in
Subsection 6.2

can be seen as improvements of this result.

6.4
Open problems

We believe that the techniques developed in this paper can be applied to other instances
of the
{\cal P}
-
deletion
problem. In particular, we conjecture the following.

Conjecture 6.1
.

If
{\cal F}
is a finite set of graphs and
\leq
is the contraction relation, then
the problem
{\cal P}_{{\cal F},\leq}
-deletion
, with inputs restricted on graphs of Euler genus at most
\textgamma
, can be solved
by an algorithm that runs in
\mathcal{O}_{h,k}(n^{c})
time, for some constant
c
.

Conjecture 6.2
.

If
{\cal F}
is a finite set of graphs and
\leq
is the induced minor relation, then
the problem
{\cal P}_{{\cal F},\leq}
-deletion
, with inputs restricted on graphs of Euler genus at most
\textgamma
, can be solved
by an algorithm that runs in
\mathcal{O}_{h,k}(n^{c})
time, for some constant
c
.

A possible pathway for proving
Conjecture 6.1
is to use the fact that, given an graph embedding
\textGamma
,
edge contractions on
G
correspond to topological minors on the dual embedding
\textGamma^{*}
.
This “translation” of contractions to topological minors was proposed in
[
36
]
in order to devise an algorithm for the problem of checking whether
a graph of Euler genus at most
\textgamma
contains a graph
H
as a contraction (this result is
the case
k=0
of
Conjecture 6.1
). Under this setting, the only significant
change is that instead of looking for a set of vertices to remove, we must find a set of faces to “shrink”.
Therefore, the main missing ingredient for a proof of
Conjecture 6.1
is a dynamic programming framework for this shrinking variant
of the problem on surfaces.

For
Conjecture 6.2
one may directly attempt to build counterparts of all the algorithms of this paper
for the induced minor relation. The only missing combinatorial ingredient for this is an “induced” version of
the model combing theorem (
Theorem 2.1
), that is proved in
[
31
]
.

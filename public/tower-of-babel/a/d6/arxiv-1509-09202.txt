Periodic measures are dense in invariant measures for residually finite amenable group actions with specification Thanks: 2010 Mathematics Subjects Classification. Primary 37B40, 37A35, 22D25.
Xiankun Ren

Abstract




Abstract
We prove that for actions of a discrete countable residually
finite amenable group on a compact metric space with specification property, periodic measures are dense in the
set of invariant measures. We also prove that some certain expansive
actions of a countable discrete group by automorphisms of compact abelian groups have specification property.

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a discrete countable residually finite amenable group
acting on a compact metric space
X.
Denote by
\mathcal{M}(X,G)

the set of
G
-invariant measures and
\mathcal{M}_{e}(X,G)
the
set of ergodic
G-
invariant measures. For a point

we call

|orb(x)|<\infty.
Denote the periodic

\mu_{x}
as a probability measure with mass
|orb(x)|^{-1}
at

orb(x)
and we denote

\mathcal{M}_{P}(X)
the set of all such
periodic measures by

For

For example in
[

invariant measures for Axiom A-diffeomorphisms. In
[
7

, Hirayama proved that the measures supported by hyperbolic periodic points are dense in

In
[
9

, Liang, Liu and Sun proved that each invariant measure in a non-uniformly hyperbolic

Throughout this paper,

with metric

We will

Our main results are as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be a discrete countable residually finite
amenable group acting on a compact metric space

Then

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be a countable discrete group and

Then the action of

2.1.
Amenable group

A countable group
G
is amenable if there exists a sequence of
finite subsets
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|F_{n}\triangle KF_{n}|}{|F_{n}|}=0,\forall K\in\mathcal{F}(G),

where
\mathcal{F}(G)
is the collection of all finite subsets of
G.

Such sequences are said Følner sequences.

The quasi tiling theory is a useful tool for amenable group actions which is set up by Ornstein and Weiss in
[
12
]
. Subsets
A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{k}\in\mathcal{F}(G)
are
\varepsilon
-disjoint if there exists
\{B_{1},B_{2},\cdots,B_{k}\}\subset\mathcal{F}(G)
such that

(1)B_{i}\subset A_{i}\qquad i=1,2,\cdots,k,

(2)\frac{|B_{i}|}{|A_{i}|}>1-\varepsilon\ \quad i=1,2,\cdots,k.

For
\alpha\in(0,1],
we say
\{A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{k}\}

\alpha
-covers
A\in\mathcal{F}(G)
if

\frac{A\cap(\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}A_{i})}{|A|}\geq\alpha.

We say that
\{A_{1},A_{2},\cdots,A_{k}\}\subset\mathcal{F}(G)

\varepsilon-
quasi-tile
A\in\mathcal{F}(G)
if there exists
\{C_{1},C_{2},\cdots,C_{k}\}\subset\mathcal{F}(G)
satisfying

(1)
A_{i}C_{i}\subset A
and
\{A_{i}c|c\in C_{i}\}
forms an
\varepsilon-
disjoint family for
i=1,2,\cdots,k,

(2)
A_{i}C_{i}\cap A_{j}C_{j}\neq\emptyset\quad 1\leq i\neq j\leq k,

(3)
\{A_{i}C_{i}:i=1,2,\cdots k\}
forms a
(1-\varepsilon)-
cover of
A.

The subsets
C_{1},C_{2},\cdots,C_{k}
are called the tiling centers.

Lemma 2.3

.

\int f(x)d\mu=\int\overline{f}(x)d\mu.

\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{|F_{n}|}\sum\limits_{g\in F_{n}}f(gx)=\int f(x){\rm d}\mu(x)\quad\text{in}\quad L^{1}.

2.2.
Residually finite group

A group is residually finite if the intersection of all its normal subgroups of finite index is trivial. Examples of groups that are residually finite are finite groups, free groups, finitely generated nilpotent groups, polycyclic-by-finite groups, finitely generated linear groups, and fundamental groups of 3-manifolds. For more information
about residually finite groups readers can refer
[
3
, Chapter 2]
.

Let
(G_{n},n\geq 1)
be a sequence of finite index normal subgroups in
G.
We say

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}G_{n}=\{e_{G}\}

if we can find, for any
K\in\mathcal{F}(G),
an
N>1
with
G_{n}\cap(K^{-1}K)=\{e_{G}\}
for every
n\geq N.
Clearly such sequence exists if
G
is countable and residually finite.

If
G^{\prime}\subset G
is a subgroup with finite index,
and
Q\subset G
is a
fundamental domain
of the right coset
space
G/G^{\prime},
i.e. a finite subset such that

\{sQ\,|\,s\in G^{\prime}\}
is a partition of
G.

The following proposition is
[
5
, Corollary 5.6]
and we will use
it to control the periodic orbits we get.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
G
be a countable discrete residually finite amenable group and let
(G_{n},n\geq 1)
be a sequence of finite index normal subgroup with
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}G_{n}=\{e_{G}\}.
Then there exists a Følner sequence
(Q_{n},n\geq 1)
such that
Q_{n}
is a fundamental domain of
G/G_{n}
for every
n\geq 1.

2.3.
Specification

Specification is an orbit tracing property which is very useful in finding periodic orbits. In
[
1
]
, Bowen was the first to state this notion and Ruelle investigated the extension of this notion to
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
-action in
[
13
]
. Chung and Li again generalized the property to general group actions in
[
4
]
.

Let
\alpha
be a continuous
G-
action on a compact metric space
X
with metric
\rho.
The action has specification property if there exist, for every
\varepsilon>0,
a nonempty finite subset
F=F(\varepsilon)
of
G
with the following property :

for any finite collection of finite subsets
F_{1},F_{2},\cdots,F_{m}
of
G
satisfying

\displaystyle FF_{i}\cap F_{j}=\emptyset\qquad 1\leq i\neq j\leq m

(1)

and for any subgroup
G^{\prime}
of
G
with

\displaystyle FF_{i}\cap F_{j}(G^{\prime}\setminus\{e_{G}\})=\emptyset\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq i,j\leq m.

(2)

Then for any collection of points
x^{1},x^{2},\dots,x^{m}\in X

there is a point
y\in X
satisfying

\displaystyle\rho(sx^{i},sy)\leq\varepsilon\quad\text{for all}\quad s\in F_{i},\ 1\leq i\leq m

(3)

and
sy=y
for all
s\in G^{\prime}.

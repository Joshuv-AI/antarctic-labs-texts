Torres-type formulas for link signatures
David Cimasoni, Maciej Markiewicz, Wojciech Politarczyk

Abstract
. We investigate the limits of the multivariable signature function σ L \sigma_{L} of a μ \mu -component link L L as some variable tends to 1 1 via two different approaches: a three-dimensional and a four-dimensional one. The first uses the definition of σ L \sigma_{L} by generalized Seifert surfaces and forms. The second relies on a new extension of σ L \sigma_{L} from its usual domain ( S 1 ∖ { 1 } ) μ (S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{\mu} to the full torus 𝕋 μ \mathbb{T}^{\mu} together with a Torres-type formula for σ L \sigma_{L} , results which are of independent interest. Among several consequences, we obtain new estimates on the value of the Levine-Tristram signature of a link close to 1 1 .



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background on the Levine-Tristram signature

Let
L
be an
m
-component oriented link in the three-sphere
S^{3}
,
and let
A
be an arbitrary Seifert matrix for
L
.
The
Levine-Tristram signature
of
L
is the function

\sigma_{L}\colon S^{1}\setminus\{1\}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z},\quad\omega\longmapsto\sigma(H(\omega))

where

H(\omega)=(1-\omega)A+(1-\overline{\omega})A^{T}

and
\sigma(H)
denotes the
signature of the Hermitian matrix
H
. As one easily checks (see e.g.
[
28
]
), this function
does not depend on the choice of the Seifert matrix, and is therefore an invariant of the link
L
. Similarly, the
Levine-Tristram nullity

of
L
is the function
\eta_{L}\colon S^{1}\setminus\{1\}\to\mathbb{Z}
given by
\eta_{L}(\omega)=\eta(H(\omega))
, where
\eta(H)
stands for the nullity of
H
.

Since its discovery by Trotter
[
42
]
in the case
\omega=-1
, its study by Murasugi
[
33
]
, and its extension by Tristram
[
41
]
and Levine
[
26
]
to
S^{1}\setminus\{1\}
, the Levine-Tristram signature has been the subject of intense activity. Among its numerous remarkable properties, let us mention
the facts that
\sigma_{L}
vanishes if
L
is amphicheiral, that it is locally constant on the complement of the roots of the Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{L}
, that it provides lower bounds on the unknotting number of
L

[
29
]
, on its splitting number
[
4
]
, as well as on its Seifert genus, i.e. on the minimal genus of an orientable surface
S\subset S^{3}
with oriented boundary
\partial S=L
.

More subtly, if
\omega
is not the root of any polynomial
p(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]
with
p(1)=\pm 1
, then
\sigma_{L}(\omega)
also provides a lower bound on the
topological four-genus
of
L
,
i.e. on the mininal genus of a locally flat orientable surface
F
in the four-ball
B^{4}
with oriented boundary
\partial F=F\cap\partial B^{4}=L
, see
[
34
]
. This fact was already noticed by Murasugi using the classical definition of
\sigma_{L}
stated above. However, the current understanding of this phenomenon relies on an alternative interpretation of
\sigma_{L}(\omega)
, as the signature of some associated four-dimensional manifold,
an approach pioneered by Rohlin
[
37
]
and Viro
[
44
]
, see also
[
22
]
.
There are several variations on this theme (see e.g.
[
21
]
and references therein), but the most practical and now most commonly used one is to consider the intersection form of the four-manifold obtained from
B^{4}
by removing
a tubular neighborhood of
F
, with so-called
twisted coefficients
determined by
\omega
(see Section
2.3
below). Remarkably, this approach is due to Viro once again
[
45
]
, some 36 years after his first
seminal contribution to the subject.
We refer the interested reader to the survey
[
7
]
and references therein for more information on the Levine-Tristram signature.

Despite all these results, some elementary properties of
\sigma_{L}
remain mysterious.
For example, it is rather frustrating not to have this function naturally extended to the full
circle
S^{1}
, as the definition above
yields a trivial signature and ill-defined
nullity at
\omega=1
.
A related question is the following: what can be said of its value at
\omega
close to
1
?
If
m=1
, i.e. if the link
L
is a knot, then one easily checks that
\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)
vanishes,
but in the general case of an
m
-component link, this elementary approach only yields the inequality
|\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)|\leq m-1
(see e.g.
[
17
]
).

In the recent article
[
2
]
, Borodzik and Zarzycki used so-called
Hermitian variation structures
to show the following result. If
L=K_{1}\cup\dots\cup K_{m}
is an oriented link
whose Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{L}
does not vanish and is not divisible by
(t-1)^{m}
, then

\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)=\sigma(\mathit{Lk}_{L})\,,

where
\mathit{Lk}_{L}
denotes the
linking matrix
of
L
defined by

(1)

(\mathit{Lk}_{L})_{ij}=\begin{cases}\lk(K_{i},K_{j})&\text{if\penalty\ $i\neq j$};\\
-\sum_{k\neq i}\lk(K_{i},K_{k})&\text{if\penalty\ $i=j$}\,.\end{cases}

The assumptions on
\Delta_{L}
are slightly mysterious and the tools rather unorthodox,
but this result puts forward the value
\sigma_{L}(1)=\sigma(\mathit{Lk}_{L})

as the natural extension of
\sigma_{L}
to
the full circle (a fact that can also
be traced back to the proof of Lemma 5.4 in
[
34
]
).
Furthermore, this indicates that a naive extension of
\sigma_{L}
to
S^{1}
using the standard
four-dimensional interpretation does not yield the correct answer in general.

1.2.
Results on the Levine-Tristram signature

Our first original result on the Levine-Tristram signature is the following inequality (Theorem
5.4
).

Theorem 1.1
.

For any oriented link
L
, we have

\left|\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)-\sigma(\mathit{Lk}_{L})\right|\leq\eta(\mathit{Lk}_{L})-1-\rank A(L)\,,

where
A(L)
denotes the one-variable Alexander module of
L
.

In particular, it implies that
\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)=\sigma(\mathit{Lk}_{L})

for all links with
\rank A(L)=\eta(\mathit{Lk}_{L})-1
.
As shown in Remark
5.7
, the equality
\eta(\mathit{Lk}_{L})=1
is equivalent to the Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{L}
not vanishing and not being divisible by
(t-1)^{m}
. Therefore, this theorem
extends the aforementioned result of
[
2
]
.
It also implies several immediate and pleasant corollaries, such as the elementary but not so obvious inequalities

\rank A(L)\leq\eta(\mathit{Lk}_{L})-1

and

\left|\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)\right|\leq m-1-\rank A(L)\,,

valid for any oriented link
L
.

As will be explained in Section
1.4
,
we have also obtained similar results for more general signatures,
results that can then be applied back to the Levine-Tristram signature.
To test the power of our methods, we have tried to determine the limit
of the Levine-Tristram signature of
an arbitrary
2
-component link, showing the following statement (Corollary
5.21
).

Corollary 1.2
.

If
L
is a 2-component oriented link with linking number
\ell

and two-variable Conway function
\nabla_{L}
, then its Levine-Tristram signature satisfies

\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)=\begin{cases}-\mathrm{sgn}(\ell)&\text{if\penalty\ $\ell\neq 0$, or if\penalty\ $\nabla_{L}=0$ (in which case\penalty\ $\ell=0$);}\\
\mathrm{sgn}(f(1,1))&\text{if\penalty\ $\ell=0$,\penalty\ $\nabla_{L}\neq 0$ and\penalty\ $f(1,1)\neq 0$};\\
\pm 1{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\text{ or $0$}}&\text{if\penalty\ $\ell=0$,\penalty\ $\nabla_{L}\neq 0$ and\penalty\ $f(1,1)=0$},\end{cases}

where in the last two cases, we have
\nabla_{L}(t_{1},t_{2})=(t_{1}-t^{-1}_{1})(t_{2}-t^{-1}_{2})f(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}_{1},t^{\pm 1}_{2}]
.

Note that the result of
[
2
]
covers
precisely the case of non-vanishing linking number,
while the last case is classical (see e.g.
[
17
]
)
; the other cases are new.

Testing our results on
3
-component links would be an entertaining
exercise that we have not attempted,
but we expect a similar outcome.

1.3.
Background and questions on the multivariable link signature

As is well-known, the Alexander polynomial admits a multivariable extension for links. A slightly less familiar fact is that
the Levine-Tristram also admits such a generalization.
The most natural setting for it is that of colored links, that we now recall.

Let
\mu
be a positive integer. A
\mu
-colored link
is an oriented link
L
each of whose components is endowed with a
color
in
\{1,\dots,\mu\}
so that all colors are used.
Such a colored link is commonly denoted by
L=L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}
,
with
L_{i}
the sublink of
L
consisting of the components of color
i
.
Two colored links are
isotopic
if they are related by an ambient isotopy which respects
the orientation and color of all components.
Obviously, a
1
-colored link is nothing but an oriented link,
while a
\mu
-component
\mu
-colored link is an oriented ordered link.
Most of our results hold for arbitrary
\mu
-colored links, but some of them (e.g. Theorem
1.8
) are restricted to such ordered links, which we often simply call
\mu
-component
links
.

Given an arbitrary
\mu
-colored link
L
in
S^{3}
,
the
multivariable signature
of
L
is the function

\sigma_{L}\colon(S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{\mu}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z},\quad\omega=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{\mu})\longmapsto\sigma(H(\omega))\,,

where
H(\omega)
is a Hermitian matrix built from

generalized Seifert matrices
associated with
generalized Seifert surfaces known as
C-complexes
, see Section
2.2
.
Similarly, the
multivariable nullity

of
L
is the function
\eta_{L}\colon(S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{\mu}\to\mathbb{Z}
given by
\eta_{L}(\omega)=\eta(H(\omega))
.
These invariants were introduced by Cooper
[
11
]
in the
2
-component
2
-colored case, and fully developed and studied
in
[
6
]
.

As one immediately sees from the definitions, the case
\mu=1

recovers the Levine-Tristram signature and nullity, justifying the slight abuse of notation.
However, there is another way
in which these multivariable functions can be applied back to their one-variable
counterparts. Indeed, given any
\mu
-colored link
L=L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}
,
we have

(2)

\sigma_{L}(\omega,\dots,\omega)=\sigma_{L^{\text{or}}}(\omega)+\sum_{i<j}\lk(L_{i},L_{j})

for all
\omega\in S^{1}\setminus\{1\}
, where
L^{\text{or}}
denotes the (
1
-colored)
oriented link underlying
L
(see
[
6
, Proposition 2.5]
).
As a consequence, this multivariable extension can be a valuable tool even if
one is only interested in the original Levine-Tristram signature.

In a nutshell, all the agreeable properties of the Levine-Tristram signature
mentioned in Section
1.1
extend to the multivariable setting.
In particular, the function
\sigma_{L}
is constant on the connected components
of the complement in
(S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{\mu}
of the zeros of the multivariable
Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{L}(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu})
, see Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.2 of
[
6
]
. Also, if
(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{\mu})
is not the root of any Laurent polynomial
p(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu})
with
p(1,\dots,1)=\pm 1
, then
\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{\mu})
and
\eta_{L}(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{\mu})
are invariant under
topological concordance
of colored links. As in the
1
-variable case, the understanding of
this fact came in incremental steps (see in particular
[
6
, Section 7]
),
its definitive treatment (and extension to
0.5
-solvability) being achieved in
[
10
]
. Once again, the modern proof relies on an alternative
definition of
\sigma_{L}(\omega)
as the twisted signature of the four-manifold
obtained from
B^{4}
by removing a tubular neighborhood of a union of surfaces
F=F_{1}\cup\dots\cup F_{\mu}
with
\partial F_{i}=F_{i}\cap\partial B^{4}=L_{i}

for all
i
.

Despite these results, several questions remain unanswered.

Question 1
.

Is there a natural extension of
\sigma_{L}
and
\eta_{L}

from
(S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{\mu}
to the full torus
\mathbb{T}^{\mu}
?

As in the
1
-variable case, the definition via (generalized) Seifert matrices
yields a trivial signature and ill-defined
nullity as soon as some coordinate is equal to
1
.
Moreover, the ‘naive’ extension of the standard four-dimensional interpretation from
[
45
,
14
,
10
]
is in general not well-defined either (see e.g.
[
14
, Section 4.4]
).

The second question is relevant to the title of this work.
The celebrated
Torres formula

[
40
]
relates the multivariable
Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{L}
of a
\mu
-component ordered link
L=L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}
to the Alexander polynomial of the
(\mu-1)
-component link
L\setminus L_{1}

via the equality

(3)

\Delta_{L}(1,t_{2},\dots,t_{\mu})\;=\;(t_{2}^{\lk(L_{1},L_{2})}\cdots t_{\mu}^{\lk(L_{1},L_{\mu})}-1)\;\Delta_{L\setminus L_{1}}(t_{2},\dots,t_{\mu})

in
\mathbb{Z}[t_{2},t_{2}^{-1},\dots,t_{\mu},t_{\mu}^{-1}]
, up to multiplication by units of this ring.
Assuming that a satisfactory answer to Question
1
has been found,
is there an anolog of the Torres formula for the multivariable signature and
nullity? In other words:

Question 2
.

Is there a simple formula relating
\sigma_{L}(1,\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{\mu})

and
\sigma_{L\setminus L_{1}}(\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{\mu})
, and one relating
\eta_{L}(1,\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{\mu})

and
\eta_{L\setminus L_{1}}(\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{\mu})
?

The third question was already posed in the
1
-variable context at the end of Section
1.1
.

Question 3
.

For a fixed
(\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{\mu})
, what can be said of the
limits
\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{\mu})
?

Here note the plural in “limits”: unlike in the
1
-variable case where the symmetry
\sigma_{L}(\overline{\omega})=\sigma_{L}(\omega)
ensures that
\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)
is well-defined, in the multivariable case
the limit might depend on whether
\omega_{1}\in S^{1}
tends to
1
from above or from below (see e.g. Example
3.5
). We shall denote these two limits by
\omega_{1}\to 1^{+}
and
\omega_{1}\to 1^{-}
.
Note that if one keeps
\omega^{\prime}=(\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{\mu})\in(S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{\mu-1}
fixed, then these two limits do exist by the locally constant behaviour of signatures described in
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
. On the other hand, if one allows for any sequence of elements
\omega\in(S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{\mu}
converging to
(1,\omega^{\prime})
,
then the corresponding limits of signature might not be well-defined (see e.g. Figure
4
with
\omega^{\prime}
a third root of unity, and Example
3.5
). However,
the estimates that we obtain on what we denote by
\lim_{\omega\to 1^{+}}\sigma_{L}(\omega)
and
\lim_{\omega\to 1^{-}}\sigma_{L}(\omega)
hold for any such sequence.

1.4.
Results on the multivariable link signature

In short, our work provides rather satisfactory answers to the three questions raised above.

First, we extend the signature and nullity to the full torus.
To give a sense that these extensions are “the right ones”, before giving more ample evidence of this fact below,
we gather in one statement several of their pleasant features.

Theorem 1.3
.

Given an arbitrary
\mu
-colored link
L
, there exist an extension of the signature
\sigma_{L}
and
of the nullity
\eta_{L}
from
(S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{\mu}
to the full torus
\mathbb{T}^{\mu}
, which satisfy the following
properties.

1.

The extensions
\sigma_{L}\colon\mathbb{T}^{\mu}\to\mathbb{Z}
and
\eta_{L}\colon\mathbb{T}^{\mu}\to\mathbb{Z}
only depend on the isotopy class of the
\mu
-colored link
L
(see Theorem
4.4
).

2.

If
L
is a (
1
-colored) oriented link, then
\sigma_{L}(1)=\sigma(\mathit{Lk}_{L})
(see Theorem
4.7
(1)).

3.

If
L=L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}
is a
\mu
-component link with
\lk(L_{1},L_{j})
not all vanishing, then
for any
\omega^{\prime}\in(S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{\mu-1}
such that
\Delta_{L}(1,\omega^{\prime})\neq 0
, we have

\sigma_{L}(1,\omega^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{+}}\sigma(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})+\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{-}}\sigma(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})\right)=\sigma_{L\setminus L_{1}}(\omega^{\prime})\,.

(See Corollary
3.4
and Theorem
4.7
(3).)

4.

For any
\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu}
(with the possible exception of
(1,\dots,1)

if
\mu\geq 2
), the integer
\sigma_{L}(\omega)
can be obtained
as the signature of a matrix evaluated at
\omega
(see Lemma
5.1
).

In a nutshell, this extension is defined as follows (see Section
4.1
for details).
We first build the
generalized Seifert surgery
on
L
,
a closed
three-dimensional manifold
M_{L}
which only depends on the colored link
L
, and which (in the ordered case) coincides with the manifold defined by Toffoli in
[
39
, Construction 4.17]
.

The point of this construction is that it admits a natural (though not unique) homomorphism
\varphi\colon H_{1}(M_{L})\to\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
, making
M_{L}
a
so-called
\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
-manifold
.
From the pair
(M_{L},\varphi)
, we then define an auxiliary link
L^{\#}
such that
M_{L^{\#}}
is a manifold that bounds over
\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
.
More precisely, we construct a four-dimensional
\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
-manifold
W_{F}
from a union of surfaces
F\subset B^{4}
bounded by
L^{\#}
, and show
that
\partial W_{F}=M_{L^{\#}}
over
\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
.
Finally, the extended signature and nullity of
L
are defined
by considering the twisted signature
and nullity of
W_{F}
.
While we provide no further detail in the present introduction, the construction of this
extension and the proof of its invariance

take up a significant portion of this article
and might be considered as its most
technical contribution (see in particular Appendix
A
).

Some explicit computations yield more evidence that these extensions are very natural indeed.

Example 1.4
.

Let
\{L(k)\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
be the family of
2
-component links illustrated in Figure
2
.
For
k\neq 0
(resp. for
k=0
) the signature function
\sigma_{L(k)}\colon(S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{2}\to\mathbb{Z}
is constant equal to
1
(resp.
0
), while
\eta_{L(k)}
is constant equal to
0
(resp.
1
), see Example
2.4
.
As computed in Examples
4.10
and
4.15
, the above extensions yield constant functions
\sigma_{L}

and
\eta_{L}
on
\mathbb{T}^{2}\setminus\{(1,1)\}
in all cases.

We now turn to the second question, i.e. to Torres-type formulas for these extended signature and nullity functions. To state these results, it is convenient to make use of the notion of
slope
, as defined and studied by Degtyarev, Florens and Lecuona in
[
14
]
. Without stating the formal definition (see Remark
4.9
), let us recall that given a
\mu
-colored link
L=L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}=:L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
with
L_{1}=:K
a knot, the associated slope is a function
assigning a value
(K/L^{\prime})(\omega^{\prime})\in\mathbb{C}\cup\{\infty\}

to each
\omega^{\prime}=(\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{\mu})\in(S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{\mu-1}
such that
\omega_{2}^{\lk(K,L_{2})}\cdots\omega_{\mu}^{\lk(K,L_{\mu})}=1
.
Most importantly for our applications, Theorem 3.2 of
[
14
]
asserts that, in generic cases, it can be computed explicitly via the Conway function
\nabla_{L}
of
L
, see Equation (
24
).

We can now state (a particular case of) our Torres formula for the signature
(see Theorem
4.7
, Remark
4.9
and Remark
4.13
for the full statement).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
L=L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}=:L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
be a
\mu
-colored link with
\mu\geq 2
and
L_{1}=:K

a knot. For all
\omega^{\prime}\in(S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{\mu-1}
, we have

\sigma_{L}(1,\omega^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})+\mathrm{sgn}((K/L^{\prime})(\omega^{\prime}))&\text{if\penalty\ $\lk(K,K^{\prime})=0$ for all\penalty\ $K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}$};\\
\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})&\text{else},\end{cases}

where
\mathrm{sgn}\colon\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}\to\{-1,0,1\}
denotes the sign function extended via
\mathrm{sgn}(\infty)=0
.

We have also obtained a Torres formula relating
\eta_{L}(1,\omega^{\prime})

with
\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})
, which involves the slope once again.
However, its formulation being rather cumbersome and not particularly illuminating, we refer the reader to Theorem
4.14
for its statement.

We now turn to the third and last question, namely the estimation of the
\omega_{1}\to 1^{\pm}
limits of multivariable signatures. Our answer to this question is among the motivations of
the results stated above. In particular, it shows that our extensions
of the signature and nullity functions are sensible ones.

First, and as already mentioned in Theorem
1.3
, these extensions are such that for any given
\mu
-colored link
L
,
and for any
\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu}
(with the possible exception of
(1,\dots,1)

if
\mu\geq 2
), the integers
\sigma_{L}(\omega)
and
\eta_{L}(\omega)
can be obtained
as the signature and nullity of a matrix evaluated at
\omega
(Lemma
5.1
).
Then, we can use elementary estimates on the difference between the limit
of the signature of a matrix and the signature of a limit (Lemma
2.1
), together with the aforementioned Torres formulas for the signature and nullity, to obtain the following result (Theorem
5.15
).

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
L=L_{1}\cup L_{2}\cup\ldots\cup L_{\mu}=:L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
be a colored link with
\mu\geq 2
and
L_{1}=:K
a knot. Consider
\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu}
with
\omega^{\prime}\in(S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{\mu-1}
.

1.

If there exists a component
K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}
with
\lk(K,K^{\prime})\neq 0
, then we have:

\left|\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{L}(\omega)-\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})\right|\leq\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})-1+\sum_{K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}}|\lk(K,K^{\prime})|-\rank A(L)\,,

where
A(L)
denotes the
\mu
-variable Alexander module of
L
.

2.

If
\lk(K,K^{\prime})=0
for all components
K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}
, then we have

\left|\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{L}(\omega)-\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})-s(\omega^{\prime})\right|\leq\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})+\varepsilon(\omega^{\prime})-\rank A(L)\,,

where

s(\omega^{\prime})=\begin{cases}+1&\text{if $(K/L^{\prime})(\omega^{\prime})\in(0,\infty)$}\\
-1&\text{if $(K/L^{\prime})(\omega^{\prime})\in(-\infty,0)$}\\
0&\text{if $(K/L^{\prime})(\omega^{\prime})\in\{0,\infty\}$}\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\varepsilon(\omega^{\prime})=\begin{cases}+1&\text{if $(K/L^{\prime})(\omega^{\prime})=0$}\\
-1&\text{if $(K/L^{\prime})(\omega^{\prime})=\infty$}\\
0&\text{else.}\end{cases}

As discussed in Sections
5.4
and
5.5
, this theorem
is quite powerful in the second, so-called
algebraically split
case.
Indeed, it implies in particular the following result (Corollary
5.16
).

Corollary 1.7
.

Let
L=K\cup L^{\prime}
be a
\mu
-colored link as above, such that
\lk(K,K^{\prime})=0
for all
K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}
. Then, we have

\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{+}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})=\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{-}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})=\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})+\mathrm{sgn}\left(-\frac{\frac{\partial\nabla_{L}}{\partial t_{1}}(1,\sqrt{\omega^{\prime}})}{\nabla_{L^{\prime}}(\sqrt{\omega^{\prime}})}\right)

for all
\omega^{\prime}\in(S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{\mu-1}
such that
\nabla_{L^{\prime}}(\sqrt{\omega^{\prime}})\neq 0
and
\frac{\partial\nabla_{L}}{\partial t_{1}}(1,\sqrt{\omega^{\prime}})\neq 0
.

In the non-algebraically split case (case 1 in Theorem
1.6
), the inequality implies a good upper bound on the difference of the two limits (see Corollary
5.19
and Remark
5.19
). However,
since it does not distinguish these two (possibly different) limits,
it does not allow
for a good estimation of each of these limits, especially if the linking
numbers are large.

To address this issue, we have also attacked this question via a totally different approach, namely coming back to the original definition of the signature and nullity
via C-complexes: this is the subject of Section
3
, and of the PhD Thesis of the second author
[
30
]
.

To put it briefly, the strategy is the same as the one of the classical proof that the limit
\lim_{\omega\to 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{K}(\omega)
vanishes if
K
is a knot:
first conjugate the Hermitian matrix
H(\omega)
by a suitable diagonal matrix,
and then estimate the difference between the limit of its signature and the signature of its limit. The result can be phrased as follows,
see Theorem
3.1
and its addendum
for the full statement.

Theorem 1.8
.

For any
\mu
-component link
L=L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}=:L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}

and any
\omega^{\prime}\in(S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{\mu-1}
, we have

\left|\lim_{\omega_{1}\rightarrow 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})-\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})\mp\rho_{\ell}(\omega^{\prime})\right|\leq\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})+\tau_{\ell}(\omega^{\prime})-\rank A(L)\,,

where
A(L)
is the multivariable Alexander module of
L
, while

\tau_{\ell}(\omega^{\prime})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if\penalty\ $\omega_{2}^{\lk(L_{1},L_{2})}\cdots\omega_{\mu}^{\lk(L_{1},L_{\mu})}=1$;}\cr 0&\text{else,}\end{cases}

and
\rho_{\ell}\colon(S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{\mu-1}\to\mathbb{Z}
is an explicit function which only depends on
\{\lk(L_{1},L_{j})\}_{j\geq 2}
.

This leads in particular to the following result (Corollary
5.5
).

Corollary 1.9
.

If
L=L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}=:L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
is a
\mu
-component link, then we have

\lim_{\omega_{1}\rightarrow 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})=\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})\pm\rho_{\ell}(\omega^{\prime})

for all
\omega^{\prime}\in(S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{\mu-1}
such that

\Delta_{L}(1,\omega^{\prime})\neq 0
.

A remarkable fact, discussed in Section
5.5
,
is that the two approaches described above are complementary.
Indeed, in the algebraically split case, the four-dimensional approach is very powerful
and the three-dimensional one less so. On the other hand, the bigger the linking numbers, the more the 4D approach looses efficiency and the 3D approach gains power.
It is quite amusing to note that in case of total linking number
|\lk(L_{1},L_{2})|+\dots+|\lk(L_{1},L_{\mu})|=1
, the two approaches give exactly the same estimate on the
limit of the signature.

2.1.
Limits of signatures for Hermitian matrices

Recall that a complex-valued square matrix
H
is said to be
Hermitian
if it coincides with its conjugate transpose
H^{*}
.

By the spectral theorem, such a matrix can be diagonalized (by a unitary matrix), and the resulting diagonal matrix has real coefficients.
As a consequence, the eigenvalues of
H
are real, and one defines
the
signature
of
H
as the integer
\sigma(H)\in\mathbb{Z}
given by the number of positive eigenvalues of
H
minus the number of negative eigenvalues. The
nullity
of
H
is defined as the
non-negative integer
\eta(H)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
equal to the number of
vanishing eigenvalues of
H
.

Many of our results are based on the following elementary but crucial lemma, whose easy proof we include for completeness.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(H(t))_{t\geq 0}
be a continuous one-parameter family of Hermitian matrices.
Then

\left|\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\sigma(H(t))-\sigma(H(0))\right|\leq\eta(H(0))-\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\eta(H(t))\,.

Proof.

By continuity, there exists some
\epsilon>0
such that
\rank(H(t))
is constant for
t\in(0,\epsilon)
. As a consequence, both
\sigma(H(t))
and
\eta(H(t))
are constant for
t\in(0,\epsilon)
. At
t=0
, precisely
\eta(H(0))-\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\eta(H(t))
eigenvalues vanish, yielding the expected upper bound on the
difference of signatures.
∎

2.2.
Signature and nullity via C-complexes

The aim of this section is to briefly recall the original definition of the signature and nullity of a colored link, following
[
11
,
6
]
.

.
A clasp intersection.

Definition 2.2
.

A
C-complex
for a
\mu
-colored link
L=L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}
is a union
S=S_{1}\cup\dots\cup S_{\mu}
of surfaces embedded in
S^{3}
such that
S
is connected and satisfies the following conditions:

1.

for all
i
, the surface
S_{i}
is a connected Seifert surface for
L_{i}
;

2.

for all
i\neq j
, the surfaces
S_{i}
and
S_{j}
are either disjoint or intersect in a finite number of
clasps
, see Figure
1
;

3.

for all
i,j,k
pairwise distinct, the intersection
S_{i}\cap S_{j}\cap S_{k}
is empty.

Such a C-complex is said to be
totally connected
if
S_{i}\cap S_{j}
is non-empty for all
i\neq j
.

The existence of a (totally connected) C-complex for any given colored link is fairly easy to establish, see
[
3
]
. On the other hand, the corresponding notion of S-equivalence is more difficult to establish, and the correct version appeared only recently
[
12
]
.

These C-complexes, which should be thought of as generalized Seifert surfaces, allow to define
generalized Seifert forms
as follows.
For any choice of signs
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{\mu})\in\{\pm 1\}^{\mu}
, let

\alpha^{\varepsilon}\colon H_{1}(S)\times H_{1}(S)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}

be the bilinear form given by
\alpha^{\varepsilon}(x,y)=\lk(x^{\varepsilon},y)
,
where
x^{\varepsilon}
denotes a well-chosen representative of the homology class
x\in H_{1}(S)
pushed-off
S_{i}
in the
\varepsilon_{i}
-normal direction (see
[
6
]
for a more precise definition). We denote by
A^{\varepsilon}
the corresponding
generalized Seifert matrices
, defined with respect to a fix basis of
H_{1}(S)
.

Consider an element
\omega=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{\mu})
of
\mathbb{T}^{\mu}_{*}\coloneqq(S^{1}\setminus\{1\})^{\mu}
, and set

H(\omega)\coloneqq\sum_{\varepsilon}\prod_{j=1}^{\mu}(1-\overline{\omega}_{j}^{\varepsilon_{j}})A^{\varepsilon}\,.

Using the identity
A^{-\varepsilon}=(A^{\varepsilon})^{T}
, one easily checks that
H(\omega)
is a Hermitian matrix, and hence admits a well-defined signature
\sigma(H(\omega))\in\mathbb{Z}
and nullity
\eta(H(\omega))\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.

Definition 2.3
.

The
signature
and
nullity
of the
\mu
-colored link
L
are the functions

\sigma_{L},\eta_{L}\colon\mathbb{T}^{\mu}_{*}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}

defined by
\sigma_{L}(\omega)\coloneqq\sigma(H(\omega))
and
\eta_{L}(\omega)\coloneqq\eta(H(\omega))
, respectively.

The fact that these functions are well-defined invariants, i.e. do not depend on the choice of the C-complex
S
for
L
, relies on the aforementioned notion of S-equivalence
[
6
,
12
]
.

Note that for any given colored link, it is not difficult
to find a C-complex and to compute the associated generalized
Seifert matrices: an algorithm has even been recently implemented in
[
15
]
.

We now present two (infinite families of) examples that will serve as running examples throughout this article.

2.3.
Signature and nullity via twisted intersection forms

We now briefly recall the four-dimensional viewpoint on the signature and nullity, following
[
10
]
and referring to Section
B.1
for details.

We first need to make a small detour into homological algebra.
Le
X
be a connected CW-complex endowed with a homomorphism
\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}=\mathbb{Z}t_{1}\oplus\dots\mathbb{Z}t_{\mu}
for some
\mu\geq 1
.
Then, any
\omega=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{\mu})\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu}
induces a group homomorphism
\pi_{1}(X)\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
by mapping
t_{i}
to
\omega_{i}
. This in turn extends to a
ring homomorphism
\phi_{\omega}\colon\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)]\to\mathbb{C}

such that
\phi_{\omega}(g^{-1})=\overline{\phi_{\omega}(g)}
for all
g\in\pi_{1}(X)
, thus endowing the field
\mathbb{C}
with a structure of right-module over the group ring
\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)]
; we denote this module by
\mathbb{C}^{\omega}
. The cellular
chain complex
C(\widetilde{X})
of the universal cover
\widetilde{X}
of
X
being a left module over this same ring, one can consider the complex vector spaces

H_{*}(X;\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\coloneqq H_{*}\left(\mathbb{C}^{\omega}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{1}(X)]}C(\widetilde{X})\right)\,.

This is one example of a construction known as the
homology of
X
with twisted coefficients
, see Section
B.1
.

Coming back to low-dimensional topology, let us consider a compact oriented
4
-manifold
W
endowed with a homomorphism
\pi_{1}(W)\to\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
.
As explained in Section
B.1
, one can define a
twisted intersection pairing

Q_{\omega}\colon H_{2}(W;\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\times H_{2}(W;\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\longrightarrow\mathbb{C}

that is Hermitian,
so one can consider the associated signature and nullity

\sigma_{\omega}(W)\coloneqq\sigma(Q_{\omega}),\quad\eta_{\omega}(W)\coloneqq\eta(Q_{\omega})\,.

We are finally ready to come back to knots and links.
Let
L=L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}
be a colored link in
S^{3}
.
A
bounding surface
for
L
is a union
F=F_{1}\cup\dots\cup F_{\mu}
of properly embedded, locally flat, compact,
connected oriented surfaces
F_{i}\subset B^{4}
which only intersect
each other transversally (in double points), and such that the oriented boundary
\partial F_{i}
is equal to
L_{i}
.
These surfaces being locally flat, they admit tubular neighborhoods whose union we denote by
\nu(F)
. Also, let us write
V_{F}
for the exterior
B^{4}\setminus\nu(F)
of
F
in
B^{4}
, which intersects
S^{3}=\partial B^{4}
in the exterior
X_{L}\coloneqq S^{3}\setminus\nu(L)
of
L
in
S^{3}
.

As one easily shows, the abelian group
H_{1}(V_{F};\mathbb{Z})
is freely generated by the meridians of the surfaces
F_{1},\dots,F_{\mu}
,
and the inclusion induced homomorphism
H_{1}(X_{L};\mathbb{Z})\to H_{1}(V_{F};\mathbb{Z})
is an isomorphism.
As a consequence, we can apply the above technology to these spaces,
yielding in particular a complex vector space
H_{1}(X_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})

and a
\mathbb{C}^{\omega}
-twisted intersection pairing
Q_{\omega}

on
H_{2}(V_{F};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})
for any
\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu}
.

The following result is due to
[
10
]
, see also
[
6
,
8
]
.
It provides the promised four-dimensional viewpoint on the signature and nullity of a colored link.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
10
]
)
.

For any
\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu}_{*}
and any bounding surface

for
L
,
we have

\sigma_{L}(\omega)=\sigma_{\omega}(V_{F})\quad\text{and}\quad\eta_{L}(\omega)=\dim H_{1}(X_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\,.

It is this point of view on the signature and nullity that we will use
in Sections
4
and
5

As described in Section

Precomposing

of submanifolds of

\mathbb{C}^{\omega}

Note that the twisted intersection form on

Given three Lagrangian subspaces

of a finite-dimensional complex vector space

endowed with a skew-Hermitian form

where

Theorem 2.8

(
[

)
.

In the setting above and for any

\sigma_{\omega}(Y)=\sigma_{\omega}(Y_{-})+\sigma_{\omega}(Y_{+})+\mathit{Maslov}(\mathcal{L}_{-},\mathcal{L}_{0},\mathcal{L}_{+})\,.

2.5.
Plumbed three-manifolds
over
\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}

The aim of this section is to
recall the definition of plumbed
3
-manifolds, and to use them to define a closed
3
-manifold
M_{L}

associated to an arbitrary
\mu
-colored link
L
, mildly extending
[
39
, Construction 4.17]
.
We also show that
M_{L}
is naturally (though not uniquely)
equipped with a homomorphism
\varphi\colon\pi_{1}(M_{L})\to\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
,
thus defining an element in the bordism group
\Omega_{3}(\mathbb{Z}^{\mu})
.
Finally, we state a technical lemma about plumbed
3-manifolds, whose proof can be found in Appendix
A
.

We start by recalling the definition of these manifolds, following and slightly extending the presentation of
[
10
, Section 4.2]
.

Let
\Gamma=(V,E)
be a finite unoriented graph. Following
the classical textbook
[
38
]
, we write
E
for the set of
oriented
edges, and
s,t\colon E\to V
for the source and target maps, respectively.
The graph is unoriented in the sense that the set
E
is endowed with an involution
e\mapsto\overline{e}
such that
\overline{e}\neq e
and
s(\overline{e})=t(e)
for all
e\in E
.
We call such a graph
\Gamma
a
plumbing graph
if it has no loop (i.e. no edge
e\in E
such that
s(e)=t(e)
) and if it is endowed
with the following decorations.

\bullet

Each vertex
v\in V
is
decorated by a compact, oriented, possibly disconnected surface
F_{v}
, possibly with boundary.

\bullet

Each edge
e\in E
is labeled by a sign
\varepsilon(e)=\pm 1
such that
\varepsilon(\overline{e})=\varepsilon(e)
, and comes with the specification of a connected component of
F_{s(e)}
.

Such a plumbing graph
\Gamma
determines an oriented 3-dimensional manifold
P(\Gamma)
via the following construction.
For each oriented edge
e\in E
, we choose an embedded open disk
D_{e}

in the corresponding connected component of
F_{s(e)}
so that the
disks
\{D_{e}\}_{e\in E}
are disjoint. For each
v\in V
, we then set

F_{v}^{\circ}\coloneqq F_{v}\setminus\bigsqcup_{s(e)=v}D_{e}\,.

The associated
plumbed
3
-manifold
is defined as

P(\Gamma)\coloneqq\Big(\bigsqcup_{v\in V}F_{v}^{\circ}\times S^{1}\Big)/\sim\,,

where each pair of edges
e,\overline{e}\in E
yields the following identification of
F^{\circ}_{s(e)}\times S^{1}
and
F^{\circ}_{s(\overline{e})}\times S^{1}
along one of their boundary components:

(6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(-\partial D_{e})\times S^{1}&\longrightarrow(-\partial D_{\overline{e}})\times S^{1}\\
(x,y)&\longmapsto(y^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-}\varepsilon(e)},x^{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}-}\varepsilon(e)})\,.\end{split}

Note that since these homeomorphisms reverse the orientation, the resulting
3
-manifold
P(\Gamma)
is endowed with an orientation which extends the orientation of each
F^{\circ}_{v}\times S^{1}
. Note also
that the boundary of
P(\Gamma)
consists of one torus for each
boundary component of
\bigsqcup_{v\in V}F_{v}
.

We now come to the construction of the closed 3-manifold
M_{L}
. Given a
\mu
-colored link
L
,
consider the plumbing graph
\Gamma_{L}
defined as follows:

•

The vertex set of
\Gamma_{L}
is given by the colors
V=\{1,\dots,\mu\}
, the vertex
i
being decorated
with the surface
\bigsqcup_{K\subset L_{i}}D_{K}
consisting of disjoint oriented closed discs indexed by the components of color
i
.

•

Given two components
K,K^{\prime}
of different colors, the corresponding discs are linked by
|\lk(K,K^{\prime})|
edges, and every such edge
e
is decorated with the sign
\varepsilon(e)=\mathrm{sgn}(\lk(K,K^{\prime}))
.

We will write
P(L)
for the associated plumbed manifold
P(\Gamma_{L})
. Note that the orientation reversing automorphisms of
D_{K}\times S^{1}
given by
(x,y)\mapsto(x,y^{-1})
define an orientation reversing homeomorphism
P(L)\to P(\overline{L})
, where
\overline{L}
stands for the mirror image of
L
, and thus an orientation preserving homeomorphism

(7)

P(\overline{L})\simeq-P(L)\,.

Note also that this oriented compact
3
-manifold has boundary
\partial P(L)=\bigsqcup_{K\subset L}\partial D_{K}\times S^{1}
.
Therefore, it is possible to glue
P(L)

and
X_{L}=S^{3}\setminus\nu(L)
along their homeomorphic boundaries, and we do so in the following way. For each component
K\subset L
, recall that a
meridian
is an oriented simple closed curve
m_{K}\subset\partial\nu(K)
whose class vanishes in
H_{1}(\nu(K))
and satisfies
\lk(m_{K},K)=1
.
A
Seifert longitude
is an oriented simple closed curve
\ell_{K}\subset\partial\nu(K)
such that
[\ell_{K}]=[K]\in H_{1}(\nu(K))
and

(8)

\lk(\ell_{K},L_{i})\coloneqq\sum_{K^{\prime}\subset L_{i}}\lk(\ell_{K},K^{\prime})=0\,.

In other words, this is the longitude obtained by the intersection of
\partial\nu(K)
with a Seifert surface for the
sublink
L_{i}
of color
i
, hence the terminology.
Let us glue
X_{L}
and
P(L)
along their boundary via the homeomorphism
\partial D_{K}\times S^{1}\simeq\partial\nu(K)
obtained by mapping
\ast_{K}\times S^{1}
(for some
\ast_{K}\in\partial D_{K}
) to a meridian
m_{K}
, and
\partial D_{K}\times\ast
(for some
\ast\in S^{1}
) to a Seifert longitude
\ell_{K}
. The orientations on
X_{L}
and on
P(L)
can be seen to induce the same orientation on
the boundary tori (see Remark
2.9
.3). Therefore, we reverse the orientation of
P(L)
and define

M_{L}\coloneqq X_{L}\cup_{\partial}-P(L)\,,

which is an oriented closed 3-manifold.
We call it the
generalized Seifert surgery
on
L
.

Example 2.10
.

1.

If
L
is an oriented link (interpreted as a 1-colored link), then
M_{L}
is the so-called
Seifert sugery

on
L
, as defined in
[

, hence the terminology. In such a case, and unless

is a knot, this manifold differs
from
[

, Construction 4.17]

2.

If

is the

is homeomorphic to

, and

is homeomorphic to the 3-dimensional torus.

3.

If

is a colored link with all linking numbers

is the
0
-surgery on

. (This includes the case of knots.)
For example, if

is the 3-colored Borromean rings, then

The main point of this construction is that
M_{L}
is naturally (though not uniquely) a
\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
-manifold
, i.e. is endowed with a homomorphism
\pi_{1}(M_{L})\to\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
. More precisely, we have the following result.

Lemma 2.11
.

The homomorphism
\varphi_{X}\colon H_{1}(X_{L})\to\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
defined by
\varphi_{X}([\gamma])=\left(\lk(\gamma,L_{i})\right)_{i}
extends to
\varphi\colon H_{1}(M_{L})\to\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
such that
\varphi([\ast_{i}\times S^{1}])=t_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
for any
\ast_{i}\in D_{K}
with
K\subset L_{i}
.

Proof.

For any component
K\subset L_{i}
,
let
\varphi_{K}\colon H_{1}(D_{K}^{\circ}\times S^{1})\to\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
be defined by

\varphi_{K}([\ast_{i}\times S^{1}])=t_{i}\,,\quad\varphi_{K}([\partial D_{e}\times\ast])=\varepsilon(e)\,t_{j}

for all
\ast_{i}\in D_{K}
with
K\subset L_{i}
and
\ast\in S^{1}
,
and for any edge
e
with
s(e)
the vertex
K
and
t(e)
a vertex
K^{\prime}\subset L_{j}
. Since this is consistent with the gluing (
6
), Mayer-Vietoris arguments show that the homomorphisms
\varphi_{K}
can be extended to a well-defined (though in general not unique) homomorphism
\varphi_{P}\colon H_{1}(P(L))\to\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
. For each
\ast_{i}
and
\ast
as above,
this map satisfies

\varphi_{P}([\ast_{i}\times S^{1}])=t_{i}=\varphi_{X}([m_{K}])\,.

Moreover, since the sign
\varepsilon(e)
of an edge
e
as above is equal to
\mathrm{sgn}(\lk(K,K^{\prime}))
, we get

\displaystyle\varphi_{P}([\partial D_{K}\times\ast])

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\sim K}\varphi_{K}([\partial D_{e}\times\ast])=\sum_{e\sim K}\varepsilon(e)\,t_{j}=\sum_{j\neq i}\sum_{K^{\prime}\subset L_{j}}\varepsilon(e)|\lk(K,K^{\prime})|\,t_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\neq i}\lk(K,L_{j})\,t_{j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Seifert}}}{{=}}\lk(\ell_{K},L_{i})\,t_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}\lk(K,L_{j})\,t_{j}=\sum_{j}\lk(\ell_{K},L_{j})\,t_{j}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{X}([\ell_{K}])\,.

Since this is consistent with the gluing
M_{L}=X_{L}\cup_{\partial}-P(L)
, a Mayer-Vietoris argument concludes the proof.
∎

.

3.
Limits of signatures: the 3D approach

The purpose of this section is to evaluate the limits of multivariable signatures using their definition via C-complexes described in Section
2.2
. More precisely, we start in Section
3.1
by the statement of the results, together
with examples and consequences. The proof of the main

3.1.
Main result and consequences

Throughout this section, we assume for simplicity that the colored link
L=L_{1}\cup L_{2}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}=:L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
is a
\mu
-component link, i.e. that each sublink
L_{i}
is a knot. Note however that we expect our methods to extend to the case of an arbitrary colored link,
see in particular Remark
5.10
.

We shall adopt the notation
\ell_{j}:=\lk(L_{1},L_{j})
together with
s_{j}:=\mathrm{sgn}(\ell_{j})
for
2\leq j\leq\mu
, and
|\ell|:=|\ell_{2}|+\dots+|\ell_{\mu}|
.
Also, we write
\rho\colon\mathbb{T}^{2}\to\{-1,0,1\}
for the symmetric function defined by

(10)

\rho(z_{1},z_{2}):=\mathrm{sgn}\left[i(z_{1}z_{2}-1)(\overline{z}_{1}-1)(\overline{z}_{2}-1)\right]

for
z_{1},z_{2}\in S^{1}
,
whose graph is sketched in Figure
6
.
Note that
i(z_{1}z_{2}-1)(\overline{z}_{1}-1)(\overline{z}_{2}-1)
is real for all
z_{1},z_{2}\in S^{1}
, so
its sign
\rho(z_{1},z_{2})\in\{-1,0,1\}
is well-defined.
Moreover, it satisfies the identity
\rho(z_{1},z_{2})=-\rho(\overline{z}_{1},\overline{z}_{2})
for all
(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{T}^{2}
.
This extends to a function
\rho\colon\mathbb{T}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}
via

(11)

\rho(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}):=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\rho(z_{j},z_{j+1}\cdots z_{n})\,.

We are now ready to state the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.1
.

For a
\mu

L=L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}=:L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
and all

\left|\lim_{\omega_{1}\rightarrow 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})-\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})\mp\rho_{\ell}(\omega^{\prime})\right|\leq\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})+\tau_{\ell}(\omega^{\prime})-\rank A(L)\,,

where
A(L)
denotes the multivariable Alexander module of
L
, while
\rho_{\ell}
and
\tau_{\ell}
are given by

(12)

\rho_{\ell}(\omega^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\rho(\underbrace{\omega^{s_{2}}_{2},\dots,\omega^{s_{2}}_{2}}_{|\ell_{2}|},\dots,\underbrace{\omega^{s_{\mu}}_{\mu},\dots,\omega^{s_{\mu}}_{\mu}}_{|\ell_{\mu}|})&\text{if\penalty\ $|\ell|>0$;}\cr 0&\text{else,}\end{cases}\qquad\tau_{\ell}(\omega^{\prime})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if\penalty\ $\omega_{2}^{\ell_{2}}\cdots\omega_{\mu}^{\ell_{\mu}}=1$;}\cr 0&\text{else,}\end{cases}

for
\omega^{\prime}=(\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{\mu})\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}
.

The function
\rho_{\ell}
can be presented via the closed formula (
12
), but it also
admits the following elementary geometric description.

Addendum
.

Given any
\ell=(\ell_{2},\dots,\ell_{\mu})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mu-1}\setminus\{0\}
, let us denote by
\Sigma_{\ell}
the hypersurface

\Sigma_{\ell}:=\{\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}\,|\,\tau_{\ell}(\omega^{\prime})=1\}=\{(\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{\mu})\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}\,|\,\omega_{2}^{\ell_{2}}\cdots\omega_{\mu}^{\ell_{\mu}}=1\}\,,

which consists of
|\ell|-1
parallel hyperplans.
Then, the function
\rho_{\ell}\colon\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}\to\mathbb{Z}
is uniquely determined by the following properties:

1.

it is constant on the connected components of
\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}\setminus\Sigma_{\ell}
and of
\Sigma_{\ell}
;

2.

it takes its maximum value
|\ell|-1
when
\omega_{j}\to 1^{s_{j}}
for all
j
such that
\ell_{j}\neq 0
;

3.

moving away from the component of
\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}\setminus\Sigma_{\ell}
described by the second point above, it jumps by
-1
when entering a component of
\Sigma_{\ell}
and by
-1
when exiting it, eventually reaching its minimal value

on the component where
\omega_{j}\to 1^{-s_{j}}
for all
j
such that
\ell_{j}\neq 0
.

We defer the proof of Theorem

and of its addendum to Section

Example 3.2
.

Let us compute the function
\rho_{\ell}
in the case
\mu=2
, with linking number
\ell
of sign

,
using the formula (

).
If
\ell=0
, then
\rho_{\ell}
is identically zero. For
\ell\neq 0
, we have

\rho_{\ell}(\omega)=\rho(\overbrace{\omega^{s},\dots,\omega^{s}}^{|\ell|})=\sum_{j=1}^{|\ell|-1}\rho(\omega^{s},\omega^{s(|\ell|-j)})=s\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{|\ell|-1}\rho(\omega,\omega^{j})\,,

where these (empty) sums are understood as vanishing if
|\ell|=1
.
Note that
\rho(\omega,\omega^{j})
is determined by the following properties: it vanishes at all
\omega\in S^{1}\setminus\{1\}
such that
\omega^{j}=1
or
\omega^{j+1}=1
, is equal to

for
\omega\to 1^{+}
, and
alternates sign at each zero.
Writing
\omega=\exp\left(2\pi i\theta\right)
with
\theta\in(0,1)
,
this easily leads to

(13)

Corollary 3.7
.

Suppose that
L=L_{1}\cup L_{2}
is a two-component link such that
\ell=\lk(L_{1},L_{2})\neq 0
. Then, for any
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}=\pm
,
the limit of
\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
as
\omega_{1}
tends to
1^{\epsilon_{1}}
and
\omega_{2}
to
1^{\epsilon_{2}}
exists and is given by

(14)

\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\epsilon_{1}},\omega_{2}\to 1^{\epsilon_{2}}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\,(\ell-\mathrm{sgn}(\ell))\,.

If
\ell=0
, then the inequality

|\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})|\leq 1-\rank A(L)

holds for all
(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
in some neighborhood of
(1,1)
in
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{\ast}

In particular, if
\Delta_{L}
vanishes, then the four limits exist and are equal to zero.

Proof.

First, observe that the assumption
\ell\neq 0
guarantees that
\Delta_{L}(1,1)\neq 0
. Hence, there exists a neighborhood
(1,1)\in U\subset S^{1}\times S^{1}
such that the signature function
\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
is constant on each connected component of

These connected components correspond to the four possible limits
of
\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})

Using Corollary
3.4
and the fact that the Levine-Tristram signature of a knot vanishes near

we get

\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\epsilon_{1}},\omega_{2}\to 1^{\epsilon_{2}}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\epsilon_{1}}}\left(\sigma_{L_{1}}(\omega_{1})+\epsilon_{2}\rho_{\ell}(\omega_{1})\right)=\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\epsilon_{1}}}\epsilon_{2}\rho_{\ell}(\omega_{1})\,.

13

If

for all

\sigma_{L}

for a fixed value of
\omega^{\prime}=(\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{\mu})\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}
, assuming
\mu\geq 2

Consider an associated C-complex
S=S_{1}\cup\ldots\cup S_{\mu}
.
Without loss of generality (e.g. via the second move in
[
6

), it may be assumed that
S^{\prime}:=S\setminus S_{1}
is connected.
Let
\mathcal{A}

be a set of curves in

\mathcal{B}
be a set of curves in

such that the classes of the elements of

H(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})=\sum_{\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}^{\mu}}\prod_{j=1}^{\mu}(1-\overline{\omega}_{j}^{\varepsilon_{j}})A^{\varepsilon}

can be presented in a block form that we denote by

H(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})=\begin{bmatrix}C&D\\
E&F\\
\end{bmatrix}

Observe that since

are disjoint from

\lk(x^{\varepsilon},-)

do not depend on
\varepsilon_{1}
. Therefore, the coefficients of the matrices

and

are multiples of
(1-\omega_{1})(1-\overline{\omega}_{1})=|1-\omega_{1}|^{2}

by polynomial functions of
\omega_{2},\dots,\omega_{\mu}

Note also that
\frac{1}{|1-{\omega_{1}}|^{2}}C
coincides with the matrix
H^{\prime}(\omega^{\prime})
obtained from the (connected) C-complex
S^{\prime}
with respect to the basis of
H_{1}(S^{\prime})
represented by
\mathcal{A}
.

Now, consider the block-diagonal matrix
P(\omega_{1})=\begin{bmatrix}(1-\omega_{1})^{-1}\mathit{Id}&0\\
0&(1-\omega_{1})^{-1/2}\mathit{Id}\\
\end{bmatrix}

\widehat{H}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime}):=P(\omega_{1})H(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})P(\omega_{1})^{*}\,.

By the considerations above, we get

\lim_{\omega_{1}\rightarrow 1^{\pm}}\widehat{H}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})=\lim_{\omega_{1}\rightarrow 1^{\pm}}\begin{bmatrix}\frac{C}{|1-\omega_{1}|^{2}}&&\frac{D}{|1-\omega_{1}|(1-\omega_{1})^{1/2}}\\
\frac{E}{|1-\omega_{1}|(1-\overline{\omega}_{1})^{1/2}}&&\frac{F}{|1-\omega_{1}|}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}H^{\prime}(\omega^{\prime})&&0\\
0&&F^{\pm}(\omega^{\prime})\end{bmatrix}\,,

with
F^{\pm}(\omega^{\prime}):=\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\pm}}\frac{F}{|1-\omega_{1}|}
.
The equality
\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\pm}}\frac{1-\overline{\omega}_{1}}{|1-\omega_{1}|}=\pm i

leads to

(15)

F^{\pm}(\omega^{\prime})=\pm i\!\!\!\!\sum_{\varepsilon^{\prime}\in\{\pm 1\}^{\mu-1}}\prod_{j=2}^{\mu}(1-\overline{\omega}_{j}^{\varepsilon_{j}})\big(A_{\mathcal{B}}^{(+1,\varepsilon^{\prime})}-A_{\mathcal{B}}^{(-1,\varepsilon^{\prime})}\big)\,,

where

is the restriction
of
A^{\varepsilon}

to the subspace spanned by the classes of the curves in
\mathcal{B}
.

Since
\sigma(\widehat{H}(\omega))=\sigma(H(\omega))=\sigma_{L}(\omega)
and
\eta(\widehat{H}(\omega))=\eta(H(\omega))=\eta_{L}(\omega)

\left|\lim_{\omega_{1}\rightarrow 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})-\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})\mp\rho_{L}(\omega^{\prime})\right|\leq\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})+\tau_{L}(\omega^{\prime})-\lim_{\omega_{1}\rightarrow 1}\eta_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})\,,

where

(16)

\rho_{L}(\omega^{\prime}):=\sigma\left(F^{+}(\omega^{\prime})\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{L}(\omega^{\prime}):=\eta\left(F^{+}(\omega^{\prime})\right)\,.

By Lemma
5.2
, it now only remains to show that the functions
\rho_{L}
and
\tau_{L}
defined via (
15
) and (
16

\rho_{\ell}

(Here, we take the liberty to appeal to the forthcoming Lemma

based the four-dimensional point of view on the nullity; alternatively, the case

The functions

For any

F_{k,k-1}=\overline{F}_{k-1,k}=\frac{i}{1-\omega^{s(k)}_{c(k)}},\qquad F_{k,k}=\frac{i\cdot(\omega^{s(k)}_{c(k)}\omega^{s(k+1)}_{c(k+1)}-1)}{(1-\omega^{s(k)}_{c(k)})(1-\omega^{s(k+1)}_{c(k+1)})}\,.

Lemma 3.11
.

The functions
\rho_{L}
and
\tau_{L}
are invariant under the following transformations:

1.

removal of two adjacent clasps of the same color and opposite signs (as long as
S
remains connected);

2.

permutation of two adjacent clasps of different colors.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.10
, we only need to check that for all
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}_{*}^{\mu-1}
, the signature and nullity of the
tridiagonal Hermitian matrix
F
given by Equations (
17
) are
unchanged by these two transformations.

To show the invariance under the first transformation, observe that
F_{k,k}
vanishes if
c(k)=c(k+1)
and
s(k)\neq s(k+1)
. Note also that
F_{k,k-1}
never vanishes for
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}
. Renumbering the clasps starting with the
(k+2)^{\text{th}}
one (and thus ending with the
(k+1)^{\text{th}}
one), the corresponding matrix
F
is of the form

F=\begin{bmatrix}F^{\prime}&\xi&0\cr\xi^{*}&\alpha&\lambda\cr 0&\overline{\lambda}&0\end{bmatrix}\,,

with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R},\lambda\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
, and
F^{\prime}
the matrix corresponding to the C-complex with both clasps removed. The fact that the signature and nullity of
F
and
F^{\prime}
coincide is well-known, see e.g. the proof of the invariance of the Levine-Tristram signature in
[
28
]
.

We are left with the proof that the signature and nullity of
F
are unchanged when permuting two adjacent clasps
of different colors. Without loss of generality,
let us assume that these two clasps are the two last ones in the linear numbering
1,\dots,n
, and let us denote the occurring variables by
z_{1}:=\omega_{c(n-2)}^{s(n-2)},\penalty\ z_{2}:=\omega_{c(n-1)}^{s(n-1)}
and
z_{3}:=\omega_{c(n)}^{s(n)}
. By Equation (
17
), we thus need to compare two matrices of the form

F=\begin{bmatrix}F_{0}&\xi&0\cr\xi^{*}&\frac{i\cdot(z_{1}z_{2}-1)}{(1-z_{1})(1-z_{2})}&\frac{-i}{1-\overline{z}_{2}}\cr 0&\frac{i}{1-z_{2}}&\frac{i\cdot(z_{2}z_{3}-1)}{(1-z_{2})(1-z_{3})}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad F^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}F_{0}&\xi&0\cr\xi^{*}&\frac{i\cdot(z_{1}z_{3}-1)}{(1-z_{1})(1-z_{3})}&\frac{-i}{1-\overline{z}_{3}}\cr 0&\frac{i}{1-z_{3}}&\frac{i\cdot(z_{2}z_{3}-1)}{(1-z_{2})(1-z_{3})}\end{bmatrix}\,.

If
z_{2}z_{3}=1
, then
F
and
F^{\prime}
both have the signature and nullity of
F_{0}
by the first step, and the invariance holds.
If
z_{2}z_{3}\neq 1
, then one can consider the matrices

P=\begin{bmatrix}\mathit{Id}&0&0\cr 0&1&0\cr 0&\frac{1-z_{3}}{1-z_{2}z_{3}}&1\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad P^{\prime}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathit{Id}&0&0\cr 0&1&0\cr 0&\frac{1-z_{2}}{1-z_{2}z_{3}}&1\end{bmatrix}\,.

A direct computation now leads to the equality

P^{*}FP=\begin{bmatrix}F_{0}&\xi&0\cr\xi^{*}&\frac{i\cdot(z_{1}z_{2}z_{3}-1)}{(1-z_{1})(1-z_{2}z_{3})}&0\cr 0&0&\frac{i\cdot(z_{2}z_{3}-1)}{(1-z_{2})(1-z_{3})}\end{bmatrix}=(P^{\prime})^{*}F^{\prime}P^{\prime}\,,

concluding the proof.

Lemma 3.12

For any

Then, we have

\sigma(G_{n}(z))=\rho(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\eta(G_{n}(z))=\begin{cases}1&\text{if\penalty\ $z_{1}\cdots z_{n}=1$};\cr 0&\text{else},\end{cases}

Proof.

We proceed by induction on

As a consequence, as in the first step of Lemma

has the same signature and nullity as

Let us now assume that

are such that

A direct computation leads to the equality

P^{*}G_{n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n})P=G_{n-1}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n-2},z_{n-1}z_{n})\oplus\left(\frac{i\cdot(z_{n-1}z_{n}-1)}{(1-z_{n-1})(1-z_{n})}\right)\,.

By the induction hypothesis, we now get

and

\eta(G_{n}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n}))=\eta(G_{n-1}(z_{1},\dots,z_{n-2},z_{n-1}z_{n}))=\begin{cases}1&\text{if\penalty\ $z_{1}\cdots z_{n}=1$};\cr 0&\text{else},\end{cases}

4.1.

Let

4.2.
Torres formulas for the signature

The aim of this section is to relate the signature of a colored link
L=L_{1}\cup L_{2}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}=:L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
evaluated at
\omega=(1,\omega^{\prime})\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu}
with the signature of
L^{\prime}
evaluated at
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}
. On any given example, the techniques used below allow us to find a relation. However, such a fully general Torres formula does not admit an easily presentable closed form (see Remark
4.13
below). For this reason, we shall make several natural assumptions.

First of all, we restrict ourselves to
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}_{*}^{\mu-1}
.
Also, we assume that
L
belongs to one of the following three classes (which include all ordered links):

1.

oriented links (i.e. 1-colored links);

2.

\mu
-colored links
L=L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
with
\mu\geq 2
and
\lk(K,K^{\prime})=0
for all
K\subset L_{1}
and
K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}
;

3.

\mu
-colored links
L=L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
with no
K\subset L_{1}
such that
\lk(K,K^{\prime})=0
for all
K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}
.

To state the corresponding Torres formulas, we need several preliminary
notations.
Let us assume that
L=L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
is
algebraically split
,
i.e. that
\lk(K,K^{\prime})=0
for all
K\subset L_{1}
and
K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}
,
and fix
\omega=(1,\omega^{\prime})\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu}
with
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}
.
Then, we have
H_{1}(\partial\nu(L);\mathbb{C}^{\omega})=H_{1}(\partial\nu(L_{1});\mathbb{C})
,
so this space admits the natural basis
\{m_{K},\ell_{K}\}_{K\subset L_{1}}
, with
m_{K}
a meridian of
\partial\nu(K)
and
\ell_{K}
a longitude of
\partial\nu(K)
, chosen so that
\lk(L_{1},\ell_{K})=0

and
m_{K}\cdot\ell_{K}=-1
(recall Remark
2.9
).
Since the kernel of the inclusion induced map
H_{1}(\partial\nu(L);\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\to H_{1}(X_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})

is half-dimensional, it is freely generated by
n\coloneqq|L_{1}|
elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
that can be expressed as

(22)

x_{j}=\sum_{K\subset L_{1}}\alpha_{jK}m_{K}+\beta_{jK}\ell_{K}

for some complex numbers
\alpha_{jK},\beta_{jK}
depending on
L
and on
\omega
. Let
\mathcal{F}=(f_{ij})
be the
n\times n
matrix defined by

(23)

f_{ij}=-\sum_{K\subset L_{1}}\alpha_{iK}\overline{\beta_{jK}}\,.

The kernel generated by the
x_{j}
s being isotropic with respect to the sesquilinear intersection form, we have
x_{i}\cdot x_{j}=0
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
, implying that
F
is a Hermitian matrix.

We are finally ready to state the main result of the section:
three Torres-type formulas for the three cases displayed above.

Theorem 4.7
.

1.

If
L
is a (1-colored) oriented link, then
\sigma_{L}(1)=\mathrm{sgn}(\mathit{Lk}_{L})
, with
\mathit{Lk}_{L}
the linking matrix defined by (
1
).

2.

If
L=L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
is an algebraically split
\mu
-colored link with
\mu\geq 2
, then for any
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}_{*}^{\mu-1}
, we have
\sigma_{L}(1,\omega^{\prime})=\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})+\sigma(\mathcal{F})
, with
\mathcal{F}
the Hermitian matrix defined by (
23
).

3.

If
L=L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
is a
\mu
-colored link with no
K\subset L_{1}
such that
\lk(K,K^{\prime})=0
for all
K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}
, then
\sigma_{L}(1,\omega^{\prime})=\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})
for all
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}_{*}^{\mu-1}
.

Let us point out that this result
immediately implies the following (general) Torres-type formula in the case where
L_{1}
is a knot.

Corollary 4.8
.

Let
L=L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
be a
\mu
-colored link with
L_{1}
a knot. If
\mu=1
, then
\sigma_{L}(1)=0
. If
\mu\geq 2
, then for all
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}
, we have

\sigma_{L}(1,\omega^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})-\mathrm{sgn}(\alpha_{L}(\omega^{\prime})\overline{\beta_{L}(\omega^{\prime})})&\text{if $\lk(L_{1},K^{\prime})=0$ for all\penalty\ $K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}$};\\
\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})&\text{else},\end{cases}

where
\alpha_{L}(\omega^{\prime}),\beta_{L}(\omega^{\prime})\in\mathbb{C}
are such that
\alpha_{L}(\omega^{\prime})m+\beta_{L}(\omega^{\prime})\ell
generates
the kernel of the inclusion induced map
H_{1}(\partial\nu(L_{1});\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\to H_{1}(X_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})
.∎

Before starting the proof of Theorem

Remark 4.9
.

1.

In theory, the matrix
\mathcal{F}
appearing in the algebraically split case can be computed from a diagram for the link
L
. Indeed, one can first compute the Wirtinger presentation of the link group from the diagram, then use Fox calculus
to determine the homology group
H_{1}(X_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})
, and eventually
find a basis of the kernel in
H_{1}(\partial X_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})
of the inclusion induced map.

2.

If
L_{1}
is a knot, then much more can be said.
In such a case indeed,
following the terminology of
[
14
]
, the correction term
-\mathrm{sgn}(\alpha_{L}(\omega^{\prime})\overline{\beta_{L}(\omega^{\prime})})
is nothing but the sign of the
slope

(L_{1}/L^{\prime})(\omega^{\prime}):=-\frac{\alpha_{L}(\omega^{\prime})}{\beta_{L}(\omega^{\prime})}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}\,,

with the convention that
\mathrm{sgn}(\infty)=0
. By
[
14
, Theorem 3.2]
, it can be computed via

(24)

(L_{1}/L^{\prime})(\omega^{\prime})=-\frac{\frac{\partial\nabla_{L}}{\partial t_{1}}(1,\sqrt{\omega^{\prime}})}{2\nabla_{L^{\prime}}(\sqrt{\omega^{\prime}})}

as long as this fraction makes sense (i.e. as long as both the numerator and denominator do not both vanish), where
\nabla
stands
for the Conway function.

3.

If
L_{1}
is a knot, then the resulting formula (stated in the introduction as Theorem
1.5
) should be compared with
[
14
, Lemma 4.9]
which deals with the
‘literal’ extension of the signature.
The latter statement illustrates how this ‘naive’ extension is in general not well-defined.

Example 4.10
.

Consider the links
L=L(k)=L_{1}\cup L_{2}
given in Figure
2
, and let us assume
k\neq 0
(i.e. that
L(k)
is non-trivial).
As was computed in Example
2.4
, we have
\sigma_{L}(\omega)=\mathrm{sgn}(k)
for all
\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{*}
.
Since
\lk(L_{1},L_{2})=0
and
L_{2}=L^{\prime}
is a trivial knot, Corollary

leads to

\sigma_{L}(1,\omega^{\prime})=-\mathrm{sgn}(\alpha_{L}(\omega^{\prime})\overline{\beta_{L}(\omega^{\prime})})=\mathrm{sgn}((L_{1}/L^{\prime})(\omega^{\prime}))

for all
\omega^{\prime}=\omega_{2}\neq 1

The well-known value
\nabla_{L^{\prime}}(t)=(t-t^{-1})^{-1}
together with Equation (

then enable us to compute

(L_{1}/L^{\prime})(\omega^{\prime})=-k\left(\sqrt{\omega^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\omega^{\prime}}}\right)^{2}=4k\sin(\pi\theta)^{2}

In the case of the torus link

Since

is a surface with boundary, the 4-manifold

To compute the correction term, first note that the 3-manifold
M_{1}\coloneqq V_{F}\cap\nu(F^{\circ}_{1})
is equal to

, with boundary
\Sigma\coloneqq\partial\nu({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(L^{\#})_{1}})\cup\bigsqcup_{e}T_{e}

\{m_{K},\ell_{K}\}_{K\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{K}^{\#}_{1}}}

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathcal{K}^{\#}_{1}}=\{K\subset{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(L^{\#})_{1}}\,|\,\omega_{2}^{\lk(K,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(L^{\#})_{2}})}\cdots\omega_{\mu}^{\lk(K,{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}(L^{\#})_{\mu}})}=1\}\,.

4.3.

As we did for the signature in the previous section, we now want to relate the nullity of a
\mu
-colored link

Theorem 4.14

1.

If
L

where

denotes the number of components of

K
of

and

of

.

Proof of Theorem
4.14
.

Let
L=L_{1}\cup\ldots\cup L_{\mu}=:L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
be a
\mu
-colored link and let us fix
\omega=(1,\omega^{\prime})
with
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}
.
By Proposition
4.6
, the nullity of
L
at
\omega
is equal to the dimension of
H_{1}(M_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})
if
\mu\geq 2
, and to
\dim H_{1}(M_{L};\mathbb{C})-1
if
\mu=1
.
Recall also that by Theorem
4.4
and the assumption
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}
, we have

(27)

\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})=\dim H_{1}(M_{L^{\prime}};\mathbb{C}^{\omega^{\prime}})=\dim H_{1}(X_{L^{\prime}};\mathbb{C}^{\omega^{\prime}})\,.

Hence, we are left with the computation of the difference between the dimensions of
H_{1}(M_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})

and of
H_{1}(X_{L^{\prime}};\mathbb{C}^{\omega^{\prime}})
.

To do so, we apply the Mayer-Vietoris exact sequence to
the decompositions

X_{L^{\prime}}=X_{L}\cup_{\partial\nu(L_{1})}\nu(L_{1})\quad\text{and}\quad M_{L}=X_{L}\cup_{\partial\nu(L)}-P(L)\,.

Let us start with the first decomposition, which leads to the exact sequence

\displaystyle H_{1}(\partial\nu(L_{1});\mathbb{C}^{\omega})

\displaystyle\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\oplus H_{1}(\nu(L_{1});\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{L^{\prime}};\mathbb{C}^{\omega^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\rightarrow H_{0}(\partial\nu(L_{1});\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\rightarrow H_{0}(X_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\oplus H_{0}(\nu(L_{1});\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\,.

Note that both spaces
H_{0}(\partial\nu(L_{1});\mathbb{C}^{\omega})
and
H_{0}(\nu(L_{1});\mathbb{C}^{\omega})
have dimension equal to the cardinal of

\mathcal{K}_{1}=\{K\subset L_{1}\,|\,\omega_{2}^{\lk(K,L_{2})}\cdots\omega_{\mu}^{\lk(K,L_{\mu})}=1\}\,,

so the last arrow above is injective. By exactness, the second is therefore surjective.
Similarly, the space
H_{1}(\partial\nu(L_{1});\mathbb{C}^{\omega})
has dimension
2|\mathcal{K}_{1}|
, with a natural
basis consisting of the meridiens and longitudes of
elements of
\mathcal{K}_{1}
. Writing
V_{m}
and
V_{\ell}
for
the subspaces spanned by these meridiens and longitudes, respectively,
the map induced by the inclusion of
\partial\nu(L_{1})
in
\nu(L_{1})
restricts to an isomorphism
V_{\ell}\simeq H_{1}(\nu(L_{1});\mathbb{C}^{\omega})
and to the zero map on
V_{m}
.
As a consequence, we have the exact sequence

V_{m}\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{L^{\prime}};\mathbb{C}^{\omega^{\prime}})\rightarrow 0\,,

which together with (
27
), yields the equality

(28)

\dim H_{1}(X_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})=\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})+|\mathcal{K}_{1}|-\dim\ker(V_{m}\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega}))\,.

Next, consider the decomposition
M_{L}=X_{L}\cup_{\partial\nu(L)}-P(L)
, which yields the exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\ker{\iota}

\displaystyle\rightarrow H_{1}(\partial\nu(L);\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\xrightarrow{\iota}H_{1}(X_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\oplus H_{1}(P(L);\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\rightarrow H_{1}(M_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})

\displaystyle\rightarrow H_{0}(\partial\nu(L);\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\rightarrow H_{0}(X_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\oplus H_{0}(P(L);\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\rightarrow H_{0}(M_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\rightarrow 0\,.

Since the Euler characteristic of this sequence is 0, we can write

\beta_{1}(M_{L})=\dim\ker(\iota)-\beta_{1}(\partial\nu(L))+\beta_{1}(X_{L})+\beta_{1}(P(L))+\beta_{0}(\partial\nu(L))-\beta_{0}(X_{L})-\beta_{0}(P(L))+\beta_{0}(M_{L})\,,

where
\beta_{i}
denotes the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
Betti number.
Now, we can simplify this equation via the following observations.

•

We have
\beta_{0}(X_{L})=\beta_{0}(M_{L})
since both of these spaces are connected and both are either trivially or non-trivially twisted.

•

As already mentioned, we have
\beta_{0}(\partial\nu(L))=|\mathcal{K}_{1}|
and
\beta_{1}(\partial\nu(L))=2|\mathcal{K}_{1}|
.

•

The Betti number
\beta_{0}(P(L))
is given by the number of components
K\subset L_{1}
that are algebraically split from
L^{\prime}
, i.e. such that
\lk(K,K^{\prime})=0
for all
K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}
.

•

We finally turn to
\beta_{1}(P(L))
: using the assumption
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}

together with the arguments of the proof of Lemma
A.1
,
we find that each disk in
P(L)
contributes the number of punctures on it minus 1 if there are punctures, and contributes 1 otherwise; in other words, we have

\beta_{1}(P(L))=\beta_{0}(P(L))+\sum_{K\subset L_{1}}\Big(\big(\sum_{K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}}|\lk(K,K^{\prime})|\big)-1\Big)\,,

where the first sum runs over components
K
of
L_{1}
that are not algebraically split from
L^{\prime}
,
and the second sum runs over all components
K^{\prime}
of
L^{\prime}
.

Using these observations together with Equation (
28
), we get the following
general result:

(29)

\beta_{1}(M_{L})=\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})+\dim\ker(\iota)-\dim\ker(V_{m}\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{L}))+\sum_{K\subset L_{1}}\Big(\big(\sum_{K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}}|\lk(K,K^{\prime})|\big)-1\Big)\,.

We now consider the three particular cases appearing in the statement.

Let us first assume that for each
K\subset L_{1}
, there exists
K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}
with
\lk(K,K^{\prime})\neq 0
. Then, we know by Lemma
A.1
that
V_{m}\rightarrow H_{1}(P(L);\mathbb{C}^{\omega})
is trivial while
V_{\ell}\rightarrow H_{1}(P(L);\mathbb{C}^{\omega})
is an isomorphism. This implies the equality
\ker(\iota)=\ker(V_{m}\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega}))
. Putting this into formula (
29
) yields

\eta_{L}(\omega)=\beta_{1}(M_{L})=\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})+\sum_{K\subset L_{1}}\Big(\big(\sum_{K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}}|\lk(K,K^{\prime})|\big)-1\Big)\,,

with the first sum now running over all components of
L_{1}
. This gives the third case in the statement.

Let us now assume that
\lk(K,K^{\prime})=0
for all components
K\subset L_{1}
and
K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}
.
In such a case, we know from Lemma
A.1
that
V_{\ell}\rightarrow H_{1}(P(L);\mathbb{C}^{\omega})
is trivial while
V_{m}\rightarrow H_{1}(P(L);\mathbb{C}^{\omega})
is an isomorphism, leading to
\ker(\iota)=\ker(V_{\ell}\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega}))
.
Therefore, the general formula (
29
) simplifies to

(30)

\beta_{1}(M_{L})=\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})+\dim\ker(V_{\ell}\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega}))-\dim\ker(V_{m}\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega}))\,,

as the final sum now runs over an empty set.
In the special case where
L_{1}
is a knot, the dimensions of these kernels are determined by the slope
(L_{1}/L)(\omega^{\prime})
by definition (recall Remark
4.9
).
This leads to the second case in the statement.

Finally, let us assume that
L=L_{1}
is a 1-colored link.
In that case, the link
L^{\prime}
being empty and the coefficients trivial, we have
\eta_{L^{\prime}}=\dim H_{1}(X_{L^{\prime}})=0

and
V_{m}\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{L})
is an
isomorphism. Therefore, the general formula (
30
) yields

\eta_{L}(1)=\beta_{1}(M_{L})-1=\dim\ker(V_{\ell}\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{L}))-1\,.

It remains to recall that the morphism
V_{\ell}\rightarrow H_{1}(X_{L})
is presented by the matrix
\mathit{Lk}_{L}
,
leading to the first case in the statement,
and concluding the proof.
∎

5.1.
Preliminary lemmas, and the general strategy

The general idea of the 4D approach for evaluating limits of signatures is to apply Lemma
2.1
to a matrix
of the intersection form on
H_{2}(W_{F},\mathbb{C}^{\omega})
,
and then to use the Torres formulas for the signature and nullity.
For this idea to go through, we need to show that every element of
\mathbb{T}^{\mu}\setminus\{(1,\cdots,1)\}
admits an open neighborhood

such
that the intersection form on
H_{2}(W_{F},\mathbb{C}^{\omega})
can be given by a common matrix
H_{U}(\omega)
for all
\omega\in U
.
This is a consequence of the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\Lambda_{\mu}
denote the group ring
\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}]
, and let
Q(\Lambda_{\mu})
be its fraction field.
Suppose that
(W,\psi)
is a compact connected oriented
4

\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
with connected boundary, such that the composition

H_{1}(\partial W)\to H_{1}(W)\xrightarrow{\psi}\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}

is surjective and
H_{1}(W;\Lambda_{\mu})=0
.
Then, for any
j=1,\ldots,\mu
,
there exists a Hermitian matrix
H_{j}
over
Q(\Lambda_{\mu})
such that for any
\omega\in U_{j}\coloneqq\{\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu}\colon\omega_{j}\neq 1\}
, the intersection form

Q_{\omega}\colon H_{2}(W;\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\times H_{2}(W;\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\to\mathbb{C}

is represented by
H_{j}(\omega)
.
Furthermore, if
\mu=1
, then
Q_{\omega}
is represented by a Hermitian matrix
H(\omega)
for all
\omega\in S^{1}
.

We defer the proof of Lemma
5.1
to Appendix
B
.
We will also need the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

For any
\mu
-colored link
L
and any
\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu}\setminus\{(1,\dots,1)\}
, the nullity
\eta_{L}(\omega)
is bounded below by the rank of the Alexander module
H_{1}(X_{L};\Lambda_{\mu})
of
L
.

Proof.

Let
L
be an arbitrary
\mu
-colored link, and fix
\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu}\setminus\{(1,\dots,1)\}
.
Consider the Universal Coefficient Spectral Sequence from Theorem
B.3

E^{2}_{p,q}=\Tor_{p}^{\Lambda_{\mu}}\left(\mathbb{C}^{\omega},H_{q}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}M_{L}};\Lambda_{\mu})\right)\Rightarrow H_{p+q}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}M_{L}};\mathbb{C}^{\omega}).

Since
H_{0}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}M_{L}};\Lambda_{\mu})\cong\mathbb{C}
,
this spectral sequence yields an exact sequence

\Tor_{2}^{\Lambda_{\mu}}(\mathbb{C}^{\omega},\mathbb{C})\to\mathbb{C}^{\omega}\otimes_{\Lambda_{\mu}}H_{1}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}M_{L}};\Lambda_{\mu})\to H_{1}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}M_{L}};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\to\Tor_{1}^{\Lambda_{\mu}}(\mathbb{C}^{\omega},\mathbb{C})\to 0\,.

Since we assumed
\omega\neq(1,\dots,1)
, the Tor terms vanish by Lemma
4.5
and we have an
isomorphism
H_{1}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}M_{L}};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\cong\mathbb{C}^{\omega}\otimes_{\Lambda_{\mu}}H_{1}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}M_{L}};\Lambda_{\mu})
.
Therefore, Proposition
4.6
leads to

\eta_{L}(\omega)=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}H_{1}(M_{L};\mathbb{C}^{\omega})=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}\left(\mathbb{C}^{\omega}\otimes_{\Lambda_{\mu}}H_{1}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}M_{L}};\Lambda_{\mu})\right)\geq\rank_{\Lambda_{\mu}}H_{1}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}M_{L}};\Lambda_{\mu})\,.

Finally, since the modules
H_{*}(P(L);\Lambda_{\mu})
and
H_{*}(\partial\nu(L);\Lambda_{\mu})
are torsion, the Mayer-Vietoris exact sequence for
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}M_{L}}=X_{L}\cup P(L)
implies that the ranks of
H_{1}({\color[rgb]{0,0,0}M_{L}};\Lambda_{\mu})
and
H_{1}(X_{L};\Lambda_{\mu})
coincide,
concluding the proof.
∎

Since the (rank of the) Alexander module will appear quite often,
we now fix a notation for it following
[
19
]
.
For any given
\mu
-colored link
L
, let us denote by

A(L)\coloneqq H_{1}(X_{L};\Lambda_{\mu})

the associated Alexander module over the ring
\Lambda_{\mu}
.

We are now ready to prove a preliminary version of our main result.

Proposition 5.3
.

For any
\mu
-colored link
L
, the inequality

\left|\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})-\sigma_{L}(1,\omega^{\prime})\right|\leq\eta_{L}(1,\omega^{\prime})-\rank A(L)

holds for all
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}\setminus\{(1,\dots,1)\}

with at most one coordinate equal to
1
.

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary
\mu
-colored link
L
, and in case
\mu>1
, some
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}\setminus\{(1,\dots,1)\}

with at most one coordinate equal to
1
.
Set
\omega_{t}=(e^{\pm it},\omega^{\prime})\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu}
, with
t
a non-negative real number.
Let
\varphi\colon H_{1}(M_{L})\to\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
be a meridional homomorphism, set
\mu_{L}=(M_{L},\varphi)\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mu\choose 3}
and let

W_{F}
be the manifold associated with
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}L^{\#}}

as in Section
4.1
. Recall form the proof of Proposition
4.6
that the meridional homomorphism
\pi_{1}(W_{F})\to\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
is an isomorphism, which
implies that we are in the setting of Lemma
5.1
.
Hence, the intersection form on
H_{2}(W_{F};\mathbb{C}^{\omega_{t}})

can be given by a matrix
H(t)

for all
t\geq 0
.
Indeed, following the notation of Lemma
5.1
, one can take
H(t)=H(\omega_{t})
if
\mu=1
and
H(t)=H_{j}(\omega_{t})
for
some

j>1
if
\mu>1
.
The statement
now follows from Lemma
2.1
applied to
H(t)
, Definition
4.3
, Lemma
5.2
,

and from the equality
\delta_{L}(1,\omega^{\prime})=\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\pm}}\delta_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})

which is a direct consequence of the assumption on
\omega^{\prime}
.

∎

5.2.
Limits of the Levine-Tristram signature

Given an oriented link, recall the definition of the associated linking matrix
\mathit{Lk}_{L}

from Equation (
1
).

Theorem 5.4
.

For any oriented link
L
, we have

\left|\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)-\sigma(\mathit{Lk}_{L})\right|\leq\eta(\mathit{Lk}_{L})-1-\rank A(L)\,.

Proof.

Let
L
be an arbitrary oriented link. The
\mu=1
case of Proposition
5.3
reads

\left|\lim_{\omega\to 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{L}(\omega)-\sigma_{L}(1)\right|\leq\eta_{L}(1)-\rank A(L)\,,

and the statement now follows immediately from the first points of Theorems
4.7
and
4.14
.
∎

The following corollary is immediate.

Corollary 5.5
.

For any oriented link
L
, we have the inequality
\rank A(L)\leq\eta(\mathit{Lk}_{L})-1
. Moreover,
\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)=\sigma(\mathit{Lk}_{L})
whenever the equality holds.∎

A first class of links for which the equality
\rank A(L)=\eta(\mathit{Lk}_{L})-1
holds
is when the right-hand side vanishes, yielding the following result.

Corollary 5.6
.

If
L
is an oriented link such that
\eta(\mathit{Lk}_{L})=1
, then
\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)=\sigma(\mathit{Lk}_{L})
.∎

Remark 5.7
.

As we now show, the condition
\eta(\mathit{Lk}_{L})=1
is equivalent to
(t-1)^{m}
not dividing the non-vanishing Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{L}(t)
in
\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]
, thus recovering the main result of
[
2
]
.
Since this is clearly true for knots, we assume without loss of generality that
m\geq 2
. Recall that in such a case, the Hosokawa polynomial of
L

[
20
]
is defined by

\nabla_{L}(t)=\frac{\Delta_{L}(t,\dots,t)}{(t-1)^{m-2}}\in\mathbb{Z}[t,t^{-1}]\,.

By
[
20
, Theorem 2]
, the value of
\nabla_{L}(1)
is equal, up to a sign, to the determinant of the reduced linking matrix
\widetilde{\mathit{Lk}_{L}}
obtained from
\mathit{Lk}_{L}
by
deleting one row and the corresponding column. Therefore, we see that
\mathit{Lk}_{L}
has nullity
1
if and only if
0\neq\det(\widetilde{\mathit{Lk}_{L}})=\pm\nabla_{L}(1)
, which is equivalent
to
(t-1)
not dividing
\nabla_{L}(t)=\frac{\Delta_{L}(t,\dots,t)}{(t-1)^{m-2}}=\frac{\Delta_{L}(t)}{(t-1)^{m-1}}
, and to
(t-1)^{m}
not dividing
\Delta_{L}(t)
.

Another class of links for which the equality
\rank A(L)=\eta(\mathit{Lk}_{L})-1
holds
is when the left-hand side is maximal, i.e. equal to
m-1
. This is easily seen to be the case for boundary links (see e.g.
[
19
]
,
or
[
6
, Corollary 3.6]
), immediately leading to the following result.

Corollary 5.8
.

If
L
is a boundary link, then
\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)
vanishes.∎

As another direct consequence of Theorem
5.4
, we obtain
the following corollary, which refines
the last part of
[
17
, Theorem 2.1]
,
namely the inequality
\left|\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)\right|\leq m-1
.

Corollary 5.9
.

For any
m
-component oriented link
L
, we have

\left|\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)\right|\leq m-1-\rank A(L)\,.

Proof.

By the triangle inequality together with Theorem
5.4
, we get

\displaystyle\left|\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)-\sigma(\mathit{Lk}_{L})\right|+\left|\sigma(\mathit{Lk}_{L})\right|

\displaystyle\leq\eta(\mathit{Lk}_{L})+\left|\sigma(\mathit{Lk}_{L})\right|-1-\rank A(L)\leq m-1-\rank A(L)\,,

yielding the proof.
∎

Remark 5.10
.

Actually, it is not difficult to obtain Theorem
5.4
(and its corollaries) using the three-dimensional method of Section
3
.

5.3.
Limits of multivariable signatures with all variables tending to 1

The results of Section
5.2
allow us to study and in some case,
determine, the limits of multivariable signatures of colored links with
all variables tending to
1
simultaneously. More involved limits are treated in Section
5.4
.

Let
L=L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}
be an arbitrary
\mu
-colored link.
For any choice of signs
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{\mu})\in\{\pm 1\}^{\mu}
, let us write

\lim_{\omega_{j}\to 1^{\epsilon_{j}}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{\mu})\coloneqq\lim_{\omega\to 1^{+}}\sigma_{L}(\omega^{\epsilon_{1}},\dots,\omega^{\epsilon_{\mu}})\,.

Also, let us denote by
L^{\epsilon}
the oriented link given by
\epsilon_{1}L_{1}\cup\dots\cup\epsilon_{\mu}L_{\mu}
,
where
+L_{i}=L_{i}
and
-L_{i}
stands for the link
L_{i}
endowed with the opposite orientation.

Theorem 5.11
.

For any colored link
L=L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}
and any signs
\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{\mu}\in\{\pm 1\}
, we have the inequality

\Big|\lim_{\omega_{j}\to 1^{\epsilon_{j}}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{\mu})-\sigma(\mathit{Lk}_{L}^{\epsilon})-\sum_{i<j}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}\lk(L_{i},L_{j})\Big|\leq\eta(\mathit{Lk}_{L}^{\epsilon})-1-\rank A(L^{\epsilon})\,,

where
\mathit{Lk}_{L}^{\epsilon}
is the linking matrix of the oriented link
L^{\epsilon}=\epsilon_{1}L_{1}\cup\dots\cup\epsilon_{\mu}L_{\mu}
.

Proof.

Let us fix an arbitrary colored link
L=L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}
and signs
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{\mu})\in\{\pm 1\}^{\mu}
,
and let
L^{\epsilon}
be the associated oriented link defined above.
Applying Theorem
5.4
to
L^{\epsilon}
, we get

\left|\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L^{\epsilon}}(\omega)-\sigma(\mathit{Lk}_{L}^{\epsilon})\right|\leq\eta(\mathit{Lk}_{L}^{\epsilon})-1-\rank A(L^{\epsilon})\,.

By Propositions 2.5 and 2.8 of
[
6
]
, we have

\sigma_{L^{\epsilon}}(\omega)=\sigma_{L^{\epsilon}}(\omega,\dots,\omega)-\sum_{i<j}\lk(\epsilon_{i}L_{i},\epsilon_{j}L_{j})=\sigma_{L}(\omega^{\epsilon_{1}},\dots,\omega^{\epsilon_{\mu}})-\sum_{i<j}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}\lk(L_{i},L_{j})\,,

concluding the proof.
∎

The following corollary is an immediate consequence of Theorem
5.11
.

Corollary 5.12
.

Let
L
be an oriented link.
Then, for any coloring
L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}
of
L
and any signs
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{\mu})\in\{\pm 1\}^{\mu}

such that the associated linking matrix
\mathit{Lk}_{L}^{\epsilon}
has nullity equal to
1
, we have

\lim_{\omega_{j}\to 1^{\epsilon_{j}}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{\mu})=\sigma(\mathit{Lk}_{L}^{\epsilon})+\sum_{i<j}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}\lk(L_{i},L_{j})\,.\qed

Example 5.13
.

Let us consider the case of a 2-component 2-colored link
L=L_{1}\cup L_{2}
. Writing
\ell\coloneqq\lk(L_{1},L_{2})
and fixing
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2})\in\{\pm 1\}^{2}
, the corresponding linking matrix is given by

\mathit{Lk}_{L}^{\epsilon}=\begin{bmatrix}-\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\ell&\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\ell\cr\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\ell&-\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\ell\end{bmatrix}\,,

which has nullity
1
if and only if
\ell\neq 0
. In such as case, we have
\sigma(\mathit{Lk}_{L}^{\epsilon})=-\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}\,\mathrm{sgn}(\ell)
, and Corollary
5.12
leads to

\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\epsilon_{1}}\omega_{2}\to 1^{\epsilon_{2}}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\epsilon_{1}\epsilon_{2}(\ell-\mathrm{sgn}(\ell))\,.

On the other hand, if
\ell=0
and the Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{L^{\epsilon}}(t)=(t-1)^{-1}\Delta_{L}(t^{\epsilon_{1}},t^{\epsilon_{2}})
vanishes, then Theorem
5.11
yields that the limit vanishes as well.
Finally, if
\ell=0
but
\Delta_{L}(t^{\epsilon_{1}},t^{\epsilon_{2}})\neq 0
, then we can only conclude that the limit belongs to
\{-1,0,1\}
(and to
\{-1,1\}
for parity reasons).
This recovers the results of Corollary
3.7
, obtained via C-complexes.

We conclude this section with one last result,
which is a multivariable extension of Corollary
5.9
.
Its proof being almost identical, it is left to the reader.

Corollary 5.14
.

For any
m
-component colored link
L=L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}
and any signs
\epsilon_{1},\dots,\epsilon_{\mu}\in\{\pm 1\}
, we have

\Big|\lim_{\omega_{j}\to 1^{\epsilon_{j}}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{\mu})\Big|\leq m-1+\Big|\sum_{i<j}\epsilon_{i}\epsilon_{j}\lk(L_{i},L_{j})\Big|-\rank A(L^{\epsilon})\,.\qed

5.4.
Limits of multivariable signatures

Everything is now in place to show the following theorem.

Theorem 5.15
.

Let
L=L_{1}\cup L_{2}\cup\ldots\cup L_{\mu}=:L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
be a colored link with
\mu\geq 2
and
L_{1}=:K
a knot. Let us consider
\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu}
with
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}
.

1.

If there exists a component
K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}
with
\lk(K,K^{\prime})\neq 0
, then we have:

\left|\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{L}(\omega)-\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})\right|\leq\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})-1+\sum_{K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}}|\lk(K,K^{\prime})|-\rank A(L)\,.

2.

If
\lk(K,K^{\prime})=0
for all components
K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}
, then there is a well-defined slope
(K/L)(\omega^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}
for any
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}
, and we have

\left|\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{L}(\omega)-\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})-s(\omega^{\prime})\right|\leq\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})+\varepsilon(\omega^{\prime})-\rank A(L)\,,

where

s(\omega^{\prime})=\begin{cases}+1&\text{if $(K/L^{\prime})(\omega^{\prime})\in(0,\infty)$}\\
-1&\text{if $(K/L^{\prime})(\omega^{\prime})\in(-\infty,0)$}\\
0&\text{if $(K/L^{\prime})(\omega^{\prime})\in\{0,\infty\}$}\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\varepsilon(\omega^{\prime})=\begin{cases}+1&\text{if $(K/L^{\prime})(\omega^{\prime})=0$}\\
-1&\text{if $(K/L^{\prime})(\omega^{\prime})=\infty$}\\
0&\text{else.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
L=L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
be a
\mu
-colored link with
\mu\geq 2
and
L_{1}=K
a knot. For any
\omega^{\prime}\in{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}}
, Proposition
5.3
yields

\left|\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})-\sigma_{L}(1,\omega^{\prime})\right|\leq\eta_{L}(1,\omega^{\prime})-\rank A(L)\,.

The statement now follows from our Torres formulas, namely Corollary
4.8
(together with Remark
4.9
) and the second and third points of Theorem
4.14
.
∎

This result is very powerful in the algebraically split case.

Corollary 5.16
.

Let
L=L_{1}\cup L_{2}\cup\ldots\cup L_{\mu}=:L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
be a colored link with
\mu\geq 2
and
L_{1}=:K
a knot such that
\lk(K,K^{\prime})=0
for all
K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}
. Then, we have

\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{+}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})=\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{-}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})=\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})+\mathrm{sgn}\left(-\frac{\frac{\partial\nabla_{L}}{\partial t_{1}}(1,\sqrt{\omega^{\prime}})}{\nabla_{L^{\prime}}(\sqrt{\omega^{\prime}})}\right)

for all
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}
such that
\nabla_{L^{\prime}}(\sqrt{\omega^{\prime}})\neq 0
and
\frac{\partial\nabla_{L}}{\partial t_{1}}(1,\sqrt{\omega^{\prime}})\neq 0
.

Proof.

For
L=K\cup L^{\prime}
and
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}
as in the statement,
we have
\Delta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})=\pm\nabla_{L^{\prime}}(\sqrt{\omega^{\prime}})\neq 0
,
which implies that
\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})=0
via Lemma
2.6
.
By
[
14
, Theorem 3.2]
(recall Remark
4.9
), the
associated slope can be computed via

(K/L^{\prime})(\omega^{\prime})=-\frac{\frac{\partial\nabla_{L}}{\partial t_{1}}(1,\sqrt{\omega^{\prime}})}{2\nabla_{L^{\prime}}(\sqrt{\omega^{\prime}})}\,.

The assumption that
\frac{\partial\nabla_{L}}{\partial t_{1}}(1,\sqrt{\omega^{\prime}})\neq 0
(which is equivalent to
\frac{\partial\Delta_{L}}{\partial t_{1}}(1,\omega^{\prime})\neq 0
) thus implies that this slope does not vanish, yielding
\varepsilon(\omega^{\prime})=0
.
Therefore, the right-hand side of the inequality in the second case of Theorem
5.15
vanishes, leading to the result.
∎

Example 5.17
.

For the 2-component links
L(k)=K\cup L^{\prime}
of
Example
2.4
, Corollary
5.16
together with Equation (
25
) leads to

\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{+}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{-}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\mathrm{sgn}(k)\,.

Recall from Example
3.6
that this result can not be obtained via Theorem
3.1
.

Theorem
5.15
is also powerful in the special case of total
linking number equal to 1,
as it easily implies the following result (using Lemma
2.6
).

Corollary 5.18
.

Let
L=L_{1}\cup L_{2}\cup\ldots\cup L_{\mu}=:L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
be a colored link with
L_{1}=:K
a knot such that
\sum_{K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}}|\lk(K,K^{\prime})|=1
. Then, we have

\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{+}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})=\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{-}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})=\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})

for all
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}
such that
\Delta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})\neq 0
.∎

On the other hand, Theorem
5.15
is quite
weak in case of large linking numbers, as it does not distinguish between
the two possible limits. However, it does immediately provide the following upper bound on the difference of these limits.

Corollary 5.19
.

Assuming the notation of Theorem
5.15
,
we have the inequalities

\left|\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{+}}\sigma_{L}(\omega)-\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{-}}\sigma_{L}(\omega)\right|\leq 2\Big(\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})-1+\sum_{K^{\prime}\subset L^{\prime}}|\lk(K,K^{\prime})|-\rank A(L)\Big)

in case 1, and

\left|\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{+}}\sigma_{L}(\omega)-\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{-}}\sigma_{L}(\omega)\right|\leq 2\Big(\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})+\varepsilon(\omega^{\prime})-\rank A(L)\Big)

in case 2.∎

Note that the inequalities of Corollary
5.19
are often sharp.
In other (slightly vague) words, even in the case of large linking numbers, Theorem
5.15
is often “as good as it can possibly be without distinguishing the two different limits”. This is made more precise by the following remark.

Remark 5.20
.

Let us assume that
L=L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}
is an ordered link with
\Delta_{L}(1,t_{2},\dots,t_{\mu})\neq 0
. (By the Torres formula, this is equivalent to
\lk(L_{1},L_{j})
not all vanishing
and
\Delta_{L^{\prime}}\neq 0
). Then Corollary
5.19
reads

\left|\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{+}}\sigma_{L}(\omega)-\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{-}}\sigma_{L}(\omega)\right|\leq 2\Big(\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})-1+\sum_{j=2}^{\mu}|\lk(L_{1},L_{j})|\Big)

for all
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}
.
By Corollary
3.4
, Lemma
2.6
, and the addendum to Theorem
3.1
,
this is a sharp inequality for
\omega_{j}
close to
1^{s_{j}}
, where
s_{j}
denotes the sign of
\lk(L_{1},L_{j})
.

We conclude this section with an application of these results to the
limit of the Levine-Tristram signature of 2-component links.
To do so, let us first recall that by
the Torres formula (
3
)
adapted to the Conway function (see e.g. Equation (5.3) of
[
18
]
), any 2-component link
L
with vanishing linking number has Conway function of the form

\nabla_{L}(t_{1},t_{2})=(t_{1}-t^{-1}_{1})(t_{2}-t^{-1}_{2})f(t_{1},t_{2})

for some
f\in\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}_{1},t^{\pm 1}_{2}]
.

Corollary 5.21
.

If
L=L_{1}\cup L_{2}
is a 2-component oriented link with linking number
\ell

and two-variable Conway function
\nabla_{L}
, then its Levine-Tristram signature satisfies

\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)=\begin{cases}-\mathrm{sgn}(\ell)&\text{if\penalty\ $\ell\neq 0$, or if\penalty\ $\nabla_{L}=0$ (in which case\penalty\ $\ell=0$);}\\
\mathrm{sgn}(f(1,1))&\text{if\penalty\ $\ell=0$,\penalty\ $\nabla_{L}\neq 0$ and\penalty\ $f(1,1)\neq 0$};\\
\pm 1{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\text{ or $0$}}&\text{if\penalty\ $\ell=0$,\penalty\ $\nabla_{L}\neq 0$ and\penalty\ $f(1,1)=0$},\end{cases}

where in the last two cases, we have
\nabla_{L}(t_{1},t_{2})=(t_{1}-t^{-1}_{1})(t_{2}-t^{-1}_{2})f(t_{1},t_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}_{1},t^{\pm 1}_{2}]
.∎

Proof.

Recall that by Equation (
2
), the Levine-Tristram
and 2-variable signatures of a 2-component link
L
are related via
\sigma_{L}(\omega)=\sigma_{L}(\omega,\omega)-\ell

for all
\omega\in S^{1}\setminus\{1\}
. Therefore, we need to compute the limit of
\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})
with both variables tending to
1
.

If
\ell\neq 0
, then we know that
\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega,\omega)=\ell-\mathrm{sgn}(\ell)
by Corollary
5.12
(see also Example
5.13
, Corollary
3.7
, and the first point of Remark
3.8
),
yielding the result. We can therefore assume
\ell=0
, and use Theorem
5.15
. In this case, it reads

(31)

\left|\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})-\sigma_{L_{2}}(\omega_{2})-s(\omega_{2})\right|\leq\eta_{L_{2}}(\omega_{2})+\varepsilon(\omega_{2})-\rank A(L)\,,

with
s(\omega_{2})
and
\varepsilon(\omega_{2})
determined by the slope
(L_{1}/L_{2})(\omega_{2})
as described in the statement.
Recall also that this slope is equal to

(32)

(L_{1}/L_{2})(\omega_{2})=-\frac{\frac{\partial\nabla_{L}}{\partial t_{1}}(1,\sqrt{\omega_{2}})}{2\nabla_{L_{2}}(\sqrt{\omega_{2}})}

whenever this fraction is not
\frac{0}{0}
.
Taking the limit
\omega_{2}\to 1^{\pm}
in (
31
) yields

\left|\lim_{\omega\to 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{L}(\omega,\omega)-\lim_{\omega\to 1^{\pm}}s(\omega)\right|\leq\lim_{\omega\to 1^{\pm}}\varepsilon(\omega)-\rank A(L)\,.

As one easily sees, the limit of the slope can be computed using Equation (
32
) together with the Torres formula
for the Conway function: if
L=L_{1}\cup L_{2}
is a 2-component link
with vanishing linking number, then we have
\nabla_{L}(t_{1},t_{2})=(t_{1}-t_{1}^{-1})(t_{2}-t_{2}^{-1})f(t_{1},t_{2})
for some
f\in\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}_{1},t^{\pm 1}_{2}]
, and
\lim_{\omega\to 1}(L_{1}/L_{2})(\omega)=f(1,1)
up to a positive multiple.
As a consequence, we have the equality

\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)=\lim_{\omega\to 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{L}(\omega,\omega)=\mathrm{sgn}(f(1,1))

in all possible cases, except possibly if
f(1,1)=0
while
\Delta_{L}\neq 0
. In this later case, the inequality reads
|\lim_{\omega\to 1}\sigma_{L}(\omega)|\leq 1
.
∎

Remark 5.22
.

1.

A family of links of the second kind is given by links of the form of the Whitehead link (or any
L(k)
of Figure
2
with
k\neq 0
) connected summed with two arbitrary knots.

2.

The third and last case can also happen.
Indeed, it is known that the Torres conditions are sufficient for
\ell=0
, see e.g.
[
36
]
. Hence, there is no additional condition, in particular on the possible values of
f(1,1)
.

3.

Given the fact that both
\sigma_{L}
and
\nabla_{L}
can be computed from generalized Seifert matrices, it is plausible that Corollary
5.21
can also be obtained using the methods of Section
3
.

5.5.
Comparison of the three and four-dimensional approaches

In this short final section, we compare the
4D-results of Section
5
with the 3D-results of Section
3
regarding
the limits of multivariable signatures, namely Theorem
5.15
and Theorem
3.1
together with their corollaries.

Throughout this section, we assume that
L=L_{1}\cup\dots\cup L_{\mu}=:L_{1}\cup L^{\prime}

is an ordered link.

Let us first assume that the total linking number
|\ell|\coloneqq\sum_{j=2}^{\mu}|\lk(L_{1},L_{j})|
is equal to
1
.
In such as case, the functions
\rho_{\ell}
and
\tau_{\ell}
of (
12
) are identically zero. Therefore, Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
5.15
yield precisely the same result, namely the inequality

\left|\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})-\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})\right|\leq\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})-\rank A(L)

for all
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}
.
In particular, Corollary
5.18
should be understood as
special case of Corollary
3.4
.

If the linking numbers satisfy
|\ell|>1
, then Theorem
5.15
is in general rather weak
for determining the limits of signatures. Indeed, and as already explained in Section
5.4
, the inequality

\left|\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{L}(\omega)-\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})\right|\leq\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})-1+\sum_{j=2}^{\mu}|\lk(L_{1},L_{j})|-\rank A(L)

is plagued by the fact that it does not distinguish between
the limits
\omega_{1}\to 1^{+}
and
\omega_{1}\to 1^{-}
. In that case,
Theorem
3.1
is obviously stronger, as it determines the limits of signatures for generic
\omega^{\prime}

(recall Corollary
3.4
).

On the other hand, Theorem
5.15
outcompetes its three-dimensional contender in the algebraically split case
\lk(L_{1},L_{2})=\dots=\lk(L_{1},L_{\mu})=0
.
Indeed, Theorem
3.1
reads

\left|\lim_{\omega_{1}\to 1^{\pm}}\sigma_{L}(\omega_{1},\omega^{\prime})-\sigma_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})\right|\leq\eta_{L^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})+1-\rank A(L)

for all
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu-1}_{*}
.
A much stronger statement is obtained via Theorem
5.15
, as it determines the
limits of signatures for generic
\omega^{\prime}

(recall Corollary
5.16
).

Therefore, and in our opinion quite remarkably,
the three- and four-dimensional approaches turn out
to give complementary results.

Corollary A.4
.

Let

be a balanced plumbing graph with vertices given by closed oriented surfaces, and let

By hypothesis, the

\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}

A.3

Y\subset X
is a subcomplex containing the basepoint, then the action of
\pi_{1}(X)
equips the chain complex
C_{\ast}(\widetilde{X},p^{-1}(Y);\mathbb{C})
with the structure of a (left)
\Lambda_{\mu}
-module.
Given any (right)
\Lambda_{\mu}
-module
M
,
let us define the chain and cochain complexes of
\Lambda_{\mu}
-modules

\displaystyle C_{\ast}(X,Y;M)

\displaystyle=M\otimes_{\Lambda_{\mu}}C_{\ast}(\widetilde{X},p^{-1}(Y)),

\displaystyle C^{\ast}(X,Y;M)

\displaystyle=\hom_{\Lambda_{\mu}}(C_{\ast}(\widetilde{X},p^{-1}(Y))^{\tr},M).

The homology
H_{\ast}(X,Y;M)
(resp. cohomology
H^{\ast}(X,Y;M)
)
of the above (co)chain complex is called the
twisted
(co)homology
of
X
.
Note that both
H_{\ast}(X,Y;M)
and
H^{\ast}(X,Y;M)
are modules
over
\Lambda_{\mu}
.
Furthermore, if
M
is an
(R,\Lambda_{\mu})
-bimodule for some ring
R
, then
H_{\ast}(X,Y;M)
and
H^{\ast}(X,Y;M)
inherit the structure of left
R
-modules.

Remark B.2
.

1.

The ring
\Lambda_{\mu}=\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}]
being commutative, there is no problem with distinguishing left and right modules, hence the
parenthesis above around these words.
In the general setting however,
M
is required to be an
(R,\mathbb{C}[\pi_{1}(X)])
-bimodule for some ring
R
, hence we tensor by
M
from the left
in the definition of
C_{\ast}(X,Y;M)

to be consistent with sources
[
9
,
8
,
10
]
.

2.

It is for the same consistency reasons that we transpose
the cellular chain complex in the definition of
C^{\ast}(X,Y;M)
, rather than the module
M
.
Note however that since
\Lambda_{\mu}
is commutative, for any two
\Lambda_{\mu}
-modules
M
and
N
, we have

\hom_{\Lambda_{\mu}}(N^{\tr},M)=\hom_{\Lambda_{\mu}}(N,M^{\tr})=\hom_{\Lambda_{\mu}}(N,M)^{\tr}\,,

which consists of the additive maps
f\colon N\to M
such that
f(\lambda\cdot n)=\overline{\lambda}\cdot f(n)
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mu}
and
n\in N
.
This leads to

H^{\ast}\left(\hom_{\Lambda_{\mu}}(C_{\ast}(\widetilde{X},p^{-1}(Y))^{\tr},M)\right)=H^{\ast}\left(\hom_{\Lambda_{\mu}}(C_{\ast}(\widetilde{X},p^{-1}(Y)),M)\right)^{\tr}\,,

so the transposed module in the definition of
C^{\ast}(X,Y;M)
simply changes the resulting cohomology groups by a transposition.

Computations of twisted (co)homology modules are usually performed with the aid of the Universal Coefficient Spectral Sequence (UCSS) whose statement we now recall, referring the reader to
[
27
, Theorem 2.3]
and
[
31
, Theorem 2.20]
for a proof.

Theorem B.3

(Universal Coefficient Spectral Sequence)
.

Let
R
and
S
be associative rings with unit.
Let
C_{\ast}
be a chain complex of finitely generated free left
R
-modules.
If
M
is any
(S,R)
-bimodule, then there are
natural spectral sequences of left
S
-modules

\displaystyle E^{2}_{p,q}

\displaystyle=\Tor_{p}^{R}(M,H_{q}(C_{\ast}))\Rightarrow H_{p+q}(M\otimes_{R}C_{*}),

\displaystyle E_{2}^{p,q}

\displaystyle=\Ext^{q}_{R}(H_{p}(C_{\ast})^{\tr},M)\Rightarrow H^{p+q}(\hom_{\mathrm{right}-R}(C_{*}^{\tr},M))

with differentials of degree
(-r,r-1)
and
(1-r,r)
, respectively.

Twisted intersection forms

We now turn to the definition of the twisted intersection form,
which requires two ingredients: twisted Poincaré-Lefschetz duality,
and the evaluation map.

Let
X
be a connected compact oriented smooth
4
-manifold with
\pi_{1}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
.
For any
\Lambda_{\mu}
-module
M
, one can define the
twisted Poincaré-Lefschetz duality

\mathit{PD}_{M}\colon H_{k}(X,\partial X;M)\xrightarrow{\cong}H^{4-k}(X;M)

as the inverse of the isomorphism

(-)\cap[X,\partial X]\colon H^{4-k}(X;M)\xrightarrow{\cong}H_{k}(X,\partial X;M)

induced by the cap product with the fundamental class
[X,\partial X]\in H_{4}(X;\mathbb{C})
, see
[
9
, Section 2.4]
.

We now come to the definition of the evaluation map.
Let
A
be a commutative
\mathbb{C}
-algebra with unit, and involution denoted by
a\mapsto\overline{a}
.
Let
\psi\colon\Lambda_{\mu}\to A
be a homomorphism of algebras with involutions which preserves units.
Observe that
A
becomes an
(A,\Lambda_{\mu})
-bimodule via
\psi
.
Let
M
be an
(A,\Lambda_{\mu})
-bimodule an let
N
be an
(A,A)
-bimodule with involution.
Suppose that we are given a
\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
-equivariant, sesquilinear, nonsingular pairing

\theta\colon M\times M\to N\,.

In other words, this pairing
\theta
satisfies the following conditions:

1.

for any
g\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
and any
m_{1},m_{2}\in M
, we have
\theta(m_{1}\cdot g,m_{2}\cdot g)=\theta(m_{1},m_{2})
;

2.

\theta
is
A
-linear in the first variable
and satisfies
\theta(m_{1},m_{2})=\overline{\theta(m_{2},m_{1})}
for all
m_{1},m_{2}\in M
;

3.

the adjoint map

\theta^{D}\colon M\to\hom_{\text{left-}A}(M,N)^{\tr}

defined by
\theta^{D}(m_{1})(m_{2})=\theta(m_{1},m_{2})
is an isomorphism of left
A
-modules.

Given this piece of data, we can construct an associated evaluation map
as follows. Firstly, consider the chain map

\displaystyle\kappa\colon\hom_{\Lambda_{\mu}}(C_{\ast}(\widetilde{X})^{\tr},M)

\displaystyle\to\hom_{A}(M\otimes_{\Lambda_{\mu}}C_{\ast}(\widetilde{X}),N)^{\tr}

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\mapsto(m\otimes\sigma\mapsto\theta(m,f(\sigma)))\,,

where
m\in M
and
\sigma\in C_{\ast}(\widetilde{X})
.
By nonsingularity of
\theta
, this is an isomorphism of cochain complexes of
A
-modules.
Secondly, the edge homomorphism in the UCSS yields a map

E\colon H^{k}(\hom_{A}(M\otimes_{\Lambda_{\mu}}C_{\ast}(\widetilde{X}),N)^{\tr})\to\hom_{A}(H_{k}(X;M),N)^{\tr}.

The
evaluation map
is defined as the composition

\ev(\theta)\colon H^{k}(X;M)\xrightarrow{\kappa_{*}}H^{k}(\hom_{A}(M\otimes_{\Lambda_{\mu}}C_{\ast}(\widetilde{X}),N)^{\tr})\xrightarrow{E}\hom_{A}(H_{k}(X;M),N)^{\tr}\,,

where
\kappa_{*}
is the isomorphism of
A
-modules induced by the chain map
\kappa
.

We are finally ready to define the twisted intersection form.
By composing the evaluation map with Poincaré-Lefschetz duality and the map induced by the inclusion of
(X,\emptyset)
in
(X,\partial X)
,
we obtain a homomorphism of
A
-modules

Q(\theta)^{D}\colon H_{2}(X;M)\xrightarrow{}H_{2}(X,\partial X;M)\xrightarrow{\mathit{PD}_{M}}H^{2}(X;M)\xrightarrow{\ev(\theta)}\hom_{A}(H_{2}(X;M),N)^{\tr}\,.

The associated Hermitian form

Q(\theta)\colon H_{2}(X;M)\times H_{2}(X;M)\to N,\quad Q(\theta)(x,y)=Q(\theta)^{D}(x)(y)

is the
twisted intersection form
of
X
.

In our setting, the most relevant examples of
\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
-equivariant sesquilinear pairings are the ones given below.

Example B.4
.

1.

For any
j=1,2,\ldots,\mu
, set
\Lambda_{\mu,j}=\Lambda_{\mu}\left[(t_{j}-1)^{-1}\right]
,
i.e.,
\Lambda_{\mu,j}
is constructed by adjoining the inverse of
t_{j}-1
to
\Lambda_{\mu}
.
Note that the involution on
\Lambda_{\mu}
extends naturally to an
involution on
\Lambda_{\mu,j}

Set

\mathbb{Z}^{\mu}
-equivariant,
and sesquilinear over
\Lambda_{\mu,j}

We denote the associated twisted intersection form by

For any

By Corollary
B.9
, it is isomorphic to
H_{2}(W;\Lambda_{\mu,j})
, which concludes the proof.
∎

B.3.
Proof of Lemma
B.1

Let us first assume
\mu>1
and fix
j=1,2,\ldots,\mu
.
By Lemma
B.7
, we know that
H_{2}(W;\Lambda_{\mu,j})
is a free
\Lambda_{\mu,j}
-module.
Hence, the twisted intersection form

Q_{j}(W)\colon H_{2}(W;\Lambda_{\mu,j})\times H_{2}(W;\Lambda_{\mu,j})\to\Lambda_{\mu,j}

can be represented by some matrix
H_{j}
.
For any
\omega\in U_{j}:=\{\omega\in\mathbb{T}^{\mu}\colon\omega_{j}\neq 1\}
, observe that the action of
\Lambda_{\mu}
on
\mathbb{C}^{\omega}
extends to an action of
\Lambda_{\mu,j}
. In other words, we have a natural homomorphism
\phi_{\omega}\colon\Lambda_{\mu,j}\to\mathbb{C}^{\omega}

given by
t_{i}\mapsto\omega_{i}
.
Consider the first part of Theorem
B.3

applied to
M=\mathbb{C}^{\omega}
seen as a module over
R=\Lambda_{\mu,j}
via
\phi_{\omega}
, and to
C_{*}=C_{*}(\widetilde{W};\Lambda_{\mu,j})
: it yields the spectral sequence

E^{2}_{p,q}=\Tor_{p}^{\Lambda_{\mu,j}}(\mathbb{C}^{\omega},H_{q}(W;\Lambda_{\mu,j}))\Rightarrow H_{p+q}(W;\mathbb{C}^{\omega}).

By Lemma
B.8
, we have
H_{i}(W;\Lambda_{\mu,j})=0
unless
i=2
and by Lemma
B.7
,
H_{2}(W;\Lambda_{\mu,j})
is free.
Consequently,
E_{2}^{p,q}=0
unless
p=0
and
q=2
.
Therefore,
H_{i}(W;\mathbb{C}^{\omega})=0
unless
i=2
and

H_{2}(W;\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\cong\mathbb{C}^{\omega}\otimes_{\Lambda_{\mu,j}}H_{2}(W;\Lambda_{\mu,j})\,.

In particular, we have

\rank_{\mathbb{C}}H_{2}(W;\mathbb{C}^{\omega})=\rank_{\Lambda_{\mu,j}}H_{2}(W;\Lambda_{\mu,j})\,.

Lemma
B.5
now implies that
for any
\omega\in U_{j}
, the intersection form
Q_{\omega}(W)
can be
represented by the matrix
H_{j}(\omega)

obtained by evaluating
H_{j}
at
(t_{1},\dots,t_{\mu})=\omega
.

We now turn to the case
\mu=1
. First note that our assumptions together with the exact sequence of the pair
(W,\partial W)
imply
that
H_{1}(W,\partial W;\Lambda_{1})
vanishes.
Since
\Lambda_{1}
is a PID, the Universal Coefficient Theorem then yields

H^{2}(W,\partial W;\Lambda_{1})\cong\hom_{\Lambda_{1}}(H_{2}(W,\partial W;\Lambda_{1}),\Lambda_{1})\,.

This shows that
H^{2}(W,\partial W;\Lambda_{1})
is torsion free, hence free. By Poincaré-Lefschetz duality, the same holds for
H_{2}(W;\Lambda_{1})
.
Since
H_{1}(W;\Lambda_{1})
vanishes by assumption, one more application of the
Universal Coefficient Theorem yields

H_{2}(W;\mathbb{C}^{\omega})\cong\mathbb{C}^{\omega}\otimes_{\Lambda_{1}}H_{2}(W;\Lambda_{1})\,.

The statement now follows from the naturality of the twisted intersection form as stated in Remark
B.6
. ∎

Green’s function analysis of the Neutron Lloyd interferometer
Christian Käding, Mario Pitschmann, Hartmut Abele

Abstract
The neutron optical Lloyd interferometer can serve as a potent experiment for probing fundamental physics beyond the standard models of particles and cosmology. In this article, we provide a full Green’s function analysis of a Lloyd interferometer in the limit that the reflecting mirror extends to the screen. We consider two distinct situations: first, we will review the theoretical case of no external fields being present. Subsequently, we will analyze the case in which a gravitational field is acting on the neutrons. The latter case provides the theory necessary for using a Lloyd interferometer as a probe of gravitational fields.

II
Green’s functions

Green’s functions are powerful tools for solving inhomogeneous linear differential equations. Before demonstrating how they can be applied in the context of Lloyd interferometry, we will now shortly review how to solve the general Schrödinger equation for a particle of mass
m
using them.

We start by making a stationary wave approximation for the wave function

\displaystyle\Psi(\mathbf{r},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi(\mathbf{r})e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}\,\,\,

(1)

which we then substitute into the Schrödinger equation:

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\Delta+V(\mathbf{r})\right)\Psi(\mathbf{r},t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Psi(\mathbf{r},t)\,\,\,.

(2)

This leads us to the Helmholtz equation

\displaystyle\left(\Delta+k^{2}\right)\psi(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,\,\,,

(3)

where
k^{2}=\frac{2m}{\hbar^{2}}(\hbar\omega-V(\mathbf{r}))
. The inhomogeneous Helmholtz equation may be solved via a Green’s function
G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})
fulfilling

\displaystyle\left(\Delta+k^{2}\right)G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\,\,\,,

(4)

which gives

\displaystyle G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}k|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r^{\prime}}|}}{|\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r^{\prime}}|}\,\,\,.

(5)

Using Eqs. (
3
) and (
4
), we can easily show that for
\mathbf{r}\in V
the following holds:

\displaystyle\int_{V}d^{3}r^{\prime}\left\{\psi(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})(\Delta^{\prime}+k^{2})G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})-G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})(\Delta^{\prime}+k^{2})\psi(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi\psi(\mathbf{r})\,\,\,.

(6)

In addition, employing Green’s theorem, we know

\displaystyle\int_{V}d^{3}r^{\prime}\left\{\psi(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})(\Delta^{\prime}+k^{2})G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\right.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,

\displaystyle\left.-G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})(\Delta^{\prime}+k^{2})\psi(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\oint_{\partial V}d\mathbf{S^{\prime}}\left\{\psi(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\nabla^{\prime}G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})-G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\nabla^{\prime}\psi(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\right\}\,\,\,,

(7)

where
d\mathbf{S^{\prime}}
is pointing outwards. Combining Eqs. (
6
) and (
II
) finally solves the Helmholtz equation (
3
) in terms of the Green’s function from Eq. (
5
):

\displaystyle\psi(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_{\partial V}d\mathbf{S^{\prime}}\left\{\psi(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\nabla^{\prime}G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})-G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\nabla^{\prime}\psi(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\right\}\,\,\,.

(8)

No external fields

Finally, we will focus on applications to Lloyd’s interferometer. At first, we will look at the idealistic case of no external field being present and compute the corresponding Green’s function, which we then use to solve for the neutron wave function. We will focus on the setup of a Lloyd interferometer as presented in Fig.

II.1.1
Exact solution

By the method of mirror charges, which is well-known from electrostatics
SchwingerEDyn
, we obtain a Green’s function

\displaystyle G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{a,b=\pm}ab\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}k\sqrt{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}+(y-ay^{\prime})^{2}+(z-bz^{\prime})^{2}}}}{\sqrt{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}+(y-ay^{\prime})^{2}+(z-bz^{\prime})^{2}}}\,\,\,,

(9)

which vanishes everywhere along the dotted integration path given in Fig.
2
. We introduced
\mathbf{r}=(x,y,z)^{T}
and
\mathbf{r}^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})^{T}
. In addition, the wave function
\psi(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})
also vanishes everywhere on this path except for the part coinciding with the entrance slit. There we have
\psi(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})=Ce^{\mathrm{i}kz}
with
C
being some normalization constant. Using these properties together with the general solution in Eq. (
8
), we find

\displaystyle\psi(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\mathrm{Sl}}d\mathbf{S^{\prime}}\psi(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\nabla^{\prime}G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx^{\prime}\int^{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}+\delta/2}_{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}-\delta/2}dy^{\prime}\psi(\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{\prime}}G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})\,\,\,,

where
\mathrm{Sl}
denotes the entrance slit area, and
y_{\mathrm{Sl}}
and
\delta
are the slit’s center and length on the
y
-coordinate. Substituting Eq. (
9
) into Eq. (
10
), we find

\displaystyle\psi(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C}{2\pi}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\int^{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}+\delta/2}_{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}-\delta/2}dy^{\prime}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx^{\prime}\left[\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}k\sqrt{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}+(y+y^{\prime})^{2}+z^{2}}}}{\sqrt{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}+(y+y^{\prime})^{2}+z^{2}}}-\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}k\sqrt{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}+(y-y^{\prime})^{2}+z^{2}}}}{\sqrt{(x-x^{\prime})^{2}+(y-y^{\prime})^{2}+z^{2}}}\right]

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{C}{2\pi}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\int^{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}+\delta/2}_{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}-\delta/2}dy^{\prime}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\left[\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}k\sqrt{x^{2}+(y+y^{\prime})^{2}+z^{2}}}}{\sqrt{x^{2}+(y+y^{\prime})^{2}+z^{2}}}-\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}k\sqrt{x^{2}+(y-y^{\prime})^{2}+z^{2}}}}{\sqrt{x^{2}+(y-y^{\prime})^{2}+z^{2}}}\right]\,\,\,.

Next, we evaluate the
x
-integral via the following integral representation of the Hankel function of the first kind
SchwingerEDyn
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\pi H_{0}^{(1)}(k\rho)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{-\infty}dz\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}k\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}}}{\sqrt{\rho^{2}+z^{2}}}\,\,\,.

(12)

In this way, we obtain

\displaystyle\psi(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{i}C}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\int^{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}+\delta/2}_{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}-\delta/2}dy^{\prime}\left[H_{0}^{(1)}(k\sqrt{(y+y^{\prime})^{2}+z^{2}})-H_{0}^{(1)}(k\sqrt{(y-y^{\prime})^{2}+z^{2}})\right]\,\,\,.

(13)

Next, using
Watson

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dz}H_{n}^{(1)}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{nH_{n}^{(1)}(z)}{z}-H_{n+1}^{(1)}(z)\,\,\,,

(14)

we find

\displaystyle\psi(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\frac{\mathrm{i}kz}{2}\int^{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}+\delta/2}_{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}-\delta/2}dy^{\prime}\left[\frac{H_{1}^{(1)}(k\sqrt{(y-y^{\prime})^{2}+z^{2}})}{\sqrt{(y-y^{\prime})^{2}+z^{2}}}-\frac{H_{1}^{(1)}(k\sqrt{(y+y^{\prime})^{2}+z^{2}})}{\sqrt{(y+y^{\prime})^{2}+z^{2}}}\right]\,\,\,,

(15)

and ultimately

\displaystyle\psi(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\frac{\mathrm{i}kz}{2}\left[\int^{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}+\delta/2-y}_{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}-\delta/2-y}dy^{\prime}\frac{H_{1}^{(1)}(k\sqrt{y^{\prime 2}+z^{2}})}{\sqrt{y^{\prime 2}+z^{2}}}-\int^{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}+\delta/2+y}_{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}-\delta/2+y}dy^{\prime}\frac{H_{1}^{(1)}(k\sqrt{y^{\prime 2}+z^{2}})}{\sqrt{y^{\prime 2}+z^{2}}}\right]\,\,\,.

(16)

II.1.2
Asymptotic expression

We will now find an approximation for Eq. (
16
) by approximating its integrals in the asymptotic case that
z
is very large and
\delta\to 0
. For this, we first use the asymptotic expression
SchwingerEDyn

\displaystyle H_{1}^{(1)}(z)

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi z}}e^{\mathrm{i}(z-3\pi/4)}\,\,\,,

(17)

which allows us to write Eq. (
16
) in the asymptotic case of
z
being large as

\displaystyle\psi(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle Cz\sqrt{\frac{k}{2\pi}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\pi/4}\left[\int^{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}+\delta/2-y}_{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}-\delta/2-y}dy^{\prime}\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}k\sqrt{y^{\prime 2}+z^{2}}}}{(y^{\prime 2}+z^{2})^{3/4}}-\int^{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}+\delta/2+y}_{y_{\mathrm{Sl}}-\delta/2+y}dy^{\prime}\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}k\sqrt{y^{\prime 2}+z^{2}}}}{(y^{\prime 2}+z^{2})^{3/4}}\right]\,\,\,.

(18)

Next, we use that for a convergent integrand and a small integration interval the following approximation holds:

\displaystyle\int^{A+\delta/2}_{A-\delta/2}dyf(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int^{+\delta/2}_{-\delta/2}dyf(y+A)\,=\,\frac{\delta}{2}\int^{+1}_{-1}dyf\left(\frac{\delta}{2}y+A\right)\,\to\,\delta f(A)\,\,\,.

(19)

Applying this to Eq. (
18
), we find that the wave function for large
z
and
\delta\to 0
can be approximated as

\displaystyle\psi(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\delta Cz\sqrt{\frac{k}{2\pi}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\pi/4}\left\{\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}k\sqrt{(y_{\mathrm{Sl}}-y)^{2}+z^{2}}}}{[(y_{\mathrm{Sl}}-y)^{2}+z^{2}]^{3/4}}-\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}k\sqrt{(y_{\mathrm{Sl}}+y)^{2}+z^{2}}}}{[(y_{\mathrm{Sl}}+y)^{2}+z^{2}]^{3/4}}\right\}\,\,\,.

(20)

II.2
Gravitational field

Now we will consider a physically realistic situation by introducing an external gravitational field. At first, we will derive a general expression for the Green’s function for this particular case, following the treatment in Ref.
SchwingerEDyn
. Later, we will apply the result explicitly to Lloyd interferometry.

II.2.1
General solution

We consider a gravitational field in
x
-direction and want to find the Green’s function for the Helmholtz equation

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}\psi(\mathbf{r})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,\,\,,

(21)

where the field operator is given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\perp}+\partial_{x}^{2}+\frac{2m}{\hbar^{2}}(E-mgx)\,\,\,,

(22)

and we use the ansatz

\displaystyle G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\int\frac{d^{2}k_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}_{\perp}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{\perp}-\mathbf{x^{\prime}}_{\perp})}\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})

(23)

for the Green’s function.
Consequently, acting with the field operator from Eq. (
22
) on the Green’s function gives us the following condition:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{L}G(\mathbf{r},\mathbf{r^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\int\frac{d^{2}k_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}_{\perp}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{\perp}-\mathbf{x^{\prime}}_{\perp})}\left[\partial_{x}^{2}-\mathbf{k}_{\perp}^{2}+\frac{2m}{\hbar^{2}}(E-mgx)\right]\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})

(24)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\pi\int\frac{d^{2}k_{\perp}}{(2\pi)^{2}}e^{\mathrm{i}\mathbf{k}_{\perp}\cdot(\mathbf{x}_{\perp}-\mathbf{x^{\prime}}_{\perp})}\left[\partial_{x}^{2}+\frac{2m}{\hbar^{2}}(\tilde{E}-mgx)\right]\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-4\pi\delta^{(3)}(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r^{\prime}})

with
\tilde{E}:=E-\hbar^{2}\mathbf{k}_{\perp}^{2}/2m
. Subsequently, we extract

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{x}^{2}+\frac{2m}{\hbar^{2}}(\tilde{E}-mgx)\right]\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\delta(x-x^{\prime})\,\,\,.

(25)

Assuming that
\mathrm{g}
has support only on
\{x\!:x\in[x^{\prime}-0,x^{\prime}+0]\}
, integrating Eq. (
25
) over
x
gives

\displaystyle\left.\partial_{x}\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\right|^{x^{\prime}+0}_{x^{\prime}-0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-1\,\,\,.

(26)

Next, we return to Eq. (
25
) and multiply it with
x
from the left, which leads us to

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\left[x\partial_{x}\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})-\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\right]+\frac{2mx}{\hbar^{2}}(\tilde{E}-mgx)\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-x^{\prime}\delta(x-x^{\prime})\,\,\,.

(27)

Integrating this over
x
, we find

\displaystyle\left.x\partial_{x}\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\right|^{x^{\prime}+0}_{x^{\prime}-0}-\left.\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\right|^{x^{\prime}+0}_{x^{\prime}-0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-x^{\prime}\,\,\,.

(28)

Combining Eqs. (
26
) and (
28
), gives

\displaystyle\left.\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\right|^{x^{\prime}+0}_{x^{\prime}-0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,\,\,.

(29)

For later convenience, we are now going to prove the reciprocity relation

\displaystyle\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{g}(x^{\prime},x;\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\,\,\,.

(30)

For this, we begin by multiplying Eq. (
25
) by
\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})
, and then subtracting a copy of the resulting equation but with
x^{\prime}\leftrightarrow x^{\prime\prime}
, which results in

\displaystyle-\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\delta(x-x^{\prime})+\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\delta(x-x^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\partial_{x}^{2}\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})

(31)

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,-\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\partial_{x}^{2}\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{x}\left[\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\partial_{x}\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\right.

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\left.-\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\partial_{x}\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\right]\,\,\,.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,

Integrating this over all of
x
yields

\displaystyle-\mathrm{g}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})+\mathrm{g}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\partial_{x}\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})-\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\partial_{x}\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\right]|^{+\infty}_{-\infty}\,\,\,.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,

(32)

Since
\mathrm{g}
has support only on
\{x\!:x\in[x^{\prime}-0,x^{\prime}+0]\}
, we know that the right-hand side of Eq. (
32
) must vanish, leaving us with the reciprocity relation

\displaystyle\mathrm{g}(x^{\prime},x^{\prime\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{g}(x^{\prime\prime},x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})\,\,\,,

(33)

which concludes the proof.

Now we continue with the computation of the function
\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})
. For this, we use that for

) takes on the form

\displaystyle\left[\partial_{x}^{2}+\frac{2m}{\hbar^{2}}(\tilde{E}-mgx)\right]\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,\,\,.

(34)

In this way, we can find a solution for the Green’s function via the homogeneous solution. Following Ref.

, the convergent solution of Eq. (

) for

is

\displaystyle\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{1}(x^{\prime})\mathrm{Ai}(\sigma)

(35)

with a dimensionless variable

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sqrt[3]{\frac{2m^{2}g}{\hbar^{2}}}\left(x-\frac{\tilde{E}}{mg}\right)\,\,\,,

(36)

while for

it is

\displaystyle\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{2}(x^{\prime})\mathrm{Ai}(\sigma)+C_{3}(x^{\prime})\mathrm{Bi}(\sigma)\,\,\,.

are Airy functions of the first and second kind, respectively.

\displaystyle C_{1}(x^{\prime})\mathrm{Ai}^{\prime}(\sigma^{\prime})-C_{2}(x^{\prime})\mathrm{Ai}^{\prime}(\sigma^{\prime})-C_{3}(x^{\prime})\mathrm{Bi}^{\prime}(\sigma^{\prime})\,=\,-\sqrt[3]{\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m^{2}g}}\,\,\,,

\displaystyle C_{1}(x^{\prime})\mathrm{Ai}(\sigma^{\prime})\,-\,C_{2}(x^{\prime})\mathrm{Ai}(\sigma^{\prime})\,-\,C_{3}(x^{\prime})\mathrm{Bi}(\sigma^{\prime})\,\,=\,0\,\,\,,

where

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sqrt[3]{\frac{2m^{2}g}{\hbar^{2}}}\left(x^{\prime}-\frac{\tilde{E}}{mg}\right)\,\,\,.

Combining Eqs. (

\displaystyle C_{1}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{2}(x^{\prime})+\sqrt[3]{\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m^{2}g}}\frac{\mathrm{Bi}(\sigma^{\prime})}{W(\mathrm{Ai},\mathrm{Bi})(\sigma^{\prime})}\,\,\,,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,C_{3}(x^{\prime})\,=\,\sqrt[3]{\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m^{2}g}}\frac{\mathrm{Ai}(\sigma^{\prime})}{W(\mathrm{Ai},\mathrm{Bi})(\sigma^{\prime})}\,\,\,,

(41)

where
W(\mathrm{Ai},\mathrm{Bi})(\sigma)
is the Wronskian of
\mathrm{Ai}
and
\mathrm{Bi}
. Using the fact that every Airy function
f(\sigma)
must fulfill
f^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)=\sigma f(\sigma)
, it is straightforward to show that this Wronskian must be constant:

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\sigma}W(\mathrm{Ai},\mathrm{Bi})(\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ai}(\sigma)\mathrm{Bi}^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)-\mathrm{Bi}(\sigma)\mathrm{Ai}^{\prime\prime}(\sigma)

(42)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma\mathrm{Ai}(\sigma)\mathrm{Bi}(\sigma)-\sigma\mathrm{Bi}(\sigma)\mathrm{Ai}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,\,\,,

which implies
W(\mathrm{Ai},\mathrm{Bi})(\sigma)=W(\mathrm{Ai},\mathrm{Bi})(0)
. Taking the values for the Airy functions and their derivatives at
\sigma=0
, we can show that

\displaystyle W(\mathrm{Ai},\mathrm{Bi})(\sigma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi}

(43)

holds.
So, substituting this into Eqs. (
35
) and (
37
) leaves us with

\displaystyle\mathrm{g}(x,x^{\prime};\mathbf{k}_{\perp})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2C_{2}(x^{\prime})\mathrm{Ai}(\sigma)+\sqrt[3]{\frac{\hbar^{2}\pi^{3}}{2m^{2}g}}\left[\Theta(x-x^{\prime})\mathrm{Bi}(\sigma^{\prime})\mathrm{Ai}(\sigma)+\Theta(x^{\prime}-x)\mathrm{Ai}(\sigma^{\prime})\mathrm{Bi}(\sigma)\right]\,\,\,.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,

(44)

Furthermore, from the reciprocity relation (

) we conclude

\displaystyle C_{2}(x^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda}{2}\mathrm{Ai}(\sigma^{\prime})\,\,\,,

(45)

where

III
Conclusions

Neutron Lloyd interferometry is a promising experimental setup that can be used to probe effects within and beyond the realms of known physics. However, previous theoretical analyses made use of approximative methods, which are expected to not always be sufficiently accurate. For this reason, in this article, we presented an exact full Green’s functions analysis of Lloyd interferometry. First, we discussed the hypothetical case of no external fields acting on the neutrons while travelling within the experimental setup. For this, we found the corresponding Green’s function for solving the Schrödinger equation and subsequently determined the resulting wave function. Second, we looked at the physically relevant case of having an external gravitational field acting on the neutrons.

The prediction made for the gravitationally modified neutron wave function serves as a theoretical basis for using a neutron Lloyd interferometer to probe gravitational fields. However, it should be stressed that the computations presented here approximate the interferometer’s slit source as being infinitely wide in
x
-direction and the mirror to be infinitely extended. For practically applying the technology developed in this article to a real experiment, a numerical analysis without these approximations would be required. Such a sophisticated numerical evaluation will be subject of future work. Furthermore, in the future, the computation shown in the present article, will serve as a blueprint for predicting neutron wave functions in Lloyd interferometers under the influence of hypothetical gravity-like fifth forces and scalar fields.

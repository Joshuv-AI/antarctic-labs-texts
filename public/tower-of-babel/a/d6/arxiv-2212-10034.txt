Persistence and asymptotic analysis of solutions of nonlinear wave equations
Igor Leite Freire 1,2 1 Institute of Advanced Studies, Loughborough University LE11 3TU Epinal Way Loughborough, United Kingdom, 2 Departamento de Matemática, Universidade Federal de São Carlos, Rodovia Washington Luís, Km 235, 13565-905, São Carlos, SP - Brasil

Abstract
We consider persistence properties of solutions for a generalised wave equation including vibration in elastic rods and shallow water models, such as the BBM, the Dai’s, the Camassa-Holm, and the Dullin-Gottwald-Holm equations, as well as some recent shallow water equations with the Coriolis effect. We establish unique continuation results and exhibit asymptotic profiles for the solutions of the general class considered. From these results we prove the non-existence of non-trivial spatially compactly supported solutions for the equation. As an aftermath, we study the equations earlier mentioned in light of our results for the general class.

In

[

Travelling waves for Dai’s equation were later investigated in
[

Notation, important concepts, and main results

The

,
1\leq p\leq\infty

\|\cdot\|_{p}
and

and

.

For the CH equation and similar models, the variables

and

denote time and space, so that we maintain the same meaning for them throughout the manuscript. The derivatives of a function

with respect to the first variable is referred to as

or

denote the derivatives with respect

. Also, whenever

is an interval in

and

is a Banach space, we write

to say that
u(t,\cdot)\in X
and
\|u\|=\sup\limits_{t\in X}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{X}
, where

denotes the norm of the function

. In addition,

, for a natural number

, means that its derivatives with respect to
t
belongs to

up to order

.

The solutions of (
1.0.6
) herein belong to
C^{0}([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
,

, for some

and write (

) as a first order non-linear (and with a non-local term) evolution equation, obtaining

u_{t}+f^{\prime}(u)u_{x}+\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}\Big(g(u)+\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(u)}{2}u_{x}^{2}\Big)=0.

(2.0.1)

Given a function

(for which the operations are defined),
\Lambda^{-2}h=p\ast h
and

\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}h=\partial_{x}p\ast h
, where

p(x)=\frac{e^{-|x|}}{2}

(2.0.2)

and

A careful look at (

{\bf H}_{1}

f,g\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

and

if and only if

We have two more theorems to complete our main results’ list, but before stating them we need a couple of concepts. To tackle our problems we use ideas introduced by Brandolese
[
2
]
, so that it is convenient to recall some important notions.

A function
f:[a,b]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is called absolutely continuous on
[a,b]
if, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{N}|f(b_{i})-f(a_{i})|<\varepsilon,

for any finite collection of non-overlapping compact subsets
[a_{i},b_{i}]\subseteq[a,b]
,
1\leq i\leq N
, with

\sum_{i=1}^{N}(b_{i}-a_{i})<\delta.

The collection of such functions is denoted by
AC[a,b]
. Moreover, a function
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is said to be locally absolutely continuous if
f\in AC[a,b]
, for all
-\infty<a<b<\infty
.

Henceforth, it will always be assumed that by a weight (or weight function)
v:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
we mean a continuous and positive function. Additional conditions will be imposed in due course.

A weight function
v
is called
sub-multiplicative
if
v(x+y)\leq v(x)v(y)
, for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
. Note that a necessary condition for a weight function
v
to be sub-multiplicative is
v(0)\geq 1
.

A function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is said to be
v-
moderate if
\phi
is positive and there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that

\phi(x+y)\leq c_{0}\,v(x)\,\phi(y).

(2.0.9)

In particular, we have
c_{0}\geq 1/v(0)
. If a function
\phi
is
v-
moderate, for some function
v
, we simply say that
\phi
is moderate.

Definition 2.1
.

An admissible weight function for the equation (

) is a locally absolutely continuous function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, such that
|\phi^{\prime}(x)|\leq A|\phi(x)|
almost everywhere (a.e.), for some

, and
v-
moderate for some continuous, sub-multiplicative weight function

satisfying
\inf_{\mathbb{R}}v>0
and
e^{-|\cdot|}v(\cdot)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

(2.0.11)

Challenges and novelties of the manuscript.
The fact that we are considering (
1.0.6
), or its non-local evolution form (
2.0.1
), which has two arbitrary functions, brings some difficulties in view of the natural arbitrariness involved. For this reason, it is not surprising that we should somewhat restrict these functions in order to be able to address the problem.

All of our results require the conditions
{\bf H}_{1}
and
{\bf H}_{2}
fulfilled, and most of them also require the additional restriction
{\bf H}_{3}
. The first two conditions are not new, in the sense that similar requirements were already used in earlier works dealing with problems related to existence of solutions, see
[
35
, Equation (1.6)]
,
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
, or
[
40
, Theorem 5.1]
. What seems to be new in our case is just
{\bf H}_{3}
, which requires that
g
is non-negative and the only solution of the equation
g(x)=0
is just
x=0
. The latter condition cannot be relaxed since some of our demonstrations are based on the following idea: find a value of
t
, say
t^{\ast}
, for which
h_{t^{\ast}}(x)=0
, where
h_{t^{\ast}}(\cdot)
is given by (
2.0.3
). Under our conditions we can guarantee that

, otherwise theorem

and its corollaries could not be proved.

The three aforesaid conditions are somewhat unsurprising, but the same cannot be said about the inequality (
2.0.5
) and the restriction

in theorems

and
2.5
, which seem to be truly new conditions when dealing with (
1.0.6
).

In the proof of theorem
4.7
(see section
4
) the condition
g(u)\leq c|u|^{2}
, for some positive constant
c
, is sine qua non. Such a theorem is an essential ingredient for the demonstration of theorem
2.5
(see subsection
4.3
), which is crucial to establish theorems
2.3
and
2.2
, see subsection
4.4
. In lieu of (
2.0.5
) we could require
|g(x)|\leq cx^{2}
, which is somewhat stronger than the original requirement because this would be a condition on the function
g
, whereas (
2.0.5
) is, in fact, a condition on the composed function

. Moreover, the sort of solutions

we are dealing with is bounded in view of the Sobolev Embedding Theorem, which makes easier to have (
2.0.5

As an example, let us consider the function

with
\beta>0
and
\gamma\geq 0
. It does not satisfy the condition

for all

because the term

is dominant for higher values of

(for

the leading term is

). On the other hand, if

, with

, is a solution of (

), then we have the estimate

g(u(t,x))\leq\Big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\beta}{3}\|u(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\frac{\gamma}{4}\|u(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\Big)u(t,x)^{2}.

Above we used the fact that

is bounded,
\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\leq\|u(0,\cdot)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}
, and the functional (

) is invariant.

These observations will be recalled in section

Proposition 3.1
.

If
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
s>3/2
, and
v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, then
f(v)v_{x}^{n}\in H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Let us first consider
n=1
and rewrite
f(v)v_{x}=(f(v)-f(0))v_{x}+f(0)v_{x}
. Clearly
f(0)v_{x}\in H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R})
, while
f(\cdot)-f(0)
satisfies the conditions in Lemma
3.1
, and thus it belongs to
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
. By the algebra property (e.g., see
[
39
, page 320, exercice 6]
) we conclude that
(f(v)-f(0))v_{x}\in H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R})
. The general case is a consequence of the algebra property and
f(v)v_{x}^{n}=(f(v)v_{x})v_{x}^{n-1}
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

If
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, then
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Proof.

Let
a:=\max\{1,\|f\|_{\infty}
}. Then
|f(x)|^{p}\leq a^{p-1}|f|
and
\|f\|_{p}\leq a\|f\|_{1}
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume that
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{0}(\mathbb{R})
and
p>1
. Then, for any
\epsilon\in(0,\|f\|_{\infty})
, there exists
L_{\epsilon}>0
and
r\in(1,p)
such that

\sqrt[p]{L_{\epsilon}}(\|f\|_{\infty}-\epsilon)\leq\|f\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{r}^{r/p}\|f\|_{\infty}^{1-r/p}.

Proof.

In view of proposition
3.2
we know that
f\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R})
,
r>1
. On the other hand, for
1\leq r<p
, we have

\|f\|_{p}^{p}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{p-r}|f(x)|^{r}dx\leq\|f\|_{\infty}^{p-r}\|f\|_{r}^{r}.

Let
\epsilon\in(0,\|f\|_{\infty})
and
X_{\epsilon}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R};\,\,|f(x)|>\|f\|_{\infty}-\epsilon\}
. Since
f
is continuous, we have
X_{\epsilon}\neq\emptyset
. Moreover, due to
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we can find
L_{\epsilon}>0
such that

0<L_{\epsilon}(\|f\|_{\infty}-\epsilon)=\int_{X_{\epsilon}}(\|f\|_{\infty}-\epsilon)dx\leq\int_{X_{\epsilon}}|f(x)|dx\leq\|f\|_{1},

therefrom we obtain

L_{\epsilon}(\|f\|_{\infty}-\epsilon)^{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}^{p}\leq\|f\|_{r}^{r/p}\|f\|_{\infty}^{1-r/p},

which implies the result.
∎

Proposition
3.3
has as a straightforward consequence the following fact: given a function
f
in the class
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\cap C^{0}(\mathbb{R})
, then
\|f\|_{p}\rightarrow\|f\|_{\infty}
, as
p\rightarrow\infty
, a fact that we shall use very often in section
4
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose that
v
is a continuous sub-multiplicative function such that
\inf_{\mathbb{R}}v>0
and
e^{-|\cdot|}v(\cdot)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then
e^{-|\cdot|}v(\cdot)\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
2\leq p\leq\infty
.

Proof.

Let us define
h(x)=e^{-|x|}v(x)
. In particular,
h
is non-negative, continuous, and a member of
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
by construction. We only need to show that
h\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, because then the result will be a foregone conclusion of proposition
3.2
.

Let
I:=\{x\in\mathbb{R};\,h(x)>1\}
. If
I=\emptyset
, then
h
is clearly bounded. By assuming
I\neq\emptyset
, we can find a cover
(a_{n},b_{n})
,
n\in\mathbb{N}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that
h(a_{n})=h(b_{n})=1
and

I\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}^{\prime}}(a_{n},b_{n}).

If the set
\mathbb{N}^{\prime}
is finite, say
\mathbb{N}^{\prime}=\{1,\cdots,n\}
, then
I
is contained in a compact set
J
and then,
\|h\|_{\infty}=\max\limits_{J}|h(x)|
.

The case in which
\mathbb{N}^{\prime}
is infinite in more challenging. We first note that we can only have a finite number of indices for which
b_{n}-a_{n}\geq 1
, otherwise the condition

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|h(x)|dx<\infty

would not be true.

Let
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{\prime}
such that
n>n_{0}
implies
b_{n}-a_{n}<1
. Moreover, we can take
n_{0}
large enough so that either
I_{n}\subseteq(0,\infty)
or
I_{n}\subseteq(-\infty,0)
. Let
J
be the closure of the reunion of the intervals with indexes up to
n_{0}
. Hence,
J
is a compact set.

Claim:
We claim that for any
n>n_{0}
,
\sup\limits_{x\in I_{n}}h(x)\leq\sup\limits_{x\in[-1,1]}h(x)
.

If our claim is true, then we have

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|h(x)|=\sup_{x\in I}|h(x)|=\sup_{x\in J\cup[-1,1]}|h(x)|,

and the same argument used for the case
\mathbb{N}^{\prime}
assures that
h
is bounded.

We now prove the claim, which is divided in two mutually exclusive cases. We recall that
h(a_{n})=h(b_{n})=1
.

•

Case
I_{n}\subseteq(-\infty,0)
.
For any
x,y\in(-\infty,0)
, note that

h(x+y)=e^{-|x+y|}v(x+y)\leq e^{x}v(x)e^{y}v(y)=h(x)h(y).

For any
x\in I_{n}
, we can decompose it as
x=b_{n}+t
, for some
t\in(-1,0)
, and then

h(x)=h(b_{n}+t)\leq h(b_{n})h(t)\leq\sup_{t\in[-1,0]}h(t).

•

Case
I_{n}\subseteq(-\infty,0)
.
Similarly as in the previous case, each
x\in I_{n}
can be written as
x=a_{n}+t
, for some
t\in(0,1)
, and we also have
h(y+z)\leq h(y)h(z)
, for all
y,z\in(0,\infty)
. Thus, we have

h(x)=h(a_{n}+t)\leq h(a_{n})h(t)\leq\sup_{t\in[0,1]}h(t).

In both cases, we conclude that
h
is bounded over the sets
I_{n}
by
\sup\limits_{x\in[-1,1]}h(x)
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Assume that
\phi
is a locally absolutely continuous function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
|\phi^{\prime}(x)|\leq A|\phi(x)|
a.e., for some
A>0
, and let us define, for each
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the function

\phi_{N}(x)=\min\{\phi(x),N\}.

(3.0.1)

Then,

1.

(\phi_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq C^{0}(\mathbb{R})
and is locally absolutely continuous;

2.

\phi_{N}(x)\leq\phi_{N+1}(x)\leq\phi(x)
, for each fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
and any
N\in\mathbb{N}
;

3.

(\phi_{N})_{N}
converges pointwise to
\phi(x)
;

4.

\|\phi_{N}\|_{\infty}\leq N
;

5.

|\phi^{\prime}_{N}(x)|\leq A|\phi_{N}(x)|
a.e.

Proof.

We begin by noticing that for any two functions
f
and
g
, we have

\min\{f,g\}=\frac{f+g}{2}-\frac{|f-g|}{2}.

1.

We observe that both
\phi(\cdot)+N
and
|\phi(\cdot)-N|
are locally absolutely continuous. The definition of minimum given above and this observation prove the result;

2.

Recall that
\phi_{N}(x)\leq\phi(x)
, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover,
\phi_{N}(x)=\phi_{N+1}(x)
, as long as
\phi(x)\leq N
, and for
x
such that
\phi(x)>N
, then
\phi_{N+1}(x)>N\geq\phi_{N}(x)
;

3.

Immediate;

4.

Note that
\phi_{N}(x)\leq N
, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
;

5.

Consider the open sets
A_{N}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R};\,\,\phi(x)<N
},
B_{N}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R};\,\,\phi(x)>N
}, and let
U_{N}=\mathbb{R}\setminus(A_{N}\cup B_{N})
. If
A_{N}\neq\emptyset
we have
\phi^{\prime}_{N}(x)=\phi^{\prime}(x)
, for any
x\in A_{N}
, whereas if
B_{N}\neq\emptyset
and
x\in B_{N}
, then
\phi_{N}^{\prime}(x)=0
. In any case, we have
|\phi_{N}^{\prime}(x)|\leq A|\phi_{N}(x)|
. Finally, if
U_{N}\neq\emptyset
, and its interior is non-empty, then for any interior point
x
we conclude that
\phi_{N}^{\prime}(x)=0
, an already treated case. In case the interior of
U_{N}
is empty, then we are forced to conclude that
U_{N}
has measure
0
. In any case we conclude that
|\phi^{\prime}_{N}(x)|\leq A|\phi_{N}(x)|
a.e.

∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\phi
and
(\phi_{N})_{N\in\mathbb{N}}
as in Proposition

. If
\phi
is
v-
moderate, for some sub-multiplicative weight function

Proof.

We only need to prove the existence of a constant

Since
\phi
is

such that (

If

Theorem 4.3

) have the form (
2.0.8
), which means that it cannot be spatially compactly supported for two different times. In particular, this holds for (
5.1.1
).

5.2
Dai’s equation

Let us now consider Dai’s equation (
1.0.3
). Taking
f(u)=\gamma u^{2}/2
and
g(u)=(3-\gamma)u^{2}/2
we obtain (
1.0.3
) from (
1.0.6
) (note that the CH equation corresponds to the case
\gamma=1
).

Again our conditions on
f
and
g
are satisfied, including (
2.0.5
), as long as
\gamma\in[0,3]
. Therefore, by theorem
2.1
we conclude that a solution
u\in C^{0}([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
,
s>3/2
, emanating from a non-trivial initial data cannot vanish on any open set of its domain, nor can be compactly supported in two different times, in view of theorem
2.3
. Moreover, solutions arising from initial data with strong decay (in the sense of (
2.0.6
)) will also inherit such property in view of theorem
2.3
.

5.3
The DGH equation

Let us now consider the DGH equation
[
24
]
equation

u_{t}-u_{txx}+3uu_{x}=2u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx}+\alpha u_{x}+\Gamma u_{xxx}.

(5.3.1)

The presence of the linear advection
\alpha u_{x}
may cause a problem at first sight, once it would violate condition (
2.0.5
). However, such a term can be eliminated under the change
u(t,x)=v(t,x+\alpha t)
. Note that Sobolev spaces are invariant under translations (in the sense that if
f(\cdot)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, then
f(\cdot+x)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
). Moreover, the map
(t,x)\mapsto(t,x+\alpha t)
is a global diffeomorphism fixing the line
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}
, meaning that it preserves initial datum, so that
u\in C^{0}([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
is a solution of (
5.3.1
) subject to
u(t,x)=u_{0}(x)
if and only if
v
is a solution of the equation

v_{t}-v_{txx}+3vv_{x}=2v_{x}v_{xx}+vv_{xxx}+\hat{\Gamma}v_{xxx}

(5.3.2)

subject to the same initial condition. Above,
\hat{\Gamma}=\Gamma-\alpha
. Equation (
5.3.2
) is a particular case of (
1.0.6
) with
g(u)=u^{2}
and
f(u)=u^{2}+\hat{\Gamma}u
.

Clearly the functions
f
and
g
above satisfy
{\bf H_{1}}-{\bf H_{3}}
, and (
2.0.5
) as well. Therefore, by theorem
2.1
we conclude that the only solution
u\in C^{0}([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
of the DGH equation vanishing on an open set
\Omega\subseteq[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
is
u\equiv 0
. However, theorems
2.2
and
2.3
can only be applied whenever
\hat{\Gamma}=0
. If this is so, then (
5.3.2
) becomes the CH equation and we re-obtain the results in
[
2
]
, see also
[
26
,
27
]
.

5.4
The rCH equation

Let us now consider equation
1.0.5
. In view of our discussions about the DGH equation, without loss of generality we may assume the form

u_{t}-u_{txx}+3uu_{x}=2u_{x}u_{xx}+uu_{xxx}+\beta u^{2}u_{x}+\gamma u^{3}u_{x}+\Gamma u_{xxx},

(5.4.1)

that can be obtained from (
1.0.5
) by taking

f(u)=u^{2}/2+\Gamma u
and

g(u)=\Big(1+\frac{\beta}{3}u+\frac{\gamma}{4}u^{2}\Big)u^{2}

(5.4.2)

Conditions
{\bf H_{1}}
and
{\bf H_{2}}
are trivially satisfied, but
{\bf H_{3}}
is more delicate, since (
5.4.2
) is not necessarily non-negative. However, in case
\beta^{2}<9\gamma
, we then have

1+\frac{\beta}{3}u+\frac{\gamma}{4}u^{2}>\frac{9\gamma-\beta^{2}}{9\gamma}>0,

where above we are assuming
\gamma>0
. Moreover, if
u\in C^{0}([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
is a solution of (
5.4.1
), then it is unique since it arises from the initial datum
u_{0}(x):=u(0,x)
in view of
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
. Therefore, in view of Sobolev Embedding Theorem, we have
\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\infty}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}
, that, jointly (
5.4.2
), enable us to conclude that

g(u)\leq\Big(1+\frac{|\beta|}{3}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\frac{\gamma}{4}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}\Big)u^{2}=:cu^{2},

and then, (
2.0.5
) is fulfilled.

Likewise the DGH equation, theorems
2.2
and
2.3
can only be applied if
\Gamma=0
. Therefore, as long as
\Gamma=0
,
\gamma>0
, and
\beta^{2}<9\gamma
, any solution
u\in C^{0}([0,T];H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
,
s>3/2
, of (
5.4.1
) vanishing on a non-empty open set
\Omega\subseteq[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}
must vanish everywhere. Also, if the constraints on the parameters hold and
u
is a solution of (
5.4.1
) with initial condition satisfying (
2.0.6
), then
u
has the asymptotic profile (
2.0.8
), which prevents the existence of compactly supported solutions for multiple values of time.

6
Discussion

Since Constantin’s paper
[
14
]
showed that any solution of the Camassa-Holm equation emanating from a compactly initial data loses such property instantly, many approaches or ways for tackling the same problem have been introduced, see
[
32
,
34
,
2
]
. All of these works have as a consequence the establishment of persistence properties for the solutions of the Camassa-Holm equation, as well as unique continuation results, see also
[
36
,
26
,
27
]
, and have been widely applied, see
[
36
,
27
,
32
,
34
,
2
,
26
,
18
,
33
,
42
,
31
]
and references therein.

The present paper was mostly concerned with equation (
1.0.6
) for basically two reasons:

•

firstly, to complement the results established in
[
35
,
3
,
40
]
looking for persistence and unique continuation results for the solutions of the equation;

•

secondly, some equations, such as (
1.0.5
), have been receiving considerable attention in recent years, but apparently nothing has been done in regard to their persistence properties. In light to these comments, by dealing with such a general model we can better understand the particular equations we studied in section
6
.

Our theorem
2.1
provides a unique continuation result for the solutions of Dai’s equation
1.0.3
, while a different proof of persistence properties of its solutions is given when compared with what was done in
[
31
]
. In fact, our main inspiration for proving persistence properties for the solutions of (
1.0.6
) is the work by Brandolese, which is based on a general framework for addressing the question using weights in
L^{p}-
spaces.

We apply our results to some relevant models, such as the BBM, Dai’s, DGH and rCH equations, which are models deduced in the study of propagation of waves in water models, with exception of the Dai’s equation, which is studied in the context of elastic rods.

For the Dai’s equation our results can only be applied under the restrictions
0\leq\gamma\leq 3
, whereas for the rCH equation the validity of our conclusions can only be assured with the restrictions
\beta^{2}<9\gamma
and
\Gamma=0
in the parameters.

The restrictions mentioned in the previous paragraph are originated from the conditions we require on the functions
f
and
g
. While we already discussed the restriction on
g
in section
2
, nothing has been said about
f^{\prime}(0)=0
. It is time to fill this gap.

The condition
f^{\prime}(0)=0
and the fact that
u
is bounded imply that
f^{\prime}
is locally Lipschitz. In case
f^{\prime}(0)\neq 0
, then (
4.4.8
) should be replaced by
{\cal R}(t,x)=te^{|x|/2}R(t,x)
, with
R(t,x)=O((1+|x|)^{-1/2}(\ln{(1+|x|)})^{-d})
. Therefore, (
4.4.9
) evaluated at
t=t_{1}
, would make (
2.0.8
) changes to

u(t_{1},x)=u_{0}(x)\pm t_{1}e^{-|x|}(\phi_{\pm}(t_{1})+\epsilon_{\pm}(t_{1},x)+e^{|x|/2}R(x)).

As such, (
4.4.10
) is no longer true and the result of theorem
2.2
is not valid.

The observations above show how important the restrictions on
f
and
g
are and, in fact, they are sharp, in the sense that they cannot be relaxed to improve or even obtain the same results (except in the situation described below). In line with fundamental ingredients for our demonstrations, the invariance of the
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})-
norm of the solutions is equally essential in the way we proceeded to establish our results, as we can infer from the demonstrations of theorems
2.1
–
2.3
.

We close this section with the following remark: If we take a careful look at (
2.0.1
), we would then note that if
u=u(t,x)
is a solution of that equation, then
v(t,x)=u(t,-x)
would be a solution of

u_{t}-f^{\prime}(u)u_{x}-\partial_{x}\Lambda^{-2}\Big(g(u)+\frac{f^{\prime\prime}(u)}{2}u_{x}^{2}\Big)=0.

(6.0.1)

Moreover, a careful analysis of our demonstrations shows that what is really crucial is that the non-local term is non-negative. Moreover, equation (
6.0.1
) can be recovered from (
2.0.1
) by replacing
f\mapsto-f
and
g\mapsto-g
. Therefore, our results would still be true if instead of
f^{\prime\prime}(x)\geq 0
and
g(x)\geq 0
in
{\cal H}_{1}
and
{\cal H}_{2}
, we assume
f^{\prime\prime}(x)\leq 0
and
g(x)\leq 0
. In this case, for consistence, condition (
2.0.5
) should be replaced by
|g(u(t,x))|\leq cu(t,x)^{2}
(which would imply the original version again).

7
Conclusion

In this paper we studied unique continuation and persistence properties of the solutions of the non-linear equation
1.0.6
, and our main results are given in section
2
. We then apply our results to some relevant equations describing wave propagation in physical media, mostly of them in shallow water models.

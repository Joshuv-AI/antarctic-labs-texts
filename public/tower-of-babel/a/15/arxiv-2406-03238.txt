The parity of Lusztig’s restriction functor and Green’s formula for a quiver with automorphism
Jiepeng Fang, Yixin Lan, Yumeng Wu

Abstract
. In [ 8 ] , Fang-Lan-Xiao proved a formula about Lusztig’s induction and restriction functors which can induce Green’s formula for the path algebra of a quiver over a finite field via the trace map. In this paper, we generalize their formula to that for the mixed semisimple perverse sheaves for a quiver with an automorphism. By applying the trace map, we obtain Green’s formula for any finite-dimensional hereditary algebra over a finite field.

1.
Introduction

In
[
14
,
15
]
, Ringel introduced the Hall algebra for any finite-dimensional hereditary algebra
\Lambda
over a finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. More precisely, the Hall algebra
{\mathcal{H}}(\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\Lambda)
is a
{\mathbb{C}}
-algebra with a basis
\{u_{[M]}|[M]\in\textrm{Iso}(\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\Lambda)\}
parametrized by the isomorphism classes of finitely generated right
\Lambda
-modules over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and the multiplication

u_{[M]}*u_{[N]}=\sum_{[L]}\sqrt{q}^{\langle M,N\rangle}g^{L}_{MN}u_{[L]},

where
\langle M,N\rangle
is the Euler form and
g^{L}_{MN}
is the number of submodules
L^{\prime}
of
L
satisfying
L/L^{\prime}\cong M,L^{\prime}\cong N
. In particular, if
\Lambda
is of finite type, he proved that the Hall algebra is isomorphic to the specialization of the nilpotent part
{\rm U}_{v}^{-}
at
v=\sqrt{q}
of the quantum group defined by the Cartan datum associated to
\Lambda
.

In
[
9
]
, Green equipped the Hall algebra
{\mathcal{H}}(\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\Lambda)
with a comultiplication

\Delta(u_{[L]})=\sum_{[M],[N]}\sqrt{q}^{\langle M,N\rangle}g^{L}_{MN}a_{M}a_{N}a_{L}^{-1}u_{[M]}\otimes u_{[N]},

where
a_{M}=|\mbox{\rm Aut}_{\Lambda}(M)|
is the order of the automorphism group, and
a_{N},a_{L}
are similar. He proved the following non-trivial homological formula

(1)

\displaystyle a_{M}a_{N}a_{M^{\prime}}a_{N^{\prime}}\sum_{[L]}g^{L}_{MN}g^{L}_{M^{\prime}N^{\prime}}a_{L}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{[M_{1}],[M_{2}],[N_{1}],[N_{2}]}q^{-\langle\hat{M_{1}},\hat{N_{2}}\rangle}g^{M}_{M_{1}M_{2}}g^{N}_{N_{1}N_{2}}g^{M^{\prime}}_{M_{1}N_{1}}g^{N^{\prime}}_{M_{2}N_{2}}a_{M_{1}}a_{M_{2}}a_{N_{1}}a_{N_{2}},

for any
M,N,M^{\prime},N^{\prime}\in\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\Lambda
, equivalently,

(2)

\displaystyle\Delta(u_{[M]}*u_{[N]})=\Delta(u_{[M]})*\Delta(u_{[N]})

for any
u_{[M]},u_{[N]}\in{\mathcal{H}}(\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\Lambda)
, such that
\Delta
is an algebra homomorphism with respect to a twisted multiplication on
{\mathcal{H}}(\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\Lambda)\otimes{\mathcal{H}}(\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\Lambda)
, see Theorem
3.3
for details. Moreover, he proved that the composition subalgebra
{\mathcal{C}}(\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\Lambda)
generated by elements corresponding to simple modules is isomorphic to the specialization of
{\rm U}_{v}^{-}
at
v=\sqrt{q}
as bialgebras.

In
[
11
,
12
]
, Lusztig established the framework of the geometric realization for Hall algebra and categorified
{\rm U}_{v}^{-}
by perverse sheaves. For any finite quiver without loops
Q
, he considered the affine variety
\mathbf{E}_{\nu}
together with the connected algebraic group
\mathbf{G}_{\nu}
-action which parametrizes the isomorphism classes of representations of
Q
having dimension vector
\nu
over the algebraic closure
k=\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
, and defined a subcategory
{\mathcal{Q}}_{\nu}
of
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}_{\mathbf{G}_{\nu}}(\mathbf{E}_{\nu})
the
\mathbf{G}_{\nu}
-equivariant bounded derived category of
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{l}
-constructible sheaves on
\mathbf{E}_{\nu}
consisting of the direct sums of simple perverse sheaves which appear as direct summands in the direct images of constant sheaves on the flag varieties up to shifts and Tate twists. For any
\nu,\nu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime\prime}
satisfying
\nu=\nu^{\prime}+\nu^{\prime\prime}
, he defined the induction and the restriction functors

\displaystyle\mathbf{Ind}^{\nu}_{\nu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime\prime}}:{\mathcal{D}}^{b}_{\mathbf{G}_{\nu^{\prime}}}(\mathbf{E}_{\nu^{\prime}})\boxtimes{\mathcal{D}}^{b}_{\mathbf{G}_{\nu^{\prime\prime}}}(\mathbf{E}_{\nu^{\prime\prime}})\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}^{b}_{\mathbf{G}_{\nu}}(\mathbf{E}_{\nu}),

\displaystyle\mathbf{Res}^{\nu}_{\nu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime\prime}}:{\mathcal{D}}^{b}_{\mathbf{G}_{\nu}}(\mathbf{E}_{\nu})\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}^{b}_{\mathbf{G}_{\nu^{\prime}}\times\mathbf{G}_{\nu^{\prime\prime}}}(\mathbf{E}_{\nu^{\prime}}\times\mathbf{E}_{\nu^{\prime\prime}})

and proved that
\mathbf{Ind}^{\nu}_{\nu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime\prime}}({\mathcal{Q}}_{\nu^{\prime}}\boxtimes{\mathcal{Q}}_{\nu^{\prime\prime}})\subset{\mathcal{Q}}_{\nu},\mathbf{Res}^{\nu}_{\nu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime\prime}}({\mathcal{Q}}_{\nu})\subset{\mathcal{Q}}_{\nu^{\prime}}\boxtimes{\mathcal{Q}}_{\nu^{\prime\prime}}
such that all induction functors induce a multiplication and all restriction functors induce a comultiplication on
{\mathcal{K}}=\bigoplus_{\nu}{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{Q}}_{\nu})
the direct sum of Grothendieck groups which has a
{\mathbb{Z}}[v,v^{-1}]
-module structure given by
v^{-1}.[A]=[A[1](\frac{1}{2})]
. Moreover, he proved the following formula

(3)

\displaystyle\mathbf{Res}_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}^{\gamma}\mathbf{Ind}_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}(A\boxtimes B)\cong\bigoplus_{\lambda=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathcal{N}}(\mathbf{Ind}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\boxtimes\mathbf{Ind}_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}^{\beta^{\prime}}){\tau_{\lambda}}_{!}

\displaystyle(\mathbf{Res}_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}^{\alpha}(A)\boxtimes\mathbf{Res}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}^{\beta}(B))[-(\alpha_{2},\beta_{1})](-\frac{(\alpha_{2},\beta_{1})}{2})

for any
A\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha},B\in{\mathcal{Q}}_{\beta}
, see
[
11
, Proposition 8.4]
or
[
8
, Theorem 3.1]
for details, such that
{\mathcal{K}}
is a bialgebra, and proved that
{\mathbb{C}}(v)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[v,v^{-1}]}{\mathcal{K}}\cong{\rm U}_{v}^{-}
as bialgebras.

The connection between the Hall algebra and Lusztig’s geometric construction is given by the sheaf-function correspondence, see
[
13
,
17
,
8

. The trace map induces a bialgebra homomorphism from
{\mathbb{C}}(v)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}[v,v^{-1}]}{\mathcal{K}}
onto the composition subalgebra

3

2

u_{[M]},u_{[N]}\in{\mathcal{C}}({\rm rep}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(Q))

In
[
8
]
, Fang-Lan-Xiao proved the formula (
3

A\in{\mathcal{D}}^{b,ss}_{\mathbf{G}_{\alpha},m}(\mathbf{E}_{\alpha}),B\in{\mathcal{D}}^{b,ss}_{\mathbf{G}_{\beta},m}(\mathbf{E}_{\beta})

beyond the composition subalgebra, equivalently, Green’s formula (

In the present paper, we generalize Fang-Lan-Xiao’s result and prove the following theorem, see section

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
(A,\varphi)\in\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}_{\alpha}
and
(B,\psi)\in\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}_{\beta}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{Res}_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}^{\gamma}\mathbf{Ind}_{\alpha,\beta}^{\gamma}((A,\varphi)\boxtimes(B,\psi))\cong(C,\phi)\oplus\bigoplus_{\lambda=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in\mathcal{N}^{a}}(\mathbf{Ind}_{\alpha_{1},\beta_{1}}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\boxtimes\mathbf{Ind}_{\alpha_{2},\beta_{2}}^{\beta^{\prime}})\tilde{{\tau_{\lambda}}_{!}}

\displaystyle(\mathbf{Res}_{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}^{\alpha}(A,\varphi)\boxtimes\mathbf{Res}_{\beta_{1},\beta_{2}}^{\beta}(B,\psi))[-(\alpha_{2},\beta_{1})](-\frac{(\alpha_{2},\beta_{1})}{2}),

where
(C,\phi)\in\tilde{{\mathcal{D}}}_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}
is traceless.

This formula is a generation of the formula (
3
) for equivariant mixed semisimple complexes for the quiver
Q
with the admissible automorphism
a
. As an application, by applying the trace map, we obtain Green’s formula (
1
) for the Hall algebra
{\mathcal{H}}({\rm rep}_{k}^{F_{Q,a}}(Q))
of
F_{Q,a}
-stable representations, see section

\Lambda

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, there is a finite quiver
Q_{\Lambda}
without loops and an admissible automorphism
a

\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\Lambda\simeq{\rm rep}_{k}^{F_{Q_{\Lambda},a}}(Q_{\Lambda}),\ {\mathcal{H}}(\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\Lambda)\cong{\mathcal{H}}({\rm rep}_{k}^{F_{Q_{\Lambda},a}}(Q_{\Lambda})),

see section
3

1
) for
\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\Lambda

2.
Quiver with automorphism and hereditary algebra

Definition 2.1
.

(1)
A finite quiver
(I,H,s,t)
consists of two finite sets
I,H
and two maps
s,t:H\rightarrow I
, where
I
is the set of vertices,
H
is the the set of arrows, and for any
h\in H
, the images
s(h)
and
t(h)\in I
are its source and target respectively. A loop of the quiver is an arrow
h\in H
satisfying
s(h)=t(h)
.

(2)
Let
(I,H,s,t)
be a finite quiver without loops, an admissible automorphism
a
of the quiver consists of two permutations
a:I\rightarrow I
and
a:H\rightarrow H
satisfying

(a)

a(s(h))=s(a(h)),a(t(h))=t(a(h))
for any
h\in H
;

(b)

s(h),t(h)\in I
belong to different
a
-orbits for any
h\in H
.

Definition 2.2
.

(1)
Let
V
be a
k
-vector space, a Frobenius map on
V
is a
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear isomorphism
F_{V}:V\rightarrow V
satisfying

(a)
for any
\lambda\in k
and
v\in V
, we have
F_{V}(\lambda v)=\lambda^{q}F_{V}(v)
;

(b)
for any
v\in V
, there exists
n\geqslant 1
such that
F_{V}^{n}(v)=v
.

If there exists such a Frobenius map, then the fixed point set
V^{F_{V}}
is a
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subspace such that
V=k\otimes_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}V^{F_{V}}
, and we say
V
has a
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-structure.

(2)
Let
A
be an algebra over
k
, a Frobenius morphism on
A
is a Frobenius map
F_{A}:A\rightarrow A
on the underlying
k
-vector space preserving the unit and the multiplication. If there exists such a Frobenius morphism, then the fixed point set
A^{F_{A}}
is a
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-subalgebra such that
A=k\otimes_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}A^{F_{A}}
, and we say
A
has a
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-structure.

(3)
Let
A
be an algebra over
k
with the Frobenius morphism
F_{A}:A\rightarrow A
and
M\in\mbox{\rm mod}_{k}A
, a Frobenius morphism on
M
is a Frobenius map
F_{M}:M\rightarrow M
on the underlying
k
-vector space satisfying
F_{M}(ma)=F_{M}(m)F_{A}(a)
for any
m\in M
and
a\in A
. If there exists such a Frobenius morphism, then the fixed point set
M^{F_{M}}\in\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(A^{F_{A}})
such that
M=k\otimes_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}M^{F_{M}}
, and we say
M
is
F_{A}
-stable.

(4)
Let
A
be a
k
-algebra with the Frobenius morphism
F_{A}:A\rightarrow A
, we define
\mbox{\rm mod}_{k}^{F_{A}}A
to be the category of
F_{A}
-stable modules. More precisely, its objects are of the form
(M,F_{M})
, where
M\in\mbox{\rm mod}_{k}A
is
F_{A}
-stable and
F_{M}:M\rightarrow M
is the Frobenius morphism; and its morphisms
(M,F_{M})\rightarrow(M^{\prime},F_{M^{\prime}})
are morphisms
f:M\rightarrow M^{\prime}
of
A
-modules satisfying
fF_{M}=F_{M^{\prime}}f
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
6
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

There is an equivalence of categories

\mbox{\rm mod}_{k}^{F_{A}}A\xrightarrow{\simeq}\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(A^{F_{A}})

defined by
(M,F_{M})\mapsto M^{F_{M}}
.

For any
(M,F_{M}),(M^{\prime},F_{M^{\prime}})\in\mbox{\rm mod}_{k}^{F_{A}}A
, we have
(M,F_{M})\cong(M^{\prime},F_{M^{\prime}})
in
\mbox{\rm mod}_{k}^{F_{A}}A
if and only if
M^{F_{M}}\cong M^{\prime F_{M^{\prime}}}
in
\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(A^{F_{A}})
. Moreover, by
[
6
, Lemma 3.1]
, we have
M^{F_{M}}\cong M^{\prime F_{M^{\prime}}}
in
\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(A^{F_{A}})
if and only if
M\cong M^{\prime}
in
\mbox{\rm mod}_{k}A
.

Example 2.4

(
[
6
, Example 3.5]
)
.

Let
Q=(I,H,s,t)
be a finite quiver without loops,
a
be an admissible automorphism and
kQ
be the path algebra. For any trivial path
e_{i}
at
i\in I
, we define
a(e_{i})=e_{a(i)}
; for any non-trivial path
p=h_{1}...h_{t}
, where
h_{1},...,h_{t}\in H
, we define
a(p)=a(h_{1})...a(h_{t})
. Then there is a Frobenius morphism
F_{Q,a}:kQ\rightarrow kQ
defined by

\displaystyle\sum_{s}\lambda_{s}p_{s}\mapsto\sum_{s}\lambda_{s}^{q}a(p_{s})\ \textrm{for any $\lambda_{s}\in k$ and paths $p_{s}$.}

Theorem 2.5

(
[
6
, Theorem 6.5]
)
.

Let
\Lambda
be a finite-dimensional hereditary basic algebra over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, then there exists a finite quiver
Q_{\Lambda}
without loops and an admissible automorphism
a
such that
\Lambda\cong(kQ_{\Lambda})^{F_{Q_{\Lambda},a}}
.

By Theorem
2.3
and
2.5
, there are equivalences of categories

\mbox{\rm mod}_{k}^{F_{Q_{\Lambda},a}}(kQ_{\Lambda})\simeq\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}((kQ_{\Lambda})^{F_{Q_{\Lambda},a}})\simeq\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\Lambda.

Let
Q=(I,H,s,t)
be a finite quiver without loops,
a
be an admissible automorphism and
{\rm rep}_{k}(Q)
be the category of finite-dimensional representations of
Q
over
k
. It is well known that there is an equivalence of categories

\Psi:\mbox{\rm mod}_{k}(kQ)\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\rm rep}_{k}(Q),

see
[
2
, Theorem 1.6]
. More precisely, for any
M\in\mbox{\rm mod}_{k}(kQ)
, the representation
\Psi(M)=(V,x)=(\bigoplus_{i\in I}V_{i},(x_{h}:V_{s(h)}\rightarrow V_{t(h)})_{h\in H})
is given by

\bullet

V_{i}=Me_{i}
, where
e_{i}
is the trivial path at
i
for any
i\in I
;

\bullet

x_{h}:V_{s(h)}\rightarrow V_{t(h)}
maps
m\in Me_{s(h)}
to
mh=me_{s(h)}he_{t(h)}\in Me_{t(h)}
for any
h\in H
.

Definition 2.6
.

We define
{\rm rep}_{k}^{F_{Q,a}}(Q)
to be the category of
F_{Q,a}
-stable representations. More precisely, its objects are of the form
(V,x,F_{V})
, where
(V,x)\in{\rm rep}_{k}(Q)
and
F_{V}:V\rightarrow V
is a Frobenius map on
V
satisfying

(a)

F_{V}(V_{i})=V_{a(i)}
for any
i\in I
;

(b)

F_{V}x_{h}=x_{a(h)}F_{V}:V_{s(h)}\rightarrow V_{t(a(h))}
for any
h\in H
,

and its morphisms
(V,x,F_{V})\rightarrow(V^{\prime},x^{\prime},F_{V^{\prime}})
are morphisms
(f_{i})_{i\in I}:(V,x)\rightarrow(V^{\prime},x^{\prime})
of quiver representations satisfying
f_{a(i)}F_{V}=F_{V^{\prime}}f_{i}
for any

.

It is routine to check that
\Psi
induces to an equivalence of categories

\Psi:\mbox{\rm mod}_{k}^{F_{Q,a}}(kQ)\xrightarrow{\simeq}{\rm rep}_{k}^{F_{Q,a}}(Q),

see

(V,x,F_{V}),(V^{\prime},x^{\prime},F_{V^{\prime}})\in{\rm rep}_{k}^{F_{Q,a}}(Q)
, we have
(V,x)\cong(V^{\prime},x^{\prime})
in

if and only if
(V,x,F_{V})\cong(V^{\prime},x^{\prime},F_{V^{\prime}})

Summarizing above results, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
\Lambda
be a finite-dimensional hereditary basic algebra over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, then there exists a finite quiver
Q_{\Lambda}
without loops and an admissible automorphism
a
such that

\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\Lambda\simeq{\rm rep}_{k}^{F_{Q_{\Lambda},a}}(Q_{\Lambda}).

3.
Hall algebra

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an essentially small,
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear, hereditary, finitary, abelian category such that any object has finitely many subobjects,
\textrm{Iso}({\mathcal{A}})
be the set of isomorphism classes of objects of
{\mathcal{A}}
and
K({\mathcal{A}})
be the Grothendieck group of
{\mathcal{A}}
. For any
M\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we denote by
[M]\in\textrm{Iso}({\mathcal{A}})
its isomorphism class,
\hat{M}\in K({\mathcal{A}})
its image.

The Euler form
\langle-,-\rangle
is a bilinear form on
K({\mathcal{A}})
with values in
{\mathbb{Z}}
induced by

\langle\hat{M},\hat{N}\rangle=\mbox{\rm dim}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\,\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)-\mbox{\rm dim}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\,\mbox{\rm Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)\ \textrm{for any}\ M,N\in{\mathcal{A}}.

The symmetric Euler form
(-,-)
is given by

(\alpha,\beta)=\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle+\langle\beta,\alpha\rangle\ \textrm{for any}\ \alpha,\beta\in K({\mathcal{A}}).

Let
{v_{q}}\in{\mathbb{C}}
be a fixed square root of
q
.

Definition 3.1
.

(1)
The Hall algebra
\mathcal{H}({\mathcal{A}})
for the category
{\mathcal{A}}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-algebra with a basis
\{u_{[M]}|[M]\in{\rm{Iso}}({\mathcal{A}})\}
and the multiplication

u_{[M]}*u_{[N]}={v_{q}}^{\langle\hat{M},\hat{N}\rangle}\sum_{[L]\in{\rm{Iso}}({\mathcal{A}})}g_{MN}^{L}u_{[L]},

where
g_{MN}^{L}
is the number of submodules
L^{\prime}\subset L
satisfying
L/L^{\prime}\cong M,L^{\prime}\cong N
.

(2)
The comultiplication on
\mathcal{H}({\mathcal{A}})
is defined by

\Delta(u_{[L]})=\sum_{[M],[N]\in{\rm{Iso}}({\mathcal{A}})}{v_{q}}^{\langle\hat{M},\hat{N}\rangle}g^{L}_{MN}a_{M}a_{N}a_{L}^{-1}u_{[M]}\otimes u_{[N]},

where
a_{-}=|\mbox{\rm Aut}_{\mathcal{A}}(-)|
is the order of the automorphism group.

By Riedtmann-Peng’s formula, we have

g^{L}_{MN}=\frac{|\mbox{\rm Ext}^{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(M,N)_{L}|}{|\mbox{\rm Hom}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(M,N)|}\frac{a_{L}}{a_{M}a_{N}},

where
\mbox{\rm Ext}^{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(M,N)_{L}\subset\mbox{\rm Ext}^{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(M,N)
is the subset consisting of extensions whose middle terms are isomorphic to
L
. Hence we have

\Delta(u_{[L]})=\sum_{[M],[N]\in{\rm{Iso}}({\mathcal{A}})}{v_{q}}^{\langle\hat{M},\hat{N}\rangle}\frac{|\mbox{\rm Ext}^{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(M,N)_{L}|}{|\mbox{\rm Hom}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(M,N)|}u_{[M]}\otimes u_{[N]}

Theorem 3.2

(
[
14
,
9
]
)
.

(1)
The algebra
\mathcal{H}({\mathcal{A}})
is associative with the unit
u_{[0]}
given by the zero object
0\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

(2)
The coalgebra
\mathcal{H}({\mathcal{A}})
is coassociative with the counit
u_{[M]}\mapsto\delta_{M,0}
.

Theorem 3.3

(Green’s formula,
[
9
]
)
.

For any
M,N,M^{\prime},N^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{A}}
, we have

\displaystyle a_{M}a_{N}a_{M^{\prime}}a_{N^{\prime}}\sum_{[L]\in{\rm{Iso}}({\mathcal{A}})}g^{L}_{MN}g^{L}_{M^{\prime}N^{\prime}}a_{L}^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{[M_{1}],[M_{2}],[N_{1}],[N_{2}]\in{\rm{Iso}}({\mathcal{A}})}q^{-\langle\hat{M_{1}},\hat{N_{2}}\rangle}g^{M}_{M_{1}M_{2}}g^{N}_{N_{1}N_{2}}g^{M^{\prime}}_{M_{1}N_{1}}g^{N^{\prime}}_{M_{2}N_{2}}a_{M_{1}}a_{M_{2}}a_{N_{1}}a_{N_{2}}.

As a result, the comultiplication map
\Delta:{\mathcal{H}}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow{\mathcal{H}}({\mathcal{A}})\otimes{\mathcal{H}}({\mathcal{A}})
is an algebra homomorphism, where the multiplication on
{\mathcal{H}}({\mathcal{A}})\otimes{\mathcal{H}}({\mathcal{A}})
is defined by

(u_{[M_{1}]}\otimes u_{[M_{2}]})*(u_{[N_{1}]}\otimes u_{[N_{2}]})={v_{q}}^{(\hat{M_{2}},\hat{N_{1}})}(u_{[M_{1}]}*u_{[N_{1}]})\otimes(u_{[M_{2}]}*u_{[N_{2}]}).

Let
\Lambda
be a finite-dimensional hereditary basic algebra over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, by Corollary
2.7
, there is an equivalence of categories

\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\Lambda\simeq{\rm rep}_{k}^{F_{Q_{\Lambda},a}}(Q_{\Lambda}).

Note that these categories satisfy the assumptions on
{\mathcal{A}}
in the beginning of this section, thus their Hall algebras can be defined. Since the categories are equivalent, we have the bialgebra isomorphism

{\mathcal{H}}(\mbox{\rm mod}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\Lambda)\cong{\mathcal{H}}({\rm rep}_{k}^{F_{Q_{\Lambda},a}}(Q_{\Lambda})).

4.
Induction functor and restriction functor

4.1.
Preliminary

4.1.1.
Periodic functor

In this subsection, we review the definition of the periodic functor and refer
[
12
, Chapter 11]
for more details. Let
n
be a fixed positive integer.

Definition 4.1
.

(1)
Let
{\mathcal{C}}
be a
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}_{l}
-linear category, a periodic functor on
{\mathcal{C}}
is a linear functor
a^{*}:{\mathcal{C}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{C}}
such that
a^{*n}
is the identity functor on
{\mathcal{C}}
.

(2)
Let
a^{*}
be a periodic functor on
{\mathcal{C}}
, we define the category
\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}
, also denoted by
{\mathcal{C}}^{\ \widetilde{}}
.

\bullet
Its objects are pairs
(A,\varphi)
, where
A\in{\mathcal{C}}
and
\phi:a^{*}(A)\rightarrow A
is an isomorphism in
{\mathcal{C}}
such that the composition

A=a^{*n}(A)\xrightarrow{a^{*(n-1)(\varphi)}}a^{*(n-1)}(A)\rightarrow....\rightarrow a^{*}(A)\xrightarrow{\varphi}A

is the identity morphism on
A
.

\bullet
For any
(A,\varphi),(A^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})\in\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}
, the morphism space

\mbox{\rm Hom}_{\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}}((A,\varphi),(A^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}))=\{f\in\mbox{\rm Hom}_{{\mathcal{C}}}(A,A^{\prime})|f\varphi=\varphi^{\prime}(a^{*}(f))\}.

(3)
The direct sum of
(A,\varphi),(A^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})\in\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}
is
(A\oplus A^{\prime},\varphi\oplus\varphi^{\prime})
.

(4)
An object
(A,\phi)\in\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}
is called traceless, if there exists an object
B\in{\mathcal{C}}
and an integer
t\geqslant 2
dividing
n
such that
a^{*t}(B)\cong B
,
A\cong B\oplus a^{*}(B)\oplus...\oplus a^{*(t-1)}(B)
and
\varphi:a^{*}(A)\rightarrow A
corresponds to the isomorphism
a^{*}(B)\oplus a^{*2}(B)\oplus...\oplus a^{*t}(B)
taking
a^{*s}(B)
onto
a^{*s}(B)
for
1\leqslant s\leqslant t-1
and the taking
a^{*t}(B)
onto
B
.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
12
, Section 11.1.3]
)
.

Let
(A,\varphi),(A^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime}),(A^{\prime\prime},\varphi^{\prime\prime})
be objects in
\tilde{{\mathcal{C}}}
and

p^{\prime}i^{\prime}=1_{A^{\prime}},\ p^{\prime}i^{\prime\prime}=0,\ p^{\prime\prime}i^{\prime}=0,\ p^{\prime\prime}i^{\prime\prime}=1_{A^{\prime\prime}},\ i^{\prime}p^{\prime}+i^{\prime\prime}p^{\prime\prime}=1_{A},

Let

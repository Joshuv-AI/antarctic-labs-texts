Rigidity and Vanishing Theorems for Almost Even-Clifford Hermitian Manifolds
Ana Lucia GARCIA-PULIDO and Rafael HERRERA

Abstract
We prove the rigidity and vanishing of several indices of “geometrically natural” twisted Dirac operators on almost even-Clifford Hermitian manifolds admitting circle actions by automorphisms.

Introduction

There are two classical vanishing theorems for the
\widehat{A}
-genus (the index of the Dirac operator) on Spin manifolds: the Lichnerowicz vanishing
[
17
]
which assumes a metric of positive scalar curvature, and the Atiyah–Hirzebruch vanishing
[
3
]
which assumes smooth circle action. These vanishings can be seen and have been used frequently as obstructions to the existence of such metrics or actions. More vanishing theorems for the indices of Spin
c
Dirac operators were explored by Hattori
[
11
]
on almost complex manifolds and Spin
c
manifolds with compatible circle actions, which have parallels on complex manifolds with ample line bundles (a positivity condition for certain curvature) as in the case of the Kodaira vanishing theorem. Vanishing theorems have also been proven for indices of twisted Dirac operators on compact quaternion-Kähler manifolds with positive scalar curvature
[
16
]
, and for almost quaternion-Hermitian manifolds with isometric circle actions that preserve the almost quaternion-Hermitian structure
[
12
]
.

The vanishings of such indices on manifolds with isometric circle actions are instances of the rigidity of elliptic operators under such actions,
an important property in the context of elliptic genera
[
6
,
8
,
13
,
15
,
18
,
22
,
23
,
24
]
. In this paper, we prove the rigidity and vanishing of the indices of several “geometrically natural” twisted Dirac operators on almost even-Clifford manifolds admitting circle actions by automorphisms, resembling those studied on almost quaternionic-Hermitian manifolds.

The note is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall some material on Clifford algebras, Spin groups and representations, maximal tori of classical Lie groups, almost even-Clifford Hermitian manifolds and their structure groups. In Section
3
, we examine the weights of the Spin representation in terms of the weights of the aforementioned structure groups and explore which representations to use in the twisted Dirac operators. In Section
4
, we prove the vanishing Theorems
4.7
,
4.8
and
4.9
, using the Atiyah–Singer fixed point theorem.

2.1
Clifford algebra, spin group and representation

Let
{\rm Cl}_{n}
denote the
2^{n}
-dimensional real Clifford algebra generated by the orthonormal vectors
e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
subject to
the relations

\displaystyle e_{i}e_{j}+e_{j}e_{i}=-2\delta_{ij},

and
\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{n}={\rm Cl}_{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
its complexification. The even Clifford subalgebra
{\rm Cl}_{r}^{0}
is defined as the invariant
(+1)
-subspace of the involution of
{\rm Cl}_{r}
induced by the map
-{\rm Id}_{\mathbb{R}^{r}}
.

There exist algebra isomorphisms

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{n}\cong\begin{cases}{\rm End}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2^{k}}\big)&\text{if $n=2k$},\\
{\rm End}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2^{k}}\big)\oplus{\rm End}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2^{k}}\big)&\text{if $n=2k+1$},\end{cases}

(2.1)

and the space of (complex) spinors is defined to be

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}:=\mathbb{C}^{2^{k}}=\underbrace{\mathbb{C}^{2}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathbb{C}^{2}}_{k\,\,{\rm times}}.

The map

\displaystyle\kappa\colon\ \mathbb{C}{\rm l}_{n}\longrightarrow{\rm End}\big(\mathbb{C}^{2^{k}}\big)

is defined to be either the aforementioned isomorphism for
n
even, or the isomorphism followed by the projection onto the first summand for
n
odd. In order to make
\kappa
explicit, consider the following matrices

\displaystyle{\rm Id}=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right),\qquad g_{1}=\left(\begin{matrix}i&0\\
0&-i\end{matrix}\right),\qquad g_{2}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&i\\
i&0\end{matrix}\right),\qquad T=\left(\begin{matrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{matrix}\right).

In terms of the generators
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
of the Clifford algebra,
\kappa
can be described explicitly as follows

\displaystyle e_{1}\mapsto{\rm Id}\otimes{\rm Id}\otimes\dots\otimes{\rm Id}\otimes{\rm Id}\otimes g_{1},

\displaystyle e_{2}\mapsto{\rm Id}\otimes{\rm Id}\otimes\dots\otimes{\rm Id}\otimes{\rm Id}\otimes g_{2},

\displaystyle e_{3}\mapsto{\rm Id}\otimes{\rm Id}\otimes\dots\otimes{\rm Id}\otimes g_{1}\otimes T,

\displaystyle e_{4}\mapsto{\rm Id}\otimes{\rm Id}\otimes\dots\otimes{\rm Id}\otimes g_{2}\otimes T,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots

\displaystyle e_{2k-1}\mapsto g_{1}\otimes T\otimes\dots\otimes T\otimes T\otimes T,

\displaystyle e_{2k}\mapsto g_{2}\otimes T\otimes\dots\otimes T\otimes T\otimes T,

and, if
n=2k+1
,

\displaystyle e_{2k+1}\mapsto iT\otimes T\otimes\dots\otimes T\otimes T\otimes T.

The vectors

\displaystyle u_{+1}={1\over\sqrt{2}}(1,-i)\qquad\text{and}\qquad u_{-1}={1\over\sqrt{2}}(1,i),

form a unitary basis of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
with respect to the standard Hermitian product. Thus,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}=\{u_{\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{k}}=u_{\varepsilon_{1}}\otimes\dots\otimes u_{\varepsilon_{k}}\,|\,\varepsilon_{j}=\pm 1,\,j=1,\dots,k\},

is a unitary basis of
\Delta_{n}=\mathbb{C}^{2^{k}}
with respect to the naturally induced Hermitian product.

The Spin group
{\rm Spin}(n)\subset{\rm Cl}_{n}
is the subset

\displaystyle{\rm Spin}(n)=\big\{x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{2l-1}x_{2l}\,|\,x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\,|x_{j}|=1,\,l\in\mathbb{N}\big\},

endowed with the product of the Clifford algebra. It is a Lie group and its Lie algebra is

\displaystyle\mathfrak{spin}(n)=\mbox{span}\{e_{i}e_{j}\,|\,1\leq i<j\leq n\}.

The restriction of
\kappa
to
{\rm Spin}(n)
defines the Lie group representation

\displaystyle\kappa_{n}:=\kappa|_{{\rm Spin}(n)}\colon\ {\rm Spin}(n)\longrightarrow{\rm GL}(\Delta_{n}),

which is, in fact, special unitary. We have the corresponding Lie algebra representation

\displaystyle\kappa_{n_{*}}\colon\ \mathfrak{spin}(n)\longrightarrow\mathfrak{gl}(\Delta_{n}).

Recall that the Spin group
{\rm Spin}(n)
is the universal double cover of
{\rm SO}(n)
,
n\geq 3
. For
n=2
we consider
{\rm Spin}(2)
to be the connected double cover of
{\rm SO}(2)
. The covering map will be denoted by

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}\colon\ {\rm Spin}(n)\rightarrow{\rm SO}(n)\subset{\rm GL}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big).

Its differential is given by
\lambda_{n_{*}}(e_{i}e_{j})=2E_{ij}
, where
E_{ij}=e_{i}^{*}\otimes e_{j}-e_{j}^{*}\otimes e_{i}
is the standard basis of the skew-symmetric matrices, and
e^{*}
denotes the metric dual of the vector
e
. Furthermore, we will abuse the notation and also denote by
\lambda_{n}
the induced representation on the exterior algebra
\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle\bigwedge$}^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

By means of
\kappa
, we have the Clifford multiplication

\displaystyle\mu_{n}\colon\ \mathbb{R}^{n}\otimes\Delta_{n}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Delta_{n},

\displaystyle x\otimes\phi

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mu_{n}(x\otimes\phi)=x\cdot\phi:=\kappa(x)(\phi).

The Clifford multiplication
\mu_{n}
is skew-symmetric with respect to the Hermitian product

\displaystyle\langle x\cdot\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle=\langle\mu_{n}(x\otimes\phi_{1}),\phi_{2}\rangle=-\langle\phi_{1},\mu_{n}(x\otimes\phi_{2})\rangle=-\langle\phi_{1},x\cdot\phi_{2}\rangle,

is
{\rm Spin}(n)
-equivariant and can be extended to a
{\rm Spin}(n)
-equivariant map

\displaystyle\mu_{n}\colon\ \raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle\bigwedge$}^{*}\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)\otimes\Delta_{n}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Delta_{n},

\displaystyle\omega\otimes\psi

\displaystyle\longmapsto\omega\cdot\psi.

When
n
is even, we define the following involution

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\Delta_{n},

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle\longmapsto(-i)^{n\over 2}{\rm vol}_{n}\cdot\psi,

where
\mbox{\rm vol}_{n}=e_{1}\cdots e_{n}
. The
\pm 1
eigenspace of this involution is denoted
\Delta_{n}^{\pm}
. These spaces have equal dimension and are irreducible representations of
{\rm Spin}(n)
. Note that our definition differs from the one given in
[
9
]
by a
(-1)^{n\over 2}
. The reason for this difference is that we want the spinor
u_{1,\dots,1}
to be always positive. In this case, we will denote the two representations by

\displaystyle\kappa_{n}^{\pm}\colon\ {\rm Spin}(n)\longrightarrow{\rm GL}\big(\Delta_{n}^{\pm}\big).

For future use, let us recall the effect of
{\rm vol}_{n}
on
\Delta_{n}=\Delta_{n}^{+}\oplus\Delta_{n}^{-}
when
n
is even:

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|}\hline\cr n\,\,\text{(mod 8)}&\Delta_{n}^{+}&\Delta_{n}^{-}\rule{0.0pt}{11.19443pt}\\
\hline\cr 0&1&-1\\
\hline\cr 2&i&-i\\
\hline\cr 4&-1&1\\
\hline\cr 6&-i&i\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

Furthermore, for
n\equiv 0
(mod 4),
n\not=4
,

\displaystyle\ker(\kappa_{n}^{+})=\begin{cases}\{1,{\rm vol}_{r}\}&\text{if $r\equiv 0$ (mod 8)},\\
\{1,-{\rm vol}_{r}\}&\text{if $r\equiv 4$ (mod 8)},\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle\ker(\kappa_{n}^{-})=\begin{cases}\{1,-{\rm vol}_{r}\}&\text{if $r\equiv 0$ (mod 8)},\\
\{1,{\rm vol}_{r}\}&\text{if $r\equiv 4$ (mod 8)}.\end{cases}

For
r
even, let

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}{\rm SO}(r):={{\rm SO}(r)\over\{\pm{\rm Id}_{r}\}}\cong{{\rm Spin}(r)\over\{\pm 1,\pm{\rm vol}_{r}\}},

and for
r\equiv 0
(mod 4) let

\displaystyle{\rm Spin}_{\pm}(r)\cong{{\rm Spin}(r)\over\{1,\pm{\rm vol}_{r}\}}.

Note that we will always denote by
1
and
{\rm Id}_{r}
the identity elements of
{\rm Spin}(r)
and
{\rm SO}(r)
respectively.

2.2
Maximal tori

2.2.1
\boldsymbol{{\rm SO}(n)}

Recall that a maximal torus of
{\rm SO}(n)
is given by

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}\cos(\eta_{1})&-\sin(\eta_{1})&&&\\
\sin(\eta_{1})&\cos(\eta_{1})&&&\\
&&\ddots&&\\
&&&\cos(\eta_{n/2})&-\sin(\eta_{n/2})\\
&&&\sin(\eta_{n/2})&\cos(\eta_{n/2})\end{matrix}\right)

if
n
is even, and

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}\cos(\eta_{1})&-\sin(\eta_{1})&&&&\\
\sin(\eta_{1})&\cos(\eta_{1})&&&&\\
&&\ddots&&&\\
&&&\cos(\eta_{[n/2]})&-\sin(\eta_{[n/2]})&\\
&&&\sin(\eta_{[n/2]})&\cos(\eta_{[n/2]})&\\
&&&&&1\end{matrix}\right)

if
n
is odd.

2.2.2
\boldsymbol{{\rm Spin}(n)}

Each one of the
2\times 2
rotation blocks is a transformation that can be achieved by using Clifford product. For instance the rotation

\displaystyle\left(\begin{matrix}\cos(\varphi_{1})&-\sin(\varphi_{1})&&&\\
\sin(\varphi_{1})&\cos(\varphi_{1})&&&\\
&&1&&\\
&&&\ddots&\\
&&&&1\end{matrix}\right)

can be achieved by using the element

\displaystyle e_{1}(-\cos(\varphi_{1}/2)e_{1}+\sin(\varphi_{1}/2)e_{2})=\cos(\varphi_{1}/2)+\sin(\varphi_{1}/2)e_{1}e_{2}\in{\rm Spin}(n)

as follows

\displaystyle(\cos(\varphi_{1}/2)+\sin(\varphi_{1}/2)e_{1}e_{2})y(\cos(\varphi_{1}/2)+\sin(\varphi_{1}/2)e_{2}e_{1})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(y_{1}\cos(\varphi_{1})-y_{2}\sin(\varphi_{1}))e_{1}+(y_{1}\sin(\varphi_{1})+y_{2}\cos(\varphi_{1}))e_{2}+y_{3}e_{3}+\cdots+y_{n}e_{n},

for
y=y_{1}e_{1}+\cdots+y_{n}e_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Thus, we see that the corresponding elements
in
{\rm Spin}(n)
are

\displaystyle\pm(\cos(\varphi_{1}/2)+\sin(\varphi_{1}/2)e_{1}e_{2}).

Furthermore, we see that a maximal torus of
{\rm Spin}(n)
is given by elements of the form

\displaystyle t(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{k})=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(\cos(\varphi_{j}/2)+\sin(\varphi_{j}/2)e_{2j-1}e_{2j}).

By using the explicit description (
2.2
) of the isomorphisms (
2.1
), we can check that

\displaystyle t(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{k})\cdot u_{\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{k}}=e^{{i\over 2}{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{k+1-j}\varphi_{j}}}\cdot u_{\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{k}},

i.e., the basis vectors
u_{\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{k}}
are weight vectors of the spin representation with weight

\displaystyle{1\over 2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\varepsilon_{k+1-j}\varphi_{j},

which in coordinate vectors with respect to the basis
\{\varphi_{j}\}
give the well known expressions

\displaystyle\left(\pm{1\over 2},\pm{1\over 2},\dots,\pm{1\over 2}\right).

Indeed, in terms of the (appropriately ordered) basis
\mathcal{B}
, the matrix associated to an element
t(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{[{n\over 2}]})
is

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}l@{}}e^{{i\over 2}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}+\cdots+\varphi_{[{n\over 2}]})}\\
\qquad e^{{i\over 2}(-\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}+\cdots+\varphi_{[{n\over 2}]})}\\
\qquad\qquad e^{{i\over 2}(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}+\cdots+\varphi_{[{n\over 2}]})}\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\ddots\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad e^{{i\over 2}(-\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}+\cdots+\varphi_{[{n\over 2}]})}\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ddots\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad e^{{i\over 2}(-\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}-\cdots-\varphi_{[{n\over 2}]})}\end{array}\right).

Note that, when
n
is even,
\Delta_{n}^{+}
is generated by the basis vectors
u_{\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{{n\over 2}}}
with an even number
of
\varepsilon_{j}
equal to
-1
, and
\Delta_{n}^{-}
is generated by the basis vectors
u_{\varepsilon_{1},\dots,\varepsilon_{{n\over 2}}}
with an odd number of
\varepsilon_{j}
equal to
-1
. Therefore, after reordering the basis, the matrix above can be rearranged to have two diagonal blocks of equal size: one block in which the exponents contain an even number of negative signs

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}l@{}}e^{{i\over 2}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}+\cdots+\varphi_{{n\over 2}})}\\
\qquad e^{{i\over 2}(-\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}+\cdots+\varphi_{{n\over 2}})}\\
\qquad\qquad e^{{i\over 2}(-\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}-\cdots+\varphi_{{n\over 2}})}\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\ddots\end{array}\right),

and another block in which the exponents contain an odd number of negative signs

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{@{}l@{}}e^{{i\over 2}(-\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}+\cdots+\varphi_{{n\over 2}})}\\
\qquad e^{{i\over 2}(\varphi_{1}-\varphi_{2}+\cdots+\varphi_{{n\over 2}})}\\
\qquad\qquad e^{{i\over 2}(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}-\varphi_{3}+\cdots+\varphi_{{n\over 2}})}\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\ddots\end{array}\right).

2.3
Almost even-Clifford Hermitian structures

Definition 2.1
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
(e_{1},\dots,e_{r})
an orthonormal frame of
\mathbb{R}^{r}
.

•

A
linear even-Clifford structure of rank
r
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an algebra representation

\displaystyle\Phi\colon\ {\rm Cl}_{r}^{0}\longrightarrow{\rm End}\big(\mathbb{R}^{N}\big).

•

A
linear even-Clifford Hermitian structure of rank
r
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
(endowed with a positive definite inner product) is a linear even-Clifford structure of rank
r
such that each bivector
e_{i}e_{j}
,
1\leq i<j\leq r
, is mapped to a skew-symmetric endomorphism
\Phi(e_{i}e_{j})=J_{ij}
.

Remark 2.2
.

•

Note that
J_{ij}^{2}=-{\rm Id}_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}
.

•

Given a linear even-Clifford structure of rank
r
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we can average the standard inner product
\langle\,,\,\rangle
on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
as follows

\displaystyle(X,Y)=\sum_{k=1}^{[r/2]}\bigg[\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{2k}<r}\langle\Phi(e_{i_{1}\dots i_{2k}})(X),\Phi(e_{i_{1}\dots i_{2k}})(Y)\rangle\bigg],

where
(e_{1},\dots,e_{r})
is an orthonormal frame of
\mathbb{R}^{r}
, so that the linear even-Clifford structure is Hermitian with respect to the averaged inner product.

•

Given a linear even-Clifford Hermitian structure of rank
r
, the subalgebra
\mathfrak{spin}(r)
is mapped injectively into the skew-symmetric endomorphisms
{\rm End}^{-}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
r\geq 2
.

•

A
rank
r
almost even-Clifford structure
on a smooth manifold
M
is a smoothly varying choice of a rank
r
linear even-Clifford structure on each tangent space of
M
.

•

A smooth manifold carrying an almost even-Clifford structure will be called an
almost even-Clifford manifold
.

•

A
rank
r
almost even-Clifford Hermitian structure
on a Riemannian manifold
M
is a smoothly varying choice of a linear even-Clifford Hermitian structure on each tangent space of
M
.

•

A Riemannian manifold carrying a rank
r
almost even-Clifford Hermitian structure will be called a
rank
r
almost even-Clifford Hermitian manifold
, or an
almost-
{\rm Cl}_{r}^{0}
-Hermitian manifold
for short.

Remark 2.4
.

Our definition of almost even-Clifford Hermitian structure does not require the existence of a Riemannian vector bundle of rank
r
. Therefore, it includes both the notions of even Clifford structure and projective even Clifford structure introduced in
[
19
, Definition 2.2 and Remark 2.5]

In this subsection, we present some calculations relevant to the global definiton of twisted spinor bundles.

When the structure group of an oriented

The elements of the finite subgroups involved in the structure groups of almost-

3.2.1
\boldsymbol{r=3}

Recall (
3.2

\displaystyle\eta_{2j-1}=\theta_{j}+{\varphi_{1}\over 2},\qquad\eta_{2j}=\theta_{j}-{\varphi_{1}\over 2},

\displaystyle\pm{\eta_{1}\over 2}\pm\cdots\pm{\eta_{2m}\over 2}=\sum_{j\in I_{1}}{\theta_{j}+{\varphi_{1}\over 2}\over 2}-\sum_{j\in\bar{I_{1}}}{\theta_{j}+{\varphi_{1}\over 2}\over 2}+\sum_{j\in I_{2}}{\theta_{j}-{\varphi_{1}\over 2}\over 2}-\sum_{j\in\bar{I_{2}}}{\theta_{j}-{\varphi_{1}\over 2}\over 2},

, and
\bar{I_{j}}=\{1,\dots,m\}-I_{j}
denote their complements,
j=1,2
. The element

corresponds to the parameters

\displaystyle\theta_{j}=\pi,\qquad\varphi_{1}=2\pi,

for

, so that such a sum is equal to

\displaystyle 2|I_{1}|\pi-m\pi

and the effect of

\displaystyle e^{-im\pi}=(-1)^{m}.

Thus,
(-{\rm Id}_{2m},-1)\in{\rm Sp}(m)\times{\rm Spin}(3)
acts trivially on
\Delta_{4m}
if
m
is even and as multiplication by
(-1)
if
m
is odd.

Thus, in order to have a twisted Spin representation

\displaystyle\Delta_{4m}\otimes\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle\bigwedge$}^{u}\mathbb{C}^{2m}\otimes(\Delta_{3})^{\otimes s}

which is a well known fact for almost quaternion-Hermitian manifolds

4.1
Rigidity of elliptic operators

Definition 4.1
.

Let
D\colon\Gamma(E)\longrightarrow\Gamma(F)
be an elliptic operator acting on sections of the vector bundles
E
and
F
over a compact manifold
M
. The index of
D
is the virtual vector space
\mbox{\rm ind}(D)=\ker(D)-\mbox{\rm coker}(D)
. If
M
admits a circle action preserving
D
, i.e., such that
S^{1}
acts on
E
and
F
, and commutes with
D
,
\mbox{\rm ind}(D)
admits a Fourier decomposition into complex
1
-dimensional irreducible representations of
S^{1}

\mbox{\rm ind}(D)=\sum a_{m}L^{m}
, where
a_{m}\in\mbox{$\mathbb{Z}$}
and
L^{m}
is the representation of
S^{1}
on
\mathbb{C}
given by
\lambda\mapsto\lambda^{m}
. The elliptic operator
D
is called
rigid
if
a_{m}=0
for all
m\neq 0
, i.e.,
\mbox{\rm ind}(D)
consists only of the trivial representation with multiplicity
a_{0}
.

Let us recall three examples.

Example 4.2
.

The deRham complex

\displaystyle d+d^{*}\colon\ \Omega^{\rm even}\longrightarrow\Omega^{\rm odd}

from even-dimensional forms to odd-dimensional ones, where
d^{*}
denotes the adjoint of the exterior derivative
d
, is
rigid
for any circle action on
M
by isometries since by Hodge theory the kernel and the cokernel of this operator consist of harmonic forms, which by homotopy invariance stay fixed under the circle action.

Example 4.3
.

The signature operator on an oriented manifold

\displaystyle d_{s}\colon\ \Omega_{c}^{+}\longrightarrow\Omega_{c}^{-}

from even to odd complex forms under the Hodge
*
operator is
rigid
for any circle action on
M
by isometries since the kernel and cokernel of this operator consist of harmonic forms.

Example 4.4
.

The Dirac operator on a Spin manifold is
rigid
for any circle action by isometries
[
3
]
.

4.2
Twisted Dirac operators

In this subsection, let
M
be a
4n
-dimensional oriented Riemannian manifold.
M
is Spin if its orthonormal frame bundle
P_{{\rm SO}(4n)}
admits a double cover by a principal bundle
P_{{\rm Spin}(4n)}
with structure group
{\rm Spin}(4n)
, which gives rise to the spinor bundle

\displaystyle P_{{\rm Spin}(4n)}\times_{\kappa}\Delta_{4n}.

The Levi-Civita connection on
P_{{\rm SO}(4n)}
can be lifted to
P_{{\rm Spin}(4n)}
to define a covariant differentiation
\nabla
on
\Delta

\displaystyle\nabla\colon\ \Gamma(\Delta)\longrightarrow\Gamma(T^{*}\otimes\Delta),

and the (elliptic and self-adjoint) Dirac operator

\displaystyle/\kern-5.0pt\partial(\psi)=\sum_{i=1}^{4n}e^{i}\cdot\nabla_{e_{i}}\psi

for
\psi\in\Gamma(\Delta)
, where
(e_{1},\dots,e_{4n})
is a local orthonormal frame. Since the spin representation decomposes, the Dirac operator can be split into two parts

\displaystyle/\kern-5.0pt\partial\colon\ \Gamma(\Delta_{+})\longrightarrow\Gamma(\Delta_{-}),\qquad/\kern-5.0pt\partial^{*}\colon\ \Gamma(\Delta_{-})\longrightarrow\Gamma(\Delta_{+}).

We are interested in Dirac operators with coefficients in auxiliary vector bundles
F
equipped with a covariant derivative
\nabla^{F}\colon\Gamma(F)\longrightarrow\Gamma(T^{*}\otimes F)
. The Dirac operator twisted by
F
(or with coefficients
in
F
)

\displaystyle(/\kern-5.0pt\partial\otimes F)\colon\ \Gamma(\Delta_{+}\otimes F)\longrightarrow\Gamma(\Delta_{-}\otimes F)

is defined by

\displaystyle(/\kern-5.0pt\partial\otimes F)(\psi\otimes f)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{4n}e^{i}\cdot\nabla_{e_{i}}\psi\right)\otimes f+\sum_{i=0}^{4n}\mu\big(e^{i}\otimes\psi\big)\otimes\nabla_{e_{i}}^{F}f,

where
\psi\in\Gamma(\Delta)
,
f\in\Gamma(F)
.

Remark 4.5
.

If the manifold is not Spin, there may exist well defined twisted spinor bundles (as above), as it happens when the structure group of
M
reduces to a subgroup of
{\rm SO}(4n)
and
\Delta_{4n}\otimes F
is a representation of such subgroup.

4.3
Index formula and localization

Let
M
be an compact
4n
-dimensional oriented Riemannian manifold. Let us assume that the bundle

\displaystyle\Delta_{4n}\otimes F

is well defined, where we will use the same symbol to denote the representation and the associated vector bundle, where the
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(F)=p
. Since
\Delta_{4n}\otimes F
is a Clifford bundle, by the Atiyah–Singer index theorem
[
5
,
20
]
, the index of the twisted Dirac operators can be computed as

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm ind}(/\kern-5.0pt\partial\otimes F)=\big\langle\mbox{$\widehat{A}$}(M)\mbox{\rm ch}(F),[M]\big\rangle,

where
\mbox{\rm ch}(\cdot)
denotes the Chern character,
\mbox{$\widehat{A}$}(M)
denotes the
\widehat{A}
-genus, and

denotes the fundamental cycle of
M
. In terms of formal roots,

\displaystyle c(TM\otimes\mathbb{C})=(1+\eta_{1})(1-\eta_{1})\cdots(1+\eta_{2n})(1-\eta_{2n}),

\displaystyle p(TM)=\big(1+\eta_{1}^{2}\big)\cdots\big(1+\eta_{2n}^{2}\big),

\displaystyle c(F)=(1+\nu_{1})\cdots(1+\nu_{p}),

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm ch}(F)=\sum_{l=1}e^{\nu_{l}},

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm ind}(/\kern-5.0pt\partial\otimes F)=\left\langle\sum_{l=1}e^{\nu_{l}}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{2n}{\eta_{i}\over e^{\eta_{i}\over 2}-e^{-{\eta_{i}\over 2}}},[M]\right\rangle.

If
M
admits a non-trivial
S^{1}
action that lifts to
\Delta_{4n}\otimes F
, the equivariant version of the index can be written in terms of the local data of the
S^{1}
-fixed point set
M^{S^{1}}
. More precisely, let
z\in S^{1}
be a generic element of
S^{1}
. By the Atiyah–Singer fixed point theorem
[
4
,
5
]

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm ind}(/\kern-5.0pt\partial\otimes F)_{z}=\sum_{P\subset M^{S^{1}}}\mu(P,z),

where
\mu(P,z)
is the local contribution of the oriented fixed point submanifold
P\subset M^{S^{1}}
, which can be computed as follows. The
S^{1}
action on
M
induces a decomposition of
TM
over
P
,

\displaystyle TM|_{P}=\sum_{k}N_{k},

(4.1)

where
N_{k}
is a bundle over
P
whose fibers are representations of
S^{1}
on which
z\in S^{1}
acts as an automorphism with multiple eigenvalue
z^{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Note that
P
inherits an orientation since
M
is oriented and the bundles
N_{k}
for
k\not=0
are naturally oriented. Formally, by means of the splitting principle, we can write

\displaystyle TM|_{P}=\mathcal{L}^{q_{1}}+\dots+\mathcal{L}^{q_{2n}},

(4.2)

where
\mathcal{L}
corresponds to the standard representation of
S^{1}
on
\mathbb{C}
, so that

acts by multiplication by
z^{q_{i}}
on
\mathcal{L}^{q_{i}}
. The integers
q_{i}=q_{i}(P)\in\mathbb{Z}
are the exponents of the action at
P
, which correspond to the aforementioned numbers
k
. Thus, following
[
14
, p. 67]
,

\displaystyle\mu(P,z)=\left\langle\sum z^{-n_{k}}e^{\nu_{k}}\prod_{q_{i}=0}{\eta_{i}\over e^{\eta_{i}\over 2}-e^{-{\eta_{i}\over 2}}}\prod_{q_{j}\not=0}{1\over z^{-{q_{j}\over 2}}e^{\eta_{j}\over 2}-z^{q_{j}\over 2}e^{-{\eta_{j}\over 2}}},[P]\right\rangle,

where

are the exponents of the action on

restricted to
P
. The function

is a rational function of the complex variable

with zeroes at
0
and
\infty
as long as

\displaystyle|n_{k}|<{1\over 2}\left(|q_{1}(P)|+\dots+|q_{2n}(P)|\right)

(4.3)

for all

. If such a condition is fulfilled for all

, then
\mbox{\rm ind}(/\kern-5.0pt\partial\otimes F)_{z}
is a rational function of
z
with zeroes at
0
and
\infty
. Notice that
\mbox{\rm ind}(/\kern-5.0pt\partial\otimes F)_{z}
also belongs to the representation ring

of

, which can be identified with the Laurent polynomial ring

. Hence, by Lemma

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm ind}(/\kern-5.0pt\partial\otimes F)=\mbox{\rm ind}(/\kern-5.0pt\partial\otimes F)_{1}=0,

i.e.,

\displaystyle\big\langle\mbox{$\widehat{A}$}(M)\mbox{\rm ch}(F),[M]\big\rangle=0.

4.5
Infinitesimal automorphisms

An
automorphism
of an almost-
{\rm Cl}_{r}^{0}
-Hermitian manifold
M
is an isometry which preserves the almost even-Clifford Hermitian structure. A vector field
X
on
M
is an
infinitesimal automorphism
if it is a Killing vector field that preserves the structure, i.e., locally

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{X}J_{ij}=\sum_{k<l}\alpha_{kl}^{(ij)}J_{kl},

for some (local) functions
\alpha_{kl}^{(ij)}
, where
\mathcal{L}_{X}
denotes the Lie derivative in the direction of
X
. Consider

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{X}(J_{ij}(Y))=({\mathcal{L}}_{X}J_{ij})(Y)+J_{ij}({\mathcal{L}}_{X}Y),

which can be written in terms of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
as follows

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}(J_{ij}(Y))-\nabla_{J_{ij}(Y)}X=\sum_{k<l}\alpha_{kl}^{(ij)}J_{kl}(Y)+J_{ij}(\nabla_{X}Y-\nabla_{Y}X).

Now, if
p\in M
is such that
X_{p}=0
,

\displaystyle-\nabla_{J_{ij}(Y)}X=\sum_{k<l}\alpha_{kl}^{(ij)}J_{kl}(Y)-J_{ij}(\nabla_{Y}X),

i.e.,

\displaystyle[J_{ij},\nabla X](Y)=\sum_{k<l}\alpha_{kl}^{(ij)}J_{kl}(Y).

Hence,
(\nabla X)_{p}
is a skew-symmetric endomorphism such that

\displaystyle[J_{ij},\nabla X]=\sum_{k<l}\alpha_{kl}^{(ij)}J_{kl}.

i.e.,
(\nabla X)_{p}
belongs to Lie algebra of the structure group of
M

[
1
,
2
]
.

We will say that a smooth circle action on an almost-
{\rm Cl}_{r}^{0}
-Hermitian manifold is an
action by automorphisms
if the corresponding Killing vector field is an infinitesimal automorphism.

Proof.

Since the
S^{1}
action is by automorphisms of the almost even-Clifford Hermitian structure, the action lifts to the bundles associated to the structure group, such as the twisted spin bundles we are considering. Given that the arguments are similar in all cases, we will only describe the calculation for
r\equiv 1,\,7
(mod 8) and
m
even.

Let
P\subset M^{S^{1}}
be an
S^{1}
-fixed submanifold. By Section
4.5
, over
P
the circle group of automorphisms maps non-trivially to the structure group
{\rm SO}(m){\rm Spin}(r)
, so that the fibers of the bundles
\Delta_{N}\otimes\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle\bigwedge$}^{u}E\otimes\Delta_{r}^{\otimes s}
over points of
P
decompose as sums of representations of
S^{1}
. Recall that

\displaystyle\mbox{\rm ch}\big(\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle\bigwedge$}^{u}E\big)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{u}\leq 2[\frac{m}{2}]}e^{\vartheta_{i_{1}}+\dots+\vartheta_{i_{u}}},

where

\displaystyle\vartheta_{j}=\theta_{j},\qquad\vartheta_{[m/2]+j}=-\theta_{j},\qquad j=1,\dots,[m/2].

Thus, the exponents of the twist will be of the form

\displaystyle{1\over 2}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{c}(-1)^{\varepsilon_{a}}t_{i_{a}}+\sum_{b=1}^{s}(-1)^{\delta_{b}}h_{l_{b}}\right),

where
0\leq c\leq u
,
\varepsilon_{a},\delta_{b}\in\{0,1\}
. There are two points to verify in the proof: firstly, that the contributions
\mu(P,z)
are rational functions and, secondly, that the exponents of the twisting bundles and the tangent space satisfy the inequality (
4.3
).

The first one follows from the fact that the fibers of the bundles
\Delta_{N}\otimes\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle\bigwedge$}^{u}E\otimes\Delta_{r}^{\otimes s}
over
P
decompose as sums of representations of
S^{1}
. Formally, according to the splitting principle, if

\displaystyle TM_{c}=L_{1}\oplus L_{1}^{-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus L_{N/2}\oplus L_{N/2}^{-1},

then

\displaystyle\Delta_{N}=\big(L_{1}^{1/2}\oplus L_{1}^{-1/2}\big)\otimes\cdots\otimes\big(L_{N/2}^{1/2}\oplus L_{N/2}^{-1/2}\big)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\Delta_N}{}=L_{1}^{1/2}\otimes\cdots\otimes L_{N/2}^{1/2}\oplus\cdots\oplus L_{1}^{-1/2}\otimes\cdots\otimes L_{N/2}^{-1/2},

so that the
S^{1}
-exponents on these lines will be of the form

\displaystyle\sum\left(\pm{t_{j}\pm h_{k}\over 4}\right)+\sum\left(\pm{h_{l}\over 4}\right).

The bundle
\Delta_{N}\otimes\raise 1.0pt\hbox{$\textstyle\bigwedge$}^{u}E\otimes\Delta_{r}^{\otimes s}
will have integer exponents over

of the form

\displaystyle{1\over 2}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{c}(-1)^{\varepsilon_{a}}t_{i_{a}}+\sum_{b=1}^{s}(-1)^{\delta_{b}}h_{l_{b}}\right)+\sum_{q_{j}\not=0}\left((-1)^{\gamma_{j}}{t_{j}+h_{k}\over 4}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad{}+\sum_{q_{j^{\prime}}\not=0}\left((-1)^{\gamma_{j^{\prime}}}{t_{{}^{\prime}j}-h_{k}\over 4}\right)+\sum_{q_{l}\not=0}\left((-1)^{\zeta_{l}}{h_{l}\over 4}\right).

Thus, the powers of
z
in each summand of
\mu(P,z)
can be rearranged in order to show that such a summand is a product of rational functions
such as the one described in Lemma
2.5
.

For the second point, it is sufficient to consider the exponents of the form

\displaystyle{1\over 2}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{u}(-1)^{\varepsilon_{a}}t_{i_{a}}\pm sh_{k}\right).

Since
u+s<[\frac{m}{2}]
, there exists an
s
-tuple of indices
j_{1}<\dots<j_{s}
such that
\{j_{1},\dots,j_{s}\}\subset\{1,\dots,[\frac{m}{2}]\}-\{i_{1},\dots,i_{u}\}
. Thus,

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{a=1}^{u}(-1)^{\varepsilon_{a}}t_{i_{a}}\pm sh_{k}\right|=\left|\sum_{a=1}^{u}(-1)^{\varepsilon_{a}}\left({t_{i_{a}}+h_{k}\over 2}+{t_{i_{a}}-h_{k}\over 2}\right)\pm\sum_{b=1}^{s}\left({h_{k}+t_{j_{b}}\over 2}+{h_{k}-t_{j_{b}}\over 2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\hphantom{\left|\sum_{a=1}^u (-1)^{\varepsilon_a}t_{i_a}\pm sh_k\right|}{}\leq\sum_{a=1}^{u}\left(\left|{t_{i_{a}}+h_{k}\over 2}\right|+\left|{t_{i_{a}}-h_{k}\over 2}\right|\right)+\sum_{b=1}^{s}\left(\left|{h_{k}+t_{j_{b}}\over 2}\right|+\left|{h_{k}-t_{j_{b}}\over 2}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\left|\sum_{a=1}^u (-1)^{\varepsilon_a}t_{i_a}\pm sh_k\right|}{}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left|{t_{i}+h_{k}\over 2}\right|+\left|{t_{i}-h_{k}\over 2}\right|<\sum_{l=1}^{2^{[r/2]-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left|{t_{i}+h_{l}\over 2}\right|+\left|{t_{i}-h_{l}\over 2}\right|

\displaystyle\hphantom{\left|\sum_{a=1}^u (-1)^{\varepsilon_a}t_{i_a}\pm sh_k\right|}{}\leq\sum_{c=1}^{N/2}|q_{c}|,

which is the corresponding version of the inequality (
4.3
) in Section
4.3

∎

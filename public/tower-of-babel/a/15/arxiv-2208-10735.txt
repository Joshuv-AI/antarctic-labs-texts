Robust control problems of BSDEs coupled with value functions
\fnms Chao \snm ZHOU \corref \ead [label=e3]matzc@nus.edu.sg

Abstract
A robust control problem is considered in this paper, where the controlled stochastic differential equations (SDEs) include ambiguity parameters and their coefficients satisfy non-Lipschitz continuous and non-linear growth conditions, the objective function is expressed as a backward stochastic differential equation (BSDE) with the generator depending on the value function. We establish the existence and uniqueness of the value function in a proper space and provide a verification theorem. Moreover, we apply the results to solve two typical optimal investment problems in the market with ambiguity, one of which is with Heston stochastic volatility model. In particular, we establish some sharp estimations for Heston model with ambiguity parameters.

Introduction

Since the seminal work of Merton
[
19
]
, there have been numerous studies of the optimal portfolio problem for an ambiguity averse investor under various assumptions. Most of these works conclude that it is essential to take ambiguity into account when making a decision for portfolio selection. Anderson et al.
[
1
]
studied the portfolio choice problems involving uncertainty about the return process for equities. In their setting, model uncertainty is reduced to uncertainty about the drift of the state variables. They used a statistical theory of detection to quantify how much model misspecification the decision maker should fear, given his historical data record.
Maenhout
[
16
]
adapted the general robust control framework of
[
1
]
to a dynamic portfolio and consumption problem with power utility. He proposed an important modification, namely homothetic robustness, that preserves wealth independence and analytical tractability. He showed that robustness dramatically decreases the demand for equities and is observationally equivalent to recursive preferences when removing wealth effects. As an extension, he also presented a closed-form solution for the portfolio problem for a robust Duffie-Epstein-Zin investor.
Escobar et al.
[
4
]
derived, by using the robust control approach, the optimal portfolio for an ambiguity averse investor in complete market. The stock price follows a stochastic volatility jump-diffusion process and the investor can have different levels of uncertainty about the models for the stock and its volatility. They found that the optimal exposures to stock and volatility risks are significantly affected by the ambiguity aversion to the corresponding risk factor only. However, in incomplete market, the volatility ambiguity has a smaller impact. The similar problems have also been considered in various fields, such as pension funds (see
[
2
,
23
,
25
]
), insurances (see
[
24
,
26
]
) and other related areas (see
[
20
]
). Compared to the previous works, in which the authors intended to find the closed-form solutions and study their properties, we now focus on the mathematical theory as well as the rigorous mathematical proofs.

We also want to mention some works in mathematical finance. Matoussi et al.
[
18
]
studied the problem of robust utility maximization in an incomplete market with volatility uncertainty in the framework of second-order backward stochastic differential equations (2BSDEs). They showed that the value function can be written as the initial value of some 2BSDE and proved the existence of an optimal strategy.
Pham et al.
[
21
]
considered a dynamic multi-asset mean-variance portfolio selection problem under model uncertainty and proved a general separation principle for the associated robust control problem.
Jin et al.
[
13
]
solved the optimal portfolio in closed-form to a portfolio choice problem in a multi-asset incomplete market with ambiguous jumps. They found that sizable portfolio rebalancing and subsequent wealth losses are encountered in the presence of jump ambiguity.
In all these papers, the authors considered the classical utility functions rather than the recursive type which is given by a BSDE coupled with value function. Moreover, the state SDEs are still assumed to satisfy the Lipschitz conditions, hence the results cannot be applied to some typically non-Lipschitz case in finance.

When investigating the optimal stochastic control problem involving mean-field BSDEs, in order to give the stochastic interpretation to nonlocal Hamilton-Jacobi-Bellman (HJB) equations of mean-field type, Hao and Li
[
10
]
introduced a new type of BSDEs coupled with value function. They studied the existence and uniqueness as well as the comparison theorem for such kind of BSDEs coupled with value function and proved the associated nonlocal HJB equation has a unique viscosity solution in the space of continuous functions of at most polynomial growth. Then, they continued to study the fully coupled forward backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDEs) case and the reflected mean-field BSDEs case in
[
11
]
and
[
15
]
, respectively. Notice that, in all these papers, the Lipschitz conditions of the coefficients are supposed to hold true. However, in our work, in order to apply the results to two typical examples in finance (see Section 4), we remove the Lipschitz conditions of the coefficients, this makes the problem more difficult, in particular, due to the lack of estimates for Heston model.

In this paper, we focus on the following robust control problem: the controlled state process is governed by, for
i=1,\cdots,n
and
0\leq t\leq s\leq T
,

X^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{i,\,s}=x_{i}+\int^{s}_{t}\alpha_{i}(r,X^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{r},\mathbf{u}_{r},\mathbf{v}_{r})\,dr+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}\int^{s}_{t}\beta_{i\,j}(r,X^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{r},\mathbf{u}_{r},\mathbf{v}_{r})\,dB^{j}_{r},

the payoff function is of recursive type given by

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76228ptY^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{s}=\int_{s}^{T\wedge\tau}f\left(r,X_{r}^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}},Y^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{r},Z^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{r},W\left(r,X_{r}^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}\right),\mathbf{u}_{r},\mathbf{v}_{r}\right)dr

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{s}^{T\wedge\tau}Z^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{j,r}\,dB^{j}_{r}+\Psi\left(T\wedge\tau,X_{T\wedge\tau}^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}\right),\quad 0\leq t\leq s\leq T\wedge\tau,

where
\tau
is the first escaping time and the value function is defined as follows

W(t,x)=\sup\limits_{\mathbf{u}\in{\cal U}(t,x)}\inf\limits_{\mathbf{v}\in{\cal V}(t,x)}Y^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{t}.

We prove that the above stochastic control problem admits a unique value function in some proper space under some mild conditions by the contraction mapping method and the bootstrap method.
As the generator of the BSDEs contains the value function, we first fix the value function in the generator, then our control problem becomes as a standard stochastic control problem. By using the localization method, together with dynamic programming principle (DPP) for the standard control problem and the relation between the standard control problem and our original problem, we prove the verification theorem, which states that the classical solution to HJBI equation is the unique value function to our robust control problem.
Our results can be applied to solve some typical optimal investment problems in the market with ambiguity, one of which is with Heston stochastic volatility model. In particular, some sharp estimations for Heston model with ambiguity parameters are established.

The main difficulties of our work come from the following four aspects: first, the objective functional is given by a BSDE coupling with the value function; second, the lack of estimates for Heston model in a market with ambiguity, in fact, to the best of our knowledge, even in a market with non-ambiguity, there are few results on the estimates of Heston model; third, the controlled SDEs are no longer assumed to satisfy the Lipschitz condition, so that the results can be applied to various cases, for example, to the Heston model; last, a proper space of the value functions need to be given.

One of the key points in our paper is the local estimate for the Heston model with ambiguity parameters (Conclusion (1) in Theorem A1), which may be powerful for some stochastic control problems of Heston model. For example, in
[
14
]
, Kraft et al. assumed that the speed of mean reversion in volatility divided by the bound of the admissible investment strategy is large enough in their optimal investment consumption problem under Heston model. Futhermore, Pu and Zhang
[
22
]
required that the admissible ambiguity parameters must take some special forms in the robust optimal investment consumption problem under Heston model with ambiguity. However, these conditions are completely weakened in our paper by means of the local estimate, the bootstrap method and the DPP, where the model is more generalized than those in
[
14
]
and
[
22
]
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In the next section, we present our stochastic control problem and set the notations and assumptions. Section 3 is devoted to establishing the existence and uniqueness of the value function as well as a verification theorem which shows the relationship between the stochastic control problem (SCP) and its associate HJBI equation. Then in Section 4, we discuss two applications in finance of our main results. Finally, we give the estimates for Heston model and the proof of Theorem
6
in Appendix A and Appendix B, respectively.

2.1
The state equation

The state
X=(X_{1},\cdot\cdot\cdot,X_{n})^{\rm T},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

(1)

Here
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
T\in(0,+\infty)
is fixed. The initial value

, where
{\cal X}
is an open domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with continuous boundary
\partial{\cal X}
.
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{v}
are control processes.
B
is an
m

(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
satisfying the usual conditions.

2.3
The payoff function

We consider the payoff function introduced by the following BSDE:

\displaystyle\hskip-22.76228ptY^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{s}=\int_{s}^{T\wedge\tau}f\left(r,X_{r}^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}},Y^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{r},Z^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{r},W\left(r,X_{r}^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}\right),\mathbf{u}_{r},\mathbf{v}_{r}\right)dr

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{s}^{T\wedge\tau}Z^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{j,r}\,dB^{j}_{r}+\Psi\left(T\wedge\tau,X_{T\wedge\tau}^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}\right),\quad 0\leq t\leq s\leq T\wedge\tau,

(2)

where
\tau\triangleq\inf\{s\geq t:X_{s}^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}\notin{\cal X}\}
and

W(t,x)=\sup\limits_{\mathbf{u}\in{\cal U}(t,x)}\inf\limits_{\mathbf{v}\in{\cal V}(t,x)}Y^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{t}.

(3)

Here
{\cal U}(t,x)
and
{\cal V}(t,x)
are the admissible sets which will be defined later. In BSDE (
2.3
), the generator
f:\overline{{\cal X}^{T}}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}\times{\cal U}\times{\cal V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

|f(t,x,y,z,w,u,v)-f(t,x,y^{\prime},z^{\prime},w^{\prime},u,v)|\leq K_{1}(|y-y^{\prime}|+|z-z^{\prime}|)+b_{2}(t,u,v)|w-w^{\prime}|,

for any
(t,x,u,v)\in\overline{{\cal X}^{T}}\times{\cal U}\times{\cal V},\ y,y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R},\ z,z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\ \mbox{and}\ w,w^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Assumption B2.
There exist a positive Borel-measurable function
b_{1}:\overline{{\cal X}^{T}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and a non-negative Borel-measurable function
b_{3}:[0,T]\times\mathcal{U}\times\mathcal{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

|f(t,x,0,0,0,u,v)|\leq b_{1}(t,x)b_{2}(t,u,v)+b_{3}(t,u,v),\;\forall\;(t,x,u,v)\in\overline{{\cal X}^{T}}\times{\cal U}\times{\cal V}.

Assumption B3.

\Psi\in C(\,\overline{{\cal X}^{T}}\,)
and there exists a non-negative Borel-measurable function
b_{4}:\overline{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

|\Psi(t,x)|\leq b_{4}(x),\;\;\forall\;(t,x)\in\overline{{\cal X}^{T}}.

The difference between SCP (

The main results

3.1
The existence and uniqueness of value function

In this subsection, we give a result on the existence and uniqueness of the value function for

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that Assumption B1, B2, B3 and A1, A2(1) and A3(1) with

hold. Then SCP (
3
) has a unique value function
W
in the space
{\cal B}_{(0,T)}
.

Remark 3
.

If the generator

is independent of

and

, i.e.,

Y^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{t}=\mathbb{E}\left[\Psi\left(T\wedge\tau,X_{T\wedge\tau}^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}\right)+\int_{t}^{T\wedge\tau}f\left(r,X_{r}^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}},W\left(r,X_{r}^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}\right),\mathbf{u}_{r},\mathbf{v}_{r}\right)\,dr\right].

Then the conclusion in Theorem

Proof.

Choose

W^{J}(t,x)=\sup\limits_{\mathbf{u}\in{\cal U}(t,x)}\inf\limits_{\mathbf{v}\in{\cal V}(t,x)}Y^{J;t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{t},\quad\forall\;(t,x)\in{\cal X}_{T-\epsilon}^{T}\,,

(5)

where

is determined by the following BSDE:

\displaystyle Y^{J;t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi\left(T\wedge\tau,X_{T\wedge\tau}^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}\right)+\int_{s}^{T\wedge\tau}f^{J}\left(r,X_{r}^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}},Y^{J;t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{r},Z^{J;t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{r},\mathbf{u}_{r},\mathbf{v}_{r}\right)\,dr

(6)

\displaystyle-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\int_{s}^{T\wedge\tau}Z^{J;t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{j,\,r}\,dB^{j}_{r},\qquad 0\leq t\leq s\leq T\wedge\tau,

\displaystyle\left\|f^{J}(\cdot,X^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}},0,0,\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(t,T)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(1+\|J\|_{{\cal B}_{(T-\epsilon,T)}}\right)\left\|b_{1}\left(\cdot,X^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}\right)b_{2}(\cdot,\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})+b_{3}(\cdot,\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v})\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(t,T)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K_{2}\left(1+\|J\|_{{\cal B}_{(T-\epsilon,T)}}\right)b_{1}(t,x)

\left\|\Psi\left(T\wedge\tau,X_{T\wedge\tau}^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{T\wedge\tau}}\leq\left\|b_{4}\left(X_{T\wedge\tau}^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}_{T\wedge\tau}}\leq\left\|b_{4}\left(X^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{S}^{2}(t,T)}\leq K_{2}b_{1}(t,x).

{\cal X}^{T}_{iT/N}
.
Moreover, repeating the same argument, we know that
W_{1}
is the unique value function in space
{\cal B}_{((i-1)T/N,iT/N)}
of Problem
A
, which provides
W_{1}=w
on
{\cal X}_{(i-1)T/N}^{iT/N}
. It is clear that
W_{2}
has the same properties. Therefore, we deduce
W_{1}=W_{2}=w
on
{\cal X}_{(i-1)T/N}^{T}
.
Still by Assumption A1 and the DPP for the standard SCP (
5
), we know
w(t,x)=Y_{t}^{w;t,x;\mathbf{u}^{*},\mathbf{v}^{*}}
on
{\cal X}_{(i-1)T/N}^{T}
, which means that
(\mathbf{u}^{*},\mathbf{v}^{*})
is the optimal control strategy on
{\cal X}_{(i-1)T/N}^{T}
.

Until now, we have proved the conclusion on
[(i-1)T/N,T]
. Using the bootstrap method, we can extend the conclusion to the whole interval
[0,T]
.
∎

Remark 5
.

From (
10
), we know that the following relation holds:

w(t,x)=\sup\limits_{\mathbf{u}\in{\cal U}(t,x)}\inf\limits_{\mathbf{v}\in{\cal V}(t,x)}Y^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{t}=\inf\limits_{\mathbf{v}\in{\cal V}(t,x)}\sup\limits_{\mathbf{u}\in{\cal U}(t,x)}Y^{t,x;\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}}_{t}.

4
Applications

In this section, we apply the above results to solve two typical examples in finance.

4.1
Example 1

This problem comes from
[
16
]
, in which the author gave heuristically the model and its associated HJBI equation rather than the accurate model and description. We will provide the precise model and the mathematical proofs.

Suppose that the price of the risk-free asset is governed by

S^{t,S_{0}}_{0,s}=S_{0}+\int_{t}^{s}\mu_{0}S^{t,S_{0}}_{0,r}\,dr,

(11)

where
\mu_{0}
is the constant risk-free interest rate. The price of the risky asset on the probability space
(\Omega,{\cal F},\mathbb{F},\mathbb{P})
is governed by

S^{t,S;\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{s}=S+\int_{t}^{s}(\mu_{1}+\sigma\boldsymbol{\phi}_{r})S^{t,S;\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{r}dr+\int_{t}^{s}\sigma S_{r}^{t,S;\boldsymbol{\phi}}dB_{r},

where
\mu_{1}
and
\sigma
are respectively constant reference expectation return rate and positive constant volatility rate. The ambiguity parameter
\boldsymbol{\phi}

is a progressively measurable process with respect to
\{{\cal F}_{s}^{t}\}_{t\leq s\leq T}
taking values in
\mathbb{R}
.

The investor invests in the risky asset according to the proportion
\boldsymbol{\pi}
of the wealth, consumes at the rate of
\boldsymbol{c}
and invests the remain wealth into the risk-free asset. So, the wealth
X
is expressed as following,

X_{s}^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}}=x+\int_{t}^{s}\Big\{\Big[\,\mu_{0}+(\mu_{1}-\mu_{0}+\sigma\boldsymbol{\phi}_{r})\boldsymbol{\pi}_{r}\,\Big]\,X_{r}^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}}-\boldsymbol{c}_{r}\,\Big\}\,dr+\int_{t}^{s}\sigma\boldsymbol{\pi}_{r}X_{r}^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}}dB_{r},

(12)

where
0\leq t\leq s\leq T
,
x\in(0,+\infty)
indicates the initial value,
\boldsymbol{\pi}
and
\boldsymbol{c}
are progressively measurable processes with respect to
\{{\cal F}_{s}^{t}\}_{t\leq s\leq T}
taking values in
\mathbb{R}
and
(0,+\infty)
, respectively.

The investor chooses the investment and consumption strategy
(\boldsymbol{\pi},\,\boldsymbol{c})
to maximize the robust expectation utility
\inf\{{\cal J}_{1}(t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}):\boldsymbol{\phi}\in{\Phi}_{1}(t,x)\}
with

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{1}(t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi})

\displaystyle\triangleq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg\{\,\int_{t}^{T}e^{-\delta(T-s)}\,\Big[\,{\boldsymbol{c}_{s}^{1-\gamma}\over 1-\gamma}+{(1-\gamma)\sigma^{2}\boldsymbol{\phi}_{s}^{2}\over 2\theta}\,W\left(t,X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)\,\Big]\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad+e^{-\delta(T-t)}{\left(X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{T}\right)^{1-\gamma}\over 1-\gamma}\,\bigg\},

where
\delta>0
is the constant discounted rate. The first term in the bracket represents the utility from the consumption. The constant
\gamma
is the relative risk aversion coefficient satisfying
\gamma>0
and
\gamma\neq 1
. The second term represents the entropy penalty, where the constant
\theta>0
measures the strength of the preference for robustness.
W
is defined as

W(t,x)=\sup\limits_{(\boldsymbol{\pi},\,\boldsymbol{c})\in{\Pi}_{1}(t,x)}\inf\limits_{\boldsymbol{\phi}\in{\Phi}_{1}(t,x)}{\cal J}_{1}(t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}).

(13)

In
[
16
]
, the author didn’t give the exact optimal control problem nor the admissible sets
{\Phi}_{1}(t,x)
and
{\Pi}_{1}(t,x)
. Now, we take the admissible sets as follows:

\displaystyle{\Phi}_{1}(t,x)

\displaystyle\!\!\!=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\{\boldsymbol{\phi}:|\boldsymbol{\phi}|\leq K_{4}\},

\displaystyle{\Pi}_{1}(t,x)

\displaystyle\!\!\!=\!\!\!

\displaystyle\{(\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c}):|\boldsymbol{\pi}|\leq K_{4},X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}}/K_{4}\leq\boldsymbol{c}\leq K_{4}X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}},X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}}\geq 0\},

with a large enough constant
K_{4}
.

Remark 6
.

Unlike in the classical case, we introduce the constant
K_{4}
and suppose it is large enough in order to ensure BSDE (
2.3
) admits
\mathbb{L}^{2}-
solution. Moreover, the optimal strategy falls in this admissible set.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that there exist functions
w\in C^{1,2}([0,T]\times(0,+\infty))
and
\pi^{*},c^{*},\phi^{*}\in C^{0,1}([0,T]\times[0,+\infty))
, which satisfy the following PDEs:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-{\cal L}_{1}^{\pi^{*}(t,x),c^{*}(t,x),\phi^{*}(t,x)}w(t,x)=f_{1}(w(t,x),c^{*}(t,x),\phi^{*}(t,x))\,;\\
-{\cal L}_{1}^{\pi,c,\phi^{*}(t,x)}w(t,x)\geq f_{1}(w(t,x),c,\phi^{*}(t,x)),\;\;\forall\;|\pi|\leq K_{4},\,x/K_{4}\leq c\leq K_{4}x\,;\\
-{\cal L}_{1}^{\pi^{*}(t,x),c^{*}(t,x),\phi}w(t,x)\leq f_{1}(w(t,x),c^{*}(t,x),\phi),\;\;\forall\;|\phi|\leq K_{4}\,;\\
w(T,x)=x^{1-\gamma}/(1-\gamma)\,,\end{array}\right.

where the function
f_{1}
is defined as

f_{1}(w,c,\phi)\triangleq-\delta w+{c^{1-\gamma}\over 1-\gamma}+{(1-\gamma)\sigma^{2}\phi^{2}\over 2\theta}\,w

and the differential operator
{\cal L}_{1}^{\pi,c,\phi}
is defined as

{\cal L}_{1}^{\pi,c,\phi}w\triangleq\partial_{t}w+{1\over 2}\sigma^{2}\pi^{2}x^{2}\partial_{xx}w+\Big\{\,\Big[\,\mu_{0}+(\mu_{1}-\mu_{0}+\sigma\phi)\pi\,\Big]\,x-c\,\Big\}\partial_{x}w.

Moreover, assume that
w
and
\pi^{*},c^{*},\phi^{*}
satisfy the following conditions:

1.

there exists a constant
K_{5}
such that

|w|\leq K_{5}x^{1-\gamma}
on
[0,T]\times(0,+\infty)
;

2.

|\pi^{*}|\leq K_{4}
,
|\phi^{*}|\leq K_{4}
and
x/K_{4}\leq c^{*}\leq K_{4}x
on
[0,T]\times(0,+\infty)
.

Then
W=w
is the unique value function in
{\cal B}_{(0,T)}
of SCP (
13
) with
b_{1}(t,x)=x^{1-\gamma}
, and
(\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*},\boldsymbol{c}^{*},\boldsymbol{\phi}^{*})\triangleq(\pi^{*},c^{*},\phi^{*})(\cdot,X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*},\boldsymbol{c}^{*},\boldsymbol{\phi}^{*}})
is the optimal control strategy.

Proof.

It is clear that SCP (
13
) can be described as the form of SCP (
3
) with

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}=(\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c}),\;\;\mathbf{v}=\boldsymbol{\phi},\;\;{\cal X}=(0,+\infty),\;\;{\cal U}=[\,-K_{4},K_{4}\,]\times(0,+\infty),\;\;{\cal V}=[\,-K_{4},K_{4}\,],

\displaystyle\alpha(t,x,\pi,c,\phi)=\Big[\,\mu_{0}+(\mu_{1}-\mu_{0}+\sigma\phi)\pi\,\Big]\,x-c,\quad\beta(t,x,\pi,c,\phi)=\sigma\pi x,

\displaystyle\Psi={x^{1-\gamma}\over 1-\gamma},\;\;f(t,x,y,z,w,\pi,c,\phi)=-\delta y+{c^{1-\gamma}\over 1-\gamma}+{(1-\gamma)\sigma^{2}\phi^{2}\over 2\theta}\,w.

It is not difficult to verify that Assumption B1-B3 are satisfied with

K_{1}=\delta,\quad b_{2}(\pi,\phi)={|1-\gamma|\sigma^{2}\phi^{2}\over 2\theta},\quad b_{3}(\pi,c,\phi)={c^{1-\gamma}\over|1-\gamma|},\quad b_{4}(x)=K_{5}x^{1-\gamma}.

For any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times(0,+\infty)
,
(\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c})\in\Pi_{1}(t,x)
and
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in\Phi_{1}(t,x)
, one can easily check that the coefficients of SDE (
12
) are Lipschitz continuous and linear growth with respect to
x
. Then, SDE (
12
) has a unique strong solution
X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}}
(refer to P82 in
[
9
]
). Moreover, it is not difficult to check that the admissible sets
\Pi_{1}(t,x)
and
\Phi_{1}(t,x)
satisfy the “switching condition”. So, Assumption A1 is satisfied.

A simple calculation yields that
\widetilde{X}=(X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}})^{1-\gamma}
satisfies
\widetilde{X}_{t}=x^{1-\gamma}
and

d\widetilde{X}_{s}=(1-\gamma)\left[\,\mu_{0}+(\mu_{1}-\mu_{0}+\sigma\boldsymbol{\phi}_{s})\boldsymbol{\pi}_{s}-{\boldsymbol{c}_{s}\over X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{s}}-{\gamma\over 2}\sigma^{2}\boldsymbol{\pi}^{2}_{s}\,\right]\,\widetilde{X}_{s}\,ds+(1-\gamma)\sigma\boldsymbol{\pi}_{s}\widetilde{X}_{s}dB_{s}.

Applying the theory for SDEs (refer to P86 in
[
9
]
), we get the following estimate:

\left\|\left(X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\right\|_{\mathbb{S}^{2}(t,T)}=\left\|\widetilde{X}\right\|_{\mathbb{S}^{2}(t,T)}\leq Cx^{1-\gamma},

where the constant
C
is independent of
t,x,\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}
. Then, with the expressions of
b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b_{4}
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|b_{1}\left(X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)b_{2}(\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi})\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(t,T)}+\|b_{3}(\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi})\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(t,T)}+\left\|b_{4}\left(X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{S}^{2}(t,T)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left(\left\|\left(X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(t,T)}+\left\|\boldsymbol{c}^{1-\gamma}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(t,T)}+\left\|\left(X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)^{1-\gamma}\right\|_{\mathbb{S}^{2}(t,T)}\right)\leq\widetilde{C}x^{1-\gamma},

where the constant
\widetilde{C}
is also independent of
t,x,\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}
. Hence, we have testified Assumption A2(1).

Moreover, it is not difficult to deduce

\left\|b_{1}\left(X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)b_{2}(\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi})\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}(t,t+\epsilon)}\leq\left\|b_{1}\left(X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}}\right)b_{2}(\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi})\right\|_{\mathbb{S}^{2}(t,T)})\sqrt{\epsilon}\leq\widetilde{C}\sqrt{\epsilon}x^{1-\gamma},

where the constant
\widetilde{C}
is independent of
t,x,\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}
. Until now, we have verified all conditions in Theorem 1. Therefore, we conclude that SCP (
13
) has a unique value function
W
in space
{\cal B}_{(0,T)}
.

Noting that
|w(t,x)|\leq\min(K_{5}b_{1}(t,x),b_{4}(x))
for any
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times(0,+\infty)
and

f^{w}(t,x,w(t,x),\beta(t,x,u,v)\partial_{x}w(t,x),u,v)=f_{1}(w(t,x),c,\phi),

we can achieve the desired result by means of Theorem 2 if we can prove Condition 2 in Theorem 2.

For any
\boldsymbol{\phi}\in\Phi_{1}(t,x)
, the control process is
(\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{c}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{\phi})
with
\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}}=\pi^{*}(\cdot,X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{c}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{\phi}})
and
\boldsymbol{c}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}}=c^{*}(\cdot,X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{c}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{\phi}})
, then SDE (
12
) becomes

\displaystyle X_{s}^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{c}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{\phi}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x+\int_{t}^{s}\widetilde{\alpha}_{r}\left(X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{c}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{r}\right)\,dr+\int_{t}^{s}\widetilde{\beta}_{r}\left(X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{c}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{\phi}}_{r}\right)dB_{r},

with the coefficients

\widetilde{\alpha}_{r}(x)=\Big\{\Big[\,\mu_{0}+(\mu_{1}-\mu_{0}+\sigma\boldsymbol{\phi}_{r})\pi^{*}(r,x)\,\Big]\,x-c^{*}(r,x)\,\Big\}\quad{\rm and}\quad\widetilde{\beta}_{r}(x)=\sigma\pi^{*}(r,x)\,.

Since
\pi^{*},c^{*}\in C^{0,1}([0,T]\times(0,+\infty))
,
|\pi^{*}(t,x)|\leq K_{4}
and
x/K_{4}\leq c^{*}(t,x)\leq K_{4}x
,
\forall(t,x)\in[0,T]\times(0,+\infty)
and
\forall t\in[0,T]
,
|\boldsymbol{\phi}_{t}|\leq K_{4}
a.s., we know that
\widetilde{\alpha}_{r}(x)
and
\widetilde{\beta}_{r}(x)
are locally Lipschitz continuous with respect to
x
, i.e.,
\forall n\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, there exists a constant
C_{n}
such that

|\widetilde{\alpha}_{r}(x)-\widetilde{\alpha}_{r}(x^{\prime})|+|\widetilde{\beta}_{r}(x)-\widetilde{\beta}_{r}(x^{\prime})|\leq C_{n}|x-x^{\prime}|,\ \mbox{a.s.},\;\forall\;x,x^{\prime}\in[0,n],r\in[0,T].

Also,
\widetilde{\alpha}_{r}(x)
and
\widetilde{\beta}_{r}(x)
are linear growth with respect to
x
, i.e., there exists a constant
C
such that

|\widetilde{\alpha}_{r}(x)|+|\widetilde{\beta}_{r}(x)|\leq C(1+|x|),\ \mbox{a.s.},\;\forall\;(r,x)\in[0,T]\times(0,+\infty).

By the theory for SDEs (refer to P57 in
[
17
]
), we deduce that SDE (
12
) has a unique strong solution
X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{c}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{\phi}}
. Moreover, Condition 2 in Theorem 2 indicates
X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{c}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{\phi}}/K_{4}\leq\boldsymbol{c}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}}\leq K_{4}X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{c}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{\phi}}
and
|\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}}|\leq K_{4}
. That is,
(\boldsymbol{\pi}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}},\boldsymbol{c}^{*;\boldsymbol{\phi}})\in\Pi_{1}(t,x)
.

Repeating the similar argument, we can prove that for any
(\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c})\in\Pi_{1}(t,x)
, SDE (
12
) has a unique solution if the control process is
(\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}^{*;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c}})
and
\boldsymbol{\phi}^{*;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c}}\in\Phi_{1}(t,x)
with
\boldsymbol{\phi}^{*;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c}}=\phi^{*}(\cdot,X^{t,x;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c},\boldsymbol{\phi}^{*;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{c}}})
.

Hence, we have testified all assumptions in Theorem 2, and the desired result is obvious.
∎

Example 2

, in which the authors neither proved the rationality of the stochastic control problem nor gave the explicit forms of the value function and the optimal strategy, they only guessed the expressions of the value function and the optimal strategy. Suppose that the price of the risk-free asset is the same as in (

.

\displaystyle X_{s}^{t,x,p;\boldsymbol{\pi},\boldsymbol{\phi}}

Then the stochastic control problem is described as

Theorem 5
.

Suppose that there exist

dependent only on

satisfying the following PDEs:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-{\cal L}_{2}^{\pi^{*}(t,p),\phi^{*}(t,p)}w(t,x,p)=f_{2}(w(t,x,p),\phi^{*}(t,p));\\

-{\cal L}_{2}^{\pi^{*}(t,p),\phi}w(t,x,p)\leq f_{2}(w(t,x,p),\phi),\;\;\forall\;|\phi|\leq K_{\phi}\sqrt{p};\\
w(T,x,p)={x^{1-\gamma}\over 1-\gamma},\end{array}\right.

(18)

where the function
f_{2}
is defined as

f_{2}(w,\phi)\triangleq{|\phi|^{2}\over 2\theta}(1-\gamma)w,

and the differential operator
{\cal L}_{2}^{\pi,\phi}
is defined as

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{2}^{\pi,\phi}w

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\triangleq\!\!\!\!

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w+{1\over 2}\pi^{2}x^{2}p\partial_{xx}w+\sigma\rho\pi xp\partial_{xp}w+{1\over 2}\sigma^{2}p\partial_{pp}w+\Big[\,\mu_{0}+\Big(\mu_{2}p+\phi_{1}\sqrt{p}\Big)\pi\,\Big]\,x\partial_{x}w

\displaystyle+\Big[\kappa\Big(\overline{P}-p\Big)+\Big(\rho\phi_{1}+\sqrt{1-\rho^{2}}\phi_{2}\Big)\sigma\sqrt{p}\,\Big]\,\partial_{p}w.

Assume
2\kappa\overline{P}\geq\sigma^{2}
, and
w,\pi^{*},\phi^{*}
satisfy the following conditions:

1.

there exists a constant
K_{6}
such that
K_{6}\geq 1
and
|w|\leq K_{6}x^{1-\gamma}e^{K_{6}p}
on
[0,T]\times(0,+\infty)^{2}
;

2.

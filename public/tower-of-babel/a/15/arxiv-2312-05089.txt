Analyticity and supershift with irregular sampling
F. Colombo, I. Sabadini, D. C. Struppa, A. Yger

Abstract
. The notion of supershift generalizes that one of superoscillation and expresses the fact that the sampling of a function in an interval allows to compute the values of the function outside the interval. In a previous paper we discussed the case in which the sampling of the function is regular and we are considering supershift in a bounded set, while here we investigate how irregularity in the sampling may affect the answer to the question of whether there is any relation between supershift and real analyticity on the whole real line. We show that the restriction to ℝ \mathbb{R} of any entire function displays supershift, whereas the converse is, in general, not true. We conjecture that the converse is true as long as the sampling is regular, we discuss examples in support and we prove that the conjecture is indeed true for periodic functions.



Keywords
. Real analyticity; supershift; superoscillation; sampling.

AMS classification
. 42C10, 26E05

1.
Introduction and preliminary definitions

The notion of supershift (in itself a generalization of the notion of superoscillation arising in quantum mechanics) expresses the fact that the sampling of a function in an interval allows to compute the values of the function far from the interval.
In a recent paper, see
[
17
]
, we studied the relation between supershift and real analyticity.
In particular, we used a classical result due to Serge Bernstein to
show that real analyticity for a complex valued function implies a strong form of supershift. On the other hand, we used a
parametric version of a result by
Leonid Kantorovitch to show that the converse is not true in the sense that we can construct an example of a smooth function that exhibit supershift on a bounded set and yet is not real analytic on that same set.

In
[
17
]
we restricted our attention to what we called a regular sampling. In this paper, instead, we study the more general case where the sampling points can be distributed in an interval with no regularity, but we consider the more difficult situation in which supershift occurs on the whole real line.

The first class of results, see
Section
2
, shows the superoscillatory phenomenon arises even in the case of irregular samplings in a way generalizing the standard one. The most general configuration of sampling points is treated via Lagrange-Hermite polynomials, see Theorem
2.6
and Theorem
2.7
, even though a well-known example of Erdös and Vértesi shows that such polynomials fail to approximate continuous functions almost everywhere (and in particular it is always possible to find continuous functions for which the supershift fails even for regular samplings).
This leads to the fact that the restriction to
\mathbb{R}
of any entire function displays supershift phenomena, see Theorem
2.4
which gives, using Proposition
2.3
and Theorem
2.6
respectively, Example
2.5
and Theorem
2.7
.

This leads us naturally to
Section
3
where we obtain some results that suggest the following conjecture (Conjecture
3.4
):
is any regular
\mathbb{C}
-supershift on
\mathbb{R}
(in the sense of Definition
1.8
) necessarily the restriction of an entire function
?
Proposition
2.9
and Remark
2.10
, which are inherited from Kantorovich’s example, suggest that the regularity hypothesis on the sampling is natural for our conjecture to hold.

More precisely, Proposition
2.9
shows that slightly irregular samplings on
[-1,1]
allow the construction of non real analytic functions on
\mathbb{R}
which can be extrapolated, uniformly on any compact set, from their sample values on
[-1,1]
.

As a support for Conjecture
3.4
, we prove it for periodic regular
\mathbb{C}
-supershifts on

\mathbb{R}
, see Theorem
3.6
.

The

2.
Superoscillating sequences attached to irregular sampling in
[-1,1]

In this section we consider superoscillating sequences with irregular sampling and we will prove their convergence in the space
{\rm Exp}(\mathbb{C})
.

Definition 2.1
.

We consider a collection of elements in
[0,1]
:

\boldsymbol{\Xi}=\{\xi_{N,k}\,:\,N\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\ 1\leq k\leq N\}

Let
A_{N,k}
be the set consisting of the two opposite points
\pm\frac{1}{N}\Big(1-\frac{\xi_{N,k}}{N}\Big)

and consider the Minkowski set addition

A_{N,1}+\ldots+A_{N,N}

that consists of at most
2^{N}
distinct points, all of them contained in the interval
[-1,1]
.
After reordering them in decreasing order, and using the Minkowski set addition, we define

\{h_{N,\nu}^{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}\,:\,\nu=0,...,\nu(N)\leq 2^{N}\}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}A_{N,k}.

The sampling defined by this set
is said
almost regular
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that the sampling of the domain
[-1,1]
defined by the matrix
H
in (
1.1
) is
irregular
if
\forall\,N\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\ \nu(N)=N,
and infinitely many rows
H_{N_{\iota}}
of

H
are such that
\nu\in\{0,...,N_{\iota}-1\}\mapsto h_{N_{\iota},\nu}-h_{N_{\iota},\nu+1}>0
is not constant.

To generalize
the
(\mathbb{R},H^{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}})
-sequence
\{T_{N}^{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}[\lambda]\,:\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}_{N\geq 1}
we consider a collection
\boldsymbol{\Xi}
of elements in
[0,1]
corresponding to the almost regular sampling above,
and

\{h_{N,\nu}^{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}\,:\,\nu=0,...,\nu(N)\}.

The
(\mathbb{R},H^{\boldsymbol{\Xi}})
-sequence
\{T_{N}^{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}[\lambda]\,:\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}_{N\geq 1}
, where

(2.1)

T_{N}^{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}[\lambda]\,(x)=\prod_{k=1}^{N}\Big(\frac{1+\lambda}{2}\,\exp\Big(\frac{ix}{N}\Big(1-\frac{\xi_{N,k}}{N}\Big)\Big)+\frac{1-\lambda}{2}\,\exp\Big(-\frac{ix}{N}\Big(1-\frac{\xi_{N,k}}{N}\Big)\Big)\Big)

is superoscillating (with
g_{H^{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}}(\lambda)=\lambda
and
C_{H^{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}}(\lambda)\equiv 1
) on
U=\mathbb{R}
.

When the matrix
H
in (
1.1
) is such that

\forall\,N\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\ \nu(N)=N,

the sequence of Lagrange-Hermite interpolators (with respect to the parameter
\lambda
)

(2.2)

\Big\{x\longmapsto T_{H,N}^{\rm Lag}[\lambda](x):=\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\Big(\prod_{\nu^{\prime}\not=\nu}\frac{\lambda-h_{N,\nu^{\prime}}}{h_{N,\nu}-h_{N,\nu^{\prime}}}\Big)\,e^{ih_{N,\nu}x}\,:\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\Big\}_{N\geq 1}

is also superoscillating with respect to
g:\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\lambda
and
C:\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\rightarrow 1
since

(2.3)

\begin{split}\Big|e^{i\lambda x}-T_{H,N}^{\rm Lag}[\lambda](x)\Big|\leq\frac{\prod_{\nu=0}^{N}|\lambda-h_{N,\nu}|}{(N+1)!}\sup_{|\xi|\leq\max(1,|\lambda|)}\Big|\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\Big)^{N+1}[e^{i\lambda x}](\xi)\Big|\leq\frac{\big((|\lambda|+1)|x|\big)^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}\end{split}

for any
(\lambda,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
according to the expression of the reminder term in Lagrange interpolation formula, see
[
2
, Theorem 2.2]
. Such superoscillating sequence
\{T_{H,N}^{\rm Lag}[\lambda]\,:\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}_{N\geq 1}

may correspond to
irregular sampling
of the low-frequency domain
[-1,1]
.

Using the sequence (
2.2
) in which we use
z\in\mathbb{C}
instead of the real variable

, we have:

Proposition 2.3
.

For any almost regular sampling

\boldsymbol{\Xi}

as above and any
a\in\mathbb{R}
, the sequence of entire functions with exponential growth

(2.4)

\Big(z\longmapsto T_{N}^{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}[a]\,(z)\Big)_{N\geq 1}

converges in
{\rm Exp}(\mathbb{C})
towards
z\longmapsto e^{iaz}
. Moreover, there exists a compact
K_{\mathbb{R}}
in
\mathbb{R}
such that the convergence is uniform
with respect both to
a\in K_{\mathbb{R}}
and to the collection
\boldsymbol{\Xi}
. As a consequence the
(\mathbb{R},H^{\boldsymbol{\Xi}})
-sequence
\{T_{N}^{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}[a]\,:\,a\in\mathbb{R}\}_{N\geq 1}
is superoscillating.

Proof.

We follow here the method that we previously used in
[
16
, Lemma 4.1]
.
For each
a\in\mathbb{R}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
,
\xi\in[0,1]
, one has

\begin{split}\Big|\frac{1+a}{2}\,\exp\Big(\frac{iz}{N}\Big(1-\frac{\xi}{N}\Big)\Big)&+\frac{1-a}{2}\,\exp\Big(-\frac{iz}{N}\Big(1-\frac{\xi}{N}\Big)\Big)\Big|\\
&=\Big|\cos\Big(\frac{z}{N}\Big(1-\frac{\xi}{N}\Big)\Big)+ia\,\sin\Big(\frac{z}{N}\Big(1-\frac{\xi}{N}\Big)\Big)\Big|\\
&=\Big|\cos\Big(\frac{z}{N}\Big(1-\frac{\xi}{N}\Big)\Big)+ia\frac{z}{N}\Big(1-\frac{\xi}{N}\Big)\,{\rm sinc}\,\Big(\frac{z}{N}\Big(1-\frac{\xi}{N}\Big)\Big)\Big|\\
&\leq\Big(1+\frac{|a|\,|z|}{N}\Big)\,\exp\Big(\frac{|{\rm Im}\,z|}{N}\Big(1-\frac{\xi}{N}\Big)\Big)\\
&\leq\Big(1+\frac{|a|\,|z|}{N}\Big)\,\exp\Big(\frac{|{\rm Im}\,z|}{N}\Big)\end{split}

since the entire function sinus cardinal
z\mapsto{\rm sinc}\,(z)=\sin z/z
satisfies
in the whole complex plane the upper estimate
|{\rm sinc}\,z|\leq\exp(|{\rm Im}\,z|)
in
\mathbb{C}
.
As a consequence, one has

|T_{N}^{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}[a]\,(z)|=\Big|\prod_{k=1}^{N}\Big(\frac{1+a}{2}\,\exp\Big(\frac{iz}{N}\Big(1-\frac{\xi_{N,k}}{N}\Big)\Big)+\frac{1-a}{2}\,\exp\Big(-\frac{iz}{N}\Big(1-\frac{\xi_{N,k}}{N}\Big)\Big)\Big)\Big|\\
\leq\Big(1+\frac{|a|\,|z|}{N}\Big)^{N}\,\exp(|{\rm Im}\,z|)\leq\exp\big((|a|+1)\,|z|\big),

which shows that the the family of entire functions

\Big\{z\longmapsto T_{N}^{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}[a]\,(z)\,:\,a\in K_{\mathbb{R}},\ \boldsymbol{\Xi}\Big\},

where
K_{\mathbb{R}}
is any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}
, is a bounded subset of
{\rm Exp}(\mathbb{C})
.
It remains to prove that, given a compact subset
K_{\mathbb{C}}
of the complex plane, the sequence of entire functions (
2.4
) converges uniformly on
K_{\mathbb{C}}
towards

z\mapsto e^{az}
, the convergence being uniform both with respect to
a\in K_{\mathbb{R}}
and to the collection
\boldsymbol{\Xi}
. For any
w
sufficiently close to
0
in
\mathbb{C}
, one has

(2.5)

\begin{split}\cos w+ia\,\sin w&=1+iaw-\frac{w^{2}}{2}+(|a|+1)\,O(|w|^{3})\\
\log(\cos w+ia\,\sin w)&=\log\Big(1+iaw-\frac{w^{2}}{2}\Big)+(|a|+1)\,O(|w|^{3})\\
&=ia\,w+\frac{a^{2}-1}{2}\,w^{2}(1-O(|aw|)).\end{split}

Then, for
N
large enough (depending on
K_{\mathbb{C}}
and of the compact
K_{\mathbb{R}}
), one has

\begin{split}T_{N}^{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}[a]\,(z)=\exp\bigg(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N}\Big(ia\,w_{N,k}+\frac{a^{2}-1}{2}\,w_{N,k}^{2}\Big(1-O\big(|aw_{N,k}|\big)\Big)\Big)\bigg),\end{split}

where
w_{N,k}=(z/N)\times(1-\xi_{N,k}/N)
for
k=1,...,N
. Therefore

T_{N}^{\boldsymbol{\Xi}}[a]\,(z)=\exp(iaz)\,\exp\Big(-iaz\,\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\xi_{N,k}}{N^{2}}+\frac{a^{2}-1}{2}\,\frac{z^{2}}{N^{2}}\,\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big(1-\frac{\xi_{N,k}}{N}\Big)^{2}\Big(1-O\big(|aw_{N,k}|\big)\Big)\Big).

The uniform convergence of this sequence of functions of
z
on
K_{\mathbb{C}}
towards

z\mapsto e^{iaz}
follows immediately. Moreover the convergence is uniform with respect to

a\in K_{\mathbb{R}}
and to
\boldsymbol{\Xi}
, and this concludes the proof.

subordinate to the approximation of

in

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\{T_{H,N}[\lambda]\,:\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}_{N\geq 1}
be a
(\mathbb{R},H)
sequence of generalized trigonometric polynomials such that, for any
a\in\mathbb{R}
, the sequence of entire functions

\Big(z\in\mathbb{C}\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}C_{H,\nu}(N,a)e^{ih_{N,\nu}z}\Big)_{N\geq 1}

converges towards
z\longmapsto e^{iaz}
in
{\rm Exp}(\mathbb{C})
, the convergence being locally uniform with respect to
a\in\mathbb{R}
. Then any restriction
\psi=F_{|\mathbb{R}}
of an entire function
F
satisfies the

({\rm TCSP})_{\mathbb{C}}
-property with respect to the superoscillating sequence
\{T_{H,N}[\lambda]\,:\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}_{N\geq 1}
. In other terms, the sequence of continuous functions

\Big((a,a^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}C_{H,\nu}(N,a)\psi(a^{\prime}+h_{N,\nu})\Big)_{N\geq 1}

converges towards
(a,a^{\prime})\longmapsto\psi(a+a^{\prime})
in
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{C})
locally uniformly over the compact sets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Proof.

Let
F
be an entire function and
F(-id/dz+a^{\prime})
the infinite order differential operator

F\Big(-i\frac{d}{dz}+a^{\prime}\Big)=\sum\limits_{\kappa=0}^{\infty}\frac{F^{(\kappa)}(0)}{\kappa!}\Big(-i\frac{d}{dz}+a^{\prime}\Big)\circ\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\kappa\ {\rm times}}}{{\cdots}}\circ\Big(-i\frac{d}{dz}+a^{\prime}\Big)=\sum\limits_{\kappa=0}^{\infty}\frac{F^{(\kappa)}(0)}{\kappa!}\Big(-i\frac{d}{dz}+a^{\prime}\Big)^{\kappa}

where
a^{\prime}
denotes a real parameter. Such an operator acts from
{\rm Exp}(\mathbb{C})
to itself as follows.
Entire functions with exponential growth are in bijective correspondence with analytic functionals through the Fourier-Borel transform
T\longleftrightarrow\big(\widehat{T}:z\longmapsto\langle T(\zeta)\,,\,e^{\zeta z}\rangle\big)
.
If
T\in H^{\prime}(\mathbb{C})
is an analytic functional, then the action of the infinite order differential operator

F(-id/dz+a^{\prime})
on the Fourier-Borel transform of
T
is given by

(2.6)

F\Big(-\frac{d}{dz}+a^{\prime}\Big)\Big(z\longmapsto\big\langle T(\zeta)\,,\,e^{\zeta z}\big\rangle\Big)=\Big(z\longmapsto\Big\langle T(\zeta)\,,\,F(-i\zeta+a^{\prime})\,e^{\zeta z}\Big\rangle\Big).

Such action is a continuous action from
{\rm Exp}(\mathbb{C})
to
{\rm Exp}(\mathbb{C})
, locally uniformly with respect to the parameter
a^{\prime}
, see
[
8
]
and
[
3
, Proposition 2.10]
(restricted here to the univariate setting). Since for any
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, one has that

F\Big(-i\frac{d}{dz}+a^{\prime}\Big)\Big(z\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}C_{H,\nu}(N,a)e^{ih_{N,\nu}z}\Big)=\Big(z\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}C_{H,\nu}(N,a)F(a^{\prime}+h_{N,\nu})\,e^{ih_{N,\nu}z}\Big),

it follows from the hypothesis on
\{T_{H,N}[\lambda]\,:\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\}_{N\geq 1}
that the sequence of functions

\Big(z\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}C_{H,\nu}(N,a)F(a^{\prime}+h_{N,\nu})\,e^{ih_{N,\nu}z}\Big)_{N\geq 1}

converges in
{\rm Exp}(\mathbb{C})
towards

z\longmapsto F\Big(-\frac{d}{dz}+a^{\prime}\Big)\Big(z\longmapsto e^{iaz}\Big)=F(a+a^{\prime})\,e^{iaz},

locally uniformly with respect to
(a,a^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Such convergence is in particular uniform on the compact subset
\{z=0\}
, locally uniformly with respect to the parameters
(a,a^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Theorem 2.6

The sequence of entire functions depending on the real parameter

(2.7)

\Big(z\longmapsto T_{H,N}^{{\rm Lag}}[a](z):=\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\Big(\prod_{\nu^{\prime}\not=\nu}\frac{a-h_{N,\nu^{\prime}}}{h_{N,\nu}-h_{N,\nu^{\prime}}}\Big)\,e^{ih_{N,\nu}z}\Big)_{N\geq 1}

Proof.

Let

\begin{split}\Big|e^{iaz}-\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\Big(\prod_{\nu^{\prime}\not=\nu}\frac{a-h_{N,\nu^{\prime}}}{h_{N,\nu}-h_{N,\nu^{\prime}}}\Big)\,e^{ih_{N,\nu}z}\Big|&=\Big|\frac{1}{2i\pi}\int_{\Gamma_{a}}\Big(\prod_{\nu=0}^{N}\frac{a-h_{N,\nu}}{\zeta-h_{N,\nu}}\Big)\,\frac{e^{i\zeta z}}{\zeta-a}\,d\zeta\Big|\\

towards

Proposition
3.3
, combined with Remark
3.2
, suggest naturally the following conjecture, where the regularity of the sampling has a crucial role.

Conjecture 3.4
.

A
\mathbb{C}
-valued continuous function
\psi
on the real line is a
\mathbb{C}
-regular supershift if and only if
\psi
is the restriction to the real line of an entire function.

Remark 3.5
.

If true, such conjecture would imply that for some class of potentials
x\in U\longmapsto V(x)
(for example
U=\mathbb{R}
and
V
smooth, real,
2\pi
-periodic and non-constant), the evolution
a\mapsto\psi_{a}(t,x)
through the Cauchy problem for the Schrödinger equation would be such that, for some

\varphi\in\mathcal{D}((0,T)\times U,\mathbb{C})
, the continuous map
a\mapsto\langle\psi_{a}(t,x),\varphi(t,x)\rangle
fails to inherit either Property (1) or (2) in Definition
1.8
.
Such a result would be of great interest with respect to quantum studies considerations.

We present first a positive result within the quite specific setting of continuous periodic functions on the real line.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
T>0
and
\psi
be a
T
-periodic continuous regular
\mathbb{C}
-supershift on
\mathbb{R}
. Then, there is a
T
-periodic entire function
F\penalty\ :\mathbb{C}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
such that
\psi=F_{|\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

Let

\psi(a)=\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}}\gamma_{\kappa}\exp\Big(\frac{2i\pi\kappa}{T}\,a\Big)

be the Fourier expansion of the
T
-periodic continuous function
\psi
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}/T\mathbb{Z})
. Here the spectrum
\widehat{\psi}=(\gamma_{\kappa})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
belongs to
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then the
T
-periodic continuous function

\widetilde{\psi}:a\longmapsto\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\psi(a-\tau)\,\psi(\tau)\,d\tau=\sum\limits_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}}\gamma_{\kappa}^{2}\,\exp\Big(\frac{2i\pi\kappa}{T}\,a\Big)

remains a
T
-periodic continuous
\mathbb{C}
-supershift on
\mathbb{R}
(with spectrum

(c_{\kappa}^{2})_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
)
since one has for any

\boldsymbol{\epsilon}\in[0,1[^{\mathbb{N}^{*}}
such that
\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\epsilon_{N}=0
, for any

(a,a^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and any
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, that

\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}\Big(\frac{1+a}{2}\Big)^{N-\nu}\Big(\frac{1-a}{2}\Big)^{\nu}\widetilde{\psi}\bigg(a^{\prime}+\Big(1-2\Big(\frac{\nu+\epsilon_{N}(N-\nu)}{N}\Big)\Big)\bigg)\\
=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\Bigg(\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}\Big(\frac{1+a}{2}\Big)^{N-\nu}\Big(\frac{1-a}{2}\Big)^{\nu}\psi\bigg(a^{\prime}-\tau+\Big(1-2\Big(\frac{\nu+\epsilon_{N}(N-\nu)}{N}\Big)\Big)\bigg)\Bigg)\,d\tau.

Since
(a,a^{\prime}-\tau)
remains in the compact subset
K-\{0\}\times[0,T]
when

(a,a^{\prime})\in K
and
\tau\in[0,T]
, one needs just to use the fact that, according to the hypothesis on

\psi
, for each family
\{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{\iota}\,:\,\iota\in I^{\prime}\}
such that
\lim_{N\rightarrow+\infty}\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}=0
,
the sequence of functions

\Bigg((\tau,a,a^{\prime})\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}\Big(\frac{1+a}{2}\Big)^{N-\nu}\Big(\frac{1-a}{2}\Big)^{\nu}\psi\bigg(a^{\prime}-\tau+\Big(1-2\Big(\frac{\nu+\epsilon_{\iota^{\prime},N}(N-\nu)}{N}\Big)\Big)\bigg)\Bigg)_{N\geq 1}

converges uniformly on

is any compact subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}

(\tau,a,a^{\prime})\longmapsto\psi(a+a^{\prime}-\tau)

to the index
\iota^{\prime}
. Since we know now that
\widetilde{\psi}
is a continuous
\mathbb{C}
-regular supershift, it follows that for any
R\in\mathbb{R}_{>1}

\Big(a^{\prime}\in[0,2\pi]\longmapsto\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}\Big(\frac{1+R}{2}\Big)^{N-\nu}\Big(\frac{1-R}{2}\Big)^{\nu}\widetilde{\psi}\Big(a^{\prime}+\Big(1-\frac{2\nu}{N}\Big)\Big)\Big)_{N\geq 1}

[0,2\pi]

\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}\Big(\frac{1+R}{2}\Big)^{N-\nu}\Big(\frac{1-R}{2}\Big)^{\nu}\widetilde{\psi}\Big(a^{\prime}+\Big(1-\frac{2\nu}{N}\Big)\Big)\\
=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}}\gamma_{\kappa}^{2}\bigg(\sum\limits_{\nu=0}^{N}\binom{N}{\nu}\Big(\frac{1+R}{2}\Big)^{N-\nu}\Big(\frac{1-R}{2}\Big)^{\nu}\exp\Big(i\kappa\Big(1-\frac{2\nu}{N}\Big)\frac{2\pi}{T}\Big)\bigg)\,\exp\Big(\frac{2i\pi\kappa}{T}\,a^{\prime}\Big)\\

|w|\leq\frac{1}{M}\,\frac{2\pi}{T},\quad|(iRw-w^{2}/2)|\leq|w|\Big(R+\frac{|w|}{2}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{M}\frac{2\pi}{T}\Big(R+\frac{\pi}{T}\Big)

\begin{split}\log\big(\cos w+iR\,\sin w\big)&=\log\bigg(1+iR\ w-\frac{w^{2}}{2}\bigg)+R\,O(1/M^{3})\\

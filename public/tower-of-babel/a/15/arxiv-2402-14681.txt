Decomposition of Płonka sums into direct systems
Krystyna Mruczek-Nasieniewska, Mateusz Klonowski

Abstract
The Płonka sum is an algebra determined using a structure called a direct system. By a direct system, we mean an indexed family of algebras with disjoint universes whose indexes form a join-semilattice s.t. if two indexes are in a partial order relation, then there is a homomorphism from the algebra of the first index to the algebra of the second index. The sum of the sets of the direct system determines the universe of the Płonka sum. Therefore, to speak about a Płonka sum, there must be a direct system on which this algebra is based. However, we can look at a Płonka sum the other way around, considering it as some given algebra, and ask whether it is possible to determine all direct systems of it systematically. In our paper, we will decompose the Płonka sum in such a way as to give a solution to the indicated problem. Moreover, our method works for any algebra of the kind considered in the article, and thus, we can determine if a given algebra is a Płonka sum. The proposed method is based on two concepts introduced in the paper: isolated algebra and Płonka homomorphism.



Key words
: decomposition, direct frame, direct system, isolated algebra, Płonka homomorphism, Płonka sums

In

The Płonka sum was constructed to analyze equational classes determined by certain regular identities, i.e., such identities where the sets of variables of the left and right sides are equal. Considering classes defined by regular identities is part of research on identities, in which the structure of terms creating them matters. Example of such identities are: normal, externally compatible, P-compatible, biregular, symmetric and many others (see for example

[

). This logic is an example of a formal system whose semantics take into account not only logical connections but also other dependencies, and relations between sentences (cf.

To define a Płonka sum, we use a direct system, which is an indexed family of algebras with disjoint universes whose indexes form a join-semilattice s.t. if two indexes are in a partial order relation, then there is a homomorphism from the algebra of the first index to the algebra of the second index.
The sum of the sets of the direct system determines the universe of the Płonka sum. Therefore, to speak about a Płonka sum, there must be a direct system on which this algebra is based. However, we can look at a Płonka sum the other way around, considering it as some given algebra, and ask whether it is possible to determine all direct systems of it systematically.

Therefore, we can analyze the Płonka sum from two perspectives. From the first, the direct system of a given Płonka sum is determined and known. In other words, the first perspective employs a direct system to determine the Płonka sum. From the second, the Płonka sum is presented as a universe on which operations are defined. Thus, the second perspective acknowledges the Płonka sum as an algebra with a universe that can be expressed as a union of a family of sets forming a certain direct system. Of course, a direct system for a given Płonka sum must exist in both cases.
The difference is that in the first case, it is known, and in the second, it is not.
Usually, in the literature, the first perspective is adopted without asking how, given the algebra being the Płonka sum, all the direct systems that determine it can be identified.

In our paper, we will present a method for determining the direct systems of a given Płonka sum by decomposing this sum so as to obtain an appropriate indexed family of algebras with disjoint universes forming semilattice and an appropriate set of homomorphisms.
In other words, we will represent direct systems by structures we can receive by our method of Płonka sums decomposition. Such an approach fits into the investigations on structures’ representation. A well-known example of a result related to this topic might be, for instance, Stone’s representation theorem for Boolean algebras. Moreover, since the universe of the Płonka sum is a set-theoretic union, it seems natural to ask about the decomposition of the Płonka sum, which considers the division of its universe into disjoint sets.
Such a problem fits into the general investigations on complex structures’ decomposition (or factorization). A well-known example of a result related to this topic might be, for instance, the fundamental theorem of arithmetic or the fundamental subdirect representation theorem of G. Birkhoff.

Note that in
[
18
]
, Płonka presented a partition function determined on a universe of a given algebra. Using any of such functions, one can also decompose an algebra being a Płonka sum receiving its direct systems. However, one can also notice that finding a partition function starting with an algebra is a more difficult task than applying our method as we employ less complex notions.

There are some other constructions known in the literature that propose some decompositions of Płonka sums (see
[
9
,
21
]

The first of the decompositions was presented in
[
9

. It indicates (with certain assumptions) subdirectly irreducible algebras in the class of all Płonka sums. It has been proven that they are either subdirect irreducible algebras in the initial class or are created from them after adding the so-called absorbing element.
The second of the decompositions was presented in

However, these decompositions are made assuming we know the direct system determining the initial Płonka sum. As mentioned in our paper, we will decompose algebras being Płonka sums without knowing their direct systems.
Our method works for any algebra of the kind considered in the article, and thus, we can determine if a given algebra is a Płonka sum.

2
Płonka Sum

We now present the Płonka sum formally.
We start with a definition of a direct system.

Definition 1
.

A
direct system
is a triple
\mathcal{A}=\langle\mathcal{I},\{\boldsymbol{A}_{i}\}_{i\in I},\allowbreak\{\varphi_{i,j}\}_{i\leqslant j;i,j\in I}\rangle
s.t.:

a.

\mathcal{I}=\langle I,\leqslant\rangle
is a join-semilattice,

b.

\{\boldsymbol{A}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
a non-empty family of algebras of the same type and with disjoint universes,

c.

\{\varphi_{i,j}\}_{i,j\in I}
is a family of homomorphism s.t. for all
i,j\in I
:

–

if
i\leqslant j
, then there is a homomorphism s.t.
\varphi_{i,j}:A_{i}\longrightarrow A_{j}
,

–

if
i\leqslant j\leqslant k
, then
\varphi_{j,k}\circ\varphi_{i,j}=\varphi_{i,k}
,

–

\varphi_{i,i}=\textsf{id}_{A_{i}}
.

1

For simplicity of notation, we will omit identity homomorphism in our considerations — we will assume by default that
\varphi_{i,i}=\textsf{id}_{A_{i}}
.

Additionally, we adopt the following notions:

•

a direct system
\mathcal{A}
is called a
non-trivial
one iff there are
i,j\in I
s.t.
i\neq j
,

•

an ordered pair
\langle\mathcal{I},\{\boldsymbol{A}_{i}\}_{i\in I}\rangle
s.t. the conditions a and b are satisfied we call a
direct frame
.

Let us now introduce the definition of the Płonka sum.

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\langle\mathcal{I},\{\boldsymbol{A}_{i}\}_{i\in I},\allowbreak\{\varphi_{i,j}\}_{i\leqslant j;i,j\in I}\rangle
be a direct system.
A
Płonka sum
(in symb.:
S(\mathcal{A})
) is an algebra
\langle A;F_{1}^{\boldsymbol{A}},\ldots F_{n}^{\boldsymbol{A}}\rangle
similar to algebras of family
\{\boldsymbol{A}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
s.t.:

•

A=\bigcup_{i\in I}A_{i}
,

•

for any
k\leq n
, for all
x_{1},\ldots,x_{ar(k)}\in A
:

F_{k}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{ar(k)}):=F_{k}^{\boldsymbol{A}_{m}}(\varphi_{i_{1},m}(a_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{i_{ar(k)},m}(a_{ar(k)})),

where for any
j\leq ar(k)
,
a_{j}\in A_{i_{j}}
and
m=i_{1}\vee\ldots\vee i_{ar(k)}
.

In the
Appendix
, we present several examples of Płonka sums, which we will refer to in the sequel of our paper.

Having introduced the definitions of the direct system and the Płonka sum, we can try to compare our paper’s main goal with the fundamental arithmetic theorem, Birkhoff’s subdirect representation theorem, and Stone’s representation theorem for Boolean algebras.
Examining the Płonka sum by considering its direct systems can be compared to considering a given number as the product of its prime factors. In turn, the question about direct systems that determine a given Płonka sum can be compared to the problem of determining the prime factors of a given number.
We can also make a comparison with Birkhoff’s theorem. In the case of decomposing an algebra into subdirect products, the aim is to obtain algebras that are less complex than the initial algebra and which, when properly combined, allow us to obtain the initial algebra. Our paper aims to decompose an algebraic structure into ‘smaller’ algebras so that we receive the former by union of the latter.
On the other hand, Stone’s theorem can also be recalled as a good reference point for our research. In our article, we will show that every direct system is isomorphic to a certain system of isolated algebras — we define the notion system of isolated algebras in the sequel.

In the
Introduction
, we also mentioned the notion of partition function. Let us also introduce its formal definition.

Definition 3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{A}=\langle\boldsymbol{A};F_{1}^{\boldsymbol{A}},\ldots,F_{n}^{\boldsymbol{A}}\rangle
be an algebra. A
partition function of
\boldsymbol{A}
is a function
f\colon A\times A\longrightarrow A
s.t. the following conditions are satisfied:

P1.

f(f(a,b),c)=f(a,f(b,c))
,

P2.

f(a,a)=a
,

P3.

f(a,f(b,c))=f(a,f(c,b))
,

P4.

f(F_{k}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{ar(k)}),b)=F_{k}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(f(a_{1},b),\ldots,f(a_{ar(k)},b))
,

P5.

f(b,F_{k}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{ar(k)}))=f(b,F_{k}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(f(b,a_{1}),\ldots,f(b,a_{ar(k)})))
,

P6.

f(F_{k}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{ar(k)}),a_{l})=F_{k}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{ar(k)})
, where
1\leq l\leq ar(k)
,

P7.

f(a,F_{k}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(a,\ldots,a))=a
.

In
[
18
]
, Płonka, by means of a partition function, defines an equivalence relation on the universe of a given algebra. The equivalence classes are subalgebras of the given algebra and form a join-semilattice. Moreover, he shows how to define, by means of the partition function, the set of homomorphisms satisfying the conditions presented in Definition
1
.c. Therefore, if one can define a partition function, one can also find a direct system. Płonka also shows that if we have a direct system, by means of homomorphisms, we can define a partition function. We have the following corollary:

Corollary 4
.

Algebra
\boldsymbol{A}
is a Płonka sum iff there is a partition function of
\boldsymbol{A}
.

Although we can use a partition function to decompose a Płonka sum, we don’t know how to find, in a systematic way, a function that satisfies conditions P1–P7. The conditions P1–P7 are not easy to satisfy, and connecting them straightforwardly with conditions assumed for the direct system isn’t easy either.
We think that we can offer an easier-to-be-applied and more straightforwardly aimed at receiving direct systems method of decomposition.

Since our goal is to indicate the direct system for an algebra being the Płonka sum, we have to determine the following components:

\blacktriangleright

an indexed family of algebras with disjoint universes where the set of indexes form a join-semilattice;

\blacktriangleright

a set of homomorphisms satisfying given conditions.

In the next section, we will reconstruct both the family of algebras and the set of homomorphisms. Then, we will show that the obtained system will, in fact, define the initial algebra which will mean that the goal of our article has been achieved.

Isolated Algebras and Płonka Homomorphism

Namely, if we consider a union of the universe of a given algebra and the universes of all algebras with the smaller indexes, we will obtain an isolated algebra.

3.2
Płonka Homomorphism

Płonka’s construction requires the existence of homomorphisms from algebras of a given index to algebras of a ‘greater’ index, where the algebras have disjoint universes.
As shown in Example
34
(see
Appendix
), there is not always a homomorphism from a complement algebra to a complement algebra with a ‘greater’ index.
Moreover, not every homomorphism from a complement algebra to a complement algebra is a homomorphism suitable for determining the operation of a Płonka sum in the way specified in Definition
2
. Such a situation is shown in Example
35
(see
Appendix
).

Thus, we can conclude that having a given algebra that is a Płonka sum and trying to identify the respective homomorphisms between complement algebras, we distinguish three possibilities. The first is when homomorphisms do not exist. Such a situation is presented in Example
34
. The second is when homomorphisms exist, but they do not satisfy the condition given in Definition
2
. Such a situation is described in Example
35
. Finally, the third, when homomorphisms exist and they allow us to determine the operations of the Płonka sum according to the mentioned condition. Such a situation has been presented in Examples
34
and

Below, we introduce the general notion of a Płonka homomorphism, which will allow us to identify all homomorphisms of the third possibility.

Now we can easily prove the following fact concerning the composition of P-homomorphisms.

Fact 22
.

Let
\boldsymbol{A}=\langle A;F_{1}^{\boldsymbol{A}},\ldots,F_{n}^{\boldsymbol{A}}\rangle
be an algebra and
\langle\mathcal{I},\{\boldsymbol{A}_{i}\}_{i\in I}\rangle
be a frame of algebras isolated wrt
\boldsymbol{A}
. Then, for all
i,j,k\in I
, if
i\leqslant j\leqslant k
,
\varphi_{i,j}\in\mathsf{PHom}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(\boldsymbol{A}_{i}^{-},\boldsymbol{A}_{j}^{-})
and
\varphi_{j,k}\in\mathsf{PHom}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(\boldsymbol{A}_{j}^{-},\boldsymbol{A}_{k}^{-})
, then
\varphi_{j,k}\circ\varphi_{i,j}\in\mathsf{PHom}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(\boldsymbol{A}_{i}^{-},\boldsymbol{A}_{k}^{-})
.

Proof.

Assume that
i<j<k
. In the other cases, the composition equals one of the given P-homomorphisms.

Let
a_{1},\ldots,a_{ar(m)}\in A
. Assume that
F^{\boldsymbol{A}}_{m}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{ar(m)})\in\bigcup_{k\leqslant p}A_{p}^{-}
. Hence,
F^{\boldsymbol{A}}_{m}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{ar(m)})\in\bigcup_{j\leqslant p}A_{p}^{-}
. Be Definition
18
,
F^{\boldsymbol{A}}_{m}(f_{\varphi_{i,j}}(a_{1}),\allowbreak\ldots,\allowbreak f_{\varphi_{i,j}}(a_{ar(m)}))=F^{\boldsymbol{A}}_{m}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{ar(m)})\in\bigcup_{k\leqslant p}A_{p}^{-}
.
Therefore, by Definition
18
and Fact
21
we obtain:

\displaystyle F^{\boldsymbol{A}}_{m}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})

\displaystyle=F^{\boldsymbol{A}}_{m}(f_{\varphi_{i,j}}(a_{1}),\ldots,f_{\varphi_{i,j}}(a_{m}))

\displaystyle=F^{\boldsymbol{A}}_{m}(f_{\varphi_{j,k}}(f_{\varphi_{i,j}}(a_{1})),\ldots,f_{\varphi_{j,k}}(f_{\varphi_{i,j}}(a_{m})))

\displaystyle=F^{\boldsymbol{A}}_{m}(f_{\varphi_{j,k}\circ\,\varphi_{i,j}}(a_{1}),\ldots,f_{\varphi_{j,k}\circ\,f_{\varphi_{i,j}}}(a_{m})).

In the set of all frames of isolated algebras, we have to distinguish those frames,
for which from any complement algebra to any complement
algebra of a ‘greater’ index, there is a P-homomorphism. Moreover, such homomorphisms have to satisfy the conditions for the composition of homomorphisms given in Definition
1
.
To this aim, we introduce the notion of a sound set of P-homomorphisms.

Definition 23
.

Let
\boldsymbol{A}=\langle A;F_{1}^{\boldsymbol{A}},\ldots,F_{n}^{\boldsymbol{A}}\rangle
be an algebra and

\langle\mathcal{I},\{\boldsymbol{A}_{i}\}_{i\in I}\rangle

be a frame of algebras isolated wrt
\boldsymbol{A}
.
A
sound set of P-homomorphisms
is a set
\{\varphi_{i,j}\}_{i\leqslant j;i,j\in I}
s.t. for any
i,j\in I
:

•

if
i=j
or
i\prec j
, then
\varphi_{i,j}\in\mathsf{PHom}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(\boldsymbol{A}_{i}^{-},\boldsymbol{A}_{j}^{-})
,

•

if
i<j
and
i\nprec j
, then the following conditions are satisfied:

–

\varphi_{i,j}=\varphi_{i_{m},j}\circ\ldots\circ\varphi_{i,i_{1}}
, for some
i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}\in I
s.t.
i\prec i_{1}\prec\ldots\prec i_{m}\prec j
,

–

for any
j_{1},\ldots,j_{o}\in I
, if
i\prec j_{1}\prec\ldots\prec j_{o}\prec j
, then
\varphi_{i,j}=\varphi_{j_{o},j}\circ\ldots\circ\varphi_{i,j_{1}}
.

4
Decomposition Theorem

Let us now move on to the last construction.

Definition 24
.

Let
\boldsymbol{A}=\langle A;F_{1}^{\boldsymbol{A}},\ldots,F_{n}^{\boldsymbol{A}}\rangle
be an algebra.

•

A
system of algebras isolated wrt
\boldsymbol{A}
is a triple
\langle\mathcal{I},\{\boldsymbol{A}_{i}^{-}\}_{i\in I},\allowbreak\{\varphi_{i,j}\}_{i\leqslant j;\allowbreak i,j\in I}\rangle
s.t.
\langle\mathcal{I},\{\boldsymbol{A}_{i}\}_{i\in I}\rangle
is a frame of algebras isolated wrt
\boldsymbol{A}
and
\{\varphi_{i,j}\allowbreak\}_{i\leqslant j;i,j\in I}
is a sound set of P-homomorphisms.

•

We call a system of isolated algebras
\langle\mathcal{I},\{\boldsymbol{A}_{i}^{-}\}_{i\in I},\allowbreak\{\varphi_{i,j}\}_{i\leqslant j;\allowbreak i,j\in I}\rangle
a
non-trivial
one iff there are
i,j\in I
s.t.
i\neq j
.

Let us now show that the set of systems of isolated algebras
is non-empty.

Theorem 25
.

If
\boldsymbol{A}=\langle A;F_{1}^{\boldsymbol{A}},\ldots,F_{n}^{\boldsymbol{A}}\rangle
is Płonka sum, then there exists system of algebras isolated wrt
\boldsymbol{A}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}=\langle\langle I,\leqslant\rangle,\{\boldsymbol{A}_{i}\}_{i\in I},\{\varphi_{i,j}\}_{i\leqslant j;i,j\in I}\rangle
be a direct system s.t.
\boldsymbol{A}=S(\mathcal{A})
. Let
\langle\langle J,\sqsubseteq\rangle,\{\boldsymbol{B}_{j}\}_{j\in J}\rangle
is a frame of algebras isolated wrt
\boldsymbol{A}
, where
\{\boldsymbol{B}_{j}\}_{j\in J}
is the set of all isolated algebras wrt
\boldsymbol{A}
.

By Fact
17
, we have the injection
f\colon I\longrightarrow J
defined by the formula
f(i):=j
, where
A^{+}_{i}=B_{j}
. We have also that
\langle\langle I,\leqslant\rangle,\{\boldsymbol{A}_{i}^{+}\}_{i\in I}\rangle
and
\langle\langle f(I),\allowbreak\sqsubseteq\rangle,\allowbreak\{\boldsymbol{B}_{k}\}_{k\in f(I)}\rangle
are isomorphic. Therefore, isomorphic are also
\langle\langle I,\leqslant\rangle,\{\boldsymbol{A}_{i}\}_{i\in I}\rangle
and
\langle\langle f(I),\sqsubseteq\rangle,\{\boldsymbol{B}_{k}^{-}\}_{k\in f(I)}\rangle
.

For all
j_{1},j_{2}\in J
, for every
a\in B_{j_{1}}
we put

\psi_{j_{1},j_{2}}(a):=\varphi_{i_{1},i_{2}}(a)
, where
j_{1}=f(i_{1})
and
j_{2}=f(i_{2})
. By Fact
20
and Definition
1
,
\{\psi_{j,k}\}_{j\sqsubseteq k;j,k\in f(I)}
is a sound set of P-homomorphisms.

So,
\langle\langle f(I),\sqsubseteq\rangle,\{\boldsymbol{B}_{k}\}_{k\in f(I)},\{\psi_{j,k}\}_{j\sqsubseteq k;j,k\in f(I)}\rangle
is a system of algebras isolated wrt
\boldsymbol{A}
.
∎

The following theorem shows that isolated algebras allow us to decompose the Płonka sum.

Theorem 26

(Decomposition theorem)
.

If
\boldsymbol{A}=\langle A;F_{1}^{\boldsymbol{A}},\ldots,F_{n}^{\boldsymbol{A}}\rangle
is an algebra and
\mathcal{A}=\langle\mathcal{I},\allowbreak\{\boldsymbol{A}_{i}^{-}\}_{i\in I},\allowbreak\{\varphi_{i,j}\}_{i\leqslant j;i,j\in I}\rangle
is a non-trivial system of algebras isolated wrt
\boldsymbol{A}
, then
\mathcal{A}
is a direct system and
\boldsymbol{A}=S(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

Obviously,
\mathcal{A}
is a non-trivial direct system.

Let
m\leq n
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{ar(m)}\in A
. Assume that for any
l\leq ar(m)
,
a_{l}\in A^{-}_{i_{l}}
and
j=i_{1}\vee\ldots\vee i_{ar(m)}
. Thus, for any
l\leq ar(m)
,
\varphi_{i_{l},j}a_{l}\in A^{-}_{j}
. Since for any
l\leq ar(m)
,
a_{l}\in A^{-}_{i_{l}}
, then for any
l\leq ar(m)
,
a_{l}\in A_{j}
. Hence,
F^{\boldsymbol{A}}_{m}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{ar(m)})\in A_{j}
.
Moreover,
F_{m}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(\varphi_{i_{1,j}}(a_{1}),\ldots,a_{l},\ldots,a_{ar(m)}),\allowbreak\ldots,F_{m}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(\varphi_{i_{1},j}(a_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{i_{l},j}(a_{l}),\allowbreak\ldots,\allowbreak a_{ar(m)}),\ldots,F_{m}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(\varphi_{i_{1},j}(a_{1}),\allowbreak\ldots,\allowbreak\varphi_{i_{l},j}(a_{l}),\allowbreak

\ldots,\allowbreak\varphi_{i_{ar(m)},j}(a_{ar(m)}))\in A_{j}
.

Assume indirectly that there is
k<j
s.t.
F^{\boldsymbol{A}}_{m}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{ar(m)})\in A_{k}
. By Definition
5
,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{ar(m)}\in A_{k}
. By Fact
7
.5, for any
l\leq ar(m)
,
i_{l}\leqslant k
. So,
j\leqslant k
. Similarly we can show that there is no
k<j
s.t. at least one of the following holds:
F_{m}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(\varphi_{i_{1},j}(a_{1}),\ldots,a_{l},\ldots,a_{ar(m)})\in A_{k},\ldots,F_{m}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(\varphi_{i_{1},j}(a_{1}),\ldots,\allowbreak\varphi_{i_{l},j}(a_{l}),\allowbreak\ldots,\allowbreak a_{ar(m)})\in A_{k},\ldots,F_{m}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(\varphi_{i_{1},j}(a_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{i_{l},j}(a_{l}),\ldots,\allowbreak\varphi_{i_{ar(m)},j}(\allowbreak a_{ar(m)}\allowbreak))\allowbreak\in A_{k}
.

Thus, by (
3.1
),
F^{\boldsymbol{A}}_{m}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{ar(m)})\in A_{j}^{-}\subseteq\bigcup_{j\leqslant k}A_{k}^{-}
. And also:
F_{m}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(\varphi_{i_{1},j}(\allowbreak a_{1}),\allowbreak\ldots,\allowbreak a_{l},\ldots,\allowbreak a_{ar(m)})\in A_{j}^{-},\allowbreak\ldots,\allowbreak F_{m}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(\varphi_{i_{1}},j(a_{1}),\allowbreak\ldots,\allowbreak\varphi_{i_{l},j}(a_{l}),\allowbreak\ldots,\allowbreak a_{ar(m)})\allowbreak\!\in\!\allowbreak A_{j}^{-},\allowbreak\ldots,\allowbreak F_{m}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(\varphi_{i_{1}},j(a_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{i_{l},j}(a_{l}),\ldots,\allowbreak\varphi_{i_{ar(m)},j}(\allowbreak a_{ar(m)}\allowbreak))\allowbreak\in A_{j}^{-}
.

By the definition of
\leqslant
and by (
3.1
), for all
o,p\leq ar(m)
, if
o\neq p
, then
A_{i_{o}}^{-}\neq A_{i_{p}}^{-}

Hence, by Definition
18
, the definition of
f_{\varphi}
and Fact
19
, we obtain:

\displaystyle F_{m}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{l},\ldots,a_{ar(m)})=

\displaystyle F_{m}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(f_{\varphi_{i_{1},j}}(a_{1}),\ldots,f_{\varphi_{i_{1},j}}(a_{l}),\ldots,f_{\varphi_{i_{1},j}}(a_{ar(m)}))

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle F_{m}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(\varphi_{i_{1},j}(a_{1}),\ldots,a_{l},\ldots,a_{ar(m)})

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\vdots

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle F_{m}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(f_{\varphi_{i_{l},j}}(\varphi_{i_{1},j}(a_{1})),\ldots,f_{\varphi_{i_{l},j}}(a_{l}),\ldots,f_{\varphi_{i_{l},j}}(a_{ar(m)}))

\displaystyle=\;

\displaystyle F_{m}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(\varphi_{i_{1},j}(a_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{i_{l},j}(a_{l}),\ldots,a_{ar(m)})

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\vdots

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;F_{m}^{\boldsymbol{A}}(\varphi_{i_{1},j}(a_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{i_{l},j}(a_{l}),\ldots,\varphi_{i_{ar(m)},j}(a_{ar(m)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;F_{m}^{\boldsymbol{A}_{j}}(\varphi_{i_{1},j}(a_{1}),\ldots,\varphi_{i_{l},j}(a_{l}),\ldots,\varphi_{i_{ar(m)},j}(a_{ar(m)}).\hskip 17.07182pt\qed

By Theorems
25
and
26
the following conclusion follows:

Corollary 27
.

Algebra
\boldsymbol{A}=\langle A;F_{1}^{\boldsymbol{A}},\ldots,F_{n}^{\boldsymbol{A}}\rangle
is a Płonka sum iff there is a non-trivial system of algebras isolated wrt
\boldsymbol{A}
.

By means of Corollaries
4
and
28
we receive the following fact:

Corollary 28
.

There is a non-trivial system of algebras isolated wrt
\boldsymbol{A}
iff there is a partition function of
\boldsymbol{A}
.

Summary

In the article, starting from the given algebra, which is the Płonka sum, we presented a method for determining all its direct systems. Our method consists in decomposing the Płonka sums into the so-called isolated algebras, for which we can define the so-called Płonka homomorphisms.
Our decomposition can be performed in two steps (cf. Section
5
). The first step consists of three components. First, we determine the family of algebras isolated wrt a given algebra. Secondly, we select all frames of isolated algebras. Thirdly, we modify the obtained frames by replacing the algebras of the given frame with complement algebras. In the second step, we select a family of P-homomorphisms. The structure obtained in this way constitutes a direct system of the initial algebra being a Płonka sum.
The presented decomposition method allows not only to determine all direct systems of a given Płonka sum but also check whether a given algebra is a Płonka sum.

In this paper, we focused on algebras with respect to which finitely many isolated algebras can be determined. An open problem is the question of the possibility of extending our result to algebras with respect to which we can determine infinitely many isolated algebras.
Additionally, in our work we did not analyze the problem of uniqueness of the decomposition of the Płonka sum into isolated algebras, in particular the problem of irreducibility of algebras that are elements of direct systems of the decomposed sum.
Although we already have partial results regarding these open problems, we will present a detailed analysis of them in subsequent works.

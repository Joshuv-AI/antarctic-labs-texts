Improved effective potential of Gildener-Weinberg models
Huan Souza, L. H. S. Ribeiro, A. C. Lehum

Abstract
The Gildener-Weinberg models are of particular interest in the context of extensions to the Standard Model of particle physics. These extensions may encompass a variety of theories, including double Higgs models, grand unification theories, and proposals for dark matter, among others. In order to rigorously test these models experimentally, obtaining precise results is of crucial importance. In this study, we employ the renormalization group equation and its one-loop functions to obtain a deeper understanding of the higher-loop effective potential. Our findings reveal that the radiatively generated mass of the light particle in the Gildener-Weinberg approach experiences a substantial correction. Furthermore, our results suggest that not all flat directions are equivalent and some may be preferred by nature.



I
Introduction

In 1973, Coleman and Weinberg proposed a mechanism in which radiative corrections play a central role in inducing spontaneous symmetry breaking
Coleman:1973jx
. This mechanism involves computing the effective potential for a massless model containing one scalar boson and examining the vacuum properties of the resulting effective theory. Since higher-loop corrections are computationally challenging, one can resort to the renormalization group improvement technique in order to obtain these contributions
McKeon:1998tr
;
Souza:2020hjd
;
Elias:2003zm
;
Chishtie:2005hr
;
Elias:2004bc
;
Lehum:2019msl
;
Meissner:2008uw
;
Dias:2014txa
;
Quinto:2014zaa
. This is achieved by organizing the effective potential as a power series of logarithms and solving the renormalization group equation through perturbative methods. As a result, one can gain insights into the higher-loop effective potential by utilizing only the one-loop renormalization group functions.

In 1976, Gildener and Weinberg proposed an extension of the Coleman-Weinberg (CW) mechanism, referred to as the Gildener-Weinberg (GW) mechanism
Gildener:1976ih
. This extension generalizes the CW mechanism to models containing multiple scalar bosons. In their work, Gildener and Weinberg made the assumption that scale invariance is spontaneously broken at tree level by a flat vacuum, which imposes restrictions on the coupling constants and simplifies the structure of the effective potential. This approach is particularly noteworthy in the field of particle physics as it offers a potential solution to the hierarchy problem of the Standard Model. This idea was first introduced by Lee and Pilaftsis in their study of a two Higgs model
Lee:2012jn
.

The utilization of the GW mechanism in the two Higgs extension of the Standard Model has been thoroughly explored in several studies
Lee:2012jn
;
Hashino:2015nxa
;
Lane:2019dbc
;
Lane:2018ycs
;
Kannike:2021iyh
. One of the distinctive features of GW models is the phenomenon of alignment, where the couplings of all scalars to fermions and gauge bosons are identical to those found in the single Higgs model
Gunion:2002zf
;
Weinberg:1967tq
.

In addition to the two Higgs extension, another popular extension of the Standard Model is the inclusion of dark matter particles
Steele:2013fka
;
Endo:2015ifa
;
Cosme:2018nly
;
Arcadi:2019lka
;
Arcadi:2021mag
. In such models, symmetry breaking can occur in a variety of ways, as discussed in Ref.
Chataignier:2018kay
. One potential avenue for symmetry breaking is through the GW mechanism. In these models, it is essential to conduct precise calculations, as the cosmological properties of dark matter are dependent on its mass and its coupling to the Higgs boson.

The present study focuses on developing a technique for calculating the leading logarithmic (LL) contributions to the effective potential using the renormalization group improvement method in GW models. We consider the two scalars model, which serves as a toy model for the two Higgs model or any other extension of the Standard Model (SM) that involves an additional scalar particle. The calculation of LL contributions can play a substantial role in phenomenological analyses.

The study is structured as follows: In Sec.
II
, we present the two-scalar model and derive the flatness directions of the model using the GW approach. In Sec.
III
, we compute the LL effective potential up to the forth order in the leading logarithmic expansion through the use of the renormalization group improvement technique. Finally, in Sec.
IV
, we provide our conclusions.

II
Gildener-Weinberg approach

The model under investigation in this study is a system of two interacting real scalar fields, described by the Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\sigma)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\phi)^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{4!}\sigma^{4}-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{4!}\phi^{4}-\frac{\lambda_{3}}{4}\sigma^{2}\phi^{2}.

(1)

To analyze the effective potential as a function of both scalar fields, we utilize the GW approach
Gildener:1976ih
. First, let us consider a scenario in which the tree-level potential has a nontrivial minimum along a certain direction, denoted by
\langle\rho\rangle^{2}=\langle\sigma\rangle^{2}+\langle\phi\rangle^{2}=\mu^{2}
. This implies that each field will attain a minimum value,
\langle\sigma\rangle=n_{1}\mu=\mu_{1},\langle\phi\rangle=n_{2}\mu=\mu_{2}
, that satisfies the following constraint:

\mu_{1}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}=\mu^{2}.

(2)

One way of expressing the condition
\mu_{1}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}=\mu^{2}
is to represent
n_{1}
and
n_{2}
as the sine and cosine of an angle, respectively,

\mu_{1}^{2}+\mu_{2}^{2}=n_{1}^{2}\mu^{2}+n_{2}^{2}\mu^{2}=(\sin\alpha)^{2}\mu^{2}+(\cos\alpha)^{2}\mu^{2}=\mu^{2}.

(3)

To demonstrate this in practice, the fields
\sigma
and
\phi
are relocated as
\sigma\to\eta_{1}+\sigma
and
\phi\to\eta_{2}+\phi
, where
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are now considered as the quantum fields, and
\sigma
and
\phi
serve as background fields. The Lagrangian can be expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\eta_{1})^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{\mu}\eta_{2})^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{4!}\eta_{1}^{4}-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{4!}\eta_{2}^{4}-\frac{\lambda_{3}}{4}\eta_{1}^{2}\eta_{2}^{2}-\eta_{1}\Bigr(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{6}\sigma^{3}+\frac{\lambda_{3}}{2}\sigma\phi^{2}\Bigr)

(4)

\displaystyle-\eta_{2}\Bigr(\frac{\lambda_{2}}{6}\phi^{3}+\frac{\lambda_{3}}{2}\sigma^{2}\phi\Bigr)-\eta_{2}^{2}\Bigr(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{4}\sigma^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{3}}{4}\phi^{2}\Bigr)-\eta_{2}^{2}\Bigr(\frac{\lambda_{2}}{4}\phi^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{3}}{4}\sigma^{2}\Bigr)

\displaystyle-\lambda_{3}\sigma\phi\eta_{1}\eta_{2}-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{6}\sigma\eta_{1}^{3}-\frac{\lambda_{2}}{6}\phi\eta_{2}^{3}-\frac{\lambda_{3}}{2}\Bigr(\sigma\eta_{1}\eta_{2}^{2}+\phi\eta_{2}\eta_{1}^{2}\Bigr).

Based on the Lagrangian described previously, the tadpole conditions are given by

\displaystyle\langle\eta_{1}(0)\rangle=-i\Bigr(\frac{\lambda_{1}}{6}\mu_{1}^{3}+\frac{\lambda_{3}}{2}\mu_{1}\mu_{2}^{2}\Bigr)=0;

(5a)

\displaystyle\langle\eta_{2}(0)\rangle=-i\Bigr(\frac{\lambda_{2}}{6}\mu_{2}^{3}+\frac{\lambda_{3}}{2}\mu_{1}^{2}\mu_{2}\Bigr)=0.

(5b)

Taking into account the assumption that
\sin\alpha\neq 0
and
\cos\alpha\neq 0
(a discussion about the cases in which
\sin\alpha=0
or
\cos\alpha=0
can be found in Appendix
B
), we find

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}=-3\lambda_{3}\cot^{2}(\alpha);

(6a)

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}=-3\lambda_{3}\tan^{2}(\alpha).

(6b)

Assuming that
\lambda_{3}
is negative, the potential is guaranteed to have a lower bound. Using Eqs.(
6
), the mass matrix can then be determined as

M=\begin{pmatrix}-\lambda_{3}\mu^{2}\cos^{2}(\alpha)&\lambda_{3}\mu^{2}\sin(\alpha)\cos(\alpha)\\
\lambda_{3}\mu^{2}\sin(\alpha)\cos(\alpha)&-\lambda_{3}\mu^{2}\sin^{2}(\alpha)\end{pmatrix},

(7)

which has the eigenvalues

\displaystyle m_{1}^{2}=-\lambda_{3}\mu^{2}=|\lambda_{3}|\mu^{2};

(8a)

\displaystyle m_{2}^{2}=0.

(8b)

Accordingly, we observe the generation of mass for one particle, while the other remains massless. This second particle was referred to as the “scalon” by Gildener and Weinberg. Although mass is generated, it is evident that the minimum of the potential is zero. This can be seen explicitly in the expression

V(\sigma,\phi)=-\frac{1}{8}\lambda_{3}\sigma^{4}\cot^{2}(\alpha)-\frac{1}{8}\lambda_{3}\phi^{4}\tan^{2}(\alpha)+\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{3}\sigma^{2}\phi^{2},

(9)

at its minimum,

V(\sigma,\phi)\Bigr|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma=\mu\sin\alpha\\
\phi=\mu\cos\alpha\end{subarray}}=0.

(10)

The appearance of a flat direction in the tree-level potential results in the generation of mass. This can be illustrated graphically by fixing the value of
\lambda_{3}=-0.1
and comparing the results for different values of
\alpha
(as shown in Fig.
1
).

It is noteworthy that, owing to
\lambda_{3}<0
, there exist both attractive and repulsive forces in our system, which are responsible for the mass generation at tree level.

To complete the analysis at tree level, we determine the eigenvectors of the mass matrix and the diagonalization matrix. The eigenvectors are expressed as

\displaystyle\vec{u}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}-\cot(\alpha)&1\end{pmatrix};

(11a)

\vec{u}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}\tan(\alpha)&1\end{pmatrix},

(11b)

and the diagonalization matrix is given by

S=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\cot(\alpha)&\tan(\alpha)\\
1&1\\
\end{array}\right).

(12)

III
Effective potential

Having analyzed the GW tree-level potential, we can now proceed to compute its radiative corrections. These corrections are expected to shift the minimum of the potential to a nonzero value and introduce a local maximum at
\sigma=\phi=0
. The computation of the effective potential will be performed using the renormalization group improvement method, as previously done in
Souza:2020hjd
.

III.1
Summing logarithms

The renormalization group equation (RGE) applied to the effective potential is

\Bigr(\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}+\beta_{\lambda_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{1}}+\beta_{\lambda_{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{2}}+\beta_{\lambda_{3}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{3}}+\sigma\gamma_{\sigma}\frac{\partial}{\partial\sigma}+\phi\gamma_{\phi}\frac{\partial}{\partial\phi}\Bigr)V_{eff}=0,

(13)

that we can rewrite as

\Bigr(-2\frac{\partial}{\partial L}+\beta_{\lambda_{1}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{1}}+\beta_{\lambda_{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{2}}+\beta_{\lambda_{3}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{3}}\Bigr)V_{eff}=0,

(14)

where we used the fact that the anomalous dimensions functions
\gamma_{\sigma}
and
\gamma_{\phi}
are vanishing (see for instance Appendix
A
) and

\displaystyle\mu\frac{\partial V_{eff}}{\partial\mu}=\mu\frac{\partial V_{eff}}{\partial L}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\mu}=-2\frac{\partial V_{eff}}{\partial L}.

(15)

The effective potential can be computed by utilizing the RGE organizing the potential as a logarithmic power series, represented as

\displaystyle V_{eff}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{1}}{4!}\sigma^{4}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{4!}\phi^{4}+\frac{\delta_{3}}{4}\sigma^{2}\phi^{2}+V^{LL}+V^{NLL}+...

(16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\delta_{1}}{4!}\sigma^{4}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{4!}\phi^{4}+\frac{\delta_{3}}{4}\sigma^{2}\phi^{2}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C^{LL}_{n}L^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C^{NLL}_{n}L^{n}+...

where
V^{LL}
and
V^{NLL}
are the LL and next-to-LL contributions, respectively. The coefficients of the LL series,
C^{LL}_{n}
, are proportional to
\xi^{4}x^{n+1}
, where
\xi^{4}
is a combination of the fields, such as
\sigma^{2}\phi^{2}
,
\sigma^{4}
,
\phi^{4}
, etc., and
x
is a combination of the coupling constants, e.g.
x^{2}
could be
\lambda_{1}^{2}
,
\lambda_{2}^{2}
,
\lambda_{3}^{2}
,
\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}
, etc. The coefficients of the next-to-LL series,
C^{NLL}_{n}
, are proportional to
\xi^{4}x^{n+2}
.

In this work, our objective is to determine the LL contributions to the effective potential. To do so, we consider only the LL series, expressed as

V_{eff}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}^{LL}L^{n}+\frac{\delta_{1}}{4!}\sigma^{4}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{4!}\phi^{4}+\frac{\delta_{3}}{4}\sigma^{2}\phi^{2}.

(17)

Substituting the ansatz Eq.(
17
) into the RGE Eq.(
15
) and upon organizing the result in ascending order of the logarithmic expansion, we can identify a recurrence relation that allows us to determine all the coefficients by using the following equation:

C_{n}^{LL}=\left(\beta_{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{1}}+\beta_{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{2}}+\beta_{3}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{3}}\right)\frac{C_{n-1}^{LL}}{2n}.

(18)

With this result at hand, we are able to compute the LL contribution to the effective potential up to the desired order.

III.2
One-loop effective potential

The calculation of the one-loop effective potential will be carried out first. It should be noted that only values of
\alpha
that are not equal to
\frac{n\pi}{2}
, where
n
is an integer, will be considered. By utilizing the recurrence relation specified in Eq.(
18
), the one-loop effective potential can be cast as

\displaystyle V^{LL}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(\lambda_{1}+\delta_{1})}{24}\sigma^{4}+\frac{(\lambda_{2}+\delta_{2})}{24}\phi^{4}+\frac{(\lambda_{3}+\delta_{3})}{4}\sigma^{2}\phi^{2}+C_{1}^{LL}\ln\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}+\phi^{2}}{\mu^{2}}\right),

(19)

where the coefficient
C_{1}^{LL}
is given by

\displaystyle C_{1}^{LL}=\frac{1}{256\pi^{2}}\left[\left(\lambda_{1}^{2}+\lambda_{3}^{2}\right)\sigma^{4}+2(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+4\lambda_{3})\sigma^{2}\phi^{2}+\left(\lambda_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{3}^{2}\right)\phi^{4}\right].

IV
Final remarks

In this study, we evaluated the improved Gildener-Weinberg effective potential for a two-scalar interacting model. Our results demonstrate that higher-order perturbative corrections to the effective potential can significantly impact the dynamics of symmetry breaking. This is because higher-order corrections make the minimum of the effective potential dependent on the flat direction of spontaneous symmetry breaking at tree level, leading to a preferred direction for the true minimum. It is worth noting that our approach to improving the effective potential is based on the summation of leading logarithms, which should not be confused with the RG improvement presented in Ref.
Ookane:2019iwq
. In that work, the author considers
\mu^{2}=\mu_{0}^{2}e^{2t}
and expresses the renormalized coupling constants as functions of
t
. Our approach, on the other hand, involves using the RG equation to derive a recurrence relation that enables us to calculate the leading higher-loop contributions to the effective potential, as originally described in Ref.
McKeon:1998tr
. This has led us to obtain a new result for the preferred flat direction compared to Ref.
Ookane:2019iwq
. A promising avenue for future research would be to combine our method with the one used in
Ookane:2019iwq
to achieve a better approximation of the effective potential.

This methodology can be applied to more general models and the outcomes can be highly informative. For instance, the Lee-Pilaftsis model, which is an extension of the Standard Model with two Higgs scalars, was studied in detail in Refs.
Lee:2012jn
;
Lane:2018ycs
;
Lane:2019dbc
. In
Lane:2018ycs
, the authors examine the potential experimental implications and detectability of the model in the LHC. In
Lane:2019dbc
, the authors propose that the well-known Higgs boson is actually the scalon from the Gildener-Weinberg model, while the heavier particle is a new Higgs boson with a mass of up to 550 GeV. This demonstrates the significance of higher-loop analyses, as the correction to the scalon mass can be substantial. Additionally, the preferred direction of the effective potential should be treated with caution. In previous works
Lee:2012jn
;
Lane:2018ycs
;
Lane:2019dbc
, the authors had the freedom to choose the angle
\alpha
as they saw fit. However, our higher-order corrections highlight the importance of a more careful treatment of this aspect.

Another model that our approach could be applied to is the one proposed in Ref.
Held:2022hnw
. In this work, the authors present a grand unification effective field theory in which the symmetry breaking occurs through the Gildener-Weinberg mechanism. The authors thoroughly study the possible and viable symmetry breaking patterns.

It should be noted that special attention may be required when applying this method to gauge theories. The effective potential in gauge theories can be gauge dependent, as demonstrated in Refs.
Jackiw:1974cv
;
Nielsen:1975fs
. Therefore, care should be taken to include gauge-dependent artifacts such as daisies, as discussed in Refs.
Bazeia:1988pz
;
deLima:1989yf
;
Andreassen:2014eha
. We expect to study this feature in a forthcoming paper.

Finally, one of the most intriguing applications is to Higgs portal models for dark matter
Cosme:2018nly
;
Arcadi:2019lka
;
Arcadi:2021mag
;
Steele:2013fka
. In such models, DM is represented by a scalar field that couples to the Higgs boson in a manner similar to what we have studied in our model. The electroweak symmetry breaking may occur in various ways, with one of them being the Gildener-Weinberg mechanism as discussed in
Steele:2013fka
. A crucial aspect of DM models is the abundance of DM
Gondolo:1990dk
, which is sensitive to the properties of the DM field. Hence, corrections to the masses and coupling constants play a significant role in such scenarios.

V_{1}
at the minimum of the potential can be expressed as

From Eq.(

Appendix C
Large logarithms

The purpose of this appendix is to explain why large logarithms are not present in our analysis. To begin, let us consider the problem in the context of the Coleman-Weinberg mechanism
Coleman:1973jx
. As demonstrated in the original work by Coleman and Weinberg, the effective potential for the
\lambda\phi^{4}
model can be given by

V_{eff}=\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^{4}+\frac{\lambda^{2}\phi^{4}}{256\pi^{2}}\left(\log\frac{\phi^{2}}{\mu^{2}}-\frac{25}{6}\right).

(60)

Thus, we observe that by imposing the minimum condition

\frac{dV_{eff}}{d\phi}\Bigr|_{\phi=\langle\phi\rangle}=0,

(61)

we arrive at the expression

\lambda\log\frac{\langle\phi\rangle^{2}}{\mu^{2}}=-\frac{32\pi^{2}}{3}+O(\lambda).

(62)

where
\mu
is the renormalization scale.

The effective potential suffers from large logarithms due to the quantity
\lambda\log\frac{\langle\phi\rangle}{\mu^{2}}
being outside the perturbative region. This issue arises because only one coupling constant is present in the
\lambda\phi^{4}
model, which is not the case in scalar electrodynamics. The effective potential for this model is given by

V_{eff}=\frac{\lambda}{4!}\phi^{4}+\left(\frac{5\lambda^{2}}{1152\pi^{2}}+\frac{3e^{4}}{64\pi^{2}}\right)\phi^{4}\log\frac{\phi^{2}}{\mu^{2}}.

(63)

Using the minimum condition, we can derive the expression

\frac{\left(54e^{4}+5\lambda^{2}\right)\left(3\log\frac{\langle\phi\rangle^{2}}{\mu^{2}}-11\right)}{864\pi^{2}}+\frac{\lambda}{6}=0.

(64)

We can rewrite this as

\log\frac{\langle\phi^{2}\rangle}{\mu^{2}}=\frac{11}{3}-\frac{144\pi^{2}\lambda}{3(54e^{4}+5\lambda^{2})}.

(65)

We can then find the minimum as

\langle\phi\rangle^{2}=\mu^{2}\exp\left(\frac{11}{3}-\frac{144\pi^{2}\lambda}{3(54e^{4}+5\lambda^{2})}\right).

(66)

As
\mu
is an arbitrary renormalization scale, we can choose it to be the most convenient, as discussed in Ref.
Coleman:1973jx
. In this case, we choose
\mu=\langle\phi\rangle
and solve the above equation perturbatively to find

\lambda=\frac{33e^{4}}{8}.

(67)

Henceforth, the effective potential can be expressed as

V_{eff}=\frac{3e^{4}}{64\pi^{2}}\phi^{4}\left(\log\frac{\phi^{2}}{\langle\phi\rangle^{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\right),

(68)

and there is no issue with large logarithms as long as we confine ourselves to
\phi
values close to the minimum. The same applies to the two-scalar model discussed in this study. By following the same method as described above, we obtain the coupling constant relationships presented in Eqs. (
25
). Therefore, we can conclude that the effective potential is applicable for values of
\sigma^{2}+\phi^{2}
that are sufficiently near to the minimum, such that
\lambda_{3}^{2}L
is within the perturbative range.

Appendix E
Accuracy and limitations of the
L^{4}
approximation

As discussed in Sec.
III.3
, it is possible to truncate the leading logs approximation at
L^{4}
since higher powers will not improve the generated masses or the minimum of the potential. In this appendix, we demonstrate schematically that this is true due to the renormalization conditions presented in Eqs. (
21
).

Let us take the leading logs effective potential before renormalization

V^{LL}=\frac{\delta_{1}}{24}\sigma^{4}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{24}\phi^{4}+\frac{\delta_{3}}{4}\sigma^{2}\phi^{2}+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}A_{n}L^{n}

(73)

in which
A_{n}=A_{n}(\sigma,\phi,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\lambda_{3})
. For convenience, we will express
A_{n}
as
A_{n}(\sigma,\phi)
. In this way, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{4}V^{LL}}{\partial\sigma^{4}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(\sigma^{2}+\phi^{2}\right)^{4}}\left(\sigma^{2}+\phi^{2}\right)^{4}A_{0}^{(4,0)}(\sigma,\phi)+8\sigma\left(\sigma^{2}+\phi^{2}\right)^{3}A_{1}^{(3,0)}(\sigma,\phi)

(74)

\displaystyle-12\left(\sigma^{2}-\phi^{2}\right)\left(\sigma^{2}+\phi^{2}\right)^{2}A_{1}^{(2,0)}(\sigma,\phi)+16\left(\sigma^{2}+\phi^{2}\right)\left(\sigma^{3}-3\sigma\phi^{2}\right)A_{1}^{(1,0)}(\sigma,\phi)

\displaystyle-12\left(\sigma^{4}-6\sigma^{2}\phi^{2}+\phi^{4}\right)A_{1}(\sigma,\phi)+48\sigma^{2}\left(\sigma^{2}+\phi^{2}\right)^{2}A_{2}^{(2,0)}(\sigma,\phi)+96\sigma\phi^{2}\left(\sigma^{2}+\phi^{2}\right)A_{2}^{(1,0)}(\sigma,\phi)

\displaystyle-96\sigma^{3}\left(\sigma^{2}+\phi^{2}\right)A_{2}^{(1,0)}(\sigma,\phi)+88\sigma^{4}A_{2}(\sigma,\phi)-240\sigma^{2}\phi^{2}A_{2}(\sigma,\phi)+24\phi^{4}A_{2}(\sigma,\phi)

\displaystyle+192\sigma^{3}\left(\sigma^{2}+\phi^{2}\right)A_{3}^{(1,0)}(\sigma,\phi)-288\left(\sigma^{4}-\sigma^{2}\phi^{2}\right)A_{3}(\sigma,\phi)+384\sigma^{4}A_{4}(\sigma,\phi)+O(L),

in which

A_{n}^{(a,b)}(\sigma,\phi)=\frac{\partial^{(a+b)}A_{n}(\sigma,\phi)}{\partial^{a}\sigma\partial^{b}\phi}.

(75)

It is observed that the
A_{n}
terms only exist up to
n=4
. Therefore, upon substituting
\sigma=\mu\sin\alpha
and
\phi=\mu\cos\alpha
in Eq. (
74
) (and similarly for the other two renormalization conditions), all the terms proportional to
O(L)
vanish and
A_{n}
, with
n>4
, do not contribute to the counterterms. Hence, the renormalized effective potential can be expressed as

V^{LL}=\tilde{A}_{0}(\sigma,\phi)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}(\sigma,\phi)L^{n},

(76)

where
\tilde{A}_{0}
now carries the counterterm information, and
A_{n}
remains the same as in Eq. (
73
) [as seen explicitly at one-loop order in Eq.(
23
)]. Next, the minimum conditions can be imposed as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V^{LL}}{\partial\sigma}\Bigr|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma=\mu\sin\alpha\\
\phi=\mu\cos\alpha\end{subarray}}=0;

(77a)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial V^{LL}}{\partial\phi}\Bigr|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma=\mu\sin\alpha\\
\phi=\mu\cos\alpha\end{subarray}}=0.

(77b)

Using the same argument as above, it is evident that the only terms contributing to the relations between coupling constants are
\tilde{A}_{0}
and
A_{1}
. After this step, the relevant physical quantities can be computed. First, the value of the minimum of the potential can be calculated as

V_{min}=V^{LL}\Bigr|_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma=\mu\sin\alpha\\
\phi=\mu\cos\alpha\end{subarray}}.

(78)

Since we evaluate the effective potential at the point
\sigma^{2}+\phi^{2}=\mu^{2}
, any terms proportional to
O(L)
will vanish, and the only term contributing to the minimum will be the one proportional to
L^{0}
. We conclude that the
L^{4}
approximation suffices to determine the minimum of the effective potential, and our result is robust because it does not receive any corrections from terms proportional to
L^{m}
, where
m>4
. Furthermore, the generated masses will not be corrected by any term beyond the
L^{4}
approximation. However, it should be noted that our result can be corrected by next-to-leading Logs terms (NLL) or beyond. To compute the NLL terms, we would need to use the two-loop RG equation, and for the NNLL contributions, we would need to use the three-loop RG equation, and so on.

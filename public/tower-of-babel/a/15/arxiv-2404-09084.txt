Noncommutative weighted shifts, joint similarity, and function theory in several variables
Gelu Popescu

Abstract
. The goal of this paper is to study the structure of noncommutative weighted shifts, their properties, and to understand their role as models (up to similarity) for n n -tuples of operators on Hilbert spaces as well as their implications to function theory on noncommutative (resp.commutative) Reinhardt domains. We obtain a Rota type similarity result concerning the joint similarity of n n -tuples of operators to parts of noncommutative weighted multi-shifts and provide a noncommutative multivariable analogue of Foiaş–Pearcy model for quasinilpotent operators. The model noncommutative weighted multi-shift which is studied in this paper is the n n -tuple W = ( W 1 , … , W n ) W=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}) , where W i W_{i} are weighted left creation operators of the full Fock space with n n generators associated with a weight sequence 𝝁 = { μ β } | β | ≥ 1 \boldsymbol{\mu}=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{|\beta|\geq 1} of nonnegative numbers. We also represent the injective weighted multi-shifts W 1 , … , W n W_{1},\ldots,W_{n} as ordinary multiplications by Z 1 , … , Z n Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n} on a Hilbert space of noncommutative formal power series. This leads naturally to analytic function theory in several complex variables. One of the goal for the remainder of the paper is to analyze the extent to which our noncommutative formal power series represent analytic functions in several noncommutative (resp. commutative) variables and to develop a functional calculus for arbitrary n n -tuples of operators on a Hilbert space. In particular, we show that, for any n n -tuple of operators T = ( T 1 , … , T n ) T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}) , there exists a weighted multi-shift W = ( W 1 , … , W n ) W=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}) such that the map p ⁡ ( W 1 , … , W n ) ↦ p ⁡ ( T 1 , … , T n ) p(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})\mapsto p(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}) extends to a w ∗ w^{*} -continuous F ∞ ​ ( 𝝁 ) F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu}) -functional calculus Ψ T : F ∞ ​ ( 𝝁 ) → B ⁡ ( ℋ ) \Psi_{T}:F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to B({\mathcal{H}}) which is a completely bounded algebra homomorphism and ‖ Ψ T ‖ c ​ b ≤ π 6 \|\Psi_{T}\|_{cb}\leq\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{6}} . Most of the results of the paper admit commutative versions.

Let

where

is a sequence of complex numbers.
One can easily see that

is jointly unitarily equivalent to the weighted left multi-shift associated to the weights
\{|\mu_{\beta}|\}_{|\beta|\geq 1}

n
-tuple

(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})

n
generators, which has played an important role in noncommutative multivariable operator theory and
C^{*}
-algebras.
In our study, we employ a

Since

Section 4 is devoted to noncommutative Hardy spaces associated with weighted multi-shifts.

\boldsymbol{\mu}=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}

F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})=\left\{\zeta=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}:\ \|\zeta\|_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}^{2}:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}|c_{\alpha}|^{2}<\infty,\ c_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{C}}\right\}.

is defined by
L_{Z_{i}}\zeta:=Z_{i}\zeta
for all
\zeta\in F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})

R_{Z_{i}}\zeta:=\zeta Z_{i}
for all
\zeta

We remark that the operator

U(e_{\alpha}):=\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}Z_{\alpha}

UW_{i}=L_{Z_{i}}U

U\Lambda_{i}=R_{Z_{i}}U

where

\boldsymbol{\mu}=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}

The right (resp. left) multiplier algebra
{\mathcal{M}}^{r}(F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))

(resp.

[
49

\begin{split}\{W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}\}^{\prime}&=R^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu}):={U}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}^{r}(F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))U,\\
\{\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n}\}^{\prime}&=F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu}):={U}^{*}{\mathcal{M}}^{l}(F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))U,\end{split}

and
(F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))^{\prime\prime}=F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
,
where
′

F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}({W})}^{SOT}=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}({W})}^{WOT}=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}({W})}^{w*},

and the identity.

Consequently, we can represent injective weighted left multi-shifts

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

\sigma_{p}(L_{Z_{1}}^{*},\ldots,L_{Z_{n}}^{*})=\left\{(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}:\ \sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{|\lambda_{\alpha}|^{2}}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}<\infty\right\},

containing the origin.
We show that if

H_{n}

F^{2}(H_{n}):=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}H_{n}^{\otimes k},

where
H_{n}^{\otimes 0}:={\mathbb{C}}1
and
H_{n}^{\otimes k}
is the (Hilbert)
tensor product of
k
copies of
H_{n}
.
The
left creation
operators

S_{i}:F^{2}(H_{n})\to F^{2}(H_{n}),\ i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, are given by

S_{i}\varphi:=e_{i}\otimes\varphi,\quad\varphi\in F^{2}(H_{n}).

Let
{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
be the unital free semigroup on
n
generators

g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}
and the identity
g_{0}
. The length of
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
is defined by
|\alpha|:=0
if
\alpha=g_{0}
and

|\alpha|:=k
if

\alpha=g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}
, where
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We set
e_{\alpha}:=e_{g_{i_{1}}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{g_{i_{k}}}
and
e_{g_{0}}:=1
, and note that
\{e_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
is an othonormal basis for
F^{2}(H_{n})
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|\geq 1}
be a sequence of nonnegative real numbers and define the
weighted left creation operators

W_{i}:F^{2}(H_{n})\to F^{2}(H_{n})
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, associated with the weight sequence
\boldsymbol{\mu}
by setting
W_{i}:=S_{i}D_{i}
, where

S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}
are the left creation operators on the full
Fock space
F^{2}(H_{n})
and the diagonal operators
D_{i}:F^{2}(H_{n})\to F^{2}(H_{n})

are defined by the relation

D_{i}e_{\alpha}:=\mu_{g_{i}\alpha}e_{\alpha},\qquad\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}.

Note that the weighted left shifts
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
are bounded operators if and only if
\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\mu_{g_{i}\alpha}<\infty
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. We call the
n
-tuple
W=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
the
weighted left multi-shift
associated with the weight sequence
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{|\beta|\geq 1}
. We remark that the operators
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
have orthogonal ranges. Throughout this paper, we assume that

\mu_{\beta}\geq 0\ \text{ and }\ \sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\mu_{g_{i}\alpha}<\infty\quad\text{ for any }\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

If
(T_{1},\ldots,T_{k})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
,
we set
T_{\alpha}=T_{i_{1}}\cdots T_{i_{k}}
if
\alpha=g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}
, where
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k}\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and
T_{g_{0}}:=I_{\mathcal{H}}
.
We recall from
[
39
]
that the
joint spectral radius
of an
n
-tuple
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{k})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
is defined by

r(T):=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|=k}T_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}^{*}\right\|^{1/2k}.

We note that
r(T)\leq\|T\|=\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}T_{i}T_{i}^{*}\right\|^{1/2}
. In what follows, we denote by
[T_{\alpha}:\ |\alpha|=k]
the row operator matrix with entries
T_{\alpha}
, where
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\alpha|=k
. For simplicity, we also denote by
(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
the row operator acting from
{\mathcal{H}}^{(n)}
, the direct sum of
n
copies of
{\mathcal{H}}
, to
{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proposition 1.1
.

If
W=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
is the weighted left multi-shift associated with
\boldsymbol{\mu}:=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{|\beta|\geq 1}
, where
\mu_{\beta}\geq 0
, then the the joint spectral radius of
W
satisfies the relation

r(W)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,\alpha)^{1/k},

where

\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,\alpha):=\begin{cases}\mu_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{p}}\alpha}\mu_{g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{p}}\alpha}\cdots\mu_{g_{i_{p}}\alpha}&\text{if}\ \beta=g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{p}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\\
1&\text{if}\ \beta=g_{0}\end{cases}

for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
.

Proof.

A simple calculation reveals that

(1.1)

W_{i}e_{\alpha}=\mu_{g_{i}\alpha}e_{g_{i}\alpha}\quad\text{ and }\quad W_{i}^{*}e_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}\mu_{g_{i}\gamma}e_{\gamma}&\text{ if }\alpha=g_{i}\gamma\\
0&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
and every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Note that if
\beta=g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{p}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, then the relation above implies

W_{\beta}e_{\alpha}=\mu_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{p}}\alpha}\mu_{g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{p}}\alpha}\cdots\mu_{g_{i_{p}}\alpha}e_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{p}}\alpha}.

Therefore,

W_{\beta}e_{\alpha}=\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,\alpha)e_{\beta\alpha},\qquad\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},

and, for any
\beta,\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
,

\left<W_{\beta}^{*}e_{\gamma},e_{\alpha}\right>=\left<e_{\gamma},\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,\alpha)e_{\beta\alpha}\right>=\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,\alpha)&\text{ if }\gamma=\beta\alpha\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Hence, we deduce that

W_{\beta}^{*}e_{\gamma}=\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,\alpha)e_{\alpha}&\text{ if }\gamma=\beta\alpha\ \text{ for some}\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

and, consequently,

W_{\beta}W_{\beta}^{*}e_{\gamma}=\begin{cases}\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,\alpha)^{2}e_{\gamma}&\text{ if }\gamma=\beta\alpha\ \text{ for some}\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

The later relation implies

\left(\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}W_{\beta}W_{\beta}^{*}\right)e_{\gamma}=\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,\alpha)^{2}e_{\gamma}

if
\gamma=\beta\alpha
for some
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\beta|=k
.
Otherwise, we have
\left(\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}W_{\beta}W_{\beta}^{*}\right)e_{\gamma}=0
.
Since the operator
\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}W_{\beta}W_{\beta}^{*}
is diagonal, we deduce that

\left\|\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}W_{\beta}W_{\beta}^{*}\right\|=\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,\alpha)^{2}.

Using the definition of the joint spectral radius for
n
-tuples of operators, we complete the proof.
∎

We identify
M_{m}(B({\mathcal{H}}))
, the set of

m\times m
matrices with entries in
B({\mathcal{H}})
, with

B({\mathcal{H}}^{(m)})
, where
{\mathcal{H}}^{(m)}
is the direct sum of
m

of
{\mathcal{H}}

Thus we have a natural
C^{*}
-norm on

]

[

]

Definition 1.2
.

An
n
-tuple of operators
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
is called

(i)

power bounded,

(ii)

row power bounded,

(iii)

polynomially bounded with respect to the weighted left multi-shift
(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
,

(iv)

completely polynomially bounded with respect to
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
,

if there is a constant
M>0
such that

(i’)

\|T_{\alpha}\|\leq M
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
,

(ii’)

\|[T_{\alpha}:\ |\alpha|=k]\|\leq M
for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

(iii’)

\|p(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\|\leq M\|p(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})\|
for any polynomial
p\in{\mathbb{C}}\left<Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\right>
,

(iv’)

\|[p_{s,q}(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})]_{k\times k}\|\leq M\|[p_{s,q}(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})]_{k\times k}\|
for any
p_{s,q}\in{\mathbb{C}}\left<Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\right>
, the polynomial algebra in noncommutative indeterminates
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}
, and
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
,

respectively.

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
with
n\geq 2
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

If
T
is row power bounded, then it is also power bounded and
r(T)\leq 1
.

(ii)

If
r(T)\leq 1
, then
T
is not necessarily power bounded.

(iii)

There is a power bounded
n
-tuple
T
which is not row power bounded and
r(T)>1
.

Proof.

The first part of item (i) is quite obvious.
To show that
r(T)\leq 1
, note that

\left\|\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|=k}T_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}^{*}\right\|^{1/2}=\|[T_{\alpha}:\ |\alpha|=k]\|\leq M.

To prove item (ii), we give an example of a weighted left multi-shift
W
which is not power bounded and
r(W)=1
. Consider the weights
\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
defined by

\mu_{\beta}:=\frac{|\beta|+1}{|\beta|}
if
|\beta|\geq 1
,
and
\mu_{g_{0}}:=1
.
Note that

\begin{split}\left\|\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}W_{\beta}W_{\beta}^{*}\right\|^{1/2}&=\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},\beta=g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}}\mu_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\mu_{g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\cdots\mu_{g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\\
&=\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}}\frac{|\alpha|+k+1}{|\alpha|+1}=k+1.\end{split}

Hence, we deduce that
W
is not power bounded and
r(W)=1
.
To prove item (iii), consider the
n
-tuple
(S_{1}^{*},\ldots,S_{n}^{*})
, where
S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}
are the left creation operators on the full Fock space. Since
\|S_{\alpha}^{*}\|=1
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}
and

\left\|\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}S_{\beta}^{*}S_{\beta}\right\|=n^{k}

the result follows.
∎

Next, we solve an interpolation problem for the norms

\|\varphi_{T}^{k}\|=\left\|\sum\limits_{\sigma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\sigma|=k}T_{\sigma}T_{\sigma}^{*}\right\|

of the completely positive linear maps
\varphi_{T}^{k}:=\varphi_{T}\circ\cdots\circ\varphi_{T}
(
k
times), where

\varphi_{T}(X):=T_{1}XT_{1}^{*}+\cdots+T_{n}XT_{n}^{*}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

A sequence
\{a_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
has the property that there is an
n
-tuple of operators
(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
such that

a_{k}=\left\|\sum\limits_{\sigma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\sigma|=k}T_{\sigma}T_{\sigma}^{*}\right\|^{1/2},\qquad k\in{\mathbb{N}},

if and only if
a_{k}\geq 0
and
a_{k+m}\leq a_{k}a_{m}
for any
k,m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and either one of the following conditions holds:

(i)

there is
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
a_{k}>0
if
k\leq p
and
a_{k}=0
if
k>p
,

(ii)

a_{k}>0
for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Proof.

The direct implication is due to the well-known fact that

\left\|\sum\limits_{\sigma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\sigma|=k+m}T_{\sigma}T_{\sigma}^{*}\right\|\leq\left\|\sum\limits_{\sigma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\sigma|=k}T_{\sigma}T_{\sigma}^{*}\right\|\left\|\sum\limits_{\sigma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\sigma|=m}T_{\sigma}T_{\sigma}^{*}\right\|.

To prove the converse, we construct a weighted left multi-shift
having the required property. Assume that the condition in item (i) holds. Given the sequence
\{a_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
, we define the weights

\mu_{\beta}:=\frac{a_{|\beta|}}{a_{|\beta|-1}},\qquad|\beta|\geq 1\text{ and }|\beta|\leq p,

where
a_{0}:=1
, and set
\mu_{\beta}:=0
if
|\beta|>p
. The sequence
\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{|\beta|\geq 1}
is bounded since
\mu_{\beta}\leq\frac{a_{|\beta|-1}a_{1}}{a_{|\beta|-1}}=a_{1}
. Let
W=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
be the weighted left multi-shift associated with
\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{|\beta|\geq 1}
.
According to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, if
k\leq p
, we have

\begin{split}\left\|\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}W_{\beta}W_{\beta}^{*}\right\|^{1/2}&=\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,\alpha)\\
&=\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},\beta=g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}}\mu_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\mu_{g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\cdots\mu_{g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\\
&=\sup_{{\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}\atop{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq p}}\frac{a_{|\beta|+|\alpha|}}{a_{|\beta|+|\alpha|-1}}\cdot\frac{a_{|\beta|+|\alpha|-1}}{a_{|\beta|+|\alpha|-2}}\cdots\frac{a_{|\alpha|+1}}{a_{|\alpha|}}\\
&=\sup_{{\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}\atop{|\alpha|+|\beta|\leq p}}\frac{a_{|\beta|+|\alpha|}}{a_{|\alpha|}}\leq a_{|\beta|}.\end{split}

For any
\beta:=g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, the definition of the weights
\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{|\beta|\geq 1}
implies

a_{|\beta|}=\mu_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}}a_{k-1}=\cdots=\mu_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}}\mu_{g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}}\cdots\mu_{g_{i_{k}}}.

Consequently,

\left\|\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}W_{\beta}W_{\beta}^{*}\right\|^{1/2}\leq\mu_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}}\mu_{g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}}\cdots\mu_{g_{i_{k}}}\leq\left\|\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}W_{\beta}W_{\beta}^{*}\right\|^{1/2},

which implies

\left\|\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}W_{\beta}W_{\beta}^{*}\right\|^{1/2}=\mu_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}}\mu_{g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}}\cdots\mu_{g_{i_{k}}}=a_{k},\qquad k\leq p.

If we take
p=\infty
in the considerations above, we obtain the corresponding proof if item (ii) holds.
This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 1.5
.

We say that
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
is a nilpotent
n
-tuple if there is
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
T_{\alpha}=0
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\alpha|=m
. If
m
is the smallest natural number having this property, it is called the index of
T
.
If
r(T)=0
, we say that
T
is a quasi-nilpotent
n
-tuple.

We recall that
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
is called row contraction if
T_{1}T_{1}^{*}+\cdots+T_{n}T_{n}^{*}\leq I
. A compact operator is said to be in the class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}\ (0<p<\infty)
if the eigenvalues of
(A^{*}A)^{1/2}
are in
\ell^{p}
. We also consider the lexicographic order on the unital free semigroup
{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
W=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
be the weighted left multi-shift associated with the weight sequence
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{|\beta|\geq 1}
with
\mu_{\beta}\geq 0
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

The row operator
(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
is injective if and only if all the entries
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
are injective operators, which is equivalent to all the weights being strictly positive.

(ii)

If
W
is injective and
\mu_{\beta}\to 0
as

|\beta|\to\infty
, then
r(W)=0
.

(iii)

W_{i}
is a compact operator if and only if
\lim_{\alpha\to\infty}\mu_{g_{i}\alpha}=0
. Moreover,
W_{i}
is in the class
{\mathcal{C}}^{p}
if and only if
\{\mu_{g_{i}\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\in\ell^{p}
.

(iv)

W
is a row contraction if and only if
\mu_{\alpha}\leq 1
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
,
|\alpha|\geq 1
.

(v)

If each
W_{i}
is a compact operator, then
W=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
is a
norm-limit of nilpotent weighted left multi-shifts.

(vi)

If
W
is injective, then any non-trivial joint invariant subspace under
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
is infinite dimensional.

(vii)

Every weighted multi-shift is the limit in norm of injective weighted multi-shifts.

Proof.

Part (i) follows from the fact that
W_{i}:=S_{i}D_{i}
, where

S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}
are the left creation operators on the full
Fock space
F^{2}(H_{n})
and the diagonal operator
D_{i}:F^{2}(H_{n})\to F^{2}(H_{n})
is defined by relation

D_{i}e_{\alpha}:=\mu_{g_{i}\alpha}e_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}.

It is clear that
D_{i}
is injective if and only if
\mu_{g_{i}\alpha}>0
. Since
S_{1},\ldots,S_{n}
have orthogonal ranges, the result follows.

To prove item(ii), we
recall from Proposition
1.1
that, for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
and
\beta=g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
,

\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,\alpha)=\mu_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\mu_{g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\cdots\mu_{g_{i_{k}}\alpha}
.
Setting
b_{k}:=\max\{\mu_{\beta}:\ \beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k\}
, we have
b_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty
. Due to a standard Stolz-Cesàro argument, we deduce that

\lim_{k\to\infty}(b_{k+p}b_{k-1+p}\cdots b_{1+p})^{1/k}=0

for each
p\in{\mathbb{N}}
. This implies

\lim_{k\to\infty}\sup_{|\beta|=k,\beta=g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}}\left(\mu_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\mu_{g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\cdots\mu_{g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\right)^{1/k}=0

for each
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
. Given
\epsilon>0
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, we choose
k_{0}(\alpha,\epsilon)\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\sup_{|\beta|=k,\beta=g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}}\left(\mu_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\mu_{g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\cdots\mu_{g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\right)^{1/k}<\epsilon

for any
k\geq k_{0}(\alpha,\epsilon)
.
We also choose

m_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that

\mu_{\beta}<\epsilon
for any
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\beta|\geq m_{0}
.
Note that if
k\geq m_{0}
and
k\geq k_{0}(\alpha,\epsilon)
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\alpha|\leq m_{0}
, then

\sup_{{\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\atop{|\beta|=k,\beta=g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}}}\left(\mu_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\mu_{g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\cdots\mu_{g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\right)^{1/k}<\epsilon

On the other hand, if
|\alpha|\geq m_{0}
, then

\left(\mu_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\mu_{g_{i_{2}}\cdots g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\cdots\mu_{g_{i_{k}}\alpha}\right)^{1/k}<(\epsilon^{k})^{1/k}=\epsilon
as well. Using Proposition
1.1
, we complete the proof of item (ii).
Part (iii) of this proposition is due to the fact that
W_{i}^{*}W_{i}e_{\alpha}=\mu_{g_{i}\alpha}^{2}e_{\alpha}
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
. Since
\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}W_{j}W_{j}^{*}\right)e_{\alpha}=\mu_{\alpha}^{2}e_{\alpha}
if
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\alpha|\geq 1
and
0
otherwise, item (iv) follows.

To prove part (v), let
{\mathcal{L}}_{m}:=\Span\{e_{\alpha}:|\alpha|\leq m\}
and consider the
n
-tuple
W^{(m)}=(W_{1}^{(m)},\ldots,W_{n}^{(m)})
where
W_{i}^{(m)}:=P_{{\mathcal{L}}_{m}}W_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. It is easy to see that
W^{(m)}
is a nilpotent weighted left multi-shift and

\|W-W^{(m)}\|\leq\sup_{|\alpha|\geq m}\mu_{\alpha}
. Since
\mu_{\beta}\to 0
as

|\beta|\to\infty
, we deduce that
\|W-W^{(m)}\|\to 0
as
m\to\infty
.

To prove item (vi), assume that
{\mathcal{M}}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})
,
{\mathcal{M}}\neq\{0\}
, is a joint invariant subspace under
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
and let
f\in{\mathcal{M}}
,
f\neq 0
. Since the operators
\{W_{\beta}\}_{|\beta|=k}
have orthogonal ranges and
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
are injective operators, the vectors
\{W_{\beta}f:\ |\beta|=k\}
are orthogonal and non-zero. Consequently,
\dim{\mathcal{M}}\geq k
for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
To prove part (vii), it is enough to replace the zero weights by small positive numbers.
The proof is complete.
∎

Definition 1.7
.

We say that an
n
-tuple
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{k})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}

is jointly similar to an
n
-tuple
A=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{n})\in B({\mathcal{K}})^{n}

if there is an invertible operator

such that

T_{i}=X^{-1}A_{i}X,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

We say that

Definition 1.8

Let

is invertible
C
is a row contraction and

which completes the proof.

To prove the implication (iii)
\implies
(i), let

be an invertible operator and

for any

, where

\begin{split}\left\|\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}W_{\beta}W_{\beta}^{*}\right\|&=\left\|\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}X^{-1}V_{\beta}XX^{*}V_{\beta}^{*}(X^{-1})^{*}\right\|\\
&\leq\|X\|^{2}\|X^{-1}\|^{2}\left\|\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}V_{\beta}V_{\beta}^{*}\right\|\leq\|X\|^{2}\|X^{-1}\|^{2}\end{split}

Due to the definition of the weighted left multi-shift, for any

\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}W_{\beta}W_{\beta}^{*}e_{\alpha}=0

\text{\rm SOT-}\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}W_{\beta}W_{\beta}^{*}=0,

Conversely, if the later relation holds, then using the principle of uniform boundedness,
we conclude that there is

i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we have

\begin{split}YV_{i}^{*}e_{\alpha}&=\begin{cases}Y(\mu_{\alpha}e_{\gamma})&\text{ if }\alpha=g_{i}\gamma\\
0&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}\\
&=\begin{cases}\mu_{\alpha}\sigma_{\gamma}e_{\gamma}&\text{ if }\alpha=g_{i}\gamma\\
0&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}W_{i}Y^{*}e_{\alpha}=W_{i}^{*}(\sigma_{\alpha}e_{\alpha})=\begin{cases}\sigma_{\alpha}\kappa_{\alpha}e_{\gamma}&\text{ if }\alpha=g_{i}\gamma\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}\end{split}

According to relation (
3.5
), if
\alpha=g_{i}\gamma
, then

\sigma_{g_{i}\gamma}=\sigma_{\gamma}\frac{\mu_{g_{i}\gamma}}{\mu_{g_{i}\gamma}}
.
Consequently,
Y(V_{i}^{*}\otimes I)=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I)Y
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Combining this with relation (
3.3
), we obtain

YXT_{i}^{*}=Y(V_{i}^{*}\otimes I)X=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I)YX,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

On the other hand, the operator
YX:{\mathcal{H}}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{H}}
is bounded below by
1
. Indeed, taking into account that
\sigma_{g_{0}}=a_{0}=1
, we have

\|YXh\|=\left\|Y\left(\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{1}{a_{|\alpha|}}e_{\alpha}\otimes T_{\alpha}^{*}h\right)\right\|=\left\|\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{1}{a_{|\alpha|}}\sigma_{\alpha}e_{\alpha}\otimes T_{\alpha}^{*}h\right\|\geq\|h\|

for any
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
. Consequently, the operator
YX
has closed range. Now, we can define the operator

S:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{M}}:=YX({\mathcal{H}})
by
Sh:=YXh
,
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
, and note that

T_{i}^{*}=S^{-1}(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I)|_{\mathcal{M}}S
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Since the case when
T
is a nilpotent
n
-tuple of operators of index
m
was already discussed in Theorem
2.5
, the proof is complete.
∎

In what follows, we assume that the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
is separable and infinite-dimensional.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
be a quasi-nilpotent
n
-tuple of operators. Then there exists a quasi-nilpotent weighted multi-shift
K=(K_{1},\ldots,K_{n})
of compact operators in
B({\mathcal{H}})
and a joint invariant subspace
{\mathcal{R}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}\otimes F^{2}(H_{n})
under the operators
K_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, such that
(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
is jointly similar to

(P_{\mathcal{R}}(K_{1}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})})|_{\mathcal{R}},\ldots,P_{\mathcal{R}}(K_{n}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})})|_{\mathcal{R}}).

Proof.

Since
{\mathcal{H}}
is a separable infinite dimensional Hilbert space, we can consider an
orthonormal basis
\{v_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
and define the weighted left multi-shift
K=(K_{1},\ldots,K_{n})
by setting

K_{i}v_{\alpha}:=\kappa_{g_{i}\alpha}v_{g_{i}\alpha},\qquad\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

where the weight sequence
\boldsymbol{\kappa}=\{\kappa_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
was introduced in the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
Under the identification of
{\mathcal{H}}
with
F^{2}(H_{n})
via the mapping
v_{\alpha}\mapsto e_{\alpha}
, it is clear that
K=(K_{1},\ldots,K_{n})
is unitarily equivalent to the weighted multi-shift
W=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
which was defined in Theorem
3.1
. Consequently, each
K_{i}
is a compact operator and
K
is a quasi-nilpotent
n
-tuple.
Define the unitary operator
U:F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}\otimes F^{2}(H_{n})
by setting

U(e_{\alpha}\otimes v_{\beta}):=v_{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}
,
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
. It is straightforward to check that

U(W_{i}^{*}\otimes{\mathcal{H}})=(K_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})})U
and, using the proof of Theorem
3.1
, that

UYXT_{i}^{*}=(K_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})})UYX,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Since the operator
UYX
is bounded below from
0
, it has closed range
{\mathcal{R}}:=UYX{\mathcal{H}}\subset{\mathcal{H}}\otimes F^{2}(H_{n})
. Define
S:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{R}}
by setting
Sh:=UYXh
,
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
. The relations above show that
(K_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})}){\mathcal{R}}\subset{\mathcal{R}}
and

(3.6)

ST_{i}^{*}S^{-1}=(K_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})})|_{\mathcal{R}},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
be a quasi-nilpotent
n
-tuple of operators. The
n
-tuple
(K_{1}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})},\ldots,K_{n}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})})
of Corollary
3.2
is jointly quasi-similar to an
n
-tuple
(L_{1},\ldots,L_{n})
, where each

L_{i}\in B({\mathcal{H}}\otimes F^{2}(H_{n}))
is a compact operator, i.e there exist quasi-affinities
X
and
Y
in
B({\mathcal{H}}\otimes F^{2}(H_{n}))
such that

\begin{split}(K_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})})X&=XL_{i}^{*}\\
Y(K_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})})&=L_{i}^{*}Y\end{split}

for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Proof.

Since
K=(K_{1},\ldots,K_{n})
is an
n
-tuple of operators with
r(K)=0
, we can apply Proposition 3.5 from
[
39
]
(which is a noncommutative multivariable version of Rota’s result
[
53
]
) and find, for each
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, an invertible operator
Q_{(\alpha)}\in B({\mathcal{H}})
such that

\|[L^{(\alpha)}_{1},\ldots,L_{n}^{(\alpha)}]\|\leq\frac{1}{|\alpha|},\qquad\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},

where
L^{(\alpha)}_{i}:=Q_{(\alpha)}K_{i}^{*}Q_{(\alpha)}^{-1}
. Consequently, since each operator
L_{i}^{(\alpha)}
is compact, so is the operator defined by

L_{i}:=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}L_{i}^{(\alpha)},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Choose some sequences of strictly positive numbers
\{c_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
and
\{d_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
such that

\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\|c_{\alpha}Q_{(\alpha)}^{-1}\|<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\|d_{\alpha}Q_{(\alpha)}\|<\infty

and define the operators

X:=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\alpha}Q_{(\alpha)}^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad Y:=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}d_{\alpha}Q_{(\alpha)}.

Note that
X
and
Y
are quasi-affinities and

d_{\alpha}Q_{(\alpha)}K_{i}^{*}=d_{\alpha}(L_{i}^{(\alpha)})^{*}Q_{\alpha},\qquad c_{\alpha}Q_{(\alpha)}^{-1}(L_{i}^{(\alpha)})^{*}=c_{\alpha}K_{i}^{*}Q_{(\alpha)}^{-1}

for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
.
Now, note that, under the identification of
{\mathcal{H}}\otimes F^{2}(H_{n})
with
\bigoplus_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}{\mathcal{H}}
, we have

\begin{split}(K_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})})X&=\left(\bigoplus_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}}K_{i}^{*}\right)X=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}}c_{\alpha}K_{i}^{*}Q_{(\alpha)}^{-1}\\
&=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}}c_{\alpha}Q_{(\alpha)}^{-1}(L_{i}^{(\alpha)})^{*}=X\left(\bigoplus_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}}(L_{i}^{(\alpha)})^{*}\right)=XL_{i}^{*}\end{split}

and, similarly, one can show that
Y(K_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})})=L^{*}_{i}Y
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
The proof is complete.
∎

Due to Corollary
3.2
and Theorem
3.3
, one can deduce the following result.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
be a nonzero quasi-nilpotent
n
-tuple of operators. Then there is a nonzero
n
-tuple
K=(K_{1},\ldots,K_{n})\in B({\mathcal{L}})
of compact operators such that
K
is a quasi-affine transform of
T
, i.e. there is a quasi-affinity
\Omega:{\mathcal{L}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
such that
\Omega K_{i}=T_{i}\Omega
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Proof.

According to Theorem
3.3
, there is a quasi-affinity
Y
in
B({\mathcal{H}}\otimes F^{2}(H_{n}))
such that

Y(K_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})})=L_{i}^{*}Y,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Consequently, using relation (
3.6
) and setting
{\mathcal{L}}:=\overline{Y{\mathcal{R}}}
, we have
L_{i}^{*}{\mathcal{L}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
It is clear that
K_{i}^{*}:=L_{i}^{*}|_{\mathcal{L}}:{\mathcal{L}}\to{\mathcal{L}}
is a compact operator. Let
\Gamma:{\mathcal{R}}\to{\mathcal{L}}
be the quasi-affinity defined by
\Gamma x:=Yx
for any
x\in{\mathcal{R}}
and note that the relation above implies

\Gamma(K_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})})|_{\mathcal{R}}=L_{i}^{*}Y|_{\mathcal{R}}=K_{i}^{*}\Gamma,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Due to relation (
3.6
), we have

ST_{i}^{*}S^{-1}=(K_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{F^{2}(H_{n})})|_{\mathcal{R}},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Combining these relations, and setting
\Omega:=(\Gamma S)^{*}
, we conclude that
T_{i}\Omega=\Omega K_{i}
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, which proves that
K
is a quasi-affine transform of
T
.
This completes the proof.
∎

We remark that if
(T_{1}^{*},\ldots T_{n}^{*})
is quasi-nilpotent
n
-tuple, then applying the later corollary we find an
n
-tuple
(M_{1},\ldots,M_{n})
of compact operators such that
(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
is a quasi-affine transform of
(M_{1},\ldots,M_{n})
.
We note that if
(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
is a nilpotent
n
-tuple of operators, then so is
(T^{*}_{1},\ldots,T^{*}_{n})
.

On the other hand, we mention that, due to Corollary
3.4
, if
{\mathcal{M}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}
is a joint invariant subspace under
K_{1},\ldots,K_{n}
, then

\overline{\Omega{\mathcal{M}}}
is jointly invariant under
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}
. In particular, if
\cap_{i=1}^{n}\ker K_{i}\neq\{0\}
, then
\cap_{i=1}^{n}\Omega(\ker K_{i})\neq\{0\}
is a jointly invariant subspace under
T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}
.

4.
Noncommutative Hardy spaces associated with weighted shifts

Let
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
be a sequence of strictly positive real numbers with
\mu_{g_{0}}=1

and let
F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
be the Hilbert space of formal power series in indeterminates
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}
with orthogonal basis
\{Z_{\alpha}:\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\}
such that
\|Z_{\alpha}\|_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}:=\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})
. Note that

F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})=\left\{\zeta=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}:\ \|\zeta\|_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}^{2}:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}|c_{\alpha}|^{2}<\infty,\ c_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{C}}\right\}

and
\left\{\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}Z_{\alpha}\right\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
is an orthonormal basis for
F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
.
The
left multiplication
operator
L_{Z_{i}}
on
F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
is defined by
L_{Z_{i}}\zeta:=Z_{i}\zeta
for all
\zeta\in F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
.
One can easily see that
L_{Z_{i}}
is a bounded operator if and only if

(4.1)

\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\mu_{g_{i}\alpha}<\infty.

Similarly, we define the
right multiplication
operator
R_{Z_{i}}:F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
by setting
R_{Z_{i}}\zeta:=\zeta Z_{i}
for all
\zeta\in F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
. Note that
R_{Z_{i}}
is a bounded operator if and only if

(4.2)

\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha g_{i},g_{0})}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}<\infty.

It is easy to see that
L_{Z_{i}}R_{Z_{j}}=R_{Z_{j}}L_{Z_{i}}
for any
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

We remark that the map

\mu_{\alpha}\mapsto b_{\alpha}:=\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}
is a
one-to-one correspondence between the set

{\bf M}:=\{\{\mu_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}:\ \mu_{\alpha}>0,\mu_{g_{0}}=1,\text{ and }\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\mu_{\alpha}<\infty\}

and the set

{\bf B}:=\{\{b_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}:\ b_{\alpha}>0,b_{g_{0}}=1,\text{ and }\sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}<\infty\}.

Moreover, we have
\mu_{g_{i}\alpha}=\sqrt{\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Due to this correspondence, all the results of the present paper apply in the particular setting of the regular domains
[
47
]
, the noncommutative weighted Bergman spaces
[
46
]
,
[
49
]
, and the more recent extension to admissible domains
[
50
]
.

Example 4.1
.

For each
s\in(0,\infty)
, consider the weights
\boldsymbol{\mu}^{s}:=\{\mu^{s}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
defined by
\mu^{s}_{g_{0}}:=1
and

\mu^{s}_{\alpha}:=\sqrt{\frac{|\alpha|}{s+|\alpha|-1}},\qquad|\alpha|\geq 1.

The associated Hilbert space is

F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{s})=\left\{\zeta=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}:\ \|\zeta\|_{\boldsymbol{\mu}^{s}}^{2}:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{1}{\left(\begin{matrix}s+k-1\\
k\end{matrix}\right)}|c_{\alpha}|^{2}<\infty,\ c_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{C}}\right\}.

If
s=1
, then the corresponding subspace
F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{1})
is the full Fock space on
n
generators.
On the other hand, if
s=n
(resp.
s=n+1
) we obtain the noncommutative Hardy (resp. Bergman) space over the unit ball

[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{1}
. In the particular case when
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we obtain the noncommutative weighted Bergman space over
[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{1}
, which was extensively studied in
[
46
]
,
[
47
]
, and
[
49
]
. One can easily check that
[W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}]
is a row contraction if
s\geq 1
.
We remark that when
s\in(0,1]
, the Hilbert spaces
F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{s})
are noncommutative generalizations of the classical Besov-Sobolev spaces on the unit ball
{\mathbb{B}}_{n}\subset{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
with representing kernel
\frac{1}{(1-\left<z,w\right>)^{s}}
,
z,w\in{\mathbb{B}}_{n}
.

Example 4.2
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
and consider the weights

\boldsymbol{\omega}^{s}:=\{\omega^{s}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
defined by
w^{s}_{g_{0}}:=1
and

\omega^{s}_{\alpha}:=\sqrt{\left(\frac{|\alpha|}{|\alpha|+1}\right)^{s}},\qquad|\alpha|\geq 1.

The associated Hilbert space is

F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\omega}^{s})=\left\{\zeta=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}:\ \|\zeta\|_{\boldsymbol{\omega}^{s}}^{2}:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{1}{(|\alpha|+1)^{s}}|c_{\alpha}|^{2}<\infty,\ c_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{C}}\right\}.

We remark that the scale of spaces
\{F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\omega}^{s}):s\in{\mathbb{R}}\}
contains the noncommutative Dirichlet space
F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\omega}^{-1})
over the unit ball

[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{1}
. Note that
[W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}]
is a row contraction if
s\geq 0
. When
s=0
, we recover again the full Fock space with
n
generators.

Example 4.3
.

Let
s\in[1,\infty)
and let
\varphi=\sum_{|\alpha|\geq 1}d_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
be a formal power series such that
d_{\alpha}\geq 0
and
d_{g_{i}}>0
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
Consider the formal power series

\begin{split}g&=1+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\begin{matrix}s+k-1\\
k\end{matrix}\right)\varphi^{k}=1+\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|\geq 1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{|\alpha|}\left(\begin{matrix}s+j-1\\
j\end{matrix}\right)\sum_{{\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{j}=\alpha}\atop{|\gamma_{1}|\geq 1,\ldots,|\gamma_{j}|\geq 1}}d_{\gamma_{1}}\cdots d_{\gamma_{j}}\right)Z_{\alpha}\end{split}

and let
b_{\alpha}
be the coefficient of
Z_{\alpha}
.
Consider the weights
\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\varphi}=\{\mu^{\varphi}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
defined by
\mu^{\varphi}_{g_{0}}:=1
and
\mu^{\varphi}_{g_{i}\alpha}:=\sqrt{\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}}
. In this case, the associated Hilbert space is

F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}^{\varphi})=\left\{\zeta=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}:\ \|\zeta\|_{\boldsymbol{\omega}^{s}}^{2}:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{1}{b_{\alpha}}|c_{\alpha}|^{2}<\infty,\ c_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{C}}\right\}.

Note that when
\varphi=Z_{1}+\cdots+Z_{n}
we recover the weights considered in Example
4.1
when
s\geq 1
. We remark that if
d_{g_{i}}\geq 1
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, then
[W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}]
is a row contraction. This is due to the fact that

\mu^{\varphi}_{g_{i}\alpha}:=\sqrt{\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{g_{i}\alpha}}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{g_{i}}}}
(see
[
50
]
).

We mention that for all the examples presented above the conditions (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) are satisfied.
We remark that the operator
U:F^{2}(H_{n})\to F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
, defined by
U(e_{\alpha}):=\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}Z_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, is unitary and

UW_{i}=L_{Z_{i}}U,\ i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

where
W=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
is the weighted left multi-shift associated with the weights

\boldsymbol{\mu}=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
.
We also have
U\Lambda_{i}=R_{Z_{i}}U
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, where

{\Lambda}:=(\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n})
is the weighted right multi-shift associated with the weights
\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}=\{\widetilde{\mu}_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
defined on the full Fock space
F^{2}(H_{n})
by
\Lambda_{i}e_{\alpha}:=\widetilde{\mu}_{\alpha g_{i}}e_{\alpha g_{i}}
, where

\widetilde{\mu}_{\alpha g_{i}}:=\frac{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha g_{i},g_{0})}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},

and
\widetilde{\mu}_{g_{0}}:=1
. In view of the correspondence
\mu_{\alpha}\mapsto b_{\alpha}
mentioned above, we remark that
\widetilde{\mu}_{\alpha g_{i}}=\sqrt{\frac{b_{\alpha}}{b_{\alpha g_{i}}}}
for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and

b_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, where

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{\mu}}(\beta,g_{0}):=\begin{cases}\widetilde{\mu}_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{p}}}\widetilde{\mu}_{g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{p-1}}}\cdots\widetilde{\mu}_{g_{i_{1}}}&\text{if}\ \beta=g_{i_{1}}\cdots g_{i_{p}}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\\
1&\text{if}\ \beta=g_{0}.\end{cases}

The considerations above will allow us to represent injective weighted left multi-shifts

W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
as ordinary multiplications by
Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}
on a Hilbert space of formal power series, which leads naturally to analytic function theory in several complex variables. We will use these two viewpoints interchangeable as convenient. One of the goal for the remainder of the paper is to analyze the extent to which our noncommutative formal power series represent analytic functions in several noncommutative (resp. commutative) variables.

A formal power series
\varphi=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}a_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
is called
right multiplier
on
F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
and we denote
\varphi\in{\mathcal{M}}^{r}(F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
if, for any

\zeta=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}d_{\beta}Z_{\beta}
in
F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
, we have

\zeta\varphi:=\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\left(\sum_{\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},\beta\alpha=\gamma}d_{\beta}a_{\alpha}\right)Z_{\gamma}\in F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}).

Due to the closed graph theorem, the right multiplication operator

R_{\varphi}:F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
defined by
R_{\varphi}\zeta:=\zeta\varphi
is a bounded linear operator. The (right) multiplier norm of
\varphi
is given by

\|\varphi\|^{{\mathcal{M}}^{r}}:=\|R_{\varphi}\|
.
Note that

{\mathcal{M}}^{r}(F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
becomes a Banach algebra with respect to the multiplier norm.
Similarly, we introduce the the set of
left multipliers
on
F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
and denote it by
{\mathcal{M}}^{\ell}(F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
. In this case, the left multiplication operator

L_{\varphi}:F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
is defined by
L_{\varphi}\zeta:=\varphi\zeta
and it is a bounded linear operator. The (left) multiplier norm of
\varphi
is given by

\|\varphi\|^{{\mathcal{M}}^{\ell}}:=\|L_{\varphi}\|
.

As in the proof of Theorem 4.3 from
[
49
]
, one can show that a bounded operator
X:F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
satisfies the relation

XL_{Z_{i}}=L_{Z_{i}}X,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

if and only if there is
\varphi\in{\mathcal{M}}^{r}(F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
such that

X=R_{\varphi}
. Similarly, one can prove that a bounded operator
X:F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
satisfies the relation

XR_{Z_{i}}=R_{Z_{i}}X,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

if and only if the is there is
\varphi\in{\mathcal{M}}^{l}(F^{2}((\boldsymbol{\mu}))
such that

X=L_{\varphi}
.
Consequently, we have

\begin{split}\{W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}\}^{\prime}&=R^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu}):=\{{U}^{*}R_{\varphi}U:\ \varphi\in{\mathcal{M}}^{r}(F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))\}\\
\{\Lambda_{1},\ldots,\Lambda_{n}\}^{\prime}&=F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu}):=\{{U}^{*}L_{\varphi}U:\ \varphi\in{\mathcal{M}}^{l}(F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))\}\end{split}

where
′
stands for the commutant.
Note that a power series
\zeta=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}
is in
{\mathcal{M}}^{l}(F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
if and only if

\zeta(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})p:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}p\in F^{2}(H_{n})
for any polynomial

Theorem 4.7

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

\varphi\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\ell}(F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))

\|L_{\varphi}\|=\|\varphi(L_{Z_{1}},\ldots,L_{Z_{n}})\|\leq\sup\{\|\varphi(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\|:\ \|(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\|<\|L_{Z}\|\}.

Proof.

If
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
, then
\varphi_{\lambda}
is a bounded linear functional on
F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
and the Riesz representation theorem implies the existence of an element

k_{\lambda}\in F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
such that
\varphi_{\lambda}(f)=\left<f,k_{\lambda}\right>_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
for any
f\in F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})

\left<Z_{\alpha},k_{\lambda}\right>_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}=\lambda_{\alpha}
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, we deduce that

k_{\lambda}=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}Z_{\alpha}
.
Since
k_{\lambda}\in F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
, we must have
\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{|\lambda_{\alpha}|^{2}}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}<\infty
. The converse is obvious.

To prove the second equality in the part (i), let
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
and note that

\begin{split}\left<f,L_{Z_{i}}^{*}k_{\lambda}\right>_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}&=\left<L_{Z_{i}}f,\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}b_{\alpha}Z_{\alpha}\right>_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\\
&=\lambda_{i}\left<Z_{i}f,k_{\lambda}\right>_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}=\left<Z_{i}f,\bar{\lambda}_{i},k_{\lambda}\right>_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\end{split}

for any
f\in F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Consequently,
L_{Z_{i}}^{*}k_{\lambda}=\bar{\lambda}_{i},k_{\lambda}
which shows that

(\bar{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\lambda}_{n})\in\sigma_{p}(L_{Z_{1}}^{*},\ldots,L_{Z_{n}}^{*})
and

(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\sigma_{p}(L_{Z_{1}}^{*},\ldots,L_{Z_{n}}^{*})
.

Conversely, assume that
(\bar{\lambda}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\lambda}_{n})\in\sigma_{p}(L_{Z_{1}}^{*},\ldots,L_{Z_{n}}^{*})
and let
h\in F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
,
h\neq 0
, with the property that
L_{Z_{i}}^{*}h=\bar{\lambda}_{i}h
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Consider the bounded linear functional

\psi:F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to{\mathbb{C}}
given by
\psi(f):=\left<f,wh\right>_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
with
w\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
w\neq 0
, to be determined. Note that

\begin{split}\psi(Z_{i}f)=\left<Z_{i}f,wh\right>_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}=\left<f,wL_{Z_{i}}^{*}h\right>_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}=\lambda_{i}\left<f,wh\right>_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}=\lambda_{i}\psi(f)\end{split}

for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}

\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
with
|\alpha|\geq 1
. Since
\psi\neq 0
, we must have
\psi(1)\neq 0
. Setting
w:=\frac{1}{\overline{\left<1,h\right>_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}}}
, we have
\psi(1)=1
and, consequently,

\psi(p)=p(\lambda)
for any
p\in{\mathbb{C}}\left<Z_{1},\ldots,Z_{n}\right>
. Therefore,
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
which completes the proof of part (i).

To prove part (ii), note that

g:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}|c_{\alpha}|Z_{\alpha}
is also in
F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
. If
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
, then,
setting

g_{m}:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|\leq m}|c_{\alpha}|Z_{\alpha}
, we deduce that

g_{m}(\lambda)=\varphi_{\lambda}(g_{m})=\left<g_{m},k_{\lambda}\right>_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}\to\varphi_{\lambda}(g)
as
m\to\infty
. Consequently, the series
\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}|c_{\alpha}|\lambda_{\alpha}
is convergent for any
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})

(\lambda_{1}e^{i\theta_{1}},\ldots,\lambda_{n}e^{i\theta_{n}})\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
for any
\theta_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}|c_{\alpha}||\lambda_{\alpha}|
is convergent for any
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})

\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
,

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|\leq m}c_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}=\varphi_{\lambda}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|\leq m}c_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}\right)\to\varphi_{\lambda}(f)=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}

as
m\to\infty

f(\lambda)=\varphi_{\lambda}(f)=\left<f,k_{\lambda}\right>_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
for any
f\in F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
and
\|k_{\lambda}\|_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}=\left(\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{|\lambda_{\alpha}|^{2}}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}\right)^{1/2}

for any
f\in F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
.

Now, we prove part (iii). Assume that
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and the series

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|=k}c_{\alpha}\lambda_{\alpha}

F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})

Define the linear functional on

To prove part (iv), note that

Proposition 4.9

Let
W=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})\in B(F^{2}(H_{n}))
be the weighted left multi-shift associated with
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|\geq 1}

(i)

The joint point spectrum

\sigma_{p}(W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*})
coincides with the set

\{\lambda:=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}:\ \sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{|\lambda_{\alpha}|^{2}}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}}<\infty\}.

and the joint eigenvectors for the operators
W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*}
are precisely
the vectors

z_{\lambda}:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}}e_{\alpha},

where
\lambda:=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\sigma_{p}(W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*})
. In this case, we have

W_{i}^{*}z_{\lambda}=\bar{\lambda}_{i}z_{\lambda},\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

(ii)

The joint point spectrum

\sigma_{p}(W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*})
is a complete Reinhardt set in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
containing the origin and

\sigma_{p}(W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*})\subset\{(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}:\ (|\lambda_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|\lambda_{n}|^{2})^{1/2}\leq r(W)\},

where
r(W)
is the joint spectral radius of
W=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
.

(iii)

If
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
is such that
\|\lambda\|=\|W\|
, then
\lambda\notin\sigma_{p}(W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*})
.

(iv)

The joint point spectrum
\sigma_{p}(W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*})
contains a ball centered at zero if and only if

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|=k}\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}}\right)^{1/k}<\infty.

In particular, the later relation holds if
the weights are bounded above from zero.

Proof.

As mentioned before, the operator
U:F^{2}(H_{n})\to F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
, defined by
U(e_{\alpha}):=\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}Z_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, is unitary and

UW_{i}=L_{Z_{i}}U,\ i\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

where
W=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
is the weighted left multi-shift associated with the weights
\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
. Now item (i) can be extracted from Theorem
4.7
. For the benefit of the reader, we present another proof.

Let
z=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\beta}e_{\beta}\in F^{2}(H_{n})
,
z\neq 0
, and assume that

W_{i}^{*}z=\bar{\lambda}_{i}z
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
for some
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
Using the definition of the weighted left multi-shift, we deduce that

\begin{split}c_{\alpha}&=\left<z,e_{\alpha}\right>=\left<z,e_{\alpha}\right>=\left<z,\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}}W_{\alpha}1\right>\\
&=\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}}\left<W_{\alpha}^{*}z,1\right>=\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}}\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}\left<z,1\right>=\frac{c_{g_{0}}\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}}\end{split}

for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
.
Consequently,
z=c_{g_{0}}\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}}e_{\alpha}\in F^{2}(H_{n})
, which implies

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{|\lambda_{\alpha}|^{2}}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}}<\infty.

Conversely, assume that
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
satisfies the later relation. Then
z_{\lambda}:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}}e_{\alpha}
is in the full Fock space
F^{2}(H_{n})
.
Using the fact that

\boldsymbol{\mu}(g_{i}\gamma,g_{0})=\mu_{g_{i}\gamma}\boldsymbol{\mu}(\gamma,g_{0})
, we obtain

\begin{split}W_{i}^{*}z_{\lambda}&=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}}W_{i}^{*}e_{\alpha}=\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{g_{i}\gamma}}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(g_{i}\gamma,g_{0})}}\mu_{g_{i}\gamma}e_{\gamma}\\
&=\bar{\lambda}_{i}\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}}e_{\alpha}=\bar{\lambda}_{i}z_{\lambda}\end{split}

for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. This completes the proof of item (i).

To prove part (ii),
fix
\lambda:=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\sigma_{p}(W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*})
and
note that

\begin{split}\left<\sum_{|\alpha|=k}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}z_{\lambda},z_{\lambda}\right>&=\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\|W_{\alpha}^{*}z_{\lambda}\|^{2}\\
&=\|z_{\lambda}\|^{2}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}|\lambda_{\alpha}|^{2}=\|z_{\lambda}\|^{2}(|\lambda_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|\lambda_{n}|^{2})^{k}\end{split}

and, consequently,

(|\lambda_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|\lambda_{n}|^{2})^{1/2}\leq\left\|\sum_{|\alpha|=k}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}\right\|^{1/2k}

for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Hence, we deduce that
(|\lambda_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|\lambda_{n}|^{2})^{1/2}\leq r(W)
. The fact that
\sigma_{p}(W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*})
is a complete Reinhardt set in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
containing the origin is obvious due to part (i).

To prove item (iii), let
\lambda:=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

\left\|\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}W_{\beta}W_{\beta}^{*}\right\|=\sup_{\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\beta|=k}\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,\alpha)^{2}\geq\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,g_{0})^{2}

we deduce that

\begin{split}\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{|\lambda_{\alpha}|^{2}}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}}&\geq\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{|\lambda_{\alpha}|^{2}}{\left\|\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|=k}W_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}^{*}\right\|}\\
&\geq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(|\lambda_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|\lambda_{n}|^{2})^{k}}{\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}W_{i}^{*}\right\|^{k}}\end{split}

If
\|\lambda\|=\|W\|
, the later series is divergent, which proves that
\lambda\notin\sigma_{p}(W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*})
.

If the joint point spectrum
\sigma_{p}(W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*})
contains a ball centered at zero, then there is
r_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}}\right)z^{k}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{r_{0}^{|\alpha|}}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}}<\infty

for any complex number
z
with
|z|\leq r_{0}
. Standard arguments concerning the radius of convergence of a power series show that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|=k}\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}}\right)^{1/k}\leq\frac{1}{r_{0}}.

Conversely, assume that the later relation holds for some
r_{0}>0
. Then there exists
t_{0}>0
such that

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}}\right)t_{0}^{k}<\infty
. Now, it is clear that, for any

, we have

, which completes the proof of item (iv).

Now, assume that there exists

\mu_{\alpha}\geq M
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
.
Then and
\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})\geq M^{k}

and

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{|\lambda_{\alpha}|^{2}}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{M^{2k}}(|\lambda_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|\lambda_{n}|^{2})^{k}<\infty

for any

with

Theorem 4.14
.

A map
\Phi:F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to{\mathbb{C}}
is a
w^{*}
-continuous multiplicative linear functional if and
only if there exists
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
such that

\Phi(\psi(W))=\psi(\lambda),\qquad\psi(W)\in F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu}).

In this case, we have

\psi(\lambda)=\left<\psi(W)1,z_{\lambda}\right>=\left<\psi(W)u_{\lambda},u_{\lambda}\right>\ \text{ and }\ |\psi(\lambda)|\leq\|\psi(W)\|,

where
u_{\lambda}:=\frac{z_{\lambda}}{\|z_{\lambda}\|}
and

z_{\lambda}:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}}e_{\alpha}.

Proof.

First, we prove that if
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
, then the map
\Phi_{\lambda}:F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to{\mathbb{C}}
defined by

\Phi_{\lambda}(\psi(W)):=\psi(\lambda),\qquad\psi(W)\in F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu}),

is a
w^{*}
-continuous multiplicative linear
functional, and

\Phi_{\lambda}(\psi(W))=\left<\psi(W)1,z_{\lambda}\right>,\qquad\psi(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})\in F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu}).

Indeed, if
\psi(W):=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\beta}W_{\beta}

is in the Hardy algebra
F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
, then, due to Theorem
4.7
and the identification of multiplier algebra
{\mathcal{M}}^{l}(F^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
with
F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
, we have
\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}|c_{\beta}||\lambda_{\beta}|<\infty
and

\begin{split}\left<\psi(W)1,z_{\lambda}\right>&=\left<\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\beta}\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,g_{0})e_{\beta},\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,g_{0})}\overline{\lambda}_{\beta}e_{\beta}\right>\\
&=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\beta}\lambda_{\beta}=\psi(\lambda).\end{split}

Hence,
\Phi_{\lambda}
is a
w^{*}
-continuous multiplicative linear
functional.
Moreover, for each
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
, we have

\begin{split}\left<\psi(W)^{*}z_{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,g_{0})e_{\beta}\right>&=\left<z_{\lambda},\psi(W)W_{\beta}(1)\right>\\
&=\overline{\lambda_{\beta}\psi(\lambda)}=\left<\overline{\psi(\lambda)}z_{\lambda},\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,g_{0})e_{\beta}\right>.\end{split}

Hence, we deduce that

\psi(W)^{*}z_{\lambda}=\overline{\varphi(\lambda)}z_{\lambda}
and, consequently,

\begin{split}\left<\psi(W)u_{\lambda},u_{\lambda}\right>&=\frac{1}{\|z_{\lambda}\|^{2}}\left<z_{\lambda},\psi(W)^{*}z_{\lambda}\right>\\
&=\frac{1}{\|z_{\lambda}\|^{2}}\left<z_{\lambda},\overline{\psi(\lambda)}z_{\lambda}\right>=\psi(\lambda).\end{split}

Now, let
\Phi:F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to{\mathbb{C}}
be a
w^{*}
-continuous multiplicative linear functional.
Note that
J:=\ker\Phi
is a
w^{*}
-closed two-sided ideal of

F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
of codimension one. The subspace

{\mathcal{M}}_{J}:=\overline{J(1)}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})
has codimension one and
{\mathcal{M}}_{J}+{\mathbb{C}}1=F^{2}(H_{n})
. Indeed, assume that there exists a vector
y\in({\mathcal{M}}_{J}+{\mathbb{C}}1)^{\perp}
,
\|y\|=1
. Then we can choose a polynomial

p:=p(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})\in F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
such that

\|p(1)-y\|<1
. Since
p-\varphi(p)I\in\ker\varphi=J
, we have

p(1)-\varphi(p)\in{\mathcal{M}}_{J}
. Since
y\perp{\mathcal{M}}_{J}+{\mathbb{C}}1
, we deduce
that

\begin{split}1&=\|y\|=\left<y-\varphi(p),y\right>\leq|\left<h-p(1),y\right>|+|\left<p(1)-\varphi(p),y\right>|\\
&=|\left<h-p(1),y\right>|<1,\end{split}

which is a contradiction. Therefore,
M_{J}
is an invariant subspace
under
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
and has codimension one. Due to Theorem

4.9
, there exists
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
such
that
{\mathcal{M}}_{J}=\{z_{\lambda}\}^{\perp}
.

Hence,
J:=\ker\Phi\subseteq\ker\Phi_{\lambda}\subset F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
. Since both
\ker\Phi
and
\ker\Phi_{\lambda}
are
w^{*}
-closed two-sided maximal ideals of

F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
of codimension one, we must have
\ker\Phi=\ker\Phi_{\lambda}
. Therefore,
\Phi=\Phi_{\lambda}

and the proof is complete.
∎

5.
Functional calculus for arbitrary
n
-tuples of operators

Let
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
be a sequence of strictly positive real numbers satisfying the boundedness conditions (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) and let

F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
be the noncommutative Hardy algebra associated.
Define the noncommutative set

{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}({\mathcal{H}}):=\left\{(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}:\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}X_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}^{*}<\infty\right\}.

We remark that if

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|=k}\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{4}}}\right)^{1/2k}<\infty,

then the noncommutative Reinhardt set
{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}({\mathcal{H}})
contains a ball centered at the origin in
B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
.
Indeed, according to
[
45
]
, the condition above implies that the formal series
\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}}Z_{\alpha}
is a free holomorphic function on a neighborhood of the origin in
B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
. Consequently,
if
\rho
denotes the reciprocal of the
\limsup
above and
0<t_{0}<\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{n}}
,
then
(t_{0}I_{\mathcal{H}},\ldots t_{0}I_{\mathcal{H}})\in[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{\rho}
and

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}a_{\alpha}t_{0}^{k}<\infty
. Take
0<\epsilon<\sqrt{t_{0}}
and assume that
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\in[B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}]_{\epsilon}
. Then we have

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}}\|X_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}^{*}\|\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{1}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}}\epsilon^{2}k\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}a_{\alpha}t_{0}^{k}<\infty,

which proves our assertion.

In what follows, we present a
w^{*}
-continuous
F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
-functional calculus for the elements in the noncommutative Reinhardt set
{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}({\mathcal{H}})
, where
{\mathcal{H}}
is a separable Hilbert space.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}({\mathcal{H}})
and let
\Psi_{T}:F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to B({\mathcal{H}})
be defined by

\Psi_{T}(\varphi(W))=\varphi(T)=:\text{\rm SOT-}\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\left(1-\frac{|\alpha|}{N+1}\right)c_{\alpha}T_{\alpha},

where
\varphi(W)\in F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
has the Fourier representation
\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}}c_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}
. Then
\Psi_{T}
has the following properties.

(i)

\Psi_{T}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}c_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}\right)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}c_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(ii)

\Psi_{T}
is sequentially WOT-(resp. SOT-) continuous.

(iii)

\Psi_{T}
is a completely bounded algebra homomorphism and

\|\Psi_{T}\|_{cb}\leq\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|=k}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}T_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}^{*}\right\|^{1/2}.

(iv)

\Psi_{T}
is
w^{*}
-continuous.

(v)

r(\varphi(T))\leq r(\varphi(W))
for any
\varphi(W)\in F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
, where
r(X)
denotes the spectral radius of
X
.

Proof.

According to to Corollary
2.2
, if
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}({\mathcal{H}})
, then there is a joint invariant subspace
{\mathcal{M}}\subset F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{H}}
under the operators
W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*}
such that

(5.1)

T_{i}^{*}=X^{-1}(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I)|_{\mathcal{M}}X,\qquad i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

Define the map
\Phi_{T}:F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to B({\mathcal{H}})
by setting

(5.2)

\Phi_{T}(\varphi(W)):=X^{*}P_{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi(W)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})|_{\mathcal{M}}(X^{*})^{-1},\qquad\varphi(W)\in F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu}).

Note that relation (
5.1
) implies

T_{\alpha}=X^{*}P_{\mathcal{M}}(W_{\alpha}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})|_{\mathcal{M}}(X^{*})^{-1}
for any

\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
. This implies

\Psi_{T}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}c_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}\right)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}c_{\alpha}T_{\alpha}
for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Now, we prove that
\Phi_{T}
is sequentially WOT-(resp. SOT-) continuous. Let
\{\zeta_{k}(W)\}_{k}\subset F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
be a sequence such that
WOT-
\lim_{k\to\infty}\zeta_{k}(W)=\varphi(W)
.
Since the WOT and
w^{*}
topologies concide on bounded set, we have

w^{*}
-
\lim_{k\to\infty}\zeta_{k}(W)=\varphi(W)
which implies

w^{*}
-
\lim_{k\to\infty}(\zeta_{k}(W)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})=\varphi(W)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}
.
Hence, WOT-
\lim_{k\to\infty}(\zeta_{k}(W)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})=\varphi(W)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}
and
WOT-
\lim_{k\to\infty}\Phi_{T}(\zeta_{k}(W))=\Phi_{T}(\varphi(W))
.
Similarly, if SOT-
\lim_{k\to\infty}\zeta_{k}(W)=\varphi(W)
, then
\|\zeta_{k}(W)\|\leq M
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, for some
M>0
. Consequently, we have
SOT-
\lim_{k\to\infty}(\zeta_{k}(W)\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})=\varphi(W)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}
which implies that
SOT-
\lim_{k\to\infty}\Phi_{T}(\zeta_{k}(W))=\Phi_{T}(\varphi(W))
. This proves our assertion.

Let
\{\varphi_{\iota}(W)\}_{\iota}
be a net in
F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
such that

w^{*}
-
\lim_{\iota}\varphi_{\iota}(W)=\varphi(W)\in F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
. Then we deduce that

w^{*}
-
\lim_{\iota}\varphi_{\iota}(W)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}=\varphi(W)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}
and, consequently,

w^{*}\text{-}\lim_{\iota}X^{*}P_{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi_{\iota}(W)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})|_{\mathcal{M}}(X^{*})^{-1}=X^{*}P_{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi(W)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})|_{\mathcal{M}}(X^{*})^{-1}.

This proves that

w^{*}
-
\lim_{\iota}\Phi_{T}(\varphi_{\iota}(W))=\Phi_{T}(\varphi(W))
. Therefore,
\Phi_{T}
is
w^{*}
-continuous.

Let
[\varphi_{i,j}(W)]_{s\times s}
be an
s\times s
matrix with entries in the Hardy algebra
F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
. Note that relation (
5.2
) implies

[\Phi_{T}(\varphi_{ij}(W))]_{s\times s}:=(\oplus_{1}^{s}X^{*}P_{\mathcal{M}})[\Phi_{T}(\varphi_{ij}(W))]_{s\times s}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})|_{\mathcal{M}}]_{s\times s}(\oplus_{1}^{s}(X^{*})^{-1}).

Hence,

\|[\Phi_{T}(\varphi_{ij}(W))]_{s\times s}\|\leq\|X\|\|X^{-1}\|[\Phi_{T}(\varphi_{ij}(W))]_{s\times s}\|.

Therefore,
\Phi_{T}
is a completely bounded linear map and
\|\Phi_{T}\|_{cb}\leq\|X\|\|X^{-1}\|
. Using relations (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), it is easy to see that
\Phi_{T}
is a homomorphism on the polynomials in
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
. Since these polynomials are
w^{*}
-dense in
F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
and
\Phi_{T}
is
w^{*}
-continuous, an approximation argument shows that
\Phi_{T}
is a homomorphism on
F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
.

Now, let
\varphi(W)\in F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
have the Fourier representation
\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}}c_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}
and define

p_{N}(W):=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq N}\left(1-\frac{|\alpha|}{N+1}\right)c_{\alpha}W_{\alpha}.

According to Theorem
4.4
,
\text{\rm SOT-}\lim_{N\to\infty}p_{N}(W)=\varphi(W)

and
\|p_{N}(W)\|\leq\|\varphi(W)\|
for any
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
Then
\text{\rm SOT-}\lim_{N\to\infty}(p_{N}(W)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})=\varphi(W)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}
.
On the other hand, due to relation (
5.2
), we have

p_{N}(T)=X^{*}P_{\mathcal{M}}(p_{N}(W)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})|_{\mathcal{M}}(X^{*})^{-1}.

Hence, we deduce that

\text{\rm SOT-}\lim_{N\to\infty}p_{N}(T)=X^{*}P_{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi(W)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})|_{\mathcal{M}}(X^{*})^{-1}=\Phi_{T}(\varphi(W)).

Therefore,
\Psi_{T}(\varphi(W))=\Phi_{T}(\varphi(W))
for any
\varphi(W)\in F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
.

It remains to prove part(v). To this end, note that due to the properties of the map
\Psi_{T}
, we have

\varphi(T)^{k}=X^{*}P_{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi(W)^{k}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})|_{\mathcal{M}}(X^{*})^{-1},\qquad k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Hence we deduce that
\|\varphi(T)^{k}\|^{1/k}\leq\|X\|^{1/k}\|X^{-1}\|^{1/k}\|\varphi(W)^{k}\|^{1/k}
. Passing to the limit as
k\to\infty
, we conclude that
r(\varphi(T))\leq r(\varphi(W))
.
The proof is complete.
∎

.

where

and

. When

n

n

Corollary 5.3
.

In the setting of Theorem

, the joint point spectrum
\sigma_{p}(W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*})
satisfies the following relations

({\mathbb{C}}^{n})_{r(T)}\subset\sigma_{p}(W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*})\subset\overline{({\mathbb{C}}^{n})}_{r(W)}.

4.9
, the joint point spectrum

\{\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}:\ \sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{|\lambda_{\alpha}|^{2}}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}}<\infty\}

and
\sigma_{p}(W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*})\subset\overline{({\mathbb{C}}^{n})}_{r(W)}

Analysing the convergence of the series

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(k+1)^{2}\left\|\sum\limits_{\sigma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\sigma|=|\alpha|}T_{\sigma}T_{\sigma}^{*}\right\|}(|\lambda_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|\lambda_{n}|^{2})^{k}

and taking into account that

r(T)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\|\sum\limits_{\sigma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\sigma|=k}T_{\sigma}T_{\sigma}^{*}\right\|^{1/2k}
, one can easily deduce the inclusion
({\mathbb{C}}^{n})_{r(T)}\subset\sigma_{p}(W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*})
.
∎

In what follows we present a spectral version of the Schwarz lemma for the noncommutative Hardy algebra
F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
be a sequence of strictly positive real numbers with
\mu_{\alpha}\geq M>0
for any
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
and satisfying relations (
4.1
) and (
4.2
).
If
\varphi(W)\in F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
has the properties that
\varphi(0)=0
and
\|\varphi(W)\|\leq 1
, then

r(\varphi(X))\leq r(X),\qquad X\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}({\mathcal{H}}).

Proof.

Let
\varphi(W)\in F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
have the Fourier representation

\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+},|\alpha|\geq 1}c_{\beta}W_{\beta}
. Then for any polynomial
p\in\Span\left\{e_{\alpha}:\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\right\}
, we have

\varphi(W)p=\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}\left(\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{g_{i}\gamma}W_{\gamma}p\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}\Phi_{i}(W)p

where
\Phi_{i}(W)p:=\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{g_{i}\gamma}W_{\gamma}p
.
Since the weights
\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}
are bounded away from zero, we have
W_{i}=S_{i}D_{i}
, where
D_{i}
is an invertible positive diagonal operator. Note that

\|W_{i}^{*}\varphi(W)\|\|p\|\geq\|W_{i}^{*}\varphi(W)p\|=\|D_{i}^{2}\Phi_{i}(W)p\|\geq\frac{1}{\|D_{i}^{-1}\|^{2}}\|\Phi_{i}(W)p\|

for any
p\in\Span\left\{e_{\alpha}:\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}\right\}
. Hence, we deduce that
\Phi_{i}(W)\in F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and we have the Gleason type decomposition

(5.3)

\varphi(W)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}W_{i}\Phi_{i}(W).

Taking into account that the operators
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
have orthogonal ranges, we deduce that

\|\varphi(W)^{*}\varphi(W)\|=\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Phi_{i}(W)^{*}D_{i}^{2}\Phi_{i}(W)\|\geq M\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Phi_{i}(W)^{*}\Phi_{i}(W)\|

for some constant
M>0
.
Due to Theorem
5.1
, relation (
5.3
) implies

\varphi(X)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}\Phi_{i}(X)
for any
X\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}({\mathcal{H}})
. The same theorem shows that the map
\Psi_{T}
is completely bounded, which show that

\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}\Phi_{1}(X)\\
\vdots\\
\Phi_{n}(X)\end{matrix}\right]\right\|\leq K\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}\Phi_{1}(W)\\
\vdots\\
\Phi_{n}(W)\end{matrix}\right]\right\|

for some constant
K>0
. Using this inequality, we obtain

\begin{split}\|\varphi(X)\|&\leq\|[X_{1}\cdots X_{n}]\|\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}\Phi_{1}(X)\\
\vdots\\
\Phi_{n}(X)\end{matrix}\right]\right\|\leq K\|[X_{1}\cdots X_{n}]\|\left\|\left[\begin{matrix}\Phi_{1}(W)\\
\vdots\\
\Phi_{n}(W)\end{matrix}\right]\right\|\\
&=K\|[X_{1}\cdots X_{n}]\|\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Phi_{i}(W)^{*}\Phi_{i}(W)\|^{1/2}\\
&\leq\frac{K}{\sqrt{M}}\|[X_{1}\cdots X_{n}]\|\|\varphi(W)^{*}\varphi(W)\|^{1/2}\\
&\leq\frac{K}{\sqrt{M}}\|[X_{1}\cdots X_{n}]\|\end{split}

In a similar manner, we can show that

\|\varphi(X)^{k}\|\leq C\|[X_{\alpha}:\ |\alpha|=k]||

for some constant
C>0
which depends only on
\boldsymbol{\mu}
and
X
. Hence, we deduce that

\ \|\varphi(X)^{k}\|^{1/k}\leq C^{1/k}\left\|\sum_{|\alpha|=k}X_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}^{*}\right\|^{1/2k},\qquad k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Taking
k\to\infty
, we obtain

r(\varphi(X))\leq r(X)

and complete the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
W=(W_{1},\ldots,W_{n})
be the weighted left multi-shift associated with
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{|\beta|\geq 1}
, where
\mu_{\beta}>0
and conditions (
4.1
), (
4.2
) are satisfied. If

is a row power bounded with bound

, then the map
\Phi_{W}:{\mathcal{A}}_{n}\to B(F^{2}(H_{n}))
defined by

can be extended to a
w^{*}
-continuous completely bounded map
\Phi_{W}:F^{\infty}(H_{n})\to F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
with
\|\Phi_{W}\|_{cb}\leq M
such that

for any

6.
Function theory on Reinhardt domains and multiplier algebras

Let
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{|\beta|\geq 1}
be a weight sequence, where

\mu_{\beta}>0
and conditions (
4.1
), (
4.2
) are satisfied.
For each
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
and each
n
-tuple
{\bf k}:=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
, where
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:=\{0,1,\ldots\}
, let
\lambda^{\bf k}:=\lambda_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{n}^{k_{n}}
. For each
{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, we denote

\Lambda_{\bf k}:=\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}:\ \lambda_{\alpha}=\lambda^{\bf k}\text{ for all }\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\}.

If
{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
, we define the vector

y^{(\bf k)}:=\frac{1}{\omega_{\bf k}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda_{\bf k}}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}e_{\alpha}\in F^{2}(H_{n}),\quad\text{ where }\ \omega_{\bf k}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda_{\bf k}}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}

and
\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})
is defined in Proposition
1.1
.
Note that the set
\{y^{(\bf k)}:\ {\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}\}
consists of orthogonal vectors in
F^{2}(H_{n})
and

\|y^{(\bf k)}\|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\omega_{\bf k}}}
. We denote by

F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
the closed span of these vectors, and call it the

symmetric weighted Fock space
associated with the weight sequence

\boldsymbol{\mu}=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{|\beta|\geq 1}
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{|\beta|\geq 1}
be a weight sequence
with the property that
{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
contains a neighborhood of the origin and let
W=(W_{1}.\ldots,W_{m})
be the associated weighted left multi-shift.
If

J_{c}
is the
w^{*}
-closed two-sided ideal of the Hardy algebra

F^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
generated by the commutators

W_{i}W_{j}-W_{j}W_{i},\qquad i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\},

then the following statements hold.

(i)

F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})=\overline{\text{\rm span}}\{z_{\lambda}:\ \lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\}=F^{2}(H_{n})\ominus\overline{J_{c}(1)}
, where
z_{\lambda}
is defined in Proposition
4.9
.

(ii)

The symmetric weighted Fock space
F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
can be
identified with the Hilbert space
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}({\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
of all
functions
\varphi:{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to{\mathbb{C}}
which admit a power
series representation
\varphi(\lambda)=\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}c_{\bf k}\lambda^{\bf k}
with

\|\varphi\|_{2}=\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}|c_{\bf k}|^{2}\frac{1}{\omega_{\bf k}}<\infty.

More precisely, every element
\varphi=\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}c_{\bf k}y^{(\bf k)}
in
F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
has a functional
representation on
{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
given by

\varphi(\lambda):=\left<\varphi,z_{\lambda}\right>=\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}c_{\bf k}\lambda^{\bf k},\quad\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}),

and

|\varphi(\lambda)|\leq\|\varphi\|_{2}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{|\lambda_{\alpha}|^{2}}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}\right)^{1/2},\quad\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}).

(iii)

{\mathbb{H}}^{2}({\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
is the reproducing kernel Hilbert space with kernel

\boldsymbol{\kappa}:{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\times{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to{\mathbb{C}}
defined by

\boldsymbol{\kappa}(\zeta,\lambda):=\left<z_{\lambda},z_{\zeta}\right>=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,g_{0})^{2}}\zeta_{\beta}\overline{\lambda}_{\beta}\quad\text{ for all }\ \lambda,\zeta\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}).

Proof.

First, we note that
\overline{\text{\rm span}}\{z_{\lambda}:\ \lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\}\subset F^{2}_{s}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
due to the fact that

z_{\lambda}:=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{\bar{\lambda}_{\alpha}}{{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}}e_{\alpha}=\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\bar{\lambda}^{\bf k}\omega_{\bf k}y^{({\bf k})}.

On the other hand, for any
\gamma,\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}
,
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and
{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
, we have

\begin{split}&\left<y^{({\bf k})},W_{\gamma}(W_{j}W_{i}-W_{i}W_{j})W_{\beta}1\right>\\
&\qquad=\frac{1}{\omega_{\bf k}}\left<\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda_{\bf k}}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}e_{\alpha},\boldsymbol{\mu}(\gamma g_{j}g_{i}\beta,g_{0})e_{\gamma g_{j}g_{i}\beta}-\boldsymbol{\mu}(\gamma g_{i}g_{j}\beta,g_{0})e_{\gamma g_{i}g_{j}\beta}\right>=0.\end{split}

Hence, we deduce that
F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\subset F^{2}(H_{n})\ominus\overline{J_{c}(1)}
. Now, assume that there is a vector
v=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}c_{\beta}e_{\beta}\in F^{2}(H_{n})\ominus\overline{J_{c}(1)}
such that
\left<v,z_{\lambda}\right>=0
for any
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
. Then we have

\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\left(\sum_{\beta\in\Lambda_{\bf k}}c_{\beta}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,g_{0})}\right)\lambda^{\bf k}=0,\qquad\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}).

Since
{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
contains a ball centered at the origin, the uniqueness of the power series representations of holomorphic functions on domains in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
implies

\sum_{\beta\in\Lambda_{\bf k}}c_{\beta}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,g_{0})}=0
for any
{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
.

Let
\beta_{0}=\gamma g_{j}g_{i}\omega\in\Lambda_{\bf k}
and let
\beta=\gamma g_{i}g_{j}\omega\in\Lambda_{\bf k}
, where

\gamma,\omega\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}

and
i\neq j
. Since
v\in F^{2}(H_{n})\ominus\overline{J_{c}(1)}
, we have

\left<v,W_{\gamma}(W_{j}W_{i}-W_{i}W_{j})W_{\omega}(1)\right>=0,

which implies

c_{\beta_{0}}\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta_{0},g_{0})=c_{\beta}\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,g_{0})
.
Since any element
\gamma\in\Lambda_{\bf k}
can be obtained from

\beta_{0}
by successive transpositions, repeating the above
argument, we deduce that

c_{\beta_{0}}\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta_{0},g_{0})=c_{\gamma}\boldsymbol{\mu}(\gamma,g_{0})

for all
\gamma\in\Lambda_{\bf k}.

Since
\sum_{\beta\in\Lambda_{\bf k}}c_{\beta}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\beta,g_{0})}=0
for any
{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
, we conclude that

c_{\beta}=0
for any
\beta\in\Lambda_{\bf k}
and
{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
, so
v=0
. Consequently,
we have
\overline{\text{\rm span}}\{z_{\lambda}:\ \lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\}=F^{2}(H_{n})\ominus\overline{J_{c}(1)}
, which completes the proof of part (i).

To prove part (ii), we remark that

\begin{split}\left<y^{({\bf k})},z_{\lambda}\right>&=\frac{1}{\omega_{\bf k}}\left<\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda_{\bf k}}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}e_{\alpha},z_{\lambda}\right>\\
&=\frac{1}{\omega_{\bf k}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda_{\bf k}}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}\lambda_{\alpha}=\lambda^{\bf k}\end{split}

for any
{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
and
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
.
This shows that every element
\psi=\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}c_{\bf k}y^{({\bf k})}\in F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
has a functional
representation on
{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
given by

\psi(\lambda):=\left<\psi,z_{\lambda}\right>=\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}c_{\bf k}\lambda^{\bf k}

and, due to Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,

|\psi(\lambda)|\leq\|\psi\|_{2}\left(\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}}\frac{|\lambda_{\alpha}|^{2}}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}\right)^{1/2}
. We remark that any function
\varphi\in{\mathbb{H}}^{2}({\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
is uniquely determined by the coefficients of its power series representation

\varphi(\lambda)=\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}c_{\bf k}\lambda^{\bf k}
due to the uniqueness of the power series representations of holomorphic functions on domains in
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
.
Now, it is clear that the map
U:F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to{\mathbb{H}}^{2}({\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
defined by

U\left(\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}c_{\bf k}y^{({\bf k})}\right):=\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}c_{\bf k}\lambda^{\bf k}

is a unitary operator. This completes the proof of part (ii). Note that part (iii) follows easily from (ii).
∎

We define the operator
B_{i}\in B(F^{2}_{s}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
by setting
B_{i}:=P_{F^{2}_{s}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}W_{i}|_{F^{2}_{s}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}
, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mu}=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{|\beta|\geq 1}
be a weight sequence

(iii)

the vector

(v)

\left<A1,z_{\lambda}\right>=\varphi(\lambda):=\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}c_{\bf k}\lambda^{\bf k}

Conversely, assume that
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

z\in F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
,
z\neq 0
, such that
B_{i}^{*}z=\bar{\lambda}_{i}z
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Since
B_{i}^{*}=W_{i}^{*}|_{F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}
, we also have
W_{i}^{*}z=\bar{\lambda}_{i}z
which, due to Proposition
4.9
, implies
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(W_{1}^{*},\ldots,W_{n}^{*})
. This proves item (ii).

To prove part (iii), note that, for any
{\bf k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
, we have

\begin{split}\left<B_{i}y^{({\bf k})},z_{\lambda}\right>&=\left<y^{({\bf k})},W_{i}^{*}z_{\lambda}\right>=\left<y^{({\bf k})},\bar{\lambda}_{i}z_{\lambda}\right>\\
&=\lambda_{i}\lambda^{({\bf k})}=\left<y^{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{j}+1,\ldots k_{n})},z_{\lambda}\right>\end{split}

for any
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
. Hence,
B_{i}y^{({\bf k})}=y^{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{j}+1,\ldots k_{n})}
which implies

A(y^{({\bf k})})=AB^{{\bf k}}1=B^{{\bf k}}A1
. This proves that any

A\in\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}\}^{\prime}
is uniquely determined by
A1\in F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
.
Setting
\varphi:=UA1\in{\mathbb{H}}^{2}({\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
, we complete the proof of part (iii).

To prove item (iv), assume that
A1=\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}c_{\bf k}y^{({\bf k})}\in F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
.
Note that

A(y^{({\bf p})})=B^{{\bf p}}A1=\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}c_{\bf k}B^{{\bf p}}y^{({\bf k})}=\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}c_{\bf k}y^{({\bf p}+{\bf k})},\qquad{\bf p}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}.

Now, one can easily deduce part (iv). The first part of item (v) is clear. To prove the second part, note that

\begin{split}\left<A^{*}z_{\lambda},y^{({\bf k})}\right>&=\left<z_{\lambda},Ay^{({\bf k})}\right>=\left<z_{\lambda},B^{{\bf k}}A1\right>\\
&=\left<(B^{{\bf k})^{*}}z_{\lambda},A1\right>=\left<\bar{\lambda}^{\bf k}z_{\lambda},\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}c_{\bf k}y^{({\bf k})}\right>\\
&=\left<\overline{\varphi(\lambda)}z_{\lambda},y^{({\bf k})}\right>\end{split}

for any
{\bf k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
. Consequently,

A^{*}z_{\lambda}=\overline{\varphi(\lambda)}z_{\lambda}
for any
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Under the notation of the theorem above, we call the formal series
\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}c_{\bf k}B^{\bf k}
the Fourier representation of
A
and note that

\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}c_{\bf k}B^{\bf k}y^{({\bf p})}=Ay^{({\bf p})}
for any
{\bf p}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
. This shows that
A
is uniquely determined by its Fourier representation.

Similarly to the proof of Theorem 9.2 from
[
51
]
, one can show that if
A\in\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}\}^{\prime}
has the Fourier representation
\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}c_{\bf k}B^{\bf k}
, then

A=\text{SOT-}\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n},|{\bf k}|\leq N}\left(1-\frac{|{\bf k}|}{N+1}\right)c_{\bf k}B^{\bf k},

\left\|\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n},|{\bf k}|\leq N}\left(1-\frac{|{\bf k}|}{N+1}\right)c_{\bf k}B^{\bf k}\right\|\leq\|A\|,\qquad N\in{\mathbb{N}},

and

\left\|\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n},|{\bf k}|=m}c_{\bf k}B^{\bf k}\right\|\leq\|A\|,\qquad m\in{\mathbb{N}}.

We introduce the Hardy algebra
F_{s}^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
to be the
w^{*}
-closed non-self-adjoint algebra generated by the operators
B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}
and the identity. Since
B_{i}B_{j}=B_{j}B_{i}
for any
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, one can use the results above to show that

F_{s}^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})=\{B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}\}^{\prime}=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}(B)}^{SOT}=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}(B)}^{WOT}=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}(B)}^{w^{*}},

where
{\mathcal{P}}(B)
stands for the algebra of all polynomials in
B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}
and the identity. Moreover,
F_{s}^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
is the sequential SOT-(resp. WOT-, w*) closure of
{\mathcal{P}}(B)
.

Using Theorem
6.2
and the above-mentioned results, one can easily prove the following.

Theorem 6.3
.

The Hardy algebra
F_{s}^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
can be identified with the multiplier algebra
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{H}}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
. Moreover, each
\varphi(B)\in F_{s}^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
has a unique Fourier representation
\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}c_{\bf k}B^{\bf k}
and

\varphi(B)=\text{\rm SOT-}\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n},|{\bf k}|\leq N}\left(1-\frac{|{\bf k}|}{N+1}\right)c_{\bf k}B^{\bf k}.

If
A\in B({\mathcal{H}})
then the set of all
invariant subspaces of
A
is denoted by
\text{\rm Lat}A
. For
any
{\mathcal{U}}\subset B({\mathcal{H}})
we define

\text{\rm Lat}\,{\mathcal{U}}=\bigcap_{A\in{\mathcal{U}}}\text{\rm Lat}\,A.

If
{\mathcal{S}}
is any collection of subspaces of
{\mathcal{H}}
,
then we define
\text{\rm Alg}\,{\mathcal{S}}
by setting
\text{\rm Alg}\,{\mathcal{S}}:=\{A\in B({\mathcal{H}}):\ {\mathcal{S}}\subset\text{\rm Lat}\,A\}.
We recall
that the algebra
{\mathcal{U}}\subset B({\mathcal{H}})
is
reflexive
if

{\mathcal{U}}=\text{\rm Alg Lat}\,{\mathcal{U}}
.

Theorem 6.4
.

The algebra
F_{s}^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
is reflexive.

Proof.

Let
A\in B(F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
be an operator that leaves
invariant all the invariant subspaces under the operators

B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}
. According to Theorem
6.2
, for any
\lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
, the subspace
{\mathbb{C}}z_{\lambda}
is invariant under
B_{1}^{*},\ldots,B_{n}^{*}
. Consequently,
A^{*}z_{\lambda}=\overline{\varphi(\lambda)}z_{\lambda}
for some function

\varphi:{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to{\mathbb{C}}
. Note that, if
f\in F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
,
then, due to Theorem
6.1
,
Af
has the functional
representation

\left<Af,z_{\lambda}\right>=\left<\psi,A^{*}z_{\lambda}\right>=\varphi(\lambda)\psi(\lambda)\quad\text{ for all }\ \lambda\in{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}).

In particular,

\left<A(1),z_{\lambda}\right>=\varphi(\lambda)
.
Therefore
\varphi
can be
identified with
A(1)\in F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
and the relation above shows that

\varphi\psi\in{\mathbb{H}}^{2}({\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
for any

\psi\in F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
. Therefore,
A\in{\mathcal{M}}(F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
and
\varphi\in{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{H}}^{2}({\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})))
. Since the Hardy algebra
F_{s}^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
is identified with the multiplier algebra
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{H}}^{2}({\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})))
, the proof is complete.
∎

7.
Functional calculus for commuting
n
-tuples of operators

Throughout this section, we assume that

\boldsymbol{\mu}=\{\mu_{\beta}\}_{|\beta|\geq 1}
is a weight sequence of strictly positive numbers satisfying the boundedness conditions (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) and having
the property that
{\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
is a subset of
{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
containing a neighborhood of the origin.
Let
B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}
be the compressions of
W_{1},\ldots,W_{n}
to the symmetric weighted Fock space
F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
, respectively. We recall that
F_{s}^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
is the
w^{*}
-closed non-self-adjoint algebra generated by the operators
B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}
and the identity, which is identified with the multiplier algebra
{\mathcal{M}}({\mathbb{H}}^{2}({\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu})))
. Under this identification, the
n
-tuple
(B_{1},\ldots,B_{n})
is unitarily equivalent to
(M_{\lambda_{1}},\ldots,M_{\lambda_{n}})
, where
M_{\lambda_{i}}
is the multiplication on
{\mathbb{H}}^{2}({\mathcal{D}}(\boldsymbol{\mu}))
by the coordinate function
\lambda_{i}
.

We recall from Section 5 that

\Lambda_{\bf k}:=\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{n}^{+}:\ \lambda_{\alpha}=\lambda^{\bf k}\text{ for all }\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}\},\qquad{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n},

and

y^{(\bf k)}:=\frac{1}{\omega_{\bf k}}\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda_{\bf k}}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})}e_{\alpha}\in F^{2}(H_{n}),\quad\text{ where }\ \omega_{\bf k}:=\sum_{\alpha\in\Lambda_{\bf k}}\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{\mu}(\alpha,g_{0})^{2}}.

The set
\{y^{(\bf k)}:\ {\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}\}
consists of orthogonal vectors in
F^{2}(H_{n})
and

\|y^{(\bf k)}\|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\omega_{\bf k}}}
, and the symmetric weighted Fock space
F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
is the closed span of these vectors.

We denote by

{\mathcal{D}}^{c}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}({\mathcal{H}})
the set all all
n
-tuples
X=(X_{1},\ldots,X_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}({\mathcal{H}})
such that
X_{i}X_{j}=X_{j}X_{i}
for any
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Note that
X\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}({\mathcal{H}})
if and only if
\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}\omega_{\bf k}T^{\bf k}{T^{\bf k}}^{*}
is WOT-convergent.
Let
{\mathcal{A}}_{s}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
be the norm closed non-self-adjoint algebra generated by
B_{1},\ldots,B_{n}
and the identity.

Theorem 7.1
.

If
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in B({\mathcal{H}})^{n}
is an
n
-tuple of commuting operators and there is a positive invertible operator
Q\in B({\mathcal{H}})
and positive constants
0<a\leq b
such that

aI\leq\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}\omega_{\bf k}T^{\bf k}Q{T^{\bf k}}^{*}\leq bI,

then

(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})
is jointly similar to

(P_{\mathcal{M}}(B_{1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})|_{\mathcal{M}},\ldots,P_{\mathcal{M}}(B_{n}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})|_{\mathcal{M}}),

where
{\mathcal{M}}\subset F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\otimes{\mathcal{H}}
is a joint invariant subspace under the operators
B_{i}^{*}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}
,
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
In this case, we have
r(T)\leq r(B)
and the map
\Phi_{T}:{\mathcal{A}}_{s}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to B(F^{2}(H_{n})
defined by

\Phi_{T}(p(B_{1},\ldots,B_{n})):=p(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})

is completely bounded and
\|\Phi_{T}\|_{cb}\leq\sqrt{\frac{b}{a}}
.

Proof.

A close look at the proof of Theorem
2.1
reveals that, if

T_{1},\ldots,T_{n}
are commuting operators, then the operator

K_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}:{\mathcal{H}}\to F^{2}(H_{n})\otimes{\mathcal{D}}
has the range in
F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\otimes{\mathcal{D}}
. Indeed, we have

K_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}h=\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}\omega_{\bf k}y^{(\bf k)}\otimes Q^{1/2}{T^{\bf k}}^{*}h\in F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\otimes{\mathcal{D}}.

Moreover, since
K_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}T_{i}^{*}=(W_{i}^{*}\otimes I)K_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
and
W_{i}^{*}|_{F^{2}_{s}(\boldsymbol{\mu})}=B_{i}^{*}
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, one can see that the subspace
{\mathcal{M}}:=K_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}{\mathcal{H}}\subset F_{s}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\otimes{\mathcal{D}}
is invariant under all
B_{i}^{*}\otimes I
and, consequently,

K_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}T_{i}^{*}=(B_{i}^{*}\otimes I)K_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}
.
Defining
X:{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{M}}
by
Xh:=K_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}h
,
h\in{\mathcal{H}}
, it is clear that
X
is an invertible operator and
T_{i}^{*}=X^{-1}(B_{i}^{*}\otimes I)|_{\mathcal{M}}X
for any
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.
The remaining of the proof is similar to that of Theorem
2.1
. This completes the proof.
∎

We remark that if
Q=I
in Theorem
7.1
, we obtain a Rota type similarity result for the elements
T\in{\mathcal{D}}^{c}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}({\mathcal{H}})
.
In this case, we have
\|\Phi_{T}\|_{cb}\leq\left\|\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}}\omega_{\bf k}T^{\bf k}{T^{\bf k}}^{*}\right\|^{1/2}
.

In what follows we present a
w^{*}
-continuous
F_{s}^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})
-functional calculus for the elements in the commutative Reinhardt set
{\mathcal{D}}^{c}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}({\mathcal{H}})
, where
{\mathcal{H}}
is a separable Hilbert space.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
T=(T_{1},\ldots,T_{n})\in{\mathcal{D}}^{c}_{\boldsymbol{\mu}}({\mathcal{H}})

\Psi_{T}:F_{s}^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to B({\mathcal{H}})

\Psi_{T}(\varphi(B))=\varphi(T)=:\text{\rm SOT-}\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{{\bf k}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n},|{\bf k}|\leq N}\left(1-\frac{|{\bf k}|}{N+1}\right)c_{\bf k}T^{\bf k}.

w^{*}
-continuous.

r(\varphi(T))\leq r(\varphi(B))

\varphi(B)\in F_{s}^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})

.

Proof.

Applying Theorem
7.1
in the particular case when

, we find a subspace

,

, and

.
Define the map
\Phi_{T}:F_{s}^{\infty}(\boldsymbol{\mu})\to B({\mathcal{H}})
by setting

Following the lines of the proof of Theorem
5.1

6.3

∎

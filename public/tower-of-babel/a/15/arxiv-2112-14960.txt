Bipartite Ramsey numbers of cycles
Zilong Yan, Yuejian Peng

Abstract
Given bipartite graphs H 1 H_{1} , …, H k H_{k} , the bipartite Ramsey number b ​ r ​ ( H 1 , … , H k ) br(H_{1},\dots,H_{k}) is the minimum integer N N such that any k k -edge-coloring of complete bipartite graph K N , N K_{N,N} contains a monochromatic H i H_{i} in color i i for some i ∈ { 1 , 2 , … , k } i\in\{1,2,\ldots,k\} . There are considerable results on asymptotic values of bipartite Ramsey numbers of cycles. For exact values, Zhang-Sun [ 29 ] determined b ​ r ​ ( C 4 , C 2 ​ n ) br(C_{4},C_{2n}) , Zhang-Sun-Wu [ 30 ] determined b ​ r ​ ( C 6 , C 2 ​ n ) br(C_{6},C_{2n}) , and Gholami-Rowshan [ 12 ] determined b ​ r ​ ( C 8 , C 2 ​ n ) br(C_{8},C_{2n}) . In this paper, we determine completely the exact values of b ​ r ​ ( C 2 ​ n , C 2 ​ m ) br(C_{2n},C_{2m}) for all n ≥ m ≥ 5 n\geq m\geq 5 , this answers a question concerned by Bucić-Letzter-Sudakov [ 5 ] , Gholami-Rowshan [ 12 ] , and Zhang-Sun [ 29 ] . Some observations obtained in the proof have their own interests (in our opinion). Keywords : Bipartite Ramsey number, Ramsey number of cycles.

Proof of Theorem
1.3

The Lower bound is given after Conjecture
1.2
, we only need to show the upper bound. Let us give a very rough sketch of the proof. We apply induction on
n+m
. Suppose on the contrary that there is a red/blue-edge-coloring of
G(X,Y)=K_{N,N}
such that there is neither a blue
C_{2n}
nor a red
C_{2m}
. By induction hypothesis,
G
has a blue cycle
C
with
|C|=2(n-1)
. Indeed we will show that the length of a longest blue cycle is
2(n-1)
(Lemma
2.5
, a crucial lemma). Let
A=X\setminus V(C)
and
B=Y\setminus V(C)
. We will show that there exists a ‘large’ blue component
(A_{1},B_{1})
in
G-V(C)
with
A_{1}\subseteq A
and
B_{1}\subseteq B
and
|A_{1}|+|B_{1}|\geq|A|
(See Lemma
2.2
). We extend the blue component
(A_{1},B_{1})
in
G-V(C)
to the blue cycle
C
, namely, define

X_{1}=\{u\in V(C)|uv\in E^{b}(G)\ for\ some\ v\in B_{1}\},

Y_{1}=\{u\in V(C)|uv\in E^{b}(G)\ for\ some\ v\in A_{1}\}.

Let
X_{2}=X\setminus(A\cup X_{1})
,
Y_{2}=Y\setminus(B\cup Y_{1})
,
A_{2}=A\setminus A_{1}
and
B_{2}=B\setminus B_{1}
. Therefore we get two red complete bipartite graphs
(A_{1},B_{2}\cup Y_{2})
and
(B_{1},A_{2}\cup X_{2})
. We will show that there is a red
C_{2m}
by taking advantage of these two red complete bipartite graphs. There are red paths in these two red complete bipartite graphs, but how to glue two red paths to obtain a red
C_{2m}
? It is easy to show that if there are two disjoint red edges between these two red complete bipartite graphs and these two red complete bipartite graphs have enough number of vertices, then we are fine (Lemma
2.1
). How to guarantee that there are two disjoint red edges between these two red complete bipartite graphs and the number of vertices in these two red complete bipartite graphs are enough? These are the main technical part we need to deal with. If both
X_{1}
and
Y_{1}
are non-empty, then using the fact that
C_{2(n-1)}
is a longest blue cycle (guaranteed by Lemma
2.5
, to be proved in Section
3
) and its implications (Fact

), we have two disjoint red edges in
(X_{2},Y_{2})
; by some quantitative analysis, we can also show numbers of vertices in two red complete bipartite graphs are enough, this is a relatively easy case. For another case, we will show a crucial lemma (Lemma
2.4
) to guarantee that there is no blue
K_{n-1,n-1}
in
G
. However, if there are not two disjoint red edges, we may find a blue
K_{n-1,n-1}
through some analysis (Lemma

to be proved in Section

is applied sometimes), and get a contradiction. This is a very rough sketch, to make everything fit, we need more subtle arguments. The precise argument will be given in this section. Before giving the precise proof, we state the above mentioned lemmas and leave proofs of some of them in Section
3
. In the proofs of some lemmas, we obtain some observations having their own interests (in our own opinion) in Section

.

|X|=|Y|\geq n+1
, then
d(y)\geq|X|-1\geq n
for
y\in Y
. Take
Y^{\prime}\subseteq Y
with
|Y^{\prime}|=n
and apply Theorem
3.2
to
(X,Y^{\prime})
, then
C_{2n}\subseteq(X,Y^{\prime})
, a contradiction. If
|X|=|Y|=n
, then
d(y)\geq n-1
for
y\in Y
and
e(G)\geq(n-1)n
. By Theorem
3.2
, then
C=C_{2(n-1)}\subseteq G
. Since
G
contains no
C_{2n}
, if
G
has no
K_{n-1,n-1}
, by Theorem
3.4
, we have
e(G)\leq(n-1)(n-1)-1+n=(n-1)n
. So the
C_{2n-2}
forms a
K_{n-1,n-1}
minus one edges and there are
n
edges in
E(G-C)\cup E(G-C,C)
. Let
x_{0}=X\setminus C
and
y_{0}=Y\setminus C
. Note that
d(y_{0})\geq n-1
. If
x_{0}y_{0}\in E(G)
, then
d_{C}(x_{0})=0
, otherwise there is a
C_{2n}
in
G
. Recall that there are
n
edges in
E(G-C)\cup E(G-C,C)
, i.e. there are
n
edges incident to
x_{0}
or
y_{0}
, then
d_{C}(y_{0})=n-1
. So
(X\cap C,Y\cap C\cup\{y_{0}\})
forms a
K_{n-1,n}
minus one edge. So there is a
K_{n-1,n-1}
in
(X\cap C,Y\cap C\cup\{y_{0}\})
. If
x_{0}y_{0}\notin E(G)
, since
d(y_{o})\geq n-1
, we have
d(y_{0})=d_{C}(y_{0})=n-1
. Therefore
(X\cap C,Y\cap C\cup\{y_{0}\})
forms a
K_{n-1,n}
minus one edge, there is a
K_{n-1,n-1}
in
(X\cap C,Y\cap C\cup\{y_{0}\})
.
\Box

Lemma 3.6

Let
G(X,Y)
be a bipartite graph with
|X|=|Y|=M
. Let
C
be a longest cycle in
G
with
|V(C)|=2c
. Assume that
G
is
C_{2n}
-free. If
c\geq\frac{M}{2}
and
c>n
, then
e(G)\leq\frac{M^{2}-M+6}{2}
. If
c\leq n-1\leq\frac{M}{2}
, then
e(G)\leq(n-1)(2M-2n+3)
, and if equality holds, then
G[V(C)]
must be a
K_{c,c}
.

Proof of Lemma
3.6
.
If
c\geq\frac{M}{2}
and
c>n
, by Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle e(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e(G[V(C)])+e(G-C,C)+e(G-C)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c(c-1)}{2}+2+c(M-1-c)+M

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{c^{2}}{2}+c(M-\frac{3}{2})+M+2

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{M^{2}-M+6}{2}.

If
c\leq n-1\leq\frac{M}{2}
, by Theorem
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle e(G)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e(G[V(C)])+e(G-C,C)+e(G-C)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c^{2}+c(2M+1-3c)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2c^{2}+c(2M+1)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(n-1)(2M-2n+3).

\Box

Lemma
2.5
Let
N=m+n
for
m=n
and
N=m+n-1
for
n\geq m+1
. Assume that there is a red/blue-edge-coloring of
G(X,Y)=K_{N,N}
such that there is neither a blue
C_{2n}
nor a red
C_{2m}
. Assume that
C
is a longest blue cycle with
|V(C)|=2c
and
C^{\prime}
is a longest red cycle with
|V(C^{\prime})|=2t
. If
n\geq m+1
, then
c\leq n-1
. If
n=m
, then
c\leq m-1
and
t\leq m-1
.

Proof of Lemma
2.5
.

Case 1.

m=n
,
N=2m
.

Without loss of generality, assume that
c\geq m+1
. Then by Lemma
3.6
,
e^{b}(G)\leq\frac{N^{2}-N+6}{2}
. If
t\geq m+1
, then by Lemma
3.6
,
e^{r}(G)\leq\frac{N^{2}-N+6}{2}
, a contradiction to that
e^{b}(G)+e^{r}(G)=N^{2}
. If
t\leq m-1
, then by Lemma
3.6
,
e^{r}(G)\leq(m-1)(2N-2m+3)=(m-1)(2m+3)
. Note that
\frac{N^{2}-N+6}{2}+(m-1)(2m+3)=N^{2}
, then
e^{r}(G)=(m-1)(2m+3)
. So
t=m-1
and
G[V(C^{\prime})]
forms a red
K_{m-1,m-1}
(Lemma
3.6
), a contradiction to Lemma
2.4
.

Case 2.

n\geq m+1
, and
N=n+m-1\geq 2m
.

Suppose that
c\geq n+1
, then by Lemma
3.6
,
e^{b}(G)\leq\frac{N^{2}-N+6}{2}
. Therefore
e^{r}(G)\geq\frac{N^{2}+N-6}{2}
.

If
t\geq\frac{N}{2}
, then by Lemma
3.6
,
e^{r}(G)\leq\frac{N^{2}-N+6}{2}
, a contradiction.

If
m+1\leq t\leq\frac{N}{2}
, then by Theorem
3.3
and Theorem
3.4
, we have

\displaystyle e^{r}(G)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{t(t-1)}{2}+2+t(2N+1-3t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{5t^{2}}{2}+t(2N+\frac{1}{2})+2

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4N^{2}+2N+20.25}{10}.

Then
\frac{4N^{2}+2N+20.25}{10}\geq\frac{N^{2}+N-6}{2}
, i.e.
N\leq\frac{\sqrt{210}-3}{2}<6
, a contradiction.

If
t\leq m-1
, then by Lemma
3.6
,
e^{r}(G)\leq(m-1)(2N-2m+3)
. Therefore
(m-1)(2N-2m+3)\geq\frac{N^{2}+N-6}{2}
, i.e.
\frac{N^{2}}{2}-(2m-\frac{5}{2})N+2m^{2}-5m\leq 0
. We have
2m-5\leq N\leq 2m
. Since
N\geq 2m
, we have
N=2m
and
n=m+1
. Since the equality holds,
e^{r}(G)=(m-1)(2m+3)
and
C^{\prime}
forms a red
K_{m-1,m-1}
. Without loss of generality, assume that
(X_{1},Y_{1})
forms a red
K_{m-1,m-1}
with
X_{1}\subset X
and
Y_{1}\subseteq Y
and
|X_{1}|=|Y_{1}|=m-1
. Let
A=X\setminus X_{1}
and
B=Y\setminus Y_{1}
. Note that
|A|=|B|=m+1
. Let
A_{1}=\{x\in A|d_{Y_{1}}^{r}(x)\geq 2\}
and
X_{1}^{\prime}=X_{1}\cup A_{1}
and
B_{1}=\{y\in B|d_{X_{1}^{\prime}}^{r}(y)\geq 2\}
. Since
G
has no red
C_{2m}
, we may assume that
B_{1}=\emptyset.
Note that each vertex in
B
has at most one red neighbor in
X_{1}^{\prime}
. If
|A_{1}|\geq 2
, then
|X_{1}^{\prime}|\geq|B|=m+1=n
. Since
G
has no blue
C_{2n}
, by Lemma
3.5
, we get a blue
K_{n-1,n-1}
in
(X_{1}^{\prime},B)
, a contradiction to Lemma
2.4
. Therefore
|X_{1}^{\prime}|\leq m
. By Lemma
3.1
, there is no disjoint blue edges in
(X\setminus X_{1}^{\prime},B)
. If
|A_{1}|=0
, then
(X\setminus X_{1}^{\prime},B)
contains a red
K_{m,m+1}
, a contradiction. If
|A_{1}|=1
, since
G
has no red
C_{2m}
,
(X\setminus X_{1}^{\prime},B)
contains a red
K_{m-1,m+1}=(A^{\prime},B)
with
A^{\prime}\subset X\setminus X_{1}^{\prime}
. So each vertex in
X\setminus A^{\prime}
has at most one red neighbor in
B
. Note that
|X\setminus A^{\prime}|=|B|=m+1=n
. Since
G
has no blue
C_{2n}
, by Lemma
3.5
, there is a blue
K_{n-1,n-1}
in
(X\setminus A^{\prime},B)
, a contradiction to Lemma
2.4
.

\Box

Remarks.
For more than two colors, we have very few results on exact values of Ramsey numbers. A nice result due to Jenssen and Skokan
[
18
]
gives
r^{k}(C_{2n+1})
(a conjecture of Bondy and Erdős) for large enough
n
. Most current known results are in diagonal cases (the same graph for all colors). For non-diagonal cases, we even do not know
r(P_{l},P_{m},P_{n})
and
br(P_{l},P_{m},P_{n})
.

Transposed Hom-Poisson and Hom-pre-Lie Poisson algebras and bialgebras
Ismail Laraiedh, Sergei Silvestrov

Abstract
The notions of transposed Hom-Poisson and Hom-pre-Lie Poisson algebras are introduced. Their bimodules and matched pairs are defined and the relevant properties and theorems are given. The notion of Manin triple of transposed Hom-Poisson algebras is introduced, and its equivalence to the transposed Hom-Poisson bialgebras is investigated. The notion of 𝒪 \mathcal{O} -operator is exploited to illustrate the relations existing between transposed Hom-Poisson and Hom-pre-Lie Poisson algebras.

Definition 2.1
.

A Hom-module is a pair
(M,\alpha_{M})
consisting of a

\mathbb{K}
-module
M

\alpha_{M}:M\longrightarrow M

f:(M,\alpha_{M})\rightarrow(N,\alpha_{N})

f:M\longrightarrow N
such that
f\alpha_{M}=\alpha_{N}f
.

Definition 2.2
.

A Hom-algebra is a triple
(A,\mu,\alpha)
where
(A,\alpha)
is
a Hom-module and
\mu:A^{\otimes 2}\rightarrow A
is a linear map.

Definition 2.3
.

A Hom-algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha)
is called multiplicative, if
the linear map
\alpha:A\to A
is moreover an algebra homomorphism of the algebra
(A,\mu)
, that is,

\alpha\circ\mu=\mu\circ(\alpha\otimes\alpha),

or equivalently, in multilinear maps notation,

\alpha\circ\mu(x,y)=\mu(\alpha(x),\alpha(y)),

for
x,y\in A.

In any Hom-algebra
(A,\mu,\alpha)
, Hom-associator (
\alpha

as_{A}(x,y,z)=\mu(\mu(x,y),\alpha(z))-\mu(\alpha(x),\mu(y,z))

\alpha=id
).

Definition 2.4

A Hom-associative algebra is a Hom-algebra

\displaystyle as_{A}(x,y,z)=0,

Proposition 2.19
.

Let

be a commutative Hom-associative algebra, and let

and

be commuting derivations,
D_{1}D_{2}=D_{2}D_{1}
. For all

Then,
(A,\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\},\alpha)
is a Hom-Poisson algebra.

First, we prove that
(A,\{\cdot,\cdot\},\alpha)
is a Hom-Lie algebra. For all
x,y,z\in A

\displaystyle\{x,y\}=D_{1}(x)\cdot D_{2}(y)-D_{1}(y)\cdot D_{2}(x)=-(D_{1}(y)\cdot D_{2}(x)-D_{1}(x)\cdot D_{2}(y))

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad=-\{y,x\},

\displaystyle\circlearrowleft_{x,y,z}\{\alpha(x),\{y,z\}\}=\circlearrowleft_{x,y,z}\Big(D_{1}(\alpha(x))\cdot D_{2}(\{y,z\})-D_{1}(\{y,z\})\cdot D_{2}(\alpha(x))\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\underbrace{(D_{1}^{2}(z)\cdot D_{2}(y))\cdot D_{2}(\alpha(x))}_{g_{1}(x,y,z)}+\underbrace{(D_{1}(z)\cdot D_{1}D_{2}(y))\cdot D_{2}(\alpha(x))}_{h_{1}(x,y,z)}\Big).

2.1
)-(
2.4
), observe that

\displaystyle\circlearrowleft_{x,y,z}(b_{1}(x,y,z)-d_{1}(x,y,z))

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\circlearrowleft_{x,y,z}\Big(\alpha(D_{1}(x))\cdot(D_{1}(y)\cdot D_{2}^{2}(z))-\alpha(D_{1}(x))\cdot(D_{1}(z)\cdot D_{2}^{2}(y))\Big)

\displaystyle\circlearrowleft_{x,y,z}(a_{1}(x,y,z)-f_{1}(x,y,z))

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\circlearrowleft_{x,y,z}\Big(\alpha(D_{1}(x))\cdot(D_{1}D_{2}(y)\cdot D_{2}(z))-\alpha(D_{1}(y))\cdot(D_{1}D_{2}(z)\cdot D_{2}(x))\Big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad=0,\quad\mbox{(\hbox{using} $D_{1}D_{2}=D_{2}D_{1}$)}

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\circlearrowleft_{x,y,z}\Big((D_{1}(z)\cdot D_{1}D_{2}(y))\cdot\alpha(D_{2}(x))-(D_{1}(x)\cdot D_{1}D_{2}(z))\cdot\alpha(D_{2}(y))\Big)

satisfies the
Hom-Jacobi identity
\circlearrowleft_{x,y,z}\{\alpha(x),\{y,z\}\}=0
.

\displaystyle\{x,z\}\cdot\alpha(y)+\alpha(x)\cdot\{y,z\}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{(D_{1}(x)\cdot D_{2}(z))\cdot\alpha(y)}_{i_{1}(x,y,z)}-\underbrace{(D_{1}(z)\cdot D_{2}(x))\cdot\alpha(y)}_{j_{1}(x,y,z)}

2.1
)-(
2.4

\displaystyle i_{1}(x,y,z)+k_{1}(x,y,z)

Proposition 2.31

Definition 4.1

(
[
69
]
)
.

A Hom-pre-Lie algebra is a triple
(A,\ast,\alpha)
consisting of a vector space
A
, a bilinear product
\ast:A\otimes A\rightarrow A,
and a linear map

\alpha:A\rightarrow A
satisfying, for all
x,y,z\in A
,

(x\ast y)\ast\alpha(z)-\alpha(x)\ast(y\ast z)=(y\ast x)\ast\alpha(z)-\alpha(y)\ast(x\ast z).

(4.1)

The equation (
4.1
) is called Hom-pre-Lie identity.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(A,\ast,\alpha)
be a Hom-pre-Lie algebra. Then,

(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
is a Hom-Lie algebra with

[x,y]=x\ast y-y\ast x,

for any
x,y\in A
. We say that
(A,[\cdot,\cdot],\alpha)
is the
sub-adjacent Lie algebra of
(A,\ast,\alpha)
and denoted by
A^{c}
.

Proof.

First, we prove
[\cdot,\cdot]
satisfies Hom-skewsymmetry,

[x,y]=x\ast y-y\ast x=-(y\ast x-x\ast y)=-[y,x].

Now, we prove the Hom-Jacobi-identity,

\displaystyle[\alpha(x),[y,z]]+[\alpha(y),[z,x]]+[\alpha(z),[x,y]]

\displaystyle\quad=[\alpha(x),y\ast z-z\ast y]+[\alpha(y),z\ast x-x\ast z]+[\alpha(z),x\ast y-y\ast x]

\displaystyle\quad=\alpha(x)\ast(y\ast z)-\alpha(x)\ast(z\ast y)-(y\ast z)\ast\alpha(x)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(z\ast y)\ast\alpha(x)+\alpha(y)\ast(z\ast x)-\alpha(y)\ast(x\ast z)

\displaystyle\quad\quad-(z\ast x)\ast\alpha(y)+(x\ast z)\ast\alpha(y)+\alpha(z)\ast(x\ast y)

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\alpha(z)\ast(y\ast x)-(x\ast y)\ast\alpha(z)+(y\ast x)\ast\alpha(z)

\displaystyle\quad=(y\ast x)\ast\alpha(z)-\alpha(y)\ast(x\ast z)-((x\ast y)\ast\alpha(z)-\alpha(x)\ast(y\ast z))

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(z\ast y)\ast\alpha(x)-\alpha(z)\ast(y\ast x)-((y\ast z)\ast\alpha(x)-\alpha(z)\ast(y\ast x))

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(x\ast z)\ast\alpha(y)-\alpha(x)\ast(z\ast y)-((z\ast x)\ast\alpha(y)-\alpha(z)\ast(x\ast y))

\displaystyle\quad=0+0+0=0.\qed

5
\mathcal{O}
-operators of transposed Hom-Poisson algebras

In this section, we introduce and study the notion of an
\mathcal{O}
-operator of transposed Hom-Poisson algebras generalizing the notion of Rota-Baxter operators. Their properties and relationship with
Hom-pre-Lie Poisson algebras is also described.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
be a commutative Hom-associative algebra and
(s,\beta,V)
be a bimodule. Then, a linear map
T:V\rightarrow A

is called an
\mathcal{O}
-operator associated to
(s,\beta,V)
, if
T
satisfies
for all
u,v\in V,

\displaystyle\alpha T=T\beta,

(5.1)

\displaystyle T(u)\cdot T(v)=T(s(T(u))v+s(T(v))u).

(5.2)

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
be a commutative Hom-associative algebra, and
(l,r,\beta,V)
be a bimodule.
Let
T:V\rightarrow A
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator associated to
(s,\beta,V)
. Then, there exists a commutative Hom-associative algebra structure on
V
given for all
u,v\in V
by

\displaystyle u\diamond v=s(T(u))v+s(T(v))u.

Proof.

First, we prove the commutativity condition.
For all
u,v\in V
,

u\diamond v=s(T(u))v+s(T(v))u=s(T(v))u+s(T(u))v=v\diamond u.

Now, we prove the Hom-associativity condition. For all
u,v,w\in V
,

\displaystyle(u\diamond v)\diamond\beta(w)=(s(T(u))v+s(T(v))u)\diamond\beta(w)

\displaystyle\quad=s\Big[T(s(T(u))v+s(T(v))u)\Big]\beta(w)+s(T(\beta(w)))(s(T(u))v+s(T(v))u)

\displaystyle\quad=s(T(u)\cdot T(v))\beta(w)+s(\alpha T(w))s(T(u))v+s(\alpha T(w))s(T(v))u

\displaystyle\quad=s(T(u)\cdot T(v))\beta(w)+s(T(w)\cdot T(u))\beta(v)+s(T(w)\cdot T(v))\beta(u),~~(\textsl{by}~\eqref{Cond1})

\displaystyle\beta(u)\diamond(v\diamond w)=\beta(u)\diamond(s(T(v))w+s(T(w))v)

\displaystyle\quad=s(\alpha(T(u))(s(T(v))w+s(T(w))v)+s\Big[T(s(T(v))w+s(T(w))v)\Big]\beta(u)

\displaystyle\quad=s(\alpha T(u))s(T(v))w+s(\alpha T(u))s(T(w))v+s(T(v)\cdot T(w))\beta(u)

\displaystyle\quad=s(T(u)\cdot T(v))\beta(w)+s(T(u)\cdot T(w))\beta(v)+s(T(v)\cdot T(w))\beta(u).\quad\mbox{(by \eqref{Cond1})}

Then,
(u\diamond v)\diamond\beta(w)=\beta(u)\diamond(v\diamond w)
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
be a commutative Hom-associative algebra, and
(s,\beta,V)
be a bimodule.
Let
T:V\rightarrow A
be an
\mathcal{O}
-operator associated to
(s,\beta,V)
. Then,
T
is a morphism from the commutative Hom-associative algebra
(V,\diamond,\beta)
to
(A,\cdot,\alpha)
.

\displaystyle=T\beta,

(5.4)

Example 5.5

An

Lemma 5.6

(

)

Let

Theorem 5.10
.

Assume that
\mathcal{A}=(A,\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\},\alpha)

By Lemma
5.2

is a commutative Hom-associative algebra and

is a Hom-pre-Lie algebra. Next, we prove the axiom

For any

\displaystyle(u\diamond v)\ast\beta(w)=

\displaystyle(s(T(u))v+s(T(v))u)\ast\beta(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho\Big(T(s(T(u))v+s(T(v))u)\Big)\beta(w)

(5.9)

\displaystyle\rho(T(u)\cdot T(v))\beta(w),

\displaystyle\beta(u)\diamond(v\ast w)=

\displaystyle=s(T(\rho(T(u))v)\beta(w)+s(T(\beta(w)))\rho(T(u))v

\displaystyle=s(T(\rho(T(u))v-T(\rho(T(v))u)\beta(w)

\displaystyle=s(\{T(u),T(v)\})\beta(w)+s(T(\beta(w))\rho(T(u))v

Corollary 5.11
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\},\alpha)
be a transposed Hom-Poisson algebra. There is a Hom-pre-Lie Poisson algebra structure on
(A,\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\},\alpha)
such that its sub-adjacent transposed Hom-Poisson algebra is exactly
(A,\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\},\alpha)
if and only if there exists an invertible
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
(A,\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\},\alpha).

Proof.

Suppose that there exists an invertible
\mathcal{O}
-operator
T:V\rightarrow A
associated to the representation
(l,r,\rho,V)
, then the compatible Hom-pre-Lie
Poisson algebra structure on
A
, for all
x,y\in A
is given by

x\cdot y=T(s(x)T^{-1}(y)+s(y)T^{-1}(x)),\;\;x\ast y=T(\rho(x)T^{-1}(y)).

Conversely, let
(A,\cdot,\ast,\alpha)
be a Hom-pre-Lie Poisson algebra, and
(A,\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\},\alpha)
be the sub-adjacent transposed Hom-Poisson algebra.
Then, the identity map
id
is an
\mathcal{O}
-operator on
(A,\cdot,\ast,\alpha)
with respect to the regular representation
(S,ad,\alpha,A)
.
∎

Example 5.12
.

Let
(A,\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\},\alpha)

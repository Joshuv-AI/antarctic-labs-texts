On the Column and Row Ranks of a Matrix
Jun Lu

Abstract
Every m m by n n matrix 𝑨 \bm{A} with rank r r has exactly r r independent rows and r r independent columns. The fact has become the most fundamental theorem in linear algebra such that we may favor it in an unconscious way. The sole aim of this paper is to give a self-contained introduction to concepts and mathematical tools for the rank of a matrix in order to seamlessly introduce how it works in applied linear algebra. However, we clearly realize our inability to cover all the useful and interesting results concerning this topic and given the paucity of scope to present this discussion, e.g., a proof via the injective linear map. We refer the reader to literature in the field of linear algebra for a more detailed introduction to the related fields.

1
The Theorem

In normal conditions, what we talk about the rank of a matrix
\bm{A}\in\real^{m\times n}
refers to the dimension of the column space of
\bm{A}
.
The rank of
\bm{A}
is equal to the maximal number of linearly independent columns of
\bm{A}
. By the following theorem, this is also the maximal number of linearly independent rows of
\bm{A}
. The matrix
\bm{A}
and its transpose
\bm{A}^{\top}
have the same rank. We say that
\bm{A}
has full rank, if its rank is equal to
min\{m,n\}
. In another word, this is true if and only if either all the columns of
\bm{A}
are linearly independent, or all the rows of
\bm{A}
are linearly independent. Specifically, given a vector
\bm{u}\in\real^{m}
and a vector
\bm{v}\in\real^{n}
, then the
m\times n
matrix
\bm{u}\bm{v}^{\top}
is of rank 1.
We here present the theorem rigorously as follows:

Theorem 1

(Row Rank Equals to Column Rank)
.

The dimension of the column space of a matrix
\bm{A}\in\real^{m\times n}
is equal to the dimension of its
row space, i.e., the row rank and the column rank of a matrix
\bm{A}
are equal.

This paper offers three ways to prove the equivalence of the row and column ranks:

•

An elementary way that constructs column basis from row basis;

•

A Gram-Schmidt way that results in the UTV decomposition framework which in turn gives insights on the theorem;

•

A column-row decomposition way that arises from Gaussian elimination.

2
An Elementary Way

In this section, we prove Theorem
by an elementary approach from which it also reveals the fundamental theorem of linear algebra.

of Theorem

The First Way: An Elementary Way
.

We first notice that the null space of
\bm{A}
is orthogonal complementary to the row space of
\bm{A}
(
\mathcal{C}(\bm{A}^{\top})
, i.e., the column space of
\bm{A}^{\top}
, where the row space of
\bm{A}
is exactly the column space of
\bm{A}^{\top}
):
\mathcal{N}(\bm{A})\bot\mathcal{C}(\bm{A}^{\top})
, that is, vectors in the null space of
\bm{A}
are orthogonal to vectors in the row space of
\bm{A}
. To see this, suppose
\bm{A}
has rows
\bm{a}_{1}^{\top},\bm{a}_{2}^{\top},\ldots,\bm{a}_{m}^{\top}
and
\bm{A}=[\bm{a}_{1}^{\top};\bm{a}_{2}^{\top};\ldots;\bm{a}_{m}^{\top}]
is the row partition of
\bm{A}
. For any vector
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{N}(\bm{A})
, we have
\bm{A}\bm{x}=\mathbf{0}
, that is,
[\bm{a}_{1}^{\top}\bm{x};\bm{a}_{2}^{\top}\bm{x};\ldots;\bm{a}_{m}^{\top}\bm{x}]=\mathbf{0}
. And since the row space of
\bm{A}
is spanned by
\bm{a}_{1}^{\top},\bm{a}_{2}^{\top},\ldots,\bm{a}_{m}^{\top}
. Then
\bm{x}
is perpendicular to any vectors from
\mathcal{C}(\bm{A}^{\top})
which means
\mathcal{N}(\bm{A})\bot\mathcal{C}(\bm{A}^{\top})
.

Now suppose further that the dimension of row space of
\bm{A}
is
r
.
Let
\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2},\ldots,\bm{r}_{r}
be a set of vectors in
n
and form a basis for the row space of
\bm{A}
. Then the
r
vectors
\bm{A}\bm{r}_{1},\bm{A}\bm{r}_{2},\ldots,\bm{A}\bm{r}_{r}
are apparently in the column space of
\bm{A}
, which are linearly independent. To show this, suppose we have a linear combination of the
r
vectors:
x_{1}\bm{A}\bm{r}_{1}+x_{2}\bm{A}\bm{r}_{2}+\ldots+x_{r}\bm{A}\bm{r}_{r}=\mathbf{0}
, that is,
\bm{A}(x_{1}\bm{r}_{1}+x_{2}\bm{r}_{2}+\ldots+x_{r}\bm{r}_{r})=\mathbf{0}
and the vector
\bm{v}=x_{1}\bm{r}_{1}+x_{2}\bm{r}_{2}+\ldots+x_{r}\bm{r}_{r}
is in null space of
\bm{A}
:
\bm{v}\in\mathcal{N}(\bm{A})
. But since
\{\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2},\ldots,\bm{r}_{r}\}
is a basis for the row space of
\bm{A}
,
\bm{v}
is thus also in the row space of
\bm{A}
:
\bm{v}\in\mathcal{C}(\bm{A}^{\top})
. We have shown above that vectors from null space of
\bm{A}
is perpendicular to vectors from row space of
\bm{A}
, thus
\bm{v}^{\top}\bm{v}=0
and
x_{1}=x_{2}=\ldots=x_{r}=0
. Then
\bm{A}\bm{r}_{1},\bm{A}\bm{r}_{2},\ldots,\bm{A}\bm{r}_{r}
are in the column space of
\bm{A}
and they are linearly independent
which means the dimension of the column space of
\bm{A}
is larger than
r
. This result shows that
row rank of
\bm{A}\leq
column rank of
\bm{A}
.

If we apply this process again for
\bm{A}^{\top}
. We will have
column rank of
\bm{A}\leq
row rank of
\bm{A}
. This completes the proof.
∎

An important byproduct from the above proof is that if we find a row basis
\{\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2},\ldots,\bm{r}_{r}\}
of matrix
\bm{A}
with rank
r
, then,
\{\bm{A}\bm{r}_{1},\bm{A}\bm{r}_{2},\ldots,\bm{A}\bm{r}_{r}\}
is a column basis for it. The reverse is not true normally since
\bm{A}
may not be invertible.

However, suppose
\{\bm{c}_{1},\bm{c}_{2},\ldots,\bm{c}_{r}\}
is a column basis for
\bm{A}
, i.e.,
\{\bm{c}_{1},\bm{c}_{2},\ldots,\bm{c}_{r}\}
is a row basis for
\bm{A}^{\top}
. Apply the finding above, it is trivial that
\{\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{c}_{1},\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{c}_{2},\ldots,\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{c}_{r}\}
is a row basis for
\bm{A}
(i.e., a column basis for
\bm{A}^{\top}
).

Specially, when
\bm{A}\in\real^{n\times n}
is nonsingular and
\{\bm{c}_{1},\bm{c}_{2},\ldots,\bm{c}_{n}\}
is a column basis for
\bm{A}
, then
\{\bm{A}^{-1}\bm{c}_{1},\bm{A}^{-1}\bm{c}_{2},

\ldots,

\bm{A}^{-1}\bm{c}_{n}\}
is a row basis for it. Or apparently, in this case, the
n
rows or even the
n
columns of
\bm{A}
also form a row basis as well since both the rows and columns span the whole
n
space .

2.1
Fundamental Theorem of Linear Algebra

The byproduct of the above proof is essential to prove the fundamental theorem of linear algebra. As a recap, for any matrix
\bm{A}\in\real^{m\times n}
, it can be easily verified that any vector in row space of
\bm{A}
is perpendicular to any vector in null space of
\bm{A}
. Suppose
\bm{x}_{n}\in\mathcal{N}(\bm{A})
, then
\bm{A}\bm{x}_{n}=\mathbf{0}
such that
\bm{x}_{n}
is perpendicular to every row of
\bm{A}
which agrees with our claim.

Similarly, we can also show that any vector in column space of
\bm{A}
is perpendicular to any vector in null space of
\bm{A}^{\top}
. Further, the column space of
\bm{A}
and the null space of
\bm{A}^{\top}
span the whole
m
space which is known as the fundamental theorem of linear algebra.

The fundamental theorem contains two parts, the dimension of the subspaces and the orthogonality of the subspaces. The orthogonality can be easily verified as shown above. Moreover, when the row space has dimension
r
, the null space has dimension
n-r
. This cannot be easily stated and we prove in the following theorem.

Figure 1:
Two pairs of orthogonal subspaces in
n
and
m
.
\dim(\mathcal{C}(\bm{A}^{\top}))+\dim(\mathcal{N}(\bm{A}))=n
and
\dim(\mathcal{N}(\bm{A}^{\top}))+\dim(\mathcal{C}(\bm{A}))=m
. The null space component goes to zero as
\bm{A}\bm{x}_{n}=\mathbf{0}\in\real^{m}
. The row space component goes to column space as
\bm{A}\bm{x}_{r}=\bm{A}(\bm{x}_{r}+\bm{x}_{n})=\bm{b}\in\mathcal{C}(\bm{A})
.

Theorem 2

(Fundamental Theorem of Linear Algebra)
.

Orthogonal Complement and Rank-Nullity Theorem: for any matrix
\bm{A}\in\real^{m\times n}
, we have

\bullet

\mathcal{N}(\bm{A})
is orthogonal complement to the row space
\mathcal{C}(\bm{A}^{\top})
in
n
:
dim(\mathcal{N}(\bm{A}))+dim(\mathcal{C}(\bm{A}^{\top}))=n
;

\bullet

\mathcal{N}(\bm{A}^{\top})
is orthogonal complement to the column space
\mathcal{C}(\bm{A})
in
m
:
dim(\mathcal{N}(\bm{A}^{\top}))+dim(\mathcal{C}(\bm{A}))=m
;

\bullet
For rank-
r
matrix
\bm{A}
,
dim(\mathcal{C}(\bm{A}^{\top}))=dim(\mathcal{C}(\bm{A}))=r
, that is,
dim(\mathcal{N}(\bm{A}))=n-r
and
dim(\mathcal{N}(\bm{A}^{\top}))=m-r
.

of Theorem

Follow from the proof of Theorem
. Let
\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2},\ldots,\bm{r}_{r}
be a set of vectors in
n
that form a basis for the row space, then
\bm{A}\bm{r}_{1},\bm{A}\bm{r}_{2},\ldots,\bm{A}\bm{r}_{r}
is a basis for the column space of
\bm{A}
. Let
\bm{n}_{1},\bm{n}_{2},\ldots,\bm{n}_{k}\in\real^{n}
form a basis for the null space of
\bm{A}
. Follow again from the proof of Theorem
,
\mathcal{N}(\bm{A})\bot\mathcal{C}(\bm{A}^{\top})
, thus,
\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2},\ldots,\bm{r}_{r}
are perpendicular to
\bm{n}_{1},\bm{n}_{2},\ldots,\bm{n}_{k}
. Then,
\{\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2},\ldots,\bm{r}_{r},\bm{n}_{1},\bm{n}_{2},\ldots,\bm{n}_{k}\}
is linearly independent in
n
.

For any vector
\bm{x}\in\real^{n}
,
\bm{A}\bm{x}
is in the column space of
\bm{A}
. Then it can be expressed by a combination of
\bm{A}\bm{r}_{1},\bm{A}\bm{r}_{2},\ldots,\bm{A}\bm{r}_{r}
:
\bm{A}\bm{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\bm{A}\bm{r}_{i}
which states that
\bm{A}(\bm{x}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\bm{r}_{i})=\mathbf{0}
and
\bm{x}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\bm{r}_{i}
is thus in
\mathcal{N}(\bm{A})
. Since
\{\bm{n}_{1},\bm{n}_{2},\ldots,\bm{n}_{k}\}
is a basis for the null space of
\bm{A}
,
\bm{x}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\bm{r}_{i}
can be expressed by a combination of
\bm{n}_{1},\bm{n}_{2},\ldots,\bm{n}_{k}
:
\bm{x}-\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\bm{r}_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}\bm{n}_{j}
, i.e.,
\bm{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}\bm{r}_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}\bm{n}_{j}
. That is, any vector
\bm{x}\in\real^{n}
can be expressed by
\{\bm{r}_{1},\bm{r}_{2},\ldots,\bm{r}_{r},\bm{n}_{1},\bm{n}_{2},\ldots,\bm{n}_{k}\}
and the set forms a basis for
n
. Thus the dimension sum to
n
:
r+k=n
, i.e.,
dim(\mathcal{N}(\bm{A}))+dim(\mathcal{C}(\bm{A}^{\top}))=n
. Similarly, we can prove
dim(\mathcal{N}(\bm{A}^{\top}))+dim(\mathcal{C}(\bm{A}))=m
.

3
Gram-Schmidt Way

The UTV decomposition goes further from the QR decomposition which in tern relies on the
Gram-Schmidt process
by factoring the matrix into two orthogonal matrices
\bm{A}=\bm{U}\bm{T}\bm{V}
, where
\bm{U},\bm{V}
are orthogonal. The resulting
\bm{T}
supports rank estimation. The matrix
\bm{T}
can be lower triangular which results in the ULV decomposition, or it can be upper triangular which results in the URV decomposition. The UTV framework shares similar form as the singular value decomposition (SVD) and can be regarded as inexpensive alternatives to the SVD.

We here firstly formulate the decomposition in the following theorem, and the proof of the existence of the ULV decomposition will be discussed in Section
.

Theorem 3

(Full ULV Decomposition)
.

Every
m\times n
matrix
\bm{A}
with column rank
r
can be factored as

\bm{A}=\bm{U}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{L}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}\bm{V},

where
\bm{U}\in\real^{m\times m}
and
\bm{V}\in\real^{n\times n}
are two orthogonal matrices, and
\bm{L}\in\real^{r\times r}
is a lower triangular matrix.

From the ULV decomposition, we here provide an alternative way to prove the column rank and row rank of matrix
\bm{A}
are the same. Note that, in the proof of the QR, LQ and UTV decompositions, we do not utilize the results that the column and row ranks of a matrix are the same. Otherwise, we won’t be able to prove it via the existence of the ULV decomposition.

of Theorem
,
A Second Way
.

Any
m\times n
matrix
\bm{A}
with column rank
r
can be factored as

\bm{A}=\bm{U}_{0}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{L}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}\bm{V}_{0},

where
\bm{U}_{0}\in\real^{m\times m}
and
\bm{V}_{0}\in\real^{n\times n}
are two orthogonal matrices, and
\bm{L}\in\real^{r\times r}
is a lower triangular matrix. Let
\bm{D}=\begin{bmatrix}\bm{L}&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix}
, the row rank and column rank of
\bm{D}
are apparently the same. If we could prove the column rank of
\bm{A}
equals to the column rank of
\bm{D}
, and the row rank of
\bm{A}
equals to the row rank of
\bm{D}
, then we complete the proof.

Let
\bm{U}=\bm{U}_{0}^{\top}
,
\bm{V}=\bm{V}_{0}^{\top}
, then it follows that
\bm{D}=\bm{U}\bm{A}\bm{V}
. Decompose the above idea into two steps, a moment of reflexion reveals that,

•

if we could first prove the row rank and column rank of
\bm{A}
are equal to those of
\bm{U}\bm{A}
;

•

and then, if we further prove the row rank and column rank of
\bm{U}\bm{A}
are equal to those of
\bm{U}\bm{A}\bm{V}=\bm{D}
, we could also complete the proof.

Row rank and column ranks of
\bm{A}
are equal to those of
\bm{U}\bm{A}

Let
\bm{B}=\bm{U}\bm{A}
, and let further
\bm{A}=[\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2},\ldots,\bm{a}_{n}]
and
\bm{B}=[\bm{b}_{1},\bm{b}_{2},\ldots,\bm{b}_{n}]
be the column partitions of
\bm{A}
and
\bm{B}
. Therefore,
[\bm{b}_{1},\bm{b}_{2},\ldots,\bm{b}_{n}]=[\bm{U}\bm{a}_{1},\bm{U}\bm{a}_{2},\ldots,\bm{U}\bm{a}_{n}]
. If
x_{1}\bm{a}_{1}+x_{2}\bm{a}_{2}+\ldots+x_{n}\bm{a}_{n}=0
, then we also obtain

\bm{U}(x_{1}\bm{a}_{1}+x_{2}\bm{a}_{2}+\ldots+x_{n}\bm{a}_{n})=x_{1}\bm{b}_{1}+x_{2}\bm{b}_{2}+\ldots+x_{n}\bm{b}_{n}=0.

Let
j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{r}
be distinct indices between 1 and
n
, if the set
\{\bm{a}_{j_{1}},\bm{a}_{j_{2}},\ldots,\bm{a}_{j_{r}}\}
is linearly independent, the set
\{\bm{b}_{j_{1}},\bm{b}_{j_{2}},\ldots,\bm{b}_{j_{r}}\}
must also be independent. This implies

dim(\mathcal{C}(\bm{B}))\leq dim(\mathcal{C}(\bm{A})).

Similarly, by
\bm{A}=\bm{U}^{\top}\bm{B}
, we have

dim(\mathcal{C}(\bm{A}))\leq dim(\mathcal{C}(\bm{B})).

This implies

dim(\mathcal{C}(\bm{B}))=dim(\mathcal{C}(\bm{A})).

Apply the process onto
\bm{B}^{\top}
and
\bm{A}^{\top}
, we have

dim(\mathcal{C}(\bm{B}^{\top}))=dim(\mathcal{C}(\bm{A}^{\top})).

This implies the row rank and column rank of
\bm{A}
and
\bm{B}=\bm{U}\bm{A}
are the same.
Similarly, we can also show that the row rank and column rank of
\bm{U}\bm{A}
and
\bm{U}\bm{A}\bm{V}
are the same. This completes the proof.
∎

Warning on the proof

We might ask: since the UTV framework is an inexpansive alternatives to the SVD, why do not we use the SVD to prove what we want? The answer needs closer scrutiny. The existence of the SVD relies on the fact that the
\bm{A}^{\top}\bm{A}
and
\bm{A}
have same rank which in turn is based on the fundamental theorem of linear algebra that is a byproduct of Theorem
as we have shown in last section. Therefore, this is problematic as we use claim
A
to prove claim
B
where
A
relies on
B
as well.

3.1.1
QR Decomposition

In many applications, we are interested in the column space of a matrix
\bm{A}=[\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2},...,\bm{a}_{n}]\in\real^{m\times n}
. The successive spaces spanned by the columns
\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2},\ldots
of
\bm{A}
are

\mathcal{C}([\bm{a}_{1}])\,\,\,\,\subseteq\,\,\,\,\mathcal{C}([\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2}])\,\,\,\,\subseteq\,\,\,\,\mathcal{C}([\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2},\bm{a}_{3}])\,\,\,\,\subseteq\,\,\,\,\ldots,

where
\mathcal{C}([\ldots])
is the subspace spanned by the vectors included in the brackets. The idea of the QR decomposition is the construction of a sequence of orthonormal vectors
\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2},\ldots
that span the same successive subspaces.

\mathcal{C}([\bm{q}_{1}])=\mathcal{C}([\bm{a}_{1}])\,\,\,\,\subseteq\,\,\,\,\mathcal{C}([\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2}])=\mathcal{C}([\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2}])\,\,\,\,\subseteq\,\,\,\,\mathcal{C}([\bm{q}_{1},\bm{q}_{2},\bm{q}_{3}])=\mathcal{C}([\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2},\bm{a}_{3}])\,\,\,\,\subseteq\,\,\,\,\ldots,

We provide the result of QR decomposition in the following theorem and we delay the discussion of the existence of it in the next sections.

Theorem 4

(QR Decomposition)
.

Every
m\times n
matrix
\bm{A}=[\bm{a}_{1},\bm{a}_{2},...,\bm{a}_{n}]
with
m\geq n
can be factored as

\bm{A}=\bm{Q}\bm{R},

where

1.
Reduced
:
\bm{Q}
is
m\times n
with orthonormal columns and
\bm{R}
is an
n\times n
upper triangular matrix, which is known as the
reduced QR decomposition
;

2.
Full
:
\bm{Q}
is
m\times m
with orthonormal columns and
\bm{R}
is an
m\times n
upper triangular matrix which is known as the
full QR decomposition
. If further restrict the upper triangular matrix to be a square matrix, the full QR decomposition can be denoted as

\bm{A}=\bm{Q}\begin{bmatrix}\bm{R}_{0}\\
\mathbf{0}\end{bmatrix},

where
\bm{R}_{0}
is an
m\times m
upper triangular matrix.

Specifically, when
\bm{A}
has full rank, i.e., has independent columns,
\bm{R}
also has independent columns, and
\bm{R}
is nonsingular for the reduced case (i.e., with nonzero diagonals).

The QR decomposition can be extended to matrices having dependent columns. In this case, the diagonals of
\bm{R}
might contain zeros. But for the proof of the UTV decomposition and the Theorem
, we only discuss the matrix with independent columns (i.e., full column rank).

3.1.3
Existence of the QR Decomposition via the Gram-Schmidt Process

For three independent vectors

Abstract
. Given a compact surface M M , consider the natural right action of the group of diffeomorphisms 𝒟 ⁡ ( M ) \mathcal{D}(M) of M M on 𝒞 ∞ ​ ( M , ℝ ) \mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}) defined by the rule: ( f , h ) ↦ f ∘ h (f,h)\mapsto f\circ h for f ∈ 𝒞 ∞ ​ ( M , ℝ ) f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}) and h ∈ 𝒟 ⁡ ( M ) h\in\mathcal{D}(M) . Denote by ℱ ⁡ ( M ) \mathcal{F}(M) the subset of 𝒞 ∞ ​ ( M , ℝ ) \mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}) consisting of function f : M → ℝ f:M\to\mathbb{R} taking constant values on connected components of ∂ M \partial{M} , having no critical points on ∂ M \partial{M} , and such that at each of its critical points z z the function f f is 𝒞 ∞ \mathcal{C}^{\infty} equivalent to some homogenenous polynomial without multiple factors. In particular, ℱ ⁡ ( M ) \mathcal{F}(M) contains all Morse maps. Let also 𝒪 ⁡ ( f ) = { f ∘ h ∣ h ∈ 𝒟 ⁡ ( M ) } \mathcal{O}(f)=\{f\circ h\mid h\in\mathcal{D}(M)\} be the orbit of f f . Previously it was computed the algebraic structure of π 1 ​ 𝒪 ​ ( f ) \pi_{1}\mathcal{O}(f) for all f ∈ ℱ ⁡ ( M ) f\in\mathcal{F}(M) , where M M is any orientable compact surface distinct from 2 2 -sphere. In the present paper we compute the group π 0 ​ 𝒮 ​ ( f , ∂ 𝕄 ) \pi_{0}\mathcal{S}(f,\partial\mathbb{M}) , where 𝕄 \mathbb{M} is a Möbius band, and 𝒮 ( f , ∂ 𝕄 ) = { h ∈ 𝒟 ( 𝕄 ) ∣ f ∘ h = f , h | ∂ 𝕄 = id 𝕄 } \mathcal{S}(f,\partial\mathbb{M})=\{h\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{M})\mid f\circ h=f,\ h|_{\partial\mathbb{M}}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{M}}\} is the subgroup of the corresponding stabilizer of f f consisting of diffeomorphisms fixed on the boundary ∂ 𝕄 \partial\mathbb{M} . As a consequence we obtain an explicit algebraic description of π 1 ​ 𝒪 ​ ( f ) \pi_{1}\mathcal{O}(f) for all non-orientable surfaces distinct from Klein bottle and projective plane.

2.
Main result

In what follows
\mathbf{1}
denotes the unit group, and the symbols
\lhook\joinrel\rightarrow
and
\rightarrow\!\!\!\!\!\to
denote respectively
monomorphism
and
epimorphism
.
In particular,
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}A}\lhook\joinrel\rightarrow{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}B}\rightarrow\!\!\!\!\!\to{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}C}
is a short exact sequence of groups.

First we will need to introduce several types of semidirect products corresponding to certain non-effective actions of the group
\mathbb{Z}
.
All of them are particular cases of the following construction.

Let
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G}
be a group with unit
e
and
\varphi\in\mathrm{Aut}({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G})
be any automorphism of
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G}
.
Then one can define the homomorphism
\widehat{\varphi}:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathrm{Aut}({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G})
,
\widehat{\varphi}(t)=\varphi^{t}
,
t\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Hence, we also have the corresponding semidirect product
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{Z}
which, by definition, is the Cartesian product
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G}\times\mathbb{Z}
of sets with the following multiplication:

(2.1)

(a,k)(b,l)=(a\,\varphi^{k}(b),k+l)

for all
a,b\in{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G}
and
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Furthermore, if
\varphi
is periodic of some order
{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}\geq 1
, then
\widehat{\varphi}
reduces to a monomorphism
\overline{\varphi}:\mathbb{Z}_{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}\to\mathrm{Aut}({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G})
,
\overline{\varphi}(t)=\varphi^{t}
,
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}
, and we also have the semidirect product
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{Z}_{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}
, which again by definition, is a Cartesian product
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G}\times\mathbb{Z}_{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}
with the multiplication given by same formula (
2.1
), but now

should be taken modulo
{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}
.

Evidently, there is the following short exact sequence

\{e\}\times{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}\mathbb{Z}\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ }{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\penalty\ (g,\,k)\,\mapsto\,(g,\,k\bmod{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m})\penalty\ }\!\!\!\!\!\to{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{Z}_{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}.

Groups

and
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G}\wr\mathbb{Z}_{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}

Given

Let also

\displaystyle:={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G}^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G}\wr\mathbb{Z}_{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}

\displaystyle:={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G}^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}\rtimes_{\varphi}\mathbb{Z}_{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}

Groups

and

Then the elements of the

Then there is a natural non-effective action of

(k,l)\cdot\{g_{i,j}\}=\{g_{i+k\bmod{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m},\,j+l\bmod{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}n}}\}.

This action reduces to an effective action of

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be the minimal class of groups (considered up to isomorphism) satisfying the following conditions:

1)

2)

{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G}\times{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}H}\in\mathcal{G}

3)

if
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G}\in\mathcal{G}
and
{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}\geq 1

{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G}\wr_{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}\mathbb{Z}\in\mathcal{G}

It is easy to see,

consists of groups
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G}
which can be obtained from the unit group

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}=\mathbf{1}\wr_{1}\mathbb{Z},

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}^{n},(n\geq 1),

\displaystyle\bigl((\mathbb{Z}^{3}\wr_{5}\mathbb{Z})\times(\mathbb{Z}^{17}\wr_{2}\mathbb{Z})\bigr)\wr_{11}\mathbb{Z}.

is not unique, e.g.

4.
Codimension of singularities

In this Section we give a short and elementary proof of finiteness of Milnor numbers of homogeneous polynomials
g\in\mathbb{R}[x,y]
without multiple factors, (Lemma
4.7
), which is a principal property allowing to compute the homotopy types of orbits of maps from
\mathcal{F}(M,P)
.
In fact, that statement follows from general results of algebraic geometry, see Lemma
4.4
, and is used in mentioned above papers, e.g.
[
31
]
, however the authors did not find its explicit proof in available literature.

We will also discuss structure of level-sets of isolated critical points on surfaces.
These results will not be directly used in the proofs of main results and may be skipped on first reading.

4.1.
Maps of finite codimension

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathsf{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the algebra of germs at the origin
\mathsf{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
.
For
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathsf{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denote by
J_{\mathbb{R}}(f)
the ideal in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathsf{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
generated by germs of partial derivatives of
f
.
Then the real codimension of
J_{\mathbb{R}}(f)
in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathsf{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
:

(4.1)

\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(f):=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\bigl(\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathsf{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/J_{\mathbb{R}}(f)\bigr)

is called the
codimension
or
Milnor number
or
multiplicity
of

at the critical point
\mathsf{0}
with respect to the algebra
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathsf{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The well-known Tougeron theorem
[
41
]
claims that
if
\mathsf{0}
is a critical point of a finite codimension
k
of a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
germ
f:(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathsf{0})\to(\mathbb{R},\mathsf{0})
, then
f
is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
equivalent to its Taylor polynomial of order
k+1
.

It is easy to see that
\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(f)
does not depend on local coordinates at
\mathsf{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and therefore it can be defined for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
functions on manifolds and even for
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
circle-valued maps.

Now let
M
be a smooth compact manifold and
P
be either
\mathbb{R}
or
S^{1}
.
Say that a map

is of

if it has only finitely many critical points and at each of them

has finite codimension (
4.1
).

Theorem 4.2

(
[
40
,
25
]
)
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}_{\partial}^{\infty}(M,P)
be a map of finite codimension and
Y
be any collection of boundary components of
M
.
Then

(a)

the corresponding orbit
\mathcal{O}(f,Y)
is a Fréchet submanifold of finite codimension of the Fréchet manifold
\mathcal{C}_{\partial}^{\infty}(M,P)
,

(b)

the natural map
\nu:\mathcal{D}(M,Y)\to\mathcal{O}(f,Y)
,
h\mapsto f\circ h
, is a principal locally trivial fibration with fiber
\mathcal{S}(f,Y)
;

(c)

\nu(\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}(M,Y))=\mathcal{O}_{f}(f,Y)
and the restriction map
\nu:\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}(M,Y)\to\mathcal{O}_{f}(f,Y)
,

, is also principal locally trivial fibration with fiber
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(f,Y):=\mathcal{S}(f,Y)\cap\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}(M,Y)
.

Remarks to the proof.

Note that
(c)
directly follows from
(b)
.
For the statements
(a)
and
(b)
consider first the so-called
left-right
action

\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{D}(M)\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}),\qquad(\phi,f,h)\mapsto\phi\circ f\circ h,

of the group
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{D}(M)
on
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
.
F. Sergeraert
[
40
]
proved that if
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
is a smooth function
of finite codimension
, then the corresponding orbit

\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(f)=\{\phi\circ f\circ h\mid(\phi,h)\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{D}(M)\}

is a Fréchet submanifold of finite codimension of the Fréchet space
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
, while
the natural map
\nu:\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})\times\mathcal{D}(M)\to\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(f)

(\phi,h)\mapsto\phi\circ f\circ h

In
[
25

such a result was extended to ‘‘tame actions of tame Lie groups on tame Fréchet manifolds’’, where

is understood in the sense of R. Hamilton
[
13

In particular, this implied that
(a)
and
(b)
hold for

[
25
, Section 11.30]

However, almost literally the same arguments show that the same result holds for the above map

, where
Y
is any (possibly empty) collection of boundary components of
M
and

is a map of finite codimension.
One should just

-

write in Eq. (2) of
[
25

g(x)
as

where
c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
are some constants, and
\lambda_{i}

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
functions which span the (finite-dimensional) complement to the Jacobi ideal of

at

-

note that in Eq. (3) of
[

the vector field

, and thus belongs to the tangent space at

of the group
\mathcal{D}(M,Y)
not only of
\mathcal{D}(M)

∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathsf{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be an analytic germ, i.e. a series with real coefficients converging on some neighborhood of
\mathsf{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
; for instance
f
can be a polynomial.
Regard
f
as complex series with real coefficients, i.e. as an element of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.
Then either of the conditions

(

⁢
μ
1

)

-

(

⁢
μ
5

)

implies that
\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(f)<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathsf{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Then by Hadamard lemma there are
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
germs
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathsf{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
b_{0}=\phi(\mathsf{0})
such that
\phi(\mathbf{x})=b_{0}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}(\mathbf{x})x_{j}
, where
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Applying the same statement to each
\beta_{j}
and so on we get that for each

we have the identity

(4.2)

\phi(x,y)=\sum_{0\leq a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}<p}b_{ij}x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}+\sum_{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}=p}\beta_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}}(\mathbf{x})x_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{a_{n}}

for some
b_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\beta_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathsf{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Hence, as in the proof of

(

⁢
μ
3

)

\Rightarrow

(

⁢
μ
5

)

in Lemma

, in order to prove that

,
it suffices to show that there exists
m\geq 1
such that

for all

.
Then for some large

, the second term in (

) will belong to
J_{\mathbb{R}}(g)
, whence the vector space
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{\mathsf{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/J_{\mathbb{R}}(g)
over

will be generated by a finite set of monomials of degree
<p
.

Regard
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as a subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of real coordinates.
By assumption

, as an element of
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, satisfies condition

(

⁢
μ
1

)

, so there exist

and
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{n}\in\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
such that

(4.3)

z_{i}^{m}=\tau_{1}(\mathbf{z})f^{\prime}_{z_{1}}(\mathbf{z})+\cdots+\tau_{n}(\mathbf{z})f^{\prime}_{z_{n}}(\mathbf{z}),

for all

, where
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

Let

be the decomposition of

into the real and imaginary parts.
Then

and

are converging series near

4.6.
Homogeneous polynomials in two variables

Let
g:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
be a real homogeneous polynomial with
\deg g\geq 2
.
Then it directly follows from fundamental theorem of algebra that
g
splits into a product of finitely many linear and irreducible over
\mathbb{R}
quadratic factors:

(4.4)

g(x,y)=\prod_{i=1}^{p}(a_{i}x+b_{i}y)\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{q}(c_{j}x^{2}+2d_{j}xy+e_{j}y^{2}).

Evidently,

(1)

g^{-1}(0)
is a union of straight lines
\{a_{i}x+b_{i}y=0\}
corresponding to the linear factors;

(2)

if
g
has two proportional linear factors, then all points of the corresponding line are critical for
g
;

(3)

hence,
g
has no multiple (non-proportional)
linear
factors if and only if the origin
\mathsf{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is an isolated critical point;
in this case
g^{-1}(0)
consists of
2p
rays starting from the origin;

(4)

the following conditions are equivalent due to Lemma
4.4
:

(4a)

g
has no multiple factors at all;

(4b)

the partial derivatives
g^{\prime}_{x}
and
g^{\prime}_{x}
of
g
have no common factors;

(4c)

the origin
\mathsf{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
is an isolated critical point of
g:\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}
as a complex polynomial with real coefficients;

(4d)

\mu_{\mathbb{C}}(g)<\infty
.

Moreover, by Corollary
4.5
, they also imply that
\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(g)<\infty
.

For the completeness and for the convenience of the reader we present a short explicit and not based on Lemma
4.4
proof that
(4)(4a)
implies
\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(g)<\infty
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Suppose
g\in\mathbb{R}[x,y]
is a homogeneous polynomial without multiple factors and
\deg g\geq 2
.
Then
x^{m},y^{m}\in J_{\mathbb{R}}(f)
for some large
m\geq 1
, and therefore
\mu_{\mathbb{R}}(g)<\infty
.

In particular, if
M
is a compact surface, then each
f\in\mathcal{F}(M,P)
is of finite codimension, and Theorem
4.2
holds for
f
.

Proof.

Put
k=\deg g-1
.
To simplify notation also redenote
A:=g^{\prime}_{x}
and
B:=g^{\prime}_{y}
.
Then
A
and
B
are homogeneous polynomials of degree
k
.
As mentioned above, the assumption that
g
has no multiple factors implies that
A
and
B
are relatively prime in
\mathbb{R}[x,y]
.
We will find homogeneous polynomials
P,Q\in\mathbb{R}[x,y]
such that
AP+BQ=x^{m}
for some
m\geq 1
.
This will mean that
x^{m}\in J_{\mathbb{R}}(f)
.
The proof that
y^{m}\in J_{\mathbb{R}}(f)
for some
m
is similar.

Consider the following polynomials
\alpha(t):=A(1,t)
,
\beta(t):=B(1,t)\in\mathbb{R}[t]
.
Since
A
and
B
are homogeneous of degree
k
, we have that

\displaystyle A(x,y)

\displaystyle=\alpha(y/x)x^{k},

\displaystyle B(x,y)

\displaystyle=\beta(y/x)x^{k}.

Moreover,
\mathrm{gcd}(\alpha(t),\beta(t))=1
in
\mathbb{R}[t]
, since
\mathrm{gcd}(A,B)=1
in
\mathbb{R}[x,y]
.
In particular, using Euclid division algorithm, one can find polynomials
p,q\in\mathbb{R}[t]
such that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\alpha(t)p(t)+\beta(t)q(t)=1.

This implies that
\deg(\alpha p)=\deg(\beta q)
, and we will denote this common degree by
m
.
It follows that
P:=p(y/x)x^{\deg p}
and
Q:=q(y/x)x^{\deg q}
are homogeneous polynomials in
\mathbb{R}[x,y]
.
Multiplying (
4.5
) by
x^{m}
and substituting
t=y/x
we get:

\bigl(\alpha(y/x)x^{\deg\alpha}\bigr)\,\bigl(p(y/x)x^{\deg p}\bigr)\ +\ \bigl(\beta(y/x)x^{\deg\beta}\bigr)\,\bigl(q(y/x)x^{\deg q}\bigr)\ =\ x^{m},

i.e.
AP+BQ=x^{m}\in J_{\mathbb{R}}(f)
.

Now, let
M
be a compact surface and
f\in\mathcal{F}(M,P)
.
Then
f
has finitely many critical points and at each of them its germ is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
equivalent to a homogeneous polynomial without multiple factors.
As just proved the Milnor number of
f
at each critical point is finite.
Therefore,
f
is also of finite codimension.
∎

5.
Functions on surfaces

Let
M
be a compact surface,
f\in\mathcal{F}(M,P)
, and
\Sigma_{f}
be the set of all critical points of
f
.
Then every connected component of each level set of
f
will be called a

of
f
.
A contour

of
f
is
critical
if it contains critical points of
f
, and
regular
otherwise.
Evidently, each regular contour is a submanifold of

diffeomorphic to the circle.

On the other hand, due to Axiom
(H)
, see also Figure
4.1
, a critical contour has a structure of a
1
-dimensional CW-complex whose
0
-cells are critical points of
f
belonging to
X
, while the connected components of
X\setminus\Sigma_{f}
being one-dimensional submanifolds of
M
are
1
-cells of
X
.

Denote by
\Gamma_{f}
the partition of
M
into contours of
f
, and let
p:M\to\Gamma_{f}
be the quotient map associating to each
x\in M
the contour of
f
containing
x
.
Endow
\Gamma_{f}
with the corresponding quotient topology with respect to
p
.
Then it is well known that
\Gamma_{f}
has a structure of a finite
1
-dimensional CW-complex and is called
Kronrod-Reeb graph
of

,
[
39
,
1
,
15
]
.

A subset
X\subset M
will be called
f
-adapted
if it is a union of (possibly uncountably many) contours of
f
.
In other words,
X=p^{-1}(p(X))
.

For instance, a compact
1
-dimensional submanifold
X
of
M
is
f
-adapted whenever it is a union of finitely many regular contours.
Also, a compact
2
-dimensional submanifold
X
of
M
is
f
-adapted iff
\partial X
is
f
-adapted, i.e. every boundary component of
X
is a contour.
For example, if
f\in\mathcal{F}(M,\mathbb{R})
and
a<b
are two regular values of
f
, then
f^{-1}\bigl([a;b]\bigr)
is
f
-adapted.

Let
X\subset M
be a connected
f
-adapted submanifold.
Then by an
f
-regular neighborhood
of
X
we will mean an arbitrary connected
f
-adapted subsurface
U_{X}
such that

U_{X}
is a neighborhood of
X
and
U_{X}\setminus X
contains no critical points of
f
.

More generally, suppose
X=\mathop{\cup}\limits_{i=1}^{k}X_{i}
is a disjoint union of connected
f
-adapted submanifolds
X_{i}
.
For every
i=1,\ldots,k
choose any
f
-regular neighborhood
U_{X_{i}}
of
X_{i}
such that
U_{X_{i}}\cap U_{X_{j}}=\varnothing
for
i\not=j
.
Then their union
U_{X}=\mathop{\cup}\limits_{i=1}^{k}U_{X_{i}}
, will also be called an
f
-regular neighborhood
of
X
.

Denote by
\Delta(f)
the subgroup of
\mathcal{S}(f)
consisting of diffeomorphisms
h
having the following properties:

(1)

h
leaves invariant every connected component of every level set of
f
, i.e. it preserves the elements of
\Gamma_{f}
;

(2)

if
z
is a
degenerate local extreme
, and thus
h(z)=z
, then the tangent map
T_{z}h:T_{z}M\to T_{z}M
is the identity
\mathrm{id}_{T_{z}M}
.

Evidently,
\Delta(f)
is a normal subgroup of
\mathcal{S}(f)
.
Moreover, it follows from
[
27
, Lemma 6.2]
, describing local linear symmetries of homogeneous polynomials, that the quotient
\mathcal{S}(f)/\Delta(f)
is finite.
In fact, it can be regarded as a group of automorphisms of an “enhanced” Kronrod-Reeb graph of
f
induced by elements of
\mathcal{S}(f)
, see
[
31
, Section 4]
.
That graph is obtained from
\Gamma_{f}
by adding a certain finite number edges to each vertex corresponding to a
degenerate local extreme
of
f
, so that every
h\in\mathcal{S}(f)
“cyclically rotates” those new edges.
In particular, if
f
is Morse, so it has no degenerated critical points, then that “enhanced” graph coincides with
\Gamma_{f}
, and in that case
\mathcal{S}(f)/\Delta(f)
is the group of automorphisms of
\Gamma_{f}
induced by
\mathcal{S}(f)
.
However, we will not use that interpretation in the present paper.

For a closed subset
X\subset M
denote

(5.1)

\displaystyle\Delta(f,X)

\displaystyle:=\Delta(f)\cap\mathcal{D}(M,X),

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}(f,X)

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{S}(f,X)/\Delta(f,X),

\displaystyle\Delta^{\prime}(f,X)

\displaystyle:=\Delta(f)\cap\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{id}}(M,X),

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}^{\prime}(f,X)

\displaystyle:=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(f,X)/\Delta^{\prime}(f,X).

Then we have the following short exact sequences

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}(f,X)

\displaystyle:\Delta(f,X)\lhook\joinrel\rightarrow\mathcal{S}(f,X)\rightarrow\!\!\!\!\!\to\mathbf{G}(f,X),

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}^{\prime}(f,X)

\displaystyle:\Delta^{\prime}(f,X)\lhook\joinrel\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(f,X)\rightarrow\!\!\!\!\!\to\mathbf{G}^{\prime}(f,X).

The second sequence (
5.2
) will play the most important role, and we will call it the

of
(f,X)
, see
[
31
]
.
For instance,
[
31
, Theorem 5.4]
, in the statement

of Theorem

the inclusions (

\mathbf{b}^{\prime}(f,Y)\ \cong\ \mathbf{b}^{\prime}(f,Y\cup X)\ \cong\ \prod\limits_{i=1}^{p}\mathbf{b}^{\prime}(f|_{L_{i}},\partial L_{i}).

Our results will also be given in terms of such sequences, see proofs of Lemmas

Finally, denote by

6.6.

\pi_{0}\mathcal{S}(f,\partial\mathbb{M})\ \cong\ \left({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}G},{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}H}\right)\wr_{\gamma,{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}\mathbb{Z}
, but also of an isomorphism of the diagram (
7.5
) onto
(3\times 3)
-diagram from that lemma.
We leave the details for the reader, see also Figures
8.5
and
8.6
.

9.
Proof of Theorem
7.7

Suppose the cylinder
Y_{0}
contains no critical points of
f
.

(a)

Let us check that
\ker(\eta)=\mathop{\prod}\limits_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{0}\mathcal{S}(f|_{Y_{i}},\partial Y_{i})
.
Suppose
h\in\mathcal{S}(f,\partial\mathbb{M})
is supported in
\mathop{\cup}\limits_{i=1}^{n}Y_{i}
, i.e. fixed on
U_{K}\cup Y_{0}\supset Q_{0}
.
In other words,
h|_{Q_{0}}=\mathrm{id}_{Q_{0}}
, whence its unique lifting
\mathbf{h}:\tilde{Q}_{0}\to\tilde{Q}_{0}
fixed on
\mathbb{R}\times 0
must be the identity, so
\mathbf{h}=\mathrm{id}_{\tilde{Q}_{0}}
.
Hence,
\mathbf{h}(J_{k})=J_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, and therefore
\eta(h)=0
.
This implies that
\mathop{\prod}\limits_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{0}\mathcal{S}(f|_{Y_{i}},\partial Y_{i})\subset\ker(\eta)
.

Conversely, since
\mathcal{S}(f;\hat{\mathbf{Y}})=\eta^{-1}({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}\mathbb{Z})
, we have that
\ker(\eta)\subset\mathcal{S}(f;\hat{\mathbf{Y}})
.
Moreover, by Corollary
7.5
, there is an isomorphism

\pi_{0}\mathcal{S}(f;\hat{\mathbf{Y}})\cong\bigl(\mathop{\prod}\limits_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{0}\mathcal{S}(f|_{Y_{i}},\partial Y_{i})\bigr)\times\mathbb{Z},

where the generator of the multiple
\mathbb{Z}
corresponds to the Dehn twist
\tau
along
\partial\mathbb{M}
supported in
Y_{0}
.
But, due to (
6.3
),
\eta(\tau)={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}
, whence
\ker(\eta)\subset\mathop{\prod}\limits_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{0}\mathcal{S}(f|_{Y_{i}},\partial Y_{i})
.

(b)

We need to find
g\in\mathcal{S}(f,\partial\mathbb{M})
such that

a)

\eta(g)=1
,

b)

g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}\in\pi_{0}\Delta(f,\partial\mathbb{M})
,

c)

g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}
also commutes with
\ker(\eta)
.

The proof resembles
[
31
, Lemma 13.1(3)]
, and also uses the result from the paper
[
23
]
which was especially written to include all technicalities needed for the proof of the existence of
g
.

If
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}=1
we can take
g=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{M}}
and all conditions will trivially hold.

Thus suppose
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}\geq 2
.
Take any
q\in\mathcal{S}(f,\partial\mathbb{M})
such that
\eta(q)=1
.
We will change
q
so that conditions
a)
-
c)
will be satisfied.

Recall that the disks
Y_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,n
, can be divided into disks of types
(b)(T1)
and
(b)(T2)
, and also enumerated in accordance with (
8.2
) and (
8.3
),
so that
q
will cyclically shift the columns to the right.
Also if disks of type
(b)(T2)
are presented, then
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}
is even and in this case we put
{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}/2
.

It will also be convenient to denote by

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}-1}

\displaystyle:=\mathop{\cup}\limits_{j=1}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}d}}D_{j}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}-1},

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}-1}

\displaystyle:=\mathop{\cup}\limits_{j=1}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}e}}E_{j}^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}-1},

the unions of disks standing in the last columns of (
8.2
) and (
8.3
).
These sets might be empty, if the disks of the corresponding types are not presented.

Now we need to construct two additional diffeomorphisms
h
and
k
depending on existence of disks of those types.

(1) Suppose there are disks of type
(b)(T1)
.
Note that
q^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}\in\mathcal{S}(f;\hat{\mathbf{Y}})=\eta^{-1}({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}\mathbb{Z})
, since
\eta(q^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}})={\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}
.
Recall that due to Lemma
7.4
the inclusion

j:\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{nb}}}(f,U_{K}\cup\partial\mathbb{M})\subset\mathcal{S}(f;\hat{\mathbf{Y}})

is a homotopy equivalence, whence
q^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}
is isotopic in
\mathcal{S}(f;\hat{\mathbf{Y}})
to some diffeomorphism
h_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{{\mathrm{nb}}}(f,U_{K}\cup\partial\mathbb{M})
.
Moreover, “releasing
\partial\mathbb{M}
” we see that
h_{0}
is in turn isotopic in
\mathcal{S}(f)
to a diffeomorphism
h
fixed on
U_{K}\cup Y_{0}
via an isotopy supported in
Y_{0}
.

Hence,
h^{-1}\circ q^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{id}}(f)
.
Then for each
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}-1\}
we have that

(h1)

q^{i}\circ h^{-1}\circ q^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}-i}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{id}}(f)
, since
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{id}}(f)
is a normal subgroup of
\mathcal{S}(f)
;

(h2)

h^{-1}\circ q=q:\mathsf{D}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}-1}\to\mathsf{D}^{0}
, since in particular,
h
is fixed near
\partial\mathsf{D}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}-1}
.

(2) Suppose now that there are disks of type
(b)(T2)
.
Then for each
i,j
we have a reversing orientation diffeomorphism
q^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}:E_{j}^{i}\to E_{j}^{i}
.
In particular, by
[
23
, Theorem 3.5]
, applied to each connected components of
\mathsf{E}^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}-1}
, there exists a diffeomorphism
k:\mathsf{E}^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}-1}\to\mathsf{E}^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}-1}
such that
k=q^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}
near
\partial\mathsf{E}^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}-1}
, and
k^{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{id}}(f|_{\mathsf{E}^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}-1}})
.
It follows that

(k1)

k^{-1}\circ q^{1-{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}=q:\mathsf{E}^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}-1}\to\mathsf{E}^{0}
near some neighborhood of
\partial\mathsf{E}^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}-1}
.

Now define the map
g:\mathbb{M}\to\mathbb{M}
by the following rule:

g(x)=\begin{cases}h^{-1}\circ q,&x\in\mathsf{D}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}-1},\\
k^{-1}\circ q^{1-{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}},&x\in\mathsf{E}^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}-1},\\
q(x),&x\in\mathbb{M}\setminus(\mathsf{D}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}-1}\cup\mathsf{E}^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}-1}).\end{cases}

Due to
(h2)
and
(k1)
,
g
is a diffeomorphism.
It is easy to see that
g
is also
f
-preserving, i.e.
g\in\mathcal{S}(f,\partial\mathbb{M})
.
We claim that
g
satisfies conditions
a)
-
c)
.

a)

Since
g=q
on
\mathbb{M}\setminus(\mathsf{D}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}-1}\cup\mathsf{E}^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}-1})\supset Q_{0}
, it follows that
\eta(h)=\eta(q)=1
.

b)

Since
g
, and therefore
g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}
, are fixed on
\partial\mathbb{M}
, it suffices to prove that
g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}\in\Delta(f)
.

Moreover,
g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}=q^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}\in\mathcal{S}(f;\hat{\mathbf{Y}})
, and therefore it leaves invariant each
Y_{i}
,
i=0,\ldots,n
, and
U_{K}
.
Therefore, due to Lemma
5.2
, if
A
denotes either of the surfaces
Y_{i}
or
U_{K}\cup Y_{0}
, then we need to check that the restriction of
g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}|_{A}
belongs to the corresponding group
\Delta(f|_{A})
.

Since
g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}\in\mathcal{S}(f;\hat{\mathbf{Y}})
, it follows that
g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}
leaves invariant all contours of
f
contained in
U_{K}\cup Y_{0}
, i.e.
g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}|_{U_{K}\cup Y_{0}}\in\Delta(f|_{U_{K}\cup Y_{0}})
.

Furthermore, suppose there are disks of type
(b)(T1)
.
Then for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}-1\}
the map
g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}|_{\mathsf{D}^{i}}:\mathsf{D}^{i}\to\mathsf{D}^{i}
is the following composition:

q^{i}\circ h^{-1}\circ q^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}-i}:\mathsf{D}^{i}\underbrace{\xrightarrow{q}\mathsf{D}^{i+1}\xrightarrow{q}\cdots\xrightarrow{q}}_{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}-i-1\ \text{arrows}}\mathsf{D}^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}-1}\xrightarrow{h^{-1}\circ q}\mathsf{D}^{0}\underbrace{\xrightarrow{q}\cdots\xrightarrow{q}}_{i\ \text{arrows}}\mathsf{D}^{i},

which by
(h1)
belongs to
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{id}}(f|_{\mathsf{D}^{i}})\subset\Delta(f|_{\mathsf{D}^{i}})
.

Similarly, suppose that there exist disks of type
(b)(T2)
.
Then for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}-1\}
the restriction map
g^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}:\mathsf{E}^{i}\to\mathsf{E}^{i}
is a composition of the following maps:

q^{i}\circ k^{-1}\circ q^{-i}:\mathsf{E}^{i}\underbrace{\xrightarrow{q}\mathsf{E}^{i+1}\xrightarrow{q}\cdots\xrightarrow{q}}_{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}-i-1\ \text{arrows}}\mathsf{E}^{{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}-1}\xrightarrow{k^{-1}\circ q^{1-{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}}\mathsf{E}^{0}\underbrace{\xrightarrow{q}\cdots\xrightarrow{q}}_{i\ \text{arrows}}\mathsf{E}^{i}.

Whence
g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}=g^{2{\color[rgb]{0.64,0.08,0.98}m}}=q^{i}\circ k^{-2}\circ q^{-i}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{id}}(f|_{\mathsf{E}^{i}})\subset\Delta(f|_{\mathsf{E}^{i}})
, since
k^{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{id}}(f|_{\mathsf{E}^{0}})
.

c)

It remains to check that the isotopy class of
g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}
in
\pi_{0}\mathcal{S}(f,\partial\mathbb{M})
commutes with
\ker(\eta)=\mathop{\prod}\limits_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{0}\mathcal{S}(f|_{Y_{i}},\partial Y_{i})
.
Fix any
f
-regular neighborhood
U^{\prime}
of
U_{K}
.

Let
\gamma\in\ker(\eta)=\mathop{\prod}\limits_{i=1}^{n}\pi_{0}\mathcal{S}(f|_{Y_{i}},\partial Y_{i})\subset\pi_{0}\mathcal{S}(f,\partial\mathbb{M})
be any element and
h\in\mathcal{S}(f,\partial\mathbb{M})
be any representative of
\gamma
.
Then one can assume that
h
is fixed on
U^{\prime}\cup Y_{0}
.
We will show that
g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}
is isotopic in
\mathcal{S}(f,\partial\mathbb{M})
to a diffeomorphism supported in
U^{\prime}\cup Y_{0}
, and therefore commuting with
h
.
This will imply that the isotopy classes of
g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}
and
h
commute in
\pi_{0}\mathcal{S}(f,\partial\mathbb{M})
.

Indeed, for
i=1,\ldots,n
denote
U_{i}:=Y_{i}\cap(U^{\prime}\setminus U_{K})
.
Then
U_{i}
is evidently a
f|_{Y_{i}}
-regular neighborhood (even a
collar
) of
\partial Y_{i}
.
As noted in the proof of
b)
,
g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}|_{Y_{i}}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{id}}(f|_{Y_{i}})
,
i=1,\ldots,n
.
Hence, by Lemma
5.3
,
g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}|_{Y_{i}}
is isotopic relatively some neighborhood of
\partial Y_{i}
to a diffeomorphism supported in
U_{i}
.
That isotopy extends by the identity to an isotopy of all
g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}
.

Applying these arguments for all
i=1,\ldots,n
, we deform
g^{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}b}}
in
\mathcal{S}(f,\partial\mathbb{M})
to a diffeomorphism supported in
U^{\prime}\cup Y_{0}
.

This completes the proof of Theorem
7.7
.

Abstract
The experimental results on the ratios of branching fractions ℛ ⁡ ( D ) = ℬ ⁡ ( B ¯ → D ​ τ − ​ ν ¯ τ ) / ℬ ⁡ ( B ¯ → D ​ ℓ − ​ ν ¯ ℓ ) \mathcal{R}(D)={\cal B}(\bar{B}\to D\tau^{-}\bar{\nu}_{\tau})/{\cal B}(\bar{B}\to D\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}_{\ell}) and ℛ ⁡ ( D ∗ ) = ℬ ⁡ ( B ¯ → D ∗ ​ τ − ​ ν ¯ τ ) / ℬ ⁡ ( B ¯ → D ∗ ​ ℓ − ​ ν ¯ ℓ ) \mathcal{R}(D^{\ast})={\cal B}(\bar{B}\to D^{*}\tau^{-}\bar{\nu}_{\tau})/{\cal B}(\bar{B}\to D^{*}\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}_{\ell}) , where ℓ \ell denotes an electron or a muon, show a long-standing discrepancy with the Standard Model predictions, and might hint to a violation of lepton flavor universality. We report a new simultaneous measurement of ℛ ⁡ ( D ) \mathcal{R}(D) and ℛ ⁡ ( D ∗ ) \mathcal{R}(D^{\ast}) , based on a data sample containing 772 × 10 6 772\times 10^{6} B ​ B ¯ B\bar{B} events recorded at the Υ ( 4 S ) {\mathit{{\Upsilon}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{\mathit{{\left({4S}\right)}}} resonance with the Belle detector at the KEKB e + ​ e − e^{+}e^{-} collider. In this analysis the tag-side B B meson is reconstructed in a semileptonic decay mode and the signal-side τ \tau is reconstructed in a purely leptonic decay. The measured values are ℛ ⁡ ( D ) = 0.307 ± 0.037 ± 0.016 \mathcal{R}(D)\ =0.307\pm 0.037\pm 0.016 and ℛ ⁡ ( D ∗ ) = 0.283 ± 0.018 ± 0.014 \mathcal{R}(D^{\ast})\ =0.283\pm 0.018\pm 0.014 , where the first uncertainties are statistical and the second are systematic. These results are in agreement with the Standard Model predictions within 0.2 0.2 , 1.1 1.1 and 0.8 0.8 standard deviations for ℛ ⁡ ( D ) \mathcal{R}(D) , ℛ ⁡ ( D ∗ ) \mathcal{R}(D^{\ast}) and their combination, respectively. This work constitutes the most precise measurements of ℛ ⁡ ( D ) \mathcal{R}(D) and ℛ ⁡ ( D ∗ ) \mathcal{R}(D^{\ast}) performed to date as well as the first result for ℛ ⁡ ( D ) \mathcal{R}(D) based on a semileptonic tagging method.

Semitauonic
B
meson decays, involving the transition
b\to c\tau\nu_{\tau}
, are sensitive probes for physics beyond the Standard Model (SM). Any difference in the branching fraction of these processes with respect to the SM prediction would violate lepton flavor universality, which enforces equal coupling of the gauge bosons to the three lepton generations. Indeed, in many models beyond the SM, new interactions with enhanced coupling to the third family are postulated. Among such new mediators, charged Higgs bosons, which appear in supersymmetry
Martin 1998
and other models with two Higgs doublets
Gunion et al. 2000
, may contribute measurably to the
b\to c\tau\nu_{\tau}
decay rate due to the large masses of the
\tau
and the
b
quark. Similarly, leptoquarks
Buchmuller et al. 1987
, which carry both lepton and baryon numbers, may also contribute to this process.

The ratios of branching fractions,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(D^{(\ast)})=\frac{{\cal B}(\bar{B}\to D^{(*)}\tau^{-}\bar{\nu}_{\tau})}{{\cal B}(\bar{B}\to D^{(*)}\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}_{\ell})}

(1)

where the denominator represents the average of electron and muon modes,
are typically measured instead of the absolute branching fractions of
\bar{B}\to D^{(*)}\tau^{-}\bar{\nu}_{\tau}
to reduce common systematic uncertainties, such as those due to the detection efficiency, the magnitude of the quark-mixing matrix element
|V_{cb}|
, and the semileptonic decay form factors. Hereafter,
\bar{B}\to D^{(*)}\tau^{-}\bar{\nu}_{\tau}

not
and
\bar{B}\to D^{(*)}\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}_{\ell}
will be referred to as the signal and normalization modes, respectively. The SM calculations for these ratios, performed by several groups
Bigi and Gambino 2016
;
Bernlochner et al. 2017
;
Bigi et al. 2017
;
Jaiswal et al. 2017
, are averaged by HFLAV
Amhis et al. 2019
to obtain

\mathcal{R}(D)=0.299\pm 0.003
and

\mathcal{R}(D^{\ast})=0.258\pm 0.005
.

Semitauonic
B

, with subsequent studies reported by Belle

Huschle et al. 2015

The average values of the experimental results, excluding the result presented in this Letter, are

\mathcal{R}(D)=0.407\pm 0.039\pm 0.024
and

\mathcal{R}(D^{\ast})=0.306\pm 0.013\pm 0.007

We use the full
{\mathit{{\Upsilon}{}_{\mspace{-3mu}\scriptstyle{}}^{\mspace{-1mu}\scriptstyle{}}}}{\mathit{{\left({4S}\right)}}}

Belle was a general-purpose magnetic spectrometer, which consisted of a silicon vertex detector (SVD), a 50-layer central drift chamber (CDC), an array of aerogel threshold Cherenkov counters (ACC), time-of-flight scintillation counters (TOF), and an electromagnetic calorimeter (ECL) comprising CsI(Tl) crystals. To determine the reconstruction efficiency and probability density functions (PDFs) for signal, normalization, and background modes, we use Monte Carlo (MC) simulated events generated with the EvtGen event generator

,

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{B,D^{(\ast)}\ell}\equiv\frac{2E_{\rm beam}E_{D^{(*)}\ell}-m_{B}^{2}-m_{D^{(*)}\ell}^{2}}{2|\bm{p}_{B}||\bm{p}_{D^{(*)}\ell}|}.

(2)

In each event with a selected
{B}_{\text{tag}}

Charged particle tracks are reconstructed with the SVD and CDC by requiring a point of closest approach to the interaction point smaller than 5.0 cm along the direction of the
e^{+}

To recover bremsstrahlung photons from electrons, we add the four-momentum of each photon detected within a cone of 0.05 rad of the original track direction to the electron momentum. Muons are identified by the track penetration depth and hit distribution in the KLM. Charged kaons are identified by combining information from the
dE/dx
measured in the CDC, the flight time measured with the TOF, and the response of the ACC. We do not apply any particle identification criteria for charged pion candidates.

To distinguish signal and normalization events from background processes, we use the sum of the energies of neutral clusters detected in the ECL that are not associated with any reconstructed particles, denoted as

To mitigate the varying effects of photons related to beam background in the calculation of

We extract the yields of signal and normalization modes from a two-dimensional (2D) extended maximum-likelihood fit to the variables

To improve the accuracy of the MC simulation, we apply a series of correction factors determined from control sample measurements, such as those associated to lepton and hadron identification efficiencies as well as slow pion tracking efficiencies.
Correction factors for the lepton efficiencies are evaluated as a function of the lepton momentum and direction using

Furthermore, to determine the expected yield of fake and misreconstructed

In conclusion, we have measured the ratios
\mathcal{R}(D^{(\ast)})={\cal B}(\bar{B}\to D^{(*)}\tau^{-}\bar{\nu}_{\tau})/{\cal B}(\bar{B}\to D^{(*)}\ell^{-}\bar{\nu}_{\ell})
, where
\ell
denotes an electron or a muon, using a semileptonic tagging method and a data sample containing
772\times 10^{6}B\bar{B}
events collected with the Belle detector. The results are

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(D)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.307\pm 0.037\pm 0.016

(4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(D^{\ast})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.283\pm 0.018\pm 0.014,

(5)

where the first uncertainties are statistical and the second are systematic. These results are in agreement with the SM predictions within
0.2\sigma
and
1.1\sigma
, respectively. The combined result agrees with the SM predictions within
0.8\sigma
. This work constitutes the most precise measurements of
\mathcal{R}(D)
and
\mathcal{R}(D^{\ast})
performed to date and the first result for
\mathcal{R}(D)
based on a semileptonic tagging method. The results of this analysis, together with the most recent Belle results on
\mathcal{R}(D)
and
\mathcal{R}(D^{\ast})
(
Huschle et al. 2015
;
Hirose et al. 2018
) obtained using a hadronic tag, are combined to provide the Belle combination, which yields
\mathcal{R}(D)=0.326\pm 0.034,\ \mathcal{R}(D^{*})=0.283\pm 0.018
with a correlation equal to
-0.47
between the
\mathcal{R}(D)
and
\mathcal{R}(D^{\ast})
values. This combined result is in agreement with the SM predictions within 1.6 standard deviations.

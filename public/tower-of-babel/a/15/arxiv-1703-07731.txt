Abstract
The special limit of the totally asymmetric zero range process of the low-dimensional non-equilibrium statistical mechanics described by the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian is considered. The calculation of the conditional probabilities of the model are based on the algebraic Bethe ansatz approach. We demonstrate that the conditional probabilities may be considered as the generating functions of the random multi-dimensional lattice walks bounded by a hyperplane. This type of walks we call the walks over the multi-dimensional simplicial lattices. The answers for the conditional probability and for the number of random walks in the multi-dimensional simplicial lattice are expressed through the symmetric functions.

Introduction

The zero-range process is a stochastic lattice gas where the particles hop randomly with an on-site interaction that makes the jump rate dependent only on the local particle number. The zero range processes (ZRPs) belong to a class of minimal statistical-mechanics models of the low-dimensional non-equilibrium physics
[
12
,
30
]
. Being exactly solvable the model and its other variations are intensively studied both by mathematicians and physicists
[
9
,
15
,
16
,
21
,
25
,
26
]
.

In this paper we consider the totally asymmetric simple zero range process (TASZRP)
[
13
,
17
]
, which describes a system of indistinguishable particles placed on a one-dimensional lattice, moving randomly in one direction from right to left with the equal hopping rate on a periodic ring. The dynamical variables of the model are the
phase operators

[
11
]
which can be regarded as a special limit of
q
-bosons
[
5
,
19
]
. The application of the quantum inverse method (QIM)
[
13
,
17
,
20
]
allows to calculate the scalar products and form-factors of the model and represent them in the determinantal form
[
3
,
8
]
. The relation of the considered model and the totaly asymmetric simple exclusion process (TASEP) was discussed in
[
8
,
27
]
.

Certain quantum integrable models solvable by the QIM demonstrate close relationship
[
2
,
6
,
7
]
with the different objects of the enumerative combinatorics
[
31
,
32
]
and the theory of the symmetric functions
[

]

Different types of random walks

,

,

In this paper we shall calculate the conditional probability of the model and reveal its connection with the generating function of the lattice paths
on multi-dimensional oriented lattices bounded by a hyperplane.

2
Totally asymmetric simple zero range hopping model

Consider a system with
N
particles on a periodic one-dimensional lattice of length
M+1
, i.e., on a ring, where sites
m
and
m+M+1
are identical. Each site of a lattice contains arbitrary number of particles. The particles evolve with the following dynamics rule: during the time interval [
t,t+{\rm d}t
] a particle on a site
i
jumps with probability
dt
to the neighbouring site
i+1
. There are no restrictions on the number of particles on a lattice site.

Figure 1:
Totally asymmetric simple zero range process.

A configuration
C
of the system is characterized by the list of all possible arrangements of
N
particles amongst the
M+1
available sites. The total number of configurations is, therefore,

\displaystyle\Omega=\frac{\left(N+M\right)!}{\left(N\right)!\left(M\right)!}.

(2.1)

The probability
P_{t}(C)
of finding the system in configuration
C
at time
t
satisfies the master equation

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}P_{t}(C)}{{\rm d}t}=\mathcal{M}P_{t}(C),

(2.2)

where
\mathcal{M}
is the Markov matrix of the size
\Omega\times\Omega
. For
C\neq C^{\prime}
the entry
\mathcal{M}(C^{\prime},C)
is the transition rate from
C
to
C^{\prime}
. It is equal to unity, if the transition is allowed, and is zero otherwise. The diagonal entry
-\mathcal{M}(C,C)
is equal to the number of
occupied
sites in the configuration
C
. The elements of the columns and rows of
\mathcal{M}
add up to zero and total probability is conserved

\displaystyle\sum_{C}P_{t}(C)=1.

The process described is stochastic, the unique stationary state of this system being the one, in which all
\Omega
different configurations
C
have equal weight.

A configuration
C
is represented by the sequence
(n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{M})
of occupation numbers
n_{j}
, satisfying the condition
0\leq n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{M}\leq N
,
n_{0}+n_{1}+\dots+n_{M}=N
. We can rewrite the master equation (
2.2
) for the TAZRP in the form

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}P_{t}(n_{0},\ldots,n_{M})=\sum_{m=0}^{M}P_{t}(n_{0},\ldots,n_{m}+1,n_{m+1}-1,\ldots,n_{M})

\displaystyle\hphantom{\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}P_t(n_0,\ldots,n_M) =}{}-KP_{t}(n_{0},\ldots,n_{M}),

(2.3)

where
K
is the number of occupied sites in the configuration
(n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{M})
, i.e., the number of
n_{j}\neq 0
. This equation has
to be supplemented by the condition that
P_{t}(n_{0},\ldots,n_{M})=0
, if at least one of the
n_{j}<0
.

To formulate the dynamic of the system in terms of a quantum mechanical model we denote a particle configuration as a Fock vector
|n_{0},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle
and define a probability vector

\displaystyle|P_{t}\rangle=\sum_{0\leq n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{M}\leq N\atop n_{0}+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{M}=N}P_{t}(n_{0},\ldots,n_{M})|n_{0},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle,

where
P_{t}(n_{0},\ldots,n_{M})
are the probabilities of configuration
(n_{0},\ldots,n_{M})
. The generator of the master equation (
2.3
) may be
written as

\displaystyle H=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\big(\phi_{m}\phi_{m+1}^{\dagger}-\phi_{m}^{\dagger}\phi_{m}\big)=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\big(\phi_{m+1}^{\dagger}-\phi_{m}^{\dagger}\big)\phi_{m}.

(2.4)

Here, the phase operators
\phi_{n},\phi_{n}^{\dagger}
were introduced. They satisfy commutation relations

\displaystyle[\hat{N}_{i},\phi_{j}]=-\phi_{i}\delta_{ij},\qquad[\hat{N}_{i},\phi_{j}^{\dagger}]=\phi_{i}^{\dagger}\delta_{ij},\qquad[\phi_{i},\phi_{j}^{\dagger}]=\pi_{i}\delta_{ij},

where
\hat{N}_{j}
is the number operator and
\pi_{i}=1-\phi_{i}^{\dagger}\phi_{i}
is vacuum projector:
\phi_{j}\pi_{j}=\pi_{j}\phi_{j}^{\dagger}=0
. This algebra has a representation on the Fock space:

\displaystyle\phi_{j}|n_{0},\ldots,0_{j},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\phi_{j}^{\dagger}|n_{0},\ldots,n_{j},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle=|n_{0},\ldots,n_{j}+1,\ldots,n_{M}\rangle,

\displaystyle\phi_{j}|n_{0},\ldots,n_{j},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle=|n_{0},\ldots,n_{j}-1,\ldots,n_{M}\rangle,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\hat{N}_{j}|n_{0},\ldots,n_{j},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle=n_{j}|n_{0},\ldots,n_{j},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle,

\displaystyle\pi_{j}|n_{0},\ldots,0_{j},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle=|n_{0},\ldots,0_{j},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle.

The states
|n_{0},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle
are orthogonal,
\langle p_{0},\ldots,p_{M}|n_{0},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle=\prod\limits_{l=0}^{M}\delta_{p_{l}n_{l}}
. The Fock vectors are generated from the vacuum state

\displaystyle|{\bf 0}\rangle\equiv\prod_{j=0}^{M}|0\rangle_{j}

(2.6)

by action of rising operators
\phi_{j}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle|{\bf n}\rangle\equiv|n_{0},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle=\prod_{j=0}^{M}\bigl(\phi_{j}^{\dagger}\bigr)^{n_{j}}|{\bf 0}\rangle,\qquad 0\leq n\leq N,\qquad\sum_{j=0}^{M}n_{j}=N.

(2.7)

The total number operator

\displaystyle\hat{N}=\sum_{j=0}^{M}N_{j}

commutes with the Hamiltonian (
2.4
):

\displaystyle[H,\hat{N}]=0.

(2.8)

The hopping term
\phi_{m+1}^{\dagger}\phi_{m}
of the Hamiltonian (
2.4
) annihilates the particle in the site
m
and creates it in the site
m+1
:

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{M}\phi_{m}\phi_{m+1}^{\dagger}|n_{0},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle=\sum_{m=0}^{M}|n_{0},\ldots,n_{m}-1,n_{m+1}+1,\ldots,n_{M}\rangle.

The vacuum projector
\pi_{m}
due to the definition (
2.5
) acts on the Fock vector in the following way

\displaystyle\pi_{m}|n_{0},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle=|n_{0},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle,\qquad\text{if}\quad n_{m}=0,

(2.9)

and

\displaystyle\pi_{m}|n_{0},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle=0,\qquad\text{if}\quad n_{m}\neq 0.

The operator

\displaystyle\hat{K}=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\phi_{m}^{\dagger}\phi_{m}=\sum_{m=0}^{M}(1-\pi_{m})

counts the number of occupied sites in the configuration
(n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{M})

\displaystyle\hat{K}|n_{0},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle=K|n_{0},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle.

The evolution of the quantum system
|P_{t}\rangle=e^{tH}|P_{0}\rangle
is governed by the imaginary time Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}t}|P_{t}\rangle=H|P_{t}\rangle,

which is equivalent to the master equation (
2.3
) for the
probabilities
P_{t}(n_{0},\ldots,n_{M})
equal to the matrix elements

\displaystyle P_{t}(n_{0},\ldots,n_{M})=\langle n_{0},\ldots,n_{M}|P_{t}\rangle.

The initial (
t=0
) probability distribution defines the state
|P_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle|P_{0}\rangle=\sum_{0\leq n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{M}\leq N\atop n_{0}+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{M}=N}P_{0}(n_{0},\ldots,n_{M})|n_{0},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle.

The state

\displaystyle\langle S|=\sum_{0\leq n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{M}\leq N\atop n_{0}+n_{1}+\cdots+n_{M}=N}\langle n_{0},\ldots,n_{M}|=\langle 0|\left(\sum_{j=0}^{M}\phi_{j}\pi_{j-1}\cdots\pi_{1}\right)^{N}

is a left eigenvector of the model, which obeys

\displaystyle\langle S|H=0.

(2.10)

Correspondingly,
H
has one right eigenvector with eigenvalue zero, which is associated with the state
|\Omega\rangle=\frac{1}{\Omega}|S\rangle
:
H|\Omega\rangle=0
, where
\Omega
is (
2.1
),
\langle S|\Omega\rangle=1
. The vector
\langle S|
does not evolve in time and, therefore, corresponds up to normalization factor to a steady state distribution of the system

\displaystyle\langle S|P_{t}\rangle=\langle S|P_{0}\rangle=1.

In this paper we shall calculate the conditional probability

\displaystyle P_{t}({\bf n}|{\bf m})=\frac{1}{\Omega}\langle n_{0},\ldots,n_{M}|e^{tH}|m_{0},\ldots,m_{M}\rangle,

(2.11)

which is equal to probability, that in a time
t
the system will be in a pure state defined by the occupation numbers
(n_{0},\ldots,n_{M})
provided that initially the system was prepared in a pure state
|P_{0}\rangle=|m_{0},\ldots,m_{M}\rangle
.

3
Solution of the TASZRP

To apply the scheme of the QIM to the solution of the Hamiltonian (
2.4
) we define
L
-operator
[
8
]
which is
2\times 2
matrix with the operator-valued entries acting on the Fock states according to (
2.5
):

\displaystyle L(n|u)\equiv\begin{pmatrix}u^{-1}+u\pi_{n}&\phi_{n}^{\dagger}\\
\phi_{n}&u\end{pmatrix},

(3.1)

where
u\in\mathbb{C}
is a parameter. This
L
-operator satisfies the intertwining relation

\displaystyle R(u,v)\left(L(n|u)\otimes L(n|v)\right)=\left(L(n|v)\otimes L(n|u)\right)R(u,v),

in which
R(u,v)
is the
R
-matrix

\displaystyle R(u,v)=\begin{pmatrix}f(v,u)&0&0&0\\
0&g(v,u)&1&0\\
0&0&g(v,u)&0\\
0&0&0&f(v,u)\end{pmatrix},

(3.2)

where

\displaystyle f(v,u)=\frac{u^{2}}{u^{2}-v^{2}},\qquad g(v,u)=\frac{uv}{u^{2}-v^{2}},\qquad u,v\in\mathbb{C}.

(3.3)

The monodromy matrix is the matrix product of
L
-operators

\displaystyle T(u)=L(M|u)L(M-1|u)\cdots L(0|u)=\begin{pmatrix}A(u)&B(u)\\
C(u)&D(u)\end{pmatrix}.

(3.4)

The commutation relations of the matrix elements of the monodromy matrix are given by the same
R
-matrix (
3.2
)

\displaystyle R(u,v)\left(T(u)\otimes T(v)\right)=\left(T(v)\otimes T(u)\right)R(u,v).

(3.5)

The transfer matrix
\tau(u)
is the trace of the monodromy matrix in the auxiliary space

\displaystyle\tau(u)=\operatorname{tr}T(u)=A(u)+D(u).

(3.6)

The relation (
3.5
) means that
[\tau(u),\tau(v)]=0
for arbitrary
u,v\in\mathbb{C}
.

From the definitions (
3.1
) and (
3.4
) one finds by direct calculation that the entries of the monodromy matrix are polynomials in
u^{2}
. For
A(u)
and
D(u)
one has

\displaystyle u^{M+1}A(u)=1+u^{2}\left(\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}\phi_{m}\phi_{m+1}^{\dagger}+\sum_{m=0}^{M}\pi_{m}\right)+\cdots+u^{2(M+1)}\prod_{m=0}^{M}\pi_{m},

\displaystyle u^{M+1}D(u)=u^{2}\phi_{0}^{\dagger}\phi_{M}+\cdots+u^{2(M+1)},

(3.7)

where the dots stand for the terms not important for further consideration. We also find that

\displaystyle\lim_{u\rightarrow 0}\widetilde{B}(u)\equiv\lim_{u\rightarrow 0}u^{M}B(u)=\phi_{0}^{\dagger},

(3.8)

\displaystyle\lim_{u\rightarrow 0}\widetilde{C}(u)\equiv\lim_{u\rightarrow 0}u^{M}C(u)=\phi_{M}.

(3.9)

The representation (
3.7
) allows to express the Hamiltonian (
2.4
) through the transfer matrix (
3.6
)

\displaystyle H=\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{2}}u^{M+1}\tau(u)\Bigl|_{u=0}-(M+1)=\frac{\partial}{\partial u^{2}}u^{M+1}(A(u)+D(u))\Bigl|_{u=0}-(M+1).

(3.10)

By construction this Hamiltonian commutes with the transfer matrix

\displaystyle[H,\tau(u)]=0.

Since the Hamiltonian (
2.4
) is non-Hermitian we have to distinguish between its right and left eigenvectors. The
N
-particle right state-vectors are taken in the form

\displaystyle|\Psi_{N}({\bf u})\rangle=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N}\widetilde{B}(u_{j})\right)|{\bf 0}\rangle,

(3.11)

where
\widetilde{B}(u)
is defined in (
3.8
), and
{\bf u}
implies a collection of arbitrary complex parameters
u_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
:

{\bf u}=(u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{N})
. The left state-vectors are equal to

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{N}({\bf u})|=\langle{\bf 0}|\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N}\widetilde{C}(u_{j})\right),

(3.12)

where
\widetilde{C}(u)
is given by (
3.9
). The vacuum state (
2.6
) is an eigenvector of
A(u)
and
D(u)
,

\displaystyle A(u)|{\bf 0}\rangle=\alpha(u)|{\bf 0}\rangle,\qquad D(u)|{\bf 0}\rangle=\delta(u)|{\bf 0}\rangle

with the eigen-values

\displaystyle\alpha(u)=\big(u^{-1}+u\big)^{M+1},\qquad\delta(u)=u^{M+1}.

(3.13)

The state-vectors (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) are the eigenvectors both of the Hamiltonian (
2.4
) and of the transfer matrix
\tau(u)
(
3.6
), if, and only if, the variables
u_{j}
satisfy the Bethe equations

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha(u_{n})}{\delta(u_{n})}=\prod_{m\neq n}^{N}\frac{f(u_{m},u_{n})}{f(u_{n},u_{m})},

where
f
are the elements of the
R
-matrix (
3.3
). In the explicit form the Bethe equations are given by

\displaystyle u_{n}^{-2N}\big(1+u_{n}^{-2}\big)^{M+1}=\frac{(-1)^{N-1}}{U^{2}},\qquad U^{2}\equiv\prod_{j=1}^{N}u_{j}^{2}.

(3.14)

There are
\Omega
equation (
2.1
) sets of solutions of these equations. The eigenvalues
\Theta_{N}(v,{\bf u})
of the transfer matrix (
3.6
) in the general form are equal to

\displaystyle\Theta_{N}(v;{\bf u})=\alpha(v)\prod_{j=1}^{N}f(v,u_{j})+\delta(v)\prod_{j=1}^{N}f(u_{j},v).

For the model under consideration

\displaystyle v^{M+1}\Theta_{N}(v;{\bf u})=\big(1+v^{2}\big)^{M+1}\prod_{m=1}^{N}\frac{u_{m}^{2}}{u_{m}^{2}-v^{2}}+v^{2(M+1)}\prod_{m=1}^{N}\frac{v^{2}}{v^{2}-u_{m}^{2}}

\displaystyle\hphantom{v^{M+1} \Theta_N (v; {\bf u})}{}=\bigl(\big(1+v^{2}\big)^{M+1}+(-1)^{N}v^{2(M+N+1)}U^{-2}\bigr)\mathcal{H}\big(v^{2};{\bf u}^{-2}\big).

Here, the generating function of complete symmetric functions
h_{l}\big({\bf u}^{-2}\big)\equiv h_{l}\big(u_{1}^{-2},u_{2}^{-2},\ldots
,
u_{N}^{-2}\big)

[
22
]
is introduced

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\big(v^{2};{\bf u}^{-2}\big)\equiv\prod_{m=1}^{N}\frac{1}{1-v^{2}/u_{m}^{2}}=\sum_{l\geq 0}h_{l}\big({\bf u}^{-2}\big)v^{2l}.

Equation (
3.10
) enables to obtain the spectrum of the Hamiltonian (
2.4
). The
N
-particle eigenenergies

\displaystyle H|\Psi_{N}({\bf u})\rangle=E_{N}|\Psi_{N}({\bf u})\rangle

are equal to

\displaystyle E_{N}({\bf u})=\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{2}}v^{M+1}\Theta_{N}(v;{\bf u})\Bigl|_{v=0}=h_{1}\big({\bf u}^{-2}\big)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}u_{k}^{-2}.

(3.15)

The steady state (
2.10
) corresponds to a special solution of Bethe equations (
3.14
) when all
u_{j}=\infty
.

4
The calculation of conditional probability

For the models associated with the
R
-matrix (
3.2
) the scalar product of the state-vectors (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) is given by the formula
[
1
]
:

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{N}({\bf v})|\Psi_{N}({\bf u})\rangle=\frac{\det Q}{{{\mathcal{V}}}_{N}\big({\textbf{v}}^{2}\big){{\mathcal{V}}}_{N}\big({\textbf{u}}^{-2}\big)}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left(\frac{v_{j}}{u_{j}}\right)^{M+N-1},

(4.1)

where
{{\mathcal{V}}}_{N}({\textbf{x}})
is the Vandermonde determinant,

\displaystyle{{\mathcal{V}}}_{N}({\textbf{x}})\equiv{{\mathcal{V}}}_{N}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N})=\prod_{1\leq i<k\leq N}(x_{k}-x_{i}),

(4.2)

and the matrix
Q
is characterized by the entries
Q_{jk}
,
1\leq j,k\leq N
,

\displaystyle Q_{jk}=\frac{\displaystyle\alpha(v_{j})\delta(u_{k})\left(\frac{u_{k}}{v_{j}}\right)^{N-1}-\alpha(u_{k})\delta(v_{j})\left(\frac{v_{j}}{u_{k}}\right)^{N-1}}{\displaystyle\frac{u_{k}}{v_{j}}-\frac{v_{j}}{u_{k}}},

with
\alpha(u)
and
\delta(u)
given by (
3.13
).

The norm of the state-vector
{\cal N}^{2}({\bf u})\equiv\langle\Psi_{N}({\bf u})|\Psi_{N}({\bf u})\rangle
is defined by the scalar product (
4.1
) when the arguments
{\bf v}
and
{\bf u}
satisfy the Bethe equations (
3.14
). For the present case of the generalized phase model we substitute
v_{k}=u_{k}
,
\forall\,k
, respecting the Bethe equations (
3.14
) into the entries of the matrix
Q
. The resulting matrix is denoted as
\widetilde{Q}
, and its entries at
j\neq k
are equal to

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}_{jk}=\frac{(-1)^{N}(u_{k}u_{j})^{N+M+1}}{U^{2}},

where
U^{2}
is given by (
3.14
). L’Hôspital rule gives the diagonal entries of
\widetilde{Q}

\displaystyle\widetilde{Q}_{jj}=(N-1)\alpha(u_{j})\delta(u_{j})+\bigl(\alpha(u_{j})\delta^{\prime}(u_{j})-\alpha^{\prime}(u_{j})\delta(u_{j})\bigr)\frac{u_{j}}{2}

\displaystyle\hphantom{\widetilde{Q}_{j j}}{}=\bigl(1-N-\mathfrak{G}_{j}\bigr)\frac{(-1)^{N}u_{j}^{2(N+M+1)}}{U^{2}},

where

\displaystyle\mathfrak{G}_{j}\equiv\frac{M+1}{u_{j}^{2}+1}.

As a result, the squared norm
{\cal N}^{2}({\bf u})
on the Bethe solution takes the form

\displaystyle{\cal N}^{2}({\bf u})=\frac{\det\widetilde{Q}}{{{\mathcal{V}}}_{N}\big({\textbf{u}}^{2}\big){{\mathcal{V}}}_{N}\big({\textbf{u}}^{-2}\big)},

(4.3)

\displaystyle\det\widetilde{Q}=U^{2(M+1)}\left(1-\sum_{l=1}^{N}\frac{1}{N+\mathfrak{G}_{l}}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{N}(N+\mathfrak{G}_{j}).

(4.4)

The state-vectors belonging to the different sets of solutions of the Bethe equations (
3.14
) are orthogonal. The eigenvectors (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) provide the resolution of the identity operator

\displaystyle I=\sum_{\{{\bf u}\}}\frac{|\Psi_{N}({\bf u})\rangle\langle\Psi_{N}({\bf u})|}{{\cal N}^{2}({\bf u})},

(4.5)

where the summation
\sum_{\{{\bf u}\}}
is over all independent solutions of the Bethe
equations (
3.14
).

Inserting the resolution of the identity operator (
4.5
) into (
2.11
), one obtains the general answer for the conditional probability

\displaystyle P_{t}({\bf n}\,|\,{\bf m})=\frac{1}{\Omega}\sum_{\{{\bf u}\}}e^{tE_{N}(\bf{u})}\frac{\langle\mathbf{n}|\Psi_{N}({\bf u})\rangle\langle\Psi_{N}({\bf u})|\mathbf{m}\rangle}{{\cal N}^{2}({\bf u})}.

(4.6)

For the simplicity let us consider the initial state equal to
|N,0,\ldots,0\rangle
and the final one respectively to
\langle 0,0,\ldots,N|
. The conditional probability (
2.11
) of this process is specified as follows

\displaystyle P_{t}\equiv\frac{1}{\Omega}\big\langle 0,0,\ldots,N\big|e^{tH}\big|N,0,\ldots,0\big\rangle=\frac{1}{\Omega}\big\langle{\bf 0}\big|(\phi_{M})^{N}e^{tH}(\phi^{\dagger}_{0})^{N}\big|{\bf 0}\big\rangle,

(4.7)

where equation (
2.7
) has been used. Inserting the resolution of the identity operator (
4.5
) into (
4.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle P_{t}=\frac{1}{\Omega}\sum_{\{{\bf u}\}}\frac{e^{tE_{N}({\bf u})}}{{\cal N}^{2}({\bf u})}\big\langle{\bf 0}|(\phi_{M})^{N}|\Psi_{N}({\bf u})\rangle\langle\Psi_{N}({\bf u})|(\phi^{\dagger}_{0})^{N}|{\bf 0}\big\rangle,

where the summation is over all independent solutions of equations (
3.14
).

The decomposition (
3.9
) for
B(u)
and
C(u)
gives that

\displaystyle\big\langle{\bf 0}|(\phi_{M})^{N}|\Psi_{N}({\bf u})\big\rangle=\lim_{\mathbf{v}\rightarrow 0}\langle\Psi_{N}({\bf v})|\Psi_{N}({\bf u})\rangle=1,

\displaystyle\big\langle\Psi_{N}({\bf u})|(\phi^{\dagger}_{0})^{N}|{\bf 0}\big\rangle=\lim_{\mathbf{v}\rightarrow 0}\langle\Psi_{N}({\bf u})|\Psi_{N}({\bf v})\rangle=1,

and eventually the answer is

\displaystyle P_{t}=\frac{1}{\Omega}\sum_{\{{\bf u}\}}\frac{e^{tE_{N}(\bf{u})}}{{\cal N}^{2}({\bf u})},

where
{{\cal N}^{2}({\bf u})}
is given by (
4.3
), (
4.4
).

To obtain the explicit answer for the conditional probability in the general case (
4.6
) we shall express state vectors (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) in the coordinate form. The state-vector (

) has the representation

\displaystyle|\Psi_{N}({\bf u})\rangle=\sum_{{{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}}\subseteq\{M^{N}\}}\chi^{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}({\bf u})\left(\prod_{j=0}^{M}(\phi_{j}^{{\dagger}})^{n_{j}}\right)|{\bf 0}\rangle,

(4.8)

where the symmetric function

is equal, up to a multiplicative pre-factor, to

\displaystyle\chi^{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}({\bf x})=\chi^{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{N})=\frac{1}{{\mathcal{V}}_{N}({\bf x})}\det\left(\frac{x_{i}^{2(N-j)}}{(1+x_{i}^{-2})^{\lambda_{j}}}\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}.

(4.9)

Here
{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
denotes the partition
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N})
of non-increasing non-negative integers,

\displaystyle M\geq\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{N}\geq 0,

and
{\mathcal{V}}_{N}({\bf x})
is the Vandermonde determinant (
4.2
). There is a one-to-one correspondence between a sequence of the occupation numbers
(n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{M})
,
n_{0}+n_{1}+\dots+n_{M}=N
, and the partition

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\big(M^{n_{M}},(M-1)^{n_{M-1}},\ldots,1^{n_{1}},0^{n_{0}}\big),

where each number
S
appears
n_{S}
times (see Fig.

). The sum in equation (
4.8
) is taken over all partitions
{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}
into at most
N
parts with
N\leq M
.

Acting by the Hamiltonian (

(4.10)

together with the exclusion condition

\displaystyle\chi^{R}_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{l-1}=\lambda_{l}-1,\lambda_{l},\ldots,\lambda_{N}}({\bf u})=\chi^{R}_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{l-1}=\lambda_{l},\lambda_{l},\ldots,\lambda_{N}}({\bf u}),\qquad 1\leq l\leq N.

(4.11)

The energy

). The state-vector (

2.4
) with the periodic boundary conditions if the parameters

, which is an alternative to the approach of the algebraic Bethe ansatz considered in Section 3. Although the model is solved by the algebraic Bethe ansatz, representations of the type of (

Expanding the left state-vector (

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{N}({\bf u})|=\sum_{{{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}}\subseteq\{M^{N}\}}\chi^{L}_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}({\bf u})\langle{\bf 0}\big|\left(\prod_{i=0}^{M}\phi_{i}^{n_{i}}\right),

(4.12)

where the wave function is given by the symmetric function

\displaystyle\chi^{L}_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}({\bf x})=\frac{\det\big(\big(1+x_{i}^{-2}\big)^{\lambda_{j}}x_{i}^{2(N-j)}\big)_{1\leq i,j\leq N}}{{\mathcal{V}}_{N}({\bf x})}.

It satisfies the equations

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}\chi^{L}_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{k}-1,\ldots,\lambda_{N}}({\bf u})=E_{N}({\bf u})\chi^{L}_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N}}({\bf u}),

\displaystyle\chi^{L}_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{l},\lambda_{l+1}=\lambda_{l}+1,\ldots,\lambda_{N}}({\bf u})=\chi^{L}_{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{l},\lambda_{l+1}=\lambda_{l},\ldots,\lambda_{N}}({\bf u}),\qquad 1\leq l\leq N.

From equations (
4.8

) one obtains

\displaystyle\langle n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{M}|\Psi_{N}({\bf u})\rangle=\chi^{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{R}}({\bf u}),

\displaystyle\langle\Psi_{N}({\bf u})|m_{0},m_{1},\ldots,m_{M}\rangle=\chi^{L}_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{L}}({\bf u}),

where

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{R}=\big(M^{n_{M}},(M-1)^{n_{M-1}},\ldots,1^{n_{1}},0^{n_{0}}\big),\qquad{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{L}=\big(M^{m_{M}},(M-1)^{m_{M-1}},\ldots,1^{m_{1}},0^{m_{0}}\big).

Finally, the expression for the conditional probability (

\displaystyle P_{t}(\mathbf{n}\,|\,\mathbf{m})=\frac{1}{\Omega}\sum_{\{{\bf u}\}}\frac{e^{tE_{N}({\bf u})}}{{\cal N}^{2}({\bf u})}\chi^{R}_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{R}}({\bf u})\chi^{L}_{{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}_{L}}({\bf u}).

(4.13)

Here

is the squared norm (

5
Multi-dimensional lattice walks bounded by a hyperplane

Starting from
(M+1)
-dimensional hypercubical lattice with unit spacing
\mathbb{Z}^{M+1}\ni\mathbf{m}\equiv(m_{0},m_{1},

\ldots,m_{M})
, let us define the non-negative orthant
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M+1}\equiv\{\mathbf{m}\,|\,0\leq m_{i},i\in{{\mathcal{M}}}\}
as a subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{M+1}
(hereafter
{{\mathcal{M}}}\equiv\{0,1,\ldots M\}
). Consider a subset of
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M+1}
consisting of sites with coordinates constrained by the requirement
m_{0}+m_{1}+\cdots+m_{M}=N
:

\displaystyle{\rm Simp}_{(N)}(\mathbb{Z}^{M+1})\equiv\left\{{\bf m}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{M+1}\Big|\sum_{i\in{{\mathcal{M}}}}m_{i}=N\right\}.

The set
{\rm Simp}_{(N)}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{M+1}\big)
is compact
M
-dimensional, and we shall call it
simplicial lattice
. A two-dimensional triangular simplicial lattice is presented in Fig.
3
. A sequence of
K+1
points in
\mathbb{Z}^{M+1}
is called
lattice path
of
K
steps
[
18
]
.

A two-dimensional triangular simplicial lattice.

Random walks over sites of
{\rm Simp}_{(N)}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{M+1}\big)
are defined by a set of admissible steps
\Omega_{M}
(
step set

\Omega_{M}
) so that at each step an
i^{{\rm th}}
coordinate
m_{i}
increases by unity, while the nearest neighbouring one decreases by unity. Namely, each element of
\Omega_{M}
is given by sequence
(e_{0},e_{2},\ldots,e_{M})
so that
e_{i}=\pm 1
,
e_{i+1}=\mp 1
for all pairs
(i,i+1)
with
i\in{{\mathcal{M}}}
and
M+1=0

({\rm mod}\,2)
, and
e_{j}=0
for all
j\in{{\mathcal{M}}}
and
j\neq i,i+1
. The step set
\Omega_{M}\equiv\Omega_{M}({\bf m}_{0})
ensures that trajectory of a random walk (lattice path) determined by the starting point
\mathbf{m}_{0}
lies in
M
-dimensional set
{\rm Simp}_{(N)}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{M+1}\big)
.

Directed random walks
on
M
-dimensional
oriented simplicial lattice
are defined by a step set
\Gamma_{M}=(k_{0},k_{1},\ldots,k_{M})
so that
k_{i}=-1
,
k_{i+1}=1
for all pairs
(i,i+1)
with
i\in{{\mathcal{M}}}
and
M+1=0

({\rm mod}\,2)
, and
k_{j}=0
for all
j\in{{\mathcal{M}}}\backslash\{i,i+1\}
. It may occur that some points on the boundary of the simplicial lattice also belong to a random walk trajectory. Therefore, the walker’s movements should be supplied with appropriate boundary conditions. The boundary of the simplicial lattice consists of
M+1
faces of highest dimensionality
M-1
. Under the
retaining boundary conditions
the walker comes to a node of the boundary, and either continues to move in accordance with the elements of
\Gamma_{M}
, or keeps staying in the node. An oriented two-dimensional simplicial lattice with the retaining boundary conditions is presented in Fig.
4

To establish the connection of the exponential generation function of lattice paths and the conditional probability (
2.11
) we shall interpret the coordinates
n_{j}
of a walker
\mathbf{n}=(n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{M})

\in\mathbb{Z}^{M+1}
in a simplicial lattice
{\rm Simp}_{(N)}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{M+1}\big)
as the occupation numbers of
(M+1)
-component Fock space and describe the steps of a walker with the help of the Fock state-vectors
|{\bf n}\rangle\equiv

|n_{0},n_{1},\ldots,n_{M}\rangle

\phi_{j}
shifts the value of the
j^{\rm th}
coordinate of the walker downwards
n_{j}\rightarrow n_{j}-1

\phi_{j}^{\dagger}
upwards
n_{j}\rightarrow n_{j}+1

s^{\rm th}
component of the boundary, it can make either allowed step or stay on it due the action of operator

(
2.9

N_{j}
acts as the coordinate operator

,
\ldots,n_{j},\ldots,n_{D}\rangle

2.8

\displaystyle H_{{\rm rw}}=\sum_{m=0}^{M}\bigl(\phi_{m}\phi_{m+1}^{\dagger}+\pi_{m}\bigr)

(5.1)

{\rm Simp}_{(N)}\big(\mathbb{Z}^{M+1}\big)
with the retaining boundary conditions. The operators (
5.1
) and (
2.4

\displaystyle H_{{\rm rw}}=H+M+1.

The number of lattice paths made by a walker over the
M
-dimensional simplicial lattice in

(5.2)

It is straightforward to verify that

\displaystyle G_{k}(\mathbf{l}\,|\,\mathbf{j})=\sum_{s=0}^{M}G_{k-1}(\mathbf{l}\,|\,j_{0},j_{1},\ldots,j_{s}+1,j_{s+1}-1,\ldots,j_{M})

The exponential generating function of lattice paths in

Due to definition (

\displaystyle F_{t}(\mathbf{l}\,|\,\mathbf{j})=\left\langle\mathbf{l}\big|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k}}{k!}H_{{\rm rw}}^{k}\big|\mathbf{j}\right\rangle=\big\langle\mathbf{l}\big|e^{tH_{{\rm rw}}}\big|\mathbf{j}\big\rangle.

\displaystyle P_{t}(\mathbf{l}\,|\,\mathbf{j})=\frac{e^{-t(M+1)}}{\Omega}F_{t}(\mathbf{l}\,|\,\mathbf{j}).

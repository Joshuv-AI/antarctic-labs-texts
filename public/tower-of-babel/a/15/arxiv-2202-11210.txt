Pointwise convergence of the heat and subordinates of the heat semigroups associated with the Laplace operator on homogeneous trees and two weighted L p L^{p} maximal inequalities.
I. Alvarez-Romero †,1, B. Barrios ‡,2, J. J. Betancor ‡,3

Abstract
. In this paper we consider the heat semigroup { W t } t > 0 \{W_{t}\}_{t>0} defined by the combinatorial Laplacian and two subordinated families of { W t } t > 0 \{W_{t}\}_{t>0} on homogeneous trees X X . We characterize the weights u u on X X for which the pointwise convergence to initial data of the above families holds for every f ∈ L p ​ ( X , μ , u ) f\in L^{p}(X,\mu,u) with 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty , where μ \mu represents the counting measure in X X . We prove that this convergence property in X X is equivalent to the fact that the maximal operator on t ∈ ( 0 , R ) t\in(0,R) , for some R > 0 R>0 , defined by the semigroup is bounded from L p ​ ( X , μ , u ) L^{p}(X,\mu,u) into L p ​ ( X , μ , v ) L^{p}(X,\mu,v) for some weight v v on X X .

Introduction

Along this work we will denote by
X
, a homogeneous tree of degree
q+1
, that is, a connected
graph without loops, in which every vertex has
q+1
neighbors, with
q\in\mathbb{N}
,
q\geq 1
. We consider on
X
the natural distance
d
, that is, if
x,\,y\in X
,
x\neq y
,
d(x,y)
is the number of edges between the vertexes
x
and
y
and
d(x,x)=0
,
x\in X
. By
\mu
we represent the counting measure on
X
. Note that
\mu
is not doubling because
q\geq 1
. The canonical, also called combinatorial, Laplace operator on
X
is defined by

(1.1)

\mathcal{L}(f)(x)=f(x)-\frac{1}{q+1}\sum_{y\in X,\,d(x,y)=1}f(y),\quad x\in X,

where
f
is a complex function defined on
X
. When
q=1
we clearly get that the homogeneous tree
X
is equal to
\mathbb{Z}
and
(\mathbb{Z},d,\mu)
is a homogeneous type space in the sense of Coifman and Weiss (
[
11
]
). This fact leads to use all the tools of the harmonic analysis in this framework, in particular the standard Calderón-Zygmund theory works in this space. We also observe that in this case

\mathcal{L}(f)(x)\left(=-\Delta_{1}(f)(x)\right)=-\frac{1}{2}(f(x+1)-2f(x)+f(x-1)),\quad x\in\mathbb{Z},

and the harmonic analysis associated with
\Delta_{1}
on
\mathbb{Z}

has been developed in the last years by several authors (see, for
instance
[
1
,
2
,
9
,
10
,
19
,
27
]
and the references
therein). The operator
\mathcal{L}
is bounded in
L^{p}(X,\mu)

for every
1\leq p\leq\infty
and it is selfadjoint in

L^{2}(X,\mu)
. Hence
-\mathcal{L}
generates a
C_{0}
- semigroup

\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}:=\{e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\}_{t\geq 0}
of operators in

L^{p}(X,\mu)
for every
1\leq p<\infty
. This semigroup

\{W_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
is usually called the heat semigroup associated
with the operator
\mathcal{L}
because for every
f\in L^{p}(X,\mu)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, the function
u(t,x):=W_{t}(f)(x)
,

t>0
and
x\in X
, solves the initial value problem

(1.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\mathcal{L}\right)u(t,x)=0,\quad t>0,\,x\in X,\\
u(x,0)=f(x),\quad x\in X.\end{array}\right.

We also observe that, for every
t>0
, the operator
W_{t}
is invariant under the action of the group of isometries of
(X,d)
, so that,
W_{t}
is a convolution operator given by

(1.3)

W_{t}(f)(x)=\int_{X}h_{t}(x,y)f(y)d\mu(y)=\sum_{y\in X}h_{t}(x,y)f(y),\quad x\in X.

The function
h_{t}
,
t>0
is usually called the heat kernel associated to
\mathcal{L}
and we have that
h_{t}(x,y)=H_{t}(d(x,y))
, that is,
h_{t}(x,y)
depends actually on
d(x,y)
,
x,\,y\in X
. According to
[
17
]
we have that, for every
t>0
,

H_{t}(k)=\frac{2e^{-(q+1)t}}{\pi q^{k/2-1}}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{e^{2t\sqrt{q}cos(u)}sin(u)(qsin((k+1)u)-sin((k-1)u))}{(q+1)^{2}-4qcos^{2}(u)}du,\quad k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\},

and

H_{t}(0)=\frac{2q(q+1)e^{-(q+1)t}}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{e^{2t\sqrt{q}cos(u)}sin^{2}(u)}{(q+1)^{2}-4qcos^{2}(u)}du.

We also consider other semigroups of operators defined subordinating with respect to
\{W_{t}\}_{t>0}.
For that let us take
0<\alpha<2
and we define, for every
t>0
,

f_{\alpha,t}(s):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\,i}\int_{a-i\infty}^{a+i\infty}e^{zs-tz^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}}\,dz,\quad s\geq 0,\,a>0,\\
0,\quad s<0.\end{array}\right.

Note that the last integral does not depend on
a>0
. As in
[
33
, Chapter IX,11]
, we also define, for every
t>0
and
f\in L^{p}(X,\mu),\,1\leq p\leq\infty
,

(1.4)

\displaystyle P_{t}^{\alpha}(f)(x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\alpha,t}(s)W_{s}(f)(x)\,ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in X}P_{t}^{\alpha}(d(x,y))f(y),\quad x\in X,

where

P_{t}^{\alpha}(k)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\alpha,t}(s)H_{s}(k)ds,\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

Thus, for every
1\leq p<\infty
,
\{P_{t}^{\alpha}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a
C_{0}
-semigroup of operators on
L^{p}(X,\mu)
whose infinitesimal generator is
\mathcal{L}^{\alpha/2}
(
[
33
, Theorem 2]
). Then, for every
f\in L^{p}(X,\mu)
, the function
v(t,x):=P_{t}^{\alpha}(f)(x)
,
t>0
,
x\in X
, solves the initial value problem

(1.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\mathcal{L}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)v(t,x)=0,\quad t>0,\,x\in X,\\
v(x,0)=f(x),\quad x\in X.\end{array}\right.

Here
\mathcal{L}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}
denotes the
\frac{\alpha}{2}
power of the operator
\mathcal{L}
that can be defined, for every
x\in X
, by (see
[
33
, (5), p. 260]
)

\mathcal{L}^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}f(x):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})}\int_{0}^{\infty}{\frac{W_{t}(f)(x)-f(x)}{t^{1+\frac{\alpha}{2}}}}\,dt,\quad f\in L^{p}(X,\mu),

for every
1\leq p<\infty
. Since we have that

f_{1,t}(s)=\frac{t}{2\sqrt{\pi}}s^{-3/2}e^{-t^{2}/4s},\,t,\,s\in(0,\infty),

then
\{P_{t}^{1}\}_{t\geq 0}
is the so called Poisson semigroup associated to
\mathcal{L}
. When
\alpha\neq 1
we do not know a non integral form for the function
f_{\alpha,t}
.

The last subordinate family that we will consider is the following one that was introduced in
[
31
]
. Let
\nu>0
. For every
t>0
and
f\in L^{p}(X,\mu)
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we define

\displaystyle T_{t}^{\nu}(f)(x)

\displaystyle:=\frac{t^{2\nu}}{4^{\nu}\Gamma(\nu)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t^{2}/4s}W_{s}(f)(x)\frac{ds}{s^{1+\nu}}

(1.6)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in X}T_{t}^{\nu}(d(x,y))f(y),\quad x\in X,

where

T_{t}^{\nu}(k)=\frac{t^{2\nu}}{4^{\nu}\Gamma(\nu)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t^{2}/4s}H_{s}(k)\frac{ds}{s^{1+\nu}},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

As before the family
\{T_{t}^{\nu}\}_{t\geq 0}
is a
C_{0}
- semigroup in
L^{p}(X,\mu)
when
\nu=1/2
, and, for every

the function

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}+\frac{1-2\nu}{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\mathcal{L}\right)w(t,x)=0,\quad t>0,\,x\in X,\\
w(x,0)=f(x),\quad x\in X.\end{array}\right.

We note that

for every

.

For now on we denote
\{S_{t}\}_{t>0}
one of the three uniparametric families that we have introduced, that is,

\lambda(x):=q^{l(x)},\,x\in X,

We have that

\mathbb{L}:=\frac{1}{1-b}\lambda^{-1/2}(\mathcal{L}-bI)\lambda^{1/2},\,\mbox{where }b:=\frac{(\sqrt{q}-1)^{2}}{q+1}.

We denote by

By using the last equality we can deduce from Theorems

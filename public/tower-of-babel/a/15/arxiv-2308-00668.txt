Elliptic curves with complex multiplication and abelian division fields
Asimina S. Hamakiotes, Álvaro Lozano-Robledo

Abstract
. Let K K be an imaginary quadratic field, and let 𝒪 K , f \mathcal{O}_{K,f} be an order in K K of conductor f ≥ 1 f\geq 1 . Let E E be an elliptic curve with CM by 𝒪 K , f \mathcal{O}_{K,f} , such that E E is defined by a model over ℚ ⁡ ( j K , f ) \mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f}) , where j K , f = j ⁡ ( E ) j_{K,f}=j(E) . In this article, we classify the values of N ≥ 2 N\geq 2 and the elliptic curves E E such that (i) the division field ℚ ⁡ ( j K , f , E ⁡ [ N ] ) \mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f},E[N]) is an abelian extension of ℚ ⁡ ( j K , f ) \mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f}) , and (ii) the N N -division field coincides with the N N -th cyclotomic extension of the base field.



1.
Introduction

Let
F
be a number field and let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over
F
. Let
\overline{F}
be a fixed algebraic closure of
F
, let
N\geq 2
be an integer, and let
E[N]=E(\overline{F})[N]
be the
N
-torsion subgroup of
E(\overline{F})
, i.e., the subgroup formed by all the
\overline{F}
-rational points on
E
of finite order dividing
N
. It is then natural to study the extension
F(E[N])/F
where
F(E[N])
denotes the field of definition of the coordinates of points in
E[N]
, which is usually called the
N
-division field of
E/F
. Since the extension
F(E[N])/F
is Galois, it is also natural to study its Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(F(E[N])/F)
, and the Galois representations that arise from the natural action of Galois on the
N
-torsion subgroup of the elliptic curve
E
.

The existence of the Weil pairing implies that
F(E[N])
contains all the
N
-th roots of unity of
\overline{F}
, i.e.,
F(\zeta_{N})\subseteq F(E[N])
, where
\zeta_{N}
is a primitive
N
-th root of unity (
[
18
, Ch. III, Cor. 8.1.1]
). Here we are interested in classifying all number fields
F
and elliptic curves
E/F
with one of the following properties:

(1)

The extension
F(E[N])/F
is abelian, i.e.,
\operatorname{Gal}(F(E[N])/F)
is abelian.

(2)

The
N
-division field equals the
N
-th cyclotomic field, i.e.,
F(E[N])=F(\zeta_{N})
.

Previously, the work of Halberstadt, Merel
[
12
]
, Merel and Stein
[
13
]
, and Rebolledo
[
15
]
showed that if
p
is prime, and
F(E[p])=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})
, then
p=2,3,5
or
p>1000
. When
F=\mathbb{Q}
, Paladino
[
14
]
classified all the curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
with
\mathbb{Q}(E[3])=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3})
.
In
[
8
]
, González-Jiménez and the second author proved that
\mathbb{Q}(E[N])=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{N})
only for
N=2,3,4,
or
5
. More generally, they proved that
\mathbb{Q}(E[N])/\mathbb{Q}
can be abelian only for
N=2,3,4,5,6,
or
8
, and classified all the elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}
with this property. Moreover, for each
N
, they classified the possible abelian Galois groups that occur for a division field. The classification of abelian division fields of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
has numerous applications, for example in: the classification of torsion subgroups of elliptic curves
[
4
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
; the classification of isogeny-torsion graphs
[
3
]
,
[
2
]
; Brauer groups
[
21
]
; non-monogenic number fields
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
; congruences between elliptic curves
[
7
]
; or the classification of Galois representations
[
11
]
,
[
16
]
, among others.

The goal of this article is a complete classification of all elliptic curves with complex multiplication defined over their minimal field of definition that satisfy (1) or (2). Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field, and let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be the ring of integers of
K
with discriminant
\Delta_{K}
. Let
f\geq 1
be an integer and let
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
be the order of
K
of conductor
f
. Let
j(\Lambda)
be the modular
j
-invariant function on lattices
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, and let
j(\mathbb{C}/\mathcal{O}_{K,f})
be the
j
-invariant associated to the order
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
when regarded as a complex lattice. The theory of complex multiplication shows that
j(\mathbb{C}/\mathcal{O}_{K,f})
is an algebraic integer (see
[
17
, Ch. 2, §6]
). Let
j_{K,f}
be an arbitrary Galois conjugate of
j(\mathbb{C}/\mathcal{O}_{K,f})
. Then, an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
with
j(E)=j_{K,f}
has complex multiplication by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
, and we say that
\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
is a minimal field of definition for elliptic curves with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
(note that there are several such conjugate fields). The main results of this paper are as follows.

The first main theorem classifies those curves with CM that satisfy (1), i.e., those curves with CM such that the
N
-th division field is abelian over the field of definition.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
,
f\geq 1
. Let
N\geq 2
and let
G_{E,N}=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f},E[N])/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f}))
be the Galois group of the
N
-division field of
E
. Then
G_{E,N}
is only abelian for
N=2,3
, and
4
, and
G_{E,N}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{k}
for some
0\leq k\leq 3
. Moreover:

(1)

If
N=2
, then
G_{E,2}
is abelian if and only if one of the following holds:

(a)

j_{K,f}\neq 0,1728
and either

•

\Delta_{K}f^{2}\equiv 0\bmod 4
, or

•

\Delta_{K}\equiv 1\bmod 8
and
f\equiv 1\bmod 2
.

In this case
G_{E,2}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

(b)

j_{K,f}=1728
. In this case
G_{E,2}
is always abelian with
G_{E,2}\cong\{0\}
or
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
according to whether
E
is given by
y^{2}=x^{3}-dx
with
d
a square or a non-square in
\mathbb{Z}
, respectively.

(c)

j_{K,f}=0
and
E/\mathbb{Q}
is given by
y^{2}=x^{3}+d
with
d
a cube in
\mathbb{Z}
. In this case
G_{E,2}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

(2)

If
N=3
, then
G_{E,3}
is abelian if and only if
j(E)=0
and
E/\mathbb{Q}
is given by
y^{2}=x^{3}+d
such that
-4d
is a cube in
\mathbb{Z}
. If in addition
d
and
-3d
are not squares, then
G_{E,3}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}
, and if
d
or
-3d
is a square, then
G_{E,3}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

(3)

If
N=4
, then
G_{E,4}
is abelian if and only if
j(E)=1728
and
E/\mathbb{Q}
is given by
y^{2}=x^{3}+dx
with
d\in\{\pm 1,\pm 4\}
or
d=\pm t^{2}
for some square-free integer
t\not\in\{\pm 1,\pm 2\}
, in which case
G_{E,4}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}
or
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{3}
, respectively.

Our second main theorem classifies those curves with CM that satisfy property (2), i.e., curves with CM such that the
N
-th division field is cyclotomic over their minimal field of definition.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
, with
f\geq 1
, and let
N\geq 2
. Then,
\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f},E[N])=\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f},\zeta_{N})
if and only if

(1)

N=2
, and
j(E)=1728
with
E/\mathbb{Q}
given by a form
y^{2}=x^{3}-d^{2}x
for some integer
d
, or

(2)

N=3
, and
j(E)=0
with
E/\mathbb{Q}
given by a form
y^{2}=x^{3}+d
for some integer
d
such that
d
or
-3d
is a square and
-4d
is a cube.

3.
Background and Preliminary Results

In this section, we will prove some preliminary results. For simplicity, we will establish some notation. Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field, and let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be the ring of integers of
K
with discriminant
\Delta_{K}
. Let
f\geq 1
be an integer and let
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
be the order of
K
of conductor
f
. Let
j_{K,f}
be a CM
j
-invariant as in the introduction and let
N\geq 2
. We define associated constants
\delta
and
\phi
as follows:

•

If
\Delta_{K}f^{2}\equiv 0\bmod 4
, let
\delta=\Delta_{K}f^{2}/4
, and
\phi=0
.

•

If
\Delta_{K}f^{2}\equiv 1\bmod 4
, let
\delta=\frac{(\Delta_{K}-1)}{4}f^{2}
, let
\phi=f
.

We define matrices in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
by

c_{\varepsilon}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\varepsilon&0\\
\phi&\varepsilon\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\text{ and }\quad c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a+b\phi&b\\
\delta b&a\\
\end{array}\right)

where
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
and
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
such that
\det(c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b))\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.
We define the Cartan subgroup
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
by

\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N)=\left\{c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b):a,b\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z},\ \det(c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b))\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\right\},

and
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)=\left\langle\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N),c_{1}\right\rangle
. We will sometimes call
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
the ‘‘normalizer’’ of
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
but we emphasize here that, while this is the case when
N
is prime, it may not be the actual normalizer for composite values of
N
(see
[
11
]
, Section 5). Finally, we write
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}=\varprojlim\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
.

First, we recall results from
[
11
]
that provide details about the image of Galois representations attached to CM elliptic curves.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
11
]
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
, let
N\geq 3
, and let
\rho_{E,N}
be the Galois representation
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})}/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f}))\to\operatorname{Aut}(E[N])\cong\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
, and let
\rho_{E}
be the Galois representation
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})}/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f}))\to\varprojlim\operatorname{Aut}(E[N])\cong\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
. Then,

(1)

There is a
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
E[N]
such that the image of
\rho_{E,N}

is contained in
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
, and the index of the image of
\rho_{E,N}
in
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
is a divisor of the order of
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}^{\times}/\mathcal{O}_{K,f,N}^{\times}
, where
\mathcal{O}_{K,f,N}^{\times}=\{u\in\mathcal{O}_{K,f}^{\times}:u\equiv 1\bmod N\mathcal{O}_{K,f}\}
.

(2)

There is a compatible system of bases of
E[N]
such that the image of
\rho_{E}
is contained in
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
, and the index of the image of
\rho_{E}
in
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
is a divisor of the order
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}^{\times}
. In particular, the index is a divisor of
4
or
6
.

Our goal is to understand what subgroups of
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
are images of Galois representations and abelian. First, we recall that the Cartan subgroup
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
is abelian.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
N\geq 2
. The group
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
is isomorphic to
(\mathcal{O}_{K,f}/N\mathcal{O}_{K,f})^{\times}
so, in particular, it is an abelian subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

See
[
11
]
, Lemma 2.5 and Remark 2.6.
∎

Next, we give conditions that will help us characterize when a subgroup of
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
is abelian.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
N\geq 2
and let
G\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
be a subgroup. If
c_{1},c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b)\in G
, for some
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
, such that
c_{1}\cdot c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b)=c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b)\cdot c_{1}
, then
b\phi\equiv 0\bmod N
and
2b\equiv 0\bmod N
. Moreover, if
\phi=0
, and if
c_{\varepsilon},c_{\delta,0}(a,b)\in G
for some
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
, such that
c_{\varepsilon}\cdot c_{\delta,0}(a,b)=c_{\delta,0}(a,b)\cdot c_{\varepsilon}
then the same conclusion holds.

Proof.

Suppose that
c_{1}
and
c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b)\in G
, where
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
and
a^{2}+ab\phi-\delta b^{2}\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, and suppose the two matrices commute:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a+b\phi&b\\
\delta b&a\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-1&0\\
\phi&1\\
\end{array}\right)\

\displaystyle\equiv\ \left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-1&0\\
\phi&1\\
\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a+b\phi&b\\
\delta b&a\\
\end{array}\right)\ \bmod N,\text{ and so}

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-a&b\\
-\delta b+a\phi&a\\
\end{array}\right)\

\displaystyle\equiv\ \left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-a-b\phi&-b\\
a\phi+b\phi^{2}+\delta b&b\phi+a\\
\end{array}\right)\ \bmod N.

In order for the last equivalence to hold, we must have that

•

-a\equiv-a-b\phi\bmod N
and therefore
b\phi\equiv 0\bmod N
,

•

b\equiv-b\bmod N
and so
2b\equiv 0\bmod N
,

•

-\delta b+a\phi\equiv a\phi+b\phi^{2}+\delta b\bmod N
which implies
b\phi^{2}+2\delta b\equiv 0\bmod N
, and

•

a\equiv b\phi+a\bmod N
which also implies that
b\phi\equiv 0\bmod N
.

In particular,
b\phi\equiv 0\bmod N
and
2b\equiv 0\bmod N
, as desired. If
\phi=0
, we note that
c_{\varepsilon}=(\varepsilon\cdot\operatorname{Id})\cdot c_{1}
, so
c_{\varepsilon}\cdot c_{\delta,0}(a,b)=c_{\delta,0}(a,b)\cdot c_{\varepsilon}
implies
b\phi\equiv 0\bmod N
and
2b\equiv 0\bmod N
as well.
∎

As a corollary, we obtain a simple way to prove that a subgroup is non-abelian.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
N>2
and let
G\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
be a subgroup. If
c_{1}\in G
(or
\phi=0
and
c_{\varepsilon}\in G
) and
c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b)\in G
with
b\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, then
G
is non-abelian.

Proof.

Assume for a contradiction that
G\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
is abelian. Then,
c_{1}
(or
c_{\varepsilon}
if
\phi=0
) and
c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b)
commute, and by Lemma
3.3
we have that
b\phi\equiv 0\bmod N
and
2b\equiv 0\bmod N
. If
N>2
and
b\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, then the equivalence
2b\equiv 0\bmod N
implies that
2\equiv 0\bmod N
, which is not possible since
N>2
. Therefore,
G
cannot be abelian.
∎

The following lemma determines the isomorphism type of
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(2)
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
N=2
.

\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(2)\cong\begin{cases}S_{3}&\text{ if }\delta\equiv\phi\equiv 1\bmod 2,\text{ or}\\
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

The following inclusion of matrices with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}

\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(2)\subseteq\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1+\phi&1\\
\delta&1\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi&1\\
\delta&0\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
\phi&1\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
\delta+\phi&1\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
\delta&0\\
\end{array}\right)\right\}

implies that the only elements of order
3
in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
, which are
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
1&0\\
\end{array}\right)
and
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&1\\
\end{array}\right)
, only belong to
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(2)
when
\delta\equiv\phi\equiv 1\bmod 2
(and if this is the case, all the matrices are distinct mod
2
and
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(2)\cong S_{3}
). Thus, if
\delta
or
\phi\equiv 0\bmod 2
, then
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(2)
is either trivial or isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
but at least one of the matrices

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
\phi&1\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
\delta+\phi&1\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
\delta&0\\
\end{array}\right)

is not the identity and belongs to
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(2)
, and so
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(2)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
as desired.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Let
N=p_{1}^{e_{1}}p_{2}^{e_{2}}\cdots p_{r}^{e_{r}}
, where for
1\leq i\leq r
the
p_{i}
are prime and
e_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Then, for each
p_{i}\mid N
, the projection map
\pi\colon\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)\to\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(p_{i}^{e})
is surjective, for any
1\leq e\leq e_{i}
. Indeed, by the Chinese remainder theorem, we have the following surjective projection

\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)\ \cong\ \prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(p_{i}^{e_{i}})\ \to\ \mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(p_{i}^{e_{i}}).

Moreover, if
c_{\delta,\phi}(\bar{a},\bar{b})\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(p_{i}^{e})
, for some
1\leq e\leq e_{i}
and for some
\bar{a},\bar{b}\bmod p_{i}^{e}
such that
\det(c_{\delta,\phi}(\bar{a},\bar{b}))\not\equiv 0\bmod p_{i}
, then
c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b)\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(p_{i}^{e_{i}})
for any
a\equiv\bar{a}
and
b\equiv\bar{b}\bmod p_{i}^{e}
, since
\det(c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b))\equiv\det(c_{\delta,\phi}(\bar{a},\bar{b}))\not\equiv 0\bmod p_{i}
. Since
c_{1}
maps to
c_{1}
, it follows that the projection
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(p_{i}^{e_{i}})\to\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(p_{i}^{e})
is surjective as claimed.

The following result tells us when the largest possible mod-
N
CM image is abelian.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
N\geq 2
. The group
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
is abelian if and only if
N=2
and
\phi\equiv 0\bmod 2
or
(\delta,\phi)\equiv(0,1)\bmod 2
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(2)
is abelian, then it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
as a group.

Proof.

When
N=2
, the group
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
is abelian if and only if
\phi\equiv 0\bmod 2
or
(\delta,\phi)\equiv(0,1)\bmod 2
, and if it is abelian, it is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, by Lemma
3.5
. Thus, we may assume
N\geq 3
.

Let
N=p_{1}^{e_{1}}p_{2}^{e_{2}}\cdots p_{r}^{e_{r}}>2
. Then, either
N
is divisible by
4
or there is a prime
p>2
such that
N\equiv 0\bmod p
. Further, since the projection maps
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)\to\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(p_{i}^{e})
, for any
1\leq e\leq e_{i}
, are surjective (Remark
3.6
), it suffices to show that
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(4)
and
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(p_{i})
for
p_{i}>2
are non-abelian. Thus, let
N=q
where
q=4
or an odd prime
p
.

We have that
c_{1}\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(q)
. By Lemma
3.3
, and in order to show that
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(q)
is non-abelian, it suffices to exhibit an element
c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b)\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(q)
such that
2b\not\equiv 0\bmod q
or
b\phi\not\equiv 0\bmod q
.

•

If
\delta
is a unit mod
q
, then
c_{\delta,\phi}(0,1)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi&1\\
\delta&0\\
\end{array}\right)
has determinant
\det(c_{\delta,\phi}(0,1))\equiv-\delta\bmod q
(a unit), and therefore
c_{\delta,\phi}(0,1)\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(q)
. In this case
2b\equiv 2\not\equiv 0\bmod q
.

•

If
\delta
and
\phi
are not units mod
q
, then
c_{\delta,\phi}(1,1)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1+\phi&1\\
\delta&1\\
\end{array}\right)
has determinant
\det(c_{\delta,\phi}(1,1))\equiv 1+\phi-\delta\bmod q
(a unit), and therefore
c_{\delta,\phi}(1,1)\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(q)
. In this case
2b\equiv 2\not\equiv 0\bmod q
.

•

If
q=4
and
\phi\equiv 1\bmod 2
, then
c_{\delta,\phi}(1,2)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1+2\phi&2\\
2\delta&1\\
\end{array}\right)
has determinant
\det(c_{\delta,\phi}(1,2))\equiv 1+2\phi\equiv 1\bmod 2
, and therefore
c_{\delta,\phi}(1,2)\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(4)
. In this case
b\phi\equiv 2\not\equiv 0\bmod 4
.

•

If
q=p
, and if
\delta\equiv 0\bmod p
and
\phi\not\equiv 0\bmod p
, let
b\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
such that
b\not\equiv-\phi^{-1}\bmod p
. Notice that
p\geq 3
so there are
p-2>0
choices for
b\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\setminus\{-\phi^{-1}\bmod p\}
. Then,
c_{\delta,\phi}(1,b)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1+b\phi&b\\
\delta b&1\\
\end{array}\right)
has determinant
\det(c_{\delta,\phi}(1,b))\equiv 1+b\phi\not\equiv 0\bmod p
, and therefore
c_{\delta,\phi}(1,b)\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(p)
. In this case
2b\not\equiv 0\bmod p
.

Thus, in all cases, there is
c_{1}
and a Cartan element
c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b)\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(q)
that show (via Lemma
3.3
) that
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(q)
cannot be abelian for
q=4
or an odd prime
p
. This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

4.
Abelian and non-abelian division fields in the prime power case

In
[
11
]
, the second author explicitly describes the groups of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}_{p})
that can occur as images of the Galois representation
\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}\colon\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\to\operatorname{Aut}(T_{p}(E))\cong\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, up to conjugation, for an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
with CM by an arbitrary order
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
. In this section, we will apply the results from Section
3
to all possible images
G_{E,N}=\operatorname{im}\rho_{E,N}
from
[
11
]
and analyze under what circumstances we have that
G_{E,N}
is abelian, when
N
is a prime power. We will deal with general composite values of
N
in Section
5
.

We begin with the case of primes that do not divide the discriminant of the order.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
be an elliptic curve with CM by an order
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
of
K
of conductor
f\geq 1
, with
j_{K,f}\neq 0
. Let
p
be a prime that does not divide
2\Delta_{K}f
. Then, the image
G_{E,p}
of
\rho_{E,p}
, and
G_{E,p^{\infty}}
are non-abelian.

Proof.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
and
p
be as in the statement. Then,
[
11
, Theorem 1.2.(4)]
implies that the image of
\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\infty})
and, therefore, the image
G_{E,p}
of
\rho_{E,p}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
. Since
p>2
, now Theorem
3.7
implies that
G_{E,p}
cannot be abelian, and therefore
G_{E,p^{\infty}}
is non-abelian.
∎

Next we treat the case of elliptic curves with
j(E)=0
and
p>3
(i.e., primes
p
that do not divide
2f\Delta_{K}=-6
). Before we state and prove the next two propositions we remark that even though we generally define
\delta
and
\phi
and choose a basis of
T_{p}(E)
such that the image of
\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
is contained in
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\infty})
, in some cases one can choose a different basis so that the image is contained in the simpler form group
\mathcal{N}_{\delta^{\prime},0}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\infty})
, for some value of
\delta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. See the beginning of
[
11
, Section 5]
for more details on this.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with
j(E)=0
and let
p>3
be a prime. Then, the image
G_{E,p}
of
\rho_{E,p}
is non-abelian. Therefore, the image of
\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
is also non-abelian.

Proof.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with
j(E)=0
. Thus,
E
has CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
, where

K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
,
f=1
, and
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}=\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathbb{Z}[(1+\sqrt{-3})/2]
. Let
p>3
be a prime. Then, the
p
-adic image of
\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
is described by Theorem 1.4 of
[
11
]
. In particular, the image depends on the class of
p\bmod 9
, so we consider three separate cases, which in turn correspond to parts (1), (2), and (3) of
[
11
, Thm. 1.4]
.

(1)

If
p\equiv\pm 1\bmod 9
, then there is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-adic basis of
T_{p}(E)
such that
G_{E,p}\cong\mathcal{N}_{\delta^{\prime},0}(p)
and
G_{E,p^{\infty}}\cong\mathcal{N}_{\delta^{\prime},0}(p^{\infty})
, where
\delta^{\prime}=\Delta_{K}/4=-3/4
. Since
p>2
, by Theorem
3.7
, we have that the image
G_{E,p}
is non-abelian. Since
G_{E,p^{\infty}}\equiv G_{E,p}\bmod p
and
G_{E,p}
is non-abelian, the full
p
-adic image
G_{E,p^{\infty}}
is also non-abelian.

(2)

If
p\equiv 2
or
5\bmod 9
, then either
G_{E,p}\cong\mathcal{N}_{\delta^{\prime},0}(p)
, or
G_{E,p}\cong\langle\mathcal{C}_{\delta^{\prime},0}(p)^{3},c_{1}\rangle
, with
\delta^{\prime}=-3/4
as before. In the case that
G_{E,p}\cong\mathcal{N}_{\delta^{\prime},0}(p)
, the image
G_{E,p}
is non-abelian as in part (1). If
G_{E,p}\cong\langle\mathcal{C}_{\delta^{\prime},0}(p)^{3},c_{1}\rangle
, consider
c_{\delta^{\prime},0}(0,1)^{3}=c_{\delta^{\prime},0}(0,\delta^{\prime})\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta^{\prime},0}(p)^{3}
, where
b\equiv\delta^{\prime}\bmod p
. Since
b\equiv\delta^{\prime}\equiv-3/4\in(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, by Corollary
3.4
, the image
G_{E,p}
is non-abelian, and therefore
G_{E,p^{\infty}}
is also non-abelian.

(3)

If
p\equiv 4
or
7\bmod 9
, then either
G_{E,p^{\infty}}\cong\mathcal{N}_{\delta^{\prime},0}(p^{\infty})
, or there is a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-basis of
T_{p}(E)
such that
G_{E,p^{\infty}}
is isomorphic to the subgroup

H=\left\langle\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&0\\
0&b\\
\end{array}\right):a/b\in(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times})^{3}\right\},\gamma=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\\
\end{array}\right)\right\rangle.

If
G_{E,p^{\infty}}=\mathcal{N}_{\delta^{\prime},0}(p^{\infty})
, then image
G_{E,p}
and
G_{E,p^{\infty}}
are non-abelian as before. If
G_{E,p^{\infty}}\cong H
, we note that

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-1&0\\
0&1\\
\end{array}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
1&0\\
\end{array}\right)\neq\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\\
\end{array}\right)\cdot\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-1&0\\
0&1\\
\end{array}\right),

shows that
H\bmod p
is non-abelian, since
-1\in(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times})^{3}
. Hence
G_{E,p}\equiv H\bmod p
is non-abelian for all
p>2
, and
G_{E,p^{\infty}}
is also non-abelian.

Thus, in all cases we have that
G_{E,p}
and
G_{E,p^{\infty}}
are non-abelian groups.
∎

The next result describes the possible abelian images for an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
, for any
f\geq 1
, when
p>2
divides
f\Delta_{K}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
of conductor
f\geq 1
. Let
p
be an odd prime dividing
f\Delta_{K}
(thus,
j\neq 1728
where
f\Delta_{K}=-4
), and choose a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-basis of
T_{p}(E)
such that
G_{E,p^{\infty}}
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{N}_{\delta^{\prime},0}(p^{\infty})
with
\delta^{\prime}=\Delta_{K}f^{2}/4
. Then,
G_{E,p^{n}}
for
n\geq 2
, and
G_{E,p^{\infty}}
are non-abelian. Moreover, if
G_{E,p}
is abelian, then
j_{K,f}=0
and
p=3
, and we are in one of the two following cases according to the
3
-adic image of
E
:

(1)

G_{E,3^{\infty}}
is conjugate to the subgroup generated by
c_{\varepsilon}
and

H_{1}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
-3b/4&a\\
\end{array}\right):a\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{\times},\ b\equiv 0\bmod 3\right\}.

In this case,
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta^{\prime},0}(3^{\infty}):G_{E,3^{\infty}}]=3
and
G_{E,3}\cong(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}
is abelian, but the image

is non-abelian for
n\geq 2
. Moreover,
E/\mathbb{Q}
can be given by Weierstrass form

with

such that
d
and
-3d
are not squares and
-4d
is a cube.

(2)

G_{E,3^{\infty}}
is conjugate to the subgroup generated by
c_{\varepsilon}
and one of

H_{1}^{\prime}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
-3b/4&a\\
\end{array}\right):a\equiv 1,\ b\equiv 0\bmod 3\mathbb{Z}_{3}\right\}.

In this case,
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta^{\prime},0}(3^{\infty}):G_{E,3^{\infty}}]=6
and
G_{E,3}\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
is abelian, but the image
G_{E,3^{n}}
is non-abelian for
n\geq 2
. Moreover,
E/\mathbb{Q}
can be given by Weierstrass form
y^{2}=x^{3}+d
with
d\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
d
or
-3d
is a square and
-4d
is a cube.

Proof.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
of conductor
f\geq 1
. Let
p
be an odd prime dividing
f\Delta_{K}
(this assumption automatical excludes

). The image of
\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}
is given in this case by
[
11
, Theorem 1.5]
. There are two cases to consider according to whether
j_{K,f}\neq 0
or
j_{K,f}=0
, which correspond accordingly to cases (a) and (b) of the statement of
[
11

.

(a)

If
j_{K,f}\neq 0,1728
, then either
G_{E,p^{\infty}}\cong\mathcal{N}_{\delta^{\prime},0}(p^{\infty})
or
G_{E,p^{\infty}}
is isomorphic to the group of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}_{p})
generated by
c_{\varepsilon}
and

H=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
\delta b&a\\
\end{array}\right):a\in{\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}}^{2},b\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right\},

where
\delta^{\prime}=\Delta_{K}f^{2}/4\equiv 0\bmod p

In the case that

, by Theorem
3.7
, since

3.4
implies that the image

(b)

Now let
j_{K,f}=0
, and so
\Delta_{K}f^{2}=-3
. Then,

and either

or

or

and one of the upcoming (i), (ii), or (iii) hold. In the case that

and

or

, which we consider as separate cases.

(i)

If
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta^{\prime},0}(3^{\infty}):G_{E,3^{\infty}}]=2
, then
G_{E,3^{\infty}}
is generated by
c_{\varepsilon}
and

\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
-3b/4&a\\
\end{array}\right):a,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{3},\ a\equiv 1\bmod 3\right\}.

Let
a,b\equiv 1\bmod 3
. Observe that
c_{\delta^{\prime},0}(1,1)\in G_{E,3}
. Since
b\in(\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
, by Corollary
3.4
, we have that
G_{E,3}
and

are non-abelian.

(ii)

If
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta^{\prime},0}(3^{\infty}):G_{E,3^{\infty}}]=3
, then we have the following three cases:

(1)

G_{E,3^{\infty}}
is generated by
c_{\varepsilon}
and

H_{1}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
-3b/4&a\\
\end{array}\right):a\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}^{\times},\ b\equiv 0\bmod 3\right\}.

Observe that
G_{E,3}
is generated by the diagonal matrices (i.e.,

) and

, which commute. Therefore, the image

is abelian in this case and

and

since its determinant is congruent to

, and

3.3
, we have that the image
G_{E,9}
is non-abelian. Therefore,

is non-abelian, and the image

is non-abelian for

.

(2)

G_{E,3^{\infty}}
is generated by
c_{\varepsilon}
and

H_{2}=\left\langle\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}2&0\\
0&2\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
-3/4&1\\
\end{array}\right)\right\rangle.

In this case, observe that
c_{\delta^{\prime},0}(1,1)\in G_{E,3}
. Since

, by Corollary

, we have that the image

(3)

G_{E,3^{\infty}}
is generated by
c_{\varepsilon}
and

0&2\\
\end{array}\right),\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-5/4&1/2\\

In this case, observe that

. Since

, by Corollary

, we have that the image

is non-abelian. Therefore, the image

(iii)

If
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta^{\prime},0}(3^{\infty}):G_{E,3^{\infty}}]=6
, then we have the following three cases:

(1)

G_{E,3^{\infty}}
is generated by
c_{\varepsilon}
and

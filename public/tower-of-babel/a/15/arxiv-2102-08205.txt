( ℓ , p ) (\ell,p) -Jones-Wenzl Idempotents
Stuart Martin, Robert A. Spencer

Abstract
. The Jones-Wenzl idempotents of the Temperley-Lieb algebra are celebrated elements defined over characteristic zero and for generic loop parameter. Given pointed field ( R , δ ) (R,\delta) , we extend the existing results of Burrull, Libedinsky and Sentinelli to determine a recursive form for the idempotents describing the projective cover of the trivial TL n R ​ ( δ ) {\rm TL}_{n}^{R}(\delta) -module.



Introduction

The Temperley-Lieb algebra,
{\rm TL}_{n}
, defined over ring
R
with distinguished element
\delta
by the generators
{\{u_{i}\}}_{i=1}^{n-1}
and relations

(0.1)

\displaystyle u_{i}^{2}

\displaystyle=\delta u_{i}

(0.2)

\displaystyle u_{i}u_{j}

\displaystyle=u_{j}u_{i}

\displaystyle|i-j|\geq 2

(0.3)

\displaystyle u_{i}u_{i\pm 1}u_{i}

\displaystyle=u_{i}

\displaystyle 1\leq i\pm 1<n,

has recently been recast into the limelight.

First studied over characteristic zero, as algebras of transfer operators in lattice models, these structures found extensive use in physics and, later, knot theory where Vaughan Jones famously used them to define the Jones polynomial invariant.

More recently, Temperley-Lieb algebras and their variants have become the subject of study by those seeking to understand Soergel bimodule theory
[
Eli16
]
.
Here they are intricately linked to the categorification of the Hecke algebra of type
\tilde{A_{1}}
by two-colour Soergel bimodules.
From this categorification arises interesting “canonical” bases of certain Hecke algebras, of which the Kazhdan-Lusztig basis is probably the most famed.

However, other interesting bases occur, particularly when the underlying ring has positive characteristic.
Work by Jensen and Williamson
[
TW17
]
develops the so-called
p
-canonical basis or
p
-Kazhdan-Lusztig basis for crystallographic Coxeter types.
The underlying calculations in the two-colour case reduce to results in the representation theory of the Temperley-Lieb algebra.

In the language of Soergel bimodules, Jones-Wenzl idempotents describe the indecomposable objects.
Recently, Burrull, Libedinsky and Sentinelli
[
BLS19
]
determined the corresponding elements of
{\rm TL}_{n}
defined over a field of characteristic
p\geq 2
for the parameter
\delta=2
.
The results of Erdmann and Henke
[
EH02
]
implicitly describing the
p
-canonical bases are crucial.

Throughout these results, the role of the Temperley-Lieb algebra as the centralizer ring
\operatorname{End}_{U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})}(V^{\otimes n})
has been key to understanding its modular representation theory (see
[
And19
,
AST18
]
, and the reliance on
[
EH02
]
in
[
CGM03
,
BLS19
]
for examples).
The explicit nature of the tilting theory of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
has underpinned most of the results.

However, the algebras themselves admit a pleasing diagrammatic presentation stemming from
Eqs.

0.1
,
0.2
and
0.3
.
Most of the theory of the characteristic zero case was determined purely “combinatorially” from this
[
Wes95
,
RSA14
]
.
In
[
Spe20
]
the second author re-derives many of the results known about the representation theory of
{\rm TL}_{n}
over positive characteristic without recourse to tilting theory of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
.

This paper builds on
[
Spe20
]
and
[
BLS19
]
to construct
(\ell,p)
-Jones-Wenzl idempotents giving the indecomposable objects in the most general case of any field and any parameter.
This answers one of the questions in
[
BLS19
, 1.3.5]
by showing that the construction given extends quite simply to other (non-integral) realisations.
That is, if the reflection representation of
D_{2\ell}
of the form

s\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}-1&\delta\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\quad\quad\quad\quad t\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\delta&-1\end{pmatrix}

for adjacent reflection generators
s
and
t
of
D_{2\ell}
is chosen for the
\tilde{A}_{1}
Hecke category, then the indecomposable objects are given by
(\ell,p)
-Jones-Wenzl idempotents.
If the two generators have differing off-diagonal values so the realisation is not symmetric, minor changes to the result occur with quantum numbers replaced by two-colored quantum numbers
[
Eli16
, §A.1]
.
The exact modification is not complicated (it predominantly consists of in-place replacements), but the careful handling of even and odd cases is beyond the scope of this paper.

This paper is arranged as follows.
We briefly recall some of the needed concepts and define our notation in
Section

1
.
In
Section

2
we recall the known theory of Jones-Wenzl idempotents over characteristic zero and make some observations in positive characteristic.
In
Section

3
we recount the construction of
p
-Jones-Wenzl elements due to Burrull, Libedinsky and Sentinelli
[
BLS19
]
, with a slight modification to allow for generic parameter.

Section

4
collects the properties of projective modules for
{\rm TL}_{n}
that will be needed in the result and we prove our main theorem in
Section

5
.
Finally,
Section

6
examines the action of the Markov trace on our new elements.

1.
Notation and Conventions

We will use the notation and conventions for
{\rm TL}^{R}_{n}(\delta)
set out in
[
Spe20
]
.
Note that in this formulation, closed loops resolve to a factor of
\delta
as opposed to
-\delta
as is often found in the literature.
Throughout, we will omit the
\delta
from our notation for the algebra
{\rm TL}_{n}^{R}
, as every ring
R
discussed will be naturally unambiguously pointed.

The Temperley-Lieb category
{\mathscr{T\!\!L}}
is simply the linear category on
\mathbb{N}
with morphism spaces spanned by Temperley-Lieb diagrams.
Objects in this category will be written as
\underline{n}
.

Throughout we distinguish the
diagram basis
for
{\rm TL}_{n}
.
This is a basis given by all
(n,n)
Temperley-Lieb diagrams.
Since multiplication of elements in this basis has image in its
\mathbb{Z}[\delta]
-span, this will allow us to move between base rings.

The Temperley-Lieb algebras are all equipped with an anti-automorphism, denoted
\iota
, which acts on diagrams by vertical reflection.
This linear map satisfies
\iota(xy)=\iota(y)\iota(x)
and
\iota(\iota x)=x
for each pair of elements
x,y\in{\rm TL}_{n}
.
This is known as the
cellular involution
.

Each Temperley-Lieb algebra admits a
trivial module
.
This is a one-dimensional module on which every diagram except the identity acts as zero.
The identity diagram acts as one.
Equivalently, if
I_{n}
is the maximal ideal generated by all non-identity diagrams, then the trivial module is
{\rm TL}_{n}/I_{n}
.

Of critical importance will be the
standard modules
of
{\rm TL}_{n}
also known as
cell modules
, denoted
S(n,m)
for
m\leq n
of the same parity.
These have basis given by diagrams from
n
to
m
with the natural left action of
{\rm TL}_{n}
, modulo diagrams with fewer than
m
through-strands.
The trivial module is thus
S(n,n)
.

The
quantum numbers

[n]
are polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}[\delta]
given by
[0]=0
,
[1]=1
and
[n+1]=\delta[n]-[n-1]
.
We will often specialise these to a given ring, if
\delta
is understood, by considering their image under the natural ring homomorphism.
If
\delta\in R
, we say that
\delta

satisfies

[n]
if this image of
[n]
is zero in
R
.

If
\ell
and
p
are given, then the
(\ell,p)
-digits
or the
(\ell,p)
-expansion
of the natural number
n
are the numbers
n_{i}
such that
n=\sum_{i=0}^{b}n_{i}p^{(i)}
where

p^{(i)}=\begin{cases}1&i=0\\
p^{i-1}\ell&i>0\end{cases}

and
0\leq n_{i}<p^{(i+1)}
.

2.
Jones-Wenzl Idempotents

Here we discuss the Jones-Wenzl idempotents, their construction, and when they exist.

2.1.
Semi-simple case

The Jones-Wenzl idempotent, denoted
{\rm JW}_{n}
, is a celebrated element of
{\rm TL}_{n}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}
(where
\delta
is indeterminate).
It is the unique idempotent
e
such that
{\rm TL}_{n}\cdot e
is isomorphic to the trivial module.
As such it satisfies the relations

(2.1)

u_{i}\cdot{\rm JW}_{n}=0\quad\forall\;1\leq i<n.

It is clear that since
{\rm JW}_{n}
is idempotent, the coefficient of the identity diagram
\underline{n}\to\underline{n}
is 1.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
R
be any pointed ring.
Suppose
e
is an idempotent of
{\rm TL}_{n}^{R}
such that
Eq.

2.1
holds. Then
e
is invariant under the cellular involution
\iota
, and
e\cdot u_{i}=0
for all
1\leq i<n
.
Thus
e
is the unique idempotent of
{\rm TL}_{n}^{R}
satisfying
Eq.

2.1
.

Proof.

Write
e=\operatorname{id}_{n}+f
.
Thus
f
lies in the ideal
I_{n}
, and in particular lies in the span of diagrams factoring through some
u_{i}
.
Hence
fe=0
.
However
\iota(f)
is also in
I_{n}
so
(\iota f)e=0
.
Thus

e=(\operatorname{id}_{n}+\iota f)\cdot e=(\iota e)e,

which is fixed under
\iota
.
Thus
0=\iota(u_{i}\cdot e)=\iota e\cdot\iota u_{i}=e\cdot u_{i}
.

Finally, if
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
are two such idempotents, then both have unit coefficient for the identity diagram and so
e_{1}=e_{1}e_{2}=e_{2}
.
∎

We now argue that idempotents satisfying
Eq.

2.1
exist.
Indeed, the algebra
{\rm TL}_{n}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}
is semi-simple so the trivial module is projective.
Thus the idempotent
e
such that
{\rm TL}_{n}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}\cdot e
is isomorphic to the trivial module suffices.

On the other hand, there is also a recursive formulation that explicitly constructs the idempotents:

Lemma 2.2
.

Note that
{\rm JW}_{1}=\operatorname{id}_{1}
.
For
n>1
,

(2.2)

{\rm JW}_{n}={\rm JW}_{n-1}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{1}-\frac{[n-1]}{[n]}({\rm JW}_{n-1}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{1})\circ u_{n-1}\circ({\rm JW}_{n-1}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{1}).

An equivalent formulation is given by Morrison as follows:

Lemma 2.3
.

[
Mor17
, 4.1]

Suppose
D
is a diagram
\underline{n+1}\to\underline{n+1}
. Let
\hat{D}
be the diagram
\underline{n+2}\to\underline{n}
formed by folding across the lowest target site of
D
. Let
\{i\}
be the set of positions of simple caps in
\hat{D}
and
D_{i}\in{\rm TL}_{n}
the diagrams obtained by removing those caps. Then

(2.5)

\underset{\in{\rm JW}_{n+1}}{\textnormal{coeff}}(D)=\sum_{\{i\}}\frac{[i]}{[n+1]}\,\underset{\in{\rm JW}_{n}}{\textnormal{coeff}}\,(D_{i})

There are other recursive relations both for the morphisms and their coefficients in various bases
[
Sen19
]
and expansions involving more or fewer copies of
{\rm JW}_{n}
in the second term (so-called “single” and “triple” clasp formulae).
The authors are not aware of any formula for the coefficients of diagrams in the Jones-Wenzl idempotents in the semi-simple case that is not inherently recurrent.

2.2.
Characteristic Zero

If we now specialise to a characteristic zero pointed field
(k,\overline{\delta})
where
\overline{\delta}
satisfies
[\ell]
but no
[m]
for
0<m<\ell
, the Temperley-Lieb algebras are no longer semi-simple for all
n
.
In this case the trivial module is not, in general, projective, which means that the Jones-Wenzl elements “do not exist”.

To be precise, let
m(\delta)\in\mathbb{Z}[\delta]
be the minimal polynomial of
\overline{\delta}
over the integers and
\mathfrak{p}
the prime ideal of
\mathbb{Q}[\delta]
generated by
m(\delta)
.
The element
{\rm JW}_{n}
lies in
{\rm TL}_{n}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}
.
We construct both the “integer form” of
{\rm TL}_{n}
over
\mathbb{Q}[\delta]_{\mathfrak{p}}
and the algebra of interest which is defined over the characteristic zero “target” field
\mathbb{Q}[\delta]_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq k
.
We can summarise these rings as:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}[\delta]_{\mathfrak{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}[\delta]_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}_{\mathfrak{p}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}

If
n\geq\ell
and
n\not\equiv_{\ell}-1
then in the basis of diagrams, the Jones-Wenzl idempotent in
{\rm TL}_{n}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}
does not lie in the image of
i
and thus cannot descend to an element of
{\rm TL}^{k}_{n}
.
Indeed if it did, then the trivial module would be projective which is not the case
[
RSA14
, 8.1]
.

We can rephrase as follows.
If
n\geq\ell
and
n\not\equiv_{\ell}-1
, then the Jones-Wenzl idempotent
{\rm JW}_{n}
written in terms of diagrams cannot be put over a denominator not divisible by
m(\delta)
.

For example, observe that
Eq.

2.4
makes no sense if
\overline{\delta}=\pm 1
so that
[3]=0
.
However, if
n\equiv_{3}-1
then indeed the equation makes sense. As an example, set
\ell=2
so
\delta=0
and then
Eq.

2.4
simplifies to the sum of three diagrams.

If we have the particular case
n=\ell-1
then Graham and Lehrer found an elegant “non-recursive” form for the coefficients of the diagrams in
{\rm JW}_{n}
.
Recall from
[
Spe20
]
the definition of the “candidate morphisms”

(2.6)

v_{r,s}=\sum_{x}h_{F(x)}x

and the subsequent proposition

Proposition 2.4
.

[
GL98
, 3.6]

If
s<r<s+2\ell
and
s+r\equiv_{\ell}-2
then the map
S(n,r)\to S(n,s)
given by
x\mapsto x\circ v_{r,s}
is a morphism of
{\rm TL}_{n}
modules for every
n
.

The immediate corollary (from setting
s=0
and
r=2\ell-2
) is that the Jones-Wenzl idempotent
{\rm JW}_{\ell-1}
exists over
k
and it’s diagram coefficients can be found by “rotating the target sites” and then computing the hook-formula
h_{F(x)}
.

2.3.
Positive Characteristic

Let us now focus on the positive characteristic case.
As before, if no quantum number vanishes, we are in a semi-simple case and
Eq.

2.2
gives us all
{\rm JW}_{n}
.
Thus assume that we are working over a pointed field
(k,\overline{\delta})
of characteristic
p
and that
\ell
is the least non-negative such that
[\ell]
is satisfied by
\bar{\delta}\in k
.
Thus we say that we are under
(\ell,p)
-torsion.

Let
\overline{m}(\delta)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[\delta]
be the minimal polynomial satisfied by
\overline{\delta}
and let
m(\delta)
be a preimage in
\mathbb{Z}[\delta]
.
Then
\mathfrak{m}=(p,m(\delta))
is a maximal ideal in
\mathbb{Z}[\delta]
.
Consider
S=\mathbb{Z}[\delta]_{\mathfrak{m}}
, a local noetherian domain with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}S
.
Now
m(x)\not\in(p)
so,
(0)\subset(p)\subset\mathfrak{m}
strictly and
S
is regular of Krull dimension 2.
As such, its completion with respect to
\mathfrak{m}
, which we will call
R
is regular and hence a domain.
Set
F
to be the field of fractions of
R
.
This is a characteristic zero field containing
\mathbb{Q}(\delta)
.
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R=\widehat{\mathbb{Z}[\delta]_{\mathfrak{m}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R/\mathfrak{m}R}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k}

What we have now is a “
(\ell,p)
-modular system” in that we have a triple
(F,R,k)
such that
F
is a characteristic zero field, which is the field of fractions of
R
, a complete local domain with residue field
k
.
Thus, any idempotent in an algebra defined over
k
can be raised to an idempotent over
R
and then injected in to one defined over
F
.

We would like to know if this is reversible.
That is, given an idempotent defined over
\mathbb{Q}(\delta)
, we consider it as an idempotent over
F
and ask if it lies in the algebra over
R
.
If so, we may reduce it modulo the maximal ideal to find an idempotent over
k
.

In plain terms, we wish to know if the coefficients of the diagrams in
{\rm JW}_{n}
can be written without denominators divisible by
p
or
m(\delta)
.
If so, the idempotent “exists” in our field of positive characteristic.

Should an element
e
satisfying
Eq.

2.1
exist over
k
, it is clear that
{\rm TL}_{n}^{k}\cdot e
is a trivial module and so the trivial module is projective.
Thus we can raise the idempotent
e
to an element of
{\rm TL}_{n}^{R}
where action by
u_{i}
sends the element to something divisible by
\mathfrak{m}^{r}
for every
r
and hence equal to zero.
That is to say, it lifts to
{\rm JW}_{n}
.

Thus we may consider an alternative defining property of the Jones-Wenzl element that it is the idempotent that generates the trivial module’s cover, which is equal to the trivial module, whenever that is the case.

As such, the results of
[
Spe20
]
(in particular Theorem 3.4 combined with 8.3) can be interpreted as follows:

Theorem 2.5
.

The Jones-Wenzl idempotent
{\rm JW}_{n}
in
{\rm TL}_{n}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}
descends to an element of
{\rm TL}_{n}^{k}
iff
n<\ell
or
n<\ell p
and
n\equiv_{\ell}-1
or
n=a\ell p^{k}-1
for some
k\geq 1
and
1\leq a<p
.

An equivalent statement is

Corollary 2.6
.

The Jones-Wenzl idempotent
{\rm JW}_{n}
descends to an element of
{\rm TL}^{k}_{n}(\delta)
iff the quantum binomials
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{n}{r}
are invertible in
k
for all
0\leq r\leq n
.

This has been shown by Webster in the appendix of
[
EL17
]
.
However the proof there, and the alternative provided in
[
Web14
]
both construct linear maps of representations of
U_{q}(\mathfrak{sl}_{2})
which are shown to be morphisms (and hence elements of
{\rm TL}_{n}
).
This is the only proof not to use the Schur-Weyl duality of which the authors are aware.

Regrettably, the authors are not aware of any formula in the style of
Eq.

2.6
giving the form of these idempotents in generality.
However, we are able to illuminate one further case constructively.

Given
\ell
and
p
, we write
p^{(i)}=\ell p^{i-1}
for all
i>0
and
p^{(0)}=1
.
We will explicitly construct
{\rm JW}_{2p^{(r)}-1}
.
Key in the below is that the construction of
{\rm JW}_{p^{(r)}-1}
as an “unfolding” of a trivial submodule of
S(2p^{(r)}-2,0)
shows that the first trace vanishes (it corresponds by the action of
u_{p^{(r)}-1}
on that module).

Proposition 2.7
.

The element
{\rm JW}_{2p^{(r)}-1}
exists in
{\rm TL}^{k}_{2p^{(r)}-1}
for each
r\geq 1
.

Proof.

We construct an element of
{\rm TL}_{2p^{(r)}-1}
for which the identity diagram appears with coefficient 1 and which is killed by all cups and caps.
It is clear that this element then satisfies
Eq.

2.1
.

To do this, consider the “turn up” operator which takes a morphism
\underline{n}\to\underline{m}
to a morphism
\underline{n+1}\to\underline{m-1}
by “turning the top strand back”. Formally, this is the map

(2.7)

u_{m,1}:x\mapsto(\operatorname{id}\otimes x)\circ(\cap\otimes\operatorname{id}_{m-1}).

We can iterate this to turn multiple strands:
set
u_{m,k}=u_{m-k+1,1}\circ u_{m,k-1}
.

Now, let
J_{i}=u_{i}({\rm JW}_{p^{(r)}-1})
and
J_{-i}=\iota\left(J_{i}\right)
for
0\leq i\leq p^{(r)}-1
.
Thus
J_{i}
is a morphism
\underline{p^{(r)}-1+i}\to\underline{p^{(r)}-1-i}
.

This stems from the observation that rotation of the Jones-Wenzl element,

In the general case where

p
-Jones-Wenzl Idempotents

We now turn to extending the work of Burrull, Libedinsky and Sentinelli
[
BLS19
]
in generalising the definition of the Jones-Wenzl idempotent to a sensible element of
{\rm TL}^{k}_{n}
for all

The element constructed in
[
BLS19
]
is in fact the idempotent defining the projective cover of the trivial module, although it is not explicitly stated as such.
However, the construction present only covers the
\delta=2

In this paper we present a generalisation to all
\delta

Our results will specialise in the case
\delta=2

\ell=p

We briefly recount their methodology here for completeness and introduce terminology to indicate dependence on
\delta

The definition that follows is largely a rewrite of section 2.3 in
[
BLS19
]

]

\operatorname{supp}(n)=I_{n}=\{n_{b}p^{(b)}\pm n_{b-1}p^{(b-1)}\pm\cdots\pm n_{a}p^{(a)}-1\}

BLS19

n<\ell

and
3.7
hold for all
n^{\prime}
with
|I_{n^{\prime}}|<|I_{n}|
.
In particular the results are known for
{\mathrm{f}[n]}
.
Thus
\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{{\mathrm{f}[n]}}
descends to an idempotent
\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{k}_{{\mathrm{f}[n]}}
in
{\rm TL}_{n}^{k}
which describes the projective module
P({\mathrm{f}[n]},{\mathrm{f}[n]})
.

Now consider the module
P({\mathrm{f}[n]},{\mathrm{f}[n]})\mathord{\uparrow}_{{\mathrm{f}[n]}}^{n}
.
This is isomorphic to the module
{\rm TL}_{n}^{k}\cdot g
where
g
is the idempotent
\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{k}_{{\mathrm{f}[n]}}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{n-{\mathrm{f}[n]}}
in
{\rm TL}_{n}^{k}
.
The composition factors of this module are described in
Eq.

4.5
and the discussion preceding it.
In particular, if
e
is the unique idempotent of
{\rm TL}_{n}^{k}
describing the projective cover of the trivial module, then

e\cdot P({\mathrm{f}[n]},{\mathrm{f}[n]})\mathord{\uparrow}_{{\mathrm{f}[n]}}^{n}
is a non-zero module isomorphic to
P(n,n)
.

As such
e\cdot g=g\cdot e=e
.
If we lift both of these elements to
F
, say to
\hat{e}
and
\hat{g}
we obtain
\hat{e}\cdot\hat{g}=\hat{g}\cdot\hat{e}=\hat{e}
, so in particular

(5.1)

\hat{e}\in\hat{g}\cdot{\rm TL}_{n}^{F}\cdot\hat{g}\cong\operatorname{End}_{{\rm TL}^{F}_{n}}({\rm TL}_{n}^{F}\cdot\hat{g}).

But
{\rm TL}_{n}^{F}\cdot\hat{g}
is exactly the lift of
P({\mathrm{f}[n]},{\mathrm{f}[n]})\mathord{\uparrow}_{{\mathrm{f}[n]}}^{n}
to characteristic zero and so
\hat{g}=\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{{\mathrm{f}[n]}}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{n-{\mathrm{f}[n]}}
.
Thus
Proposition

3.4
claims that
\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{n}\cdot\hat{g}=\hat{g}\cdot\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{n}=\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{n}
.
Thus too

(5.2)

\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{n}\in\hat{g}\cdot{\rm TL}_{n}^{F}\cdot\hat{g}\cong\operatorname{End}_{{\rm TL}^{F}_{n}}({\rm TL}_{n}^{F}\cdot\hat{g}).

However, recall that
{\rm TL}_{n}^{F}
is semi-simple and that
e[P({\mathrm{f}[n]},{\mathrm{f}[n]})\mathord{\uparrow}_{{\mathrm{f}[n]}}^{n}]=e[P(n,n)]+M
where
M
does not have support intersecting that of
e[P(n,n)]
.
This is to say that there is a unique idempotent in
\operatorname{End}_{{\rm TL}_{n}^{F}}\left(P({\mathrm{f}[n]},{\mathrm{f}[n]})\mathord{\uparrow}_{\mathrm{f}[n]}^{n}\right)
with image having composition factors given by
e[P(n,n)]
.
Clearly by construction
\hat{e}
is such an idempotent, and
Corollary

3.3
shows that
\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{n}
is too.
Hence they must be equal.
∎

By linear extension, this induces the trace
\tau:{\rm TL}_{n}\to{\rm TL}_{n-1}
for each
n\geq 1
as well as the “full trace”
\tau^{n}:{\rm TL}_{n}\to TL_{0}\simeq k
.
These full traces were critical in the construction of the Jones polynomial invariant of knots.
Computations of partial traces of generalized Jones-Wenzl idempotents are necessary for computations of certain truncated categories
[
STWZ21
,
Spe21
]
.
We can also compare the partial trace of generalized and traditional Jones-Wenzl idempotents to get a sense of how the mixed characteristic generalises the semi-simple case.

The action of the trace on Jones-Wenzl elements over characteristic zero is well understood and is almost folklore.

Lemma 6.1
.

[
BLS19
, Lemma 3.1]

In
{\rm TL}_{n}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}
,

(6.2)

\tau^{m}\left({\rm JW}_{n}\right)=\frac{[n+1]}{[n+1-m]}\,{\rm JW}_{n-m}.

However, this breaks down over positive characteristic.
For example, the form of
{\rm JW}_{2p^{(r)}-1}
given in
Proposition

2.7
makes clear that

(6.3)

\tau\left({\rm JW}_{2p^{(r)}-1}\right)=2\;J_{p^{(r)}-1}\otimes J_{-p^{(r)}+1}.

This can be read as stating that
\frac{[2p^{(r)}]}{[2p^{(r)}-1]}{\rm JW}_{2p^{(r)}-2}
exists over
k
and has value given by
Eq.

6.3
.
Notice that such a morphism has zero through-degree.

Recall from
[
Spe20
, Lemma 2.2]
that
[\ell m]/[\ell n]=m/n
in
k
. The following is a trivial corollary of
Lemma

6.1
.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
1\leq a\leq p
and
r\geq 1
. Then as elements in
{\rm TL}^{k}
,

(6.4)

\tau^{p}\left({\rm JW}^{k}_{ap^{(r)}-1}\right)=\frac{a}{a-1}\;{\rm JW}^{k}_{(a-1)p^{(r)}-1}.

We now ask how the trace acts on elements
\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{n}
.

Proposition 6.3
.

Recall that
n+1=\sum_{i=a}^{b}n_{i}p^{(i)}
and let
t=p^{(a)}
.

(6.5)

\tau^{t}\left(\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{n}\right)=\begin{cases}\frac{[n_{a}]}{[n_{a}-1]}\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{n-t}&n_{a}>1\text{ and }a=0\\
\frac{n_{a}}{n_{a}-1}\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{n-t}&n_{a}>1\text{ and }a>0\\
[2]\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{n-t}&n_{a}=1\text{ and }a=0\\
2\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{n-t}&n_{a}=1\text{ and }a>0\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
m=n-{\mathrm{f}[n]}=n_{a}p^{(a)}>p^{(a)}=t
so that in particular
{\mathrm{f}[n]}={\mathrm{f}[n-t]}
and

(6.6)

I_{n}=\left(I_{\mathrm{f}[n]}+m\right)\sqcup\left(I_{{\mathrm{f}[n]}}-m\right)\quad;\quad I_{n-t}=\left(I_{\mathrm{f}[n]}+m-t\right)\sqcup\left(I_{{\mathrm{f}[n]}}-m+t\right)

Then notice that for
i\in I_{\mathrm{f}[n]}
,

(6.7)

\displaystyle\tau^{t}\;U_{n}^{i-m}

\displaystyle=U_{n-t}^{i-m+t}

(6.8)

\displaystyle\tau^{t}\;U_{n}^{i+m}

\displaystyle=\frac{[i+m+1]}{[i+m-t+1]}U_{n-1}^{i+m-t}

Recall from
Eq.

3.5
that

(6.9)

\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{n}=\sum_{i+1\in I_{\mathrm{f}[n]}}\left(\frac{[i+1-m]}{[i+1]}\lambda_{\mathrm{f}[n]}^{i}U_{n}^{i-m}+\lambda_{\mathrm{f}[n]}^{i}U_{n}^{i+m}\right)

and also

(6.10)

\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{n-t}=\sum_{i+1\in I_{\mathrm{f}[n]}}\left(\frac{[i+1-m+t]}{[i+1]}\lambda_{\mathrm{f}[n]}^{i}U_{n-t}^{i-m+t}+\lambda_{\mathrm{f}[n]}^{i}U_{n-t}^{i+m-t}\right)

Then
Eqs.

6.7
and
6.8
with
Eq.

6.9
give

(6.11)

\tau^{t}\left(\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{n}\right)=\sum_{i+1\in I_{\mathrm{f}[n]}}\Bigg(\frac{[i+1-m]}{[i+1-(m-t)]}\frac{[i+1+t-m]}{[i+1]}\lambda_{\mathrm{f}[n]}^{i}U_{n-t}^{i-m+t}\\
+\frac{[i+1+m]}{[i+1+(m-t)]}\lambda_{\mathrm{f}[n]}^{i}U_{n-t}^{i+m-t}\Bigg)

Now, for all
i+1\in I_{\mathrm{f}[n]}
we have that
\ell\mid i+1
and so
[i+1+m]=\pm[m]
(depending on if
[i+1]=\pm 1
) and similarly for
[i+1-m]
.
Hence we see that all the factors actually fall out and

(6.12)

\tau^{t}\left(\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{k}_{n}\right)=\frac{[m]}{[m-t]}\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{k}_{n-t},

recovering a form of
Eq.

6.2
for
(\ell,p)
-Jones-Wenzl elements.
However, if
a>0
then this simplifies to
n_{a}/(n_{a}-1)
and if
a=0
this is simply
[n_{a}]/[n_{a}-1]
as desired.

If, on the other hand,
{\mathrm{f}[n]}=n-t
so that
m=t=p^{(a)}
, we get a slightly different beahvior (indeed, this is necessary to avoid a division by zero in
Eq.

6.12
).
Recall

(6.13)

[i][j-k]+[j][k-i]+[k][i-j]=0,

so that

(6.14)

[a+b]+[a-b]=\frac{[2b][a]}{[b]}.

In particular

(6.15)

[i+1+p^{(a)}]+[i+1-p^{(a)}]=\frac{[2p^{(a)}][i+1]}{[p^{(a)}]}=\begin{cases}[2][i+1]&a=0\\
2[i+1]&a>0\end{cases}.

Thus if
n
is
(\ell,p)
-Adam, then

\displaystyle\tau^{t}\left(\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{k}_{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i+1\in I_{\mathrm{f}[n]}}\Bigg(\frac{[i+1-t]}{[i+1]}\lambda_{\mathrm{f}[n]}^{i}U_{n-1}^{i}+\frac{[i+1+t]}{[i+1]}\lambda_{\mathrm{f}[n]}^{i}U_{n-1}^{i}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=[2]\sum_{i+1\in I_{\mathrm{f}[n]}}\lambda_{\mathrm{f}[n]}^{i}U_{\mathrm{f}[n]}^{i}

(6.16)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}[2]\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{k}_{n-t}&a=0\\
2\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{k}_{n-t}&a>0\end{cases}.

∎

A result of the above computation is that
\tau^{m}\left(\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{k}_{n}\right)=[2][n_{0}]\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{k}_{{\mathrm{f}[n]}}
if
a=0
and
2n_{a}\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{k}_{{\mathrm{f}[n]}}
otherwise.

The question of tracing
\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{k}_{{\mathrm{f}[n]}}
by amounts other than valid
t

is still not well understood.
For example, if we try trace a single strand when
n
is Adam,
is clear that
\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{k}_{n}
is the image of
{\rm JW}_{n}
and hence
\tau\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{k}_{n}
is the image of
[n+1]/[n]\,{\rm JW}_{n}
.
As such, since
{\rm JW}_{n}
is killed by the action of all
u_{i}
, so too must
\tau\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{k}_{n}
be.
By considering all diagrams of the form
|x\rangle\langle y|
(see
[
Spe20
]
for notation) where
y
is a fixed diagram of maximal degree
d
, we see that this implies the existence of a trivial submodule of
S(n,d)
.
Knowledge of where these modules could exist then give us restrictions on the valid values of
d
.

As a first attempt at understanding the trace we may simply ask if
\tau\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{k}_{n}
has maximal through degree.
That is, does it have a non-zero coefficient of the identity diagram?
Clearly this is always the case if
n_{0}\neq 0
.
If
n
is
(\ell,p)
-Adam, then
Lemma

6.1
holds and
\tau^{m}(\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{k}_{n})
has maximal through degree iff
\nu_{(p)}(n+1)=\nu_{(p)}(n+1-m)
. Note that it is guaranteed that
\nu_{(p)}(n+1)\geq\nu_{(p)}(n+1-m)
as
n
is Adam.

We can use the definition of
\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{n}
as a sum of the
U_{n}^{i}
to calculate the case when
n
has nonzero
(\ell,p)
valuation and we trace by an amount less than
p^{(a)}
(as any larger can be covered by
Corollary

6.2
first).

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
n+1=\sum_{i=a}^{b}n_{i}p^{(i)}
for
b>a>0
and
t<n_{a}p^{(a)}
.
Then the coefficient of the identity diagram in
\tau^{t}\left(\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{n}\right)
is
[2]^{t}
.

Proof.

We use the definition
\prescript{p}{\ell}{{\rm JW}}^{\mathbb{Q}(\delta)}_{n}=\sum_{i+1\in I_{n}}\lambda_{n}^{i}U_{n}^{i}
and evaluate
\tau^{t}U_{n}^{i}
.
Since
n
is not
(\ell,p)
-Adam, we can split
I_{n}
into
I_{\mathrm{f}[n]}+m
and
I_{\mathrm{f}[n]}-m
for
m=n-{\mathrm{f}[n]}>t
.

Referring to
Eq.

3.6
, we see that for
i\in I_{{\mathrm{f}[n]}}
,
\tau^{t}U_{n}^{i+m}
has the unit diagram with nonzero coefficient iff
i={\mathrm{f}[n]}
. The coefficient is
[2]^{t}
as
\lambda_{n}^{{\mathrm{f}[n]}}=1
.
On the other hand, the through degree of
U_{n}^{i-m}
is
i-m+t<n-m+t<n
.
∎

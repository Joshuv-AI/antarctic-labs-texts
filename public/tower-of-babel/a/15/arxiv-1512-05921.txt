A Sharp Threshold for van der Waerden’s Theorem in Random Subsets The cooperation of the authors was supported by the German-Israeli Foundation for Scientific Research and Development .
Mathias Schacht

Abstract
We establish sharpness for the threshold of van der Waerden’s theorem in random subsets of ℤ / n ​ ℤ \mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z} . More precisely, for k ≥ 3 k\geq 3 and Z ⊆ ℤ / n ​ ℤ Z\subseteq\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z} we say Z Z has the van der Waerden property if any two-colouring of Z Z yields a monochromatic arithmetic progression of length k k . Rödl and Ruciński (1995) determined the threshold for this property for any k k and we show that this threshold is sharp. The proof is based on Friedgut’s criterion (1999) for sharp thresholds and on the recently developed container method for independent sets in hypergraphs by Balogh, Morris and Samotij (2015) and by Saxton and Thomason (2015).

Introduction

One of the main research directions in extremal and probabilistic combinatorics over the last two decades has been the extension of classical
results for discrete structures to the sparse random setting.
Prime examples include Ramsey’s theorem for graphs and hypergraphs
[
9
,
6
,
3
]
,
Turán’s theorem in extremal graph theory, and Szemerédi’s theorem on arithmetic progressions
[
13
,
3
]

(see also
[
1
,
11
,
2
]
). Results of that form establish the
threshold
for the
classical result in the random setting. For a property the threshold is given by a function
\hat{p}=\hat{p}(n)

such that for every
p_{0}\ll\hat{p}
the random graph
G(n,p_{0})
(or a random binomial subset of
[n]=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
)
with parameter
p_{0}
, the probability the property
holds is asymptotically zero,
whereas if
p_{0}
is replaced by
some
p_{1}\gg\hat{p}
the property does hold asymptotically almost surely (a.a.s.), i.e.,
for a property
\mathcal{P}
of graphs and probabilities
p=p(n)
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathds{P}\big(G(n,p)\in\mathcal{P}\big)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if}\ p\ll\hat{p}\\
1,&\text{if}\ p\gg\hat{p}\,.\end{cases}

The two statements involving
p_{0}
and
p_{1}

are referred to as the
0-statement
and the
1-statement
. For the properties mentioned above it can be shown that the optimal parameters
p_{0}
and
p_{1}
for which
the
0
-statement and the
1
-statement hold, only differ by a
multiplicative constant.

The threshold for van der Waerden’s theorem
[
14
]
is such an example and was obtained by
Rödl and Ruciński in
[
9
,
10
]
. We denote by
[n]_{p}
the binomial random subset of
[n]
, where every element of

[n]
is included independently with probability
p=p(n)
. Furthermore, for a subset
A\subseteq[n]

we write
A\rightarrow(k\text{-AP})_{r}
to denote
the fact that no matter how one colours the elements of
A
with
r
colours there is
always a monochromatic arithmetic progression with
k
elements in
A
.

Theorem 1

(Rödl & Ruciński)
.

For every
k\geq 3
and
r\geq 2
there exist constants
c_{0}
,
c_{1}>0
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathds{P}([n]_{p}\rightarrow(k\text{-AP})_{r})=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }p\leq c_{0}n^{-\frac{1}{k-1}},\\
1,&\text{ if }p\geq c_{1}n^{-\frac{1}{k-1}}.\end{cases}

For the corresponding result in
\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z}
and for two colours
we close the gap between
c_{0}
and
c_{1}
. More precisely,
we show that there exist bounded sequences
c_{0}(n)
and
c_{1}(n)
with ratio
tending to 1 as
n
tends to infinity such that the statement holds
(see Theorem
2
below).
In other words, we establish a
sharp threshold
for
van der Waerden’s theorem for two colours in
\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z}
.

Similarly to the situation for subsets of
[n]

we write
A\rightarrow(k\text{-AP})_{r}
for subsets
A\subseteq\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z}

if any
r
-colouring of
A
yields a monochromatic arithmetic progression with
k
-elements in
\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z}

and we write
A\nrightarrow(k\text{-AP})_{r}
if
A
fails to have this property.
Moreover, we denote by
\mathds{Z}_{n,p}

the binomial random subset of
\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z}
with parameter
p
. With this notation at hand we can state our main result.

Theorem 2
.

For all
k\geq 3
there exist constants
c_{1}>c_{0}>0
and a function
c(n)
satisfying
c_{0}\leq c(n)\leq c_{1}

such that for every
\varepsilon>0
we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathds{P}(\mathds{Z}_{n,p}\rightarrow(k\text{-AP})_{2})=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }p\leq(1-\varepsilon)c(n)n^{-\frac{1}{k-1}},\\
1,&\text{ if }p\geq(1+\varepsilon)c(n)n^{-\frac{1}{k-1}}.\end{cases}

We have to insist on the setting of
\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z}
(instead of
[n]
) since the
symmetry will play a small but crucial rôle in our proof. Another shortcoming is the
restriction to two colours
r=2
and we believe it would be very interesting to extend the result
to arbitrary
r
. We remark that only a few sharp thresholds for Ramsey properties
are known (see, e.g.,
[
4
,
5
]
) so far.

Among other tools our proof relies heavily on the criterion for sharp thresholds of
Friedgut and its extension due to Bourgain
[
7
]
.
Another crucial tool is the recent
container theorem
for independent sets in hypergraphs
due to Balogh, Morris and Samotij
[
1
]
and Thomason and Saxton
[
11
]
.

Our proof extends to other Ramsey properties for two colours, as long as the corresponding extremal
problem is
degenerate
, i.e., positive density yields many copies of the target structure
and the target structure is strictly balanced with respect to its so-called
2
-density
.
For example, even cycles in graphs, complete
k
-partite,
k
-uniform hypergraphs, and strictly balanced, density regular Rado systems (see
[
10
]
)
satisfy these assumptions. Moreover, Schacht and Schulenburg
[
12
]
noted that
the approach undertaken here can be refined to give a shorter proof for the sharp threshold
of the Ramsey property for triangles and two colours from
[
5
]

and, more generally, for arbitrary odd cycles.

2
Locality of coarse thresholds

In
[
7
]
Friedgut gave a necessary condition for a graph property to have a coarse threshold, namely, that it is approximable by a “local” property. In the appendix to this work
Bourgain proved a similar result for more general discrete structures. Here we
state the special case applicable for properties in
\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z}
.

Theorem 3

(Bourgain)
.

There exist functions
\delta(C,\tau)
and
K(C,\tau)
such that the following holds. Let
p=o(1)

as
n
tends to infinity, let
\mathcal{A}
be a monotone family of subsets of
\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z}
, with

\tau<\mu(p,\mathcal{A}):=\mathds{P}(\mathds{Z}_{n,p}\in\mathcal{A})<1-\tau\,,

and assume also
p\cdot\frac{d\mu(p,\mathcal{A})}{dp}\leq C
.
Then there exists some
B\subseteq\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z}
with
|B|\leq K
such that

\mathds{P}(\mathds{Z}_{n,p}\in\mathcal{A}\,|\,B\subseteq\mathds{Z}_{n,p})>\mathds{P}(\mathds{Z}_{n,p}\in\mathcal{A})+\delta\,.

(1)

Note that whenever a property
\mathcal{A}
(or rather, a series of properties
\mathcal{A}_{n}
) has a coarse threshold there exist constants
C
and
\tau
such that for infinitely many values of
n
the hypothesis of the theorem holds.
For applications, it would be problematic if there exists a
B
with

|B|\leq K
and
B\in\mathcal{A}
, since this would trivialise the conclusion (
1
). However, as observed in
[
8
]
, the above theorem can be strengthened, without modifying the original proof, to deduce that the set of
B
’s for which the assertion holds has non-negligible measure, i.e.,
there exists a family
\mathcal{B}
such that

\mathds{P}(B\subseteq\mathds{Z}_{n,p}\ \text{for some}\ B\in\mathcal{B})>\eta,

where
\eta>0
depends only on
C
and
\tau
but not on
n
,
and every
B\in\mathcal{B}
satisfies the conclusion of Theorem
3
, i.e.,
|B|\leq K
and

\mathds{P}(\mathds{Z}_{n,p}\in\mathcal{A}\,|\,B\subseteq\mathds{Z}_{n,p})>\mathds{P}(\mathds{Z}_{n,p}\in\mathcal{A})+\delta\,.

This allows us to make assumptions about
B
in the application below, as long as the set of
B
’s violating the assumptions has negligible measure.
In particular, Lemma
4
below implies that any collection
of sets
B\subseteq\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z}
, each of bounded size and
with
B\rightarrow(k\text{-AP})_{2}
, appear only with probability tending to
zero in
\mathds{Z}_{n,p}
for
p=O(n^{-\frac{1}{k-1}})
. Consequently, in our proof we can therefore assume
that the set
B
, provided by Theorem
3
on the assumption that

\mathds{Z}_{n,p}\rightarrow(k\text{-AP})_{2}
has a coarse thresholds, itself fails to have the van der Waerden property, i.e.,

B\nrightarrow(k\text{-AP})_{2}
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a family of subsets of
\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z}
with the property that every
B\in\mathcal{B}
satisfies

|B|\leq\log\log n
and
B\rightarrow(k\text{-AP})_{2}
. Then for every
c>0
and every
sequence of probabilities
p=p(n)\leq cn^{-\frac{1}{k-1}}
we have
\mathds{P}(B\subseteq\mathds{Z}_{n,p}\ \text{for some}\ B\in\mathcal{B})=o(1)
.

Lemma
4
was implicitly proved in
[
10
]
*Section 7
(see the Deterministic and the Probabilistic Lemma there). For completeness
we include a sketch of the proof here.

Proof (Sketch).

Let
k\geq 3
be an integer and let
p=p(n)\leq cn^{-1/(k-1)}

for some
c\in\mathds{R}_{>0}
. For a set
Z\subseteq\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z}
we consider the auxiliary

k
-uniform hypergraph
H_{Z,k}
with vertex set
Z
and hyperedges
corresponding to
k
-APs in
Z
. The Deterministic Lemma
[
10
]
*p. 500
asserts that if
Z\rightarrow(k\text{-AP})_{2}
, then one of the following configurations
must appear as a subhypergraph of
H_{Z,k}
:

(

)

either
H_{Z,k}
contains a subhypergraph of type
\mathcal{T}_{1}

consisting of a hyperedge
e_{0}
and a
loose cycle
C_{\ell}

of some length
\ell\geq 3
, i.e.,
C_{\ell}
consists of
\ell
hyperedges

e_{1},\dots,e_{\ell}
satisfying for
every
1\leq i<j\leq\ell

|e_{i}\cap e_{j}|=\begin{cases}1\,,&\text{if}\ j=i+1\ \text{or}\ (i=1\ \text{and}\ j=\ell)\\
0\,,&\text{otherwise}\,,\end{cases}

while the additional hyperedge
e_{0}
has at least one vertex outside
the cycle and shares at least two vertices with the cycles, i.e.,

2\leq|e_{0}\cap V(C_{\ell})|<k\,;

(

)

or
H_{Z,k}
contains a subhypergraph of type
\mathcal{T}_{2}

consisting of a non-induced loose path
P_{\ell}

of some length
\ell\geq 2
, i.e.,
\ell
hyperedges
e_{1},\dots,e_{\ell}

satisfying for

1\leq i<j\leq\ell

|e_{i}\cap e_{j}|=\begin{cases}1\,,&\text{if}\ j=i+1\\
0\,,&\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

The condition that
P_{\ell}
is non-induced means
that there exists some hyperedge
e_{0}
in
E(H_{Z,k})\smallsetminus E(P_{\ell})

such that
e_{0}\subseteq V(P_{\ell})
.

A simple first moment argument shows that a.a.s. no hypergraph on at most
\log n
vertices
of types
\mathcal{T}_{1}
or
\mathcal{T}_{2}
appears in
H_{\mathds{Z}_{n,p},k}
.
For that let
X_{1}
(resp.
X_{2}
)
be the random variable counting the number of
copies of hypergraphs of type
\mathcal{T}_{1}
(resp.
\mathcal{T}_{2}
) on at most
\log n
vertices in
\mathds{Z}_{n,p}
. Below we show that there exists a constant
K=K(k,c)
such that

\mathds{E}X_{1}\leq(\log n)^{K}\cdot p\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathds{E}X_{2}\leq(\log n)^{K}\cdot p

(2)

and since
p\leq cn^{-1/(k-1)}\ll(\log n)^{-K}

the lemma follows from Markov’s inequality.

We start with the random variable
X_{1}
. Since the hyperedges of
H_{\mathds{Z}_{n,p},k}

correspond to
k
-APs the number
Y_{\ell}
of loose cycles
C_{\ell}
of length at
least
\ell\geq 3
in

H_{\mathds{Z}_{n,p},k}
satisfies

\mathds{E}Y_{\ell}\leq O(p^{(k-1)\ell}\cdot n^{\ell})=O(c^{(k-1)\ell}n^{-\ell}\cdot n^{\ell})=O(c^{k\ell})\,.

For a given loose cycle
C_{\ell}
the additional hyperedge
e_{0}
(to complete
C_{\ell}
to
a hypergraph of type
\mathcal{T}_{1}
) shares at least two vertices with
C_{\ell}
. However,
with these two vertices fixed there are less than
k^{2}
possibilities
to complete this choice to a
k
-AP in
\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z}
, i.e., these two vertices can be completed
in at most
k^{2}
ways to form the hyperedge
e_{0}
in
H_{\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z},k}
.
In other words, for a fixed loose cycle
C_{\ell}
on
(k-1)\ell
vertices there
are at most
(k-1)^{2}\ell^{2}\cdot k^{2}
possibilities to complete
C_{\ell}
to a hypergraph
of type
\mathcal{T}_{1}
. Furthermore, since the hyperedge
e_{0}
is required to have
at least one vertex outside
C_{\ell}
we have

\mathds{E}X_{1}<\sum_{\ell=3}^{\log\log n}k^{4}\ell^{2}p\cdot\mathds{E}Y_{\ell}=\sum_{\ell=3}^{\log\log n}O(\ell^{2}p\cdot c^{k\ell})\leq(\log n)^{K}\cdot p

for some constant
K=K(k,c)
,
which establishes the first estimate in (
2
).

Similarly, for the random variable
X_{2}
we first observe that
the expected number
Y^{\prime}_{\ell}
of loose paths
P_{\ell}
of length at
least
\ell\geq 2
in
\mathds{Z}_{n,p}
satisfies

\mathds{E}Y^{\prime}_{\ell}\leq O(p^{k}n^{2}\cdot p^{(k-1)(\ell-1)}n^{(\ell-1)})=O(pn\cdot c^{k\ell})\,.

Since the additional hyperedge
e_{0}
reduces the expected number of choices for
at least one of the hyperedges of
P_{\ell}
from
O(p^{k-1}n)
to

O(p^{k-1})
and since
e_{0}
is fixed after selecting two of its vertices within
P_{\ell}

we arrive at

\mathds{E}X_{2}\leq\sum_{\ell=2}^{\log\log n}O\left(\frac{\ell^{2}}{n}\right)\cdot\mathds{E}Y^{\prime}_{\ell}=\sum_{\ell=2}^{\log\log n}O(\ell^{2}p\cdot c^{k\ell})\leq(\log n)^{K}\cdot p

for some constant
K=K(k,c)
,
which establishes the second estimate in (
2
)
and concludes the proof of the lemma.

3
Lemmas and the proof of the main theorem

In this section we state all the necessary notation and lemmas to give the proof of Theorem
2
.
We start with an outline of this proof.

3.1
Outline of the proof

The point of departure is Corollary
5
and we will derive a contradiction to its second property.
To this end, we consider an appropriate set
Z
as given by Corollary
5
and let

\Phi
denote the set of all colourings of
Z
without a monochromatic
k
-AP. The main obstacle
is to find a partition of
\Phi
into
i_{0}=2^{o(pn)}

classes
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{i_{0}}
, such that any two colourings
\varphi
,
\varphi^{\prime}
from any partition class
\Phi_{i}
agree on a relatively dense subset
C_{i}
of
Z
, i.e.
\varphi(z)=\varphi^{\prime}(z)
for all
z\in C_{i}
.
Let
B_{i}
denote the larger monochromatic subset of
C_{i}
, say of colour blue.
We consider the the set
F(B_{i})
of those elements in
\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z}
, which extend
a blue
(k-1)
-AP in
B_{i}
to an
k
-AP.
Note that Corollary
5
allows us to impose
further conditions on
Z
as long as
\mathds{Z}_{n,p}
satisfies them almost surely.
One of these properties will assert that
F(B_{i})
is of size linear in
n

Consequently, by a quantitative version of
Szemerédi’s theorem we know that the number of
k
-APs in the focus of
B_{i}

is
\Omega(n^{2})
.
Consider
U_{i}=(\mathds{Z}/n\mathds{Z})_{\varepsilon p}\cap F(B_{i})
and note that if any element of
U_{i}
is coloured blue then this induces a blue
k
-AP with
Z
under any colouring

\varphi\in\Phi_{i}
.
Hence, to extend any
\varphi\in\Phi_{i}
to a colouring
of
Z\cup U_{i}
without a monochromatic
k
-AP it is necessary that all elements in
U_{i}
are coloured red. Consequently, the probability of
a successful extension of any colouring in
\Phi_{i}
is bounded from above by the probability that
U_{i}
does not contain a
k
-AP. This, however, is at most

\exp(-\Omega(p^{k}n^{2}))=\exp(-\Omega(pn))
by Janson’s inequality.

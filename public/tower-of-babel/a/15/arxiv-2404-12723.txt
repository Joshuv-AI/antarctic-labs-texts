Virasoro constraints for K3 surfaces and monodromy operators
Weisheng Wang

Abstract
. The Virasoro constraints for moduli spaces of stable torsion free sheaves on a surface with only ( p , p ) (p,p) -cohomology were recently proved by Bojko-Moreira-Lim. The rank 1 case, which is not restricted to surfaces with only ( p , p ) (p,p) -cohomology, was established by Moreira. We prove Virasoro constraints for K3 surfaces using Markman monodromy operators, which allow us to reduce to the rank 1 case. We also prove new Virasoro constraints in rank 0. Finally, for K3 surfaces, we introduce new Virasoro operators in negative degree which, together with the previous Virasoro operators, give a representation of Virasoro algebra with central charge 24 24 .

1.1.
Virasoro constraint in Gromov-Witten theory

Gromov-Witten theory is defined by integration over the moduli space of stable maps. Let
X
be a non-singular projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
. A map from a connected pointed nodal curve to

[

]

.
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)
denotes the moduli space of stable maps from

There are

\operatorname{ev}_{i}(\Sigma,p_{1},\dots,p_{n},f)=f(p_{i})\quad(1\leq i\leq n).

At each point

Let

(1)

\displaystyle\langle\tau_{k_{1}}(\gamma_{a_{1}})\dots\tau_{k_{n}}(\gamma_{a_{n}})\rangle_{g,\beta}^{X}=\int_{\left[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(X,\beta)\right]^{\text{vir}}}\psi_{1}^{k_{1}}\operatorname{ev}_{1}^{\ast}(\gamma_{a_{1}})\dots\psi_{n}^{k_{n}}\operatorname{ev}_{n}^{\ast}(\gamma_{a_{n}}).

Let
\{t^{a}_{k}\}
be a set of variables, and define the generating function

as:

F^{X}=\sum_{g\geq 0}\lambda^{2g-2}\sum_{\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})}q^{\beta}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\dots a_{n}\\
k_{1}\dots k_{n}\end{subarray}}t^{a_{n}}_{k_{n}}\dots t^{a_{1}}_{k_{1}}\langle\tau_{k_{1}}(\gamma_{a_{1}})\dots\tau_{k_{n}}(\gamma_{a_{n}})\rangle^{X}_{g,\beta}.

Also define the full partition function

is the partition function corresponds to the standard disconnected Gromov-Witten bracket
\langle,\rangle^{X,\bullet}_{g,\beta}
, where the domain nodal curve could be disconnected:

(2)

\displaystyle Z^{X}=\sum_{g\geq 0}\lambda^{2g-2}\sum_{\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})}q^{\beta}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a_{1}\dots a_{n}\\
k_{1}\dots k_{n}\end{subarray}}t^{a_{n}}_{k_{n}}\dots t^{a_{1}}_{k_{1}}\langle\tau_{k_{1}}(\gamma_{a_{1}})\dots\tau_{k_{n}}(\gamma_{a_{n}})\rangle^{X,\bullet}_{g,\beta}.

A set of formal differential operators

are defined in
[
9
]

and only depend upon the intersection pairing

, with
\gamma_{a},\gamma_{b}\in H^{\ast}(X,\mathbb{C})
, the Hodge decomposition

The Virasoro conjecture in Gromov-Witten theory states as follows:

Conjecture 1.1

For all non-singular projective varieties

This conjecture has been proven for curves

of genus

1.2.
GW/Pairs correspondence for 3-folds and Virasoro constraints for stable pairs

The moduli space of stable maps

Definition 1.2

A stable pair

Given a stable pair

has no embedded points; the support of the cokernel of

0

C

\chi(F)=n\in\mathbb{Z}\quad\text{and}\quad[C]=\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z}).

For a fixed

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}_{k}(\gamma)=\pi_{P\ast}\left(\operatorname{ch}_{k}(\mathbb{F}-\mathcal{O}_{X\times P_{n}(X,\beta)})\cdot\pi_{X}^{\ast}\gamma\right).

The invariant in the stable pair theory has the following form:

\displaystyle\langle\operatorname{ch}_{k_{1}}(\gamma_{1})\dots\operatorname{ch}_{k_{m}}(\gamma_{m})\rangle^{X,\text{PT}}_{n,\beta}:=\int_{\left[P_{n}(X,\beta)\right]^{\text{vir}}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\operatorname{ch}_{k_{i}}(\gamma_{i}).

The moduli of stable maps and stable pair are both based upon the geometry of curves in

Let

\displaystyle\gamma(g)(x)=\pi_{2\ast}\left(c_{\operatorname{dim}(M)}\big[-\pi_{13\ast}\left(\pi_{12}^{\ast}\left((1\otimes g)u_{v_{1}}\right)^{\vee}\cdot\pi_{23}^{\ast}u_{v_{2}}\right)\big]\cdot\pi_{1}^{\ast}x\right),

Theorem 3.1

(Markman)

Let

\mathrm{(i)}

\gamma(g)

\langle x,y\rangle=\int_{M}xy

\mathrm{(ii)}

(\gamma(g)\otimes g)(u_{v_{1}})=u_{v_{2}}

\mathrm{(iii)}

\mathrm{(iv)}

\gamma(g)\left(c_{k}(T_{M_{1}})\right)=c_{k}(T_{M_{2}})

\int_{M_{1}}\sigma=\int_{M_{2}}\gamma(g)(\sigma)\quad\forall\sigma\in H^{\ast}(M_{1},\mathbb{Q}).

(Lemma 2.7 of
[

\mathrm{(ii)}

Therefore, we have the following commutative diagram.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{\ast}(S_{1},\mathbb{Q})}

Lemma 3.3

(Lemma 2.13 of

\mathrm{(ii)}

\mathrm{(iii)}

\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle=\langle w_{i},w_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle\int_{M(v)}P(B_{i}(x_{j}),c_{l}(T_{M(v)}))

\displaystyle=\int_{M(v^{\prime})}\gamma(g)P(B_{i}(x_{j}),c_{l}(T_{M(v^{\prime})}))

\displaystyle=\int_{M(v^{\prime})}P(B_{i}(gx_{j}),c_{l}(T_{M(v^{\prime})}))

\displaystyle=\int_{M(v^{\prime})}P(B_{i}(x_{j}^{\prime}),c_{l}(T_{M(v^{\prime})})),

where the first equality is by the Theorem
3.1

(Oberdieck
[
8
]

,

\begin{pmatrix}(v,v)&(v,x_{i})_{i=1}^{k}\\

\int_{M}P(B_{i}(x_{j}),c_{l}(T_{M}))=I(P,A).

\displaystyle\sum_{a+b=k}2a!b!r^{2}

Therefore (

Now, let us look at the

The classes in

are
\mathbbm{1}\in H^{0,0}(S)
and
\sigma\in H^{0,2}(S)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{r}(k+1)!R_{-1}[\sigma]\left(\operatorname{ch}_{k+1}^{\mathrm{H}}(\overline{\sigma})D\right),

where

or a linear combination of those. Because we assumed that

Therefore we find:

\displaystyle\int_{M(v)}S_{k}D=r^{N_{p}}\int_{S^{[N]}}S_{k}D.

Because the Virasoro constraints on

2.2

\operatorname{dim}M>2

4.2.
\operatorname{dim}M=2
case

As before, let
S
be a non-singular projective K3 surface, let
v
be a Mukai vector with
\operatorname{rk}(v)>0
.
In this case, I will recall some facts following Section 2.4 of
[
8
]
. Let
M:=M_{H}(v)
be a 2-dimensional moduli space of stable sheaves, hence it is a K3 surface. There is the following well known isometry of Mukai lattices:

(27)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}:H^{\ast}(S,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{\ast}(M,\mathbb{Q}),\quad\gamma\mapsto\pi_{M\ast}(e^{-c_{1}(\mathcal{F})/rk(v)}v(F)\pi_{S}^{\ast}(\gamma)),

where
v(\mathcal{F}):=\operatorname{ch}(\mathcal{F})\sqrt{\operatorname{td}_{M\times S}}.
The fact that
\widetilde{\Phi}
is a Hodge isometry implies:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Phi}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})=\mathrm{rk}(v)\mathbbm{1},\quad\widetilde{\Phi}(L)=\phi(L),\quad\widetilde{\Phi}(\mathbbm{1})=\frac{1}{\mathrm{rk}(v)}\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}},

where
\phi:H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Q})\to H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Q})
is a Hodge isometry, which means
\phi
is an isometry with respect to the Mukai paring of
S
and
M
, since
M
is also a K3 surface.

One has the following identities by direct calculation:

(28)

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}_{i}^{\mathrm{H}}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\bigg[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\operatorname{td}_{M}}}\widetilde{\Phi}\left(\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\operatorname{td}_{S}}}\right)\bigg]_{2i-p+q}\text{for }\gamma\in H^{p,q}(S).

(29)

\displaystyle\text{ch}_{i}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\bigg[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\operatorname{td}_{M}}}\widetilde{\Phi}\left(\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{\operatorname{td}_{S}}}\right)\bigg]_{2\operatorname{deg}(\gamma)+2i-4}\text{for }\gamma\in H^{\ast}(S,\mathbb{Q}).

Here the new symbole
\operatorname{ch}_{k}(\gamma)
has its geometric realization as:

\operatorname{ch}_{k}(\gamma)=\pi_{M\ast}\left(\operatorname{ch}_{k}(\mathcal{F}\otimes(\operatorname{det}\mathcal{F})^{-1/r})\cdot\pi_{S}^{\ast}\gamma\right).

For
\gamma\in H^{p,q}(S)
one has:

(30)

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{i}(\gamma)=\operatorname{ch}_{i+1+\frac{q-p}{2}}(\gamma).

Similarly as before:

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}_{i}^{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbbm{1}+\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})=\bigg[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\operatorname{td}_{M}}}\widetilde{\Phi}\left(\frac{\mathbbm{1}+\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}}}{\sqrt{\operatorname{td}_{S}}}\right)\bigg]_{2i}=\operatorname{ch}_{i}^{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbbm{1})+\operatorname{ch}_{i}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}}).

Because
\text{dim }M=2
, one can explicitly calculate the cohomology class of
\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{i}(\gamma)
. The non zero ones among them are:

(31)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\operatorname{ch}_{0}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})&=\text{rk}(v)\mathbbm{1},\quad\\
\operatorname{ch}_{2}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})&=-\text{rk}(v)\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}},\\
\operatorname{ch}_{2}(\gamma)&=\phi(\gamma),\quad\text{where }\gamma\in H^{2}(S)\\
\operatorname{ch}_{2}^{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbbm{1})&=-\text{rk}(v)\mathbbm{1}\\
\operatorname{ch}_{4}^{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbbm{1})&=\left(\frac{1}{\text{rk}(v)}+\text{rk}(v)\right)\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}}\\
\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{2}(\mathbbm{1}+\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})&=\frac{1}{\text{rk}(v)}\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}}.\end{split}

In fact, the geometric realization of
\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})

\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{i}(\mathbbm{1})
are the same as
\operatorname{ch}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})
,
\operatorname{ch}_{i}(\mathbbm{1})
.
For
\gamma\in H^{2}(S,\mathbb{C})
, using
\operatorname{ch}_{2}(\gamma)
will make notation simpler since only
\operatorname{ch}_{2}(\gamma)\neq 0
whereas there are potentially several

such that
\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{i}(\gamma)\neq 0
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
S
be a non-singular and projective K3 surface, and let
v,M_{H}(v)
be defined as above. Suppose
M_{H}(v)
is 2 dimensional, and suppose
r:=\operatorname{rk}(v)>0
. Then

\int_{M}P\left(\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}}),\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{j}(\gamma_{l}),\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{k}(\mathbbm{1}+\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})\right)=\int_{S^{[1]}}P\left(r\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{i}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}}),\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{j}(\gamma_{l}),\frac{1}{r}\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{k}(\mathbbm{1}+\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})\right),

where
P
is a polynomial and
\gamma_{l}\in H^{2}(S,\mathbb{Q})
.

One can use (
30
) to rewrite
\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{i}(\gamma)
as
\operatorname{ch}_{2}(\gamma)
.
The only monomials whose integral on
M
are non zero have one of the following three forms (up to multiplication by a constant):

(32)

\displaystyle\big[\operatorname{ch}_{0}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})\big]^{N}\operatorname{ch}_{2}(\gamma_{1})\operatorname{ch}_{2}(\gamma_{2})

(33)

\displaystyle\big[\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{0}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})\big]^{N}\operatorname{ch}_{2}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})

(34)

\displaystyle\big[\operatorname{ch}_{0}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})\big]^{N}\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{2}(\mathbbm{1}+\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}}),

where
N
is some non-negative integer and
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in H^{2}(S,\mathbb{C})
.
Their integral over
M
are:

(35)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\big[\operatorname{ch}_{0}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})\big]^{N}\operatorname{ch}_{2}(\gamma_{1})\operatorname{ch}_{2}(\gamma_{2})

\displaystyle=r^{N}\cdot(\phi(\gamma_{1}),\phi(\gamma_{2}))

(36)

\displaystyle=r^{N}\cdot(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})

(37)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\big[\operatorname{ch}_{0}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})\big]^{N}\operatorname{ch}_{2}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})

\displaystyle=-r^{N+1}

(38)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\big[\operatorname{ch}_{0}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})\big]^{N}\operatorname{ch}_{2}^{\mathrm{H}}(\mathbbm{1}+\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})

\displaystyle=r^{N-1}.

One can also evaluate integrals on

(39)

\displaystyle\int_{S^{[1]}}\big[r\operatorname{ch}_{0}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})\big]^{N}\operatorname{ch}_{2}(\gamma_{1})\operatorname{ch}_{2}(\gamma_{2})

\displaystyle=r^{N}\cdot(\phi(\gamma_{1}),\phi(\gamma_{2}))

(40)

\displaystyle=r^{N}\cdot(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})

(41)

\displaystyle\int_{S^{[1]}}\big[r\operatorname{ch}_{0}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})\big]^{N}\big[r\operatorname{ch}_{2}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})\big]

\displaystyle=-r^{N+1}

(42)

\displaystyle\int_{S^{[1]}}\big[r\operatorname{ch}_{0}^{\mathrm{H}}(\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})\big]^{N}\cdot\frac{1}{r}\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{2}(\mathbbm{1}+\boldsymbol{\operatorname{p}})

\displaystyle=r^{N-1}.

By comparing the two evaluations, lemma
4.2
is proven. Therefore one can use the same argument as in subsection

to show the Virasoro constraints are valid in case of

. Combined with the result of previous subsection, Theorem
2.2
is proven.

5.1.

By the remark 2.14 of

Let

(43)

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\delta}=\mathcal{F}\otimes e^{-\xi_{\mathcal{F}}(\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(\delta))/\int_{S}\delta\cdot\operatorname{ch}(\alpha)}.

Also by the same remark,
\xi_{\mathbb{G}_{\delta}}=\xi_{\mathbb{G}}\circ\eta

\eta=\sum_{j\geq 0}\left(-\frac{\operatorname{ch}^{\mathrm{H}}_{1}(\delta)}{\int_{S}\delta\cdot\operatorname{ch}(\alpha)}\right)^{j}R^{j}_{-1}.

Therefore, the geometric realization with respect to
\delta
-normalized sheaf is still valid even such a sheaf does not exist in usual sense. By direct calculation one can check that

for any line bundle
\mathcal{L}
on

. Since we want that the geometric realisation of any

is independent of choice of the universal sheaf,

-normalised universal sheaf will be a good choice to formulate the Virasoro constraint. For rank zero sheaves,

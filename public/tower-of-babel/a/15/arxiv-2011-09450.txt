Another proof of BEC in the GP-limit
Christian Hainzl

Abstract
. We present a fresh look at the methods introduced by Boccato, Brennecke, Cenatiempo, and Schlein concerning the trapped Bose gas and give a conceptually very simple and concise proof of BEC in the Gross-Pitaevskii limit for small interaction potentials.



1.
Introduction

One of the major achievements in mathematical quantum mechanics within the last 25 years was the proof of Bose-Einstein condensation (BEC) for trapped
Bose gases by Lieb and Seiringer in 2002
[
13
]
, see also
[
14
,
16
]
. Their work was based on preceding works of Dyson
[
9
]
and Lieb and Yngvason
[
17
]
on the ground state energy of dilute Bose gases. Since then several different proofs of BEC in the Gross-Pitaevskii regime, and beyond, have been carried out. Nam, Rougerie and Seiringer
[
19
]
gave a proof using the quantum de Finetti theorem. Nam, Napiorkowski, Ricaud and Triaud
[
18
]
used ideas of
[
7
,
8
]
, while more recently Fournais
[
10
]
, by means of techniques developed in his joint work with Solovej
[
11
]
on the Lee-Huang-Yang conjecture, gave a relatively short proof, valid well beyond the GP-regime. While the above approaches are based on localization techniques in configuration space,
Boccato, Brennecke, Cenatiempo and Schlein (BBCS), prior to
[
18
,
10
,
7
,
11
]
, developed a different approach, more in the spirit of Bogolubov’s original work. They use unitary rotations to encode the expected ground state. On the one hand they achieved optimal error bounds in their proof of BEC
[
2
,
4
]
, on the other hand this approach culminated in the rigorous establishment of Bogolubov theory on a periodic box
[
3
]
.
Although their works are voluminous, the methods are conceptually quite accessible and rather straight forward.

The aim of the present work is to take a fresh look at the approach of BBCS, with the obvious difference
that we treat the system in a grand-canonical way, inspired by Brietzke and Solovej
[
7
]
.
This allows us to conceptually simplify the approach of BBCS and additionally streamline the error estimates.
A further advantage of this approach is that the emergence of the scattering length in the final result comes out automatically and does
not have to be put in from the beginning. Thanks to the smallness assumption on the interaction potential we achieve to present a concise proof of BEC in the GP-limit with optimal error bounds.
It should also be remembered that another advantage of the use of unitary rotations is the fact that one simultaneously produces precise upper and lower bounds.
On the downside it is fair to mention that one needs regularity assumptions on the interaction
V
, excluding the hard-core potential which is included in the results
[
13
,
19
,
10
,
18
]
.
The smallness assumption of the interaction potential simplifies our approach significantly. In
[
1
]
Adhikari, Brennecke and Schlein managed to overcome the smallness condition by an independent argument, without relying on previous results
[
13
,
19
]
, using additional cubic and quartic transformations, which makes the proof technically even harder than
[
2
,
4
]
. This suggests that getting rid of this smallness condition, however, seems to be a major task within this approach.

We consider a system of bosons in the Gross-Pitaevskii regime by means of the
grand canonical Hamiltonian

H_{\mu}=H_{N}-\mu\mathcal{N},

with
H_{N}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}H_{N}^{n}
, where the
n
-particle Hamiltonian is given by

H_{N}^{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}-\Delta_{i}+\kappa\sum_{i<j}^{n}V_{N}(x_{i}-x_{j}).

H_{\mu}
is
acting on the bosonic Fock space

\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{H})=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{H}^{\otimes_{s}n}

with
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\Lambda)
, with
\Lambda=[-1/2,1/2]^{3}
.
Further the GP-regime is reflected by the scaling of the potential

V_{N}(x):=N^{2}V(Nx),

where
V(x)
is assumed to be positive and compactly supported and
\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. We impose periodic boundary conditions on the
box
\Lambda=[-1/2,1/2]^{3}
. In that sense the Hamiltoinan
H_{\mu}
should actually contain the periodized potential. However, we will work mainly with the variant in momentum space, where the periodization is automatic.

Notice that
N
in the Hamiltonian
H_{\mu}
acts as a parameter. However, we choose the chemical potential so that the expected number of particles in the ground state, to the leading order, is
N
. More precisely, we follow
[
7
]
and choose the chemical potential as
\mu=8\pi a
, where
a
is the scattering length of
\kappa V
.

In contrast to previous works we define the scattering length via its Born series, in the form

(1)

4\pi a:=\frac{\kappa}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}V(x)dx-\left\langle\frac{\kappa}{2}V,\frac{1}{-\Delta+\frac{\kappa}{2}V}\frac{\kappa}{2}V\right\rangle,

for the simple reason that this is exactly the way how the scattering length appears in our approach.
In a more concise way, see
[
12
]
, the scattering length (
1
) can also be expressed as

4\pi a=\left\langle\sqrt{v},\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{v}\frac{1}{-\Delta}\sqrt{v}}\sqrt{v}\right\rangle,

with
v=\frac{\kappa}{2}V
.

2.
Main results

For convenience we rewrite the Hamiltonian
H_{\mu}
in momentum space via

H_{\mu}=\sum_{p}(p^{2}-\mu)a_{p}^{\dagger}a_{p}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\sum_{r,p,q}\hat{V}_{N}(r)a_{p+r}^{\dagger}a_{q}^{\dagger}a_{p}a_{q+r},

with
p\in\Lambda^{\ast}:=2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, and
a^{\dagger}_{p}:=a^{\dagger}(\phi_{p})
,
a_{p}:=a(\phi_{p})

the usual creation and annihilation operators on the Fock space over the periodic box, i.e.,
\phi_{p}(x)=e^{ix\cdot p}
.
Notice,

\hat{V}_{N}(r)=\frac{1}{N}\hat{V}(r/N).

Let

E_{\mu}(N):=\inf\{\langle\psi,H_{\mu}\psi\rangle|\psi\in\mathcal{F},\|\psi\|=1\}

be the ground stat energy on the Fock space. The first theorem concerns the grand-canonical ground state energy.
The statement resembles well known results in the literature, e.g.
[
15
,
2
,
3
]
.
Its proof forms the basis for the subsequent establishment of BEC.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mu=8\pi a
. Then for
\kappa
small enough

E_{\mu}(N)=-4\pi aN+O(1),

asymptotically as
N
tends to infinity.

In the proof of Theorem
1
we use the fact, that the number operator
\mathcal{N}
commutates with the Hamiltonian, i.e.
[H_{\mu},\mathcal{N}]=0
, which allows us to restrict the determination of the ground state energy as well as the proof of BEC to eigenfunctions
\psi
of the number operator,
\mathcal{N}\psi=n\psi
.

Remark 2
.

As a consequence of the proof of Theorem
1
we see that any approximate ground state
\psi_{n}
, with fixed particle number
n
,
\mathcal{N}\psi_{n}=n\psi_{n}
,
whose energy is
O(1)
away from
E_{\mu}(N)
, i.e.,
\langle\psi_{n},H_{\mu}\psi_{n}\rangle\leq-4\pi aN+O(1),
satisfies

|n/N-1|\leq O(1/\sqrt{N}),
meaning that a-priori
n
equals the external parameter
N
only up to an error of
\sqrt{N}
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mu=8\pi a
, and let
\psi\in\mathcal{F}
be normalized, with
\mathcal{N}\psi=n\psi
and satisfy

\langle\psi,H_{\mu}\psi\rangle\leq-4\pi aN+O(1).

Then, for
\kappa
small enough,

(2)

\langle\psi,\mathcal{N}_{+}\psi\rangle\leq O(1).

Since,

\langle\phi_{0},\gamma_{\psi}\phi_{0}\rangle=\langle\psi,a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{0}\psi\rangle=\langle\psi,(\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{N}_{+})\psi\rangle=n-\langle\psi,\mathcal{N}_{+}\psi\rangle=n+O(1),

this implies complete BEC.

More precisely, the highest eigenvalue of the one particle density matrix
\gamma_{\psi}
of any approximate ground state
\psi
, with
\mathcal{N}\psi=n\psi

is macroscopically occupied,
implying BEC with optimal error bounds in terms of
n
. Let us recall that in terms of creation and annihilation operators the one particle density matrix
\gamma_{\psi}
can be expressed via the matrix elements
\langle\psi,a^{\dagger}_{p}a_{q}\psi\rangle
, with
p,q\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}^{3}
, i.e.,
\hat{\gamma}_{\psi}(p,q)=\langle\psi,a^{\dagger}_{p}a_{q}\psi\rangle.

Remark 4
.

As easy consequence of the proof of Theorem
1
we will see that one can find a state
\Psi_{N}
, with
\mathcal{N}\psi_{N}=N\psi_{N}
, and

\langle\psi_{N},H_{\mu}\psi_{N}\rangle\leq-4\pi aN+O(1),

such that the corresponding one-particle density matrix
\gamma_{\psi_{N}}
satisfies

\langle\phi_{0},\gamma_{\psi_{N}}\phi_{0}\rangle=N+O(1),

with optimal rate in the parameter
N
.

As corollary of Theorem
1
and Remark
4
we immediately obtain the energy asymptotic of the ground state energy of an
N
-particle system.
Recall,

H_{N}^{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}-\Delta_{i}+\kappa\sum_{i<j}^{N}V_{N}(x_{i}-x_{j}).

Corollary 5
.

Let
E_{N}=\inf{\rm{spec}}\,H^{N}_{N}
, then

E_{N}=4\pi aN+O(1).

Proof.

The upper bound is provided in the proof of Theorem
1
, cf. Remark
4
, by an explicit trial state with particle number
N
.
The lower bound follows from a simple variational argument
[
7
]
. Let
\psi_{N}
be the ground state of
H_{N}^{N}
. Then, (
\mu=8\pi a
),

E_{N}=\langle\psi_{N},H_{N}^{N}\psi_{N}\rangle=\langle\psi_{N},H_{N}\psi_{N}\rangle=8\pi aN+\langle\psi_{N},H_{\mu}\psi_{N}\rangle\geq 8\pi aN+E_{\mu}\geq 4\pi aN+O(1),

using Theorem
1
.
∎

Remark 6
.

The proof of Theorem
1
can easily be extended to general values of the chemical potential
\mu>0
.
Indeed, for any
\mu>0
and
\kappa
small enough one gets

E_{\mu}(N)=-\frac{\mu^{2}}{16\pi a}N+O(1),

as
N
tends to infinity.
For approximate ground states
\psi\in\mathcal{F}
, with
\mathcal{N}\psi=n\psi
, and

\langle\psi,H_{\mu}\psi\rangle\leq-\frac{\mu^{2}}{16\pi a}N+O(1)

one obtains again complete condensation
\langle\psi,\mathcal{N}_{+}\psi\rangle\leq O(1)
, however, the expectation number of particles is now

n=\frac{N\mu}{8\pi a}+O(\sqrt{N})
.

In the following section
3
we present the main steps of the proof of Theorem
1
and complete the proof of BEC in section
4
.
The rest of the paper is concerned with technical estimates which are not important for the understanding of the main ideas of the proof.

3.
Strategy and main steps of the proofs

Notice that the Hamiltonian
H_{\mu}
and the number operator
\mathcal{N}
commute, which tells us that we can restrict to states with
fixed quantum number
\mathcal{N}\psi=n\psi
. Following ideas from Brietzke and Solovej
[
7
]
we can restrict our attention to the case where

\mathcal{N}\leq 10N
, with
10
being chosen for aesthetic reasons (anything larger than
4
would do).

The key observation from
[
7
]
is that whenever there are more than
10N
particles one can combine them in groups with each group consisting of a number of particles
between
5N
and
10N
. Since the interaction is positive, one can simply drop the interaction between different groups for a lower bound. Since we will further show that the energy of a system with more than
5N
particles is actually nonnegative, this tells us
that we can restrict from the very beginning to
\mathcal{N}\leq 10N
. Notice that in the grand canonical case with positive chemical potential it is easy to see that the ground state must necessarily be negative.

Under the assumption of
\mathcal{N}\leq 10N
we are now in the position to apply the strategy developed by Boccato, Brennecke, Cenatiempo, and Schlein
[
2
,
4
,
3
]
, based on ideas of
[
6
]
.

We will look for an appropriate unitary rotation
e^{{\mathcal{B}}}
, with
{\mathcal{B}}=B-B^{*}
a number conserving operator on the Fock space, which encodes the ground state,
in the sense that

e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}H_{\mu}e^{{\mathcal{B}}}
has, to leading order,
\Pi_{i=1}^{N}\phi_{0}
as approximate ground state. This further implies that

\psi~\simeq e^{{\mathcal{B}}}\Pi_{i=1}^{N}\phi_{0}
is an approximate minimizer for
H_{\mu}
.

First we follow Bogolubov’s way and decompose the interaction potential in different terms depending on the number of
a_{0}
’s and
a^{\dagger}_{0}
’s:

(3)

\frac{\kappa}{2}\sum_{p,q,r}\hat{V}_{N}(r)a_{p+r}^{\dagger}a_{q}^{\dagger}a_{p}a_{q+r}=\frac{\kappa}{2}\hat{V}_{N}(0)a^{\dagger}_{0}a^{\dagger}_{0}a_{0}a_{0}+\kappa\sum_{p\neq 0}\hat{V}_{N}(0)a_{p}^{\dagger}a_{p}a^{\dagger}_{0}a_{0}\\
+\kappa\sum_{r\neq 0}\hat{V}_{N}(r)a_{r}^{\dagger}a_{r}a^{\dagger}_{0}a_{0}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\sum_{r\neq 0}\hat{V}_{N}(r)\left[a_{r}^{\dagger}a_{-r}^{\dagger}a_{0}a_{0}+a_{-r}a_{r}a^{\dagger}_{0}a^{\dagger}_{0}\right]+\\
\kappa\sum_{q,r,q+r\neq 0}\hat{V}_{N}(r)\left[a_{q+r}^{\dagger}a_{-r}^{\dagger}a_{q}a_{0}+a_{q}^{\dagger}a_{-r}a_{q+r}a_{0}^{\dagger}\right]+\frac{\kappa}{2}\sum_{p,q\neq 0,r\neq-p,r\neq-q}\hat{V}_{N}(r)a_{p+r}^{\dagger}a_{q}^{\dagger}a_{p}a_{q+r}

Let us denote the number operator counting the number of particles in the state
\phi_{0}
as

\mathcal{N}_{0}=a^{\dagger}_{0}a_{0}=\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{N}_{+}.

Since

a^{\dagger}_{0}a^{\dagger}_{0}a_{0}a_{0}=\mathcal{N}_{0}(\mathcal{N}_{0}-1)=(\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{N}_{+})(\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{N}_{+}-1)=\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{N}-1)-\mathcal{N}_{+}(2\mathcal{N}-1)+\mathcal{N}_{+}^{2},

we can
rewrite the Hamiltonian

H_{\mu}
in the form

H_{\mu}=H_{0}(\mu)+H_{1}+H_{2}+Q_{2}+Q_{3}+Q_{4},

where

H_{0}(\mu)=\frac{\kappa\hat{V}(0)}{2N}\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{N}-1)-\mu\mathcal{N},\qquad H_{1}=\sum_{p\neq 0}p^{2}a_{p}^{\dagger}a_{p},

H_{2}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{p\neq 0}\hat{V}(p/N)a^{\dagger}_{p}a_{p}(\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{N}_{+})-\frac{\kappa\hat{V}(0)}{2N}\mathcal{N}_{+}(\mathcal{N}_{+}-1)

The rest of the interaction then has the form

\displaystyle Q_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{p\neq 0}\hat{V}(p/N)[a_{p}^{\dagger}a_{-p}^{\dagger}a_{0}a_{0}+h.c.]

\displaystyle Q_{3}=

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{q,r,q+r\neq 0}\hat{V}(r/N)\left[a_{q+r}^{\dagger}a_{-r}^{\dagger}a_{q}a_{0}+h.c.\right]

\displaystyle Q_{4}=

\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{p,q\neq 0,r\neq-p,r\neq-q}\hat{V}(r/N)a_{p+r}^{\dagger}a_{q}^{\dagger}a_{p}a_{q+r}

In the following we will assume that
a_{p}
or
a^{\dagger}_{p}
automatically means that
p\neq 0
which allows us to skip
the distinctions in the sums.
E. g., instead of
\sum_{p\neq 0}a^{\dagger}_{p}a_{p}
we simply write
\sum_{p}a^{\dagger}_{p}a_{p}
.

Let us recall Duhamel’s formula

e^{-B}Ae^{B}=A+\int_{0}^{1}e^{-sB}[A,B]e^{sB}{\rm d}s=A+[A,B]+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-tB}[[A,B],B]e^{tB}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s.

Applying the second equality of the formula to
H_{1}+Q_{4}
and the first to
Q_{2}
we obtain

(4)

\displaystyle e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}H_{\mu}e^{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}[H_{0}(\mu)+H_{1}+Q_{4}+Q_{2}+H_{2}+Q_{3}]e^{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=H_{0}(\mu)+H_{1}+Q_{4}+[H_{1}+Q_{4},{\mathcal{B}}]+Q_{2}+e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}(H_{2}+Q_{3})e^{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}\Big[[H_{1}+Q_{4},{\mathcal{B}}],{\mathcal{B}}\Big]e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{1}e^{-s{\mathcal{B}}}[Q_{2},{\mathcal{B}}]e^{s{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}s.

Let us explain the main idea of the strategy. The term
H_{0}(\mu)
clearly contributes to the leading term. In Bogolubov’s original approach

Q_{3}
and
Q_{4}
was omitted and Bogolubov
[
5
]
diagonalized the quadratic part
H_{1}+H_{2}+Q_{2}
. But he did not get the leading term correctly, since he missed the contribution coming from
Q_{4}
. We perform an “almost” diagonalization
by choosing
{\mathcal{B}}
in such a way that

[H_{1}+Q_{4},{\mathcal{B}}]+Q_{2}\simeq 0.

We will treat
H_{2}
and
Q_{3}
as error terms, since they do not contribute to the leading order.
The requirement that
[H_{1}+Q_{4},{\mathcal{B}}]+Q_{2}
vanishes apart from higher order terms, suggests a choice of
{\mathcal{B}}
, of the form

(5)

{\mathcal{B}}=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{p}{\varphi}_{p}[a^{\dagger}_{p}a^{\dagger}_{-p}a_{0}a_{0}-\rm{h.c.}]=B-B^{*},

with
{\varphi}_{p}
appropriately chosen. In fact, to leading order,
{\varphi}_{p}
will satisfy the scattering equation.

Lemma 7
.

Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be defined as in (
5
).
If
{\varphi}_{p}
satisfies the equation

(6)

p^{2}{\varphi}_{p}+\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{q}\hat{V}\left((p-q)/N\right){\varphi}_{q}=-\frac{\kappa}{2}\hat{V}(p/N),

then

(7)

[H_{1}(\mu)+Q_{4},{\mathcal{B}}]=-Q_{2}+\Gamma,

with

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{N^{2}}\sum_{r,p,q}\hat{V}(r/N){\varphi}_{p}[a_{p+r}^{\dagger}a_{q}^{\dagger}a_{-p}^{\dagger}a_{q+r}a_{0}a_{0}+h.c.]

Proof.

Straightforward calculations yield

[H_{1},{\mathcal{B}}]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{p}p^{2}{\varphi}_{p}[a_{p}^{\dagger}a_{-p}^{\dagger}a_{0}a_{0}+h.c.]

and

(8)

[Q_{4},{\mathcal{B}}]=\frac{\kappa}{2N^{2}}\sum_{p,q}(\hat{V}((p-q)/N){\varphi}_{q}a_{p}^{\dagger}a_{-p}^{\dagger}a_{0}a_{0}\\
+\frac{\kappa}{2N^{2}}\sum_{r,p,q}\hat{V}(r/N)\left({\varphi}_{p}a_{p+r}^{\dagger}a_{q}^{\dagger}a_{-p}^{\dagger}a_{q+r}a_{0}a_{0}+{\varphi}_{q+r}a_{p+r}^{\dagger}a_{q}^{\dagger}a_{-q-r}^{\dagger}a_{p}a_{0}a_{0}\right)+h.c.,

where the two terms in the last line are actually equal, which can be seen by changing variables,
p\to p-r
,
q\to q-r
and then
r\to-r
.
Collecting all terms involving
a_{p}^{\dagger}a_{-p}^{\dagger}a_{0}a_{0}
and recalling the form of
Q_{2}

we see that

[H_{1}+Q_{4},{\mathcal{B}}]=-Q_{2}+\Gamma

is satisfied if

{\varphi}_{p}
solves the equation (
6
).
∎

Remark 8
.

Let us apply the
discrete inverse Fourier transform

(9)

P_{0}^{\perp}\check{\varphi}(x)=\frac{1}{N^{3}}\sum_{p}e^{{\rm i}\frac{p}{N}\cdot x}{\varphi}_{p},\quad P_{0}^{\perp}\check{\hat{V}}(x)=\frac{1}{N^{3}}\sum_{p}e^{{\rm i}\frac{p}{N}\cdot x}\hat{V}(p/N),

where
P_{0}
is the projection on the constant function
\phi_{0}
, and the orthogonal projection comes about because all sums run over
p\neq 0
.
Applying this transformation to
equation (
6
) and assume that
N
is large enough that
\check{\hat{V}}(x)=V(x)
on
[-N/2,N/2]
, then we obtain the equation

(10)

P_{0}^{\perp}(-\Delta+\frac{\kappa}{2}V(x))P_{0}^{\perp}\check{\varphi}(x)=-\frac{\kappa}{2N^{2}}P_{0}^{\perp}V(x),

which can be inverted by

(11)

P_{0}^{\perp}\check{\varphi}(x)=-\frac{\kappa}{2N^{2}}\frac{1}{P_{0}^{\perp}(-\Delta+\frac{\kappa}{2}V)P_{0}^{\perp}}P_{0}^{\perp}V(x).

Among others this shows that equation (
6
) has a unique solution
{\varphi}_{p}
. Useful properties of this function
{\varphi}_{p}
are provided
in Lemma
14
.

We continue with equation (
4
). We plug
[H_{1}+Q_{4},{\mathcal{B}}]=-Q_{2}+\Gamma
into the last two terms in (
4
) and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}\Big[[H_{1}+Q_{4},{\mathcal{B}}],{\mathcal{B}}\Big]e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{1}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}[Q_{2},{\mathcal{B}}]e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}[Q_{2},{\mathcal{B}}]e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}[Q_{2},{\mathcal{B}}]e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}[\Gamma,{\mathcal{B}}]e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}[Q_{2},{\mathcal{B}}]e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}[\Gamma,{\mathcal{B}}]e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s.

Rewriting (
4
) accordingly we arrive at

\displaystyle e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}H_{\mu}e^{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=H_{0}(\mu)+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}[Q_{2},{\mathcal{B}}]e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s+H_{1}+Q_{4}

(12)

\displaystyle+\Gamma+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}[\Gamma,B]e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s+e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}(H_{2}+Q_{3})e^{\mathcal{B}}.

The idea now is rather simple. The leading order is contained in the first two terms on the right hand side,

H_{0}(\mu)+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}[Q_{2},{\mathcal{B}}]e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s.

The positive term
H_{1}+Q_{4}
is used to dominate the error terms coming from the rest. Here the smallness of the potential
\kappa
will be used. Thanks to the gap,
all errors of the form
\kappa C\mathcal{N}_{+}
will be absorbed by
H_{1}
for small enough
\kappa
.
In the following we extract the leading contribution of the term
\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}[Q_{2},B]e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s.

Lemma 9
.

One has

(13)

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}[Q_{2},B]e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s=\frac{\kappa\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{N}-1)}{2N^{2}}\sum_{p}\hat{V}(p/N){\varphi}_{p}+\Xi,

with

(14)

\Xi=-\frac{\kappa}{N^{2}}\sum_{p}\hat{V}(p/N){\varphi}_{p}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}\mathcal{N}_{+}(2\mathcal{N}-\mathcal{N}_{+}-1)e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s\\
+\frac{2\kappa}{N^{2}}\sum_{p}\hat{V}(p/N){\varphi}_{p}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}\mathcal{N}_{0}(\mathcal{N}_{0}-1)a^{\dagger}_{p}a_{p}e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s\\
-\frac{\kappa}{N^{2}}\sum_{p,q}\hat{V}(p/N){\varphi}_{q}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}a^{\dagger}_{p}a^{\dagger}_{-p}a_{-q}a_{q}(1+2\mathcal{N}_{0})e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s.

Proof.

We calculate

\displaystyle[Q_{2},B]

\displaystyle-[a_{p}^{\dagger}a_{-p}^{\dagger}a_{0}a_{0},a_{-q}a_{q}a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{0}^{\dagger}]

\displaystyle=[a_{-q}a_{q},a_{p}^{\dagger}a_{-p}^{\dagger}]a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{0}a_{0}-a_{p}^{\dagger}a_{-p}^{\dagger}a_{-q}a_{q}[a_{0}a_{0},a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{0}^{\dagger}],

Plugging into (

Proof of BEC

Let us restrict to states
\psi\in\mathcal{F}
, with

Lemma 13

(
[

]
)

, as operator on

More general one has for

Proof.

Denote

Therefore, via explicit calculation,

\frac{d}{dt}F(t)=e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}[\mathcal{N}_{+},{\mathcal{B}}]e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}=\frac{1}{2N}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}\sum_{p,k}{\varphi}_{k}[a^{\dagger}_{p}a_{p},a^{\dagger}_{k}a^{\dagger}_{-k}a_{0}a_{0}-\rm{h.c.}]e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}\\

\leq\frac{1}{N}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}\sum_{k}(a^{\dagger}_{k}a_{0}a_{k}a_{0}^{\dagger}+{\varphi}_{k}^{2}a_{k}a^{\dagger}_{k}a_{0}^{\dagger}a_{0})e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}\\

where we used that
\mathcal{N}_{0}\leq\mathcal{N}\leq 10N
, and the fact that
{\varphi}_{k}
has bounded infinity- and

), (
29

With Groenwall’s Lemma we obtain (

With respect to (

Lemma 14
.

For small enough
\kappa

(28)

\displaystyle\|{\varphi}_{p}\|_{\infty}\lesssim\kappa

(29)

\displaystyle\|{\varphi}_{p}\|_{2}\lesssim\kappa

(30)

\displaystyle\|{\varphi}_{p}\|_{1}\lesssim\kappa N

(31)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{p}|\hat{V}(p/N){\varphi}_{p}|\lesssim\kappa

Proof.

The estimates (

To see this, recalling (

|\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{q}\hat{V}((p-q)/N){\varphi}_{q}|\leq\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{q}\frac{|\hat{V}((p-q)/N)|}{q^{2}}|q^{2}{\varphi}_{q}|\lesssim\kappa\sup_{q}|q^{2}{\varphi}_{q}|,

with

\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{q}\frac{|\hat{V}((p-q)/N)|}{q^{2}}=\frac{\kappa}{N^{3}}\sum_{q}\frac{|\hat{V}((p-q)/N)|}{(q/N)^{2}}\leq C\kappa,

which can be seen by treating the latter expression as the Riemann sum of the convolution
|\hat{V}|\ast 1/p^{2}
which is a uniformly bounded function.
Hence one can bound the absolute value of the left hand side of (

) from below by

\sup_{p}|p^{2}{\varphi}_{p}|(1-C\kappa)
which implies (

) using the boundedness of
\hat{V}
.
The inequality

immediately implies (

), (
28
), (

In order to see (

Plugging

which implies

(33)

with

G(p/N)\lesssim 1/(1+(p/N)^{2}).

Continuing the bootstrap argument allows to improve the fall off properties of

further, which however, is not necessary for our purpose.
The bound (

) by considering the sum as Riemann sum.
∎

Let us remark that we perform all our estimates on states

Remark 15
.

In the following it is important to absorb some error terms by the interaction term
Q_{4}
. To this aim let us recall first that
the bosonic Fock space can written as

\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{H})=\mathcal{F}(\{\phi_{0}\}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{\perp})=\mathcal{F}_{0}\otimes\mathcal{F}_{\perp}

where
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is the Fock space spanned by the one dimensional space
\{\phi_{0}\}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\perp}=\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{H}_{\perp})
, the Fock space built by all states orthogonal to
\phi_{0}
. In order to see the positivity of
Q_{4}
one has to rewrite the term in configuration space. With

\check{a}_{x}=\sum_{p}a_{p}e^{ip\cdot x},\quad a_{p}=\int_{\Lambda}\check{a}_{x}e^{-ix\cdot p},

one can check that for states
\eta\in\mathcal{F}_{\perp}

\langle\eta,Q_{4}\eta\rangle=\langle\eta,\iint dxdy\kappa V_{N}(x-y){\check{a}}^{\dagger}_{x}{\check{a}}^{\dagger}_{y}{\check{a}}_{y}{\check{a}}_{x}\eta\rangle=\iint dxdy\kappa V_{N}(x-y)\|{\check{a}}_{y}{\check{a}}_{x}\eta\|^{2},

where the last term is fundamentally positive for any positive interaction. For that reason it turns out to be convenient to estimate some of the error terms in configuration space.
Hence, whenever we use the interaction
\iint dxdy\kappa V_{N}(x-y){\check{a}}^{\dagger}_{x}{\check{a}}^{\dagger}_{y}{\check{a}}_{y}{\check{a}}_{x}
it has to be remembered that it
only
acts on

\mathcal{F}_{\perp}
. For sake of convenience we omit the corresponding symbols indicating the restrictions on
\mathcal{F}_{\perp}
.

Lets come back to the term
\Gamma

Denoting

\displaystyle\sum\hat{V}_{N}(r){\varphi}_{p}a_{p+r}^{\dagger}a_{q}^{\dagger}a_{-p}^{\dagger}a_{q+r}\,a_{0}a_{0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{r,p,q}\hat{V}_{N}(r){\varphi}_{p}\int{\check{a}}_{x}^{\dagger}e^{ix(p+r)}dx\int_{\Lambda}{\check{a}}_{y}^{\dagger}e^{iyq}dy\int_{\Lambda}{\check{a}}_{z}^{\dagger}e^{-izp}dz\int_{\Lambda}{\check{a}}_{w}e^{-iw(q+r)}{d}wa_{0}a_{0}

\kappa\sum_{r,p}\hat{V}_{N}(r){\varphi}_{p}{\varphi}_{p+r}a_{p}^{\dagger}a_{p}\lesssim\kappa^{3}\mathcal{N}_{+}.

The analogue estimates hold after performing the integrals
\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}...e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s
using Lemma
13
.

Concerning the quartic terms in (
39
), the term where both functions
{\varphi}_{\ast}
have the same index cannot be estimated solely by
\mathcal{N}_{+}
either, but also needs the interaction
Q_{4}
.
The other terms, however, can simply be bounded by
\mathcal{N}_{+}
. Thanks to Lemma
13
the application of
e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}...e^{{\mathcal{B}}}
lets the bounds unchanged.
Let us demonstrate such an estimate on the term including
{\varphi}_{p}{\varphi}_{q}
. Using Cauchy-Schwarz in
p,q,r
we obtain

(41)

\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{p,q,r}\hat{V}(r/N){\varphi}_{p}{\varphi}_{q}\langle\xi,a_{p+r}^{\dagger}a_{-p}^{\dagger}a_{-q}a_{q+r}\xi\rangle\\
\lesssim\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{p,q,r}|{\varphi}_{q}|\|a_{-p}a_{p+r}\xi\|\|a_{-q}a_{q+r}\xi\||{\varphi}_{p}|\lesssim\kappa^{3}\frac{\|\mathcal{N}_{+}\xi\|^{2}}{N}\lesssim\kappa^{3}\|\mathcal{N}_{+}^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}.

5.1.
Remaining terms

Finally we collect the remaining terms

(42)

e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}Q_{3}e^{{\mathcal{B}}}-\frac{\kappa}{N^{2}}\sum_{r,p,q}\hat{V}(r/N){\varphi}_{p}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{s}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}a_{p+r}^{\dagger}a_{q}^{\dagger}a_{p}a_{q+r}\mathcal{N}_{0}(\mathcal{N}_{0}+1)e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s\\
-\frac{\kappa}{N^{3}}\sum_{p,q,r}\hat{V}(r/N){\varphi}_{q+r}{\varphi}_{p}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}a_{p+r}^{\dagger}a_{q}^{\dagger}a_{-p}^{\dagger}a_{-q-r}^{\dagger}a_{0}a_{0}a_{0}a_{0}e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s+h.c.\\
-\frac{\kappa}{2N^{2}}\sum_{p,q}\hat{V}(p/N){\varphi}_{q}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}a^{\dagger}_{p}a^{\dagger}_{-p}a_{-q}a_{q}(1+2\mathcal{N}_{0})e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s\\
+\frac{\kappa}{N^{2}}\sum_{p,q,r,k}\hat{V}_{N}(r){\varphi}_{p}{\varphi}_{k}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}a_{p+r}^{\dagger}a_{q}^{\dagger}a_{-p}^{\dagger}a_{q+r}a_{-k}a_{k}(\mathcal{N}_{0}+1)e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s,

whose estimates are more elaborate.
Brennecke and Schlein realized in
[
6
]
that these terms can be expressed via a convergent geometric sum where the bounds of each terms can be classified in a straight forward way. Thereby the convergence is guaranteed by the smallness of
\kappa
.
In the following we present an alternative way of estimating these terms.
The method is essentially the same for all terms in (
42
). We will demonstrate the method on the first and the last two terms. The others work analogously.
We start with the term
e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}Q_{3}e^{{\mathcal{B}}}
. To this aim we rewrite it as

(43)

e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}Q_{3}e^{{\mathcal{B}}}=\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{q,r,q+r}\hat{V}(r/N)\left[e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}a_{q+r}^{\dagger}a_{-r}^{\dagger}e^{{\mathcal{B}}}e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}a_{q}a_{0}e^{{\mathcal{B}}}+h.c.\right].

Via Duhamel’s formula we have

(44)

e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}a_{q+r}^{\dagger}a_{-r}^{\dagger}e^{{\mathcal{B}}}=a^{\dagger}_{q+r}a^{\dagger}_{-r}+\int_{0}^{1}e^{-s{\mathcal{B}}}[a^{\dagger}_{q+r}a^{\dagger}_{-r},{\mathcal{B}}]e^{s{\mathcal{B}}}ds.

The idea behind this is the simple fact that the corresponding term in (
43
) involving
a^{\dagger}_{q+r}a^{\dagger}_{-r}
will be estimated by
Q_{4}
.
The remaining terms, however, coming from
[a^{\dagger}_{q+r}a^{\dagger}_{-r},{\mathcal{B}}]
can be bounded by
\mathcal{N}_{+}
, which is stable under application of
e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}..e^{{\mathcal{B}}}
.
In order to recover
Q_{4}
the term

\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{q,r}\hat{V}(r/N)a_{q+r}^{\dagger}a_{-r}^{\dagger}e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}a_{q}a_{0}e^{{\mathcal{B}}}

has to be estimated in configuration space, where the term reads

\kappa\int_{\Lambda^{2}}dxdyV_{N}(x-y){\check{a}}^{\dagger}_{x}{\check{a}}_{y}^{\dagger}e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}{\check{a}}_{x}a_{0}e^{{\mathcal{B}}},

whose expectation value of
\xi
is bounded by

(45)

\left(\int_{\Lambda^{2}}dxdy\kappa V_{N}(x-y)\|{\check{a}}_{y}{\check{a}}_{x}\xi\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\Lambda^{2}}dxdy\kappa V_{N}(x-y)\|e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}{\check{a}}_{x}a_{0}e^{{\mathcal{B}}}\xi\|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\\
\lesssim\delta\langle\xi,Q_{4}\xi\rangle+\kappa\langle\xi,(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)\xi\rangle.

The remaining term has the form

\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{q,r}\hat{V}(r/N)\int_{0}^{1}e^{-s{\mathcal{B}}}[a_{q+r}^{\dagger}a_{-r}^{\dagger},{\mathcal{B}}]e^{s{\mathcal{B}}}e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}a_{q}a_{0}e^{{\mathcal{B}}}ds.

Since

[a_{q+r}^{\dagger}a_{-r}^{\dagger},{\mathcal{B}}]=-\frac{2}{N}\left({\varphi}_{r}a^{\dagger}_{q+r}a_{r}+{\varphi}_{r+q}a^{\dagger}_{-r}a_{-q-r}+{\varphi}_{r}\delta_{q,0}\right)a^{\dagger}_{0}a^{\dagger}_{0},

and the fact that the sum
\sum_{q,r}
, by assumption, does only include indices different from
0
, only the first two terms need to be estimated. Since they are similar we only consider

(46)

\frac{\kappa}{N^{2}}\sum_{q,r}\hat{V}(r/N){\varphi}_{r}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-s{\mathcal{B}}}a_{q+r}^{\dagger}a_{r}a^{\dagger}_{0}a^{\dagger}_{0}e^{s{\mathcal{B}}}e^{-{\mathcal{B}}}a_{q}a_{0}e^{{\mathcal{B}}}ds.

Using Cauchy-Schwarz for the expectation value of
\xi
we deduce

|\eqref{re1}|\lesssim\frac{\kappa}{N^{2}}\sum_{r,q}|{\varphi}_{r}|\int_{0}^{1}ds\|a_{q+r}a_{0}a_{0}e^{s{\mathcal{B}}}\xi\|\|a_{r}e^{(s-1){\mathcal{B}}}a_{q}a_{0}e^{{\mathcal{B}}}\xi\|\lesssim\kappa^{2}\|(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)\xi\|^{2},

where we used
|a_{0}a_{0}|\lesssim\mathcal{N}\lesssim N
, Lemma
13
, and

(47)

\sum_{r,q}\|a_{r}e^{(s-1){\mathcal{B}}}a_{q}a_{0}e^{{\mathcal{B}}}\xi\|^{2}\leq\sum_{q}\|\mathcal{N}^{1/2}e^{(s-1){\mathcal{B}}}a_{q}a_{0}e^{{\mathcal{B}}}\xi\|^{2}=\sum_{q}\|e^{(s-1){\mathcal{B}}}\mathcal{N}^{1/2}a_{q}a_{0}e^{{\mathcal{B}}}\xi\|^{2}\\
\leq\sum_{q}\|\mathcal{N}^{1/2}\mathcal{N}_{0}^{1/2}a_{q}e^{{\mathcal{B}}}\xi\|^{2}\lesssim N^{2}\|(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)^{1/2}\xi\|^{2}.

Next we look at the second to last term in (
42
).
To this aim, notice
that the operator norm of

\Phi=\sum_{p}{\varphi}_{p}a_{-p}a_{p}
can be estimated by

(48)

|\Phi|\lesssim\kappa(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1),

which can be seen by applying Cauchy-Schwarz

|\sum_{p}{\varphi}_{p}a_{-p}a_{p}|\leq\sum_{p}(\kappa a^{\dagger}_{p}a_{p}+(|{\varphi}_{p}|^{2}/\kappa)a_{-p}a_{-p}^{\dagger})\lesssim\kappa(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1).

Further we write

(49)

\frac{\kappa}{2N^{2}}\sum_{p,q}\hat{V}(p/N){\varphi}_{q}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}a^{\dagger}_{p}a^{\dagger}_{-p}a_{-q}a_{q}(2+2\mathcal{N}_{0})e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s\\
=\frac{\kappa}{2N}\sum_{p}\hat{V}(p/N)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}a^{\dagger}_{p}a^{\dagger}_{-p}e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}\Phi(2+2\mathcal{N}_{0})/Ne^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s

where

(50)

e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}a^{\dagger}_{p}a^{\dagger}_{-p}e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}=a^{\dagger}_{p}a^{\dagger}_{-p}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-s{\mathcal{B}}}[a^{\dagger}_{p}a^{\dagger}_{-p},{\mathcal{B}}]e^{s{\mathcal{B}}}ds,

and

-[a^{\dagger}_{p}a^{\dagger}_{-p},{\mathcal{B}}]=\frac{a^{\dagger}_{0}a^{\dagger}_{0}}{N}{\varphi}_{p}(a^{\dagger}_{p}a_{p}+a^{\dagger}_{-p}a_{-p}+1).

Hence, the expression coming from the second term on the right hand side of (
50
) gives

(51)

\frac{\kappa}{N}\sum_{p}\hat{V}(p/N){\varphi}_{p}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\tau{\mathcal{B}}}\frac{a^{\dagger}_{0}a^{\dagger}_{0}}{N}(a^{\dagger}_{p}a_{p}+1)e^{\tau{\mathcal{B}}}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}\Phi(1+2\mathcal{N}_{0})/Ne^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s\lesssim\kappa^{3}(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1).

The first term on the right hand side of (
50
), including
a^{\dagger}_{p}a^{\dagger}_{-p}
, is again evaluated by rewriting it in configuration space

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}\int_{\Lambda^{2}}dxdy\kappa V_{N}(x-y){\check{a}}_{x}^{\dagger}{\check{a}}_{y}^{\dagger}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}\Phi(2+2\mathcal{N}_{0})/Ne^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s,

which can be bounded by

\delta Q_{4}+\kappa^{2}(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1).

Finally, consider the last term in (
42
), which we conveniently rewrite as

\frac{\kappa}{N^{2}}\sum_{p,q,r}\hat{V}_{N}(r){\varphi}_{p}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}a_{p+r}^{\dagger}a_{q}^{\dagger}e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}a_{-p}^{\dagger}a_{q+r}\Phi(\mathcal{N}_{0}+1)e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s,

using again the notation
\Phi=\sum_{k}{\varphi}_{k}a_{-k}a_{k}
. Next we apply Duhamel again, similar to (
44
), to
e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}a_{p+r}^{\dagger}a_{q}^{\dagger}e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}

and obtain two terms, where the first one including
a_{p+r}^{\dagger}a_{q}^{\dagger}
has to be estimated by
Q_{4}
. More precisely,
in configuration space that term has the form

\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}\int_{\Lambda^{2}}dxdy\kappa V_{N}(x-y){\check{a}}_{x}^{\dagger}{\check{a}}_{y}^{\dagger}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}\frac{{\check{a}}^{\dagger}({\varphi}_{x}){\check{a}}_{y}\Phi(\mathcal{N}_{0}+1)}{N^{2}}e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}{\rm d}t{\rm d}s\lesssim\delta Q_{4}+\kappa^{2}(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1).

The second term involving

\int_{0}^{t}e^{-s{\mathcal{B}}}[a^{\dagger}_{p+r}a^{\dagger}_{q},{\mathcal{B}}]e^{s{\mathcal{B}}}ds,

i.e.,

\frac{\kappa}{N^{2}}\sum_{p,q,r}\hat{V}_{N}(r){\varphi}_{p}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{s}^{1}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\tau{\mathcal{B}}}[a^{\dagger}_{p+r}a^{\dagger}_{q},{\mathcal{B}}]e^{\tau{\mathcal{B}}}e^{-t{\mathcal{B}}}a_{-p}^{\dagger}a_{q+r}\Phi(\mathcal{N}_{0}+1)e^{t{\mathcal{B}}}d\tau{\rm d}t{\rm d}s,

is again estimated by
\kappa^{3}(\mathcal{N}_{+}+1)
.

6.
Proof of Lemma
11

Using (
9
) we can write

(52)

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{p}\hat{V}(p/N){\varphi}_{p}=N^{2}\frac{1}{N^{3}}\sum_{p\neq 0}\hat{V}(p/N){\varphi}_{p}=N^{2}\langle P_{0}^{\perp}{\varphi},P_{0}^{\perp}V\rangle_{N}\\
=-\left\langle V,P_{0}^{\perp}\frac{1}{P_{0}^{\perp}(-\Delta+\frac{\kappa}{2}V)P_{0}^{\perp}}P_{0}^{\perp}\frac{\kappa}{2}V\right\rangle_{N},\qquad\qquad

where we used (
11
) to obtain the last equality.

This implies now for
a_{N}

(53)

4\pi a_{N}=\frac{\kappa\hat{V}(0)}{2}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{p}\frac{\kappa}{2}\hat{V}(p/N){\varphi}_{p}\\
=\frac{\kappa}{2}\int_{[-\frac{N}{2},\frac{N}{2}]^{3}}V(x)-\left\langle\frac{\kappa}{2}V,P_{0}^{\perp}\frac{1}{P_{0}^{\perp}(-\Delta+\frac{\kappa}{2}V)P_{0}^{\perp}}P_{0}^{\perp}\frac{\kappa}{2}V\right\rangle_{N}.

On a formal level this converges for
N\to\infty
as

(54)

\frac{\kappa}{2}\int_{[-\frac{N}{2},\frac{N}{2}]^{3}}V(x)-\left\langle\frac{\kappa}{2}V,P_{0}^{\perp}\frac{1}{P_{0}^{\perp}(-\Delta+\frac{\kappa}{2}V)P_{0}^{\perp}}P_{0}^{\perp}\frac{\kappa}{2}V\right\rangle_{N}\to_{N\to\infty}\\
\frac{\kappa}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}V(x)-\left\langle\frac{\kappa}{2}V,\frac{1}{(-\Delta+\frac{\kappa}{2}V)}\frac{\kappa}{2}V\right\rangle=\left\langle\sqrt{v},\frac{1}{1+\sqrt{v}\frac{1}{-\Delta}\sqrt{v}}\sqrt{v}\right\rangle,

with
v=\frac{\kappa}{2}V
. The right hand side is
4\pi a
, with
a
being the scattering length.
In order to obtain the bound
|a-a_{N}|\leq O(1/N)
,
lets denote

{\mathds{1}}_{N}=\sum_{p\neq 0}|e_{p}\rangle\langle e_{p}|,\qquad|e_{p}\rangle=e^{-i\frac{p}{N}\cdot x}/N^{3/2},

such that
{\mathds{1}}_{N}+|e_{0}\rangle\langle e_{0}|
is the identity on
L^{2}([-N/2,N/2]^{3})
. Then we can write

\left\langle v,P_{0}^{\perp}\frac{1}{P_{0}^{\perp}(-\Delta+v)P_{0}^{\perp}}P_{0}^{\perp}v\right\rangle_{N}=\left\langle v,{\mathds{1}}_{N}\frac{1}{-\Delta+v}{\mathds{1}}_{N}v\right\rangle=\left\langle v,({\mathds{1}}-\delta_{N})\frac{1}{-\Delta+v}({\mathds{1}}-\delta_{N})v\right\rangle,

with
\delta_{N}={\mathds{1}}-{\mathds{1}}_{N}
, where
{\mathds{1}}
denotes the identity on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Notice, we implicitly assume that the application of
{\mathds{1}}_{N}
means that one only integrates over
[-N/2,N/2]^{3}
. We also assume that
N
is large enough such that the support of
v
is in
[-N/2,N/2]^{3}
.
Hence, we can write the difference of
a-a_{N}
as

(55)

a-a_{N}=\left\langle v,{\mathds{1}}_{N}\frac{1}{-\Delta+v}{\mathds{1}}_{N}v\right\rangle-\left\langle v,\frac{1}{-\Delta+v}v\right\rangle\\
=-2\Re\left\langle v,\delta_{N}\frac{1}{-\Delta+v}v\right\rangle+\left\langle v,\delta_{N}\frac{1}{-\Delta+v}\delta_{N}v\right\rangle.

Observe that
{\varphi}=-\frac{1}{-\Delta+v}v
is the solution of the scattering equation on the whole space, which is smooth function with falloff
1/|x|
in configuration space, due to the properties of
V
.
The first term of the right hand side of (
55
) is

\left\langle v,\delta_{N}\frac{1}{-\Delta+v}v\right\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\hat{V}(p)\hat{\varphi}(p)dp-\frac{1}{N^{3}}\sum_{p}\hat{\varphi}(p/N)\hat{v}(p/N),

which is the difference of the Riemann sum and its integral. A second order Taylor expanding of
\hat{\varphi}(p)
shows that this error is of order
1/N

due to the
1/p^{2}
behavior for small
p
. Notice that a quick first order expansion gives an error of
\log N/N
.

For the second term in (
55
), observe

(56)

\left\langle v,\delta_{N}\frac{1}{-\Delta+v}\delta_{N}v\right\rangle\leq\left\langle v,\delta_{N}\frac{1}{-\Delta}\delta_{N}v\right\rangle=\\
=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|\hat{v}(p)|^{2}}{p^{2}}dp-\frac{1}{N^{3}}\sum_{p}\frac{|\hat{v}(p/N)|^{2}}{(p/N)^{2}}=O(1/N),

which is again the difference of a specific integral and its Riemann approximation.

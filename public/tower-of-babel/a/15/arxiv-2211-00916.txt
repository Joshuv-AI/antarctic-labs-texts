1.
Introduction

The
N
-body problem studies motion of
N
point masses,
m_{i}>0
,
i\in\mathbf{N}:=\{1,\dots,N\}
, under Newton’s universal gravitational law. The trajectories of the masses
q(t)=(q_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathbf{N}}
satisfy

(1)

\ddot{q}_{i}(t)=-\sum_{j\in\mathbf{N}\setminus\{i\}}\frac{m_{j}(q_{i}(t)-q_{j}(t))}{|q_{i}(t)-q_{j}(t)|^{3}},\;\;\forall i\in\mathbf{N}.

One of the most important aspects of the problem is to understand final motions of the masses as time goes to infinity. When
N=3
, a complete classification of the final behavior was given by Chazy
[
8
]
(also see
[
2
]
, a similar classification can be obtained for
N>3
). Although a solution of (
1
) may end at a finite time due to singularity, by results from
[
31
]
,
[
32
]
and
[
17
]
, such solutions should be rare in the measure sense.

By introducing an additional massless body into the system, we get the so-called
restricted
(N+1)
-body problem
, where the motion of the massless body is governed by the gravitational forces of the
N
positive masses (will be referred as primaries), while the massless body does not produce any gravitational force, so the motion of the primaries are not affected by its presence. As a result, the trajectory
z(t)
of the massless body satisfies

(2)

\ddot{z}(t)=\partial_{z}U(z(t),t)=-\sum_{i\in\mathbf{N}}\frac{m_{i}(z(t)-q_{i}(t))}{|z(t)-q_{i}(t)|^{3}},

where
U(z,t)
is a time-dependent potential function

(3)

U(z,t)=\sum_{i\in\mathbf{N}}\frac{m_{i}}{|z-q_{i}(t)|}.

The restricted
(N+1)
-body problem can be seen as certain limit of the
(N+1)
-body problem, when one of the masses is much smaller than the others. Hence it is a good model in describing the motion of comets and satellites in gravitational systems, and has been studies by many authors including Poincaré and Birkhoff.

Solutions of (
2
) may also experience singularities at finite time. However if we assume there is no collision between the primaries, then all these singularities must be binary collisions between the massless body and one of the primaries. As is well-known such singularities can be regularized (see
[
23
]
,
[
29
]
and
[
35
]
), so solutions can be extended through these binary collisions as elastic bounce. Hence the maximum domain of any solution of (
2
) will be the whole time interval
\mathbb{R}
. Then following Chazy, a complete classification of all the solutions of (
2
) according to the final behaviors of the massless body can be given as below.

•

hyperbolic solution
:
|z(t)|\to\infty
and
|\dot{z}(t)|\to c>0
, as
t\to\pm\infty
;

•

parabolic solution
:
|z(t)|\to\infty
and
|\dot{z}(t)|\to 0
, as
t\to\pm\infty
;

•

bounded solution
:
\limsup_{t\to\pm\infty}|z(t)|<\infty
;

•

oscillatory solution
:
\limsup_{t\to\pm\infty}|z(t)|<\infty
and
\liminf_{t\to\pm\infty}|z(t)|<\infty
.

Remark 1.1
.

z|_{\mathbb{R}}
will be called
bi-hyperbolic
, if both
z|_{[0,\infty)}
and
z|_{(-\infty,0]}
are hyperbolic solutions.

In this paper, we will study (bi)-hyperbolic solutions of the planar restricted
(N+1)
-body problem, and we set
\mathbb{R}^{2}=\mathbb{C}
for the rest of the paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
q(t)=(q_{i}(t))_{i\in\mathbf{N}}
is a planar collision-free periodic solution of (
1
), i.e.,

(*)

\begin{cases}&q(t)=q(t+T)\in\mathbb{C}^{N},\;\forall t\in\mathbb{R},\;\text{ for some }T>0;\\
&\rho_{0}=\inf\{|q_{i}(t)-q_{j}(t)|:t\in\mathbb{R},\;i\neq j\in\mathbf{N}\}>0.\par\end{cases}

Given arbitrarily an energy constant
h>0
and asymptotic directions
\theta_{\pm}\in[0,2\pi)
, for any
x\in\mathbb{C}
and
t_{x}\in\mathbb{R}
, there are hyperbolic solutions
\gamma^{\pm}:[t_{x},\pm\infty)\to\mathbb{C}
of (
2
) satisfies
\gamma^{\pm}(t_{x})=x
and

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\gamma^{\pm}(t)/|\gamma^{\pm}(t)|=e^{i\theta_{\pm}(\text{mod }2\pi)},\;\;\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\dot{\gamma}^{\pm}(t)=\pm\sqrt{2h}e^{i\theta_{\pm}(\text{mod }2\pi)}.

Moreover
\gamma^{\pm}(t)
must be collision-free, whenever
t\neq t_{x}
.

Remark 1.2
.

For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we say
\gamma(t)\in\mathbb{C}
is
collision-free
, if
\gamma(t)\neq q_{i}(t)
, for all
i\in\mathbf{N}
.

Next we will compare the above result with some previous results of the
N
-body problem.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
q|_{[t_{0},\pm\infty)}
be a solution of (
1
). We say it is
a hyperbolic solution
, if

|q_{i}(t)-q_{j}(t)|\simeq O(|t|),\text{ as }t\to\pm\infty,\;\forall\{i\neq j\}\subset\mathbf{N},

it is
a parabolic solution
, if

|q_{i}(t)-q_{j}(t)|\simeq O(|t|^{\frac{2}{3}}),\text{ as }t\to\pm\infty,\;\forall\{i\neq j\}\subset\mathbf{N},

and it is
a hyperbolic-elliptic solution
, if there is a
\mathbf{K}\subset\mathbf{N}
with
2\leq|\mathbf{K}|<N
, such that

|q_{i}(t)-q_{j}(t)|\leq C_{1}<\infty,\;\forall|t|\geq t_{0}\text{ and }\forall\{i\neq j\}\subset\mathbf{K};

|q_{i}(t)-q_{j}(t)|\simeq O(|t|),\text{ as }t\to\pm\infty,\;\forall(i,j)\in\mathbf{N}\times(\mathbf{N}\setminus\mathbf{K})\text{ with }i\neq j.

Remark 1.3
.

Recall that the energy of a solution of the
N
-body is a constant. In particular the energy of a hyperbolic solution must be positive, and of a parabolic solution must be zero. In contrary, the energy of a hyperbolic-elliptic solution can be any real number.

In
[
26
]
Maderna and Venturelli showed that for any
h>0
, starting from any initial configuration, there is a
h
-energy hyperbolic solution with any prescribed non-collision limiting shape at infinity (a different proof of this result was given in
[
24
]
recently). A similar result for parabolic solutions was obtained by Maderna and Venturelli in
[
25
]
, when the energy is zero and the limiting shapes at infinity are minimal central configurations. While hyperbolic and parabolic solutions only affect dynamics on positive and zero energy surfaces, hyperbolic-elliptic solutions will affect dynamics at any energy surface as there is no restriction on energies of these solutions. However so far it is unclear whether similar results hold for hyperbolic-elliptic solutions.

Since hyperbolic solutions of the restricted
(N+1)
-body problem could be seen as certain limit of hyperbolic-elliptic solutions of the
(N+1)
-body problem with a single escaping mass, Theorem
1.1
may also help us understand hyperbolic-elliptic solutions of the unrestricted problem.

Under different coordinates, solutions mentioned above form stable manifolds (when time goes to positive infinity) or unstable manifolds (when time goes to negative infinity) of different invariant subsets at infinity parameterized by the limiting energy and shape or direction (see
[
27
]
,
[
30
]
and
[
15
]
). The geometric and topological properties of these manifolds have great influences on the global dynamics. A basic question is:
with given limiting energies and shapes or directions at infinity corresponding to negative and positive infinite time, does the corresponding stable and unstable manifolds intersect?
The intersection of these stable and unstable manifolds is equivalent to the existence of solutions with the given limiting energies and shapes as time goes to negative and positive infinity.

For the
N
-body problem, this is a difficult problem with very few results. Up to our knowledge there is only some partial result in
[
15
]
for hyperbolic solutions. Here we obtain the following result for the restricted
(N+1)
-body problem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Under the assumption (
*
‣
1.1
), given arbitrarily an energy constant
h>0
and a pair of asymptotic directions
\theta_{\pm}\in[0,2\pi)
. There is a bi-hyperbolic solution
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
of (
2

\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\gamma(t)/|\gamma(t)|=e^{i\theta_{\pm}(\text{mod }2\pi)},\;\;\lim_{t\to\pm\infty}\dot{\gamma}(t)=\pm\sqrt{2h}e^{i\theta_{\pm}(\text{mod }2\pi)}.

Moreover

has at most one collision.

For the
N
-center problem, which is a simplified model of the restricted
(N+1)
-problem with the primaries fixed, there are much more results. In
[
21
]
and
[
22
]
, (bi)-hyperbolic solutions of the planar and spatial
N
-center problem were studied correspondingly. More recently using variational methods,
[

]
and
[
3
]
studied bi-parabolic solutions and bi-hyperbolic solutions for the spatial
N
-center problem correspondingly, and
[
4
]
studied bi-parabolic solutions in the planar
N
-center problem.

Our proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
are variational in nature. Since the work of Chenciner and Montgomery
[
14
]
, variational methods have had a lot of success in the
N
-body problem, first in proving periodic solutions (see
[
16
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
39
]
and the references within), and recently in proving parabolic and hyperbolic solutions (see
[
25
]
and
[
26
]
given above). The success is similar in the
N
-center problem. Besides references mentioned above, periodic solutions were studied in
[
33
]
,
[
38
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
12
]
, while homoclinic and heteroclinic solutions were studied in
[
13
]
.

Compare to the
N
-body problem and the
N
-center problem, very few results were obtained using variational methods in the restricted
(N+1)
-body problem. Up to our knowledge, so far there are only a few results for periodic solutions, see
[
1
]
,
[
20
]
and
[
11
]
, and no result seems to be available for parabolic or hyperbolic solutions.

The main difficulties in proving (bi)-hyperbolic solutions of the restricted
(N+1)
-body problems using variational methods are as below. First, energy is not conserved in this case as the system is time periodic, and the energy conservation plays an important role in controlling the asymptotic behaviors at infinity. Second, to obtain a bi-hyperbolic solution as a minimizer, we need to find some proper topological constraint. Without it the minimizer is likely to be a trivial solution staying at infinity all the time. On the other hand, if the topological constraint was not chosen properly then the corresponding minimizer may be a collision-ejection solution that is not regularizable, as indicated by Gordon’s result
[
19
]
for the Kepler problem.

Our paper is organized as following: Section
2
contains results that are essential for applying action minimization methods to the restricted
(N+1)
-body problem; Section
3
and Section

are devoted to the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
correspondingly; Appendix A shows how to lower the action of a collision-ejection solution near an isolated collision by local deformation; Appendix B contains proofs of two propositions essential to control the asymptotic behavior of the massless body at infinity.

Properties of action minimizers

In this section we prove some basic results that will be needed when applying action minimization methods to the restricted

x,y\in\mathbb{C}
satisfying
\sqrt{2}R_{1}\leq|x|,|y|\leq R
, the following results hold.

(a).

\psi_{0}(x,y;t_{1},t_{2})\leq 16\frac{R^{2}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}+12m\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{R}
,
\forall t_{1}<t_{2}
.

(b).

For any
h>0
, there exist positive constants
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
independent of
R
, such that

\sup\{\phi_{h}(x,y;s_{1},s_{2}):s_{1},s_{2}\in[0,1)\}\leq\beta_{1}R+\beta_{2}.

Proof.

(a). Choose a point
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z|=R
. Let
\overrightarrow{ox},\overrightarrow{oy}
and
\overrightarrow{oz}
denote the three rays starting from the origin and passing the points
x,y
and
z
correspondingly. Moreover we assume
\overrightarrow{oz}
divide the angle between
\overrightarrow{ox}
and
\overrightarrow{oy}
into two equal angles with each angle less than or equal to
\pi/2
.

Define a straight line segment
\xi(t)=(1-\lambda(t))x+\lambda(t)z
,
t\in[t_{1},\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}]
with

\lambda(t)=\begin{cases}\mu\left[1-\left(\frac{\tau-t}{\tau-t_{1}}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\right],&\text{ if }t\in[t_{1},\tau];\\
\mu+(1-\mu)\left(\frac{t-\tau}{\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}-\tau}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}},&\text{ if }t\in[\tau,\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}],\end{cases}

satisfying
\lambda(t_{1})=0
and
\lambda(\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2})=1
. For this, we need to let

\mu=\frac{|x|}{|x|+|z|}\in(0,1),\;\;\tau=t_{1}+\frac{\mu^{3/2}}{\mu^{3/2}+(1-\mu)^{3/2}}\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{2}\in(t_{1},\frac{1}{2}(t_{1}+t_{2})).

By the triangle inequality, for any
t\in[t_{1},\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}]
,

|\xi(t)|=|(1-\lambda(t))x+\lambda(t)z|\geq\big|(1-\lambda(t))|x|-\lambda(t)|z|\big|=|\mu-\lambda(t)|\big(|x|+|z|\big).

Since
|x|,|z|\geq\sqrt{2}R_{1}
,
|\xi(t)|\geq R_{1}
,
\forall t\in[t_{1},\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}]
. Then (
21
) and the above inequality imply

U(\xi(t),t)\leq\frac{2m}{|\xi(t)|}\leq\frac{2m}{(|x|+|z|)|\mu-\lambda(t)|}\leq\frac{2m}{|z|\cdot|\mu-\lambda(t)|}.

Using this, we get

\displaystyle\int_{t_{1}}^{\tau}L(\xi,\dot{\xi},t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{1}}^{\tau}\frac{1}{2}|\dot{\xi}|^{2}+U(\xi,t)\,dt\leq\frac{|z-x|^{2}}{2}\int_{t_{1}}^{\tau}|\dot{\lambda}(t)|^{2}\,dt+\frac{2m}{|z|}\int_{t_{1}}^{\tau}\frac{1}{|\mu-\lambda(t)|}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{2|z-x|^{2}}{9}\frac{\mu^{2}}{(\tau-t_{1})^{2}}\int_{t_{1}}^{\tau}\left(\frac{\tau-t}{\tau-t_{1}}\right)^{-\frac{2}{3}}\,dt+\frac{2m}{|z|}\frac{1}{\mu}\int_{t_{1}}^{\tau}\left(\frac{\tau-t}{\tau-t_{1}}\right)^{-\frac{2}{3}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}|z-x|^{2}\frac{\mu^{2}}{\tau-t_{1}}+\frac{6m}{|z|}\frac{\tau-t_{1}}{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{3}\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mu^{\frac{3}{2}}+(1-\mu)^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\frac{|z-x|^{2}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}+3m\frac{\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\mu^{\frac{3}{2}}+(1-\mu)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{|z|};

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}}L(\xi,\dot{\xi},t)\,dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau}^{\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}}\frac{1}{2}|\dot{\xi}(t)|^{2}+U(\xi(t),t)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|z-x|^{2}}{2}\int_{\tau}^{\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}}|\dot{\lambda}(t)|^{2}\,dt+\frac{2m}{|z|}\int_{\tau}^{\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}}\frac{1}{|\mu-\lambda(t)|}\,dt

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2|z-x|^{2}}{9}\frac{(1-\mu)^{2}}{(\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}-\tau)^{2}}+\frac{2m}{|z|}\frac{1}{1-\mu}\right)\int_{\tau}^{\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}}\left(\frac{t-\tau}{\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}-\tau}\right)^{-\frac{2}{3}}\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3}|z-x|^{2}\frac{(1-\mu)^{2}}{\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}-\tau}+\frac{6m}{|z|}\frac{\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}-\tau}{1-\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{3}(1-\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\mu^{\frac{3}{2}}+(1-\mu)^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\frac{|z-x|^{2}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}+3m\frac{(1-\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\mu^{\frac{3}{2}}+(1-\mu)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{|z|}.

These two estimates imply

(22)

\int_{t_{1}}^{\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}}L(\xi,\dot{\xi},t)\,dt\leq\frac{4}{3}f_{1}(\mu)f_{2}(\mu)\frac{|z-x|^{2}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}+3m\frac{f_{1}(\mu)}{f_{2}(\mu)}\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{|z|},

where

f_{1}(\mu)=\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}+(1-\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}},\;f_{2}(\mu)=\mu^{\frac{3}{2}}+(1-\mu)^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Since
\mu\in(0,1)
,

f_{1}(\mu)=2\left(\frac{1}{2}\mu^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}(1-\mu)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)\leq 2\left(\frac{1}{2}\mu+\frac{1}{2}(1-\mu)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{2};

1\geq f_{2}(\mu)=2\left(\frac{1}{2}\mu^{\frac{3}{2}}+\frac{1}{2}(1-\mu)^{\frac{3}{2}}\right)\geq 2\left(\frac{1}{2}\mu+\frac{1}{2}(1-\mu)\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}\geq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}.

Plug these into (
22
), we have

\int_{t_{1}}^{\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}}L(\xi,\dot{\xi})\,dt\leq\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{3}\frac{|z-x|^{2}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}+6m\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{|z|}\leq\frac{16\sqrt{2}}{3}\frac{R^{2}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}+6m\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{R}.

A similarly straight line segment
\zeta|_{[\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2},t_{2}]}
going from
z
to
y
can be found, which satisfies

\int_{\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}}^{t_{2}}L(\zeta,\dot{\zeta},t)\,dt\leq\frac{16\sqrt{2}}{3}\frac{R^{2}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}+6m\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{R}.

As a result,

\psi_{0}(x,y;t_{1},t_{2})\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}}L(\xi,\dot{\xi},t)\,dt+\int_{\frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{2}}^{t_{2}}L(\zeta,\dot{\zeta},t)\,dt\leq 16\frac{R^{2}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}+12m\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{R}.

(b). Let
t_{1}=s_{1}
, we can always find a
t_{2}\in[s_{1}+R,s_{1}+R+1]
satisfying
\{t_{2}\}=s_{2}.
Then
R\leq t_{2}-t_{1}=t_{2}-s_{1}\leq R+1
. Hence

\displaystyle\phi_{h}(x,y;s_{1},s_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\psi_{h}(x,y;t_{1},t_{2})\leq 16\frac{R^{2}}{t_{2}-t_{1}}+6m\frac{t_{2}-t_{1}}{R}+h(t_{2}-t_{1})

\displaystyle\leq 16R+6m(1+R^{-1})+hR+h

\displaystyle\leq(16+h)R+6m+\frac{3\sqrt{2}m}{R_{1}}+h.

∎

For the rest of the section, we fix arbitrarily a positive constant
h
, a position
x\in\mathbb{C}
, a moment
t_{x}
(without loss of generality we further assume

By Proposition
2.7

, for any

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
R_{2}\geq\max\{2\sqrt{2}R_{1},|x|+1\}
and
y\in\Lambda_{\theta_{+}}(R_{2})
, let

(25)

\tau_{y}:=\min\{t\in[t_{x},t_{y}]:|\gamma_{y}(t)|=R_{2}\}.

The following results hold.

(a).

t_{x}<\inf\{\tau_{y}:\;y\in\Lambda_{\theta_{+}}(R_{2})\}\leq\sup\{\tau_{y}:\;y\in\Lambda_{\theta_{+}}(R_{2})\}<\infty
.

(b).

When
R_{2}
is large enough,
\dot{r}_{y}(t)\geq\sqrt{3m/2}R_{2}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
,
\forall t\in[\tau_{y},t_{y}]
, where
r_{y}(t)=|\gamma_{y}(t)|
.

Proof.

(a). The second inequality in the statement is trivial. Let’s give a proof of the third inequality first. Fix an
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, by Proposition
2.6
,

\sup\{\psi_{h}(x,z;t_{x},t):\;z\in\partial B_{R_{2}}\text{ and }t\in[n_{0},n_{0}+1]\}\leq C_{1}.

Since
\gamma_{y}|_{[t_{x},t_{y}]}
is a free-time minimizer, so is
\gamma_{y}|_{[t_{x},\tau_{y}]}
. Then

(26)

h(\tau_{y}-t_{x})\leq\mathcal{A}_{h}(\gamma_{y};t_{x},\tau_{y})=\phi_{h}(x,\gamma_{y}(\tau_{y});t_{x},\{\tau_{y}\})\leq\psi_{h}(x,\gamma_{y}(\tau_{y});t_{x},n_{0}+\{\tau_{y}\})\leq C_{1}.

This implies

\sup\{\tau_{y}:\;y\in\Lambda_{\theta_{+}}(R_{2})\}\leq t_{x}+C_{1}/h<\infty.

Meanwhile by the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,

(\tau_{y}-t_{x})\int_{t_{x}}^{\tau_{y}}|\dot{\gamma}_{y}|^{2}\,dt\geq\left(\int_{t_{x}}^{\tau_{y}}|\dot{\gamma}_{y}|\,dt\right)^{2}\geq|\gamma(\tau_{y})-x|^{2}\geq(R_{2}-|x|)^{2}.

Combining this with (
26
), we get

C_{1}\geq\mathcal{A}_{h}(\gamma_{y};t_{x},\tau_{y})\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{t_{x}}^{\tau_{y}}|\dot{\gamma}_{y}|^{2}\,dt\geq\frac{(R_{2}-|x|)^{2}}{2(\tau_{y}-t_{x})}.

Assuming the first inequality in the statement does not hold, then we can find a sequence
\{y_{n}\in\Lambda_{\theta_{+}}(R_{2})\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
with the corresponding
\{\tau_{y_{n}}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}
satisfying
\lim_{n\to\infty}\tau_{y_{n}}=t_{x}
. Then

C_{1}\geq\mathcal{A}_{h}(\gamma_{y_{n}};t_{x},\tau_{y_{n}})\geq\frac{(R_{2}-|x|)^{2}}{2(\tau_{y_{n}}-t_{x})}\to\infty,\;\text{ as }n\to\infty,

which is absurd.

(b). For each
y\in\Lambda_{\theta_{+}}(R_{2})
, let
a_{y}=\max\{t\in[t_{x},\tau_{y}]:r_{y}(t)=R_{2}/2\}
. Then

r_{y}(t)\geq R_{2}/2\geq R_{1},\;\;\forall t\in[a_{y},\tau_{y}].

Now it is enough to show
\dot{r}_{y}(t_{0})\geq\sqrt{6m/r_{y}(t_{0})}
, for some
t_{0}\in[a_{y},\tau_{y}]
, as Proposition
3.1
implies

\dot{r}_{y}(t)\geq\frac{1}{2}\dot{r}(t_{0})\geq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{6m/r_{y}(t_{0})}\geq\sqrt{3m/2}R^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{2},\;\;\forall t\in[t_{0},t_{y}],

where the last inequality follows from
r_{y}(t_{0})\leq r_{y}(\tau_{y})=R_{2}
.

By a contradiction argument, let’s assume
\dot{r}_{y}(t)<\sqrt{6m/r_{y}(t)}
,
\forall t\in[a_{y},\tau_{y}].
Then

\frac{R_{2}}{2}=r_{y}(\tau_{y})-r_{y}(a_{y})=\int^{\tau_{y}}_{a_{y}}\dot{r}_{y}(t)\,dt<\int^{\tau_{y}}_{a_{y}}\sqrt{6m/r_{y}(t)}\,dt\leq\sqrt{\frac{12m}{R_{2}}}(\tau_{y}-a_{y}).

Notice that
r_{y}(t)\geq R_{2}/2
,
\forall t\in[a_{y},\tau_{y}]
. As a result,

\mathcal{A}_{h}(\gamma_{y};a_{y},\tau_{y})\geq h(\tau_{y}-a_{y})>\frac{h}{4\sqrt{3m}}R_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}.

However with Lemma
3.1
, they imply

\frac{h}{4\sqrt{3m}}R_{2}^{\frac{3}{2}}<\mathcal{A}_{h}(\gamma_{y};a_{y},\tau_{y})=\phi_{h}(\gamma_{y}(a_{y}),\gamma_{y}(\tau_{y});\{a_{y}\},\{\tau_{y}\})\leq\beta_{1}R_{2}+\beta_{2},

which is absurd, when
R_{2}
is large enough.
∎

In the rest of the section, we fix an
R_{2}\geq\max\{2\sqrt{2}R_{1},|x|+1\}
large enough, such that the above lemma always holds.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
\tau>\sup\{\tau_{y}:\;y\in\Lambda_{\theta_{+}}(R_{2})\}
, the following results hold.

(a).

There is a constant
R=R(\tau,R_{2})>0
(independent of
y
), such that for any
y\in\Lambda_{\theta_{+}}(R_{2})
with
t_{y}\geq\tau
, we have
|\gamma_{y}(t)|\leq R
,
\forall t\in[t_{x},\tau]
.

(b).

\{\gamma_{y}|_{[t_{x},\tau]}:y\in\Lambda_{\theta_{+}}(R_{2})\text{ with }t_{y}\geq\tau\}
is a bound subset in
H^{1}([t_{x},\tau],\mathbb{C})
.

Proof.

(a). By (
25
),
|\gamma_{y}(t)|\leq R_{2}
, when
t\in[t_{x},\tau_{y}]
. Meanwhile Lemma
3.2
implies
|\gamma_{y}(t)|
is strictly increasing, when
t\in[\tau_{y},\tau]
. Hence it is enough to show
|\gamma_{y}(\tau)|\leq R
, for some finite constant
R
independent of
y
. By the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,

|\gamma_{y}(\tau)|\leq|\gamma_{y}(\tau_{y})|+|\gamma_{y}(\tau)-\gamma_{y}(\tau_{y})|\leq R_{2}+\int_{\tau_{y}}^{\tau}|\dot{\gamma}_{y}|\,dt\leq R_{2}+\left((\tau-\tau_{y})\int_{\tau_{y}}^{\tau}|\dot{\gamma}_{y}|^{2}\,dt\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

At the same time, Lemma
3.1
implies

\int_{\tau_{y}}^{\tau}|\dot{\gamma}_{y}|^{2}\,dt\leq 2\mathcal{A}_{h}(\gamma_{y};\tau_{y},\tau)=2\phi_{h}(\gamma_{y}(\tau_{y}),\gamma_{y}(\tau);\{\tau_{y}\},\{\tau\})\leq 2\beta_{1}|\gamma_{y}(\tau)|+2\beta_{2}.

These two estimates implies
|\gamma_{y}(\tau)|\leq R_{2}+\sqrt{2\tau}(\beta_{1}|\gamma_{y}(\tau)|+\beta_{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
, which is equivalent to

|\gamma_{y}(\tau)|^{2}-2(R_{2}+\beta_{1}\tau)|\gamma_{y}(\tau)|+R_{2}^{2}-2\beta_{2}\tau\leq 0.

Solving this inequality, we get

|\gamma_{y}(\tau)|\leq R_{2}+\beta_{1}\tau+(\beta_{1}^{2}\tau^{2}+2\beta_{1}\tau R_{2}+2\beta_{2}\tau)^{\frac{1}{2}}:=R.

(b). Let
a=\inf\{\tau_{y}:y\in\Lambda_{\theta_{+}}(R_{2})\text{ and }\tau_{y}\geq\tau\}
,
b=\sup\{\tau_{y}:y\in\Lambda_{\theta_{+}}(R_{2})\text{ and }\tau_{y}\geq\tau\}.
By Lemma
3.2
,
t_{x}<a<b<\infty
. As
|\gamma_{y}(\tau_{y})|=R_{2}
,
\forall y\in\Lambda_{\theta_{+}}(R_{2})
with
t_{y}\geq\tau
. By Proposition
2.6

\mathcal{A}_{h}(\gamma_{y};t_{x},\tau_{y})=\psi_{h}(x,\gamma_{y}(\tau_{y});t_{x},\tau_{y})\leq\sup_{t\in[a,b],z\in\partial B_{R_{2}}}\psi_{h}(x,z;t_{x},t)\leq C_{2}.

Meanwhile as
\sqrt{2}R_{1}\leq R_{2}=|\gamma_{y}(\tau_{y})|\leq|\gamma_{y}(\tau)|\leq R
, Lemma
3.1
implies

\mathcal{A}_{h}(\gamma_{y};\tau_{y},\tau)=\phi_{h}(\gamma_{y}(\tau_{y}),\gamma_{y}(\tau);\{\tau_{y}\},\{\tau\})\leq\beta_{1}R+\beta_{2}.

Combining these two estimates, we get

\int_{t_{x}}^{\tau}|\dot{\gamma}_{y}|^{2}\,dt\leq 2\mathcal{A}_{h}(\gamma_{y};t_{x},\tau)\leq 2(\beta_{1}R+\beta_{2}+C_{2}).

On the other hand, property (a) implies

\int_{t_{x}}^{\tau}|\gamma_{y}(t)|^{2}\,dt\leq(\tau-t_{x})R^{2}.

As a result, for any

with

\|\gamma_{y}|_{[t_{x},\tau]}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\int_{t_{x}}^{\tau}|\gamma_{y}|^{2}+|\dot{\gamma}_{y}|^{2}\,dt\leq(\tau-t_{x})R^{2}+2(\beta_{1}R+\beta_{2}+C_{2}).

∎

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-2T}^{-2T+\delta_{n}}+\int_{2T-\delta_{n}}^{2T}\frac{1}{2}|\dot{\xi}^{\lambda_{n}}(s)|^{2}+\frac{m_{i_{0}}}{|\xi^{\lambda_{n}}(s)|}\,ds\leq 2\left(\frac{1}{2}C_{1}^{2}+m_{i_{0}}C_{2}\right)\delta_{n}\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

Plug the above inequality into (
85
), we get

\bar{\mathcal{A}}(\bar{\zeta};-2T,2T)-\bar{\mathcal{A}}(\xi^{\lambda_{n}};-2T,2T)\geq 5\varepsilon_{0}.

Then (
77
) implies

(87)

\bar{\mathcal{A}}(\zeta^{\lambda_{n}};-2T,2T)-\bar{\mathcal{A}}(\xi^{\lambda_{n}};-2T,2T)\geq 4\varepsilon_{0}.

Since
\rho_{1}=\min\{|\gamma(t)-q_{i}(t)|:\forall t\in[t_{0}-\delta,t_{0}+\delta]\text{ and }i\neq i_{0}\}>0
,
\forall i\neq i_{0}
, (
80
) implies,

|\eta^{\lambda_{n}}(t)+q_{i_{0}}(t)-q_{i}(t)|\geq|\zeta(t)+q_{i_{0}}(t)-q_{i}(t)|-|\eta^{\lambda_{n}}(t)-\zeta(t)|\geq\rho_{1}-\varepsilon,\;\forall t\in[t_{0}-\delta,t_{0}+\delta].

As a result, when
0<\varepsilon\leq\rho_{1}/2
, for
n
large enough, we have

(88)

\displaystyle\int_{-2T}^{2T}

\displaystyle\sum_{i\neq i_{0}}\frac{m_{i}}{|\xi^{\lambda_{n}}(s)+q^{\lambda_{n}}_{i_{0}}(s)-q^{\lambda_{n}}_{i}(s)|}\,ds=\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{3}}\int_{t_{0}-\frac{2T}{\lambda_{n}}}^{t_{0}+\frac{2T}{\lambda_{n}}}\sum_{i\neq i_{0}}\frac{m_{i}}{|\eta^{\lambda_{n}}(t)+q_{i_{0}}(t)-q_{i}(t)|}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{3}}\int_{t_{0}-\frac{2T}{\lambda_{n}}}^{t_{0}+\frac{2T}{\lambda_{n}}}\frac{\sum_{i\neq i_{0}}m_{i}}{\rho_{1}-\varepsilon}\,dt\leq\frac{8T(\sum_{i\neq i_{0}}m_{i})}{\rho_{1}}\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{2}{3}}\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

Plug (
87
) and (
88
) into (
84
), we get that when
n
is large enough,

\mathcal{A}^{\lambda_{n}}(\zeta^{\lambda_{n}};-\delta/\lambda_{n},\delta/\lambda_{n})-\mathcal{A}^{\lambda_{n}}(\xi^{\lambda_{n}};-\delta/\lambda_{n},\delta/\lambda_{n})\geq 3\varepsilon_{0}+\int_{-2T}^{2T}\langle\dot{\zeta}^{\lambda_{n}}(s)-\dot{\xi}^{\lambda_{n}}(s),\dot{q}_{i}^{\lambda_{n}}(s)\rangle\,ds.

As a result, it is enough to show that when
n
is large enough,

\int_{-2T}^{2T}|\dot{\zeta}^{\lambda_{n}}(s)|\cdot|\dot{q}_{i_{0}}^{\lambda_{n}}(s)|\,ds+\int_{-2T}^{2T}|\dot{\xi}^{\lambda_{n}}(s)|\cdot|\dot{q}_{i_{0}}^{\lambda_{n}}(s)|\,ds\leq 2\varepsilon_{0}.

By the definition of
\lambda_{n}
-blow up,

(89)

\int_{-2T}^{2T}|\dot{\zeta}^{\lambda_{n}}(s)|\cdot|\dot{q}_{i_{0}}^{\lambda_{n}}(s)|\,ds=\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{3}}\int_{t_{0}-\frac{2T}{\lambda_{n}}}^{t_{0}+\frac{2T}{\lambda_{n}}}|\dot{\zeta}(t)|\cdot|\dot{q}_{i_{0}}(t)|\,dt

Since
q|_{[t_{0}-\delta,t_{0}+\delta]}
, is a collision-free solution of (
2
),

(90)

C_{3}=\sup\{|\dot{q}_{i_{0}}(t)|:t\in[t_{0}-\delta,t_{0}+\delta]\}<\infty.

Meanwhile by Proposition
2.2
, there exist two positive constants
C_{4},C_{5}
,

(91)

|\dot{\zeta}(t)|\leq C_{4}|t-t_{0}|^{-\frac{1}{3}}+C_{5},\;\forall t\neq t_{0}\text{ small enough}.

As a result, for
n
large enough,

\displaystyle\int_{-2T}^{2T}|\dot{\zeta}^{\lambda_{n}}(s)|\cdot|\dot{q}_{i_{0}}^{\lambda_{n}}(s)|\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{3}}C_{3}\int_{t_{0}-\frac{2T}{\lambda_{n}}}^{t_{0}+\frac{2T}{\lambda_{n}}}C_{4}|t-t_{0}|^{-\frac{1}{3}}+C_{5}\,dt

\displaystyle=C_{3}(3C_{4}(2T)^{\frac{2}{3}}\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{3}}+4C_{5}T\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{2}{3}})\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

By (
79
) and the first inequality in (
86
), we can find a
C_{6}
, such that for
n
large enough,

\int_{-2T}^{2T}|\dot{\xi}^{\lambda_{n}}(s)|\,ds\leq C_{6}.

Since
|\dot{q}^{\lambda_{n}}_{i_{0}}(s)|=\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{3}}|\dot{q}_{i_{0}}(t_{0}+\lambda_{n}^{-1}s)|\leq\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{3}}C_{3}
,
\forall s\in[-2T,2T]
, for

large enough, we have

\int_{-2T}^{2T}|\dot{\xi}^{\lambda_{n}}(s)|\cdot|\dot{q}_{i_{0}}^{\lambda_{n}}(s)|\,ds\leq\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{3}}C_{3}C_{6}\leq\varepsilon_{0}.

This finishes our proof.

∎

6.
Appendix B: The Perturbed Kepler problem

In this appendix, instead of the restricted
(N+1)
-body problem, we will prove two propositions of the perturbed Kepler problem (
92
), that imply Proposition
3.1
and
3.2
, as we believe these results will be useful in more general setting.

(92)

\ddot{z}=-\frac{mz}{|z|^{3}}+F(z,t),\;\;F\in C^{0}(\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{R},\mathbb{R}).

Let
z|_{[t_{1},t_{2}]}
, is a solution of (
92
) for the rest of the appendix. Rewrite it in polar coordinates with
z(t)=r(t)e^{i\theta(t)}
, we get

(93)

\begin{cases}&\ddot{r}-r\dot{\theta}^{2}=-m/r^{2}+F_{1}(r,\theta,t);\\
&r\ddot{\theta}+2\dot{r}\dot{\theta}=F_{2}(r,\theta,t),\end{cases}

where
F_{1}(r,\theta,t)=\text{Re}(F(re^{\theta},t)e^{-i\theta}),
and
F_{2}(r,\theta,t)=\text{Im}(F(re^{\theta},t)e^{-i\theta})
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Assume there are two positive constants
R
and
C
, such that

(94)

|F(x,t)|\leq C/|x|^{2},\;\;\forall x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus B_{R}^{o}.

If
r(t_{1})\geq R
and
\dot{r}(t_{1})\geq\sqrt{\frac{3(m+C)}{r(t_{1})}}>0
, then
\dot{r}(t)>\frac{1}{2}\dot{r}(t_{1}),\;\forall t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
.

Proof.

Assume the desired result does not hold, then there is a
\tau_{1}\in(t_{1},t_{2}]
, such that

\dot{r}(\tau_{1})=\dot{r}(t_{1})/2\text{ and }\dot{r}(t)>\dot{r}(t_{1})/2,\;\forall t\in[t_{1},\tau_{1}).

By (
94
), for
i=1,2
,

(95)

|F_{i}(r,\theta,t)|<C/r^{2},\;\;\forall r\geq R.

Let
g(t)=\frac{1}{2}\dot{r}(t)^{2}-\frac{m+C}{r(t)}
. Then

\dot{g}=\dot{r}(\ddot{r}+\frac{m+C}{r^{2}}).

Combining this with (
93
) and (
95
), we get

\ddot{r}=r\dot{\theta}^{2}-\frac{m}{r^{2}}+F_{1}\geq-\frac{m+C}{r^{2}},\;\;\forall t\in[t_{1},\tau_{1}].

As a result,
\dot{g}(t)\geq 0
,
\forall t\in[t_{1},\tau_{1}]
. This implies
g(\tau_{1})\geq g(t_{1})
and

\frac{\dot{r}^{2}(t_{1})}{4}=\dot{r}^{2}(\tau_{1})>2g(\tau_{1})\geq 2g(t_{1})\geq\dot{r}^{2}(t_{1})-\frac{2(m+C)}{r(t_{1})},

which is a contradiction to the condition that
\dot{r}(t_{1})\geq\sqrt{3(m+C)/r(t_{1})}
.

∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Assume there are two positive constants
R
and
C
, such that

(96)

|F(x,t)|\leq C/|x|^{3},\;\;\forall x\in\mathbb{C}\setminus B^{o}_{R}.

If
r(t_{1})\geq R
and
\dot{r}(t)\geq v_{0}
,
\forall t\geq t_{1}
, for some constant
v_{0}>0
, then

(a).

\sup\{|\omega(t)|:\;t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]\}\leq|\omega(t_{1})|+\frac{C}{v_{0}r(t_{1})}
, where
\omega(t)=z(t)\wedge\dot{z}(t)
;

(b).

when
t_{2}=\infty
,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\theta(t)=\theta_{0}
, for some
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

(a). By (
92
) and (
96
),

|\dot{\omega}(t)|=|z(t)\wedge\ddot{z}(t)|=|z(t)\wedge F(z,t)|\leq\frac{C}{r^{2}(t)}.

For any
t\in[t_{1},t_{2}]
, since
r(t)\geq v_{0}(t-t_{1})+r(t_{1})
,

\displaystyle|\omega(t)|

\displaystyle\leq|\omega(t_{1})|+\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\frac{C}{r^{2}(\tau)}d\tau\leq|\omega(t_{1})|+\int_{t_{1}}^{t}\frac{C/v_{0}^{2}}{(\tau-\tau_{0}+\frac{r(t_{1})}{v_{0}})^{2}}d\tau

\displaystyle=|\omega(t_{1})|+\frac{C}{v_{0}^{2}}\left(\frac{v_{0}}{r(t_{1})}-\frac{v_{0}}{v_{0}(t-t_{1})+r(t_{1})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq|\omega(t_{1})|+\frac{C}{v_{0}r(t_{1})}.

(b). Notice that in polar coordinates
\omega(t)=r^{2}(t)\dot{\theta}(t)
. Hence

\dot{\theta}(t)=\frac{\omega(t)}{r^{2}(t)}\leq\frac{v_{0}r(t_{1})|\omega(t_{1})|+C}{v_{0}^{3}r(t_{1})}\frac{1}{(t-t_{1}+r(t_{1})/v_{0})^{2}}.

Therefore for any
t>t_{1}
,

(97)

|\theta(t)-\theta(t_{1})|\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t}|\dot{\theta}(\tau)|d\tau\leq\frac{|\omega(t_{1})|}{v_{0}r(t_{1})}+\frac{C}{v_{0}^{2}r^{2}(t_{1})}.

Since
\dot{r}(t)\geq v_{0}>0
,
\forall t\geq t_{0}
,
r(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
. As a result, the upper bound given in the above inequality goes to zero, as
t_{1}
goes to infinity. By Cauchy’s criterion for convergence,
\theta(t)
converges to a finite
\theta_{0}
, as
t
goes to infinity.
∎

By (
20
), Proposotion
3.1
and
3.2
follows directly from the above two results correspondingly.

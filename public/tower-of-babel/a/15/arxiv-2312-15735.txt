Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the following Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality: ( ∫ ℝ n | x | − p ​ a ​ | ∇ u | p ​ 𝑑 x ) 1 p ≥ S ⁡ ( p , a , b ) ​ ( ∫ ℝ n | x | − q ​ b ​ | u | q ​ 𝑑 x ) 1 q , ∀ u ∈ D a p ​ ( ℝ n ) , \left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-pa}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq S(p,a,b)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-qb}|u|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\hskip 9.24994pt\forall\;u\in D_{a}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}), where S ⁡ ( p , a , b ) S(p,a,b) is the sharp constant and a , b , p , q a,b,p,q satisfy the relations: 0 ≤ a < n − p p , a ≤ b < a + 1 , 1 < p < n , q = n ​ p n − p ⁡ ( 1 + a − b ) . 0\leq a<\frac{n-p}{p},\hskip 9.24994pta\leq b<a+1,\hskip 9.24994pt1<p<n,\hskip 9.24994ptq=\frac{np}{n-p(1+a-b)}. Our main results involve establishing gradient stability within both the functional and the critical settings and deriving some qualitative properties for the stability constant. In the functional setting, the main method we apply is a simple but clever transformation inspired by Horiuchi [ 30 ] , which enables us to reduce the inequality to some well-studied ones. Based on the gradient stability, we establish several refined Sobolev-type embeddings involving weak Lebesgue norms for functions supported in general domains. In the critical point setting, we use some careful expansion techniques motivated by Figalli and Neumayer [ 24 ] to handle the nonlinearity appeared in the Euler-Lagrange equations. We believe that the ideas presented in this paper can be applied to treat other weighted Sobolev-type inequalities. Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the following Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality proved in
[
6
]
:

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-pa}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\geq S(p,a,b)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-qb}|u|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},\quad\forall\;u\in D_{a}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(1.1)

where
S(p,a,b)
is the sharp constant and
a,b,p,q
satisfy the relations:

0\leq a<\frac{n-p}{p},\quad a\leq b<a+1,\quad 1<p<n,\quad q=\frac{np}{n-p(1+a-b)}.

(1.2)

The space
D_{a}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the completion of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with respect to the norm

{\lVert u\rVert}_{D_{a}^{p}}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-pa}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

When
a=b=0
, (
1.1
) reduces to the classical Sobolev inequality. It is well-known that Aubin
[
2
]
and Talenti
[
42
]
found the explicit sharp constant
S(p,0,0)
and determined all extremal functions for this case. Later, relying on symmetrization techniques, Lieb
[
33
]
extended their results to the case
p=2,a=0
. For the case
p=2,(a,b)\neq(0,0)
, the first complete classification result was given by Chou and Chu
[
9
]
. In fact, based on a generalized moving plane method, they characterized all positive critical points for the Euler-Lagrange equation related to (
1.1
). As for the case
p\neq 2
and
(a,b)\neq(0,0)
, to the best of our knowledge, the first result in this direction was given by Horiuchi
[
30
]
. Specifically, he showed that

\displaystyle S(p,a,b)=

\displaystyle n^{\frac{1}{p}}(p-1)^{\frac{\gamma}{n}-1+\frac{1}{p}}(n-\gamma p)^{\frac{\gamma}{n}-\frac{1}{p}}(n-p-pa)^{1-\frac{\gamma}{n}}\left(\frac{2\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}{p\gamma}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{n}}

(1.3)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{\gamma p}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{n(p-1)}{\gamma p}\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{n}{\gamma}\right)}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{n}}

and the equality in (
1.1
) can be achieved by radial functions taking the form:

\frac{A}{\Big(1+B|x|^{\frac{p\gamma(n-p-pa)}{(p-1)(n-p\gamma)}}\Big)^{\frac{n}{p\gamma}-1}},

(1.4)

where
\gamma=1+a-b,A\in\mathbb{R}
and
B>0
. Assuming
a=0
, Ghoussoub and Yuan
[
29
]
proved that (
1.4
) actually gave all extremal functions for (
1.1
). Later, Caldiroli and Musina
[
13
]
studied the symmetry-breaking phenomenon and showed all extremal functions are radially symmetric. Subsequently, Musina
[
38
]
showed that every radially symmetric extermal function must have the form (
1.4
). Recently, Ciraolo and Corso
[
11
]
found that, in some special cases, (
1.4
) gives all positive critical points for the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation. Their proof relies on some delicate analysis on a suitable Riemannian manifold.

Let us summarize all the above conclusions as the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
a,b,p,q
satisfy the relations in (
1.2
). Then the equality in (
1.1
) holds if and only if
u
takes the form in (
1.4
) (if
a=b=0
, then up to a translation). We use
\mathcal{M}_{p,a,b}
to denote the manifold formed by extremal functions:

\mathcal{M}_{p,a,b}:=\left\{A\Big(1+B|x|^{\frac{p\gamma(n-p-pa)}{(p-1)(n-p\gamma)}}\Big)^{1-\frac{n}{p\gamma}}\Big|A\in\mathbb{R},B>0,\gamma=1+a-b\right\},\quad(a,b)\neq(0,0),

\mathcal{M}_{p,0,0}:=\left\{A\Big(1+B|x-x_{0}|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\Big)^{1-\frac{n}{p}}\Big|A\in\mathbb{R},B>0,x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}.

For the reader’s convenience, we provide a simple proof here for the case
a>0
using ideas from
[
13
,
30
]
. We also point out that the proof here indicates an interesting phenomenon: if the classification of radial extremizers in the case
a=0
is known, then we can characterize all extremizers in the case
a>0
.

Our first main result concerns the gradient stability for (
1.1
). The question on the stability of functional inequalities was initially raised by Brézis and Lieb
[
5
]
. They obtained a sharpened Sobolev embedding on bounded domains and posed the question of whether similar results hold for homogeneous Sobolev inequalities in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This question was answered affirmatively by Bianchi and Egnell
[
4
]
in the case
p=2
. They showed that for any function
u\in D_{0}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, the gap in the Sobolev inequality can control the
D_{0}^{2}
-distance between
u
and an extremizer. Subsequently, their results were extended to high-order and fractional-order Hardy-Sobolev inequalities. Related works can be found in
[
3
,
8
,
36
,
40
]
. Assuming
p=2
, Wang and Willem
[
43
]
were the first to establish gradient stability in the full parameter domain (
1.2
). For the case
a<0,p=2
, we refer to
[
26
,
44
]
for some related works.

For the case
p\neq 2
, due to the absence of the Hilbertian structure, more delicate arguments are required to handle the stability. We refer to
[
10
,
20
,
24
,
37
]
and references therein for various previous works and partial results. The first sharp stability result was given by Figalli and Zhang
[
25
]
. Relying on refined spectral estimates and compactness arguments, they proved that, for any
1<p<n
, there exists a positive constant
K
such that

\frac{{\lVert\nabla u\rVert}_{L^{p}}}{{\lVert u\rVert}_{L^{\frac{np}{n-p}}}}-S(p,0,0)\geq K\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,0,0}}\left(\frac{{\lVert\nabla u-\nabla v\rVert}_{L^{p}}}{{\lVert\nabla u\rVert}_{L^{p}}}\right)^{\max\{2,p\}},\quad\forall\;u\in D_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\backslash\{0\}.

The exponent
\max\{2,p\}
is optimal. Later, Deng and Tian
[
16
,
17
]
generalized their results to the case
a=0\leq b<1
:

\frac{{\lVert\nabla u\rVert}_{L^{p}}}{{\lVert|x|^{-b}u\rVert}_{L^{q}}}-S(p,0,b)\geq K\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,0,b}}\left(\frac{{\lVert\nabla u-\nabla v\rVert}_{L^{p}}}{{\lVert\nabla u\rVert}_{L^{p}}}\right)^{\max\{2,p\}},\quad\forall\;u\in D_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

(1.5)

where
K
is a universal positive constant and
\max\{2,p\}
is optimal. When considering radial functions, the constraint
a=0
can be removed.

In this paper, our first main result is to establish gradient stability for (
1.1
) in the full parameter region (
1.2
). Specifically, we prove:

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume
a,b,p,q
satisfy the relations in (
1.2
) and
0<a<b
. Then there exists a positive constant
K
such that, for any nontrivial function
u\in D_{a}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\frac{{\lVert|x|^{-a}\nabla u\rVert}_{L^{p}}}{{\lVert|x|^{-b}u\rVert}_{L^{q}}}-S(p,a,b)\geq K\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,a,b}}\left(\frac{{\lVert|x|^{-a}(\nabla u-\nabla v)\rVert}_{L^{p}}}{{\lVert|x|^{-a}\nabla u\rVert}_{L^{p}}}\right)^{\max\{2,p\}}.

(1.6)

Recall that
\mathcal{M}_{p,a,b}
denotes the extremal manifold. Moreover, the exponent
\max\{2,p\}
is optimal.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
a,b,p,q
satisfy the relations in (
1.2
) and
0<a=b
. Then there exists a positive constant
K
such that, for any nontrivial function
u\in D_{a}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

\frac{{\lVert|x|^{-a}\nabla u\rVert}_{L^{p}}}{{\lVert|x|^{-a}u\rVert}_{L^{q}}}-S(p,a,a)\geq K\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,a,a}}\left(\frac{{\lVert|x|^{-a}(\nabla u-\nabla v)\rVert}_{L^{p}}}{{\lVert|x|^{-a}\nabla u\rVert}_{L^{p}}}\right)^{\max\{4,2p\}}.

(1.7)

If we assume
u
is n-symmetric (i.e., u is symmetric about n mutually orthogonal hyperplanes), the exponent
\max\{4,2p\}
can be replaced by
\max\{2,p\}
.

Remark 1.4

The optimality of the exponent
\max\{2,p\}
in (

[

and

Remark 1.5
.

We believe that when
0<a=b
, the estimate (
1.7
) still holds for general functions, but with the exponent
\max\{4,2p\}
replaced by
\max\{2,p\}
. For

Remark 1.7
.

The method of using gradient stability to get strengthened Sobolev-type embeddings with weak Lebesgue norms was first discovered by Bianchi and Egnell

{\lVert\nabla u\rVert}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-S(2,0,0)^{2}{\lVert u\rVert}^{2}_{L^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}(\Omega)}\geq\bar{K}(\Omega){\lVert u\rVert}_{L_{w}^{\frac{n}{n-2}}(\Omega)}^{2}

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Here
\bar{K}(\Omega)
is a positive constant depending only on
n
and
\Omega

[

[

applied similar methods to derive improved versions of the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequalities (
1.1
) with parameters

and
p=2,a>0,b>0
, respectively. This method can also be used to treat other Sobolev-type inequalities. We refer to
[
8
,
19
,
27
,
28
,
46
]
and the references therein for further discussions. It is worth mentioning that an improvement on this approach was first given by Chen, Frank and Weth
[
8
]

When it comes to domains with infinite measures, we need to make some assumptions. For a domain

Our third main result relates to the stability for (
1.1
) in the critical point setting. Consider the corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation:

\div(|x|^{-pa}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)+|x|^{-qb}|u|^{q-2}u=0.

(1.16)

We define
\mathcal{M}_{p,a,b}^{s}
as a subset of

that contains all positive solutions of the form (
1.4
) of the equation (
1.16
) (When

\mathcal{M}^{s}_{p,a,b}:=\left\{A_{p,a,b}\lambda^{\frac{n-p-pa}{p}}\Big(1+|\lambda x|^{\frac{p\gamma(n-p-pa)}{(p-1)(n-p\gamma)}}\Big)^{1-\frac{n}{p\gamma}}\Big|\lambda>0,\gamma=1+a-b\right\},\quad(a,b)\neq(0,0),

\mathcal{M}^{s}_{p,0,0}:=\left\{A_{p,0,0}\lambda^{\frac{n-p}{p}}\Big(1+|\lambda(x-x_{0})|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\Big)^{1-\frac{n}{p}}\Big|\lambda>0,x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}.

is a positive normalization factor such that, for any

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-pa}|\nabla v|^{p}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-qb}|v|^{q}dx=S(p,a,b)^{\frac{pq}{q-p}}.

The functions in
\mathcal{M}^{s}_{p,a,b}

is an

-dimensional scaling-translation-invariant manifold. We remark that in some special regions,

gives all positive solutions of (
1.16
). We refer to
[

,
11
,
22
,
42
]
and the references therein for further discussions.

This stability problem typically refers to whether being almost a sign-invariant solution of (
1.16
) implies being close to a Talenti bubble. This problem can be traced back to the well-known global compactness principle of Struwe
[
41
]
, which provided a qualitative analysis in the case
p=2,a=b=0
. The first quantitative version of this principle was given by Ciraolo, Figalli and Maggi
[
12
]
. In particular, they obtained the linear estimate

C\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{2,0,0}^{s}}{\lVert u-v\rVert}_{D_{0}^{2}}\leq\left\|\Delta u+|u|^{\frac{4}{n-2}}u\right\|_{D_{0}^{-2}},

under the assumption that
u
is close to a Talenti bubble. Here, the space
D_{a}^{-p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
denotes the dual space of
D_{a}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Subsequently, Figalli and Glaudo
[
23
]
established analogous results in the case where
u
is close to the sum of several weak-interacting Talenti bubbles and
3\leq n\leq 5
. They discovered that these estimates are strongly dependent on the dimension
n
and showed that linear estimate does not hold when
n\geq 6
. The remaining case
n\geq 6
was eventually solved by Deng, Sun and Wei
[
15
]
using the method of finite dimensional reduction. They established logarithmic and sublinear estimates for the case
n=6
and
n>6
, respectively. Recently, Wei and Wu
[
44
]

extended the results in
[
15
]
to the general equation (
1.16
) in the case
p=2
. We refer to
[
1
,
14
,
35
,
39
,
46
]
for various interesting results on other Sobolev-type inequalities.

To the best of our knowledge, the stability problem in the case
p\neq 2
has not been investigated so far. Our third main result aims to fill this gap in a weak sense. Let us first give a definition:

Definition 1.13
.

Assume
a,b,p,q
satisfy the relations in (
1.2
). For any
u\in D_{a}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we define

P_{u}:=\left\{V\in\mathcal{M}_{p,a,b}^{s}\;|\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-qb}|V|^{q-2}Vu=\max_{W\in\mathcal{M}_{p,a,b}^{s}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-qb}|W|^{q-2}Wu\right\}.

(1.17)

Note that, when
p=2
, the set
P_{u}
consists precisely of functions
V
such that

\inf_{W\in\mathcal{M}_{2,a,b}^{s}}{\lVert u-W\rVert}_{D_{a}^{2}}={\lVert u-V\rVert}_{D_{a}^{2}}.

Heuristically,
P_{u}
can be used to describe the distance between
u
and
\mathcal{M}_{p,a,b}^{s}
. The main reason for using
P_{u}
instead of directly minimizing the distance is that
P_{u}
provides a suitable framework for the applications of spectral gap inequalities. Here is our main theorem.

Theorem 1.14
.

Assume
a,b,p,q
satisfy the relations in (
1.2
) and
p>2
. Then there exists three positive constants
c_{1},C_{1}
and
\delta
, depending only on
a,b,p,q,n
, such that, for any function
u\in D_{a}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
V\in P_{u}
, if

\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,a,b}^{s}}{\lVert u-v\rVert}_{D_{a}^{p}}\leq\delta,

(1.18)

then the following two estimates hold:

\displaystyle c_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-pa}|\nabla V|^{p-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}-C_{1}{\lVert\rho\rVert}^{p}_{D_{a}^{p}}

(1.19)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\lVert\div(|x|^{-pa}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)+|x|^{-qb}|u|^{q-2}u\rVert}_{D_{a}^{-p}}{\lVert\rho\rVert}_{D_{a}^{p}},

\displaystyle c_{1}{\lVert\rho\rVert}^{p}_{D_{a}^{p}}-C_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-pa}|\nabla V|^{p-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}

(1.20)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\lVert\div(|x|^{-pa}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)+|x|^{-qb}|u|^{q-2}u\rVert}_{D_{a}^{-p}}{\lVert\rho\rVert}_{D_{a}^{p}}.

Here
\rho=u-\mu V
and
\mu=\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-qb}|V|^{q-2}Vu}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-qb}|V|^{q}}
.

A direct consequence is the following alternative-type stability result.

Corollary 1.15
.

Assume
a,b,p,q
satisfy the relations in (
1.2
) and
p>2
. Then there exists three positive constants
\kappa,\eta
and
\delta
, depending only on
a,b,p,q,n
, such that, for any function
u\in D_{a}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
V\in P_{u}
, if

\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,a,b}^{s}}{\lVert u-v\rVert}_{D_{a}^{p}}\leq\delta,

then at least one of the following two estimates holds:

\kappa{\lVert u-V\rVert}_{D_{a}^{p}}^{p-1}\leq{\lVert\div(|x|^{-pa}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)+|x|^{-qb}|u|^{q-2}u\rVert}_{D_{a}^{-p}},

(1.21)

\kappa{\lVert u_{t}-V\rVert}_{D_{a}^{p}}^{p-1}\leq{\lVert\div(|x|^{-pa}|\nabla u_{t}|^{p-2}\nabla u_{t})+|x|^{-qb}|u_{t}|^{q-2}u_{t}\rVert}_{D_{a}^{-p}}\quad\text{for any }0<t\leq\eta.

(1.22)

Here
u_{t}:=tu+(1-t)V
.

Remark 1.16
.

It is worth mentioning that Theorem
1.14
makes sense only if the value of

\frac{{\lVert\rho\rVert}^{p}_{D_{a}^{p}}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-pa}|\nabla V|^{p-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}}=:A_{u}

(1.23)

falls outside the interval
\left[\frac{c_{1}}{C_{1}},\frac{C_{1}}{c_{1}}\right]
. In fact, if
A_{u}\notin\left[\frac{c_{1}}{2C_{1}},\frac{2C_{1}}{c_{1}}\right]
, we deduce the uniform estimate (
1.21
). If
A_{u}\in\left[\frac{c_{1}}{2C_{1}},\frac{2C_{1}}{c_{1}}\right]
, we can obtain (
1.22
).

Remark 1.17
.

We wonder whether (
1.21
) always holds. Our result at least shows that, under some restrictions on
|\nabla(u-V)|
, we can get
A_{u}\notin\left[\frac{c_{1}}{2C_{1}},\frac{2C_{1}}{c_{1}}\right]
and (
1.21
). For example, one can assume
|\nabla u-\nabla V|\leq\varepsilon|\nabla V|
pointwisely in
\text{Supp}(u-V)
for some
\varepsilon
sufficiently small.

Remark 1.18
.

The exponent
p-1
is optimal here, as one can choose the function
u=V+\epsilon\zeta(\cdot+R_{\epsilon})
, where
0<\epsilon<\delta
,
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
R_{\epsilon}\gg 1
.

2
Stability in the functional setting

Before starting the proof, let us give some important observations: Assume
a,b,p,q
satisfy the relations in (
1.2
) and
a>0
. For any function
u\in D_{a}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, utilizing polar coordinate, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-pa}|\nabla u|^{p}dx=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(|\nabla_{r}u|^{2}+r^{-2}|\nabla_{\theta}u|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}r^{n-1-ap}drd\theta

(2.1)

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-qb}|u|^{q}dx=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|u|^{q}r^{n-1-bq}drd\theta.

(2.2)

It is direct to compute

|\nabla_{\theta}u|^{2}=\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\left(x_{i}\partial_{j}u-x_{j}\partial_{i}u\right)^{2}.

(2.3)

Set
k=\frac{n-p}{n-p-ap}
. Consider the new function
\bar{u}(r\theta):=k^{\frac{1}{q}}u(r^{k}\theta)
(This transformation may traced back to the work
[
30
]
). We see that the formulas (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) transform into

k^{1-p-\frac{p}{q}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(|\nabla_{r}\bar{u}|^{2}+k^{2}r^{-2}|\nabla_{\theta}\bar{u}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}r^{n-1}drd\theta

(2.4)

and

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}|\bar{u}|^{q}r^{n-1-(b-a)q}drd\theta=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-q(b-a)}|\bar{u}|^{q}dx

(2.5)

respectively. Since
a>0
, we have
k>1
. Thus
\bar{u}
is a well-defined function in
D_{0}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Moreover, one can easily check that the correspondence
u\leftrightarrow\bar{u}
provides a bijection between
\mathcal{M}_{p,0,b-a}
and
\mathcal{M}_{p,a,b}
(when
b=a
, we restrict to radial functions in
\mathcal{M}_{p,0,0}
).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By homogeneity, we can assume

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-qb}|u|^{q}dx=1.

Using the above transformations, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-pa}|\nabla u|^{p}dx=

\displaystyle\,k^{1-p-\frac{p}{q}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(|\nabla_{r}\bar{u}|^{2}+k^{2}r^{-2}|\nabla_{\theta}\bar{u}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}r^{n-1}drd\theta

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,k^{1-p-\frac{p}{q}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(|\nabla_{r}\bar{u}|^{2}+r^{-2}|\nabla_{\theta}\bar{u}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}r^{n-1}drd\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,k^{1-p-\frac{p}{q}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\bar{u}|^{p}dx

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,k^{1-p-\frac{p}{q}}S^{p}(p,0,b-a)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-q(b-a)}|\bar{u}|^{q}dx\right)^{\frac{p}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,k^{1-p-\frac{p}{q}}S^{p}(p,0,b-a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,S^{p}(p,a,b).

The first inequality becomes equality precisely when
u
is radially symmetric about the origin. The second inequality becomes equality if and only if
\bar{u}
belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{p,0,b-a}
(see for example
[
29
]
). Combining these arguments, it is easy to conclude our results.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By homogeneity, we suppose

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-qb}|u|^{q}dx=1.

Using similar arguments as in the above proof and the stability estimate (
1.5
) (see
[
17
]
for detalis), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-pa}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}-S(p,a,b)\geq

\displaystyle k^{\frac{1}{p}-1-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\bar{u}|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}-S(p,a,b)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k^{\frac{1}{p}-1-\frac{1}{q}}\left(\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\nabla\bar{u}|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}-S(p,0,b-a)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,0,b-a}}{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla v\rVert}_{L^{p}}^{\max\{2,p\}}.

Since
u\leftrightarrow\bar{u}
gives a bijection between
\mathcal{M}_{p,0,b-a}
and
\mathcal{M}_{p,a,b}
, one has

\displaystyle\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,0,b-a}}{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla v\rVert}_{L^{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\,\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,0,b-a}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(|\nabla_{r}(\bar{u}-v)|^{2}+r^{-2}|\nabla_{\theta}(\bar{u}-v)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}r^{n-1}drd\theta

\displaystyle\geq\,ck^{1-p-\frac{p}{q}}\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,0,b-a}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(|\nabla_{r}(\bar{u}-v)|^{2}+k^{2}r^{-2}|\nabla_{\theta}(\bar{u}-v)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}r^{n-1}drd\theta

\displaystyle=\,c\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,a,b}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(|\nabla_{r}(u-v)|^{2}+r^{-2}|\nabla_{\theta}(u-v)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}r^{n-1-ap}drd\theta

\displaystyle=\,c\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,a,b}}{\lVert|x|^{-a}(\nabla u-\nabla v)\rVert}_{L^{p}}^{p}.

Thus we get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-pa}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}-S(p,a,b)\geq c\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,a,b}}{\lVert|x|^{-a}(\nabla u-\nabla v)\rVert}_{L^{p}}^{\max\{2,p\}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

To prove Theorem
1.3
, we need the following two lemmas. The first lemma is an elementary estimate.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\alpha,\beta
be two positive constants. Then there exists a number
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\alpha,\beta)
such that, for any positive numbers
A,B,D,E,F
with
B\geq\alpha,F\leq\beta,|A-D|+|E-B|\leq\varepsilon F
, one has

\frac{A}{B}-1\geq F\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\frac{D}{E}-1\geq\frac{F}{2}.

Proof.

Since
A\geq B(1+F)
, we have

\frac{D}{E}\geq\frac{(1+F)B-|A-D|}{B+|B-E|}.

Thus

\frac{D}{E}-1\geq\frac{F}{2}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\frac{1}{2}FB\geq(|A-D|+|B-E|)\left(1+\frac{F}{2}\right).

We just need to take
\varepsilon=\frac{\alpha}{\beta+2}
.
∎

The second lemma is an integral estimate, which enables us to deal with the translation-invariant property of
\mathcal{M}_{p,0,0}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

There exists a positive number
c
such that, for any nontrivial radially symmetric function
U\in\mathcal{M}_{p,0,0}
and vector
\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\lVert U\rVert}_{L^{\frac{np}{n-p}}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(|\nabla_{r}U(\cdot+\rho)|^{2}+k^{2}r^{-2}|\nabla_{\theta}U(\cdot+\rho)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}r^{n-1}drd\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}-S(p,0,0)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle c\left(\frac{{\lVert\nabla U-\nabla U(\cdot+\rho)\rVert}_{L^{p}}}{{\lVert U\rVert}_{L^{\frac{np}{n-p}}}}\right)^{2}.

Proof.

By homogeneity and dilation invariance, we may assume

U(x)=L\left(1+|x|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{\frac{p-n}{p}},

where
L
is a suitable constant such that
{\lVert U\rVert}_{L^{\frac{np}{n-p}}}=1
. The concentration compactness principle (see for example
[
34
,
30
]
) allows us to focus on the case when
\rho
is small. In this case, we claim that the values on both side are comparable to
\rho^{2}
. To demonstrate it, we can apply the mean value theorem on the right-hand. As for the left-hand side, we need to estimate the difference between

\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(|\nabla_{r}U(\cdot+\rho)|^{2}+k^{2}r^{-2}|\nabla_{\theta}U(\cdot+\rho)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}r^{n-1}drd\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and

\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(|\nabla_{r}U(\cdot+\rho)|^{2}+r^{-2}|\nabla_{\theta}U(\cdot+\rho)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}r^{n-1}drd\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Note that the values of the above two formulas fall within the interval
[S(p,0,0),kS(p,0,0)]
, we can apply the basic inequality:

c(a^{p}-b^{p})\leq a-b\leq C(a^{p}-b^{p})\quad\text{for any }S(p,0,0)\leq b\leq a\leq kS(p,0,0).

Now it suffices to estimate

\left(|\nabla_{r}U(\cdot+\rho)|^{2}+k^{2}r^{-2}|\nabla_{\theta}U(\cdot+\rho)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}-\left(|\nabla_{r}U(\cdot+\rho)|^{2}+r^{-2}|\nabla_{\theta}U(\cdot+\rho)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}

pointwisely. Without loss of generality, we assume
\rho=(|\rho|,0,\cdots,0)
. From (
2.3
), we get that

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\theta}U(x+\rho)|^{2}=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{j=2}^{n}(x_{1}\partial_{j}U-x_{j}\partial_{1}U)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,C_{2}|\rho|^{2}\sum\limits_{j=2}^{n}|x_{j}|^{2}|x+\rho|^{\frac{4-2p}{p-1}}\left(1+|x+\rho|^{\frac{p}{p-1}}\right)^{-\frac{2n}{p}}.

Since the upper bounds and the decay rates of
|\nabla_{r}U(\cdot+\rho)|
and
\frac{|\nabla_{\theta}U(\cdot+\rho)|}{|\rho|r}
can be controlled uniformly, our claim follows directly from the mean value theorem.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

By homogeneity, we suppose

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-qb}|u|^{q}dx=1.

As with the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we obtain

\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-pa}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}-S(p,a,a)\geq c\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,0,0}}{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla v\rVert}_{L^{p}}^{\max\{2,p\}}.

Note that we cannot transform
\bar{u}
back to
u
directly due to the fact that
\mathcal{M}_{p,0,0}
is translation-invariant. Define
\mathcal{M}^{R}_{p,0,0}
as a subset of
\mathcal{M}_{p,0,0}
, consisting of all radially symmetric functions in
\mathcal{M}_{p,0,0}
.

When the function
u
is
n
-symmetric, using similar arguments as in
[
10
, Lemma 7]
, one can show

\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,0,0}}{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla v\rVert}_{L^{p}}\geq c\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}^{R}_{p,0,0}}{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla v\rVert}_{L^{p}}.

To be specific, take
v_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{p,0,0}^{R}
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,0,0}}{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla v\rVert}_{L^{p}}={\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla v_{0}(\cdot+x_{0})\rVert}_{L^{p}}.

Then we have

\displaystyle{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla v_{0}\rVert}_{L^{p}}\leq

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla v_{0}(\cdot+x_{0})\rVert}_{L^{p}}+{\lVert\nabla v_{0}(\cdot+x_{0})-\nabla v_{0}\rVert}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla v_{0}(\cdot+x_{0})\rVert}_{L^{p}}+C{\lVert\nabla v_{0}(\cdot+x_{0})-\nabla v_{0}(\cdot-x_{0})\rVert}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla v_{0}(\cdot+x_{0})\rVert}_{L^{p}}+C{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla v_{0}(\cdot-x_{0})\rVert}_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla v_{0}(\cdot+x_{0})\rVert}_{L^{p}}.

The second inequality above is a simple consequence of the mean value theorem. Therefore, (
1.7
) holds with optimal exponent
\max\{2,p\}
.

For a general function
u
, we need to use some careful arguments. Fix a number
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
which will be determined later. From the concentration compactness principle (see for example
[
34
,
30
]
), there exists a positive number
\delta<1
such that, when
{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}\rVert}_{L^{p}}<(1+\delta)S(p,0,0)
, one has

\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,0,0}}{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla v\rVert}_{L^{p}}\leq\frac{1}{100}S(p,0,0).

To prove (
1.7
), it suffices to consider the case
{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}\rVert}_{L^{p}}<(1+\delta)S(p,0,0)
. Now if

\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,0,0}}{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla v\rVert}_{L^{p}}\geq\varepsilon\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}^{R}_{p,0,0}}{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla v\rVert}_{L^{p}}^{2},

then (
1.7
) holds. If not, then we can find a function
U\in\mathcal{M}^{R}_{p,0,0}
and a vector
\rho\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, such that

{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla U(\cdot+\rho)\rVert}_{L^{p}}\leq\varepsilon{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla U\rVert}_{L^{p}}^{2}

and

{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla U(\cdot+\rho)\rVert}_{L^{p}}<\frac{1}{10}S(p,0,0).

Since
\varepsilon
is small, we have

{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla U(\cdot+\rho)\rVert}_{L^{p}}\leq 2\varepsilon{\lVert\nabla U(\cdot+\rho)-\nabla U\rVert}_{L^{p}}^{2},

and

\frac{1}{2}{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla U\rVert}_{L^{p}}\leq{\lVert\nabla U(\cdot+\rho)-\nabla U\rVert}_{L^{p}}.

Now let us apply Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.2
with

\displaystyle A=\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(|\nabla_{r}U(\cdot+\rho)|^{2}+k^{2}r^{-2}|\nabla_{\theta}U(\cdot+\rho)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}r^{n-1}drd\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

\displaystyle B=S(p,0,0){\lVert U\rVert}_{L^{\frac{np}{n-p}}},

\displaystyle D=\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\left(|\nabla_{r}\bar{u}|^{2}+k^{2}r^{-2}|\nabla_{\theta}\bar{u}|^{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{2}}r^{n-1}drd\theta\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

\displaystyle E=S(p,0,0){\lVert\bar{u}\rVert}_{L^{\frac{np}{n-p}}}=S(p,0,0),

\displaystyle F=c\left(\frac{{\lVert\nabla U-\nabla U(\cdot+\rho)\rVert}_{L^{p}}}{{\lVert U\rVert}_{L^{\frac{np}{n-p}}}}\right)^{2},

provided
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small. Note that
|A-D|+|B-E|
can be estimated directly by the Minkowski inequality. Therefore one get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|x|^{-pa}|\nabla u|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}-S(p,a,a)=

\displaystyle\,c\left(\frac{D}{E}-1\right)\geq\,cF

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,c{\lVert\nabla U(\cdot+\rho)-\nabla U\rVert}_{L^{p}}^{2}\geq\,c{\lVert\nabla\bar{u}-\nabla U\rVert}_{L^{p}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,c\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,a,a}}{\lVert|x|^{-a}(\nabla u-\nabla v)\rVert}_{L^{p}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,c\inf_{v\in\mathcal{M}_{p,a,a}}{\lVert|x|^{-a}(\nabla u-\nabla v)\rVert}_{L^{p}}^{\max\{4,2p\}}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.11
.

(i)
Without loss of generality, we assume

\limsup_{l\rightarrow+\infty}K(p_{l},a_{l},b_{l})=\lim_{l\rightarrow+\infty}K(p_{l},a_{l},b_{l})=:K_{0}.

For any nonzero function

Proof of Corollary

.

) and (

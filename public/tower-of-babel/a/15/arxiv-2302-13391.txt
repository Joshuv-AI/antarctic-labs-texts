Adiabatic compactness for holomorphic curves with boundary on nearby Lagrangians
Dylan Cant, Daren Chen

Abstract
. In the paper [ Flo89 ] , Floer established a connection between holomorphic strips with boundary on a Lagrangian L L and a small Hamiltonian push-off L f L_{f} , and gradient flow lines for the function f f . The present paper studies the compactness theory for holomorphic curves u n u_{n} whose boundary components lie on Hamiltonian perturbations L n 1 , … , L n N L_{n}^{1},\dots,L^{N}_{n} of a fixed Lagrangian L L , where each sequence of nearby Lagrangians L n j L^{j}_{n} converges to L L as n → ∞ n\to\infty . Generalizing earlier work of Oh, Fukaya, Ekholm, and Zhu, we prove that the limit of a sequence of such holomorphic maps is a configuration consisting of holomorphic curves with boundary on L L joined by gradient flow lines connecting points on the boundary of holomorphic pieces. The key new result is an exponential estimate analyzing the interface between the holomorphic parts and the gradient flow line parts.

Outline of paper

The overall strategy of the paper is rather straightforward: prove that low energy holomorphic strips with adiabatic boundary conditions converge uniformly to broken Morse flow lines; paying close attention to the endpoints of the interval. Roughly speaking, the convergence of a holomorphic strip to a flow line is only seen after rescaling, i.e., the reparametrization
v_{n}(s,t)=u_{n}(s/\epsilon_{n},t/\epsilon_{n})
is what converges. Since
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
, gradient bounds on
u_{n}
do not imply gradient bounds on
v_{n}
and hence we cannot apply Arzelà-Ascoli to
v_{n}
. The technical core of the paper, §
4
, concerns a delicate exponential decay estimate needed to bound the derivatives of
v_{n}
; the hardest place to control
v_{n}
is at the ends of the strip, which is where the limit flow line will connect to the holomorphic curves in the limiting configuration.

In §
2
, we review the Levi-Civita coordinate system for cotangent bundles, and in §
3
we review the basic a priori estimates for holomorphic curves; both sections can be safely skipped by any experts.

In §
5
, we give a fairly detailed account of the compactness theory for Riemann surfaces, essentially to make precise the informal idea that, in order to analyze the compactness phenomena of holomorphic curves, one only needs to understand the behaviour of strips/cylinders (necks) with low energy and large modulus. These low energy regions are analyzed in §
6
.

\int_{D(z,r)}e_{n}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t<\epsilon^{\prime}\implies e_{n}(z)\leq\frac{c^{\prime}}{r^{2}}\int_{D(z,r)}e_{n}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t.

Finally, using the fact that the ratio
g_{x,\infty}(v,v)/g_{x}(v,v)
is bounded for
x\in K
and
v\neq 0
, we conclude from (
20
) the desired result (
19
).

The exposition of the argument in
[
MS12
, §4.3]
is very clear, and so we simply refer to them for the rest of the details.

3.2.
Elliptic bootstrapping to bound higher derivatives

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(W,J)
be an almost complex manifold with a Riemannian metric
g
, and let
L
be a compact totally real submanifold. Let
K\subset W
be a compact neighbourhood of
L
.

Suppose that
J_{n}
is a sequence of almost complex structures which converge to
J
in the
C^{\infty}
topology, and
L_{n}\subset K
is a sequence of
J_{n}
-totally real submanifolds which converge to
L
. Fix
r>0
. If
u_{n}:D(z_{n},r)\cap\bar{\mathbb{H}}\to(K,L_{n})
is a sequence of
J_{n}
-holomorphic curves then

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{D(z_{n},r)\cap\bar{\mathbb{H}}}\left|\partial_{s}u_{n}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\wedge\mathrm{d}t=0\implies\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\nabla^{\ell}\mathrm{d}u_{n}(z_{n})\right|=0,

for all
\ell\geq 0
.

The proof of Lemma
3.3
is given in §
A
. Briefly, the argument uses the mean value property to conclude that
\left|\mathrm{d}u_{n}(z_{n})\right|
is bounded, and then uses elliptic estimates and bootstrapping to bound the higher derivatives.

We will also find the following corollaries of Lemma
3.3
useful.

Corollary 3.4
.

Assume the setup of Lemma
3.3
. Pick constants
M>0
,
r>0
, and an integer
\ell\geq 0
. Then there is a constant
\epsilon_{\ell}=\epsilon_{\ell}(M,r)>0
so that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\int_{D(z_{n},r)\cap\bar{\mathbb{H}}}\left|\partial_{s}u_{n}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}s\wedge\mathrm{d}t\leq\epsilon_{\ell}\implies\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\nabla^{\ell}\mathrm{d}u_{n}(z_{n})\right|\leq M.

Proof
.

Suppose not, then we can find a (diagonal) sequence of curves with energy tending to zero but with
\left|\nabla^{\ell}\mathrm{d}u_{n}(z_{n})\right|>M
for all
n
, contradictng Lemma
3.3
.

Corollary 3.5
.

Assume the setup of Lemma
3.3
. Then for all
\ell\geq 1
we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in D(z_{n},r)}\left|\partial_{s}u_{n}(z)\right|<\infty\implies\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\nabla^{\ell}\mathrm{d}u_{n}(z_{n})\right|<\infty.

Proof
.

Suppose not. Then we can find
u_{n},z_{n},r,C,\ell
and
\delta_{n}\to 0
so that
\left|\partial_{s}u_{n}(z)\right|
remains bounded by
C
for all
z\in D(z_{n},r)
but

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\delta_{n}^{\ell+1}\left|\nabla^{\ell}\mathrm{d}u_{n}(z_{n})\right|>0.

Indeed this holds whenever
\left|\nabla^{\ell}\mathrm{d}u_{n}(z_{n})\right|
is unbounded. Then we set

w_{n}(z)=u_{n}(z_{n}+\delta_{n}z),

so that
w_{n}(z)
is defined on
D(\delta_{n}^{-1}r)
. Clearly the first derivative of
w_{n}
is bounded by
\delta_{n}C
, which tends to
0
. On the other hand
\nabla^{\ell}\mathrm{d}w_{n}(0)
does not tend to
0
, by our assumption on
\delta_{n}^{\ell+1}\nabla^{\ell}\mathrm{d}u_{n}(z_{n})
.

For
n
large enough,
D(1)\subset D(\delta_{n}^{-1}r)
, and clearly the energy of
w_{n}
on
D(1)
is bounded by the energy of
u_{n}
on
D(z_{n},\delta_{n}r)
which tends to zero. Thus we can apply Lemma
3.3
to conclude that
\left|\nabla^{\ell}\mathrm{d}w_{n}(0)\right|\to 0
, contradicting our earlier deduction. This completes the proof.

4.
Exponential estimates for low-energy holomorphic strips.

Throughout this section, we fix the following data:

(i)

a symplectic manifold
(W,\omega,J)
with a tame almost complex structure
J
,

(ii)

a compact Lagrangian
L\subset W
, so that
\omega(-,J-)
is a Riemannian metric along
L
,

(iii)

a compact neighbourhood

of
L
, and

(iv)

a symplectic tubular neighbourhood

, with the property that the pullback

agrees with
J_{0}
along
L
for some Riemannian metric

on
L
. See §
2.2
for the definition of

. Recall that the induced metric
g
on
L
is determined by

.

The results of §
2.2.6
guarantee that there always exists a tubular neighbourhood satisfying (iv) given the data of (i), (ii), (iii).

Recall that if
L^{\prime}
is any submanifold
C^{1}
close enough to
L
then (by definition)
L^{\prime}
can be expressed as the graph of some one-form
\mathfrak{a}\in\Gamma(T^{*}L)
. We introduce the following notation:

Note that

can be considered as a submanifold of
K
using the tubular neighbourhood

if
\mathfrak{a}
is sufficiently small.

The main result of this section is an exponential

-bound for holomorphic strips whose derivatives satisfy
\left|\mathrm{d}u_{n}\right|\to 0
and which take boundary values on Lagrangians

,

which converge to

. The idea is similar to the one in Floer’s paper, §
1.1
. Our weakened assumptions on the complex structure lead to additional terms in the calculation, but using the low energy assumption and the bootstrapping Lemma

, we show they do not make a significant difference in the limit. The exponential bound is essential in proving the holomorphic strips converging to broken Morse flow lines, as will be shown in the proof of Lemma
6.3
.

4.1.

To set-up the notation, recall that for a sequence of maps

tend to zero. Since
K_{n}
tends to zero from
(a)
, we conclude that
\theta_{n}
tends to zero, as desired.

Proof

(of Lemma
4.2
)
.

This is inspired by the proof of
[
RS01
, Lemma 3.1]
. To simplify the notation, introduce
R^{\prime}=R+0.75
. Define
\beta:[-R^{\prime},R^{\prime}]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
to be:

\beta(s)=\gamma(s)-\frac{K}{\delta^{2}}\epsilon^{2}-Ae^{-\delta(R^{\prime}-s)},

where
A=\max\left\{(2\delta)^{-1}(\dot{\gamma}(R^{\prime})+\delta\gamma(R^{\prime})),0\right\}
. The reason for this choice of
A
will be made apparent momentarily.

Compute

\ddot{\beta}(s)=\ddot{\gamma}(s)-\delta^{2}Ae^{-\delta(R^{\prime}-s)},

and hence

\ddot{\beta}(s)-\delta^{2}\beta(s)=\ddot{\gamma}(s)-\delta^{2}\gamma(s)+K\epsilon^{2}\geq 0.

This implies

\dfrac{d}{ds}\big(e^{-\delta s}(\dot{\beta}+\delta\beta)\big)\geq 0

In particular, if
\dot{\beta}(R^{\prime})+\delta\beta(R^{\prime})\leq 0
, then
\dot{\beta}(s)+\delta\beta(s)\leq 0
for all
s\in\left[-R^{\prime},R^{\prime}\right]
.

By our choice of
A
, we have

\dot{\beta}(R^{\prime})+\delta\beta(R^{\prime})=\dot{\gamma}(R^{\prime})+\delta\gamma(R^{\prime})-\frac{K}{\delta}\epsilon^{2}-2\delta A=-\frac{K}{\delta}\epsilon^{2}\leq 0,

so
\dot{\beta}(s)+\delta\beta(s)\leq 0
for all
s\in\left[-R^{\prime},R^{\prime}\right]
.

It follows that
e^{\delta s}\beta(s)
is decreasing on
\left[-R^{\prime},R^{\prime}\right]
. Hence

\beta(s)\leq e^{-\delta(s+R^{\prime})}\beta(-R^{\prime})\leq e^{-\delta(s+R^{\prime})}\gamma(-R^{\prime}).

This implies that:

(33)

\gamma(s)\leq B_{1}e^{-\delta(R^{\prime}+s)}+B_{2}e^{-\delta(R^{\prime}-s)}+B_{3}K\epsilon^{2},

where
B_{1}=\gamma(-R^{\prime})
,
B_{2}=A\leq(2\delta)^{-1}\left|\dot{\gamma}(R^{\prime})+\delta\gamma(R^{\prime})\right|,
and
B_{3}=\delta^{-2}.

We estimate the integral
\int_{s-0.5}^{s+0.5}\alpha(s)ds
. As
\gamma\geq 0
, we have
\alpha\leq\ddot{\gamma}+K\epsilon^{2}
, so

\int_{s-0.5}^{s+0.5}\alpha(s)ds\leq\dot{\gamma}(s+0.5)-\dot{\gamma}(s-0.5)+K\epsilon^{2}.

To bound
\dot{\gamma}(s+0.5)
, notice the bound on
\gamma
by the previous analysis, and since
\ddot{\gamma}\geq\delta^{2}\gamma+\alpha-K\epsilon^{2}\geq-K\epsilon^{2}
,
\dot{\gamma}
is almost an increasing function, up to order
\epsilon^{2}
.
More precisely, for
s^{\prime}>s
, we have:

\dot{\gamma}(s^{\prime})-\dot{\gamma}(s)=\int_{s}^{s^{\prime}}\ddot{\gamma}(x)dx\geq-K(s^{\prime}-s)\epsilon^{2}.

Integrating both sides against
s^{\prime}
:

\int_{s^{\prime}=s}^{s+0.25}\dot{\gamma}(s^{\prime})ds^{\prime}-\int_{s^{\prime}=s}^{s+0.25}\dot{\gamma}(s)ds^{\prime}\geq\int_{s^{\prime}=s}^{s+0.25}-K(s^{\prime}-s)\epsilon^{2}ds^{\prime}=\dfrac{-K}{32}\epsilon^{2},

which yields:

\gamma(s+0.25)-\gamma(s)-\frac{1}{4}\dot{\gamma}(s)\geq\dfrac{-K}{32}\epsilon^{2}.

Rearranging:

\displaystyle\dot{\gamma}(s)

\displaystyle\leq 4\gamma(s+0.25)-4\gamma(s)+\dfrac{K}{8}\epsilon_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\gamma(s+0.25)+\dfrac{K}{8}\epsilon^{2}.

Switching the roles of
s
and
s^{\prime}
and integrating
s^{\prime}
from
s-0.25
to
s
, we obtain the inequality in the other direction:

\dot{\gamma}(s)\geq-4\gamma(s-0.25)-\dfrac{K}{8}\epsilon^{2}.

Therefore:

\displaystyle\int_{s-0.5}^{s+0.5}\alpha(s)ds

\displaystyle\leq\dot{\gamma}(s+0.5)-\dot{\gamma}(s-0.5)+K\epsilon^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\gamma(s+0.75)+4\gamma(s-0.75)+\dfrac{5K}{4}\epsilon^{2}.

Appealing to (
33
), conclude:

\gamma(s)+\int_{s-0.5}^{s+0.5}\alpha(s)ds\leq 9B_{1}e^{-\delta(R+s)}+9B_{2}e^{-\delta(R-s)}+(9B_{3}+\frac{5}{4})K\epsilon^{2}.

The expressions for
B_{i}
in (
33
) imply that we can set
C_{i}
as in (
32
). This completes the proof.

4.2.
Bootstrapping
W^{1,2}

C^{1}
estimates

The
W^{1,2}
estimates on
\tilde{P}_{n}
will be upgraded to obtain

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume the setup and hypotheses of Lemma
4.1
. Recall that this involved a choice of
\epsilon_{n}\to 0
and convergent sequences of
1

\mathfrak{a}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{b}_{n}
, a sequence
J_{n}\to J
so that
J_{n}|_{L}=J|_{L}
and supposed a sequence of holomorphic maps

u_{n}:[-R_{n}-1,R_{n}+1]\times[0,1]\to K

with boundary on

and
L_{\epsilon_{n}\mathfrak{b}_{n}}
whose first derivatives converge to

.

The conclusions of Lemma

\kappa_{n}=\kappa_{n}(u_{n},K,J,\mathfrak{a}_{n},\mathfrak{b}_{n})
so that

and

\left|\tilde{P}_{n}(s,t)\right|+\left|\nabla_{s}\tilde{P}_{n}(s,t)\right|+\left|\nabla_{t}\tilde{P}_{n}(s,t)\right|\leq\kappa_{n}(e^{-d(R_{n}+s)}+e^{-d(R_{n}-s)}+\epsilon_{n}),

for all
(s,t)\in[-R_{n},R_{n}]\times[0,1]
and
n
sufficiently large. Here
2d=\delta
is the constant from Lemma
4.1
.

Proof
.

This proof mainly uses the elliptic estimates for the Levi-Civita Laplacian proved in Lemma
2.8
:

(34)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{k+1,2}(\Omega(r))}\leq C_{k}(\left\lVert\Delta^{\mathrm{lc}}\tilde{P}_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{k-1,2}(\Omega(r+\delta))}+\left\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{k,2}(\Omega(r+\delta))}).

Together with the exponential estimates on the
W^{1,2}
-norm of
\tilde{P}_{n}
developed in Lemma
4.1
, use (
36
) to estimate the
W^{3,2}
-norm of
\tilde{P}_{n}
. Then applying the Sobolev embedding theorem (see Lemma
A.1
) gives the desired

estimates on
\tilde{P}_{n}
.

The most important calculations needed to apply (
34
) were performed in the proof of Lemma
4.1
. In particular, equation (
29
) can be rearranged to obtain

(35)

\Delta^{\mathrm{lc}}\tilde{P}_{n}=\epsilon_{n}R^{10}_{n}+\tilde{P}_{n}\cdot R^{11}_{n}+\nabla_{s}\tilde{P}_{n}\cdot R^{3}_{n}+\nabla_{t}\tilde{P}_{n}\cdot R^{8}_{n},

where the remainder terms and their derivatives converge to zero as
n\to\infty
, uniformly for
(s,t)\in[-R_{n},R_{n}]\times[0,1]
.

Now we give the details of the proof. Consider a sequence of
J
-holomorphic curves
u_{n}
satisfying the conditions of Lemma
4.1
. For
n
large enough, we can assume
u_{n}
lies entirely in the tubular neighbourhood
N
, and write
u_{n}=(Q_{n},P_{n})
in the Levi-Civita coordinates. Define the perturbed maps
\tilde{P}_{n}
as in equation (
22
). Note that the perturbation is along the fiber direction of
T^{*}L
, so
\tilde{P}_{n}
is also a section of
Q^{*}_{n}T^{*}L
, and we will apply Lemma
2.8
to
Q_{n}
and
\tilde{P}_{n}
.

Choose any point
(s_{0},t_{0})\in[-R_{n},R_{n}]\times[0,1]
. Define the rectangle

\Omega(r)=[-s_{0}-r,s_{0}+r]\times[0,1].

around
(s_{0},t_{0})
.

Take
r=0.25
,
\delta=0.25
,
k=1
and
N=1
in Lemma
2.8
, defining constants
c_{1}=c(r,\delta)
and
C_{1}=C(r,\delta,k,N)
. Then take
r=0.125
,
\delta=0.125
,
k=2
and
N=2
, defining
c_{2}
and
C_{2}
. Take
c=\mathrm{min}(c_{1},c_{2})
.

By our assumption that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left\lVert\mathrm{d}u_{n}\right\rVert_{C^{0}}=0
and the a priori estimates from Corollary
3.4
, we have

\left|\partial_{s}Q_{n}\right|+\left|\partial_{t}Q_{n}\right|\leq c\text{ and }\sum_{\ell=0}^{2}\left|\nabla^{\ell}\partial_{s}Q_{n}\right|+\sum_{\ell=0}^{2}\left|\nabla^{\ell}\partial_{t}Q_{n}\right|\leq 1

on the strip
[-R_{n}-1,R_{n}+1]\times[0,1]
for
n
sufficiently large. The a priori estimates in Lemma
2.8
yield:

(36)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{2,2}(\Omega(0.25))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(\left\lVert\Delta^{\mathrm{lc}}\tilde{P}_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{0,2}(\Omega(0.5))}+\left\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{1,2}(\Omega(0.5))})

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{3,2}(\Omega(0.125))}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(\left\lVert\Delta^{\mathrm{lc}}\tilde{P}_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{1,2}(\Omega(0.25))}+\left\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{2,2}(\Omega(0.25))}),

for
n
large enough. The right hand side of (

) can be estimated by the previous
W^{1,2}
bound of
\tilde{P}_{n}
as follows.

For the term
\left\lVert\Delta^{\mathrm{lc}}\tilde{P}_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{0,2}(\Omega(0.5))}

) implies

\left\lVert\Delta^{\mathrm{lc}}\tilde{P}_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{0,2}(\Omega(0.5))}\leq K^{\prime}_{n}(\epsilon_{n}+\left\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{1,2}(\Omega(0.5))}),

where

It follows from the proof of Lemma

For the second term

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{1,2}(\Omega(0.5))}

\displaystyle=\big(\int_{s^{\prime}=s-0.5}^{s+0.5}\int_{t=0}^{1}\left|\tilde{P}_{n}\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla_{s}\tilde{P}_{n}\right|^{2}+\left|\nabla_{t}\tilde{P}_{n}\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}s^{\prime}\big)^{1/2}

where

Using equation (
36

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{2,2}(\Omega(0.25))}

\displaystyle\left\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}\right\rVert_{W^{3,2}(\Omega(0.125))}

5.
Compact partial domains

In this section we develop the theory of
compact partial domains
as the primary tool to analyze the degeneration of Riemann surfaces. The first three sections are concerned with the convergence of domains, while in §
5.4
, we consider domains
\Sigma_{n}
equipped with smooth maps
u_{n}:\Sigma_{n}\to W
. In §
5.8
, we tie all the theory together and complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
, modulo the technical arguments postponed to §
6
; in particular, the analysis involving the convergence to broken flow lines is postponed to §
6.2
, where we make use of the estimates proved in §
4
. The reader who wishes to skip to the part requiring the exponential estimates should refer to Lemma
6.3
.

See
[
Gro85
,
Hum97
,
BEH+03
,
MS12
,
KL06
,
MT09
,
Abb14
]
for related discussion on compactness in the space of Riemann surfaces and holomorphic curves.

Definition 5.1
.

A
compact partial domain
is a connected Riemann surface
\Sigma
with corners together with a decomposition of boundary into two pieces
\partial\Sigma\cup\partial C
, so that:

(i)

\partial\Sigma\cap\partial C
forms the finite set of corners,

(ii)

each component of
\partial C
can be covered by a neighbourhood
U
conformally identified with
[0,r]\times S
, where
S=[0,1]
or
S=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
,

(iii)

\partial C\cap U
is mapped on to
\left\{r\right\}\times S
,

(iv)

if
S=[0,1]
,
\partial\Sigma\cap U
is mapped onto
[0,r]\times\left\{0,1\right\}
,

(v)

if
S=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
,
\partial\Sigma\cap U=\emptyset
.

For example, if
\Sigma_{\infty}
is a punctured Riemann surface, and we remove open ends
C
around each puncture, then
\Sigma:=\Sigma_{\infty}\mathbin{\hbox to5.38pt{\vbox to4.38pt{\pgfpicture\makeatletter\hbox{\hskip 0.2pt\lower 1.7918pt\hbox to0.0pt{\lxSVG@begingroup@{_scopebegin} \lxSVG@begingroup@{stroke} \lxSVG@begingroup@{fill} \lxSVG@setlinewidth{\the\pgflinewidth}\lxSVG@begingroup@{stroke-width} \lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nullfont\lxSVG@begingroup@{_scopebegin} \lxSVG@closescope \hbox to0.0pt{\lxSVG@begingroup@{_scopebegin} {{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces}{{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces}}{}{{}}{}

is a compact partial domain. Clearly every compact partial domain arises in this fashion, via an obvious completion operation.

5.1.
Notions of convergence for compact partial domains

In this section we define a notion of
strong convergence
for a sequence compact partial domains
(\Sigma_{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n})
, where
\mathfrak{e}_{n}
are ends; see §
5.1.2
. First we explain a more naive notion of weak convergence.

5.1.1.
Weakly convergent sequences of domains

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\Sigma_{\infty}
be a punctured Riemann surface, with punctures
\Gamma
. A weakly convergent sequence of domains is the data of a sequence
(\Sigma_{n},\psi_{n})
where
\Sigma_{n}
is a compact partial domain and
\psi_{n}:\Sigma_{n}\to\Sigma_{\infty}
is an embedding so that:

(a)

\psi_{n}^{-1}(\partial\Sigma_{\infty})=\partial\Sigma_{n}
.

(b)

the compact sets
\psi_{n}(\Sigma_{n})
exhaust
\Sigma_{\infty}
,

(c)

\psi_{n,*}(j_{n})
converges to
j_{\infty}
on compact subsets of
\Sigma_{\infty}
.

The limit
\Sigma_{\infty}
is not uniquely determined by
\Sigma_{n}
, however it will be unique if we impose the following condition:

Definition 5.3
.

Let
(\Sigma_{n},\psi_{n})
and
(\Sigma_{n},\psi_{n}^{\prime})
be two weakly convergent sequences (with the same
\Sigma_{n}
). We say that the sequences are
relatively proper
if

\psi_{n}(z_{n})\to\infty\iff\psi_{n}^{\prime}(z_{n})\to\infty

for all sequences
z_{n}\in\Sigma_{n}
.

Definition 5.4
.

A punctured surface
\Sigma
is
semi-stable
if there are no non-constant holomorphic maps
S^{2}\to\Sigma
or
(D,\partial D)\to(\Sigma,\partial\Sigma)
.

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose
\Sigma
and
\Sigma^{\prime}
are the limits of two relatively proper sequences with the same underlying
\Sigma_{n}
, and
\Sigma,\Sigma^{\prime}
are semi-stable. Consider
f_{n}:=\psi_{n}^{\prime}\psi_{n}^{-1}
as a sequence which is eventually defined on any compact set. Then, after passing to a subsequence,
f_{n}
converges to a biholomorphism
f:\Sigma\to\Sigma^{\prime}
.

Proof
.

The relatively proper assumption implies that this sequence is uniformly proper, in the sense that if
f_{n}(z_{n})\in\Sigma^{\prime}
diverges to
\infty
, then
z_{n}\in\Sigma
must be also be diverging to
\infty
.

In particular, if
K
is a compact set in
\Sigma
, then
f_{n}(K)
remains in some compact set
K^{\prime}
(independent of
n
). Moreover, for any choice of global metrics, the derivative of
f_{n}
will be uniformly bounded on
K
, otherwise we would conclude by a bubbling argument the existence of a holomorphic sphere or disk in
\Sigma^{\prime}
(contradicting semi-stability). Here we use that
f_{n}
is approximately holomorphic
K\to K^{\prime}
, by assumption on
\psi_{n},\psi_{n}^{\prime}
.

After passing to a subsequence,
f_{n}
converges in
C^{0}_{\mathrm{loc}}
by Arzelà-Ascoli to a limit
f:\Sigma\to\Sigma^{\prime}
. The usual elliptic regularity arguments imply that the convergence is actually in
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
, and hence
f
is holomorphic. The same argument proves that
f_{n}^{-1}
converges to a holomorphic map which must be
f^{-1}
. Thus
f
is a biholomorphism, as desired.

5.1.2.
Markings and ends

The data
\mathfrak{e}
of a collection of holomorphic embeddings
[0,r_{k}]\times S\to\Sigma
satisfying Definition
5.1
is called a
collection of ends
. Here
r_{k}
is allowed to vary amongst the components of
\partial C
.

A
marked
compact partial domain is the additional data of a metric on
\partial C
. Ends
\mathfrak{e}
which parametrize
\partial C
with constant speed (in time
1
) are said to be compatible with the marking.

Proposition 5.6
.

If
\mathfrak{e},\mathfrak{e}^{\prime}
are ends compatible with a marking, then

\mathfrak{e}(r_{k}-s,t)=\mathfrak{e}^{\prime}(r_{k}^{\prime}-s,t+t_{0})

for all
s\leq\mathrm{min}(r_{k},r_{k}^{\prime})
, and for some
t_{0}
. In words, the ends agree up to a rotation.

Proof
.

This follows by analytic continuation. The inclusions of
\mathfrak{e},\mathfrak{e}^{\prime}
into
\Sigma
, after precomposing with a rotation, if necessary, have infinitely many intersections (as holomorphic maps). Then by analytic continuation they agree on their entire domain.

5.1.3.
Strong convergence of compact partial domains with ends

In order to define a good notion of convergence for a sequence
(\Sigma_{n},\psi_{n})
, we require an additional property which forces the
relatively proper
condition for any other convergent sequence
(\Sigma_{n},\psi_{n}^{\prime})
. The additional property we use depends on the notion of ends from §
5.1.2
.

Definition 5.7
.

A sequence
(\Sigma_{n},\psi_{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n})

strongly converges
to
\Sigma
if
(\Sigma_{n},\psi_{n})
is weakly convergent, and
\mathfrak{e}_{n}
is a collection of ends with conformal modulus tending to
\infty
, so that
\psi_{n}(z_{n})
converges to
\infty
if and only if
z_{n}
eventually enters
\mathfrak{e}_{n}
and converges to
\infty
.

Definition 5.8
.

Two sequences of ends
\mathfrak{e}_{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n}^{\prime}
are
compatible
provided
z_{n}
converges to
\infty
as measured by
\mathfrak{e}_{n}
if and only if it does as measured by
\mathfrak{e}_{n}^{\prime}
, for all sequences
z_{n}\in\Sigma_{n}
.

Proposition 5.9
.

If
(\Sigma_{n},\psi_{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n})
and
(\Sigma_{n},\psi_{n}^{\prime},\mathfrak{e}_{n}^{\prime})
strongly converge, and
\mathfrak{e}_{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n}^{\prime}
are compatible, then
\psi_{n},\psi_{n}^{\prime}
are relatively proper (and hence
\psi_{n}^{\prime}\psi_{n}^{-1}
converges to a biholomorphism
\Sigma\to\Sigma^{\prime}
).

Proof
.

Suppose not. Then we can find
\psi_{n}^{\prime}(z_{n})\to\infty
so that
\psi_{n}(z_{n})
converges to
p
. However, by the strong convergence assumption,
z_{n}
eventually enters
\mathfrak{e}_{n}
and converges to
\infty
, which then implies that
\psi_{n}(z_{n})
converges to
\infty
, contradicting the earlier convergence to
p
.

5.1.4.
Strong convergence and markings

If
\mathfrak{e}_{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n}^{\prime}
are two ends, then the induced change of parametrization of a component of
\partial C_{n}
is the diffeomorphism
\varphi_{n}(t)
so that
\mathfrak{e}_{n}^{\prime}(r_{n}^{\prime},t)=\mathfrak{e}_{n}(r_{n},\varphi_{n}(t))
. Here
r_{n},r_{n}^{\prime}
are the moduli of
\mathfrak{e}_{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n}^{\prime}
at the boundary component under consideration.

Proposition 5.10
.

If
\mathfrak{e}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{e}_{n}^{\prime}
are compatible ends, then
\varphi_{n}
converges to a rotation after passing to a subsequence.

Proof
.

Double
\mathfrak{e}_{n}
and
\mathfrak{e}_{n}^{\prime}
and consider the change of trivialization as a partially defined map:

f_{n}:[-r_{n},r_{n}]\times S\to[-r^{\prime}_{n},r^{\prime}_{n}]\times S,

where
\partial C_{n}
is identified with
0
, and
f_{n}
maps
\left\{0\right\}\times S
onto
\left\{0\right\}\times S
via
\varphi_{n}
.

The compatibility condition guarantees that for finite
s
,
f_{n}
is eventually defined on
[-s,s]\times S
(and holomorphic). This is because otherwise we would have a sequence of points which went to infinity for
\mathfrak{e}_{n}
but not for
\mathfrak{e}^{\prime}_{n}
.

Thus there is
s_{n}\to\infty
so that
f_{n}
is defined on
[-s_{n},s_{n}]\times S
. The derivative of
f_{n}
must be bounded on compact subsets, using the translation invariant metric on the source and target. This is because there is no non-constant map
\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
with finite Hofer energy. Recall that the Hofer energy is a translation invariant energy assigned to holomorphic maps valued in
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, which assigns finite energy to any embedding but infinite energy to any non-constant map
\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
.

In particular,
f_{n}
is proper, in the sense that
f_{n}(z_{n})\to\infty
implies
z_{n}\to\infty
. It follows from the same arguments in Lemma
5.5
that
f_{n}
converges to a proper biholomorphism
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
(after passing to a subsequence).

The only proper biholomorphisms are rotations and translations. Clearly
f_{\infty}
must preserve
\left\{0\right\}\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
by its construction, and hence
\varphi_{\infty}=f_{\infty}|_{\left\{0\right\}\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}
is a rotation, as desired.

5.1.5.
Strong convergence implies convergence of the ends

A strongly convergent sequence has the auxiliary data of a sequence of ends. As we now explain, we can pass to a subsequence so that this sequence of ends converges.

Proposition 5.11
.

Suppose that
(\Sigma_{n},\psi_{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n})
converges strongly to
\Sigma
. Then, after passing to a subsequence,

v_{n}:=\psi_{n}|\mathfrak{e}_{n}([1,r_{n}]\times S)

converges on compact subsets of
[1,\infty)\times S
to a proper holomorphic embedding.

Proof
.

The strong convergence assumption implies
v_{n}|_{[0,r]}
remains bounded independently of
n
, for any fixed
r
. By assumption,
\Sigma
has at least one puncture, and hence is semi-stable. Bubbling analysis then implies that
v_{n}
has bounded derivative. Elliptic regularity theory for approximately holomorphic maps (with
C^{1}
bounds) implies

converges in

to a limit

(after passing to a subsequence). We shrink from
[0,\infty)
to
[1,\infty)
in order to apply the elliptic estimates. See
5.1.7
for more details on elliptic regularity we use.

We claim that
v_{\infty}
is non-constant. Suppose not, and let
v_{\infty}=p
. Then we could find
z_{n}\to\infty
so that
v_{n}(z_{n})
converged to
p
. But this contradicts the definition of strong convergence.

Indeed, more generally, if
v_{\infty}
has
p
as an asymptotic limit point, then we can find
z_{n}\to\infty
so
v_{n}(z_{n})
converges to
p
. The argument is as follows: let
\rho_{k}\to\infty
and, for
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
k(N)
be the largest integer so that for
n\geq N
we have

\mathrm{dist}(v_{\infty}(z),v_{n}(z))<1/k

for all
z\in[1,\rho_{k}]\times S
.

If we define
r_{n}:=\rho_{k(n)}
, then
v_{n}
converges uniformly to
v_{\infty}
on
[1,r_{n}]\times S
.

In particular, if
p
is an asymptotic limit point of
v_{\infty}
, then we can find
z_{n}\to\infty
so
v_{n}(z_{n})
converges to
p
, contradicting the definition of strong convergence.

Thus
v_{\infty}
has no asymptotic limit points, which is equivalent to
v_{\infty}
being proper.

Finally, we prove that
v_{\infty}
is an embedding. If not, then we can find
z^{0}\neq z^{1}
so that
v_{n}(z^{0})
converges to
v_{n}(z^{1})
.

If
\left|\mathrm{d}v_{\infty}(z^{0})\right|>\delta
, then
\left|\mathrm{d}v_{n}(z^{0})\right|>\delta
for

sufficiently large, and hence

cannot enter a ball of fixed radius around
v_{n}(z^{0})
(using the fact that

is known to be injective and
z_{0}\neq z_{1}
). Here we use an arbitrary metric on
\Sigma
.

Thus
\mathrm{d}v_{\infty}(z_{0})=0
. However, by local representation results for holomorphic maps,

must appear as

in local coordinates around
z_{0},v_{\infty}(z_{0})
. Let

be a small enough disk around
z_{0}
, so that
v_{\infty}(\partial D)
is disjoint from
v_{\infty}(z_{0})
. Then for
n
sufficiently large,
v_{n}(\partial D)
is disjoint from
v_{\infty}(z_{0})
. Let
\Delta
be a disk around
v_{\infty}(z_{0})
which contains all of
v_{\infty}(\partial D)
.

Then
v_{n}|_{\partial D}
converges in
\Delta\mathbin{\hbox to5.38pt{\vbox to4.38pt{\pgfpicture\makeatletter\hbox{\hskip 0.2pt\lower 1.7918pt\hbox to0.0pt{\lxSVG@begingroup@{_scopebegin} \lxSVG@begingroup@{stroke} \lxSVG@begingroup@{fill} \lxSVG@setlinewidth{\the\pgflinewidth}\lxSVG@begingroup@{stroke-width} \lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\nullfont\lxSVG@begingroup@{_scopebegin} \lxSVG@closescope \hbox to0.0pt{\lxSVG@begingroup@{_scopebegin} {{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces}{{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces}}{}{{}}{}

5.1.6.
Strong convergence implies convergence of coordinate charts

The next result ensures the existence of convergent families of coordinate charts. We state the results for boundary holomorphic charts (defined on
\Omega(1):=D(1)\cap\bar{\mathbb{H}}
), and leave the interior case to the reader. Let
s_{n}\in\partial\Sigma_{n}
be a sequence of points which remains bounded, as measured by ends
\mathfrak{e}_{n}
, and suppose that
\Sigma_{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n}
converge strongly to
\Sigma
for some
\psi_{n}
. Pick metrics
g_{n}
so that
\psi_{n,*}g_{n}
converges on compact sets to a complete metric
g
.

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
h_{n}:\Omega(1)\to(\Sigma_{n},\partial\Sigma_{n})
be a sequence of holomorphic maps so that
h_{n}(0)=s_{n}
, and
\left|\mathrm{d}h_{n}\right|\in[\delta,\delta^{-1}]
for some
\delta
, as measured by
g_{n}
. Then, after passing to a subsequence,
\psi_{n}\circ h_{n}
converges in

to a holomorphic map

with
\left|\mathrm{d}h_{\infty}\right|\in[\delta,\delta^{-1}]
(here
C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
means on compact subsets disjoint from
\partial D(1)
).

Proof
.

It suffices to prove the existence of subsequences so that
\psi_{n}\circ h_{n}
converges in
C^{\infty}
on
\Omega(r)
, for every
r<1
.

Observe that
h_{n}(\Omega(1))
remains bounded. This is because the
g_{n}
length of a path joining
h_{n}(0)
to
h_{n}(x)
can be bounded by
\delta^{-1}
. Thus, if
h_{n}(x_{n})
converges to
\infty
, there are paths in
\Sigma
whose left endpoint converges but right endpoint diverges to
\infty

with bounded

. This contradicts the definition of completeness.

Since
h_{n}(\Omega(1))
is bounded, we can apply the elliptic regularity result for approximately holomorphic maps given in the next section §

. This completes the proof.

5.1.7.
Elliptic regularity for approximately holomorphic maps

Here we state the elliptic regularity statement used in the previous sections.

Lemma 5.13
.

Suppose that
u_{n}:\Omega(1)\to(W,L,J)
is
bounded
and satisfies:

\partial_{s}u_{n}+J\partial_{t}u_{n}=a_{n}\cdot\mathrm{d}u_{n}+b_{n}

where
a_{n},b_{n}
are smooth (tensor valued) functions on the disk which converge to zero (along with all their covariant derivatives) as
n\to\infty
.

(a)

If
u_{n}
has bounded derivative on
\Omega(2/3)
, then all derivatives of
u_{n}
are bounded on
\Omega(1/2)
. In particular, a subsequence of
u_{n}
converges in
C^{\infty}
on
\Omega(1/2)
.

(b)

If
u_{n}
has an unbounded derivative on
\Omega(2/3)
, then there exists a non-constant bounded holomorphic map
\mathbb{C}\to(W,J)
or
\mathbb{H}\to(W,L,J)
with bounded derivative.

The same result holds with
\Omega
replaced by
D
.

Proof
.

We explain how (a) implies (b). If the derivative was unbounded, then we could apply Hofer’s lemma to conclude a sequence of rescaled approximately holomorphic maps defined on
\Omega(r_{n})
or
D(r_{n})
with
r_{n}\to\infty
,
with bounded first derivative
, and uniformly non-zero derivative at the origin. Then we can apply (a) to conclude all the higher derivatives of this rescaling are bounded on compact subsets. Hence Arzelà-Ascoli implies the rescalings converge to a holomorphic plane or half-plane, with non-zero derivative at the origin.

The proof of (a) follows from standard bootstrapping, see, e.g., Lemma
A.4
.

Remark 5.14
.

Here
(W,J,L)
is an arbitrary almost complex manifold with totally real submanifold
L
. We use any metric on
W
, all of which give equivalent results since
u_{n}
remains uniformly bounded as a function of
n
. The submanifold
L
is required to be properly embedded, i.e., have closed image.

5.2.
Stability of domains

We say that a domain
\Sigma
is
stable
provided its double
D(\Sigma)
has a negative Euler characteristic.

5.2.1.
Ends for unstable domains

An unstable compact partial domain domain is either
\bar{D}(r),\Omega(r)
,
[a,b]\times[0,1]
or
[a,b]\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
, i.e., the compact approximations of
\mathbb{C},\mathbb{H}
,
\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]
or
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
.

The ends of the form
[a,b]\times S
have canonical markings, while the domains
\bar{D}(1)
,
\Omega(1)=\bar{D}(1)\cap\mathbb{H}
have canonical markings up to the action of their automorphism group (which is a certain subgroup of the group of Möbius transformations). We note that
\Omega(1)
has
\partial C
equal to
\partial D(1)\cap\Omega(1)
.

Henceforth, we require that unstable domains are given ends which are compatible with these canonical markings.

5.2.2.
Hyperbolic metrics for stable surfaces

Let
\Sigma_{n}
be a stable compact partial domain, with boundaries
\partial\Sigma_{n},\partial C_{n}
.

Proposition 5.15
.

There is a unique hyperbolic metric in the conformal class prescribed by
j_{n}
so that
\partial\Sigma_{n}
and
\partial C_{n}
are geodesic.

Proof
.

The double of
D(\Sigma_{n})
along
\partial\Sigma_{n}
then has boundary circles obtained by doubling
\partial C_{n}
. We can double across these circles a second time and obtain a compact Riemann surface
D(D(\Sigma_{n}))
. If
\Sigma_{n}
is stable, the uniformization theorem implies that
\mathbb{H}
is the universal cover of
D(D(\Sigma_{n}))
, and this induces a hyperbolic metric in the conformal class prescribed by the complex structure. This metric renders the doubling loci
\partial\Sigma_{n},\partial C_{n}
geodesic, as they are preserved under the doubling involution, which must preserve the hyperbolic metric. This proves existence.

For uniqueness, it suffices to observe that any such metric on
\Sigma_{n}
can be doubled to obtain a hyperbolic metric on
D(D(\Sigma_{n}))
, by a standard gluing operation from hyperbolic geometry. In particular, the uniqueness reduces to the case when
\Sigma_{n}
is closed.

If
\mu_{1}=e^{f}\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{0}
is hyperbolic, then one can show that
\mu_{1}
is hyperbolic if and only if:

\Delta f=\frac{2(e^{f}-1)}{y^{2}},

where
x+iy
is a coordinate chart where
\mu_{0}
appears as
y^{-2}(\mathrm{d}x^{2}+\mathrm{d}y^{2})
. In particular,
f
cannot have a local positive maximum or negative minimum, and hence
f=0
. This proves uniqueness. This above equation for

follows from a tedious computation of the curvature:

\kappa=\frac{g(R(X,Y)Y,X)}{g(X,X)g(Y,Y)}\text{ (for any orthogonal frame $X,Y$)},

and setting the result equal
-1
.

5.2.3.
Hyperbolic ends for stable domains

Let
\Sigma_{n}
be a stable compact partial domain equipped with its canonical hyperbolic structure.

Definition 5.16
.

A sequence of ends
\mathfrak{e}_{n}
is
strongly compatible
with the hyperbolic structure provided that:

\mathfrak{i}(z_{n})\to 0\iff z_{n}\text{ eventually enters $\mathfrak{e}_{n}$ and converges to $\infty$},

where
\mathfrak{i}
is the injectivity radius. This determines an (at most) unique compatibility class of ends.

Definition 5.17
.

A sequence of ends
\mathfrak{e}_{n}
is
weakly compatible
with the hyperbolic structure provided that the outermost end of
\mathfrak{e}_{n}
has injectivity radius converging to zero, while the innermost end of
\mathfrak{e}_{n}
has injectivity radius bounded below.

The following theorem restates the well-studied phenomenon of degenerations of hyperbolic surfaces:

.

induced by

.

The argument is rather technical, implicitly relying on results involving pairs of pants decompositions and hyperbolic hexagons. We defer the reader to

For the claim about the parametrization of

can be doubled and will map larger and larger regions centered around
\partial C_{n}
properly onto the hyperbolic annulus centered on
\partial C_{n}
determined by the marking. This map will converge to a proper holomorphic map

onto

The results in

In general, the set

If

(\Sigma_{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n})

The case of necks is similar, although the argument ruling out the existence of a constant limit is different. Let
\gamma_{n}

Thus we obtain a definition of convergence for sequences
\Sigma_{n}
of stable domains:

Definition 5.19
.

A sequence of stable compact partial domains
\Sigma_{n}

converges in the hyperbolic sense

so that
(\Sigma,\psi_{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n})

\mathfrak{e}_{n}
is weakly compatible with the canonical hyperbolic structure.

The limit
\Sigma

\psi_{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n}

\psi_{n},\psi_{n}^{\prime}
the map
\psi_{n}^{\prime}\psi_{n}^{-1}
converges to a biholomorphism
\Sigma\to\Sigma^{\prime}
.

5.3.3.
Marked points

Marked points are the data of a finite subset
\Theta_{n}\subset\Sigma_{n}
. We do not consider these as punctures. A sequence
(\Sigma_{n},\Theta_{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n})
converges
with marked points
to

so that
\psi_{n}(\Theta_{n})=\Theta

\psi_{n}:\Theta_{n}\to\Theta
is a bijection, and
(\Sigma_{n},\psi_{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n})
strongly converges to
\Sigma

so that

converges to a complete metric on
\Sigma

Lemma 5.22
.

Let
\Sigma_{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n}
be a sequence of compact partial domains which converges strongly to
\Sigma
, and suppose that
\Theta_{n}
is a sequence of marked points which satisfies the following assumptions:

(i)

\Theta_{n}
has bounded cardinality,

(ii)

the marked points remain bounded (as measured by
\mathfrak{e}_{n}
),

(iii)

there is a minimum distance between interior marked points and
\partial\Sigma_{n}
,

(iv)

there is a minimum distance between distinct marked points,

Then we can pass to a subsequence and construct a set
\Theta
so that
\Sigma_{n},\Theta_{n},\mathfrak{e}_{n}
converges to
\Sigma,\Theta
with marked points.

Proof
.

By passing to a subsequence, one ensures that the cardinalities of
\Theta_{n}\cap\partial\Sigma_{n}
and
\Theta_{n}
are constant, and moreover that their images
\psi_{n}(\Theta_{n})\cap\partial\Sigma
and
\psi_{n}(\Theta_{n})
converge to sets
\Theta\cap\partial\Sigma
and
\Theta
.

The “nearest element” map

is injective, and hence is a bijection. By perturbing
\psi_{n}
by a

small map which converges to zero, we may suppose that
\psi_{n}(\Theta_{n})=\Theta

Lemma 5.36
.

Let

Proof
.

For every puncture
\theta

[0,\infty)\times S

Using the cutting lemmas, decompose this end into two pieces

\Sigma_{1}

is low-energy. There are now two cases to consider.

If

\Sigma_{0}

When the incident boundary conditions

Proposition 6.1
.

There exists a constant

(38)

\liminf_{n\to\infty}E(u_{n})>0\implies\liminf_{n\to\infty}\max_{s,t}\left|\mathrm{d}u_{n}(s,t)\right|>0\implies\liminf_{n\to\infty}E(u_{n})>\hbar.

On the other hand:

(39)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\max_{s,t}\left|\mathrm{d}u_{n}(s,t)\right|=0\implies\lim_{n\to\infty}\text{diam}(u_{n}([-R_{n},R_{n}]\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}))=0.

Proof
.

Our argument is similar to the ones
[
MS12
, Chapter 4]

37

\mathrm{d}u_{n}(z_{n})
converges to zero provided

remains a finite distance from the boundary circles
\left\{\pm R_{n}\right\}\times\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}

Start with the second implication of (

so that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|s_{n}\pm R_{n}\right|=\infty

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left|\mathrm{d}u_{n}(s_{n},t_{n})\right|>0

(s,t)\mapsto u_{n}(s_{n}+s,t)
has derivative bounded on compact sets, then
u_{n}
converges in

to a holomorphic cylinder, which has removable singularities and so extends to a holomorphic sphere. This limit is necessarily non-constant since
\left|\mathrm{d}u_{n}(s_{n},t_{n})\right|
is bounded below. On the other hand, if the derivative is unbounded, then the bubbling arguments in the proof Lemma
C.1
constructs a non-constant holomorphic plane, which again extends to a
J

Consider the first implication of (
38

u_{n}
has a subsequence so that
\max_{s,t}\left|\mathrm{d}u_{n}(s,t)\right|

also converges to zero, after potentially passing to another subsequence. In the course of our argument we will also prove (
39

The Arzelà-Ascoli theorem produces a subsequence for which the central loop
u_{n}(0,t)
converges uniformly to a point
p

\epsilon>0
and a primitive
\lambda

\omega
(i.e.,
\omega=\mathrm{d}\lambda
) defined on the closed
\epsilon

\bar{B}(p,\epsilon)

u_{n}
lies inside of

, Chapter 4]
for similar results). Here is the argument: by contradiction, for

Clearly, as the derivative of

Consider the quantity

6.2.2.
Other cases of low energy strips

There are two other cases of low energy strips to consider. The first is when
u_{n}(s,0),u_{n}(s,1)\in L_{n}
and
L_{n}
converges to a Lagrangian
L
. In this case, one can apply Proposition
6.2
with
\mathfrak{a}_{n}=\mathfrak{b}_{n}
. The following result classifies what happens when the first derivative tends to zero:

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
K\subset(W,\omega)
be a compact neighbourhood of a Lagrangian
L
, and let
J_{n}\to J
be a
C^{\infty}
convergent family of almost complex structures, and
L_{n}\to L
be
C^{\infty}
convergent family of Lagrangians. Suppose that
J
is
\omega
-tame and
L
is
J
-totally real. If
u_{n}:[-R_{n},R_{n}]\to K
is a sequence of
J_{n}
-holomorphic curves with
u_{n}(s,0)\in L_{n}
and
u_{n}(s,1)\in L_{n}
. If
\left|\mathrm{d}u_{n}(s,t)\right|\to 0
uniformly in
s,t
, as
n\to\infty
, then
u_{n}
converges uniformly to a point.

Proof
.

The argument is similar to the proof of Proposition
6.1
. As in the proof of Proposition
6.2
, let
\lambda_{n}
be a primitive for
\omega
which vanishes when restricted to
L_{n}
. For instance, we can fix a Weinstein neighbourhood of
L
and take
\lambda_{n}=-\lambda_{\mathrm{can}}+\mathrm{pr}^{*}\beta_{n}
where
L_{n}
is the graph of the
closed
one-form
\beta_{n}\to 0
. Note that
\lambda_{n}
vanishes on
TW|_{L_{n}}
, not just
TL_{n}
.

Similarly to the proof of
6.1
, let

E_{n}(r)=\int_{-r}^{r}\int_{0}^{1}u_{n}^{*}\omega=\int_{-r}^{r}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\partial_{s}u\right|_{J,\omega}^{2}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{1}\gamma_{r,n}^{*}\lambda_{n}-\int_{0}^{1}\gamma_{-r,n}^{*}\lambda_{n},

where
\left|v\right|^{2}_{J,\omega}=\omega(v,J_{n}v)
is strictly positive for
n
sufficiently large. Note also that in our application of Stokes theorem, we use the fact that
u_{n}^{*}\lambda_{n}
vanishes on
both
horizontal boundary components.

We also estimate
\gamma_{r,n}^{*}\lambda_{n}\leq C\left|\gamma^{\prime}_{r,n}(t)\right|^{2}_{g}\mathrm{d}t\leq C^{\prime}\left|\partial_{s}u\right|^{2}_{J,\omega}\mathrm{d}t
.

One can then show that:

E_{n}(r)\leq CE_{n}^{\prime}(r),

and the rest of the proof proceeds exactly as in the proof of
6.1
.

The other case is when the boundary conditions converge to different Lagrangians which have isolated intersections:

Proposition 6.5
.

Let
K
be a compact neighbourhood of
(W,\omega)
containing two Lagrangians
L^{0},L^{1}
, with
L^{0}\cap L^{1}
a finite set. Suppose that
J_{n}\to J
is a convergent sequence of
\omega
-tame almost complex structures, and
u_{n}:[-R_{n},R_{n}]\times[0,1]\to K
is sequence of
J_{n}
-holomorphic strips satisfying the boundary conditions:

u_{n}(s,0)\in L_{n}^{0}\text{ and }u_{n}(s,1)\in L_{n}^{1},

where
L_{n}^{i}\to L^{i}
. Suppose moreover that:

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left|\mathrm{d}u_{n}(-R_{n}+s,t)\right|+\left|\mathrm{d}u_{n}(R_{n}-s,-t)\right|=0\text{ for $(s,t)$ in compact sets.}

Then there is a constant
\hbar>0
depending on
L^{0},L^{1},J,\omega
so that

\limsup_{n\to\infty}E(u_{n})>0\implies\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{s,t}\left|\mathrm{d}u_{n}(s,t)\right|>0\implies\limsup_{n\to\infty}E(u_{n})\geq\hbar.

Secondly, if
\left|\mathrm{d}u_{n}(s,t)\right|\to 0
uniformly in
s,t
as
n\to\infty
, then
u_{n}
converges uniformly to an intersection point
L^{0}\cap L^{1}
.

Proof
.

The proof relies on slightly different techniques from the proofs of Propositions
6.2
and
6.4
, and is a bit more topological in nature.

Suppose that
\left|\mathrm{d}u_{n}(s,t)\right|\to 0
uniformly in
s,t
as
n\to\infty
. Then Arzelà-Ascoli implies that for any sequence
s_{n}\in(-R_{n},R_{n})
a subsequence
v_{n}(s,t)=u_{n}(s_{n}+s,t)
converges uniformly on compact sets to a single point
which must lie on

L^{0}\cap L^{1}
. Since the set of intersection points is isolated, a standard subsequence argument implies that
u_{n}(s,t)
converges uniformly to a single limit point, say
p
. This proves the second part of the proposition, and will be used to establish the first part.

Near
p
, pick
\omega
-primitives
\lambda_{n}\to\lambda_{\infty}
so that
\lambda_{n}|_{L^{0}_{n}}=0
and
\lambda_{n}|_{L^{1}_{n}}=\mathrm{d}f_{n}
with
f_{n}
convergent. Stokes’ theorem yields:

E(u_{n},\omega)\leq\left|f_{n}(u_{n}(R_{n},1))-f_{n}(u_{n}(-R_{n},1))\right|+C\sup_{t}\left|\mathrm{d}u_{n}(\pm R_{n},t)\right|.

Taking the limit
n\to\infty
, conclude that
\limsup_{n\to\infty}E(u_{n})=0
. This proves the contrapositive to the first implication in the proposition. To complete the proof, it remains to prove the implication involving
\hbar
. Define:

\displaystyle{U}_{0}(K,J)

\displaystyle=\left\{\text{nonconstant $J$-holomorphic spheres $S^{2}\to K$}\right\},

\displaystyle{U}_{1}(K,J,L_{0})

\displaystyle=\left\{\text{nonconstant $J$-holomorphic disks $(D(1),\partial D(1))\to(K,L_{0})$}\right\},

\displaystyle{U}_{2}(K,J,L_{1})

\displaystyle=\left\{\text{nonconstant $J$-holomorphic disks $(D(1),\partial D(1))\to(K,L_{1})$}\right\},

\displaystyle{U}_{3}(K,J,L_{0},L_{1})

\displaystyle=\left\{\text{nonconstant $J$-holomorphic strips $\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]\to(K,L_{0},L_{1})$}\right\},

where in the last case we require the top boundary is mapped to
L_{1}
and the bottom boundary to
L_{0}
. Then we set:

\hbar(K,L,J,\omega)=\inf\left\{E(u,\omega)\text{ such that }u\in{U}_{0}\cup{U}_{1}\cup{U}_{2}\cup{U}_{3}\right\}.

It follows from the mean-value property that the infimum over
{U}_{0}\cup{U}_{1}\cup{U}_{2}
is bounded below. To bound the infimum over
{U}_{3}
, argue by contradiction: if not, there is a sequence
u_{n}\in{U}_{3}
whose energy tended to zero. The mean-value theorem still applies in this setting, indeed, on any half-disk of sufficiently small radius
u_{n}
is a half-disk with boundary on
L_{0}
or
L_{1}
. Thus
\left|\mathrm{d}u_{n}(s,t)\right|
converges uniformly to
0
, and, as argued above,
u_{n}
converges uniformly to a point
p\in L^{0}\cap L^{1}
.

Let
n
be large enough so that
u_{n}
remains entirely in a small ball
B
around
p
. We require that
p
is the only intersection point of
L_{0}
and
L_{1}
in the ball. By applying similar arguments to the translations
u_{n,k,\pm}(s,t)=u_{n}(s\pm k,t)
, we conclude that
u_{n,k,\pm}(s,t)
must both converge to
p
as
k\to\infty
, uniformly in
t
. Writing
\omega=\mathrm{d}\lambda
, where
\lambda|_{L_{0}}=0
and
\lambda|_{L_{1}}=\mathrm{d}f
(on
B
), Stokes’ theorem implies that
E(u_{n},\omega)=0
. Thus
u_{n}
was not non-constant, contradicting the definition of
{U}_{3}
. Thus
\hbar>0
.

Finally, if
\limsup_{n}\sup_{s,t}\left|\mathrm{d}u_{n}(s,t)\right|>0
, pick
s_{n}
so that a subsequence of
u_{n}(s_{n}+s,t)
either (i) forms a sphere or disk bubble (as in
C.1
), or (ii) converges on compact sets to a curve in
{U}_{3}
. In either case,
\limsup_{n}E(u_{n},\omega)\geq\hbar
, as desired. This completes the proof.

Appendix A
On elliptic regularity

The proof of Lemma
3.3
requires three analytical prerequisites: the
Sobolev embedding theorem
, the
elliptic estimates for the Laplacian
, and
quadratic estimates for
W^{k,2}
spaces
.

Lemma A.1

(Sobolev embedding theorem)
.

For every bounded Lipshitz domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
there exists constants
c_{2}(\Omega)

c_{1}(\Omega)>0
so that

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{C^{0}(\Omega)}\leq c_{1}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{W^{1,4}(\Omega)}\leq c_{2}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}.

Proof
.

See
[
MS12
, Theorem B.1.11]
for a more general result.

Lemma A.2

(Elliptic estimates for the Laplacian)
.

For every pair of domains
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset\mathbb{H}
with
\bar{\Omega}_{1}\subset\Omega_{2}
there exists a constant
c(\ell,\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2})
so that

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{W^{k+2,2}(\Omega_{1})}\leq c(\left\lVert\Delta u\right\rVert_{W^{k,2}(\Omega_{2})}+\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{W^{k+1,2}(\Omega_{2})}),

for all smooth functions
u:\Omega_{2}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying the Dirichlet boundary conditions
u(\mathbb{R}\cap\Omega_{2})=0
or the Neumann boundary conditions
\partial_{t}u(\mathbb{R}\cap\Omega_{2})=0
.

Proof
.

See
[
RS01
, Lemma C.2]
for a short proof. This also follows from the general
L^{p}
,
p>1
, results in
[
MS12
, Appendix B]
.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipshitz domain. There are constants
Q_{k}=Q_{k}(\Omega)
so that for all smooth functions
f,g
on
\Omega
we have

(48)

\left\lVert fg\right\rVert_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\leq Q_{1}(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{C^{0}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{C^{0}(\Omega)}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}),

and for
k\geq 2
we have

(49)

\left\lVert fg\right\rVert_{W^{k,2}(\Omega)}\leq Q_{k}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{W^{k,2}(\Omega)}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{W^{k,2}(\Omega)}.

Proof
.

The estimate on the
W^{1,2}
norm holds by observing that

\nabla(fg)=(\nabla f)\cdot g+f\cdot\nabla g,

and the estimate
\left\lVert ab\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{C^{0}}
. The estimate on the
W^{2,2}
spaces follows from:

\left\lVert fg\right\rVert_{W^{2,2}(\Omega)}\leq\left\lVert fg\right\rVert_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}+\left\lVert\nabla f\cdot\nabla g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\nabla\nabla f\cdot g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert f\cdot\nabla\nabla g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

We claim that each term can be bounded by some constant times
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{W^{2,2}}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{W^{2,2}}
. The first term can be estimated using the quadratic estimate on the
W^{1,2}
norm (
48
), together with the Sobolev embedding theorem for
C^{0}\subset W^{2,2}
. The last two terms can be estimated using
\left\lVert ab\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq\left\lVert a\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\left\lVert b\right\rVert_{C^{0}}
and the Sobolev embedding theorem. The hard term to estimate is
\left\lVert\nabla f\cdot\nabla g\right\rVert_{L^{2}}
. To do so, use the Sobolev embedding theorem for

One technical result needed is known as “Hofer’s lemma:”

Lemma C.2

(Hofer’s Lemma)
.

Let
d:X\to[0,\infty)
be a continuous function on a complete metric space; and let
\epsilon^{\prime}>0
and
x^{\prime}\in X
. One can find
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon^{\prime}
and
x\in X
so that

(i)
\mathrm{dist}(x,x^{\prime})<2\epsilon^{\prime}
,

(ii)
d(y)\leq 2d(x)
for all
y\in D(x,\epsilon)
.

(iii)
\epsilon d(x)\geq\epsilon^{\prime}d(x^{\prime})
,

Hofer’s lemma was introduced in
[
HV92
, Lemma 3.3]
(moreover, they show that the lemma gives a
characterization
of completeness).

Proof

(of Lemma
C.2
)
.

Let
\epsilon_{n}=2^{-n}\epsilon^{\prime}
, and define a (potentially terminating) sequence
x_{n}
as follows: let
x_{0}=x^{\prime}
, and choose
x_{n+1}\in D(x_{n},\epsilon_{n})
so that
d(x_{n+1})>2d(x_{n})
. If no such
x_{n+1}
exists (i.e., the sequence terminates at
x_{n}
), then we conclude that, for all
y\in D(x_{n},\epsilon_{n})
we have
d(y)\leq 2d(x_{n})
, so (ii) is satisfied with
x=x_{n}
,
\epsilon=\epsilon_{n}
. By construction, we have

\epsilon_{n}d(x_{n})\geq 2\epsilon_{n}d(x_{n-1})=\epsilon_{n-1}d(x_{n-1})\geq\cdots\geq\epsilon_{0}d(x_{0})=\epsilon^{\prime}d(x^{\prime}),

so (iii) would also be satisfied. Since
\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},x_{n})\leq\epsilon_{0}+\epsilon_{1}+\cdots+\epsilon_{n}\leq 2\epsilon_{0}
, we conclude (i) also holds.

Thus the proof of the lemma is reduced to proving that the above recursion terminates. In search of a contradiction suppose it does not converge. Then the sequence
x_{n}
converges, however,
d(x_{n})
is unbounded since
d(x_{n})>2d(x_{n-1})
. This is impossible, and so we complete the proof.

Now returning to the proof, consider

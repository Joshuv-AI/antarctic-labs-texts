Three Factors to Improve Out-of-Distribution Detection
Hyunjun Choi ,, JaeHo Chung, Hawook Jeong, Jin Young Choi

Abstract
In the problem of out-of-distribution (OOD) detection, the usage of auxiliary data as outlier data for fine-tuning has demonstrated encouraging performance. However, previous methods have suffered from a trade-off between classification accuracy (ACC) and OOD detection performance (AUROC, FPR, AUPR). To improve this trade-off, we make three contributions: (i) Incorporating a self-knowledge distillation loss can enhance the accuracy of the network; (ii) Sampling semi-hard outlier data for training can improve OOD detection performance with minimal impact on accuracy; (iii) The introduction of our novel supervised contrastive learning can simultaneously improve OOD detection performance and the accuracy of the network. By incorporating all three factors, our approach enhances both accuracy and OOD detection performance by addressing the trade-off between classification and OOD detection. Our method achieves improvements over previous approaches in both performance metrics.

The performance of detecting OOD samples is measured using metrics like AUROC, FPR, and AUPR, which are calculated based on a scalar score (e.g. MSP score) generated by the neural network. Many efforts have been made to improve OOD detection performance using observer mode

In our work, we pay attention to the approach of using auxiliary data to improve performance on OOD detection through fine-tuning. The first paper using auxiliary data is
Outlier Exposure (OE)
[
11
]
. After that, various types of regularization loss have been proposed subsequently such as OECC
[
25
]
with calibration loss, EnergyOE
[
22
]
with energy score and double hinge regularization loss.
Recently, Balanced EnergyOE
[
5
]
has been proposed to address the imbalance problem in outlier data.
The aforementioned methods use regularization losses for fine-tuning to improve OOD performance over the base model. However, they suffer from a slight decrease in classification accuracy compared to the base model.
That is, there is a trade-off between OOD detection performance and classification accuracy
[
34
]
.

To tackle the trade-off, we propose three factors that enhance both OOD detection performance and classification accuracy at the same time.
The first factor for improving
the trade-off is the performance of the backbone model, as demonstrated in
[
7
]
.
To improve the performance of the backbone model, we adopt self-knowledge distillation
[
8
]
.
By incorporating Softening Targets distillation loss
[
12
]
into the previous OE method, we can enhance both the OOD detection performance and accuracy of classification.

The second factor is to enhance the quantity and quality of outlier data.
One perspective is the ideal number of outlier data used in training, and the other is the difficulty of distinguishing outlier data from in-distribution data, referred to as hardness
[
1
]
.
We define hardness as the maximum softmax probability (MSP) obtained by inferring outlier data on a pre-trained model.
Higher hardness leads to better OOD performance but lower classification accuracy.
Hence, we employ Semi-hard Outlier Sampling to achieve superior OOD performance by sacrificing a marginal amount of accuracy.

The third factor is to improve the quality of representation learning.
We propose a novel representation learning called Outlier-aware Supervised Contrastive Learning, which takes into account outlier data in addition to the traditional supervised contrastive learning (SCL)
[
9
]
.
The key difference of our OSCL from the traditional SCL is the use of outlier data as negative samples to drive the contrastive embeddings away from in-distribution samples, resulting in improved OOD performance.
Another new point is that we introduce the multi-batch transform as a way to boost the effectiveness of contrastive learning in our approach.

By combining all three factors, we are able to significantly enhance the performance of the existing OE as shown in Figure
1
.
In experiments, we validate the effectiveness of our method on both balanced and long-tailed data as follows.
(i) We evaluate our method on various out-of-distribution (OOD) benchmarks including SC-OOD
[
33
]
and MOOD
[
21
]
.
(ii) We demonstrate our method has wide applicability incorporated with existing methods such as OE, OECC, EnergyOE, and Balanced EnergyOE, leading to improved results.
(iii) We verify that our method outperforms existing methods and achieves state-of-the-art results on the SC-OOD benchmark.

Related Works

OOD Detection using Auxiliary Data

Outlier Exposure (OE)
[
11
]
is the first paper that introduces the utilization and definition of auxiliary data as outlier data.
The auxiliary data comprises classes that are non-overlapping with both the in-distribution data and the test out-of-distribution (OOD) data.
OE leverages cross-entropy loss on the in-distribution training data and regularization loss on the outlier training data.
The regularization loss in OE is equivalent to the cross-entropy loss obtained by assigning uniform labels to the outlier data.
Various types of regularization loss have been proposed, and in OECC
[
25
]
, calibration loss is added to the OE regularization loss.
In EnergyOE
[
22
]
, the energy score is defined and a double hinge regularization loss for the energy is proposed.
Recently, in Balanced EnergyOE
[
5
]
, which addresses the imbalance problem in outlier data by precomputing the classwise prior probability of outlier data and using an adaptive loss for each data point. Adaptive double hinge regularization loss for the energy is proposed.

OOD Detection with Observer Mode

Observer mode is a methodology to obtain meaningful measures from a base model without changing its architecture or loss function. There have been several representative measures, including MSP
[
10
]
, Mahalanobis distance
[
19
]
, Gram matrix
[
26
]
, and Free Energy
[
22
]
.
In recent years, various techniques have been proposed to improve OOD performance in observer mode. These include using rectified activation
[
28
]
, considering the norm of gradients as a measure
[
13
]
, and layer sparsification
[
29
]
.

OOD Detection with Contrastive Learning

Contrastive learning has significantly improved unsupervised performance, starting with self-supervised contrastive learning as SIMCLR
[
4
]
. On the other hand, supervised contrastive learning
[
16
]
enhance supervised performance. Additionally, in long-tailed distribution, balanced contrastive learning
[
37
]
has been proposed.
The introduction of contrastive learning has enhanced accuracy and improved OOD detection performance
[
32
]
.
SSD
[
27
]
utilizes self-supervision to improve OOD detection.
Methods like CIDER
[
23
]
have applied repulsion loss between class prototypes, while PASCL
[
30
]
has leveraged partiality and asymmetry in contrastive learning for long-tailed distribution.

3.1
Problem Statements

We can formalize a classifier as
\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{x}):\mathbb{R}^{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{K}

, which maps an input image vector
\mathbf{x}
with
D
dimension to a real-valued vector (logit) with
K
dimension where
K
is the number of classes.
\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{x})=[z_{1}(\mathbf{x}),\dots,z_{K}(\mathbf{x})]
and
\mathbf{y}
is
K
-dimensional one-hot target vector. Probability vector
\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{x})=[p_{1}(\mathbf{x}),\dots,p_{K}(\mathbf{x})]
is computed as
Softmax(\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{x}))
, which satisfies
\mathbf{1}^{T}\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{x})=1
and
\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{x})\geq 0
.
On the other hand,
\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{x}):\mathbb{R}^{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{K}
is a composition of encoder
\overline{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x}):\mathbb{R}^{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{L}
and linear classifer
\mathbf{w}(\overline{\mathbf{x}}):\mathbb{R}^{L}\to\mathbb{R}^{K}
,
i.e.,
\mathbf{z}(\mathbf{x})=(\mathbf{w}\circ\mathbf{g})(\mathbf{x})
.
Final projection layer
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x}):\mathbb{R}^{D}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
for contrastive embedding is defined as
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})=(\mathbf{h}\circ\mathbf{g})(\mathbf{x})
, where we use another linear layer

\mathbf{h}(\overline{\mathbf{x}}):\mathbb{R}^{L}\to\mathbb{R}^{K}

in composition of the feature
\overline{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x})
. Finally,
\mathbf{f}(\mathbf{x})=[f_{1}(\mathbf{x}),\dots,f_{N}(\mathbf{x})]
and
L_{k}
-normalized contrastive feature is defined as
\widetilde{\mathbf{f}}(\mathbf{x})=[\frac{f_{1}(\mathbf{x})}{\lVert f\rVert_{k}},\dots,\frac{f_{N}(\mathbf{x})}{\lVert f\rVert_{k}}]^{T}
. The contrastive embedding
\widetilde{\mathbf{f}}
lies on the unit hypersphere.

The model using outlier data for fine-tuning is trained by a loss function that combines the classification loss
L_{classification}
for in-distribution data and the regularization loss
L_{reg}
for the outlier data. The loss is written as

\small L=L_{classification}+\lambda_{reg}L_{reg}=\hskip 9.24994pt\mathbb{E}_{(\mathbf{x},y)\sim{D_{in}^{train}}}[H(\mathbf{y},\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{x}))]+\lambda_{reg}L_{reg},

(1)

where,
H(\mathbf{a},\mathbf{b})
is cross-entropy between probability distribution
\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{b}
.
L_{reg}
is designed depending on the Outlier Exposure (OE) strategy. In OE
[
11
]
, the regularization loss is used to encourage outlier data to have a uniform probability distribution
\mathbf{u}
.

\small L_{reg}=\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{x}\sim{D_{out}^{train}}}[H(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{x}))].

(2)

The above fine-tuning model has suffered from a trade-off between classification accuracy (ACC) and OOD
detection performance (AUROC,FPR, AUPR)
[
34
]
.
In our work, to address the trade-off, we explore two additional regularization terms: Self-Knowledge Distillation loss (Section
3.2
) and Outlier-aware Supervised Contrastive loss (Section
3.4
), along with a new strategy for Outlier Data Sampling (Section
3.3
).

3.2
Self-Knowledge Distillation

As demonstrated in
[
7
]
, improving the backbone model leads to enhancements in both OOD detection performance and classification accuracy.
By leveraging self-knowledge distillation
[
8
]
within the same model, we enhance the accuracy of the backbone, thereby improving the trade-off between OOD detection performance and accuracy of classification.

The process of self-knowledge distillation comprises two steps. Firstly, the teacher model and the student model load the same pre-trained network. Secondly, with the parameters of the teacher model frozen, the student model employs fixed targets derived from the teacher model as references. By computing the knowledge distillation loss based on these fixed targets, the student model updates its parameters during training.
We adopt the knowledge distillation method of Softening Targets distillation loss
[
12
]
. The formal expression of the loss is as follows:

\small\widetilde{p_{i}}(\mathbf{x};T_{KD})=\frac{exp(z_{i}(\mathbf{x})/T_{KD})}{\sum_{j}exp(z_{j}(\mathbf{x})/T_{KD})},

(3)

\small L_{KD}=T_{KD}^{2}H(\widetilde{\mathbf{p}}^{T}(\mathbf{x};T_{KD}),\widetilde{\mathbf{p}}^{S}(\mathbf{x};T_{KD})).

(4)

We incorporate the knowledge distillation loss with the fine-tuning loss as

\small L=L_{classification}+\lambda_{reg}L_{reg}+\lambda_{KD}L_{KD}.

(5)

As seen in Table
1
, when we use Self-Knowledge Distillation with the previous OE method, our method outperforms OE in OOD detection performance and accuracy. Section
4.1
contains a comprehensive description of the hyperparameter settings. The detailed effect of using Self-Knowledge Distillation loss is discussed in Section
5.1
.

3.4
Outlier-aware Supervised Contrastive Learning

Supervised contrastive learning (SCL) improves classification accuracy by collapsing class representations to the vertices of a regular simplex, as demonstrated in
[
9
]
.
We propose a novel representation learning called Outlier-aware Supervised Contrastive Learning (OSCL), which takes into account outlier data in addition to the traditional supervised contrastive learning (SCL)
[
16
]
.

The key difference of our OSCL from the existing SCL is the use of outlier data as negative samples to drive the contrastive embeddings away from in-distribution samples, resulting in improved OOD performance. The formal expression is described as follows:

\small L_{SC}=\sum_{i\in B_{in}}L_{i},

(7)

\small L_{i}=-\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{|B^{in}_{y_{i}}|>1\}}}{|B^{in}_{y_{i}}|-1}\sum_{p\in B^{in}_{y_{i}}\setminus\{i\}}{\log\frac{exp(\widetilde{\mathbf{f}}_{i}\cdot\widetilde{\mathbf{f}}_{p}/\tau_{sc})}{\sum_{k\in B^{all}\setminus\{i\}}{exp(\widetilde{\mathbf{f}}_{i}\cdot\widetilde{\mathbf{f}}_{k}/\tau_{sc})}}}.

(8)

where we follow the notation of
[
9
]
\quad(\frac{\mathbbm{1}_{\{|B^{in}_{y_{i}}|>1\}}}{|B^{in}_{y_{i}}|-1}
is
0
, when
|B^{in}_{y_{i}}|=1
)

\small L=L_{classification}+\lambda_{reg}L_{reg}+\lambda_{SC}L_{SC}.

(9)

For an instance
\mathbf{x}_{i}
of representation
\widetilde{\mathbf{f}}_{i}
in a total batch
B^{all}
.

B^{all}
is a set of partition
B^{in}
and
B^{out}
, which is a set of in-distribution data and a set of out-of-distribution, respectively.

B_{y_{i}}
is a subset of the set
B
where the labels of the data points in
B_{y_{i}}
match
y_{i}
.

Another new point is that we introduce the multi-batch transform as a way to boost the effectiveness of contrastive learning in our approach.
In the previous SIMCLR
[
4
]
, two augmentation transformations denoted as
t_{1},t_{2}\sim\mathcal{T}
, are applied to the input data
\mathbf{x}
. This generates augmented samples
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{1}=t_{1}(\mathbf{x})
and
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{2}=t_{2}(\mathbf{x})
.
We refer to this approach as "two-batch transform". On the other hand, in supervised contrastive learning, where labels are available, a single-batch transform
t\sim\mathcal{T}
is sufficient.

However, we propose a multi-batch transform that can enhance the effectiveness of contrastive learning. This is a general extension of the two-batch transform, known as the
n
-batch transform. For each augmentation transformation
t_{1},t_{2},\dots,t_{n}\sim\mathcal{T}
, we obtain corresponding augmented samples
\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{k}=t_{k}(\mathbf{x})
for all
k\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}
.
We concatenate these augmented samples to form
\mathbf{x}_{multi-batch}(n)=[\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{1},\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{2},\dots,\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}_{n}]
, which serves as the input for contrastive learning. Our multi-batch transform is a simple yet effective approach to leverage multiple augmentation transformations for enhanced contrastive learning. In practice, we select the optimal value of
n\in\{2,4,6,8\}
.

is described in Section

6
Conclusion

To address the trade-off between classification accuracy and OOD detection performance in fine-tuning-based approaches with auxiliary outlier data, we analyze and improve three key elements: the backbone model used for training, the outlier data used for training, and the representation learning. For each element, we employ Self-Knowledge Distillation, Semi-hard Outlier Sampling, and Outlier-aware Supervised Contrastive Learning to enhance their effectiveness. Furthermore, by jointly improving these three elements, we achieve outstanding accuracy and OOD performance compared to four existing fine-tuning-based algorithms.

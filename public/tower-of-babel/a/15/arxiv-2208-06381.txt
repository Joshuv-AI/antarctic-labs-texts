Tilting theory in exact categories
Julia Sauter

Abstract
. We define tilting subcategories in arbitrary exact categories to archieve the following. Firstly: Unify existing definitions of tilting subcategories to arbitrary exact categories. Discuss standard results for tilting subcategories: Auslander correspondence, Bazzoni description of the perpendicular category. Secondly: We treat the question of induced derived equivalences separately - given a tilting subcategory 𝒯 \mathcal{T} , we ask if a functor on the perpendicular category induces a derived equivalence to a (certain) functor category mod ∞ − 𝒯 \modu_{\infty}-\mathcal{T} over 𝒯 \mathcal{T} . If this is the case, we call the tilting subcategory ideq tilting. We prove a generalization of Miyashita’s theorem (which is itself a generalization of a well-known theorem of Brenner-Butler) and characterize exact categories with enough projectives allowing ideq tilting subcategories. In particular, this is always fulfilled if the exact category is abelian with enough projectives.

2.
Some definitions of subcategories

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an idempotent complete category.
Let
\mathcal{E}=(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{S})
be throughout this section be an exact category in the sense of Quillen (it consists of an additive category
\mathcal{A}
together with a class of kernel-cokernel pairs
\mathcal{S}
, referred to as
short exact sequences
which satisfy the axioms of
[
12
]
, Def. 2.1).

For a kernel-cokernel pair
(i,d)\in\mathcal{S}
we call
i
an
inflation
and
d
a
deflation
.
We will denote by
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{E})
the projective objects in
\mathcal{E}
and by
\mathcal{I}(\mathcal{E})
the injectives. Since this is common practice, we will also often denote the underlying additive category
\mathcal{A}
again by
\mathcal{E}
- we think the reader can handle this level of ambiguity.

Definition 2.1
.

If
\mathcal{E}=(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{S})
is an exact category and
\mathcal{X}
a full subcategory which is closed under extensions. Then we call
\mathcal{X}

fully exact subcategory
if we consider it together with the exact structure
\mathcal{S}\lvert_{\mathcal{X}}
(i.e. the short exact sequences in
\mathcal{S}
where all three terms lie in
\mathcal{X}
). We will write
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
for the Ext-projectives in
\mathcal{X}.

2.1.
Subcategories generated by or orthogonal to a subcategory

We call a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
in
\mathcal{E}

admissible
if it factors as
f=d\circ i
for an inflation
i
and a deflation
d
. We say
X\xrightarrow{f}Y\xrightarrow{g}Z
with
f,g
admissible is exact at
Y
if
\Bild f=\Ker g
and
\Ker g\to Y\to\Bild g
is an exact sequence in
\mathcal{E}
. A sequence of composable morphisms is
exact
if every morphism is admissible and the sequence is exact at every intermediate object (for short exact sequences we sometimes leave out the zeros in the beginning and end). We call a sequence

X_{n}\xrightarrow{f_{n}}X_{n-1}\xrightarrow{f_{n-1}}\cdots\to X_{0}\xrightarrow{f_{0}}X_{-1}\xrightarrow{f_{-1}}0

right exact
if there is an exact sequence

0\to Z\to X_{n}\xrightarrow{f_{n}}X_{n-1}\xrightarrow{f_{n-1}}\cdots\to X_{0}\xrightarrow{f_{0}}X_{-1}\xrightarrow{f_{-1}}0

We say a (co- or contravariant) functor
F\colon\mathcal{E}\to(Ab)
into abelian groups
is exact on
the right exact sequence if
F
maps all short exact sequences

\Ker f_{i}\to X_{i}\to\Bild f_{i},\quad-1\leq i\leq n

to short exact sequences in abelian groups.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a full additive subcategory of
\mathcal{E}
and an integer
n\geq 0
and a subset
I\subset\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we define the following full subcategories of
\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle\gen_{n}(\mathcal{X})

\displaystyle=\{M\in\mathcal{E}\mid\exists\text{ right exact }X_{n}\to\cdots\to X_{0}\to M\to 0,\;X_{i}\in\mathcal{X}

\displaystyle}{}{}{}{}{}{\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(X,-)\text{ exact on it for every }X\in\mathcal{X}\}

\displaystyle\pres_{n}(\mathcal{X})

\displaystyle=\{M\in\mathcal{E}\mid\exists\text{ right exact }X_{n}\to\cdots\to X_{0}\to M\to 0,\;X_{i}\in\mathcal{X}\}

\displaystyle\Reso_{n}(\mathcal{X})

\displaystyle=\{M\in\mathcal{E}\mid\exists\text{ exact }0\to X_{n}\to\cdots\to X_{0}\to M\to 0,\;X_{i}\in\mathcal{X}\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}^{\perp_{I}}

\displaystyle=\{M\in\mathcal{E}\mid\Ext_{\mathcal{E}}^{i}(X,M)=0\text{ for all }i\in I,X\in\mathcal{X}\}

We write
\mathcal{X}^{\perp_{\geq n}}:=\mathcal{X}^{\perp_{\left[n,\infty\right)}},\mathcal{X}^{\perp}:=\mathcal{X}^{\perp_{\geq 1}}
. We define
\gen_{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
(resp.
\pres_{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
) analogously to
\gen_{n}(\mathcal{X})
(resp.
\pres_{n}(\mathcal{X})
) with infinite resolutions.

We define
\gen(\mathcal{X}):=\gen_{0}(\mathcal{X}),\pres(\mathcal{X}):=\pres_{0}(\mathcal{X})
and observe
\gen(\mathcal{X})=\pres(\mathcal{X})
is equivalent to
\mathcal{X}
is contravariantly finite in
\pres(\mathcal{X})
. Also, we set

\Reso(\mathcal{X}):=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\Reso_{n}(\mathcal{X})

We have obvious inclusions

\Reso_{n}(\mathcal{X})\subset\pres_{n}(\mathcal{X})\supset\gen_{n}(\mathcal{X}),\quad\Reso(\mathcal{X})\subset\pres_{\infty}(\mathcal{X})\supset\gen_{\infty}(\mathcal{X})

We denote the dual notions with
\cogen^{n}(\mathcal{X}),\copres^{n}(\mathcal{X}),n\leq\infty
and
\Cores_{n}(\mathcal{X}),\Cores(\mathcal{X}),{}^{{}_{I}\perp}\mathcal{X}
respectively.

Observe that these categories depend on the exact structure
\mathcal{S}
, so if there is the possibility of confusion, we will endow them with an index
\mathcal{E}
(or
\mathcal{S}
).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
X
be an object in
\mathcal{E}
. We say
\pdim_{\mathcal{E}}X\leq n
(resp.
\idim_{\mathcal{E}}X\leq n
) if
\Ext_{\mathcal{E}}^{n+1}(X,-)=0
(resp.
\Ext_{\mathcal{E}}^{n+1}(-,X)=0
).

For a subcategory
\mathcal{X}
we define

\pdim_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{X}:=\sup\{\pdim_{\mathcal{E}}X\mid X\in\mathcal{X}\}\quad\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}

and analogously
\idim_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{X}
. We call

\mathcal{P}^{\leq n}
the full subcategory of all objects
X
with
\pdim_{\mathcal{E}}X\leq n
and
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}
the subcategory of all objects
X
with
\pdim_{\mathcal{E}}X<\infty
. The subcategories
\mathcal{I}^{\leq n}
,
\mathcal{I}^{<\infty}
are defined dually.

Remark 2.3
.

We would like to know the common definition of an exact category
\mathcal{E}
with ”
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}
” since we use it so frequently.

If the exact category has the Jordan-Hölder property and only finitely many
\mathcal{E}
-simples, then
\gldim\mathcal{E}=\max_{S\text{ simple}}\pdim S
. Therefore, in that case
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}
is equivalent to
\gldim\mathcal{E}<\infty
.

The
finitistic dimension conjecture
holds for
\mathcal{E}
if

\gldim(\mathcal{P}^{<\infty})<\infty.

So, also in this case,
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}
is equivalent to
\gldim\mathcal{E}<\infty
.

(As far, as we know this conjecture is open for
\mathcal{E}=\modu-\Lambda
where
\Lambda
is an artin algebra. But is known to fail in some other abelian categories.)

We observe the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an exact category and
\mathcal{X}
a full, self-orthogonal subcategory.

(a)

Then we have
\Reso(\mathcal{X})\subset\mathcal{X}^{\perp}
and
\Reso(\mathcal{X})
is an extension-closed subcategory.

(b)

Let
n\geq 1
. If
\pdim_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{X}\leq n
, then we have
\pres_{n-1}(\mathcal{X})\subset\mathcal{X}^{\perp}
and
\pres_{m}(\mathcal{X})
is extension-closed for all
n-1\leq m\leq\infty
.

Proof.

ad (a) Since
\mathcal{X}^{\perp}
is inflation-closed it follows
\Reso(\mathcal{X})\subset\mathcal{X}^{\perp}
. To see that
\Reso(\mathcal{X})
is extension closed one can use literally proof of the horseshoe lemma (replacing the projectives with
\mathcal{X}
), cf.
[
12
]
, Thm. 12.8.

ad (b) Given an exact sequence
0\to Z\to X_{n-1}\to\cdots\to X_{0}\to S\to 0
with
X_{i}\in\mathcal{X}
, we see by dimension shift that
S\in\mathcal{X}^{\perp}
using
\pdim_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{X}\leq n
. This proves
\pres_{n-1}(\mathcal{X})\subset\mathcal{X}^{\perp}
and therefore
\pres_{m}(\mathcal{X})\subset\mathcal{X}^{\perp}
,
n-1\leq m\leq\infty
. Again by the horseshoe lemma as in loc. cit it follows that
\pres_{m}(\mathcal{X})
is extension closed.

∎

2.2.
Thick subcategories

Recall that a full subcategory
\mathcal{L}
of
\mathcal{E}
is called
thick
, if it is closed under summands and for every short exact sequence
A\to B\to C
in
\mathcal{S}
the following holds: If any two objects of
A,B,C
are in
\mathcal{L}
, then the third is also in
\mathcal{L}
.

We denote by
\Thick(\mathcal{X})
the smallest thick subcategory of
\mathcal{E}
that contains
\mathcal{X}
. By definition, we have
\Reso(\mathcal{X})\subset\Thick(\mathcal{X})
and
\Cores(\mathcal{X})\subset\Thick(\mathcal{X})
.

Example 2.5
.

The categories
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}
and
\mathcal{I}^{<\infty}
are thick subcategories of
\mathcal{E}
.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an exact category and
\mathcal{X}
a full subcategory. We call
\mathcal{X}

inflation-closed
if for every inflation
i\colon X\to Y
with
X,Y
in
\mathcal{X}
one also has
\coKer i
in
\mathcal{X}
. Dually, one defines
deflation-closed
.

Proposition 2.7
.

(
[
5
]
, Prop. 3.5 and Prop. 3.6, Thm 1.1)
Let
\mathcal{E}
be an exact category and
\mathcal{X}
a fully exact subcategory which has enough projectives
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{X})
.

Assume
\mathcal{X}
is an inflation-closed subcategory.
Then we have

(1)

\Thick(\mathcal{X})=\Cores(\mathcal{X})
,

(2)

\Cores_{n}(\mathcal{P})=\Cores_{n}(\mathcal{X})\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{X}
and therefore also
\Cores(\mathcal{P})=\Cores(\mathcal{X})\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{X}
,

(3)

\mathcal{X}
is covariantly finite in
\Cores(\mathcal{X})

Example 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an exact category. We denote by
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{E})
the projectives. Then,
\mathcal{P}
fulfills the dual conditions of Prop.
2.7
and therefore

\Thick(\mathcal{P})=\Reso(\mathcal{P})

are all objects which admit a finite projective resolution. If
\mathcal{E}
has enough projectives, then
\mathcal{P}^{<\infty}=\Reso(\mathcal{P})
.

2.3.
Resolving subcategories

Definition 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an exact category.
Let
\mathcal{X}
be a full subcategory.

We say
\mathcal{X}
is

\pres(\mathcal{X})=\mathcal{E}
.

We say
\mathcal{X}
is
coresolving
if it is extension closed, inflation-closed and
\copres(\mathcal{X})=\mathcal{E}
.

We say
\mathcal{X}
is

(*)

\Reso(\mathcal{X})=\mathcal{E}

(*)

n-

,

(*)

n
-resolving for some

.

Dually one defines finitely coresolving,
n
-coresolving and uniformly finitely coresolving.

Example 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an exact category with enough projectives and

2.4.
Functor categories

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an additive category.
If
\mathcal{X}
is essentially small we can define the category
\Modu-\mathcal{X}
of right
\mathcal{X}
-modules as the category of contravariant additive functors on
\mathcal{X}
to abelian groups (if
\mathcal{X}
is not essentially small the class of all natural transformations between two
\mathcal{X}
-modules is not necessarily a set). This is an abelian category with enough projectives and injectives. The projectives are summands of arbitrary direct sums of representable functors

for some

which we call the

.
Similarly, one can define the category
\mathcal{X}-\Modu:=\Modu-\mathcal{X}^{op}
of left
\mathcal{X}
-modules. We define full subcategories

\modu_{\infty}-\mathcal{X}\subset\modu_{n}-\mathcal{X}\subset\Modu-\mathcal{X},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

via
F\in\modu_{n}-\mathcal{X}
if there is a projective
n
-presentation (indexed by
0
up to
n
) by finitely generated projectives, i.e.
there is an exact sequence

\Hom_{\mathcal{X}}(-,X_{n})\to\cdots\to\Hom_{\mathcal{X}}(-,X_{0})\to F\to 0,

we define
\modu_{\infty}-\mathcal{X}
analogue with an infinite sequence as above and we call
\modu-\mathcal{X}:=\modu_{0}-\mathcal{X}
the
finitely generated

\mathcal{X}
-modules,
\modu_{1}-\mathcal{X}
as the
finitely presented

\mathcal{X}
-modules. All these are fully exact subcategories of
\Modu-\mathcal{X}
.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
f\colon X_{1}\to X_{0}
be a morphism in
\mathcal{X}
, we say that
f

admits a weak kernel
if there exists a morphism
g\colon X_{2}\to X_{1}
such that

\Hom_{\mathcal{X}}(-,X_{2})\xrightarrow{g\circ}\Hom_{\mathcal{X}}(-,X_{1})\xrightarrow{f\circ}\Hom_{\mathcal{X}}(-,X_{0})

. We say that

.

(

]

It is common practice to ignore the assumption

2.5.
Embedding exact categories with enough projectives into a functor category

If
\mathcal{E}
is an exact category with enough projectives
\mathcal{P}
, then we the Yoneda embedding induces by composition with the restriction-on-
\mathcal{P}
functor a functor

\mathbb{P}\colon\mathcal{E}\to\modu_{\infty}-\mathcal{P},\quad E\mapsto\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(-,E)\lvert_{\mathcal{P}}

We write
\Bild\mathbb{P}
for the essential image. For us, the main observation is the following:

Proposition 2.14
.

(
[
15
]
Prop. 2.2.1, Prop. 2.2.8)
The functor
\mathbb{P}
is fully faithful and induces isomorphisms on all higher extension groups,
\Bild\mathbb{P}
is extension closed.

If
\mathcal{E}
is idempotent complete, then
\Bild\mathbb{P}
is a resolving subcategory of
\modu_{\infty}-\mathcal{P}
.

We observe also the following obvious lemma.

Lemma 2.15
.

Given two exact categories
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
with enough projectives
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
respectively. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
are equivalent as exact categories.

(2)

There is an equivalence
\Psi\colon\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
of additive categories with the property: A morphism
f
in
\mathcal{P}
is admissible in
\mathcal{E}
if and only if
\Psi(f)
in
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is admissible in
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
.

(3)

There is an additive equivalence
\Phi\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
with
\Phi(\mathcal{P})=\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

(2) implies (1):
Given
\Psi
as in (2). We extend
\Psi
to an equivalence
\Psi\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
. For an (in
\mathcal{E}
) admissible
f\colon P_{1}\to P_{0}
we map
\coKer(f)
to
\coKer\Psi(f)
(and then check that this is well-defined and gives an equivalence of categories).

Conversely, if
\Psi\colon\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is an equivalence of exact categories it maps admissible morphsims to admissible morphisms, projectives to projectives. Then it restricts to an equivalence as in (2).

(3) implies (1): An additive equivalence preserves kernel-cokernel pairs. Kernel-cokernel pairs are short exact sequences in
\mathcal{E}
(resp.
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
) if and only if
\Hom(P,-)
are exact on them for all
P\in\mathcal{P}
(resp.
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
). Therefore
\Phi
is an equivalence of exact categories.
∎

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an exact category with enough projectives
\mathcal{P}
.
The following are equivalent:

(a)

\mathbb{P}\colon\mathcal{E}\to\modu_{\infty}-\mathcal{P}
is an equivalence.

(b)

The
\mathcal{E}
-admissible morphisms in
\mathcal{P}
are precisely those such that
\Ker\Hom_{\mathcal{P}}(-,h)(\in\Modu-\mathcal{P})
lies in
\modu_{\infty}-\mathcal{P}

Proof.

3.
The derived category of an exact category

We recall from Buehler
[
12
]
, section 9: Let
\mathcal{A}
be an idempotent complete additive category.
One can define the category of chain complexes
Ch(\mathcal{A})
and the homotopy category
K(\mathcal{A})
, whose objects equal those if
Ch(\mathcal{A})
but the morphisms are the quotient group given by chain maps modulo chain maps homotopic to the zero map.
K(\mathcal{A})
has the structure of a triangulated category induced by strict triangles in
Ch(\mathcal{A})
. A chain complex

A=(\cdots\xrightarrow{d^{n-1}}A^{n}\xrightarrow{d^{n}}A^{n+1}\xrightarrow{d^{n+1}}\cdots)\quad\in Ch(\mathcal{A})

is called
left bounded
if
A^{n}=0
for
n<<0
,
right bounded
if
A^{n}=0
for
n>>0
and
bounded
if
A^{n}=0
for
\lvert n\rvert>>0
. We denote by
K^{+}(\mathcal{A}),K^{-}(\mathcal{A})
and
K^{b}(\mathcal{A})
the full subcategories of
K(\mathcal{A})
whose objects are the left bounded, right bounded and bounded chain complexes respectively. By definition
K^{b}(\mathcal{A})=K^{+}(\mathcal{A})\cap K^{-}(\mathcal{A})
. The subcategories
K^{*}(\mathcal{A})
with
*\in\{+,-,b\}
are triangulated subcategories but not closed under isomorphism in
K(\mathcal{A})
unless
\mathcal{A}=0
.

Now, let us assume that
\mathcal{E}=(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{S})
is an exact category.
A chain complex
A=(A^{\bullet},d^{\bullet})
in
Ch(\mathcal{A})
is called
acyclic
if every differential factors as
d^{n}\colon A^{n}\to Z^{n+1}A\to A^{n+1}
such that
Z^{n}A\to A^{n}\to Z^{n+1}A
is an exact sequence (i.e. in
\mathcal{S}
).

Neeman proved that the mapping cone of a chain map between acyclic complexes is acyclic. This implies that the full subcategory
Ac(\mathcal{E})=Ac_{\mathcal{S}}(\mathcal{A})
of
K(\mathcal{A})
given by acyclic complexes is a triangulated subcategory of
K(\mathcal{A})
. If
\mathcal{A}
is idempotent complete then
Ac(\mathcal{E})
is closed under isomorphism, every null-homotopic chain complex is acyclic and
Ac(\mathcal{E})
is a thick subcategory of
K(\mathcal{A})
.
We define
Ac^{*}(\mathcal{E}):=Ac(\mathcal{E})\cap K^{*}(\mathcal{A})
for
*\in\{+,-,b\}
. If
\mathcal{A}
is weakly idempotent complete then the categories
Ac^{*}(\mathcal{E})
are thick subcategories of
K^{*}(\mathcal{A})
for
*\in\{+,-,b\}
.

Given any triangulated category
\mathcal{C}
and thick subcategory
\mathcal{T}
the
Verdier quotient

\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{T}

is defined via a localization (cf.
[
20
]
, section 3.2, Prop. 3.2.2). It is again a triangulated category and the canonical functor
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{T}
is an exact functor.

As explained in
[
12
]
, since
\mathcal{A}
is idempotent complete,
Ac^{*}(\mathcal{E})
is a thick subcategory of
K^{*}(\mathcal{A})
for
*\in\{\pm,b,\emptyset\}
.
The
derived category
of an exact category
\mathcal{E}=(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{S})
is defined as the Verdier quotient

D(\mathcal{E}):=K(\mathcal{A})/Ac(\mathcal{E})

Similarly, we define the
bounded/left bounded derived category
as the Verdier quotients

D^{*}(\mathcal{E}):=K^{*}(\mathcal{A})/Ac^{*}(\mathcal{E}),\quad*\in\{b,\pm\}

For more details and basic properties we refer to
[
20
]
, chapter 4 and
[
12
]
, section 10.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an exact category with enough projectives
\mathcal{P}
. We define

K^{b,-}(\mathcal{P})
as the full subcategory of
K^{-}(\mathcal{P})
given by all complexes
X
such that there exists an
n\geq 1
such that

and
d^{-n}
are admissible and the truncated complexes

\tau_{\leq(-n)}X=(\cdots\to X^{(-n)-1}\to X^{(-n)}\to\Ker d^{(-n)}\to 0\cdots)
,

\tau_{\geq n}X=(\cdots 0\to\coKer d^{n-1}\to X^{n+1}\to X^{n+2}\to\cdots)

are acyclic, i.e. in

. Observe, that this depend on the ambient exact category
\mathcal{E}
, even though the notation suggests otherwise.

(2)

4.
Tilting theory in exact categories

We define tilting subcategories and go through the list of results (cf. introduction) that we demand for tilting theory (i.e. induced derived equivalence, Aulsander correspondence, Bazzoni-like result and Brenner-Butler theorem).

4.1.
Tilting subcategories

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{S})
be an exact category and
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{E}
a full subcategory. We call
\mathcal{T}
a
tilting
subcategory if

(T1)

\mathcal{T}
is self-orthogonal and
\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
has enough projectives which are given by
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{T}^{\perp})=\mathcal{T}

(equivalently:
\mathcal{T}
is closed under taking summands in
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{T}^{\perp}\subset\gen(\mathcal{T})
)

(T2)

\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
is finitely coresolving, i.e. it is coresolving and
\Cores(\mathcal{T}^{\perp})=\mathcal{E}
.

By Prop.
2.7
, if we assume (T1), we can replace (T2) by an equivalent:

(T2’)

\Thick(\mathcal{T}^{\perp})=\mathcal{E}
.

We call
\mathcal{T}

n
-
tilting
if it is tilting with
\Cores_{n}(\mathcal{T}^{\perp})=\mathcal{E}
. We call an object
T
in
\mathcal{E}

tilting
if
\add(T)
is a tilting subcategory.
Assumption (T1) implies
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
are closed under summands and we have a well-defined fully faithful exact functor into infinitely presented
\mathcal{T}
-modules
f_{\mathcal{T}}\colon\mathcal{T}^{\perp}\to\modu_{\infty}-\mathcal{T}
,
X\mapsto\Hom_{\mathcal{E}}(-,X)\lvert_{\mathcal{T}}
since
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{T}^{\perp})
and
\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
has enough projectives.

Assumption (T2) implies that the inclusion
\mathcal{T}^{\perp}\subset\mathcal{E}
induces a triangle equivalence
D^{b}(\mathcal{T}^{\perp})\to D^{b}(\mathcal{E})
.
Assumption (T1) and (T2) imply that the inclusion
\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{E}
gives rise to a triangle equivalence

K^{b,-}(\mathcal{T})\cong D^{b}(\mathcal{T}^{\perp})\to D^{b}(\mathcal{E}).

Here is another way to express (T2) but we will need the assumption that the exact category has enough injectives.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an additive subcategory in an exact category
\mathcal{E}
. Then:

\Cores(\mathcal{T}^{\perp})=\mathcal{E}
(resp.
\Cores_{n}(\mathcal{T}^{\perp})=\mathcal{E}
) implies
\mathcal{E}=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{T}^{\perp_{\geq n+1}}
(resp.
\pdim_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{T}\leq n
).

If
\mathcal{E}
has enough injectives , then the converse is true.

Proof.

Observe that
A\in\Cores_{n}(\mathcal{T}^{\perp})
implies
\Ext^{i}(T,A)=0,i\geq n+1
for every
T\in\mathcal{T}
, i.e.
A\in\mathcal{T}^{\perp_{\geq n+1}}
. If
\mathcal{E}
has enough injectives and
A\in\mathcal{T}^{\perp_{\geq n+1}}
, then we have by dimension shift
\Omega^{-n}A\in\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
. So, the injective coresolution of
A
truncated at the
n
-th cosyzygy shows that
A\in\Cores_{n}(\mathcal{T}^{\perp})
.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Assume that
\mathcal{T}
is a tilting subcategory of an exact category
\mathcal{E}
and
n\geq 0
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{T}
is
n
-tilting

(2)

\pdim_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{T}\leq n

Proof.

By Lemma
4.2
, we have the implication (1) implies (2). Assume (2), we claim:
\Cores(\mathcal{T}^{\perp})\subset\Cores_{n}(\mathcal{T}^{\perp})
. Let
A\in\Cores(\mathcal{T}^{\perp})
, then there exists an exact sequence

0\to A\to X_{0}\to\cdots\to X_{s}\to 0,\quad X_{i}\in\mathcal{T}^{\perp},0\leq i\leq s

Wlog. assume that
s>n-1
. Let
Z_{i}=\Bild(X_{i}\to X_{i+1}),Z_{-1}:=A
.
Applying
\Hom(T,-)
,
T\in\mathcal{T}
, gives by dimension shift
\Ext^{j}_{\mathcal{E}}(T,Z_{n-1})\cong\Ext^{j+1}_{\mathcal{E}}(T,Z_{n-2})\cong\cdots\cong\Ext^{j+n}_{\mathcal{E}}(T,A)=0
for all
j\geq 1
since
\pdim_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{T}\leq n
. So,
Z_{n-1}\in\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be an additive category of an exact category
\mathcal{E}
.
If
\mathcal{T}
fulfills (T1) and
\pdim_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{T}\leq n
, then we have

\mathcal{T}^{\perp}=\gen_{n-1}(\mathcal{T})=\pres_{n-1}(\mathcal{T})=\gen_{\infty}(\mathcal{T})=\pres_{\infty}(\mathcal{T}).

Proof.

The assumption
\mathcal{T}^{\perp_{\geq n+1}}=\mathcal{E}
together with
\mathcal{T}
self-orthogonal implies by an easy dimension shift argument that
\pres_{n-1}(\mathcal{T})\subset\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
. Now, by assumption (T1) we have
\mathcal{T}^{\perp}\subset\gen_{\infty}(\mathcal{T})
since
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{T}^{\perp})
and
\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
has enough projectives. Since
\gen_{\infty}(\mathcal{T})\subset\pres_{n-1}(\mathcal{T})
trivially and
\gen_{n-1}(\mathcal{T})
and
\pres_{\infty}(\mathcal{T})
are intermediate between these two, the claim follows.
∎

For infinitely generated tilting modules, the perpendicular category of a tilting module of finite projective dimension has been described in
[
9
]
, Theorem 3.11. In
[
29
]
, Theorem 1.1, this description has been proven for relative tilting modules over artin algebras. Therefore, we call the generalization to tilting subcategories in exact categories
Bazzoni’s description
.

Corollary 4.5
.

(
Bazzoni’s description
)
Let
\mathcal{T}
be an additive category of an exact category
\mathcal{E}
which is closed under taking summands and assume
\pdim_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{T}\leq n
, then (T1) is equivalent to the following:

\text{\emph{(T1')}}\quad\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{T}^{\perp}\subset\pres_{n}(\mathcal{T})

and also to

\text{\emph{(T1'')}}\quad\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{T}^{\perp}\subset\add(\pres_{n}(\mathcal{T}))

where for a full subcategory
\mathcal{M}
of
\mathcal{E}
:
\add(\mathcal{M})
denotes the full subcategory of all summands of finite direct sums of objects in
\mathcal{M}
.

Furthermore, in this case we have
\pres_{n-1}(\mathcal{T})=\pres_{n}(\mathcal{T})=\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
.

Proof.

The implication ”(T1) implies (T1’)” is given by Lemma
4.4
using the inclusions
\pres_{\infty}(\mathcal{T})\subset\pres_{n}(\mathcal{T})\subset\pres_{n-1}(\mathcal{T})
. Also loc. cit. proves the statement
\pres_{n-1}(\mathcal{T})=\pres_{n}(\mathcal{T})
. The implication ”(T1’) implies (T1”)” is trivial since
\mathcal{T}^{\perp}=\add(\mathcal{T}^{\perp})
.

Assume (T1”), i.e.
\pdim_{\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{T}\leq n
and
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{T}^{\perp}\subset\add(\pres_{n}(\mathcal{T}))
. To see (T1), it is enough to prove
\mathcal{T}^{\perp}\subset\gen(\mathcal{T})
. Observe that
\pres_{n-1}(\mathcal{T})\subset\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
is fulfilled by Lemma
2.4
, (b).
Let
X\in\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
, then there exists an
L\in\mathcal{E}
such that
X\oplus L\in\pres_{n}(\mathcal{T})
- in particular
L\in\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
and there is an exact sequence

0\to X_{n+1}\to T_{n}\xrightarrow{f_{n}}\cdots\xrightarrow{f_{1}}T_{0}\to X\oplus L\to 0

with
T_{i}\in\mathcal{T}
.
Let
X_{i}:=\Bild(f_{i}),X\oplus L:=X_{0}
. For every
j\geq 1,T\in\mathcal{T}
we have
\Ext^{j}(T,X_{i})\cong\Ext^{j+1}(T,X_{i+1})
. This implies
X_{1}\in\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
since
\Ext^{j}(T,X_{1})\cong\Ext^{n+j}(T,X_{n+1})=0
by assumption. Therefore
T_{0}\to X\oplus L
is a deflation in
\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
. This implies that the composition with the projection onto the summand
T_{0}\to X\oplus L\to X
is a deflation in
\mathcal{T}^{\perp}
- (since projections onto summands are deflation in every exact category and the composition of two deflations is a deflation).
Therefore, (T1) is fulfilled.
∎

Lemma 6.35

We apply the restriction functor to a projective resolution of

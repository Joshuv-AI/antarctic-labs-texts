Chebyshev distances associated to the second members of systems of max-product/Lukasiewicz fuzzy relational equations
Ismaïl Baaj

Abstract
In this article, we study the inconsistency of a system of max \max -product fuzzy relational equations and of a system of max \max -Lukasiewicz fuzzy relational equations. For a system of max − min \max-\min fuzzy relational equations A ​ □ min max ​ x = b A\Box_{\min}^{\max}x=b and using the L ∞ L_{\infty} norm, ( Baaj 2023 ) showed that the Chebyshev distance Δ = inf c ∈ 𝒞 ‖ b − c ‖ \Delta=\inf_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\|b-c\| , where 𝒞 \mathcal{C} is the set of second members of consistent systems defined with the same matrix A A , can be computed by an explicit analytical formula according to the components of the matrix A A and its second member b b . In this article, we give analytical formulas analogous to that of ( Baaj 2023 ) to compute the Chebyshev distance associated to the second member of a system of max \max -product fuzzy relational equations and that associated to the second member of a system of max \max -Lukasiewicz fuzzy relational equations.

Introduction

Artificial Intelligence (AI) applications based on systems of fuzzy relational equations emerged thanks to
(

Sanchez 1977

(

;
Li and Fang 2010
)
. In a recent preprint
(
Baaj 2023
)
, for an inconsistent system of
\max-\min

(

In our opinion, it would be relevant to study how these results can be extended to systems of
\max-T
fuzzy relational equations, where

As it is well-known, many applications are based on systems of fuzzy relational equations with the max-product composition or the max-Lukasiewicz composition e.g.,

2.1
Notations

The following notations were given in
(
Baaj 2023
)
for the case of a system of
\max-\min
fuzzy relational equations and we reuse them in this article.

By
[0,1]^{n\times m}
we denote the set of matrices of size
(n,m)
i.e.,
n
rows and
m
columns, whose components are in
[0,1]
. In particular:

•

[0,1]^{n\times 1}
denotes the set of column vectors of
n
components,

•

[0,1]^{1\times m}
denotes the set of row matrices of
m
components.

On the set
[0,1]^{n\times m}
, we use the order relation
\leq
defined by:

A\leq B\quad\text{iff we have}\quad a_{ij}\leq b_{ij}\quad\text{ for all }\quad 1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m,

where
A=[a_{ij}]_{1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m}
and
B=[b_{ij}]_{1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m}
.

For
x,y,z,u,\delta\in[0,1]
, we put:

•

x^{+}=\max(x,0)
,

•

\overline{z}(\delta)=\min(z+\delta,1)
,

•

\underline{z}(\delta)=\max(z-\delta,0)=(z-\delta)^{+}

and we have the following equivalence in
[0,1]
:

\mid x-y\mid\leq\delta\Longleftrightarrow\underline{x}(\delta)\leq y\leq\overline{x}(\delta).

(1)

For our work, to a column-vector
b=[b_{i}]_{1\leq i\leq n}
and a number
\delta\in[0,1]
, we associate two column-vectors:

\underline{b}(\delta)=[(b_{i}-\delta)^{+}]_{1\leq i\leq n}\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{b}(\delta)=[\min(b_{i}+\delta,1)]_{1\leq i\leq n}.

(2)

These vectors
\underline{b}(\delta)
and
\overline{b}(\delta)
were already introduced e.g., in
(
Cuninghame-Green and
Cechlárová 1995
)
(with others notations) and in
(
Li and Fang 2010
)
.

Then, from (
1
), we deduce for any
c=[c_{i}]_{1\leq i\leq n}\in[0,1]^{n\times 1}
:

\|b-c\|\leq\delta\Longleftrightarrow\underline{b}(\delta)\leq c\leq\overline{b}(\delta).

(3)

where
\|b-c\|=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\mid b_{i}-c_{i}\mid
.

2.2
T-norms and their associated residual implicators

A triangular-norm (t-norm, see
(
Klement et al. 2013
)
) is a map
T:[0,1]\times[0,1]\mapsto[0,1]
, which satisfies:

T
is commutative:
T(x,y)=T(y,x)
,

T
is associative:
T(x,T(y,z))=T(T(x,y),z)
,

T
is increasing with respect to the second variable:
y\leq z\,\Longrightarrow\,T(x,y)\leq T(x,z)
,

T
has
1
as neutral element:
T(x,1)=x
.

The residual implicator associated to the t-norm
T
is the map

{\cal I}_{T}:[0,1]\times[0,1]\rightarrow[0,1]:(x,y)\mapsto{\cal I}_{T}(x,y)=\sup\{z\in[0,1]\,\mid\,T(x,z)\leq y\}
.

The main properties of the residual implicator
{\cal I}_{T}
of a continuous t-norm
T
are: for all
a,b\in[0,1]
, we have:

•

{\cal I}_{T}(a,b)=\max\{z\in[0,1]\,\mid\,T(a,z)\leq b\}
. Therefore,
T(a,{\cal I}_{T}(a,b))\leq b
.

•

{\cal I}_{T}
is left-continuous and decreasing in its first argument as well as right-continuous and increasing in its second argument.

•

For all
z\in[0,1]
, we have:

T(a,z)\leq b\Longleftrightarrow z\leq{\cal I}_{T}(a,b).

•

We have

\,b\leq{\cal I}_{T}(a,T(a,b))
.

The t-norm defined by the usual product is denoted by
T_{P}
. Its associated residual implicator is the Goguen product, and we have:

T_{P}(x,y)=x\cdot y\quad;\quad{\cal I}_{T_{P}}(x,y)=x\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}y=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }x\leq y\\
\frac{y}{x}&\text{ if }x>y\end{cases}.

(4)

Lukasiewicz’s t-norm is denoted by
T_{L}
and we have:

T_{L}(x,y)=\max(x+y-1,0)={(x+y-1)}^{+}\quad;\quad{\cal I}_{T_{L}}(x,y)=x\underset{L}{\longrightarrow}y=\min(1-x+y,1).

(5)

In the rest of this section, we set a continuous t-norm denoted
T
.

2.3
Solving systems of
\max-T
fuzzy relational equations

Let
A=[a_{ij}]\in[0,1]^{n\times m}
be a matrix of size
(n,m)
and
b=[b_{i}]\in[0,1]^{n\times 1}
be a vector of
n
components. The system of
\max-T
fuzzy relational equations associated to
(A,b)
is of the form:

(S):A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=b,

(6)

where
x=[x_{j}]_{1\leq j\leq m}\in[0,1]^{m\times 1}
is an unknown vector of
m
components and the operator
\Box_{T}^{\max}
is the matrix product that uses the continuous t-norm
T
as the product and
\max
as the addition. Equivalently, the system can also be written as:

\max_{1\leq j\leq m}T(a_{ij},x_{j})=b_{i},\,\forall i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\}.

The studies on systems of fuzzy relational equations adopt two equivalent notation conventions, which differ in their representation of the unknown part and the second member: either as column vectors or as row vectors. The transpose map allows us to switch from one notation to the other.

To check if the system (S), see (
6
), is consistent, we compute the following vector:

e=A^{t}\Box_{{\cal I}_{T}}^{\min}b,

(7)

where
A^{t}
is the transpose of
A
and the matrix product
\Box_{{\cal I}_{T}}^{\min}
uses the residual implicator
{\cal I}_{T}
(associated to
T
) as the product and
\min
as the addition.
The vector
e
is the potential maximal solution of the system
(S)
.

Thanks to
(
Sanchez 1976
)
’s seminal work on the solving of systems
\max-\min
fuzzy relational equations, and
(
Pedrycz 1982
;
Pedrycz 1985
)
and
(
Miyakoshi and Shimbo 1985
)
(using other t-norms than
\min
), we have the following equivalence:

A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=b\text{ is consistent}\Longleftrightarrow A\Box_{T}^{\max}e=b.

(8)

In the rest of this subsection, we will give (Proposition
1
) and show that the proof of its second statement implies the equivalence (
8
).

The set of solutions of the system
(S)
is denoted by:

{\cal S}={\cal S}(A,b)=\{v\in[0,1]^{m\times 1}\,\mid\,A\,\Box_{T}^{\max}v=b\}.

(9)

The structure of the solution set was described by
(
Sanchez 1977
)
(with the
\max-\min
composition) and
(
Di Nola et al. 1982
;
Di Nola et al. 1984
;
Di Nola et al. 1989
)
.

Example 1
.

Let:

A=\begin{bmatrix}0.31&0.49&0.76\\
0.34&0.9&0.15\\
0.94&0.47&0.05\\
\end{bmatrix}\text{ and }b=\begin{bmatrix}0.73\\
0.84\\
0.61\end{bmatrix}.

(10)

To check if the systems
A\Box_{T_{P}}^{\max}x=b
and
A\Box_{T_{L}}^{\max}x=b
are consistent, we compute their respective potential greatest solution:

e_{T_{P}}=A^{t}\Box_{{\cal I}_{T_{P}}}^{\min}b=\begin{bmatrix}0.65\\
0.93\\
0.96\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{ and }\quad e_{T_{L}}=A^{t}\Box_{{\cal I}_{T_{L}}}^{\min}b=\begin{bmatrix}0.67\\
\lx@nobreakspace 0.94\\
0.97\end{bmatrix}.

These two systems are consistent because:

A\Box_{T_{P}}^{\max}e_{T_{P}}=A\Box_{T_{L}}^{\max}e_{T_{L}}=b.

In
(
Baaj 2023
)
, the author introduced an idempotent application denoted
F
, to check if a system of
\max-\min
fuzzy relational equations defined with a fixed matrix
A=[a_{ij}]_{1\leq i\leq n,1\leq j\leq m}\in[0,1]^{n\times m}
and a given vector
c\in[0,1]^{n\times 1}
used as second member is a consistent system. The application is a reformulation of
(
Sanchez 1976
)
’s result. For systems of
\max-T
fuzzy relational equations which uses a continuous t-norm
T
, we extend the definition of the application
F
as follows:

F:[0,1]^{n\times 1}\longrightarrow[0,1]^{n\times 1}:c=[c_{i}]\mapsto F(c)=[F(c)_{i}]

(11)

where:

\forall i\in\{1,2,\dots,n\},\,F(c)_{i}=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}T(a_{ij},\min_{1\leq k\leq n}{\cal I}_{T}(a_{kj},c_{k})).

(12)

The properties of the application
F
given in
(
Baaj 2023
)
for a system of
\max-\min
fuzzy relational equations are valid for systems of
\max-T
fuzzy relational equations:

Proposition 1
.

1.

\forall c\in[0,1]^{n\times 1}
,
F(c)\leq c
.

2.

For any vector
c\in[0,1]^{n\times 1}
we have:

F(c)=c\Longleftrightarrow\text{ the system }A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=c\text{ is consistent.}

3.

F
is idempotent i.e.,
\forall c\in[0,1]^{n\times 1},F(F(c))=F(c)
.

4.

F
is increasing and right-continuous.

The application
F
being right-continuous at a point
c\in[0,1]^{n\times 1}
means: for any sequence
(c^{(k)})
in
[0,1]^{n\times 1}
such that
(c^{(k)})
converges to
c
when
k\rightarrow\infty
and verifying
\forall k,c^{(k)}\geq c
, we have:

F(c^{(k)})\rightarrow F(c)
when
k\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

We adapt the proofs given in
(
Baaj 2023
)
for the
\max-\min
composition to the
\max-T
composition in the following way:

The first assertion is deduced from the inequality

T(a,{\cal I}_{T}(a,b))\leq b
.

For the second statement, if we suppose that
F(c)=c
, it means that
e=A^{t}\Box_{{\cal I}_{T}}^{\min}c
is a solution of the system
A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=c
(so it is consistent).

For the proof of the implication
\Longleftarrow
, we use the general inequality:

\forall x\in[0,1]^{m\times 1},\,x\leq A^{t}\Box_{{\cal I}_{T}}^{\min}(A\Box_{T}^{\max}x),

which is deduced from the inequality
b\leq{\cal I}_{T}(a,T(a,b))
. We also obtain that
e=A^{t}\Box_{{\cal I}_{T}}^{\min}c
is the greatest solution of the system
A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=c
. Clearly, we have also proven the equivalence (
8
).

Thanks to the two assertions above, the idempotence of the application
F
is proved as in the case of the

\max-\min
composition.

It is easy to prove that the application
F
is increasing. The right-continuity of the residual implicator
{\cal I}_{T}
in its second argument implies the right-continuity of the application
F
.

∎

Example 2
.

(continued)
Let
c=\begin{bmatrix}0.74\\
0.66\\
0.62\end{bmatrix}
. When we use any of the two t-norms
T_{P}
or
T_{L}
, we have the equality
F(c)=c
. So the systems
A\Box_{T_{P}}^{\max}x=c
and
A\Box_{T_{L}}^{\max}x=c
are consistent.

2.4
Chebyshev distance associated to the second member of a system of
\max-T
fuzzy relational equations

To the matrix
A
and the second member
b
of the system
(S)
of
\max-T
fuzzy relational equations, see (
6
), let us associate the set of vectors
c=[c_{i}]\in[0,1]^{n\times 1}
such that the system
A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=c
is consistent:

\mathcal{C}=\{c=[c_{i}]\in{[0,1]}^{n\times 1}\mid A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=c\text{ is consistent}\}.

(13)

This set allows us to define the Chebyshev distance associated to the second member
b
of the system
(S)
.

Definition 1
.

The Chebyshev distance associated to the second member
b
of the system
(S):A\Box_{T}^{\max}x=b
is:

\Delta=\Delta(A,b)=\inf_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\|b-c\|

(14)

where:

\|b-c\|=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\mid b_{i}-c_{i}\mid.

We have the following fundamental result, proven for
\max-\min
composition in
(
Cuninghame-Green and
Cechlárová 1995
)
, that we extend for
\max-T
composition:

Theorem 1
.

\Delta=\min\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid\underline{b}(\delta)\leq F(\overline{b}(\delta))\}.

(15)

The proof of (Theorem
1
) is given in three steps.

Proof.

We put
\Delta^{\prime}=\inf\{\delta\in[0,1]\,|\,\underline{b}(\delta)\leq F(\overline{b}(\delta))\}
.

\bullet
Step 1.
We want to prove that we have
\Delta^{\prime}=\min\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid\underline{b}(\delta)\leq F(\overline{b}(\delta))\}
. Indeed, as we have

\underline{b}(1)=0\leq F(\overline{b}(1))
, the set
\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid\underline{b}(\delta)\leq F(\overline{b}(\delta))\}
is non-empty. Therefore,
we can find a sequence
(\delta_{k})
in
[0,1]
verifying:

1.

\forall k,\,\underline{b}(\delta_{k})\leq F(\overline{b}(\delta_{k}))
,

2.

\forall k,\,\delta_{k}\geq\Delta^{\prime}
,

3.

\delta_{k}\rightarrow\Delta^{\prime}
.

As
\forall k,\overline{b}(\delta_{k})\geq\overline{b}(\Delta^{\prime})
and
\overline{b}(\delta_{k})\rightarrow\overline{b}(\Delta^{\prime})
, the right-continuity of the application
F
at
\overline{b}(\Delta^{\prime})
implies:

F(\overline{b}(\delta_{k}))\rightarrow F(\overline{b}(\Delta^{\prime})).

The inequalities
\underline{b}(\delta_{k})\leq F(\overline{b}(\delta_{k}))
lead, by the passage to the limit, to:

\underline{b}(\Delta^{\prime})\leq F(\overline{b}(\Delta^{\prime})).

So
\Delta^{\prime}=\min\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid\underline{b}(\delta)\leq F(\overline{b}(\delta))\}.

\bullet
Step 2. We want to prove that
\Delta\leq\Delta^{\prime}
.

We have:

\underline{b}(\Delta^{\prime})\leq F(\overline{b}(\Delta^{\prime}))\leq\overline{b}(\Delta^{\prime}).

By (
3
) and by noticing that
F(\overline{b}(\Delta^{\prime}))\in{\cal C}
, we deduce:

\Delta\leq\|b-F(\overline{b}(\Delta^{\prime}))\|\leq\Delta^{\prime}.

\bullet
Step 3. We want to prove that
\Delta^{\prime}\leq\Delta
. For this it is sufficient to show that:

\forall c\in{\cal C},\,\Delta^{\prime}\leq\|b-c\|.

Indeed, for any
c\in{\cal C}
, we put
\delta=\|b-c\|
, and we have by (
3
):

\underline{b}(\delta)\leq c\leq\overline{b}(\delta).

Therefore
F(c)=c
and the growth of
F
lead to:

\underline{b}(\delta)\leq c=F(c)\leq F(\overline{b}(\delta)).

So:

\Delta^{\prime}\leq\delta=\|b-c\|.

We have proved that
\Delta=\Delta^{\prime}=\min\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid\underline{b}(\delta)\leq F(\overline{b}(\delta))\}
.

∎

We define the set of Chebyshev approximations of
b
:

Definition 2
.

The set of Chebyshev approximations of
b
is defined using the set
\mathcal{C}
, see (
13
), and the Chebyshev distance associated to
b
(Definition
1
):

{\cal C}_{b}=\{c\in{\cal C}\,\mid\,\|b-c\|=\Delta(A,b)\}.

(16)

We have:

Corollary 1
.

F(\overline{b}(\Delta))\in{\cal C}_{b}
is the greatest Chebyshev approximation of the second member
b
of the system
(S)
.

Proof.

Let us first show that we have
\|b-F(\overline{b}(\Delta))\|=\Delta
.

Indeed, from (Theorem
1
) and (Proposition
1
), we know that
\underline{b}(\Delta)\leq F(\overline{b}(\Delta))\leq\overline{b}(\Delta)
, so by (
3
),
\|b-F(\overline{b}(\Delta))\|\leq\Delta
.

As

F(\overline{b}(\Delta))\in{\cal C}
(Proposition
1
), we know by (Definition
1
):

\Delta\leq\|b-F(\overline{b}(\Delta))\|.

We have proven that

F(\overline{b}(\Delta))\in{\cal C}_{b}
.

To prove that
F(\overline{b}(\Delta))
is the greatest Chebyshev approximation of the second member
b
of the system
(S)
we must prove that
c\leq F(\overline{b}(\Delta))
for any
c\in{\cal C}_{b}
.

Let
c\in{\cal C}_{b}
. As

\Delta=\|b-c\|
, by (
3
) we have:

\underline{b}(\Delta)\leq c\leq\overline{b}(\Delta).

By the growth of
F
, we deduce:

c=F(c)\leq F(\overline{b}(\Delta)).

∎

As a consequence, we have:

Corollary 2
.

\Delta=\min_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\|b-c\|.

\Delta=0\Longleftrightarrow\text{ the system $(S)$ is consistent.}

Therefore,
\Delta=0
is a necessary and sufficient condition for the system
(S)
to be consistent.

3.1
T-norm product
T=T_{P}

Let
x,y,z,u\in[0,1]
be fixed.
For the t-norm product
T_{P}
, let us study the following inequality that involves the Goguen product
\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}
(see (
4
)), for
\delta\in[0,1]
:

\underline{x}(\delta)\leq u\cdot(y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta))

(17)

where
y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }y-z\leq\delta\\
\frac{z+\delta}{y}&\text{ if }y-z>\delta\end{cases}
.

We look for the smallest value of
\delta
so that the inequality (
17
) is true
.

Let:

\sigma_{GG}(u,x,y,z)=\max[(x-u)^{+},\min(\varphi(u,x,y,z),(y-z)^{+})]

(18)

where
\varphi(u,x,y,z)=\begin{cases}\frac{(x\cdot y-u\cdot z)^{+}}{u+y}&\text{ if }u>0\\
x&\text{ if }u=0\end{cases}
.

Proposition 2
.

For all
\delta\in[0,1]
, we have:

\underline{x}(\delta)\leq u\cdot(y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta))\Longleftrightarrow\sigma_{GG}(u,x,y,z)\leq\delta.

Proof.

If
u=0
, then
\sigma_{GG}(0,x,y,z)=x
and we immediately get the desired equivalence:

(x-\delta)^{+}\leq 0\Longleftrightarrow x\leq\delta\Longleftrightarrow\sigma_{GG}(0,x,y,z)\leq\delta.

It remains for us to study the case where
u>0
.

Suppose that
(x-\delta)^{+}\leq u(y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta))
.

•

If
y-z\leq\delta
, we have:

(x-\delta)^{+}\leq u(y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta))=u\cdot 1=u.

We know that the inequality
(x-\delta)^{+}\leq u
is equivalent to the inequality
(x-u)^{+}\leq\delta

(
Baaj 2023
)
.
As
\min(\varphi(u,x,y,z),(y-z)^{+})\leq(y-z)^{+}\leq\delta
, we obtain:

\sigma_{GG}(u,x,y,z)=\max[(x-u)^{+},\min(\varphi(u,x,y,z),(y-z)^{+})]\leq\delta.

•

If
y-z>\delta
, we have:

(x-\delta)^{+}\leq u(y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta))=u\dfrac{z+\delta}{y}\leq u

so,
(x-u)^{+}\leq\delta
and moreover

\displaystyle(x-\delta)^{+}-u(y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta))

\displaystyle=\max(x-\delta-\dfrac{uz+u\delta}{y},-\dfrac{uz+u\delta}{y})

\displaystyle=\max(\dfrac{xy-\delta y-uz-u\delta}{y},-\dfrac{uz+u\delta}{y})\leq 0.

The last inequality is equivalent to
xy-\delta y-uz-u\delta\leq 0
and also:

\dfrac{xy-uz}{u+y}\leq\delta.

Then:

\bullet
If
(xy-uz)^{+}=0
, we have:

\varphi(u,x,y,z)=\dfrac{(xy-uz)^{+}}{u+y}=0

and also

\sigma_{GG}(u,x,y,z)=\max[(x-u)^{+},\min(\varphi(u,x,y,z),(y-z)^{+})=(x-u)^{+}\leq\delta.

\bullet
If
(xy-uz)^{+}>0
, we have:

\varphi(u,x,y,z)=\dfrac{xy-uz}{u+y}\leq\delta

and also

\displaystyle\sigma_{GG}(u,x,y,z)

\displaystyle=\max[(x-u)^{+},\min(\varphi(u,x,y,z),(y-z)^{+})]

\displaystyle=\max[(x-u)^{+},\varphi(u,x,y,z)]

\displaystyle\leq\delta.

Let us prove now the remaining implication.

Suppose that
\sigma_{GG}(u,x,y,z)\leq\delta
:

•

If
(x-\delta)^{+}=0
, we get trivially that
(x-\delta)^{+}=0\leq u(y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta))
.

•

If
y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta)=1
, we must prove that
(x-\delta)^{+}\leq u
, which is equivalent to

(x-u)^{+}\leq\delta
. We have:

(x-u)^{+}\leq\max[(x-u)^{+},\min(\varphi(u,x,y,z),(y-z)^{+}]=\sigma_{GG}(u,x,y,z)\leq\delta.

It remains for us to study the case where
(x-\delta)^{+}=x-\delta>0
and

y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta)<1
.

From
y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta)<1
, we deduce:

y-z>\delta\quad\text{and}\quad y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta)=\dfrac{z+\delta}{y}.

Then, we have:

\displaystyle(x-\delta)^{+}-u(y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta))

\displaystyle=x-\delta-\dfrac{uz+u\delta}{y}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{u+y}{y}(\dfrac{xy-uz}{u+y}-\delta).

To obtain
(x-\delta)^{+}-u(y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta))\leq 0
, it is sufficient to prove that

\dfrac{xy-uz}{u+y}\leq\delta.

•

If
xy\leq uz
, we have
\dfrac{xy-uz}{u+y}\leq 0\leq\delta
.

•

If

xy>uz
and taking into account the inequality
y-z>\delta
, we deduce:

\delta\geq\sigma_{GG}(u,x,y,z)\geq\min(\dfrac{(xy-uz)^{+}}{u+y},y-z)=\dfrac{xy-uz}{u+y}.

∎

From (Proposition
2
), we immediately deduce:

Theorem 2
.

For any
x,y,z,u\in[0,1]
, we have:

\sigma_{GG}(u,x,y,z)=\min\{\delta\in[0,1]\,\mid\,\underline{x}(\delta)\leq u\cdot(y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta))\}.

(19)

We illustrate this result:

Example 3
.

Let
x=0.4
,

y=0.3
,

z=0.6
and

u=0.2
. We want to obtain the smallest value of
\delta\in[0,1]
so that
\underline{x}(\delta)\leq u\cdot(y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta))
is true. We have
u\cdot(y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}z)=0.2
.

We compute
\varphi(u,x,y,z)=\frac{(0.4\cdot 0.3-0.2\cdot 0.6)^{+}}{0.2+0.3}=0.

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle\!\begin{aligned} &=\sigma_{GG}(u,x,y,z)\\
&=\max[(0.4-0.2)^{+},\min(0,(0.3-0.6)^{+})]\\
&=0.2.\end{aligned}

We have
\underline{x}(\delta)=x-0.2=0.2
and
\overline{z}(\delta)=z+0.2=0.8
. Therefore,
u\cdot(y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta))=0.2
and:

\underline{x}(\delta)=u\cdot(y\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta)).

3.2
Lukasiewicz’s t-norm
T=T_{L}

For Lukasiewicz’s t-norm
T_{L}
, we study the following inequality that involves Lukasiewicz’s implication
\underset{L}{\longrightarrow}
(see (
5
)) for
\delta\in[0,1]
:

\underline{x}(\delta)\leq\max(0,\,y\underset{L}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta)-u),

(20)

where
y\underset{L}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }y-z\leq\delta\\
1-y+z+\delta&\text{ if }y-z>\delta\end{cases}
.

Let
v=x+u-1
and:

\sigma_{L}(u,x,y,z)=\min(x,\max(v^{+},\frac{(v+y-z)^{+}}{2})).

(21)

Then:

Proposition 3
.

For all
\delta\in[0,1]
, we have:

\underline{x}(\delta)\leq\max(0,y\underset{L}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta)-u)\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\sigma_{L}(u,x,y,z)\leq\delta.

(22)

Proof.

We remark that for
x\leq\delta
the two assertions of the equivalence are verified: we have

(x-\delta)^{+}=0
and
\sigma_{L}(u,x,y,z)\leq x\leq\delta
. It remains for us to show the equivalence in the case where
x>\delta
.

Set
f(u,x)=(x+u-1)^{+},\,g(u,x,y,z)=\dfrac{(x+u-1+y-z)^{+}}{2}
. Then, we have:

\sigma_{L}(u,x,y,z)=\min(x,\max(f(u,x),g(u,x,y,z))).

Let us prove that

\underline{x}(\delta)\leq\max(0,y\underset{L}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta)-u)\,\Longrightarrow\,\sigma_{L}(u,x,y,z)\leq\delta
.

As we suppose that
x>\delta
, we must prove:

\max(f(u,x),g(u,x,y,z))\leq\delta.

This last inequality will be proven in the following two steps:

1.

If
y-z\leq\delta
, then
y\underset{L}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta)-u=1-u
and we have:

x-\delta\leq\max(0,1-u)

which we put in the form

\displaystyle x-\delta-\max(0,1-u)

\displaystyle=x-\delta+\min(0,u-1)

\displaystyle=\min(x-\delta,x+u-1-\delta)

\displaystyle\leq 0.

As
x-\delta>0
, we deduce that
x+u-1\leq\delta
and also:

f(u,x)=(x+u-1)^{+}\leq\delta.

Let us show that we also have:

g(u,x,y,z)=\dfrac{(x+u-1+y-z)^{+}}{2}\leq\delta.

Indeed, if
g(u,x,y,z)=0
, we have trivially
g(u,x,y,z)=0\leq\delta
.

If
g(u,x,y,z)>0
, then we have:

\displaystyle g(u,x,y,z)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{x+u-1+y-z}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{x+u-1+\delta}{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{x+u-1}{2}+\dfrac{\delta}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{(x+u-1)^{+}}{2}+\dfrac{\delta}{2}\quad\text{(we already know that $f(u,x)=(x+u-1)^{+}\leq\delta$)}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\delta}{2}+\dfrac{\delta}{2}=\delta.

In summary, we have shown
y-z\leq\delta\,\Longrightarrow\,\max(f(u,x),g(u,x,y,z))\leq\delta
.

2.

If
y-z>\delta
, then

y\underset{L}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta)-u=1-y+z+\delta-u
and we have:

x-\delta\leq\max(0,1-y+z+\delta-u).

We rewrite this last inequality in the form:

\displaystyle x-\delta-\max(0,1-y+z+\delta-u)

\displaystyle=x-\delta+\min(0,u-1+y-z-\delta)

\displaystyle=\min(x-\delta,\,\,x+u-1\lx@nobreakspace+y-z-2\delta)

\displaystyle\leq 0.

As
x-\delta>0
, we deduce
x+u-1\lx@nobreakspace+y-z\leq 2\delta
and also:

g(u,x,y,z)=\dfrac{(x+y-z+u-1)^{+}}{2}\leq\delta.

It remains for us to show that
f(u,x)=(x+u-1)^{+}\leq\delta
.

In fact, if
f(u,x)>0
, then we have:

\displaystyle\dfrac{x+u-1+y-z}{2}

\displaystyle>\dfrac{x+u-1+\delta}{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{x+u-1}{2}+\dfrac{\delta}{2}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{f(u,x)}{2}+\dfrac{\delta}{2}.

We deduce that
x+u-1+y-z>0
, and also:

g(u,x,y,z)=\dfrac{x+u-1+y-z}{2}>\dfrac{f(u,x)}{2}+\dfrac{\delta}{2}.

As we have already shown that
g(u,x,y,z)\leq\delta
, we finally obtain:

f(u,x)<2\,g(u,x,y,z)-\delta\leq 2\delta-\delta=\delta.

In summary, we have shown
y-z>\delta\,\Longrightarrow\,\max(f(u,x),g(u,x,y,z))\leq\delta
.

We have completed the proof of the implication
\Longrightarrow
.

Let us assume now that:

\sigma_{L}(u,x,y,z)=\min(x,\max((x+u-1)^{+},\dfrac{(x+u-1+y-z)^{+}}{2}))\leq\delta

and let us show that:

(x-\delta)^{+}\leq\max(0,y\rightarrow_{L}\overline{z}(\delta)-u).

As we suppose that
x>\delta
, the inequality

\sigma_{L}(u,x,y,z)\leq\delta
becomes:

\max(f(u,x),g(u,x,y,z))=\max((x+u-1)^{+},\dfrac{(x+u-1+y-z)^{+}}{2})\leq\delta.

Let us put the inequality to be shown in the form:

(x-\delta)^{+}-\max(0,y\underset{L}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta)-u)\leq 0

and distinguish the two cases:
y-z\leq\delta
and
y-z>\delta
.

1.

If
y-z\leq\delta
, the inequality to be shown becomes:

\displaystyle x-\delta-\max(0,1-u)

\displaystyle=x-\delta+\min(0,u-1)

\displaystyle=\min(x-\delta,x+u-1-\delta)

\displaystyle\leq 0.

As
x-\delta>0
, we must show that
x+u-1-\delta\leq 0
, i.e.,
x+u-1\leq\delta
.

But, we have:

x+u-1\leq(x+u-1)^{+}=f(u,x)\leq\max(f(u,x),g(u,x,y,z))\leq\delta.

2.

If
y-z>\delta
, the inequality to be shown becomes:

\displaystyle x-\delta-\max(0,1-y+z+\delta-u)

\displaystyle=x-\delta+\min(0,u-1+y-z-\delta)

\displaystyle=\min(x-\delta,x+u-1+y-z-2\delta)

\displaystyle\leq 0.

So, we must show that
x+u-1+y-z-2\delta\leq 0
, i.e

\dfrac{x+u-1+y-z}{2}\leq\delta
.

But, we have:

\dfrac{x+u-1+y-z}{2}\leq\dfrac{(x+u-1+y-z)^{+}}{2}=g(u,x,y,z)\leq\max(f(u,x),g(u,x,y,z))\leq\delta.

We have completed the proof of implication
\Longleftarrow
.

∎

From (Proposition
3
), we immediately deduce:

Theorem 3
.

For any
x,y,z,u\in[0,1]
, we have:

\sigma_{L}(u,x,y,z)=\min\{\delta\in[0,1]\,\mid\,\underline{x}(\delta)\leq\max(0,\,y\underset{L}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta)-u)\}.

(23)

We illustrate this result:

Example 4
.

Let
x=0.4
,

y=0.6
,

z=0.3
and

u=0.6
. We want to obtain the smallest value of
\delta\in[0,1]
so that
\underline{x}(\delta)\leq\max(0,y\underset{L}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta)-u)
is true.

We have
\max(0,y\underset{L}{\longrightarrow}z-u)=0.1
and
v=x+u-1=0
.

\displaystyle\delta

\displaystyle\!\begin{aligned} &=\sigma_{L}(u,x,y,z)\\
&=\min(0.4,\max(0,\frac{(0+0.6-0.3)^{+}}{2}))\\
&=0.15.\end{aligned}

We have
\underline{x}(\delta)=x-0.15=0.25
and
\overline{z}(\delta)=z+0.15=0.45
. Therefore,
\max(0,y\underset{L}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta)-u)=0.25
and:

\underline{x}(\delta)=\max(0,y\underset{L}{\longrightarrow}\overline{z}(\delta)-u).

4
Analytical formulas to compute the Chebyshev distances associated to the second members of the systems
A\Box_{T_{P}}^{\max}x=b
and
A\Box_{T_{L}}^{\max}x=b

In this section, we compute the Chebyshev distance
\Delta
(Definition
1
) associated to the second member of a system of
\max
-product fuzzy relational equations
A\Box_{T_{P}}^{\max}x=b
, by an explicit analytical formula in terms of components of the matrix
A
and those of the second member
b
. We also give an analytical formula for the Chebyshev distance associated to the second member of a system of
\max
-Lukasiewicz fuzzy relational equations
A\Box_{T_{L}}^{\max}x=b
.

We rely on the equality:

\Delta=\min\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid\underline{b}(\delta)\leq F(\overline{b}(\delta))\},
see (
15
), which was proven for any continuous t-norm.

Notation 1
.

Using a continuous t-norm
T
whose associated implication operator is
{\cal I}_{T}
, we set for
1\leq i,k\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq m
:

•

K_{i}=\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid\underline{b}(\delta)_{i}\leq F(\overline{b}(\delta))_{i}\}
,

•

\beta_{j}=\min_{1\leq k\leq n}\,{\cal I}_{T}(a_{kj},\overline{b}(\delta))_{k})
,

•

W_{ij}^{T}=\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid\underline{b}(\delta)_{i}\leq T(a_{ij},\beta_{j})\}
.

4.1
Analytical formula to compute the Chebyshev distance associated to the second member
b
of the system
A\Box_{T_{P}}^{\max}x=b

For any
i=1,2,\dots,n
and
j=1,2,\dots,m
, we have in this case:

W_{ij}^{T_{P}}=\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid\underline{b}(\delta)_{i}\leq T_{P}(a_{ij},\beta_{j})\}=\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid\underline{b}(\delta)_{i}\leq a_{ij}\beta_{j}\}.

where
\beta_{j}=\min_{1\leq k\leq n}\,{\cal I}_{T_{P}}(a_{kj},\overline{b}(\delta)_{k})=\min_{1\leq k\leq n}\,a_{kj}\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{b}(\delta)_{k}
.
Then, we have:

W_{ij}^{T_{P}}=\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid\underline{b}(\delta)_{i}\leq\min_{1\leq k\leq n}\,a_{ij}\,(a_{kj}\underset{GG}{\longrightarrow}\overline{b}(\delta)_{k})\}.

(24)

As
F(\overline{b}(\delta))_{i}=\max_{1\leq j\leq m}\,T_{P}(a_{ij},\beta_{j})
, we still have:

K_{i}=\bigcup_{1\leq j\leq m}\,W_{ij}^{T_{P}}.

(25)

The Chebyshev distance
\Delta
associated to the second member
b
of the system of
\max
-product fuzzy relational equations
A\Box_{T_{P}}^{\max}x=b
is given by the following formula:

Theorem 4
.

\Delta=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\,\delta_{i}

(26)

where for
i=1,2,\dots,n
:

\delta_{i}=\min_{1\leq j\leq m}\,\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\,\sigma_{GG}\,(a_{ij},b_{i},a_{kj},b_{k}).

(27)

See (
18
) for the definition of
\sigma_{GG}
.

Proof.

For any
i=1,2,\dots,n
and
j=1,2,\dots,m
, we deduce from (
24
) that for

\delta\in[0,1]
, we have:

\delta\in W_{ij}^{T_{P}}\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\delta\geq\,\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\,\sigma_{GG}\,(a_{ij},b_{i},a_{kj},b_{k}).

Using (
25
), we get:

\delta\in K_{i}\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\exists\,j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}\,\,\text{such that}\,\,\delta\geq\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\,\sigma_{GG}\,(a_{ij},b_{i},a_{kj},b_{k}).

So, we obtain:

\delta\in K_{i}\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\delta\geq\min_{1\leq j\leq m}\,\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\,\sigma_{GG}\,(a_{ij},b_{i},a_{kj},b_{k}).

As by definition
\delta\in K_{i}\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\underline{b}(\delta)_{i}\leq F(\overline{b}(\delta))_{i}
and
\Delta=\min\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid\underline{b}(\delta)\leq F(\overline{b}(\delta))\}
, we get:

\Delta=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\,\min_{1\leq j\leq m}\,\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\,\sigma_{GG}\,(a_{ij},b_{i},a_{kj},b_{k}).

∎

The following example illustrates this result:

.

For any
i=1,2,\dots,n
and
j=1,2,\dots,m
, we have in this case:

W_{ij}^{T_{L}}=\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid\underline{b}(\delta)_{i}\leq T_{L}(a_{ij},\beta_{j})\}=\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid\underline{b}(\delta)_{i}\leq\max(0,\,\,a_{ij}+\beta_{j}-1)\}.

is given by the following formula:

.

\Delta=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\,\delta_{i}

(31)

where for any

:

) for the definition of
\sigma_{L}

Proof.

For any
i=1,2,\dots,n
and
j=1,2,\dots,m
, we deduce from (

\delta\in[0,1]
, we have:

\delta\in W_{ij}^{T_{L}}\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\delta\geq\,\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\,\sigma_{LK}\,(1-a_{ij},b_{i},a_{kj},b_{k}).

Using (
30
), we get:

\delta\in K_{i}\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\exists\,j\in\{1,2,\dots,m\}\,\,\text{such that}\,\,\delta\geq\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\,\sigma_{LK}\,(1-a_{ij},b_{i},a_{kj},b_{k}).

So, we obtain:

\delta\in K_{i}\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\delta\geq\min_{1\leq j\leq m}\,\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\,\sigma_{LK}\,(1-a_{ij},b_{i},a_{kj},b_{k}).

As by definition
\delta\in K_{i}\,\Longleftrightarrow\,\underline{b}(\delta)_{i}\leq F(\overline{b}(\delta))_{i}
and
\Delta=\min\{\delta\in[0,1]\mid\underline{b}(\delta)\leq F(\overline{b}(\delta))\}
, we get:

\Delta=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}\,\min_{1\leq j\leq m}\,\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\,\sigma_{LK}\,(1-a_{ij},b_{i},a_{kj},b_{k}).

∎

5
Conclusion

In this article, we gave explicit analytical formulas for computing the Chebyshev distance associated to the second member of a system of
\max
-product fuzzy relational equations and that associated to the second member of a system of
\max
-Lukasiewicz fuzzy relational equations.

The results of
(
Baaj 2023
)
may be then extended to these two systems. For each system, we can describe the sets of Chebyshev approximations of its second member and its approximate solutions set. Similarly to the
\max-\min
learning paradigm introduced in
(
Baaj 2023
)
, in which the learning error is expressed in terms of
L_{\infty}
norm, we can tackle the development of a
\max
-product (resp.
\max
-Lukasiewicz) learning paradigm. The formula of
(
Baaj 2023
)
, which computes the minimum value of the learning error according to training data and the method to construct approximate weight matrices whose learning error is minimal can be directly extended to the case of the
\max
-product composition or the
\max
-Lukasiewicz composition.

As applications, we have the complete solution of the problem of the invertibility of a fuzzy relation for
\max
-Lukasiewicz composition: we know the set of matrices
A
which admit a pre-inverse or a post-inverse. Moreover, for
\max-\min
composition, if a matrix
A
has no preinverse (resp. postinverse), we know how to compute, using the
L_{\infty}
norm, an approximate preinverse (resp. postinverse) for
A
.
For both cases of the
\max-\min
composition and the
\max
-product composition, the problem of the invertibility of a fuzzy relation is already solved
(
Wu et al. 2021
;
Wu et al. 2022
)
.

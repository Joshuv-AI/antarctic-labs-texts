Supercloseness of the NIPG method for a singularly perturbed convection diffusion problem on Shishkin mesh in 2D
Xiaoqi Ma, Jin Zhang

Abstract
As a popular stabilization technique, the nonsymmetric interior penalty Galerkin (NIPG) method has significant application value in computational fluid dynamics. In this paper, we study the NIPG method for a typical two-dimensional singularly perturbed convection diffusion problem on a Shishkin mesh. According to the characteristics of the solution, the mesh and numerical scheme, a new composite interpolation is introduced. In fact, this interpolation is composed of a vertices-edges-element interpolation within the layer and a local L 2 L^{2} -projection outside the layer. On the basis of that, by selecting penalty parameters on different types of interelement edges, we further obtain the supercloseness of almost k + 1 2 k+\frac{1}{2} order in an energy norm. Here k k is the degree of piecewise polynomials. Numerical tests support our theoretical conclusion.



1
Introduction

We consider a typical two-dimensional singularly perturbed problem:

\displaystyle Lu:=-\varepsilon\Delta u+\textbf{b}\cdot\nabla u+cu=f,\quad\text{in $\Omega:=(0,1)\times(0,1)$},

(1)

\displaystyle u=0,\quad\text{on $\Gamma=\partial\Omega$},

where the perturbation parameter
\varepsilon
satisfies
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
,
b
,
c
,
f
are sufficiently smooth functions and satisfy the following conditions

\displaystyle\textbf{b}(x,y)=(b_{1}(x,y),b_{2}(x,y))\geq(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})>0,\quad c(x,y)\geq 0,\quad(x,y)\in\overline{\Omega},

(2)

\displaystyle c_{0}^{2}(x,y):=\left(c-\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\textbf{b}\right)(x,y)\geq\gamma_{0}>0,\quad(x,y)\in\overline{\Omega},

\displaystyle f(0,0)=f(1,0)=f(0,1)=f(1,1)=0,

with some fixed constants
\beta_{1}
,
\beta_{2}
and
\gamma_{0}
. Because the parameter
\varepsilon
can be arbitrarily small, the solution to (
1
) usually exhibits boundary layers of width
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon\ln(1/\varepsilon))
near
x=1
and
y=1
. This is one of the classical behaviors of singular perturbation problems, namely that the solutions of these problems might change rapidly in a thin region.
However, most traditional numerical methods can not capture this rapid change. To overcome this difficulty, many strategies have been formulated. Among them, as a local refinement strategy, layer-adapted meshes are becoming more and more popular
[
9
,
12
,
13
]
. There are two typical layer-adapted meshes in the literature—Bakhvalov meshes and Shishkin meshes (see
[
16
]
). Due to the simple structure of Shishkin meshes, they have been widely used in the convergence theory of finite element methods; see
[
6
,
11
,
13
,
23
]
and its references.

Discontinuous Galerkin finite element methods (DGFEMs) were developed in the early 1970s.
So far, many variants have been introduced and studied; see
[
1
,
2
,
4
,
20
,
24
]
. They allow the discontinuity of finite element functions on the element interface, and allow high-order schemes to be constructed in a natural way.
As a variant of DGFEMs, the nonsymmetric interior penalty Galerkin (NIPG) method has its unique advantages.
For example, the NIPG method is increasingly favored by researchers
[
14
,
22
,
24
]
, because
it is coercive on any mesh.

In order to obtain more accurate numerical solutions, we focus on the supercloseness of the NIPG method. Here ’supercloseness’ means that the numerical solution is closer to the interpolation of the exact solution rather than the exact solution itself. For general theory and development of supercloseness, readers can refer to
[
6
,
8
,
11
,
18
,
23
]
and their references. In
[
15
]
and
[
21
]
, the authors used the combination of the standard Galerkin finite element method and the NIPG method to study two different convection diffusion problems in 2D. By means of a nodal bilinear interpolating function in the layer and a local
L^{2}
-projection outside the layer, they obtained the supercloseness of almost
\frac{3}{2}
order. However, this approach is neither pure nor high-order NIPG method. Compared with this low-order coupling method, on the one hand, the pure NIPG method will be simpler in the implementation of programming, and will be more flexible in the selection of meshes and polynomial degrees on the elements; on the other hand, the high-order NIPG method can achieve higher accuracy. To the best of our knowledge, there is no literature that studies the supercloseness of two-dimensional singularly perturbed convection diffusion problems by using high-order NIPG methods.

The purpose of this paper is to prove the supercloseness of a two-dimensional singularly perturbed problem by using a high-order NIPG method on Shishkin mesh.
In this process, we construct a new composite interpolation, which is composed of a local
L^{2}
-projection outside the layer and a vertices-edges-element interpolation inside the layer. For one thing, we can use the continuity of vertices-edges-element interpolation to eliminate the estimation of some jump terms; and use some convergence properties of this interpolation to improve the accuracy of diffusion term in the layer. For another, according to the definition of the local
L^{2}
projection, the convergence order of convective term outside the layer can be improved.
Actually, these are the two main difficulties that we need to overcome in the analysis of supercloseness.
Based on this new interpolation, by defining penalty parameters at different interfaces, we obtain the supercloseness of almost
k+\frac{1}{2}
order in an energy norm, which appears in the literature for the first time. Finally, a numerical experiment is presented to verify the main conclusion.

The following is the outline of this article. In Section 2, we present
a prior
information of the solution and some basic concepts. Then in Section 3, a piecewise uniform mesh and the corresponding NIPG method are introduced. In Section 4, we design a new composite interpolation and derive some interpolation error estimates. Finally, we provide the main result of supercloseness, which can be verified by a numerical experiment.

For any element

, let

\kappa
. Then we use
\textbf{n}_{\kappa}(x,y)
, or simply
\textbf{n}_{\kappa}

. On the basis of that, the inflow and outflow parts of

\displaystyle\partial_{-}\kappa=\{(x,y)\in\partial\kappa:\textbf{b}(x,y)\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa}(x,y)<0\},

Let

.

, there exist indices

\kappa:=\kappa_{j}

. Then for

\kappa^{\prime}

For any element
\kappa\in\mathcal{T}

3
Shishkin mesh and NIPG method

3.1
Shishkin mesh

Based on the information of layers presented in Theorem
1
, we use a piecewise uniform mesh—Shishkin mesh.
First, the set of mesh points
\Omega_{N}=\{(x_{i},y_{j})\in\overline{\Omega}:i,j=0,1,\cdots,N\}
is constructed. Set
h_{i,x}:=x_{i+1}-x_{i}
and
h_{j,y}:=y_{j+1}-y_{j}
for all
0\leq i,j\leq N-1
. And a mesh rectangle is often written as
\kappa_{ij}=(x_{i},x_{i+1})\times(y_{j},y_{j+1})
for a specific element and as
\kappa
for a generic mesh rectangle.

In order to distinguish the layer part from the smooth part, we introduce the mesh transition parameters
\lambda_{x}
and
\lambda_{y}

\lambda_{x}=\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{\sigma_{x}\varepsilon}{\beta_{1}}\ln N\right\},\quad\lambda_{y}=\min\left\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{\sigma_{y}\varepsilon}{\beta_{2}}\ln N\right\},

where
\beta_{1}
and
\beta_{2}
are the lower bounds from (
2
) for the convective part
b
. For convenience, we take
\sigma_{x}=\sigma_{y}=\sigma
and set
\sigma\geq k+\frac{3}{2}
. Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
N\geq 8
, be divisible by
2
in both the
x
– and
y
–directions.
Then divide the subintervals
[0,1-\lambda_{x}]
and
[1-\lambda_{x},1]
into
N/2
uniform mesh intervals respectively, and the division of

–direction is similar.
Therefore, Shishkin mesh points can be denoted as

x_{i}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{2(1-\lambda_{x})}{N}i\quad&&\text{for $i=0,1,...,N/2$},\\
&1-\lambda_{x}+\frac{2\lambda_{x}}{N}(i-\frac{N}{2})&&\text{for $i=N/2+1,...,N$}\end{aligned}\right.

and

y_{j}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{2(1-\lambda_{y})}{N}j\quad&&\text{for $j=0,1,...,N/2$},\\
&1-\lambda_{y}+\frac{2\lambda_{y}}{N}(j-\frac{N}{2})&&\text{for $j=N/2+1,...,N$}.\end{aligned}\right.

Moreover, divide the domain
\Omega
as
\Omega=\Omega_{11}\cup\Omega_{12}\cup\Omega_{21}\cap\Omega_{22}
, where

\displaystyle\Omega_{11}:=(0,1-\lambda_{x})\times(0,1-\lambda_{y}),\quad\quad\Omega_{12}:=(1-\lambda_{x},1)\times(0,1-\lambda_{y}),

\displaystyle\Omega_{21}:=(0,1-\lambda_{x})\times(1-\lambda_{y},1),\quad\quad\Omega_{22}:=(1-\lambda_{x},1)\times(1-\lambda_{y},1)

and we can easily see that

3.2
The NIPG method

On the above Shishkin mesh, define the finite element space by

V_{N}^{k}:=\{v\in L^{2}(\Omega):v|_{\Gamma}=0,v|_{\kappa}\in\mathcal{Q}_{k}(\kappa)\quad\forall\kappa\in\mathcal{T}\},

where
\mathcal{Q}_{k}=span\{x^{i}y^{j}:1\leq i,j\leq k\}
.
The function
v\in V_{N}^{k}
is piecewise continuous on each element
\kappa

Now the weak formulation of (
1

u_{h}\in V_{N}^{k}
such that

B(u_{h},v_{h})=L(v_{h})\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{N}^{k},

(4)

where
B(u,v)=B_{1}(u,v)+B_{2}(u,v)+B_{3}(u,v)
and

\displaystyle B_{1}(u,v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\varepsilon\int_{\kappa}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y-\varepsilon\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{e}\langle\nabla u\cdot\nu\rangle[v]\mathrm{d}s+\varepsilon\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{e}[u]\langle\nabla v\cdot\nu\rangle\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}}\int_{e}\rho_{e}[u][v]\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle B_{2}(u,v)=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\left(\int_{\kappa}(\textbf{b}\cdot\nabla u)v\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\partial_{-}\kappa\cap\Gamma}(\textbf{b}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa})u^{+}v^{+}\mathrm{d}s-\int_{\partial_{-}\kappa/\Gamma}(\textbf{b}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa})\lfloor u\rfloor v^{+}\mathrm{d}s\right),

\displaystyle B_{3}(u,v)=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{\kappa}cuv\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle L(v)=\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{\kappa}fv\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y,

The edges of type

4.1

In this section, we design a new interpolation

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon N^{-(k+1)}\left(\sum_{e\subset\Omega^{*}}\int_{e}1^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{e\subset\Omega^{*}}\int_{e}\langle\nabla\xi\cdot\nu\rangle^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon N^{-(k+1)}N^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{e\subset\Omega^{*}}\int_{e}\langle\nabla\xi\cdot\nu\rangle^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon N^{-(k+1)}N^{\frac{1}{2}}h_{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{\kappa\subset\Omega^{*}}\int_{\kappa}(\nabla\xi)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}N^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{\kappa\subset\Omega^{*}}\varepsilon\int_{\kappa}(\nabla\xi)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{-k}\|\xi\|_{NIPG},

here
h_{e}
represents the minimum length of all edges
e
in
\kappa\subset\Omega_{11}
.
Furthermore, for
e\subset\partial\Omega_{11}/\Gamma
, using the similar method and (
17
),

\displaystyle|\sum_{e\subset\partial\Omega_{11}/\Gamma}\varepsilon\int_{e}[\eta]\langle\nabla\xi\cdot\nu\rangle\mathrm{d}s|\leq C\sum_{e\subset\partial\Omega_{11}/\Gamma}\varepsilon\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega/\Omega_{22})}\int_{e}\langle\nabla\xi\cdot\nu\rangle\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon N^{-(k+1)}(\ln N)^{k+1}\left(\sum_{e\subset\partial\Omega_{11}/\Gamma}\int_{e}1^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{e\subset\partial\Omega_{11}/\Gamma}\int_{e}\langle\nabla\xi\cdot\nu\rangle^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon N^{-(k+1)}(\ln N)^{k+1}h_{e}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{\kappa\subset\Omega/\Omega_{22}}\int_{\kappa}(\nabla\xi)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon N^{-(k+1)}(\ln N)^{k+1}(\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{\kappa\subset\Omega/\Omega_{22}}\int_{\kappa}(\nabla\xi)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{\kappa\subset\Omega/\Omega_{22}}\varepsilon\int_{\kappa}(\nabla\xi)^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}.

where
h_{e}
represents the minimum length of all edges in
\kappa\subset\Omega/\Omega_{22}
.
Therefore, we can derive

|\mathrm{IV}|\leq CN^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{k+\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}.

(30)

Then for
\mathrm{V}
, using the nature of vertices-edges-element interpolation on
\overline{\Omega/\Omega_{11}}
, we just analyze it on
\overline{\Omega}_{11}
. Through (
5
), (
16
) and
\rho_{e}=1
for
e\subset\Omega^{*}
,

\displaystyle|\sum_{e\subset\Omega^{*}}\int_{e}\rho_{e}[\eta][\xi]\mathrm{d}s|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{e\subset\Omega^{*}}\int_{e}\rho_{e}[\eta]^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{e\subset\Omega^{*}}\int_{e}\rho_{e}[\xi]^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{e\subset\Omega^{*}}\int_{e}\rho_{e}[\eta]^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{11})}N^{2}N^{-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}.

Furthermore, for
e\subset\partial\overline{\Omega}_{11}/\Gamma
, one also obtains

\displaystyle|\sum_{e\subset\partial\overline{\Omega}_{11}/\Gamma}\int_{e}\rho_{e}[\eta][\xi]\mathrm{d}s|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{e\subset\partial\overline{\Omega}_{11}/\Gamma}\int_{e}\rho_{e}[\eta]^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(N\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega/\Omega_{22})}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{k+1}\|\xi\|_{NIPG},

where
\rho_{e}=N
for
e\subset\partial\overline{\Omega}_{11}/\Gamma
have been used. So far, there is

|\mathrm{V}|\leq CN^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{k+1}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}.

(31)

Finally, we consider
\mathrm{VI}
. Applying integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\left(\int_{\kappa}(\textbf{b}\cdot\nabla\eta)\xi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y-\int_{\partial_{-}\kappa\cap\Gamma}(\textbf{b}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa})\eta^{+}\xi^{+}\mathrm{d}s-\int_{\partial_{-}\kappa/\Gamma}(\textbf{b}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa})\lfloor\eta\rfloor\xi^{+}\mathrm{d}s\right)

(32)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{\kappa}\eta(\textbf{b}\cdot\nabla\xi)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+2\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{\kappa}(c_{0}^{2}-c)\eta\xi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{\partial_{+}\kappa\cap\Gamma}(\textbf{b}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa})\eta^{+}\xi^{+}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle+\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\left(\int_{\partial_{+}\kappa/\Gamma}(\textbf{b}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa})\eta^{+}\xi^{+}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\partial_{-}\kappa/\Gamma}(\textbf{b}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa})\eta^{-}\xi^{+}\mathrm{d}s\right)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}_{1}+\mathcal{R}_{2}+\mathcal{R}_{3}+\mathcal{R}_{4},

where the function
c_{0}^{2}
is defined in (
2
). Then for
\mathcal{R}_{2}
, through the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality one has

\displaystyle|\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{\kappa}(c_{0}^{2}-c)\eta\xi\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y|

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\|\eta\|_{L^{2}(\kappa)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}

(33)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(N^{-(k+1)}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}N^{-(k+1)}(\ln N)^{k+1}\right)\|\xi\|_{NIPG}.

For
\mathcal{R}_{3}
, using (
16
) and (
17
), we have

\displaystyle|\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{\partial_{+}\kappa\cap\Gamma}(\textbf{b}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa})\eta^{+}\xi^{+}\mathrm{d}s|

(34)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\left(\int_{\partial_{+}\kappa\cap\Gamma}(\textbf{b}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa})(\eta^{+})^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\partial_{+}\kappa\cap\Gamma}(\textbf{b}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa})(\xi^{+})^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{\partial_{+}\kappa\cap\Gamma}(\textbf{b}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa})(\eta^{+})^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{\partial_{+}\kappa\cap\Gamma}(\textbf{b}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa})(\xi^{+})^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{\partial_{+}\kappa\cap\Gamma}(\textbf{b}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa})(\eta^{+})^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}NN^{-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-(k+1)}(\ln N)^{k+1}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}.

Furthermore, the last two integrals
\mathcal{R}_{4}
in (
32
) can be estimated as in
[
7
]
, that is

\displaystyle|\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\left(\int_{\partial_{+}\kappa/\Gamma}(\textbf{b}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa})\eta^{+}\xi^{+}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\partial_{-}\kappa/\Gamma}(\text{b}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa})\eta^{-}\xi^{+}\mathrm{d}s\right)|

(35)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{\partial_{-}\kappa/\Gamma}(\textbf{b}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa})(\eta^{-})^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{\kappa\in\mathcal{T}}\int_{\partial_{-}\kappa/\Gamma}(\textbf{b}\cdot\textbf{n}_{\kappa})(\xi^{+}-\xi^{-})^{2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}N^{2}N^{-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{k+1}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}.

Below, let us estimate
\mathcal{R}_{1}
. On the one hand, for
\kappa\subset\Omega_{11}
, let
\overline{\textbf{b}}=(\overline{b}_{1},\overline{b}_{2})
denote a local piecewise constant approximation of
\textbf{b}=(b_{1},b_{2})
. Then, we can derive

\displaystyle\sum_{\kappa\subset\Omega_{11}}\int_{\kappa}(\textbf{b}\cdot\nabla\xi)\eta\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y=\sum_{\kappa\subset\Omega_{11}}\int_{\kappa}(\textbf{b}-\overline{\textbf{b}})\cdot\nabla\xi\eta\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y,

which follows from the definition of the local
L^{2}
-projection operator (
9
). Therefore,

\displaystyle|\sum_{\kappa\subset\Omega_{11}}\int_{\kappa}(\textbf{b}-\overline{\textbf{b}})\cdot\nabla\xi\eta\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y|

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\kappa\subset{\Omega_{11}}}N^{-1}\|\eta\|_{L^{2}(\kappa)}\|\nabla\xi\|_{L^{2}(\kappa)}

(36)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{\kappa\subset{\Omega_{11}}}\|\eta\|_{L^{2}(\kappa)}\|\xi\|_{L^{2}(\kappa)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{\kappa\subset{\Omega_{11}}}\|\eta\|_{L^{2}(\kappa)}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-(k+1)}\|\xi\|_{NIPG},

where
b
is sufficiently smooth, then the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the local inverse inequality
[
3
, Theorem 3.2.6]
for
\xi\in V_{N}^{k}
have been applied.

On the other hand, for
\kappa\subset\Omega/\Omega_{11}
, from Hölder inequalities and (
12
),

\displaystyle|\sum_{\kappa\subset\Omega/\Omega_{11}}\int_{\kappa}\eta(\textbf{b}\cdot\nabla\xi)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y|

(37)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{\kappa\subset\Omega/\Omega_{11}}\|\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\kappa)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{\kappa\subset\Omega/\Omega_{11}}\|\nabla\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\kappa)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\varepsilon N^{-2(k+1)}(\ln N)^{2(k+1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{\kappa\subset\Omega/\Omega_{11}}\|\nabla\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\kappa)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-(k+1)}(\ln N)^{k+1}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}.

Substituting (
33
)-(
37
) into (
32
), we can derive the following estimate directly, that is

\mathrm{VI}\leq CN^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{k+1}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}.

(38)

Last but not least, by (
27
), (
28
), (
29
), (
30
), (
31
) and (
38
), there is

\displaystyle\|\xi\|^{2}_{NIPG}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{I}+\mathrm{II}+\mathrm{III}+\mathrm{IV}+\mathrm{V}+\mathrm{VI}\leq CN^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{k+1}\|\xi\|_{NIPG}.

Now we give the main conclusion of supercloseness.

Theorem 3
.

Let Assumption
1
hold. Suppose that
\rho_{e}
for
e\in\mathcal{E}
are defined as (
5
) and the definition of
I_{N}u
is presented in Section 4.1.
Then on the Shishkin mesh with
\sigma\geq k+\frac{3}{2}
we derive

\displaystyle\|I_{N}u-u_{h}\|_{NIPG}+\|\Pi u-u_{h}\|_{NIPG}\leq CN^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{k+1},

where
\Pi u
is the interpolation of the exact solution to (
1
), and
u_{h}
is the solution of (
4
).

Proof.

First, by using the triangle inequality, there is

\displaystyle\|I_{N}u-u_{h}\|_{NIPG}\leq\|I_{N}u-\Pi u\|_{NIPG}+\|\Pi u-u_{h}\|_{NIPG}.

Here
I_{N}u
denotes vertices-edges-element interpolation on
\Omega
,
\Pi u
is the interpolation defined in (
8
), while
u_{h}
is the numerical solution of (
1
).
Note that
\|\Pi u-u_{h}\|_{NIPG}
is known, so we just analyze the bound of
\|I_{N}u-\Pi u\|_{NIPG}
in the following.

According to the definition of
\Pi u
, we just analyze the estimate of
\|I_{N}u-P_{h}u\|_{NIPG}
outside the layer, where
P_{h}u
represents the local
L^{2}
projection. From the triangle inequality, one has

\|I_{N}u-P_{h}u\|_{NIPG,\overline{\Omega}_{11}}^{2}\leq C\|I_{N}u-u\|_{NIPG,\overline{\Omega}_{11}}^{2}+C\|u-P_{h}u\|_{NIPG,\overline{\Omega}_{11}}^{2}.

According to (
18
) and (
19
), we obtain

\|I_{N}u-P_{h}u\|_{NIPG,\overline{\Omega}_{11}}\leq CN^{-(k+\frac{1}{2})}(\ln N)^{k+1}.

So far, the proof of this theorem has done.
∎

Well-posedness for the incompressible Hall-MHD system with initial magnetic field belonging to H 3 2 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) H^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
Shunhang Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we first prove the local well-posedness of strong solutions to the incompressible Hall-MHD system for initial data ( u 0 , B 0 ) ∈ H 1 2 + σ ​ ( ℝ 3 ) × H 3 2 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) (u_{0},B_{0})\in H^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}) with σ ∈ ( 0 , 2 ) \sigma\in(0,2) . In particular, if the viscosity coefficient is equal to the resistivity coefficient, we can reduce σ \sigma to 0 0 with the aid of the new formulation of the Hall-MHD system observed by Danchin and Tan (Commun Partial Differ Equ 46(1):31-65, 2021). Compared with the previous works, our local well-posedness results improve the regularity condition on the initial data. Moreover, we establish the global well-posedness for small initial data in H 1 2 + σ ​ ( ℝ 3 ) × H 3 2 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) H^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}) with σ ∈ ( 0 , 2 ) \sigma\in(0,2) , and get the optimal time-decay rates of solutions.



Keywords:
Hall-MHD; Well-posedness; Sobolev spaces; Optimal time-decay rates

Introduction

This paper is concerned with the Cauchy problem of the viscous and resistive incompressible Hall-magnetohydrodynamics (Hall-MHD) system in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\mu\Delta u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla P=B\cdot\nabla B,\\
\partial_{t}B-\nu\Delta B-\nabla\times(u\times B)+\kappa\nabla\times((\nabla\times B)\times B)=0,\\
\Div u=\Div B=0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad B(x,0)=B_{0}(x).\end{cases}

(1.1)

Here,
u=u(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
B(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and the scalar function
P=P(t,x)
with
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
stand for the velocity field, the magnetic field and the scalar pressure, respectively. The positive parameters
\mu
,
\nu
and
\kappa
are viscosity coefficient, resistivity coefficient and Hall effect coefficient, respectively.

Notice that if
\Div B_{0}=0
, the divergence-free property
\Div B=0
is preserved all the time yielding

-\nabla\times(u\times B)=u\cdot\nabla B-B\cdot\nabla u.

(1.2)

Compared with the classical MHD system, the Hall-MHD system possesses the so-called Hall term
\kappa\nabla\times((\nabla\times B)\times B)
in the magnetic equation. Due to the appearance of this extra term, the Hall-MHD system can be applied to describing the magnetic reconnection phenomenon, which occurs in plasmas, star formation, solar flares, neutron stars or geo-dynamo, etc (see
[
15
,
14
,
23
,
3
,
19
,
18
]
). Meanwhile, since the Hall term contains the second order derivatives, it seems that the mathematical analysis of the Hall-MHD system becomes more complicated than that for the classical MHD system.

Owing to its significance in physics and mathematics, the Hall-MHD sytem has drawn much attention from various researchers. Next, we briefly introduce some of them which are related to our problem. Acheritogaray et al. in
[
1
]
gave a rigorous mathematical derivation of the Hall-MHD system both from a two-fluids model and from a kinetic model. Chae et al.
[
6
]
established the global existence of weak solutions as well as the local well-posedness of smooth solutions for initial data in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
s>{5\over 2}
. Moreover, they obtained the global well-posedness of smooth solutions for small initial data. Subsequently, some authors
[
7
,
21
,
22
,
20
]
relaxed the smallness condition by only requiring some suitable Sobolev or Besov norms of initial data to be small. Serrin type blow-up criteria for smooth solutions were derived by Chae and Lee in
[
7
]
. Local well-posedness of strong solutions for initial data with improved regularity condition in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
were obtained in
[
4
,
10
,
26
]
, these results relax the restriction on
s
to
s>{3\over 2}
. Recently, Dai
[
11
]
proved the local well-posedness of
(
1.1
)
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{s+1-\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
s>{1\over 2}
and sufficiently small
\sigma>0
such that
s+1-\sigma>{3\over 2}
. Fan et al.
[
13
]
established the global well-posedness of
(
1.1
)
for a class of axisymmetric initial data. The authors in
[
12
,
17
]
proved the global well-posedness of
(
1.1
)
for small initial data in critical Besov spaces by means of some new observations. In addition, it is significant to investigate the large time behavior of solutions to
(
1.1
)
. Chae and Schonbek
[
8
]
obtained the time-decay estimates for weak solutions and smooth solutions with small initial data by using the Fourier splitting techniques. Weng
[
25
,
24
]
studied the analyticity and space-time decay estimates for smooth solutions.

On the other hand, observe that the system
(
1.1
)
does not have any scaling invariance as the classical MHD system. However, if we set
B\equiv 0
in
(
1.1
)
, then the system
(
1.1
)
becomes the incompressible Navier-Stokes system:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\mu\Delta u+u\cdot\nabla u+\nabla P=0,\\
\Div u=0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(1.3)

which is invariant for all
\lambda>0
under the change

(u(t,x),P(t,x),u_{0}(x))\mapsto(\lambda u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),\lambda^{2}P(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),\lambda u_{0}(\lambda x)).

(1.4)

Also, if
u\equiv 0
, one may see that

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}B-\nu\Delta B+\kappa\nabla\times((\nabla\times B)\times B)=0,\\
B(x,0)=B_{0}(x),\end{cases}

(1.5)

which is invariant for all
\lambda>0
under the change

(B(t,x),B_{0}(x))\mapsto(B(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x),B_{0}(\lambda x)).

(1.6)

We should point out that all previous results on the local well-posedness of
(
1.1
)
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
require
s>{1\over 2}
and
r>{3\over 2}
. Whereas, from the viewpoint of scaling invariance, the scalings
(
1.4
)
and
(
1.6
)
suggest that
u_{0}\in H^{{1\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
or
B_{0}\in H^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
may be a suitable data choice for
(
1.1
)
to establish local well-posedness. Motivated by this, we attempt to do this in paper. The first result of this paper is to prove the local well-posedness of
(
1.1
)
for
B_{0}\in H^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
u_{0}
is slightly regular than
H^{{1\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, which is stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<\sigma<2
and
(u_{0},B_{0})\in H^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\Div u_{0}=\Div B_{0}=0
. Then there exists a positive time
T
such that the system
(
1.1
)
admits a unique solution
(u,B)
with

\begin{split}u&\in C([0,T];H^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{{3\over 2}+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\\
B&\in C([0,T];H^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{{5\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).\end{split}

(1.7)

Remark 1.1
.

Compared with the previous local well-posedness results in nonhomogeneous Sobolev spaces (see e.g.
[
11
,
26
,
10
,
6
,
4
]
), our result improves the regularity condition on the initial magnetic field
B_{0}
to
B_{0}\in H^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Remark 1.2
.

Let us explain why we can tackle the magnetic field
B
in
H^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. In
[
11
,
26
,
10
,
6
,
4
]
, the authors derived the uniform bounds for approximate solutions by establishing the inequality of the following type:

\begin{split}&{d\over dt}\Big(\|u(t)\|_{{H}^{s}}^{2}+\|B(t)\|_{{H}^{r}}^{2}\Big)+\|\nabla u(t)\|_{{H}^{s}}^{2}+\|\nabla B(t)\|_{{H}^{r}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim\Big(\|u(t)\|_{{H}^{s}}^{2}+\|B(t)\|_{{H}^{r}}^{2}\Big)^{1+\alpha}\quad\text{for some}\quad\alpha>0.\end{split}

(1.8)

However, when tackling the
\dot{H}^{r}
estimates of the magnetic equation, one usually use Lemma
2.6
and interpolation to treat the Hall term as

\begin{split}\Big(\Lambda^{r}\nabla\times(B\times(\nabla\times B)),\Lambda^{r}B\Big)&\lesssim\|[\Lambda^{r},B\times](\nabla\times B)\|_{L^{2}}\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{r+1}}\\
&\lesssim\|\Lambda^{r}B\|_{L^{p_{1}}}\|\nabla B\|_{L^{p_{2}}}\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{r+1}}\\
&\lesssim\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{r+{3\over p_{2}}}}\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{1+{3\over p_{1}}}}\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{r+1}}\\
&\lesssim\Big(\|B\|_{{H}^{r}}^{1-{3\over p_{2}}}\|\nabla B\|_{{H}^{r}}^{{3\over p_{2}}}\Big)\Big(\|B\|_{{H}^{r}}^{r-{3\over p_{1}}}\|\nabla B\|_{{H}^{r}}^{{3\over p_{1}}+1-r}\Big)\|\nabla B\|_{{H}^{r}}\\
&\lesssim\|B\|_{{H}^{r}}^{r-{1\over 2}}\|\nabla B\|_{{H}^{r}}^{{7\over 2}-r},\end{split}

(1.9)

where
\Lambda:=(-\Delta)^{{1\over 2}}
and
{1\over p_{1}}+{1\over p_{2}}={1\over 2}
with
p_{2}\geq 3
. From above, the requirement
r>{3\over 2}
seems inevitable due to the use of Young’s inequality for splitting the last term. Thus, such an analysis inspires us to choose another method to deal with the case
B_{0}\in H^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Instead, we will use the classical continuity method to establish the uniform bounds in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, where the Fourier localization techniques and paradifferential calculus play a crucial role.

Notice that the product estimate

\|\nabla\times(u\times B)\|_{\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}-\eta}}\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}+\eta}}+\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}+\theta}}\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}-\theta}}

(1.10)

only holds for
\eta,\theta>0
, so in order to bound
\|\nabla\times(u\times B)\|_{L_{t}^{2}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}})}
in the proof of Theorem
1.1
, the velocity
u
should belong to
L^{p}_{t}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}+\theta})
for some
p>2
. To attain this, we need to let initial velocity
u_{0}\in\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}
for some
\sigma>\theta>0
due to the parabolic smooth effect, which is the reason why the restriction
\sigma>0
exists in Theorem
1.1
. Recently, Danchin and Tan in
[
12
]
observed that if
\mu=\nu
, regarding
v:=u-\kappa\nabla\times B
as a new unknown, the system
(
1.1
)
can be rewritten as (one can see
[
12
, P36-37]
for derivation):

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\mu\Delta u=B\cdot\nabla B-u\cdot\nabla u-\nabla P,\\
\partial_{t}B-\mu\Delta B=\nabla\times(v\times B),\\
\partial_{t}v-\mu\Delta v=B\cdot\nabla B-u\cdot\nabla u-\kappa\nabla\times((\nabla\times v)\times B)\\
+\nabla\times(v\times u)+2\kappa\nabla\times(v\cdot\nabla B)-\nabla P,\\
\Div u=\Div B=\Div v=0.\end{cases}

(1.11)

Based on this new formulation, they prove the global well-posedness of
(
1.1
)
for small data in critical Besov spaces
\dot{B}^{{1\over 2}}_{2,r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times(\dot{B}^{{1\over 2}}_{2,r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap\dot{B}^{{3\over 2}}_{2,r}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
with
1\leq r\leq\infty
and local well-posedness for large data in
\dot{B}^{{1\over 2}}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times(\dot{B}^{{1\over 2}}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap\dot{B}^{{3\over 2}}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
. Actually, in the case of
\mu=\nu
, considering
(
1.11
)
instead of
(
1.1
)
brings us more advantages. Specifically, as
\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}}}+\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}}}
, it suffices to derive the
\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}}
-bound of
(u,B,v)
, which can help us avoid using the estimate
(
1.10
)
. Besides, the quasilinear term
\kappa\nabla\times((\nabla\times v)\times B)
has a cancelation property. By ultilizing
(
1.11
)
, we indeed can reduce
\sigma
to
0
in Theorem
1.1
when
\mu=\nu
. Precisely, we have

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
\mu=\nu
. Let
(u_{0},B_{0})\in H^{{1\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
\Div u_{0}=\Div B_{0}=0
. Then there exists a positive time
T
such that the system
(
1.1
)
admits a unique solution
(u,B)
with

\begin{split}u&\in C([0,T];H^{{1\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\\
B&\in C([0,T];H^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{{5\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).\end{split}

(1.12)

Remark 1.3
.

Because
\dot{B}^{s}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
are not embedded into each other when
s>0
, Theorem
1.2
is different from the local well-posedness result in
\dot{B}^{{1\over 2}}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times(\dot{B}^{{1\over 2}}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap\dot{B}^{{3\over 2}}_{2,1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
mentioned above (see
[
12
, Theorem 2.2]
).

To the best of our knowledge, there are no available global well-posedness result concerning
(
1.1
)
with
B_{0}\in H^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for the general
\mu
and
\nu
, so the natural next step is to extend the local strong solutions constructed in Theorem
1.1
globally in time. Actually, we find that this can be achieved for small initial data. Furthermore, we obtain the optimal time-decay rates of solutions.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that the initial data
(u_{0},B_{0})
satisfies the conditions in Theorem
1.1
. There exists a small positive constant
c_{0}=c_{0}(\mu,\nu,\kappa,\sigma)
such that if

\|u_{0}\|_{H^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}+\|B_{0}\|_{H^{{3\over 2}}}\leq c_{0},

(1.13)

then the system
(
1.1
)
has a unique global solution
(u,B)
with

\begin{split}u&\in C([0,\infty);H^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\quad\nabla u\in L^{2}(0,\infty;H^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\\
B&\in C([0,\infty);H^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),\quad\nabla B\in L^{2}(0,\infty;H^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).\end{split}

(1.14)

Moreover, if in addition
(u_{0},B_{0})\in\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
for some
\gamma\in[0,{5\over 2}]
, then it holds that for all
t\geq 1
,

\|\Lambda^{s}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{s}B(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-{s+\gamma\over 2}}\quad\text{if}\quad s\geq 0.

(1.15)

Remark 1.4
.

The time-decay rates
(
1.15
)
are optimal in the sense that they concide with that of the heat semi-group.

Remark 1.5
.

From the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we see that the solution
(u,B)
belongs to all Sobolev spaces after
t>0
, so the time-decay rates
(
1.15
)
make sense.

Remark 1.6
.

Actually, we do not need to impose additional regularity on initial data when
\gamma=0
because
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{0}_{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we briefly introduce the Littlewood-Paley decomposition, the definition of the homogeneous Besov space and some related analysis tools. For more details, we refer readers to
[
2
]
.

Let
\varphi,\chi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
be two radial functions valued in the interval
[0,1]
and supported in
\mathcal{C}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:{3\over 4}\leq|\xi|\leq{8\over 3}\}
and
\mathcal{B}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|\xi|\leq{4\over 3}\}
respectively such that

\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1\,\,\,\,\text{in \,\,}\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\{0\},

and

\chi(\xi)+\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1\,\,\,\,\text{in \,\,}\mathbb{R}^{3}.

The dyadic block
\dot{\Delta}_{j}
and the low frequency cut-off operator
\dot{S}_{j}
with
j\in\mathbb{Z}
are defined as follows

\begin{split}\dot{\Delta}_{j}u&:=\varphi(2^{-j}D)u=2^{3j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}g(2^{j}y)u(x-y)dy\quad\text{with}\quad g=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\phi,\\
\dot{S}_{j}u&:=\chi(2^{-j}D)u=2^{3j}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\tilde{g}(2^{j}y)u(x-y)dy\quad\text{with}\quad\tilde{g}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi.\end{split}

We denote by
\mathcal{S}_{h}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
the set of tempered distribution
u
such that

\lim_{j\rightarrow-\infty}\|\dot{S}_{j}u\|_{L^{\infty}}=0,

then the following decompositions hold for all
u\in\mathcal{S}_{h}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
:

\begin{split}u=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\quad\text{and}\quad\dot{S}_{j}u=\sum_{j^{\prime}\leq j-1}\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}u.\end{split}

Moreover, it is easy to check that the Littlewood-Paley decomposition satisfies the almost orthogonality:

\dot{\Delta}_{k}\dot{\Delta}_{j}u=0\quad\text{if}\quad|k-j|\geq 2,\quad\dot{\Delta}_{k}(\dot{S}_{j-1}u\dot{\Delta}_{j}v)=0\quad\text{if}\quad|k-j|\geq 5.

(2.1)

The following well-known Berstein inequalities will be frequently used in this sequel.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
)
.

Let
0<r<R
. There exists a constant
C
such that for any nonnegative integer
k
, any couple
(p,q)\in[1,\infty]^{2}
with
q\geq p\geq 1
, and any function
u
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, there holds

\begin{split}&\operatorname{supp}\hat{u}\subset\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|\xi|\leq\lambda R\}\Longrightarrow\sup_{|\alpha|=k}\|\partial^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{q}}\leq C^{k+1}\lambda^{k+3({1\over p}-{1\over q})}\|u\|_{L^{p}},\\
&\operatorname{supp}\hat{u}\subset\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:\lambda r\leq|\xi|\leq\lambda R\}\Longrightarrow C^{-k-1}\lambda^{k}\|u\|_{L^{p}}\leq\sup_{|\alpha|=k}\|\partial^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{p}}\leq C^{k+1}\lambda^{k}\|u\|_{L^{p}}.\end{split}

Let us recall the definition of the homogeneous Besov space.

Definition 2.1

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
, the homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is defined by

\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}_{h}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}}<\infty\}

with

\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}}:=\Big\|\Big(2^{js}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{p}}\Big)_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}.

Remark 2.1
.

It is easy to verify that the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{2,2}}
and the classical homogeneous Sobolev norm
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}:=\|\Lambda^{s}\cdot\|_{L^{2}}
with
\Lambda^{s}:=(-\Delta)^{{s\over 2}}
are equivalent, so
\dot{B}^{s}_{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
coincides with
\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty
, the following properties hold true:

(i)

Derivatives: for any
k
-th order derivative, it holds that

\sup_{|\alpha|=k}\|\partial^{\alpha}u\|_{\dot{{B}}^{s}_{p,r}}\approx\|u\|_{\dot{{B}}^{s+k}_{p,r}}.

(ii)

Embedding: for
1\leq p\leq\tilde{p}\leq\infty
and
1\leq r\leq\tilde{r}\leq\infty
, one has

\begin{split}&\dot{{B}}^{s}_{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow\dot{{B}}^{s-3({1\over p}-{1\over\tilde{p}})}_{\tilde{p},\tilde{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\quad\dot{{B}}^{{3\over p}}_{p,1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\\
&\quad\text{and}\quad L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow\dot{{B}}^{0}_{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).\end{split}

(iii)

Interpolation: for any
s_{1}<s_{2}
and
0<\theta<1
, there holds

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\theta s_{1}+(1-\theta)s_{2}}_{p,r}}\leq\|u\|^{\theta}_{\dot{B}^{s_{1}}_{p,r}}\|u\|^{1-\theta}_{\dot{B}^{s_{2}}_{p,r}},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\theta s_{1}+(1-\theta)s_{2}}_{p,1}}\leq{C\over s_{2}-s_{1}}\Big({1\over\theta}+{1\over 1-\theta}\Big)\|u\|^{\theta}_{\dot{B}^{s_{1}}_{p,\infty}}\|u\|^{1-\theta}_{\dot{B}^{s_{2}}_{p,\infty}}.

(2.3)

(iv)

Action of multiplier: Let
f
be a smooth function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}
which is homogeneous of degree
m
, then we have

\|f(D)u\|_{\dot{B}^{s-m}_{p,r}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}}.

We need the following mixed time-space norm, which was introduced by Chemin in
[
9
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
T>0
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
1\leq p,r,q\leq\infty
. For any tempered distribution
u
on
(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we set

\|u\|_{\widetilde{L}^{q}_{T}(\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r})}:=\Big\|\Big(2^{js}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L_{T}^{q}(L^{p})}\Big)_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}.

Remark 2.2
.

By the Minkowski’s inequality, one easily observe that

\begin{cases}\|u\|_{\widetilde{L}^{q}_{T}(\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r})}\leq\|u\|_{{L}^{q}_{T}(\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r})}\quad\text{if}\quad q\leq r,\\
\|u\|_{{L}^{q}_{T}(\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r})}\leq\|u\|_{\widetilde{L}^{q}_{T}(\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r})}\quad\text{if}\quad q\geq r.\end{cases}

To obtain the nonlinear estimates in Besov spaces, we shall make use of the following Bony’s decomposition from
[
5
]
in the homogeneous context:

uv=\dot{T}_{u}v+\dot{T}_{v}u+\dot{R}(u,v),

(2.4)

where

\dot{T}_{u}v:=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\dot{S}_{j-1}u\dot{\Delta}_{j}v\quad\text{and}\quad\dot{R}(u,v):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 1}\dot{\Delta}_{j}u{\dot{\Delta}}_{j^{\prime}}v.

The operators
\dot{T}
and
\dot{R}
above are called paraproduct operator and remainder operator, respectively.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
2
]
)
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
t<0
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty

such that

\begin{split}\|\dot{T}_{u}v\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}}&\leq C\|u\|_{{L^{\infty}}}\|v\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}}\quad\text{and}\quad\|\dot{T}_{u}v\|_{\dot{B}^{s+t}_{p,r}}\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{t}_{\infty,\infty}}\|v\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p,r}}.\end{split}

(2.5)

Let
s_{1},s_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and

with

and
{1\over r}={1\over r_{1}}+{1\over r_{2}}

If
r=1

We have the following product law in Besov spaces.

Lemma 2.4

Proof.

Since

and

2.3

\begin{split}\|\dot{T}_{f}g\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{2,r}}&\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{-\eta}_{\infty,\infty}}\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\eta}_{2,r}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{{3\over 2}-\eta}_{2,\infty}}\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\eta}_{2,r}},\\

(2.10)

This completes the proof of

We establish the following commutator estimates, which plays a key role in the analysis of the Hall term.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
s>-{3\over 2}
,
1\leq r\leq\infty
,
0<\eta<{5\over 2}
and
\theta>0
, the following estimate holds true,

\|2^{js}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},f]g\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{{5\over 2}-\eta}_{2,r}}\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\eta-1}_{2,r}}+\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{{3\over 2}-\theta}_{2,\infty}}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\theta}_{2,r}}.

(2.11)

Proof.

By the Bony’s decomposition,

[\dot{\Delta}_{j},f]g=[\dot{\Delta}_{j},\dot{T}_{f}]g+\dot{\Delta}_{j}(\dot{T}_{g}f+\dot{R}(f,g))-(\dot{T}_{\dot{\Delta}_{j}g}f+\dot{R}(\dot{\Delta}_{j}g,f)).

(2.12)

From
(
2.1
)
, the first term can be written as

[\dot{\Delta}_{j},\dot{T}_{f}]g=\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}[\dot{\Delta}_{j},\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-1}f]\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g.

(2.13)

According to
[
2
, Lemma 2.97]
, one has

\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-1}f]\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-j}\|\nabla\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-1}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.14)

Because
\eta>0
, we have

\begin{split}\|\nabla\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-1}f\|_{L^{\infty}}&\leq\sum_{j^{\prime\prime}\leq j^{\prime}-2}\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime\prime}}f\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\leq\|\nabla f\|_{\dot{B}^{-\eta}_{\infty,\infty}}\sum_{j^{\prime\prime}\leq j^{\prime}-2}2^{j^{\prime\prime}\eta}\lesssim 2^{j^{\prime}\eta}\|\nabla f\|_{\dot{B}^{-\eta}_{\infty,\infty}}.\end{split}

(2.15)

Therefore,

\begin{split}2^{js}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},\dot{T}_{f}]g\|_{L^{2}}&\leq 2^{js}\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}-1}f]\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\|\nabla f\|_{\dot{B}^{-\eta}_{\infty,\infty}}\sum_{|j-j^{\prime}|\leq 4}2^{j^{\prime}(s+\eta-1)}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}g\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

(2.16)

Taking the
\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})
norm for both sides of
(
2.16
)
and using Lemma
2.2
(i), (ii) implies that

\begin{split}\|2^{js}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},\dot{T}_{f}]g\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}&\lesssim\|\nabla f\|_{\dot{B}^{-\eta}_{\infty,\infty}}\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\eta-1}_{2,r}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{{5\over 2}-\eta}_{2,\infty}}\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\eta-1}_{2,r}}.\end{split}

(2.17)

Since
\theta>0
and
s>-{3\over 2}
, we get by applying Lemma
2.3
and embeddings that

\displaystyle\|2^{js}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\dot{T}_{g}f\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{-\theta}_{\infty,\infty}}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\theta}_{2,r}}\lesssim\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{{3\over 2}-\theta}_{2,\infty}}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\theta}_{2,r}},

(2.18)

\displaystyle\|2^{js}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\dot{R}(f,g)\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\dot{R}(f,g)\|_{\dot{B}^{s+{3\over 2}}_{1,r}}\lesssim\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{{3\over 2}-\theta}_{2,\infty}}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\theta}_{2,r}}.

(2.19)

Note that

\dot{T}_{\dot{\Delta}_{j}g}f+\dot{R}(\dot{\Delta}_{j}g,f)=\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-2}\dot{S}_{j^{\prime}+2}\dot{\Delta}_{j}g\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}f.

(2.20)

Hence, by using Berstein inequalities, we get

\begin{split}2^{js}\|\dot{T}_{\dot{\Delta}_{j}g}f+\dot{R}(\dot{\Delta}_{j}g,f)\|_{L^{2}}&\lesssim\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-2}2^{js}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}g\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}f\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-2}2^{j(s+{3\over 2})}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}g\|_{L^{2}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}f\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\lesssim\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\eta-1}_{2,\infty}}\sum_{j^{\prime}\geq j-2}2^{(j-j^{\prime})({5\over 2}-\eta)}2^{j^{\prime}({5\over 2}-\eta)}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j^{\prime}}f\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

(2.21)

Since
\eta<{5\over 2}
, taking the
\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})
norm of both sides of
(
2.21
)
and using the convolution inequality, we have

\begin{split}\|2^{js}\|\dot{T}_{\dot{\Delta}_{j}g}f+\dot{R}(\dot{\Delta}_{j}g,f)\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}&\lesssim\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{s+\eta-1}_{2,\infty}}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{{5\over 2}-\eta}_{2,r}}\end{split}

(2.22)

Thus, the proof of
(
2.11
)
is finished.

&\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\Big(\|u\|_{L^{2}_{T}(L^{2})}^{{\sigma\over 2\sigma+3}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}^{{\sigma+3\over 2\sigma+3}}\Big)\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\Big(T^{{\sigma\over 4\sigma+6}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(L^{2})}^{{\sigma\over 2\sigma+3}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}^{{\sigma+3\over 2\sigma+3}}\Big)\\
&\lesssim T^{{\sigma\over 4\sigma+6}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}({H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}^{{3\sigma+3\over 2\sigma+3}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{T}({H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}^{{\sigma+3\over 2\sigma+3}},\end{split}

(3.19)

and

\begin{split}\|B\cdot\nabla B\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{\sigma-{1\over 2}})}&\lesssim\|B\otimes B\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}\\
&\lesssim\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\|B\|_{L_{T}^{2}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\\
&\lesssim\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\|B\|_{L_{T}^{2}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}})}^{1-{\sigma\over 2}}\|\nabla B\|_{L_{T}^{2}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}})}^{{\sigma\over 2}}\\
&\lesssim T^{{1\over 2}-{\sigma\over 4}}\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}^{2-{\sigma\over 2}}\|\nabla B\|_{L_{T}^{2}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}^{{\sigma\over 2}}.\end{split}

(3.20)

Likewise, resorting to
(
3.18
)
, Hölder’s inequality and interpolation gives

\begin{split}&\|\nabla\times(u\times B)\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}})}\lesssim\|u\times B\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}})}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\|B\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{5\over 2}-{\sigma\over 2}})}+\|u\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}-{\sigma\over 2}})}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\Big(\|B\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}})}^{{\sigma\over 2}}\|\nabla B\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}})}^{1-{\sigma\over 2}}\Big)\\
&\qquad+\Big(\|u\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}^{{\sigma\over 2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}^{1-{\sigma\over 2}}\Big)\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}-{\sigma\over 2}})}\\
&\lesssim T^{{\sigma\over 4}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}({H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}^{{\sigma\over 2}}\|\nabla B\|_{L^{2}_{T}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}^{1-{\sigma\over 2}}\\
&\qquad+T^{{\sigma\over 4}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}({H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}^{{\sigma\over 2}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{T}({H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}^{1-{\sigma\over 2}}\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}.\end{split}

(3.21)

For the commutator term, applying Lemma
2.5
with
(s,r,\eta,\theta)=({3\over 2},2,{1\over 2},{1\over 2})
yields that

\begin{split}\Big\|\|2^{j{3\over 2}}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},B\times](\nabla\times B)\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{T}}\lesssim&\|B\|_{L^{4}_{T}(\dot{H}^{2})}\|\nabla\times B\|_{L^{4}_{T}(\dot{H}^{1})}\lesssim\|B\|_{L^{4}_{T}(\dot{H}^{2})}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.22)

Thus, plugging
(
3.19
)
-
(
3.22
)
into
(
3.15
)
and
(
3.16
)
yields
(
3.1
)
and
(
3.2
)
respectively.
∎

Second step: Construction of approximate solutions

We will construct a sequence of smooth approximate solutions
\{(u_{n},B_{n})\}
. For each positive integer
n
, the spectral cut-off operator
\mathcal{J}_{n}
is defined as

\mathcal{F}(\mathcal{J}_{n}f)(\xi)=1_{\{|\xi|\leq n\}}\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi).

We smooth out the initial data
(u_{0},B_{0})
by
u_{0,n}=\mathcal{J}_{n}u_{0}
and
B_{0,n}=\mathcal{J}_{n}B_{0}
. It is clear that
(u_{0,n},B_{0,n})\in H^{\infty}
where
H^{\infty}:=\bigcap_{s>0}H^{s}
, and

\begin{split}(u_{0,n},B_{0,n})\rightarrow(u_{0},B_{0})\quad\text{in}\quad H^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}\times H^{{3\over 2}}.\end{split}

(3.23)

Consider the Hall-MHD system supplemented with smooth initial data
(u_{0,n},B_{0,n})
:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{n}-\Delta u_{n}+u_{n}\cdot\nabla u_{n}+\nabla P_{n}=B_{n}\cdot\nabla B_{n},\\
\partial_{t}B_{n}-\Delta B_{n}+u_{n}\cdot\nabla B_{n}+\nabla\times((\nabla\times B_{n})\times B_{n})=B_{n}\cdot\nabla u_{n},\\
\Div u_{n}=\Div B_{n}=0,\\
u_{n}(x,0)=u_{0,n}(x),\quad B_{n}(x,0)=B_{0,n}(x).\end{cases}

(3.24)

According to the local well-posedness result for smooth initial data (see
[
6
]
), the above system admits a unique solution
(u_{n},B_{n})\in C([0,T_{n});H^{\infty})
on some maximal time interval
[0,T_{n})
. Furthermore, the blow-up criterion in
[
7
, Theorem 2]
indicates that if the maximal time
T_{n}<\infty
, there holds

\int_{0}^{T_{n}}(\|u_{n}\|_{BMO}^{2}+\|\nabla B_{n}\|_{BMO}^{2})dt=\infty.

(3.25)

Third step: Uniform bounds for the approximate solutions

Notice that the definition of cut-off operator ensures
\|\mathcal{J}_{n}u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}
,
\|\mathcal{J}_{n}B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}\leq\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}
and
\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\mathcal{J}_{n}B_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}B_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
. Hence, denote

\begin{split}X_{n}(t)&:=\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}({H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}+\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{t}({H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}+\|B_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}+\|\nabla B_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{t}({H}^{{3\over 2}})},\\
Y_{n}(t)&:=\|B_{n}\|_{{L}^{4}_{t}(\dot{H}^{2})},\end{split}

we can deduce from Proposition
3.1
that for all
t\in[0,T_{n})
,

\begin{split}X_{n}(t)&\leq C_{1}\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}+\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}+t^{{\sigma\over 4\sigma+6}}X_{n}^{2}(t)+t^{{1\over 2}-{\sigma\over 4}}X_{n}^{2}(t)+2t^{{\sigma\over 4}}X_{n}^{2}(t)+Y_{n}^{2}(t)\Big),\\
Y_{n}(t)&\leq C_{2}\Big(\Big[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}({1-e^{-4c2^{2j}t}})^{{1\over 2}}(2^{j{3\over 2}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}B_{0}\|_{L^{2}})^{2}\Big]^{{1\over 2}}+2t^{{\sigma\over 4}}X_{n}^{2}(t)+Y_{n}^{2}(t)\Big).\end{split}

(3.26)

Next, we define

T_{n}^{\prime}:=\sup\Big\{t\in[0,T_{n}):X_{n}(t)\leq 2C_{1}(\|u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}+\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}),\,\,Y_{n}(t)\leq\delta\Big\},

(3.27)

where the constant
\delta
will be determined later, and choose a positive constant
T_{*}
such that

\begin{cases}T_{*}^{{\sigma\over 4\sigma+6}}4C_{1}^{2}(\|u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}+\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}})^{2}\leq{1\over 8}(\|u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}+\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}),\\
T_{*}^{{1\over 2}-{\sigma\over 4}}4C_{1}^{2}(\|u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}+\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}})^{2}\leq{1\over 8}(\|u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}+\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}),\\
\Big[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}({1-e^{-4c2^{2j}T_{*}}})^{{1\over 2}}(2^{j{3\over 2}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}B_{0}\|_{L^{2}})^{2}\Big]^{{1\over 2}}\leq{\delta\over 8C_{2}},\\
T_{*}^{{\sigma\over 4}}4C_{1}^{2}(\|u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}+\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}})^{2}\leq{\delta\over 8C_{2}}.\end{cases}

(3.28)

Note that
T_{*}
is a fixed constant independent of
n
. Let us explain why the third condition in
(
3.28
)
can be fulfilled. Actually, since
\|B_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}}}=\Big[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}(2^{j{3\over 2}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}B_{0}\|_{L^{2}})^{2}\Big]^{{1\over 2}}<\infty
, taking
N_{0}
large enough can ensure that

\Big[\sum_{|j|>N_{0}}(2^{j{3\over 2}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}B_{0}\|_{L^{2}})^{2}\Big]^{{1\over 2}}\leq{\delta\over 16C_{2}}.

(3.29)

For the remaining finite terms, choosing
T_{*}
sufficiently small such that

\Big[\sum_{|j|\leq N_{0}}({1-e^{-4c2^{2j}T_{*}}})^{{1\over 2}}(2^{j{3\over 2}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}B_{0}\|_{L^{2}})^{2}\Big]^{{1\over 2}}\leq{\delta\over 16C_{2}}.

(3.30)

Therefore,

\begin{split}&\Big[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}({1-e^{-4c2^{2j}T_{*}}})^{{1\over 2}}(2^{j{3\over 2}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}B_{0}\|_{L^{2}})^{2}\Big]^{{1\over 2}}\\
&\leq\Big[\sum_{|j|\leq N_{0}}({1-e^{-4c2^{2j}T_{*}}})^{{1\over 2}}(2^{j{3\over 2}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}B_{0}\|_{L^{2}})^{2}\Big]^{{1\over 2}}\\
&\quad+\Big[\sum_{|j|>N_{0}}(2^{j{3\over 2}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}B_{0}\|_{L^{2}})^{2}\Big]^{{1\over 2}}\\
&\leq{\delta\over 8C_{2}}.\end{split}

(3.31)

We assert that when
\delta
is sufficiently small, there holds
T_{*}<T_{n}^{\prime}
. Actually, if
T_{n}^{\prime}\leq T_{*}<\infty
, it follows from
(
3.26
)
,
(
3.27
)
and
(
3.28
)
that for all
t\in[0,T_{n}^{\prime})
,

\begin{split}X_{n}(t)&\leq{5C_{1}\over 4}\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}+\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}\Big)+{C_{1}\delta\over 4C_{2}}+C_{1}\delta^{2},\\
Y_{n}(t)&\leq{3\delta\over 8}+C_{2}\delta^{2}.\end{split}

(3.32)

Taking
\delta
small enough such that

\begin{split}{C_{1}\delta\over 4C_{2}}+C_{1}\delta^{2}\leq{C_{1}\over 4}(\|u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}+\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}})\quad\text{and}\quad C_{2}\delta\leq{1\over 8},\end{split}

which leads to that for all
t\in[0,T_{n}^{\prime})
,

\begin{split}X_{n}(t)&\leq{3C_{1}\over 2}\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}+\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}\Big),\\
Y_{n}(t)&\leq{\delta\over 2}.\end{split}

(3.33)

At this stage, by the definition of
T_{n}^{\prime}
and the continuity of
X_{n}(t)
and
Y_{n}(t)
, one may conclude that
T_{n}=T_{n}^{\prime}<\infty
. However, due to the embedding
\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow BMO(\mathbb{R}^{3})
(see
[
2
, Theorem 1.48]
),

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{T_{n}}(\|u_{n}\|_{BMO}^{2}+\|\nabla B_{n}\|_{BMO}^{2})dt&\leq C\int_{0}^{T_{n}}(\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}}}^{2}+\|\nabla B_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}}}^{2})dt\\
&\leq C\sup_{t\in[0,T_{n}^{\prime})}X_{n}^{2}(t)<\infty,\end{split}

(3.34)

which contradicts with
(
3.25
)
. Thus,
T_{*}<T_{n}^{\prime}\leq T_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. By the definition of
T_{n}^{\prime}
, we have for all
n\geq 1
,

\begin{split}&\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}({H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}+\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{T_{*}}({H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}+\|B_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}+\|\nabla B_{n}\|_{L^{2}_{T_{*}}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}\\
&\leq 2C_{1}(\|u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}+\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}).\end{split}

(3.35)

Fourth step: Existence and uniqueness

We first show that the solution sequence
\{(u_{n},B_{n})\}
constructed in the second step tends to some limit
(u,B)
in
C([0,T_{*}];L^{2})
. Making the difference between the system
(
3.24
)
with
m
and
n
, and taking the inner product with
u_{m}-u_{n}
and
B_{m}-B_{n}
respectively, one has

\begin{split}&{1\over 2}{d\over dt}\Big(\|u_{m}-u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|B_{m}-B_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\|\nabla(u_{m}-u_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla(B_{m}-B_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
=&-\Big(u_{m}\cdot\nabla u_{m}-u_{n}\cdot\nabla u_{n},u_{m}-u_{n}\Big)+\Big(B_{m}\cdot\nabla B_{m}-B_{n}\cdot\nabla B_{n},u_{m}-u_{n}\Big)\\
&-\Big(u_{m}\cdot\nabla B_{m}-u_{n}\cdot\nabla B_{n},B_{m}-B_{n}\Big)+\Big(B_{m}\cdot\nabla u_{m}-B_{n}\cdot\nabla u_{n},B_{m}-B_{n}\Big)\\
&-\Big(\nabla\times((\nabla\times B_{m})\times B_{m}))-\nabla\times((\nabla\times B_{n})\times B_{n})),B_{m}-B_{n}\Big)\\
=&A_{1}+A_{2}+A_{3}+A_{4}+A_{5}.\end{split}

(3.36)

We split
A_{2}
and
A_{4}
as follows:

\begin{split}A_{2}=&\Big((B_{m}-B_{n})\cdot\nabla B_{m},u_{m}-u_{n}\Big)+\Big(B_{n}\cdot\nabla(B_{m}-B_{n}),u_{m}-u_{n}\Big),\\
=&A_{2,1}+A_{2,2},\\
A_{4}=&\Big((B_{m}-B_{n})\cdot\nabla u_{m},B_{m}-B_{n}\Big)+\Big(B_{n}\cdot\nabla(u_{m}-u_{n}),B_{m}-B_{n}\Big)\\
=&A_{4,1}+A_{4,2}.\end{split}

(3.37)

Applying Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
2.7
and Young’s inequality gives

\begin{split}A_{2,1}&\leq\|B_{m}-B_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla B_{m}\|_{{L}^{3}}\|u_{m}-u_{n}\|_{L^{6}}\\
&\leq C\|B_{n}-B_{m}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla B_{m}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}}}\|\nabla(u_{n}-u_{m})\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|\nabla B_{m}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}}}^{2}\|B_{m}-B_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+{1\over 8}\|\nabla(u_{m}-u_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\end{split}

(3.38)

Proceeding in a similar way,

\begin{split}A_{4,1}&\leq C\|\nabla u_{m}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}}}^{2}\|B_{n}-B_{m}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+{1\over 8}\|\nabla(B_{m}-B_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.39)

By the divergence-free property, it is easily seen that
A_{2,2}+A_{4,2}=0
. As a result,

\begin{split}A_{2}+A_{4}=&A_{2,1}+A_{4,1}\\
\leq&C(\|\nabla B_{m}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{m}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}^{2})\|B_{m}-B_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&+{1\over 8}\|\nabla(u_{m}-u_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+{1\over 8}\|\nabla(B_{m}-B_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\end{split}

(3.40)

Similarly,

\begin{split}A_{1}\leq&C\|\nabla u_{m}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}^{2}\|u_{m}-u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+{1\over 8}\|\nabla(u_{m}-u_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\\
A_{3}\leq&C\|\nabla B_{m}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}^{2}\|u_{m}-u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+{1\over 8}\|\nabla(B_{m}-B_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.41)

For the last term
A_{5}
, we rewrite it as

\begin{split}A_{5}=&-\Big(\nabla\times((\nabla\times(B_{m}-B_{n}))\times B_{m}),B_{m}-B_{n}\Big)\\
&-\Big(\nabla\times((\nabla\times B_{n})\times(B_{m}-B_{n})),B_{m}-B_{n}\Big)\\
=&A_{5,1}+A_{5,2}.\end{split}

(3.42)

Obviously,

\begin{split}A_{5,1}=-\Big((\nabla\times(B_{m}-B_{n}))\times B_{m},\nabla\times(B_{m}-B_{n})\Big)=0.\end{split}

(3.43)

Therefore, we get by using Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
2.7
, interpolation and Young’s inequality that

\begin{split}A_{5}&=A_{5,2}\\
&=-\Big((\nabla\times B_{n})\times(B_{m}-B_{n}),\nabla\times(B_{m}-B_{n})\Big)\\
&\leq\|\nabla\times B_{n}\|_{L^{4}}\|B_{m}-B_{n}\|_{L^{4}}\|\nabla\times(B_{m}-B_{n})\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|B_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{{7\over 4}}}\|B_{m}-B_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{{3\over 4}}}\|\nabla(B_{m}-B_{n})\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\Big(\|B_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}}}^{{3\over 4}}\|\nabla B_{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}}}^{{1\over 4}}\Big)\Big(\|B_{m}-B_{n}\|_{{L}^{2}}^{{1\over 4}}\|\nabla(B_{m}-B_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{{3\over 4}}\Big)\|\nabla(B_{m}-B_{n})\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|B_{n}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}^{{3\over 4}}\|\nabla B_{n}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}^{{1\over 4}}\|B_{m}-B_{n}\|_{{L}^{2}}^{{1\over 4}}\|\nabla(B_{m}-B_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{{7\over 4}}\\
&\leq C\|B_{n}\|_{H^{{3\over 2}}}^{6}\|\nabla B_{n}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}^{2}\|B_{m}-B_{n}\|_{{L}^{2}}^{2}+{1\over 8}\|\nabla(B_{m}-B_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.44)

Plugging
(
3.40
)
,
(
3.41
)
and
(
3.44
)
into
(
3.36
)
, we arrive at

\begin{split}&{d\over dt}\Big(\|u_{m}-u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|B_{m}-B_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+\|\nabla(u_{m}-u_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla(B_{m}-B_{n})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\leq C\Big(\|\nabla B_{m}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}^{2}+\|\nabla u_{m}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}^{2}+\|B_{n}\|_{H^{{3\over 2}}}^{6}\|\nabla B_{n}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}^{2}\Big)\Big(\|u_{m}-B_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|B_{m}-B_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big).\end{split}

(3.45)

By Gronwall’s inequality and uniform bounds in
(
3.35
)
, one has

\begin{split}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T_{*}}\|u_{m}-u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|B_{m}-B_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\Big(\|u_{0,m}-u_{0,n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|B_{0,m}-B_{0,n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)\end{split}

(3.46)

for some constant
C=C(\|u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}},\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}})
. Obviously, the right-hand side above tends to zero as
n,m\rightarrow\infty
, which means that
\{(u_{n},B_{n})\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C([0,T_{*}];L^{2})
. Consequently, there is a function pair
(u,B)\in C([0,T_{*}];L^{2})
such that

(u_{n},B_{n})\rightarrow(u,B)\quad\text{in}\quad C([0,T_{*}];L^{2}),

(3.47)

From
(
3.45
)
, we also get

(\nabla u_{n},\nabla B_{n})\rightarrow(\nabla u,\nabla B)\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(0,T_{*};L^{2}).

(3.48)

Using the uniform bounds in
(
3.35
)
and interpolation, one can easily show that for any
s_{1}\in(0,{1\over 2}+\sigma)
and any
s_{2}\in(0,{3\over 2})
,

\begin{split}u_{n}\rightarrow u\quad&\text{in}\quad C([0,T_{*}];H^{s_{1}}),\\
\nabla u_{n}\rightarrow\nabla u\quad&\text{in}\quad L^{2}(0,T_{*};H^{s_{1}}),\\
B_{n}\rightarrow B\quad&\text{in}\quad C([0,T_{*}];H^{s_{2}}),\\
\nabla B_{n}\rightarrow\nabla B\quad&\text{in}\quad L^{2}(0,T_{*};H^{s_{2}}).\end{split}

(3.49)

Furthermore, due to the uniform bounds in
(
3.35
)
, the Banach-Alaoglu theorem ensures that there exists a subsequence
\{(u_{n,k},B_{n,k})\}
such that

\begin{split}u_{n,k}\rightharpoonup u\quad&\text{weakly* in}\quad L^{\infty}(0,T_{*};H^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})\cap L^{2}(0,T_{*};H^{{3\over 2}+\sigma}),\\
B_{n,k}\rightharpoonup B\quad&\text{weakly* in}\quad L^{\infty}(0,T_{*};H^{{3\over 2}})\cap L^{2}(0,T_{*};H^{{5\over 2}}).\end{split}

(3.50)

In order to verify the limit
(u,B)
is indeed the solution to
(
1.1
)
, we need to pass to the limit in the nonlinear terms in
(
3.24
)
. This procedure is relatively simple, as an example, we only deal with the Hall term, the other nonlinear terms can be treated by a similar way. Observe that

\begin{split}&\nabla\times((\nabla\times B_{n})\times B_{n})-\nabla\times((\nabla\times B)\times B)\\
=&\nabla\times((\nabla\times(B_{n}-B))\times B_{n})+\nabla\times((\nabla\times B)\times(B_{n}-B)).\end{split}

(3.51)

Using Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
2.7
gives

\begin{split}\|\nabla\times((\nabla\times(B_{n}-B))\times B_{n})\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}&\leq C\|\nabla\times(B_{n}-B)\|_{L^{3}}\|B_{n}\|_{L^{6}}\\
&\leq C\|\nabla(B_{n}-B)\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}}}\|B_{n}\|_{H^{1}},\\
\|\nabla\times((\nabla\times B)\times(B_{n}-B))\|_{\dot{H}^{-1}}&\leq C\|\nabla\times B\|_{L^{3}}\|B_{n}-B\|_{L^{6}}\\
&\leq C\|\nabla B\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}}}\|B_{n}-B\|_{H^{1}},\end{split}

(3.52)

In view of
(
3.35
)
,
(
3.49
)
and
(
3.50
)
, we have

\nabla\times((\nabla\times B_{n})\times B_{n})\rightarrow\nabla\times((\nabla\times B)\times B)\quad\text{in}\quad L^{2}(0,T_{*};\dot{H}^{-1}).

Thus, we end up with that the limit
(u,B)
solves the Hall-MHD system
(
1.1
)
with some suitable pressure function
P
. The proof of uniqueness is quite similar to the
L^{2}
-convergence procedure above and so is omitted.

What is left is to prove the continuity of
(u,B)
in
H^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}\times H^{{3\over 2}}
with respect to time. We see that
u
satisfies the heat equation

\begin{split}\partial_{t}u-\Delta u=\mathcal{P}(B\cdot\nabla B-u\cdot\nabla u),\end{split}

(3.53)

where
\mathcal{P}:=\Id+(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\Div
is the Leray projection operator. Since
\mathcal{P}
is bounded on Sobolev spaces, it follows from
(
3.19
)
and
(
3.20
)
that the right-hand side above belongs to
L^{2}(0,T_{*};\dot{H}^{\sigma-{1\over 2}})
. Therefore, by the standard property of solutions to the linear heat equation (see
[
12
, Proposition A.4]
), we can deduce that
u\in C([0,T_{*}];H^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})
. However, the classical product laws do not allow us to prove that
\nabla\times((\nabla\times B)\times B)
belongs
L^{2}(0,T_{*};\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}})
, so we can not obtain the continuity of
B
in
H^{{3\over 2}}
by the way mentioned above. Observe that
(u(t),B(t))\in H^{{3\over 2}+\sigma}\times H^{{5\over 2}}
almost everywhere on
[0,T_{*}]
since
(u,B)\in L^{2}(0,T_{*};H^{{3\over 2}+\sigma}\times H^{{5\over 2}})
. Hence, for any
\delta>0
, there exists some
T_{1}\in(0,\delta)
such that
(u(T_{1}),B(T_{1}))\in H^{r}\times H^{r}
with
r=\min\{{3\over 2}+\sigma,{5\over 2}\}>{3\over 2}
. Taking
(u(T_{1}),B(T_{1}))
as initial data for
(
1.1
)
at
t=T_{1}
, according to the local well-posedness result for the Hall-MHD system in
H^{s}
with
s>{3\over 2}
(see
[
10
,
26
]
), we obtain a solution
(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{B})
on some time interval
[T_{1},T_{1}^{\prime}]\subset[0,T_{*}]
with
(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{B})\in C([T_{1},T_{1}^{\prime}];H^{r})\cap L^{2}(T_{1},T_{1}^{\prime};H^{r+1})
. Thanks to the uniqueness stated above, we have
(u,B)=(\widetilde{u},\widetilde{B})
on
[T_{1},T_{1}^{\prime}]
. By a same way, we also can get that there exists some
T_{2}\in(T_{1},\delta)
such that
(u,B)\in C([T_{2},T_{2}^{\prime}];H^{r+1})\cap L^{2}(T_{2},T_{2}^{\prime};H^{r+2})
on some time interval
[T_{2},T_{2}^{\prime}]\subset[0,T_{*}]
. Arguing as in
(
3.34
)
(the blow-up criterion
(
3.25
)
is corresponding to the local smooth solution in
H^{s}
with
s>{5\over 2}
, see
[
7
, Theorem 2]
),
T_{2}^{\prime}
can be choosen to be equal to
T_{*}
, which together with the arbitrariness of
\delta
yields that
(u,B)\in C((0,T_{*}];H^{r+1})
. It is not hard to see that by iteration, we can prove that
(u,B)\in C((0,T_{*}];H^{\infty})
. On the other hand, remember that
B\in C([0,T_{*}];H^{s_{2}})
with
s_{2}<{3\over 2}
and
B\in L^{\infty}(0,T_{*};H^{{3\over 2}})
, since
H^{-s_{2}}
is dense in
H^{-{3\over 2}}
, by means of an
{\epsilon\over 2}
-argument, it is easy to show that
B
is continuous in the weak topology of
H^{{3\over 2}}
with respect to time, which implies that

\varliminf_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\|B(t)\|_{H^{{3\over 2}}}\geq\|B_{0}\|_{H^{{3\over 2}}}.

(3.54)

Besides, it follows from
(
3.14
)
with
p=\infty
that for all
t\in(0,T_{*}]
,

\begin{split}\|B\|_{{L}_{t}^{\infty}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}})}\leq&\|B_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}}}+C\Big(\|B\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}})}+\|u\cdot\nabla B\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}})}\\
&+\Big\|\|2^{j{3\over 2}}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},B\times](\nabla\times B)\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big\|_{L_{t}^{2}}\Big),\end{split}

(3.55)

so taking
t\rightarrow 0^{+}
gives

\varlimsup_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\|B(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}}}\leq\|B_{0}\|_{\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}}}.

(3.56)

Meanwhile, recall that
B\in C([0,T_{*}];L^{2})
, we thus have

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\|B(t)\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}=\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}},

(3.57)

namely,
B
is strongly right continuous at
t=0
. As a result,
B\in C([0,T_{*}];H^{{3\over 2}})
.

This means that the proof of Theorem
1.1
is finished.

4
Proof of Theorem
1.2

From the previous section, we see that the core of the proof of Theorem
1.1
is to establish the uniform bounds for the approximate solutions. In what follows, we only show how to use
(
1.11
)
to derive the desired uniform bounds, the remainder of the proof of Theorem
1.2
can proceed in a similar way as that in the previous section and thus is omitted.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that
\mu=\nu
. If
(u,B)
is a smooth solution to
(
1.1
)
on the maximal time interval
[0,T)
, then there exists a positive time
T_{*}<T
and a positive constant
C
such that

\begin{split}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}({H}^{{1\over 2}})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{T_{*}}({H}^{{1\over 2}})}+\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}+\|\nabla B\|_{L^{2}_{T_{*}}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}}}+\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}+1),\end{split}

(4.1)

where
T_{*}
depends on the initial data and is uniform with respect to all approximate solutions.

Proof.

Apply the operator
\dot{\Delta}_{j}
to both sides of
(
1.11
)
, notice that

\begin{split}&\Big(\dot{\Delta}_{j}(\nabla\times(B\times(\nabla\times v))),\dot{\Delta}_{j}v\Big)\\
=&\Big(\dot{\Delta}_{j}(B\times(\nabla\times v))-B\times(\nabla\times\dot{\Delta}_{j}v),\nabla\times\dot{\Delta}_{j}v\Big)\\
=&\Big([\dot{\Delta}_{j},B\times](\nabla\times v),\nabla\times\dot{\Delta}_{j}v\Big),\end{split}

(4.2)

then following the arguments similar as
(
3.7
)
-
(
3.13
)
, we have that for any
t<T
and any
p,q\in[2,\infty]
with
1+{1\over p}={1\over 2}+{1\over q}
,

\begin{split}\|(\dot{\Delta}_{j}u,&\dot{\Delta}_{j}B,\dot{\Delta}_{j}v)\|_{L^{p}_{t}(L^{2})}\lesssim\Big({1-e^{-pc2^{2j}t}\over pc2^{2j}}\Big)^{{1\over p}}\|(\dot{\Delta}_{j}u_{0},\dot{\Delta}_{j}B_{0},\dot{\Delta}_{j}v_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\\
&+\Big({1-e^{-qc2^{2j}t}\over qc2^{2j}}\Big)^{{1\over q}}\Big(\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\Div(B\otimes B)\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}+\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\Div(u\otimes u)\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}\\
&+\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla\times(v\times B)\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}+\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla\times(v\times u)\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}\\
&+\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla\times(v\cdot\nabla B)\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}+2^{j}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},B\times](\nabla\times v)\|_{L^{2}_{t}(L^{2})}\Big).\end{split}

(4.3)

Multiplying both sides of
(
3.13
)
by
2^{j({1\over 2}+{2\over p})}
and taking the
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
norm, we arrive at

\begin{split}\|(u,B&,v)\|_{\widetilde{L}_{t}^{p}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+{2\over p}})}\lesssim\Big[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}({1-e^{-pc2^{2j}t}})^{{2\over p}}(2^{j{1\over 2}}\|(\dot{\Delta}_{j}u,\dot{\Delta}_{j}B,\dot{\Delta}_{j}v)\|_{L^{2}})^{2}\Big]^{{1\over 2}}\\
&+\|B\otimes B\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}})}+\|u\otimes u\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}})}+\|v\times B\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}})}+\|v\times u\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}})}\\
&+\|v\cdot\nabla B\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}})}+\Big\|\|2^{j{1\over 2}}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},B\times](\nabla\times v)\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{t}}.\end{split}

(4.4)

Set
(s,r,\eta,\theta)=({1\over 2},2,{1\over 2},{1\over 2})
in Lemma
2.4
, we have the following product law

\|fg\|_{\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}.

(4.5)

Hence, applying
(
4.5
)
, the first five nonlinear terms in the right-hand side of
(
4.4
)
can be bounded by
C\|(u,B,\nabla B,v)\|_{L^{4}_{t}(\dot{H}^{1})}^{2}
. Thanks to Lemma
2.5
with

(s,r,\eta,\theta)=({1\over 2},2,{1\over 2},{3\over 2})
, the commutator term can be bounded as follows

\begin{split}\Big\|\|2^{j{1\over 2}}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},B\times](\nabla\times v)\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{t}}\lesssim\|B\|_{L^{4}_{t}(\dot{H}^{2})}\|v\|_{L^{4}_{t}(\dot{H}^{1})}.\end{split}

In view of the vector identity

\nabla\times(\nabla\times w)+\Delta w=\nabla\Div w,

one sees that

B=(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\times(\nabla\times B)=(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\times(u-v).

Since
(-\Delta)^{-1}\nabla\times
is a homogeneous Fourier multiplier of degree
-1
, it follows from Lemma
2.2
(iv) that

\begin{split}\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{s+1}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}.\end{split}

Therefore, all the nonlinear terms are controlled by
C\|(u,B,v)\|_{L^{4}_{t}(\dot{H}^{1})}^{2}
. Taking
p=4
in
(
4.4
)
gives us that there exists a positive constant
C_{3}
such that for any
t<T
,

\begin{split}\|(u,B,v)\|_{{L}_{t}^{4}(\dot{H}^{1})}\leq&C_{3}\Big(\Big[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}({1-e^{-4c2^{2j}t}})^{{1\over 2}}(2^{j{1\over 2}}\|(\dot{\Delta}_{j}u,\dot{\Delta}_{j}B,\dot{\Delta}_{j}v)\|_{L^{2}})^{2}\Big]^{{1\over 2}}\\
&+\|(u,B,v)\|_{L^{4}_{t}(\dot{H}^{1})}^{2}\Big).\end{split}

(4.6)

Recall the fact that for any given
\delta>0
, there exists a positive time
T_{*}
such that

\Big[\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}({1-e^{-4c2^{2j}T_{*}}})^{{1\over 2}}(2^{j{1\over 2}}\|(\dot{\Delta}_{j}u,\dot{\Delta}_{j}B,\dot{\Delta}_{j}v)\|_{L^{2}})^{2}\Big]^{{1\over 2}}<\delta.

Define

T_{**}=\sup\Big\{t\in[0,\min\{T,T_{*}\}),\,\,\|(u,B,v)\|_{{L}_{t}^{4}(\dot{H}^{1})}\leq 2C_{3}\delta\Big\},

where
\delta
will be determined later on. Hence, it follows from
(
4.6
)
that for all
t\in[0,T_{**})
,

\begin{split}\|(u,B,v)\|_{{L}_{t}^{4}(\dot{H}^{1})}&\leq C_{3}\delta(1+4C_{3}^{2}\delta).\end{split}

(4.7)

Taking
\delta
small enough such that
4C_{3}^{2}\delta<{1\over 2}
yields that for all
t\in[0,T_{**})
,

\begin{split}\|(u,B,v)\|_{{L}_{t}^{4}(\dot{H}^{1})}&<{3\over 2}C_{3}\delta.\end{split}

(4.8)

Then by the continuity of
\|(u,B,v)\|_{{L}_{t}^{4}(\dot{H}^{1})}
, we have
T_{**}=\min\{T,T_{*}\}
.

We assert that
T_{*}<T
. Indeed, if
T\leq T_{*}<\infty
, we infer from
(
4.4
)
with
p=2
that

\|(u,B,v)\|_{{L}_{T}^{2}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}})}\lesssim\|(u_{0},B_{0},v_{0})\|_{\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}}}+\|(u,B,v)\|_{{L}_{T}^{4}(\dot{H}^{1})}^{2}<\infty.

By
\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow\text{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, the above inequality contradicts with the blow-up criterion
(
3.25
)
. Consequently,
T_{*}<T
. Thus, combining
(
3.4
)
and
(
4.4
)
leads to

\begin{split}&\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}({H}^{{1\over 2}})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{T_{*}}({H}^{{1\over 2}})}+\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}+\|\nabla B\|_{L^{2}_{T_{*}}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}\\
\lesssim&\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}(L^{2})}+\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{T_{*}}(L^{2})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{T_{*}}(L^{2})}+\|\nabla B\|_{L^{2}_{T_{*}}(L^{2})}\\
&+\|u\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{T_{*}}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}})}+\|v\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{T_{*}}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}})}+\|u\|_{\widetilde{L}^{2}_{T_{*}}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}})}+\|v\|_{\widetilde{L}^{2}_{T_{*}}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}})}\\
\lesssim&\|u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}}}+\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}+\|(u,B,v)\|_{{L}_{T_{*}}^{4}(\dot{H}^{1})}^{2}\\
\lesssim&\|u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}}}+\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}+\delta^{2}.\end{split}

(4.9)

This completes the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
∎

5
Proof of Theorem
1.3

This section is devoted to Theorem
1.3
. In the first subsection, we extend the local solutions constructed in Theorem
1.1
globally in time provided the initial data is small enough. The optimal time-decay rates of solutions will be derived in the second subsection.

5.1
Global existence for small initial data

We denote by
T^{*}
the largest time such that there holds
(
1.7
)
in Theorem
1.1
. It suffices to prove that
T^{*}=\infty
under the assumption
(
1.13
)
.

From
(
3.15
)
, we have that for all
t\in[0,T^{*})
,

\begin{split}&\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}({H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{t}({H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}+\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}+\|\nabla B\|_{L^{2}_{t}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}\\
&\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}+\|B_{0}\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}+\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\dot{H}^{\sigma-{1\over 2}})}+\|B\cdot\nabla B\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\dot{H}^{\sigma-{1\over 2}})}\\
&\quad+\|\nabla\times(u\times B)\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}})}+\Big\|\|2^{j{3\over 2}}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},B\times](\nabla\times B)\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{t}}.\end{split}

(5.1)

Thanks to the product laws
(
3.17
)
and
(
3.18
)
, we derive

\begin{split}\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{\sigma-{1\over 2}})}&\lesssim\|u\otimes u\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}({H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{T}({H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}.\end{split}

(5.2)

and

\begin{split}\|B\cdot\nabla B\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{\sigma-{1\over 2}})}&\lesssim\|B\otimes B\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}\\
&\lesssim\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\|B\|_{L_{T}^{2}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\\
&\lesssim\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\|\nabla B\|_{L_{T}^{2}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\\
&\lesssim\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}\|\nabla B\|_{L_{T}^{2}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}.\end{split}

(5.3)

and

\begin{split}&\|\nabla\times(u\times B)\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}})}\lesssim\|u\times B\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}})}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\|B\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{5\over 2}-{\sigma\over 2}})}+\|u\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}-{\sigma\over 2}})}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\|\nabla B\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}-{\sigma\over 2}})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}+{\sigma\over 2}})}\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}-{\sigma\over 2}})}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}({H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}\|\nabla B\|_{L^{2}_{T}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{T}({H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}.\end{split}

(5.4)

Tackling the commutator term as
(
3.22
)
and using the interpolation, we can get

\begin{split}&\Big\|\|2^{j{3\over 2}}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},B\times](\nabla\times B)\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})}\Big\|_{L^{2}_{t}}\\
&\lesssim\|B\|_{L^{4}_{t}(\dot{H}^{2})}^{2}\lesssim\Big(\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}})}^{{1\over 2}}\|\nabla B\|_{L^{2}_{t}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}})}^{{1\over 2}}\Big)^{2}\\
&\lesssim\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}\|\nabla B\|_{L^{2}_{t}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}.\end{split}

(5.5)

Denote

\mathcal{E}(t):=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}({H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}_{t}({H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma})}+\|B\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}+\|\nabla B\|_{L^{2}_{t}({H}^{{3\over 2}})}.

Plugging
(
5.2
)
-
(
5.5
)
into
(
5.1
)
, we can deduce that there exists a constant
C_{4}>1
such that for all
t\in[0,T^{*})
,

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}(t)\leq C_{4}(\mathcal{E}(0)+\mathcal{E}^{2}(t)).\end{split}

(5.6)

Define

T^{**}:=\sup\Big\{t\in[0,T^{*}):\,\mathcal{E}(t)\leq 2C_{4}\mathcal{E}(0)\Big\}.

(5.7)

It follows from
(
5.6
)
that for all
t\in[0,T^{**})
that

\mathcal{E}(t)\leq C_{4}(\mathcal{E}(0)+4C_{4}^{2}\mathcal{E}^{2}(0)).

(5.8)

Since
\mathcal{E}(0)\leq c_{0}
where
c_{0}
is given in
(
1.13
)
, taking
c_{0}
small enough such that
4C_{4}^{2}c_{0}<{1\over 2}
, we can get

\mathcal{E}(t)<{3\over 2}C_{4}\mathcal{E}(0)\quad\text{for}\quad t\in[0,T^{**}).

(5.9)

Then, by the continuity of
\mathcal{E}(t)
, we conclude that
T^{**}=T^{*}
.

We assert that
T^{*}=\infty
. Becasue
\mathcal{E}(t)
is uniformly bounded on
[0,T^{*})
(and notice that
\|B\|_{\widetilde{L}^{\infty}_{T^{*}}(\dot{H}^{{3\over 2}})}<\infty
), it follows from Theorem
1.1
and
(
3.28
)
that for any
t_{0}\in[0,T^{*})
, we can solve
(
1.1
)
on
[t_{0},t_{0}+T^{\prime}]
with some fixed constant
T^{\prime}
. If
T^{*}<+\infty
, choosing

means that the solution
(u,B)
can be continued beyond
T^{*}
, which contradicts with the definition of
T^{*}
. Thus,
T^{*}=+\infty
. This completes the proof of the global well-posedness part of Theorem
1.3
.

5.2
Optimal time-decay rates of solutions

In this subsection, we are going to derive the optimal time-decay rates of the global solutions constructed in the previous subsection. Recall that
(u(t),B(t))\in H^{\infty}
after
t>0
. By performing a standard energy argument, one easily have

\begin{split}&{d\over dt}(\|\Lambda^{s}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{s}B\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\|\Lambda^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{s+1}B\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim(\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}}+\|B\cdot\nabla B\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}})\|\Lambda^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\quad+(\|\nabla\times(u\times B)\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}}+\|[\Lambda^{s},B\times](\nabla\times B)\|_{L^{2}})\|\Lambda^{s+1}B\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

(5.10)

Set
(s,r,\eta,\theta)=(l,2,1,1)
in Lemma
2.4
, the following product law holds:

\|fg\|_{\dot{H}^{l}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}}}\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{l+1}}+\|g\|_{\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}}}\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{l+1}}\quad\text{for}\quad l>-{3\over 2}.

(5.11)

Hence, using
(
5.11
)
gives

\begin{split}\|\nabla\times(u\times B)\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}}&\lesssim\|u\times B\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}}}\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{s+1}}+\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{{1\over 2}}}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s+1}}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{s+1}}+\|B\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s+1}}.\end{split}

(5.12)

Similarly,

\begin{split}\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}}&\lesssim\|u\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{s+1}},\\
\|B\cdot\nabla B\|_{\dot{H}^{s-1}}&\lesssim\|B\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}}\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{s+1}}.\end{split}

(5.13)

Taking advantage of Lemma
2.6
and Lemma
2.7
, we have

\begin{split}\|[\Lambda^{s},B\times](\nabla\times B)\|_{L^{2}}&\lesssim\|\Lambda^{s}B\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla\times B\|_{L^{3}}+\|\Lambda^{s-1}(\nabla\times B)\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla B\|_{L^{3}}\\
&\lesssim\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{s+1}}\|B\|_{H^{{3\over 2}}}.\end{split}

(5.14)

Then, inserting
(
5.12
)
-
(
5.14
)
into
(
5.10
)
yields

\begin{split}&{d\over dt}(\|\Lambda^{s}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{s}B\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+\|\Lambda^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{s+1}B\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\lesssim(\|u\|_{{H}^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}+\|B\|_{{H}^{{3\over 2}}})(\|\Lambda^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{s+1}B\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\\
&\lesssim\mathcal{E}(0)(\|\Lambda^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{s+1}B\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).\end{split}

(5.15)

Since
\mathcal{E}(0)\ll 1
, we conclude that

\begin{split}{d\over dt}(\|\Lambda^{s}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{s}B\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+c_{1}(\|\Lambda^{s+1}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{s+1}B\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\leq 0\end{split}

(5.16)

for some constant
c_{1}>0
. Assume for the moment that there holds

\|u(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}+\|B(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}\leq C_{0}\quad\text{for all}\quad t\geq 0

(5.17)

with some constant
C_{0}>0
, which will be proved in Proposition
5.1
below later. If
s>0
, it follows from the interpolation (see
(
2.3
)
in Lemma
2.2
(iii)) that

\|\Lambda^{s}(u,B)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\Big(\|(u,B)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}\Big)^{{1\over s+\gamma+1}}\Big(\|\Lambda^{s+1}(u,B)\|_{L^{2}}\Big)^{1-{1\over s+\gamma+1}}.

(5.18)

By means of
(
5.17
)
, we get

\begin{split}\|\Lambda^{s+1}(u,B)\|_{L^{2}}\geq C\Big(\|\Lambda^{s}(u,B)\|_{L^{2}}\Big)^{1+{1\over s+\gamma}}.\end{split}

(5.19)

Thus, thanks to
(
5.16
)
, there exists a constant
c_{2}>0
such that the following Lyapunov-type inequality holds

\begin{split}{d\over dt}(\|\Lambda^{s}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{s}B\|_{L^{2}}^{2})+c_{2}\Big(\|\Lambda^{s}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{s}B\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{1+{1\over s+\gamma}}\leq 0.\end{split}

(5.20)

Solving this inequality directly gives that for all
t\geq 1
,

\begin{split}\|\Lambda^{s}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{s}B(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}&\leq\Big[\Big(\|\Lambda^{s}u(1)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\Lambda^{s}B(1)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)^{-{1\over s+\gamma}}+{c_{2}\over s+\gamma}(t-1)\Big]^{-(s+\gamma)}\\
&\lesssim(1+t)^{-(s+\gamma)},\end{split}

(5.21)

which implies
(
1.15
)
with
s>0
. On the other hand, if
\gamma>0
,
(
1.15
)
with
s=0
can be obtained by using the interpolation

\begin{split}\|(u,B)(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|(u,B)(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{{1\over 1+\gamma}}\|(u,B)(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{{\gamma\over 1+\gamma}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-{\gamma\over 2}}.\end{split}

(5.22)

If
\gamma=0
,
(
1.15
)
with
s=0
certainly holds. This means that the proof of
(
1.15
)
is finished.

At the last step, we should get back to prove
(
5.17
)
, namely, the evolution of negative Besov norms of solutions. Actually, we have the following statement.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\gamma\in[0,{5\over 2}]
and
(u_{0},B_{0})\in\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, it holds that for all
t\geq 0
,

\|u(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}+\|B(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}\leq C_{0},

(5.23)

where the constant
C_{0}>0
depends on the norms of the initial data.

Proof.

We first consider the case that
\gamma\in[0,{3\over 2})
. Apply the operator
\dot{\Delta}_{j}
to
(
1.1
)
, by means of a standard energy argument, the Berstein inequality and Young’s inequality, we have

\begin{split}&{1\over 2}{d\over dt}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\Big(\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u\cdot\nabla u)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(B\cdot\nabla B)\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C2^{-2j}\Big(\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(u\cdot\nabla u)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(B\cdot\nabla B)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+{1\over 2}\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\\
&{1\over 2}{d\over dt}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}B\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla B\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla\times(u\times B)\|_{L^{2}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}B\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\qquad+\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},B\times](\nabla\times B)\|_{L^{2}}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}(\nabla\times B)\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\Big(2^{-2j}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}\nabla\times(u\times B)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},B\times](\nabla\times B)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)+{1\over 2}\|\nabla\dot{\Delta}_{j}B\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

(5.24)

whence, after time integration, multiplying both sides by
2^{-2j\gamma}
and taking the
\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})
norm yields

\begin{split}&\|u(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2}+\|B(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2}\\
&\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2}+\|B_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma-1}_{2,\infty}}^{2}+\|B\cdot\nabla B\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma-1}_{2,\infty}}^{2}\\
&\qquad+\|\nabla\times(u\times B)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma-1}_{2,\infty}}^{2}+\|2^{-j\gamma}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},B\times](\nabla\times B)\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}^{2}\Big)d\tau,\end{split}

(5.25)

Set
(s,r,\eta,\theta)
=
(-\gamma,\infty,\gamma+{3\over 2},\gamma+{3\over 2})
in Lemma
2.4
, the following product law holds:

\|fg\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{{3\over 2}}_{2,\infty}}+\|g\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{{3\over 2}}_{2,\infty}}.

(5.26)

Hence, resorting to
(
5.26
)
gives

\begin{split}\|\nabla\times(u\times B)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma-1}_{2,\infty}}&\lesssim\|u\times B\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}\|\nabla B\|_{\dot{B}^{{1\over 2}}_{2,\infty}}+\|B\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{\dot{B}^{{1\over 2}}_{2,\infty}}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}\|\nabla B\|_{H^{{3\over 2}}}+\|B\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{H^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}.\end{split}

(5.27)

Similarly,

\begin{split}\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma-1}_{2,\infty}}&\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{H^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}},\\
\|B\cdot\nabla B\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma-1}_{2,\infty}}&\lesssim\|B\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}\|\nabla B\|_{H^{{3\over 2}}}.\end{split}

(5.28)

Applying Lemma
2.5
with
(s,r,\eta,\theta)=(-\gamma,\infty,{1\over 2},{1\over 2})
and interpolation yields

\begin{split}&\|2^{-j\gamma}\|[\dot{\Delta}_{j},B\times](\nabla\times B)\|_{L^{2}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z})}\\
\lesssim&\|B\|_{\dot{B}^{2}_{2,\infty}}\|\nabla\times B\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma-{1\over 2}}_{2,\infty}}+\|\nabla\times B\|_{\dot{B}^{1}_{2,\infty}}\|B\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma+{1\over 2}}_{2,\infty}}\\
\lesssim&\Big(\|B\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{{1\over 2\gamma+5}}\|\nabla B\|_{\dot{B}^{{3\over 2}}_{2,\infty}}^{{2\gamma+4\over 2\gamma+5}}\Big)\Big(\|B\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{{2\gamma+4\over 2\gamma+5}}\|\nabla B\|_{\dot{B}^{{3\over 2}}_{2,\infty}}^{{1\over 2\gamma+5}}\Big)\\
\lesssim&\|B\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}\|\nabla B\|_{H^{{3\over 2}}}.\end{split}

(5.29)

Therefore, inserting
(
5.27
)
-
(
5.29
)
into
(
5.25
)
, we deduce that

\begin{split}\|u(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2}&+\|B(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2}+\|B_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2}\\
&+C\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\|\nabla u\|_{H^{{1\over 2}+\sigma}}^{2}+\|\nabla B\|_{H^{{3\over 2}}}^{2}\Big)\Big(\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2}+\|B\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2}\Big)d\tau.\end{split}

(5.30)

Applying the Gronwall’s inequality to
(
5.30
)
yields
(
5.23
)
with
\gamma\in[0,{3\over 2})
.

Next, we consider the case that
\gamma\in[{3\over 2},{5\over 2}]
. By performing a routine procedure, one can reach

\begin{split}&\|u(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2}+\|B(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2}\\
&\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2}+\|B_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}+\|B\cdot\nabla B\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}\\
&\quad+\|\nabla\times(u\times B)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}+\|\nabla\times((\nabla\times B)\times B)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}\Big)\Big(\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}+\|B\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}\Big)d\tau.\end{split}

(5.31)

By means of embeddings, Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
2.7
, we have

\begin{split}\|\nabla\times(u\times B)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}&\lesssim\|u\times B\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{{6\over 2\gamma+1},\infty}}\lesssim\|u\times B\|_{L^{{6\over 2\gamma+1}}}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{{12\over 2\gamma+1}}}\|B\|_{L^{{12\over 2\gamma+1}}}\\
&\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{{5-2\gamma\over 4}}}\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{{5-2\gamma\over 4}}}.\end{split}

(5.32)

Similarly,

\begin{split}\|u\cdot\nabla u\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}&\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{{5-2\gamma\over 4}}}^{2},\\
\|B\cdot\nabla B\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}&\lesssim\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{{5-2\gamma\over 4}}}^{2},\\
\|\nabla\times((\nabla\times B)\times B)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}&\lesssim\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{{9-2\gamma\over 4}}}\|B\|_{\dot{H}^{{5-2\gamma\over 4}}}.\end{split}

(5.33)

Observe that
(u_{0},B_{0})\in\dot{B}^{-{5\over 4}}_{2,\infty}
because of the embedding
\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}\cap L^{2}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}^{-\gamma^{\prime}}_{2,\infty}
for any
\gamma^{\prime}\in[0,\gamma]
. Since the evolution of
\dot{B}^{-{5\over 4}}_{2,\infty}
norm has been derived in
(
5.30
)
and
0\leq{5-2\gamma\over 4}\leq{1\over 2}\leq{9-2\gamma\over 4}\leq{3\over 2}
, we can obtain from what we have proved for
(
1.15
)
with
\gamma={5\over 4}
that for all
t\geq 0
,

\begin{split}\|(u,B)(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{{5-2\gamma\over 4}}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-{5-\gamma\over 4}},\quad\|B(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{{9-2\gamma\over 4}}}\lesssim(1+t)^{-{7-\gamma\over 4}}.\end{split}

(5.34)

Therefore, denote

\mathcal{F}(t):=\|u(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2}+\|B(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2},

then it follows from
(
5.31
)
that

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}(t)&\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2}+\|B_{0}\|_{\dot{B}^{-\gamma}_{2,\infty}}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}(1+\tau)^{-{5-\gamma\over 2}}d\tau\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\sqrt{\mathcal{F}(\tau)}\\
&\lesssim 1+\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\sqrt{\mathcal{F}(\tau)},\end{split}

(5.35)

which implies
(
5.23
)
with
\gamma\in[{3\over 2},{5\over 2}]
. This completes the proof of Proposition
5.1
.
∎

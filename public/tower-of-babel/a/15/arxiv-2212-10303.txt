Abstract
. Given a smoothly bounded non-contractible domain Ω ⊂ ℝ 2 \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2} , we prove the existence of positive critical points of the Trudinger-Moser embedding for arbitrary Dirichlet energies. This is done via degree theory, sharp compactness estimates and a topological argument relying on the Poincaré-Hopf theorem. Keywords: Moser-Trudinger, critical inequalities, Leray-Schauder degree, Poincaré-Hopf theorem.



1.
Introduction

Given a smoothly bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, i.e. a bounded domain with smooth boundary, let
H^{1}_{0}=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be the usual Sobolev space of functions with zero trace on
\partial\Omega
endowed with the norm
\|\cdot\|_{H^{1}_{0}}
given by

\|u\|_{H^{1}_{0}}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx.

Given any positive real number
\beta>0
, our main purpose is to discuss the existence of nonnegative critical points of the classical Moser-Trudinger functional

F(u):=\int_{\Omega}\left(e^{u^{2}}-1\right)dx\,,

(1.1)

constrained to the submanifold

\mathcal{M}_{\beta}:=\left\{v\in H^{1}_{0}~:~\|v\|^{2}_{H^{1}_{0}}=\beta\right\}

(1.2)

of
H^{1}_{0}
. This is equivalent to finding a solution of the following elliptic problem

\begin{cases}&\Delta u=2\lambda ue^{u^{2}}\,,\,u>0\,\text{ in }\Omega\,,\\
&u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\,,\\
&\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx=\beta\,,\end{cases}

(
\mathcal{E}_{\beta}
)

for some real number
\lambda
, where
\Delta=-\partial_{xx}-\partial_{yy}
is the (nonnegative) Laplacian.

When
\beta>4\pi
, this is a long-standing open problem, that can be traced back to the seminal work of Struwe
[
37
]
, as we shall briefly explain.

Let us first state the celebrated Moser-Trudinger inequality
[
32
,
34
,
39
]
as

\sup_{u\in\mathcal{M}_{\beta}}F(u)<\infty\quad\text{if and only if }\beta\leq 4\pi.

(
\mathcal{MT}

A simple consequence of (

⁢
M
T

\beta\in(0,4\pi)
the functional

admits a maximizer. The existence of maximizers even in the critical case

, when the Palais-Smale condition does not hold anymore (see
[
2
]
), is much more delicate, and was proven by Carleson-Chang
[
6
]
when
\Omega
is a disk and extended to general bounded domains by Struwe
[
37
]
and Flucher
[
20
]
.

The existence of critical points of
F|_{\mathcal{M}_{\beta}}
in the supercritical regime
\beta>4\pi
is even more delicate, due to the critical nature of the nonlinearity which, in addition to the failure of the Palais-Smale condition for every
\beta\geq 4\pi
(as shown in
[
10
]
), makes the blow-up estimates very subtle and a priori leaves open possibility of no critical points for any
\beta>4\pi
. Instead, relying on the concentration-compactness principle of Lions and on his own monotonicity trick, still in
[
37

, Struwe proved the existence of

has a positive local maximizer for every

[
24

using a flow approach. In these results, there is no condition on the smoothly bounded domain

That this is not just a technical issue is confirmed by the result of the first and second authors

On the other hand, Del Pino-Musso-Ruf

As pointed out in

We recall next a variant of the classical Poincaré-Hopf index theorem, which can be found e.g. in

Proposition 3.1
.

Let

be an open set and consider

. Assume also that, for some

,

has compact closure, and that

has no critical points in

. Then we have

\deg(\nabla f,f^{b},0)=\chi(f^{b}).

(3.24)

Lemma 3.4
.

We have

\deg(\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{N},\Omega^{N}\setminus D_{N},0)=\chi(\Omega)(\chi(\Omega)-1)...(\chi(\Omega)-N+1).

(3.25)

Proof.

Since

\lim_{(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\to\partial(\Omega^{N}\setminus D_{N})}\tilde{\Phi}_{N}(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})=+\infty,

we can take

large enough such that

where

.

contains no critical points of
\tilde{\Phi}_{N}
by Lemma

, we can apply Proposition
3.1
to get

\deg(\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{N},\Omega^{N}\setminus D_{N},0)=\deg(\nabla\tilde{\Phi}_{N},(\tilde{\Phi}_{N})^{b},0)=\chi((\tilde{\Phi}_{N})^{b}).

Since
(\Omega^{N}\setminus D_{N})\setminus(\tilde{\Phi}_{N})^{b}
contains no critical points of
\tilde{\Phi}_{N}
, we can use a gradient flow to retract

to
(\tilde{\Phi}_{N})^{b}
. Therefore
\chi((\tilde{\Phi}_{N})^{b})=\chi(\Omega^{N}\setminus D_{N}),

and we conclude with Proposition

below.
∎

(3.35)

\Delta
in
\Omega
(not in

Proof.

We assume by contradiction that there exist sequences

(3.36)

where the notation

depends on
\delta_{k}^{\prime}
, due to the last term in (

.
Set

where

for every

Appendix A
Properties of the Green’s function

For some fixed
L,\hat{L}>0
, let
H
and
\hat{H}
denote the half spaces

H=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:y<L\},\quad\hat{H}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:y>-\hat{L}\}\,.

(A.1)

On the half-space
H
, the Green’s function and its regular part are given explicitly by

\mathcal{G}_{H}(z,\eta)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{|z-\eta^{*}|}{|z-\eta|},\quad\mathcal{H}_{H}(z,\eta)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|z-\eta^{*}|,

(A.2)

where
\eta^{*}
is the reflection of
\eta
across
\partial H
.

Consider now
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
smoothly bounded. For
x_{k}\in\Omega
,
d_{k}:=d(x_{k},\partial\Omega)
,
r_{k}>0
, let
\mathcal{G}_{k}(z,\eta)
and
\mathcal{H}_{k}(z,\eta)
denote the Green’s function of
\Delta
in
\Omega_{k}:=r_{k}^{-1}(\Omega-x_{k})
and its regular part.

Lemma A.1
.

With the above notation, assume that, up to a subsequence,
r_{k}=o(d_{k})
, so that
\Omega_{k}
invades
\mathbb{R}^{2}
as
k\to\infty
. Then, for every compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, there exists
C_{K}
such that

\left|\nabla_{z}\left(\mathcal{G}_{k}(z,\eta)-\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln\frac{1}{|z-\eta|}\right)\right|\leq C_{K}\frac{r_{k}}{d_{k}}

and

|\nabla_{z}\mathcal{H}_{k}(z,z)|\leq C_{K}\frac{r_{k}}{d_{k}},

uniformly for
z\in K
,
\eta\in\Omega_{k}
.

Proof.

This follows from Appendix B in
[
19
]
.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

With the above notation, assume that, up to a subsequence and a rotation,
r_{k}\to 0
,
d_{k}/r_{k}\to L\in(0,\infty)
, and
\Omega_{k}
converges to the half-space
H
as
k\to\infty
. Then for every compact set
K\subset H
and for
k
large enough

\|\nabla(\mathcal{H}_{k}-\mathcal{H}_{H})\|_{L^{\infty}(K\times\Omega_{k,\rho})}\leq C_{K}r_{k}\max\{1,\rho^{-1}\},

and

\|\nabla(\mathcal{G}_{k}-\mathcal{G}_{H})\|_{L^{\infty}(K\times\Omega_{k,\rho})}\leq C_{K}r_{k}\max\{1,\rho^{-1}\},

where

\Omega_{k,\rho}:=\{z\in\Omega_{k}:d(z,\partial\Omega_{k})>\rho\}.

Proof.

We have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{z}\mathcal{H}_{k}(z,\eta)=0&\text{in }\Omega_{k}\times\Omega_{k}\\
\mathcal{H}_{k}(z,\eta)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|z-\eta|&\text{for }z\in\partial\Omega_{k},\;y\in\Omega_{k}.\end{array}\right.

(A.3)

We apply the maximum principle to the function

\mathcal{H}_{k}(z,\cdot)-\mathcal{H}_{H}(z,\cdot):\Omega_{k}\to\mathbb{R},

where
z\in K
and
K\subset H
is a fixed compact set.
We want to prove that

\sup_{z\in K,\eta\in\partial\Omega_{k}}|\mathcal{H}_{k}(z,\eta)-\mathcal{H}_{H}(z,\eta)|\leq C(K)r_{k},

(A.4)

for
k
sufficiently large.
By definition we have

\mathcal{H}_{k}(z,\eta)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|z-\eta|,\quad\mathcal{H}_{H}(z,\eta)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|z-\eta^{*}|\quad\text{for }\eta\in\partial\Omega_{k}.

Let
\delta>0
be so small that
\partial\Omega\cap B_{\delta}(p(x_{k}))
can be written as the graph over
[-\delta,\delta]
of a function
f
, where
p(x_{k})
is the nearest point projection of
x_{k}
onto
\partial\Omega
. Let
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the tangent line to
\partial\Omega
at
p(x_{k})
and
f:\mathcal{L}\cap B_{\delta}(x_{k})\to\mathbb{R}
. Up to a rotation we can assume that
f^{\prime}(p(x_{k}))=0
and by a Taylor expansion we obtain

|f(t)|\leq\frac{\sup_{[-\delta,\delta]}f^{\prime\prime}}{2}t^{2},\quad t\in[-\delta,\delta].

In particular for each
x\in\partial\Omega\cap B_{\delta}(x_{k})
we have
d(x,L)\leq C_{\Omega,\delta}|x-x_{k}|^{2}
.
Scaling to
\Omega_{k}
we then obtain

\eta\in\partial\Omega_{k},\quad|\eta|\leq\frac{\delta}{r_{k}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad d(\eta,\partial H)=O(r_{k}).

Then, for such
\eta
and for
z\in K
, we have
|z-\eta|\leq|z-\eta^{*}|+O(r_{k})
and

|\mathcal{H}_{k}-\mathcal{H}_{H}|=\left|\ln\frac{|z-\eta|}{|z-\eta^{*}|}\right|\leq\ln\left(1+\frac{O(r_{k})}{|z-\eta^{*}|}\right)=C(K)O(r_{k}),

since
\frac{1}{|z-\eta^{*}|}\geq\varepsilon(K)>0
uniformly for
z\in K
. On the other hand

\eta\in\partial\Omega_{k},\quad|\eta|\geq\frac{\delta}{r_{k}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad|z-\eta^{*}|-|z-\eta|\leq C(K).

Then

|\mathcal{H}_{k}-\mathcal{H}_{H}|=\left|\ln\frac{|z-\eta|}{|z-\eta^{*}|}\right|\leq\ln\left(1+\frac{r_{k}C(K)}{\delta}\right)=C(K)O(r_{k}),

so that (
A.4
) is proven. By the maximum principle we obtain

\sup_{z\in K,\eta\in\Omega_{k}}|\mathcal{H}_{k}(z,\eta)-\mathcal{H}_{H}(z,\eta)|=C(K)O(r_{k}),

(A.5)

and the bound on
\nabla(\mathcal{H}_{k}-\mathcal{H}_{H})
follows by elliptic estimates, since both
\mathcal{H}_{H}(z,\cdot)
and
\mathcal{H}_{k}(z,\cdot)
are harmonic.
The estimate of
\nabla(\mathcal{G}_{k}-\mathcal{G}_{H})
follows at once since
\mathcal{G}_{k}-\mathcal{G}_{H}=\mathcal{H}_{k}-\mathcal{H}_{H}
.
∎

We state Lemmata
A.3
,
A.4
and
A.5
below with general positive coefficients
\alpha_{i,j}
and
\beta_{i}
for future reference, and also to remark that the coefficients

and
4\pi
appearing in the definitions of
\Phi_{N,h}
,
\Phi_{N}
, etc…, can be replaced by any other positive constants.

Lemma A.3
.

Consider for some
J\geq 2
the functional
\Xi_{J,\mathbb{R}^{2}}:(\mathbb{R}^{2})^{J}\setminus D_{J}\to\mathbb{R}

\Xi_{J,\mathbb{R}^{2}}(z_{1},\dots,z_{J}):=\sum_{\scriptstyle 1\leq i,j\leq J\atop\scriptstyle i\neq j}\alpha_{i,j}\ln\frac{1}{|z_{i}-z_{j}|},

with
\alpha_{i,j}>0
for
1\leq i,j\leq J
,

\Xi_{J,\mathbb{R}^{2}}

Proof.

Consider
1\leq i\leq J
such that

lies on the boundary of the convex hull of

∎

Lemma A.4
.

Consider for some

\gamma_{i}>0

∎

Appendix B
The Euler characteristic of configuration spaces

We will need the following general fact about
configuration spaces
.

Proposition B.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a smoothly bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and call for any
N\in\mathbb{N}^{\star}

F(\Omega,N):=\Omega^{N}\setminus D_{N}=\left\{(x_{1},\dots,x_{N})\in\Omega^{N}\;:\;x_{i}\neq x_{j}\hbox{ for }i\neq j\right\}.

Then

\chi(F(\Omega,N))=\chi(\Omega)(\chi(\Omega)-1)...(\chi(\Omega)-N+1).

Proof.

This result follows from the fact that
F(\Omega,N)
fibers on
F(\Omega,N-1)

with fiber
\Omega_{N-1}
(projecting on the last of the
N
points), where we denote by
\Omega_{j}
the set
\Omega
with
j
points removed, see
[
35
, Chapter 2]
.

Claim
The above fibration is
orientable
, in the sense that
\pi_{1}(F(\Omega,N-1))
acts trivially on the homology
H_{*}(\Omega_{N-1},\mathbb{R})
of the fiber.

Proof of the claim.

Since
\Omega_{N-1}
is homotopically equivalent to a
(connected) finite union of circles, the only relevant homology group of
\Omega_{N-1}
is
H_{1}(\Omega_{N-1},\mathbb{R})
. Consider now a closed path
\gamma\in C^{0}([0,1],F(\Omega,N-1))
representing a homotopy class
[\gamma]\in\pi_{1}(F(\Omega,N-1))
. Set

(x_{1}(t),\dots,x_{N-1}(t)):=\gamma(t).

For the point
\gamma(0)=:(x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1})\in F(\Omega,N-1)
consider the fiber

\Omega\setminus\{x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1}\}\simeq\Omega_{N-1}

and choose generators of its first homology group as follows.
Since
\Omega
is a two-dimensional domain, it is homotopic to a disk with
r
points removed,
for
r=0,1,2,\dots
. Given the
i
-th hole of
\Omega
,
i=1,\dots,r
,
choose an element
\sigma_{i}
of
H_{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
in the form of a smooth Jordan curve that does not contain any of the points
x_{1}(t),\dots,x_{N-1}(t)
with
t\in[0,1]
. Then it is clear that the action of
\gamma
on
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}
is trivial.

Now, for each
x_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,k-1
, choose a small and smooth Jordan curve
\eta_{j}
around
x_{j}
not intersecting
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}
, for instance the oriented boundary of
B_{\varepsilon}(x_{j})
. By compactness, if we choose
\varepsilon
small enough we get

\overline{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{j}(t))}\cap\overline{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{i}(t))}=\emptyset,\quad\text{for }1\leq i<j\leq N-1,\;t\in[0,1]

and

\overline{B_{\varepsilon}(x_{j}(t))}\cap\sigma_{i}=\emptyset,\quad\text{for }1\leq j\leq N-1,\;1\leq i\leq r,\;t\in[0,1].

Again it follows immediately that the action of
[\gamma]
of
\eta_{j}
is trivial.
Since

H_{1}(\Omega_{N-1},\mathbb{R})=\text{span}\{\sigma_{i},\eta_{j},1\leq i\leq r,1\leq j\leq N-1\}

the claim is proven.
∎

By the product formula for orientable fibrations, see Spanier
[
35
, p.481]
, we infer

\chi(F(\Omega,N))=\chi(F(\Omega,N-1))\chi(\Omega_{N-1})=\chi(F(\Omega,N-1))(\chi(\Omega)-N+1).

Now the proposition follows at once by induction in
N
.
∎

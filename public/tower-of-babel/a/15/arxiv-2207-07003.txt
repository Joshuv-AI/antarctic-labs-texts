The Yamabe flow on asymptotically Euclidean manifolds with nonpositive Yamabe constant
Gilles Carron, Eric Chen, Yi Wang

Abstract
We study the Yamabe flow on asymptotically flat manifolds with non-positive Yamabe constant Y ≤ 0 Y\leq 0 . Previous work by the second and third named authors [ CW21 ] showed that while the Yamabe flow always converges in a global weighted sense when Y > 0 Y>0 , the flow must diverge when Y ≤ 0 Y\leq 0 . We show here in the Y ≤ 0 Y\leq 0 case however that after suitable rescalings, the Yamabe flow starting from any asymptotically flat manifold must converge to the unique positive function which solves the Yamabe problem on a compactification of the original manifold.



1
Introduction

In this article we continue the study of the convergence of the Yamabe flow

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\frac{\partial g}{\partial t}=-Rg,\\
g(0)=g_{0},\end{cases}

(1.1)

starting from an asymptotically flat (AF) manifold
(M^{n},g_{0})
. Above,
R
denotes the scalar curvature of the Riemannian metric
g=g(t)
. This flow preserves the conformal class of
g_{0}
in the sense that
g(t)\in[g_{0}]
for all times
t
, and is the natural analogue of the volume-normalized Yamabe flow on compact manifolds introduced by Hamilton
[
Ham89
]
.
It is well known that on a compact manifold, the normalized Yamabe flow is the gradient flow of the Einstein–Hilbert functional within a fixed conformal class. It can be viewed as a natural evolution equation which could potentially evolve a given metric to a constant scalar curvature metric within the same conformal class.
For long-time existence and convergence of the Yamabe flow on compact manifolds, we refer interested readers to the work of Hamilton, Chow, Ye, Schwetlick–Struwe, and Brendle
[
Ham89
,
Cho92
,
Ye94
,
SS03
,
Bre05
,
Bre07
]
.
The study on noncompact manifolds is less developed. On noncompact manifolds, long-time existence has been proved under some assumptions of suitable pointwise bounds on curvature and conformal factors. See for instance
[
Ma19
]
,
[
Sch19
]
.
Other works give long-time existence results in the settings of conformally hyperbolic and singular spaces
[
Sch20
,
BV19
,
LV20
]
. Similar to the compact case, convergence results for the Yamabe flow on noncompact manifolds have been slower to develop—we are aware of
[
Ma19
,
Ma21
]
in which
C^{\infty}_{loc}
convergence to a scalar flat limit metric is shown, using crucially an assumption that the initial metric has non-negative scalar curvature, as well as
[
COV21
]
, which studies convergence of Yamabe flow on singular spaces with positive Yamabe constant.

The study of Yamabe flow on asymptotically flat manifolds was initiated by
[
CZ15
]
. They proved short-time existence and that asymptotic flatness is preserved under the flow. They also discussed the ADM mass under the flow.

In a previous article
[
CW21
]
by the second and third authors, we proved the long-time existence of Yamabe flow (
1.1
) on asymptotically flat manifolds. Moreover, we showed that the flow converges in a global weighted sense (defined by
[
Bar86
]
) if and only if the Yamabe constant
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])
is positive. Long-time existence was also studied independently by
[
Ma21
]
, who also considered local convergence assuming nonnegative scalar curvature.
The convergence/divergence behavior of the Yamabe flow on
asymptotically flat metrics is quite different from that of the Ricci flow; see for example
[
Li18
]
regarding the Ricci flow in this setting in dimension
n=3
. We also refer readers to related results on the Ricci flow in
[
DM07
,
Che19
]
.

For convenience, we recall the main theorems in
[
CW21
]
here, referring to Section
2.1
for definitions and notation related to asymptotically flat manifolds. When
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])>0
, the flow converges in a weighted global sense.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
CW21
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0})
,
n\geq 3
be a
C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau}
AF manifold with
Y(M,[g_{0}])>0
,
k\geq 3
, and
\tau>1
. Then there exists a Yamabe flow
(M^{n},g(t))
starting from
(M^{n},g_{0})
defined for all positive times and a metric
g_{\infty}
on
M^{n}
which is
C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau^{\prime}}
AF for all
\tau^{\prime}<\min\{\tau,n-2\}
so that for any such
\tau^{\prime}
we have

\displaystyle\|g(t)-g_{\infty}\|_{C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau^{\prime}}}=O(t^{-\delta_{0}}),\quad\mbox{as}\ t\rightarrow\infty,

(1.2)

for some
\delta_{0}>0
. In particular, this Yamabe flow converges in
C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau^{\prime}}
to the asymptotically flat, scalar flat metric
g_{\infty}
.

This theorem holds for
n\geq 3
, but in order to make sense the ADM mass of an asymptotically flat manifold when
n=3
, we have a different version in the
n=3
case by adding the natural conditions
R_{g_{0}}\geq 0
and
R_{g_{0}}\in L^{1}
(if we are concerned with the mass).

Theorem 1.2

(
[
CW21
, Theorem 1.4]
)
.

Let
(M^{3},g_{0})
be a
C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau}
AF manifold with
Y(M,[g_{0}])>0
,
k\geq 3
,
\tau>\frac{1}{2}
,
R_{g_{0}}\geq 0
, and
R_{g_{0}}\in L^{1}
. Then there exists a Yamabe flow
(M^{n},g(t))
starting from
(M^{n},g_{0})
defined for all positive times and a metric
g_{\infty}
on
M^{n}
which is
C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau^{\prime}}
AF for all
\tau^{\prime}<\min\{\tau,1\}
so that for any such
\tau^{\prime}
we have

\displaystyle\|g(t)-g_{\infty}\|_{C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau^{\prime}}}=O(t^{-\delta_{0}}),\quad\mbox{as}\ t\rightarrow\infty,

(1.3)

for some
\delta_{0}>0
. In particular, this Yamabe flow converges in
C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau^{\prime}}
to the asymptotically flat, scalar flat metric
g_{\infty}
.

In contrast to the positive Yamabe case, when
Y(M,[g_{0}])\leq 0
, while the flow still exists for all positive times, it must diverge.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
CW21
, Theorem 1.2 (2)]
)
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0})
be a
C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau}
AF manifold with
k\geq 3
.
If
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])\leq 0
, then the Yamabe flow
(M^{n},g(t))
starting from
(M^{n},g_{0})
does not converge. In particular,
g(t)=u(t)^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}
will fail to remain uniformly equivalent to
g_{0}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, and both
\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}
and the
L^{2}
Euclidean-type Sobolev constant of
g(t)
will tend to positive infinity.

Here
Y(M,[g_{0}])
is a conformally invariant quantity. Motivated by the definition of the Yamabe constant in the compact case,
Y(M,[g_{0}])
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle Y(M,[g_{0}]):=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}v\in C^{\infty}_{0}(M),\\
{v\neq 0}\end{subarray}}\frac{\int_{M}a_{n}|\nabla v|_{g_{0}}^{2}+R_{g_{0}}v^{2}\ dV_{g_{0}}}{\left(\int|v|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\ dV_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}},

(1.4)

with
a_{n}=\frac{4(n-1)}{n-2}
.
This Yamabe constant plays an important role in the prescribed scalar curvature problem on conformal classes of asymptotically flat metrics
[
CB81
,
Max05
,
DM18
]
.

The main goal of this article is to study the behavior of the flow in this latter case
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])\leq 0
. More precisely, we prove that even though the Yamabe flow diverges (which in our case is equivalent to saying the solution
u
blows up as
t\rightarrow\infty
,
by the proof of
[
CW21
, Lemma 3.4]
), the rescaled flow

t^{-\frac{n-2}{4}}u(x,t)
is convergent when
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])\leq 0
. Working from here, we obtain precise profiles of the blow up behavior of the flow.

Recall that if we write a Yamabe flow as
g(x,t)=u(x,t)^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}
, then
u
satisfies the parabolic equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}=\frac{n+2}{4}(a_{n}\Delta_{g_{0}}u-R_{g_{0}}u).

(1.5)

Sometimes we will also write
N=\frac{n+2}{n-2}
for the exponent on the left. To simplify writing the right-hand side, we denote by
L_{g_{0}}
the conformal Laplacian

L_{g_{0}}:=-a_{n}\Delta_{g_{0}}+R_{g_{0}}.

Throughout what follows,
u
will always denote a solution of this equation with
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}u(x,t)=1
.
By
[
CZ15
, Theorem 1.3]
,
g(t)
remains asymptotically flat along the Yamabe flow if
g_{0}
is asymptotically flat.

There are two main results of this paper—one when
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])<0
, and
the other when
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])=0
. In both these cases, the
u(x,t)
associated with the Yamabe flow
(M^{n},g(t))
blows up at a rate no faster than
O(t^{\frac{n-2}{4}})
. But the limiting profiles of
\tilde{u}(x,t):=t^{-\frac{n-2}{4}}u(x,t)
behave differently.

Theorem A
.

If
(M^{n},g_{0})
is a
C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau}
AF manifold with
k\geq 3
and
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])<0
,
then the Yamabe flow
(M^{n},g(t))
starting from
(M^{n},g_{0})
blows up at the rate
u(x,t)=O(t^{\frac{n-2}{4}})
.
Moreover,
\tilde{u}(x,t):=t^{-\frac{n-2}{4}}u(x,t)
converges in
C^{k,\alpha^{\prime}}_{loc}
for any
\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
to a limiting function
\tilde{u}_{\infty}>0
, where
\tilde{u}_{\infty}
is the unique solution to the equation of prescribed constant scalar curvature
-1
:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-a_{n}\Delta_{g_{0}}\tilde{u}_{\infty}+R_{g_{0}}\tilde{u}_{\infty}=-\tilde{u}_{\infty}^{N},\\
\tilde{u}_{\infty}\rightarrow 0.\end{cases}

(1.6)

Moreover,
\tilde{u}_{\infty}(x)
satisfies the sharp spatial decay
\tilde{u}_{\infty}(x)=O(|x|^{2-n})
, and
\tilde{u}_{\infty}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}
extends to the unique
-1
constant scalar curvature metric on the compactified space
\overline{M}
.

When
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])=0
, the rescaled function
\tilde{u}(x,t)
once again has a limit.
But in this case the limiting function vanishes on
M^{n}
.

Theorem B
.

If
(M^{n},g_{0})
is a
C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau}
AF manifold with
k\geq 3
and
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])=0
,
then the Yamabe flow
(M^{n},g(t))
starting from
(M^{n},g_{0})
satisfies
u(x,t)=o(t^{\frac{n-2}{4}})
.

Thus in order to describe the blow-up profile of the limit, we need a more delicate estimate. We prove in the following that the limit of the flow
u(x,t)
, after being renormalized by its maximum value on a compact set
K
at
t
is convergent. Moreover, such a limit is the positive canonical solution (up to a multiplicative constant) of a prescribed zero scalar curvature equation.

Theorem C
.

If
(M^{n},g_{0})
is a
C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau}
AF manifold with
k\geq 3
and
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])=0
,
then for the Yamabe flow
(M^{n},g(t))
starting from
(M^{n},g_{0})
and any fixed compact set
K\subset M^{n}
we have that
\frac{u(x,t)}{\max_{x\in K}u(x,t)}
converges in
C^{k,\alpha^{\prime}}_{loc}
for any
\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
to the unique positive solution
w(x)
on
(M^{n},g_{0})
which satisfies

\displaystyle-a_{n}\Delta_{g_{0}}w+R_{g_{0}}w=0,\quad\max_{x\in K}w(x)=1.

(1.7)

Moreover,
w(x)
satisfies the sharp spatial decay
w(x)=O(|x|^{2-n})
, and
w^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}
extends to the constant zero scalar curvature metric on the compactified space
\overline{M}
, which is unique up to scaling.

Remark
.

It is worth noting that it is unclear whether
\max_{x\in M}u(x,t)
may always be attained on some fixed compact set. However, given any compact set
K
, we can construct auxiliary functions
v_{b}(x,t)
and
v_{B}(x,t)
to bound
u(x,t)
from above and below. Moreover
v_{b}
and
v_{B}
both take their maximum values in
M
on
K
. These maximum values are suitable for use in the renormalization, and
\max_{x\in M}u(x,t)
will be no larger than a fixed constant multiple of
\max_{x\in K}u(x,t)
.

Organization of the article

The organization of the article is as follows:
In Section

2
Preliminaries

After recalling some relevant definitions and notation, we describe in this section some properties which hold for any Yamabe flow starting from an asymptotically flat manifolds. In the last part we discuss the existence of Yamabe metrics on certain compactifications of asymptotically flat manifolds with
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])\leq 0
.

2.1
Asymptotically flat manifolds

Here we recall as in
[
CW21
]
some standard function spaces and related definitions used in the analysis and definition of asymptotically flat (AF) manifolds. See for instance
[
Bar86
,
DM18
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
M^{n}
be a complete differentiable manifold such that there exists a compact
K\subset M^{n}
and a diffeomorphism
\Phi:M^{n}\backslash K\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R_{0}}(0)
, for some
R_{0}>0
. Let
r\geq 1
be a smooth function on
M^{n}
that agrees under the identification
\Phi
with the Euclidean radial coordinate
|x|
in a neighborhood of infinity, and let
\hat{g}
be a smooth metric on
M^{n}
which is equal to the Euclidean metric in a neighborhood of infinity under the identification
\Phi
. Then with all quantities below computed with respect to the metric
\hat{g}
, we have the following function spaces:

The weighted Lebesgue spaces
L^{q}_{\beta}(M)
, for
q\geq 1
and weight
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, consist of those locally integrable functions on
M
such that the following respective norms are finite:

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L_{\beta}^{q}(M)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\left(\int_{M}|v|^{q}r^{-\beta q-n}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},}&{q<\infty},\\
{\operatorname{ess}\sup_{M}\left(r^{-\beta}|v|\right),}&{q=\infty}.\end{array}\right.

The weighted Sobolev spaces
W^{k,q}_{\beta}(M)
are then defined in the usual way with the norms

\displaystyle\|v\|_{W_{\beta}^{k,q}(M)}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left\|D_{x}^{j}v\right\|_{L_{\beta-j}^{q}(M)}.

The weighted
C^{k}
spaces
C^{k}_{\beta}(M)
consist of the
C^{k}
functions for which the following respective norms are finite:

\displaystyle\|v\|_{C_{\beta}^{k}(M)}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sup_{M}r^{-\beta+j}\left|D_{x}^{j}v\right|.

The weighted Hölder spaces
C_{\beta}^{k+\alpha}(M)
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, consist of those
v\in C_{\beta}^{k}(M)
for which the following respective norms are finite:

\displaystyle\|v\|_{C_{\beta}^{k+\alpha}(M)}=\|v\|_{C_{\beta}^{k}(M)}+\sup_{x\neq y\in M}\min(r(x),r(y))^{-\beta+k+\alpha}\frac{\left|D_{x}^{k}v(x)-D_{x}^{k}v(y)\right|}{d(x,y)^{\alpha}}.

An asymptotically flat manifold is then a smooth manifold with an asymptotically flat metric.

Definition 2.2

(Asymptotically flat metrics)
.

Given
M^{n}
as in Definition
2.1
, a metric
g
is said to be a
W^{k,q}_{-\tau}
(respectively
C^{k}_{-\tau}
,
C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau}
) asymptotically flat (AF) metric if
\tau>0
and

\displaystyle g-\hat{g}\in W^{k,q}_{-\tau}(M)\quad\text{(respectively $C^{k}_{-\tau}(M)$, $C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau}(M)$)}.

(2.3)

The number
\tau>0
is called the order of the asymptotically flat metric.

2.2
Comparison principle

We wish to be able to compare solutions of the parabolic equation (
1.5
). The proof of the result below follows some of the arguments in
[
Sch19
, Lemma 1.4]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
u_{1},u_{2}
be two positive solutions of (
1.5
) for all
t\geq 0
. Suppose moreover that

(1)

For all
T>0
, there exists
C_{T}>0
such that

0<C_{T}^{-1}\leq u_{1},u_{2}\leq C_{T}\quad\text{on }M\times[0,T].

(2)

For all
t\geq 0
,

\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}u_{1}(x,t)<\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}u_{2}(x,t),

and these limits are achieved uniformly in space on
[0,T]
.

(3)

We have
u_{1}(x,0)\leq u_{2}(x,0)
.

Then for all
t\geq 0
we have
u_{1}(x,t)\leq u_{2}(x,t)
.

Proof.

Now by conditions (
2
) and (
3
), for any
(x,T)
there exists a set
\Omega\subset M
such that
x\in\Omega
and
u_{1}\leq u_{2}
on
(\Omega\times\{0\})\cup(\partial\Omega\times[0,T])
. Indeed, those conditions imply that
\ell_{i}(t)=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}u_{i}(x,t)
for
i=1,2
are continuous functions, so that
\inf_{t\in[0,T]}\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}u_{2}(x,t)-u_{1}(x,t)>0
. Since these limits are achieved uniformly in space, this last inequality allows us to find
\Omega
sufficiently large with the desired properties.

Then by the linear parabolic maximum principle
[
PW84
, §3.3]
we must have
u_{1}\leq u_{2}
in
\Omega\times[0,T]
. Since
(x,T)
was arbitrary the result follows.
∎

2.3
Growth control and rescaled solutions

As mentioned in the Introduction, from
[
CW21
]
we know that the solutions
u(x,t)
of (
1.5
) corresponding to Yamabe flows starting from AF manifolds with
Y\leq 0
must blow up. Below we first observe that a standard estimate on the evolution of the scalar curvature allows us to control the growth of
u
in general.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(M^{n},g_{0})
be a
C^{2+\alpha}_{-\tau}
AF manifold. Along the Yamabe flow
(M^{n},g(t))
, we have

R(g(t))\geq-\frac{1}{t}.

Proof.

Recall that under the Yamabe flow we have
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}R=(n-1)\Delta_{g_{t}}R+R^{2}
, and let
\alpha<0
be such that
R_{g_{\rho}}\geq\alpha
when
t=0
. Choose an
\epsilon>0
, and set

\phi(t)=\frac{\alpha}{\epsilon-\alpha t},

which satisfies

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\phi=\phi^{2}=(n-1)\Delta_{g_{t}}\phi+\phi^{2}.

Then

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}(\phi-R)=(n-1)\Delta_{g_{t}}(\phi-R)+(\phi+R)(\phi-R),

and we may apply the Ecker–Huisken maximum principle
[
EH91
, Theorem 4.3]
on
M\times[0,T]
for any
T>0
to conclude that
\phi-R\leq 0
on
M\times[0,\infty)
. Taking
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
yields
R(x,t)\geq-\frac{1}{t}
.
∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
u(x,t)
be a solution of (
1.5
) corresponding to the Yamabe flow starting from a
C^{2+\alpha}_{-\tau}
AF manifold. Then
t^{-\frac{n-2}{4}}u(x,t)
is nonincreasing, and thus has a nonnegative limit, so that

\max_{x\in M}u(x,t)=O(t^{\frac{n-2}{4}}).

Proof.

Integrate the equation

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u=-\frac{n-2}{4}Ru

and apply the estimate of Lemma
2.4
.
∎

In light of Proposition
2.5
, we define

\tilde{u}(x,t)=t^{-\frac{n-2}{4}}u(x,t).

(2.4)

Since
\tilde{u}(x,t)\geq 0
and is monotonically decreasing, it has a pointwise limit as
t\rightarrow\infty
, and we set

\tilde{u}_{\infty}(x)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{u}(x,t).

(2.5)

When
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])<0
we will see that
\tilde{u}_{\infty}>0
is a smooth function solving a naturally associated elliptic equation, while when
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])=0
we will find that
\tilde{u}_{\infty}\equiv 0
converges to zero. In this latter case, a different normalization of
u
yields a (smooth) positive limit.

2.4
Conformally compactifying
M

In order to carry out our study of the cases
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])\leq 0
, we check below in this setting that there exists a conformal compactification of
M
which topologically is given by adding a point at infinity to
M
, and which solves the Yamabe problem on this compactified manifold, so that it has constant scalar curvature
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])
.

First we have the following result on compactifying
M
by adding a single point at infinity, without yet adding a requirement on its scalar curvature. See also
[
Her97
]
for related compactification results.

Lemma 2.6

(
[
DM18
, Lemmas 5.2, 5.3]
)
.

Suppose
(M,g)
is a
W^{2,p}_{-\tau}
AE manifold, with
p>n/2
and
\tau\geq\frac{n}{p}-2
. Then there exists a smooth conformal factor
\phi
decaying to zero at the rate
r^{2-n}
such that
\overline{g}=\phi^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g
extends to a
W^{2,p}
metric on the one-point compactification
\overline{M}=M\cup\{q\}
. Moreover,
Y(M,g)=Y(\overline{M},[\overline{g}])
.

Remark
.

Note that
\overline{g}
is smooth away from
q
; the metric
\overline{g}
is
W^{2,p}
in the sense that in a coordinate ball
B
about
q
, the components of
\overline{g}
along with its first and second derivatives are
L^{p}
integrable with respect to
dV_{\overline{g}}
. Hence the metric
\overline{g}
is Hölder continuous and bilipschitz equivalent to a smooth metric on the manifold
\overline{M}
—that is to say there is a smooth Riemannian metric
\underline{g}
on
\overline{M}
such that for some positive constant
\gamma:

\gamma^{-2}\underline{g}\leq\overline{g}\leq\gamma^{2}\underline{g}.

Hence

\gamma^{-n}dV_{\underline{g}}\leq dV_{\overline{g}}\leq\gamma^{n}dV_{\underline{g}}\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma^{-1}d_{\underline{g}}\leq d_{\overline{g}}\leq\gamma d_{\underline{g}}.

Moreover for any smooth function
\varphi:

\gamma^{-1}|d\varphi|_{\underline{g}}\leq|d\varphi|_{\overline{g}}\leq\gamma|d\varphi|_{\underline{g}}.

So the spaces
L^{p}(\overline{M},\underline{g})
and
L^{p}(\overline{M},\overline{g})
are the same with equivalent norms, and the same is true for the
W^{1,2}
-spaces.

Next, we need to solve the Yamabe problem on
(\overline{M},[\overline{g}])
. We apply a result of
[
ACM14
]
to do so.

Lemma 2.7
.

Suppose
(M,g)
is a
W^{2,p}_{-\tau}
AE manifold for some
p>n/2
and
\tau\geq\frac{n}{p}-2
, with
Y(M,[g])<0
. Then in the notation of Lemma
2.6
, on
(\overline{M},\overline{g})
there exists a function
u\in W^{1,2}\cap L^{\infty}(\overline{M})
with
\inf_{\overline{M}}u>0
such that on
\overline{M}\setminus\{q\}
,

-a_{n}\overline{\Delta}u+\overline{R}u=Y(M,[g])u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}.

(2.6)

Here
\overline{\Delta}
and
\overline{R}
denote the Laplacian operator and the scalar curvature of
\overline{g}
respectively.

Proof.

The result follows from
[
ACM14

Proposition 2.9
.

The function

given by Lemma

Proof.

Let

be two weak solutions of (

The usual arguments in the smooth case to establish uniqueness can be adapted in the non-smooth setting.
In fact the equation (

\int_{\overline{M}}a_{n}\langle d\varphi,dv\rangle_{g_{2}}dV_{g_{2}}=Y\int_{\overline{M}}\varphi(v^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}-v)dV_{g_{2}}

We can then test against

and the equality (

\int_{\left\{v\geq 1\right\}}a_{n}|dv|^{2}_{g_{2}}dV_{g_{2}}=Y\int_{\left\{v\geq 1\right\}}(v-1)\,(v^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}-v)dV_{g_{2}}.

3
The case
Y<0

In the case
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])<0
, we compare the conformal factor
u_{0}
as defined in (
2.13
) to the rescaled Yamabe flow solution
\tilde{u}
as defined in (
2.4
), and find that
\tilde{u}
in fact converges to a scalar multiple of
u_{0}
, which will prove Theorem
A
. For convenience we will often write
Y
instead of
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])
. We first show that
|Y|^{\frac{n-2}{4}}u_{0}
always bounds
\tilde{u}
from below.

Lemma 3.1
.

If
Y<0
, then
|Y|^{\frac{n-2}{4}}u_{0}(x)\leq\tilde{u}(x,t)
for all times
t
.

Proof.

Let
u_{1}(x,t)=(t|Y|)^{\frac{n-2}{4}}u_{0}(x)
, and define
g_{u_{1}}=u_{1}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}
. Then we have
R_{g_{u_{1}}}=(t|Y|)^{-1}R_{g_{u_{0}}}=-t^{-1}
, and

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u_{1}=\frac{n-2}{4}t^{-1}(t|Y|)^{\frac{n-2}{4}}u_{0}(x)=-\frac{n-2}{4}R_{g_{u_{1}}}(t|Y|)^{\frac{n-2}{4}}u_{0}=-\frac{n-2}{4}R_{g_{u_{1}}}u_{1}.

Equivalently,
u_{1}
is a solution of the Yamabe flow. Therefore by the asymptotics of
u_{0},u_{1}
, we can apply the comparison principle of Lemma
2.3
to conclude that
u_{1}(x,t)\leq u(x,t)
on
M\times[0,\infty)
(we know
u_{1}\leq u
for small times, and
u_{1}\leq u
as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
for each fixed
t>0
, so we can apply the comparison starting at a sufficiently small positive time). Rewriting, we see that
|Y|^{\frac{n-2}{4}}u_{0}(x)\leq t^{-\frac{n-2}{4}}u(x,t)=\tilde{u}(x,t)
.
∎

By Proposition
2.5
and Lemma
3.1
together, we see that the pointwise limit
\tilde{u}_{\infty}(x)
of
\tilde{u}(x,t)
as
t\rightarrow\infty
satisfies
\tilde{u}_{\infty}(x)\geq|Y|^{\frac{n-2}{4}}u_{0}(x)
.
We now continue our study of the convergence of
\tilde{u}(x,t)
and properties of
\tilde{u}_{\infty}(x)
.

Proposition 3.2
.

We have that
\tilde{u}(x,t)
converges to
\tilde{u}_{\infty}(x)
in
C^{k,\alpha}_{loc}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, and
\tilde{u}_{\infty}(x)
satisfies

-a_{n}\Delta_{g_{0}}\tilde{u}_{\infty}+R_{g_{0}}\tilde{u}_{\infty}=-\tilde{u}_{\infty}^{N}.

(3.1)

Proof.

Transform the time parameter
t
to
s
via the relation
t=e^{s}
, for
s\in(-\infty,\infty)
. Then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\tilde{u}^{N}

\displaystyle=e^{s}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(t^{-\frac{n+2}{4}}u^{N}\right)

\displaystyle=t\left(-\frac{n+2}{4}t^{-\frac{n+2}{4}-1}u^{N}+t^{-\frac{n+2}{4}}\frac{n+2}{4}\left(a_{n}\Delta_{g_{0}}u-R_{g_{0}}u\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n+2}{4}\left(a_{n}\Delta_{g_{0}}\tilde{u}-R_{g_{0}}\tilde{u}-\tilde{u}^{N}\right).

Since
\tilde{u}
is monotonically decreasing and bounded from below by
|Y|^{\frac{n-2}{4}}u_{0}
, we have that
\tilde{u}
is bounded from above and below uniformly in time for
t>0
on any compact region
\Omega\subset M
. Therefore from the above computation we see that
\tilde{u}(x,e^{s})
satisfies a uniformly parabolic equation on any such
\Omega
,
s\in(s_{0},+\infty)
for
s_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, and therefore by the Krylov–Safonov and (higher order) Schauder estimates for parabolic equations

\|\tilde{u}(x,e^{s})\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\Omega\times[s,s+1])}\leq C(\Omega).

Consequently by Arzela-Ascoli we obtain for some sequence
\{t_{j}\}
with
t_{j}\rightarrow\infty
that

\tilde{u}(x,t_{j})\rightarrow\tilde{u}_{\infty}(x)

in
C^{k,\alpha^{\prime}}_{loc}
, for any
\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
. We can deduce more generally since
\tilde{u}
is monotonically decreasing that
\tilde{u}(x,t)
converges to
\tilde{u}_{\infty}
in
C^{k,\alpha^{\prime}}_{loc}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. As a result, we see that
\tilde{u}_{\infty}
satisfies the steady state equation (
3.1
), which is also satisfied by
u_{0}
.
∎

We now check that
\tilde{u}_{\infty}
also decays at spatial infinity.

Lemma 3.3
.

We have
\tilde{u}_{\infty}(x)\rightarrow 0
as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
.

Proof.

Suppose the property does not hold. Then there exists a sequence of points
\{x_{i}\}
with
|x_{i}|\rightarrow\infty
and some
\epsilon>0
such that
\tilde{u}_{\infty}(x_{i})\geq\epsilon>0
. Since
\tilde{u}
decreases monotonically to
\tilde{u}_{\infty}
, this implies

\epsilon\leq\tilde{u}_{\infty}(x_{i})\leq t^{-\frac{n-2}{4}}u(x_{i},t),\quad\text{for all }x_{i}\text{ and }t>0.

For
t
sufficiently large, we therefore have

2\leq t^{\frac{n-2}{4}}\epsilon\leq u(x_{i},t),

which contradicts the fact that
u(x,t)\xrightarrow{|x|\rightarrow\infty}1
for all
t>0
.
∎

Because
\tilde{u}_{\infty}\xrightarrow{|z|\rightarrow\infty}0
, we will have on
(\overline{M},\hat{g})
that

\left(\frac{\tilde{u}_{\infty}}{|Y|^{\frac{n-2}{4}}u_{0}}\right)(p)=o(d(p,q)^{2-n})\quad\text{as }p\rightarrow q.

(3.2)

Using this and our previous estimates, we can now prove Theorem
A
.

Proof of Theorem
A
.

In light of the above results, and using the same notation as before, it suffices to show that
\tilde{u}_{\infty}=|Y|^{\frac{n-2}{4}}u_{0}
. Recall that
(\overline{M},\hat{g})
has constant scalar curvature
\hat{R}=Y<0
. Since
R(\tilde{u}_{\infty}^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0})=-1
by Proposition
3.2
, we have that
v:=\frac{\tilde{u}_{\infty}}{|Y|^{\frac{n-2}{4}}u_{0}}
satisfies

-a_{n}\hat{\Delta}v+Yv=Yv^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}

(3.3)

on
\overline{M}\setminus\{q\}
. We will show that
v\equiv 1
. Recall from Lemma
3.1
that we already know
v\geq 1
.

Fix a small neighborhood
U
about
q
, and let
G_{q}
be the Green’s function of
\hat{\Delta}
with pole at
q
associated with the Dirichlet problem on
U
, as provided by Proposition
2.10
(recall that
\hat{g}
is

on
\overline{M}
and smooth away from
q
). We will use the following properties of

:

-\hat{\Delta}G_{q}=\delta_{q}\quad\text{and}\quad G_{q}\geq 0\quad\text{ on }U,\quad G_{q}(p)\sim d(p,q)^{2-n}\quad\text{near }q.

Now we have for any

and any

-a_{n}\hat{\Delta}(v-1-c-\epsilon G_{q})=-Yv+Yv^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\leq 0,\quad\text{on }U\setminus\{q\},

and since

Moreover, by our estimates on the growth of

Strictly slower blowup

Lemma 4.1

The function

Proof.

Starting from the evolution equation (

\displaystyle\underbrace{t^{-\frac{n+2}{4}}\left(\int_{M}u(x,t)^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\varphi(x)\ dV_{g_{0}}-\int_{M}u(x,0)^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\varphi(x)\ dV_{g_{0}}\right)}_{A(t)}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{-\frac{n+2}{4}t^{-\frac{n+2}{4}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{M}u(x,s)L_{g_{0}}\varphi(x)\ dV_{g_{0}}\ ds}_{B(t)}.

Clearly we have

\left|\int_{M}\tilde{u}_{\infty}(x)^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\varphi(x)\ dV_{g_{0}}-A(t)\right|\xrightarrow{t\rightarrow\infty}0.

We next claim that

\left|-\int_{M}\tilde{u}_{\infty}(x)L_{g_{0}}\varphi(x)\ dV_{g_{0}}-B(t)\right|\xrightarrow{t\rightarrow\infty}0.

(4.2)

To see this, we compute

\displaystyle-\frac{n+2}{4}t^{-\frac{n+2}{4}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{M}u(x,s)L_{g_{0}}\varphi(x)\ dV_{g_{0}}\ ds

\displaystyle=-\frac{n+2}{4}t^{-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{M}\tilde{u}(x,s)\frac{s^{\frac{n-2}{4}}}{t^{\frac{n-2}{4}}}L_{g_{0}}\varphi(x)\ dV_{g_{0}}\ ds

\displaystyle=-\frac{n+2}{4}\int_{M}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\tilde{u}(x,wt)w^{\frac{n-2}{4}}\ dw\right)L_{g_{0}}\varphi(x)\ dV_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{t\rightarrow\infty}-\frac{n+2}{4}\int_{M}\frac{4}{n+2}\tilde{u}_{\infty}(x)L_{g_{0}}\varphi(x)\ dV_{g_{0}}

\displaystyle\quad\qquad=-\int_{M}\tilde{u}_{\infty}(x)L_{g_{0}}\varphi(x)\ dV_{g_{0}}.

where above in the third line we substituted

, and in the fourth line we used that

is integrable (for any

) and monotonically decreases to

as

to apply the dominated convergence theorem.

and we can choose

4.3
) we have

0\leq(-a_{n}\hat{\Delta}v)(p)=-v(p)^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}\leq 0.

Hence
\max_{\overline{M}}v=v(p)=0
so that
v=0
and
\tilde{u}_{\infty}=0
.
∎

4.2
Convergence after a different rescaling

Above, we have seen that unlike in the
Y<0
case, when
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])=0
rescaling
u(x,t)
by
t^{-\frac{n-2}{4}}
only gives the trivial limit
\tilde{u}_{\infty}\equiv 0
. We now describe how to normalize
u(x,t)
in a different way so as to identify a nontrivial limiting behavior as
t\rightarrow\infty
, proving Theorem
C
. For this we need the following existence result for a certain conformal change of
g_{0}
to another asymptotically flat metric with compactly supported non-positive scalar curvature as described below, which follows from a result of
[
DM18
]
.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
DM18
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

For any
K\subset M
compact and when
\tau\in(0,n-2)
, there exists a metric
g_{\rho,0}=\rho^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{0}
with
\rho-1\in C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau}
such that
R_{g_{\rho,0}}
is compactly supported within
K
, and
\left.R_{g_{\rho,0}}\right|_{K}\leq 0
.

Proof.

By
[
DM18
, Theorem 5.1]
and the embedding
C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau}\subset W^{k,p}_{-\tau^{\prime}}
for any
\tau^{\prime}\in(0,\tau)
, we have that the above result holds with
C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau}
replaced by
W^{k,p}_{-\tau^{\prime}}
. Then by applying
[
CW21
, Lemma B.3]
, we see that moreover
\rho-1\in C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau}
.
∎

We can then rewrite the evolution equation satisfied by
u
in terms of the metric
g_{\rho,0}
as

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u_{\rho}^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}=\frac{n+2}{4}(a_{n}\Delta_{g_{\rho,0}}u_{\rho}-R_{g_{\rho,0}}u_{\rho}),

(4.4)

with
u_{\rho}(x,0)=\rho(x)^{-1}
. We will then have that

u_{\rho}(x,t)=\rho(x)^{-1}u(x,t).

(4.5)

Let
v=v(x,t)
be the solution of (
4.4
) satisfying the initial condition
v(x,0)\equiv 1
with
v-1\in C^{k+\alpha}_{-\tau}
, and for
c>0
, set

v_{c}(x,t)=cv\left(x,c^{-\frac{4}{n-2}}t\right).

Then
v_{c}
also solves (
4.4
).

We then have the following inequalities which follow directly from the comparison principle of Lemma
2.3
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
u
and
v
be as above, and set

\displaystyle b

\displaystyle=\min_{x\in M}u_{\rho}(x,0)=\min_{x\in M}\rho^{-1}(x)\leq 1,

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\max_{x\in M}u_{\rho}(x,0)=\max_{x\in M}\rho^{-1}(x)\geq 1.

Then for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in M
we have

v_{b}(x,t)\leq u(x,t)\leq v_{B}(x,t).

In order to study the convergence of
v
(and subsequently
u
) under appropriate rescalings, we first need control of the scalar curvatures of the associated metrics. Let

g_{\rho}(x,t)=v(x,t)^{\frac{4}{n-2}}g_{\rho,0}

be the family of metrics which make up the Yamabe flow starting from
g_{\rho,0}
.

Lemma 4.4
.

The scalar curvature of the metric
g_{\rho}
satisfies

-\frac{1}{t}\leq R_{g_{\rho}}\leq 0.

Proof.

Proposition
2.4
gives us the lower bound. For the upper bound, we again apply the Ecker–Huisken maximum principle
[
EH91
, Theorem 4.3]
, this time directly to
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}R=(n-1)\Delta R+R^{2}
on
M\times[0,T]
, where
T>0
is arbitrary. Since
R_{g_{\rho}}(0)\leq 0
by assumption, we conclude that
R_{g_{\rho}}\leq 0
for all times.
∎

Although
v
is not bounded in time, since it corresponds to a Yamabe flow starting from an asymptotically flat manifold with
Y(M^{n},[g_{0}])=0
, we have that its maximum values remain in the compact set
K
, which contains the support of
R_{\rho_{0}}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
B(t)=\max_{x\in M}v(x,t)
, and let
K\subset M
be a compact set. Then

B(t)=\max_{x\in K}v(x,t).

Proof.

Since
R_{\rho,0}
is supported within
K
for all
t\geq 0
and
R_{g_{\rho}}\leq 0
by Lemma
4.4
, the function
v(x,t)-1
is subharmonic on
M\setminus K
and tends to zero at spatial infinity. So the maximum principle yields that

\sup_{x\in M\setminus K}v(x,t)-1\leq\max_{x\in\partial K}v(x,t)-1,

which leads to the desired conclusion.
∎

Next, with the help of the functions
v_{b}
and
v_{B}
, we establish a Harnack inequality for
u_{\rho}
.

Proposition 4.6
.

For
p\in M
and
R>0
, there exists
C
such that

\sup_{x\in B(p,R)}u_{\rho}(x,t)\leq C\inf_{x\in B(p,R)}u_{\rho}(x,t),

for any
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove a Harnack inequality for
v
. Indeed, a Harnack inequality for
v
implies a Harnack inequality for both
v_{b}
and
v_{B}
. Moreover, since

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}v=-\frac{n-2}{4}R_{g_{\rho}}v,

with
-\frac{1}{t}\leq R_{g_{\rho}}\leq 0
by Lemma
4.4
, we have
v_{B}\leq\frac{B}{b}v_{b}
. Putting things together, we then see that (omitting the
x\in B(p,R)
subscript) we would have

\sup u_{\rho}\leq\sup v_{B}\leq\frac{B}{b}\sup v_{b}\leq C\frac{B}{b}\inf v_{b}\leq C\frac{B}{b}\inf u_{\rho},

as desired. So we will now prove that
v
satisfies a Harnack inequality.

Recall that
v
satisfies

-a_{n}\Delta_{g_{\rho,0}}v+R_{g_{\rho,0}}v=R_{g_{\rho}}v^{\frac{4}{n-2}}v.

(4.6)

By Lemma
4.4
and Proposition
2.5
, we have that
\left|R_{g_{\rho}}v^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\right|
is bounded by a uniform constant, while (
4.6
) is an elliptic equation, with the operator
\Delta_{g_{\rho,0}}
defined using the fixed metric
g_{\rho,0}
. Therefore the Harnack inequality for
v
follows (for instance, by
[
GT83
, Theorem 8.20]
).
∎

Corollary 4.7
.

We have that
\max_{x\in K}v(x,t)=o(t^{\frac{n-2}{4}})
, which in turn implies
\max_{x\in K}u(x,t)=o(t^{\frac{n-2}{4}})
. More generally, we have that
\max_{x\in M}v(x,t)=o(t^{\frac{n-2}{4}})
and
\max_{x\in M}u(x,t)=o(t^{\frac{n-2}{4}})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.6
, there exists some fixed
C>0
such that for all times
t>0
there holds

\sup_{x\in K}v(x,t)\leq C\inf_{x\in K}v(x,t).

(4.7)

Then for any
\epsilon>0
, by Theorem
A
we can pick
x_{0}\in K
and find
T>0
large enough so that whenever
t\geq T
we have

v(x_{0},t)\leq\frac{\epsilon}{C}t^{\frac{n-2}{4}}.

So by (
4.7
) we conclude that when
t\geq T
we also have
\sup_{x\in K}v(x,t)\leq\epsilon t^{\frac{n-2}{4}}
. Lemmas
4.5
and
4.3
imply the decay of
\sup_{x\in M}v(x,t)
and
\sup_{x\in M}u(x,t)
.
∎

Using the above facts, we can now show that we have a strictly positive subsequential limit of
\frac{u(x,t)}{\max_{x\in K}u(x,t)}
.

Proposition 4.8
.

The functions
\frac{u(x,t)}{\max_{x\in K}u(x,t)}
subconverge in
C^{k,\alpha^{\prime}}_{loc}
for any
\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
to a positive function
w(x)>0
satisfying

-a_{n}\Delta_{g_{0}}w+R_{g_{0}}w\geq 0.

(4.8)

Proof.

For
\tau>0
, we define

L(\tau)=\max_{x\in K}u(x,\tau),

and

U_{\tau}(x,t)=L(\tau)^{-1}u\left((x,L(\tau)^{\frac{4}{n-2}}t+\tau\right).

Then
U_{\tau}
also solves (
1.5
). Moreover for
\tau>0
sufficiently large,
U_{\tau}
is uniformly bounded for
t\in\left[-\frac{1}{2},1\right]
from above on
M
, and uniformly bounded from below away from zero on compact subsets of
M
. To see the upper bound, first observe that by Corollary
4.7
,

L(\tau)^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\leq\tau

whenever
\tau
is sufficiently large. Therefore

\sup_{t\in\left[-\frac{1}{2},1\right]}U_{\tau}(x,t)\leq L(\tau)^{-1}\sup_{s\in\left[\frac{\tau}{2},2\tau\right]}u(x,s).

Next, we have that

\sup_{s\in\left[\frac{\tau}{2},2\tau\right]}u(x,s)\leq B\sup_{s\in\left[\frac{\tau}{2},2\tau\right]}v_{B}(x,s)\leq B^{2}2^{\frac{n-2}{4}}B(\tau),

using for the second inequality that
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}v=-\frac{n-2}{4}R_{\rho}v
.
Since we also have that

b^{2}B(\tau)\leq b\sup_{x\in K}v_{b}(x,\tau)\leq L(\tau),

putting all these estimates together yields the upper bound

U_{\tau}(x,t)\leq\frac{B^{2}}{b^{2}}2^{\frac{n-2}{4}},

(4.9)

which hold for all
t\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
.

For the lower bound, we first note that positive constant multiples of
u(x,t)
also satisfy a Harnack inequality as in Proposition
4.6
because
u_{\rho}(x,t)
does, since

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in B(p,R)}u(x,t)

\displaystyle\leq B\sup_{x\in B(p,R)}u_{\rho}(x,t)

\displaystyle\leq BC\inf_{x\in B(p,R)}u_{\rho}(x,t)\leq\frac{B}{b}C\inf_{x\in B(p,R)}u(x,t).

We also have that

b^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{4}}B(\tau)\leq b\sup v_{b}(x,\tau/2)\leq b\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in K\\
s\in\left[\frac{\tau}{2},2\tau\right]\end{subarray}}u_{\rho}(x,s)\leq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in K\\
s\in\left[\frac{\tau}{2},2\tau\right]\end{subarray}}u(x,s),

and that

L(\tau)\leq B\sup_{x\in K}u_{\rho}(x,\tau)\leq B^{2}B(\tau).

Hence

\frac{b^{2}}{B^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{4}}\leq L(\tau)^{-1}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in K\\
s\in\left[\frac{\tau}{2},2\tau\right]\end{subarray}}u(x,s).

Applying the Harnack inequality and translating back, this shows that
U_{\tau}
is indeed bounded from below away from zero uniformly on compact sets for
t\in\left[-\frac{1}{2},1\right]
.

Because
U_{\tau}
is uniformly bounded above and below away from zero on compact sets, we can now apply local parabolic Krylov-Safonov and Schauder estimates to (
1.5
) to obtain uniform local Hölder estimates on
\overline{\Omega}\times\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
. In particular we have uniform
C^{k,\alpha}_{loc}
control of
U_{\tau}(x,0)=\frac{u(x,\tau)}{\max_{x\in K}u(x,\tau)}
and therefore subconvergence in
C^{k,\alpha^{\prime}}_{loc}
for any
\alpha^{\prime}<\alpha
to some
w(x)>0
as
\tau\rightarrow\infty
. The positivity of
w(x)
is a consequence of our uniform lower bound on
\frac{u(x,\tau)}{\max_{x\in K}u(x,\tau)}
on compact sets.

To verify the equation satisfied by
w
, recall that

\displaystyle-a_{n}\Delta_{g_{0}}\frac{u(x,t)}{\max_{x\in K}u(x,t)}+R_{g_{0}}\frac{u(x,t)}{\max_{x\in K}u(x,t)}

\displaystyle\quad=R_{g(t)}u(x,t)^{\frac{4}{n-2}}\frac{u(x,t)}{\max_{x\in K}u(x,t)}.

On the right-hand side we have

R_{g(t)}\geq-\frac{1}{t}
by Lemma
2.4
and
u_{\rho}(x,t)=o(t^{\frac{n-2}{4}})
by Theorem
B
. So it follows that in the limit we have (
4.8
) in the classical sense.

∎

From the discussion leading up to (
2.13
), we know there exists a function
u_{0}
which makes (
4.8
) an equality. The following uniqueness result will allow us to identify the limit of the rescaling
\frac{u_{\rho}(x,t)}{\max_{x\in K}u(x,t)}
.

Proposition 4.9
.

Suppose
(M,g)
is a
W^{2,p}_{-\tau}
AF manifold for some
p>n/2
and
\tau\geq\frac{n}{2}-2
, with
Y(M,[g])=0
. Recall that there is a positive function
u_{0}
solving the equation

-a_{n}\Delta_{g}u_{0}+R_{g}u_{0}=0,

such that

u_{0}=O(|z|^{2-n})
as
|z|\rightarrow\infty
.
Then if
w
is a positive function on
(M,g)
in
W^{1,2}_{loc}
such that

-a_{n}\Delta_{g}w+R_{g}w\geq 0,

then there is a positive constant
c
such that

w=cu_{0}.

Proof.

We write
w=\psi u_{0}
and a classical calculation yields the equality:

\displaystyle-a_{n}\Delta w+R_{g}w

\displaystyle=\left(-a_{n}\Delta u_{0}+R_{g}u_{0}\right)\psi-2a_{n}\langle du_{0},d\psi\rangle-a_{n}u_{0}\Delta\psi

\displaystyle=-2a_{n}\langle du_{0},d\psi\rangle-a_{n}u_{0}\Delta\psi

\displaystyle=-a_{n}u_{0}^{-1}\text{div}\left(u_{0}^{2}d\psi\right).

Integration this identity against
\varphi^{2}\psi^{-1}u_{0}
where
\varphi
is a non negative compactly supported function and taking in account our hypothesis, one gets

\int_{M}\varphi^{2}\frac{|d\psi|^{2}_{g}}{\psi^{2}}u_{0}^{2}dV_{g}\leq 2\int_{M}\varphi\langle d\varphi,d\psi\rangle\frac{u_{0}^{2}}{\psi}dV_{g}.

And with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, one obtains the inequality

\int_{M}\varphi^{2}\frac{|d\psi|^{2}_{g}}{\psi^{2}}u_{0}^{2}dV_{g}\leq 4\int_{M}|d\varphi|^{2}_{g}u_{0}^{2}dV_{g}.

Using a sequence of functions
\varphi_{\ell}
satisfying

\begin{cases}\varphi_{\ell}=1&\text{ on }~|z|\leq\ell\\
\varphi_{\ell}=0&\text{ on }~|z|\geq 2\ell\\
|d\varphi_{\ell}|\leq 2/\ell&\text{on }\ell\leq|z|\leq 2\ell.\end{cases}

together with the decay estimate of
u_{0}
and the fact that

V_{g}\left(\{\ell\leq|z|\leq 2\ell\}\right)=O\left(\ell^{n}\right)

one obtains that

\int_{\{|z|\leq\ell\}}\frac{|d\psi|^{2}_{g}}{\psi^{2}}u_{0}^{2}dV_{g}\leq O(\ell^{2-n})

Hence letting
\ell\to+\infty
, one deduces that

d\psi=0
.
∎

Putting everything together, we can now prove Theorem
C
.

Proof of Theorem
C
.

This follows immediately from Propositions
4.8
and
4.9
; note that
\sup_{x\in K}\frac{u(x,t)}{\max_{x\in K}u(x,t)}=1
for all
t
implies
\sup_{x\in K}w(x)=1
. The uniqueness of
u_{0}
up to scaling is provided by either Proposition
4.9
or Proposition
2.9
. Because any convergent subsequence of
\frac{u(x,t)}{\max_{x\in K}u(x,t)}
must converge to the same limit, we in fact have convergence of
\frac{u(x,t)}{\max_{x\in K}u(x,t)}
as
t\rightarrow\infty
to
w(x)
.

∎

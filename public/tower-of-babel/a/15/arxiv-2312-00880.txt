Matter correlators through a wormhole in double-scaled SYK
Kazumi Okuyama

Abstract
We compute the two-point function of matter operators in the double-scaled SYK (DSSYK) model, where the two matter operators are inserted at each end of the cylindrical wormhole. We find that the wormhole amplitude in DSSYK is written as a trace over the chord Hilbert space. We also show that the length of the wormhole is stabilized in the semi-classical limit, by the same mechanism worked for the JT gravity case.

However, in the AdS/CFT correspondence, the existence
of a wormhole connecting disjoint boundaries of bulk spacetime
leads to a puzzle, known as the factorization problem

Maldacena:2004rf
.
At least in lower dimensional examples of quantum gravity,
such as the JT gravity and the SYK model,
this factorization problem is naturally solved by assuming that
the boundary theory involves an ensemble average over the random
Hamiltonians.
Indeed, as shown in
Saad:2019lba
the JT gravity is holographycally
dual to a random matrix model,
where the random matrix plays the role of random Hamiltonians.

According to the classification in

deBoer:2023vsm
, there are three types of averaging in the boundary theory:
(i) averaging over Hamiltonians, (ii) averaging over operators, and (iii)
averaging over states.
In this paper, we consider the wormhole amplitude in the double-scaled SYK
(DSSYK) model

Cotler:2016fpe
;
Berkooz:2018jqr
.
In this case, the wormhole
originates from the hybrid of averagings (i) and (ii) above.
We compute the two-point function of matter operators
in DSSYK, where the two matter operators
are inserted at each end of the cylindrical wormhole.
Our computation is a natural generalization of the similar
computation for JT gravity in
Stanford:2020wkf
.
We find that the wormhole amplitude in DSSYK is written as a trace over the
chord Hilbert space, which was interpreted in
Lin:2022rbf

as a Hilbert space of the bulk gravity theory.
Moreover, we find that the length of the wormhole is stabilized in
the semi-classical limit, by the same mechanism worked for
the JT gravity case
Stanford:2020wkf
.

known as the ETH matrix model.
In section

on the wormhole geometry.
In section
4

confirm that the length of the wormhole is stabilized at a non-zero length.
Finally we conclude in section
5

In appendix

the JT gravity limit of the wormhole amplitude.

2
Review of DSSYK

In this section we briefly review the result of DSSYK in
Berkooz:2018jqr

and the ETH matrix model for DSSYK in
Jafferis:2022wez
.

The SYK model is defined by the Hamiltonian for

M
Majorana fermions
\psi_{i}~(i=1,\cdots,M)

obeying
\{\psi_{i},\psi_{j}\}=2\delta_{i,j}

with all-to-all
p
-body interaction

\displaystyle H=\mathrm{i}^{p/2}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{p}\leq M}J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}}\psi_{i_{1}}\cdots\psi_{i_{p}},

(1)

where
J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}}
is a random coupling drawn from the Gaussian distribution.
DSSYK is defined by the scaling limit

\displaystyle M,p\to\infty\quad\text{with}\quad\lambda=\frac{2p^{2}}{M}:\text{fixed}.

(2)

As shown in
Berkooz:2018jqr
, the ensemble average of the moment
\Tr H^{k}

reduces to a counting problem
of the intersection number of chord diagrams

\displaystyle\langle\Tr H^{k}\rangle_{J}=\sum_{\text{chord diagrams}}q^{\#(\text{intersections})}

(3)

with
q=e^{-\lambda}
.
This counting problem is solved by introducing the transfer matrix
T

\displaystyle T=A_{+}+A_{-},

(4)

where
A_{\pm}
denotes the

q
-deformed oscillator acting on the chord number state
|n\rangle

\displaystyle A_{+}|n\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{1-q^{n+1}}{1-q}}|n+1\rangle,\qquad A_{-}|n\rangle=\sqrt{\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}}|n-1\rangle.

(5)

Then the disk amplitude of DSSYK is given by

\displaystyle\langle\Tr e^{-\beta H}\rangle_{J}=\langle 0|e^{\beta T}|0\rangle.

(6)

The transfer matrix
T
becomes diagonal in the
\theta
-basis

2
2

2

Our definition of
E(\theta)

in (
7
) differs from that in
Berkooz:2018jqr

by the overall minus sign. Note that the sign of
E(\theta)
can
be flipped by
sending
\theta\to\pi-\theta
. Our definition (
7
) is convenient to study
the low energy regime since the low energy limit
corresponds to
\theta\to 0
,
not
\theta\to\pi
.

\displaystyle T|\theta\rangle=-E(\theta)|\theta\rangle,\qquad E(\theta)=-\frac{2\cos\theta}{\sqrt{1-q}},

(7)

and the overlap of
\langle n|
and
|\theta\rangle
is given by the
q
-Hermite polynomial

H_{n}(\cos\theta|q)

\displaystyle\langle n|\theta\rangle=\frac{H_{n}(\cos\theta|q)}{\sqrt{(q;q)_{n}}},

(8)

where the
q
-Pochhammer symbol is defined by

\displaystyle(a;q)_{n}=\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-aq^{k}),\qquad(a_{1},\cdots,a_{s};q)_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(a_{i};q)_{n}.

(9)

|\theta\rangle
and
|n\rangle
are normalized as

\displaystyle\langle\theta|\theta^{\prime}\rangle=\frac{2\pi}{\mu(\theta)}\delta(\theta-\theta^{\prime}),\quad\langle n|m\rangle=\delta_{n,m},\quad 1=\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\mu(\theta)|\theta\rangle\langle\theta|=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|n\rangle\langle n|,

(10)

and the measure factor
\mu(\theta)
is given by

\displaystyle\mu(\theta)=(q,e^{\pm 2\mathrm{i}\theta};q)_{\infty}.

(11)

Then the disk partition function (
6
) is written as

\displaystyle\langle 0|e^{\beta T}|0\rangle=\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\mu(\theta)e^{-\beta E(\theta)}.

(12)

As discussed in
Berkooz:2018jqr
, we can also consider
the matter operator
\mathcal{O}

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}=\mathrm{i}^{s/2}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{s}\leq M}K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}\psi_{i_{1}}\cdots\psi_{i_{s}}

(13)

with Gaussian random coefficients
K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}

which is drawn independently from the random coupling

J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}}
in the SYK Hamiltonian.
In the double scaling limit (
2
),
the effect of this operator can be made finite by taking the limit

s\to\infty
with
\Delta=s/p
held fixed.
Then the correlator of
\mathcal{O}
’s is also written as a
counting problem of the chord diagrams

\displaystyle\sum_{\text{chord diagrams}}q^{\#(H\text{-}H\,\text{intersections})}q^{\Delta\#(H\text{-}\mathcal{O}\,\text{intersections})}q^{\Delta^{2}\#(\mathcal{O}\text{-}\mathcal{O}\,\text{intersections})}.

(14)

Note that there appear two types of chords in this computation:
H
-chords and

\mathcal{O}
-chords coming from the Wick contraction of random couplings

J_{i_{1}\cdots i_{p}}
and
K_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}
, respectively.
The
\mathcal{O}
-chord is also called the matter chord.
For instance, the disk two-point function of
\mathcal{O}
is given by

\displaystyle\langle\Tr(e^{-\beta_{1}H}\mathcal{O}e^{-\beta_{2}H}\mathcal{O})\rangle_{J,K}=\langle 0|e^{\beta_{1}T}q^{\Delta\widehat{N}}e^{\beta_{2}T}|0\rangle,

(15)

where
\widehat{N}
is the number operator

\displaystyle\widehat{N}|n\rangle=n|n\rangle.

(16)

In terms of the
\theta
-basis, the disk two-point function
in (
15
)
is written as

\displaystyle\langle 0|e^{\beta_{1}T}q^{\Delta\widehat{N}}e^{\beta_{2}T}|0\rangle=\int_{0}^{\pi}\prod_{i=1,2}\frac{d\theta_{i}}{2\pi}\mu(\theta_{i})e^{-\beta_{i}E(\theta_{i})}G(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})

(17)

with

\displaystyle G(\theta_{1},\theta_{2})=\langle\theta_{1}|q^{\Delta\widehat{N}}|\theta_{2}\rangle=\frac{(q^{2\Delta};q)_{\infty}}{(q^{\Delta}e^{\mathrm{i}(\pm\theta_{1}\pm\theta_{2})};q)_{\infty}}.

(18)

In the rest of this paper, we will ignore
the intersections between the matter chords
for simplicity.
This amounts to consider a
bulk free field which is holographically dual to the matter operator
\mathcal{O}
.

Next, let us consider the ETH matrix model for DSSYK introduced in
Jafferis:2022wez
. As discussed in
Jafferis:2022wez
,
we can define the two-matrix model

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}=\int dAdBe^{-N\Tr V(A,B)}

(19)

which reproduces the disk partition function (
6
)
and the two-point function (
15
) of DSSYK in the large
N
limit.
The
N\times N
matrices
A
and
B
correspond to
H
and
\mathcal{O}
, respectively,
and the size
N
of the matrices is given by the dimension of
the Fock space of
M
Majorana fermions

\displaystyle N=2^{\frac{M}{2}}.

(20)

The potential
V(A,B)
in (
19
)
is given by
Jafferis:2022wez

\displaystyle\Tr V(A,B)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{N}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n-1}\left(1-\frac{q^{2n\Delta}}{1-q^{2n}}\right)\frac{q^{\frac{1}{2}n^{2}}}{n}(q^{\frac{1}{2}n}+q^{-\frac{1}{2}n})T_{2n}\left(\frac{\sqrt{1-q}}{2}E_{a}\right)

(21)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{a,b=1}^{N}B_{ab}B_{ba}G(\theta_{a},\theta_{b})^{-1},

where
T_{n}(\cos\theta)=\cos(n\theta)
denotes the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind
and
E_{a}
is the eigenvalue of the matrix
A
given by
E(\theta_{a})
in (
7
).
The first line of (
21
)
is determined by requiring that the eigenvalue density of the matrix
A

reproduces the measure factor
\mu(\theta)
in (
11
) in the large
N

limit.
The second line of (
21
) is constructed in such a way that
the two-point function of the matrix
B

reproduces the disk two-point function of
\mathcal{O}
in (
17
).

From (
21
), we can read off the propagator of the matrix
B
as

\displaystyle\langle B_{ab}B_{cd}\rangle=\frac{1}{N}G(\theta_{a},\theta_{b})\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc},

(22)

where the expectation value is taken in the two-matrix model

\displaystyle\langle\cdots\rangle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{Z}}\int dAdB(\cdots)e^{-N\Tr V(A,B)}.

(23)

We can check that the disk two-point function of
\mathcal{O}
in (
17
)
is reproduced from the two-matrix model

\displaystyle\big\langle\Tr(e^{-\beta_{1}A}Be^{-\beta_{2}A}B)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b=1}^{N}\langle e^{-\beta_{1}E_{a}}B_{ab}e^{-\beta_{2}E_{b}}B_{ba}\rangle

(24)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b=1}^{N}e^{-\beta_{1}E_{a}-\beta_{2}E_{b}}\frac{1}{N}G(\theta_{a},\theta_{b})

\displaystyle=N\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta_{1}}{2\pi}\mu(\theta_{1})\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta_{2}}{2\pi}\mu(\theta_{2})e^{-\beta_{1}E(\theta_{1})-\beta_{2}E(\theta_{2})}G(\theta_{1},\theta_{2}).

Here we have used the relation

\displaystyle\sum_{a=1}^{N}f(E_{a})=N\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\mu(\theta)f(E(\theta))

(25)

which is valid in the large
N
limit.
In the next section, we compute the matter correlator of DSSYK on the wormhole geometry using the propagator in (
22
).

3
Matter correlators through a wormhole

Let us consider the matter correlator of DSSYK on the wormhole geometry.
In the two-matrix model description of DSSYK, it is given by

\big\langle\Tr(e^{-\beta_{L}A}B)\Tr(e^{-\beta_{R}A}B)\big\rangle
.
Using the propagator of
B
in (
22
) we find

\displaystyle\big\langle\Tr(e^{-\beta_{L}A}B)\Tr(e^{-\beta_{R}A}B)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b=1}^{N}\langle e^{-\beta_{L}E_{a}}B_{aa}e^{-\beta_{R}E_{b}}B_{bb}\rangle

(26)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a=1}^{N}e^{-(\beta_{L}+\beta_{R})E_{a}}\frac{1}{N}G(\theta_{a},\theta_{a})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\mu(\theta)e^{-(\beta_{L}+\beta_{R})E(\theta)}G(\theta,\theta).

Note that in this computation only the
a=b
terms survive,
which is reminiscent of the Berry’s “diagonal approximation”
for the spectral form factor
berry1985semiclassical
.
Note also that the power of
N
in (
26
) is down by one
compared to the disk two-point function
in (
24
).
Using (
10
) and (
18
), we can easily
show that (
26
) is written as

\displaystyle\big\langle\Tr(e^{-\beta_{L}A}B)\Tr(e^{-\beta_{R}A}B)\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\Tr_{\mathcal{H}}\bigl[e^{(\beta_{L}+\beta_{R})T}q^{\Delta\widehat{N}}\bigr],

(27)

where
\Tr_{\mathcal{H}}
denotes the trace over the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}

spanned by the chord number states
\{|n\rangle\}_{n=0,1,2\cdots}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathbb{C}|n\rangle.

(28)

More explicitly, the trace in (
27
) is written as

\displaystyle\Tr_{\mathcal{H}}\bigl[e^{(\beta_{L}+\beta_{R})T}q^{\Delta\widehat{N}}\bigr]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{\Delta n}\langle n|e^{(\beta_{L}+\beta_{R})T}|n\rangle.

(29)

4
Stabilization of the wormhole length

In general, the wormhole geometry with a fixed length
is not a classical solution of the bulk gravity theory.
However, as discussed in
Stanford:2020wkf

for the JT gravity case, the length of the wormhole
is stabilized by including the effect of matter correlator.
This is similar in spirit to the
construction of wormhole solutions as
gravitational constrained instantons

Cotler:2020lxj
;
Cotler:2021cqa
.
Below we will see that the length
n
of the wormhole
in (
29
) is stabilized in the semi-classical
saddle-point approximation.

The wormhole amplitude in (
29
)
is written as

\displaystyle\Tr_{\mathcal{H}}\bigl(e^{\beta T}q^{\Delta\widehat{N}}\bigr)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{\Delta n}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\mu(\theta)|\langle n|\theta\rangle|^{2}e^{-\beta E(\theta)},

(36)

where
\beta
is defined in (

).
Let us consider the saddle-point approximation of the
\theta
-integral
in the semi-classical limit
\lambda\to 0
.
Using the relation

\displaystyle(a;q)_{\infty}=\exp\left(-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a^{n}}{n(1-q^{n})}\right)\approx e^{-\frac{1}{\lambda}\text{Li}_{2}(a)},

(37)

the measure factor
\mu(\theta)
is approximated as

\displaystyle\mu(\theta)

\displaystyle=(q,e^{\pm 2\mathrm{i}\theta};q)_{\infty}\approx\exp\left[-\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{\varepsilon=\pm 1}\text{Li}_{2}(e^{2\varepsilon\mathrm{i}\theta})\right].

(38)

Here
\text{Li}_{2}(z)

denotes the dilogarithm function.
Also, using the relation

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t^{n}|\langle n|\theta\rangle|^{2}=\frac{(t^{2};q)_{\infty}}{(te^{\pm 2\mathrm{i}\theta};q)_{\infty}(t;q)_{\infty}^{2}},

(39)

the factor
|\langle n|\theta\rangle|^{2}
in
(
36
)
is written as

\displaystyle|\langle n|\theta\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}e^{-\mathrm{i}n\phi}\frac{(e^{2\mathrm{i}\phi};q)_{\infty}}{(e^{\mathrm{i}\phi\pm 2\mathrm{i}\theta};q)_{\infty}(e^{\mathrm{i}\phi};q)_{\infty}^{2}}

(40)

\displaystyle\approx\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}e^{-\mathrm{i}n\phi}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{\lambda}\text{Li}_{2}(e^{2\mathrm{i}\phi})+\frac{1}{\lambda}\sum_{\varepsilon=\pm 1}\text{Li}_{2}(e^{\mathrm{i}\phi+2\varepsilon\mathrm{i}\theta})+\frac{2}{\lambda}\text{Li}_{2}(e^{\mathrm{i}\phi})\right].

If we further assume that

\displaystyle n\sim\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-1}),\quad\beta\sim\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{0}),

(41)

the integral in (
36
) is written as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\mu(\theta)|\langle n|\theta\rangle|^{2}e^{-\beta E(\theta)}\approx\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{d\phi}{2\pi}e^{-\frac{1}{\lambda}F(\theta,\phi)-\beta E(\theta)},

(42)

where

\displaystyle F(\theta,\phi)=\mathrm{i}\lambda n\phi+\text{Li}_{2}(e^{2\mathrm{i}\phi})-2\text{Li}_{2}(e^{\mathrm{i}\phi})+\sum_{\varepsilon=\pm 1}\Bigl[\text{Li}_{2}(e^{2\varepsilon\mathrm{i}\theta})-\text{Li}_{2}(e^{\mathrm{i}\phi+2\varepsilon\mathrm{i}\theta})\Bigr].

(43)

We can easily show that the solution of the saddle-point equation

\displaystyle\partial_{\theta}F(\theta,\phi)\Big|_{\theta=\theta_{*},\phi=\phi_{*}}=\partial_{\phi}F(\theta,\phi)\Big|_{\theta=\theta_{*},\phi=\phi_{*}}=0

(44)

is given by

\displaystyle\phi_{*}=0,\quad\sin^{2}\theta_{*}=q^{n}.

(45)

Then
E(\theta)
in (
36
)
is approximated as

\displaystyle E(\theta_{*})=-\frac{2\cos\theta_{*}}{\sqrt{1-q}}=-2\sqrt{\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}}.

(46)

Alternatively, the same approximation can be obtained from the recursion relation
obeyed by
\psi_{n}(\theta)=\langle n|\theta\rangle

\displaystyle 2\cos\theta\psi_{n}(\theta)=\sqrt{1-q^{n}}\psi_{n-1}(\theta)+\sqrt{1-q^{n+1}}\psi_{n+1}(\theta).

(47)

From this relation,
\cos\theta
is approximated as

\displaystyle\cos\theta\approx\frac{\sqrt{1-q^{n}}+\sqrt{1-q^{n+1}}}{2}\approx\sqrt{1-q^{n}},

(48)

which agrees with the result of saddle-point approximation
(
45
).
We should stress that our saddle-point analysis is different from

Goel:2018ubv
.
In
Goel:2018ubv
, the
\beta E(\theta)
term is assumed to be of order

\mathcal{O}(\lambda^{-1})
and hence the saddle-point value of
\theta
depends on
\beta
.
In our case we assumed the scaling in (
41
),
which implies that
\beta E(\theta)
is sub-leading compared to the leading
term
\lambda^{-1}F(\theta,\phi)

in (
42
).
Thus the
\beta E(\theta)
term does not enter into the saddle-point equation
(
44
).

Finally, we arrive at the saddle-point approximation
of the wormhole amplitude

\displaystyle\Tr_{\mathcal{H}}\bigl(e^{\beta T}q^{\Delta\widehat{N}}\bigr)\approx\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}q^{\Delta n}e^{-\beta E(\theta_{*})}\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}e^{-S_{\text{eff}}(n)},

(49)

where

\displaystyle S_{\text{eff}}(n)

\displaystyle=\Delta\lambda n-2\beta\sqrt{\frac{1-e^{-\lambda n}}{1-e^{-\lambda}}}.

(50)

In Figure

, we show the plot of

in (

).
We can see that

has a minimum at a non-zero value of
\lambda n

and hence the wormhole length
n
is stabilized
by the effect of matter correlator.
This stabilization mechanism of the wormhole length for DSSYK is
essentially the same as that for the JT gravity discussed in
Stanford:2020wkf

(see appendix

for the JT gravity limit of the wormhole amplitude

)).

\displaystyle\tilde{\beta}=\frac{2\beta}{\sqrt{1-q}}.

the

Thus, DSSYK can be thought of as a UV completion of the JT gravity.
As discussed in

the UV finiteness of DSSYK is closely related to the existence of
a minimal length in the bulk geometry.
Note that the wormhole solution with a finite length ceases to exist
in the thin cylinder limit
\beta\to 0

since the saddle-point solution in (

)
collapses to a zero-length wormhole
n_{*}=0
.
In other words,
the semiclassical approximation breaks down in this limit. Instead,
we have to sum over all
n
as shown in (
54
).

5
Conclusion and outlook

In this paper we have studied the matter two-point function through
the wormhole geometry in DSSYK. We find that this two-point function
(
27
) is
written as a trace on the Hilbert space spanned by the chord number states,
which can be identified as the Hilbert space of the bulk gravity theory

Lin:2022rbf
. In the saddle-point approximation,
the sum over
n
in (
29
)
is dominated by a non-zero value of
n

due to the balance between the matter contribution
q^{\Delta n}

and the Boltzmann factor
e^{-\beta E(\theta_{*})}
in (
49
).
This stabilization mechanism of the wormhole length is the same as that appeared in
the computation of the wormhole amplitude in JT gravity
Stanford:2020wkf
.
This is similar in spirit to the gravitational constrained instantons in

Cotler:2020lxj
;
Cotler:2021cqa
.
One important difference from
Stanford:2020wkf
is that
the wormhole amplitude (
27
)
in DSSYK is finite even in the thin cylinder limit (
54
).

There are many interesting open questions.
In this paper, we have ignored the intersections of matter chords,
which amounts to consider the free field in the bulk theory.
It would be interesting to incorporate the matter interaction and see how the result in this paper is modified.
It would also be
interesting to generalize our computation to higher genus topologies
along the lines of
Okuyama:2023kdo
;
Okuyama:2023aup
.
The higher dimensional generalization
of our computation would be also interesting (see e.g.
Cotler:2020hgz

for a wormhole in the AdS
3
/CFT
2
correspondence).

We have seen that the stabilization mechanism of the wormhole length advocated in

Stanford:2020wkf
works for DSSYK as well. However, we should stress
that the existence of a semi-classical saddle in the gravitational path integral
is not a necessary condition for the definition of quantum gravity.
In our case, the wormhole length
n
is a dynamical variable and the wormhole
amplitude (
29
) is defined by summing over all possible
n
.
The wormhole
amplitude (
29
) itself is well-defined without
requiring the existence of a semi-classical saddle.
In fact, the wormhole
amplitude of DSSYK is finite
in the thin cylinder limit (
54
),
which is far from the semi-classical regime.
By definition, quantum gravity should make sense in the quantum regime, which
is indeed the case in our computation of
the wormhole amplitude in DSSYK.

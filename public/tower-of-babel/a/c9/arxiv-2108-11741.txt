The singularities for a periodic transport equation
Yong Zhang, Fei Xu, Fengquan Li

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider a 1D periodic transport equation with nonlocal flux and fractional dissipation u t − ( H ​ u ) x ​ u x + κ ​ Λ α ​ u = 0 , ( t , x ) ∈ R + × S , u_{t}-(Hu)_{x}u_{x}+\kappa\Lambda^{\alpha}u=0,\quad(t,x)\in R^{+}\times S, where κ ≥ 0 \kappa\geq 0 , 0 < α ≤ 1 0<\alpha\leq 1 and S = [ − π , π ] S=[-\pi,\pi] . 1.
Introduction

We will discuss the following equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}-(H^{a}\partial_{x}^{b}u)u_{x}+\kappa\Lambda^{\alpha}u=0,~~~(t,x)\in R^{+}\times S,\\
u(0,x)=\varphi(x),\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
a,b\in\{0,1\}
,
Hu(x)
is the Hilbert transform of
u(x)
defined by (see
[
13
]
)

Hu(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}P.V.\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}\frac{u(x-y)}{tan\frac{y}{2}}dy

and
H^{0}
denotes the identity operator.

\Lambda^{\alpha}u=(-\partial_{xx})^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u
is defined by the Fourier transform

\widehat{\Lambda^{\alpha}u}(\xi)=|\xi|^{\alpha}\hat{u}(\xi).

Here
\kappa\geq 0
is viscosity coefficient which controls the strength of dissipation and
0<\alpha\leq 2
controls the magnitude of the dissipation.

If
a=b=0
, (
1.1
) is transformed into the classical Brugers equation with fractional dissipation. This equation has been investigated in
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. The authors in
[
1
,
2
]
proved the occurrence of shock-type singularities in supercritical case with responding to
0<\alpha<1
and the global existence in critical case corresponding with
\alpha=1
and subcritical case corresponding with
1<\alpha\leq 2
. In
[
3
]
, the authors studied comprehensively the existence, uniqueness, blow up and regularity properties of solutions.

If
a=1,b=0
, (
1.1
) can be written a famous nonlocal transport equation, which was firstly considered by A.Córdoba et. in
[
4
]
, where the authors concluded that the solutions would develop into gradient blow-up as
\kappa=0
and exist globally as
\kappa>0,\alpha>1
. References
[
5
,
6
]
are recommended for some recent results on this transport equation.

If
a=0,b=1
, (
1.1
) can be reduced to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}-u^{2}_{x}+\kappa\Lambda^{\alpha}u=0,~~~~(t,x)\in R^{+}\times S,\\
u(0,x)=\varphi(x),\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

which can be regarded as a fractional order heat equation with a special nonlinearity term
u^{2}_{x}
. On the other hand, it also can be viewed as another form of Burgers equation. For example, taking derivative to (
1.2
) with respect to
x
and letting
v=u_{x}
, then we obtain that
v_{t}-2vv_{x}+\kappa\Lambda^{\alpha}v=0
. Moreover, we can deduce

\frac{d}{dt}\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}[\pi,\pi]}\leq 0,

which implies that
\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}[-\pi,\pi]}\leq\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}[-\pi,\pi]}\leq\|\varphi\|_{H^{1}[-\pi,\pi]}
. Hence the solution to (
1.2
) would not blow up.

In this paper, we mainly consider the case of
a=b=1
, that is to say

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}-(Hu)_{x}u_{x}+\kappa\Lambda^{\alpha}u=0,~~~~(t,x)\in R^{+}\times S,\\
u(0,x)=\varphi(x),\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

which is a simplified equation of a class of ill-posed problems arising in the theory of vortex sheets (see
[
12
,
14
]
). As before, if taking derivative to (
1.3
) with respect to
x
and letting
\theta=u_{x}
, we would get

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\theta_{t}-(\theta H\theta)_{x}+\kappa\Lambda^{\alpha}\theta=0,~~~~(t,x)\in R^{+}\times S,\\
\theta(0,x)=f(x),\end{array}\right.

where
f(x)=\varphi_{x}(x)
. This is a 1D model of the quasi-geostrophic equation (see
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
).
In
[
9
]
, the authors showed that there is no
C^{1}([-\pi,\pi]\times[0,+\infty))
solutions in the case of
\kappa=0
or
\kappa>0,\alpha=1
. In fact, our results in this paper are consistent with the conclusion in
[
9
]
. Besides, we also make up the gap of
\kappa>0,0<\alpha<1
by using a different method. For the non-periodic case, the global existence for strictly positive initial data
f(x)>0
has been proved in
[
10
]
. Other important results on this equation can be seen in
[
11
]
.

The paper is organized as follows. In section 2, we first give the local well-posedness result of equation (
1.3
), and then state the main theorems on blow-up. In section 3, we try to reduce the equation (
1.3
) to a family of ordinary differential equations under a class of given initial data. Section 4 is devoted to the proof of the main results on blow-up.

2.
Local well-posedness and main results

Before stating to state the main results, we first discuss the local well-posedness result of the problem (
1.3
). Combining a priori bound with the compactness argument, one may be able to establish local well-posedness for (
1.3
) in
H^{3}[-\pi,\pi]
. The details of proof are the same as that of
[
15
]
. Below we merely show how to obtain a priori bound for
\|u\|_{H^{3}[-\pi,\pi]}
, and other parts of the proof are omitted.

Theorem 2.1
.

If
\varphi\in H^{3}[-\pi,\pi]
, then there exists a
T>0
such that the problem (
1.3
) has a unique solution
u\in C([0,T),H^{3}[-\pi,\pi])\cap C^{1}([0,T),H^{2}[-\pi,\pi])
.

Proof.

Taking three derivatives to equation (
1.3
) and multiplying by
u_{xxx}
over
[\pi,\pi]
, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{xxx}\|_{L^{2}[\pi,\pi]}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}[(Hu)_{x}u_{x}]_{xxx}u_{xxx}dx-\kappa\|\Lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}u_{xxx}\|_{L^{2}[\pi,\pi]}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}[(\Lambda u_{x})u_{x}+(\Lambda u)u_{xx})]_{xx}u_{xxx}dx

\displaystyle\leq(\|\Lambda u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}[-\pi,\pi]}+\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{\infty}[-\pi,\pi]})\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}u^{2}_{xxx}+(\Lambda u_{xx})u_{xxx}dx.

(2.1)

Thanks to the domain is limited to
[-\pi,\pi]
, the Sobolev embedding theorem and Plancherel formula can be used to show that

\frac{d}{dt}\|u\|_{H^{3}[\pi,\pi]}^{2}\leq c\|u\|_{H^{3}[\pi,\pi]}^{3},

(2.2)

where
c
is a positive constant.
Furthermore, a priori bound

\|u\|_{H^{3}[\pi,\pi]}\leq\frac{\|\varphi\|_{H^{3}[\pi,\pi]}}{1-c\|\varphi\|_{H^{3}[\pi,\pi]}t}

(2.3)

follows.
∎

We are now in a position to state the blow-up results. It’s known that the smooth solution
u(t,x)
of (
1.3
) can be expanded by

u(t,x)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}u_{n}(t)e^{inx},~~u_{n}(t)\in\mathbb{C}.

(2.4)

Here we assume that the initial data
\varphi\in H^{3}[-\pi,\pi]
is odd with the form of

\varphi(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}sin(nx),

(2.5)

where
A_{n}
are constants. In the case
\kappa\geq 0
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
, we establish that

Theorem 2.2
.

(singularity for
\kappa=0
)
Assume there exists a positive number
\delta
such that
A_{n}\geq\frac{2\delta}{n^{5}}
, then the solution
u(t,x)
to equation (
1.3
) with
\kappa=0
would blow up at some finite time
T_{1}
, i.e.

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(-\pi,\pi]}\rightarrow\infty,~~~as~~~t\rightarrow T_{1}.

Moreover, there is a rough estimate

T_{1}=(\frac{1}{2\delta})^{+}.

Theorem 2.3
.

(singularity for
\kappa>0,~\alpha=1
)
Assume there exists a positive number
\delta
such that
A_{n}\geq\frac{2\delta}{n^{5}}
and
0<\kappa<\frac{2\delta}{2e-1}
, then the solution
u(t,x)
to equation (
1.3
) would blow up at
T_{2}=\frac{1}{\kappa}
, i.e.

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(-\pi,\pi]}\rightarrow\infty,~~~as~~~t\rightarrow T_{2}=\frac{1}{\kappa}.

Theorem 2.4
.

(singularity for
\kappa>0,~0<\alpha<1
)
Assume there exists a positive number
\delta
such that
A_{n}\geq\frac{2\delta}{n^{5}}
and
0<\kappa<\frac{2\delta}{2e-1}
, then the solution
u(t,x)
to equation (
1.3
) would blow up at
T_{2}=\frac{1}{\kappa}
, i.e.

\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(-\pi,\pi]}\rightarrow\infty,~~~as~~~t\rightarrow T_{2}=\frac{1}{\kappa}.

Remark 2.5
.

In Theorem
2.2
–
2.4
, the assumption on initial data
A_{n}\geq\frac{2\delta}{n^{5}}
is required to be compatible with the convergence of series (
2.5
) in
H^{3}[-\pi,\pi]
. (For instance, we can take
A_{n}=\frac{2\delta+1}{n^{5}}
.) In addition, the exponential 5 at denominator in
\frac{2\delta}{n^{5}}
can be replaced by any larger real number. Essentially, we need to restrict the Fourier coefficients
A_{n}
of initial data to be positive due to the technique taken here .

3.
The reduction of the equation (
1.3
)

Thanks to the reasonable assumptions of the initial data, the equation (
1.3
) can be reduced to a family of ordinary differential equations (ODEs) in this section. Moveover, we can write easily down its implicit solutions.

Firstly, let’s reduce the equation in (
1.3
). The Hilbert transform of periodic smooth solution
u(t,x)
in (
2.4
) can be expressed by

Hu=-\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}isgn(n)u_{n}(t)e^{inx}=-i\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{n}(t)e^{inx}+i\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}u_{-n}(t)e^{-inx},~u_{n}(t)\in\mathbb{C}.

(3.1)

Then (
2.4
) and (
3.1
) give

\displaystyle(Hu)_{x}u_{x}

\displaystyle=(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nu_{n}(t)e^{inx}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nu_{-n}(t)e^{-inx})(i\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nu_{n}(t)e^{inx}-i\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nu_{-n}(t)e^{-inx})

\displaystyle=i(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nu_{n}(t)e^{inx})^{2}-i(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nu_{-n}(t)e^{-inx})^{2}.

(3.2)

Let

a(t,x):=(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nu_{n}(t)e^{inx})^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(t)e^{inx},

(3.3)

where the coefficients
a_{n}(t)
can be defined by

\displaystyle a_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}a(t,x)e^{-inx}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}lu_{l}e^{ilx}ku_{k}e^{ikx})e^{-inx}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int^{\pi}_{-\pi}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}lu_{l}ku_{k}e^{i(l+k-n)x}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}lu_{l}ku_{k}\delta(k+l-n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}lu_{l}(n-l)u_{n-l}.

(3.4)

It follows from (
3.3
) and (
3
) that

a(t,x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}lu_{l}(n-l)u_{n-l}]e^{inx}.

(3.5)

A similar argument shows that

b(t,x):=(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nu_{-n}(t)e^{-inx})^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}lu_{-l}(n-l)u_{-n+l}]e^{-inx}.

(3.6)

Therefore, we can obtain

\displaystyle(Hu)_{x}u_{x}

\displaystyle=i(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nu_{n}(t)e^{inx})^{2}-i(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nu_{-n}(t)e^{-inx})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[i\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}lu_{l}(n-l)u_{n-l}]e^{inx}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[i\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}lu_{-l}(n-l)u_{-n+l}]e^{-inx}.

(3.7)

Besides, the diffusion term
\Lambda^{\alpha}u
also can be expressed by

\Lambda^{\alpha}u(t,x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{\alpha}u_{n}(t)e^{inx}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{\alpha}u_{-n}(t)e^{-inx}.

(3.8)

Taking (
2.4
), (
3
) and (
3.8
) into (
1.3
) and equating coefficients of
e^{inx}
and
e^{-inx}
respectly, we can obtain the following ODEs for
\{u_{n}(t)\}_{n\geq 1}
,
\{u_{-n}(t)\}_{n\geq 1}
and
u_{0}(t)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{du_{n}(t)}{dt}-i\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}lu_{l}(t)(n-l)u_{n-l}(t)+\kappa n^{\alpha}u_{n}(t)=0,\\
\frac{du_{0}(t)}{dt}=0,\\
\frac{du_{-n}(t)}{dt}+i\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}lu_{-l}(t)(n-l)u_{-n+l}(t)+\kappa n^{\alpha}u_{-n}(t)=0.\end{array}\right.

(3.9)

Secondly let’s reduce the initial data in (
1.3
). It’s easy to see that

\displaystyle\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=u(0,x)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}u_{n}(0)e^{inx}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}[iu_{n}(0)sin(nx)+u_{n}(0)cos(nx)]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[i(u_{n}(0)-u_{-n}(0))sin(nx)]+u_{0}(0)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}[(u_{n}(0)+u_{-n}(0))cos(nx)].

(3.10)

Comparing the coefficients of the initial data
\varphi(x)
in (
2.5
) with ones in (
3
), we find

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}A_{n}=i(u_{n}(0)-u_{-n}(0)),\\
u_{0}(0)=0,\\
u_{-n}(0)=-u_{n}(0),\end{array}\right.

(3.11)

which implies

u_{n}(0)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,~~~~n=0,\\
\frac{A_{n}}{2i},~~~n\geq 1.\end{array}\right.

(3.12)

Considering the ODEs (
3.9
) with initial condition (
3.12
), we can get that

u_{0}(t)=0
. It’s worth noting that
\{-u_{n}(t)\}
will solve the third equation in (
3.9
) if
\{u_{n}(t)\}
solve the first equation in (
3.9
).
Hence the classical local well-posedness theorem of ODEs would ensure that

u_{-n}(t)=-u_{n}(t).

(3.13)

Then the fact
u_{0}(t)=0
and (
3.13
) allow us to write the solution
u(t,x)
by

u(t,x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2iu_{n}(t)sin(nx).

(3.14)

On the other hand,
\{u_{n}(t)\}
are coefficients of Fourier series (
2.4
), which yields

u_{-n}(t)=\overline{u_{n}(t)}.

(3.15)

(
3.13
) and (
3.15
) imply that
\{u_{n}(t)\}
are pure imaginary numbers with respect to
x
. For convenience, in the following we will take the real-valued functions
\{iu_{n}(t)\}
as a whole, where
i
is the imaginary unit. To sum up, if let

w_{n}(t)=iu_{n}(t),

the ODEs (
3.9
) with initial condition (
3.12
) would be reduced to the following equations only for
\{w_{n}(t)\}_{n\geq 1}

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{dw_{n}(t)}{dt}-\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}lw_{l}(t)(n-l)w_{n-l}(t)+\kappa n^{\alpha}w_{n}(t)=0,\\
w_{n}(0)=\frac{A_{n}}{2}.\end{array}\right.

(3.16)

The solutions to (
3.16
) can be written as

w_{n}(t)=\frac{A_{n}}{2}e^{-n^{\alpha}\kappa t}+e^{-n^{\alpha}\kappa t}\int^{t}_{0}e^{n^{\alpha}\kappa s}\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}lw_{l}(s)(n-l)w_{n-l}(s)ds,~~~n\geq 1.

(3.17)

4.
The proof of the main results

In this section, we mainly give the proofs of blow-up results. Relying on the formal symmetry of solutions in (
3.17
), we will finish the proofs by the mathematical induction method.

4.1.
The proof of Theorem
2.2

Proof.

As
\kappa=0
, (
3.17
) gives

nw_{n}(t)=\frac{nA_{n}}{2}+n\int^{t}_{0}\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}lw_{l}(s)(n-l)w_{n-l}(s)ds\geq 0,~~for~~n\geq 1.

(4.1)

In addition, we claim that
\{nw_{n}(t)\}_{n\geq 1}
satisfy

nw_{n}(t)\geq\frac{\delta}{n^{4}}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta t)^{n-1}.

(4.2)

Now we use the mathematical induction to prove this claim.

If
n=1
, then

w_{1}(t)=\frac{A_{1}}{2}\geq\delta.

As
1\leq l\leq n-1
, we assume that

lw_{l}(t)\geq\frac{\delta}{l^{4}}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta t)^{l-1}

(4.3)

holds. (
4.1
) and (
4.3
) imply that

\displaystyle nw_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{nA_{n}}{2}+n\int^{t}_{0}\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}lw_{l}(s)(n-l)w_{n-l}(s)ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{nA_{n}}{2}+\delta^{2}n\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{l^{4}(n-l)^{4}}\int^{t}_{0}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta s)^{n-2}ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta}{n^{4}}+\delta^{2}\frac{n-1}{n^{4}}\int^{t}_{0}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta s)^{n-2}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{n^{4}}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta t)^{n-1}+\frac{\delta}{n^{4}}(1-\frac{1}{2^{n-1}})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta}{n^{4}}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta t)^{n-1},

and (
4.2
) follows.

(
3.14
), (
4.2
) and Paserval equality show that

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(-\pi,\pi]}

\displaystyle=2\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|2w_{n}(t)|^{2}=8\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\frac{1}{n}nw_{n}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 8\pi\delta^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{[(\frac{1}{2}+\delta t)^{2}]^{n-1}}{n^{10}}.

(4.4)

Let
g(t):=\frac{1}{2}+\delta t
be bigger than 1, then the series in (
4.1
) would diverge by the basic fact of calculus. Thus, we only need to choose
T_{1}
is bigger than
\frac{1}{2\delta}
and the proof is completed.
∎

4.2.
The proof of Theorem
2.3

Proof.

In this case, we also have from (
3.17
)

nw_{n}(t)=\frac{nA_{n}}{2}e^{-n\kappa t}+ne^{-n\kappa t}\int^{t}_{0}e^{n\kappa s}\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}lw_{l}(s)(n-l)w_{n-l}(s)ds,~~~n\geq 1.

(4.5)

Similarly we can infer that the following estimate holds

nw_{n}(t)\geq\frac{\delta}{n^{4}}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta t)^{n-1}e^{-n\kappa t}.

(4.6)

Here the mathematical induction method is used again.

As
n=1
, we have

w_{1}(t)=\frac{A_{1}}{2}e^{-\kappa t}\geq\delta e^{-\kappa t}.

For
1\leq l\leq n-1
, we assume that

lw_{l}(t)\geq\frac{\delta}{l^{4}}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta t)^{l-1}e^{-l\kappa t}.

(4.7)

Hence we can obtain that from (
4.5
) and (
4.7
)

\displaystyle nw_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{nA_{n}}{2}e^{-n\kappa t}+ne^{-n\kappa t}\int^{t}_{0}e^{n\kappa s}\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}lw_{l}(s)(n-l)w_{n-l}(s)ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{nA_{n}}{2}e^{-n\kappa t}+\delta^{2}n\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{l^{4}(n-l)^{4}}e^{-n\kappa t}\int^{t}_{0}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta s)^{n-2}ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta}{n^{4}}e^{-n\kappa t}+\delta^{2}\frac{n-1}{n^{4}}e^{-n\kappa t}\int^{t}_{0}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta s)^{n-2}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{n^{4}}e^{-n\kappa t}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta t)^{n-1}+\frac{\delta}{n^{4}}e^{-n\kappa t}(1-\frac{1}{2^{n-1}})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta}{n^{4}}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta t)^{n-1}e^{-n\kappa t},

and (
4.6
) follows. On the other hand, (
3.14
), (
4.6
) and Paserval equality imply that

\displaystyle\|u(t,\cdot)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(-\pi,\pi]}

\displaystyle=2\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|2w_{n}(t)|^{2}=8\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\frac{1}{n}nw_{n}(t)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 8\pi\delta^{2}e^{-2\kappa t}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{([(\frac{1}{2}+\delta t)e^{-\kappa t}]^{2})^{n-1}}{n^{10}}

(4.8)

Similarly let
g(t)=(\frac{1}{2}+\delta t)e^{-\kappa t}>1
, then the series in (
4.2
) fails to converge. Since
0<\kappa<\frac{2\delta}{2e-1}
, we only need to choose
T_{2}=\frac{1}{\kappa}
to deduce the occurrence of singularity.
∎

4.3.
The proof of Theorem
2.4

Proof.

Similar to the proof of Theorem
2.3
, it’s sufficient for us to estimate

nw_{n}(t)\geq\frac{\delta}{n^{4}}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta t)^{n-1}e^{-n\kappa t}.

(4.9)

In the case of
n=1
, it’s easy to see

w_{1}(t)=\frac{A_{1}}{2}e^{-\kappa t}\geq\delta e^{-\kappa t}.

Then we assume that (
4.9
) holds for
l=1,2,...,n-1
,

lw_{l}(t)\geq\frac{\delta}{l^{4}}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta t)^{l-1}e^{-l\kappa t}.

(4.10)

(
3.17
) and (
4.10
) yield that

\displaystyle nw_{n}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{nA_{n}}{2}e^{-n^{\alpha}\kappa t}+ne^{-n^{\alpha}\kappa t}\int^{t}_{0}e^{n^{\alpha}\kappa s}\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}lw_{l}(s)(n-l)w_{n-l}(s)ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{nA_{n}}{2}e^{-n^{\alpha}\kappa t}+\delta^{2}n\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{l^{4}(n-l)^{4}}e^{-n^{\alpha}\kappa t}\int^{t}_{0}e^{(n^{\alpha}-n)\kappa s}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta s)^{n-2}ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta}{n^{4}}e^{-n\kappa t}+\delta^{2}\frac{n-1}{n^{4}}e^{-n^{\alpha}\kappa t}\int^{t}_{0}e^{(n^{\alpha}-n)\kappa s}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta s)^{n-2}ds

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{n^{4}}e^{-n\kappa t}+e^{-n\kappa t}\delta^{2}\frac{n-1}{n^{4}}e^{(n-n^{\alpha})\kappa t}\int^{t}_{0}e^{(n^{\alpha}-n)\kappa s}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta s)^{n-2}ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta}{n^{4}}e^{-n\kappa t}+e^{-n\kappa t}\delta^{2}\frac{n-1}{n^{4}}\int^{t}_{0}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta s)^{n-2}ds

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta}{n^{4}}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta t)^{n-1}e^{-n\kappa t},

(4.11)

where the second inequality in (
4.3
) uses
0<\alpha<1
and the third inequality in (
4.3
) follows from the fact

e^{(n-n^{\alpha})\kappa t}\int^{t}_{0}e^{(n^{\alpha}-n)\kappa s}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta s)^{n-2}ds\geq\int^{t}_{0}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta s)^{n-2}ds,~~for~t\geq 0,~0<\alpha<1.

Define

h(t)=e^{(n-n^{\alpha})\kappa t}\int^{t}_{0}e^{(n^{\alpha}-n)\kappa s}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta s)^{n-2}ds-\int^{t}_{0}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta s)^{n-2}ds.

It’s obvious that
h(0)=0
, and a simple computation shows that

h^{\prime}(t)=(n-n^{\alpha})\kappa e^{(n-n^{\alpha})\kappa t}\int^{t}_{0}e^{(n^{\alpha}-n)\kappa s}(\frac{1}{2}+\delta s)^{n-2}ds>0,~~for~t\geq 0,~0<\alpha<1,

then
h(t)\geq 0,~~for~t\geq 0,~0<\alpha<1
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

The technology in our proof mainly relies on the formal symmetry of ODEs in (
3.16
). In fact, if this symmetry is lost, we find that the approach also can be used to deduce the singularity. For example, let’s consider following ODEs

\frac{dw_{n}(t)}{dt}-\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}lw_{l}(t)w_{n-l}(t)+\kappa n^{\alpha}w_{n}(t)=0.

(4.12)

Based on the fact

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}lw_{l}(t)w_{n-l}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\sqrt{l}w_{l}(t)\sqrt{l}w_{n-l}(t)

\displaystyle=w_{1}w_{n-1}+2w_{2}w_{n-2}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+(n-2)w_{n-2}w_{2}+(n-1)w_{n-1}w_{1}

\displaystyle=w_{1}w_{n-1}+(n-1)w_{n-1}w_{1}+2w_{2}w_{n-2}+(n-2)w_{n-2}w_{2}+\cdot\cdot\cdot

\displaystyle\geq 2(\sqrt{1}\sqrt{n-1})w_{1}w_{n-1}+2(\sqrt{2}\sqrt{n-2})w_{2}w_{n-2}+\cdot\cdot\cdot

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\sqrt{l}w_{l}(t)\sqrt{n-l}w_{n-l}(t)

(4.13)

and the ODEs’ comparison principle, we only need to deal with

\frac{dw_{n}(t)}{dt}-\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\sqrt{l}w_{l}(t)\sqrt{n-l}w_{n-l}(t)+\kappa n^{\alpha}w_{n}(t)=0,

(4.14)

which possess the construction of symmetry. Thus the similar argument in this section indicates that the solutions to (
4.14
) would develop into singularities in finite time, hence holds for (
4.12
).

Remark 4.2
.

Based on these singularity results, it’s not difficult to find that smoothing effects due to weak dissipation can’t prevent the formation of singularity, thus the global existence is expected when with strong dissipation (
\alpha>1
) for this model. However, here we can’t give a super bound for solutions due to the limitation of the spectral method.

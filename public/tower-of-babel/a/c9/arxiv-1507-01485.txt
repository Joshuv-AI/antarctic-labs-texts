Abstract
. This note (originally from 2015) provides a proof of a 1985 conjecture of Montiel and Ros concerning the conformal volume of tori. This updated version adds a proof of the claim made in Remark 5 below about the value of the conformal volume of tori in the cases not covered by the conjecture of Montiel and Ros.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Conformal volume

In a celebrated paper
[
3
]
,
Li and Yau introduced and studied the notion of
conformal volume

of a compact conformal manifold
\bigl(M,[g]\bigr)
.

To recall this definition, let
G_{n}
be the group
of conformal automorphisms of the standard unit
n
-sphere
\bigl(S^{n},g_{0}\bigr)
;
it is known to be isomorphic to
\operatorname{O}(n{+}1,1)
.
Given a smooth mapping
f:M^{m}\to S^{n}
, let
V(f)

denote the volume of the (possibly degenerate) metric
f^{*}(g_{0})
on
M
.
One then defines the
conformal volume
of
f
to be

V_{c}(f)=\sup_{\psi\in G_{n}}V(\psi{\circ}f).

(This is also what Gromov
[
2
]

has called the
visual volume
of
f
.)
If
f
is an immersion at some point, then
V_{c}(f)\geq V(S^{m},g_{0})>0
.
Indeed, if
m>1
(or
m\geq 1
if
M
is compact),
one has
V_{c}(f)>V(S^{m},g_{0})

unless
f(M)
lies in a totally umbilic
m
-sphere in
S^{n}
.
See
[
1
, Proposition 1]
.)

Now, every conformal automorphism
\psi:S^{n}\to S^{n}

extends uniquely to a conformal automorphism
of the
(n{+}1)
-ball
B^{n+1}\subset{\mathbb{E}}^{n+1}

of which
S^{n}
is the boundary.
If
\mathrm{d}V_{f}
is the volume form on
M

induced by
f:M^{m}\to S^{n}
, then one finds that

\mathrm{d}V_{\psi{\circ}f}=\frac{\bigl(1-\psi(0){\cdot}\psi(0)\bigr)^{m/2}}{(1-\psi(0){\cdot}f)^{m}}\mathrm{d}V_{f}\,.

Thus, one has the alternative formula

V_{c}(f)=\sup_{a\in B^{n+1}}\ \ \int_{M}\frac{(1-a{\cdot}a)^{m/2}}{(1-a{\cdot}f)^{m}}\ \mathrm{d}V_{f}\,.

When
M^{m}
is endowed with a conformal structure
[g]
,
Li and Yau define the
conformal
n
-volume
of
\bigl(M,[g]\bigr)
to be

V_{c}\bigl(M,[g],n\bigr)=\inf\left\{\ V_{c}(f)\ \mid f:\bigl(M,[g]\bigr)\to\bigl(S^{n},[g_{0}]\bigr)\ \text{is branched conformal}\ \right\}.

Of course,
V_{c}\bigl(M,[g],n\bigr)
is only defined when
n
is large enough
for there to exist a branched conformal immersion
f:M^{m}\to S^{n}
.
Since one has
V_{c}\bigl(M,[g],n\bigr)\geq V_{c}\bigl(M,[g],n{+}1\bigr)

when both quantities are defined,
one can define the
conformal volume
of
\bigl(M,[g]\bigr)
to be

V_{c}\bigl(M,[g]\bigr)=\lim_{n\to\infty}V_{c}\bigl(M,[g],n\bigr).

Naturally, in the compact case, one has
V_{c}\bigl(M^{m},[g]\bigr)\geq V(S^{m},g_{0})
.

In
[
3
]
,
Li and Yau give a number of applications of this notion of conformal volume,
showing, in particular, how it is related to the Willmore problem,
eigenvalue estimates,
and a number of other interesting differential geometric issues.
They developed methods of estimating the conformal volume,
but were able explicitly to compute it only in a small number of cases.

1.2.
The case of 2-tori

Montiel and Ros
[
5
]
carried out a careful study
of the conformal volume in the case of a torus
T_{\tau}={\mathbb{C}}/\Lambda_{\tau}

where
\Lambda_{\tau}
is the discrete lattice in
{\mathbb{C}}\simeq{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

generated by
1
and
\tau=x+{\mathrm{i}}y
,
where
0\leq x\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
and
y\geq\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
,
and the metric on the torus
is
|\mathrm{d}z|^{2}=\mathrm{d}z{\circ}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}=\mathrm{d}u^{2}+\mathrm{d}v^{2}
.
(Every metric on the
2
-dimensional torus
is conformal to this metric on
{\mathbb{C}}/\Lambda_{\tau}

for some unique
\tau=x+{\mathrm{i}}y
satisfying these inequalities.)

For this conformal torus,
they constructed a conformal (in fact, homothetic)
embedding
f_{\tau}:T_{\tau}\to S^{5}

with volume

(1.1)

V(f_{\tau})=\frac{4\pi^{2}y}{y^{2}+x^{2}-x+1}\,,

and they showed that

(1.2)

V_{c}\bigl(T_{\tau},\bigl[|\mathrm{d}z|^{2}\bigr]\bigr)\geq\frac{4\pi^{2}y}{y^{2}+x^{2}-x+1}\,.

Since, the right hand side of (
1.1
)
tends to zero as
y
goes to
\infty
,
the inequality (
1.2
)
must become strict for
y
sufficiently large.

Montiel and Ros showed
[
5
, Theorem 8]
that,
when
x=0
and
1\leq y\leq\sqrt{5/3}
, equality
does, in fact, hold in (
1.2
).
Moreover, they conjectured that equality holds in (
1.2
)
as long as
(x{-}\tfrac{1}{2})^{2}+y^{2}<\tfrac{9}{4}
, i.e.,
|\tau{-}\tfrac{1}{2}|<\tfrac{3}{2}
.
(See Remark
1
for their motivation.)

In this note (see Theorem
2
),
I prove (a slightly stronger version of) their conjecture, i.e.,

(1.3)

V_{c}\bigl(T_{\tau},\bigl[|\mathrm{d}z|^{2}\bigr]\bigr)=\frac{4\pi^{2}y}{y^{2}+x^{2}-x+1}\qquad\text{when}\ |\tau{-}\tfrac{1}{2}|\leq\tfrac{3}{2}.

The proof proceeds in two steps:
First, I show, as did Montiel and Ros,
that, for all
\tau
,
one has
V_{c}\bigl(T_{\tau},\bigl[|\mathrm{d}z|^{2}\bigr]\bigr)\geq V(f_{\tau})
.
Second, I show that
V_{c}(f_{\tau})=V(f_{\tau})

when
|\tau{-}\tfrac{1}{2}|\leq\tfrac{3}{2}
, i.e., that, for such
\tau
,

(1.4)

\int_{T_{\tau}}\frac{(1-a{\cdot}a)}{(1-a{\cdot}f_{\tau})^{2}}\ \mathrm{d}V_{f_{\tau}}\leq\frac{4\pi^{2}y}{y^{2}+x^{2}-x+1}\qquad\text{for all $a\in{\mathbb{E}}^{6}$ with $a{\cdot}a<1$.}

Since, by definition,

V_{c}(f_{\tau})\geq V_{c}\bigl(T_{\tau},\bigl[|\mathrm{d}z|^{2}\bigr],n\bigr)\geq V_{c}\bigl(T_{\tau},\bigl[|\mathrm{d}z|^{2}\bigr]\bigr)

for
n\geq 5
, the conjecture follows.

Remark 1

(Motivation for the conjecture)
.

By a Taylor series expansion, Montiel and Ros
showed that the function of
a
on the left hand side of (
1.4
)
attains a local maximum at
a=0\in{\mathbb{E}}^{6}
when
|\tau{-}\tfrac{1}{2}|<\tfrac{3}{2}

and that it does not have a local maximum there when
|\tau{-}\tfrac{1}{2}|>\tfrac{3}{2}
.

However, they were not able to estimate the integral accurately
enough to show that
a=0
actually is the maximum of the function
on the unit ball in
{\mathbb{E}}^{6}
when
|\tau{-}\tfrac{1}{2}|<\tfrac{3}{2}
.
My contribution to proving their conjecture is to show that,
when
|\tau{-}\tfrac{1}{2}|\leq\tfrac{3}{2}
, the function on the left
hand side of (
1.4
) does indeed attain its supremum
on the unit ball in
{\mathbb{E}}^{6}
at
a=0
.

2.
Balanced Volume

Throughout this section,
\tau\in{\mathbb{C}}
denotes
x+{\mathrm{i}}\,y

where
0\leq x\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
and
y\geq\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
,
and
\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is the lattice generated by
1
and
\tau
.
Let
T={\mathbb{C}}/\Lambda
and let
\mathrm{d}A=\tfrac{{\mathrm{i}}}{2}\,\mathrm{d}z{\mathchoice{\,{\scriptstyle\wedge}\,}{{\scriptstyle\wedge}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\wedge}}{{\scriptscriptstyle\wedge}}}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}

be the area form on
T
. (I will write
z=u+{\mathrm{i}}v

to denote the real and imaginary parts of the coordinate
z
on
{\mathbb{C}}
.
While
z
is not a well-defined coordinate on
T
,
the differential
\mathrm{d}z
is well-defined on
T
, and, consequently,
for any vector-valued function
f
on
T
,
one has
\mathrm{d}f=f_{z}\,\mathrm{d}z+f_{\bar{z}}\,\mathrm{d}\bar{z}
, so that
the quantities
f_{z}
and
f_{\bar{z}}
are also well-defined.)

A
C^{\infty}
map
\phi:T\to S^{n}
is said to be
\mathrm{d}A
-balanced
if

(2.1)

\int_{T}\phi\>\mathrm{d}A=0\in{\mathbb{E}}^{n+1}.

The map
\phi
is said to be
weakly conformal

if
\phi_{z}\cdot\phi_{z}=0
. When
\phi
is weakly conformal,
set

(2.2)

V(\phi)=2\int_{T}\phi_{z}{\cdot}\phi_{\bar{z}}\ \mathrm{d}A.

Note that, when
\phi
is weakly conformal, one has
V(\phi)>0

unless
\phi
is constant.

Definition 1
.

The
balanced volume
of
T
is the infimum
V_{b}(n,T)

of the numbers
V(\phi)
where
\phi
ranges over the set of

\mathrm{d}A
-balanced, weakly conformal smooth maps
\phi:T\to S^{n}
.

The following result was proved by Montiel and Ros

[
5
, Proposition 5]
(and, independently, by me) in 1985:

Theorem 1
.

The following formulae hold

•

V_{b}(2,T)=8\pi
for all
\tau=x+{\mathrm{i}}\,y
.

•

V_{b}(n,T)={\displaystyle\frac{4\pi^{2}y}{y^{2}+1}}

for all
n\geq 3
if
x=0
.

•

V_{b}(n,T)={\displaystyle\frac{4\pi^{2}y}{y^{2}+x^{2}-x+1}}

for all
n\geq 5
.

Moreover, there is a
\mathrm{d}A
-balanced, weakly conformal map

f_{\tau}:T\to S^{5}\subset S^{n}
, unique up to rigid motions in
S^{n}
,
for which
V(f_{\tau})=V_{b}(5,T)
. This
f_{\tau}
is homothetic and,
when
x=0
, it is a linearly full immersion in an
S^{3}\subset S^{5}

while, when
x>0
, it is a linearly full immersion in
S^{5}
.

Remark 2
.

I do not know the values of
V_{b}(3,T)
or
V_{b}(4,T)
when
x>0
,
but, since
V_{b}(n,T)\geq V_{b}(n{+}1,T)
, one has the inequalities

V_{b}(3,T)\geq V_{b}(4,T)\geq V_{b}(5,T)=\frac{4\pi^{2}y}{y^{2}+x^{2}-x+1}.

Proof.

The case
n=2
is special
because a
\mathrm{d}A
-balanced, weakly conformal map
\phi:T\to S^{2}

is either holomorphic or anti-holomorphic and hence
V(\phi)=4|d|\pi

where
d
is the degree of
\phi
, which necessarily satisfies
|d|\geq 2
.
(The degree
d
cannot be zero, since, in this case,

\phi
is constant and hence cannot be
\mathrm{d}A
-balanced.
Moreover,
d\not=\pm 1
since such a map would necessarily
be a biholomorphism between
T
and
S^{2}
.)
Thus,
V(\phi)\geq 8\pi
, so
V_{b}(2,T)\geq 8\pi
.
On the other hand, every torus
T

does admit a holomorphic mapping
\phi:T\to S^{2}
with degree
d=2
.
Moreover, it is possible to find such a
\phi
that is
\mathrm{d}A
-balanced.
Thus,
V_{b}(2,T)=8\pi
.

Returning to general
n
, let
\phi:T\to S^{n}

be a
\mathrm{d}A
-balanced, weakly conformal mapping.
The proof will now proceed by expanding
\phi
in its Fourier series.

To do this, let
\Lambda^{*}\subset{\mathbb{C}}

be the lattice dual to
\Lambda
via the pairing

\langle\xi,z\rangle=\Re(\xi\bar{z})=\tfrac{1}{2}(\xi\bar{z}+\bar{\xi}z).

Then
\Lambda^{*}
is generated as a lattice by
\{{\mathrm{i}}/y,1-{\mathrm{i}}\,x/y\}
.

The Fourier series expansion of
\phi
then takes the form

\phi=\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda^{*}}\phi_{\xi}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}\langle\xi,z\rangle}

where
\phi_{0}=0
(since
\phi
is
\mathrm{d}A
-balanced)
and
\phi_{-\xi}=\overline{\phi_{\xi}}
(since
\phi
is real-valued).
Since
\phi\cdot\phi\equiv 1
, one has the identities

1=\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda^{*}}|\phi_{\xi}|^{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0=\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda^{*}}\phi_{\xi}\cdot\overline{\phi_{\xi-\eta}}

for all
\eta\in\Lambda^{*}\setminus\{0\}
.
The equation
\phi_{z}\cdot\phi_{z}\equiv 0
(weak conformality) becomes

0=\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda^{*}}\xi(\xi{-}\eta)\,\phi_{\xi}\cdot\overline{\phi_{\xi-\eta}}

for all
\eta\in\Lambda^{*}\setminus\{0\}
.
In particular, setting
\eta=0
, one finds
1
1

1

It will be seen that
V(\phi)\geq V_{b}(5,T)

holds for all
\mathrm{d}A
-balanced maps
\phi:T\to S^{n}

that satisfy (
2.3
),
which, of course, is equivalent to the very weak condition

\int_{T}(\phi_{z}{\cdot}\phi_{z})\,\mathrm{d}A=0
.

(2.3)

0=\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda^{*}}\xi^{2}\,|\phi_{\xi}|^{2}.

Meanwhile, computation yields

V(\phi)=2\pi^{2}y\,\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda^{*}}|\xi|^{2}\,|\phi_{\xi}|^{2},

and it is this latter quantity for which I seek to determine a lower bound.

Let

\Lambda^{*}_{+}=\left\{\xi\in\Lambda^{*}\ \vrule\ \Re(\xi)>0\ \text{or}\left(\Re(\xi)=0\ \text{and}\ \Im(\xi)>0\right)\right\},

so that
\Lambda^{*}=\Lambda^{*}_{+}\cup\{0\}\cup(-\Lambda^{*}_{+})
.

For
\xi\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}
, set
p_{\xi}=2|\phi_{\xi}|^{2}
.
Thus, one seeks the minimum value of

L=\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}}|\xi|^{2}\,p_{\xi}

subject to the conditions that
p_{\xi}\geq 0
when
\xi\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}

while

1=\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}}p_{\xi}\qquad\text{and}\qquad 0=\sum_{\xi\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}}\xi^{2}\,p_{\xi}.

Geometrically, this amounts to the following:
For
\xi\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}
, define

\hat{\xi}=\bigl(\Re(\xi^{2}),\Im(\xi^{2}),|\xi|^{2}\bigr)\in N_{+}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3},

where
N_{+}=\left\{\bigl(a,b,\sqrt{a^{2}{+}b^{2}}\bigr)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\mid\ (a,b)\not=(0,0)\ \right\}

is the upper right circular cone in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
,
and let
C
be the convex hull in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
of the set
\left\{\hat{\xi}\ \vrule\ \xi\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}\right\}\subset N_{+}
. Thus,

C=\left\{\ \sum_{\xi\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}}p_{\xi}\,\hat{\xi}\ \ \vrule\ \ p_{\xi}\geq 0\ \text{and}\ \sum_{\xi\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}}p_{\xi}=1\ \right\}.

Then one seeks the infimum of the numbers
\ell\in{\mathbb{R}}

such that
(0,0,\ell)
lies in
C
.

To determine this,
let
\xi_{m,n}=m(1-{\mathrm{i}}\,x/y)+n({\mathrm{i}}/y)
.
Thus,
\xi_{m,n}
lies in
\Lambda^{*}_{+}

if and only if either
m>0
or
m=0
and
n>0
.
Let
P
be the plane in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

that contains the three (non-collinear!) points
\hat{\xi}_{0,1}
,

\hat{\xi}_{1,0}
, and
\hat{\xi}_{1,1}
.

I claim that
\hat{\xi}_{m,n}
lies strictly above
P
for all

\xi_{m,n}\in\Lambda^{*}_{+}
except when
(m,n)
is one of
(0,1)
,
(1,0)
, or
(1,1)
. (Here, ‘above’ means on the opposite side of
P
from
(0,0,0)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.) To see why, consider the determinant
\Delta(m,n)
of the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}\hat{\xi}_{0,1}&1\\
\hat{\xi}_{1,0}&1\\
\hat{\xi}_{1,1}&1\\
\hat{\xi}_{m,n}&1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}-1/y^{2}&0&1/y^{2}&1\\
1-x^{2}/y^{2}&-2x/y&1+x^{2}/y^{2}&1\\
1-(x{-}1)^{2}/y^{2}&-2(x{-}1)/y&1+(x{-}1)^{2}/y^{2}&1\\
m^{2}-(mx{-}n)^{2}/y^{2}&-2m(mx{-}n)/y&m^{2}+(mx{-}n)^{2}/y^{2}&1\end{pmatrix}.

One finds that
\Delta(m,n)=(4/y^{3})\bigl(1-(m^{2}-mn+n^{2})\bigr)
.
The claim now follows by noting that
\Delta(0,0)>0
; that
\Delta(m,n)=0

if and only
(m,n)
is one of
\pm(0,1)
,
\pm(1,0)
, or
\pm(1,1)
;
and that
\Delta(m,n)<0
otherwise.

Set
\xi_{1}=\xi_{0,1}
,
\xi_{2}=\xi_{1,0}
, and
\xi_{3}=\xi_{1,1}
.
It now follows that the triangle
T

with vertices
\hat{\xi}_{1}
,
\hat{\xi}_{2}
,
\hat{\xi}_{3}
is a face of
C
.
Moreover, one finds that the ray
\left\{(0,0,\ell)\ \vrule\ \ell\geq 0\right\}

meets
T
uniquely in the point

r_{1}\,\hat{\xi}_{1}+r_{2}\,\hat{\xi}_{2}+r_{3}\,\hat{\xi}_{3}=\bigl(0,0,S(x,y)\bigr)

where
r_{1}\geq r_{2}\geq r_{3}\geq 0
satisfy
r_{1}+r_{2}+r_{3}=1

and are given by

r_{1}=\frac{y^{2}+x^{2}-x}{(y^{2}+x^{2}-x+1)}\,,\quad r_{2}=\frac{1-x}{(y^{2}+x^{2}-x+1)}\,,\quad r_{3}=\frac{x}{(y^{2}+x^{2}-x+1)},

while

S(x,y)=\frac{2}{(y^{2}+x^{2}-x+1)}.

Thus, it follows that

V(\phi)\geq\frac{4\pi^{2}y}{(y^{2}+x^{2}-x+1)}\,,

and that, moreover, equality holds only if
\phi_{\xi}=0

for
\xi\not\in\left\{\pm\xi_{1},\pm\xi_{2},\pm\xi_{3}\right\}

while
|\phi_{\xi_{i}}|^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}r_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
.

To show that this lower bound is actually achieved by a
\mathrm{d}A
-balanced
weakly conformal immersion, one must show that it is possible to choose
the
\phi_{\xi_{i}}
for
i=1,2,3
so that the resulting
\phi
is
actually weakly conformal, i.e., so that
\phi_{z}\cdot\phi_{z}\equiv 0
.
It is easy to see that necessary and sufficient conditions for this
are the relations

\phi_{\xi_{i}}\cdot\phi_{\xi_{j}}=0\ \text{for all $i$ and $j$}

while

\phi_{\xi_{i}}\cdot\overline{\phi_{\xi_{j}}}=0\ \text{for all $i\not=j$}.

Of course, one must also require
|\phi_{\xi_{i}}|^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}r_{i}

for
i=1,2,3
. These equations can be satisfied
(uniquely up to orthogonal transformation in
{\mathbb{E}}^{n+1}
because all of the
inner products have been specified) by the vectors

\displaystyle\phi_{\xi_{1}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{r_{1}}(1,{\mathrm{i}},0,0,0,0),

\displaystyle\phi_{\xi_{2}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{r_{2}}(0,0,1,{\mathrm{i}},0,0),

\displaystyle\phi_{\xi_{3}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\sqrt{r_{3}}(0,0,0,0,1,{\mathrm{i}}).

Using these vectors, one finds that the corresponding map
f_{\tau}:T\to S^{5}

given by

f_{\tau}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{3}\left(\phi_{\xi_{j}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{2\pi{\mathrm{i}}\langle\xi_{j},z\rangle}+\overline{\phi_{\xi_{j}}}\,{\mathrm{e}}^{-2\pi{\mathrm{i}}\langle\xi_{j},z\rangle}\right)

satisfies

\left|\mathrm{d}f_{\tau}\right|^{2}=\frac{4\pi^{2}}{(y^{2}+x^{2}-x+1)}\,|\mathrm{d}z|^{2},

so this
f_{\tau}
is a homothetic immersion of
T={\mathbb{C}}/\Lambda

with its flat conformal metric, and its induced area has the minimal value

V(f_{\tau})=\frac{4\pi^{2}y}{(y^{2}+x^{2}-x+1)}=V_{b}(5,T)\,.

The uniqueness up to orthogonal transformation
of this
V_{b}
-minimizer is now clear.

\psi_{xy}

3.
Conformal volume of the fundamental immersion

In this section, I study the conformal volume of the conformal immersion
of minimal
\mathrm{d}A
-balanced volume of the torus
T={\mathbb{C}}/\Lambda
, where

\Lambda\subset{\mathbb{C}}
is the lattice generated by
1
and
\tau=x+{\mathrm{i}}\,y

where
0\leq x\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
and
y\geq\sqrt{1-x^{2}}
. The three
shortest elements in the dual lattice
\Lambda^{*}
are
\xi_{1}={\mathrm{i}}/y
,

\xi_{2}=1-{\mathrm{i}}\,x/y
, and
\xi_{3}=\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}=1-{\mathrm{i}}\,(x{-}1)/y
.

I will use
z=u+{\mathrm{i}}\,v
as the standard coordinate on
{\mathbb{C}}
,
so that the differential
\mathrm{d}z
is well-defined on
T
,
with induced flat metric
|\mathrm{d}z|^{2}=\mathrm{d}z{\circ}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}
.

As was explained in §
2
,
the fundamental immersion
f_{\tau}:T\to S^{5}\subset{\mathbb{E}}^{6}
is given by

(3.1)

f_{\tau}(u+{\mathrm{i}}\,v)=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{r_{1}}\,\cos\bigl(2\pi\,v/y\bigr)\hfill\\
\sqrt{r_{1}}\,\sin\bigl(2\pi\,v/y\bigr)\hfill\\
\sqrt{r_{2}}\,\cos\bigl(2\pi\,(u-v\,x/y)\bigr)\hfill\\
\sqrt{r_{2}}\,\sin\bigl(2\pi\,(u-v\,x/y)\bigr)\hfill\\
\sqrt{r_{3}}\,\cos\bigl(2\pi\,(u-v\,(x{-}1)/y)\bigr)\\
\sqrt{r_{3}}\,\sin\bigl(2\pi\,(u-v\,(x{-}1)/y)\bigr)\end{pmatrix}\,,

where

(3.2)

r_{1}=\frac{y^{2}+x^{2}-x}{(y^{2}{+}x^{2}{-}x{+}1)}\,,\quad r_{2}=\frac{1-x}{(y^{2}{+}x^{2}{-}x{+}1)}\,,\quad r_{3}=\frac{x}{(y^{2}{+}x^{2}{-}x{+}1)}.

These numbers satisfy

An elliptic integral

Since the integral formula (
3.6
) is not
entirely standard (in fact, none of the symbolic integration packages

Thus, assume that
a
and
b

a,b\geq 0

To evaluate the integral

I(a,b)=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{(1-a\,\cos t)\,\mathrm{d}t}{\bigl((1{-}a\,\cos t)^{2}-b^{2}\bigr)^{3/2}}=2\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{(1-a\,\cos t)\,\mathrm{d}t}{\bigl((1{-}a\,\cos t)^{2}-b^{2}\bigr)^{3/2}},

in the integral, noting that

\cos t=\frac{\tau^{2}-\tan^{2}\theta}{\tau^{2}+\tan^{2}\theta}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{d}t=\frac{2\tau\sec\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta}{\tau^{2}+\tan^{2}\theta}.

One then obtains

\displaystyle I(a,b)

\displaystyle=\frac{4\tau^{2}}{\bigl(1{-}(b{-}a)^{2}\bigr)^{3/2}(1{-}b{+}a)^{2}}

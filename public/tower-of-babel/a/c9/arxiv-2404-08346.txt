New instanton on a warped Kerr spacetime Journal: Physics Letters B
Reinoud Jan Slagter

Abstract
We find an exact time-dependent instanton solution on a vacuum Kerr-like warped spacetime in conformal dilaton gravity. Remarkably, the metric solution results from a first-order PDE, allowing the connection with self-duality. The antipodal boundary condition on the hypersurface of a Klein bottle ∼ ℂ 1 × ℂ 1 \sim\mathbb{C}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{1} is applied to describe the Hawking particles. We used the Hopf fibration to get S 2 S^{2} as the black hole horizon, where the centrix is not in a torus but in the Klein bottle. The twist fits very well with the antipodal identification of the point on the horizon. No "cut and past" is necessary, so the Hawing particles remain pure without instantaneous information transport. A local observer passing the horizon will not notice a central singularity in suitable coordinates. The black hole paradoxes are also revisited in our new black hole model. A connection is made with the geomeric quantization of ℂ 1 × ℂ 1 ∼ S 3 \mathbb{C}^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{1}\sim S^{3} , by considering the symplectic 2-form. The model can be easily extended to the non-vacuum situation by including a scalar field. Both the dilaton and the scalar field can be treated as quantum fields when approaching the Planck area.

Introduction

Following the pioneering work of
hawking1975
on the evaporation of black holes, many scientists began to study the quantum-gravitational properties of black holes. However, there are some profound unsolved problems as one approaches the Planck scale. Due to the extremely high curvature, quantum effects can no longer be denied. It is thought that knowledge of the quantum effects near the horizon of a black hole will eventually lead to a consistent quantum gravity model. The properties of the black hole, i.e. its entropy, mass and emission, need to be revised because there are some serious paradoxes. When fields are quantized on a manifold, one needs a one-to-one map between the entire asymptotic domain
of the maximally extended Penrose diagram.
We will follow here the approach of
thooft2016
, i.e. an antipodal topological boundary condition, already proposed by
schrod1957
.
This approach differs significantly from other approaches to attack the paradoxes, i.e. the information-, the firewall-, the complementarity paradox and the unitarity of the S-matrix.
The Randall-Sundrum (RS) warped brane world model (
ran1999
,
ran1999a
) is a simplification of the full string model. The extra-dimensional bulk space has a negative cosmological constant. The dilution of gravity by the presence of the bulk effectively weakens gravity on the brane. The result is that the true higher dimensional Planck scale can be much lower than the effective 4D Planck scale, possibly down to the electroweak scale. It also extends the range of graviton modes that can be felt on the brane, the so-called Kaluza-Klein (KK) modes. The model does not rely on compactification to localize gravity on the brane, but on the curvature of the bulk.
The notion of an instanton, or pseudoparticle, was introduced by ’t Hooft and represents a localized finite action topological invariant in field theory. It was first developed in Yang-Mills theory, in particular in
SU(n)
gauge theories.
The classical field equations with self-dual field strength then gave rise to a new non-perturbative treatment. The Minkowski spacetime was replaced by a Wick rotation
t\rightarrow i\tau
, where
\tau
is a special parameterization of the favoured tunneling path through the configuration space. Feynman’s path-integral approach to quantization is then unambiguous. Solutions are concentrated around a point in four-dimensional Euclidean space, and at one instant there is a "bump" in the field strength, which quickly dies away. There are striking similarities between these instantons and their gravitational counterparts. They are obtained in general relativity by a Wick rotation on Riemannian spacetime. The field equations then lead to first order differential equations. For an overview see
gibb1993
.
The structure of this letter is as follows. In section two we present the model under consideration. In section three we treat the Hopf fibration needed to describe the embedding of the Klein surface in the 5D model.
In section four we make the connection with the instanton and in section five a possible treatment of the Hawking particles.

Extracting the Luttinger parameter from a single wave function
Bi-Yang Tan 1, Yueshui Zhang 2, Hua-Chen Zhang 3 , Wei Tang 4 , Lei Wang 5,6, Hong-Hao Tu 2, Ying-Hai Wu 1

Abstract
The low-energy physics of Tomonaga-Luttinger liquids (TLLs) is controlled by the Luttinger parameter. We demonstrate that this parameter can be extracted from a single wave function for one-component TLLs with periodic boundary condition. This method relies on the fact that TLLs are described by conformal field theory in which crosscap states can be constructed. The overlaps between the crosscap states and the ground state as well as some excited states are proved to be universal numbers that directly reveal the Luttinger parameter. In microscopic lattice models, crosscap states are formed by putting each pair of antipodal sites into a maximally entangled state. Analytical and numerical calculations are performed in a few representative models to substantiate the conformal field theory prediction. The extracted Luttinger parameters are generally quite accurate in finite-size systems with moderate lengths, so there is no need to perform data fitting and/or finite-size scaling.



Introduction
— The theory of Tomonaga-Luttinger liquid (TLL) is a great triumph of strongly correlated physics
Giamarchi 2003
. Based on previous work of Tomonaga
Tomonaga 1950
, Luttinger introduced a model to study interacting fermions in one dimension
Luttinger 1963

;
Mattis 1974
;
Luther and Peschel 1974
;
Haldane 1981
;

, the bosonization framework was established to provide a unified treatment for many strongly correlated problems in one dimension. Spin chains are usually studied using the Jordan-Wigner transformation that converts spins to fermions. Gapless phases of bosons or fermions can be understood using free boson fields that correspond to density fluctuations. Instabilities of the TLL lead to symmetry-breaking ordered phases. It is not easy to realize one-dimensional systems, but experimental investigations have been carried out in solid state and cold atom platforms

;
Schwartz et al. 1998
;
Bockrath et al. 1999
;
Yao et al. 1999

;

;

;

. Another context in which TLL thrives is the edge of certain two-dimensional topological states
Wen 1995
;
Chang 2003
;
Wu et al. 2006
;
Li et al. 2015a
.

The low-energy physics of one-component TLLs is captured by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H=\frac{v}{8\pi}\int^{L}_{0}\mathrm{d}x\,\left[(\partial_{x}\hat{\varphi})^{2}+(\partial_{x}\hat{\theta})^{2}\right],

In this Letter, we propose that the Luttinger parameter in microscopic models can be extracted from the eigenstate crosscap overlap (ESCO). It has three favorable features: i) a single wave function instead of multiple states is used; ii) data fitting or finite-size scaling is not needed; iii) microscopic U(1) symmetry is not required. To the best of our knowledge, no other method enjoys all three advantages simultaneously. Our method is motivated by classical results and recent progresses in CFT and integrable models. When a CFT is defined on the Klein bottle, important information can be inferred from the thermal entropy

;

We aim to establish a general framework for studying crosscap states in CFT (without and with perturbations) such that physical properties can be deduced from their overlaps with eigenstates.

General construction of crosscap states
— While the crosscap is easy to visualize, the problem of what kind of states qualify as conformal crosscap states in CFTs has not been completely settled
Fioravanti et al. 1994
;
Pradisi et al. 1996
;
Fuchs et al. 2000
. We take the viewpoint that a conformal crosscap state should satisfy two sets of constraints. First, the field operators on antipodal spatial coordinates are identified with each other in some way. If a CFT has Lagrangian description, this can be made precise using the path integral formalism
Blumenhagen and Plauschinn 2009
;
Brunner and Hori 2004
;
Cho et al. 2015
. These requisites are physical manifestations of the geometric structure of crosscap and referred to as “sewing conditions”. Second, the partition functions on a cylinder with crosscap boundaries (tree channel) and on a Klein bottle (loop channel) should be equal. This “loop-tree channel correspondence” allows one to determine certain coefficients in crosscap states. As we turn to lattice models, the problem becomes even more complicated because the lattice counterpart of a field operator is generally difficult to find. It is thus unlikely that a conformal crosscap state can be converted rigorously to a lattice one. Based on the intuitive picture of identifying antipodal spatial points, recent works have studied lattice crosscap states in which antipodal sites form Bell states
Caetano and Komatsu 2022
;
Ekman
;
Gombor 2022
;
Gombor 2023
;
He and Jiang 2023
, but their relation to the conformal crosscap states has not been elucidated.

is proposed as a crosscap state. Numerical results for the
q=5
case with
N=50,\cdots,90
and various
h
are presented in Fig.
2
(d). The MPS bond dimension is
1500
for
N=50
and
2500
for
N=90
. At the SD point, finite-size systems are described by CFT with irrelevant perturbations, and the Luttinger parameter has a relative error
0.79\%
at
N=90
. In the Luttinger liquid regime, the crosscap overlap only exhibits weak dependence on the system size, so the data points for different
N
almost coincide. For the small and large
h
phases, the overlap strongly depends on
N
.

Summary and outlook
— To summarize, we have unveiled that the Luttinger parameter can be extracted from the overlaps between individual eigenstates and the crosscap states. The advantages of our method are corroborated by analytical and numerical results. This method may be applied to check if a trial wave function indeed describes the physics of TLL or other CFTs (see, e.g., Refs.
Cirac and Sierra 2010
;
Patil et al. 2017
) in the absence of a Hamiltonian. From the theoretical perspective, our results open up an exciting new avenue and many interesting directions are yet to be explored. The crosscap state used here is constructed from a certain type of Bell states. For integrable spin chains, other types of crosscap states have been studied
Ekman
. Do they have clear counterparts in CFTs? An extension of our method to multicomponent Luttinger liquids, such as the fermionic Hubbard model and SU(3) spin chains, is certainly very desirable. Finally, experimental protocols for measuring the crosscap overlap should be pursued. We hope that interesting results on these topics will be reported in the future.

Appendix A
Appendix A: Tomonaga-Luttinger liquid and compactified boson conformal field theory

The bosonized version of the (single-component) Tomonaga-Luttinger liquid is formulated as a free massless compactified boson field theory in (1+1)-dimensional spacetime. This section is devoted to a brief review of the operator formalism of the compactified boson field theory, and we mostly follow the notation used in Refs.
Francesco et al. 1997
;
Zhang et al. 2022
. We assume that the spatial direction is compact with length
L
, and the system admits periodic boundary condition along this direction. Therefore, the spacetime manifold (or “world sheet”), parametrized by coordinates
(x,t)
, is effectively a cylinder of circumference
L
. The Hamiltonian reads

\displaystyle H=\frac{v}{8\pi}\int^{L}_{0}\mathrm{d}x\,\left[(\partial_{x}\hat{\varphi})^{2}+(\partial_{x}\hat{\theta})^{2}\right],

(A1)

where
\hat{\varphi}
and
\hat{\theta}
are compactified boson fields, and
v
is the “speed of light” in this theory. Apart from
v
, the theory is characterized by the compactification radius
R
of
\hat{\varphi}
, which is related to the Luttinger parameter via
K=R^{2}/4
. The field
\hat{\theta}
is dual to
\hat{\varphi}
in the sense that
\partial_{x}\hat{\theta}(x,t)=v^{-1}\partial_{t}\hat{\varphi}(x,t)
.

On this manifold, the mode expansion of the
\hat{\varphi}
-field is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}(x,t)=\varphi_{0}+\frac{4\pi}{L}\left(\pi_{0}vt+\tilde{\pi}_{0}x\right)+i\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{1}{k}\left(a_{k}e^{2\pi ik\frac{x-vt}{L}}+\bar{a}_{k}e^{-2\pi ik\frac{x+vt}{L}}\right),

(A2)

where the zero-mode operators satisfy canonical commutation relations (we have formally introduced
\tilde{\varphi}_{0}
as the operator canonically conjugate to
\tilde{\pi}_{0}
),

\displaystyle[\varphi_{0},\pi_{0}]=[\tilde{\varphi}_{0},\tilde{\pi}_{0}]=i,

(A3)

whilst the other modes generate the Heisenberg algebra

\displaystyle[a_{k},a_{k^{\prime}}]=[\bar{a}_{k},\bar{a}_{k^{\prime}}]=k\delta_{k+k^{\prime}},\quad[a_{k},\bar{a}_{k^{\prime}}]=0.

(A4)

The fact the
\hat{\varphi}
-field is compactified with radius
R
means that the field configurations
\hat{\varphi}
and
\hat{\varphi}+2\pi R
are identified with each other. This implies that the field can have non-trivial winding around the cylinder, i.e.,
\hat{\varphi}(x+L,t)\equiv\hat{\varphi}(x,t)-2\pi mR
with
m\in\mathbb{Z}
; Eq. (
A2
) then shows that the operator
\tilde{\pi}_{0}
has eigenvalues
-mR/2
.

Before proceeding, let us remark on the physical significance of the zero-mode operators
\varphi_{0}
and
\pi_{0}
. By definition, the “canonical momentum” (density) corresponding to the
\hat{\varphi}
-field is

\displaystyle\Pi(x,t)\equiv\frac{1}{4\pi v}\partial_{t}\hat{\varphi}(x,t)=\frac{\pi_{0}}{L}+\frac{1}{2L}\sum_{k\neq 0}\left(a_{k}e^{2\pi ik\frac{x-vt}{L}}+\bar{a}_{k}e^{-2\pi ik\frac{x+vt}{L}}\right).

(A5)

Indeed, the canonical equal-time commutation relation,
[\hat{\varphi}(x,0),\Pi(x^{\prime},0)]=i\delta(x-x^{\prime})
, can readily be verified. The resulting
\mathrm{U}(1)
conserved charge,
\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x~\Pi(x,t)
, is precisely
\pi_{0}
; this indicates that the latter admits an interpretation as the “centre-of-mass momentum” of the field. In particular, the single-valuedness of the operator
e^{2\pi iR\pi_{0}}
translating around the compactified dimension (in the space of field configurations; not to be confused with the worldsheet) enforces that the eigenvalues of
\pi_{0}
are quantized to be
n/R
with
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. As
\varphi_{0}
is conjugate to
\pi_{0}
, the former is naturally interpreted as the “centre-of-mass coordinate” operator of the field.

Next, we recombine these zero-mode operators as follows:

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\varphi_{0}-\tilde{\varphi}_{0}),\quad P=\pi_{0}-\tilde{\pi}_{0};

\displaystyle\bar{Q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\varphi_{0}+\tilde{\varphi}_{0}),\quad\bar{P}=\pi_{0}+\tilde{\pi}_{0}.

(A6)

It is easily verified that these new operators still obey the canonical commutation relations,

\displaystyle[Q,P]=[\bar{Q},\bar{P}]=i.

(A7)

In terms of this new set of zero-mode operators as well as the “light-cone” coordinates
z\equiv-(x-vt)/L
and
\bar{z}\equiv-(x+vt)/L
, one arrives at the chiral decomposition of the
\hat{\varphi}
-field,
\hat{\varphi}(x,t)=\phi(z)+\bar{\phi}(\bar{z})
, where

\displaystyle\phi(z)

\displaystyle=Q+2\pi Pz+i\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{1}{k}a_{k}e^{-2\pi ikz},

\displaystyle\bar{\phi}(\bar{z})

\displaystyle=\bar{Q}-2\pi\bar{P}\bar{z}+i\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{1}{k}\bar{a}_{k}e^{2\pi ik\bar{z}}.

(A8)

Using the relations
\partial_{x}=-L^{-1}(\partial+\bar{\partial})
and
\partial_{t}=vL^{-1}(\partial-\bar{\partial})
with
\partial\equiv\partial_{z}
and
\bar{\partial}\equiv\partial_{\bar{z}}
, one finds that
\hat{\theta}(x,t)=\bar{\phi}(\bar{z})-\phi(z)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}(x,t)=\tilde{\varphi}_{0}+\frac{4\pi}{L}\left(\pi_{0}x+\tilde{\pi}_{0}vt\right)-i\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{1}{k}\left(a_{k}e^{2\pi ik\frac{x-vt}{L}}-\bar{a}_{k}e^{-2\pi ik\frac{x+vt}{L}}\right).

(A9)

As
\hat{\theta}(x+L,t)=\hat{\theta}(x,t)+4\pi\pi_{0}
, the quantization of the eigenvalues of
\pi_{0}
implies that the
\hat{\theta}
-field is also compactified, for which the radius is
2/R
.

Finally, substituting the mode expansions (
A2
) and (
A9
) into the expression (
A1
) of the Hamiltonian, and making use of (
A4
) as well as the zeta-function regularization (“
1+2+3+\cdots=-1/12
”), one finds after a straightforward computation that

\displaystyle H=\frac{2\pi v}{L}\left(L_{0}+\bar{L}_{0}-\frac{1}{12}\right),

(A10)

where

\displaystyle L_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}a_{0}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{-k}a_{k},

\displaystyle\bar{L}_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\bar{a}_{0}^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bar{a}_{-k}\bar{a}_{k},

(A11)

with
a_{0}\equiv P,~\bar{a}_{0}\equiv\bar{P}
, are the zeroth Virasoro generators. The Virasoro primary states (which are normalized by assumption),
|n,m\rangle
, are labelled by the eigenvalues of
a_{0}
and
\bar{a}_{0}
as

\displaystyle a_{0}|n,m\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{n}{R}+\frac{1}{2}mR\right)|n,m\rangle,

\displaystyle\bar{a}_{0}|n,m\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{n}{R}-\frac{1}{2}mR\right)|n,m\rangle.

(A12)

By definition, they are annihilated by the modes
a_{k}
and
\bar{a}_{k}
with
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
; other states in the corresponding conformal tower, which are orthogonal to each other and properly normalized, are constructed by acting modes with negative indices on the primary states:

\displaystyle|n,m;\{n_{k}\},\{\bar{n}_{k}\}\rangle=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{n_{k}!\bar{n}_{k}!}}\left(\frac{a_{-k}}{\sqrt{k}}\right)^{n_{k}}\left(\frac{\bar{a}_{-k}}{\sqrt{k}}\right)^{\bar{n}_{k}}\right]|n,m\rangle,\quad n_{k},\bar{n}_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}_{0}\,.

(A13)

One obviously has
|n,m;\{0\},\{0\}\rangle\equiv|n,m\rangle
. This basis trivializes the computation of partition functions, as
L_{0}
and
\bar{L}_{0}
act diagonally:

\displaystyle L_{0}|n,m;\{n_{k}\},\{\bar{n}_{k}\}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n}{R}+\frac{1}{2}mR\right)^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kn_{k}\right]|n,m;\{n_{k}\},\{\bar{n}_{k}\}\rangle\,,

\displaystyle\bar{L}_{0}|n,m;\{n_{k}\},\{\bar{n}_{k}\}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n}{R}-\frac{1}{2}mR\right)^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}k\bar{n}_{k}\right]|n,m;\{n_{k}\},\{\bar{n}_{k}\}\rangle\,.

(A14)

Appendix B
Appendix B: Determining crosscap states from constraints

The mode expansion form of the crosscap states
|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle
is given in Eq. (9) of the main text. Below we derive it in two steps:

i) Substitute the mode expansions of the compactified boson fields (
A8
) into the constraints

\displaystyle e^{i[\hat{\theta}(x)\mp\hat{\theta}(x+L/2)]/R^{\prime}}|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle\,,

\displaystyle e^{i[\hat{\varphi}(x)\pm\hat{\varphi}(x+L/2)]/R}|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle

(A15)

to determine the crosscap states
|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle
up to overall factors
\kappa_{\pm}
.

ii) Compare the cylinder partition function with two crosscap boundaries (“tree-channel”),
Z^{\mathcal{C}_{\pm}}(L,\beta)=\langle\mathcal{C}_{\pm}|e^{-\beta H}|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle
, with the Klein bottle partition function (“loop channel”),
Z^{\mathcal{K}_{\pm}}(2\beta,L/2)=\mathrm{Tr}(\Omega_{\pm}e^{-\beta H})
to fix the factors
\kappa_{\pm}
.

Step i):

Consider the mode expansions of the compactified boson fields in Eqs. (
A2
) and (
A9
) at
t=0
:

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}(x)=\tilde{\varphi}_{0}+\frac{4\pi x}{L}\pi_{0}-i\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{1}{k}\left(a_{k}e^{2\pi ik\frac{x}{L}}-\bar{a}_{k}e^{-2\pi ik\frac{x}{L}}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}(x)=\varphi_{0}+\frac{4\pi x}{L}\tilde{\pi}_{0}+i\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{1}{k}\left(a_{k}e^{2\pi ik\frac{x}{L}}+\bar{a}_{k}e^{-2\pi ik\frac{x}{L}}\right).

(A16)

To analyze the constraints in Eq. (
A15
), we consider

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}(x)-\hat{\theta}(x+L/2)

\displaystyle=-2\pi\pi_{0}-2i\sum_{k\neq 0,\,k\;\mathrm{odd}}\frac{1}{k}\left(a_{k}e^{2\pi ik\frac{x}{L}}-\bar{a}_{k}e^{-2\pi ik\frac{x}{L}}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}(x)+\hat{\varphi}(x+L/2)

\displaystyle=2\varphi_{0}+4\pi\tilde{\pi}_{0}\left(\frac{2x}{L}+\frac{1}{2}\right)+2i\sum_{k\neq 0,\,k\;\mathrm{even}}\frac{1}{k}\left(a_{k}e^{2\pi ik\frac{x}{L}}+\bar{a}_{k}e^{-2\pi ik\frac{x}{L}}\right),

(A17)

and

\displaystyle\hat{\theta}(x)+\hat{\theta}(x+L/2)

\displaystyle=2\tilde{\varphi}_{0}+4\pi\pi_{0}\left(\frac{2x}{L}+\frac{1}{2}\right)-2i\sum_{k\neq 0,\,k\;\mathrm{even}}\frac{1}{k}\left(a_{k}e^{2\pi ik\frac{x}{L}}-\bar{a}_{k}e^{-2\pi ik\frac{x}{L}}\right),

\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}(x)-\hat{\varphi}(x+L/2)

\displaystyle=-2\pi\tilde{\pi}_{0}+2i\sum_{k\neq 0,\,k\;\mathrm{odd}}\frac{1}{k}\left(a_{k}e^{2\pi ik\frac{x}{L}}+\bar{a}_{k}e^{-2\pi ik\frac{x}{L}}\right).

(A18)

By inserting these into Eq. (
A15
) and noting that the constraints (
A15
) are valid for all
x\in[0,L]
, it is then obvious that the oscillatory part should satisfy

\displaystyle\left[a_{k}\mp(-1)^{k}\bar{a}_{-k}\right]|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle=0,\quad k\neq 0\,,

(A19)

which fixes the form of
|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle
to be

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle

\displaystyle\propto\exp\left[\pm\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}a_{-k}\bar{a}_{-k}\right]|\mathcal{C}^{0}_{\pm}\rangle\,.

(A20)

Here
|\mathcal{C}^{0}_{\pm}\rangle=\sum_{n,m\in\mathbb{Z}}c^{\pm}_{n,m}|n,m\rangle
encode the zero mode information of the crosscap states, where
c^{\pm}_{n,m}
are the superposition coefficients that should be determined by using the zero mode constraints from Eq. (
A15
):

\displaystyle e^{-2\pi i\pi_{0}/R^{\prime}}|\mathcal{C}^{0}_{+}\rangle=|\mathcal{C}^{0}_{+}\rangle\,,

\displaystyle e^{2i\varphi_{0}/R}e^{4\pi i\left(\frac{2x}{L}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\tilde{\pi}_{0}/R}|\mathcal{C}^{0}_{+}\rangle=|\mathcal{C}^{0}_{+}\rangle\,,

(A21)

and

\displaystyle e^{2i\tilde{\varphi}_{0}/R^{\prime}}e^{4\pi i\left(\frac{2x}{L}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\pi_{0}/R^{\prime}}|\mathcal{C}^{0}_{-}\rangle=|\mathcal{C}^{0}_{-}\rangle\,,

\displaystyle e^{-2\pi i\tilde{\pi}_{0}/R}|\mathcal{C}^{0}_{-}\rangle=|\mathcal{C}^{0}_{-}\rangle\,.

(A22)

For
|\mathcal{C}^{0}_{+}\rangle=\sum_{n,m\in\mathbb{Z}}c^{+}_{n,m}|n,m\rangle
, the first equation in Eq. (
A21
) requires
c^{+}_{2n+1,m}=0
, since
e^{-2\pi i\pi_{0}/R^{\prime}}|n,m\rangle=(-1)^{n}|n,m\rangle
(note that
\pi_{0}|n,m\rangle=\tfrac{n}{R}|n,m\rangle
and
R^{\prime}=2/R
); the second equation in Eq. (
A21
) requires
c^{+}_{n,m\neq 0}=0
and
c^{+}_{2n,0}=c^{+}_{0,0}\;\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}
, since
e^{4\pi i(\frac{2x}{L}+\frac{1}{2})\tilde{\pi}_{0}/R}|n,m\rangle=e^{2\pi i(\frac{2x}{L}+\frac{1}{2})m}|n,m\rangle
and
e^{2i\varphi_{0}/R}|n,m\rangle=|n+2,m\rangle
(note that
\tilde{\pi}_{0}|n,m\rangle=-\tfrac{1}{2}mR|n,m\rangle
and
[\varphi_{0},\pi_{0}]=i
). Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}^{0}_{+}\rangle\propto\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|2n,0\rangle\,.

(A23)

The determination of
|\mathcal{C}^{0}_{-}\rangle
is similar and will not be repeated. It is given by

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}^{0}_{-}\rangle\propto\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|0,2m\rangle\,.

(A24)

Altogether, the constraints in Eq. (
A15
) determine the mode expansion form of the crosscap states

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\kappa_{+}\exp\left[\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}a_{-k}\bar{a}_{-k}\right]\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|2n,0\rangle\,,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=\kappa_{-}\exp\left[-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k}a_{-k}\bar{a}_{-k}\right]\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|0,2m\rangle

(A25)

up to overall factors
\kappa_{\pm}
. Without loss of generality, we choose
\kappa_{\pm}>0
below.

Step ii):

To fix the factors
\kappa_{\pm}
in Eq. (
A25
), we first calculate the (“tree channel”) cylinder partition function with crosscap boundary states

\displaystyle Z^{\mathcal{C}_{\pm}}(L,\beta)=\langle\mathcal{C}_{\pm}|e^{-\beta H}|\mathcal{C}_{\pm}\rangle\,,

(A26)

where the Hamiltonian
H
is given in Eq. (
A10
). The crosscap states in Eq. (
A25
) can be expanded in the orthonormal basis states in Eq. (
A13
) as

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=\kappa_{+}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{k}=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}!}\left[\frac{(-1)^{k}a_{-k}\bar{a}_{-k}}{k}\right]^{n_{k}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|2n,0\rangle

\displaystyle=\kappa_{+}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}kn_{k}}|2n,0;\{n_{k}\},\{n_{k}\}\rangle\,,

(A27)

and

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{-}\rangle

\displaystyle=\kappa_{-}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n_{k}=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n_{k}!}\left[\frac{(-1)^{k+1}a_{-k}\bar{a}_{-k}}{k}\right]^{n_{k}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}|0,2m\rangle

\displaystyle=\kappa_{-}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}(k+1)n_{k}}|0,2m;\{n_{k}\};\{n_{k}\}\rangle\,.

(A28)

As
|n,m;\{n_{k}\},\{\bar{n}_{k}\}\rangle
is the Hamiltonian eigenbasis [Eq. (
A14
)],
Z^{\mathcal{C}_{\pm}}(L,\beta)
are easy to calculate

\displaystyle Z^{\mathcal{C}_{+}}(L,\beta)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{+}^{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots=0}^{\infty}\langle 2n,0;\{n_{k}\},\{n_{k}\}|q^{L_{0}+\bar{L}_{0}-1/12}|2n,0;\{n_{k}\},\{n_{k}\}\rangle

\displaystyle=\kappa_{+}^{2}\;q^{-1/12}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{{R^{\prime}}^{2}n^{2}}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots=0}^{\infty}q^{2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kn_{k}}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{+}^{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{{R^{\prime}}^{2}n^{2}}\cdot q^{-1/12}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{1-q^{2k}}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{+}^{2}\,\frac{\vartheta_{3}(2{R^{\prime}}^{2}\tau)}{\eta(2\tau)}\,,

(A29)

and similarly

\displaystyle Z^{\mathcal{C}_{-}}(L,\beta)=\kappa_{-}^{2}\,\frac{\vartheta_{3}(2{R}^{2}\tau)}{\eta(2\tau)}\,,

(A30)

where
\tau=iv\beta/L
is the modular parameter and
q=e^{2\pi i\tau}
. The results are expressed in terms of the Dedekind eta function
\eta(\tau)=q^{1/24}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{k})
and one of the standard Jacobi’s theta functions
\vartheta_{3}(\tau)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}q^{k^{2}/2}
. Under the modular
S
transformation (
\tau\rightarrow-1/\tau
), Dedekind eta function and Jacobi’s theta function transform as
\sqrt{-i\tau}\vartheta_{3}(\tau)=\vartheta_{3}(-1/\tau)
and
\sqrt{-i\tau}\eta(\tau)=\eta(-1/\tau)
, respectively. This allows us to rewrite
Z^{\mathcal{C}_{\pm}}
as

\displaystyle Z^{\mathcal{C}_{+}}(L,\beta)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{+}^{2}\,\frac{1}{R^{\prime}}\,\frac{\vartheta_{3}(-1/2{R^{\prime}}^{2}\tau)}{\eta(-1/2\tau)}\,,

\displaystyle Z^{\mathcal{C}_{-}}(L,\beta)

\displaystyle=\kappa_{-}^{2}\,\frac{1}{R}\,\frac{\vartheta_{3}(-1/2{R^{\prime}}^{2}\tau)}{\eta(-1/2\tau)}\,.

(A31)

Now we turn to the (“loop channel”) Klein bottle partition function
Tang et al. 2019

\displaystyle Z^{\mathcal{K}_{\pm}}(2\beta,L/2)=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\Omega_{\pm}e^{-\tilde{H}L/2}\right),

(A32)

where
\tilde{H}=\frac{2\pi}{\tilde{L}}(L_{0}+\bar{L}_{0}-\frac{1}{12})
is the compactified boson Hamiltonian [c.f. Eqs. (
A1
) and (
A10
)] defined on a circle (in the imaginary time direction) with circumference
\tilde{L}=2v\beta
, which generates evolution along the spatial direction (it can be viewed as the generator of the quantum transfer matrix). The action of the “reflection operator”
\Omega_{\pm}
on oscillatory modes is given by
\Omega^{-1}_{\pm}a_{k}\Omega_{\pm}=\mp\bar{a}_{k}
and
\Omega^{-1}_{\pm}\bar{a}_{k}\Omega_{\pm}=\mp a_{k}
for
k\neq 0
. Applying them to Virasoro primary states gives

\displaystyle\Omega_{+}|n,m\rangle

\displaystyle=|-n,m\rangle\,,

\displaystyle\Omega_{-}|n,m\rangle

\displaystyle=|n,-m\rangle\,.

(A33)

Evaluating the Klein bottle partition function
Z^{\mathcal{K}_{\pm}}
in the Hamiltonian eigenbasis [Eq. (
A14
)] and noticing that only “left-right-symmetric” states
|0,m;\{n_{k}\},\{n_{k}\}\rangle
(
|n,0;\{n_{k}\},\{n_{k}\}\rangle
) contribute to
Z^{\mathcal{K}_{+}}
(
Z^{\mathcal{K}_{-}}
), we obtain

\displaystyle Z^{\mathcal{K}_{+}}(2\beta,L/2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots=0}^{\infty}\langle 0,m;\{n_{k}\},\{n_{k}\}|\tilde{q}^{2L_{0}-1/12}|0,m;\{n_{k}\},\{n_{k}\}\rangle

\displaystyle=\tilde{q}^{-1/12}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\tilde{q}^{m^{2}/{R^{\prime}}^{2}}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots=0}^{\infty}\tilde{q}^{2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}kn_{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\vartheta_{3}(-1/2{R^{\prime}}^{2}\tau)}{\eta(-1/2\tau)}\,,

(A34)

and similarly

\displaystyle Z^{\mathcal{K}_{-}}(2\beta,L/2)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots=0}^{\infty}\langle n,0;\{n_{k}\},\{n_{k}\}|\tilde{q}^{2L_{0}-1/12}|n,0;\{n_{k}\},\{n_{k}\}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\vartheta_{3}(-1/2{R}^{2}\tau)}{\eta(-1/2\tau)}

(A35)

with
\tilde{q}=e^{-\pi L/2v\beta}=e^{-\pi i/2\tau}
.

Considering the spacetime symmetry, the CFT partition function evaluated in tree and loop channels should be equal (“loop channel-tree channel equivalence”), so we expect

\displaystyle Z^{\mathcal{C}_{\pm}}(L,\beta)=Z^{\mathcal{K}_{\pm}}(2\beta,L/2)\,.

(A36)

Comparing Eqs. (
A31
), (
A34
) and (
A35
), the factors in the crosscap states (
A25
) are fixed to be

\displaystyle\kappa_{+}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{R^{\prime}}\,,

\displaystyle\kappa_{-}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{R}\,.

(A37)

Appendix C
Appendix C: Crosscap state overlap of Jastrow wave functions

In this section, we calculate the overlap between the lattice crosscap state

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle=\prod_{j=1}^{N/2}\left(|\uparrow\rangle_{j}|\uparrow\rangle_{j+N/2}+|\downarrow\rangle_{j}|\downarrow\rangle_{j+N/2}\right)

(A38)

and the Jastrow wave function

\displaystyle|\Psi^{\lambda}(M)\rangle=\sum_{1\leq x_{1}<\cdots<x_{M}\leq N}\Psi^{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})\sigma_{x_{1}}^{+}\cdots\sigma_{x_{M}}^{+}|\downarrow\cdots\downarrow\rangle

(A39)

with

\displaystyle\Psi^{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})=\mathcal{N}_{N,M,\lambda}\,(-1)^{\sum_{l=1}^{M}x_{l}}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M}\left|\sin\frac{\pi(x_{j}-x_{k})}{N}\right|^{\lambda}

(A40)

for
\mathrm{mod}(N,4)=0
. The number of hardcore bosons,
M
, is taken to be even. For
\lambda=1
(
\lambda=2
),
|\Psi^{\lambda}(M)\rangle
is an exact eigenstate of the spin-1/2 XY (Haldane-Shastry) chain.
\mathcal{N}_{N,M,\lambda}
ensures normalization of
|\Psi^{\lambda}(M)\rangle
and is chosen to be positive.

To calculate the overlap
\langle\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}|\Psi^{\lambda}(M)\rangle
, we first introduce a unitary
U=\exp\left[i\tfrac{\pi}{2}\sum_{j:\mathrm{odd}}(\sigma^{z}_{j}+1)\right]
to remove the Marshall sign
(-1)^{\sum_{l=1}^{M}x_{l}}
in
|\Psi^{\lambda}(M)\rangle
:

\displaystyle|\tilde{\Psi}^{\lambda}(M)\rangle=U|\Psi^{\lambda}(M)\rangle

(A41)

with

\displaystyle\tilde{\Psi}^{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}_{N,M,\lambda}\,\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M}\left|\sin\frac{\pi(x_{j}-x_{k})}{N}\right|^{\lambda}\,

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}_{N,M,\lambda}\cdot 2^{-\lambda M(M-1)/2}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M}\left|z_{x_{j}}-z_{x_{k}}\right|^{\lambda}\,

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N,M,\lambda}\,\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M}\left|z_{x_{j}}-z_{x_{k}}\right|^{\lambda}\,,

(A42)

where
z_{x_{j}}=\exp(i\frac{2\pi}{N}x_{j})
and
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N,M,\lambda}=2^{-\lambda M(M-1)/2}\mathcal{N}_{N,M,\lambda}
.

For
\mathrm{mod}(N,4)=0
, it is obvious that the lattice crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle
in Eq. (
A38
) is invariant under the action of
U
,
U|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle=|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle
. The crosscap state overlap is then rewritten as

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}|\Psi^{\lambda}(M)\rangle=\langle\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}|U^{{\dagger}}U|\Psi^{\lambda}(M)\rangle=\langle\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}|\tilde{\Psi}^{\lambda}(M)\rangle\,.

(A43)

For even
M
, the overlap
\langle\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}|\tilde{\Psi}^{\lambda}(M)\rangle
can be simplified as follows:

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}|\tilde{\Psi}^{\lambda}(M)\rangle

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N,M,\lambda}\sum_{1\leq x_{1}<\cdots<x_{M/2}\leq N/2}\Psi^{\lambda}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M/2},x_{1}+N/2,\ldots,x_{M/2}+N/2)

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N,M,\lambda}\sum_{1\leq x_{1}<\cdots<x_{M/2}\leq N/2}\,\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M/2}\left|(z_{x_{j}}-z_{x_{k}})(z_{x_{j+N/2}}-z_{x_{k+N/2}})\right|^{\lambda}\cdot\prod_{1\leq j,k\leq M/2}\left|z_{x_{j}}-z_{x_{k+N/2}}\right|^{\lambda}

\displaystyle=\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N,M,\lambda}\sum_{1\leq x_{1}<\cdots<x_{M/2}\leq N/2}\,\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M/2}\left|z_{x_{j}}-z_{x_{k}}\right|^{2\lambda}\cdot\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M/2}\left|z_{x_{j}}+z_{x_{k}}\right|^{2\lambda}\cdot 2^{\lambda M/2}

\displaystyle=2^{\lambda M/2}\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N,M,\lambda}\sum_{1\leq x_{1}<\cdots<x_{M/2}\leq N/2}\,\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M/2}\left|z^{2}_{x_{j}}-z^{2}_{x_{k}}\right|^{2\lambda}

\displaystyle=2^{\lambda M/2}\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N,M,\lambda}\cdot\frac{1}{|\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N/2,M/2,\lambda}|^{2}}\,.

(A44)

For the last step, we have used
\sum_{1\leq x_{1}<\ldots<x_{M/2}\leq N/2}\,\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M/2}|z_{x_{j}}^{2}-z_{x_{k}}^{2}|^{2}=|\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N/2,M/2,\lambda}|^{-2}
, which is the normalization condition of
|\tilde{\Psi}^{\lambda}(M/2)\rangle
defined on a chain with length
N/2
. Remarkably, the crosscap overlap for the Jastrow wave function
|\Psi^{\lambda}(M)\rangle
defined in Eqs. (
A39
) and (
A40
) is almost solely determined by the normalization factor. Although this interesting result applies to generic
\lambda
, analytical solutions for the normalization factor
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N,M,\lambda}
are available only for
\lambda=1
and
2
(see, e.g., Ref.
Kuramoto and Kato 2009
). For completeness, we provide a self-contained derivation of
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N,M,\lambda}
for
\lambda=1
and
2
below.

C.1
XY chain

For the XY chain with
\lambda=1
, we use the normalization condition
\langle\tilde{\Psi}^{\lambda=1}(M)|\tilde{\Psi}^{\lambda=1}(M)\rangle=1
to obtain

\displaystyle|\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N,M,\lambda=1}|^{-2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq x_{1}<\cdots<x_{M}\leq N}|\Psi^{\lambda=1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq x_{1}<\cdots<x_{M}\leq N}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M}|z_{x_{j}}-z_{x_{k}}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M!}\sum_{1\leq x_{1},\ldots,x_{M}\leq N}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M}|z_{x_{j}}-z_{x_{k}}|^{2}\,.

(A45)

The Jastrow factor
\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq M}(z_{x_{j}}-z_{x_{i}})
can be written as a Vandermonde determinant

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M}(z_{x_{k}}-z_{x_{j}})=\mathrm{det}\begin{pmatrix}1&z_{x_{1}}&\cdots&z_{x_{1}}^{M-1}\\
1&z_{x_{2}}&\cdots&z_{x_{2}}^{M-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&z_{x_{M}}&\cdots&z_{x_{M}}^{M-1}\end{pmatrix}\equiv\mathrm{det}(\mathbb{V})\,,

(A46)

so we obtain

\displaystyle|\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N,M,\lambda=1}|^{-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M!}\sum_{1\leq x_{1},\ldots,x_{M}\leq N}\mathrm{det}(\mathbb{V})\;\mathrm{det}(\mathbb{V^{*}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M!}\sum_{1\leq x_{1},\ldots,x_{M}\leq N}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{M}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)z_{x_{1}}^{\sigma(1)-1}\cdots z_{x_{M}}^{\sigma(M)-1}\sum_{\tau\in S_{M}}\mathrm{sgn}(\tau){(z_{x_{1}}^{*})}^{\tau(1)-1}\cdots{(z_{x_{M}}^{*})}^{\tau(M)-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{M}}\sum_{\tau\in S_{M}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{sgn}(\tau)\left(\sum_{x_{1}=1}^{N}z_{x_{1}}^{\sigma(1)-\tau(1)}\right)\cdots\left(\sum_{x_{M}=1}^{N}z_{x_{M}}^{\sigma(M)-\tau(M)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{M}}\sum_{\tau\in S_{M}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\,\mathrm{sgn}(\tau)\,\left(N\delta_{\sigma(1),\tau(1)}\right)\cdots\left(N\delta_{\sigma(M),\tau(M)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{M}}N^{M}

\displaystyle=N^{M}\,,

(A47)

where we have used the definition of the determinant [
S_{M}
is the permutation of
\{1,2,\ldots,M\}
and
\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)
denotes the signature of the permutation
\sigma
] and the identity
\sum_{x=1}^{N}z_{x}^{k}=N\delta_{k,0}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, we obtain the normalization factor
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N,M,\lambda=1}=N^{-M/2}
. By using this result and Eq. (
A44
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}|\Psi^{\lambda=1}(M)\rangle=2^{M/2}\cdot N^{-M/2}\cdot\frac{1}{[(N/2)^{-M/4}]^{2}}=1\,.

(A48)

C.2
Haldane-Shastry chain

We now turn to the Haldane-Shastry chain (
\lambda=2
) and determine the normalization factor
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N,M,\lambda=2}
. Using the normalization condition
\langle\tilde{\Psi}^{\lambda=2}(M)|\tilde{\Psi}^{\lambda=2}(M)\rangle=1
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N,M,\lambda=2}|^{-2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq x_{1}<\cdots<x_{M}\leq N}|\Psi^{\lambda=2}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{M})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M!}\sum_{1\leq x_{1},\ldots,x_{M}\leq N}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M}|z_{x_{j}}-z_{x_{k}}|^{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M!}\sum_{1\leq x_{1},\ldots,x_{M}\leq N}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M}(z_{x_{j}}-z_{x_{k}})^{4}\frac{1}{z_{x_{j}}^{2}z_{x_{k}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M!}\sum_{1\leq x_{1},\ldots,x_{M}\leq N}\prod_{i=1}^{M}z_{x_{i}}^{-2(M-1)}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M}(z_{x_{j}}-z_{x_{k}})^{4}\,.

(A49)

The product
\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M}(z_{x_{j}}-z_{x_{k}})^{4}
also has a determinant representation called “confluent alternant”
Kuramoto and Kato 2009
:

\displaystyle\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M}(z_{x_{j}}-z_{x_{k}})^{4}=\mathrm{det}\begin{pmatrix}1&z_{x_{1}}&z_{x_{1}}^{2}&z_{x_{1}}^{3}&\cdots&z_{x_{1}}^{2M-1}\\
0&1&2z_{x_{1}}&3z_{x_{1}}^{2}&\cdots&(2M-1)z_{x_{1}}^{2M-2}\\
1&z_{x_{2}}&z_{x_{1}}^{2}&z_{x_{1}}^{3}&\cdots&z_{x_{2}}^{2M-1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
1&z_{x_{M}}&z_{x_{M}}^{2}&z_{x_{M}}^{3}&\cdots&z_{x_{M}}^{2M-1}\\
0&1&2z_{x_{M}}&3z_{x_{M}}^{2}&\cdots&(2M-1)z_{x_{M}}^{2M-2}\end{pmatrix}\equiv\mathrm{det}(\mathbb{M})\,.

(A50)

After expanding the “confluent alternant”
\mathrm{det}(\mathbb{M})
as a summation of permutation terms (definition of the determinant), the last term in Eq. (
A49
) can be calculated as follows:

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\;\sum_{1\leq x_{1},\ldots,x_{M}\leq N}\prod_{i=1}^{M}z_{x_{i}}^{-2(M-1)}\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq M}(z_{x_{j}}-z_{x_{k}})^{4}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq x_{1},\ldots,x_{M}\leq N}\prod_{i=1}^{M}z_{x_{i}}^{-2(M-1)}\;\mathrm{det}(\mathbb{M})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq x_{1},\ldots,x_{M}\leq N}\prod_{i=1}^{M}z_{x_{i}}^{-2(M-1)}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2M}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\,z_{x_{1}}^{\sigma(1)-1}(\sigma(2)-1)z_{x_{1}}^{\sigma(2)-2}\cdots z_{x_{M}}^{\sigma(2M-1)-1}(\sigma(2M)-1)z_{x_{M}}^{\sigma(2M)-2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2M}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{j=1}^{M}(\sigma(2j)-1)\prod_{j=1}^{M}\left(\sum_{x_{j}=1}^{N}z_{x_{j}}^{\sigma(2j-1)+\sigma(2j)-(2M+1)}\right)

\displaystyle=N^{M}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2M}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{j=1}^{M}(\sigma(2j)-1)\prod_{j=1}^{M}\delta_{\sigma(2j-1)+\sigma(2j),2M+1}\,.

(A51)

To evaluate the sum, one observes that nonvanishing contributions come from the permutation elements satisfying
\sigma(2j-1)+\sigma(2j)=2M+1
. All such permutation elements are generated from
\sigma=\{1,2M,2,2M-1,\ldots,M,M+1\}
[which has
\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)=+1
] by using two types of permutations: i)
\sigma(2j-1)\leftrightarrow\sigma(2j)
[which has
\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)=-1
] and ii)
\{\sigma(2j-1),\sigma(2j)\}\leftrightarrow\{\sigma(2l-1),\sigma(2l)\}
[which has
\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)=+1
]. Making use of the first type of permutation, the sum can be restricted to
\sigma(2j-1)<\sigma(2j)\;\forall j=1,\ldots,M
and is then reduced to a simple sum over
\{\sigma(1),\sigma(3),\ldots,\sigma(2M-1)\}\in S_{M}
:

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\;\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2M}}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{j=1}^{M}(\sigma(2j)-1)\prod_{j=1}^{M}\delta_{\sigma(2j-1)+\sigma(2j),2M+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2M},\,\sigma(2j-1)<\sigma(2j)}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{j=1}^{M}[(\sigma(2j)-1)-(\sigma(2j-1)-1)]\prod_{j=1}^{M}\delta_{\sigma(2j-1)+\sigma(2j),2M+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2M},\,\sigma(2j-1)<\sigma(2j)}\mathrm{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{j=1}^{M}[\sigma(2j)-\sigma(2j-1)]\prod_{j=1}^{M}\delta_{\sigma(2j-1)+\sigma(2j),2M+1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\{\sigma(1),\sigma(3),\ldots,\sigma(2M-1)\}\in S_{M}}(+1)\prod_{j=1}^{M}[2M+1-2\,\sigma(2j-1)]

\displaystyle=M!\;(2M-1)(2M-3)\cdots 3\cdot 1

\displaystyle=M!\;\frac{(2M)!}{(2M)(2M-2)\cdots 2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(2M)!}{2^{M}}\,.

(A52)

Combining the above results, we obtain

\displaystyle|\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N,M,\lambda=2}|^{-2}=\frac{1}{M!}\cdot N^{M}\cdot\frac{(2M)!}{2^{M}}=\left(\frac{N}{2}\right)^{M}\frac{(2M)!}{M!}\,.

(A53)

and
\tilde{\mathcal{N}}_{N,M,\lambda=2}=\left(\tfrac{2}{N}\right)^{M/2}\sqrt{\tfrac{M!}{(2M)!}}
. By using this result and Eq. (
A44
), the crosscap state overlap for the Haldane-Shastry chain is written as

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}|\Psi^{\lambda=2}(M)\rangle

\displaystyle=2^{M}\cdot\left(\frac{2}{N}\right)^{M/2}\sqrt{\frac{M!}{(2M)!}}\cdot\left(\frac{N}{4}\right)^{M/2}\frac{M!}{(M/2)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{M/2}(M!)^{3/2}}{(M/2)!\sqrt{(2M)!}}\,.

(A54)

Using the Stirling approximation
M!=\sqrt{2\pi M}\left(\tfrac{M}{e}\right)^{M}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{12M}+\mathcal{O}(M^{-2})\right)
(valid for
M
large), we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}|\Psi^{\lambda=2}(M)\rangle=2^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(1-\frac{1}{16M}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{M^{2}}\right)\right)=2^{\frac{1}{4}}+\mathcal{O}(M^{-1}).

(A55)

Appendix D
Appendix D: Logarithmic correction to the crosscap overlap at the Heisenberg point

In this section, we provide a field theoretical analysis for understanding the finite-size scaling behavior of the overlap between the (lattice) crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle
and the ground state of the spin-1/2 Heisenberg chain. In particular, we focus on the influence of the marginally irrelevant term.

The effective theory of the spin-1/2 Heisenberg chain is the
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{1}
Wess-Zumino-Witten (WZW) CFT, with an SU(2) symmetric
marginal
perturbation:

\displaystyle H=H_{0}+H_{1}

(A56)

with

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{v}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\left[\frac{1}{3}:\mathbf{J}\cdot\mathbf{J}:(x)+\frac{1}{3}:\bar{\mathbf{J}}\cdot\bar{\mathbf{J}}:(x)\right]-\frac{2\pi v}{L}\frac{c}{12}\,,

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=-vg\int_{0}^{L}\mathrm{d}x\;\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}:\mathbf{J}\cdot\bar{\mathbf{J}}:(x)\,,

(A57)

where
\mathbf{J}=(J^{0},J^{1},J^{2})
and
\bar{\mathbf{J}}=(\bar{J}^{0},\bar{J}^{1},\bar{J}^{2})
are the
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{1}
Kac-Moody chiral and anti-chiral currents, respectively. Here,
v
is the velocity,
c=1
is the central charge, and
g
is the coupling of the marginal term.

In the free-field representation, the
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{1}
Kac-Moody currents are expressed in terms of the compactified boson field
\hat{\varphi}=\phi+\bar{\phi}
with compactification radius
R=\sqrt{2}
:

\displaystyle J^{0}(w)

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\partial\phi(w)\,,\quad J^{\pm}(w)=J^{1}(w)\pm iJ^{2}(w)=:e^{\pm i\sqrt{2}\phi(w)}:\,,

\displaystyle\bar{J}^{0}(\bar{w})

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\bar{\partial}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w})\,,\quad\bar{J}^{\pm}(\bar{w})=\bar{J}^{1}(\bar{w})\pm i\bar{J}^{2}(\bar{w})=:e^{\pm i\sqrt{2}\bar{\phi}(\bar{w})}:\,,

(A58)

where
w=e^{\frac{2\pi}{L}(v\tau+ix)}
is the complex coordinate on the plane.
The unperturbed Hamiltonian
H_{0}
, corresponding to the
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{1}
WZW CFT, represents a Tomonaga-Luttinger liquid with Luttinger parameter
K=R^{2}/4=1/2
. The effective coupling
g>0
in
H_{1}
can be understood from the bosonization treatment of the spin-1/2 Heisenberg chain,
S_{j}^{z}S_{j+1}^{z}\sim(\partial_{x}\hat{\vartheta}(x))^{2}:-\alpha^{2}\left(\frac{2\pi}{L}\right)^{2}:\cos[\sqrt{2}\hat{\vartheta}(x)]:
, with
\alpha
being a non-universal constant.

Denoting the ground state of effective Hamiltonian [Eq. (
A56
)] as
|\Psi_{0}(g)\rangle
, its overlap with the conformal crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{+}\rangle
[Eq. (
A25
) with
\kappa_{+}=2^{1/4}
for the
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{1}
WZW CFT], which has been identified as the continuum counterpart of the lattice crosscap state
|\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}\rangle
, should be a universal scaling function of
g
. This overlap, denoted as
|\langle\mathcal{C}_{+}|\Psi_{0}(g)\rangle|=F(g)
, can be analytically calculated using the conformal perturbation theory
Saleur and Itzykson 1987
;
Zhang et al.
. Using the conformal perturbation formalism developed in Ref.
Zhang et al.
, the leading correction to the overlap is given by

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{C}_{+}|\Psi_{0}(g)\rangle|\equiv F(g)=2^{1/4}+\frac{\pi\mathcal{A}}{2}\cdot g+\mathcal{O}\left(g^{2}\right)\,,

(A59)

where
\mathcal{A}=-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}
is the amplitude of the one-point crosscap correlator

\displaystyle\langle 0,0|\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}:\mathbf{J}\cdot\bar{\mathbf{J}}:(w,\bar{w})|\mathcal{C}_{+}\rangle=-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\cdot\frac{1}{(1+|w|^{2})^{2}}\equiv\frac{\mathcal{A}}{(1+|w|^{2})^{2}}\,.

(A60)

Here
|0,0\rangle
is the vacuum of the
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{1}
WZW CFT.

The perturbation operator
\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}:\mathbf{J}\cdot\bar{\mathbf{J}}:
in Eq. (
A57
), with conformal weight
h=\bar{h}=1
, is marginal at first order. The one-loop renormalization group (RG) equation, derived by considering an infinitesimal scale transformation
a\to(1+\delta l)a
(
a
: short distance cutoff at a given scale) shows that the perturbation is indeed marginal irrelevant
Cardy 1986
;
Affleck et al. 1989
:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}g}{\mathrm{d}l}=-\pi bg^{2}+\mathcal{O}(g^{3})\,,

(A61)

where
b=4/\sqrt{3}
is determined by the three-point correlator on the plane

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}:\mathbf{J}\cdot\bar{\mathbf{J}}:(w_{1},\bar{w}_{1})\cdot\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}:\mathbf{J}\cdot\bar{\mathbf{J}}:(w_{2},\bar{w}_{2})\cdot\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}:\mathbf{J}\cdot\bar{\mathbf{J}}:(w_{3},\bar{w}_{3})\right\rangle=\frac{-b}{|w_{1}-w_{2}|^{2}|w_{1}-w_{3}|^{2}|w_{2}-w_{3}|^{2}}\,.

(A62)

To account for the finite-size correction arising from the marginally irrelevant perturbation, we consider a spin-1/2 Heisenberg chain with
N
sites and lattice spacing
a
(short-distance cutoff at the ultraviolet scale), with periodic boundary condition imposed. The RG flow is from a theory at the ultraviolet (UV) scale with microscopic coupling
g_{\mathrm{uv}}
to its low-energy effective description [Eq. (
A56
)] at the infrared (IR) scale with effective coupling
g
. The length of the system
L=Na=N^{\prime}a^{\prime}
is fixed under the RG flow,
e^{l}=\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}=\frac{N}{N^{\prime}}
, where
a^{\prime}
is the short-distance cutoff at the IR scale. Solving the RG equation [Eq. (
A61
)] gives the effective coupling
g
as the function of
N
:

\displaystyle g\equiv g(l)=\frac{g_{\mathrm{uv}}}{1+\pi bg_{\mathrm{uv}}l}=\frac{g_{\mathrm{uv}}}{1+\pi bg_{\mathrm{uv}}\ln(N/N^{\prime})}\,.

(A63)

Specifically, in the limit
\ln N\gg 1/g_{\mathrm{uv}}
, the asymptotic finite-size scaling behavior of the effective coupling
g
is independent of microscopic details
Cardy 1986
;
Affleck et al. 1989
:

\displaystyle g\to\frac{1}{\pi b\ln N}\,.

(A64)

This leads to the universal marginal correction of the crosscap overlap [Eq. (
A59
)]:

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{C}_{+}|\Psi_{0}(g)\rangle|=F(g)\to F(1/\pi b\ln N)=2^{1/4}-\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{\ln N}+\mathcal{O}\left(1/(\ln N)^{2}\right)\,.

(A65)

As discussed above, the universal finite-size correction due to the marginal term is valid for
\ln N\gg 1/g_{\mathrm{uv}}
. However, in lattice simulations, the total number of sites
N
is typically not sufficiently large to reach that regime. Nevertheless, the form of Eq. (
A59
) suggests that the lattice crosscap overlap can be expanded in terms of
1/\ln N
, i.e.,
|\langle\mathcal{C}_{\mathrm{latt}}|\Psi_{0}(N)\rangle|=a_{1}+a_{2}/\ln N
, where
|\Psi_{0}(N)\rangle
is the ground state of the Heisenberg chain and
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
are fitting parameters. The fitted Luttinger parameter is given by
K=1/a_{1}^{4}
(theoretical value for the
\mathrm{SU}(2)_{1}
WZW CFT:
K=1/2
).

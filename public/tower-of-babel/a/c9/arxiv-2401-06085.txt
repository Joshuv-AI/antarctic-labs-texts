On the stabilizer of the graph of linear functions over finite fields
Valentino Smaldore, Corrado Zanella, Ferdinando Zullo

Abstract
In this paper we will study the action of 𝔽 q n 2 × 2 \mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{2\times 2} on the graph of an 𝔽 q {{\mathbb{F}}_{q}} -linear function of 𝔽 q n {{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}} into itself. In particular we will see that, under certain combinatorial assumptions, its stabilizer (together with the sum and product of matrices) is a field. We will also see some examples for which this does not happen. Moreover, we will establish a connection between such a stabilizer and the right idealizer of the rank-metric code defined by the linear function and give some structural results in the case in which the polynomials are partially scattered.



Introduction

Let
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, the
graph
of
f
is defined as the following set of affine points

\mathcal{G}_{f}=\{(y,f(y))\colon y\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}\}\subseteq\mathrm{AG}(2,q).

We can see the projective plane
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
as the union of
\mathrm{AG}(2,q)
and the line at infinity
\ell_{\infty}
.
In coordinates, the points of
\mathrm{PG}(2,q)
are of the form
\langle(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}
for some
(x_{0},x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{3}\setminus\{(0,0,0)\}
and we may assume that
\ell_{\infty}
is the set of points defined by the vectors with last component equal to zero and so the points in
\mathrm{AG}(2,q)
are those of the form
\langle(a,b,1)\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}
for some
a,b\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, which in
\mathrm{AG}(2,q)
is defined by the pair
(a,b)
.

The set of
directions
of
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
is defined as

\mathcal{D}_{f}=\{PQ\cap\ell_{\infty}\colon P,Q\in\mathcal{G}_{f},\,\,\,P\neq Q\},

where
PQ
denotes the line through the points
P
and
Q
.
Note that

\mathcal{D}_{f}=\{\langle(1,m,0)\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}\colon m\in D_{f}\},

where
D_{f}
is the set of slopes of the lines used in
\mathcal{D}_{f}
, that is

D_{f}=\left\{\frac{f(y)-f(z)}{y-z}\colon y,z\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}},\,\,\,y\neq z\right\}.

Combinatorial conditions on
\mathcal{G}_{f}
and/or
\mathcal{D}_{f}
can give algebraic properties on
f
; see for instance the well-celebrated results in
[
1
,
2
]
where some conditions on the intersections between
\mathcal{G}_{f}
and the affine lines together with bounds on the number of directions yield some linearity conditions on
f
.
In this paper we will mainly consider the case in which
f\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}[x]
and it is an
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-linear function, i.e.
f
is a linearized polynomial.
In this case,

\mathcal{D}_{f}=\{\langle(y,f(y),0)\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}\colon y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\}

and

D_{f}=\left\{\frac{f(y)}{y}\colon y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\right\}.

Since
f
is
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-linear,
the affine lines meet
\mathcal{G}_{f}
in either zero points or in a power of
q
points.

In this paper we investigate the natural action of the group
(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{2\times 2},+)
on
\mathcal{G}_{f}
by considering the set

\mathbb{S}_{f}=\{A\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{2\times 2}\colon A\mathcal{G}_{f}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{f}\},

where
A\mathcal{G}_{f}=\left\{A\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}y\\
f(y)\end{array}\right)\colon y\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}\right\}
.
Clearly
\mathbb{S}_{f}
, together with
+
and
\cdot
the usual sum and product of matrices in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{2\times 2}
and
\star
the multiplication by a scalar in
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
, forms an
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-algebra.

Section
1
provides the necessary notions related to linearized polynomials, scattered and partially scattered polynomials, linear sets, their relations with the graph of a linear function, and rank-metric codes.

In Section
2
we will prove that when
f
has
low weight
, that is if for every affine line
\ell

|\ell\cap\mathcal{G}_{f}|<q^{n/2},

(1)

then
(\mathbb{S}_{f},+,\cdot)
is a field.
Counterexamples in the case that
f
has not low weight are added.

In Section
3
the set
\mathbb{S}_{f}
will be determined for many
classes of linearized polynomials, containing all known scattered polynomials.
Proposition
3.4
shows that the partially scattered polynomials are almost of low weight.
The
\operatorname{L-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered polynomials
f
such that
\mathbb{S}_{f}
is not a field are then completely characterized.
As regards
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered polynomials, a class of polynomials is exhibited such that
\mathbb{S}_{f}
is not a field.

In Section
4
, it is proved that the right idealizer of the rank distance code
\mathcal{C}_{f}
associated with a linearized polynomial
f

is isomorphic to
\mathbb{S}_{f}
.
This implies that many of the results in this paper can be translated
in terms of rank distance codes.

1
Preliminaries

1.1
Linearized polynomials, scattered and partially scattered polynomials

Let
q
be a prime power, and
n
a positive integer.
A
q
-polynomial
(or
linearized polynomial
) over the finite field
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
has form

f=\sum_{i=0}^{k}a_{i}x^{q^{i}}\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}[x]
;
if
a_{k}\neq 0
then the
q
-degree
of
f
is
k
.
The set of linearized polynomials over
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
will be denoted as
L_{n,q}
.
Such set, equipped with the operations of sum and multiplication by elements of
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
and the composition, results to be an
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-algebra.
The quotient algebra
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}=L_{n,q}/(x^{q^{n}}-x)
has the property that its elements are in one-to-one correspondence with the
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-linear
endomorphisms of
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
.
Note that we can identify the elements of
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
with the
q
-polynomials having
q
-degree smaller than
n
.

Following
[
14
]
, let
f
be a
q
-linearized polynomial over
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
,
t
a divisor of
n
such that
1<t<n
, so that
n=tt^{\prime}
.
We say that
f
is
\operatorname{L-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered
if for any
y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
,

\frac{f(y)}{y}=\frac{f(z)}{z}\Longrightarrow\frac{y}{z}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}},

(2)

and that
f
is
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered
if for any

y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
,

\frac{f(y)}{y}=\frac{f(z)}{z}\,\mbox{ and }\,\frac{y}{z}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}\Longrightarrow\frac{y}{z}\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}.

(3)

A polynomial
f
which is both
\operatorname{L-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered and
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered is called
scattered
(see
[
23
]
).

Let
f
and
g
be two linearized polynomials over
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
and consider the two related graphs
\mathcal{G}_{f}
and
\mathcal{G}_{g}
in AG
(2,q^{n})
.
We say that
f
and
g
are
equivalent
if there exists
\varphi\in\mathrm{\Gamma L}(2,q^{n})
such that
\varphi(\mathcal{G}_{f})=\mathcal{G}_{g}
, that is, there exist
A\in\mathrm{GL}(2,q^{n})
and
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}({{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}})
with the property that for each
x\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
there exists
y\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
satisfying

A\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}x^{\sigma}\\
f(x)^{\sigma}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}y\\
g(y)\end{array}\right),

see
[
3
, Section 1]
and
[
6
, Section 1]
.

This definition of equivalence preserves the property of being
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
and
\operatorname{L-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered:

Proposition 1.1
.

[
4
, Proposition 7.1]

Let
f
and
g
be two equivalent
q
-polynomials in
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
.
If
f
is
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered (resp.
\operatorname{L-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered),
then
g
is
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered (resp.
\operatorname{L-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered).

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{G}_{f}
and
\mathcal{G}_{g}
are
\mathrm{\Gamma L}(2,q^{n})
-equivalent,
\mathcal{G}_{f}
and
\mathcal{G}_{g}
are also
\mathrm{\Gamma L}(2t^{\prime},q^{t})
-equivalent. If
f
is
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered, by
[
14
, Theorem 2.2]
,
\mathcal{G}_{f}
is a scattered subspace of
\mathrm{PG}(2t^{\prime}-1,q^{t})
. Therefore
\mathcal{G}_{g}
is a scattered subspace of
\mathrm{PG}(2t^{\prime}-1,q^{t})
, and hence
g
is
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered by
[
14
, Theorem 2.2]
. Otherwise, if
f
is
\operatorname{L-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered, by
[
14
, Remark 2.5]
,
\mathcal{G}_{f}
is scattered with respect to a normal
(t^{\prime}-1)
-spread of
\mathrm{PG}(2t^{\prime}-1,q^{t})
. Therefore
\mathcal{G}_{g}
is scattered with respect to a normal
(t^{\prime}-1)
-spread of
\mathrm{PG}(2t^{\prime}-1,q^{t})
, and hence
g
is
\operatorname{L-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered by
[
14
, Remark 2.5]
.
∎

1.2
Linear sets and their relations with the graph of a linear function

Let
V
be an
r
-dimensional
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
-vector space and let
\Lambda=\PG(V,{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}})=\PG(r-1,q^{n})
.
Let
U
be an
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-subspace of
V
such that
\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}(U)=k
, then the set

L_{U}=\{\langle u\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}\colon u\in U\setminus\{0\}\}\subseteq\PG(r-1,q^{n})

is said to be an
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-
linear set
of rank
k
.
The
weight
of a point
P=\langle v\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}\in\Lambda
in
L_{U}
is defined as
w_{L_{U}}(P)=\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}(U\cap\langle v\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}})
.

Furthermore,
L_{U}
is called
scattered
if it has the maximum number
\frac{q^{k}-1}{q-1}
of points or, equivalently, if all points of
L_{U}
have weight one.
Blokhuis and Lavrauw in
[
5
]
proved the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

The rank of a scattered
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-linear set in
\PG(r-1,q^{n})
is
upper bounded by
\frac{rn}{2}
.

When
\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}(U)=n
and
r=2
, then there exists a
q
-polynomial
f\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}[x]
such that
L_{U}
is mapped by a collineation of
\mathrm{PGL}(2,q^{n})
into

L_{f}=L_{\mathcal{G}_{f}}=\{\langle(y,f(y))\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}\colon y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}\}.

Note that the polynomial
f\in\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
is scattered if and only if
L_{f}
is.
We will now describe the connection between linear sets of the projective line and graphs of linear functions.
Indeed, if
f\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}[x]
is an
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-linear function, then
\mathcal{D}_{f}
coincides with the linear set
L_{f}
defined by
f
adding a zero component to each of its points.
Assume that
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{t}\}
is the set of weights of the points with respect to
L_{f}
and let
\ell
be any line in
\mathrm{PG}(2,q^{n})
.
If
\ell
meets
\ell_{\infty}
in a point of
\mathcal{D}_{f}
of weight
j\in\{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{t}\}
then
\ell
meets
\mathcal{G}_{f}
in exactly
q^{j}
points or in zero points.
Moreover, there are exactly
q^{n-i_{j}}
lines hitting
\mathcal{G}_{f}
in
q^{i_{j}}
points for every point of weight
i_{j}
.
If
\ell
meets
\ell_{\infty}
in a point outside
\mathcal{D}_{f}
then
\ell
meets
\mathcal{G}_{f}
in exactly one point. For more details we refer to
[
10
]
.
As a consequence, a low weight polynomial
f
is a polynomial for which the associated linear set
L_{f}
has all points of weight less than
n/2
.
These can give interesting constructions of Hamming metric codes with few weights, see
[
17
]
.

1.3
Rank-metric codes

Rank-metric codes have been originally introduced by Delsarte
[
9
]
in 1978.
They have been intensively investigated in recent years because of their applications; we refer to
[
24
]
for a survey on this topic.
The set
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times n}
of
m\times n
matrices over
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
can be endowed with the
rank-metric
defined by

d(A,B)=\mathrm{rk}\,(A-B).

A subset
\C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times n}
equipped with the rank-metric is called a
rank-metric code
.
The
minimum distance
of
\C
is defined as

d=\min\{d(A,B)\colon A,B\in\C,\,\,A\neq B\}.

We will denote the parameters of a rank-metric code
\C\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times n}
with minimum distance
d
by
(m,n,q;d)
.
Delsarte showed in
[
9
]
that the parameters of these codes must
fulfill a Singleton-like bound.

Theorem 1.3
.

[
9
]

If
\C
is a rank-metric code with parameters
(m,n,q;d)
, then

|\C|\leq q^{\max\{m,n\}(\min\{m,n\}-d+1)}.

When equality holds, we say that
\C
is a
maximum rank distance
(
MRD
for short) code.

We will be mainly interested in
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-
linear
rank-metric codes, that is
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times n}
.
Two
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-linear rank-metric codes
\C
and
\C^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times n}
are
equivalent
if and only if there exist
X\in\mathrm{GL}(m,q)
,
Y\in\mathrm{GL}(n,q)
, and a field automorphism
\sigma
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that

\C^{\prime}=\{XC^{\sigma}Y\colon C\in\C\}.

The
left
and
right idealizers
of a rank-metric code
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times n}
are defined as

L(\C)=\{Y\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times m}\colon YC\in\C\hskip 2.84544pt\text{for all}\hskip 2.84544ptC\in\C\},

R(\C)=\{Z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times n}\colon CZ\in\C\hskip 2.84544pt\text{for all}\hskip 2.84544ptC\in\C\},

respectively.
They are powerful tools to study the equivalence issue among rank-metric codes.
These notions have been introduced by Liebhold and Nebe in
[
12
, Definition 3.1]
; they are invariant under equivalences of rank-metric codes. Further invariants have been introduced in
[
11
,
20
]
.
In
[
16
]
, idealizers have been studied in details (under the name of
middle
and
right nuclei
) and the following result has been proved.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
16
]

Let
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-linear rank-metric codes of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times n}
.

•

If
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
are equivalent, then their left and right idealizers are isomorphic as
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-algebras (
[
16
, Proposition 4.1]
).

•

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an MRD code with minimum distance
d>1
.
If
m\leq n
, then
L(\C)
is a finite field with
|L(\C)|\leq q^{m}
.
If
m\geq n
, then
R(\C)
is a finite field with
|R(\C)|\leq q^{n}
.
In particular, when
m=n
,
L(\C)
and
R(\C)
are both finite fields (
[
16
, Theorem 5.4 and Corollary 5.6]
).

We may see the nonzero elements of an
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-linear rank-metric code
\mathcal{C}
with parameters
(m,n,q;d)
as:

•

matrices of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times n}
having rank at least
d
and with at least one matrix of rank exactly
d
;

•

{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-linear maps
V\to W
where
V=V(n,q)
and
W=V(m,q)
, having usual map rank at least
d
and with at least one map of rank exactly
d
;

•

when
m=n
, elements of the
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-algebra
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
of
q
-polynomials over
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
modulo
x^{q^{n}}-x
, having rank at least
d
and with at least one polynomial of rank exactly
d
.

2
On the stabilizer of low weight polynomials

In this section we will study the action of the group
(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{2\times 2},+)
on the graph of a low weight linearized polynomial
f
, by showing that the stabilizer
\mathbb{S}_{f}
of its graph is a field. The following lemma is clear.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
4
, Lemma 7.2]

Let
f
and
g
be two
q
-polynomials over
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
. If
f
and

g
are equivalent then
\mathbb{S}_{f}
and
\mathbb{S}_{g}
are isomorphic.

We now prove that
\mathbb{S}_{f}
is stable under the sum and the product of matrices.

Proposition 2.2
.

If
A,B\in\mathbb{S}_{f}
, then
A+B,\ AB\in\mathbb{S}_{f}
.

Proof.

Since
A\mathcal{G}_{f}
and
B\mathcal{G}_{f}
are both contained in
\mathcal{G}_{f}
, for any
x\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
there are
y,z\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
such that

A\begin{pmatrix}x\\
f(x)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}y\\
f(y)\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad B\begin{pmatrix}x\\
f(x)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}z\\
f(z)\end{pmatrix},

hence

(A+B)\begin{pmatrix}x\\
f(x)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}y+z\\
f(y)+f(z)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}y+z\\
f(y+z)\end{pmatrix},

so that
A+B\in\mathbb{S}_{f}
.
The same argument can be performed for the product.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
f
be a
q
-polynomial in
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
.
If
f
is a low weight polynomial, then
(\mathbb{S}_{f},+,\cdot)
is a field.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2
,
\mathbb{S}_{f}
is closed under the sum and the product of matrices.
Since
\mathbb{S}_{f}
contains the opposite of any of
its elements and the inverse of its invertible matrices, it
only remains to prove that if
A\in\mathbb{S}_{f}
,
A\neq O
,
then
A\in\mathrm{GL}(2,q^{n})
.
So, it is enough to prove
that for any rank-one
2\times 2
matrix
M
with elements in
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
,

M\mathcal{G}_{f}
is not contained in
\mathcal{G}_{f}
.
To this aim, recall that from Section
1.2
that since
f
is a low weight polynomial,

w_{L_{f}}(\langle v\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}})=\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}(\langle v\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}\cap\mathcal{G}_{f})<n/2
for any
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{2}
.
Let
Z\neq O
be a column such that
MZ=O
and let
C
be a nonzero
column of
M
.

Define
\mu:\mathcal{G}_{f}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{2}
,
(y,f(y))\mapsto M\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}y\\
f(y)\end{array}\right)
.
Since
\ker\mu\subseteq\langle Z\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}\cap\mathcal{G}_{f}
, the
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-dimension
of
\ker\mu
is less than
n/2
since
f
is a low weight polynomial; so,

\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}(\im\mu)>n/2
.

Assume now that
M\mathcal{G}_{f}\subseteq\mathcal{G}_{f}
.
This would imply
\im\mu\subseteq\langle C\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}\cap\mathcal{G}_{f}
,
contradicting
w_{L_{f}}(\langle C\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}})<n/2
.
∎

Theorem
2.3
allows us to find a large class of polynomials
for which
\mathbb{S}_{f}
is a field.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
f
be a
q
-polynomial in
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
.
If
f
has
q
-degree
k
with
1<k<n/2
then it is a low weight polynomial.
In particular
\mathbb{S}_{f}
is a field.

Proof.

By Section
1.2
, we need the prove that the points in
L_{f}
have weight at most
n/2-1
.
Observing that

w_{L_{f}}(\langle(1,m)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\ker(f-mx))

and that the
q
-degree of
f-mx
is at most
n/2-1
, the assertion follows.
∎

As shown in the next examples, Theorem
2.3
cannot be improved in general.

Example 2.5
.

Let
n=tt^{\prime}
,
t^{\prime}\geq 2
, and let
f=\Tr_{q^{n}/q^{t}}(x)=x+x^{q^{t}}+\ldots+x^{q^{n-t}}
be the trace function over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
.
One point of
\mathcal{D}_{f}
has weight
n-t
, and the remaining ones have weight
t
.
Therefore,
f
is not a low weight polynomial.
The matrix
\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}

is an element of
\mathbb{S}_{f}
if and only if for any
x\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
there exists
y\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
such that

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
f(x)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}y\\
f(y)\end{pmatrix},

from which we obtain the following polynomial identity

cx+(d-f(b))\sum_{j=0}^{t^{\prime}-1}x^{q^{tj}}-\sum_{j=0}^{t^{\prime}-1}a^{q^{tj}}x^{q^{tj}}=0.

(4)

From (
4
) in the case
t^{\prime}=2
we only get two equations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}c+d-f(b)-a=0,\\
d-f(b)-a^{q^{t}}=0,\end{array}\right.

which imply that

\mathbb{S}_{f}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\

From (

By comparing the coefficients of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}c=0,\\
d-f(b)-a=0.\end{array}\right.

Hence

0&f(b)+a\end{pmatrix}\colon a\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}},b\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}\right\}.

In both cases,

contains non invertible nonzero matrices and hence it is not a field.

The next example shows that for every divisor

Example 2.6

\mathcal{B}=(\xi_{0},\ldots,\xi_{n-1})
, then

p_{T,S}=\sum_{i=t}^{n-1}\xi_{i}\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(\xi_{i}^{*}x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{i=t}^{n-1}\xi_{i}\cdot\xi_{i}^{*q^{j}}\right)x^{q^{j}}.

We will now show that when
f=p_{T,S}
, for some
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-subspaces
S
and
T
of
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
,
\mathbb{S}_{f}
is not a field. Note that, by definition, projection maps are not low weight polynomials.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
S
and
T
be two
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-subspaces of
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
such that
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}=S\oplus T
and both of dimension greater than zero. Then
\mathbb{S}_{p_{T,S}}
is not a field.

Proof.

Let us start by observing that

p_{S,T}=x-p_{T,S}

(11)

and

p_{T,S}\circ p_{S,T}=0,

(12)

so that the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{S}_{p_{T,S}},

since

\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
p_{T,S}(x)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}x-p_{T,S}(x)\\
0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathcal{G}_{p_{T,S}},

by (
11
) and (
12
).
So,
\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
is a non-invertible matrix in
\mathbb{S}_{p_{T,S}}
.
∎

We will now show examples of R-
q^{n/2}
-partially scattered polynomials whose linear set has exactly two points of weight
n/2
.
To this aim we start with the following definition.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
U
be an
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-subspace of
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}\times{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
and let
t
be a divisor of
n
. Then we say that
U
is
R-
q^{t}
-partially scattered
if and only if for every
u\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}\times{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
we have

\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}(U\cap\langle u\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}})\leq 1.

In particular,
f
is R-
q^{t}
-partially scattered if and only if
U_{f}
is R-
q^{t}
-partially scattered, and so our definition can be seen as a slight extension to the notion of R-
q^{t}
-partially scattered given for linearized polynomials in
[
14
]
.

We show that the following example of linear set with two points of complementary weights in
[
18
, Corollary 4.9]
defines also an R-
q^{t}
-partially scattered subspace.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
s
be a positive integer coprime with
t
,
f=x^{q^{s}}
and
g=\mu x^{q^{s}}
with
\mu\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}^{*}
such that
\N_{q^{t}/q}(\mu)\neq 1
and
\N_{q^{t}/q}(-\xi^{q^{t}+1}\mu)\neq(-1)^{t}
,
with
\xi\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2t}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
.
Let

U=T\times S=\{(v+\xi\mu v^{q^{s}},u+\xi u^{q^{s}})\colon u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}\}.

Then
U
is an R-
q^{t}
-partially scattered.

Proof.

By
[
18
, Corollary 4.9]
, for every point
u\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}\times{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}\setminus\{\langle(1,0)\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}\cup\langle(0,1)\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}\}
we have that

\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}(U\cap\langle u\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}})\leq 1,

and clearly this property holds when replacing
\langle u\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}
by
\langle u\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}
.
Now, assume that
u=(v,0)
for some
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
. By assumptions, we have that
v=v_{0}+\xi v_{1}
for some
v_{0},v_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
and so

\langle(v,0)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}\cap U_{f}=\langle(v,0)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}\cap T\times\{0\},

and hence, since
\mu x^{q^{s}}
is a scattered polynomial,

\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}(\langle(v,0)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}\cap U_{f})=\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}(\langle(v_{0},v_{1})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}\cap\overline{U}_{\mu x^{q^{s}}})\leq 1,

where
\overline{U}_{\mu x^{q^{s}}}=\{(y,\mu y^{q^{s}})\colon y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}\}
.
The same argument can be applied to the case
u=(0,v)
, obtaining the assertion.
∎

Since the equivalence preserves the
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered property, the above example give examples of R-
q^{t}
-partially scattered polynomials by the following result.

Theorem 3.11
.

[
18
, Theorem 4.20]

Let
U
be as in the previous result.
Then
L_{U}
is
\mathrm{PGL}(2,q^{n})
-equivalent to

, where

p=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{t-1}(u_{\ell}+u_{\ell}^{q^{s}}\xi){\lambda_{\ell}^{*}}^{q^{k}}\right)x^{q^{k}},

\{u_{0},\ldots,u_{t-1}\}
is an
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
and
(\lambda_{0}^{*},\ldots,\lambda_{n-1}^{*})
is the dual basis of
(u_{0}+\mu u_{0}^{q^{s}}\xi,\ldots,u_{t-1}+\mu u_{t-1}^{q^{s}}\xi,u_{0}+u_{0}^{q^{s}}\xi,\ldots,u_{t-1}+u_{t-1}^{q^{s}}\xi)
.

Since the property of being R-

-partially scattered is invariant by equivalence, then Theorem

gives an examples of R-
q^{t}
-partially scattered whose associated linear set has exactly two points of weight

, because of Proposition

.
Therefore, plugging together the above results, we have the following.

Corollary 3.12
.

There exist examples of R-
q^{t}
-partially scattered polynomials

with the property that

The polynomial as in Theorem

is R-

∎

4
Right idealizer of two-dimensional linear rank-metric codes

When
m=n
,
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-linear rank-metric codes can be studied in terms of linearized polynomials.
Indeed, an
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-linear rank-metric code
\mathcal{C}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
{\mathcal{L}}_{n,q}
endowed with the rank-metric and all the notions already given for rank-metric codes can be read in this context.
Indeed, two
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-linear rank-metric codes
\C_{1},\C_{2}\subseteq{\mathcal{L}}_{n,q}
are
equivalent
if there exist two invertible linearized polynomials
g,h\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n,q}
and a field automorphism
\rho\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
such that

\C_{1}=g\circ\C_{2}^{\rho}\circ h=\{g\circ{f^{\rho}}\circ h:f\in\C_{2}\},

where
f^{\rho}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\rho(f_{i})x^{q^{i}}
if
f=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}f_{i}x^{q^{i}}
.
In addition, for an
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-linear rank-metric code
\C
in
{\mathcal{L}}_{n,q}
, the set

L(\C)=\{g\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n,q}:g\circ f\in\C,\,\,\,\text{for any}\,\,\,f\in\C\}

corresponds to the
left idealizer
of
\C
and

R(\C)=\{g\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n,q}:f\circ g\in\C,\,\,\,\text{for any}\,\,\,f\in\C\}

corresponds to the
right idealizer
of
\C
.
We say that a linear rank-metric code
\C
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-
linear rank-metric code
if
(L(\C),+,\circ)
, where
+
and
\circ
are the sum and the composition of linearized polynomials respectively, contains a subring isomorphic to

\mathcal{F}_{n}=\{\alpha x:\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\},

which is an
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-algebra isomorphic to
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
.
As shown in
[
21
, Remark III.3]
, if
\C
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-linear rank-metric code in
{\mathcal{L}}_{n,q}
then it is equivalent to an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-linear rank-metric code
\C^{\prime}
that is also an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-subspace of
{\mathcal{L}}_{n,q}
.
For any linearized polynomial
f\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n,q}
, we can consider the following
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
-linear rank-metric code

\mathcal{C}_{f}=\langle x,f\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}.

Sheekey in
[
23
]
pointed out that
\C_{f}
is an MRD code if and only if
f
is a scattered polynomial.
When
\C_{f}
is an MRD code, we already know that its right idealizer is a field, cf. Theorem
1.4
. In the next result we will see that we can relax the condition of being MRD codes when considering two-dimensional
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
-linear rank-metric codes.
To this aim we first prove a relation between
\mathbb{S}_{f}
and
R(\C_{f})
, extending
[
13
, Lemma 4.1]
where the result was proved under the assumption that
f
is scattered.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f\in{\mathcal{L}}_{n,q}
and denote by
\C_{f}
the associated rank-metric code in
{\mathcal{L}}_{n,q}
.
Suppose that
f\notin\langle x\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}
.
Then the
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-algebras
\mathbb{S}_{f}
and
R(\C_{f})
are isomorphic.

Proof.

We claim that the isomorphism is

\psi\colon\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\mapsto ax+bf.

Let
A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{S}_{f}
, and define

g_{A}=ax+bf
.
The condition
A\in\mathbb{S}_{f}
implies
f\circ g_{A}=cx+df\in\C_{f}
.
In combination with
x\circ g_{A}\in\C_{f}
, this implies

g_{A}\in R(\C_{f})
.
So,
\psi
maps
\mathbb{S}_{f}
into
R(\C_{f})
.
Conversely, for any
g\in R(\C_{f})
there are unique
a,b,c,d\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
such that

g=ax+bf\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,f\circ g=cx+df.

Therefore

\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
f(x)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}y\\
f(y)\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{for }y=g(x).

This implies that
\psi
is bijective.

As regards the fact that
\psi
preserves the algebra operations, the only nontrivial check remaining is
g_{AA^{\prime}}=g_{A}\circ g_{A^{\prime}}
for any
A,A^{\prime}\in\mathbb{S}_{f}
.
Let
A
be as above, and
A^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}.

Then

\displaystyle g_{AA^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=(aa^{\prime}+bc^{\prime})x+(ab^{\prime}+bd^{\prime})f=

\displaystyle=ag_{A^{\prime}}+b(c^{\prime}x+d^{\prime}f)=ag_{A^{\prime}}+bf\circ g_{A^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle=g_{A}\circ g_{A^{\prime}}

as desired.
∎

As a consequence we obtain the following.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
f
be a linearized polynomial in
\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
.
If
d(\C_{f})>n/2
, then
R(\C_{f})
is a field.

Proof.

By
[
22
, Theorem 2]
it follows that
w_{L_{f}}(P)<n/2
for any point
P
, that is
f
has low weight, and the assertion then follows from Theorems
2.3
and
4.1
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

The above corollary is part of
[
16
, Lemma 4.4]
.

Example 4.4
.

Let
n=tt^{\prime}
with
t^{\prime}>2
, and let
f=\mathrm{Tr}_{q^{n}/q^{t}}(x)=x+x^{q^{t}}+\ldots+x^{q^{n-t}}
. Thanks to the isomorphism
\psi
provided in the proof of Theorem
4.1
and Example
2.5
, the right idealizer of
\mathcal{C}_{f}
is

R(\mathcal{C}_{f})=\{ax+bf\colon a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n_{t^{\prime}}}},b\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}\},

where
n_{t^{\prime}}=t
if
t^{\prime}>2
and
n_{t^{\prime}}=n
if
t^{\prime}=2
.
Such a

is not a field because
\mathbb{S}_{f}
is not.

4.1
The right idealizer of the MRD codes associated with partially scattered polynomials

In Theorem 3.3 of
[
14
]
, the authors showed that if
n=tt^{\prime}
and
f\in\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
is an R-
q^{t}
-partially scattered polynomial then

\tilde{\C}_{f}=\{F_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}\colon\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}\rightarrow{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}\colon F\in\C_{f}\}

is an MRD code with parameters
(n,t,q;t-1)
The left idealizer
L(\tilde{\C}_{f})
contains a copy of
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
and, since
t\leq n
,
R(\tilde{\C}_{f})
is a field with
|R(\tilde{\C}_{f})|\leq q^{t}
by Theorem
1.4
.

In the next result we find a relation between the right idealizer of
\C_{f}
and those of
\tilde{\C}_{f}
.
In order to do so, we need some preliminary results.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
with
f\notin\langle x\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}
and such that
f
is
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered. Then every
h\in\mathcal{C}_{f}\setminus\langle x\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}
is an
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered polynomial.

Proof.

Suppose that
h=ax+bf
, for some
a,b\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
with
b\neq 0
. Suppose that for some
y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
with
y/z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
we have that
h(y)/y=h(z)/z
, then

\frac{ay+bf(y)}{y}=\frac{az+bf(z)}{z}

implies
f(y)/y=f(z)/z
and, since
f
is
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered we have that
y/z\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
with
f\notin\langle x\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}
and such that
f
is
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered. Then
h=h^{\prime}
for any
h,h^{\prime}\in\C_{f}
such that
h_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}=h^{\prime}_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}
(that is, they coincide when evaluating over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
).

Proof.

Let
g=h-h^{\prime}=ax+bf
, for some
a,b\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}
. Since for any
y,z\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}^{*}
we have that
g(y)/y=g(z)/z
then
g
is not
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered. So, the above lemma,we have that
b=0
and

g=h-h^{\prime}=ax.

In particular,
h(1)-h^{\prime}(1)=a
and this is zero, because of the assumptions.
∎

The next lemma is easy to check.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
with
f\notin\langle x\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}
and such that
f
is
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered. If
\ell\in R(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}_{f})
then there exists
m\in\mathcal{C}_{f}
such that
l=m_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}
.

We are now ready to establish a relation between the right idealizers of the codes
\C_{f}
and
\tilde{\C}_{f}
. To this aim we will need the following notation.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{t,n,q}=\{g\in\mathcal{L}_{n,q}\mid g(\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}})=\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}\}
be the
{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
-vector space of the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-endomorphisms of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
which fix setwise
\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
. Then consider the equivalence relation
\approx
, such that
g\approx g^{\prime}
if and only if
g_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}=g^{\prime}_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}
. Then the projection map

\tilde{\pi}:\mathcal{L}_{n,q}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}_{n,q}/\approx,

maps
\mathcal{L}_{t,n,q}
onto a vector space isomorphic to
\mathcal{L}_{t,q}
, and let

\Phi:\tilde{\pi}(g)\in\mathcal{L}_{t,n,q}/\approx\longrightarrow g_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}\in\mathcal{L}_{t,q}.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
with
f\notin\langle x\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}
and such that
f
is
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered.
Consider

\C_{f}=\langle x,f\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{n,q}

and

\tilde{\C}_{f}=\{F_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}\colon\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}\rightarrow{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}\colon F\in\C_{f}\}\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}},{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}).

Then

(\Phi\circ\tilde{\pi})(R(\C_{f})\cap\mathcal{L}_{t,n,q})\subseteq R(\tilde{\C}_{f}).

Proof.

Let
g\in R(\C_{f})\cap\mathcal{L}_{t,n,q}
, so that
\Phi(\tilde{\pi}(g))=g_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}
.
We show that for any
k\in\tilde{\C}_{f}
we have that
k\circ g_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}\in\tilde{\C}_{f}
.
Let
k\in\tilde{\C}_{f}
, and
h\in\C_{f}
such that
k=h_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}
. Hence,

k\circ g_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}=k_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}\circ g_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}=h_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}\circ g_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}=h\circ g_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}},

and since
h\circ g\in\C_{f}
,
k\circ(g_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}})\in\tilde{\C}_{f}
.

∎

Note that since
f
is
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered, the restriction of
\Phi\circ\tilde{\pi}
to
R(\C_{f})\cap\mathcal{L}_{t,n,q}
is injective. In fact, for any
h\in\C_{f}\setminus\{0\}
,
\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}(\ker(h)\cap\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}})\leq 1
and for any
k\in\tilde{\C}_{f}\setminus\{0\}
,
\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}}(\ker(k))\leq 1
. A corollary of this property is the following.

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{L}_{n,q}
with
f\notin\langle x\rangle_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}}
and such that
f
is
\operatorname{R-}q^{t}\operatorname{-}
partially scattered. Then

|R(\tilde{\C}_{f})|\geq|\mathcal{L}_{t,n,q}\cap R(\C_{f})|.

(13)

We will now see some examples in which we compute the two cardinalities.

Example 4.10
.

Let consider

with

and

.
It is easy to see that

. It holds
4.8
,

(\Phi\circ\tilde{\pi})(\mathcal{L}_{t,n,q}\cap R(\C_{f}))=\{\alpha{x}_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}\colon\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}\}.

Furthermore,

=\{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{t,q}\colon(ax+bx^{q^{s}})_{|\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}}\circ\ell\in\tilde{\C}_{f},\,\,\forall a,b\in{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{n}}}\},

4.7

\alpha^{q^{t}}x+\beta^{q^{t}}x^{q^{s}}=\alpha x+\beta x^{q^{s}}.

Next,

(14)

) is equivalent to ask that for every

.

Example 4.11

Let consider

Let

such that

Since

\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
. For any
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
there exist
c,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that

a\alpha x+b\alpha^{q^{s}}x^{q^{s}}+b\delta\alpha^{q^{s(t-1)}}x^{q^{s(t-1)}}=cx+dx^{q^{s}}+d\delta x^{q^{s(t-1)}},

for any
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
.
From the above identity we get

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}c=a\alpha,\\
d=b\alpha^{q^{s}},\\
d=b\alpha^{q^{s(t-1)}},\end{array}\right.

from which we get
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{\gcd(t,2)}}
.
Hence,

R(\tilde{\C}_{f})=\{\alpha x\colon\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{\gcd(t,2)}}\}.

Arguing similarly, but with more involved computations, one gets the same conclusion for the case
\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{t}}
.

In both examples, apart from the case
t=2
, we have that
R(\tilde{\C}_{f})
and
\mathcal{L}_{t,n,q}\cap R(\C_{f})
are isomorphic fields.

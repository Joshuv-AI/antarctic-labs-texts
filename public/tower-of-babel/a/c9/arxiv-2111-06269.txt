Abstract
. We analyze the inversion of the photo-acoustic imaging modality using electromagnetic plasmonic nano-particles as contrast agents. We prove that the generated pressure, before and after injecting the plasmonic nano-particles, measured at a single point, located away from the targeted inhomogeneity to image, and at a given band of incident frequencies is enough to reconstruct the (eventually complex valued) permittivity. Indeed, from these measurements, we define an indicator function which depends on the used incident frequency and the time of measurement. This indicator function has differentiating behaviors in terms of both time and frequency. First, from the behavior in terms of time, we can estimate the arrival time of the pressure from which we can localize the injected nano-particle. Second, we show that this indicator function has maximum picks at incident frequencies close to the plasmonic resonances. This allows us to estimate these resonances from which we construct the permittivity. To justify these properties, we derive the dominant electric field created by the injected nano-particle when the incident frequency is close to plasmonic resonances. This is done for the full Maxwell system. To this end, we use a natural spectral decomposition of the vector space ( L 2 ​ ( D ) ) 3 (L^{2}(D))^{3} based on the spectra of the Newtonian and the Magnetization operators. As another key argument, we provide the singularity analysis of the Green’s tensor of the Maxwell problem with varying permittivity. Such singularity is unusual if compared to the ones of the elliptic case (as the acoustic or elastic models). In addition, we show how the derived approximation of the electric fields propagates, as a source, in the induced pressure with the time.

1.1.
Introduction

The photo-acoustic experiment, in the general setting, applies to targets that are electrically conducting, in other words the imaginary part of the ’permittivity’ is quite pronounce, and it goes as follows. Exciting the target, with laser, or by sending an incident electric field, will create heat in its surrounding. This heat, in its turn, creates fluctuations, i.e. a pressure field, that propagates along the body to image. This pressure can be collected in an accessible part of the boundary of the target. The photo-acoustic imaging is to trace back the pressure and reconstruct the permittivity that created it.

In our settings, the source of the heat is given by the injected electromagnetic nano-particles. To describe the mathematical model behind this experiment, let us set

\dfrac{1}{c^{2}}\dfrac{\partial^{2}p}{\partial t^{2}}-\Delta p=\rho_{0}\;\beta_{0}\;\dfrac{\partial^{2}\bm{T}}{\partial t^{2}},\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}_{+},\end{array}\right.

We have two classes of such nano-particles: dielectric and plasmonic nano-particles. The dielectric nano-particles enjoy the following features. They are highly localized as they are nano-scaled and they have high contrast permittivity. Under these scales, we can choose the incident frequency so that we excite the dielectric resonances which are related to the eigenvalues of the Newtonian operator. The main feature of the plasmonic nano-particles is that they enjoy negative values of the real part of their permittivity if we choose incident frequencies close to the plasmonic frequencies of the nano-particle. With such negative permittivity, we can excite the plasmonic resonances which are related to the eigenvalues of the Magnetization operator. , see
(\ref{RD})

, see
(\ref{RDIj})

(1.16)

\nabla M(I)(x)=\frac{1}{3}\,I,\quad x\in B.

Therefore,
\frac{1}{3}
is the only eigenvalue for which the corresponding eigenfunctions might have non-zero average. In addition, using the fact that
e^{(3)}_{n}=\nabla SL(u_{n})
, where
SL
is the Single Layer operator and
u_{n}
are the eigenfunctions of the Double Layer operator, which are computed explicitly in several references, see for example
[

(1.17)

\int_{B}e^{(3)}_{n}(x)\,dx=\frac{2}{9}\,\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{3}}\,\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\\
1\end{pmatrix}\delta_{n,1},

where
\delta_{n,1}
is the Kronecker symbol.

Combining
(\ref{NablaMIBall}),(\ref{IntBen3})
and
(\ref{LambdaInt=})
we end up with
\lambda^{(3)}_{1}=\frac{1}{3}
, which is the first eigenvalue of the Magnetization operator
\nabla M(\cdot)
in the unit ball, and the corresponding eigenfunctions have non-zero average.

Hence, in the imaging procedure described above, using a nanoparticle with a spherical shape, the functional

I_{z}(\omega,\gamma):=|p^{\star}(x,s,\omega)-p_{0}^{\star}(x,s,\omega)|

has one and only one pick
in the square
\left(\omega_{0};\penalty\ \penalty\ \sqrt{\omega^{2}_{p}+\omega^{2}_{0}}:=\omega_{max}\right)\times\left(0;\penalty\ \penalty\ \omega_{max}\,\|\frac{{\mathrm{Im\,}}\left(\epsilon_{0}(\cdot)\right)}{{\mathrm{Re\,}}\left(\epsilon_{0}(\cdot)\right)}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}:=\gamma_{max}\right)
.

Remark 1.2

Remark 1.3
.

In our analysis, as we have seen, it is mandatory to vary both the incident frequencies
\omega
and the damping frequencies
\gamma
. This is can turn out to be expensive from the point of view of applications as it would mean that one should change the nano-particles to change
\gamma
. In the following, we give two ways to overcome this eventual issue.

(1)

In the case where
{\mathrm{Im\,}}\left(\epsilon_{0}(z)\right)
is very small, or mathematically zero, then we can take the damping frequency
\gamma
small as well but fixed. In this case, we only need to vary the incident frequency. Observe that the traditional photo-acoustic experiment applies only to electrically highly conducting tissues. However, the photo-acoustic experiment based on using contrast agents can deal with non-conductive tissues as well (as for benign or early stage tumors).

(2)

Instead of varying both the incident and damping frequencies, we allow the frequencies
\omega
to be in the complex plan. In this case, we can derive similar reconstruction formulas.

More details can be found in Remark
5.8
.

\displaystyle F(\cdot,z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\alpha(\cdot)-\alpha_{\infty}\right)}{\alpha_{\infty}}\;Curl\circ Curl\left(\Phi_{k}\,I\right)(\cdot,z)+\frac{\left(\alpha_{\infty}-\alpha(\cdot)\right)}{\alpha_{\infty}}\underset{z}{\delta}(\cdot)\,I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\alpha(\cdot)-\alpha_{\infty}\right)}{\alpha_{\infty}}\;Curl\circ Curl\left(\Phi_{k}\,I\right)(\cdot,z)+\frac{\left(\epsilon_{\infty}-\epsilon_{0}(z)\right)}{\epsilon_{\infty}}\underset{z}{\delta}(\cdot)\,I

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\alpha_{\infty}}\;\left[Curl\left(\left(\alpha(\cdot)-\alpha_{\infty}\right)Curl\left(\Phi_{k}\,I\right)(\cdot,z)\right)-\nabla\alpha(\cdot)\times Curl\left(\Phi_{k}\,I\right)(\cdot,z)\right]+\frac{\left(\epsilon_{\infty}-\epsilon_{0}(z)\right)}{\epsilon_{\infty}}\underset{z}{\delta}(\cdot)\,I.

This allows to write
F(\cdot,z)
as

(3.6)

F(\cdot,z)=Curl(f)(\cdot,z)+g(\cdot,z)+\frac{\left(\epsilon_{\infty}-\epsilon_{0}(z)\right)}{\epsilon_{\infty}}\underset{z}{\delta}(\cdot)\,I,

where
f(\cdot,z)
is given by

f(\cdot,z)=\frac{\left(\alpha(\cdot)-\alpha_{\infty}\right)}{\alpha_{\infty}}\;Curl\left(\Phi_{k}\,I\right)(\cdot,z)\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{z\}

and
g(\cdot,z)
is given by

(3.7)

g(\cdot,z)=\frac{-1}{\alpha_{\infty}}\;\nabla\alpha(\cdot)\times Curl\left(\Phi_{k}\,I\right)(\cdot,z)\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{z\}.

Remark that even if the two functions
f
and
g
are defined in the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, except the point
z
, they still have a compact support, given by
\Omega
, and this is due to the definition of the permittivity function
\varepsilon(\cdot)
. Using the decomposition
(\ref{F=Curlf+g})
, of
F(\cdot,z)
, we rewrite
(\ref{LZB})
as:

(3.8)

\left(Curl\circ Curl-\alpha(\cdot)\,I\right)\Gamma(\cdot,z)=Curl(f)(\cdot,z)+g(\cdot,z)+\frac{\left(\epsilon_{\infty}-\epsilon_{0}(z)\right)}{\epsilon_{\infty}}\underset{z}{\delta}(\cdot)\,I.

Clearly, the regularity of
\Gamma
depends on the regularity of the data sources
f
and
g
. For this, as first step, we need
the next lemma to get precisions about the integrability of
f
and
g
.

Lemma 3.1
.

For the functions
g
and
f
, we have:

g\in\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad Curl(f)\in\left(\mathbb{H}_{0}\left(Curl,q\right)\right)^{\prime},

where

p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta\quad\text{and q its conjugate number}\quad q:=\frac{p}{p-1}=\frac{3-2\delta}{1-2\delta}.

Proof.

From the definition of the function
g
, see
(\ref{FW})
, we obtain:

\left\|g\right\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\epsilon^{-p}_{\infty}\,\left\|\nabla\epsilon_{0}(\cdot)\right\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\int_{\Omega}\left|Curl\left(\Phi_{\omega}\,\bm{I}\right)(x,z)\right|^{p}\,dx\leq\epsilon^{-p}_{\infty}\,\left\|\nabla\epsilon_{0}(\cdot)\right\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,2^{1/2p}\,\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla\Phi_{\omega}(x,z)\right|^{p}\,dx.

As
\epsilon_{0}(\cdot)
is smooth and knowing that
\nabla\Phi_{\omega}\sim\Phi^{2}_{0}
, in singularity analysis point of view, we obtain

\left\|g\right\|^{p}_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\lesssim\,\int_{\Omega}\left|\Phi_{0}(x,z)\right|^{2p}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\left|x-z\right|^{2p}}\,dx.

This last integral is finite if
p<\frac{3}{2}
, which is equivalent to take,
p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta,\,\,\forall\;\delta>0.

We denote by
q
the conjugate number of
p
given by

q=\frac{3-2\delta}{1-2\delta}.

Similarly
f
is in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
, therefore
Curl(f)
is a distribution that extends as an element of

\left(\mathbb{H}_{0}\left(Curl,q\right)\right)^{\prime}
since
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\Omega)
is dense in
\mathbb{H}_{0}\left(Curl,q\right)
.

∎

Now that we know the regularity of the second term of the problem
(\ref{NLCS})
, we investigate its solvability and the integrability of its solution. To do this, we split the problem
(\ref{NLCS})
into three sub-problems:

\displaystyle\left(Curl\circ Curl-\alpha(\cdot)\,I\right)W_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Curl(f),\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\,\,\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3}

(3.9)

\displaystyle\left(Curl\circ Curl-\alpha(\cdot)\,I\right)W_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g,\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\,\,\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\left(Curl\circ Curl-\alpha(\cdot)\,I\right)\Gamma^{\delta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(\epsilon_{\infty}-\epsilon_{0}(z)\right)}{\epsilon_{\infty}}\underset{z}{\delta}(\cdot)\,I,\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3}.

For the first sub-problem, to analyze the regularity of
W_{1}
, we start by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists one and only one solution
W_{1}
of:

(3.11)

Curl\circ Curl(W_{1})-W_{1}=Curl(f),\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3},

satisfying the Silver-Müller radiation condition

(3.12)

\underset{\left|x\right|\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim}\,\left|x\right|\,\left(\nabla\times W_{1}(x)\times\frac{x}{\left|x\right|}-i\,W_{1}(x)\right)=0.

In addition it is in
\mathbb{L}_{Loc}^{\frac{3(3-2\delta)}{(3+2\delta)}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Proof.

By taking the divergence on both sides of
(\ref{KdrJap})
we deduce that
\mathrm{div}\,\left(W_{1}\right)=0
, in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and then
(\ref{KdrJap})
will be reduced to the following vectorial Helmholtz system:

\Delta W_{1}+W_{1}=-Curl\left(f\right),\qquad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3},

and each component of
W_{1}
satisfies the Sommerfeld radiation condition.
The solution to this problem is unique and it is given by:

(3.13)

W_{1}=CurlN^{1}\left(f\right),

where
N^{1}(\cdot)
is the operator defined by
(\ref{DefNDefMk})
. Using the continuity of the operator
CurlN^{1}(\cdot)
, see Theorem 1 of
[
10
]
, we get:

(3.14)

\left\|W_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}_{Loc}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C(1,p)\,\,\left\|f\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}.

This proves that
W_{1}\in\mathbb{L}_{Loc}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\,p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta
. Now taking the Curl operator on both sides of
(\ref{equa448})
, we get

Curl(W_{1})=Curl\circ CurlN^{1}\left(f\right)=(-\Delta+\nabla\mathrm{div}\,)N^{1}\left(f\right)=N^{1}\left(f\right)+f+\nabla\mathrm{div}\,N^{1}\left(f\right).

Then,

\left\|Curl(W_{1})\right\|_{\mathbb{L}_{Loc}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq\left\|N^{1}\left(f\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}_{Loc}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\left\|f\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\left\|\nabla\mathrm{div}\,N^{1}\left(f\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}_{Loc}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Now, using the Calderon-Zygmund inequality, see Theorem 9.9 of
[
26
]
, and the continuity of the Newtonian potential operator
N(\cdot)
, see for instance Theorem 1, page 132, of
[
37
]
, we obtain

\left\|Curl(W_{1})\right\|_{\mathbb{L}_{Loc}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq(C(0,p)+1+C(2,p))\,\left\|f\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}.

This proves that
Curl\left(W_{1}\right)\in\mathbb{L}_{Loc}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\,p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta
. More regularity for
W_{1}
can be obtained by taking the gradient operator on both sides of
(\ref{equa448})
to get:

\nabla W_{1}=\nabla CurlN^{1}\left(f\right),

and then

(3.15)

\left\|\nabla W_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}_{Loc}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}=\left\|\nabla CurlN^{1}\left(f\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}_{Loc}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}<\left\|HessN^{1}\left(f\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}_{Loc}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C(2,p)\,\,\left\|f\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)},

which proves, by gathering
(\ref{normWLpR3})
with
(\ref{G2})
, that
W_{1}\in\mathbb{W}_{Loc}^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\,p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta
. At this stage we use Sobolev embedding theorem to get the following injection

\mathbb{W}_{Loc}^{1,\frac{3}{2}-\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}_{Loc}^{\frac{3(3-2\delta)}{(3+2\delta)}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Finally,

W_{1}\in\mathbb{L}_{Loc}^{\frac{3(3-2\delta)}{(3+2\delta)}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

As we have equivalence between the Silver-Müller radiation condition for
W_{1}
and the Sommerfeld radiation condition for the Cartesian component of
W_{1}
, see for instance
[
11
]
, Theorem 6.8, then
W_{1}
solves the problem (
3.11
)-(
3.12
). Finally, we have uniqueness of the problem (
3.11
)-(
3.12
) with standard arguments, see
[
11
]
.
∎

The next lemma extends the previous result to the perturbed problem case.

Lemma 3.3
.

For
\alpha(\cdot)\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, constant outside a bounded domain and such that
{\mathrm{Im\,}}\left(\alpha(\cdot)\right)>0
the solution
W_{1}
to

(3.16)

Curl\circ Curl(W_{1})-\alpha(\cdot)\,W_{1}=Curl(f),\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3},

satisfying the Silver-Müller radiation condition

\underset{\left|x\right|\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim}\,\left|x\right|\,\left(\nabla\times W_{1}(x)\times\frac{x}{\left|x\right|}-i\,\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}\,W_{1}(x)\right)=0,

is in
\mathbb{L}^{\frac{3(3-2\delta)}{(3+2\delta)}}(\Omega)
, where
\Omega
is the support of the function
f
.

Proof.

Without loss of generalities, we set:

(3.17)

\alpha_{\infty}:=\alpha(\cdot)_{\displaystyle|_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\Omega}},

where
\Omega
is the support of the function
f
and we rewrite
(\ref{KdrJap})
as

Curl\circ Curl\left(W_{1}\right)-\alpha_{\infty}W_{1}=Curl\left(f\right)+\left(\alpha(\cdot)-\alpha_{\infty}\right)W_{1}.

As already done in the unperturbed problem case, see Lemma
3.2
, we represent the solution
W_{1}
as

(3.18)

W_{1}=CurlN^{\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}}(f)-\frac{1}{\alpha_{\infty}}\nabla M^{\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}}\left(\left(\alpha(\cdot)-\alpha_{\infty}\right)W_{1}\right)+N^{\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}}\left(\left(\alpha(\cdot)-\alpha_{\infty}\right)W_{1}\right),\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3}.

As the support of
\left(\alpha(\cdot)-\alpha_{\infty}\right)
is
\Omega
, we can see that the value of
W_{1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is given by its value in
\Omega
. So we need to prove that, when restricted to
\Omega
, the solution
W_{1}
is well defined. It is natural to look for solutions of
(\ref{PMMS})
in the
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
-spaces. For this, taking the
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
-norm on both sides of (
3.18
) and using the triangular inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|W_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|CurlN^{\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}}(f)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}+\frac{1}{\left|\alpha_{\infty}\right|}\left\|\nabla\nabla N^{\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}}\left(\left(\alpha_{\infty}-\alpha(\cdot)\right)W_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left\|N^{\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}}\left(\left(\alpha_{\infty}-\alpha(\cdot)\right)W_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\left\|W_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(1,p)\,\left\|f\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}+\left(\frac{C(2,p)}{\left|\alpha_{\infty}\right|}+C(0,p)\right)\left\|\left(\alpha_{\infty}-\alpha(\cdot)\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\left\|W_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}.

Now, for
p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta
, we assume that:

(3.19)

1>\left(\frac{C(2,p)}{\left|\alpha_{\infty}\right|}+C(0,p)\right)\left\|\left(\alpha_{\infty}-\alpha(\cdot)\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Then, under this condition, we end up with:

(3.20)

\left\|W_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C(1,p)}{\left[1-\left(\frac{C(2,p)}{\left|\alpha_{\infty}\right|}+C(0,p)\right)\left\|\left(\alpha_{\infty}-\alpha(\cdot)\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right]}\,\left\|f\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}.

This proves that, under the condition
(\ref{Cdtalpha})
, the equation
(\ref{PMMS})
is invertible in
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
. Therefore, again from
(\ref{PMMS})
, we see that
W_{1}
is well defined in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and satisfies the Silver-Müller radiation condition. Hence
W_{1}
in
(\ref{PMMS})
solves
(\ref{KdrJapCS})
. The uniqueness of the solution of the problem
(\ref{KdrJapCS})
is known under the condition
{\mathrm{Im\,}}\left(\alpha(\cdot)\right)>0
, see for instance Theorem 9.4 in
[
11
]
.

Let us now examine the regularity of this solution. For this, taking the
Curl
operator on both sides of
(\ref{PMMS})
to obtain:

Curl\left(W_{1}\right)=Curl\circ CurlN^{\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}}(f)-CurlN^{\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}}\left(\left(\alpha_{\infty}-\alpha(\cdot)\right)W_{1}\right)

and by the use of the relation
Curl\circ Curl(\cdot)=-\Delta(\cdot)+\nabla\nabla\cdot(\cdot)
we obtain:

Curl\left(W_{1}\right)=\alpha_{\infty}N^{\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}}(f)+f+\nabla\mathrm{div}\,N^{\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}}(f)-CurlN^{\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}}\left(\left(\alpha_{\infty}-\alpha(\cdot)\right)W_{1}\right).

Hence,

\displaystyle\left\|Curl\left(W_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|\alpha_{\infty}\right|\,\,\left\|N^{\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}}(f)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}+\left\|f\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}+\left\|\nabla\mathrm{div}\,N^{\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}}(f)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\left\|CurlN^{\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}}\left(\left(\alpha_{\infty}-\alpha(\cdot)\right)W_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\left\|Curl\left(W_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\left|\alpha_{\infty}\right|\,C(0,p)+1+C(2,p)\right]\,\left\|f\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}+C(1,p)\left\|\left(\alpha_{\infty}-\alpha(\cdot)\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\,\left\|W_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}.

This last inequality combined with
(\ref{Wfctf})
give us:

(3.21)

\displaystyle\left\|Curl\left(W_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Bigg[\left|\alpha_{\infty}\right|\,C(0,p)+1+C(2,p)+

\displaystyle\frac{(C(1,p))^{2}\,\left\|\left(\alpha_{\infty}-\alpha(\cdot)\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}}{\left[1-\left(\frac{C(2,p)}{\left|\alpha_{\infty}\right|}+C(0,p)\right)\left\|\left(\alpha_{\infty}-\alpha(\cdot)\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right]}\Bigg]\left\|f\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}.

This proves that
Curl\left(W_{1}\right)\in\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega),\,p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta
. In addition, by taking the divergence operator on both sides of
(\ref{PMMS})
we get
\mathrm{div}\,\left(\alpha(\cdot)\,W_{1}\right)=0,
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,which, under the condition
\alpha(\cdot)_{\displaystyle|_{\Omega}}\neq 0
, is equivalent to

\mathrm{div}\,\left(W_{1}\right)=-\,\alpha(\cdot)^{-1}\,W_{1}\cdot\nabla\alpha(\cdot).

From the last equality and the constancy of
\alpha(\cdot)
outside
\Omega
, see
(\ref{alphaCte})
, we deduce that:

(3.22)

\mathrm{div}\,\left(W_{1}\right)\in\mathbb{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\,\,p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta\quad\text{and}\quad Supp\left(\mathrm{div}\,(W_{1})\right)\subseteq\Omega.

Now, set
\phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
such that:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\phi=1&\text{in $\Omega$,}\\
0\leq\phi\leq 1&\text{in $B(0,R)\setminus\Omega$,}\\
\phi=0&\text{in $\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus B(0,R)$,}\end{cases}

where
B(0,R)
is a ball of center
0
and a large radius
R
containing the domain
\Omega
. Clearly, from
(\ref{Wfctf})
, the vector field
\phi\,W_{1}\in\mathbb{L}^{p}(B(0,R))
with
p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta
. Also, we have

\displaystyle Curl\left(\phi\,W_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla\phi\times W_{1}+\phi\,Curl\left(W_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\left\|Curl\left(\phi\,W_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(B(0,R))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{2}\,\left\|\nabla\phi\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(B(0,R))}\,\left\|W_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(B(0,R))}+\left\|Curl\left(W_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(B(0,R))},

then, from
(\ref{Wfctf})
and
(\ref{CurlWfctf})
, we deduce that
Curl\left(\phi\,W_{1}\right)\in\mathbb{L}^{p}(B(0,R))
with
p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta
. In a similar manner, we have:

\displaystyle\mathrm{div}\,\left(\phi\,W_{1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W_{1}\cdot\nabla\phi+\phi\,\mathrm{div}\,\left(W_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathrm{div}\,\left(\phi\,W_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(B(0,R))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla\phi\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(B(0,R))}\,\left\|W_{1}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(B(0,R))}+\left\|\mathrm{div}\,\left(W_{1}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(B(0,R))},

then, from
(\ref{Wfctf})
and
(\ref{LRRA})
, we deduce that
\mathrm{div}\,\left(\phi\,W_{1}\right)\in\mathbb{L}^{p}(B(0,R))
with
p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta
.

At this stage we have
\phi\,W_{1},Curl(\phi\,W_{1})
and
\mathrm{div}\,(\phi\,W_{1})
are elements in the space
\mathbb{L}^{p}\left(B(0,R)\right),\,p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta,
and
\nu\times\phi\,W_{1}=0
on the boundary
\partial B(0,R)
. This is sufficient to justify, for reference see
[
4
]
, that
\phi\,W_{1}\in\mathbb{W}^{1,p}\left(B(0,R)\right),\,p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta
. Using embedding results for Sobolev spaces, we obtain:

\phi\,W_{1}\in\mathbb{W}^{1,\frac{3}{2}-\delta}\left(B(0,R)\right)\lhook\joinrel\xrightarrow{\text{continously}}\mathbb{L}^{\frac{3(3-2\delta)}{(3+2\delta)}}\left(B(0,R)\right).

Finally, by restriction to the domain
\Omega
, we get
W_{1}\in\mathbb{L}^{\frac{3(3-2\delta)}{(3+2\delta)}}(\Omega).
∎

Let us now study the existence and regularity of
W_{2}
, solution of
(\ref{NLCSW2})
.

Lemma 3.4
.

For
\alpha(\cdot)\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, constant outside a bounded domain and such that
{\mathrm{Im\,}}\left(\alpha(\cdot)\right)>0
there exists one and only one solution
W_{2}
of

\left(Curl\circ Curl-\alpha(\cdot)\,I\right)W_{2}=g,\,\,\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3},

satisfying the Silver-Müller radiation condition

\underset{\left|x\right|\rightarrow+\infty}{\lim}\,\left|x\right|\,\left(\nabla\times W_{2}(x)\times\frac{x}{\left|x\right|}-i\,\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}\,W_{2}(x)\right)=0.

In addition, it is in
\mathbb{H}(Curl,p,\Omega)
, with
p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta
.

Proof.

Similarly as in the previous lemma, we begin by the integral equation representation of the solution
W_{2}
, which will be given by:

(3.23)

W_{2}=N^{\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}}\left(\left(\alpha(\cdot)-\alpha_{\infty}\right)W_{2}+g\right)-\frac{1}{\alpha_{\infty}}\nabla M^{\sqrt{\alpha_{\infty}}}\left(\left(\alpha(\cdot)-\alpha_{\infty}\right)W_{2}+g\right),\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Again as in the previous lemma, restricted to
\Omega
, we see at (
3.23
) as an integral equation in the
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
-spaces. Taking the
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
-norm on both sides of the previous equation and using the condition, on the function
\alpha(\cdot)
, given by
(\ref{Cdtalpha})
, we obtain:

(3.24)

\left\|W_{2}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{\left(C(0,p)+\frac{C(2,p)}{\left|\alpha_{\infty}\right|}\right)}{\left[1-\left(\frac{C(2,p)}{\left|\alpha_{\infty}\right|}+C(0,p)\right)\left\|\left(\alpha_{\infty}-\alpha(\cdot)\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right]}\,\,\left\|g\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)},\quad\text{where}\quad p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta.

This proves that
W_{2}\in\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)
, with
p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta
, and then
W_{2}
is well defined. Now, by taking the Curl operator of
(\ref{ASVS})
, the
\mathbb{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
-norm and using the estimation
(\ref{EstimationW2})
we get:

(3.25)

\left\|Curl\left(W_{2}\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{C(1,p)}{\left[1-\left(\frac{C(2,p)}{\left|\alpha_{\infty}\right|}+C(0,p)\right)\left\|\left(\alpha_{\infty}-\alpha(\cdot)\right)\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right]}\,\,\left\|g\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega)},\,\text{where}\,\,p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta.

By gathering
(\ref{EstimationW2})
with
(\ref{CurlEstimationW2})
we deduce that
W_{2}
, solution of
(\ref{NLCSW2})
, will be in
\mathbb{H}(Curl,p,\Omega)
with
p=\frac{3}{2}-\delta
.
∎

The previous lemma give us the global regularity of
W_{2}
, solution of
(\ref{NLCSW2})
. The goal of the coming lemma is to provide a more explicit expression of the dominant term of
W_{2}
near the fixed point
z
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For
x
in
D^{\star}
, where
D^{\star}
is the ball of center
z
and radius
a^{\star}
such that
a^{\star}>a
, we have:

W_{2}(x,z)=\frac{-1}{\alpha(z)\,\epsilon_{\infty}}\,\underset{x}{\nabla}\underset{x}{\nabla}M\left(\Phi_{0}(\cdot,z)\nabla\epsilon_{0}(z)\right)(x)+W_{3}(x,z),

where
W_{3}(\cdot,z)\in\mathbb{L}^{\frac{3(3-2\delta)}{(3+2\delta)}}\left(D^{\star}\right)
.

Proof.

We begin by setting, in the ball
D^{\star}
, the PDE satisfied by
W_{2}(\cdot,z)
, which is:

(3.26)

\underset{x}{Curl}\circ\underset{x}{Curl}\left(W_{2}\right)(x,z)-\alpha(x)W_{2}(x,z)=g(x,z),\quad x\in D^{\star}.

As it was shown previously, see
(\ref{EstimationW2})
, the solution
W_{2}(\cdot,z)\in\mathbb{L}^{\frac{3}{2}-\delta}(D^{\star})
. Thanks to
[
4
]
, Lemma 5.4, we can rewrite
W_{2}(\cdot,z)
as:

(3.27)

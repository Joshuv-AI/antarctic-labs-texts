Periodic homogenization of a class of weakly coupled systems of linear PDEs
Nikola Sandrić

Abstract
. In this article, basing upon probabilistic methods, we discuss periodic homogenization of a class of weakly coupled systems of linear elliptic and parabolic partial differential equations. Under the assumption that the systems have rapidly periodically oscillating coefficients, we first prove that the appropriately centered and scaled continuous component of the associated regime switching diffusion process converges weakly to a Brownian motion with covariance matrix given in terms of the coefficients of the systems. The homogenization results then follow by employing probabilistic representation of the solutions to the systems and the continuous mapping theorem. The presented results generalize the well-known results related to periodic homogenization of the classical elliptic boundary-value problem and the classical parabolic initial-value problem for a single equation.

1.
Introduction

One of the classical directions in the analysis of partial differential equations (PDEs) centers around their homogenization (averaging) properties. In this article, we discuss periodic homogenization of a class of weakly coupled systems of linear elliptic and parabolic PDEs.
Let
\{\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\varepsilon}\}_{i\in[n]}
,
\varepsilon>0
, be a family of second-order elliptic differential operators of the form

\mathcal{L}^{\varepsilon}_{i}\,=\,2^{-1}\mathrm{Tr}\bigl(\upsigma(\cdot/\varepsilon,i)\upsigma(\cdot/\varepsilon,i)^{\mathrm{T}}\,\nabla\nabla^{\mathrm{T}}\bigr)+\bigl(\varepsilon^{-1}\mathrm{b}(\cdot/\varepsilon,i)^{\mathrm{T}}+\mathrm{c}(\cdot/\varepsilon,i)^{\mathrm{T}}\bigr)\nabla\,,

and let
\mathcal{Q}=(\mathrm{q}_{ij})_{i,j\in[n]}
be an
n\times n
real matrix with
\mathrm{q}_{ij}\geq 0
for
i\neq j
. If
n=1
, we put
\mathcal{Q}\coloneqq 0
. For
f:[n]\to\mathbb{R}
and
i\in[n]
, define
\mathcal{Q}f(i)\coloneqq\sum_{j\in[n]}\mathrm{q}_{ij}f(j).

The main goal of this article is to discuss periodic homogenization (that is, asymptotic behavior of the solution as
\varepsilon\to 0
) for the following weakly coupled system (the system is coupled in the terms which are not differentiated) of elliptic boundary-value problems

(1.1)

\displaystyle\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{-2}\mathcal{Q}\bigr)u^{\varepsilon}(x,i)+e(x/\varepsilon,i)\,u^{\varepsilon}(x,i)+f(x)

\displaystyle=\,0\,,\qquad(x,i)\in\mathscr{D}\times[n]\,,

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}(x,i)

\displaystyle=\,g(x)\,,\qquad(x,i)\in\partial\mathscr{D}\times[n]\,,

as well as the coupled system of parabolic initial-value problems

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{\varepsilon}(t,x,i)\,=

\displaystyle\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{-2}\mathcal{Q}\bigr)u^{\varepsilon}(t,x,i)

\displaystyle+e(x/\varepsilon,i)u^{\varepsilon}(t,x,i)+f(x)\,,\qquad(t,x,i)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[n]\,,

\displaystyle u^{\varepsilon}(0,x,i)\,=

\displaystyle g(x)\,,\qquad(x,i)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[n]\,.

Without loss of generality, in the sequel we assume
\mathrm{q}_{ii}=-\sum_{j\neq i}\mathrm{q}_{ij}
; otherwise we replace
e(x/\varepsilon,i)
in
eqs.

1.1
and
1.2
by
e(x/\varepsilon,i)+\varepsilon^{-2}\sum_{j\in[n]}\mathrm{q}_{ij}
.
These problems are a generalization of the classical elliptic boundary-value problem and the classical parabolic initial-value problem for a single equation (
n=1
). Typical examples of such systems are the Maxwell’s equations, Hamilton–Jacobi equations and semiconductor equations, see
[
5
]
and
[
20
]
and the references therein.

Our approach relies on probabilistic techniques. By combining classical PDE results (existence of a smooth solution to the corresponding Poisson equation) and the fact that the underlying regime switching diffusion process
\{(\mathrm{X}^{\varepsilon}(x,i;t),\Lambda(i;t/\varepsilon^{2}))\}_{t\geq 0}
associated to
\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon^{-2}\mathcal{Q}
does not show a singular behavior in its motion (that is, it is irreducible),
we first show that the (appropriately centered) process
\{\mathrm{X}^{\varepsilon}(x,i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
satisfies a functional CLT with Brownian limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
(see
Theorem

3.1
).
The homogenization results (see
Theorems

4.1
and
4.2
) then follow
by employing probabilistic representation (the Feynman-Kac formula) of the solutions to the problems in
eqs.

1.1
and
1.2
, obtained in
[
6
, Theorems 3.2 and 3.3]
, and the continuous mapping theorem. This idea goes back to M. I. Freĭdlin
[
24
]
(see also
[
7
, Chapter 3]
).

Organization of the article

.

\displaystyle\bigl|\bigl(\mathcal{P}^{\varepsilon}_{t}f(x,i)-f(x,i)\bigr)/t-\mathcal{L}^{\varepsilon}f(x,i)\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\,\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\mathcal{P}^{\varepsilon}_{s}\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\varepsilon}f(x,i)-\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\varepsilon}f(x,i)\bigr)\mathrm{d}s\right|+\left|\frac{\varepsilon^{-2}}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\mathcal{P}^{\varepsilon}_{s}\mathcal{Q}f(x,i)-\mathcal{Q}f(x,i)\bigr)\mathrm{d}s\right|\,.

. Recall that
\uptau^{i}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(-\mathrm{q}_{ii})

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\bigl(\mathcal{P}^{\varepsilon}_{s}\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\varepsilon}f(x,i)-\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\varepsilon}f(x,i)\bigr)\mathrm{d}s\right|

\displaystyle\,\leq\,\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\varepsilon}f\bigl(\mathrm{X}^{\varepsilon}(x,i;s),\Lambda(i;s/\varepsilon^{2})\bigr)-\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\varepsilon}f(x,i)\bigr)\mathbb{1}_{\{\uptau^{i}/\varepsilon^{2}>t\}}\bigr]\mathrm{d}s\right|

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\varepsilon}f\bigl(\mathrm{X}^{\varepsilon}(x,i;s),\Lambda(i;s/\varepsilon^{2})\bigr)-\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\varepsilon}f(x,i)\bigr)\mathbb{1}_{\{\uptau^{i}/\varepsilon^{2}\leq t\}}\bigr]\mathrm{d}s\right|

\displaystyle\,\leq\,\left|\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\bigl[\bigl(\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\varepsilon}f\bigl(\mathrm{X}^{\varepsilon,i}(x;s),i\bigr)-\mathcal{L}_{i}^{\varepsilon}f(x,i)\bigr)\mathbb{1}_{\{\uptau^{i}/\varepsilon^{2}>t\}}\bigr]\mathrm{d}s\right|

\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\par\int_{0}^{\infty}f\bigl(Y(t)\bigr)\,\mathrm{e}^{\eta(t)}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,&\uptau(Y)=\infty\ \ \text{and}\ \ \eta(t)\leq-\alpha t\ \ \forall t\geq 0\,,\\
\infty\,,&\text{otherwise}\,,\end{cases}

where
\uptau(Y)\coloneqq\inf\{t\geq 0\colon Y(t)\notin\mathscr{D}\}
.
Clearly,

u^{\varepsilon}(x,i)\,=\,\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{F}\left(\{(\mathrm{X}^{\varepsilon},\zeta^{\varepsilon})(x,i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}\right)\right]\qquad\text{and}\qquad u(x)\,=\,\mathbb{E}\left[\mathrm{F}\left(\{(\mathrm{W}^{\mathsf{a},\mathsf{b}},\zeta)(x;t)\}_{t\geq 0}\right)\right]\,.

The function
\mathrm{F}(Y,\eta)
has the following properties:

(i)

it is measurable and bounded a.s. with respect to
\upmu_{x,i}^{\varepsilon}
and
\upmu_{x}

(ii)

it is continuous a.s. with respect to
\upmu_{x}
.

This, together with
eq.

4.7
, gives

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\to 0}u^{\varepsilon}(x,i)\,=\,u(x)\qquad\forall(x,i)\in\mathscr{D}\times[n]\,,

which concludes the proof.

To this end, let us verify (i) and (ii). To see that (i) holds, note that if
\eta(t)\leq-\alpha t
for all
t\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{F}(Y,\eta)\right|

\displaystyle\,\leq\,\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha t}\,\mathrm{d}t\,=\,\left\|g\right\|_{\infty}+\frac{\left\|f\right\|_{\infty}}{\alpha}<\infty\,.

Due to the definition of processes
\{\zeta^{\varepsilon}(x,i;t)\}_{t\geq 0}
and
\{\zeta(x,t)\}_{t\geq 0}
, and the fact that
e(x,i)\leq-\alpha
, property (i) follows.
To see property (ii), we need to check that if
\{Y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
Y
uniformly on compact intervals, then

(4.8)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathrm{F}(Y_{k},\eta)\,=\,\mathrm{F}(Y,\eta)

for
\eta(t)=\zeta(x,t)
. Recall that
\zeta(x,t)\leq-\alpha t
.
The relation in
eq.

4.8
will follow from the proof of
[
7
, Lemma 3.4.3]
where it has been shown that if
\{Y_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
converges to
Y
uniformly on compact intervals, then
\lim_{k\to\infty}\uptau(Y_{k})=\uptau(Y)
. Namely, from this fact, and the dominated convergence theorem, we immediately see that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\uptau(Y_{k})}f\bigl(Y_{k}(t)\bigr)\,\mathrm{e}^{\eta(t)}\mathrm{d}t\,=\,\int_{0}^{\uptau(Y)}f\bigl(Y(t)\bigr)\,\mathrm{e}^{\eta(t)}\mathrm{d}t\,.

Finally, we need to prove that

\lim_{k\to\infty}g\bigl(Y_{k}\bigl(\uptau(Y_{k})\bigr)\bigr)\,\mathrm{e}^{\eta(\uptau(Y_{k}))}\mathbb{1}_{\{\uptau(Y_{k})<\infty\}}\,=\,\begin{cases}g\bigl(Y\bigl(\uptau(Y)\bigr)\bigr)\,\mathrm{e}^{\eta(\uptau(Y))}\,,&\uptau(Y)<\infty\,,\par\par\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\par 0\,,&\uptau(Y)=\infty\,.\\
\end{cases}

If
\uptau(Y)=\infty
, then
\lim_{k\to\infty}\uptau(Y_{k})=\infty
and since
g(x)
is bounded and
\eta(t)\leq-\alpha t
for all
t\geq 0
, the assertion follows. If
\uptau(Y)<\infty
, then there exist
T>0
ad
k_{Y}\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
\uptau(Y_{k})\in[0,T]
for all
k\geq k_{Y}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\left|g\bigl(Y_{k}\bigl(\uptau(Y_{k})\bigr)\bigr)\mathrm{e}^{\eta(\uptau(Y_{k}))}-g\bigl(Y\bigl(\uptau(Y)\bigr)\bigl)\mathrm{e}^{\eta(\uptau(Y))}\right|

\displaystyle\,\leq\,\|g\|_{\infty}\left|\mathrm{e}^{\eta(\uptau(Y_{k}))}-\mathrm{e}^{\eta(\uptau(Y))}\right|+\mathrm{e}^{\eta(\uptau(Y))}\left|g\bigl(Y_{k}\bigl(\uptau(Y_{k})\bigr)\bigr)-g\bigl(Y\bigl(\uptau(Y_{k})\bigr)\bigl)\right|

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\mathrm{e}^{\eta(\uptau(Y))}\left|g\bigl(Y\bigl(\uptau(Y_{k})\bigr)\bigr)-g\bigl(Y\bigl(\uptau(Y)\bigr)\bigl)\right|

\displaystyle\,\leq\,\|g\|_{\infty}\left|\mathrm{e}^{\eta(\uptau(Y_{k}))}-\mathrm{e}^{\eta(\uptau(Y))}\right|+\mathrm{e}^{\eta(\uptau(Y))}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left|g\bigl(Y_{k}(t)\bigr)-g\bigl(Y(t)\bigr)\right|

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +\mathrm{e}^{\eta(\uptau(Y))}\left|g\bigl(Y\bigl(\uptau(Y_{k})\bigr)\bigr)-g\bigl(Y\bigl(\uptau(Y)\bigr)\bigl)\right|\,.

Clearly, the first and last terms in the above inequality tend to zero as
k
tends to infinity.
Suppose that
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}|g(Y_{k}(t))-g(Y(t))|>0
. Then there exist
\epsilon>0
and sequences
\{k_{l}\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
and
\{t_{l}\}_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\subseteq[0,T]
, such that
\lim_{l\to\infty}t_{l}=t\in[0,T]
and
|g(Y_{k_{l}}(t_{l}))-g(Y(t_{l}))|>\epsilon
for all
l\in\mathbb{N}
. However, this is not possible since
\lim_{l\to\infty}g(Y(t_{l}))=g(Y(t))
and

\displaystyle\lim_{l\to\infty}\left|Y_{k_{l}}(t_{l})-Y(t)\right|

\displaystyle\,\leq\,\lim_{l\to\infty}\left|Y_{k_{l}}(t_{l})-Y(t_{l})\right|+\lim_{l\to\infty}\left|Y(t_{l})-Y(t)\right|

\displaystyle\,\leq\,\lim_{l\to\infty}\sup_{0\leq s\leq T}\left|Y_{k_{l}}(s)-Y(s)\right|+\lim_{l\to\infty}\left|Y(t_{l})-Y(t)\right|

\displaystyle\,=\,0\,.

From this we conclude that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left|g\bigl(Y_{k}\bigl(\uptau(Y_{k})\bigr)\bigr)\mathrm{e}^{\eta(\uptau(Y_{k}))}-g\bigl(Y\bigl(\uptau(Y)\bigr)\bigl)\mathrm{e}^{\eta(\uptau(Y))}\right|\,=\,0\,,

which proves the assertion. ∎

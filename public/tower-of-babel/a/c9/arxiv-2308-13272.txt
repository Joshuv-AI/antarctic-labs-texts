Probing inflationary moduli space with gravitational waves
Shinsuke Kawai, Jinsu Kim

Abstract
We investigate the spectrum of gravitational waves arising from primordial inflation in the presence of a string-theoretical higher curvature correction, specifically, the Gauss-Bonnet coupling term for the inflaton (modulus) field. We show that if the modulus field exhibits a wall-crossing like behavior in the moduli space, there can be a period of Gauss-Bonnet coupling term domination during the usual slow-roll. This phenomenon is potentially detectable as the gravitational wave spectrum exhibits a characteristic peak caused by the brief domination of the Gauss-Bonnet coupling term. We explore the possibility of measuring such gravitational waves with pulsar timing array experiments such as NANOGrav, and future space-borne interferometers such as LISA, DECIGO, and Taiji.

III
Primordial Gravitational Waves

Let us consider the tensor perturbation around the flat Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) background metric in the exponential gauge,

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}[e^{h}]_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}\,.

(8)

We define

\displaystyle h_{ij}=he_{i}e_{j}+\bar{h}\bar{e}_{i}\bar{e}_{j}\,,

(9)

where

\bar{e}_{i}
are the complex dyad that satisfy
\delta^{ij}e_{i}\bar{e}_{j}=1

\delta^{ij}e_{i}e_{j}=\delta^{ij}\bar{e}_{i}\bar{e}_{j}=0

\displaystyle h_{\bf k}^{\prime\prime}+2\frac{A_{t}^{\prime}}{A_{t}}h_{\bf k}^{\prime}+k^{2}C_{t}^{2}h_{\mathbf{k}}=0\,,

where the prime denotes the conformal-time derivative, and

\displaystyle A_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv a^{2}\left(1-\frac{\sigma_{1}}{2}\right)\,,

(11)

\displaystyle C_{t}^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv 1+\frac{a^{2}\sigma_{1}}{2A_{t}^{2}}\left(1-\sigma_{2}-\epsilon_{H}\right)\,.

(12)

Expanding

with the mode functions

\displaystyle h_{\bf k}

\displaystyle=a_{\bf k}\mathfrak{h}_{\bf k}+\bar{a}_{-{\bf k}}^{\dagger}\bar{\mathfrak{h}}^{*}_{-{\bf k}}\,,

(13)

\displaystyle\bar{h}_{\bf k}

\displaystyle=a_{-{\bf k}}^{\dagger}\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{-{\bf k}}+\bar{a}_{{\bf k}}\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\bf k}}\,,

(14)

the tensor perturbation is canonically quantized using the ladder operators satisfying

[a_{\bf k},a^{\dagger}_{-{\bf q}}]=[\bar{a}_{\bf k},\bar{a}^{\dagger}_{-{\bf q}}]=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{(3)}({\bf k}+{\bf q})

Introducing
v_{\bf k}\equiv(M_{\rm P}/2)A_{t}\mathfrak{h}_{\bf k}
and
\bar{v}_{\bf k}\equiv(M_{\rm P}/2)A_{t}\bar{\mathfrak{h}}_{\bf k}

\displaystyle v_{\bf k}^{\prime\prime}+\omega_{\bf k}^{2}v_{\bf k}=0\,,

(15)

\displaystyle\omega_{\bf k}\equiv\sqrt{k^{2}C_{t}^{2}-\frac{A_{t}^{\prime\prime}}{A_{t}}}\,.

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle=\int\frac{da}{a^{2}H}

\displaystyle v_{\bf k}(\tau)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}e^{i\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\nu_{t}+\frac{1}{2}\right)}\sqrt{-\tau}H_{\nu_{t}}^{(1)}\left(-C_{t}k\tau\right)\,.

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{T,{\rm prim}}(k)

V
Conclusion

We have investigated primordial inflationary gravitational waves in the presence of the Gauss-Bonnet coupling function which typically arises in string-theoretical modification of gravity. Assuming the form of Gauss-Bonnet coupling function that models a wall-crossing like dynamics in the moduli space, we have shown that brief domination of the Gauss-Bonnet coupling term over the potential term is possible.

The primordial tensor fluctuations are enhanced by such temporary Gauss-Bonnet domination. We have presented analytical discussion for the enhancement and verified it by numerically solving the system of equations. Applying the transfer function representing the standard thermal history of the Universe after inflation to the primordial tensor power spectrum, we computed the present-day gravitational wave spectrum. The spectrum exhibits a characteristic peak.

Depending on the parameter values in the Gauss-Bonnet coupling function, the position of the peak in the gravitational wave spectrum varies in a wide frequency range. In this work, we have presented two cases; one where the peak is situated at the nano-Hertz frequency range and the other at the Hertz frequency range. The nano-Hertz case is thus in the prospective range of pulsar timing array experiments, such as NANOGrav, and especially of SKA, while the Hertz case may be probed by near-future gravitational wave observatories such as DECIGO and BBO.

Although the quadratic scalar potential was chosen in this work for the sake of simplicity and concreteness, we stress that the dynamics of the gravitational waves that we described in this work are insensitive to the choice of the potential, as long as the Gauss-Bonnet coupling term dominates the potential term such that
V_{,\varphi}\xi_{,\varphi}>0
. Therefore, we suppose that the enhancement of the primordial inflationary gravitational waves may be realized for various other types of inflationary models once the Gauss-Bonnet coupling is included. Furthermore, we anticipate that a similar enhanced gravitational wave signal would arise in the presence of other higher curvature correction terms. We plan to investigate such scenarios in the future.

CONFORMAL TIME

In this appendix, we derive Eq. (
19
) for the conformal time
\tau=\int da/(a^{2}H)
.
Performing integration by parts, we find

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{aH}+\int\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{a^{2}H}da\,,

(35)

and

\displaystyle\int\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{a^{2}H}da

\displaystyle=-\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{aH}+\int\frac{\epsilon_{H}\epsilon_{2}}{a^{2}H}da+\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon_{H}^{2}\right)\,,

(36)

where
\epsilon_{2}\equiv\dot{\epsilon}_{H}/(H\epsilon_{H})
.
The time derivative of the Friedmann equation combined with the Klein-Gordon equation gives

\displaystyle\epsilon_{H}\epsilon_{2}=\frac{\sigma_{1}}{4-\sigma_{1}}\left(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}-2\epsilon_{H}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon_{H}^{2}\right)\,.

(37)

Thus,

\displaystyle\int\frac{\epsilon_{H}\epsilon_{2}}{a^{2}H}da

\displaystyle=-2\int\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{a^{2}H}\frac{\sigma_{1}}{4-\sigma_{1}}da

\displaystyle\quad+\int\frac{da}{a^{2}H}\frac{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}(1-\sigma_{3})}{4-\sigma_{1}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon_{H}^{2}\right)\,.

(38)

The first term can be integrated by parts to give

\displaystyle\int\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{a^{2}H}\frac{\sigma_{1}}{4-\sigma_{1}}da

\displaystyle=-\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{aH}\frac{\sigma_{1}}{4-\sigma_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int\frac{da}{a^{2}H}\left(\frac{\sigma_{1}}{4-\sigma_{1}}\right)^{2}(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}-2\epsilon_{H})

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon_{H}^{2}\right)\,.

(39)

Thus,

\displaystyle\int\frac{\epsilon_{H}\epsilon_{2}}{a^{2}H}da

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{aH}\frac{2\sigma_{1}}{4-\sigma_{1}}+4\int\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{a^{2}H}\left(\frac{\sigma_{1}}{4-\sigma_{1}}\right)^{2}da

\displaystyle\quad+\int\frac{da}{a^{2}H}\left[\frac{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}{4-\sigma_{1}}(1-\sigma_{3})-2\left(\frac{\sigma_{1}}{4-\sigma_{1}}\right)^{2}\sigma_{2}(1-\sigma_{3})\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon_{H}^{2}\right)\,.

(40)

Integrating by parts the second term, we obtain

\displaystyle\int\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{a^{2}H}\left(\frac{\sigma_{1}}{4-\sigma_{1}}\right)^{2}da

\displaystyle=-\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{aH}\left(\frac{\sigma_{1}}{4-\sigma_{1}}\right)^{2}-2\int\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{a^{2}H}\left(\frac{\sigma_{1}}{4-\sigma_{1}}\right)^{3}da

\displaystyle\quad+\int\frac{da}{a^{2}H}\frac{\sigma_{1}^{3}\sigma_{2}(1-\sigma_{3})}{(4-\sigma_{1})^{3}}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon_{H}^{2}\right)\,.

(41)

Substituting this result into Eq. (
40
), we get

\displaystyle\int\frac{\epsilon_{H}\epsilon_{2}}{a^{2}H}da

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{aH}\frac{2\sigma_{1}}{4-\sigma_{1}}-\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{aH}\left(\frac{2\sigma_{1}}{4-\sigma_{1}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-8\int\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{a^{2}H}\left(\frac{\sigma_{1}}{4-\sigma_{1}}\right)^{3}da

\displaystyle\quad+\int\frac{da}{a^{2}H}\left[\frac{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}(1-\sigma_{3})}{4-\sigma_{1}}\right]-2\int\frac{da}{a^{2}H}\left[\frac{\sigma_{1}^{2}\sigma_{2}(1-\sigma_{3})}{(4-\sigma_{1})^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\quad+4\int\frac{da}{a^{2}H}\frac{\sigma_{1}^{3}\sigma_{2}(1-\sigma_{3})}{(4-\sigma_{1})^{3}}+\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon_{H}^{2}\right)\,.

(42)

The third term can again be integrated by parts. Repeating this process, we find

\displaystyle\int\frac{\epsilon_{H}\epsilon_{2}}{a^{2}H}da

\displaystyle=-\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{aH}s\left(1+s+s^{2}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\int\frac{da}{a^{2}H}\frac{\sigma_{2}(1-\sigma_{3})}{2}s\left(1+s+s^{2}+\cdots\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon_{H}^{2}\right)\,,

(43)

where
s\equiv 2\sigma_{1}/(\sigma_{1}-4)
.
Let us first consider the case where
s<1
,
i.e.
,
-4<\sigma_{1}<4/3
; note that
\sigma_{1}<0
in our case.
In this case,

\displaystyle\int\frac{\epsilon_{H}\epsilon_{2}}{a^{2}H}da

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{aH}\left(\frac{2\sigma_{1}}{4+\sigma_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\int\frac{da}{a^{2}H}\frac{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}{4+\sigma_{1}}-\int\frac{da}{a^{2}H}\frac{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}}{4+\sigma_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon_{H}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{aH}\left(\frac{2\sigma_{1}}{4+\sigma_{1}}\right)-\frac{1}{aH}\frac{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}}{4+\sigma_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon_{H}^{2}\right)\,,

(44)

where we have performed integration by parts.
We thus conclude that

\displaystyle\tau=-\frac{1}{aH}-\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{aH}\left(\frac{\sigma_{1}}{4+\sigma_{1}}\right)+\mathcal{O}\left(\epsilon_{H}^{2}\right)\,.

(45)

where we have used Eq. (
6
).

Let us now consider the
s>1
case. We take the limit
|\sigma_{1}|\gg 1
. In this case, since

\displaystyle\int\frac{\epsilon_{H}\epsilon_{2}}{a^{2}H}\simeq 2\int\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{a^{2}H}da-\int\frac{da}{a^{2}H}\sigma_{2}(1-\sigma_{3})\,,

(46)

up to the leading order, we find

\displaystyle\int\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{a^{2}H}da\simeq\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{aH}+\int\frac{\sigma_{2}}{a^{2}H}da-\int\frac{\sigma_{2}\sigma_{3}}{a^{2}H}da\,.

(47)

Integrating by parts the second term, we obtain

\displaystyle\tau\simeq-\frac{1}{aH}+\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{aH}-\frac{\sigma_{2}}{aH}\simeq-\frac{1}{aH}-\frac{\epsilon_{H}}{aH}\,,

(48)

where we have used Eq. (
6
) in the
|\sigma_{1}|\gg 1
limit.
We note that Eq. (
45
) captures Eq. (
48
) in the
|\sigma_{1}|\gg 1

45
) in the main text.

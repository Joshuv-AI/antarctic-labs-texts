Parton model description of quark and antiquark correlators and TMDs
F. Aslan 1 , S. Bastami 1 , P. Schweitzer 1

Abstract
Model studies play an important role for the understanding and elucidation of the nonperturbative properties of transverse momentum dependent parton distribution functions (TMDs). The parton model is often a helpful framework and starting point for first explorations of TMD properties and the description of deep-inelastic processes in which TMDs can be accessed. Based on a systematic exploration of the parton model concept, we reconcile the claims in literature that there are 2 independent structures in the quark correlator in the parton model vs the claim that there are 3, and explain the underlying assumptions leading to the different conclusions. We also systematically explore the antiquark correlator and, to the best of our knowledge, for the first time derive the model expressions for all T-even leading and subleading antiquark TMDs. We demonstrate the consistency of the framework which can be generalized in future studies for more sophisticated TMD modelling.

II
Quark and antiquark correlators
and TMDs in quark models

In this section, we will review the properties of

Quark case

In the parton model the quark fields satisfy the Dirac equation

(i\not{\partial}-m_{q})\,\Psi^{q}(z)=0
. Writing out all Dirac
indices and performing an integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}z}{(2\pi)^{4}}\;\mathrm{e}^{ikz}\,\langle N|\,\overline{\Psi}_{j}^{\,q}(0)\;\Gamma_{jl}\biggl[(i\overrightarrow{\not{\partial}}-m_{q})_{li}\Psi_{i}^{q}(z)\,\biggr]|N\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\Gamma_{jl}(\not{k}-m_{q})_{li}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{4}z}{(2\pi)^{4}}\;\mathrm{e}^{ikz}\,\langle N|\,\overline{\Psi}_{j}^{\,q}(0)\Psi_{i}^{q}(z)\ |N\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\Gamma_{jl}(\not{k}-m_{q})_{li}\phi^{q}_{ij}(k,P,S)={\rm tr}\biggl[\Gamma(\not{k}-m_{q})\phi^{q}(k,P,S)\biggr]=0\,,

(7)

where
\Gamma
can be any Dirac matrix. It is convenient
to define identities for the quark correlator (
II.1
) as follows

{\rm Tr}^{q}[\Gamma]\equiv\frac{1}{4}\;{\rm tr}\,\biggl[(\not{k}-m_{q})\,\Phi^{q}(k,P,S)\Gamma\biggr]=0\,.

(8)

Exploring these identities for

\Gamma=\mathbb{1},\,\gamma_{5},\,\gamma^{\mu},\,\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5},\,i\,\sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}
yields relations among the
amplitudes in the parton model. It is instructive to show
the derivation of these relations in detail.
Let us consider first
\Gamma=\gamma^{\mu}
which yields

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}^{q}[\gamma^{\mu}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k^{\mu}\biggl\{MA_{1}^{q}-m_{q}A_{3}^{q}+i\frac{(P\cdot k)}{M}\,A_{4}^{q}\biggr\}-P^{\mu}m_{q}\biggl\{A_{2}^{q}+i\,\frac{m_{q}}{M}\,A_{4}^{q}\biggr\}+\varepsilon^{\mu\alpha\beta\gamma}k_{\alpha}P_{\beta}S_{\gamma}\,\biggl\{iA_{9}^{q}-\frac{m_{q}}{M}\,A_{12}^{q}\biggr\}=0\,.\quad

(9)

As the four-vectors
k_{\mu}
,
P_{\mu}
,
S_{\mu}
and

\varepsilon_{\mu\rho\sigma\tau}k^{\rho}P^{\sigma}S^{\tau}
are
linearly independent, each of the expressions in the curly
brackets must vanish separately. Since the
A_{i}
’s are real,
in each of the curly brackets the real and the imaginary parts
must vanish separately. In this way, we find the relations

\displaystyle A_{1}^{q}=\frac{m_{q}}{M}\,A_{3}^{q},\quad A_{2}^{q}=0,\quad A_{4}^{q}=0,\quad A_{9}^{q}=0,\quad A_{12}^{q}=0.

(10)

Exploring
{\rm Tr}^{q}[\mathbb{1}]
yields no new information
beyond what we found in (
10
).
Considering
\Gamma=\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}
yields

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}^{q}[\gamma^{\mu}\gamma_{5}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k^{\mu}(k\cdot S)\,\biggl\{-iA_{5}^{q}+\frac{m_{q}}{M}\,A_{8}^{q}+A_{10}^{q}-\frac{(P\cdot k)}{M^{2}}\,A_{11}^{q}\biggr\}

(11)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle P^{\mu}(k\cdot S)\,\biggl\{\frac{m_{q}}{M}A_{7}^{q}+A_{9}^{q}+\frac{k^{2}}{M^{2}}\,A_{11}^{q}\biggr\}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle S^{\mu}\,\biggl\{m_{q}MA_{6}^{q}-(P\cdot k)A_{9}^{q}-k^{2}A_{10}^{q}\biggr\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,.

We again explore that the
A_{i}^{q}
’s
are real and
k_{\mu}
,
P_{\mu}
,
S_{\mu}
,

\varepsilon_{\mu\rho\sigma\tau}k^{\rho}P^{\sigma}S^{\tau}

are linearly independent such that the real and
imaginary parts in each curly bracket in
(
11
) vanish. Considering that

k^{2}=m_{q}^{2}
and using
A_{9}^{q}=0
from
(
10
) we obtain

A_{5}^{q}=0,\quad A_{6}^{q}=\frac{m_{q}}{M}\,A_{10}^{q},\quad A_{7}^{q}=-\frac{m_{q}}{M}\,A_{11}^{q},\quad A_{10}^{q}=\frac{(P\cdot k)}{M^{2}}\,A_{11}^{q}-\frac{m_{q}}{M}\,A_{8}^{q}\,.

(12)

Considering
{\rm Tr}^{q}[\gamma_{5}]
or

{\rm Tr}^{q}[i\sigma^{\mu\nu}\gamma_{5}]
gives no
new information beyond (
12
).

Several comments are in order. The T-odd amplitudes

A_{4}^{q}
,
A_{5}^{q}
,
A_{12}^{q}
vanish in the parton model
which is expected as we deal with a quark model without
explicit gauge field degrees of freedom
Pobylitsa:2002fr
. Interestingly, in
the parton model also the T-even amplitudes
A_{2}^{q}
and
A_{9}^{q}

vanish. In QCD, these amplitudes receive contributions from
”genuine twist-3” quark-gluon correlators and are non-zero.
In the parton model, the amplitudes
A_{1}^{q}
,

A_{6}^{q}
,
A_{7}^{q}
are proportional to current quark masses.
In QCD, these amplitudes contain, besides mass
terms, also contributions from quark-gluon correlators.

For our purposes it is important to notice that, after exploring the
equations of motion, all non-zero amplitudes are related in one or
another way to
A_{3}^{q}
,
A_{8}^{q}
,
A_{11}^{q}
, i.e. to one unpolarized
amplitude (
A_{3}^{q}
), one chiral even polarized amplitude (
A_{8}^{q}
),
and one chiral odd polarized amplitude (
A_{11}^{q}
).

III.2
Antiquark case

In the antiquark case, we start from the free Dirac equation

\bar{\Psi}^{q}(z)(i\overleftarrow{\not{\partial}}+m_{q})=0

where the arrow indicates which field is differentiated.
Proceeding similarly to the quark case, we have

\displaystyle\int\dfrac{d^{4}z}{(2\pi)^{4}}\;\mathrm{e}^{ik\cdot z}\,\langle N|\,\Gamma_{li}\Psi_{i}^{q}(0)\;\biggl[\overline{\Psi}_{j}^{\,q}(z)(i\overleftarrow{\not{\partial}}+m_{q})_{jl}\biggr]|N\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\Gamma_{li}\int\dfrac{d^{4}z}{(2\pi)^{4}}\;\mathrm{e}^{ik\cdot z}\,\langle N|\Psi_{i}^{q}(0)\;\overline{\Psi}_{j}^{\,q}(z)|N\rangle\,(\not{k}+m_{q})_{jl}

\displaystyle\quad\quad=\Gamma_{li}\phi^{\bar{q}}_{ij}(k,P,S)(\not{k}+m_{q})_{jl}={\rm tr}\biggl[\Gamma\phi^{\bar{q}}(k,P,S)(\not{k}+m_{q})\biggr]=0\,.

(13)

It is convenient to introduce the notation for the identities

{\rm Tr}^{\bar{q}}[\Gamma]\equiv\frac{1}{4}\;{\rm tr}\,\biggl[\Gamma\,\Phi^{\bar{q}}(k,P,S)\,(\not{k}+m_{q})\biggr]=0\,,

(14)

where
\Gamma
can be again any Dirac matrix. Proceeding
analog to the quark correlator case, we obtain from
(
14
) the following relations
among the antiquark amplitudes,

\displaystyle A_{1}^{\bar{q}}=-\,\frac{m_{q}}{M}\,A_{3}^{\bar{q}},\quad A_{2}^{\bar{q}}=0,\quad A_{4}^{\bar{q}}=0,\quad A_{5}^{\bar{q}}=0,\quad A_{6}^{\bar{q}}=-\frac{m_{q}}{M}\,A_{10}^{\bar{q}},

(15)

\displaystyle A_{7}^{\bar{q}}=\frac{m_{q}}{M}\,A_{11}^{\bar{q}},\quad A_{9}^{\bar{q}}=0,\quad A_{10}^{\bar{q}}=\frac{(P\cdot k)}{M^{2}}\,A_{11}^{\bar{q}}+\frac{m_{q}}{M}\,A_{8}^{\bar{q}}\,,\quad A_{12}^{\bar{q}}=0.

(16)

Analogously to the quark case, also the T-odd antiquark
amplitudes
A_{4}^{\bar{q}}
,
A_{5}^{\bar{q}}
,
A_{12}^{\bar{q}}

vanish and so do the T-even amplitudes
A_{2}^{\bar{q}}
and

A_{9}^{\bar{q}}
. All non-zero amplitudes are related
in one or another way to three amplitudes
A_{3}^{\bar{q}}
,

A_{8}^{\bar{q}}
,
A_{11}^{\bar{q}}
which remain unconstrained
by the equations of motion. The relations of the antiquark
amplitudes in
(
15
,
16
)
resemble those in the quark case in
(
10
,
12
)
except that the current quark mass
m_{q}
enters with
opposite sign.

IV
Quark correlator in massive case

Inserting the relations in (
10
,
12
)
in the quark correlator in (
II.1
) we obtain

\Phi^{q}(k,P,S)=\Phi^{q}(k,P,S)_{\rm unp}+\Phi^{q}(k,P,S)_{\rm pol}

(17)

where

\displaystyle\Phi^{q}(k,P,S)_{\rm unp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\not{k}+m_{q})\,A_{\rm unp}^{q}\quad\mbox{with}\quad A_{\rm unp}^{q}=A_{3}^{q}\,,

\displaystyle\Phi^{q}(k,P,S)_{\rm pol}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\not{k}+m_{q})\gamma_{5}\biggl\{\not{P}\,\frac{(k\cdot S)}{M^{2}}A_{11}^{q}-\not{S}\,\frac{(P\cdot k)\,A_{11}^{q}-m_{q}M\,A_{8}^{q}}{M^{2}}+\frac{(k\cdot S)}{M}\;A_{8}^{q}\biggr\}\,.

(18)

In the parton model
\not{k}\not{k}=k^{2}=m_{q}^{2}

and the last term in the curly bracket of
(
IV
) can be
written for
m_{q}\neq 0
as

(\not{k}+m_{q})\gamma_{5}\,\frac{(k\cdot S)}{M}\;A_{8}^{q}=-(\not{k}+m_{q})\gamma_{5}\,\frac{\not{k}}{m_{q}}\,\frac{(k\cdot S)}{M}\;A_{8}^{q}\,.

(19)

Inserting the relation (
19
) into
(
IV
) allows us to write
the polarization-dependent part of the correlator as

\displaystyle\Phi^{q}(k,P,S)_{\rm pol}=(\not{k}+m_{q})\gamma_{5}\,\not{w}\,A_{\rm pol}^{q}\;\;

(20)

where we introduce the polarized amplitude
A_{\rm pol}^{q}

and the axial 4-vector
w_{q}^{\mu}
defined as

\displaystyle A_{\rm pol}^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{(P\cdot k)\,A_{11}^{q}-m_{q}M\,A_{8}^{q}}{M^{2}}\,,

(21a)

\displaystyle w_{q}^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{\mu}-P^{\mu}\frac{(k\cdot S)\,A_{11}^{q}}{(P\cdot k)\,A_{11}^{q}-m_{q}M\,A_{8}^{q}}+k^{\mu}\frac{M}{m_{q}}\,\frac{(k\cdot S)A_{8}^{q}}{(P\cdot k)\,A_{11}^{q}-m_{q}M\,A_{8}^{q}}\;.

(21b)

We note that for
w^{\mu}_{q}
to be well-defined in
(
21a
)
it must be
m_{q}\neq 0
(which is the case in this section)
and the condition
(P\cdot k)\,A_{11}^{q}-m_{q}M\,A_{8}^{q}\neq 0

must hold. We will follow up on this shortly.

IV.1
Quark polarization vector

The 4-vector
w_{q}^{\mu}
has the following important properties.
It has the transformation properties of a polarization vector,
i.e. of an axial 4-vector, and satisfies the condition

w_{q}\cdot k=0\,.

(22)

These 2 properties are necessary conditions for
w^{\mu}_{q}

to be a candidate expression for a quark polarization vector.
Remarkably, the condition
w_{q}\cdot k=0
holds for any
A_{8}^{q}

and
A_{11}^{q}
without imposing a relation between these
amplitudes.

In order to be a quark polarization vector,

w^{\mu}_{q}
must in addition satisfy the requirements

Condition (A)

\displaystyle-1<\;

\displaystyle w_{q}^{2}

\displaystyle<0

mixed-spin state,

Condition (B)

\displaystyle w_{q}^{2}

\displaystyle=-1\quad

pure-spin state.

The square of the quark polarization vector is given by

w_{q}^{2}=S^{2}+\frac{(k\cdot S)^{2}M^{2}}{[(P\cdot k)\,A_{11}^{q}-m_{q}M\,A_{8}^{q}]^{2}}\,\biggl((A_{11}^{q})^{2}-(A_{8}^{q})^{2}\biggr)\,.

(23)

In the following, we will explore both possibilities
(A) to which we will refer as the mixed-spin version
of the model, and (B) to which we will refer as the
pure-spin version of the model.

IV.2
Mixed-spin model

Let us first discuss the mixed-spin version of the
model which requires
-1<\omega_{q}^{2}<0
.
The condition
\omega^{2}_{q}>-1
is equivalent to

|A^{q}_{11}|>|A^{q}_{8}|
.
The condition
\omega^{2}_{q}<0
is satisfied as long as

(P\cdot k\;A^{q}_{11}-m_{q}MA^{q}_{8})^{2}>0
which is always the
case because we had to assume
in (
21a
) that
(P\cdot k)A^{q}_{11}-m_{q}MA^{q}_{8}\neq 0
.
Thus, in the mixed-spin case, besides the inequality

|A^{q}_{11}|>|A^{q}_{8}|
, the amplitudes
A_{8}^{q}
and

A_{11}^{q}
remain unrelated.

In this approach, the quark correlator is described in terms of
3 independent amplitudes, namely
A^{q}_{3}
,
A^{q}_{8}
,
A^{q}_{11}
,
i.e. in terms of one unpolarized amplitude and two polarized
amplitudes: one chiral even and one chiral odd.

IV.3
Pure-spin state model

If we demand the quarks to be in a pure-spin state,

w^{\mu}_{q}
must be normalized as
w^{2}_{q}=-1
in
(
23
).
This means that the second term on the right-hand-side of
(
23
) must vanish which implies the following
condition

w_{q}^{2}=-1\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad A_{11}^{q}=\pm\,A_{8}^{q}\,.

(24a)

The 2 solutions in (
24a
) lead to 2
different solutions for
A^{q}_{\rm pol\pm}
and
w^{\mu}_{q\pm}
.
One way of writing the pertinent solutions consists in
eliminating the chiral odd amplitude
A_{11}^{q}
which yields

\displaystyle A_{\rm pol\pm}^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{(\pm P\cdot k)-m_{q}M}{M^{2}}\,\,A_{8}^{q}\,,

(24b)

\displaystyle w^{\mu}_{q\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{\mu}-\frac{(\pm k\cdot S)}{(\pm P\cdot k)-m_{q}M}\,P^{\mu}+\frac{M}{m_{q}}\,\frac{(\pm k\cdot S)}{(\pm P\cdot k)-m_{q}M}\,(\pm k^{\mu})\;.

(24c)

This is not the most economic notation, but we have chosen
it to avoid the usage of
\mp
and in this way the
\pm

always appear together with
k
.
We will show below in Sec.
VI
that one of
the 2 solutions is physical, and the other one is unphysical.
Before that, however, we will discuss the massless case.

V
Quark correlator in the massless case

When
m_{q}=0
, the analysis of the previous section cannot
be carried out because for massless quarks a polarization
vector
w_{q}^{\mu}
cannot be defined, see e.g.
Barone:2001sp
;
Collins-book
. This can be seen directly
in (
24b
) where for
m_{q}\to 0
one
would encounter a
1/m_{q}
-singularity.
In the case
m_{q}=0
, one has to proceed in a different way
as shown below.

The relations derived from the equations of motion in
(
10
,
12
) are of course
valid also for
m_{q}=0
. Inserting these relations for

m_{q}=0
in the quark correlator in (
II.1
)
yields an unpolarized and a polarized contribution to
the quark correlator in (
17
)
which are given by

\displaystyle\Phi^{q}(k,P,S)_{\rm unp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\not{k}\,A_{\rm unp}^{q}\,,

\displaystyle\Phi^{q}(k,P,S)_{\rm pol}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\not{k}\,\gamma_{5}\biggl\{-\lambda\;+\not{b}_{T}\biggr\}\,A^{q}_{\rm pol}\,

(25)

where, assuming
A^{q}_{8}\neq 0
, we defined

A_{\rm unp}^{q}=A_{3}^{q},\quad A^{q}_{\rm pol}=-\,\frac{(P\cdot k)}{M^{2}}\;A_{8}^{q}\,,\quad\lambda=\frac{M(k\cdot S)}{(P\cdot k)}\,,\quad b_{T}^{\mu}=\biggl(S^{\mu}-P^{\mu}\,\frac{(k\cdot S)}{(P\cdot k)}\biggr)\,\frac{A_{11}^{q}}{A_{8}^{q}}\,.

(26)

The quantities
\lambda
and
b_{T}^{\mu}
are defined in
(
26
) following the common
conventions in literature, see for instance (2.3.8)
in Ref.
Barone:2001sp
or Appendix A of
Ref.
Collins-book
.
Their properties are discussed in the next section.

V.1
Spin density matrix of massless quarks

The quantities
\lambda
and
b_{T}^{\mu}
in
(
26
)
have the following properties.

(i)

In the high-energy limit when the nucleon moves very fast,
i.e.
P^{\mu}\to\infty
, and is polarized along its direction of
motion, the nucleon polarization vector is given by

S^{\mu}=\lambda_{N}P^{\mu}/M+\dots

where
\lambda_{N}=\pm 1
is (twice) the helicity of the nucleon

Barone:2001sp

(see Sec.
IX.5
for
a reminder) and the dots indicate
terms suppressed in the high-energy limit. In such an
infinite momentum frame, the quantity
\lambda
defined
in (
26
) is
given by
\lambda=\lambda_{N}
, i.e. depending on the sign
of
A^{q}_{\rm pol}
, the massless quark has the same or opposite
helicity as the nucleon. Thus in the infinite momentum frame,

\lambda
has the expected intuitive interpretation as
(twice) the quark helicity.

(ii)

In the general case, a quark may of course have transverse
polarization. In the massless case, this is described by the
vector
b_{T}^{\mu}
which is transverse with respect to the quark
momentum
k^{\mu}
.
It is important to stress that
b_{T}^{\mu}
introduced in
(
26
) has the property

k\cdot b_{T}=0
for any
A_{8}^{q}
and
A_{11}^{q}
.

(iii)

Also in the massless case, we can distinguish pure-spin
and mixed-spin states. They are defined as follows:

Condition

(A)

\displaystyle-1<\;

\displaystyle b_{T}^{2}-\lambda^{2}

\displaystyle<0

mixed-spin state,

Condition

(B)

\displaystyle b_{T}^{2}-\lambda^{2}

\displaystyle=-1\quad

pure-spin state.

(27)

V.2
Massless mixed-spin model

If one chooses to work with massless quarks in a mixed-spin
state, then

-1<b_{T}^{2}-\lambda^{2}<0
must hold.
The condition
b_{T}^{2}-\lambda^{2}>-1
will always be satisfied as long as

|A_{11}^{q}|>|A_{8}^{q}|
. The condition
b_{T}^{2}-\lambda^{2}<0
is trivial
and always satisfied without imposing any new requirements.
Recalling that we had to exclude the case
A_{8}^{q}=0
from the
very beginning, we conclude that the mixed-spin parton model
is consistently defined provided
0<|A_{8}^{q}|<|A_{11}^{q}|
.
Similarly to the massive case, in this approach the quark correlator
is described in terms of 3 independent amplitudes, namely
the unpolarized
A^{q}_{3}
, chiral even polarized
A^{q}_{8}
, and
chiral odd polarized
A^{q}_{11}
amplitude.
In practice, these 3 independent amplitudes can be determined from,
for instance
f_{1}^{q}(x)
,
g_{1}^{q}(x)
,
h_{1}^{q}(x)
at some scale which
is part of the model. This corresponds to the parton model
version discussed in Ref.
DAlesio:2009cps
.

V.3
Massless pure-spin model

If we choose to work with a parton model of massless quarks
in a pure-spin state, then the condition (B) in (
27
) implies

b_{T}^{2}-\lambda^{2}=-\,\frac{M^{2}(k\cdot S)^{2}}{(P\cdot k)^{2}}\biggl(1-\frac{(A_{11}^{q})^{2}}{(A_{8}^{q})^{2}}\biggr)+S^{2}=-1\,\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad A_{8}^{q}=\pm\,A_{11}^{q}\,.

(28)

Also in the massless case, we have two choices for a
pure-spin state parton model, one of which will turn out to
be physical and the other unphysical. We will
discuss this in the next section.

VI
Quark TMDs

In this section we will discuss the results for TMDs
following from the quark correlator in the parton model
derived in the previous sections. We will keep
m_{q}\neq 0

and comment on the massless case where necessary.

VI.1
Unpolarized TMDs

The results for the T-even unpolarized TMDs are of course
independent of the (mixed-spin or pure-spin state)
polarization of partons, and can be uniquely expressed
in terms of the amplitude
A_{3}^{q}
in the following way

\displaystyle f_{1}^{q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\biggl[xA_{3}^{q}(P\cdot k)\biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(29a)

\displaystyle f^{\perp q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\biggl[A_{3}^{q}(P\cdot k)\biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(29b)

\displaystyle e^{q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\biggl[\frac{m_{q}}{M}\,A_{3}^{q}(P\cdot k)\biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}}.

(29c)

Here and in the following we shall abbreviate
the notation for the amplitudes as

A_{3}^{q}(P\cdot k,\,k^{2})=A_{3}^{q}(P\cdot k)
because
in the parton model
k^{2}=m_{q}^{2}
is fixed
(but we will reinstate the notation

A_{3}^{q}(P\cdot k,\,k^{2})
when it will become important
to stress the specific
k^{2}
dependence in the parton
model).

We see that
e^{q}(x,k_{T})
is proportional to the current
quark mass and becomes zero if one considers the parton
model with massless quarks. This is the only TMD in the
parton model with this property.

VI.2
Polarized TMDs for partons in mixed-spin state

The T-even chiral even polarized TMDs in the
mixed-spin state parton model with massive quarks
are expressed in terms of the chiral even amplitude

A_{8}^{q}
and the chiral odd amplitude
A_{11}^{q}

entering as current quark mass effect.
The model expressions are given by

\displaystyle g_{1}^{q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[\frac{x^{2}M^{2}-x\,P\cdot k+m_{q}^{2}}{M^{2}}\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)-\frac{m_{q}}{M}\,x\,A_{11}^{q}(P\cdot k)\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(30a)

\displaystyle g_{1T}^{\perp q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[xA_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)-\dfrac{m_{q}}{M}A_{11}^{q}(P\cdot k)\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(30b)

\displaystyle g_{T}^{q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[\dfrac{\vec{k}_{T}^{2}+2m_{q}^{2}}{2M^{2}}\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)-\dfrac{m_{q}}{M}\;\dfrac{P\cdot k}{M^{2}}\,A_{11}^{q}(P\cdot k)\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(30c)

\displaystyle g_{L}^{\perp q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[\frac{x\,M^{2}-P\cdot k}{M^{2}}\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(30d)

\displaystyle g_{T}^{\perp q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}}.

(30e)

For the T-even chiral odd polarized TMDs the situation
is opposite: they are given in terms of the
chiral odd amplitude
A_{11}^{q}
while the chiral even
amplitude
A_{8}^{q}
enters as current
quark mass effect. The model expressions read

\displaystyle h_{1}^{q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[\dfrac{\vec{k}_{T}^{2}-2\,x\,P\cdot k}{2M^{2}}\,A_{11}^{q}(P\cdot k)+x\,\dfrac{m_{q}}{M}\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(31a)

\displaystyle h_{1L}^{\perp q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[xA_{11}^{q}(P\cdot k)-\dfrac{m_{q}}{M}A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(31b)

\displaystyle h_{1T}^{\perp q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[A_{11}^{q}(P\cdot k)\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(31c)

\displaystyle h_{L}^{q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[\frac{x^{2}M^{2}-2\,x\,P\cdot k}{M^{2}}\,A_{11}^{q}(P\cdot k)+\dfrac{m_{q}}{M}\;\dfrac{P\cdot k}{M^{2}}\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(31d)

\displaystyle h_{T}^{q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[\frac{x\,M^{2}-P\cdot k}{M^{2}}\,A_{11}^{q}(P\cdot k)\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(31e)

\displaystyle h_{T}^{\perp q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[-\dfrac{P\cdot k}{M^{2}}A_{11}^{q}(P\cdot k)+\dfrac{m_{q}}{M}A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}}.

(31f)

For massless partons the situation simplifies. Then all
chiral even polarized TMDs are expressed in terms of the
amplitude
A_{8}^{q}
, while all chiral odd polarized TMDs are expressed in terms of the amplitude

A_{11}^{q}
.

VI.3
Polarized TMDs for partons in pure-spin state

In the pure-spin state parton model all polarized
(chiral even
and
chiral odd) TMDs, can be expressed in terms
of one single amplitude. We choose
A_{8}^{q}
for that, and replace

A_{11}^{q}
by
A_{11}^{q}=\pm A_{8}^{q}
. In the following the upper
(lower) sign in
(\pm)
is associated with the upper
(lower) sign in the two solutions
w^{q}_{\pm}
for the quark
polarization vector and polarized amplitude
A^{q}_{\rm pol\pm}

which correspond to the choices
A_{11}^{q}=\pm A_{8}^{q}
.
For massive partons in a pure-spin state, the model expressions
for the chiral even polarized TMDs are given by

\displaystyle g_{1}^{q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[\frac{x^{2}M^{2}-x\,P\cdot k+m_{q}^{2}}{M^{2}}\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)-\dfrac{m_{q}}{M}\,x\,\biggl[\pm A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)\biggr]\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(32a)

\displaystyle g_{1T}^{\perp q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[x\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)-\dfrac{m_{q}}{M}\biggl[\pm A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)\biggr]\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(32b)

\displaystyle g_{T}^{q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[\dfrac{\vec{k}_{T}^{2}+2m_{q}^{2}}{2M^{2}}\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)-\dfrac{m_{q}}{M}\biggl[\pm\,\dfrac{P\cdot k}{M^{2}}\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)\biggr]\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(32c)

where we do not quote the expressions for
g_{L}^{\perp q}(x,k_{T})

and
g_{T}^{\perp q}(x,k_{T})
since they do not depend on
A_{11}^{q}
and
are the same as in (
30d
,
30e
).
Since
m_{q}\ll M
, the difference between the two solutions for

w^{q}_{\pm}
and
A^{q}_{\rm pol\pm}
is in practice numerically
negligibly small, and disappears in the massless case. In fact,
in the massless case the mixed-spin and pure-spin versions of
the parton model give exactly the same results for the chiral even
polarized TMDs.

The model expressions for the polarized chiral odd
TMDs are given by

\displaystyle h_{1}^{q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[\pm\,\dfrac{\vec{k}_{T}^{2}-2\,x\,P\cdot k}{2M^{2}}\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)+\dfrac{m_{q}}{M}\,x\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(33a)

\displaystyle h_{1L}^{\perp q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[\pm\,x\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)-\dfrac{m_{q}}{M}\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(33b)

\displaystyle h_{1T}^{\perp q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[\pm\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(33c)

\displaystyle h_{L}^{q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[\pm\,\frac{x^{2}M^{2}-2\,x\,P\cdot k}{M^{2}}\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)+\dfrac{m_{q}}{M}\,\dfrac{P\cdot k}{M^{2}}\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(33d)

\displaystyle h_{T}^{q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[\pm\,\frac{x\,M^{2}-P\cdot k}{M^{2}}\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}},

(33e)

\displaystyle h_{T}^{\perp q}(x,k_{T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2P^{+}\int dk^{-}\Biggl[\pm\,\biggl(-\,\dfrac{P\cdot k}{M^{2}}\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)\biggr)+\dfrac{m_{q}}{M}\,A_{8}^{q}(P\cdot k)\Biggr]_{k^{+}=xP^{+}}.

(33f)

VII
Antiquarks in the parton model

The discussion of the antiquark correlator parallels
that of the quark correlator such that we can abbreviate
many details and focus on showing the main results.

VII.1
Correlator for massive antiquarks

Inserting the relations in (
16
) in
the antiquark correlator in (
1b
)
and performing exactly the same steps as in the polarized
correlator as in (
19
) we obtain

\Phi^{\bar{q}}(k,P,S)=\Phi^{\bar{q}}(k,P,S)_{\rm unp}+\Phi^{\bar{q}}(k,P,S)_{\rm pol}

(36a)

with

\displaystyle\Phi^{\bar{q}}(k,P,S)_{\rm unp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\not{k}-m_{q})\,A_{\rm unp}^{\bar{q}}\;,

(36b)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\bar{q}}(k,P,S)_{\rm pol}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\not{k}-m_{q})\gamma_{5}\,\not{w}_{\bar{q}}\,A_{\rm pol}^{\bar{q}}\;\;

(36c)

where we introduced
A_{\rm unp}^{\bar{q}}
,

A_{\rm pol}^{\bar{q}}
,
w^{\mu}_{\bar{q}}

defined as
3
3

3

We recall that by convention
antifermions are polarized opposite to the
polarization vector, cf. Sec.
IX.5
.
We stress that this has no practical relevance
for our results and the TMDs of both quarks
and antiquarks have their usual partonic
interpretations.

\displaystyle A_{\rm unp}^{\bar{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{3}^{\bar{q}}\,,

\displaystyle A_{\rm pol}^{\bar{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{(P\cdot k)\,A_{11}^{\bar{q}}+m_{q}M\,A_{8}^{\bar{q}}}{M^{2}}\,,

(36d)

\displaystyle w^{\mu}_{\bar{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{\mu}-P^{\mu}\frac{(k\cdot S)\,A_{11}^{\bar{q}}}{(P\cdot k)\,A_{11}^{\bar{q}}+m_{q}M\,A_{8}^{\bar{q}}}-k^{\mu}\frac{M}{m_{q}}\,\frac{(k\cdot S)A_{8}^{\bar{q}}}{(P\cdot k)\,A_{11}^{\bar{q}}+m_{q}M\,A_{8}^{\bar{q}}}\;.

(36e)

In (
36e
)
we assumed that
m_{q}\neq 0
and

(P\cdot k)\,A_{11}^{\bar{q}}+m_{q}M\,A_{8}^{\bar{q}}\neq 0
.
The axial 4-vector
w_{\bar{q}}
plays the role of the
antiquark polarization vector. It satisfies

k\cdot w_{\bar{q}}=0
for any

A_{8}^{\bar{q}}
and
A_{11}^{\bar{q}}
.

In the mixed-spin version of the parton model,
we have

-1<w^{2}_{\bar{q}}<0

(37)

where the upper bound is always satisfied. The lower bound
is satisfied provided
|A_{11}^{\bar{q}}|<|A_{8}^{\bar{q}}|
.
In this version of the model, the antiquark correlator
is described in terms of three independent amplitudes

A_{3}^{\bar{q}}
,
A_{8}^{\bar{q}}
,
A_{11}^{\bar{q}}
.

In the pure-spin state version of the model, the
polarized amplitudes
A_{8}^{\bar{q}}
and
A_{11}^{\bar{q}}

are related as

w^{2}_{\bar{q}}=-1\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad A_{11}^{\bar{q}}=\pm A_{8}^{\bar{q}}\;.

(38a)

The two solutions
A_{8}^{\bar{q}}=\pm A_{11}^{\bar{q}}
imply

\displaystyle A_{\rm pol\pm}^{\bar{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\,\frac{(\pm P\cdot k)+m_{q}M}{M^{2}}\,A_{8}^{\bar{q}},

(38b)

\displaystyle w^{\mu}_{{\bar{q}}\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{\mu}-P^{\mu}\frac{(\pm k\cdot S)}{(\pm P\cdot k)+m_{q}M}-\frac{M}{m_{q}}\,\frac{(\pm k\cdot S)}{(\pm P\cdot k)+m_{q}M}\,(\pm k^{\mu})\;.

(38c)

IX.2
Relations among antiquark TMDs in the CPM

In QCD all TMDs are independent functions. But simpler
model dynamics or additional model symmetries can
generate relations between TMDs in models.
In the quark case, relations among TMDs were
found in several models.
Based on the results of the previous section we present
relations among antiquark TMDs which to the best of our
knowledge have not been derived before.
In fact, as shown in the previous section,
the antiquark TMDs are formally given by same
expressions as the quark TMDs with

{\cal G}^{q}(P\cdot k)
replaced by

{\cal G}^{\bar{q}}(P\cdot k)
and analog for

{\cal H}^{\bar{q}}(P\cdot k)
. Therefore,
the antiquark TMDs and quark TMDs satisfy
in the CPM the same model relations.
Considering how little is known from models about
nonperturbative properties of antiquark TMDs,
the new relations are of interest.

In Sec.
VIII.2
we already discussed
the EOM relations (
49
,
50
),
which are satisfied in the model with the tilde terms
absent also in the antiquark case.
Next, we consider the so-called
quark model Lorentz invariance relations (qLIRs) which
arise in effective theories without gluonic degrees
of freedom where T-odd
A_{i}^{a}
amplitudes are absent
and the 14 T-even TMDs are described in terms of 9
T-even amplitudes implying 5 relations given for
antiquarks by

Mulders:1995dh

\displaystyle g_{T}^{\bar{q}}(x)\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm qLIR}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\;g_{1}^{\bar{q}}(x)+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}g^{\perp(1){\bar{q}}}_{1T}(x)\,,

(65a)

\displaystyle h_{L}^{\bar{q}}(x)\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm qLIR}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\;h_{1}^{\bar{q}}(x)-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}h^{\perp(1){\bar{q}}}_{1L}(x)\,,

(65b)

\displaystyle h_{T}^{\bar{q}}(x)\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm qLIR}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\;-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}h^{\perp(1){\bar{q}}}_{1T}(x)\,,

(65c)

\displaystyle g_{L}^{\perp\bar{q}}(x)+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}x}g_{T}^{\perp(1)\bar{q}}(x)\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm qLIR}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\;0\,,

(65d)

\displaystyle h_{T}^{\bar{q}}(x,p_{T})-h_{T}^{\perp\bar{q}}(x,p_{T})\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm qLIR}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\;h^{\perp\bar{q}}_{1L}(x,p_{T})\,.

IX.3
Kinematic constraints, and limitations of the approach

The partonic interpretation of TMDs in QCD is done in infinite
momentum frame where
f_{1}^{a}(x,k_{T})dx
is interpreted as the
probability to find a parton of flavor
a
carrying a fraction of
the longitudinal nucleon momentum in the interval
[x,\,x+dx]

and a transverse momentum
k_{T}=|\vec{k_{T}}|
. The other twist-2
TMDs have analogous interpretations, albeit involving polarization
of the parton and/or the nucleon
Boer:1997nt
.
Bjorken-
x
is a Lorentz scalar, and transverse momenta
are not affected by longitudinal boosts. Thus, we of course
have the same
x
and
k_{T}
also in the nucleon rest
frame (where, however, we no longer a partonic interpretation
is applicable in QCD).
If
x
and
k_{T}
are specified for an on-shell parton, then
the parton 4-momentum
k^{\mu}=(E_{q},\,k^{1},\,\vec{k}_{T})
with

\vec{k}_{T}=(k^{2},\,k^{3})
is completely fixed, and we have

Zavada:2011cv

\displaystyle E_{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{xM}{2}+\frac{\vec{k}_{T}^{2}+m_{q}^{2}}{2xM}\,,

(67a)

\displaystyle k^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{xM}{2}-\frac{\vec{k}_{T}^{2}+m_{q}^{2}}{2xM}\,.

(67b)

In the nucleon rest frame, the step function
\Theta_{+}(P\cdot k)

introduced in (
60
) can be expressed and rewritten
thank to the on-shell condition as follows

\Theta_{+}(P\cdot k)=\Theta(M^{2}+m_{q}^{2}-2ME_{q})=\Theta(M^{2}-m_{q}^{2}-2M|\vec{k}|)\,.

(68)

Hence we see that
E_{q}<(M^{2}+m_{q}^{2})/(2M)
and

|\vec{k}|\leq(M^{2}-m_{q}^{2})/(2M)
, i.e. the parton energy
and parton 3-momentum are constrained in the nucleon
rest frame
Zavada:2011cv
.
From these bounds and the relations
(
67
) we see that
x
and
k_{T}
constrain
each other. For instance, for a given
k_{T}
the Bjorken
variable is in the range

\frac{1+x_{\rm min}}{2}-\sqrt{\Bigl(\frac{1-x_{\rm min}}{2}\Bigr)^{2}+\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{M^{2}}\;}\;\;<\;\;x\;\;<\;\;\frac{1+x_{\rm min}}{2}+\sqrt{\Bigl(\frac{1-x_{\rm min}}{2}\Bigr)^{2}+\frac{k_{T}^{2}}{M^{2}}\;}

(69a)

where
x_{\rm min}
is the smallest kinematically
possible
x
-value

x_{\rm min}=\frac{m_{q}^{2}}{M^{2}}\,.

(69b)

The extreme
x
-values in (
69a
) are assumed
only when
k_{T}=0
. The allowed
x
-range is then

x_{\rm min}\;\;<\;\;x\;\;<\;\;1\,.

(69c)

This is also the
x
-range in which colinear PDFs
have a finite support. In QCD, the range is
0<x<1
.
Neglecting quark masses (as it is done in all practical
applications in QCD and in the CMP), we see that the
model respects this constraint.
When
m_{q}
is finite or
k_{T}
is non-zero,
then the
x
-range is more restricted
according to (
69a
).
Considering a fixed value of
x
, we also
obtain a bound on the allowed transverse
parton momenta, namely
Zavada:2011cv

k_{T}^{2}\;\;<\;\;(1-x)\,(x-x_{\rm min})\,M^{2}\,.

(69d)

This result shows a limitation of the model.
In QCD, a necessary condition for the applicability
of TMD factorization is that
k_{T}\ll Q
. The hard scale

Q
can in practice be large enough, such that transverse
momenta
k_{T}\sim M
and larger may be relevant for the
phenomenological description of a DIS reaction

Schweitzer:2010tt
. However, from
(
69d

Comparison to other approaches in literature

It is instructive to review first the spinor description
in free theory. The spinors of a spin-
\frac{1}{2}

fermion or antifermion of mass

customarily denoted by

If

(\not{k}-m_{q})\,u(k,n)=0\,,\quad(\not{k}+m_{q})\,v(k,n)=0\,.

(71)

We choose the normalization

\bar{u}(k,n)u(k,n)=-\bar{v}(k,n)v(k,n)=2m_{q}

The polarization is revealed by acting on the spinors

\frac{(-W\cdot n)}{m_{q}}\,u(k,n)=\pm\,\frac{1}{2}\,u(k,n)\,,\quad\frac{(-W\cdot n)}{m_{q}}\,v(k,n)=\pm\,\frac{1}{2}\,v(k,n)\,,

(72)

with the two signs depending on whether the particles
are spin-up or spin-down. If in the particle rest frame
one chooses

(-W\cdot n)/m_{q}
coincides with the

of the familiar intrinsic spin operator.
The spinors satisfy the completeness relations

\displaystyle u(k,n)\otimes\bar{u}(k,n)

\displaystyle(\not{k}+m_{q})P(n)\,\,,

(73a)

\displaystyle v(k,n)\otimes\bar{v}(k,n)

\displaystyle(\not{k}-m_{q})P(n)\,\,.

(73b)

X
Conclusions

We have studied the description of TMDs in the
parton model. We have explored the equations
of motion to show that the quark correlator can be
expressed in terms of a spin density matrix which must
be treated differently in the cases of massive
and massless quarks.
In either case, one has a choice how to describe
the quark polarization state.

If one chooses to work with quarks in a pure-spin
state, the nonperturbative information content of
the quark correlator is described in terms of two
independent amplitudes, which can be chosen to be

A_{3}^{q}
describing unpolarized TMDs and
A_{8}^{q}

describing polarized TMDs.
If one chooses to work with quarks in a mixed-spin
state, the nonperturbative information is described
in terms of three independent amplitudes, which can
be chosen to be
A_{3}^{q}
describing unpolarized TMDs
and
A_{8}^{q}
and
A_{11}^{q}
describing, respectively,
polarized chiral even and chiral odd TMDs.

One central result of our work is that we
have reconciled conflicting results in
literature regarding how many independent
covariant functions are needed to describe
the nonperturbative information contained
in the quark correlator in the parton model,
namely 2 vs 3 in
Refs.
Bastami:2020rxn
vs

DAlesio:2009cps
.
In fact, there really is only one unifying
parton model framework to which we refer as
Covariant Parton Model (CPM) where, however,
one can choose to work with quarks in pure- or
mixed-spin states. The pure-spin state
version of the CPM was
explored in Refs.
Zavada:1996kp
;
Zavada:2001bq
;
Zavada:2002uz
;
Efremov:2004tz
;

;
Efremov:2009ze
;
Zavada:2009ska
;
Efremov:2009vb
;
Efremov:2010mt
;
Zavada:2011cv
;
Zavada:2013ola
;
Zavada:2015gaa
;
Zavada:2019yom
;
Bastami:2020rxn
and describes the nucleon
structure in terms of two independent amplitudes.
The mixed-spin version of the CPM
was studied in Ref.
DAlesio:2009cps
for
massless partons and describes quark TMDs in
terms of 3 independent amplitudes.

The assumption of massless quarks is natural since
in DIS processes current quark mass effects are
suppressed by powers of

where

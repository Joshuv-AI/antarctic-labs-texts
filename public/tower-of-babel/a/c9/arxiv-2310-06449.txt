Stability Analysis of a Non-Separable Mean-Field Games for Pedestrian Flow in Large Corridors
Mohamed Ghattassi Nader Masmoudi Eliot Pacherie

Abstract
We investigate the existence and stability of small perturbations of constant states of the generalized Hughes model for pedestrian flow in an infinitely large corridor. We show that constant flows are stable under a condition on the density. Our findings indicates that when the density is less than half of the maximum density ρ m / 2 \displaystyle\rho_{m}/2 , which is the Lasry-Lions monotonicity condition, we can control the perturbation and prove postive stability results for the nonlinear Generalized Hughes model. However, due to wave propagation phenomena, we are unable to provide an answer for stability results when the density is higher. Our approach involves constructing an explicit solution for the linear problem in Fourier analysis and demonstrating, through a fixed-point argument, how to construct the solution for the full nonlinear mean-field games system. Keywords: Stability Analysis, Generalized Hughes model, Non-separable Mean-fields games.

1.2.1

Lemma 1.4
.

Consider the linear problem without viscosity

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\psi-(f(\bar{\rho})-2\bar{\rho})\partial_{x}\psi-\bar{\rho}f^{2}(\bar{\rho})\Delta\varphi=0&\quad(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,\,\,\,t\in(0,T)\\

\varphi=B\sin(ax+by+ct)

these solutions do not prevent solutions of the linear problem with finite
energy to decay, since they have infinite energy. We are not able at this
point to say whether or not small solutions of the equation with
\displaystyle\bar{\rho}>\frac{\rho_{m}}{2}
and finite energy are stable or not. What is clear, however,
is that we lose the regularizing effect that happened when
\displaystyle\bar{\rho}<\frac{\rho_{m}}{2}

stability result (for instance, the decay in time will be slower than in
Theorem
1.2

here.

2.1
Diagonalization of
\displaystyle A(\xi)

From classical computations we check the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Take
\displaystyle\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\displaystyle\theta(\xi)\neq 0
.
Then, the matrix
\displaystyle A(\xi)
is diagonalizable. Its eigenvalues are

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\xi)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(-\theta(\xi)+2i(f(\bar{\rho})-\bar{\rho})\xi_{1})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(\xi)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(\theta(\xi)+2i(f(\bar{\rho})-\bar{\rho})\xi_{1}),

with the associated eigenvectors

V_{1}(\xi):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{f(\bar{\rho})}{2}(\theta(\xi)+2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1})\\
1\end{array}\right),V_{2}(\xi):=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\frac{f(\bar{\rho})}{2}(-\theta(\xi)+2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1})\\
1\end{array}\right)

and we have

A(\xi)=P\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda_{1}(\xi)&0\\
0&\lambda_{2}(\xi)\end{array}\right)P^{-1}

where

P=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{f(\bar{\rho})}{2}(\theta(\xi)+2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1})&\frac{f(\bar{\rho})}{2}(-\theta(\xi)+2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1})\\
1&1\end{array}\right)

and

P^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{f(\bar{\rho})\theta(\xi)}&\frac{\theta(\xi)-2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1}}{\theta(\xi)}\\
\frac{-1}{f(\bar{\rho})\theta(\xi)}&\frac{\theta(\xi)+2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1}}{\theta(\xi)}\end{array}\right).

Now, if
\displaystyle\theta(\xi)\neq 0

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{u}(\xi,t)\\
\hat{v}(\xi,t)\end{array}\right):=P^{-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{\psi}(\xi,t)\\
\hat{\varphi}(\xi,t)\end{array}\right)

\hat{v}\end{array}\right).

This decouples the two equations. We have
\displaystyle\partial_{t}\hat{u}=\lambda_{1}(\xi)\hat{u}
that we integrate forward in time and
\displaystyle\partial_{t}\hat{v}=\lambda_{2}(\xi)\hat{v}
that we integrate backward in time. That is,

\hat{u}(\xi,t)=e^{\lambda_{1}(\xi)t}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi),\hat{v}(\xi,t)=e^{-\lambda_{2}(\xi)(T-t)}\hat{v}_{T}(\xi).

(2.2)

2.2
Computation of

and

We recall that
\displaystyle\theta(\xi)=2\sqrt{\bar{\rho}(f(\bar{\rho})-\bar{\rho})|\xi_{1}|^{2}+\bar{\rho}f(\bar{\rho})|\xi_{2}|^{2}}

if
\displaystyle f(\bar{\rho})-\bar{\rho}>0

\frac{|\xi|}{c}\geqslant\theta(\xi)\geqslant c|\xi|.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
\displaystyle\bar{\rho}<\frac{\rho_{m}}{2}
. Given

\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{0}(\xi),\hat{\varphi}_{T}(\xi)
, define the functions for
\displaystyle\xi\neq 0

\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)=\frac{(\theta(\xi)^{2}+4\bar{\rho}\xi_{1}^{2})\left(\frac{2e^{-\lambda_{2}(\xi)T}}{\theta(\xi)+2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1}}\hat{\varphi}_{T}(\xi)+\frac{2}{f(\bar{\rho})(\theta^{2}(\xi)+4\bar{\rho}^{2}\xi_{1}^{2})}\hat{\psi}_{0}(\xi)\right)}{\theta(\xi)(1+e^{-\theta(\xi)T})+2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1}(1-e^{-\theta(\xi)T})}

and

\hat{v}_{T}(\xi)=\frac{(\theta(\xi)^{2}+4\bar{\rho}\xi_{1}^{2})\left(\frac{2\hat{\varphi}_{T}(\xi)}{\theta(\xi)-2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1}}-\frac{2e^{\lambda_{1}(\xi)T}}{f(\bar{\rho})(\theta^{2}(\xi)+4\bar{\rho}^{2}\xi_{1}^{2})}\hat{\psi}_{0}(\xi)\right)}{\theta(\xi)(1+e^{-\theta(\xi)T})+2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1}(1-e^{-\theta(\xi)T})}.

Then, the functions

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{\psi}(\xi,t)\\
\hat{\varphi}(\xi,t)\end{array}\right)=P\left(\begin{array}[]{c}e^{\lambda_{1}(\xi)t}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\\
e^{-\lambda_{2}(\xi)(T-t)}\hat{v}_{T}(\xi)\end{array}\right),

where
\displaystyle P,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
are defined in Lemma
2.1
, are
solutions of equation (
2.1
) on
\displaystyle[0,T]
for
\displaystyle\xi\neq 0

with
\displaystyle\hat{\psi}(\xi,0)=\hat{\psi}_{0}(\xi),\hat{\varphi}(\xi,T)=\hat{\varphi}_{T}(\xi)
.

Proof.

From the definition of
\displaystyle\hat{u},\hat{v}
and (
2.2
), we compute that

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\\
e^{-\lambda_{2}(\xi)T}\hat{v}_{T}(\xi)\end{array}\right)=P^{-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{\psi}_{0}(\xi)\\
\hat{\varphi}_{0}(\xi)\end{array}\right)

and

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}e^{\lambda_{1}(\xi)t}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\\
\hat{v}_{T}(\xi)\end{array}\right)=P^{-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{\psi}_{T}(\xi)\\
\hat{\varphi}_{T}(\xi)\end{array}\right)

where
\displaystyle\hat{\varphi}_{0}(\xi)=\hat{\varphi}(\xi,0)
and
\displaystyle\hat{\psi}_{T}(\xi)=\hat{\psi}(\xi,T)
are not given. We want to remove them from the

The first system can be written by Lemma

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)=\frac{1}{f(\bar{\rho})\theta(\xi)}\hat{\psi}_{0}(\xi)+\frac{\theta(\xi)-2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1}}{\theta(\xi)}\hat{\varphi}_{0}(\xi)\\

and therefore

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{\theta(\xi)-2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1}}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)-\frac{1}{\theta(\xi)+2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1}}e^{-\lambda_{2}(\xi)T}\hat{v}_{T}(\xi)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{2}{f(\bar{\rho})(\theta^{2}(\xi)+4\bar{\rho}^{2}\xi_{1}^{2})}\hat{\psi}_{0}(\xi).

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}e^{\lambda_{1}(\xi)T}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)=\frac{1}{f(\bar{\rho})\theta(\xi)}\hat{\psi}_{T}(\xi)+\frac{\theta(\xi)-2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1}}{\theta(\xi)}\hat{\varphi}_{T}(\xi)\\

and therefore summing the two equations yield

e^{\lambda_{1}(\xi)T}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)+\hat{v}_{T}(\xi)=2\hat{\varphi}_{T}(\xi).

We deduce that

\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{\theta(\xi)-2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1}}&\frac{-e^{-\lambda_{2}(\xi)T}}{\theta(\xi)+2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1}}\\
e^{\lambda_{1}(\xi)T}&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\\

for
\displaystyle\xi\neq 0
since
\displaystyle\theta(\xi)
is real. Therefore,

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)\\
\hat{v}_{T}(\xi)\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{D(\xi)}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\frac{e^{-\lambda_{2}(\xi)T}}{\theta(\xi)+2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1}}\\

.

|\widehat{\nabla\varphi}|(\xi,t)=|\xi\hat{\varphi}(\xi,t)|\leqslant K(e^{-c|\xi|t}|\xi\hat{u}_{0}(\xi)|+e^{-c|\xi|(T-t)}|\xi\hat{v}_{T}(\xi)|)

3.2
Estimates of some convolutions

We define the norm

\|\hat{f}(.,t)\|:=\left\|\frac{(1+|\xi|)^{3}\hat{f}}{e^{-c|\xi|t}+e^{-c|\xi|(T-t)}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists
\displaystyle K>0
such that for any function
\displaystyle f,g
such
that
\displaystyle\|\hat{f}\|,\|\hat{g}\|<+\infty
, we have

\|\widehat{(fg)}\|\leqslant K\|\hat{f}\|\|\hat{g}\|

Proof.

We have

\widehat{(fg)}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\hat{f}(\nu)\hat{g}(\xi-\nu)d\nu

and thus

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{|\widehat{(fg)}(\xi)|}{\|\hat{f}\|\|\hat{g}\|}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{(e^{-c|\nu|t}+e^{-c|\nu|(T-t)})(e^{-c|\xi-\nu|t}+e^{-c|\xi-\nu|(T-t)})}{(1+|\nu|)^{3}(1+|\xi-\nu|)^{3}}d\nu

and we infer that

\displaystyle\displaystyle(e^{-c|\nu|t}+e^{-c|\nu|(T-t)})(e^{-c|\xi-\nu|t}+e^{-c|\xi-\nu|(T-t)})

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle K(e^{-c|\xi|t}+e^{-c|\xi|(T-t)})

for any
\displaystyle\nu,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2},0\leqslant t\leqslant T
. Indeed, by
triangular inequality we have

e^{-c|\nu|t}e^{-c|\xi-\nu|t}\leqslant e^{-c|\xi|t},

and separating the cases
\displaystyle t\leqslant\frac{T}{2}
and
\displaystyle t\geqslant\frac{T}{2}
we check that

e^{-c|\nu|t}e^{-c|\xi-\nu|(T-t)}\leqslant e^{-c|\xi|t}+e^{-c|\xi|(T-t)}.

We check that all other products can be estimates similarly.
Furthermore, since
\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1+|\xi|)^{3}}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
we
check by standard estimates that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{(1+|\nu|)^{3}(1+|\xi-\nu|)^{3}}d\nu\leqslant\frac{K}{(1+|\xi|)^{3}},

hence

|\widehat{(fg)}(\xi)|\leqslant K\|\hat{f}\|\|\hat{g}\|\frac{(e^{-c|\xi|t}+e^{-c|\xi|(T-t)})}{(1+|\xi|)^{3}},

concluding the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists
\displaystyle K>0
such that for any function
\displaystyle f

such that
\displaystyle\|\hat{f}\|<+\infty
, we have

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c|\xi|(t-s)}|\xi|\hat{f}(\xi,s)ds\right\|\leqslant K\|\hat{f}\|.

Proof.

We check that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c|\xi|(t-s)}|\xi|\hat{f}(\xi,s)ds\right|(1+|\xi|)^{3}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle\|\hat{f}\|\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c|\xi|(t-s)}|\xi|(e^{-c|\xi|s}+e^{-c|\xi|(T-s)})ds.

Now, we compute that

\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c|\xi|(t-s)}|\xi|e^{-c|\xi|s}ds\leqslant e^{-c|\xi|t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c|\xi|(t-s)}|\xi|ds\leqslant Ke^{-c|\xi|t}

and

\int_{0}^{t}e^{-2c|\xi|(t-s)}|\xi|e^{-c|\xi|(T-s)}ds\leqslant e^{-c|\xi|(T-t)}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-c|\xi|(t-s)}|\xi|ds\leqslant e^{-c|\xi|(T-t)},

which concludes the proof of this lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.4

3.3.2
Estimates on the nonlinear parts

Lemma 3.5
.

Suppose that
\displaystyle\bar{\rho}<\frac{\rho_{m}}{2}
. Then, there exists
\displaystyle c,K>0

such that, for any
\displaystyle T>0
and any
\displaystyle\psi,\nabla\varphi
such that
\displaystyle\|\hat{\psi}\|,\|\widehat{\nabla\varphi}\|<+\infty
, we have

\|S_{1}(\hat{\psi},\hat{\varphi})\|+\|S_{2}(\hat{\psi},\hat{\varphi})\|\leqslant K(\|\hat{\psi}\|+\|\widehat{\nabla\varphi}\|)^{2}(1+\|\hat{\psi}\|+\|\widehat{\nabla\varphi}\|)^{2}.

As such, the function
\displaystyle S_{1},S_{2}
are contractions on the space of functions

\displaystyle\psi,\nabla\varphi
such that
\displaystyle\|\hat{\psi}\|+\|\widehat{\nabla\varphi}\|\leqslant\varepsilon_{0}
for some small universal constant

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{0}
.

Proof.

We recall that

S_{1}(\hat{\psi},\hat{\varphi})(\xi,t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{\lambda_{1}(\xi)(t-s)}|\xi|\left(P^{-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{\operatorname{NL}_{1}}(\psi,\varphi)\\
\widehat{\operatorname{NL}_{2}}(\psi,\varphi)\end{array}\right)(\xi,s).\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\end{array}\right)\right)ds.

By Lemma
3.3
we have, for
\displaystyle c>0
small enough that

\|S_{1}(\hat{\psi},\hat{\varphi})\|\leqslant K\left\|P^{-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{\operatorname{NL}_{1}}(\psi,\varphi)\\
\widehat{\operatorname{NL}_{2}}(\psi,\varphi)\end{array}\right).\left(\begin{array}[]{c}1\\
0\end{array}\right)\right\|.

We recall that

P^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{f(\bar{\rho})\theta(\xi)}&\frac{\theta(\xi)-2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1}}{\theta(\xi)}\\
\frac{-1}{f(\bar{\rho})\theta(\xi)}&\frac{\theta(\xi)+2i\bar{\rho}\xi_{1}}{\theta(\xi)}\end{array}\right)

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\operatorname{NL}_{1}(\psi,\varphi)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\left(\frac{\bar{\rho}}{f(\bar{\rho})}-2\right)\partial_{x}(\psi^{2})+\frac{1}{f(\bar{\rho})}\partial_{x}(\psi^{3})

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\nabla.((\psi f^{2}(\bar{\rho})-2f(\bar{\rho})(\bar{\rho}+\psi)\psi+(\bar{\rho}+\psi)\psi^{2})\nabla\varphi).

A key remark here is that all the terms
\displaystyle\operatorname{NL}_{1}(\psi,\varphi)
are
the derivative of some quantities, and thus we check that

\frac{|\widehat{\operatorname{NL}_{1}}(\psi,\varphi)|(\xi,t)}{|\xi|}\leqslant K(|\widehat{(\psi^{2})}|+|\widehat{(\psi^{3})}|+|\widehat{(\psi^{2}\nabla\varphi)}|+|\widehat{(\psi^{3}\nabla\varphi)}|).

We recall that

\displaystyle\displaystyle\operatorname{NL}_{2}(\psi,\varphi)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}f^{2}(\bar{\rho})|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\psi^{2}-2f(\bar{\rho})\psi)|\nabla\varphi|^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{2f(\bar{\rho})}\psi^{2}+\partial_{x}\varphi\left(\frac{1}{f(\bar{\rho})}\psi^{2}-2\psi\right)

and thus

\displaystyle\displaystyle|\widehat{\operatorname{NL}_{2}}(\psi,\varphi)|(\xi,t)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\displaystyle K(|\widehat{(|\nabla\varphi|^{2})}|+|\widehat{(\psi|\nabla\varphi|^{2})}|+|\widehat{(\psi^{2}|\nabla\varphi|^{2})}|)

\displaystyle\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\displaystyle K(|\widehat{(\psi^{2})}|+|\widehat{(\partial_{x}\varphi\psi)}|+|\widehat{(\partial_{x}\varphi\psi^{2})}|).

Now, we check easily that if
\displaystyle\bar{\rho}<\frac{\rho_{m}}{2}
, then

\left|P^{-1}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\widehat{\operatorname{NL}_{1}}(\psi,\varphi)\\
\widehat{\operatorname{NL}_{2}}(\psi,\varphi)\end{array}\right)\right|\leqslant K\left(\frac{|\widehat{\operatorname{NL}_{1}}(\psi,\varphi)|}{|\xi|}+|\widehat{\operatorname{NL}_{2}}(\psi,\varphi)|\right)

and we can then conclude using Lemma
3.2
. A similar proof holds
for
\displaystyle S_{2}(\hat{\psi},\hat{\varphi})

∎

3.3.3
End of the proof of Theorem

Proof.

Take an intial data that satisfies

Convergence of a spatial semidiscretization for a three-dimensional stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with multiplicative noise Thanks: This work was partially supported by National Natural Science Foundation of China under grant 12301525 and Natural Science Foundation of Sichuan Province under grant 2023NSFSC1324.
Binjie Li, Qin Zhou

Abstract
This paper studies the convergence of a spatial semidiscretization of a three-dimensional stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with multiplicative noise. For non-smooth initial data, the regularity of the mild solution is investigated, and an error estimate is derived within the spatial L 2 L^{2} -norm setting. In the case of smooth initial data, two error estimates are established within the framework of general spatial L q L^{q} -norms.



Keywords:

stochastic Allen-Cahn equation, spatial semidiscretization,
discrete stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity

1
Introduction

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a bounded convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with a smooth boundary
\partial\mathcal{O}
, and let
\Delta
denote the Laplace operator with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. We consider the following stochastic Allen-Cahn equation:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}y(t)=(\Delta y+y-y^{3})(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+F(y(t))\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t),\quad 0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle y(0)=v,

(1.1b)

where the initial value
v
is given, and
W_{H}
is an infinite-dimensional
H
-cylindrical
Brownian motion.

The stochastic Allen-Cahn equations are widely used to model
phase transitions in materials science;
see e.g.,
[
4
,
10
,
15
,
27
,
33
]
.
They are also an important class of nonlinear stochastic partial
differential equations from a mathematical perspective. Recently,
researchers in the field of numerical analysis have shown
considerable interest in this topic; see
[
2
,
3
,
5
,
6
,
9
,
14
,
20
,
21
,
23
,
27
,
31
,
36
]
and
the references therein. We briefly summarize some closely related
works of numerical analysis of three-dimensional stochastic
Allen-Cahn equations as follows.
Feng et al.
[
9
]
analyzed finite element approximations
of a stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with gradient-type multiplicative noise.
Majee and Prohl
[
23
]
derived strong convergence rates for
a stochastic Allen-Cahn equation driven by a multiplicative
finite-dimensional Wiener process.
Bréhier et al.
[
5
]
analyzed an explicit temporal
splitting numerical scheme for a stochastic Allen-Cahn equation driven
by an additive infinite-dimensional Wiener process.
Jentzen and Pusnik
[
14
]
proposed a numerical method for
approximating a class of semilinear stochastic evolution equations
with non-globally Lipschitz continuous nonlinearities.
Qi and Wang
[
31
]
derived strong approximation errors for a
stochastic Allen-Cahn equation driven by an additive infinite-dimensional
Wiener process.
Liu and Qiao
[
21
]
established a general theory of the numerical
analysis for the stochastic Allen-Cahn equations driven by a multiplicative
infinite-dimensional Wiener process.
Qi et al.
[
32
]
proposed an efficient approximation method to the
stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with random diffusion coefficient field
and multiplicative noise.

The main motivation of this work includes two components:

•

To the best of our knowledge, existing works on the numerical analysis of the three-dimensional stochastic Allen-Cahn equation have not adequately addressed scenarios involving non-smooth initial data. This scenario is of practical significance, as initial phases may include randomly distributed particles. The inclusion of non-smooth initial data introduces heightened complexity to numerical analysis compared to cases with smooth initial data. Firstly, the theoretical analysis of the three-dimensional stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with multiplicative noise and non-smooth initial data is relatively scarce (see, e.g.,
[
18
,
19
]
). Secondly, the singularity introduced by non-smooth initial data significantly complicates the process of numerical analysis.

•

In the existing literature, the numerical analysis of the three-dimensional stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with multiplicative noise primarily focuses on the spatial
L^{2}
-norm. However, error estimates using the more general spatial
L^{q}
-norms are relatively scarce.

The main contributions of this work are as follows:

•

For the model problem
Eq.

1.1
with non-smooth initial
data
v\in L^{\infty}
, we derive some regularity estimates within the
framework of the general spatial
L^{q}
-norms, which are crucial for
the numerical analysis. Specifically, we affirm that

y\in L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];L^{q})),\quad\forall p,q\in(2,\infty).

Our analysis uses a suite of primary tools, including:
(i) stochastic integration and Itô’s formula in UMD spaces
[
7
]
;
(ii) the stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity estimates developed in
[
25
,
26
]
;
(iii) the theory of stochastic partial differential equations with Lyapunov
conditions from
[
18
,
19
]
; (iv) the maximal
L^{p}
-regularity
theory for deterministic parabolic equations
[
37
]
.

•

To handle the singularity caused by the non-smooth initial data,
by decomposing the error into a singular part and a relatively non-singular
part, we derive the error estimate

\lVert{(y-y_{h})(t)}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2})}\leqslant c\big(h^{\alpha/p}+h^{2/p}t^{-1/p}\big),\quad\forall t\in(0,T],

where
p\in(2,\infty)
and
\alpha\in(1,2)
can be arbitrarily chosen.
This result is a stochastic counterpart of the well-known error estimate
in
[
35
, Theoerem 3.2]
.

•

Given smooth initial data
v\in W_{0}^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{O})\cap W^{2,\infty}(\mathcal{O})
,
we mainly use the standard theory of finite element methods
(see, e.g.,
[
1
,
35
]
),
the discrete maximal
L^{p}
-regularity
[
16
]
,
and the discrete stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity
[
17
]

to derive two rigorous error estimates within the framework of
general spatial
L^{q}
-norms.
Specifically, for arbitrary parameters
p\in(2,\infty)
and
q\in[2,\infty)
,
and for any chosen
\epsilon>0
, we establish the following error estimates:

\displaystyle\lVert{y-y_{h}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q})}\leqslant ch^{2},

\displaystyle\lVert{y-y_{h}}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];L^{q}))}\leqslant ch^{2-\epsilon}.

To achieve a second-order spatial convergence rate, prior numerical analyses in the literature
have typically enforced stringent regularity assumptions on the diffusion coefficients.
In contrast, this study imposes relatively moderate regularity conditions on the diffusion coefficient
F

(see Section
2
). While our numerical analysis assumes deterministic initial data,
extending the results to accommodate random initial data is straightforward.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Section

Section

and establishes the convergence of the finite element-based spatial semidiscretization for both non-smooth and smooth initial conditions.
A rigorous proof of these results is provided in Section
4

Finally, Section
5
concludes the paper by summarizing the key findings of this study.

E_{2}

.
The identity operator is indicated by

.
Given

[

]

The conjugate exponent of each

is denoted by
p^{\prime}
.
The symbol
\mathcal{O}
represents a bounded convex domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with a boundary

\partial\mathcal{O}
that belongs to the

class (see Section 6.2 of
[
11
]

For

to

,
and use
W_{0}^{1,q}(\mathcal{O})
and

to denote two standard Sobolev spaces (see, e.g.,
[
34
]
).
Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
represent a complete probability space
equipped with a right-continuous filtration
\mathbb{F}:=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
,
on which a sequence of independent
\mathbb{F}
-adapted Brownian motions

(\beta_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is defined. The expectation operator
associated with this probability space is denoted by
\mathbb{E}
.
Let
H
be a separable Hilbert space with inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{H}
and an orthonormal
basis
(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
The
H
-cylindrical Brownian motion
W_{H}
is defined such that, for each
t\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,

W_{H}(t)\in\mathcal{L}(H,L^{2}(\Omega))
is defined by

W_{H}(t)h:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\beta_{n}(t)(h,h_{n})_{H},\quad\forall h\in H.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
and a Banach space
X
, the space of all
\mathbb{F}
-adapted processes
in
L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);X)
is denoted by
L^{p}_{\mathbb{F}}(\Omega\times(0,T);X)
.
For any Banach space
X
, the notation
\gamma(H,X)
represents the space
of all
\gamma
-radonifying operators from
H
to
X
, as defined in
[
13
, Chapter 9]
.
For a comprehensive treatment of the theory of stochastic integration with respect to
the
H
-cylindrical Wiener process
W_{H}
, we refer the reader to
[
24
]
.

Definition of the Operator
F
.

Let
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of continuous functions mapping from

\overline{\mathcal{O}}\times\mathbb{R}
to
\mathbb{R}
, which satisfy
the following conditions for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

\displaystyle f_{n}(x,0)=0,

\displaystyle\forall x\in\partial\mathcal{O},

(2.1)

\displaystyle\lvert{f_{n}(x,y)}\rvert\leqslant C_{F}(1+\lvert{y}\rvert),

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathcal{O},\,\forall y\in\mathbb{R},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\lvert{f_{n}(x,y_{1})-f_{n}(x,y_{2})}\rvert\leqslant C_{F}\lvert{y_{1}-y_{2}}\rvert,

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathcal{O},\,\forall y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathbb{R},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\lvert{f_{n}(x_{1},y)-f_{n}(x_{2},y)}\rvert\leqslant C_{F}(1+\lvert{y}\rvert)\lvert{x_{1}-x_{2}}\rvert,

\displaystyle\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{O},\,\forall y\in\mathbb{R},

(2.4)

where
C_{F}
is a positive constant independent of
n
,
x
,
y
,

x_{1}
,
x_{2}
,
y_{1}
, and
y_{2}
. Hereafter, with a slight abuse
of notation, we will also employ
f_{n}
to denote the Nemytskii operator defined by

f_{n}(v):=f_{n}(\cdot,v(\cdot)),\quad\forall v\in L^{1}.

Let
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of non-negative numbers satisfying
the summability condition

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{n}<\infty.

(2.5)

For any
v\in L^{2}
, we define
F(v)\in\gamma(H,L^{2})
as

F(v):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\lambda}_{n}h_{n}\otimes f_{n}(v).

(2.6)

Accordingly, the model equation (
1.1
) can be explicitly formulated as follows:

\begin{cases}\mathrm{d}y(t)=(\Delta y+y-y^{3})(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}f_{n}(y(t))\,\mathrm{d}\beta_{n}(t),\quad 0\leqslant t\leqslant T,\\
y(0)=v.\end{cases}

Mild Solutions.

Let
p,q\in[2,\infty)
. We introduce the deterministic convolution operator
S_{0}
acting on functions
g\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q})
as follows:

(S_{0}g)(t):=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad\text{for }0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

(2.7)

Additionally, for any process
g\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);\gamma(H,L^{q}))
, we define the stochastic convolution operator
S_{1}
by

(S_{1}g)(t):=\int_{0}^{t}e^{(t-s)\Delta}g(s)\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(s),\quad\text{for }0\leqslant t\leqslant T.

(2.8)

Here,
e^{t\Delta}
, for
t\in[0,\infty)
, denotes the analytic semigroup generated by the Laplace operator
\Delta
subject to homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.
A process
y\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{6}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{6})
is called a mild solution to equation (
1.1
) if it satisfies the equality

y=G+S_{0}(y-y^{3})+S_{1}F(y),

(2.9)

where the function
G
is given by

G(t):=e^{t\Delta}v,\quad\text{for }t\in[0,T].

(2.10)

Note that the conditions in the above definition of a mild solution can be relaxed (see, e.g.,
[
29
, Chapter 6]
). However, the present formulation is adequate for the purposes of this study.

Spatial Semidiscretization.

Consider
\mathcal{K}_{h}
to be a conforming and quasi-uniform triangulation of the domain
\mathcal{O}
,
composed of three-dimensional simplexes. We introduce the finite element space
X_{h}
as

X_{h}:=\Bigl\{v_{h}\in C(\overline{\mathcal{O}})\bigm|v_{h}=0\text{ on }(\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathcal{O}_{h})\cup\partial\mathcal{O}_{h},\text{ and }v_{h}|_{K}\in P_{r}(K)\text{ for each }K\in\mathcal{K}_{h}\Bigr\},

where
C(\overline{\mathcal{O}})
denotes the space of continuous functions on the closure of
\mathcal{O}
,

\mathcal{O}_{h}
is the closure of the union of the simplexes in
\mathcal{K}_{h}
,
r\geqslant 1
is a positive integer,
and
P_{r}(K)
is the space of polynomials on simplex
K
up to degree
r
.
Let the spatial mesh size
h
be the maximum diameter of the elements in
\mathcal{K}_{h}
.
Define
P_{h}
as the
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection operator onto
X_{h}
.
The discrete Laplace operator
\Delta_{h}:X_{h}\to X_{h}
is defined by

\int_{\mathcal{O}}(\Delta_{h}u_{h})\cdot v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=-\int_{\mathcal{O}}\nabla u_{h}\cdot\nabla v_{h}\,\mathrm{d}\mu,\quad\text{for all }u_{h},v_{h}\in X_{h},

where
\mu
denotes the three-dimensional Lebesgue measure on
\mathcal{O}
.
We consider the following spatial semidiscretization:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}y_{h}(t)=\left(\Delta_{h}y_{h}+y_{h}-P_{h}(y_{h}^{3})\right)(t)\,\mathrm{d}t+P_{h}F(y_{h}(t))\,\mathrm{d}W_{H}(t),\quad 0\leqslant t\leqslant T,

(2.11a)

\displaystyle y_{h}(0)=P_{h}v,

(2.11b)

where
v
is the given initial value. Assume that
v\in L^{2}
.
Since this spatial semidiscretization is a finite-dimensional stochastic
differential equation (SDE), we can use the classical theory of finite-dimensional
SDEs (see, e.g.,
[
28
, Theorem 3.21]
)
to conclude that
Eq.

2.11
has a unique strong solution.

Main results

In the remainder of this paper, the symbol
c
denotes a generic positive constant that depends only
on
T
,
\mathcal{O}
,
\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{n}
, the constant
C_{F}

in
Eqs.

2.1
,

,

,
and the exponents associated with the spatial mesh size

.
The value of

For any

and

, let

denote the domain of

,
endowed with the standard norm defined as follows:

\|v\|_{\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}}:=\|(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}v\|_{L^{q}}\quad\text{for all $v\in\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}$}.

The dual space of
\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}
is denoted by
\dot{H}^{-\alpha,q^{\prime}}
. It is well-established that

has a unique extension as a bounded linear operator from
\dot{H}^{\alpha,q}
to
\dot{H}^{\alpha-2,q}
for all

. Specifically, when
\alpha=2
, the space
\dot{H}^{2,q}
is identical to the intersection
W_{0}^{1,q}(\mathcal{O})\cap W^{2,q}(\mathcal{O})
and is endowed with a norm equivalent to that of
W^{2,q}(\mathcal{O})
(see
[
11
, Theorem 9.15]
). Moreover, for
\alpha=1
, the space
\dot{H}^{1,q}
coincides with the standard Sobolev space
W_{0}^{1,q}(\mathcal{O})
, and the two norms are equivalent (see
[
38
, Theorem 16.14]
).

The main results of this paper are summarized in the following three theorems.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
v\in L^{\infty}
. Then equation
Eq.

1.1
admits a unique
mild solution
y
, which satisfies that

y\in L_{\mathbb{F}}^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q}),\qquad\forall p,q\in(2,\infty).

Furthermore, for any
p\in(2,\infty)
,
q\in[2,\infty)
, and
\epsilon>0
,
the solution

possesses the following regularity properties:

\displaystyle y\in L^{p}\big(\Omega;C([0,T];L^{q})\big),

(3.2)

\displaystyle y\in L^{p}\big(\Omega\times(0,T);\dot{H}^{2/p-\epsilon,q}\big),

(3.3)

\displaystyle y-G\in L^{p}\big(\Omega;C([0,T];\dot{H}^{1-\epsilon,q})\big),

(3.4)

\displaystyle y-G\in L^{p}\big(\Omega\times(0,T);\dot{H}^{1+2/p-\epsilon,q}\big),

(3.5)

where

is defined by
Eq.

2.10
.

.

Assume that
v\in L^{\infty}
. Let

Remark 3.1
.

Liu
[
18
]
proposed a theoretical framework for a broad spectrum of nonlinear stochastic partial differential equations. Applying this framework to our model yields

y\in L^{p}(\Omega;C([0,T];\dot{H}^{1,2})),\quad\forall p\in(2,\infty),

provided that
v\in\dot{H}^{1,2}
.
Furthermore, Liu and Qiao
[
21
]
have established certain regularity estimates in the context of the spatial
L^{2}
-norm, and some regularity estimates with the spatial
L^{\infty}
-norm were derived by using Sobolev’s embedding theorem. Regarding the stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with additive noise, Bréhier et al.
[
5
, Lemma 3.1]
provided some a priori estimates involving general spatial

Remark 3.2
.

For
Theorems

3.2
and
3.3
, we have the following comments.

1.

In the numerical analysis of the three-dimensional stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with
multiplicative noise, the existing literature often imposes strict regularity conditions
on the diffusion coefficients. For instance, the study in
[
23
]
demands that
the diffusion coefficients possess bounded second-order derivatives. Our choice of diffusion
coefficient

adheres to the criteria outlined in Assumption 2.2 of
[
21
]
;
however, it does not meet the more stringent requirements stated in Assumption 2.3 of
the same reference. Investigating the numerical approximations of
Eq.

under less restrictive conditions on

, especially the loosening of

), has important theoretical and practical implications.

2.

In Section

, we show that the norm of

in

is uniformly bounded with respect to the spatial
mesh size

for all

,
assuming

(see

also holds when

This finding is critical for the numerical analysis of the fully discrete

4.1

The combination of Proposition 1.4 from

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
0\leqslant\beta<\gamma\leqslant 1

(i)

The space
\dot{H}^{\gamma,q}

(L^{q},\dot{H}^{1,q})_{\beta,q}

(ii)

The space
(L^{q},\dot{H}^{1,q})_{\gamma,q}

\dot{H}^{\beta,q}

Theorem 2.12 and inequality (2.128) in

Lemma 4.2

Let

\lVert{e^{t\Delta}}\rVert_{\mathcal{L}(\dot{H}^{\alpha,q},\dot{H}^{\beta,q})}\leqslant ct^{(\alpha-\beta)/2}.

For the deterministic convolution operator

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
. Then for any

\displaystyle\leqslant c\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\lVert{\eta_{h}(s)}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{p-1}\lVert{(y-P_{h}y)(s)}\rVert_{L^{4}}\Big(\lVert{y(s)}\rVert_{L^{8}}^{2}+\lVert{P_{h}y(s)}\rVert_{L^{8}}^{2}\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant c\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\lVert{\eta_{h}(s)}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{p-1}\lVert{(y-P_{h}y)(s)}\rVert_{L^{4}}\lVert{y(s)}\rVert_{L^{8}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg]

\displaystyle\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\bigg[\int_{0}^{t}\lVert{\eta_{h}(s)}\rVert_{L^{2}}^{p}+\lVert{(y-P_{h}y)(s)}\rVert_{L^{4}}^{p}\lVert{y(s)}\rVert_{L^{8}}^{2p}\,\mathrm{d}s\bigg].

By utilizing
Eq.

4.31
, we arrive at the following bound:

I_{3}\leqslant ch^{2-\epsilon}+c\int_{0}^{t}\lVert{\eta_{h}(s)}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2})}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad\forall\epsilon>0.

(4.41)

To estimate
I_{4}
, we proceed as follows.
Using the definitions in (
4.34
) and (
4.35
), along with the relation
z_{h}-P_{h}z=\xi_{h}+\eta_{h}
, we deduce that
P_{h}y-y_{h}=-\xi_{h}-\eta_{h}+P_{h}G-G_{h}
. For any
s\in[0,t]
, we can then write:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{O}}\eta_{h}(s)\left[(P_{h}y)^{3}-y_{h}^{3}\right](s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{O}}\eta_{h}(s)(P_{h}y-y_{h})(s)\big[(P_{h}y)^{2}+(P_{h}y)y_{h}+y_{h}^{2}\big](s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle={}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{O}}\eta_{h}(s)(-\xi_{h}-\eta_{h}+P_{h}G-G_{h})(s)\big[(P_{h}y)^{2}+(P_{h}y)y_{h}+y_{h}^{2}\big](s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu

\displaystyle\leqslant{}

\displaystyle c\int_{\mathcal{O}}\left[\xi_{h}^{2}(s)+(P_{h}G-G_{h})^{2}(s)\right]\big[(P_{h}y)^{2}+y_{h}^{2}\big](s)\,\mathrm{d}\mu.

Hence, applying Hölder’s inequality, Young’s inequality,
and the stability property of
P_{h}
from (
4.18
),
we obtain the following bound for
I_{4}

\displaystyle I_{4}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}\bigg[\|\eta_{h}(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{p}+\left(\|\xi_{h}(s)\|^{p}_{L^{2\beta^{\prime}}}+\|(P_{h}G-G_{h})(s)\|^{p}_{L^{2\beta^{\prime}}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad{}\times\Big(\|y(s)\|^{p}_{L^{2\beta}}+\|y_{h}(s)\|^{p}_{L^{2\beta}}\Big)\bigg]\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad\forall\beta\in\big(1,\frac{3p}{3p-4}\big).

Using (
4.32
) and (
4.33
), we further simplifies this bound to:

\displaystyle I_{4}\leqslant c\int_{0}^{t}\|\eta_{h}(s)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2})}\,\mathrm{d}s+ch^{2-\frac{3}{2}p\left(1-\frac{1}{\beta}\right)},\quad\forall\beta\in\left(1,\frac{3p}{3p-4}\right).

By selecting

sufficiently small within
\big(1,\frac{3p}{3p-4}\big)
for any
\epsilon>0
, we arrive at the final bound for

I_{4}\leqslant ch^{2-\epsilon}+c\int_{0}^{t}\|\eta_{h}(s)\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2})}\,\mathrm{d}s.

(4.43)

which, in conjunction with

4.27

4.28
and
4.29

Consequently, we obtain the following bound for

(4.44)

Finally, by integrating the estimates for

,
I_{2}
,
I_{3}
,
I_{4}

4.40

\displaystyle ch^{\alpha}+c\int_{0}^{t}\|\eta_{h}(s)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2})}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}s,\quad\forall t\in(0,T].

\max_{t\in[0,T]}\|\eta_{h}(t)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2})}+\left[\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}\|\eta_{h}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{p-2}\|\eta_{h}(t)\|_{\dot{H}_{h}^{1,2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t\right]^{1/p}\leqslant ch^{\alpha/p}.

Moreover, for any
\epsilon>0

\displaystyle\lVert{(z-P_{h}z)(t)}\rVert_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{2})}\leqslant ch^{1-\epsilon}

(by

\displaystyle\lVert{(G-G_{h})(t)}\rVert_{L^{2}}\leqslant ch^{2/p}t^{-1/p}

\displaystyle\text{(by \lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eq:Deltah-Delta-2})}.

Since the equality

Conclusions

In this work, we have rigorously investigated the regularity properties of the three-dimensional stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with multiplicative noise, particularly in the presence of non-smooth initial data. For the spatial semidiscretization using the finite element method, we have established a convergence rate of
O(h^{\alpha/p}+h^{2/p}t^{-1/p})
with non-smooth initial data. Meanwhile, under the condition of smooth initial data, employing the discrete stochastic maximal
L^{p}
-regularity theory, we have achieved a convergence rate of
O(h^{2})
in the
L^{p}(\Omega\times(0,T);L^{q})
norm, along with a pathwise uniform convergence rate of
O(h^{2-\epsilon})
. Significantly, these findings cover a variety of spatial
L^{q}
-norms, enhancing their adaptability and universality. The theoretical results derived here lay a foundation for the numerical analysis of fully discrete approximations based on the finite element method for the three-dimensional stochastic Allen-Cahn equation with multiplicative noise, especially within the context of general spatial
L^{q}
-norms.

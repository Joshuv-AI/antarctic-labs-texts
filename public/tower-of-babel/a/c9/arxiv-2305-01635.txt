A characterization of pseudo complete finitely ramified valued fields through a Hahn-like construction
Anna De Mase

Abstract
We give a characterization of finitely ramified ω \omega -pseudo complete valued fields of mixed characteristic ( 0 , p ) (0,p) , with fixed residue field k k and value group G G of cardinality ℵ 1 \aleph_{1} in terms of a Hahn-like construction over the Cohen field C ⁡ ( k ) C(k) , modulo the Continuum Hypothesis. This is a generalization of results due to Ax and Kochen in ’65 for formally p p -adic fields and by Kochen in ’74 for unramified valued fields with perfect residue field. We consider a more general context of finitely ramified valued fields of mixed characteristic with arbitrary residue field and a cross-section.

Introduction

In their paper (
[
3
]
), Ax and Kochen give a complete axiomatization of the theory of the class of formally
p
-adic fields, by the following properties:

•

the value group is a
\mathbb{Z}
-group;

•

the residue field is
\mathbb{F}_{p}
;

•

v(p)
is the minimal positive element of the value group.

Thus, a valued field

is formally

-adic if and only if it is elementarily equivalent to
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
.
In
[
15
]
Macintyre gives a broad analysis of the field
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, both from an algebraic and a model theoretical point of view, focusing also on its comparison with the field of real numbers. Indeed, they share the property of being completions of the field of rational numbers,
\mathbb{R}
with respect to the usual archimedean absolute value, and
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
with respect to the non-archimedean one associated to the
p
-adic valuation. Hasse’s local-global principle (
[
10
]
) shows the role played by both fields in the existence of solutions of rational equations. We are mainly interested in a property not shared by both fields. It is a well-known property of real closed fields that a field elementarily equivalent to a finite extension of the field of real numbers
\mathbb{R}
contains a subfield that is elementarily equivalent to
\mathbb{R}
. This is not the case for formally
p
-adic fields. Indeed, in
[
4
]
the authors show that for any finite extension
L
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, there is a valued field elementarily equivalent to
L
that does not have any subfield elementarily equivalent to
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. This “pathology” of the
p
-adics can be avoided considering
\omega
-pseudo complete formally
p
-adic fields, which have a subfield that is an isomorphic copy of the field of
p
-adic numbers, as it follows as a particular case of Lemma
4.5
. There we show that all henselian valued fields with a fixed residue field
k
and discrete value group
G
have

, the Cohen ring over
k

[
7
]
, and for a recent model-theoretic analysis see
[
1
]
). In particular, we note that if
K
has finite ramification

K
contains a copy of a finite extension of
C(k)
, that is the totally ramified extension of
C(k)
of ramification
e
. This is obtained by adding to
C(k)
a root of an Eisenstein polynomial. We then use this property to generalize a result due to Ax and Kochen in
[
3
]
, which gives a characterization of
\omega
-pseudo complete valued fields elementarily equivalent to the field of
p
-adic numbers
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. In particular, in Theorems 1 and 2 of
[
3
]
, assuming the Continuum Hypothesis, the authors give a concrete description of
\omega
-pseudo complete formally
p
-adic fields with a fixed value group

of cardinality

, using a Hahn-like construction over

(see also
[
13

). We will refer to this structure as the field of “twisted” power series since it is characterized by having the multiplication defined by adding an extra factor that is a power of

such that

for all

The problem of characterizing valued fields in terms of power series has been addressed by many mathematicians, in both the equicharacteristic case (by Ax and Kochen in
[

, other than by Ax and Kochen in the aforementioned paper). In particular, in the mixed characteristic case, the motivations behind the “twisted” power series construction can be understood in the following argument. Suppose

is a mixed characteristic valuation from a field

to an ordered group

be the smallest convex subgroup of

with values in the quotient group

(1)

1
Exact sequences of ordered abelian groups

We start by recalling some results on exact sequences of groups. This is done in general for modules (see e.g.
[
22
]
), here we review it for abelian groups equipped with a total order.

Let
G
be an ordered abelian group and
A
a convex subgroup of
G
. Let
H=G/A
be the abelian group with the order induced by the order of
G
, and consider the exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{}A\xrightarrow{i}G\xrightarrow{\rho}H\xrightarrow{}0.

For each
h\in H
, choose
\alpha(h)\in G
any preimage of
h
with respect to the projection map. We call
\alpha:H\longrightarrow G
a
transversal map
. By definition of the quotient order on

, any choice of
\alpha
would be order-preserving. Then
\rho\alpha=id_{H}
, and
\alpha
does not need to be a group homomorphism. Then define the following function
\beta:H\times H\longrightarrow G
by

\beta(h,h^{\prime})=\alpha(h+h^{\prime})-\alpha(h)-\alpha(h^{\prime}),

(2)

for all
h,h^{\prime}\in H
.

Proposition 1.1
.

For every
h,h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime}\in H
,
\beta
satisfies the following property

\beta(h,h^{\prime})+\beta(h+h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime})=\beta(h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime})+\beta(h,h^{\prime}+h^{\prime\prime}).

(3)

Proof.

By the associative law of
G
, for every
h,h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime}\in H
,

(\alpha(h)+\alpha(h^{\prime}))+\alpha(h^{\prime\prime})=\alpha(h)+(\alpha(h^{\prime})+\alpha(h^{\prime\prime})).

From (
2
), the following holds

\alpha(h)+\alpha(h^{\prime})=\alpha(h+h^{\prime})-\beta(h,h^{\prime}),

and so we have

(\alpha(h+h^{\prime})-\beta(h,h^{\prime}))+\alpha(h^{\prime\prime})=\alpha(h)+(\alpha(h^{\prime}+h^{\prime\prime})-\beta(h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime})).

Repeating the substitution in

\alpha(h+h^{\prime})+\alpha(h^{\prime\prime})=\alpha(h+h^{\prime}+h^{\prime\prime})-\beta(h+h^{\prime},h^{\prime\prime})

and

\alpha(h)+\alpha(h^{\prime}+h^{\prime\prime})=\alpha(h+h^{\prime}+h^{\prime\prime})-\beta(h,h^{\prime}+h^{\prime\prime})

we have the assert. ∎

In the following remark, we prove that

takes values in

.

Remark 1.2
.

Let

0\xrightarrow{}A\xrightarrow{i}G\xrightarrow{\rho}H\xrightarrow{}0

be a short exact sequence of ordered abelian groups. If

, then
\beta(h,h^{\prime})\in A
.

Proof.

If

, then

\displaystyle\rho(\beta(h,h^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=\rho(\alpha(h)+\alpha(h^{\prime})-\alpha(h+h^{\prime}))=

\displaystyle=\rho(\alpha(h))+\rho(\alpha(h^{\prime}))-\rho(\alpha(h+h^{\prime}))=

\displaystyle=h+h^{\prime}-(h+h^{\prime})=0,

since
\rho\alpha=id_{H}
and
\rho
is an epimorphism. Thus,

\beta(h,h^{\prime})\in ker\,\rho=Im\,i\cong A.

∎

Fact 1.7
.

Let

\displaystyle\beta(h,h^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\alpha(h+h^{\prime})-\alpha(h)-\alpha(h^{\prime})

Proposition 2.1
.

If

is a 2-cocycle, the structure

(K(t^{H},\beta)_{\delta},+,\cdot)

Proof.

The axioms of abelian groups hold in

as in the field of formal power series defined on

a=\sum_{h\in S}a_{h}t^{h},\,b=\sum_{h\in S^{\prime}}b_{h}t^{h},\,c=\sum_{h\in S^{\prime\prime}}c_{h}t^{h}

5.1
Lifting automorphisms

One consequence of having an explicit description of a valued field in terms of power series, is the existence of an explicit lift of the automorphisms of the residue field and the value group to automorphisms of the valued field. For instance, in the equicharacteristic case, if
k
is a field and
G
an ordered abelian group, the Hahn-field

is naturally equipped with an automorphism
\phi(\sum_{g}a_{g}t^{g})=\sum_{g}\overline{\phi}(a_{g})^{\hat{\phi}(g)}
, where
\overline{\phi}\in Aut(k)
and
\hat{\phi}\in Aut(G)
. In the mixed characteristic case, we notice that the same situation occurs for the Hahn-like construction
K(t^{H},\beta)_{\aleph_{0}}
, with some necessary adjustments. Indeed, in this case, the valuation is the composition of the
t
-adic valuation and the valuation on the field
K
of the coefficients. Thus, a lifting of the automorphisms of the residue field and the value group is possible if they respect the cross-section of
K
, in the sense of (
5
) in Lemma
5.2
. Recall that if
G
is an ordered abelian group with smallest convex subgroup
A
and
H:=G/A
, then
G_{\beta}
defined as in Lemma
1.8
, is an ordered abelian group isomorphic to
G
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\hat{\phi}_{\beta}\in Aut(G_{\beta})

\hat{\phi}_{1}
of

and
\hat{\phi}_{2}

, such that

\hat{\phi}_{1}(-\beta(h,h^{\prime}))=-\beta(\hat{\phi}_{2}(h),\hat{\phi}_{2}(h^{\prime})).

Proof.

From the definition of

5.2
The equicharacteristic
p
case

In this section, we show that Theorem
4.14
, and in particular Corollary
4.15
, can be generalized to the case in which the fixed residue field of characteristic
p
is also equipped with a henselian valuation. Indeed, in the particular case in which this equicharacteristic
p
valued field is isomorphic to a Hahn field, we have a whole description of the starting mixed characteristic valued field in terms of power series. As said in the Introduction, the question of characterizing valued fields in terms of power series in the equicharacteristic
p
case has been addressed by many mathematicians. In
[
12
]
, Kaplansky proves that an equicharacteristic
p
henselian valued field K with no immediate extensions is uniquely determined by its value group and residue field if it satisfies what he calls the
Hypothesis A
, that is (
[
14
]
) the value group is
p
-divisible and the residue field is perfect with no finite separable extensions of degree divisible by
p
. This follows from the uniqueness of the maximal immediate extension of a valued field which satisfies that hypothesis. This result works in the non-discrete case. The discrete case was addressed by Schilling, who proved the following result.

Theorem 5.4
.

(
[
23
, Theorem 2]
) Let
(K,v)
be an equicharacteristic
p
valued field with value group
G
of finite rank
m
. If
(K,v)
is pseudo complete, then it is isomorphic to the field of power series in
m
variables and with coefficients in its residue field.

Before stating the next result as a consequence of Theorem
4.14
, we need to recall the setting in which we are going to work. For this purpose, we use the standard decomposition of the valued field through the coarsening method.

Let
(K,v)
be a pseudo complete mixed characteristic valued field with finite ramification, residue field
k_{v}
, and valued in a discretely ordered abelian group
G
. Generally, the residue field can bring its own non-trivial valuation, that is an equicharacteristic
p
valuation. In this case, the standard decomposition obtained by coarsening appears as follows:

K\xrightarrow{\dot{v}}\mathring{K}\xrightarrow{v_{0}}k_{v}\xrightarrow{v_{p,p}}k_{p},

where

•

(K,\dot{v},G/A,\mathring{K})
is an equicharacteristic
0
valued field;

•

(\mathring{K},v_{0},A,k_{v})
is a mixed characteristic valued field;

•

(k_{v},v_{p,p},A,k_{p})
is an equicharacteristic
p
valued field;

where
A
is the smallest convex subgroup of
G
.

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
(K,v)
be a pseudo complete mixed characteristic valued field of cardinality
\aleph_{1}
with finite ramification, residue field
k_{v}
, and valued in a group
G
of cardinality
\aleph_{1}
. Let
v_{p,p}
be a henselian valuation on
k_{v}
with respect to which it is complete, and let
k_{p}
be the correspondent residue field. Then,
(K,v)
is isomorphic to
L(t^{H},\beta)_{\aleph_{0}}
, where
L
is a finite extension of the Cohen field
C(k_{p}((t)))
over
k_{p}((t))
,
H=G/A
and
\beta:H\times H\longrightarrow A
a
2
-cocycle, modulo the Continuum Hypothesis.

Proof.

By the standard coarsening decomposition, the field
k_{v}
has value group
A
, that is canonically isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, by Schilling’s theorem (Theorem
5.4
),
(k_{v},v_{p,p})
is isomorphic to the field of power series
k_{p}((t))
on its residue field
k_{p}
. Then, the assert follows directly from Theorem
4.14
. ∎

Now, we state this result in a saturated contest.

Fact 5.6
.

An
\aleph_{1}
-saturated valued field is
\omega
-pseudo complete.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
(K,v)
be a mixed characteristic valued field with finite ramification, value group
G
, and residue field
k_{v}
. Let
v_{p,p}
be a henselian valuation on
k_{v}
, and let
k_{p}
be the correspondent residue field. If
(K,v)
is
\aleph_{1}
-saturated, then it is isomorphic to
L(t^{H},\beta)_{\aleph_{0}}
, where
L
is a finite extension of the Cohen field
C(k_{p}((t)))
over
k_{p}((t))
,
H=G/A
and
\beta:H\times H\longrightarrow A
a
2
-cocycle, modulo the Continuum Hypothesis.

Proof.

If
(K,v)
is
\aleph_{1}
-saturated, then also
k_{v}
is. The henselian valuation
v_{p,p}
on
k_{v}
has values in
A
that is an
\aleph_{1}
-saturated
\mathbb{Z}
-group. Then
A
is a regular not divisible group. By
[
11
, Theorem 4]
,
v_{p,p}
is definable without parameters in the language of rings. This implies that
(k_{v},v_{p,p})
is also
\aleph_{1}
-saturated as a valued field. Then by Fact
5.6
,
k_{v}
is pseudo complete, and in particular, it is Cauchy complete. By
[
23
, Theorem 2]
,
(k_{v},v_{p,p})
is isomorphic to
k_{p}((t))
as valued fields. By Theorem
4.14
and Corollary
5.5
, we have the assert. ∎

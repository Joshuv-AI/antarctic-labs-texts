The relative Brauer group of K ⁡ ( 1 ) K(1) -local spectra
Itamar Mor

Abstract
Using profinite Galois descent, we compute the Brauer group of the K ⁡ ( 1 ) K(1) -local category relative to Morava E-theory. At odd primes this group is generated by a cyclic algebra formed using any primitive ( p − 1 ) (p-1) st root of unity, but at the prime two is a group of order 32 32 with nontrivial extensions; we give explicit descriptions of the generators, and consider their images in the Brauer group of K ​ O KO . Along the way, we compute the relative Brauer group of completed K ​ O KO , using the étale locally trivial Brauer group of Antieau, Meier and Stojanoska.

Introduction

The classification of central simple algebras over a field is a classical question in number theory, and by the Wedderburn theorem any such algebra is a matrix algebra over some division algebra, determined up to isomorphism. If one identifies those algebras which arise as matrix algebras over the same division ring, then tensor product defines a group structure on the resulting set of equivalence classes. This relation is
Morita equivalence
, and the resulting group is the
Brauer group

\mathrm{Br}(K)
. One formulation of class field theory is the determination of
\mathrm{Br}(K)
in the case that
K
is a number field.

One consequence of Wedderburn’s theorem is that every central simple algebra is split by some extension
L/K
; in fact, one can take
L
to be Galois. This opens the door to cohomogical descriptions of the Brauer group: by Galois descent, one obtains the first isomorphism in

\mathrm{Br}(K)\cong H^{1}(K,\mathrm{PGL}_{\infty})\cong H^{2}(K,\mathbb{G}_{m}),

where
\mathrm{PGL}_{\infty}
denotes the Galois module
\varinjlim\mathrm{PGL}_{k}(K^{\mathrm{s}})
. The second isomorphism is
[
Ser79
, Proposition X.9]
, and follows from Hilbert 90. This presents
\mathrm{Br}(K)
as an étale cohomology group, and allows the use of cohomological techniques in its determination. Conversely, it gives a concrete interpretation of
2
-cocycles, analogous to the relation between
1
-cocycles and Picard elements.

Globalising this picture was a deep problem in algebraic geometric, initiated by the work of Azumaya, Auslander and Goldman, and the Grothendieck school. There are two ways to proceed: on one hand, one can generalise the notion of central simple algebra, obtaining that of an
Azumaya algebra
on
X
, and the group
\mathrm{Br}(X)
classifying such algebras (again, up to Morita equivalence). On the other, one can use the right-hand side of
Section

1
, which globalises in an evident way; this yields the more computable group
\mathrm{Br}^{\prime}(X)\coloneqq H^{2}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})_{\mathrm{tors}}
. A famous problem posed by Grothendieck asks if these groups agree in general. While there is always an injective map
\mathrm{Br}(X)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Br}^{\prime}(X)
, surjectivity is known to fail for arbitrary schemes (e.g.
[
Edi+01
, Corollary 3.11]
). The fundamental insight of Toën was that it is key to pass to
derived
Azumaya algebras, in which case one
can
represent any class in
H^{2}(X,\mathbb{G}_{m})
as a derived Azumaya algebra, including nontorsion classes
[
Toë12
]
. Toën’s work shows that even the Brauer groups of classical rings are most naturally studied in the context of derived or homotopical algebraic geometry. This initiated a study of Brauer groups through the techniques of higher algebra, and in recent years they have become objects of intense study in homotopy theory.

In this context, the basic definitions appear in
[
BRS12
]
: as in the classical case, the Brauer group of a ring spectrum
R
classifies Morita equivalence classes of Azumaya algebras, defined by entirely analogous axioms. This gives a useful class of noncommutative algebras over an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring: for example, Hopkins and Lurie
[
HL17
]
computed Brauer groups as part of a program to classify
E_{h}
-algebra structures on Morava K-theory, where
E_{h}
denotes Lubin-Tate theory. Categorifying, the Azumaya condition on an
R
-algebra
A
is equivalent to the requirement that the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Mod}_{A}
be
R
-linearly invertible, and so the Brauer group is also the Picard group of the
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathrm{Mod}_{R}\in\Pr^{L}
; in this guise the Brauer space and its higher-categorical analogues are the targets for (invertible) factorisation homology, as an extended quantum field theory
[
Lur09
,
Sch14
,
AF17
,
Hau17
,
FH21
]
. Computations of Brauer groups of ring spectra of particular interest appear in
[
AG14
,
GL21
,
AMS22
]
.

In this document we study Brauer groups in the monochromatic setting. Recall that Hopkins and Lurie focus on the
K(h)
-local Brauer group of
E_{h}
. Our objective is to extend this to the Brauer group of the entire
K(h)
-local category: explicitly, this group classifies of
K(h)
-local Azumaya algebras up to Morita equivalence. The computations of
op. cit.
show that the groups
\mathrm{Br}(E_{h})
are highly nontrivial, unlike the analogous Picard groups. We therefore complement this by computing the
relative
Brauer group, which classifies those
K(h)
-local Azumaya algebras which become trivial after basechange to
E_{h}
. In analogy with the established notation for Picard groups (e.g.
[
Goe+15
, p.5]
), this group will be denoted
\mathrm{Br}_{h}^{0}
. It fits in an exact sequence

1\to\mathrm{Br}_{h}^{0}\to\mathrm{Br}_{h}\to\mathrm{Br}(E_{h})^{\mathbb{G}_{h}},

where
\mathbb{G}_{h}
denotes the Morava stabiliser group, and this gives access (at least in theory) to the group
\mathrm{Br}_{h}\coloneqq\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{S}p_{K(h)})
. The group
\mathrm{Br}_{h}^{0}
also has a concrete interpretation in terms of chromatic homotopy theory: it classifies twists of the
\mathbb{G}_{h}
-action on the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Mod}_{E_{h}}(\mathcal{S}p_{K(h)})
. For the standard action (by basechange along the Goerss-Hopkins-Miller action on
E_{h}
), one has
\mathcal{S}p_{K(h)}\simeq\mathrm{Mod}_{E_{h}}(\mathcal{S}p_{K(h)})^{h\mathbb{G}_{h}}
(see
[
Mat16
, Proposition 10.10]
and
[
Mor23
, Theorem A.II]
for two formulations). Taking fixed points for a twisted action therefore gives a twisted version of the
K(h)
-local category.

Our main theorems give the computation of the relative Brauer group at height one:

Theorem A

(
5.13
)
.

At the prime two,

\mathrm{Br}_{1}^{0}\cong\mathbb{Z}/8\{Q_{1}\}\oplus\mathbb{Z}/4\{Q_{2}\}.

(i)

The
\mathbb{Z}/4
-factor is mapped injectively to
\mathrm{Br}(KO_{2})
, and
Q_{2}^{\otimes 2}\otimes KO_{2}
is the image of the generator under

\mathbb{Z}/2\cong\mathrm{Br}(KO\mid KU)\to\mathrm{Br}(KO_{2}\mid KU_{2}).

The
\mathbb{Z}/8
factor is the relative Brauer group
\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{S}p_{K(1)}\mid KO_{2})
.

(ii)

Q_{4}\coloneqq Q_{1}^{\otimes 4}
is the cyclic algebra formed using the
C_{2}
-Galois extension
\mathbb{S}_{K(1)}\to KU_{2}^{h(1+4\mathbb{Z}_{2})}
and the
strict
unit

1+\varepsilon\in\pi_{0}\mathrm{GL}_{1}(\mathbb{S}_{K(1)})=(\mathbb{Z}_{2}[\varepsilon]/(2\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{2}))^{\times}.

We have indexed generators on the filtration in which they are detected in the descent spectral sequence, which we recall later in the introduction. For the final part, note
[
BRS12
, §4]
that cyclic algebras are defined using
strict
units: that is, maps of spectra

u:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathfrak{gl}_{1}(E).

We give an alternative construction of cyclic algebras from strict units in
Section

4
, and using this we show that they are detected in the HFPSS by a
symbol
in the sense of
[
Ser79
, Chapter XIV]
. This allows us to deduce when they give rise to nontrivial Brauer classes.

Any strict unit has an underlying unit, and we abusively denote these by the same symbol. The unit
1+\varepsilon
was shown to be strict in
[
CY23
]
, which is what is what gives rise to the claimed representative for the class
Q_{4}
at the prime two. Likewise, at odd primes the roots of unity are strict, which leads to our second main computation:

Theorem B

(
5.1
)
.

At odd primes,

\mathrm{Br}_{1}^{0}\cong\mathbb{Z}/(p-1).

A generator is given by the cyclic algebra
(KU_{p}^{h(1+p\mathbb{Z}_{p})},\chi,\omega)
, where
\chi:\mu_{p-1}\cong\mathbb{Z}/(p-1)
is a character and
\omega\in(\pi_{0}\mathbb{S}_{K(1)})^{\times}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
is a primitive
(p-1)
st root of unity.

We now give an outline of the computation. Since Grothendieck, the main approach to computing Brauer groups has been
étale
or
Galois descent
, and this is the case for us too. Namely, recall that at any height
h
, Morava E-theory defines a
K(h)
-local Galois extension

\mathbb{S}_{K(h)}\to E_{h},

with profinite Galois group
\mathbb{G}_{h}
. In
[
Mor23
]
, we used condensed mathematics to prove a Galois descent statement of the form

\mathcal{S}p_{K(h)}\simeq\mathrm{Mod}_{E_{h}}(\mathcal{S}p_{K(h)})^{h\mathbb{G}_{h}},

and deduced from this a homotopy fixed point spectral sequence for Picard and Brauer groups, extending the Galois descent results of Mathew and Stojanoska
[
MS16
]
and Gepner and Lawson
[
GL21
]
. For our purposes, the main computational upshot of that paper is:

Theorem 1.1
.

There is a descent spectral sequence

E_{2}^{s,t}=H^{s}(\mathbb{G}_{h},\pi_{t}\mathfrak{pic}(E_{h}))\implies\pi_{t-s}\mathfrak{pic}(\mathcal{S}p_{K(h)}).

whose
(-1)
-stem gives an upper bound on
\mathrm{Br}_{h}^{0}
. In a large range, there is an explicit comparison of differentials with the
K(h)
-local
E_{h}
-Adams spectral sequence.

A more precise form of the theorem is recalled in
Section

2
. In the present paper, we determine this spectral sequence completely at height one, at least for
t-s\geq-1
. Unsurprisingly, the computation looks very different in the cases
p=2
and
p>2
, and the former represents the majority of our work. To prove a lower bound on
\mathrm{Br}_{1}^{0}
, we also prove a realisation result in the spirit of Toën’s theorem. Namely, we show in a very general context that all classes on the
E_{\infty}
-page may be represented by Azumaya algebras:

Theorem C

(
3.9
)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a nice symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category and
A\in\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C})
a faithful and dualisable Galois extension of the unit. Then the map sending an Azumaya algebra to its module
\infty
-category,

\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{C}\mid A)\to\mathrm{Br}^{\prime}(\mathcal{C}\mid A)\coloneqq\{\mathcal{D}:\mathrm{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{D})\simeq\mathrm{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{C})\}\subset\mathrm{Pic}(\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}({\Pr}^{L})),

is an isomorphism.

See also
[
AG14
, §6.3]
and
[
GL21
, §6.4]
for related results. We refer the reader to
Section

3
for the exact conditions in the theorem; most pertinently, the chromatic localisations of spectra give examples of such
\infty
-categories. This accounts immediately for most of the classes in
\mathrm{Br}_{1}^{0}
, by sparsity in the descent spectral sequence. At the prime two there is one final computation necessary, which is the relative Brauer group of
KO_{2}
. Recall that the group
\mathrm{Br}(KO\mid KU)
was computed by Gepner and Lawson
[
GL21
]
. In
Section

5.2
, we prove:

Theorem D

(
5.3
)
.

The relative Brauer group of
KO_{2}
is

\mathrm{Br}(KO_{2}\mid KU_{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}/4,

and the basechange map from
\mathrm{Br}(KO\mid KU)\cong\mathbb{Z}/2
is injective.

The generator, which we denote
P_{2}
, may be thought of as a cyclic algebra for the unit
1+4\zeta
, where
\zeta
is a topological generator of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
(see
Section

4
). We remark that this unit is
not
strict, and so the cyclic algebra cannot be constructed by hand; instead, we invoke
C
to prove that the possible obstructions vanish. We then show that
P_{2}
survives the descent spectral sequence for the extension
\mathbb{S}_{K(1)}\to KO_{2}
, and therefore gives rise to the class
Q_{2}\in\mathrm{Br}_{1}^{0}
.

As an aside, we observe that
D
implies the following result, which may be of independent interest:

Theorem E
.

There is no
C_{2}
-equivariant splitting

\mathfrak{gl}_{1}KU_{2}\simeq\tau_{\leq 3}\mathfrak{gl}_{1}KU_{2}\oplus\tau_{\geq 4}\mathfrak{gl}_{1}KU_{2}.

We do not know if such an equivariant splitting exists for
\mathfrak{gl}_{1}KU
. It seems conceivable that one would arise from a discrete model for K-theory, and this is an interesting problem.

Outline of the paper

In
Section

2
, we compute the
E_{2}
-page of the descent spectral sequences and most differentials, giving an upper bound on the relative Brauer groups. The objective of the rest of the document is to show this bound is achieved. In
Section

3
we prove that the property of “admitting a compact generator” satisfies
\mathcal{S}p_{K(h)}
-linear Galois descent, which allows us to lift certain
E_{\infty}
-classes to Azumaya algebras. In
Section

4
we give a construction of cyclic algebras which makes clear where they are detected in the descent spectral sequence; this allows us to assert that the cyclic algebras we form are distinct, and completes the odd-prime computation. We also give a construction of Brauer classes from
1

Section

5
we compute the relative Brauer group
\mathrm{Br}(KO_{2}\mid KU_{2})
, and use this to complete the computation of
\mathrm{Br}_{1}^{0}
at the prime two.

The descent spectral sequence

In this short section, we record the descent spectral sequence that will be the starting point for our computations. At any characteristic
(p,h)
, this arises as the descent spectral sequence for the sheaf

\mathfrak{pic}(\mathcal{E})\in\mathrm{Sh}(B{\mathbb{G}}_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},\mathcal{S}p_{\geq 0})

of
[
Mor23
, §3.2]
. The main input from
op. cit.
is the following theorem:

Theorem 2.1

(
[
Mor23
]
, Theorem A and Proposition 5.11)
.

(i)

There is a strongly convergent spectral sequence

E_{2}^{s,t}=H^{s}(\mathbb{G},\pi_{t}\mathfrak{pic}(\mathbf{E}))\implies\pi_{t-s}\mathfrak{pic}(\mathcal{S}p_{\mathbf{K}}).

(ii)

Its
(-1)
-stem converges to
\pi_{0}(B\mathfrak{Pic}(\mathbf{E}))^{h\mathbb{G}}
, which contains
\mathrm{Br}_{h}^{0}
as a subgroup.

(iii)

Differentials on the
E_{r}
page agree with those in the
\mathbf{K}
-local
\mathbf{E}
-Adams spectral sequence in the region
t\geq r+1
, and for classes
x\in E_{r}^{r,r}
we have

d_{r}(x)=d_{r}^{ASS}(x)+x^{2}.

In
[
Mor23
, §4]
, we used this spectral sequence to recover the computation of
\mathrm{Pic}_{1}\coloneqq\mathrm{Pic}(\mathcal{S}p_{K(1)})
(due to
[
HMS94
]
). In this case, Morava E-theory is the
p
-completed complex K-theory spectrum

, acted upon by
\mathbb{G}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
via Adams operations
\psi^{a}
.

.

We will write
\mathfrak{Br}^{\prime}(\mathcal{S}p_{\mathbf{K}}\mid\mathbf{E})\coloneqq(B\mathfrak{Pic}(\mathbf{E}))^{h\mathbb{G}}
for the global sections of the (sheafified) proétale sheaf
\mathfrak{Br}(\mathcal{E}\mid\mathbf{E})
, and
\mathrm{Br}^{\prime}(\mathcal{S}p_{\mathbf{K}}\mid\mathbf{E})\coloneqq\pi_{0}\mathfrak{Br}^{\prime}(\mathcal{S}p_{\mathbf{K}}\mid\mathbf{E})

Proposition 2.4
.

At odd primes,
\mathrm{Br}_{1}^{0}

Proof.

The only possible differentials are

3
Descent for compact generators

Given a Galois extension
\bm{1}\to A
in a stable homotopy theory
\mathcal{C}
, we showed in
[
Mor23
, §5]
that the Picard spectral sequence computes the subgroup

\mathrm{Br}^{\prime}(\bm{1}\mid A)\subset\mathrm{Br}^{\prime}(A)=\mathrm{Pic}(\mathrm{Cat}_{A})

of the cohomological Brauer group. To relate this to the Brauer-Azumaya group classifying Azumaya algebras in
\mathcal{C}
, we prove a descent result for compact generators valid in the
\mathbf{K}
-local setting. This is entirely analogous to the theory of
[
AG14
, §6.3]
and
[
GL21
, §6.4]
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a presentably symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category, and
\mathcal{D}\in\mathrm{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}}(\Pr^{L})
. An object
D\in\mathcal{D}
is
\mathcal{C}
-compact
if the functor

\underline{\smash{\mathrm{Map}}}_{\mathcal{D}}(D,-):\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}

preserves filtered colimits.
D
is an
\mathcal{C}
-compact generator
if it is
\mathcal{C}
-compact. We say that
D
is a
\mathcal{C}
-generator
if the functor
3.1
is conservative; when
\mathcal{C}
is stable, it is equivalent that
3.1
detects zero objects. A
\mathcal{C}
-compact generator
of
\mathcal{D}
is an object
D\in\mathcal{D}
that is both
\mathcal{C}
-compact and a
\mathcal{C}
-generator, and we shall write
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{ecg}}\subset\mathcal{D}
for the full subcategory of such
D

1
1

1

These might also be called
e
nriched
c
ompact
g
enerators.
.

Example 3.2
.

The
\mathbf{K}
-local sphere is an
\mathcal{S}p_{\mathbf{K}}
-compact generator of
\mathcal{S}p_{\mathbf{K}}
. More generally, we always have
\bm{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{ecg}}
since in this case
3.1
is the identity functor.

Our first objective is to show that Schwede-Shipley theory goes through in the presence of a
\mathcal{C}
-compact generator.

Definition 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable homotopy theory. We say
\mathcal{C}
is
rigidly generated
if it is generated under colimits by dualisable objects. That is, the localising category generated by
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{dbl}}
is
\mathcal{C}
itself.

Example 3.4
.

(i)

\mathcal{S}p
is generated under colimits by shifts of
\bm{1}
, and so rigidly generated.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{C}
is a rigidly generated stable homotopy theory and
L\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
a monoidal localisation, then
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is rigidly generated. Thus
\mathcal{S}p_{\mathbf{K}}
is rigidly generated.

(iii)

For a compact lie group
G
, the
\infty
-category
\mathcal{S}^{G}
of
G
-spaces is generated under colimits by orbits
G/H
(e.g.
[
MM02
, Theorem 1.8]
). Its stabilisation
\mathcal{S}p_{\mathscr{U}}^{G}
at any
G
-universe
\mathscr{U}
(as defined in
[
GM23
, Corollary C.7]
) is generated under colimits by shifts
\Sigma^{-V}\Sigma^{\infty}_{\mathscr{U}}G/H_{+}
as
V
ranges over representations in
\mathscr{U}
, and if
\mathscr{U}
is complete then these are invertible by virtue of the Wirthmüller isomorphism
[
GM95
, (4.16)]
. Thus the
\infty
-category
\mathcal{S}p^{G}
of genuine
G
-spectra is rigidly generated.

Proposition 3.5

(Enriched Schwede-Shipley)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a rigidly generated stable homotopy theory and
\mathcal{D}\in\mathrm{Cat}_{\mathcal{C}}
. Suppose that
D\in\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{ecg}}
, and write
A\coloneqq\underline{\mathrm{End}}_{\mathcal{D}}(D)\in\mathrm{Alg}(\mathcal{C})
. Then there is an
\mathcal{C}
-linear equivalence

\mathcal{D}\simeq\mathrm{LMod}_{A}(\mathcal{C}).

Proof.

The object
D\in\mathcal{D}
determines canonically a
\mathcal{C}
-linear left adjoint
F\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
, with right adjoint
G\coloneqq\underline{\smash{\mathrm{Map}}}_{\mathcal{D}}(D,-)
. According to
[
HA
, Proposition 4.8.5.8]
, it is enough to check the following:

(i)

G
preserves colimits of simplicial objects: in fact
G
preserves all colimits. Indeed,
G
preserves filtered colimits since
D
is
\mathcal{C}
-compact, and finite colimits as it is a right adjoint.

(ii)

G
is conservative: this is by definition of
\mathcal{C}
-compact generators.

(iii)

for every
D^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
and
C\in\mathcal{C}
, the map

C\otimes FG(D^{\prime})=C\otimes G(D^{\prime})\otimes D\to C\otimes D^{\prime}

is adjoint to an equivalence

C\otimes G(D^{\prime})\xrightarrow{\sim}G(C\otimes D^{\prime}).

But by
(i)
, the functor
G
preserves all colimits, so by rigid generation we reduce to
C
dualisable. In this case,
Item

3
is the composite equivalence

\displaystyle C\otimes\underline{\smash{\mathrm{Map}}}_{\mathcal{D}}(D,D^{\prime})

\displaystyle\simeq\underline{\smash{\mathrm{Map}}}_{\mathcal{C}}(C^{\vee},\underline{\smash{\mathrm{Map}}}_{\mathcal{D}}(D,D^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\simeq\underline{\smash{\mathrm{Map}}}_{\mathcal{D}}(C^{\vee}\otimes D,D^{\prime})

\displaystyle\simeq\underline{\smash{\mathrm{Map}}}_{\mathcal{D}}(D,C\otimes D^{\prime}).\qed

To use this to produce Azumaya algebras, we first need to be able to produce
\mathcal{C}
-compact generators. For this we will prove the following result:

Proposition 3.6

(Descent for
\mathcal{C}
-compact generators)
.

Let
\bm{1}\to A
be a descendable extension in a rigidly generated stable homotopy theory
\mathcal{C}
, and suppose that

is dualisable. Then
\mathcal{D}\in\mathrm{Cat}_{\mathcal{C}}
admits a

The proof relies on the following basic lemma:

Lemma 3.7
.

Suppose
\mathcal{C}\in\mathrm{CAlg}(\Pr^{L})
and

is dualisable, then

is faithful if and only if

Proof.

Assume that
A
is faithful, and that
A^{\vee}\otimes X=0
; the converse is given by taking duals. Then the identity on

factors as

A\otimes X\to A^{\vee}\otimes A\otimes A^{\vee}\otimes X\to A\otimes X,

and in particular
A\otimes X=0
. By faithfulness of
A
, this implies
X=0
.
∎

Proof (

As in

Remark 3.10
.

In a previous version, we claimed that Morava E-theory is dualisable in
\mathcal{S}p_{\mathbf{K}}
. As pointed out to us by Maxime Ramzi, while
\mathbf{E}
is Spanier-Whitehead self-dual
[
Str00
,
Bea+22
]
and hence
reflexive
, it is not dualisable: for example,
\mathbf{K}_{*}\mathbf{E}
would otherwise be finite by
[
HS99
, Theorem 8.6]
. We will bypass this issue at height one by showing that all generators of
\mathrm{Br}^{\prime}(\mathcal{S}p_{\mathbf{K}}\mid\mathbf{E})
are in fact trivialised in a
finite
Galois extension of the sphere, and hence lift to Azumaya algebras by
3.9
; we do not know if
\mathrm{Br}_{h}^{0}\cong\mathrm{Br}^{\prime}(\mathcal{S}p_{\mathbf{K}}\mid\mathbf{E})
at arbitrary height.

4
Explicit generators

In this section, we give some explicit constructions of Azumaya algebras from Galois extensions. Most of this section works in an arbitrary stable homotopy theory
\mathcal{C}
. We will use these constructions in
Section

5
to describe generators of the group
\mathrm{Br}_{1}^{0}
, and hence to solve extension problems.

4.1
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-extensions

We will begin with a straightforward construction for extensions with Galois group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, using the fact that
\operatorname{cd}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})=1
.

Remark 4.1
.

Let
G
be a profinite group, and
\mathcal{B}\in\mathrm{Sh}(B{G}_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},\mathcal{S}_{*})
. Under suitable assumptions on
\mathcal{B}
, décalage gives an isomorphism between the descent spectral sequence and the spectral sequence for the Čech nerve of
G\to*

[
Mor23
, Appendix A]
. Moreover, the homotopy groups of the latter can often be identified with the complex of continuous cochains with coefficients in
\pi_{t}B
, yielding an isomorphism on the
E_{2}
-pages

H^{*}(G,\pi_{t}B)\to H^{*}(B{G}_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},\pi_{t}\mathcal{B}).

For example, this is the case for the sheaf
\mathfrak{pic}(\mathcal{E})
for any Morava
E
-theory
E(k,\Gamma)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
G=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
or
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
, and write
\zeta\in G
for a topological generator. Suppose
\mathcal{C}
is a stable homotopy theory and
\mathcal{B}\in\mathrm{Sh}(B{G}_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},\mathcal{S}_{*})
, with
B\coloneqq\mathcal{B}(G/*)
and
B^{hG}\coloneqq\Gamma\mathcal{B}
. If the canonical map
Remark

4.1
is an isomorphism, then

B^{hG}\simeq\operatorname{Eq}\left(id,\zeta:B\rightrightarrows B\right).

Proof.

Write
B^{\prime}
for the equaliser in
4.2
. The
G
-map
(id,\zeta)\colon G\to G\times G
gives rise to maps

B\rightrightarrows\mathcal{B}(G\times G)\to B

factoring
id,\zeta\colon B\rightrightarrows B
, and the identification
B^{hG}\simeq\lim\mathcal{B}(G^{\bullet+1})
gives a distinguished nullhomotopy in
Section

4.1
after precomposing with the coaugmentation
\eta\colon B^{hG}\to B
. Thus
\eta
factors through
\theta\colon B^{hG}\to B^{\prime}
. Taking fibres, the descent spectral sequence for
\mathcal{B}
implies that
\pi_{*}\theta
fits in a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\pi_{t+1}B)_{G}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi_{t}B^{hG}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\pi_{t}B)^{G}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\pi_{t+1}B)_{G}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\pi_{t}B^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\pi_{t}B)^{G}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{t}\theta}

and is therefore an equivalence.
∎

Construction 4.3
.

Suppose
\bm{1}\to A
is a descendable Galois extension in a stable homotopy theory
\mathcal{C}
, with group
G=\mathbb{Z}_{p}
or
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then
\mathcal{B}\coloneqq B\mathfrak{Pic}(A)\in\mathrm{Sh}(B{G}_{\operatorname{pro\acute{e}t}},\mathcal{S}_{*})
, and in good cases
\mathcal{B}
satisfies the assumption that
Remark

4.1
be an isomorphism: for example, this is the case whenever each
\pi_{t}\mathfrak{pic}(A)
is the limit of a tower of finite sets by
[
BS14
, Lemma 4.3.9]
. Thus

\mathfrak{Br}^{\prime}(\bm{1}\mid A)\simeq\mathrm{Eq}\left(id,\zeta^{*}:B\mathfrak{Pic}(A)\rightrightarrows B\mathfrak{Pic}(A)\right),

and so a relative cohomological Brauer class is given by the data of an
A
-linear equivalence

\xi:\mathrm{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{C})\xrightarrow{\sim}\zeta^{*}\mathrm{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{C}).

In fact, if
X\in\mathrm{Pic}(A)
, then we may form the
A
-linear composite

\zeta_{!}^{X}:\mathrm{Mod}_{A}\xrightarrow{X\otimes_{A}-}\mathrm{Mod}_{A}\xrightarrow{\zeta_{!}}\zeta^{*}\mathrm{Mod}_{A}.

This gives an isomorphism

(A,-)\coloneqq\zeta_{!}^{(-)}:\mathrm{Pic}(A)_{G}\cong H^{1}(G,\mathrm{Pic}(A))\to\mathrm{Br}^{\prime}(\bm{1}\mid A).

Example 4.4
.

At the prime
2
, the extension
\bm{1}_{\mathbf{K}}\to KO_{2}
is a descendable
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-Galois extension. As a result,

applies, and we can form the Brauer class
(KO_{2},X)
associated to any

as above. For example, since

is

-periodic one can form the cohomological Brauer class

(KO_{2},\Sigma KO_{2})\in\mathrm{Br}^{\prime}(\bm{1}_{\mathbf{K}}\mid KO_{2}).

Example 4.5
.

Let

be the ind-étale

; since étale extensions are uniquely determined by their

preserves finite limits, and

is descendably

applies, and we can form the Brauer class

associated to any

as above. For example, since

-periodic one can form the cohomological Brauer class

(KO_{2}^{\mathrm{nr}},\Sigma KO_{2}^{\mathrm{nr}})\in\mathrm{Br}^{\prime}(KO_{2}\mid KO_{2}^{\mathrm{nr}}).

In fact,
(KO_{2}^{\mathrm{nr}},\Sigma KO_{2}^{\mathrm{nr}})
is an element of the

\mathrm{LBr}(KO_{2})
of

Section

.

4.2
Cyclic algebras

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a stable homotopy theory and
\bm{1}\to A
a finite Galois extension in
\mathcal{C}
with group
G
. Suppose also given the following data:

(i)

an isomorphism
\chi:G\cong\mathbb{Z}/k
.

(ii)

a
strict
unit
u\in\pi_{0}\mathbb{G}_{m}(\bm{1})
.

In this section, we will use this to define a relative Azumaya algebra
(A,\chi,u)\in\mathrm{Br}(\mathcal{C}\mid A)
.

Let us first recall the construction when
\mathcal{C}
is the category of modules over a classical ring. Then we begin with a
G
-Galois extension
R\to A
of rings, and define a
G
-action on the matrix algebra
M_{k}(A)
as follows: we let
\sigma\coloneqq\chi^{-1}(1)
act as conjugation by the matrix

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}\coloneqq\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}0&&&u\\
1&0&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&1&0\end{array}\right]

Since
\widetilde{u}^{k}=uI_{k}\in\mathrm{GL}_{k}A
is central, this gives a well-defined action on
M_{k}A
. We can use this to twist the usual action of
G
on the matrix algebra; passing to fixed points, we obtain the cyclic algebra

\displaystyle(A,\chi,u)\in\mathrm{Br}(R\mid A).

(17)

The construction for general
\mathcal{C}
will be directly analogous. Moreover, when
R
is a field it is well-known (see for example
[
CS21
]
) that under the isomorphism

\mathrm{Br}(R\mid A)\cong H^{2}(G,A^{\times}),

the cyclic algebra
(A,\chi,u)
maps to the cup-product
\beta(\chi)\cup u
, where
\beta
denotes the Bockstein homomorphism

\chi\in\mathrm{Hom}(G,\mathbb{Z}/k)=H^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}/k)\xrightarrow{\beta}H^{2}(G,\mathbb{Z}),

and we use the
\mathbb{Z}
-module structure on
A^{\times}
. As a result, we obtain an isomorphism

\widehat{H}^{0}(G,A^{\times})=A^{\times}/N_{e}^{G}A^{\times}\to\mathrm{Br}(R\mid A)

sending
u\mapsto(A,\chi,u)
. We will prove an analogous result for cyclic algebras in arbitrary stable homotopy theories, which will allow us to detect permanent cycles in the descent spectral sequence; conversely, this will allow us to assert that the cyclic algebras we construct are nontrivial.

We now begin the construction of
(A,\chi,u)
for arbitrary
\mathcal{C}
.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable homotopy theory. Given
A\in\mathrm{CAlg}(\mathcal{C})
and
k\geq 1
, we will write

•

M_{k}(A)\coloneqq\mathrm{End}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{C})}(A^{\oplus k})
,

•

\mathrm{GL}_{k}(A)\coloneqq\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{A}(\mathcal{C})}(A^{\oplus k})
,

•

\mathrm{PGL}_{k}(A)\coloneqq\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{Alg}_{A}(\mathcal{C})}(M_{k}(A))
.

These are all
\mathbb{E}_{1}
-monoids under composition, and we have
\mathbb{E}_{1}
maps

\mathrm{GL}_{k}(A)\to M_{k}(A)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathrm{GL}_{k}(A)\to\mathrm{PGL}_{k}(A),

the first of which is by definition. The second will be defined immediately below.

Remark 4.7
.

If
R
is an
\mathbb{E}_{\infty}
-ring, then Gepner and Lawson
[
GL21
, Corollary 5.19]
show there is a fibre sequence

\displaystyle\coprod_{\pi_{0}\mathrm{Mod}_{R}^{\mathrm{cg}}}B\mathrm{Aut}_{A}(M)\to\coprod_{\pi_{0}\mathfrak{Az}_{R}^{\mathrm{triv}}}B\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{Alg}_{R}}(A)\to B\mathfrak{Pic}(R).

(18)

Here
\mathrm{Mod}_{R}^{\mathrm{cg}}\subset\iota\mathrm{Mod}_{R}
denotes the classifying space of

, and

the space of

Azumaya algebras. To see this, we consider the components over
\mathrm{Mod}_{R}\in\mathrm{Br}(R)
in the commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{{}_{A}\mathrm{BMod}_{A}^{\mathrm{cg}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\{\mathrm{LMod}_{A}\}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{Az}_{R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{Br}_{R}}}

noting that this diagram is Cartesian
[
GL21
, Proposition 5.17]
: this follows by identifying those objects in

which correspond to the

-linear equivalences. By
[
HL17
, Corollary 2.1.3]
,
\mathcal{C}

Theorem 4.14

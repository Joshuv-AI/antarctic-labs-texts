Abstract




Two-loop splitting in double parton distributions:

the colour non-singlet case

M. Diehl
{}^{\mskip 1.5mu1}
, J. R. Gaunt
{}^{\mskip 1.5mu2}
and P. Plößl
{}^{\mskip 1.5mu1}

1
Deutsches Elektronen-Synchrotron DESY, Notkestr. 85, 22607 Hamburg, Germany

2
Department of Physics and Astronomy, University of Manchester, Manchester, M13 9PL,

2
United Kingdom

At small inter-parton distances, double parton distributions receive their dominant contribution from the splitting of a single parton. We compute this mechanism at next-to-leading order in perturbation theory for all colour configurations of the observed parton pair. Rapidity divergences are handled either by using spacelike Wilson lines or by applying the
\delta
regulator. We investigate the behaviour of the two-loop contributions in different kinematic limits, and we illustrate their impact in different channels.

Introduction

To interpret measurements at the Large Hadron Collider in its high-luminosity phase, it is of great importance to understand the strong-interaction part of proton-proton collisions as much as possible. This provides a strong motivation for the study of double parton scattering (DPS), which is a mechanism in which two pairs of partons initiate two separate hard-scattering processes in a single collision. Due to an increased parton flux for decreasing parton momentum fractions, the mechanism becomes more important for higher collision energy. It is therefore relevant also to the planning of future hadron colliders at the energy frontier.

The two observed partons are then produced from a single parton in a perturbative splitting process, so that the DPD is given by the convolution of a splitting kernel with a PDF. The perturbative splitting mechanism in DPS is intimately connected with a double counting problem between DPS and SPS in physical cross sections

The quantum numbers of two partons in a hadron can be correlated in various ways
[
6
,
13

14

It was realised early on that colour correlations between the partons are suppressed in DPS cross sections by Sudakov factors
[
63

[

[

To leading order (LO) in
\alpha_{s}
, the splitting kernels for DPDs have been computed in
[
13

This is typically true for hard-scattering cross sections in hadron-hadron collisions, which motivates an analysis of DPS at next-to-leading order (NLO). Since such an analysis requires all perturbative ingredients to be available at NLO, we computed the splitting kernels for DPDs at this order in
[
64
]

x

Such rapidity divergences are familiar from factorisation for processes with small measured transverse momenta, and it is interesting that they appear in DPS even if transverse momenta are integrated over. 2
General theory

We begin this section by recalling the basic properties of DPDs with a non-trivial dependence on the parton colour in the formalism set up in
[
13
,
78
]
. We then describe the interplay between ultraviolet renormalisation and the treatment of rapidity divergences, and we show how these operations are to be carried out at the level of hard splitting kernels for DPDs at small inter-parton distance
y
. The main result of this section is a set of formulae that specify how to compute the two-loop splitting kernels for renormalised DPDs from bare kernels and the soft factor for double parton scattering.

2.1
Overview

We are interested in collinear, i.e. transverse-momentum integrated DPDs, which we denote by
{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{a_{1}a_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},y,\mu,\zeta_{p})
. These distributions depend on a number of variables.

•

x_{1}
and
x_{2}
are the longitudinal momentum fractions of partons
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
in a fast moving proton. In general, the labels
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
also specify the polarisation of the two partons, but throughout this work we consider the case where both partons are unpolarised. With proper adjustments, the results of the present section readily generalise to the polarised case.

•

y
is the spatial distance between the two partons in the plane transverse to the proton momentum. We write
\boldsymbol{y}
for the distance vector and
y
for its length
|\boldsymbol{y}|
.

•

\mu
is the renormalisation scale. As shown in
[
78
]
, it is possible to define separate scales
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
associated with the two partons. Throughout the present work, we take these scales to be equal. This is natural when computing the contribution to DPDs in which the two partons originate from a single hard splitting process. From this, one can obtain distributions with different
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
by using the appropriate evolution equations.

•

\zeta_{p}
is a parameter associated with the regularisation of rapidity divergences. It is boost invariant and has dimension mass squared. Its precise definition depends on the rapidity regulator used and will be given below. A corresponding dependence is familiar from transverse-momentum dependent parton distributions (TMDs). It is remarkable that we encounter the same type of dependence for collinear distributions.

We now discuss the colour dependence. The operator matrix elements associated with DPDs have four open colour indices, two for the partons
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
in the process amplitude and two for the same partons in the conjugate process amplitude. Examples are shown in figure
1
. We organise the colour dependence by coupling the two indices for
a_{1}
to an irreducible representation
R_{1}
and those for
a_{2}
to an irreducible representation
R_{2}
such that one has an overall colour singlet. The relevant representations depend on the parton type. In all cases, both partons can be in the colour singlet (
R_{1}=R_{2}=1
). In addition, we have the following possibilities.

•

For
F_{qq}
, the two quark pairs can each be coupled to an octet (
R_{1}=R_{2}=8
).

•

For
F_{gq}
, the two gluons can be coupled to an asymmetric or a symmetric octet (
R_{1}=A
or
R_{1}=S
). The two quarks are coupled to an octet (
R_{2}=8
) in both cases. Analogous combinations exist for
F_{qg}
.

•

For
F_{gg}
, each of the two gluons can be coupled to an antisymmetric or a symmetric octet (in all four combinations), or to the 27 representation (
R_{1}=R_{2}=27
). Furthermore, one gluon can be coupled to a decuplet and the other to an anti-decuplet (
R_{1}=10
and
R_{2}=\overline{10}
or vice versa).

We have the same representations when quarks are replaced by antiquarks. Of course, the octet representations of SU(3) are readily generalised to the adjoint representations of SU(
N
). The coupling of two adjoint indices in SU(
N
) with
N\neq 3
involves the generalisations of
R=10
,
\overline{10}
,
27
, and an additional irreducible representation
[
82
,
83
]
. Throughout this work, we give results for singlet and octet representations for general number of colours
N
. Results for the decuplet and antidecuplet are given for general
N
or for
N=3
, whereas for
R=27
we always set
N=3
.

We call the colour space basis for DPDs just described the “
t
channel basis”. An alternative, which we call “
s
channel basis”, is to couple the indices of
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
to irreducible representations in the amplitude and in the conjugate amplitude
[
6
]
. The conversion between the two colour bases can be found in
[
84
]
. We note that the combinations of
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
considered in
[
6
,
13
,
84
]
and in the original version of
[
78
]
are incomplete; this mistake is corrected in the erratum of
[
78
]
.

We now recall some basic properties of the DPDs, which were derived in
[
78
]
. Their
\zeta_{p}
dependence is described by a Collins-Soper equation

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\zeta_{p}}\,\log{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}F_{a_{1}a_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},y,\mu,\zeta_{p})

\displaystyle={}^{R_{1}}\!J(y,\mu)\big/2\,,

(2.1)

which is analogous to the one derived by Collins and Soper for TMDs
[
79
]
. We write
J
instead of
K
for the kernel relevant to collinear DPDs in order to avoid confusion with the TMD case. The kernel
{}^{R}\!J
depends only on the multiplicity of the representation
R
, so that
{}^{R_{1}}\!J={}^{R_{2}}\!J
for all possible colour combinations in a DPD. Moreover, it is independent of the type of partons
a_{1}
and
a_{2}
.
In the singlet channel, one has the exact relation

\displaystyle{}^{1}\mskip-1.5muJ(y,\mu)

\displaystyle=0\,,

(2.2)

so that colour singlet DPDs have no rapidity dependence. Quite remarkably, the octet kernel
{}^{8}\mskip-1.5muJ(y,\mu)
is equal to the Collins-Soper kernel for the rapidity evolution of gluon TMDs
[
81
]
.
The renormalisation scale dependence of
J
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\mu^{2}}\,{}^{R}\!J(y,\mu)

\displaystyle={}-{}^{R}\gamma_{J}(\mu)

(2.3)

with an anomalous dimension that has a perturbative expansion

\displaystyle{}^{R}\gamma_{J}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{s}^{n+1}(\mu)\,{}^{R}\gamma_{J}^{(n)}\,.

(2.4)

Here and in the following we write

\displaystyle a_{s}(\mu)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{s}(\mu)}{2\pi}\,.

(2.5)

The one-loop coefficients in (
2.4
) read

\displaystyle{}^{8}\gamma_{J}^{(0)}={}^{A}\gamma_{J}^{(0)}={}^{S}\gamma_{J}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=2C_{A}\,,

\displaystyle{}^{10}\gamma_{J}^{(0)}={}^{\scalebox{0.7}{$\overline{10}$}}\gamma_{J}^{(0)}

\displaystyle\underset{N=3}{=}12\,,

\displaystyle{}^{27}\gamma_{J}^{(0)}

\displaystyle\underset{N=3}{=}16

(2.6)

with
C_{A}=N
.

The scale dependence of the DPDs is given by the double DGLAP equations
[
78
]

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\log\mu^{2}}\,{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}F_{a_{1}a_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},y,\mu,\zeta_{p})

\displaystyle=\sum_{b_{1},R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}_{1}}{}^{R_{1}\overline{R}{}^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}_{1}}P_{a_{1}b_{1}}(\mu,x_{1}^{2}\mskip 1.5mu\zeta_{p})\underset{1}{\otimes}{}^{R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}_{1}R_{2}}F_{b_{1}a_{2}}(y,\mu,\zeta_{p})

\displaystyle+\sum_{b_{2},R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}_{2}}{}^{R_{2}\overline{R}{}^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}_{2}}P_{a_{2}b_{2}}(\mu,x_{2}^{2}\mskip 1.5mu\zeta_{p})\underset{2}{\otimes}{}^{R_{1}R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}_{2}}F_{a_{1}b_{2}}(y,\mu,\zeta_{p})\,,

(2.7)

where
\overline{R}
denotes the conjugate of the representation
R
. Note that conjugation is only relevant for the decuplet and anti-decuplet, whilst
\overline{R}=R
for the singlet, all octets, and
R=27
. The convolutions are defined as

\displaystyle\Bigl[P\underset{1}{\otimes}F\mskip 1.5mu\Bigr](x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{x_{1}}^{1}\frac{dz}{z}\;P(z)\,F\biggl(\frac{x_{1}}{z},x_{2}\biggr)\,,

\displaystyle\Bigl[P\underset{2}{\otimes}F\mskip 1.5mu\Bigr](x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{x_{2}}^{1}\frac{dz}{z}\;P(z)\,F\biggl(x_{1},\frac{x_{2}}{z}\biggr)\,.

(2.8)

The effective integration range in these convolutions is smaller than indicated, because DPDs
F(x_{1},x_{2})
vanish for
x_{1}+x_{1}>1
as a consequence of momentum conservation. The same support property holds for DPD splitting kernels and renormalisation factors.

Note that the factors
x_{1}^{2}
and
x_{2}^{2}
that multiply
\zeta_{p}
in the arguments of the evolution kernels in (
2.1
) are fixed by the arguments of the DPD on the l.h.s., i.e., they are not part of the convolution integrals on the r.h.s. The reason for this rescaling of
\zeta_{p}
is explained in section
2.5
.

The rapidity dependence of the DGLAP kernels is given by

\displaystyle{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}}\!P_{ab}(z,\mu,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\frac{{}^{R}\gamma_{J}(\mu)}{4}\,\delta_{R\overline{R}{}^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}}\,\delta_{ab}\,\delta(1-z)\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{\zeta}+{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}}\!\hat{P}_{ab}(z,\mu)\,,

(2.9)

where we abbreviate
{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}}\!\hat{P}(z,\mu)={}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}}\!P(z,\mu,\mu^{2})
. The evolution kernels have a perturbative expansion

\displaystyle{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}}\!P_{ab}(z,\mu,\zeta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{s}^{n+1}(\mu)\;{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}}\!P_{ab}^{(n)}(z,\mu^{2}/\zeta)\,,

(2.10)

and the colour singlet kernels
{}^{11}\mskip-1.5muP(z,\mu)
are the same as the DGLAP kernels for ordinary PDFs.

Notice that Collins-Soper and DGLAP evolution of collinear DPDs is diagonal in the
t
channel colour basis, as seen in (
2.1
) and (
2.1
). This does not hold in the
s
channel basis, where different representations mix under evolution.

Perturbative splitting.

In the limit of small
y
, the DPDs are given by the perturbative splitting form

\displaystyle{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}\mskip-1.5muF_{a_{1}a_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2},y;\mu,\zeta_{p})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\frac{1}{\pi\mskip 1.5muy^{2}}\,\sum_{a_{0}}\,\int\limits_{x_{1}+x_{2}}^{1}\!\!\frac{dz}{z^{2}}\;{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}V_{a_{1}a_{2},a_{0}}\biggl(\frac{x_{1}}{z},\frac{x_{2}}{z},a_{s}(\mu),L,\log\frac{\mu^{2}}{x_{1}x_{2}\mskip 1.5mu\zeta_{p}}\biggr)\,f_{a_{0}}(z,\mu)\,,

(2.11)

where we abbreviate

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=\log\frac{y^{2}\mu^{2}}{b_{0}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle b_{0}

\displaystyle=2e^{-\gamma}\,,

(2.12)

with
\gamma
being the Euler-Mascheroni constant. Corrections to (
2.1
) are of order
y^{2}\Lambda^{2}
, where
\Lambda
is a hadronic scale. They arise from twist-four contributions in the operator product expansion, as explained in section 3.3 of
[
19
]
.

We define a convolution product between a two-variable and a one-variable function as

\displaystyle\Bigl[V\underset{12}{\otimes}f\mskip 1.5mu\Bigr](x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{x}^{1}\frac{dz}{z^{2}}\;V\biggl(\frac{x_{1}}{z},\frac{x_{2}}{z}\biggr)\,f(z)=\frac{1}{x}\int\limits_{x}^{1}dz\;V(uz,\bar{u}z)\,f\biggl(\frac{x}{z}\biggr)

(2.13)

with abbreviations

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=x_{1}+x_{2}\,,

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{1}}{x_{1}+x_{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\bar{u}

\displaystyle=1-u\,.

(2.14)

When denoting the kernel in the rightmost expression of (
2.13
) by
V(z_{1},z_{2})
, we also have the relations

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=z_{1}+z_{2}\,,

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\frac{z_{1}}{z_{1}+z_{2}}\,,

(2.15)

so that
u
can be expressed either in terms of the momentum fractions of the full convolution integral or the momentum fractions of its integrand.
Note that the factors
x_{1}x_{2}
multiplying
\zeta_{p}
on the r.h.s. of (
2.1
) are fixed by the arguments of the DPD on the l.h.s., in analogy to our earlier discussion for evolution kernels.

Convolutions w.r.t. different variables are associative, i.e. we have

\displaystyle[A\underset{1}{\otimes}D]\underset{12}{\otimes}B

\displaystyle=A\underset{1}{\otimes}[D\underset{12}{\otimes}B]\,,

\displaystyle[A\underset{1}{\otimes}B\underset{2}{\otimes}D]\underset{12}{\otimes}C

\displaystyle=A\underset{1}{\otimes}B\underset{2}{\otimes}[D\underset{12}{\otimes}C]\,,

(2.16)

where
A
,
B
and
C
depend on one momentum fraction, whilst
D
depends on two momentum fractions and a rapidity parameter. We can hence write these convolutions without brackets. Here we have defined the combined convolution

\displaystyle A\underset{1}{\otimes}B\underset{2}{\otimes}D

\displaystyle=A\underset{1}{\otimes}[B\underset{2}{\otimes}D]\,.

(2.17)

We also have

\displaystyle[D\underset{12}{\otimes}A]\underset{12}{\otimes}B

\displaystyle=D\underset{12}{\otimes}[A\otimes B]\,,

(2.18)

where
A\otimes B
is the usual Mellin convolution for functions of a single momentum fraction. We note in passing that for rescaled functions, the convolution integrals take the same form as for the usual Mellin convolution,

\displaystyle x_{1}\mskip 1.5mux_{2}\,\Bigl[V\underset{12}{\otimes}f\Bigr](x_{1},x_{2})

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{x}^{1}\frac{dz}{z}\;\widetilde{V}\biggl(\frac{x_{1}}{z},\frac{x_{2}}{z}\biggr)\,\tilde{f}(z)=\int\limits_{x}^{1}\frac{dz}{z}\;\widetilde{V}(uz,\bar{u}z)\,\tilde{f}\biggl(\frac{x}{z}\biggr)\,,

(2.19)

where
\widetilde{V}(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{1}\mskip 1.5muz_{2}\,V(z_{1},z_{2})
and
\tilde{f}(z)=zf(z)
.

We extend our notation for convolutions to include appropriate summation over indices denoting the parton type (quarks, antiquarks, gluons). For functions
A_{ab}(x),B_{ab}(x)
and
f_{a}(x)
of a single momentum fraction, we write as usual

\displaystyle[A\otimes B]_{ac}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b}A_{ab}\otimes B_{bc}\,,

\displaystyle[A\otimes f]_{a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b}A_{ab}\otimes f_{b}\,.

(2.20)

We do
not
use the summation convention for parton indices, i.e. sums are indicated explicitly when indices are given. For functions
V_{a_{1}a_{2},a_{0}}(x_{1},x_{2})
and
F_{a_{1}a_{2}}(x_{1},x_{2})
of two momentum fractions, we define

\displaystyle[A\underset{1}{\otimes}V]_{a_{1}a_{2},a_{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b}A_{a_{1}b}\underset{1}{\otimes}V_{b\mskip 1.5mua_{2},a_{0}}\,,

\displaystyle[A\underset{1}{\otimes}F]_{a_{1}a_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b}A_{a_{1}b}\underset{1}{\otimes}F_{b\mskip 1.5mua_{2}}\,,

\displaystyle[A\underset{2}{\otimes}V]_{a_{1}a_{2},a_{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b}A_{a_{2}b}\underset{2}{\otimes}V_{a_{1}b,a_{0}}\,,

\displaystyle[A\underset{2}{\otimes}F]_{a_{1}a_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b}A_{a_{2}b}\underset{2}{\otimes}F_{a_{1}b}\,,

\displaystyle[V\underset{12}{\otimes}A]_{a_{1}a_{2},a_{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b}V_{a_{1}a_{2},b}\underset{12}{\otimes}A_{b\mskip 1.5mua_{0}}\,,

\displaystyle[V\underset{12}{\otimes}f]_{a_{1}a_{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b}V_{a_{1}a_{2},b}\underset{12}{\otimes}f_{b}\,.

(2.21)

For the combination of convolutions in the first and second momentum fraction, we then have

\displaystyle[A\underset{1}{\otimes}B\underset{2}{\otimes}V]_{a_{1}a_{2},a_{0}}

2.2
Bare distributions and soft factors

As specified in
[
78

[
68

This is important for minimising the amount of non-perturbative functions, given that the cross section includes the region of large

With the

\displaystyle\ell_{R}+\ell_{L}

\displaystyle=\log\frac{b_{0}^{2}}{2\delta^{+}\delta^{-}\mskip 1.5muy^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle=\log\frac{\bar{p}^{-}}{\delta^{-}}+\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{b_{0}^{2}}{y^{2}\mskip 1.5mu\zeta_{\bar{p}}}\,,

\displaystyle=\log\frac{p^{+}}{\delta^{+}}+\frac{1}{2}\log\frac{b_{0}^{2}}{y^{2}\mskip 1.5mu\zeta_{p}}\,,

where we abbreviate

for the remainder of this section.

\displaystyle\quad+L\,\biggl(\hat{P}^{(0)}\underset{1}{\otimes}V^{(1)}+\hat{P}^{(0)}\underset{2}{\otimes}V^{(1)}-V^{(1)}\underset{12}{\otimes}P^{(0)}+\frac{\beta_{0}}{2}\,V^{(1)}\biggr)

Scheme dependence.

We have seen that
V^{(2)}_{\text{fin}}
is independent of the
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme implementation, both for the
\delta
and for the Collins regulator. The only scheme dependence of the two-loop kernels therefore comes from the term with
c_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$}}
in (
2.6
). This leads to a
\overline{\text{MS}}
scheme dependence of the DPD at small
y
,

\displaystyle{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}F^{(2)}_{a_{1}a_{2},a_{0}}

\displaystyle=c_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$}}\,\frac{a_{s}\mskip 1.5mu{}^{R_{1}}\gamma_{J}^{(0)}}{2}\,{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}F^{(1)}_{a_{1}a_{2},a_{0}}+\{\text{scheme independent}\}\,,

(2.101)

where

\displaystyle{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}F^{(n)}_{a_{1}a_{2},a_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi y^{2}}\;a_{s}^{n}\;\sum_{b}{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}V^{(n)}_{a_{1}a_{2},b}\mskip 1.5mu\underset{12}{\otimes}\mskip 1.5muf_{b}

(2.102)

denotes the term of order
a_{s}^{n}
in the expansion (
2.6
).
Of course, this dependence must cancel in a physical quantity. To see how this happens, let us consider the double Drell-Yan process. As is well-known, the unsubtracted hard scattering cross section
{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}}\mskip-1.5mu\hat{\sigma}_{\text{us}}
for
R=R^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}=1
has a single
1/\epsilon
pole at
\mathcal{O}(\alpha_{s})
, due to collinear divergences related to the incoming quark and antiquark. Double poles
1/\epsilon^{2}
cancel between real and virtual corrections. This cancellation does not take place if
R\neq 1
, because the colour factors of real and virtual graphs differ in this case
[
63
]
. At order
a_{s}
, the product
S_{\epsilon}^{-1}\mskip 1.5mu\epsilon^{-2}
leads to a scheme dependent term proportional to
c_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$}}
in
{}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}}\mskip-1.5mu\hat{\sigma}_{\text{us}}
. We can determine its coefficient by using that the subtraction of collinear and soft-collinear poles is tantamount to the convolution with an inverse renormalisation factor
({}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}}\!Z)^{-1}
for each of the incoming partons. This ensures the
\mu
independence of the physical cross section. According to (
2.68
) we have

\displaystyle\bigl({}^{RR^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}}\!Z\mskip 1.5mu\bigr)^{-1}_{ab}

\displaystyle=\delta_{R\overline{R}{}^{\mskip 1.5mu\prime}}\,\delta_{ab}\,\delta(1-x)\,\Bigl[1+a_{s}\mskip 1.5mu{}^{R}\gamma_{J}^{(0)}\bigl/(4\epsilon^{2})+\ldots\,\Bigr]

(2.103)

for the double pole at
\mathcal{O}(a_{s})
. The scheme dependent part of the subtracted hard cross section at this order is therefore
-c_{\scalebox{0.6}{$\overline{\mathrm{MS}}$}}\,a_{s}{}^{R_{1}}\gamma_{J}^{(0)}\bigl/2
times the Born term
\hat{\sigma}^{(0)}
. Expanding the double Drell-Yan cross section to NLO, we have twice this factor times the LO cross section from the scheme dependence of the hard cross sections. This cancels the scheme dependence due to the NLO part (
2.101
) of the splitting DPDs for each incoming proton.

in a parton

or

For real emission graphs, the situation is less simple. We find that graphs that have the same colour factors in the singlet channel do not necessarily have the same colour factors for other representations. Examples are graphs
3(a)
,

,
3(c)

and

for

and

Despite these complications, we do find a number of simple patterns in the colour factors for the real graphs. Together with the simple scaling rule for virtual graphs, we find the following.

•

Denoting the sum of real and virtual two-loop graphs for a channel by
{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}\mskip 1.5mu\Gamma_{a_{1}a_{2},a_{0}}

\displaystyle\frac{{}^{AA}\mskip 1.5mu\Gamma_{gg,g}}{{}^{SS}\mskip 1.5mu\Gamma_{gg,g}}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{gg,g}(AA)}{c_{gg,g}(SS)}=-1\,,

\displaystyle\frac{{}^{8A}\mskip 1.5mu\Gamma_{qg,q}}{{}^{8S}\mskip 1.5mu\Gamma_{qg,q}}=\frac{{}^{8A}\mskip 1.5mu\Gamma_{qg,g}}{{}^{8S}\mskip 1.5mu\Gamma_{qg,g}}

At the point

is a gluon, which must couple to an eikonal line if one computes in Feynman gauge.

1.

Soft singularities
are due to the region where both
\boldsymbol{k}_{3}
and
z_{3}
go to zero at fixed
\boldsymbol{k}_{3}^{2}/z_{3}
. The minus-momentum
k_{3}^{-}
of the final state parton

remains finite in this limit and thus does not lead to a suppression due to other lines in the graph going far off shell.

We regulate these singularities by working in
4-2\epsilon
dimensions. The volume of the transverse momentum integration

then contributes a fractional power
z_{3}^{1-\epsilon}
in the relevant momentum region. With one factor
1/z_{3}
from the invariant phase space of
a_{3}
and another factor
1/z_{3}
from the eikonal propagator, one obtains a behaviour
z_{3}^{-1-\epsilon}
for the graph, unless there are additional factors of
z_{3}
in the numerator. The convolution integral in
z_{3}
is well defined, provided that one takes
\epsilon<0
, as is appropriate for the regularisation of divergences in the infrared.

2.

Rapidity singularities
are due to the region in which
z_{3}
becomes small but
\boldsymbol{k}_{3}
does not. With
k_{3}^{-}
scaling like
1/z_{3}
in this limit, at least one line in the graph goes far off shell, and its propagator denominator gives a factor
z_{3}
. With
1/z_{3}
from the phase space of
a_{3}
and
1/z_{3}
from the unregulated eikonal propagator, one obtains a singular

behaviour of the kernel if there are no additional powers of
z_{3}
from additional off-shell lines or from the numerator of the graph.

We find that the only graphs with rapidity divergences are those that have UND topology (see figure

Let us note in passing that the two kinematic regions just discussed resemble the ultrasoft and soft modes in soft-collinear effective theory (SCET)

Symmetries.

Let us now discuss the properties of splitting kernels that follow from charge conjugation invariance. Channels in which all external partons are quarks or antiquarks will be called “pure quark channels” in the following. The kernels for pure quark channels that are related by charge conjugation are equal. For kernels with external gluons, we have

\displaystyle{}^{RR}V_{q\bar{q},g}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle={}^{RR}V_{\bar{q}q,g}(z_{1},z_{2})\,,

\displaystyle{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}V_{qg,q}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=\eta(R_{2})\,{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}V_{\bar{q}g,\bar{q}}(z_{1},z_{2})\,,

\displaystyle{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}V_{qg,g}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=\eta(R_{2})\,{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}V_{\bar{q}g,g}(z_{1},z_{2})\,,

\displaystyle{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}V_{gg,q}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=\eta(R_{1})\mskip 1.5mu\eta(R_{2})\,{}^{\overline{R}_{1}\mskip-1.5mu\overline{R}_{2}}V_{gg,\bar{q}}(z_{1},z_{2})\,,

\displaystyle{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}V_{gg,g}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=\eta(R_{1})\mskip 1.5mu\eta(R_{2})\,{}^{\overline{R}_{1}\mskip-1.5mu\overline{R}_{2}}V_{gg,g}(z_{1},z_{2})\,,

(4.10)

where the sign factor
\eta(R)
is
-1
for
R=A
and
+1
otherwise.
The relation in the last line implies

\displaystyle{}^{AS}V_{gg,g}

\displaystyle={}^{S\mskip-1.5muA}V_{gg,g}=0\,,

\displaystyle{}^{\scalebox{0.7}{$10\phantom{\overline{1}}\hskip-3.01389pt$}\mskip 1.5mu\scalebox{0.7}{$\overline{10}$}}V_{gg,g}

\displaystyle={}^{\scalebox{0.7}{$\overline{10}$}\,\scalebox{0.7}{$10\phantom{\overline{1}}\hskip-3.01389pt$}}V_{gg,g}\,.

(4.11)

A detailed proof of these relations is somewhat lengthy and shall not be given here. In a first step, one can prove relations for the unsubtracted distributions
F_{\text{us},a_{1}a_{2}}
, using the charge conjugation properties for the colour projected twist-two operators given in (
A.8
). At this stage, a sign factor
\eta(A)=-1
appears for each gluon pair coupled to an antisymmetric octet. Extending these relations to the subtracted distributions
F_{a_{1}a_{2}}
is easy, because the soft factor appearing in that step depends only on the multiplicity of the colour representations and does not distinguish between quarks and antiquarks. Corresponding relations for the splitting kernels are then obtained from the splitting formula (
2.1
) by using the charge conjugation relations for PDFs.

The preceding relations hold at all orders in the coupling. In addition, explicit calculation yields

\displaystyle{}^{RR}V^{(2)}_{q^{\prime}\mskip-1.5mu\bar{q}^{\prime}\!,q}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle={}^{RR}V^{(2)}_{\bar{q}^{\prime}\mskip-1.5muq^{\prime}\!,q}(z_{1},z_{2})

(4.12)

\displaystyle{}^{AS}V_{gg,q}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle=-\;{}^{S\mskip-1.5muA}V_{gg,q}(z_{1},z_{2})

(4.13)

and

\displaystyle{}^{\scalebox{0.7}{$10\phantom{\overline{1}}\hskip-3.01389pt$}\mskip 1.5mu\scalebox{0.7}{$\overline{10}$}}V_{gg,g}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle={}^{\scalebox{0.7}{$10\phantom{\overline{1}}\hskip-3.01389pt$}\mskip 1.5mu\scalebox{0.7}{$\overline{10}$}}V_{gg,q}(z_{1},z_{2})={}^{\scalebox{0.7}{$\overline{10}$}\,\scalebox{0.7}{$10\phantom{\overline{1}}\hskip-3.01389pt$}}V_{gg,q}(z_{1},z_{2})=0

(4.14)

at two-loop accuracy.

Together with the trivial symmetry relation

\displaystyle{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}V_{a_{1}a_{2},a_{0}}(z_{1},z_{2})

\displaystyle={}^{R_{2}\mskip-1.5muR_{1}}V_{a_{2}\mskip 1.5mua_{1},a_{0}}(z_{2},z_{1})\,,

(4.15)

we find that the kernels

\displaystyle{}^{RR}V^{(2)}_{q^{\prime}\mskip-1.5mu\bar{q}^{\prime}\!,q}(z_{1},z_{2})\,,\qquad{}^{RR}V_{q\bar{q},g}(z_{1},z_{2})\,,\qquad{}^{R_{1}\mskip-1.5muR_{2}}V_{gg,g}(z_{1},z_{2})

are even under the exchange

, whereas

is odd for the combinations

and

We now analyse the limit in which

The limit

\displaystyle V^{[2,k]}_{\text{reg}}(uz,\bar{u}z)

\displaystyle=V^{[2,k]}_{r}(uz,\bar{u}z)+V^{[2,k]}_{s}(uz,\bar{u}z)

\displaystyle\text{for }k=0,1\,,

where the first term is integrable over

in the limit

, whereas the second one develops a
1/(1-z)
singularity at

. This singularity arises from graphs in which the initial parton
a_{0}
splits into
a_{2}
and another parton. That parton has a momentum fraction
1-z_{2}=1-z(1-u)
, which appears in the denominator of its propagator.

For
V_{r}
and for the terms with a plus or

. One obtains
1/u
for the leading behaviour. For
V_{s}
, one must take the

and not at the level of the kernel. This part of the kernel has the general form

\displaystyle z\mskip 1.5muV_{s}(uz,\bar{u}z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2,3,4}\frac{\bar{z}^{n-2}\,h_{n}(\bar{z},u)}{(\bar{z}+u-\bar{z}u)^{n}}+\frac{\log u-\log(\bar{z}+u-\bar{z}u)}{\bar{z}}\,\frac{h_{1,a}}{\bar{z}+u-\bar{z}u}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\log\bar{z}-\log(\bar{z}+u-\bar{z}u)}{u}\,\frac{h_{1,b}}{\bar{z}+u-\bar{z}u}+\frac{h_{1,c}(\bar{z},u)}{\bar{z}+u-\bar{z}u}\,,

(4.50)

where on the r.h.s. we have changed variables from
z
to
\bar{z}=1-z
. For ease of writing, we omit the superscript
[2,k]
on
V_{s}
and on the coefficients

henceforth.

and
h_{1,b}
are numerical constants, whilst

or

Notice that no explicit factor

\displaystyle V_{s}\underset{12}{\otimes}f

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x}\,\int_{0}^{1-x}\!d\bar{z}\;V_{s}(uz,\bar{u}z)\,f\left(\frac{x}{1-\bar{z}}\right)\,.

This is revealed by power counting (with
\log u
counting as order

and thus obtains a contribution of order

to the integral. By contrast,

[

]
, we can compute the leading behaviour of the integral by expanding the integrand for small
\bar{z}
at fixed

. To implement this, we rescale

and

, and finally set

\displaystyle V_{s}\underset{12}{\otimes}f=\frac{1}{x}\,\int_{0}^{1-x}\!d\bar{z}\;

\displaystyle\Biggl[\,\sum_{n=2,3,4}\bar{z}^{n-2}\;\frac{h_{n,0}+h_{n,1}\mskip 1.5mu\log\bar{z}}{(\bar{z}+u)^{n}}+\frac{\log u-\log(\bar{z}+u)}{\bar{z}}\,\frac{h_{1,a}}{\bar{z}+u}

\displaystyle\,+\frac{\log\bar{z}-\log(\bar{z}+u)}{u}\,\frac{h_{1,b}}{\bar{z}+u}\,\Biggr]\,f(x)+\ldots\,,

(4.52)

where we used the leading behaviour

in the limit just specified.
The ellipsis denotes subleading terms in the
\varepsilon
expansion and in particular contains the full contribution from the term with

in (

). One can readily carry out the integral over
\bar{z}
for the terms given in (
4.4
) and then take the limit

1/u
and is enhanced by
\log u
for the terms with nonzero coefficients

.

6
Conclusions

DPDs at small inter-parton distance
y
are dominated by the splitting of one parton into two. We computed the kernels that describe this splitting at order
a_{s}^{2}
for all colour configurations of the observed partons. All partons are taken to be unpolarised. The colour dependence of the DPDs is classified by a pair of irreducible representations
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
of the colour group. For representations different from the singlet, Sudakov double logarithms in the renormalisation scale
\mu
and the rapidity parameter
\zeta_{p}
appear in the splitting kernels. These logarithms go along with rapidity divergences in the intermediate stages of the calculation, which need to be regularised appropriately. We computed the kernels independently with the Collins regulator, which uses spacelike Wilson lines, and with the
\delta
regulator of Echevarria et al. Intermediate results differ in the two cases, for instance by the order of ultraviolet poles in DPD matrix elements and the soft factor, but in both cases we find complete cancellation of ultraviolet and rapidity divergences when all building blocks are assembled. The resulting splitting kernels are identical for both regulators.

In the context of multi-loop calculations, the Collins regulator is more involved because eikonal propagators depend not only on plus-momenta. However, in the limit of large Wilson line rapidities, these propagators take a simple distributional form. With this form, loop integrals are no more difficult than those to be computed with the
\delta
regulator, where eikonal propagators depend only on plus-momenta. Whilst in our two-loop calculation, there is at most one eikonal propagator per graph, we see no impediment to using the same procedure at higher orders.

At order
a_{s}
, the splitting DPDs for a given parton combination are proportional to each other for the different colour channels
R_{1}R_{2}
. This no longer holds at order
a_{s}^{2}
, where the colour dependence of the splitting kernels presents a rather varied picture. Nevertheless, we identified a number of simple patterns.

1.

At two-loop accuracy, the splitting DPD for
gg
in decuplet representations is zero, and the distributions
{}^{8A}\mskip-1.5muF_{qg}
and
{}^{8S}\mskip-1.5muF_{qg}
for the gluon in the antisymmetric and symmetric octet are proportional to each other.

2.

At two loops, terms containing Sudakov double logarithms are proportional to the one-loop splitting result times the one-loop anomalous dimension (
2.6
) of the Collins-Soper kernel for the rapidity dependence of the DPD.

3.

A threshold logarithm
\log(1-x_{1}-x_{2})
appears in the splitting DPDs at two loops and is accompanied by a renormalisation group logarithm
L=2\log(y\mu/b_{0})
. Its coefficient is nonzero for the distributions
{}^{11}\mskip-1.5muF_{gg}
,
{}^{27\,27}\mskip-1.5muF_{gg}
,
{}^{11}\mskip-1.5muF_{q\bar{q}}
,
{}^{88}\mskip-1.5muF_{q\bar{q}}
, and
{}^{11}\mskip-1.5muF_{qg}
, but vanishes for
gg
and
qg
distributions in the octet channels.

4.

When the sum
z=z_{1}+z_{2}
of parton momentum fractions in the two-loop kernel becomes small, a leading
1/z^{2}
behaviour is found for
g\to gg
,
q\to gg
, and
g\to q\bar{q}
. This gives rise to a high-energy logarithm
\log(x_{1}+x_{2})
when convoluted with PDFs of a suitable shape, as specified in (
4.44
). If
\mu
equals
\mu_{y}=b_{0}/y
, the leading
1/z^{2}
terms for different channels
R_{1}R_{2}
scale exactly like the one-loop kernels, but for other values of
\mu
they do not.

5.

There is no simple pattern for the colour dependence of splitting DPDs in the limit of small
u=x_{1}/(x_{1}+x_{2})
. The strongest enhancement in this limit is by a factor
u^{-1}\log u
. It appears in the
g\to gg
and
q\to gq
channels and goes along with a renormalisation group logarithm
L
.

6.

In the triple Regge limit
x_{1}\ll x_{1}+x_{2}\ll 1
, a double enhancement like
u^{-1}\log(x_{1}+x_{2})
appears in
{}^{11}\mskip-1.5muF_{gg}
,
{}^{SS}\mskip-1.5muF_{gg}
, and
{}^{27\,27}\mskip-1.5muF_{gg}
for PDFs of a suitable shape. It originates from a
1/(uz^{2})
behaviour of the splitting kernels and is accompanied by a factor
L
. In the case of
{}^{SS}\mskip-1.5muF_{gg}
, the subprocess
q\to gg
is enhanced in this way, whereas
g\to gg
is not. The behaviour of other DPDs depends on the order in which the limits
x_{1}\ll x_{1}+x_{2}
and
x_{1}+x_{2}\ll 1
are taken.

7.

Because of charge conjugation invariance, the distributions
{}^{AS}\mskip-1.5muF_{gg}
or
{}^{S\mskip-1.5muA}\mskip-1.5muF_{gg}
receive no contribution from
g\to gg
, whilst
q\to gg
and
\bar{q}\to gg
contribute with opposite sign. The splitting formula for these channels hence involves the valence distributions
f_{q}-f_{\bar{q}}
. At two-loop accuracy we find that
{}^{AS}\mskip-1.5muF_{gg}={}-{}^{S\mskip-1.5muA}\mskip-1.5muF_{gg}
.

Full analytic expressions of the kernels are given in the ancillary files associated with this paper on
arXiv
.

A detailed numerical study of two-loop splitting effects in DPDs is beyond the scope of this work. However, the plots in section
5
show a few examples in selected kinematics. We find that for
gg
distributions, the
a_{s}^{2}
kernels provide corrections not exceeding a few
10\%
relative to the
a_{s}
terms if we evaluate the splitting formula at
\mu=\sqrt{x_{1}x_{2}\mskip 1.5mu\zeta_{p}}=\mu_{y}
. However, when we change
\mu
by a factor of
2
in either direction, the
a_{s}^{2}
corrections become of order
100\%
and larger in parts of the
x_{1}
–
x_{2}
plane. To obtain reliable perturbative results for scales different from
\mu_{y}
, it is therefore better to evaluate the splitting formula at
\mu_{y}
and then use the appropriate evolution equations. The distribution
{}^{AS}\mskip-1.5muF_{gg}
is found to be tiny compared with distributions that receive a contribution from
g\to gg
splitting.

For
qg
distributions at
\mu=\sqrt{x_{1}x_{2}\mskip 1.5mu\zeta_{p}}=\mu_{y}
, we find small to moderate
a_{s}^{2}
corrections for
x_{1}\sim x_{2}
and
x_{1}>x_{2}
. For
x_{1}\ll x_{2}
, however, the
a_{s}^{2}
contribution from
g\to qg
splitting completely dominates
{F}_{qg}
, because it has a
1/u
enhancement that is absent in the
q\to qg
channel up to that order.

With the results of the present paper, we have added a further ingredient for an analysis of double parton scattering at NLO. An extension to the case of polarised DPDs seems straightforward with the methods developed here and in
[
64
]
. Complications due to the use of
\gamma_{5}
and
\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
in dimensional regularisation can be avoided using the procedure employed in
[
78
]
. We intend to come back to this in future work.

\delta(z_{3})
read

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mskip 1.5mu\delta(z_{3})\int d^{2-2\epsilon}\boldsymbol{k}_{1}\,d^{2-2\epsilon}\boldsymbol{k}_{2}\;\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{k}_{2}^{2}\,(\boldsymbol{k}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\Delta})^{2}}\,\biggl[\log\frac{\rho}{\boldsymbol{\Delta^{2}}}-\log(z_{1}z_{2})-\log\frac{\boldsymbol{k}_{3}^{2}}{\boldsymbol{\Delta^{2}}}\biggr]

\displaystyle\quad=-\frac{1}{2}\mskip 1.5mu\delta(z_{3})\int d^{2-2\epsilon}\boldsymbol{k}_{1}\,d^{2-2\epsilon}\boldsymbol{k}_{2}\;\frac{1}{\boldsymbol{k}_{2}^{2}\,(\boldsymbol{k}_{1}-\boldsymbol{\Delta})^{2}}\,\log\frac{\boldsymbol{k}_{3}^{2}}{\boldsymbol{\Delta^{2}}}\,,

(C.2)

where we recall that
\boldsymbol{k}_{3}=-\boldsymbol{k}_{1}-\boldsymbol{k}_{2}
. The integrals going with the first two terms in the square brackets on the l.h.s. are scaleless and therefore give zero. If one takes the
\delta
regulator instead, this applies to all terms going with
\delta(z_{3})
, because the corresponding identity (
3.17
) does not involve transverse loop momenta.

This example provides a caveat to our statement in section
3.2
that graphs which give no rapidity divergence can simply be evaluated with the unregulated
1/k_{3}^{+}
eikonal propagator. This is not true for the Collins regulator when a would-be rapidity divergence is absent because an eikonal propagator is multiplied by a loop integral that is scaleless in the unregulated case but not scaleless in the presence of the regulator. In other words, for integrals of the type (
C.1
) it matters whether one removes the rapidity regulator before or after performing the transverse loop integrals.
We find, however, that such integrals do not occur for the graphs in our calculation.

When using the Collins regulator to compute the real graphs, we take the
\rho\to\infty
limit (
3.18
) of the eikonal propagators before evaluating the loop integrals. One may wonder whether it is advantageous to calculate with the propagators (
3.16
) at finite
\rho
instead, postponing the
\rho\to\infty
limit to a later stage.

It is indeed possible to apply integration-by-part reduction to the integrals with finite
\rho
. The set of master integrals is then augmented, because one has an additional denominator factor
(z_{3}^{2}-\boldsymbol{k}_{3}^{2}\mskip 1.5muz_{1}z_{2}\big/\rho)
. After the reduction, each graph is a sum over different terms with a
z_{3}
and
\rho
dependence of the form

\displaystyle\frac{z_{3}^{n+1}}{z_{3}^{2}-\boldsymbol{k}_{3}^{2}\mskip 1.5muz_{1}z_{2}\big/\rho}

\displaystyle\text{ with }n=-2,-1,0,1,2,3

(C.3)

or

\displaystyle\frac{\rho\mskip 1.5muz_{3}^{n+1}}{z_{3}^{2}-\boldsymbol{k}_{3}^{2}\mskip 1.5muz_{1}z_{2}\big/\rho}

\displaystyle\text{ with }n=0,1,2,3

(C.4)

times a function that is finite in the limit
z_{3}\to 0
. Evaluating the augmented set of master integrals with the differential equation technique would be a formidable task, so that it seems preferable to take
\rho\to\infty
at this point.

The form (
C.3
) with
n\geq 0
has a simple
\rho\to\infty
limit according to (
3.18
) and in this sense is unproblematic. For
n<0
, however, one obtains a non-integrable singularity at
z_{3}=0
when taking
\rho\to\infty
. Moreover, terms of the form (
C.4
) grow linearly with
\rho
and thus have a stronger rapidity divergence than the full result, which we know to behave like
\log\rho
. One thus finds that the individual terms after integration-by-part reduction are more singular than the graphs one needs to compute. Combining individual terms so as to obtain less singular expressions would be at odds with the very idea of a reduction to master integrals. We find this unattractive and prefer to take the limit
\rho\to\infty
as early as possible in the calculation, as described in section
3.2
.

Appendix D
Distributional identities

In this appendix, we derive the distributional identity (
3.18
) required for the Collins regulator. Its analogue (
3.17
) for the
\delta
regulator can be established in a very similar manner, as discussed at the end.

To begin with, we cast (
3.18
) into a simpler form. Replacing
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
in terms of
u
,
\bar{u}
and
z_{3}
as specified in (
3.14
), we obtain

\displaystyle\underset{\sigma\to 0}{\lim}\;\operatorname{PV}\frac{t}{t^{2}-\sigma^{2}\,(1-t)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{0}(t)-\frac{\log\sigma^{2}}{2}\mskip 1.5mu\delta(t)\,,

(D.1)

where we write
t=z_{3}
and
\sigma^{2}=u\bar{u}\,\boldsymbol{k}_{3}^{2}\big/\rho
. It is understood that the identity (
D.1
) is to be integrated over
t\in[0,1]
with a sufficiently regular test function
\varphi(t)
.

Let us first show that the term
\sigma^{2}\,(1-t^{2})
on the l.h.s. of (
D.1
) can be replaced with
\sigma^{2}
. Integrating over a test function, we have for the difference of the two versions

\displaystyle\Delta I

\displaystyle=\underset{\sigma\to 0}{\lim}\;\operatorname{PV}\int_{0}^{1}dt\,\varphi(t)\biggl[\frac{t}{t^{2}-\sigma^{2}}-\frac{t}{t^{2}-\sigma^{2}\,(1-t)^{2}}\biggr]

\displaystyle=\underset{\sigma\to 0}{\lim}\;\sigma^{2}\operatorname{PV}\int_{0}^{2\sigma}dt\;\varphi(t)\,\frac{2-t}{t^{2}-\sigma^{2}\,(1-t)^{2}}\;\frac{t^{2}}{t^{2}-\sigma^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\underset{\sigma\to 0}{\lim}\;\sigma^{2}\int_{2\sigma}^{1}dt\;\varphi(t)\,\frac{2-t}{t^{2}-\sigma^{2}\,(1-t)^{2}}\;\frac{t^{2}}{t^{2}-\sigma^{2}}\,,

(D.2)

where in the second step we have split the integration into two parts. The PV prescription applies to both singularities at
t=\sigma
and
t=\sigma/(1+\sigma)
. In the first integral on the r.h.s., we can replace
\varphi(t)
with
\varphi(0)
, using that the test function is sufficiently smooth. In the second integral, the factor multiplying
\varphi(t)
is a monotonic function if
\sigma
is sufficiently small, so that we can use the mean value theorem for integrals and replace
\varphi(t)
with
\varphi(t_{\sigma})
at some point
t_{\sigma}\in[2\sigma,1]
. Rescaling the integration variable as
t=\sigma\tau
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta I

\displaystyle\quad=\underset{\sigma\to 0}{\lim}\;\sigma\varphi(0)\,\operatorname{PV}\int_{0}^{2}d\tau\;\frac{2-\sigma\tau}{\tau^{2}-(1-\sigma\tau)^{2}}\;\frac{\tau^{2}}{\tau^{2}-1}

\displaystyle\qquad+\underset{\sigma\to 0}{\lim}\;\sigma\varphi(t_{\sigma})\int_{2}^{1/\sigma}d\tau\;\frac{2-\sigma\tau}{\tau^{2}-(1-\sigma\tau)^{2}}\;\frac{\tau^{2}}{\tau^{2}-1}\,.

(D.3)

The two integrals over
\tau
remain finite for
\sigma\to 0
, as can be checked by explicit calculation, so that
\Delta I=0
in that limit.

We now show (
D.1
) without the factor
(1-t)^{2}
. To this end, we decompose the integral over a test function into three terms

\displaystyle\underset{\sigma\to 0}{\lim}\;\operatorname{PV}\int_{0}^{1}dt\;\varphi(t)\,\frac{t}{t^{2}-\sigma^{2}}

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}

(D.4)

with

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}dt\;\frac{\varphi(t)-\varphi(0)}{t}\,,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\underset{\sigma\to 0}{\lim}\;\operatorname{PV}\int_{0}^{1}dt\;\frac{\varphi(t)-\varphi(0)}{t}\,\frac{\sigma^{2}}{t^{2}-\sigma^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=\varphi(0)\;\underset{\sigma\to 0}{\lim}\;\operatorname{PV}\int_{0}^{1}dt\;\frac{t}{t^{2}-\sigma^{2}}\,.\hskip-30.00005pt

(D.5)

One readily sees that
I_{1}=\int_{0}^{1}dt\;\varphi(t)\,\mathcal{L}_{0}(t)
, and straightforward evaluation of the integral in
I_{3}
yields

\displaystyle I_{3}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\log\sigma^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{1}dt\;\varphi(t)\,\delta(t)\,,

(D.6)

so that (
D.1
) without the factor
(1-t)^{2}
is established by showing that
I_{2}=0
.

We now assume that
\varphi(t)-\varphi(0)
is continuous and finite in the interval
t\in[0,1]
. We also assume that it behaves like
ct^{\alpha}
with
\alpha>0
for
t\to 0
. Notice that this is less than requiring
\varphi(t)
to be differentiable at
t=0
, because
\alpha
need not be an integer. The function

\displaystyle\psi(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi(t)-\varphi(0)}{t^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\text{ with }\beta

\displaystyle=\min(\alpha,1)

(D.7)

is then finite for
t\in[0,1]
, with
\psi(0)=c
for
\alpha=\beta
and
\psi(0)=0
otherwise. Splitting the integration into two terms, we have

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\underset{\sigma\to 0}{\lim}\;\operatorname{PV}\int_{0}^{2\sigma}dt\;\frac{\psi(t)}{t^{1-\beta}}\,\frac{\sigma^{2}}{t^{2}-\sigma^{2}}+\underset{\sigma\to 0}{\lim}\;\int_{2\sigma}^{1}dt\;\frac{\psi(t)}{t^{1-\beta}}\,\frac{\sigma^{2}}{t^{2}-\sigma^{2}}\,.

(D.8)

For sufficiently small
\sigma
, we can replace
\psi(t)
with
\psi(0)
in the first term on the r.h.s., and for the second term we can use the mean value theorem for integrals. This gives

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\psi(0)\,\underset{\sigma\to 0}{\lim}\;\operatorname{PV}\int_{0}^{2\sigma}\frac{dt}{t^{1-\beta}}\,\frac{\sigma^{2}}{t^{2}-\sigma^{2}}+\underset{\sigma\to 0}{\lim}\;\psi(t_{\sigma})\int_{2\sigma}^{1}\frac{dt}{t^{1-\beta}}\,\frac{\sigma^{2}}{t^{2}-\sigma^{2}}

(D.9)

with some value
t_{\sigma}\in[2\sigma,1]
different from the one that appears in (
D
). Rescaling the integration variable as
t=\sigma\tau
, we finally obtain

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\psi(0)\,\underset{\sigma\to 0}{\lim}\;\sigma^{\beta}\operatorname{PV}\int_{0}^{2}\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{1-\beta}}\;\frac{1}{\tau^{2}-1}+\underset{\sigma\to 0}{\lim}\;\psi(t_{\sigma})\,\sigma^{\beta}\int_{2}^{\infty}\frac{d\tau}{\tau^{1-\beta}}\;\frac{1}{\tau^{2}-1}=0\,,

(D.10)

which concludes our proof.

The distributional identity (
3.17
) we use with the
\delta
regulator has the same form as (
D.1
) with
t^{2}+\sigma^{2}\,(1-t)^{2}
instead of
t^{2}-\sigma^{2}\,(1-t)^{2}
on the l.h.s. The PV prescription is not needed in that case. The proof of this identity proceeds in analogy to what we have shown, with the simplification that one does not need to split integrals into two parts as we did in (
D
) and (
D.8
).

Group actions on multitrees and the K K -theory of their crossed products
Nathan Brownlowe, Jack Spielberg, Anne Thomas, Victor Wu

Abstract
. We study group actions on multitrees, which are directed graphs in which there is at most one directed path between any two vertices. In our main result we describe a six-term exact sequence in K K -theory for the reduced crossed product C 0 ​ ( ∂ E ) ⋊ r G C_{0}(\partial E)\rtimes_{r}G induced from the action of a countable discrete group G G on a row-finite, finitely-aligned multitree E E with no sources. We provide formulas for the K K -theory of C 0 ​ ( ∂ E ) ⋊ r G C_{0}(\partial E)\rtimes_{r}G in the case where G G acts freely on E E , and in the case where all vertex stabilisers are infinite cyclic. We study the action G ↷ ∂ E G\curvearrowright\partial E in a range of settings, and describe minimality, local contractivity, topological freeness, and amenability in terms of properties of the underlying data. In an application of our main theorem, we describe a six-term exact sequence in K K -theory for the crossed product induced from a group acting on the boundary of an undirected tree.

Group actions on trees are also central to the field of geometric group theory. Here, they are studied using Bass–Serre theory, which establishes a bijection between group actions on trees and quotient structures called graphs of groups

•

2.
Preliminaries on group actions on graphs

2.1.
Graphs and trees

An
undirected graph

\Gamma=(\Gamma^{0},\Gamma^{1},r,s)
(called simply a
graph
in
[
8
,
17
,
23
]
) consists of countable sets of
vertices

\Gamma^{0}
and
edges

\Gamma^{1}
together with
range
and
source maps

r,s\colon\Gamma^{1}\to\Gamma^{0}
, as well as an involutive “edge reversal” map
e\mapsto\overline{e}
on
\Gamma^{1}
. We insist that
e\neq\overline{e}
and
r(e)=s(\overline{e})
. We say that an undirected graph
\Gamma
is
locally finite
if
\lvert r^{-1}(v)\rvert<\infty
for all
v\in\Gamma^{0}
, and
nonsingular
if
\lvert r^{-1}(v)\rvert>1
for all
v\in\Gamma^{0}
. All undirected graphs in this paper will be locally finite and nonsingular.

A
path (of length
n
)
in
\Gamma
is either a vertex
v\in\Gamma^{0}
(when
n=0
) or, if
n>0
, a sequence of edges
e_{1}e_{2}\dots e_{n}
with
s(e_{i})=r(e_{i+1})
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
(we note that we are using the right-to-left “Australian” convention for notating paths, to be consistent with
[
8
]
and
[
17
]
). A path
e_{1}e_{2}\dots e_{n}
is
reduced
if either
n=0
, or
e_{i+1}\neq\overline{e_{i}}
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
(that is, there is no back-tracking in the path). We write
\Gamma^{n}
(respectively,
\Gamma^{n}_{r}
) for the collection of all paths (respectively, all
reduced
paths) of length
n
in
\Gamma
, and we write
\Gamma^{*}_{r}
for the collection of all reduced paths of any length in
\Gamma
. For any vertex
v\in\Gamma^{0}
, we write
v\Gamma^{*}_{r}
(respectively
v\Gamma^{n}_{r}
) for the collection of reduced paths in
\Gamma
(respectively, the collection of reduced paths of length
n
) with range
v
, and we write
\Gamma^{*}_{r}v
(respectively
\Gamma^{n}_{r}v
) for the collection of reduced paths in
\Gamma
(respectively, the collection of reduced paths of length
n
) with source
v
.

We call a graph
\Gamma

connected
if for any two vertices
v,w\in\Gamma^{0}
there is a path in
\Gamma
between them. We call
\Gamma
a
tree
if for any two vertices
v,w\in\Gamma^{0}
, there is a unique reduced path from
v
to
w
.

We will also be considering
directed
graphs. A
directed graph

E=(E^{0},E^{1},r,s)
consists of countable sets of vertices
E^{0}
and edges
E^{1}
together with range and source maps
r,s\colon E^{1}\to E^{0}
, but without an edge reversal map. Equivalently, we can think of a directed graph
E
as an undirected graph
\Gamma
(called the
underlying undirected graph
of
E
) equipped with an
orientation
, which is a subset
\Gamma_{+}^{1}
of
\Gamma^{1}
which contains exactly one element of
\{e,\overline{e}\}
for each
e\in\Gamma^{1}
; this orientation specifies a direction for each edge in the graph. A
directed tree
is a directed graph whose underlying undirected graph is a tree.

Paths in a directed graph are defined and notated in the same way as for undirected graphs. If we form a directed graph from an orientation on an undirected graph, then paths can only consist of edges in the orientation. Note however that the concept of a reduced path is redundant for directed graphs, since the reversal of an edge in an orientation cannot be an element of that orientation. We will therefore omit the subscript ‘
r
’ in the
\Gamma^{*}_{r}
notation when
\Gamma
is directed.

We say that a directed graph
E
is
row-finite
if
\lvert r^{-1}(v)\rvert<\infty
for all
v\in E^{0}
, and we say that a vertex
v\in E^{0}
is a
source
if
\lvert r^{-1}(v)\rvert=0
. All directed graphs in this paper will be row-finite and have no sources.

2.2.
Group actions on graphs

A group
G
is said to
act
(on the left) on a graph
\Gamma
(undirected or directed) if it acts on the sets
\Gamma^{0}
and
\Gamma^{1}
in such a way that
r(g\cdot e)=g\cdot r(e)
,
s(g\cdot e)=g\cdot s(e)
and (for undirected
\Gamma
)
\overline{g\cdot e}=g\cdot\overline{e}
for all
e\in\Gamma^{1}
and
g\in G
. For each
v\in\Gamma^{0}
, we write
G_{v}\leq G
for the stabiliser subgroup of
G
at
v
, and similarly for each
e\in\Gamma^{1}
we write
G_{e}\leq G
for the stabiliser subgroup of
G
at
e
. These stabiliser subgroups have the following properties:

(1)

If
v,w\in\Gamma^{0}
are in the same orbit under the action of
G
, then the subgroups
G_{v}
and
G_{w}
are conjugate. Indeed, if
g\in G
satisfies
g\cdot v=w
, then
G_{w}=gG_{v}g^{-1}
. Similarly, if
e,f\in\Gamma^{1}
are in the same orbit under the action of
G
, then the subgroups
G_{e}
and
G_{f}
are conjugate.

(2)

For any
e\in\Gamma^{1}
, the group
G_{e}
is a subgroup of both
G_{r(e)}
and
G_{s(e)}
. This is because any automorphism of
\Gamma
fixing
e
must also fix both the vertices
r(e)
and
s(e)
.

(3)

By the orbit-stabiliser theorem, for any
e\in\Gamma^{1}
, the orbit
G_{r(e)}\cdot e
is in one-to-one correspondence with the set of cosets
G_{r(e)}/G_{e}
. In particular, if
\Gamma
is locally finite or row-finite, then
G_{e}
is a finite-index subgroup of
G_{r(e)}
.

If a group
G
acts on the directed graph
E
, then we can also consider the
quotient directed graph

G\backslash E
associated to this action, which is defined as follows. The vertices
(G\backslash E)^{0}
are the vertex orbits
\{G\cdot v:v\in E^{0}\}
; the edges
(G\backslash E)^{1}
are the edge orbits
\{G\cdot e:e\in E^{1}\}
; and the range and source maps are given by
r(G\cdot e)=G\cdot r(e)
and
s(G\cdot e)=G\cdot s(e)
, respectively, for
e\in E^{1}
.

2.3.
Boundaries

We recall the definition of the boundary of a locally finite, nonsingular tree.

An
infinite path
in a graph
\Gamma
(undirected or directed) is an infinite sequence of edges
e_{1}e_{2}\dots
such that
s(e_{i})=r(e_{i+1})
for all
i\geq 1
. Like for finite paths, we call an infinite path
e_{1}e_{2}\dots

reduced
if
e_{i+1}\neq\overline{e_{i}}
for
i\geq 1
. We write
\Gamma^{\infty}
(respectively,
\Gamma^{\infty}_{r}
) for the collection of all infinite paths (respectively, all reduced infinite paths) in
\Gamma
. For any
v\in\Gamma^{0}
, we write
v\Gamma^{\infty}_{r}
for the collection of all reduced infinite paths with range
v
. For any infinite path
\lambda=e_{1}e_{2}\dots
in
\Gamma
and any integer
n\geq 1
, we write
\sigma^{n}(\lambda)
for the infinite path
e_{n+1}e_{n+2}\dots
, and
\lambda_{n}
for the finite path
e_{1}\ldots e_{n}
.

If
T
is a locally finite, nonsingular tree, then we can define its
boundary
as follows. Define an equivalence relation on
T^{\infty}_{r}
by
\lambda\sim\mu
if and only if
\sigma^{m}(\lambda)=\sigma^{n}(\mu)
for some
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
(in this case we say that
\lambda
and
\mu
are
shift-tail equivalent
). The
boundary
of
T
is the set of equivalence classes of reduced infinite paths in
T
, and is denoted
\partial T
. For
\lambda\in T^{\infty}_{r}
we write
[\lambda]
for its shift-tail equivalence class in
\partial T
.

We can put a topology on
\partial T
in the following way. For a path
\alpha\in T^{*}_{r}
of length
n
, we say that an infinite path
\lambda\in T^{\infty}_{r}

extends

\alpha
if
\lambda_{n}=\alpha
, and we define the
cylinder set

Z_{\partial}(\alpha)
to be the subset
\{[\lambda]:\lambda\in T^{\infty}_{r}\ \text{extends}\ \alpha\}
of
\partial T
. The collection
\{Z_{\partial}(e):e\in T^{1}\}
is a base for a topology on
\partial T
, turning it into a totally disconnected compact Hausdorff space.

If
T
is directed, then the boundary is defined as above, and now
\{Z_{\partial}(v):v\in T^{0}\}
is a base for a totally disconnected locally compact Hausdorff topology.

An action of a group
G
on a tree
T
naturally induces an action of the same group on the boundary of tree, by
g\cdot[\lambda]=[g\cdot\lambda]
for all
\lambda\in T^{\infty}_{r}
. In this paper, we are interested in understanding actions arising in this way.

(via finite unions, finite intersections, and differences);

(2)

\mathcal{V}
is independent; and

(3)

\mathcal{V}
is finitely aligned.

The set
\mathcal{J}_{H}
is a cofinal subset of
(\mathcal{F}_{H},\subseteq)
.

Remark 3.3
.

Let
H
be any compact subgroup of
G
. These assumptions on
\mathcal{V}
in the statement of Proposition
3.2
generalise the notion of a
regular basis
in
[
11
, Definition 2.9]
, where instead of finite alignment (3), the authors assume that
\mathcal{V}
is closed under intersections (up to the empty set).

To prove Proposition
3.2
we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
F\subseteq I
be finite. Then
\bigcap_{i\in F}V_{i}
decomposes uniquely as a finite disjoint union of sets in
\mathcal{V}
.

Proof.

A standard inductive argument shows that
\mathcal{V}
finitely aligned means that the intersection of finitely many elements of
\mathcal{V}
decomposes as a (possibly empty) finite disjoint union of sets in
\mathcal{V}
. So we just need to prove uniqueness. Suppose that

(3.1)

\bigcap_{i\in F}V_{i}=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}W_{i}=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{n}W^{\prime}_{j},

where
m,n\geq 1
and each
W_{i},W^{\prime}_{j}\in\mathcal{V}
. We want to prove that

\{W_{1},\dots,W_{m}\}=\{W^{\prime}_{1},\dots,W^{\prime}_{n}\}.

Fix
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. By Equation (
3.1
), we have that

W_{i}=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{n}(W_{i}\cap W^{\prime}_{j}),

where each
W_{i}\cap W^{\prime}_{j}
is a disjoint union of sets in
\mathcal{V}
. Then the independence of
\mathcal{V}
implies that
W_{i}\subseteq W_{i}\cap W^{\prime}_{j}
for some
j
, which in turn implies that
W_{i}\subseteq W^{\prime}_{j}
. By symmetry, there is some
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
such that
W^{\prime}_{j}\subseteq W_{k}
, and so
W_{i}\subseteq W_{k}
. But then disjointness of the sets
W_{1},\dots,W_{m}
implies that we must have
i=k
, and so
W_{i}=W^{\prime}_{j}
. This shows that
\{W_{1},\dots,W_{m}\}\subseteq\{W^{\prime}_{1},\dots,W^{\prime}_{n}\}
, and a symmetric argument gives equality.
∎

We are now able to prove Proposition
3.2
.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

Fix
F\in\mathcal{F}_{H}
. For each
Y\subseteq F
we know from Lemma
3.4
that there is a unique (finite)
J_{Y}\subseteq I
with
\bigcap_{i\in Y}V_{i}=\bigsqcup_{j\in J_{Y}}V_{j}
. We claim that
J:=\bigcup_{Y\subseteq F}J_{Y}
satisfies
F\subseteq J
and
J\in\mathcal{J}_{H}
.

To see that
F\subseteq J
, let
i\in F
and
Y=\{i\}
. Then
J_{Y}=\{i\}
, and so
i\in J
. We now show that
J\in\mathcal{J}_{H}
. We first claim that
J
is
H
-invariant. Fix
j\in J
and
h\in H
. Let
Y\subseteq F
with
j\in J_{Y}
, and consider
\bigcap_{i\in h\cdot Y}V_{i}
. Since
F
is
H
-invariant, we know that
h\cdot Y\subseteq F
, and so

\bigcap_{i\in h\cdot Y}V_{i}=\bigsqcup_{k\in J_{h\cdot Y}}V_{k}.

We also have

\bigcap_{i\in h\cdot Y}V_{i}=\bigcap_{i\in Y}V_{h\cdot i}=\bigcap_{i\in Y}h\cdot V_{i}=h\cdot\left(\bigcap_{i\in Y}V_{i}\right)=h\cdot\left(\bigsqcup_{k\in J_{Y}}V_{k}\right)=\bigsqcup_{k\in J_{Y}}h\cdot V_{k}.

This gives two decompositions of
\bigcap_{i\in h\cdot Y}V_{i}
as disjoint unions of sets in
\mathcal{V}
; since by Lemma
3.4
such a decomposition is unique, the set
h\cdot V_{j}
appearing in the second decomposition must be equal to
V_{k}
for some
k\in J_{h\cdot Y}
. Hence
h\cdot j=k\in J
, and so
J
is
H
-invariant. Since
J
is finite (being a finite union of finite sets), we have
J\in\mathcal{F}_{H}
.

To prove that
\mathcal{V}_{J}
is finitely aligned, we need to show that for any
i,j\in J
, there is
J^{\prime}\subseteq J
such that
V_{i}\cap V_{j}=\bigsqcup_{k\in J^{\prime}}V_{k}
.

Let
Y,Y^{\prime}\subseteq F
with
i\in J_{Y}
and
j\in J_{Y^{\prime}}
, and consider
\bigcap_{k\in Y\cup Y^{\prime}}V_{k}
. We have

\bigcap_{k\in Y\cup Y^{\prime}}V_{k}=\bigsqcup_{l\in J_{Y\cup Y^{\prime}}}V_{l}.

We also have

\bigcap_{k\in Y\cup Y^{\prime}}V_{k}=\left(\bigcap_{m\in Y}V_{m}\right)\cap\left(\bigcap_{m^{\prime}\in Y^{\prime}}V_{m^{\prime}}\right)=\left(\bigsqcup_{n\in J_{Y}}V_{n}\right)\cap\left(\bigsqcup_{n^{\prime}\in Y^{\prime}}V_{n^{\prime}}\right)=\bigsqcup_{n\in J_{Y},n^{\prime}\in J_{Y^{\prime}}}(V_{n}\cap V_{n^{\prime}}).

For each
n\in J_{Y}
and
n^{\prime}\in J_{Y^{\prime}}
, finite alignment ensures that there is a set
Y_{n,n^{\prime}}\subseteq I
with
V_{n}\cap V_{n^{\prime}}=\bigsqcup_{p\in Y_{n,n^{\prime}}}V_{p}
, so

\bigcap_{k\in Y\cup Y^{\prime}}V_{k}=\bigsqcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in J_{Y},n^{\prime}\in J_{Y^{\prime}}\\
p\in Y_{n,n^{\prime}}\end{subarray}}V_{p}.

So we have two decompositions of
\bigcap_{k\in Y\cup Y^{\prime}}V_{k}
as disjoint unions of sets in
\mathcal{V}
, and since by Lemma
3.4
such a decomposition is unique, we get that

V_{i}\cap V_{j}=\bigsqcup_{p\in Y_{i,j}}V_{p}=\bigsqcup_{l\in J^{\prime}\subseteq J_{Y\cup Y^{\prime}}}V_{l}

for some subset

. Hence

is finitely aligned.
∎

3.2.
Results on projections

We know from
[
11
, Lemmas 2.2 and 2.3]
that studying sets of compact open subsets of a totally disconnected space which generate all compact open subsets is equivalent to studying sets of projections generating a commutative
C^{*}
-algebra. This subsection is dedicated to proving Proposition
3.6
, which is a generalisation of
[
11
, Lemma 3.8]
, and uses the language of projections and
C^{*}
-algebras.

Notation 3.5
.

For
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
a set of projections, for each
1\leq i\leq n
we define

e^{\prime}_{i}:=e_{i}-\bigvee_{e_{j}<e_{i}}e_{j}.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
be a finite set of commuting projections generating a
C^{*}
-algebra
D
.

(1)

The set
\{e^{\prime}_{1},\dots,e^{\prime}_{n}\}
is a family of non-zero, pairwise orthogonal projections linearly spanning
D
if and only if
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
is independent and
e_{i}e_{j}=\bigvee_{e_{k}\leq e_{i}e_{j}}e_{k}
for any
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

(2)

In this case, the transition matrix
\Gamma=(\gamma_{ij})
determined by the equations
e_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{ij}e_{i}^{\prime}
is a unipotent,
\{0,1\}
-matrix, and hence invertible over
\mathbb{Z}
.

To prove Proposition
3.6
, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
be a finite set of commuting projections generating a
C^{*}
-algebra
D
. Suppose that
\{e^{\prime}_{1},\dots,e^{\prime}_{n}\}
is a family of non-zero, pairwise orthogonal projections linearly spanning
D
. Then
e^{\prime}_{a}\leq e_{i}\iff e_{a}\leq e_{i}
, for any
a,i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
.

Proof.

The implication
e_{a}\leq e_{i}\implies e^{\prime}_{a}\leq e_{i}
is immediate. For the reverse implication, note that we can write each
e_{i}
as
e_{i}=\sum_{a=1}^{n}\gamma_{ai}e_{a}^{\prime}
, where for each
a=1,\dots,n
, we have
\gamma_{ai}=1
if
e^{\prime}_{a}\leq e_{i}
and
\gamma_{ai}=0
otherwise. Pairwise orthogonality of the
e^{\prime}_{i}
means that for each pair of distinct
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we have

0=e^{\prime}_{i}e^{\prime}_{j}=\left(e_{i}-\bigvee_{e_{k}<e_{i}}e_{k}\right)\left(e_{j}-\bigvee_{e_{l}<e_{j}}e_{l}\right)=e_{i}e_{j}-\bigvee_{e_{l}<e_{j}}e_{i}e_{l}-\bigvee_{e_{k}<e_{i}}e_{j}e_{k}+\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}e_{k}<e_{i}\\
e_{l}<e_{j}\end{subarray}}e_{k}e_{l},

and so

\displaystyle e_{i}e_{j}

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{e_{l}<e_{j}}e_{i}e_{l}+\bigvee_{e_{k}<e_{i}}e_{j}e_{k}-\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}e_{k}<e_{i}\\
e_{l}<e_{j}\end{subarray}}e_{k}e_{l}

\displaystyle=\bigvee_{e_{l}<e_{j}}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{n}\gamma_{ai}\gamma_{al}e^{\prime}_{a}\right)+\bigvee_{e_{k}<e_{i}}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{n}\gamma_{aj}\gamma_{ak}e^{\prime}_{a}\right)-\bigvee_{\begin{subarray}{c}e_{k}<e_{i}\\
e_{l}<e_{j}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{n}\gamma_{ak}\gamma_{al}e^{\prime}_{a}\right).

If we define
\mu_{ai}:=\max\{\gamma_{ak}:e_{k}<e_{i}\}
for all
a,i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, then

(3.2)

e_{i}e_{j}=\sum_{a=1}^{n}\gamma_{ai}\mu_{aj}e^{\prime}_{a}+\sum_{a=1}^{n}\gamma_{aj}\mu_{ai}e^{\prime}_{a}-\sum_{a=1}^{n}\mu_{ai}\mu_{aj}e^{\prime}_{a}=\sum_{a=1}^{n}(\gamma_{ai}\mu_{aj}+\gamma_{aj}\mu_{ai}-\mu_{ai}\mu_{aj})e^{\prime}_{a}.

Now suppose that
e^{\prime}_{a}\leq e_{i}
. If
a=i
then
e_{a}\leq e_{i}
trivially, so suppose that
a\neq i
. Multiplying both sides of the inequality
e^{\prime}_{a}\leq e_{i}
by
e_{a}
gives that
e^{\prime}_{a}=e_{a}e^{\prime}_{a}\leq e_{a}e_{i}
. From Equation (
3.2
), this implies that

\gamma_{aa}\mu_{ai}+\gamma_{ai}\mu_{aa}-\mu_{aa}\mu_{ai}=1.

Now,
\gamma_{aa}=1
by definition, since
e^{\prime}_{a}\leq e_{a}
. We also claim that
\mu_{aa}=0
. Indeed, the definition of
\mu_{aa}
is

\mu_{aa}=\max\{\gamma_{ak}:e_{k}<e_{a}\}.

But if
e_{k}<e_{a}
, then by the definition of
e^{\prime}_{a}
we have that
e_{k}e^{\prime}_{a}=0
, and so
\gamma_{ak}=0
. So
\mu_{aa}=0
. This implies that

(1)\mu_{ai}+\gamma_{ai}(0)-(0)\mu_{ai}=\mu_{ai}=1.

By definition of
\mu_{ai}
, this means that there is some
j
such that

e^{\prime}_{a}\leq e_{j}<e_{i}.

If
j=a
, then
e^{\prime}_{a}\leq e_{a}<e_{i}
, and we are done. So suppose
j\neq a
. Then repeating the above argument with
j
instead of
i
yields some
k
such that

e^{\prime}_{a}\leq e_{k}<e_{j}<e_{i}.

Once again, if
k=a
, then
e^{\prime}_{a}\leq e_{a}<\cdots<e_{i}
, and we are done. Otherwise, we can repeatedly apply the above argument, and at each step we have indices
j,k,\dots,b
such that

e^{\prime}_{a}\leq e_{b}<\cdots<e_{k}<e_{j}<e_{i}.

Now since there are a finite number of projections, this process must eventually terminate (otherwise we would get an infinite, strictly decreasing chain of projections, which is impossible). So at some point the chain will look like

e^{\prime}_{a}\leq e_{a}<\cdots<e_{j}<e_{i},

which shows that
e_{a}\leq e_{i}
as desired.
∎

Proof of Proposition
3.6
.

For (1), we first assume that
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
is independent and
e_{i}e_{j}=\bigvee_{e_{k}\leq e_{i}e_{j}}e_{k}
for any
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. The independence of
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n}\}
is equivalent to each
e^{\prime}_{i}
being non-zero, essentially by definition. Note also that for each
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, we have
e^{\prime}_{i}\leq e_{i}
(again, by definition). Fix distinct
i,j\in\{1,\dots,n\}
. Independence implies that
e_{i}\neq e_{j}
. If
e_{j}<e_{i}
, then by definition of
e^{\prime}_{i}
, we get that
e^{\prime}_{i}e_{j}=0
, which implies that
e^{\prime}_{i}e^{\prime}_{j}=e^{\prime}_{i}e_{j}e^{\prime}_{j}=0
. If
e_{j}
is not less than
e_{i}
, then we must have
e_{i}e_{j}<e_{j}
. In this case,

e_{i}e_{j}=\bigvee_{e_{k}\leq e_{i}e_{j}}e_{k}\leq\bigvee_{e_{k}<e_{j}}e_{k},

and multiplying through by
e^{\prime}_{j}
gives that
e_{i}e^{\prime}_{j}<0
, and hence
e_{i}e^{\prime}_{j}=0
. This in turns implies that
e^{\prime}_{i}e^{\prime}_{j}=0
. Thus,
e^{\prime}_{1},\dots,e^{\prime}_{n}
are pairwise orthogonal. To see that
e^{\prime}_{1},\dots,e^{\prime}_{n}
linearly span
D
, it suffices to show that
\dim D\leq n
. For this, note that
e_{i}e_{j}
is either
e_{i}
or
e_{j}
, or is the join
\bigvee_{e_{k}<e_{i},e_{j}}e_{k}
, which is a linear combination of products of the projections
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
. If we keep writing products as single projections or joins, then since there are only finitely many
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
, we eventually get
e_{i}e_{j}
expressed as a linear combination of these projections. An inductive argument gives that any finite product of the
e_{i}
’s can be written as a linear combination of the
e_{i}
’s, and it follows that
\dim D\leq n
.

Now suppose that
\{e^{\prime}_{1},\dots,e^{\prime}_{n}\}
is a family of non-zero, pairwise orthogonal projections linearly spanning
D
. Then each
e_{i}e_{j}
, being a projection in
D
, can be expressed as a sum

e_{i}e_{j}=\sum_{e^{\prime}_{a}\leq e_{i}e_{j}}e^{\prime}_{a},

and so Lemma
3.7
implies that

e_{i}e_{j}=\bigvee_{e^{\prime}_{a}\leq e_{i}e_{j}}e_{a},

as required.

For (2), we just note that Lemma
3.7
gives
\gamma_{ij}=1\iff e_{i}\leq e_{j}
, and then the arguments in the proof of
[
11
, Lemma 3.8]
give the result.
∎

3.3.

K
-theory

We now state and prove Theorem

, which is a generalisation of
[

.

Notation 3.8
.

In the below statement, we denote by
\xi_{i}
the standard basis element in

the rank-one partial isometry in

the canonical unitary representation of a group into any crossed product induced from an action of the group.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
G
be a second countable locally compact group acting continuously on a second countable totally disconnected locally compact space

, and denote by
\tau
the induced action of
G
on

. Let

be a separable

-algebra, and

\mathcal{V}

)

5
.

We start with a discussion of multitrees in Section
4.1
, and then in Section
4.2
we explain how Theorem
3.9
can be applied to this setting. Following this, we give formulae for the
K
-theory of the crossed product in the special cases where the group acts freely on the multitree (Section
4.3
) and where the vertex stabilisers of the action are infinite cyclic (Section
4.4
).

4.1.
Multitrees

In this subsection, we give a definition of multitrees, and prove some useful results about their structure.

Definition 4.1
.

A
multitree
is a directed graph
E=(E^{0},E^{1},r,s)
such that for any two vertices
v,w\in E^{0}
, there is at most one (directed) path from
w
to
v
(or equivalently, at most one directed path between
v
and
w
). This condition ensures that there is a partial order on the vertex set
E^{0}
defined by
v\leq w
if and only if there is a directed path from
w
to
v
. We call a multitree
E

finitely aligned
if for any pair of vertices
v,w\in E^{0}
, the collection
v\vee w
of minimal common upper bounds of
v
and
w
, under the partial order described above, is finite.

Multitrees have also been called
strongly unambiguous graphs
or
mangroves
in
[
1
]
.

Example 4.2

(Example of a multitree)
.

In this paper, we restrict our attention to multitrees
E
which are row-finite, finitely aligned and have no sources. We can define the boundary
\partial E
of a multitree
E
in the same way as for directed trees (as described in Section
2.3
), and it will also be a totally disconnected locally compact Hausdorff space. Given an action of a countable discrete group
G
on
E
, we wish to apply Theorem
3.9
to calculate the
K
-theory of the reduced crossed product induced from the action of
G
on the boundary
\partial E
. However, this boundary will not have a suitable collection of compact open subsets, so in order to apply the theorem, we will need to enlarge the space with the vertices
E^{0}
.

Definition 4.3
.

Suppose that
E
is a row-finite, finitely aligned multitree without sources, and consider the space
E^{0}\cup\partial E
. We define the cylinder set of a vertex
v\in E^{0}
to be
Z(v):=\{w\in E^{0}:vE^{*}w\neq\emptyset\}\cup Z_{\partial}(v)
. The collection
\{\{v\}:v\in E^{0}\}\cup\{Z(v):v\in E^{0}\}
forms a basis for a topology of compact open sets turning
E^{0}\cup\partial E
into a totally disconnected locally compact Hausdorff space.

The following proposition will be useful for Section
4.2
.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
E
be a row-finite, finitely aligned multitree with no sources. For any two vertices
v,w\in E^{0}
, we have that

Z(v)\cap Z(w)=\bigsqcup_{u\in v\vee w}Z(u).

We will use the following lemma in the proof of Proposition
4.4
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
E
be a row-finite, finitely aligned multitree with no sources, and let
v,w\in E^{0}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

v\leq w
;

(2)

Z(w)\subseteq Z(v)
;

(3)

w\in Z(v)
.

Proof.

We show that (i)
\implies
(ii)
\implies
(iii)
\implies
(i).

First suppose (i), so
v\leq w
. This means that there is a path
\lambda\in vE^{*}w
. Now take
x\in Z(w)
. We treat the two cases
x\in E^{0}
and
x\in\partial E
separately. If
x\in E^{0}
, then by definition of
Z(w)
, there is a path
\mu\in wE^{*}x
. So
\lambda\mu\in vE^{*}x
, which implies that
x\in Z(v)
. If
x\in\partial E
, then
x=[\nu]
for some
\nu\in wE^{\infty}
. So
\lambda\nu\in vE^{\infty}
, and so
[\lambda\nu]=[\nu]=x
is again in
Z(v)
. This shows that
Z(w)\subseteq Z(v)
, so (ii) holds.

The implication (ii)
\implies
(iii) follows immediately from the fact that
w\in Z(w)
. Finally, suppose (iii), so
w\in Z(v)
. Since
w\in E^{0}
, this means that there is a path
\lambda\in vE^{*}w
, which exactly means that
v\leq w
, which is (i).
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

Fix
v,w\in E^{0}
. First we show that

(4.1)

Z(v)\cap Z(w)=\bigcup_{u\in v\vee w}Z(u).

For the backwards inclusion, fix a minimal common upper bound
u
of
v
and
w
. Since both
v\leq u
and
w\leq u
, Lemma
4.5
implies that
Z(u)\subseteq Z(v)\cap Z(w)
. So the right-hand side of Equation (
4.1
) is also contained in
Z(v)\cap Z(w)
.

For the forwards inclusion, take
x\in Z(v)\cap Z(w)
. We treat the two cases
x\in E^{0}
and
x\in\partial E
separately. First suppose that
x\in E^{0}
. Then by Lemma
4.5
, we have that
v\leq x
and
w\leq x
. So
x
is a common upper bound of
v
and
w
, which means that there must be some minimal common upper bound
u
of
v
and
w
such that
u\leq x
. Then Lemma
4.5
gives that
x\in Z(u)
. Now suppose that
x=[\lambda]\in\partial E
, for some
\lambda\in E^{\infty}
. This means that there are infinite paths
\lambda_{v}\in vE^{\infty}
and
\lambda_{w}\in wE^{\infty}
such that
\lambda
,
\lambda_{v}
and
\lambda_{w}
are shift-tail equivalent to each other. So there is an infinite path
\mu\in E^{\infty}
and finite paths
\nu_{v}\in vE^{*}r(\mu)
and
\nu_{w}\in wE^{*}r(\mu)
such that
\lambda_{v}=\nu_{v}\mu
and
\lambda_{w}=\nu_{w}\mu
. Then the vertex
r(\mu)
is a common upper bound of
v
and
w
, and by the same argument as in the first case, there is some minimal common upper bound
u
of
v
and
w
such that
u\leq r(\mu)
. Let
\nu_{u}
be the path from
r(\mu)
to
u
. Then
\nu_{u}\mu\in uE^{\infty}
is shift-tail equivalent to
\lambda
and so
x=[\lambda]=[\nu_{u}\mu]
lies in
Z(u)
. This proves the forwards inclusion and hence we have shown Equation (
4.1
).

It remains to prove that the union
\cup_{u}Z(u)
is disjoint. Suppose for a contradiction that there are two distinct minimal common upper bounds
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
of
v
and
w
such that
Z(u_{1})\cap Z(u_{2})\neq\emptyset
. Equation (
4.1
), with
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
in place of
v
and
w
respectively, implies that
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
have a (minimal) common upper bound
u^{\prime}\in E^{0}
. So there are paths
\lambda_{1}\in u_{1}E^{*}u^{\prime}
and
\lambda_{2}\in u_{2}E^{*}u^{\prime}
. Also, since
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are both upper bounds of
v
, there are paths
\mu_{1}\in vE^{*}u_{1}
and
\mu_{2}\in vE^{*}u_{2}
. So then we have two paths
\mu_{1}\lambda_{1}
and
\mu_{2}\lambda_{2}
which both go from
u^{\prime}
to
v
. Moreover, the paths
\mu_{1}\lambda_{1}
and
\mu_{2}\lambda_{2}
must be distinct, since otherwise
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
would lie on the same directed path and would therefore be comparable, contradicting the fact that
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
are distinct minimal common upper bounds of
u
. But since there are two distinct paths from
u^{\prime}
to
v
, this contradicts the fact that
E
is a multitree, and so the intersection
Z(u_{1})\cap Z(u_{2})
must be empty. This shows that the union
\cup_{u}Z(u)
is disjoint.
∎

4.2.
K
-theory

Suppose a group
G
acts on a row-finite, finitely aligned multitree
E
with no sources. Our main result of this section is Theorem
4.8
, which describes the
K
-theory of the reduced crossed product of
G
acting on the boundary of
E
. Before we can state the theorem, we introduce some notation.

Notation 4.6
.

In this subsection, we will always write
\Omega
for the topological space
E^{0}\cup\partial E
from Definition
4.3
. We will write
\tau
for the induced action of
G
on
C_{0}(\Omega)
, and we will write
\tau_{0}
and
\tau_{\partial}
for the restriction of
\tau
to
C_{0}(E^{0})
and
C_{0}(\partial E)
respectively. For each
v\in E^{0}
, we write
\xi_{v}\in C_{0}(E^{0})
for the indicator function of the singleton set
\{v\}
, and we write
1_{Z(v)}\in C_{0}(\Omega)
for the indicator function of the set
Z(v)\subseteq\Omega
.

For each
e\in E^{1}
, we will fix some transversal
\Sigma_{e}
for
G_{r(e)}/G_{e}
containing the identity element
1_{G_{r(e)}}
(which is just
1_{G}
). Note that
\Sigma_{e}
has a natural left action of
G_{r(e)}
defined by setting
g\cdot h
to be the representative in
\Sigma_{e}
of the coset
ghG_{e}
, for
g\in G_{r(e)}
,
h\in\Sigma_{e}
. For
h,k\in\Sigma_{e}
, we denote by
e_{h,k}\in\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(\Sigma_{e}))
the rank-one partial isometry sending the standard basis element
\xi_{k}\in\ell^{2}(\Sigma_{e})
at

to the standard basis element
\xi_{h}
at
h
. We denote by
u
the canonical unitary representation of a group into its reduced group
C^{*}
-algebra. Finally, we write
\theta_{e}
for the map defined by

\displaystyle\theta_{e}\colon C^{*}_{r}(G_{r(e)})

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(\Sigma_{e}))\otimes C^{*}_{r}(G_{s(e)})

\displaystyle u_{g}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{h\in\Sigma_{e}}e_{h,g^{-1}\cdot h}\otimes u_{h^{-1}g(g^{-1}\cdot h)}.

Note that for any
g\in G_{r(e)}
and

, the elements
g^{-1}h
and
g^{-1}\cdot h
are in the same coset in
G_{r(e)}/G_{e}

h^{-1}g(g^{-1}\cdot h)
is in
G_{e}
and therefore also in
G_{s(e)}
. Also, recall that the row-finiteness of

implies that

is finite, so the sum in the above formula for

is well-defined.

We are primarily interested in the induced map
(\theta_{e})_{*}
on
K
-theory; the following lemma shows that this map is (essentially) independent of the choice of
e\in E^{1}
in a fixed edge orbit. Note that the lemma also implies that
(\theta_{e})_{*}
is independent of the choice of transversal
\Sigma_{e}
, by taking
e^{\prime}=e
and
g
to be the identity element of
G
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
G
be a countable discrete group acting on a row-finite, finitely aligned multitree
E
with no sources. Let
e,e^{\prime}\in E^{1}
be edges in the same orbit, and let
g\in G
be an element such that
e^{\prime}=g\cdot e
. Write
\Ad u_{g}\colon C^{*}_{r}(G_{r(e)})\to C^{*}_{r}(G_{r(e^{\prime})})
for the *-homomorphism defined by
u_{h}\mapsto u_{ghg^{-1}}
for
h\in G_{r(e)}
. We have the following commutative diagram on
K
-theory

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{*}(C^{*}_{r}(G_{r(e)}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{*}(C^{*}_{r}(G_{s(e)}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{*}(C^{*}_{r}(G_{r(e^{\prime})}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{*}(C^{*}_{r}(G_{s(e^{\prime})})),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\theta_{e})_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Ad u_{g})_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\Ad u_{g})_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\theta_{e^{\prime}})_{*}}

where the downwards arrows are isomorphisms.

Proof.

The claim about the isomorphisms follows from the fact that
G_{r(e^{\prime})}=gG_{r(e)}g^{-1}
and similarly
G_{s(e^{\prime})}=gG_{s(e)}g^{-1}
. Note also that
G_{e^{\prime}}=gG_{e}g^{-1}
. For each
h\in\Sigma_{e}
, write
h^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{e^{\prime}}
for the representative in
\Sigma_{e^{\prime}}
of the coset
ghg^{-1}G_{e^{\prime}}
, and write
h^{\prime\prime}\in G_{s(e^{\prime})}
for the element
(h^{\prime})^{-1}ghg^{-1}
(note that
h^{\prime\prime}
is in
G_{e^{\prime}}
and therefore
G_{s(e^{\prime})}
because both
h^{\prime}
and
ghg^{-1}
are in the same coset in
G_{r(e^{\prime})}/G_{e^{\prime}}
).

First we claim that for any
k\in G_{r(e)}
and any
h\in\Sigma_{e}
, we have that
(gkg^{-1})^{-1}\cdot h^{\prime}=(k^{-1}\cdot h)^{\prime}
. To see this, first note that
(gkg^{-1})^{-1}\cdot h^{\prime}
is the representative in
\Sigma_{e^{\prime}}
of the coset

gk^{-1}g^{-1}h^{\prime}G_{e^{\prime}}=gk^{-1}g^{-1}ghg^{-1}G_{e^{\prime}}=gk^{-1}hg^{-1}G_{e^{\prime}}.

On the other hand,
(k^{-1}\cdot h)^{\prime}
is the representative in
\Sigma_{e^{\prime}}
of the coset

g(k^{-1}\cdot h)g^{-1}G_{e^{\prime}}=g(k^{-1}\cdot h)G_{e}g^{-1}=gk^{-1}hG_{e}g^{-1}=gk^{-1}hg^{-1}G_{e^{\prime}}.

So
(gkg^{-1})^{-1}\cdot h^{\prime}
and
(k^{-1}\cdot h)^{\prime}
are both representatives in
\Sigma_{e^{\prime}}
of the same coset, and hence they must be equal, as claimed.

Now note that for
h\in\Sigma_{e}
, we have
h^{\prime}=ghg^{-1}(h^{\prime\prime})^{-1}
. So for any
k\in G_{r(e)}
, we have

\displaystyle(h^{\prime})^{-1}(gkg^{-1})((gkg^{-1})^{-1}\cdot h^{\prime})

\displaystyle=(h^{\prime})^{-1}(gkg^{-1})(k^{-1}\cdot h)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(h^{\prime\prime}gh^{-1}g^{-1})(gkg^{-1})[g(k^{-1}\cdot h)g^{-1}((k^{-1}\cdot h)^{\prime\prime})^{-1}]

\displaystyle=h^{\prime\prime}gh^{-1}k(k^{-1}\cdot h)g^{-1}[(k^{-1}\cdot h)^{\prime\prime}]^{-1}.

So, writing
\varphi\colon\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(\Sigma_{e}))\to\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(\Sigma_{e^{\prime}}))
for the isomorphism induced by the map

, and writing
u\in\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(\Sigma_{e^{\prime}}))\otimes C^{*}_{r}(G_{s(e^{\prime})})
for the unitary defined by

u=\sum_{h\in\Sigma_{e}}e_{h^{\prime},h^{\prime}}\otimes u_{h^{\prime\prime}},

we have

\displaystyle(\theta_{e^{\prime}}\circ\Ad u_{g})(u_{k})

\displaystyle=\theta_{e^{\prime}}(u_{gkg^{-1}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{h^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{e^{\prime}}}e_{h^{\prime},(gkg^{-1})^{-1}\cdot h^{\prime}}\otimes u_{(h^{\prime})^{-1}(gkg^{-1})((gkg^{-1})^{-1}\cdot h^{\prime})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{h\in\Sigma_{e}}e_{h^{\prime},(k^{-1}\cdot h)^{\prime}}\otimes u_{h^{\prime\prime}gh^{-1}k(k^{-1}\cdot h)g^{-1}[(k^{-1}\cdot h)^{\prime\prime}]^{-1}}

\displaystyle=u\left(\sum_{h\in\Sigma_{e}}e_{h^{\prime},(k^{-1}\cdot h)^{\prime}}\otimes u_{g(h^{-1}k(k^{-1}\cdot h))g^{-1}}\right)u^{*}

\displaystyle=(\Ad u)((\varphi\otimes\Ad u_{g})(\theta_{e}(u_{k}))).

Since
\theta_{e^{\prime}}\circ\Ad u_{g}
and
\Ad u\circ(\varphi\otimes\Ad u_{g})\circ\theta_{e}
agree on a generating set of a dense subspace of

, they must coincide. This means that their induced maps on
K
-theory must also be the same, and noting that
\Ad u
induces the identity map in
K
-theory as it is an inner automorphism, we get the commutative diagram in the statement of the lemma.
∎

to freely identify the groups

in the same vertex orbit, as well as to identify the maps
(\theta_{e})_{*}
for edges

(\theta_{e})_{*}
as a map
\oplus_{[v]}K_{*}(C^{*}_{r}(G_{v}))\to\oplus_{[w]}K_{*}(C^{*}_{r}(G_{w}))
by defining

to be the zero map on all summands of the domain apart from the

.

Remark 4.9
.

We note that the action
G\curvearrowright\Omega
is amenable if and only if
G_{v}
is amenable for all
v\in E^{0}
and the action
G\curvearrowright\partial E
is amenable. This is because:

•

G\curvearrowright\Omega
is amenable if and only if both induced actions
G\curvearrowright E^{0}
and
G\curvearrowright\partial E
are amenable, by Proposition 3.23 and Theorem 5.15 of
[
9
]
taken together; and

•

G\curvearrowright E^{0}
is amenable if and only if
G_{v}
is amenable for all
v\in E^{0}
: per
[
3
, Remarque 4.10]
, the amenability of
G\curvearrowright E^{0}
is equivalent to the nuclearity of the crossed product
C_{0}(E^{0})\rtimes G\cong\bigoplus_{[v]\in G\backslash E^{0}}\mathcal{K}(\ell^{2}(G\cdot v))\otimes C^{*}(G_{v})
, and this is nuclear if and only if
G_{v}
is amenable for all

.

The six-term exact sequence in the theorem is essentially the one coming from the short exact sequence of

0\to C_{0}(E^{0})\rtimes_{\tau_{0},r}G\xrightarrow{\iota}C_{0}(\Omega)\rtimes_{\tau,r}G\to C_{0}(\partial E)\rtimes_{\tau_{\partial},r}G\to 0,

is the canonical inclusion. The following two propositions together yield the sequence in the theorem.

Proposition 4.10
.

G
be a countable discrete group acting on a row-finite, finitely aligned multitree

\displaystyle l_{v}\colon C^{*}_{r}(G_{v})

\displaystyle\to C_{0}(E^{0})\rtimes_{\tau_{0},r}G

\displaystyle l_{Z(v)}\colon C^{*}_{r}(G_{v})

\displaystyle\to C_{0}(\Omega)\rtimes_{\tau,r}G

The claims about the first isomorphism follow from
[

, Remarks 3.13 and 3.15]
.

For the second isomorphism, we claim that the collection of compact open sets

satisfies the conditions of Theorem

. The collection
\mathcal{V}
is

for all

. To see that

, it is enough to show that every basis set of

, we have

and

In order to apply Theorem

, we also need the group

to satisfy the Baum–Connes conjecture with coefficients in

. By

that each vertex stabiliser is amenable, and the amenability of

are satisfied, and the claims about the second isomorphism follow directly from that theorem.

\theta_{e}(u^{(r(e))}_{1})=e_{0,\lvert\omega_{e}\rvert-1}\otimes u^{(s(e))}_{\sgn(\omega_{e})\omega_{\overline{e}}}+\sum_{h=1}^{\lvert\omega_{e}\rvert-1}e_{h,h-1}\otimes u^{(s(e))}_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}0&u^{(s(e))}_{\sgn(\omega_{e})\omega_{\overline{e}}}\\
\diag(u^{(s(e))}_{0},\dots,u^{(s(e))}_{0})&0\end{pmatrix}

in block matrix form. But this matrix is homotopy equivalent to the diagonal matrix
\diag(u^{(s(e))}_{\sgn(\omega_{e})\omega_{\overline{e}}},u^{(s(e))}_{0},\dots,u^{(s(e))}_{0})
.

To summarise, we have

\theta_{e}(u_{0})=\diag(u_{0},\dots,u_{0})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\theta_{e}(u_{1})\sim_{h}\diag(u_{\sgn(\omega_{e})\omega_{\overline{e}}},u_{0},\dots,u_{0}),

so the induced maps on
K
-theory are given by

\displaystyle(\theta_{e})_{*,0}\colon\mathbb{Z}[u_{0}]_{0}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{Z}[u_{0}]_{0}

\displaystyle(\theta_{e})_{*,1}\colon\mathbb{Z}[u_{1}]_{1}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{Z}[u_{1}]_{1}

\displaystyle[u_{0}]_{0}

\displaystyle\mapsto\lvert\omega_{e}\rvert[u_{0}]_{0},

\displaystyle[u_{1}]_{1}

\displaystyle\mapsto[u_{\sgn(\omega_{e})\omega_{\overline{e}}}]_{1}=\sgn(\omega_{e})\omega_{\overline{e}}[u_{1}]_{1},

and hence
(\theta_{e})_{*,0}
is multiplication by
\lvert\omega_{e}\rvert
, and
(\theta_{e})_{*,1}
is multiplication by
\sgn(\omega_{e})\omega_{\overline{e}}
. The theorem then follows from the commutativity of the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{[v]\in G\backslash E^{0}}K_{i}(C^{*}(G_{v}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{[v]\in G\backslash E^{0}}K_{i}(C^{*}(G_{v}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma^{0}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}[\Gamma^{0}]}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A_{i}}

for
i=0,1
.
∎

5.
Group actions on undirected trees

We can also use Theorem
4.8
to study crossed products coming from actions on the boundaries of
undirected
trees. Such actions arise naturally for example in the study of graph-of-groups
C^{*}
-algebras (see
[
8
]
, in particular Theorem 4.1).

In Section
5.1
we discuss the notion of a dual graph, and how it brings group actions on undirected trees into our setting of group actions on multitrees. Then, in Section
5.2
, we explain how Theorem

can be applied to yield a six-term exact sequence for the
K
-theory of crossed products induced by a group action on an undirected tree.

5.1.
Dual graphs

In this subsection, we explain how we can use a dual graph construction to associate to any undirected tree a suitable multitree. We mirror the construction of the dual graph for directed graphs (see, for example,
[
6
, Page 313]
), except that we only consider
reduced
paths.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be a undirected graph. The
dual graph
of
\Gamma
is the directed graph
\widehat{\Gamma}=(\widehat{\Gamma}^{0},\widehat{\Gamma}^{1},\widehat{r},\widehat{s})
defined by

\widehat{\Gamma}^{0}=\Gamma^{1},\quad\widehat{\Gamma}^{1}=\Gamma^{2}_{r},\quad\widehat{r}(ef)=e,\quad\widehat{s}(ef)=f,\quad ef\in\Gamma^{2}_{r}.

Example 5.2
.

Below is an example of an undirected tree (on the left), along with its dual multitree (on the right).

\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
\cdots
e
\overline{e}

e
\overline{e}

\cdots

\cdots

\cdots

\cdots

Note that there is a natural identification of paths (finite or infinite) in
\widehat{\Gamma}
with reduced paths (finite or infinite) of length at least one in
\Gamma
, given by viewing vertices in
\widehat{\Gamma}
as edges in
\Gamma
. We will use this identification in the proof of the following proposition, which explains why this dual graph construction is useful.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
T
be a locally finite nonsingular undirected tree. Its dual graph
\widehat{T}
is a row-finite, finitely aligned multitree with no sources, and
\partial T\cong\partial\widehat{T}
.

Suppose further that a discrete group
G
acts on
T
. Then
G
acts naturally on
\partial\widehat{T}
and
G\curvearrowright\partial T\cong G\curvearrowright\partial\widehat{T}
.

Proof.

First we show that
\widehat{T}
is a multitree. Take two vertices
e,f\in\widehat{T}^{0}
, and suppose for a contradiction that there are two distinct paths
\lambda,\mu\in e\widehat{T}^{*}f
. This implies that there are two distinct reduced paths in
T
from
r(f)
to
s(e)
, contradicting that
T
is a tree. Hence there is at most one directed path between any two vertices in
\widehat{T}^{0}
, so
\widehat{T}
is a multitree.

To see that
\widehat{T}
is row-finite and has no sources, note that for any vertex
e\in\widehat{T}^{0}
, the set
\widehat{r}^{-1}(e)\subseteq\widehat{T}^{1}
is in a one-to-one correspondence with the set
s(e)T^{1}\setminus\{\overline{e}\}
. In particular, we have that
\lvert\widehat{r}^{-1}(e)\rvert=\lvert s(e)T^{1}\rvert-1
. So the assumption that
T
is locally finite and nonsingular implies that
\widehat{T}
is row-finite and has no sources.

To see that
\widehat{T}
is finitely aligned, fix
e,f\in\widehat{T}^{0}
. We need to show that the set of minimal common upper bounds for
e
and
f
is finite. If
e\leq f
(respectively
f\leq e
) then the only minimal common upper bound for
e
and
f
is
f
(respectively
e
), so we are done. So suppose that
e
and
f
are not comparable but have common upper bounds. Take some common upper bound
x
of
e
and
f
, so there are paths
\lambda\in e\widehat{T}^{*}x
and
\mu\in f\widehat{T}^{*}x
, which we will view as reduced paths in
T
. Write
\nu
for the longest path such that
\lambda=\alpha\nu
and
\mu=\beta\nu
for some paths
\alpha
and
\beta
. Then, writing
\overline{\beta}
for the reversal of the path
\beta
, we have that
\alpha\overline{\beta}
is the (unique) reduced path in
T
from
r(f)
to
r(e)
, and note that
r(\nu)=s(\alpha)
lies on this path. Write
y
for the range-most edge in
\nu
. Then
y
is a common upper bound of
e
and
f
, and we have
y\leq x
. This shows that any common upper bound of
e
and
f
lies above some common upper bound
y
with
r(y)\in T^{0}
lying on the reduced path in
T
from
r(f)
to
r(e)
. But this path is finite, and since
T
is locally finite, there are only finitely many such
y
’s. Hence
e
and
f
have only finitely many minimal common upper bounds.

We now show that
\partial T\cong\partial\widehat{T}
. First notice that the sets
T^{\infty}_{r}
and
\widehat{T}^{\infty}
are isomorphic under the natural identification. This isomorphism respects shift-tail equivalence, and so the boundaries
\partial T
and
\partial\widehat{T}
are isomorphic as sets. Moreover, for any edge
e\in T^{1}
, the cylinder set
Z_{\partial}(e)\subseteq\partial T
is sent by the isomorphism to the cylinder set
Z_{\partial}(e)\subseteq\partial\widehat{T}
, where
e
is considered as a vertex of
\widehat{T}
. Thus
\partial T\cong\partial\widehat{T}
as topological spaces.

Finally, suppose that a discrete group
G
acts on
T
. This induces an action of
G
on
\widehat{T}
in the natural way, which in turn induces an action of
G
on
\partial\widehat{T}
, and the isomorphism
\partial T\cong\partial\widehat{T}
described above intertwines the actions of
G
on
\partial T
and
\partial\widehat{T}
, which implies that
G\curvearrowright\partial T\cong G\curvearrowright\partial\widehat{T}
, as required.
∎

5.2.
Group actions on boundaries of undirected trees

In this subsection we apply Theorem
4.8
to arrive at the following result for groups acting on the boundaries of
undirected

G_{ef}
for the stabiliser subgroup of

at

, and the map

, where

Theorem 5.4
.

Let
G
be a countable discrete group acting on a locally finite nonsingular undirected tree

. Suppose that
G_{v}
is amenable for all

. Then for
\widehat{\alpha}_{i}=\sum_{[ef]\in G\backslash T^{2}_{r}}(\theta_{ef})_{*,i}
we have the following six-term exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{[e]\in G\backslash T^{1}}K_{0}(C^{*}_{r}(G_{e}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{[ef]\in G\backslash T^{2}_{r}}K_{0}(C^{*}_{r}(G_{ef}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(C_{0}(\partial T)\rtimes_{\tau_{\partial},r}G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{1}(C_{0}(\partial T)\rtimes_{\tau_{\partial},r}G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{[ef]\in G\backslash T^{2}_{r}}K_{1}(C^{*}_{r}(G_{ef}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{[e]\in G\backslash T^{1}}K_{1}(C^{*}_{r}(G_{e})).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\id-\widehat{\alpha}_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\id-\widehat{\alpha}_{1}}

Proof.

By Proposition
5.3
, we have that the dual graph of
T
is a row-finite, finitely aligned multitree with no sources. The vertex stabilisers of
\widehat{T}
are the edge stabilisers of

4.9
allows us to apply Theorem
4.8
for the dual graph to get a six-term exact sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{[e]\in G\backslash\widehat{T}^{0}}K_{0}(C^{*}_{r}(G_{e}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{[ef]\in G\backslash\widehat{T}^{1}}K_{0}(C^{*}_{r}(G_{ef}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{0}(C_{0}(\partial\widehat{T})\rtimes_{\tau_{\partial},r}G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K_{1}(C_{0}(\partial\widehat{T})\rtimes_{\tau_{\partial},r}G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{[ef]\in G\backslash\widehat{T}^{1}}K_{1}(C^{*}_{r}(G_{ef}))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\bigoplus_{[e]\in G\backslash\widehat{T}^{0}}K_{1}(C^{*}_{r}(G_{e})),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\id-\widehat{\alpha}_{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\id-\widehat{\alpha}_{1}}

where

. But
\widehat{T}^{0}=T^{1}
and

C_{0}(\partial\widehat{T})\rtimes_{\tau_{\partial},r}G\cong C_{0}(\partial T)\rtimes_{\tau_{\partial},r}G

Remark 5.5
.

The assumption in Theorem

that all vertex stabilisers are amenable means that the reduced group

-algebras and crossed products are naturally isomorphic to their respective

are described in terms of the

-correspondences rather than in terms of induced homomorphisms. It is not immediately clear to the authors how these pictures correspond to each other. However, we note that in the special cases where all vertex stabilisers are trivial or where all vertex stabilisers are infinite cyclic, the formulas for

reduce to the formulas for

in
[

Remark 5.6
.

In

, the authors associate to each locally finite nonsingular graph of discrete groups

6.
Properties of the action
G\curvearrowright\partial E

In this section we examine dynamical properties for the action of a discrete group
G
on the boundary
\partial E
of a row-finite, finitely aligned multitree
E
with no sources, induced from an action of
G
on
E
. We investigate minimality in Section
6.1
, local contractivity in Section
6.2
, topological freeness in Section
6.3
, and finally amenability in Section
6.4
. Note that it follows from Proposition
5.3
that the results in this section generalise the results in
[
8
, Section 5]
, which deals only with group actions on undirected trees. We will use the same notation as in Notation
4.18
.

6.1.
Minimality

Recall that an action of a discrete group
G
on a locally compact Hausdorff space
X
is called
minimal
if every orbit of points of
X
is dense in
X
. We have the following characterisation in our context.

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose that a discrete group
G
acts on a row-finite, finitely aligned multitree
E
with no sources. The induced action
G\curvearrowright\partial E
is minimal if and only if the graph
\Gamma
is cofinal, that is, for every infinite path
\lambda\in\Gamma^{\infty}
and every vertex
v\in\Gamma^{0}
, there is some vertex
w
on
\lambda
so that there is a path in
\Gamma
from
w
to
v
.

Proof.

First suppose that the action
G\curvearrowright\partial E
is minimal. Fix an infinite path
\lambda
in
\Gamma
and a vertex
v\in\Gamma^{0}
. Then
\lambda
and
v
lift to some infinite path
\lambda^{\prime}\in E^{\infty}
and some vertex
v^{\prime}\in E^{0}
respectively. By minimality, there is
g\in G
so that
g\cdot[\lambda^{\prime}]=[g\cdot\lambda^{\prime}]\in Z_{\partial}(v^{\prime})
. This means there is an infinite path
\mu^{\prime}\in v^{\prime}E^{\infty}
so that
\mu^{\prime}
and
g\cdot\lambda^{\prime}
agree after some vertex, say
w^{\prime}\in E^{0}
. So there is a path from
w^{\prime}
(which is on
g\cdot\lambda^{\prime}
) to
v^{\prime}
, which descends to a path from
w\in\Gamma^{0}
to
v
. But since
w^{\prime}
is in the same orbit as
g^{-1}\cdot w^{\prime}
, which is on
\lambda^{\prime}
, we get that
w
is on
\lambda
, so there is a path from some
w
on
\lambda
to
v
.

Now suppose that
\Gamma
is cofinal. Fix
\lambda^{\prime}\in E^{\infty}
and
v^{\prime}\in E^{0}
arbitrary. It is enough to prove that there is
g\in G
such that
g\cdot[\lambda^{\prime}]=[g\cdot\lambda^{\prime}]\in Z_{\partial}(v^{\prime})
. Write
\lambda
and
v
for the images of
\lambda^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}
respectively under the quotient map. Then
\lambda
is an infinite path in
\Gamma
and
v\in\Gamma^{0}
is a vertex, so there is some vertex
w
on
\lambda
such that there is a path in
\Gamma
from
w
to
v
. Now, there is a lift
w^{\prime}\in E^{0}
of
w
such that
w^{\prime}
is on
\lambda^{\prime}
. Also, the path from
w
to
v
lifts to a path from some vertex
w^{\prime\prime}\in E^{0}
to
v^{\prime}
, and
w^{\prime\prime}
is in the same vertex orbit as
w^{\prime}
. Let
g\in G
be an element such that
g\cdot w^{\prime}=w^{\prime\prime}
. Then
w^{\prime\prime}
will be on
g\cdot\lambda^{\prime}
, so
g\cdot\lambda^{\prime}
is shift-tail equivalent to an infinite path with range
v^{\prime}
. Hence
g\cdot[\lambda^{\prime}]\in Z_{\partial}(v^{\prime})
.
∎

6.2.
Local contractivity

Recall that an action of a discrete group
G
on a locally compact Hausdorff space
X
is called
locally contractive
if for every non-empty open set
U\subseteq X
there is a non-empty open set
V\subseteq U
and an element
g\in G
such that
g\overline{V}\subsetneq V
. Like in
[
8
]
, we give a sufficient condition for the action
G\curvearrowright\partial E
to be locally contractive.

First recall that a loop
\eta=\eta_{1}\cdots\eta_{m}
in a directed graph
\Gamma
has an
entrance at vertex

x\in\Gamma^{0}
if there exists
\eta_{i}
such that
r(\eta_{i})=x
and
|r^{-1}(x)|\geq 2
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Suppose that a discrete group
G
acts on a row-finite, finitely aligned multitree
E
with no sources. Suppose that for every vertex
v\in\Gamma^{0}
there is a loop
\eta=\eta_{1}\dots\eta_{m}
in
\Gamma
such that

(i)

\eta
has an entrance, or
[G_{r(\eta_{i})}:G_{\eta_{i}}]\geq 2
for some
1\leq i\leq m
; and

(ii)

there is a path from
r(\eta)
to
v
.

Then the induced action
G\curvearrowright\partial E
is locally contractive.

Proof.

Let
U\subseteq\partial E
be a non-empty open subset, and let
v^{\prime}\in E^{0}
be such that
Z_{\partial}(v^{\prime})\subseteq U
. Let
v\in\Gamma^{0}
be the image of
v^{\prime}
under the quotient map. Let
\eta
be as in the assumption. The path from
r(\eta)
to
v
lifts to a path from some vertex
w^{\prime}\in E^{0}
to
v^{\prime}
, and so
Z_{\partial}(w^{\prime})\subseteq Z_{\partial}(v^{\prime})\subseteq U
by Lemma
4.5
. Now
\eta
lifts to some path
\eta^{\prime}
from some vertex
w^{\prime\prime}\in E^{0}
to
w^{\prime}
, and
w^{\prime\prime}
is in the vertex orbit of
w^{\prime}
. So
w^{\prime\prime}=g\cdot w^{\prime}
for some
g\in G
, and
g\cdot Z_{\partial}(w^{\prime})=Z_{\partial}(g\cdot w^{\prime})\subseteq Z_{\partial}(w^{\prime})
by Lemma
4.5
. Note that
Z_{\partial}(w^{\prime})
is closed, so all that is left to show is that the containment
Z(g\cdot w^{\prime})\subseteq Z(w^{\prime})
is proper.

By assumption, there is
x\in\Gamma^{0}
such that
\eta
either has an entrance at
x
, or that there is
\eta_{i}
with
r(\eta_{i})=x
and
[G_{x}:G_{\eta_{i}}]\geq 2
. Write
x^{\prime}\in E^{0}
for a lift of
x
which lies on
\eta^{\prime}
. Note that by the orbit-stabiliser theorem,
\sum_{e\in x\Gamma^{1}}[G_{x}:G_{e}]
is equal to the number of edges in
E^{1}
with range
x^{\prime}
; and the assumption ensures that this number is at least
2
. In particular, if
e^{\prime}
is the edge on
\eta^{\prime}
with range
x^{\prime}
, then there is a distinct edge
e^{\prime\prime}\in x^{\prime}E^{1}
. Note that
w^{\prime}\leq s(e^{\prime\prime})
, so by Lemma
4.5
we have that
Z_{\partial}(s(e^{\prime\prime}))\subseteq Z_{\partial}(w^{\prime})
.

We claim that
Z_{\partial}(s(e^{\prime\prime}))\cap Z_{\partial}(g\cdot w^{\prime})=\emptyset
. Indeed, suppose for a contradiction that
Z_{\partial}(s(e^{\prime\prime}))\cap Z_{\partial}(g\cdot w^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
. Then Proposition
4.4
implies that
s(e^{\prime\prime})
and
w^{\prime\prime}=g\cdot w^{\prime}
have a common upper bound
u^{\prime}\in E^{0}
. Write
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}
for the paths from
u^{\prime}
to
s(e^{\prime\prime})
and
w^{\prime\prime}
respectively, and write
\mu
for subpath of
\eta^{\prime}
from
w^{\prime\prime}
to
x^{\prime}
. Then
e^{\prime\prime}\lambda_{1}
and
\mu\lambda_{2}
are both paths from
u^{\prime}
to
x^{\prime}
, and they are distinct since they have distinct range-most edges by construction. This contradicts that
E
is a multitree, and so
Z_{\partial}(s(e^{\prime\prime}))\cap Z_{\partial}(g\cdot w^{\prime})=\emptyset
. This implies that the containment
Z_{\partial}(g\cdot w^{\prime})\subseteq Z_{\partial}(w^{\prime})
is proper, completing the proof.
∎

6.3.
Topological freeness

Recall that an action of a group
G
on a locally compact Hausdorff space
X
is called
topologically free
if for all
g\in G\setminus\{1\}
, the set
\{x\in X:g\cdot x\neq x\}
is dense in
X
. For
G
discrete, an action
G\curvearrowright X
is topologically free if and only if the set of points in
X
with trivial isotropy is dense in
X
.

It seems to be difficult in general to characterise topological freeness of an action
G\curvearrowright\partial E
in terms of the corresponding quotient directed graph and the stabiliser subgroups. However, we have characterisations in the special cases where
G
acts freely on
E
(Proposition
6.4
) and where all vertex stabilisers are infinite cyclic (Proposition
6.6
).

We start with a lemma.

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose that a discrete group
G
acts on a row-finite, finitely aligned multitree
E
with no sources. For any finite path
\alpha=e_{1}e_{2}\dots e_{n}
in
\Gamma
and any lift
v^{\prime}\in E^{0}
of
r(\alpha)
, the number of lifts of
\alpha
with range
v^{\prime}
is equal to

[G_{r(e_{1})}:G_{e_{1}}][G_{r(e_{2})}:G_{e_{2}}]\cdots[G_{r(e_{n})}:G_{e_{n}}].

Proof.

The claim follows from the fact that for any edge
e\in\Gamma^{1}
and any lift
v^{\prime}\in E^{0}
of
r(e)
, the number of lifts of
e
with range
v^{\prime}
is equal to
[G_{r(e)}:G_{e}]
.
∎

We start with the case where
G
acts freely on
E
. First, recall that a directed graph
\Gamma
is called
aperiodic
if every loop has an entrance.

Proposition 6.4
.

Suppose that a discrete group
G
acts freely on a row-finite, finitely aligned multitree
E
with no sources. The induced action
G\curvearrowright\partial E
is topologically free if and only if
\Gamma
is aperiodic.

Proof.

First suppose that
\Gamma
is aperiodic. This means that for every vertex
v\in\Gamma^{0}
, there is an infinite path
\lambda\in v\Gamma^{\infty}
with aperiodic edge sequence
[
6
, Lemma 3.2]
(note the opposite convention for path direction used in that paper). To show that
G\curvearrowright\partial E
is topologically free, we prove that every cylinder set in
\partial E
contains a point with trivial isotropy.

Fix
v^{\prime}\in E^{0}
. Write
v\in\Gamma^{0}
for its image under the quotient map, and let
\lambda\in v\Gamma^{\infty}
be an infinite path with aperiodic edge sequence. Let
\lambda^{\prime}\in v^{\prime}E^{\infty}
be a lift of
\lambda
. We claim that
[\lambda^{\prime}]\in Z_{\partial}(v^{\prime})
has trivial isotropy. Indeed, suppose that
g\in G
fixes
[\lambda^{\prime}]
, so there exist
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sigma^{m}(g\cdot\lambda^{\prime})=\sigma^{n}(\lambda^{\prime})
. This implies that
\sigma^{m}(\lambda)
and
\sigma^{n}(\lambda)
are the same infinite path in
\Gamma^{\infty}
, and so by aperiodicity of
\lambda
we must have that
m=n
. This means that
g
fixes the infinite path
\sigma^{n}(\lambda^{\prime})
and hence the vertex
r(\sigma^{n}(\lambda^{\prime}))
. But since
G
is assumed to act freely on
E
, this implies that
g
is the identity element. Hence
[\lambda^{\prime}]
has trivial isotropy as claimed.

Now suppose that
\Gamma
is not aperiodic. Then
G
is not trivial (since if it were, then
\Gamma=G\backslash E
would simply be the multitree
E
, which is aperiodic since it cannot contain any loops), and there is a loop
\eta
in
\Gamma
with no entrance. Let
v=r(\eta)
, and let
v^{\prime}\in E^{0}
be a lift of
v
. First we claim that there is a unique lift
\lambda^{\prime}\in v^{\prime}E^{\infty}
of
\eta^{\infty}:=\eta\eta\cdots
with range
v^{\prime}
. Indeed, since all vertex stabilisers are trivial, we have
[G_{r(e)}:G_{e}]=1
for all
e\in\Gamma^{1}
, and so Lemma
6.3
implies that for every lift
v^{\prime\prime}\in E^{0}
of
v
, there is a unique lift
\eta^{\prime\prime}\in v^{\prime\prime}E^{*}
of
\eta
. This in turn implies that
\lambda^{\prime}
is the only lift of
\eta^{\infty}
with range
v^{\prime}
, as claimed.

Now since
\eta
has no entrance, the only infinite path in
\Gamma
with range
v
is the infinite path
\eta^{\infty}
. This implies that
\lambda^{\prime}
is the only infinite path in
E
with range
v^{\prime}
, and so
Z_{\partial}(v^{\prime})
is the singleton set
\{[\lambda^{\prime}]\}
. We claim that
[\lambda^{\prime}]
has non-trivial isotropy. Write
m
for the length of
\eta
. Then
\sigma^{m}(\lambda^{\prime})
is the unique lift of
\eta^{\infty}
with range
r(\sigma^{m}(\lambda^{\prime}))
. Since
r(\sigma^{m}(\lambda^{\prime}))
is also a lift of
v\in\Gamma^{0}
, it is in the same orbit as
v^{\prime}
, and so there is some (necessarily non-identity)
g\in G
such that
g\cdot v^{\prime}=r(\sigma^{m}(\lambda^{\prime}))
. But
g\cdot\lambda^{\prime}
must be a lift of
\eta^{\infty}
with range
r(\sigma^{m}(\lambda^{\prime}))
, and so uniqueness gives that
g\cdot\lambda^{\prime}=\sigma^{m}(\lambda^{\prime})
. Hence
g\cdot[\lambda^{\prime}]=[\lambda^{\prime}]
, showing that
[\lambda^{\prime}]
has non-trivial isotropy.

This implies that
Z_{\partial}(v^{\prime})
is an open set in
\partial E
which does not contain a point with trivial isotropy. Hence the set of points in
\partial E
with trivial isotropy is not dense in
\partial E
, so the action
G\curvearrowright\partial E
is not topologically free.
∎

We now turn to the situation where the vertex stabilisers of the action
G\curvearrowright E
are all infinite cyclic. Recall from Notation
4.18
the definition of the integers
\omega_{e},\omega_{\overline{e}}
for each
e\in\Gamma^{1}
. We need the following concepts from
[

]
, adapted to our context.

Definition 6.5
.

For a path
\alpha=e_{1}e_{2}\dots e_{n}
in
\Gamma
, we define the
signed index ratio

q(\alpha)\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
by

q(\alpha)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\omega_{\overline{e_{i}}}}{\omega_{e_{i}}}.

Consistently with
[
8
]
, for
q\in\mathbb{Q}
we write
\langle q\rangle
for the smallest positive integer denominator that can be used to express
q
as a fraction. Recall that for any infinite path
\lambda=e_{1}e_{2}\cdots
and any
n\geq 1
, we denote by
\lambda_{n}
the path
e_{1}\ldots e_{n}
. For any finite path
\alpha=e_{1}e_{2}\dots e_{k}
and any
1\leq n\leq k
, we similarly denote by
\alpha_{n}
the path
e_{1}\dots e_{n}
.

Proposition 6.6
.

Suppose that a discrete group
G
acts on a row-finite, finitely aligned multitree
E
with no sources, and suppose that
G_{v}\cong\mathbb{Z}
for all
v\in E^{0}
. The induced action
G\curvearrowright\partial E
is topologically free if and only if for all
v\in\Gamma^{0}
there exists an infinite path
\lambda=e_{1}e_{2}\ldots\in v\Gamma^{\infty}
such that

\limsup_{k\to\infty}\,\langle q(\lambda_{k-1})/\omega_{e_{k}}\rangle=\infty.

Remark 6.7
.

We note that the terms
\langle q(\gamma_{n})\rangle
appearing in condition (2) of
[
8
, Theorem 7.5]
are not quite correct, and should be replaced with
\langle q(\gamma_{n})/\omega_{e_{n+1}}\rangle
for respective
n\geq 1
. However, this does not affect the validity of the other results in
[
8
]
.

To prove Proposition
6.6
, we need the following string of lemmas.

Lemma 6.8
.

Suppose that a discrete group
G
acts on a row-finite, finitely aligned multitree
E
with no sources, and suppose that
G_{v}\cong\mathbb{Z}
for all
v\in E^{0}
. Fix a finite path
\alpha=e_{1}e_{2}\dots e_{n}
in
\Gamma
. For any lift
\alpha^{\prime}\in E^{n}
of
\alpha
, an element
m1_{r(\alpha^{\prime})}\in G_{r(\alpha^{\prime})}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
fixes
\alpha^{\prime}
if and only if
\langle q(\alpha_{k-1})/\omega_{e_{k}}\rangle\mid m
for all
1\leq k\leq n
.

Proof.

We show by induction on
n
that
m1_{r(\alpha^{\prime})}
fixes
\alpha^{\prime}
if and only if
mq(\alpha_{k-1})/\omega_{e_{k}}
is an integer for all
1\leq k\leq n
; the claim of the lemma follows from this. (Note that
\alpha_{0}:=r(\alpha)
.) Moreover, we show in the same induction that if
m1_{r(\alpha^{\prime})}
fixes
\alpha^{\prime}
, then
mq(\alpha)
is an integer and
m1_{r(\alpha^{\prime})}=mq(\alpha)1_{s(\alpha^{\prime})}
up to sign.

If
n=1
, then
\alpha=e_{1}
, and for any lift
e^{\prime}
of
e_{1}
, we have that
G_{e^{\prime}}
is the subgroup of
G_{r(e^{\prime})}
of index
\lvert\omega_{e_{1}}\rvert
. Hence
m1_{r(\alpha^{\prime})}
fixes
\alpha^{\prime}
if and only if
\omega_{e_{1}}\mid m
, or equivalently, if
m/\omega_{e_{1}}
is an integer. Since
q(\alpha_{0})=q(r(\alpha))=1
the first claim holds for
n=1
. For the second claim, note that
m1_{r(e^{\prime})}=(m/\omega_{e_{1}})1_{e^{\prime}}=(m\omega_{\overline{e_{1}}}/\omega_{e_{1}})1_{s(e^{\prime})}
up to sign, and
m\omega_{\overline{e_{1}}}/\omega_{e_{1}}=mq(e_{1})
is an integer since
m/\omega_{e_{1}}
is.

Now suppose that the inductive claim holds for all paths of length
n=l
. Fix a finite path
\alpha=e_{1}e_{2}\dots e_{l+1}
in
\Gamma
of length
l+1
, and a lift
\alpha^{\prime}=e^{\prime}_{1}e^{\prime}_{2}\dots e^{\prime}_{l+1}\in E^{l+1}
of
\alpha
. Fix
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Now
m1_{r(\alpha^{\prime})}
fixes
\alpha^{\prime}
if and only if it fixes both
\alpha^{\prime}_{l}
and
e^{\prime}_{l+1}
. The inductive assumption gives that
m1_{r(\alpha^{\prime})}
fixes
\alpha^{\prime}_{l}
if and only if
mq(\alpha_{k-1})/\omega_{e_{k}}
is an integer for all
1\leq k\leq l
, and that in this case,
m^{\prime}:=mq(\alpha_{l})
is an integer and
m^{\prime}1_{s(\alpha^{\prime}_{l})}=m1_{r(\alpha^{\prime})}
(up to sign) in
G
. Now from the
n=1
case, we know that
m^{\prime}1_{s(\alpha^{\prime}_{l})}=m^{\prime}1_{r(e^{\prime}_{l+1})}
fixes
e^{\prime}_{l+1}
if and only if
m^{\prime}/\omega_{e_{l+1}}=mq(\alpha_{l})/\omega_{e_{l+1}}
is an integer. This proves the first inductive claim for
n=l+1
.

For the second claim, note that the inductive assumption gives that
mq(\alpha_{l})
is an integer and that
m1_{r(\alpha^{\prime})}=mq(\alpha_{l})1_{s(\alpha^{\prime}_{l})}=mq(\alpha_{l})1_{r(e^{\prime}_{l+1})}
up to sign. But the
n=1
case for the edge
e^{\prime}_{l+1}
(with
m^{\prime}
in place of
m
) gives that
m^{\prime}q(e_{l+1})=mq(\alpha_{l})q(e_{l+1})=mq(\alpha)
is an integer and that
mq(\alpha_{l})1_{r(e^{\prime}_{l+1})}=mq(\alpha)1_{s(e^{\prime}_{l+1})}=mq(\alpha)1_{s(\alpha^{\prime})}
up to sign. Thus the second claim also holds.

Hence the inductive claim holds for all
n\geq 1
, and we are done.
∎

Corollary 6.9
.

Suppose that a discrete group
G
acts on a row-finite, finitely aligned multitree
E
with no sources, and suppose that
G_{v}\cong\mathbb{Z}
for all
v\in E^{0}
. Every finite path in
E
has non-trivial isotropy.

Lemma 6.10
.

Suppose that a discrete group
G
acts on a row-finite, finitely aligned multitree
E
with no sources, and suppose that
G_{v}\cong\mathbb{Z}
for all
v\in E^{0}
. Fix an infinite path
\lambda=e_{1}e_{2}\ldots\in\Gamma^{\infty}
and a lift
\lambda^{\prime}\in E^{\infty}
of
\lambda
. We have

G_{\lambda^{\prime}}=0\quad\iff\quad\limsup_{k\to\infty}\,\langle q(\lambda_{k-1})/\omega_{e_{k}}\rangle=\infty.

Proof.

First note that
G_{\lambda^{\prime}}
is a subgroup of
G_{r(\lambda^{\prime})}
. Now, an element
m1_{r(\lambda^{\prime})}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
fixes
\lambda^{\prime}
if and only if it fixes
\lambda^{\prime}_{k}
for all
k\geq 1
. By Lemma
6.8
this is equivalent to the condition that
\langle q(\lambda_{k-1})/\omega_{e_{k}}\rangle\mid m
for all
k\geq 1
. If
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\,\langle q(\lambda_{k-1})/\omega_{e_{k}}\rangle=\infty
, then the set
\{\langle q(\lambda_{k-1})/\omega_{e_{k}}\rangle:k\geq 1\}
is unbounded, and so the only value of
m
satisfying
\langle q(\lambda_{k-1})/\omega_{e_{k}}\rangle\mid m
for all
k\geq 1
is
m=0
. Hence
G_{\lambda^{\prime}}=0
in this case. On the other hand, if
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\,\langle q(\lambda_{k-1})/\omega_{e_{k}}\rangle<\infty
, then the set
\{\langle q(\lambda_{k-1})/\omega_{e_{k}}\rangle:k\geq 1\}
is bounded. This set therefore has a (finite) least common multiple
M
, and by definition we have that
\langle q(\lambda_{k-1})/\omega_{e_{k}}\rangle\mid M
for all
k\geq 1
. Hence
M1_{r(\lambda^{\prime})}\neq 0
fixes
\lambda^{\prime}
, so
G_{\lambda^{\prime}}
is not trivial in this case.
∎

Proof of Proposition
6.6
.

For the forward direction, suppose that the action
G\curvearrowright\partial E
is topologically free, and fix
v\in\Gamma^{0}
. Choose any lift
v^{\prime}\in E^{0}
of
v
. Since
G\curvearrowright\partial E
is topologically free, there is some infinite path
\lambda^{\prime}\in v^{\prime}E^{\infty}
such that
[\lambda^{\prime}]
has trivial isotropy. In particular,
G_{\lambda^{\prime}}=0
, and so writing
\lambda\in v\Gamma^{\infty}
for the image of
\lambda^{\prime}
under the quotient map, Lemma
6.10
shows that
\lambda
satisfies the required condition.

We now prove the backward direction. Fix
v^{\prime}\in E^{0}
and
g\in G\setminus\{1\}
; it is enough to show that there is some infinite path
\lambda^{\prime}\in v^{\prime}E^{\infty}
such that
g
does not fix
[\lambda^{\prime}]
. Write
v\in\Gamma^{0}
for the image of
v^{\prime}
under the quotient map. By assumption, there is an infinite path
\lambda=e_{1}e_{2}\ldots\in v\Gamma^{\infty}
satisfying
\limsup_{k\to\infty}\,\langle q(\lambda_{k-1})/\omega_{e_{k}}\rangle=\infty
. If
g
does not fix
[\lambda^{\prime}]
for any lift
\lambda^{\prime}\in v^{\prime}E^{\infty}
of
\lambda
, then we are done, so suppose that there is some lift
\lambda^{\prime\prime}\in v^{\prime}E^{\infty}
of
\lambda
such that
g\cdot[\lambda^{\prime\prime}]=[\lambda^{\prime\prime}]
. This means that there are
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\sigma^{m}(g\cdot\lambda^{\prime\prime})=\sigma^{n}(\lambda^{\prime\prime})
.

First we claim that
m\neq n
. Suppose for a contradiction that
m=n
. Then
g
fixes
\sigma^{n}(\lambda^{\prime\prime})
, so
\sigma^{n}(\lambda^{\prime\prime})
has non-trivial isotropy. This means that the stabiliser
G_{\sigma^{n}(\lambda^{\prime\prime})}
is some finite-index subgroup of
G_{r(\sigma^{n}(\lambda^{\prime\prime}))}
. Now by Corollary
6.9
, we have that
\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{n}
also has non-trivial isotropy, and thus
G_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{n}}
is also some finite-index subgroup of
G_{r(\sigma^{n}(\lambda^{\prime\prime}))}=G_{s(\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{n})}
. Since
G_{r(\sigma^{n}(\lambda^{\prime\prime}))}\cong\mathbb{Z}
, the intersection of two finite-index subgroups is again a finite-index subgroup, and so is in particular not the trivial subgroup

. This means that
G_{\sigma^{n}(\lambda^{\prime\prime})}\cap G_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{n}}
is non-trivial. But an element in
G_{r(\sigma^{n}(\lambda^{\prime\prime}))}
fixes
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
if and only if it fixes both
\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{n}
and
\sigma^{n}(\lambda^{\prime\prime})
, so
G_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}=G_{\sigma^{n}(\lambda^{\prime\prime})}\cap G_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{n}}
. So
G_{\lambda^{\prime\prime}}
is non-trivial, contradicting Lemma
6.10
and the choice of
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
. Hence
m\neq n
.

Now suppose that
m<n
. Writing
\mu^{\prime\prime}=\sigma^{n}(\lambda^{\prime\prime})
, we have that
g\cdot\mu^{\prime\prime}=\sigma^{n-m}(\mu^{\prime\prime})
. This implies that
\mu:=\sigma^{n}(\lambda)
, which is the image of
\mu^{\prime\prime}
under the quotient map, has the form
\eta\eta\cdots
for some cycle
\eta
in
\Gamma
of length
n-m
. We claim that
\langle q(\eta)\rangle>1
. Suppose for a contradiction that
\langle q(\eta)\rangle=1
, so
q(\eta)
is an integer. Write
a_{k}:=q(\lambda_{k-1})/\omega_{e_{k}}
for

. Since
\sigma^{n}(\lambda)
is periodic (with period

), we get that
a_{k+n-m}=q(\eta)a_{k}
for all
k>n
. Since
q(\eta)
is an integer, this implies that
\langle a_{k+n-m}\rangle\leq\langle a_{k}\rangle
for all

, and so the sequence
\{\langle a_{k}\rangle\}_{k=1}^{\infty}=\{\langle q(\lambda_{k-1})/\omega_{e_{k}}\rangle\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
is bounded, contradicting the choice of
\lambda
. Hence
\langle q(\eta)\rangle>1
.

Now we must have that
\lvert\omega_{e}\rvert>1
for some edge
e
in
\eta
; otherwise
q(\eta)
would be an integer and hence
\langle q(\eta)\rangle=1
, contradicting the above. By Lemma
6.3
, this means that for every lift
w^{\prime}\in E^{0}
of

\eta
with range
w^{\prime}
.

We are now ready to construct an infinite path
\lambda^{\prime}\in v^{\prime}E^{\infty}
such that
g\cdot[\lambda^{\prime}]\neq[\lambda^{\prime}]
. Notice that
\mu^{\prime\prime}_{n-m}
is a lift of
\eta
with range
r(\mu^{\prime\prime})
. By the above discussion, there is a distinct lift
\eta^{\prime}
of
\eta
with the same range. Let
\xi^{\prime}
be a lift of
\eta\eta\cdots
with range
s(\eta^{\prime})
, and write
\lambda^{\prime}=\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{n}\eta^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}
. We show that
g
does not fix

. Note that
g
sends

to
s(\mu^{\prime\prime}_{n-m})
, since
g\cdot\mu^{\prime\prime}=\sigma^{n-m}(\mu^{\prime\prime})

g\cdot\eta^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}\in s(\mu^{\prime\prime}_{n-m})E^{\infty}

We claim that
Z_{\partial}(s(\eta^{\prime}))\cap Z_{\partial}(s(\mu^{\prime\prime}_{n-m}))=\emptyset

\xi^{\prime}\in s(\eta^{\prime})E^{\infty}
(and therefore
\lambda^{\prime}
) is not shift-tail equivalent to
g\cdot\eta^{\prime}\xi^{\prime}\in s(\mu^{\prime\prime}_{n-m})E^{\infty}
(and hence to

). Suppose for a contradiction that
Z_{\partial}(s(\eta^{\prime}))\cap Z_{\partial}(s(\mu^{\prime\prime}_{n-m}))\neq\emptyset

implies that
s(\eta^{\prime})
and

and

. Note that

6.4.
Amenability

For actions on undirected trees, we have that the amenability of all vertex stabilisers ensures the amenability of the action
G\curvearrowright\partial T

[
8
, Theorem 5.29]
. We would like something similar in the multitree context. It is unclear to the authors what the situation is in general, however we are able to prove Proposition
6.11
, which is the corresponding result for actions on directed trees.

In this section we use the formulation of an amenable group action given in
[
4
, Definition 2.1]
, which is as follows. For any locally compact group
G
, write
\Prob(G)
for the space of probability measures on
G
equipped with the weak*-topology. Given
s,t\in G
,
f\in C_{c}(G)
and
m\in\Prob(G)
, set

(s\cdot f)(t)=f(s^{-1}t)\quad\text{and}\quad(s\cdot m)(f)=m(s^{-1}\cdot f).

An action of
G
on some locally compact Hausdorff space
X
is
amenable
if there is a net
(m_{i})_{i\in I}
of continuous maps
x\mapsto m_{i}^{x}
from
X
to
\Prob(G)
such that

\lim_{i}\lVert s\cdot m_{i}^{x}-m_{i}^{s\cdot x}\rVert_{1}=0

uniformly for
(x,s)
in compact subsets of
X\times G
.

Proposition 6.11
.

Let
G
be a discrete group acting on a directed tree
T_{+}
. If for all
v\in T^{0}
the stabiliser
G_{v}\leq G
is an amenable group, then the induced action
G\curvearrowright\partial T_{+}
is amenable.

We first prove the following lemma.

Lemma 6.12
.

Let
G
be a locally compact group, and let
X
and
Y
be locally compact spaces on which
G
acts. Suppose that
G\curvearrowright X
is amenable, and that there exists a continuous,
G
-equivariant map
\varphi\colon Y\to X
. Then the action
G\curvearrowright Y
is amenable.

Proof.

By definition of the amenability of
G\curvearrowright X
, there is a net
(m_{i})_{i\in I}
of continuous maps
x\mapsto m_{i}^{x}
from
X
to
\Prob(G)
such that

(6.1)

\lim_{i}\lVert s\cdot m_{i}^{x}-m_{i}^{s\cdot x}\rVert_{1}=0

uniformly for
(x,s)
in compact subsets of
X\times G
. Now since
\varphi\colon Y\to X
is assumed to be continuous, each
m_{i}\circ\varphi
is a continuous map from
Y
to
\Prob(G)
, where
y\mapsto m_{i}^{\varphi(y)}
. Now, using Equation (
6.1
) and the
G
-equivariance of
\varphi
, we get that

\lim_{i}\lVert s\cdot(m_{i}\circ\varphi)^{y}-(m_{i}\circ\varphi)^{s\cdot y}\rVert_{1}=\lim_{i}\lVert s\cdot m_{i}^{\varphi(y)}-m_{i}^{\varphi(s\cdot y)}\rVert_{1}=\lim_{i}\lVert s\cdot m_{i}^{\varphi(y)}-m_{i}^{s\cdot\varphi(y)}\rVert_{1}=0

uniformly for
(\varphi(y),s)
in compact subsets of
X\times G
. But
\varphi\times\id_{G}
maps any compact set in
Y\times G
to a compact subset of
X\times G
, since continuous images of compact sets are compact and
\varphi\times\id_{G}
is continuous. Hence the limit is uniform for
(y,s)
in compact subsets of
Y\times G
, and so
G\curvearrowright Y
is amenable.
∎

Proof of Proposition
6.11
.

Write
T
for the underlying undirected tree of
T_{+}
. The undirected tree
T
inherits an action of
G
from
T_{+}
, with the same vertex stabilisers. In particular,
[
8
, Theorem 5.29]
implies that the action
G\curvearrowright\partial T
is amenable. We aim to apply Lemma
6.12
with
X=\partial T
and
Y=\partial T_{+}
.

For
\mu\in T_{+}^{\infty}
we write
[\mu]_{+}
for the shift-tail equivalence class of
\mu
in
\partial T_{+}
to distinguish it from the shift-tail equivalence class
[\mu]
in
\partial T
. Define a map
\varphi\colon\partial T_{+}\to\partial T
by
\varphi([\mu]_{+})=[\mu]
for all
\mu\in T_{+}^{\infty}
. This map is well-defined since for any
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in T_{+}^{\infty}
, we have
[\mu_{1}]_{+}=[\mu_{2}]_{+}\implies[\mu_{1}]=[\mu_{2}]
. Moreover,
\varphi
is
G
-equivariant since for all
g\in G
and
\mu\in T_{+}^{\infty}
we have

\varphi(g\cdot[\mu]_{+})=\varphi([g\cdot\mu]_{+})=[g\cdot\mu]=g\cdot[\mu]=g\cdot\varphi([\mu]_{+}).

We now show that
\varphi
is continuous. Recall that
\partial T
has a basis of (compact) open sets
\{Z_{\partial}(e):e\in T^{1}\}
, so it is enough to show that
\varphi^{-1}(Z_{\partial}(e))
is open in
\partial T_{+}
for all
e\in T^{1}
. Fix
e\in T^{1}
. We have

\displaystyle\varphi^{-1}(Z_{\partial}(e))

\displaystyle=\{[\mu]_{+}:\mu\in T_{+}^{\infty},\ [\mu]\in Z_{\partial}(e)\}

\displaystyle=\{[\mu]_{+}:\mu\in T_{+}^{\infty},\ \exists\lambda\in s(e)T^{\infty}_{r}\ \text{such that}\ [\mu]=[\lambda]\ \text{and}\ \lambda_{1}\neq\overline{e}.\}

Now recall that
[\mu]=[\lambda]
means that there are some
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and edges
e_{i}\in T^{1}
for
1\leq i\leq n
such that
\lambda=e_{1}e_{2}\dots e_{n}\sigma^{m}(\mu)
. Note that we can write
\sigma^{m}(\mu)=e_{n+1}\sigma^{m+1}(\mu)
where
e_{n+1}\in T_{+}^{1}
, and so
\lambda
has the form

\lambda=e_{1}e_{2}\dots e_{n}e_{n+1}\sigma^{m+1}(\mu).

In particular, relabelling
n+1
as
n
, we can assume without loss of generality that
n\geq 1
and that
e_{n}\in T_{+}^{1}
. Moreover, since
\lambda
is a reduced infinite path, we get that
\alpha:=e_{1}e_{2}\dots e_{n}
is also reduced. Note also that
\mu^{\prime}:=\sigma^{m+1}(\mu)
is an infinite directed path. So we can write

\displaystyle\varphi^{-1}(Z_{\partial}(e))

\displaystyle\qquad=\{[\mu^{\prime}]_{+}:\mu^{\prime}\in T_{+}^{\infty},\ \exists\alpha=e_{1}e_{2}\dots e_{n}\in s(e)T^{*}_{r}\ \text{such that}

\displaystyle\hskip 150.00023ptn\geq 1,\ e_{1}\neq\overline{e},\ e_{n}\in T_{+}^{1}\ \text{and}\ \mu^{\prime}\in s(\alpha)T_{+}^{\infty}\}

\displaystyle\qquad=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}\alpha=e_{1}\dots e_{n}\in s(e)T^{*}_{r},\\
n\geq 1,\ e_{1}\neq\overline{e},\ e_{n}\in T_{+}^{1}\end{subarray}}Z_{\partial}(s(\alpha)),

which implies that
\varphi^{-1}(Z_{\partial}(e))
is open. Hence
\varphi
is continuous, so we can apply Lemma
6.12
to conclude that the action
G\curvearrowright\partial T_{+}
is amenable.
∎

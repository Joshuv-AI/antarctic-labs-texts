Abstract
We study the performance of nonbinary low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes over finite integer rings over two channels that arise from the Lee metric. The first channel is a discrete memory-less channel (DMC) matched to the Lee metric. The second channel adds to each codeword an error vector of constant Lee weight, where the error vector is picked uniformly at random from the set of vectors of constant Lee weight. It is shown that the marginal conditional distributions of the two channels coincide, in the limit of large block length. Random coding union bounds on the block error probability are derived for both channels. Moreover, the performance of selected LDPC code ensembles is analyzed by means of density evolution and finite-length simulations, with belief propagation decoding and with a low-complexity symbol message passing algorithm and it is compared to the derived bounds.

Let

The Lee weight
[

]

\displaystyle\weight_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{L}}(a):=\min(a,q-a).

The Lee weight of a vector

\weight_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{L}}(\bm{x})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\weight_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{L}}(x_{i}).

(1)

Note that the Lee weight of an element

is upper bounded by
\lfloor q/2\rfloor
. Hence, the Lee weight of a length-

\displaystyle\weight_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{L}}(a)=\weight_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{L}}(q-a)\;\text{ for every }a\in\{1,\dots,r\}.

(2)

The Lee distance of two scalars

is

\dist_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{L}}(a,b):=\weight_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{L}}(a-b)
.
The Lee distance between
\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}^{n}
is

\displaystyle\dist_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{L}}(\bm{x},\bm{y})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dist_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{L}}(x_{i},y_{i}).

For

III-A

Marginal Distribution of Constant-Weight Lee Channels

Consider a constant-weight Lee channel

\bm{y}=\bm{x}+\bm{e}

(11)

with
\bm{y},\bm{x},\bm{e}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}_{q}
, and where
\bm{e}
is drawn, with uniform probability, from the set
\mathcal{S}_{n\delta}^{n}

We have

P_{\bm{E}}(\bm{e})=\left|\mathcal{S}_{n\delta}^{n}\right|^{-1}
for all
\bm{e}\in\mathcal{S}_{n\delta}^{n}
, with
P_{\bm{E}}(\bm{e})=0
otherwise. We are interested the marginal distribution

in the limit for

LDPC

17

. While the focus here is in the asymptotic (in the block length

The derivation follows by seeking the composition that dominates the set
\mathcal{S}_{n\delta}^{n}
. More specifically, we should look for the empirical distribution
\bm{\phi}
that maximizes the cardinality of
\mathcal{T}_{\bm{\phi}}^{n}
under the constraint

\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\weight_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{L}}(i)\phi_{i}=\delta.

(12)

The task is closely related to the problem, in statistical mechanics, of finding the distribution of a systems state by relating it to that states energy and temperature
[
20
,
21
]
. Owing to (

\bm{\phi}^{\star}=\arg\max\mathrm{H}_{e}(\bm{\phi})

\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\weight_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{L}}(i)\phi_{i}=\delta

By introducing the Lagrange multiplier

\mathsf{f}(\bm{\phi},\beta):=\mathrm{H}_{e}(\bm{\phi})-\beta\left(\sum_{i=0}^{q-1}\weight_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{L}}(i)\phi_{i}-\delta\right).

The result yields the distribution

\phi_{i}^{\star}=\frac{1}{Z}\exp\left(-\beta\weight_{\scriptscriptstyle\mathsf{L}}(i)\right)

with

The

The choice of the

For the case of

V
Conclusions

The performance of nonbinary low-density parity-check (LDPC) codes over finite integer rings has been studied, over two channels that arise from the Lee metric. The first channel is a discrete memory-less channel matched to the Lee metric, whereas the second channel adds to each codeword an error vector of constant Lee weight. It is shown that the marginal conditional distribution of the two channels coincides, in the limit of large block lengths. The result is used to provide a suitable marginal distribution to the initialization of the message-passing decoder of LDPC codes. The performance of selected LDPC code ensembles, analyzed by means of density evolution and finite-length simulations under belief propagation (BP) and symbol message passing (SMP) decoding, shows that BP decoding largely outperforms SMP decoding. Nevertheless, the SMP algorithm retains a performance that is appealing for applications (e.g., code-based cryptosystems in the Lee metric) demanding low-complexity decoding.

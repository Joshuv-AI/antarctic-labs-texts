2
Introduction

Let
\alpha
be an ordinal number such that
0\leqslant\alpha<\omega_{1}
.
We consider the following
Extension Problem
:

Given
\alpha
,
A
and
\Omega
as above, does every element
f\in{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}(A)

have an extension
\overline{f}\in{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
?

So far, all known solutions to the Extension Problem deal with the case
\alpha
finite
(of course for
\Omega
normal,
A
closed and
\alpha=0
, the Tietze theorem is a solution).
The first result in this direction was proved in
1933
by Kuratowski

[
6
, p.434]
:

(K) If
\Omega
is a metric space and
A
is a
G_{\delta}
subset of
\Omega
then every element
f\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(A)

has an extension
\overline{f}\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(\Omega)
.

In the last twenty years a few refinements and generalizations of Kuratowski’s theorem
have been proven. In
2003
Leung and Tang showed
[
8
, Theorem 3.6]
that there are extensions
\overline{f}
preserving the so-called oscillation index of
f
,
whenever
A
in (K) is closed (cf.
[
9
]
). On the other hand, in
2005
Kalenda and Spurný

[
5
, Theorem 10(a)]
strengthened (K) with the condition

(*)

\sup{f(A)}=\sup\overline{f}(\Omega)
and
\inf f(A)=\inf\overline{f}(\Omega)

and showed
[
5
, Example 19]
that if in (K) the word ”
G_{\delta}
-subset”
is replaced by ”
F_{\sigma}
-subset”, the implication becomes false, in general
(e.g., for
A=
the rational numbers of
X=[0,1]
endowed with the natural metric).
In
2004
Shatery and Zafarani
[
10
, Theorem 1.7]
, using a number of technical
lemmas, obtained a generalization of the Kuratowski theorem (K). They proved (without any straight formula) that

The Extension Problem has a positive answer for
\alpha
finite,
\Omega
perfectly normal
and a Borel subset
A\subset\Omega
of multiplicative class
\alpha
(in particular, for
A\in G_{\delta}(\Omega)
).

During our research we were looking for a brief generalization of the Kuratowski Extension Theorem. It turned out that this can be done by using of the Tietze method of proof and the Lebesgue-Hausdorff Theorem with the assumption that
\Omega
is perfectly normal. The investigation gave us an explicit extension formula for every bounded function
f\in{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}(A)
. For simplicity, in Section 3 we present a full proof for the case
\alpha=1
only
(Theorem
3
), and in Section 4 we show how to obtain a similar result for
1<\alpha<\omega_{1}
.

3
Extensions of Baire-one functions

The proof of the main theorem of this section is based on two auxiliary results. First of them
is (the Lebesgue-Hausdorff) Proposition
1
. The second one is the following special case of the well-known Separation Theorem. It allows to separate two disjoint
G_{\delta}
-subsets of a perfectly normal topological space
\Omega
by the set which is
F_{\sigma}
and
G_{\delta}
in
\Omega
. In the cited Srivastava’s book
[
11
]
the result is formulated for a metrizable space, but simple transformations allows us to state that it is also true for a domain that is perfectly normal.

Proposition 2
.

(
[
11
, Theorem 3.6.11]
)
Let
M,N
be two (non-empty) disjoint
G_{\delta}
-subsets of a perfectly normal topological space
\Omega
. Then there exists a set
H
of both
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
-subset of
\Omega
such that
M\subset H
and
N\cap H=\emptyset
.

We are now ready to formulate our first main result.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\Omega
be a perfectly normal topological space and let
A
be a
G_{\delta}
-subset of
\Omega
. Then every function
f\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(A)
has an extension
\overline{f}\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(\Omega)
such that

\|f(A)\|_{\infty}=\|\overline{f}(\Omega)\|_{\infty}
.

More exactly, if
\sup_{x\in A}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert}=c<\infty
, then there exists a sequence

\left(H_{n}\right)
of both
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
-subsets of
\Omega
, such that the function

\overline{f}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=c\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n+1}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\chi_{H_{n}}(x)\right)~\mathrm{(uniform~convergence~on\ \Omega)}

extends
f
with
\sup_{x\in\Omega}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\overline{f}(x)\right\rvert}=c
.

Proof.

As in the Tietze theorem, it is enough to consider the case
c=1
.
Put
M=f^{-1}\left(\left[-1,-\frac{1}{3}\right]\right)
and

N=f^{-1}\left(\left[\frac{1}{3},1\right]\right)
. By Proposition
1
,
both the (disjoint) sets
M
and
N
are
G_{\delta}
-subsets of
A
. Since
G_{\delta}(A)=A\cap G_{\delta}(\Omega)

and
A\in G_{\delta}(\Omega)
, we have
M,N\in G_{\delta}(\Omega)
. By Proposition
2
,
there is a set
H_{0}
of both
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
-subset of
\Omega

such that
M\subset H_{0}
and
N\cap H_{0}=\emptyset
. By Proposition
1
again, the characteristic function
\chi_{H_{0}}
belongs to
{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(\Omega)

(cf.
[
10
, Lemma 1.4]
).
Put
g_{0}(x)=\frac{2}{3}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\chi_{H_{0}}(x)\right)
. Hence
g_{0}\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(\Omega)
,

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert g_{0}(x)\right\rvert}=\frac{1}{3}\textrm{ for all }x\in\Omega\textrm{ and}

(1)

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(x)-g_{0}(x)\right\rvert}\leqslant\frac{2}{3}\textrm{ for all }x\in A.

(2)

Now we mimic the classic proof of the Tietze theorem
[
1
, p.69]
.
Repeating the above arguments we obtain a sequence
\left(H_{n}\right)_{n=0}^{\infty}

of both
F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
-subsets of
\Omega
such that, for the functions

g_{n}\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(\Omega)
,
n=0,1,\ldots
, of the form

g_{n}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n+1}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\chi_{H_{n}}(x)\right)

we have

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert g_{n}(x)\right\rvert}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n+1}\textrm{ for all }x\in\Omega\textrm{ and}

(3)

\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(x)-(g_{0}(x)+\ldots+g_{n}(x))\right\rvert}\leqslant\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n+1}\textrm{ for all }x\in A.

(4)

By (
3
) and by the fact that the space
{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(\Omega)
is closed with respect to uniform limits
(see
[
2
, p.269]
), the series
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}g_{n}

converges uniformly on
\Omega
to a function
g\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(\Omega)
. By (
4
) we obtain

g_{\mathord{\restriction}A}=f
and, by (
3
) again,
\sup_{x\in\Omega}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert g(x)\right\rvert}=1
.
Hence
\overline{f}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=g
is the required extension of
f
.
∎

Note that Theorem
3
has a version for non-negative mappings.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\Omega
be a perfectly normal topological space and let
A
be a
G_{\delta}
-subset of
\Omega
. Moreover,
let
0\leqslant f\in B_{1}^{bd}(A)
with
\sup_{x\in A}f(x)=1
. Then there is a sequence
(G_{n})
of both

F_{\sigma}
- and
G_{\delta}
-subsets of
\Omega
such that the function

\overline{f}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n+1}\chi_{G_{n}}(x)~\mathrm{(uniform~convergence)}

is a (positive) extension of
f
with
\sup_{x\in\Omega}\overline{f}(x)=1
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega
be a perfectly normal topological space and let
A
be a
G_{\delta}
-subset of
\Omega
.
Let also
f\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}^{bd}(A)
be a function such that
0\leqslant f\leqslant 1
.
Obviously
0\leqslant 2f\leqslant 2
and
-1\leqslant 2f-1\leqslant 1
. By Theorem
3
, every function
g=2f-1
has an extension
\overline{g}\in{\mathscr{B}}_{1}(\Omega)
such that
-1\leqslant\overline{g}(x)\leqslant 1
for every
x\in\Omega
. Thus

\overline{g}=2\overline{f}-1=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n+1}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\chi_{H_{n}}\right)

and

2\overline{f}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n+1}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n+1}\chi_{H_{n}}+1.

Since

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n+1}=2,

\overline{f}=1-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n+1}\chi_{H_{n}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n+1}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n+1}\chi_{H_{n}}.

Thus

\overline{f}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n+1}(1-\chi_{H_{n}})

and

\overline{f}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n+1}\chi_{G_{n}},

where
G_{n}=H_{n}^{\prime}
for every
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. That completes the proof.
∎

4
Extensions of Baire-alpha functions

Throughout this section the following abbreviations for some classes of subsets of
\Omega
will be used:

\prod^{0}_{1}(\Omega)\ \left[\sum^{0}_{1}(\Omega)\right]
- closed [open] subsets of
\Omega
;

\prod_{\alpha}^{0}(\Omega)\ \left[\sum_{\alpha}^{0}(\Omega)\right]
- the multiplicative [additive]
class
\alpha
of Borel subsets of
\Omega
,
0<\alpha<\omega_{1}
;

{\mathscr{H}}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
- the family of Borel subsets of
\Omega
of
ambiguous class

\alpha
is defined to be
\prod_{\alpha}^{0}(\Omega)\cap\sum_{\alpha}^{0}(\Omega)
,
0<\alpha<\omega_{1}
;

Z_{0}(\Omega)\ \left[CZ_{0}(\Omega)\right]
- the Baire subsets of
\Omega
of multiplicative
[additive] class
0
(these are zero [co-zero] sets of continuous
mappings defined on
\Omega
);

Z_{\alpha+1}(\Omega)
- countable intersections of sets of additive class
\alpha

0<\alpha<\omega_{1}
;

CZ_{\alpha+1}(\Omega)
- the complements of
Z_{\alpha+1}(\Omega)
,
0<\alpha<\omega_{1}
;

{\mathcal{A}}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
- the family of Baire subsets of
\Omega
of
ambiguous class

\alpha
is defined to be
Z_{\alpha}^{0}(\Omega)\cap CZ_{\alpha}^{0}(\Omega)
,

.

The family off all Borel [Baire] subsets of
\Omega
we denote by
Borel(\Omega)
[
Baire(\Omega)
].
It is well known that in a perfectly normal topological space
\Omega
every closed set is a zero set.
Thus, if we assume that
\Omega
is perfectly normal then
\prod_{\alpha}^{0}(\Omega)=Z_{\alpha}(\Omega)
,
\sum_{\alpha}^{0}(\Omega)=CZ_{\alpha}(\Omega)
(
\alpha=1,2,\ldots
) and

The Extension Theorem for Baire-alpha functions is a consequence of Theorem
3
(for Baire-one functions), the general form of the Separation Theorem
(see
[
11
, Theorem 3.6.11]
) and the following general form of the Lebesgue-Hausdorff result.

Theorem 5
.

(Lebesgue-Hausdorff,
[
7
, p.393]
)
Let
\Omega
be a completely regular topological space and let a function
f\colon\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
be bounded.
For each ordinal
\alpha<\omega_{1}
,
f\in{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
if and only if
f^{-1}(F)\in Z_{\alpha}(\Omega)
for every closed subset
F
of
{\mathbb{R}}
.

We are now ready to present our second main result.

Theorem 6
.

Let
\Omega
be a perfectly normal topological space and let
A\subset\Omega
be a Borel subset of multiplicative class
\alpha<\omega_{1}
.
Every function
f\in{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}(A)
has an extension
\overline{f}\in{\mathscr{B}}_{\alpha}(\Omega)
such that

\|f(A)\|_{\infty}=\|\overline{f}(\Omega)\|_{\infty}
.

More exactly, if
\sup_{x\in A}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert f(x)\right\rvert}=c<\infty
, then there exists a sequence

\left(H_{n}\right)
of Borel ambiguous class
\alpha
subsets of
\Omega
, such that the function

\overline{f}(x)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=c\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^{n+1}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\chi_{H_{n}}(x)\right)

extends
f
with
\sup_{x\in\Omega}\mathinner{\!\left\lvert\overline{f}(x)\right\rvert}=c
.

It also has a version for non-negative functions.

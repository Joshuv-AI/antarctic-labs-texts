2.1
.

Let
A
be a ring and let
M
be an
A
-module. We say that

satisfies Serre’s
S_{2}
property if for every prime ideal
P
of
A

0ptM_{P}\geq\inf\{2,\dim M_{P}\}.

We say that the ring
A
satisfies
S_{2}
if it satisfies
S_{2}
as an
A
-module.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
A
be a domain with quotient field
Q(A)
. We say that a domain
B
is an
S_{2}
-ification of A if:

(1)

A\subseteq B\subseteq Q(A)
and
B
is module-finite over
A
,

(2)

B
is
S_{2}
as an
A
-module, and

(3)

for all
b
in
B\setminus A
,
0ptD(b)\geq 2
where
D(b)=\{a\in A\mid ab\in A\}
.

Remark 2.3
.

In general, the
S_{2}
-ification of a domain might not exist, but if there is one, then it must be unique; see
[
3
, 2.4]
.

2.4
.

Coefficient ideals:
Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring and let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. For sufficiently large values of
n
,
\ell(A/I^{n+1})
is a polynomial
P_{I}(n)
in
n
of degree
d=\dim A
, the Hilbert polynomial of
I
. We write this polynomial in terms of binomial coefficients:

P_{I}(n)=e_{0}(I)\binom{n+d}{d}-e_{1}(I)\binom{n+d-1}{d-1}+\cdots+(-1)^{d}e_{d}(I).

The coefficients
e_{i}(I)
are integers and we call them the Hilbert coefficients of
I
. In
[
6
, Theorem 1]
the following theorem is proved.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be a quasi-unmixed local ring of dimension
d>0
with infinite residue field and
I
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Then for each integer
k
in
\{0,1,\cdots,d\}
there exists a unique largest ideal
I_{k}
containing
I
such that
e_{i}(I)=e_{i}(I_{k})
for
i=0,1,\cdots,k
. The ideal
I_{k}
is called the
k
-th coefficient ideal of
I
.

I_{0}
is the integral closure of
I
and if
I
contains a regular element, then
I_{d}
is the Ratliff-Rush closure of
I
.

Let

. Let

Lemma 2.9
.

(with setup as in
2.8
) Let

\dim M\leq\max\{\dim M^{(c)},0\}.

Proof.

We may assume
\dim M>0
. Set
N=M_{\geq c}
. Note
\dim N=\dim M
. After going mod annhilator of

is a faithful

Let

. Note we have an exact sequence

0\rightarrow B\xrightarrow{\phi}N^{l}(+c)

0\rightarrow B^{(c)}\xrightarrow{\phi^{<c>}}(N^{<c>})^{l}(+1)

So it follows that

Thus

In this section we prove two technical results that we need. The first technical result is :

Theorem 3.1

Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section
(A,\mathfrak{m})
is an excellent normal local ring of dimension
d\geq 2
and
I
is an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. Throughout
\mathcal{R}=A[It,t^{-1}]
is the extended Rees algebra of
I
and
\mathcal{S}=A[It]
is the Rees algebra of
I
. Set
\mathcal{R}^{*}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}(I^{n})^{*}
and
\mathcal{S}^{*}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}(I^{n})^{*}
. Note
\mathcal{R}^{*}
is a finite
\mathcal{R}
-module and they have the same field of fractions.
Similarly
\mathcal{S}^{*}
is a finite
\mathcal{S}
-module and they have the same field of fractions. Furthermore both
\mathcal{R}^{*}
and
\mathcal{S}^{*}
are normal domains.

4.1
.

Set
\mathcal{B}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}(I^{n})^{*}/I^{n}.
Then the following short exact sequences give
\mathcal{B}
a structure both as an
\mathcal{R}
-module and as a
\mathcal{S}
-module.

0\rightarrow\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}\rightarrow 0.

0\rightarrow\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}\rightarrow 0.

As
\ell(\mathcal{B}_{n})
is finite for all
n\geq 1
and so
\dim\mathcal{B}
is determined by its Hilbert function. It follows that
\mathcal{B}
has the same dimension both as an
\mathcal{R}
-module and as an
\mathcal{S}
-module.

The following result covers Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be an excellent normal local ring of dimension
d\geq 1
and let
I
be an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary ideal. The following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

\mathcal{R}
is
R_{1}
.

(ii)

\dim\mathcal{B}\leq d-1
as an
\mathcal{R}
-module.

(iii)

\mathcal{S}
is
R_{1}
.

(iv)

\dim\mathcal{B}\leq d-1
as an
\mathcal{S}
-module.

(v)

\operatorname{Proj}\mathcal{S}
is
R_{1}
.

(vi)

(I^{n})^{*}=(I^{n})_{1}
for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

\rm{(i)}\implies\rm{(ii)}
: Let
P
be a height one prime of
\mathcal{R}
.
We have an exact sequence
0\rightarrow\mathcal{R}_{P}\rightarrow(\mathcal{R}^{*})_{P}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}_{P}\rightarrow 0
.
By our hypothesis
\mathcal{R}_{P}
is a DVR. We also have that
(R^{*})_{P}
is a finite
\mathcal{R}_{P}
-module and it has same field of fractions as that of
\mathcal{R}_{P}
. It follows that
\mathcal{R}_{P}=(\mathcal{R}^{*})_{P}
. So
\mathcal{B}_{P}=0
.
The result follows.

\rm{(ii)}\implies\rm{(i)}
: Let
P
be a prime of height one in
\mathcal{R}
. Then
\mathcal{B}_{P}=0
. It follows that
\mathcal{R}_{P}=(\mathcal{R}^{*})_{P}
. So
\mathcal{R}_{P}
is a DVR. It follows that
\mathcal{R}
is
R_{1}
.

\rm{(ii)}\Leftrightarrow\rm{(iv)}
: This follows from
4.1
.

\rm{(iii)}\Leftrightarrow\rm{(iv)}
: This is similar to
\rm{(i)}\Leftrightarrow\rm{(ii)}
.

\rm{(iii)}\implies\rm{(v)}
: This is trivial.

\rm{(v)}\implies\rm{(iv)}
: We may assume
\dim\mathcal{B}>0
, otherwise there is nothing to prove. Let
P
be a minimal prime of
\mathcal{B}
. By assumption
P\neq\mathfrak{M}
, the maximal homogeneous ideal of
\mathcal{S}
. We claim that
P\nsupseteq\mathcal{S}_{+}
. Suppose if possible
P\supseteq\mathcal{S}_{+}
. As
\mathcal{B}
is a finite
\mathcal{S}
-module and as
\ell(\mathcal{B}_{n})
is finite for all
n
there exists
r
such that
\mathfrak{m}^{r}\mathcal{B}=0
. So
P
contains

(\mathfrak{m}^{r}\oplus\mathcal{S}_{+})
. It follows that
P=\mathfrak{M}
, a contradiction. Rest of the argument follows as in
\rm{(i)}\implies\rm{(ii)}
.

\rm{(iv)}\implies\rm{(vi)}
: Let
\mathcal{C}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}(I^{n})^{*}/(I^{n})_{1}
considered as an
\mathcal{S}
-module. Then
\mathcal{C}
is a quotient of
\mathcal{B}
. So
\dim\mathcal{C}\leq d-1
. By
3.1

we get
\mathcal{C}=0
. So
(I^{n})^{*}=(I^{n})_{1}
for all
n\geq 1
.

\rm{(vi)}\implies\rm{(iv)}
: By our assumption
\mathcal{B}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}(I^{n})_{1}/I^{n}.

By
3.4
, it follows that
\dim\mathcal{B}\leq d-1
.
∎

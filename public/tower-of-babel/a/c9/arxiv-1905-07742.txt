Radiative transitions and magnetic moments of the charmed and bottom vector mesons in chiral perturbation theory
Bo Wang 1,2, Bin Yang 1, Lu Meng 1, Shi-Lin Zhu 1,2,3

Abstract
In this work, we systematically study the radiative decays and magnetic moments of the charmed and bottom vector mesons with chiral perturbation theory up to one-loop level. We present the results in SU(2) and SU(3) cases with the mass splitting in loop diagrams kept and unkept, respectively. The obtained decay rates for D ∗ D^{\ast} and B ∗ B^{\ast} mesons in SU(3) case with the mass splitting kept are: Γ D ¯ ∗ 0 → D ¯ 0 ​ γ = 16.2 − 6.0 + 6.5 \Gamma_{\bar{D}^{\ast 0}\to\bar{D}^{0}\gamma}=16.2^{+6.5}_{-6.0} keV, Γ D ∗ ⁣ − → D − ​ γ = 0.73 − 0.3 + 0.7 \Gamma_{D^{\ast-}\to D^{-}\gamma}=0.73^{+0.7}_{-0.3} keV, Γ D s ∗ ⁣ − → D s − ​ γ = 0.32 − 0.3 + 0.3 \Gamma_{D_{s}^{\ast-}\to D_{s}^{-}\gamma}=0.32^{+0.3}_{-0.3} keV, and Γ B ∗ ⁣ + → B + ​ γ = 0.58 − 0.2 + 0.2 \Gamma_{B^{\ast+}\to B^{+}\gamma}=0.58^{+0.2}_{-0.2} keV, Γ B ∗ 0 → B 0 ​ γ = 0.23 − 0.06 + 0.06 \Gamma_{B^{\ast 0}\to B^{0}\gamma}=0.23^{+0.06}_{-0.06} keV, Γ B s ∗ 0 → B s 0 ​ γ = 0.04 − 0.03 + 0.03 \Gamma_{B_{s}^{\ast 0}\to B_{s}^{0}\gamma}=0.04^{+0.03}_{-0.03} keV. The decay width for D ∗ ⁣ − → D − ​ γ D^{\ast-}\to D^{-}\gamma is consistent with the experimental measurement. As a byproduct, the full widths of D ¯ ∗ 0 \bar{D}^{\ast 0} and D s ∗ ⁣ − D_{s}^{\ast-} are Γ tot ​ ( D ¯ ∗ 0 ) ≃ 77.7 − 20.5 + 26.7 ​ keV \Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}(\bar{D}^{\ast 0})\simeq 77.7^{+26.7}_{-20.5}~\mathrm{keV} and Γ tot ​ ( D s ∗ ⁣ − ) ≃ 0.62 − 0.50 + 0.45 ​ keV \Gamma_{\mathrm{tot}}(D_{s}^{\ast-})\simeq 0.62^{+0.45}_{-0.50}~\mathrm{keV} , respectively. We also calculate the magnetic moments of the heavy vector mesons. The analytical chiral expressions derived in our work shall be helpful for the extrapolations of lattice QCD simulations in the coming future.

Magnetic moments can be related to the form factors by extrapolating

II
electromagnetic form factors and magnetic moments

We first consider the radiative transition process
V\to P\gamma
,
where
V
stands for the vector mesons (
D^{\ast}
or
B^{\ast}
), and

P
denotes the pseudoscalar mesons (
D
or
B
). The M1 transition
form factor
\mu^{\prime}(q^{2})
can be defined through a covariant
expression of the hadronic matrix elements
Casalbuoni:1996pg
,

\langle P(p^{\prime})|J_{em}^{\mu}(q^{2})|V(p,\varepsilon_{V})\rangle=e\mu^{\prime}(q^{2})\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}p_{\nu}q_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{V\beta},

(1)

where
J_{em}^{\mu}
is the electromagnetic current at hadronic level.

q_{\alpha}=(p-p^{\prime})_{\alpha}
is the transferred momentum, and

\varepsilon_{V\beta}
denotes the polarization vector of the
initial vector meson.

The interaction Hamiltonian can then be written as

H_{\mathrm{int}}=\int d^{3}xeA_{\mu}J^{\mu}_{em},

(2)

where
A_{\mu}
is the photon field.

For a heavy meson
M
that is composed of a heavy antiquark

\bar{Q}
and a light quark
q
, the ground spin doublet

(P,P^{\ast})
can be represented by a
4\times 4
Dirac-type matrix

\mathcal{H}
. We use the
\mathcal{H}(p)
and
\mathcal{H}(v)
to
denote the heavy meson fields in relativistic and heavy meson
effective theory (HMET) convention, respectively. They can be
related with each other by

|\mathcal{H}(p)\rangle=\sqrt{m_{H}}\left[|\mathcal{H}(v)\rangle+\mathcal{O}(1/m_{H})\right].

(3)

Then, in the framework of HMET, Eq. (
1
) can be
reexpressed as

\langle P(p^{\prime})|J_{em}^{\mu}|V(p,\varepsilon_{V})\rangle=e\sqrt{m_{V}m_{P}}\mu^{\prime}(q^{2})\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}v_{\nu}q_{\alpha}\varepsilon_{V\beta},

(4)

where the recoil effect is negligible in the above equation.

With the above preparation, one can easily get the expression of the
decay rate,

\Gamma\left[V\to P\gamma\right]=\frac{1}{3}\int d\Omega_{\hat{q}}\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\frac{|\bm{q}|}{m_{V}^{2}}\sum|\mathcal{M}|^{2},

(5)

where
\mathcal{M}
represents the transition amplitude, and a sum
over the final-state photon polarization and an average over the
initial
V
polarization has been performed.

Explicitly, we have

\Gamma\left[V\to P\gamma\right]=\frac{\alpha}{3}\frac{m_{P}}{m_{V}}\left|\mu^{\prime}(0)\right|^{2}|\bm{q}|^{3},

(6)

where
\alpha=1/137
is the fine-structure constant. The transition
magnetic moment
\mu_{V\to P\gamma}
can be defined as

\mu_{V\to P\gamma}=\frac{e}{2}\mu^{\prime}(0).

(7)

In the following, we derive the magnetic moment of a vector meson.
The matrix element of
J_{em}^{\mu}(q^{2})
are defined in terms of the
standard Lorentz covariant decomposition
Arnold:1979cg
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\mu}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle V(p^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime\ast})|J_{em}^{\mu}(q^{2})|V(p,\varepsilon)\rangle

(8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathcal{G}_{1}(q^{2})(\varepsilon\cdot\varepsilon^{\prime\ast})(p+p^{\prime})^{\mu}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{G}_{2}(q^{2})\left[(\varepsilon\cdot q)\varepsilon^{\prime\ast\mu}-(\varepsilon^{\prime\ast}\cdot q)\varepsilon^{\mu}\right]

\displaystyle+\mathcal{G}_{3}(q^{2})\frac{(\varepsilon\cdot q)(\varepsilon^{\prime\ast}\cdot q)}{2m_{V}^{2}}(p+p^{\prime})^{\mu}.

This expression can be simplified under Breit frame. In our
calculations, we define

\displaystyle q^{\mu}=(p-p^{\prime})^{\mu}=(0,\bm{Q}),\quad\bm{Q}=Q\hat{z},\quad p^{\mu}=(p^{0},\frac{1}{2}\bm{Q}),

\displaystyle p^{\prime\mu}=(p^{0},-\frac{1}{2}\bm{Q}),\quad-q^{2}=Q^{2}\geq 0,\quad p^{0}=\sqrt{m_{V}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}Q^{2}}.

A straightforward derivation under Breit frame gives the time
component of
\mathcal{G}^{\mu}(q^{2})
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{0}(Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2p^{0}\Bigg\{\mathcal{G}_{C}(Q^{2})(\bm{\varepsilon}\cdot\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime\ast})+\frac{\mathcal{G}_{Q}(Q^{2})}{2m_{V}^{2}}\Big[(\bm{\varepsilon}\cdot\bm{Q})(\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime\ast}\cdot\bm{Q})

(9)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{3}(\bm{\varepsilon}\cdot\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime\ast})Q^{2}\Big]\Bigg\},

where
\mathcal{G}_{C}
and
\mathcal{G}_{Q}
represent charge and
quadrupole form factors, respectively. In deriving
Eq. (
9
), we have used the transverse condition of the
initial and final state polarization vectors, i.e.,

p\cdot\varepsilon=0
, and

p^{\prime}\cdot\varepsilon^{\prime\ast}=0
.

Similarly, the space component of
\mathcal{G}^{\mu}(q^{2})
is

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{G}}(Q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2}(Q^{2})\left[(\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime\ast}\cdot\bm{Q})\bm{\varepsilon}-(\bm{\varepsilon}\cdot\bm{Q})\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime\ast}\right]

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2p^{0}\frac{\mathcal{G}_{M}(Q^{2})}{2m_{V}}\left[(\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime\ast}\cdot\bm{Q})\bm{\varepsilon}-(\bm{\varepsilon}\cdot\bm{Q})\bm{\varepsilon}^{\prime\ast}\right],

where
\mathcal{G}_{M}
is the magnetic dipole form factor. The
expressions of
\mathcal{G}_{C}
,
\mathcal{G}_{Q}
and
\mathcal{G}_{M}

read

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{1}+\frac{2}{3}\eta\mathcal{G}_{Q},

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{3}+\mathcal{G}_{2}(1+\eta)^{-1}+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{G}_{1}(1+\eta)^{-1},

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{2},

(11)

where
\eta={Q^{2}}/{(4m_{V}^{2})}
.

V.2
Numerical results and discussions

The numerical results for the magnetic moments
\mu_{V}
calculated in
the SU(2) and SU(3) cases are given order by order in the lower half
parts of Tables
10
and
11
, respectively. We
see that the convergence of the chiral expansion in the SU(2) case
remains very good and the convergence is also reasonable in SU(3).

In the SU(2) case, the magnetic moments at
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}(p^{1})

are independent of
\Delta
. The
\Delta\neq 0
correction reduces
the
\mu_{V}
at
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}(p^{2})
and
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}(p^{3})
.
Consequently, the total results are increased. In the heavy quark
limit, there exists a strict relationship between
\mu_{V}
and

\mu_{V\to P\gamma}
at each order, i.e.,
|\mu_{V}|=|\mu_{V\to P\gamma}|
when we take
D=4
and
\Delta=0
in the loop functions.
Both the radiative transitions and magnetic moments of the heavy
vector mesons are solely governed by the light quark since the heavy
quark decouples completely.

In the SU(3) case, one notices the similar variation trend at

\mathcal{O}_{\mu}(p^{2})
as in SU(2). At
\mathcal{O}_{\mu}(p^{3})
, there
is a moderate increasement when the mass splitting is included. The
total results are enhanced in the
\Delta\neq 0
case. It’s
interesting to diagnose the convergence of the chiral expansion for
magnetic moments from a straightforward dimensional analysis.

The magnetic moments
\mu_{V}
at the leading order (LO),
next-to-leading order (NLO), and next-to-next-to-leading order
(NNLO) can be parameterized as follows,

\displaystyle\mathrm{LO}:\quad

\displaystyle A\frac{1}{m_{q}}+B\frac{1}{m_{Q}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{NLO}:\quad

\displaystyle C\frac{m_{\phi}}{\Lambda_{\chi}^{2}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{NNLO}:\quad

\displaystyle\left(D\frac{1}{m_{q}}+E\frac{1}{m_{Q}}\right)\times\frac{m_{\phi}^{2}}{\Lambda_{\chi}^{2}},

(87)

where the coefficients
A,\dots,E
are order-one dimensionless
constants.
\Lambda_{\chi}\sim 1
GeV denotes the chiral breaking
scale.

For the
D^{\ast-}
and
B^{\ast 0}

pseudoscalar lines in the

magnitude as the
\mathcal{O}(p^{3})

VI
Summary

For the ground vector
\bar{Q}q
states, heavy quark spin symmetry
implies the mass splitting between the spin triplets
V
and spin
singlets
P
is very small, which is of the same order as the pion
mass
m_{\pi}
. Thus the decay modes of
V
are largely restricted.
For the ground-state charmed vector mesons, the dominant decay
channels are
V\to P\pi
and
V\to P\gamma
. For the
b\bar{q}

states, the only dominant decay modes are
V\to P\gamma
.

In this work, we calculate the decay rates of
V\to P\gamma
for the
charmed and bottom vector mesons. Our result for
D^{\ast-}\to D^{-}\gamma
is in accordance with the experimental measurement. We
also investigate the convergence of the chiral expansion of the
transition magnetic moments in the SU(2) and SU(3) cases with the
mass splitting kept and unkept. The results indicate that the
convergence in SU(2) case is very good, and it is reasonable for
SU(3) likewise. The effect of the mass splitting for the charmed
mesons is more significant than that for the bottom mesons. The
radiative decay widths of the
D^{\ast}
and
B^{\ast}
mesons from
other theoretical models and lattice QCD simulations also are
consistent with ours. As a byproduct, the full widths of

\bar{D}^{\ast 0}
and
D_{s}^{\ast-}
are estimated to be

77.7^{+26.7}_{-20.5}
keV and
0.62^{+0.45}_{-0.50}
keV,
respectively.

In this work, we also calculate the magnetic moments of the
D^{\ast}

and
B^{\ast}
mesons. Our results agree with the predictions of bag
model
Bose:1980vy
;
Simonis:2018rld
and NJL
model
Luan:2015goa
. The magnetic moments of heavy vector
mesons are good platforms to probe their inner structures. For
example, the magnetic moment of
\bar{D}^{\ast 0}
should be zero if
we use the classical formula

\bm{\mu}=\frac{e}{2m}\mathbf{S}
(where
e
,
m
and

\mathbf{S}
denote the charge, mass and spin, respectively).
The large anomalous magnetic moment of
\bar{D}^{\ast 0}
clearly
demonstrates that it is not a point particle.

In summary, we have systematically studied the radiative transitions
and magnetic moments of charmed and bottom vector mesons with

\chi
PT up to
\mathcal{O}(p^{4})
. Our numerical results are
presented up to this order with different scenarios. The LECs

\tilde{a}
,
a
and
b
in the
\mathcal{O}(p^{2})
Lagrangians are
estimated with the quark model and resonance saturation model,
respectively. We notice the one-loop chiral correction plays a very
important role in mediating the (transition) magnetic moments. Our result
indicates the quark model prediction is not enough to describe the
magnetic properties of the charmed and bottom vector mesons.
The quark dynamics of the light degree of freedom that is related with
the spontaneous breaking of chiral symmetry is non-negligible.

The present investigations of the radiative decays of
D^{\ast}
and

B^{\ast}
shall be helpful to the future measurement at facilities
such as BelleII and LHCb. Furthermore, the analytical expressions
derived in
\chi
PT shall be helpful for the chiral extrapolations
of lattice QCD simulations on the electromagnetic transitions and
magnetic moments of heavy vector mesons.

Loop integrals

Here, we show the detailed forms of the
\mathcal{J}
functions used
in the text. One can find the complete forms in Ref.

Wang:2018atz
.

\displaystyle i\int\frac{d^{D}l\lambda^{4-D}}{(2\pi)^{D}}\frac{1}{l^{2}-m^{2}+i\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{0}^{c}(m),

(100)

\displaystyle i\int\frac{d^{D}l\lambda^{4-D}}{(2\pi)^{D}}\frac{l^{\alpha}l^{\beta}}{\left(v\cdot l+\omega+i\varepsilon\right)\left(l^{2}-m^{2}+i\varepsilon\right)}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left[v^{\alpha}v^{\beta}\mathcal{J}_{21}^{a}+g^{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{J}_{22}^{a}\right](m,\omega),

(101)

\displaystyle i\int\frac{d^{D}l\lambda^{4-D}}{(2\pi)^{D}}\frac{l^{\alpha}l^{\beta}}{\left(v\cdot l+\omega+i\varepsilon\right)\left[v\cdot l+\delta+i\varepsilon\right]\left(l^{2}-m^{2}+i\varepsilon\right)}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left[v^{\alpha}v^{\beta}\mathcal{J}_{21}^{g}+g^{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{J}_{22}^{g}\right](m,\omega,\delta),

(102)

\displaystyle i\int\frac{d^{D}l\lambda^{4-D}}{(2\pi)^{D}}\frac{l^{\alpha}l^{\beta}}{\left(l^{2}-m^{2}+i\varepsilon\right)\left[(l+q)^{2}-m^{2}+i\varepsilon\right]}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left[q^{\alpha}q^{\beta}\mathcal{J}_{21}^{F}+g^{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{J}_{22}^{F}\right](m,q),

(103)

\displaystyle i\int\frac{d^{D}l\lambda^{4-D}}{(2\pi)^{D}}\frac{l^{\alpha}l^{\beta}}{\left(v\cdot l+\omega+i\varepsilon\right)\left(l^{2}-m^{2}+i\varepsilon\right)\left[(l+q)^{2}-m^{2}+i\varepsilon\right]}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\left[g^{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{J}_{21}^{T}+q^{\alpha}q^{\beta}\mathcal{J}_{22}^{T}+v^{\alpha}v^{\beta}\mathcal{J}_{23}^{T}+(q\vee v)\mathcal{J}_{24}^{T}\right](m,\omega,q),

where
q\vee v\equiv q^{\alpha}v^{\beta}+q^{\beta}v^{\alpha}
. The

\mathcal{J}
functions defined above can be calculated with the
dimensional regularization in
D
dimensions. In the following, we
write out the expressions of the used
\mathcal{J}
functions.

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{0}^{c}(m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2m^{2}L+\frac{m^{2}}{16\pi^{2}}\ln\frac{m^{2}}{\lambda^{2}},

(105)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{22}^{a}(m,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\omega\left(m^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\omega^{2}\right)L+\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\int_{-\omega}^{0}\tilde{\Delta}\ln\frac{\tilde{\Delta}}{\lambda^{2}}dy+\frac{1}{24\pi}\tilde{A}^{3/2},

(106)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{22}^{g}(m,\omega,\delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\frac{1}{\delta-\omega}\left[\mathcal{J}_{22}^{a}(m,\omega)-\mathcal{J}_{22}^{a}(m,\delta)\right]&\textrm{if $\omega\neq\delta$}\\
-\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\mathcal{J}_{22}^{a}(m,x)\Big|_{x\to\omega(\textrm{or}\ \delta)}&\textrm{if $\omega=\delta$}\end{array}\right.,

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{22}^{F}(m,q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(m^{2}-\frac{q^{2}}{6}\right)L+\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\bar{\Delta}\ln\frac{\bar{\Delta}}{\lambda^{2}}dx,

(110)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{21}^{T}(m,\omega,q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\omega L+\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}dx\int_{-\omega}^{0}\left(1+\ln\frac{\Delta}{\lambda^{2}}\right)dy+\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{0}^{1}A^{1/2}dx,

(111)

where

\tilde{\Delta}=y^{2}+\tilde{A},~\tilde{A}=m^{2}-\omega^{2}-i\varepsilon
;

\bar{\Delta}=x(x-1)q^{2}+m^{2}-i\varepsilon
;
\Delta=y^{2}+A
,

A=x(x-1)q^{2}+m^{2}-(\omega-xq_{0})^{2}-i\varepsilon
.

L
is defined as

\displaystyle L=\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{D-4}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\gamma_{E}-1-\ln 4\pi\right)\right],

(112)

where
\gamma_{E}
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant
0.5772157
. We
adopt the
\overline{\mathrm{MS}}
scheme to renormalize the loop
integrals, which is equivalent to making use of the following
relation,

\displaystyle\{X\}_{r}=\lim_{D\to 4}\left(X-L\frac{\partial}{\partial L}X\right)+\frac{1}{16\pi^{2}}\lim_{D\to 4}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial D}\frac{\partial}{\partial L}X\right),

(113)

where
\{X\}_{r}
represents the finite part of

.

Appendix C
Estimating the light quark mass with vector meson dominance model

In general, the transition form factor of
V\to P\gamma
at the leading order can be parameterized as follows,

\displaystyle\mu_{\bar{Q}q}^{\prime}=\mathcal{Q}_{\bar{Q}}\frac{1}{\Lambda_{\bar{Q}}}-\mathcal{Q}_{q}\frac{1}{\Lambda_{q}},

(114)

where
\mathcal{Q}_{\bar{Q}}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{q}
denote the charge matrices of
\bar{Q}
and
q
, respectively.
\Lambda_{\bar{Q}}
and
\Lambda_{q}
are
the mass parameters that can be understood as the masses of the constituent quarks in the quark model. Heavy quark symmetry guarantees
\Lambda_{\bar{Q}}\approx m_{\bar{Q}}
(see the discussions in Ref.
Manohar:2000dt
).
However, the photon coupling to the light quark part of the electromagnetic current is not fixed by the heavy quark symmetry, thus the
\Lambda_{q}
is not a “well-defined” constant, its value is largely model dependent to some extent. Here, we adopt the vector meson dominance (VMD) model
Pacetti:2015iqa
;
Colangelo:1993zq
to estimate the value of
\Lambda_{q}
.

In the VMD model, the light quark part of the electromagnetic current
\langle P|J_{\mu}^{\ell}|V\rangle
can be expressed as follows by inserting the light vector resonance
\mathcal{V}
,

\displaystyle\langle P_{a}(p^{\prime})|J_{\mu}^{\ell}(q^{2})|V_{a}(p,\varepsilon_{V})\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ie_{a}\sum_{\mathcal{V},\lambda}\frac{\langle 0|\bar{q}_{a}\gamma_{\mu}q_{a}|\mathcal{V}(q,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{V}}^{\lambda})\rangle}{q^{2}-m_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times\langle P_{a}(p^{\prime})\mathcal{V}(q,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{V}}^{\lambda})|V_{a}(p,\varepsilon_{V})\rangle,

where the
\langle P_{a}\mathcal{V}|V_{a}\rangle
vertex is given in Eq. (
54
) (A diagrammatic presentation of Eq. (
C
) is shown in Fig.
6
). The matrix element
\langle 0|\bar{q}_{a}\gamma_{\mu}q_{a}|\mathcal{V}(q,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{V}}^{\lambda})\rangle
can be calculated by assuming the SU(3) symmetry with

\displaystyle\langle 0|\bar{q}_{a}\gamma_{\mu}q_{a}|\mathcal{V}(q,\varepsilon_{\mathcal{V}}^{\lambda})\rangle=f_{\mathcal{V}}\varepsilon_{\mathcal{V}}^{\mu}\mathrm{Tr}(\mathcal{V}T^{a}),

(116)

where
f_{\mathcal{V}}
and
\varepsilon_{\mathcal{V}}
denote the decay constant and polarization vector of the light vector meson, respectively.
(T^{a})_{lm}=\delta_{al}\delta_{am}
, and
a=1,2,3
for
u,d,s
, respectively. The
f_{\mathcal{V}}
can be determined by the electromagnetic decay
\mathcal{V}\to e^{+}e^{-}
.
f_{\rho}=0.17
GeV
2
for the
\rho
meson, and
f_{\phi}=0.25
GeV
2
for the
\phi
meson
Colangelo:1993zq
.

denotes the light vector meson

2
.

Following the same procedure in obtaining Eq. (
53
), one can get

\displaystyle\Lambda_{q}^{-1}=2\sqrt{2}g_{v}\lambda\sqrt{\frac{m_{V}}{m_{P}}}\frac{f_{\mathcal{V}}}{m_{\mathcal{V}}^{2}},

(117)

where the values of
g_{v}
and
\lambda
are the same as those in Eq. (
61
). One can obtain
\Lambda_{q}
by considering the SU(3) breaking effect in Eq. (
117
), eventually,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{u}=\Lambda_{d}=0.366~\mathrm{GeV},\quad\Lambda_{s}=0.596~\mathrm{GeV}.

(118)

These values are very close to the
m_{u}
,
m_{d}
and
m_{s}
given in Eq. (
IV.2
).

Foldings in relatively hyperbolic groups
Richard Weidmann, Thomas Weller

Abstract
Carrier graphs of groups representing subgroups of a given relatively hyperbolic groups are introduced and a combination theorem for relatively quasi-convex subgroups is proven. Subsequently a theory of folds for such carrier graphs is introduced and finiteness results for subgroups of locally relatively quasiconvex relatively hyperbolic groups and Kleinian groups are established.



Introduction

Generalizing a result of Thurston
[
45
]
formulated in the context of surface subgroups of Kleinian groups, Gromov
[
23
]
established finiteness of conjugacy classes of subgroups of a torsion-free hyperbolic group
G
isomorphic to a given finitely presented one-ended group
H
, a proof using the Rips machine was given in
[
39
]
. Delzant generalized this result in
[
19
]
to hyperbolic groups with torsion and strengthened the result by establishing finiteness of the number of conjugacy classes of images of a given finitely presented group in a hyperbolic group under homomomorphisms that do not factor through one-ended groups.

Dahmani
[
18
]
generalized this result to relatively hyperbolic groups and established finiteness of homomorphic images under the additional assumption that the homomorphism does not have accidental hyperbolics, i.e. does not factor through a group that splits over a subgroup that is mapped to a parabolic subgroup.

Makanin-Razborov diagrams refine the above arguments as they give a complete parametrization of all homomorphisms from a given finitely presented (or even finitely generated) group to a given (toral relatively) hyperbolic group. They rely essentially on the ideas of Makanin
[
34
]
and Razborov
[
37
]
and are in their modern form due to Sela
[
40
]
and Kharlampovich-Myasnikov
[
31
,
32
]
in the case of free groups, see
[
41
]
and
[
38
]
for hyperbolic groups and
[
25
,
24
]
for toral relatively hyperbolic groups.

In the present paper we are interested in the totality of
k
-generated subgroups of a given relatively hyperbolic group, i.e. images of the free group
F_{k}
. The above mentioned methods fail in this context as all homomorphisms factor through a free product. Thus the question is in some sense complementary to the above results.

Clearly no finiteness can be expected, not even of isomorphism classes of
k
-generated subgroups. Examples are the fundamental groups of closed hyperbolic 3-manifolds. Indeed any such manifold
M
has a finite sheeted cover
\tilde{M}
that fiberes over the circle
[
2
]
and therefore
\pi_{1}(M)
has infinitely many finite index subgroups of the same rank which are pairwise non-isomorphic. It turns out that the absence of non-quasiconvex subgroups rules out such phenomena.

In the case of a torsion-free locally quasiconvex hyperbolic group
G
I. Kapovich and the first named author
[
28
]
generalized ideas from
[
47
]
and showed that there are only finitely many conjugacy classes of one-ended
k
-generated subgroups and therefore by Grushko’s theorem only finitely many isomorphism classes of
k
-generated subgroups. The main objective of the present paper is to generalize the results from
[
28
]
in the appropriate way to the relatively hyperbolic setting.

That the finiteness of conjugacy classes of one-ended
k
-generated subgroups does not hold in the relatively hyperbolic setting was already observed by Thurston
[
45
]
. Thurston observed that the fundamental group of a cusped finite volume hyperbolic 3-manifold can contain infintely many conjugacy classes of subgroups isomorphic to to the fundamental group of a fixed closed surface, hower all but finitely many of those must split over a cyclic subgroup
C
contained in the peripheral subgroup corresponding to some cusp, this group is then said to have accidental parabolics. Conversely, if one such surface group is found, then infinitely many can be constructed as in (1) below if the simple closed curve is separating or as in (2) below if it is non-separating.

Suppose that
G
is a group that is hyperbolic relatively to a collection of subgroups
\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}
. The following three settings can (and often do) yield infinitely many conjugacy classes of one-ended isomorphic subgroups of
G
:

1.

Let
H=H_{1}*_{C}H_{2}
be a subgroup of
G
such that
C\leq P_{i}
for some
i
and let
p\in P_{i}\setminus C
such that
p
centralizes
C
. Then
\varphi_{p}:H\to G
given by
\varphi_{p}(h_{1})=h_{1}
for all
h_{1}\in H_{1}
and
\varphi_{p}(h_{2})=ph_{2}p^{-1}
for all
h_{2}\in H_{2}
defines a homomorphism. It can often be ensured using a combination theorem of Martinez-Perdrosa that
\varphi_{p}
is injective and that there are infinitely many such
p
such that images of the
\varphi_{p}
yield isomorphic and pairwise non-conjugate subgroups isomorphic to
H
.

2.

Let
H=H_{1}*C=\langle H,t\mid tct^{-1}=\psi(c)\hbox{ for all }c\in C\rangle
where
C\subset H_{1}\subset G
and
C\subset P_{i}
. Let
p\in P_{i}\setminus C
such that
p
centralizes
C
. Then
\varphi_{p}:H\to G
given by
\varphi_{p}(h_{1})=h_{1}
for all
h_{1}\in H_{1}
and
\varphi_{p}(t)=tp
defines a homomorphism. As in (1) this construction often yields infintely many isomorphic and pairwise non-conjugate subgroups isomorphic to
H
.

3.

H=H_{1}*_{C}Q
with
Q\subset P_{i}
. Let
Q^{\prime}\subset P_{i}
such that
C\subset Q^{\prime}
and that there exists an isomorphism
\psi:Q\to Q^{\prime}
with
\psi|_{C}=\hbox{id}|_{C}
. Then
\varphi_{\psi}:H\to G
given by
\varphi_{\psi}(h_{1})=h_{1}
for all
h_{1}\in H_{1}
and
\varphi_{\psi}(q)=\psi(q)
for all
q\in Q
defines a homomorphism. Again this often yields infinitely many isomorphic and pairwise non-conjugate subgroups isomorphic to
H
.

The homomorphisms discussed in the above cases are sometimes refered to as bending moves. They were introduced in the context of Makanin-Razborov diagrams for toral relatively hyperbolic groups, see Alibegovic
[
3
]
and Groves
[
24
]
.

The strategy in
[
28
]
is to reduce a given generating set to a generating set consisting of elements of bounded length using ideas similar to Nielsen reduction or foldings. In the current paper we follows a similar strategy, however the notions that need to be introduced are significantly more complicated.

This is achieved by the following three steps, here
(G,\mathbb{P})
is a torsion-free group
G
that is hyperbolic relative to a collection of subgroups
\mathbb{P}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}
.

(A)

Define so called
(G,\mathbb{P})
-carrier graphs of groups
\mathcal{A}
representing subgroups of
G
. These consist of a graph of groups
\mathbb{A}
with some additional structure that defines a map
\nu_{\mathcal{A}}:\pi_{1}(\mathbb{A})\to G
. This can be thought of as a variation of Stallings graphs
[
44
]
or of the
\mathbb{A}
-graphs defined in
[
29
]
. We then say that the image of
\nu_{\mathcal{A}}
is represented by
\mathcal{A}
. It is easily verified that any subgroup of
G
is represented by a
(G,\mathbb{P})
-carrier graph of groups.

(B)

Establish sufficient conditions for the map
\nu_{\mathcal{A}}
to be injective. This amounts to establishing a general combination theorem for relatively quasiconvex subgroups of a relatively hyperbolic group, see Theorem
3.2
and Corollary
3.6
. The combination theorem generalizes previous results from Arzhantseva
[
4
,
5
]
and Martinez-Pedrosa
[
35
]
.

(C)

Define folds and related moves that allow the transformation of a given
(G,\mathbb{P})
-carrier graph into one that satisfies the conditions of the combination theorem and represents the same subgroup. This is closely related to Stallings folds for graphs (of groups), see
[
44
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
8
]
and
[
29
]
. We then observe that under the appropriate conditions all
k
-generated subgroups are represented by
(G,\mathbb{P})
-carrier graphs from only finitely many appropriately defined equivalence classes, for details see Theorem
4.10
. The equivalence relation is defined in such a way that isomorphic subgroups that are related by the above bending moves are represented by equivalent
(G,\mathbb{P})
-carrier graphs.

Most of the above mentioned notions and results are too technical to state in the introduction. However, having these tools at hand, finiteness results for subgroups of locally relatively quasiconvex torsion-free relatively hyperbolic groups are easily established. In particular we obtain the following two facts, the first of which applies in particular to limit groups (over free groups):

Theorem 0.1
.

[Corollary
4.12
]Let
G
be a finitely generated torsion-free locally relatively quasiconvex toral relatively hyperbolic group and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of
n
-generated subgroups.

Theorem 0.2
.

[Corollary
4.14
] Let
G
be a finitely generated torsion-free Kleinian group and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

1.

If
G
is of infinite covolume then there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of
n
-generated subgroups.

2.

If
G
is of finite covolume then there are only finitely many isomorphism classes of
n
-generated subgroups of infinite index.

In chapter one we recall basic notions for relatively hyperbolic groups and establish a sufficient condition for a word to be a quasigeodesic. In Chapter 2 we introduce
(G,\mathbb{P})
carrier graphs of groups and study their basic properties before we prove the combination theorem in Section 3. Section 4 is then dedicated to the introduction of folds and the proofs of the finiteness theorems. We conclude the paper with a brief discussion of the case of relatively hyperbolic groups with torsion. The first named author would like to thank Ilya Kapovich for numerous discussions on topics closely related to this paper. Many of these discussions are reflected in the present paper.

1
Preliminaries

In this chapter relatively hyperbolic groups and relatively quasiconvex subgroups are reviewed. Moreover a sufficient condition for a path in the relative Caley graph to be quasi-geodesic is established.

1.1
Relatively hyperbolic groups

Relatively hyperbolic groups we introduced by Gromov
[
23
]
. Various ultimately equivalent definitions have since appeared in the literature
[
21
,
12
,
26
]
, see
[
27
]
for a comparison of the various notions. The discussion in this paper follows Osin
[
36
]
.

Definition 1.1
(Osin
[
36
]
, Definition 2.35).

Let
G
be a group. A finite set
\mathbb{P}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}
of subgroups of
G
is called a
peripheral structure of
G
and the elements of the
P_{i}
are called
peripheral
. Any element conjugate to some peripheral element is called
parabolic
.

Let
\langle X,P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\mid R\rangle
be a finite relative presentation of
(G,\mathbb{P})
. For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
let
\tilde{P}_{i}
be an isomorphic copy of
P_{i}
, such that
\tilde{P}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{P}_{n},X
are mutually disjoint. Let
\mathcal{P}:=\bigcup(\tilde{P}_{i}\setminus\{1\})
. Any word in
X\cup X^{-1}\cup\mathcal{P}
can be interpreted as an element of
F:=F(X)\ast P_{1}\ast\ldots\ast P_{n}
and as an element of
G
in the obvious way.

G
is
hyperbolic relative to
\mathbb{P}
, or
(G,\mathbb{P})
is relatively hyperbolic, if the relative Dehn function of this presentation is linear, i.e. if there exists
C>0
, such that any for word
w
in
X\cup X^{-1}\cup\mathcal{P}
of length at most
l
representing the trivial element in
G
the element
\bar{w}\in F
represented by
w
can be written as the product of at most
C\cdot l
conjugates of elements of
R
in
F
.

Note that
X
is only required to be a relative generating set of
G
in Definition
1.1
. In the remainder of this paper
X
is always a generating set, in particular all relatively hyperbolic groups under consideration will be finitely generated.

For any path
s
the initial vertex of
s
is denoted by
s_{-}
and the terminal vertex by
s_{+}
. Any vertex of
s
that is distinct from
s_{-}
and
s_{+}
is called an inner vertex of
s
.
The following terminology was introduced by Osin
[
36
]
:

Definition 1.2
.

A subpath of a path
p
in
\hbox{Cay}(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
is called a
P_{i}
-component
of
p
, if it is a maximal non-trivial subpath, that is labeled by letters in
\tilde{P}_{i}
. We call a subpath of a path

a
\mathcal{P}
-component
or
a peripheral component
if it is a
P_{i}
-component for some
i
.

Every vertex of
p
which is not an inner vertex of some
P_{i}
-component is called a
phase vertex
of
p
.

A path is
locally minimal
if every
P_{i}
-component has length
1
.

Two
P_{i}
-components are said to be
connected
if there is an edge labeled by an element of
P_{i}
connecting them. A
P_{i}
-component of a path
p
is
isolated
, if it is not connected to another
P_{i}
-component of
p
.

A path
p
is
without backtracking
if all its components are isolated.

Osin showed that the relative Cayley graph of a relatively hyperbolic group is Gromov hyperbolic and that it fulfill the BCP-property introduced by Farb
[
21
]
. Thus Osin’s notion of relative hyperbolicity implies Farb’s notion of (strong) relative hyperbolicity.

Lemma 1.3
(Osin
[
36
]
, Theorem 3.23 and Theorem 3.26).

Let
(G,\mathbb{P})
be a relatively hyperbolic group and

. Let
\lambda\geq 1
,

.

Then
\Cay(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
is Gromov hyperbolic and there exists a constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(G,\mathbb{P},X,\lambda,C)
(or simply

(\lambda,C)
-quasigeodesics

with the same initial and terminal vertex:

1.

The sets of phase vertices of p and q are contained in the closed
\varepsilon

) of each other.

2.

Suppose that
s
is a
P_{i}
–component of
p
such that
d_{X}(s_{-},s_{+})>\varepsilon
. Then there exists a
P_{i}
–component
t
of
q
which is connected to
s
.

3.

Suppose that
s
and
t
are connected
P_{i}
–components of

Then

\max\{d_{X}(s_{-},t_{-}),d_{X}(s_{+},t_{+})\}\leq\varepsilon.

The following definition gives two different notions of length for paths in the relative Cayley graph of a relatively hyperbolic group.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
(G,\mathbb{P})
be a relatively hyperbolic group with finite generating set
X
. For any path
s=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{k})
in
\Cay(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
,
\len(s):=k
is called the
length
of
s
.

The
X
-length
of
s
is defined as

\len_{X}(s):=\sum_{i=1}^{k}d_{X}(\alpha(e_{i}),\omega(e_{i})).

If
s
is a piecewise geodesic, i.e. there is a tuple
\mathfrak{s}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l})
of geodesics in
\Cay(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
with
s=s_{1}\ldots s_{l}
, the
piecewise
X
-length
of
s
is defined as

\len_{X}^{p}(\mathfrak{s}):=\sum_{i=1}^{l}d_{X}((s_{i})_{-},(s_{i})_{+}).

If
\mathfrak{s}
is clear from the context, write
\len_{X}^{p}(s)
for
\len_{X}^{p}(\mathfrak{s})
.

There are also natural notions of lengths for words and group elements. For some set
X
and some word
w\in X^{\ast}
over
X
,
\lvert w\rvert
will denote the number of letters in
w
.

For some group
G
with generating set
X
and some element
g\in G
,
\lvert g\rvert_{X}
denotes the word length of
g
with respect to
X
, i.e. the length of some shortest word in
X\cup X^{-1}
representing
g
.

In a common abuse of notation, a word

will often be identified with the element which it represents. So, while
\lvert w\rvert
denotes the length of
w
,
\lvert w\rvert_{X}
denotes the word length with respect to
X
of the element represented by
w
. The label of a path
s
in a Cayley graph, denoted by
\lab(s)
is the group element
s_{-}^{-1}s_{+}
, i.e. the element represented by the word read by
s
.

1.2
Relatively quasiconvex subgroups

A natural class of subgroups of a relatively hyperbolic group
(G,\mathbb{P})
are the relatively quasiconvex subgroups.

Definition 1.5
(Osin
[
36
]
, Def. 4.9).

Let
(G,\mathbb{P})
be a relatively hyperbolic group and
X
a finite generating system of
G
. Let
\nu\geq 0
.
H\leq G
is called a
\nu
-relatively quasiconvex subgroup
of
(G,\mathbb{P})
with respect to
X
, if every vertex of a geodesic
p
in
\Gamma(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
with endpoints in
H
has at most a
d_{X}
-distance of
\nu
from
H
.

If
X
and
\mathbb{P}
are clear from the context,
H
is simply called a
\nu
-relatively quasiconvex subgroup of
G
. Moreover,
H
is called a relatively quasiconvex subgroup of
G
, if it is a
\nu
-relatively quasiconvex subgroup for some
\nu\geq 0
.

Osin (
[
36
]
, Prop. 4.10) shows that the definition of a relatively quasiconvex subgroup does not depend on the choice of
X
. If
Y
is another finite generating set of
G
and
H
is a
\nu
-relatively quasiconvex subgroup of
G
with respect to
X
, then it is a
\nu^{\prime}
-relatively quasiconvex subgroup of
G
with respect to
Y
for some
\nu^{\prime}\geq 0
.

Relatively quasiconvex subgroups inherit the geometric structure of
G
and are themselves hyperbolic relative to a peripheral structure that is induced by
\mathbb{P}
.

Theorem & Definition 1.6
(Hruska
[
27
]
, Thm. 9.1).

Let
(G,\mathbb{P})
be relatively hyperbolic and
H
a relatively quasiconvex subgroup of
G
.

Then the set

\bar{\mathbb{O}}=\{H\cap P^{g}\mid g\in G,P\in\mathbb{P},\lvert H\cap P^{g}\rvert=\infty\}

consists of finitely many
H
-conjugacy classes of subgroups of
H
. For any set of representatives
\mathbb{O}
of these conjugacy classes,
(H,\mathbb{O})
is relatively hyperbolic. Any such peripheral structure
\mathbb{O}
is called an
induced structure
of
(G,\mathbb{P})
on
H
.

Moreover, the inclusion
(H,d_{Y\cup\mathcal{O}})\to(G,d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}})
is a quasiisometric embedding for any finite relative generating set
Y
of
(H,\mathbb{O})
.

Now let
(G,\mathbb{P})
be a relatively hyperbolic group with symmetric finite generating set
X
, let
Y\subseteq X^{*}
be a generating set of some subgroup
H\leq G
and let
\mathbb{O}=\{O_{1},\ldots,O_{m}\}
where each
O_{j}
is a subgroup of
H\cap P_{i_{j}}^{g_{j}}
with
1\leq i_{j}\leq n
and
g_{j}\in X^{*}
. Then there is a canonical way of mapping each word in
(Y\cup\mathcal{O})^{*}
to some word in
(X\cup\mathcal{P})^{*}
representing the same group element.

Definition 1.7
.

The
canonical map

\iota\colon(Y\cup\mathcal{O})^{*}\to(X\cup\mathcal{P})^{*}
is defined as the canonical extension of the map
Y\cup\mathcal{O}\to(X\cup\mathcal{P})^{*}
that maps each element of
Y\subseteq(X\cup\mathcal{P})^{*}
to itself and that maps every
o\in O_{j}
to the unique word
g_{j}^{-1}pg_{j}\in(X\cup\mathcal{P})^{*}
representing
o
with
p\in P_{i_{j}}
.

A finitely generated subgroup of a hyperbolic group is quasiconvex, if and only if the inclusion of the subgroup is a quasiisometric embedding. Theorem & Definition
1.6
generalizes the forward implication by stating that for a relatively quasiconvex subgroup
H
of
G
with induced structure
\mathbb{O}
the inclusion
(H,d_{Y\cup\mathcal{O}})\to(G,d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}})
is a quasiisometric embedding.

The following theorem and its proof are straightforward generalizations of a theorem by Osin (
[
36
]
, Thm. 4.13) and can be viewed as generalizations of the converse implication in the above statement.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
(G,\mathbb{P})
be a relatively hyperbolic group where
\mathbb{P}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}
and let
X
be a symmetric finite generating set of
G
. Let
H\leq G
be a subgroup with finite generating set
Y\subseteq X^{*}
.

Let
\mathbb{O}=\{O_{1},\ldots,O_{m}\}
such that for each
1\leq j\leq m
there is some
1\leq i_{j}\leq n
and
g_{j}\in X^{*}
with
O_{j}\subseteq H\cap P_{i_{j}}^{g_{j}}
.

Suppose the inclusion
(H,d_{Y\cup\mathcal{O}})\to(G,d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}})
is a
(\lambda,c)
-quasiisometric embedding.

Then
H
is relatively quasiconvex in
G
.

Proof
.

Let

\mu:=\max(\{\lvert y\rvert\mid y\in Y\}\cup\{2\lvert g_{j}\rvert+1\mid 1\leq j\leq m\}).

Let
h\in H
and
V=z_{1}\ldots z_{l}\in(Y\cup\mathcal{O})^{*}
be a minimal word representing
h
, i.e.
l=\lvert h\rvert_{Y\cup\mathcal{O}}
.

Define
U:=\iota(V)
, and let
U_{0}
be a subword of
U
, i.e.

U_{0}=A\iota(z_{r})\ldots\iota(z_{r+s})B

where
A
is trivial or a suffix of
\iota(z_{r-1})
and
B
is trivial or a prefix of
\iota(z_{r+s+1})
. In particular
\lvert A\rvert,\lvert B\rvert\leq\mu
.

Since every subword of
V
is geodesic, the following holds:

\displaystyle\lvert U_{0}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq 2\mu+\mu(s+1)

\displaystyle=2\mu+\mu\lvert z_{r}\ldots z_{r+s}\rvert_{Y\cup\mathcal{O}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\mu+\mu(\lambda\lvert z_{r}\ldots z_{r+s}\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}+\lambda c)

\displaystyle\leq 2\mu+\mu(\lambda(\lvert U_{0}\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}+2\mu)+\lambda c)

Therefore, the path
p
in
\Gamma(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
from
1
to
h
, which is labeled by
U
, is a
(\mu\lambda,2\mu+2\mu^{2}\lambda+\mu\lambda c)
-quasigeodesic.

Assume
p
has connected
P_{i}
-components. Then there is some subword
p_{1}wp_{2}
of
U
for some
w\in(X\cup\mathcal{P})^{\ast}
and
p_{1},p_{2}\in P_{i}
, which represents an element
p_{3}\in P_{i}
. Replace the subpath of
p
labeled by
p_{1}wp_{2}
with the single edge connecting its endpoints and labeled by
p_{3}
. This new path is still a
(\mu\lambda,2\mu+2\mu^{2}\lambda+\mu\lambda c)
-quasigeodesic and its vertex set is a subset of the vertex set of
p
.

Repeating this process eventually yields a
(\mu\lambda,2\mu+2\mu^{2}\lambda+\mu\lambda c)
-quasigeodesic
\bar{p}
without backtracking, whose vertex set is a subset of the vertex set of
p
.

For any geodesic
q
in
\Gamma(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
from
1
to
h
and any vertex
v
of
q
it follows with
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\mu\lambda,2\mu+2\mu^{2}\lambda+\mu\lambda c)
as in the conclusion of Lemma
1.3
, that there exists a vertex

of
\bar{p}
such that:

d_{X}(u,v)\leq\varepsilon.

Since
d_{X}(u,H)\leq\mu
for any vertex
u
of
p
and therefore in particular for any vertex of
\bar{p}
, it follows that
d_{X}(v,H)\leq\varepsilon+\mu
. Hence,

is relatively quasiconvex as in Definition
1.5
with
\nu:=\varepsilon+\mu
.

\square

1.3
Elements of Infinite Order in Relatively Hyperbolic Groups

This section records several simple results on the action of elements of infinite order on
\Cay(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
.

Definition 1.12
(cf. Osin
[
36
]
, Def. 4.23).

Let
(G,\mathbb{P})
be a relatively hyperbolic group and
X
a symmetric finite generating set of
G
. Let
g\in G
. The
translation length
\tau(g)
of
g
is defined by:

\tau(g):=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\frac{\lvert g^{n}\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}}{n}.

In the following we call an element of
G

hyperbolic
if it acts hyperbolically on the Cayley graph
\Cay(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
. In the torsion-free setting this is equivalent to being non-parabolic.

Remark 1.13
.

The limit inferior in the above definition is in fact a limit. The argument is the same as for the non-relative case which was proven by Gersten and Short (
[
22
]
, Lemma 6.3).

Note further that
\tau(hgh^{-1})=\tau(g)
for all
g,h\in G
and that
g
is a hyperbolic if and only if
\tau(g)>0
.

The next lemma follows from the fact, that there is a lower bound on the translation lengths of hyperbolic elements in a relatively hyperbolic group (
[
36
]
, Thm. 4.25). Recall that the axis of an element acting hyperbolically on a hyperbolic space is the union of all geodesics between its two fixed points in the ideal boundary at infinity.

Lemma 1.14
.

Let
(G,\mathbb{P})
be a relatively hyperbolic group,
X
a symmetric finite generating set of
G
. Then there exists
D_{1}=D_{1}(G,\mathbb{P},X)
such that the following holds:

Let
h\in G
be hyperbolic of infinite order with axis
Y
in
\Cay(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
. Then for every
z\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
:

\lvert z\rvert\tau(h)+2d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}(1,Y)-D_{1}\leq\lvert h^{z}\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}\leq\lvert z\rvert\tau(h)+2d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}(1,Y)+D_{1}

Proof
.

Since the axis of a non-trivial power of a hyperbolic element
h
is the same as the axis of
h
and since
\tau(h^{z})=\lvert z\rvert\tau(h)
for all
z\in\mathbb{Z}
, it suffices to show the claim for
z=1
.

Let
\delta\geq 0
be such that
\Cay(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
is
\delta
-hyperbolic. Let
h\in G
be hyperbolic of infinite order with axis
Y
in
\Cay(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
. Let
g\in VY
be of minimal
X\cup\mathcal{P}
-length, i.e. a projection of
1
onto
Y
.

Since
g\in VY
,
d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}(g,hg)\leq\tau(h)+C
for some constant
C
independent of
g
and
h
. It therefore suffices to show that the difference of lengths of the (piecewise) geodesic paths
[1,h]
and
[1,g]\cup[g,hg]\cup[hg,h]
is bounded by a constant independent of
h
.

If
\tau(h)\geq 100\delta
, the claim follows from the minimality of
g
and the
2\delta
-thinness of the quadrilateral
[1,g]\cup[g,hg]\cup[hg,h]\cup[h,1]
.

Thus, it can be assumed that
\tau(h)<100\delta
. Let
x\in[1,g]
with
d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}(x,g)=d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}(Y,[1,h])
. Then
d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}(x,hx)\leq\eta
and therefore
d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}(h^{n-1}x,h^{n}x)\leq\eta
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
for some constant
\eta=\eta(\delta)
.

Applying the fact that detours in a hyperbolic space are exponentially large (see Lemma 1.8 in
[
16
]
) to the path

\gamma=[g,x]\cup[x,hx]\cup[hx,h^{2}x]\cup\ldots\cup[h^{N-1}x,h^{N}x]\cup[h^{N}x,h^{N}g]

for an appropriate
N\in\mathbb{N}
yields that
d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}(Y,[1,h])
must be bounded by some constant depending only on
\delta
and
\tau(h)
, such that this bound is monotonically decreasing in
\tau(h)
.

Figure 1
:
The path
\gamma=[g,x]\cup[x,hx]\cup[hx,h^{2}x]\cup\ldots\cup[h^{N}x,h^{N}g]
cannot be far from
Y
.

By Osin (
[
36
]
, Thm. 4.25), there is some
d=d(G,\mathbb{P},X)>0
such that
\tau(h)\geq d
for all hyperbolic
h\in H
. Hence, there is an upper bound on
d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}(Y,[1,h])
independent of
h
.

Since
\tau(h)
was assumed to be at most
100\delta
, this implies the claim.

\square

Lemma 1.15
.

Let
(G,\mathbb{P})
be a relatively hyperbolic group,
X
a symmetric finite generating set of
G
. Then there exists
D_{2}=D_{2}(G,\mathbb{P},X)
such that the following holds:

Let

2d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}(1,gP_{i})-D_{2}\leq\lvert h\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}\leq 2d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}(1,gP_{i})+1

Proof
.

Let
\delta\geq 0
such that the Cayley graph
\Cay(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
is
\delta
-hyperbolic. Let
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(2,12\delta)
be the constant from Lemma
1.3
. Let
h=gpg^{-1}
, where
p\in P_{i}
and
g
is of minimal
X\cup\mathcal{P}
-length in
gP_{i}
.

Suppose first that
\lvert p\rvert_{X}\geq\varepsilon
.
Let
w\in(X\cup\mathcal{P})^{\ast}
be a geodesic word representing
g
,
t
be a path in
\Cay(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
labeled by
wpw^{-1}
and
e
be the edge of
t
with label
p
. As
g
is of minimal
X\cup\mathcal{P}
-length in
gP_{i}
,

is an isolated
P_{i}
-component of

.

We claim that any subpath

of

that is a
(2,12\delta)
-quasigeodesic is in fact a geodesic. Let
s
be such a subpath of
t
. If
s
does not contain

then

is a geodesic as a subpath of a geodesic. Hence, assume

contains
e
. Let
s^{\prime}
be a geodesic from
s_{-}
to
s_{+}
. Since

(2,12\delta)

Trivially, any subpath

of

-quasigeodesic, thus

\lvert gpg^{-1}\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}=2\lvert g\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}+1=2d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}(1,gP_{i})+1.

Now suppose

. Since

is finite, there are only finitely many such

by considering a quadrilateral with vertices

in

and noting that

,

\square

The following lemma shows, that the length of the product of an element which is essentially orthogonal to a relatively quasiconvex subgroup with an element of this subgroup is the sum of the lengths of the two elements, up to an additive constant.

Lemma 1.16
.

Let
(G,\mathbb{P})
be a relatively hyperbolic group,
X
a finite generating set of
G
. Let
\delta\geq 0
such that
\Cay(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
is
\delta
-hyperbolic and
H
a
\nu
-relatively quasiconvex subgroup of
G
. Let
g\in G
such that
g
has minimal
X\cup\mathcal{P}
-length in
gH
and let

. Then:

\lvert gh\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}\geq\lvert g\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}+\lvert h\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}-8\delta-2\nu

The non-relative version of Lemma
1.16
was proven by Arzhantseva (
[
4
]
, Lemma 9). The proof uses only the hyperbolicity of the Cayley graph and the fact that a quasiconvex subgroup of a hyperbolic group is a quasiconvex subset of the Cayley graph. Hence, it immediately generalizes to the relatively hyperbolic case. Note that in
[
4
]
a slightly different but equivalent definition of hyperbolicity is used, which is why the constants also differ slightly.

Combining all the previous lemmas of this section yields the following.

Lemma 1.17
.

Let
(G,\mathbb{P})
be a relatively hyperbolic group,
X
a finite generating set of
G
and
H

\nu
-relatively quasiconvex subgroup of

. Then there exists a

\lvert ghg^{-1}\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}\geq\lvert h\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}+2\lvert g\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}-D

Proof
.

Let
D_{1}=D_{1}(G,\mathbb{P},X)
and

1.14
and Lemma

. Let
\delta\geq 0
such that
\Cay(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
is
\delta
-hyperbolic. Let
h\in H
be of infinite order and
g
of minimal
X\cup\mathcal{P}
-length in
gH
.

Suppose
h
is hyperbolic, let
Y
be the axis of
h
in
\Cay(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
and

such that
\lvert gx\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}=d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}(1,gx)=d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}(1,gY)

-neighborhood of a geodesic connecting two elements of
\langle h\rangle\subset H
. As

is

-relatively quasiconvex, this implies that there is some

1.16
implies:

\displaystyle d_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}(1,gY)

\displaystyle=\lvert gx\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}

\displaystyle\geq\lvert gk\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}-2\delta-\nu

\displaystyle\geq\lvert g\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}+\lvert k\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}-10\delta-3\nu

\displaystyle\geq\lvert g\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}+\lvert x\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}-12\delta-4\nu

1.4
Paths with Long Peripheral Edges

The technical lemma proven in this section is one of the main tools for the proof of the first combination theorem (Theorem
3.2
). A similar result has been given by Martínez-Pedrosa (
[
35
]
, Prop. 3.1). It states, that a path
s=s_{0}p_{1}s_{1}\ldots s_{k-1}p_{k}s_{k}
in
\Cay(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
, where the
s_{j}
are geodesics and where the
p_{j}
are long peripheral components such that
p_{j-1}
and
p_{j}
are not connected for
2\leq j\leq k
, is a quasigeodesic.

The main differences between their result and the following lemma is that the
s_{j}
are only required to be quasigeodesics. These weaker assumptions necessitate stronger restrictions on the backtracking that is allowed to occur.

Lemma 1.18
.

Let
(G,\mathbb{P})
be a relatively hyperbolic group with symmetric finite generating set
X
. For any

,
\varepsilon_{1}\geq 0
let
\varepsilon:=\varepsilon(G,\mathbb{P},X,1,K)
be the constant from Lemma
1.3
and let
\varepsilon_{2}:=2^{K}(K\varepsilon+\varepsilon_{1})
. Then the following holds:

Let

s=s_{0}p_{1}s_{1}\ldots s_{k-1}p_{k}s_{k}

be a path of length at most
K
in
\Cay(G,X\cup\mathcal{P})
, such that the following conditions are satisfied:

1.

The
p_{j}
are
\mathbb{P}
-edges of
s
with
\len_{X}(p_{j})>\varepsilon_{2}
,

2.

every
s_{j}
is a
(C,C)
-quasigeodesic, and

3.

if
ps^{\prime}p^{\prime}
is a subpath of
s
with

(a)

p
and
p^{\prime}
are
P_{i}
-paths,

(b)

\len_{X}(p),\len_{X}(p^{\prime})\geq\varepsilon_{1}
, and

(c)

\len_{X}(s^{\prime})\leq\varepsilon_{2}

then
\lab(s^{\prime})\notin P_{i}
.

Then
s
is a
(C^{2}+3C,C^{2}+3C)
-quasigeodesic.

Proof
.

Let
s=s_{0}p_{1}s_{1}\ldots s_{k-1}p_{k}s_{k}
be a path of length at most

in

fulfilling 1.-3. Since 1.-3. are inherited by subpaths, it suffices to show that

\lvert\lab(s)\rvert_{X\cup\mathcal{P}}\geq\frac{\len(s)}{C^{2}+3C}-(C^{2}+3C).

It can further be assumed w.l.o.g. that the
p_{j}
are precisely the
\mathbb{P}
-edges of

with
\len_{X}(p_{j})>\varepsilon_{2}
.

The following lemma is the main step in the proof of Lemma
1.18
. It implies in particular that for any

any geodesic

from
s_{-}
to
s_{+}
contains a peripheral edge connected to

.

Lemma 1.19
.

There is some
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
N\leq\len(s)-1\leq K-1
and paths

to

is without backtracking, and for each

1.

Every

Carrier Graphs of groups

It is often useful to represent subgroups of a given group
G
by graphs labeled by generators of
G
, in the case of subgroups of free groups these are just Stallings graphs
[
44
]
. Representing subgroups by labeled graphs has further proven fruitful in studying free subgroups of small cancellation groups and more generally hyperbolic groups, see e.g.
[
6
,
5
]
.

For subgroups of a fundamental group

of a closed hyperbolic manifold

a related notion has been introduced by White

are graphs

together with a
\pi_{1}
-surjective map

and the rank of
\pi_{1}(M)
. Carrier graphs have been employed successfully by Biringer and Souto
[
11
]
and
[
43
]
to study the ranks of the fundamental groups of mapping tori of closed orientable surfaces, see also
[
9
]
and
[
10
]
for results related to the results of this paper.

The
(G,\mathbb{P})
-carrier graph of groups introduced in this chapter are geared towards studying subgroups of relatively hyperbolic groups.

Let

4
Folds and applications

The purpose of this chapter is two-fold. Firstly a version of Stallings folds are introduced that can be applied to
(G,\mathbb{P})
-carrier graphs of groups that do not induce a quasi-isometric embedding on the group level and secondly those folds are used to prove finiteness results for locally quasiconvex subgroups of a given torsion free relatively hyperbolic group with Noetherian parabolic subgroups.

4.1
Equivalence classes of carrier graphs of groups

To formulate the finiteness results in Section
4.3
we need the appropriate equivalence classes of carrier graphs of groups.

The following trivial facts are used implicitly in the subsequent discussion:

1.

If
e
is an essential edge with
\alpha(e)\in C_{i}
then
\alpha_{e}(A_{e})
is a subgroup of

If all peripheral subgroups are Abelian then

Definition 4.1
.

Let

be a torsion-free relatively hyperbolic group.
Two

-carrier graphs without trivial edge groups

and

for

.

2.

for all non-peripheral

, and

3.

for all non-peripheral

.

If in addition for any

there exists an isomorphism

such that
\eta_{i}(\alpha_{e}(A_{e}))
is conjugate to
\alpha_{f(e)}(B_{f(e)})
for any essential edge
e
with
\alpha(e)\in VC_{i}
then
(\mathcal{A},((\mathcal{C}_{i},c_{i}))_{1\leq i\leq k})
and

.

It is immediate that the fundamental groups of the graphs of groups underlying strongly equivalent

We record the following consequence of the classification of finitely generated Abelian groups.

5
Torsion

The theory developed in this article is, similarly to that in
[
28
]
, mainly geared towards torsion-free groups. This route was taken as the notions are quite technical as they are and as dealing with torsion would have further complicated matters. We conclude this article by commenting on the changes that are necessary to encompass torsion.

The problem is not torsion itself but torsion elements with infinite centralizers, the absence of such torsion elements was called almost torsion-free in
[
28
]
. If torsion elements have infinite centralizers then the conclusion of various lemmas of Section
1.3
and in particular Lemma
1.17
may fail. To deal with this situation one needs to allow finite edge groups for non-essential non-peripheral edge groups, these edge groups will have infinite centralizers. The proof of finiteness results similar to those in Chapter
4
would then need a combination of the arguments of the present paper and those of the folding proofs of Linnell accessibility
[
33
]
, see
[
20
]
and
[
48
]
.

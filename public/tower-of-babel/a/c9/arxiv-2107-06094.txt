Long time behaviors for the inhomogeneous NLS with a potential in ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3}
Fanfei Meng, Sheng Wang, Chengbin Xu

Abstract
. In this article, we aim to study the scattering of the solution to the focusing inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation with a potential of form i ​ ∂ t u + Δ ​ u − V ​ u = − | x | − b ​ | u | p − 1 ​ u \displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u-Vu=-|x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}u in the energy space H 1 ​ ( ℝ 3 ) H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}) . We prove a scattering criterion, and then we use it together with Morawetz estimate to show the scattering theory, which generalizes the results of Dinh [ 6 ] to the non-radial symmetric case. Key Words: Nonlinear Schrödinger equation; Kato class potential; Virial-Morawetz estimate; Scattering theory.



1.
Introduction

We consider the Cauchy problem of the inhomogeneous nonlinear Schrödinger equation
with a potential:

(
\rm{INLS_{V}}
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u-Vu=-|x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}u,\ \ \ \ (t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\\
&u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\end{cases}

where
u:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,
p>1
,
0<b<1
,
and
V
is a real-valued potential satisfying

(1.1)

\displaystyle V\in{\mathcal{K}_{0}}\cap L^{\frac{3}{2}},

and

(1.2)

\displaystyle\|V_{-}\|_{\mathcal{K}_{0}}<4\pi.

Here,
\mathcal{K}_{0}
denotes the closed space
of bounded and compactly supported functions
endowed with the Kato norm

\|V\|_{\mathcal{K}_{0}}:=\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|V(y)|}{|x-y|}{\rm d}y

with
V_{-}:=\min\{V,0\}
being the negative part of
V
.
We recall from
[
6
,
17
]
that
the operator
\mathcal{H}:=-\Delta+V
has no eigenvalues,
and the dispersive and Strichartz estimates hold
for the corresponding Schrödinger flow
\{e^{-it\mathcal{H}}\}

under the conditions (
1.1
) and (
1.2
).

It is known that solutions to (

INLS
V

) with suitable regularity
conserve the mass and energy, defined respectively by

M(u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u(t,x)|^{2}{\rm d}x=M(u_{0}),

E(u):=\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla u(t,x)|^{2}{\rm d}x+\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}V(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}{\rm d}x-\displaystyle\frac{1}{p+1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|u(t,x)|^{p+1}}{|x|^{b}}{\rm d}x=E(u_{0}).

Firstly, let us recall the existence results for
the inhomogenous Schrödinger equation without potentials:

(
\rm{INLS}
)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}&i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=\lambda|x|^{-b}|u|^{p-1}u,\ \ \ \ (t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
&u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),\end{cases}

where
\lambda=1
(resp.
\lambda=-1
) corresponds to the defocusing(resp. focusing) case.
Equation (

INLS

) is invariant under the scaling

u_{\lambda}(x,t):=\lambda^{\frac{2-b}{p-1}}u(\lambda x,\lambda^{2}t),\quad\lambda>0

which preserves the

\dot{H}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-norm with
s_{c}:=\frac{d}{2}-\frac{2-b}{p-1}.

Thus, the equation (

INLS

) is called
\dot{H}^{s_{c}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-critical.
The case where

(resp.

and the case where

Genoud-Stuart
[
13

firstly obtained the

[

Later, Guzmán

Theorem 1.1

(Scattering criterion)
.

For
0<b<1
,
1+\frac{4-2b}{3}<p<1+4-2b
, and
V:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

satisfies (
1.1
), (
1.2
) in (

INLS
V

).
If

is a solution to (
1.1
) defined on
[0,+\infty)
with the following priori bound

(1.4)

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t}\|u(t)\|_{H^{1}}:=E<+\infty,

then there exist two constants

and
\epsilon>0
,
depending only on
E
,
p
and b (but not on

or

),
such that if

(1.5)

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{t\to+\infty}\displaystyle\int_{B(0,R)}|u(t,x)|^{2}{\rm d}x\leqslant\epsilon^{2},

then u scatters forward in time in

,
that is,
there exists a function

such that

\liminf_{t\to+\infty}\|u(t)-e^{-it\mathcal{H}}u_{+}\|_{H^{1}}=0.

Next, we formulate the scattering result
for the solutions to (

INLS
V

).
We shall use the Generalized Sobolev norms

(1.6)

\displaystyle\|\Gamma f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}:=\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla f|^{2}{\rm d}x+\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}V|f|^{2}{\rm d}x.

Theorem 1.2

(Scattering)
.

Consider equation (

INLS
V

) with
0<b<1
and

Assume that

satisfies (

),
and in addition

,

,

x\cdot\nabla V\in L^{r}
, where

.
Let

satisfy

(1.7)

\displaystyle E(u_{0})[M(u_{0})]^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}<\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}\|Q\|^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}_{L^{2}}.

Then, if

\displaystyle\|\Gamma u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\|u_{0}\|^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}_{L^{2}}<\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}\|Q\|^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}_{L^{2}},

we have that the corresponding solution

to (

INLS
V

) exists globally in time
and satisfies

(1.9)

\displaystyle\|\Gamma u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|u(t)\|^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}_{L^{2}}<\|\nabla Q\|_{L^{2}}\|Q\|^{\frac{1-s_{c}}{s_{c}}}_{L^{2}}

Remark 1.3
.

In fact, the Theorem
1.2
not only extends the results of Dinh
[
6
]
to the non-radial case but also extends the integrability condition of
x\cdot\nabla V\in L^{r}
to any
r\in[\frac{3}{2},\infty)
. Because of the absence of translation symmetry in the potential, the non-radial case cannot be achieved directly by the method of Miao–Murphy–Zheng
[
22
]
and Dodson–Murphy
[
9
]
. Here, we construct a special class of cut off functions whose derivatives are bounded and do not depend on compact set
B_{R}(0)
, and use the decay of the factor
|x|^{-b}
in the nonlinear term which can replace the radial Sobolev embedding in some sense. Based on this, we establish the non-radial scattering criterion and more complicated Morawetz estimate with potential for non-radial. In the Morawetz estimate, we find that the error term

\displaystyle\left|\int_{|x|\geqslant\frac{R}{2}}\nabla a\cdot\nabla V|u|^{2}{\rm d}x\right|\leqslant C\|x\cdot\nabla V\|_{L^{r}(|x|\geqslant\frac{R}{2})}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2r^{\prime}}}

tends to
0
as
R\to\infty
. From this, we can extend the integrability of
x\cdot\nabla V\in L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
to
r\in[\frac{3}{2},\infty)
and generalize the results of Dinh
[
6
]
to the non-radial symmetric case.

4.
Proof of the scattering criterion

In this section, we will prove Theorem
1.1
, which reduces the proof of Theorem
1.2
to the verfication of condition (
1.5
) on local mass in long time.
The history of Theorem
1.1
comes back to Dodson-Murphy
[
9
]
for classical nonlinear Schrödinger equation in 3D and then F. Meng
[
21
]
for the nonlinear Hartree equation in 5D.
In
[
3
]
, it was proved for the (

INLS

) without potential.
For the (

INLS
V

) with a potential, it was proved in
[
6
]
under the radial assumption.
Here, we prove the result for non-radial initial data and the full subcritical range in dimension three.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
0<b<1
and
1+\frac{4-2b}{3}<p<1+4-2b.
Let
V:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

satisfy (
1.1
), (
1.2
) and
u
be a (possibly non-radial)
H^{1}
-solution to (
1.4
). If
u
satisfies (
1.5
) for some
0<\epsilon<1
, then there exist
\gamma
,
T>0
such that

\|e^{-i(t-T)\mathcal{H}}u(T)\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s_{c}},[0,+\infty))}\lesssim\epsilon^{\gamma}.

Proof.

First, we fix the parameters
\alpha,\gamma>0
(to be chosen later). By Strichartz estimate, there exists
T_{0}>\epsilon^{-\alpha}
such that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|e^{-it\mathcal{H}}u_{0}\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s_{c}},[T_{0},+\infty))}\leqslant\epsilon^{\gamma}.

For
T\geqslant T_{0}
to be chosen later, define
I_{1}:=[T-\epsilon^{-\alpha},T]
,
I_{2}:=[0,T-\epsilon^{-\alpha}]
and let
\eta
denote a smooth, spherically symmetric function which equals 1 on
B(0,1/2)
and 0 outside
B(0,1)
.
Set
\eta_{R}(x):=\eta(x/R)
for any
R>0
.

From Duhamel’s formula

u(T)=e^{iT\mathcal{H}}u_{0}-i\int_{0}^{T}e^{i(t-s)\mathcal{H}}\frac{|u|^{p-1}u}{|x|^{b}}{\rm d}s,

we obtain

e^{i(t-T)\mathcal{H}}u(T)=e^{-it\mathcal{H}}u_{0}+iG_{1}+iG_{2},

where

\displaystyle G_{j}(t):=\int_{I_{j}}e^{i(t-s)\mathcal{H}}\frac{|u|^{p-1}u}{|x|^{b}}{\rm d}s,\qquad j=1,2.

Estimate on
G_{1}
.

By hypothesis (
1.5
), we can fix
T\geqslant T_{0}
such that

(4.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\eta_{R}(x)|u(T,x)|^{2}{\rm d}x\lesssim\epsilon^{2}.

Multiplying (

INLS
V

) by
\eta_{R}\bar{u}
, taking the imaginary part and integrating by parts, we obtain

\partial_{t}\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\eta_{R}(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}{\rm d}x\lesssim\frac{1}{R},

so that, by (
4.2
), for
t\in I_{1}
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\eta_{R}(x)|u(t,x)|^{2}{\rm d}x\lesssim\epsilon^{2}+\frac{\epsilon^{-\alpha}}{R}.

If
R\geqslant\epsilon^{-(\alpha+2)}
, then we have
\|\eta_{R}u\|_{L^{\infty}_{I_{1}}L^{2}_{x}}\lesssim\epsilon
.

We choose
(s,l)\in\Lambda_{s_{c}}
as

s=\frac{4(p-1)(p+1-\theta)}{(p-1)[3(p-1)+2b]-\theta[3(p-1)-4+2b]},\quad l=\frac{3(p-1)(p+1-\theta)}{(p-1)(3-b)-\theta(2-b)}.

By Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev embedding, for
t\in I_{1}
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\Big\|\eta_{R}\frac{|u|^{p-1}u}{|x|^{b}}\Big\|_{L_{x}^{l^{\prime}}}\lesssim\|u(t)\|^{\theta}_{H_{x}^{1}}\|u(t)\|^{p-1-\theta}_{L_{x}^{l}}\|\eta_{R}u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{l}}\lesssim\|\eta_{R}u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{l}}.

Now, let
0<\mu<1
satisfy
\frac{1}{l}=\frac{\mu}{2}+\frac{1-\mu}{6}
, we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\|\eta_{R}u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{l}}\leqslant\|u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{6}}^{1-\mu}\|\eta_{R}u(t)\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{\mu}\lesssim\epsilon^{\mu},

uniformly for
t\in I_{1}
.
We now exploit the decay of the nonlinearity.
By Hölder’s inequality and Sobolev embedding again, for
R\geqslant 0
sufficiently large (depending on
\epsilon
) and
t\in I_{1}
,

\displaystyle\Big\|(1-\eta_{R})\frac{|u|^{p-1}u}{|x|^{b}}\Big\|_{L_{x}^{l^{\prime}}}\lesssim

\displaystyle~\Big\|\frac{|u|^{p-1}u}{|x|^{b}}\Big\|_{L_{x}^{l^{\prime}}(|x|>\frac{R}{2})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\Big\|\frac{1}{|x|^{b}}\Big\|_{L_{x}^{l_{1}}(|x|>\frac{R}{2})}\|u\|_{L_{x}^{\theta l_{2}}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L_{x}^{l}}^{p-\theta}

(4.5)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\frac{1}{R^{bl_{1}-3}}\|u\|_{H_{x}^{1}}^{p}\lesssim\epsilon^{\mu},

where
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
are such that
bl_{1}>3
,
2<\theta l_{2}<\frac{3(p-1)}{2-b}
and

\frac{1}{l^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{l_{1}}+\frac{1}{l_{2}}+\frac{p-\theta}{l}.

Using the Strichartz estimates, together with estimates (
4.3
), (
4.4
), and (
4.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle~\left\|\int_{I_{1}}e^{i(t-s)\mathcal{H}}\frac{|u|^{p-1}u(s)}{|x|^{b}}{\rm d}s\right\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s_{c}},[T,\infty))}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\Big\|\frac{|u|^{p-1}u}{|x|^{b}}\Big\|_{S^{\prime}(\dot{H}^{-s_{c}},I_{1})}\lesssim\Big\|\frac{|u|^{p-1}u}{|x|^{b}}\Big\|_{L_{t}^{s^{\prime}}(I_{1},L_{x}^{l^{\prime}})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\Big\|\eta_{R}\frac{|u|^{p-1}u}{|x|^{b}}\Big\|_{L_{t}^{s^{\prime}}(I_{1},L_{x}^{l^{\prime}})}+\Big\|(1-\eta_{R})\frac{|u|^{p-1}u}{|x|^{b}}\Big\|_{L_{t}^{s^{\prime}}(I_{1},L_{x}^{l^{\prime}})}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~|I_{1}|^{1/s^{\prime}}\epsilon^{\mu}=\epsilon^{\mu-\alpha/s^{\prime}}=\epsilon^{\mu/2},

where we choose
\alpha:=s^{\prime}\mu/2
.

Estimate on
G_{2}
.

Let
(q,r)\in\Lambda_{s_{c}}
.
Define, for small
\delta\geqslant 0
,

\frac{1}{h}=\left(\frac{1}{1-s_{c}}\right)\left[\frac{1}{q}-\delta s_{c}\right],

and

\frac{1}{k}=\left(\frac{1}{1-s_{c}}\right)\left[\frac{1}{r}-s_{c}\left(\frac{3-2-4\delta}{6}\right)\right].

We see that
(h,k)\in\Lambda_{0}
.
By interpolation,

\|G_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{q}([T,+\infty),L_{x}^{r})}\lesssim\|G_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{h}([T,+\infty),L_{x}^{k})}^{1-s_{c}}\|G_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{1}{\delta}}([T,+\infty),L_{x}^{\frac{6}{3-2-4\delta}})}^{s_{c}}.

By the dispersive estimate (
2.3
) and Lemma
2.4
, one can get

\displaystyle~\|G_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{1}{\delta}}([T,\infty),L_{x}^{\frac{6}{3-2-4\delta}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\left\|\int_{I_{2}}|t-s|^{-(1+2\delta)}\Big\|\frac{|u|^{p-1}u}{|x|^{b}}\Big\|_{L_{x}^{\frac{6}{3+2+4\delta}}}{\rm d}s\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{1}{\delta}}([T,\infty))}

(4.7)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\|u\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}H_{x}^{1}}^{p}\left\|\left(t-T+\epsilon^{-\alpha}\right)\right\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{1}{\delta}}([T,\infty))}\lesssim\epsilon^{\alpha\delta}.

Using Duhamel’s principle, rewrite

iG_{2}=e^{it\mathcal{H}}\left[e^{-i(T-\epsilon^{-\alpha})\mathcal{H}}u(T-\epsilon^{-\alpha})-u_{0}\right],

thus, by the Strichartz estimate and (
4.7
),

\displaystyle~\|G_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{q}([T,+\infty),L_{x}^{r})}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\left\|e^{-it\mathcal{H}}\left[e^{-i(T-\epsilon^{-\alpha})\mathcal{H}}u(T-\epsilon^{-\alpha})-u_{0}\right]\right\|_{L_{t}^{h}([T,+\infty),L_{x}^{k})}^{1-s_{c}}\|G_{2}\|_{L_{t}^{\frac{1}{\delta}}([T,+\infty),L_{x}^{\frac{6}{3-2-4\delta}})}^{s_{c}}

(4.8)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle~\|u\|^{1-s_{c}}_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}}\|G_{2}\|^{s_{c}}_{L_{t}^{\frac{1}{\delta}}([T,+\infty),L_{x}^{\frac{6}{3-2-4\delta}})}\lesssim\epsilon^{\alpha\delta}.

Therefore, defining
\gamma:=\min\{\mu/2,\alpha\delta s_{c}\}
and recalling that

e^{-i(t-T)\mathcal{H}}u(T)=e^{-it\mathcal{H}}u_{0}+iG_{1}+iG_{2},

we have

\|e^{-i(t-T)\mathcal{H}}u(T)\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s_{c}},[T,\infty))}\lesssim\epsilon^{\gamma}.

Then, we complete the proof of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Choose
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small, by Theorem
2.5
and
2.6
,

\|e^{-it\mathcal{H}}u(T)\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s_{c}},[0,\infty))}=\|e^{-i(t-T)\mathcal{H}}\|_{S(\dot{H}^{s_{c}},[T,\infty))}\lesssim\epsilon^{\gamma}\lesssim\delta_{sc},

where

.
Thus by small data scattering theory,

∎

Lemma 5.1

\displaystyle~-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|u|^{2}\Delta\Delta a{\rm d}x+4\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}a_{i,j}\overline{u_{i}}u_{j}{\rm d}x-2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla a\cdot\nabla V|u|^{2}{\rm d}x.

We omit the proof of Lemma

Let

For given

It is easy to see

Lemma 5.2

(Morawetz estimate)
.

For
0<b<1
and

V

), let

V

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{|x|\leqslant\frac{R}{2}}\frac{|u(t,x)|^{p+1}}{|x|^{b}}{\rm d}x{\rm d}t\lesssim_{u_{0},Q}\frac{R}{T}+\frac{1}{R^{b}}+o_{R}(1).

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}|Z(t)|\lesssim R.

Denote the angular derivative as

So, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}t}Z(t)=

\displaystyle~+\int_{|x|\geqslant\frac{R}{2}}\left[\left(\frac{4}{p+1}-2\right)\Delta a-\frac{4b}{1+p}\frac{x\cdot\nabla a}{|x|^{2}}\right]\frac{|u|^{p+1}}{|x|^{b}}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle~\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x\cdot\nabla V||u(t,x)|^{2}{\rm d}x

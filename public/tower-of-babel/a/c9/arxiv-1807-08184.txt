Relations between Schoenberg Coefficients on Real and Complex Spheres of Different Dimensions
Pier Giovanni BISSIRI †, Valdir A. MENEGATTO ‡ and Emilio PORCU †§

Abstract
Positive definite functions on spheres have received an increasing interest in many branches of mathematics and statistics. In particular, the Schoenberg sequences in the spectral representation of positive definite functions have been studied by several mathematicians in the last years. This paper provides a set of relations between Schoenberg sequences defined over real as well as complex spheres of different dimensions. We illustrate our findings describing an application to strict positive definiteness.

2
Schoenberg sequences on real spheres

2.1
Background and notation

For a positive integer
d
, let
\mathbb{S}^{d}=\big\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\,\|\mathbf{x}\|=1\big\}
denote the
d
-dimensional unit sphere embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, with
\|\cdot\|
being the Euclidean norm.
We define the great-circle distance
\theta\colon\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}\to[0,\pi]
as the continuous mapping defined through

\displaystyle\theta(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2})=\arccos\big(\mathbf{x}_{1}^{\top}\mathbf{x}_{2}\big)\in[0,\pi],

for
\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2}\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
, where
\top
is the transpose operator. A mapping
C\colon\mathbb{S}^{d}\times\mathbb{S}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
that satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}c_{i}c_{j}C(\mathbf{x}_{i},\mathbf{x}_{j})\geq 0

for all
n\geq 1
, distinct points
\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2},\ldots,\mathbf{x}_{n}
on
\mathbb{S}^{d}
and real numbers
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
, is called
positive definite
. Further, if the inequality is strict, unless the vector
(c_{1},\dots,c_{n})^{\top}
is the zero vector, then it is called
strictly positive definite
(see
[
8
]
and references therein). If, in addition,

\displaystyle C(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2})=\psi(\theta(\mathbf{x}_{1},\mathbf{x}_{2})),\qquad\mathbf{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{d},\qquad i=1,2,

(2.1)

for some mapping
\psi\colon[0,\pi]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, then
C
is called a
geodesically isotropic covariance
by
[
39
]
. With no loss of generality, we assume through the paper that the function
\psi
is continuous along with the normalization
\psi(0)=1
.

Gneiting
[
19
]
calls
\Psi_{d}
the class of continuous functions
\psi\colon[0,\pi]\to\mathbb{R}
with
\psi(0)=1
such that the function
C
in equation (
2.1
) is positive definite. The inclusions
\Psi_{d}\supset\Psi_{d+1}
,
d\geq 1
, are known to be strict. Following
[
41
]
, for every continuous function
\psi\colon[0,\pi]\to\mathbb{R}
with
\psi(0)=1
, and every integer
d\geq 2
, define

\displaystyle b_{n,d}=\kappa(n,d)\int_{0}^{\pi}\psi(\theta)C_{n}^{(d-1)/2}(\cos\theta)\left(\sin\theta\right)^{d-1}{\rm d}\theta,

(2.2)

where, for any
\lambda>0
,
C_{n}^{\lambda}
denotes the
n
-th Gegenbauer polynomial of order
\lambda

[
1
]
, and

\displaystyle\kappa(n,d)=\frac{(2n+d-1)(\Gamma((d-1)/2)^{2}}{2^{3-d}\pi\Gamma(d-1)}.

(2.3)

Moreover, we define

\displaystyle b_{0,1}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\psi(\theta){\rm d}\theta,\qquad b_{n,1}=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\cos(n\theta)\psi(\theta){\rm d}\theta,\qquad n\geq 1.

(2.4)

Note that in the cases
d=1
(the circle) and
d=2
(the unit sphere of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
), Gegenbauer polynomials simplify to Chebyshev and Legendre polynomials
[
1
]
, respectively.

The coefficient sequences
\{b_{n,d}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
play a crucial role in the spectral representations for positive definite functions on spheres, which are the equivalent of Bochner and Schoenberg’s theorems in Euclidean spaces (see
[
15
]
with the references therein) and are provided by
[
41
]
, who shows that a mapping
\psi\colon[0,\pi]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
belongs to the class
\Psi_{d}
if and only if
it can be uniquely written as

\displaystyle\psi(\theta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n,d}c_{n}^{(d-1)/2}(\cos\theta),\qquad\theta\in[0,\pi],

(2.5)

where
c_{n}^{\lambda}
denotes the normalized
\lambda
-Gegenbauer polynomial of degree
n
,
namely,

\displaystyle c_{n}^{\lambda}(u)=\frac{C_{n}^{\lambda}(u)}{C_{n}^{\lambda}(1)},\qquad u\in[-1,1],

and
\{b_{n,d}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a probability mass sequence. The series (
2.5
) is known to be uniformly convergent. We follow
[
15
]
when calling the sequence
\{b_{n,d}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
in (
2.5
) the
d
-Schoenberg sequence of coefficients
, to emphasize the dependence on the index
d
in the class

\Psi_{d}
. Accordingly, we say that
(\psi,\{b_{n,d}\})
is a uniquely determined
d
-Schoenberg pair
if
\psi
belongs to the class
\Psi_{d}
and admits the expansion (
2.5
) with
d
-Schoenberg sequence
\{b_{n,d}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
.

The following recursive relations among the coefficients
b_{n,d}
and
b_{n,d+2}
attached to a
d
-Schoenberg pair
(\psi,\{b_{n,d+2}\})
(see
[
19
, Corollary 1]
)

\displaystyle b_{0,3}=b_{0,1}-\frac{1}{2}b_{2,1},

(2.6)

\displaystyle b_{n,3}=\frac{1}{2}(n+1)(b_{n,1}-b_{n+2,1}),\qquad n\geq 1,

(2.7)

\displaystyle b_{n,d+2}=\frac{(n+d-1)(n+d)}{d(2n+d-1)}b_{n,d}-\frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{d(2n+d+3)}b_{n+2,d},\qquad d\geq 2,\qquad n\geq 0,

(2.8)

have actually opened for challenging questions. Fiedler
[
16
]
has shown relationships between sequences
\{b_{n,2d+1}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
\{b_{n,1}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, on the one hand, and sequences
\{b_{n,2d}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
\{b_{n,2}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
, on the other. Proposition 1 in
[
2
]
encompasses Fiedler’s result and provides a relation between the sequences
\{b_{n,d}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
,
d>1
, and
\{b_{n,1}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
. A projection operator relating Schoenberg sequences on Hilbert spheres with
d
-Schoenberg sequences has been proposed by
[
38
]
. Yet, there are some relations that have not been discovered and these will be illustrated throughout.

2.2
Results

We start with a very simple result, that we report formally for the convenience of the reader.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
d
,
d^{\prime}
be positive integers, with
d>d^{\prime}
. If
(\psi,\{b_{n,d}\})
is a
d
-Schoenberg pair, then the
d^{\prime}
-Schoenberg sequence of coefficients of
\psi
is uniquely determined as follows.

(i)

For
d^{\prime}\geq 2
,

\displaystyle b_{n,d^{\prime}}=\frac{\kappa(n,d^{\prime})}{C_{n}^{(d-1)/2}(1)}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n,d}\int_{0}^{\pi}C_{n}^{(d-1)/2}(\cos\theta)C_{n}^{(d^{\prime}-1)/2}(\cos\theta){\rm d}\theta,

(2.9)

with
\kappa(n,d)
as defined in (
2.3
).

(ii)

For
d^{\prime}=1
,

\displaystyle b_{0,1}=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n,d}\int_{0}^{\pi}c_{n}^{(d-1)/2}(\cos\theta){\rm d}\theta,

\displaystyle b_{n,1}=\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n,d}\int_{0}^{\pi}c_{n}^{(d-1)/2}(\cos\theta)\cos(n\theta){\rm d}\theta,\qquad n\geq 1.

(2.10)

Proof.

The identity (
2.9
) is obtained substituting (
2.5
) into (
2.2
), whereas the identities (
2.10
) are obtained substituting (
2.5
) into (
2.4
). In both cases, exchanging integral and series is allowed owing to both bounded and uniform convergence of the series (
2.5
).
∎

We are not aware of any closed-form expression for the integrals appearing in (
2.10
) and (
2.9
), and therefore of the relationships between the sequences
\{b_{n,d}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
and
\{b_{n,d^{\prime}}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
attached to a
d^{\prime}
-Schoenberg pair
(\psi,b_{n,d^{\prime}})
, apart from the specific case where
d^{\prime}=d+2
. Indeed,
[
19
]
provides a closed-form expression for
\{b_{n,d+2}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
as a function of
\{b_{n,d}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
that is given by (
2.6
)–(
2.8
). Our first main results provide an explicit expression for the inverse function

Theorem 2.2
.

If
(\psi,\{b_{n,3}\})
is a
3
-Schoenberg pair, then the
1
-Schoenberg sequence of coefficients of
\psi
is given by

\displaystyle b_{0,1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2j+1}b_{2j,3},

(2.11)

\displaystyle b_{n,1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{2}{n+2j+1}b_{n+2j,3},\qquad n\geq 1.

(2.12)

Proof.

From identity (
2.7
), if
(\psi,\{b_{n,3}\})
is a
3
-Schoenberg pair, we have that

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n+1}b_{n,3}=b_{n,1}-b_{n+2,1},\qquad n\geq 1.

Hence, for every nonnegative integer
j
, and for any positive integer
n
,

\displaystyle\frac{2}{n+2j+1}b_{n+2j,3}=b_{n+2j,1}-b_{n+2j+2,1}.

(2.13)

Summing up both sides of (
2.13
) from
0
to
m
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{2}{n+2j+1}b_{n+2j,3}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\big(b_{n+2j,1}-b_{n+2j+2,1}\big),\qquad m\geq 1.

(2.14)

We now use the fact that the right-hand side in equation (
2.13
) is telescopic. Hence, (
2.14
) can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{2}{n+2j+1}b_{n+2j,3}=b_{n,1}-b_{n+2m+2,1},\qquad m\geq 1.

(2.15)

Since
\psi
belongs to
\Psi_{1}
, the series
\sum\limits_{n=0}^{\infty}b_{n,1}
converges to
1
and, therefore, the sequence
\{b_{n,1}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
converges to zero. We can thus take the limit for
m\to\infty
in equation (
2.15
) and this will provide (
2.12
). In particular, we now take
n=2
to deduce that
b_{2,1}=2\sum\limits_{j=1}^{\infty}b_{2j,3}/\{1+2j\}
which combined with (
2.6
) yields (
2.11
).
∎

We are now able to provide an extension of Theorem
2.2
for
d>3
. For a positive integer
m
and
x>0
,
(x)^{m}
will denote the standard rising factorial (Pochhammer symbol).

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
d\geq 2
be a positive integer. If
(\psi,\{b_{n,d+2}\})
is a
(d+2)
-Schoenberg pair, then the
d
-Schoenberg sequence of coefficients
\{b_{n,d}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
of
\psi
is given by

\displaystyle b_{n,d}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}w_{j,n,d}b_{n+2j,d+2},\qquad n\geq 1,

where

\displaystyle w_{0,n,d}

\displaystyle=\frac{d(2n+d-1)}{(n+d-1)(n+d)},

\displaystyle w_{j,n,d}

\displaystyle=d(2n+d-1)\frac{(n/2+1/2)^{(j)}(n/2+1)^{(j)}}{(n/2+(d-1)/2)^{(j+1)}(n/2+d/2)^{(j+1)}},\qquad j\geq 1.

Proof.

We give a constructive proof. Define

\displaystyle a_{n,d}:=\frac{d(2n+d-1)}{(n+d-1)(n+d)},\qquad u_{n,d}:=2n+d-1,\qquad v_{n,d}:=\frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{(n+d-1)(n+d)}.

We can now rewrite equation (
2.8
) as

\displaystyle a_{n,d}b_{n,d+2}=b_{n,d}-\frac{u_{n,d}}{u_{n+2,d}}v_{n,d}b_{n+2,d},\qquad n\geq 0.

(2.16)

Identity (
2.16
) shows that for every pair of nonnegative integers
(j,n)
, it is true that

\displaystyle a_{n+2j,d}b_{n+2j,d+2}=b_{n+2j,d}-\frac{u_{n+2j,d}}{u_{n+2j+2,d}}v_{n+2j,d}b_{n+2j+2,d}.

(2.17)

Multiplying each side of (
2.17
) by
(u_{n,d}/u_{n+2j,d})\prod\limits_{l=0}^{j-1}v_{n+2l,d}
and summing up both sides from
0
to
m
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}\left(\prod_{l=0}^{j-1}v_{n+2l,d}\right)\frac{u_{n,d}}{u_{n+2j,d}}a_{n+2j,d}b_{n+2j,d+2}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=u_{n,d}\sum_{j=0}^{m}\left(\prod_{l=0}^{j-1}v_{n+2l,d}\right)\left(\frac{b_{n+2j,d}}{u_{n+2j,d}}-\frac{b_{n+2j+2,d}}{u_{n+2j+2,d}}v_{n+2j,d}\right).

Since the sum in the right-hand side is telescopic, we are left with

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}\left(\prod_{l=0}^{j-1}v_{n+2l,d}\right)\frac{u_{n,d}}{u_{n+2j,d}}a_{n+2j,d}b_{n+2j,d+2}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=b_{n,d}-\frac{u_{n,d}}{u_{n+2m+2,d}}\left(\prod_{l=0}^{m}v_{n+2l,d}\right)b_{n+2j+2,d}.

(2.18)

At this stage, note that

\displaystyle v_{n,d}-1=-(d-2)\frac{2n+d+1}{(n+d-1)(n+d)}\leq-(d-2)\frac{1}{n+d-1},\qquad n\geq 0.

(2.19)

We can now show that
\prod\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}v_{n+2l,d}\in\{0,1\}
.
Indeed, if
d=2
, then
v_{n,d}=1
for each
n\geq 0

and, therefore,
\prod\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}v_{n+2l,2}=1
. If
d>2
, then by (
2.19
)

\displaystyle\prod_{l=0}^{m}v_{n+2l,d}=\exp\left[\sum_{l=0}^{m}\log(v_{n+2l,d})\right]

\displaystyle\hphantom{\prod_{l=0}^{m} v_{n+2l,d}}{}\leq\exp\left[\sum_{l=0}^{m}(v_{n+2l,d}-1)\right]\leq\exp\left[-(d-2)\sum_{l=0}^{m}\frac{1}{n+2l+d-1}\right],

and, therefore,
\prod\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}v_{n+2l,d}=0
. Since
\psi\in\Psi_{d}
, the sequence
\{b_{n,d}\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
converges to zero, while

\displaystyle\lim_{m\to\infty}\frac{u_{n,d}}{u_{n+2m+2,d}}=0,\qquad n\geq 0.

So, letting
m\to\infty
in (
2.2
) yields

\displaystyle b_{n,d}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{l=0}^{j-1}v_{n+2l,d}\right)\frac{u_{n,d}}{u_{n+2j,d}}a_{n+2j,d}b_{n+2j,d+2},\qquad n\geq 0.

Finally, direct computation shows that for
n\geq 0
and
j\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\prod_{l=0}^{j-1}v_{n+2l,d}=\frac{(n/2+1/2)^{(j)}(n/2+1)^{(j)}}{(n/2+(d-1)/2)^{(j)}(n/2+d/2)^{(j)}},

and

\displaystyle\frac{u_{n,d}}{u_{n+2j,d}}a_{n+2j,d}=\frac{d(2n+d-1)}{(n+2j+d-1)(n+2j+d)}.

The proof is completed.
∎

3
Schoenberg sequences on complex spheres

In analogy with the results obtained in Section
2
, we consider similar results on complex spheres.

3.1
Background and notation

For a positive integer
q
, denote by
\Omega_{2q}
the unit sphere in
\mathbb{C}^{q}
. A mapping
C\colon\Omega_{2d}\times\Omega_{2q}\to\mathbb{C}
is
positive definite
if

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}c_{i}\bar{c}_{j}C(\mathbf{z}_{i},\mathbf{z}_{j})\geq 0.

for all
n\geq 1
, distinct points
\mathbf{z}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{z}_{n}
of
\Omega_{2q}
and complex numbers
c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}
.
Let “
\cdot
” denote the usual inner product in
\mathbb{C}^{q}
. If
q\geq 2
and
B[0,1]=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon z\cdot z\leq 1\}
, the function
C
is called
isotropic
if

\displaystyle C(\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{z}_{2})=\varphi(\mathbf{z}_{1}\cdot\mathbf{z}_{2}),\qquad\mathbf{z}_{1},\mathbf{z}_{2}\in\Omega_{2q},

(3.1)

for some function
\varphi\colon B[0,1]\to\mathbb{C}
. This nomenclature is not universal but it is quite adequate in our setting. Observe that in the case
q=1
, if
z,w\in\Omega_{2}
, then
z\cdot z\in\Omega_{2}
. Hence, the previous definition becomes an extreme case once the domain of
\varphi
needs to be
\Omega_{2}
itself.

Keeping the analogy with the previous section, for
q\geq 2
, we call
\Upsilon_{2q}
the class of continuous functions
\varphi
, with
\varphi(1)=1
such that
C
in (
3.1
) is positive definite.
We also denote by
\Upsilon_{2q}^{+}
the class of functions
\varphi
belonging to
\Upsilon_{2q}
such that
C
in (
3.1
) is strictly positive definite. Both classes
\Upsilon_{2q}
and
\Upsilon_{2q}^{+}
are nested, that is, if
q\leq q^{\prime}
, then
\Upsilon_{2q^{\prime}}\subset\Upsilon_{2q}
and
\Upsilon_{2q^{\prime}}^{+}\subset\Upsilon_{2q}^{+}
.

To present the characterization of the class
\Upsilon_{2q}
described in
[
37
]
, we denote by
R^{q-2}_{m,n}
the disk polynomial of bi-degree
(m,n)
with respect to the nonnegative integer
q-2
.
The set
\{R^{q-2}_{m,n}\colon m,n=0,1,\ldots\}
is a complete orthogonal system in
L^{2}(B[0,1],\nu_{q-2})
, with

\displaystyle{\rm d}\nu_{q-2}(z)=\frac{q-1}{\pi}\big(1-|z|^{2}\big)^{q-2}{\rm d}x{\rm d}y,\qquad z=x+\mathsf{i}y\in B[0,1].

(3.2)

In particular,

\displaystyle\int_{B[0,1]}R^{q-2}_{m,n}(z)\overline{R^{q-2}_{k,l}(z)}{\rm d}\nu_{q-2}(z)=\frac{\delta_{mk}\delta_{nl}}{h^{q-2}_{m,n}},

(3.3)

where

\displaystyle h^{q-2}_{m,n}=\frac{m+n+q-1}{q-1}\binom{m+q-2}{q-2}\binom{n+q-2}{q-2}.

(3.4)

Expressions and main properties of disk polynomials can be found in
[
43
]
and in references quoted there. We recall the following recursion satisfied for every
z
in
B[0,1]
,
m\geq 1
and
n\geq 0

[
35
]
:

\displaystyle\big(1-|z|^{2}\big)R^{q-1}_{m-1,n}(z)=\frac{q-1}{m+n+q-1}\big[R^{q-2}_{m-1,n}(z)-R^{q-2}_{m,n+1}(z)\big].

(3.5)

For every continuous function
\varphi\colon B[0,1]\to\mathbb{C}
and every triplet
(m,n,q)
of nonnegative integers, we can define

\displaystyle a^{q-2}_{m,n}:=h^{q-2}_{m,n}\int_{B[0,1]}\varphi(z)\overline{R^{q-2}_{m,n}(z)}{\rm d}\nu_{q-2}(z).

(3.6)

The functions belonging to the class
\Upsilon_{2q}
are uniquely characterized through the expansion
[
37
]

\displaystyle\varphi(z)=\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}a^{q-2}_{m,n}R^{q-2}_{m,n}(z),\qquad z\in B[0,1],

(3.7)

where
a^{q-2}_{m,n}\geq 0
,
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
\sum\limits_{m,n=0}^{\infty}a^{q-2}_{m,n}=1
. Following Section
2
, we finally define a
2q
-Schoenberg pair

\big(\varphi,\big\{a_{m,n}^{q-2}\big\}\big)
any function belonging to the class
\Upsilon_{2q}
with expansion defined according to (
3.7
). In this case, the double sequence
\big\{a_{m,n}^{q-2}\big\}_{m,n=0}^{\infty}
will be called the
2q
-Schoenberg sequence of coefficients
of
\varphi
.

3.2
Results

Since the classes
\Upsilon_{2q}
are nested, here we prove a recursive relation among the coefficients
a_{m,n}^{q-1}
and
a_{m,n}^{q-2}
attached to a
2(q+1)
Schoenberg pair
\big(\varphi,\big\{a_{m,n}^{q-1}\big\}\big)
that resembles (
2.8
).

Proposition 3.1
.

If
\big(\varphi,\big\{a_{m,n}^{q-1}\big\}\big)
is a
2(q+1)
-Schoenberg pair, then for
m-1,n\geq 0
,

\displaystyle a^{q-1}_{m-1,n}=\frac{(m+q-2)(n+q-1)}{(q-1)(m+n+q-2)}a^{q-2}_{m-1,n}-\frac{m(n+1)}{(q-1)(m+n+q)}a^{q-2}_{m,n+1}.

(3.8)

Proof.

Equation (
3.4
) shows that

\displaystyle h^{q-2}_{m-1,n}=\frac{(m+n+q-2)m(n+1)}{(m+n+q)(m+q-2)(n+q-1)}h^{q-2}_{m,n+1},\qquad m-1,n\geq 0,

(3.9)

\displaystyle h^{q-2}_{m-1,n}=\frac{(m+n+q-2)q(q-1)^{2}}{(m+n+q-1)q(m+q-2)(n+q-1)}h^{q-1}_{m-1,n},\qquad m-1,n\geq 0.

(3.10)

We now multiply both sides of (
3.5
) by
h^{q-2}_{m-1,n}\varphi(z)
and integrate with respect to the measure
\nu_{\alpha}
defined in (
3.3
). After we use (
3.6
) and (
3.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle h^{q-2}_{m-1,n}\int_{B[0,1]}\big(1-|z|^{2}\big)R^{q-1}_{m-1,n}(z)f\varphi(z){\rm d}\nu_{q-2}(z)

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\frac{q-1}{m+n+q-1}\left[a^{q-2}_{m-1,n}-\frac{(m+n+q-2)m(n+1)}{(m+n+q)(m+q-2)(n+q-1)}a^{q-2}_{m,n+1}\right].

(3.11)

However, equation (
3.2
) yields the equality

\displaystyle\big(1-|z|^{2}\big)d\nu_{q-2}=\frac{q-1}{q}d\nu_{q-1}.

Therefore, by (
3.10
) and (
3.6
), the left-hand side of (
3.11
) is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{(m+n+q-2)(q-1)^{2}}{(m+n+q-1)(m+q-2)(n+q-1)}a^{q-1}_{m-1,n},

so that (
3.11
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{(m+n+q-2)(q-1)}{(m+q-2)(n+q-1)}a^{q-1}_{m-1,n}=a^{q-2}_{m-1,n}-\frac{(m+n+q-2)m(n+1)}{(m+n+q)(m+q-2)(n+q-1)}a^{q-2}_{m,n+1}.

This yields (
3.8
).
∎

Here is the main result of the section.

Theorem 3.2
.

If
\big(\varphi,\big\{a_{m,n}^{q-1}\big\}\big)
is a
2(q+1)
-Schoenberg pair, then the
2q
-Schoenberg sequence of coefficients
\big\{a^{q-2}_{m,n}\big\}_{m,n=0}^{\infty}
of
\varphi
is given by

\displaystyle a^{q-2}_{m,n}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}v^{q-2}_{j,m+1,n}a^{q-1}_{m+j,n+j},\qquad m,n\geq 0,

where

\displaystyle v^{q-2}_{j,m,n}:=\frac{m^{(j)}(n+1)^{(j)}(m+n+q-2)}{(m+q-2)^{(j)}(n+q-1)^{(j)}(m+n+2j+q-2)},\qquad j\geq 0.

Proof.

First of all we introduce the following notations:

\displaystyle u^{q-2}_{m,n}:=\frac{(q-1)(m+n+q-2)}{(m+q-2)(n+q-1)},\qquad m,n\geq 0,

\displaystyle w^{q-2}_{m,n}:=\frac{(m+n+q-2)m(n+1)}{(m+q-2)(n+q-1)(m+n+q)},\qquad m,n\geq 0.

(3.12)

In this way, (
3.8
) becomes

\displaystyle u^{q-2}_{m,n}a^{q-1}_{m-1,n}=a^{q-2}_{m-1,n}-w^{q-2}_{m,n}a^{q-2}_{m,n+1},\qquad m-1,n\geq 0.

(3.13)

By (
3.13
), we have that for every triplet
(j,m,n)
of nonnegative integers,

\displaystyle u^{q-2}_{m+j,n+j}a^{q-1}_{m+j-1,n+j}=a^{q-2}_{m+j-1,n+j}-w^{q-2}_{m+j,n+j}a^{q-2}_{m+j,n+j+1}.

(3.14)

Now, we can multiply each side of (
3.14
) by the product
\prod\limits_{l=1}^{j}w^{q-2}_{m+l-1,n+l-1}
and sum up each side from
0
to
k
,
obtaining that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(\prod_{l=1}^{j}w^{q-2}_{m+l-1,n+l-1}\right)u^{q-2}_{m+j,n+j}a^{q-1}_{m+j-1,n+j}\\
&\qquad{}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(\prod_{l=1}^{j}w^{q-2}_{m+l-1,n+l-1}\right)\left(a^{q-2}_{m+j-1,n+j}-w^{q-2}_{m+j,n+j}a^{q-2}_{m+j,n+j+1}\right).\end{split}

Since the sum in the right-hand side is telescopic, we are reduced to

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(\prod_{l=1}^{j}w^{q-2}_{m+l-1,n+l-1}\right)u^{q-2}_{m+j,n+j}a^{q-1}_{m+j-1,n+j}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=a^{q-2}_{m-1,n}-\left(\prod_{j=0}^{k}w^{q-2}_{m+j,n+j}\right)a^{q-2}_{m+k,n+k+1}.

(3.15)

Since

\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{k}w^{q-2}_{m+j,n+j}

\displaystyle\qquad{}\leq\exp\left[-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(\frac{q-2}{m+j+q-2}+\frac{q-2}{n+j+q-1}+\frac{2}{m+n+2j+q}\right)\right],\qquad k\geq 0,

we end up with

\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}w^{q-2}_{m+j,n+j}=0.

Moreover, since

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}a^{q-2}_{l+k,j+k+1}\leq\sum_{m,n=0}^{\infty}a^{q-2}_{m,n}<\infty,\qquad j,l\geq 0,

we have that
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}a^{q-2}_{l+k,j+k+1}=0
, for
j,l\geq 0
. Therefore, letting
k\to\infty
, (
3.15
) leads to

\displaystyle a^{q-2}_{m-1,n}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\prod_{l=1}^{j}w^{q-2}_{m+l-1,n+l-1}\right)u^{q-2}_{m+j,n+j}a^{q-1}_{m+j-1,n+j},\qquad m-1,n\geq 0,

which in turn by (
3.12
) yields the desired result.
∎

4
Applications involving the classes
\boldsymbol{\Psi_{d}^{+}}
and
\boldsymbol{\Upsilon_{2q}^{+}}

In this section, we present applications of the previous results involving the classes
\Psi_{d}^{+}
and
\Upsilon_{2q}^{+}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
q,q^{\prime}\geq 2
be integers. The following assertions hold:

(i)

If a function
\varphi
belongs to
\Upsilon_{2q}^{+}\cap\Upsilon_{2q^{\prime}}
, then
\varphi
belongs to
\Upsilon_{2q^{\prime}}^{+}
.

(ii)

If a function
\varphi
belongs to
(\Upsilon_{2q}\setminus\Upsilon_{2q}^{+})\cap\Upsilon_{2q^{\prime}}
, then
\varphi
belongs to
\Upsilon_{2q^{\prime}}\setminus\Upsilon_{2q^{\prime}}^{+}
.

Proof.

(i)
If
q\geq q^{\prime}
, the assertion follows from the inclusion
\Upsilon_{2q}^{+}\subset\Upsilon_{2q^{\prime}}^{+}
. So, we may assume that
q<q^{\prime}
. If
\varphi\in\Upsilon_{2q}^{+}
, Theorem 1.1 in
[
20
]
reveals that the
2q
-Schoenberg sequence of coefficients
\big\{a_{m,n}^{q-2}\big\}_{m,n=0}^{\infty}
of
\varphi
has the following property:
\big\{m-n\colon a_{m,n}^{q-2}>0\big\}
intersects every arithmetic progression of
\mathbb{Z}
. Taking into account that
\varphi\in\Upsilon_{2(q+1)}
and the fact that
v^{q^{\prime}-2}_{j,m+1,n}>0
for all
j
, Theorem
3.2
shows that
a_{m,n}^{q-2}>0
if and only if
a_{m+j,n+j}^{q-1}>0
, for at least one
j\geq 0
. In particular, the set
\big\{m-n\colon a_{m,n}^{q-1}>0\big\}
intersects every arithmetic progression of
\mathbb{Z}
as well. In other words,
\varphi\in\Upsilon_{2(q+1)}^{+}
, due to Theorem 1.1 in
[
20
]
once again. If
q+1=q^{\prime}
,
\varphi\in\Upsilon_{2q^{\prime}}^{+}
and we are done. Otherwise, we iterate this procedure until we reach the desired conclusion.

(ii)
Assume
\varphi\in(\Upsilon_{2q}\setminus\Upsilon_{2q}^{+})\cap\Upsilon_{2q^{\prime}}
. If
\varphi\in\Upsilon_{2q^{\prime}}^{+}
, then
\varphi\in\Upsilon_{2q^{\prime}}^{+}\cap\Upsilon_{2q}
and
(i)
would imply that
\varphi\in\Upsilon_{2q}^{+}
, a contradiction.
∎

A similar result holds for real spheres with a similar proof. In particular, if
\psi
belongs to
\Psi_{d}\cap\Psi_{d^{\prime}}^{+}
, then
\psi
belongs to
\Psi_{d}^{+}
. However, this result was proved earlier in
[
19
, Corollary 1]
via a slightly different argument.

Theorem
4.1
allows the following obvious consequences. If
\varphi
is a function in
\Upsilon_{2q}
, we write
\varphi_{r}
to indicate the restriction of
\varphi
to
[-1,1]
.

Corollary 4.2
.

For
d\geq 1
and
q\geq 2
, the following assertions hold:

(i)

If a function
\varphi
belongs to
\Upsilon_{2q}
and
\varphi_{r}\circ\cos
belongs to
\Psi_{d}^{+}
, then
\varphi_{r}\circ\cos
belongs to
\Psi_{2q-1}^{+}
.

(ii)

If a function
\varphi
belongs to
\Upsilon_{2q}^{+}
and
\varphi_{r}\circ\cos
belongs to
\Psi_{d}
, then
\varphi_{r}\circ\cos
belongs to
\Psi_{d}^{+}
.

Proof.

It suffices to observe that if
\varphi\in\Upsilon_{2q}
(respectively,
\Upsilon_{2q}^{+}
), then
f_{r}\circ\cos\in\Psi_{2q-1}
(respectively,
\Psi_{2q-1}^{+}
) and to apply the remark in the paragraph preceding the theorem.
∎

5
Discussion

This paper contributes to the literature about the classes
\Psi_{d}
,
\Upsilon_{2q}
and
\Upsilon_{2q}^{+}
in terms of their Schoenberg sequences. Yet, there are many challenges that involve Schoenberg sequences, for instance in product spaces. Berg and Porcu
[
7
]
consider the analogue of Schoenberg pairs introduced in this paper, but on the product space
\mathbb{S}^{d}\times G
, for
G
a locally compact group. Generalizations of the results in
[
7
]
have been provided by
[
22
]
. It would be very interesting to inspect whether the results provided in this paper can be generalized to these cases. Another important challenge would be to inspect the Schoenberg pairs related to matrix-valued kernels (see open problem 2 in
[
39
]
).

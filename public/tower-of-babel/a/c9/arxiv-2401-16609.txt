Turbulent Threshold for Continuum Calogero-Moser Models
James Hogan, Matthew Kowalski

Abstract
. We determine the sharp mass threshold for Sobolev norm growth for the focusing continuum Calogero–Moser model. It is known that below the mass of 2 ​ π 2\pi , solutions to this completely integrable model enjoy uniform-in-time H s H^{s} bounds for all s ≥ 0 s\geq 0 . 1.
Introduction

This paper is devoted to the focusing
continuum Calogero–Moser model

(CCM)

iu_{t}+u_{xx}-2iu\Pi^{+}\partial_{x}(|u|^{2})=0,

known in some sources as the
Calogero–Moser derivative nonlinear Schrödinger equation
. Here
\Pi^{+}
is the Cauchy–Szegő projection; see (
1.7
). Solutions are complex-valued functions of spacetime that belong to a Hardy–Sobolev space

(1.1)

H^{s}_{+}(\mathbb{R}):=\{f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}):\widehat{f}(\xi)=0\text{ for }\xi<0\},\quad s\geq 0,

for each time
t
.

A defocusing version of (
CCM
) appeared in
[
16
]
as a model for internal waves in a stratified fluid, with the nonlinear Schrödinger equation emerging as a small-depth limit.
The focusing version studied here is derived in
[
1
]
as a formal hydrodynamic limit of the Calogero-Moser system — a Hamiltonian system of particles on a line interacting with an inverse square potential:

(CM)

V(x_{j})=\sum_{k\neq j}\frac{1}{(x_{j}-x_{k})^{2}}.

This particle system, and its generalizations, are well-studied; it is solved in the quantum mechanical case
[
4
,
5
]
and is integrable in the classical case
[
15
]
.
The discrete periodic case was studied by Sutherland
[
18
,
19
]
and the periodic continuum limit has been studied under the name of
Calogero–Sutherland derivative NLS
in
[
2
,
3
]
.

As a continuum limit of an integrable system, it is unsurprising that (
CCM
) is completely integrable. It has a Lax pair given by

\mathcal{L}_{u}=-i\partial_{x}-u\Pi^{+}\overline{u}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{P}_{u}=i\partial_{x}^{2}+2u\Pi^{+}\partial_{x}\overline{u}

where
u,\overline{u}
are understood as multiplication by
u,\overline{u}
, respectively; see
[
10
]
.
This Lax pair generates an infinite hierarchy of conserved quantities
[
10
]

(1.2)

I_{k}(u):=\langle u,\mathcal{L}_{u}^{k}u\rangle.

The first and most relevant of these are the mass

M(u):=I_{0}(u)=\int|u|^{2},

the momentum

P(u):=I_{1}(u)=\int-i\overline{u}u_{x}-\tfrac{1}{2}|u|^{4},

and the Hamiltonian

E(u):=\tfrac{1}{2}I_{2}(u)=\tfrac{1}{2}\int\big|u_{x}-iu\Pi^{+}(|u|^{2})\big|^{2}.

This Hamiltonian generates the flow of (
CCM
), albeit with a nonstandard Poisson structure arising from a gauge transformation in
[
1
]
.

Given the infinite family of conserved quantities (
1.2
), one might expect that solutions to (
CCM
) satisfy a priori bounds in
H^{s}
for all
s\geq 0
.
Indeed, for initial data
u_{0}\in H^{\infty}_{+}
with
M(u_{0})<2\pi
,
Gérard and Lenzmann showed in
[
10
]
that the corresponding maximal lifespan solution
u(t)
satisfies

(1.3)

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}H_{x}^{n}}\leq C(I_{0}(u),I_{1}(u),...,I_{2n}(u))

for all integers
n\geq 0
.
Using (
1.3
) they showed that for initial data with mass less than
2\pi
, (
CCM
) is globally well-posed in
H^{n}_{+}
for all integers
n\geq 1
.

On the other hand, Gérard and Lenzmann also showed that (
CCM
) has multisoliton solutions for which these a priori bounds fail.
Specifically, for
N\geq 2
, the
N
-soliton solutions, which have mass
2\pi N
, exhibit a growth of Sobolev norms of the form
\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\sim|t|^{2s}
for large
|t|
.
This raised the question of what is the true threshold for the growth of Sobolev norms.
We answer this question by proving the following:

Theorem 1.1

(Turbulent behaviour)
.

For any sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, there exist initial data
u_{0}\in H^{\infty}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
with
M(u_{0})=2\pi+\varepsilon
, a time
T\in(0,\infty]
, and a maximal lifespan solution
u(t)
to (
CCM
) such that for all
s>0
,
u\in C_{t}H^{s}_{x}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R})
and

\lim_{t\nearrow T}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}=+\infty.

In particular, if
T=\infty
, then we have the bounds

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\gtrsim t^{s}.

An analogous statement holds backwards in time.

This theorem shows that the global-in-time
H^{s}
bounds (
1.3
) fail above the mass of
2\pi
.
This presents a significant obstacle to existing techniques for proving global well-posedness of (
CCM
) above the mass of
2\pi
.

The
2\pi
mass threshold appears in this analysis through the sharp inequality

(1.4)

\|\Pi^{+}\overline{u}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\tfrac{1}{2\pi}M(u)\langle f,-i\partial_{x}f\rangle

valid for
u\in L^{2}_{+}
and
f\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{+}
; see
[
10
]
.
This inequality is saturated by the function

Q(x)=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{x+i}

which is the unique nonzero optimizer up to phase, translation, and
L^{2}
-scaling symmetries.
It is known as the soliton solution to (
CCM
) and does not evolve in time.
The soliton satisfies
M(Q)=2\pi
and
I_{k}(Q)=0
for all
k\geq 1
.

Within our proof, this mass threshold also occurs prominently in the existence of eigenvalues for the Lax operator. In
[
10
]
, it was shown that

(1.5)

2\pi N\leq M(u)

where
N
is the number of eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}_{u}
. Our proof requires initial data
u_{0}
for which the Lax operator
\mathcal{L}_{u_{0}}
has a negative eigenvalue, which is consistent with our solutions having mass at least
2\pi
.
The soliton
Q
and the multisolitons are introduced in
[
10
]
as optimizers of (
1.5
).

Let us briefly review the available well-posedness results on this model. Local well-posedness in
H^{s}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
for
s>\tfrac{1}{2}
was established by Gérard and Lenzmann in
[
10
]
.
This was shown for
s>\frac{3}{2}
via a Kato-type iterative scheme and standard energy methods and extended to
s>\frac{1}{2}
following an argument in
[
7
]
.
For
M(u)<2\pi
, Gérard and Lenzmann used (
1.4
) to control
\|u\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}
in terms of
P(u)
and
M(u)
.
They obtained the bounds (
1.3
) on
H^{s}
for all
s\geq 0
iteratively using the conserved quantities
I_{k}(u)
defined in (
1.2
).
This extended local well-posedness in
H^{s}_{+}
to global well-posedness in
H^{n}_{+}
for integers
n\geq 1
, so long as
M(u)<2\pi
.
They also showed that
H^{s}_{+}
solutions for
s\geq 1
to (
CCM
) with mass exactly
2\pi
cannot blow up in finite time, which extended global well-posedness to mass at most
2\pi
for such regular solutions.

Recent work by Laurens, Killip, and Vişan
[
12
]
extended global well-posedness below the mass of
2\pi
to
H^{s}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
for
s\geq 0
.
Central to their analysis was the concept of
L^{2}
-equicontinuity:

Definition 1.2

(
L^{2}
Equicontinuity)
.

A bounded set
U\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is said to be
equicontinuous
in the
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
topology if

\limsup_{\delta\to 0}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in U,\;|y|<\delta\end{subarray}}\|u(\cdot+y)-u(\cdot)\|_{L^{2}}=0,

or equivalently,

\limsup_{\kappa\to\infty}\,\sup_{u\in U}\int_{|\xi|\geq\kappa}|\widehat{u}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi=0.

The argument in
[
12
]
relied on showing that any
L^{2}
-equicontinuous family
U\subset H^{\infty}_{+}
of initial data leads to a family of partial orbits

U_{T}^{*}=\{u(t):u\text{ solves \eqref{CM-DNLS}},u(0)\in U,t\in[0,T)\}

which is
L^{2}
-equicontinuous for any choice of
T>0
.
They defined the sharp mass threshold
M^{*}
such that if
U\subset H^{\infty}_{+}
is
L^{2}
-equicontinuous and
\sup_{u_{0}\in U}M(u_{0})<M^{*}
, then
U_{T}^{*}
is also
L^{2}
-equicontinuous for all
T>0
.
They showed that
M^{*}\geq 2\pi
and that (
CCM
) is globally well-posed in
H^{s}_{+}
for all
s\geq 0
below the mass
M^{*}
.
The exact value of
M^{*}
is currently unknown, but Theorem
1.1
shows that global-in-time
L^{2}
-equicontinuity fails in general for mass greater than
2\pi
.
Specifically, for any
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, we find initial data
u_{0}\in H^{\infty}_{+}
with
M(u_{0})=2\pi+\varepsilon
for which the maximal orbit fails to be
L^{2}
-equicontinuous.

The local theory in
[
10
]
allows us to consider
H^{\infty}_{+}
solutions of (
CCM
), which are sufficient for our analysis.
For
H^{\infty}_{+}
solutions, Laurens, Killip, and Vişan showed in
[
12
]
that finite-time blowup must be accompanied by a loss of
L^{2}
-equicontinuity and hence simultaneous blowup in
H^{s}
for all
s>0
.
The well-posedness necessary for our main theorem is then stated as follows:

Proposition 1.3
.

For initial data
u_{0}\in H^{\infty}_{+}
there exists a maximal time
T\in(0,\infty]
and a unique solution
u(t)
such that
u\in C_{t}H^{s}_{+}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R})
for all
s\geq 0
.
Moreover, if
T<\infty
then

\lim_{t\nearrow T}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}=+\infty

for all
s>0
.
An analogous statement holds backwards in time.

1.1.
Overview of proof

We start by proving an orbital stability result for the soliton for a restricted class of initial data in Theorem
2.1
.
Specifically, we show that for any
\varepsilon>0
, if
u_{0}
is sufficiently close to
Q
in
H^{1}
and
u(t)
is uniformly bounded below in
\dot{H}^{1}
, then

\inf_{\lambda>0;\;\theta,y\in\mathbb{R}}\|e^{i\theta}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t,\lambda x+y)-Q(x)\|_{H^{1}_{x}}<\varepsilon,

for all times of existence
t
.
This allows us to choose parameters
\lambda(t)>0
and
\theta(t),y(t)\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(1.6)

\|e^{i\theta(t)}\lambda(t)^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t,\lambda(t)x+y(t))-Q(x)\|_{H^{1}_{x}}<\varepsilon.

The scaling parameter
\lambda(t)
then represents the characteristic width of
u(t)
.
We verify that this set of solutions is non-empty by showing that initial data
u_{0}\in H^{\infty}_{+}
for which
\mathcal{L}_{u_{0}}
has a negative eigenvalue lead to solutions that satisfy a uniform lower bound in
\dot{H}^{1}
; see Lemma
2.3
.
An example of such initial data is
u_{0}=(1+\varepsilon)Q
for
\varepsilon>0
as shown in Lemma
2.4
.

We then prove a dispersive decay bound in Theorem
3.1
that shows that global-in-time
H^{\infty}_{+}
solutions to (
CCM
) satisfy
|u(t,z)|\to 0
for
\Im z>0
as
t\to\infty
, where
u(t,z)
is defined by holomorphic extension to the upper half-plane.
For solutions satisfying (
1.6
), this dispersive decay is inconsistent with
\lambda(t)\sim 1
uniformly in
t
; we must either have that
\lambda(t)\to 0
or
\lambda(t)\to\infty
as
t\to\infty
.
The lower bound on
\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
rules out the case of
\lambda(t)\to\infty
and so we must have
\lambda(t)\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.
This forces
\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}
to grow to
\infty
as
t\to\infty
, as desired.
The lower bound
\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\gtrsim t^{s}
then follows from the rate of dispersive decay that we obtain.

1.3.
Notation

We use the standard notation
A\lesssim B
to indicate that
A\leq CB
for some universal constant
C>0
. If both
A\lesssim B
and
B\lesssim A
then we use the notation
A\sim B
.
When the implied constant fails to be universal, the relevant dependencies will be indicated within the text.

Our conventions for the Fourier transform are

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int e^{-i\xi x}f(x)dx\quad\text{so}\quad f(x)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int e^{i\xi x}\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi.

These Fourier transforms are unitary on
L^{2}
and hence yield the standard Plancherel identities. With these conventions, we define the Cauchy–Szegö projector
\Pi^{+}:L^{2}\to L^{2}_{+}
as

(1.7)

[\Pi^{+}f](x)=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{ix\xi}\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi.

For
s\geq 0
, we define the Sobolev spaces
H^{s}
as the completion of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
with respect to the norm

\|f\|^{2}_{H^{s}}=\int\big(1+|\xi|^{2}\big)^{s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi.

The homogeneous Sobolev spaces
\dot{H}^{s}
are defined similarly by the norm

\|f\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}=\int|\xi|^{2s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi.

We work in the Hardy-Sobolev spaces, denoted
H_{+}^{s}=H_{+}^{s}(\mathbb{R})\subset H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
, comprised of those functions whose Fourier transforms are supported on
[0,\infty)
as in (
1.1
). Equivalently,
H^{s}_{+}
is comprised of
f\in H^{s}
whose Poisson integral

f(z)=\int\frac{\Im z}{\pi|x-z|^{2}}f(x)dx\quad\text{defined for}\quad\Im z>0

is holomorphic in the upper half-plane. This formulation of
H^{s}_{+}
allows us to work interchangeably with
f(x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
f(z)
for
\Im z>0
. With the Poisson formulation, Hölder’s inequality implies

(1.8)

|f(z)|\lesssim(\Im z)^{-1/p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}

for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
. In this paper, we mainly work in the spaces
L^{2}_{+}=H^{0}_{+}
and
H^{\infty}_{+}=\cap_{s}H^{s}_{+}
.

We observe that (
CCM
) is invariant under phase rotation, translation, and
L^{2}
-critical scaling

u(t,x)\mapsto e^{i\theta}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}u(\lambda^{2}t,\lambda x+y)

for
\lambda>0
and
y,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. We adopt the notation

(1.9)

u_{\lambda,y,\theta}(t,x)=e^{i\theta}\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}u(t,\lambda x+y)

for
\lambda>0
and
y,\theta\in\mathbb{R}
. Note that only the spatial component is altered here.

2.
Orbital stability of the soliton

In this section we seek to prove

Theorem 2.1

(Restricted orbital stability of
Q
)
.

Fix
c>0
. For all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that if
u_{0}\in H^{1}
satisfies

\|u_{0}-Q\|_{H^{1}}<\delta

and the corresponding maximal lifespan solution
u
satisfies
\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\geq c
, then

\inf_{\lambda>0;\;\theta,y\in\mathbb{R}}\|u_{\lambda,\theta,y}(t)-Q\|_{H^{1}}<\varepsilon

for all times of existence
t
, using the notation from (
1.9
).

Note that in our orbital distance, the symmetries are applied to
u
rather than
Q
. This is reminiscent of modulation theory for gKdV and NLS,
[
13
,
14
,
20
]
.
In the case of
s=0
, scaling-criticality implies

\inf_{\lambda>0;\;\theta,y\in\mathbb{R}}\|u_{\lambda,\theta,y}-Q\|_{L^{2}}=\inf_{\lambda>0;\;\theta,y\in\mathbb{R}}\|u-Q_{\lambda,\theta,y}\|_{L^{2}},

which is the standard distance from
u
to the manifold of solitons. In contrast, for
s\neq 0
, the scaling term prevents the symmetries from being moved to
Q
without accruing additional factors of
\lambda
. Because of this, our orbital distance for
s\neq 0
differs from the standard notion.

We argue via a variational approach akin to Cazenave and Lions
[
6
]
. We therefore recall the characterization of
Q
as a minimizer of the energy, as given by Gérard and Lenzmann in
[
10
]
.

Lemma 2.2

(Minimal mass bubble,
[
10
]
)
.

Suppose that
(v^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset H^{1}
is a
L^{2}
-bounded sequence which satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}E(v^{n})=0,\;\;\;\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|v^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|Q\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\|v^{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=\mu>0\quad\text{for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then after possibly passing to a subsequence,

\|v^{n}_{\lambda,\theta,x_{n}}-Q\|_{L^{2}}\to 0

for some constants
\lambda>0
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, and some sequence
(x_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}
.

As
Q
is the unique energy minimizer up to symmetries (see
[
10
]
), the preceding lemma informally asserts that convergence of mass and energy to those of
Q
implies convergence in
L^{2}
to
Q
up to symmetries. Since mass and energy are conserved and
Q
is stationary, this suggests the flow of (
CCM
) should preserve closeness to
Q
in the
L^{2}
topology. To upgrade this to the
H^{1}
topology, we use the conservation of energy. This is formalized in what follows.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that there exist some
\varepsilon>0
and a sequence of initial data
u^{n}_{0}\to Q
in
H^{1}
with maximal lifespan solutions
u^{n}
such that
\|u^{n}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\geq c
and

\inf_{\lambda>0;\;\theta,y\in\mathbb{R}}\|u^{n}_{\lambda,\theta,y}(t_{n})-Q\|_{H^{1}}\geq\varepsilon

for some time of existence
t_{n}
.

To reach a contradiction, we aim to apply Lemma
2.2
to
u^{n}(t_{n})
. We first normalize
u^{n}(t_{n})
in
\dot{H}^{1}
and define

\lambda_{n}=\|u^{n}(t_{n})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{-1}\leq c^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad v^{n}(x)=u^{n}_{\lambda_{n},0,0}(t_{n}).

We note that

\sup_{n}\|v^{n}\|_{L^{2}}=\sup_{n}\|u^{n}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\|v^{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=\lambda_{n}\|u^{n}(t_{n})\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=1.

Furthermore,

0\leq E(v^{n})=\lambda_{n}^{2}E(u_{0}^{n})\leq c^{-2}E(u_{0}^{n})\to 0

because
u_{0}^{n}\to Q
in
H^{1}
.
Passing to a subsequence, Lemma
2.2
then guarantees that

\|v^{n}_{\lambda,\theta,x_{n}}-Q\|_{L^{2}}\to 0

for some
\lambda>0
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, and a sequence
(x_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}
.

Next, we upgrade this
L^{2}
-convergence to
H^{1}
-convergence using an energy argument. Define

w^{n}=v^{n}_{\lambda,\theta,x_{n}}-Q.

To show that
v^{n}_{\lambda,\theta,x_{n}}\to Q
in
H^{1}
it then suffices to show that
\|w^{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\to 0
.
Because

\mathcal{L}_{Q}Q=-iQ_{x}-\Pi_{+}(|Q|^{2})Q=0

we compute that

\displaystyle E(v^{n}_{\lambda,\theta,x_{n}})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\int|\partial_{x}w^{n}-i\Pi_{+}(|Q|^{2})w^{n}-i\Pi_{+}(|w^{n}|^{2}+2\mathrm{Re}\,\overline{Q}w^{n})(Q+w^{n})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}\int|\partial_{x}w^{n}|^{2}+O(\|w^{n}\|_{L^{2}}+\|w^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}).

We note that the preceding calculations repeatedly use the fact that
\|w^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 1
uniformly in
n
, which follows from Sobolev embedding.

Because
w^{n}\to 0
in
L^{2}
and
E(v^{n}_{\lambda,\theta,x_{n}})=\lambda^{2}E(v^{n})\to 0
, it then follows that
\|w^{n}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\to 0
and so
v^{n}_{\lambda,\theta,x_{n}}\to Q
in
H^{1}
.
This implies that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\inf_{\lambda>0;\;\theta,y\in\mathbb{R}}\|u^{n}_{\lambda,\theta,y}(t_{n})-Q\|_{H^{1}}=0

which is a contradiction.
∎

To verify that this stability result is not vacuous, we show that a solution
u(t)
for which
\mathcal{L}_{u}
has a negative eigenvalue satisfies
\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\gtrsim 1
uniformly over the times of existence.
As
\mathcal{L}_{u},\mathcal{P}_{u}
form a Lax pair, we expect that the map
t\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{u(t)}
is an isospectral deformation and thus that the negative eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}_{u}
is preserved in time.
This was shown in
[
15
]
in the case that
\mathcal{L}_{u}
and
\mathcal{P}_{u}
are matrices and applied to (
CM
).
To extend this result to the case where
\mathcal{L}_{u}
and
\mathcal{P}_{u}
are unbounded operators, more careful analysis is required.
For an example of this analysis for (
CCM
), see
[
12
]
, where it is shown that
t\mapsto\mathcal{L}_{u(t)}
is an isospectral deformation provided that
u(t)\in H^{\infty}_{+}
.

Lemma 2.3

(Uniform lower bound in
\dot{H}^{1}
)
.

Suppose there exist
\varepsilon>0
and
u_{0}\in H^{\infty}_{+}
such that
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\pi+\varepsilon
and such that
\mathcal{L}_{u_{0}}
has an eigenvalue in
(-\infty,-c\varepsilon]
for some
c>0
.
Let
u
denote the corresponding maximal lifespan solution to (
CCM
).
Then

\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\gtrsim c

uniformly for all times of existence
t
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{L}_{u(t)}
is isospectral, there exists an eigenvector
\psi(t)\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{+}
for all times of existence
t
with
\|\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}}=1
such that

\langle\psi(t),\mathcal{L}_{u(t)}\psi(t)\rangle\leq-c\varepsilon.

Expanding
\mathcal{L}_{u(t)}
yields

\displaystyle-c\varepsilon

\displaystyle\geq\langle\psi(t),-i\partial_{x}\psi(t)\rangle-\Bigg[\frac{2\pi}{\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}+\frac{\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2\pi}{\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\Bigg]\|\Pi^{+}\overline{u(t)}\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Inequality (
1.4
) then implies

\displaystyle c\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-2\pi}{\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\|\Pi^{+}\overline{u(t)}\psi(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2\pi+\varepsilon}\|u(t)\|_{\infty}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|u(t)\|_{\infty}^{2}.

Therefore
c\leq\|u\|_{\infty}^{2}
and so
c\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\lesssim\|u\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
as desired.
∎

We now show that initial data of the form
u_{0}=(1+\varepsilon)Q
for
\varepsilon>0
has a uniformly negative eigenvalue. This data will be the starting point for our proof of the turbulent threshold.

Lemma 2.4

(Existence of negative eigenvalue)
.

For any
\varepsilon>0
, the Lax operator
\mathcal{L}_{(1+\varepsilon)Q}
has a negative eigenvalue in
(-\infty,-\varepsilon]
.

Proof.

Because the Lax operator is self-adjoint,

\displaystyle 2\pi\inf\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{(1+\varepsilon)Q})

\displaystyle=\min_{\psi\neq 0}\frac{2\pi}{\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}\left\langle\psi,\mathcal{L}_{(1+\varepsilon)Q}\psi\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\left\langle Q,\mathcal{L}_{(1+\varepsilon)Q}Q\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle Q,-i\partial_{x}Q\right\rangle-(1+\varepsilon)^{2}\|\Pi_{+}|Q|^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\langle Q,\mathcal{L}_{Q}Q\right\rangle-(2\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{2})\pi

\displaystyle\leq-2\pi\varepsilon,

where the final equality follows from
\mathcal{L}_{Q}Q=0
. Therefore
\inf\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{(1+\varepsilon)Q})\leq-\varepsilon
. Because
\sigma_{\text{ess}}(\mathcal{L}_{(1+\varepsilon)Q})=[0,\infty)
(see
[
10
,
12
]
), this concludes the proof.
∎

3.
Dispersive decay

Our goal in this section is to establish the following dispersive decay estimate for solutions to (
CCM
) with suitable initial data:

Theorem 3.1

(Dispersive decay)
.

Given a set of initial data
U\subset H^{\infty}_{+}
which is bounded and equicontinuous in
L^{2}_{+}
and satisfies
\langle x\rangle u_{0}\in L^{2}
for all
u_{0}\in U
,

(3.1)

|u(t,z)|\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\big[1+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(1+(\Im z)^{-1})\big]

uniformly for
\Im z>0
,
u_{0}\in U
, and all times of existence
t
.

Using Theorem
2.1
, we will consider solutions
u(t)
for which

\|u_{\lambda(t),\theta(t),y(t)}(t)-Q\|_{H^{1}}<\varepsilon,

for some
\lambda(t)>0
and
\theta(t),y(t)\in\mathbb{R}
. Theorem
3.1
then implies that the characteristic width
\lambda(t)
of
u(t)
cannot satisfy
\lambda(t)\sim 1
uniformly in
t
.
This deviates from many classical stability results for which the scaling remained constant; e.g.
[
21
]
.

To prove Theorem
3.1
, we use the explicit formula established in
[
12
]
for
H^{\infty}_{+}
solutions to (
CCM
).
This explicit formula is of the type developed by Gérard and collaborators; see
[
8
,
9
,
11
]
.
A similar explicit formula was established for an intertwined matrix version of (
CCM
) in
[
17
]
.
The explicit formula for (
CCM
) was also shown to hold for initial data in
L^{2}_{+}
below the mass of
2\pi
in
[
12
]
, which can be used to expand the application of Theorem
3.1
. As we are largely interested in
H^{\infty}_{+}
solutions, we recall the explicit formula in that case.

Theorem 3.2

(Explicit formula)
.

For any
H^{\infty}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
solution
u(t)
to (
CCM
) with initial data satisfying
\langle x\rangle u_{0}\in L^{2}
,

u(t,z)=\tfrac{1}{2\pi i}I_{+}\Big\{(X+2t\mathcal{L}_{u_{0}}-z)^{-1}u_{0}\Big\}

for all
\Im z>0
and all times of existence
t
.

Here
X
and
I_{+}
are defined as follows:

Definition 3.3
.

The unbounded linear functional
I_{+}
is defined by

I_{+}(f):=\lim_{y\to\infty}\sqrt{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}ye^{-y\xi}\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi

with domain
D(I_{+})
given by the set of those
f\in L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
for which the limit exists.

Definition 3.4
.

The unbounded operator
X:L^{2}_{+}\to L^{2}_{+}
is given by

\widehat{Xf}(\xi):=i\tfrac{d\widehat{f}}{d\xi}(\xi)\quad\text{for}\quad f\in D(X)

where
D(X)
is the domain
D(X)=\{f\in L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R}):\widehat{f}\in H^{1}([0,\infty])
.

To simplify notation, let

A(t,z;u):=(X+2t\mathcal{L}_{u}-z)^{-1},

so that the explicit formula in Theorem
3.2
becomes

u(t,z)=\tfrac{1}{2\pi i}I_{+}A(t,z;u_{0})u_{0}.

Using the resolvent identity as was done in
[
12
]
, we may write

(3.2)

A(t,z;u_{0})=A_{0}(t,z)+2tA_{0}(t,z)u_{0}\Pi^{+}\overline{u_{0}}A(t,z;u_{0})

where
A_{0}(t,z)=A(t,z;0)
.

We will employ the following bounds on
A_{0}
and
A
:

Proposition 3.5

(Inequalities (4.21) and (4.41) from
[
12
]
)
.

The operator
I_{+}A_{0}
is bounded on
L^{1}
, namely

|I_{+}A_{0}(t,z)f|\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{L^{1}},

uniformly for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\Im z>0
.

Given a bounded and equicontinuous set
U\subset L^{2}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
, the bound

\|A(t,z;u)f\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim|t|^{-1}\big[1+(\Im z)^{-1}\big]\|f\|_{L^{1}}

holds uniformly for
u\in U
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
f\in L^{1}
, and
\Im z>0
.

Combining these, we establish a dispersive decay for
H^{\infty}_{+}
solutions.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Fix
u_{0}\in U
.
A simple Grönwall argument ensures that
\langle x\rangle u(t)\in L^{2}
for all times of existence and Hölder’s inequality then guarantees that
U\subset L^{1}
.
Combining the explicit formula in Theorem
3.2
, the resolvent identity (
3.2
), and the bounds from Proposition
3.5
, we may estimate

\displaystyle|u(t,z)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\left|I_{+}\{A_{0}(t,z)u_{0}\}\right|+|t|\left|I_{+}\{A_{0}(t,z)u_{0}\Pi^{+}\overline{u_{0}}A(t,z;u_{0})u_{0}\}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+|t|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\Pi^{+}\overline{u_{0}}A(t,z;u_{0})u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\big[1+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(1+(\Im z)^{-1})\big]

uniformly for
\Im z>0
,
u_{0}\in U
, and all times of existence
t
.
∎

4.
Growth of Sobolev norms

In this section, we find
H^{\infty}_{+}
solutions arbitrarily close to, but above, the mass threshold of
2\pi
that exhibit global Sobolev growth:

Theorem 4.1

(Turbulent behaviour)
.

For any sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, there exist initial data
u_{0}\in H^{\infty}_{+}(\mathbb{R})
with
M(u_{0})=2\pi+\varepsilon
, a time
T\in(0,\infty]
, and a solution
u(t)
to (
CCM
) such that for all
s>0
,
u\in C_{t}H^{s}_{x}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R})
and

\lim_{t\nearrow T}\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}=+\infty.

In particular, if
T=\infty
, then we have the bounds

\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\gtrsim t^{s}.

An analogous statement holds backwards in time.

Our initial data will be a perturbation of the soliton
Q
belonging to
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}_{+}
.
This is more than sufficient to satisfy the regularity constraints arising in Theorem
3.1
.
Our proof will require that the implicit constants which come from Theorem
3.1
are uniform; see the progression from (
4.2
) to (
4.3
).
We therefore construct an
L^{2}
-equicontinuous family of initial data as follows:

Lemma 4.2
.

There exists an
L^{2}
-equicontinuous family
U=\{u_{0}^{\varepsilon}:0<\varepsilon\leq 1\}\subset L^{2}_{+}\cap\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\|u_{0}^{\varepsilon}-Q\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon
and the corresponding solutions
u^{\varepsilon}(t)
satisfy

\|u^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\gtrsim 1\quad\text{and}\quad\inf_{\lambda>0;\;\theta,y\in\mathbb{R}}\|u_{\lambda,\theta,y}^{\varepsilon}(t)-Q\|_{H^{1}}<\varepsilon

uniformly for all times of existence
t
and initial data
u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\in U
.

Proof.

To define
u_{0}^{\varepsilon}
, we begin with
(1+\varepsilon)Q
which has a negative eigenvalue in
(-\infty,-\varepsilon]
as shown in Lemma
2.4
.
We recall that
Q
has the Fourier transform

(4.1)

\widehat{Q}(\xi)=2\sqrt{\pi}ie^{-\xi}\chi_{\{\xi\geq 0\}}.

Therefore the only obstruction to
(1+\varepsilon)Q
belonging to
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
is that
(1+\varepsilon)\widehat{Q}
has a discontinuity at
\xi=0
.

We define
u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\in\mathcal{S}\cap L^{2}_{+}
to be a smoothing of
(1+\varepsilon)Q
on the Fourier side which removes the discontinuity at
\xi=0
. This can be done with sufficiently small perturbation in
H^{1}
to ensure that
\mathcal{L}_{u^{\varepsilon}_{0}}
has a negative eigenvalue in
(-\infty,\frac{\varepsilon}{2}]
.

Let
u^{\varepsilon}(t)
denote the maximal solution to (
CCM
) with initial data
u_{0}^{\varepsilon}
. The bounds
\|u^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\gtrsim 1
and
\inf_{\lambda>0;\theta,y\in\mathbb{R}}\|u_{\lambda,\theta,\varepsilon}^{\varepsilon}(t)-Q\|_{H^{1}}<\varepsilon
then follow from Theorem
2.1
and Lemma
2.3
.
Since
L^{2}
-equicontinuity is equivalent to tightness on the Fourier side, the equicontinuity of
U
follows from the exponential decay of
\widehat{Q}
.
∎

With the initial data established, we now prove the main result.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Fix initial data
u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\in U
from Lemma
4.2
for
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
to be chosen later. Let
u^{\varepsilon}(t)
denote the maximal solution to (
CCM
) with initial data
u^{\varepsilon}_{0}
.

Choose
\lambda(t)>0
and
y(t),\theta(t)\in\mathbb{R}
for all times of existence
t
so that

\|u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda(t),\theta(t),y(t)}(t)-Q\|_{H^{1}}<\varepsilon.

Define

q(t,x)=e^{-i\theta(t)}\lambda(t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}Q\Big(\frac{x-y(t)}{\lambda(t)}\Big).

By scaling criticality, we note that
\|u^{\varepsilon}(t)-q(t)\|_{L^{2}}<\varepsilon
.
We will start by showing that
\lambda(t)\to 0
and thus
\|q(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\to\infty
for all
s>0
.

We initially show a uniform upper bound for
\lambda(t)
. Since
\|u^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\gtrsim 1
, we find

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u^{\varepsilon}(t)-q(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\|q(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle=\lambda(t)^{-1}\|u^{\varepsilon}_{\lambda(t),\theta(t),y(t)}(t)-Q\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}+\lambda(t)^{-1}\|Q\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda(t)^{-1}\varepsilon+\lambda(t)^{-1}\|Q\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}.

Therefore
\lambda(t)\lesssim\varepsilon+\|Q\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\lesssim 1
for
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
.

To conclude
\lambda(t)\to 0
, we use the dispersive decay bounds. By Theorem
3.1
and inequality (
1.8
), for all
\Im z>0
,

\displaystyle|q(t,z)|

\displaystyle\leq|q(t,z)-u^{\varepsilon}(t,z)|+|u^{\varepsilon}(t,z)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|q(t)-u^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{2}}(\Im z)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\big[1+\|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(1+(\Im z)^{-1})\big]

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon(\Im z)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\big[1+\|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(1+(\Im z)^{-1})\big].

Note that the implicit constants are uniform in
\varepsilon
by the
L^{2}
-equicontinuity of
U
.

We now evaluate this inequality at the point
z(t)=y(t)+i\lambda(t)
.
Direct calculation shows

(4.2)

(2\lambda(t))^{-\frac{1}{2}}=|q(z(t))|\lesssim\varepsilon\lambda(t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\big[1+\|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(1+\lambda(t)^{-1})\big].

Because the implicit constants are uniform in
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
, we may take
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small to obtain

(4.3)

\lambda(t)^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\big[1+\|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}(1+\lambda(t)^{-1})\big],

and so

|t|^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\Big[\big(1+\|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\lambda(t)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\lambda(t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Big].

The upper bounds on
\lambda(t)
and
\|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
then imply

|t|^{\frac{1}{2}}\lesssim\|u^{\varepsilon}_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\big[1+\lambda(t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big].

Since
\|u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}}<\infty
, it follows that
\lambda(t)\lesssim|t|^{-1}
for sufficiently large
|t|
.

Using the formula (
4.1
) for
\widehat{Q}
, direct calculation yields

\displaystyle\|u^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}^{2}

\displaystyle\gtrsim|\lambda(t)|^{-2s}\int_{1/\lambda(t)}^{\infty}|\widehat{u^{\varepsilon}}(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\gtrsim|\lambda(t)|^{-2s}\bigg(\int_{1/\lambda(t)}^{\infty}|\widehat{q}(t,\xi)|^{2}d\xi-\varepsilon\bigg)

\displaystyle=|\lambda(t)|^{-2s}\bigg(\int_{1/\lambda(t)}^{\infty}4\pi\lambda(t)e^{-2\lambda(t)\xi}d\xi-\varepsilon\bigg)

\displaystyle=|\lambda(t)|^{-2s}\big(2\pi e^{-2}-\varepsilon\big).

Choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, this implies that
\|u^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\gtrsim|t|^{s}
for sufficiently large
|t|
.
Because
u^{\varepsilon}
is either global in time or exhibits finite-time blowup, this concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

A natural further question is whether there is interesting behaviour below the
2\pi
mass threshold.
From the a priori
H^{s}
bounds, we cannot expect Sobolev norm growth, but there is potential for some decay in the homogeneous Sobolev spaces
\dot{H}^{s}
for
s>0
.
This is formalized in the following corollary.

Corollary 4.3
.

For all
c,\varepsilon,s>0
there exists initial data
u_{0}\in H^{\infty}_{+}
such that
M(u_{0})<2\pi
and
\|u_{0}-Q\|_{L^{2}}<\varepsilon
for which the global solution
u(t)
satisfies

\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\leq c.

Proof.

We begin with the case
s=1
.
Suppose for the sake of contradiction that there exist
c_{0},\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for all initial data
u_{0}\in H^{\infty}_{+}
with
M(u_{0})<2\pi
and
\|u_{0}-Q\|_{L^{2}}<\varepsilon_{0}
, the corresponding global solution
u(t)
satisfies

\inf_{t}\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}>c_{0}.

Starting with initial data of the form
(1-\varepsilon)Q
for
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
, we follow the proof of Lemma
4.2
to construct an
L^{2}
-equicontinuous family
U=\{u_{0}^{\varepsilon}:0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}\}
satisfying all the conditions in Lemma
4.2
along with
M(u_{0}^{\varepsilon})<2\pi
for all
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
.
By only smoothing
(1-\varepsilon)\widehat{Q}
near
\xi=0
, we further ensure for all
r>0
,

(4.4)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\|u_{0}^{\varepsilon}-Q\|_{H^{r}}=0.

This will be necessary to establish the case when
0\leq s<1
.
Note that in the proof of Lemma
4.2
, we used initial data with a negative eigenvalue in order to gain a lower bound in
\dot{H}^{1}
.
In contrast, here we have supposed for the sake of contradiction that such a lower bound exists.
This is necessary because (
1.5
) implies that eigenvalues cannot exist below the mass of
2\pi
.

Fix
u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\in U
for
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
. Because
M(u_{0}^{\varepsilon})<2\pi
, there exists a global solution
u^{\varepsilon}(t)
to (
CCM
) with initial data
u_{0}^{\varepsilon}
.
Following the same computations as in the proof of Theorem
4.1
, we choose sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
and find that
\|u^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\gtrsim|t|
for sufficiently large
|t|
. This contradicts the a priori
H^{1}
bounds from
[
10
]
, concluding the proof of the corollary in the case
s=1
.

For
0<s<1
, we use the bound

\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{s}\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}}^{1-s}

along with conservation of mass to prove the result.

For
s>1
, we choose an integer
n>2s-1
.
Since
u_{0}^{\varepsilon}\to Q
in
H^{n}
by (
4.4
), it follows that the conserved quantities
I_{0}(u^{\varepsilon}),...,I_{2n}(u^{\varepsilon})
are uniformly bounded and thus
\|u^{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{H^{n}}
satisfy global-in-time
H^{s}
bounds uniformly in both
t
and
\varepsilon
; see
[
10
]
.
We then use the inequality

\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}\lesssim\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u^{\varepsilon}\|_{\dot{H}^{2s-1}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

to conclude the proof.
∎

Despite this corollary, it is impossible for individual
H^{\infty}_{+}
solutions to decay to zero in
\dot{H}^{1}
.
A quick calculation shows that

E(u)\lesssim\big[1+M(u)^{2}\big]\|u(t)\|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{1}}

and so energy provides a non-trivial lower bound for
\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}
as soon as
E(u)>0
.
In
[
10
]
, it was shown that
\mathcal{L}_{u}u=0
, and hence
E(u)=0
, if and only if either
u=0
or
u=Q_{\lambda,\theta,y}
for some
\lambda>0
and
\theta,y\in\mathbb{R}
.
Since these solutions do not evolve in time, this rules out the possibility of an individual nonzero solution satisfying
\inf_{t}\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}=0
.

For
s>1
, the bound

\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\lesssim\|f\|^{1-\frac{1}{s}}_{L^{2}}\|f\|^{\frac{1}{s}}_{\dot{H}^{s}}

shows that decay in
\dot{H}^{s}
implies decay in
\dot{H}^{1}
.
Thus there cannot exist an individual solution
u
in
H^{\infty}_{+}
which satisfies
\inf_{t}\|u(t)\|_{\dot{H}^{s}}=0
.
For
0\leq s<1
, the global-in-time bounds on
H^{s}
and the bound

\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{1}}\lesssim\|f\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\dot{H}^{s}}\|f\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\dot{H}^{2-s}}

imply that the same statement holds for
H^{\infty}_{+}
solutions with mass below
2\pi
.

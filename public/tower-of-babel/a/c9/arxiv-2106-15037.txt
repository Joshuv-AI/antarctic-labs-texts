Directional asymptotics of Fejér monotone sequences
Heinz H. Bauschke, Manish Krishan Lal, Xianfu Wang

Abstract
The notion of Fejér monotonicity is instrumental in unifying the convergence proofs of many iterative methods, such as the Krasnoselskii–Mann iteration, the proximal point method, the Douglas-Rachford splitting algorithm, and many others. In this paper, we present directionally asymptotical results of strongly convergent subsequences of Fejér monotone sequences. We also provide examples to show that the sets of directionally asymptotic cluster points can be large and that weak convergence is needed in infinite-dimensional spaces.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence in

. Then the following hold:

(i)

The largest Fejér monotone set of

and is closed convex.

(ii)

If

is a Fejér monotone set of
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

\overline{\operatorname{conv}}\,C

, is a Fejér monotone set of
(x_{n})_{{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}}
.

(iii)

If
C_{1},C_{2}
are Fejér monotone sets of
(x_{n})_{{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}}
, then

C_{1}\cup C_{2}
is a Fejér monotone set of
(x_{n})_{{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}}
.

(iv)

If

is a Fejér monotone set of

of every subsequence of

Proof.

Item

\Leftrightarrow

2\left\langle{x_{n}-x_{n+1}},{z}\right\rangle\leq\|x_{n}\|^{2}-\|x_{n+1}\|^{2}

Item

(ii)
&
Item

(iii)
: These follow from
Item

(i)
.

Item

: Obvious from the definition of Fejér monotonicity.

We now localize the set of weak cluster points of a sequence.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
C
be a nonempty subset of
X
and let
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence in
X
.
Suppose that

\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\sigma_{C}(x_{n})\leq 0.

Then

\mathcal{C}\big((x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\big)\subseteq C^{\ominus}.

Proof.

Suppose that

x\in\mathcal{C}\big((x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\big)
and to the contrary that

x\notin C^{\ominus}
.
Then
\sigma_{C}(x)>0
and there exists
a weakly convergent subsequence
(x_{k_{n}})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that

x_{k_{n}}\rightharpoonup x
.
The weak lower semicontinuity of
\sigma_{C}
now implies

0<\sigma_{C}(x)\leq\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\sigma_{C}(x_{k_{n}})\leq\varlimsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{C}(x_{k_{n}})\leq\varlimsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{C}(x_{n})\leq 0,

which is absurd!
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose that
X
is finite-dimensional,
let
C
be a nonempty closed subset of
X
, and let
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a bounded sequence
in
X
. Then

d_{C}(x_{n})\to 0\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;\mathcal{C}\big((x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\big)\subseteq C.

Proof.

“
\Rightarrow
”:
Let
x
be a cluster point in
\mathcal{C}\big((x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\big)
, say

x_{k_{n}}\rightarrow x
.
The continuity of

and the assumption yield

d_{C}(x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{C}(x_{k_{n}})=\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{C}(x_{n})=0.

Hence
x\in C
because

is closed.

“
\Leftarrow
”:
Suppose to the contrary that

\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}d_{C}(x_{n})>0
.
Then there exists a subsequence
(x_{k_{n}})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
of
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}d_{C}(x_{k_{n}})=\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}d_{C}(x_{n})>0.

(2.4)

Recall that
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is bounded and

is finite-dimensional.
Using Bolzano-Weierstrass and after passing to another subsequence
and relabeling, we may and do assume that

.

.
But then

2.4
.
∎

We end this section with results on linear isometries.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
A:X\to X
be a linear isometry.
Then the following hold:

(i)

A
is injective.

(ii)

If
T:=\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id}+\tfrac{1}{2}A
, then

(\forall x\in X)

Tx\perp(x-Tx)
.

(iii)

If
A^{*}=A^{-1}=-A
, then

J_{A}=\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id}-\tfrac{1}{2}A
and

(\forall x\in X)

J_{A}x\perp(x-J_{A}x)
.

Proof.

Let
x\in X
.

Item

(i)
: If
Ax=0
, then
0=\|Ax\|=\|x\|
and so
x=0
.

Item

(ii)
:
Note that
\operatorname{Id}-T=\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id}-\tfrac{1}{2}
. Hence

4\left\langle{Tx},{x-Tx}\right\rangle=\left\langle{x+Ax},{x-Ax}\right\rangle=\|x\|^{2}-\|Ax\|^{2}=0.

Item

(iii)
:
Clearly,
\pm A
is monotone and
A^{2}=-\operatorname{Id}
.
Hence,
[
3
, Proposition 2.10]
yields
J_{A}=\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id}-\tfrac{1}{2}A=\operatorname{Id}-T
, where
T=\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id}+\tfrac{1}{2}A
.
Now apply
Item

(ii)
.
∎

.

,

Proof.

is a maximally monotone isometry.

Item

(iii)

Directional asymptotics of Fejér monotone sequences

We are now ready for our main results on the directionally asymptotic behaviour of

The proofs significantly extend the reach of those brought to light by

[

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence in
X
that is Fejér monotone with respect to
some nonempty closed convex subset
Z
of
X
.
Suppose that
x_{n}\to\widebar{z}\in X
and that

(\forall{n\in{\mathbb{N}}})

x_{n+1}\neq x_{n}\neq\widebar{z}
.
Then

\mathcal{C}\bigg(\Big(\frac{x_{n}-x_{n+1}}{\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\|}\Big)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\bigg)\cup\mathcal{C}\bigg(\Big(\frac{x_{n}-\widebar{z}}{\|x_{n}-\widebar{z}\|}\Big)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\bigg)\subseteq(Z-\widebar{z})^{\ominus};

(3.1)

in particular, if
\widebar{z}\in Z
, then we may replace
(Z-\widebar{z})^{\ominus}
by

N_{Z}(\widebar{z})
in
Equation

3.1
.

Proof.

Let
{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
.
Taking the supremum over
z\in Z
in
Equation

2.1
yields

\displaystyle\sigma_{Z-\widebar{z}}(x_{n}-x_{n+1})

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{n+1}-\widebar{z}\|\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\|+\tfrac{1}{2}\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\|^{2}.

(3.2)

Dividing
Equation

3.2
by
\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\|
and using the positive
homogeneity of
\sigma_{Z-\widebar{z}}
, we have

\sigma_{Z-\widebar{z}}\Big(\frac{x_{n}-x_{n+1}}{\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\|}\Big)\leq\|x_{n+1}-\widebar{z}\|+\tfrac{1}{2}\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\|.

(3.3)

Because
x_{n}\to\widebar{z}
and so
x_{n}-x_{n+1}\to 0
, we let

n\to\infty
in
Equation

3.3
to learn that

\varlimsup_{n\to\infty}\sigma_{Z-\widebar{z}}\Big(\frac{x_{n}-x_{n+1}}{\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\|}\Big)\leq 0.

(3.4)

Combining
Equation

3.4
with
Lemma

2.3
,
we obtain

\mathcal{C}\bigg(\Big(\frac{x_{n}-x_{n+1}}{\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\|}\Big)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\bigg)\subseteq(Z-\widebar{z})^{\ominus}.

(3.5)

Next, let
m\geq n+1
.
Taking the supermum over
z\in Z
in
Equation

2.2
yields

\displaystyle\sigma_{Z-\widebar{z}}(x_{n}-x_{m})

\displaystyle\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\|x_{n}-\widebar{z}\|^{2}-\|x_{m}-\widebar{z}\|^{2}\big).

(3.6)

Passing to the limit as
m\to\infty
and using the lower semicontinuity of

\sigma_{Z-\widebar{z}}
in
Equation

3.6
, we obtain

\sigma_{Z-\widebar{z}}(x_{n}-\widebar{z})\leq\varliminf_{m\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{Z-\widebar{z}}(x_{n}-x_{m})\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|x_{n}-\widebar{z}\|^{2}.

(3.7)

Dividing
Equation

3.7
by

\|x_{n}-\widebar{z}\|
and
using the positive homogeneity of
\sigma_{Z-\widebar{z}}
, we have

\sigma_{Z-\widebar{z}}\Big(\frac{x_{n}-\widebar{z}}{\|x_{n}-\widebar{z}\|}\Big)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\|x_{n}-\widebar{z}\|.

(3.8)

Because
x_{n}\to\widebar{z}
, we let
n\to\infty
in
Equation

3.8
and get

\varlimsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sigma_{Z-\widebar{z}}\Big(\frac{x_{n}-\widebar{z}}{\|x_{n}-\widebar{z}\|}\Big)\leq 0.

(3.9)

Combining
Equation

3.9
with our trusted
Lemma

2.3
, we obtain

\mathcal{C}\bigg(\Big(\frac{x_{n}-\widebar{z}}{\|x_{n}-\widebar{z}\|}\Big)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\bigg)\subseteq(Z-\widebar{z})^{\ominus}.

(3.10)

Altogether,
Equation

3.5
and

imply
Equation

3.1
.
∎

4
Large sets of directionally asymptotic cluster points

In this section, we give an example illustrating that the sets of directionally asymptotic

3.4
,

3.12

We start with a fact from real analysis:

Fact 4.1

(
Dirichlet).

(See, e.g.,
[
12
, page 88]
)

Let
\alpha\in{{\mathbb{R}}}\smallsetminus{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. Then the set

\{{n\alpha-\left\lfloor n\alpha\right\rfloor}~|~{{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}}\}
is dense in
[0,1]
.

For the remainder of this section,
R_{\alpha}
denotes
the counterclockwise rotator in the Euclidean plane by
\alpha
.

Example 4.2

5
Missing the sphere: an infinite-dimensional example

It is interesting to ask whether in infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
the nonempty sets of weak cluster points in

lie in
the sphere
{{\mathbb{S}}}
.
It turns out that the answer is negative, and the sequence provided
is obtained by iterating a resolvent. To this end,
we assume in this section that

X=\ell^{2}\big(\{1,2,\ldots\}\big),

with the standard Schauder basis

e_{1}:=(1,0,0,\ldots),e_{2}:=(0,1,0,0,\ldots)
, and so on.
We define the
right-shift operator
by

R\colon X\to X\colon(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots)\mapsto(0,\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\ldots),

Then
R
is a linear isometry with
\operatorname{Fix}R=\{0\}
.
We shall also require the following classical identity

Fact 5.1

(
Vandermonde’s identity).

(See
[
9
, Section 5.1]
.)

Let

be in
{{\mathbb{N}}}
. Then

\binom{m+n}{r}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\binom{m}{r}\binom{n}{r-k}.

Example 5.2
.

Define the firmly nonexpansive operator
T\colon X\to X
by

T:=\tfrac{1}{2}\operatorname{Id}+\tfrac{1}{2}R,

set
x_{0}:=e_{1}
, and

(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}:=(T^{n}x_{0})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
with
x_{0}=e_{1}.

Then the following hold:

(i)

(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is Fejér monotone with respect to
\operatorname{Fix}T=\{0\}
, and

x_{n}\to 0
. Moreover,
(\forall{n\in{\mathbb{N}}})

x_{n+1}\neq x_{n}\neq 0
.

(ii)

(\forall{n\in{\mathbb{N}}})\ \left\langle{x_{n+1}},{x_{n}-x_{n+1}}\right\rangle=0.

(iii)

Both

\Big(\frac{x_{n}}{\|x_{n}\|}\Big)_{{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}}\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\Big(\frac{x_{n}-x_{n+1}}{\|x_{n}-x_{n+1}\|}\Big)_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

.

Proof.

Item

(i)
: It is well known that
(x_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}

, because
T

, we have

(5.1)

Hence, by
5.1
,

\|x_{n}\|^{2}=\frac{1}{4^{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}^{2}=\frac{1}{4^{n}}\binom{2n}{n}=\frac{1}{4^{n}}\frac{(2n)!}{(n!)^{2}};

in particular,
x_{n}\neq 0
.
x_{n+1}\neq x_{n}
because
x_{n+1}
contains a nonzero term of

e_{n+2}
and
e_{n+2}\perp e_{k+1}
for

.

Now recall

(see, e.g.,
[
13
, Theorem 5.44]
)
which states that

(5.2)

for large

,

Item

(ii)
: Since

Lemma

2.5
Item

(ii)
.

Item

(iii)

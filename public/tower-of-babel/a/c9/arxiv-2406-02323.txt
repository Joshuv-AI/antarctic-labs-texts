The geometric Toda equations for noncompact symmetric spaces. Thanks: For the purposes of open access, the author has applied a Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY) to any Author Accepted Manuscript version arising from this submission.
Ian McIntosh

Abstract
. This paper has two purposes. The first is to classify all those versions of the Toda equations which govern the existence of τ \tau -primitive harmonic maps from a surface into a homogeneous space G / T G/T for which G G is a noncomplex noncompact simple real Lie group, τ \tau is the Coxeter automorphism which Drinfel’d & Sokolov assigned to each affine Dynkin diagram, and T T is the compact torus fixed pointwise by τ \tau . Here τ \tau may be either an inner or an outer automorphism. We interpret the Toda equations over a compact Riemann surface Σ \Sigma as equations for a metric on a holomorphic principal T ℂ T^{\mathbb{C}} -bundle Q ℂ Q^{\mathbb{C}} over Σ \Sigma whose Chern connection, when combined with holomorphic field φ \varphi , produces a G G -connection which is flat precisely when the Toda equations hold. The second purpose is to establish when stability criteria for the pair ( Q ℂ , φ ) (Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi) can be used to prove the existence of solutions. We classify those real forms of the Toda equations for which this pair is a principal pair and we call these totally noncompact Toda pairs: stability theory then gives algebraic conditions for the existence of solutions. Every solution to the geometric Toda equations has a corresponding G G -Higgs bundle. We explain how to construct this G G -Higgs bundle directly from the Toda pair and show that Baraglia’s cyclic Higgs bundles arise from a very special case of totally noncompact cyclic Toda pairs.

1.
Introduction.

The version of the Toda equations we are concerned with here comes from the study of a special class of
harmonic maps from a Riemann surface
\Sigma
into a Riemannian symmetric space
N
. In the case where surface is a torus and the
symmetric space is of compact type with simple compact isometry group
G
the Toda equations have the form

\Delta_{\Sigma}w_{j}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\hat{C}_{jk}e^{w_{k}},\quad\text{with}\ \sum_{j=0}^{r}m_{j}w_{j}=0.

(1.1)

Here
w_{j}:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
are smooth functions,
\Delta_{\Sigma}=d^{*}d
is the (non-negative) Laplacian on functions on
\Sigma
, the matrix

\hat{C}
is an
(r+1)\times(r+1)
affine Cartan matrix associated to the complexification
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
of the Lie algebra of
G
, and the
positive integers
m_{j}
are the coefficients of linear dependence of the rows of
\hat{C}
normalized so that
m_{0}=1

(a summary of the relevant facts about affine Cartan matrices is given in §
3
below).
Equations (
1.1
) are a real form of what is often called the affine Toda (field) equations: there is also a
version when
\hat{C}
is replaced by the standard Cartan matrix
C
for
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
, which we will refer to as the non-affine Toda equations.

Equations (
1.1
) govern the existence of a “
\tau
-primitive” harmonic map into a
homogeneous space
G/T
which lies over the symmetric space
N\simeq G/H
by homogeneous projection. Here
T
is a maximal
torus in the isotropy subgroup
H<G
and
r=\dim(T)
. This
\tau
-primitive
map projects down to produce a conformal harmonic, and hence minimal, surface in
N
. Here
\tau
denotes a
specific finite order automorphism on
G
, called the Coxeter automorphism, whose fixed-point subgroup is
T
.
The harmonic map into
G/T
is often referred to as the
\tau
-primitive lift of the harmonic map to

N
and the idea has its origins in Riemannian twistor theory (see especially
[
9
,
29
,
5
]
).

In the literature the affine Cartan matrix
\hat{C}
is usually taken to correspond to an extended Dynkin diagram, in which case
\tau
is an
inner automorphism,
T<G
is a maximal torus and
N
is an inner symmetric space (one can also use a symmetry trick
to adapt these inner Coxeter Toda systems to get harmonic maps into outer symmetric spaces: see e.g.
[
28
,
6
,
7
,
3
]
).
However, to treat inner and outer symmetric spaces on equal footing we allow
\hat{C}
to be the affine Cartan matrix of any affine
diagram, following Drinfel’d & Sokolov
[
12
]
.

To adapt the Toda equations to a noncompact real group
G
it must be preserved by the Coxeter
automorphism
\tau
and the compact torus
T<G
fixed pointwise. The effect on (
1.1
) is to produce a
different distribution of plus and minus signs
on the coefficients. Classifying which of these are possible amounts to answering the question: which real forms
\mathfrak{g}
of
a complex simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
are invariant under a Coxeter automorphism
\tau
which fixes a compact
toral subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}\subset\mathfrak{g}
?
We originally thought this question had been answered, at least for inner Coxeter automorphisms, by Carberry &
Turner in
[
10
]
, but closer inspection shows that they have overlooked many cases (essentially they overlook the
fact that a real involution can also change the sign as it swaps root vectors and is therefore not determined by its
action on roots alone).

Our classification goes as follows. Let
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
have Dynkin diagram
\Gamma
.
Recall (from e.g.
[
17
, Ch. X]
) that for each symmetry

\nu
of
\Gamma
there is a unique affine diagram
\Gamma^{(n)}
where
n\in\{1,2,3\}
is the order of
\nu

(these can be found in Appendix
B
). To each

\Gamma^{(n)}
we assign a unique, up to conjugacy, Coxeter automorphism
\tau
which is an inner automorphism when
n=1

(the more usual case) and outer otherwise.
Every noncompact real form can be classified up to equivalence by its Cartan involution
\sigma
, thought of as a complex involution.
Since we are assuming
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
is simple the noncompact symmetric spaces we describe are those of Type III in the standard
classification (cf.
[
17
]
), i.e., the real form
\mathfrak{g}
is not itself a complex Lie algebra.
We say
\sigma
is compatible with
\tau
when it commutes with
\tau
and fixes pointwise the fixed-point subalgebra of
\tau
.
We say two such involutions are
\tau
-equivalent when they are conjugate in
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}})
by an automorphism which commutes
with
\tau
. The following theorem combines Prop.
3.9
and Cor.
3.10
below.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma^{(n)}
,
n\in\{1,2,3\}
, be an affine Dynkin diagram with vertex set indexed by
I=\{0,\ldots,r\}
and
Coxeter automorphism
\tau
. Every involution
\sigma

of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
which is compatible with
\tau
is determined by a map
\ell:I\to\{-1,1\}
for which
-1\in\ell(I)

and satisfying
\prod_{j\in I}\ell(j)^{nm_{j}}=1
.
Two such involutions are
\tau
-equivalent if and only if they are related by a symmetry of
\Gamma^{(n)}
.
Every noncompact symmetric space of Type III has a least one compatible Coxeter automorphism.

The vertices of
\Gamma^{(n)}
correspond to the simple affine roots, and the labels
\ell(j)
are the eigenvalues of

\sigma
on the corresponding root space. Since those root spaces generate
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
under Lie bracket
this determines
\sigma
completely, with the relation being the sole condition required to ensure that
\sigma
is well-defined
as an automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
. The distribution
of signs within the Toda equations follows easily. Each labelling
\ell
partitions
I
into
I_{+}\cup I_{-}
according to the
sign, and the corresponding affine Toda equations become

\Delta_{\Sigma}w_{j}=\sum_{k\in I_{+}}^{r}\hat{C}_{jk}e^{w_{k}}-\sum_{k\in I_{-}}^{r}\hat{C}_{jk}e^{w_{k}},\quad\ \sum_{j=0}^{r}m_{j}w_{j}=0.

(1.2)

When
\Sigma
is a torus these equations are the zero curvature conditions for a certain flat
G
-connection, where
G
is
the real form determined by the choice of signs, and their solution
corresponds to a
\tau
-primitive map
\psi:\tilde{\Sigma}\to G/T
which is equivariant with respect to the holonomy
representation
\pi_{1}\Sigma\to G
of that flat connection.

When the compact Riemann surface
\Sigma
is not a torus these equations need to be modified to fulfil the same purpose.
In the second half of the paper we derive these
geometric Toda equations
. Motivated
by the relationship between Higgs bundles and harmonic maps, we take the point of view that the Toda equations are
really the equations for a metric, or equivalently a reduction of structure group, from a holomorphic principal
T^{\mathbb{C}}
-bundle
Q^{\mathbb{C}}

over
\Sigma
to a
T
-bundle
Q\subset Q^{\mathbb{C}}
. The equations governing the metric require an initial choice of Hermitian metric on

\Sigma
and the bundle
Q^{\mathbb{C}}
, together with a holomorphic section
\varphi
of the associated bundle
Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})\otimes K_{\Sigma}
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
is the direct sum of root spaces for the simple affine roots.

Since
T^{\mathbb{C}}
is abelian and its action on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
is completely reducible we can equate this pair
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)

with a choice of line bundles
and holomorphic sections. These choices determine the precise form of our geometric Toda equations.

Definition 1.2
.

Fix a compact Riemann surface
\Sigma
of genus
g
, equipped with a Kähler metric of constant curvature
2-2g
and
let
\omega_{\Sigma}
denote its Kähler form.
Fix an affine Dynkin diagram, with Cartan matrix
\hat{C}
, vertex index set
I=\{0,1,\ldots,r\}
and simple affine roots

\{\bar{\alpha}_{k}:k\in I\}
. Choose a vertex
labelling
\ell:I\to\{-1,1\}
satisfying the conditions of Theorem
1.1
and let

I=I_{+}\cup I_{-}
be the partition it induces. Choose holomorphic
line bundles
Q_{k}(\mathbb{C})
,
k\in I
, over
\Sigma
such that
\otimes_{k\in I}Q_{k}(\mathbb{C})^{m_{k}}\simeq\underline{\mathbb{C}}
and set
d_{k}=\deg(Q_{k})
.
For each
k\in I
take the unique, up to constant scaling, Hermitian metric on
Q_{k}(\mathbb{C})

whose Chern connection
has curvature
-id_{k}\omega_{\Sigma}
. Finally, for each
k\in I
choose a holomorphic section
\varphi_{k}\in H^{0}(Q_{k}(\mathbb{C})\otimes K_{\Sigma})
.
Then the
geometric Toda equations
for this data are the system of elliptic p.d.e.

\Delta_{\Sigma}w_{j}=\sum_{k\in I_{+}}\|\varphi_{k}\|^{2}\tfrac{|\alpha_{k}|^{2}}{2}e^{w_{k}}\hat{C}_{jk}-\sum_{k\in I_{-}}\|\varphi_{k}\|^{2}\tfrac{|\alpha_{k}|^{2}}{2}e^{w_{k}}\hat{C}_{jk}-d_{j},\quad j\in I,

(1.3)

for functions
w_{j}:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
with
\sum_{j=0}^{r}m_{j}w_{j}=0
.

We call the pair
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
a
Toda pair
. We say it is
cyclic
when all
\varphi_{k}
are non-trivial and

non-cyclic
otherwise. A particular case of the latter is the
simple non-affine
case, for which
\varphi_{k}=0

only for
k=0
, and we show that every non-cyclic case is a union of simple non-affine cases.
For the holomorphic section
\varphi_{k}
to be non-trivial the degree
d_{k}
must satisfy
d_{k}\geq 2-2g
.
Hence a necessary condition for the existence of cyclic pairs is that
g\geq 1
and the vector
(d_{0},\ldots,d_{r})
of degrees lies in
a bounded polytope

2-2g\leq d_{j},\ j=0,\ldots,r,\quad\sum_{k=0}^{r}m_{k}d_{k}=0.

(1.4)

Note that for
g=1
this polytope is a single point
(0,\ldots,0)
. Since
K_{\Sigma}\simeq\underline{\mathbb{C}}
this forces
Q_{k}(\mathbb{C})\simeq\underline{\mathbb{C}}
to obtain non-trivial

\varphi_{k}
and leads to the more standard form of the Toda equations where all coefficients are constants.

Regarding existence of solutions, when
G
is noncompact the only general method we are aware of is when we can treat

(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
as a
principal pair
in the language of Bradlow et. al
[
4
]
(cf. Banfield
[
2
]
).
We show that for cyclic Toda pairs this only applies when
\ell(j)=-1
for
j=0,\ldots,r
. We call this case
totally
noncompact
since it is the case where every affine simple root is noncompact (its root space lies in the
complexification of the noncompact summand
\mathfrak{m}
in the Cartan decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
).
There is precisely one such case for every affine Dynkin
diagram except
\mathfrak{a}_{2k}^{(1)}
, since this is the only case for which the relation
\prod_{j\in I}\ell(j)^{nm_{j}}=1
cannot hold.
It is not immediately obvious which symmetric spaces these are in the standard classification of, say,
[
17
, Ch. X, Table V]

so we provide a list in Table
1
.

The geometric Toda equations for this totally noncompact case are

\Delta_{\Sigma}w_{j}+\sum_{k\in I}\|\varphi_{k}\|^{2}\tfrac{|\alpha_{k}|^{2}}{2}e^{w_{k}}\hat{C}_{jk}+d_{j}=0,\quad j\in I.

(1.5)

In this case by checking the stability conditions for the Toda pair we prove the following (Prop.
5.3
and

5.4
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
be a totally noncompact Toda pair. If it is cyclic
then (
1.5
) has a unique solution. If it is simple non-affine then a solution exists (and it is unique) whenever
the vector of degrees
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})
lies in the convex polytope

2-2g\leq d_{j},\quad\sum_{k=1}^{r}R_{jk}d_{k}<0,

(1.6)

where
R_{jk}
are the entries to the inverse of the matrix whose entries are
\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha_{k}\rangle
.
In particular, this requires
g\geq 2
.

For any Toda pair the existence of a solution to
1.3
means there is an equivariant
\tau
-primitive harmonic
map
\psi:\tilde{\Sigma}\to G/T
on the universal cover of
\Sigma
(the equivariance is with respect to the holonomy
representation
\pi_{1}\Sigma\to G
of the flat
G
-connection). It is a property of
\tau
-primitive maps that after homogeneous
projection
G/T\to G/H\simeq N
we obtain an equivariant harmonic map
f:\tilde{\Sigma}\to N
into the symmetric space. When

G
is noncompact this has a corresponding
G
-Higgs bundle. In §
6
we explain how the Toda pair

(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
is related to this Higgs bundle. We show that this Higgs bundle is a Hodge bundle (i.e., fixed by the

\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-action on Higgs bundles) if and only if the Toda pair is non-cyclic.

2.
Primitive harmonic maps.

2.1.
Some reductive homogeneous geometry.

For what follows we will need the following facts about reductive homogeneous spaces of noncompact Lie groups

G
. We will deal exclusively with real noncompact simple Lie groups
G
which are not complex Lie groups.
We will always choose
G
to have trivial centre (i.e., to be the adjoint group).
Given a maximal compact subgroup
H<G
the homogeneous space

G/H
can be given the structure of a noncompact symmetric space (these are the symmetric spaces of Type III in Cartan’s
classification cf.
[
17
, Ch 5,Thm 5.4]
). Let
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}
denote the Lie algebras of
H<G
. The real form

\mathfrak{g}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
is determined by a real involution
\rho
on
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
whose fixed point subalgebra is
\mathfrak{g}
.
Write the corresponding Cartan decomposition as
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{m}
. Let
\mathfrak{u}
be a compatible compact real form, i.e., whose
real involution
\kappa
commutes with
\rho
. Then
\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{g}\cap\mathfrak{u}
and
\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{g}\cap(i\mathfrak{u})
.

Let
T<H
be any closed subgroup. Then
its Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}\subset\mathfrak{g}
is part of a reductive decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{t}+\mathfrak{p}
: since
\mathfrak{h}
the Killing form
\langle\ ,\ \rangle
is non-degenerate on
\mathfrak{g}
we can choose
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{t}^{\perp}
to be the orthogonal complement with respect to the Killing form.
Then
[\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{p}]\subset\mathfrak{p}
and
G/T
is a reductive homogeneous space whose tangent space at any
point is modelled on
\mathfrak{p}
.
We equip
G/T
with the
G
-invariant Riemannian metric given by restricting the
\operatorname{Ad}_{H}
-invariant Hermitian form

\mu:\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\times\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{C},\quad\mu(\xi,\eta)=-\langle\xi,\kappa\eta\rangle,

(2.1)

to
\mathfrak{p}
. When
G/H
is equipped with the symmetric space metric given by the
restriction of
\mu
to
\mathfrak{m}
the homogeneous projection
\pi:G/T\to G/H
is a Riemannian submersion.

Let
\omega_{G}:TG\to\mathfrak{g}
be the left Maurer-Cartan form and use the reductive decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
to write this as

\omega_{\mathfrak{t}}+\omega_{\mathfrak{p}}
. The first factor,
\omega_{\mathfrak{t}}
, provides the
canonical
connection
for the right principal
T
-bundle
G\to G/T
, while the second factor is tensorial and therefore descends
to a
1
-form
\beta:T(G/T)\to[\mathfrak{p}]
, where
[\mathfrak{p}]=G\times_{T}\mathfrak{p}
is the
associated bundle over
G/T
. In fact
\beta
is an isomorphism known as the
Maurer-Cartan
form of
G/T
. Now consider
[\mathfrak{p}]
as a subbundle of
[\mathfrak{g}]
. The latter has canonical trivialisation

G\times_{T}\mathfrak{g}\simeq G/T\times\mathfrak{g};\quad(g,\xi)_{T}\mapsto(gT,\operatorname{Ad}g\cdot\xi).

This carries two
connections: the flat one
d
from this trivialisation and the connection
D
induced from
\omega_{\mathfrak{t}}
. It is shown in

[
9
, Prop 1.1]
that
d=D+\beta
and therefore the curvatures satisfy

0=F^{d}=F^{D}+D\beta+\tfrac{1}{2}[\beta\wedge\beta].

Now
[\mathfrak{g}]=[\mathfrak{t}]+[\mathfrak{p}]
and this splitting is
D
-parallel. Since
[\mathfrak{t},\mathfrak{p}]\subset\mathfrak{p}
when we project this equation onto
the subbundles
[\mathfrak{t}],[\mathfrak{p}]
we obtain two equations

\displaystyle F^{D}+\tfrac{1}{2}[\beta\wedge\beta]_{\mathfrak{t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle D\beta+\tfrac{1}{2}[\beta\wedge\beta]_{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Now let
M
be any smooth manifold and

\psi:M\to G/T
a smooth map. Let
Q=\psi^{-1}G
be the pull-back
T
-bundle over
M
and set
\theta=\psi^{*}\beta
. For any

\operatorname{Ad}_{T}
-invariant subspace
\mathfrak{v}\subset\mathfrak{g}
define
Q(\mathfrak{v})=Q\times_{T}\mathfrak{v}=\psi^{*}[\mathfrak{v}]
. This inherits the
connection
\nabla=\psi^{*}D
. Then we obtain what can be considered the structure equations for maps into
G/T
:

\displaystyle F^{\nabla}+\tfrac{1}{2}[\theta\wedge\theta]_{\mathfrak{t}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\nabla\theta+\tfrac{1}{2}[\theta\wedge\theta]_{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

(2.3)

These are exactly the conditions for the connection
\nabla+\theta
on
Q(\mathfrak{g})
to be flat. By considering the corresponding
flat connection on the principal
G
-bundle
Q\times_{T}G
one obtains the following well-known theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
Q\to M
be a principal
T
-bundle with connection
\nabla
and
\theta\in\mathcal{E}^{1}(Q(\mathfrak{p}))
be such that
(
2.2
) and (
2.3
) both hold. Then there exists a smooth map
\psi:\tilde{M}\to G/T

from the universal cover of
M
, unique up to isometries,
for which
\psi^{*}\beta=\theta
and
\psi
is equivariant with respect to a representation
\chi:\pi_{1}M\to G
.

The representation
\chi
is the holonomy of the flat connection
\nabla+\theta
.

The application which interests us most is when
M
is a Riemann surface
\Sigma

and
\theta
satisfies the conditions

[\theta\wedge\theta]_{\mathfrak{p}}=0\ \text{and}\ \nabla^{\prime\prime}\theta^{\prime}=0,

(2.4)

where
T^{\mathbb{C}}\Sigma=T^{\prime}\Sigma\oplus T^{\prime\prime}\Sigma
is the type decomposition determined by the complex structure on
\Sigma
.
Both of these conditions hold when
\psi
is a
\tau
-primitive harmonic map, the definition of which
we will now recall.

2.2.
\tau
-primitive harmonic maps.

Suppose we have an automorphism
\tau
of

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
of finite order
m\geq 3
which commutes with

\rho
and whose fixed points all lie in
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
. It induces a
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
-grading on the complexified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}

which we will write as

\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}=\bigoplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}}\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}_{k},\quad[\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}_{j},\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}_{k}]\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}_{j+k}.

(2.5)

Here
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}_{k}
is the eigenspace of

3.1.

compact
when
\ell(\bar{\alpha})=1
, since this is equivalent to

\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\alpha}}
lying in the complexification
\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}
of the compact summand in the Cartan decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
.
When
\ell(\bar{\alpha})=-1
we will say it is
noncompact
.

Given two such maps
\ell,\ell^{\prime}
we obtain real forms
G,G^{\prime}
of
G^{\mathbb{C}}
each containing
T
in their maximal compact
subgroup.
\tau
-primitive maps into
G/T
can be identified with those into
G^{\prime}/T
when there is an isomorphism
G/T\to G^{\prime}/T

identifying primitive distributions. The existence of a real group isomorphism
\phi:G\to G^{\prime}
is equivalent to the existence
of a complex linear automorphism
\chi
of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
which maps
\mathfrak{g}
to
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
:
\chi
is just the complex linear
extension of the tangent map to
\phi
at the identity. The additional conditions require that
\chi
preserves both

\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, and since
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
generates
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
this is equivalent to saying
\chi

commutes with
\tau
.
Now since
\chi
maps the Cartan decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}+\mathfrak{m}
to a Cartan decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
we
may, by altering
\chi
by an inner automorphism if necessary, choose
\chi
so that

\chi(\mathfrak{h})=\mathfrak{h}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{u},\quad\chi(\mathfrak{m})=\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\cap\mathfrak{u}.

It follows that
\chi
commutes with
\kappa
.
Thus the natural equivalence for
\rho,\rho^{\prime}
for our situation can be written in terms of
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
, namely, that
there exists
\chi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}})
such that
\sigma^{\prime}=\chi\sigma\chi^{-1}
and
\chi
commutes with
\tau
.
When these conditions holds we will say
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
are
\tau
-equivalent
.
This leads us to the following characterisation.

Proposition 3.9
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between complex involutions
\sigma
which are compatible with the Coxeter
automorphism
\tau
for
(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}},\nu,\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}},\bar{B})
and additive maps
\ell
of the type (
3.5
). Two
such involutions
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}
are
\tau
-equivalent if and only if
\ell,\ell^{\prime}
satisfy
\ell=\ell^{\prime}\circ w

where
w:\bar{R}\to\bar{R}
is a symmetry of the affine root system which preserves
\bar{B}
. All such symmetries come
from symmetries of the affine Dynkin diagram.

Proof.

The first statement is clear from the discussion above. Now suppose
\sigma^{\prime}=\chi\sigma\chi^{-1}
for some

\chi\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}})
which preserves
\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
. Clearly
\chi:\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\sigma}\to\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\sigma^{\prime}}
and therefore
whenever
h\in\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}
and
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\alpha}}
we have

[h,\xi]=\alpha(h)\xi\ \Rightarrow[\chi(h),\chi(\xi)]=(\alpha\circ\chi^{-1})(\chi(h))\chi(\xi),

so that
(\alpha,j)\in\bar{R}
implies
(\alpha\circ\chi^{-1},j)\in\bar{R}
.
Since every automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
leaves the Killing form invariant
\chi
induces a symmetry of
\bar{R}
:

w:\bar{R}\to\bar{R};\quad(\alpha,j)\mapsto(\alpha\circ\chi^{-1},j),

for which

\chi:\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\alpha}}\to\mathfrak{g}^{w(\bar{\alpha})}.

(3.7)

Now let
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\alpha}}
be non-zero, then

\ell(\bar{\alpha})\xi=\sigma(\xi)=\chi^{-1}\sigma^{\prime}(\chi\xi)=\ell^{\prime}(w(\bar{\alpha}))\xi.

(3.8)

Thus
\ell=\ell^{\prime}\circ w
. Clearly
w
must preserve
\bar{B}
since
\chi
preserves
\mathfrak{g}_{1}
.

Conversely, suppose
\ell=\ell^{\prime}\circ w
. A standard argument (cf.
[
17
, Ch. IX, Thm 5.1]
) shows that
w
extends to an
automorphism
\chi
of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
satisfying (
3.7
). Now rearranging (
3.8
) shows that on every root space

\sigma^{\prime}=\chi\sigma\chi^{-1}
. Since the root spaces generate
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
as a Lie algebra, this equation holds on all of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
.
∎

When we combine this construction of compatible involutions with the classification of noncompact symmetric spaces in

[
17
]
we deduce that every noncompact symmetric space has at least one compatible Coxeter automorphism.

Corollary 3.10
.

For every noncompact symmetric space
G/H
of Type III there is at least one compatible Coxeter automorphism.

Proof.

First we recall from
[
17
, Ch. X, §5]
that each symmetric space is determined by a involution of type
(s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{r};n)
,
obtained by labelling the
j
-th vertex of the affine Dynkin diagram
\Gamma^{(n)}
with
s_{j}\in\{0,1\}
such that

\sum_{j=0}^{r}nm_{j}s_{j}=2
. The involution fixes
\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}
and acts by
(-1)^{s_{j}}
on
\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\alpha}_{j}}
. Thus we choose

\ell
to have
\ell(\bar{\alpha}_{j})=(-1)^{s_{j}}
.
∎

Finally, we can be quite explicit about what the involution is for a given map
\ell
. First, by (
3.6
)

\prod_{j=0}^{r}\ell(\bar{\alpha}_{j})^{m_{j}}=\pm 1

and can only equal
-1
when
n=2
.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
\ell:\bar{R}\to\{-1,1\}
satisfy (
3.6
) and
\sigma
be the involution determined by it.
Let
h\in\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}
be the unique element which satisfies, for
j=1,\ldots,r
,

\alpha_{j}(h)=\begin{cases}0\ \text{when}\ \ell(\bar{\alpha}_{j})=1,\\
1\ \text{when}\ \ell(\bar{\alpha}_{j})=-1.\end{cases}.

If
\prod_{j=0}^{r}\ell(\bar{\alpha}_{j})^{m_{j}}=1
then
\sigma=\exp(i\pi\operatorname{ad}h)
and is hence an inner automorphism.
If
\prod_{j=0}^{r}\ell(\bar{\alpha}_{j})^{m_{j}}=-1
then
\sigma=\nu\exp(i\pi\operatorname{ad}h)
and is therefore an outer automorphism.

Proof.

For simplicity set
\ell_{j}=\ell(\bar{\alpha}_{j})
. For
j=1,\ldots,r

\exp(i\pi\operatorname{ad}h)\cdot e_{j}=e^{i\pi\alpha_{j}(h)}e_{j}=\ell_{j}e_{j}.

Since
\alpha_{0}=-\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}\alpha_{j}
, when
\prod_{j=0}^{r}\ell_{j}^{m_{j}}=1
we have

\exp(i\pi\operatorname{ad}h)\cdot e_{0}=e^{-i\pi\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}\alpha_{j}(h)}e_{j}=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\ell_{j}^{-m_{j}}e_{j}.

Hence when
\prod_{j=0}^{r}\ell_{j}^{m_{j}}=1
we have
\exp(i\pi\operatorname{ad}h)\cdot e_{0}=\ell_{0}e_{0}
and therefore

\sigma=\exp(i\pi\operatorname{ad}h)
.

When
\prod_{j=0}^{r}\ell_{j}^{m_{j}}=-1
then
\exp(i\pi\operatorname{ad}h)\cdot e_{0}=-\ell_{0}e_{0}
. But in this case
\nu
is an
involution with
\nu(e_{j})=e_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,r
and
\nu(e_{0})=-e_{0}
, therefore
\sigma=\nu\exp(i\pi\operatorname{ad}h)
.
∎

An important consequence of this Lemma is that the symmetric space
G/H
need not be an outer symmetric space when
the Coxeter automorphism is outer.

Example 3.12
.

As an illustration, consider the diagram
\mathfrak{a}_{2}^{(2)}
(see Appendix
B
).
The root system is

\bar{R}=\{(0,0),(0,1),\pm\bar{\alpha}_{0},\pm\bar{\alpha}_{1},\pm(\bar{\alpha}_{0}+\bar{\alpha}_{1})\},

where
\bar{\alpha}_{1}=(\alpha_{1},1)
and
\bar{\alpha}_{0}=(-2\alpha_{1},1)
. This diagram has three distinct labellings:

(1)

(\ell_{0},\ell_{1})=(-1,1)
with
\ell_{0}\ell_{1}^{2}=-1
. Hence
\sigma
is an outer automorphism with fixed-point subalgebra

\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{g}^{(0,0)}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\alpha}_{1}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{-\bar{\alpha}_{1}}\simeq\mathfrak{a}_{1}.

(2)

(\ell_{0},\ell_{1})=(1,-1)
with
\ell_{0}\ell_{1}^{2}=1

is an inner automorphism with fixed-point subalgebra

(3)

(\ell_{0},\ell_{1})=(-1,-1)

\ell_{0}\ell_{1}^{2}=-1
. Hence
\sigma
is an outer automorphism with fixed-point subalgebra

\mathfrak{h}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{g}^{(0,0)}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\alpha}_{0}+\bar{\alpha}_{1}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{-\bar{\alpha}_{0}-\bar{\alpha}_{1}}\simeq\mathfrak{a}_{1}.

In the second case the real form

\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
minimal Lagrangian surfaces in
\mathbb{CH}^{2}

\mathbb{R}^{3}
(equally, convex

[
24
]
.

4.
Geometric Toda equations.

In this section we will derive the geometric Toda equations as given in Definition
1.2
.
From now on we assume that
\Sigma
is a compact Riemann
surface of genus
g
and equip it with a metric of constant curvature

2-2g
, so that the Kähler form satisfies
\int_{\Sigma}\omega_{\Sigma}=2\pi
.
Motivated by Higgs bundles and more generally principal pairs
[
2
,
4
,
13
]
, we want to view equations (
2.7
) and
(
2.8
) as equations on the gauge orbit of an
T
-connection
\nabla
in a holomorphic
T^{\mathbb{C}}
-bundle
Q^{\mathbb{C}}
, given

\varphi\in H^{0}(Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})\otimes K_{\Sigma})
. The gauge group is
\mathcal{G}=C^{\infty}(\Sigma,T^{\mathbb{C}})
and these equations will be our geometric Toda
equations: they are the equations which
ensure that the reduction of structure group
Q\subset Q^{\mathbb{C}}
corresponds to a
\tau
-primitive harmonic map using
Corollary
2.6
. Since
T
acts unitarily on
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}_{1}

when it is equipped with the metric
\mu
from (
2.1
), a reduction of structure group
Q\subset Q^{\mathbb{C}}

equips the holomorphic bundle
Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}_{1})
with a Chern connection.

Remark 4.1
.

Note that any complex automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
which fixes
\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}
pointwise, such as
\tau
or
\sigma
, induces a
holomorphic automorphism on
Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}})
which we will denote by the same name. For every reduction of structure group

Q\subset Q^{\mathbb{C}}
the bundle
Q(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}})
also inherits the real involutions
\rho,\kappa
.
What follows also applies when
Q^{\mathbb{C}}
is a holomorphic
(T^{\prime})^{\mathbb{C}}
-bundle where
T^{\prime}<T
. Such a reduction can apply when
considering Toda solutions with additional symmetry. Henceforth we will assume that this is understood.

Given
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
, choose an initial
T
-bundle
Q_{0}\subset Q^{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
\|\cdot\|
denote the norm of this initial
metric and
\nabla
denote the corresponding Chern connection.
The action of the gauge group
\mathcal{G}
on connections is such that, for
s\in\mathcal{G}
,

s\cdot\nabla
is the Chern connection compatible with
s\nabla^{\prime\prime}s^{-1}
. Specifically this means

s\cdot\nabla=\nabla-(s^{-1}\bar{\partial}s+\rho(s^{-1}\partial s)).

Here
d=\partial+\bar{\partial}
is the type decomposition of the exterior derivative on
\Sigma
. To simplify notation
define
\bar{s}=\rho(s)^{-1}=\kappa(s)^{-1}
, and then we have

s\cdot\nabla=\nabla-(\bar{\partial}\log s-\partial\log\bar{s}).

Note that
\operatorname{Ad}s\cdot\varphi
is holomorphic with respect to
(s\cdot\nabla)^{\prime\prime}
. Hence

F^{s\cdot\nabla}=F^{\nabla}-2\partial\bar{\partial}\log(s\bar{s}),

and note that
2\partial\bar{\partial}=i*\Delta_{\Sigma}
. Now observe that

[\operatorname{Ad}s\cdot\varphi\wedge\rho(\operatorname{Ad}s\cdot\varphi)]=[\operatorname{Ad}s\bar{s}\cdot\varphi\wedge\rho\varphi],

since
T^{\mathbb{C}}
is abelian. From now on set
u=s\bar{s}
. Since
\rho=\kappa
on
T^{\mathbb{C}}
it follows that
u:\Sigma\to\exp(i\mathfrak{t})

and
\log(u)
is well-defined.
Using the root spaces we may decompose
Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}_{1})=\oplus_{j=0}^{r}Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\alpha}_{j}})
and
hence write
\varphi=\sum_{j=0}^{r}\varphi_{j}
for
\varphi_{j}\in H^{0}(Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\alpha}_{j}})\otimes K_{\Sigma})
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
0\leq j\leq r
,

[\operatorname{Ad}u\cdot\varphi_{j}\wedge\rho\varphi_{j}]=ie^{\alpha_{j}(\log u)}\|\varphi_{j}\|^{2}\ell_{j}\frac{|\alpha_{j}|^{2}}{2}h_{j}\omega_{\Sigma},

(4.1)

where
\ell_{j}=\ell(\bar{\alpha}_{j})
determine the real involution
\rho
.

Proof.

First we note that

\operatorname{Ad}u\cdot\varphi_{j}=e^{\alpha_{j}(\log u)}\varphi_{j}.

It is also clear that
[\varphi_{j}\wedge\rho\varphi_{k}]=0
for
k\neq j
. Now let
\tau_{j}
be a
\mu
-unitary local section
of
Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\alpha}_{j}})
and write
\varphi_{j}=A_{j}\tau_{j}dz
locally, where
A_{j}
is a locally smooth function.
Then

[\varphi_{j}\wedge\rho\varphi_{j}]=[\tau_{j},\rho\tau_{j}]|A_{j}|^{2}dz\wedge d\bar{z}.

Now
\|\varphi_{j}\|^{2}=|A_{j}|^{2}\|dz\|^{2}
and
dz\wedge d\bar{z}=-i\|dz\|^{2}\omega_{\Sigma}
so that

|A_{j}|^{2}dz\wedge d\bar{z}=-i\|\varphi_{j}\|^{2}\omega_{\Sigma}.

Finally,
[\tau_{j},\rho\tau_{j}]
is a local section of the adjoint bundle
Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}})
which is trivial since
\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}
is abelian.
To calculate this we need a unit length vector in each
\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\alpha}_{j}}
. We note that

\mu(e_{j},e_{j})=-\langle e_{j},f_{j}\rangle=-\tfrac{1}{2}\langle[h_{j},e_{j}],f_{j}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{2}\langle h_{j},h_{j}\rangle=\frac{2}{|\alpha_{j}|^{2}},

by (
3.1
).
Therefore relative to the
\mu
-induced metric on
Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}_{1})\simeq Q\times_{T}\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}_{1}
we can take
\tau_{j}
to be the
equivalence class of

(q,\frac{|\alpha_{j}|}{\sqrt{2}}e_{j})\in Q^{\mu}\times\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\alpha}_{j}}.

Hence

[\tau_{j},\rho\tau_{j}]=\frac{|\alpha_{j}|^{2}}{2}[e_{j},\rho e_{j}]=-\ell(\bar{\alpha}_{j})\frac{|\alpha_{j}|^{2}}{2}h_{j}.

∎

Hence (
2.7
) holds if and only if

\Delta_{\Sigma}\log(u)-\sum_{j=0}^{r}\ell_{j}e^{\alpha_{j}(\log u)}\|\varphi_{j}\|^{2}\frac{|\alpha_{j}|^{2}}{2}h_{j}+i*F^{\nabla}=0.

(4.2)

Since
u:\Sigma\to\exp(i\mathfrak{t})
it has a well-defined logarithm
w=\log(u)
. To make this look a little more familiar we define
functions

w_{j}=\alpha_{j}(\log(u)),

(4.3)

and partition the index set
I=\{0,1,\ldots,r\}
into the union of

I_{+}=\{j\in I:\ell_{j}=1\},\quad I_{-}=\{j\in I:\ell_{j}=-1\}.

Then equation (
4.2
) is equivalent to the system of equations

\Delta_{\Sigma}w_{j}=\sum_{k\in I_{+}}\|\varphi_{k}\|^{2}\tfrac{|\alpha_{k}|^{2}}{2}e^{w_{k}}\hat{C}_{jk}-\sum_{k\in I_{-}}\|\varphi_{k}\|^{2}\tfrac{|\alpha_{k}|^{2}}{2}e^{w_{k}}\hat{C}_{jk}-\alpha_{j}(i*F^{\nabla}),\quad j=0,\ldots,r.

(4.4)

These variables satisfy the relation
\sum_{j=0}^{r}m_{j}w_{j}=0
.

Remark 4.3
.

Note that after the gauge transformation
s
the new metric
\|\cdot\|_{s}^{2}
on
Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{g}^{\bar{\alpha}_{j}})
is given by

\|\cdot\|_{s}^{2}=e^{w_{j}}\|\cdot\|^{2}
.

We can simplify the term
\alpha_{j}(*F^{\nabla})
by choosing the initial connection as
follows. First we observe that, since we have chosen
G
to have trivial centre, the basis
\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{r}\in i\mathfrak{t}
dual to the root basis
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
for
\operatorname{Hom}(i\mathfrak{t},\mathbb{R})
generates the kernel
\Gamma
of the exponential map

\mathbf{e}=\exp(2\pi i\cdot):\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}\to T^{\mathbb{C}},

(see, e.g.,
[
30
, Ch VIII]
). Therefore the root basis freely generates the weight lattice

\hat{\Gamma}=\{\lambda\in(\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}})^{*}:\lambda(\Gamma)\subset\mathbb{Z}\},

and this is isomorphic to the character group of
T^{\mathbb{C}}
by

\hat{\Gamma}\to\operatorname{Hom}(T^{\mathbb{C}},\mathbb{C}^{\times});\quad\lambda\mapsto\hat{\lambda}=\mathbf{e}\circ\lambda\circ\mathbf{e}^{-1}.

Therefore we use the root basis to identify
T^{\mathbb{C}}
with
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r}
. Thus our holomorphic
T^{\mathbb{C}}
-bundle
Q^{\mathbb{C}}
can be canonically
identified with a product of holomorphic
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-bundles

Q^{\mathbb{C}}\simeq Q_{1}\times_{\Sigma}\ldots\times_{\Sigma}Q_{r};\quad Q_{j}=Q^{\mathbb{C}}\times_{\hat{\alpha}_{j}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}.

Now let
Q_{j}(\mathbb{C})
denote the line bundle associated to the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
-bundle
Q_{j}
. Then

Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{g}_{1})=\oplus_{j=0}^{r}Q_{j}(\mathbb{C}).

It follows that

Q_{0}(\mathbb{C})\simeq\bigotimes_{j=1}^{r}Q_{j}(\mathbb{C})^{-m_{j}}.

Set
d_{j}=\deg(Q_{j})
, so
d_{0}=-\sum_{j=1}^{r}d_{j}m_{j}
. Then in
H^{2}(\Sigma,\mathbb{Z})

[\frac{i}{2\pi}\alpha_{j}(F^{\nabla})]=[\frac{d_{j}}{2\pi}\omega_{\Sigma}],\quad j=0,\ldots,r.

Since we are working with line bundles we may choose the initial metric so that
i\alpha_{j}(F^{\nabla})=d_{j}\omega_{\Sigma}
,
and therefore

\alpha_{j}(i*F^{\nabla})=d_{j},\ 0\leq j\leq r.

With this choice of initial metric we finally arrive at the system of equations

\Delta_{\Sigma}w_{j}=\sum_{k\in I_{+}}\|\varphi_{k}\|^{2}\tfrac{|\alpha_{k}|^{2}}{2}e^{w_{k}}\hat{C}_{jk}-\sum_{k\in I_{-}}\|\varphi_{k}\|^{2}\tfrac{|\alpha_{k}|^{2}}{2}e^{w_{k}}\hat{C}_{jk}-d_{j},\quad j\in I,

(4.5)

for functions
w_{j}:\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
with
\sum_{j=0}^{r}m_{j}w_{j}=0
. These are the
geometric Toda equations

of Definition
1.2
.

Remark 4.4
.

We choose not to absorb the multipliers
|\alpha_{k}|^{2}/2
into either
e^{w_{k}}
, by a
change of variable, or
\|\varphi_{k}\|^{2}
, so as to retain the clear link between the initial data and the equations.
For simply-laced diagrams (those with no multiple edges)
|\alpha_{k}|^{2}/2=1
for all
k
.
Note also that the curvature only specifies the initial metric
Q\subset Q^{\mathbb{C}}
up to constant scaling on each
Q_{j}(\mathbb{C})
,

j=1,\ldots,r
, but any two such
metrics are equivalent under the action of the constant gauge transformations
g\in T^{\mathbb{C}}
,
\varphi\mapsto\operatorname{Ad}g\cdot\varphi
.
With the initial metric fixed, the constant gauge transformations
act on solutions by constant translation,
w\mapsto w+\log(g\kappa(g)^{-1})
.

As one knows from the earlier studies of Toda systems there is a significant qualitative change in solutions when
one or more
\varphi_{k}
is identically zero.

Definition 4.5
.

We will call the system (
1.3
)
cyclic
whenever
\varphi_{k}
is not identically zero for all

k=0,\ldots,r
, and refer to
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
as a cyclic Toda pair. Otherwise we will say it is
non-cyclic
.
We will say it is
simple non-affine
when
\varphi_{k}=0
only for
k=0
.

An obvious necessary condition for the existence of a cyclic pair is that for
k=0,\ldots,r
each line bundle
Q_{k}(\mathbb{C})\otimes K_{\Sigma}
must
admit a non-trivial globally holomorphic section
\varphi_{k}
and must therefore have non-negative degree. This immediately leads to
restrictions on the degrees
d_{k}
.

Lemma 4.6
.

A necessary condition for a
T^{\mathbb{C}}
-bundle
Q^{\mathbb{C}}
to admit a cyclic Toda pair is that the vector of degrees
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}

lies in the closed convex polytope given by

2-2g\leq d_{k},\quad\sum_{k=1}^{r}m_{k}d_{k}\leq 2g-2,\quad k=1,\ldots,r.

(4.6)

Corollary 4.7
.

For
g=0
there are no cyclic pairs. For
g=1
there is, up to gauge equivalence, a real one-parameter family of cyclic pairs.

Proof.

The first statement is immediate from (
4.6
). For
g=1
we are forced to have
d_{k}=0
and
Q_{k}(\mathbb{C})\simeq\underline{\mathbb{C}}
for all
k
,
and thus each
\varphi_{k}
is constant, so that we may consider
\varphi\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{\tau}
. Now it is easy to see that each
\operatorname{Ad}_{T^{\mathbb{C}}}
-orbit
of a cyclic element of
\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{\tau}
contains exactly one element of the form
c\sum_{k=0}^{r}e_{k}
for some

c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.
∎

Now let us consider the non-cyclic cases.

Proposition 4.8
.

Suppose
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
is a non-cyclic Toda pair with
\varphi_{k}=0
for
k\not\in J
where
J\subset I
is proper and non-empty.
Then (
1.3
) reduces to a union of simple non-affine Toda systems for
\{w_{j}:j\in J\}
while for
l\not\in J
,
w_{l}
and

d_{l}
are determined by a linear combination of
\{w_{j}:j\in J\}
and
\{d_{j}:j\in J\}
respectively.

Proof.

Set
f_{k}=\ell(\bar{\alpha}_{k})\|\varphi_{k}\|^{2}\tfrac{|\alpha_{k}|^{2}}{2}e^{w_{k}}
. Since
f_{k}=0
for
k\not\in J
we can write (
1.3
) simply as

\Delta_{\Sigma}w_{l}+d_{l}=\sum_{k\in J}\hat{C}_{lk}f_{k},\quad l\in I.

(4.7)

Let
B
be the submatrix of
\hat{C}
obtained by removing the rows and columns not indexed by
J
. This is the (possibly
decomposable) Cartan matrix for the subdiagram of the affine Dynkin diagram for
\hat{C}
obtained by removing the vertices
labelled by
I\setminus J
. Therefore

\Delta_{\Sigma}w_{j}+d_{j}=\sum_{k\in J}B_{jk}f_{k},\quad j\in J.

This is a union of simple non-affine Toda systems. Since every Cartan matrix is invertible, the matrix
B
has inverse
A

so that

\sum_{j\in J}A_{kj}(\Delta_{\Sigma}w_{j}+d_{j})=f_{k},\quad k\in J.

Substituting this into (
4.7
) for
l\not\in J
leads to

\Delta_{\Sigma}(w_{l}-\sum_{j,k\in J}\hat{C}_{lk}A_{kj}w_{j})=\sum_{j,k\in J}\hat{C}_{lk}A_{kj}d_{j}-d_{l}.

Since
\Sigma
is compact the integral of the left hand side is zero and therefore the right hand side is zero, which
determines each
d_{l}
. Then, after possibly a constant gauge transformation which leaves
\varphi
invariant,

w_{l}=\sum_{j,k\in J}\hat{C}_{lk}A_{kj}w_{j}.

∎

4.1.
Non-cyclic Toda pairs which are not simple non-affine.

Here is the geometric interpretation of non-cyclic Toda pairs which are not simple non-affine. On the Dynkin diagram

\Gamma^{(n)}
label the vertices by
s_{j}=1
if
j\in I\setminus J
(i.e.,
\varphi_{j}=0
) and
s_{j}=0
otherwise.
Using the method of Kac
[
19
]

this provides an automorphism
\gamma\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}})
of type
(s_{0},\ldots,s_{r};n)

which commutes with
\sigma
,
\kappa
(and hence
\rho
) and
\tau
.
It has order
o(\gamma)=\sum_{j=0}^{r}nm_{j}s_{j}
. Inspection shows that

o(\gamma)=1
if and only if
n=1
and the Toda pair is equivalent to a simple non-affine Toda pair by a symmetry of the extended
Dynkin diagram
\Gamma^{(1)}
. Otherwise the fixed point subalgebra
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{\gamma}
is
a reductive proper subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
which we can write as a Lie algebra direct sum

\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{k}\oplus\mathfrak{z}.

Here each
\mathfrak{a}_{j}
is simple and is determined by a connected subdiagram of the nodes labelled by
J
. The ideal

\mathfrak{z}
is the centre and it is contained in
\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
A=G^{\gamma}<G
have Lie algebra
\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{g}
, and
similarly
A_{l},Z<G
have Lie algebras
\mathfrak{a}_{l}\cap\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{z}\cap\mathfrak{g}
respectively. Then

A\simeq A_{1}\times\ldots\times A_{k}\times Z.

Moreover,
T<A
and we can write
T\simeq T_{1}\times\ldots\times T_{k}\times Z
where
T_{l}<A_{l}
is the
\tau
-fixed torus in
A_{l}
.
The factor
A_{l}
will be noncompact provided
\ell(\bar{\alpha}_{j})=-1
for some node on the connected subdiagram
corresponding to
\mathfrak{a}_{l}
.
Clearly, for each
A_{l}
we have a simple non-affine Toda system and each determines a primitive harmonic map into

A_{j}/T_{j}
(using the primitive distribution for non-affine roots). Now

A/T\simeq(A_{1}/T_{1})\times\ldots\times(A_{k}/T_{k}),

since
T/Z\simeq T_{1}\times\ldots\times T_{k}
. Therefore the product of maps into
A_{l}/T_{l}
corresponds to the map obtained
from the original non-cyclic Toda pair and factors through the (totally geodesic) embedding
A/T\subset G/T
.

4.2.
Inner versus outer Coxeter automorphisms.

Most of the literature considers only the Toda equations for an extended Dynkin diagram (i.e., the inner Coxeter
automorphism). It was observed by Olive &
Turok
[
28
]
that when the Dynkin diagram admits a non-trivial symmetry this can
be imposed upon those Toda equations to obtain a reduced system.
The aim here is to explain how to interpret the geometry of this when
\nu
is an involution, which is the
only case relevant to a discussion of real forms.

Suppose the Dynkin diagram of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
admits a non-trivial involution.
As before let
\nu
denote both the symmetry on the nodes
of the diagram and the corresponding outer automorphism of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
\tau^{\prime}=\nu\tau_{\mathit{inn}}
and recall from Remark

3.4
that this is not a Coxeter automorphism unless
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\simeq\mathfrak{a}_{2k}
. When
\tau_{\mathit{inn}}
has order

m
the order of
\tau^{\prime}
is
m
if
m
is even and
2m
otherwise. If
\tau_{\mathit{inn}}(\xi)=\lambda\xi
and
\nu(\xi)=\xi
then

\tau^{\prime}(\xi)=\lambda\xi
and therefore

\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{\tau_{\mathit{inn}}}\cap\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{\nu}\supset\mathfrak{g}^{\tau^{\prime}}_{j},\quad j=\begin{cases}1,\ o(\tau^{\prime})=m,\\
2,\ o(\tau^{\prime})=2m.\end{cases}

For the Toda equations (
1.3
) to admit the symmetry of
\nu
we require
\ell(\alpha_{j})=\ell(\alpha_{\nu(j)})
and the Toda pair
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
to admit this symmetry, i.e., there is an isomorphism
from
Q_{j}(\mathbb{C})
to
Q_{\nu(j)}
which identifies
\varphi_{j}
with
\varphi_{\nu(j)}
.
In particular the involution
\sigma

corresponding to
\ell
commutes with
\nu
and
\sigma^{\prime}=\nu\sigma
is an outer involution. Let
G<G^{\mathbb{C}}
be the
real form determined by
\rho=\sigma\kappa
and
G^{\prime}
the real form determined by
\rho^{\prime}=\sigma^{\prime}\kappa
,
with maximal compact subgroups
H,H^{\prime}
and
in each of these maximal toral subgroups
T,T^{\prime}
, where
T^{\prime}<T
is the
\nu
-fixed subgroup. Using
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau^{\prime}}_{j}
above we
can equip
G^{\prime}/T^{\prime}
with a horizontal
F
-structure.

Now set
A=G\cap G^{\prime}
with Lie
algebra
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{g}\cap\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\subset\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{\nu}
and notice
T^{\prime}<A
. Choose a
(T^{\prime})^{\mathbb{C}}
-bundle
Q^{\mathbb{C}}

and
\varphi\in H^{0}(Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{g}_{j}^{\tau^{\prime}})\otimes K)
, then
\nu\varphi=\varphi
.
The Toda equations for this
Toda pair
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
are the equations which govern the existence of a
T^{\prime}
-subbundle
Q\subset Q^{\mathbb{C}}
so that the connection

\nabla+\varphi+\rho\varphi=\nabla+\varphi+\rho^{\prime}\varphi,

is flat. Because of the symmetry this is an
A
-connection and provides an equivariant map into
A/T^{\prime}
with holonomy
representation
\chi:\pi_{1}\Sigma\to A
. When we post-compose with
A/T^{\prime}\to G/T
we obtain a primitive harmonic map which
projects onto a harmonic map
f_{1}:\tilde{\Sigma}\to G/H
into the inner symmetric space. On the other hand, post-composition with
A/T^{\prime}\to G^{\prime}/T^{\prime}
gives an
F
-holomorphic map which projects down to a harmonic map
f_{2}:\tilde{\Sigma}\to G^{\prime}/H^{\prime}
into
the outer symmetric space. Note that these maps
f_{1},f_{2}
are equivariant with respect to
the same
holonomy
representation
\chi
. When
\varphi
is cyclic the map
f_{2}
does not agree with any map obtained using
a cyclic Toda pair for an outer Coxeter automorphism except when
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}\simeq\mathfrak{a}_{2k}
since the two primitive distributions are inequivalent.

5.
Existence of solutions via principal pairs.

To apply the theory of principal pairs
[
4
,
13
]
(cf.
[
2
]
)
it is necessary to have
\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}_{1}\subset\mathfrak{m}^{\mathbb{C}}
. This is because the principal pair equations for
this situation are

\displaystyle F^{\nabla}-[\varphi\wedge\kappa\varphi]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle\nabla^{\prime\prime}\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

and therefore we require
\rho\varphi=-\kappa\varphi
for the first equation to agree with (
2.7
).
Thus we need
\sigma=-1
on
\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{\tau}
and therefore the pair
(\tau,\sigma)
is totally noncompact.
Given this, a pair
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)

consisting of a holomorphic
T^{\mathbb{C}}
-bundle
Q^{\mathbb{C}}
and a holomorphic section
\varphi
of
Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}_{1})\otimes K_{\Sigma}
, is
an example of a
K_{\Sigma}
-twisted principal pair
[
13
]
. We will refer to
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)

as a
totally noncompact Toda pair
.

Because the Toda equations (
1.3
) are equivalent to the system (
2.7
) and (
2.8
) they possess a solution
when
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
is a
0
-polystable principal pair. Since
T^{\mathbb{C}}
is abelian the conditions for
0
-stability are relatively easy
to formulate, following
[
13
]
. For each
\chi\in\operatorname{Hom}(i\mathfrak{t},\mathbb{R})
let
h_{\chi}\in i\mathfrak{t}
be given by
\chi=\langle h_{\chi},\cdot\rangle
. Define

B_{\chi}^{-}=\{\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}_{1}:\operatorname{Ad}\exp(th_{\chi})\cdot\xi\text{ is bounded as }t\to\infty,\ t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\}

and its subset

B_{\chi}^{0}=\{\xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{\tau}_{1}:\operatorname{Ad}\exp(th_{\chi})\cdot\xi=\xi\ \text{ for all }t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Note that these are both
T^{\mathbb{C}}
-invariant and therefore the associated bundles

Q^{\mathbb{C}}(B_{\chi}^{0})\subset Q^{\mathbb{C}}(B_{\chi}^{-})\subset Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{g}_{1}^{\tau})

are well-defined. We also write
\chi=\sum_{j=1}^{r}x_{j}\alpha_{j}
, where
x_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
, and define

\deg(Q^{\mathbb{C}})_{\chi}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}x_{j}d_{j}.

Definition 5.1

(cf.
[
13
]
)
.

The pair
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
is
0
-polystable if for every non-zero
\chi\in\operatorname{Hom}(i\mathfrak{t},\mathbb{R})
for which
\varphi\in H^{0}(Q^{\mathbb{C}}(B_{\chi}^{-})\otimes K_{\Sigma})
we have
\deg(Q^{\mathbb{C}})_{\chi}\geq 0
, with equality only if
\varphi\in H^{0}(Q^{\mathbb{C}}(B_{\chi}^{0})\otimes K_{\Sigma})
.
When
\deg(Q^{\mathbb{C}})_{\chi}>0
we say the pair is
0
-stable.

Note that in the language of
[
13
]

\chi
is an antidominant character. The main theorem we wish to apply can be
stated as follows, based on the exposition in
[
13
]
.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
be a totally noncompact Toda pair which is
0
-polystable. Then the Toda equations (
1.3
)
possess a solution, and there
is a corresponding equivariant harmonic map
f:\mathcal{D}\to G/T
. This solution is uniquely determined when
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
is

0
-stable.

It is easy to check that cyclic Toda pairs are always
0
-stable.

Proposition 5.3
.

Every totally noncompact cyclic Toda pair
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
is
0
-stable.

Proof.

We will show that
0
-stability is vacuously satisfied by a cyclic pair. Suppose
\chi\in\operatorname{Hom}(i\mathfrak{t},\mathbb{R})
, then

\operatorname{Ad}\exp(th_{\chi})\cdot\varphi=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\operatorname{Ad}\exp(th_{\chi})\cdot\varphi_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}\exp(t\langle\alpha_{k},\chi\rangle)\varphi_{k}.

(5.1)

This is bounded as
t\to\infty
if and only if

\langle\alpha_{k},\chi\rangle\leq 0\ \text{for all}\ k=0,\ldots,r.

Since
\alpha_{0}=-\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}\alpha_{j}
and
m_{j}>0
this cannot hold when
\chi\neq 0
.
∎

By Prop.
4.8
the non-cyclic case is entirely determined by simple non-affine cases. To understand the
stability conditions for the non-affine case we need the following ideas.
Recall that
\epsilon_{1},\ldots,\epsilon_{r}\in i\mathfrak{t}
is the dual basis,
\alpha_{j}(\epsilon_{k})=\delta_{jk}
. The roots
determine the closure
W
of the positive Weyl chamber and the interior
\operatorname{int}(W^{o})
of its polar cone:

W=\{h\in i\mathfrak{t}:\alpha_{j}(h)\geq 0\},\quad\operatorname{int}(W^{o})=\{t\in i\mathfrak{t}:\langle t,h\rangle<0\ \forall\ h\in W\}.

Proposition 5.4
.

A totally noncompact simple non-affine Toda pair
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
is
0
-polystable if and only if it is
0
-stable, which
occurs if and only if the degrees
d_{1},\ldots,d_{r}
are such that
2-2g\leq d_{j}
and
\sum_{j=1}^{r}d_{j}\epsilon_{j}
lies in
\operatorname{int}(W^{o})
.
Equally,

2-2g\leq d_{j},\quad\sum_{k=1}^{r}R_{jk}d_{k}<0,

(5.2)

where
R_{jk}
are the entries to the inverse of the matrix whose entries are
\langle\alpha_{j},\alpha_{k}\rangle
.
In particular, this requires
g\geq 2
.

Proof.

From (
5.1
) we see that
\varphi\in H^{0}(Q^{\mathbb{C}}(B_{\chi}^{-})\otimes K_{\Sigma})
precisely when

\langle\alpha_{k},\chi\rangle\leq 0\ \text{for all}\ k=1,\ldots,r.

(5.3)

In particular
\operatorname{Ad}\exp h_{\chi}\cdot\varphi=\varphi
implies
\chi=0
, hence
0
-polystable implies
0
-stable.
Now (
5.3
) is equivalent to
-h_{\chi}\in W
. If we set
\chi=x_{j}\alpha_{j}
and
d=\sum_{j=1}^{r}d_{j}\epsilon_{j}
then
we require

0>-\sum_{j=1}^{r}x_{j}d_{j}=-\chi(d)=\langle-h_{\chi},d\rangle,\ \forall-h_{\chi}\in W,

and therefore
d\in\operatorname{int}(W^{o})
. Clearly
h\in W
if and only if
h=\sum_{k=1}^{r}a_{k}\epsilon_{k}
with
a_{k}\geq 0
. Hence the
condition above is equivalent to

\sum_{j=1}^{r}d_{j}\langle\epsilon_{j},\epsilon_{k}\rangle<0,\ \forall\ k=1,\ldots,r.

Now
h_{\alpha_{k}}=\sum_{k=1}^{r}\langle\alpha_{l},\alpha_{k}\rangle\epsilon_{l}
and therefore

\delta_{kj}=\langle h_{\alpha_{k}},\epsilon_{j}\rangle=\sum_{l=1}^{r}\langle\alpha_{l},\alpha_{k}\rangle\langle\epsilon_{l},\epsilon_{j}\rangle.

Thus we obtain (
5.2
).
∎

Remark 5.5
.

It is not necessary for
(\tau,\sigma)
to be totally noncompact for Prop.
5.4
to hold. Clearly all that is required is that the
roots
\bar{\alpha}_{1},\ldots,\bar{\alpha}_{r}
are noncompact (
\ell(\bar{\alpha}_{0})
can have either sign).

6.
Toda pairs and Higgs bundles.

Whenever the geometric Toda equations (
1.3
) admit solutions there is a corresponding primitive harmonic map

\psi:\tilde{\Sigma}\to G/T
from the universal cover
\tilde{\Sigma}
of
\Sigma
by Theorem
2.1
. This is
equivariant with respect to some representation
\chi:\pi_{1}\Sigma\to G
. By
Theorem
2.3
and Proposition
2.5
after homogeneous projection
\pi:G/T\to G/H
we obtain an equivariant
harmonic map
f:\tilde{\Sigma}\to G/H
. This has a corresponding
G
-Higgs bundle,
i.e., a holomorphic principal
H^{\mathbb{C}}
bundle

P^{\mathbb{C}}
over
\Sigma
equipped with a holomorphic section
\Phi\in H^{0}(P^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{m}^{\mathbb{C}})\otimes K_{\Sigma})
.
For practical purposes we
can take the Higgs bundle to be the pair

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
be a Toda pair which provides a primitive harmonic map
\psi:\tilde{\Sigma}\to G/T
. Let
\nabla
be
the connection and
\rho
the real involution this induces on
Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{m}^{\mathbb{C}})
by the reduction of
structure group
Q\subset Q^{\mathbb{C}}
. Then the Higgs
bundle
(E,\Phi)
for its projection
f=\pi\circ\psi:\tilde{\Sigma}\to G/H
is such that

E\simeq Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{m}^{\mathbb{C}})
as a smooth bundle but equipped with the holomorphic structure

\nabla^{\prime\prime}+\rho\varphi_{\mathfrak{k}}
, and
\Phi=\varphi_{\mathfrak{m}}
.

Note that the assumption that
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
corresponds to a primitive harmonic map implies that
(E,\Phi)
is a
polystable
G
-Higgs bundle since it corresponds to the harmonic map
f
.

Proof.

Recalling §
2.1
, the connections and Maurer-Cartan
1
-forms of respectively
G/T
and
G/H
come from the two ways of splitting
the left Maurer-Cartan form of
G
:

\omega_{G}=\omega_{\mathfrak{t}}+\omega_{\mathfrak{p}}=\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}+\omega_{\mathfrak{m}}.

Let us write
\beta_{1}:T(G/T)\to G\times_{\mathfrak{t}}\mathfrak{p}
and
\beta_{2}:T(G/H)\to G\times_{\mathfrak{h}}\mathfrak{m}
for the respective Maurer-Cartan

1
-forms of these spaces.
By definition
Q=\psi^{-1}G/\pi_{1}\Sigma
and
\varphi+\rho\varphi=\psi^{*}\beta_{1}
. Define
P=f^{-1}G/\pi_{1}\Sigma
,
then
P\simeq Q\times_{T}H
and hence
E\simeq Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{m}^{\mathbb{C}})
as a smooth bundle.
This bundle carries two connections: a connection
D
from
\omega_{\mathfrak{h}}
and, since
\operatorname{Ad}_{T}
preserves the splitting
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{k}+\mathfrak{m}
, the
connection
\nabla
on
Q^{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{p}^{\mathbb{C}})
coming from
\omega_{\mathfrak{t}}
restricted to
E
. It follows that

D-\nabla=(\psi^{*}\beta_{1})_{\mathfrak{k}}=(\varphi+\rho\varphi)_{\mathfrak{k}}.

Therefore the holomorphic structure of
E
is
D^{\prime\prime}=\nabla^{\prime\prime}+\rho\varphi_{\mathfrak{k}}
.
Using a local lift
F:U\to G
of
\psi
, and hence of
f
, it is easy to see that
f^{*}\beta_{2}=(\psi^{*}\beta_{1})_{\mathfrak{m}}
and
therefore

\Phi=(f^{*}\beta_{2})^{\prime}=(\varphi+\rho\varphi)_{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}=\varphi_{\mathfrak{m}}.

∎

Remark 6.2
.

In general we need to know
Q\subset Q^{\mathbb{C}}
, i.e. have solved the geometric Toda equations, to find
\rho\varphi_{\mathfrak{k}}
to
obtain the holomorphic structure of
E
. However, when
\varphi_{\mathfrak{k}}=0
(equally
\varphi=\varphi_{\mathfrak{m}}
), which includes
the totally noncompact case, the holomorphic
structure of
E
comes directly from
Q^{\mathbb{C}}
without solving the Toda equations, and
\Phi=\varphi
.

An important class of
G
-Higgs bundles are those which are invariant under the
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
action
e^{t}\cdot(E,\Phi)=(E,e^{t}\Phi)
. These are usually referred to as
variations of Hodge structure
(or simply
Hodge
bundles
).

Proposition 6.3
.

Suppose
(E,\Phi)
is a
G
-Higgs bundle obtained from a Toda pair
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
in the manner above.
Then
(E,\Phi)
is a Hodge bundle if and only if
(Q^{\mathbb{C}},\varphi)
is non-cyclic.

Proof.

(E,\Phi)
is a Hodge bundle when there is a one-parameter family of gauge transformations
g_{t}:\Sigma\to H^{\mathbb{C}}
for which

\operatorname{Ad}g_{t}\cdot D^{\prime\prime}=D^{\prime\prime},\quad\operatorname{Ad}g_{t}\cdot\Phi=e^{t}\Phi.

Hence
\operatorname{Ad}g_{t}
must restrict to a holomorphic section on each
\operatorname{End}(Q_{j}(\mathbb{C}))\simeq\underline{\mathbb{C}}
for which
\varphi_{j}\neq 0
. Therefore without
loss of generality we may assume
g_{t}
is constant over
\Sigma
and
g_{t}=\exp(\gamma_{t})
for
\gamma_{t}\in\mathfrak{t}^{\mathbb{C}}
. This must satisfy

\operatorname{Ad}\exp(\gamma_{t})\cdot\varphi_{\mathfrak{m}}=e^{t}\varphi_{\mathfrak{m}}.

Let
J=\{j\in I:\varphi_{j}\neq 0\}
and write its partition as
J=J_{+}\cup J_{-}
. Then for the equations above to hold we require
integers
k_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
j\in J
, such that

\alpha_{j}(\gamma_{t})=t+2\pi ik_{j},\quad j\in J_{-}.

(6.1)

If
J=I
(the cyclic case) then these must satisfy the single equation

0=\sum_{j=0}^{r}m_{j}\alpha_{j}(\gamma_{t})=2\pi i\sum_{j=0}^{r}m_{j}k_{j}+t\sum_{j\in I_{-}}m_{j}.

This cannot hold for all
t
since
\sum_{j\in I_{-}}m_{j}\neq 0
.
However, if
J\neq I
then there is no relation between the
\alpha_{j}
for
j\in J
and therefore (
6.1
) can
be solved for any
t
(e.g., for all
j\in J
take
k_{j}=0
and choose
\gamma_{t}=t\xi
where
\alpha_{j}(\xi)=1
for
j\in J
).
∎

6.1.
Baraglia’s cyclic Higgs bundles.

In
[
3

Baraglia pointed out that certain real forms of the Toda equations arise from special cases within

is constant for

-symmetry upon the Higgs field, and therefore

the outer symmetric space of the split real form: Baraglia’s explanation of this is essentially the same as the mechanism
described in §

The purpose of this section is to show what happens if we drop the insistence that the Coxeter automorphism be inner.
By Table

(or Remark
3.4
) the outer Coxeter automorphisms provide different Toda pairs from

We begin by adapting Kostant’s construction of the principal three dimensional subgroup

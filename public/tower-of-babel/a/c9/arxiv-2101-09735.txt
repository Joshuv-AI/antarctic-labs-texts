Abstract
. For the Hodge–Laplace equation in finite element exterior calculus, we introduce several families of discontinuous Galerkin methods in the extended Galerkin framework. For contractible domains, this framework utilizes seven fields and provides a unifying inf-sup analysis with respect to all discretization and penalty parameters. It is shown that the proposed methods can be hybridized as a reduced two-field formulation.

Primary 65N12, 65N15, 65N30

1.
Introduction

Finite element exterior calculus (FEEC) is a powerful and elegant framework unifying numerical analysis on the de Rham complex, or more generally, closed Hilbert complexes.
In recent decades, theory of FEEC has been under intensive investigation and applied to a variety of problems such as vector Laplacian
[
7
]
, Stokes
[
31
,
19
]
, Maxwell
[
34
]
, and elasticity equations
[
8
,
4
]
. For a thorough introduction to FEEC, readers are referred to
[
7
,
9
,
2
]
and references therein. As a model problem in FEEC, the continuous Galerkin/Arnold–Falk–Winther (AFW) method for the following Hodge–Laplace equation (without harmonic forms)

(1.1)

(d\delta+\delta d)u=f

has been discussed in many aspects, see, e.g.,
[
7
,
20
,
30
,
45
]
for commuting projections and a priori error estimates,
[
27
,
26
,
18
,
43
,
44
]
for a posteriori error estimates and adaptive algorithms,
[
32
,
6
]
for time-dependent problems, and
[
3
,
33
,
28
]
for FEEC on cubical and polyhedral meshes.

The discontinuous
Galerkin (DG) methods can be traced back to the late 1960s

[
46
,
10
]
. DG methods have achieved great success in purely convective or convection-dominated problems in fluids, see, e.g.,
[
41
,
54
,
23
,
22
]
and references therein. In recent decades, DG methods have also been applied to purely
elliptic problems, see, e.g., interior penalty methods
[
29
,
12
,
56
,
1
]
, enriched Galerkin methods
[
13
,
55
]
, local DG methods
[
24
]
, and hybridized DG methods
[
17
,
21
]
. A unified DG analysis for elliptic problems could be found in
[
5
]
. To achieve
consistency and stability, DG methods often utilize additional finite element spaces on the boundaries of elements. In most existing DG methods, only one boundary discrete space (Lagrangian multiplier
space) is introduced, cf.
[
16
,
36
]
. Recently, an extended Galerkin (XG) framework for Poisson’s equation was presented in
[
37
]
. This framework makes use of two auxiliary finite element spaces (check spaces) on element boundaries and unifies several classes of popular DG methods in the literature. The XG method was further generalized to linear elasticity equations in
[
35
]
.

To the best of our knowledge, a detailed analysis of DG methods in FEEC is still
missing
. Very recently, the work
[
11
]
derived hybridization and postprocessing technique for the AFW mixed method in FEEC and discussed possible extensions to hybridized DG methods. In addition, there are several relevant works on non-standard discretizations of the Maxwell equations, see, e.g., DG methods
[
38
,
39
,
50
,
42
]
, weak Galerkin methods
[
47
]
, and virtual
element methods
[
25
]
. Comparing to Maxwell equations, the DG discretization of the mixed Hodge Laplacian is expected to involve more unknown variables, numerical fluxes and stabilization terms.

In this work, we present several DG methods for the Hodge–Laplace equation in a unified XG framework, which employs three unknowns approximating
u,

du
,
\delta u
as well as four “check” variables defined on element boundaries. Similarly to the XG method for Poisson’s equation
[
37
]
, the seven-field XG formulation is motivated by the so called Nitsche’s trick
[
51
,
52
]
. In fact, the solution
u
of the Hodge Laplace equation (
1.1
) satisfies two types of inter-element continuity conditions based on
d
and
\delta
. In the existing literature, the classical AFW mixed method essentially utilizes one of them. Working in totally discontinuous Galerkin subspaces, the XG method penalizes the inter-element continuity w.r.t. both
d
and
\delta
by check variables. Therefore, the proposed XG method stays in an intermediate stage between AFW conforming methods. In addition, it is shown that the solution of our XG method converges to the classical AFW mixed method as penalty parameters approach suitable limits.

By eliminating one or more fields, the seven-field XG formulation could be further reduced to a variety of more compact DG methods. In the extreme case, we hybridize the XG method to obtain a condensed system with only two unknown numerical fluxes. It is well known that the Hodge Laplacian covers the Poisson’s equation and vector Laplacian as special cases. Correspondingly, the proposed seven-field XG method reduces to the original four-field XG method in
[
37
]
for the Poisson equation in primal and mixed forms. It also leads to several new families of DG methods for the vector Laplacian.

The XG framework also facilitates a transparent convergence analysis for DG discretizations of the Hodge–Laplace equation. In particular, we derive the quasi-optimal error estimate of the XG method through constructing a discrete inf-sup condition uniform w.r.t. all discretization and penalty parameters. In doing so, several fields on element boundaries are eliminated and the remaining variables are carefully grouped to obtain a standard perturbed saddle point system (
4.7
). It turns out that the resulting saddle system could be analyzed block-wise using the classical Babuska–Brezzi inf-sup theory, cf.
[
14
]
.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce notation and preliminaries in FEEC. In Section
3
, we derive the XG formulations for the Hodge–Laplace equation. Section
4
is then devoted to the analysis of continuous and discrete inf-sup conditions. In Section
5
, we discuss the
relationship between XG methods and the classical mixed methods. In Section
6
, a hybridized version of the proposed XG method is proposed. Finally, we restate XG methods for the Hodge–Laplace equation in the language of vector calculus in Section
7
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we follow the convention in
[
7
,
9
]
to present minimal preliminaries in FEEC for developing XG methods with differential forms.

2.1.
De Rham complex

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz polyhedron. For simplicity of presentation, throughout the rest of this paper, we assume that the cohomology of
\Omega
is trivial, e.g.,
\Omega
is contractible.

2.1.1.
Continuous de Rham Complex

Given an integer
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,n\}
, let
S^{n}_{k}
denote the set of increasing multi-indices in the form
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{k})\in\mathbb{N}^{k}
with
1\leq\alpha_{1}<\cdots<\alpha_{k}\leq n
. Let
\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)
be the space of smooth differential
k
-forms written as

\omega=\sum_{\alpha\in S_{k}^{n}}\omega_{\alpha}dx^{\alpha_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{\alpha_{k}},

where each coefficient
\omega_{\alpha}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
, and
\wedge
is the wedge product. The space
\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)
is naturally endowed with the
L^{2}
-inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
given as

(\omega,\eta):=\sum_{1\leq\alpha_{1}<\cdots<\alpha_{k}\leq n}\int_{\Omega}\omega_{\alpha}\eta_{\alpha}dx,\quad\omega,\eta\in\Lambda^{k}(\Omega).

The induced
L^{2}
-norm is
\|\cdot\|
. We further denote the
L^{2}
-inner product and
L^{2}
-norm on a Lipschitz submanifold
U\subseteq\Omega
as
(\cdot,\cdot)_{U}
and
\|\cdot\|_{U}
, respectively.
Let
d^{k}:\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)\rightarrow\Lambda^{k+1}(\Omega)
denote the exterior derivative for differential forms, that is,

d^{k}\omega=\sum_{\alpha\in S_{k}^{n}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial\omega_{\alpha}}{\partial x_{i}}dx^{i}\wedge dx^{\alpha_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge dx^{\alpha_{k}}.

The space
L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)
is the completion of
\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)
w.r.t. the norm
\|\cdot\|
, which is simply the space of
k
-forms with
L^{2}(\Omega)
coefficients.
The derivative
d^{k}
can be extended as a weak derivative
d^{k}:L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}\Lambda^{k+1}(\Omega)
, which is a densely defined unbounded operator with domain

H\Lambda^{k}(\Omega):=\big\{\omega\in L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega):d^{k}\omega\in L^{2}\Lambda^{k+1}(\Omega)\big\}.

We shall also make use of
H^{s}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)
, the Sobolev space of differential
k
-forms whose coefficients are in
H^{s}(\Omega)
. Due to
d^{k}\circ d^{k-1}=0
, the following sequence

(2.1)

\begin{CD}H\Lambda^{0}(\Omega)@>{d^{0}}>{}>H\Lambda^{1}(\Omega)@>{d^{1}}>{}>\cdots @>{d^{n-2}}>{}>H\Lambda^{n-1}(\Omega)@>{d^{n-1}}>{}>H\Lambda^{n}(\Omega)\end{CD}

is called the de Rham complex.
Let
\mathfrak{Z}^{k}:=N(d^{k})
be the kernel of
d^{k}
,
\mathfrak{B}^{k}:=R(d^{k-1})
be the range of
d^{k-1}
, and
\mathfrak{H}^{k}:=\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\cap\mathfrak{B}^{k\perp}
be the space of harmonic
k
-forms. Here
\perp
is the operation of taking
L^{2}
-orthogonal complement in
H\Lambda^{k}(\Omega).
Due to the assumption on
\Omega,
we have

(2.2)

\mathfrak{H}^{k}=\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathbb{R},\quad k=0,\\
\{0\},\quad k\geq 1,\end{aligned}\right.

and thus
\mathfrak{B}^{k}=\mathfrak{Z}^{k}
if
k\geq 1.
It then follows that

(2.3)

H\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)=\mathfrak{Z}^{k}\oplus\mathfrak{Z}^{k\perp}=\mathfrak{B}^{k}\oplus\mathfrak{Z}^{k\perp},\quad k\geq 1.

In addition, there exists an absolute constant
c_{P}>0
relying on
\Omega
such that

(2.4)

\|v\|\leq c_{P}\|d^{k}v\|,\quad\forall v\in\mathfrak{Z}^{k\perp}.

For each index
k
, the Hodge star
\star:L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}\Lambda^{n-k}(\Omega)
is a linear isomorphism determined by the equation

\int_{\Omega}\omega\wedge\mu=(\star\omega,\mu),\quad\forall\mu\in L^{2}\Lambda^{n-k}(\Omega).

It is well-known that for
\omega,\mu\in L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)
,

(2.5a)

\displaystyle\star\star\omega=(-1)^{k(n-k)}\omega,

(2.5b)

\displaystyle(\star\omega,\star\mu)=(\omega,\mu).

With the help of
\star,
the coderivative
\delta^{k}:H^{1}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}\Lambda^{k-1}(\Omega)
is then defined as

(2.6)

\star\delta^{k}\omega=(-1)^{k}d^{n-k}\star\omega.

Given Lipschitz manifolds
M\subseteq\Omega_{0}\subseteq\Omega
, let
i_{M,\Omega_{0}}:M\rightarrow\Omega_{0}
be the inclusion, and
\tr_{M,\Omega_{0}}=i_{M,\Omega_{0}}^{*}
be the pullback for differential forms based on
i_{M,\Omega_{0}}
, i.e., the trace operator on
M
. We denote
\tr_{M}=\tr_{M,\Omega}
and may suppress the subscript
M
provided relevant domains are clear from the context.
It is well known that
d^{k}
and
\delta^{k+1}
are related by the Stokes’ formula

(2.7)

(d^{k}\omega,\mu)_{U}=(\omega,\delta^{k+1}\mu)_{U}+\int_{\partial U}\tr_{\partial U,U}(\omega\wedge\star\mu),

where
\omega\in H^{1}\Lambda^{k}(U),~\mu\in H^{1}\Lambda^{k+1}(U)
, and
\partial U
is set to be
outward orientated
.
The Sobolev space of
k
-forms with essential boundary condition is

\mathring{H}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega):=\big\{\omega\in H\Lambda^{k}(\Omega):\tr_{\partial\Omega}\omega=0\big\}.

Such spaces form a de Rham complex with essential boundary condition

(2.8)

\begin{CD}\mathring{H}\Lambda^{0}(\Omega)@>{d^{0}}>{}>\mathring{H}\Lambda^{1}(\Omega)@>{d^{1}}>{}>\cdots @>{d^{n-2}}>{}>\mathring{H}\Lambda^{n-1}(\Omega)@>{d^{n-1}}>{}>\mathring{H}\Lambda^{n}(\Omega)\end{CD}

The coderivative
\delta^{k}
could be extended as a closely defined and unbounded operator
\delta^{k}:L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}\Lambda^{k-1}(\Omega)
with domain

\displaystyle\mathring{H}^{*}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega):=\star\mathring{H}\Lambda^{n-k}(\Omega)

\displaystyle=\{\omega\in L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega):\delta^{k}\omega\in L^{2}\Lambda^{k-1}(\Omega),\ \tr_{\partial\Omega}\star\omega=0\}.

As a result of
\delta^{k}\circ\delta^{k+1}=0
, we have the following exact sequence

(2.9)

\begin{CD}\mathring{H}^{*}\Lambda^{0}(\Omega)@<{\delta^{1}}<{}<\mathring{H}^{*}\Lambda^{1}(\Omega)@<{\delta^{2}}<{}<\cdots @<{\delta^{n-1}}<{}<\mathring{H}^{*}\Lambda^{n-1}(\Omega)@<{\delta^{n}}<{}<\mathring{H}^{*}\Lambda^{n}(\Omega),\end{CD}

which is
the dual complex of (
2.1
).

2.1.2.
Discrete de Rham Complex

In the following, we present the discrete analogue of the de Rham complex (
2.1
). Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}
be a conforming partition of
\Omega
, which is shape-regular in the sense that

\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}(r_{T}/\rho_{T}):=C_{\text{mesh}}<\infty,

where
r_{T}
,
\rho_{T}
are radii of circumscribed and inscribed spheres of
T
, and
C_{\text{mesh}}
is an absolute constant.
Given a Lipschitz manifold
U
and an integer
r\geq 0
, define

\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(U):=\{\omega\in L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(U):\omega_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{P}_{r}(U)\text{ for all }\alpha\in S^{n}_{k}\},

which is the space of
k
-forms on
U
with polynomial coefficients of degree at most
r
.
Let

\kappa:L^{2}\Lambda^{k+1}(U)\rightarrow L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(U)
denote the interior product for differential forms.
We shall also make use of the space of
k
-forms on
U
with incomplete polynomial coefficients

\mathcal{P}^{-}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(U):=\mathcal{P}_{r-1}\Lambda^{k}(U)+\kappa\big(\mathcal{P}_{r-1}\Lambda^{k+1}(U)\big).

There exist two families of conforming finite element subspaces of
H\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}):=H\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)\cap\prod_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(T),

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{-}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}):=H\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)\cap\prod_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\mathcal{P}^{-}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(T).

Under essential boundary conditions, we define

\displaystyle\mathring{\mathcal{P}}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}):=\mathring{H}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),

\displaystyle\mathring{\mathcal{P}}^{-}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}):=\mathring{H}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{P}^{-}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}).

For each index
k
, we may choose
V^{k}_{h}=\mathcal{P}_{r_{k}}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
or
\mathcal{P}^{-}_{r_{k}}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
. In addition,
\{V_{h}^{k}\}_{k=0}^{n}
is assumed to form a discrete de Rham complex

(2.10)

\begin{CD}V_{h}^{0}@>{d^{0}}>{}>V_{h}^{1}@>{d^{1}}>{}>\cdots @>{d^{n-2}}>{}>V_{h}^{n-1}@>{d^{n-1}}>{}>V_{h}^{n},\end{CD}

which could be connected with (
2.1
) via commuting interpolations, see (
2.17
) and
[
7
,
9
]
for more details. In the discrete level,

\mathring{V}_{h}^{k}:=\mathring{H}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)\cap V_{h}^{k},\quad 0\leq k\leq n

form a discrete complex with essential boundary conditions.
Similarly to the continuous case, let
\mathfrak{Z}_{h}^{k}:=N(d^{k}|_{V_{h}^{k}})
,
\mathfrak{B}_{h}^{k}:=d^{k-1}(V_{h}^{k-1})
, and
\mathfrak{H}_{h}^{k}:=\mathfrak{Z}_{h}^{k}\cap\mathfrak{B}_{h}^{k\perp}
. The space of discrete harmonic
k
-forms
\mathfrak{H}_{h}^{k}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{H}^{k}
. In particular,

(2.11)

\mathfrak{H}_{h}^{k}=\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathbb{R},\quad k=0,\\
\{0\},\quad k\geq 1.\end{aligned}\right.

For
k\geq 1,

it is straightforward to obtain

(2.12)

V_{h}^{k}=\mathfrak{B}_{h}^{k}\oplus\mathfrak{Z}_{h}^{k\perp}=d^{k-1}V_{h}^{k-1}\oplus\mathfrak{Z}_{h}^{k\perp},

i.e., the discrete Hodge decomposition. In addition, there exists an absolute constant
c_{dP}>0
dependent on
\Omega
and
C_{\text{mesh}}
such that

(2.13)

\|v_{h}\|\leq c_{dP}\|d^{k}v_{h}\|,\quad\forall v_{h}\in\mathfrak{Z}_{h}^{k\perp}.

In the literature, (
2.13
) is known as the discrete Poincaré inequality and the proof could be found in e.g.,
[
7
]
.

Throughout the rest of this paper, we may suppress the super-index of
\delta^{k}
,
d^{k}
,
L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)
,
H\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)
etc. for a fixed
k
and
adopt the notation

\displaystyle d=d^{k},\quad\delta=\delta^{k},\quad d^{-}=d^{k-1},\quad\delta^{+}=\delta^{k+1},

\displaystyle V^{-}=H\Lambda^{k-1}(\Omega),\quad V=H\Lambda^{k}(\Omega),\quad W^{+}=L^{2}\Lambda^{k+1}(\Omega).

Similar notation will be used in the discrete level. The
V^{-}
- and
V
-norms are

\displaystyle\|\tau\|_{V^{-}}^{2}=\|\tau\|^{2}+\|d^{-}\tau\|^{2},\quad\tau\in V^{-},

\displaystyle\|v\|_{V}^{2}=\|v\|^{2}+\|dv\|^{2},\quad v\in V.

2.2.
Hodge Laplacian and approximation

Given an index
k\geq 1
and data
f\in L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)
,
we consider the the Hodge–Laplace equation

(2.14)

(d^{-}\delta+\delta^{+}d)u=f.

The solution
u
is contained in the space
H\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)\cap\mathring{H}^{*}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega),
which is the intersection of domains of
d
and
\delta
. Therefore the primal variational formulation for (
2.14
) is to find
u\in H\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)\cap\mathring{H}^{*}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)
such that

(2.15)

(du,dv)+(\delta u,\delta v)=(f,v),\quad\forall v\in H\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)\cap\mathring{H}^{*}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega).

Although (
2.15
) is well-posed, a practical finite element subspace of
H\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)\cap\mathring{H}^{*}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega)
is not available yet. To remedy this situation, Arnold, Falk, and Winther
[
9
]
considered the mixed formulation of (
2.14
): Find
(\sigma,u)\in V^{-}\times V
, such that

(2.16a)

\displaystyle(\sigma,\tau)+(d^{-}\tau,u)

\displaystyle=0,\quad

\displaystyle\tau\in V^{-},

(2.16b)

\displaystyle(d^{-}\sigma,v)-(du,dv)

\displaystyle=-(f,v),

\displaystyle v\in V.

From (
2.16a
),
it could be observed that
\sigma=-\delta u
. The well-posedness of the variational formulation (
2.16
) was verified in
[
7
]
.

To discretize the Hodge–Laplace equation (
2.16
), the finite element pair
V_{h}^{-}\times V_{h}=V_{h}^{k-1}\times V_{h}^{k}

must be carefully chosen such that a discrete inf-sup stability is satisfied. In this paper, we choose the Arnold–Falk–Winther finite element space

(2.17)

V_{h}^{-}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{P}_{r+1}\Lambda^{k-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\\
&\mathcal{P}^{-}_{r+1}\Lambda^{k-1}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\end{aligned}\right\},\quad V_{h}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{P}^{-}_{r+1}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),\\
&\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})\end{aligned}\right\}.

The AFW mixed method for (
3.6
) is to find
(\sigma^{c}_{h},u^{c}_{h})\in V_{h}^{-}\times V_{h}
, such that

(2.18)

\displaystyle(\sigma^{c}_{h},\tau_{h})+(d^{-}\tau_{h},u^{c}_{h})

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\tau_{h}\in V_{h}^{-},

\displaystyle(d^{-}\sigma^{c}_{h},v_{h})-(du^{c}_{h},dv_{h})

\displaystyle=-(f,v_{h}),

\displaystyle v_{h}\in V_{h}.

In
[
7
,
9
]
, the discrete inf-sup condition and a priori error estimates of (
2.18
) were established.

3.
Extended Galerkin Formulation

In this section, we derive an XG framework unifying several DG methods for the Hodge Laplacian (
2.14
). Those DG methods converge to the classical AFW mixed method (
2.18
) in limiting cases.

3.1.
DG Notation

In a partition
\mathcal{T}_{h},
let
\mathcal{E}_{h}
denote the set of faces (of dimension
n-1
), and
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\partial}\subset\mathcal{E}_{h}
be the collection of faces that are contained in
\partial\Omega
. The set of interior faces is then
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}:=\mathcal{E}_{h}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\partial}
. Given subsets
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{h}
and
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{h},
let

\displaystyle H^{1}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{T}):=\prod_{T\in\mathcal{T}}H^{1}\Lambda^{k}(T),\quad L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{E}):=\prod_{E\in\mathcal{E}}L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(E),

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{T}):=\prod_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(T),\quad\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{E}):=\prod_{E\in\mathcal{E}}\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(E),

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{P}}^{-}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{T}):=\prod_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\mathcal{P}^{-}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(T).

Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{E}
be the
L^{2}
-inner product of differential forms on
E
. We adopt the notation

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{E},\quad\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}}=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\partial T},\quad\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}.

The
L^{2}
-norm based on
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{E}}
is denoted as
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{E}}
.
Each face
E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
is assigned with a unit norm
\nu_{E}
. Given an interior face
E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o},
let
T_{E}^{+}
,
T_{E}^{-}
denote elements in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
sharing
E
as a face such that
\nu_{E}
is pointing out of
T_{E}^{+}
. In addition,
\nu_{E}
is chosen to be outward and
T_{E}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
denotes the element containing
E
if
E\subset\partial\Omega
is a boundary face, see Figure
1
.

Figure 1.
An interior element pair and a boundary element.

On a face
E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
, the face Hodge star
\bar{\star}:L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(E)\rightarrow L^{2}\Lambda^{n-k}(E)
is a linear isometry such that for
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(E)

and
\eta_{3}\in L^{2}\Lambda^{n-k-1}(E)
,

(3.1a)

\displaystyle\langle\bar{\star}\eta_{1},\bar{\star}\eta_{2}\rangle_{E}=\langle\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\rangle_{E},

(3.1b)

\displaystyle\int_{E}\eta_{1}\wedge\eta_{3}=\langle\bar{\star}\eta_{1},\eta_{3}\rangle_{E}.

Given
\omega\in H^{1}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
and
E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
, we introduce the trace average
\{\!\!\{\tr\omega\}\!\!\}\in L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{E}_{h})
and trace jump
\llbracket\tr\omega\rrbracket\in L^{2}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{E}_{h})
, which are widely used in the DG literature. In particular, for each interior edge
E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}
,

\displaystyle\{\!\!\{\tr\omega\}\!\!\}|_{E}:=\frac{1}{2}\left\{\tr_{E}(\omega|_{T_{E}^{+}})+\tr_{E}(\omega|_{T_{E}^{-}})\right\},\quad E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o},

\displaystyle\llbracket\tr\omega\rrbracket|_{E}:=\tr_{E}(\omega|_{T_{E}^{+}})-\tr_{E}(\omega|_{T_{E}^{-}}),\quad E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}.

Furthermore, for each boundary edge
E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\partial}
, we distinguish the trace averages/jumps of
\omega
and
\star\omega
as

Consider the kernel of

Next we verify the inf-sup condition of

For any

) and (

5.1.
An alternative discretization

The AFW method (

\displaystyle(\xi,\eta)+(\delta^{+}\eta,u)

\displaystyle=0,\quad

\displaystyle\eta\in\mathring{H}^{*}\Lambda^{k+1}(\Omega),

\displaystyle(\sigma,\tau)+(\delta u,\tau)

\displaystyle=0,\quad

\displaystyle\tau\in L^{2}\Lambda^{k-1}(\Omega),

\displaystyle(\delta^{+}\xi,v)+(\sigma,\delta v)

\displaystyle=-(f,v),

\displaystyle v\in\mathring{H}^{*}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega).

In fact (
5.2
) is based on the dual complex (
2.9
), which is
isomorphic
to the de Rham complex (
2.8
) via the identification

\begin{CD}\mathring{H}\Lambda^{n-k}(\Omega)@>{\star}>{}>\mathring{H}^{*}\Lambda^{k}(\Omega).\end{CD}

Therefore, (
5.2
) is nothing but the variational formulation (
3.6
) for the Hodge Laplacian with index

under essential boundary conditions.

To discretize (

\displaystyle\quad=-(f,u_{h})_{T}+\langle{\bar{\star}\tr u_{h}},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{\star}\rangle^{s}_{\partial T}+\langle 2\textsf{a}\hat{v}_{h}^{\star},\tr\star u_{h}\rangle_{\partial T}.

Using (
6.8a
), (
6.9a
), (
6.5c
), we have

\displaystyle\langle 2\textsf{a}(\hat{u}_{h}^{\star}-\tr\star u_{h}),\hat{v}_{h}^{\star}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle=\langle\bar{\star}\tr\sigma_{h},\hat{v}_{h}^{\star}\rangle^{s}_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}=(\tau_{h},\sigma_{h})+(d_{h}^{-}\sigma_{h},v_{h})

\displaystyle=(\tau_{h},\sigma_{h})-(\xi_{h},dv_{h})-\langle 2\textsf{a}\tr\star u_{h},\tr\star v_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle-(f,v_{h})+\langle{\bar{\star}\tr v_{h}},\hat{\xi}_{h}^{\star}\rangle^{s}_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle 2\textsf{a}\hat{u}_{h}^{\star},\tr\star v_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}.

Simplifying the above equation yields

(6.10)

\displaystyle\langle 2\textsf{a}(\hat{u}_{h}^{\star}-\tr\star u_{h}),\hat{v}_{h}^{\star}-\tr\star v_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle=(\tau_{h},\sigma_{h})-(\xi_{h},dv_{h})-(f,v_{h})+\langle{\bar{\star}\tr v_{h}},\hat{\xi}_{h}^{\star}\rangle^{s}_{\partial T}.

On the other hand, it follows from (
6.8b
) and (
6.9b
) that

(6.11)

\displaystyle\langle 2\textsf{c}\hat{\xi}_{h}^{\star},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{\star}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle\bar{\star}\tr u_{h},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{\star}\rangle^{s}_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}=\langle 2\textsf{c}\tr\star\xi_{h},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{\star}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle=(\eta_{h},\xi_{h})+(\xi_{h},d_{h}v_{h})+\langle 2\textsf{c}\tr\star{\eta}_{h},\tr\star\xi_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}.

Summing (
6.10
) and (
6.11
) leads to

(6.12)

\displaystyle\langle 2\textsf{a}\big(\hat{u}_{h}^{\star}-\tr\star u_{h}\big),\hat{v}_{h}^{\star}-\tr\star v_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle 2\textsf{c}\hat{\xi}_{h}^{\star},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{\star}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle\bar{\star}\tr u_{h},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{\star}\rangle^{s}_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle=(\tau_{h},\sigma_{h})-(f,v_{h})+\langle{\bar{\star}\tr v_{h}},\hat{\xi}_{h}^{\star}\rangle^{s}_{\partial T}+(\eta_{h},\xi_{h})+\langle 2\textsf{c}\tr\star{\eta}_{h},\tr\star\xi_{h}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}.

Therefore, (
6.12
) translates into the hybridized globally coupled problem: Find
\hat{u}_{h}^{\star}\in\check{\mathcal{V}}^{\star}_{h}
,
\hat{\xi}_{h}^{\star}\in\check{\mathcal{V}}^{+\star}_{h}
such that

(6.13)

\displaystyle(H_{h}^{-}(\hat{u}_{h}^{\star},\hat{\xi}_{h}^{\star}),H_{h}^{-}(\hat{v}_{h}^{\star},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{\star}))+(H_{h}^{+}(\hat{u}_{h}^{\star},\hat{\xi}_{h}^{\star}),H_{h}^{+}(\hat{v}_{h}^{\star},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{\star}))

\displaystyle+\langle 2\textsf{c}\tr\star H_{h}^{+}(\hat{u}_{h}^{\star},\hat{\xi}_{h}^{\star}),\tr\star H_{h}^{+}(\hat{v}_{h}^{\star},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{\star})\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}-\langle 2\textsf{c}\hat{\xi}_{h}^{\star},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{\star}\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle-\langle 2\textsf{a}(\hat{u}_{h}^{\star}-\tr\star H_{h}(\hat{u}_{h}^{\star},\hat{\xi}_{h}^{\star})),\hat{v}_{h}^{\star}-\tr\star H_{h}(\hat{v}_{h}^{\star},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{\star})\rangle_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}

\displaystyle+\langle{\bar{\star}\tr H_{h}(\hat{v}_{h}^{\star},\hat{\eta}_{h}^{\star})},\hat{\xi}_{h}^{\star}\rangle^{s}_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}+\langle\bar{\star}\tr H_{h}(\hat{u}_{h}^{\star},\hat{\xi}_{h}^{\star}),\hat{\eta}_{h}^{\star}\rangle^{s}_{\partial\mathcal{T}_{h}}=(f,v_{h}),

for all
\hat{v}_{h}^{\star}\in\check{\mathcal{V}}^{\star}_{h}
,
\hat{\eta}_{h}^{\star}\in\check{\mathcal{V}}^{+\star}_{h}
.

,
\sigma_{h}^{c}\in V^{-}_{h}

.

Proof of Lemma
4.2
.

First we consider the case
V_{h}=\mathcal{P}_{r}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
. Similar arguments apply to
V_{h}=\mathcal{P}_{r}^{-}\Lambda^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})
. Let
\Delta(T)
denote the set of all subsimplexes of
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and
\Delta(\mathcal{T}_{h})=\cup_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\Delta(T)
. Given a simplex
g\in\Delta(\mathcal{T}_{h}),
we fix a basis
\{\mu_{g}^{i}\}_{i\in I_{g}}
of
\mathcal{P}_{r+k-\dim(g)}^{-}\Lambda^{\dim(g)-k}(g)
such that
\|\mu_{g}^{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(g)}=1
, where
\text{dim}(g)
is the dimension of
g
. It has been shown in
[
7
,
9
]
that the degrees of freedom of
V_{h}
consist of

(7.10)

|g|^{-1}\int_{g}(\tr_{g}w_{h})\wedge\mu_{g}^{i},\quad g\in\Delta(\mathcal{T}_{h}),~i\in I_{g}

for
w_{h}\in V_{h},
where
|g|
is the
\dim(g)
-measure of
g.
Let
\{\phi_{g}^{i}\}_{g\in\Delta(\mathcal{T}_{h}),i\in I_{g}}
be the dual basis of
V_{h}
w.r.t. the degrees of freedom in (
7.10
). It holds that

(7.11)

\|\phi_{g}^{i}\|_{T}\simeq h_{T}^{\frac{n}{2}},\quad\|d\phi_{g}^{i}\|_{T}\simeq h_{T}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}.

Let
\omega_{g}
denote the set of elements in
\mathcal{T}_{h}
sharing
g
as a common subsimplex. For any
v_{h}\in\mathcal{V}_{h},
we define the average of
\tr v_{h}
surrounding
g
as

(7.12)

\bar{v}^{g}_{h,i}=\frac{1}{\#\omega_{g}}\sum_{T^{\prime}\in\omega_{g}}|g|^{-1}\int_{g}\tr_{g}(v_{h}|_{T^{\prime}})\wedge\mu_{g}^{i}.

The conforming approximation
v_{h}^{c}\in V_{h}
is constructed as

(7.13)

v_{h}^{c}=\sum_{g\in\Delta(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\sum_{i\in I_{g}}\bar{v}^{g}_{h,i}\phi_{g}^{i}.

On each element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},
the function
v_{h}|_{T}
is expressed as

(7.14)

v_{h}|_{T}=\sum_{g\in\Delta(\mathcal{T}_{h})}\sum_{i\in I_{g}}\left(|g|^{-1}\int_{g}\tr_{g}(v_{h}|_{T})\wedge\mu_{g}^{i}\right)\phi_{g}^{i}.

It then follows from (
7.11
), (
7.13
), (
7.14
) that

(7.15)

\displaystyle h^{-1}_{T}\|(v_{h}-v_{h}^{c})\|_{T}+\|d(v_{h}-v_{h}^{c})\|_{T}

\displaystyle\lesssim h_{T}^{\frac{n}{2}-1}\sum_{g\subset\Delta(T)}\sum_{i\in I_{g}}\sum_{T^{\prime}\in\omega_{g}}\left||g|^{-1}\int_{g}\{\tr_{g}(v_{h}|_{T})-\tr_{g}(v_{h}|_{T^{\prime}})\}\wedge\mu_{g}^{i}\right|.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{h}(g)=\{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}:\bar{E}\cap\bar{g}\neq\emptyset\}
.
If
T
and
T^{\prime}\in\omega_{g}
share a common face
E
in
\mathcal{E}_{h}(g)
, we have

(7.16)

\|\tr_{g}(v_{h}|_{T})-\tr_{g}(v_{h}|_{T^{\prime}})\|_{L^{\infty}(g)}\lesssim h_{E}^{-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}\|\llbracket\tr v_{h}\rrbracket\|_{E}.

If
T
and
T^{\prime}\in\omega_{g}
share some low dimensional simplex
g^{\prime}
with
\text{dim}(g)<\text{dim}(g^{\prime})<n-1
, one could find a chain of pairs of adjacent elements in
\omega_{g}
sharing a
\dim(g^{\prime})+1
dimensional simplex in
\omega_{g}
. Therefore, for an arbitrary
T^{\prime}\in\omega_{g}
, there exists a chain of pairs of adjacent elements (possibly repeated) in
\omega_{g}
that share a face in
\mathcal{E}_{h}(g)
. Therefore applying (
7.16
) and the triangle inequality, we obtain

(7.17)

\|\tr_{g}(v_{h}|_{T})-\tr_{g}(v_{h}|_{T^{\prime}})\|_{L^{\infty}(g)}\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}(g)}h_{E}^{-\frac{n}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}\|\llbracket\tr v_{h}\rrbracket\|_{E},\quad\forall T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}_{h}(g).

We also refer to
[
40
]
for a detailed argument in nodal DG methods.
Combining (
7.15
) and (
7.17
), we obtain

(7.18)

h^{-1}_{T}\|(v_{h}-v_{h}^{c})\|_{T}+\|d(v_{h}-v_{h}^{c})\|_{T}\lesssim\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}(g)}h_{E}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\llbracket\tr v_{h}\rrbracket\|_{E}.

Summing (
7.18
) over all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
finishes the proof.

∎

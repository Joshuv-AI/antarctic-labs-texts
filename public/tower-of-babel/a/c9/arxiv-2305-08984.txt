II
General problem statement

The most efficient non-perturbative evaluation of the VP-charge density
\varrho_{VP}(\vec{r})
is based on the Wichmann and Kroll (WK) approach
Wichmann and Kroll 1956
;
Gyulassy 1975
;
Mohr et al. 1998
. The starting point of the latter is the vacuum value

\varrho_{VP}(\vec{r})=-\frac{|e|}{2}\left(\sum\limits_{\epsilon_{n}<\epsilon_{F}}\psi_{n}(\vec{r})^{\dagger}\psi_{n}(\vec{r})-\sum\limits_{\epsilon_{n}\geqslant\epsilon_{F}}\psi_{n}(\vec{r})^{\dagger}\psi_{n}(\vec{r})\right)\ .

(8)

In (
8
)
\epsilon_{F}=-1
is the Fermi level, which in such problems with strong Coulomb fields is chosen at the lower threshold, while
\epsilon_{n}
and
\psi_{n}(\vec{r})
are the eigenvalues and properly normalized set of eigenfunctions of the corresponding DC problem. The expression (
8
) for the VP-charge density is a direct consequence of the well-known Schwinger prescription for the fermionic current in terms of the fermion fields commutators

j_{\mu}(\vec{r},t)=-{|e|\over 2}\,\left[\bar{\psi}(\vec{r},t)\,,\gamma_{\mu}\psi(\vec{r},t)\right]\ .

(9)

An important point here is that in the case under question
\varrho_{VP}(\vec{r})
turns out to be not an average, but an eigenvalue of the fermionic charge density operator
j_{0}(\vec{r},t)
acting on the vacuum state. The last statement follows immediately from the expansion of the fermionic fields in terms of creation-annihilation operators in the Furry picture

\psi(\vec{r},t)=\sum\limits_{\epsilon_{n}\geqslant\epsilon_{F}}b_{n}\,\psi_{n}(\vec{r})\,\mathrm{e}^{-i\epsilon_{n}t}+\sum\limits_{\epsilon_{n}<\epsilon_{F}}d_{n}^{\dagger}\,\psi_{n}(\vec{r})\,\mathrm{e}^{-i\epsilon_{n}t}\ ,

(10)

where

\{b_{n}\,,b_{n^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\}=\delta_{nn^{\prime}}\ ,\quad\{d_{n}\,,d_{n^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\}=\delta_{nn^{\prime}}\ ,

(11)

while all the other anticommutators vanish, and the complete set
\{\psi_{n}(\vec{r})\}
is chosen in accordance with definitions given above. Then the charge density operator can be equivalently represented as the sum of its normal-ordered form and the VP-density introduced in (
8
)

j_{0}(\vec{r},t)=:j_{0}(\vec{r},t):+\varrho_{VP}(\vec{r})\ ,

(12)

and since by definition the vacuum state
|vac\rangle
is subject of relations

b_{n}\,|vac\rangle=d_{n}\,|vac\rangle=0\ ,

(13)

one obtains

j_{0}(\vec{r},t)\,|vac\rangle=\varrho_{VP}(\vec{r})\,|vac\rangle\ .

(14)

Therefore
\varrho_{VP}(\vec{r})
turns out to be a true c-number component of the charge density. This result should be contrasted with, e.g., the classical EM-wavepacket, which appears as an average of the EM-field operator over the coherent state with indefinite number of photons, created by the classical external EM-source. The latter is indeed an average with non-vanishing dispersions, both in electric and magnetic components of the wavepacket, which are caused by fluctuations of the photon number in each oscillator mode.

For quadratic in fermion fields theories like QED and other gauge field models the same statement holds also for the vacuum energy. In QED it happens because the Schwinger prescription for the current (
9
) dictates the following form of the Dirac Hamiltonian in the external EM-field (for details see, e.g., Ref.
Plunien et al. 1986
)

\hbox{$\cal H$}_{D}(\vec{r},t)=\\
={1\over 4i}\,\Big\{\left[\psi(\vec{r},t)^{\dagger}\,,\vec{\alpha}\,\vec{\nabla}\psi(\vec{r},t)\right]+\left[\psi(\vec{r},t)\,,\vec{\nabla}\psi(\vec{r},t)^{\dagger}\,\vec{\alpha}\right]\Big\}\ +\\
+{1\over 2}\left[\psi(\vec{r},t)^{\dagger}\,,\beta\,\psi(\vec{r},t)\right]+j_{\mu}(\vec{r},t)A^{\mu}(\vec{r},t)\ ,

(15)

whence by the same arguments as for the current one obtains

\hbox{$\cal H$}_{D}(\vec{r},t)\,|vac\rangle=h_{D,VP}(\vec{r})\,|vac\rangle\ ,

(16)

where

h_{D,VP}(\vec{r})=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum\limits_{\epsilon_{n}<\epsilon_{F}}\epsilon_{n}\,\psi_{n}(\vec{r})^{\dagger}\psi_{n}(\vec{r})\ -\right.\\
\left.-\ \sum\limits_{\epsilon_{n}\geqslant\epsilon_{F}}\epsilon_{n}\,\psi_{n}(\vec{r})^{\dagger}\psi_{n}(\vec{r})\right)\ .

(17)

However, the VP-density
h_{D,VP}(\vec{r})
itself turns out to be of no use in our approach, since it doesn’t provide the direct application of the WK method and so requires a more sophisticated techniques to deal with.

Upon integrating eq.(
17
) over the whole space we derive the expression for the VP-energy from the polarized Dirac sea

\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum\limits_{\epsilon_{n}<\epsilon_{F}}\epsilon_{n}-\sum\limits_{\epsilon_{n}\geqslant\epsilon_{F}}\epsilon_{n}\right)\ ,

(18)

which again appears to be the eigenvalue of the total Dirac Hamiltonian acting on the vacuum state.

The origin of the Coulomb term (
2
) lies in the structure of the EM-Hamiltonian

H_{EM}={1\over 8\pi}\,\int\!d{\vec{r}}\,\left[\hbox{ $\vec{\cal{E}}$}^{2}+\hbox{$\vec{\cal{H}}$}^{2}\right]\ ,

(19)

where

\begin{gathered}\hbox{ $\vec{\cal{E}}$}(\vec{r},t)=-\vec{\nabla}A^{0}(\vec{r},t)-{\partial\over\partial t}\vec{A}(\vec{r},t)\,\ ,\\
\hbox{$\vec{\cal{H}}$}(\vec{r},t)=\vec{\nabla}\times\vec{A}(\vec{r},t)\ .\end{gathered}

(20)

Upon splitting the electric field
\hbox{ $\vec{\cal{E}}$}(\vec{r},t)
into the longitudinal and transversal components

\hbox{ $\vec{\cal{E}}$}(\vec{r},t)=\hbox{ $\vec{\cal{E}}$}_{||}(\vec{r},t)+\hbox{ $\vec{\cal{E}}$}_{\perp}(\vec{r},t)\ ,

(21)

where
\hbox{ $\vec{\cal{E}}$}_{||}(\vec{r},t)=-\vec{\nabla}A^{0}(\vec{r},t)
,

Being combined with the relation (

The purpose of this work is to deal indeed with the expression (

IV
VP-energy in the non-perturbative approach

The most consistent way to explore the spontaneous emission is to deal with the total VP-energy
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{VP}
, introduced in eq. (
3
), since there are indeed the changes in
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{VP}
, caused by discrete levels diving, which are responsible for creation of vacuum positrons. Following the general prescription for the Casimir energy calculations
Itzykson and Zuber 1980
;
Plunien et al. 1986
, the natural choice of the reference point for
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{VP}
is the free case
A_{ext}=0
. The Coulomb term
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{C,VP}
is automatically consistent with such reference point. In the case of Dirac sea VP-energy it must be combined with the circumstance that, unlike the purely photonic Casimir effect,
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP}
includes also an infinite set of discrete Coulomb levels. To pick out exclusively the interaction effects, it is therefore necessary to subtract in addition from each discrete level the mass of the free electron at rest.
In fact, such definition of
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP}
follows from the basic properties of QFT (see, e.g., the basic monographs
Bogoli’ubov and Shirkov 1959
;
Bjorken and Drell 1965
), which imply that the in- and out- fields and states in any QFT-problem under question should be subject of a special limiting procedure. The essence of the latter is that in any interacting system the asymptotically free in- and out- fields and states cannot be treated independently of dynamics. Actually, the free case should be defined via pertinent turning-off operation, applied only to the final steps of calculation. In the case under consideration it is the limit
Q\to 0
. The specifics of the present problem is that for any infinitesimally small
Q
the discrete spectrum of Coulomb states survives. So apart of continua, the free field limit in this case includes an infinite set of units, corresponding to discrete levels, concentrated in the infinitesimal vicinity of the condensation point, which must be subtracted in such a way, that preserves the physical content of the problem.

Thus, in the physically motivated form and in agreement with
\varrho_{VP}(\vec{r})
, which is defined so that it automatically vanishes in the free case, the initial expression for the Dirac sea VP-energy should be written as

\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP}=\hbox{${1\over 2}$ }\left(\sum\limits_{\epsilon_{n}<\epsilon_{F}}\epsilon_{n}-\sum\limits_{\epsilon_{n}\geqslant\epsilon_{F}}\epsilon_{n}+\sum\limits_{-1\leqslant\epsilon_{n}<1}\!1\right)_{A}\ -\\
-\ \hbox{${1\over 2}$ }\left(\sum\limits_{\epsilon_{n}\leqslant-1}\epsilon_{n}-\sum\limits_{\epsilon_{n}\geqslant 1}\epsilon_{n}\right)_{0}\ ,

(60)

where the label A denotes the non-vanishing external field
A_{ext}
, while the label 0 corresponds to
A_{ext}=0
. Defined in such a way, the total VP-energy (
3
) vanishes by turning off the external field, while by turning on it contains only the interaction effects. Therefore the expansion of
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{VP}
in (even) powers of the external field starts from
O\left(Q^{2}\right)
.

Now let us extract from (
60
) separately the contributions from the discrete and continuous spectra for each value of angular quantum number
k
, and afterwards use for the difference of integrals over the continuous spectrum
(\int d{\vec{q}}\,\sqrt{q^{2}+1})_{A}-(\int d{\vec{q}}\,\sqrt{q^{2}+1})_{0}
the well-known technique, which represents this difference in the form of an integral of the elastic scattering phase
\delta_{k}(q)

Voronina et al. 2017
;
Rajaraman 1982
;
Sveshnikov 1991
;
Sundberg and Jaffe 2004
;
Grashin and Sveshnikov 2022
.
The final answer for
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP}(Z,R)
, written as a partial series over angular number
k
, reads
Grashin and Sveshnikov 2022

\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP}(Z,R)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP,k}(Z,R)\ ,

(61)

where

\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP,k}(Z,R)=k\,\left(\frac{1}{\pi}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\!\ \frac{q\,dq}{\sqrt{q^{2}+1}}\ \delta_{tot}(k,q)\ +\right.\\
\left.+\ \sum\limits_{\pm}\sum\limits_{-1\leqslant\epsilon_{n,\pm k}<1}\left(1-\epsilon_{n,\pm k}\right)\right)\ .

(62)

In (
62
)
\delta_{tot}(k,q)
is the total phase shift for the given values of the wavenumber
q
and angular number
\pm k
, including the contributions from the scattering states from both continua and both parities for the radial DC problem with the hamiltonian (
30
). In the contribution of the discrete spectrum to
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP,k}(Z,R)
the additional sum
\sum_{\pm}
takes also account of both parities.

Such approach to evaluation of
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP}(Z,R)
turns out to be quite effective, since for the external potentials of the type (
4
,
5
) each partial term in
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP}(Z,R)
turns out to be finite without any special regularization. First,
\delta_{tot}(k,q)
behaves both in IR and UV-limits of the
q
-variable much better, than each of the scattering phase shifts separately. Namely,
\delta_{tot}(k,q)
is finite for
q\to 0
and behaves like
O(1/q^{3})
for
q\to\infty
, hence, the phase integral in (
62
) is always convergent. Moreover,
\delta_{tot}(k,q)
is by construction an even function of the external field. Second, in the contribution of bound states to
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP,k}
the condensation point
\epsilon_{n,\pm k}\to 1
turns out to be regular, because
1-\epsilon_{n,\pm k}\sim O(1/n^{2})
for
n\to\infty
. The latter circumstance permits to avoid intermediate cutoff of the Coulomb asymptotics of the external potential for
r\to\infty
, what significantly simplifies all the subsequent calculations.

In the case of
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP}(Z,R)
the divergence of the theory shows up in divergence of the partial series (
61
). The latter problem can be solved along the lines of Refs.
Davydov et al. 2018b
;
Sveshnikov et al. 2019b
, which deal with the similar expansion for
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP}
in 2+1 D. In the present 3+1 D case due to a lot of technical details this topic is considered separately in Ref.
Grashin and Sveshnikov 2022
. The main result is that, as expected from general grounds discussed above in terms of
\varrho_{VP}(\vec{r})
and in accordance with similar results in 1+1 and 2+1 D cases
Davydov et al. 2017
;
*Sveshnikov2017
;
Davydov et al. 2018a
;
Davydov et al. 2018b
;
Sveshnikov et al. 2019a
;
Sveshnikov et al. 2019b
, the partial series (
61
) for
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP}(Z,R)
diverges in the leading
O(Q^{2})
-order. So it requires regularization and subsequent renormalization, although each partial
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP,k}(Z,R)
is finite. Moreover, the divergence of the partial series (
61
) is formally the same as in 3+1 QED for the fermionic loop with two external lines.

The need in the renormalization via fermionic loop follows also from the analysis of the properties of
\varrho_{VP}
, which shows that without such UV-renormalization the integral VP-charge cannot acquire the expected integer value in units of
(-2|e|)
. In fact, the properties of
\varrho_{VP}
play here the role of a controller, which provides the implementation of the required physical conditions for a correct description of VP-effects beyond the scope of PT, since the latters cannot be tracked via direct evaluation of
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP}
by means of the initial relations (
3
),(
60
).

Thus, in the complete analogy with the renormalization of VP-density (
43
), we should pass to the renormalized Dirac sea VP-energy by means of relations

\hbox{$\cal E$}^{ren}_{D,VP}(Z,R)=\sum\limits_{k=1}\hbox{$\cal E$}^{ren}_{D,VP,k}(Z,R)\ ,

(63)

where

\hbox{$\cal E$}^{ren}_{D,VP,k}(Z,R)=\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP,k}(Z,R)+\zeta_{D,k}(R)Z^{2}\ ,

(64)

while the renormalization coefficients
\zeta_{D,k}(R)
are defined in the following way

\zeta_{D,k}(R)=\lim\limits_{Z_{0}\to 0}\left[{\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP}^{PT}(Z_{0})\,\delta_{k,1}-\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP,k}(Z_{0})\over Z_{0}^{2}}\right]_{R}\ .

(65)

The essence of relations (
63
-
65
) is to remove (for fixed
R
!) the divergent
O(Q^{2})
-components from the non-renormalized partial terms
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP,k}(Z,R)
in the series (
61
) and replace them further by renormalized via fermionic loop perturbative contribution to the Dirac sea VP-energy
\hbox{$\cal E$}^{PT}_{D,VP}\,\delta_{k,1}
. Such procedure provides simultaneously the convergence of the regulated this way partial series (
63
) and the correct limit of
\hbox{$\cal E$}^{ren}_{D,VP}(Z,R)
for
Q\to 0
with fixed
R
.

Another way to achieve the renormalization prescription (
63
-
65
) can be based on the Schwinger relation
Schwinger 1949
;
*Schwinger1951
;
Voronina et al. 2017
;
Plunien et al. 1986
;
Greiner and Reinhardt 2009
for the renormalized VP-quantities

\delta\hbox{$\cal E$}^{ren}_{D,VP}=\int\!\mathrm{d}\vec{r}\ \varrho^{ren}_{VP}(\vec{r})\,\delta A_{0}^{ext}(\vec{r})+\delta\hbox{$\cal E$}_{N}\ ,

(66)

where
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{N}
is responsible only for jumps in the VP-energy, caused by discrete levels crossing through the border of the lower continuum, and so is an essentially non-perturbative quantity, which doesn’t need any renormalization. From (
66
) there follows that the Dirac sea VP-energy and VP-density must be renormalized in a similar way by means of the same subtraction procedure.

The relation (
66
) can be represented also in the partial form

\delta\hbox{$\cal E$}^{ren}_{D,VP,k}=\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\!r^{2}\,dr\ \varrho^{ren}_{VP,k}(r)\,\delta A_{0}^{ext}(r)+\delta\hbox{$\cal E$}_{N,k}\ ,

(67)

from which there follows that the convergence of partial series for VP-density implies the convergence of partial series for VP-energy and vice versa.
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{N,k}
is always finite and, moreover, for any finite
Z
vanishes for
k\geq k_{max}(Z)
, therefore doesn’t influence the convergence of the partial series.

The perturbative term
\hbox{$\cal E$}^{PT}_{D,VP}\,\delta_{k,1}
is obtained from the general first-order Schwinger relation
Schwinger 1949
;
*Schwinger1951

\hbox{$\cal E$}^{PT}_{D,VP}=\frac{1}{2}\int d\vec{r}\,\varrho^{PT}_{VP}(\vec{r})\,A_{0}^{ext}(\vec{r})\ ,

(68)

where
\varrho^{PT}_{VP}(\vec{r})
is defined in (
44
-
47
).
From (
68
) and (
44
,
45
) one finds

\hbox{$\cal E$}^{PT}_{D,VP}=\frac{1}{64\pi^{4}}\,\int\!d\vec{q}\ {\vec{q}}^{2}\,\Pi_{R}(-{\vec{q}}^{2})\,\Big|\int\!d\vec{r}\,\mathrm{e}^{i\vec{q}\vec{r}}\,A_{0}^{ext}(\vec{r})\Big|^{2}\ .

(69)

It should be noted that, since the function
S(x)
, defined in (
46
,
47
), is strictly positive, the perturbative VP-energy is also positive.

In the spherically-symmetric case with
A_{0}^{ext}(\vec{r})=A_{0}(r)
the perturbative VP-term belongs to the
s
-channel, which gives the factor
\delta_{k,1}
, and

\hbox{$\cal E$}^{PT}_{D,VP}=\frac{1}{\pi}\,\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\!dq\ q^{4}\,\Pi_{R}(-q^{2})\ \times\\
\times\ \left(\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\!r^{2}\,dr\,j_{0}(qr)\,A_{0}(r)\right)^{2}\ ,

(70)

whence for the sphere there follows

\hbox{$\cal E$}^{PT}_{D,VP,\,sphere}={Q^{2}\over 2\pi R}\,\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\!{dq\over q}\ S(q/m)\ J_{1/2}^{2}(qR)\ ,

(71)

while for the ball one obtains

\hbox{$\cal E$}^{PT}_{D,VP,\,ball}={9\,Q^{2}\over 2\pi R^{3}}\,\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\!{dq\over q^{3}}\ S(q/m)\ J_{3/2}^{2}(qR)\ .

(72)

For
R
close to
R_{min}(Z)
, under condition

\ln\left(1/2mR\right)\gg 1,

(73)

the integrals (
71
,
72
) can be calculated analytically (see Ref.
Plunien et al. 1986
for details). In particular,

\hbox{$\cal E$}^{PT}_{D,VP,\,sphere}={Q^{2}\over 3\pi R}\,\left[\ln\left({1\over 2mR}\right)-\gamma_{E}+{1\over 6}\right]

(74)

for the sphere and

\hbox{$\cal E$}^{PT}_{D,VP,\,ball}={2\,Q^{2}\over 5\pi R}\,\left[\ln\left({1\over 2mR}\right)-\gamma_{E}+{1\over 5}\right]

(75)

for the ball. In this case the ratio

\hbox{$\cal E$}^{PT}_{D,VP,\,ball}/\hbox{$\cal E$}^{PT}_{D,VP,\,sphere}\simeq 6/5\

(76)

is just the same as for their classical electrostatic self-energies
3Z^{2}\alpha/5R
and
Z^{2}\alpha/2R
.

However, for our purposes the condition (
73
) is too restrictive and so the evaluation of
\hbox{$\cal E$}^{PT}_{D,VP}(R)
is performed numerically. By means of existing nowadays soft- and hardware this task does not pose any problems.

The renormalization of the Coulomb term
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{C,VP}(R)
completely repeats the preceding ones. However, it by no means doesn’t imply the direct replacement
\varrho_{VP}(\vec{r})\to\varrho^{ren}_{VP}(\vec{r})
in the expression (
2
). Rather, it must follow the general lines of subtracting (for fixed
R
!) the
O(Q^{2})
-component from the non-renormalized expression (
2
) and replacing it further by renormalized via fermionic loop perturbative contribution to the Coulomb VP-energy
\hbox{$\cal E$}^{PT}_{C,VP}
. That means

\hbox{$\cal E$}^{ren}_{C,VP}(Z,R)=\hbox{$\cal E$}_{C,VP}(Z,R)+\zeta_{C}(R)Z^{2}\ ,

(77)

while the renormalization coefficients
\zeta_{C}(R)
should be defined now as follows

\zeta_{C}(R)=\lim\limits_{Z_{0}\to 0}\left[{\hbox{$\cal E$}_{C,VP}^{PT}(Z_{0})-\hbox{$\cal E$}_{C,VP}(Z_{0})\over Z_{0}^{2}}\right]_{R}\ ,

(78)

where

\hbox{$\cal E$}_{C,VP}^{PT}=\frac{1}{2}\int\!d{\vec{r}}\,d{\vec{r}^{\prime}}\,{\varrho_{VP}^{PT}(\vec{r})\ \varrho_{VP}^{PT}(\vec{r}^{\prime})\over|\vec{r}-\vec{r}^{\prime}|}\ ,

(79)

with
\varrho_{VP}^{PT}(\vec{r})
being defined in eqs. (
44
-
47
).

Indeed such procedure deals with
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{C,VP}(R)
in a way consistent with renormalization of
\varrho_{VP}(\vec{r})
and
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP}
by means of the same subtraction procedure and provides the correct limit of
\hbox{$\cal E$}^{ren}_{C,VP}(Z,R)
for
Q\to 0
with fixed
R
. A more explicit form of the expression (
77
) can be also written as follows

\hbox{$\cal E$}^{ren}_{C,VP}(Z,R)=\\
\frac{1}{2}\int\!d{\vec{r}}\,d{\vec{r}^{\prime}}\,{\varrho_{VP}^{PT}(\vec{r})\,\varrho_{VP}^{PT}(\vec{r}^{\prime})-\varrho_{VP}^{(1)}(\vec{r})\,\varrho_{VP}^{(1)}(\vec{r}^{\prime})\over|\vec{r}-\vec{r}^{\prime}|}+\\
+\frac{1}{2}\int\!d{\vec{r}}\,d{\vec{r}^{\prime}}\,{\varrho_{VP}(\vec{r})\,\varrho_{VP}(\vec{r}^{\prime})\over|\vec{r}-\vec{r}^{\prime}|}\ .

(80)

The main point here is that in the supercritical region the Born term turns out to be much larger, than the perturbative one, which leads to a very peculiar effect of Coulomb supercriticality: Coulomb interaction between vacuum shells, being charged with the same (negative) sign, is attractive. This peculiar effect will be demonstrated by concrete calculations below.

VI
Spontaneous positron emission: what is wrong and what is possible

Now — having dealt with the general consequences of levels diving in terms of VP-density and VP-energy this way — let us consider the total
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{VP}^{ren}(Z,R)
as a function of
R
. This exercise turns out to be quite informative, since this behavior of
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{VP}(Z,R)
simulates in a quite reasonable way the non-perturbative VP-effects in slow heavy ion collisions, caused by levels diving into the lower continuum, including the problem of spontaneous emission of positrons in the supercritical region
Z>Z_{cr,1}
7
7

7

In this paper we give only a qualitative picture of what is wrong and what is possible concerning spontaneous emission in the supercritical region, based on the behavior of VP-density and VP-energy. More quantitative analysis with concrete calculations of emission rates and corresponding probabilities requires much more details including positron emission spectra, estimates of time intervals for emission, a set of additional plots and drawings, etc., and so will be presented separately.
.

First, let us mention that the general theory
Greiner et al. 1985
;
Plunien et al. 1986
;
Greiner and Reinhardt 2009
;
Rafelski et al. 2017
, based on the framework
Fano 1961
, predicts that after level diving there appears a metastable state with lifetime
\sim 10^{-19}
sec, which decays into the spontaneous positron emission accompanied with vacuum shells formation according to the Fano rule (
59
). An important point here is that due to spherical symmetry of the source, during this process all the angular quantum numbers and parity of the dived level are preserved by the metastable state and further by positrons created. Furthermore, the spontaneous emission of positrons should be caused solely by VP-effects without any other channels of energy transfer. The corresponding positron spectra have been calculated first in Refs.
Reinhardt et al. 1981
;
*Mueller1988
;
Ackad and Horbatsch 2008
, and explored quite recently with more details in Refs.
Popov et al. 2018
;
*Novak2018
;
*Maltsev2018
;
Maltsev et al. 2019
;
*Maltsev2020
. These spectra demonstrate, in particular, that the emission of low-energy positrons should be strongly suppressed by the repulsive interaction with the nuclei, while at high energy the spectra fall off exponentially. Actually, strong suppression of spontaneous emission at low energies is the direct consequence of the well-known result of Coulomb scattering for the probability to find the scattering particles at zero relative distances

|\Psi(0)|^{2}={2\,\pi\varkappa\over v\,|\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\varkappa}-1|}\ ,

(87)

where
\varkappa=e_{1}e_{2}/\hbar v
with
v
being the relative velocity. In the case of repulsion for small
v
the relation (
87
) implies

|\Psi(0)|^{2}\to{2\pi e_{1}e_{2}\over\hbar\,v^{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-2\pi e_{1}e_{2}/\hbar v}\ ,

(88)

and since the spontaneous positrons should be created in the vicinity of the Coulomb center, there follows from (
88
) that one should expect strong suppression of emission at low energies.

VII
Concluding remarks

First, it should be noted that the answer
Z^{\ast}\simeq 250-260
for the start-up of spontaneous emission, given above, is just an estimate from below. Actually,
Z^{\ast}
should be even larger, since this answer corresponds to a strongly underestimated size of the Coulomb source via relation (
81
) in comparison with the realistic heavy ions supercritical configuration. Moreover, there exists at least one correction to
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{VP}^{ren}(Z,R)
, which leads to a positive shift of
Z^{\ast}
. It is the self-energy (SE) contribution due to virtual photon exchange, which in the context of essentially nonlinear VP-effects of super-criticality, caused by fermionic loop, is usually dropped. Near the lower continuum SE shows up also as a perturbative correction and so cannot seriously alter the results, presented above
Roenko and Sveshnikov 2018
. Nevertheless, SE is always positive and so leads to the same consequences for
Z^{\ast}
. So the realistic estimate for
Z^{\ast}
turns out to be even higher than
\simeq 250-260
. In any case, however, such
Z^{\ast}
lies far beyond the interval
170\leqslant Z\leqslant 192
, which is nowadays the main region of theoretical and experimental activity in heavy ion collisions aimed at the study of such VP-effects
Popov et al. 2018
;
Maltsev et al. 2019
;
Gumberidze et al. 2009
;
Ter-Akopian et al. 2015
;
Ma et al. 2017
.
The negative result of early investigations at GSI
Müller-Nehler and Soff 1994
can be at least partially explained by the last circumstance

It should be also mentioned that

Second, in collisions with spontaneous emission the emitted positrons carry away the lepton number equal to
(-1)\times
their total number. Hence, the corresponding amount of positive lepton number should be left to the VP-density, concentrated in vacuum shells. Otherwise, either the lepton number conservation in such a processes must be broken, or the positron emission prohibited. Therefore such collision should proceed via a specific intermediate state, which contains a spatially extended lepton number VP-density. Moreover, it should be a real intermediate state, which appears with the first emitted positron and ceases to exist with the last created valence electron. For slow heavy ion collisions its lifetime can be estimated as
\sim 10^{-19}-10^{-21}
sec, and during this time the lepton number VP-density should exist as a real and, in principle, measurable extended quantity.
So any reliable answer concerning the spontaneous positron emission — either positive or negative — is important for our understanding of the nature of the lepton number, since so far leptons show up as point-like particles with no indications on existence of any kind intrinsic structure (for a more detailed discussion of this topic see Refs.
Krasnov and Sveshnikov
;
*Krasnov2022a
).

Therefore, the reasonable conditions, under which the vacuum positron emission can be unambiguously detected on the nuclear conversion pairs background, should play an exceptional role in slow ions collisions, aimed at the search of such events. However, the estimates for
Z^{\ast}
, achieved above, show that for such conditions two heavy ion collisions are out of work. The gap between the highest achievable nowadays
Z=192
for Cm+Cm colliding beams and the estimate from below
Z^{\ast}\simeq 250-260
is to large for any kind special efforts in searching for the signal of spontaneous emission in such collisions. Note also that with further growth of
Z
the decline of
\hbox{$\cal E$}_{VP}^{ren}(Z,R)
into the negative range with decreasing
R
becomes more and more pronounced, as it is clearly seen in Fig.
14

\hbox{$\cal E$}_{D,VP}^{ren}(Z,R)

One possible way to access QED at a supercritical Coulomb field is to consider more complicated collisions, which are based on the regular polyhedron symmetry — synchronized slow heavy ion beams move from the vertices of the polyhedron towards its center. The simplest collision of such kind contains 4 beams, arranged as the mean lines of the tetrahedron with the intersection angle
\vartheta=2\arcsin(\sqrt{2/3})\simeq 109^{\small 0}\,28^{\prime}
, akin to s-p hybridized tetrahedron carbon bonds. The next and probably the most preferable one is the 6-beam configuration, reproducing 6 semi-axes of the rectangular coordinate frame. The advantage of such many-beam collisions is evident. On the other hand, such collisions require serious additional efforts for their implementation. Therefore, an additional tasty candy is needed to arouse sufficient interest in such a project, and it is really possible, but this issue requires for a special discussion.

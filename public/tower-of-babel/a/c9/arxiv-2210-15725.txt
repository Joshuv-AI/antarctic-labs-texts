The Adiabatic Wigner-Weisskopf Model
Alain Joye, and, Marco Merkli

Abstract
We consider a slowly varying time dependent d − d- level atom interacting with a photon field. Restricted to the single excitation atom-field sector, the model is a time-dependent generalization of the Wigner-Weisskopf model describing spontaneous emission of an atomic excitation into the radiation field. We analyze the dynamics of the atom and of the radiation field in the adiabatic and small coupling approximations, in various regimes. In particular, starting with an excited atomic state, we provide a description of both the radiative decay of the atom and of the buildup of the photon excitation in the field.



1
Introduction

This paper is concerned with the dynamics of an open ‘system-bath’ model. The system is a
d
-level ‘atom’, placed in a ‘bath’, or radiation field, which is modeled by a free Bose field. The system Hamiltonian,
H_{\rm a}(t)
, depends slowly (adiabatically) on time. Generally, rigorously deriving the effective reduced dynamics of an open quantum system is a primary task in qunatum theory. Even in the easier case when
H_{\rm a}
does not depend on time, a treatment is not simple. In the literature, often the ‘bath’ (radiation field) is considered in a state of thermal equilibrium and one asks whether the coupled system-bath complex converges to the joint, interacting equilibrium in the limit of large times – a phenomenon called ‘return to equilibrium’. This effect happens when atomic and field modes are exchanging energy, generating transitions between the system levels leading to the thermal distribution of the original system energies, to lowest order in the system-bath interaction. A different line of inquiry, towards which we aim to contribute here, considers the process of emission of an excitation. In this setting, the interaction between the atom and the field enables transitions between the excited atomic states and its ground state only, not between excited states directly. As a result, the atom is driven, generically, towards its ground state, losing the excitation to the field. It describes the spontaneous emission of an excitation.

The Wigner-Weisskopf model introduced in
[
WW
]
provides a simple description of the exponential in time de-excitation of an atom coupled to a field of photons. It considers a single-excitation process between the levels of the atom and one photon only. The interaction term in the Hamiltonian is a rank two coupling operator, a structure which allows for a mathematically rigorous analysis of the dynamics. Our version of this model includes a possible time dependence (an additional external influence) of the atomic Hamiltonian. We study this generalized Wigner-Weisskopf atom in the adiabatic and small coupling asymptotic regime. Our main results are a detailed expansion of the dynamics of the initially excited atom (the population of each level), as well as the buildup of the excitation in the field in various asymptotic regimes.

From the point of view of adiabatic quantum control, the problem at hand addresses the following situation. Given a smooth time dependent
d
-level Hamiltonian with simple eigenvalues, an initial eigenstate evolves to a state close to the corresponding instantaneous eigenstate obtained by continuity, provided the time variation of the Hamiltonian is slow enough. This simple version of the adiabatic theorem of quantum mechanics
[
BF
,
K1
,
N1
,
ASY
]
allows one to perform quantum engineering on the system provided one has sufficient control on its time dependent Hamiltonian. When the
d
-level system is further subject to interactions with an environment, the adiabatic picture is blurred and a quantification of the effect of the environment becomes of practical interest. This question was addressed in
[
JMS
]
for a two-level system coupled to a Bose field by means of an instantaneous energy conserving interaction, in which the interaction operator commutes with the system two-level Hamiltonian at any moment in time. By contrast, the interaction between the
d
-level atom and the field we consider in this present work is much more generic, as it is not assumed to be energy conserving. Accordingly, the dynamics of the atom in the adiabatic and small coupling regime differs from that found in
[
JMS
]
, and it is closer to that expected for a generic energy-exchanging model. Indeed, our results are in keeping with those established in
[
J2
]
for a similar physical situation addressed within the framework of an effective dynamics generated by a Lindbladian with a time dependent Hamiltonian and a generic dissipator, in the corresponding adiabatic and small amplitude of the dissipator regimes. Correspondingly, the results of
[
JMS
]
are comparable to those obtained for effective dynamics generated by dephasing Linbdbladians, in the sames asymptotic regimes, see
[
AFGG1
,
AFGG2
]
.

Model and main results

We consider an idealized atom having
d
possibly time-dependent, simple excited energy levels
0<\alpha_{1}(t)<\ldots<\alpha_{d}(t)
with normalized eigenstates
\phi_{j}(t)
and a ground state energy
\alpha_{0}=0
with a corresponding time-independent, normalized eigenstate
\phi_{0}
. The atom is in contact with a radiation field, modeled by photons with momenta
k\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
in a state of thermal equilibrium at temperature
T\geq 0

\inf_{t\geq 0}\min_{j,k:\,j\neq k}|\alpha_{j}(t)-\alpha_{k}(t)|=\Delta_{0}>0.

(2.1)

The interaction allows for excitations to be exchanged between the atom and the field, such that the total number of excitations is conserved. The total (possibly time-dependent) Hamiltonian, acting on the Hilbert space
{\mathbb{C}}^{d+1}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}
, where
{\mathcal{F}}
is the Fock space over the single-particle space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3},d^{3}k)
describing the field, is given by

H(t)=H_{\rm a}(t)+H_{\rm f}+\lambda V(t),

(2.2)

where the free atom and field Hamiltonians are

H_{\rm a}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{j}(t)|\phi_{j}(t)\rangle\langle\phi_{j}(t)|,\qquad H_{\rm f}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\omega(k)a^{*}(k)a(k)d^{3}k.

(2.3)

To simplify the notation, we write
H_{\rm a}(t)
and
H_{\rm f}
instead of
H_{\rm a}(t)\otimes\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}
and
\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}\otimes H_{\rm f}
, and
we recall

H_{\rm a}(t)\phi_{0}=0.

The quantity
\lambda
in (
2.2
) is a (real) interaction constant and

V(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}v_{j}(t)|\phi_{j}(t)\rangle\langle\phi_{0}|\otimes a(g)+{\rm h.c.\ }

(2.4)

Here,
v_{j}(t)
are smooth complex valued functions of time and

a(g)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\overline{g(k)}\,a(k)d^{3}k

(2.5)

for a form factor
g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3},d^{3}k)
. The term
|\phi_{j}(t)\rangle\langle\phi_{0}|\otimes a(g)
in the interaction (
2.4
) describes the process of absorption of an excitation from the field accompanied by a transition from the ground state to the
j
th excited state in the atom.

Remark: Positive temperatures.
For concreteness, the field is presented to be described by the Fock space
\mathcal{F}
over the single-particle space
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3},d^{3}k)
. The vacuum state in

is the equilibrium state of the field at zero temperature. However, our analysis carries through without modification to the positive temperature case. Indeed, it suffices to replace the Fock space

]
.

The atomic Hamiltonian

is the field correlation function, and we view

. We use the notation, for any time-dependent vector

Adiabatic scaling.
We consider the time-dependence of the Hamiltonian to be governed by a parameter
\varepsilon>0
, that is, we examine the Schrödinger equation

i\partial_{s}\psi_{\varepsilon}(s)=H(\varepsilon s)\psi_{\varepsilon}(s),\quad\psi_{\varepsilon}(0)=\psi^{(0)},\quad s\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}.

(2.8)

For
\varepsilon
small, the Hamiltonian varies very slowly in time and undergoes a change of order one on a time scale of order

.
Introducing the rescaled time
t=\varepsilon s
, the corresponding transformed wave function
\phi(t,\varepsilon)\equiv\psi_{\varepsilon}(t/\varepsilon)
satisfies

i\partial_{t}\phi(t,\varepsilon)=\frac{i}{\varepsilon}(\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon})(t/\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}H(t)\psi_{\varepsilon}(t/\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}H(t)\phi(t,\varepsilon).

(2.9)

We thus consider the adiabatic Schrödinger equation (rename the wave function
\phi(\cdot,\varepsilon)\rightarrow\psi(\cdot)
)

i\varepsilon\partial_{t}\psi(t)=H(t)\psi(t),\quad\psi(0)=\psi^{(0)},\quad t\in{\mathbb{R}}^{+}.

(2.10)

If
\psi(t)
solves (
2.10
) then
\psi(\varepsilon s)
solves the original (
2.8
). In the equation involving the rescaled time
t
, (
2.10
), the Hamiltonian undergoes a change of order one on a time scale of order one. We will analyze the solutions of (
2.10
).

Note that for a time-independent Hamiltonian
H(t)=H
for all
t
, the solutions of (
2.8
) and (
2.10
) read

\displaystyle\psi_{\varepsilon}(s)=e^{-isH}\psi^{(0)},\quad\psi(t)=e^{-\frac{i}{{\varepsilon}}tH}\psi^{(0)}.

(2.11)

Therefore, in this autonomous setup, we can recover the long time behaviour of the system described by (
2.10
) by setting
t=1
and considering
{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 0
, or setting
{\varepsilon}=1
and allowing
t\rightarrow\infty
.

In what follows, we will always consider
0<{\varepsilon}\leq 1
.

Single excitation manifold.
The Schrödinger evolution (
2.10
) leaves invariant the space of

atom-field wave functions

{\mathcal{P}}_{1}=\Big\{\psi\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}\otimes{\mathcal{F}}\,:\,\psi=\sum_{j=1}^{d}z_{j}\,\phi_{j}(0)\otimes\Omega_{\rm f}+\phi_{0}\otimes a^{*}(f)\Omega_{\rm f},\ z_{j}\in{\mathbb{C}},f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3},d^{3}k)\Big\},

(2.12)

where

is the vacuum state in
{\mathcal{F}}
. Then the solution of (
2.10
) within the subspace

has the form

\psi(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}z_{j}(t)\,\phi_{j}(0)\otimes\Omega_{\rm f}+\phi_{0}\otimes a^{*}(f_{t})\Omega_{\rm f}

and since

is self-adjoint, the norm of

is conserved,

\|\psi(t)\|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}|z_{j}(t)|^{2}+\|f_{t}\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3},d^{3}k)}=1,\qquad t\geq 0.

(2.14)

We collect the components
z_{j}(t)
into a vector, written in the eigenbasis
\{\phi_{j}(0)\}_{j=1}^{d}
of
H_{\rm a}(0)
, as

z(t)=\begin{pmatrix}z_{1}(t)\\
\vdots\\
z_{d}(t)\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}z_{j}(t)\phi_{j}(0).

(2.15)

At time
t=0
, the probability of finding the system in its excited state

is simply
p_{j}(0)=|z_{j}(0)|^{2}
.
The probability of finding the system in the
j
th instantaneous excited state

at time

p_{j}(t)=\langle\psi(t),\big(|\phi_{j}(t)\rangle\langle\phi_{j}(t)|\otimes\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}_{\rm f}\big)\psi(t)\rangle=\Big|\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}z_{\ell}(t)\langle\phi_{j}(t),\phi_{\ell}(0)\rangle\Big|^{2}=\big|\langle\phi_{j}(t),z(t)\rangle\big|^{2}.

(2.16)

The sum
\sum_{j=1}^{n}p_{j}(t)
is the probability to find the excitation in the atom (that is, in any of the instantaneous excited states) at time
t
, and one gets from (
2.16
), and since

is an orthonormal basis,

\sum_{j=1}^{d}p_{j}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}|z_{j}(t)|^{2}=\|z(t)\|^{2}.

(2.17)

We denote both inner products of
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
and of
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3},d^{3}k)
by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, the arguments making it clear which space is meant.

2.2
Discussion of main results

Generally, our results show the details of the emission of the excitation from the atom to the field, meaning that
|z(t)|\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
. In the time-independent case (
H(t)=H
), Corollary
2.3
shows that the population
p_{j}(t)=|z_{j}(t)|^{2}
of the excited level
j
satisfies

p_{j}(t)=e^{-2\lambda^{2}\beta_{j}t}p_{j}(0)+O(\lambda^{2}),

uniformly in
t\geq 0
, and where
\beta_{j}
is defined in (
2.22
). When
H_{\rm a}(t)
depends on time and varies slowly with adiabatic parameter
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
, the decay rates
\beta_{j}(t)
become time-dependent (see (
2.18
)) and the population of the atomic excitations follow the estimate (
2.21
) of Corollary
2.2
,

p_{j}(t)=e^{-2\frac{\lambda^{2}}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{j}(u)du}\,p_{j}(0)\,+O({\varepsilon}+\lambda^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{4}}{{\varepsilon}}).

In neither case will initially unpopulated atomic excited levels become populated at later times, beyond an amount bounded by the error terms.

2.2.1
Discussion of Theorem
2.1

For the error term in (
2.20
) to be small both the coupling constant
\lambda
and the adiabatic parameter
{\varepsilon}

as well as the ratio

\lambda^{4}/{\varepsilon}
have to be small. We identify
three regimes
:

1)

Strong coupling relative to adiabatic speed:
{\varepsilon}\ll\lambda^{2}\ll\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\ll 1
.
In this case, the adiabatic time scale of the Hamiltonian is large with respect to the relaxation time of the system induced by field (
\propto\lambda^{-2}
) and the coupled system has time enough for the field to absorb the initial excitation on the atom. Namely, for
0\leq t\leq 1
,

\|z(t)\|\leq e^{-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{{\varepsilon}}\min_{1\leq j\leq d}\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{j}(u)du}+O(\lambda^{4}/{\varepsilon}).

At the ‘end of the process’, when
t=1
(which corresponds to the physical time
s=1/{\varepsilon}
which is very large), the excitation survival amplitude is bounded above by
e^{-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{{\varepsilon}}\tau}+O(\lambda^{4}/{\varepsilon})
, which is very small if the coupling is effective, meaning that
\tau=\sqrt{\pi/2}\min_{1\leq j\leq d}\int_{0}^{1}|v_{j}(u)|^{2}\hat{\gamma}(\alpha_{j}(u))du>0
.

2)

Comparable coupling strength and adiabatic speed:
\lambda^{2}={\varepsilon}\ll 1
.
The estimate (
2.20
) gives

z(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{j}(u)du}e^{-i\int_{0}^{t}[\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\alpha_{j}(u)+\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}(u)]du}e^{i\xi_{j}(t)}\,z_{j}(0)\,\phi_{j}(t)+O({\varepsilon}).

In this regime, in which the coupling constant multiplied by the time scale is fixed, the effect of the field is to decrease the initial amplitude of the excited state of the atom by an explicit finite correction along each level of the atom. This is the analog of the weak coupling regime which Davies considered for the stationary case (
H
independent of time),
[
D1
]
.

3)

Weak coupling relative to adiabatic speed:
\lambda^{2}\ll{\varepsilon}\ll 1
.
We distinguish two subregimes. For
{\varepsilon}^{2}\ll\lambda^{2}\ll{\varepsilon}\ll 1
an expansion of the exponentials in (
2.20
) gives

z(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}e^{-\frac{i}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\alpha_{j}(u)du}e^{i\xi_{j}(t)}z_{j}(0)\phi_{j}(t)\\
\times\Big(1-i\frac{\lambda^{2}}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}(u)du-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{j}(u)du\Big)+O\big({\varepsilon}+\frac{\lambda^{4}}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}\big),

where the integral terms are significant relative to the remainder (as
{\varepsilon}^{2}\ll\lambda^{2}
). In this regime, we find an explicit decrease of the initial amplitude of the excitation of the atom of the order
\lambda^{2}/{\varepsilon}
. When this ratio tends to zero the initial excitation is not transferred to the field, the atom stays excited,
\|z(t)\|\sim 1
.

In the second regime,
\lambda^{2}\leq{\varepsilon}^{2}\ll{\varepsilon}\ll 1
, the expansion of the exponentials in (
2.20
) gives

z(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}e^{-\frac{i}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\alpha_{j}(u)du}e^{i\xi_{j}(t)}\,z_{j}(0)\phi_{j}(t)+O({\varepsilon}).

Again,
\|z(t)\|\sim 1
and the atom does not get de-excited. In both regimes, the interaction with the reservoir is too weak to significantly alter the dynamics of the atom alone, which evolves adiabatically with its own Hamiltonian.

Recall that we consider initially excited atoms, meaning that
\|z(0)\|^{2}=1
. The probability for the atom to be in the ground state
\phi_{0}
at time
t
is given by
1-\|z(t)\|^{2}
, see (
2.16
), (
2.17
). We thus define the de-excitation probability at time

as

p_{\downarrow}(t)=1-\|z(t)\|^{2}.

(2.27)

Our findings in the different regimes 1)-3) discussed above then imply the following.

1’)

For
{\varepsilon}\ll\lambda^{2}\ll\sqrt{{\varepsilon}}\ll 1
we have virtually full de-excitation:
p_{\downarrow}(t)\geq 1-C\frac{\lambda^{8}}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}\sim 1
.

2’)

For
\lambda^{2}={\varepsilon}\ll 1
we have partial de-excitation:
p_{\downarrow}(t)=1-\sum_{j=1}^{d}e^{-2\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{j}(u)du}|z_{j}(0)|^{2}+O({\varepsilon})
.

3’)

For
\lambda^{2}\ll{\varepsilon}\ll 1
we have virtually no de-excitation:

If
{\varepsilon}^{2}\ll\lambda^{2}\ll{\varepsilon}\ll 1
then
p_{\downarrow}(t)=2\frac{\lambda^{2}}{{\varepsilon}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|z_{j}(0)|^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\beta_{j}(u)du+O({\varepsilon}+\frac{\lambda^{4}}{{\varepsilon}^{2}})\ll 1
.

If
\lambda^{2}\leq{\varepsilon}^{2}\ll{\varepsilon}\ll 1
then
p_{\downarrow}(t)=O({\varepsilon})\ll 1
.

In
[
JMS
]
the authors studied the adiabatic transition (scale
{\varepsilon}
) probability of a time dependent two-level system interacting with a Bose field by means of an instantaneous energy conserving interaction, with coupling constant
\lambda
.
This kind of coupling induces transitions between the two levels that are likely to be small, since in a time independent situation it conserves the populations exactly. By contrast, the interaction we consider in the present work allows for instantaneous energy exchange and is more likely to induce transitions between excited and ground states.

The transition probability from the excited to the ground state for the energy conserving model, found in
[
JMS
]
, is given by

p_{\downarrow}^{\rm ec}(t)=\left\{\begin{matrix}[l]\lambda^{2}{\varepsilon}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}K(s)\widehat{\gamma}(\Delta(s))ds+o(\lambda^{2}{\varepsilon})&\mbox{if }\ {\varepsilon}\ll\lambda^{2}\ll\sqrt{\varepsilon}\ll 1\\
&\\
{\varepsilon}^{2}\displaystyle\Big(\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}K(s)\widehat{\gamma}(\Delta(s))ds+Q(t)\Big)+o({\varepsilon}^{2})\quad&\mbox{if }\ \lambda^{2}={\varepsilon}\ll 1\\
&\\
{\varepsilon}^{2}Q(t)+o({\varepsilon}^{2})&\mbox{if }\ \lambda^{2}\ll{\varepsilon}\ll 1.\end{matrix}\right.

(2.28)

Here
K
and
Q
are explicit non negative functions constructed from the Hamiltonian,
\Delta(s)>0
is the energy gap of the two-level system,
\widehat{\gamma}
is the Fourier transform of the correlation function of the field. Comparing (
2.28
) with
p_{\downarrow}(t)
we observe the following:

(a)
p_{\downarrow}^{\rm ec}(t)\ll p_{\downarrow}(t)
in all regimes, and

(b)
p_{\downarrow}^{\rm ec}(t)
vanishes in all regimes (as
{\varepsilon},\lambda\rightarrow 0
), while
p_{\downarrow}(t)
is of order one unless
\lambda^{2}\ll{\varepsilon}\ll 1
.

The behaviour of
p_{\downarrow}(t)
detailed in 1’) – 3’) above is similar to that of the adiabatic transition probabilities between the levels of a time dependent gapped Hamiltonian evolving according to a Lindblad equation modeling a weak reservoir interaction, with a dissipator of order
g>0
,
[
J2
]
. In this setup, the adiabatic transition probability

between the excited state of a two-level system at time zero to its ground state at time

[

]
to be

p_{\downarrow}^{\rm L}(t)=\left\{\begin{matrix}[l]R_{1}(t)+O(g^{2}/{\varepsilon}+{\varepsilon}/g)&\mbox{if }\ {\varepsilon}\ll g\ll\sqrt{\varepsilon}\ll 1\\
\ \\
R_{2}(t)+O({\varepsilon})&\mbox{if }\ g={\varepsilon}\ll 1\\
\ \\
\displaystyle\frac{g}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}J(s)ds+O(g+{\varepsilon}^{2}+g^{2}/{\varepsilon}^{2})&\mbox{if }\ g\ll{\varepsilon}\ll 1.\end{matrix}\right.

Here, the non negative functions

and

and the adiabatic parameter

. The actual values of the time dependent coefficients depend on the details of the models.

2.3
Links to previous literature

The dynamics of the Wigner-Weisskopf model in the time
in
dependent setup (time independent
H_{\rm a}
) was investigated in details by means of spectral methods in
[
D1
]
; see also
[
JKP
]
for a more recent account. In the time
de
pendent framework (time dependent
H_{a}(t)
) we consider in the current work, we use a different approach, similar in spirit to that used for deriving master equations, along the lines of
[
D2
,
DS
]
. For detailed information on master equations and the weak coupling limit, the reader may consult
[
Tr
,
M1
,
M2
,
M3
,
AL
,
DF2
]
and their references. The authors of
[
DS
]
address the adiabatic dynamics of a time dependent
d
-level system weakly coupled to a Fermi field, corresponding to the regime
\lambda^{2}={\varepsilon}<\!\!<1
. They derive the asymptotic system state, for a somewhat different class of interactions than we consider here, and they do not analyze the details of the state (such as the evolution of the populations – which is one of our goals). Related models, in which the bath effect is incorporated into the system Hamiltonian as an effective time dependent term (so-called ‘classical noise’) is commonly used to describe noise assisted quantum excitation transfer processes, see for instance
[
Ne
]
and references therein.
Very recently, time dependent variants of the Wigner-Weisskopf model have been used to investigate properties of non-autonomous Lindblad dynamics, with particular focus on their markovian properties
[
CHL
,
CL
]
. Variants of the Wigner-Weisskopf model are used to describe, more abstractly, the coupling of a small quantum system coupled to a large environment characterized by continuous spectrum, see
e.g.

[
Ma
,
DF1
,
AJPP
,
DK
]
and references therein.

There are adiabatic approximation results for instantaneous generators with eigenvalues embedded in continuous spectrum
[
AE
,
Te
]
. However, in our situation, the instantaneous Hamiltonian (
2.2
) restricted to the single excitation sector, has purely absolutely continuous spectrum over the whole time span considered, for small enough non zero coupling strength
[
D1
,
JKP
]
. The above mentioned results and tools are thus not amenable for us. In particular, our model and results differ from those of
[
CJKN
]
, where the authors consider a Wigner-Weisskopf type model with a single (
d=1
) uncoupled excited energy level
\alpha(t)
, varying in such a way that the eigenvalue of the coupled model moves in and out of the absolutely continuous spectrum. They analyze the adiabatic limit of
p_{\downarrow}(t)
in the situations where the instantaneous eigenvalue remains embedded in the spectrum, or where it becomes a resonance. Related results on adiabatic pair creation processes in the Dirac equation are studied in
[
N2
,
PD
]
.

In our master equation approach, on the other hand, we have an effective time dependent generator with simple eigenvalues which. However, the generator is not self-adjoint and also depends on the adiabatic parameter
{\varepsilon}
in a singular way. Non self-adjoint generators are known to be amenable to adiabatic techniques,
[
NR
,
J1
,
AFGG1
,
Sc
]
, but in the present situation, extra care and detail in the analysis is required to control the propagators, due to the singular
{\varepsilon}
-dependence.

3.1
Dynamics of the atom and proof of Theorem
2.1

Let
\{\phi_{j}(0)\}_{j=1}^{d}
be the fixed, time-independent basis of the excited atomic space
{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
consisting of normalized excited eigenstates of
H_{\rm a}(0)
. With respect to this basis, each instantaneous eigenvector

is given by a
d

\phi_{j}(t)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\langle\phi_{\ell}(0),\phi_{j}(t)\rangle\,\phi_{\ell}(0).

(3.1)

The atomic Hamiltonian
H_{\rm a}(t)
and the interaction
V(t)
then take the form

\displaystyle H_{\rm a}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m,n=1}^{d}[H_{\rm a}(t)]_{m,n}|\phi_{m}(0)\rangle\langle\phi_{n}(0)|,\quad[H_{\rm a}(t)]_{m,n}=\langle\phi_{m}(0),H_{\rm a}(t)\,\phi_{n}(0)\rangle,

(3.2)

\displaystyle V(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{d}w_{j}(t)|\phi_{j}(0)\rangle\langle\phi_{0}|\otimes a(g)+{\rm h.c.},\quad w_{j}(t)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}v_{\ell}(t)\langle\phi_{j}(0),\phi_{\ell}(t)\rangle.

(3.3)

Note that

in (
2.4
) is obtained from

by the unitary map (
3.3
).

The parameters
z_{j}(t)\in{\mathbb{C}}
and
f_{t}:k\mapsto f_{t}(k)\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3},d^{3}k)
of (
2.13
) satisfy the following closed system of coupled equations,

\displaystyle i\varepsilon\partial_{t}z_{j}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}[H_{\rm a}(t)]_{j\ell}\,z_{\ell}(t)+\lambda w_{j}(t)\langle g,f_{t}\rangle

(3.4)

\displaystyle i\varepsilon\partial_{t}f_{t}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(k)f_{t}(k)+\lambda\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\overline{w_{\ell}(t)}z_{\ell}(t)\,g(k).

(3.5)

We write (
3.4
), (
3.5
) in vector form,

\displaystyle i\varepsilon\partial_{t}z(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A(t)z(t)+\lambda w(t)\langle g,f_{t}\rangle,

(3.6)

\displaystyle i\varepsilon\partial_{t}f_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega f_{t}+\lambda\langle w(t),z(t)\rangle\,g,

(3.7)

where

is the hermitian

matrix representing the restriction of

to the subspace spanned by

\vdots\\
z_{d}(t)\end{pmatrix},\quad w(t)=\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}(t)\\
\vdots\\

(3.8)

Let

be the free atomic propagator, solving the evolution equation

i\varepsilon\partial_{t}U_{\varepsilon}(t,s)=A(t)U_{\varepsilon}(t,s),\quad U_{\varepsilon}(s,s)=\mathchoice{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.0mul}{\rm 1\mskip-4.5mul}{\rm 1\mskip-5.0mul}.

(3.9)

As

is hermitian,

is unitary,

For a time-dependent vector

Proposition 3.1
(Expansion of excited state amplitudes)

Suppose the initial condition
\psi\in\mathcal{P}_{1}
is such that
f_{t=0}=0
, so the excitation is initially in the atom. Then

i\varepsilon\partial_{t}z(t)=\big[A(t)-i\lambda^{2}w(t)\langle Q_{\varepsilon}(t)w(t)|\big]z(t)+R_{0}(t,\lambda,\varepsilon),

(3.10)

where

(3.11)

with a remainder satisfying

\|R_{0}(t,\lambda,\varepsilon)\|\leq\lambda^{4}\ \|v\|_{\infty}^{4}\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}\,\|t\gamma(t)\|_{L^{1}}+\lambda^{2}\varepsilon\ \|w\|_{\infty}\ \|\partial_{t}w\|_{\infty}\ \|t\gamma(t)\|_{L^{1}}.

(3.12)

In the time-independent case

and a simple change of variables in (

Then we have the following result.

Proposition 3.2

We have

\Big\|Q_{\varepsilon}(t)-\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(t)^{*}\Big\|\leq{\varepsilon}C_{1}+{\varepsilon}^{2}C_{2}

where

\displaystyle=

V_{\varepsilon}(t,s)=W(t,s)\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,s),\qquad\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,s)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}P_{j}(s)e^{-\frac{i}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{j}(u)du}.

(4.22)

Both
W(t,s)
and
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(t,s)
are unitary. The point of this factorization of
V_{\varepsilon}
is to separate out the quickly varying (
{\varepsilon}
small) phase term
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
. The adiabatic evolution
V_{\varepsilon}(t,s)
approximates
U_{\varepsilon}(t,s)
,

\big\|V_{\varepsilon}(t,s)-U_{\varepsilon}(t,s)\big\|\leq{\varepsilon}\big[C_{1}^{\prime}+(t-s)C_{2}^{\prime}\big],

(4.23)

where

\displaystyle C_{1}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\frac{d^{2}}{\Delta_{0}}\max_{j}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle C_{2}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c\frac{d^{2}}{\Delta_{0}}\Big[(1+d\|A\|_{\infty})\max_{j}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}\|^{2}_{\infty}+\max_{j}\|\partial_{t}^{2}P_{j}\|_{\infty}+\frac{\max_{j}|\partial_{t}\alpha_{j}|_{\infty}}{\Delta_{0}}\max_{j}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}\|_{\infty}\Big],

and where
c
is a numerical constant. A proof (
4.23
) is rather standard. We present the details in Section
4.4
, (
4.61
)-(
4.80
), in the more complicate setting where
V_{\varepsilon},U_{\varepsilon}
((
3.9
), (
4.19
)) are replaced by
V_{{\varepsilon},\lambda},U_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}
((
3.32
), (
3.28
)).

Approximating
U_{\varepsilon}(t,s)
in (
3.11
) by
V_{\varepsilon}(t,s)
we obtain from (
4.22
), (
4.23
)

\Big\|Q_{\varepsilon}(t)-\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\overline{\gamma}\big(\frac{t-s}{{\varepsilon}}\big)V_{\varepsilon}(t,s)ds\Big\|\\
\leq{\varepsilon}C^{\prime}_{1}\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\Big|\gamma\big(\frac{t-s}{{\varepsilon}}\big)\Big|ds+{\varepsilon}C^{\prime}_{2}\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\Big|\gamma\big(\frac{t-s}{{\varepsilon}}\big)\Big|(t-s)ds\\
\leq{\varepsilon}C^{\prime}_{1}\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}+{\varepsilon}^{2}C^{\prime}_{2}\|t\gamma\|_{L^{1}}.

(4.24)

Next we use (
4.22
),

V_{\varepsilon}(t,s)=\sum_{j}W(t,s)P_{j}(s)e^{-\frac{i}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{j}(u)du}=\sum_{j}P_{j}(t)W(t,s)e^{-\frac{i}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{j}(u)du}\\
=\sum_{j}P_{j}(t)e^{-\frac{i}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{j}(u)du}+\sum_{j}P_{j}(t)\big(\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{u}W(u,s)du\big)e^{-\frac{i}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{j}(u)du}.

(4.25)

Now
\|\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{u}W(u,s)du\|\leq(t-s)\|K\|_{\infty}\leq d(t-s)\max_{j}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}\|_{\infty}
. Then,

\Big\|\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\overline{\gamma}\big(\frac{t-s}{{\varepsilon}}\big)V_{\varepsilon}(t,s)ds-\sum_{j=1}^{d}P_{j}(t)\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\overline{\gamma}\big(\frac{t-s}{{\varepsilon}}\big)e^{-\frac{i}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{j}(u)du}ds\Big\|\\
\leq d^{2}\max_{j}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}|\overline{\gamma}\big(\frac{t-s}{{\varepsilon}}\big)|(t-s)ds\leq{\varepsilon}d^{2}\max_{j}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}\|_{\infty}\|t\gamma\|_{L^{1}}.

(4.26)

Combining (
4.24
) and (
4.26
) yields

\Big\|Q_{\varepsilon}(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{d}P_{j}(t)\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\overline{\gamma}\big(\frac{t-s}{{\varepsilon}}\big)e^{-\frac{i}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{j}(u)du}ds\Big\|\\
\leq{\varepsilon}\big(C_{1}^{\prime}\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}+d^{2}\max_{j}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}\|_{\infty}\|t\gamma\|_{L^{1}}\big)+{\varepsilon}^{2}C_{2}^{\prime}\|t\gamma\|_{L^{1}}.

(4.27)

Next, we have

\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\overline{\gamma}\big(\frac{t-s}{{\varepsilon}}\big)e^{-\frac{i}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{j}(u)du}ds=\int_{0}^{t/{\varepsilon}}\overline{\gamma}(x)e^{-\frac{i}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{t-{\varepsilon}x}^{t}\alpha_{j}(u)du}dx

(4.28)

and since
|e^{ia}-e^{ib}|=|\int_{a}^{b}e^{iy}dy|\leq|b-a|
,

\displaystyle\big|e^{-\frac{i}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{t-{\varepsilon}x}^{t}\alpha_{j}(u)du}-e^{-ix\alpha_{j}(t)}\big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{t-{\varepsilon}x}^{t}|\alpha_{j}(u)-\alpha_{j}(t)|du

(4.29)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}\alpha_{j}\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{t-{\varepsilon}x}^{t}(t-u)du=\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}x^{2}\|\partial_{t}\alpha_{j}\|_{\infty}.

Combining this with (
4.28
) and (
4.27
) gives

\Big|\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{0}^{t}\overline{\gamma}\big(\frac{t-s}{{\varepsilon}}\big)e^{-\frac{i}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{s}^{t}\alpha_{j}(u)du}ds-\int_{0}^{t/{\varepsilon}}\overline{\gamma}(x)e^{-ix\alpha_{j}(t)}dx\Big|\leq\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}\|\partial_{t}\alpha_{j}\|_{\infty}\,\|t^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{1}}

(4.30)

and finally

\Big\|Q_{\varepsilon}(t)-\sum_{j=1}^{d}P_{j}(t)\int_{0}^{t/{\varepsilon}}e^{-ix\alpha_{j}(t)}\gamma(x)dx\Big\|\leq{\varepsilon}\big(C^{\prime}_{1}\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}+d^{2}\max_{j}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}\|_{\infty}\|t\gamma\|_{L^{1}}\big)\\
+{\varepsilon}^{2}C_{2}^{\prime}\|t\gamma\|_{L^{1}}+\frac{d}{2}{\varepsilon}\|\partial_{t}\alpha_{j}\|_{\infty}\,\|t^{2}\gamma\|_{L^{1}}.\quad

(4.31)

This is the result (
3.14
), showing Proposition
3.2
.

4.59
) shows that
W_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t,s)\Psi_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t,s)
satisfies the same differential equation and the same initial condition as
V_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t,s)
, (
3.32
), so the two are equal. This shows (
3.33
).

To prove (
3.36
) we proceed in a standard fashion following
[
ASY
]
, see also
[
J1
,
AFGG1
,
J2
]
. For the remainder of this proof, we omit from the notation the dependence of operators on
{{\varepsilon},\lambda}
. That is, we simply write
K(t)
,
P_{j}(t)
,
{\mathcal{G}}(t)
,
V(t,s)
,
U(t,s)
instead of
K_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t)
,
P_{j}(t,{{\varepsilon},\lambda})
,
{\mathcal{G}}_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t)
,
V_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t,s)
,
U_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t,s)
(the latter quantities having been defined in (
3.29
), Lemma
2.6
(b), (
3.26
), (
3.32
), (
3.28
) respectively).

We have

K(t)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}[\partial_{t}P_{j}(t),P_{j}(t)]

(4.61)

and for any operator
X
,

[X,P_{j}(t)]=[{\mathcal{G}}(t),{\mathcal{R}}_{j}(t,X)],

(4.62)

where

{\mathcal{R}}_{j}(t,X)\equiv\sum_{\ell=1,\ldots,d,\,\ell\neq j}\frac{P_{j}(t)XP_{\ell}(t)+P_{\ell}(t)XP_{j}(t)}{\widetilde{\alpha}_{\ell}(t)-\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}(t)}.

(4.63)

In (
4.63
),
\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}(t)=\alpha_{j}(t)+\lambda^{2}\alpha_{j}^{\prime}(t,{\varepsilon})
are the eigenvalues of
{\mathcal{G}}(t)
, see (
3.26
). Integrating the relation

\partial_{r}\big[V(t,r)U(r,s)\big]=-V(t,r)K(r)U(r,s)

over the domain
r
ranging from
s
to
t
, and using (
4.61
)-(
4.63
), we obtain

\displaystyle V(t,s)-U(t,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}V(t,r)K(r)U(r,s)dr

(4.64)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\int_{s}^{t}V(t,r)\big[{\mathcal{G}}(r),{\mathcal{R}}_{j}\big(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\big)\big]U(r,s)dr.

Now we use
V(t,r){\mathcal{G}}(r)=-i{\varepsilon}\partial_{r}V(t,r)-i{\varepsilon}V(t,r)K(r)
and
{\mathcal{G}}(r)U(r,s)=i{\varepsilon}\partial_{r}U(r,s)
to write

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}V(t,r)\big[{\mathcal{G}}(r),{\mathcal{R}}_{j}\big(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\big)\big]U(r,s)dr

(4.65)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i{\varepsilon}\int_{s}^{t}\Big[\{\partial_{r}V(t,r)+V(t,r)K(r)\}{\mathcal{R}}_{j}\big(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\big)U(r,s)

\displaystyle\qquad\ +V(t,r){\mathcal{R}}_{j}\big(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\big)\partial_{r}U(r,s)\Big]dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i{\varepsilon}\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{r}\big[V(t,r){\mathcal{R}}_{j}\big(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\big)U(r,s)\big]dr

\displaystyle-i{\varepsilon}\int_{s}^{t}V(t,r)\big[K(r){\mathcal{R}}_{j}\big(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\big)-\partial_{r}\{{\mathcal{R}}_{j}\big(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\big)\}\big]U(r,s)dr.\quad

We prove (
3.34
) below, which yields for all
0\leq s\leq t

\big(\|U(t,s)\|+\|V(t,s)\|\big)\leq C_{0}e^{\tilde{C}_{0}(t-s)}

(4.66)

for constants
C_{0},\tilde{C}_{0}
independent of
{\varepsilon}
and
\lambda
.
Next, we observe that the identity
\partial_{t}P_{j}(t)=(\partial_{t}P_{j}(t))P_{j}(t)+P_{j}(t)\partial_{t}P_{j}(t)
allows us to simplify (
4.63
) with
X=\partial_{t}P_{j}(r))
to

\displaystyle{\mathcal{R}}_{j}(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r))=\sum_{\ell=1,\ldots,d,\,\ell\neq j}\frac{\partial_{t}P_{j}(t)P_{\ell}(t)+P_{\ell}(t)\partial_{t}P_{j}(t)}{\widetilde{\alpha}_{\ell}(t)-\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}(t)},

(4.67)

so that we estimate

\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r))\|\leq\frac{2d\,\|\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\|\max_{1\leq j\leq d}\|P_{j}\|_{\infty}}{\min_{j,k\,:\,j\neq k}\,\min_{0\leq t\leq 1}|\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}(t)-\widetilde{\alpha}_{k}(t)|}\leq\frac{8d}{\Delta_{0}}\,\|\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\|.

(4.68)

To arrive at the second estimate in (
4.68
), we
use
\|P_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq 2
(see Lemma
2.6
(c)) and we estimate the denominator in terms of
\Delta_{0}
, (
2.1
), see also (
3.20
):

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}(t)-\widetilde{\alpha}_{k}(t)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\alpha_{j}(t)-\alpha_{k}(t)+\lambda^{2}(\alpha^{\prime}_{j}(t,{\varepsilon})-\alpha^{\prime}_{k}(t,{\varepsilon}))\big|

(4.69)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\big|\alpha_{j}(t)-\alpha_{k}(t)\big|-\lambda^{2}|(\alpha^{\prime}_{j}(t,{\varepsilon})-\alpha^{\prime}_{k}(t,{\varepsilon}))|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Delta_{0}-2\lambda^{2}\|v\|_{\infty}^{2}\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\Delta_{0}/2,

where we have used the condition (
2.6
) in the last step. We now estimate the first term on the right side of (
4.65
) by

\displaystyle{\varepsilon}\,\big\|\int_{s}^{t}\partial_{r}\big[V(r,t){\mathcal{R}}_{j}\big(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\big)U(r,s)\big]dr\big\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\varepsilon}\|V(r,t){\mathcal{R}}_{j}\big(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\big)U(r,s)\|\,\big|_{r=s}^{r=t}

(4.70)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\varepsilon}\frac{16C_{0}d}{\Delta_{0}}\max_{r=s,t}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\|e^{\tilde{C}_{0}(t-s)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C({\varepsilon}+\lambda^{2})e^{\tilde{C}_{0}(t-s)},

for a constant
C
independent of
{{\varepsilon},\lambda}
and
0\leq s\leq t\leq 1
. In the last step, we have made use of the estimate (
3.22
) on the derivative of the projection.

Next we estimate the second term on the right side of (
4.65
). We start with

\displaystyle{\varepsilon}\,\big\|\int_{s}^{t}V(t,r)K(r){\mathcal{R}}_{j}\big(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\big)U(r,s)dr\big\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\varepsilon}C_{0}^{2}e^{\tilde{C}_{0}(t-s)}\int_{s}^{t}\|K(r)\|\,\|{\mathcal{R}}_{j}(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r))\|dr

(4.71)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\varepsilon}\frac{8d^{2}C_{0}^{2}}{\Delta_{0}}e^{\tilde{C}_{0}(t-s)}\max_{j}\int_{s}^{t}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\|^{2}dr,

where we have taken into account (
4.68
) and that
\|K(r)\|\leq 2d\max_{j}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\|
(see (
3.29
) and use
\|P_{j}\|\leq 2
). Next, according to (
3.22
),
\|\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\|^{2}\leq C\big(1+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{{\varepsilon}}|\gamma(r/{\varepsilon})|+\frac{\lambda^{4}}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}|\gamma(r/{\varepsilon})|^{2}\big)
, so

\int_{s}^{t}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\|^{2}dr\leq C\Big(t-s+\lambda^{2}\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}+\frac{\lambda^{4}}{{\varepsilon}}\|\gamma^{2}\|_{L^{1}}\Big).

Combining this bound with (
4.71
) yields

{\varepsilon}\,\big\|\int_{s}^{t}V(t,r)K(r){\mathcal{R}}_{j}\big(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\big)U(r,s)dr\big\|\leq Ce^{\tilde{C}_{0}(t-s)}\big({\varepsilon}(t-s)+{\varepsilon}\lambda^{2}+\lambda^{4}).

(4.72)

Next we deal with the other term within the second integral on the right side of (
4.65
),

{\varepsilon}\,\big\|\int_{s}^{t}V(t,r)\partial_{r}\{{\mathcal{R}}_{j}\big(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\big)\}U(r,s)dr\big\|\leq{\varepsilon}C_{0}^{2}e^{\tilde{C}_{0}(t-s)}\int_{s}^{t}\|\partial_{r}\{{\mathcal{R}}_{j}(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r))\}\|dr.

(4.73)

By using the quotient rule for derivatives in (
4.67
),

\|\partial_{r}\{{\mathcal{R}}_{j}(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r))\}\|\leq\frac{2d}{\Delta_{0}^{2}}\Big[\big(\max_{j}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\|^{2}\\
+\max_{j}\|P_{j}\|_{\infty}\max_{j}\|\partial^{2}_{t}P_{j}(r)\|\big)\max_{\ell,j}|\widetilde{\alpha}_{\ell}-\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}|_{\infty}\\
+\max_{j}\|P_{j}\|_{\infty}\max_{j}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\|\max_{\ell,j}|\partial_{t}(\widetilde{\alpha}_{\ell}-\widetilde{\alpha}_{j})|_{\infty}\Big].

(4.74)

Using the bounds
\|P_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq 2
(see before (
3.22
)) and
|\widetilde{\alpha}_{\ell}|_{\infty}\leq|\alpha_{\ell}|_{\infty}+\lambda^{2}\|v\|_{\infty}^{2}\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}
, we simplify the estimate (
4.74
) to

\|\partial_{r}\{{\mathcal{R}}_{j}(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r))\}\|\leq\frac{8d}{\Delta_{0}^{2}}\Big[(\max_{j}|\alpha_{j}|_{\infty}+\lambda^{2}\|v\|_{\infty}^{2}\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}})\big(\max_{j}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\|^{2}+\max_{j}\|\partial_{t}^{2}P_{j}(r)\|\big)\\
+\max_{j}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\|\max_{j}|\partial_{t}\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}|_{\infty}\Big].

(4.75)

We have

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}\widetilde{\alpha}_{j}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}\alpha_{j}(t)|+\lambda^{2}\big|\partial_{t}\big[|v_{j}(t)|^{2}\int_{0}^{t/{\varepsilon}}e^{ix\alpha_{j}(t)}\gamma(x)dx\big]\big|

(4.76)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}\alpha_{j}|_{\infty}+\lambda^{2}\big[2\|v\|_{\infty}\|\partial_{t}v\|_{\infty}\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}+\|v\|_{\infty}\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}|\gamma(t/{\varepsilon})|\big].

Combining (
4.75
) and (
4.76
) gives the bound

\|\partial_{r}\{{\mathcal{R}}_{j}(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r))\}\|\leq C\max_{j}\big(\|\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\|(1+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{{\varepsilon}}|\gamma(r/{\varepsilon})|)+\|\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\|^{2}+\|\partial_{t}^{2}P_{j}(r)\|\big).

(4.77)

The norms
\|\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\|
and
\|\partial_{t}^{2}P_{j}(r)\|
are estimated in (
3.22
) and (
3.24
) and so (
4.77
) becomes

\|\partial_{r}\{{\mathcal{R}}_{j}(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r))\}\|\leq C\big(1+\lambda^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}(|\gamma(r/{\varepsilon})|(1+r)+|(\partial_{t}\gamma)(r/{\varepsilon})|)+\frac{\lambda^{4}}{{\varepsilon}^{2}}|\gamma(r/{\varepsilon})|^{2}\big).

(4.78)

This bound inserted into (
4.73
), and using
\int_{s}^{t}|\gamma(r/{\varepsilon})|rdr\leq{\varepsilon}^{2}\|r\gamma(r)\|_{L^{1}}
, yields

{\varepsilon}\,\big\|\int_{s}^{t}V(t,r)\partial_{r}\{{\mathcal{R}}_{j}\big(r,\partial_{t}P_{j}(r)\big)\}U(r,s)dr\big\|\leq Ce^{\tilde{C}_{0}(t-s)}\big({\varepsilon}(t-s)(1+\lambda^{2})+{\varepsilon}\lambda^{2}\|r\gamma(r)\|_{L^{1}}\\
\ +\lambda^{2}(\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}+\|\partial_{t}\gamma\|_{L^{1}})+\lambda^{4}\|\gamma^{2}\|_{L^{1}}\big).

(4.79)

Putting together the relations (
4.64
), (
4.65
), (
4.70
), (
4.72
) and (
4.79
) we are rewarded with the bound

\|V(t,s)-U(t,s)\|\leq Ce^{\tilde{C}(t-s)}({\varepsilon}+\lambda^{2}),

(4.80)

where
\tilde{C}>\tilde{C}_{0}
, which holds for
\lambda
satisfying (
2.6
), and all
0<{\varepsilon}\leq 1
,
0\leq s\leq t

as given in (
3.36
).

Finally we need to check the validity of (
3.34
), point 2) of Proposition
3.4
, which in term implies (
4.66
). To bound
V_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t,s)
we use the relation (
3.33
). First we see that

\|W_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t,s)\|\leq e^{\int_{s}^{t}\|K_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(u)\|du},

(4.81)

which follows from the series expansion based on (
3.30
). Now by Lemma
3.3
, point (c),

\|K_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t)\|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}(t,{{\varepsilon},\lambda})P_{j}(t,{{\varepsilon},\lambda})\|\leq 2d\max_{j}\|\partial_{t}P_{j}(t,{{\varepsilon},\lambda})\|.

(4.82)

Then by (
3.22
),

\int_{s}^{t}\|K_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(u)\|du\leq(t-s)C_{1}+\|w\|_{\infty}^{2}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{s}^{t}|\gamma(u/{\varepsilon})|du,

(4.83)

with

C_{1}=\frac{8}{\Delta_{0}}\Big[\|\partial_{t}A\|_{\infty}+\lambda^{2}\big(2\|\partial_{t}w\|_{\infty}\|w\|_{\infty}\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}+\|v\|_{\infty}^{2}\|t\gamma(t)\|_{L^{1}}\,\|\partial_{t}A\|_{\infty}\big)\Big].

(4.84)

As
\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{s}^{t}|\gamma(u/{\varepsilon})|du\leq\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}
, we conclude from (
4.81
) that for all
0\leq s\leq t<\infty
,

\|W_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t,s)\|\leq e^{(t-s)C_{1}+\lambda^{2}\|w\|_{\infty}^{2}\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}}.

(4.85)

This upper bound is uniform in
{\varepsilon}>0
. The next step in our quest to control
\|V_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t,s)\|
is an upper bound on the phase term
\Psi_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t,s)
in (
3.33
). As
\|P_{j}(s,{{\varepsilon},\lambda})\|\leq 2
(Lemma
3.3
(c)),

\|\Psi_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t,s)\|\leq 2d\max_{j}e^{\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{s}^{t}{\rm Im}\,[\alpha_{j}(u)+\lambda^{2}\alpha^{\prime}_{j}(u,{\varepsilon})]du}=2d\max_{j}e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{s}^{t}{\rm Im}\,\alpha^{\prime}_{j}(u,{\varepsilon})du}.

(4.86)

From (
3.20
) and the property
\gamma(-x)=\overline{\gamma}(x)
(see (
2.7
)), we obtain

{\rm Im}\,\alpha_{j}^{\prime}(t,{\varepsilon})=-|v_{j}(t)|^{2}{\rm Re\,}\int_{0}^{t/{\varepsilon}}e^{ix\alpha_{j}(t)}\gamma(x)dx=-\frac{1}{2}|v_{j}(t)|^{2}\int_{-t/{\varepsilon}}^{t/{\varepsilon}}e^{ix\alpha_{j}(t)}\gamma(x)dx.

(4.87)

For any
0\leq u_{0}<t-s
we have

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}{\rm Im}\alpha_{j}^{\prime}(u,{\varepsilon})du

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t-s}{\rm Im}\alpha_{j}^{\prime}(u+s,{\varepsilon})du

(4.88)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{u_{0}}{\rm Im}\alpha_{j}^{\prime}(u+s,{\varepsilon})du+\int_{u_{0}}^{t-s}{\rm Im}\alpha_{j}^{\prime}(u+s,{\varepsilon})du.

As
|\alpha^{\prime}_{j}(t,{\varepsilon})|\leq\|v_{j}\|^{2}_{\infty}\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}
(see (
3.20
)), the first integral on the right side of (
4.88
) is bounded above by
u_{0}\|v_{j}\|^{2}_{\infty}\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}
. The second integral is

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{u_{0}}^{t-s}|v_{j}(u+s)|^{2}\Big(\int_{-(u+s)/{\varepsilon}}^{(u+s)/{\varepsilon}}e^{ix\alpha_{j}(u+s)}\gamma(x)dx\Big)du

(4.89)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{u_{0}}^{t-s}|v_{j}(u+s)|^{2}\Big(\sqrt{2\pi}\widehat{\gamma}\big(\alpha_{j}(u+s)\big)-\int_{|x|\geq(u+s)/{\varepsilon}}e^{ix\alpha_{j}(u+s)}\gamma(x)dx\Big)du,

where

\widehat{\gamma}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{ix\alpha}\gamma(x)dx\geq 0.

(4.90)

Using the bound (
3.35
) we estimate

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{1}{2}\int_{u_{0}}^{t-s}|v_{j}(u+s)|^{2}\Big(\int_{|x|\geq(u+s)/{\varepsilon}}e^{ix\alpha_{j}(u+s)}\gamma(x)dx\Big)du\Big|

(4.91)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{\gamma}\|v_{j}\|_{\infty}^{2}}{m-1}\int_{u_{0}}^{t-s}\frac{du}{(1+\frac{u+s}{{\varepsilon}})^{m-1}}\leq\frac{C_{\gamma}\|v_{j}\|_{\infty}^{2}}{(m-1)(m-2)}\frac{{\varepsilon}}{(1+\frac{u_{0}}{{\varepsilon}})^{m-2}}.

Since
\widehat{\gamma}(x)\geq 0
, we obtain from (
4.88
), (
4.89
) and (
4.91
)

\int_{s}^{t}{\rm Im}\alpha_{j}^{\prime}(u,{\varepsilon})du\leq u_{0}\|v_{j}\|_{\infty}\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}+\frac{C_{\gamma}\|v_{j}\|_{\infty}^{2}}{(m-1)(m-2)}\frac{{\varepsilon}}{(1+\frac{u_{0}}{{\varepsilon}})^{m-2}}.

(4.92)

Upon choosing
u_{0}={\varepsilon}
we get for
0\leq s\leq t<\infty
,

e^{\frac{\lambda^{2}}{{\varepsilon}}\int_{s}^{t}{\rm Im}\,\alpha^{\prime}_{j}(u,{\varepsilon})du}\leq e^{\lambda^{2}[\|v_{j}\|^{2}_{\infty}\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}+\frac{C_{\gamma}\|v_{j}\|_{\infty}^{2}}{(m-1)(m-2){2^{m-2}}}]}.

(4.93)

Combining this bound with (
3.33
), (
4.85
) and (
4.86
) we arrive at

\|V_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t,s)\|\leq 2de^{(t-s)C_{1}+\lambda^{2}[(\|w\|^{2}_{\infty}+\|v\|^{2}_{\infty})\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}+\frac{C_{\gamma}\|v\|_{\infty}^{2}}{(m-1)(m-2){2^{m-2}}}]},

(4.94)

where
C_{1}
and
C_{\gamma}
are from (
4.84
), (
3.35
). This shows the bound on
V_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}
in (
3.34
).

Next, we bound
\|U_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t,s)\|\equiv\|U(t,s)\|
. We use the first equality in (
4.64
) and iterate it,

\displaystyle U(t,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V(t,s)+\sum_{n\geq 1}(-1)^{n}\int_{s}^{t}dr_{1}\int_{s}^{r_{1}}dr_{2}\cdots\int_{s}^{r_{n-1}}dr_{n}V(t,r_{1})K(r_{1})V(r_{1},r_{2})K(r_{2})\cdots

(4.95)

\displaystyle\cdots V(r_{n-1},r_{n})K(r_{n})V(r_{n},s).

The bound on
V_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t,s)
in (
3.34
) implies

\|V(t,r_{1})K(r_{1})\cdots K(r_{n})V(r_{n},s)\|\leq(2d)^{n+1}e^{(t-s)C_{1}}e^{(n+1)\lambda^{2}C_{2}}\|K(r_{1})\|\cdots\|K(r_{n})\|,

from which we obtain, by (
4.95
),

\|U(t,s)\|\leq 2d\,e^{(t-s)C_{1}}e^{\lambda^{2}C_{2}}\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{n!}\big[2de^{\lambda^{2}C_{2}}\int_{s}^{t}\|K(r)\|dr\big]^{n}\\
=2d\,e^{(t-s)C_{1}}e^{\lambda^{2}C_{2}}\exp\big[2de^{\lambda^{2}C_{2}}\int_{s}^{t}\|K(r)\|dr\big].

(4.96)

Now as in (
4.83
) and following that bound,
\int_{s}^{t}\|K(r)\|dr\leq(t-s)C_{1}+\lambda^{2}\|w\|^{2}_{\infty}\|\gamma\|_{L^{1}}
, so that (
4.96
) (upon possibly relabeling constants) indeed implies the bound on
U_{{\varepsilon},\lambda}(t,s)
in (
3.34
).
This completes the proof of Proposition
3.4
.

a note on squeezing function and its generalizations
Naveen Gupta, Sanjay Kumar Pant

Abstract
. This note investigates the relation between squeezing function and its generalizations. Using the relation obtained, we present an alternate method to find expression of generalized squeezing function of unit ball corresponding to the generalized complex ellipsoids.

1.
introduction

This note clarifies the relation between squeezing function and its generalizations, namely, generalized squeezing function and
d
-balanced squeezing function.
The notion of squeezing function was first introduced by Deng, Guan and Zhang
[
3
]
. Although
its genesis can be traced back to the work of Liu et al
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
and of Yeung
[
13
]
.
Rong and Yang
[
11
]
introduced the notion of generalized squeezing function. The authors in

[
6
]
extended it to

d
-balanced squeezing function. Let us see the definitions of all these terms first.

\mathbb{B}^{n}
denotes unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
is used for
bounded domain. The set of all injective holomorphic
maps from
D
to a domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
is denoted by

\mathcal{O}_{u}(D,\Omega)
.

For
z\in{D}

the squeezing function
S_{D}
on
D
is defined as

S_{D}(z):=\sup_{f}\{r:\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,r)\subseteq f(D),f\in{\mathcal{O}_{u}(D,\mathbb{B}^{n})}\},

where
\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,r)
denotes ball of radius
r
centered at the origin.

A domain is said to be holomorphic homogeneous regular if its squeezing function has a positive lower bound. Notion of
holomorphic homogeneous regular (HHR) manifolds was studied by Liu et al
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
. In 2009
[
13
]
,
Yeung renamed HHR as uniform squeezing property. It was then formally defined in terms of squeezing function by Deng et al in 2012
[
3
]
.
Holomorphic homogeneous regular domains have important significance due to several anlaytic and geometric properties they ensure.

In
[
11
]
, Rong and Yang introduced the concept of generalized squeezing function

S^{\Omega}_{D}
for bounded domains
D,\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
, where
\Omega
is a
balanced domain.

Let us quickly see the definitions of a balanced domain and Minkowski function. A
domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
is said to be balanced if
\lambda z\in{\Omega},

for each
z\in{\Omega}
and
|\lambda|\leq 1
.
The Minkowski function denoted by
h_{\Omega}
on

\mathbb{C}^{n}
is defined as

h_{\Omega}(z):=\inf\{t>0:\frac{z}{t}\in{\Omega}\}.

For
0<r\leq 1
, let
\Omega(r):=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}^{n}:h_{\Omega}(z)<r\}}
. For
a bounded domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
and a bounded, balanced, convex domain

\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
, Rong and Yang introduced the notion of generalized squeezing
function
S_{D}^{\Omega}
on
D
as

S^{\Omega}_{D}(z):=\sup\{r:\Omega(r)\subseteq f(D),f\in{\mathcal{O}_{u}(D,\Omega)},f(z)=0\}.

Notion of balanced domains was introduced by Nikolov in his work
[
10
]
. Let

d=(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}_{n}^{+}},n\geq 2
, we say that a domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}

is
d
-balanced if for each
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\in\Omega

and
\lambda\in{\overline{\mathbb{D}}}
,
\left(\lambda^{d_{1}}z_{1},\lambda^{d_{2}}z_{2},\ldots,\lambda^{d_{n}}z_{n}\right)\in\Omega,
where
\mathbb{D}
denotes unit disk in
\mathbb{C}
.
It is easy to see that balanced domains are
(1,1,\ldots,1)
-balanced.

For a
d
-balanced domain
\Omega
, an analogue of Minkowski function,
called
d
-Minkowski function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
denoted by
h_{d,\Omega}
, is defined as

h_{d,\Omega}(z):=\inf\{t>0:\left(\frac{z_{1}}{t^{d_{1}}},\frac{z_{2}}{t^{d_{2}}},\ldots,\frac{z_{n}}{t^{d_{n}}}\right)\in{\Omega}\}.

For each
0<r\leq 1
, we fix

\Omega^{d}(r):=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}^{n}:h_{d,\Omega}(z)<r\}}
.

Definition 1.1
.

For a bounded domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and a bounded, convex,

d=(d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n})
-balanced domain
\Omega
,

d
-balanced squeezing function corresponding to
\Omega
of the domain
D
,
denoted by
S_{d,D}^{\Omega}
(also called the

d
-balanced squeezing function
for brevity)
is given by:

S_{d,D}^{\Omega}(z):=\sup\{r:\Omega^{d}(r)\subseteq f(D),f\in{\mathcal{O}_{u}(D,\Omega)},f(z)=0\}.

Similar to the case of squeezing function, holomorphic homogeneous regular domains are the ones whose generalized squeezing function
(or
d
-balanced squeezing function) has a positive lower bound.

As we know that squeezing function was introduced in the context of uniform squeezing property (HHR), so
it is natural to expect that regularity remains unaltered when one shifts from
squeezing function to its generaliations.
More precisely, it is natural to expect that if a domain
D
is regular in

S_{D}
, then it should be regular in
S_{D}^{\Omega}
or
S_{d,D}^{\Omega}
. To address this we analyze relations between these
objects in Theorems
2.1
and
3.2
.
We would like to
point out that apart from addressing the question about regularity of a domain, these results will also serve its purpose in estimating squeezing function
for different domains. Another similar question related to regularity is behaviour of these generalizations corresponding to different model domains.
What we mean by this is: For a given domain
D
and two bounded, balanced, convex domains
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}
, if
D
is regular as per the setup of

S_{D}^{\Omega_{1}}
, will it be regular as per
S_{D}^{\Omega_{2}}
? A similar question can be formed for
S_{d,D}^{\Omega}.

One can not be unjust for expecting this and this is the content of our Corollary
2.3

and Corollary
3.3
.

2.
S_{D}
and
S_{D}^{\Omega}

In
[
5
, Lemma 1.2]
a relation between the squeezing function and the squeezing function corresponding
to polydisk is presented. Such a relation immediately deduces the equivalence between holomorphic homogeneous
regular domains in these two setups and is helpful in obtaining estimate for squeezing function [See examples in

[
5
]
]. We present a similar relation between squeezing function and generalized squeezing function, which
in particular will imply that the holomorphic homogeneous regular domains are the same for
S_{D}
and
S_{D}^{\Omega}
.
We point out here that equivalence of holomorprhic homogeneous regular domains is established in

[
11
, Theorem 3.9]
, but the inequality
established here is more useful in estimating generalized squeezing function.
For a domain
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
z_{1},z_{2}\in D
, the Carathéodory pseudodistance
c_{D}
on
D
is defined as

c_{D}(z_{1},z_{2})=\sup_{f}\{p(0,\mu):f\in{\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{D},D),f(z_{1})=0,f(z_{2})=\mu}\},

where
p
denotes the Poincaré metric on unit disc
\mathbb{D}
. We take
c_{D}^{*}=\tanh c_{D}
. Using
[
12
, Lemma 3.2]
, for a bounded, balanced, convex domain
D
, we have
c_{D}^{*}(0,z)=h_{D}(z)
for
z\in D
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
be bounded and
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
be bounded, balanced, convex domain.
Then for
a\in D
the following holds:

(1)

S_{D}(a)\geq\dfrac{\alpha}{R}S_{D}^{\Omega}(a),

(2)

S_{D}^{\Omega}(a)\geq\dfrac{\alpha}{R}S_{D}(a),

where
\alpha=dist(0,\partial\Omega)
and
R=\dfrac{1}{2}diam(\Omega)
.

Proof.

(1)

For
a\in D
, let
f:D\to\mathbb{B}^{n}
be an injective holomorphic map with
f(a)=0
. Let
r>0

be such that
B^{n}(0,r)\subseteq f(D)
. Consider
g:D\to\Omega
defined as

g(z):=\alpha f(z).

Note that
g

is injective holomorphic with
g(a)=0
and
g(z)\in{\Omega}
for all
z\in D
. Also note that

B^{n}(0,\alpha r)\subseteq g(D)
. We claim that
\Omega\left(\dfrac{\alpha r}{R}\right)\subseteq g(D).

Before proving our claim, we make an
observation that
\Omega\subseteq B^{n}(0,R)
and therefore

(2.1)

c^{*}_{B^{n}(0,R)}(0,z)\leq c^{*}_{\Omega}(0,z)=h_{\Omega}(z)\ \mbox{for all}\ z\in\Omega.

In order to prove our claim, see that for
z\in{\Omega\left(\dfrac{\alpha r}{R}\right)},

using equation (
2.1
), we have

c^{*}_{B^{n}(0,R)}(0,z)\leq c^{*}_{\Omega}(0,z)=h_{\Omega}(z)<\dfrac{\alpha r}{R}.

This gives us
z\in{\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,\alpha r)}
and thus
z\in g(D).
Therefore, we get our
claim and hence

S_{D}^{\Omega}(a)\geq\dfrac{\alpha r}{R}
, which implies
r\leq\dfrac{R}{\alpha}S_{D}^{\Omega}(a)
.
This establishes (1).

(2)

Let
f:D\to\Omega
be an injective holomorphic map with
f(a)=0
. Let
r>0
be such that
\Omega(r)\subseteq f(D)
. Consider
g:D\to\mathbb{B}^{n}
defined as

g(z):=\dfrac{f(z)}{R}.

Observe that
g
is injective
holomorphic with
g(a)=0
. Also,
\Omega\left(\dfrac{r}{R}\right)=\dfrac{1}{R}\Omega(r)\subseteq g(D)\subseteq\mathbb{B}^{n}.
Since

\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,\alpha)\subseteq\Omega
, therefore

(2.2)

h_{\Omega}(z)=c^{*}_{\Omega}(0,z)\leq c^{*}_{B^{n}(0,\alpha)}(0,z)\ \mbox{for all}\ z\in\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,\alpha).

We claim that
\mathbb{B}^{n}\left(0,\dfrac{\alpha r}{R}\right)\subseteq g(D).

For
z\in\mathbb{B}^{n}\left(0,\dfrac{\alpha r}{R}\right),
using
equation (
2.2
) (note that
r/R<1
), we have

h_{\Omega}(z)=c^{*}_{\Omega}(0,z)\leq c^{*}_{B^{n}(0,\alpha)}(0,z)=\dfrac{1}{\alpha}|z|<\dfrac{r}{R}.

Thus we get
z\in\Omega\left(\dfrac{r}{R}\right)\subseteq g(D)
, which proves our claim and
therefore
S_{D}(a)\geq\dfrac{\alpha r}{R}
.
This establishes (2).

∎

Remark 2.2
.

The two inequalities in the above theorem can not be attained simultaneously unless
\Omega=\mathbb{B}^{n}
. To see this, note that the two inequalities are attained simultaneously only when
\alpha=R
.

Note that previous theorem in particular implies that holomorphic homogeneous regular domains in
the set up of
S_{D}
and
S_{D}^{\Omega}
are the same.

The interaction between generalized squeezing function for different model domains
\Omega
is presented
in the following easy
corollary to the above proposition.
For relation between
S_{D}^{\Omega}
for different model domains, where
\Omega
is
h
omogeneous as
well, one can see
[
11
, Theorem 4.4]
. We will see analogue of this result for
d
-balanced squeezing function
in Theorem
3.4
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded domain and
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
be bounded, balanced,
covex domains, then the following holds:

(1)

S_{D}^{\Omega_{2}}(a)\geq\dfrac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}{R_{1}R_{2}}S_{D}^{\Omega_{1}}(a),

(2)

S_{D}^{\Omega_{1}}(a)\geq\dfrac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}}{R_{1}R_{2}}S_{D}^{\Omega_{2}}(a),

where
\alpha_{i}=dist(0,\partial\Omega_{i})
and
R_{i}=\dfrac{1}{2}diam(\Omega_{i}),\ i=1,2
.

Proof.

To prove (1), use part (2) of Proposition
2.1
for
\Omega_{2}
and then use part
(1) for
\Omega_{1}
. One can prove (2) in similar way.
∎

A simple consequence of the above stated corollary is:

S_{D}^{\Omega_{1}}
has a positive lower bound if and only if
S_{D}^{\Omega_{2}}

has positive lower bound. We would like to mention here, this statement has been proved in
[
11
, Theorem 3.9 ]

separately, but the bounds obtained here are the simplest possible and are useful in its own right.

3.
S_{D}
and
S_{d,D}^{\Omega}

Let us recall few properties of d-Minkowski function and
d
-balanced domains.

Result 3.1
.

For a
d
-balanced domain
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
, the following holds:

( see
[
7
, Remark 2.2.14]
)

(1)

\Omega=\{z\in{\mathbb{C}^{n}}:h_{d,\Omega}(z)<1\}
.

(2)

h_{d,\Omega}\left(\lambda^{d_{1}}z_{1},\lambda^{d_{2}}z_{2},\ldots,\lambda^{d_{n}}z_{n}\right)=|\lambda|h_{d,\Omega}(z)
for each
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}^{n}}

and
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}.

(3)

h_{d,\Omega}
is upper semicontinuous.

For a bounded,
d
-balanced, convex domain
\Omega
, using
[
2
, Theorem 1.6]
, we have

\tanh^{-1}h_{d,\Omega}(z)^{L}\leq c_{\Omega}(0,z)=k_{\Omega}(0,z)\leq\tanh^{-1}h_{d,\Omega}(z),

where
L=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}d_{i}.

Similar to the case of generalized squeezing function, the following theorem for

d
-balanced squeezing function holds.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
be bounded and
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
be bounded,

d
-balanced, convex domain.
Then for
a\in D
the following holds:

(1)

S_{D}(a)\geq\dfrac{\alpha}{P}S_{d,D}^{\Omega}(a)^{L},

(2)

S_{d,D}^{\Omega}(a)\geq\dfrac{\alpha}{R}S_{D}(a),

where
\alpha=dist(0,\partial\Omega)
and
R=\dfrac{1}{2}diam(\Omega),P=R+1,L=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}d_{i}.

Proof.

Let
a\in D
and
f:D\to\Omega
be injective holomorphic map with
f(a)=0
. Let
r>0
be such that

\Omega^{d}(r)\subseteq f(D)\subseteq\Omega=\Omega^{d}(1).
Consider
g:D\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
defined as

g(z):=\left(\dfrac{f_{1}(z)}{P^{d_{1}}},\dfrac{f_{2}(z)}{P^{d_{2}}},\ldots,\dfrac{f_{n}(z)}{P^{d_{n}}}\right).

It is obvious that
g
is injective holomorphic with
g(a)=0
.
Also using Result
3.1
(2) and the fact that
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,P)

it is easy to infer that

g(D)\subseteq\mathbb{B}^{n}
. Therefore,
g:D\to\mathbb{B}^{n}
. Next we claim that

\Omega^{d}\left(\dfrac{\alpha r^{L}}{P}\right)\subseteq g(D)
.
Let
z\in\Omega^{d}\left(\dfrac{\alpha r^{L}}{P}\right)
, that is,

h_{d,\Omega}(z)<\dfrac{\alpha r^{L}}{P}<1
. Therefore, we get
z\in\Omega
.
Also using the decreasing property of Caratheodory metric,
we have
c^{*}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,P)}(0,z)\leq c^{*}_{\Omega}(0,z)
for
z\in{\Omega}
.
Thus we have
\dfrac{1}{P}|z|\leq c^{*}_{\Omega}(0,z)\leq h_{d,\Omega}(z)<\dfrac{\alpha r^{L}}{P}
. Thus,
|z|<\alpha r^{L}\leq\alpha
and for
z\in{\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,\alpha)}
, using the decreasing property,
we have
h_{d,\Omega}(z)^{L}\leq c^{*}_{\Omega}(0,z)\leq c^{*}_{\mathbb{B}^{n}(0,\alpha)}(0,z)=\dfrac{1}{\alpha}|z|.

Therefore, we obtain
h_{d,\Omega}(z)<\dfrac{\alpha r}{P}
. Now using Result
3.1
(2),
we get
z\in g(D)
.
Therefore,
\Omega^{d}\left(\dfrac{\alpha r^{L}}{P}\right)\subseteq g(D)
and this finally yeilds (1).
Proceding on the similar lines, one can prove (2). ∎

The following corollary ensures that the holomorohic homogeneous regular domains are same
corresponding to different
d
-balanced domains.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
be bounded and
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
be
bounded, convex domains. Assume that
\Omega_{1}
is
d
-balanced, and
\Omega_{2}
is

d^{\prime}
-balanced, then for
a\in D
, the following holds:

(1)

S_{d^{\prime},D}^{\Omega_{2}}(a)\geq\dfrac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}S_{d,D}^{\Omega_{1}}(a)^{L}}{P_{1}R_{2}},

(2)

S_{d,D}^{\Omega_{1}}(a)\geq\dfrac{\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}S_{d^{\prime},D}^{\Omega_{2}}(a)^{L^{\prime}}}{R_{1}P_{2}},

where
P_{i}=R_{i}+1,\alpha_{i}=dist(0,\partial\Omega_{i})
,

R_{i}=\dfrac{1}{2}diam(\Omega_{i}),\ \ i=1,2
;
L=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}d_{i}
and
L^{\prime}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}d_{i}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

One can prove this by proceeding on the same lines as in the Corollary
2.3
.
∎

Similarly to the Theorem
[
12
, Theorem 4.4]
the following theorem gives relation between

d
-balanced squeezing function for different model domains,
when domains in consideration are bounded,
d
-balanced, convex and
homogeneous
.

Theorem 3.4
.

let
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
be bounded, convex, homogeneous domains and
D\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a bounded domain. Assume that

\Omega_{1}
is
d
-balanced and
\Omega_{2}
is
d^{\prime}
-balanced. Then

(1)

S_{d^{\prime},D}^{\Omega_{2}}(z)\geq S_{d,\Omega_{1}}^{\Omega_{2}}(0)S_{d,D}^{\Omega_{1}}(z)^{L},

(2)

S_{d,D}^{\Omega_{1}}(z)\geq S_{d^{\prime},\Omega_{2}}^{\Omega_{1}}(0)S_{d^{\prime},D}^{\Omega_{2}}(z)^{L^{\prime}},

where
L=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}d_{i}
and
L^{\prime}=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}d_{i}^{\prime}
.

We will need the following lemma to prove this theorem.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a
d
-balanced domain and
\Omega_{1}=\Omega^{d}(r),
where

0<r\leq 1
. Then

h_{d,\Omega_{1}}=\dfrac{1}{r}h_{d,\Omega}.

Proof.

First of all, observe that
\Omega_{1}
is bounded,
d
-balanced and
\Omega_{1}\subseteq\Omega^{\prime}
.
Let
A=\{t>0:\left(\frac{z_{1}}{t^{d_{1}}},\frac{z_{2}}{t^{d_{2}}},\ldots,\frac{z_{n}}{t^{d_{n}}}\right)\in{\Omega}\}
and
A_{1}=\{t>0:\left(\frac{z_{1}}{t^{d_{1}}},\frac{z_{2}}{t^{d_{2}}},\ldots,\frac{z_{n}}{t^{d_{n}}}\right)\in{\Omega^{\prime}}\}
. It is easy to see that
t\in A
if and only if
\dfrac{t}{r}\in A_{1}
. Therefore we get

A_{1}=\dfrac{1}{r}A
and hence the result follows.
∎

Proof of theorem
3.4
.

Let
z\in D
and
f:D\to\Omega_{1}
be injective holomorpphic with
f(z)=0
. Let
r>0
be such that

(3.1)

\Omega_{1}^{d}(r)\subseteq f(D).

Let
\alpha=S_{d,\Omega_{1}}^{\Omega_{2}}(0)
, then using Theorem
[
6
, Theorem 4.6 ]
,
there exists an injective holomorphic map
g:\Omega_{1}\to\Omega_{2}
with
g(0)=0
such that

(3.2)

\Omega_{2}^{d^{\prime}}(\alpha)\subseteq g(\Omega_{1}).

Consider
F:D\to\Omega_{2}
defined as
F=g\circ f
, then
F
is injective holomorphic with
F(z)=0
.
Let us fix
\Omega=\Omega_{2}^{d^{\prime}}(\alpha)
.
We will first prove that
B_{\Omega}^{l}\left(0,\beta\right)\subseteq g\left(\Omega_{1}^{d}(r)\right),

where
\beta=\tanh^{-1}r^{L}
and
l
denotes Lempert’s function.

Let
z\in B_{\Omega}^{l}\left(0,\beta\right)
, that is,
l_{\Omega}(0,z)<\beta
. Since
\Omega\subseteq g(\Omega_{1})
, therefore
z=g(w)
for some
w\in\Omega
and using decreasing property of
Lempert’s function, we get
l_{g(\Omega_{1})}(0,g(w))<\beta
, thus we get
l_{\Omega_{1}}(0,w)<\beta
. Also
using
[
2
, Theorem 1.6]
, we have
\tanh^{-1}h_{d,\Omega_{1}}(z)\leq l_{\Omega_{1}}(0,w)
. Thus we
obtain
h_{d,\Omega_{1}}(w)<r
and therefore
z=g(w)\in{g\left(\Omega_{1}^{d}(r)\right)}.

So we have obtained
B_{\Omega}^{l}\left(0,\beta\right)\subseteq g\left(\Omega_{1}^{d}(r)\right)
and therefore
using Equation (
3.1
), we have

(3.3)

B_{\Omega}^{l}\left(0,\beta\right)\subseteq g(f(D))=F(D).

Next we claim to prove
\Omega_{2}^{d^{\prime}}(\alpha r^{L})\subseteq B_{\Omega}^{l}\left(0,\beta\right)
. For
z\in{\Omega_{2}^{d^{\prime}}(\alpha r^{L})}
,
h_{d^{\prime},\Omega_{2}}(z)<\alpha r^{L}
. Using Lemma
3.5
, we get
h_{d^{\prime},\Omega}(z)<r^{L}
. Now observing that
the right hand side of inequality in
[
2
, Theorem 1.6]
is true without pseudoconvexity assumption
of the domain, we get
\tanh l_{\Omega}(0,z)\leq h_{d^{\prime},\Omega}(z)<r^{L}
. This implies
z\in{B_{\Omega}^{l}\left(0,\beta\right)}
. This proves our claim and thus using Equation
(
3.3
), we get

\Omega_{2}^{d^{\prime}}(\alpha r^{L})\subseteq F(D).

Hence we get
S_{d^{\prime},D}^{\Omega_{2}}(z)\geq\alpha r^{L}
and this completes the proof of (1).
Similarly one can prove (2).
∎

4.
generalized squeezing function for complex ellipsoid

A
complex ellipsoid
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is a domain of the form

E(m)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\sum_{j\in{I_{1}}}|z_{j}|^{2m_{j}}<1,\,|z_{k}|<1\,\forall k\in{I_{2}}\},

where
1\leq m_{i}\leq\infty
,
I_{1}=\{1\leq j\leq n:m_{j}\neq\infty\}
and
I_{2}=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}\setminus I_{1}
.
E(m)
is convex if and only if
m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n}\geq\frac{1}{2}
.
E(m)=\mathbb{B}^{n},
when

m=(1,1,\ldots,1)
and
E(m)=\mathbb{D}^{n}
, when
m_{j}=\infty
for all
1\leq j\leq n
.

A
generalized complex ellipsoid
denoted by
E(p,m)
with
p=(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{k}),\,m=(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{k})
,
p_{1}+p_{2}+\ldots+p_{k}=n
is given by

E(p,m)=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{p_{1}}\times\mathbb{C}^{p_{2}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{C}^{p_{k}}:\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|z_{j}\|^{2m_{j}}<1\}.

If any of
m_{j}=\infty
, then
E(p,m)
is understood as in the case of
E(m).
In particular,
when
m_{j}=\infty
for each
j=1,2,\ldots,k
,

E(p,\infty)=\mathbb{B}^{p_{1}}\times\mathbb{B}^{p_{2}}\times\ldots\times\mathbb{B}^{p_{k}}.

Note that
E(p,\infty)
is bounded, balanced and convex. Also, it is easy to see that
\Omega
is
homoegeneous.

Let us denote by
\Omega_{i}=\mathbb{B}^{p_{i}}\,,i=1,2,\ldots,k
, and
D=\mathbb{B}^{n},

then
\Omega=E(p,\infty)=\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}\times\ldots\times\Omega_{k}
. Therefore, using
[
12
, Corollary 4.3 ]
, we get that

S_{D}^{\Omega}(z)=S^{D}_{\Omega}(w),

for each
z\in D
and
w\in\Omega
. It is readily seen using

[
3
, Theorem 7.5]
that

S^{D}_{\Omega}(w)=\left(S_{\Omega_{1}}(w_{1})^{-2}+S_{\Omega_{2}}(w_{2})^{-2}+\ldots+S_{\Omega_{k}}(w_{k})^{-2}\right)^{-1/2}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{k}}

and therefore
S_{D}^{\Omega}(z)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{k}}

We will present here an alternate method to obtain this expression using the inequality obtained in
Proposition
2.1
. We will require the following observation for this.

It is easy to see that
\alpha=dist(0,\partial\Omega)=1
and

R=\frac{1}{2}diam(\Omega)=\sqrt{k}
. We also need the following lemma, whose proof is based on the
technique used by H. Alexander in the proof of
[

.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f:D\to\Omega
be injective holomorphic with
f(0)=0
. Let
r>0
be such that

\Omega(r)\subseteq f(D)
, then
r\leq\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{k}}.

Proof.

Let us consider a function
g:\Omega(r)\to D
defined as

g(z):=f^{-1}(z).

For any
\epsilon>0
, consider
\mathbb{B}^{n}\left(0,\dfrac{1+\epsilon}{\sqrt{k}}\right)
. Note that using
[
3
, Theorem 7.5]
, it follows that

S_{\Omega(r)}^{D}=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{k}}
, therefore
g(\Omega(r))
does not contain

\mathbb{B}^{n}\left(0,\dfrac{1+\epsilon}{\sqrt{k}}\right)
. Let
a\in{\mathbb{B}^{n}\left(0,\dfrac{1+\epsilon}{\sqrt{k}}\right)}
be such that
b=f(a)\notin{\Omega(r)}=\mathbb{B}^{p_{1}}(r)\times\mathbb{B}^{p_{2}}(r)\times\ldots\times\mathbb{B}^{p_{k}}(r)
. This yields one of
b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{k}
, say
b_{1}
, has modulus

\geq r
. Therefore, using Schwarz’s lemma, we get

r\leq\|b_{1}\|=\|f_{1}(a)\|\leq\|a\|\leq\dfrac{1+\epsilon}{\sqrt{k}},

which completes the proof.

∎

Example 4.2
.

For
D=\mathbb{B}^{n}
,
\Omega=E(p,\infty)
,

S_{D}^{\Omega}(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}},

for
z\in D.

Before we prove this, observe that when
p_{i}=1
for all
i=1,2,\ldots,k
, then
\Omega=\mathbb{D}^{n}
. For
this case, we have

Proof.

Using Proposition

where

On the other hand, using Lemma

S_{D}^{\Omega}(z)\geq\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{k}}.

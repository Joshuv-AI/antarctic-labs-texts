Several arithmetic functions, such as the divisor function and the Euler totient function,
play a fundamental role in our understanding of the theory of numbers.
They are explicitly defined, in the sense that values of the function are not defined in terms of prior values of the function.
However, it is possible to write down meaningful recursive arithmetic functions.
We consider two of them in this paper, and show that they are intimately related to a number of standard arithmetic functions.

2.
Proof of Theorem 1

It is convenient to rewrite (
1
) as

(14)

2\,\kappa_{x}(n)=n^{x}+\sum_{d|n}\kappa_{x}(d).

Dividing by
n^{s}
and summing over
n
,

\displaystyle 2\,\widetilde{\kappa}_{x}=2\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\kappa_{x}(n)}{n^{s}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{n^{x}}{n^{s}}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{s}}\sum_{d|n}\kappa_{x}(d).

Swapping the order of summation,

\displaystyle 2\,\widetilde{\kappa}_{x}

\displaystyle=\zeta(s-x)+\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\kappa_{x}(d)\sum_{n:\,d|n}\frac{1}{n^{s}}

\displaystyle=\zeta(s-x)+\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\kappa_{x}(d)\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(dn)^{s}}

\displaystyle=\zeta(s-x)+\sum_{d=1}^{\infty}\frac{\kappa_{x}(d)}{d^{s}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n^{s}}

\displaystyle=\zeta(s-x)+\widetilde{\kappa}_{x}\,\zeta(s).

From this, we arrive at Theorem
1
:

\widetilde{\kappa}_{x}=\frac{\zeta(s-x)}{2-\zeta(s)}.\qed

3.
Proof of Theorem 2

In what follows, we use the standard identities

{\bf 1}*\mu=\varepsilon
,

{\bf 1}*J_{x}={\rm id}_{x}
and

{\bf 1}*{\rm id}_{x}=\sigma_{x}
.

Since
\widetilde{\sigma}_{x}/\widetilde{\kappa}_{x}=\zeta(s)(2-\zeta(s))
is independent of

\widetilde{\kappa}_{x}\,\widetilde{\sigma}_{y}=\widetilde{\kappa}_{y}\,\widetilde{\sigma}_{x}

The Dirichlet convolutions of
\kappa_{x}
and
\sigma_{x}
must follow the analogous relation, so we arrive at (
3

\displaystyle\kappa_{x}*\sigma_{y}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{y}*\sigma_{x}.\qed

We can immediately rewrite (
14

4

\kappa_{x}=({\rm id}_{x}+1*\kappa_{x})/2.\qed

Iterating this recursive definition gives

\displaystyle\kappa_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm id}_{x}}{2}+\frac{{\bf 1}*{\rm id}_{x}}{4}+\frac{{\bf 1}*{\bf 1}*\kappa_{x}}{4},

and so on, leading to the infinite series (

\displaystyle\kappa_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{{\rm id}_{x}}{2}+\frac{{\bf 1}*{\rm id}_{x}}{2^{2}}+\frac{{\bf 1}*{\bf 1}*{\rm id}_{x}}{2^{3}}+\ldots.\qed

Since

3

\displaystyle\kappa_{x}

\displaystyle=J_{x}*\kappa_{0}.\qed

Convolving of (

\displaystyle\kappa_{x}^{-1}

\displaystyle=J_{x}^{-1}*(2\mu-\varepsilon).\qed

From (

Convolving with

\displaystyle\sigma_{x}=\kappa_{x}*(2\,{\bf 1}-d).\qed

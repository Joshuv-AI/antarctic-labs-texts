Number of full exceptional collections modulo spherical twists for elliptic orbifolds
Atsushi Takahashi, Hongxia Zhang

Abstract
. This paper calculates the number of full exceptional collections modulo an action of a group as the set generated by spherical twists for an abelian category of coherent sheaves on an orbifold projective line with a zero orbifold Euler characteristic. This is done by a recursive formula naturally generalizing the one for the Dynkin case by Deligne whose categorical interpretation is due to Obaid-Nauman-Shammakh-Fakieh-Ringel and an abelian category of coherent sheaves on an orbifold projective line with a positive orbifold Euler characteristic is due to Otani-Shiraishi-Takahashi.



1.
Introduction

Inspired by the correspondence between maximal chains in the proof of noncrossing partitions for a Dynkin quiver
\vec{\triangle}
and complete exceptional sequences in the derived category
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{C}}\vec{\Delta})
of finitely generated modules over the path algebra
{\mathbb{C}}\vec{\Delta}
, Obaid-Nauman-Shammakh-Fakien-Ringel
[
ONSFR
]
found the following recursive formula for
e({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{C}}\vec{\Delta})):=|{\rm FEC}({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{C}}\vec{\Delta}))/{\mathbb{Z}}^{\mu}|
, where
{\rm FEC}({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{C}}\vec{\Delta}))
is the set of isomorphism classes of full exceptional collection in
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{C}}\vec{\Delta})
,
\mu
is the number of vertices of
\vec{\Delta}
and
{\mathbb{Z}}^{\mu}
is the group whose
i
-th generator acts by the translation functor
[1]
on the
i
-th object of a full exceptional collection.

e({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{C}}\vec{\Delta}))=\frac{h}{2}\sum_{\nu\in\Delta_{0}}e({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{C}}\vec{\Delta}^{(\nu)})),

where
\vec{\Delta}^{(\nu)}
is the full subquiver of
\vec{\Delta}
given by removing the vertex
\nu
and arrows connecting with
\nu
and
h\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
is the Coxeter number. As is mentioned in
[
ONSFR
]
, the above recursive formula is a categorification of Deligne’s recursive formula
[
De
]
which is originally answered to Looijenga’s conjecture for simple singularities concerning the number of distinguished bases of vanishing cycles modulo signs and the degree of a map
LL
, called the Lyashko-Looijenga map, describing the topology of the bifurcation set
[
Lo
]
. As a summary of the above story, the following equation holds:

e({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{C}}\vec{\Delta}))=\frac{\mu!}{d_{1}\cdots d_{\mu}}h^{\mu}={\rm deg}LL,

where
2\leq d_{1}\leq\dots\leq d_{\mu}=h
are degrees of algebraic independent invariants of the Weyl group for the Dynkin diagram
\Delta
. Here
LL
was originally defined by using the characteristic polynomial associated to the discriminant for the singularity, or equivalently, the square of Jacobian for the Wely group invariant theory. More generally,
LL
can be defined for massive
F
-manifolds (see
[
DZ
,
He
]
), In
[
HR
]
, Hertling-Roucairol extended the above correspondence between the number of distinguished bases of vanishing cycles modulo signs and the degree of the Lyashko-Looijenga map to simple elliptic singularities with Legendre normal forms. Therefore, it is very important to understand what happens when the setting falls between the two classes, corresponding to simple singularities and the simple elliptic singularities.

Recently,
[
OST
]
generalized the above recursive formula and equality for the derived category
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{1})
of coherent sheaves over an orbifold projective line
{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{1}
with three orbifold points
(0,1,\infty)
whose orders are given by
A:=(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
satisfying
\chi_{A}:=\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\frac{1}{a_{2}}+\frac{1}{a_{3}}-1\textgreater 0,
that is, orbifold projective line of domestic type. They define
e({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{1}))
from the perspective of mirror symmetry and the space of stability conditions. They reach to definition of
e({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{1}))
as

e({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{1})):=|{\rm FEC}({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{1}))/\langle{\rm ST}({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{1})),{\mathbb{Z}}^{\mu_{A}}\rangle|.

where
{\rm ST}({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{1}))
generated by the spherical twist
=(-)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(\vec{c})
,
\mu_{A}=a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}-1.

They prove the following holds

e({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{1}))=\frac{\mu_{A}!}{a_{1}!a_{2}!a_{3}!\chi_{A}}a_{1}^{a_{1}}a_{2}^{a_{2}}a_{3}^{a_{3}}.

2.1.
Set of full exceptional collections

First, we recall the notion of a full exceptional collection and basic properties.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
be a
{\mathbb{C}}
-linear triangulated category.

(i)

An object
E\in{\mathcal{D}}
is called
exceptional
if
{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(E,E)\cong{\mathbb{C}}
and
{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(E,E[p])\cong 0
when
p\neq 0
.

(ii)

For an exceptional object
E\in{\mathcal{D}}
,
E^{\perp}
is a full triangulated subcategory of
{\mathcal{D}}
defined as

E^{\perp}:=\{X\in{\mathcal{D}}\mid{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(E,X[p])=0~\text{for all}~p\in{\mathbb{Z}}\},

(iii)

An ordered set
{\mathcal{E}}=(E_{1},\dots,E_{\mu})
consisting of exceptional objects
E_{1},\dots,E_{\mu}
is called
exceptional collection
if
{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{D}}(E_{i},E_{j}[p])\cong 0
for all
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
i>j
.

(iv)

An exceptional collection
{\mathcal{E}}
is called
full
if the smallest full triangulated subcategory of
{\mathcal{D}}
containing all elements in
{\mathcal{E}}
is equivalent to
{\mathcal{D}}
as a triangulated category.

(v)

Two full exceptional collections
{\mathcal{E}}=(E_{1},\dots,E_{\mu})
,
{\mathcal{F}}=(F_{1},\dots,F_{\mu})
in
{\mathcal{D}}
are said to be
isomorphic
if
E_{i}\cong F_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,\mu
.
The set of isomorphism classes of full exceptional collections in
{\mathcal{D}}
is denoted by
{\rm FEC}({\mathcal{D}})
.

An object
E\in{\mathcal{D}}
is said to be
indecomposable
if
E
is nonzero and
E
has no direct sum decomposition
E\cong A\oplus B,
where
A
and
B
are nonzero objects in
{\mathcal{D}}
. Note that an exceptional object is indecomposable.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(E,F)
be an exceptional collection. Define two objects
{\mathcal{R}}_{F}E
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{E}F
by the following exact triangles respectively:

{\mathcal{R}}_{F}E\longrightarrow E\overset{{\rm ev}^{*}}{\longrightarrow}{\rm Hom}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{D}}(E,F)^{*}\otimes F\longrightarrow({\mathcal{R}}_{F}E)[1],

({\mathcal{L}}_{E}F)[-1]\longrightarrow{\rm Hom}^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{D}}(E,F)\otimes E\overset{\rm ev}{\longrightarrow}F\longrightarrow{\mathcal{L}}_{E}F,

where
(-)^{*}
denotes the duality
{\rm Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(-,{\mathbb{C}})
. The object
{\mathcal{R}}_{F}E
(
resp.

{\mathcal{L}}_{E}F
) is called the
right mutation
of
E
through
F
(
resp.
left mutation of
F
through
E
). Then,
(F,{\mathcal{R}}_{F}E)
and
({\mathcal{L}}_{E}F,E)
form new exceptional collections.

Remark 2.3
.

Our definition of mutations differs from the usual one (cf.
[
BP
, Section 1]
. In our notation, the usual left and right mutations are given by
{\mathcal{R}}_{F}E[1]
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{E}F[-1]

\bar{\gamma}\in Br_{3}/{\mathbb{Z}}[1]=\overline{\Gamma}

such that
\bar{\gamma}(E)=E_{i,j;k}
for some
i,j,k
.
The above description of the semi-orthogonal complement together with Lemma
3.3
gives
a correspondence between exceptional objects and the cusps of the group
\overline{\Gamma(\ell_{A})}
.
Namely, since the subgroup
\langle(-)\otimes{\mathcal{O}}(\vec{c})\rangle\subseteq{\rm ST}({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{A,\Lambda}))
acts trivially on
E_{i,j;k}
but
defines a nontrivial subgroup of
\overline{\Gamma}
due to Lemma
3.3
, we see that an infinite cyclic subgroup of
\overline{\Gamma}
acts trivially on
E
.
Note that for
\overline{\Gamma(\ell_{A})}
the number of cusps are
[\overline{\Gamma}:\overline{\Gamma(\ell_{A})}]/\ell_{A}
.
Therefore, we obtain the following recursion relation:

(5.1)

e({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{A,\Lambda}))=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{a_{i}-1}\binom{\mu_{A}-1}{j-1}\cdot e({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{A_{i,j},\Lambda}))\cdot e({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{C}}\vec{A}_{j-1}))\cdot a_{i}\cdot\frac{[\overline{\Gamma}:\overline{\Gamma(\ell_{A})}]}{\ell_{A}}.

For simplicity, we consider the part of the above sum with
i=1
. Recall that

e({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{A_{1,j},\Lambda}))=\frac{(\mu_{A}-j)!}{(a_{1}-j)!a_{2}!\cdots a_{r}!\chi_{A_{1,j}}}(a_{1}-j)^{a_{1}-j}a_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots a_{r}^{a_{r}},\quad e({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{C}}\vec{A}_{j-1}))=j^{j-2}.

In particular, since
\chi_{A}=0
it turns out that

\chi_{A_{1,j}}=2+\frac{1}{a_{1}-j}-1+\sum_{i=2}^{r}\left(\frac{1}{a_{i}}-1\right)=\frac{1}{a_{1}-j}-\frac{1}{a_{1}}+\chi_{A}=\frac{j}{a_{1}(a_{1}-j)}.

Lemma 5.1
.

We have the following identities:

(5.2a)

nx^{n-1}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(x-j)^{n-j}j^{j-1},

(5.2b)

\frac{n(n-1)}{2}x^{n-2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(x-j)^{n-j}(j-1)j^{j-2}.

Proof
.

It follows from Abel’s identity

(x+y)^{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(x-j)^{n-j}y(y+j)^{j-1}

that

\frac{(x+y)^{n}-x^{n}}{y}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(x-j)^{n-j}(y+j)^{j-1}.

Setting
y=0
, we obtain the first identity.
Taking the partial derivative of both sides with respect to
y
and then setting
y=0
, we obtain the second one.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

We have

(5.3)

\frac{1}{2}(n-1)n^{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(n-j)^{n-j+1}j^{j-2}.

Proof
.

It follows from the identity (
5.2a
) and (
5.2b
) that

\displaystyle\frac{n(n-1)}{2}x^{n-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(x-j)^{n-j}(j-1)j^{j-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(x-j)^{n-j}(xj-j-x+j)j^{j-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle nx^{n}-nx^{n-1}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}(x-j)^{n-j+1}j^{j-2}.

Setting
x=n
, we obtain the statement.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

We have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{a_{1}-1}\binom{\mu_{A}-1}{j-1}\frac{(\mu_{A}-j)!}{(a_{1}-j)!a_{2}!\cdots a_{r}!\frac{j}{a_{1}(a_{1}-j)}}(a_{1}-j)^{a_{1}-j}a_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots a_{r}^{a_{r}}j^{j-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{A}!}{a_{1}!a_{2}!\cdots a_{r}!}a_{1}^{a_{1}}a_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots a_{r}^{a_{r}}\cdot\frac{a_{1}(a_{1}-1)}{2\mu_{A}}.

Proof
.

Applying the previous Corollary, it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{a_{1}-1}\binom{\mu_{A}-1}{j-1}\frac{(\mu_{A}-j)!}{(a_{1}-j)!a_{2}!\cdots a_{r}!\frac{j}{a_{1}(a_{1}-j)}}(a_{1}-j)^{a_{1}-j}a_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots a_{r}^{a_{r}}j^{j-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{A}!}{a_{1}!a_{2}!\cdots a_{r}!}a_{1}^{a_{1}}a_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots a_{r}^{a_{r}}\cdot\frac{1}{\mu_{A}}\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{a_{1}-1}\binom{a_{1}}{j}\frac{(a_{1}-j)^{a_{1}-j+1}j^{j-2}}{a_{1}^{a_{1}-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{A}!}{a_{1}!a_{2}!\cdots a_{r}!}a_{1}^{a_{1}}a_{2}^{a_{2}}\cdots a_{r}^{a_{r}}\cdot\frac{a_{1}(a_{1}-1)}{2\mu_{A}}.

∎

The calculation is similar for
i=2,\dots,r
. Therefore, we can calculate the RHS of (
5.1
) as

\displaystyle e({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{A,\Lambda}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{a_{i}-1}\binom{\mu_{A}-1}{j-1}\cdot e({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{A_{i,j},\Lambda}))\cdot e({\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathbb{C}}\vec{A}_{j-1}))\cdot a_{i}\cdot\frac{[\overline{\Gamma}:\overline{\Gamma(\ell_{A})}]}{\ell_{A}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{A}!}{a_{1}!\cdots a_{r}!}a_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots a_{r}^{a_{r}}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\frac{a_{i}(a_{i}-1)}{2\mu_{A}}\cdot a_{i}\cdot\frac{[\overline{\Gamma}:\overline{\Gamma(\ell_{A})}]}{\ell_{A}}\right),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{A}!}{a_{1}!\cdots a_{r}!}a_{1}^{a_{1}}\cdots a_{r}^{a_{r}}\cdot\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}^{2}(a_{i}-1)}{2\mu_{A}}\frac{[\overline{\Gamma}:\overline{\Gamma(\ell_{A})}]}{\ell_{A}}.

We have finished the proof of Theorem
3.7
.

What if Red Can Talk? Dynamic Dialogue Generation Using Large Language Models
Navapat Nananukul, Wichayaporn Wongkamjan

Abstract
Role-playing games (RPGs) provide players with a rich, interactive world to explore. Dialogue serves as the primary means of communication between developers and players, manifesting in various forms such as guides, NPC interactions, and storytelling. While most games rely on written scripts to define the main story and character personalities, player immersion can be significantly enhanced through casual interactions between characters. With the advent of large language models (LLMs), we introduce a dialogue filler framework that utilizes LLMs enhanced by knowledge graphs to generate dynamic and contextually appropriate character interactions. We test this framework within the environments of Final Fantasy VII Remake and Pokémon, providing qualitative and quantitative evidence that demonstrates GPT-4’s capability to act with defined personalities and generate dialogue. However, some flaws remain, such as GPT-4 being overly positive or more subtle personalities, such as maturity, tend to be of lower quality compared to more overt traits like timidity. This study aims to assist developers in crafting more nuanced filler dialogues, thereby enriching player immersion and enhancing the overall RPG experience.

Human Assistance — RPG-dialogue Large Language Model

Role-playing games have been identified as a significant challenge for the next stage of artificial intelligence research. These games present a complex, open-ended creative arena where players interact through dialogue to shape the narrative and game outcomes. Unlike traditional games such as Chess and Go, which are zero-sum and deterministic.

One of the critical challenges presented by role-playing games is the necessity for nuanced, context-aware dialogue. Players create characters that interact within a fictional setting, making decisions and taking actions that influence the game’s progression. One example is Dungeon and Dragons (DND) where the game’s facilitator, often known as the Dungeon Master (DM) or Game Master (GM), describes scenarios, locations, and the roles of non-player characters (NPCs) and monsters. The DM’s role highlights the importance of narrative and improvisation, further complicating the AI’s task.

Another challenges in RPG dialogue is the occurrence of unnatural silences from main characters or NPCs. In real-world interactions, conversations flow continuously, with participants actively contributing to the dialogue. However, in RPGs, there can be instances where characters remain silent, breaking the immersion and disrupting the natural flow of the narrative.

These challenges become even more critical in popular games where main characters have strong personalities. For example, in
Final Fantasy VII Remake (FFVIIR)
, one of the most popular story-based RPGs, the main character
“Cloud Strife”
is known for his brooding and complex personality, characterized by his introspective nature and struggle with his identity. Another example is
Pokémon
, where the main character,
“Ash Ketchum”
(or Red in the original games), exhibits a determined and optimistic personality, always eager to engage with others and face new challenges. The inconsistency of dialogue, especially moments of unnatural silence, can disrupt the portrayal of these rich, well-defined characters, breaking player immersion and diminishing the storytelling experience. Addressing this challenge requires advanced AI capable of maintaining continuous, contextually appropriate dialogue that aligns with each character’s established traits, ensuring a more engaging and authentic player experience.

With the advent of AI and large language models (LLMs), role-playing games (RPGs) have become valuable testbeds due to their ability to simulate complex real-world dynamics, such as language generation, knowledge acquisition, and strategic planning tailored to each character and game scenario. This evolution presents developers with the opportunity to leverage these advances as tools for generating nuanced filler dialogues (Figure
1
). Our contributions in this paper include:

•

Integration of Knowledge Graphs and LLMs for Dialogue Generation:
We built game-specific knowledge graph and combined knowledge graphs with large language models (LLMs) to produce dialogues that are not only contextually rich but also character-specific, thus providing character-specific information to dialogue generation step.

•

Evaluate the Capability of Current LLMs Technology:
We assessed how well current version of GPT-4, when integrated with knowledge-based systems, can generate high-quality game dialogues that contribute to a more engaging and interactive narrative.

2.1
Knowledge Graph Acquisitions

Crawling:
We begin by crawling data from fandom websites of Final Fantasy VII and Pokémon as mentioned above. In Final Fantasy VII Remake, we gather detailed information on characters, including their personalities, abilities, relationships, and the events they influence or participate in. Additionally, we compile comprehensive data on boss battles, covering skills, weaknesses, and various states, such as low health or being engulfed in flames.
In Pokémon, we compiled comprehensive data on the personality traits, appearances, and Pokémon ownership of all main NPCs in the game. Additionally, we utilized the existing Pokémon knowledge graph, which encompasses detailed information about individual Pokémon, their attributes, evolutions, and relationships. This data forms the foundation for creating AI dialogue that can utilize knowledge-based to enhance player immersion.

Knowledge Graph Triples:
From the dataset and ontology developed in previous steps, we have constructed small knowledge graphs tailored to FFVIIR and Pokémon game. These knowledge graphs are designed to store and organize the information about non-player characters (NPCs) and their associated Pokémon, facilitating efficient information retrieval and enhancing the interaction experience in gameplay.

Related works

Dialogue and Text Generation in Games:
Text-based games have proven to be excellent test beds for the development of agents operating within linguistic frameworks. As these games involve interactions such as input observations and action commands that are entirely text-driven, they demand a thorough understanding of language to facilitate successful gameplay. Prior studies have explored various text generation techniques in such gaming environments. For instance,
Fulda et al. 2017
utilized Word2vec embeddings to introduce inference capabilities, enabling the generated text to learn associations between suitable verbs and objects. Further research
Urbanek et al. 2019
extended this approach by training BERT
Devlin et al. 2018
to produce contextually relevant dialogue in action and fantasy game settings.

Knowledge Graph enhancing Text Generation:
Knowledge graphs have become an reliable resource for knowledge-grounded conversation across various research domains
Tuan et al. 2019
;
Kang et al. 2022
. They are instrumental in enhancing dialogue generation in fields such as medicine
Varshney et al. 2023
, question-answering systems
Indurthi et al. 2017
, and gaming
Ashby et al. 2023
. By integrating KGs, these systems are able to leverage structured knowledge to improve the relevance and accuracy of generated text, significantly boosting performance.

Generation in Text-based Games using LLMs:
Large language models (LLMs) have been increasingly employed to enhance and innovate text-based games.
Ammanabrolu et al. 2019
leveraged Markov chains alongside neural language models to dynamically generate quests in AI Dungeon 2
Walton 2020
, expanding gameplay complexity and narrative depth. Building on this, a recent approach outlined in
Sudhakaran et al. 2023
introduced MarioGPT, an LLM-based text-to-level model that enables the creation of diverse game levels tailored to different play styles, further demonstrating the adaptability of LLMs in gaming. Additionally, in the realm of narrative generation,
Kumaran et al. 2023
proposed a novel application of LLMs for generating narrative scenes in digital games, enhancing the storytelling aspect significantly. These advancements highlight the transformative potential of LLMs in redefining gaming experiences by providing rich, adaptive, and personalized content

5
Conclusion

In this study, we have explored the capacity of GPT-4 to accurately emulate game characters and provide responses that align with their personalities and situational contexts. By focusing on main characters of two popular game Final Fantasy VII and Pokémon, we investigated how well GPT-4 can maintain character consistency and utilize in-game knowledge when generating dialogue.

Our qualitative suggests that GPT-4 can maintain a high degree of character consistency. Additionally, the task of highlighting knowledge-based and situational responses has provided further insights into the model’s ability to integrate specific game details into its responses.

Future steps involve refining the model through supervised fine-tuning to better align GPT-4’s responses with both of the main characters personality traits. Furthermore, we aim to extend our evaluation methods to human evaluation and apply it to other characters and dialogue generation tasks within various game contexts. These efforts will help enhance the interactive experience for players, ensuring that AI-generated dialogues remain immersive and true to character.

Overall, this study demonstrates the potential of GPT-4 in enhancing character-driven interactions in gaming, paving the way for more sophisticated and engaging LLMs-driven dialogue systems.

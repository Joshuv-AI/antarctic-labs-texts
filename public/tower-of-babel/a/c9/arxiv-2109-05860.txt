On the prime graph of a finite group with unique nonabelian composition factor Thanks: This work was supported by the Program of Fundamental Scientific Researches of the Siberian Branch of Russian Academy of Sciences, I.1.1, project 0314-2019-0001
Maria A. Grechkoseeva, Andrey V. Vasil’ev

Abstract
. We say that finite groups are isospectral if they have the same sets of orders of elements. It is known that every nonsolvable finite group G G isospectral to a finite simple group has a unique nonabelian composition factor, that is, the quotient of G G by the solvable radical of G G is an almost simple group. The main goal of this paper is prove that this almost simple group is a cyclic extension of its socle. To this end, we consider a general situation when G G is an arbitrary group with unique nonabelian composition factor, not necessarily isospectral to a simple group, and study the prime graph of G G , where the prime graph of G G is the graph whose vertices are the prime numbers dividing the order of G G and two such numbers r r and s s are adjacent if and only if r ≠ s r\neq s and G G has an element of order r ​ s rs . Namely, we establish some sufficient conditions for the prime graph of such a group to have a vertex adjacent to all other vertices. Besides proving the main result, this allows us to refine a recent result by P. Cameron and N. Maslova concerning finite groups almost recognizable by prime graph. Keywords: almost simple group, group of Lie type, order of an element, recognition by spectrum, prime graph

(1.1)

1\leqslant K<H\leqslant G,

is an almost simple group with socle

, and then

. Observe that

If

Theorem 1.

Let

. Suppose that

is a simple group of Lie type other than

, then

does not contain diagonal automorphisms.

If

is a group of Lie type, the proof has several ingredients. The first is the well-known property of spectra of groups of Lie type stated in Lemma

[
18
]

Theorem 2.

Suppose that a finite group

has a normal series

, where

is the solvable radical of
G
,

is a finite simple group of Lie type, and

(i)

If

contains a diagonal
automorphism of

(ii)

Theorem 3.

There exists a function

such that for each labeled graph

, the following conditions are equivalent:

there exist infinitely many groups

such that

there exist more than

groups

such that

is the set of the vertices of

In fact, Theorem 1.4 of

3.
Groups almost recognizable by prime graph

Given a positive integer

and almost recognizable by prime graph if it is

-recognizable for some

By

is almost simple and each group

is almost simple. So if

is a

-recognizable group, then

is at most the number of almost simple groups

such that

By
[

, this number is at most
O(|\pi(G)|^{7})
. A direct corollary of this discussion is the following theorem.

Theorem A 0
[
1
, Theorem 1.4]

There exists a function
F(x)=O(x^{7})
such that for each labeled graph

there exist infinitely many groups
H
such that

(ii)

there exist more then
F(|V(\Gamma)|)
groups
H
such that
GK(H)=\Gamma
, where

is the set of the vertices of
\Gamma
.

It is clear that estimating

we do not need to calculate all almost simple groups

such that

. It is sufficient to calculate those

whose prime graph satisfies some necessary conditions for

A Simple Proof of the Johnson–Lindenstrauss Extension Theorem
Manor Mendel

Abstract
. Johnson and Lindenstrauss proved that any Lipschitz mapping from an n n -point subset of a metric space into Hilbert space can be extended to the whole space, while increasing the Lipschitz constant by a factor of O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) O(\sqrt{\log n}) . We present a simplification of their argument that avoids dimension reduction and the Kirszbraun theorem.



The Lipschitz constant of a mapping
f:T\to Y
between metric spaces is defined as

\mathrm{Lip}(f)=\sup_{x\neq y\in T}d_{Y}(f(x),f(y))/d_{T}(x,y)
.
The following is a Lipschitz extension theorem due to Johnson and Lindenstrauss
[
2
]
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
T
be an arbitrary
n
-point metric space, and
X\supset T
an arbitrary superspace. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space and
f:T\to\mathcal{H}
a mapping . Then there exists an extension
F:X\to\mathcal{H}
of
f
such that
F|_{T}=f
, and
\mathrm{Lip}(F)\lesssim\sqrt{\log n}\cdot\mathrm{Lip}(f)
.

As was pointed out in
[
3
]
, Theorem
1
also follows from Ball’s
extension theorem
[
1
]
used in conjunction with the Markov type 2 estimates in
[
4
]
.
The proof of Theorem
1
in
[
2
]
introduced the celebrated
Johnson–Lindenstrauss lemma about dimension reduction of finite point sets in Hilbert space.
In this note we give a simple argument which is a reinterpretation of the original from
[
2
]

that uses neither dimension reduction nor the Kirszbraun extension theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We may assume without loss of generality that
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.,

f:T\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Furthermore, assume that by rescaling
\mathrm{Lip}(f)=1
.
Let
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}
be i.i.d. standard Gaussian random variables defined on a probability space
\Omega
, and consider the real-valued Hilbert space

H=L_{2}(\Omega)
.

Consider the linear mapping
k:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to H
defined by

k(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}g_{i}.

It is a property of the Gaussian distribution that if
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}
are independent standard Gaussian variables, then
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}g_{i}
is a centered Gaussian random variable with variance
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2}
, which means that

\|\sum_{i}x_{i}g_{i}\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}=\|(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\|_{2}
.
Hence
k
is an isometric
isomorphism of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in
k(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset H
.
Thus,
u:=k\circ f:T\to k(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is also 1-Lipschitz.
We will use the notation
u=(u_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
, and
k=(k_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
.

Fix
\omega\in\Omega
.
Since
u_{\omega}:T\to\mathbb{R}
,
by the McShane extension theorem (also known as the nonlinear Hahn–Banach theorem),

u_{\omega}
can be extended to
U_{\omega}:X\to\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}

such that
U_{\omega}|_{T}=u_{\omega}
, and

\mathrm{Lip}(U_{\omega})=\mathrm{Lip}(u_{\omega})
.
The McShane extension is a simple result.
For completeness, we provide a quick proof at the end of this note, see also
[
5
, Theorem 1.33]
.
We thus obtain
U:=(U_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}:X\to H
, an extension of
u
.
Using the orthogonal projection
P:H\to k(\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
,
we see that
F:=k^{-1}\circ P\circ U:X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an extension of
f
. Since

\mathrm{Lip}(k^{-1})=\mathrm{Lip}(P)=1
, we have
\mathrm{Lip}(F)\leq\mathrm{Lip}(U)
.
We next bound
\mathrm{Lip}(U)
. For
x,y\in X
:

\displaystyle\|U(x)-U(y)\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\Bigl(\mathbb{E}_{\Omega}\bigl[|U_{\omega}(x)-U_{\omega}(y)|^{2}\bigr]\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq d(x,y)\Bigl(\mathbb{E}_{\Omega}[\mathrm{Lip}(U_{\omega})^{2}]\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=d(x,y)\Bigl(\mathbb{E}_{\Omega}[\mathrm{Lip}(u_{\omega})^{2}]\Bigr)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=d(x,y)\left(\mathbb{E}_{\Omega}\max_{s\neq t\in T}\Bigl(\frac{\sum_{i}f(s)_{i}g_{i}(\omega)-\sum_{i}f(t)_{i}g_{i}(\omega)}{d(s,t)}\Bigr)^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq d(x,y)\biggl(\mathbb{E}_{\Omega}\max_{s\neq t\in T}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{f(s)_{i}-f(t)_{i}}{\|f(s)-f(t)\|_{2}}g_{i}(\omega)\Bigr)^{2}\biggr)^{1/2}

(1)

\displaystyle=d(x,y)\Bigl(\mathbb{E}_{\Omega}\max_{s\neq t\in T}G_{f(s)-f(t)}^{2}\Bigr)^{1/2},

where
G_{z}:=\sum_{i}\frac{z_{i}}{\|z\|_{2}}g_{i}
.
\{G_{f(s)-f(t)}\}_{s,t\in T}
are standard Gaussians (but not necessarily stochastically independent).
It is a classical and elementary fact that for
m\geq 2
standard Gaussians
G_{1},\ldots,G_{m}
, with any dependence structure,

(2)

\mathbb{E}[\max\{G_{1}^{2},\ldots,G_{m}^{2}\}]\lesssim\log m.

For completeness, we provide a quick proof of (
2
) below.
Since
|T|=n
, the maximum in (
1
) is over at most
n^{2}
squared Gaussians, and therefore (
1
) is bounded above by a constant times
d(x,y)\cdot\sqrt{\log n}
.
∎

Proof of (
2
).

By a classical Gaussian tail bound,
\Pr[G_{i}\geq t]\leq e^{-t^{2}/2}
, and therefore
\Pr[G_{i}^{2}\geq t]\leq 2e^{-t/2}
. By the union bound,

\Pr[\max\{G_{1}^{2},\ldots,G_{m}^{2}\}\geq t]\leq 2me^{-t/2}
.
For any
s\geq 0
, we can estimate
\mathbb{E}[\max\{G_{1}^{2},\ldots,G_{m}^{2}\}]

by partitioning the integral up to parameter
s\geq 0
and from
s
to
\infty
as follows:

\mathbb{E}[\max\{G_{1}^{2},\ldots,G_{m}^{2}\}]\leq s+\int_{s}^{\infty}\Pr\left[\max\{G_{1}^{2},\ldots,G_{m}^{2}\}\geq t\right]dt\\
\leq s+\int_{s}^{\infty}2me^{-t/2}dt=s+4me^{-s/2}.

Substituting
s=2\log m
(and noting that
m\geq 2
)
proves (
2
).
∎

Proof of McShane extension for finite domains.

Let
T\subset X
,
T
finite, and
q:T\to\mathbb{R}

L
-Lipschitz.
Define
Q:X\to\mathbb{R}
by

Q(x)=\min_{t\in T}q(t)+Ld_{X}(x,t)
.
It is left to the reader to check that
Q|_{T}=q
using the Lipschitz condition on
q
.
Next we prove the Lipshitz condition for
Q
. Fix
x,y\in X
, and let
t\in T

satisfy
Q(x)=q(t)+Ld_{X}(x,t)
. Applying the definition of
Q(y)
, and the triangle inequality in
X
, we have

Q(y)\leq q(t)+Ld_{X}(y,t)\leq q(t)+Ld_{X}(x,t)+Ld_{X}(x,y)=Q(x)+Ld_{X}(x,y).

Hence
Q(y)-Q(x)\leq Ld_{X}(x,y)
. By symmetry, we also have

Q(x)-Q(y)\leq Ld(x,y)
.
∎

Stably semiorthogonally indecomposable varieties
Dmitrii Pirozhkov

Abstract




Abstract.
A triangulated category is said to be indecomposable if it admits no nontrivial semiorthogonal decompositions. We introduce a definition of a noncommutatively stably semiorthogonally indecomposable (NSSI) scheme. This property implies, among other things, that each connected closed subscheme has indecomposable derived category of coherent sheaves and that if
Y
is NSSI, then for any variety
X
all semiorthogonal decompositions of
X\times Y
are induced from decompositions of
X
. We prove that any scheme which admits an affine morphism to an abelian variety is NSSI and that the total space of a fibration over a NSSI base with NSSI fibers is also NSSI. We apply this indecomposability to deduce that there are no phantom subcategories in some varieties, including surfaces
C\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, where
C
is any smooth proper curve of positive genus.

Keywords.
Semiorthogonal decomposition, phantom subcategories

1. Introduction

The derived category of coherent sheaves on an algebraic variety is an interesting but complicated invariant. It is a triangulated category, and sometimes it can be built out of smaller triangulated categories using the notion of a semiorthogonal decomposition. One basic question is to identify which smooth proper varieties have indecomposable derived categories,
i.e.
, admit no nontrivial semiorthogonal decompositions. Examples of such varieties are Calabi–Yau varieties, see
[
Bri99
]
, curves of positive genus, see
[
Oka11
]
, or more generally varieties with a globally generated canonical bundle, see
[
KO15
]
.

We propose a stronger notion of indecomposability for derived categories of algebraic varieties. Let
Y
be an algebraic variety over a field
\Bbbk
. Our definition is a constraint on possible semiorthogonal decompositions for categories equipped with the action of the symmetric monoidal category
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
of perfect complexes on
Y
. We work in the formalism of stable
\infty
-categories as in
[
Lur
]
, but we mostly follow the exposition given in
[
Per19
]
.

Roughly speaking, a
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
-linear structure on a
\Bbbk
-linear stable
\infty
-category
\mathfrak{D}
consists of an
action functor

a_{\mathfrak{D}}\colon\mathfrak{D}\otimes_{\Bbbk}\operatorname{Perf}(Y)\to\mathfrak{D}
which is associative up to an isomorphism, together with higher associativity data. When there is no risk of confusion, for objects
D\in\mathfrak{D}
and
S\in\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
, we denote the result of the action
a_{\mathfrak{D}}(D\otimes S)\in\mathfrak{D}
by
D\cdot S
. For the duration of this paper, the term
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
-linear category
means a
\Bbbk
-linear stable
\infty
-category with the
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
-linear structure as above. A basic example of a
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
-linear category comes from geometry: if
f\colon X\to Y
is a morphism of schemes, then
\operatorname{Perf}(X)
is a
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
-linear category with the action
D\cdot S:=D\otimes_{k}f^{*}S
.

We need a couple of preliminary definitions.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
Y
be a scheme over the field
\Bbbk
. Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
-linear category, and let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
be a strictly full triangulated
\Bbbk
-linear subcategory. For a subset
\mathcal{S}\subset\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
, we say that
\mathcal{A}
is closed under the action of
\mathcal{S}
if for any object
S\in\mathcal{S}
and any object
A\in\mathcal{A}
, the result of the action
A\cdot S
in
\mathfrak{D}
is an object in the subcategory
\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 1.2

(
cf.

[
BK90
]
)
.

Let
T
be a triangulated category. A strictly full triangulated subcategory
\mathcal{A}\subset T
is a
left admissible subcategory
if the inclusion functor
\mathcal{A}\hookrightarrow T
has a left adjoint functor.

Now we can state the main definition of this paper. For the notion of a linear category proper over the base, see
[
Per19
, Lemma 4.7]
. As an example, if
f\colon X\to Y
is a proper perfect morphism of schemes, then
\operatorname{Perf}(X)
is a proper
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
-linear category by
[
Per19
, Lemma 4.9]
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
Y
be a scheme over the field
\Bbbk
. We say that
Y
is
noncommutatively stably semiorthogonally indecomposable
, or NSSI for brevity, if for arbitrary choices of

(1)

\mathfrak{D}
, a
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
-linear category which is proper over
Y
and has a classical generator,

(2)

\mathcal{A}
, a left admissible subcategory of
\mathfrak{D}
,

the subcategory
\mathcal{A}
is closed under the action of
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
on
\mathfrak{D}
in the sense of Definition
1.1
.

Remark
.

The same definition may be given for an arbitrary symmetric monoidal stable
\infty
-category, not necessarily of the form
(\operatorname{Perf}(Y),\otimes)
. The author does not know whether this would be useful. Note that the variety
Y
is completely determined by the monoidal structure on
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
, see
[
Bal02
]
, so the definition above is a property of the variety
Y
, not of something weaker.

When
T
is a triangulated category of perfect complexes on a scheme, a subcategory
\mathcal{A}\subset T
is left admissible if and only if there exists a semiorthogonal decomposition of
T
which has
\mathcal{A}
as the first component; see
[
BK90
]
. Using this relation, in Lemma
2.13
we confirm that the condition in Definition
1.3
implies the indecomposability in the usual sense;
i.e.
, all semiorthogonal decompositions of
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
are trivial. We also show in Example
2.16
that stable indecomposability is a strictly stronger condition than indecomposability. In particular, it implies that the derived category of sheaves on any smooth proper subvariety is also indecomposable.

The main results of this paper are the following two theorems.

Theorem 1.4

( = Theorem
3.5
)
.

Let
Y
be a scheme over a field
\Bbbk
. If
Y
admits an affine morphism to an abelian variety over
\Bbbk
, then
Y
is NSSI.

Theorem 1.5

( = Theorem
4.1
)
.

Let
\pi\colon Y\to B
be a flat proper morphism of quasi-compact separated schemes over a field
\Bbbk
. Assume that
B
is NSSI and that for any closed point
b\in B
, the fiber
Y_{b}:=\pi^{-1}(b)
is a NSSI scheme.
Then
Y
is NSSI.

We use these results to deduce the nonexistence of phantom subcategories in some varieties. Recall that a nonzero admissible subcategory
\mathcal{A}\subset T
of a triangulated category
T
is called a
phantom subcategory
if the class of any object of
\mathcal{A}
in the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(T)
is zero. In general, such subcategories exist, but it is expected that they should not appear in many simple cases.

Proposition 1.6

( = Proposition
5.4
)
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Let
Y
be a smooth projective variety over
\Bbbk
which is NSSI.

(1)

Let
X
be either the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
or a del Pezzo surface. Then there are no phantom subcategories in the derived category
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X\times Y)
.

(2)

Let
\pi\colon\mathfrak{X}\to Y
be an étale-locally trivial fibration with fiber
\mathbb{P}^{1}
or
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Then there are no phantom subcategories in the derived category
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(\mathfrak{X})
.

Structure of the paper

In Section
2
, we recall various notions useful in the rest of the paper and establish some basic properties of NSSI varieties. In Section
3
, we prove a general rigidity statement for admissible subcategories and deduce Theorem
1.4
from it. In Section
4
, we prove Theorem
1.5
. Finally, in Section
5
, we prove Proposition
1.6
.

Notation and conventions

All triangulated categories and varieties in this paper are over a field
\Bbbk
. Tensor products, pullbacks, and pushforwards are assumed to be derived. For a subcategory
\mathcal{A}
and an object
F
in some category the symbol
F\otimes\mathcal{A}
denotes the set of all tensor products
F\otimes A
for objects
A\in\mathcal{A}
.

2. Preliminaries

2.1. Background on semiorthogonal decompositions and generators

We recall the notion and basic properties of semiorthogonal decompositions from
[
BK90
]
. For a triangulated category
T
, a
semiorthogonal decomposition

T=\langle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\rangle
is a pair of strictly full triangulated subcategories
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
such that for any
A\in\mathcal{A}
and any
B\in\mathcal{B}
, the graded
\operatorname{Hom}
-space
\operatorname{RHom}(B,A)
vanishes, and such that for any object
E\in T
there exists a distinguished triangle

B\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow A,

where
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
B\in\mathcal{B}
. We call this the
projection triangle
of
E
. It is unique up to a unique isomorphism.

When
T=\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
is a triangulated category of perfect complexes on a scheme
Y
over a field
\Bbbk
, in any semiorthogonal decomposition, the component
\mathcal{A}\subset T
is left admissible,
i.e.
, its inclusion functor into
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
admits a left adjoint, and similarly
\mathcal{B}
is right admissible.
The adjoint functors are called the left and the right projection functors, respectively. Moreover, if
\mathcal{A}\subset D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(Y)
is a left admissible subcategory, then the category

{}^{\perp}\mathcal{A}:=\{B\in D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(Y)\,\,|\,\,\forall A\in\mathcal{A},\,\,\operatorname{RHom}(B,A)=0\}

is right admissible, and there is a semiorthogonal decomposition
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(Y)=\langle\mathcal{A},{}^{\perp}\mathcal{A}\rangle
.

The derived category
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(Y)
is said to be
indecomposable
if for any semiorthogonal decomposition
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(Y)=\langle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\rangle
, either
\mathcal{A}=0
, or
\mathcal{B}=0
. Equivalently, the only admissible subcategories of
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(Y)
are zero and the
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(Y)
.

We also need the notion of a classical generator of a category.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category. An object
G\in\mathcal{T}
is called a
classical generator
of
\mathcal{T}
if the smallest triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
containing
G
and closed under taking direct summands is the whole
\mathcal{T}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
be an affine morphism of schemes. If
G\in\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
is a classical generator of
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
, then its pullback
f^{*}G
is a classical generator of
\operatorname{Perf}(Y^{\prime})
.

Proof.

By
[
BVdB03
, Theorem 2.1.2]
, a perfect complex
f^{*}G
is a classical generator for
\operatorname{Perf}(Y^{\prime})
if and only if for any nonzero
\mathcal{F}\in D_{\operatorname{QCoh}}(Y^{\prime})
, the space
\operatorname{Hom}_{Y^{\prime}}(f^{*}G,\mathcal{F})
is nonzero. By adjunction, there is an isomorphism

(2.1)

\operatorname{Hom}_{Y^{\prime}}(f^{*}G,\mathcal{F})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{Y}(G,f_{*}\mathcal{F}).

Since
f
is an affine morphism, the pushforward
f_{*}\mathcal{F}
of a nonzero object is nonzero. Then applying
[
BVdB03
, Theorem 2.1.2]
on the variety
Y
, we see that the right-hand space in (
2.1
) is nonzero, and hence
f^{*}G
is a classical generator.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a triangulated category, and let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
be a left admissible subcategory. If an object
E\in\mathfrak{D}
is a classical generator of the subcategory
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{A}
, then for any object
F\in\mathfrak{D}
, the vanishing
\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathfrak{D}}(E,F)=0
holds if and only if
F\in\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{A}
is a left admissible subcategory, it induces a semiorthogonal decomposition
\mathfrak{D}=\langle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\rangle
, where
\mathcal{B}
is the orthogonal subcategory
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
is a right admissible
subcategory; see
[
Bon89
, Lemma 3.1]
.

Consider the projection triangle for the object
F
:

B\longrightarrow F\longrightarrow A,

where
B\in\mathcal{B}
and
A\in\mathcal{A}
.
An application of the functor
\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathfrak{D}}(E,-)
to it results in a distinguished triangle of graded vector spaces:

\operatorname{RHom}(E,B)\longrightarrow\operatorname{RHom}(E,F)\longrightarrow\operatorname{RHom}(E,A).

Here the rightmost object
\operatorname{RHom}(E,A)
is zero since
E\in\mathcal{B}={}^{\perp}\mathcal{A}
. Thus
\operatorname{RHom}(E,F)=0
if and only if
\operatorname{RHom}(E,B)=0
. But the object
B
lies in
\mathcal{B}
, and
E
is a classical generator of that subcategory. Since any classical generator is a generator, see
[
BVdB03
, Equation (2.1)]
, the vanishing of
\operatorname{RHom}(E,B)
implies that
B
is zero,
i.e.
, that the object
F\simeq A
lies in
\mathcal{A}
, as claimed.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a triangulated category which admits a classical generator. Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
be a left admissible or right admissible subcategory. Then
\mathcal{A}
has a classical generator.

Proof.

Let
G\in\mathfrak{D}
be a classical generator. For any essentially surjective functor
\Phi\colon\mathfrak{D}\to\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
between triangulated categories, it is easy to see that
\Phi(G)
is a classical generator in
\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}
. By assumption, the inclusion functor
\mathcal{A}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{D}
has either a left or a right adjoint functor, which is a projection and hence surjective on objects. Thus
\mathcal{A}
has a classical generator.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a triangulated category which admits a classical generator. Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
be a left admissible subcategory. Then there exists an object
E\in\mathfrak{D}
such that an object
F\in\mathfrak{D}
lies in
\mathcal{A}
if and only if
\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathfrak{D}}(E,F)=0
.

Proof.

By
[
Bon89
, Lemma 3.1]
, the subcategory
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
is right admissible. Then by Lemma
2.4
, it has a classical generator. So we conclude by Lemma
2.3
.
∎

The proof of Theorem
4.1
uses some technical properties of linear categories. We discuss them in the two subsections below.

2.2. Linearity for subcategories closed under the action

Let
Y
be a scheme over the field
\Bbbk
. Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
-linear category. Assume that
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
is a strictly full subcategory that is closed under the action of
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
on
\mathfrak{D}
in the sense of Definition
1.1
. Then it would be reasonable to expect that
\mathcal{A}
has the structure of a
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
-linear category on its own. We use this structure in the proof of Theorem
1.5
. To define the structure, we need to construct some higher associativity data on
\mathcal{A}
. The next proposition shows that this can be done in a canonical way if the subcategory is left admissible (or right admissible, with an analogous proof). The result essentially follows from the definition of an action of a symmetric monoidal
\infty
-category given in
[
Lur
]
. Since we were not able to find this statement in the literature, we give a sketch of the proof.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
Y
be a scheme over the field
\Bbbk
. Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
-linear category, and let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
be a left admissible
\Bbbk
-linear subcategory. Assume that
\mathcal{A}
is closed under the action of
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
on
\mathfrak{D}
. Then the subcategory
\mathcal{A}
is canonically equipped with the structure of a
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
-linear category, and the inclusion functor
\mathcal{A}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{D}
has a canonical lift to a
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
-linear functor between
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
-linear categories.

The structure of a
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
-action on the stable
\infty
-category

together with higher associativity data. As a first step, we will define the action functor on
\mathcal{A}
and check that it agrees with the action functor on
\mathfrak{D}
.

2.3. Some properties of linear categories

For the proof of Theorem
1.5
, we need some properties of linear categories. They are more or less formal consequences of definitions as given in
[
Lur
]
or
[
Per19
]
.

First, we recall the notion of a mapping object and its properties.

Definition 2.7

(
cf.

[
Lur09
]
)
.

Let
Y
be a scheme, and let
\mathfrak{D}
be a
Y
-linear category. Let
E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathfrak{D}
be two objects. An object

\mathcal{H}\in\operatorname{Perf}(Y)

is called a
mapping object
between
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
if it satisfies the following universal property:

\operatorname{RHom}_{Y}(-,\mathcal{H})\cong\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathfrak{D}}((-)\cdot E_{1},E_{2}).

If a mapping object exists, it is unique and is denoted by
\mathcal{H}om_{\mathfrak{D}}(E_{1},E_{2})\in\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
.

Remark
.

In more general contexts, a mapping object is usually defined as an object in the larger category
D_{\operatorname{QCoh}}(Y)
, not necessarily in
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
. As shown by the following lemma, in all situations we encounter in this paper, the mapping object is perfect.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
Y
be a quasi-compact separated scheme over a field
\Bbbk
, and let
\mathfrak{D}
be a
Y
-linear
category which is proper over
Y
. Then for any two objects
E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathfrak{D}
, the mapping object
\mathcal{H}om(E_{1},E_{2})\in\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
exists.

Proof.

The mapping object exists by
[
Lur09
, Proposition 5.5.2.2]
as an object in
D_{\operatorname{QCoh}}(Y)
, and it is a perfect object by
[
Per19
, Lemma 4.7]
.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
Y
be a quasi-compact separated scheme over a field
\Bbbk
. Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a
Y
-linear category which is proper over
Y
. Let
E_{1},E_{2}\in\mathfrak{D}
be two objects. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{H}om(E_{1},E_{2})\in\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
is a zero object.

(2)

E_{2}\in\langle\operatorname{Perf}(Y)\cdot E_{1}\rangle^{\perp}
.

(3)

The orbit
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)\cdot E_{2}
lies in
E_{1}^{\perp}
.

Proof.

This follows directly from Definition
2.7
and the adjunction isomorphism

\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathfrak{D}}(F\cdot E_{1},E_{2})\cong\operatorname{RHom}_{\mathfrak{D}}(E_{1},F^{\scriptstyle\vee}\cdot E_{2})

for any object
F\in\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
.
∎

We continue with a discussion of base change for linear categories.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a morphism of schemes such that the pushforward
f_{*}
sends perfect complexes on
X
to perfect complexes on
Y
. Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a
Y
-linear category, and let
\mathfrak{D}_{X}:=\mathfrak{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{Perf}(Y)}\operatorname{Perf}(X)
be its base change along the morphism
f
. Then we define the pushforward and pullback functors between
\mathfrak{D}
and
\mathfrak{D}_{X}
as follows:

\displaystyle f_{\mathfrak{D},*}\colon\mathfrak{D}_{X}\cong\mathfrak{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{Perf}(Y)}\operatorname{Perf}(X)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}\otimes f_{*}}\mathfrak{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{Perf}(Y)}\operatorname{Perf}(Y)\cong\mathfrak{D},

\displaystyle f_{\mathfrak{D}}^{*}\colon\mathfrak{D}\cong\mathfrak{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{Perf}(Y)}\operatorname{Perf}(Y)\xrightarrow{\;\mathrm{id}\otimes f^{*}\!}\mathfrak{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{Perf}(Y)}\operatorname{Perf}(X)\cong\mathfrak{D}_{X}.

To simplify the notation, we often refer to these functors as just
f_{*}
and
f^{*}
when there is no risk of confusion.

Remark
.

The condition that the pushforward sends perfect complexes to perfect complexes is satisfied, for example, for a flat proper morphism or for a proper morphism to a regular variety.

For the future use, we record two properties of these functors.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a morphism of schemes such that the pushforward
f_{*}
sends perfect complexes on
X
to perfect complexes on
Y
. Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a
Y
-linear category, and let
\mathfrak{D}_{X}:=\mathfrak{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{Perf}(Y)}\operatorname{Perf}(X)
be its base change along the morphism
f
.

(1)

For any objects
D\in\mathfrak{D}
and
E\in\operatorname{Perf}(X)
, the following variant of the projection formula holds in
\mathfrak{D}
:

(2.4)

f_{*}(E)\cdot D\cong f_{\mathfrak{D},*}(E\cdot f_{\mathfrak{D}}^{*}(D)),

where on the left-hand side, we use the
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
-action on
\mathfrak{D}
and on the right-hand side we use the
\operatorname{Perf}(X)
-action on
\widetilde{\mathfrak{D}}
.

(2)

If
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
is a
Y
-linear left admissible subcategory and
\mathcal{A}_{X}:=\mathcal{A}\otimes_{\operatorname{Perf}(Y)}\operatorname{Perf}(X)\subset\mathfrak{D}_{X}
is its base change along the morphism
f
, then

f_{\mathfrak{D}}^{*}(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{A}_{X}
and
f_{\mathfrak{D},*}(\mathcal{A}_{X})\subset\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

This follows directly from Definition
2.10
.
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
f\colon X\to Y
be a morphism of varieties such that the pushforward
f_{*}
sends perfect complexes on
X
to perfect complexes on
Y
. Suppose that
f
fits into a Cartesian square of schemes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X^{\prime}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Y^{\prime}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\lrcorner}

such that the morphism
g
is flat.

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a
Y
-linear category. It can be considered as a category linear over
Y^{\prime}
via the morphism
g\colon Y\to Y^{\prime}
. Let
\mathfrak{D}_{X}
be the base change of
\mathfrak{D}/Y
along the morphism
f
, and let
\mathfrak{D}_{X^{\prime}}
be the base change of
\mathfrak{D}/Y^{\prime}
along the morphism
f^{\prime}
. Then

(1)

there is a natural equivalence of
X^{\prime}
-linear categories
\mathfrak{D}_{X}\cong\mathfrak{D}_{X^{\prime}}
;

(2)

that equivalence identifies the functors
f_{\mathfrak{D},*}
and
f_{\mathfrak{D}}^{*}
with the functors
f_{\mathfrak{D}^{\prime},*}
and
f_{\mathfrak{D}^{\prime}}^{*}
, respectively.

Proof.

According to
[
Per19
, Section 2.3]
, the base change
\mathfrak{D}_{X}
of the category
\mathfrak{D}
is obtained as a tensor product
\mathfrak{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{Perf}(Y)}\operatorname{Perf}(X)
. Since the morphism
g
is flat, by
[
Per19
, Equation (2.1)]
, the derived fiber product
Y\times^{L}_{Y^{\prime}}X^{\prime}
is isomorphic to the usual geometric fiber product
X
, which means, explicitly, that

\operatorname{Perf}(X)\cong\operatorname{Perf}(Y)\otimes_{\operatorname{Perf}(Y^{\prime})}\operatorname{Perf}(X^{\prime}).

Thus we get a natural equivalence of categories

(2.5)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{X}:=\mathfrak{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{Perf}(Y)}\operatorname{Perf}(X)\cong\mathfrak{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{Perf}(Y)}\left(\operatorname{Perf}(Y)\otimes_{\operatorname{Perf}(Y^{\prime})}\operatorname{Perf}(X^{\prime})\right)\cong

\displaystyle\cong\mathfrak{D}\otimes_{\operatorname{Perf}(Y^{\prime})}\operatorname{Perf}(X^{\prime})=:\mathfrak{D}_{X^{\prime}}.

It is easy to check that this equivalence is
\operatorname{Perf}(X^{\prime})
-linear and that it respects the pushforward and the pullback functors.
∎

2.4. Basic properties of NSSI schemes

Now we start working with the notion of NSSI schemes, introduced in Definition
1.3
.

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
Y
be a connected scheme over a field
\Bbbk
. If
Y
is a NSSI scheme, then
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
is an indecomposable category.

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)=\langle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\rangle
be a semiorthogonal decomposition. Consider
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
as a category linear over itself, with the action given by tensor product. Then from Definition
1.3
, we deduce that both
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are closed under tensor products by arbitrary objects of
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
. In particular, for any two objects
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
B\in\mathcal{B}
we get
A\otimes B\in\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{B}=0
, which implies
\operatorname{supp}(A)\cap\operatorname{supp}(B)=\emptyset
, where
\operatorname{supp}
denotes the union of set-theoretic supports of cohomology sheaves of the complex. It follows that
\operatorname{RHom}(A,B)=0
, and hence the decomposition
\langle\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\rangle
is bidirectionally orthogonal, which on a connected scheme implies that one of the components is zero; see
[
Bri99
, Example 3.2]
.
∎

Lemma 2.14
.

Let
f\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
be an affine morphism between schemes. If
Y
is a quasi-compact separated NSSI scheme, then
Y^{\prime}
is also a quasi-compact separated NSSI scheme.

Proof.

Since
Y
is a quasi-compact separated scheme, by
[
BVdB03
, Theorem 3.1.1]
, there exists a classical generator
G\in\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
. The morphism
f
is affine, so by Lemma
2.2
, the pullback object
f^{*}G\in\operatorname{Perf}(Y^{\prime})
is a classical generator in
\operatorname{Perf}(Y^{\prime})
.

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a
\operatorname{Perf}(Y^{\prime})
-linear category. By definition, we need to check that any admissible subcategory
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
is closed under the action of
\operatorname{Perf}(Y^{\prime})
. Since
\mathfrak{D}
can also be considered as a
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
-linear category, by the assumption that
Y
is NSSI, we know that the subcategory
\mathcal{A}
is closed under the action of the subset
\operatorname{im}\,(f^{*}\colon\operatorname{Perf}(Y)\to\operatorname{Perf}(Y^{\prime}))
. In particular,
\mathcal{A}
is closed under the action of the classical generator
f^{*}G\in\operatorname{Perf}(Y^{\prime})
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}
be an object. Consider the subcategory
\mathcal{C}\subset\operatorname{Perf}(Y^{\prime})
consisting of those objects
C\in\operatorname{Perf}(Y^{\prime})
such that the result of the action
A\cdot C
belongs to
\mathcal{A}
. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{C}
is a triangulated subcategory closed under taking direct summands. Since it contains the classical generator
f^{*}G
, it coincides with the whole
\operatorname{Perf}(Y^{\prime})
. This holds for an arbitrary object
A\in\mathcal{A}
; hence
\mathcal{A}
is closed under the action of
\operatorname{Perf}(Y^{\prime})
on
\mathfrak{D}
. This shows that
Y^{\prime}
is a NSSI scheme.
∎

Corollary 2.15
.

Let
Y
be a quasi-compact separated scheme which is NSSI. Then for any connected closed subscheme
Y^{\prime}\subset Y
, the category
\operatorname{Perf}(Y^{\prime})
is indecomposable.

Proof.

The inclusion
Y^{\prime}\subset Y
is a finite morphism. By Lemma
2.14
, this implies that
Y^{\prime}
is NSSI, and hence by Lemma
2.13
, the category
\operatorname{Perf}(Y^{\prime})
is indecomposable.
∎

Example 2.16
.

Let
Y
be a K3 surface containing a smooth rational curve
C\subset Y
. Then the derived category
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(Y)
is indecomposable, but
Y
is not NSSI. Indecomposability follows from
[
Bri99
, Example 3.2]
since the canonical bundle of
Y
is trivial and hence by Serre duality any semiorthogonal decomposition is completely orthogonal. However,
Y
is not NSSI by Corollary
2.15
: the curve
C\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}
in
Y
admits a nontrivial semiorthogonal decomposition
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(\mathbb{P}^{1})=\langle\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O}(1)\rangle
.

3. NSSI property via rigidity of admissible subcategories

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.4
. The main ingredient in the proof is a general rigidity property for admissible subcategories. It generalizes some results by Kawatani and Okawa
[
KO15
]
. Namely, they proved the following rigidity statement. Let
Y
be a smooth and proper variety over the field
\Bbbk
. Let
\mathcal{A}\subset D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(Y)
be a left admissible subcategory. Then by
[
KO15
, Theorem 1.4]
, the subcategory
\mathcal{A}
is closed under tensor products by line bundles from
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
. The ideas for our proofs are more or less the same as in
[
KO15
]
, but translated to a more general setting.

Informally speaking, the following theorem shows that in sufficiently nice situations, given a family of objects in some
\Bbbk
-linear category
\mathfrak{D}
, the condition that an object lies in a fixed left admissible subcategory
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
is open. To make this rigorous, we need to put some restrictions on the category and the family, as seen below.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a proper category over a field
\Bbbk
which has a classical generator. Let
U
be a quasi-compact separated scheme over
\Bbbk
, and let
\mathfrak{D}_{U}
be the base change
\mathfrak{D}\boxtimes\operatorname{Perf}(U)
of
\mathfrak{D}
from
\Bbbk
to
U
.
Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
be a left admissible subcategory.

Let
F\in\mathfrak{D}_{U}
be an object. Then there exists the
largest Zariski-open subset
U^{\prime}\subset U
such that the base change of
F
to
\mathfrak{D}_{U^{\prime}}:=\mathfrak{D}\boxtimes\operatorname{Perf}(U^{\prime})
lies in the subcategory
\mathcal{A}_{U^{\prime}}:=\mathcal{A}\boxtimes\operatorname{Perf}(U^{\prime})
. Moreover, a closed point
u\in U
lies in
U^{\prime}

if and only if the base change
F_{u}\in\mathfrak{D}_{u}
of the object
F
along the morphism
\{u\}\hookrightarrow U
lies in
\mathcal{A}_{u}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{D}
has a classical generator, by Lemma
2.5
, there exists an object
E\in\mathfrak{D}
such that
\mathcal{A}=E^{\perp}
. Since
\mathfrak{D}_{U}
is a
U
-linear category which is proper over
U
, see
[
Per19
, Lemma 4.10]
,
by Lemma
2.8
,
the internal
\operatorname{Hom}
-object
\mathcal{H}om_{U}(E\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{U},F)\in\operatorname{Perf}(U)
exists. Denote it by
\mathcal{H}
.

Let
V
be a quasi-compact and separated scheme with a morphism
f\colon V\to Y
. Let us show that the base change
f^{*}F\in\mathfrak{D}_{V}
lies in the subcategory
\mathcal{A}_{V}
if and only if the pullback
f^{*}\mathcal{H}\in\operatorname{Perf}(V)
is a zero object. In order to do this, note that we have the base change isomorphism, see
[
Per19
, Lemma 2.10]
,

f^{*}\mathcal{H}\cong\mathcal{H}om_{V}(f^{*}E,f^{*}F).

By Lemma
2.9
, we see that
f^{*}\mathcal{H}=0
if and only if
f^{*}F\in\langle\operatorname{Perf}(V)\cdot f^{*}E\rangle^{\perp}
. Since
V
is quasi-compact and separated,
by
[
BVdB03
, Theorem 3.1.1]
, there is a classical generator
G_{V}\in\operatorname{Perf}(V)
, and hence
f^{*}\mathcal{H}=0
if and only if
f^{*}F\in\langle G_{V}\cdot f^{*}E\rangle^{\perp}
. Note that the object
E\in\mathfrak{D}
is a classical generator of the subcategory
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{A}
by construction in Lemma
2.5
.
Then by
[
Per19
, Lemma 2.7]
, the object
G_{V}\cdot f^{*}(E)
is a classical generator for the right admissible subcategory
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{A}\boxtimes\operatorname{Perf}(V)
, and by Lemma
2.3
, we conclude that
f^{*}\mathcal{H}=0
if and only if
f^{*}F\in\mathcal{A}_{V}
.

From the discussion above, it is clear that the largest Zariski-open subset
U^{\prime}\subset U
with the property that
F|_{U^{\prime}}\in\mathcal{A}_{U^{\prime}}
is the complement to the set-theoretic support of
\mathcal{H}\in\operatorname{Perf}(U)
. The last claim of the theorem also follows since the embedding of a closed point
f\colon\{u\}\to U
satisfies
f^{*}\mathcal{H}=0
if and only if the point
u
is contained in
U^{\prime}=U\setminus\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{H})
.
∎

Let
Y
be a scheme over a field
\Bbbk
.
We use the notation
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
to refer to the connected component of the identity in the Picard scheme of
Y
over
\Bbbk
. We assume the following condition on
Y
:

(3.1)

\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
exists, is proper, and there exists a Poincaré bundle
\mathcal{P}
on
Y\times\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
.

The properness of
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
implies that
the Fourier–Mukai transform along the object
\mathcal{P}
defines a functor
\operatorname{Perf}(\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y))\to\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
. While Theorem
3.3
only uses the condition (
3.1
) abstractly, we note some sufficient conditions that imply (
3.1
).

Theorem 3.2

(
cf.

[
Mur64
]
)
.

Let
Y
be a proper geometrically normal scheme over a field
\Bbbk
. Then
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
exists. If moreover
Y(\Bbbk)\neq\emptyset
, then the Poincaré line bundle
\mathcal{P}
exists on
Y\times\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
, and hence
Y
satisfies (
3.1
).

Proof.

The existence of the scheme
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
is proved in
[
Mur64
]
.
If
Y(\Bbbk)\neq\emptyset
, then by,
e.g.
,
[
Kle05
, Theorem 2.5]
, the relative Picard functor of
Y/\Bbbk
is a Zariski sheaf, and thus the inclusion morphism
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(Y)
corresponds to a universal line bundle
\mathcal{P}
on
Y\times\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
.
∎

Theorem
3.1
gives a general infinitesimal rigidity property for admissible subcategories. In a manner conceptually similar to
[
KO15
, Theorem 1.4]
, we can deduce from it a global result for the invariance of admissible subcategories under the action of
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}
, though since we do not assume
\Bbbk
to algebraically closed, we have to state the result in a slightly less transparent way.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
Y
be a proper scheme over a field
\Bbbk
that satisfies condition (
3.1
). Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a
Y
-linear category which is proper over
Y
and has a classical generator. Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
be a left admissible subcategory. Let
\mathcal{P}
be a Poincaré line bundle on
Y\times\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
, and let
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}\colon\operatorname{Perf}(\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y))\to\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
be the Fourier–Mukai transform along
\mathcal{P}
.
Then
\mathcal{A}
is closed under the action of
\operatorname{im}\,(\Phi_{\mathcal{P}})\subset\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
.

Remark
.

Assume that
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
is smooth (
e.g.
, if the characteristic is zero).
If
p
is a
\Bbbk
-point of
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
, then
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathcal{O}_{\{p\}})=\mathcal{L}_{p}
, where
\mathcal{O}_{\{p\}}\in\operatorname{Perf}(\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y))
is a skyscraper sheaf and
\mathcal{L}_{p}
is the corresponding line bundle on
Y
. Hence Theorem
3.3
implies that
\mathcal{A}
is closed under the action by all elements of
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)(\Bbbk)\subset\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
. When the field
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed, this statement is in fact equivalent to Theorem
3.3
, but to work with arbitrary fields, we need a more powerful result.

Proof.

Consider the base change of the
Y
-linear category
\mathfrak{D}
to the product
Y\times\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)}\subset\mathfrak{D}_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Y\times\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(Y)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathrm{Pic}^{0}(Y)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\operatorname{Spec}\,\Bbbk\hbox to0.0pt{.\hss}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)}}

By Lemma
2.4
, we can choose some classical generator
G_{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathcal{A}
. To prove the theorem, it is enough to show that the set of objects
\operatorname{im}\,(\Phi_{\mathcal{P}})\cdot G_{\mathcal{A}}\subset\mathfrak{D}
lies in the subcategory
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
. We can simplify this statement using the definition of the Fourier–Mukai transform
\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}
and the projection formula from Lemma
2.11
:

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mathcal{P}}(-)\cdot G_{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{Y*}\left(\pi_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)}^{*}(-)\otimes\mathcal{P}\right)\cdot G_{\mathcal{A}}\cong\pi_{Y*}\left(\left(\pi_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)}^{*}(-)\otimes\mathcal{P}\right)\cdot\pi_{Y}^{*}(G_{\mathcal{A}})\right)\cong

\displaystyle\cong\pi_{Y*}\left(\pi_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)}^{*}(-)\cdot(\mathcal{P}\cdot\pi_{Y}^{*}(G_{\mathcal{A}}))\right).

If the object
\mathcal{P}\cdot\pi_{Y}^{*}(G_{\mathcal{A}})\in\mathfrak{D}_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)}
lies in the subcategory
\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)}
, then we are done: the action of
\operatorname{Perf}(\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y))
preserves
\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)}
by definition, and the pushforward functor
\pi_{Y*}
sends
\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)}
to
\mathcal{A}
by the second part of Lemma
2.11
, so we would conclude that
\operatorname{im}\,(\Phi_{\mathcal{P}})\cdot G_{\mathcal{A}}\subset\mathcal{A}
. Thus, it remains to prove that
\mathcal{P}\cdot\pi_{Y}^{*}(G_{\mathcal{A}})\in\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)}
.

Since
\mathfrak{D}
is proper over
Y
and
Y
is proper over the base field, by Theorem
3.1
, there exists the
largest Zariski-open subset
U\subset\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
such that the restriction
(\mathcal{P}\cdot\pi_{Y}^{*}(G_{\mathcal{A}}))|_{U}\in\mathfrak{D}_{U}
lies in the subcategory
\mathcal{A}_{U}\subset\mathfrak{D}_{U}
. Since the restriction of the object
\mathcal{P}\cdot\pi_{Y}^{*}(G_{\mathcal{A}})\in\mathfrak{D}_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)}
to the origin
\{\mathcal{O}_{Y}\}\subset\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
is the object
G_{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathfrak{D}
, which by definition lies in
\mathcal{A}
, by the second part of Theorem
3.1
, we know that
U
contains the origin, and in particular is not empty.

We claim that
U
is closed under multiplication in the sense that for any finite field extension
L\supset\Bbbk
, the set of
L
-points
U(L)\subset\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)(L)
is closed under multiplication. To simplify the notation, we only spell out the full argument for
\Bbbk
-points. By the definition of the Poincaré line bundle
\mathcal{P}
, the restriction of the object
\mathcal{P}\cdot\pi_{Y}^{*}(G_{\mathcal{A}})\in\mathfrak{D}_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)}
to a point
p\in\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)(\Bbbk)
corresponding to a line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{p}\in\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
is the object
\mathcal{L}_{p}\cdot G_{\mathcal{A}}
in the category
\mathfrak{D}
. Thus by the second part of Theorem
3.1
, it is enough to show that if
\mathcal{L}_{1}
,
\mathcal{L}_{2}
are two objects in
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
such that
\mathcal{L}_{i}\cdot G_{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathcal{A}
for
i=1,2
, then
(\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2})\cdot G_{\mathcal{A}}\in\mathcal{A}
. Note that by the definition of
G_{\mathcal{A}}
, we have
\mathcal{A}=\langle G_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle
, so we see that

(\mathcal{L}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{2})\cdot G_{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{L}_{1}\cdot(\mathcal{L}_{2}\cdot G_{\mathcal{A}})\in\mathcal{L}_{1}\cdot\mathcal{A}=\langle\mathcal{L}_{1}\cdot G_{\mathcal{A}}\rangle\subset\mathcal{A}.

The case of a nontrivial finite extension
L\supset\Bbbk
is handled similarly, using line bundles on
Y_{L}
and the fact that the base change
(G_{\mathcal{A}})_{L}\cong G_{\mathcal{A}}\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{L}
is a generator for
\mathcal{A}_{L}
since the morphism
\operatorname{Spec}\,L\to\operatorname{Spec}\,\Bbbk
is affine.

Thus
U
is a nonempty Zariski-open subset of
\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
which is closed under multiplication. This implies that
U=\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)
;
i.e.
, the object
\mathcal{P}\cdot\pi_{Y}^{*}(G_{\mathcal{A}})
lies in the subcategory
\mathcal{A}_{\operatorname{Pic}^{0}(Y)}
, which is what we wanted to show.
∎

Now we deduce Theorem
1.4
from Theorem
3.3
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over a field
\Bbbk
. Then
A
is a NSSI variety.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a
\operatorname{Perf}(A)
-linear category which is proper over
A
and has a classical generator. Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
be a left admissible subcategory of
\mathfrak{D}
. According to Definition
1.3
, we need to show that
\mathcal{A}
is closed under the action of
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
.

Let
\hat{A}
be the dual abelian variety.
Consider the functor

\Phi\colon\operatorname{Perf}(\hat{A})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Perf}(A)

given by the Fourier–Mukai transform with respect to the Poincaré line bundle
\mathcal{P}
on
A\times\hat{A}
. Since
A
satisfies the condition (
3.1
), by Theorem
3.3
, the subcategory
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
is closed under the action of
\operatorname{im}\,(\Phi)\subset\operatorname{Perf}(A)
. By
[
Muk81
]
, the functor
\Phi
is an equivalence of categories, in particular essentially surjective. Thus
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
is closed under the action of
\operatorname{Perf}(A)
, and hence
A
is NSSI.
∎

Theorem 3.5

( = Theorem
1.4
)
.

Let
Y
be a scheme over a field
\Bbbk
. If
Y
admits an affine morphism to an abelian variety over
\Bbbk
, then
Y
is NSSI.

Remark
.

If
Y
is a smooth projective variety and
\Bbbk
is algebraically closed, then the condition in Theorem
3.5
is satisfied if and only if the Albanese morphism of
Y
is a finite morphism.

Proof.

Let
f\colon Y\to A
be an affine morphism to an abelian variety. By Proposition
3.4
, the variety
A
is NSSI. Since
f
is affine, Lemma
2.14
implies that
Y
is also NSSI.
∎

4. Families of NSSI schemes

In this section, we show that the converse to Theorem
1.4
does not hold. In Theorem
4.1
, we give a new way to construct NSSI schemes, and the resulting schemes do not necessarily admit affine maps to abelian varieties. Namely, we show that the total space of a flat proper family of NSSI schemes over a NSSI base is itself a NSSI scheme. This can be applied, for example, to the Albanese map of a bielliptic surface: all fibers are elliptic curves, and the base of the fibration is an elliptic curve. This example is discussed in more detail in Corollary
4.2
.

Theorem 4.1

( = Theorem
1.5
)
.

Let
\pi\colon Y\to B
be a flat proper morphism of quasi-compact separated schemes over a field
\Bbbk
. Assume that
B
is NSSI and that for any closed point
b\in B
, the fiber
Y_{b}:=\pi^{-1}(b)
is a NSSI scheme over the field
\Bbbk(b)
.
Then
Y
is NSSI.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{D}
be a
Y
-linear category which is proper over
Y
and has a classical generator. Let
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
be a left admissible subcategory. Via the morphism
\pi\colon Y\to B
, we may consider
\mathfrak{D}
as a
B
-linear subcategory. Since
\pi
is a proper flat morphism, the category
\mathfrak{D}
is proper over
B
; see
[
Per19
, Lemma 4.9]
. By assumption,
B
is NSSI, which implies that the left admissible subcategory
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}
is closed under the action of
\operatorname{Perf}(B)
. By Proposition
2.6
, this implies that
\mathcal{A}
can be considered as a
B
-linear category. In particular, it is valid to consider the base change of
\mathcal{A}
along an arbitrary map to
B
.

Pick some closed point
b\in B
, and let
j\colon Y_{b}\hookrightarrow Y
be the fiber over that point. Consider the base change diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}_{b}\subset\mathfrak{D}_{b}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}\subset\mathfrak{D}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{Y_{b}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\{b\}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B\hbox to0.0pt{.\hss}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}

Here
\mathcal{A}_{b}
and
\mathfrak{D}_{b}
are defined as base changes of
B
-linear categories
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathfrak{D}
, respectively, along the inclusion
\{b\}\hookrightarrow B
. By
[
Per19
, Lemma 3.17]
, the subcategory
\mathcal{A}_{b}
is left admissible in
\mathfrak{D}_{b}
. Since
the category
\mathfrak{D}_{b}
is obtained from
\mathfrak{D}
by the base change along an affine morphism
\{b\}\hookrightarrow B
, the pullback of the classical generator of
\mathfrak{D}
is a classical generator of the category
\mathfrak{D}_{b}
by
[
Per19
, Lemma 2.7]
.

Moreover, since
\mathfrak{D}
is equipped with a
Y
-linear structure, by Lemma
2.12
, the category
\mathfrak{D}_{b}
, which as defined is only
\{b\}
-linear, has the induced structure of a
Y_{b}
-linear category as the base change of
\mathfrak{D}
along the morphism
j\colon Y_{b}\hookrightarrow Y
. Thus over
Y_{b}
, we have a
Y_{b}
-linear category
\mathfrak{D}_{b}
which is proper over
Y_{b}
and has a classical generator, and a left admissible subcategory
\mathcal{A}_{b}\subset\mathfrak{D}_{b}
. By assumption, the fiber
Y_{b}
is NSSI, so we conclude that
\mathcal{A}_{b}
is closed under the action of
\operatorname{Perf}(Y_{b})
on
\mathfrak{D}_{b}
.

By Lemma
2.5
, there exists an object
E\in\mathfrak{D}
such that
E^{\perp}=\mathcal{A}
. To show that
\mathcal{A}
is closed under the action of
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
, it is enough to prove that for any
A\in\mathcal{A}
, we have the inclusion
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)\cdot A\in E^{\perp}
. By Lemma
2.9
, this is equivalent to the fact that the mapping object
\mathcal{H}om(E,A)\in\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
is zero.

Recall from Definition
2.10
that the morphism
j
induces the pullback functor
j^{*}\colon\mathfrak{D}\to\mathfrak{D}_{b}
. We have a compatibility result for mapping objects, see
[
Per19
, Lemma 2.10]
:

j^{*}\mathcal{H}om(E,A)=\mathcal{H}om(j^{*}E,j^{*}A).

By the second part of Lemma
2.11
, we know that
j^{*}A\in\mathcal{A}_{b}
and that
j^{*}E\in\mathcal{A}_{b}^{\perp}
. The subcategory
\mathcal{A}_{b}\subset\mathfrak{D}_{b}
is closed under the action of
\operatorname{Perf}(Y_{b})
; in particular,
\operatorname{Perf}(Y_{b})\cdot j^{*}A\in\mathcal{A}_{b}
. Thus, applying Lemma
2.9
on
Y_{b}
, we see that
j^{*}\mathcal{H}om(E,A)=0
.

Since
b
is an arbitrary closed point of
B
, we conclude that the pullback of the mapping object
\mathcal{H}om(E,A)\in\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
to a fiber over any closed point of
B
is zero; hence
\mathcal{H}om(E,A)=0
for any object
A\in\mathcal{A}
. As discussed above, this implies that
\mathcal{A}
is closed under the action of
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
, and thus
Y
is a NSSI scheme, as claimed in the statement.
∎

As an explicit application of the theorem, we show that bielliptic surfaces are NSSI varieties, even though their Albanese morphism is not finite.

Corollary 4.2
.

Any bielliptic surface over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
is a NSSI variety.

Proof.

Let
Y
be a bielliptic surface. Recall from
[
Bea96
, Chapter VI]
that the Albanese morphism of
Y
is a fibration
f\colon Y\to E
for some elliptic curve
E
, and each fiber is isomorphic to some fixed elliptic curve
F
. Since elliptic curves are NSSI by Theorem
1.4
, we can apply Theorem
1.5
to the Albanese morphism of
Y
to conclude that
Y
is a NSSI variety.
∎

5. Products and fibrations

In this section, we discuss some applications of the NSSI property. We restrict ourselves to the class of smooth projective varieties, and in this section, we assume that
\Bbbk
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. In Lemma
5.3
, we show that NSSI varieties are stably indecomposable in the following naive sense: if
Y
is a NSSI variety, then for an arbitrary smooth projective variety
X
, any admissible subcategory in
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X\times Y)
is induced by an admissible subcategory in
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X)
. Note that when applied to the case
X=\{\mathrm{pt}\}
, this result recovers Lemma
2.13
.

After that, in Proposition
5.4
, we use the NSSI property to deduce the nonexistence of phantom subcategories in some varieties, such as total spaces of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
- and
\mathbb{P}^{2}
-families over NSSI varieties. For example, this shows that there are no phantom subcategories in the surface
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times E
, where
E
is an elliptic curve.

We start with an observation about linear admissible subcategories. Given a morphism
\pi\colon\mathfrak{X}\to U
, a
U
-linear admissible subcategory in
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(\mathfrak{X})
can be thought of as a family of admissible subcategories in the fibers of the morphism
\pi
. The moduli spaces of such fiberwise admissible subcategories have been studied in the paper
[
BOR20
]
. When applied to a special situation where
\pi
is an étale-locally trivial fibration, their results imply the following.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathfrak{X}\to U
be a smooth and proper morphism which is an étale-locally trivial fibration with fiber
X
. Assume that
U
is a connected excellent scheme over
\mathbb{Q}
and that
\pi
admits a relative ample invertible sheaf. Then for any point
u\in U
, the base change map

\left\{\parbox{99.58464pt}{\begin{center}$U$-linear admissible \\
subcategories \\
$\mathcal{A}\subset D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(\mathfrak{X})$
\end{center}}\right\}\xrightarrow{\text{restriction to $\mathfrak{X}_{u}\simeq X$}}\left\{\parbox{99.58464pt}{\begin{center}admissible \\
subcategories \\
$\mathcal{A}_{X}\subset D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X)$
\end{center}}\right\}

is an injection.

Proof.

By
[
BOR20
, Theorem A]
, there exists an algebraic space
\mathrm{SOD}_{\pi}\to U
which is étale over
U
such that the set of global sections
\mathrm{SOD}_{\pi}(U)
is the set of
U
-linear admissible subcategories of
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(\mathfrak{X})
and the fiber of
\mathrm{SOD}_{\pi}
over the point
u\in U
is the set of admissible subcategories in
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X)
. Since
\pi
is assumed to be étale-locally trivial, the étale morphism
\mathrm{SOD}_{\pi}\to U
is by construction also étale-locally trivial over
U
, in particular separated; see
[
Sta15
, Tag 02KU]
. A global section of a separated étale morphism over a connected scheme is uniquely determined by its value over a single point
u
, and this is exactly the claim of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be smooth projective varieties over a field
\Bbbk
of characteristic zero. Let
\pi\colon X\times Y\to Y
be the projection morphism. Let
\mathcal{A}\subset D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X\times Y)
be an admissible subcategory. If
\mathcal{A}
is closed under the action of
\pi^{*}(D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(Y))\subset D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X\times Y)
, then there exists an admissible subcategory
\mathcal{A}_{X}\subset D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X)
such that
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{X}\boxtimes D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(Y)
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.6
, the subcategory
\mathcal{A}\subset D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X\times Y)
can be equipped with
Y
-linear structure. Pick a point
y\in Y
. By Lemma
5.1
, the subcategory
\mathcal{A}
is uniquely determined by its restriction to the fiber
\pi^{-1}(y)
, which is an admissible subcategory
\mathcal{A}_{X}\subset D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X)
. Since the base change
\mathcal{A}_{X}\boxtimes D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(Y)
is a
Y
-linear admissible subcategory of
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X\times Y)
with the same restriction to the fiber, we conclude that
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{X}\boxtimes D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(Y)
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
Y
be a smooth projective variety which is NSSI. Let
X
be any smooth projective variety, and let
\mathcal{A}\subset D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X\times Y)
be an admissible subcategory. Then there exists an admissible subcategory
\mathcal{A}_{X}\subset D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X)
such that
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{X}\boxtimes D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(Y)
.

Proof.

Consider the projection morphism

\pi\colon X\times Y\to Y
.
The category
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X\times Y)
thus may be viewed as a
Y
-linear category, with action given by the tensor product with the pullbacks along
\pi
. Since
Y
is assumed to be a NSSI variety, the subcategory
\mathcal{A}
is closed under the action of
\operatorname{Perf}(Y)
. Then we are done by Lemma
5.2
.
∎

This work started with the following application in mind. It provides many new examples of varieties which have nontrivial semiorthogonal decompositions but do not contain any phantom subcategories.

Proposition 5.4

( = Proposition
1.6
)
.

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Let
Y
be a smooth projective variety over
\Bbbk
which is NSSI.

(1)

Let
X
be either the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
or a del Pezzo surface. Then there are no phantom subcategories in the derived category
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X\times Y)
.

(2)

Let
\pi\colon\mathfrak{X}\to Y
be an étale-locally trivial fibration with fiber
\mathbb{P}^{1}
or
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Then there are no phantom subcategories in the derived category
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(\mathfrak{X})
.

Proof.

We deal with part (
1
) first. Let
\mathcal{A}\subset D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X\times Y)
be an admissible subcategory. Since
Y
is assumed to be NSSI, by Lemma
5.3
, there exists an admissible subcategory
\mathcal{A}_{X}\subset D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X)
such that
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{X}\boxtimes D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(Y)
. In particular,
\mathcal{A}
contains pullbacks of all objects in
\mathcal{A}_{X}
along the projection
p\colon X\times Y\to X
. Since the pullback map
p^{*}\colon K_{0}(X)\to K_{0}(X\times Y)
is an injection,
\mathcal{A}
is a phantom in
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X\times Y)
only if
\mathcal{A}_{X}
is a phantom in
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X)
.

By assumption,
X
is either
\mathbb{P}^{1}
or a del Pezzo surface. For
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, it is easy to show that any nontrivial admissible subcategory in
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
is generated by a single line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(n)
, and in particular there are no phantom subcategories. If
X
is a del Pezzo surface, then under our assumptions on the base field,
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X)
has no phantom subcategories by
[
Pir20
, Theorem 6.35]
. So in both cases, there are no phantoms in
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X)
. Thus
\mathcal{A}
is not a phantom subcategory.

Now consider part (
2
). Let
\mathcal{A}\subset D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(\mathfrak{X})
be an admissible subcategory. By Definition
1.3
and Proposition
2.6
, it is a
Y
-linear subcategory. Note that since the fibers of the morphism
\pi
are projective spaces, the relative anticanonical bundle provides a relatively ample line bundle. For any étale-locally trivial fibration admitting a relatively ample line bundle, Lemma
5.1
shows that
\mathcal{A}
is uniquely determined by its restriction
\mathcal{A}_{X}
to some fiber
j\colon X\subset\mathfrak{X}
. However, since the base change procedure in Lemma
5.1
involves not only the restriction of objects from
\mathcal{A}
to the fiber, but also the closure of the resulting subcategory with respect to direct summands, it is in principle possible that a phantom subcategory in the total space may restrict to a nonphantom subcategory in the fiber.

Another compatibility condition, proved in
[
Kuz11
, Theorem 5.6]
, is that for any object
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
, the pushforward
j_{*}A\in D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(\mathfrak{X})
lies in
\mathcal{A}
. For general étale-locally trivial fibrations, the induced pushforward map
j_{*}\colon K_{0}(X)\to K_{0}(\mathfrak{X})
does not have to be an injection. However, for the cases considered in (
2
), it is, as we show below.

Assume that
\pi\colon\mathfrak{X}\to Y
is an étale-locally trivial fibration whose fiber is a projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
. By the Blanchard–Deligne theorem
[
Del68
, Proposition (2.1) and Section (2.6.2)]
the Leray–Serre spectral sequence computing the cohomology of the total space
\mathfrak{X}
degenerates at
E_{2}
, and the answer is given in terms of the local systems formed by the cohomology of the fibers of the map
\pi
. Since all fibers are projective spaces, the cohomology is generated by a unique primitive ample class; hence the action of the (étale) fundamental group of
Y
on the cohomology
H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
can only be trivial. Thus
H^{*}(\mathfrak{X})
is abstractly isomorphic to the tensor product
H^{*}(Y)\otimes H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
, and the Gysin morphism
j_{*}\colon H^{*}(\mathbb{P}^{n})\to H^{*}(\mathfrak{X})
is an injection. Since the normal bundle to the fiber
j\colon\mathbb{P}^{n}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{X}
is trivial, by the Grothendieck–Riemann–Roch theorem, the pushforward morphism on
K
-theory
j_{*}\colon K_{0}(\mathbb{P}^{n})\to K_{0}(\mathfrak{X})
can be computed by taking Chern characters and applying the Gysin map; thus in particular it also is an injection.

To sum up, any admissible subcategory
\mathcal{A}\subset D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(\mathfrak{X})
is uniquely determined by the admissible subcategory
\mathcal{A}_{X}\subset D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X)
on some fiber. If
X
is
\mathbb{P}^{1}
or
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, then as explained in part (
1
), there are no phantom subcategories in
D^{b}_{\!\mathrm{coh}}(X)
. In particular,
\mathcal{A}_{X}
contains some object
A\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
with nonzero class in
K_{0}(X)
. The object
j_{*}A
lies in
\mathcal{A}
, and as argued above, when
X
is a projective space, the class of the pushforward in
K_{0}(\mathfrak{X})
is nonzero; hence
\mathcal{A}
is not a phantom subcategory.
∎
